Title: IVC Town Planning

1. AT A GLANCE
- Town planning (grid, drains) = the most distinctive feature of the IVC.
- Large-scale use of burnt bricks in almost all constructions.
- No temples or palaces clearly found, unlike Mesopotamia and Egypt.
- Only the corbelled arch was known, never the true arch; no iron.

2. CITADEL AND LOWER TOWN
- Mohenjodaro, Harappa: citadel on the west (higher), lower town on the east.
- Kalibangan: citadel and lower town, both fortified.
- Dholavira: only city in three parts – citadel, middle town, lower town.
- Chanhudaro: the only Harappan city without a citadel.
- Lothal, Surkotada: citadel inside one fortified enclosure.

3. BRICKS, STREETS AND HOUSES
- Standard brick ratio 1:2:4 (thickness : width : length).
- House bricks 7.5×15×30 cm; city-wall bricks 10×20×40 cm.
- Grid layout: streets crossed at right angles; main streets about 10 m wide.
- Banawali: only city with radial streets.
- Houses round a central courtyard; doors opened on side lanes (Lothal is the exception).

4. DRAINAGE AND WATER
- Covered street drains with manholes; better than Mesopotamia and Egypt.
- Mohenjodaro: about 700 brick wells.
- Dholavira: 16 reservoirs, dams and channels; some rock-cut.
- Banawali: no systematic drainage.

5. GREAT BATH AND PUBLIC BUILDINGS
- Great Bath: Mohenjodaro citadel; 11.88 × 7.01 m, 2.43 m deep.
- Burnt bricks in gypsum mortar; bitumen waterproofing; used as a ritual bath.
- Great Granary (45.71 × 15.23 m): largest building, Mohenjodaro.
- Harappa granary: 12 rooms in two rows of six, near the Ravi.
- Pillared assembly hall (20 pillars) and college of priests: Mohenjodaro.
- Fire altars: Kalibangan (seven), Lothal, Banawali, Rakhigarhi.
- Dholavira: stadium (~283 × 47 m); ten-sign signboard near the North Gate.

6. EXAM TRAPS
- "The IVC knew iron" is always false.
- Dholavira = reservoirs; Mohenjodaro = wells.
- Great Granary = Mohenjodaro; two rows of six = Harappa.
- Bricks had the same ratio, not the same size.
