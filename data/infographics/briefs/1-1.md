Title: Indus Valley Civilization – Discovery, Chronology, Extent

1. AT A GLANCE
- Proto-historic (script undeciphered), Bronze Age; India's first urbanisation.
- Area about 1.3 million sq km – larger than Egypt and Mesopotamia; population ~5 million.
- Most sites on the Ghaggar–Hakra system (about 250 sites, 6 cities).
- Metals: copper, bronze, gold, silver (first use in India), lead, tin – no iron.
- Urban culture, unlike the pastoral Rigvedic Aryans.

2. DISCOVERY TIMELINE
- 1826: Charles Masson described the Harappa ruins.
- 1921: Harappa found by Daya Ram Sahni.
- 1922: Mohenjodaro found by R. D. Banerji.
- 1924: Sir John Marshall announced the civilisation; coined "Indus Valley Civilisation".
- 1931: Chanhudaro (N. G. Majumdar). 1954: Lothal (S. R. Rao).
- Ropar (B. B. Lal, 1950): first site dug in independent India.
- Dholavira: excavated by R. S. Bisht from 1990. Rakhigarhi: Amarendra Nath (1997).

3. CHRONOLOGY
- Early Harappan: 3300–2600 BCE – Kot Diji, Amri, Sothi; villages.
- Mature Harappan: 2600–1900 BCE – planned cities, 1:2:4 bricks, script.
- Late Harappan: 1900–1300 BCE – de-urbanisation; OCP, Cemetery-H.
- D. P. Agrawal (radiocarbon): 2500–1750 BCE.

4. BOUNDARY SITES
- North: Manda – Chenab, Jammu.
- South: Daimabad – Pravara, Ahmednagar, Maharashtra.
- East: Alamgirpur – Hindon, Meerut, western UP.
- West: Sutkagendor – Makran coast, Baluchistan.

5. SITES AND RIVERS
- Harappa – Ravi (Punjab, Pakistan). Mohenjodaro, Chanhudaro – Indus (Sindh).
- Lothal – Bhogava (Gujarat). Kalibangan – Ghaggar (Rajasthan).
- Ropar – Sutlej. Banawali – Rangoi. Rangpur – Bhadar.
- Rakhigarhi (Hisar, Haryana; ~550 ha): largest Harappan site in India.
- Dholavira: UNESCO WHS 2021 – India's 40th. Mohenjodaro: UNESCO WHS 1980.
- Meluhha = Mesopotamian name for the Indus region; Dilmun = Bahrain.

6. EXAM TRAPS
- Rakhigarhi is in Haryana, not Rajasthan.
- Lothal is on the Bhogava, not the Narmada.
- Lothal = "Mini Harappa"; Mohenjodaro = "Mound of the Dead".
- Sohgaura, Mehrgarh and Burzahom are not Harappan sites.
