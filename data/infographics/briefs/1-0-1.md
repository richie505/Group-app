Title: Pre-Harappan Cultures – Mehrgarh: Earliest Farming and Settled Life

1. AT A GLANCE
• Mehrgarh: Kachhi plain, Baluchistan, near the Bolan Pass; c. 7000 BCE.
• Earliest agriculture (wheat, barley), domestication and settled life in the subcontinent.
• Excavated by J.-F. Jarrige (1974).
• Seven periods (I–VII): remains from the Stone Age (Neolithic) to the Harappan period – unlike Amri, Kot Diji or Kalibangan.

2. TIMELINE – MEHRGARH PERIODS
• Period I (c. 7000 BCE): aceramic Neolithic; mud-brick houses; barley, wheat; sheep, goat, cattle.
• Period II: pottery and cotton begin (c. 5000 BCE, oldest cotton).
• Period III onward: copper working, wheel-made painted pottery, beads.

3. BURIALS AND FARMING
• Burials: under or near the houses, flexed, with grave goods, sometimes with goats.
• Neolithic Baluchistan and Indus plains – crops: wheat, barley, dates, cotton.
• Main animals: sheep and goats.

4. EARLY VILLAGES (table: Village | Area | Excavator)

5. EXAM TRAPS
• Mehrgarh is a Neolithic site, not a Harappan city.
• "Earliest settled life" and "earliest agriculture" = Mehrgarh – not Kili Gul Muhammad, Quetta or Kalat.
