page title: Orbits and Launch Physics
t: Term | Meaning
   row: **LEO** | **Low Earth Orbit** (up to ~2,000 km)
   row: SSO | Sun-synchronous, near-polar; passes a place at the **same local solar time** – PSLV's speciality
   row: **GTO** | **Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit**: elliptical, apogee near 36,000 km – GSLV, LVM3
   row: GEO | Geostationary orbit over the equator; satellite appears **fixed in the sky** (reached via GSLV, not PSLV's polar orbits)
   row: Apogee / perigee | **Farthest / nearest** point of an orbit
   row: Strap-on booster | Extra motors fixed to the core for lift-off thrust
b: **Eastward launch:** satellites are launched **eastward** to use the Earth's **west-to-east rotation** (a free speed boost); sites **nearer the equator** gain most. The boost does **not** give "escape velocity" – an orbiting satellite never reaches escape velocity.
b: **Not always east:** polar / SSO launches go **southward** (from Sriharikota a **dog-leg around Sri Lanka**).
b: **Sriharikota** (SDSC-SHAR, Andhra Pradesh) on the **east coast**: rockets fly over the sea; all Indian orbital launches, incl. private ones, go from here.

## page d7ea38400a56
topic: Communication Satellites – Insat and Gsat Series
page title: Geostationary Orbit – Physics of a Communication Satellite
t: Orbit | Fact
   row: Low Earth | 160–2,000 km; ISS ~400 km
   row: **Geostationary (GEO)** | About **36,000 km** (35,786 km) above the equator = orbit **radius ~42,000 km** (42,164 km from Earth's centre); period **24 hours (1 day)**
   row: Polar | Passes over the poles
   row: Elliptical | Apogee and perigee differ
   row: Circular | Centripetal force equals gravitational force
b: **Geostationary:** revolves **in the same direction as the Earth rotates** (west to east) with the same period, so it appears fixed over one point; it stays at a **fixed height** that does **not depend on its mass**.
b: **What keeps it in orbit:** the **centripetal force**, supplied by the **Earth's gravitational pull on the satellite** – not a rocket engine, not the Sun.
b: **No energy needed** to keep orbiting: in near-vacuum there is no drag; solar panels only power the instruments.
b: **Global TV:** **geostationary satellites** relay TV across the world (three cover nearly the whole globe); ground and space waves are short-range.
b: **Uplink / downlink:** sending data **up to the satellite / network** = **uplink**; receiving from it = downlink.
b: Launcher → elliptical **GTO** → satellite's own motor raises it to GEO.

h: Order
b: Wavelength falls: **radio (incl. microwave) → infrared → visible (red … violet) → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays**.

h: Passive vs active sensors
t: Type | Energy | Examples
   row: Passive | Reflected sunlight or emitted heat | Cameras (visible, infrared, multispectral), radiometers, MODIS

b: Laser uses: LASIK, LiDAR, holography – **MRI is not** a laser use (it uses magnets and radio waves).
h: Which band for what
t: Band | Use
   row: Optical (visible/NIR) | Reflected light; needs daylight and clear sky
   row: **Thermal infrared** | Emitted heat → **volcanic eruptions**, forest fires, urban heat
   row: **Microwave (radio region)** – SAR | **Penetrates clouds** and darkness → all-weather imaging of cloud-covered disaster areas
   row: Hyperspectral | Hundreds of narrow bands → materials and chemicals

page title: Radar, Doppler Effect and Stealth
h: RADAR and the Doppler effect
b: **RADAR** (Radio Detection And Ranging): **detects and fixes the position** (range, direction, speed) of objects with radio waves.
b: **Doppler effect:** apparent change in frequency from relative motion – source **approaching → higher** pitch; **receding → lower** (a train moving away sounds lower).
b: **Uses:** **police speed radar guns**, **satellite tracking**, medical ultrasound (blood-flow velocity), **Doppler Weather Radar** (cyclones, **heavy rainfall and thunderstorms**), **expanding universe** (red shift = moving away, Hubble).
t: Radar type | Principle / use
   row: Doppler | Speed from frequency shift
   row: SAR | Imaging at night and through clouds
   row: **AESA** | T/R module on every element; electronic beam, no moving antenna; **tracks multiple targets** – Uttam, MF-STAR
   row: VHF anti-stealth | Long waves pick up stealth aircraft
   row: Over-the-horizon | HF waves bounced off the ionosphere
b: **Uttam AESA** (LRDE): solid-state active phased-array fire-control radar, wide-band RF front end, adaptable to fighters (Tejas Mk-1A); "**not capable of tracking multiple targets**" is the false statement.
h: Indigenous radars
t: Radar | Role
   row: **Swathi** | **Weapon-locating radar** (guns, mortars, rockets); BEL; exported to Armenia
   row: **Surya** | **VHF radar that tracks stealth aircraft**
   row: Swordfish | BMD long-range tracking (Green Pine based)
   row: Arudhra | IAF medium-power 4D surveillance radar
   row: Ashwini | Low-level lightweight AESA radar (cruise missiles, drones)
   row: Rajendra | Fire-control radar of Akash
   row: INDRA | Low-level surveillance (Indian Doppler Radar)
h: AWACS vs AEW&C
t: AWACS | AEW&C (Netra)
   row: Large aircraft (Il-76 **Phalcon**), long range, full battle management | Smaller jet (**Embraer EMB-145**), early warning + limited control; **DRDO-CABS Bengaluru**
h: Stealth
b: **Stealth technology:** **absorbing / deflecting radar waves** – very small **radar cross-section (RCS)** + **Radar Absorbing Material**; not "invisible to the eye".
b: Stealth objects **can** be detected at specific (low, VHF/UHF) frequencies; metamaterials are used to **reduce**, not increase, scattering.
b: **IIT Kharagpur:** low-cost **radar-absorbing metamaterial** to cloak soldiers and equipment from enemy radar.
b: **AWACS** (Airborne Warning and Control System) = an **air-based long-range radar surveillance and control system** for air defence, called the **"eye in the sky"** (IAF: Il-76 Phalcon).
