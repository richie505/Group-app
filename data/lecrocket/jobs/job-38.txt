page title: Why Seasons Occur – Tilt, Revolution, Perihelion
b: **Seasons = revolution of the Earth + inclination (tilt) of its axis**; **day-length variation** from season to season = **revolution on a tilted axis** – not rotation, not the elliptical orbit, not oceans/mountains/altitude.
b: Axis tilted **23½° (23.5°) from the perpendicular** = **66½° to the orbital plane**.
b: **Parallelism**: the axis keeps pointing the same way (towards the **Pole Star**) through the year – because of **angular momentum** – so hemispheres tilt alternately towards (summer) and away (winter) → **opposite seasons**.
b: Full set: **axial inclination + parallelism + revolution + spherical shape** → changing **angle of insolation** and **day length**.
b: Tilt gives seasons, unequal days, the tropics and polar circles – **not day and night** (that is rotation, with the **circle of illumination** dividing day from night; rotation also gives the **Coriolis** deflection, to the **right in the NH**).
b: **What-if:** tilt raised from 23.5° to **30°** → **more intense seasonal contrasts**; axis **perpendicular** to the orbit → no seasons, equal day and night all year, Sun always over the Equator – but the North Pole would **not** lie always in darkness.
t: Point | Date | Distance | NH season
   row: **Perihelion** (nearest the Sun) | ~**3/4 January** | ~147 million km | **Winter**
   row: **Aphelion** (farthest) | ~**4 July** | ~152 million km | Summer
b: Earth is **nearest the Sun in northern winter**, so distance does not cause seasons.
b: Long term: **precession** (axial wobble) – **Vega** was pole star ~14,000 years ago and will be again in ~12,000 years; **Milankovitch cycles** drive long-term climate change. Melting ice moving mass away from the axis **slows rotation**.

## page 89e97e56eb59
topic: Insolation and Heat Budget; Distribution of Temperature, Inversion
page title: Heating of the Atmosphere – Radiation, Conduction, Convection, Advection
h: How the air is heated (asked 8 times)
t: Process | Mechanism
   row: **Insolation** | **Direct heating** by incoming short-wave solar radiation
   row: **Conduction** | Heats the **lower layers** of air in contact with the warm ground
   row: **Convection** | **Vertical** transfer – warm air rises, cool air sinks (troposphere)
   row: **Advection** | **Horizontal** transfer by wind (e.g. the **Loo**)
   row: **Terrestrial radiation** | Long-wave infra-red from the Earth = **main heater** of the air
b: Match: **Advection – horizontal; Convection – vertical; Insolation – direct; Conduction – lower layers** (a-I, b-II, c-III, d-IV).
b: The atmosphere is **heated from below**: the troposphere is warmest near the ground because it is **heated by the Earth's surface** – **not** because it is closest to the Sun or makes its own heat. It is not mainly heated by incoming sunlight.
b: Sun's radiation = **short-wave**: mostly **visible light + short-wave (near) infra-red**; the Earth re-radiates **long-wave infra-red**, not ultraviolet (Asst. Comm. 2009: both "receives at infra-red end" and "re-radiates as UV" are wrong → **neither**).
b: Long-wave terrestrial radiation is absorbed mainly by **carbon dioxide and water vapour** (concentrated in the lower atmosphere) – correct statement; infra-red is **not** part of the visible spectrum and is **not** two-thirds of insolation (about half of insolation is infra-red).
b: **Conduction, convection and radiation** are tied to long-wave (terrestrial) heat transfer; **scattering** is a short-wave process.
b: **Radiation needs no medium** (UPRO 2017); conduction and convection need matter.
b: Wind on a hot summer day = **convection current** of air; trade winds too arise from **convection** (Hadley cell).
