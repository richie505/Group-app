## page b4494f3b7815
topic: Tribe – Definition, Classification, Distribution and Characteristics
page title: Characteristics and Classification of Tribes
h: Meaning and features
b: **'Tribe'** comes from the **Latin 'tribus'**. **Marshall Sahlins:** a tribe is a **segmental** organisation of equivalent, **unspecialised multi-family groups, clans or bands**.
b: Features: **segmentary but egalitarian**; **no mutual interdependence** (unlike caste); **direct access to land**; primitive subsistence technology (**shifting cultivation**, hunting, gathering); **animism**; monogamy with polygamy, **polyandry** among **Toda** and **Khasa**; mostly patrilineal, **matrilineal** among **Khasi, Jaintia, Garo**.
b: **Animism** = belief in and worship of **nature spirits**; **Totemism** = a clan's sacred bond with an **animal or plant totem**, revered and **not killed**; goes with **clan exogamy**.
b: **Shifting cultivation** = slash-and-burn; called **Jhum** in North-East India.
b: **Division of labour** in tribal society rests mainly on **age and sex (gender)**; **age-groups (age-sets)** are a basis of tribal stratification.
b: **Gender taboos:** among the **Santhal**, women may **not touch the plough** or **thatch (cover) the roof** of a house.
b: **Kula ring** (Trobriand Islands, Malinowski): ceremonial exchange of **shell necklaces (soulava)** and **armshells/bracelets (mwali)**, moving in opposite directions.
b: **Change:** loss of languages (e.g. **Bo**, Andamans, extinct 2010), new economic classes among Chotanagpur tribes in Assam tea gardens.
h: Classification

   row: Racial – H.H. Risley | Dravidian (Paniyan, Santals), Mongoloid (Himalayan, NE)

   row: Linguistic – Austro-Asiatic | Santhal, Munda, Khasi, Shompen
   row: Linguistic – Dravidian | Gond, Oraon/Kurukh, Khond/Kui
   row: Linguistic – Tibeto-Burman | NE tribes, e.g. Nyishi
   row: Economic – Vidyarthi and Rai | Food-gatherers/hunters (Chenchu), shifting cultivators, settled agriculturists, artisans, pastoralists, industrial labour
b: **Khasi = Austro-Asiatic**, not Tibeto-Burman.
