b: **Two components:** **biotic** (producers, consumers, decomposers) and **abiotic** (energy, water, air, soil, nutrients, climate). "Evolution" is a process, not a component.
b: An ecosystem is a **natural, open system**: energy and matter enter and leave; it has its own **productivity**; it is **not a closed system with fixed boundaries** (UPSC/APPSC "NOT correct" item).
b: The **biosphere** (narrow zone where **lithosphere, hydrosphere and atmosphere** meet) is treated in most books as an **open system** – energy enters as sunlight and leaves as heat (UPPSC RO/ARO 2017 key: A true, R false). Strictly, the Earth is open for energy but nearly **closed for matter**.
b: All components are **interdependent**: a change in one population affects the rest – so "components are not interdependent" is false, while "human activities affect the environment" is true.
b: **Functions:** energy flow (one-way), nutrient cycling, production, **self-regulation (homeostasis)**; the **food chain** is the **dynamic part** of an ecosystem, while producers, consumers and decomposers form its structure.
b: **Biogeochemical (nutrient) cycle:** movement of **chemical elements** (C, N, P) through **biotic and abiotic** components – energy does not cycle; **photosynthesis removes CO₂**; the **phosphorus cycle begins with rock weathering**.
h: Abiotic components
t: Type | Factors
   row: Energy | Sunlight – primary source and driving force
   row: Climatic | Light, temperature, rainfall, humidity, wind
   row: **Edaphic** | **Soil** – texture, pH, minerals, drainage
   row: Topographic | Altitude, slope, aspect
   row: Materials | CO₂, water, nitrates, phosphates; humus and other organic compounds
b: **Abiotic factor:** any **non-living** component that affects organisms or their environment – air, water, light are abiotic; plants, bacteria and animals are biotic.
b: **Liebig's Law of the Minimum:** growth limited by the nutrient in shortest supply. **Shelford's Law of Tolerance:** both too little and too much limit.
b: **Stenothermal** organisms tolerate only a **narrow** temperature range; **eurythermal** a wide range (likewise stenohaline / euryhaline for salinity).
b: **Cold-blooded (poikilothermic)** animals – **frogs**, snakes, lizards, fish, crocodiles – take the temperature of their surroundings.
h: Producers (autotrophs) – trophic level I
b: **Autotrophs** make their own food; their **carbon and energy needs are met by photosynthesis** (NCERT X): they take in **CO₂ and water** (not oxygen) and, with **sunlight and chlorophyll**, convert them into **carbohydrates**, which give the plant energy.
b: **Photosynthesis** turns **light energy into chemical energy**; it needs light – normally daytime sunlight, but it also runs under moonlight or artificial light, so it is not tied to clock time.
b: On land the major producers are **herbaceous and woody plants**; in water, **phytoplankton** / microscopic **algae** and seaweeds.
b: **Chemoautotrophs:** bacteria of deep-sea vents / active volcanoes oxidise **hydrogen sulphide**; many **archaea** (methanogens) use energy from chemical compounds – they too are producers.
b: Some **bacteria** (green sulphur bacteria) photosynthesise with **invisible infrared** light.
b: **Producers act as intermediaries between the biotic and abiotic components** (many books also call **decomposers** the link, because they return nutrients to the abiotic pool).
b: **Chlorella** (a green alga) has the highest efficiency of converting solar energy into chemical energy among the usual options.
b: **Oxygen:** most of Earth's oxygen comes from **algae** / marine phytoplankton – ocean phytoplankton and photosynthetic bacteria give **about 50%** of the world's oxygen, more than rainforests; oxygen-rich surface water holds far **less** oxygen than air.
b: **Cactus** photosynthesises in its green **stem** (leaves reduced to spines).
h: Consumers and decomposers
t: Component | Examples
   row: Primary consumers (herbivores) | Cow, deer, goat, rabbit, caterpillar, **zooplankton**
   row: Secondary consumers | Wolves, frog, fox
   row: Tertiary / top carnivores | Lion, tiger, hawk, eagle
   row: Omnivores | **Man** (primary and secondary consumer), bear
   row: **Decomposers** (saprotrophs) | **Bacteria and fungi** – not viruses
   row: **Detritivores** | **Earthworms, millipedes, woodlice**, nematodes – not jellyfish or seahorses
b: **Fungi are heterotrophs** (mostly saprotrophs that absorb food from dead matter; some parasitic or symbiotic).
b: **Parasitic nutrition:** obtaining food from another living organism **without killing it**.
b: **Decomposition:** breakdown of dead organic matter by **microorganisms (bacteria, fungi)** into simple inorganic nutrients; biodegradable waste is converted into useful compost by **bacteria**.
b: Steps: **fragmentation → leaching → catabolism → humification → mineralisation**; fully decomposed litter = **humus**. Fast when detritus is rich in nitrogen and sugars and climate warm and moist; slow with lignin, chitin, cold or anaerobic conditions.
b: If **decomposers are removed**, **mineral (nutrient) movement is blocked** – energy flow would continue for a while, but nutrients are not recycled.
b: Macroconsumers = phagotrophs; microconsumers = saprotrophs (osmotrophs). Free-living **Azotobacter** fixes nitrogen.
b: **Protists** are unicellular **eukaryotes**, not prokaryotes; cyanobacteria are photosynthetic prokaryotes.
h: Types of ecosystem
t: Type | Examples
   row: Natural terrestrial | Forest, grassland, desert, tundra, mountain
   row: Natural aquatic | Pond, **lake**, river, wetland, estuary, ocean
   row: **Artificial (man-modified)** | **Crop field**, aquarium, park, village; **city** = extreme example
b: A **lake / pond** is the classic **self-sufficient (complete, self-regulating) natural ecosystem**; an aquarium, farmland or polyhouse depends on human inputs.
n: Shankar Environment 10th ed. Part 3 p.13-16
b: **Frost** is an **abiotic** stressor of forests (freezes soil moisture, injures tissue); oomycetes, parasites and pathogens are biotic.
b: **Water balance:** organisms must maintain body water to live; **terrestrial** life needs **fresh water** because it cannot tolerate the high salt of saline water.
b: **Biotic match:** humans – **top-order heterotrophs**; saprotrophs – **recycle nutrients**; reptiles – **stenothermal** (narrow temperature tolerance); camel – **passes little urine**.
