page title: Features of a Developing (Underdeveloped) Economy
h: The label
b: India is a **developing economy** – not developed, backward or stagnant; it still shows **underdeveloped** features.
b: India is categorised as a "developing country" mainly because of **low per capita income** – not the service-sector share, quality of manpower or lack of industrial growth.
h: Features of the Indian economy
b: **Low per capita income and unemployment** – the salient features; **massive unemployment**.
b: **Predominance of agriculture:** the Indian economy is **primarily an agricultural economy**; **industrial dominance** marks a developed economy, not India (UP 2004: **I, III and IV**).
b: **Low capital formation** (needs a high savings rate) and **low productivity of labour**.
b: **Inequitable distribution of income, high dependency ratio, slower rate of increase in national income** – underdevelopment features.
b: **NOT a feature: lack of natural resources** – India has resources; they are under-utilised.
b: **Indicators of a developed country:** high literacy, low death rate, high per capita income – a **high proportion of labour in the primary sector is NOT** one.
b: **Dualistic economy** = **coexistence of a modern and a traditional economy** (not inequality, population growth or resource waste).
b: Agriculture of underdeveloped countries has **surplus labour**; their trade = primary exports, manufactured imports.
h: Causes of underdevelopment
b: **Most important cause:** co-existence of **under-utilised manpower and under-utilised natural resources**.
b: Recorded causes: rapid population growth with high dependency ratio, low human development (HDI rank about **130**), high poverty – **NOT mainly the working efficiency of the population**.
b: **Vicious circle of poverty (Ragnar Nurkse):** low income → low saving → low investment → low productivity → low income; the vicious circle argument points out the **supply-side connection between productivity and income**.
b: **Labour-surplus economy:** surplus farm labour whose marginal productivity is near zero.
b: **Disguised unemployment:** more workers than needed; marginal productivity of the extra workers **zero**; removing them would **not** reduce output; mainly in **agriculture** and the unorganised sector.
b: Capital formation in a developing economy can rise only with an adequate **savings rate**.

h: Production
