h: Direct vs indirect
t: Basis | Direct tax | Indirect tax
   row: Burden | **Cannot be shifted** – impact and incidence on the **same person** | **Shifted** to the consumer through prices – impact and incidence on different persons
   row: Examples | **Income tax, corporation tax, wealth tax**, capital gains, property, gift, estate duty, expenditure tax, **STT** | **Customs, excise, sales tax, VAT, service tax, GST**, entertainment, octroi, entry tax
   row: Nature | Generally **progressive** | Generally **regressive**
   row: Price effect | **Non-inflationary**; does **not directly raise the price** of goods | **Inflationary** – raises prices
   row: Administered by | **CBDT** – statutory body under the **Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963** | **CBIC** (formerly CBEC)
b: **Not direct**: customs duty, sales tax, excise, octroi – the usual "odd one out" against income, corporation, wealth and gift taxes.
b: **Indirect pair**: **customs and excise** (corporation and wealth taxes are direct).
b: **Income, corporate and wealth taxes – all direct**; "1 and 2 only" for income + corporate tax against service tax and customs.
b: **Income tax** does not directly increase the price of a commodity – trade/business tax, import and excise (manufacturing) duties do.
b: **GST** – a comprehensive **indirect** tax on supply, **destination-based**; it is not related to income.
b: **Service tax** – an **indirect tax levied by the Central Government**, introduced **1994-95** (subsumed in GST 2017).
b: **STT** – a **direct tax** on **transactions through the stock market** (from **1 October 2004**), though collected by the exchange.
b: **"Paper taxes"**: wealth, gift and estate duty – never yielded much revenue; **wealth tax** gives the **least** revenue of the major taxes.
b: **Impact** = on whom the tax is first imposed; **incidence** = who finally bears it; moving the burden forward (consumers) or backward (suppliers) = **tax shifting**. Property tax cannot be shifted through prices.
b: **Incidence and elasticity**: if demand is **perfectly inelastic (elasticity zero)** the price rises by the full tax and the **consumer bears it all**; with infinite elasticity the price cannot rise.
b: **Cut in direct taxes** → higher **disposable income** and consumption demand.
h: Base / taxable event of each tax
t: Tax | Taxable event / base
   row: **Central excise** | **Manufacture / production** of goods (paid by the manufacturer) – "manufacture only"

   row: Sales tax / VAT | **Sale** of goods
   row: Service tax | Provision of service
   row: GST | **Supply** of goods or services (from 1 July 2017)
   row: **Corporation tax** | **Income / net profit of companies** – not production, sales or stock
