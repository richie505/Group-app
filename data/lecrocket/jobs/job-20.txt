topic: Amendment of the Constitution – Article 368, Majorities, Procedure, Limitations
page title: Article 368 – Where It Is, What It Says, Nature of the Procedure
b: **Article 368**, the **only Article of Part XX**, gives Parliament the **power to amend the Constitution and the procedure** for it (not Arts. 14, 320, 330, 352, 356, 360, 366, 367, 369, 370 or 395).
b: Wording: Parliament may, "in exercise of its **constituent power**, amend by way of **addition, variation or repeal** any provision of this Constitution in accordance with the procedure laid down" – subject to the **basic structure** (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973).
b: **Art. 368 itself provides for two types** of amendment – **special majority**, and **special majority + ratification by half the States**. The **simple-majority** changes (Arts. 2-4, 169 etc.) lie **outside Art. 368**, so books also speak of "three ways" of amending the Constitution.
b: **Art. 13(4)** (added by the **24th Amendment, 1971**): an amendment made under Art. 368 is **not "law"** under Art. 13, so **Art. 13(2) does not apply** to it (it still applies to laws of Parliament, ordinances and executive orders).
b: Art. 368 has itself been amended by the **24th (1971)** and the **101st (2016**, GST Council Art. 279A added to the ratification list) Amendments – both statements true.
b: Borrowed from **South Africa** (with the election of Rajya Sabha members).
b: Nature: **neither rigid nor flexible but a synthesis of both** – "partly rigid, partly flexible". Dual nature shown by: some provisions need a **special majority**, some only a **simple majority** like ordinary laws, and the **federal structure cannot be altered easily** (State ratification). FRs are **not** amendable "without judicial review".
b: **K.C. Wheare**: the amending process strikes a **good balance between rigidity and flexibility** ("variety in the amending process is wise but rarely found"); he called the Constitution **quasi-federal**.
b: **Granville Austin**: the process "appears **complicated**" but is "**merely diverse**" – one of the most ably conceived aspects of the Constitution.
b: Latest enacted amendment (as of Nov 2026): the **106th (2023)**, women's reservation; the 129th and 130th are pending Bills and the 131st Bill was defeated (Apr 2026).

page title: Procedure under Article 368 – Step by Step
b: 1. Initiated **only by a Bill in either House of Parliament** (Lok Sabha **or** Rajya Sabha) – **not** in a State Legislature and **not** by the President.
b: 2. Moved by a **minister or a private member**; **no prior permission/recommendation of the President**.
b: 3. Passed in **each House separately** by a **special majority** – a majority of the **total membership** and **two-thirds of members present and voting**.
b: 4. **No joint sitting** (Art. 108 does not apply): if either House rejects it, the Bill **fails**.
b: 5. If it touches a **federal provision**, it must also be **ratified by the legislatures of half the States** by a **simple majority** (not all States).
b: 6. The **President must give assent** – he can neither withhold assent nor return the Bill; made obligatory by the **24th Amendment, 1971** (368(2) "**shall give his assent**").
b: So "prior recommendation of the President" is **false**, "President must assent" is **true**, "special majority in both Houses, no joint sitting" is **true**. "Initiated in Lok Sabha only" and "ratified by all the States" are **false**.
