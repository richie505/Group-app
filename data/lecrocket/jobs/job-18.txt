## page 5cedfe4646e9
topic: Salient Features of the Indian Constitution
page title: Constitutionalism and Supremacy of the Constitution
h: Constitutionalism
b: **Constitutionalism** = government whose powers are **limited** by a supreme Constitution; it **recognises the need to limit the powers of government**.
b: It is **not inherent in every constitution** – a country can have a constitution without constitutionalism (a dictator's constitution).
b: **Antithesis of constitutionalism = despotism** (absolute, unlimited rule); limited power and restraint of power are its features.
b: **Constitutional government** = one that places **effective restrictions on the authority of the State in the interest of individual liberty** – not restrictions on liberty in the interest of the State.
b: **Limits in India:** Fundamental Rights (**Art. 13** – laws inconsistent with FRs are void), judicial review, federal division of powers, **basic structure** (**Kesavananda Bharati, 1973**).
b: Parliament's amending power is limited by the **basic structure doctrine**; basic-structure elements include **supremacy of the Constitution**, rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, limited amending power.
h: Supremacy of the Constitution
b: The **Constitution is supreme** in the Indian political system – not Parliament, not the Supreme Court, not religion.
b: **Governmental organs owe their origin to the Constitution** – the Union and States derive their powers from it (APPSC Asst. Social Welfare).
b: **Why the Constitution is above Parliament:** the **Constitution specifies how Parliament is formed and what its powers are** (not because it is older or its makers were more eminent).
h: Judicial review
b: **Judicial review** = power of the SC/HCs to declare laws and executive orders **unconstitutional**; the phrase is **nowhere used in the Constitution**, but **Arts. 13, 32, 226** (and others) **explicitly confer** the power.
b: **Basic structure is NOT defined in the Constitution** – it was evolved by the Supreme Court (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973).
b: **Synthesis** of British **parliamentary sovereignty** and American **judicial supremacy**: courts can strike down laws; Parliament can amend most of the Constitution.
b: **Scope narrower than in the USA:** India has **"procedure established by law"** (Art. 21), the USA **"due process of law"**; courts test a law only **against the supreme written Constitution** (FR violation, lack of competence, repugnancy), not its wisdom → judicial review is **restricted because the Constitution is supreme**, not because the executive or legislature is supreme.
x: II (Constitution provides for judicial review) is correct on the text of Arts. 13, 32, 226 → **Only II**.
n: Indian_Polity Part 1 ch.1 p.3-4 (constitutionalism, K.C. Wheare), ch.2; mrunal.org/2021/11/upsc-prelims-2020-answerkey-error-discrepancy.html
b: **Nature:** the Constitution is the **fundamental (foundation) law** of the land – every other law derives its authority from it (not common, derived or divine law).
b: **What a constitution cannot do:** it sets basic rules, limits power, protects rights and expresses shared values – it **cannot ensure that the 'right' people come to power**.
