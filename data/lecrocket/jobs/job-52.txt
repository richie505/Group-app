page title: rDNA Technology – Tools, Vectors and Steps
h: What genetic engineering does
b: **Genetic engineering / recombinant DNA (rDNA) technology:** cutting, joining and transferring genes between organisms; **rDNA = DNA formed by combining genetic material from different sources** – also called **chimaeric (chimeric) DNA**.
b: **Transgenic organism:** an organism whose DNA carries a **foreign gene from another species** (the "transgene"), inserted artificially for a **desired characteristic or product**.
b: **GMO:** any organism whose genome is altered by genetic engineering – genes moved **in ways that would not occur naturally**.
b: **Across species:** rDNA moves genes **plant to plant, animal to plant and microbe to higher organism** – all three possible.
b: **Mouse eight times its size** with a human growth-hormone gene = **genetic engineering** (not hybridisation, mutation breeding or hormonal feeding).
b: **Tried with success:** **disease resistance, growth promotion, animal cloning**; **human cloning is NOT** (banned, no fruitful result) → "1, 2 and 3".
h: Tools
t: Tool | Role
   row: **Restriction endonuclease** | **"Molecular scissors" / chemical knives** – **cuts DNA at specific recognition (base) sequences**, not at random
   row: **DNA ligase** | **"Molecular glue"** – **joins DNA fragments** (gene + vector); in replication it **joins Okazaki fragments**
   row: **Plasmid** | Small, extra-chromosomal, usually **circular double-stranded DNA** of bacteria; replicates independently; the commonest **vector**
   row: **pBR322** | Early, widely used **E. coli** cloning vector; p = plasmid, **BR = Bolivar and Rodriguez**
   row: **Origin of replication (ori)** | **The specific DNA sequence that initiates replication**; alien DNA linked to it can multiply in the host
   row: **PCR** | **Amplifies (copies) a specific DNA segment in a test tube – no living cells needed** (heat-stable Taq polymerase, 20-40 cycles)
   row: **RNAi** | Gene silencing
b: **Two bacteria most used:** ***Escherichia coli*** (host for cloning) and ***Agrobacterium tumefaciens*** (plant vector).
b: ***Agrobacterium tumefaciens*:** soil bacterium that **causes crown gall disease** (dicots) by transferring **T-DNA** of its **Ti plasmid**; the disarmed Ti plasmid makes it "**Nature's genetic engineer**" → useful for agriculture, crown gall agent and gene-transfer tool = **all of the above**.

b: **Isolate DNA → cut at specific sites (restriction enzyme) → join to vector with DNA ligase (rDNA) → insert into host (transformation) → select, multiply, express product.**
b: Recombinant plasmid enters a bacterium by **transformation**; bacteria multiply fast and act as factories.
b: **Gene cloning:** making many copies of a desired gene or DNA fragment; **genetic library** = a collection of cloned DNA of an organism.
t: Field | Biotech use
   row: Agriculture | Transgenic / GM crops
   row: Medicine | Drugs, vaccines, diagnostics, gene therapy; **human insulin from GE bacteria**
   row: Industry | **Microbial production of useful products**
   row: Research | **Biochips** – DNA, RNA **and protein** chips for diagnostics and drug discovery

page title: The Greenhouse Effect and Heating of the Atmosphere
h: Definition and mechanism
b: **Greenhouse effect** = natural warming of the **lower atmosphere** (surface and troposphere) by **absorption and re-radiation of heat** by GHGs – exam words: "**increase in atmospheric temperature due to concentration of GHGs**"; a **naturally occurring** phenomenon (not caused by biodegradable waste in water).
b: Concept first proposed by **Joseph Fourier** in the **1820s**.
b: Without it, Earth's average temperature would be about **−18°C** (Shankar: −19°C) instead of **+15°C**.
b: **Step 1:** atmosphere is **transparent to incoming short-wave** solar radiation.
b: **Step 2:** the surface re-emits **long-wave infrared** (8,000-25,000 nm) – **not ultraviolet**.
b: **Step 3:** CO2 and water vapour **strongly absorb infrared** (14,000-25,000 nm) – the atmosphere is **opaque to outgoing long-wave**.
b: **Step 4:** GHGs re-radiate heat in all directions, partly back to the surface: GHGs warm the air by **trapping heat** (not generating it).
b: **Glass analogy:** the glass of a greenhouse or of a **solar cooker** lets light in and stops long-wave heat going out – the same principle.
b: **Global warming** = gradual long-term rise in the average temperature of the atmosphere and oceans; mainly human (fossil fuels); GHGs **intensify** the natural effect (TSPSC: all four statements true).
h: Heating of the atmosphere – heat budget
t: Insolation (100 units) | Share
   row: Reflected (albedo): 27 from cloud tops, 2 from snow/ice, rest scattered | **35**
   row: Absorbed by the atmosphere | **14**
   row: Absorbed by the surface | **51**
b: The atmosphere is heated **mainly by long-wave terrestrial radiation**, indirectly from the earth, not by incoming solar radiation.
b: **Clouds and gases reflect about one-fourth** of insolation (27 units from cloud tops); the surface does **not** re-emit heat as UV.
b: **Cloudy nights are warmer**: clouds stop heat radiating away from the ground and send it back.
b: **Fresh snow** has the highest albedo – reflects about **80-85%** of sunlight (more than sand desert, paddy or prairie).
b: Heat trapped by the atmosphere maintains the average temperature, and CO2 is a good absorber (II, III true); "below freezing **everywhere** without an atmosphere" is too sweeping – the day side would be very hot, like the Moon.
x: The atmosphere is warmed from below: short-wave in, long-wave out. "Heated more by incoming solar radiation" is always the wrong statement.

b: **Ocean acidification** = ongoing **fall in ocean pH** caused by the **uptake (dissolution) of atmospheric CO2** – the **primary cause**; not oil spills, sewage, microplastics or acid rain (acid rain matters only locally).
b: Called the **"evil twin of global warming"** and **"the other CO2 problem"**.

h: Chemistry
b: **Step 1:** CO2 + H2O → **carbonic acid** → H+ + bicarbonate.
b: **Step 2:** extra **H+ binds carbonate ions** – carbonate concentration falls.
b: **Step 3:** **less carbonate** left for shells and skeletons; the ocean's ability to neutralise CO2 (buffering) falls.
b: Surface pH **8.2 (pre-industrial) → ~8.1**: a fall of 0.1 = about **26-30% rise in acidity**; the ocean is **still alkaline**.
h: Factors and results
t: Factor | Result
   row: CO2 uptake by oceans | **Steady decline in ocean pH**
   row: Melting of Arctic ice | Exposes water to air → **more surface CO2**, faster acidification
   row: Plankton blooms (eutrophication) | Bloom collapse → **bacterial respiration** adds CO2
   row: Decreased carbonate ion concentration | **Lower carbonate** → less ability to neutralise CO2
b: Worse in **cold, temperate and Arctic waters**: CO2 is more soluble in cold water.
b: **Cement** sector: about 5-8% of global anthropogenic CO2.
x: pH falls only 0.1, yet acidity rises ~30% (log scale). The ocean has not turned acidic.
