b: **Location:** ~**90%** of capacity in a **100 km x 3 km belt along the Hooghly** – most mills in the **Hugli basin**, **West Bengal** (APPSC G-I 2024).
b: **Why Hooghly:** raw jute of the delta, **soft/standing water for retting**, NW-1 and **Kolkata port**, coal from Raniganj, cheap labour, capital.
b: **Retting:** soaking jute stems in water so microbes loosen the fibre – the process that separates fibre from the stem.
b: **Mills:** WB 71 of 97 (2026); **AP 2nd** – Chittivalasa, Nellimarla; **Bhajrang Jute Mill, Guntur** – shut down, a long labour/management dispute.
b: **Bhatpara** = jute centre (Titagarh = paper). **National Jute Policy 2005**; **JUTE-ICARE** = certified seed + microbial retting with **SONA**.
b: **Largest exporter:** Bangladesh. **Jute Packaging Materials Act 1987:** 100% foodgrains, 20% sugar in jute bags.
n: Thenewsminute.com (Bhajrang Jute Mill shutdown)

b: Factors: raw material, power, market, labour, transport, **water**, government policy, clustering, historical (colonial ports).
t: Orientation | Examples
   row: Raw-material (weight-losing) | Iron and steel (~4 t input per t), sugar (cane perishable), **cement (limestone)**
   row: Power | **Aluminium** (bauxite ore; 14,000–15,000 kWh/t)
   row: Market (weight-gaining, fragile) | Bakery, soft drinks; refineries (Koyali, Mathura, Barauni)
   row: Footloose | **Software**, diamond polishing, electronics assembly
b: **Raw material NOT the primary factor:** **electronics** – unlike iron and steel, sugar and cement.
b: **Footloose example:** **software** (not oil refinery, sugar or aluminium). Clusters: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Noida.
b: **Principal raw materials:** limestone → **cement**; bauxite → **aluminium**; manganese → ferro-manganese.
b: **Weber (1909)**: MI above 1 (iron and steel, sugar) → near raw material; ubiquitous inputs → market.
b: **Cotton** = pure material (no weight loss), so cotton textiles spread all over India.
b: Railways let industry move inland; colonial nodes Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai.
