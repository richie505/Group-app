b: **National income (NI):** the **money value of all final goods and services** produced by the **normal residents** of a country during a year – only **final** goods, so no double counting.
b: **Income view:** NI = **sum of factor incomes** (wages, rent, interest, profit) earned by normal residents (Police Constable 2019) – not government income, budget or PSU profits.
b: **Source:** generated only from **productive activity** – not "any money-making activity".
b: **Flow, not stock:** NI is measured over a period; **national wealth** is a stock at a point of time.
b: **Product = income:** the value of output (national product) is distributed as factor incomes, so national product and national income are **synonymous**; production by labour and capital **automatically generates income**.
h: The standard measure

topic: Monetary Policy – Objectives, Instruments (crr, Slr, Repo, Reverse Repo, Msf, Omo, Laf), Transmission
page title: CRR and SLR – Reserve Ratios
h: Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)
b: **CRR = Cash Reserve Ratio:** the share of a bank's **Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL)** that it must keep as **cash balances with the RBI**.
b: **"Required reserve ratio"** = the **certain percentage of deposits every bank must keep as reserves** = **CRR** (APPSC Degree Lecturers 2023).
b: CRR is **fixed by the RBI (the central bank)**, not the Finance Ministry or the market; governed by **Section 42 of the RBI Act, 1934**; **no floor or ceiling** since 2006.
b: Banks get **no interest** on CRR balances; CRR is in **cash only**.
b: **Raising CRR:** commercial banks have **less money to lend** (not the RBI or the Union Government) → **liquidity falls**, **credit creation falls** → helps control inflation.
b: **Lowering CRR:** **credit creation increases**; banks get **higher leverage to liquidity** and the economy **may see increased investment**.
b: **Temporary I-CRR (Aug 2023):** **10%** on the increase in NDTL (19 May–28 Jul 2023), announced **10 Aug 2023**, effective fortnight from 12 Aug; **impounded about ₹1.1 lakh crore**; released in phases (9 Sep, 23 Sep, 7 Oct 2023).
b: CRR applies to the **whole NDTL**; the I-CRR applied only to the **incremental deposit base** (not to "new borrowers").
h: Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)
b: **SLR:** minimum share of NDTL a bank keeps **with itself** in liquid assets **before lending** – **cash, gold and unencumbered approved/Government securities (G-secs, T-bills, SDLs)**; **not priority-sector loans**.
b: **SLR = mechanism by which commercial banks provide credit to the Government** (they buy G-secs to meet it).
b: Older wording "ratio between cash in hand and total assets" = **SLR** – strictly SLR is liquid assets to NDTL.
b: Governed by **Section 24, Banking Regulation Act, 1949**; range **0%–40% of NDTL**; the **25% floor** was removed by the **BR (Amendment) Act, 2007**; **MSS securities count** towards SLR.
b: **Shortfall penalty:** **bank rate + 3%**, then **bank rate + 5%** if it continues.
b: **SLR cut of 50 bps** → more lendable funds → **scheduled commercial banks may cut lending rates** – not "GDP up drastically" or "liquidity down".
b: **Raise SLR to fight inflation; cut SLR for growth.**
h: Trend
t: Date | SLR | CRR
   row: 1991-92 | **38.5%** | 15%
   row: Since reforms | **Both reduced** (Narasimham Committee) | **Both reduced**

   row: 11 Apr 2020 | **18%** | 3% (Mar 2020)
   row: 2026 (now) | **18%** | **3%** (cut 4% → 3% in 2025, ~₹2.5 lakh crore released)
h: CRR vs SLR
t: Basis | CRR | SLR
   row: Kept | **With RBI** | **By the bank itself**

h: Meaning
t: Usage | Meaning
   row: Indian | Budget gap met by **borrowing from the RBI**; RBI creates new money – the government **"buys money" from the RBI**

   row: Effect | **Increase in money supply**; creates **extra paper currency**
b: **Deficit financing** = the act of financing a **deficit budget** – **spending in excess of revenue** (total expenditure > total receipts) and sustaining that gap.
b: In India, deficit financing = borrowing from the **RBI** (money creation), not from the public.
b: **Fiscal deficit** (Union Budget) = budgetary deficit + net increase in internal and external borrowings and other liabilities.
b: An economic unit that **spends more than it receives** in a period is a **deficit unit** (APPSC Degree Lecturers 2017).
h: Purpose in Indian Planning
b: Purpose: **raising resources for economic development** – not redemption of public debt, BoP adjustment or cutting foreign debt; a "pragmatic tool" to finance the **Five Year Plans**.
b: **Small doses** of deficit financing for productive investment need not be inflationary, and it is an **often-used tool** for financing budget deficits.
b: Needed after 1947 because of a **low tax base**, poor compliance, weak private banking and **low domestic savings** (not a high savings rate).
b: Objectives: remedy for depression, raise aggregate demand, subsidies, anti-poverty programmes, PSU losses.
b: Independence to **late 1960s**: little use, to fund plans. **Second Plan**: about **₹1,100 crore** against ₹1,200 crore planned. **Third Plan**: reliance to be **strictly limited**.
b: 1970S onwards: heavy reliance (oil shocks, welfare spending) led to debt and inflation.

b: Deficit financing = the largest source of financing public-sector Plan outlay (Eighth Plan) – ahead of external borrowing.
