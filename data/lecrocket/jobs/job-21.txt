## page d87b453ebbf3
topic: Legislative Procedure – Ordinary, Money, Financial and Constitutional Amendment Bills; Joint Sitting
page title: Money Bill – Articles 109 and 110
h: Definition – Art. 110(1)
b: A Bill is a Money Bill if it contains **only** provisions on: (a) imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any **tax**; (b) regulation of **borrowing** or the **giving of any guarantee** by the Government of India; (c) **custody** of the Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund, payments into or withdrawals from them; (d) **appropriation** out of the CFI; (e) declaring expenditure **charged** on the CFI; (f) receipt, custody or issue of money of the CFI or Public Account, or the **audit of accounts of the Union or of a State**; (g) incidental matters.
b: **Not** Money Bills merely because they provide for (Art. 110(2)): **fines/penalties**, **fees for licences or services**, **taxes by local bodies** for local purposes.
b: **Speaker** decides finally whether a Bill is a Money Bill and **certifies** it (Art. 110(3)); the decision cannot be questioned in any court, in either House or by the President.
h: Procedure – Art. 109
b: Introduced **only in the Lok Sabha**, **only by a minister**, **only on the President's recommendation** (not the PM's). Never in the Rajya Sabha or a joint sitting.
b: **Rajya Sabha**: **cannot amend or reject** it; can only make **recommendations** and must return it within **14 days** (two weeks) – the maximum it can delay it.
b: **Lok Sabha may accept or reject** all or any of the RS recommendations – it is **not bound** to accept them, and no 14-day limit applies to the LS.
b: RS amends it substantially → the LS **may still proceed**, accepting or not accepting the recommendations. Not returned within 14 days → **deemed passed by both Houses** in the form passed by the LS, and sent to the President.
b: So **RS approval is not necessary** to pass a Money Bill; RS "has no power" over it beyond delay of 14 days.

h: What each Article says
t: Article | Subject
   row: 1 | "**India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States**"; States and territories in the **First Schedule**; Art. 1(3) territory of India
   row: 2 | **Admission or establishment of new States** (not part of the Union of India)
   row: 3 | **Formation of new States; alteration of areas, boundaries or names** of existing States

b: Part I = **Articles 1–4**; the Constitution calls the Centre the **"Union"** (Union Government, Union List), not "Centre".
b: **"Union" not "Federation" (Ambedkar):** the Indian federation is **not the result of an agreement among States**, and **no State has the right to secede** – "an **indestructible Union of destructible States**".
h: Union of India vs Territory of India
t: Union of India | Territory of India (Art. 1(3))
   row: **States only** – members of the federal system sharing powers with the Union | **States + Union Territories + territories that may be acquired**
b: So **UTs are covered by "Territory of India" and not by "Union of India"** (APPSC: **Only IV**); both expressions are in the Constitution, and the **First Schedule** lists **States and UTs**.
b: Grama: the **Union of India** includes **States only (A only)**.
b: **Acquiring territory:** India as a sovereign State acquires foreign territory **"as understood in public international law"** – cession, conquest, occupation (**N. Masthan Sahib v. Chief Commissioner, Pondicherry**); e.g. **Pondicherry, Goa, Daman and Diu, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Sikkim**. Such territory need not join an existing State (it may be a UT).
h: First Schedule
b: **First Schedule = names of the States and UTs and their territorial extent** → it **must be amended to create a new State**. Fourth Schedule = Rajya Sabha seats (also amended).
h: Article 3 – power and procedure
b: **Power vests in Parliament** (not the President, the Council of Ministers or an SRC): form a new State by **separating** territory, **uniting** States or parts, uniting territory to a part of a State; **increase or diminish** area; alter **boundaries or name**.
b: **Admission of new States is NOT under Art. 3** – it is **Art. 2**; creating a new State out of existing States is **Art. 3**.
b: **Procedure:** Bill only on the **President's recommendation**; President **refers it to the State legislature concerned** for views within a time limit; **Parliament is not bound** by the views; **no fresh reference** needed for each amendment to the Bill; for a **UT, no reference** at all.
b: **Consent of the State is not required** to alter its boundaries – Parliament acts "without their consent".
b: Passed by a **simple majority** in the ordinary legislative process (**Art. 4(2)**).
b: **Renaming Kerala → 'Keralam'**: Union Cabinet approval began the **Article 3** procedure.
b: Parliament **cannot cede** territory under Art. 3 – cession needs an **Art. 368** amendment (see Cession).

b: **House of introduction:** an Art. 3 Bill may be introduced in **either House** of Parliament – there is no rule that it goes first to the Rajya Sabha.
b: **Art. 3 Explanation I:** in clauses (a)–(e) 'State' **includes a Union territory** – an interpretive explanation, not itself a provision for forming new States.
b: **'State' defined (political science):** a community of persons **permanently occupying a definite territory**, **independent of external control** (sovereign) and possessing an **organised government**.

h: Office
b: **Article 63**: "There shall be a Vice-President of India" – the **second-highest office** (second highest dignitary) after the President; modelled on the **American Vice-President**, but unlike the US VP he does **not succeed** to the Presidency for the rest of the term – he only **acts** until a new President enters office.
b: **Articles 64 and 89(1)**: the VP is the **ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Council of States)** – the VP **presides over the sessions of the Rajya Sabha**. Not the President, PM, Speaker, Deputy Speaker, CJI, CEC or Leader of Opposition; the Chairman is **not separately elected, appointed or nominated**.
b: The VP is **not a member** of either House or of any State Legislature (a member elected VP vacates the seat) – so "VP is a member of the Rajya Sabha" is **false**.
b: As Chairman he does **not vote in the first instance**; he has a **casting vote** in a tie. He does not preside while a resolution for **his own removal** is under consideration.
b: He has a **formal function** – chairing the Rajya Sabha (so "no formal function attached to his office" is **false**). Ex officio **President of the Indian Council of World Affairs (ICWA)**.
b: **Article 79**: Parliament = **the President + the Council of States + the House of the People** – the VP, the PM and the Council of Ministers are **not** parts of Parliament.
h: Election (Article 66)
b: **Elected, not appointed** – the only one of VP / PM / Governor / CEC who is **not appointed by the President**.
b: Electoral college = **all members of both Houses of Parliament** – **elected and nominated** MPs (**788** when full). **State Legislative Assemblies and Councils have no role** – so the VP's electoral college is **not** "similar to the President's".
b: **Proportional representation by single transferable vote**, **secret ballot**; each MP's vote has equal value; **20 proposers + 20 seconders**; governed by the **Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952**.

h: Term, oath, resignation (Articles 67, 69)
b: Term **5 years**; continues **until his successor enters upon office** (Art. 67(c)); **re-election any number of times**.
b: **Oath** administered by the **President** (or a person appointed by him) – Art. 69.
b: **Resigns** by writing to the **President** (Art. 67(a)) – not the PM or Parliament.
h: Removal (Article 67(b))
b: Resolution moved **only in the Rajya Sabha** (not the Lok Sabha, either House or a joint sitting), passed by a **majority of all the then members** of the Rajya Sabha (effective majority) and **agreed to by the Lok Sabha** (simple majority); **14 days' notice**; **no grounds** stated; not impeachment.

h: Vacancy (Article 68)
b: Vacancy by expiry: election before the term ends. Casual vacancy (death, resignation, removal): filled **as soon as possible** – **no six-month deadline**; the new VP gets a **full 5-year term**.

h: Acting President (Article 65)
b: On a **vacancy** in the President's office (death, resignation, removal) the VP **acts as President until a new President is elected** and enters office – a **maximum of six months**, because **Art. 62** requires the new President to be elected **within six months** of the vacancy.

b: If both offices are vacant (e.g. the acting VP resigns), the **Chief Justice of India** (then the senior-most SC judge) discharges the President's functions – **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969**.

h: Responsibility
b: **Article 75(3)**: the Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the House of the People (Lok Sabha)** – a **constitutional obligation**; **not to both Houses / "the Parliament"**, even if the PM or Ministers sit in the Rajya Sabha.

h: Non-member Ministers (Article 75(5))

h: Ministers in Parliament (Article 88)
b: Every Minister has the **right to speak and take part** in the proceedings of **either House**, any **joint sitting** and any committee of which he is named a member – but **votes only in the House of which he is a member**. b: **Article 75(1B)**: a member disqualified for **defection** cannot be a Minister until **re-elected**.

b: **Article 93**: the Lok Sabha **chooses (elects)** two of **its own members** as Speaker and Deputy Speaker – the Speaker is **not nominated or appointed by the President**, not elected by the public or by both Houses, but by **all members of the Lok Sabha**. Speaker's provisions: **Arts. 93-96**, 97 (salary), **100** (casting vote), **110** (Money Bill), **118** (joint sitting).
b: Must already be a **member of the House** when elected – the "six months to become a member" grace belongs to **Ministers** (Art. 75(5)), not to the Speaker.
b: Date of the Speaker's election is fixed by the **President** (Rule 7); the Deputy **Speaker**'s by the Speaker (Rule 8).
b: **No separate oath of office** – the Speaker (and Deputy Speaker) take only the **oath as an MP**; the President (Art. 60), VP (Art. 69), PM / ministers (Art. 75(4)) and judges take oaths of office.
b: **Tenure**: not at the pleasure of the President – he holds office **so long as he is a member**; vacates on ceasing to be a member (**Art. 94(a)**).
b: **Resignation (Art. 94(b))**: Speaker → **Deputy Speaker**; Deputy **Speaker** → Speaker (not the President, PM, CJI or RS Chairman). In a State: Speaker → **Deputy Speaker of the Assembly** (Art. 179).
b: **Removal (Art. 94(c))** – of the Speaker **or the Deputy Speaker**: resolution of the **Lok Sabha** passed by a **majority of all the then members** (effective majority) after **at least 14 days' notice** – not two-thirds, not by the President, not by both Houses.
b: **Dissolution**: the Speaker **does not vacate** office – continues **until immediately before the first meeting** of the new Lok Sabha (second proviso to Art. 94); the Deputy Speaker and members go at once. Same for a State Speaker (**Art. 179**); State Speaker and Deputy Speaker are provided by **Art. 178**.
b: No constitutional duty to **resign from his party** on becoming Speaker – that is a British convention, not Indian law.
b: **Article 96** – while a resolution for **his own removal** is under consideration:

   row: Normal sitting | Yes | Yes | **No** | **Yes** (Art. 100(1))
   row: Own removal resolution | **No**, even if present | **Yes** | **Yes** | **No**
b: **Chairman (RS)** during his own removal resolution: cannot preside and **cannot vote** (not a member), but may speak.
b: **Art. 97**: salaries of the Speaker and Deputy Speaker are **charged on the Consolidated Fund of India** (not voted).
b: **14 days' notice** is needed to remove the **President, Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Deputy Chairman**; a **Governor** is removed by the President at will without notice.
b: **Warrant of Precedence**: 1 President, 2 VP, 3 PM, **4 Governor within his State**, 5 former Presidents (5A Deputy PM), **6 CJI and Speaker of the Lok Sabha**, 7 Cabinet Ministers – so a Governor (in his State) ranks above the Speaker, and the Speaker ranks above all Cabinet Ministers except the PM.
b: The Speaker is **not appointed by the President** – the CJI and the service chiefs are.

b: **Art. 85(1)**: the **President summons** each House (separately or together) at such time and place as he thinks fit, but **six months shall not intervene** between the **last sitting of one session** and the **first sitting of the next** → Parliament must meet **at least twice a year**.
b: **Three sessions** – **Budget** (Feb-May, **longest**), **Monsoon** (Jul-Sep), **Winter** (Nov-Dec, **shortest**) – a **convention**, not in the Constitution; no fixed number of sessions or minimum sitting days is laid down. States: **Art. 174** (same six-month rule); State Assemblies averaged only **24 sittings in 2025**.
b: **Session**: first sitting → prorogation/dissolution. **Recess**: the period between **prorogation** and the reassembly in a new session.
b: **Quorum**: **one-tenth** of the total members of the House, **including the presiding officer** – **Art. 100(3)**.

   row: **Adjournment** | Presiding officer | A **sitting**, for hours/days/weeks | Session continues

   row: **Prorogation – Art. 85(2)(a)** | **President** on the **Council of Ministers'** advice (Governor in States) | The **session** | **Bills do not lapse**; notices lapse (except to introduce bills)

b: **Automatic dissolution**: the LS term is **five years from the date appointed for its first meeting** (Art. 83(2)); at its end it dissolves by efflux of time even without a formal order.
b: "Adjournment sine die = terminating a sitting for an indefinite period" (key); prorogation ends the session; dissolution applies only to the LS; adjournment **never** ends a session.

b: The address is **prepared by the Union Cabinet** (it states the Government's policy) and is debated on a **Motion of Thanks**; defeat of the Motion of Thanks = defeat of the **Government**, which must **resign**.
b: **Gap between two sessions** – "shall not exceed **six months**" (flashcard).

b: The **Vice-President** is the **ex officio Chairman** of the Rajya Sabha (**Arts. 64, 89(1)**) – not the President, Speaker or Deputy Speaker.
b: The Chairman is **not a member** of the **Rajya Sabha** – so the Rajya Sabha is the House presided over by a **non-member** (the Speaker of the LS / Vidhan Sabha and the Chairman of a Vidhan Parishad are members of their Houses).
b: Vote: a **casting vote only in a tie** – not "like other members", not for Amendment Bills.
b: Functions: presides and conducts business, protects members' privileges, is the House's spokesperson; he **cannot dissolve the Rajya Sabha** – it is a **permanent House**, never dissolved.
b: Removal: only by **removal as Vice-President** (Art. 67(b)); during that resolution he cannot preside or vote but may speak.
b: **Art. 91**: when the VP **acts as President** (or the Chairman's office is vacant), the **Deputy Chairman** performs the Chairman's duties; the VP then draws the **President's** salary. **Jan 2026 (Justice Varma case)**: SC upheld the Deputy Chairman's Art. 91 power to decide a judge-removal motion while the Chairman's office was vacant.
b: **Deputy Chairman**: elected by the RS from its members (Art. 89(2)); resigns to the **Chairman**; removed by an RS effective majority after 14 days' notice (**Art. 90(c)**). **Panel of Vice-Chairpersons** nominated by the Chairman.

page title: Parliament Secretariat – Article 98
b: **Art. 98(1)**: **each House** has a **separate secretarial staff**, but **posts common to both Houses** may be created.
b: **Art. 98(2)**: **Parliament** may by law regulate recruitment and service conditions of the staff of either House.
b: **Art. 98(3)**: until such a law, the **President**, **after consultation with the Speaker** (or the Chairman for the RS), makes the rules; individual appointment orders are issued by the Speaker / Secretary-General.
b: **Independent of the Government (executive)**: the **Lok Sabha Secretariat** works under the **direct control and supervision of the Speaker** (the RS Secretariat under the Chairman) – not the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs, Home Ministry, PM or President. It is a permanent body, not a five-year one.
b: Head: **Secretary-General** of each House – appointed by the **presiding officer**; rank of the **Cabinet Secretary**. The idea of a separate legislative secretariat was raised in **1926** by **Vithalbhai Patel**.
b: **Presidential election returning officer**: the **Secretary-General of the Lok Sabha or of the Rajya Sabha, by rotation** (appointed by the ECI).
n: Constitution Art. 98; Lok Sabha Secretariat (loksabha.nic.in)

b: **Deputy Speaker**: elected by the LS from its members (Art. 93) on a date fixed by the **Speaker** (Rule 8), communicated to members by the **Secretary-General**.
b: That the Deputy Speaker comes **from the Opposition** is only a **convention** – no constitutional or mandatory provision. Likewise no rule that the Speaker comes from the majority party. (The winner of an LS/Assembly election needs only a **plurality**, not 50% of votes.)
b: **Art. 95**: performs the Speaker's duties when the office is **vacant** and presides when the Speaker is **absent**; when presiding he has the **same powers as the Speaker** and **no appeal lies to the Speaker** against his rulings – he is **not subordinate** to the Speaker.
b: Out of the Chair he is an ordinary member – may debate, move motions, **vote in the first instance**. On being appointed to a committee he becomes its **chairman automatically**.
b: Own removal resolution: cannot preside; may speak and vote in the first instance; no casting vote.
b: Date fixed by the Speaker ✔; Deputy Speaker has the Speaker's powers in the Chair, no appeal ✔; "mandatory opposition/ruling party" ✘; "moved by Speaker, seconded by PM" ✘ → **A and C only**.
b: **Vacancy**: the LS Deputy Speaker's post has been **vacant since June 2019** – through the whole 17th and the 18th Lok Sabha (as of 2026); in the States, **Jharkhand**'s post was vacant for **21 years** (June 2026). Last LS Deputy Speaker: **M. Thambidurai** (2014-19).
b: **Pro-Tem Speaker (Art. 95(1))**: **appointed and sworn in by the President**; usually **one of the senior-most members** of the newly elected House (list prepared by the Legislative section, sent to the **Minister of Parliamentary Affairs**, who seeks the President's approval).
