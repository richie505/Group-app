h: Cloud basics (asked 15 times)
b: **Cloud** = mass of minute water droplets or ice crystals formed by **condensation** of water vapour in free air around **hygroscopic nuclei**, mainly by **adiabatic cooling** of rising air below its dew point – clouds are the result of **condensation**, not evaporation.
b: Clouds **float** because of their **low density**: the droplets are tiny, fall extremely slowly and are held up by gentle updrafts.
b: Four basic forms: **cirrus, cumulus, stratus, nimbus** ("nimbus" = rain-bearing; black or dark grey, dense, opaque).
t: Family (height) | Cloud | Appearance | Weather
   row: High (8,000–12,000 m; **ice crystals**) | **Cirrus** | Thin, detached, **feathery, silky**, always white | Fair; no rain
   row: High | **Cirrostratus** | Thin **milky** sheet; produces the **halo** | Warm front coming
   row: High | **Cirrocumulus** | **Small globular masses**, ripples ("mackerel sky") | Fair
   row: Middle (2–6 km) | **Altostratus / altocumulus** | Grey-blue sheet; Sun as through **ground glass** | Light continuous rain
   row: Low (below 2 km) | **Stratus, stratocumulus, nimbostratus** | Layered; nimbostratus dark, thick, shapeless | Stratus – **drizzle**; nimbostratus – **steady, continuous rain**
   row: Vertical development | **Cumulus** | Heaped **cauliflower**, **flat base**, cotton wool (4,000–7,000 m) | Fair; convective; grows into Cb
   row: Vertical development (to ~15 km) | **Cumulonimbus** | Towering, **anvil** top | Thunderstorm, heavy short rain, hail, cloudburst

b: **Cirrus clouds are composed of ice crystals**; rising domes with flat base = cumulus, and cumulus is **not** a high cloud.
b: **Low cloud set: stratus, nimbus (nimbostratus), cumulus**; **nimbostratus is a low cloud**, cirro- = high, alto- = middle.
b: **Continuous precipitation = nimbostratus**; **highly intense rain = cumulonimbus**.
b: Highest of a given set: **cirrostratus** is the only high cloud among altocumulus, altostratus, cumulus, cirrostratus.
b: **Greatest vertical extent**, convectional rain with lightning and thunder = **cumulonimbus**; rain-bearing clouds = **nimbostratus and cumulonimbus**.
b: **Tornadoes form from cumulonimbus** (supercell) clouds; the funnel cloud and wall cloud are parts hanging from the Cb, not the parent cloud.
b: Rain clouds look **dark/black** because they are thick and dense with droplets, so very little sunlight gets through to the base (strictly the light is mostly scattered/reflected away).

b: Thundercloud (Cb) development needs **condensation, high temperature and humidity, orography and vertical winds – all four** (orographic thunderstorms are common).

b: **Waterspout**: funnel extending from a cumulus/cumulonimbus cloud **over water**; one recorded over the Brahmaputra in Barpeta, Assam (26 Aug 2026).

## page ed87834e819f
topic: Humidity, Evaporation, Condensation, Clouds and Forms of Precipitation
page title: Evaporation, Latent Heat, Boiling Point and Adiabatic Change
h: Evaporation (asked 15 times)
b: **Evaporation / vaporisation** = change from liquid to gas; it is a **physical change** and goes on at all temperatures from the surface.
b: Rate **rises with larger surface area**, higher temperature, **lower humidity**, **stronger wind** and lower pressure; falls with salinity.
b: Oceans give **84%** of annual evaporation, continents 16%.
h: Latent heat
b: **Evaporation absorbs** latent heat; **condensation releases** it → powers **thunderstorms and cyclones**. Freezing (snow forming in clouds) also releases heat = **exothermic**; melting ice absorbs heat = **endothermic**.
t: Change | Latent heat | Note
   row: Fusion of ice | **80 cal/g** (3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg) | Melts at 0°C; temperature constant
   row: Vaporisation of water | **540 cal/g** (2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg) | Boils at 100°C; temperature constant
b: Heat taken to turn liquid into gas **without change in temperature = latent heat of vaporisation**; at the boiling point the **temperature remains constant**.
b: Latent heat of vaporisation is **much greater** than that of fusion; **steam at 100°C holds more heat** than water at 100°C → **steam burns are worse** – it releases latent heat on condensing.
b: Only "an air-conditioner extracts heat at the **evaporator** coils and rejects it at the **condenser** coils" is correct. **Refrigerator**: refrigerant absorbs heat on evaporation, releases it on condensation.
h: Pressure and boiling point
b: **Lower pressure lowers the boiling point** (Asst. Comm. 2018): at high altitude water boils below 100°C because **air pressure is less** → a **pressure cooker** raises pressure and boiling point.
h: Adiabatic change and the uplift chain
b: **Adiabatic** temperature change = by **expansion (cooling) and compression (warming)** of air, without heat being added or removed; compressing gas in a bicycle pump = **adiabatic heating**.
b: Air **descending** a mountain is compressed → **adiabatic warming** (AE 2017), as in the leeward rain shadow.
b: Uplift chain: warm air rises → **adiabatic cooling to the dew point** → **condensation** around nuclei → clouds → droplets grow and fall → precipitation. Rising air does **not** become warmer.
n: NCERT Science (latent heat, boiling point)

h: Measures of humidity (asked 7 times)
b: **Humidity** = water vapour present in the air – the result of the **presence of moisture in the air**; humid summer heat = **muggy** weather (UPUDA 2002).
t: Measure | Meaning | Unit
   row: **Absolute humidity** | **Mass of water vapour per unit volume** of air | g/m³
   row: Specific humidity | Vapour per unit **weight** of air; unchanged by volume/temperature change | g/kg
   row: Relative humidity (RH) | Actual vapour ÷ capacity at that temperature × 100 | %
   row: **Dew point** | Temperature at which air becomes **saturated**; condensation begins below it | °C
b: When air temperature **equals the dew point, RH = 100%** = saturation.
b: **Saturation deficit** = capacity minus actual vapour; **dew point** = temperature to which air must be cooled to saturate → both correct.
b: Warmer air can hold **more** vapour – capacity **increases** with temperature; same vapour + warming → **RH falls**.
b: Water vapour: 0–**4%** of the air (up to 4% in humid tropics), **decreases with height and latitude**; RH higher over oceans than continents.
b: AP: coastal Andhra RH **70–90%**; Rayalaseema below **30%** in peak summer.

page title: Air Masses – Nature, Source Regions and Classification
h: Air mass (asked 2 times)
b: **Air mass** = a large body of air with relatively **uniform temperature and humidity**, formed over a uniform **source region** (land **or** ocean); it controls **temperature, humidity, stability and precipitation**, and a new air mass changes the weather.
b: Ideal source regions are **high-pressure areas with gentle, divergent (anticyclonic) circulation** – the subtropical highs and the polar highs (Siberian, Canadian). Air masses do **not** develop in **cyclonic** conditions, and there are no major source regions in the mid-latitudes.
b: **Bergeron** code: **first letter = moisture** (c = continental, dry; m = maritime, moist); **second letter = thermal source region** (T tropical, P polar, A arctic, E equatorial).
t: Code | Source region | Properties
   row: **mE** | **Warm equatorial oceans** | Hot, very humid
   row: mT | Tropical oceans (Gulf of Mexico, Pacific, Atlantic) | Warm, moist, unstable
   row: cT | Hot deserts – Sahara, Arabia, Australia | Hot, dry, stable
   row: mP | Cold high-latitude (polar) oceans, 40°–60° | Cool, moist
   row: cP | Siberia, northern Canada (cold land) | Cold, dry, stable
   row: cA | Arctic basin, Greenland ice | Very cold, very dry
b: Correct pair: **Maritime Equatorial – warm oceans in the equatorial zone**; mP is not from warm tropical oceans, cT is a desert (not ocean) air mass, cP forms over land.
b: **Polar anticyclones** (Siberian, Canadian, Antarctic Highs) are built by cold, dense **cP/cA** air: **cold-core, strongest near the surface**; clear skies, fog, frost.

page title: Fronts and the Polar Front Theory
h: Fronts
b: **Front** = sloping boundary zone between two contrasting air masses; lighter **warm air rises over denser cold air** (never the reverse) → adiabatic cooling, cloud, rain. **Mid-latitude (temperate) cyclones form along fronts**.
t: Front | Formation | Slope | Weather
   row: Warm | Warm air rides over cold | **Gentle**, gradual uplift | Prolonged light–moderate rain
   row: Cold | Cold air undercuts warm, lifts it sharply | **Steep** | Cumulonimbus, heavy short showers, squalls, sudden temperature fall
   row: Occluded | **Cold front overtakes warm front** | – | Mixed; **last stage** of a temperate cyclone
   row: Stationary | Neither air mass advances | – | Cloud and rain for days
b: **Frontogenesis** = forming/sharpening of a front; **frontolysis** = its weakening and dissolution.
b: Warm front approach: **cirrus → cirrostratus → altostratus → nimbostratus**; after a cold front passes: **cold, clear air**. **Arcus (shelf/roll) clouds** form at the leading edge of thunderstorm outflows or cold fronts.
h: Polar front theory
b: By **Vilhelm and Jacob Bjerknes**, **Bergen School (Norway)**: wave on the polar front → warm and cold fronts → **occlusion** → dissipation.
b: **Western disturbances** reach NW India at the **occluded** stage.

h: What El Nino is (asked 9 times)
b: **El Nino** = an **abnormally warm ocean current** that appears off the coast of **Peru and Ecuador** in the **eastern (and central) tropical Pacific** and flows south along the west coast of South America, **replacing the cold Peruvian (Humboldt) current**. b: **Ocean:** El Nino develops only in the **Pacific Ocean** – not in the Indian Ocean, Bay of Bengal, Atlantic or Mediterranean.
b: **Name:** Spanish for **"the little boy / Christ child"**, because it appears around **Christmas (December)**.
b: **Recurrence:** **irregular**, about every **3–7 years** (2–7 in some books); it does **not occur every year**.
b: The El Nino current carries **warmer, less saline** water (the warm, rain-freshened pool of the western Pacific spreads east) – "all four" statements.
b: **Atmospheric counterpart = the Southern Oscillation**; together they are **ENSO**.
h: Mechanism
t: Step | Normal year | El Nino year
   row: Pressure | **Low over Indonesia / N Australia**, **high off Peru** | Pressure falls over the central/eastern Pacific; a weak high forms in the west

b: Stratosphere **warms with height** because ozone turns absorbed UV into heat.

b: **Himadri:** also called Greater, Higher, Inner or **Central** Himalaya; highest point **Mount Everest** (~8,850 m; now 8,848.86 m). Dehradun is **not** in the Himadri (it is a dun between Lesser Himalaya and Shiwalik).
b: **Himachal:** names for the same range – Lesser, Middle, Lower Himalaya; "Himachal stands for the Middle Himalaya".
b: **Pir Panjal:** the most important Lesser Himalayan range, **~300 km from the Jhelum to the upper Beas** in **J&K and Himachal Pradesh**; Banihal pass crosses it. South-east of the Ravi it continues as the **Dhauladhar**, then the **Mussoorie** range (Uttarakhand) and the **Mahabharat Lekh** in **Nepal**.
b: **Shiwalik:** formed from riverine deposits of the "Siwalik river" (Pascoe, Pilgrim) about **2–5 million years ago** (Pliocene, **Cenozoic**); local names – **Jammu Hills** (J&K), **Dhang/Dundwa** (Uttarakhand), **Churia Ghat** (Nepal).
b: **Youngest mountain chain** among Vindhyas, Aravalli, **Shiwaliks**, Annamalai = Shiwaliks; **oldest Himalayan range** = **Greater Himalaya**.
b: **In Arunachal Pradesh** the Lesser/outer Himalayan hills are the **Dafla, Miri, Abor and Mishmi hills** (the Shiwalik is almost absent there).
h: The Trans-Himalaya (Tibetan Himalaya)
b: Lies **north of the Himadri**; mostly in Tibet, hence the **Tibet Himalaya**; **formed before** the Himalayas.
b: Ranges: **Karakoram** (K2), **Ladakh** (continues into Tibet as the **Kailash Range**, not the **Zaskar**), Zaskar (Kashmir Valley separates the Pir Panjal from it), **Kailash** (Mt Kailash 6,714 m; **Lake Manasarovar** lies near it in Tibet).
b: **Ladakh Plateau:** north-east of the Karakoram; **highest plateau in India**; includes the **Aksai Chin**, Soda Plains, Lingzi Tang, Depsang Plains (Chinese-occupied Aksai Chin is a cold desert).
b: **Kailash Mansarovar** is in **Chinese Tibet** – Indian pilgrims need a visa.
