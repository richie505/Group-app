## page c8e1b3d004e2
topic: Biological Characteristics of Oceans – Marine Ecosystems, Coral Reefs, Bleaching, Upwelling
page title: Coral Reefs – Nature, Conditions and Types
h: Nature
b: Built from the **calcium carbonate** skeletons of tiny colonial **coral polyps** (phylum **Cnidaria**); the CaCO3 is **aragonite**. **Oyster shells** too are **calcium carbonate**.
b: **Mutualism with zooxanthellae** (single-celled dinoflagellate algae): the algae photosynthesise and give the coral **food and colour**; the coral gives **shelter and CO2/nutrients**.
b: **"Rainforests of the Sea"** – the marine counterpart of **tropical rain forests** – because of exceptional **species richness**: under **1%** of the ocean floor, over **25%** of marine species.
b: Reefs **break storm waves** and prevent coastal erosion; also nurseries, fisheries, tourism. APPSC: both statements true, but wave-breaking does **not explain** the epithet.
b: **Biorock (mineral accretion)**: a small electric current on a steel frame precipitates CaCO3 – used to **restore damaged reefs** (ZSI trial, Gulf of Kachchh).
h: Growth conditions
t: Condition | Requirement
   row: Temperature | **Not below about 20°C** (20–21°C minimum); optimum **23–25°C**; tropical seas
   row: Light | **Photic**, shallow water – under about 50 m (Leong: not over 180 ft; ideal under 100 ft)
   row: Water | **Clean, sediment-free**, normal sea salinity (about 3.5%, not 6%); freshwater and very saline water harm growth
   row: Avoid | Muddy river mouths, cold currents
b: Astt. Comm. 2017: photic + clean water + 20–21°C = **I, II and IV**; "salinity of 6%" is wrong.
h: Reef types
t: Type | Feature | Example
   row: **Fringing** | Close to shore; shallow lagoon or none; **commonest in India** | Andamans, Gulf of Mannar
   row: **Barrier** | Parallel to the coast, separated by a **wide, deep lagoon** | **Great Barrier Reef**
   row: **Atoll** | **Ring / horseshoe** reef enclosing a lagoon, no central land | **Lakshadweep**
n: G.C. Leong ch.11; Shankar Environment ch.4
b: **Subsidence theory** of reef formation: **Charles Darwin (1842)** – fringing reef → barrier reef → atoll as the island slowly sinks (Daly: glacial-control theory).

## page af6e3ba86dbc
topic: Biological Characteristics of Oceans – Marine Ecosystems, Coral Reefs, Bleaching, Upwelling
page title: Coral Bleaching and Ocean Acidification
h: Coral bleaching
b: **Meaning:** stressed polyps **expel (eject) their zooxanthellae** and lose pigment, turning **white**; a bleached coral is **stressed, not necessarily dead** (it can recover if conditions improve).
b: **Most significant cause:** **global warming** – rising sea-surface temperature, **marine heatwaves** (1°C above normal for weeks can trigger it); also UV, turbidity, pollution, salinity change, **extreme low tides**, **ocean acidification**, El Nino – **not** more carbonate, not mining or disease as the main cause.
t: Global bleaching event | Period | Note
   row: 1st | **1998** | Strong El Nino
   row: 2nd | **2010** | –
   row: 3rd | **2014–2017** | **68.2%** of reef areas
   row: 4th | **2023 – about mid-2025** | About **84%** of reefs, **83 countries** (NOAA–ICRI)
b: Every strong El Nino since 1998 came with a global bleaching event; recovery takes **more than 15 years**. GCRMN 2026: major disturbances now every **4–6 years**; **87%** of reefs faced heat stress in 2024. **Andaman** mass bleaching linked to about a **2°C** rise in Indian Ocean temperature.
h: Ocean acidification
b: CO2 + water → **carbonic acid** → bicarbonate + hydrogen (hydronium) ions → **pH falls** (sea water about **8.1**, alkaline) → **carbonate ions fall** → corals, calcareous plankton and shellfish **form shells less easily** (it does not strengthen reefs).
b: Oceans absorb about **30–40%** of human CO2; worst in **cold seas** (higher CO2 solubility). Nicknames: **"evil twin of global warming"**, "the other CO2 problem".

b: **Ramsar Convention (Convention on Wetlands):** adopted **2 Feb 1971** at **Ramsar, Iran**; in force 1975; **India joined 1 Feb 1982**; Secretariat **Gland, Switzerland**. Aim: **conservation and wise use of wetlands** – not oceans, deserts, arid lands, hills or ozone.
b: **World Wetlands Day: 2 February**.
b: Other days (distractors): **22 March World Water Day**; **22 April Earth Day**; 5 June World Environment Day.
b: **Wetland definition:** marsh, fen, peatland or water; **natural or artificial**; fresh, brackish or salt; includes **marine water up to 6 m deep at low tide**.
b: **Montreux Record:** register of Ramsar sites where **changes in ecological character have occurred, are occurring or are likely to occur** because of technology, pollution or other human interference – not a WHS status, not a 5-km ban.
b: **Supreme Court:** no mining within **10 km** of a Ramsar site without approval of **NBWL** and **MoEFCC**.
b: **National Wetland Conservation Programme (NWCP, 1985-86):** **115 wetlands** identified for urgent conservation; merged with NLCP into **NPCA** (2013).

b: **Footloose**: not tied to raw material or a fixed place; light, high-value, low transport cost, low pollution – **diamond cutting**, electronics, software, watch-making.
b: **Sunrise industries**: new, fast-growing sectors – **information technology**, electronics, **hospitality**/tourism; iron and steel and textiles are old ("sunset") industries.

h: Key terms
t: Term | Meaning

b: **DRR works best when it addresses the underlying causes of vulnerability**; objective of DM = **reduce loss of life and property**.

b: Rising vulnerability comes from population growth, climate change, pollution – **not "stagnation in S&T"**. Development and disaster deaths are **inversely** related.
b: Most vulnerable: people in an **old city with narrow roads**; women and children.

t: Class | Examples

b: Tsunami causes: undersea earthquake, underwater landslide, underwater volcano (and big explosions) – **underwater mining explosions** are the "not a cause" option.
