page title: Rowlatt Act, 1919
t: Particular | Fact
   row: Official name | **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919**
   row: Popular names | **Rowlatt Act**; **"Black Act"** (Gandhi); "**No Vakil, No Dalil, No Appeal**"
   row: Committee behind it | **Sedition Committee** (Rowlatt Committee), appointed **1917**, headed by **Justice Sidney Rowlatt**; reported 1918
   row: Bills | Two bills in the Imperial Legislative Council, **6 February 1919** – one dropped, one passed
   row: Passed | **March 1919** (10 March)
   row: Basis | Permanent replacement / extension of the **Defence of India Act, 1915**
   row: Repealed | Under Viceroy **Lord Reading** (1921-26), along with the **Press Act, 1910**
b: **Aim:** to crush **revolutionary nationalists** and curb national / revolutionary activities – a perceived threat from revolutionaries, not press control, the Congress or constitutional reform.
b: **Provisions:** **arrest without warrant**; **detention / imprisonment without trial for up to two years**; in-camera trial by a special court of **three High Court judges**, **no appeal**; evidence not admissible under the Evidence Act; **habeas corpus** suspended.
b: Why it caused popular indignation: it **authorised imprisonment without trial** and **limited individual liberty** – it had nothing to do with religion, education, trade unions or the press as such.
b: Passed despite opposition of **every Indian member** of the Imperial Legislative Council; **Jinnah, Madan Mohan Malaviya, Mazhar-ul-Haq** resigned in protest.
b: **Mohammad Ali and Abul Kalam Azad did not resign from the Council in 1916** – they were interned during the war.
b: **1919 = Gandhi's entry into active all-India politics** (returned from South Africa 1915) – he led the protest against this Act.
