h: Value Added Tax
b: **VAT** = tax on the **value added at each stage** between production and final sale – a **multi-point**, **destination-based**, **non-cascading** tax on final consumption, borne by the consumer; invoice-credit trail makes it **self-enforcing**; a **harmonised** commodity tax that **improves revenue buoyancy** ("will diminish revenue buoyancy" is **not** VAT's idea).
b: State VAT was a **State subject** (State List Entry 54) – not "basically a subject of the Central Government".
b: VAT applies to **commodity taxes – excise duties** (MODVAT/CENVAT) and sales tax; not to income tax, estate duty or agricultural income tax.
b: Concept first proposed by **Wilhelm von Siemens** (Germany); first implemented by **France (1954)**.
b: **L.K. Jha Indirect Taxation Enquiry Committee (1976)** – suggested VAT at the manufacturing stage.
b: **VAT rate for medicines/drugs**: the **4%** slab (pre-GST State VAT).
b: Some goods (petroleum, alcohol) are still outside GST – **State VAT** continues on them.
h: Chronology
t: Event | Date
   row: **MODVAT** – on **Union excise** duties, to end cascading (first indirect tax reform) | **1 March 1986** (Budget 1986-87)
   row: **Service tax** (Chelliah Committee advice; 3 services) | **1994-95**
   row: **Uniform floor rates of sales tax** (Standing Committee of State Finance Ministers) | **1 January 2000**
   row: **CENVAT** – replaced MODVAT; **Union excise** (an **indirect** tax) | **1 April 2000**
   row: **First State VAT – Haryana** | **1 April 2003**
   row: State VAT nationwide (20 States/UTs) | **1 April 2005**
   row: Last States to join (TN 2007, UP 2008) | by 2008-09
   row: GST | 1 July 2017
b: **Uniform sales tax**: in **January 2000** the Standing Committee of State Finance Ministers recommended **uniform floor rates of sales tax**, implemented from **1 January 2000**.
b: About **7 States** had not implemented VAT by December 2005 (Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand – 2006; TN 2007; UP 2008).
b: **Karnataka** – early e-payment of VAT/commercial taxes; **Maharashtra** – the State the **IMF** had advised earlier not to adopt VAT (no official source found).
b: **Sales tax** was **not levied in Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep** (question period); GST now covers all India.
b: **Central Sales Tax (CST)** – levied on **inter-State trade** and in **Union Territories**; **SEZs exempt**; subsumed in GST.
h: Service tax
b: **Indirect**, **Central** tax; introduced **1994-95** (Manmohan Singh as FM) on **Raja Chelliah Committee** advice.
b: Rates: **10% → 12% (Budget 2006-07)**; **12% → 14% (Budget 2015-16)**; from 2012-13, 12% on all services except the **negative list**.
b: **T.R. Rastogi (Rustagi) Panel** (2006) – Finance Ministry panel to review **service tax** issues and circulars (report March 2007: withdraw 57, modify 108, retain 136 CBEC circulars; not a CBDT panel).
b: **Swachh Bharat Cess** – **0.5%** on **all taxable services** (from 15 Nov 2015).
b: **Multiple tax rates** – chief drawback: **misclassification** of goods and services (disputes, evasion).

## page 89d2363e09ba
topic: Indian Tax System – Direct and Indirect Taxes, Tax Buoyancy, Tax-gdp Ratio, Progressivity
page title: Progressive, Regressive and Degressive Taxes; Laffer Effect
t: Type | Feature | Example
   row: **Progressive** | **Tax rate rises as income rises** – average burden rises with income ("ability to pay") | **Income tax**
   row: **Regressive** | Takes a **higher proportion of income from low incomes** than from high | Indirect taxes, GST on essentials
   row: **Proportional** | **One rate for all** incomes | Flat-rate tax
   row: **Degressive** | Rate rises at first, then becomes flat | Progressive slabs with a top flat rate
b: **Indirect taxes are regressive** because they are charged at the **same rate for all income groups**.
b: **Income tax in India**: **progressive and direct** – not collected by States, not proportional; **GST, sales tax, motor vehicles tax** are not progressive.
b: **India's tax system**: **progressive in nature but overall degressive** (progressive slabs up to a flat top rate; regressive indirect taxes).
b: **Redistribution** of income is best achieved by **progressive taxation combined with progressive expenditure**.
b: **Laffer effect**: **reduction in tax rates leads to a more than proportionate increase in tax yield**; Laffer curve – **inverted U**, zero revenue at 0% and 100%; **Arthur Laffer** (1974); term coined by **Jude Wanniski**.
b: Beyond a point, **higher tax rates may reduce revenue**.
b: **Widening the tax base** (lower exemption threshold, fewer exemptions) raises revenue without changing rates – the **Chelliah approach: lower rates, broader base**.
n: Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. Part 5 p.204

h: Tax-GDP ratio
b: **Tax-GDP ratio = total tax revenue / nominal GDP** – yardstick of fiscal capacity.
b: **Low ratio** → large **informal sector** or high **evasion**; **falling ratio** → revenue growing **slower than GDP** (buoyancy < 1), less fiscal space, more borrowing – it does **not** by itself show less equitable distribution.
t: Measure | Value
   row: Centre gross tax revenue | **9.9% of GDP in 2019-20** (ES 2021-22); ~**11.5%** post-pandemic (FY22-FY25, ES 2025-26)
   row: Combined Centre + States | ~**19.6%** (States' own tax ≈ 7% of GSDP)
   row: **2008-09** (general government) | **16.39%**
   row: India average 2001-22 | **16.36%** – lowest among emerging economies; below OECD
   row: World Bank tipping point | **15%**
h: Buoyancy and elasticity
t: Term | Meaning
   row: **Buoyancy** | % change in revenue / % change in GDP, **including discretionary changes** (rates, base); above 1 = revenue grows faster than GDP
   row: **Elasticity** | Same response **excluding** discretionary changes
b: **2026-27 BE**: gross buoyancy **0.8**; **direct 1.1**, **indirect 0.3**.

## page 142aca930b09
topic: Goods and Services Tax – Structure, Slabs, Gst Council, Igst Settlement, Compensation Cess
page title: GST – Nature and Objectives
b: **GST (Goods and Services Tax)**: an **indirect tax**; **single, comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based** tax levied on **every value addition** – a **value-added consumption tax**, **not a gross value tax**.
b: **Taxable event: supply** of goods and services (not manufacture, not sale).
b: **Destination principle**: tax accrues to the State where goods/services are **consumed**, **not where produced**.
b: Constitutional definition: **Article 366(12A)** – a tax on supply of goods or services except **alcoholic liquor for human consumption**.
b: In force **1 July 2017**; **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016**; biggest indirect-tax reform since Independence – a unified framework for many indirect taxes.
b: Objectives: **"one nation, one tax"**; **end cascading ("tax on tax")** through input tax credit; **one national market** (replaces multiple taxes of multiple authorities); transparency in **indirect** taxes; designed to be **non-inflationary** (A-R: avoids tax-on-tax → correct explanation).
b: Not an advantage: GST does not "drastically cut the current account deficit" or make India "overtake China".
b: GST involves **both Centre and States** (dual GST) – "only at the central level" is false.
n: Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. Part 5 p.204-206

page title: Effects of Devaluation
b: Devaluation makes **exports cheaper abroad** (their foreign-currency price falls) and **imports dearer at home** → it **promotes exports**; it is an **expenditure-switching** policy (spending switches to home-produced goods) used to **correct the balance of trade / BoP**.

page title: Food Security – Concept, Dimensions and Entitlements
h: Definition
b: **World Food Summit 1996 / FAO:** food security exists when **all people, at all times**, have **physical and economic access** to **sufficient, safe and nutritious food** for an active and healthy life.
b: Food security does NOT mean access to "**the food of their choice**".
h: Dimensions
t: Dimension | Meaning
   row: **Availability** | Production + imports + previous years' stock in government granaries
   row: **Accessibility** | Food within reach of every person
   row: **Affordability** | Enough money to buy sufficient, safe, nutritious food
   row: Absorption / utilisation | Body's ability to use the food
   row: Stability | Access at all times
b: **NOT dimensions:** **Accountability**, price, **quantity of exports**.
h: National vs household
t: Level | Meaning
   row: National | Aggregate production + buffer stock (sufficient food stock)
   row: Household | Each household can actually access and afford food
b: "**National but not household food security**" = **sufficient food stock, but not all households can get it** – hence PDS and NFSA.
b: Food security is needed **even in surplus years**: starvation comes from **unequal distribution and lack of access** (A-R: both true, R explains A).
h: Entitlement approach
b: **Amartya Sen**, *Poverty and Famines* (**1981**): famines come from a **collapse of entitlements** (command over food through income, exchange and transfers), not merely a fall in food availability; Sen added "**access**" to the 1970s "availability" idea.
b: **Per capita norms:** the credits the **440 g/day foodgrain norm to FAO**; Indian nutrition bodies use about 400-420 g (ICMR/NIN) – treat 440 g as an exam figure only.
