 Climate Change & Conventions (30)
 CLIMATE AGREEMENTS

 • Under the Paris Agreement, no country has legally binding emission targets
    Countries set their own Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs). Submitting and updating NDCs is required, but
    the targets are not binding. The Kyoto Protocol had binding targets for developed (Annex I) countries.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-2 (B1 p.53 Q93)

 CLIMATE CHANGE

 • Loss of Arctic sea ice is a possible climate tipping point
    Melting ice lowers albedo, so the darker ocean absorbs more heat, which speeds up warming. Other tipping points
    include Greenland ice sheet loss, Amazon dieback and AMOC collapse.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-2 (B1 p.55 Q120)

 • The Gangotri glacier's retreat is mainly due to global warming
    Black carbon and dust deposits also speed up melting. Gangotri, the source of the Bhagirathi, has retreated over a
    kilometre and a half since the early 20th century.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-2 (B1 p.57 Q141)

 • Deforestation increases (not decreases) atmospheric CO2
    Cutting and burning forests releases stored carbon and removes carbon sinks. Oil spills cut oxygen in water, and
    plastic burning harms soil.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-2 (B1 p.57 Q148)

 • Methane is the major greenhouse gas from lowland (flooded) rice fields.
    Anaerobic conditions in flooded paddies allow methanogenic bacteria to produce methane. Alternate wetting and
    drying and SRI reduce emissions.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-1 (B1 p.91 Q80; answer p.99)

 • In 2012 India had the lowest per capita GHG emissions among USA, UK, India, China.
    India is the third largest total emitter but its per capita emissions are far below the world average.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.122 Q14)

 • The National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture became operational in 2014-15.
    NMSA is one of the eight missions of the National Action Plan on Climate Change (2008).
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.123 Q18)

 • The Paris Agreement (COP-21, 2015) is based on Nationally Determined Contributions.
    It aims to keep warming well below 2 C, preferably 1.5 C. India's updated NDC targets 45% lower emission intensity
    by 2030 (2005 base) and 50% non-fossil power capacity.
    Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-1 (held 15-07-2017; printed twice in book) (B1 p.146 Q140 (dup p.156))

 • The Kigali Amendment (2016) modified the Montreal Protocol to phase down HFCs.
