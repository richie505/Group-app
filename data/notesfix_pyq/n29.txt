 Making of the Constitution & Preamble (49)
 BASIC STRUCTURE

 • Parliament can amend any part except the basic structure; limited amending power is itself a basic feature
                      The doctrine is judge-made (Kesavananda 1973), not in any Act. Minerva Mills declared limited amending power as
                      part of basic structure.
                      Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.9 Q53)

 • Basic structure is what cannot be amended under Art 368
                      It includes supremacy of the Constitution, secularism, federalism, judicial review, free elections etc.
                      Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.71 Q32)

 • The feature that is not a basic feature of the Constitution: Secularism (book answer)
                      The basic structure doctrine came from Kesavananda Bharati (1973) and lists features Parliament cannot destroy by
                      amendment. The book's key marks Secularism as the one not basic among the options.
                      Trap: Distractors: federation, judicial review and judicial activism. Note: some standard sources hold secularism to be part
                      of the basic structure (S.R. Bommai, 1994).
                      Group-1 Prelims 2002 (B3 p.225 Q118)

 CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY

 • The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (Constitution Day)
                      It came fully into force on 26 January 1950. Some provisions, like those on citizenship and elections, came into force
                      on 26 November 1949.
                      Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.31 Q111)

 • Rajendra Prasad chaired the Finance and Staff Committee of the Constituent Assembly
                      Prasad also chaired the Rules of Procedure Committee and the Steering Committee. Nehru headed the Union
                      Powers, Union Constitution and States committees; Patel the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights and
                      Minorities and the Provincial Constitution Committee; Ambedkar the Drafting Committee.
                      Group-1 Prelims 2012 (B3 p.119 Q23)

 • Constituent Assembly committees: the pair not correctly matched is Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee -
   J.B. Kripalani (book answer)
                      The book points out that the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities etc. was headed by
                      Vallabhbhai Patel, and treats the Kripalani pairing as the mismatch. Rajendra Prasad chaired the Rules Committee.
                      Trap: Distractors: Rules Committee - Rajendra Prasad, Advisory Committee - Patel, and Steering Committee - Nehru. Note:
                      some standard sources give Steering Committee - Nehru as the wrong pair, since Rajendra Prasad chaired it.
                      Group-1 Prelims 2004 (Oct) (B3 p.184 Q2)

 FORMS OF GOVERNMENT

 • As per the book's answer, in a presidential system statements i (President is head of State) and iii (President
   is directly elected by the people) are correct
                      In a presidential system the President is the head of State and is chosen by the people for a fixed term, as in the
                      USA. The executive is separate from the legislature and is not accountable to it, so statement iv is wrong.
                      Trap: Options including statement iv (accountable to legislature) describe the parliamentary system. Note: some standard
                      sources also treat the President as head of government in a presidential system.
                      Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.30 Q100)

 MAKING OF THE CONSTITUTION

 • The Drafting Committee was appointed on 29 August 1947.
    It had seven members with Dr B.R. Ambedkar as chairman. The draft was published in February 1948.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.109 Q101)

 • H.V. Kamath remarked on the 'elephantine' size of the Constitution (book key).
    Critics in the Constituent Assembly called the Constitution too long; it is the world's longest written constitution.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.109 Q102)

 • Nehru chaired the Union Constitution Committee of the Constituent Assembly.
    He also chaired the Union Powers and States Committees. Patel chaired the Provincial Constitution and

 PREAMBLE

 • 'Secular' (with 'Socialist' and 'Integrity') was added to the Preamble by the 42nd Amendment (1976)
    The Preamble has been amended only once. In Kesavananda Bharati (1973) the Court held the Preamble is part of
    the Constitution.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.29 Q87)

 • The Preamble lists liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship, but not 'political liberty'
    It gives justice (social, economic, political), liberty (of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship), equality (of
    status and opportunity) and fraternity.
    Trap: 'Political' appears only with justice, not with liberty.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.29 Q92)

 • 'Freedom' is not used in the Preamble (the word is 'Liberty')
    The Preamble uses Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity.
    Group-2 Prelims 2019 (B1 p.73 Q82)

 • As per the book's answer, the Preamble of the Indian Constitution is taken to declare India a 'Quasi-federal
   State'
    The Preamble describes India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic. The book reads the
    Constitution as quasi-federal, combining federal features with strong unitary ones such as single citizenship,
    residuary powers with the Centre and emergency provisions.
    Trap: Unitary and Federal are distractors. Note: some standard sources point out that the Preamble itself does not use the
    word federal or quasi-federal, so they choose 'None of the above'.
    Group-2 Prelims 2019 (B1 p.73 Q86)

 • Kesavananda Bharati (1973) held that the Preamble is part of the Constitution and linked to the basic
   structure.
    This overruled the Berubari (1960) view. In LIC case (1995) the SC again said the Preamble is part of the Constitution.
    It was amended once, by the 42nd Amendment.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.111 Q140)

 • The people are the ultimate source of political power in India.
    The Preamble begins 'We, the People of India', showing popular sovereignty.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.111 Q141)

 • The Preamble declares India a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic.
    'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976), along with 'integrity'.
    Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-1 (held 15-07-2017; printed twice in book) (B1 p.139 Q28 (dup p.149))

 • The Preamble promises Social, Economic and Political justice.
    These ideas were inspired by the Russian Revolution.
    Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-2 (held 15-07-2017) (B1 p.171 Q84)

 • Republic means elected head of state (President); liberty in Preamble is qualified
    'Democratic' in the Preamble covers political, social and economic democracy. Indian socialism is called democratic
    socialism (Marxism and Gandhism mix). Fraternity originally assured dignity of individual and unity of the nation.
    Trap: All statements invalid.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.10 Q60)

 • Preamble was adopted last but is part of the Constitution; it is non-justiciable
    The Objectives Resolution (Nehru, 1946) formed its basis. Berubari (1960) said it is not a part; Kesavananda (1973)
    said it is.
    Trap: Incorrect: I and III.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.44 Q45)

 • Preamble may be invoked to interpret both Fundamental Rights and DPSPs
    It is a key to the minds of the framers and aids interpretation when language is ambiguous.
    Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.72 Q42)

 • Preamble secures liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship
    Equality of status and opportunity and justice (social, economic, political) are separate objectives.
    Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.73 Q52)

 • The chief source of political power in India is the Parliament and state legislatures (book answer)
    In India's federal parliamentary system, power is exercised through elected representatives in Parliament and the
    State Legislatures. The book's key marks this option, treating the legislatures as the channel through which authority
    flows.
    Trap: Distractors: the people, the Constitution and Parliament alone. 'The people' (popular sovereignty in the Preamble) is
    the usual confusion.
    Group-1 Prelims 2002 (B3 p.223 Q76)

 SCHEDULES

 SCHEDULES OF CONSTITUTION

 • Ninth Schedule shields Central and State laws from FR-based review and is not limited to land reforms
    Added by the 1st Amendment (1951) with Art 31B. It now includes reservation and other laws. I.R. Coelho (2007):
    laws added after 24 April 1973 are open to basic structure review, but Art 31B continues. Additions are made only
    by Parliament through amendments.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.7 Q46)

 • Entry 64 of Union List covers centrally funded scientific/technical institutions of national importance
    Entry 63 covers BHU, AMU, Delhi University etc.; Entry 65 covers union agencies for professional training; Entry 66
    covers standards in higher education.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.43 Q42)

 SOURCES OF THE CONSTITUTION

 • Borrowed features: UK-Rule of law, Ireland-DPSP, USA-judicial review, Canada-residuary powers with the
   Centre
    From the UK come parliamentary government and the rule of law. Ireland gave DPSPs and nomination to the Rajya
    Sabha. The USA gave Fundamental Rights and judicial review. Canada gave a federation with a strong Centre and
    residuary powers.
    Trap: Code a-ii, b-iii, c-iv, d-i.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.28 Q78)

 • Emergency provisions-GoI Act 1935, suspension of FRs in emergency-Weimar Germany, Concurrent
   List-Australia, single citizenship-UK
    The 1935 Act gave the federal scheme, the Governor's office and emergency provisions. The Weimar Constitution
    gave suspension of FRs during emergency. Australia gave the Concurrent List and joint sittings.
    Trap: Code a-iii, b-iv, c-ii, d-i.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.28 Q82)

 • Judicial review was borrowed from the USA.
    Other US features: Fundamental Rights, independent judiciary, impeachment of President, removal of judges, Vice
    President's post.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.109 Q113)

 • The Concurrent List idea came from the Australian Constitution.
    Australia also gave the joint sitting of Parliament and freedom of trade and commerce provisions.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.110 Q119)

 • The federal scheme was largely adopted from the Government of India Act, 1935.
    The 1935 Act also provided the Governor's office, judiciary, PSCs, emergency provisions and administrative details.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.110 Q121)

 • The feature borrowed from the US constitutional tradition: The Rule of Law (book answer)
    The book treats the Rule of Law as the answer, noting that the idea, though British in origin, is embedded in
    American constitutionalism too. It means that law is supreme and everyone, including government, is subject to it.
    Trap: Distractors include Fundamental Rights and other borrowed features. Note: some standard sources give
    Fundamental Rights as the US borrowing and treat the Rule of Law as British.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Oct) (B3 p.140 Q107)

 • The British Constitution exercised the most profound influence on the Constitution of India (book answer)
    The British model gave India its parliamentary form of government, cabinet system, rule of law, single citizenship
    and bicameral legislature. The book's key marks the British Constitution.
    Trap: Distractors: Government of India Act 1935, US Constitution and UN Charter. The GoI Act 1935, which supplied much
    of the administrative text, is the usual confusion.
    Group-1 Prelims 2002 (B3 p.223 Q77)

 STATES & UNION TERRITORIES

 • Sikkim became an associate state in 1974 and a full state in 1975
    The 35th Amendment (1974) gave Sikkim associate-state status (Tenth Schedule, now repealed). The 36th
    Amendment (1975), after a referendum abolishing the Chogyal monarchy, made it the 22nd state.

 • Order of formation: Maharashtra (1960), Haryana (1966), Himachal Pradesh (1971), Mizoram (1987)
    Bombay State split into Maharashtra and Gujarat in 1960; Haryana was carved from Punjab in 1966; HP became a
    state in 1971; Mizoram, Arunachal and Goa became states in 1987.
    Group-1 Prelims 2008 (B3 p.172 Q85)

 • State capitals: AP - Hyderabad (then); Meghalaya - Shillong, Mizoram - Aizawl, Tripura - Agartala
    Kohima is Nagaland's and Imphal Manipur's capital. After 2014 Hyderabad was joint capital for 10 years; AP's capital

 TELANGANA & SEPARATE STATE MOVEMENTS

 • The Six-Point Formula was given constitutional backing by the 32nd Amendment (1973)
    The 32nd Constitutional Amendment inserted Article 371D (regional equality in education and public employment)
    and Article 371E (central university at Hyderabad). It came into force on 1 July 1974, giving legal cover to the
    Six-Point Formula and creating the AP Administrative Tribunal.

 UNION & ITS TERRITORY

 • 'Territory of India' is wider than 'Union of India' as it includes UTs and territories acquired in future

    expressions appear in Art 1.
