EARLY ANDHRA / SOURCES

• The Bhimasena Jataka refers to 'Andhra Patha' (book key). Buddhist Jatakas are early literary sources for the name Andhra. The Serivanija Jataka mentions Andhapura (Andhakapura) on the Telavaha river. Trap: Serivanija Jataka is the trap - it mentions Andhapura, not Andhra Patha. Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.103 Q2)

• Buddhist literature refers to Andhras as 'Andhakarattha'. The Aitareya Brahmana is the earliest literary mention of Andhras, calling them a people outside the Aryan fold. Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-2 (held 15-07-2017) (B1 p.168 Q5)

PRE-SATAVAHANA ANDHRA

• Bhattiprolu inscriptions name King Kubiraka (Kuberaka) as ruler in the 3rd century BC. The Bhattiprolu stupa relic casket inscriptions (Guntur district) are in an early Brahmi variant and are among the oldest records of Andhra; the script is considered an ancestor of Telugu-Kannada scripts. Trap: Asoka ruled the empire, but the local ruler named is Kubiraka. Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.103 Q3)

PREHISTORIC & MEGALITHIC ANDHRA

• The incorrect statement: 'Sarcophagus burials are more in number in Chittoor and Anantapuramu districts' Megalithic burial types vary by region in Andhra: cist burials cluster in Visakhapatnam, dolmens in Krishna and Chittoor, and Tirupati has the largest collection of anthropomorphic burials. Sarcophagus (terracotta coffin) burials belong mainly to the Nagarjunakonda/Krishna valley region, not Chittoor or Anantapuramu. Trap: All four options name real megalithic burial types, so the trap is in the regional pairing; only the sarcophagus-Chittoor/Anantapuramu link is wrong. Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.23 Q14)

• Billa Surgam caves are in Kurnool district (not Anantapur) Billa Surgam yielded Upper Palaeolithic bone tools and animal fossils. Belum Caves (Nandyal/Kurnool) are the second-longest natural caves in the Indian subcontinent. Muchchatla Chintamani Gavi also yielded bone tools. Trap: Only statement a is incorrect. Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.25 Q37)

PREHISTORIC ANDHRA

• Giddaluru (Prakasam district) is recognised as a microlithic implement centre. Microliths are tiny stone tools of the Mesolithic age used as arrow tips and blades in composite tools. Giddaluru yielded large numbers of such tools; other prehistoric sites in AP include Nagarjunakonda, Kurnool caves (Betamcherla) and Rallakalava valley. Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.103 Q1)

• Billa Surgam caves (Kurnool) are an Old Stone Age site. Robert Bruce Foote explored them in 1884; they yielded Palaeolithic fauna and tools. Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-2 (held 15-07-2017) (B1 p.168 Q3)

• Patapadu (Kurnool) is cited as evidence of the Chalcolithic age in Andhra (book key). Chalcolithic (copper-stone) sites in the Deccan show early farming communities; Neolithic sites in AP include Utnoor and Sanganakallu-region sites. Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-2 (held 15-07-2017) (B1 p.168 Q4)

• Bhimasena Jataka mentions Andhapatha (Andhra route) Buddhist Jatakas carry early references to Andhra: the Bhimasena Jataka refers to Andhapatha, and the Serivanija Jataka to Andhapura on the Telavaha river. The Aitareya Brahmana is the earliest mention of the Andhras. Trap: Suttanipata mentions Assaka (Asmaka) and Mulaka rather than Andhapatha. Group-2 (paper not stated) (B2 p.9 Q11)

SATAVAHANA LITERATURE

• Gatha Saptasati of Hala contains verses by women poets. It is a Prakrit anthology of 700 verses on love and rural life compiled by King Hala. Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-2 (held 15-07-2017) (B1 p.168 Q14)

• The earliest Satavahana inscriptions were found in Maharashtra The Naneghat inscription of Queen Naganika and the Nashik cave records lie in western Deccan. The dynasty's early base was Pratishthana (Paithan); later rulers moved east to Dhanyakataka (Amaravati) in Andhra. Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.22 Q3)

• Gatha Saptashati is a Prakrit anthology of love poems; Brihatkatha was written in Paishachi, not Sanskrit Gatha Saptashati is attributed to King Hala. Gunadhya's Brihatkatha is lost but survives in Sanskrit versions like Kathasaritsagara and Brihatkathamanjari. Trap: A true, B false. Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.23 Q24)

• A is false, R is true: Indo-Roman trade peaked in the Satavahana era and declined afterwards; ship-motif coins were not issued after the Satavahanas The Satavahana period was the peak of maritime trade with Rome, as Roman coin hoards in Andhra and sites like Kondapur show. Yajna Sri Satakarni's ship-type coins reflect this sea trade, and later dynasties did not continue the ship motif. Trap: Students often assume the trade carried on after the Satavahanas; it declined. Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.24 Q32)

• Satavahanas had no marriage ties with the Kakatiyas (who came a thousand years later) The Puranas call them Andhra-bhrityas. They issued bilingual Prakrit-Tamil coins, and patronised both Brahmanical religion and Buddhism. Their marriage ties were with the Maharathis and Western Kshatrapas. Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.27 Q75)

• Hala's Gathasaptasati is the key literary source for Satavahana social and cultural life This Prakrit anthology of 700 verses shows village life, love, festivals and occupations in the Deccan. Group-2 Prelims 2019 (B1 p.71 Q51); Group-2 Prelims 2019 (B2 p.1 Q1)

• Queen Naganika (Devi Naganika) ruled as regent for her minor son. Wife of Satakarni I, her Naneghat inscription records Vedic sacrifices performed by the family. Gautami Balasri, mother of Gautamiputra Satakarni, is known from the Nasik prasasti. Trap: Gautami Balasri is a common distractor. Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.103 Q4)

• Guild deposits in the Satavahana age generally earned about 12% interest. Nasik inscriptions of Ushavadata record permanent deposits with weavers' guilds at 1% per month (12% a year) and 3/4% per month. Guilds (srenis) thus worked like banks. Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.103 Q5)
