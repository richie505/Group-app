 Space Technology & Astronomy (51)
 ASTRONOMY

 • The longest total solar eclipse of the 21st century occurred on 22 July 2009
                        Totality lasted up to about 6 min 39 s and was visible across India, Nepal, Bangladesh, Bhutan and China. A solar
                        eclipse occurs on a new-moon day when the Moon comes between the Sun and the Earth.
                        Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.149 Q1)

 • Nicolaus Copernicus was a 16th-century mathematician (book answer)
                        Copernicus worked as a mathematician as well as an astronomer, and his heliocentric model in 'De revolutionibus'
                        (1543) rested on mathematical reasoning. The book's key marks Mathematician.
                        Trap: Distractors: astronomer, navigator and philosopher; 'astronomer' is the option most often chosen instead.
                        Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.154 Q119)

 • The millennium's longest annular solar eclipse occurred on 17 January 2010 (book answer)
                        The January 2010 annular eclipse was notable for an annular phase of about 11 minutes, seen over Africa, the
                        Maldives, south India and Sri Lanka. The book's key gives 17 January 2010.
                        Trap: Distractors are the neighbouring dates 15, 16 and 18 January 2010.
                        Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.155 Q127)

 • A blue moon is the second full moon in a calendar month
                        It happens roughly once every 2-3 years since the lunar cycle (29.5 days) is shorter than most months.
                        Group-1 Prelims 2004 (Oct) (B3 p.191 Q108)

 • Pluto is called 'Yama' in Indian naming
                        Uranus is Arun/Indra and Neptune is Varun. Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet by the IAU in 2006.
                        Group-1 Prelims 2004 (Mar) (B3 p.207 Q88)

 • Venus is Earth's 'twin' in mass, size and density
                        Venus is the hottest planet due to a runaway greenhouse effect and rotates backward (retrograde). Mars is the 'Red
                        Planet'.
                        Group-1 Prelims 2004 (Mar) (B3 p.208 Q110)

 • We always see the same face of the Moon because it revolves on its axis in a direction opposite to the Earth
   (book answer)
                        The Moon shows synchronous rotation with respect to the Earth. The book's key explains the same-face effect with
                        the 'opposite direction' option.
                        Trap: Distractors: smaller than the Earth, equal time for rotation and revolution, and same speed as the Earth. Note: some
                        standard sources give equal periods of rotation and revolution (about 27.3 days).
                        Group-1 Prelims 2002 (B3 p.224 Q91)

 • Saturn takes one year to revolve once around the Sun (book answer)
                        Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun, famous for its ring system. The book's key marks one year for its revolution.
                        Trap: Distractors: 12 years (Jupiter), 29 years and 248 years (Pluto). Note: some standard sources give about 29.5 years.
                        Group-1 Prelims 2002 (B3 p.226 Q126)

 • STUDSAT, ANUSAT and Jugnu are student satellites; GSAT-1 is not.
