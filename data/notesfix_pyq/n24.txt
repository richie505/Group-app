 Universe, Earth & Its Motions (18)
 EARTH & SEASONS

 • Winter solstice 22 Dec, summer solstice 21 June, vernal equinox 21 March, autumnal equinox 23 Sept (NH)
             At solstices the sun is overhead at the tropics; at equinoxes over the equator.
             Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.76 Q91)

 EARTH'S INTERIOR

 • The outermost layer of the Earth is the crust
             Layers are crust, mantle and core (outer liquid, inner solid). Continental crust is mainly sial and oceanic crust sima;
             the Moho separates crust from mantle.
             Trap: Cirrus is a cloud type.
             Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.149 Q5)

 EARTH'S MOTIONS

 • On the equinoxes (21 March, 23 September) the Sun is overhead at the Equator
             Day and night are nearly equal everywhere. On 21 June the Sun is overhead at the Tropic of Cancer and on 22
             December at the Tropic of Capricorn.
             Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.149 Q9)

 • Uttarayana is the Sun's northward journey from about 22 December to 21 June
             It starts at the winter solstice (traditionally marked by Makar Sankranti) and ends at the summer solstice;
             Dakshinayana is the southward half.
             Group-1 Prelims 2008 (B3 p.170 Q62)

 • Perihelion is when Earth is closest to the Sun (about 3 January)
             Aphelion (farthest) is about 4 July. Seasons are caused by axial tilt, not distance.
             Group-1 Prelims 2008 (B3 p.171 Q69)

 • Srinagar (highest latitude among options) has the longest summer days
             In the northern summer, day length increases with latitude, so northern cities get longer days.
             Group-1 Prelims 2004 (Mar) (B3 p.204 Q42)

 • Among Hyderabad, Delhi, Kolkata and Mumbai, the city that never gets the vertical rays of the Sun is
   Mumbai (book answer)
             The Sun can be directly overhead only between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn. The book's key
             marks Mumbai.
             Trap: Distractors: Hyderabad, Delhi and Kolkata. Note: some standard sources give Delhi (28.6 N), the only one of these
             north of the Tropic of Cancer.
             Group-1 Prelims 2002 (B3 p.224 Q92)

 EARTHQUAKES

 • The epicentre is the point on the surface directly above the focus (not below)
             The focus (hypocentre) is where energy is released inside the earth. The epicentre is the surface point vertically
             above it and is the first to receive body waves at the surface, so damage is usually highest there.
             Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.4 Q32)

 EARTHQUAKES & VOLCANOES

 • Earthquake intensity is greatest at the epicentre
             The focus (hypocentre) is the origin underground; the epicentre is the surface point directly above. Magnitude is
             measured on the Richter scale and intensity on the Mercalli scale.
             Group-1 Prelims 2008 (B3 p.171 Q70)

 LATITUDE, LONGITUDE & TIME

 • 15 degrees of longitude = 1 hour; 60 W to 90 E is 150 degrees = 10 hours, so 10 AM at A means 8 PM at B
    Earth turns 360 degrees in 24 hours, i.e. 1 degree in 4 minutes. Places to the east are ahead in local time. With A at
    60 W and B at 90 E the difference is 150 degrees, giving 10 hours ahead.
    Trap: OCR shows 06/09 degrees, but the key answer 8 PM fits 060 W and 090 E (same question repeats in 2018 paper).
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.49 Q110)

 • 60 W to 90 E = 150 degrees = 10 hours; 10 AM at A = 8 PM at B
    Local time increases eastward by 4 minutes per degree.
    Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.77 Q111)

 LATITUDES & LONGITUDES

 • Latitudes run east-west, parallel to the Equator
    Latitudes measure angular distance north or south of the Equator (0 to 90 degrees). Longitudes (meridians) run
    north-south and meet at the poles; 15 degrees equals one hour.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.149 Q7)

 • Hipparchus first divided the circle into 360 degrees
    The Greek astronomer drew on Babylonian sexagesimal ideas and is also called the father of trigonometry.
    Eratosthenes measured Earth's circumference and Ptolemy wrote the Almagest.
    Group-1 Prelims 2008 (B3 p.167 Q16)

 • Batholiths, dykes and sills correctly described; phacoliths are wavy lens-shaped bodies in folds
    Batholiths: large granitic masses deep in the crust. Laccoliths are dome-shaped. Phacoliths lie at anticline

    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.17 Q93)

 • Mt Etna (Sicily) is a volcanic mountain
    Everest and Mont Blanc are fold mountains; Aravalli is an ancient residual fold range.
    Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.76 Q95)

 GRASSLANDS OF THE WORLD

 • As per the book's answer, the Prairies are the temperate grasslands present in the United States and
   Mexico

    system and used intensively for wheat and maize. The book takes their extent as the United States stretching south
    into Mexico. Other temperate grasslands: Steppes (Eurasia), Pampas (Argentina), Veld (South Africa), Downs

 LANDFORMS

 • Landforms and agents: delta-running water, cirque-glaciers, cliffs-waves, sand dunes-wind
    Deltas form where rivers deposit sediment at the mouth. Cirques are armchair hollows carved by glaciers. Sea cliffs
    are erosional features of waves and currents. Sand dunes are deposits of wind in deserts.
    Trap: Code a-III, b-IV, c-I, d-II.
    Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.4 Q34)

 • Stalactites and stalagmites are formed by groundwater in limestone caves
    Dripping water deposits calcium carbonate: stalactites hang down, stalagmites rise up and pillars form when they
    join. Borra caves (Araku, AP) and Belum caves (Kurnool) are examples.
    Group-1 Prelims 2008 (B3 p.168 Q24)

 MINERALS & ROCKS

 • Mohs scale measures mineral hardness
    It ranges from talc (1) to diamond (10). The Mercalli and Rossi-Forel scales measure earthquake intensity, Beaufort
    measures wind speed.
    Group-1 Prelims 2008 (B3 p.168 Q28)

 Climatology & Atmosphere (28)
 ATMOSPHERE

 • Meteors burn up in the mesosphere
    The mesosphere extends roughly 50-80 km above the earth and is the coldest layer. Friction with air burns most
    meteoroids there. The ionosphere lies within the thermosphere and reflects radio waves.
    Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.4 Q35)

 • Heating processes: advection-horizontal, convection-vertical, insolation-direct, conduction-lower layers
    Advection transfers heat horizontally by wind. Convection lifts heated air vertically. Insolation is incoming solar
    radiation. Conduction heats air layers in contact with the warm ground.
    Trap: Code a-I, b-II, c-III, d-IV.
    Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.4 Q36)

 ATMOSPHERE & HEAT BUDGET

 • Incoming solar radiation is called insolation
    Insolation is highest near the tropics and decreases poleward; the Earth's albedo is about 30%.
    Group-1 Prelims 2008 (B3 p.171 Q68)

 CLIMATIC REGIONS

 • Mediterranean climate: hot dry summers, mild wet winters
    So dry winter, no dry season and severe dry winter are not its features. Found 30-40 degrees on western coasts
    (California, central Chile, SW Australia).
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.17 Q98)

 CLIMATOLOGY

 • A tornado is a land-based storm.
    Hurricanes (Atlantic), typhoons (Pacific) and willy-willies (Australia) are tropical cyclones forming over oceans.
    Mains 2017 Paper-I (second set labelled 'held 15-07-2017' in book) (B1 p.161 Q53)

 • In a northern hemisphere cyclone, air circulates inward anticlockwise.
    This is due to the Coriolis force; in the southern hemisphere the motion is clockwise.
    Mains 2017 Paper-I (second set labelled 'held 15-07-2017' in book) (B1 p.161 Q58)

 • In north India heat waves normally occur from March to June (book key).
    IMD declares a heat wave when temperature exceeds 40 C in plains and is 4.5 C or more above normal.
    Mains 2017 Paper-I (second set labelled 'held 15-07-2017' in book) (B1 p.162 Q62)

 • Cirrus-silky/fibrous, Cirrostratus-milky, Cirrocumulus-small globular, Cumulus-cauliflower
    Cirrus are high feathery clouds; cirrostratus form thin milky sheets producing halos; cirrocumulus appear as small
    globules; cumulus are puffy cauliflower clouds of fair weather.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.17 Q95)

 • Doldrums are calm equatorial low-pressure belts; NH trade winds blow from the northeast
    Doldrums = ITCZ, light variable winds. Trade winds are NE in Northern Hemisphere and SE in Southern Hemisphere
    due to Coriolis force.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.17 Q96)

 • Extra-tropical cyclones have clear frontal systems
    They form in mid-high latitudes where warm and cold air meet, cover large areas, and form over land and sea,
    unlike tropical cyclones which lack fronts.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.17 Q97)

 • Sun halo is a 22-degree ring formed by refraction in hexagonal ice crystals of cirrus/cirrostratus
    Halos form from thin high clouds, not cumulonimbus.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-II (GS & Aptitude) (B3 p.33 Q75)

 ISLANDS OF INDIA

 • Neither statement is correct: Kavaratti is not the largest city of Lakshadweep, and Minicoy is separated
   from the Maldives by the Eight Degree Channel (not the Nine Degree Channel)
    Kavaratti is the administrative capital of Lakshadweep, but it is not the largest island/city. The Eight Degree Channel
    lies between Minicoy and the Maldives, while the Nine Degree Channel separates Minicoy from the main
    Lakshadweep group. The Ten Degree Channel separates Andaman from Nicobar.
    Trap: Options pair the capital with 'largest city' and swap the Nine and Eight Degree Channels; both statements look
    plausible but neither holds, so the answer is 'None of them'.
    Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.5 Q37)

 • Ocean relief: shelf-thick sediments, slope-canyons, deep sea plain-fine sediments, deeps-volcanoes and
   earthquakes
    The continental shelf is a shallow, gently sloping, sediment-rich extension of land. The continental slope is steep and
    cut by submarine canyons. Abyssal plains are flat and covered by fine sediments. Trenches lie at plate convergences
    with earthquakes and volcanoes.
    Trap: Code a-III, b-I, c-IV, d-II.
    Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.5 Q38)

 • Salinity order: Lake Van > Dead Sea > Great Salt Lake
    NCERT gives Lake Van (Turkey) about 330 parts per thousand, Dead Sea about 238 and Great Salt Lake (Utah) about
    220. Average ocean salinity is about 35 per thousand.
    Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.5 Q39)
