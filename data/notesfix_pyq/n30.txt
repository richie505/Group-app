 Fundamental Rights & Writs (41)
 FR VS DPSP

 • FR vs DPSP: only statement I is incorrect (book key 4).
    The 25th Amendment (1971) added Art 31C; in Kesavananda Bharati the Court struck down its second part (the bar
    on judicial review), not the first, so statement I is wrong. In Minerva Mills (1980) the Court struck down the 42nd
    Amendment's widening of 31C and stressed harmony between FRs and DPSPs. The book treats statements II, III and
    IV as correct.
    Trap: Mixing up the first and second parts of Art 31C is the main trap.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.8 Q51)

 FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS

 • Dr Ambedkar called Article 32 the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution
    Article 32 lets people go directly to the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights through writs. It is part of the
    basic structure.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.30 Q106)

 • Right to travel abroad is part of Article 21; no express FR to marry (per book)
    In Satwant Singh Sawhney (1967) and Maneka Gandhi (1978), the right to go abroad was read into personal liberty.
    In Shafin Jahan (2018) the right to marry a person of one's choice was linked to Art 21. In Supriyo (2023) the Court
    held there is no unqualified fundamental right to marry.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-1 (B1 p.31 Q109)

 • Article 25(1) guarantees freedom of conscience and the right to profess, practise and propagate religion
    It is subject to public order, morality, health and other FRs. Article 26 deals with freedom to manage religious
    affairs.

 • Article 20(2) bars double jeopardy (no prosecution and punishment twice for the same offence)
    Article 20 also bars ex post facto criminal laws (20(1)) and self-incrimination (20(3)). It cannot be suspended in an
    emergency.
    Group-2 Prelims 2019 (B1 p.74 Q89)

    It removed Articles 19(1)(f) and 31. Now no person can be deprived of property except by authority of law.
