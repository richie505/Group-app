 National Income & Growth (78)
 BASIC ECONOMICS

 • Goods whose demand rises with income are normal goods.
                  For inferior goods demand falls as income rises; Giffen goods are a special inferior good whose demand rises with
                  price.
                  Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.122 Q8)

 • Money used by entrepreneurs to buy what they need for production is financial capital.
                  Physical capital is machines, tools and buildings bought with financial capital.
                  Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.124 Q36)

 DEVELOPMENT FINANCE

 • SIDBI is an all-India development finance institution.
                  SIDBI (1990) serves MSMEs; other AIFIs include NABARD, EXIM Bank and NHB, and NaBFID (2021).
                  Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-3 (held 16-07-2017) (B1 p.178 Q14)

 DEVELOPMENT FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS

 • IFCI (1948) was India's first development financial institution; ICICI came in 1955
                  IDBI followed in 1964, NABARD in 1982, EXIM Bank in 1982 and SIDBI in 1990. NaBFID (2021) is the new
                  infrastructure DFI.
                  Group-1 Prelims 2004 (Mar) (B3 p.205 Q50)

 ECONOMIC CONCEPTS

 • Environmental Kuznets Curve: degradation first rises then falls with income
                  Original Kuznets curve concerned inequality. Phillips curve: inflation-unemployment; Laffer: tax rate-revenue;
                  Lorenz: inequality.
                  Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.74 Q63)

 • Bhagwati-Sen debate: growth first vs social investment first
                  Bhagwati favours growth-led trickle down (Gujarat model); Sen favours public investment in health and education
                  (Kerala model).
                  Group-1 Screening Test 2017 (GS & Mental Ability) (B3 p.93 Q8)

 ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT

 • Corruption is a non-economic factor in development.
                  Economic factors include capital, savings, investment and foreign exchange; non-economic factors include
                  institutions, governance and social attitudes.
                  Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.129 Q120)

 • The new view of development does not stress simple GDP growth only.
                  It stresses poverty removal, unemployment, inequality and human welfare (Dudley Seers, Amartya Sen).
                  Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-3 (held 16-07-2017) (B1 p.179 Q29)

 • India's underdevelopment is not mainly due to working efficiency of its population.
                  Main reasons given: rapid population growth, high dependency ratio, low human development and poverty.
                  Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-3 (held 16-07-2017) (B1 p.180 Q34)

 ECONOMIC INDICATORS

 • Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI) indicates health of the manufacturing (and services) sector.
                  A reading above 50 means expansion and below 50 contraction.
                  Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.126 Q76)

 GROWTH

 • Miracle economies are those with about 7% or more growth for a decade or more.
    A 7% growth rate doubles income in about 10 years; East Asian tigers and China are examples.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.123 Q23)

 • Economic Survey 2015-16 described recent growth as consumption-driven.
    Private consumption is the largest component of India's GDP (about 55-60%).
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.125 Q51)

 • India's per capita rank improves under purchasing power parity (PPP).
    PPP adjusts for differences in price levels, so India ranks much higher than in nominal terms.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.126 Q79)

 • Arvind Subramanian (2019) argued GDP growth post-2011 was overestimated as key indicators diverged.
    He estimated overstatement of about 2.5 percentage points a year for 2011-12 to 2016-17.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.129 Q121)

 • India's real GDP growth in 2014-15 was about 7.3% (2011-12 series).
    The new 2011-12 base series released in 2015 put real GDP growth for 2014-15 at about 7.3%. This made India one
    of the fastest-growing large economies that year.
    Trap: 7.8%, 7% and 7.5% are the distractors.
    Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-1 (held 15-07-2017; printed twice in book) (B1 p.146 Q129 (dup p.156))

 • Raj Krishna coined 'Hindu rate of growth' for India's slow 3.5% growth.
    It described growth from the 1950s to 1980s.
    Mains 2017 Paper-I (second set labelled 'held 15-07-2017' in book) (B1 p.164 Q109)

 • GDP growth was highest during 2004-05 to 2012-13 among the given periods.
    India grew around 8% a year in this period.
    Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-3 (held 16-07-2017) (B1 p.181 Q51)

 GROWTH & DEVELOPMENT

 • Saving is one of the determinants of growth
    In the Harrod-Domar model growth = saving rate / capital-output ratio, but technology, human capital and
    institutions also matter.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.150 Q24)

 GROWTH & NATIONAL INCOME

 • India's high growth projection rests on strong private consumption (PFCE) and better rabi prospects as El
   Nino fades
    PFCE is the largest component of India's GDP by expenditure. Public capex, not a shift away from it, has been a
    growth driver. Favourable monsoon/rabi output supports rural demand.
    Trap: FMCG volume decline is not a growth reason.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.10 Q61)

 • National product and national income measure the same activity from output and income sides
    Production uses factors of production which earn incomes; hence value of output equals factor incomes (circular
    flow). Key accepts A and R true with R explaining A.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.14 Q81)

    As economies grow, workforce and output shift to industry and services.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.45 Q61)

 • J.R. Hicks said Net Social Product and Social Income are the same
    Hicks's 'Social Framework' explained the identity of output and income in national accounts.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.46 Q62)

 • Per capita income can fall even as national income rises if population grows faster
    Per capita income = National income / population.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.46 Q63)

 • Trade-services; water supply-industry; fisheries-primary; GDP estimates-CSO (now NSO)
    Electricity, gas and water supply are counted in the industrial sector in Indian national accounts.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.46 Q69)

 • Tax revenue (and interest on public debt) is not part of national income
    These are transfers. Imputed rent of owner-occupied houses and profits of public enterprises are included.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.46 Q70)

 • Water supply belongs to the secondary sector
    Electricity, gas and water supply are classed with industry.
    Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.74 Q69)

 • GNP = GDP + Net factor income from abroad
    NNP = GNP - depreciation; NNP at factor cost is national income.
    Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.75 Q71)

 HUMAN DEVELOPMENT

 • As per the book, India's HDI value increased by nearly 40 percent between 1990 and 2017
    UNDP's Human Development update of 2018 showed India's HDI rising sharply from 1990 to 2017, and ranked India
    130th. HDI combines life expectancy, education and per capita income.
    Trap: 20%, 30% and 50% are the distractors; tie the answer to the book's 40%.
    Group-2 Prelims 2019 (B1 p.75 Q107)

 • GDI and GEM measure gender inequalities.
    Introduced in the 1995 HDR; GEM was replaced by the Gender Inequality Index (GII) in 2010.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.127 Q84)

 • Survival (under-5 mortality), expected quality-adjusted schooling and health are components of the Human
   Capital Index.
    The World Bank launched the HCI in 2018.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.128 Q101)

 • Ownership of basic assets is not an HDI component.
    HDI uses life expectancy, education (mean and expected years of schooling) and GNI per capita (PPP).
    Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-1 (held 15-07-2017; printed twice in book) (B1 p.147 Q145 (dup p.157))

 • Loss due to inequality is not part of HDI (it is in the IHDI).
    HDI covers health, knowledge and standard of living; IHDI (2010) adjusts HDI for inequality.
    Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-3 (held 16-07-2017) (B1 p.180 Q30)

 • PHDI adjusts HDI for planetary pressures (intergenerational); HDR 2021-22 spoke of a new uncertainty
   complex
    IHDI equals HDI when there is no inequality. GDI below 1 means female development lags male. HDR 2021/22
    'Uncertain Times, Unsettled Lives' identified planetary change, social transformation and polarisation.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.15 Q85)

 • India ranked 119 of 169 in HDR 2010
    HDR 2010 introduced IHDI, GII and MPI.
    Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.74 Q64)

 • The Human Development Index is published by UNDP
    Created by Mahbub ul Haq and Amartya Sen in 1990, it combines life expectancy, education and per capita income
    (GNI PPP).
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Oct) (B3 p.140 Q120)

 NATIONAL INCOME

 • Transfer payments are excluded from national income because they are not paid for factor services
    Pensions, scholarships, unemployment allowance and gifts are transfers. Including them would inflate national
    income without any matching output.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-2 (B1 p.46 Q12)

 • National income is measured by product (value added), income and expenditure methods
    'Cost and accounting method' is not one of them. India's NSO mainly uses the production approach for GDP.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-2 (B1 p.48 Q37)

 • GDP at market prices minus net indirect taxes (indirect taxes minus subsidies) = GDP at factor cost
    India now reports GVA at basic prices, which accounts for production taxes and subsidies.
    Group-2 Mains 2025 Paper-2 (B1 p.51 Q65)

 • The difference between GDP and GNP is net factor income from abroad
    GNP = GDP + NFIA. Depreciation separates gross and net measures (GNP vs NNP).
    Group-2 Prelims 2019 (B1 p.75 Q105)

 • India's per capita income at current prices rose by about 8.6% from 2016-17 to 2017-18
    The figures were from the CSO's provisional estimates.
    Group-2 Prelims 2019 (B1 p.75 Q106)

 • National income is the market value of all final goods and services produced in a year
    Only final goods are counted, to avoid double counting. Intermediate goods are left out.
    Group-2 Prelims 2019 (B1 p.75 Q109)

 • GDP at factor cost = GDP at market price - indirect taxes + subsidies.
    Net indirect taxes (indirect taxes minus subsidies) are the gap between market price and factor cost. Since 2015,
    India reports GVA at basic prices instead.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.122 Q7)

 • Transfer payments like pensions and scholarships are excluded from GNP.
    They are not payments for current production, so they are not part of national income.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.124 Q48)

 • Share of mining and quarrying in GVA (2011-12 base) in 2016-17 was about 3.0%.
    National accounts data on the 2011-12 base show mining and quarrying at around 3% of Gross Value Added in
    2016-17. It is a small sector by output share but important as a supplier of raw materials.
    Trap: Nearby percentages are the distractors; remember about 3%.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.124 Q49)

 • GDP (expenditure method) = consumption + investment + government purchases + net exports.
    This is the C + I + G + (X - M) identity.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.125 Q53)

 • Real GDP values output at the prices of a single base year.
    It removes price changes; nominal GDP uses current prices. Current base year is 2011-12 (revision to 2022-23
    underway).
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.125 Q54)

 • Income method: GDP is measured as domestic factor incomes minus transfer payments (book key C).
    The income method adds the incomes earned by factors of production within the country: wages, rent, interest,
    profit and mixed income. Transfer payments such as pensions or subsidies are kept out because they are not earned
    through production.
    Trap: Adding net factor income from abroad gives GNP, not GDP; 'All of these' is the other distractor.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.125 Q55)

 • Average income of a country is per capita income.
    It is national income divided by population.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.125 Q56)

 • Goa had the highest per capita income among states (book key).
    Goa and Sikkim lead among states; Delhi and Chandigarh are high among UTs. Recent data often place Sikkim first.
    Trap: Answer is time-sensitive.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.125 Q57)

 • Nominal GNP / real GNP x 100 is the GNP deflator.
    The GDP deflator is the broadest price index as it covers all goods and services produced.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-3 (B1 p.129 Q128)

 • National income refers to NNP at factor cost.
    NNP at factor cost = GNP at market prices - depreciation - net indirect taxes.
    Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-3 (held 16-07-2017) (B1 p.179 Q23)

 • The CSO changed the GDP base year from 2004-05 to 2011-12 (in 2015).
    The revision also moved headline growth to GVA at basic prices and used the MCA-21 database of company
    accounts. It gave a fuller picture of the corporate sector.
    Trap: 2012-13, 2013-14 and 2014-15 are the distractor years.
    Group-2 Mains 2017 Paper-3 (held 16-07-2017) (B1 p.179 Q24)

 • Dadabhai Naoroji gave the first estimate of India's national income (1867-68)
    In 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India' he estimated per capita income at Rs 20 and set out the Drain Theory.
    V.K.R.V. Rao made the first scientific estimate (1931-32); the National Income Committee (1949) was chaired by P.C.
    Mahalanobis.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Oct) (B3 p.140 Q118)

 • GDP grew about 4% in 2002-03 and 8.2% in 2003-04
    The 2002 drought cut farm output, causing the dip; a good monsoon in 2003 drove the rebound.
    Group-1 Prelims 2004 (Oct) (B3 p.184 Q12)

 • National income in India is estimated by the Indian Standard Institution (book answer)
    National income measures the total value of goods and services produced in a year. The book's key marks the Indian
    Standard Institution for this question.
    Trap: Distractors include the Central Statistical Organisation and other agencies. Note: some standard sources give the
    CSO (now the National Statistical Office); the Indian Standards Institution is the former name of BIS.
    Group-1 Prelims 2002 (B3 p.223 Q75)
