 Indus Valley Civilisation (8)
 HARAPPAN CIVILISATION

 • Late Harappan cultures: statements I, II and III are correct (chalcolithic, no complex metal casting,
   village-based); the Bhagwanpura 'no burnt bricks' statement is wrong.
                       After the urban decline around 1900 BCE, the late Harappan phase had smaller rural settlements using stone and
                       copper tools. The advanced casting skills of the mature phase faded. At Bhagwanpura (Haryana) burnt bricks
                       continued in use, so statement IV is wrong.
                       Trap: Statement IV on Bhagwanpura is the trap; it looks plausible because of reduced urban features.
                       Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.1 Q1)

 • Chanhudaro was the bead-making industrial centre of the Indus civilisation
                       Excavations found bead-making workshops, drills and furnaces using carnelian and other stones. Chanhudaro had
                       no citadel. Lothal was a dockyard town also known for beads.
                       Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.39 Q1)

 • Harappans used seals and sealings for long-distance communication/trade
                       Seals of steatite carry script and animal motifs; found in Mesopotamia too.
                       Group-1 Screening Test 2017 (GS & Mental Ability) (B3 p.102 Q121)

 • Sohgaura is not a Harappan site (it is known for a Mauryan-period copper plate)
                       Sutkagendor (Baluchistan, westernmost), Surkotada (Gujarat, horse remains) and Rakhigarhi (Haryana, among the
                       largest) are Harappan sites. Sohgaura in Gorakhpur district (UP) yielded a copper plate in Brahmi script about
                       famine relief, from the Mauryan age.
                       Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.3 Q17)

 INDUS VALLEY CIVILIZATION

 • A graduated ivory scale was found at Lothal
                       Lothal in Gujarat (on the Bhogavo river) is famous for its dockyard, bead-making factory and an ivory measuring
                       scale, showing Harappan precision in measurement. Mohenjo-daro yielded a shell scale.
                       Group-1 Prelims 2012 (B3 p.118 Q11; answer B3 p.125 Q11)

 • Traces of cultivation in the Harappan culture were found at Lothal and Rangpur
                       Rice husks have been found at Lothal and Rangpur in Gujarat, giving clear evidence of crop cultivation. Kalibangan in
                       Rajasthan is separately famous for a ploughed field with criss-cross furrows.
                       Trap: Distractors: Kalibangan and Ropar, Chanhudaro and Khetri, and Mohenjo-daro. Kalibangan is known for the
                       ploughed field, so it tempts as an answer.
                       Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Oct) (B3 p.137 Q51)

 • Harappans had close external contacts with Mesopotamia
                       Mesopotamian records mention 'Meluhha' (the Indus region); Dilmun (Bahrain) and Magan (Oman) were
                       intermediate stations. Harappan seals have been found at Ur and Kish.
                       Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.155 Q144)

 • Harappa is on the Ravi river
                       Daya Ram Sahni excavated Harappa in 1921 (Montgomery district, Punjab). Mohenjo-daro is on the Indus, Lothal on
                       the Bhogava, Kalibangan on the Ghaggar.
                       Group-1 Prelims 2004 (Mar) (B3 p.206 Q70)

 • Asura marriage involved paying a bride-price to the bride's family
    The Dharmashastras list eight forms of marriage: Brahma, Daiva, Arsha, Prajapatya (approved) and Asura,
    Gandharva, Rakshasa, Paishacha (less approved). In Arsha a token gift of cattle was given; in Asura the bride was
    effectively bought.
    Trap: Arsha involved only a token gift of a cow and bull, not a price.
    Group-1 Prelims 2012 (B3 p.118 Q12; answer B3 p.125 Q12)

 • The four varnas are first mentioned in the Purusha Sukta
    The Purusha Sukta is in the 10th Mandala of the Rigveda and describes the varnas emerging from the cosmic man.
    The 10th Mandala is a later addition.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Oct) (B3 p.137 Q52)

 • Sage Agastya is credited with Aryanising South India
    Tradition says Agastya crossed the Vindhyas and spread Vedic culture in the south; he is also linked with early Tamil
    grammar.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Oct) (B3 p.137 Q53)

 • The Boghazkoi inscription (c.1400 BCE, Turkey) names Vedic gods Indra, Varuna, Mitra and Nasatyas
    It is a treaty between the Hittite king and the Mitanni king, invoking these deities as witnesses, which suggests
    westward movement of Indo-Aryan-speaking groups. Mansehra and Shahbazgarhi have Ashokan edicts in Kharosthi;
    Junagarh has Rudradaman's inscription.
    Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.3 Q18)

 • Later Vedic pottery, per the book key (1): Black Slipped Ware, Painted Grey Ware and Red Ware (II, III and
   IV only).
    The later Vedic phase (c.1000-600 BCE) is linked with settled farming and iron in the Ganga plains, where PGW,
    black slipped and red wares are found. NBPW belongs to the later urban phase of the Mahajanapadas and Mauryas
    (c.600-200 BCE).
    Trap: NBPW is the main distractor. Black-and-Red Ware is the other option to watch. Note: some standard sources (R.S.
    Sharma) also include Black-and-Red Ware for the later Vedic people.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.1 Q2)

 • Rig Vedic religion: Dyaus like Greek Zeus/Jupiter, Agni as intermediary carrying offerings
    Rig Vedic Aryans worshipped nature gods: Indra (most important), Agni, Varuna, Dyaus. Prithvi was earth goddess
    but not 'mother of all gods' in the sense given. Key accepts cow sacrifice as part of ritual.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.39 Q2)

 VEDIC LITERATURE

 • Rigveda manuscripts were inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register (2007)
    Thirty Rigveda manuscripts held at the Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute, Pune were listed. Other Indian
    entries include the Tarikh-e-Khandan-e-Timuriyah and Shaivite manuscripts of Pondicherry.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.151 Q58)

 • Subject in which Vedic Aryans were least proficient (book key: Astronomy)
    Vedic texts show knowledge of astronomy (Vedanga Jyotisha), geometry (Sulba Sutras) and medicine (Atharvaveda
    herbs). Chemistry is generally seen as least developed then.
    Trap: The key's Astronomy answer is doubtful; Chemistry (C) fits better.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.155 Q141)

 • The Vedic text that first refers to money lending is the Atharvaveda
    The book finds the earliest Vedic references to lending and interest in the Atharvaveda, showing early economic
    practices. Later Vedic texts discuss the moneylender (kusidin) more fully.
    Trap: Distractors: Yajurveda, Satapatha Brahmana and Gopatha Brahmana. The Satapatha Brahmana is the one most
    often picked instead.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.155 Q145)


 Buddhism, Jainism & Philosophy (19)
 BHAKTI & PHILOSOPHY

 • Sankaracharya taught Advaita
    Adi Shankara (8th century, Kalady, Kerala) taught non-dualism of Atman and Brahman and set up four mathas:
    Sringeri, Dwarka, Puri, Joshimath. Ramanuja taught Vishishtadvaita, Madhva Dvaita and Nimbarka Dvaitadvaita.
    Group-1 Prelims 2012 (B3 p.120 Q49)

 • Smriti is not one of the six orthodox darshanas
    The six are Nyaya (Gautama), Vaisheshika (Kanada), Sankhya (Kapila), Yoga (Patanjali), Mimamsa (Jaimini) and
    Vedanta (Badarayana).
    Group-1 Prelims 2004 (Mar) (B3 p.206 Q69)

    Jaimini founded Purva Mimamsa, while the six orthodox schools also include Sankhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika and

 BUDDHISM

 • Buddha gave his first sermon at Sarnath (Deer Park)
    The first sermon, Dhammachakka-pavattana (Turning the Wheel of Law), was given to five ascetics at Sarnath near
    Varanasi. Enlightenment was at Bodh Gaya, birth at Lumbini and death (Mahaparinirvana) at Kushinagar.
    Group-1 Prelims 2012 (B3 p.118 Q14; answer B3 p.126 Q14)

 • Atisha Dipankara Srijnana went to Tibet in the 11th century
    He was a teacher at Vikramashila under the Palas and revived Buddhism in Tibet.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Oct) (B3 p.138 Q64)

 • The Buddha died (Mahaparinirvana) at Kushinagara
    Kushinagar (UP) was in the Malla territory. Buddhist councils: Rajagriha, Vaishali, Pataliputra and Kashmir
    (Kundalvana).
    Group-1 Prelims 2004 (Mar) (B3 p.209 Q120)

 BUDDHIST ARCHITECTURE

 • Anda, harmika and chhatri are structural parts of a Buddhist stupa
    The anda is the hemispherical dome holding relics, the harmika is the square railed platform on top, and the chhatri
    is the umbrella-like finial symbolising honour. Stupas such as Sanchi and Amaravati show this layout, often with a
    circumambulatory path (pradakshinapatha) and gateways (toranas).
    Trap: Chaityas are prayer halls and viharas are monasteries; basadis are Jain temples.
    Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.2 Q1)

 BUDDHIST SITES

 • A rare life-sized stucco Buddhist sculpture was found at Phanigiri, Telangana (2019)
    Phanigiri (Suryapet) is a Satavahana-era Buddhist site.
    Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-II (GS & Aptitude) (B3 p.90 Q99)

 INDIAN PHILOSOPHY

 • Kumarila Bhatta propagated Purva Mimamsa. REPEATED
    Purva Mimamsa (founder Jaimini) stresses Vedic ritual (karma kanda); Uttara Mimamsa is Vedanta. Kumarila
    (7th-8th century) defended Vedic ritualism against Buddhism.
    Trap: Yogachara is a Buddhist school.
    Group-2 Mains 2019 Paper-2 (held 19-07-2019) (B1 p.104 Q26); Group-2 (paper not stated) (B2 p.13 Q67); Group-2 Paper-2 2012 (B2 p.51 Q11)

 • Sankhya-dualism of prakriti/purusha; Vaisheshika-atoms; Lokayata-materialism; Ajivika-fate (niyati)
    Sankhya (Kapila) explains creation through nature; Vaisheshika (Kanada) proposed atomic theory;
    Lokayata/Charvaka denied afterlife; Ajivikas (Makkhali Gosala) believed everything predetermined.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.39 Q3)

 JAINISM

 • The Lingayat (Veerashaiva) movement dealt a serious blow to Jainism in Karnataka
    Basavanna's Veerashaiva movement in the 12th century drew away Jain followers in Karnataka. In Tamil Nadu the
    Nayanars and Alvars weakened Jainism and Buddhism.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Oct) (B3 p.137 Q55)

 • Vardhamana Mahavira was a Kshatriya
    He was born at Kundagrama near Vaishali to Siddhartha (Jnatrika clan) and Trishala, a Lichchhavi princess. He
    attained kaivalya at Jrimbhikagrama and died at Pavapuri.
    Group-1 Prelims 2008 (B3 p.173 Q107)

 JAINISM & BUDDHISM

 • Jainas used Prakrit/Ardhamagadhi and also pioneered Apabhramsa literature
    Early Jainas preached in Prakrit and their canon is in Ardhamagadhi, avoiding Sanskrit at first. They were the first to
    use Apabhramsa for important works and wrote its first grammar; later they also wrote in Kannada, Tamil and
    Sanskrit.
    Trap: Incorrect statement = 'never used Apabhramsa'.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.1 Q3)

 RELIGION & BUDDHISM IN ANDHRA

 • Bhadrabahu came south with Chandragupta Maurya during a famine
    Jain tradition says Bhadrabahu led monks to Shravanabelagola during a 12-year famine, with Chandragupta Maurya.
    Monks who stayed under Sthulabhadra became the Svetambaras.
    Group-2 2003 (B2 p.92 Q10)

 • The Alvars were the first Vaishnava devotees of South India
    The 12 Tamil Alvars (6th-9th century) and 63 Saiva Nayanars started the South Indian Bhakti movement.
    Trap: The book dates it to the 11th century, which is too late.
    Group-2 2000 (Optional) (B2 p.103 Q37)

 • Madhvacharya taught Dvaita
    Sankara taught Advaita, Ramanuja taught Visishtadvaita, and Madhva taught Dvaita.
    Group-2 1989 (B) (B2 p.123 Q52)

 RELIGION & PHILOSOPHY

 • Syadvada belongs to Jainism
    Syadvada (the 'maybe' theory) is the Jain doctrine of conditional predication. It goes with Anekantavada: reality has
    many aspects and any statement is true only from a given standpoint.
    Group-1 1987 (B2 p.146 Q73)


 Mahajanapadas & Mauryas (15)
 MAGADHA & MAHAJANAPADAS

 • Ajatashatru fortified Pataligrama, which grew into Pataliputra
    His son Udayin made Pataliputra the capital. Ajatashatru (Haryanka) also hosted the First Buddhist Council at
    Rajagriha.
    Group-1 Prelims 2002 (B3 p.221 Q32)

 MAHAJANAPADAS

 • Capitals: Avanti-Ujjain, Kosala-Shravasti, Vatsa-Kaushambi, Anga-Champa
    Avanti's northern capital was Ujjain (southern Mahishmati); Kosala's capital Shravasti; Vatsa under Udayana had
    Kaushambi; Anga's capital Champa was later annexed by Magadha under Bimbisara.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.1 Q4)

 MAURYAN ADMINISTRATION

 • Pautavadhyaksha was the Mauryan officer in charge of weights and measures
    Kautilya's Arthashastra lists many adhyakshas (superintendents). Sulkadhyaksha collected tolls/customs,
    Panyadhyaksha supervised trade/commerce, Samsthadhyaksha looked after markets, and Pautavadhyaksha
    standardised weights and measures.
    Trap: Do not confuse Panyadhyaksha (commerce) with Pautavadhyaksha (weights).
    Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.2 Q4)

 MAURYAN ART & ARCHITECTURE

 MAURYAS

 • Ashokan inscriptions use Brahmi, Kharosthi, Greek and Aramaic; name 'Ashoka' appears in Maski and
   Gujarra edicts
    Elsewhere he is 'Devanampiya Piyadasi'. Rock Edict XIII names five Greek kings (Antiochus, Ptolemy, Antigonus,
    Magas, Alexander). His war of succession is not mentioned in the edicts.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.39 Q5)

 • Earliest deciphered inscriptions in India are in Prakrit in Brahmi script (Ashokan)
    In the north-west Kharosthi was used.
    Group-1 Screening Test 2017 (GS & Mental Ability) (B3 p.102 Q122)

 • Ashoka released prisoners on anniversaries of his coronation
    Pillar Edict V says that up to the 26th anniversary of his coronation he had released prisoners 25 times. He also
    banned killing of certain animals.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Oct) (B3 p.137 Q54)

 • Chandragupta Maurya was the first Indian king to abdicate
    Jain tradition says he gave the throne to Bindusara, went with Bhadrabahu to Shravanabelagola and died by

    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.154 Q97)

 • 'Devanampiya' (beloved of the gods) was Asoka's title
    His edicts call him Devanampiya Piyadasi; the Maski and Gujarra edicts name him as Asoka. James Prinsep
    deciphered Brahmi in 1837.
    Group-1 Prelims 2008 (B3 p.173 Q108)

 • The work with Chandragupta Maurya's revolt against the Nanda king as its plot: Patanjali's Mahabhashya
   (book answer)
    Patanjali's Mahabhashya is a commentary on Panini's grammar from about the 2nd century BC. The book's key
    marks it for this question.
    Trap: Distractors: Kautilya's Arthasastra, Visakhadatta's Mudrarakshasa and Megasthenes' Indica. Note: some standard
    sources give Mudrarakshasa.
    Group-1 Prelims 2002 (B3 p.223 Q81)

 MAURYAS - ASHOKA

 • Ashoka's Rummindei (Lumbini) pillar inscription records reduction of Lumbini's land revenue to one-eighth
    After visiting the Buddha's birthplace in his 20th regnal year, Ashoka exempted Lumbini village from bali and
    reduced its bhaga (share of produce) to 1/8. The pillar is in present-day Nepal and is one of the clearest records of
    Mauryan revenue terms.
    Trap: Maski is a minor rock edict (gives name 'Ashoka'); Kalsi is a major rock edict site.
    Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.2 Q2)

 Post-Mauryan Period & Sangam Age (8)
 KALINGA & KHARAVELA

 • Hathigumpha inscription says a canal in Kalinga was first dug by a Nanda king (Mahapadma Nanda)
    Kharavela's inscription at Udayagiri (Bhubaneswar) records extending the canal built 'Nandaraja' 300 years earlier.

    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.39 Q4)

 SANGAM AGE

 • Vellalars in Sangam society were rich peasants forming the fourth caste
    Sangam texts show Brahmanas, ruling warriors, and the Vellalas (rich peasants) as a significant class; ploughing was
    done by labourers (kadaisiyar). Lowest groups like Pulaiyans worked with animal skins.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.2 Q9)

 SANGAM AGE & TRADE

 • Muziris (Kerala) was the chief centre of Indo-Roman trade
    Muziris (near Kodungallur/Pattanam) exported pepper; Arikamedu near Puducherry had Roman pottery. Pliny
    complained about Rome's gold drain to India.
    Group-1 Prelims 2008 (B3 p.173 Q112)

 SATAVAHANAS

 • Ajanta Cave 10 has the Shaddanta/Svetagaja Jataka painting of the Satavahana period
    Ajanta caves 9 and 10 are early Satavahana-period chaityas. Cave 10 has early paintings including the white
    elephant (Chhaddanta) Jataka.
    Group-2 Paper-2 2012 (B2 p.50 Q4)

 • The Sungas came to power in Magadha after the Mauryas
    Pushyamitra Sunga killed the last Mauryan, Brihadratha (c. 185 BCE), and founded the Sunga dynasty.
    Group-2 2003 (B2 p.92 Q9)

 • Samudragupta defeated the Pallava Vishnugopa of Kanchi
    The Allahabad prasasti lists Vishnugopa of Kanchi among the southern kings he defeated and then restored.
    Group-2 1993 (B2 p.112 Q30)

 • Arikamedu was the Roman trading base on the east coast
    Arikamedu near Pondicherry yielded Roman amphorae, Arretine ware and beads, marking it as an Indo-Roman
    trading station.
    Trap: No key in the book.
    Group-2 1984 (A) (B2 p.126 Q107)

 • 'Katakas' and 'Skandhavaras' in Satavahana inscriptions = military camps that acted as administrative
   centres
    Satavahana administration had a military character; these camps/settlements functioned as administrative centres.
    Satavahanas also gave tax-free land grants to Brahmanas and Buddhist monks.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.1 Q7)

 GUPTA AGE SCIENCE

 • Varahamihira's Romaka Siddhanta deals with astronomy
    In his Pancha Siddhantika, Varahamihira summarised five astronomical systems: Paulisa, Romaka, Vasishtha, Surya
    and Paitamaha. The Romaka system reflects Graeco-Roman astronomy. He also wrote Brihat Samhita
    (encyclopaedic) and Brihat Jataka (astrology).
    Group-2 Prelims 2024 (B1 p.2 Q5)

 GUPTAS

 • Samudragupta's South Indian campaign did not lead to annexation; Sakas were defeated by Chandragupta
   II, not I
    Samudragupta (Prayaga prashasti by Harisena) defeated 12 southern rulers and reinstated them
    (grahana-moksha-anugraha). Chandragupta II Vikramaditya ended Saka rule in western India.
    Trap: Statement on Chandragupta-I is the trap.
    Group-1 Prelims 2024 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.1 Q8)

 • Gupta-era guilds were autonomous bodies with own laws, judicial powers and even militias
    Narada and Brihaspati smritis recognise guild laws; guilds also acted as banks (e.g. Mandasor silk weavers'
    inscription).
    Trap: 'Non-autonomous' is the wrong feature.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.39 Q7)

 • Gupta order: Samudragupta > Chandragupta II > Kumaragupta I > Skandagupta
    Chandragupta I founded the Gupta era (319-20 CE). Skandagupta repulsed the Hunas.
    Group-1 Screening Test 2018 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.70 Q14)

 • The Gupta era was started by Sri Gupta (book answer)
    Sri Gupta is regarded as the founder of the Gupta line, and the book credits him with the start of the Gupta era. The
    era is reckoned from 319-320 AD.
    Trap: Distractors: Samudragupta, Chandragupta I and other Gupta rulers. Note: some standard sources give Chandragupta
    I.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Oct) (B3 p.138 Q71)

 • The founder of Nalanda University was Asoka (book answer)
    Nalanda in Bihar became one of the world's great centres of Buddhist learning; Hiuen Tsang studied there and
    Bakhtiyar Khalji destroyed it around 1193. The book's key credits Asoka as its founder.
    Trap: Distractors: Kumaragupta, Samudragupta and 'Nalanda'. Note: some standard sources give Kumaragupta I (5th
    century) as the founder.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Sep) (B3 p.154 Q98)

 HARSHA & POST-GUPTA

 • Harshavardhana bore the title Siladitya
    Harsha (606-647 AD) of the Pushyabhuti dynasty ruled from Thanesar and Kanauj. Hiuen Tsang visited his court;
    Banabhatta wrote Harshacharita; Harsha himself wrote Nagananda, Ratnavali and Priyadarsika.
    Group-1 Prelims 2012 (B3 p.120 Q47)

 • The Jajmani system became common in the post-Gupta period
    It was a hereditary exchange where service castes served landowning patrons (jajmans) for a share of produce. Land
    grants and feudalisation of that period strengthened it.
    Group-1 Prelims 2010 (Oct) (B3 p.137 Q59)

 • Harsha wrote Nagananda (also Ratnavali and Priyadarshika)
    Lilavati is a mathematics text by Bhaskaracharya; Naishadhacharita is by Sriharsha (12th century).
    Group-1 Prelims 2002 (B3 p.221 Q36)

 HARSHA & PUSHYABHUTIS

 • At Nalanda in Harsha's time the head was Silabhadra, teacher of Xuanzang
    Xuanzang studied there for years. Nalanda was founded by Kumaragupta I and later patronised by Palas.
    Dharmapala was a Pala king who founded Vikramashila.
    Group-1 Prelims 2022 Paper-I (GS) (B3 p.40 Q9)
