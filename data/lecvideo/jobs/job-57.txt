### part 1: Veterinary Practice – Vaccination, Feeding and Livestock Management
[0] h: Vaccination
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Purpose of vaccinating animals** | **Prevention of infectious diseases**
  [1.1] **Brucellosis** | Female calves vaccinated once at about **4-6 (up to 8) months** (S19 / RB51); the live **Brucella S19 vaccine can infect the vaccinator** – handle carefully
  [1.2] **Foot-and-mouth disease** | Controlled with **inactivated (killed)** vaccine
  [1.3] **Broilers** | LaSota (Newcastle, lentogenic) and IBD vaccines
  [1.4] **R2B (Mukteswar, mesogenic)** | Booster for older layers only – not for broilers
[2] h: Feeding and digestion
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **Concentrate for dairy cows** | About **0.4 kg per litre of milk** (1 kg per 2.5 litres) over maintenance; buffalo about 0.5 kg per litre
  [3.1] **Excess protein** to ruminants | Ammonia in the rumen, high pH → **alkaline indigestion** (excess grain → acid indigestion)
  [3.2] **Hay** (well-cured) | Keeps **15-18% moisture**; wetter hay moulds
[4] h: Breeding and poultry
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] **Frozen semen extender** | **Tris** (tris-egg yolk-citrate-glycerol) – the most widely used
  [5.1] **Semen collection from cocks** | By abdominal massage = **'milking'** (candling = checking eggs; brooding = rearing chicks)
  [5.2] **Egg turning** in chicken incubation | **Day 1 to 18**; stopped in the last three days (hatcher)
  [5.3] **CO2 in the incubator** | **0.3-0.5%** for optimum hatchability
[6] h: Meat animals and identification
[7] table: Fact | Key detail
  [7.0] **Dressing percentage** | Highest in the **pig** (about 70-80%); cattle, sheep, goats about 45-55%
