### part 0: Bretton Woods Conference and the IMF
[0] h: Bretton Woods (1944)
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Name | **United Nations Monetary and Financial Conference** = **Bretton Woods Conference**
  [1.1] When and where | **July 1944**, Mount Washington Hotel, **Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, USA**
  [1.2] Participants | **44 allied nations**, 730 delegates
  [1.3] Plans | **Harry Dexter White** (US, adopted) and **John Maynard Keynes** (UK)
  [1.4] "Bretton Woods twins" | **IMF + IBRD (World Bank)**, both HQ **Washington, D.C.**
  [1.5] Trade body | The planned **ITO** was never born → **GATT (1947)** filled the gap until the **WTO (1995)**
  [1.6] Exchange rates | Fixed (dollar pegged to gold); **Smithsonian Agreement (1971)** widened the band to **2.25%**, lasted about **14 months**
[2] table: Event | Date
  [2.0] IMF and IBRD founded (conceived) | July 1944
  [2.1] IMF Articles in force – formal existence (29 members) | **27 December 1945**
  [2.2] IMF began operations | 1 March 1947
  [2.3] First IMF borrower | France, 8 May 1947
  [2.4] SDR created ("paper gold") | 1967-69 (first allocation 1970)
[3] b: **India** is a founding member of both the IMF and the IBRD – an IMF member from **27 December 1945**.
[4] h: IMF – functions and lending
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] **IMF** | **International Monetary Fund** – monetary stability; **short-term balance-of-payments (BoP)** support; technical assistance (project/development finance is the World Bank's job)
  [5.1] Borrowers | **Only member countries**
  [5.2] Facilities | **Stand-By Arrangement**, **Extended Fund Facility**, **Rapid Financing Instrument (RFI)** and **Rapid Credit Facility (RCF)** (urgent BoP needs; RCF concessional for low-income members), PRGT
  [5.3] **PRGF** | Poverty Reduction and Growth Facility, 1999 (now the PRGT) – set up by the **IMF**
  [5.4] **Singapore Regional Training Institute (STI)** | One of the **IMF's regional training centres**
  [5.5] **Political-risk guarantees** | MIGA
  [5.6] **1991 BoP crisis** | India borrowed from the **IMF** (SDR 3.56 billion) – with the World Bank about **$7 bn** in all → New Economic Policy
[6] h: Quotas, votes, SDR
[7] table: Fact | Key detail
  [7.0] **Members** | **191** – Liechtenstein the 191st (21 Oct 2024); **189** in January 2020 (Nauru 190th, 2021)
  [7.1] **Quotas** (in SDRs) | Decide financial contribution, **borrowing access** and **voting power** (they do not fix exchange rates)
  [7.2] Quota formula | GDP **50%**, openness **30%**, variability **15%**, reserves **5%**
  [7.3] Major decisions | Need **85%** of votes → the **US** (~16.5%) alone holds a **veto**
  [7.4] India | **8th** by quota (~2.75%)
  [7.5] **SDR** basket | **Five** currencies since **1 October 2016**: US dollar, euro, **Chinese renminbi (yuan)** (added 2016), yen, pound
  [7.6] **IMFC** (International Monetary and Financial Committee) | Advises the Board of Governors; discusses concerns affecting the global economy and **advises the IMF** on the direction of its work; the **World Bank participates as an observer**
  [7.7] **Article IV consultations** | Regular review of each member's economy
[8] x: **1944** = founding conference; **27 Dec 1945** = formal existence; **1 March 1947** = operations; HQ = **Washington**.

### part 0: Democracy, Republic and Parliamentary Government
[0] h: Democracy
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Word origin** | **Greek** – *demos* (people) + *kratos* (rule)
  [1.1] **India** | A **representative (indirect) democracy**: people elect representatives who govern
  [1.2] Direct democracy | Absent at the national level – no **referendum, initiative or recall**
  [1.3] Participation | Democracy fails without people's participation and co-operation → **people should participate and co-operate with the government**
  [1.4] Constitution | India is a **democratic country** and has a **Constitution of its own**; having a constitution alone does not make a country democratic
  [1.5] Nature | Democratic government, rule of law, a government that follows the Constitution – not authoritarian

  [2.2] **Initiative** | People propose a law

[3] h: Republic
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **Republic** | **No hereditary ruler; the head of State is elected** (directly or indirectly) for a fixed term

  [4.3] India (Art. 1) | A **"Union of States"**; a **Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic with a parliamentary system**

[5] h: Parliamentary form of government
[6] table: Fact | Key detail

### part 2: Constitutionalism and Supremacy of the Constitution
[0] h: Constitutionalism
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Constitutionalism** | Government whose powers are **limited** by a supreme Constitution; it **recognises the need to limit the powers of government**
  [1.1] Not automatic | It is not inherent in every constitution – a country can have a constitution without constitutionalism (a dictator's constitution)
  [1.2] Antithesis | **Despotism** (absolute, unlimited rule); limited power and restraint of power are its features
  [1.3] **Constitutional government** | Places **effective restrictions on the authority of the State in the interest of individual liberty**
  [1.4] **Limits in India** | Fundamental Rights (**Art. 13** – laws inconsistent with FRs are void); judicial review; federal division of powers; **basic structure** (**Kesavananda Bharati, 1973**)
  [1.5] Amending power | Limited by the **basic structure doctrine**
  [1.6] Basic-structure elements | **Supremacy of the Constitution**, rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, limited amending power
[2] h: Supremacy of the Constitution
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] Supreme authority | The **Constitution** – not Parliament, the Supreme Court or religion
  [3.1] **Nature** | The **fundamental (foundation) law** of the land – every other law derives its authority from it
  [3.2] Organs | **Governmental organs owe their origin to the Constitution** – the Union and States derive their powers from it
  [3.3] **Why the Constitution is above Parliament** | The **Constitution specifies how Parliament is formed and what its powers are**
  [3.4] **What a constitution cannot do** | It sets basic rules, limits power, protects rights and expresses shared values – it **cannot ensure that the 'right' people come to power**
[4] h: Judicial review
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] **Judicial review** | Power of the SC/HCs to declare laws and executive orders **unconstitutional**
  [5.1] In the text | The phrase is **nowhere used in the Constitution**, but **Arts. 13, 32, 226** (and others) **explicitly confer** the power
  [5.2] **Basic structure** | Not defined in the Constitution – evolved by the Supreme Court (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973)
  [5.3] **Synthesis** | British **parliamentary sovereignty** + American **judicial supremacy**: courts can strike down laws; Parliament can amend most of the Constitution
  [5.4] **Scope narrower than in the USA** | India: **"procedure established by law"** (Art. 21); USA: **"due process of law"**
  [5.5] Test of a law | Only **against the supreme written Constitution** (FR violation, lack of competence, repugnancy), not its wisdom → judicial review is **restricted because the Constitution is supreme**
