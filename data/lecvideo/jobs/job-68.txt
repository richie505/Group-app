## topic group2 4-96 | Climate Change – Evidence, Causes, Global Warming, Ipcc Assessments, Impacts
### part 0: Evidence of Climate Change – Past Climates and Recent Warming
[0] h: Past climates
[1] table: Evidence | Key detail
  [1.0] **Tree rings (dendroclimatology)** | Ring width is a fairly accurate index of a year's **precipitation** (water available in the growing season); the best record of a place's **past local climate over centuries** (weather logs cover only recent decades)
  [1.1] Other evidence | Glacial landforms (advances and retreats), sediments of glacial lakes, archaeological finds (Rajasthan wet and cool about **8,000 BC**)
  [1.2] **Proxy records** | **Tree rings**, **ice cores** from the cryosphere (trapped air bubbles, isotopes), **coral reef** growth bands, ocean sediments and pollen
[2] table: Period | Key detail
  [2.0] **Great Ice Age** | **Pleistocene** (cooling began in the **Pliocene**); last glacial peak about **18,000** years ago; present interglacial began about **10,000** years ago
  [2.1] **Glaciation of peninsular India** | **Permo-Carboniferous (Carboniferous) ice age** of Gondwana – **Talchir tillite** at the base of the Gondwana rocks; Pleistocene glaciers reached only the Himalaya
  [2.2] **Little Ice Age** | About **1550–1850** (others 1300–1850; glaciers greatest **1500–1850**; also given as **1650–1870**); much colder winters in Europe and N America
  [2.3] Medieval Warm Period | **950–1250** AD
[3] h: Recent warming
[4] table: Indicator | Record
  [4.0] 2024 | Hottest year on record, about **1.55°C** above pre-industrial
  [4.1] 2025 | 2nd/3rd warmest (~1.43°C); **warmest La Nina year**
  [4.2] 2015–2025 | The **11 warmest years** (WMO State of the Global Climate 2025)
  [4.3] India | Warmed about **0.7°C** since 1900 (less than global land, ~1.59°C); IMD: **2024** warmest year since 1901
  [4.4] Gangotri Glacier | Retreated about **1,700 m** since 1935
  [4.5] Sea level | Up about **11 cm** since 1993
  [4.6] **20th century** | Global temperature **rose** by about **0.6°C (1.1°F)**; sea level rose **10–20 cm** (IPCC TAR 2001)
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] **Global warming** | Rise in global surface temperature, caused by rising greenhouse-gas concentrations
  [5.1] **Sea-level rise** | Thermal expansion + melting ice
### part 1: Causes – Natural and Anthropogenic
[0] h: Natural causes
[1] table: Cause | Meaning / effect
  [1.0] **Eccentricity** | Shape of Earth's orbit
  [1.1] **Obliquity** | Axial tilt
  [1.2] **Precession** (of the equinoxes) | Wobble of the rotational axis
  [1.3] Solar output (sunspots) | Satellites since **1978** show **no net increase** – the Sun does not explain recent warming
  [1.4] Volcanoes | **SO2** + ash form aerosols that reflect sunlight: **short-term cooling**
[2] b: **Milankovitch** (Serbian, **1920s**): astronomical theory – orbital forcing by eccentricity, tilt (shifting of the axis) and precession. Solar irradiance lies outside the Milankovitch factors (sunspots are a separate solar cause).
[3] h: Anthropogenic causes
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **Chief contributor** to warming (last and present century) | **Anthropogenic impact** (IPCC AR6)
  [4.1] Human sources | Fossil fuels (CO2), **land-use change** (deforestation removes sinks), **agriculture** (methane, N2O)
  [4.2] CO2 share | About **three-quarters** of warming
  [4.3] Effects of rising GHGs | Global warming, **CO2 fertilisation** of plants, stratospheric cooling linked to **ozone depletion**
  [4.4] **Water vapour** | Most abundant greenhouse gas (feedback)
  [4.5] **China** | Largest CO2 emitter, and largest in the developing world; India 3rd (after China and USA)
[5] table: Gas | GWP (100 yr, AR6) | Main sources
  [5.0] CO2 | 1 | Fossil fuels, deforestation, cement
  [5.1] Methane | ~27–30 | Rice paddies, livestock (**enteric fermentation**), landfills
  [5.2] Nitrous oxide | **~273** | **Fertilisers** (largest farm source), manure
  [5.3] CFCs / HFCs | Very high | Refrigeration, air conditioning (synthetic)
  [5.4] **SF6** | **~25,200 (highest)** | Electrical switchgear (**entirely synthetic**)
[6] h: Short-lived climate pollutants (SLCPs)
[7] table: Fact | Key detail
  [7.0] Lifetime | **Days to a few decades** – far **shorter** than CO2
  [7.1] Members | **Black carbon** (= **soot**), **tropospheric ozone**, methane, HFCs
  [7.2] Tropospheric ozone | Not emitted directly – formed by sunlight on methane, CO, VOCs, NOx
  [7.3] **Climate and Clean Air Coalition** | UNEP + **six countries**, **16 Feb 2012**

### part 1: Population Theories – Malthus, Ehrlich and Others
[0] h: Malthusian theory
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Author and work | **Thomas Robert Malthus**, *An Essay on the Principle of Population* (**1798**)

  [1.2] Outcome | Unchecked growth outruns food and ends in **scarcity, famine, disease and war** – the classic **overpopulation** theory
[2] table: Check | Acts on | Examples
  [2.0] **Preventive** | Lowers births | Moral restraint, **late marriage**, celibacy
  [2.1] **Positive** | Raises deaths | **Famine, disease**, war
[3] b: **Criticism:** food production and living standards rose faster than he predicted – **technology, innovation and sustainable resource management**; Marxists and liberals say poverty comes from unequal distribution, not numbers.

## topic group2 4-98 | World Population Growth, Demographic Transition and Regional Patterns
### part 0: Growth Terms – Natural Increase, RNI and Demographic Gap
[0] h: Terms
[1] table: Term | Meaning
  [1.0] **Natural increase** | **Births − deaths** (crude birth rate − crude death rate); excludes migration and marriage
  [1.1] **Demographic gap** | The difference **between the birth rate and the death rate** (widest in stage 2 of the transition)
  [1.2] Actual growth | Natural increase **+ net migration** (in-migrants − out-migrants)
  [1.3] Positive growth | Births exceed deaths, or permanent net in-migration
  [1.4] Negative growth | Births below deaths, or out-migration
  [1.5] Over-urbanisation | Urban growth faster than urban jobs and services (slums)
  [1.6] **CBR** | Live births per 1,000 people a year
  [1.7] **CDR** | Deaths per 1,000 a year
[2] h: Rate of natural increase (RNI)
[3] x: **RNI (%) = (CBR − CDR) / 10** when both are per 1,000.
[4] table: CBR | CDR | RNI
  [4.0] 44 | 11 | 44 − 11 = 33 per 1,000 = **3.3%** a year
  [4.1] 30 | 10 | **2.0%**
  [4.2] 25 | 7 | **1.8%**
### part 2: Low Fertility and Ageing Countries
[0] h: Total fertility rate
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Replacement-level TFR** | **2.1**
