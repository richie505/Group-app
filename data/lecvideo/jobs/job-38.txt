[0] h: Classification
[1] table: Federal features | Unitary (non-federal) features

  [1.2] **Division of powers** (Seventh Schedule) | **Flexibility** – most provisions amended by Parliament alone; States not indestructible (Art. 3)
  [1.3] **Supremacy of the Constitution** | **Unequal representation** of States in the Rajya Sabha
  [1.4] **Rigid Constitution** – federal provisions need **ratification by half the States** | **Emergency provisions** (esp. Art. 356)
  [1.5] **Independent judiciary** | **Single citizenship** (**Art. 9**); **integrated judiciary**
  [1.6] **Bicameralism** – Rajya Sabha represents the States | **All India Services**; **integrated audit (CAG)**; **integrated election machinery**

### part 2: Who Said What – Nature of Indian Federalism
[0] h: Who said what
[1] table: Scholar | Description
  [1.0] **K.C. Wheare** | **“Quasi-federal”** – “a **unitary state with subsidiary federal features** rather than a federal state with subsidiary unitary features”
  [1.1] **Ivor Jennings** | “A **federation with a strong centralising tendency**”
  [1.2] **Granville Austin** | “**Cooperative federalism**”; author of *The Indian Constitution: Cornerstone of a Nation*
  [1.3] **Morris-Jones** | “**Bargaining federalism**”
  [1.4] **Paul Appleby** | “**Extremely federal**”
  [1.5] **Alexandrowicz** | “A case **sui generis**”
  [1.6] **B.R. Ambedkar** | Constitution “avoids the **tight mould of federalism**” and “could be **both unitary as well as federal** according to the requirements of time and circumstances”
  [1.7] **K. Santhanam** | Unitary bias grew from financial dependence of States and the Planning Commission
  [1.8] **K.M. Nambiar** | Emergency powers a “**fraud on the Constitution**”
  [1.9] **H.V. Kamath** | “**Elephantine**” Constitution (the CA's seal was an elephant)
  [1.10] **Carl Friedrich** | India “not a nation in the traditional western sense but a comprehensive **culture of continental scope**”
  [1.11] **D.D. Basu** | Neither purely federal nor purely unitary but a combination of both – “quasi-federal”
  [1.12] **Supreme Court** | “Fundamentally federal with **pronounced unitary elements**”
[2] b: Standard label: “**federal system with unitary bias**” / “**federal in form, unitary in spirit**” – the reverse of Wheare's “unitary with federal features”. The unitary features tilt **the balance of power in favour of the Centre**, so India is a **quasi-federation** – a federal system with a very strong Centre.
[3] b: The **Preamble** does not describe India as federal, unitary or quasi-federal – it says “**Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic**”.

### part 5: Switching to Unitary Mode – Emergency and Overriding Powers
[0] h: Unitary mode
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Effect of an **emergency** | The Centre becomes all-powerful; the federal structure **converts into a unitary one without a formal amendment** – unique among federations
  [1.1] **National Emergency (Art. 352)** | Declared for all of India or **any part** (42nd Amendment); the only case where the Union assumes **full legislative and executive control** (President's Rule covers one State; ordinances or martial law (Art. 34) do not)
  [1.2] Place | **Part XVIII (Arts 352-360)**; **three** kinds: National (352), State/President's Rule (356), Financial (360)
  [1.3] **Art. 353** | The Centre can give **executive directions to a State on any matter**
[2] h: Overriding powers
[3] table: Article | Power
  [3.0] 249 | **Rajya Sabha** resolution (**2/3 present and voting**) in national interest → Parliament legislates on a State subject (1 year, renewable)
  [3.1] 250 | During a National Emergency Parliament legislates on the State List; laws lapse **6 months** after it ends
  [3.2] 252 | **Two or more States** request Parliament to legislate
  [3.3] 253 | Laws to implement **international agreements**
  [3.4] 354 | President may **reduce or cancel** (never increase) the transfer of finances – tax shares and grants – to the States during a National Emergency; the order lasts till the **end of the financial year** in which the Emergency ends
[4] h: Emergencies compared
[5] table: Basis | 352 National | 356 President's Rule | 360 Financial
  [5.0] Ground | War, external aggression, **armed rebellion** (44th replaced “internal disturbance”) | Failure of constitutional machinery | Threat to financial stability
  [5.1] Approval | **1 month**, special majority | **2 months**, simple | 2 months, simple
  [5.2] Duration | 6 months at a time, no maximum | 6 months at a time, **max 3 years** (beyond 1 year: National Emergency + EC certificate) | Until revoked
  [5.3] Used | **3 times – 1962, 1971, 1975** | Many times (AP **1973**, Jai Andhra) | **Never**

  [6.0] **1962** | Chinese aggression (Oct; in force till Jan 1968) – **external aggression**

[7] h: Effects
[8] table: Fact | Key detail
  [8.0] **44th Amendment (1978)** | Cabinet's **written advice**; **1/10th** of Lok Sabha members can seek a sitting to disapprove; **Arts 20-21** never suspended; **Art. 19** suspended automatically only in war/external aggression; the **38th Amendment** had barred judicial review
  [8.1] Fundamental Rights | **Only a National Emergency** affects them; others suspended only by a **Presidential order under Art. 359** (Art. 19 automatically under Art. 358 only in war/external aggression) – not all FRs

### part 6: Federalism – Meaning, Types and Federal Countries
[0] h: Meaning
[1] table: Term | Meaning
  [1.0] **Federalism** | Two sets of governments **co-exist** with a **constitutional distribution of powers**, under a **written Constitution**, with an independent judiciary as umpire
  [1.1] **Unitary government** | One supreme central government; local units are its creations with delegated powers; **no constitutional division of powers**
  [1.2] **Confederation** | A loose league of **sovereign States** that keep the **right to secede**
  [1.3] **Federal polity** | Union-State relations, inter-State relations, mechanisms for **cooperation** and for **resolving disputes**
[2] h: Unitary government – merits and demerits
[3] table: Merits | Demerits
  [3.0] **Quick (swift) decisions**, **uniformity of laws**, flexibility | Over-centralisation; local needs neglected
  [3.1] **Strong state** – firm crisis response | **Not suited to large, diverse countries**
[4] b: Adaptability, wide participation and less authoritarianism are federal/democratic merits.
[5] b: **Power sharing** reduces conflict, prevents majority imposition and consults all affected – it does not speed up decision-making.
[6] h: Coming-together vs holding-together
[7] table: Type | Meaning | Examples
  [7.0] Coming-together | Independent States pool sovereignty | **USA, Switzerland, Australia**
  [7.1] Holding-together | A large country divides power; stronger Centre | **India, Spain, Belgium**
[8] h: Federal and unitary countries
[9] table: Federal | Unitary
  [9.0] **USA**, Canada, Australia, **Argentina**, Switzerland, Germany, Russia, Brazil, **Belgium** (since 1993) | **Britain (UK)**, **France**, **Italy**, Japan, **China**, **Cuba**, **Sri Lanka**
