### part 2: Environmental Principles
[0] table: Principle | Meaning
  [0.0] **Precautionary** | Take **preventive action even without full scientific certainty** of harm; burden of proof on the **actor** to show the activity is harmless (Rio Principle 15)

  [0.2] **Public trust** | The **State holds natural resources in trust** for the public
  [0.3] **Absolute liability** | Hazardous industries – liable with **no exceptions**
  [0.4] **Sustainable development** | Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations
[1] b: **Environmental sustainability** aims to **conserve natural resources and protect global ecosystems** – not humans, wildlife or animals alone.

## topic group2 3-140 | Sustainable Development – Definition, Brundtland Report, Sdgs and India's Progress
### part 0: Meaning and Goals of Sustainable Development
[0] x: **Definition (Brundtland, 1987):** development that **meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs**.
[1] h: Meaning
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] Nature | A case of **inter-generational** sensibility in the use of **natural resources**
  [2.1] In practice | Present use with **minimum degradation** of natural resources; "**saving energy and other resources for the future without sacrificing people's comfort in the present**"; **balancing the need to use resources and conserve them for the future**
  [2.2] **Main goal** | **Balanced growth of environment and economy** – not growth only or protection only
  [2.3] Base | **Environmental approach**
  [2.4] Importance | Matters for the **well-being of human society** because of the definition itself
  [2.5] Limit | Intertwined with **carrying capacity** – Earth's finite capacity
  [2.6] **Three pillars / components** | **Economic growth, social inclusion, environmental protection**
  [2.7] **Green Agenda** | **Concern for environment and for the future generations**
  [2.8] **Economic sustainability** | Creating wealth that **improves living standards** over time without exhausting resources
[3] table: Objective | Contrary to it
  [3.0] Meet present needs while keeping choices for the future | **Prioritise individual wealth accumulation**
  [3.1] Balance economy, society and environment | Profit maximisation, growth at any cost
  [3.2] Intergenerational equity | Unrestricted resource exploitation
[4] b: **Mid-1980s debate** on the **negative aspects of development**: degradation of natural resources, pollution, **displacement and rehabilitation**.
[5] b: Major challenge to SDG 1 (no poverty): **economic inequality and unequal distribution of resources**.

## topic group2 4-2 | Motions of the Earth – Rotation and Revolution; Effects (day and Night, Coriolis Force, Tides)
### part 0: Rotation – Axis, Speed and Effects
[0] h: Rotation basics
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Rotation** | The Earth spinning on its **axis** (line through both poles); **revolution** = moving round the Sun
  [1.1] Direction | **West to east** – **counter-clockwise** seen from above the North Pole; only **Venus and Uranus** spin the other way
  [1.2] Axis | **Tilted 23½° (23°30') from the perpendicular to the orbital plane** ("from the ecliptic axis"); 66½° with the orbital plane
  [1.3] Angular speed | **15° per hour** (1° in 4 min)
  [1.4] Linear speed | **~1,670 km/h at the Equator – the maximum velocity of rotation** – falling to **zero at the poles**
  [1.5] Not felt | Motion is uniform and the atmosphere moves along with the Earth
  [1.6] **Pole Star** | Seems fixed – it lies almost **in line with the Earth's axis of rotation**; it marks **north**, so walking **east** it is on your **left**
[2] table: Day | Measured against | Length
  [2.0] **Sidereal** (one true rotation) | A distant star | **23 h 56 min 4.09 s**
  [2.1] Solar | The Sun | 24 h (~4 min longer, as the Earth also moves along its orbit)
[3] h: Effects of rotation
[4] table: Effect | Key detail
  [4.0] **Day and night** | The diurnal rhythm of daylight and air temperature
  [4.1] **Coriolis deflection** | Flow paths of air and water turned sideways
  [4.2] **Movement of the tides** | A place is carried through the tidal bulges twice a day
  [4.3] Also | Time difference between longitudes, centrifugal bulge
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] Seasons | Caused by revolution + tilt (not rotation); day and night come from rotation, not revolution
  [5.1] **No rotation** | No sunrise / sunset and no day-night cycle; seasons would remain
  [5.2] **Faster rotation** | Stronger centrifugal effect → **g decreases at the Equator, unchanged at the poles**
[6] h: Evidence and pioneers
[7] table: Fact | Key detail
  [7.0] **Aryabhata** (5th century) | First explained that the **rotation of the Earth** causes the daily rising and setting of the Sun (*Aryabhatiya*; also *Aryasiddhanta*)
  [7.1] **Foucault pendulum**, Paris **1851** | The plane of swing slowly turns
  [7.2] Other proofs | Coriolis deflection, equatorial bulge, apparent east-to-west motion of the sky
[8] h: The axis is shifting
[9] b: Studies (ETH Zurich, *Nature Geoscience* / *PNAS* 2024) show the rotation axis drifting and the day lengthening; cause = **mass redistribution**: as **polar ice melts, water moves towards the Equator** (also groundwater loss) – day longer by **~1.33 ms per century** (2000–2020).
[10] b: **Solar flares and CMEs** bombard the upper atmosphere with energy but do not shift the axis.
### part 1: Coriolis and Centrifugal Effects
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **Coriolis force** | An **apparent force** caused by the **Earth's rotation**; shapes winds, ocean currents, cyclones and **large-scale weather patterns**
  [0.1] **Ferrel's law** | Moving air and water are deflected to the **right in the Northern Hemisphere** and to the **left in the Southern Hemisphere**
  [0.2] Force = **2Ωv sin(latitude)** | **Zero at the Equator**, **maximum at the poles**; grows with **latitude and wind speed**; linked to the **decreasing rotational velocity with increasing latitude**
  [0.3] Cyclones | Rarely form within **5° of the Equator**; NH cyclones spin **anticlockwise**, SH clockwise
  [0.4] **Cyclone Montha (Oct 2025)** | Steered north-northwest to the **Kakinada** coast
[1] b: **Centrifugal force** of rotation: maximum at the Equator, zero at the poles → **equatorial bulge, polar flattening (oblate spheroid)**; the **weight of a body is maximum at the poles**, least at the Equator.
### part 2: Tides
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] Causes | Gravitational pull of the **Moon** (main) and the **Sun**, and the **centrifugal force** of the Earth–Moon system (bulge on the far side)
  [0.1] Tide-generating force | Gravitational attraction − centrifugal force
  [0.2] Semi-diurnal tide | **Two high and two low tides a day**; successive high tides **~12 h 25 min apart**
  [0.3] Daily delay | The **lunar day is ~24 h 52 min**, so high tide comes **~52 min later each day**: **8:00 am → 8:52 am**
[1] table: Tide | Position | When
  [1.0] **Spring** (highest) | Sun, Earth, Moon **in a straight line** | **New moon and full moon** – twice a month
  [1.1] **Neap** (lowest range) | Sun and Moon at **right angles** | First and third quarter, ~7 days after spring tides – twice a month
[2] b: Highest spring tides: full / new moon at an **equinox**; tides are larger when the Moon is at **perigee**. Ebb = falling tide; flow / flood = rising tide.
### part 3: Day and Night, Day Length, Midnight Sun
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **Day and night** | Caused by the **rotation of the Earth on its axis**
  [0.1] **Circle of illumination** | Terminator = "**grey line**"; passes through both poles **only at the equinoxes**
  [0.2] **Unequal day and night** | Tilt + revolution; difference **least near the Equator** (~12 h day all year), **increasing away from it** to the poles
  [0.3] **Poles** | ~6 months of day + 6 months of night (**revolution with axial tilt**)

[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] Orbit | Slightly **elliptical**, Sun at one focus
  [0.1] Period | **~365¼ days** (365.25; sidereal year 365 d 6 h 9 min 9.5 s)
  [0.2] Mean speed | **29.78 km/s**; the **Sun's gravity** is the centripetal force

### part 0: Longitude and Local Time – Calculations
[0] h: The rule
[1] b: Earth turns **360° in 24 h**, **west to east** → **15° = 1 hour**, **1° = 4 minutes**, **1' of arc = 4 seconds**.
[2] table: Longitude gap | Time gap
  [2.0] 1° | **4 minutes**
  [2.1] 15° | **1 hour (60 min)**
  [2.2] 90° | 6 hours

  [2.4] 1' of arc | 4 seconds
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] Direction | **East is ahead (add), west is behind (subtract)**; moving westward, local time **falls behind**

  [3.2] **Same side** of Greenwich | Subtract the longitudes
  [3.3] **Opposite sides** | Add them (25°E and 35°W = 60° = **4 h**; 6°W and 9°E = 15° = 1 h)

### part 1: Overhead Sun, Shadows and Insolation
[0] h: Vertical rays and shadows
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Overhead noon Sun | Only **between the Tropics**, **twice a year** (**zero-shadow days**)
  [1.1] Never vertical rays | North of 23½°N – **Delhi, Srinagar**
  [1.2] Get vertical rays | **Hyderabad, Mumbai, Kolkata** (south of the Tropic); **Andhra Pradesh** (12°41'N–19°07'N) **twice a year**
  [1.3] **21 June** | Sun overhead at the **Tropic of Cancer** – no noon shadow there; in north India the noon **shadow is smallest on 21 June**, longest in December; the Sun **does not set at the Arctic Circle**
  [1.4] **Equator** | No noon shadow in **March and September**
  [1.5] **Delhi is hotter at noon in summer** | In winter the Sun's rays fall **more slanting (oblique)**; the cause is not the Earth's distance from the Sun

### part 2: Why Seasons Occur – Tilt, Revolution, Perihelion
[0] h: Cause of seasons
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Seasons | **Revolution of the Earth + inclination (tilt) of its axis**
  [1.1] **Day-length variation** | **Revolution on a tilted axis** – not rotation, the elliptical orbit, oceans, mountains or altitude
  [1.2] Tilt | **23½° (23.5°) from the perpendicular** = **66½° to the orbital plane**
  [1.3] **Parallelism** | Axis keeps pointing the same way (towards the **Pole Star**) all year because of **angular momentum** → hemispheres tilt alternately towards (summer) and away (winter) → **opposite seasons**
  [1.4] Full set | **Axial inclination + parallelism + revolution + spherical shape** → changing **angle of insolation** and **day length**
  [1.5] Tilt gives | Seasons, unequal days, the tropics and polar circles
  [1.6] Rotation gives | Day and night (the **circle of illumination** divides them) and the **Coriolis** deflection, to the **right in the NH**
[2] table: Change | Result
  [2.0] Tilt raised from 23.5° to **30°** | **More intense seasonal contrasts**
  [2.1] Axis **perpendicular** to the orbit | No seasons, equal day and night all year, Sun always over the Equator; the North Pole would not lie always in darkness
[3] table: Point | Date | Distance | NH season
  [3.0] **Perihelion** (nearest the Sun) | ~**3/4 January** | ~147 million km | **Winter**
  [3.1] **Aphelion** (farthest) | ~**4 July** | ~152 million km | Summer
[4] b: Earth is **nearest the Sun in northern winter**, so distance does not cause seasons.
[5] b: Long term: **precession** (axial wobble) – **Vega** was pole star ~14,000 years ago and will be again in ~12,000 years; **Milankovitch cycles** drive long-term climate change. Melting ice moving mass away from the axis **slows rotation**.
[6] x: Seasons come from **revolution on an inclined axis** – not from rotation or the Sun's distance.
