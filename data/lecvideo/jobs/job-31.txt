## topic group2 2-36 | Sc/st (prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989 and Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955
### part 0: Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955
[0] h: Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Enacted | **1955** as the **Untouchability (Offences) Act** (8 May 1955; **in force 1 June 1955**) – the first law enforcing **Art. 17**
  [1.1] Renamed | **Protection of Civil Rights Act** by Act 106 of **1976** (w.e.f. 19 Nov 1976); **Rules 1977**
  [1.2] **Extent – s.1(2)** | **"The whole of India"** (J&K never excluded)
  [1.3] **Civil right** | Any right accruing from the abolition of untouchability by Art. 17
  [1.4] **"Untouchability"** | Defined neither in Art. 17 nor in the Act
  [1.5] Punishes | The **preaching and practice of untouchability**: religious and social disabilities
[2] table: Section | Provision
  [2.0] 3 | Religious disabilities – entry/worship in public places of worship (including a **private temple allowed for public worship**)
  [2.1] 4 | Social disabilities – shops, hotels, water sources, roads, jewellery and finery
  [2.2] 5 | **Refusing admission to hospital, dispensary, educational institution, hostel**
  [2.3] 6 | **Refusing to sell goods or render services**
  [2.4] 7 | Other offences – insulting an SC member (7(1)(d)); **preaching or justifying** untouchability
  [2.5] **7A** | Compelling scavenging, **removing carcasses**, flaying – deemed untouchability
  [2.6] 10 | Abetment – a public servant wilfully neglecting investigation
  [2.7] **10A** | **Collective fine** imposed by the **State Government** on inhabitants of an area
  [2.8] **12** | Court **presumes** the untouchability ground when the victim is a **Scheduled Caste** member
  [2.9] **14** | **Offences by companies** – person in charge, and any **director, manager, secretary** or officer consenting
  [2.10] **15** | **Cognizable**; triable summarily (except where the minimum term exceeds 3 months)
  [2.11] 15A | State duties – legal aid, special courts, committees, surveys of prone areas, annual report
  [2.12] 16A | No probation above 14 years
[3] h: Penalties and implementation
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **First offence** | **1–6 months** and fine **₹100–500**
  [4.1] Compounding | Offences are **non-compoundable**
  [4.2] Same social group | No offence when accused and victim belong to the **same social group** (no ground of untouchability)
  [4.3] Disqualification | Convict **disqualified** from elections to Parliament/State Legislatures for a fixed period (**s.8, RPA 1951**)
  [4.4] **Implementation** | States/UTs; nodal at the Centre – **Social Justice and Empowerment** with MHA
  [4.5] NHRC | Under the Protection of Human Rights Act 1993
  [4.6] **Sukanya Shantha (2024)** | Art. 17 bars caste-based **purity stigma**
[5] x: PCR **s.12** = presumption, **s.14** = companies, **s.10A** = collective fine; in force since **1955**.
### part 1: PoA Act 1989 – Enactment, Scope and Offences
[0] h: Enactment and scope
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Act | **Act 33 of 1989**, assent **11 Sept 1989**; **in force 30 January 1990**; **23 sections**; Rules **1995**
  [1.1] Amendments | **2015** (in force 26 Jan 2016) and **2018** (20 Aug 2018)
  [1.2] J&K | Excluded until **2019**
  [1.3] **Why** | The PCR Act 1955 and the IPC proved **inadequate** to check atrocities against SCs/STs
  [1.4] **Long title (preamble)** | Prevent atrocities, set up **Special Courts**, provide **relief and rehabilitation of victims**
  [1.5] **Who** | Offences by persons **"not being a member of a SC or ST"**, including government employees; crimes among SCs/STs are outside it
[2] h: Offences and punishment
[3] table: Provision | Punishment
  [3.0] s.3(1) atrocities (e.g. **dumping excreta, waste, carcasses** in premises; begar; land grab; witchcraft accusation) | **Not less than 6 months, up to 5 years**, and fine
  [3.1] s.3(2)(i) false evidence to convict an SC/ST of a **capital offence** | **Life imprisonment** and fine (death if an innocent is executed)
  [3.2] s.3(2)(vii) offence by a **public servant** | **Not less than 1 year**
  [3.3] s.4 wilful neglect of duty by a public servant | 6 months to 1 year
  [3.4] s.5 subsequent conviction | Not less than 1 year
[4] b: **2015 additions to s.3(1):** tonsuring, **garlanding with footwear**, carcass disposal, manual scavenging, abuse by caste name, devadasi dedication, **witchcraft (zb)**, **social/economic boycott (zc)**, preventing entry to **educational institutions**, hospitals, places of worship.
[5] b: **s.6:** applies IPC **s.34, Chapters III, IV (general exceptions – private defence), V, V-A, s.149, XXIII** – so the accused **can** plead private defence or that he belongs to the victim's caste.
[6] b: **s.8 presumptions:** financial help → **abetment**; group offence from a land dispute → **common intention / common object**.
[7] x: Enacted **1989**, in force **1990**; covers offences by **non-SC/ST** persons only.

### part 2: Types of Disability – Learning Disability and Blindness
[0] h: Learning disability
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Learning disability** | Difficulty in a specific academic skill despite normal intelligence
  [1.1] Types | **Dyslexia** (reading), dysgraphia (writing), dyscalculia (maths)
  [1.2] Separate conditions | **Autism spectrum disorder**, **ADHD** and **mental retardation (intellectual disability)** are developmental conditions, distinct from specific learning disabilities
  [1.3] RPwD Act 2016 | Lists **specific learning disabilities** among its 21 disabilities, separately from autism and intellectual disability
[2] h: Blindness
[3] b: **New definition of blindness (April 2017, MoHFW, WHO criteria):** a person is blind if unable to **count fingers from 3 metres** (the 1976 definition used **6 metres**); programme renamed the National Programme for Control of Blindness and Visual Impairment.

[0] h: Composition and voting
[1] table: Fact | Key detail

  [1.1] Non-permanent members | Elected by the **UNGA**, two-thirds secret ballot, for **2 years**; **no immediate re-election (Art. 23)**
