## topic group2 5-45 | Cyber Security Concerns – Cyber Crime Types, Malware, Ransomware, Phishing, Social Engineering
### part 0: Malware – Virus, Worm, Trojan and Other Types
[0] h: Basics
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Malware** (malicious software) | Single word for viruses, worms, trojans etc.: software **designed to disrupt, damage or gain unauthorised access** to a computer (not merely buggy software)
  [1.1] **VIRUS** | "**Vital Information Resources Under Siege**"; a program that **replicates itself**, **attaches to files** and spreads from computer to computer
  [1.2] Virus entry | Via **images, audio/video files, downloads, attachments**; infects storage such as a **pen drive**, not a display or keyboard
  [1.3] **Bug** | A fault in a program that stops it working correctly
  [1.4] **Father of computer virology** | **John von Neumann** (1949 theory of self-reproducing automata)
[2] h: Types of malware
[3] table: Type | Defining behaviour | Example
  [3.0] **Virus** | **Attaches to files**, reproduces and spreads; the user must run the infected file | Brain, Michelangelo
  [3.1] **Worm** | Uses the **network to send copies of itself without user action**; needs no host program | WannaCry, Morris
  [3.2] **Trojan** | **Looks like legitimate software**, tricks the user into installing it, then acts like a virus/worm; no self-replication; backdoor | Zeus
  [3.3] Ransomware | Encrypts files, demands ransom (often crypto) | WannaCry, Petya
  [3.4] Spyware | Secret surveillance; steals passwords, banking data | Pegasus
  [3.5] Adware | Unwanted ads; bundled with free/pirated software | Fireball
  [3.6] **Rootkit** | Administrator-level access; **modifies the host OS to hide malware**; hardest to detect | Stuxnet parts
  [3.7] **Stealth virus** | Designed to **hide from antivirus** detection | –
  [3.8] Keylogger | Records keystrokes (passwords, PINs, OTPs) | DarkHotel
  [3.9] Botnet | Network of hijacked "zombie" devices for DDoS, spam | Mirai (IoT)
[4] b: **Antivirus** software: McAfee, Kaspersky, Avast, **Webroot**, Norton; a rootkit is malware.
[5] b: Viruses, worms and trojans all threaten **electronic payment systems**.
[6] x: Spreads over the network by itself without user action = **worm**; disguised as genuine software, no self-replication = **trojan**.
### part 1: Attack Techniques – Phishing, Spoofing, Spam, DoS
[0] h: Attack techniques
[1] b: **Cyber attack** = any attempt to harm, damage or threaten a computer system or network.
[2] table: Threat | Meaning
  [2.0] Hacking | Unauthorised access to or manipulation of a system
  [2.1] **Phishing** | **Deceptive e-mails/texts** that look legitimate, tricking users into **revealing sensitive information** (card details, PIN) or installing software
  [2.2] Vishing | Phishing by voice calls
  [2.3] **Spoofing** | E-mail **appears to come from one source but is sent from another**; a **fake website posing as a legitimate one** steals user data
  [2.4] **Spam** | **Junk / unsolicited bulk e-mail** (a computer term)
  [2.5] **E-mail bombing** | **Identical e-mail sent repeatedly** to one address to overflow it
  [2.6] Sniffing | Capturing data packets on a network
  [2.7] **DoS / DDoS** | **Denial of Service**: **flooding** a server with fake traffic so genuine users **cannot access** it; DDoS uses many machines (botnet)
  [2.8] Ransomware | Locks data and demands payment
  [2.9] Man-in-the-middle | Secretly intercepts/alters communication between two parties
  [2.10] SQL injection | Malicious database code via website input fields
  [2.11] Zero-day | Exploits an unknown, unpatched flaw (e.g. Pegasus)
  [2.12] Supply-chain attack | Compromises a trusted vendor, e.g. SolarWinds (2020)
  [2.13] **Social engineering** | **Exploiting the human element** (trust, urgency, curiosity) to gain access

[2] h: CIA triad
[3] table: Principle | Meaning
  [3.0] **Confidentiality** | Procedures, policies and controls securing data **against illicit access or use**
  [3.1] Integrity | Protection against alteration
  [3.2] Availability | Access for authorised users (denial = availability lost)

[4] b: **IRDAI** (2021) retail cyber cover excludes **gross negligence**.
[5] h: Cryptography
[6] table: Term | Key point
  [6.0] **Symmetric** | Same key encrypts and decrypts – AES, **Blowfish** (**Bruce Schneier, 1993**; faster alternative to DES)
  [6.1] Asymmetric | Public-private key pair – **RSA**; basis of PKI
  [6.2] Digital signature | Signed with the sender's **private** key, verified with the **public** key
  [6.3] Hashing | One-way, fixed-length digest (**SHA-256**)
  [6.4] **Plaintext** | Original, **readable** data **before encryption** – the input to encryption
  [6.5] **Ciphertext** | Encrypted, **unreadable** output of an encryption algorithm; turned back only with the key
  [6.6] **Decryption** | Converting **ciphertext back into plaintext** with the algorithm and the **correct key**, by authorised users (encryption is the reverse)

### part 0: IT Act 2000 – Purpose, Sections and Shreya Singhal
[0] h: IT Act 2000 – purpose
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Information Technology Act, 2000** | India's **first cyber law**: **legal recognition** to transactions by **electronic data interchange** ("**electronic commerce**") and to electronic records; framework against computer crimes
  [1.1] Concepts (Sec. 2) | **Electronic record, digital signature, certifying authority**
  [1.2] **Special law** | May override the **IPC** where an offence is fully covered
  [1.3] **Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA)** | Licenses CAs, which issue Digital Signature Certificates
  [1.4] **2008 amendment** | Legal framework for cybercrime today: technology-neutral "**electronic signature**"; added sexually explicit material, **video voyeurism**, data leakage by intermediaries, e-commerce fraud; inserted 70A, 70B
[2] h: Sections
[3] table: Sec. | Subject
  [3.0] 4 / 5 | Recognition of e-records / e-signatures
  [3.1] 43 / 43A | Unauthorised access / compensation for failure to protect data
  [3.2] 66 | Hacking-type offences
  [3.3] **66A** | Offensive messages – **struck down (2015)**
  [3.4] 66C / 66D | **Identity theft** / cheating by personation
  [3.5] 66E / 66F | Privacy / **cyber terrorism (life)**
  [3.6] 67 / 67A | Obscene / sexually explicit
  [3.7] 69 / 69A | Interception / **blocking**
  [3.8] 70 / 70A / 70B | Protected systems / **NCIIPC** / **CERT-In**
  [3.9] 79 / 79A | **Safe harbour** / the **Centre** notifies Examiners of Electronic Evidence
  [3.10] 87 | Rule-making (IT Rules 2021)
[4] h: Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (24 March 2015)
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] Sec. 66A | **Struck down** as violating **Art. 19(1)(a)** (not saved by 19(2))
  [5.1] Sec. 69A | **Upheld**
  [5.2] Sec. 79 | **Read down** – "actual knowledge" = court order or government notice
  [5.3] **IT Rules 2021** | No due diligence = loss of **safe harbour**; takedown within **three hours** (27 Mar 2026)

### part 0: Fission vs Fusion – Chain Reaction, Reactor vs Bomb, Energy of the Sun
[0] table: Point | Fission | Fusion
  [0.0] Process | **Heavy nucleus splits** (U-235 + slow neutron → barium + krypton + **2–3 neutrons** + energy) | **Two light nuclei join** (deuterium + tritium → helium + neutron)
  [0.1] Trigger / condition | **Neutron** hit on a fissile nucleus; **critical mass** | Very high temperature (10⁷–10⁸ K) and pressure – "thermonuclear"
  [0.2] Energy | Large | **More per unit mass** (3–4 times fission)
  [0.3] Where | **Nuclear reactors** (controlled), **atom bomb** (uncontrolled); all present-day nuclear electricity | **Sun and stars**, **hydrogen bomb**; fusion power still at R&D stage
[1] x: **Breaking apart of a nucleus = fission**; joining of light nuclei = fusion.
[2] h: Chain reaction and criticality
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **Chain reaction** | Each fission releases **more neutrons** (2–3), which split more nuclei; not every neutron causes a fresh fission, and not all react at once
  [3.1] Moderator | Fast neutrons are **slowed by a moderator (graphite, heavy water)** so that U-235 captures them
  [3.2] **Critical mass** | Minimum fissile mass that sustains a chain reaction
  [3.3] **Criticality** | Self-sustaining chain reaction – every operating reactor is "critical"
  [3.4] **Reactor vs atom bomb** | Both use a chain reaction: in a **reactor it is controlled** (moderator, control rods), in a **bomb it is uncontrolled**
[4] h: Atom bomb and hydrogen bomb
[5] table: Feature | Atom (fission) bomb | Hydrogen (thermonuclear) bomb
  [5.0] Principle | **Uncontrolled fission** of U-235 / Pu-239 | **Uncontrolled fusion** of **deuterium and tritium** into helium, **triggered by a fission bomb**
  [5.1] Person | **Otto Hahn** (with F. Strassmann, 1938-39) – discovered nuclear fission | **Edward Teller** – "father of the hydrogen bomb"; first US test Nov 1952, Eniwetok
  [5.2] Power | Large | Far more powerful
[6] table: City (only two atom-bombed) | Bomb | Fuel
  [6.0] Hiroshima, 6 Aug 1945 (first) | **Little Boy** | **U-235**
  [6.1] Nagasaki, 9 Aug 1945 | **Fat Man** | **Plutonium-239**

[8] h: Energy of the Sun and stars
