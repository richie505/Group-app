## topic group2 5-85 | Air Pollution – Sources, Pollutants, Naaqs, Aqi, Ncap, Stubble Burning and Acid Rain
### part 0: Acid Rain – Causes, Chemistry, pH and Effects
[0] h: Definition and chemistry
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Acid rain** | Precipitation (rain, snow, fog, mist) with **pH below 5.6**; normal rain ≈ **5.6** (carbonic acid from CO2)
  [1.1] Term coined | **1872** by **Robert Angus Smith** (Manchester coal burning)
  [1.2] **pH scale** | Measures **acidity / basicity**; **0–14**; **7 neutral**, below 7 acidic; each unit = **tenfold** change
  [1.3] **Human blood** | pH ≈ **7.4** (slightly basic)
  [1.4] Causing gases | **SO2 and NOx** (oxides of **sulphur and nitrogen**); main single factor: **SO2**
  [1.5] **Ozone** | Only speeds up the oxidation (photo-oxidant)
  [1.6] **Chemistry** | SO2 / NOx + water + O2 + photo-oxidants → **H2SO4 (~two-thirds)** + **HNO3 (~one-third)**
  [1.7] Formed by | **Reaction between clouds (water) and sulphur dioxide**
  [1.8] **Maximum acid** | **Sulphuric acid (H2SO4)**, then nitric acid; HCl and carbonic acid are minor
  [1.9] Coal thermal plants | Cause acid rain through **SO2** (sulphur in coal)
[2] table: Gas | Anthropogenic | Natural
  [2.0] SOx | Coal power plants, smelting of sulphide ores, H2SO4 plants, refineries, **industries** | Volcanoes, oceans, decay
  [2.1] NOx | Vehicles, power plants, biomass burning | Lightning, forest fires
[3] h: Deposition and effects
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **Deposition** | Wet (rain, snow, fog) and **dry** – about **half** of atmospheric acidity returns dry
  [4.1] **Effects** | Acidifies lakes (fish die, aluminium released); damages forests and soils (leaches Ca, Mg); corrodes limestone / marble (**Taj Mahal "marble cancer"** – SO2, Mathura refinery); **respiratory ailments**
  [4.2] **Taj Trapezium Zone** | ~**10,400** sq km (Agra, Fatehpur Sikri, Agra Fort); **M.C. Mehta v. UoI (1996)** barred coal / coke
  [4.3] **Transboundary** | Scandinavia receives acid rain from the UK / central Europe; **India = largest SO2 emitter (2022)**
  [4.4] Affected countries | **Norway** and **Canada** – classic transboundary receivers; East Asia / China now has heavy acid rain; Germany's Black Forest was also hit
  [4.5] **Control** | Low-sulphur fuel, **FGD scrubbers** (trap SO2), low-NOx burners, catalytic converters; **liming** is remedial, not preventive
[5] table: Instrument | Year | Focus
  [5.0] LRTAP (UNECE) | **1979** | First binding treaty on transboundary air pollution
  [5.1] Helsinki Protocol | **1985** | **30%** sulphur cut
  [5.2] Sofia Protocol | **1988** | Freeze NOx
  [5.3] Gothenburg Protocol | **1999** | SO2, NOx, NH3, VOCs
### part 1: Carbon Monoxide and Other Pollutants – Health Effects
[0] h: Carbon monoxide (CO)
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Source** | **Incomplete combustion** of carbon fuels – **vehicle exhaust** (main pollutant gas in exhaust), **cigarette smoke**, coal / wood burning; CO2 comes from **complete** combustion
  [1.1] **Nature** | **Colourless, odourless**, poisonous – the odourless poisonous gas in coal smoke
  [1.2] **Effect** | Binds **haemoglobin** → **carboxyhaemoglobin**; affinity **200–250 times** that of oxygen; cuts the blood's **oxygen-carrying capacity**
  [1.3] Danger | Affects the **bloodstream**, can lead to death – usually called the "**most dangerous**" air pollutant
  [1.4] Mechanism | **Competes with oxygen in the blood**; not converted to CO2 in the body; does not destroy haemoglobin's structure
  [1.5] Bond | Often called **irreversible**; strictly reversible but very stable (CO is released slowly)
[2] h: Pollutants and health
[3] table: Pollutant | Main source | Part / effect
  [3.0] **Carbon monoxide** | Incomplete combustion, vehicles, cigarettes | **Blood stream** – carboxyhaemoglobin, hypoxia, death
  [3.1] **Asbestos dust** | Asbestos mining, cement sheets | **Lung** – asbestosis
  [3.2] **Lead** | Leaded petrol (old), smelters, batteries, paints | **Brain** / nervous system; blood pressure, kidney
  [3.3] **Mercury** | Coal burning, industry | **Stomach** / nervous system
  [3.4] **SO2** | Coal / oil burning, power plants, smelting | Eye, throat and lung irritation, asthma; acid rain
  [3.5] **NOx** | High-temperature combustion – vehicles, power plants, **lightning** | Eye / airway irritation; smog, acid rain
  [3.6] **H2S** | **Decaying vegetation and animal matter** (chief), sewage, refineries | Rotten-egg smell; toxic
  [3.7] **Fly ash** | **Burning coal** in thermal power plants | Lung disease; captured by ESP
  [3.8] **PM2.5 / PM10** | Combustion, road and construction dust, biomass | Heart / lung disease; PM2.5 enters blood, crosses the placenta
  [3.9] **Ground-level O3** | Secondary (photochemical) | **Asthma**, chest pain, crop damage
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **Lead** in city air | From **petrol** combustion (anti-knock **tetraethyl lead, TEL**); leaded petrol **phased out in India (2000)**
  [4.1] **Most injurious exhaust components** | **CO and lead** (old leaded-petrol era)
  [4.2] **Cigarette smoke** | **CO and benzene** (also nicotine, HCN, arsenic, formaldehyde)
  [4.3] **Inorganic gaseous pollutants** | HCN, H2S, CO, CO2, SO2, NOx (methane and phosgene are usually grouped as organic)
  [4.4] **Asthma** | Caused / aggravated by **air pollution**
  [4.5] **Rickets** | A deficiency disease; smog cuts UV → less vitamin D
  [4.6] **Bio-gas / gobar gas** | Mainly **methane** (**50–65%**) + CO2 (**35–50%**); traces of H2, N2, H2S
  [4.7] Gases | **Ozone = O3**; **CFCs** deplete the stratospheric ozone layer
[5] h: Other links and burden
[6] b: **Ammonia** (fertilisers + livestock, **77–80%**) drives secondary particles; **ammonium sulphate** (from SO2 + NH3) ≈ one-third of India's PM2.5; **magnetite** particles (combustion, brake wear) found in brain tissue – possible Alzheimer's link; **cadmium** from metal smelting; PAHs from incomplete burning; PM2.5 crosses the placenta (AIIMS, **3 June 2026**).
[7] b: WHO: ~**7 million** deaths a year; fossil-fuel air pollution ~**1.72 million** premature deaths in India (**2022**); NCR winter PM2.5 ~**35** times the WHO limit; IIT Delhi: curbing power-plant SO2 could prevent ~**1.24** lakh deaths a year.

### part 4: Smog, Primary and Secondary Pollutants
[0] table: Type | Formed from | Conditions
  [0.0] **Classical / London smog** (sulphurous, 1952) | **Smoke + fog + SO2** | Cold, humid; grey/black
  [0.1] **Photochemical / Los Angeles / summer smog** | **NO2/NOx + hydrocarbons (VOCs) + sunlight** → **ozone, PAN** (oxidising agents) | Warm, sunny, traffic; **brownish – "brown air"**
[1] b: **Smog** = smoke + fog; essentially caused by **oxides of nitrogen and sulphur**. Photochemical smog **always contains ozone**, which is **produced** during its formation (hydrocarbons and NOx are inputs); it has a high concentration of **oxidising agents**.
[2] h: Primary and secondary pollutants
[3] table: Type | Key detail
  [3.0] **Primary** (emitted directly) | CO, CO2, SO2, NOx, fly ash, lead
  [3.1] **Secondary** (formed in air) | **O3, PAN, smog**, ammonium sulphate – ozone is **not a primary pollutant**; smog is a secondary pollutant
  [3.2] **Photooxidants (photochemical oxidants)** | Secondary: **ground-level ozone, PAN, hydrogen peroxide**, organic peroxides/aldehydes; strong **oxidising agents** – eye and throat irritation, reduced lung function, **crop damage**, **rubber cracking**/fading of dyes
  [3.3] **Tropospheric ozone precursors** | NOx, CO, methane/VOCs; **water vapour is not** one
  [3.4] Ozone | Stratospheric ozone shields UV and is not an air pollutant; ground-level ozone is
[4] b: **Smog effects:** breathing problems, bronchitis; heavy smog cuts UV → less vitamin D, so rickets rise (**rickets is not a smog disease**).
[5] b: **Asian Brown Cloud (UNEP, 2002):** layer of soot, black carbon and sulphate aerosols over **South Asia** (Indo-Gangetic plain) and the Indian Ocean, **January–March** (dry season), from biomass and fossil-fuel burning.

  [2.2] Fuel injection | Promotes **complete combustion**

### part 0: Ozone Depletion – CFCs, Chlorine and Bromine
[0] h: Cause
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons)** | **Main cause** of ozone depletion and of the "ozone hole"
  [1.1] **Full form** | CFC = **Chlorofluorocarbon** – carbon + chlorine + fluorine; entirely **man-made**, non-toxic, non-flammable
  [1.2] **Mechanism** | Under UV in the stratosphere CFCs release **chlorine**; **chlorine and bromine atoms destroy ozone catalytically** (one Cl atom breaks thousands of O3 molecules) → more UV reaches the Earth
  [1.3] Ozone-splitting atom | The **chlorine** of CFCs, not the carbon
  [1.4] **Bromine** | From **halons** (fire extinguishers) – about **100 times more potent** than CFCs
  [1.5] Other chlorine sources | **Carbon tetrachloride**, **methyl chloroform**
  [1.6] Where | The **stratosphere, not mesosphere** (not the lower atmosphere where humans live)
  [1.7] **CFCs are also greenhouse gases** | They cause both warming and ozone loss
[2] b: **Ozone depletion** = reduction of stratospheric ozone, caused by high chlorine and bromine from **CFCs and halons**.
[3] h: Sources of CFCs and related gases

  [4.0] **CFCs** | **Freon** refrigerant, ACs, aerosol propellants, plastic foams, cleaning electronic parts
  [4.1] **Halons** | **Fire extinguishers**
  [4.2] **Nitrogen oxides** | **Fertiliser plants**, vehicles, combustion
  [4.3] **Methane** | **Bacterial decomposition** (wetlands, paddy, livestock)
[5] b: **CFC uses:** **plastic foams, cleaning electronic components, aerosol pressurising agents** (not tubeless tyres).
[6] x: Gas most responsible for ozone depletion: **CFC**.

### part 2: Ozone Layer, Dobson Unit and the Ozone Hole
[0] h: Ozone layer
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Ozone** | **O3** (three oxygen atoms); formed when **UV radiation splits O2** (UV + O2 → O3)
  [1.1] Stratospheric ozone | About **90%**, in the **stratosphere** (**10–50 km**; main layer ~**15–30** km) – **protective**, absorbs harmful **UV (UV-B)**
  [1.2] Tropospheric ozone | ~**10%** – a **pollutant**
  [1.3] Protects from | **Ultraviolet rays**

[2] h: Dobson Unit
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **Dobson Unit (DU)** | Measures the **thickness / concentration of ozone in a column of air from the ground to the top of the atmosphere**
  [3.1] 1 DU | **0.01 mm** of pure ozone at **0°C and 1 atm**
  [3.2] **"Ozone hole"** | Total column ozone **below 220 DU**
[4] h: Ozone hole
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] Most affected region | **Antarctica**; seasonal thinning in the **Southern Hemisphere spring (Aug–Nov)**
  [5.1] **Polar stratospheric clouds** | In extreme cold turn stable chlorine compounds into **active chlorine**, which destroys ozone when sunlight returns
  [5.2] History | Ozone began to **drop sharply in the 1980s**; Antarctic hole **reported in 1985 by the British Antarctic Survey**
  [5.3] **Tibetan ozone low ("halo") 2005** | Discovered by **G.W. Kent Moore**, University of Toronto
  [5.4] **Recovery** | NASA / WMO report healing of the Antarctic hole, mainly due to the **ban on CFCs** under Montreal

## topic group2 5-93 | Genetic Engineering – rDNA, Gm Crops, Gene Editing and Environmental Impact of Transgenics
### part 0: rDNA Technology – Tools, Vectors and Steps
[0] h: What genetic engineering does
[1] table: Term | Meaning
  [1.0] **Genetic engineering / recombinant DNA (rDNA) technology** | Cutting, joining and transferring genes between organisms
  [1.1] rDNA | **DNA formed by combining genetic material from different sources** – also called **chimaeric (chimeric) DNA**
  [1.2] **Transgenic organism** | DNA carries a **foreign gene from another species** (the "transgene"), inserted artificially for a **desired characteristic or product**
  [1.3] **GMO** | Any organism whose genome is altered by genetic engineering – genes moved **in ways that would not occur naturally**
  [1.4] **Across species** | rDNA moves genes **plant to plant, animal to plant and microbe to higher organism**
[2] b: A **mouse eight times its size**, made with a human growth-hormone gene, is a product of **genetic engineering** (not hybridisation, mutation breeding or hormonal feeding).
[3] b: **Achieved:** **disease resistance, growth promotion, animal cloning**; **human cloning** has not been achieved (banned, no fruitful result).
[4] b: Also achieved: artificial functional DNA, DNA replication outside a cell (PCR), cell division in petri dishes (tissue culture); functional chromosomes made by joining DNA segments from different species have not been made.
[5] h: Tools
[6] table: Tool | Role
  [6.0] **Restriction endonuclease** | **"Molecular scissors" / chemical knives** – **cuts DNA at specific recognition (base) sequences**, not at random
  [6.1] **DNA ligase** | **"Molecular glue"** – **joins DNA fragments** (gene + vector); in replication it **joins Okazaki fragments**
  [6.2] **Plasmid** | Small, extra-chromosomal, usually **circular double-stranded DNA** of bacteria; replicates independently; the commonest **vector**
  [6.3] **pBR322** | Early, widely used **E. coli** cloning vector; p = plasmid, **BR = Bolivar and Rodriguez**
  [6.4] **Origin of replication (ori)** | **The specific DNA sequence that initiates replication**; alien DNA linked to it can multiply in the host
  [6.5] **PCR** | **Amplifies (copies) a specific DNA segment in a test tube – no living cells needed** (heat-stable Taq polymerase, 20-40 cycles)
  [6.6] **RNAi** | Gene silencing
[7] table: Bacterium | Role
  [7.0] ***Escherichia coli*** | Host for cloning
  [7.1] ***Agrobacterium tumefaciens*** | Plant vector; soil bacterium that **causes crown gall disease** (dicots) by transferring **T-DNA** of its **Ti plasmid**
[8] b: The disarmed Ti plasmid makes *Agrobacterium* "**Nature's genetic engineer**" – useful for agriculture, a crown gall agent and a gene-transfer tool.
[9] h: Steps
[10] x: **Isolate DNA → cut at specific sites (restriction enzyme) → join to vector with DNA ligase (rDNA) → insert into host (transformation) → select, multiply, express product.**
[11] table: Fact | Key detail
  [11.0] Entry of recombinant plasmid | **Transformation**; bacteria multiply fast and act as factories
  [11.1] **Gene cloning** | Making many copies of a desired gene or DNA fragment
  [11.2] **Genetic library** | A collection of cloned DNA of an organism
[12] table: Field | Biotech use
  [12.0] Agriculture | Transgenic / GM crops

  [12.2] Industry | **Microbial production of useful products**
