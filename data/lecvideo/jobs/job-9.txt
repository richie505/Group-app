[0] h: Third Battle of Panipat
[1] table: Item | Key detail
  [1.0] Date | **14 January 1761** (Panipat I = 1526, II = 1556)
  [1.1] Opponents | **Marathas vs Ahmad Shah Abdali (Afghans)**
  [1.2] Maratha commander | **Sadashiv Rao Bhau** (overall); **Vishwas Rao** (Peshwa's son, nominal) – both killed
  [1.3] Artillery | **Ibrahim Khan Gardi** – the Muslim commander who **died fighting for the Marathas** (loyal)
  [1.4] Peshwa | **Balaji Baji Rao** – died soon after (June 1761)
  [1.5] Abdali's allies | **Najib-ud-Daula** (Rohilla, invited him) and **Shuja-ud-Daula** of Awadh
  [1.6] Eyewitness historian | **Kashiraj Pandit**, an official of Shuja-ud-Daula

[2] h: Defeat and result
[3] table: Cause | Effect
  [3.0] **Bhau's tactical errors**, **Abdali's superior generalship** | Maratha defeat
  [3.1] **Large numbers of non-combatants** and baggage; supply collapse | Weakened the army
  [3.2] No northern allies | Jats, Rajputs alienated
[4] b: **Coded message to the Peshwa:** "**Two pearls have been dissolved, 27 gold coins lost …**" – the pearls were Vishwas Rao and Bhau; the loss was the **Peshwa's**.
[5] b: **Result:** Maratha northern ambition halted; the vacuum helped the Company; "it decided not who was to rule India, but who was not"; recovery under **Madhav Rao I** within a decade.

[1] b: **Rule:** a dependent state whose ruler died **without a natural male heir** lapsed to the Company; an **adopted son** could inherit the ruler's **private property only**, not the throne.
[2] b: **Lord Dalhousie** (1848-56) applied it with zeal, but he was **not its originator** (earlier cases existed); first applied **1848**.
[3] table: State | Year

  [3.4] Udaipur (Chhattisgarh) | 1852

  [3.6] **Nagpur** (Bhonsle, Raghuji III) | **1854**
