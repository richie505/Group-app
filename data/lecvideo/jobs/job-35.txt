[0] table: Fact | Key detail

[5] b: **Article 32** is amended by a **special majority ("two-thirds")**, **without** State ratification – though the SC's power under it is part of the basic structure.

### part 4: Majorities in Parliament
[0] table: Majority | Meaning | Used for
  [0.0] **Simple** | Majority of members **present and voting** | Ordinary, Money and Financial Bills; confidence motions; Arts. 2-4, 169 laws
  [0.1] **Absolute** | Majority of the **total membership** | Never alone – always with another majority
  [0.2] **Effective** | Majority of the House's **effective strength** (total minus vacancies) | Removal of the **Vice-President (RS)**, Deputy Chairman, **Speaker/Deputy Speaker**

### part 5: Limits on the Amending Power – FRs and the Basic Structure
[0] table: Case / Act | Year | Held

  [0.8] **Waman Rao** | 1981 | Doctrine applies to amendments after **24 April 1973**

## topic group2 2-81 | Legislative Procedure – Ordinary, Money, Financial and Constitutional Amendment Bills; Joint Sitting
### part 0: Money Bill – Articles 109 and 110
[0] h: Definition – Art. 110(1)
[1] b: A Bill is a Money Bill if it contains **only** provisions on: (a) imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any **tax**; (b) regulation of **borrowing** or the **giving of any guarantee** by the Government of India; (c) **custody** of the Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund, payments into or withdrawals from them; (d) **appropriation** out of the CFI; (e) declaring expenditure **charged** on the CFI; (f) receipt, custody or issue of money of the CFI or Public Account, or the **audit of accounts of the Union or of a State**; (g) incidental matters.
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] Excluded (Art. 110(2)) | Not Money Bills merely because they provide for **fines/penalties**, **fees for licences or services**, or **taxes by local bodies** for local purposes
  [2.1] Who decides | The **Speaker** decides finally and **certifies** it (Art. 110(3)); the decision cannot be questioned in any court, in either House or by the President
  [2.2] **Appropriation Bill** | A Money Bill (Art. 114)
[3] h: Procedure – Art. 109
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] Introduction | **Only in the Lok Sabha**, **only by a minister**, **only on the President's recommendation** – never in the Rajya Sabha or a joint sitting
  [4.1] **Rajya Sabha** | **Cannot amend or reject** it; only makes **recommendations**; must return it within **14 days** (two weeks) – its maximum delay
  [4.2] Lok Sabha | **May accept or reject** all or any RS recommendations; **not bound** by them; no 14-day limit applies to it
  [4.3] RS amends substantially | The LS **may still proceed**, accepting or not accepting the recommendations
  [4.4] Not returned in 14 days | **Deemed passed by both Houses** in the form passed by the LS; sent to the President
  [4.5] RS approval | **Not necessary** to pass a Money Bill
  [4.6] **President** | May **give or withhold assent**; **cannot return** a Money Bill (normally assents, as it came on his recommendation)
  [4.7] Joint sitting | **No joint sitting** for a Money Bill
[5] b: **Aadhaar Bill, 2016** was introduced and passed as a **Money Bill**; upheld in **K.S. Puttaswamy (2018)**; the Money Bill question went to a 7-judge bench in **Rojer Mathew (2019)**.
[6] h: Rajya Sabha in financial matters
[7] table: Rajya Sabha can | Rajya Sabha cannot
  [7.0] **Discuss** the Annual Financial Statement (budget) | **Vote** on the Demands for Grants (only LS votes, Art. 113)

### part 1: Joint Sitting of the Two Houses – Article 108
[0] h: When it is held
[1] table: Article | Subject
  [1.0] **Article 108** | Joint sitting
  [1.1] **Art. 109** | Money Bill procedure
  [1.2] **Art. 110** | Definition of a Money Bill
  [1.3] **Art. 118(4)** | Speaker presides
[2] b: **Deadlock grounds** (after one House passes a Bill and sends it to the other): (1) the other House **rejects** it; (2) the Houses **finally disagree on amendments**; (3) more than **six months** pass without the other House passing it (periods of prorogation or adjournment for more than **4 consecutive days** are not counted).
[3] b: A joint sitting is only a deadlock-breaking device – both Houses agreeing to one for speedy legislation is no ground.
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **Applies to** | **Ordinary Bills** and **Financial Bills (I and II)**
  [4.1] Excluded | **Money Bills** (the Lok Sabha has **overriding powers**, so no deadlock arises) and **Constitution Amendment Bills** (each House passes them separately by special majority)
  [4.2] Elections | Not used to elect the President or Vice-President
[5] h: Procedure
[6] table: Point | Fact
  [6.0] Who summons | **President** – he "**notifies his intention**" to summon the Houses (Art. 108(1)); not the Speaker, PM or Chairman
  [6.1] Who presides | **Speaker of the Lok Sabha** (Art. 118(4)) → Deputy Speaker → **Deputy Chairman of RS** → person chosen by members present
  [6.2] Chairman of RS (Vice-President) | **Never presides** – he is not a member of either House
  [6.3] Quorum | **One-tenth** of total members of both Houses
  [6.4] Majority | **Simple majority** of members of both Houses **present and voting** (Art. 108(4))
  [6.5] Rules followed | Rules of Procedure of the **Lok Sabha**
  [6.6] New amendments | Only those causing final disagreement or made necessary by delay
  [6.7] After notification | **Neither House can proceed further** with the Bill
  [6.8] President does not summon | The **Bill comes to an end**
  [6.9] Bill **lapsed** on dissolution | No joint sitting

### part 0: Kesavananda Bharati (1973) – Where the Doctrine Was Propounded
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] Doctrine first propounded | **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)** – not in Golaknath (1967), Sajjan Singh (1965) or Shankari Prasad (1951)

### part 1: What the Basic Structure Means
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] Meaning | The **core features** of the Constitution that **cannot be amended or altered by Parliament** under **Art. 368** – features so essential that they **cannot be abrogated**
  [0.1] Scope | Wider than FRs or DPSPs alone; it does not concern what is amendable by simple majority, the order of Articles or procedural matters
  [0.2] Source | **Judicial interpretation** – **judge-made**, nowhere defined in the text; not inserted by any **Act of Parliament** or amendment
  [0.3] List | **Not exhaustive** – never fully listed; grows **case by case**
  [0.4] Limit on Parliament | The **basic structure** (not FRs as such, customs or distribution of powers)

[1] b: As a principle of judicial review, the doctrine **accepts the supremacy of the Constitution and of the courts** (the American principle of judicial supremacy, accepted "to a limited extent"). ### part 4: Vice-President – Term, Oath, Resignation, Removal, Vacancy; Acting President (Arts. 65, 67-70)
[0] h: Term, oath, resignation (Articles 67, 69)
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Term | **5 years**; continues **until his successor enters upon office** (Art. 67(c)); **re-election any number of times**
  [1.1] **Oath** | Administered by the **President** (or a person appointed by him) – Art. 69
  [1.2] **Resigns** | By writing to the **President** (Art. 67(a))
[2] h: Removal (Article 67(b))
[3] b: Resolution moved **only in the Rajya Sabha**, passed by a **majority of all the then members** of the Rajya Sabha (effective majority) and **agreed to by the Lok Sabha** (simple majority); **14 days' notice**; **no grounds** stated; not impeachment (two-thirds applies to the President's impeachment).
[4] h: Vacancy (Article 68)
[5] b: Vacancy by expiry: election before the term ends. Casual vacancy (death, resignation, removal): filled **as soon as possible** – **no six-month deadline**; the new VP gets a **full 5-year term**.
[6] b: The Constitution is **silent** on who performs the VP's duties during a vacancy; the **Deputy Chairman** performs only the duties of the **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**.
[7] h: Acting President (Article 65)
[8] b: On a **vacancy** in the President's office (death, resignation, removal) the VP **acts as President until a new President is elected** and enters office – a **maximum of six months**, because **Art. 62** requires the election **within six months** of the vacancy.
[9] b: When the President is **absent, ill or unable**, the VP **discharges his functions** until he resumes.
[10] b: While acting he gets the **powers, immunities and emoluments of the President**; he does **not perform the duties of Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** (the **Deputy Chairman** presides) and draws no Chairman's salary.
[11] b: If both offices are vacant (e.g. the acting VP resigns), the **Chief Justice of India** (then the senior-most SC judge) discharges the President's functions – **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969**.
[12] table: Point | President | Vice-President
  [12.0] Resigns to | **Vice-President** (Art. 56(1)(a)) | **President** (Art. 67(a))
  [12.1] Removal | Impeachment (Art. 61), two-thirds of total membership of each House | RS resolution (majority of all members) + LS agrees
  [12.2] Vacancy filled | **Within 6 months** (Art. 62) | **As soon as possible** (Art. 68)
[13] b: On the President's resignation, the VP immediately informs the **Speaker of the Lok Sabha** (Art. 56(2)).
