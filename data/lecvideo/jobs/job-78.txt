  [1.1] Micronutrients (8) | **Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu**, B, Mo, Cl, Ni – trace amounts (< 1% of dry weight)
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Magnesium** | **A macronutrient** (secondary)
  [2.1] **Manganese and zinc** | **Micronutrients** supplied by the soil
  [2.2] Inorganic fertilisers | Supply **nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium (NPK)**
  [2.3] Desirable NPK use ratio in India | **4:2:1**
  [2.4] **Zinc deficiency in maize** | **"White bud"** / white tip of young leaves
  [2.5] **Khaira** in rice | Zinc deficiency
  [2.6] **Excess nitrogen** | **Delays maturity** (lush vegetative growth, lodging)
  [2.7] Phosphorus | **Hastens** maturity
[3] h: Fertiliser facts
[4] table: Fertiliser | Key detail
  [4.0] **Urea** CO(NH2)2 | **~46% N – highest of common solid N fertilisers**; nitrogen in **amide** form; **100/46 ≈ 2.2 kg urea gives 1 kg N**; most popular for **foliar spray** (high solubility)
  [4.1] Ammonium nitrate / CAN / ammonium sulphate | 34% / 26% / 20.6% N
  [4.2] **DAP** (NH4)2HPO4 | **18% N, 46% P2O5** – balanced N + P supply
  [4.3] Superphosphate | **Calcium dihydrogen phosphate** (+ gypsum); acidic, so continued use **lowers soil pH (< 7)**
  [4.4] Potassic (MOP, SOP) | Disease and drought resistance, quality
  [4.5] Phosphatic | Root development, flowering, fruiting
  [4.6] **Neem-coated urea** | **Slows dissolution of urea and nitrification** → slow N release, higher N-use efficiency
[5] b: Urea → ammoniacal (NH4+) → nitrate in soil; **ammoniacal N is preferred for paddy and potato** (held by soil, less lost in flooded fields).
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] Wheat | Nitrogen is the main fertiliser (vegetative growth)
  [6.1] **Nitrogen-use efficiency** | Raised by **split application, nitrification inhibitors and slow-release N fertilisers**
  [6.2] **Balanced fertilisation** | Raises production, improves fertiliser-use efficiency, maintains soil productivity
  [6.3] Legumes | Need little N – NPK is P- and K-heavy (**1:2:2**)
  [6.4] Excess N | **Soil acidification, nitrate leaching, eutrophication**
  [6.5] Continuous chemical use | **Lower soil organic matter**, fewer microbes, lower nutrient density in food
  [6.6] Broadcasting granular fertiliser | Loses **50-70%** of the nitrogen
  [6.7] **PM-PRANAM** | States keep **50%** of the subsidy saved, as grants
[7] h: Nano fertilisers and Soil Health Card

[9] h: Organic manures and amendments
[10] table: Input | Meaning
  [10.0] **Animal manure** (FYM) | Dung, urine and litter of farm animals
  [10.1] **Compost** | Decomposed plant and animal waste – raises organic matter and microbial activity
  [10.2] **Crop residue** | Remains of harvested crops worked back into the soil
  [10.3] **Green manure** | Fresh crops (mostly **legumes**) ploughed in green – **sunhemp (most suitable)**, dhaincha, sesbania, cowpea

[12] b: **Earthworms** improve soil fertility – **"farmers' friends"**; an earthworm has **more than two pairs** of "hearts" (aortic arches – 4 pairs in Pheretima).

### part 1: Biofertilisers
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **Biofertilisers** | Living microbial inoculants (bacteria, algae, fungi) that **fix nitrogen, solubilise phosphorus** or decompose organic matter
  [0.1] Application | **Applied to seed/soil** (not only leaves)
  [0.2] Effect on soil | **Do not alter soil chemistry** like chemicals
  [0.3] Combination | **Can be used with organic manures**
[1] table: Biofertiliser | Type | Crop / role
  [1.0] **Rhizobium** | Symbiotic bacterium | **Legumes** (pea, pulses) – root nodules fix N2
  [1.1] **Azotobacter** | Free-living aerobic bacterium | Cereals – non-symbiotic N fixation
  [1.2] Azospirillum | Associative bacterium | Cereal roots (rice rhizosphere), no nodules
  [1.3] **Blue-green algae** (Anabaena, Nostoc) | Cyanobacteria | **Paddy** – fix atmospheric N into a form crops absorb (heterocysts)
  [1.4] **Azolla** | **Aquatic fern** with the **BGA Anabaena** | **Water-logged rice fields**; 2-3% N; often used with BGA
  [1.5] PSB | Bacteria | Solubilise phosphate
  [1.6] **VAM / Mycorrhiza** | **Fungus**–root association | **Phosphatic biofertiliser** (P uptake)
[2] b: **Mycorrhiza** increases **absorptive area / drought resistance**, helps plants **tolerate extreme pH** and **resists disease** – hence used to rehabilitate degraded sites.
[3] b: **Legumes** (pea) enrich soil with nitrogen; wheat, coffee, rubber, potato and sorghum do not.
[4] b: **Alfalfa** is a legume fodder, not a biofertiliser; Salvinia, Marsilea, Pteridium (ferns), Wolffia, Trapa and Lemna (other aquatic plants) are not biofertilisers either.

  [1.1] Multi-, inter-, mixed and **contour cropping**, green manure | **Intense mono-cropping / monoculture**
  [1.2] IPM, drip irrigation, reduced tillage | **Over-irrigation**
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Eco-friendly technology in agriculture** | **Organic farming** (GM crops, pesticide spraying and chemical fertilisers are not)
  [2.1] **Integrated farming system (IFS)** | Crops + livestock + fish/poultry on one farm; **higher profitability**, **use of by-products**, **recycling of farm waste**

[3] h: Impact of modern farming
[4] b: Synthetic fertilisers/pesticides **pollute soil and water**; sustainable farming **prioritises soil health and biodiversity**. Modern agriculture **does** deplete freshwater, and monoculture **harms** ecosystem stability.
[5] b: Most conspicuous effects: **soil degradation, pollution and ecological imbalance**; indiscriminate agro-chemicals cause **secondary pest outbreaks, pesticide resistance** and ecosystem contamination.
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] **Rachel Carson, *Silent Spring* (1962)** | Pesticides (DDT) harming wildlife and the environment – a founding text of **environmental protection**

### part 0: Soilless and Protected Cultivation – Hydroponics, Aeroponics, Phytotron
[0] table: Technique | Meaning
  [0.0] **Hydroponics** | Growing plants **without soil**, roots in **mineral nutrient solution** (sand, gravel or water may support them); still needs water, light, nutrients; ~90% less water

  [0.4] Greenhouse / polyhouse | Controlled microclimate; protection from weather, pests and diseases
  [0.5] **Phytotron** | Enclosed research greenhouse to **grow plants under controlled conditions**
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Aeroponics and disease | **Aeroponics limits disease transmission** (less plant-to-plant contact, oxygen-rich roots)

  [1.2] "Hydrophonics" | Old spelling of hydroponics – growth **without soil**, not without water

### part 1: The Greenhouse Effect and Heating of the Atmosphere
[0] h: Definition and mechanism
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Greenhouse effect** | Natural warming of the **lower atmosphere** (surface and troposphere) by **absorption and re-radiation of heat** by GHGs
  [1.1] In short | **Increase in atmospheric temperature due to concentration of GHGs**
  [1.2] Nature | A **naturally occurring** phenomenon (not caused by biodegradable waste in water)
  [1.3] First proposed | **Joseph Fourier**, **1820s**
  [1.4] Without it | Earth's average temperature about **−18°C** (also given as −19°C) instead of **+15°C**
[2] table: Step | What happens
  [2.0] **Step 1** | Atmosphere is **transparent to incoming short-wave** solar radiation
  [2.1] **Step 2** | The surface re-emits **long-wave infrared** (8,000-25,000 nm) – **not ultraviolet**
  [2.2] **Step 3** | CO2 and water vapour **strongly absorb infrared** (14,000-25,000 nm) – the atmosphere is **opaque to outgoing long-wave**
  [2.3] **Step 4** | GHGs re-radiate heat in all directions, partly back to the surface – they warm the air by **trapping heat** (not generating it)
[3] b: **Glass analogy:** the glass of a greenhouse or of a **solar cooker** lets light in and stops long-wave heat going out – the same principle.
[4] b: **Global warming** = gradual long-term rise in the average temperature of the atmosphere and oceans; mainly human (fossil fuels); GHGs **intensify** the natural effect.
[5] h: Heating of the atmosphere – heat budget
[6] table: Insolation (100 units) | Share
  [6.0] Reflected (albedo): 27 from cloud tops, 2 from snow/ice, rest scattered | **35**
  [6.1] Absorbed by the atmosphere | **14**
  [6.2] Absorbed by the surface | **51**
[7] table: Fact | Key detail
  [7.0] Main heat source of the atmosphere | **Long-wave terrestrial radiation** – indirectly from the earth, not incoming solar radiation
  [7.1] Reflection | **Clouds and gases reflect about one-fourth** of insolation (27 units from cloud tops)
  [7.2] **Cloudy nights are warmer** | Clouds stop heat radiating away from the ground and send it back
  [7.3] **Fresh snow** | Highest albedo – reflects about **80-85%** of sunlight (more than sand desert, paddy or prairie)
  [7.4] Role of trapped heat | Maintains the average temperature; CO2 is a good absorber
  [7.5] Without an atmosphere | Extremes – the day side very hot, like the Moon (not below freezing everywhere)
[8] x: The atmosphere is warmed from below: short-wave in, long-wave out.

### part 1: Cause and Chemistry of Ocean Acidification
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **Ocean acidification** | Ongoing **fall in ocean pH** caused by the **uptake (dissolution) of atmospheric CO2** – the **primary cause**
  [0.1] Not the cause | Oil spills, sewage, microplastics; acid rain matters only locally
  [0.2] Nicknames | **"Evil twin of global warming"**, **"the other CO2 problem"**
  [0.3] Ocean sink | Oceans absorb about **one-third** of human CO2, buffering climate change
[1] h: Chemistry
[2] table: Step | What happens
  [2.0] **Step 1** | CO2 + H2O → **carbonic acid** → H+ + bicarbonate
  [2.1] **Step 2** | Extra **H+ binds carbonate ions** – carbonate concentration falls
  [2.2] **Step 3** | **Less carbonate** left for shells and skeletons; the ocean's ability to neutralise CO2 (buffering) falls
[3] b: Surface pH **8.2 (pre-industrial) → ~8.1**: a fall of 0.1 = about **26-30% rise in acidity**; the ocean is **still alkaline**.
[4] h: Factors and results
[5] table: Factor | Result
  [5.0] CO2 uptake by oceans | **Steady decline in ocean pH**
  [5.1] Melting of Arctic ice | Exposes water to air → **more surface CO2**, faster acidification
  [5.2] Plankton blooms (eutrophication) | Bloom collapse → **bacterial respiration** adds CO2
  [5.3] Decreased carbonate ion concentration | **Lower carbonate** → less ability to neutralise CO2
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] Worst-hit waters | **Cold, temperate and Arctic waters** – CO2 is more soluble in cold water
  [6.1] **Cement** sector | About 5-8% of global anthropogenic CO2
[7] x: pH falls only 0.1, yet acidity rises ~30% (log scale); the ocean is still alkaline.

### part 2: Principles, Nature and Scope
[0] table: Principle | Meaning
  [0.0] **Intergenerational equity** | Present must not deplete (natural) resources at the cost of future generations – "inter-generational sensibility" in the use of **natural resources**

  [0.2] **Precautionary principle** | Lack of full scientific certainty is no reason to postpone measures against harm

  [0.4] CBDR | Shared duty; developed nations carry more
  [0.5] Integration | Environment and economy are integrated, not opposites
[1] b: Over-exploitation of resources, growth at the cost of environment and more pollution for industry are against sustainable development.
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **NCERT principles** | Respect all life, improve quality of life, **conserve the earth's vitality and diversity**, **minimise depletion of natural resources**, change attitudes, let communities care for their own environment
  [2.1] **Measures** | Renewables, recycling, lower and more efficient energy use; all pollution minimised; consumption must **not exceed** the rate of regeneration
  [2.2] **Challenges** | **Over-consumption**; **increased use of fossil fuels** (a negative impact of technology)
  [2.3] **Education** | Helps by **promoting environmental awareness**; communication for SD aims at **transformative learning**
  [2.4] Decision-making | **Environmental sustainability needs participatory, open and democratic decision-making**
  [2.5] **Environmental protection** | Maintains (and restores) a healthy natural environment; **is not a luxury** at today's level of development
  [2.6] Capital | SD includes a **constant or rising stock of capital assets**; natural **climatic change over time** is outside the concept
  [2.7] **Scope includes** | Meeting present needs, **equity within and across generations**, inclusion ("leave no one behind")
  [2.8] Scope **excludes** | Growth that runs down natural capital; treating environment and development as opposites
  [2.9] **Principle stressed by international conventions** | **Reducing greenhouse-gas emissions** for environmental sustainability

### part 4: Antibiotics and Antimicrobial Resistance
[0] h: Antibiotics
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Fleming** | **Penicillin, 1928** – from the **fungus** *Penicillium notatum*
  [1.1] **Waksman** | Coined "antibiotic"; found **streptomycin** (first anti-TB drug)
  [1.2] Origin | Most antibiotics are **natural or semi-synthetic**, mostly from bacteria (*Streptomyces*); sulfa drugs synthetic
  [1.3] **Prontosil** | Discovered **1932** (Bayer; Domagk), published 1935 – first sulpha drug, a **pro-drug** → sulfanilamide
  [1.4] **Sulpha drugs** | Sulfanilamide, **Cibazole (sulfathiazole)**, sulfadiazine, sulphaguanidine – synthetic antibacterials
  [1.5] **Chloramphenicol (chloromycetin)** | **Broad-spectrum** antibiotic – classic **typhoid** drug
  [1.6] **Rifampicin** | Treats **both TB and leprosy** (leprosy MDT = rifampicin + dapsone + clofazimine); PAS and isoniazid are TB-only
  [1.7] **Griseofulvin** | Treats ringworm
  [1.8] Other | **Acetobacter** → vinegar; chloroquine = antimalarial
  [1.9] Scope | **Antibiotics act only on bacteria** – viruses lack the cell wall and metabolic pathways they target
  [1.10] **Antipyretic** | Reduces **fever** (paracetamol, aspirin); analgesic = pain relief
[2] h: How AMR builds
[3] b: Causes: incorrect dose, **incomplete course** (resistant survivors left to multiply), overuse, **self-medication**, livestock use, **mutation**, horizontal gene transfer.

[5] b: **Biofilm:** microbes in a self-made **EPS (extracellular polymeric substance)** matrix on **implants, catheters, tissues, food surfaces**; blocks antibiotics → chronic infection; food spoilage and contamination.
