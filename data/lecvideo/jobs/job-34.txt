### part 2: Article 14 – Equality Before Law, Equal Protection, Reasonable Classification
[0] h: Two faces of Article 14
[1] table: Equality before law | Equal protection of the laws
  [1.0] **British** origin (Dicey) | **American** origin (US 14th Amendment)
  [1.1] **Negative** concept: **absence of special privileges**; no one above law | **Positive** concept: like treated alike in equal circumstances
  [1.2] All equally subject to ordinary law and ordinary courts | Permits **reasonable classification**
[2] b: Art. 14 contains **both** concepts. An implication of equality is the **absence of privileges**.
[3] b: "Equals must be treated equally, unequals differently": the State **can treat unequals differently** to create a level playing field, because **among equals the law should be equal and equally administered**.
[4] h: Reasonable classification and arbitrariness
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] Art. 14 | **Forbids class legislation**; permits **reasonable classification**
  [5.1] **Twin test** | **Intelligible differentia** + **rational nexus** with the law's object
  [5.2] **E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu (1974)** | "Equality and arbitrariness are **sworn enemies**" – equality is **antithetic to arbitrariness**
  [5.3] **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** | Any arbitrary State action breaks Art. 14
  [5.4] Executive discretion | A law giving the executive **unguided and uncontrolled discretionary power** violates **Article 14**
  [5.5] Effect on Parliament | Article 14 puts a **limitation on legislative power**
  [5.6] **11 Apr 2026** | SC: the State cannot treat **pensioners** differently from serving employees on **dearness allowance** without valid classification (Kerala/KSRTC)
  [5.7] **Feb 2026** | SC questioned the caste-discrimination clause of the **UGC equity regulations** (**Mritunjay Tiwari v. Union of India**)
[6] h: Exceptions – equality is not absolute
[7] table: Provision | Immunity / special status
  [7.0] **Art. 361** | President and Governors not answerable to courts; **no criminal case** during the term; civil case only after **two months'** notice
  [7.1] **Art. 361A** (44th, 1978) | No liability for a true, non-malicious report of proceedings of Parliament/State legislatures
  [7.2] **Arts. 105 / 194** | MPs / MLAs: immunity for anything said or any vote given in the House
  [7.3] International law | **Foreign sovereigns, ambassadors**, diplomats and UN agencies immune from civil and criminal proceedings
[8] b: **Arts. 194 and 361A** show that equality in India is **not absolute**; in India equality is guaranteed by the **Constitution** (Dicey's "rights from courts" idea does not fit India).
[9] h: Cases and Articles
[10] table: Case | Article
  [10.0] Maneka Gandhi (1978) | **Art. 21**
  [10.1] Shayara Bano (2017, triple talaq) | **Art. 14**

### part 4: Rule of Law – Dicey
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **A.V. Dicey** | British jurist; *Introduction to the Study of the Law of the Constitution* (**1885**)
  [0.1] Borrowed from | **Britain** (with parliamentary government, single citizenship, cabinet system)
  [0.2] **GoI Act 1935** | Gave the federal scheme, Governor, emergency powers – not the Rule of Law
  [0.3] Basis in India | **Article 14**; a **basic feature** – **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**, **Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)**
  [0.4] Setting | Operates in a **democracy**; the force that **moves the machine of democracy**
  [0.5] Dicey opposed | The French **droit administratif** (officials subject only to special administrative tribunals)
[1] h: Dicey's three elements
[2] table: Element | Key detail
  [2.0] (1) **Absence of arbitrary power** (**limitation of powers**) | Regular law supreme; no punishment except for a distinct breach of law
  [2.1] (2) **Equality before law** | –
  [2.2] (3) **Primacy of individual rights** (**liberty and civil rights**) | Rights flow from judicial decisions
  [2.3] Outside the elements | "**People's responsibility to the Government**"; the State has no inherent unlimited police powers
[3] b: **India accepts (1) and (2) but reverses (3)**: in India the **Constitution is the source of rights**.
[4] b: Rule of Law means every citizen is **equal before law**, gets **equal protection**, suffers **no discrimination** and enjoys **no special privileges**.
[5] table: Rule of Law | Rule by Law
  [5.0] **Government subordinate to law** | Ruler above law; law as a weapon of state control
  [5.1] Laws just, fair, non-arbitrary | Governs citizens only
[6] b: **Rule of Law Index** – released by the **World Justice Project**; measuring since 2008.
[7] x: The Rule of Law is British; the Indian Constitution resembles the **US** in **Fundamental Rights** (Bill of Rights), judicial review and equal protection.

### part 0: The Five Writs – Literal Meaning and Purpose
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **Article 32(2)** | Names **five writs**: **Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto** (an injunction is not one of them)
  [0.1] Issuing courts | Both the **Supreme Court (Art. 32)** and the **High Courts (Art. 226)** issue **all five**
  [0.2] Origin | **Prerogative writs of English common law**; before 1950 only the **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras** High Courts could issue them
[1] table: Writ | Literal meaning | Purpose | Lies against
  [1.0] **Habeas Corpus** | "**To have the body (of)**" / "you may have the body" | Detained person **produced before the court**; **release from illegal detention**; court inquires into the **legality of detention** | Public authorities **and private individuals**
  [1.1] **Mandamus** | "**We command**" (an **order**) | Orders a **public official**, body, inferior court or government to **perform a public duty** it refused or neglected; can also order it **not to enforce an unconstitutional law** | Public officials, statutory bodies, government companies, inferior courts, tribunals, government
  [1.2] **Prohibition** | "**To forbid**" / restrain | Higher court stops a lower court/tribunal **exceeding its jurisdiction** while the case is **pending** | **Judicial and quasi-judicial bodies only**
  [1.3] **Certiorari** | "**To be certified**" / informed | Higher court calls up the **record** of a lower court/tribunal for review; **quashes an order already passed**; also **transfers** a pending case | Judicial, quasi-judicial and (since **1991**) certain administrative bodies
  [1.4] **Quo Warranto** | "**By what authority (or warrant)?**" / "what is your authority" | Court asks a holder to **show his right to a public office**; **ousts an ineligible person / usurper** | Holder of a **substantive public office** created by statute or the Constitution
[2] table: Writ | Key detail
  [2.0] **Habeas Corpus** | The writ of **personal liberty** – the "**bulwark of individual liberty**", the **greatest safeguard of personal liberty**; also produces a person **missing or in custody**; the term belongs to **law**
  [2.1] **Mandamus** | Judiciary **orders the Executive to perform its duty** – "an order to act legally and abstain from an unlawful act"
  [2.2] **Certiorari** | The "**postmortem**" writ – examines the working of **subordinate courts**; sought to get an order **quashed**; grounds: lack/excess of jurisdiction, **error of law on the face of the record**

### part 3: Scope and Limits of Each Writ
[0] h: Habeas Corpus
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Reach | The **only writ that lies against private persons** as well as the State; safeguards the liberty of **all persons** in the territory, citizens or not
  [1.1] Not issued | Where detention is **lawful**, by a **competent court** (judicial custody), for contempt of court/legislature, or outside the court's territory
  [1.2] Pleadings | A petition is **not dismissed for imperfect pleadings**
[2] h: Mandamus
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] Lies against | **Public officials, government, public corporations and government companies** performing public duties, inferior courts and tribunals
  [3.1] Does not lie against | A **private individual or body** (unless entrusted with a **public duty** or **colluding with a public authority**); the **President or Governors** (**Art. 361**); a **contractual** obligation; a **discretionary** duty; non-statutory instructions; a Chief Justice acting judicially
  [3.2] **Law and policy** | Mandamus **cannot compel the Government to make a law or a policy** (e.g. to **appoint a commission to study climate change**), but **enforces a policy once laid down**
  [3.3] Public duties | It lies to compel statutory/public duties (filling seats, cleanliness, cyclone shelters)
[4] h: Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto
[5] table: Writ | Key detail
  [5.0] **Prohibition** | Only against **judicial and quasi-judicial bodies** – not administrative authorities, legislatures, private persons; it goes to the **lower court**
  [5.1] **Certiorari** | Not against **legislative bodies** or **private persons**; since **1991** also against administrative bodies affecting rights
  [5.2] **Quo Warranto** | **Any public-minded person** may seek it (need not be aggrieved); not for a **ministerial or private** office

### part 2: Part IV Basics – Articles 36-51, Welfare State and Aims
[0] h: Where and what
[1] table: Part | Articles
  [1.0] **Part IV** (DPSPs) | **Articles 36 to 51** (16 Articles)
  [1.1] Part I | 1-4
  [1.2] Part II | 5-11
  [1.3] Part III (FRs) | 12-35
  [1.4] Part IVA | Fundamental Duties
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Article 36** | "State" has the **same meaning as in Part III** (Art. 12)
  [2.1] **Aim** | **Social and economic democracy** (political democracy is secured by the FRs) and a **welfare state** – a **secular** welfare state, **not a religious State**, and not a capitalist, police or communist State
  [2.2] **Welfare state** | Its ideal is enshrined in the **DPSPs**; "**The State is everywhere: it leaves hardly a gap**" describes a welfare State; it gives **special care to the welfare of weaker sections**
  [2.3] **Economic justice** | In the **Preamble and the DPSPs**; **economic democracy** is envisaged through the DPSPs
  [2.4] Social justice | Runs through the Preamble, FRs, DPSPs and the special provisions for Scheduled and Tribal Areas
  [2.5] **Economic goals** | Two dominant goals flow from the DPSPs – **increasing national income** and **improving its distribution**
  [2.6] **Nature** | **Positive** and **expansive** (they widen the State's role; FRs are restrictive/negative); their spirit is welfare and **humanism**
### part 3: Article 37 – Non-justiciable, yet Fundamental in Governance
[0] h: The text
[1] b: **Article 37:** the provisions of Part IV "**shall not be enforced by any court**", but the principles are "**fundamental in the governance of the country**" and "it shall be the **duty of the State to apply these principles in making laws**".
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] Justiciability | **Non-justiciable** – not enforceable, no justiciable rights for individuals, **no legal duty** on the State
  [2.1] Sanction | **Moral and political** (public opinion, elections)
  [2.2] Role | **Guide-posts for legislation** and a **positive mandate** to the State – **not limitations** on the legislative or executive function, not prohibitions or "mere moral precepts"
  [2.3] "Fundamental in the governance of the country" | The **DPSPs** – not FRs or FDs
[3] h: FR vs DPSP
[4] table: Basis | Fundamental Rights | Directive Principles
  [4.0] Place | Part III, Arts. 12-35 | Part IV, Arts. 36-51
  [4.1] Courts | **Justiciable** | **Non-justiciable** (Art. 37)
  [4.2] Character | **Negative** – restrain the State | **Positive** – exhort the State to act
  [4.3] Aim | Political democracy | **Social and economic democracy**, welfare state
  [4.4] Sanction | Legal | Moral and political
  [4.5] Conflict | Generally prevail (Champakam Dorairajan 1951) | Subordinate; laws for 39(b), (c) protected by Art. 31C
[5] table: Case | Held
  [5.0] **Minerva Mills (1980)** | **Harmony and balance** between Part III and Part IV is part of the **basic structure**
  [5.1] **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** | Justices Shelat and Grover listed "the **mandate to build a welfare State contained in Part IV**" among the basic features
[6] x: DPSPs are not independently enforceable **because** they serve as guide-posts for legislation; they impose no legal duty on the State and do not aim at making India an advanced capitalist country.
