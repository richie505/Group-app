### part 0: Cotton Textiles – Oldest Industry, Centres, Nicknames and Location
[0] h: History and status
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Oldest industry of India** | **Cotton textiles** (muslin, calico, chintz famous worldwide) – older than the modern jute, tea and sugar mill industries
  [1.1] **First cotton mill** | **1818, Fort Gloster near Kolkata (Calcutta)** – a commercial failure
  [1.2] **First successful mill** | **1854, Mumbai**, by Parsi entrepreneur **C.N. Davar** (local capital); growth surged after the American Civil War (1860s-70s)
  [1.3] End of the 19th century | **Cotton textiles** – the only major industry in Indian hands (jute, mining and tea British-owned; Indian-owned steel only with TISCO, 1907)
  [1.4] **Largest organised modern industry** | Cotton textiles; three tiers – handspun khadi, handloom / powerloom, capital-intensive mill sector (powerlooms now make most cloth)
[2] h: Location and dispersal
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] Beginning | **Black-soil cotton belt of Maharashtra and Gujarat**; **Bombay–Ahmedabad** – the most prominent cotton textile region
  [3.1] **Dispersed** all over India | Raw cotton is a **pure raw material that does not lose weight** – mills followed markets, ports, power and cheap labour (Kanpur, Kolkata, Coimbatore)
  [3.2] **Tamil Nadu – mill-made yarn** | Hydel power, **rich pool of skilled / cheap labour**, local cotton (black cotton soil is not predominant in TN)
  [3.3] **Largest number of mills** | **Tamil Nadu** (most produce yarn; Coimbatore has nearly half); earlier **Maharashtra** (Mumbai the largest centre)
  [3.4] Cotton regions | Mumbai–Pune, Madurai–Coimbatore, Indore–Ujjain (**Dhanbad–Jamshedpur** = coal and steel)
  [3.5] **Raw cotton exports** | Largest buyers – **Bangladesh and China**
[4] table: Nickname | City
  [4.0] **Manchester of India** | **Ahmedabad**
  [4.1] Cottonopolis (largest centre) | Mumbai
  [4.2] Manchester of South India | Coimbatore
  [4.3] Manchester of the North | Kanpur
  [4.4] Pittsburgh of India / Steel City | Jamshedpur
[5] h: Allied centres

[6] table: North (UP, Bihar) | South (Maharashtra, Karnataka, TN, AP)

### part 5: Jute – Hooghly Belt, Retting and Andhra Mills
[0] h: Jute industry
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **First jute mill** | **Rishra, near Kolkata, 1855**
  [1.1] Rank | India's 2nd textile industry after cotton
  [1.2] **Location** | ~**90%** of capacity in a **100 km x 3 km belt along the Hooghly** – most mills in the **Hugli basin**, **West Bengal**
  [1.3] **Why Hooghly** | Raw jute of the delta, **soft / standing water for retting**, NW-1 and **Kolkata port**, coal from Raniganj, cheap labour, capital
  [1.4] **Retting** | Soaking jute stems in water so microbes loosen the fibre – separates fibre from the stem
  [1.5] **Mills** | WB 71 of 97 (2026); **AP 2nd** – Chittivalasa, Nellimarla
  [1.6] **Bhajrang Jute Mill, Guntur** | Shut down after a long labour / management dispute
  [1.7] **Bhatpara** | Jute centre (Titagarh = paper)
  [1.8] Policy | **National Jute Policy 2005**; **JUTE-ICARE** – certified seed + microbial retting with **SONA**
  [1.9] **Largest exporter** | Bangladesh
  [1.10] **Jute Packaging Materials Act 1987** | 100% foodgrains, 20% sugar in jute bags
