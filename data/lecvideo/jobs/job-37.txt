## topic group2 2-96 | Veto Powers, Ordinance-making Power (art. 123) and Pardoning Power (art. 72)
### part 0: Pardoning Power – Article 72: Forms and Nature
[0] b: **Art. 72:** the President may "grant **pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions** of punishment or **suspend, remit or commute** the sentence of any person convicted of any offence".
[1] h: Forms
[2] table: Form | Meaning
  [2.0] **Pardon** | Removes **both sentence and conviction** – as if the offence was never committed
  [2.1] **Commutation** | Substitutes a **lighter form** of punishment (death → rigorous life imprisonment)
  [2.2] **Remission** | **Reduces the period** without changing its character (2 years RI → 1 year RI)
  [2.3] **Respite** | A **lesser sentence** for special reasons – pregnancy, physical or mental disability
  [2.4] **Reprieve** | **Temporary stay of execution** (esp. death) to allow time to seek a pardon
[3] b: Commutation changes the **form**; remission reduces the **duration**.
[4] h: Scope and nature
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] Covers | (a) sentence by a **court-martial**; (b) offence against a **Union law**; (c) every **death sentence**; amnesty is outside Art. 72
  [5.1] Nature | Listed among the President's **judicial powers**; strictly an **executive power, independent of the judiciary** – he is not a court of appeal
  [5.2] Advice | Exercised on the **aid and advice of the Council of Ministers** (**Maru Ram, 1980**) – he **cannot** act without the Central Government's advice
  [5.3] Review | **Limited judicial review** (mala fide, arbitrary, irrelevant grounds)

### part 2: Ordinance-making Power – Article 123: Basis, Nature, Scope
[0] h: Basis and nature
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Art. 123** (Part V) | The **President** promulgates ordinances during the **recess of Parliament**; Governor: **Art. 213** (Part VI)
  [1.1] Nature | The **most important legislative power of the President** – a **legislative power of the Executive** (President is part of Parliament, Art. 79), distinct from an executive order; pardon is a judicial power; **adjourning** a sitting is the presiding officer's job
  [1.2] When | Only when **both Houses, or either House, are not in session** and he is **satisfied** that circumstances need **immediate action**; on the **Council of Ministers' advice**
  [1.3] Force | The **same force and effect as an Act of Parliament** and the same limits; **co-extensive** with Parliament's legislative power, though not a parallel power
  [1.4] Origin | A **relic of the Government of India Act, 1935** (Governor-General's ordinance power)
[2] h: Scope
[3] table: Can | Cannot
  [3.0] **Amend or repeal any Central Act** (or an earlier ordinance) | **Amend the Constitution** (Art. 368)
  [3.1] **Operate retrospectively** (from a back date) | **Abridge Fundamental Rights** – it is "law" under **Art. 13(2)**
  [3.2] Introduce or change tax provisions | Go beyond Parliament's legislative competence
### part 3: Ordinance – Life, Lapse, Withdrawal and Judicial Review
[0] h: Life of an ordinance
[1] b: 1. Promulgated during recess. 2. **Laid before both Houses** on reassembly. 3. **Ceases six weeks after reassembly** unless approved – if the Houses reassemble on **different dates**, the six weeks run from the **later date**. 4. Ends **earlier** if **both Houses pass resolutions disapproving** it within those six weeks, or if the President **withdraws** it.
[2] b: The President **can withdraw an ordinance at any time** (Art. 123(2)).
[3] table: Item | Key detail
  [3.0] Lapse | 6 weeks after reassembly
  [3.1] Maximum gap between sessions | 6 months (**Art. 85**; States **Art. 174**)
  [3.2] Maximum life | **6 months + 6 weeks**
  [3.3] Linked limit – **Art. 101(4)** | MP's unexcused absence for **60 days** vacates the seat (prorogation and adjournments of more than four consecutive days excluded)
[4] h: Satisfaction and judicial review
[5] table: Year | Event | Effect
  [5.0] 1975 | **38th Amendment** | Satisfaction under Arts. 123, 213, 352 made **final**
  [5.1] 1978 | **44th Amendment** | **Reversed** the 38th – satisfaction justiciable
  [5.2] **1987** | **D.C. Wadhwa v State of Bihar** | Routine **re-promulgation** without placing before the legislature = **fraud on the Constitution**
  [5.3] **2017** | **Krishna Kumar Singh v State of Bihar** | Seven judges; re-promulgation may be a fraud on the Constitution; **laying is mandatory**
[6] b: Ordinances are **subject to judicial review**; criticism – they **bypass parliamentary deliberation**.
[7] h: In the news
[8] table: Ordinance | Key detail
  [8.0] **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026** | Judges 33 → 37 besides the CJI; revived the debate on **ordinance misuse and executive overreach**
  [8.1] AP | A Governor's ordinance (**Art. 213**) on **organ-transplant committees**
  [8.2] **GNCTD (Amendment) Ordinance, 19 May 2023** | Nullified the Supreme Court's **11 May 2023** ruling that gave Delhi's elected government control over services → **GNCTD (Amendment) Act, 2023**
### part 4: President (Art. 72) vs Governor (Art. 161) and Clemency Cases
[0] b: **Both the President (Art. 72) and the Governor (Art. 161)** have pardoning power.
[1] table: Point | President (Art. 72) | Governor (Art. 161)
  [1.0] Offences | Against **Union laws** | Against **State laws**
  [1.1] **Death sentence** | **Can pardon** | **Cannot pardon**; can suspend, remit or commute
  [1.2] Court-martial | **Yes** | **No**
[2] b: The Governor is part of the State Legislature, does not appoint HC judges and has no emergency powers.
[3] h: Clemency cases
[4] table: Case | Holding
  [4.0] **Maru Ram (1980)** | Power exercised on the **Council of Ministers' advice**
  [4.1] **Kehar Singh (decided 1988, reported AIR 1989)** | President may examine the evidence afresh; no right of oral hearing
  [4.2] **Epuru Sudhakar v Govt of AP (2006)** | **Limited judicial review** – mala fide, arbitrary or irrelevant grounds
### part 5: Veto Power of the President – Types and Article 111
[0] h: Article 111
[1] b: **Article 111:** a Bill passed by both Houses is presented to the President, who **declares assent**, **withholds assent** or (for a **non-Money Bill**) **returns** it for reconsideration.
[2] b: The Indian President has three of the four kinds of veto: **absolute, suspensive and pocket**; **no qualified veto**.
[3] table: Veto | Meaning | Applies to
  [3.0] **Absolute** | Withholds assent; the Bill ends and never becomes an Act | **Private members' Bills**; **Government Bills when the Cabinet resigns** before assent and the new Cabinet advises against it
  [3.1] **Suspensive** | Returns the Bill **once**; if re-passed (with or without amendment) he **must assent** | **Ordinary Bills** (and Financial Bills) – **not Money Bills**
  [3.2] **Pocket** | Neither assents, rejects nor returns – keeps the Bill pending **indefinitely** | Possible because **Art. 111 prescribes no time limit**
  [3.3] **Qualified** | Can be overridden only by a **higher (two-thirds) majority** | **USA**
[4] h: Veto by type of Bill
[5] table: Bill | President (Union) | President (State Bill reserved, Art. 201)
  [5.0] Ordinary | Assent, withhold or **return once** – must assent if re-passed | Assent, withhold or direct return – **not bound** to assent even if re-passed
  [5.1] **Money** | Assent or withhold; **cannot return** (usually assents – introduced on his prior recommendation) | Assent or **withhold** – he **can veto a State Money Bill**
  [5.2] **Constitutional Amendment** | **Must assent** (**24th Amendment, 1971**); cannot reject or return | Cannot be introduced in a State Legislature
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] Return power | Applies to **Financial Bills of Categories I and II**; a Money Bill and a Constitutional Amendment Bill cannot be returned
  [6.1] Purpose of the veto | To **prevent hasty, ill-considered or unconstitutional legislation**
  [6.2] Pocket veto | The President can keep a Bill pending **indefinitely**; the American President has a qualified veto
[7] table: Feature | India | USA
  [7.0] Absolute veto | **Yes** | No
  [7.1] Qualified veto | No | **Yes** – Congress overrides by **two-thirds**
  [7.2] Pocket veto | Yes – **no time limit** | Yes – President must act within **10 days** (the **10-day rule is American**)
### part 6: State Bills Reserved for the President – Articles 200 and 201
[0] h: Articles 200 and 201
[1] table: Article | Provision
  [1.0] **Art. 200** | Governor may **assent, withhold, return** (non-Money Bill) or **reserve** a Bill for the President; the **Governor has no pocket veto**
  [1.1] **Art. 201** | For a reserved Bill the President **declares or withholds assent**, or directs its return (non-Money Bill); no time limit written in Arts. 200 or 201
[2] h: Cases
[3] table: Date | Case | Holding
  [3.0] **8 April 2025** | **State of Tamil Nadu v Governor** | Indefinite inaction under **Art. 200** **justiciable**; **ten** TN Bills deemed assented
  [3.1] **Nov 2025** | **Presidential reference (Art. 143, five judges)** | Courts **cannot fix timelines** for the Governor or President; **no deemed assent**; the powers cannot be used arbitrarily

## topic group2 2-97 | Cabinet System, Cabinet Committees, Collective and Individual Responsibility; Attorney General of India
### part 0: Attorney General of India – Article 76 and Article 88
[0] h: Attorney General – Article 76
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Office | **Attorney General (AG) for India** – the **highest (first) law officer** of the country; appointed by the **President**
  [1.1] **Qualification** | **Qualified to be appointed a Judge of the Supreme Court** – a citizen who has been a High Court judge for 5 years, a High Court advocate for 10 years, or an eminent jurist in the President's opinion
  [1.2] **Term** | **Not fixed** by the Constitution; **during the pleasure of the President**; no procedure or grounds of removal, so he is **not removed like a Supreme Court judge**; by convention he **resigns when the government changes**
  [1.3] **Remuneration** | As the **President may determine** (Art. 76(4))
  [1.4] **Duties** (Art. 76(2)) | **Advise the Government of India on legal matters**; perform duties of a legal character **referred or assigned by the President**; appears for the Government in the Supreme Court – its **chief legal adviser and primary lawyer**
  [1.5] **Right of audience** (Art. 76(3)) | In **all courts in the territory of India**
  [1.6] **Article 88** | Though **not a member**, may **speak and take part** in the proceedings of **either House**, a **joint sitting** and any **committee** of which he is named a member – **without a right to vote**
  [1.7] **Article 105(4)** | Enjoys **all the privileges and immunities of an MP**
  [1.8] **Limits** | Outside the Cabinet; not a full-time government servant – **private practice allowed**, but he cannot advise or brief **against the Government of India**, defend accused persons in criminal cases without permission, or become a company director without permission
[2] h: Law officers compared
[3] table: Item | Attorney General | Solicitor General | Advocate General (State)
  [3.0] Basis | **Art. 76** | **Not constitutional** (statutory / Law Officers Rules) | **Art. 165**
  [3.1] Appointed by | **President** | **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet** | **Governor**
  [3.2] Tenure | President's pleasure | Fixed at appointment (usually 3 years) | Governor's pleasure
  [3.3] Rights in the House | **Art. 88** (no vote) | None | **Art. 177** (no vote)
  [3.4] Rank | **First law officer** | **Second law officer** – assists the AG | State's legal adviser
  [3.5] First holder | **M.C. Setalvad** | **C.K. Daphtary** | –
[4] b: **R. Venkataramani** – AG since **1 October 2022** (after K.K. Venugopal); re-appointed for **two years from 1 October 2025** (as of 2026).
### part 1: Collective Responsibility – Article 75(3) and the Parliamentary Principle
[0] h: Article 75(3)
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Article 75(3)** | The Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha**; States: **Art. 164(2)** (to the Legislative Assembly)
  [1.1] **Collective responsibility** | The feature that marks India as a **parliamentary (cabinet / responsible / Westminster) system**
  [1.2] Principle of Cabinet government | **A mechanism of parliamentary democracy for ensuring collective responsibility of the Government to the people**
  [1.3] **Oath of secrecy** | Third Schedule, administered by the **President** – keeps Cabinet deliberations confidential and the ministry united in public
  [1.4] Why India chose it | Experience of the **GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935**; to avoid a personality cult
[2] b: **Features of the parliamentary system:** **nominal and real executives**, **majority party rule**, **collective responsibility**, **double membership** (ministers are MPs), **PM's leadership**, **dissolution of the Lok Sabha**. Parliament as a whole is never elected every 5 years: the **Rajya Sabha is permanent** (one-third retire every 2 years).
[3] h: Collective responsibility in action
[4] table: Event | Consequence
  [4.0] **No-confidence motion** passed (moved **only in the Lok Sabha**) | Whole Council, incl. RS ministers, resigns – "**sink or swim together**"
  [4.1] **Union Budget / Money Bill defeated in the Lok Sabha** | Loss of confidence – the **PM submits the resignation of the Council of Ministers**
  [4.2] **Government defeated in the Rajya Sabha** | **None** – no resignation, no dissolution, no President's Rule
  [4.3] **PM dies or resigns** | Council of Ministers dissolves
  [4.4] A minister disagrees with a Cabinet decision | Must **accept and defend it or resign**
[5] b: Ministers make policy and civil servants adhere to it; this rests on ministerial direction, while collective responsibility binds ministers to the Lok Sabha.

## topic group2 2-98 | Governor – Appointment, Powers, Discretion, Article 200/201 and Article 356
### part 0: Office of the Governor – Articles 153-161, Term, Removal, Immunity
[0] h: Articles on the office
[1] table: Article | Subject
  [1.0] **153** | **A Governor for each State**; proviso added by the **7th Amendment, 1956** – the **same person** may be Governor of **two or more States**
  [1.1] **154** | **Executive power** of the State vested in the Governor, exercised directly or through subordinate officers
  [1.2] **155** | **Appointed by the President** by warrant under his hand and seal – neither directly nor indirectly elected
  [1.3] **156** | **Term** – holds office **during the pleasure of the President**; resigns to the President; normal term **5 years** (156(3))
  [1.4] **157** | **Qualifications** – **citizen of India** (not necessarily by birth) and **completed 35 years**
  [1.5] **158** | **Conditions** – not a member of Parliament or a State Legislature (seat vacated on appointment), no **office of profit**; emoluments fixed by **Parliament by law**, not reduced during the term
  [1.6] **159** | **Oath** before the **Chief Justice of the High Court** (in his absence, the senior-most judge)
  [1.7] **160** | President provides for the Governor's functions in an unforeseen contingency
  [1.8] **161** | Pardoning power (offences against State laws)
  [1.9] **Article 168** | Governor is an **integral part of the State Legislature**, though not a member
  [1.10] 202(3), 158(3A) | Emoluments **charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State**; a Governor of two or more States has them shared in the **proportion the President fixes**
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] Position | **Nominal / constitutional head** of the State and its **executive head**; the **Chief Minister and Council of Ministers** are the **real executive**
  [2.1] **Conventions** | Usually an **outsider** to the State; the State **CM is consulted** (not compulsory)
[3] h: Term and removal
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] Term | **5 years**, but he **continues until his successor enters office** (Art. 156(3))
  [4.1] Security of term | **Not secure**: serves at the President's pleasure (= Union Council of Ministers); can be **removed or transferred at any time**, and reappointed
  [4.2] **Reappointment** | No limit in the Constitution – **any number of terms**
  [4.3] Removal | **No procedure or grounds for removal** in the Constitution; **not impeached**; **the State Legislature cannot remove** him (unlike the President, Election Commissioner and HC judges, removed through Parliament)
  [4.4] Responsibility | **Responsible to the President**; **not answerable** to either House of Parliament, either House of the State Legislature or the State Council of Ministers
  [4.5] **B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010)** | Pleasure doctrine **not unfettered** – removal must not be **arbitrary** or merely because the **Government at the Centre changed**; limited judicial review
  [4.6] **Hargovind Pant v. Raghukul Tilak (1979)** | Office of Governor is an **independent constitutional office**, neither subordinate to nor under the control of the **Union Government**
[5] h: Immunity – Article 361
[6] table: Clause | Immunity
  [6.0] **361(1)** | Not answerable to **any court** for acts done in the exercise of his powers and duties
  [6.1] **361(2)** | **No criminal proceedings** instituted or continued during the term – not even with a court's permission
  [6.2] **361(3)** | No arrest or imprisonment
  [6.3] **361(4)** | **Civil proceedings** for **personal acts** only after **two months' written notice**
  [6.4] **Art. 194(2)** | Members of a State Legislature immune for anything said or any vote in the House
[7] x: **No procedure has been laid down in the Constitution for the removal of a Governor** – he holds office at the President's pleasure.

## topic group2 2-102 | Parliamentary Form of Government – Features, Merits, Demerits; Comparison with Presidential System
### part 0: Features – Nominal Executive, Collective Responsibility, Double Membership
[0] h: Features of the parliamentary system
[1] table: Feature | Key detail
  [1.0] Nominal and real executive | **President** = de jure head (Head of State), like the **British Monarch**; **PM + Council of Ministers** = real (de facto) executive
  [1.1] Aid and advice | **Art. 74(1)**: advice **binding** on the President
  [1.2] Majority party rule | President appoints (**Art. 75(1)**) the **leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha**, or of the **alliance** with a majority there, as PM – a convention
  [1.3] **Collective responsibility** | **Art. 75(3)**: to the **Lok Sabha only**; ministers hold office together while they command a majority – “swim and sink together”
  [1.4] Individual responsibility | **Art. 75(2)**: ministers hold office during the **pleasure of the President** – individually responsible to the **President** (dismissed on the PM's advice)
  [1.5] Double membership | Ministers are MPs of **either House**; a **non-member** must enter Parliament within **six months** (Art. 75(5)), else ceases to be a minister
  [1.6] Leadership of the PM | Heads the Council of Ministers; **Leader of the Lok Sabha** (if a member) and chief spokesperson
  [1.7] Dissolution | Lok Sabha can be dissolved early on the PM's advice
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Not in the Constitution** | “PM is leader of the majority party” (convention); the **ranks** of ministers (Cabinet, State, Deputy)
  [2.1] Cabinet | Headed by the PM, not the Head of State; the President is not the real head of the executive
  [2.2] **Cabinet membership** | **Not restricted** to Lok Sabha members
### part 1: Parliamentary System – Meaning and Why India Is Parliamentary
[0] h: Meaning
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Names** | **Cabinet government**, **responsible government**, Westminster model
  [1.1] Essence | The executive stays in power only while it has a **majority (confidence) in the popular House**
  [1.2] **Defining test** | India is parliamentary because the **Council of Ministers is responsible to the Lok Sabha** (Art. 75(3); States Art. 164(2))
  [1.3] **Parliamentary executive** | An executive **dependent on the support of a majority in Parliament**
  [1.4] **Main principle** | **Responsibility of the executive to the legislature**; the legislature controls the executive
  [1.5] **Removal** | The government is **responsible to Parliament and can be removed by it** (no-confidence)
  [1.6] **Basis** | **Fusion of powers** – co-operation of legislature and executive (presidential = separation of powers; independent legislature and executive)
  [1.7] **Source of authority** | The Constitution sets up the parliamentary system at the **Centre and in the States**; both draw authority from the **Constitution**
[2] b: India = **secular, federal, parliamentary** – parliamentary, cabinet and responsible government; ministers are amateurs, not experts.
[3] b: The President **represents the nation but does not rule** it; the executive is accountable to the legislature.
### part 2: Presidential System – Features and Contrast
[0] h: Presidential system
[1] table: Feature | Key detail
  [1.0] Executive | President = **Head of State and Head of Government** – **single executive**; all executive power vested in him
  [1.1] Election and term | Directly **elected** for a **fixed term** (US: electoral college, 4 years); removable only by **impeachment**
  [1.2] Legislature | President is **not accountable** to it and **cannot dissolve** it
  [1.3] Ministers | **Appoints his own Council of Ministers** (secretaries) – not legislators, responsible only to him
  [1.4] **Principle** | **Separation of powers** with **checks and balances** – veto, Senate confirmation, impeachment
  [1.5] Other terms | **Fusion of powers** = parliamentary; **division of powers** = federalism
  [1.6] **Origin** | First formed in the **USA** (oldest and purest form); India, Canada and Britain are parliamentary
[2] h: Parliamentary vs presidential
[3] table: Parliamentary | Presidential
  [3.0] Nominal head + real head (PM) – dual executive | One person is both heads – single executive
  [3.1] Collective executive (cabinet); ministers are legislators | Secretaries outside the legislature
  [3.2] Fusion of powers | Separation of powers
  [3.3] Removable by no-confidence | **Fixed term**; removable only by impeachment
  [3.4] Lower House can be dissolved early | No dissolution
[4] table: System | Examples
  [4.0] **Semi-presidential** – directly elected President with real powers + **PM responsible to the legislature** | **France (Fifth Republic, 1958), Russia, Sri Lanka (1978 Constitution)**; **not India** (President indirectly elected, nominal)
  [4.1] **Parliamentary** | UK, Japan, Canada, India

### part 4: Merits, Demerits and the Constituent Assembly's Choice
[0] h: Merits and demerits

### part 3: Judicial Review – Meaning, Origin, Basis
[0] h: Meaning and origin
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Meaning** | Power of the **SC and HCs** to pronounce upon the **constitutionality of laws and executive orders** (Centre and States) and declare them **void** – hence the SC can declare illegal any law of the State
  [1.1] Scope | Examines the **constitutional validity of laws** – not their **wisdom**; not pre-assent review, Art. 137 review of its own judgments or appeals
  [1.2] Courts | The **Supreme Court as well as the High Courts** (Arts **32** and **226**)
  [1.3] Term | **Not used in the Constitution**; part of the **basic structure** (**Kesavananda 1973**; **L. Chandra Kumar 1997**)
  [1.4] **Origin USA** | **Marbury v. Madison (1803)**, CJ **John Marshall**; India borrowed it from the **USA**
  [1.5] **UK** | Parliamentary sovereignty – courts cannot void Acts
  [1.6] **Indian vs British parliamentary system** | The difference is **judicial review**; both have real and nominal executives, collective responsibility, bicameral legislatures
[2] h: Role and basis
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] Checks | **Legislative excesses** of Parliament and State Assemblies; **restricts the sovereignty of Parliament** – laws cannot break the Basic Structure
  [3.1] Balance | India = **balance of parliamentary sovereignty (British) and judicial supremacy (American)**
  [3.2] **Custodian / guardian of Fundamental Rights** | The **Supreme Court** (Art. 32), with the **High Courts** (Art. 226)
  [3.3] Basis | Written Constitution + **federalism** + FRs; **symbol of federal government** – courts as umpire of the Centre-State division
  [3.4] **Theory** | **Supremacy of the Constitution** / limited government
  [3.5] **Grounds** | Violation of FRs; lack of **legislative competence**; repugnance to the Constitution / Basic Structure
  [3.6] **Shankari Prasad (1951)** | FRs first held amendable
[4] h: Constitutional basis

  [5.0] **13(2)** | Laws abridging FRs void – primary textual basis (incl. pre-Constitution laws)
