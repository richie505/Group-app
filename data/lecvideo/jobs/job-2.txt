## topic group2 1-11 | Aryan Question – Original Home Theories and Archaeological Correlates
### part 0: Meaning of 'Arya' – A Speech Group, Not a Race
[0] h: Meaning of 'Arya'
[1] b: **'Aryan' = a speech (linguistic) group:** speakers of Indo-Aryan, a branch of the **Indo-European** family – a language group, not an ethnic group, a nomadic people or a "superior race".
[2] b: **'Arya' in classical Sanskrit:** a **noble man**; originally associated with "**high birth**".
[3] table: Scholar | Contribution
  [3.0] **Max Muller** | Popularised the term in modern scholarship; the first European to speak of an "**Aryan race**", though he later said it meant only a language group
  [3.1] **William Jones** (18th c.) | Found the Sanskrit–Latin–Greek kinship that led to the idea of the Indo-European family
[4] b: **Languages in India:** Indo-European (Aryan) about **73%** of speakers; the **Dardic** branch – Jammu and Kashmir.
### part 1: Avesta and Boghazkoi – Linguistic and Epigraphic Evidence
[0] h: Avesta
[1] b: **Avesta:** the **oldest Iranian** (Zoroastrian) text, in **Avestan, an old Iranian language**; closely similar to the Rig Veda → relates to **Iran**.
[2] h: Boghazkoi inscription
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] Place | **Asia Minor (Turkey)** – the Hittite capital
  [3.1] Date | c. **1380 BCE** (14th century BCE)
  [3.2] Content | Treaty between a **Hittite** king and a **Mitanni** king
  [3.3] Gods named | **Indra, Mitra, Varuna, Nasatyas** (Agni is absent)
  [3.4] Significance | Vedic-speaking people or gods in **West Asia**; taken as evidence of Aryan movement via Iran
[4] b: Mansehra and Shahbazgarhi are Ashokan rock edicts and Junagarh is Rudradaman's; none of them names Vedic gods.
[5] b: **Other early Indo-European records:** Kassite (Iraq, c. 1600 BCE), Mitanni (Syria, 14th c. BCE).

### part 4: Migration Debate – Archaeology and Genetics
[0] h: Migration debate

[0] h: Royal sacrifices

### part 2: Assemblies and Taxes
[0] h: Assemblies
[1] b: The **Vidatha disappeared**; the **Sabha and Samiti continued** but declined, dominated by chiefs, nobles and Brahmanas; **women no longer sat** in the Sabha; village assemblies tried local cases.
[2] h: Taxes

  [3.0] **Bali** | Tribute: voluntary in the Rigveda, now **regular and compulsory** – the king's tax on the people

  [3.2] **Shulka** | **Customs / tolls**

[5] b: Varman = armour and Vidatha = assembly – neither is a tax.
### part 3: From Tribe to Territory – Kingship
[0] h: From tribe to territory
[1] b: **Jana** (tribe) → **janapada / rashtra** (territory); the word **rashtra** first appears in the Later Vedic texts; tribal chiefdom → **hereditary territorial monarchy**, which prepared the way for the **Mahajanapadas** (6th century BCE).
[2] b: Kingship was **hereditary**, usually to the **eldest son** (primogeniture); men became rajas by performing big sacrifices; traces of election survived. Kingship was not traced to election by village assemblies.
[3] b: **Ekarat** (Atharvaveda, Aitareya Brahmana) = the **sole / paramount ruler**.
[4] table: Region (Aitareya Brahmana) | Ruler's title

  [4.1] South | **Bhoja**
  [4.2] West | **Svarat**
  [4.3] North | **Virat**
[5] b: State forms in the Aitareya Brahmana: **Rajya, Bhojya, Swarajya, Vairajya, Samrajya**; **Ganatantra is not** one of them.
[6] h: Divine origin of kingship
[7] table: Text | Statement
  [7.0] **Aitareya Brahmana** | Earliest account: the gods, losing to the Asuras, chose a king to lead them; king = **protector of Brahmins**

  [7.2] **Shatapatha Brahmana** | Linked the king with **Prajapati**; the Rajasuya and Vajapeya gave him divine qualities
[8] table: Early Vedic | Later Vedic
  [8.0] Tribe; cattle; elective chief | Territory; agriculture; hereditary king
  [8.1] Voluntary bali; strong assemblies | Compulsory taxes (bali, bhaga, shulka); assemblies decline; **Ratnins**
[9] x: East = Samrat, North = Virat; the word rashtra first appears now.

[0] h: Ratnins

## topic group2 1-17 | Later Vedic Society – Varnas, Ashramas, Gotra, Status of Women
### part 0: Varna Order, Untouchability and Guilds
[0] h: Varna order
[1] b: Society was stratified in the order **Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra** = the **Varna system** (varna came to rest on **birth**; the spread of jatis is post-Vedic).
[2] table: Varna | Role
  [2.0] **Brahmana** | Priests, teachers; **most powerful**
  [2.1] Kshatriya (**Rajanya**) | Rulers, warriors
  [2.2] **Vaishya** | Farmers, herders, traders, artisans; the only tribute-payers
  [2.3] Shudra | Service; no upanayana, no Vedas, no Sabha
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] First statement of the four varnas | **Purusha Sukta, Rigveda 10th Mandala**
  [3.1] **Dvija** (twice-born) | The upper three varnas, entitled to upanayana
  [3.2] **Aitareya Brahmana** | Shudra = "**servant of another**", who could be expelled or beaten at will
  [3.3] Outside the varna order | **Vratyas, Nishadas**
  [3.4] Varna + ashrama | **Varnashrama Dharma**
[4] h: Untouchability
[5] b: The **Chhandogya Upanishad** names the **Chandala** as a person of despised birth – an early sign only; untouchability was absent in the Rigvedic age.
[6] b: Untouchability took **clear, formal shape in the Dharmashastra phase** (early Christian era), not in the Rigvedic or Later Vedic phase.
[7] h: Guilds and money-lending
[8] b: **Sarthavaha** (caravan leader, head of a trade guild / Srenipramukh) = a **Vaishya**; guild rules = **Samay, Srenidharma**; Vaishya guilds (gana) were headed by a **Shreshthin**.
[9] b: The first reference to **money-lending** is in the **Shatapatha Brahmana**; usurer = **kusidin**.

### part 2: Gotra and Kinship
[0] h: Gotra and kinship

[0] h: Samskaras

[0] h: Status of women
