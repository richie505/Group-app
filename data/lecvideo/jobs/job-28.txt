## topic group2 2-9 | Social Stratification and Mobility in India
### part 0: Caste as Stratification – Features, Caste vs Class
[0] h: Features of caste
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Basis | Membership and status fixed by **birth** (ascribed); based on the **Chaturvarna** (four-varna) scheme; **hierarchy** is the essence of caste (Dumont)
  [1.1] **Essential features** | Hierarchy, **endogamy** ("closed marriage"; "intermarriage" in Telugu-English usage = marriage within the caste), restrictions on feeding and social intercourse, hereditary occupation, **caste panchayat**, purity-pollution
  [1.2] Non-essential | **Belief in reincarnation**, "social dynamics"
  [1.3] **Closed system** | A person can **neither rise nor descend** in the hierarchy (A.W. Green: ideally no mobility up or down); caste is a **closed group** that no outsider can join
  [1.4] **Varna** vs **Jati** | Varna = four-fold, theoretical; jati = real, local birth groups; ranking by **ritual purity and pollution**
  [1.5] **Vertical** / **horizontal** | Vertical = ranking by birth; horizontal = division into separate caste groups
  [1.6] Occupation | Caste status linked to **occupation**; hereditary occupations (potter, **blacksmith**, weaver) weakened by education, **industrialisation**, urbanisation, occupational mobility
  [1.7] **Jajmani system** | Barber, washerman etc. are **service castes** working for patron (jajman) families; weakened today
  [1.8] **S.C. Dube** | Ritual purity has two stages – **purification procedures** and **initiation rites**
  [1.9] History | Harappa: citadel vs lower town, **no rigid caste**; varna hardened in the **Later Vedic** period
  [1.10] Today | Inter-caste conflicts are about **power**, not ritual; Muslims: **Ashraf, Ajlaf, Arzal**
[2] h: Caste vs class
[3] table: System | Feature
  [3.0] Caste | **Ascribed** at birth; endogamy, inherited occupation; **closed**; cumulative inequality
  [3.1] Class | **Achieved** – education, wealth, occupation; **open**, mobility possible
  [3.2] Estate | Medieval Europe – clergy, nobility, commoners
  [3.3] Slavery | Person as property – Greece, Rome
[4] b: Both caste and class are forms of stratification and give the individual a status; castes are **innumerable** (only the varnas are four). **Social classes** = people of similar income, occupation, education.
[5] table: Scholar | Key detail
  [5.0] **Andre Beteille** | *Caste, Class and Power* (**1965**, **Sripuram, Thanjavur**) – used **Weber's** three dimensions; caste, class and power **diverge**; also *Studies in Agrarian Social Structure*; **varna is a hierarchy** term, **race a classification** term
  [5.1] **Louis Dumont** | *Homo Hierarchicus*: purity-pollution; the **Brahman is at the top**, the **king second** (status encompasses power)
  [5.2] **Ghurye** | *Caste and Race in India* (1932): hierarchy, endogamy, restrictions

  [0.1] **Bases** | Caste, class, **gender (sex stratification = between genders)**, **age**, race, ethnicity; traditional – caste, varna, tribe, religion; modern – education, occupation, income, **political power**, urbanisation (language is not a basis)

### part 2: Article 17, Untouchability and Its Removal
[0] h: Article 17
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Article 17** | "Untouchability" is **abolished**; its practice **in any form** forbidden; enforcing any disability from it is an **offence punishable in accordance with law**
  [1.1] Place | In the **Right to Equality** group
  [1.2] Adoption | Adopted with cries of "Mahatma Gandhi ki Jai"; **Ambedkar** ensured it became a punishable offence
  [1.3] Definition | **Not defined** in the Constitution or any Act
  [1.4] **Mysore HC** | The practice as historically developed (social disabilities by birth in certain castes); excludes social boycott of a few individuals
  [1.5] **Art. 35(a)(ii)** | **Only Parliament** prescribes punishment for Art. 17 offences
  [1.6] **Horizontal** | Binds **private persons** too (**PUDR v Union of India, 1982**)
  [1.7] **Sabarimala reference (Apr 2026)** | Justice **B.V. Nagarathna**: Art. 17 cannot apply "for three days a month"
  [1.8] Related Articles | Art. 23 = forced labour; Art. 18 = titles
[2] h: Untouchability and its removal
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] Forms | Temple-entry denial, separate wells, two-tumbler system, segregation, forced "polluting" work
  [3.1] Seat reservation | **Protective**, not untouchability
  [3.2] **Structural violence (Johan Galtung)** | Harm built into social structures
  [3.3] **Removal** | By **laws, education and public awareness**
  [3.4] State steps | Fundamental rights, Art. 17, PCR 1955, PoA 1989 (conversion is not a State step)
  [3.5] **PoA Act 1989** | Gives effect to equality before law (14), non-discrimination (15) and Art. 17
  [3.6] "Scheduled Castes" | First used by the **Simon Commission**; GoI Act 1935; first list **1936**; first post-1950 list: **Constitution (SC) Order, 1950**
[4] x: The Untouchability Act (PCR Act) = the **first law** enforcing Art. 17.
