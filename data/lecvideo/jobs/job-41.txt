### part 4: Recognition, Symbols and Legal Framework
[0] h: Registration and recognition
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Registration** | By the **ECI** under **Sec. 29A, RPA 1951** – apply **within 30 days** of formation; pledge allegiance to the Constitution, to **socialism, secularism and democracy**, and to India's sovereignty, unity and integrity
  [1.1] **Recognition** | National/State, under the **Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968** – an ECI order (not a constitutional provision); based on poll performance (not the number of States contested)
  [1.2] **National party** (any one) | 6% votes in 4+ States + 4 Lok Sabha seats; or 2% of Lok Sabha seats from 3 States; or State party in 4 States
  [1.3] **State party** routes | 6% votes + 2 Assembly seats; 6% + 1 Lok Sabha seat; 3% of Assembly seats or 3 seats; 1 Lok Sabha seat per 25; **8% of votes alone**
  [1.4] **National parties (2025)** | **Six** – **BJP, INC, BSP, CPI(M), AAP, NPP** (AAP got status in 2023; CPI, NCP, AITC lost it)
  [1.5] **NPP** | First national party from the **North-East**
[2] h: Symbols and legal framework
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **Symbols** | Recognised parties → **reserved symbols**; RUPPs and independents → **free symbols**
  [3.1] **BSP – elephant** | The elephant is also the US **Republican** Party's symbol
  [3.2] **Bicycle** | TDP, SP, J&K Panthers Party
  [3.3] **RJD** | **Hurricane lantern**
  [3.4] **Split disputes** | Name and symbol decided by the **ECI** (para 15, Symbols Order)
  [3.5] **Parties in the Constitution** | Not in the original text; entered via the **Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985)**; Art. 19(1)(c) protects forming **associations** in general
  [3.6] **INC v Institute of Social Welfare (2002)** | The ECI **cannot ordinarily deregister** a party
  [3.7] **Funding** | Donors above **₹20,000** disclosed yearly; donations above **₹2,000** only by cheque/bank transfer; **ADR v UoI (15 Feb 2024)** struck down electoral bonds

### part 1: Nature of the Indian Party System
[0] h: Types of party system
[1] table: System | Examples
  [1.0] **One-party** | USSR; today China, **Cuba, North Korea, Eritrea**
  [1.1] **Two-party** | USA, Britain
  [1.2] **Multi-party** | France, Switzerland, Italy, **India**; **Chile** is also a multi-party democracy
[2] h: The Indian party system
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **India is multi-party** | Continental size, diverse society (region, language, caste, religion, class), universal adult franchise; the **largest number of parties** in the world; no fixed party system because there are too many parties
  [3.1] Place in the polity | The **party system is a part (sub-system) of the political system**; parties are the life-blood of democracy
  [3.2] **Features** | One-dominant party (Congress) till 1967; lack of clear ideology (except BJP, CPI, CPM); **personality cult**; parties on traditional factors; rise of **regional parties** (they indicate regional aspirations); factions and defections (after 1967); lack of effective opposition
  [3.3] Constitution | The **original Constitution had no provision on parties**; they entered via the **Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985)**
  [3.4] **ECI classes** | National, State, **registered-unrecognised** parties (RUPPs – over 2,800; only ~750 contested in 2024)
  [3.5] **Ruling-party misuse** | Government servants, vehicles and discretionary grants may be misused to woo voters
[4] b: People blame parties for bad governance – this does not explain why parties are the life-blood of democracy.
