### part 2: Meaning and Definitions
[0] h: Indian meaning
[1] b: Communalism is the belief that because a group follows one religion it also has **common social, political and economic interests**.
[2] b: In India it is most often seen as **religious difference** between groups that leads to tension and rioting; a group promotes its own interests **at the expense of others**.
[3] h: Definition → scholar
[4] table: Scholar | Definition / view
  [4.0] Robert Melson and Howard Wolpe | "The political assertiveness of a community to maintain its identity in a plural society undergoing modernisation"
  [4.1] Bipan Chandra | The belief that because a group follows one religion, it also has common social, political and economic interests; gave the **three stages**
  [4.2] Ram Ahuja | An ideology that society is divided into religious communities whose interests differ and often oppose each other; "communal persons" do politics through religion
  [4.3] **T.K. Oommen** | **Six dimensions** of communalism (assimilationist to secessionist)
  [4.4] W.C. Smith (*Modern Islam in India*) | Ideology that treats each religious group as the basic social, political and economic unit and stresses antagonism between such groups
### part 3: Bipan Chandra's Three Stages
[0] h: Bipan Chandra's three stages
[1] table: Stage | Features
  [1.0] 1. Communal nationalism | Followers of one religion share common secular (political, economic) interests; still nationalist in outlook
  [1.1] 2. Liberal communalism | Interests of one community seen as different but reconcilable within one nation
  [1.2] 3. Extreme communalism | Interests seen as mutually antagonistic; politics of fear, hatred and violence
[2] b: After the **1937 elections**, the Muslim League turned to extreme communalism "based on fear, hatred and violence"; echoed by the **Hindu Mahasabha and RSS**.
[3] x: **Order:** communal nationalism → liberal communalism → extreme communalism.
### part 4: Causes of Communalism
[0] h: Causes of communalism
[1] table: Type | Causes
  [1.0] Historical / colonial | British divide and rule; separate electorates; caste and religion recorded in the first census (1871-72); 19th-century revivalist movements
  [1.1] Political | Vote-bank and identity politics; religion-based parties
  [1.2] Economic | Poverty, unemployment, lopsided development, relative deprivation
  [1.3] Legal / administrative | Delayed justice, lenient punishment, police inaction, biased intelligence
  [1.4] Social / media | Fake news and hate messages on social media; media amplifying communal narratives
  [1.5] Psychological / international | Prejudices, rumours; foreign funding
[2] b: **1920s:** Arya Samaj **Shuddhi and Sangathan** vs Muslim **Tabligh and Tanzim**.

### part 6: Forms – Oommen's Six Dimensions
[0] h: Oommen's six dimensions
[1] table: Dimension | Meaning | Example
  [1.0] Assimilationist | Small religious groups absorbed into a larger one | Claiming STs, Jains, Sikhs, Buddhists are Hindus
  [1.1] Welfarist | Works for one community's welfare only | Christian or Parsi associations
  [1.2] Retreatist | A small community keeps away from politics | Bahai community
  [1.3] Retaliatory | Tries to harm members of other communities | Riots, revenge cycles
  [1.4] Separatist | Separate territorial State **within India** | Gorkhaland, Bodoland
  [1.5] Secessionist | Independent State **outside India** | Khalistan, "Azad Kashmir"
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Political** communalism | Uses religion for votes
  [2.1] **Economic** communalism | Grows from competition for jobs and resources
### part 7: Religious Fundamentalism vs Communalism
[0] h: Religious fundamentalism
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Fundamentalism** | Strict adherence to religious beliefs; often rejects modern interpretations
  [1.1] Features | **Literal reading of scripture**, conservatism, resistance to change, **group exclusiveness**
  [1.2] Causes | **Identity crisis**, globalisation, **social insecurity**, political mobilisation, economic inequality
[2] table: Religious fundamentalism | Communalism
  [2.0] Return to "fundamentals"; mainly doctrinal | Political mobilisation of religious identity
  [2.1] Reforms one's own community (often anti-modern) | Directed against other communities; seeks power and resources
  [2.2] Can exist without inter-group conflict | Always "us vs them"

## topic group2 2-18 | Regionalism – Meaning, Causes, Forms and Sons-of-the-soil Movements
### part 0: Meaning of Regionalism
[0] h: Meaning
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Definition | Regionalism = a feeling of **loyalty to a particular part of a country** and a wish for it to be **more politically independent**; a culmination of socio-economic and political factors, not just a territorial unit
  [1.1] Clearest expression | Regional demands for autonomy or, at the extreme, **secession by certain States**
  [1.2] Related terms | Preference for one's religion = **communalism**; caste loyalty = **casteism**; favouring one's language = **linguism**
[2] h: Terms
[3] table: Term | Meaning
  [3.0] Region | Area shaped by history, with its own language, culture, identity and political implications
  [3.1] Regionalism | Emotional attachment to one area; may resist a dominant culture or challenge the nation-state
  [3.2] Supra-state | Several States sharing interests (e.g. the North-East)
  [3.3] Inter-state | Conflict between States
  [3.4] Intra-state | A region within a State seeking recognition; a **positive** form – self-dependence and development of the region (Saurashtra, East UP, Vidarbha)
[4] h: Positive vs negative regionalism
[5] table: Positive | Negative
  [5.0] Regional parties give backward regions a voice | Parochialism; hostility to migrants (Shiv Sena, MNS)
  [5.1] Cultural pride, balanced development, local innovation | Law-and-order breakdowns; openings for extremists
  [5.2] Solidarity (Tripura Tribal Autonomous District Council) | Inter-state disputes; new linguistic minorities
[6] b: Regional feeling turns **negative** when exploited for votes by regional parties.
[7] h: Regionalism and tribal welfare
[8] table: Fact | Key detail
  [8.0] Regionalism | **Empowers local governance** for better tribal welfare and **strengthens State-centric tribal-welfare policies**
  [8.1] **Sub-regionalism** | **Enhances the focus on local tribal needs within smaller regions** (agency tracts, autonomous councils)

### part 0: Social Policy – Social Justice, Social Legislation, Social Protection
[0] h: Social justice
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Social justice** | Ending social and economic inequalities (Art. 38, 46)
  [1.1] **John Rawls** | Equal access to liberties, rights **and** opportunities, and care for the interests of the deprived and disadvantaged
  [1.2] Effect | Lets the courts **uphold** laws protecting weaker sections, **reservations** for backward classes and **affirmative action**
  [1.3] Criticism | Critics list **compulsory military service** and **state interference in private life** as negative repercussions of the **social justice** idea (state-driven equality)
  [1.4] **Utilitarianism** | "Greatest happiness of the greatest number" – founded by **Jeremy Bentham**; the classical basis of welfare policy
  [1.5] **World Day of Social Justice – 20 February** | 2019 theme: "**If you want Peace & Development, Work for Social Justice**"
[2] h: Welfare state and DPSPs
[3] table: Article | Welfare mandate
  [3.0] 38 | Social order of justice; reduce inequalities
  [3.1] 39 | Livelihood, equal pay, no concentration of wealth
  [3.2] 41 | Right to work, education, public assistance
  [3.3] 42 | Just and humane work conditions; maternity relief
  [3.4] 43 | Living wage for workers
  [3.5] 46 | Interests of SCs, STs, weaker sections
  [3.6] 47 | Nutrition, living standards, public health
[4] h: Terms
[5] table: Term | Meaning
  [5.0] Social policy | The State's overall framework for social well-being
  [5.1] Social welfare | Targeted services for the vulnerable – disabled, destitute children, widows (general health services for all are not targeted welfare)
  [5.2] Social defence | Rehabilitation in deviant/vulnerable situations (drugs, beggary, juveniles)
  [5.3] **Social protection** | Policies and programmes that reduce poverty and vulnerability and **improve capacity to manage risks such as disability and old age**
  [5.4] Welfare vs development | **Welfare** = short-term, consumption; **development** = long-term, production
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] **RSBY** (Rashtriya Swasthya Bima Yojana) | Health insurance – a **social protection programme**
  [6.1] Social development programmes | Mid-Day Meal, SSA, Rural Health Mission

### part 1: Policy Cycle – Formulation, Implementation, Monitoring, Evaluation
[0] h: Policy cycle
[1] table: Stage | What happens
  [1.0] 1. Agenda setting | Problem identified; media, advocacy, experts push it
  [1.1] 2. Formulation | Research, stakeholder consultation, **cost-benefit analysis**, impact assessment
  [1.2] 3. Adoption | A course formally chosen – **policy making is the major function of the Cabinet** (Centre and States)
  [1.3] 4. Implementation | Planning, resources, coordination
  [1.4] 5. Monitoring and evaluation | Data test whether goals are met; feedback
  [1.5] 6. Termination / revision | A failing policy ended or changed; policy learning goes back to agenda setting
[2] b: **Communication and transparency** run through **every stage**.
[3] h: Terms
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **Monitoring** | Keeping a **careful watch on specific events or activities** that form the main components of a programme, while it runs
  [4.1] **Formative evaluation** (internal) | Judges a programme's worth **while its activities are forming / in progress**
  [4.2] Summative evaluation | At the end, on outcomes
  [4.3] **Data analysis** | Critical examination of the assembled and grouped data to study the characteristics of the object under study
