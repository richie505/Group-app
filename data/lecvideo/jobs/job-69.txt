### part 6: Bioremediation – Meaning and Methods
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **Bioremediation** | Removal or degradation of pollutants by **organisms or their enzymes**; **enhances natural biodegradation**
  [0.1] Scope | Soil, water, air – oil spills, heavy metals, pesticides, solvents
  [0.2] Engineered microbes | GE microbes can be designed for it
  [0.3] Merits | **Slower but cheaper** than conventional (thermal, chemical) clean-up; no secondary pollution
  [0.4] Limits | Results vary with pollutant and site; heavy metals (Cd, Pb) are **not readily and completely** treated
[1] table: Process | Key detail
  [1.0] Burning / incineration | Thermal treatment, not biological
  [1.1] **Biomagnification** | DDT, mercury rising up food chains
  [1.2] **Eutrophication** | Nutrient-driven algal blooms
  [1.3] Biofortification | Raising nutrients in crops
[2] table: Basis | In-situ | Ex-situ
  [2.0] Site | At the site, **no excavation**; passive, cheapest, slow | Excavated, treated elsewhere
  [2.1] Techniques | Bioventing, biosparging, bioaugmentation | **Landfarming, composting, biopiles, bioreactors** (bioreactors fastest)
[3] h: Techniques
[4] table: Technique | How / target
  [4.0] **Bioaugmentation** | Adds chosen **degrading microbes** – oil spills
  [4.1] **Dehalococcoides** | Bioaugmentation: **Dehalococcoides spp.** injected to **dechlorinate trichloroethylene (TCE)** and other chlorinated solvents in **groundwater**
  [4.2] **Biostimulation** | Adds N, P, oxygen to native microbes – polluted lakes
  [4.3] **Bioventing** | Air/nutrients via wells into **soil**
  [4.4] **Biosparging** | Air injected **below the water table** – groundwater
  [4.5] **Biopiling** | **Ex-situ**: contaminated soil **excavated and heaped into piles (biopiles)**; aeration, moisture, nutrients controlled so **indigenous microbes** degrade pollutants (mainly **hydrocarbons**)
  [4.6] Mycoremediation | **Fungi** – organic pollutants
  [4.7] Rhizofiltration | Roots absorb/precipitate contaminants from **water**
  [4.8] Rhizoremediation | Roots stimulate rhizosphere microbes
  [4.9] **Bioleaching** | Microorganisms (mainly **acidophilic bacteria and archaea**) **dissolve metals out of ores, mine tailings or e-waste** – they oxidise sulphides or ferrous iron, creating acidic conditions
  [4.10] **Biosorption (Bioadsorption)** | **Passive binding** of heavy metals or dyes onto the **cell walls of live or dead microbes** (non-metabolic, physicochemical) – e.g. **yeast removing lead and cadmium** from wastewater
  [4.11] **Bioelectrochemical Systems (BES)** | **Electroactive microbes** treat wastewater while converting chemical energy in organic matter into **electricity** or valuable products; **Microbial Fuel Cells (MFCs)** are a type
  [4.12] **Constructed wetlands** | Plants and microbes break down and absorb sewage pollutants
  [4.13] **Bregoli** | Paper-industry by-product resembling **sawdust**; absorbs **oil spills** (with oil zapper and microbes)

[6] table: Sub-type / plant | Key detail

  [6.1] Phytostabilisation | Roots immobilise contaminants
  [6.2] Phytodegradation | Plant enzymes break organics
  [6.3] **Phytovolatilisation** | Released to air in volatile form (mercury, selenium)
  [6.4] Pteris vittata (fern) | **Arsenic**

  [6.9] Eucalyptus (sewage ponds) | Transpires surplus wastewater
  [6.10] Bacillus flexus / Acinetobacter junii | Arsenate / arsenite
  [6.11] **Ideonella sakaiensis** (PETase) | **PET plastic** degradation
