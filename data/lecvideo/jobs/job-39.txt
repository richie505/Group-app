## topic group2 2-120 | Human Rights – Concept, Generations, Udhr, Iccpr, Icescr and Un Machinery
### part 0: Concept of Human Rights – Meaning, Thinkers and Milestones
[0] h: Meaning
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Human rights** | Rights of **all human beings by nature**, simply because they are human – **inherent, inalienable, universal**; essential conditions of social life
  [1.1] **Core value** | **Dignity of man as a human being** (UDHR Preamble: inherent dignity and equal rights)
  [1.2] Nature | **Indivisible and interdependent** – civil-political and socio-economic rights stand together (**Vienna Declaration, 1993**)
  [1.3] **Rights vs the State** | **Claims of the citizens against the State** – limits on State power
  [1.4] Fundamental Rights | Every **Fundamental Right** is a human right, but **not every human right is a Fundamental Right**
  [1.5] Values | "Respect for human rights and responsibilities" is a **constitutional value**; in a **democracy** equality is a **legal norm**, which helps guarantee women's freedom and dignity
  [1.6] Violations | Detaining a person on the way to vote and killing a civilian in a combing operation
[2] h: Kinds of rights
[3] table: Kind | Key detail
  [3.0] **Economic rights** | A **reasonable and legal source of livelihood** (work, wages, leisure)
  [3.1] **Political rights** | Vote, contest, hold office – the **right to vote is a political right**; in India only a **statutory right** (**Sec. 62, RP Act 1951**)
  [3.2] Civil rights | Life, liberty, religion
  [3.3] **Social rights** | Education, health, social security, welfare of the weak
[4] h: Thinkers
[5] table: Thinker | Idea
  [5.0] **John Locke** (English / **British** thinker) | **Natural rights** – life, liberty, property; popularised the doctrine; "father" of the human-rights idea
  [5.1] **J.J. Rousseau** | "**Man is born free, but everywhere he is in chains**" (Social Contract, 1762)
  [5.2] **Ronald Dworkin** | Rights as **trumps**: a protective shield for the individual when the State interferes with civil liberties
  [5.3] **Jack Donnelly** | Human rights as a **new standard of civilisation** (1998)
  [5.4] **Olympe de Gouges** | **Declaration of the Rights of Woman and the Female Citizen (1791)**, French Revolution
  [5.5] Mary Wollstonecraft | A Vindication of the Rights of Woman (1792)
