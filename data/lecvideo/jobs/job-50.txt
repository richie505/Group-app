### part 4: Microfinance and SHG-Bank Linkage
[0] h: Microfinance
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Microfinance** | Financial services for low-income people: **microcredit, savings, microinsurance, payments / fund transfer**
  [1.1] Roots | **Grameen Bank, Bangladesh (1976)** – **Muhammad Yunus**, pioneer of microfinance (Nobel Peace Prize 2006)
  [1.2] UN **International Year of Microcredit** | **2005**
  [1.3] **AP microfinance crisis (2010)** | Began with **SKS Microfinance** (IPO, coercive recoveries, borrower suicides) → AP MFI (Regulation of Money Lending) **Ordinance, 15 October 2010**: MFIs had to **register with a state-appointed authority**; replaced by an Act (Dec 2010)
  [1.4] National impact | The crisis **reshaped national regulation** → RBI **Y.H. Malegam** sub-committee (Oct 2010) → **NBFC-MFI** category created **2 December 2011**
  [1.5] After the crisis | Moneylenders met about **60%** of the cut-off borrowers' credit needs (at 36-120% interest; MicroSave studies)
  [1.6] **MFIN** | Self-regulatory national body of RBI-regulated MFIs (2009)
[2] h: SHG-Bank Linkage

  [3.3] DAY-NRLM | 2011 | **Ministry of Rural Development**; rural livelihoods through SHGs
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] Joint liability | In an SHG **all members take responsibility** for a member's loan

  [4.2] **NABARD** | Promotes and refinances – no implementer
  [4.3] **SHGs** | Combine the advantages of the **traditional moneylender** (reach, flexibility, no collateral) and the **commercial bank** (low-cost formal finance)

[0] h: Who issues what

### part 1: Central Banking Functions – Banker to Government and Banker's Bank
[0] h: The central bank
[1] b: The **RBI performs the central banking functions** in India – it is the **Central Bank** (SBI, NABARD, SBH and Central Bank of India are banks / DFIs) and the regulator of the entire banking system.
[2] table: Function | Legal basis / content
  [2.0] Bank of issue | Sec 22: sole right to issue notes except the ₹1 note
  [2.1] **Banker, agent and adviser to Government** | **Sec 20** (Centre), **Sec 21/21A** (States, by agreement); payments, receipts, **public debt management**, **borrowing programme**, Ways and Means Advances, overdrafts
  [2.2] **Banker's bank** | Holds SCBs' deposits and reserves (CRR); **lender of last resort**; guides and advises banks
  [2.3] Monetary authority | Price stability with growth in mind; MPC sets the repo rate
  [2.4] **Custodian of forex** | Manages forex reserves under **FEMA 1999**; stabilises the rupee's external value
  [2.5] Controller of credit | Controls money and credit (quantitative and qualitative measures)
  [2.6] Payments | Regulates and upgrades payment systems (PSS Act 2007)
  [2.7] Regulator | Banks, NBFCs (Chapter IIIB), AIFIs, HFCs
[3] h: Banker to Government
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] Public debt | Manages the **public debt of Centre and States** and handles the **Government of India's borrowing programme**; maintains Government accounts; the **Public Debt Office** functions under it
  [4.1] States | Banker to **all State Governments except Sikkim** (Sikkim: limited agreement, public debt only)
  [4.2] **Fiscal agent and adviser** | To Government in monetary and financial matters; **represents India at the IMF** (Government's agent for India's IMF membership) and the World Bank
  [4.3] Overall | Banker to the Centre, formulates monetary policy, agent for IMF membership, handles GoI borrowing
[5] h: Banker's bank and lender of last resort
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] **Banker's bank** | Other banks **keep deposits / reserves with the RBI**; the RBI **lends to banks in need**; it **advises banks on monetary matters**
  [6.1] **Lender of last resort (LoLR)** | Exclusive RBI function: **emergency liquidity to banks facing a temporary crisis** (no lending to trade / industry, no financing of budget deficits)
  [6.2] **Bank Rate** | Rate at which the **RBI lends to / rediscounts bills of commercial banks** (also called the discount rate)
  [6.3] **FEMA** | RBI administers FEMA 1999 "to facilitate external trade and payments and promote orderly development of the forex market"; it separately upgrades **payment systems**
### part 2: Monetary Authority, Credit Control and What RBI Does Not Do
[0] h: Monetary policy and credit control
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Monetary (and credit) policy** | **Formulated and implemented by the RBI** (MPC since 2016; six members); the RBI **regulates and announces the repo and reverse repo rates**
  [1.1] **Price stability / controlling inflation** | **RBI** (flexible inflation targeting; target set by the **Finance Ministry in consultation with the RBI**)
  [1.2] Credit control | RBI **controls "money and credit"** and **controls credit creation by commercial banks** – quantitative (Bank Rate, repo, CRR, SLR, OMO) and qualitative (selective credit control, margins, moral suasion) tools
  [1.3] Exchange rate | Besides market forces, the **RBI intervenes (buys / sells dollars) to manage the exchange rate**
  [1.4] Stability | Central banks use repo and CRR to manage liquidity and inflation – this is how monetary policy ensures stability
  [1.5] **Regulating and controlling banks** | An activity of the **RBI** (powers under the **Banking Regulation Act 1949**)
  [1.6] 2016 framework | MPC + inflation-targeting law gave **operational independence** on policy rates
[2] h: Outside the RBI's role
[3] table: Activity | Done by
  [3.0] **Credit creation** | **Commercial banks** – the RBI only **controls** it
  [3.1] **Regulation of foreign trade** | Commerce Ministry / DGFT – the RBI regulates **currency, credit and forex reserves**
  [3.2] **Fiscal policy** | **Ministry of Finance**
  [3.3] **Granting corporate deeds**; lending to the public; minting coins; issuing the ₹1 note; income-tax collection | Others

## topic group2 3-60 | Monetary Policy – Objectives, Instruments (crr, Slr, Repo, Reverse Repo, Msf, Omo, Laf), Transmission
### part 0: CRR and SLR – Reserve Ratios
[0] h: Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **CRR = Cash Reserve Ratio** | Share of a bank's **Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL)** kept as **cash balances with the RBI**
  [1.1] **"Required reserve ratio"** | The **certain percentage of deposits every bank must keep as reserves** = **CRR**
  [1.2] Fixed by | **The RBI (the central bank)**; **Section 42 of the RBI Act, 1934**; **no floor or ceiling** since 2006
  [1.3] Return / form | **No interest**; **cash only**
  [1.4] **Raising CRR** | Commercial banks have **less money to lend** → **liquidity falls**, **credit creation falls** → helps control inflation
  [1.5] **Lowering CRR** | **Credit creation increases**; banks get **higher leverage to liquidity**; the economy **may see increased investment**
  [1.6] **Temporary I-CRR (Aug 2023)** | **10%** on the increase in NDTL (19 May–28 Jul 2023), announced **10 Aug 2023**, effective fortnight from 12 Aug; **impounded about ₹1.1 lakh crore**; released in phases (9 Sep, 23 Sep, 7 Oct 2023); applied only to the **incremental deposit base** (CRR applies to the **whole NDTL**)
  [1.7] **9 Feb 2013** | CRR cut 25 bps to **4%** (Third Quarter Review, Jan 2013)
[2] h: Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **SLR** | Minimum share of NDTL a bank keeps **with itself** in liquid assets **before lending** – **cash, gold and unencumbered approved / Government securities (G-secs, T-bills, SDLs)**; priority-sector loans do not count
  [3.1] Role | **Mechanism by which commercial banks provide credit to the Government** (they buy G-secs to meet it)
  [3.2] Older wording | "Ratio between cash in hand and total assets" – strictly SLR is liquid assets to NDTL
  [3.3] Law | **Section 24, Banking Regulation Act, 1949**; range **0%–40% of NDTL**; the **25% floor** removed by the **BR (Amendment) Act, 2007**; **MSS securities count** towards SLR
  [3.4] **Shortfall penalty** | **Bank rate + 3%**, then **bank rate + 5%** if it continues
  [3.5] **SLR cut of 50 bps** | More lendable funds → **scheduled commercial banks may cut lending rates**
  [3.6] Use | **Raise SLR to fight inflation; cut SLR for growth**
[4] h: Trend
[5] table: Date | SLR | CRR
  [5.0] 1991-92 | **38.5%** | 15%
  [5.1] Since reforms | **Both reduced** (Narasimham Committee) | **Both reduced**
  [5.2] 5 Jan 2019 | **19.25%** (Dec 2018 review: −25 bps every quarter) | 4%
  [5.3] 11 Apr 2020 | **18%** | 3% (Mar 2020)
  [5.4] 2026 (now) | **18%** | **3%** (cut 4% → 3% in 2025, ~₹2.5 lakh crore released)
[6] h: CRR vs SLR
[7] table: Basis | CRR | SLR
  [7.0] Kept | **With RBI** | **By the bank itself**
  [7.1] Form | Cash only | Cash, gold, approved securities
  [7.2] Return | No interest | Earns interest on G-secs
  [7.3] Law | Sec 42, RBI Act 1934 | Sec 24, BR Act 1949
### part 1: Policy Rates – Repo, Reverse Repo, Bank Rate, MSF and LAF
[0] h: Policy rates
[1] table: Rate | Who lends to whom | Key point
  [1.0] **Repo** | **RBI lends to banks** short term (overnight) against G-secs (repurchase agreement) | Principal policy rate; **injects liquidity**, regulates money supply; part of **monetary policy**
  [1.1] **Reverse repo** | **Banks lend to / park funds with RBI** | **Absorbs** surplus liquidity; always below repo
  [1.2] **Bank Rate** | RBI's **official / discount rate** for **long-term** loans; **rediscounts bills of exchange** | = **official rate of interest charged by the central bank**; penal rates linked to it; signal rate
  [1.3] **MSF** | Banks borrow **overnight** from RBI in emergency, dipping into SLR | **Contains volatility in the overnight inter-bank rate**; ceiling of the corridor
  [1.4] **SDF** | Banks park surplus with RBI **without collateral** | Floor of the corridor (since April 2022)
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Repo** = "Repurchase Option" | Bank sells G-secs to the RBI and buys them back later; the difference is the interest (no cost of collateral, no link to the rupee's exchange value)
  [2.1] Tenor | **Bank Rate is long-term, repo short-term**
  [2.2] **LAF (June 2000)** | Window through which banks **borrow via repos** and **lend via reverse repos** – borrowing through repurchase agreements
  [2.3] **MSF (May 2011)** | Began at repo + **100 bps**, up to **1% of NDTL**; now repo + **25 bps**, up to **2%** of NDTL; aligned with Bank Rate in **Feb 2012**; minimum ₹1 crore
  [2.4] **MSF rate and NDTL** | Terms of **banking operations**
  [2.5] **LAF access** | Scheduled commercial banks and **standalone Primary Dealers** (registered as NBFCs); other NBFCs (HFCs, MFIs, asset-finance cos) are excluded
  [2.6] **Repo − reverse repo gap** | **1.00 percentage point** in June 2010 – June 2011 (from Sep 2010); from **May 2011** only repo is fixed and reverse repo set **100 bps below** it
[3] h: The corridor
[4] table: Date | SDF (floor) | Repo | MSF = Bank Rate (ceiling) | Stance
  [4.0] 5 Aug 2026 | 5.00% | **5.25%** (unanimous hold) | 5.50% | Neutral
  [4.1] **7 Oct 2026** | **5.25%** | **5.50%** (+25 bps, unanimous) | **5.75%** | **Calibrated tightening**
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] Corridor width | **50 bps**: SDF = repo − 25 bps; MSF = Bank Rate = repo + 25 bps
  [5.1] Reverse repo | **Fixed-rate reverse repo 3.35%** (May 2026), used at the RBI's discretion since SDF
  [5.2] **SDF** | Proposed 2015, enabled by **Budget 2018-19**, began **April 2022**: absorbs surplus liquidity **without collateral** – the only collateral-free absorption tool
[6] h: Stances
[7] table: Stance | Meaning
  [7.0] Accommodative | Supports growth; cuts likely
  [7.1] Neutral | Either way, data-dependent
  [7.2] **Calibrated tightening** | No cuts; hikes measured, not at every meeting
  [7.3] Hawkish / Dovish | Worried about inflation, tightens / worried about growth, easing bias
[8] b: Path: neutral → accommodative (Apr 2025) → **neutral (June 2025)**, kept through Aug 2026 → **calibrated tightening (Oct 2026)**.
### part 2: OMO, Sterilisation and Liquidity Management
[0] h: Open Market Operations
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **OMO** | **Purchase and sale of Government securities (and T-bills) by the RBI** – trading in securities
  [1.1] Channel | RBI deals **through banks**, not directly with the public
  [1.2] Effect | **Sell G-secs → absorbs liquidity** (fights inflation); **buy G-secs → injects liquidity**, money supply up, **interest rates fall**
  [1.3] Type | A **quantitative** tool and "**a part of credit policy**"; main tool for **durable** liquidity
  [1.4] **Money supply rises** | With **central-bank purchase of G-secs from the public** and **Government borrowing from the central bank**; depositing currency in banks only shifts money; sale of G-secs reduces it
[2] h: Sterilisation
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **Sterilisation** | Offsetting the rupee liquidity created by forex inflows (RBI buying dollars) by **selling G-secs through OMO / MSS** or VRRR – i.e. **conducting OMOs**
  [3.1] **MSS (April 2004)** | T-bills / dated securities issued to withdraw excess liquidity; proceeds in a **separate account the Government cannot spend**
[4] h: Other liquidity tools
[5] table: Tool | Feature
  [5.0] VRR / VRRR | Auction-based repo / reverse repo; VRRR absorbs for longer than the overnight SDF
  [5.1] **Fine-tuning operations** | Short VRR / VRRR auctions to keep liquidity **in line with the monetary policy stance** – **49 in FY24**
  [5.2] LTRO (2020) | **1–3 year** funds at repo against G-secs
  [5.3] USD/INR buy-sell swap | Injects rupees without changing the RBI's balance-sheet size ($5 bn, 3-year, Dec 2025)
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] **Surplus liquidity** | Pulls money-market rates **below repo** and speeds transmission; 4 Sep 2026: RBI absorbed **₹6.02 lakh crore** through two 3-day VRRRs
  [6.1] **G-sec yields** | Influenced by **US Fed actions, RBI actions and domestic inflation and short-term rates**

## topic group2 3-61 | Monetary Policy Committee and the Flexible Inflation Targeting Framework
### part 0: Inflation Target – What, Who and How Much
[0] h: Inflation target
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Inflation targeting** | **Public announcement of a medium-term numerical target** for inflation by the central bank
  [1.1] **Target** | **4% CPI-Combined, tolerance ±2 percentage points** (band **2–6%**)
  [1.2] **Who fixes it** | The **Government of India, in consultation with the RBI** (Sec 45ZA); the **RBI / MPC must achieve it**
  [1.3] Frequency | Set **once every five years**, notified in the **Official Gazette**
  [1.4] **Anchor** | Headline **CPI-C** compiled by the **NSO (MoSPI)**; new series **base 2024** released **12 Feb 2026** (earlier base 2012); not WPI

### part 0: NPA Definition and Asset Classification
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **NPA** | A loan or advance whose **instalment of principal or interest** stays **overdue for more than 90 days** – it has stopped producing income for the bank
  [0.1] **Farm loans** | Short-duration crops = overdue **2 crop seasons**; long-duration crops = **1 crop season**
[1] table: Class | Criterion
  [1.0] Standard | Regular; no overdue beyond 90 days
  [1.1] SMA-0 / SMA-1 / SMA-2 | Overdue 1-30 / **31-60** / 61-90 days: early warning, **still standard**
  [1.2] Sub-standard | NPA for **12 months or less**
  [1.3] Doubtful | NPA for **more than 12 months**
  [1.4] Loss | Loss identified by the bank, auditors or RBI inspection, but not wholly written off
[2] b: Sequence: Standard → SMA-0/1/2 → NPA (sub-standard → doubtful → loss).
[3] b: **Pannir Selvam Committee (1998)** gave a detailed report on the **NPAs of public sector banks** (recovery by arbitration/conciliation).
### part 1: Stress Indicators and Key Terms
[0] table: Term | Meaning
  [0.0] Gross NPA | All NPAs
  [0.1] Net NPA | Gross NPA − provisions
  [0.2] Stressed assets | NPAs + **restructured loans** + **written-off assets**
  [0.3] Interest coverage ratio (ICR) | **EBIT ÷ interest due**; below 1 = cannot cover interest from earnings; shows the **present and emerging risk** of a borrower; a **higher** ICR = a **better** ability to service debt
  [0.4] Evergreening | Fresh loans to repay old ones → default stays hidden
  [0.5] **Balance sheet** | Statement of a firm's **assets and liabilities** on a date – shows their **size and composition** (profitability is in the profit and loss account)
### part 2: Recognition and Provisioning
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **IRACP** | **Income Recognition, Asset Classification, Provisioning** (prudential norms from 1992)
  [0.1] **Income recognition** | Interest on an NPA is booked as income **only when actually received**
  [0.2] **Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR)** | Provisions held ÷ gross NPAs; higher PCR → more resilient bank
  [0.3] **From 1 April 2027** | **Borrower-level tagging** – one NPA loan → all that borrower's loans at the bank become NPAs; **upgrade only after all arrears** on every facility are paid; 90-day test stays; automated identification
