### part 5: Missile Classes, FOBS and Hypersonic Technology
[0] h: Ballistic vs cruise
[1] table: Feature | Ballistic missile | Cruise missile
  [1.0] Power | – | **Air-breathing (jet)**, powered throughout
  [1.1] Path | High parabolic (free-fall) arc | Flies low, constant speed, aerodynamic lift
  [1.2] Examples | **Agni**, Prithvi, Pralay, Shaurya | **BrahMos**, Nirbhay
[2] b: Agni-V is a ballistic missile and BrahMos a cruise missile; a ballistic missile is not jet-propelled.
[3] table: Class | Speed | Indian example
  [3.0] **Subsonic** | Below Mach **1** | Nirbhay
  [3.1] **Supersonic** | Mach **1–5** | BrahMos (Mach **2.8–3.0**)
  [3.2] **Hypersonic** | Above **Mach 5** | BrahMos-II (planned), LR-AShM, HSTDV
[4] h: Terms
[5] table: Term | Meaning
  [5.0] **FOBS** (Fractional Orbital Bombardment System) | Warhead put into a (fractional) low Earth orbit, then **de-orbits over the target**; can come from any direction (e.g. over the South Pole); Soviet Cold War concept
  [5.1] **HGV** (Hypersonic Glide Vehicle) | Hard to intercept
  [5.2] **MIRV** | One missile, several warheads, different targets; first Indian test on **Agni-V** in **Mission Divyastra** (**11 March 2024**)
  [5.3] **Scramjet** | **Supersonic combustion**, no rotating parts; uses air, does not carry its oxidiser
  [5.4] **SFDR** (Solid Fuel Ducted Ramjet) | Uses atmospheric air; demonstrated from ITR Chandipur; for **long-range air-to-air missiles**
[6] h: Indian hypersonic systems
[7] table: System | Key detail
  [7.0] **HSTDV** | Indigenous **scramjet** demonstrator; flew above **Mach 6**; scramjet combustion and thermal protection above **2,000°C**; combustor run over **12** minutes (Jan **2026**)
  [7.1] **16 November 2024** | First flight-trial of India's first long-range hypersonic missile
  [7.2] **LR-AShM** | Hypersonic **glide anti-ship** missile for Navy coastal batteries; **~1,500 km**; **peak Mach 10**, average Mach 5 via "skipping"; shown at the **77th** Republic Day Parade; **AP coast ranges** used for validation rounds (Jan 2026)

### part 3: Nuclear Doctrine, No First Use and Command
[0] h: Timeline
[1] table: Year | Event
  [1.0] **August 1999** | **Draft doctrine** by the **National Security Advisory Board (NSAB)**
  [1.1] After Pokhran-II | **No First Use** adopted by the **Vajpayee government**
  [1.2] **4 January 2003** | Doctrine formally adopted by the **CCS**
[2] h: Key tenets
[3] table: Tenet | Key detail
  [3.0] **Credible minimum deterrence** | Backed by an assured **second-strike capability** (survivable SSBNs)
  [3.1] **No First Use** | Nuclear weapons only in **retaliation against a nuclear attack on Indian territory or Indian forces**
  [3.2] **Massive retaliation** | Causing unacceptable damage
  [3.3] Non-nuclear states | **No use against non-nuclear-weapon states**; nuclear option kept against a **major biological or chemical attack**
  [3.4] Testing | **Unilateral moratorium** on nuclear tests (since **1998**)
  [3.5] Inspection | India kept the bomb option open and would **not open its strategic nuclear plants to inspection**
[4] h: Nuclear Command Authority
[5] table: Body | Chaired by | Role
  [5.0] **Political Council** | **Prime Minister** | **Only body that can authorise use**
  [5.1] **Executive Council** | **National Security Advisor** | Advises; carries out directions
  [5.2] **Strategic Forces Command** | Tri-service (set up **2003**) | **Manages and operates** the nuclear arsenal
[6] b: The **CCS** oversees civilian political control; it does not manage the forces. Aug **2026**: Agni user trials held "under the aegis of SFC".

### part 2: Radar, Doppler Effect and Stealth
[0] h: RADAR and the Doppler effect
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **RADAR** | **Radio Detection And Ranging** – **detects and fixes the position** (range, direction, speed) of objects with radio waves
  [1.1] **Doppler effect** | Apparent change in frequency from relative motion: source **approaching → higher** pitch; **receding → lower** (a train moving away sounds lower)
  [1.2] Doppler uses | **Police speed radar guns**, **satellite tracking**, medical ultrasound (blood-flow velocity), **Doppler Weather Radar** (cyclones, **heavy rainfall and thunderstorms**), **expanding universe** (red shift = moving away, Hubble)
[2] table: Radar type | Principle / use
  [2.0] Doppler | Speed from frequency shift
  [2.1] SAR | Imaging at night and through clouds
  [2.2] **AESA** | T/R module on every element; electronic beam, no moving antenna; **tracks multiple targets** – Uttam, MF-STAR
  [2.3] VHF anti-stealth | Long waves pick up stealth aircraft
  [2.4] Over-the-horizon | HF waves bounced off the ionosphere
[3] b: **Uttam AESA** (LRDE): solid-state active phased-array fire-control radar, wide-band RF front end, adaptable to fighters (Tejas Mk-1A); it tracks multiple targets.
[4] h: Indigenous radars
[5] table: Radar | Role
  [5.0] **Swathi** | **Weapon-locating radar** (guns, mortars, rockets); BEL; exported to Armenia
  [5.1] **Surya** | **VHF radar that tracks stealth aircraft**
  [5.2] **Swordfish** | BMD long-range tracking (Green Pine based)
  [5.3] **Arudhra** | IAF medium-power 4D surveillance radar
  [5.4] **Ashwini** | Low-level lightweight AESA radar (cruise missiles, drones)
  [5.5] **Rajendra** | Fire-control radar of Akash
  [5.6] **INDRA** | Low-level surveillance (Indian Doppler Radar)
[6] h: AWACS vs AEW&C
[7] table: AWACS | AEW&C (Netra)
  [7.0] Large aircraft (Il-76 **Phalcon**), long range, full battle management | Smaller jet (**Embraer EMB-145**), early warning + limited control; **DRDO-CABS Bengaluru**
[8] b: **AWACS** (Airborne Warning and Control System): an **air-based long-range radar surveillance and control system** for air defence – the **"eye in the sky"** (IAF: Il-76 Phalcon).
[9] h: Stealth
[10] table: Fact | Key detail
  [10.0] **Stealth technology** | **Absorbing / deflecting radar waves** – very small **radar cross-section (RCS)** + **Radar Absorbing Material**; not "invisible to the eye"
  [10.1] Detection | Stealth objects **can** be detected at specific (low, VHF/UHF) frequencies
  [10.2] Metamaterials | Used to **reduce** scattering
  [10.3] **IIT Kharagpur** | Low-cost **radar-absorbing metamaterial** to cloak soldiers and equipment from enemy radar

### part 0: Netiquette and Online Behaviour Terms
[0] h: Netiquette
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Netiquette** | The **Internet's ethical protocol** (net + **etiquette**)
  [1.1] Etiquette | The customary code of polite behaviour in a social group
  [1.2] Core rule | **Constructive online dialogue** and **respect privacy**
  [1.3] Principle | "**Digital freedom** should be accompanied by **digital responsibility**"
[2] table: Medium | Do | Don't
  [2.0] Email | Clear subject line, polite greeting, check recipients | Type in **ALL CAPS** (reads as shouting); forward **chain mails**
  [2.1] Social media | Verify before sharing, respect privacy, credit sources | Post others' photos without consent (even if credited); troll
  [2.2] Online meetings | Join on time, **mute when not speaking** | Talk over others; record without permission
  [2.3] Online forums | Stay on topic, civil discourse | Trolling, **spamming**
[3] h: Terms
[4] table: Term | Meaning
  [4.0] **Cyber venting** | Using websites to **pour out one's grievances** (e.g. against an employer)
  [4.1] **Nomophobia** | Irrational fear of being **without one's mobile** (no signal, low battery, no balance)
  [4.2] Trolling | Deliberately provocative or abusive content
  [4.3] Cyberbullying | Repeated online harassment, especially of children
  [4.4] Doxxing | Publicly sharing someone's private information without consent; **no universal legal definition**; prosecuted under stalking/harassment/cyber laws
  [4.5] Cyberstalking | Repeated unwanted monitoring or contact online
  [4.6] Digital footprint | Trail of data a person leaves online
  [4.7] Filter bubble | Algorithms rank content by **user preference**, isolating users

  [8.1] **Communication barriers** | **Personal** barriers arise from the sender and receiver themselves (attitudes, emotions); organisational = structure; semantic = language

### part 3: Misinformation, Fact-checks and Critical Thinking
[0] h: Types of false information
[1] table: Type | Meaning
  [1.0] **Misinformation** | False content, **no intent** to deceive
  [1.1] **Disinformation** | False content **deliberately** created for political/social/economic goals
  [1.2] **Malinformation** | **Genuine** information shared to cause harm (leaked private data)
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] Echo chambers / filter bubbles | Strengthen fake news
  [2.1] **Deepfakes** | AI-made fake audio/video
  [2.2] **Critical thinking** | Determining the **quality of our beliefs** (evidence, reasoning) – not speed, memory or brainstorming
  [2.3] **NEP 2020** | Stresses **critical thinking and civic engagement** as an answer to fake news
[3] h: Fact-check timeline
[4] table: Date | Event
  [4.0] **Nov 2019** | **PIB Fact Check Unit** set up – debunks fake news about the **Government of India, ministries, PSUs**
  [4.1] 2021 | IT (Intermediary Guidelines & Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules under **Sec. 87**; SSMIs file **monthly** compliance reports
  [4.2] April 2023 | Amendment: an intermediary loses safe harbour if content on Central Government business is flagged "fake" by the **PIB FCU**
  [4.3] March 2024 | FCU notified; the **Supreme Court stayed** it
  [4.4] Sept 2024 | The **Bombay High Court** struck down the 2023 amendment
