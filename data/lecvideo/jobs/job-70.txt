## topic group2 5-14 | Nanotechnology – Nature, Scope, Applications, Nano Mission, Health and Environmental Concerns
### part 0: Graphene, Fullerene and Carbon Nanotubes
[0] h: Carbon nanomaterials (all sp², all pure carbon)
[1] table: Material | Structure | Key detail
  [1.0] **Graphene** | **Single atom-thick layer** of graphite; **2D** hexagonal (honeycomb) lattice | Made of **carbon only**; thinnest and **strongest material** (~200 times steel; stronger than steel, brass, German silver); excellent electrical and heat conductor; ~97% transparent; flexible
  [1.1] **Fullerene C60** | Hollow **cage** ("buckyball", soccer ball): **12 pentagons + 20 hexagons**, 60 C atoms | A **carbon allotrope** (Teflon is the fluorine polymer); semiconductor; drug delivery, lubricants, catalysts
  [1.2] **Carbon nanotubes (CNTs)** | **Rolled graphene** cylinders (**1D** tubes) | Stronger than steel by weight, light; metallic or semiconducting; drug delivery, biosensors, batteries
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Graphene uses** | **Conducting electrodes for touch screens, LCDs and OLEDs**, fast transistors, flexible circuits, sensors, batteries and supercapacitors
  [2.1] **CNT uses** | Drug and antigen carriers; biochemical sensors (CNT biosensors)
  [2.2] CNT degradation | Can be degraded by some bacteria, fungi and enzymes
  [2.3] CNT and blood | Trigger platelet activation and clotting, so unsuited to artificial blood capillaries
[3] h: Discoverers
[4] table: Material | Discovery | Key detail
  [4.0] Graphene | Isolated **2004** by **Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov** (Manchester) | **Nobel Physics 2010**

  [4.2] **CNTs** | **Sumio Iijima (1991)** | –

### part 2: Nanoscale – Size, Units and Properties
[0] h: Size and units
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **1 nm** | **10^-9 m** (one billionth of a metre)
  [1.1] **1 Å** | **10^-10 m**, so **1 nm = 10 Å**
  [1.2] **Nanoparticle size** | **1-100 nm** (0.1-1 nm is the atomic scale); a particle with at least one dimension **below 10^-7 m (100 nm)** is a **nanoparticle**
  [1.3] **Scale** | Human hair ~80,000-100,000 nm; red blood cell ~7,000 nm; virus ~50-100 nm; DNA width ~2 nm
[2] h: Properties
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **Why different** | Very high **surface-area-to-volume ratio** (more reactive, catalytic) + **quantum size effect** (discrete energy levels); also superparamagnetism, lower melting point, self-assembly
  [3.1] Friction | The huge surface-to-volume ratio makes friction and wear dominant at the nanoscale – the most important property of nanomaterials
  [3.2] **Device miniaturisation** | **Nanotechnology**, the 21st-century technology
  [3.3] **Nanogold** | Gold nanoparticles show **surface plasmon resonance** – look **red, purple (or blue)** instead of yellow, yet stay gold ("**blue gold with properties similar to gold**"); biocompatible; conductive inks and lateral-flow test kits
[4] h: Quantum structures
[5] table: Structure | Nano-confined dimensions | Example
  [5.0] **Quantum well** | **One** dimension nano, two large (2D) | Thin films
  [5.1] **Quantum wire** | Two dimensions nano (1D) | Nanowires, nanotubes
  [5.2] **Quantum dot** | All three nano (0D) | **Semiconductor nanostructure**, about **2-10 nm**
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] **Quantum dots** | Size-tuned colour – **smaller dots → blue**, larger → red; QLED displays, bio-imaging, solar cells
  [6.1] Nobel | **Nobel Chemistry 2023 – Bawendi, Brus, Ekimov**
[7] table: Nanomaterial | Use
  [7.0] **Nanoporous materials** | **Separation and catalysis**
  [7.1] Nanofibres | Filtration, smart clothing, scaffolds
  [7.2] Nanocrystals | LEDs, imaging

### part 0: Quantum Computing and Supercomputers
[0] h: Quantum terms
[1] table: Term | Meaning
  [1.0] **Qubit** | Basic unit of quantum information – used in **quantum computing**
  [1.1] **Superposition** | A quantum system can be in **multiple states at once until measured** (qubit = 0 and 1)
  [1.2] Entanglement | Links qubits' states regardless of distance
  [1.3] **Decoherence** | Loss of quantum state to the environment – the main barrier
  [1.4] Shor's algorithm / Q-Day | Can break public-key cryptography; Q-Day = the day it becomes able to
  [1.5] **Quantum key distribution (QKD)** | Bits sent as **polarised photons** (optical fibre or satellite); sender and receiver compare a small part of the data – any **discrepancy reveals eavesdropping**; if none, a secure key is generated
[2] b: **Applications:** **cryptography**, **drug discovery**, **financial modelling**, logistics, materials.
[3] h: Machines

### part 1: Optical Fibre and Total Internal Reflection
[0] h: Total internal reflection (TIR)
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Conditions | Light goes from a **denser → rarer** medium **and** the angle of incidence is **> critical angle**
  [1.1] Result | Light is reflected **with no loss of intensity**
  [1.2] Refractive index | **Diamond (~2.42) > sapphire (~1.77) > dense flint glass > glass (~1.5) > fused quartz > water > air**
  [1.3] Possible TIR | **Diamond → glass**, **diamond → air**; never air → water/glass
  [1.4] Speed of light | Higher refractive index = **slower light** (among **glass**, water, air, vacuum: slowest in glass)
[2] table: Phenomenon / device | Principle
  [2.0] **Optical fibre** | **TIR** (successive TIRs let light follow a bent, zig-zag path)
  [2.1] **Endoscope** | Bundle of optical fibres, **TIR**
  [2.2] **Brilliance of diamond** | **TIR** (critical angle ≈ 24.4°)
  [2.3] **Mirage** (desert) | **TIR** in hot, rarer air near the ground
  [2.4] Glass beads on traffic signs | **TIR**
  [2.5] Crack in glass seen obliquely | Looks **silvery** (TIR)
  [2.6] **Rainbow** | **Dispersion + (total) internal reflection** + refraction
  [2.7] Stick bent in water | **Refraction**
  [2.8] Sea looks blue | **Reflection of sky + scattering** of light by water
[3] h: Optical fibre communication
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] Structure | Thin strand of **glass (silica)** or plastic; **core (higher n) + cladding (lower n)**
  [4.1] Signal | **Pulses of light (light waves)**
  [4.2] Merits | **Fastest** medium vs copper, radio, satellite; very **low power**; **free from radio-frequency interference**
  [4.3] Main use | **Communication** (telecom, internet)
  [4.4] Maker in India | **Sterlite (Sterlite Tech)**
  [4.5] Modes | **Single-mode** = long distances; **multi-mode** = short runs
  [4.6] **Light source** | **Semiconductor laser (laser diode)** – narrow, coherent, high-speed; LEDs only for short, low-speed multimode runs

[1] table: Fact | Key detail

  [1.1] Cryogenic liquids | Liquid nitrogen, hydrogen and oxygen; obtaining/using liquid O2 or H2 at industrial level = **cryogenics**

[0] table: Fact | Key detail
