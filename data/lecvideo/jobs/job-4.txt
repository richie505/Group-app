## topic group2 1-31 | Decline of the Mauryan Empire – Competing Explanations
### part 0: Kharavela of Kalinga – the Hathigumpha Inscription
[0] h: Hathigumpha inscription
[1] b: **Hathigumpha inscription** (2nd–1st c. BCE): the year-by-year record of **Kharavela** of Kalinga.
[2] b: **Place:** **Udayagiri** hill near Bhubaneswar, Odisha.
[3] b: **Udayagiri and Khandagiri:** Both natural and artificial caves; **Udayagiri 18, Khandagiri 15** caves.
[4] b: **Dynasty:** **Mahameghavahana** family of the **Chedi** line.
[5] b: **Religion:** A **Jaina** – the king who "heavily leaned towards Jainism"; gave caves to Jain monks; brought back from Magadha the **Jina image** taken by the Nandas.
[6] b: **Campaigns:** Sent an army west **ignoring Satakarni**; raided Magadha; southern victories; canal work begun by a Nanda king.
[7] b: **Krishna river = "Kannabenna"** (Kanhabemna) in the Hathigumpha inscription.
[8] b: **Other pairs:** Nasik – Gautami Balashri; **Nanaghat – Naganika**; Junagadh – Rudradaman.
### part 1: End of the Dynasty and the Successors
[0] b: **Brihadratha**, the last Maurya and **7th king after Ashoka**, was killed by his **senani Pushyamitra Sunga** at an **army review** – a military coup.
[1] table: Successor | Key detail
  [1.0] **Sungas** | **Immediate successors of the Mauryas in Magadha**; dynasty founded **187 BC**
  [1.1] **Satavahanas** | **Succeeded the Mauryas in the Deccan**; earlier Mauryan feudatories; founder **Simuka (Srimukha)**; the Puranas call them **Andhra-bhrityas**
  [1.2] **Kanvas** | **Vasudeva Kanva** killed the last Sunga **Devabhuti**; **four** kings (c. 73–28 BC); the last, **Susharman**, fell to the **Satavahanas**
[2] b: **Pushyamitra:** Brahmana of the **Bharadvaja gotra**, **Senapati**; two Ashvamedhas (**Ayodhya inscription of Dhanadeva**); **Malavikagnimitram** – grandson **Vasumitra** beat a Yavana army on the Sindhu.
[3] b: **Persecution debate:** the Divyavadana calls Pushyamitra a persecutor, yet **Sanchi and Bharhut** grew under the Sungas.
### part 2: Theories of Decline → Historian
[0] h: Theories of decline
[1] table: Theory | Historian | Core argument
  [1.0] Brahmanical reaction | **H.P. Shastri** | Pro-Buddhist policy and ban on sacrifices angered Brahmins; the Sungas and Kanvas were Brahmins
  [1.1] Ashoka's pacifism | **H.C. Raychaudhuri** | Non-violence after Kalinga dulled the army
  [1.2] Financial crisis | **D.D. Kosambi** | Army, bureaucracy and gifts to the Sangha emptied the treasury; heavy taxes even on actors and prostitutes
  [1.3] Over-centralisation, no national consciousness | **Romila Thapar** | Rule depended on the king; weak successors
  [1.4] Oppressive provincial rule | **Divyavadana** evidence | Revolts at Taxila against wicked officials
[2] b: **Financial-crisis evidence:** spending on **Dhamma missions** and the **Sangha**; **debasement of later punch-marked silver coins**; gold images melted.
[3] b: **Other factors:** spread of iron and material culture to outlying areas; neglect of the **north-west frontier** while China built the Great Wall (c. 220 BC).
### part 3: Later Mauryas, Division and Foreign Threats
[0] b: **Later Mauryas:** Kunala, Dasharatha, **Samprati** (Jain patron), Shalishuka, Devavarman, Shatadhanvan, **Brihadratha** – the Vishnu Purana lists seven successors.
[1] table: Part / threat | Ruler / power
  [1.0] Western part | **Kunala, later Samprati**; Indo-Greeks supplanted it by **180 BC**
  [1.1] Eastern part | Pataliputra – down to Brihadratha
  [1.2] North-west | Bactrian Greeks **Demetrius** and **Menander**
  [1.3] Hindu Kush, c. 206 BC | **Antiochus III** took tribute from **Subhagasena**
  [1.4] Great Wall of China (c. 220 BC) | Diverted Scythian tribes westward and southward towards India
  [1.5] Andhra / Deccan | **Satavahanas**, former feudatories
  [1.6] Vidarbha | **Yajnasena** (Malavikagnimitram)
[2] b: **Menander = Milinda**; capital **Sagala (Sialkot)**; dialogue with **Nagasena** in the Milindapanha.
[3] b: There was no permanent alliance with the Indo-Greeks.

## topic group2 1-33 | Mauryan Economy, Society, Urban Life and Art – Pillars, Sarnath Capital, Barabar Caves, Didarganj Yakshi
### part 0: Stupas – Sanchi, Bharhut and the Stupa Cult
[0] b: **Stupa cult:** the idea predates Buddhism (Vedic burial mounds); **Ashoka popularised it** – by tradition he redistributed the relics of the 8 original stupas into **84,000**. Stupas held relics of the Buddha and monks and stood in towns **near trade routes**, funded by merchants and guilds – **not in rural areas**.
[1] b: **Sanchi Stupa was built by Ashoka** (brick core).
[2] table: Stupa | Key detail
  [2.0] **Sanchi** (ancient **Kakanaya / Kaknam**) | Raisen district, MP, near Vidisha; Ashoka's brick core → **Sunga** stone casing, railings and toranas → **Satavahana** carving of gateways → Gupta shrines; considered the **best** stupa; **oldest stone structure in India** among Ajanta, Konark, Brihadeeswara
  [2.1] Sanchi No. 2 / No. 3 | Relics of 10 monks (incl. Dhamma Mahamatras) / **Sariputta and Mahamoggallana**
  [2.2] **Bharhut** | Satna, MP; **Sunga**, c. 150 BCE; found by Cunningham
  [2.3] Dharmarajika | Sarnath and Taxila

### part 2: Scientists – Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara
[0] table: Scientist | Works | Known for
