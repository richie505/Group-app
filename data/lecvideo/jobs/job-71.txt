### part 5: Orbits and Launch Physics
[0] table: Term | Meaning
  [0.0] **LEO** | **Low Earth Orbit** (up to ~2,000 km)
  [0.1] SSO | Sun-synchronous, near-polar; passes a place at the **same local solar time** – PSLV's speciality
  [0.2] **GTO** | **Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit**: elliptical, apogee near 36,000 km – GSLV, LVM3
  [0.3] GEO | Geostationary orbit over the equator; satellite appears **fixed in the sky** (reached via GSLV)
  [0.4] Apogee / perigee | **Farthest / nearest** point of an orbit
  [0.5] Strap-on booster | Extra motors fixed to the core for lift-off thrust
  [0.6] **Molniya satellites (Russia)** | **Communication over polar regions** (also Sirius XM satellite radio)
[1] h: Launch direction and site
[2] b: **Eastward launch:** satellites are launched **eastward** to use the Earth's **west-to-east rotation** (a free speed boost); sites **nearer the equator** gain most. The boost does not give escape velocity – an orbiting satellite never reaches escape velocity.
[3] b: **Polar / SSO launches** go **southward** – from Sriharikota a **dog-leg around Sri Lanka**.
[4] b: **Sriharikota** (SDSC-SHAR, Andhra Pradesh) is on the **east coast**: rockets fly over the sea; all Indian orbital launches, including private ones, go from here.

### part 2: Geostationary Orbit – Physics of a Communication Satellite
[0] table: Orbit | Key detail
  [0.0] Low Earth | **160–2,000 km**; ISS ~**400 km**
  [0.1] **Geostationary (GEO)** | About **36,000 km** (**35,786 km**) above the equator = orbit **radius ~42,000 km** (**42,164 km** from Earth's centre); period **24 hours (1 day)**
  [0.2] Polar | Passes over the poles
  [0.3] Elliptical | Apogee and perigee differ
  [0.4] Circular | Centripetal force equals gravitational force
[1] h: Physics of a geostationary satellite
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] Motion | Revolves **in the same direction as the Earth rotates** (west to east) with the same period – appears fixed over one point
  [2.1] Height | **Fixed height**, independent of the satellite's **mass**
  [2.2] What keeps it in orbit | **Centripetal force**, supplied by the **Earth's gravitational pull on the satellite** (not a rocket engine, not the Sun)
  [2.3] Energy | **None needed** to keep orbiting – no drag in near-vacuum; solar panels only power the instruments
  [2.4] **Global TV** | **Geostationary satellites** relay TV across the world (three cover nearly the whole globe); ground and space waves are short-range
  [2.5] **Uplink / downlink** | Sending data **up to the satellite / network** = **uplink**; receiving from it = **downlink**
[3] b: Launcher → elliptical **GTO** → the satellite's own motor raises it to **GEO**.

### part 0: Electromagnetic Spectrum, Sensors and Bands
[0] h: Order of the spectrum
[1] b: Wavelength falls: **radio (incl. microwave) → infrared → visible (red … violet) → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays**.
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Colour** | Set by the light's **wavelength**; **red** longest and **violet** shortest in visible light
  [2.1] **Infrared** | Longer than red
  [2.2] Gamma rays | Shortest of all
  [2.3] **X-rays** | Strongly darken (expose) **photographic plates and film** – the basis of radiography
[3] h: Passive and active sensors
[4] table: Type | Energy | Examples
  [4.0] **Passive** | Reflected sunlight or emitted heat | Cameras (visible, infrared, multispectral), radiometers, **MODIS**
  [4.1] **Active** | Sends its own pulse, reads the echo | **Radar/SAR** (microwave), **laser / LiDAR**, altimeters
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] **LiDAR** | **Light Detection And Ranging**: pulsed laser measures distances → elevation, 3D terrain, canopy height
  [5.1] **Airborne Laser Terrain Mapping (ALTM)** | Airborne LiDAR – fast, accurate, cost-effective 3D picture of the ground, mainly for mapping and survey
  [5.2] Laser uses | **LASIK**, LiDAR, holography
  [5.3] **MRI** | Uses magnets and radio waves, not a laser
[6] h: Which band for what
[7] table: Band | Use
  [7.0] Optical (visible/NIR) | Reflected light; needs daylight and clear sky
  [7.1] **Thermal infrared** | Emitted heat → **volcanic eruptions**, forest fires, urban heat
  [7.2] **Microwave (radio region)** – SAR | **Penetrates clouds** and darkness → all-weather imaging of cloud-covered disaster areas
  [7.3] Hyperspectral | Hundreds of narrow bands → materials and chemicals
[8] x: Cloud-covered disaster area → **radio/microwave** band; visible light cannot pass through cloud.

### part 7: Orbits for Earth Observation and Communication
[0] table: Orbit | Altitude | Use
  [0.0] **LEO** (Low Earth Orbit) | **200–2,000 km** | Earth observation, ISS
  [0.1] **SSPO** (sun-synchronous polar) | ~**500–900 km** | Most EO satellites (IRS, Cartosat, NISAR)
  [0.2] **MEO** | **2,000–35,786 km** | Navigation (GPS)
  [0.3] **GEO** (geostationary) | **35,786 km** (**22,236 miles**) over the equator | Communication, weather; appears fixed
  [0.4] **GSO** (geosynchronous, inclined) | Same height | Figure-8 track; NavIC's **4** inclined satellites
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Geostationary** | Geosynchronous + **circular** + in the **plane of the equator**; also called **Clarke's orbit**
  [1.1] Period | One sidereal day, **23 h 56 min 4 s**
  [1.2] Height in miles | **22,236** is the height in miles, not km
  [1.3] **Sun-synchronous** | Passes each place at the **same local solar time** → even lighting
  [1.4] Why a satellite does not fall | Earth's gravity **provides the centripetal acceleration** for its orbit
  [1.5] LEO vs geosynchronous imagers | LEO: finer detail, periodic revisit; geosynchronous: coarser detail, near-continuous viewing

### part 10: Space Physics – Escape Velocity, Re-entry, Lagrange Points
[0] table: Term | Meaning
  [0.0] **Escape velocity** | Minimum speed to escape a body's gravity; **Earth 11.2 km/s**
  [0.1] Orbital velocity | ~7.8-7.9 km/s in low Earth orbit
  [0.2] Lagrange points | **Five** points (L1-L5) where a small object stays balanced; Sun-Earth L1 ~1.5 million km
  [0.3] Soft landing | Controlled low-speed touchdown
  [0.4] Hohmann transfer | Fuel-saving elliptical transfer with two burns (used by MOM)
[1] b: An object thrown at **8 km/s** (below 11.2 km/s) **returns to Earth**; strictly, a horizontal 8 km/s above the air would orbit.
[2] b: **No atmosphere on the Moon:** low gravity, so its escape velocity is **less than the RMS speed** of gas molecules, which escape.
[3] b: **Re-entry blackout:** a returning shuttle heats up and **ionises the surrounding air**, so radio contact is lost for a while.
[4] b: **Oberth effect:** an engine burn gives the **maximum gain when made at the highest-speed point** (perigee); lunar-mission orbit raising used repeated **perigee burns**, each raising the apogee, to save fuel.

### part 1: Formation, Motto, Vision and Mission
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] Formed | **1958**, by amalgamating the **Technical Development Establishments (TDEs)** of the Indian Army, the **Directorate of Technical Development & Production (DTDP)** and the **Defence Science Organisation (DSO)**
  [0.1] **DRDO** | **Defence Research and Development Organisation** – R&D wing of the **Ministry of Defence**
  [0.2] HQ | **DRDO Bhawan, New Delhi**
  [0.3] Motto | **"Balasya Mulam Vigyanam"** – the source of strength is science
  [0.4] Growth | Started with **10 establishments**; now about **41 laboratories and 5 DRDO Young Scientist Laboratories** (older count **about 52** labs, the pre-merger figure)
  [0.5] Achievements | Agni and Prithvi missiles, LCA **Tejas**, **Pinaka** MBRL, **Akash** air defence, radars and EW systems
[1] b: **Vision:** empower the nation with state-of-the-art indigenous **defence and security** technologies (older wording: make India prosperous through a world-class S&T base and give the Services a decisive edge with internationally competitive systems).
[2] h: Mission (three points)
[3] b: (1) Design, develop and lead to production **state-of-the-art sensors, weapon systems, platforms** and allied equipment; (2) **technological solutions** to optimise **combat effectiveness** and the **well-being of troops**; (3) **infrastructure, quality manpower and a strong indigenous technology base**. International collaboration is not part of the mission.
