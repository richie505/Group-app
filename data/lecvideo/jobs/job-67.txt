### part 2: Coral Reefs – Nature, Conditions and Types
[0] h: Nature
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Built from | **Calcium carbonate** skeletons of tiny colonial **coral polyps** (phylum **Cnidaria**); the CaCO3 is **aragonite** (**oyster shells** too are **calcium carbonate**)
  [1.1] **Mutualism with zooxanthellae** | Single-celled dinoflagellate algae photosynthesise and give the coral **food and colour**; the coral gives **shelter and CO2/nutrients**
  [1.2] **"Rainforests of the Sea"** | Marine counterpart of **tropical rain forests** for their **species richness**: under **1%** of the ocean floor, over **25%** of marine species
  [1.3] Services | Reefs **break storm waves** and prevent coastal erosion; nurseries, fisheries, tourism
  [1.4] **Biorock (mineral accretion)** | Small electric current on a steel frame precipitates CaCO3 – **restores damaged reefs** (ZSI trial, Gulf of Kachchh)
[2] h: Growth conditions
[3] table: Condition | Requirement
  [3.0] Temperature | Not below about **20°C** (**20–21°C** minimum); optimum **23–25°C**; tropical seas
  [3.1] Light | **Photic**, shallow water – under about **50 m** (not over 180 ft; ideal under 100 ft)
  [3.2] Water | **Clean, sediment-free**, normal sea salinity (about **3.5%**, not 6%); freshwater and very saline water harm growth
  [3.3] Avoid | Muddy river mouths, cold currents
[4] h: Reef types
[5] table: Type | Feature | Example
  [5.0] **Fringing** | Close to shore; shallow lagoon or none; **commonest in India** | Andamans, Gulf of Mannar
  [5.1] **Barrier** | Parallel to the coast, separated by a **wide, deep lagoon** | **Great Barrier Reef**
  [5.2] **Atoll** | **Ring / horseshoe** reef enclosing a lagoon, no central land | **Lakshadweep**
[6] b: **Subsidence theory** of reef formation: **Charles Darwin (1842)** – fringing reef → barrier reef → atoll as the island slowly sinks (Daly: glacial-control theory).
### part 3: Coral Bleaching and Ocean Acidification
[0] h: Coral bleaching
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Meaning** | Stressed polyps **expel (eject) their zooxanthellae** and lose pigment, turning **white**; a bleached coral is **stressed, not necessarily dead** (it can recover if conditions improve)
  [1.1] **Most significant cause** | **Global warming** – rising sea-surface temperature, **marine heatwaves** (1°C above normal for weeks can trigger it)
  [1.2] Other causes | UV, turbidity, pollution, salinity change, **extreme low tides**, **ocean acidification**, El Nino
[2] table: Global bleaching event | Period | Note
  [2.0] 1st | **1998** | Strong El Nino
  [2.1] 2nd | **2010** | –
  [2.2] 3rd | **2014–2017** | **68.2%** of reef areas
  [2.3] 4th | **2023 – about mid-2025** | About **84%** of reefs, **83 countries** (NOAA–ICRI)
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] El Nino link | Every strong El Nino since 1998 came with a global bleaching event
  [3.1] Recovery | Takes **more than 15 years**
  [3.2] GCRMN 2026 | Major disturbances now every **4–6 years**; **87%** of reefs faced heat stress in 2024
  [3.3] **Andaman** mass bleaching | Linked to about a **2°C** rise in Indian Ocean temperature
[4] h: Ocean acidification
[5] b: CO2 + water → **carbonic acid** → bicarbonate + hydrogen (hydronium) ions → **pH falls** (sea water about **8.1**, alkaline) → **carbonate ions fall** → corals, calcareous plankton and shellfish **form shells less easily**.
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] CO2 uptake | Oceans absorb about **30–40%** of human CO2
  [6.1] Worst in | **Cold seas** (higher CO2 solubility)
  [6.2] Nicknames | **"Evil twin of global warming"**, "the other CO2 problem"
