### part 0: Biosphere Reserves and the MAB Programme
[0] h: Concept
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Biosphere Reserve (BR)** | Large terrestrial / coastal / marine area recognised under **UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere (MAB) Programme** (launched **1971**); "learning places for sustainable development"
  [1.1] **Purpose** | Conserve the **genetic diversity** of the whole landscape **together with the traditional life of local / tribal people** – the strategy for conserving biodiversity together with traditional human life
  [1.2] Legal status | Designation **does not change legal status or ownership**; may contain one or more NPs / sanctuaries; not a WPA 1972 protected-area category (those are NP, sanctuary, conservation reserve, community reserve)
  [1.3] Process | **IN-MAB Committee (MoEFCC)** recommends sites; State prepares the management plan
  [1.4] Funding | **60:40** (**90:10** for NE and three Himalayan States)
  [1.5] **Seville Strategy (1995)** | Added social, cultural and economic needs
[2] table: Zone | What is allowed
  [2.0] Core | Strictly protected (usually NP/WLS); **no human activity**
  [2.1] Buffer | Limited activity: research, education, training, limited recreation
  [2.2] Transition | **Settlements**, farming, forestry, tourism

### part 2: Gene Banks, Seed Vaults and Cryopreservation
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **Cryopreservation** | In **liquid nitrogen at −196 °C** (the gas used in cryo-banks is **nitrogen**); metabolism stops
  [0.1] Stored | Gametes, embryos, seeds, pollen, tissues, DNA – **semen straws / cattle sperm for artificial insemination**
  [0.2] Seed storage | Seeds keep longest in **cool and dry** conditions (low moisture and temperature slow respiration and fungi)
  [0.3] **Field gene banks** | Living collections for **vegetatively propagated crops and crops with recalcitrant seeds** (mango, coconut, banana)

### part 3: In-situ and Ex-situ Conservation
[0] table: Basis | In-situ (on-site) | Ex-situ (off-site)
  [0.0] Where | Within the **natural habitat** | Outside it, in a controlled setting
  [0.1] Examples | **National park, wildlife sanctuary, biosphere reserve**, reserved / protected forest, nature reserve, **sacred groves** | **Botanical garden**, zoo, safari park, **seed / gene bank**, **cryobank (cryopreservation)**, tissue culture, captive breeding
  [0.2] Saves | Whole ecosystem and its genetic diversity | The species or its genes
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Genetic diversity in **National Parks** | Maintained by **in-situ conservation**
  [1.1] **In-situ is generally most effective** | Species keep interacting with their environment
  [1.2] Ex-situ | Helps education, research and reintroduction; needs space and money; cannot save the ecosystem
  [1.3] **Captive breeding** | For species whose habitat is lost – Gangetic gharials bred and released; 10 captive-bred **Asian Giant Tortoises** released in **Ntangki NP (Nagaland)**
  [1.4] **Meristem culture** | Virus-free propagation
  [1.5] "Biological diversity" | Coined by **Raymond F. Dasmann**
### part 4: Protected-Area Categories, Eco-sensitive Zones and Global Coverage
[0] h: Categories under WPA 1972
[1] table: Category | Key feature
  [1.0] **National Park** | Declared by State (Centre also may); **no rights, no grazing, no collection of biomass / forest produce**; boundary altered only on NBWL recommendation
  [1.1] **Wildlife Sanctuary** | Notified by the **State Government** under the WPA; some rights allowed; grazing and firewood collection regulated by the **Chief Wildlife Warden**
  [1.2] Conservation Reserve | 2002/2003 amendment; **government land**, usually adjoining / linking PAs
  [1.3] **Community Reserve** | 2002/2003 amendment; **private or community land** a community volunteers; declared by the **State Government**; managed by a **Community Reserve Management Committee**; sanctuary restrictions (no hunting) apply; residents' rights not affected; protects **fauna, flora and traditional / cultural values and practices**
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] Biomass collection | Not allowed in **National Parks**; allowed in BR transition zones and Ramsar wetlands, regulated in sanctuaries
  [2.1] **Network (2026)** | **1,134 PAs** (981 in 2020): 109 NPs, 573 WLS, 127 conservation, 75 community reserves; ~**5.32%** of India's area; 58 tiger reserves
  [2.2] First community reserve | **Keshopur-Chhamb (Punjab)**
  [2.3] Biosphere reserve | Not a PA category
  [2.4] **Clearance** | Projects affecting wildlife in or around PAs – **Standing Committee of the National Board for Wildlife (NBWL)**
[3] h: Eco-sensitive Zones (ESZ)
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **Purpose** | **"Shock absorbers"** / transition zones around NPs and sanctuaries (MoEFCC guidelines); minimise forest depletion and human–animal conflict
  [4.1] Law | **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**
  [4.2] Activities | Prohibited: mining, polluting industry, major hydro; regulated: hotels, tree felling; permitted: agriculture, organic farming
  [4.3] Width | **Wildlife Action Plan 2002-16** / MoEF 2011 guidelines: up to **10 km**; **Supreme Court (June 2022)**: minimum **1 km**, no mining inside PAs or within 1 km
[5] h: Global coverage
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] **2014** | Protected areas covered **15.2% of land** and **8.4% of coastal-marine areas** (under national jurisdiction)
  [6.1] **30x30** | Target 3 of the **Kunming-Montreal GBF** (2022): 30% of land and oceans by 2030
  [6.2] **OECMs** | Count towards 30x30; recognised by conservation **outcomes**; do **not change land ownership or legal authority** (sacred groves, greenbelts, wetlands); India's first: Aravalli Biodiversity Park, Gurugram (2022)
  [6.3] **WDPA** | Run by **UNEP-WCMC** with IUCN
  [6.4] **Ocean protection (Apr 2026)** | Nearly **10%** of the ocean protected, only ~**3%** fully / highly; 1.66% of the high seas

## topic group2 5-75 | Forest and Wildlife Legal Framework – Ifa 1927, Fca 1980, Wpa 1972, Bd Act 2002
### part 0: Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 – Year, Scope and Key Sections
[0] h: Year and scope
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Enacted | **By Parliament in 1972** (assent 9 Sept 1972); first comprehensive wildlife law
  [1.1] Protects | **Wild animals, birds and plants** and their habitats
  [1.2] Regulates | **National Parks and Wildlife Sanctuaries**
  [1.3] **Poaching** | Illegal hunting of wild animals – barred by the Act
  [1.4] Structure | **66 sections, 7 chapters**
  [1.5] Amended | 1982, 1986, 1991, 1993, 2002, 2006, 2013, **2022**
[2] h: Key sections
[3] table: Section | Provision
  [3.0] 2(1) | "Animal" = amphibians, birds, mammals, reptiles, their young, eggs of birds and reptiles
  [3.1] 2(37) | "Wildlife"
  [3.2] 9 | Hunting of scheduled animals **prohibited**, subject to exceptions
  [3.3] 11 | Animal **dangerous to human life** (or crops), or diseased or disabled beyond recovery, may be captured / killed with the Chief Wildlife Warden's permission
  [3.4] 12 | Permits for education, research, scientific management, translocation
  [3.5] 38J | **Teasing, molesting, injuring or feeding** animals in a zoo prohibited
  [3.6] 39(1) | Wild animals = **State Government property**; in a Centre-declared sanctuary / NP = Central Government
  [3.7] 38V(3) | Tiger Conservation Plan for every TR (2006)
  [3.8] 62 | Centre may notify a non-Schedule I animal as vermin
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **Gharial, Indian wild ass, wild buffalo** | Cannot be hunted by anyone except as the law permits
  [4.1] Protection | Equal inside or outside PAs; mere *apprehension* of danger is not enough to kill a protected animal
  [4.2] **Protected animal may be killed** | When it endangers human life, and when it is diseased (two separate grounds)
  [4.3] **Mining** | Barred inside NPs and sanctuaries; SC bars mining within **1 km** of every NP / sanctuary unless a larger ESZ is notified
### part 1: WPA Schedules and the 2022 Amendment
[0] h: Schedules
[1] table: Before 2022 (6 schedules) | After 2022 (4 schedules)
  [1.0] I and II: absolute protection (tiger, rhino) | I: highest protection
  [1.1] III and IV: lesser (game species) | II: lower protection
  [1.2] V: **vermin** (common crow, fruit bats, rats, mice) | III: **protected plants**

[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Number of schedules** | 6 in the 1972 Act; 4 since the 2022 amendment (in force 1 April 2023)
  [2.1] **Schedule I effect** | A tortoise in Schedule I gets **the same protection as the tiger**
  [2.2] **Schedule VI (old)** | **Licence required to cultivate**; a specified plant cannot be picked, uprooted or traded without permission
  [2.3] **Flying fox** | A **fruit bat** (Pteropus); does not feed on blood; was **vermin (Schedule V)**, since 2022 in **Schedule II**
  [2.4] **Schedule I examples** | Asiatic lion, tiger, elephant, snow leopard, rhino, Gangetic dolphin, dugong, GIB, **blackbuck**
  [2.5] In news | Mugger (Kurnool, Sep 2026), rock eagle-owl (Dharmavaram, Aug 2026)
[3] h: 2022 amendment
[4] table: Fact | Key detail

  [4.2] Invasives | Centre regulates **invasive alien species** (African catfish, Lantana)
  [4.3] Other | Voluntary **surrender of captive animals without compensation**; Chief Wildlife Warden in every State; Centre may notify conservation reserves

## topic group2 5-82 | Forest Rights Act 2006, Campa, Compensatory Afforestation, Jfm, Agroforestry
### part 0: Afforestation, Forestry Terms and Their Benefits
[0] h: Forestry terms
[1] table: Term | Meaning
  [1.0] Afforestation | New forest on denuded, degraded or unproductive land
  [1.1] Reforestation | Re-establishing forest cover in existing / depleted forest areas
  [1.2] Social forestry | Trees for community needs; term used by the **National Commission on Agriculture (1976)**
  [1.3] Extension forestry | Trees along **roadsides, canals and railway strips**
  [1.4] Agroforestry | **Trees + crops + livestock** on the same land
  [1.5] **Miyawaki method** | Dense, multi-layered **mini-forests of native species in urban areas**; Japanese botanist **Akira Miyawaki**, **1970s**
  [1.6] **Seed bomb (seed ball)** | Ball of **mud/clay, dung and seeds** thrown on land to grow trees
  [1.7] **Craib's thinning** | Heavy early (advance) thinning of a regular plantation crop **in anticipation of suppression** (I.J. Craib, South Africa)
  [1.8] **Vanamahotsav** | **K.M. Munshi, 1950**; first week of July
[2] h: Benefits and criticism
[3] b: **Afforestation controls floods** and **checks soil erosion by running water** (roots bind soil, slow run-off); deforestation, overgrazing, urbanisation, tilling or removing topsoil worsen them.
[4] b: **Floods are worsened by** unscientific agriculture, disturbing natural drainage channels and colonising flood-plains and river-beds; reforestation reduces them.
[5] b: **Criticism of social forestry:** it drifted into **commercial monocultures** (eucalyptus); the fix is mixed native species.
[6] b: **Wild animals** help natural regeneration through **seed dispersal** (e.g. elephants, hornbills).

### part 2: Disposal and Treatment Methods
[0] h: Disposal and treatment methods
[1] table: Method | Process | Suited to
  [1.0] **Open dumping** | Untreated, uncovered, unsegregated waste on land | Not viable – flies, rats, leachate
  [1.1] **Landfill** | Most **common and economic** method in India; most urban household waste ends here | Residual waste; last resort
  [1.2] **Sanitary landfill** | Waste **compacted** in cells and **covered with earth every day**; **liners** (clay/plastic) + leachate collection protect groundwater; adopted as a **substitute for open-burning dumps** | Only residual inert waste; costly
  [1.3] **Composting (aerobic)** – **Indore method** | Microbes (bacteria, **fungi**) oxidise organics; material **turned at intervals**; **2–4 weeks** | Wet / organic waste
  [1.4] **Bangalore method (anaerobic)** | Pits with alternate layers of MSW and night soil; **4–6 months** | Organic waste
  [1.5] **Vermicomposting** | **Earthworms** break waste down (vermiculture) | Household organic waste
  [1.6] **Incineration** | Controlled **burning** at high heat in oxygen; cuts volume ~**90%**; steam for power; emits pollutants, toxic ash | Hazardous, infectious / biomedical
  [1.7] **Pyrolysis** | Thermo-chemical decomposition (heating) **without oxygen** → gas, oil, char (carbon-based fuels) | Mixed waste; costly
  [1.8] **Plasma gasification** | **Extreme thermal** process (electricity + very high temperature), no combustion → **syngas** (H2 + CO); inorganics → slag | All organic wastes
  [1.9] **RDF** | Shredded, dehydrated high-calorific dry MSW | Fuel in **cement kilns** and thermal plants
[2] b: **Pyrolysis and plasma gasification** are **waste-to-energy technologies**; compost and vermicompost give manure, not energy.
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **Compost** | Biodegradable waste decomposed in pits; rich in organic matter and nutrients; earthworms and microbes **speed up** decomposition
  [3.1] Composting microbes | Both aerobes and anaerobes degrade organic matter
  [3.2] **Vermiculture** | Science of **raising earthworms**
  [3.3] **Biomining** | Bacteria, **fungi** or plants extract metals from ores and solid wastes (composting also uses fungi)
  [3.4] Urban India uses | **Composting, incineration and landfilling**
  [3.5] Best way to reduce solid waste | **Recycling**
  [3.6] **Composting household waste** | Cuts methane; helps reduce global warming (burning household waste does not reduce GHG emissions)

### part 5: Solid Waste Management Rules and Indian Data
[0] h: Solid waste rules
[1] table: Rules | Key points
  [1.0] **MSW (Management and Handling) Rules 2000** | ULBs collect, **segregate**, store, transport, process, dispose; primary goal **promoting segregation**
  [1.1] **SWM Rules 2016** (8 Apr 2016) | **Replaced the MSW Rules 2000**; apply **beyond municipal areas** (urban agglomerations, census towns, industrial townships, railways, airports, defence, pilgrimage places); **3 streams** – wet (biodegradable), dry, domestic hazardous

  [1.3] SWM Rules 2016 – siting | **Exact criteria for siting landfills and waste-processing facilities**: landfill **100 m** from a river, **200 m** from a pond, **500 m** from highways / habitations, **20 km** from airports; industries within **100 km** of an RDF plant replace **5%** of fuel with RDF
  [1.4] **SWM Rules 2026** (under EPA **1986**; in force **1 Apr 2026**; replaced 2016) | **4 streams: wet, dry, sanitary, special care** (green, blue, red, black bins); landfills only for non-recyclable inert residue; higher landfill fee for unsegregated waste; legacy dumps mapped with time-bound biomining; **Environmental Compensation = polluter pays**
