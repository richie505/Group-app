[0] h: The Pact
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Signed | **5 March 1931** at Delhi (talks began 14 February 1931)
  [1.1] Background | The CWC, released unconditionally on 25 January 1931, authorised Gandhi to talk to Viceroy **Lord Irwin**
  [1.2] Mediators | **Tej Bahadur Sapru and M.R. Jayakar** (Liberals; with V.S. Srinivasa Sastri)

[2] h: Terms
[3] table: Irwin agreed | Irwin refused | Congress agreed

  [3.2] Return of confiscated lands **not yet sold to third parties** (lands already sold stayed sold) | – | Discuss federation, Indian responsibility and **reservations and safeguards** (defence, external affairs, minorities, **financial credit of India**) – Clause 2
  [3.3] **Salt-making** by coastal villagers **for own use** (not sale); peaceful picketing; withdrawal of ordinances | – | –
[4] b: The **18% reservation for depressed classes** in the Central Legislature belongs to the **Poona Pact** (1932).

[6] table: Person | View

  [6.1] **Sarojini Naidu** | Called Gandhi and Irwin the **"Two Mahatmas"**
[7] b: The Pact placed the Congress **on an equal footing** with the government; youth and the left opposed it because Bhagat Singh was not saved.

### part 2: Karachi Session, March 1931 – Fundamental Rights Resolution
[0] h: Karachi session, March 1931
[1] table: Fact | Key detail

[3] table: Fundamental Rights | Economic Programme

  [3.3] Religious neutrality of the State; free primary education | **State ownership of key industries, mines, transport**

## topic group2 1-151 | Individual Satyagraha, August Offer, Cripps Mission
### part 0: Cripps Mission, 1942 – Context and Proposals
[0] h: Context
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Arrival | **March 1942** (Delhi, 22-23 March); Cripps returned **mid-April 1942** after talks broke down
  [1.1] Sent by | British PM **Winston Churchill** (Conservative; PM 1940-45)
  [1.2] **Sir Stafford Cripps** | Left-wing **Labour** leader, Leader of the House of Commons, member of Churchill's **War Cabinet**; later also in the **Cabinet Mission (1946)**
  [1.3] Why sent | Japanese advance (Pearl Harbor Dec 1941; **Rangoon fell March 1942**); pressure from the Allies (**Roosevelt, Chiang Kai-shek**); Congress offer to help if power was transferred
[2] h: Proposals (Draft Declaration)
[3] table: Proposal | Key detail
  [3.0] Status | **Indian Union with Dominion status** soon **after the war**, free to leave the Commonwealth – complete independence was not offered
  [3.1] Constitution | **Constitution-making body after the war**, **elected by provincial lower houses** (proportional representation, electoral college **1/10th** of their size) + **nominees of the princes** – the first concrete plan for an **elected constituent assembly**
  [3.2] Provincial option | Any province (or state) unwilling could **stay outside** and form a separate Union – the **"fatal flaw"**; Pakistan conceded in principle
  [3.3] Minorities | Treaty between the constitution-making body and Britain to protect minorities
  [3.4] Interim | **Defence stays with Britain**; Governor-General's powers intact
[4] b: An executive council of equal representation came later, in the **Wavell Plan**.
[5] b: Cripps on the urgency: **"Here is a revolution taking place and we should move quickly."**
[6] x: **Cripps (March 1942):** a constitution-making body, but no full independence; provinces and states free to opt out; defence with Britain.

### part 5: August Offer, 1940
[0] h: August Offer, 1940
[1] b: Made by Viceroy **Lord Linlithgow** on **8 August 1940** (at Simla), in reply to Ramgarh / the Congress offer, when Britain was losing in Europe.
[2] table: Proposal | Key detail
  [2.0] Goal | **Dominion status**
  [2.1] Constitution | **Representative constitution-making body after the war** – "mainly" Indians to frame it
  [2.2] Executive | Expanded Viceroy's Executive Council with more Indians; **War Advisory Council**
  [2.3] Minorities | **No constitution without minorities' consent** (League's veto)
  [2.4] Left out | Immediate transfer of **defence** / a national government
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **Significance:** | **First time** the British conceded that **framing the constitution was primarily the Indians' responsibility**
  [3.1] Cripps plan (1942) | Made it "solely" Indian and gave the concrete elected body
  [3.2] **Responses:** | Congress **rejected** it – **Nehru: Dominion status "dead as a doornail"**; the League welcomed the veto but wanted partition
  [3.3] Constituent Assembly demand | First raised by **M.N. Roy (1934)**; Congress adopted it in 1935
[4] x: **August Offer** = Linlithgow, **8 Aug 1940** – first acceptance of an Indian-framed constitution.

## topic group2 1-152 | Quit India Movement 1942 - Causes, Course, Underground Activity, Repression, Significance
### part 0: The Quit India Resolution – Causes, Dates and Venue
[0] h: Why 1942
[1] table: Cause | Key detail
  [1.0] **Failure of the Cripps Mission** (March-April 1942) | No immediate transfer of power – the **main trigger**; QIM was launched **in response to the Cripps Proposals**
  [1.1] Japanese advance | Malaya, Singapore overrun; **Rangoon fell in March 1942**; Japanese troops at India's eastern border
  [1.2] Economic distress | Rising prices, shortage of rice and salt, boats commandeered in Bengal and Orissa
  [1.3] Loss of faith in British rule | Deposits withdrawn from banks; racist evacuation from South-East Asia ("Black Road" / "White Road")
[2] b: **British PM:** **Winston Churchill**; **Viceroy:** **Lord Linlithgow**.
[3] h: Meetings and dates
[4] table: Date | Meeting / place | What happened
  [4.0] 6 July 1942 | CWC (Wardha) | Gandhi first discussed his Quit India plan; **Congress President = Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** (1940-46)
  [4.1] **14 July 1942** | **CWC at Wardha** | Resolution accepted – the **"Wardha Resolution"**; Gandhi authorised to lead a non-violent mass struggle
  [4.2] **7-8 August 1942** | **AICC, Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay** (now **August Kranti Maidan**) | Resolution **ratified on 8 August** – the **"August Resolution"**; "Do or Die" speech the same night
  [4.3] **9 August 1942** | All India | Leaders arrested at dawn; the movement **began**
[5] h: The resolution
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] **Year:** | **1942**; other name of the movement – "August Kranti"
  [6.1] **Moved by:** | **Jawaharlal Nehru**, **seconded by Sardar Patel**
  [6.2] **President of the Congress when it passed:** | **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**
  [6.3] **Launched at:** | **Bombay** (Wardha was only the CWC meeting)
  [6.4] **Leader:** | **Mahatma Gandhi** was named leader of the struggle
  [6.5] Gandhi's paper | **Harijan**
  [6.6] Demands | **Immediate end of British rule**; a **provisional government** after withdrawal; free India to resist fascism; a **mass civil disobedience** struggle under Gandhi
[7] h: Gandhi's instructions (spelt out at Gowalia Tank, never formally issued)
[8] table: Group | Instruction
  [8.0] Government servants | **Do not resign** – declare allegiance to the Congress
  [8.1] Soldiers | **Do not leave the army** – but do not fire on compatriots
  [8.2] Princes | **Accept the sovereignty of your own people**
  [8.3] Students | Leave studies if confident
  [8.4] Peasants | Pay agreed rent to anti-government zamindars; none to pro-government ones
[9] h: Order of events
[10] b: **CDM (1930) → Individual Satyagraha (Oct 1940) → Cripps Mission (March 1942) → Quit India (Aug 1942)**; Congress ministries resigned in 1939 and the Executive Council was expanded in July 1941.
[11] b: **Gandhi (1942):** if the Congress did not back an immediate struggle, he would build a movement "**out of the sands of India**" that would be **larger than the Congress itself**.
[12] x: **Quit India resolution:** ratified by the AICC at Bombay on **8 August 1942**; movement began **9 August 1942**.

### part 3: Nature, Parties and Significance
[0] h: Nature
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Spontaneous:** | The arrests left it leaderless; historian **F.G. Hutchins** called it a **"Spontaneous Revolution"** (book, 1971)

  [1.2] Labour | Joined in: strikes in **Ahmedabad (textile mills, over three months)**, Bombay, Jamshedpur, Ahmednagar, Poona
  [1.3] **Storm centres:** | **Bihar and eastern UP** (Azamgarh, Ballia, Gorakhpur; Gaya, Bhagalpur, Saran), Midnapore, Maharashtra, Karnataka
