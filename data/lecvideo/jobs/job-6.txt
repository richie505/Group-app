  [0.0] **Qutbuddin Aibak** (1206-10) | Turkish slave of **Muhammad Ghori**; got Ghori's Indian territories; capital **Lahore**; **Lakh Baksh**; built **Quwwat-ul-Islam** mosque and Arhai Din ka Jhonpra; **began Qutub Minar**; patron of **Hasan Nizami** and Fakhr-i-Mudabbir; died playing **chaugan**

## topic group2 1-52 | Sultanate Provincial and Military Administration – Iqta, Dagh and Huliya, Army
### part 0: The Iqta System
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **Iqta** | Revenue of a territory **assigned** to an officer **in lieu of salary** – not ownership of land; holder = **iqtadar / muqti** (wali of a province)
  [0.1] **Origin** | An **Arabic / Caliphate** institution (land revenue to officers for limited periods; described in Nizam-ul-Mulk Tusi's **Siyasatnama**) – a **foreign Turkish import**, not an ancient Indian institution
  [0.2] **First iqta in India** | **Hansi**, granted by **Muhammad Ghori** to **Qutbuddin Aibak**
  [0.3] System | **Iltutmish introduced** the iqta as a **systematic institution** (iqtas to his Turkish officers)
  [0.4] **Muqti's duties** | Collect revenue, **maintain troops** from it, keep law and order, pay his men, send the **surplus (fawazil)** to the treasury; revenue was not deposited directly in the Sultan's treasury
  [0.5] **Fawazil** | The **excess amount** paid by the iqtadars to the exchequer after salary and troops
[1] x: Early Turkish administration was **essentially military** because the country was parcelled out as **iqtas among military leaders**.
[2] table: Sultan | Change
  [2.0] Iltutmish | Organised the system
  [2.1] Balban | Tried to resume old soldiers' iqtas; **khwajas** (auditors) checked muqtis
  [2.2] Alauddin, MBT | Strict control: income assessed, accountants, **cash pay**; enlarged **khalisa** (took back milk, idrarat, waqf lands)
  [2.3] Firoz | Relaxed control; iqtas became **hereditary**; **revived the jagir** – army efficiency fell
[3] b: Iqtas were **transferable** (muqtis rotated every 3-4 years) until Firoz; the iqta is the ancestor of the Mughal **jagir and mansab** system.

## topic group2 1-53 | Sultanate Economy – Kharaj, Jizya, Zakat, Khams; Agriculture, Towns, Trade and Craft
### part 0: Sharia Taxes and Cesses
[0] table: Tax | Nature | Payer
  [0.0] **Kharaj** | **Land tax**, usually 1/3 (up to 1/2) of produce | Non-Muslim cultivators
  [0.1] **Ushr** | **Land tax**, 1/10 (5-10%) of produce | Muslim landholders
  [0.2] **Jizya** | Poll / protection tax in place of military service | Non-Muslims
  [0.3] **Zakat** | Religious tax, about 2.5% of wealth | **Muslims only**
  [0.4] **Khams (Khums)** | **State's 1/5 share of war booty** (and treasure-troves) – not a tax on land produce | Army booty
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Khams in practice | **Alauddin and MBT took 4/5** of booty; **Firoz restored the sharia 1/5**
  [1.1] **Firoz Tughlaq** | Kept only the four sharia taxes – **kharaj, jizya, zakat, khams**; abolished about 24 cesses (incl. ghari, charai); added **haq-i-sharb**

### part 3: Sher Shah – Rise, Titles, Rajput Wars and Death
[0] h: Rise
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Birth name | **Farid (Farid Khan)**; of the **Sur** tribe
  [1.1] Father | **Hasan Sur**, an Afghan **jagirdar of Sasaram** (Bihar)
  [1.2] Education | Left home (1494 / 1501) and **studied Arabic and Persian at Jaunpur** – the "**Siraj-i-Hind**"; one of the most learned medieval rulers
  [1.3] Jagir | Managed his father's jagir from **1511**
  [1.4] Title "Sher Khan" | From **Bahar Khan** of Bihar, for killing a **tiger**
  [1.5] Career | Served Babur; deputy regent and de facto ruler of Bihar
  [1.6] Turning point | Victory at **Surajgarh** (1534); took **Gaur** (1538) and **Rohtas**
  [1.7] **Sher Khan's offer** | Surrender Bihar and pay 10 lakh dinars a year if allowed to keep Bengal – Humayun refused
[2] h: Titles in order
[3] table: Title | When
  [3.0] Sher Khan | From Bahar Khan (tiger)
  [3.1] **Hazrat-e-Ala** | After defeating **Nusrat Shah** of Bengal
  [3.2] **Sher Shah / Sultan** | After **Chausa, 26 June 1539**; khutba read and coins struck in his name

[5] h: Conquests and Rajput resistance
