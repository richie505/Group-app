## topic group2 2-56 | Separation of Powers and Checks and Balances
### part 0: Constitutional Government = Limited Government
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **Constitutional government** | By definition = **limited government** – a government **limited by the terms of the Constitution**
  [0.1] "**Persons may change but rules should not change**" | Principle of **constitutional government** (rule of law, not of persons)
  [0.2] Powers of government in a democracy | Limited by the **Constitution**
  [0.3] **Constitutionalism** | Limits + rule of law + checks and balances + judicial review + FRs; a **legislative majority cannot override constitutional limitations**
  [0.4] Marks of true democracy | Free and fair elections, respect for the individual, equality before law – more than "**rule by majority only**"
  [0.5] **PIL** | **Not indispensable** for the rule of law, which existed before PIL through **Arts. 14, 32, 226**
### part 1: Thinkers on the State and Limited Government
[0] table: Thinker / school | View
  [0.0] **Thomas Hobbes** (Leviathan, 1651) | **Absolute sovereign power** of the monarch
  [0.1] **John Locke** | Limited government, natural rights (life, liberty, property)
  [0.2] **J.-J. Rousseau** | **The Social Contract (1762)**; general will, popular sovereignty
  [0.3] **D.G. Ritchie** (*Natural Rights*, 1895) | **Customary (historical) rights** – rights grow out of custom
  [0.4] **Individualists** | State a **necessary evil** – keep it minimal
  [0.5] Anarchists | State an unnecessary evil – abolish it
### part 2: Separation of Powers – Montesquieu and the Indian Degree
[0] h: Montesquieu
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Montesquieu** | **Division of powers** best protects liberty; "**The Spirit of Laws**" (**1748**); drew on the **British** constitution
  [1.1] Best safeguard of liberty in a liberal democracy | **Separation of powers**
  [1.2] **Presidential** government | Operates on **separation of powers**
  [1.3] **Parliamentary** government | **Fusion** – ministers drawn from and responsible to the legislature
  [1.4] Division of powers between Centre and States | Federalism
  [1.5] Organ of the **people's elected representatives** | The **legislature** (judges are appointed; the media is not an organ of government)
[2] h: The Indian degree
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] Heads | **Nominal head** President; **real executive** Council of Ministers
  [3.1] **Ambedkar** | Preferred **responsibility over stability**; reasons: familiarity (1919/1935 Acts), diversity, avoiding American-style deadlock
  [3.2] Nature in India | **No rigid separation** – implicit, not absolute, **functional**; overlaps → checks and balances (**Ram Jawaya Kapur, 1955**)
  [3.3] Basic feature | Separation of powers (**Kesavananda, 1973**)
  [3.4] **Art. 50 (DPSP)** | Separate the judiciary from the executive **in the public services of the State**
  [3.5] Judiciary insulated | **Arts. 50, 121, 211, 235**
  [3.6] **Munro's Madras Regulations (1816)** | Reversed the Cornwallis separation – the **Collector** again got **magisterial and police powers**

## topic group2 2-57 | Constitutional Evolution – Regulating Act 1773 to the Government of India Act 1935
### part 0: Government of India Act, 1935 – Federation, Provincial Autonomy and the Constitution's Blueprint
[0] h: Basis and size
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Based on** | The **Simon Commission** report, the **Round Table Conferences** (the third RTC) and the **White Paper (1933)**
  [1.1] **Size** | **321 sections, 10 schedules** (14 parts) – the **longest Act** passed by the British Parliament up to then
  [1.2] **Nehru's verdict** | A **"Charter of Slavery"** / "a machine with strong brakes but no engine"
[2] h: Federation (never formed)
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **All-India Federation** | British provinces + princely states – the first provision for a **federal polity** in India; the Act's own name = **"Federation of India"** (Part II, s.5)
  [3.1] **Never came into being** | Joining was **optional** for the princes and the **rulers of princely states did not consent** to join
  [3.2] **Real British intention** | To use the princes to **counter-balance the nationalist leaders**
  [3.3] **Three lists** | **Federal (59 items), Provincial (54), Concurrent (36)** – first division of legislative powers between Centre and provinces; basis of today's Centre–State scheme
  [3.4] **Residuary powers** | **The Governor-General** (not the federal or provincial legislature, not the Governors)
  [3.5] **Dyarchy at the Centre** | Proposed: **defence and foreign affairs** = reserved subjects under the **Governor-General** – **never implemented**
  [3.6] **Bicameral Centre** (proposed) | **Federal Assembly + Council of States**
[4] h: Provinces
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] **Provincial autonomy** | The **most important feature**; it **abolished provincial dyarchy** (of 1919); responsible ministries from **April 1937**
  [5.1] **Bicameral legislatures in 6 of 11 provinces** | **Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, United Provinces** (Punjab was unicameral)
  [5.2] **Governors** | Kept special powers: **veto**, ordinances and power to **legislate on their own** (Governor's Acts)
  [5.3] Communal representation | **Extended** to **depressed classes, women and labour**
  [5.4] New provinces | **Burma separated** from India; **Sindh** carved from **Bombay**, **Orissa** from **Bihar**
  [5.5] **Franchise** | About **14%** of the population (about 3.5 crore voters); still **no adult franchise**
  [5.6] **1937 elections** | Congress formed ministries in most provinces, but not in **Punjab** (Unionist Party) or Bengal
[6] b: The 1935 Act **ended** provincial dyarchy and only **proposed** central dyarchy – it brought no dyarchy at **both** levels.
[7] h: Institutions
[8] table: Fact | Key detail
  [8.0] **Federal Court** (Delhi) | Inaugurated **1 October 1937**; first CJ **Sir Maurice Gwyer**; replaced by the Supreme Court in **1950**
  [8.1] **RBI** | Set up on **1 April 1935** under the **RBI Act, 1934**
  [8.2] Public Service Commissions | **Federal, Provincial and Joint PSCs** (no "All-India Services Commission")
  [8.3] **Council of India** | **Abolished**; the Secretary of State got a team of advisers instead
[9] h: Legacy in the Constitution
[10] table: Fact | Key detail
  [10.0] **Main source** of the Constitution | About **250 provisions / "nearly 75%"** – federal scheme, office of Governor, judiciary, PSCs, emergency provisions, administrative details
  [10.1] **Section 42 → Article 123** | President's ordinance power – a relic of the 1935 Act
  [10.2] **Section 63 → Article 175** | Governor's address
  [10.3] **Centre–State relations** pattern | Traced to the **1935 Act** (not the US Constitution or the Nehru Report)
[11] x: The 1935 Act **abolished dyarchy in the Governors' provinces** and kept the **Governors' power to veto and legislate**; it **extended** communal representation.
### part 1: Charter Acts 1793–1853 – From Trading Company to Administrator
[0] table: Act | Key provisions
  [0.0] 1793 | Monopoly extended **20 years**; override power extended to all later GGs and Presidency Governors; more control over Bombay and Madras; Board of Control salaries charged to **Indian revenues**; C-in-C not a council member
  [0.1] 1813 | **Trade monopoly in India ended** except **tea and China trade**; **Christian missionaries allowed**; **Rs 1 lakh a year for education** (first such provision); first asserted **Crown sovereignty** over Company territories
  [0.2] 1833 (Saint Helena Act) | **GG of Bengal → Governor-General of India** (**William Bentinck** – last GG of Bengal, first GG of India); Company's **commercial functions ended** – purely administrative; **Law Member** added (council to 4) – **Macaulay**; Law Commission under Macaulay; legislation centralised; **Section 87**: no Indian debarred by religion, birth, descent or colour; GG-in-Council to **mitigate and abolish slavery**
  [0.3] 1853 (last Charter Act) | **Legislative and executive functions separated**; **6 legislative members** added – Indian (Central) Legislative Council, the **first legislature** / "mini-parliament"; **open competition** for civil services (Macaulay Committee 1854); territories held **"in trust for Her Majesty"**, no fixed term
[1] h: Charter Act, 1813
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] Trade | **Ended the trade monopoly** in India – but kept **tea and China** trade
  [2.1] Missionaries | **Christian missionary activity** permitted (Charles Grant, Wilberforce)
  [2.2] **English education chain** | **1813** grant of Rs 1 lakh → **General Committee of Public Instruction (1823)** → **Orientalist–Anglicist controversy** → Macaulay's Minute and **Bentinck's English Education Act (1835)**
[3] h: Charter Act, 1833
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] Designation | **Governor-General of Bengal designated Governor-General of India** – first GG of India **Bentinck** (1833)
  [4.1] Centralisation | **Final step towards centralisation**; GG-in-Council got **all law-making powers** for the whole of British India – often called the **beginning of constitution-making** under British rule
  [4.2] **Law Member** | **Macaulay** (first; not Impey) – the **Father of the Indian Penal Code**; the Act did not require an Indian Law Member
  [4.3] **Slavery** | Government to take measures to **abolish slavery** and improve the condition of slaves (→ Slavery Act V of **1843**)
  [4.4] **IPC dates** | Drafted by Macaulay's First Law Commission (1837); **enacted 6 Oct 1860**; **came into force 1 Jan 1862**
[5] h: Charter Act, 1853
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] **First official legislature** | The separate Legislative Council of 1853
  [6.1] **Civil services** | Appointments by **patronage of the Directors till 1853**; the 1853 Act threw them open to **competition** (exam from **1855**)
  [6.2] **Order** of Acts | Regulating Act 1773 → Pitt's India Act 1784 → Charter Acts (1793 onwards) → Indian Councils Act 1861
### part 2: Government of India Act, 1919 – Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms
[0] h: Basis
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Montagu's declaration, 20 August 1917** | "Increasing association of Indians in every branch of administration" and gradual **responsible government**
  [1.1] Report | **Montagu-Chelmsford Report 1918**
  [1.2] Persons | **Edwin Montagu** = Secretary of State; **Lord Chelmsford** = Viceroy
[2] h: Provisions
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **Dyarchy in the provinces** | Greek *di-arche*, double rule – first time; provincial subjects split into **reserved** and **transferred**
  [3.1] **Reserved subjects** | Administered by the **Governor** with his **Executive Council** (not answerable to the legislature)
  [3.2] **Transferred subjects** | Governor with **Indian ministers** responsible to the legislative council
  [3.3] **Central and provincial subjects** separated | It **clearly defined the jurisdiction of the central and provincial governments**; **budgets separated** for the first time
  [3.4] **Bicameral central legislature** | First time: **Council of State** (upper, 60) + **Legislative Assembly** (lower, **145**: 104 elected + 41 nominated); **direct elections** on a limited franchise (property, tax, education)
  [3.5] Responsible government | Term **"responsible government"** first used; 3 of the 6 members of the Viceroy's Executive Council to be Indians
  [3.6] **Communal representation extended** | To **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans**
  [3.7] New offices | **High Commissioner for India in London**; **Public Service Commission** – set up **1 October 1926** (Chairman + 4); **Public Accounts Committee 1921**
  [3.8] **Civil services** | ICS examination held **in India** too (from 1922); by **1941** Indians outnumbered Europeans in the ICS
  [3.9] **Chamber of Princes** | **120** members; King George V's Proclamation, **23 December 1919**; first Chancellor **Ganga Singh of Bikaner**
  [3.10] **Statutory commission after 10 years** | **Simon Commission** appointed **1927** (two years early) – 7 British members under **Sir John Simon**; "Simon Go Back"
  [3.11] **First elections** | **November 1920** (boycotted by the Congress during Non-Cooperation); the new legislatures met in 1921
  [3.12] **Last election** of the central legislature under this Act | **1945**
[4] h: Reserved vs Transferred
[5] table: Reserved (Governor + Executive Council) | Transferred (Governor + Indian Ministers)
  [5.0] **Police**, **administration of justice**, **land revenue**, law and order, irrigation, finance | **Local self-government**, education, public health, agriculture, public works
### part 3: Crown Rule – Government of India Act, 1858
[0] table: Fact | Key detail
  [0.0] **Rule transferred from the East India Company to the British Crown** | Main administrative consequence of the **Revolt of 1857**; British India under **direct Crown rule from 1858**
  [0.1] Name and purpose | **"Act for the Good Government of India"** – for a more responsible management of Indian affairs after the revolt exposed Company misrule
  [0.2] **Governor-General also styled Viceroy** | **Lord Canning** first; last Viceroy **Mountbatten**
  [0.3] **Board of Control and Court of Directors abolished** | Dual government ended
  [0.4] **Secretary of State for India** | Created – a member of the British cabinet with a **15-member Council of India**; he **issued instructions to the Governor-General**; the Crown's powers were exercised through him
  [0.5] **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1 Nov 1858)** | Called the **"Magna Carta of Indian liberties"**
### part 4: Regulating Act 1773, Pitt's India Act 1784 and the Act of 1786
[0] h: Background
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Act of 1767** | The Company to pay the British Government **£400,000 a year**
  [1.1] **1772 crisis** | Famine, falling revenue, **corruption and oppression by Company officials** (also in justice), loan of about £1 million sought → Parliament intervened; **Lord North** committee report
  [1.2] **Before 1773** | The head of Fort William was the **Governor of Bengal** (1757–1772)
[2] h: Regulating Act, 1773
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] Significance | **First** step by Parliament to **control** the Company; it **altered the Company's constitution at home** (Court of Directors, Court of Proprietors) and its structure in India
  [3.1] **Governor of Bengal → Governor-General of Bengal** | **Warren Hastings** (first), with a **4-member Executive Council**
  [3.2] Presidencies | **Bombay and Madras made subordinate** to Bengal (Calcutta)
  [3.3] **Supreme Court at Calcutta (Fort William)** | Provided by the Act, set up by Charter in **1774**: **1 Chief Justice + 3 judges**; first CJ **Sir Elijah Impey**
  [3.4] Company servants | **Banned private trade and bribes**
  [3.5] Correspondence | Directors to send letters on revenue, civil and military affairs to the British Government
  [3.6] Court of Directors | Kept; both it and the Board of Control were abolished only in **1858**
  [3.7] **Amending Act 1781 (Act of Settlement)** | Separated judicial and executive functions
[4] h: Pitt's India Act, 1784 and the Act of 1786
[5] table: Fact | Key detail
  [5.0] **Dual control / double government** | **Board of Control** (political, **6 members**) + **Court of Directors** (commercial)
  [5.1] Territories | Called **"British possessions in India"**; GG's council cut to 3
  [5.2] **Act of 1786** | GG may **override his Council** and act as **Commander-in-Chief** – first used by **Lord Cornwallis**
[6] x: **1813** – monopoly ended; **Regulating Act** – directors to submit correspondence; **1858** – power to the Crown; **Pitt's Act** – Board of Control.

[0] h: Indian Councils Act, 1861

### part 0: National Emergency (Article 352) – Grounds, Proclamation, Approval
[0] h: Grounds
[1] table: Fact | Key detail

[2] h: Proclamation
[3] table: Fact | Key detail

[0] h: Grounds and approval
[1] table: Fact | Key detail

[0] h: Features

### part 6: Doctrines under Article 13
[0] table: Doctrine | Meaning | Case

  [0.5] **Absolute liability** | Hazardous industry liable without exceptions | **M.C. Mehta (1987)** – Oleum gas leak

  [1.0] "The State" includes | **Government and Parliament of India**; **Government and Legislature of each State**; **all local authorities** (municipalities, panchayats, district boards); **other authorities** within India or under the control of the Government of India (LIC, ONGC, electricity boards)
  [1.1] **Ambiguity – courts** | Whether the **judiciary** is "other authority" is the grey area; in its judicial function it is **not** "State" (**Naresh Mirajkar v. State of Maharashtra, 1967**)
  [1.2] **Private bodies** | A writ does not ordinarily lie merely because a private body performs a public activity, unless the State is involved
[2] table: Case | Year | Holding
  [2.0] **Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal** | 1967 | **Statutory bodies** are "other authorities"
  [2.1] **R.D. Shetty v. International Airport Authority** | 1979 | Laid down tests for an **instrumentality or agency** of the State
  [2.2] **Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib** | 1981 | Society running a Regional Engineering College = State; test is **creation and control, not legal form**
  [2.3] **Pradeep Kumar Biswas** | 2002 | State if **financially, functionally and administratively dominated** by government
