## topic group2 4-45 | Disaster Management Framework – Dm Act 2005, Ndma, Ndrf, Early Warning Systems
### part 0: Disaster Management Cycle – Phases and Measures
[0] h: Phases of the DM cycle
[1] table: Phase | Stage | Measures
  [1.0] **Prevention** | Pre-disaster | Proactive, long-term; stops a hazard becoming a disaster: land-use zoning, **regulating deforestation**, control of industrial siting
  [1.1] **Mitigation** | Pre-disaster | **Reduces the damaging effects / risk** (lowers the likelihood of a disaster); **most cost-effective** phase: quake-resistant buildings, building codes, embankments, afforestation, insurance, **hazard maps**, **retrofitting**; **vulnerability analysis and risk assessment**
  [1.2] **Preparedness** | Pre-disaster | "**First and critical step**": early warning (CAP-SACHET), **public education on evacuation routes**, **mock / evacuation drills** (practise escape routes), **stockpiling food, water and supplies**, Aapda Mitra, Emergency Operation Centres, **contingency plans**
  [1.3] **Response** | **During / just after** | **Time-critical**: **search and rescue** (the major post-disaster operation), evacuation, first aid, temporary shelter, food and water, law and order
  [1.4] **Relief** | During / post | **Providing food, shelter and medical help** to victims
  [1.5] **Recovery** (rehabilitation + reconstruction) | **Post-disaster** | **Rebuilding infrastructure and restoring normalcy** – restore the area to its **pre-disaster state**, livelihoods, compensation, psycho-social care, **Build Back Better**
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Four phases** of the cycle | **Mitigation, Preparedness, Response, Recovery**
  [2.1] **Pre-disaster** | **Prevention + mitigation + preparedness**
  [2.2] During | **Response**
  [2.3] **Post-disaster** | **Recovery, rehabilitation, reconstruction**
  [2.4] Nature | After a disaster the phases are **sequential and sometimes simultaneous**; the cycle is **continuous** – lessons from recovery feed prevention
  [2.5] During – sequence | Early warning → evacuation → rescue → shelters → emergency medical / water
  [2.6] After – sequence | **Relief → restoration → reconstruction → resilient rebuilding**
  [2.7] Strategy | A **proactive strategy**; main aim **saving lives and reducing damage**
  [2.8] Contingency plan for people | In **simple language with basic information and steps**
[3] table: Phase (FEMA) | Definition
  [3.0] **Mitigation** | Minimising the effects of disaster
  [3.1] **Preparedness** | Planning how to respond
  [3.2] **Response** | Efforts to minimise the hazards created by a disaster
  [3.3] **Recovery** | Returning the community to normal
[4] h: Mitigation – types and examples
[5] table: Strategy | Example / meaning
  [5.0] **Structural mitigation** | **Seismic-resistant buildings**, flood barriers, embankments, cyclone shelters; earthquakes – **building codes**; cyclones – **planting trees along the coast**
  [5.1] **Non-structural mitigation** | **Public awareness campaigns / public education**, community awareness, early warning, land-use rules
  [5.2] **Early warning system** | **Tsunami detection buoys**; a **crucial component of mitigation** because it gives **timely information** for preparedness and response
  [5.3] **Seismic retrofitting** | **Strengthening existing (old) buildings** to withstand earthquakes
  [5.4] **Floodplain zoning** | Regulating land use to restrict construction in flood-prone areas
  [5.5] **Afforestation** | Planting trees to reduce landslide and flood risk
  [5.6] **Disaster-resistant design** | Materials and techniques that minimise damage
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] Scope | Mitigation is hazard-specific, not a **single strategy for all disasters**; it gives rapid recovery, fewer deaths and lower financial impact
  [6.1] **Community action** | CBDM – involving local people in preparedness and response
  [6.2] Cyclone pre-disaster mitigation | Revamp drainage, **conserve wetlands**, cyclone shelters, keep coastal trees and forests
  [6.3] Education | Educated communities more often **follow land-use rules and early-warning instructions** (long-term mitigation)
  [6.4] Types of mitigation | **Avoidance, minimization, compensatory**; hazard-specific control – e.g. **flood levees**
  [6.5] **HAZOP** study | Assessment of the **adequacy of safety measures**
  [6.6] **MAT** | **Mitigation Assessment Team**
  [6.7] **Hazard map** | Identifies **areas at risk for specific disasters**
[7] h: Response
[8] table: Fact | Key detail
  [8.0] **First responder** | The **community**
  [8.1] Urban response | Civic bodies + fire brigade + police; the **district administration** has basic responsibility (Collector pivotal)
  [8.2] Search and rescue principles | **Search, Find, Rescue**
  [8.3] Immediate rescue essentials (tsunami, earthquake) | Trained people, equipment, communication, first aid and transport
  [8.4] **Incident Command System (ICS)** | Standard management system for incidents of **all kinds and sizes**; in India the **Incident Response System (IRS)** (NDMA guidelines 2010)
[9] table: Triage colour | Meaning
  [9.0] **Red** | Cannot survive without immediate treatment but can be saved
  [9.1] Yellow | Treatment can be delayed
  [9.2] Green | Minor (walking wounded)
  [9.3] Black | Dead or beyond help
[10] x: **Mitigation** reduces the likelihood and effects of disaster; **preparedness** = drills, stockpiling, evacuation education; **response** = during the disaster.

### part 5: Key Concepts – Hazard, Disaster, Vulnerability, Types
[0] h: Terms
[1] table: Term | Meaning
  [1.0] **Hazard** | Dangerous condition or event with the **potential** to harm; an **earthquake in an uninhabited desert** is a hazard / **natural event**, not a disaster
  [1.1] **Disaster** | Occurs **when people are unable to cope** with the impact of a hazard: Hazard × Vulnerability with low capacity; French "desastre" = bad star
  [1.2] **Vulnerability** | Conditions raising susceptibility: limited health care, **elderly / disabled residents**, poverty, weak housing
  [1.3] **Capacity** | Strengths that reduce risk: **strong social networks and community cohesion** (the least contributor to vulnerability), volunteerism (communities **recover better**), early warning
  [1.4] Risk | Likelihood of loss; rises with hazard, exposure, vulnerability; **falls with coping capacity**
[2] table: Vulnerability type | Examples
  [2.0] **Physical** | Weak houses
  [2.1] **Social** | Age, gender, caste, disability
  [2.2] **Economic** | Poverty, daily wage
  [2.3] **Environmental** | Mangrove loss, deforestation
  [2.4] **Systemic / institutional** | Weak governance, poor coordination, weak early warning
[3] b: Community factors: kutcha housing, thin health infrastructure, weak early warning; **high crop-insurance coverage reduces** vulnerability. **Religion** is not a vulnerability factor.
[4] b: A vulnerability profile covers people, housing, infrastructure, livelihoods, environment and institutions – **not popular tourist attractions**.
[5] h: Types of disasters
[6] table: Type | Examples
  [6.0] **Geological** | **Earthquakes**, landslides, tsunamis, volcanoes
  [6.1] Hydro-meteorological (water and climate) | Cyclones, floods, droughts, cloudbursts, heat waves
  [6.2] Biological | Epidemics, pest attacks
  [6.3] **Man-made** (chemical / industrial, accidents, societal) | **Bhopal gas leak (MIC, 2–3 Dec 1984)**, **car accidents**, **stampedes**, fires; stubble burning, red-sanders felling, over-irrigation
[7] table: Fact | Key detail
  [7.0] Four broad emergency types | **Natural disasters, environmental emergencies, complex emergencies, pandemic emergencies**
  [7.1] **Pandemic emergency** | **Sudden onset of contagious disease** that affects health, disrupts services and businesses, with high economic and social cost
  [7.2] **Biological** disasters | Grouped by NDMA with chemical, radiological and nuclear (CBRN) disasters as **human-made**; urban floods, heat waves and tsunamis are natural hazards
  [7.3] **Podu** (shifting) cultivation | Tribal tree-cutting treated as a traditional livelihood, not a man-made disaster
  [7.4] **Not a disaster** | An ordinary event people cope with easily – e.g. a **low-pressure trough giving rain but no wind**
[8] h: India
[9] table: Fact | Key detail
  [9.0] Damage | Natural disasters cause most damage in **developing countries**
  [9.1] Deaths 1980–2010 | **1,43,039**
  [9.2] **National Disaster Risk Index** (MHA–UNDP, 2019) | **Maharashtra** the most vulnerable State, then West Bengal and Uttar Pradesh
  [9.3] Disaster-prone | **27 of India's 36 States/UTs** (NDMA / NIDM)

### part 7: Technology – GIS and Remote Sensing
[0] h: GIS in disaster management
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **GIS** | **Geographic Information System** – stores, analyses and presents **spatial data**; a tool of disaster management
  [1.1] Uses | Maps hazard zones and vulnerable people; in floods **analyses flood patterns and topography**; it cannot give details of individual victims
  [1.2] GIS with **GPS** | Guides rescue teams and builds emergency databases of people needing help; remote sensing and GIS **cannot solve all natural disasters**
  [1.3] **"What-if" analysis** | Varying parameters to generate alternative scenarios in a spatial context
  [1.4] **Landslide Hazard Zonation** | Divides hill areas into homogeneous zones by **degree of susceptibility, hazard or risk**
  [1.5] **DEM = Digital Elevation Model** | Digital terrain / elevation surface used in GIS for flood, inundation and landslide modelling
  [1.6] **Alerts today** | Real-time remote-sensing data, **social media and mobile apps** – beyond traditional communication
[2] table: Phase / hazard | GIS use
  [2.0] Prevention / mitigation | Hazard zonation, vulnerability and risk mapping
  [2.1] Preparedness | **Evacuation route planning**, resource mapping
  [2.2] Response / recovery | Real-time damage assessment; Build Back Better
  [2.3] Cyclone | Track monitoring, **storm surge modelling**
  [2.4] Landslide | Susceptibility mapping (GSI NLSM)

[0] h: Cropping systems
[1] table: System | Meaning

  [1.7] Crop rotation | Planned sequence; pulse–cereal leaves fixed nitrogen
  [1.8] Mixed farming | Crops + rearing animals
