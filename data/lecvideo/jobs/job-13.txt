### part 0: Rammohan Roy – Titles, Life and Works
[0] h: Titles
[1] table: Title | Person
  [1.0] **Father of Indian Renaissance**; **Maker / Father of Modern India**; first **'Modern Man'** of India | **Rammohan Roy**
  [1.1] First Indian **political reformer**; pioneer of political agitation | **Rammohan Roy**
  [1.2] Father of Indian Nationalism | Rammohan Roy in most books
  [1.3] **'Raja'** | Conferred on Roy by Mughal emperor **Akbar II** (1830)
  [1.4] **Maharishi** | **Debendranath Tagore**
[2] h: Life and ideas
[3] b: **Life:** 1772-1833, born Radhanagar (Hooghly); knew more than a dozen languages – **Sanskrit**, Persian, Arabic, English, Greek, Hebrew.
[4] b: **Views:** greatly respected the **traditional philosophic systems of the East**, yet wanted Indians to accept the **rational and scientific approach**, human dignity and social equality.
[5] b: **England:** went in **1830** as envoy of **Akbar II**; **died at Bristol, 27 Sep 1833**; samadhi/grave at **Arnos Vale cemetery, Bristol** (reburied 1843). Honoured by the **Société Asiatique of Paris** (founded 1822).
[6] b: **Political demands:** Indianisation of superior services, separation of executive and judiciary, **trial by jury** and judicial equality – opposed the **Jury Act 1827**; fixed maximum rents for ryots; protested the **Press Ordinance 1823**.
[7] b: Worked for women, ryots and the nation as a whole; saw religions as embodiments of **universal theism**.
[8] b: **Social campaigns:** against **sati, polygamy, child marriage**; for **widow remarriage**, women's education and property rights.

  [10.3] **Sambad Kaumudi** | 1821, **Bengali** weekly
  [10.4] **Mirat-ul-Akhbar** | 1822, **Persian** weekly
[11] h: Institutions
[12] table: Body | Key detail

  [12.1] **Hindu College** | **1817** – founded by **David Hare** with Roy's support; later **Presidency College** (Alexander Duff only reached Calcutta in 1830)

### part 2: Brahmo Samaj, 1828 – Founding and Beliefs
[0] h: Founding
[1] b: **Founded:** **Brahmo Sabha**, **20 August 1828**, Calcutta, by **Raja Rammohan Roy**; later called the **Brahmo Samaj** (**Renamed:** under **Debendranath Tagore**). First secretary Tarachand Chakravarti.
[2] table: Year | Event
  [2.0] **1828** | Brahmo Sabha founded
  [2.1] 1829 | Sati ban
  [2.2] 1830 | **Radhakant Deb's Dharma Sabha** – orthodox opponent, against the sati ban
  [2.3] **1843** | Debendranath joined
[3] h: Beliefs
[4] b: **Monotheism:** worship of **one formless, eternal God**; denounced **polytheism and idol worship**; no images in Samaj halls; no faith in **avatars**.
[5] b: **Reason + Upanishads:** rested on reason and the Vedas/Upanishads but **rejected the infallibility of the Vedas** – no scripture above reason and conscience.
[6] b: **No priestly class** needed to interpret texts; criticised **caste**; took in the best of Western and other religious thought.
[7] b: **Nature:** a **monotheistic reformist** movement within Hinduism (not political); **reformist**, while the **Arya Samaj** is **revivalist**.
[8] b: **Significance:** condemned sati, purdah, child marriage, polygamy; backed widow remarriage and women's education; influence hardly went beyond **Bengal**.
[9] b: **Brahmo Dharma Gitanjali:** collection of devotional songs sung in Samaj prayers.
[10] x: Brahmo = one God + reason; no idols, no avatars, no priests, no infallible scripture.
### part 3: Debendranath Tagore, Keshab Chandra Sen and the Splits
[0] h: Splits of the Brahmo Samaj
[1] table: Year | Event
  [1.0] **1839** | **Debendranath Tagore** founds the **Tattvabodhini Sabha** (organ Tattvabodhini Patrika, Bengali) to spread Roy's ideas
  [1.1] 1857-58 | **Keshab Chandra Sen** joins; **Debendranath makes him acharya** – the first **non-Brahmin acharya**
  [1.2] 1859 | **Tattvabodhini Sabha** merged into the Brahmo Samaj
  [1.3] 1865-66 | Keshab dismissed; forms the **Brahmo Samaj of India** (Bharatvarshiya) in **1866**; Debendranath's body = **Adi Brahmo Samaj**
  [1.4] **1870** | Keshab founds the **Indian Reform Association**
  [1.5] **1872** | **Native (Civil / Brahmo) Marriage Act** – after Keshab's petition; legal Brahmo/inter-caste marriage; minimum age 14 (bride), 18 (groom)
  [1.6] **1878** | Keshab marries his minor daughter to the Maharaja of **Cooch Behar** → **Sadharan Brahmo Samaj** (Ananda Mohan Bose, Shibchandra Deb, Umesh Chandra Datta / Shivnath Shastri)
  [1.7] 1881 | Keshab's **Naba Bidhan** (New Dispensation)
[2] b: After founding the Tattvabodhini Sabha, Debendranath **joined and revitalised** the Brahmo Samaj.
[3] b: **Debendranath Tagore** (1817-1905): 'Maharishi', father of Rabindranath; first **secretary of the British Indian Association (1851)**; the Atmiya Sabha and the Brahmo Samaj were Roy's foundations.
[4] b: **Keshab** spread the Samaj to U.P., Punjab, **Bombay** and **Madras** – his visits inspired the **Prarthana Samaj** (1867) and the **Veda Samaj** (Madras, 1864); under him the Samaj campaigned for **women's education**.
[5] b: **Dev Samaj:** **Shiv Narayan Agnihotri**, an ex-Brahmo, Lahore **1887**.
[6] x: Atmiya 1815 → Brahmo 1828 → Tattvabodhini 1839 → split 1866 → Indian Reform Assn 1870 → Marriage Act 1872 → Sadharan 1878.
### part 4: Abolition of Sati, 1829
[0] h: Abolition of sati, 1829
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Campaign | **Rammohan Roy**, from **1818** – scriptures do not sanction it; vigilance at cremation grounds, petitions
  [1.1] Law | **Bengal Sati Regulation XVII, 4 Dec 1829** – sati a crime
  [1.2] Governor-General | **Lord William Bentinck**
  [1.3] Extended | **Madras and Bombay, 1830**
  [1.4] Opposition | **Dharma Sabha** (Radhakant Deb, 1830); Privy Council appeal rejected 1832
[2] b: **Rammohan Roy opposed sati**.
[3] b: **Widow remarriage:** Roy demanded it; **Vidyasagar** got it legalised – **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act 1856**.
[4] x: Sati banned = Bentinck, 1829, Regulation XVII; campaign = Rammohan Roy.

### part 1: Prarthana Samaj (1867), Ranade and the Social Conference
[0] h: Prarthana Samaj
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Founded** | **1867, Bombay**, by **Dr. Atmaram Pandurang**, helped by **Keshab Chandra Sen** (visits 1864, 1867); grew out of the **Paramahansa Sabha**

### part 2: Paramahansa Mandali (1849)
[0] h: Paramahansa Mandali (1849)
[1] b: **Founded:** **1849, Maharashtra (Bombay)**, by **Dadoba Pandurang**, **Durgaram Mehtaji** (of the Manav Dharma Sabha, Surat) and others; Bipan Chandra dates religious reform in Bombay from **1840** by the Mandali.
[2] b: **First socio-religious organisation in Maharashtra** – religious reform in Bombay began with it, before the Prarthana Samaj (1867).
[3] b: **A secret society**; believed in **one God**; aim: **against idolatry and the caste system** – members **ate food cooked by lower castes**; backed **widow remarriage and women's education**.
[4] b: **Branches:** **Poona**, Satara; exposure in 1860 ended it; reorganised as the Prarthana Samaj (1867).
[5] b: **Manav Dharma Sabha:** founded at **Surat, 1844**, by **Durgaram Manchharam (Mehtaji)** with Dadoba Pandurang – forerunner of the Paramahansa Mandali.
[6] x: Paramahansa Mandali = secret, 1849, Dadoba Pandurang, anti-caste and anti-idolatry.
### part 3: Young Bengal Movement – Derozio
[0] h: Young Bengal Movement
[1] b: **Leader:** **Henry Louis Vivian Derozio** (1809-31), Anglo-Indian teacher at **Hindu College** (1826-31); inspired by the **French Revolution**; first nationalist poet of modern India.

### part 0: Justice Party (South Indian Liberal Federation)
[0] h: Background, aim and social base
[1] b: **Why:** Brahmins were about **3%** of the Madras Presidency population but held **over 90%** of government jobs.
[2] b: **Aim:** to further the interests of **non-Brahmins** – end Brahmin dominance in jobs, education, politics and religion; basically an **anti-Brahmin party** – "the first political party in India founded against Brahmins".
[3] b: **Agenda of SILF:** social justice, a rightful place in services, political representation.
[4] b: **Demand:** separate **communal representation** for non-Brahmins, on the lines given to Muslims by the Morley-Minto Reforms; opposed the Brahmin-dominated Congress.
[5] b: **Social base:** non-Brahmin landed gentry, **intermediate landed castes** (Andhra: **Reddys, Kammas, Kapus**). Dalits built a parallel "Adi" movement.
[6] b: **British view:** backed it to weaken **Home Rule**; used against Home Rule and Gandhi's movements; it **supported the British in the Second World War**.
[7] b: **Andhra Mahasabha (Vijayawada, 1917)** sought reserved seats for non-Brahmins – **Ponaka Pattabhirama Reddy** and **Tripuraneni Ramaswamy Chowdary**.

### part 1: Jyotiba Phule and the Satyashodhak Samaj
[0] h: Jyotiba Phule (1827-1890)
[1] b: Born **1827**, Satara; a gardener's son – **Mali community**, Maharashtra.
[2] b: Title **"Mahatma"** given by **Vithalrao Krishnaji Vandekar (1888)**; called the **"Father of Indian Social Revolution"**.
[3] b: **Creed:** all men have the same features and intellect – **nobody is sacred by birth**; aimed at the complete abolition of caste; against Sanskritic Hinduism.
[4] b: **1848**: girls' school, **Pune**; taught by **Savitribai Phule** (Maharashtra) – **India's first woman teacher**; home for widows 1854; pioneer of widow remarriage → the Indian women's movement owes much to Phule's inspiration.
[5] b: **Savitribai** headed the Samaj's women's section; worked for lower-caste girls.
[6] h: Satyashodhak Samaj ("Society of Truth Seekers")
[7] table: Fact | Key detail
  [7.0] Founded | **24 September 1873, Pune**, by **Jyotiba Phule** – its **first president**
  [7.1] Nature | An **anti-caste movement in Maharashtra**
  [7.2] Aim | **Upliftment of the backward classes** – **Shudras and Ati-Shudras** against **Arya-Brahmin** exploitation; end of **priestly monopoly**; education of women and lower castes; social service
  [7.3] Leaders | From malis, telis, kunbis, saris, dhangars
  [7.4] **Satyashodhak marriages** | **Without priests**; **inter-caste** marriage allowed
  [7.5] Symbol | **King Bali** (the oppressed) against the Brahmanical symbol of **Rama** (Justice Party symbol = **Scales**)
  [7.6] Ideas | Brahmins = progeny of **'alien' Aryans**; his 1880s-90s focus on the **Kunbi** peasantry privileged **Maratha identity**
  [7.7] **Din Bandhu** | Marathi weekly, **Pune, 1877** – the Samaj's organ, started by **Krishnarao Bhalekar**, part of **Phule's** movement; spread it to remote Maharashtra; later edited by **N.M. Lokhande**. Phule also ran Dinmitra
[8] h: Works of Phule
[9] table: Work | Key detail
  [9.0] **Gulamgiri** (1873) | "Slavery"; compared caste oppression with slavery
  [9.1] Shetkaryacha Asud | "The Cultivator's Whipcord"
  [9.2] Sarvajanik Satyadharma Pustak | Published posthumously, 1891
  [9.3] Tritiya Ratna, Brahmananche Kasab | Also Phule
[10] b: **Current:** the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment began a two-year nationwide celebration of Phule's **200th birth anniversary** on **11 April 2026**.
[11] x: Satyashodhak = Phule, Pune, 1873, anti-caste, Maharashtra; Gulamgiri; Din Bandhu.
