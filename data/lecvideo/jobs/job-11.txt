[1] h: Terms
[2] table: Party | Terms

### part 2: Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley, 1798-1805)
[0] h: Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley, 1798-1805)
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] System | **Introduced as a system by Lord Wellesley** (Governor-General **1798-1805**)
  [1.1] Forerunners | **Dupleix** first hired out troops; **Awadh (1765)** the first state in the protection trap; Cornwallis (Carnatic, 1787) first barred foreign relations
  [1.2] **Terms** | British force permanently stationed in the state, paid by a **subsidy or ceded territory**; a **British Resident** at the court; no Europeans in service without consent; no war or negotiation without the Governor-General
  [1.3] In return | Protection and (nominal) non-interference
  [1.4] **Effects** | States lost independence and revenue; rulers became irresponsible; the Company spread its troops at strategic points and kept the French out
[2] h: Order of acceptance
[3] b: **Hyderabad (Sept 1798, 1800)** – the **first** → **Mysore (1799)** → Tanjore (1799) → **Awadh (Nov 1801)** → **Peshwa (Bassein, Dec 1802)** → Bhonsle (Dec 1803) → **Sindhia (Feb 1804)** → Rajput states, Holkar (1818).
[4] b: **Wellesley against Napoleon:** while Napoleon broke the empires of Europe, Wellesley's forward policy (Mysore 1799, Marathas 1803-05, Tanjore, Surat, Carnatic) **kept the British flag flying high** – "converted the British Empire in India into the British Empire of India".

## topic group2 1-109 | Charter Acts of 1793, 1813, 1833 and 1853 – Provisions and Significance
### part 0: Charter Act 1813 – End of India Trade Monopoly, Education, Missionaries
[0] h: Charter Act 1813
[1] table: Provision | Key detail
  [1.0] Charter | Renewed for **20 years**
  [1.1] **Missionaries** | Allowed to come to India and preach (Wilberforce, Charles Grant)
  [1.2] Other | Courts in India got more power over European British subjects; local governments could tax persons; separate commercial and territorial accounts
  [1.3] Revenues | Not placed under Parliament's control
[2] b: **Trade:** Ended the Company's **monopoly of Indian trade** (open to all British merchants) **except tea, opium and the China trade**.
[3] b: **Sovereignty:** Asserted the **sovereignty of the British Crown** over the Company's Indian territories.
[4] b: **Education:** At least **Rs 1 lakh a year** for the revival of literature and promotion of science among Indians – first State responsibility for education (the chief reason the Act is important).
[5] x: Company's India trade monopoly ended **1813**; its tea/China monopoly ended 1833.
### part 1: Charter Act 1833 – Governor-General of India and Centralisation
[0] h: Charter Act 1833
[1] table: Provision | Key detail
  [1.0] Centralisation | **GG of Bengal → Governor-General of India** (first: **Lord William Bentinck**) – the **final step towards centralisation**
  [1.1] Law-making | GG-in-Council got exclusive law-making for all British India; laws called **Acts** (earlier Regulations)
  [1.2] **Trade** | Ended the remaining monopoly in **tea and the China trade**; Company a purely **administrative** body; Europeans free to settle
  [1.3] **Law Member** | Added as **4th member**: **T.B. Macaulay** (a Briton) – no Indian Law Member; origin of the **Ministry of Law and Justice**
  [1.4] **First Law Commission (1834)** | Under **Macaulay**, to codify Indian laws (IPC)
  [1.5] **Section 87** | No Indian to be barred from office by religion, birth, descent or colour; slavery to be abolished (done 1843)
  [1.6] Tenure | Territories held **"in trust for His Majesty"**; the 1853 Act repeated it without a term
  [1.7] **Agra Presidency** | Almost all of present UP separated from Bengal Presidency in **1834** → North-Western Provinces (1836)
[2] b: **Macaulay** (Commons, 1833 Bill): "The Company is an **anomaly** but it is part of a system where everything is an anomaly".
### part 2: Charter Act 1853 – Legislative Council and Open Competition
[0] h: Charter Act 1853
[1] table: Provision | Key detail
  [1.0] Status | **Last** Charter Act; Company's rule renewed **with no fixed term** ("in trust" for the Crown till Parliament provides otherwise)
  [1.1] **First functioning Legislative Council** | Legislative and executive functions separated
  [1.2] Members | **6 new legislative members** (4 from **Madras, Bombay, Bengal, Agra** + **Chief Justice** + one Calcutta judge) → Central Legislative Council of 12, a **"mini-Parliament"**
  [1.3] **Civil services** | Directors' patronage ended → **open competitive examination** (the Act of **1853**); **Macaulay Committee 1854**
[2] b: The **Imperial Legislative Council** (under the Indian Councils Act) was British India's legislature from **1861** to 1947.
