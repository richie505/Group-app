## topic group2 1-70 | Mughal Architecture – Fatehpur Sikri, Humayun's Tomb, Taj Mahal, Red Fort; Garden and Tomb Traditions
### part 0: Fatehpur Sikri – Akbar's Capital
[0] h: Fatehpur Sikri
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Name | **Fatehpur Sikri** ("City of Victory")
  [1.1] Period | Akbar's planned capital, **1571–85**
  [1.2] Site | About 37–40 km from Agra, at Sikri, where the Chishti saint **Shaikh Salim Chishti** lived (title **Shaikh-ul-Hind**; Prince **Salim** named after him)
  [1.3] Material | Mainly **red sandstone**
  [1.4] UNESCO | 1986
  [1.5] Importance | The **finest monuments of Akbar** are here
[2] h: Buildings
[3] table: Building | Key detail
  [3.0] **Buland Darwaza** | "Gate of Magnificence", ~41 m; built by **Akbar** (c. 1575) to commemorate the **victory over Gujarat** (1572–73); southern entrance to the Jama Masjid; red and buff sandstone with marble inlay
  [3.1] **Jama Masjid** | Congregational mosque, among the first buildings of the city; called **'Shan-i-Fatehpur'**
  [3.2] **Tomb of Salim Chishti** | **White-marble** tomb in the Jama Masjid courtyard (built under Akbar)
  [3.3] **Panch Mahal** | **Five-storeyed**, entirely columnar pavilion in the style of a **Buddhist monastery / vihara**; ground floor 84 pillars; Persian badgir (wind-catcher)
  [3.4] **Jodha Bai's palace** | Largest palace; Hindu **trabeate** + Islamic **arcuate**; Gujarati and Rajasthani patterns
  [3.5] **Maryam's palace** | Gilded paintings – the **Golden Palace** (Sunehra Makan)
  [3.6] Diwan-i-Khas | Private audience; single central carved pillar
  [3.7] Ibadat Khana | House of Worship (**1575**) – debates of scholars of different religions
  [3.8] Others | Diwan-i-Aam, Birbal ki Kothi, Turkish Sultana's house, Anup Talao, Hiran Minar
[4] b: The **Akbari Mahal** is in **Agra Fort**, not at Fatehpur Sikri.
[5] b: Built in Akbar's reign: the Tomb of Salim Chishti, the Buland Darwaza, the Jami Masjid (Fatehpur Sikri) and Humayun's Tomb (1565–72).
[6] b: **Materials:** the Buland Darwaza and the Khanqah are of red/buff sandstone, not white marble; the Bara Imambara and Rumi Darwaza of Lucknow are of **brick and lime plaster**.
[7] b: **Style:** Persian, Indian, Central Asian; introduced the **Tudor (four-centred) arch**, continued at **Jahangir Mahal, Orchha**.

### part 2: Tomb Tradition – Lodi to Bibi ka Maqbara
[0] h: Tomb tradition
[1] table: Stage | Tomb | Feature
  [1.0] Lodi | Sikandar Lodi's tomb – built by his son **Ibrahim Lodi** | First **double dome**
  [1.1] Sur | **Sher Shah's tomb, Sasaram** – he built it himself; set in a tank; finished under Islam Shah (c. 1545) | Transition Lodi → Mughal
  [1.2] Akbar | **Humayun's tomb**, Delhi (**1565–72**) | First **garden tomb**; first full Persian double dome; red sandstone with white marble; **Persian + Indian** synthesis
  [1.3] Jahangir | **Itimad-ud-Daula**, Agra (**1622–28**) – built by **Nur Jahan** for her father Mirza Ghiyas Beg ("Baby Taj") | First tomb wholly of **white marble**; first **pietra dura**
  [1.4] Shah Jahan | **Taj Mahal** | Climax
  [1.5] Aurangzeb | **Bibi ka Maqbara**, Aurangabad – tomb of **Rabia-ud-Daurani** (Dilras Banu), "Taj of the Deccan", **"Second Taj"** | Last major imitation of the garden tomb
[2] b: **Humayun's tomb:** built by his widow **Haji Begum (Bega Begum)** – the woman who built her **emperor husband's** mausoleum; architects **Mirak Mirza Ghiyas** and his son **Sayyid Muhammad**.
[3] b: **Bibi ka Maqbara:** built in **Aurangzeb's** reign by his son **Azam Shah**; architect **Ata-ullah**, son of Ustad Ahmad Lahori.
[4] b: **Akbar's mausoleum – begun by Akbar, completed by Jahangir**; **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan's tomb** – **Delhi**, near Humayun's tomb.
[5] b: **Chronology:** Atala Mosque, Jaunpur (**1408**) → Sher Shah's tomb, Sasaram (c. 1545) → Humayun's tomb (1565–72) → Bibi ka Maqbara (c. 1660).
### part 3: Taj Mahal
[0] h: Taj Mahal
[1] table: Item | Key detail
  [1.0] For | **Mumtaz Mahal** (Arjumand Banu Begum)
  [1.1] Place | Agra, right bank of the Yamuna
  [1.2] Built | **1632–48** (complex finished 1653)
  [1.3] Chief architect | **Ustad Ahmad Lahori** (modern view); also attributed to **Ustad Isa**
  [1.4] Marble | **Makrana**, Rajasthan
  [1.5] Material | Mainly **white marble**; pietra dura at its peak
  [1.6] Plan | Tomb **equal in length and breadth** (square plan with chamfered corners); stands at the **river end** of its charbagh
  [1.7] Style | **Indian + Persian (Iranian) + Central Asian** – the Hindu–Iranian synthesis
  [1.8] Design ancestry | Humayun's tomb (garden tomb, double dome)
  [1.9] UNESCO | **1983**; New Seven Wonders **2007**
[2] h: Historians on the Taj
[3] table: Historian | View on style
  [3.0] **V.A. Smith** | "Combination of **European and Asiatic** genius"
  [3.1] James Fergusson, Percy Brown | Product of the Indo-Persian tradition; Fergusson: Fatehpur Sikri "a reflex of the mind of a great man"
[4] h: Pollution and conservation
[5] b: **Pollution:** "marble cancer" from **sulphur dioxide**; Supreme Court, **M.C. Mehta case (30 Dec 1996)** – the **Taj Trapezium Zone** (~10,400 sq km) to shift / clean up industries.
[6] b: **Conservation:** the ASI removes surface stains with a **mud pack** of fuller's earth (**multani mitti**) – "mud pack therapy".

### part 6: Mughal Style and Features
[0] h: Mughal style
[1] b: Mughal style = **Indo-Islamic + Persian (Timurid) grandeur + Hindu** grace: bulbous domes, slender minarets, vaulted gateways, **charbagh**, **double dome**, **jali**, calligraphy.
[2] b: **Akbar** built in **red sandstone**; **Shah Jahan** in **white marble** – his reign is the **"Golden Age"** of Mughal architecture.
[3] table: Feature | Meaning | First / best seen
  [3.0] **Pietra dura** | Decorating walls with **floral designs of inlaid semi-precious stones** | Began under **Jahangir**; first at **Itimad-ud-Daula**; peak at the Taj
  [3.1] Double dome | Inner and outer shells | Sikandar Lodi's tomb; first full Mughal one – Humayun's tomb
  [3.2] Charbagh | Four-quartered garden | Introduced by Babur (Ram Bagh)
  [3.3] Jali | Lattice work | Humayun's tomb
  [3.4] Sheesh Mahal | Mirror-work chambers | Agra Fort, Red Fort
[4] b: **Contrast:** the **Bahmani Jama Masjid, Gulbarga** has a **fully covered courtyard**, unlike the Mughal open-court mosques.

### part 1: Music at the Mughal Court
[0] h: Musicians
[1] table: Musician | Key detail
  [1.0] **Tansen** | Original name **Ramtanu Pande** (Tanna Mishra); trained at Man Singh Tomar's Gwalior school; disciple of **Swami Haridas**; first at the court of **Raja Ramchandra of Rewa** (Bandhavgarh) – Akbar sent a firman for him; one of Akbar's **Navaratnas**; composed dhrupads on Hindu gods and on his patrons; invented ragas (**Mian ki Todi, Mian ki Malhar, Darbari Kanada**); **tomb at Gwalior**
  [1.1] Tansen's titles | **'Tansen' from Raja Vikramjit of Gwalior**; **'Mian' from Akbar**
  [1.2] **Baiju Bawra** | Dhrupad singer at the court of **Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior**, later of **Bahadur Shah of Gujarat**
  [1.3] Swami Haridas | Dhrupad singer of Akbar's period (Vrindavan), Tansen's guru
  [1.4] Vilas Khan | Tansen's son; main singer under **Jahangir**
  [1.5] Baz Bahadur | Last Sultan of Malwa (1555-62), musician-poet; Rupmati; later served Akbar
[2] b: **Dhrupad singers of Akbar's reign: Tansen and Haridas**; Vilas Khan belongs to Jahangir.
[3] b: Abul Fazl lists **36 singers** at Akbar's court. **Aurangzeb** ended court music yet **played the veena**; many Persian works on music date from his reign.
[4] h: Form → origin / patron
[5] table: Form | Origin / patron
  [5.0] **Dhrupad** | **Oldest** Hindustani vocal form; roots in Vedic chanting; **devotional/spiritual**; alap uses **Sanskrit syllables from mantras**; developed by **Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior (1486-1516)** – before the Mughals; **non-Islamic (indigenous)** in character
  [5.1] Khayal | Persian-influenced; **Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela'** – composers **Sadarang and Adarang**
  [5.2] **Tappa** | Folk songs of camel riders of the north-west; made a court form under **Muhammad Shah**; refined by **Shori Miyan** (Lucknow, late 18th c.)
  [5.3] Qawwali | Sufi (Islamic) devotional form
[6] b: **Mal Gurjari** = a **raga** of Man Singh Tomar, not a person; the greats = Man Singh Tomar, Tansen, Sadarang-Adarang.
[7] b: **Sitar** = Persian long-necked lute (**setar**) + Indian **veena**.
[8] h: Hindustani music – instruments and ragas
[9] table: Fact | Key detail
  [9.0] Instruments | **Ustad Amjad Ali Khan = sarod**; Ravi Shankar = sitar; Zakir Hussain = tabla
  [9.1] Ragas by time | **Todi** = early morning; Bhimpalasi = afternoon; Bhopali = evening; Darbari Kanada = late night
[10] h: Related facts
[11] b: **Man Bai, Man Singh's sister, married Jahangir (Salim)**; Akbar married Harkha Bai, Bharmal's daughter; **Todar Mal headed revenue**, not the army; Abul Fazl wrote the Ain-i-Akbari.
[12] b: **Radha Govind Sangeet Sar:** the great music treatise of Jaipur, credited to **Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh** (r. 1778–1803); its compilation is also credited to his court scholar Devarshi Brajpal Bhatt.
