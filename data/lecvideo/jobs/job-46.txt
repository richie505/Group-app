## topic group2 3-24 | National Income Concepts – Gdp, Gnp, Ndp, Nnp, Factor Cost vs Market Price, Gva, Deflator
### part 0: National Income – Meaning, NNP at Factor Cost and Formulas
[0] h: Meaning
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **National income (NI)** | The **money value of all final goods and services** produced by the **normal residents** of a country during a year – only **final** goods, so no double counting
  [1.1] **Income view** | NI = **sum of factor incomes** (wages, rent, interest, profit) earned by normal residents – not government income, budget or PSU profits
  [1.2] **Source** | Generated only from **productive activity**, not from any money-making activity
  [1.3] **Flow, not stock** | NI is measured over a period; **national wealth** is a stock at a point of time
  [1.4] **Product = income** | Output is distributed as factor incomes, so national product and national income are **synonymous**; production by labour and capital **automatically generates income**
  [1.5] **Economic activity** | Done for income or payment – e.g. a **nurse paid in a charity hospital**
  [1.6] **Non-economic** activity | Done for love or self-use – tuition to one's own child, medicine for one's family, repairing one's own table
[2] h: The standard measure
[3] b: **National income = NNP at factor cost** – not GDP/GNP at market prices or NDP.
[4] table: Route | Formula
  [4.0] From NNP at MP | **NI = NNP(MP) − (indirect taxes − subsidies)**
  [4.1] From GNP at MP | **NI = GNP(MP) − depreciation − indirect taxes + subsidies**
  [4.2] From NDP | **NI = NDP(FC) + net factor income from abroad**
  [4.3] National product at FC | National product at MP − indirect taxes + subsidies
  [4.4] NNP at FC | **= National Income**
[5] table: Worked example | Steps
  [5.0] Example 1 | GDP(MP) **110** + NFIA **10** = GNP **120**; − dep **15** = NNP(MP) **105**; − NIT **5** = **NI 100**
  [5.1] Example 2 | GDP(MP) **500**, depreciation **50**, NFIA **−10**, NIT **40** → GNP **490**; NNP(MP) **440**; **NI 400**
### part 1: Nominal vs Real Income; Measuring Growth
[0] h: Nominal and real
[1] table: Basis | Nominal (current prices) | Real (constant prices)
  [1.0] Prices used | Current-year prices | Prices of a **single base year**
  [1.1] Includes price rise | Yes | **No** – price effect removed
  [1.2] Use | Size of the economy in rupees | **Growth of output**
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Real GDP** | Value of total production at **prices of a single year** = GDP at **constant prices**
  [2.1] **Real national income** | NI at **constant prices**; market value of national output in base-year prices = **GNP at constant prices**
  [2.2] Formula | **Real NI = nominal NI ÷ price index × 100**
  [2.3] Example | Base **1990-91**, NI **Rs 20,000 crore**, index **250** → **Rs 8,000 crore**
  [2.4] **Prices and money income both doubled** | Real income **unchanged**
  [2.5] Per capita income | Grows **faster at current prices** – the constant-price series allows for the **rise in the price level**
  [2.6] India | GDP at market prices in rupees has **risen steadily**; real GDP growth has **fluctuated** (e.g. contraction in **2020-21**)
  [2.7] Nominal vs real growth | Nominal exceeds real by roughly the **inflation rate**; **2025-26** estimates (old series): nominal **8.0%**, real **7.4%**
[3] h: Base year
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **New series** (released **27 Feb 2026**) | Base **2022-23** (earlier **2011-12**); uses **double deflation** and Supply-Use Tables
  [4.1] GDP at current prices **2024-25** | **Rs 318.07 lakh crore** (new series)
[5] h: Measuring economic growth
[6] table: Fact | Key detail
  [6.0] **Growth** | Rise in **real** output: a growing country has **rising GNP at constant prices**; GDP growth data is the usual yardstick
  [6.1] **Best measure / sign of growth** | A **sustained increase in real per capita income** (per capita product)
  [6.2] Growth measures | GDP, GNP and per capita GNP – growth in **financial aid from the World Bank** is not one
### part 2: Income Determination – Consumption, Saving, Investment, Multiplier
[0] h: Concepts
[1] table: Concept | Meaning
  [1.0] **Consumption function** | **C = C̄ + cY** – C = **total consumption spending**, C̄ = autonomous consumption, c = MPC
  [1.1] **MPC** | ΔC/ΔY = **slope of the consumption function**
  [1.2] Saving | **S = Y − C**; **MPS = ΔS/ΔY**
  [1.3] MPC and MPS | **MPC + MPS = 1**
  [1.4] **Autonomous spending** | Independent of income
  [1.5] **Autonomous consumption** (C̄) | Consumption even at **zero income** (from past savings or borrowing)
  [1.6] Induced consumption | Changes with income
  [1.7] **Induced spending / investment** | Varies with the **level of national income**
  [1.8] Aggregate demand | Total demand for final goods and services
  [1.9] Inventory | Stock of unsold goods
  [1.10] **Multiplier (k)** | **k = 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/MPS** – a **low MPC gives a low multiplier**; MPS **0.25** → k = **4**
  [1.11] Accelerator | Induced investment ÷ **change** in income (J.M. Clark)
  [1.12] **Marginal efficiency of investment** | Invest while MEI > interest rate: MEI **7%** > **6%** → **profitable**
  [1.13] **Ex-ante / ex-post** | Planned (intended) values / actual (realised) values
[2] h: Saving and investment
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] **Mobilisation of domestic savings** | Depends on past behaviour, the **propensity to save** and **tax policy**
  [3.1] Net investment | Gross − depreciation; gross investment that only covers depreciation → net investment **zero**
  [3.2] Realised investment | Planned + unplanned (unintended change in inventories)
  [3.3] Equilibrium | Planned saving = planned investment
[4] h: Shifts and history
[5] b: **After World War II** many economists expected a slump because of the **sharp cut in government purchases** at demobilisation; pent-up consumer demand prevented it.
[6] b: **IS-LM:** an **easy money policy** shifts the LM (L = M) curve **right/down** – interest falls, income rises; the IS (I = S) curve shifts **right** when autonomous spending rises at every interest rate (e.g. technology raising investment), and left with more saving, higher taxes or lower government purchases.
### part 3: Personal, Disposable and Per Capita Income
[0] h: Income measures
[1] table: Measure | Derivation
  [1.0] **Personal income (PI)** | NI − undistributed corporate profits − social security contributions − corporate tax + government and business **transfer payments** + net interest paid by government
  [1.1] **Personal disposable income (PDI)** | **PI − personal direct taxes − non-tax payments (fines, fees)**; always less than PI
  [1.2] PI − PDI | = **direct taxes**
  [1.3] **Per capita income (PCI)** | **National income ÷ population** = NNP ÷ total population (not GDP or NDP ÷ population)
  [1.4] NDP | GDP − depreciation
  [1.5] National savings | National disposable income − consumption
[2] h: Per capita income
[3] table: Fact | Key detail
  [3.0] Meaning | **PCI = average income** of a country; shows the **standard of living** and **regional imbalances**
  [3.1] Welfare | PCI is a better welfare measure than GNP
  [3.2] **PCI falls while NI rises** | When **population grows faster than NI**; PCI rises only when NI grows faster than population
  [3.3] GNP vs GDP | GNP is not always higher than GDP – India's NFIA is negative
  [3.4] Comparing economies | GDP is used, not NDP
  [3.5] **Purchasing power** | The goods and services a person can buy – indicated by his **income**
### part 4: GDP and GNP – Definitions and NFIA
[0] h: Definitions
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** | Value of **all final goods and services** produced **within the domestic territory** of a country during a year, whoever produces them
  [1.1] **GNP (Gross National Product)** | Value of final goods and services produced by the **normal residents** and their property, **inside or outside** the country, in a year = **Gross National Income**
[2] table: Basis | GDP | GNP
  [2.0] Based on | Domestic territory (location) | Normal residents (nationality)
  [2.1] Indians earning abroad | Excluded | Included
  [2.2] Link | **GDP = GNP − NFIA** | **GNP = GDP + NFIA**
[3] h: NFIA
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **Main difference** GDP vs GNP | **Net factor income from abroad** (not transfer payments or depreciation)
  [4.1] **NFIA** | Factor income **received from** abroad − factor income **paid to** abroad
  [4.2] Formula | GNP = GDP + X − M (X = income of nationals abroad, M = income of foreigners at home)
  [4.3] **GDP = GNP is possible** | When NFIA = 0 (e.g. a closed economy)
  [4.4] **India** | NFIA usually **negative** → GNP **below** GDP
  [4.5] Income earned in Saudi Arabia by an Indian resident | Counts in India's **GNP only**

[0] h: Depreciation
[1] b: **Depreciation** (consumption of fixed capital) = loss in value of **machinery** and other fixed capital through wear and tear – deducted because output that only replaces worn-out capital is **not new output**.
[2] h: Gross to net
[3] table: Aggregate | Formula
  [3.0] **NNP** | **GNP − depreciation** = **GDP + NFIA − depreciation**

  [3.3] **NDP** | **GDP − depreciation**

[0] h: Transfer payments

[0] h: Expenditure method

[0] h: The three methods
[1] table: Method | Also called | What is summed
  [1.0] **Product / production** | Output method, **value added method**, net product method, inventory method, final product method | Value added by every producing unit (output − intermediate consumption) = total **final product**

### part 2: Income Method – Factor Incomes, Inclusions and Exclusions
[0] h: Income method
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **National income** | **Income of factors of production** – rent + wages + interest + profit (not government income, PSU profits as such, or a State budget)
  [1.1] **Factor income** | Income accruing to a factor of production in return for services rendered to the production unit
  [1.2] Economic Survey formula | **GDP = compensation of employees + consumption of fixed capital + (taxes on production − subsidies on production) + gross operating surplus**
  [1.3] **Transfer income** | Income **not produced by any production process** – received without any service in return; pensions, scholarships, gifts, old-age allowances
[2] h: Inclusions and exclusions
[3] table: Item | In NI? | Reason
  [3.0] Rent, wages, interest on productive loans, dividends, **undistributed profits**, **mixed income** | Yes | Factor incomes
  [3.1] **Profits of public enterprises** | Yes | Factor income (profit) of the State's enterprises
  [3.2] Imputed rent of owner-occupied houses; income from agriculture; self-consumed farm output | Yes | Production / imputed service
  [3.3] Change in inventories (production − sales) | Yes | Part of output
  [3.4] **Pension**, scholarships, gifts | No | Transfer payments
  [3.5] **Tax revenue** | No | Transfer to government, not a factor income
  [3.6] Interest on public debt | No | Treated as a transfer
  [3.7] **Housewife's services** | No | Not marketed
  [3.8] Second-hand sales | No | Only the dealer's / broker's commission counts
  [3.9] Shares and bonds | No | Financial transaction – no new production
  [3.10] Transfer earnings | No | Not payments for current factor services
[4] x: Imputed rent and PSU profits are **in** NI; tax revenue, pension, interest on public debt and housewives' work are **out**.

### part 5: Product (Value Added) Method and Double Counting
[0] h: Value added method
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] **Value added** | **Value of output − intermediate consumption**; summed at every stage = value of the final product
  [1.1] **Double counting** | Adding sales at every stage (cotton farmer → weaver → tailor) counts the cotton again and again
  [1.2] Remedy | Count **only final goods** or the **value added** at each stage; **intermediate goods are excluded** – both routes give the same total
  [1.3] Net value added | **Gross value added − depreciation**
  [1.4] **Domestic income** | **Sum of net value added at factor cost** of all sectors within the domestic territory = **Net Domestic Product at factor cost**
  [1.5] National income | NDP at FC + NFIA = NNP at FC
  [1.6] **Change in inventories** | **Production − sales**; treated as investment
  [1.7] **Double deflation** (**2022-23** series) | **Real GVA = real output − real intermediate consumption**, output and inputs deflated separately

[0] h: Central tendency

  [1.2] **Median** | The value that **divides an ordered series into two equal parts**

[5] table: Fact | Key detail
