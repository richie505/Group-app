### part 0: Religious Policy – Ibadat Khana, Mahzar, Din-i-Ilahi, Sulh-i-Kul
[0] h: Measures in order
[1] table: Year | Measure
  [1.0] **1563** | **Pilgrimage tax abolished** by Akbar
  [1.1] **1564** | **Jizya abolished**

[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] **Sulh-i-Kul** | "**Peace with all**" (universal harmony) – guiding principle **promulgated by Akbar**; the result of **political generosity, religious tolerance and a liberal cultural attitude**

  [4.4] Popularity | Rested on **mansabdari, religious policy, land revenue and social reforms**

[8] h: Din-i-Ilahi (1582)
[9] table: Fact | Key detail
  [9.0] Founder | **Promoted / promulgated by Akbar**; **launched 1582**
  [9.1] Features | **One God**, greeting "Allah-hu-Akbar", reverence for **sun and fire**, avoiding meat; piety, prudence, kindness
  [9.2] Membership | **Never forced** – a voluntary small circle of about **18**, not a mass religion; **Birbal the only Hindu**
  [9.3] Chief exponent | **Abul Fazl**
  [9.4] End | **It ended with Akbar's death**
  [9.5] Why it failed | Not patronised after Akbar, rejected by orthodox Muslims, never projected to the masses
  [9.6] Nature | An ideology based on **Sufi pantheism**, not a religion; **Mohsin Fani** (Jahangir's time) called it a religion in **Dabistan-i-Mazahib**
  [9.7] Orthodox critics | **Badauni** and **Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi** (Naqshbandi, "Mujaddid Alf-i-Sani")

## topic group2 1-61 | Akbar's Institutions – Mansabdari System, Todar Mal's Bandobast, Dahsala, Zabti and Other Revenue Systems
### part 0: Revenue Systems – Todar Mal's Bandobast, Dahsala, Zabti
[0] h: Road to the Dahsala
[1] b: Sher Shah's survey system → **Karori experiment (1574)** → **Dahsala (1580)** under **Raja Todar Mal**.
[2] table: Fact | Key detail
  [2.0] **Karori experiment (1574)** | Core provinces split into circles each yielding **one crore dams**, under officers called **karoris**, who collected revenue and cultivation data
  [2.1] Result | Mansabdari + zabti-dahsala = a **salaried, transferable nobility** + **predictable cash revenue**
[3] h: Raja Todar Mal
[4] table: Fact | Key detail
  [4.0] Career | Earlier served **Sher Shah** and built on his survey-based system; **Diwan** (finance minister); one of the Navratnas
  [4.1] Known for | **Land revenue reforms**
  [4.2] First Diwan | **Muzaffar Khan Turbati** – the **first to be given the proper status of Diwan** by Akbar, with Todar Mal as assistant; Todar Mal and **Khwaja Shah Mansur** came later
[5] table: Unit | Key detail
  [5.0] Gaz-i-Ilahi | Unit of length, about **82 cm**
  [5.1] Jarib | **Bamboo pieces joined with iron rings** – replaced hemp rope
  [5.2] State share | **One-third** of average produce, paid in cash (**dam**); kind allowed in some areas
[6] h: The systems
[7] table: System | Meaning | Region / year
  [7.0] **Zabti** | Land measured; **dasturs** = cash rates per crop per bigha | **Introduced by Akbar**; core North Indian provinces (Lahore to Allahabad, Malwa, Gujarat)
  [7.1] **Dahsala (Ain-i-Dahsala)** | Revenue on the **average produce and prices of the last ten (10) years (1571-80)**, converted into cash | **1580**
  [7.2] Batai / **Ghalla-bakhshi** | Crop-sharing | Thatta, Kabul, Kashmir
  [7.3] **Nasaq** | Rough assessment from past revenue records | Mainly Bengal
  [7.4] **Kankut** | Yield estimated on the standing crop | Where measuring was impractical
  [7.5] **Plough assessment** | By **number of ploughs** | **Parts of the Deccan** under Akbar (J.N. Sarkar)
[8] b: **"Bandobast system" = Dahsala**; also called Zabti-Bandobast.

### part 2: Mansabdari System
[0] h: Mansabdari system
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Origin | **Introduced by Akbar**; of **Central Asian / Mongol** military origin; basis of Mughal civil and military administration
  [1.1] Nature | **Combined civil-military, non-hereditary** ranks, paid by **jagir** (or cash)
  [1.2] Ranks | **10 to 5,000** for nobles (later **7,000**); higher ranks only for **royal princes**
  [1.3] **Zat** | Personal rank and pay
  [1.4] **Sawar** | Horsemen to maintain; dual ranks from **1595-96**; sawar equal to zat = **1st class**
  [1.5] **Dagh** | Horse branding – **1573-74**
  [1.6] Horses | For **every ten cavalrymen, twenty horses** – horses had to be **rested on the march and replaced in war**
  [1.7] **Du-aspa sih-aspa** | Troopers with two/three horses without raising zat – **Jahangir** (first for Mahabat Khan)

## topic group2 1-69 | Sikhism - the Ten Gurus, Guru Granth Sahib, the Khalsa; Banda Bahadur and Ranjit Singh
### part 0: Guru Gobind Singh and the Khalsa
[0] h: Guru Gobind Singh
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Position | **Guru Gobind Singh** (1666-1708) = **tenth and last human Guru** of the Sikhs
  [1.1] Birth | **Born 1666 at Patna** (Bihar) – Takht Sri Patna Sahib marks the birthplace
  [1.2] **Khalsa** | **Founded the Khalsa** ("army of the pure") on **Baisakhi, 13 April 1699**, at **Anandpur** (Takht Sri **Keshgarh Sahib**, Rupnagar); the **Panj Pyare** (five volunteers) were baptised first with sacred nectar
  [1.3] **His greatness** | He **turned the Sikhs into a military order** (Khalsa; surnames **Singh / Kaur**); the Sikh state was founded by Banda Bahadur (1710)
  [1.4] **Five Ks** | **Kesh** (hair), **Kangha** (comb), **Kara** (steel bangle), **Kachera** (undergarment), **Kirpan** (sword)
  [1.5] Writings | The **Zafarnama** (Persian letter to Aurangzeb accusing the Mughals of betrayal); Dasam Granth at **Paonta Sahib**
  [1.6] **Chali Mukte** | The 40 liberated ones who died defending him at **Muktsar** (Maghi)
  [1.7] Last years | Fought Aurangzeb from 1699; made peace with **Bahadur Shah**; killed at **Nanded** (1708)
  [1.8] Succession | Declared the **Guru Granth Sahib the eternal Guru** – personal Guruship ended
  [1.9] **350th birth anniversary (Prakash Parv)** | The PM attended the celebrations at **Patna** (Gandhi Maidan) on **5 January 2017** and released a stamp
[2] x: Gobind Singh: Patna 1666 → Khalsa, Anandpur, Baisakhi 1699 → Nanded 1708; 10th and last Guru.

  [1.0] Birth | **1780** (13 Nov) at **Gujranwala**
  [1.1] Father | **Maha (Mahan) Singh**, chief of the **Sukerchakia misl**; died when Ranjit Singh was a boy
  [1.2] Title | **"Sher-e-Punjab" (Lion of Punjab)**

  [1.4] Capitals | **1799: captured Lahore** → **political capital Lahore**, **religious capital Amritsar** (Amritsar taken 1802/1805)
  [1.5] **1801** | Proclaimed **Maharaja**
  [1.6] Army | On European lines (**Allard**, Ventura; **Fauj-i-Khas**); commander **Hari Singh Nalwa**
  [1.7] **Treaty of Amritsar, 25 April 1809** | Lord **Minto**; negotiated by **Charles Metcalfe**: **Sutlej** = boundary; Ranjit Singh gave up the cis-Sutlej states and turned west
  [1.8] Conquests | **Multan 1818, Kashmir (Srinagar) 1819**, Peshawar 1834 – his empire included **Srinagar**
  [1.9] Kohinoor | **1813:** got the **Kohinoor** from **Shah Shuja** (Nadir Shah had taken it from Delhi in 1739)

  [1.12] Golden Temple | Covered the Harmandir Sahib in gold → "Golden Temple"
[2] b: **Secular ruler:** Hindu and Muslim ministers; blind in one eye – "**God intended me to look upon all religions with one eye, that is why He took away the light from the other**" = **Ranjit Singh**.
[3] x: Sukerchakia → Lahore 1799 (Zaman Shah's "Raja") → Amritsar treaty 1809 (Sutlej) → Kohinoor 1813 → Kashmir 1819 → died 1839.

### part 7: Guru Granth Sahib
[0] h: Guru Granth Sahib
[1] table: Fact | Key detail
  [1.0] Compilation | **Adi Granth compiled by Guru Arjan (5th) in 1604**
  [1.1] Final version | **By Guru Gobind Singh at Damdama Sahib (1705)**
  [1.2] Status | Declared the **eternal Guru** (1708)
  [1.3] Script and language | Script **Gurmukhi**; language "Sant Bhasha" (a mix of Punjabi, Hindi and dialects)

  [1.5] Marriage | Sikh marriage **Anand Karaj** = four circumambulations of the Granth while **Lavan** hymns are sung
