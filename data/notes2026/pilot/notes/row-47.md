=== Gathasaptasati and Hala
- **Compiler:** the Satavahana king **Hala** (17th king, ruled 5 years), himself a poet – the most repeated fact of the topic (G2-A 1984, G2-B 1989, G2-A 1991, G2 2008 ×2; Welfare Officers 2017 ×2; APPSC PYQ ×5).
- **Distractors:** Gunadhya, Sarvavarma, Kuntala Satakarni, Shudraka, Pulomavi, Lilavati, Rudramadevi, Yajnasri.
- **Work:** **Gathasaptasati** (Sattasai) – **700** Prakrit gathas, in **Maharashtri Prakrit**.
- **Themes:** **love and romance**, rural life and nature (G2 PYQ statement ✓).
- **Women poets:** **Anulakshmi, Anupalabdha (Anupamalabdha), Reva (Revati), Madhavi** – the text "containing writing by women" (G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ); not Brihatkatha, Kamasutra or Lilavati.
- **Religion in it:** opens with a prayer to **Shiva**, ends with a Gauri stotra; names Gauri, Indra, Ganesha; Radha-Krishna stories.
- **Social picture:** sugar and jaggery, hanging of criminals, sati, no child marriage, prostitution, group dances, plight of wives of seafaring merchants.
- **Desi (Telugu) words:** atta, addam, potta, pilla, padi, pandi, tuppu – earliest traces of Telugu.
- **⚠ Trap:** a source for **society**, not for early **political** history (G2 2017).
# Hala
- **Title:** **Kavivatsala**; honoured poets **Kumarila** and **Sripalita**; married the Sri Lankan princess **Lilavati**.
- **Golden age of Prakrit:** Hala's reign. **⚠ Trap:** NextGen p.23 prints "golden age of Sanskrit" – its own p.9 says Prakrit; Sanskrit was Kuntala Satakarni's cause.
~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.1-2, p.9, p.23; Rocket AP History p.9, p.52; app notes 1:47 (sub 10)

=== Guilds (Srenis) and Guild Banking
- **Shreni:** guild of traders or artisans of one craft; fixed work rules, **quality and prices**, controlled its members' conduct; head = **Shreshthi (Setti)**.
- **⚠ Trap:** guilds existed in **north and south** India – "prevalent only in northern India" is the untrue statement (State PSC).
- **Number:** **18** guilds in Satavahana records; they later hardened into castes.

|---|---|

| **Kularika** | **Potters** (JA Panchayat Raj 2012) |
| **Gandhika** | **Perfumers** – noted donors to Buddhist causes (JA NTR UHS) |
| Tilapishaka | Oil pressers |
| Udayantrika | Makers / operators of water machines |
| Halika | Farmers |
| Malakara | Florists |

| Chammakara | Leather workers |
- **⚠ Trap:** butchers = Katika, oil pressers = Ganugavaru, weavers = Salevaru – distractors for Padhika.
# Guilds as banks
- **Interest:** guilds took deposits and lent at **12% a year** (1% a month) (G2 2008 ×2; G2 2019 ×2).
- **Rishabhadatta (Ushavadatta):** Nahapana's son-in-law; his **Nasik inscription** records **2,000 karshapanas** with the **Govardhana weavers' guild** at 12% for monks' robes (G2 2017; G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ).
  - A second deposit: 1,000 karshapanas at 3/4% a month. NextGen says "borrowed" – the inscription records a permanent deposit.
- **⚠ Trap:** Sopara, Kanheri and Pratishthana inscriptions are distractors.
- **Not Satavahana guilds:** **Chitrameli** = guild of **agriculturists** in medieval Deccan and South India (G2 2019); **Nigamalu** = Ikshvaku trade guilds.
~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.17-18; Rocket AP History p.41, p.48; app notes 1:47 (sub 4)

=== Administration – Units and Officers
# Units
- **Largest administrative division:** the **Ahara** (APPSC PYQ; APSLPRB 2023) – not Janapada, Vishaya, Nadu, Sima or Sthala.
- **Hierarchy:** kingdom → **Ahara** → Nigamas (towns) / Vishaya → **Grama** (village); Janapadas = vassal territories.
- **Head of an Ahara:** the **Amatya**, usually a blood relative or son of the king (Sachivalayam 2019 ×2; APPSC PYQ); distant aharas under **Mahasenadhipatis**.
- **⚠ Trap:** not Yuvaraja, Rajuka, Mahamatra, Kumaramatya or Vishayapati.
- **'Amatya' meant:** **minister** (APPSC PYQ); four grades – Vishva-amatya (inner adviser), Raja-amatya (executes orders), Maha-amatya (special/finance), Amatya.
- **Mantri Parishad:** council that helped the king.
# Town and village
- **Nigama Sabha:** ran **cities/towns**, not villages (G2 2008; APPSC PYQ); members = **Gahapatis**; named in the **Bhattiprolu** inscription.
- **Gramika:** **village headman** (APPSC PYQ); Gramani – over 5 or 10 villages; Gahapati – farmers' representative.
- **Gaulmika (Gulmika):** head of a **gulma** (cluster of villages / military outpost) – controlled the **military bases** (APPSC PYQ); also border-defence commander (Myakadoni).
- **⚠ Trap:** NextGen calls the Gulmika the village head; the exam answer for "village headman" is **Gramika**.
# Officers

| Bhandagarika | Stores; revenue in kind |

- **⚠ Trap:** Mahataraka, Dhutaka, Pratihari, Patlika-palaka are distractors for treasurer and archivist.

=== Official Language – Prakrit
- **Official, royal and inscription language:** **Prakrit** (G2 2011; Port Officer 2011; GS common paper; APPSC PYQ ×3); script **Brahmi**.
- **Which Prakrit:** **Maharashtri Prakrit** (Welfare Officers).
- **⚠ Trap:** not Sanskrit, Pali, Telugu, Tamil, Marathi or Aramaic.
- **Early Ikshvaku inscriptions:** also **Prakrit** (Gazetted 2006); later Ikshvaku records turned to Sanskrit.
- **Later shift:** Salankayanas – Sanskrit replaced Prakrit (5 of 8 inscriptions); Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi – Telugu.
- **Sanskrit grew alongside:** **Kuntala Satakarni** made Sanskrit the royal language; Mahayana texts were written in Sanskrit.
> A-R angle (UP RO 2017): "Sanskrit developed along with Prakrit and folk languages" ✓ because "kings promoted Sanskrit and folk languages in literature" ✓ – R explains A.
> Statement angle (UPPCS 2021): "Satavahanas were nurturers of Prakrit" ✓; "public aspect of the arts got more encouragement" ✓ → both.
- **Firsts:** first complete Sanskrit inscription = **Junagadh** (Rudradaman); first Telugu word **"Nagabu"** (Amaravati); "Vepuru" in Myakadoni.
- **Desi bhasha:** language of the common people, of the **Dravidian** family.
~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.9, p.23-24; Rocket AP History p.16; app notes 1:47 (sub 8)

=== Coinage
- **Currency:** the **karshapana** (APPSC PYQ); 1 suvarna (gold unit) = **35 karshapanas**; Sathamana, Rama Raya, Venkataraya are later coins.
- **Metals:** **lead** (most common), copper, bronze, potin, silver – **nickel was NOT used** (ACF 2022).
- **⚠ Sources differ:** NextGen lists gold among Satavahana coin metals — settled: they minted base metals, lead dominant; gold was not a mint metal (https://iaspoint.com/satavahana-coinage/).
- **⚠ Trap:** **iron coins with a thin copper coating** = **Ikshvakus** (APPSC key; G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ) – not Satavahanas, Salankayanas or Vishnukundins.
- **Ship-motif coins:** **Vasishthiputra Pulumavi** and **Yajnasri Satakarni** – proof of **sea trade** (G2-A 1989).
- **Who minted ship coins:** standard answer **Yajnasri Satakarni** – anchored-ship mark (JL 2004).
- **Typical symbol:** the **ship** (Asst Social Welfare) – not Lakshmi, Garuda or arrow; no later ruler issued ship-motif coins.
- **Simuka's coins:** mostly at **Kotilingala** (Karimnagar), legend "Siri Chimuka Sata"; also Kondapur (Medak) (Asst Social Welfare); Dhulikatta is a distractor.
> A-R angle (Welfare Officers): "Coins are a crucial source" ✓; "Satakarni's coins show only religious symbols, no portraits" ✗ – they carry elephant, horse, lion and the Ujjain symbol, and portrait coins began with Gautamiputra.
- **Jogalthambi:** Nahapana's silver coins restruck by Gautamiputra.
- **Bilingual silver:** Vasishthiputra Satakarni – Prakrit + a Dravidian language.
~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.10, p.19-21; Rocket AP History p.14, p.19; app notes 1:47 (sub 7); https://iaspoint.com/satavahana-coinage/

=== Trade, Ports and Roman Contact
| Coast | Ports (Satavahana period) |
|---|---|
| West | **Sopara (Shurparaka)**, Bharukachha (Broach / Barygaza), Kalyan |
| East | **Maisolia (Machilipatnam)**, Kantakossyla (Ghantasala), Koddura (Gudur, Krishna), Arikamedu (Puducherry) |
- **Western port:** **Sopara** – in the Satavahanas' Konkan heartland (G2-A 1984, owner's key).
- **⚠ Trap:** Broach was often in Western Kshatrapa hands — exams mark Sopara as the Satavahana western port; Broach came under the Satavahanas only after Nahapana's defeat, and Kalyan was the main port before it.
- **⚠ Sources differ:** NextGen puts Kalyan in Karnataka — settled: Kalyan is in Thane district, **Maharashtra**; the Periplus records it (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalyan).
- **East-coast ports:** Ghantasala and Masulipatnam (Welfare Officers: "2 and 4"); Barygaza and Kalyan are western; Koddura is eastern too.
- **Ptolemy's "great market":** **Maisolia (Machilipatnam)** (APPSC PYQ); not Nizampatnam, Kalingapatnam, Kotilingala.
- **⚠ Trap:** Motupalli is an east-coast port that rose under the Kakatiyas — not a western Satavahana port (Rocket Sheets misprint); "port not used in Satavahana times" = **Motupalli** (APPSC PYQ). Today in Bapatla district.
- **Koddura:** the Periplus' only east-coast port of the Satavahanas.
# Evidence of sea trade
- **All three testify:** the **Periplus**, **Roman coins** found in Andhra-Telangana, and the **ship coins** of Pulomavi and Yajnasri (Welfare Officers Mains).
- **Roman objects found most in India:** **coins** (IAS 1999) – Bhattiprolu, Nagarjunakonda, Attirala, Salihundam, Vijayawada, Vinukonda, Nellore.
- **Pliny:** Roman gold drained to India for fine cloth (mallasella, muslin).
- **Reach:** Rome, and the eastern islands – Siam, Java, Borneo.
> A-R angle (G2 PYQ): "Trade with Rome flourished after the Satavahanas" ✗ (it peaked under them and declined); "no ruler issued ship-motif coins after them" ✓.

=== Land Revenue, Taxes and Tax-free Grants
- **Bhaga:** land revenue = king's share of **1/6** of the produce (JA NTR UHS) – the chief income.
- **First tax-free land grants:** to **Brahmanas and Buddhist monks** – by the **Satavahanas** (Deputy screening 2023; Welfare Officers) – not Parthians, Kushanas, Shakas, Guptas, Mauryas or Ikshvakus.
- **Satakarni I:** gave tax-free land, cows and gold to Brahmanas (Naneghat); granted land could not be entered by royal officers.
- **Other dues:** Karukara (artisans' and professionals' tax), Kara, Sulka (water tax), Deya-meya (king's share in kind).
- **Vetti:** forced unpaid labour; also the term for the exploitation of village servant castes by **feudal lords in Telangana** (Social Structure 2016).
- **⚠ Trap:** **Moturpha** was a **British** tax on trades and occupations (looms) in Andhra, not Satavahana; and the British salt monopoly did apply in Andhra (A ✓, R ✗; Welfare Officers).
- **Nivartana:** land unit, about 1.5 acres.
~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.7, p.16-17; Rocket AP History p.48; app notes 1:47 (sub 3)

=== Society and Women
- **G2 2008 statement:** kings followed Hinduism (Vedic), some queens patronised Buddhism ✓; **chaturvarna** existed ✓; shipping developed ✓; "**women were looked down upon**" ✗.
- **Women respected:** joined festivals, donated to viharas, issued inscriptions; titles Maharathini, Mahabhoji, Mahasenapatni.
- **Patriarchal:** **both family and kingship** were patriarchal (G2 2011; APPSC PYQ); joint families.
# Four status classes
| Class | Members |
|---|---|
| 1st | Feudal nobility – **Maharathis**, Mahabhojas, Mahasenapatis |
| 2nd | Officials – Amatyas, **Mahamatras**, Bhandagarikas |
| 3rd | Professionals – Vaidya, Lekhaka, **Suvarnakara, Gandhika**, Halakiya |
| 4th | Artisans and petty traders – Malakara, Vadhaki, **Lohavanija** |
- **Order question:** Maharathas → Mahamatras → Gandhikas → Lohavanijas (Sachivalayam).
- **Third class:** Suvarnakara and Gandhika ("1 and 5") (Welfare Officers); ⚠ some lists put the Lekhaka in the 2nd class.
- **Others:** Sakas and Yavanas absorbed with Indian names; slavery (Brihatkatha, Kamasutra); a sati statue at Nagarjunakonda.

=== Feudatories and Military Camps
- **Not strongly centralised:** many vassal janapadas run by local kings, supervised by **Mahabhojas** and **Maharathas**.
> A-R angle (Welfare Officers): "Semi-autonomous Bhojas and Maharathis made the polity decentralised" ✓; "the centre discouraged local leaders and ruled all areas directly" ✗.
| Feudatory | Feature |
|---|---|
| Maharathas | Hereditary; collected (and could remit) taxes |
| Mahabhojas | Hereditary; inscriptions omit the emperor → near-independent |
| Mahasenapatis / Mahasenadhipatis, Mahatalavaras | Commanders of border provinces against the Sakas |
- **⚠ Trap:** **Mahajanapadas** were NOT Satavahana feudal chiefs (Social & Cultural History).
- **Kataka:** permanent garrison; **Skandhavara:** temporary camp.
- **Meaning together:** **military camps and settlements** that served as **administrative centres** when the king was present (G1 2024; JA NTR UHS); not monks' tax-free villages, rock-cut areas or paddy lands.
- **Army:** chaturanga bala (Hathigumpha); battle order in the Amaravati sculptures.
