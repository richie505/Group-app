## SUB 0 | Amaravati Mahachaitya
[0:0] - **Where:** ancient **Dhanyakataka** (Amaravati–Dharanikota) on the **Krishna**, Andhra Pradesh (UPSC 2006 match); old Guntur district (now Palnadu district).
[0:1] - **Built under:** the **Satavahanas** (APPSC PYQ) – not Vengi Chalukyas, Ikshvakus or Salankayanas; NextGen dates the main building to **Pulomavi II**.
[0:2] - **Enlarged, with the stone railing round the Mahachaitya:** in the reign of **Yajnasri Satakarni**, by his contemporary **Acharya Nagarjuna** (G2 2001; Sachivalayam ×2; G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ).
[0:3]   - Options printed "Gautamiputra Yagnasri" = Yajnasri Satakarni. When the question asks for the king, do not mark Nagarjuna.
[0:4] - **⚠ Trap:** Vasishthiputra Satakarni (earlier vedika sculptures), Vasishthiputra Pulomavi, Gautamiputra and Srimukha are distractors; final enlargement under the Ikshvakus.
[0:5] - **Origin:** Ashokan age – **Mahadeva Bhikshu**, first Buddhist teacher in Andhra, enshrined the Buddha's relics; Naga king **Nagashoka** helped build it at Dharanikota.
[0:6] # Size
[0:7] - **Biggest stupa in Andhra Pradesh:** Amaravati (G2 2019 prelims; G2 PYQ) and the **largest in South India** (Welfare Officers 2017); Bhattiprolu is the **oldest**.
[0:8] - **Larger and more artistic:** than Sanchi and Bharhut (Welfare Officers 2017).
[0:9] - **⚠ Sources differ:** NextGen calls Sanchi "the largest stupa in India".
[0:10]   - Settled: Amaravati's railing was ~59 m across vs Sanchi's 36.6 m dome, so Amaravati was larger (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaravati_Stupa; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanchi).
[0:11] # Ayaka platform
[0:12] - **Platform with ayaka (Aryaka) pillars:** hallmark of the **Amaravati** stupa (UPPCS 2022); unique to Andhra stupas.
[0:13] - **Layout:** 4 ayaka platforms at the cardinal points × 5 pillars = five events of the Buddha's life.
[0:14] TABLE head: Event | Symbol
  [0:14.0] Birth | Lotus
  [0:14.1] Great Departure | Horse
  [0:14.2] Enlightenment | Bodhi (peepal) tree
  [0:14.3] First sermon | Wheel
  [0:14.4] Mahaparinirvana | Stupa
[0:15] # Discovery and the Amaravati school
[0:16] - **Identified:** Col. **Colin Mackenzie**, 1797; excavated by Robert Sewell, 1877; James **Fergusson** (Tree and Serpent Worship): "epitome of Indian architecture".
[0:17] - **Ayaka gift:** the artisan **Siddhattha** gave five ayaka pillars at the eastern gate.
[0:18] - **Amaravati school:** white **Palnadu limestone**; narrative Jataka reliefs; early aniconic Buddha (tree, throne, footprints), later human form.
[0:19] - **Sculptures today:** British Museum (London), Government Museum (Chennai), Amaravati site museum.

## SUB 1 | Stupa, Chaitya, Vihara – Terms
[1:0] - **Chaitya:** **prayer hall / place of worship** (Chaitya-shala, Chaitya-griha); rectangular, ending in an **apse (horseshoe plan)** with the stupa; horseshoe **chaitya window** on the facade.
[1:1] - **Vihara:** **monastery – dwelling of the monks**; square, cells round a central hall (**Madhyashala**).
[1:2] > Most-asked angle (UPSC 2013; IAS 2013; UP 2001; APPSC PYQ): "Chaitya = place of worship, Vihara = dwelling of monks".
[1:3] - **Stupa, chaitya and vihara:** all **Buddhist** structures (State PSC) – not Ajivika, Vaishnava or Shaiva.
[1:4] - **Stupa:** a **mound**, generally a **repository of relics**, and a **votive and commemorative** structure.
[1:5] - **⚠ Trap:** the stupa concept is **not Buddhist in origin** – it predates Buddhism (UPSC 2023: only two of three statements correct).
[1:6] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:6.0] Stupa | Mound
  [1:6.1] Garbhagriha | Place where the deity's image is installed
  [1:6.2] Mandapa | Place in temples where people assemble
  [1:6.3] Pradakshina patha | Circular path around the stupa
[1:7] > Match angle (APPSC): 1-(b), 2-(a), 3-(d), 4-(c).
[1:8] - **Arama:** stupa + vihara + chaitya together (e.g. Guntupalli).
[1:9] - **Stupa types:** Dhatugarbha (bodily relics), Paribhogika (objects used by the Buddha), Uddeshika (symbolic).
[1:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.22; app notes 1:48 (sub 0)
## SUB 2 | Relic Stupas and Other Andhra Buddhist Sites
[2:0] - **Built over whose relics:** the stupas at **Amaravati, Jaggayyapeta and Nagarjunakonda** – over **Gautama Buddha's** relics (G2 2002; APPSC PYQ); not Nagarjuna, Mahavira or Gomateshwara.
[2:1] - **Centres of Buddhism in AP:** Amaravati, Bhattiprolu, Guntupalli – "all the above" (AEE 2011).
[2:2] TABLE head: Site | Key fact
  [2:2.0] Bhattiprolu (Bapatla dt, old Guntur) | Oldest stupa in AP; relic-casket inscriptions name Raja Kubiraka (3rd c. BC) and the Nigama Sabha; excavated by Alexander Rea, 1892
  [2:2.1] Jaggayyapeta (NTR dt, old Krishna) | Mandhata (chakravartin) sculpture with raised hand; excavated by James Burgess, 1882
  [2:2.2] Ghantasala (Krishna) | Stupa at the port Kantakossyla
  [2:2.3] Guntupalli (Eluru) | Oldest chaitya in AP; rock-cut
  [2:2.4] Salihundam (Srikakulam) | Relic stupa
  [2:2.5] Sankaram–Bojjannakonda (Anakapalli) | Rock-cut monastery on twin hills
  [2:2.6] Thotlakonda, Bavikonda (Visakhapatnam) | Hill-top monasteries; Thotlakonda spotted in an Indian Navy aerial survey
  [2:2.7] Kondapur (Sangareddy, Telangana) | Fortified Satavahana town; terracotta, beads, coins
  [2:2.8] Phanigiri (Suryapet, Telangana) | Life-size stucco sculpture found recently; 3,730 Ikshvaku lead coins (2024)

[2:4] - **Mandhata sculpture:** **Jaggayyapeta** (G1 1991) – not Simhachalam, Kondapuram.
[2:5] - **Current link:** the rare life-size stucco sculpture came from a Buddhist site in **Telangana** (Phanigiri) (G1 PYQ).
[2:6] > A-R angle (Welfare Officers): "Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda became big stupa-monastery complexes" ✓ because "they lay near urban centres on trade and pilgrimage routes" ✓ – R explains A.

## SUB 4 | Tambaya Danamu – Kotturu
[4:0] - **Term:** **"Tambaya Dana(mu)"** – a donation term at the Buddhist site of **Kotturu (Dhanadibbalu)** on the **Sarada** river near Yelamanchili (G2 2017; APPSC PYQ; Police Constable 2019).
[4:1] - **District key:** **Visakhapatnam** (G2 2017) (now Anakapalli district).
[4:2] - **⚠ Outdated:** Kotturu's district was "Visakhapatnam" — now **Anakapalli district** (Rambilli mandal), after the April 2022 district reorganisation (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotturu_Dhanadibbalu).
[4:3] - **⚠ Trap:** Chandavaram, Amaravati, Bhattiprolu; Kurnool, Krishna, West Godavari are distractors.

## SUB 5 | Rock-cut Caves – Karle, Guntupalli
[5:0] - **Karle (Pune):** the **largest and most evolved rock-cut chaitya hall of the Hinayana phase** (UPRO 2014); not Pitalkhora, Junnar or Bedsa. Bhaja is earlier.
[5:1] - **Guntupalli (West Godavari, now Eluru district):** its early chaityagrihas and viharas are **rock-cut** ✓ (UPPCS 2017 ×2).
[5:2] - **⚠ Trap:** eastern Deccan chaityas and viharas are usually **structural**, not rock-cut – Guntupalli is the exception → "only 1 is correct".
[5:3] - **⚠ Outdated:** "Guntupalli in West Godavari" — now **Eluru district** (Kamavarapukota mandal) after the 2022 reorganisation.
[5:4] - **Later imitation:** Buddhists cut the first rock-cut chaityas; Vaishnavas, Shaivas and Jainas imitated them later ✓ – at the same sites, not far away ✗ (NDA 2011).
[5:5] - **Western Deccan caves:** Nasik, Naneghat, Bhaja, Kanheri, Junnar, Bedsa, Pitalkhora; **Ajanta** Caves 9-10 – Shwetagaja (Chhaddanta) Jataka painting in Cave 10.
[5:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.22; Rocket AP History p.8; app notes 1:48 (sub 9)
## SUB 6 | Nagarjunakonda (Ikshvaku)
[6:0] - **Dynasty:** the **Ikshvakus** built the stupas, viharas and temples at Nagarjunakonda, backing both Vedic and Buddhist traditions (Welfare Officers) – not Mauryas, Satavahanas or Pallavas.
[6:1] - **Names:** **Vijayapuri** / **Sriparvata** – the Ikshvaku capital; tutelary goddess **Hariti**.
[6:2] - **Monuments:** 30+ stupas, viharas, apsidal chaityas; Ikshvaku queens chief donors; a Sinhala vihara; the first structural Hindu temples of Andhra.
[6:3] - **Satavahana link:** **Yajnasri Satakarni** built the 1,500-room **Sriparvata Sangharama** for Nagarjuna.
[6:4] - **Sects:** Bahushrutiya, Aparasaila and **Mahishasaka** (the only Hinayana sect there).
[6:5] - **Discovery:** A.R. Saraswati, 1926; excavated by Longhurst, 1927-31; drowned by the Nagarjunasagar reservoir – monuments rebuilt on an island.
[6:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.10, p.30; app notes 1:48 (sub 3)
