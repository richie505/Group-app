=== Social Groups – Primary, Secondary, Crowd and Mob
| Group | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** (C.H. Cooley) | **Small size**, face-to-face, **physical proximity**, intimacy, **similarity of background**, **common interests**, person-oriented | Family, village, friends, peer group |
| **Secondary** | **Large size**, formal, impersonal, **indirect relationships**, goal-oriented, **voluntary membership** (not compulsory) | School, office, hospital, association |
| Community | Personal, intimate, enduring relations | Family, close-knit group |
| Association | Impersonal, formed for a specific interest | Commerce, industry, a sports club |
- **Not a secondary-group trait:** **physical proximity** (it is a condition of the primary group) and **compulsory membership** (membership is voluntary).
- **'Primordial' groups/ties:** concept of **Edward Shils** (1957) – blood bonds of family and kin; "primary group" itself is Cooley's term.
- **By duration:** **permanent groups** – family, school, club, gymnasium, sports teams; **temporary groups** – crowd, audience.
- **Organisation:** a group of people **organised for a particular need** (purpose).
- **In-group / out-group:** a group becomes **more conscious of its unity when another group threatens it**.
- **Crowd vs mob:** a **mob** is a crowd that is **excited and engages in, or prepares for, violent action**; an aggregate (passengers at a bus stop) is not a group.

=== Social Control, Socialisation and Social Processes
- **Social control:** means by which society brings unruly members back into line. **Formal** – law, **police**, courts, State; **informal** – **family, community**, religion, gossip and ridicule (incl. social media).
- **Socialisation:** the process through which **society reproduces itself socially and biologically** – the individual learns the culture of the group.
- **Anticipatory socialisation** (R.K. Merton): learning the norms of a group or role one **expects to join** – e.g. parents preparing an unmarried daughter to adjust to her husband's family.
- **Social processes:** **conflict** is a **modified (sharpened) form of competition**; competition is impersonal, conflict personal and direct.
- **Social capital – three dimensions:** **structural** (network of relationships), **relational** (**trust** in ties), **resource** (benefits gained by virtue of ties and participation) – financial investment is economic, not social, capital.
- **Language** is important to society because it **facilitates social relations** (communication, transmission of culture).

=== Social Work – Values, Methods and Scope
- **Values of the social work profession:** dignity and worth of the person, **self-determination**, social justice, confidentiality, acceptance – "all the above" items.
- **Methods of social work:** primary – **social case work, social group work, community organisation**; secondary – social welfare administration, **social action, social work research**. A **case study** is a research tool, **not** a method.
- **Scope:** draws on **sociology, psychology and law** and covers **public health and community development** (both).
