=== Meaning and Goals of Sustainable Development
- **Definition (Brundtland, 1987):** development that **meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs**.
- It is a case of **inter-generational** sensibility in the use of **natural resources**; present use with **minimum degradation** of natural resources = sustainable development; "**saving energy and other resources for the future without sacrificing people's comfort in the present**"; **balancing the need to use resources and conserve them for the future**.
- **Main goal:** **balanced growth of environment and economy** – not growth only or protection only. Base of sustainable development = **environmental approach**.
- It matters for the **well-being of human society** because of the definition itself (A-R: both true, R explains A).
- Intertwined with **carrying capacity** (UPSC 2010) – Earth's finite capacity.
- **Three pillars / components:** **economic growth, social inclusion, environmental protection** – not "scientific association", political or military.
| Is an objective | NOT an objective |
|---|---|
| Meet present needs while keeping choices for the future | **Prioritise individual wealth accumulation** |
| Balance economy, society and environment | Profit maximisation, growth at any cost |
| Intergenerational equity | Unrestricted resource exploitation |
- **Green Agenda** = **concern for environment and for the future generations**.
- Mid-1980s debate on the **negative aspects of development**: degradation of natural resources, pollution, **displacement and rehabilitation** – not "politics and development".
- Major challenge to SDG 1 (no poverty): **economic inequality and unequal distribution of resources**.

=== Milestones – Club of Rome to Rio+20
| Year | Milestone | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **1968** (Apr) | **Club of Rome** founded, Rome | First advocated **resource conservation in a systematic way** at the international level (NCERT) |
| **1972** | "**The Limits to Growth**" (Club of Rome) | A book on **computer simulation of growth with finite resource supplies** |
| 1972 | Stockholm – UN Conference on the Human Environment | Created UNEP |
| **1980** | **World Conservation Strategy** (IUCN with UNEP and WWF) | Term "sustainable development" **first introduced** |
| 1983 | **WCED** set up by UNGA | Chair **Gro Harlem Brundtland** (Prime Minister of **Norway**) |
| **1987** | "**Our Common Future**" – **Brundtland Report** | **Defined and popularised** sustainable development (first clear modern explanation) |
| **1992** | **UNCED, Rio** (Earth Summit) | Rio Declaration, **Agenda 21** (signed **1992**, not 1995), UNFCCC, CBD |
| 2002 | Johannesburg – World Summit on SD (Rio+10) | Johannesburg Declaration |
| **2012** | **Rio+20 = UN Conference on Sustainable Development** | "The Future We Want"; SDGs born |
| 2015 | Agenda 2030, 25 Sep 2015 | 17 SDGs |
- The Brundtland Report is **not** "The Limits to Growth"; the term was defined by the **Brundtland Commission**, not WHO, UNESCO, UNICEF or UNDP.
- **2017** = UN International Year of **Sustainable Tourism for Development**.

=== MDGs, Agenda 2030 and the 17 SDGs
- **MDGs:** **8** goals from the **Millennium Summit, September 2000**, New York; period 2000-2015.
- **SDGs:** **17** goals, **169** targets, **231** unique indicators; adopted **25 Sep 2015** by the UN; 2016-2030.
| Goal | Theme |
|---|---|
| **1** | **No poverty** |
| **2** | Zero hunger |
| **4** | Quality education – free and compulsory education, school infrastructure and digital transformation |
| 5 | Gender equality |
| **6** | **Clean water and sanitation** – water availability and its sustainable management |
| **7** | **Affordable and clean energy** |
| **9** | Industry, innovation and infrastructure |
| 10 | Reduced inequalities |
| 11 | Sustainable cities and communities |
| **13** | **Climate action** – urgent action to combat climate change |
| 14 | Life below water |
| **15** | **Life on land** – terrestrial ecosystems |
- Agenda 2030 emphasises **ending all discrimination and violence against women and girls**; principles: universal, leave no one behind, integrated and indivisible.
- **UN Secretary-General** presents an **annual SDG Progress Report** (global indicator framework); the **Global Sustainable Development Report** is produced **every four years** for the quadrennial review – not every quarter.
- **National Indicator Framework** for SDGs is developed by **MoSPI**, not NITI Aayog; **Mission LiFE** (**Lifestyle for Environment**, 2021) aims to change individual demand, industry supply and **government and industrial policy** for sustainable consumption and production; SDG 4 "global citizenship" is education for global citizenship – not flexible citizenship rights.
- **Roots of Agenda 2030:** the **MDGs** + the **Rio+20** outcome; grounded in the **UDHR**, human rights treaties, the Millennium Declaration and the 2005 World Summit Outcome; informed by the **Declaration on the Right to Development**. **Three** of the 8 MDGs were health goals (4, 5, 6) – merged into **SDG 3**.
- **India's success is critical** for the global SDGs because India has nearly **one-sixth** of the world population (A-R: R explains A).

=== Related Concepts and Rio Principles
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Carrying capacity | Maximum population an environment can support indefinitely without degradation |
| Earth Overshoot Day | Date when humanity's yearly demand exceeds what Earth regenerates; **Global Footprint Network** |
| Intergenerational equity | Fairness between present and future generations |
- **Rio 1992 endorsed:** **precautionary principle** (lack of full scientific certainty is no reason to postpone action), **polluter pays**, **CBDR** (developed countries carry more – historical emissions, greater capacity). **Not endorsed:** unrestricted resource exploitation.
~~ Sources: Rio Declaration 1992 (un.org); Global Footprint Network; app notes 3:20 sub 4-5
