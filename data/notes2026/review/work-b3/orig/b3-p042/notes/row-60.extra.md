=== Veterinary Practice – Vaccination, Feeding and Livestock Management
# Vaccination
- **Purpose of vaccinating animals:** **prevention of infectious diseases** – not treatment, not non-infectious diseases.
- **Brucellosis:** female calves vaccinated once at about **4-6 (up to 8) months** (S19 / RB51); the live **Brucella S19 vaccine can infect the vaccinator** – handle carefully.
- **Foot-and-mouth disease:** controlled with **inactivated (killed)** vaccine.
- **Broilers:** LaSota (Newcastle, lentogenic) and IBD vaccines are used; **R2B (Mukteswar, mesogenic)** is a booster for older layers – **not used in broilers**.
# Feeding and digestion
- **Concentrate for dairy cows:** about **0.4 kg per litre of milk** (1 kg per 2.5 litres) over maintenance; buffalo about 0.5 kg per litre.
- **Excess protein** to ruminants → ammonia in the rumen, high pH → **alkaline indigestion**; excess grain → acid indigestion.
- **Hay** (well-cured) keeps **15-18% moisture**; wetter hay moulds.
# Breeding and poultry
- **Frozen semen extender:** **Tris** (tris-egg yolk-citrate-glycerol) – the most widely used.
- **Semen collection from cocks** by abdominal massage = **'milking'**; candling = checking eggs; brooding = rearing chicks.
- **Egg turning** during chicken incubation: **day 1 to 18**; stopped in the last three days (hatcher).
- **CO2 in the incubator** for optimum hatchability: **0.3-0.5%**.
# Meat animals and identification
- **Dressing percentage** highest in the **pig** (~70-80%); cattle, sheep, goats ~45-55%.
- **Pigs** are marketed at about **80-90 kg** live weight (6-8 months).
- **Rabbit** = the most **prolific meat animal** (short gestation, large litters).
- **Sheep and goats** are commonly identified by **ear tags** (ear notching); hot-iron branding is for cattle.
