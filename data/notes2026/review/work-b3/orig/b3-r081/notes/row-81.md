=== Money – Functions, Kinds and Key Terms
| Class | Functions of money |
|---|---|
| Primary | **Medium of exchange**; measure of value (**unit of account**) |
| Secondary | **Store of value**; standard of deferred payments; transfer of value |
| Contingent | Basis of credit; distribution of national income; **giving liquidity** |
- **Not a function:** **production of goods** (APPSC).
# Kinds of money
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fiat money | Notes and coins with **no intrinsic value**; money by government order; also legal tender |
| Legal tender | Money a **creditor is under compulsion to accept** in settlement of claims (UPSC 2018); RBI notes **unlimited**, coins **limited** legal tender |
| Commodity money | Money with **intrinsic value** (gold, silver); a paper note is not commodity money |
| Near money | Liquid assets that cannot directly pay: **bills of exchange**, time deposits, T-bills, bonds near redemption, savings certificates |
| Plastic money | **Credit cards** (and debit/prepaid cards), **mainly issued by banks**; used in place of cash but **not legal tender** |
| Cheque | Bank (credit) money – an **alternative to cash**; can be refused, so **not legal tender** |
| Hard / soft money | Hard = backed by **gold or credible assets**; soft = paper backed by **government bonds** |
| Bank money / cash | Bank money = **secondary money**; cash = high-powered money |
| Token coin | **Face value higher than metal value** (rupee coin) |
- **Digital Rupee** = legal tender under the RBI Act, amended by the **Finance Act 2022**; not every highly liquid asset is legal tender.
# Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Seigniorage | **Face value minus cost of producing** currency = issuer's (**central bank's**) **profit** from issuing notes; a ₹500 note costs about ₹4 – not a tax |
| Gresham's Law | **Bad money drives good money out**: good (full-value) coins are **hoarded, melted and exported**; applies to metallic coins |
| Velocity | Average number of times a unit of money changes hands in a period; ₹10 used twice = **₹20** of transactions |
| Required Reserve Ratio | Fixed percentage of deposits every bank must keep as reserves |
- **Money supply** = **currency + deposit money** (deposits of the public with banks).
- **Small coins** are phased out when their **production cost exceeds face value**.
- **Currency expansion** → value of money falls → **higher prices**.
- **Money supply is NOT part of a country's wealth** (mines, dams, livestock are).
> Traps: plastic money = credit card, not a coated note; seigniorage = profit, not a tax; Gresham = good money hoarded, melted AND exported.

=== Demonetisation and Currency Issue
| Year | Notes withdrawn | How |
|---|---|---|
| January 1946 | ₹1,000 and ₹10,000 | By **ordinance** |
| 16 January 1978 | **₹1,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000** | High Denomination Bank Notes (Demonetisation) Act, 1978 – PM **Morarji Desai** |
| **8 November 2016** | **₹500 and ₹1,000** (Mahatma Gandhi series) | Announced by the PM on the night of 8 Nov, effective midnight; new ₹500 and ₹2,000 issued |
- **Legal basis:** **Section 26(2), RBI Act 1934** (withdrawal of legal tender), on the recommendation of RBI's **Central Board** – not PMLA or the Banking Regulation Act.
- **Aims (Economic Survey 2016-17):** curb **corruption**, **counterfeiting**, use of high-denomination notes for **terrorist activities**, and accumulation of **black money**. Digital payments was a later, added aim.
- **Size:** SBNs were nearly **86%** of the value of notes in circulation on 8 Nov 2016 (RBI Annual Report 2016-17).
- **Returned:** RBI AR 2016-17 – ₹15.28 of ₹15.44 lakh crore = **98.96%** (to 30 June 2017); final, RBI AR 2017-18 – ₹15.31 of ₹15.42 lakh crore = **99.3%**.
- **₹2,000 notes:** **50.2%** of the value of banknotes in circulation at end-March 2017 (RBI AR 2016-17); withdrawn **May 2023**, still legal tender.
- **Specified Bank Notes (Cessation of Liabilities) Act, 2017:** after the grace period a person may hold **not more than 10 notes** (any denomination), or 25 for study/research/numismatics; fine up to ₹10,000 or five times the face value.
- **Paradox noticed:** the cash crunch cut spending – the **paradox of thrift** (APPSC G2 Mains 2019).
- SC upheld the 2016 move **4:1** (2023). **FICN**: NCRB Crime in India 2024 → **₹54.61 crore** fake currency seized.
- **Abroad – Venezuela (Dec 2016):** Maduro withdrew the **100-bolivar** note, then **backtracked** after protests and cash shortages.

=== Monetary Aggregates M1 to M4 and Liquidity Aggregates
| Aggregate | Components | Type |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Currency with public + demand deposits with banks + other deposits with RBI** (NCERT: **CU + DD**) | **Narrow money**, most liquid |
| M2 | M1 + **post office savings bank deposits** | Narrow |
| M3 | M1 + **time deposits with banks** | **Broad money** ("aggregate monetary resources") |
| M4 | M3 + all post office deposits (**excluding NSCs**) | Broad, least liquid |
- **Second Working Group on Money Supply (1977)** → RBI publishes M1–M4 besides Reserve Money.
- **M3** = most commonly used broad money = **currency with public + demand deposits + time deposits + other deposits with RBI** (all four).
- **M3 − M1 = time deposits**; time deposits are **not in M1**; M3 = M1 (not M2) + time deposits.
- **Liquidity order:** M1 > M2 > M3 > M4; assets: **currency > demand deposits > savings deposits > time deposits** (4-1-3-2).
- **Highly liquid asset** = converted into cash in a very short time with little loss of value.
- **Banks' liabilities:** deposits are the main liability; **time deposits** = the largest component, far above demand deposits.
# New aggregates (1998)
- **Third Working Group** (1998), chaired by **Y. V. Reddy**: residency-based **NM0–NM3** (separate short-term and long-term deposits).
- **L1, L2, L3** = **liquidity aggregates**: add post-office deposits, term money and CDs of FIs, NBFC public deposits.
> APPSC repeats: L1-L3 = liquidity aggregates; M1 = narrow money; M3 components; liquidity order.

=== Money Multiplier and Credit Creation
- **Money multiplier = money supply ÷ reserve money (M3 ÷ M0)**; m = (1 + cdr) / (cdr + rdr).
- 10% reserves, no leakages: m = 1/0.10 = **10**; ₹100 supports about **₹1,000** of deposits.
- **Credit creation:** banks lend beyond their cash; **loans exceeding deposits = credit creation**; capacity depends on **cash reserves** (strictly, a bank lends out its **excess reserves**).
- **Interbank transfer** to a bank brings it **reserves**, raising its lending potential.
| Money creator | Creates |
|---|---|
| RBI | Reserve money (notes) |
| Government | Coins and ₹1 note |
| Commercial banks | Deposit money by lending beyond CRR/SLR |
# What moves the multiplier and money supply
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Higher CRR, SLR or cash preference (cdr) | **Lower** multiplier, less credit creation |
| Stronger **banking habit** | **Higher** multiplier (UPSC 2019, 2021) |
| RBI **buys** G-secs from the public; **Government borrows from RBI** | Money supply **rises** (UPSC 2012: 1 and 3) |
| RBI sells G-secs; public deposits currency in banks | Falls / no rise |
| **Cash withdrawal** ₹1 lakh from a demand deposit | **Unchanged** (DD −₹1 lakh, currency +₹1 lakh) (UPSC 2020) |
- **Raising SLR** locks more funds in liquid assets (kept by the bank itself; CRR is kept with RBI) → fewer loanable funds; an SLR cut frees funds.
- **CRR increase** → banks have less to lend.

=== Indian Currency History
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| Late 18th century | Paper money introduced by private and state-sponsored banks: **Bank of Hindostan (1770)**, **General Bank of Bengal and Bihar (1773)**; then Presidency Banks |
| **1861** | **Paper Currency Act**: Government of (British) India gets the **monopoly of note issue** – "paper currency introduced by the British Government" |
| 1899 | Indian Coinage and Paper Currency Act (Curzon): India on the **gold standard** |
| 1935 | Note issue transferred to **RBI** (1 April 1935) |
| **1 April 1957** | **Decimal coinage** (Indian Coinage (Amendment) Act, **1955**); ₹1 = 100 naye paise |
| **1 June 1964** | "Naya" dropped – naya paisa became **paisa** |
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** issued **token copper/brass coins**, not paper currency.

=== Demand for Money, Interest and Monetary Theories
| Theory | Economist |
|---|---|
| Quantity theory MV = PT | Irving Fisher |
| Cash-balance M = kPY (k = 1/V) | Marshall, Pigou (Cambridge) |
| **Liquidity preference** (transactions, precautionary, speculative motives) | **J. M. Keynes** |
| Inventory model (square-root rule) | Baumol (1952), Tobin (1956) |
| Modern quantity theory, monetarism | Milton Friedman |
| IS-LM model | J. R. Hicks |
| **Pure Monetary Theory of Trade Cycle** | **R. G. Hawtrey** |
| Austrian cycle theory; free-market economics | Friedrich Hayek |
| Effective demand; paradox of thrift popularised | Keynes |
- Demand for money is **positively** related to **income** (more transactions); at **higher interest rates** money demand falls; speculative demand varies **inversely** with interest.
- **Money supply fixed + money demand rises → interest rate rises** (UPSC 2013); demand falls → rate falls.

=== High-powered Money (Reserve Money, M0)
- Names: **reserve money, monetary base, base money, central bank money** = **M0** – the "most important indicator"; **basis of credit creation**.
- **H = currency held by the public + cash reserves of banks (with themselves and with RBI) + other deposits with RBI**; no time deposits, no post-office deposits, no forex held by persons.
| Side | Components |
|---|---|
| Uses side | Currency with public + banks' cash in hand + **bankers' deposits with RBI** (legal reserves) + other deposits with RBI |
| Sources side | Net RBI credit to Government + RBI credit to banks and commercial sector + **net forex assets of RBI** + Government's currency liabilities − net non-monetary liabilities of RBI |
- **Currency in circulation** = notes + rupee coins + small coins (held by public + banks' cash in hand).
- **"Other" deposits with RBI**: quasi-government and financial institutions, foreign central banks and governments, international agencies such as the **IMF**.
> High-powered money = M0, not M2 (an old APPSC key gave M2).

=== e-Rupee (CBDC) and Current Data
| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Pilots | Wholesale **Nov 2022**; retail **1 Dec 2022** |
| Nature | Sovereign currency issued by RBI **in alignment with monetary policy**; **liability on RBI's balance sheet** (UPI money = bank liability); legal tender |
| Convertibility | **Freely convertible against commercial bank money and cash**, 1:1 |
| Interest | **Non-interest bearing**, like cash; **not inflation-proof** |
| Form | Same denominations as paper currency and coins; distributed through banks; P2P and P2M |
| Uses | Offline payments, programmable for DBT, UAE cross-border pilot; not a mandatory replacement of bank deposits |
| 2026 | Digital Food Currency pilot in **Puducherry** |
- **31 Dec 2025**: money multiplier **6.21**; M3 grew 12.1%, M0 2.9% (Economic Survey 2025-26).
- **CRR cut 100 bps to 3.0%** of NDTL → about **₹2.5 lakh crore** released (Dec 2025).
- Notes in circulation (May 2026): **174 billion** notes worth over ₹42.12 lakh crore.
