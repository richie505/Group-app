=== Growth vs Development – Concepts and the New View
| Basis | Economic growth | Economic development |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Rise in real output / income (GDP, GNP, per capita income) | Growth **plus** better quality of life – health, education, nutrition, less poverty, unemployment, inequality |
| Nature | **Quantitative**; value-neutral (can be negative) | **Qualitative + quantitative**; normative |
| Scope | Narrow | **Wider** – includes growth |
| Relation | **Necessary but not sufficient** | The end; needs conscious public policy |
- Growth can be measured; development is a **policy intervention endeavour aimed at the economic and social well-being of people** – the definition asked by APPSC is **economic development** (not "sustainable growth").
- Rise in absolute and per capita real GNP is **not** higher development if **poverty and unemployment increase** (UPSC 2018).
- **Best / most appropriate measure of growth:** growth of **per capita real income** year to year – not national income at current prices (inflation, population), not GDP/NDP/NNP totals.
- **Western (modernisation) model** (Rostow): key factor = **economic growth** (industrialisation, GDP).
- **New view** (1970s): **Dudley Seers**, *The Meaning of Development* (**1969**) – development = falling **poverty, unemployment and inequality**; it does **not** stress the **simple growth rate**. Its key part: **eradication of poverty**.
- **Trickle-down theory:** benefits of growth **percolate down** to the poor automatically (early Plans).
- **UNDP HDR 1996:** five bad growths – **jobless, ruthless, voiceless, rootless, futureless**.
- **Social development** = comprehensive change – economic, political, social and cultural.
- **Negative economic development** (development going backward) is called **regression**; falling output itself is a **recession** / negative growth.
- Economic welfare is linked to trickle-down benefits, sustainable development and reduced inequality – **not** to larger imports of consumption goods.
- Development planning breaks obstacles to raise **income and employment**.
- A-R asked: "development = improving living standards and welfare" (A) and "per capita income growth is not a sufficient measure" (R) – both true, **R explains A**.
- **Other models:** **Gandhian** – decentralisation and village industry; **Directive Principles** – adequate livelihood and less inequality, not just higher national income; **capability approach** – broader than GDP.
- **Indicators of development:** institutional change, inclusive growth, human resource development, rising capital formation and **structural transformation** (shift from agriculture to industry and services; best sign: rising share of **manufactured goods in exports**). A **larger GDP share from the primary sector** is NOT an indicator.
> Kerala model: high human development at modest per capita income – a State with a lower income rank can do better in human development.

=== Determinants of Growth – Economic and Non-economic Factors
| Economic factors | Non-economic factors |
|---|---|
| **Capital formation**, savings, investment, natural resources, technical progress, foreign exchange / foreign trade, **marketable agricultural surplus** | **Human resources**, social organisation / social behaviour, institutions, political stability, **corruption**, rule of law |
- **Capital formation** = the factor that **necessarily** brings growth in a country (UPSC 2013); Planning Commission: the most important factor.
- Capital formation depends on **savings** and effective **financial institutions**; **investment** is its essential factor – both statements true.
- **Saving is one of the determinants** of growth – not the only or the major one.
- "Not a major factor of growth": **technocrats and bureaucrats**; "not a non-economic factor": **market surplus in agriculture**.
- Population and development affect **each other** (both directions true).
- **Steel plough replacing a wooden one** = **capital-augmenting** technical progress (better tool, UPSC 2015).
- **Resource curse / paradox of plenty**: resource-rich countries showing less growth, less democracy and less development.
- Capital formation may fail to raise output when capital is idle or misused, complementary inputs (skills, infrastructure) are missing or demand is weak – **all of these**.
- Workers moving out of agriculture raise productivity; **social overhead capital** and **human resource development** are part of development.
- **Harrod-Domar**: saving (investment-income) ratio ÷ capital-output ratio = growth rate of **national income**; higher ICOR = lower growth.

=== Theories of Development and Books
| Theory / idea | Economist |
|---|---|
| **Vicious circle of poverty** – "a country is poor because it is poor" | **Ragnar Nurkse** – *Problems of Capital Formation in Underdeveloped Countries* (1953) |
| Balanced growth | Nurkse (with Rosenstein-Rodan) |
| **Big push** – large simultaneous investment in industry | **Paul Rosenstein-Rodan** (1943) |
| Unbalanced growth | A.O. Hirschman – *The Strategy of Economic Development* (1958) |
| Critical minimum effort | Harvey Leibenstein |
| **Unlimited supplies of labour** (dual economy) | **W. Arthur Lewis** (1954) |
| Stages of growth | **W.W. Rostow** |
| Cumulative causation | Gunnar Myrdal – *Economic Theory and Underdeveloped Regions* (1957) |
| Strategic Factors in Economic Development | Charles P. Kindleberger |
| Basic needs / welfare approach | ILO (1976) and Paul Streeten / Pigou |
- **Rostow's stages:** traditional society → pre-conditions → **take-off** → drive to maturity → high mass consumption.
- **Criticisms of Rostow** – data contradict the take-off thesis, no explanation of growth after take-off, stages hard to test empirically: **all of the above**.
- ***The Limits to Growth*** (1972, **Donella Meadows** and colleagues, Club of Rome): interactions of **population, resources and pollution** would limit growth (the geometric-ratio idea is Malthus).

=== Measures of Development – Per Capita Income, PQLI, HDI, GNH
| Measure | Developer / year | Components |
|---|---|---|
| Per capita income | **Simon Kuznets**, US, **1934** (Nobel 1971) | Income only – oldest; hides distribution |
| **PQLI** | **Morris D. Morris**, Overseas Development Council, mid-1970s (book 1979) | **Literacy, infant mortality, life expectancy at age one**; 0-100 – no income |
| HDI | UNDP, **1990**; **Mahbub ul Haq** (with Sen, Inge Kaul) | Life expectancy **at birth**, education, **GNI per capita (PPP)**; 0 to 1 |
| MPI | OPHI + UNDP, 2010 | Health, education, living standards |
| BNI | Economic Survey | Water, sanitation, housing |
| **GNH** | **Bhutan**, 4th King Jigme Singye Wangchuck, 1970s | Alternative to GDP |
| MEW / NEW | Nordhaus-Tobin (1972) / Paul Samuelson | Welfare-adjusted income |
- **Order of introduction:** per capita income → **PQLI** → **HDI** (→ MPI).
- "Most suitable estimate of physical quality of life": **infant mortality rate and literacy**.
- Basic indices of quality of life in developing countries: **real income, health and educational attainment** (the HDI trio).
- First State to publish a Human Development Report: **Madhya Pradesh**.
