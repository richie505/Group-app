=== FDI, FPI and FII – Meaning and What Counts
# FDI
- **FDI**: investment for **lasting interest and management control**; a **largely non-debt-creating** capital flow (UPSC 2020) – not debt-servicing, not FII buying G-secs; longer-term and **stable**.
- Legal line: capital instruments bought by a person resident outside India in an **unlisted** Indian company (any stake), or **10% or more** of post-issue paid-up equity of a **listed** company; 10% threshold from the **Arvind Mayaram Committee**.
- Instruments: equity shares, debentures, preference shares, share warrants.
- **Counts as FDI (UPSC 2012):** **subsidiaries of foreign companies**, **majority foreign equity** in Indian companies, **companies exclusively financed by foreign companies** – **portfolio investment is NOT** → "**1, 2 and 3 only**".
- **Can be included in FDI (UPSC 2021):** **FCCBs**, **FII investment with certain conditions**, **GDRs** – **NRE deposits are not** → "**1, 2 and 3**".
- **Not FDI:** buying **bonds or stocks** of a company overseas (portfolio). FDI = acquiring a mill abroad, a wholly owned firm abroad, building a new plant abroad.
| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Greenfield | New facility | Hyundai, Sriperumbudur |
| Brownfield | Acquisition | Vodafone-Hutchison Essar |
- Brings capital, **technology**, management skills, competition, jobs; finances the CAD with stable capital. **Gross FDI is not all fresh capital** (includes reinvested earnings, accounting changes).
- **MNCs** locate near markets (A) because they seek **more profit** (R) – both true, R explains A.
# FDI vs FPI / FII
| Basis | FDI | FPI / FII |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold | ≥10% listed, or any unlisted stake | <10% of a listed company |
| Nature | Long-term, stable, control | Liquid, short-term "hot money", **more volatile** |
| Brings | Capital + technology + management | Only financial capital – **raises capital availability in general** |
| Target | **Specific sectors / enterprises** | Securities (secondary market) |
| Regulator | DPIIT + RBI | **SEBI** (registration) + RBI |
- **Key difference (UPSC 2011):** "**FII helps in increasing capital availability in general, while FDI only targets specific sectors**".
- **FII** = portfolio investment by foreign institutional investors. **SEBI FPI Regulations, 2019** unified **FIIs, sub-accounts and QFIs** as FPIs. FPI opened to India in **1992**.
- **Most volatile flows:** **FPI and NRI deposits** (interest-rate driven) – "Both". Excess portfolio flows drove the **East Asian and Latin American crises** → **FDI** is the good inflow for a host country (UPSC 2002).
- **SEBI relaxing norms for sovereign wealth funds** (CDS 2011) – to **attract more foreign investment**.
> Most asked (×18): what counts as FDI, non-debt character, FII vs FDI difference, P-Notes. Trap: "FII is more stable than FDI".

=== Forms of Foreign Capital – ADR/GDR, P-Notes, ECB, NRI Deposits
- Foreign capital includes **FDI, trade credit, NRI deposits and ECBs**.
| Form | Classification |
|---|---|
| FDI | Non-debt, long-term, control (≥10%) |
| FPI / FII | Non-debt, portfolio (<10%) |
| ECB, trade credit, **FCCB** | **Debt** |
| NRI deposits (FCNR(B), NRE) | Banking capital (debt) |
| **ADR / GDR** | Equity raised abroad = **Foreign Equity Investment**; counted under **portfolio investment** in BoP |
| **Participatory Notes** | **Offshore derivative instruments** |
| **Masala bond** | **Rupee-denominated bond issued abroad** |
- **P-Notes**: issued by **registered FIIs/FPIs** to overseas investors who want exposure to Indian stocks **without registering with SEBI** (UPSC 2007, 2019).
- **FCNR(B)** replaced FCNR(A) on **15 May 1993** – term deposit in foreign currency.
- **ECBs** are regulated by the **RBI under FEMA**.
- **Old data PYQs:** largest forex inflow in **2005-06 = portfolio investment**; FII investment in **calendar 2005 ≈ US $10.7 bn**.

=== Routes, Sectoral Caps and Prohibited Sectors
# Routes and bodies
| Route | Approval |
|---|---|
| **Automatic** | No prior approval; **post-facto reporting to RBI** |
| **Government** | Prior approval of the concerned Ministry via the **Foreign Investment Facilitation (FIF) Portal** |
- **Negative-list approach**; **over 90%** of sectors automatic – not all.
| Act / body | Role |
|---|---|
| FEMA 1999 | Replaced **FERA 1973** |
| **FIPB** | Abolition announced in **Budget 2017-18**; abolished **June 2017** |
| **DPIIT** | Nodal for FDI policy and data; under **Ministry of Commerce and Industry** |
| NSWS | FIF Portal part of the National Single Window System since August 2022 |
# Sectoral caps (2026)
| Sector | Cap / route |
|---|---|
| **Insurance** | **100%** (Union Budget 2025-26, if the **entire premium is invested in India**); IRDAI HQ **Hyderabad**; insurance = Entry 47, **Union List** |
| Defence | **74% automatic**; up to 100% Government route |
| Telecom | **100% automatic** |
| Single-brand retail | 100% automatic |
| **Multi-brand retail** | **51%, Government route** (since 2012) |
| Private banks | **74%** (49% automatic) |
| Public sector banks | **20%**, Government route |
| Scheduled civil aviation | 100% (**49% automatic**; NRIs 100% automatic) |
| Pharma | Greenfield 100% automatic; **brownfield 74% automatic** |
| Food products made in India – trading incl. e-commerce | **100%, Government route** |
| E-commerce | **Marketplace 100% automatic**; inventory model not allowed (July 2026: allowed where inventory is used to **export domestically made goods**) |
# History of caps (PYQ dates)
| Year | Change |
|---|---|
| 2001-02 | Tea: foreign firm to disinvest **26%** to an Indian partner in **5 years** (not 33% in 4); print media 26% FDI with the **largest Indian shareholder holding more than 26%** |
| 2002-03 | Private banks: **49% automatic** FDI from all sources; no 49% ceiling on Indian promoters (minimum 40%) |
| Budget 2004-05 | Caps raised in **telecom, insurance, civil aviation** – **banking** is the odd one |
| 2004 | Private banks 49% → **74%** |
| 2006 | **Retail opened**: single-brand up to 51% |
| 2012 | **Multi-brand 51%** (Cabinet Sept 2012; announced 2011-12); **foreign airlines up to 49%**; single-brand 100%; insurance **26 → 49% proposed (4 Oct 2012)**; **FDI from Pakistan** allowed (decision 13 April 2012) |
| Insurance | 26% → 49% (2015) → **74% (Budget 2021-22, from 19 May 2021)** → **100% (Budget 2025-26)** |
- **Not a post-2010 change:** 100% FDI in the **automotive** sector (allowed earlier, 2002).
- **Defence production** opened **fully to private (Indian) investment** in 2001.
- Post-2014: caps raised in defence, insurance, pension.
# Prohibited sectors
- **Lottery**; gambling, betting, casinos; **chit funds**; **Nidhi companies**; trading in **TDRs**; real estate business and farmhouses; **cigars and cigarettes** of tobacco; **atomic energy**; **railway operations** (except permitted activities).
- **Railway infrastructure is NOT prohibited** (100% automatic); petroleum refining, cable networks, newspapers, defence, health care allow FDI.
> Most asked: multi-brand retail 51% (×4), insurance caps (×4), prohibited sectors (×3), cap history.

=== FDI Trends – Sources, Sectors, States
# Sources
| Period asked | Top source |
|---|---|
| 1991-95 | **USA and Japan** |
| Cumulative (2006, 2010 PYQs) | **Mauritius** – the **Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement** made it the route |
| 2016-17 | **Mauritius** > Singapore > Japan |
| FY23 | Mauritius 26%, Singapore 23% |
| 2023-24, 2024-25 | **Singapore** – the India-Mauritius tax treaty amendment shifted flows (A and R true, R explains A) |
| FY 2024-25 | Singapore (30%) > Mauritius (17%) > **US (11%)** |
# Sectors
- **2008-12 and last decade:** **telecommunications** among the top (ahead of software, pharma); **services** = highest overall.
- **Services = 80.2%** of FDI in FY23-FY25 (Economic Survey 2025-26); **information and communication 25.8%**.
# Flows
| Period | Figures |
|---|---|
| CY 2016 | FDI inflows up about **18%** (DIPP) |
| Net FDI peak | **$44.0 bn (2020-21)** |
| Economic Survey 2023 | FDI **above pre-pandemic levels** but **moderated amid a weak global economy** |
| FY 2024-25 | Gross $81 bn; **net only $353 mn** |
| FY 2025-26 | Record gross **$94.6 bn**; net $7.6 bn |
- **Weak net FDI:** **profit repatriation, outward FDI, PE/VC exits**; gross FDI may include accounting changes (CEA vs critics debate).
- **World Investment Report 2026:** the **US** was both the largest recipient and largest source of FDI in 2025.

=== FPI Limits and Flows
- **G-sec limits (General Route):** **6%** of Central G-secs, **2%** of State securities; also VRR and FAR routes. Limits for 2024-25 were kept **unchanged**.
- **Corporate bonds:** FPI limit raised to **15% (from 9%)** in **Union Budget 2020-21**.
- **2024-25 (APPSC Group 2 Mains 2025):** rupee moved in a **narrow range** despite FPI outflows; RBI set FPI limits for debt and **Credit Default Swaps**; G-sec and corporate bond limits **unchanged**; RBI **framework (Nov 2024)** to **reclassify FPI holdings above 10% as FDI** → "**All the above**".
- **JP Morgan EM bond index:** India's target weight **10%**.
- Drivers: **US Fed rates and global risk appetite**. FY26 outflows about **₹2.2 lakh crore**.

=== Press Note 3 – Land-border Countries
- **April 2020**: all FDI from a country sharing a **land border** with India needs the **Government route**.
- Covers China, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Myanmar, Bhutan, Afghanistan – **Sri Lanka not covered**.
- **Amended March 2026, effective 1 May 2026:** firms with up to **10%** Chinese/Hong Kong beneficial ownership may use the **automatic route**; beneficial owner as per **PML Rules, 2005**.

=== FTAs, CEPAs and RCEP
| Agreement | Partner | Date |
|---|---|---|
| SAFTA | 8 SAARC members | In force **2006** |
| ASEAN-India FTA (goods) | ASEAN | In force **2010** |
| **BTIA** | **European Union** | Talks since June 2007 (Brussels) |
| CEPA | UAE | In force **1 May 2022** |
| **ECTA** | **Australia** | In force 29 December 2022 |
| TEPA | EFTA | In force **1 October 2025** |
| CETA | UK | In force **15 July 2026** |
- **ASEAN's FTA partners:** **Australia, China, India, Japan**, New Zealand, South Korea (and Hong Kong) – **not Canada or USA**.
- **RCEP**: signed **15 Nov 2020** by **15** members (ASEAN 10 + China, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand); in force **1 January 2022**. **India is not a member** – opted out in 2019 over **China's market dominance** and the trade deficit (**$53.5 bn in 2018-19**).
