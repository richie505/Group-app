# Satavahanas : Level II

## Indigenous Literature:

The texts of this period are in Prakrit and Sanskrit languages.

**Brihatkatha:**

- **Author:** Gunadyudu
- **Language:** Prakrit

**Details:**

- **Gunadaya was the court poet of Kuntala Satakarni.**
- Narrated 7 stories with 7000 verses in Brihatkatha.
- The book is no longer available.
- ***Translated into Sanskrit by Durvinuta***
- Contains ethical stories similar to **Panchatantra.**

**Texts Based on Brihatkatha:**

- ***Brihatkatha Manjari*** by Kshemendra
- ***Kathasaritsagaram*** by Somadevasuri
- ***Compilation of Muddakatha Shloka*** by Buddhaswamy

**Kathasaritsagaram:**

- **Author**: Somadevara Suri
- **Language**: Sanskrit

**Details:**

- *Based on Brihatkatha*, Somadevara Suri wrote Kathasaritsagaram in Sanskrit.
- Provides insight into the social conditions of the Satavahana period.
- Mentions that Satavahana possessed the vehicle of *Satayaksha*.
- States that Acharya Nagarjuna was killed by a Satavahana prince.

**Gathasaptasati:**

- **Author:** Hala
- **Language:** Maharashtrian Prakrit

**Details:**

- King Satavahana **Hala** was a poet himself.
- Compiled **Sattasai (Gathasaptasati) with 700 Prakrit verses.**
- Gathas are poems with high literary value.
- Halu was titled ***Kavivatsaladu***.
- Honoured poets like Kumariludu and Sripalitadudu.

<!-- page 2 -->

**Women Contributors**: Anulakshmi, Anupalabda, Reva, Madhavi.

**Social and Cultural Insights:**

- Reflects the social conditions of the time.
- Describes rural living conditions and beautiful nature.
- Begins with prayers to Lord Shiva.
- Mentions deities like Gauri, Indra, and Ganesha.
- References to sugar, jaggery, and the hanging of criminals from trees.

**Conclusion of Lilavati:**

**Lilavati Granth:**

- **Author:** Kutuhaludu
- **Language**: Prakrit

**Details:**

- Describes the story of the *Sinhala princess Lilavati of Haludi*.
- The marriage took place in the presence of the **Bhimeswara temple** near Saptama Godavari.
- Mentions that a Satavahana king patronized **Acharya Nagarjuna**.

**Kamasutras:**

- **Author:** Vatsayanadu
- **Language:** Sanskrit

**Details:**

- **Kuntala Satakarni's Court Poet:** Vatsayana.
- Explained everything related to romance and romantic life in an unabashedly scientific manner.
- The Kamasutras mentions that Kuntala Satakarni died during his sexual intercourse with his wife Malayavati due to the sport of a rat called Karirtha.

**Spahallekha:**

- **Author** : Acharya Nagarjuna
- **Language:** Sanskrit

**Details:**

- *Acharya Nagarjuna was a contemporary of Yagnashri Satakarni*.
- Contains *summaries of letters and replies between Satavahana King Yagnashri and Acharya Nagarjuna on Buddhist issues*, known as Srihallekha.
- The **Chinese traveller Itsing**, who visited India around 700 AD, mentioned that every student memorized the Srihallekha.

## Foreign Literature:

**Natural History**

- **Author:** Pliny
- **Language:** Latin

**Details:**

- Pliny was from the city of Rome.
- He wrote "Natural History" in Latin.
- The treatise states, *"All the gold in Europe will move to India."*
- This text discusses *trade and commerce* during the time of the Satavahanas.

**Periplus of the Erythraean Sea**

- **Author:** An anonymous Greek sailor
- **Language**: Greek

<!-- page 4 -->

- **Another Name**: Daily Routine of a Sailor on the Red Sea

**Details:**

- Describes *trade and commerce* during the time of the Satavahanas.
- Details the *trade of the Andhras with Rome, Southeast Asia, and port towns* engaged in foreign trade.
- **Arikamedu is identified as an international port town**, known as the market street of the Romans during the time of the Satavahanas.
- States that **Koddura was the only port town on the east coast** during the time of the Satavahanas.
- Mentions conflicts between the *Shaka and the Satavahanas*.

**Guide to Geography**

- **Author:** Ptolemy

**Details:**

- Describes the rule and trading centers of the Satavahanas in his book "Guide to Geography."
- Discusses the *port towns of the Satavahanas*.
- According to this text, **Bharukachha coastal town was important on the west coast**, followed by the port towns of Sopara and Kalyani.
- Describes **Masulia (Machilipatnam)** as a great trading centre during the Satavahanas.

**Si-Yu-Ki (Records of the Western World)**

- **Author:** Huan Tsang
- **Another Name**: Buddhist Records

**Details:**

- Huan Tsang was from China and belonged to the 7th century.
- Known as a pilgrim with the title of King.
- He visited India from Kashmir to Tamil Nadu and provided detailed descriptions.
- His records include details about the Satavahanas in Buddhist contexts.

**Indica**

- **Author:** Megasthenes
- **Language:** Greek

**Details:**

- Megasthenes was the first foreign traveller to visit India, hailing from Greece.
- He came as an ambassador during the Mauryan period and gave detailed descriptions of Pataliputra and the political system of the Satavahanas.
- His text is the **first foreign source to mention the Andhras**.
- Stated that the **Andhras had 30 forts and one lakh infantry**.

## Reign of the Satavahanas:

**According to Vayu Purana:**

- 17 kings
- Reigned for 272 years (not considered standard)

**According to Matsya Purana:**

- 30 kings
- Reigned for 456 years
- *Clearly states their genealogy and the reign period of each king*
- Period of Satavahanas: 271 BC to 174 AD
- **Political history of Satavahanas is based on Matsya Purana**

**Division:**

- First Satavahanas (1st to 23rd kings)
- Later Satavahanas (24th to 30th kings)

**Notable Kings:**

- **First Satakarni (3rd King):** Greatest of the early Satavahanas
- **Gautamiputra Satakarni (23rd King):** Greatest of all Satavahanas
- **Yajnasri Satakarni (27th King):** Greatest of the later Satavahanas
- **2nd Satakarni (6th King):** Reigned for many years (56 years)
- **Pulomavi III (30th King):** Last of the Satavahanas

**Founder (Mula Purusha):**

- **According to inscriptions, coins, and Somadeva's Kathasaritsagara:** Progenitor was Satavahana
- **According to Puranas:** Progenitor was Srimukha

**Capital of the Satavahanas:**

**First Capital:**

- **According to Barnett, V.A. Smith, Barges:** Srikakulam (Krishna District)
- **According to Mirasi, Rama Rao, and Roy Chaudhary:** Prathishtanapuram/Python (Aurangabad District, Maharashtra)

**Second Capital:** Dhanyakatakam/Dharanikota (Guntur District)

**Third Capital:** Prathishtanapuram

**Alternative Views:**

- **According to Barnett, V.A. Smith, Barges:** First capital was Srikakulam in Krishna District
- **According to R.G. Bhandarkar:** First capital was Dhanyakatakam near Amaravati
- **According to Roy Chaudhary:** First capital was Vijayawada

## Political History of Satavahana (following Matsya Purana):

**Srimukha**

- Founder of Satavahana Kingdom

**Family:** Srimukha's father was Satavahana / Salivahana

**Capital:** Ruled with 'Pratishtanapuram' as his capital

**Reign:**

- According to a legend, he ruled for 23 years
- According to the Puranas, *Srimukha killed the Kanva dynasty king Susarma, removed the nominal power of Simbula, and established the kingdom*

**Puranas Description:** Described as Sinthaka

**Evidences:**

- *Nanaghat Inscription:* In Prakrit, by his daughter-in-law Devi Naganika
- *Coins:* Minted by him are found in Kondapur (Medak) and Kotilingala (Karimnagar)
- His coins bear the names **Ranagobhadra and Ranagoswami**

**Mentions:**

- Devi Naganika's Nanaghat rock inscription mentions **"Rayasimukasatavahanasirimato"**
- *Ashoka mentions the Andhras as one of his vassal races in his 13th inscription*, referring to Srimukha
- Srimukha is considered a contemporary of Ashoka
- Historian **D.C. Sarkar's** opinion that Ashoka gave the title 'Raya' to Srimukha

**Religion:**

- First embraced Jainism and then Vedicism
- His Jain Guru was **Kalakasuri (Vimaladitya-Trikalayogi)**

**Succession:**

- After Srimukha's death, his son, the first Satakarni, was a minor, so his brother Kanha (or Krishna) came to power

**Kannakrishna:**

- 2nd King of the Satavahanas
- According to Puranas, he ruled for 18 years

**Territorial Expansion:**

- Penetrated into Maharashtra
- Extended the kingdom up to Nasik
- Built two viharas for Buddhist monks: **Nashik and Kanhari**

<!-- page 7 -->

**Religious Influence:**

- **Bhagavatism entered Andhra from North India** during his reign (originated during Pushyamitra Shunga's time in North India)
- **Founder of Bhagavata Religion:** Lord Krishna (Vasu God)

**Religious Practices:**

- **Nashik inscription indicates he adopted Vedic-Brahmanic religion** but also embraced Buddhism
- Appointed **Mahamatra** as an employee for Buddhist monks
- Evidence of Bhagavatism: **"Besnagar" inscription of Heliodorus**

**Satakarni:**

**Declaration of Independence**: Satavahanas declared independence from Mauryas during his period

**Family:** Son of Srimukha

**Titles and Achievements:**

- Greatest of the first Satavahanas
- Called **"Mallakarna" "Great" by Matsya Purana**
- First Satavahana king to add the **surname "Satavahana"** to his name
- Expanded the kingdom through *marital relations*

**Marriage:**

- Married Devi Naganika (Nagavaradaini), daughter of Trainakairo, king of Ruru, Maharathi (progenitor of Angiukula)
- Devi Naganika inscribed the **"Nanaghat Inscription" (Prakrit)**

**Territorial Conquests:**

- Expanded kingdom westward by conquering Dandetti, Asmaka (Nizamabad), Akara, Avanti, Mulaka (Aurangabad), and Malwa
- **Defeated Pushyamitra Shunga and minted coins with Ujjain town arch symbol**
- *Struck coins with "Gajagurtu"*

**Donations:**

- *First to donate silver coins to Brahmins and tax-exempt lands*
- Donated lands free of taxes, 36000 karshapanas (gold coins), 44000 cows, 13 villages, 10 elephants, and 1000 horses

**Military Engagements:**

- Mentioned in **Hathigumpa inscription and Guntupalli inscriptions** that MahaMeghavahana Kharavela, ruler of Kalinga, attacked and defeated the first Satakarni
- Defeated the Yavana king *Demetrius*

**Religious Ceremonies:**

*Performed various Yajnas:*

- 2 Aswamedha Yagas
- 1 Rajasuya Yagam
- 1 Anarabhaniya Yagam
- 1 Fire sacrifice
- 3 Dasaratra Yagam

**Contemporary Kings:**

- Magadha (North India) - **Pushyamitra Shunga**
- Kalinga (Orissa) - **Kharavela**
- Indo-Greek Kings - **Demetrius, Menander**

**Titles:**

- *Ekavira*
- *Shura*
- *Dakshinapadhapathi*
- *Asmakadisa*
- *Apratihata Chakra*

**Vedasiri:**

- Son of Devi Naganikala and Satakarni I - Vedasiri
- Known as **Purnotsanga**, indicating patronage of Vedic religion.
- Carved idols of Srimukha, Kanhu, mother Naganika, and grandfather Trinakaro in Nanaghat caves.
- Reign witnessed **Kalinga Kharavela's attack on Bhattiprolu**.
- Succeeded by *Skandhastambhi*, who was succeeded by the second Satakarni.

**Satakarni II:**

- 6th king
- **Longest reigning king** (56 years)
- Built the **southern arch of the Sanchi stupa** and engraved the inscription.
- *Conflicts between Saka and Satavahana* started during his reign.
- Recaptured previously lost Satavahana areas → mentioned in **Bilsa inscription**.
- Coins found in Malwa, Maharashtra, Telangana with **"RanosiriSatakanika" inscription**.
- *Title:* Rajanya Sri Satakarni
- *Court artist:* Vashishtiputra Ananda

**Kuntala Satakarni:**

- 13th king
- **Title:** Vikramarka (proposed by Nanduri Krishnamacharya)
- Replaced Prakrit with **Sanskrit as the royal language**.
- Mentioned in Rajasekhara's Kavya Mimamsa, Vatsayana's Kamasutra, and Gunadhya's Brihatkatha.

**Court poets:**
Sarvavarma: Wrote Sanskrit grammar book Katantra Vyakaranam.

**Pulomavi I:**

- Ruled Magadha for 10 years (Vayu Purana).
- Defeated Magadha ruler Susarma (Kanva clan) and conquered Magadha (Matsya Purana).
- *Yugapurana of Gargasamhita* confirms Andhra's conquest of Magadha.
- **Satavahana coins found in ancient Pataliputra (Kumrahar).**
- Transformed Satavahana kingdom into a great empire, earning glory for the Andhras.

**Gaurava Krishna:**

- Ruled for 25 years after the Pulomavi I.
- *Kushans established their empire during his reign.*
- Suffered defeats from Kshatraps.
- Mentioned in Puranas as Nikuvarma, Vaikrishna, Arishtakarma, Anishtakarma, and Rikta Varna.

- **Title:** Kavivatsaladu; poet himself.

- **Mentioned in Harshacharitra by Banu.**
- Main advisor: Senapati Vijayanadha.
- *Married Sri Lankan princess Lilavati.*

## Administration of the Satavahanas:

- The administration of the Satavahanas is detailed in **inscriptions from Nasik and Usmanfar**.
- Their governance style is also described in *Kautilya's Arthashastra, Vatsayana's Kamasutra, and the Manu Dharmashastra.*
- The Satavahanas followed the administrative style of the Mauryas.
- In the Mauryan period, the king was seen as a human figure, but by the time of the Satavahanas, the king was considered divine.
- Ashoka called himself Rajan, while Gautamiputra Satakarni declared himself Rajaraja.
- The **titles of Maharaja and Rajadhiraja** were first used by the Kushans in North India, and the Satavahanas were the first to use these titles in the southern region.

**Administrative Structure**

**Secretariat (Akshapatala):** Implemented the king's advice.

**Ministers (Amatyas):**

There were four ministers who assisted the king:

1) Vishva Amatyulu: King's inner advisor (Pradhani).
2) Raja Amatyulu: Executor of the king's orders.
3) Maha Amatyulu: Finance Minister.
4) Amatyas: Lords of food.

**Other Officials**

- ***Bhandagarika:*** Treasurer (collector of the system in kind).
- ***Hiranikadu:*** Treasurer (collector of tax in monetary form).
- ***Nibandhanakara:*** Keeper of statutory central government documents.
- ***Mahadharmika:*** Settler of educational and religious disputes.
- ***Mahaaryaka***: Resolver of legal disputes.
- ***Lekhakudu:*** Inner secretary of the king, prepared documents and edicts.
- ***Maha Talavara:*** Chief military commander.
- ***Pratihara:*** Defender of the fortress.
- ***Rajyabhashakuda:*** Measured land and collected tax.
- ***Akshapatakas:*** Wrote and preserved the king's orders and affairs in documents.
- ***Mahabhoja:*** Highest official after the king.

**Military System**

- Frequent raids by the Satavahana kings indicate they had a large military force.
- The **Hathigumpha inscription** mentions the Chaturanga Balas of the Satavahanas.

<!-- page 13 -->

- Military maneuvers and strategy of the Satavahana period are depicted in the **Amaravati sculptures.**
- *Skandavaram:* Temporary military camp.
- Cuttack: Permanent garrison (modern cantonment).
- Every state had a military base and every city had defences.
- The Satavahanas divided the kingdom into administrative units called **Aharas,** which were further divided into Nigamas, and Nigamas into villages.

**Administrative Divisions**

- *Kingdom*: Headed by the king, whose authority was often seen as divine.
- *Aharas:* Governed by Amatyas (usually blood relatives or sons of the king).
- *Villages*: Governed by a Gulmika (overseer of village governance).
- *Nigamas:* Represented the administration of towns.

**Nigamas**

- Towns in the kingdom were referred to as *Nigamas*.
- Assemblies that managed the town affairs were called *Nigam Sabha*.
- Members of the Corporation Council were known as *Gahapathi*.
- **Nigam Sabha is mentioned in the Bhattiprolu inscription and Megasthenes Indica**.

**Major Cities of the Satavahana Period:**

- Barukaccha
- Sopaara
- Kalyanam
- Govardhana
- Pratisthana
- Kondapur
- Dhanyakataka
- Vijayapuri
- Nasik

**Village Administration**

- Described in the **Myakadoni inscription.**
- Villages had gram sabhas for governance.
- The village head was called gramani during the Mauryan period and **Gulmika during the Satavahana period**.
- Amalgamation of several villages was called Gulma, headed by Gulmikudu.
- Gulmikas served as army commanders for border defence.
- A government officer called Mahakaryaka addressed village problems.

**State Administration**

- The Satavahana empire was not a strongly centralized kingdom, with many vassal kingdoms or Janapadas.
- Feudal kingdoms were ruled by kings and their descendants, supervised by officers named *Mahabhoja and Maharatha*.
- Vassal kings sometimes held noble titles.

<!-- page 14 -->

**Officials and their Roles:**

**Kings:**

- Often from small royal families.
- Example: An ossuary with *King Kubheraka's name was found at Bhattiprolu*.

**Maharathas:**

- The post was hereditary.
- Duties included tax collection and, to some extent, canceling taxes.
- Assisted by magistrates in settling disputes and taxation.

**Mahabhojas:**

- The post was also hereditary.
- Inscriptions do not include emperor names, suggesting independent status.
- Names of Janapadas like Anupa and Rushika appear in inscriptions.
- *Mahabhojaka tribe in Poona and Radhika tribe in Nashik*.

**Border States**

**Shaka Invasions:**

- The Shakas frequently invaded the northern borders of the Satavahana Empire.
- Able commanders were appointed to protect these regions from invasions.
- These commanders were known as *Mahasenadhipati and Mahatalavaru*.

## Social Conditions during the Satavahana Period

**Caste System**

**Patriarchal and Joint Family System:**

- The society followed a patriarchal and joint family system.
- The Gathasaptasati and inscriptions from Amaravati Mahachaityam and Western Ghats caves support this view.

**Caste formation based on Professions:**

- Castes were formed based on professions, marking the origin of the caste system as profession-based.

**Assimilation of Foreigners:**

- Foreigners like the Sakas, Kardhamakas, Western Kshatrapas, and Hindus were assimilated into the community and adopted Aryan names.
- Examples include Shaka Nahapana's daughter Dakshmitra, son-in-law Rushabha Dutta, and Shaka Rudradama.

<!-- page 15 -->

**Respect for Brahmins:**

- Brahmins, as symbols of Vedic Dharma, were respected.
- The **Nanaghat Inscription mentions Brahmins trained in Vedic education** and capable of performing Vedic rituals.
- Tax-free villages were donated to Brahmins.

**Family System**

**Joint Family System:**

- The joint family system was prevalent.
- Despite this, joint careers were not common.

**System of Marriage**

**Sacredness of Marriage:**

- Marriage was considered sacred.
- Polygamy was practiced in the upper classes.
- Widows observed celibacy.

**Types of Marriages:**

- *Anuloma Marriage*: Between an upper-caste boy and a lower-caste girl.
- *Viloma Marriage*: Between a low-caste boy and an upper-caste girl.
- Children born out of such marriages were given specific designations.

**Position of Women**

**Participation in Society:**

- Women participated equally with men in festivals and gatherings.
- They donated generously to Buddhist Viharas and Chaityas.
- Example: **Gautami Balashri donated to the construction of a Buddhist Vihara on Bhadrayanakonda**.

**Names and Titles:**

- Women combined their husbands' names with their own, indicating their status.

## Economic Conditions during the Satavahana Period:

**Agriculture:**

- Agriculture was the primary occupation.
- Farmers had recognized rights over land, which did not belong to the king.

- **Nivarthana:** Unit of land measurement, approximately 1.5 acres.

**Revenue Sources:**

- Land was the main source of revenue.

**Agricultural Practices:**

- Major cash crop was coconut.
- Other crops included sorghum, sugar cane, rice, kandi, sesame seeds, and janum (used as fodder).

**Agricultural Tools:**

- **Odayantrikas** were a group of technical experts who manufactured modern agricultural implements.
- Tools developed by them included **Udaga Yantra (plough), Ghati Yantra (water-lifting machine), and Garika Yantra (seed-separating machine).**

**Transportation and Travel:**

- Bullock carts were the main means of travel.

**Taxes:**

- Main Source of Income: The main income came from land tax.

- **Bhaga:** Represented 1/6th of the ripe crop.
- **Karukara:** Professional associations that paid taxes.

- **Sulka:** Water tax.
