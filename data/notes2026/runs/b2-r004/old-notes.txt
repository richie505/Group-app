## SUB 1 | Preamble Text – Order of Words and Date
[4:1:0] # Nature of the State
[4:1:1] - "**We, the People of India**" – opens the Preamble; shows **people's sovereignty**; the Constitution derives its authority from the people.
[4:1:2] - People resolved to constitute India into a **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic** (that exact sequence).
[4:1:3] - **42nd Amendment** put **Socialist and Secular between 'Sovereign' and 'Democratic'**.
[4:1:4] - Enacting clause: "IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this **twenty-sixth day of November, 1949**, do HEREBY **ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES** THIS CONSTITUTION."
[4:1:5] - **Main source of political power**: the **People**; sovereignty is with them (⚠ APPSC key (2002) gave Parliament and State Legislatures).
[4:1:6] # Original 1949 Text vs Now
[4:1:7] TABLE head: Item | 1949 / 26 Jan 1950 | Now (after 1976)
  [4:1:7.0] Description of India | "Sovereign Democratic Republic" | "Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic"
  [4:1:7.1] Fraternity clause | "unity of the Nation" | "unity and integrity of the Nation"
  [4:1:7.2] Fraternity | Present in original | Unchanged
  [4:1:7.3] Words absent in 1949 | Socialist, Secular, Integrity | Added
[4:1:8] # Words NOT in the Preamble at All
[4:1:9] - **Federal / federation**
[4:1:10] - **Fundamental Duties**
[4:1:11] - **Welfare state** / **Public Welfare**
[4:1:12] - **Adult franchise**
[4:1:13] - **Religious Justice**
[4:1:14] - **"People's"**
[4:1:15] - **"Freedom"** (it uses Liberty, Equality, Justice, Fraternity)
[4:1:16] - **Liberty "of action"**, **equality "of rights"**
[4:1:17] - **Political Liberty** and **Economic liberty** as separate categories
[4:1:18] - **Gram Swarajya**
[4:1:19] "Federal" is nowhere in the Preamble, and "Freedom" is not used – only "Liberty". ⚠ APPSC key (2019) gave 'Quasi-federal State' as what the Preamble declares.
## SUB 2 | Keywords – Meaning
[4:2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:2:0.0] Sovereign | Supreme authority; internally and externally free; own foreign policy; free to frame own policies
  [4:2:0.1] Socialist | Aims at reducing inequality; democratic socialism – a mixed economy (public + private sectors); "a blend of Marxism and Gandhism, leaning towards Gandhian socialism"
  [4:2:0.2] Secular | Equal respect for all religions; no official/State religion; not against religion
  [4:2:0.3] Democratic | Government of, by and for the people; representative parliamentary democracy covering political, social and economic democracy
  [4:2:0.4] Republic | Elected head of State, not hereditary; public offices open to all
[4:2:1] - 'Secular' before 1976 appeared only in **Article 25(2)(a)** (secular activities linked with religious practice); linked to **Arts. 25–28**.
[4:2:2] - **Communist State** is NOT the philosophy of the Constitution; a socialist state is a goal.
[4:2:3] - India is a **democratic polity, sovereign state, democratic society and welfare state** (welfare state via **DPSP**).
[4:2:4] APPSC G1 2024: "'Democratic' is a mix of British Fabian Socialism and American Populism" – NOT valid (answer: none of the statements valid).
## SUB 3 | Objectives Secured for All Citizens
[4:3:0] # Kinds of Each Objective
[4:3:1] TABLE head: Objective | Of / Kinds
  [4:3:1.0] Justice | Social, economic and political (three types)
  [4:3:1.1] Liberty | Thought, expression, belief, faith and worship (only these)
  [4:3:1.2] Equality | Status and opportunity
  [4:3:1.3] Fraternity | Assuring dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the Nation
[4:3:2] - Preamble secures to all: Justice (social, economic, political), Liberty of thought and expression, Equality of opportunity, Dignity of the Individual – **all of these**.
[4:3:3] # Meaning of the Objectives
[4:3:4] - **Political justice**: equal political rights and access to office.
[4:3:5] - **Liberty**: **absence of restraints** on individual activity **plus opportunities to develop personality**; not freedom from every restraint.
[4:3:6] - **Equality**: three dimensions – **civic, political, economic**.
[4:3:7] - **Fraternity**: sense of **brotherhood**, promoted by **single citizenship**.
[4:3:8] - **Economic justice:** no discrimination on economic grounds; removing glaring inequalities in wealth, income and property.
## SUB 4 | Is the Preamble Part of the Constitution? – Cases
[4:4:0] TABLE head: Case | Year | Held
  [4:4:0.0] Berubari Union (President's reference) | 1960 | Not a part of the Constitution; "key to the minds of the makers"; aid to interpretation where an article is ambiguous
  [4:4:0.1] Golaknath | 1967 | Dealt with amending Fundamental Rights; did not settle the Preamble's status (distractor)
  [4:4:0.2] Kesavananda Bharati | 1973 | Preamble is a part; amendable under Art. 368, but basic structure cannot be destroyed; overruled Berubari
  [4:4:0.3] Minerva Mills | 1980 | Struck down 42nd Amendment clauses (Art. 368 clauses (4) and (5))
  [4:4:0.4] S.R. Bommai | 1994 | Reaffirmed secularism as basic structure; Preamble an integral part
  [4:4:0.5] LIC of India | 1995 | Reaffirmed Preamble is an integral part
  [4:4:0.6] Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India | 25 Nov 2024 | Upheld 'Socialist' and 'Secular'; rejected challenges to 42nd Amendment insertions
[4:4:1] - **Berubari** first declared the Preamble **not a part** of the Constitution.
[4:4:2] - **Kesavananda Bharati**: **13-judge bench** gave the **Basic Structure doctrine (1973)**; Preamble seen as part of the basic structure.
[4:4:3] APPSC GS 2025: "Preamble is an integral part – which case?" = Kesavananda Bharati. Distractors: Golaknath, Minerva Mills, Gopalan.
## SUB 5 | Status and Legal Effect
[4:5:0] - Reflects the **philosophy, ideals and objectives** of the Constitution; the **objective of the Constitution** is reflected in it.
[4:5:1] - **Non-justiciable**: cannot be enforced in a court of law.
[4:5:2] - **Does not confer any power** and does not create any right by itself.
[4:5:3] - **Neither a source of power** to the legislature **nor a limit** on powers expressly granted.
[4:5:4] - Part of the Constitution, but **no legal effect independently** of other parts (Kesavananda Bharati).
[4:5:5] - Used as an **aid to interpretation** of ambiguous provisions (Berubari).
[4:5:6] # Status at a Glance
[4:5:7] TABLE head: Question | Answer
  [4:5:7.0] Part of the Constitution? | Yes (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973; reaffirmed LIC 1995)
  [4:5:7.1] Enforceable in court? | No – non-justiciable
  [4:5:7.2] Source of power? | No
  [4:5:7.3] Limit on express powers? | No
  [4:5:7.4] Aid to interpretation? | Yes, where a provision is ambiguous
  [4:5:7.5] Amendable? | Yes, under Art. 368, but not its basic features
  [4:5:7.6] Amended how many times? | Once – 42nd Amendment, 1976
  [4:5:7.7] Enacted when? | After the rest of the Constitution was adopted
[4:5:8] - Status of India on **26 January 1950**: "**A Sovereign Democratic Republic**"; the Constitution was adopted on **26 November 1949** and came **into force on 26 January 1950**.
[4:5:9] - The Preamble **declares India Secular** ('Secular' added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976).
## SUB 6 | Amendability and Secularism As Basic Structure
[4:6:0] TABLE head: Case | Year | Holding
  [4:6:0.0] Kesavananda Bharati | 1973 | Amendable under Article 368; basic features cannot be destroyed
  [4:6:0.1] Waman Rao | 1981 | Basic-structure review applies to amendments made after 24 Apr 1973

[4:6:1] - **Secularism** was part of the basic structure **even before** the word was inserted in 1976.
[4:6:2] - **Kesavananda Bharati came before** the 1976 amendment.
[4:6:3] - Amendments so far: **only one** (42nd, 1976).
## SUB 7 | 42ND Amendment Act, 1976
[4:7:0] # Words Added
[4:7:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [4:7:1.0] Words added | Socialist, Secular, Integrity
  [4:7:1.1] Change | "Unity of the Nation" → "Unity and Integrity of the Nation"
  [4:7:1.2] Change | "Sovereign Democratic Republic" → "Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic" (held valid)
  [4:7:1.3] Context | Emergency (1975–77), Indira Gandhi
  [4:7:1.4] Based on | Swaran Singh Committee
  [4:7:1.5] Nickname | "Mini Constitution" (sweeping changes)
[4:7:2] - **Integrity** was not in the Preamble in **1975**; added **1976**.
[4:7:3] - **Not added** by the 42nd: **Fraternity** (original), **Sovereign** (original), Gram Swarajya.
[4:7:4] - 'Secular' / 'Secularism' and 'Socialist' added by the **42nd** – not 43rd, 44th, 45th, 52nd or 86th.
[4:7:5] # Other Changes
[4:7:6] - Added **Fundamental Duties**, new DPSPs.
[4:7:7] - **Curtailed judicial review** and tried to remove it in some matters – did **not abolish** it fully.
[4:7:8] # Minerva Mills (1980)
[4:7:9] - Struck down clauses giving Parliament **unlimited amending power** and **excluding judicial review**.
[4:7:10] - Struck down clauses giving **DPSPs full priority over Fundamental Rights**; restored the **balance of Parts III and IV**, strengthening basic structure.
[4:7:11] "1976 Second Amendment" is WRONG – it is the 42nd Amendment.
[4:7:12] # Debates
[4:7:13] - Words inserted during the Emergency **without a fresh popular mandate**.
[4:7:14] - Counter-view: secularism always inherent (Arts. 25–28).
[4:7:15] - Political debate on the words: **Jul 2025**; SC had upheld them in **Nov 2024** (Balram Singh).
