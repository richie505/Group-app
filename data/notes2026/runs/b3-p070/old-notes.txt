=== NOTES 3:70 | Energy and Power Policy - Installed Capacity Mix, Distribution Reform (uday), Renewable Targets, Energy Security
## SUB 0 | Distribution Reform – UDAY, Electricity Act 2003 and Power Tariffs
[70:0:0] # UDAY (Ujwal DISCOM Assurance Yojana)
[70:0:1] - **Launched:** Cabinet approval **5 Nov 2015** (Ministry of Power) for the **financial and operational turnaround of DISCOMs** – not household electrification (Saubhagya) or LED bulbs (UJALA).
[70:0:2] - **Debt:** States take over **75% of DISCOM debt** (50% in 2015-16, 25% in 2016-17), cutting interest from 14-15% to 8-9% – the scheme 'to reduce interest payment in the electricity sector'.
[70:0:3] - **Four initiatives:** **improving operational efficiency; reduction of cost of power; reduction in interest cost; enforcing financial discipline** – regularising DISCOM employees was NOT one.
[70:0:4] - Targets: AT&C loss ~22% → **15%**, ACS-ARR gap to zero by 2018-19; optional for States.
[70:0:5] - Replaced **Indian Electricity Act 1910, Electricity (Supply) Act 1948, ERC Act 1998**.
[70:0:6] - **Section 63** = tariffs through **competitive bidding**.
[70:0:7] TABLE head: Scheme | Year | Objective
  [70:0:7.0] DDUGJY | 2014 | Rural feeder separation
  [70:0:7.1] UDAY (Ujwal DISCOM Assurance Yojana) | Cabinet 5 Nov 2015 | DISCOM financial turnaround; States take over 75% of DISCOM debt
  [70:0:7.2] Saubhagya | 2017 | Household electrification
  [70:0:7.3] RDSS | 2021 | Cut AT&C losses; smart metering
[70:0:8] - AT&C losses **22.62% (FY14) → 15.04% (FY25)**; demand-supply gap **4.2% → zero** (Nov 2025).
[70:0:9] - **UDAY demand-side measure:** promoting **energy-efficient LED bulbs**; its other measures were operational-efficiency steps for DISCOMs.
[70:0:10] # Tariffs, consumption and policy
[70:0:11] - **Cross-subsidisation:** low domestic/farm tariffs are paid for by high industrial and commercial tariffs – why Indian household power is cheap and industrial power dear.
[70:0:12] - **Largest consumer of electricity:** **industry** (~40%), ahead of domestic and agriculture.
[70:0:13] - **Highest per capita consumption (larger States, exam answer):** **Punjab, Haryana, Gujarat**.
[70:0:14] - **National Electricity Policy:** power for all households, demand fully met by 2012, protection of consumers' interests; **'reducing the per unit price' was NOT an objective**.
[70:0:15] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Indian Economy (10th ed.) part 05 p.28-30; app notes 3:70 sub 7; general knowledge (tariffs, consumption), not checked
[70:0:16] - Under UDAY, **Andhra Pradesh** was keyed as the State yet to take over **100%** of its DISCOM debt.

## SUB 5 | Coal Sector
[70:5:0] - **Nationalisation**: coking coal mines 1971-72; non-coking under **Coal Mines (Nationalisation) Act, 1973**.
[70:5:1] - **Coal Mines (Special Provisions) Act 2015**: after the Supreme Court cancelled coal block allocations; allocation by **auction**, run by the **Nominated Authority**.
[70:5:2] - **Coal Controller's Organisation** (Ministry of Coal; HQ **Kolkata**): checks **coal quality and grading**, settles quality complaints, collects coal statistics.
[70:5:3] - **Energy Statistics India** - published by **NSO, MoSPI** (2026 edition, 30 March 2026); coal supplies about 79% of domestic energy.
[70:5:4] - **Washed coal:** cuts ash and raises plant efficiency, but little used – washing cost raises variable cost and hurts **merit-order** dispatch ranking.
[70:5:5] - **Why India imports coal:** domestic supply of **high-quality, low-ash coking coal** (for steel) is limited; power plants **designed on imported coal** and high-grade coal for **blending** cannot use domestic coal. Coal imports are under **Open General Licence (OGL)** (PIB, Dec 2021).
[70:5:6] - **Nationalisation sequence:** **life insurance 1956 (LIC) → banks 1969 → coal 1971-73 → private oil companies 1974-76** (all under Indira Gandhi except LIC).
[70:5:7] > UPSC 2019: coal nationalised under Indira Gandhi ✓; blocks by lottery ✗ (auction); India self-sufficient, no imports ✗ → 'Only I'.

## SUB 6 | Biofuels
[70:6:0] - **National Policy on Biofuels 2018** (amended 2022) / EBP: **20% ethanol blending by 2025** - to cut oil imports and help sugarcane farmers. **E20 achieved in 2025**.
[70:6:1] - Ethanol feedstocks: sugarcane juice, sugar beet, sweet sorghum, **damaged food grains**, rotten potatoes, maize, cassava.
[70:6:2] - **National Biofuel Coordination Committee (NBCC)**: headed by **Minister of Petroleum and Natural Gas**; approves use of **surplus food grains** for ethanol.
[70:6:3] > A-R (APPSC G2): NBCC approval is needed for surplus food grains because in surplus years farmers risk not getting remunerative prices - **A and R true, R explains A**.

## SUB 8 | Energy Security – Imports, Reserves and Pricing
[70:8:0] - Imports: about **85% of petroleum** (over 80%), about **50% of gas**.
[70:8:1] - **Strategic Petroleum Reserves**: **Visakhapatnam, Mangaluru, Padur** - about **9.5 days** cover vs IEA norm **90 days**.
[70:8:2] - Price decontrol: **petrol 26 June 2010**; **diesel 19 October 2014**.
[70:8:3] - **Power imports:** India imports hydropower from **Bhutan** (Chukha, Tala, Kurichhu, Mangdechhu); also trades power with Nepal and Bangladesh.
[70:8:4] - **HELP (2016):** replaced NELP; **revenue-sharing** contracts (not production sharing); a **single licence** for all hydrocarbons.
