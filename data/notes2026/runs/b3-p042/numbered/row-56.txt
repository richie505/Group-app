## SUB 1 | e-NAM – National Agriculture Market
[1:0] - Launched **April 2016**; "**One Nation, One Market**"; online platform **linking APMC mandis and other yards** – a virtual market backed by physical mandis (does not replace them).
[1:1] - Lead agency **SFAC**; Central Sector, funded via **Agri-Tech Infrastructure Fund** (2015); up to **Rs 75 lakh per mandi**.
[1:2] - **Reforms required of States:** **electronic trading / e-auction** for price discovery, **single licence valid across the State**, **single-point levy** of market fee – NOT abolition of market trading or State price regulation.
[1:3] - **E-auction** = bidding through an **electronic trading portal**.
[1:4] - **Direct payment** to the farmer's bank account; warehouse trading with **e-NWRs**.
[1:5] - **Economic Survey 2022-23:** online, competitive, transparent bidding with **1.74 crore farmers and 2.39 lakh traders** = e-NAM. Mar 2026: **1,656 mandis**.
[1:6] - **AP:** **33 markets** on e-NAM; **11 mandis** in inter-mandi trade (2024-25).
[1:7] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Indian Economy (10th ed.) part 04 p.168; AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.95; app notes 3:56 sub 5
## SUB 2 | APMC Acts, Model Acts and the Farm Laws 2020
[2:0] - Agricultural markets = **State List**; markets are regulated under **APMC Acts enacted by States** (UPSC 2015) – not the Essential Commodities Act or the Grading and Marking Act.
[2:1] - AP law: **AP (Agricultural Produce and Livestock) Markets Act, 1966**. Set-up order: **area notified** → Market Committee → market yard.
[2:2] - Regulated markets ensure **open auction, standard weights, recorded prices, licensed buyers**; ~**2,477** regulated markets; market fee **1%**.
[2:3] - APMC problems: market/licence fees, commission charges, **cartels of licensed traders**, too few mandis; **price spread** = **consumer price − farmer's price**; NCF: a regulated market within **~5 km**; poor storage → **distress sale**.
[2:4] - Farmer's share in the consumer rupee (RBI, Oct 2024): tomato ~**33%**, pulses higher (gram ~**75%**).
[2:5] TABLE head: Model Act | Key feature
  [2:5.0] Model APMC Act, 2003 | Direct sale to contract-farming sponsors; private markets; farmer-consumer markets
  [2:5.1] Model APLM Act, 2017 | Whole State one market area; single-point fee, unified licence
  [2:5.2] Model Contract Farming Act, 2018 | Outside APMC; sponsor buys the entire pre-agreed quantity; farmer = weaker party; registration by RARC
[2:6] # Farm laws 2020
[2:7] TABLE head: Farm law (Sept 2020) | Core provision
  [2:7.0] Farmers' Produce Trade and Commerce Act | Trade in a "trade area" outside APMC yards, no State market fee; mentions APMCs and essential commodities but not MSP
  [2:7.1] Farmers (Empowerment and Protection) Agreement on Price Assurance and Farm Services Act | Contract farming
  [2:7.2] Essential Commodities (Amendment) Act | Removed stock limits except in extraordinary situations
[2:8] - Passed under **Concurrent List Entry 33**; **repealed November 2021**.
[2:9] - **Post-1991 reforms (match):** GM crops → debates on food security and farmer rights; **WTO Agreement on Agriculture → market access beyond borders**; trade liberalisation → private participation/contract farming; price reforms → less government price control.
[2:10] - AP Markets (Amendment) Act **2005** added contract farming.

## SUB 3 | Contract Farming, e-Choupal and Small Farmer Large Field
[3:0] - **Contract farming** = arrangement between farmer producers and agri-business firms to sell produce at a **pre-fixed Price or Quantity or Time or All Three**; buyer often supplies inputs and advice; **no land transfer**. Pioneer: **Punjab (PepsiCo, late 1980s)**.
[3:1] - **e-Choupal** = **ITC**'s internet-kiosk rural marketing network (2000).
[3:2] - **Small Farmer Large Field (UPSC 2023):** many marginal farmers in an area **organise into groups and synchronise/harmonise selected operations** – they work as one large field (no land surrender).
[3:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy P1 - APPSC 02 p.31; app notes 3:56 sub 3; general knowledge (e-Choupal, SFLF key), not checked
## SUB 4 | Cooperative Marketing, NAFED and FPOs
[4:0] - **NAFED** (2 October 1958) = **apex cooperative marketing body at national level** – agricultural marketing (not FCI); PM-AASHA PSS: **pulses, oilseeds, copra**.
[4:1] - **TRIFED** (1987) = tribal produce marketing. **AP MARKFED** = apex of Cooperative Marketing Societies in AP; **Rs 3,000 crore Price Stabilisation Fund**.
[4:2] - **10,000 FPO scheme (2020):** Central Sector, **Rs 6,865 crore**; agencies **SFAC, NABARD, NCDC, NAFED**; one FPO per **block**; CBBO handholding **5 years**; management support Rs 18 lakh over 3 years; equity grant **Rs 2,000 per member** (max Rs 15 lakh); credit guarantee up to **Rs 2 crore**. Aim: bargaining power, scale, lower costs (not land transfer).
[4:3] - **NCDC** promotes cooperative-sector FPOs through PACS.
[4:4] - **Producer companies:** introduced by the **Companies (Amendment) Act, 2002** (Part IXA).
[4:5] - **SFAC "Millets Giveaway"** (IYM 2023): buy directly from FPOs, try ONDC's MyStore, adopt Shree Anna – **not FDI**.
[4:6] ~~ Sources: AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.62, p.65; app notes 3:56 sub 7; general knowledge (Producer companies 2002), not checked
## SUB 5 | Operation Greens, Export Schemes and Agri Infrastructure
[5:0] - **Operation Greens:** **Union Budget 2018-19**, for **TOP (tomato, onion, potato)** price volatility, "**on the lines of Operation Flood**"; promotes FPOs, agri-logistics, processing; Ministry of **Food Processing Industries**.
[5:1] - **TOP to TOTAL:** Budget **2021-22**, **22** perishables; long-term grant 50% (**70% for FPOs**).
[5:2] - **Vishesh Krishi (and Gram) Upaj Yojana:** promotes **exports of agricultural produce** by offsetting freight costs.
[5:3] - **AIF (2020):** **Rs 1 lakh crore**; **3%** subvention on loans up to **Rs 2 crore**; guarantee via **CGTMSE**.
[5:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy P1 - APPSC 02 p.11; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy (10th ed.) part 07 p.11 (Budget 2018-19); app notes 3:56 sub 9
