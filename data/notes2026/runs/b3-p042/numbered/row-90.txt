## SUB 0 | Stock Exchanges – BSE, NSE, India INX and Depositories
[0:0] TABLE head: Exchange | Facts
  [0:0.0] BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange) | Brokers moved to Dalal Street, Mumbai in 1874; set up 1875 as the Native Share and Stock Brokers' Association; Asia's oldest exchange; first exchange recognised under the SCRA, on 31 Aug 1957; index SENSEX; promoted the depository CDSL
  [0:0.1] NSE (National Stock Exchange) | Set up November 1992, Mumbai, on the Manohar J. Pherwani Committee; recognised by SEBI April 1993; trading from 1994; first segment Wholesale Debt Market, then Cash Market; India's first fully automated electronic exchange; largest by trading volume
  [0:0.2] India INX | Set up by BSE at GIFT City IFSC, Gandhinagar on 30 Dec 2016 – India's first international exchange
  [0:0.3] NSE IX | Also GIFT City; NSE IX Global Access lets Indians buy US stocks
  [0:0.4] MSE (Metropolitan Stock Exchange) | Mumbai; began 2008 as MCX-SX, renamed 2015
[0:1] - **NSE promoters:** leading financial institutions at the Government's behest – **IDBI, SBI, IFCI, LIC, GIC, IDFC** etc. → "SBI, LIC and GIC, IDBI – **all of the above**".
[0:2] - **Bulk of the business:** the **NSE** handles the bulk of Indian stock-market trading (Ramesh Singh: about 75%); it had the **highest turnover in 2000-01** (₹8.39 lakh crore) and is still the largest by volume. The RBI is not an exchange.
[0:3] - **BSE statements** "oldest exchange of India", "Dalal Street", "index = SENSEX" are **all correct**; "BSE was set up in 1992 by financial institutions" is false (that is the NSE).
[0:4] - **Regulator:** stock exchanges (and both primary and secondary markets) are regulated by **SEBI**; exchanges in the **IFSC (India INX, NSE IX)** are regulated by **IFSCA**, not SEBI.
[0:5] - **Depositories** (Depositories Act, 1996) hold shares in **electronic (demat)** form: **NSDL** (National Securities Depository Ltd, **first**, Mumbai, 1996) and **CDSL** (promoted by **BSE**, not NSE).
[0:6] - **NSE chair:** **Girish Chandra Chaturvedi** became chairman in **Dec 2019** (term ended July 2024); **now Srinivas Injeti**, chairperson of the NSE Governing Board from **9 Sep 2025** (as of 2026).
[0:7] > Most-asked: NSE/BSE location = **Mumbai** (6 PYQs); first international exchange = **Gandhinagar (GIFT City)**; BSE year = **1875**.

## SUB 2 | Market Players and Terms – Bull, Bear, Stag, Blue Chip, Beta
[2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [2:0.0] Bull (Tejadia) | Expects prices to rise; bull market = rising
  [2:0.1] Bear (Mandadia) | Expects prices of a security to fall; sells now to buy back lower
  [2:0.2] Stag | Buys IPO shares to sell soon after listing
  [2:0.3] Lame duck | A share-market term (with Bull, Bear, Stag)
  [2:0.4] Blue chip | Shares of large, well-established, financially sound companies giving consistent high returns
  [2:0.5] Beta | Measures a stock's fluctuations (systematic risk) relative to the overall market (beta 1 = moves with the market)
  [2:0.6] Short selling | Selling borrowed shares to buy back lower
  [2:0.7] Circuit breaker | Trading halt when an index or stock moves past limits
  [2:0.8] Sweat equity | Shares issued to employees or directors for know-how or services, often at a discount
  [2:0.9] Insider trading | Trading on non-public, material information – a share-market offence
[2:1] - **Bull and Bear** are **share-market** terms, not foreign trade, banking or manufacturing.
[2:2] - **Speculators:** Bull, Bear, Stag. A **broker** is a registered member who buys and sells on clients' behalf for a commission – **not a speculator** (nor a "bookie").
[2:3] - **Settlement:** **T+1** (one trading day after the trade); optional **T+0**.
[2:4] > NOT-angle: "not a speculator" / "not a bookie" → **Broker** (UP RO/ARO 2017, twice).
[2:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #55 p.81, #56 p.82, p.83 (blue chip, beta); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.33 (insider trading); Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy 05 p.133 (broker), 06 p.141 (short selling); app notes 3:90 sub 4
## SUB 3 | Primary vs Secondary Market and the IPO Process
[3:0] TABLE head: Basis | Primary (new issue) market | Secondary market
  [3:0.0] Deals in | New securities (issued for the first time) | Existing securities
  [3:0.1] Transactions | Issuing company → investors | Investor ↔ investor
  [3:0.2] Examples | IPOs, FPOs, rights issues, private placement | Stock-exchange trading; trading in the gilt-edged (G-Sec) market
  [3:0.3] Price | Fixed by the firm | Demand and supply
  [3:0.4] Money goes to | The issuer | The selling investor
[3:1] - Transactions in the **stock exchange and the gilt-edged market** = **secondary market**; SEBI regulates both markets.
[3:2] - **Gilt-edged market** = market for government and semi-government securities ("of the best quality", no default risk), backed by the RBI.
[3:3] - **Offer for sale (OFS):** proceeds go to **selling shareholders**; only a **fresh issue** brings money to the company.
[3:4] TABLE head: IPO term | Meaning
  [3:4.0] Book building | Bids within a price band; final price set from the bids
  [3:4.1] Anchor investors | QIBs bidding before the issue opens, with a lock-in
  [3:4.2] SME IPOs | Separate platforms BSE SME and NSE Emerge (2012)
  [3:4.3] Listing | Within T+3 days of issue closing
  [3:4.4] Large-company float | Very large firms may offer only 2.75% (earlier 5%) (SEBI, Sep 2025)
  [3:4.5] ASBA | Application Supported by Blocked Amount (SEBI, 2008): money stays blocked in the investor's account until allotment
  [3:4.6] Greenshoe option | Over-allotment of usually up to 15% extra shares

## SUB 4 | Instruments – Shares, Debentures, Mutual Funds and ETFs
[4:0] TABLE head: Holder / instrument | Claim
  [4:0.0] Shareholders | Owners; dividends; residual claim on earnings and assets
  [4:0.1] Debenture holders | Creditors – debentures are long-term debt at a fixed rate of interest; paid before shareholders in liquidation
  [4:0.2] Mutual funds | Pooled money priced at NAV (computed at the end of each trading session); arbitrage funds keep at least 65% in equity
  [4:0.3] ETF | Fund traded on exchanges like a share, price changes through the day; NAV still computed daily; high liquidity and low costs
[4:1] - Debenture holders are **not** shareholders, debtors or directors of the company.
[4:2] - **ETF NOT-statement (CDS 2018):** "ETFs have **lower** liquidity and **higher** fees than mutual funds" is **wrong** – ETFs are known for low costs.
[4:3] - **Equity crowdfunding** is not permitted: SEBI called online platforms offering it unauthorised (2016).
[4:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Exams 10 p.23, 11 p.1 (shares, debentures, mutual funds, NAV); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.25 (ETF); Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy 05 p.65 (ETFs), p.140 (debenture holders' primacy); app notes 3:90 sub 5
## SUB 5 | Derivatives and Transaction Taxes (STT, CTT)
[5:0] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [5:0.0] Futures | Obligation to buy/sell at a fixed future price
  [5:0.1] Options | Right, not obligation: call = right to buy, put = right to sell
  [5:0.2] Swaps | Exchange of cash flows (fixed vs floating interest)
  [5:0.3] Commodity derivatives | Regulated by SEBI since the FMC merger (2015); NCDEX RAINMUMBAI = India's first weather derivative
[5:1] - Derivatives derive their value from an **underlying** (equity, index, interest rate, commodity); they are securities under the **SCRA, 1956**.
[5:2] - F&O market designed by the **L.C. Gupta (1998)** and **J.R. Varma (2002)** Committees.
[5:3] - Indian exchanges handled about **84%** of equity options traded worldwide (Q1 2024); SEBI: **9 in 10** option traders lost money.
[5:4] - **STT** (Securities Transaction Tax, 2004) on exchange trades; **Budget 2026-27**: futures **0.02% → 0.05%**, options **0.10% → 0.15%** – aim: **curb speculation**.
[5:5] - **Commodity Transaction Tax (CTT):** introduced in the **Union Budget 2013-14**, **0.01%** on non-agricultural commodity futures.
