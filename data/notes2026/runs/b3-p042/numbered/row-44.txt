## SUB 0 | Human Capital Formation – Meaning, Means and Indicators
[0:0] - **Human capital** = the **stock of skill and productive knowledge** (and health) embodied in people; population becomes human capital when there is investment in **education, training and medical care**.
[0:1] - **Human capital formation** = a process of **increasing the knowledge, skill levels and capacities** of the people – **intangible wealth** (UPSC 2018 key: "2 and 4"); it is not accumulation of tangible wealth or of more (physical) capital.
[0:2] - **Means:** investment in **education, health, on-the-job training, migration and information**. **Skill development** programmes enhance **human capital** (not physical, working or fixed capital). **Purchase of machinery** = physical capital; **irrigation** has no direct impact on human capital.
[0:3] - **Returns:** investment in human capital **yields returns in the future** because **education and health make people more productive** (UPPSC 2025 A-R: both true, R explains A). India's IT revolution shows human capital ranking above plant and machinery.
[0:4] - **Kerala:** low **infant mortality rate** because of **adequate provision of basic health and educational facilities** (APPSC 2025).
[0:5] - **Economic Survey 2025-26** (ch. 11): shift from **access to quality**, learning outcomes and **preventive care**; new concerns obesity and digital addiction.
[0:6] - Indicators cited: **MMR 88** per lakh live births; **stunting 29.3%**, wasting 19%.
[0:7] > Human capital = people (education, health, skills); physical capital = machines, buildings. Statement items test "intangible" and "process of raising knowledge and skills".

## SUB 10 | Learning Theories, Learning Curve and Behaviour Therapy
[10:0] - **Gagne's hierarchy of learning:** signal (conditioned-reflex) → stimulus-response → chaining → verbal association → discrimination → concept → rule → **problem-solving (highest)**.
[10:1] - **Guthrie's contiguity theory:** we forget **not by disuse but by other (new) learning** – interference.
[10:2] - **Plateau** in the learning curve: a period of no visible progress when the learner is **consolidating previous learning and preparing for further learning** – not the final stage.
[10:3] - **Bandura** – social (observational) learning theory, **Bobo doll** experiment (1961); rats/pigeons = Skinner, dog = Pavlov.
[10:4] - **Assimilated learning:** a group learns something and **its behaviour and attitudes change** as a result (printed key).
[10:5] - **Behaviour therapy:** counselling based on **learning theories and principles** (conditioning).
[10:6] - **Motives** are not observed directly – they are **inferences from behaviour**.
[10:7] - **Classification / sorting** by similarities and differences develops **cognitive** skills (Piaget).
[10:8] - **Word mapping errors:** **under-extension** = defining a word too narrowly ('dog' only for the family pet); **over-extension** = using it too broadly.

## SUB 11 | Teaching Aids, Micro-teaching and Evaluation
[11:0] - **Projected aids:** need a projector – **epidiascope**, slides, filmstrips, films, OHP. **Non-projected aids:** charts, pictures, graphs, models, pictographs, **globe, diorama, blackboard**.
[11:1] - **Micro-teaching** (Stanford, **1963**): a **skill-based** teacher-training technique – one skill practised in a short, scaled-down lesson with feedback.
[11:2] - **A good test** must have **validity, reliability and objectivity** (all three).
[11:3] - **Test-retest reliability:** the **same test given twice** to the same group over a time gap; parallel forms = two equivalent tests; split-half = one test divided.
[11:4] - **Diagnostic test:** finds specific weaknesses (reading, maths) so that **remedial instruction** or level placement can follow; achievement test measures what was learnt.
[11:5] - **Shanghai (maths mastery) technique:** one concept per lesson, interactive whole-class teaching of an **all-ability group**, topic covered in depth, class moves on only when all have mastered it; **piloted in the UK** – all four statements correct.
[11:6] - **The World of Learning** (Europa/Routledge annual): directory of **universities, colleges, libraries, museums, research institutes and learned societies** worldwide.
