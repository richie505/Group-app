## SUB 0 | New Industrial Policy 1991 – Delicensing and Liberalisation
[0:0] # The Policy
[0:1] - **Date:** **24 July 1991** – PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**, FM **Manmohan Singh**; announced amid the **balance-of-payments crisis** (forex for about two weeks of imports, June 1991).
[0:2] - **Liberal industrial policy = 1991:** start of **LPG**; ended the **'Licence-Permit-Quota Raj'** (term coined by C. Rajagopalachari, Swatantra Party) – licensing, expansion limits, price controls and import-export curbs removed.
[0:3] - **Liberalisation started with** **substantial changes in industrial licensing policy** (UPSC 2000) – not rupee convertibility or tax cuts.
[0:4] - **Result (APPSC AEE 2011):** **liberalisation of industrial licensing and foreign-trade regulations** – not privatisation of core PSEs, not an immediate abolition of FERA.
[0:5] - **Repealed Act:** **FERA 1973** was replaced by **FEMA 1999** (in force 1 June 2000) – the Act repealed as part of liberalisation; FEMA is the successor, not the repealed Act.
[0:6] - **Liberalisation tools:** **delicensing, import deregulation, lower import duty on capital goods** → cheaper technology and capital equipment, more competitive home manufacturing.
[0:7] - Government role moved from **regulator → facilitator**; NIP 1991 was the primary anchor of **liberalisation**.
[0:8] # Delicensing – the numbers
[0:9] TABLE head: Stage | Industries under compulsory licensing
  [0:9.0] July 1991 | 18 (coal and lignite, petroleum, alcohol, sugar, animal fats, cigarettes, asbestos, plywood, hides and leather, fur skins, motor cars, paper, electronic aerospace and defence, explosives, hazardous chemicals, drugs, entertainment electronics, white goods)
  [0:9.1] 1993 | Automobiles, white goods, leather delicensed
  [0:9.2] Dec 1996 / Jul 1997 | Electronic goods; then 5 more delicensed
  [0:9.3] 1998-99 | Coal and petroleum exempted → 6 (incl. drugs and pharmaceuticals)
  [0:9.4] Most books (NextGen, Ramesh Singh) | 5 – alcohol, cigarettes, electronic aerospace and defence equipment, industrial explosives, hazardous chemicals
  [0:9.5] Now (DPIIT Press Note 3, 2019 Series, 11 Sep 2019) | 4 – cigars and cigarettes; electronic aerospace and defence equipment; industrial explosives; hazardous chemicals
[0:10] - **Alcoholic drinks** no longer appear in DPIIT's list; **drugs and pharmaceuticals** were delicensed earlier.
[0:11] - Delicensed units file only an **Industrial Entrepreneur Memorandum (IEM)** with the Secretariat for Industrial Assistance.
[0:12] - **'White goods'** (refrigerators, washing machines, ACs) = **items purchased for conspicuous consumption** (UPSC 1998); delicensed **1993**.
[0:13] > Most-asked angle (about 14 PYQs): the year 1991 and the licensing count. APPSC's "at present" answer is **4** (DPIIT 2019); "18" is the July 1991 figure.

## SUB 1 | Industrial Policy Resolution 1956 – the 'Economic Constitution'
[1:0] # Background
[1:1] - Announced **30 April 1956** (placed before Parliament by **Jawaharlal Nehru**); reviewed IPR 1948 after the First Plan (1951-56).
[1:2] - Goal: **"Socialistic pattern of society"** – Lok Sabha resolution Dec 1954, Congress **Avadi session, 1955**.
[1:3] - Labels: **"Economic Constitution of India"**, **"Bible of State Capitalism"**; **basis of the Second Five-Year Plan** (Mahalanobis model – heavy and basic industries).
[1:4] - Significance: established the **public-sector-led, licence-based** model that lasted, with minor changes, until **1991**.
[1:5] # Three Schedules
[1:6] TABLE head: Schedule | Industries | Role of the State
  [1:6.0] A | 17 basic and strategic (defence, heavy industry, minerals, transport and communication, power); arms and ammunition, atomic energy, railways, air transport = State monopolies | Exclusive responsibility of the State
  [1:6.1] B | 12 (other minerals, road and sea transport, machine tools, ferro-alloys, basic chemicals, drugs, fertilisers, synthetic rubber, aluminium and non-ferrous metals) | Progressively State-owned; private sector may supplement
  [1:6.2] C | All remaining | Left to private initiative, subject to licensing
[1:7] # Major Provisions
[1:8] - **Provision of licensing, expansion of the public sector, emphasis on small and cottage industries** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025: all three correct); also **balanced regional development**, labour welfare, khadi and village industries.
[1:9] - **Licence raj:** compulsory licensing for Schedule B and many Schedule C industries was cemented in IPR 1956 – the start of the 'Licence-Quota-Permit raj'.
[1:10] - Licensing itself drew its legal strength from the **Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951** (not from IPR 1948 or 1956, and not from the MRTP Act).
[1:11] - **Basic industries:** iron and steel, cement, fertilisers, coal, power – **paper is not a basic industry** (it is a consumer-goods industry).
[1:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy APPSC 21 p.7-9; NextGen Indian Economy P1 APPSC 04 p.25, 35; Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 05 p.9.4-9.5; Rocket Indian Economy p.2, 5, 54
## SUB 2 | Industrial Policy Resolution 1948 – First Policy and Mixed Economy
[2:0] - **First industrial (economic) policy of independent India:** **6 April 1948** (some books print 8 April), by Industry Minister **Dr Shyama Prasad Mukherjee**.
[2:1] - It provided for a **mixed economy** – it **defined the roles and spheres of both public and private sectors**; it neither nationalised all key industries nor left everything to private enterprise.
[2:2] - Preceded by the **Statement of Industrial Policy, 1945** (pre-independence); followed by the **IDR Act, 1951**.
[2:3] TABLE head: Category | Industries | Arrangement
  [2:3.0] Exclusive Central monopoly | Arms and ammunition, atomic energy, railways | State only
  [2:3.1] Mixed sector (6) | Coal, mineral oils, iron and steel, aircraft, shipbuilding, telephone/telegraph apparatus | New units by the State; existing private units allowed 10 years, then review
  [2:3.2] Centrally regulated (18) | Salt, automobiles, heavy chemicals, fertilisers, power, textiles, cement, sugar, paper etc. | Private, under Central regulation
  [2:3.3] Residual | All others | Private and co-operative
[2:4] - Also: importance to **cottage and small-scale industries**; foreign capital welcomed with control in **Indian hands**.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy APPSC 21 p.5-7; NextGen Indian Economy P1 APPSC 04 p.25, 35; Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 05 p.9.3
## SUB 3 | Licensing Committees, Joint Sector and MRTP
[3:0] TABLE head: Person / committee | Contribution
  [3:0.0] Swaminathan (1964), Mahalanobis (1964) | Early reviews of licensing
  [3:0.1] R.K. Hazari report (1967) | Industrial licensing – big business houses had cornered the licences
  [3:0.2] Subimal Dutt – Industrial Licensing Policy Inquiry Committee (1967, report 1969) | Led to the MRTP Act 1969; recommended the joint sector
[3:1] - **Outcome of the licence regime:** it did **not** prevent concentration of economic power – a powerful industrial house could always procure fresh licences, so the result was the **expansion of big industrial houses**.
[3:2] - **MRTP Act 1969** (in force 1970): prevent concentration of economic power and monopolistic practices; replaced by the **Competition Act, 2002**.
[3:3] - **Joint sector** (State + private partnership): recommended by the **Dutt Committee (1969)**, developed in the **Industrial Policy Statement 1973**; many keys (UPPCS 2004, Rocket) link it to **IPR 1956**.
[3:4] - Licence raj criticised for **rent-seeking**, entry barriers, delays and red tape.
[3:5] > Trap: Hazari = report 1967; Dutt = Inquiry Committee 1969 → MRTP and joint sector.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy APPSC 22 p.5; NextGen Indian Economy P1 APPSC 04 p.29; Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 05 p.9.5-9.6; Rocket Indian Economy p.2
## SUB 4 | Policy Statements 1973, 1977 and 1980
[4:0] # 1973 Statement
[4:1] - First identified a list of **'core sector'** industries (iron and steel, cement, coal, crude oil, refining, electricity); developed the joint sector; **FERA 1973**.
[4:2] - **Reservation of items for SSI** began in **1967** and continued after 1991; fully removed in **2015**.
[4:3] # Industrial Policy Statement 1977 (Janata Government)
[4:4] - Drafted largely by **George Fernandes**.
[4:5] - Main thrust: **small-scale, tiny and cottage industries**, khadi, handlooms – because they generate **large employment** (APPSC G1 1990-91 and G2 2017 – not heavy industry, FDI or mining).
[4:6] - **District Industries Centres (DICs):** one-roof support to small and cottage industries in every district.
[4:7] - Curbed large houses through **MRTP and FERA** → **IBM and Coca-Cola** left India; Coca-Cola left (1977) rather than dilute equity and **reveal its secret formula** to the Government.
[4:8] # Industrial Policy Statement 1980 (Congress)
[4:9] - Built on **IPR 1956**; removed the 'artificial barrier' between large and small units.
[4:10] - Focus: **technological upgradation**, modernisation and **export orientation** (first strong export emphasis).
[4:11] - **Economic federalism:** tax holidays and cheap credit for **backward districts**.
[4:12] - Regularised excess capacity; MRTP firms could expand capacity **25%** without approval; SSI investment limits raised.
[4:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy APPSC 21 p.9-10; Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 05 p.9.6-9.7; app notes 3:63 sub 4
## SUB 5 | NIP 1991 – Foreign Investment, MRTP and Finance
[5:0] - **FDI:** **automatic approval up to 51% foreign equity** in a list of **high-priority, high-investment, high-technology** industries (capital goods, entertainment electronics, food processing) – **not all industries**; NRIs were encouraged, not barred.
[5:1] - Automatic approval for **foreign technology agreements** in priority industries; foreign brand names allowed; 100% in EOUs, power, software parks; foreign equity up to **24%** in SSI units.
[5:2] - Policy towards foreign **multinationals: encouraging them** (Enron, Coke).
[5:3] - **FIPB** set up to speed approvals; **abolished June 2017** (Cabinet decision 24 May 2017) → approvals by ministries through the **Foreign Investment Facilitation Portal** run by **DIPP (now DPIIT)** → **National Single Window System** (Aug 2022).
[5:4] - **MRTP:** asset threshold (₹100 crore) **scrapped** → no prior approval for expansion, mergers, takeovers → M&A rose.
[5:5] - **Mandatory convertibility clause** (FIs converting term loans into equity) **no longer applicable** – abolished for new projects, ending the indirect threat of nationalisation.
[5:6] - Now: **100% automatic route** in most sectors.
[5:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy APPSC 22 p.2; NextGen Indian Economy APPSC 13 p.5-7; Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 05 p.9.10-9.12

## SUB 7 | Impact and Criticism of the 1991 Policy
[7:0] - **Criticisms of NIP 1991:** ambiguous policy for weak industries, **threat from foreign competition**, **misplaced faith in foreign investment**, **erratic industrial growth**, regional concentration (Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka), small units closed by cheap imports.
[7:1] - **"Neglect of agriculture"** is a criticism of the reform package as a whole (NCERT), **not** of the industrial policy (Jharkhand PSC 2013).
[7:2] - **NOT criticisms (achievements):** higher growth, FDI and forex surge, **increase in consumer choice**, global Indian MNCs.
[7:3] - MSMEs hurt by **import competition** after protection was removed.
[7:4] - Forex reserves record **USD 702.28 bn (Nov 2025)**.
[7:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy P1 APPSC 03 p.42; NextGen Indian Economy APPSC 14 p.8; app notes 3:63 sub 8

## SUB 9 | Andhra Pradesh Industrial Policies
[9:0] - **AP Industrial Policy 2005-10:** proposed changes in the State **Cooperative Act** (State subjects – the IDR and MRTP Acts are Central laws).
[9:1] - **AP Industrial Investment Promotion Policy 2015-20**.
[9:2] - **AP Industrial Development Policy 2020-23:** objectives – **employment for the youth**, match the national average of industrial GVA per capita, **balanced growth across regions and communities**, **environmentally sustainable growth** ("enhance gainful employment" is a stated goal of the national industrial policy, not this one).
[9:3] - **AP Industrial Development Policy 4.0 (2024-29):** **G.O.Ms.No.68, 26 Oct 2024**; valid **5 years or till a new policy**.
[9:4] - Targets: **₹30 lakh crore** investment, **USD 10 bn FDI** (not 100 bn), **5 lakh** jobs, exports **$20 → $40 bn**, **175+ industrial parks**.
[9:5] - Approvals: **SIPB = Chief Minister**; **SIPC = Chief Secretary**.
[9:6] TABLE head: Sector type / policy | Content
  [9:6.0] Sustenance sectors | Automobiles, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, textiles
  [9:6.1] Propelling sectors | Semiconductors and electronics, EVs, aerospace-defence, blue economy, green energy

  [9:6.4] Maritime Policy 2024-29 | Nodal AP Maritime Board; one port in the world's top 20 by 2047; 20% of India's port cargo
