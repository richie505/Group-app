## SUB 0 | NABARD – Establishment, Role, Refinance and RIDF
[0:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [0:0.0] Full form | National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (traps: Nation, Real, Net, Research)
  [0:0.1] Recommended by | CRAFICARD (1979-81), B. Sivaraman
  [0:0.2] Act | NABARD Act, 1981 (Act 61 of 1981 of Parliament)
  [0:0.3] Established | 12 July 1982 (Sixth Five Year Plan)
  [0:0.4] Headquarters | Mumbai; initial corpus Rs 100 crore
  [0:0.5] Ownership | RBI sold its stake in 2019 → fully owned by the Government of India
[0:1] - **Merged/replaced at birth:** RBI's **Agricultural Credit Department (ACD)**, **Rural Planning and Credit Cell (RPCC)** and the **Agricultural Refinance and Development Corporation (ARDC)** – NOT IFCI, IDBI or SIDBI.
[0:2] - **Apex development finance institution** for **agriculture AND rural development** (not agriculture alone; also cottage, small and village industries) – the **apex bank for rural / agricultural credit** and the **only body catering to all types of rural credit**; set up as an apex body to **coordinate all institutions in the rural financing system**.
[0:3] - **Primary role = refinance**: refinances **Scheduled Commercial Banks, RRBs, State Cooperative Banks, State Land Development Banks** – **not EXIM Bank**; does not lend directly to farmers (lends to **States** via RIDF). Refinance only for agriculture and rural development (not export or industrial finance).
[0:4] - **Supervises RRBs and State/District cooperative banks**; launched **SHG-Bank Linkage (1992)**.
[0:5] - **Credit planning:** NABARD prepares district-wise **Potential Linked Credit Plans (PLPs)** → roll up into the **State Focus Paper** → base of the State's **Annual Credit Plan**. AP 2026-27 plan: **Rs 5.11 lakh crore**.
[0:6] - **NABARD Award for Rural Innovations** instituted in **2012** to mark NABARD's **30 years**.
[0:7] TABLE head: NABARD fund | Year / size | Purpose
  [0:7.0] RIDF (Rural Infrastructure Development Fund) | 1995-96; funded by banks' PSL shortfall deposits | Loans to States for rural roads, bridges, godowns, irrigation, fisheries, forests

  [0:7.2] Warehouse Infrastructure Fund | 2013-14, Rs 5,000 crore | Warehousing

## SUB 1 | Cooperative Credit Structure and Land Development Banks
[1:0] TABLE head: Tier / body | Level | Credit
  [1:0.0] PACS | Village | Short-term, direct to farmers
  [1:0.1] DCCB | District | Short-term; main function finances PACS
  [1:0.2] State Cooperative Bank (e.g. APCOB) | State apex | Short-term; refinanced by NABARD
  [1:0.3] Land Development Banks (now Cooperative Agriculture and Rural Development Banks) | State/primary | Long-term – land development, wells, machinery
  [1:0.4] RRBs | Region | Direct to small farmers, labourers, artisans
[1:1] - Short-term cooperative chain: **StCB → DCCB → PACS** (3-tier); long-term: **LDBs / CARDBs**. LDBs are part of the **Cooperative Credit Structure** (quasi-commercial), not commercial banks, IDBI or FCI.
[1:2] - **First Land Development Bank: Jhang (Punjab), 1920.**
[1:3] - Cooperative banks work on the **"No Profit No Loss"** model and "one member one vote"; they lend beyond agriculture; **NABARD is not a cooperative bank**.
[1:4] - **Direct credit to rural households:** **RRBs and Land Development Banks**; **NABARD only refinances** (UPSC 2013: 1 and 3).
[1:5] - UPSC 2020: **Scheduled Commercial Banks**, not DCCBs, deliver the most short-term farm credit; financing PACS is a key DCCB function.
[1:6] - Institutional sources: **cooperative societies/banks, commercial banks, RRBs**, government (**taccavi** loans); non-institutional: moneylenders, relatives, traders.
[1:7] - **SIDBI** = main institution for **MSMEs**, not agriculture.

## SUB 3 | Kisan Credit Card
[3:0] - Launched **August 1998 (1998-99)** – not 2001 – as a **NABARD model scheme** (with RBI), on the **R.V. Gupta Committee** recommendation; aim: **adequate and timely credit** for cultivation needs.
[3:1] - **Issuers:** **commercial banks, RRBs, cooperative banks/credit societies (PACS)**, small finance banks – not NABARD, EXIM Bank, payment banks or local area banks.
[3:2] - **Short-term purposes (UPSC 2020: 1, 3, 4):** crop cultivation, **post-harvest expenses**, marketing loans, **consumption requirements** of farm households, **working capital for maintenance of farm assets**; also investment credit for allied activities → KCC covers **consumption and investment credit**.
[3:3] - **Not** short-term KCC credit: **combine harvesters, tractors, mini trucks**, family house, village cold storage.
[3:4] - Extended to allied/non-farm activities **2004**; **animal husbandry and fisheries 2019**; electronic KCC per **T.M. Bhasin** Working Group.
[3:5] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [3:5.0] Collateral-free | Up to Rs 2 lakh; Rs 3 lakh with hypothecation tie-up
  [3:5.1] Operative KCCs (31 March 2025) | 7.72 crore
  [3:5.2] RBI KCC Directions (19 June 2026) | Tenure 6 years for loans from 1 January 2027
[3:6] - **Pashu Kisan Credit Card:** **Haryana (2020)**; first cards to 101 animal farmers at Bhiwani (6 December).

## SUB 4 | Sources and Shares of Agricultural Credit
[4:0] - **Largest source:** **commercial banks** – about **three-fourths** of institutional farm credit (2010-11: 73.86%; cooperatives 16.68%).
[4:1] - **Agency order:** **commercial banks > cooperative banks > RRBs** (3, 1, 2); **least: foreign private banks**.
[4:2] - After the **1991 reforms**, credit flow to **agriculture** rose notably.
[4:3] - **Rural branches** of SCBs incl. RRBs ≈ **37%** of all branches (RBI, 30 June 2012).
[4:4] - Non-institutional share in rural credit: ~90% (1950) → **23.4%** (2021-22).
[4:5] - **Ground Level Credit FY25 Rs 28.69 lakh crore** vs target **Rs 27.50 lakh crore**; **South** gets ~48% of credit with ~17% of cropped area.
[4:6] ~~ Sources: Economic Survey 2025-26 Highlights part 1 p.23 (GLC FY25); app notes 3:55 sub 7; general knowledge (RBI branch share 2012), not checked
