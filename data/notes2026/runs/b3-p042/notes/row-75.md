=== Meaning of BoP – BoP vs Balance of Trade
# Definition
- **Balance of Payments (BoP):** a **systematic and complete record of all economic transactions between the residents of a country and the rest of the world** during a period, **normally one year**.
- BoP has **two main accounts – the current account and the capital account** (NextGen); it is not "only capital" or "only current" or "only exports and imports".
- **IMF BPM6** uses **three accounts – current, capital and financial**; the **RBI generally combines capital and financial** under "Capital Account".
- BoP includes **visible trade, invisible trade and borrowings** – "all of the above"; the term is used in relation to **exports and imports** (foreign trade), not factory sales or tax collection.
- UPSC 2013 wording: BoP = systematic record of "**all import and export transactions** of a country during a given period, normally a year" – the official key, the closest fit (goods exports only, government-to-government deals or capital movements alone are parts).
# Balance of Trade vs Balance of Payments
| Basis | Balance of Trade (BoT) | Balance of Payments (BoP) |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | **Goods only (visible)** | All transactions: goods, services, transfers, income, capital |
| Formula | **Goods exports − goods imports** | Whole external account, double-entry |
| Place | **Part of the current account** | Current + capital/financial account |
| Picture | Not the complete record | **Better, complete picture** |
- **A-R (UPSC 2006):** BoP gives a better picture than BoT **because** it covers both **visible and invisible** items – A and R both true, R explains A.
- **BoT surplus**: goods exports > goods imports; **BoT deficit**: goods imports > goods exports. "Exports minus imports" is the BoT, **not** the BoP.
- **Favourable BoP** – achieved by **stepping up exports and reducing imports** (both).

=== Structure – Current Account vs Capital Account
| Current account | Capital (and financial) account |
|---|---|
| Merchandise exports and imports (visible trade; trade balance) | **Foreign loans**, **FDI**, **portfolio investment** (FPI/FII), ADRs/GDRs |
| **Invisibles – services**: software, travel, transport, banking, insurance | ECB, external assistance |
| Invisibles – **primary income**: interest, dividends, profits, wages | **NRI deposits (FCNR(B))**, banking capital |
| **Secondary income**: **private remittances**, gifts, grants | Short-term trade credit; change in forex reserves |
- **Current account** = goods + services + income + transfers; it **includes the Balance of Trade**. **Capital receipts and payments are NOT** part of it (CDS 2014).
- **Invisibles** = services + income + transfers; **invisible export = export of services**. Services = **non-factor** (shipping, banking, tourism, software) + factor income.
  - "Invisible items" in the classic sense = **service transactions**; goods are the visible items.
- **Capital account** (UPSC 2013): **foreign loans, FDI and portfolio investment** – **private remittances are current account** (so codes with remittances are wrong). **FDI is NOT current account.**
- **Current-account balance** = exports − imports of goods and services + net primary income + net secondary income.
# Credit and debit
- Every **inflow of foreign exchange = credit side**; every outflow = debit (double-entry book-keeping).
- **Gift to foreigners = debit** in the current account; gift from foreigners = credit; exports of goods/services = credit.
- Residents **buying foreign assets = capital-account debit**; **sale of Indian assets to foreigners = credit**.
| Item | BoP placement |
|---|---|
| Software services exports | Current account (services) |
| Foreign loans, FDI, FPI | Capital account |
| Private remittances | Current account – secondary income (transfers) |
| Crude oil imports | Merchandise trade |
# The identity
- **Current + Capital + Financial account + Net Errors & Omissions = 0** – BoP **always balances** in accounting terms; a surplus in one account = deficit in another. **BoT need not balance.**
- **Autonomous** transactions ("above the line") happen for their own sake; **accommodating** ones ("below the line") fill the gap – **official reserve changes, official borrowing**.
- Overall BoP **positive → adds to reserves; negative → draws them down**; monetary authority = ultimate financier.

=== Convertibility, FERA/FEMA and Rupee Milestones
| Type | Freedom to convert for |
|---|---|
| **Current account convertibility** | Trade, income and transfers (current international transactions) |
| **Capital account convertibility** | Investment and financial assets (FDI, portfolio, borrowing) without capital controls |
- The rupee is **fully convertible only on the current account** – **not** on the capital account (still partial) and **not into gold** (UPSC 2000).
- **IMF Article VIII** makes **current account convertibility** obligatory; India accepted it in **August 1994** (operationalised 19 Aug 1994).
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 1948 | Rupee linked to sterling till 1948 |
| 1973 | **FERA** (prevent outflow of foreign exchange) |
| **Sep 1975** | **Rupee delinked from pound sterling**, pegged to a basket of currencies |
| July 1991 | **Rupee devalued in two steps (1 and 3 July)** – about 22% – to meet the BoP crisis |
| 1992-93 Budget | **LERMS**: dual rate – **40% official, 60% market**; partial convertibility |
| 1 Mar 1993 | Unified, market-determined exchange rate (trade account convertibility) |
| **Aug 1994** | **Full current account convertibility** – Article VIII accepted |
| **1999 / 1 June 2000** | **FEMA** enacted / in force, replacing FERA |
- **FEMA preamble:** "to **facilitate external trade and payments**" and promote orderly development of the forex market – not import control.
| Feature | FERA 1973 | FEMA 1999 |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Criminal | **Civil** |
| Approach | Control of outflow | **Management and regulation** |
| Character | Strict, restrictive | Liberal |

| Committee | Chair | Key point |

=== Forex Reserves
| Component (RBI) | Note |
|---|---|
| **Foreign Currency Assets (FCA)** | **Largest** (most of the total) |
| **Gold** | Second |
| **SDRs** | IMF allocation; third |
| **Reserve Tranche Position (RTP) in the IMF** | Smallest; unconditional drawing right |
- Order of share (March 2025): **FCA > Gold > SDRs > RTP**. Loans from foreign countries or the World Bank, NRI deposits, "fiscal deficit deposits" are **NOT** reserves.
- **1991 low:** reserves fell to about **$1.2 bn (Jan 1991)**; FCA **below $1 bn**, about two weeks of imports.
- **Milestones:** crossed **US$ 100 bn in Dec 2003** ($110.48 bn on 19 Dec 2003); **$608.99 bn on 25 June 2021 – 5th largest** (after China, Japan, Switzerland, Russia; Ministry of Finance); **$640.3 bn (27 Dec 2024)**.
- **2026:** $701.4 bn (Jan 2026) ≈ **94% of external debt**; **$682.3 bn (29 May 2026) ≈ 11 months of import cover**; all-time high **$785.71 bn (15 Sep 2026)**, India **4th** after China, Japan, Switzerland; $721.0 bn (16 Oct 2026); FCA $597.6 bn (9 Oct 2026).
- **July 2026:** overall BoP surplus **$20.8 bn**, capital account $27.7 bn, driven by RBI's **special FCNR(B) deposit scheme** – possibly one-time comfort.
- **Import cover** = months of imports the reserves can pay for.

=== CAD, Invisibles and Remittances – Trends
- **CAD**: payments exceed receipts from **goods, services, transfers and net income** (excess of total imports over total exports). A CAD is **not** caused by exporting more.
- India runs a **persistent trade deficit** in goods; services and remittances only partly offset it.
| Period | CAD / current account |
|---|---|
| 1990-91 | 3.1-3.2% of GDP (crisis); IMF drawn in 1991 |
| **2001-02 to 2003-04** | **Surplus three years running** (first in 24 years in 2001-02) – the only such run |
| FY13 | Peak **4.8%** of GDP (taper tantrum) – higher than 1990-91 |
| 2013-14 | Trade deficit, **net invisibles positive**, **capital account in surplus** (financed the CAD) |
| **Q4 2023-24** | **Surplus $5.7 bn (0.6%)** – net **services** receipts $42.7 bn (software, travel, business services) |
| FY 2025-26 | **$25.2 bn (~0.6% of GDP)**; merchandise deficit $337.3 bn; Q3 FY26 $13.2 bn (1.3%) |
| Q1 2026-27 | **$4.2 bn, 0.5% of GDP** |
- **FY24 external recovery:** **moderation in merchandise export growth + sharp decline in import growth**; **services exports cushioned the trade deficit** (up 4.85% to **USD 341.06 bn**).
- **Causes of CAD:** structural – oil and gold imports, low export competitiveness, savings-investment gap; **cyclical – temporary global slowdown**, commodity shocks.
# Invisibles and remittances
- **Largest source of net invisibles among the classic items: private transfers = foreign remittances** (APPSC 2008); since the 2010s **net software services** are larger still – **record services surplus $188.8 bn (FY25)**, about **two-thirds** of the merchandise deficit; **software = largest part**.
- **India = world's largest remittance recipient** (also in **2015**): **$135.4 bn (3.5% of GDP) in FY25**; World Bank 2024: India $129 bn > **Mexico** $68 bn > China > Philippines > Pakistan.
- **Sources (RBI survey 2023-24): USA 27.7% > UAE 19.2% > UK 10.8%.**
- Remittances are **counter-cyclical** and create **no debt**; **net primary income is an outflow** for India.

=== Correcting BoP Disequilibrium
| Expenditure-switching | Expenditure-reducing / financing |
|---|---|
| **Devaluation**/depreciation | Tight fiscal policy: **cut government spending**, raise taxes |
| **Import duties (tariffs)**, quotas, import substitution | **Tight monetary policy**: higher interest rates |
| Export incentives, tourist promotion | Foreign loans, IMF assistance, **foreign investment (FDI)**; exchange control |
| Higher **gold import duty** | |
- **Not a correction:** **levying higher export duties** (cuts exports); **increasing** government spending on domestic projects (raises demand and imports).
- **Encouraging FDI** *finances* a deficit rather than correcting it – the "NOT typically used" answer when devaluation, tariffs and cutting government spending are the other options.
- **Removing a BoP surplus** (elastic demand): **appreciate/revalue** the currency; devaluation, import controls or inflation abroad would widen the surplus.
- A country with a **large current-account surplus** has a **larger capacity to give aid and loans** abroad.
- **1991:** rupee **devalued twice** (1 and 3 July 1991) to adjust the BoP deficit.
- **May 2026:** appeal to cut gold and fuel imports + gold import-duty hike = expenditure-switching steps.
- **Rekhi Committee (1992) = indirect taxes** (not export-import simplification); Rangarajan = BoP; Goiporia = bank customer service.

=== IMF and SDR
- **IMF main function:** help members solve **balance of payments problems**; loans meet **temporary BoP deficits** (not development projects – that is the World Bank); core function **surveillance**; **lender of last resort** when reserves cannot cover a deficit. India drew on the IMF in **1991**.
| Facility | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Reserve (gold) tranche** | Member's **unconditional drawing right** from its quota – a **credit system of the IMF** |
| **Rapid Financing Instrument (RFI)** | Rapid help for **any member** with an urgent BoP need |
| **Rapid Credit Facility (RCF)** | **Concessional**, for **low-income countries** |
| Stand-By Arrangement / **Extended Fund Facility** | Regular lending windows |
- RBI swaps, SAARC swap framework and **Special Rupee Vostro Accounts** are **NOT** IMF instruments.
- **SDR** = IMF reserve asset ("paper gold", 1969), exists only as **book-keeping entries**, not paper currency or metal.
- Original SDR rule (reconstitution, abolished 1981): members kept on average **30%** of allocations over five years – so about **70% could be used without repayment**.

| **US dollar** | **43.38%** |
| Euro | 29.31% |
| **Chinese renminbi** | **12.28%** |
| Japanese yen | 7.59% |
| Pound sterling | 7.44% |
- **Five** currencies; next review due by end-July 2027.

=== External Vulnerability and Debt
- **Immunity to a global financial crisis (UPSC 2020): not depending on short-term foreign borrowings** – only this; more foreign banks and full capital account convertibility raise exposure.
- **Reduce currency-crisis risk (UPSC 2019): IT-sector forex earnings and remittances** – higher government spending does not.
- Safeguards: **adequate reserves** (import cover), **low short-term debt**, less reliance on volatile **hot money** (FPI, debt flows). FCNR(B) and ECB inflows are **temporary**; **FDI** supports a self-sustaining capital surplus.
- **US Fed rate hikes + strong dollar** → higher US yields → hot money leaves emerging markets → rupee weakens.
- **2013 taper tantrum:** Morgan Stanley's "**Fragile Five**" included India. Morgan Stanley 1998 emerging-markets rating: India **3rd** (printed key).
- **FY26:** FPIs pulled out **$16.4 bn**; net FDI only **$6.9 bn**.

| Total | **$778.2 bn** |
| Debt-to-GDP | **20.8%** |
| Short-term debt | 19.7% of total |
| Largest currency | **US dollar 54.8%**, rupee 29.8% |
