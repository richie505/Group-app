=== Measuring Inflation – CPI, WPI and the Headline Index
# Who compiles what
| Index | Compiled by | Base year (as of 2026) | Main use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CPI (Rural / Urban / Combined)** | **NSO, MoSPI** (earlier CSO) | **2024** (replaced 2012; first release 12 Feb 2026 for Jan 2026) | **Headline inflation**; RBI's inflation target |
| **CPI-IW** (Industrial Workers) | **Labour Bureau**, Ministry of Labour and Employment | **2016** (from Sep 2020) | **Dearness allowance** of industrial workers and **Central Government employees** |
| CPI-AL / CPI-RL (Agricultural / Rural Labourers) | Labour Bureau | 2019 (from June 2025; earlier 1986-87) | Farm and rural labourers' wages |
| CPI-UNME (Urban Non-Manual Employees) | **CSO** (not the Labour Bureau) | 1984-85 | Discontinued from **January 2011** after CPI-Urban began |
| **WPI** | **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce and Industry** | **2022-23** (revised 2026; earlier 2011-12, 2004-05) | Wholesale prices; contract escalation |
| CFPI (Consumer Food Price Index) | NSO | Same as CPI | Food inflation |
| GDP deflator | NSO | National-accounts base | Widest measure – all goods and services produced |
- **CPI-IW base**: **1982 → 2001 → 2016** (w.e.f. September 2020).
- **Labour Bureau**: set up **1920**, at **Chandigarh**, under the **Ministry of Labour and Employment**.
- **Odd one out** among CPI-IW, CPI-AL, CPI-RL, CPI-UNME: **CPI-UNME** – the only one compiled by the **CSO** (UPSC 2010).
- **Dearness allowance (DA)** is fixed on the **Consumer Price Index – CPI-IW** (not national income or per capita income); linking pay to a price index is **indexation** ("fixing of wage compensation").
- **IIP** measures **industrial output, not prices** (NSO).
# Headline measure – from WPI to CPI
- WPI was India's **headline / "primary" / most common** inflation measure **till April 2014**.
- **April 2014**: RBI adopted **CPI (Combined)** as its key measure (Urjit Patel Committee); **headline inflation is now based on CPI-C**.
- India still publishes inflation on CPI, WPI and the labour CPIs, but the **policy (headline) measure is CPI-Combined**.
- Inflation rate = **year-on-year % change in CPI** (current month's CPI over the CPI of the same month last year).
# WPI
- Prices of goods traded in bulk (first point); **no services** – CPI covers goods **and services**.
- **2011-12 series**: **697 items**; **manufactured products = largest weight, 64.23%** (primary articles 22.62%, fuel and power 13.15%; 2004-05 series: manufactured 64.97%).
- **New WPI (base 2022-23, 2026)**: **957 items**; weights on **Gross Value of Output**; runs with a new **Producer Price Index (PPI)** for **5 years**, then WPI is phased out.
- **PPI** measures the **average change in prices received by producers** (output PPI: goods leaving production; input PPI: inputs entering it).
- WPI was released **weekly** (primary articles, fuel) until 2012, so "WPI only monthly" was wrong in 2010; WPI gives **less weight to food** than CPI-IW.
- WPI series on base **2004-05** was introduced on **14 September 2010**.
# CPI structure
- New CPI (base 2024): **12 divisions** (COICOP 2018), **358 items**; **food and beverages 36.75%** (about 46% in the 2012 series); 12 online platforms and rural house rent added.
- **Food has a bigger weight in CPI than in WPI** (UPSC 2020).

| Committee | Subject |
|---|---|
| **Abhijit Sen** (2007; report May 2008) | WPI revision – base **2004-05** |
| Saumitra Chaudhuri (2012) | WPI revision – base 2011-12 |

# GDP deflator
- **GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100**; the **broadest** measure (all goods and services produced).
- A **rise in the GDP/GNP deflator** = rising prices → **fall in the purchasing power of money**.
> Exam angle: "headline / most common measure" now = **CPI-Combined**; older keys (pre-2014) = WPI. DA = CPI-IW (Labour Bureau). WPI = Commerce Ministry, manufactured goods ~64%.

=== Causes and Types of Inflation by Cause
# By cause
| Type | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Demand-pull** | **Aggregate demand > aggregate supply**: expansionary fiscal and monetary policy, **fiscal stimulus**, higher purchasing power, rising money supply – "too much money chasing too few goods" |
| **Cost-push** | **Rise in prices of factors of production** (wages, raw materials, fuel) → **supply falls** while demand is constant |
| **Structural / bottleneck** | Deficiencies in the economy – poor farm productivity, **supply bottlenecks**, **inefficient distribution** and storage; persists long and is **hard for government to tackle** |
| Export-led | Strong export demand drains domestic supply |
- **Inflationary gap**: excess of aggregate demand (spending) over aggregate supply at **full-employment** output.
- Demand-pull is **not** caused by **inflation-indexing of wages** (cost side) or **rising interest rates** (they cut demand) – UPSC 2021: 1, 2 and 4 only.
- A **permanent fall in input costs** reduces cost-push pressure.
# Causes of price rise in India
| Side | Factors |
|---|---|
| Demand | **Increase in money supply**, rise in non-developmental public expenditure, **deficit financing / deficit budget**, loose monetary policy, rapid GDP growth, population growth |
| Supply | **Erratic / less agricultural production**, **hoarding**, higher MSPs, **rise in crude oil and fertiliser prices** (oil shocks), inefficient storage and marketing, rupee depreciation, cartelisation |
- General price rise may come from **more money supply**, **lower aggregate output** and **higher effective demand** (UPSC 2013: all three).
- **Food inflation** (UPSC 2011): changing **consumption pattern with rising incomes** and **structural constraints in the food supply chain** – not a 30% fall in foodgrain area.
- **Unstable currency** works **against** inflation management; stability, sound monetary policy and balanced supply-demand help.
# Crude oil benchmarks
- **Brent** (North Sea), **West Texas Intermediate (WTI)** (US) and **Dubai** – grades of **crude oil** used as price benchmarks; oil-price shocks feed cost-push inflation.

=== Meaning of Inflation and Its Kinds by Speed
# Definition
- **Inflation**: a sustained **rise in the general price level** and a **fall in the purchasing power (value) of money**; the essential attribute is **increase in prices**.
- During inflation prices **increase**; when all prices rise, the purchasing power of money **decreases**.
- A **rise in a commodity's price** = **fall in the value of currency and rise in the value of the commodity** (in money terms).
- Effects together: prices rise **and** the value of money falls (not "exchange rate improves").
- **Economic growth** is usually coupled with **(moderate) inflation** – rising income and demand push prices up.
# Related terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Deflation** | **Persistent fall in the general price level** (negative inflation) – not a currency fall or a fall in the inflation rate |
| **Disinflation** | **Fall in the rate of inflation**, still positive (e.g. 3% → 2%) |
| **Reflation** | Inflation returning after deflation – sign of recovery |
| **Stagflation** | High inflation + **slow growth / stagnant output** + high unemployment |
| **Skewflation** | Inflation in some items (e.g. food) while others are stable or falling |
- A **fall in the inflation rate** means only that **prices are rising more slowly** – not that prices fell, food supply rose or industry stagnated.
# Stagflation factors
- Inflationary pressure (e.g. from **higher government spending**) **plus** **declining industrial production and sluggish growth** → stagflation.
- A **fall in aggregate demand** and **currency appreciation** pull prices **down** – they slow growth but do not create the inflation half of stagflation.
# By speed
| Type | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Creeping** | Gradual, below about **3%** |
| **Walking / trotting** | **3-10%** |
| **Galloping** | Double or triple digit (20%, 100%, 200% a year) |
| **Hyperinflation** | Large and accelerating; prices shoot up overnight |

=== Controlling Inflation
# Tools
| Type | Measures |
|---|---|
| **Monetary (RBI)** | **Decrease money circulation**: raise repo, reverse repo, Bank Rate, CRR, SLR; **sell government securities (OMO)** – **reducing interest rates is not** an anti-inflation step |
| **Fiscal (Government)** | Cut non-developmental spending, avoid deficit financing, raise direct taxes, cut indirect taxes on essentials |
| **Supply / administrative** | Control demand, **rationing**, prevent hoarding and black marketing, ban exports of scarce goods, suspend futures trading, buffer stocks |
- Control needs a **combination of monetary, fiscal and exchange-rate policies** – monetary policy alone cannot fix supply-side inflation; controlling inflation is **not** the Government's job only – the **RBI** has the key role.
- **Dual price policy** (to control prices): **sugar** – a share of output (**levy sugar**) bought at a controlled price for the PDS, the rest sold in the open market (levy abolished 2013).
- **Price Stabilisation Fund (PSF)**: Central Sector Scheme, **2014-15 (launched March 2015)**; began with **onion and potato**; pulses added later.
- **Bharat Atta** (subsidised wheat flour) helped contain **wheat inflation**; **Bharat Dal** (chana dal, July 2023) was extended to **moong dal**; sold via NAFED, NCCF, Kendriya Bhandar (APPSC G1 2024: II and IV).
- OMSS (Domestic): FCI sells **wheat and rice**.
- **RBI's biggest concern** in its policy statements: **inflation**.
- Repo rate (as of 7 Oct 2026): **5.50%** (was 5.25% in Aug 2026); repo acts on demand, **cannot directly fix supply shocks** (onion, crude oil).
# Inflation-Indexed Bonds (IIBs)
- **Principal and/or interest linked to an inflation index** → protect investors from **uncertainty about inflation** (real return).
- Government can **reduce coupon rates** on its borrowing (no inflation-risk premium); interest and capital gains are **taxable** (UPSC 2022: A and B only).
- RBI issued IIBs in **2013**.
# AP measures
- **State-level Price Monitoring Committee** headed by the **Chief Secretary**; **DES monitors 8 essential commodities daily** (groundnut and palm oil added Feb 2025).

=== Effects of Inflation and Deflation
# Gainers and losers of inflation
| Gain | Lose |
|---|---|
| **Debtors (borrowers)** – repay money of lower real value | **Creditors (lenders)** – hurt most |
| Businessmen, producers, big farmers (profits rise faster than costs) | **Fixed-income groups** – pensioners, salary and wage earners |
| Holders of real assets, equity investors | **Bond / debenture holders**, savings-account holders |
| Exporters (if the rupee depreciates) | Consumers, the poor |
- Rule: **inflation benefits debtors**; it does **not** benefit bond-holders, consumers or fixed-income groups.
- Price-hike: businessmen gain and consumers must curtail needs (most keys: both true, R not the explanation; strictly not "all" businessmen gain).
- Other effects: falling saving (MPS falls), shift to gold, rising inequality, wage-price spiral, discourages exports; **food inflation is regressive** (the poor spend more of income on food); **menu costs** of re-pricing.
# Deflation
- Purchasing power of money **rises** → **fixed-income groups (pensioners, salary earners)** and **creditors gain**; **debtors lose** (heavier real debt).
- **Deflationary spiral**: postponed purchases → lower profits → higher unemployment → heavier real debt burden.

=== Headline, Core and Food Inflation
| Basis | Headline inflation | Core inflation |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | **Total** inflation, **including food and energy** (oil, gas) | Excludes volatile **food and fuel / energy** |
| Shows | Raw figure – what consumers feel | Underlying, long-run trend |
| Volatility | **More volatile** | More stable |
| Index | Change in **CPI-Combined** | CPI excluding food and fuel |
- **Headline** = rate of change of the CPI (basket of a typical family); **core** = CPI change after excluding volatile items such as food and fuel – both statements true (RAS 2023).
- Core does **not** include food and energy; headline does **not** exclude food (RAS 2024: only "headline is more volatile" is correct).
- Inflation that leaves out commodities with volatile prices = **core inflation**.
- **Food inflation**: measured by the **CFPI** (10 sub-groups; cereals carry the highest weight).

=== Index Numbers and the Business Cycle
- A **price index** is a **statistical device** to measure changes in the general price level.
- **Laspeyres** index: **base-year (initial-year) quantities** as weights; **Paasche**: current-year weights; **Fisher's ideal index** = geometric mean of the two.
- **Irving Fisher** is the economist most associated with **index numbers** (ideal index, tests of index numbers).
- **Business cycle** phases: **expansion / recovery** (real GDP rising) → **peak / boom** → **recession** (falling) → **trough / depression** (bottom).

=== Inflation Targeting and the Phillips Curve
| Step | Year | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Urjit Patel Committee | 2014 | CPI as nominal anchor |
| Monetary Policy Framework Agreement | Feb 2015 | Led to RBI Act amendment |
| RBI Act 1934 amended | 2016 | Statutory flexible inflation targeting; **Sec 45ZA** target set by Centre with RBI every 5 years; **Sec 45ZB** six-member MPC; **Sec 45ZN** report after **3 consecutive quarters** of breach (first invoked 2022) |
- **Target (1 Apr 2026 – 31 Mar 2031)**: **4% CPI-Combined ± 2% (2-6%)**; 6% is only the upper limit.
- Average CPI fell from 6.8% (2012-16) to about 4.9% under the framework.
- **Phillips curve** (A.W. Phillips, 1958): **inverse relation between unemployment and (wage) inflation**; Friedman: long-run curve **vertical**; India's curve is flat (about 92% informal workers).
