=== Establishment, Nationalisation and Headquarters
# Origin
- **Hilton Young Commission** = **Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance (1926)**: recommended a central bank; the RBI was set up **on its recommendation** (not Narasimham, Gorwala or Malhotra, which came later).
- **B.R. Ambedkar's 1925** evidence to this Commission shaped the idea of the RBI.
- Earlier, the **Royal Commission on Indian Finance and Currency (1913)** was chaired by **Austen Chamberlain** (Chamberlain Commission) – not Keynes, who was only a member.
# Timeline
| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Hilton Young Commission recommends a central bank | **1926** |
| **RBI Act** passed | **1934** |
| RBI **established / starts functioning** (shareholders' bank, Central Office Calcutta) | **1 April 1935** |
| Central Office moves Calcutta → **Bombay** (permanent) | **1937** |
| **Nationalised** – fully State-owned, under RBI (Transfer to Public Ownership) Act, **1948** | **1 January 1949** |
| RBI **Hyderabad office** becomes operational | **1956** |
| RBI@90 | 1 April 2024 |
- Act **1934** → established **1935** → nationalised **1949**. "Established in 1949" is wrong; "nationalised on 26 January 1950" is wrong (UPSC 2004); "nationalised in 1937" is wrong (1937 = move to Bombay).
- **Headquarters: Mumbai** (Mint Street) since 1937; it is a **statutory body** and the **central bank** of India.
- **Seal**: **tiger and palm tree** – the tiger replaced the **lion** of the East India Company double mohur.

=== Organisation and Governors
# Governance
- **Central Board of Directors**: **21** members, appointed by the Government of India under the RBI Act; directors hold office for **4 years**.
- **Governor and Deputy Governors**: appointed (nominated) by the **Central Government**; tenure **not more than 5 years**. The head of the RBI is called the **Governor**; the Governor draws power from the **RBI Act**.
- **Four Deputy Governors**: two from within RBI + one commercial banker + one **economist** (heads monetary policy). **Rohit Jain** appointed May 2026 for 3 years, replacing T. Rabi Sankar.
- **Local Boards: four** – Mumbai (West), Kolkata (East), Chennai (South), New Delhi (North); members appointed by the Central Government for 4 years; each sends one director to the Central Board (not functioning since 2022 for lack of quorum). Hyderabad has none.
- Offices at 31 locations; **AP regional office** began 22 Aug 2022 (working from Hyderabad), moved to **Vijayawada** in 2025; AP currency work stays with the **Hyderabad** office.

=== Central Banking Functions – Banker to Government and Banker's Bank
# The central bank
- The **RBI performs the central banking functions** in India; it is the **Central Bank** (not SBI, NABARD, SBH or Central Bank of India, which are banks/DFIs). It is the regulator of the entire banking system.
| Function | Legal basis / content |
|---|---|
| Bank of issue | Sec 22: sole right to issue notes except the ₹1 note |
| **Banker, agent and adviser to Government** | **Sec 20** (Centre), **Sec 21/21A** (States, by agreement); payments, receipts, **public debt management**, **borrowing programme**, Ways and Means Advances, overdrafts |
| **Banker's bank** | Holds SCBs' deposits and reserves (CRR); **lender of last resort**; guides and advises banks |
| Monetary authority | Price stability with growth in mind; MPC sets the repo rate |
| **Custodian of forex** | Manages forex reserves under **FEMA 1999**; stabilises the rupee's external value |
| Controller of credit | Controls money and credit (quantitative and qualitative measures) |
| Payments | Regulates and upgrades payment systems (PSS Act 2007) |
| Regulator | Banks, NBFCs (Chapter IIIB), AIFIs, HFCs |
# Banker to Government
- Manages **public debt of Centre and States** and handles the **Government of India's borrowing programme** (not the Department of Expenditure – UPSC 2004); maintains Government accounts; **Public Debt Office** functions under it.
- Banker to **all State Governments except Sikkim** (Sikkim: limited agreement, public debt only).
- **Fiscal agent and adviser** to Government in monetary and financial matters; **represents India at the IMF** (Government's agent for India's IMF membership) and World Bank.
- **UPSC 2001:** banker to Centre, formulates monetary policy, agent for IMF membership, handles GoI borrowing – **all four correct**.
# Banker's bank and lender of last resort
- **Banker's bank** (IAS 2012): (i) other banks **keep deposits/reserves with the RBI**, (ii) the RBI **lends to banks in need**, (iii) it **advises banks on monetary matters** – **all three**.
- **Lender of last resort (LoLR)** – exclusive RBI function: **emergency liquidity to banks facing a temporary crisis** (UPSC 2021: **2 only**); it is **not** lending to trade/industry or financing budget deficits.
- **Bank Rate** = rate at which the **RBI lends to / rediscounts bills of commercial banks** (also called the discount rate) – not the rate banks charge borrowers or pay depositors.
- **FEMA A-R (APPSC):** RBI administers FEMA 1999 "to facilitate external trade and payments and promote orderly development of the forex market"; it also upgrades **payment systems** – both true, **R does not explain A**.

=== Monetary Authority, Credit Control and What RBI Does Not Do
# Monetary policy and credit control
- **Monetary (and credit) policy is formulated and implemented by the RBI** (MPC since 2016; six members) – the RBI **regulates and announces the repo and reverse repo rates**, not the Finance Ministry, PM, President, CAG or SBI.
- **Price stability / controlling inflation** = **RBI** (flexible inflation targeting; target set by the **Finance Ministry in consultation with the RBI**) – not the FSDC, Dept of Consumer Affairs or Expenditure Management Commission.
- RBI **controls "money and credit"** and **controls credit creation by commercial banks** through quantitative (Bank Rate, repo, CRR, SLR, OMO) and qualitative (selective credit control, margins, moral suasion) tools.
- Other than market forces, the **RBI intervenes (buys/sells dollars) to manage the exchange rate**.
- **A-R (APPSC 2024):** institutions ensure stability through monetary policy **because** central banks use repo and CRR to manage liquidity and inflation – **R explains A**.
- **Regulating and controlling banks** = an activity of the **RBI** (powers under the **Banking Regulation Act 1949**).
- 2016 framework (MPC + inflation-targeting law) gave **operational independence** on policy rates.
# What the RBI does NOT do
- **Credit creation** – done by **commercial banks**; the RBI only **controls** it.
- **Regulation of foreign trade** (Commerce Ministry/DGFT) – the RBI regulates **currency, credit and forex reserves**.
- **Fiscal policy** – formulated by the **Ministry of Finance** (not RBI, Finance Commission or Planning Commission).
- **Granting corporate deeds**; lending to the public; minting coins; issuing the ₹1 note; income-tax collection.
- **Savings bank interest rate** in nationalised banks: fixed by the **RBI until it was deregulated on 25 Oct 2011** – now each bank fixes it; never the Finance Ministry, Finance Commission or IBA (UPSC 2010: **None of the above**).
- Does not regulate securities (SEBI), insurance (IRDAI) or pensions (PFRDA).

=== Currency Management – Note Issue, Reserve System and Demonetisation
# Who issues what
| Item | Issued by | Signed by | Legal tender |
|---|---|---|---|
| **₹1 note** | **Government of India (Ministry of Finance)** | **Finance Secretary** | Unlimited |
| All other notes (₹2 upward; ₹10 and above in the new series) | **RBI** (Sec 22, sole right, on behalf of the Centre) | **RBI Governor** | Unlimited |
| **Coins** | **Government of India** (minted by GoI; RBI only distributes them as GoI's agent) | – | **Limited** (up to ₹1,000, Coinage Act) |
- So "RBI issues **all** types of currency notes" is **wrong**; the ₹100 note carries the **Governor's** signature.
- RBI can issue notes up to **₹10,000** (Sec 24); first ₹10,000 note 1938, demonetised 1946, re-introduced 1954, demonetised 1978.
# Reserve system for note issue
| System | Rule |
|---|---|
| Proportional Reserve System (till 1956) | 2/5 of assets in gold or sterling securities; gold at least ₹40 crore |
| Minimum Reserve System (1956) | ₹515 crore = ₹400 crore foreign securities + ₹115 crore gold |
| **Amended 1957 (present)** | **₹200 crore of gold and forex, of which at least ₹115 crore in gold** |
- India's note issue is based on the **Minimum Reserve System**; beyond the floor the RBI can issue currency **to any extent**.
- Notes are issued against **gold coin and bullion, foreign securities, rupee coin and GoI securities** (Sec 33) – "all of the above".
# Demonetisation
| Year | Notes withdrawn |
|---|---|
| 1946 | High-denomination notes (before Independence) |
| **16 Jan 1978** | ₹1,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000 – **first after Independence** (HDBN Demonetisation Act; Morarji Desai) |
| **8 Nov 2016 (from midnight)** | ₹500 and ₹1,000 (Mahatma Gandhi series); SC upheld 4:1 (2023) |
- Power: **Sec 26(2) of the RBI Act** – the Government notifies **on the recommendation of the RBI Central Board** (the 2016 notification followed the Board's recommendation).
- **Specified Bank Notes (Cessation of Liabilities) Act, 2017**: holding old ₹500/₹1,000 notes above the limit → fine of **₹10,000** or five times the face value, whichever is higher.
- **₹2,000** note: issued Nov 2016, withdrawn from circulation **May 2023**, still legal tender.
- Printing cost of a new ₹500 note: about **₹2.87–3.09** (2016-17) → nearest **₹3**.

=== Regulatory Role, Committees and Publications
# Regulation of banks
- **Banking Regulation Act, 1949** gave the **RBI** extensive powers: licensing, branches, management, amalgamation, inspection, audit.
- **UPSC 2013:** the RBI regulates banks in **liquidity of assets, branch expansion, merger and winding-up** – **all four**.
| Act / Section | Provision |
|---|---|
| BR Act Sec 22(1) | Licensing of banks, incl. payments banks |
| BR Act Sec 26A | **DEA Fund** (2014): deposits unclaimed **10+ years**; depositor's right survives |
| RBI Act Chapter IIIB | NBFC regulation |
| PSS Act 2007 | Payment systems |
- No bank or NBFC may open without an **RBI licence**; **mergers approved by the RBI**, which can seek the **winding up** of a bank.
- **Branch expansion**: banks, SFBs, payments banks, LABs, RRBs may open branches without prior approval if **25%** of new branches are in **unbanked rural** areas.

=== RBI Autonomy, Income and Developmental Role
# Key sections of the RBI Act
| Section | Provision |
|---|---|
| **7** | **Centre may give directions in public interest after consulting the Governor** (a power in the **RBI Act**, not the Constitution) |
| 22 | Sole right of note issue |
| 24 | Highest denomination ₹10,000 |
| 26(2) | Demonetisation |
| **30** | Centre can **supersede the Central Board** |
| **47** | **Net profit after provisions → Central Government** (surplus transfer) |
- **UPSC 2021:** Governor appointed by the Centre ✓; directions power is in the **RBI Act**, not "provisions in the Constitution" ✗; Governor draws power from the RBI Act ✓ → **1 and 3 only**.
- **Finance Commission** is formed by the President under **Article 280** (not an RBI body).
# Income and surplus
- Income: **interest on GoI securities and profit from buying/selling Government bonds (OMO)**, **interest and gains on foreign currency assets (buying/selling forex)**, interest on loans to Governments and banks, fees/commission.
- **Not income:** printing and distributing notes (a **cost** – currency printing is an expense), pension fund management (PFRDA), lending to private companies, income tax → **UPSC 2025: 1 and 2 only** (official key).
- **Bimal Jalan Committee** (Economic Capital Framework, 2018-19): separated **realised equity from revaluation balances**; **Contingent Risk Buffer 5.5–6.5%** → revised **4.5–7.5%** (2025).
- Surplus **FY 2025-26: record ₹2.86 lakh crore**, 6.7% above FY25.
# Developmental role
- RBI helped set up **IDBI, NABARD, SIDBI, EXIM Bank and NHB** (developmental role; ownership later moved to the Government – RBI sold its NABARD stake to GoI). But the **EXIM Bank** (Jan 1982) was **established by the Government of India** under the EXIM Bank Act, 1981 – "who established EXIM Bank?" → **GoI**, not RBI.
- SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA are separate regulators, not RBI creations.
