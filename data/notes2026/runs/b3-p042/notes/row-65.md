=== Disinvestment – Meaning, Methods and Strategic Sales
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Disinvestment** | Sale of government equity in PSEs – reduces equity; control may stay |
| Privatisation | Ownership + management control to the private sector |
| **Strategic sale** | A block of shares (usually **50% or more**) **with transfer of management control** to a strategic partner |
| Minority disinvestment | Govt keeps **>51%** and control |
| Liberalisation | Removing licences, quotas, controls (no sale) |
| Complete privatisation | 100% sold – most comprehensive mode |
- **Methods:** cross-holding, **warehousing**, minority sale, **OFS**, IPO/FPO, **CPSE ETF**, Bharat 22, buyback, **strategic sale** – methods of **disinvestment** (SLR, CRR, repo are NOT).
- **Policy recast (1999-2000):** shift from token minority sales to **strategic sale** – **majority holding (51%+) sold to a strategic private partner with change in management**.
- **Rationale:** poor PSU returns, **fiscal deficit**, efficiency, listing; disinvestment is an **integral part of liberalisation**; it reduces – never raises – government equity.
- **Why disinvest (UPSC 2011):** proceeds are **not** mainly for repaying external debt, and government does **not** give up control in every CPSE – so neither statement holds.
- **A-R (UPPCS 2000):** disinvestment is true; "PSUs have not generated enough employment" is **false** as a reason.
- **Criticism:** **"selling the family silver"** – proceeds bridge the **fiscal deficit** instead of building assets; **undervaluation**; lost dividends.

=== Competition Act, 2002 and the CCI
- **Competition Act, 2002:** recommended by the **S.V.S. Raghavan Committee (2000)**; replaced the MRTP Act; **commenced 31 March 2003**.
- **CCI** established **14 October 2003**; Chairperson + **2-6 members**; **Ministry of Corporate Affairs**.
- Fully operational: anti-competitive provisions from **20 May 2009**; merger (combination) control from **June 2011**.
| Chairperson | Term |
|---|---|
| Dhanendra Kumar (first) | 2009 – June 2011 |
| **Ashok Chawla** | **Oct 2011 – Jan 2016** |
| **Ravneet Kaur** | from **May 2023** |
- **Key sections of the Act:**

| Section | Covers |
|---|---|
| 3 | **Anti-competitive agreements** – cartels, bid-rigging, price-fixing |
| 4 | **Abuse of dominance** |
| 5-6 | Combinations – M&A above thresholds; **deemed approved** if CCI misses the deadline |
- **NCLAT** hears appeals against CCI orders (since 26 May 2017).
- Cases: **Jet Airways, IndiGo, SpiceJet** fined (Nov 2015) for cartelisation in the cargo **fuel surcharge**; **Google ₹1,337 crore (2022)**; **Meta/WhatsApp ₹213.14 crore (2024)**.
- **2024:** CCI engaged **Management Development Institute Society** for a market study on **AI and competition**.
- **M&A (UPPCS 2004 A-R):** the M&A wave after 1991 was **explained by dilution of MRTP provisions**.

=== Market Structures – the Economics behind Competition Law
| Structure | Feature |
|---|---|
| Perfect competition | Many sellers, homogeneous product |
| **Monopolistic competition** | **Many sellers, differentiated but close-substitute products** |
| **Oligopoly** | **A few large firms** |
| Duopoly | Two sellers |
| **Monopoly** | One seller – **the firm's demand curve is the market demand curve** |
| **Bilateral monopoly** | **One seller and one buyer** |
- **Prisoner's dilemma:** each player is **better off confessing** whatever the other does (dominant strategy) – the logic of cartels breaking down.

=== Regulatory Acts – Licensing and MRTP
| Act | Year | Replaced by |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum Wages Act | 1948 | – |
| **IDRA** | **1951** (amended **1953**) | Licensing; Development Councils |
| **MRTP** | **1969** (in force 1970) | **Competition Act, 2002** |
| **FERA** | **1973** | **FEMA, 1999** |
| SICA (BIFR) | 1985 | **IBC, 2016** |
- Order: **Minimum Wages 1948 → IDRA 1951 → MRTP 1969 → FERA 1973** (codes 4, 2, 1, 3); then SICA 1985 → FEMA 1999 → Competition Act 2002 → IBC 2016.
- **Hazari Report (1967)** and **Dutt Committee (1969)** showed licences favoured **large business houses**; Dutt recommended the **MRTP Act**.
- **MRTP purpose:** control **private monopolies** and **concentration of economic power**.
| Year | MRTP threshold |
|---|---|
| Original | Assets **₹20 crore**+ or 33% market share |
| 1984-85 | Share 25%; assets **₹100 crore** |
| NIP 1991 | **Asset limit scrapped** |
- Compulsory licensing **18 (1991) → 4 now**; leather delicensed **1993**. After 1991, **APIIC** turned **facilitator**.

=== New PSE Policy 2021, Monetisation and Restructuring
- **New PSE Policy for Atmanirbhar Bharat (2021):** **minimise the Government's presence** in PSEs; **4 strategic sectors** (incl. banking, insurance, financial services) with **1-4 CPSEs** each; non-strategic → privatise, merge or close.
- ES 2025-26: stake in listed CPSEs may fall to **26%**.
- **Asset monetisation:** **leases** brownfield assets for a fixed period, ownership stays (disinvestment **sells** equity); objective – **unlock value of existing assets through new financial structures**.
- **NMP 1.0:** Aug 2021, **₹6 lakh crore** (FY22-25); **NMP 2.0:** **23 Feb 2026**, **₹16.72 lakh crore** (FY26-30).
- **NLMC** (2022, under **DPE**): monetises **surplus land of CPSEs**, incl. loss-making units.
- AP PPP ports: **Krishnapatnam, Gangavaram**.
- **NRF (1992):** VRS and a safety net for workers.
- **IBC** enacted **28 May 2016** (T.K. Viswanathan BLRC); BIFR dissolved **1 Dec 2016**; **NCLT** under **s.408** Companies Act 2013.
