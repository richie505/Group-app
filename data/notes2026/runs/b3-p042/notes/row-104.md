=== Meaning and Purpose of Deficit Financing
# Meaning
| Usage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Indian | Budget gap met by **borrowing from the RBI**; RBI creates new money – the government **"buys money" from the RBI** |
| Western | Any financing of a budget deficit, mainly **borrowing from the public/market** |
| Effect | **Increase in money supply**; creates **extra paper currency** |
- **Deficit financing** = the act of financing a **deficit budget** – **spending in excess of revenue** (total expenditure > total receipts) and sustaining that gap (APPSC G1 1999).
- In India, deficit financing = borrowing from the **RBI** (money creation), not from the public (APPSC AD Economics).
- **Fiscal deficit** (Union Budget) = budgetary deficit + net increase in internal and external borrowings and other liabilities.
- An economic unit that **spends more than it receives** in a period is a **deficit unit** (APPSC Degree Lecturers 2017).
# Purpose in Indian Planning
- Purpose: **raising resources for economic development** – not redemption of public debt, BoP adjustment or cutting foreign debt (UPSC 2013 ×2); a "pragmatic tool" to finance the **Five Year Plans**.
- **Small doses** of deficit financing for productive investment need not be inflationary, and it is an **often-used tool** for financing budget deficits (CDS 2011: both true).
- Needed after 1947 because of a **low tax base**, poor compliance, weak private banking and **low domestic savings** (not a high savings rate).
- NextGen objectives: remedy for depression, raise aggregate demand, subsidies, anti-poverty programmes, PSU losses.
- Independence to **late 1960s**: little use, to fund plans. **Second Plan**: about **₹1,100 crore** against ₹1,200 crore planned. **Third Plan**: reliance to be **strictly limited**.
- **1970s onwards** (NextGen: "period of deficit financing 1970-1991"): heavy reliance (oil shocks, welfare spending) led to debt and inflation.
> Exam angle: "In India deficit financing is used for" = **economic development**.

=== Methods and Their Inflationary Effect
# Methods (NextGen)
- Running down **cash balances** with the RBI; **issue of new currency**; **borrowing from the RBI** (RBI prints notes); also internal and external borrowing, external aid and grants.
- Ramesh Singh's order of preference: external aid/grants → external borrowing → internal borrowing → **printing currency (last resort, most inflationary)**.
- **Monetised deficit**: borrowing from the RBI through fresh currency (**high-powered money**); the FRBM Act disallows it in normal times.
# Inflationary Ranking
| Mode | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Creation of new money** | **Most inflationary** (UPSC 2013, 2021; State PSC) |
| Borrowing from banks | High |
| Borrowing from public | Lower – uses existing savings |
| Repayment of public debt | **Not inflationary** – withdraws money |
- Deficit financing is **inflationary in character** (APPSC G1); it **increases the money supply** (UPPSC 2016); if it fails to raise output it **generates inflation**, not deflation (UPPSC 1993).
# Control and Phase-out
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| Mid-1950s | **Ad hoc Treasury Bills** – automatic RBI financing of the government begins |
| **Sep 1994** | RBI-Government agreement begins phasing out ad hoc T-bills (₹6,000 crore cap for 1994-95) |
| **31 Mar 1997** | Ad hoc T-bills abolished; **Ways and Means Advances (WMA)** from **1 April 1997** |
| 2003 | FRBM Act |
| **1 Apr 2006** | RBI barred from subscribing to **primary issues** of G-Secs |
| 2018 | Monetised borrowing only on **escape-clause** grounds; deviation cap **0.5% of GDP** a year |
- Market borrowing; liquidity is managed by **OMOs**: **sale** of G-Secs **drains** liquidity, **purchase adds** it.
- **15 Sep 2026**: RBI announced **OMO sales of ₹1 lakh crore** with 91/182/364-day T-bill auctions.

=== Consequences – Inflation, Debt Trap, Crowding Out
| Effect | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Demand-pull inflation | Money supply and demand rise **without more goods and services**; hurts the poor most |
| Crowding out | Market borrowing raises interest rates, cuts **private** investment |
| **Debt trap** | Government **borrows to make interest payments** (APPSC G2); little room for health, education |
| Others (NextGen) | Adverse effect on savings and investment, rising inequality, BoP deficit, higher cost of production |
| Rating downgrades | Higher borrowing cost for government and firms |
# Public Debt Heads
| Head | Items |
|---|---|
| Internal debt | Market loans, Treasury Bills, special securities to NSSF |
| Other liabilities (Public Account) | Small savings, **State Provident Funds**, reserve funds and deposits |
| External debt | Loans from foreign sources |
# Current Data
- Interest = **20 paise** of every rupee spent (Budget 2026-27); about **40% of revenue receipts**.
- States borrowed **₹5.23 trillion** till Oct 2025 (**62%** of FY26 plan); RBI wants a shift from **subsidies to capital expenditure**.
- **AP** fiscal deficit 2024-25: **5.05%** of GSDP vs 4% limit; **market loans ≈ 75% of AP's debt** – after bifurcation AP met its heavy deficit mainly through **market loans** (APPSC AD Economics).
