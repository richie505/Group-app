=== Landmark Judgments – Article 21 and Religion
# Harish Rana – passive euthanasia
- SC **reserved judgment (January 2026)** in **Harish Rana v. Union of India** – plea to withdraw life support (13 years in a vegetative state).
- Right to die with dignity is part of **Article 21**; active euthanasia illegal, passive legal under guidelines.
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **Maruti Dubal (1987)** | **A High Court (Bombay) first recognised a right to die** |
| **Gian Kaur (1996)** | **Right to die rejected** |
| Aruna Shanbaug (2011) | Passive euthanasia permitted |
| Common Cause (2018) | Living wills recognised |
- **2023 simplification:** primary and secondary boards; doctors' experience cut **20 → 5 years**.
- BNS 2023: Sec 100 culpable homicide, **Sec 101 murder**, Sec 108 abetment of suicide.
# Menstrual health – Dr Jay Thakur v Union of India
- Menstrual health part of **Article 21**; **free biodegradable sanitary pads for girls in Classes 6–12**; provision is **a constitutional responsibility, not charity**.
# Other rulings

- **Delhi HC (6 January 2026)**: under the MTP Act the **woman's consent suffices; husband's consent not required**.
- **Swaraj Abhiyan v Union of India (May 2016)** – Bench of **Justices Madan B. Lokur and N.V. Ramana**; Centre to set up the **National Disaster Mitigation Fund** (DM Act 2005).

=== SHANTI Act, 2025 – Nuclear Sector Opened
# The Act (Winter Session, December 2025)
- **Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India (SHANTI) Act, 2025**.
- **Replaces** the **Atomic Energy Act, 1962** and the **Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage (CLND) Act, 2010**.
- **Ends the NPCIL monopoly** – private and foreign firms may **build, own, operate** plants and **manufacture equipment**.
- **Kept for the Centre:** enrichment beyond the prescribed limit, **spent fuel management**, heavy water, reprocessing.
- **AERB** (set up **1983**) gets **statutory status**, accountable to Parliament.
- **Section 39:** Centre may declare nuclear information "restricted" – overrides the **RTI Act**.
- Appeals go to the **Appellate Tribunal for Electricity**; Nuclear Damage Claims Commission handles claims.
| Item | SHANTI Act |
|---|---|
| Liability cap | **300 million SDR** (~₹3,900 crore); operator ₹100–3,000 crore by reactor size |
| Supplier liability | **Eliminated** (no right of recourse) |
| Above the cap | **Nuclear Liability Fund** |
# Background and targets
- The **Atomic Energy Act, 1962 replaced the 1948 Act** (not the reverse).
- **IAEA safeguards** apply to reactors using **imported uranium**.
| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Target 2032 | **22 GW** |
| Target 2047 | **100 GW** |
| Nuclear Energy Mission (Budget 2025–26) | **₹20,000 crore** |

=== Judiciary – Appointments, NJAC and Reforms
# Judges cases and NJAC
| Case | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **First Judges (1981)** | **Executive primacy** |
| **Second Judges (1993)** | **Created the collegium** |
| Third Judges (1998) | Collegium = **CJI + 4** senior-most judges |
| **Fourth Judges / NJAC (2015)** | **99th Amendment, 2014** struck down – violated the basic structure |
- **NJAC** – **six members**: CJI (**Chairperson**, not the Law Minister), two senior-most SC judges, Union Law Minister, two eminent persons; **any two could veto** a name.
- **Collegium is not in the Constitution** – it grew out of the Three Judges Cases.
- Judicial independence part of the basic structure – **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**.
- CJI **Surya Kant** called for a **National Judicial Policy** to **reduce divergence across courts**.
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| 124 | Appointment of SC judges |
| 126 | Acting CJI |
| **127** | **Ad hoc SC judges** |
| 128 | Retired judges in the SC |
| **130** | Seat of the SC – elsewhere by the **CJI** with the President's approval |
| 217 | Appointment of HC judges |
| **233** | **District judges** |

- Moved by **P. Wilson** (DMK, Rajya Sabha); regional benches at **New Delhi, Kolkata, Mumbai, Chennai** (not Hyderabad); constitutional matters stay in Delhi; SC pendency 90,000+.

=== Parliamentary Privileges
- **News:** breach-of-privilege notice against eight Congress MPs over disruption during the VB-G RAM G Bill debate; LS **Privileges and Ethics Committees not constituted for nearly two years**.
- Articles **105, 122** (Parliament); **194, 212** (States).
- Privileges extend to the **Attorney General** – **not the President**.
- **Art 105(3):** until defined by law, privileges = **British House of Commons as on 26 January 1950**; **no defining law has been enacted**.
- **Freedom from civil arrest:** during session and **40 days before and after**.
- **44th Amendment, 1978:** protects fair press reporting of proceedings (except secret sittings).
- **Committee of Privileges:** **15** members (Lok Sabha), **10** (Rajya Sabha) – **semi-judicial, not judicial**.
- Rajya Sabha maximum **250**; RS Chairman – VP **C.P. Radhakrishnan**.
| Case | Year | Held |
