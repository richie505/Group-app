=== NOTES 1:18 | Later Vedic Period – Eastward Expansion, Iron (krishna Ayas), Painted Grey Ware
## SUB 0 | Eastward Expansion
[18:0:0] # Course of Expansion
[18:0:1] - Order: **Punjab → Ganga-Yamuna Doab** (Kurukshetra, Delhi-upper Doab) → **Kosala** (eastern UP), **Kashi**, **Videha** (north Bihar).
[18:0:2] - Reached Kosala, Kashi and Videha by about **600 BCE**.
[18:0:3] - **Kuru-Panchala** (Delhi-Meerut region; capital **Hastinapur**, Meerut district) = **centre of Aryan culture**, with gains in science, mathematics, astronomy, religion and philosophy.
[18:0:4] - **Kurus** = union of the **Bharatas and Purus**.
[18:0:5] # Videgha Madhava Legend
[18:0:6] - Told in the **Shatapatha (Satapatha) Brahmana**.
[18:0:7] - **Videgha Madhava** and his priest **Gautama Rahugana** followed **Agni Vaishvanara** as it burnt forests eastward from the **Sarasvati**.
[18:0:8] - Crossed the **Sadanira (Gandak)**; the land beyond was settled as **Videha**.
[18:0:9] - Shows the role of **iron axes and fire** in clearing the Ganga forests.
[18:0:10] # Upanishadic (Kshatriya Scholar) Kings
[18:0:11] TABLE head: King | Kingdom
  [18:0:11.0] Janaka | Videha
  [18:0:11.1] Ashvapati | Kekaya
  [18:0:11.2] Ajatashatru | Kashi
  [18:0:11.3] Pravahana Jaivali | Panchala
[18:0:12] - These kings **taught Brahmins**.
[18:0:13] Ashvapati = Kekaya, not Kashi; Ajatashatru of the Upanishads = Kashi (not the Magadha king).
## SUB 1 | Iron – Terms, Dates and Sites
[18:1:0] # Vedic Metal Terms
[18:1:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [18:1:1.0] Ayas (Rigveda) | Copper or bronze
  [18:1:1.1] Krishna ayas / Shyama ayas ("black metal") | Iron (Later Vedic texts)
  [18:1:1.2] Lohita ayas | Copper
  [18:1:1.3] Hiranya | Gold
  [18:1:1.4] Nishka | Gold ornament; later a unit of value
[18:1:2] # Iron in North India
[18:1:3] - Iron technology about **1000-800 BCE**; common at most **PGW sites**; smiths and smelters important after 1000 BCE.
[18:1:4] - Sites: **Atranjikhera, Hastinapur, Kaushambi, Taxila** (Gandhara, c. 1000 BC).
[18:1:5] - Iron axes + fire cleared Gangetic forests; iron weapons + horse-drawn chariots helped the eastward push.
[18:1:6] # Earliest Iron – Tamil Nadu Dates
[18:1:7] TABLE head: Site | District (TN) | Finding
  [18:1:7.0] Sivagalai | Thoothukudi | Iron dated to about 2953 BCE (2025) – now the oldest
  [18:1:7.1] Mayiladumparai | Krishnagiri | Iron AMS-dated to 2172 BCE; the earliest claim before Sivagalai
  [18:1:7.2] Thirumalapuram | Tenkasi | Iron Age urn chamber
  [18:1:7.3] Adichanallur | Thoothukudi | Urn burials; not a PGW site
[18:1:8] - South Indian Iron Age now placed around **2100 BCE or earlier**, before the Gangetic Iron Age (Jun 2026).
[18:1:9] - These dates push iron use far earlier than 1000 BCE; they say **nothing about where PGW began**.
[18:1:10] - **Earliest metal:** **Copper** was the **first metal** used in India, in the **Chalcolithic** (copper-stone) age; the name comes from Greek *khalkos* (copper) + *lithos* (stone), as copper and stone tools were used together. Iron came later.
## SUB 2 | Pottery Sequence of Indian Protohistory
[18:2:0] TABLE head: Order | Pottery | Period
  [18:2:0.0] 1 | Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) | Post-Harappan, 2nd millennium BCE
  [18:2:0.1] 2 | Black and Red Ware (BRW) | Chalcolithic onwards; also Later Vedic; Megalithic south
  [18:2:0.2] 3 | Black Slipped Ware (BSW) | Later Vedic
  [18:2:0.3] 4 | Painted Grey Ware (PGW) | Later Vedic, 1000-600 BC, Gangetic plain
  [18:2:0.4] 5 | Red Ware | Later Vedic
  [18:2:0.5] 6 | Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) | From about 600 BCE; Mahajanapadas, second urbanisation
[18:2:1] - OCP named at **Hastinapur by B.B. Lal (1950-52)**; leaves an **ochre colour on the fingers** when rubbed.
[18:2:2] - Four Later Vedic wares: **BRW, BSW, PGW, Red Ware**. NBPW is not in this group. ⚠ APPSC key (2024) gave only **BSW, PGW and Red Ware**.
[18:2:3] Later Vedic wares exclude NBPW (APPSC G1 2024). Shiny black pottery = NBPW, not PGW.
## SUB 3 | Painted Grey Ware Culture
[18:3:0] # Features
[18:3:1] - Grey pottery with **linear painted designs**; mostly **bowls and dishes**; c. 1000-600 BCE; probably used by the upper strata.
[18:3:2] - About **700 sites**, mainly in the **upper Ganga basin** (not the lower Indus basin).
[18:3:3] - Finds: **iron weapons, earthen bowls and dishes, copper objects**; copper possibly from **Khetri** (Rajasthan).
[18:3:4] - First found at **Ahichchhatra (1944)**; Hastinapur excavation (1950-52, B.B. Lal) established it as a culture.
[18:3:5] # PGW Sites → Region
[18:3:6] TABLE head: Site | Region
  [18:3:6.0] Hastinapur | Meerut, western UP (Kuru capital)
  [18:3:6.1] Atranjikhera | Western UP (Ganga-Yamuna Doab)
  [18:3:6.2] Ahichchhatra | Bareilly, UP
  [18:3:6.3] Bhagwanpura | Haryana (house plan found)
  [18:3:6.4] Noh | Rajasthan
[18:3:7] PGW is Later Vedic, not Early Vedic.
## SUB 4 | Agriculture, Settlements, Crafts and Trade
[18:4:0] - **Rice = Vrihi**: main crop with ritual importance; **wheat** grown; **barley** continues.
[18:4:1] - Ploughs yoked with **6, 8, 12 and even 24 oxen**.
[18:4:2] - Settlements: mainly **villages** of mud and wattle houses; **burnt bricks not common**.
[18:4:3] - **True towns** came only after the Vedic period, in the **NBPW phase** (second urbanisation: Shravasti, Kausambi, Hastinapur).
[18:4:4] - Crafts: iron-working, copper, weaving (by women), leather, pottery, carpentry.
[18:4:5] - **Shresthins** = merchant groups; **Nishka** = unit of value; **barter** still main.
[18:4:6] - AP angle: Iron Age in Andhra = **Megalithic culture with BRW** (Brahmagiri, Nagarjunakonda, Gudivada, Jonnawada); Sankalia: rice first harvested in AP in this era.
[18:4:7] - Sep 2026: BHU opened 22 sealed boxes of 10,000+ artefacts from **Rajghat** (Varanasi); pre-NBPW pottery ~3,000 years old dates Varanasi's urban origins to about **800 BCE**.
## SUB 5 | Later Vedic Texts and Priests
[18:5:0] TABLE head: Text | Content
  [18:5:0.0] Samaveda | Rigvedic hymns set to music (Udgatri)
  [18:5:0.1] Yajurveda | Ritual steps in prose (Adhvaryu); Krishna and Shukla branches
  [18:5:0.2] Atharvaveda | Spells and charms against disease; latest Veda
  [18:5:0.3] Brahmanas | Ritual commentaries (Shatapatha, Aitareya)
  [18:5:0.4] Aranyakas | "Forest texts"; symbolic reading of ritual
  [18:5:0.5] Upanishads | c. 800-600 BCE; 108 (13 principal); Atman, Brahman, Karma, Moksha
[18:5:1] - **Hotri** recited hymns; **Adhvaryu** did practical, manual work of sacrifice; **Upadhyaya** taught Veda and Vedanga.
## SUB 6 | Kingship, Ratnins and Taxes
[18:6:0] # Rise of Territorial Kingship
[18:6:1] - Tribe gave way to territory: **rashtra** and **janapadas**; kingship stronger and hereditary; prepared the rise of Mahajanapadas.
[18:6:2] - **Sabha and Samiti declined**: dominated by chiefs, nobles and Brahmanas; the Sabha became a royal court.
[18:6:3] # Officials and Ratnins
[18:6:4] TABLE head: Official | Function
  [18:6:4.0] Bhagadugha | Revenue / tax collector (king's share)
  [18:6:4.1] Sangrahitri | Treasurer
  [18:6:4.2] Akshavapa | Accountant; superintendent of dice
  [18:6:4.3] Palagala | Messenger
  [18:6:4.4] Senani | Army chief
  [18:6:4.5] Purohita | Chief priest
  [18:6:4.6] Suta | Charioteer and herald
[18:6:5] - **Ratnins ("jewels")**: king's council of **12** officials, honoured in the **Ratnahavimshi** rite of the **Rajasuya** (offerings at the Ratnins' houses).
[18:6:6] - List: purohita, rajan, mahisi, senani, bhagadugha, sangrahitri, suta, kshata, govikartana, gramani, parivakti, akshavapa (some add palagala).
[18:6:7] # Taxes
[18:6:8] TABLE head: Tax | Meaning
  [18:6:8.0] Bali | Tribute
  [18:6:8.1] Bhaga | King's share, one-sixth of produce
  [18:6:8.2] Shulka | Customs / tolls
[18:6:9] - Taxes became **compulsory**; **Vaishyas** = tribute-payers.
[18:6:10] Bhagadugha = collector (not gambling officer, messenger or forest officer); Sangrahitri = treasurer.
[18:6:11] # Royal Rituals

=== NOTES 1:21 | Vedic Literature – Vedas, Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads, Vedangas, Sutras
## SUB 0 | Layers of Vedic Literature
[21:0:0] - Order: **Samhitas → Brahmanas → Aranyakas → Upanishads**; all = **Shruti** ("heard"), **apaurusheya**.
[21:0:1] - **Samhitas + Brahmanas = Karma-kanda**; **Aranyakas + Upanishads = Jnana-kanda**.
[21:0:2] - **Brahmanas**: prose ritual commentaries (karma-marga). **Aranyakas**: "forest texts", last parts of Brahmanas; bridge karma-marga → jnana-marga (not domestic law books).
[21:0:3] - **Veda Vyasa (Krishna Dvaipayana)** classified the Vedas.
## SUB 1 | The Four Vedas
[21:1:0] TABLE head: Veda | Content | Priest | Upaveda
  [21:1:0.0] Rigveda | Hymns; oldest | Hotri | Ayurveda
  [21:1:0.1] Yajurveda | Sacrificial formulas; rituals of yajnas | Adhvaryu | Dhanurveda
  [21:1:0.2] Samaveda | Rig hymns set to melody; shortest | Udgatri | Gandharvaveda
  [21:1:0.3] Atharvaveda | Spells, charms, healing; latest | Brahman | Shilpaveda (architecture)
[21:1:1] - **Samaveda**: about **1,549** verses, all but about **75** from the Rigveda; root of Indian music.
[21:1:2] - **Atharvaveda**: earliest description of charms; **Ayurveda** ("Science of Life") first appears here; **kshetra-patni** ("mistress of the field"); **Sabha and Samiti** as separate institutions, "daughters of **Prajapati**"; mentions Pashupati.
[21:1:3] - **Trayi** = Rig, Yajur, Sama; Atharvaveda excluded.
[21:1:4] Shilpa Veda = architecture, not astronomy. Hotri-Udgatri-Adhvaryu-Brahman = Rig-Sama-Yajur-Atharva.
## SUB 2 | Rigveda – Structure
[21:2:0] - **10 mandalas, 1,028 hymns** (1,017 + 11 Valakhilya); similar to the Iranian **Avesta**; "First Testament of Mankind".
[21:2:1] - Hymns = prayers to nature deities for cattle, progeny, victory.
[21:2:2] TABLE head: Mandala | Content
  [21:2:2.0] II-VII | Family books, oldest core (Gritsamada, Vishvamitra, Vamadeva, Atri, Bharadvaja, Vasishtha)
  [21:2:2.1] III | Gayatri Mantra (Vishvamitra; deity Savitri)
  [21:2:2.2] VI / VII | Bharadvaja / Vasishtha; VII = Battle of Ten Kings
  [21:2:2.3] IX | Wholly Soma (Soma Pavamana, 114 hymns)
  [21:2:2.4] X | Purusha Sukta (four varnas), Nasadiya Sukta (creation)
[21:2:3] - Gayatri meaning: "May the Almighty illuminate our intellect to lead us along the righteous path."
[21:2:4] - **Memory of the World (2007):** **30 Rigveda manuscripts** at the **Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute, Pune** inscribed on UNESCO's register.
[21:2:5] - Word **gotra** occurs in the Rigveda meaning **cow shelter / herd of cows**.
