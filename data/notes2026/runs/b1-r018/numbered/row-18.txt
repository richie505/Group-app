## SUB 0 | Eastward Expansion – Kuru-Panchala, Videgha Madhava, Agastya
[0:0] # Period and spread
[0:1] - **Later Vedic (post-Rigvedic) period = 1000–600 BC (BCE).** Texts composed in the upper Gangetic basin.
[0:2] - **Course:** **Punjab → Ganga-Yamuna Doab** (Kurukshetra, Delhi, upper Doab) → **Kosala** (eastern UP), **Kashi** (Varanasi), **Videha** (north Bihar), reached by about 600 BCE.
[0:3] - **Kurus** = union of the **Bharatas and Purus**; first between the Sarasvati and Drishadvati, then Delhi and the upper Doab (Kurukshetra). **Panchalas** = middle Doab (Bareilly, Badaun, Farrukhabad).
[0:4] - **Kuru-Panchala** = **centre / axis of Aryan culture** in the post-Vedic period: the Vedic texts were compiled here (not Anga-Magadha, Kosala-Videha or Matsya-Shurasena).
[0:5] TABLE head: Place | Capital of
  [0:5.0] Hastinapur (Meerut district) | Kuru kingdom (Kauravas)
  [0:5.1] Indraprastha (Purana Qila, Delhi) | Kuru kingdom (Pandavas)
  [0:5.2] Ahichchhatra (Ramnagar, Bareilly) | Panchala
[0:6] # Videgha Madhava legend (Shatapatha Brahmana)
[0:7] - **Videgha Madhava** (Mathava), king of Videha, lived on the **Sarasvati**; with his priest **Gautama Rahugana** he followed **Agni Vaishvanara** (sacrificial fire) as it burnt the forests eastward.
[0:8] - They crossed the **Sadanira (Gandak)**; the land beyond was settled as **Videha**.
[0:9] - Read as the eastward movement and agricultural colonisation by **burning and clearing forests** (fire and iron axes).
[0:10] # Aryanisation of the South
[0:11] - **Agastya** = the sage said to have **Aryanised South India** (crossed the Vindhyas); not Vishvamitra, Vasishtha or Apastamba.
[0:12] > Axis of Aryan culture = Kuru-Panchala; Videgha Madhava's priest = Gautama Rahugana; river crossed = Sadanira (Gandak).
[0:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.30; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.131, p.139, p.148; old app notes 1:18
## SUB 1 | Iron – Krishna Ayas, Dates and Earliest Sites
[1:0] # Vedic metal terms
[1:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:1.0] Ayas (Rigveda) | Copper or bronze
  [1:1.1] Krishna ayas / Shyama ayas ("black metal") | Iron (Later Vedic texts; first in the Yajurveda)
  [1:1.2] Lohita ayas | Copper
  [1:1.3] Hiranya | Gold
  [1:1.4] Nishka | Gold ornament; later a unit of value
[1:2] # Iron in North India
[1:3] - Iron technology about **1000–800 BCE**; common at most **PGW sites** of the Doab; iron weapons (arrowheads, spearheads) common in western UP from about 800 BCE.
[1:4] - Iron axes and fire cleared Gangetic forests; knowledge of iron reached Kosala and Videha by the end of the period.
[1:5] - **Earliest iron among the classic northern sites = Atranjikhera** (Etah, UP; excavated by R.C. Gaur) – not Taxila, Kaushambi or Hastinapur. Other early sites: Hastinapur, Kaushambi, Taxila (Gandhara, c. 1000 BC).
[1:6] - **Jakhera** (UP): iron in the **Black and Red Ware phase, before 1200 BCE**; iron in Dharwar (Karnataka) c. 1000 BCE.
[1:7] # Earliest iron – Tamil Nadu dates (2025)
[1:8] TABLE head: Site | District (TN) | Finding
  [1:8.0] Sivagalai | Thoothukudi | Iron dated to about 2953 BCE (to 3345 BCE) – now the oldest (TN report, Jan 2025)
  [1:8.1] Mayiladumparai | Krishnagiri | Iron AMS-dated to 2172 BCE; earliest claim before Sivagalai
  [1:8.2] Thirumalapuram | Tenkasi | Iron Age urn chamber
  [1:8.3] Adichanallur | Thoothukudi | Urn burials; not a PGW or northern iron site
[1:9] - These dates push iron use far earlier than the Gangetic 1000 BCE; they say **nothing about where PGW began**.
[1:10] - **First metal used in India = copper** (Chalcolithic = "copper-stone" age, Greek *khalkos* + *lithos*); iron came later.
[1:11] > Krishna ayas = iron, not copper; Sivagalai (2953 BCE) is older than Mayiladumparai (2172 BCE).

## SUB 2 | Pottery Sequence and Painted Grey Ware
[2:0] # Sequence of protohistoric pottery (Ganga-Yamuna Doab)
[2:1] TABLE head: Order | Pottery | Date | Phase
  [2:1.0] 1 | Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) | 2000–1500 BCE | Chalcolithic
  [2:1.1] 2 | Black and Red Ware (BRW) | 1450–1200 BCE | Chalcolithic; also Later Vedic; Megalithic south
  [2:1.2] 3 | Painted Grey Ware (PGW) | 1200–500 BCE | Early Iron Age / Later Vedic
  [2:1.3] 4 | Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) | 700–100 BCE (from c. 600 BCE in the Ganga valley) | Mahajanapadas, second urbanisation
[2:2] - **OCP** is an ill-fired red ware that leaves an **ochre colour on the fingers**; **christened (named) at Hastinapur by B.B. Lal (1950–52)**, not Ahichchhatra, Noh or Red Fort.
[2:3] - **Four wares known to the Later Vedic people:** **Red Ware** (most popular), **Black-slipped Ware**, **Black-and-Red Ware**, **Painted Grey Ware** (most distinctive) = I, II, III, IV. **NBPW is NOT** Later Vedic (post-Vedic, shiny black).
[2:4] # Painted Grey Ware culture
[2:5] - **Grey pottery with black linear / geometric painted designs**; mainly **bowls and dishes**; deluxe tableware of the upper strata; not polished black.
[2:6] - **Pottery of 1000–600 BC in the Gangetic plain = PGW.**
[2:7] - About **700 sites**, mainly in the **upper Ganga basin** and the Indo-Gangetic divide (Punjab, Haryana, western UP) – not the lower Indus basin.
[2:8] - First found at **Ahichchhatra** (excavations 1940–44); **Hastinapur** excavation (1950–52, **B.B. Lal**) established it as a culture.
[2:9] - Finds: **iron weapons**, copper objects (copper possibly from **Khetri**, Rajasthan), glass, bone, semi-precious beads (trade, barter; no coins).
[2:10] TABLE head: Site | Region
  [2:10.0] Hastinapur | Meerut, western UP (Kuru capital)
  [2:10.1] Atranjikhera | Etah, western UP (Doab)
  [2:10.2] Ahichchhatra | Bareilly, UP
  [2:10.3] Bhagwanpura | Haryana – 13-roomed mud house; pre-iron PGW with late Harappan pottery
  [2:10.4] Noh | Rajasthan (OCP–BRW–PGW sequence)
[2:11] - Early PGW (Ghaggar-Hakra, Bhagwanpura) has **no iron**; later Doab PGW is iron-using.
[2:12] > Later Vedic wares exclude NBPW; PGW = upper Ganga basin, 1000–600 BC; OCP named at Hastinapur.
[2:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.30, p.32; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.133; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.6-9; old app notes 1:18
## SUB 3 | Agriculture, Settlements and Crafts
[3:0] - **Agriculture** became the main livelihood; wooden ploughshare common; ploughs drawn by **6, 8, 12 and even 24 oxen**.
[3:1] TABLE head: Crop | Later Vedic term
  [3:1.0] Rice | Vrihi (ritual use; rice remains of 8th c. BCE at Hastinapur, Atranjikhera)
  [3:1.1] Wheat | Godhuma
  [3:1.2] Barley | Yava (continued)
  [3:1.3] Sugarcane | Ikshu
[3:2] - **Settlements:** mainly **villages** of mud-brick and wattle-and-daub houses; **burnt bricks not common**; Hastinapur and Kaushambi only proto-urban; **true towns** came in the **NBPW phase** (second urbanisation: Shravasti, Kausambi, Hastinapur).
[3:3] - **Crafts:** carpentry (chariots), leather, pottery, weaving (by women), copper-bronze-iron smiths; **Shresthins** = merchant groups; units of value **nishka, satamana, krishnala**; barter still main.
[3:4] - **AP angle:** Iron Age in Andhra = **Megalithic culture with BRW** (Brahmagiri, Nagarjunakonda, Gudivada, Jonnawada); H.D. Sankalia: rice first harvested in AP in the Iron Age (Megalithic) era.
[3:5] - **Sep 2026:** BHU opened 22 sealed boxes of 10,000+ **Rajghat** (Varanasi) artefacts; pre-NBPW pottery dates Varanasi's urban origins to about **800 BCE**.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.32; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.132-133; old app notes 1:18
