## SUB 0 | From Tribe to Territory – Kingship
[0:0] - **Jana** (tribe) → **janapada / rashtra** (territory); the word **rashtra** first appears in the Later Vedic texts; tribal chiefdom → **hereditary territorial monarchy**, which prepared the way for the **Mahajanapadas** (6th century BCE).
[0:1] - Kingship **hereditary**, usually to the **eldest son** (primogeniture); men became rajas by performing big sacrifices; traces of election survived.
[0:2] - **Ekarat** (Atharvaveda, Aitareya Brahmana) = **sole / paramount ruler**.
[0:3] TABLE head: Region (Aitareya Brahmana) | Ruler's title
  [0:3.0] East | Samrat
  [0:3.1] South | Bhoja
  [0:3.2] West | Svarat
  [0:3.3] North | Virat
[0:4] - State forms in the Aitareya Brahmana: **Rajya, Bhojya, Swarajya, Vairajya, Samrajya** – **Ganatantra is NOT** one.
[0:5] # Divine origin of kingship
[0:6] TABLE head: Text | Statement
  [0:6.0] Aitareya Brahmana | Earliest account: the gods, losing to the Asuras, chose a king to lead them; king = protector of Brahmins
  [0:6.1] Taittiriya Brahmana | Theory of the divine origin of kingship
  [0:6.2] Shatapatha Brahmana | Linked the king with Prajapati; Rajasuya and Vajapeya gave him divine qualities
[0:7] - Kingship was **not** traced to election by village assemblies.
[0:8] TABLE head: Early Vedic | Later Vedic
  [0:8.0] Tribe; cattle; elective chief | Territory; agriculture; hereditary king
  [0:8.1] Voluntary bali; strong assemblies | Compulsory taxes (bali, bhaga, shulka); assemblies decline; Ratnins
[0:9] > East = Samrat, North = Virat; rashtra first appears now.

## SUB 2 | Royal Sacrifices – Rajasuya, Ashvamedha, Vajapeya
[2:0] TABLE head: Sacrifice | Purpose
  [2:0.0] Rajasuya | Royal consecration; conferred supreme power; includes the Ratnahavimshi (offerings at the Ratnins' houses)
  [2:0.1] Ashvamedha | Horse sacrifice; supremacy over other kings and control of the area the horse roamed
  [2:0.2] Vajapeya | "Drink of strength"; chariot race won by the king over his kinsmen
  [2:0.3] Punarabhisheka | Second anointing at the end of the Rajasuya
[2:1] - **Muvendavelan** is a title (Chola-period chief), **not** a royal sacrifice.
[2:2] - Large **Shrauta** sacrifices needed many priests and heavy **dakshina** (up to 2,40,000 cows in a Rajasuya) → strengthened the **Brahmanas**.
[2:3] - **AP angle:** Satavahanas, **Ikshvakus**, Vishnukundins performed these rites; Ikshvaku titles: **Vajapeya = Samrat/Virat; Ashvamedha = Swarat**; an **Ashvamedha altar at Nagarjunakonda**.
[2:4] > Ashvamedha = over other kings; Vajapeya = over kinsmen; Rajasuya = consecration.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.31; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.134, p.140; old app notes 1:19
## SUB 3 | Ratnins and Officials
[3:0] - **Ratnins ("jewels")** = the king's council of **12** royal functionaries: purohita, rajan, mahishi, senani, bhagadugha, sangrahitri, suta, kshata, govikartana, gramani, parivakti, akshavapa (some add palagala); honoured in the **Ratnahavimshi** rite of the Rajasuya.
[3:1] TABLE head: Official | Function
  [3:1.0] Purohita | Chief priest
  [3:1.1] Senani | Army chief
  [3:1.2] Suta | Charioteer and herald
  [3:1.3] Bhagadugha | Revenue collector (king's share)
  [3:1.4] Sangrahitri | Treasurer
  [3:1.5] Akshavapa | Accountant; superintendent of dice
  [3:1.6] Palagala | Messenger
[3:2] - More officers but no regular bureaucracy and **no standing army**.
[3:3] > Bhagadugha collects; Sangrahitri stores.
[3:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.31; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.135; old app notes 1:19
## SUB 4 | Assemblies and Taxes
[4:0] - **Vidatha disappeared**; **Sabha and Samiti continued** but declined, dominated by chiefs, nobles and Brahmanas; **women no longer sat** in the Sabha; village assemblies tried local cases.
[4:1] TABLE head: Tax | Nature
  [4:1.0] Bali | Tribute: voluntary in the Rigveda, now regular and compulsory – the king's tax on the people
  [4:1.1] Bhaga | King's share of produce (one-sixth; texts give 1/6 to 1/2)
  [4:1.2] Shulka | Customs / tolls
[4:2] - **Vaishyas** were the main (only) **tribute-payers**; Bhagadugha collected, Sangrahitri managed the revenue.
[4:3] - Varman = armour, Vidatha = assembly – not taxes.
[4:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.31-32; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.134-136; old app notes 1:19
