## SUB 0 | Central Departments – Kandachara, Athavane and the Council
[0:0] # Departments
[0:1] - **Rayavachakam** records **nine departments**, each headed by a **Samprati**.
[0:2] TABLE head: Department | Function
  [0:2.0] Kandachara | Military – standing army, headed by the Senapati / Dalavayi
  [0:2.1] Athavane / Attavana | Revenue / finance
  [0:2.2] Bhandara (Sri Bhandara) | Treasury
  [0:2.3] Dharmasana | Judicial; Pradvivaka = judge
[0:3] - **Kandachara dealt with military affairs / defence** – asked ×7 (APPSC G2 2019; G2 Mains; SI 2016; AP History PYQ ×3; G2 PYQ). Not revenue, finance, land survey (Bhoomi Sisthu), police, justice or religious institutions; **Athavana** = revenue, **Dandaniti** / **Mansabdari** are distractors.
[0:4] # Officers and the council
[0:5] TABLE head: Officer | Role
  [0:5.0] Mahapradhani / Prime Minister | Chief minister presiding over the council of pradhanis; also called Sarva Sirah Pradhani, Sabhanayaka, Tantranayaka; e.g. Saluva Timmarusu
  [0:5.1] Dalavayi | Commander-in-chief
  [0:5.2] Rayasams | Secretaries (Kondamarasu, Ayyamarasu)
  [0:5.3] Karanikas | Scribes
[0:6] - **President of the imperial council:** the **Prime Minister** (AMVI 2009) – "Sabhanayaka" is only another name of the same office; the king was advised by, not president of, the council.

## SUB 1 | Amara-Nayankara System and the Palegars
[1:0] # Amaram and Amaranayakas
[1:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:1.0] Amaram | Land granted to military chiefs instead of cash salary; the Rayas owned all land
  [1:1.1] Amaranayakas | Holders; also Nayaks, Palaiyagars, Poligars
  [1:1.2] Mahamandaleshwara | Their title; more autonomy than Durgadannayakas
[1:2] - **Nayakas were:** **military chiefs controlling particular territories** (CDS 2001) – not central ministers, cavalry soldiers or temple priests.
[1:3] - **Amaranayakas were NOT** "wise men who advised the rulers" – they were **military commanders**, **collected taxes**, and their **contingents** fought for the king.
[1:4] - **Administrative system (G1 2007):** the **Amaranayaka** (Amara-Nayankara) system – a military governor (Nayaka) given amarams for salary and army expenses.
[1:5] - Within the amaram: **collected taxes**, kept **law and order**, administered their territory (**palaiyam**).
[1:6] - **Obligations:** tribute (**Kappam**); a quota of troops, horses, elephants; attend court at **Mahanavami** with gifts; maintain forts, temples, irrigation.
[1:7] # Kakatiya nayankara vs Vijayanagara amara-nayankara
[1:8] TABLE head: Point | Kakatiya nayankara | Vijayanagara amara-nayankara
  [1:8.0] Origin | Prataparudra II | Grew out of the Kakatiya system
  [1:8.1] Scale | 75 or 77 nayakas | About 200 nayakas
  [1:8.2] Outcome | Discontent among older feudatories | Semi-independent, then independent Nayaka states after 1565 (Madurai, Tanjore, Keladi)
[1:9] # Palegars (Poligars)
[1:10] - **Origin:** **amara-nayakas** → Palegars when the centre weakened after **Talikota (1565)**; hereditary; **forerunners of the zamindars**.
[1:11] - **Who were they:** **territorial administrative and military governors** (UPPSC 2015) – not ordinary zamindars, Mahajans or traders.
[1:12] - **Duties under the Nayakas:** administer palaiyams, collect taxes, maintain law and order – **NOT building health and education centres**.
[1:13] - **Thomas Munro**, with **Campbell**, crushed the Rayalaseema Palegars and then introduced the **Ryotwari** system.
[1:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History – Vijayanagara p.28-29; app notes 1:121 (sub 4, 5, 6)
## SUB 2 | Revenue and Taxes
[2:0] - **Chief feature of the financial system:** **land revenue** (BPSC 1999) – usually **1/6** of produce, **1/4 or 1/3** on fertile land; land-tax documents = **Adhuvanatantram**.
[2:1] # Land classes
[2:2] TABLE head: Term | Land
  [2:2.0] Nanja | Wet
  [2:2.1] Punja | Dry
  [2:2.2] Bagayati | Garden
  [2:2.3] Banjara | Waste
[2:3] - **Neerarambamu / Magani** (irrigated): tax in **grain**, higher; **Kaadarambamu / Metta** (dry): cash, lower.
[2:4] # Tax terms
[2:5] TABLE head: Tax | Meaning
  [2:5.0] Navidadere / Kondaju | Barbers' tax
  [2:5.1] Maneyanna / Manevana / Illari Pannu | House tax
  [2:5.2] Melivamsam | Plough tax (G1 match)
  [2:5.3] Tudimeya | Marriage tax (G1 match)
  [2:5.4] Meruka | Tax on sale and purchase (APPSC G2 2011)
  [2:5.5] Putrodaya Kanuka | Birth of a son
  [2:5.6] Kalyana Kanika | Wedding tax on both parties
  [2:5.7] Siddhaya | Fixed levy: Pinje (cotton ginners), Eidipi (toddy tappers), Jati (professions)
[2:6] - **G1 match:** Navidadere–barbers, Melivamsam–ploughs, Manevana–houses, Tudimeya–marriage = **a-II, b-III, c-IV, d-I**.
[2:7] - **Meruka ≠ Mera:** Mera = the Ayagars' harvest share; Merulu = the Talari's grain gifts; Meruka = sale–purchase tax (not Magani/Metta land or export–import).
[2:8] - **Marriage tax:** first remitted by **Saluva Narasimha**; **totally withdrawn by Krishnadevaraya** (Rocket; NextGen previous papers – APPSC G1 2010). ⚠ Some keys give Sadasiva Raya; Sadasiva's inscriptions (Rama Raya's regency) record the **barbers'** tax remission (at Kandoja's request).
[2:9] - Market (**santhalu**) tax rights auctioned to **Sunkandarlu**.

## SUB 3 | Justice and Police
[3:0] - **Pradvivaka:** adviser to the king in the **judiciary**; judge who explained legal texts (APPSC G2 2019; G2 PYQ) – not military, revenue or espionage.
[3:1] - **Authoritative law book:** **Parasara Madhaviyam** – Madhavacharya's commentary on the Parashara Smriti (with Yajnavalkya) – not Amuktamalyada or the Arthashastra.
[3:2] - **Himsodbhava** = criminal; **Dhanodbhava** = civil.
[3:3] - The **Raya** was the supreme judge; ministers like **Timmarusu** ("Dharma Pratipalaka") also tried cases.
[3:4] - **Punishments:** treason – **death**; theft – **mutilation**; Brahmins – **life imprisonment** instead of death.
[3:5] # Police
[3:6] TABLE head: Level | Police (Kavali system)
  [3:6.0] Village | Talari
  [3:6.1] Sthala | Kavali Daru
  [3:6.2] Towns | Peta Kavalidaru
[3:7] - **Padikaval:** the system in which **police rights were sold** to individuals (AP History PYQ) – not Athavana or Kandachara.
[3:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History – Vijayanagara p.28; Rocket AP History p.15 (Pradvivaka); NextGen previous papers p.14; app notes 1:121 (sub 10)
## SUB 4 | Provincial and Village Administration – the Ayagars
[4:0] # Provincial units
[4:1] TABLE head: Unit | Head
  [4:1.0] Rajya / Mandala (province) – the largest unit | Prince (Sangama princes titled Odeyar) or Durga Dannayaka
  [4:1.1] Nadu / Seema (district) | Nadagauda; Parupatyagar
  [4:1.2] Sthala / Kampana | Reddi, Gauda, Sthalakaranam
  [4:1.3] Grama (village) | 12 Ayagars
[4:2] - **Biggest unit:** **Mandalam** (RPSC 2012) – not Kottam, Nadu or Kurram.
[4:3] - **Parupatyagar:** the king's officer of the nadu/sima who **collected revenue** and **repaired and maintained forts** (AP History PYQ).
[4:4] - About **17 rajyams** under **Achyutaraya**. Governors could **mint coins** and levy or remit taxes, and were **frequently transferred**.
[4:5] TABLE head: Andhra Rajya | Fact
  [4:5.0] Udayagiri | Most important; Paka and Muliki districts
  [4:5.1] Chandragiri | Saluva Narasimha was its governor before 1485
  [4:5.2] Penukonda | Tirumala's headquarters after Talikota
  [4:5.3] Gooty (Gutti) | Fort raised to rajya status
[4:6] # Village – the Ayagars
[4:7] - **12 hereditary Ayagars** per village: Karanam (accountant), Reddi (head), Talari (watchman), Purohita, plus artisans – Kamsali, Kummari, Vadrangi, **Kase (mason / house-builder)**, Kammari, Chakali, Mangali.
[4:8] - **Pay:** no cash salary – tax-free **Manyam** lands and a harvest share **Mera**.
[4:9] TABLE head: Officer | Duties
  [4:9.0] Karanam | Accounts, land and crop registers
  [4:9.1] Reddi | Village head (Gauda / Gowda in Karnataka) and revenue collector
  [4:9.2] Talari | Watchman; collected Merulu
  [4:9.3] Begari | Supervised forced labour (vetti)
[4:10] - **Village headman:** **Gowda** (APPSC Jr Asst key) – the Karnataka name of the Andhra **Reddi**; Chowdhary and Parupatyadar are wrong.
[4:11] - **Brahmins as Karanams:** **Harihara I** followed the **Kakatiya** practice (APPSC Jr Asst) – the Sangamas inherited Kakatiya village administration.

## SUB 5 | Army, Forts and the Nature of the State
[5:0] # Army
[5:1] - Two divisions: the **Siddha (standing) army** under the **Dalavayi** (via Kandachara), paid every **4 months**, and the **Amaranayaka** contingents.
[5:2] - **Bukka I** = first to use **cannon**; **Saluva Narasimha** introduced **Samugaris** (gunmen).
[5:3] - **Horses** (Arabian, Persian) imported through **Goa, Honnavar, Bhatkal**.
[5:4] - **Four kinds of forts:** **Giri** (hill), **Sthala**, **Jala**, **Vana** – under **Durga Dannayakas**.
[5:5] - **Against sieges:** Vijayanagara enclosed **large agricultural tracts within the fortified area** (APPSC G1 2017; NCERT Themes II ch. 7) – granaries were the usual practice elsewhere.
[5:6] # Nature of the state
[5:7] - **King:** supreme head and fountain of justice, acting within **Dharma** and with **his ministers'** counsel.
[5:8] - **Nilakanta Sastri:** "**military administration**"; **Burton Stein:** "**segmentary state**".
[5:9] # Political maxims in Amuktamalyada
[5:10] TABLE head: Area | Maxim
  [5:10.0] Revenue | Bulk of revenue to the army
  [5:10.1] Trade | Improve harbours; encourage import of horses, elephants, gems; treat foreign merchants generously
  [5:10.2] Welfare | Build tanks; win over forest tribes rather than only crush them
  [5:10.3] Forts | Brahmins should command forts
[5:11] - It refers to the king's **campaigns** and gives political advice, but gives **no genealogy from Bukka**.
