=== NOTES 1:120 | Saluva and Tuluva Dynasties; Krishnadevaraya - Conquests, Amuktamalyada, the Ashtadiggajas
## SUB 0 | Ashtadiggajas, Bhuvana Vijayam and the Golden Age of Telugu
[120:0:0] # The Ashtadiggajas
[120:0:1] - **Who they were:** **eight Telugu poets** ("eight elephants" holding up the eight directions) – not ministers, warriors or advisers (UP UDA 2001).
[120:0:2] - **Patron:** **Krishnadevaraya** (APPSC G2 2019; AP History PYQ; RAS 2010) – not Rama Raya, Achyutaraya, Tirumala Raya, Devaraya II, Shivaji or Rajendra I.
[120:0:3] - **Golden age:** his reign (1509–29) = **golden age of Telugu literature**, the **Prabandha** era (UP UDA 2006) – not Konkani, Malayalam or Tamil.
[120:0:4] - **Why we remember him:** for his **literary patronage** (AP History PYQ) – the Ashtadiggajas in the **Bhuvana Vijayam**.
[120:0:5] # Poet → work → title
[120:0:6] TABLE head: Poet | Main work | Title / note
  [120:0:6.0] Allasani Peddana | *Manucharitramu* (Swarochisha Manu Sambhavam) | Andhra Kavita Pitamaha; greatest of the eight; poet of Telugu and Sanskrit
  [120:0:6.1] Nandi Timmana | *Parijatapaharanamu* | Mukku Timmana; the king's favourite
  [120:0:6.2] Dhurjati | *Sri Kalahasteeswara Mahatyam* and Satakam | Shaiva poet of Kalahasti
  [120:0:6.3] Madayyagari Mallana | *Rajasekhara Charitramu* | Early social narrative
  [120:0:6.4] Ayyalaraju Ramabhadrudu | *Ramabhyudayamu*, Sakalakathasara Sangrahamu | Ramayana in Telugu
  [120:0:6.5] Pingali Surana | *Raghava Pandaviyamu*, *Kalapurnodayamu*, Prabhavati Pradyumnamu | Dvyarthi kavya (double meaning)
  [120:0:6.6] Ramarajabhushana (Bhattumurti) | *Vasucharitramu*, Narasabhupaliyamu | Vasucharitra dedicated to Tirumala Raya
  [120:0:6.7] Tenali Ramakrishna | *Panduranga Mahatmyamu* (also Ghatikachala Mahatmyam, Udbhataradhya Charitra) | Court wit
[120:0:7] - **Manucharitramu:** by **Allasani Peddana** (APPSC G2 2019; IAS 2007) – not Kalapurnodayam or Raghava Pandaviyam (Pingali Surana), or Haravilasam (Srinatha).
[120:0:8] - **Andhra Kavita Pitamaha:** **Allasani Peddana** (Raj. PCS 2023; WBCS 2007) – not Bhattumurti, Tenali Ramakrishna, Pingali Surana, Srinatha, Sayana or Madhava. **Andhra Bhoja** is the king's own title.
[120:0:9] - **Panduranga Mahatyam:** **Tenali Ramakrishna** (APPSC Jr Asst) – not Peddana, Dhurjati or the king.
[120:0:10] - **RAS 2021 statements:** Krishnadevaraya wrote Amuktamalyada ✓; he is called Andhra Bhoja ✓; Peddana "well versed in **Sanskrit and Tamil**" ✗ (he was a **Telugu and Sanskrit** poet) → **1 and 2 only**.
[120:0:11] - **Gandapenderam:** the king himself fixed this anklet on **Peddana's** foot.
[120:0:12] - **"Prabandha Parameshwara"** is **Errana's** title, not Nandi Timmana's.
[120:0:13] # Bhuvana Vijayam and the Tippalur grant
[120:0:14] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [120:0:14.0] Bhuvana Vijayam | "Conquest of the world"; Krishnadevaraya's court hall of poets; House of Victory
  [120:0:14.1] Ashtadiggajas | Eight elephants of the eight directions → eight pillar-poets
  [120:0:14.2] Kavula Samstha | Institution of poets – the Tippalur inscription's name for them
[120:0:15] - **Tippalur inscription:** gift of **Tippalur agrahara** (Kadapa) to the poets by **Krishnadevaraya**, not Achyutaraya.
[120:0:16] # Languages patronised
[120:0:17] - Vijayanagara patronised **Telugu, Kannada, Sanskrit and Tamil** – all four (Grama Sachivalayam) – so "**Tamil, Telugu and Sanskrit**" is the SSC 2007 answer (Hindi, Marathi, Malayalam, Urdu are wrong).
[120:0:18] - **Kannada:** **Mallanarya** of Krishnadevaraya's court wrote *Bhava Chinta Ratna* and *Veerasaivamrita* in **Kannada** (Raj. PCS 2024).
[120:0:19] - **Correct combination (Grama Sachivalayam):** **Krishnadevaraya – Amuktamalyada – Sahiti Samarangana Sarvabhauma** ✓. Wrong: Hala's title is not Devanampriya (Ashoka's); Peddana wrote **Manucharitra**, not Vasucharitra; Srinatha's title is **Kavisarvabhauma**.

## SUB 1 | Amuktamalyada and Krishnadevaraya's Own Works
[120:1:0] # Amuktamalyada
[120:1:1] - **Author:** **Krishnadevaraya** himself (APPSC AEE; Tribal Welfare Officer; AP History PYQ) – not Peddana, Tenali Ramakrishna, Nannaya, Nanne Choda, Palkuriki Somanatha, Gona Budda Reddy, Gangamba or Praudha Devaraya.
[120:1:2] - **Language:** **Telugu** – his only Telugu work; a work on **statecraft** (rajaniti) as well as devotion. NDA 2017 "NOT correct": "**wrote Amuktamalyada in Sanskrit**".
[120:1:3] - **Other name:** **Vishnuchittiyam**; meaning "**one who offers the garland she has worn**".
[120:1:4] - **Legend:** **Andhra Mahavishnu of Srikakulam** (Krishna district) told the king in a **dream** to write it, while he was **resting at Srikakulam** during the **Kalinga campaign**; dedicated to **Lord Venkateswara**.
[120:1:5] - **Central theme:** the **wedding of Goda Devi (Andal) with Sri Ranganatha** (Endowment 2022) – not his victories, the Ashtadiggajas or his literary achievements; sub-stories **Yamunacharya Charitra** and **Maladasari Katha**.
[120:1:6] - **Political maxims:** kingship, administration and statecraft; social, religious and economic life.
[120:1:7] - Preface: "**Desa Bhashalandu Telugu Lessa**" (Telugu is the best of the regional languages).
[120:1:8] - One of the **Telugu Pancha Kavyas**; the **first Telugu work named after its heroine**.
[120:1:9] # What Amuktamalyada does NOT contain (G2 2003; AP History PYQ)
[120:1:10] - Its preface **describes the king's Kalinga campaign** (the march to Vijayawada and the halt at Srikakulam), and the work **discusses political matters**.
[120:1:11] - It does **not** reveal a **genealogy from Bukkaraya to Krishnadevaraya** – the Tuluvas were not descendants of Bukka → this is the answer.
[120:1:12] - ⚠ Some keys of the G2 2003 paper print "describes the invasions"; that is wrong, since the preface narrates the Kalinga campaign.
[120:1:13] # Works → language
[120:1:14] TABLE head: Work | Language
  [120:1:14.0] Amuktamalyada | Telugu (his only Telugu work)
  [120:1:14.1] Jambavati Kalyanam / Parinayam (drama; Krishna weds Jambavati; mentions Virupaksha, Vasantotsavams) | Sanskrit (APPSC G2 2019; G2 Mains; AP History PYQ)
  [120:1:14.2] Usha Parinayam | Sanskrit
  [120:1:14.3] Madalasa Charita | Sanskrit
  [120:1:14.4] Rasamanjari | Sanskrit
  [120:1:14.5] Jnana Chintamani | Sanskrit
  [120:1:14.6] Satyavadhu Parinayam | Sanskrit
[120:1:15] - **Court musician:** **Lakshminatha** wrote *Sangita Suryodaya*.
[120:1:16] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History – Vijayanagara p.4; Wikipedia, Amuktamalyada (preface: Kalinga campaign, Srikakulam dream); app notes 1:120 (sub 7)
[120:1:17] - **Rayavachakam** (Telugu prose, by **Sthanapati**): describes **Krishnadevaraya's court and daily life**.
## SUB 2 | Tuluva Succession, Court and Contemporaries
[120:2:0] # Tuluva origin
[120:2:1] - From **Tuluvanadu** near Mysore; progenitor **Timmaraju**; mother tongue **Kannada**, but **Telugu** favoured at court.
[120:2:2] - **Tuluva Narasa Nayaka:** Saluva general and real ruler under Immadi Narasimha; died 1503. **Founder of the Tuluva dynasty = Vira Narasimha** (1505), not Narasa Nayaka.
[120:2:3] - **Krishnadevaraya belonged to the Tuluva dynasty** (AMVI 2023) – not Pandya, Kakatiya or Qutb Shahi.
[120:2:4] # Succession
[120:2:5] TABLE head: Ruler | Immediate successor
  [120:2:5.0] Praudha Deva Raya | Saluva Narasimha
  [120:2:5.1] Immadi Narasimha | Vira Narasimha
  [120:2:5.2] Vira Narasimha (1505–09) | Krishnadevaraya (his younger half-brother)
  [120:2:5.3] Krishnadevaraya (1509–29) | Achyuta Deva Raya (his half-brother)
[120:2:6] - **King before Krishnadevaraya:** **Vira Narasimha** (G2-A 1989). **After him:** **Achyuta Devaraya** (Asst. Archaeologist 2011) – not Rama Raya or Sadasiva.
[120:2:7] - **Aravidu rule began with:** **Tirumala Deva Raya** (Penukonda).
[120:2:8] - **Death of Krishnadevaraya:** **1529** (NextGen; reign often written 1509–1529/30).
[120:2:9] # Court
[120:2:10] - **Prime Minister:** **Saluva Timmarusu (Appaji)** – minister of **Krishnadevaraya** (AP History PYQ), not Saluva Narasimha, Rajaraja Narendra or Kyatya Varma; later blinded on suspicion.
[120:2:11] - **Rajaguru:** **Vyasatirtha (Vyasaraya)**, Madhva saint (AP History PYQ) – not Vidyaranya (Sangama founders' guru). His disciple **Purandaradasa** (father of Carnatic music) was a contemporary.
[120:2:12] # Contemporaries
[120:2:13] - **Babur** (1526–30): Krishnadevaraya was his contemporary (FRO 2009; SI 2011); Babur praised him in the **Tuzuk-i-Baburi** as the most powerful ruler of India. Not Akbar, Aurangzeb, Firoz Tughlaq or Alauddin Khalji.
[120:2:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History – Vijayanagara p.3-5, p.17-20; app notes 1:120 (sub 2)
## SUB 3 | The Saluva Dynasty and the Two Usurpations
[120:3:0] # Decline of the Sangamas
[120:3:1] - **Nuniz** called **Virupaksha II** a drunkard and **Praudha Raya** worthless; Gajapati and Bahmani invasions ate into the kingdom.
[120:3:2] # The two usurpations
[120:3:3] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [120:3:3.0] 1485 | First usurpation: Saluva Narasimha of Chandragiri expels Praudha Raya; ends the Sangama dynasty, begins the Saluva dynasty
  [120:3:3.1] 1505 | Second usurpation: Vira Narasimha (son of Tuluva Narasa Nayaka) kills Immadi Narasimha, the last Saluva, at Penugonda; begins the Tuluva dynasty
  [120:3:3.2] 1509 | Krishnadevaraya crowned on 8 August 1509
  [120:3:3.3] 1529 | Achyutaraya crowned at Tirupati
  [120:3:3.4] 1543 | Sadasiva made a figurehead under Aliya Rama Raya
  [120:3:3.5] Jan 1565 | Talikota (Rakshasi-Tangadi / Bannihatti): Rama Raya beheaded; flight to Penukonda
[120:3:4] - **Dynasty after the first usurpation:** **Saluva** (AP History PYQ). **Came to power:** **1485** (G2 2012).
[120:3:5] - **Founder of the Saluva dynasty:** **Saluva Narasimha (Narasimharaya)** (SSC 2005; AP History PYQ) – not Immadi Narasimha (last Saluva), Vira Narasimha (Tuluva founder), Praudha Devaraya or Rama Raya.
[120:3:6] - **UPSC 2003 A–R:** A "Saluva Narasimha ended the old dynasty and took the royal title" ✓; R "to save the kingdom from further degeneration and disintegration" ✓ and **explains A**.
[120:3:7] - **IAS 2004 (×3): all four correct** – Saluva Narasimha ended the Sangamas; Vira Narasimha deposed the last Saluva; Vira Narasimha → younger brother **Krishnadevaraya**; Krishnadevaraya → half-brother **Achyuta Raya**.
[120:3:8] - **Chronology (APSLPRB 2023):** Deva Raya II (Sangama) → Saluva Narasimha → Achyuta Deva Raya (Tuluva) → Venkatapati II (Aravidu) = **ii, iv, i, iii**.
[120:3:9] # Saluva Narasimha (1485–91)
[120:3:10] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [120:3:10.0] Origin | Telugu clan from Kalyana; governor of Chandragiri
  [120:3:10.1] Capital | Kalyanapuram → Chandragiri
  [120:3:10.2] Title | Raya Maharasu; his general Tuluva Narasa Nayaka took the throne for him
  [120:3:10.3] Army | Arab horses from Ormuz, Arab cavalry; introduced Samugaris (gunmen)
  [120:3:10.4] Lost | Udayagiri to Gajapati Purushottama
  [120:3:10.5] Court poet | Rajanatha Dindima – *Saluvabhyudayam*
  [120:3:10.6] Contemporary | Annamacharya (Tallapaka, 1408–1503): 32,000 kirtanas on Venkateswara; wife Timmakka wrote Subhadra Kalyanam
[120:3:11] - **Annamayya was a contemporary of:** **Saluva Narasimha Raya** (APPSC G2 2011; AP History PYQ) – not Harihara, Devaraya I, II or III.

## SUB 5 | Krishnadevaraya's Campaigns – Raichur, Golconda and Kalinga
[120:5:0] # Campaigns
[120:5:1] TABLE head: Campaign | Year | Result
  [120:5:1.0] Diwani / Kovilkonda | 1509–10 | Bahmani "jihad" crushed; Yusuf Adil Khan killed
  [120:5:1.1] Raichur Doab and Bidar | 1510–12 | Raichur taken; freed Sultan Mahmud Shah from Barid → title Yavanarajya Sthapanacharya
  [120:5:1.2] Ummattur / Sivasamudram | 1512–13 | Gangaraja defeated; new province at Srirangapatna
  [120:5:1.3] Kalinga | 1513–1519 | Udayagiri, Kondaveedu, Kondapalli, Simhachalam, Cuttack (1518)
  [120:5:1.4] Battle of Raichur | 1520 | Ismail Adil Shah of Bijapur routed with Portuguese help; Gulbarga taken 1523
[120:5:2] - **Bijapur Sultan at Raichur (1520):** **Ismail Adil Khan / Shah** (G2 2000; AP History PYQ) – not Ahmed Barid, Muhammad Shah I or Mujahid Shah.
[120:5:3] - **"Great battle of 1520":** the battle of Raichur, which Telugu texts name **Gobburu** (the field near Raichur) – G2-A 1989; **Mudgal** was Devaraya I vs Firoz Shah Bahmani.
[120:5:4] - **Battle of Golconda:** fought against **Quli Qutb Shah** of Golconda (BPSC 1999), who had taken forts while the king fought the Gajapatis – not Qutbuddin Aibak, Ismail Adil Shah or the Gajapati.
[120:5:5] - **Yusuf Adil Khan** (Kovilkonda, 1509–10) ≠ **Ismail Adil Shah** (Raichur, 1520).
[120:5:6] # The Kalinga campaign against Prataparudra Gajapati
[120:5:7] TABLE head: Fort / place | What happened
  [120:5:7.0] Udayagiri (1513–14) | Fell after an 18-month siege; Balakrishna idol taken to Hampi (Krishna temple built for it)
  [120:5:7.1] Kondaveedu | Taken with Saluva Timmarusu's help (split Veerabhadra Gajapati's army); king performed Talapurusha Danam at Amaravati
  [120:5:7.2] Kondapalli and Bejawada | Gajapati forces defeated
  [120:5:7.3] Vinukonda, Addanki, Kandukuru | Also taken
  [120:5:7.4] Cuttack | Reached in 1518; Gajapati sued for peace
[120:5:8] - **Idol taken from Udayagiri:** **Balakrishna** (G2-A 1989) – not Ramachandra, Ugranarasimha or Virupaksha.
[120:5:9] - **Victory pillar (Jayastambha) for the conquest of coastal Andhra:** **Potnuru** (Padmanabham mandal, near Visakhapatnam; Bezawada inscription 1516: "Simhadri-Potnuru") – AP History PYQ; G2 PYQ. Not Kondaveedu, Kondapalli, Udayagiri, Rajahmundry or Nagarjunakonda. Lists of his pillars give **Potnuru and Simhachalam** (the inscription's "Simhadri-Potnuru").
[120:5:10] - ⚠ The old note "APPSC key (2025) gave Kondaveedu" is unverified; Kondaveedu was a fort taken, not the pillar site.
[120:5:11] - **Temple grants:** **Simhachalam** – diamond necklace to Varaha Narasimha; **Mangalagiri** – land to **Lakshmi Narasimha**.
[120:5:12] - **Peace terms:** Prataparudra's daughter (**Tukka / Bhadradevi**) married Krishnadevaraya; lands **north of the Krishna** went back to the Gajapati.
[120:5:13] - **Salt:** **Pedaganjam and Chinaganjam** were famous for salt; the salt pans at **Pedaganjam** were taxed.

## SUB 8 | Achyutaraya, Sadasiva and Aliya Rama Raya
[120:8:0] - **Achyutaraya** (1529–42): named heir by Krishnadevaraya; crowned at **Tirupati** and **Srikalahasti** (the only king crowned at Tirumala-Tirupati).
[120:8:1] - **Assembly hall:** Achyutaraya's sabha = **Venkatavilasa Mantapam** (AP History PYQ); Krishnadevaraya's = **Bhuvana Vijayam**. (NextGen also has Venkatapati II's literary meets at "Venkatapati Vilasam".)
[120:8:2] - **Aliya Rama Raya:** Krishnadevaraya's **son-in-law**, of the **Aravidu** family; killed **Salakam Tirumala** (1543); made **Sadasiva** a figurehead.
[120:8:3] - **Talikota** (January **1565**), also **Rakshasi-Tangadi** / Bannihatti: Rama Raya beheaded; Sadasiva and Tirumala fled to **Penukonda**. **Last Tuluva ruler = Sadasiva**.
[120:8:4] - **Latest – Sadasivakona inscriptions** (ASI, **June 2026**): three **trilingual** (Telugu, Tamil, Kannada) inscriptions of **1554 CE** in the **Seshachalam forest, Tirupati district**; record Sadasiva Raya's holy bath and transfer of **Kaanika** taxes from Gudimallam temple lands.

=== NOTES 1:121 | Vijayanagara Administration - Nayankara and Ayagar Systems, Amaram Tenure, Revenue and Army
## SUB 0 | Central Departments – Kandachara, Athavane and the Council
[121:0:0] # Departments
[121:0:1] - **Rayavachakam** records **nine departments**, each headed by a **Samprati**.
[121:0:2] TABLE head: Department | Function
  [121:0:2.0] Kandachara | Military – standing army, headed by the Senapati / Dalavayi
  [121:0:2.1] Athavane / Attavana | Revenue / finance
  [121:0:2.2] Bhandara (Sri Bhandara) | Treasury
  [121:0:2.3] Dharmasana | Judicial; Pradvivaka = judge
[121:0:3] - **Kandachara dealt with military affairs / defence** – asked ×7 (APPSC G2 2019; G2 Mains; SI 2016; AP History PYQ ×3; G2 PYQ). Not revenue, finance, land survey (Bhoomi Sisthu), police, justice or religious institutions; **Athavana** = revenue, **Dandaniti** / **Mansabdari** are distractors.
[121:0:4] # Officers and the council
[121:0:5] TABLE head: Officer | Role
  [121:0:5.0] Mahapradhani / Prime Minister | Chief minister presiding over the council of pradhanis; also called Sarva Sirah Pradhani, Sabhanayaka, Tantranayaka; e.g. Saluva Timmarusu
  [121:0:5.1] Dalavayi | Commander-in-chief
  [121:0:5.2] Rayasams | Secretaries (Kondamarasu, Ayyamarasu)
  [121:0:5.3] Karanikas | Scribes
[121:0:6] - **President of the imperial council:** the **Prime Minister** (AMVI 2009) – "Sabhanayaka" is only another name of the same office; the king was advised by, not president of, the council.
[121:0:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History – Vijayanagara p.28; NextGen previous papers p.15 (Kandachara); app notes 1:121 (sub 1)
## SUB 1 | Amara-Nayankara System and the Palegars
[121:1:0] # Amaram and Amaranayakas
[121:1:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [121:1:1.0] Amaram | Land granted to military chiefs instead of cash salary; the Rayas owned all land
  [121:1:1.1] Amaranayakas | Holders; also Nayaks, Palaiyagars, Poligars
  [121:1:1.2] Mahamandaleshwara | Their title; more autonomy than Durgadannayakas
[121:1:2] - **Nayakas were:** **military chiefs controlling particular territories** (CDS 2001) – not central ministers, cavalry soldiers or temple priests.
[121:1:3] - **Amaranayakas were NOT** "wise men who advised the rulers" – they were **military commanders**, **collected taxes**, and their **contingents** fought for the king.
[121:1:4] - **Administrative system (G1 2007):** the **Amaranayaka** (Amara-Nayankara) system – a military governor (Nayaka) given amarams for salary and army expenses.
[121:1:5] - Within the amaram: **collected taxes**, kept **law and order**, administered their territory (**palaiyam**).
[121:1:6] - **Obligations:** tribute (**Kappam**); a quota of troops, horses, elephants; attend court at **Mahanavami** with gifts; maintain forts, temples, irrigation.
[121:1:7] # Kakatiya nayankara vs Vijayanagara amara-nayankara
[121:1:8] TABLE head: Point | Kakatiya nayankara | Vijayanagara amara-nayankara
  [121:1:8.0] Origin | Prataparudra II | Grew out of the Kakatiya system
  [121:1:8.1] Scale | 75 or 77 nayakas | About 200 nayakas
  [121:1:8.2] Outcome | Discontent among older feudatories | Semi-independent, then independent Nayaka states after 1565 (Madurai, Tanjore, Keladi)
[121:1:9] # Palegars (Poligars)
[121:1:10] - **Origin:** **amara-nayakas** → Palegars when the centre weakened after **Talikota (1565)**; hereditary; **forerunners of the zamindars**.
[121:1:11] - **Who were they:** **territorial administrative and military governors** (UPPSC 2015) – not ordinary zamindars, Mahajans or traders.
[121:1:12] - **Duties under the Nayakas:** administer palaiyams, collect taxes, maintain law and order – **NOT building health and education centres**.
[121:1:13] - **Thomas Munro**, with **Campbell**, crushed the Rayalaseema Palegars and then introduced the **Ryotwari** system.
[121:1:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History – Vijayanagara p.28-29; app notes 1:121 (sub 4, 5, 6)
[121:1:15] - **Statements (I and III ✓):** Vijayanagara markets dealt in **spices, textiles and precious stones**; **amara-nayakas** were military commanders given territories by the Rayas; ✗ Krishnadevaraya's reign was **not** marked by strain within the imperial structure – it was the empire's peak.
## SUB 2 | Revenue and Taxes
[121:2:0] - **Chief feature of the financial system:** **land revenue** (BPSC 1999) – usually **1/6** of produce, **1/4 or 1/3** on fertile land; land-tax documents = **Adhuvanatantram**.
[121:2:1] # Land classes
[121:2:2] TABLE head: Term | Land
  [121:2:2.0] Nanja | Wet
  [121:2:2.1] Punja | Dry
  [121:2:2.2] Bagayati | Garden
  [121:2:2.3] Banjara | Waste
[121:2:3] - **Neerarambamu / Magani** (irrigated): tax in **grain**, higher; **Kaadarambamu / Metta** (dry): cash, lower.
[121:2:4] # Tax terms
[121:2:5] TABLE head: Tax | Meaning
  [121:2:5.0] Navidadere / Kondaju | Barbers' tax
  [121:2:5.1] Maneyanna / Manevana / Illari Pannu | House tax
  [121:2:5.2] Melivamsam | Plough tax (G1 match)
  [121:2:5.3] Tudimeya | Marriage tax (G1 match)
  [121:2:5.4] Meruka | Tax on sale and purchase (APPSC G2 2011)
  [121:2:5.5] Putrodaya Kanuka | Birth of a son
  [121:2:5.6] Kalyana Kanika | Wedding tax on both parties
  [121:2:5.7] Siddhaya | Fixed levy: Pinje (cotton ginners), Eidipi (toddy tappers), Jati (professions)
[121:2:6] - **G1 match:** Navidadere–barbers, Melivamsam–ploughs, Manevana–houses, Tudimeya–marriage = **a-II, b-III, c-IV, d-I**.
[121:2:7] - **Meruka ≠ Mera:** Mera = the Ayagars' harvest share; Merulu = the Talari's grain gifts; Meruka = sale–purchase tax (not Magani/Metta land or export–import).
[121:2:8] - **Marriage tax:** first remitted by **Saluva Narasimha**; **totally withdrawn by Krishnadevaraya** (Rocket; NextGen previous papers – APPSC G1 2010). ⚠ Some keys give Sadasiva Raya; Sadasiva's inscriptions (Rama Raya's regency) record the **barbers'** tax remission (at Kandoja's request).
[121:2:9] - Market (**santhalu**) tax rights auctioned to **Sunkandarlu**.
[121:2:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Sheets AP History (src/ap-history/rocket-ap-history.txt) p.18; NextGen previous papers (src/ap-history/nextgen-previous-papers.txt) p.45; NextGen AP History – Vijayanagara p.6, p.28; South Indian Inscriptions IX (Amarapuram 1544, barbers' exemption); app notes 1:121 (sub 8)
[121:2:11] - **Krishnadevaraya's taxation (UPSC 2016 – both ✓):** land-tax rate **fixed by the quality of the land**; **private owners of workshops paid an industries tax**.
## SUB 3 | Justice and Police
[121:3:0] - **Pradvivaka:** adviser to the king in the **judiciary**; judge who explained legal texts (APPSC G2 2019; G2 PYQ) – not military, revenue or espionage.
[121:3:1] - **Authoritative law book:** **Parasara Madhaviyam** – Madhavacharya's commentary on the Parashara Smriti (with Yajnavalkya) – not Amuktamalyada or the Arthashastra.
[121:3:2] - **Himsodbhava** = criminal; **Dhanodbhava** = civil.
[121:3:3] - The **Raya** was the supreme judge; ministers like **Timmarusu** ("Dharma Pratipalaka") also tried cases.
[121:3:4] - **Punishments:** treason – **death**; theft – **mutilation**; Brahmins – **life imprisonment** instead of death.
[121:3:5] # Police
[121:3:6] TABLE head: Level | Police (Kavali system)
  [121:3:6.0] Village | Talari
  [121:3:6.1] Sthala | Kavali Daru
  [121:3:6.2] Towns | Peta Kavalidaru
[121:3:7] - **Padikaval:** the system in which **police rights were sold** to individuals (AP History PYQ) – not Athavana or Kandachara.

## SUB 4 | Provincial and Village Administration – the Ayagars
[121:4:0] # Provincial units
[121:4:1] TABLE head: Unit | Head
  [121:4:1.0] Rajya / Mandala (province) – the largest unit | Prince (Sangama princes titled Odeyar) or Durga Dannayaka
  [121:4:1.1] Nadu / Seema (district) | Nadagauda; Parupatyagar
  [121:4:1.2] Sthala / Kampana | Reddi, Gauda, Sthalakaranam

[121:4:2] - **Biggest unit:** **Mandalam** (RPSC 2012) – not Kottam, Nadu or Kurram.
[121:4:3] - **Parupatyagar:** the king's officer of the nadu/sima who **collected revenue** and **repaired and maintained forts** (AP History PYQ).
[121:4:4] - About **17 rajyams** under **Achyutaraya**. Governors could **mint coins** and levy or remit taxes, and were **frequently transferred**.

  [121:4:5.2] Penukonda | Tirumala's headquarters after Talikota
  [121:4:5.3] Gooty (Gutti) | Fort raised to rajya status
[121:4:6] # Village – the Ayagars
[121:4:7] - **12 hereditary Ayagars** per village: Karanam (accountant), Reddi (head), Talari (watchman), Purohita, plus artisans – Kamsali, Kummari, Vadrangi, **Kase (mason / house-builder)**, Kammari, Chakali, Mangali.
[121:4:8] - **Pay:** no cash salary – tax-free **Manyam** lands and a harvest share **Mera**.
[121:4:9] TABLE head: Officer | Duties
  [121:4:9.0] Karanam | Accounts, land and crop registers
  [121:4:9.1] Reddi | Village head (Gauda / Gowda in Karnataka) and revenue collector
  [121:4:9.2] Talari | Watchman; collected Merulu
  [121:4:9.3] Begari | Supervised forced labour (vetti)
[121:4:10] - **Village headman:** **Gowda** (APPSC Jr Asst key) – the Karnataka name of the Andhra **Reddi**; Chowdhary and Parupatyadar are wrong.
[121:4:11] - **Brahmins as Karanams:** **Harihara I** followed the **Kakatiya** practice (APPSC Jr Asst) – the Sangamas inherited Kakatiya village administration.

## SUB 5 | Army, Forts and the Nature of the State
[121:5:0] # Army
[121:5:1] - Two divisions: the **Siddha (standing) army** under the **Dalavayi** (via Kandachara), paid every **4 months**, and the **Amaranayaka** contingents.
[121:5:2] - **Bukka I** = first to use **cannon**; **Saluva Narasimha** introduced **Samugaris** (gunmen).
[121:5:3] - **Horses** (Arabian, Persian) imported through **Goa, Honnavar, Bhatkal**.
[121:5:4] - **Four kinds of forts:** **Giri** (hill), **Sthala**, **Jala**, **Vana** – under **Durga Dannayakas**.
[121:5:5] - **Against sieges:** Vijayanagara enclosed **large agricultural tracts within the fortified area** (APPSC G1 2017; NCERT Themes II ch. 7) – granaries were the usual practice elsewhere.
[121:5:6] # Nature of the state
[121:5:7] - **King:** supreme head and fountain of justice, acting within **Dharma** and with **his ministers'** counsel.
[121:5:8] - **Nilakanta Sastri:** "**military administration**"; **Burton Stein:** "**segmentary state**".
[121:5:9] # Political maxims in Amuktamalyada
[121:5:10] TABLE head: Area | Maxim
  [121:5:10.0] Revenue | Bulk of revenue to the army
  [121:5:10.1] Trade | Improve harbours; encourage import of horses, elephants, gems; treat foreign merchants generously
  [121:5:10.2] Welfare | Build tanks; win over forest tribes rather than only crush them
  [121:5:10.3] Forts | Brahmins should command forts
[121:5:11] - It refers to the king's **campaigns** and gives political advice, but gives **no genealogy from Bukka**.

=== NOTES 1:124 | Folk Culture of Andhra - Burrakatha, Harikatha, Veedhi Natakam, Oggu Katha, Tolu Bommalata
## SUB 0 | Folk Entertainment under Vijayanagara – Yakshaganam and Tolu Bommalata
[124:0:0] TABLE head: Form | Audience under Vijayanagara
  [124:0:0.0] Yakshaganam | Originated in the royal courts; entertained the court and elites
  [124:0:0.1] Tolu Bommalata (leather shadow puppets) | The chief entertainment of rural Andhra besides Yakshagana (APPSC G2 2019 ×2) – not Harikatha, dramas or Burrakatha
  [124:0:0.2] Vipra-Vinodins | Brahmin jugglers for Brahmin households
[124:0:1] - **Yakshaganam** was performed by the **Jakkula (Jakkula Varu)** community; **Jakkula Purandhri** is a character of the **Yakshaganam** (APPSC G1 2004) – not Perini, Oggu Katha or puppetry.
[124:0:2] - **Koravanji** = precursor of the Telugu Yakshagana; **Sugreeva Vijayam** by **Kandukuri Rudrakavi** = first (published) Telugu Yakshaganam; performed in Telugu with rich costumes and Puranic stories.
[124:0:3] - **Veedhi Natakam / Veedhi Bhagavatam** (street dance-drama) → forerunner of **Kuchipudi**.
[124:0:4] - Under the **Qutb Shahis** Yakshaganam became folk theatre and Kuchipudi flourished; folk forms later carried Telugu consciousness to the villages in the **Andhra Movement**.
[124:0:5] ~~ Sources: Singhania Art & Culture 03 p.84 (Yakshagana – Vijayanagar courts, Jakkula Varu), p.100 (Tholu Bommalata); NextGen previous papers; app notes 1:124 (sub 0, 1), 1:123 (sub 9)
[124:0:6] - **Not Andhra:** **Lavani** (dance-song with the dholki, part of **Tamasha**) is from **Maharashtra**.
## SUB 1 | Burrakatha
[124:1:0] - **Earlier name: Jangam Katha** (APPSC JL; Singhania) – not Kuchipudi, Bhamakalapam or Veeranatyam; in Rayalaseema called **Thandana Katha**.
[124:1:1] - Folk narrative art of **Andhra Pradesh** (and Telangana); birthplace **Guntur**.
[124:1:2] - Troupe of **three**: the main narrator (with the **burra / tambura**) and **two co-performers (Vantalu)**; instruments Gummata, Burra, Tambura, Ukki; chorus "Thandana Taana".
