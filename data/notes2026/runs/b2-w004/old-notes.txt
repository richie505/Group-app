=== NOTES 2:4 | Preamble – Text, Keywords, 42ND Amendment and Its Status
## SUB 0 | 42nd Amendment, 1976 – Socialist, Secular, Integrity
[4:0:0] # What the 42nd Amendment did to the Preamble
[4:0:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [4:0:1.0] Words added | Socialist, Secular, Integrity (three words)
  [4:0:1.1] Description of India | "Sovereign Democratic Republic" → "Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic"
  [4:0:1.2] Fraternity clause | "unity of the Nation" → "unity and integrity of the Nation"
  [4:0:1.3] Year / context | 1976, during the Emergency (1975-77), Indira Gandhi government
  [4:0:1.4] Based on | Swaran Singh Committee
  [4:0:1.5] Nickname | "Mini Constitution" – it changed the Preamble, the Seventh Schedule and 53 Articles
[4:0:2] - **Only amendment of the Preamble so far:** the **42nd (1976)**; asked about 25 times (APPSC G2 2008, 2011, 2025 Mains; AEE 2008; Endowment 2008; JL 2003; Polytechnic 2019; Degree Lecturers 2020; Constable 2016; SSC, NDA 2018, UPPSC, MPPSC 2023).
[4:0:3] - **⚠ Trap:** not the 1st, 2nd, 17th, 24th, 25th, 38th, 40th, 41st, 43rd, 44th, 45th, 46th, 52nd, 73rd or 86th Amendment ("1976 Second Amendment" is wrong).
[4:0:4] - **'Socialist' and 'Secular' were inserted between 'Sovereign' and 'Democratic'.**
[4:0:5] - **Integrity** was **not** in the Preamble in **1975**; it came in **1976** (SSC 2019; UPPSC 2006). The word **"unity"** was in the **original** text – only "and integrity" was added (a question saying "'Unity of the Nation' was added by the 42nd" means this change).
[4:0:6] - **NOT added by the 42nd:** **Fraternity**, **Sovereign**, Democratic, Republic (all original); **Gram Swarajya**, Dignity of person, Privacy, Liberal are not 42nd words.
[4:0:7] - **Assertion-reason:** "India is a sovereign socialist secular democratic republic" (A) is true and its reason – the 42nd Act **substituted** these words for "Sovereign Democratic Republic" (R) – correctly explains it.
[4:0:8] # Other changes of the 42nd Amendment (for "not associated" items)
[4:0:9] - Added **Fundamental Duties** (Part IVA) and new DPSPs; **curtailed** judicial review in some matters but did **not abolish** it entirely.
[4:0:10] - **Minerva Mills (1980)** struck down its Art. 368 clauses **(4) and (5)** (unlimited amending power, exclusion of judicial review) and the clause giving **DPSPs full priority over all Fundamental Rights** – restoring the balance of Parts III and IV.
[4:0:11] > Status today: in **Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India (25 Nov 2024)** the Supreme Court **upheld 'Socialist' and 'Secular'** and dismissed the challenges to their insertion (as of 2026; the words were again debated politically in Jul 2025).
[4:0:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.11 (42nd Amendment), p.230, p.232; Rocket Polity #14 p.36-37; app notes 2:4 sub 7; sci.gov.in (Balram Singh, 25 Nov 2024)
## SUB 1 | We, the People – Source of Authority, Date and Adoption
[4:1:0] - **Source of the Constitution / of political power:** the **people of India** – the Preamble opens "**We, the People of India**", showing **people's sovereignty**; the Constitution derives its authority from the people (APPSC G1 2002, G2, AEE 2012, CDPO 2013; HWO 2017).
[4:1:1] - **⚠ Trap:** not the Constitution, Parliament, "Parliament and State Legislatures", the President or the Supreme Court.
[4:1:2] - The Preamble **indicates the source** from which the Constitution comes (APPSC G2 2011).
[4:1:3] - **"We, the People of India"** is used in the **Preamble**, not in the FRs, DPSPs or Citizenship part.
[4:1:4] - **Enacting clause:** "We, the people of India … **in our Constituent Assembly** … do hereby **adopt, enact and give to ourselves** this Constitution" – the Constitution is declared adopted and enacted by the **people of India**, acting through the **Constituent Assembly** (when only the CA is offered, it is the answer for "adopted by").
[4:1:5] # The date
[4:1:6] - **Date in the Preamble:** **26 November 1949** = **adoption** of the Constitution (drafting completed; signed by the President of the CA) – not 26 January 1950 (commencement), 24 January 1950 or 13 December 1946.
[4:1:7] - **Commencement:** **26 January 1950** (some provisions – citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament – took effect on 26 Nov 1949).
[4:1:8] - **Constitution Day:** **26 November**, observed **since 2015** (Constitution Day 2019 = 26 Nov) (APPSC G2 Mains 2017; JL 2020).
[4:1:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.136-137, p.229-230; Rocket Polity #14 p.36-37; app notes 2:4 sub 0-1
## SUB 2 | Is the Preamble Part of the Constitution? – Cases and Legal Status
[4:2:0] TABLE head: Case | Year | Held on the Preamble
  [4:2:0.0] Berubari Union (President's reference) | 1960 | Not a part of the Constitution (the first such ruling); a "key to the minds of the makers"; aid to interpretation where an Article is ambiguous
  [4:2:0.1] Golaknath | 1967 | About amending Fundamental Rights; not the Preamble case (distractor)
  [4:2:0.2] Kesavananda Bharati | 1973 | Is a part of the Constitution (overruled Berubari); amendable under Art. 368 but the basic structure cannot be destroyed; 13 judges
  [4:2:0.3] S.R. Bommai | 1994 | Relied on the Preamble – secularism a basic feature; Preamble treated as integral part
  [4:2:0.4] LIC of India | 1995 | Reaffirmed the Preamble is an integral part
  [4:2:0.5] Minerva Mills | 1980 | Struck down 42nd Amendment Art. 368 clauses – not a Preamble-status case
[4:2:1] - **"Preamble is a part" =** **Kesavananda Bharati** (APPSC TWO 2012, G2 2008, ZSWO 2025); with no Kesavananda option, **Bommai** (UPPSC 2012); A.K. Gopalan, Maneka Gandhi, Mohini Jain, Sajjan Singh are distractors.
[4:2:2] - **"Not a part" =** **Berubari, 1960** (not 1968; Kesavananda was 1973, not 1970).
[4:2:3] # Legal nature
[4:2:4] - **Non-justiciable** – **not enforceable** in a court of law (UPPSC 2019).
[4:2:5] - **Neither a source of power** to the legislature **nor a prohibition** on its powers; confers no power and creates no right by itself.
[4:2:6] - **Part of the Constitution but has no legal effect independently of other parts** (UPSC 2020).
[4:2:7] - **Cannot override** the specific provisions of the Constitution; where a provision is clear, the Preamble yields.
[4:2:8] - **Aid to interpretation:** may be invoked to determine the **ambit of Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles** (APPSC G1).
[4:2:9] - **Amendable** under Art. 368 (done once, 1976) – so "it cannot be amended" is false; "not a part of the Constitution" is false.
[4:2:10] - **Enacted after** the rest of the Constitution, so that it conformed to the text – "enacted before the entire Constitution" is false (APPSC G1 2023 → I and III incorrect).
[4:2:11] - Reflects the **philosophy, ideals and objectives** of the Constitution – the **mind of the makers** (UPSC 2017) and the **objective of the Constitution** (UPPSC 2013) are reflected in the Preamble, not in FRs, DPSP or FDs.
[4:2:12] > Statement sets: "Objectives Resolution became the Preamble ✓; non-justiciable ✓; can't be amended ✗; can't override specific provisions ✓" → **I, II and IV** (UPPSC 2009, Panchayat Secretary). "Not a part ✗; amended only under Art. 368 ✓; three words added in 1976 ✓" → **B and C**.
[4:2:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.159 (cases), p.229 (status); Rocket Polity #14 p.37; app notes 2:4 sub 4-5
[4:2:14] - **S.R. Bommai (1994):** a State government acting against **secularism** can be dismissed under Art. 356.
[4:2:15] - **Waman Rao (1981):** basic-structure review applies to amendments made **after 24 April 1973** (the Kesavananda judgment date).
[4:2:16] - **Secularism** was part of the basic structure **even before** the word 'Secular' was inserted in 1976 (Kesavananda 1973; Bommai 1994).
## SUB 3 | Preamble Text – Order of Words, Original 1949 Text, Words Not in It
[4:3:0] # The text (as amended in 1976)
[4:3:1] - **WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA**, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a **SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC** and to secure to all its citizens: **JUSTICE**, social, economic and political; **LIBERTY** of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; **EQUALITY** of status and of opportunity; and to promote among them all **FRATERNITY** assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation; IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.
[4:3:2] - **Order:** **Sovereign → Socialist → Secular → Democratic → Republic** (code C, A, D, B for Socialist A, Democratic B, Sovereign C, Secular D); objectives in order **Justice → Liberty → Equality → Fraternity**.
[4:3:3] - **India is declared secular by the Preamble** (Fundamental Rights, DPSP and 9th Schedule do not "describe" India as secular).
[4:3:4] - **Equality of** → **status** (and of opportunity); not "of nationality", "religion" or "community".
[4:3:5] # Original 1949 text vs now
[4:3:6] TABLE head: Item | 26 Nov 1949 / 26 Jan 1950 | Now (after 1976)
  [4:3:6.0] Description of India | "Sovereign Democratic Republic" | "Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic"
  [4:3:6.1] Fraternity clause | "unity of the Nation" | "unity and integrity of the Nation"
  [4:3:6.2] Words absent in 1949 | Socialist, Secular, Integrity | added
[4:3:7] - **Exact status on 26 January 1950:** a **Sovereign Democratic Republic** (UPSC 2021).
[4:3:8] - **Not in the 1949 Preamble:** Socialism / Socialist, Secular, Integrity; "Reliable" and "Non-religious" were never in it; **Republic** was in the original (UPPSC 2009; Uttarakhand 2024 → 2, 3, 4).
[4:3:9] # Words NOT in the Preamble at all
[4:3:10] - **Federal / federation** (APPSC Deputy Surveyors 2012; UPPSC 2021) – "Quasi-federal" is not in it either.
[4:3:11] - **Freedom** (it uses **Liberty**), **Right** (as a word), **Public Welfare** / **Welfare State**, **Adult franchise**, **Fundamental Duties**, "**People's**".
[4:3:12] - **Religious Justice**, **Natural** or **Cultural** justice (justice is only **social, economic, political**).
[4:3:13] - **Political Liberty** and **Economic Liberty** as categories; liberty "of action" (liberty is only of **thought, expression, belief, faith, worship**) (UPSC 2017; APPSC G2).
[4:3:14] - Words that ARE in it: Fraternity, Equality, Justice, **Unity**, Integrity, Thought, Opportunity, **Dignity**, Republic, Sovereign.
[4:3:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.229-230 (text, 42nd words); Rocket Polity #14 p.36-37; app notes 2:4 sub 1
## SUB 4 | Keywords and Objectives – Meaning
[4:4:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:4:0.0] Sovereign | Supreme authority; free internally and externally; not subject to any external authority
  [4:4:0.1] Socialist | Democratic socialism – a mixed economy (public and private sectors); a blend of Marxism and Gandhism, leaning towards Gandhian socialism
  [4:4:0.2] Secular | Equal respect for all religions; no State religion; before 1976 the word appeared only in Art. 25(2)(a); the term is not defined in any Article
  [4:4:0.3] Democratic | Government by the people; representative parliamentary democracy; covers political, social and economic democracy; universal adult franchise and rule of law are its marks
  [4:4:0.4] Republic | Elected head of State (the President, indirectly elected for 5 years); no hereditary element; public offices open to all
[4:4:1] - **Republic ≠ PM:** India is a republic because its **head of State (President) is elected** – not because ministers are responsible to Parliament or hold office at the President's pleasure.
[4:4:2] - **Philosophy of the Constitution:** welfare state, socialist state, political equality – but **not a Communist State** (UPPSC 2015).
[4:4:3] - India is a **democratic polity, a sovereign State, a democratic society and a welfare state** – all four (UPPSC 2012); the **welfare-state** idea is found in the **Preamble and the DPSP** (UPPSC 1997).
[4:4:4] - **Not valid (APPSC G1 2024):** "'democratic' is a mix of British Fabian Socialism and American Populism"; "Republic = elected Prime Minister"; "Liberty is neither absolute nor qualified"; "Fraternity originally denoted only dignity of the individual" → **none valid**.
[4:4:5] # Objectives
[4:4:6] TABLE head: Objective | Of / kinds
  [4:4:6.0] Justice | Social, economic and political – three kinds
  [4:4:6.1] Liberty | Thought, expression, belief, faith and worship – five
  [4:4:6.2] Equality | Status and opportunity – civic, political, economic dimensions
  [4:4:6.3] Fraternity | Sense of brotherhood; assures dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the Nation; promoted by single citizenship
[4:4:7] - **Goals secured to all:** justice (social, economic, political), liberty of thought and expression, equality of opportunity and **dignity of the individual** – **all** of them (UPPSC 1998).
[4:4:8] - **Liberty** = **absence of restraints plus opportunity to develop one's personality**; UPSC 2019 "most appropriate definition" = **opportunity to develop oneself fully** (positive liberty); liberty is not licence to do whatever one likes.
[4:4:9] - **Law and liberty (UPSC 2018):** "**If there are no laws, there is no liberty**" – law is the condition of liberty.
[4:4:10] - **Political theory links:** popular/political **sovereignty** – **Rousseau** (general will); **distributive justice** – **Aristotle**.
[4:4:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.166 (secular state), p.230-232 (keywords, justice, liberty, equality, fraternity); Rocket Polity #14 p.36; app notes 2:4 sub 2-3; UPSC CSE 2018, 2019 official keys
[4:4:12] - **Kinds of justice:** economic factors are treated as a source of justice (**economic justice**); **social justice** = equal treatment of all, **equality before law**, no discrimination by caste, creed or sex; **political justice** = **political equality** (equal political rights and voice) – not inequality.
## SUB 5 | Making and Sources of the Preamble's Ideals
[4:5:0] - **Basis:** the **Objectives Resolution** of **Jawaharlal Nehru** – moved **13 December 1946**, adopted **22 January 1947**; its modified version became the Preamble (Nehru "proposed the Preamble"; not Ambedkar, B.N. Rau or Gandhi) (APPSC ASWO; Uttarakhand 2006; APPSC JA).
[4:5:1] - The Constitution of India was **not** the first to begin with a Preamble (the **US** Constitution did); the Preamble has been amended **once**, not twice.
[4:5:2] TABLE head: Element | Source
  [4:5:2.0] Idea of a Preamble; "We, the people" | USA
  [4:5:2.1] Language of the Preamble | Australia
  [4:5:2.2] Liberty, Equality, Fraternity (the "trinity"); Republic | French Revolution (1789) / French Constitution
  [4:5:2.3] Justice – social, economic and political | Russian Revolution (1917), USSR
  [4:5:2.4] Fraternity (possible) | Art. 1 of the UDHR (1948)
[4:5:3] - **⚠ Trap:** Liberty-equality-fraternity is **not** from the Russian, American or Glorious Revolution or the UN Charter; justice is **not** from the French Revolution.
[4:5:4] - **Third reading** of the Constitution: **17-26 November 1949**; Preamble enacted last.

[4:6:0] TABLE head: Description | Person
  [4:6:0.0] "Identity card of the Constitution" | N.A. Palkhivala
  [4:6:0.1] "Political horoscope" / "horoscope of our sovereign democratic republic" | K.M. Munshi
  [4:6:0.2] "Key-note" to the Constitution | Ernest Barker
  [4:6:0.3] "Key to the minds of the makers" | Supreme Court, Berubari (1960)
  [4:6:0.4] "Soul" of the Constitution | Thakurdas Bhargava (the Preamble is called the soul of the Constitution)

[4:6:1] - **"Key of the Constitution"** – the standard attributions are **Ernest Barker** ("key-note") and the **Supreme Court** (Berubari, Sajjan Singh: "key to open the mind of the makers"); no reliable source gives it to Gandhi, Motilal Nehru, Ambedkar or Indira Gandhi.
[4:6:2] - **Critic of inserting 'Secular' (1976):** constitutional scholar **D.D. Basu** held the insertion unnecessary and productive of vagueness – "productive of more mischief than benefit" (owner to confirm against Basu's *Introduction to the Constitution of India*).

=== NOTES 2:5 | Parts, Schedules and Articles of the Constitution
## SUB 0 | Eighth Schedule – Languages
[5:0:0] # The 22 scheduled languages
[5:0:1] - **Eighth Schedule = languages recognised by the Constitution** (Arts. **344(1) and 351**) – the "regional languages" / "official languages" schedule; **not** the Fourth, Sixth, Seventh or Ninth.
[5:0:2] - **Number:** originally **14**, now **22** (not 18, 21 or 24).
[5:0:3] - **The 22:** Assamese, Bengali, **Bodo**, **Dogri**, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, **Kashmiri**, **Konkani**, **Maithili**, Malayalam, **Manipuri**, Marathi, **Nepali**, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, **Santhali**, **Sindhi**, Tamil, Telugu, **Urdu**.
[5:0:4] - **NOT in the Eighth Schedule:** **English**, **Rajasthani**, **Bhojpuri**, **Gondi**, Bhutia (Bhoti), Tulu, Chhattisgarhi, Gor Boli (Lambadi) – all asked as the odd one out.
[5:0:5] - Hindi, Sanskrit, Urdu, Punjabi, Gujarati, Kashmiri were among the **original 14**.
[5:0:6] # Languages added by amendment
[5:0:7] TABLE head: Amendment | Year | Languages added
  [5:0:7.0] 21st | 1967 | Sindhi
  [5:0:7.1] 71st | 1992 | Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali
  [5:0:7.2] 92nd | 2003 | Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali
[5:0:8] - **71st ≠ Maithili:** Maithili came with the **92nd** (UPSC 2024: 71st = Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali only).
[5:0:9] - **Konkani** entered in **1992** (not 2003). 96th Amendment (2011) renamed Oriya as **Odia** (no new language).
[5:0:10] - In **1990** Sindhi was the only language added by amendment; since 1992 Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali also are.
[5:0:11] # Official Language Commission (Art. 344)
[5:0:12] - First **Official Language Commission** set up **7 June 1955** under Art. 344 – chairman **B.G. Kher**; report 1956, reviewed by a parliamentary committee under **G.B. Pant**; **Official Languages Act 1963**.
[5:0:13] - Official language provisions = **Part XVII, Arts. 343–351**.
[5:0:14] # Demands for inclusion (current linkage)
[5:0:15] - No fixed criteria for inclusion (**Pahwa Committee 1996**, **Sitakant Mohapatra Committee 2003**); demands are raised in Parliament – e.g. **Chhattisgarhi** in the Rajya Sabha (CGPSC 2019, 2020) → the **8th Schedule**.
[5:0:16] - **Jun 2026**: fresh demands to include **Bhojpuri** (Konkani, Maithili, Santali are already in).
[5:0:17] - **Jul 2026**: Ministry of Tribal Affairs told Parliament of continuing demands to include **Gor Boli (Lambadi)**, the **Banjara** language of AP and Telangana (PIB).
[5:0:18] - **Sep 2026**: Karnataka made **Tulu** an additional official language in Dakshina Kannada and Udupi – Tulu is **not** in the Eighth Schedule.
[5:0:19] > Odd-one-out pattern: English (3 times), Rajasthani, Gondi, Bhojpuri, Bhutani – none is scheduled; Urdu, Sindhi, Nepali, Dogri, Kashmiri, Sanskrit are.
[5:0:20] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.157; Indian_Polity Part 1 ch.2 (8th Schedule list); PMF Lens Jul 2026 1.23; Praveen CA360 Sep 2026 (Tulu); Official Languages Commission – Lok Sabha debates 5 May 1955 and Report 1956
[5:0:21] - **English as a State official language:** **Nagaland** (also Meghalaya and Arunachal Pradesh) – though English is not in the Eighth Schedule.
[5:0:22] - **Official-language Articles:** **Art. 343** – **Hindi in Devanagari** = official language of the **Union** (from **26 Jan 1950**; adopted 14 Sep 1949), English for 15 years; **Art. 345** – a **State legislature** may adopt Hindi or any language of the State; **Art. 346** – Centre–State / inter-State communication; **Art. 348** – language of the SC, HCs and laws.
[5:0:23] - **Official Languages Act, 1963 (amended 1967):** English may continue alongside Hindi for official Union purposes **indefinitely** (beyond the 15 years of Art. 343(2)).

## SUB 2 | Ninth Schedule – Land Reforms and Judicial Review
[5:2:0] # Basics
[5:2:1] - Added by the **First Amendment Act, 1951** – the **first Schedule added** – during the prime ministership of **Jawaharlal Nehru** (not the 8th, 9th or 42nd Amendment).
[5:2:2] - Linked with **Article 31B**: Acts placed in it cannot be challenged for violating Fundamental Rights.
[5:2:3] - Purpose: to protect **state land-reform and zamindari-abolition laws** from judicial scrutiny – so **land reform laws are in the 9th Schedule** (not the 7th, 8th or 10th).
[5:2:4] - Acts: **13** originally → about **284** now (NextGen counts 282).
[5:2:5] - **I.R. Coelho (2007):** laws added **after 24 April 1973** (the Kesavananda date) are **open to judicial review** on the basic-structure test.
[5:2:6] # Tamil Nadu 69% reservation
[5:2:7] - The **Tamil Nadu Reservation Act, 1993/94** (69% reservation for SCs, STs and OBCs – above the 50% Indra Sawhney limit) was placed in the **IX Schedule** by the **76th Amendment Act, 1994** to shield it from judicial review.

=== NOTES 2:6 | Union and Its Territory – Articles 1-4, New States and Cession
## SUB 0 | Articles 1–4 – Union, Territory and the First Schedule
[6:0:0] # What each Article says
[6:0:1] TABLE head: Article | Subject
  [6:0:1.0] 1 | "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States"; States and territories in the First Schedule; Art. 1(3) territory of India
  [6:0:1.1] 2 | Admission or establishment of new States (not part of the Union of India)
  [6:0:1.2] 3 | Formation of new States; alteration of areas, boundaries or names of existing States
  [6:0:1.3] 4 | Laws under Arts. 2 and 3 amend the First and Fourth Schedules; Art. 4(2): not deemed an amendment under Art. 368
[6:0:2] - Part I = **Articles 1–4**; the Constitution calls the Centre the **"Union"** (Union Government, Union List), not "Centre".
[6:0:3] - **"Union" not "Federation" (Ambedkar):** the Indian federation is **not the result of an agreement among States**, and **no State has the right to secede** – "an **indestructible Union of destructible States**".
[6:0:4] # Union of India vs Territory of India
[6:0:5] TABLE head: Union of India | Territory of India (Art. 1(3))
  [6:0:5.0] States only – members of the federal system sharing powers with the Union | States + Union Territories + territories that may be acquired
[6:0:6] - So **UTs are covered by "Territory of India" and not by "Union of India"** (APPSC G1: **Only IV**); both expressions are in the Constitution, and the **First Schedule** lists **States and UTs**.
[6:0:7] - Grama Sachivalayam 2020: the **Union of India** includes **States only (A only)**.
[6:0:8] - **Acquiring territory:** India as a sovereign State acquires foreign territory **"as understood in public international law"** – cession, conquest, occupation (**N. Masthan Sahib v. Chief Commissioner, Pondicherry**); e.g. **Pondicherry, Goa, Daman and Diu, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Sikkim**. Such territory need not join an existing State (it may be a UT).
[6:0:9] # First Schedule
[6:0:10] - **First Schedule = names of the States and UTs and their territorial extent** → it **must be amended to create a new State** (UPSC 2001; APPSC G2 Mains 2019; APPSC HO 2021). Fourth Schedule = Rajya Sabha seats (also amended).
[6:0:11] # Article 3 – power and procedure
[6:0:12] - **Power vests in Parliament** (not the President, the Council of Ministers or an SRC): form a new State by **separating** territory, **uniting** States or parts, uniting territory to a part of a State; **increase or diminish** area; alter **boundaries or name**.
[6:0:13] - **Admission of new States is NOT under Art. 3** – it is **Art. 2**; creating a new State out of existing States is **Art. 3** (CGPSC 2014).
[6:0:14] - **Procedure:** Bill only on the **President's recommendation**; President **refers it to the State legislature concerned** for views within a time limit; **Parliament is not bound** by the views; **no fresh reference** needed for each amendment to the Bill; for a **UT, no reference** at all.
[6:0:15] - **Consent of the State is not required** to alter its boundaries – Parliament acts "without their consent".
[6:0:16] - Passed by a **simple majority** in the ordinary legislative process (**Art. 4(2)**).
[6:0:17] - **Renaming Kerala → 'Keralam'**: Union Cabinet approval began the **Article 3** procedure.
[6:0:18] - Parliament **cannot cede** territory under Art. 3 – cession needs an **Art. 368** amendment (see Cession).
[6:0:19] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams (full text) p.233-235, p.237 (Articles 1-4 table); Indian_Polity Part 1 (src/polity/indian-polity-part1.txt) p.47-50 (Union vs Territory, Masthan Sahib, Art. 2, Art. 3); app notes 2:6 sub 0-1
[6:0:20] - **House of introduction:** an Art. 3 Bill may be introduced in **either House** of Parliament – there is no rule that it goes first to the Rajya Sabha.
[6:0:21] - **Art. 3 Explanation I:** in clauses (a)–(e) 'State' **includes a Union territory** – an interpretive explanation, not itself a provision for forming new States.
[6:0:22] - **'State' defined (political science):** a community of persons **permanently occupying a definite territory**, **independent of external control** (sovereign) and possessing an **organised government**.

[7:0:1] TABLE head: Mode | Section | Key condition
  [7:0:1.0] Birth | 3 | jus soli (modified) – see periods below
  [7:0:1.1] Descent | 4 | Born outside India, either parent an Indian citizen at birth; birth registered at an Indian consulate – jus sanguinis
  [7:0:1.2] Registration | 5 | PIO ordinarily resident 7 years; spouses of citizens; minor children of citizens
  [7:0:1.3] Naturalisation | 6 | 12 years (11 of the preceding 14 + the last 12 months); oath of allegiance; waivable for distinguished service to science, art, literature, world peace
  [7:0:1.4] Incorporation of territory | 7 | Centre notifies who become citizens when a territory joins – Goa, Pondicherry, Sikkim
[7:0:2] - **Five modes:** **Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, Incorporation of territory** (Act passed **1955**).
[7:0:3] - **Not modes:** **property or land ownership**, **depositing money in Indian banks**, **ethnic identity**; **marriage** is not a separate mode (only a registration category). **Domicile** was a condition only under Art. 5.
[7:0:4] # Citizenship by birth – three periods
[7:0:5] TABLE head: Born in India | Rule
  [7:0:5.0] 26 Jan 1950 – 30 Jun 1987 | Citizen regardless of parents' nationality (1986 Amendment)
  [7:0:5.1] 1 Jul 1987 – 2 Dec 2004 | At least one parent an Indian citizen
  [7:0:5.2] From 3 Dec 2004 | Both parents citizens, or one Indian and the other not an illegal migrant
[7:0:6] - So today a person born in India is a citizen **only if at least one parent is Indian**, subject to other conditions (APPSC ASWO key).
[7:0:7] - **Excluded** from birth: children of **foreign diplomats** and **enemy aliens**.
[7:0:8] # Residence periods – then and now
[7:0:9] - Before the 2003 Act (in force 3 Dec 2004) naturalisation needed **1 year + 4 of the preceding 7 years = 5 years** (APPSC G1 2003 key: 5 years); **now 12 years**; registration of PIO now **7 years**; CAA communities **5 years**.

## SUB 1 | Part II – Articles 5 to 11
[7:1:0] # Constitutional framework
[7:1:1] - Citizenship = **Part II, Articles 5–11** (Part I = Arts. 1–4 Union and territory; Part III = Arts. 12–35 Fundamental Rights).
[7:1:2] - Part II only **identifies who became citizens at the commencement (26 January 1950)**; acquisition and loss afterwards are left to **Parliament (Art. 11)** → **Citizenship Act, 1955**.
[7:1:3] - **Arts. 5–8** are the Articles that define the categories of citizens at commencement (APPSC G2 Mains 2017 key "Articles 5 to 8"); Arts. 9–11 are general provisions.
[7:1:4] TABLE head: Article | Deals with
  [7:1:4.0] 5 | Citizenship at the commencement – domiciled in India + (a) born in India, or (b) either parent born in India, or (c) ordinarily resident 5 years before 1950
  [7:1:4.1] 6 | Migrants from Pakistan to India – cut-off 19 July 1948 (after it, registration after 6 months' residence)
  [7:1:4.2] 7 | Migrants to Pakistan after 1 March 1947 who returned under a resettlement permit
  [7:1:4.3] 8 | Persons of Indian origin residing outside India – register at an Indian diplomatic or consular office
  [7:1:4.4] 9 | Voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship → not a citizen
  [7:1:4.5] 10 | Continuance of the rights of citizenship, subject to Parliament's law
  [7:1:4.6] 11 | Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by law
[7:1:5] - **Wrong statement trap:** "Articles **4 to 10** deal with migrants to Pakistan and persons of Indian origin abroad" – false (that is **Arts. 7 and 8**; Part II is 5–11).
[7:1:6] - Art. 6 = migrants **FROM** Pakistan; Art. 7 = migrants **TO** Pakistan.
[7:1:7] # What citizenship gives
[7:1:8] - Citizen-only rights: **Arts. 15, 16, 19, 29, 30**; the **right to vote** for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies; the **right to become a member** of Parliament and State legislatures – "citizenship means **full civil and political rights**" (CDS 2014: all three).
[7:1:9] - Citizen-only offices: **President, Vice-President, SC/HC judges, Governor, Attorney General, Advocate General**.
[7:1:10] - **President:** in India both a citizen **by birth and a naturalised** citizen are eligible; in the **USA only a natural-born** citizen.

## SUB 2 | Single Citizenship – No Dual Citizenship
[7:2:0] - India: **single citizenship** and a **single domicile** for the Union and the States – allegiance only to the Union; **no separate State citizenship** (UPSC 2021: "one citizenship and one domicile" is the only correct statement).
[7:2:1] - **Source:** **Britain**; **Canada** also has single citizenship.
[7:2:2] - **USA** and **Switzerland**: **dual** – national + State/cantonal (UPSC 2005: "India and USA both have single citizenship" is false).
[7:2:3] - India does **not allow dual citizenship** with another country (Art. 9, Sec. 9): a citizen who **acquires another country's citizenship loses Indian citizenship** – APPSC A–R: both true, R explains A (key).
[7:2:4] TABLE head: Country | Citizenship
  [7:2:4.0] India, Britain, Canada | Single
  [7:2:4.1] USA, Switzerland | Dual (national + state)

[7:3:0] # Amendments to the 1955 Act
[7:3:1] TABLE head: Year | Change
  [7:3:1.0] 1986 | Birth from 1 July 1987 needs one parent an Indian citizen
  [7:3:1.1] 2003 | Birth rule from 3 Dec 2004 (other parent not an illegal migrant); Overseas Citizenship for PIOs of 16 specified countries (Sec. 7A); Sec. 14A (National Register)

[7:3:3] - Basis moved from **jus soli to jus sanguinis**.
[7:3:4] # CAA, 2019

  [7:3:5.0] Lok Sabha | 9 December 2019

  [7:3:5.2] President's assent | 12 December 2019
  [7:3:5.3] Rules effective | 11 March 2024
[7:3:6] - **Purpose:** citizenship for **persecuted minorities** – **Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi, Christian** (**six**; Muslims not covered) from **Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Pakistan** (not Sri Lanka) – A–R: both true, R explains A.

## SUB 5 | NRI, PIO and OCI
[7:5:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [7:5:0.0] NRI | Indian citizen ordinarily residing outside India; holds an Indian passport
  [7:5:0.1] PIO | Foreign citizen who, or any of whose ancestors, was an Indian national; card merged into OCI (2015)
  [7:5:0.2] OCI | Person registered as an OCI Cardholder under Sec. 7A of the Citizenship Act, 1955
  [7:5:0.3] Commonwealth citizen | Citizen of a member country of the Commonwealth of Nations
  [7:5:0.4] Single citizenship | One citizenship for all of India
[7:5:1] - **OCI eligible:** foreign citizens of Indian origin, their **children/minor children of Indian citizens**, **foreign-origin spouses** of Indian citizens or OCIs.
[7:5:2] - **OCI barred:** if the person, a **parent or grandparent** was a citizen of **Pakistan or Bangladesh** – so an Indian who migrated to Pakistan after Partition is not eligible.
[7:5:3] - **OCI gets:** **lifelong (multiple-entry) visa**; OCI card alone enough to enter India (since 2016). **Not:** voting, contesting elections, constitutional offices; OCIs are **not citizens**.
[7:5:4] - **2015 Amendment** did **not** give NRIs any new voting right; **2026 Rules:** e-OCI cards.

## SUB 6 | Loss of Citizenship – Three Modes
[7:6:0] TABLE head: Mode | Section | How
  [7:6:0.0] Renunciation | 8 | Voluntary, by declaration; minor children lose it too but may resume within 1 year of majority
  [7:6:0.1] Termination | 9 | Automatic on voluntarily acquiring foreign citizenship
  [7:6:0.2] Deprivation | 10 | Compulsory, by the Central Government; only citizens by naturalisation, registration or Art. 5(c)
[7:6:1] - **Deprivation grounds:** fraud; **disloyalty** to the Constitution; helping an enemy in war; **2 years' imprisonment within 5 years** of naturalisation/registration; **7 years' continuous residence abroad**.
[7:6:2] - **A naturalised citizen can be deprived** (Sec. 10) – "never deprived" is false.
[7:6:3] - **No effect** on citizenship: **marrying a foreigner**, **criticising the government**, bank deposits, property → loss only by **renouncing** or **acquiring foreign citizenship** (UPPSC: **1 and 2 only**); all three modes → "All of the above" (CGPSC 2013).

  [7:6:4.0] 7A | Registration of OCI cardholders
  [7:6:4.1] 14A | National Register of Indian Citizens and national identity card

## SUB 7 | NRC, NPR and Proof of Citizenship
[7:7:0] - **NRC (Assam):** identifies **genuine citizens in Assam**; eligibility = names in the **NRC of 1951** or electoral rolls up to **24 March 1971** (cut-off of the **Assam Accord, 1985**); **draft NRC published for Assam** (2018). The **2019 CAA was not an NRC amendment** – the Accord was given effect by **Sec. 6A** (1985).
[7:7:1] - **NRC** (citizens only) is a **subset of the NPR** (all usual residents); Sec. **14A** of the Citizenship Act.
[7:7:2] - **Not proof of citizenship:** **Aadhaar** (identity only, **Sec. 9, Aadhaar Act**), voter ID, ration card, passport.

=== NOTES 2:8 | Fundamental Rights – Article 12, Article 13 and Doctrines
## SUB 0 | Part III at a Glance – Six Groups, Number, Location
[8:0:0] # Where and how many
[8:0:1] - **Location:** **Part III, Articles 12–35** of the Constitution; called the **"Magna Carta of India"** (not Part I, II or IV; not Arts. 14–32 only).
[8:0:2] - **Number:** originally **seven**, now **six** Fundamental Rights (groups) – the **Right to Property (Art. 31)** was deleted by the **44th Amendment, 1978** (Janata Party government) and is now a **legal/constitutional right under Art. 300A (Part XII)**.
[8:0:3] - **Borrowed from:** the **Bill of Rights of the USA**; the **Motilal Nehru Report (1928)** had demanded a bill of rights.
[8:0:4] - **Why "fundamental":** they are **guaranteed and specifically protected by the Constitution**, the fundamental law of the land – not rights granted by any ordinary law, nor merely natural-law rights.
[8:0:5] # The six groups
[8:0:6] TABLE head: Group | Articles | Contents
  [8:0:6.0] Right to Equality | 14–18 | 14 equality before law; 15 no discrimination (religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth); 16 public employment; 17 untouchability abolished; 18 titles abolished
  [8:0:6.1] Right to Freedom | 19–22 | 19 six freedoms (speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession); 20 conviction; 21 life and personal liberty; 21A education; 22 arrest and detention
  [8:0:6.2] Right against Exploitation | 23–24 | 23 traffic in human beings, forced labour; 24 child labour in factories
  [8:0:6.3] Right to Freedom of Religion | 25–28 | conscience, religious affairs, taxes, religious instruction
  [8:0:6.4] Cultural and Educational Rights | 29–30 | minorities' culture; minorities' institutions
  [8:0:6.5] Right to Constitutional Remedies | 32 alone | writs; Art. 226 (High Courts) is outside Part III
[8:0:7] - **Classification traps:** **Art. 15** (prohibition of discrimination) and **Art. 17** (untouchability) fall under the **Right to Equality** – not Freedom of Religion, Exploitation or Cultural rights (UPSC 1995, 2020; SSC 2018).
[8:0:8] - **Wrong pair:** "Right to protection of life – **Art. 20**" is wrong; life and liberty is **Art. 21** (Art. 20 = protection in respect of conviction). Art. 15 discrimination, Art. 19 association and Art. 32 remedies are correct pairs.
[8:0:9] - **Description:** "a pledge to our people and a pact with the civilised world" – **S. Radhakrishnan**.
[8:0:10] > Asked as: "How many FRs?" (six – APPSC Drug Inspectors, Polytechnic Lecturers; SSC CGL 2017), "Which Part?" (III – APPSC G2 2008; SSC 2008, 2017), "Which Articles?" (12–35; Equality 14–18; Freedom 19–22), CDS 2003 "originally seven, now six – both correct".

## SUB 1 | Citizens Only vs All Persons; Aliens
[8:1:0] # Who gets which right
[8:1:1] TABLE head: Citizens only | All persons (citizens + foreigners)
  [8:1:1.0] Art. 15 – no discrimination | Art. 14 – equality before law, equal protection of laws
  [8:1:1.1] Art. 16 – public employment | Art. 20 – protection in respect of conviction
  [8:1:1.2] Art. 19 – six freedoms (speech, movement, trade and profession…) | Art. 21 / 21A – life and liberty, education
  [8:1:1.3] Art. 29 – minorities' language and culture | Art. 22 – arrest safeguards (not enemy aliens)
  [8:1:1.4] Art. 30 – minorities' institutions | Arts. 23–24 – exploitation; Arts. 25–28 – religion; Art. 32 – remedies
[8:1:2] - **Five citizens-only Articles: 15, 16, 19, 29, 30** (Raj. PCS 2024; APPSC G1 2023: denied to aliens = Arts. **29–30, 19, 16** – **not 23–24**).
[8:1:3] - A **British citizen** (any foreigner) in India cannot claim **freedom of trade and profession** or **freedom of speech and expression**, free movement or residence (all Art. 19) – but gets equality before law, life and liberty, religious freedom (UPSC 1999).
[8:1:4] - **Non-citizens** get the **right to life and liberty** and **constitutional remedies** – not freedom of expression or of association (Art. 19).
[8:1:5] - **Aliens** are not given the citizens-only FRs – they are the category to whom FRs are "not given" (bankrupt or sick persons and political sufferers keep all their FRs).
[8:1:6] - Also citizens only: **contesting elections** (not an FR at all).
[8:1:7] - **Aliens:** the Constitution **does distinguish friendly and enemy aliens** – **enemy aliens** lose even the arrest-and-detention safeguards of **Art. 22(1)-(2)**, by **Art. 22(3)**.
[8:1:8] - **Companies** (legal persons) can claim the all-persons rights, but **not Art. 19**.

## SUB 3 | Features and Nature of Fundamental Rights
[8:3:0] # Features
[8:3:1] - **Justiciable:** a person can move the **Supreme Court directly (Art. 32)** and **High Courts (Art. 226)**; FRs are **above ordinary laws** (Art. 13).
[8:3:2] - **Not absolute but qualified:** the State can impose **reasonable restrictions** by law; the **courts judge reasonableness**; **Art. 19** lists **11 grounds** of restriction in all.
[8:3:3] - **Claimed against the State:** most FRs are available against **arbitrary State action** and **limit the power of the State – even Parliament**; some (15(2), 17, 23, 24) also bind private persons.
[8:3:4] - **Not sacrosanct:** Parliament can curtail them **only by a constitutional amendment** (not an ordinary law), without destroying the **basic structure**; they can be **suspended only as the Constitution prescribes** (Arts. 358-359).
[8:3:5] - **Negative and positive:** many are **negatively worded** (14, 15(1), 16(2), 18(1), 20, 22(1), 28(1)); **21A, 29, 30** confer privileges; **15, 17, 18, 20, 24** are absolute limits on legislative power.
[8:3:6] - **Both natural and legal**; they protect **individuals and minority groups** from arbitrary State action (both statements correct).
[8:3:7] - **Britain** has **no written FRs**; it relies on conventions and the rule of law.
[8:3:8] # Statement traps
[8:3:9] - **Wrong:** "FRs are **unamendable**", "**absolute**", "partly justiciable and absolute", "not limited by the Constitution", "all FRs are suspended in an emergency".
[8:3:10] - **Right:** "not absolute", "some only for citizens", "drawn from the American Constitution", "justiciable", "six in number", "granted by the Constitution".
[8:3:11] - **CDS 2004:** FRs are fundamental because they are protected and enforced by the Constitution, can be suspended only as it prescribes, and are enforceable against the State (I, II, III) – **not** because they cannot be amended.
[8:3:12] - **A–R (UPPSC 2015):** the Constitution is the longest (A true) and the FR chapter follows the American model (R true), but R does not explain A.
[8:3:13] - **Right to die:** **Gian Kaur (1996)** – no right to die (suicide) under Art. 21; **Common Cause (2018)** – right to **die with dignity** (passive euthanasia, living will) is part of Art. 21.
[8:3:14] - **India is not a fully federal constitution** – it is federal with a unitary bias ("quasi-federal").
[8:3:15] - **Golden Triangle:** **Arts. 14, 19 and 21** (Maneka Gandhi 1978; Minerva Mills 1980) – not 14-20-21, 14-15-16, 19-20-21 or 14-21-32.
[8:3:16] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity p.169-173 (features), p.200 (Golden Triangle 14, 19, 21); Rocket Polity Part 1 p.38-40, Part 1 p.59 (Maneka Gandhi: golden triangle); sci.gov.in (Common Cause v. UoI, 9 Mar 2018); app notes 2:8 sub 1
## SUB 4 | Limits on FRs – Restrictions, Armed Forces (Arts. 33-35), Emergency (358-359)
[8:4:0] # Who can restrict
[8:4:1] - **Reasonable restrictions** are imposed by **law** made by **Parliament** (and State legislatures within their field) – not by the President, Prime Minister or Supreme Court; the courts only **judge** their reasonableness.
[8:4:2] # Articles 33–35
[8:4:3] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [8:4:3.0] 33 | Parliament alone may restrict or abrogate FRs of the armed forces, forces charged with maintenance of public order (police), intelligence/counter-intelligence staff, and persons in telecommunication systems set up for those forces – including non-combatants (barbers, cooks, carpenters). Examples: Army Act 1950, Air Force Act 1950, Navy Act 1957
  [8:4:3.1] 34 | Restrictions while martial law is in force; Parliament may pass an indemnity Act; "martial law" is not defined in the Constitution
  [8:4:3.2] 35 | Only Parliament (not States) legislates on 16(3), 32(3), 33, 34 and punishments for Part III offences – for uniformity
[8:4:4] - **CDS 2015 trap:** staff of **communication systems set up in the country in general** are **not** covered by Art. 33 – they enjoy FRs at par with other citizens; the armed forces, public-order forces and **their** communication staff are the restricted categories.
[8:4:5] # Emergency
[8:4:6] - **Art. 358:** when a National Emergency is declared on **war or external aggression**, **Art. 19 (Right to Freedom) is suspended automatically** (not on armed rebellion).
[8:4:7] - **Art. 359:** the **President** may by order suspend the right to move courts for **specified FRs** – **never Arts. 20 and 21** (**44th Amendment, 1978**).

[8:5:0] # Clauses

  [8:5:1.0] 13(1) | Pre-constitution laws inconsistent with FRs are void to the extent of inconsistency

## SUB 6 | Doctrines under Article 13
[8:6:0] TABLE head: Doctrine | Meaning | Case
  [8:6:0.0] Severability | Law is void only to the extent of inconsistency; the consistent, severable part remains valid | A.K. Gopalan (1950); R.M.D.C. (1957); Motor General Traders v. State of AP (1984)
  [8:6:0.1] Eclipse | A pre-constitution law inconsistent with an FR is not wiped out – it is dormant (overshadowed) and revives when the conflict is removed | Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v. State of MP (1955); State of Gujarat v. Ambica Mills (1974) – extended to post-constitution laws for non-citizens
  [8:6:0.2] Waiver | A person cannot waive an FR | Basheshar Nath v. CIT (1959)
  [8:6:0.3] Prospective overruling | A ruling operates only for the future | Golaknath (1967)
  [8:6:0.4] Progressive interpretation | Constitution as a living document | Maneka Gandhi (1978) widened Art. 21; SaveLIFE Foundation v. UoI (2026) – right to trauma care under Art. 21
  [8:6:0.5] Absolute liability | Hazardous industry liable without exceptions | M.C. Mehta (1987) – Oleum gas leak
  [8:6:0.6] Territorial nexus | State law can operate outside the State if there is sufficient connection | State of Bombay v. R.M.D.C. (1957)
  [8:6:0.7] Pith and substance | Encroachment on another List – look at the true nature of the law | Federal doctrine (with aspect, colourable legislation, incidental and ancillary powers, repugnancy)
[8:6:1] - **Eclipse vs severability:** eclipse = law **sleeps and revives**; severability = **bad part cut off, rest lives**. Eminent domain and pleasure are unrelated doctrines.
[8:6:2] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity p.180-182 (doctrines; Motor General Traders 1984; Ambica Mills 1974); app notes 2:8 sub 5
## SUB 7 | Article 12 – Meaning of "the State"
[8:7:0] # Definition
[8:7:1] - **Government and Parliament of India**; **Government and Legislature of each State**; **all local authorities** (municipalities, panchayats, district boards); **other authorities** within India or under the control of the Government of India (LIC, ONGC, electricity boards).
[8:7:2] - **Ambiguity – courts:** whether the **judiciary** is "other authority" is the grey area; in its judicial function it is **not** "State" (**Naresh Mirajkar v. State of Maharashtra, 1967**).
[8:7:3] - **Private bodies:** a writ does not ordinarily lie merely because a private body performs a public activity, unless the State is involved.
[8:7:4] TABLE head: Case | Year | Holding
  [8:7:4.0] Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal | 1967 | Statutory bodies are "other authorities"
  [8:7:4.1] R.D. Shetty v. International Airport Authority | 1979 | Laid down tests for an instrumentality or agency of the State
  [8:7:4.2] Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib | 1981 | Society running a Regional Engineering College = State; test is creation and control, not legal form
  [8:7:4.3] Pradeep Kumar Biswas | 2002 | State if financially, functionally and administratively dominated by government
[8:7:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity p.174-175, p.178-179 (Naresh 1967; other authorities); app notes 2:8 sub 3
