## SUB 0 | 42nd Amendment, 1976 – Socialist, Secular, Integrity
[0:0] # What the 42nd Amendment did to the Preamble
[0:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [0:1.0] Words added | Socialist, Secular, Integrity (three words)
  [0:1.1] Description of India | "Sovereign Democratic Republic" → "Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic"
  [0:1.2] Fraternity clause | "unity of the Nation" → "unity and integrity of the Nation"
  [0:1.3] Year / context | 1976, during the Emergency (1975-77), Indira Gandhi government
  [0:1.4] Based on | Swaran Singh Committee
  [0:1.5] Nickname | "Mini Constitution" – it changed the Preamble, the Seventh Schedule and 53 Articles
[0:2] - **Only amendment of the Preamble so far:** the **42nd (1976)**; asked about 25 times (APPSC G2 2008, 2011, 2025 Mains; AEE 2008; Endowment 2008; JL 2003; Polytechnic 2019; Degree Lecturers 2020; Constable 2016; SSC, NDA 2018, UPPSC, MPPSC 2023).
[0:3] - **⚠ Trap:** not the 1st, 2nd, 17th, 24th, 25th, 38th, 40th, 41st, 43rd, 44th, 45th, 46th, 52nd, 73rd or 86th Amendment ("1976 Second Amendment" is wrong).
[0:4] - **'Socialist' and 'Secular' were inserted between 'Sovereign' and 'Democratic'.**
[0:5] - **Integrity** was **not** in the Preamble in **1975**; it came in **1976** (SSC 2019; UPPSC 2006). The word **"unity"** was in the **original** text – only "and integrity" was added (a question saying "'Unity of the Nation' was added by the 42nd" means this change).
[0:6] - **NOT added by the 42nd:** **Fraternity**, **Sovereign**, Democratic, Republic (all original); **Gram Swarajya**, Dignity of person, Privacy, Liberal are not 42nd words.
[0:7] - **Assertion-reason:** "India is a sovereign socialist secular democratic republic" (A) is true and its reason – the 42nd Act **substituted** these words for "Sovereign Democratic Republic" (R) – correctly explains it.
[0:8] # Other changes of the 42nd Amendment (for "not associated" items)
[0:9] - Added **Fundamental Duties** (Part IVA) and new DPSPs; **curtailed** judicial review in some matters but did **not abolish** it entirely.
[0:10] - **Minerva Mills (1980)** struck down its Art. 368 clauses **(4) and (5)** (unlimited amending power, exclusion of judicial review) and the clause giving **DPSPs full priority over all Fundamental Rights** – restoring the balance of Parts III and IV.
[0:11] > Status today: in **Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India (25 Nov 2024)** the Supreme Court **upheld 'Socialist' and 'Secular'** and dismissed the challenges to their insertion (as of 2026; the words were again debated politically in Jul 2025).
[0:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.11 (42nd Amendment), p.230, p.232; Rocket Polity #14 p.36-37; app notes 2:4 sub 7; sci.gov.in (Balram Singh, 25 Nov 2024)
## SUB 1 | Preamble Text – Order of Words, Original 1949 Text, Words Not in It
[1:0] # The text (as amended in 1976)
[1:1] - **WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA**, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a **SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC** and to secure to all its citizens: **JUSTICE**, social, economic and political; **LIBERTY** of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; **EQUALITY** of status and of opportunity; and to promote among them all **FRATERNITY** assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation; IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.
[1:2] - **Order:** **Sovereign → Socialist → Secular → Democratic → Republic** (code C, A, D, B for Socialist A, Democratic B, Sovereign C, Secular D); objectives in order **Justice → Liberty → Equality → Fraternity**.
[1:3] - **India is declared secular by the Preamble** (Fundamental Rights, DPSP and 9th Schedule do not "describe" India as secular).
[1:4] - **Equality of** → **status** (and of opportunity); not "of nationality", "religion" or "community".
[1:5] # Original 1949 text vs now
[1:6] TABLE head: Item | 26 Nov 1949 / 26 Jan 1950 | Now (after 1976)
  [1:6.0] Description of India | "Sovereign Democratic Republic" | "Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic"
  [1:6.1] Fraternity clause | "unity of the Nation" | "unity and integrity of the Nation"
  [1:6.2] Words absent in 1949 | Socialist, Secular, Integrity | added
[1:7] - **Exact status on 26 January 1950:** a **Sovereign Democratic Republic** (UPSC 2021).
[1:8] - **Not in the 1949 Preamble:** Socialism / Socialist, Secular, Integrity; "Reliable" and "Non-religious" were never in it; **Republic** was in the original (UPPSC 2009; Uttarakhand 2024 → 2, 3, 4).
[1:9] # Words NOT in the Preamble at all
[1:10] - **Federal / federation** (APPSC Deputy Surveyors 2012; UPPSC 2021) – "Quasi-federal" is not in it either.
[1:11] - **Freedom** (it uses **Liberty**), **Right** (as a word), **Public Welfare** / **Welfare State**, **Adult franchise**, **Fundamental Duties**, "**People's**".
[1:12] - **Religious Justice**, **Natural** or **Cultural** justice (justice is only **social, economic, political**).
[1:13] - **Political Liberty** and **Economic Liberty** as categories; liberty "of action" (liberty is only of **thought, expression, belief, faith, worship**) (UPSC 2017; APPSC G2).
[1:14] - Words that ARE in it: Fraternity, Equality, Justice, **Unity**, Integrity, Thought, Opportunity, **Dignity**, Republic, Sovereign.
[1:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.229-230 (text, 42nd words); Rocket Polity #14 p.36-37; app notes 2:4 sub 1
## SUB 2 | We, the People – Source of Authority, Date and Adoption
[2:0] - **Source of the Constitution / of political power:** the **people of India** – the Preamble opens "**We, the People of India**", showing **people's sovereignty**; the Constitution derives its authority from the people (APPSC G1 2002, G2, AEE 2012, CDPO 2013; HWO 2017).
[2:1] - **⚠ Trap:** not the Constitution, Parliament, "Parliament and State Legislatures", the President or the Supreme Court.
[2:2] - The Preamble **indicates the source** from which the Constitution comes (APPSC G2 2011).
[2:3] - **"We, the People of India"** is used in the **Preamble**, not in the FRs, DPSPs or Citizenship part.
[2:4] - **Enacting clause:** "We, the people of India … **in our Constituent Assembly** … do hereby **adopt, enact and give to ourselves** this Constitution" – the Constitution is declared adopted and enacted by the **people of India**, acting through the **Constituent Assembly** (when only the CA is offered, it is the answer for "adopted by").
[2:5] # The date
[2:6] - **Date in the Preamble:** **26 November 1949** = **adoption** of the Constitution (drafting completed; signed by the President of the CA) – not 26 January 1950 (commencement), 24 January 1950 or 13 December 1946.
[2:7] - **Commencement:** **26 January 1950** (some provisions – citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament – took effect on 26 Nov 1949).
[2:8] - **Constitution Day:** **26 November**, observed **since 2015** (Constitution Day 2019 = 26 Nov) (APPSC G2 Mains 2017; JL 2020).
[2:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.136-137, p.229-230; Rocket Polity #14 p.36-37; app notes 2:4 sub 0-1
## SUB 3 | Is the Preamble Part of the Constitution? – Cases and Legal Status
[3:0] TABLE head: Case | Year | Held on the Preamble
  [3:0.0] Berubari Union (President's reference) | 1960 | Not a part of the Constitution (the first such ruling); a "key to the minds of the makers"; aid to interpretation where an Article is ambiguous
  [3:0.1] Golaknath | 1967 | About amending Fundamental Rights; not the Preamble case (distractor)
  [3:0.2] Kesavananda Bharati | 1973 | Is a part of the Constitution (overruled Berubari); amendable under Art. 368 but the basic structure cannot be destroyed; 13 judges
  [3:0.3] S.R. Bommai | 1994 | Relied on the Preamble – secularism a basic feature; Preamble treated as integral part
  [3:0.4] LIC of India | 1995 | Reaffirmed the Preamble is an integral part
  [3:0.5] Minerva Mills | 1980 | Struck down 42nd Amendment Art. 368 clauses – not a Preamble-status case
[3:1] - **"Preamble is a part" =** **Kesavananda Bharati** (APPSC TWO 2012, G2 2008, ZSWO 2025); with no Kesavananda option, **Bommai** (UPPSC 2012); A.K. Gopalan, Maneka Gandhi, Mohini Jain, Sajjan Singh are distractors.
[3:2] - **"Not a part" =** **Berubari, 1960** (not 1968; Kesavananda was 1973, not 1970).
[3:3] # Legal nature
[3:4] - **Non-justiciable** – **not enforceable** in a court of law (UPPSC 2019).
[3:5] - **Neither a source of power** to the legislature **nor a prohibition** on its powers; confers no power and creates no right by itself.
[3:6] - **Part of the Constitution but has no legal effect independently of other parts** (UPSC 2020).
[3:7] - **Cannot override** the specific provisions of the Constitution; where a provision is clear, the Preamble yields.
[3:8] - **Aid to interpretation:** may be invoked to determine the **ambit of Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles** (APPSC G1).
[3:9] - **Amendable** under Art. 368 (done once, 1976) – so "it cannot be amended" is false; "not a part of the Constitution" is false.
[3:10] - **Enacted after** the rest of the Constitution, so that it conformed to the text – "enacted before the entire Constitution" is false (APPSC G1 2023 → I and III incorrect).
[3:11] - Reflects the **philosophy, ideals and objectives** of the Constitution – the **mind of the makers** (UPSC 2017) and the **objective of the Constitution** (UPPSC 2013) are reflected in the Preamble, not in FRs, DPSP or FDs.
[3:12] > Statement sets: "Objectives Resolution became the Preamble ✓; non-justiciable ✓; can't be amended ✗; can't override specific provisions ✓" → **I, II and IV** (UPPSC 2009, Panchayat Secretary). "Not a part ✗; amended only under Art. 368 ✓; three words added in 1976 ✓" → **B and C**.
[3:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.159 (cases), p.229 (status); Rocket Polity #14 p.37; app notes 2:4 sub 4-5
## SUB 4 | Keywords and Objectives – Meaning
[4:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:0.0] Sovereign | Supreme authority; free internally and externally; not subject to any external authority
  [4:0.1] Socialist | Democratic socialism – a mixed economy (public and private sectors); a blend of Marxism and Gandhism, leaning towards Gandhian socialism
  [4:0.2] Secular | Equal respect for all religions; no State religion; before 1976 the word appeared only in Art. 25(2)(a); the term is not defined in any Article
  [4:0.3] Democratic | Government by the people; representative parliamentary democracy; covers political, social and economic democracy; universal adult franchise and rule of law are its marks
  [4:0.4] Republic | Elected head of State (the President, indirectly elected for 5 years); no hereditary element; public offices open to all
[4:1] - **Republic ≠ PM:** India is a republic because its **head of State (President) is elected** – not because ministers are responsible to Parliament or hold office at the President's pleasure.
[4:2] - **Philosophy of the Constitution:** welfare state, socialist state, political equality – but **not a Communist State** (UPPSC 2015).
[4:3] - India is a **democratic polity, a sovereign State, a democratic society and a welfare state** – all four (UPPSC 2012); the **welfare-state** idea is found in the **Preamble and the DPSP** (UPPSC 1997).
[4:4] - **Not valid (APPSC G1 2024):** "'democratic' is a mix of British Fabian Socialism and American Populism"; "Republic = elected Prime Minister"; "Liberty is neither absolute nor qualified"; "Fraternity originally denoted only dignity of the individual" → **none valid**.
[4:5] # Objectives
[4:6] TABLE head: Objective | Of / kinds
  [4:6.0] Justice | Social, economic and political – three kinds
  [4:6.1] Liberty | Thought, expression, belief, faith and worship – five
  [4:6.2] Equality | Status and opportunity – civic, political, economic dimensions
  [4:6.3] Fraternity | Sense of brotherhood; assures dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the Nation; promoted by single citizenship
[4:7] - **Goals secured to all:** justice (social, economic, political), liberty of thought and expression, equality of opportunity and **dignity of the individual** – **all** of them (UPPSC 1998).
[4:8] - **Liberty** = **absence of restraints plus opportunity to develop one's personality**; UPSC 2019 "most appropriate definition" = **opportunity to develop oneself fully** (positive liberty); liberty is not licence to do whatever one likes.
[4:9] - **Law and liberty (UPSC 2018):** "**If there are no laws, there is no liberty**" – law is the condition of liberty.
[4:10] - **Political theory links:** popular/political **sovereignty** – **Rousseau** (general will); **distributive justice** – **Aristotle**.
[4:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.166 (secular state), p.230-232 (keywords, justice, liberty, equality, fraternity); Rocket Polity #14 p.36; app notes 2:4 sub 2-3; UPSC CSE 2018, 2019 official keys
## SUB 5 | Making and Sources of the Preamble's Ideals
[5:0] - **Basis:** the **Objectives Resolution** of **Jawaharlal Nehru** – moved **13 December 1946**, adopted **22 January 1947**; its modified version became the Preamble (Nehru "proposed the Preamble"; not Ambedkar, B.N. Rau or Gandhi) (APPSC ASWO; Uttarakhand 2006; APPSC JA).
[5:1] - The Constitution of India was **not** the first to begin with a Preamble (the **US** Constitution did); the Preamble has been amended **once**, not twice.
[5:2] TABLE head: Element | Source
  [5:2.0] Idea of a Preamble; "We, the people" | USA
  [5:2.1] Language of the Preamble | Australia
  [5:2.2] Liberty, Equality, Fraternity (the "trinity"); Republic | French Revolution (1789) / French Constitution
  [5:2.3] Justice – social, economic and political | Russian Revolution (1917), USSR
  [5:2.4] Fraternity (possible) | Art. 1 of the UDHR (1948)
[5:3] - **⚠ Trap:** Liberty-equality-fraternity is **not** from the Russian, American or Glorious Revolution or the UN Charter; justice is **not** from the French Revolution.
[5:4] - **Third reading** of the Constitution: **17-26 November 1949**; Preamble enacted last.
