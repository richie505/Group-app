## SUB 0 | Union Territories – Count, Administration and Legislatures
[0:0] # How many States and UTs
[0:1] - **Now (2026): 28 States and 8 Union Territories** (APPSC 2021 key; Indian_Polity Part 1 p.48).
[0:2] - **29 States + 7 UTs** was true only from 2014 (Telangana) to **5 August 2019**; **28 + 7** was the count from 1987 to 2014 (APPSC Draftsman 2012 key) and again from 31 Oct 2019 to 25 Jan 2020; **28 + 8** from **26 January 2020** (merger of DNH and Daman & Diu).
[0:3] - **8 UTs:** **Andaman and Nicobar, Chandigarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Delhi (NCT), Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Lakshadweep, Puducherry**.
[0:4] - **Goa is a State** (since 1987), not a UT; **Delhi is a UT** (First Schedule) with special status as the **National Capital Territory** by the **69th Amendment, 1991**.
[0:5] - **Smallest UT** by area: **Lakshadweep** (about 32 sq km); largest: **Ladakh**.
[0:6] - UTs replaced the **Part C and Part D States** under the **7th Amendment, 1956**.
[0:7] # Puducherry – spread over three States
[0:8] - **Puducherry** UT = **Puducherry and Karaikal (in Tamil Nadu)**, **Mahe (in Kerala)** and **Yanam (in Andhra Pradesh)** → spread over **3 States**; **Yanam** is the part of a UT located in AP.
[0:9] - **Coastal UTs (other than island UTs):** **Puducherry** and **Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu** → **two** (Lakshadweep and A&N are islands).
[0:10] - **Dadra and Nagar Haveli** was under **Portuguese** rule (not French) until **1954**; **Diu** is an island in the **Gulf of Khambhat**; **Daman and Diu** were separated from Goa in 1987 (Goa statehood, 56th Amendment).
[0:11] # Administration
[0:12] - **Art. 239:** the **President administers every UT through an Administrator** (LG / Chief Commissioner / Administrator).
[0:13] - **Art. 239(2):** the **Governor of an adjoining State** may be the administrator – the **Governor of Punjab is the Administrator of Chandigarh**; he then acts **independently of his State Council of Ministers**.
[0:14] - **Lakshadweep** has its own **Administrator**, not the Governor of Kerala (UPSC 2009: only the Chandigarh statement is correct).
[0:15] - **Art. 240:** the **President makes regulations** for **A&N Islands, Lakshadweep, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Ladakh**, and for **Puducherry** only while its legislature is suspended or dissolved (the old "Goa, Daman and Diu" entry was omitted in 1987).
[0:16] # Legislatures and High Courts
[0:17] TABLE head: UT | Legislature | High Court
  [0:17.0] Delhi | Art. 239AA (69th, 1991) | Own High Court (the only UT with one of its own)
  [0:17.1] Puducherry | Art. 239A (14th, 1962) | Madras HC
  [0:17.2] Jammu and Kashmir | J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019 (Art. 239A applied) | Common HC of J&K and Ladakh
  [0:17.3] Ladakh | None | Common HC of J&K and Ladakh
  [0:17.4] Chandigarh | None | Punjab and Haryana HC
  [0:17.5] Lakshadweep | None | Kerala HC
  [0:17.6] A&N Islands | None | Calcutta HC
  [0:17.7] DNH and Daman & Diu | None | Bombay HC (also covers Goa)
[0:18] - **"State" in Art. 54 (election of the President)** includes **NCT of Delhi and UT of Puducherry** (Explanation added by the **70th Amendment, 1992**); the 2019 Act applied Art. 239A to J&K but did not amend Art. 54.
[0:19] > Bombay HC covers Maharashtra, Goa, DNH and Daman & Diu – **Lakshadweep** is the odd one out (APSLPRB SI 2019).

## SUB 2 | Articles 1–4 – Union, Territory and the First Schedule
[2:0] # What each Article says
[2:1] TABLE head: Article | Subject
  [2:1.0] 1 | "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States"; States and territories in the First Schedule; Art. 1(3) territory of India
  [2:1.1] 2 | Admission or establishment of new States (not part of the Union of India)
  [2:1.2] 3 | Formation of new States; alteration of areas, boundaries or names of existing States
  [2:1.3] 4 | Laws under Arts. 2 and 3 amend the First and Fourth Schedules; Art. 4(2): not deemed an amendment under Art. 368
[2:2] - Part I = **Articles 1–4**; the Constitution calls the Centre the **"Union"** (Union Government, Union List), not "Centre".
[2:3] - **"Union" not "Federation" (Ambedkar):** the Indian federation is **not the result of an agreement among States**, and **no State has the right to secede** – "an **indestructible Union of destructible States**".
[2:4] # Union of India vs Territory of India
[2:5] TABLE head: Union of India | Territory of India (Art. 1(3))
  [2:5.0] States only – members of the federal system sharing powers with the Union | States + Union Territories + territories that may be acquired
[2:6] - So **UTs are covered by "Territory of India" and not by "Union of India"** (APPSC G1: **Only IV**); both expressions are in the Constitution, and the **First Schedule** lists **States and UTs**.
[2:7] - Grama Sachivalayam 2020: the **Union of India** includes **States only (A only)**.
[2:8] - **Acquiring territory:** India as a sovereign State acquires foreign territory **"as understood in public international law"** – cession, conquest, occupation (**N. Masthan Sahib v. Chief Commissioner, Pondicherry**); e.g. **Pondicherry, Goa, Daman and Diu, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Sikkim**. Such territory need not join an existing State (it may be a UT).
[2:9] # First Schedule
[2:10] - **First Schedule = names of the States and UTs and their territorial extent** → it **must be amended to create a new State** (UPSC 2001; APPSC G2 Mains 2019; APPSC HO 2021). Fourth Schedule = Rajya Sabha seats (also amended).
[2:11] # Article 3 – power and procedure
[2:12] - **Power vests in Parliament** (not the President, the Council of Ministers or an SRC): form a new State by **separating** territory, **uniting** States or parts, uniting territory to a part of a State; **increase or diminish** area; alter **boundaries or name**.
[2:13] - **Admission of new States is NOT under Art. 3** – it is **Art. 2**; creating a new State out of existing States is **Art. 3** (CGPSC 2014).
[2:14] - **Procedure:** Bill only on the **President's recommendation**; President **refers it to the State legislature concerned** for views within a time limit; **Parliament is not bound** by the views; **no fresh reference** needed for each amendment to the Bill; for a **UT, no reference** at all.
[2:15] - **Consent of the State is not required** to alter its boundaries – Parliament acts "without their consent".
[2:16] - Passed by a **simple majority** in the ordinary legislative process (**Art. 4(2)**).
[2:17] - **Renaming Kerala → 'Keralam'**: Union Cabinet approval began the **Article 3** procedure.
[2:18] - Parliament **cannot cede** territory under Art. 3 – cession needs an **Art. 368** amendment (see Cession).

## SUB 3 | Reorganisation Commissions, Andhra State and the SRA 1956
[3:0] # Commissions
[3:1] TABLE head: Body | Year | Members | Recommended
  [3:1.0] Dhar Commission | June 1948 | S.K. Dhar (chairman), Panna Lall, Jagat Narain Lal | Administrative convenience, not language
  [3:1.1] JVP Committee (Congress) | December 1948 | Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, Pattabhi Sitaramayya | Rejected language as the basis
  [3:1.2] Fazl Ali Commission (SRC) | 22 December 1953 | Fazl Ali (chairman), K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru | Broadly accepted language, rejected "one language–one State"; reported 30 Sept 1955
[3:2] - **Dhar Commission** was appointed by **Rajendra Prasad** (President of the Constituent Assembly); it held linguistic States a threat to national unity.
[3:3] - **Pattabhi Sitaramayya** was in the **JVP Committee**, **not in the SRC**.
[3:4] - **SRC appointment:** announced **22 December 1953** (MHA resolution dated 29 Dec 1953); suggested **Telangana** stay separate till about **1961**.
[3:5] - **SRC's four factors:** unity and security of India; linguistic and cultural homogeneity; financial, economic and administrative considerations; welfare of the people.
[3:6] - **SRC recommended:** abolish the **Part A/B/C** classification; abolish the **Rajpramukh** institution and special agreements with princely States; abolish the Centre's general control under **Art. 371** (a question that prints "Article 171" makes that item false → "Only C" is not a recommendation).
[3:7] # Andhra State, 1953
[3:8] - **Potti Sriramulu** died (**15 December 1952**) after a **56-day fast** → **Andhra State** formed **1 October 1953** from the **Telugu-speaking districts of Madras State** – the **first linguistic State**; capital **Kurnool**.
[3:9] - Exams also ask "Andhra Pradesh created as a language-based State in **1953**" – they mean Andhra State.
[3:10] # States Reorganisation Act, 1956
[3:11] - In force **1 November 1956**: **14 States + 6 UTs**; **Andhra State + Telangana region of Hyderabad = Andhra Pradesh** (capital **Hyderabad**); Kerala formed.
[3:12] - Large-scale reorganisation **on a linguistic basis = 1956**; the related amendment = **7th Amendment, 1956** (Part A/B/C/D classification ended).
[3:13] - Set up **five Zonal Councils** (the Indian territory divided into **five zones**).
