## SUB 5 | Historical Agreements
[120:5:0] - **Sri Bagh Pact (16 Nov 1937):** first Coastal-Rayalaseema accord; drafted by **Pattabhi Sitaramayya**; Rayalaseema to choose **either the capital or the High Court**; aimed to raise Rayalaseema's farm and economic level to coastal districts. Andhra State capital → **Kurnool** (1953).
[120:5:1] - **Gentlemen's Agreement – 1956** (not 1969): reused the Sri Bagh template for Telangana safeguards.
[120:5:2] - Such pacts institutionalised sub-regional fault lines; poor implementation fed agitations.

## SUB 2 | Meaning, Types and Theories
[121:2:0] - **Deviance:** behaviour that **violates the norms** of a group or society (crime = law-breaking deviance).
[121:2:1] - **Youth unrest:** collective frustration among young people when society's norms seem **ineffective or harmful**.
[121:2:2] TABLE head: Type | Example
  [121:2:2.0] Persuasive | Exam postponement
  [121:2:2.1] Resistance | Against attendance rules
  [121:2:2.2] Revolutionary | Overthrow the system (AASU, Bodo)
[121:2:3] - **Life cycle:** **Discontent** (first) → initiation → formalisation → public support → official action.
[121:2:4] - **Theories:** discontent; **personal maladjustment**; **relative deprivation (Stouffer)**; **resource mobilisation** (tactics and leadership matter more than grievances).
[121:2:5] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:121 sub 0; general knowledge (deviance)
[121:2:6] - **Adolescence** is marked by a **greater need for independence** and identity search ('storm and stress') – a root of youth restlessness.

## SUB 1 | Human Trafficking
[122:1:0] - **Meaning:** trade in humans for sexual slavery, forced labour or commercial sexual exploitation – a form of **modern slavery**.
[122:1:1] - **Article 23 (Fundamental Right – Right against Exploitation)** prohibits **traffic in human beings, begar and forced labour** – it is **NOT a Directive Principle** (UPSC 2008).
[122:1:2] - **Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956** (SITA 1956, renamed ITPA in 1986) is the main law on sex trafficking; **BNS Sec. 143** defines trafficking; also POCSO 2012, Bonded Labour (Abolition) Act 1976; India signed the **Palermo Protocol**.

=== NOTES 2:123 | Poverty, Unemployment and Social Exclusion As Social Problems
## SUB 0 | Social Exclusion
[123:0:0] - **Meaning:** a **process** that shuts people out of **facilities, benefits and opportunities** that others enjoy; it is **involuntary and systematic** (NCERT).
[123:0:1] - Term coined by **René Lenoir** (France, **1974**).
[123:0:2] TABLE head: Basis | Example
  [123:0:2.0] Caste | Untouchability against SCs
  [123:0:2.1] Gender | Denial of property, work, mobility
  [123:0:2.2] Race / tribe | Adivasis in remote areas, racial discrimination
  [123:0:2.3] Disability | Lack of access to schools, jobs; segregated special schools
  [123:0:2.4] Religion / region | Minorities; backward, remote areas
[123:0:3] - **All of caste, gender, race and tribe** are bases of exclusion (TSPSC 2018).
[123:0:4] - **Practices leading to exclusion:** **untouchability**, **segregation** (e.g. a **special school for the blind** instead of inclusive schooling), denied access to **land, water and jobs**. MGNREGA wage work is a remedy, not a practice of exclusion.
[123:0:5] - **Results:** hurts **human-capital development**, **widens inequalities**, drives **social unrest and crime** – it does **not** bridge historical divisions (TSPSC 2023).
[123:0:6] # Constitutional remedies
[123:0:7] - **Reservations** for SCs, STs, OBCs aim at **social justice and equal opportunity**.
[123:0:8] - **Art. 46 (DPSP):** promotion of **educational and economic interests** of SCs, STs and other weaker sections.
[123:0:9] - **10 Aug 2026:** the Union **opposes a creamy layer for SCs/STs**, since income alone does not end caste-based exclusion.
[123:0:10] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society 02 p.1; NextGen Society G2 Part 3 p.36; NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.66; Laxmikanth 8th ed. (Art. 46); app notes 2:123 sub 7
## SUB 1 | MGNREGA and the VB-G RAM G Act
[123:1:0] TABLE head: Event | Date
  [123:1:0.0] NREGA passed by Parliament | August 2005 (assent September 2005)
  [123:1:0.1] In force | 2 February 2006, launched in Anantapur district (AP); all districts from 1 April 2008
  [123:1:0.2] Renamed MGNREGA | 2 October 2009
  [123:1:0.3] VB-G RAM G Act, 2025 | Passed 18 Dec 2025; in force 1 July 2026, replacing MGNREGA
[123:1:1] # Provisions (MGNREGA)
[123:1:2] - **Legal right** to at least **100 days** of unskilled wage work in a **financial year** per **rural household**; **adult members of any rural household** willing to do unskilled manual work are eligible (not only SC/ST/BPL).
[123:1:3] - Aim: guarantee the **"Right to Work"**; livelihood security, durable assets, **strengthening the livelihood resource base of the poor**, **proactive social inclusion**, **strengthening PRIs** – not higher education.
[123:1:4] - Work within **5 km**; no work within **15 days** of demand → **unemployment allowance**.
[123:1:5] - At least **one-third** of workers must be **women** (not half).
[123:1:6] - **Gram Sabha:** recommends works, monitors them, holds **social audits**; **Gram Panchayat** registers households, **receives work applications and issues dated receipts**.
[123:1:7] - Follows the **Directive Principles** (Art. 41); the right to work is **not** in Part III (Fundamental Rights).
[123:1:8] # VB-G RAM G Act, 2025 (now)
[123:1:9] - **Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin)**; Ministry of Rural Development.
[123:1:10] - **125 days** per rural household per year; wage floor **₹300/day** (2026-27); funding **60:40** (NE/Himalayan 90:10).
[123:1:11] - **AP ranked first** in implementation (2.10 crore person-days in the first 45 days).
[123:1:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy P1 01 p.57; Rocket CA 2026-01 p.156; PMF Lens Jul 2026 p.61; Praveen CA360 Aug 2026 p.35; app notes 2:123 sub 6

## SUB 3 | Poverty – Concepts, Causes and Indicators
[123:3:0] TABLE head: Concept | Meaning | Measured by
  [123:3:0.0] Absolute poverty | Spending below the minimum for basic needs | A fixed poverty line (MPCE cut-off)
  [123:3:0.1] Relative poverty | Being poor compared with others in the same society | Inequality / share of income
  [123:3:0.2] Multidimensional poverty | Deprivation in health, education, living standards | National MPI (NITI Aayog)
[123:3:1] - India's official poverty line is **consumption-based (MPCE)**, not income-based.
[123:3:2] - **MPCE 2011-12 (NSS 68th round, MMRP):** about **₹1,430 rural** and **₹2,630 urban** – a gap of about **₹1,200** (urban ~84% higher).
[123:3:3] - **Causes (NextGen):** **illiteracy and lack of quality education** (main cause for rural and tribal poor), unemployment, disguised unemployment in agriculture, discrimination, health costs, inequality.
[123:3:4] - **Social indicators:** illiteracy, malnutrition, poor health care, lack of safe drinking water and sanitation; **size of the house is NOT** one.
[123:3:5] - **Culture of poverty:** **Oscar Lewis (1959)** – fatalism and poverty passed down generations.

=== NOTES 2:124 | Human Rights – Concept, Generations, Udhr, Iccpr, Icescr and Un Machinery
## SUB 0 | Concept of Human Rights – Meaning, Thinkers and Milestones
[124:0:0] # Meaning
[124:0:1] - **Human rights:** rights given to **all human beings by nature**, simply because they are human – **inherent, inalienable, universal**; essential conditions of social life without which no one can lead a normal life.
[124:0:2] - **Core value:** **dignity of man as a human being** (UDHR Preamble: inherent dignity and equal rights); **indivisible and interdependent** – civil-political and socio-economic rights stand together (**Vienna Declaration, 1993**).
[124:0:3] - **Rights vs the State:** rights are **claims of the citizens against the State** – limits on State power, not claims of the State on citizens or privileges of a few.
[124:0:4] - Every **Fundamental Right** is a human right, but **not every human right is a Fundamental Right**.
[124:0:5] - **Kinds of rights:** **economic rights** give a **reasonable and legal source of livelihood** (work, wages, leisure); **political rights** = vote, contest, hold office (the **right to vote is a political right**; in India only a **statutory right** – **Sec. 62, RP Act 1951**, not an FR); civil rights = life, liberty, religion.
[124:0:6] - **Values:** "respect for human rights and responsibilities" is a **constitutional value**; in a **democracy** equality is accepted as a **legal norm**, which makes it easier to guarantee women's freedom and dignity (it does not ensure equal representation).
[124:0:7] - **Violations (NDA 2010):** detaining a person on the way to vote and killing a civilian in a combing operation are **both** human-rights violations.
[124:0:8] # Thinkers
[124:0:9] TABLE head: Thinker | Idea
  [124:0:9.0] John Locke (English/British thinker) | Natural rights – life, liberty, property; popularised the doctrine; "father" of the human-rights idea
  [124:0:9.1] J.J. Rousseau | "Man is born free, but everywhere he is in chains" (Social Contract, 1762)
  [124:0:9.2] Ronald Dworkin (printed "Ronald Darwin") | Rights as trumps: a protective shield for the individual when the State interferes with civil liberties
  [124:0:9.3] Jack Donnelly (printed "John Dowski") | Human rights as a new standard of civilisation (1998)
  [124:0:9.4] Olympe de Gouges | Declaration of the Rights of Woman and the Female Citizen (1791), French Revolution
  [124:0:9.5] Mary Wollstonecraft | A Vindication of the Rights of Woman (1792) – not the 1791 Declaration

## SUB 1 | UDHR 1948 and Human Rights Day
[124:1:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [124:1:0.0] Adopted by | UN General Assembly, 10 December 1948 (Paris)
  [124:1:0.1] Human Rights Day | 10 December every year (UNGA resolution 423(V), 1950)
  [124:1:0.2] Drafting chair | Eleanor Roosevelt
  [124:1:0.3] Structure | Preamble + 30 articles
  [124:1:0.4] Legal status | A declaration – not a treaty, not legally binding
  [124:1:0.5] Core value | Human dignity
[124:1:1] - **Covers** civil, political, economic, social and cultural rights; **guides the SDGs Agenda** (not the Paris Agreement, Magna Carta or NAFTA).
[124:1:2] - **Included:** dignity, liberty, equality (Art. 1); **life, liberty, security (Art. 3)**; equality before law (Art. 7); **privacy (Art. 12)**; social security (Art. 22); **work (Art. 23)**; **adequate standard of living (Art. 25)**; **education (Art. 26)**; **duties to the community (Art. 29)**.
[124:1:3] - **Art. 25(1):** security in the event of **unemployment, sickness, disability, widowhood, old age** – linked to an adequate standard of living (food, clothing, housing, medical care).
[124:1:4] - **NOT included:** **right to universal basic income** (JPSC 2023).
[124:1:5] - **⚠ Privacy IS a UDHR right (Art. 12)** and an ICCPR right (Art. 17); the JPSC 2016 key that called privacy "not a UN-listed human right" is wrong.

## SUB 4 | Generations of Human Rights
[124:4:0] - **Karel Vasak (1977)** classified rights into three generations, linked to **liberty, equality, fraternity**.
[124:4:1] TABLE head: Generation | Ideal | Rights
  [124:4:1.0] First | Liberty | Civil and political: life, speech, fair trial, vote, participation in political life
  [124:4:1.1] Second | Equality | Economic, social, cultural: work, education, health, social security, eradication of social discrimination
  [124:4:1.2] Third | Fraternity / solidarity | Collective: healthy environment, development, peace

## SUB 4 | Functions and Powers
[126:4:0] # Functions (Section 12)
[126:4:1] - **Inquire** into violation of human rights or **negligence by a public servant** – **suo motu**, on a petition by a victim or any person, or on a court order.
[126:4:2] - **Intervene** in court proceedings involving human-rights allegations – **with the court's approval**.
[126:4:3] - **Visit jails** and detention places (State-run institutions too, not "Central only"; since 2006 without intimating the State Govt) and recommend.
[126:4:4] - Review constitutional and legal safeguards and factors **including terrorism**; study international treaties.
[126:4:5] - **Undertake and promote research**; spread **human-rights literacy**; **encourage NGOs** (both "encourages NGOs" and "conducts research" are true).
[126:4:6] # Powers and working

[126:4:9] - **Cannot punish** violators or award relief itself; recommendations **not binding**; Government informs action taken within **one month**.
