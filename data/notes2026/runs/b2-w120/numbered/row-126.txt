## SUB 0 | Status and Establishment of the NHRC
[0:0] - **NHRC = statutory body**, **not constitutional**, set up under the **Protection of Human Rights Act (PHRA), 1993** passed by Parliament – **not by an executive order** of the President.
[0:1] TABLE head: Event | Date
  [0:1.0] Ordinance (Art. 123); Act deemed in force | 28 September 1993
  [0:1.1] NHRC constituted | 12 October 1993
  [0:1.2] Act received assent | January 1994
[0:2] - **"Constitutional body" and "established in 1994 under the PHRA 1994"** are **both false** (NHRC: 1993, PHRA 1993).
[0:3] - **Constitutional vs statutory (UPSC 2023):** of NCBC, NHRC, Law Commission, NCDRC only **NCBC** is constitutional (**102nd Amendment, 2018**, Art. 338B); NHRC statutory; Law Commission executive; NCDRC statutory (Consumer Protection Act).
[0:4] - Watchdog of rights relating to **life, liberty, equality and dignity** – rights like **education are not listed** in the PHRA definition.
[0:5] - Framed in the light of the **Paris Principles** (independence, pluralism, broad mandate). Headquarters: **New Delhi**.
[0:6] - **First Chairperson:** Justice **Ranganath Misra** (12 Oct 1993 – 24 Nov 1996). **Present:** Justice **V. Ramasubramanian** (as of Feb 2026).
[0:7] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. Part 2 p.226, 228; Rocket Polity p.68; app notes 2:126 sub 0
## SUB 1 | NHRC Composition
[1:0] - **Multi-member body: Chairperson + five full-time members** (since 2019; under the 1993 Act Chairperson + **four** members).
[1:1] TABLE head: Component | Who
  [1:1.0] Chairperson | Former Chief Justice of India or (since 2019) a former Judge of the Supreme Court; under the 1993 Act only a retired CJI
  [1:1.1] Member | Serving or retired SC Judge
  [1:1.2] Member | Serving or retired HC Chief Justice
  [1:1.3] Members | Three persons with human-rights knowledge, at least one a woman (earlier two)
  [1:1.4] Ex-officio (deemed) members | Seven chairpersons/commissioners (below)
[1:2] - **Deemed members:** chairpersons of **NCM, NCSC, NCST, NCW** (before 2019; NCSC and NCST added 2006) + **NCBC, NCPCR** and the **Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities** (added 2019). Today **all of NCSC, NCM, NCW and NCBC** are deemed members.
[1:3] - Deemed members are **not equal in status** to full-time members; the **CCPD** is the only one who is not a commission chair.
[1:4] - **Secretary-General** (Chief Executive Officer of the Commission): **administrative and financial powers** (not judicial) under the Chairperson's control.
[1:5] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. Part 2 p.226, 233-234; app notes 2:126 sub 1
## SUB 2 | Appointment, Term and Removal
[2:0] - **Appointed by the President** (not the PM, CJI or Home Minister) on the recommendation of a **six-member committee**:
[2:1] TABLE head: Selection committee (Section 4)
  [2:1.0] Prime Minister – head
  [2:1.1] Speaker of the Lok Sabha
  [2:1.2] Union Home Minister
  [2:1.3] Leader of Opposition, Lok Sabha
  [2:1.4] Leader of Opposition, Rajya Sabha
  [2:1.5] Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha
[2:2] - The **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** (Vice-President) and the **CJI** are **NOT** members.
[2:3] - A sitting SC Judge or sitting HC Chief Justice is appointed only **after consulting the CJI**.
[2:4] - **Term (since 2019):** **3 years or age 70**, whichever earlier; **eligible for reappointment**. **Before 2019:** **5 years** or 70; a member could be reappointed for **one more five-year term**, the Chairperson could not.
[2:5] - After office: **ineligible for further employment** under the Central or any State Government. Salaries fixed by the Centre, not varied to disadvantage.
[2:6] - **Resignation** in writing to the **President**.
[2:7] - **Removal by the President:** insolvency, paid outside employment, infirmity of mind or body, unsound mind, conviction and imprisonment; for **proved misbehaviour or incapacity** only after the **Supreme Court inquires** on the President's reference and advises removal.
[2:8] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. Part 2 p.226-227; Rocket Polity p.68; app notes 2:126 sub 2
## SUB 3 | Functions and Powers
[3:0] # Functions (Section 12)
[3:1] - **Inquire** into violation of human rights or **negligence by a public servant** – **suo motu**, on a petition by a victim or any person, or on a court order.
[3:2] - **Intervene** in court proceedings involving human-rights allegations – **with the court's approval**.
[3:3] - **Visit jails** and detention places (State-run institutions too, not "Central only"; since 2006 without intimating the State Govt) and recommend.
[3:4] - Review constitutional and legal safeguards and factors **including terrorism**; study international treaties.
[3:5] - **Undertake and promote research**; spread **human-rights literacy**; **encourage NGOs** (both "encourages NGOs" and "conducts research" are true).
[3:6] # Powers and working
[3:7] - **All powers of a civil court** (CPC 1908) – summon, examine on oath, documents, requisition public records; proceedings have a **judicial character**; own investigating staff; may use any Central/State officer or agency.
[3:8] - On inquiry it may **recommend** compensation or damages, prosecution, **interim relief**; approach the SC/HC for directions or writs.
[3:9] - **Cannot punish** violators or award relief itself; recommendations **not binding**; Government informs action taken within **one month**.
[3:10] # Specialised mandates
[3:11] TABLE head: Mandate | Detail
  [3:11.0] Silicosis | NHRC interventions and a special report to Parliament on silicosis
  [3:11.1] Bonded labour | SC (1997) entrusted monitoring of the Bonded Labour Act 1976
  [3:11.2] Custodial deaths | Report to NHRC within 24 hours; post-mortem videography; independent investigation
  [3:11.3] Encounter deaths | Reporting + magisterial inquiry (NHRC guidelines; PUCL v Maharashtra 2014)
  [3:11.4] Transgender welfare | Advisory 2.0 (May 2026)

## SUB 4 | Limitations of the Commissions
[4:0] TABLE head: Limitation | Detail
  [4:0.0] Time bar | No inquiry after one year of the alleged act (not two years)
  [4:0.1] Armed forces | Only seeks a report from the Central Government and recommends; Centre informs action within three months
  [4:0.2] Purview | Violations by public servants; not purely private disputes
  [4:0.3] Overlap | Not a matter already before an SHRC or another statutory commission
  [4:0.4] Non-binding | Recommendations advisory – "toothless tiger"
[4:1] - **Constraints (NHRC itself):** administrative, financial (grants-in-aid from MHA) and **manpower** shortages; about one lakh complaints a year.
[4:2] - **30 Apr 2026:** Allahabad HC said NHRC exceeded its mandate by directing an EOW probe into 558 aided madrasas.
[4:3] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. Part 2 p.228-230; app notes 2:126 sub 6
## SUB 5 | State Human Rights Commissions
[5:0] - **Optional:** a State Government **may** constitute an SHRC under the PHRA 1993; headquarters as the State notifies.
[5:1] TABLE head: Item | SHRC
  [5:1.0] Strength | Chairperson + two members (three in all; cut from five in 2006)
  [5:1.1] Chairperson | Retired HC Chief Justice or HC Judge (Judge added 2019)
  [5:1.2] Members | Serving/retired HC Judge or District Judge with 7 years' experience; a human-rights expert
  [5:1.3] Selection committee (Sec. 22) | Chief Minister (head), Speaker, State Home Minister, LoP in the Assembly; + Chairman and LoP of the Council where bicameral
  [5:1.4] Appointed by | Governor
  [5:1.5] Removed by | President only (same grounds and SC-inquiry procedure as NHRC)
  [5:1.6] Consultation | Sitting HC Judge or District Judge appointed only after consulting the Chief Justice of the HC concerned
  [5:1.7] Acting Chairperson | Governor authorises a member in a vacancy
  [5:1.8] Resignation | To the Governor
  [5:1.9] Annual report | To the State Government → laid before the State Legislature with a memorandum of action taken
[5:2] - **Jurisdiction:** only **State List and Concurrent List** subjects (not State List alone); does not inquire into a case the NHRC is probing.
[5:3] - **UTs:** the Centre may confer UT functions on an SHRC **except Delhi, J&K and Ladakh** – those are with the **NHRC** (2019 amendment + 2020 adaptation orders).
[5:4] - **Powers:** like the NHRC – inquires suo motu, visits jails, reviews safeguards; can only **recommend** compensation to the Government; **cannot punish**.
[5:5] - **AP SHRC:** constituted by the AP Government; headquarters **Kurnool** (office inaugurated **Sept 2021**, chair Justice **M. Seetharama Murti**); reported without a chairperson since March 2024; **28 Jun 2026:** NHRC sought reports from the **AP SHRC, DGP and Chief Secretary** in the Gade Sai Krishna custodial death.
[5:6] > SHRC: appointed by the Governor (5 PYQs), removed by the President, CM heads the committee, report to the State Govt.
[5:7] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. Part 2 p.231-234; web: search results on PHRA s.21 (Ladakh adaptation order 2020); app notes 2:126 sub 3, 10
## SUB 6 | Amendments 2006 and 2019; Human Rights Courts; GANHRI
[6:0] TABLE head: Year | Change
  [6:0.0] 2006 | NCSC and NCST chairs became deemed members; resignation to President / Governor
  [6:0.1] 2006 | Jail visits without intimating the State Govt; interim relief during inquiry; complaints transferable to SHRCs
  [6:0.2] 2006 | SHRC 5 → 3 members
  [6:0.3] 2019 | SC Judge eligible as NHRC Chair; term 5 → 3 years; reappointment allowed
  [6:0.4] 2019 | Expert members 2 → 3 (one woman); deemed members 4 → 7; HC Judge eligible as SHRC Chair; UT functions to SHRCs (not Delhi)
[6:1] - **All three (APPSC Group 1, 2023):** SC Judge can chair, 3-year term, reappointment – **true**.
[6:2] - **Human Rights Courts (Sec. 30):** a **Court of Session** in every district (State Govt + **HC Chief Justice's concurrence**); **Special Public Prosecutor (Sec. 31)** – advocate of **7 years**.
[6:3] - **NHRC annual report** → Central Govt (and State Govt concerned) → laid before **each House of Parliament** with a memorandum of action taken.
[6:4] - **GANHRI** accredits NHRIs (Sub-Committee on Accreditation; **'A' = fully compliant**). **May 2024:** re-accreditation of NHRC-India **deferred** for the second year running.
