## SUB 1 | Lok Sabha – Composition and Strength (Articles 81, 330, 331)
[1:0] - **Art. 81**: not more than **530** members from **States** (direct election from territorial constituencies) + not more than **20** from **UTs** (as Parliament provides – UT (Direct Election to the House of the People) Act, 1965) → **maximum 550**.
[1:1] TABLE head: Figure | Meaning
  [1:1.0] 550 | Present maximum (530 + 20) – "full/maximum strength" today
  [1:1.1] 552 | Old maximum "fixed by the Constitution" = 530 + 20 + 2 Anglo-Indians (Art. 331), till 25 Jan 2020
  [1:1.2] 545 | Old actual strength = 543 elected + 2 nominated Anglo-Indians
  [1:1.3] 543 | Present strength = 524 from States + 19 from UTs
  [1:1.4] 20 | Maximum UT seats ("reserved for / represent UTs" in book keys); 19 actually filled now
[1:2] - Some keys ask the 104th-era question as "the maximum per the Constitution" → **552 before 2020, 550 now**.
[1:3] - **Art. 331**: the **President** (not the PM or the Minorities Commission) could nominate **2 Anglo-Indians** if the community was not adequately represented. **104th Amendment Act, 2019** did **not extend** it – nomination **done away with from 25 Jan 2020**; **no nominated members in the LS now**. (Governor: one Anglo-Indian per Assembly, Art. 333 – also ended.)
[1:4] - **104th Amendment** also **extended SC/ST reservation in the LS and State Assemblies by 10 years (to 2030)**. (10% EWS = 103rd; NCBC constitutional status = 102nd; Sixth Schedule councils' powers = 125th Amendment Bill.)
[1:5] - **Art. 330**: SC/ST seats in the LS (first for **10 years**, extended every 10 years); also in Assemblies (Art. 332) and Panchayats (Art. 243D) – **no reservation in the Rajya Sabha**.
[1:6] - Strength history: **7th Amendment, 1956** – LS **520** (uniform representation, Art. 81(2)); **31st Amendment, 1973** – **525 → 545** (States 500 → **525**, UTs 25 → 20) and the 530 + 20 limits. (The **1st Amendment, 1951** dealt with Art. 15(4) SC/ST special provisions, Art. 19(2) reasonable restrictions and the Ninth Schedule for zamindari abolition – **not** LS seats.)
[1:7] - **Art. 326**: elections to the LS and Assemblies on **universal adult suffrage** – voting age 21 → **18** by the **61st Amendment, 1988** (in force 1989).
[1:8] - LS = the **Popular Chamber / House of the People / First chamber** – members directly elected by the people (RS = indirect).
[1:9] - First LS: **489** elected seats.

## SUB 2 | Parliament – Article 79 and the President's Place
[2:0] - **Article 79**: "There shall be a Parliament for the Union which shall consist of the **President** and two Houses – the **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)** and the **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**." So Parliament = **President + RS + LS** – **not** the PM, the Council of Ministers, the Vice-President, the Speaker/Chairman or the Secretariat.
[2:1] - Parliament is the **Union Legislature** (legislative body), in **Part V**. Laxmikanth: **Articles 79–122** deal with Parliament (NextGen and keys: "**79 to 123**", counting the ordinance Article).
[2:2] - President is an **integral part** because **a Bill passed by both Houses cannot become law without his assent** (Art. 111) – the "**President-in-Parliament**" like the **Crown-in-Parliament** in Britain; the US President is not part of Congress. This "President is part of Parliament" is called the most distinctive feature.
[2:3] - **Article 59(1)**: the President is **not a member** of either House or a State Legislature; an MP/MLA elected President vacates his seat. He does **not sit or take part in discussions**.
[2:4] - President's legislative role: summons, prorogues, dissolves the LS; **Art. 86** – may address either or both Houses; **Art. 87** – **special address** to both Houses at the first session after each general election and the first session of each year; assent (absolute, suspensive, pocket veto); nominates **12** to RS; **ordinances (Art. 123)** when Parliament is not in session.
[2:5] - The **Lok Sabha** is a **constitutional** body (Arts. 79, 81) – the Planning Commission, NDC and the Deputy PM post are **extra-constitutional / not in the Constitution**.

## SUB 3 | Rajya Sabha – Composition, Election and Nominated Members (Article 80)
[3:0] - **Maximum 250** = not more than **238** representatives of **States and UTs** (indirectly elected) + **12 nominated** by the President. **Present 245** = 225 States + **8 UTs** + 12 nominated (233 elected).
[3:1] - Election (Art. 80(4)): by the **elected members of the State Legislative Assembly**, by **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote (STV)** – not non-transferable, cumulative or weighted vote. Nominated MLAs and MLCs do not vote.
[3:2] - **UTs**: only **Delhi (3), Puducherry (1), J&K (4)** – by an electoral college; **Lakshadweep, A&N, Chandigarh, Ladakh, DNH&DD have no RS seat**.
[3:3] - **Nominated (Art. 80(3))**: **12** by the **President** for special knowledge or practical experience in **literature, science, art and social service** – **not judicial matters, not sport** (sport is not listed).
[3:4] - Nominees take full part in debates; **vote in the VP's election, not the President's**; may join a party within **6 months**.

## SUB 4 | Terms of the Houses; Lok Sabhas and General Elections (Article 83)
[4:0] - **Rajya Sabha – Art. 83(1)**: a **permanent House**, **never dissolved** (not even by the President); **one-third of members retire every second year**; a member's **6-year** term is fixed by the **RPA, 1951**; casual vacancy filled for the rest of the term; first RS (1952) – retirees chosen by **lottery**.
[4:1] - **Lok Sabha – Art. 83(2)**: **5 years from its first meeting**; may be **dissolved earlier** by the President on the PM's advice. During a National Emergency it can be extended by law **one year at a time**, not beyond **6 months** after the Emergency ends (5th LS, 1971–77).

## SUB 6 | Rajya Sabha vs Lok Sabha – Equal Powers and Special Powers
[6:0] TABLE head: Equal | Lok Sabha superior
  [6:0.0] Ordinary and Constitutional Amendment Bills (no joint sitting for amendments – Bill fails) | Money Bill only in LS (Art. 109); RS cannot reject or amend it – only recommends within 14 days
  [6:0.1] Election and impeachment of President; election of VP | Speaker certifies Money Bill; presides over joint sitting (Art. 108)
  [6:0.2] Ordinances; all three emergencies | RS discusses Budget but cannot vote on demands for grants
  [6:0.3] Removal of judges, CEC, CAG | Only LS can discontinue a National Emergency
  [6:0.4] Considering CAG, UPSC, Finance Commission reports | No-confidence / censure only in LS – ministers are responsible to LS (Art. 75(3))
[6:1] - **No joint sitting for a Money Bill** because the **Lok Sabha has overriding powers** – the RS cannot block it, so no deadlock can arise.
[6:2] - **Financial Bill (I)**: introduced only in LS; equal powers in passage. **No tax without LS approval.**
[6:3] - A Bill becomes law only when the **President gives assent** (Art. 111) – not on Cabinet approval, passage alone or SC validation.
[6:4] TABLE head: Article | Special power of RS | Condition
  [6:4.0] 249 | Authorise Parliament to legislate on a State List subject in national interest | Two-thirds present and voting; valid 1 year, renewable
  [6:4.1] 312 | Create new All-India Services (so RS does not share this "equally" with LS) | Same two-thirds resolution
  [6:4.2] 67(b) | Start removal of the VP | Only in RS
[6:5] - RS can **not** initiate censure of ministers, Money Bills or appointment of judges.
[6:6] - **LS dissolved**: an Emergency survives if **RS approves**; new LS must approve within **30 days** of its first sitting.
[6:7] - **Presiding officers**: RS is presided over by a **non-member** – the **Vice-President, ex-officio Chairman (Art. 89)**, elected by the **members of both Houses** (Art. 66); Deputy Chairman elected by RS from its members. (LS Speaker, Council Chairman, Assembly Speaker are members.)

## SUB 8 | Disqualification and Vacation of Seats
[8:0] - **Art. 102** grounds: **office of profit** (exempted posts apart – by a **law of Parliament**, Prevention of Disqualification Act 1959), **unsound mind declared by a competent court**, **undischarged insolvent**, loss of citizenship, a law of Parliament (RPA 1951) – **not lack of a degree**. "Office of profit" is **defined neither in the Constitution nor in the RPA**.
[8:1] - **Art. 103**: the **President** decides, on the **Election Commission's opinion**; **Art. 101(3)(a)**: seat vacant **immediately** – no House resolution needed.
[8:2] - **Tenth Schedule**: decided by the **Speaker / Chairman**, subject to judicial review – **Kihoto Hollohan (1992)**; vote against whip unless **condoned within 15 days**; nominated member joining a party after 6 months.
[8:3] TABLE head: Situation | Result

  [8:3.1] Two seats in the LS | Choose one, else both vacant
  [8:3.2] Parliament + State legislature | Resign State seat within 14 days, else Parliament seat vacant
  [8:3.3] Absence 60 days (Art. 101(4)) | House may declare seat vacant; prorogation and adjournments of more than 4 consecutive days excluded
[8:4] - Resignation in writing to the **Speaker / Chairman**; **33rd Amendment, 1974** – may be rejected if not voluntary or genuine.

## SUB 9 | Sessions, Procedure and Privileges
[9:0] - **Art. 85**: President summons each House; **maximum six months** between sessions; **no minimum sitting days**. Usually **Budget, Monsoon, Winter**.
[9:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [9:1.0] Adjournment / adjournment sine die | Ends a sitting – Speaker/Chairman
  [9:1.1] Prorogation (85(2)) | President ends the session, not the House
  [9:1.2] Recess | Gap between prorogation and new session – ordinances possible (Art. 123)
  [9:1.3] Dissolution | Ends the House – Lok Sabha only
[9:2] - Sequence: adjournment sine die → prorogation → recess → new session. **Quorum (Art. 100(3))**: **one-tenth**. **Art. 118**: rules of procedure; **Art. 120**: business in **Hindi or English**. RS Secretariat under the **Chairman**.
[9:3] - **Privileges**: **Art. 105** – freedom of speech in the House; **Art. 105(3)** uncodified, historically those of the **British House of Commons**; also extend to ministers and the **Attorney-General**; **Art. 122** – courts cannot question proceedings for **irregularity of procedure**.
