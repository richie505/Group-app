## SUB 2 | Vice-President – Office, Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, Election (Arts. 63, 64, 66, 71, 79, 89)
[27:2:0] # Office
[27:2:1] - **Article 63**: "There shall be a Vice-President of India" – the **second-highest office** (second highest dignitary) after the President; modelled on the **American Vice-President**, but unlike the US VP he does **not succeed** to the Presidency for the rest of the term – he only **acts** until a new President enters office.
[27:2:2] - **Articles 64 and 89(1)**: the VP is the **ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Council of States)** – the VP **presides over the sessions of the Rajya Sabha**. Not the President, PM, Speaker, Deputy Speaker, CJI, CEC or Leader of Opposition; the Chairman is **not separately elected, appointed or nominated**.
[27:2:3] - The VP is **not a member** of either House or of any State Legislature (a member elected VP vacates the seat) – so "VP is a member of the Rajya Sabha" is **false**.
[27:2:4] - As Chairman he does **not vote in the first instance**; he has a **casting vote** in a tie. He does not preside while a resolution for **his own removal** is under consideration.
[27:2:5] - He has a **formal function** – chairing the Rajya Sabha (so "no formal function attached to his office" is **false**). Ex officio **President of the Indian Council of World Affairs (ICWA)**.
[27:2:6] - **Article 79**: Parliament = **the President + the Council of States + the House of the People** – the VP, the PM and the Council of Ministers are **not** parts of Parliament.
[27:2:7] # Election (Article 66)
[27:2:8] - **Elected, not appointed** – the only one of VP / PM / Governor / CEC who is **not appointed by the President**.
[27:2:9] - Electoral college = **all members of both Houses of Parliament** – **elected and nominated** MPs (**788** when full). **State Legislative Assemblies and Councils have no role** – so the VP's electoral college is **not** "similar to the President's".
[27:2:10] - **Proportional representation by single transferable vote**, **secret ballot**; each MP's vote has equal value; **20 proposers + 20 seconders**; governed by the **Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952**.
[27:2:11] TABLE head: Point | President | Vice-President
  [27:2:11.0] Electoral college | Elected MPs + elected MLAs (States, Delhi, Puducherry) | All MPs (elected + nominated); no MLAs
  [27:2:11.1] Qualified for election as member of | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha
  [27:2:11.2] Proposers / seconders | 50 / 50 | 20 / 20
[27:2:12] - **Qualifications**: citizen; **35 years**; qualified for election as a member of the **Rajya Sabha** (not the Lok Sabha); no office of profit.
[27:2:13] - **Article 71**: all doubts and disputes about the VP's election are decided by the **Supreme Court** – final (not the Speaker, President or Parliament); acts done before the election is declared void stay valid. **39th Amendment (1975)** removed the SC's power over President/VP election disputes; **44th Amendment (1978)** restored it.

## SUB 4 | Vice-President – Term, Oath, Resignation, Removal, Vacancy; Acting President (Arts. 65, 67-70)
[27:4:0] # Term, oath, resignation (Articles 67, 69)
[27:4:1] - Term **5 years**; continues **until his successor enters upon office** (Art. 67(c)); **re-election any number of times**.
[27:4:2] - **Oath** administered by the **President** (or a person appointed by him) – Art. 69.
[27:4:3] - **Resigns** by writing to the **President** (Art. 67(a)) – not the PM or Parliament.
[27:4:4] # Removal (Article 67(b))
[27:4:5] - Resolution moved **only in the Rajya Sabha** (not the Lok Sabha, either House or a joint sitting), passed by a **majority of all the then members** of the Rajya Sabha (effective majority) and **agreed to by the Lok Sabha** (simple majority); **14 days' notice**; **no grounds** stated; not impeachment.
[27:4:6] - "Removed by a two-thirds majority in the Rajya Sabha" – **false** (two-thirds is the President's impeachment).
[27:4:7] # Vacancy (Article 68)
[27:4:8] - Vacancy by expiry: election before the term ends. Casual vacancy (death, resignation, removal): filled **as soon as possible** – **no six-month deadline**; the new VP gets a **full 5-year term**.
[27:4:9] - The Constitution is **silent** on who performs the VP's duties during a vacancy; the **Deputy Chairman** only performs the duties of the **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** – he does **not** "act as Vice-President".
[27:4:10] # Acting President (Article 65)
[27:4:11] - On a **vacancy** in the President's office (death, resignation, removal) the VP **acts as President until a new President is elected** and enters office – a **maximum of six months**, because **Art. 62** requires the new President to be elected **within six months** of the vacancy.
[27:4:12] - When the President is **absent, ill or unable**, the VP **discharges his functions** until he resumes.
[27:4:13] - While acting: he gets the **powers, immunities and emoluments of the President**; he does **not perform the duties of Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** (the **Deputy Chairman** presides) and draws no Chairman's salary.
[27:4:14] - If both offices are vacant (e.g. the acting VP resigns), the **Chief Justice of India** (then the senior-most SC judge) discharges the President's functions – **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969**.
[27:4:15] TABLE head: Point | President | Vice-President
  [27:4:15.0] Resigns to | Vice-President (Art. 56(1)(a)) | President (Art. 67(a))
  [27:4:15.1] Removal | Impeachment (Art. 61), two-thirds of total membership of each House | RS resolution (majority of all members) + LS agrees
  [27:4:15.2] Vacancy filled | Within 6 months (Art. 62) | As soon as possible (Art. 68)
[27:4:16] - On the President's resignation, the VP immediately informs the **Speaker of the Lok Sabha** (Art. 56(2)).
[27:4:17] > Exam angle: removal starts only in the RS; while acting as President he does not chair the RS but gets the President's emoluments.

## SUB 5 | Prime Minister – Appointment, Membership, Tenure (Art. 75)
[27:5:0] # Appointment (Article 75(1))
[27:5:1] - **"The Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President"** – **Art. 75(1)** (not Arts. 32, 70, 74, 79, 356, 348); **other Ministers** are appointed by the President **on the advice of the PM**. The PM is **not elected** by Parliament or the Lok Sabha and is not merely "nominated by the majority party".
[27:5:2] - By convention the President appoints the **leader of the majority party / coalition in the Lok Sabha**; in a hung House he uses **discretion** and may ask for a floor test later.
[27:5:3] - The PM has **full discretion** in choosing his Ministers (any person, MP or not) – the President does not counsel him and has no discretion here. The President **cannot appoint a Minister not recommended by the PM**.
[27:5:4] TABLE head: Article | Content
  [27:5:4.0] 54 | Election of the President
  [27:5:4.1] 75 | Appointment of the PM and Council of Ministers
  [27:5:4.2] 155 | Appointment of the Governor
  [27:5:4.3] 164 | Appointment of the CM and State Council of Ministers
[27:5:5] # Membership
[27:5:6] - The PM **need not be an MP when appointed** but must become a member of **either House within six months** (not "of the Lok Sabha" only).
[27:5:7] - May belong to **either House** (UK: Commons by convention); **generally a member of the Lok Sabha**. A PM from the **Rajya Sabha cannot vote in the Lok Sabha** (e.g. on a no-confidence motion) but can speak there.
[27:5:8] # Tenure, salary, death
[27:5:9] - **No fixed tenure**; formally holds office during the **President's pleasure** (not Parliament's); in practice while he has the **Lok Sabha's confidence**. No limit on terms.
[27:5:10] - Salary and allowances fixed by **Parliament** (not the President).
[27:5:11] - **Death or resignation of the PM automatically dissolves the Council of Ministers**; a Minister's death only creates a vacancy.
[27:5:12] - **Convention, not text**: the **PM resigns on losing the majority in the Lok Sabha** (the Constitution does not say so). The Finance Minister's House, regional representation and the Speaker acting as President are not rules at all.
[27:5:13] - The **Prime Minister is the most important political institution in the country** (NCERT Class 9, "Working of Institutions").
[27:5:14] > Exam angle: "appointed by the President" (×8) and "within six months, either House".

## SUB 6 | Articles 74, 77, 78 – Advice, Name of the President, Duties of the PM
[27:6:0] TABLE head: Article | Content
  [27:6:0.0] 74(1) | A Council of Ministers headed by the PM to aid and advise the President, who shall act in accordance with the advice; he may ask once for reconsideration (44th Amdt, 1978), then the advice is binding
  [27:6:0.1] 74(2) | Advice tendered cannot be inquired into by any court
  [27:6:0.2] 77(1) | All executive action of the Government of India is expressed to be taken in the name of the President (not the PM or "the Government of India")
  [27:6:0.3] 77(2) | Orders authenticated as per rules made by the President
  [27:6:0.4] 77(3) | The President makes rules for the convenient transaction of business and its allocation among Ministers – Allocation of Business Rules and Transaction of Business Rules, 1961
  [27:6:0.5] 78 | Duties of the PM: (a) communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to administration and proposals for legislation; (b) furnish information the President calls for; (c) submit for the Council's consideration a matter decided by a single Minister if the President requires

[27:6:2] - Under **Art. 78** the PM is the **bridge / link between the President and the Council of Ministers** – not Arts. 76, 79 or 81. Administering Ministers' oaths is **not** an Art. 78 duty (the President does it).
[27:6:3] - **Article 53**: the **executive power of the Union is vested in the President** (not the PM); executive power means **carrying on the business of government** (residuary functions after legislation and adjudication) – true, but not the reason for vesting it in the President.
[27:6:4] - State parallel: executive action of a State is taken in the **name of the Governor** (Art. 166).

## SUB 7 | Size of the Council of Ministers – 91st Amendment, 2003
[27:7:0] - Before 2003 the Constitution set **no limit**; the **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003** inserted **Art. 75(1A)**: total Ministers **including the PM** shall **not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha** (543 → max **81**) – not 10%, 12% or 20%.
[27:7:1] - **Art. 164(1A)** (States): **15% of the Assembly**, including the CM, **minimum 12**; 294-member Assembly → max **44**.
[27:7:2] - Aim: curb jumbo ministries and **strengthen the anti-defection law** (also inserted 75(1B), 164(1B)).

  [27:7:3.2] 93rd (2005) | OBC reservation in educational institutions (Art. 15(5))

## SUB 9 | Council of Ministers – Responsibility, Non-member Ministers, Rights in Parliament (Arts. 75, 88)
[27:9:0] # Responsibility
[27:9:1] - **Article 75(3)**: the Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the House of the People (Lok Sabha)** – a **constitutional obligation**; **not to both Houses / "the Parliament"**, even if the PM or Ministers sit in the Rajya Sabha.
[27:9:2] - **Article 75(2)**: Ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the President** (not of the PM) – so the **President determines the term and membership** of the Council, on the PM's advice.
[27:9:3] - Members of **both** Houses can be Ministers – true, but that is not why the Council answers to the Lok Sabha.
[27:9:4] # Non-member Ministers (Article 75(5))
[27:9:5] - A person who is not an MP **can be appointed a Minister** (including PM), but a Minister who for **six consecutive months** is not a member of either House **ceases to be a Minister** – **Art. 75(5)** (75(2) = pleasure, 75(3) = collective responsibility, 75(4) = oath).
[27:9:6] - **S.R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001)**: a non-member cannot be **re-appointed repeatedly** without getting elected.
[27:9:7] # Ministers in Parliament (Article 88)
[27:9:8] - Every Minister has the **right to speak and take part** in the proceedings of **either House**, any **joint sitting** and any committee of which he is named a member – but **votes only in the House of which he is a member**. So a **Rajya Sabha Minister can take part in Lok Sabha deliberations** (unlike a nominated member, the Deputy Chairman or the Leader of the House in the RS) but **cannot vote** there.
[27:9:9] - **Article 75(1B)**: a member disqualified for **defection** cannot be a Minister until **re-elected**.
[27:9:10] - **July 2026**: a Minister of State's resignation was accepted under **Article 75(2)** after his RS term ended.

=== NOTES 2:28 | Cabinet System, Cabinet Committees, Collective and Individual Responsibility; Attorney General of India
## SUB 0 | Attorney General of India – Article 76 and Article 88
[28:0:0] - **Article 76**: office of the **Attorney General (AG) for India** – the **highest (first) law officer** of the country; appointed by the **President** (not by Parliament, the Speaker, the PM or on Parliament's recommendation). Not Arts. 77, 78 or 79.
[28:0:1] - **Qualification**: a person **qualified to be appointed a Judge of the Supreme Court** – a citizen who has been a High Court judge for 5 years, or a High Court advocate for 10 years, or an eminent jurist in the President's opinion.
[28:0:2] - **Term**: **not fixed** by the Constitution; holds office **during the pleasure of the President** (not the PM, not "for life", not "six years", not "as long as Parliament has confidence"). No procedure or grounds of removal are laid down, so he is **not removed like a Supreme Court judge**. By convention he **resigns when the government changes**.
[28:0:3] - **Remuneration**: such as the **President may determine** (Art. 76(4)) – not Parliament.
[28:0:4] - **Duties** (Art. 76(2)): **advise the Government of India on legal matters** and perform duties of a legal character **referred or assigned by the President** – not a Governor, the CJI or the Law Commission. He appears for the Government in the Supreme Court – the Government's **chief legal adviser and primary lawyer**.
[28:0:5] - **Right of audience** (Art. 76(3)): in **all courts in the territory of India**.
[28:0:6] - **Article 88**: though **not a member**, he may **speak and take part** in the proceedings of **either House**, a **joint sitting** and any **committee** of which he is named a member – **without a right to vote**. So he is the non-member who can participate in Lok Sabha proceedings (not the CJI, the Vice-President, the Solicitor General, the CAG or a Governor).
[28:0:7] - **Article 105(4)**: he enjoys **all the privileges and immunities of an MP** – "not entitled to MP privileges" is false.
[28:0:8] - **Limits**: not a member of the Cabinet; not a full-time government servant – **private practice allowed**, but he cannot advise or brief **against the Government of India**, defend accused persons in criminal cases without permission, or become a company director without permission.

## SUB 1 | Collective Responsibility – Article 75(3) and the Parliamentary Principle
[28:1:0] - **Article 75(3)**: the Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** – not to the Rajya Sabha, Parliament as a whole or the President. States: **Art. 164(2)** (to the Legislative Assembly).
[28:1:1] - **Collective responsibility** is the feature that marks India as a **parliamentary (cabinet / responsible / Westminster) system** – not separation of powers, rule of law or judicial review.
[28:1:2] - UPSC 2017: the principle underlying the Cabinet form of government = **a mechanism of parliamentary democracy for ensuring collective responsibility of the Government to the people** (not minimising criticism, speeding up work or strengthening the head of government).
[28:1:3] - Features of the parliamentary system: **nominal and real executives**, **majority party rule**, **collective responsibility**, **double membership** (ministers are MPs), **PM's leadership**, **dissolution of the Lok Sabha**. "**Election of the whole Parliament every 5 years**" is **not** a feature – the **Rajya Sabha is permanent** (one-third retire every 2 years).
[28:1:4] TABLE head: Event | Consequence
  [28:1:4.0] No-confidence motion passed (moved only in the Lok Sabha) | Whole Council, incl. RS ministers, resigns – "sink or swim together"
  [28:1:4.1] Union Budget / Money Bill defeated in the Lok Sabha | Loss of confidence – the PM submits the resignation of the Council of Ministers (not just the Finance Minister; the Budget is not sent to the RS or re-presented)
  [28:1:4.2] Government defeated in the Rajya Sabha | None – no resignation, no dissolution, no President's Rule
  [28:1:4.3] PM dies or resigns | Council of Ministers dissolves
  [28:1:4.4] A minister disagrees with a Cabinet decision | Must accept and defend it or resign
[28:1:5] - UPPCS 1993 A-R: "Ministers make policy and civil servants adhere to it" (true) and "parliamentary government works on cabinet collective responsibility" (true), but collective responsibility (ministers to the Lok Sabha) does **not explain** why civil servants carry out policy – **both true, R not the correct explanation**.
[28:1:6] - **Oath of secrecy** (Third Schedule, administered by the **President**) keeps Cabinet deliberations confidential and the ministry united in public.
[28:1:7] - India chose the parliamentary form from experience of the **GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935** and to avoid a personality cult.
[28:1:8] > Exam angle: "responsible to whom?" → Lok Sabha; Budget defeat → whole ministry resigns; RS defeat → nothing.

## SUB 2 | Cabinet Secretariat, Cabinet Secretary and Chief Secretary
[28:2:0] - **Cabinet Secretariat** works **directly under the PM**; administers the Business Rules. Functions: **preparation of the agenda for Cabinet meetings**, recording and circulating decisions, **secretarial assistance to Cabinet Committees**, inter-ministerial coordination – **not allocation of financial resources to Ministries** (UPSC 2014 → **1 and 2 only**).
[28:2:1] - **Cabinet Secretary**: the **senior-most / highest civil servant (bureaucrat)** of the Union – not the Home Secretary, the PM's Secretary, the Lok Sabha Secretary-General, a Chief Secretary or the Attorney General (a law officer, not a civil servant). "First among equals" among Secretaries; **11th** in the Table of Precedence; chairs the **Civil Services Board**.
[28:2:2] - He heads the **National Crisis Management Committee (NCMC)** for disaster management – not the Home Secretary, Defence Secretary or Central Relief Commissioner.
[28:2:3] - Appointed by the **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet** for **2 years**, extendable up to a total of **4 years**.
[28:2:4] - **6 Aug 2026**: Cabinet Secretary **T.V. Somanathan** given a one-year extension beyond 30 Aug 2026; Home Secretary **Govind Mohan** extended up to 22.8.2027.

## SUB 5 | Cabinet in Practice – Kitchen Cabinet, Shadow Cabinet, Coalitions
[28:5:0] - **Shadow cabinet**: a cabinet formed by the **main opposition party**, its members assigned to "shadow" (watch) particular ministries – a **British (UK)** institution; India has no formal counterpart. Not the PM's inner circle or ministers awaiting promotion.
[28:5:1] - **Kitchen cabinet**: the PM's **informal inner circle** of trusted colleagues and friends (ministers and non-ministers); criticised as an **extra-constitutional power centre** that may bypass the Cabinet.
[28:5:2] - **Single-party majority** eras → criticism of "**cabinet dictatorship**" over Parliament; **coalition era (1989–2014)** → coordination committees and allies' demands weakened Cabinet cohesion and collective responsibility.

## SUB 0 | Bicameral States and Legislative Councils – Articles 168, 169, 171
[29:0:0] - Part VI, **Articles 168–212** – State Legislature. Unicameral = Governor + Assembly; bicameral = **Governor + Legislative Assembly + Legislative Council**.
[29:0:1] - **Bicameral States now (2026): 6** – **Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh**. Before the **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019** there were **7** (J&K too – its Council of **36** members was abolished in 2019). So APPSC Group-II 2017 "how many bicameral States?" = **7** then, **6** now.
[29:0:2] - No Council: **West Bengal** (abolished 1969), **Tamil Nadu** (abolished 1986), Rajasthan, Goa, Mizoram, Sikkim and **Madhya Pradesh** – the **7th Amendment, 1956** provided a Council for MP but it was **never created**.
[29:0:3] - Largest Council: **UP (100)**.
[29:0:4] - **Art. 168(1)(a)** names the bicameral States (AP and Telangana included); **Art. 169** lets **Parliament create or abolish** a Council **by ordinary law** (not an Art. 368 amendment) after the Assembly passes a resolution by a **majority of total membership + two-thirds present and voting**. The AP Council (abolished 1985) was **revived in 2007** under Art. 169; AP's 2020 resolution to abolish it again was **withdrawn in 2021**.
[29:0:5] - **Art. 171**: Council not more than **one-third** of the Assembly and **not less than 40**.
[29:0:6] - Composition: 1/3 by MLAs, 1/3 by local authorities, 1/12 graduates, 1/12 teachers, 1/6 nominated by the Governor.

## SUB 4 | Parliament – Article 79 and the President's Place
[30:4:0] - **Article 79**: "There shall be a Parliament for the Union which shall consist of the **President** and two Houses – the **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)** and the **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**." So Parliament = **President + RS + LS** – **not** the PM, the Council of Ministers, the Vice-President, the Speaker/Chairman or the Secretariat.
[30:4:1] - Parliament is the **Union Legislature** (legislative body), in **Part V**. Laxmikanth: **Articles 79–122** deal with Parliament (NextGen and keys: "**79 to 123**", counting the ordinance Article).
[30:4:2] - President is an **integral part** because **a Bill passed by both Houses cannot become law without his assent** (Art. 111) – the "**President-in-Parliament**" like the **Crown-in-Parliament** in Britain; the US President is not part of Congress. This "President is part of Parliament" is called the most distinctive feature.
[30:4:3] - **Article 59(1)**: the President is **not a member** of either House or a State Legislature; an MP/MLA elected President vacates his seat. He does **not sit or take part in discussions**.
[30:4:4] - President's legislative role: summons, prorogues, dissolves the LS; **Art. 86** – may address either or both Houses; **Art. 87** – **special address** to both Houses at the first session after each general election and the first session of each year; assent (absolute, suspensive, pocket veto); nominates **12** to RS; **ordinances (Art. 123)** when Parliament is not in session.
[30:4:5] - The **Lok Sabha** is a **constitutional** body (Arts. 79, 81) – the Planning Commission, NDC and the Deputy PM post are **extra-constitutional / not in the Constitution**.
