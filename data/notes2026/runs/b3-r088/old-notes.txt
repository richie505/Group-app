=== NOTES 3:88 | Financial Inclusion – Pmjdy, Mudra, Microfinance, Shg-bank Linkage, Digital Payments and Upi
## SUB 0 | Financial Inclusion – Meaning and Committees
[88:0:0] # Meaning
[88:0:1] - **Financial inclusion**: basic banking and financial services to **everybody, whatever their income or savings**.
[88:0:2] - **Rangarajan definition**: access to financial services and **timely and adequate credit** for **vulnerable groups** (weaker sections, low-income groups) at **affordable cost**.
[88:0:3] - Aims: take banking to the poor and **underserved and rural areas** (not metros); credit for weaker sections; **financial literacy**; formal payments and remittances; insurance. It **raises** financial security.
[88:0:4] **Shrinking of banking infrastructure** is NOT an aim of financial inclusion.
[88:0:5] TABLE head: Financial inclusion | Financial exclusion
  [88:0:5.0] Access to a bank account, credit, insurance | No or limited banking/insurance access
  [88:0:5.1] Payments and remittances | Limited payment facilities
  [88:0:5.2] Financial literacy | Lack of awareness
[88:0:6] # Earlier Inclusion Measures
[88:0:7] - **Lead Bank Scheme (LBS)**: a financial inclusion measure.
[88:0:8] - **Swabhimaan (10 February 2011)**: banking to the doorsteps of the rural poor.
[88:0:9] # Committees → Chair → Subject

  [88:0:10.0] C. Rangarajan | Formed 26 June 2006; final report 4 January 2008 | Committee on Financial Inclusion – widening banking access

## SUB 1 | Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (pmjdy)
[88:1:0] # Launch and Objective
[88:1:1] - **National Mission for Financial Inclusion**: announced **15 August 2014**, launched **28 August 2014**; nodal: **DFS, Ministry of Finance**.
[88:1:2] - Objective: **a bank account for every household** (APPSC GS 2024).
[88:1:3] - Tenets: **banking the unbanked, securing the unsecured, funding the unfunded**.
[88:1:4] - Groundwork: **Nachiket Mor Committee (2013)**.
[88:1:5] # Features

  [88:1:6.2] Overdraft | Up to ₹10,000 (after 6 months of good conduct)
  [88:1:6.3] Life cover | ₹30,000
  [88:1:6.4] Others | DBT, financial literacy; linked to PMJJBY, PMSBY, APY

## SUB 3 | Mudra
[88:3:0] - **PM MUDRA Yojana (PMMY)**: launched **8 April 2015**; **Ministry of Finance (DFS)**.
[88:3:1] - **MUDRA = Micro Units Development and Refinance Agency**; MUDRA Ltd set up **18 March 2015** as a **SIDBI subsidiary**; **refinances** banks, NBFCs, MFIs.
[88:3:2] - **Collateral-free** loans to **non-corporate, non-farm micro and small enterprises** (not large corporates); brings small entrepreneurs into the formal system.

[88:3:6] - **Launch order**: PMJDY (28 Aug 2014) → Digital Gender Atlas (9 Mar 2015) → MUDRA (8 Apr 2015) → PMSBY (9 May 2015).

## SUB 4 | Microfinance
[88:4:0] - **Microfinance services**: credit, savings, insurance, cash transfers, leasing for **low-income households**.
[88:4:1] - **AP microfinance crisis (2010)**: coercive recoveries and borrower suicides → AP **Micro Finance Institutions (Regulation of Money Lending) Ordinance, 15 October 2010**: MFIs had to **register with a state-appointed authority**; replaced by an Act (Dec 2010).
[88:4:2] - The crisis **changed national regulation** of MFIs → **Malegam Committee (2010)**.
[88:4:3] - **NBFC-MFI**: a new RBI category of NBFC created on **2 December 2011**.
[88:4:4] - **MFIN (Microfinance Institutions Network)**: self-regulatory national body of RBI-regulated MFIs, formed **October 2009**.

## SUB 6 | Npci and Payment Systems
[88:6:0] # NPCI Basics
[88:6:1] - **National Payments Corporation of India**: set up **December 2008** by the **RBI and Indian Banks' Association (IBA)**.
[88:6:2] - **Not-for-profit**, **Section 25 company** (Companies Act 1956) = Section 8 of the 2013 Act.
[88:6:3] - Works under the **Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007**; umbrella body for retail payments.
[88:6:4] # Milestones
[88:6:5] TABLE head: Year | Milestone
  [88:6:5.0] 2004 | NFS designed and launched by RBI's IDRBT
  [88:6:5.1] 2007 | PSS Act
  [88:6:5.2] 2008 | NPCI incorporated
  [88:6:5.3] 2009 | NPCI took over NFS from IDRBT
  [88:6:5.4] 2010 | IMPS and AePS
  [88:6:5.5] 2012 | RuPay; NACH
  [88:6:5.6] 2016 | UPI (April, 21 banks); BHIM; NETC FASTag
[88:6:6] # Products → Purpose
[88:6:7] TABLE head: Product | Purpose
  [88:6:7.0] UPI | Instant account-to-account P2P and P2M payments via a VPA; runs on the IMPS framework (not NEFT); 24x7
  [88:6:7.1] UPI 2.0 (2018) | Overdraft linking, mandates, signed QR
  [88:6:7.2] IMPS | 24x7 instant interbank transfer
  [88:6:7.3] RuPay | Domestic card network; cuts dependence on Visa and Mastercard
  [88:6:7.4] AePS | Basic banking at micro-ATMs through Aadhaar authentication; supports inclusion
  [88:6:7.5] NACH | Bulk recurring payments: salaries, dividends, bills
  [88:6:7.6] NETC / FASTag | Electronic toll collection (RFID)
  [88:6:7.7] BHIM | NPCI's UPI app
  [88:6:7.8] Bharat BillPay | Interoperable bill payment
  [88:6:7.9] NFS | Largest interoperable ATM network; links all ATMs (APPSC GS 2024)
  [88:6:7.10] e-RUPI | Person- and purpose-specific prepaid voucher
  [88:6:7.11] 123Pay | UPI for feature phones
[88:6:8] - Result of UPI: **mobile wallets are not necessary** for online payments (direct bank-to-bank).
[88:6:9] # RTGS vs NEFT
[88:6:10] TABLE head: Basis | RTGS | NEFT
  [88:6:10.0] Settlement | Real-time, gross, almost instantaneous | Deferred net, half-hourly batches
  [88:6:10.1] Minimum | ₹2 lakh | No minimum
[88:6:11] - **No charge** on the beneficiary for inward RTGS credit; RBI removed NEFT/RTGS charges.
[88:6:12] - **Bharat QR**: launched **20 February 2017**; developed by **NPCI, Mastercard and Visa**; the **world's first interoperable QR code acceptance solution**: a customer scans the merchant's one QR code to pay instantly from the phone.

## SUB 8 | Digital Payments Policy and Indices
[88:8:0] # Nodal Ministry and MDR
[88:8:1] - Promotion of digital payments **moved from MeitY to DFS (Ministry of Finance)** (APPSC-G1 2024).
[88:8:2] - **MDR (Merchant Discount Rate)**: fee a bank charges a merchant for accepting card payments; shared by **issuing bank, PoS bank and payment network**.
[88:8:3] - **Zero MDR** on **RuPay debit and BHIM-UPI** since **1 Jan 2020** (**Sec 10A, PSS Act 2007**).
[88:8:4] - **RuPay-BHIM UPI incentive scheme**: **0.15%** on P2M payments up to **₹2,000** to small merchants; run by **DFS**; **NPCI** nodal agency.
[88:8:5] - **Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026**: UPI stays **free for consumers**; MDR may apply to **high-value** payments (Aug 2026). Zero-MDR blamed for cost burden on PSPs.
[88:8:6] # RBI Indices
[88:8:7] TABLE head: Index | Key facts
  [88:8:7.0] RBI-DPI | Base March 2018 = 100; published from January 2021; semi-annual; 5 parameters: payment enablers, payment infrastructure (demand side), payment infrastructure (supply side), payment performance, consumer centricity; 516.76 (Sep 2025)
  [88:8:7.1] FI-Index | Since 2021; annual (July); 0-100; 97 indicators (banking, investment, insurance, postal, pension); Access 35%, Usage 45%, Quality 20%; 67.0 (2025)
[88:8:8] APPSC G1 2024: "RBI-DPI has 2021 as base year" = **FALSE**; base is **March 2018**.
## SUB 9 | Jam Trinity and New Digital Finance Architecture
[88:9:0] - **JAM** = **Jan Dhan – Aadhaar – Mobile**; PMJDY is its first layer → made **DBT** possible (paperless, presence-less delivery).
[88:9:1] - **India Stack**: Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker, Account Aggregator, ONDC.
[88:9:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [88:9:2.0] Account Aggregator | Consent-based sharing of financial data (bank transactions, GST records); RBI-regulated NBFC-AAs
  [88:9:2.1] ONDC | Open, interoperable digital commerce network (buyers, sellers, logistics, payments); cuts dominance of e-commerce platforms; DPIIT initiative
  [88:9:2.2] OCEN | Common protocol linking lenders and marketplaces for small-ticket credit
[88:9:3] # Payments Banks vs Small Finance Banks
[88:9:4] - **Payments banks** (Nachiket Mor): deposits up to **₹1 lakh** initially (₹2 lakh from 2021); **cannot lend** → **no credit cards**; may issue ATM/debit cards and hold G-secs; **no credit risk**.
[88:9:5] - Promoters allowed: NBFCs, individuals, corporations, **mobile phone companies**, supermarket chains, real estate co-operatives, public sector entities.
[88:9:6] - **Small finance banks**: **50% of loans up to ₹25 lakh**.
## SUB 10 | Cryptocurrency vs Digital Rupee vs Upi
[88:10:0] TABLE head: Basis | Cryptocurrency | Digital Rupee (e₹, CBDC) | UPI
  [88:10:0.0] Nature | Private, decentralised; not legal tender | Legal tender issued by RBI | Payment system, not a currency
  [88:10:0.1] Liability | None | RBI | Commercial bank money – your bank
  [88:10:0.2] Rules | 30% tax on gains + 1% TDS (2022) | RBI Act amended by Finance Act 2022 | NPCI under PSS Act 2007
[88:10:1] - **Bitcoin**: created **January 2009** by **Satoshi Nakamoto**; peer-to-peer; not issued by banks or governments.
[88:10:2] - Facebook's cryptocurrency (2019): **Libra** (later Diem).
[88:10:3] - **Supreme Court, 2020** struck down RBI's **2018** circular barring banks from crypto; 2021 bill to ban private crypto pending.
[88:10:4] - **e-rupee pilots**: wholesale (**e₹-W**) **1 Nov 2022**; retail (**e₹-R**) **1 Dec 2022**. Benefits: programmable, targeted subsidy, cheaper remittances.
[88:10:5] - **Sand Dollar (Bahamas, 2020)**: world's first live retail CBDC.
[88:10:6] The Finance Act 2022 did **not** make crypto legal tender; e-rupee is money itself, UPI only moves bank money.

=== NOTES 3:89 | Financial Markets – Money Market Instruments and Capital Market Segments
## SUB 0 | Money Market vs Capital Market
[89:0:0] TABLE head: Basis | Money market | Capital market
  [89:0:0.0] Tenure | Up to 1 year (overnight to 364 days) | Above 1 year (long-term debt and equity)
  [89:0:0.1] Regulator | RBI | SEBI
  [89:0:0.2] Instruments | T-bills, CP, CD, call money, TREPS/CBLO, commercial bills | Equities, debentures, bonds
  [89:0:0.3] Sub-markets | Call Money Market, Treasury Bill Market | Government Bond Market, Stock Market
[89:0:1] - Individuals do not directly take part in the money market; it has no physical location.
[89:0:2] - Per the PYQ key, **RBI is not a money-market participant** (it regulates it).
[89:0:3] **Equity shares** are a capital-market (pure) instrument; T-bills and CPs are money-market instruments.
## SUB 1 | Organised vs Unorganised Money Market
[89:1:0] TABLE head: Organised | Unorganised
  [89:1:0.0] RBI, commercial banks, DFHI, regulated NBFCs, money market mutual funds, insurers | Indigenous bankers, moneylenders, chit funds, hundis, unregulated finance companies
  [89:1:0.1] Controlled and coordinated by RBI | Not controlled by RBI
[89:1:1] - **Co-operative credit institutions** sit between the two.
[89:1:2] - **Call money market** is the **most unstable part** of the organised money market: repayable on demand.
## SUB 2 | Money Market Instruments
[89:2:0] TABLE head: Instrument | Maturity | Issuer / notes
  [89:2:0.0] Commercial Paper (CP) | 7 days to 1 year | Started 1990 (Vaghul); unsecured promissory note of eligible corporates; credit source for corporate industry
  [89:2:0.1] Certificate of Deposit (CD) | Banks: up to 1 year; FIs: 1-3 years | Started 1989 (Vaghul); banks and FIs
  [89:2:0.2] Call money | Overnight | Inter-bank
  [89:2:0.3] Notice money | 2-14 days | Inter-bank
  [89:2:0.4] CBLO | Short term | Collateralised Borrowing and Lending Obligation; by CCIL from 20 January 2003; needs collateral
  [89:2:0.5] TREPS | Short term | Tri-party repo; replaced CBLO; third party handles collateral and settlement
  [89:2:0.6] Commercial bills | Usually 90 days | Trade bills drawn by firms, discounted by banks
[89:2:1] **Vaghul Committee**: CD **1989**, CP **1990**.
## SUB 3 | Government Securities and the Gilt-edged Market
[89:3:0] # Types
[89:3:1] TABLE head: Security | Issued by | Maturity
  [89:3:1.0] Treasury Bills | Centre, sold by RBI auction | 91, 182, 364 days
  [89:3:1.1] Dated G-Secs | Central Government | One year or more; pay coupon
  [89:3:1.2] SDLs | State Governments | Long-term dated securities
  [89:3:1.3] Cash Management Bills | Centre | Very short term; temporary cash mismatches
[89:3:2] - **G-Sec**: tradeable paper showing debt of the Central or a State Government; issued by **RBI** under the **Government Securities Act, 2006**.
[89:3:3] # Treasury Bills
[89:3:4] - **Zero-coupon**: issued at a **discount**, redeemed at **face value**; no periodic interest (Government bonds pay interest).
[89:3:5] - **First issued in India in 1917** (First World War).
[89:3:6] # Gilt-Edged Market
[89:3:7] - Market of **Treasury Bills and Government bonds**; interest and repayment on due date are sure → **default risk almost zero**.
[89:3:8] - **Not** corporate paper.
[89:3:9] - **Primary dealers**: RBI-regulated underwriters and market makers in G-Secs; **SBI DFHI = largest**.
[89:3:10] - **RBI Retail Direct**: individuals buy G-Secs directly via a **Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) account**.
[89:3:11] - Who trades: banks, PDs, insurers, MFs, provident funds, FPIs, individuals.
[89:3:12] - **OMO**: RBI **sells** G-Secs → **absorbs** liquidity; **buys** → **injects** liquidity.
[89:3:13] - **FAR** (June 2026) now covers new 15-, 30-, 40-year G-Secs and **sovereign green bonds**; record FPI bond inflow ₹55,518 crore (June 2026).
[89:3:14] - **AP angle**: AP plans SDL borrowings of **₹73,383 crore** in 2026-27.
[89:3:15] - US government debt is backed only by the faith of the Government; a default does not cancel bondholders' legal claims.
## SUB 4 | Capital Market – Structure and Instruments
[89:4:0] # Structure
[89:4:1] - Gilt-edged market; **industrial securities market** (**primary + secondary**); DFIs (IFCI, ICICI, IDBI, SIDBI, UTI); intermediaries (merchant banks, MFs, leasing, venture capital companies).
[89:4:2] # Instruments
[89:4:3] TABLE head: Type | Instruments
  [89:4:3.0] Pure | Equity, preference shares, debentures, bonds
  [89:4:3.1] Hybrid | Convertible preference shares, partly/optionally convertible debentures
  [89:4:3.2] Venture capital | Long-term start-up capital for new firms with growth potential; a form of private equity; promoting it = entrepreneur development
  [89:4:3.3] Private equity | Investment in unlisted firms
  [89:4:3.4] ETFs | Trade on exchanges close to NAV (CPSE ETF, Bharat-22 ETF)
[89:4:4] - **Sweat equity shares**: given to directors/employees at a discount or for non-cash value (know-how, IPR).
[89:4:5] - **CCP regulators**: RBI – **CCIL** (money, G-Sec, forex); SEBI – **NSCCL, ICCL** (capital markets); IFSCA only IFSC entities.
[89:4:6] - Corporate bonds: 16% of GDP (Korea 79%); ~98% private placement.
[89:4:7] - **Budget 2026-27**: market-making for corporate bonds, derivatives on corporate bond indices, larger municipal bond markets.
## SUB 5 | Alternative Investment Funds, REITs and InvITs
[89:5:0] - **AIFs**: privately pooled funds from expert investors; defined under **SEBI (AIF) Regulations, 2012**; include hedge funds and venture capital (not plain bonds, stocks).
[89:5:1] TABLE head: SEBI AIF category | Examples
  [89:5:1.0] Category I | Venture capital, angel, SME, social venture, infrastructure funds
  [89:5:1.1] Category II | Private equity, debt funds (e.g. Self-Reliant India Fund)
  [89:5:1.2] Category III | Hedge funds, leveraged trading; may trade commodity derivatives
[89:5:2] - **September 2025**: SEBI added an AIF category only for **accredited investors**.
[89:5:3] - **REITs** pool **real-estate** assets; **InvITs** pool **infrastructure** assets; used for asset monetisation. InvIT dividends and interest are **taxable at slab rate**.
## SUB 6 | Bond Types and Yields
[89:6:0] # Bond Terms
[89:6:1] - **Bondholder** = creditor to the issuer; **maturity** = repayment date; **coupon** = interest; **face value** = nominal value; **yield** = investor's return at market price.
[89:6:2] # Types
[89:6:3] TABLE head: Bond | Feature
  [89:6:3.0] Green bond | Only environmental/climate projects; long-term; tax incentives; first by EIB (2007); World Bank labelled one 2008
  [89:6:3.1] Social bond | Only social projects
  [89:6:3.2] Sustainability bond | Both environmental and social projects
  [89:6:3.3] Masala bond | Rupee-denominated, issued overseas; investor bears currency risk
  [89:6:3.4] Zero-coupon | Discount, no periodic interest (e.g. T-bills)
  [89:6:3.5] Inflation-indexed | Principal/coupon adjusted to inflation
  [89:6:3.6] Sovereign Gold Bond | 2015; 2.5% interest + gold price; new issues stopped
[89:6:4] # What Moves Yields
[89:6:5] - **Bond price and yield move in opposite directions**.
[89:6:6] - **Higher policy rates, inflation, government borrowing** → yields **rise**.
[89:6:7] - **RBI OMO purchases** + foreign inflows → yields **fall**.
