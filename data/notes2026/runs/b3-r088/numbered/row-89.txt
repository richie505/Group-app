## SUB 0 | Money Market vs Capital Market
[0:0] - Indian financial system = **money market + capital market**.
[0:1] TABLE head: Basis | Money market | Capital market
  [0:1.0] Meaning | Market for short-term funds / loans – where loans of money are obtained | Market for medium- and long-term funds – where bonds, shares and securities are bought and sold
  [0:1.1] Tenure | Up to 1 year (overnight to 364 days) | More than 1 year
  [0:1.2] Regulator | RBI | SEBI
  [0:1.3] Instruments | T-bills, CP, CD, call money, CBLO/TREPS, commercial bills | Equities, preference shares, debentures, bonds
  [0:1.4] Sub-markets | Call Money Market, Treasury Bill Market | Government Bond Market, Stock Market
  [0:1.5] Participants | RBI, commercial banks, FIs, MFs, corporates – individuals do not take part | Individuals and institutions
[0:2] - **Capital market** in the common sense = **share market** (not commodity or money market).
[0:3] - **UPSC 2023**: of G-bond, call money, T-bill and stock markets, only **G-bond and stock markets** are capital markets → "**Only two**".
[0:4] - **Difference** (money vs securities market): the former deals in **short-term debt obligations**, the latter in **long-term securities**.
[0:5] - **RBI's place**: NextGen lists the RBI among the **major participants** (and controlling authority) of the money market; the UPPCS 1999 key treats **RBI** as the one that is "not a participant" (it regulates) – IDBI, ICICI and MMMFs do operate in it.
[0:6] - Global firms (UPSC 2009): JP Morgan Chase – financial services; WL Ross and Warburg Pincus – private equity; **Roche Holding AG is pharmaceuticals**, not financial services.
[0:7] > Asked ×9. Trap: T-bill and call money markets counted as capital markets.

## SUB 1 | Organised vs Unorganised Money Market
[1:0] TABLE head: Organised | Unorganised
  [1:0.0] RBI, commercial banks, co-operative and foreign banks, DFHI, STCI, money market mutual funds, regulated FIs, insurers, PSUs | Indigenous bankers, moneylenders, chit funds, nidhis, hundis, unregulated finance / loan companies, finance brokers
  [1:0.1] Controlled and coordinated by RBI | Not controlled by RBI
[1:1] - Organised segments: call and notice money, **T-bills**, commercial bills, **CDs**, CPs, repos, MMMFs, DFHI.
[1:2] - **Finance companies** (unregulated non-bank intermediaries) are keyed as **not part of the organised** money market (APPSC Group 2 2017), though NextGen also names finance companies among the formal market's other players.
[1:3] - **SEBI** is the capital-market regulator – it does **not** participate in the organised money market.
[1:4] - **Co-operative credit institutions** sit between the two.
[1:5] - **Call money market** = the **most volatile / unstable part** of the organised money market (repayable on demand; overnight to 14 days).
[1:6] > Asked ×6 (APPSC Group 2 Mains 2017 ×2, RAS 2018 ×2).

## SUB 2 | Money Market Instruments
[2:0] TABLE head: Instrument | Maturity | Issuer / notes
  [2:0.0] Commercial Paper (CP) | 7 days to 1 year (older rule 14 days-1 year) | Unsecured short-term promissory note of eligible corporates; 1990 (Vaghul Committee); source of credit for corporate industry
  [2:0.1] Certificate of Deposit (CD) | Banks: up to 1 year; FIs: 1-3 years | Banks and FIs (not RBI) to depositors; 1989 (Vaghul)
  [2:0.2] Call money | Overnight | Inter-bank short-term finance; no collateral
  [2:0.3] Notice money | 2-14 days | Inter-bank

[2:2] - **Vaghul Committee (1987)**: money market reform – CD **1989**, CP **1990**.

## SUB 3 | Government Securities and the Gilt-edged Market
[3:0] # Gilt-edged market
[3:1] - **Gilt-edged market** = market for **government securities** (and those **guaranteed by government**) – "of the best quality"; **no default risk**, highly liquid; **RBI** is the sole supplier.
[3:2] - Two parts: **Treasury Bill market** and **Government bond market** – not bullion, shares, MFs, derivatives, corporate bonds or bank CDs.
[3:3] - **Gilt-edged securities = Government securities**, not company shares or MF units.
[3:4] # Types
[3:5] TABLE head: Security | Issued by | Maturity
  [3:5.0] Treasury Bills | Central Government only, sold by RBI auction | 91, 182, 364 days
  [3:5.1] Dated G-Secs | Central Government | One year or more; pay coupon
  [3:5.2] State Development Loans | State Governments | Dated securities
  [3:5.3] Cash Management Bills | Centre | Under 91 days; temporary cash mismatches
[3:6] - **G-Sec** (Government Securities Act, 2006): tradeable instrument acknowledging debt of the **Central or a State Government**; RBI manages and services **both** Central and State securities.
[3:7] # Treasury Bills
[3:8] - Short-term **promissory notes issued by RBI on behalf of the Government** for its short-term borrowing; **no State Government T-bills**.
[3:9] - **Zero-coupon**: issued at a **discount**, redeemed at **face value**; first issued in India in **1917**. (14-day intermediate T-bills go only to State Govts / central banks.)
[3:10] - **UPSC 2018**: A false (RBI manages State securities too), B true, C true → "**B and C only**".
[3:11] # Market players
[3:12] - **Primary dealers**: **SBI DFHI = largest**. **RBI Retail Direct**: individuals via a **Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) account**.
[3:13] - **OMO**: RBI **sells** G-Secs → **absorbs** liquidity; buys → injects.
[3:14] - **FAR** (June 2026) now covers new 15-, 30-, 40-year G-Secs and **sovereign green bonds**.
[3:15] - **AP**: SDL borrowings of **₹73,383 crore** planned for 2026-27.
[3:16] > Most asked: gilt-edged = government securities (×7), T-bill tenors 91/182/364 (×2, APPSC Group 1 2019). Trap: "14 and 91 days".

## SUB 4 | Capital Market Instruments, Venture Capital, AIFs and InvITs
[4:0] # Structure and instruments
[4:1] - Capital market: gilt-edged market; **industrial securities market** (**primary + secondary**); DFIs (IFCI, ICICI, IDBI, SIDBI, UTI); intermediaries (merchant banks, MFs, leasing, venture capital companies).
[4:2] TABLE head: Type | Instruments
  [4:2.0] Pure | Equity, preference shares, debentures, bonds
  [4:2.1] Hybrid | Convertible preference shares, partly / optionally convertible debentures
  [4:2.2] Private equity | Investment in unlisted firms
  [4:2.3] ETFs | Trade close to NAV (CPSE ETF, Bharat-22)
[4:3] - **CCP regulators**: RBI – **CCIL** (money, G-Sec, forex); SEBI – **NSCCL, ICCL**.
[4:4] - Corporate bonds ≈ **16% of GDP**; CRISIL (Dec 2024): corporate bond market to reach **₹100-120 lakh crore by FY2030**. Budget 2026-27: derivatives on **corporate bond indices**.
[4:5] # Venture capital
[4:6] - **Venture capital** = **long-term start-up capital** for new / early-stage firms with high growth potential; a form of **private equity** (not short-term, loss-funding or renovation funds).
[4:7] - **Promoting venture capital** is keyed as **entrepreneur development** (APPSC Group 2).
[4:8] # AIFs, REITs and InvITs
[4:9] - **AIFs**: privately pooled funds under **SEBI (AIF) Regulations, 2012**.
[4:10] TABLE head: Category | Examples
  [4:10.0] Category I | Venture capital, angel, SME, social venture, infrastructure funds
  [4:10.1] Category II | Private equity, debt funds
  [4:10.2] Category III | Hedge funds, leveraged trading
[4:11] - UPSC 2025: of bonds, hedge funds, stocks and venture capital, **hedge funds and venture capital** are AIFs → "**Only two**". Sept 2025: AIF category for **accredited investors**.
[4:12] - **REITs** pool **real estate**; **InvITs** pool **infrastructure**. InvIT interest and dividend are **taxable** (Statement I wrong); InvITs are **borrowers under SARFAESI, 2002** (Finance Act 2021) → "Statement-I incorrect, Statement-II correct".

## SUB 5 | Bonds – Types and Yields
[5:0] TABLE head: Bond | Feature
  [5:0.0] Green bond | Only environmental / climate projects; a financial market innovation; long-term (not short-dated); tax incentives; first by European Investment Bank (2007); World Bank labelled one 2008
  [5:0.1] Social bond | Only social projects
  [5:0.2] Sustainability bond | Both environmental and social projects
  [5:0.3] Masala bond | Rupee-denominated, issued overseas; investor bears currency risk
  [5:0.4] Zero-coupon | Discount, no periodic interest (e.g. T-bills)
  [5:0.5] Inflation-indexed | Principal / coupon adjusted to inflation
  [5:0.6] Sovereign Gold Bond | 2015; 2.5% + gold price; new issues stopped
[5:1] - Terms: bondholder = **creditor**; **maturity** = repayment date; **coupon** = interest; face value = nominal value; **yield** = return at market price.
[5:2] - **Price and yield move inversely**; higher policy rates, inflation, **government borrowing** → yields rise; **RBI OMO purchases** → yields fall.
[5:3] - Other own-MCQ facts: Jim O'Neill coined **BRIC (2001)**; first BRIC summit **2009**.
