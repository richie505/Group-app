=== Money Market vs Capital Market
- Indian financial system = **money market + capital market**.
| Basis | Money market | Capital market |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Market for **short-term funds / loans** – where **loans of money** are obtained | Market for **medium- and long-term funds** – where **bonds, shares and securities** are bought and sold |
| Tenure | Up to **1 year** (overnight to 364 days) | **More than 1 year** |
| Regulator | **RBI** | **SEBI** |
| Instruments | T-bills, CP, CD, call money, CBLO/TREPS, commercial bills | **Equities**, preference shares, debentures, bonds |
| Sub-markets | **Call Money Market, Treasury Bill Market** | **Government Bond Market, Stock Market** |
| Participants | RBI, commercial banks, FIs, MFs, corporates – individuals do not take part | Individuals and institutions |
- **Capital market** in the common sense = **share market** (not commodity or money market).
- **UPSC 2023**: of G-bond, call money, T-bill and stock markets, only **G-bond and stock markets** are capital markets → "**Only two**".
- **Difference** (money vs securities market): the former deals in **short-term debt obligations**, the latter in **long-term securities**.
- **RBI's place**: NextGen lists the RBI among the **major participants** (and controlling authority) of the money market; the UPPCS 1999 key treats **RBI** as the one that is "not a participant" (it regulates) – IDBI, ICICI and MMMFs do operate in it.
- Global firms (UPSC 2009): JP Morgan Chase – financial services; WL Ross and Warburg Pincus – private equity; **Roche Holding AG is pharmaceuticals**, not financial services.
> Asked ×9. Trap: T-bill and call money markets counted as capital markets.

=== Organised vs Unorganised Money Market
| Organised | Unorganised |
|---|---|
| RBI, commercial banks, co-operative and foreign banks, **DFHI**, STCI, **money market mutual funds**, regulated FIs, insurers, PSUs | **Indigenous bankers, moneylenders**, chit funds, nidhis, hundis, **unregulated finance / loan companies**, finance brokers |
| Controlled and coordinated by **RBI** | **Not controlled by RBI** |
- Organised segments: call and notice money, **T-bills**, commercial bills, **CDs**, CPs, repos, MMMFs, DFHI.
- **Finance companies** (unregulated non-bank intermediaries) are keyed as **not part of the organised** money market (APPSC Group 2 2017), though NextGen also names finance companies among the formal market's other players.
- **SEBI** is the capital-market regulator – it does **not** participate in the organised money market.
- **Co-operative credit institutions** sit between the two.
- **Call money market** = the **most volatile / unstable part** of the organised money market (repayable on demand; overnight to 14 days).
> Asked ×6 (APPSC Group 2 Mains 2017 ×2, RAS 2018 ×2).

=== Money Market Instruments
| Instrument | Maturity | Issuer / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Commercial Paper (CP)** | **7 days to 1 year** (older rule 14 days-1 year) | **Unsecured short-term promissory note** of eligible corporates; 1990 (Vaghul Committee); source of credit for **corporate industry** |
| **Certificate of Deposit (CD)** | Banks: up to 1 year; FIs: **1-3 years** | **Banks and FIs** (not RBI) to depositors; 1989 (Vaghul) |
| **Call money** | Overnight | **Inter-bank** short-term finance; no collateral |
| Notice money | 2-14 days | Inter-bank |
| **CBLO** | Short term | **Collateralised Borrowing and Lending Obligation** – a **money market** instrument of **CCIL** (20 January 2003) |
| TREPS | Short term | Tri-party repo; **replaced CBLO** |
| Commercial bills | Usually **90 days** | Trade bills drawn by firms, discounted by banks |
- **UPSC 2020**: CP short-term unsecured ✔, call money interbank ✔; CD is **not** an RBI long-term instrument, zero-coupon bonds are **not** interest-bearing → "**A and C only**".
- **Vaghul Committee (1987)**: money market reform – CD **1989**, CP **1990**.

=== Government Securities and the Gilt-edged Market
# Gilt-edged market
- **Gilt-edged market** = market for **government securities** (and those **guaranteed by government**) – "of the best quality"; **no default risk**, highly liquid; **RBI** is the sole supplier.
- Two parts: **Treasury Bill market** and **Government bond market** – not bullion, shares, MFs, derivatives, corporate bonds or bank CDs.
- **Gilt-edged securities = Government securities**, not company shares or MF units.
# Types
| Security | Issued by | Maturity |
|---|---|---|
| **Treasury Bills** | **Central Government only**, sold by **RBI** auction | **91, 182, 364 days** |
| Dated G-Secs | Central Government | One year or more; pay coupon |
| **State Development Loans** | State Governments | Dated securities |
| Cash Management Bills | Centre | Under 91 days; temporary cash mismatches |
- **G-Sec** (Government Securities Act, 2006): tradeable instrument acknowledging debt of the **Central or a State Government**; RBI manages and services **both** Central and State securities.
# Treasury Bills
- Short-term **promissory notes issued by RBI on behalf of the Government** for its short-term borrowing; **no State Government T-bills**.
- **Zero-coupon**: issued at a **discount**, redeemed at **face value**; first issued in India in **1917**. (14-day intermediate T-bills go only to State Govts / central banks.)
- **UPSC 2018**: A false (RBI manages State securities too), B true, C true → "**B and C only**".
# Market players
- **Primary dealers**: **SBI DFHI = largest**. **RBI Retail Direct**: individuals via a **Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) account**.
- **OMO**: RBI **sells** G-Secs → **absorbs** liquidity; buys → injects.
- **FAR** (June 2026) now covers new 15-, 30-, 40-year G-Secs and **sovereign green bonds**.
- **AP**: SDL borrowings of **₹73,383 crore** planned for 2026-27.
> Most asked: gilt-edged = government securities (×7), T-bill tenors 91/182/364 (×2, APPSC Group 1 2019). Trap: "14 and 91 days".

=== Capital Market Instruments, Venture Capital, AIFs and InvITs
# Structure and instruments
- Capital market: gilt-edged market; **industrial securities market** (**primary + secondary**); DFIs (IFCI, ICICI, IDBI, SIDBI, UTI); intermediaries (merchant banks, MFs, leasing, venture capital companies).
| Type | Instruments |
|---|---|
| Pure | **Equity**, preference shares, debentures, bonds |
| Hybrid | **Convertible preference shares**, partly / optionally convertible debentures |
| Private equity | Investment in **unlisted** firms |
| ETFs | Trade close to **NAV** (CPSE ETF, Bharat-22) |
- **CCP regulators**: RBI – **CCIL** (money, G-Sec, forex); SEBI – **NSCCL, ICCL**.
- Corporate bonds ≈ **16% of GDP**; CRISIL (Dec 2024): corporate bond market to reach **₹100-120 lakh crore by FY2030**. Budget 2026-27: derivatives on **corporate bond indices**.
# Venture capital
- **Venture capital** = **long-term start-up capital** for new / early-stage firms with high growth potential; a form of **private equity** (not short-term, loss-funding or renovation funds).
- **Promoting venture capital** is keyed as **entrepreneur development** (APPSC Group 2).
# AIFs, REITs and InvITs
- **AIFs**: privately pooled funds under **SEBI (AIF) Regulations, 2012**.
| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Category I** | **Venture capital**, angel, SME, social venture, infrastructure funds |
| Category II | Private equity, debt funds |
| **Category III** | **Hedge funds**, leveraged trading |
- UPSC 2025: of bonds, hedge funds, stocks and venture capital, **hedge funds and venture capital** are AIFs → "**Only two**". Sept 2025: AIF category for **accredited investors**.
- **REITs** pool **real estate**; **InvITs** pool **infrastructure**. InvIT interest and dividend are **taxable** (Statement I wrong); InvITs are **borrowers under SARFAESI, 2002** (Finance Act 2021) → "Statement-I incorrect, Statement-II correct".

=== Bonds – Types and Yields
| Bond | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Green bond** | Only environmental / climate projects; a financial market innovation; **long-term** (not short-dated); tax incentives; first by **European Investment Bank (2007)**; World Bank labelled one 2008 |
| Social bond | Only social projects |
| **Sustainability bond** | **Both environmental and social** projects |
| Masala bond | Rupee-denominated, issued overseas; **investor** bears currency risk |
| Zero-coupon | Discount, no periodic interest (e.g. T-bills) |
| Inflation-indexed | Principal / coupon adjusted to inflation |
| Sovereign Gold Bond | 2015; **2.5%** + gold price; new issues stopped |
- Terms: bondholder = **creditor**; **maturity** = repayment date; **coupon** = interest; face value = nominal value; **yield** = return at market price.
- **Price and yield move inversely**; higher policy rates, inflation, **government borrowing** → yields rise; **RBI OMO purchases** → yields fall.
- Other own-MCQ facts: Jim O'Neill coined **BRIC (2001)**; first BRIC summit **2009**.
