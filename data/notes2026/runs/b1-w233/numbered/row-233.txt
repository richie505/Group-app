## SUB 1 | Jallianwala Bagh Massacre – 13 April 1919
[1:0] # The Incident
[1:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [1:1.0] Place | Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar (Punjab) – an enclosed ground, buildings on all sides, one narrow exit
  [1:1.1] Date | 13 April 1919 – Baisakhi day ("black day" of Indian history)
  [1:1.2] Context | Rowlatt Satyagraha – protest against the Rowlatt Act (passed March 1919)
  [1:1.3] Officer who ordered firing | Brig.-Gen. Reginald Dyer
  [1:1.4] Lt. Governor of Punjab | Sir Michael O'Dwyer (1912-19) – backed Dyer
  [1:1.5] Viceroy | Lord Chelmsford (1916-21; also Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms / GoI Act 1919)
[1:2] - **Why the crowd gathered:** to protest the arrest (**9 April 1919**) of the two Amritsar Congress leaders **Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr Satyapal** and the Rowlatt Act; most of the ~20,000 had come from villages for the **Baisakhi** fair, unaware of Dyer's ban on meetings.
[1:3] - 10 April: protestors marching for the leaders' release were fired on → riots, five Englishmen killed, missionary **Marcella Sherwood** assaulted.
[1:4] - Dyer **blocked the exit**, gave **no warning**, fired on an **unarmed, non-violent crowd** for 10-15 minutes – **1,650 rounds**, until the ammunition ran out.
[1:5] - Casualties: **official 379 dead** (~1,100-1,200 wounded); Congress estimate **about 1,000-1,200 dead**.
[1:6] # Martial Law in Punjab
[1:7] - Martial law proclaimed in **Punjab** (Amritsar, **Lahore**, Gujranwala …): **public floggings**, **aircraft strafing**.
[1:8] - **Crawling order**: Indians made to **crawl on their bellies** down the Amritsar lane where **Marcella Sherwood** had been assaulted.
[1:9] # Impact
[1:10] - **Faith in British justice declined**; hostility to British rule deepened; a turning point for Gandhi – background to the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)**.
[1:11] > Dyer = the officer who fired; O'Dwyer = Lt. Governor; Chelmsford = Viceroy. "General Dyer" PYQs always point to Jallianwala Bagh.

## SUB 2 | Rowlatt Act, 1919
[2:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [2:0.0] Official name | Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919
  [2:0.1] Popular names | Rowlatt Act; "Black Act" (Gandhi); "No Vakil, No Dalil, No Appeal"
  [2:0.2] Committee behind it | Sedition Committee (Rowlatt Committee), appointed 1917, headed by Justice Sidney Rowlatt; reported 1918
  [2:0.3] Bills | Two bills in the Imperial Legislative Council, 6 February 1919 – one dropped, one passed
  [2:0.4] Passed | March 1919 (10 March)
  [2:0.5] Basis | Permanent replacement / extension of the Defence of India Act, 1915
  [2:0.6] Repealed | Under Viceroy Lord Reading (1921-26), along with the Press Act, 1910
[2:1] - **Aim:** to crush **revolutionary nationalists** and curb national / revolutionary activities – a perceived threat from revolutionaries, not press control, the Congress or constitutional reform.
[2:2] - **Provisions:** **arrest without warrant**; **detention / imprisonment without trial for up to two years**; in-camera trial by a special court of **three High Court judges**, **no appeal**; evidence not admissible under the Evidence Act; **habeas corpus** suspended.
[2:3] - Why it caused popular indignation: it **authorised imprisonment without trial** and **limited individual liberty** – it had nothing to do with religion, education, trade unions or the press as such.
[2:4] - Passed despite opposition of **every Indian member** of the Imperial Legislative Council; **Jinnah, Madan Mohan Malaviya, Mazhar-ul-Haq** resigned in protest.
[2:5] - **Mohammad Ali and Abul Kalam Azad did not resign from the Council in 1916** – they were interned during the war (UPSC 2003 A-R: A false, R true).
[2:6] - **1919 = Gandhi's entry into active all-India politics** (returned from South Africa 1915) – he led the protest against this Act.
[2:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History 04 p.135-137; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.352-353 and p.853 (Lord Reading: repeal of the Press Act 1910 and Rowlatt Act); NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 06 p.56; NextGen Modern India L3 Part 7 (src/ap-history/nextgen-modern-l3-07.txt) p.1; old app notes (10 March)

## SUB 4 | Rowlatt Satyagraha
[4:0] # 1919 in Dates
[4:1] TABLE head: Date (1919) | Event
  [4:1.0] 24 February | Satyagraha Sabha formed at Bombay – mostly young radicals of the Home Rule League, plus Pan-Islamists
  [4:1.1] March | Rowlatt Act passed
  [4:1.2] 6 April | Nationwide hartal – fasting and prayer (first all-India mass strike)
  [4:1.3] 8 April | Gandhi at Vijayawada
  [4:1.4] 9 April | Kitchlew and Satyapal arrested in Amritsar
  [4:1.5] 13 April | Jallianwala Bagh massacre
  [4:1.6] 18 April | Satyagraha withdrawn after violence (Punjab, Ahmedabad) – Gandhi's "Himalayan blunder"
  [4:1.7] 31 May | Tagore renounced knighthood
  [4:1.8] 14 October | Hunter Committee announced
  [4:1.9] December | Amritsar Congress session (Motilal Nehru)
[4:2] # Nature
[4:3] - Gandhi's **first all-India struggle**; he **used the Home Rule League** network; mainly **urban**.
[4:4] - The **Sedition Committee** was the basis of the Act; the **Simon Commission (1928)** protests did **not** coincide with it (UPSC 2015: 1 and 2 only).
[4:5] - The massacre happened **in the context of the Rowlatt Satyagraha**; the event that immediately preceded it = **enactment of the Rowlatt Act** (and the 6 April hartal).
[4:6] - A **no-tax campaign** against the Act was suggested by **Swami Shraddhanand** (Arya Samaj leader who led the Delhi protests).
[4:7] # Andhra Response
[4:8] - 6 April hartal observed peacefully at **Visakhapatnam, Rajahmundry, Guntur, Vijayawada** (also Nellore).
[4:9] - Gandhi's speech at **Vijayawada (8 April 1919)** was translated by **Ayyadevara Kaleswara Rao**.
[4:10] - **Andhra Patrika** and **Krishna Patrika** opposed the Act.
[4:11] - **Ganjam, Godavari and Krishna** district conferences condemned the massacre; **Prakasam** (Nellore meeting, Sept 1919) demanded fundamental rights.
[4:12] - The **Guntur Bar Association** (under **Konda Venkatappayya**) demanded the jailing of General Dyer.

## SUB 5 | Hunter Committee and the Congress Inquiry
[5:0] # Hunter Committee (official)
[5:1] - Official name **Disorders Inquiry Committee**; announced **14 October 1919** by the Government of India (ordered by Montagu).
[5:2] - Chairman **Lord William Hunter** (former Solicitor-General for Scotland).
[5:3] - Probed the **Jallianwala Bagh massacre** and the disturbances in **Bombay, Delhi and Punjab**.
[5:4] - **Eight members – five British, three Indian** (Rocket Sheets say 7 = 4 + 3): **Chimanlal H. Setalvad**, **Pandit Jagat Narayan**, **Sultan Ahmed Khan**; the Indians wrote a **minority report** more critical of Dyer.
[5:5] - Report (March 1920): Dyer's action an **"error of judgement"** / grave error; Dyer **censured and relieved of command**, but **no penal action**; Indemnity Act had already protected officers.
[5:6] - Gandhi called the findings a **"whitewash"**; the Amritsar Congress (Dec 1919) resolved to **boycott** it; the **House of Lords** backed Dyer; the Morning Post raised a fund for him.
[5:7] # Congress Inquiry Committee (non-official)
[5:8] - Congress **Punjab Sub-Committee**: **Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Abbas Tyabji, M.R. Jayakar, Gandhi**.
[5:9] - Report ("The Congress Punjab Inquiry", 1920) **drafted by Gandhi**: firing **inhuman**; official toll (379) rejected – about 1,200 dead.
[5:10] TABLE head: Point | Hunter Committee | Congress committee
  [5:10.0] Nature | Official | Non-official
  [5:10.1] Members | Hunter + 3 Indians (Setalvad, Jagat Narayan, Sultan Ahmed) | Motilal, C.R. Das, Tyabji, Jayakar, Gandhi
  [5:10.2] Verdict | "Error of judgement" | Firing inhuman
[5:11] > Hunter **Committee (1919)** ≠ Hunter **Education Commission (1882)**.
