## SUB 0 | Lahore Session, 1929 – Purna Swaraj
[0:0] # Run-up
[0:1] - **Calcutta session (Dec 1928, president Motilal Nehru):** accepted the Nehru Report (Dominion Status) with a **one-year ultimatum** – if Dominion Status was not granted by **31 December 1929**, Congress would adopt **complete independence** and launch civil disobedience.
[0:2] - **Irwin's (Deepavali) Declaration, 31 Oct 1929:** Dominion Status as the goal, no timeline; promised a Round Table Conference. The **Delhi Manifesto** (2 Nov 1929) demands were rejected in December 1929.
[0:3] # Session facts
[0:4] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [0:4.0] Session | 44th session, Lahore, 29 Dec 1929 – 1 Jan 1930
  [0:4.1] President | Jawaharlal Nehru – his first Congress presidency (backed by Gandhi though most PCCs opposed him); succeeded his father Motilal (1928)
  [0:4.2] Viceroy | Lord Irwin (1926-31)
  [0:4.3] Resolution passed | Purna Swaraj – at midnight, 31 December 1929 (not Calcutta 1928, not Karachi 1931)
  [0:4.4] Flag | Nehru hoisted the newly adopted tricolour at midnight, 31 Dec 1929, on the banks of the Ravi
[0:5] - Nehru declared in his presidential address: "We have now an open conspiracy to free this country from foreign rule"; he **declared the aim** and is credited in most keys with **moving** the Purna Swaraj resolution (some accounts say Gandhi moved it).
[0:6] # Decisions of the Lahore session
[0:7] - **Purna Swaraj (complete independence)** declared the goal; the **Nehru Report / Dominion Status** scheme declared lapsed.
[0:8] - **Round Table Conference to be boycotted.**
[0:9] - **Congress Working Committee authorised to launch civil disobedience** (including non-payment of taxes); members of legislatures asked to resign.
[0:10] - **26 January 1930** fixed as the **first Independence (Purna Swaraj) Day**, to be observed every year.
[0:11] - **Not** a Lahore resolution: **abolition of untouchability**; not the session that launched the NCM (1920) or decided to attend the RTC.
[0:12] # 26 January 1930
[0:13] - **First Independence Day:** flag hoisted and the **Independence Pledge** read out all over India ("the inalienable right of the Indian people ... to have freedom"). Drafting: PMF – **Nehru** (issued by the Working Committee); Spectrum – "supposed to have been drafted by **Gandhi**".
[0:14] - **26 January 1950** was chosen for the Constitution because Congress had celebrated **26 January 1930** as the first Independence Day.
[0:15] - **Early demand:** **Hasrat Mohani** was the first to demand complete independence from a Congress forum (Ahmedabad, **1921**).
[0:16] - **AP angle:** **Bulusu Sambamurthy (East Godavari)** got a Purna Swaraj resolution passed at the district level first.
[0:17] > Lahore 1929 = Jawaharlal Nehru = Purna Swaraj = 31 Dec 1929, flag on the Ravi = 26 January 1930. The "19 December 1929" date seen in some keys is wrong – the session met only from 29 December.
[0:18] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.183-185; Spectrum pdf p.400-402; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.6, p.71; NextGen AP History Part 19 p.3; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purna_Swaraj; old app notes (district resolution, 26 Jan 1950)
## SUB 1 | Dandi March and the Salt Satyagraha, 1930
[1:0] # Prelude
[1:1] - **31 Jan 1930:** Gandhi's **eleven demands** to Viceroy **Irwin** (ultimatum to 11 March); the peasant demand **abolition of the salt tax and the government's salt monopoly**. **2 March 1930:** letter informing Irwin of the Salt March. Irwin refused.
[1:2] - **Feb 1930:** CWC at Sabarmati gave Gandhi full powers to launch the movement.
[1:3] # Why salt
[1:4] - Salt = a **daily necessity of rich and poor alike**; the tax (at times 14 times the value) and the **state monopoly** showed the most oppressive face of British rule.
[1:5] - Gandhi: the government even **destroyed naturally formed salt** to stop untaxed use – it had no right to destroy what nature produced.
[1:6] - **Immediate issue:** salt tax / monopoly; **ultimate goal:** **Purna Swaraj**.
[1:7] # The march
[1:8] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [1:8.0] Start | 12 March 1930 (not 15 March) from Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad
  [1:8.1] Marchers | 78 followers (79 with Gandhi; often rounded to "80"); the largest group (about half) from Gujarat
  [1:8.2] Only Telugu marcher | Yerneni (Erneni) Subrahmanyam
  [1:8.3] Distance / time | About 240 miles (~385-390 km) – not 240 km – in 24 days, entirely on foot
  [1:8.4] Destination | Dandi, a coastal village in Navsari district, Gujarat
  [1:8.5] Reached Dandi | 5 April 1930 – "I want world sympathy in this battle of Right against Might" (message to the press, 5.4.1930)
  [1:8.6] Salt law broken | 6 April 1930 – Gandhi picked up a lump of natural salt on the beach; the CDM formally began
  [1:8.7] Viceroy | Lord Irwin
[1:9] - **"Sedition has become my religion":** said during the Salt March (**Borsad speech, 18 March 1930** – "I have made sedition my dharma"; UPSC 2025 key: at the time of violating the Salt Law).
[1:10] - **Subhas Bose** likened the march to **Napoleon's march from Elba to Paris**; the **US press (Time)** followed the march – Time named Gandhi Man of the Year (1931).
[1:11] - **Arrest:** Gandhi arrested on the night of **4-5 May 1930** at Karadi and lodged in **Yerwada Jail**; successors **Abbas Tyabji** (1st), then **Sarojini Naidu** (2nd).
[1:12] > The CDM "began with" the Dandi March; Gandhi set out from Sabarmati but **launched** the movement at **Dandi** on 6 April (some keys give Sabarmati for "started the CDM from").
[1:13] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.189-192, p.195; Spectrum pdf p.402-404; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.58, p.71; NextGen AP History Part 19 p.3; mkgandhi.org/articles/dandi.php; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_March; governancenow.com (Borsad speech); old app notes (Elba, Karadi)

[2:0] TABLE head: Region / place | Leader / form
  [2:0.0] Dharasana (Gujarat) | Abbas Tyabji began it (7 May); Sarojini Naidu, Imam Sahib and Manilal led the raid on 21 May 1930 – unarmed beatings reported by US journalist Webb Miller (United Press)

[2:2] - **Regional PYQs:** **Charan Paduka massacre** (Chhatarpur, 14 Jan 1931) = "Jallianwala Bagh of Madhya Pradesh"; Chhattisgarh 1930 order: Poorna Swaraj week → Bilaspur flag attempt → Raipur imprisonments → Ravi Shankar Shukla elected Raipur District Council chairman from jail.

## SUB 3 | Khudai Khidmatgars, Peshawar and the Garhwal Rifles
[3:0] - **Khudai Khidmatgars ("Servants of God") = "Red Shirts"**, founded by **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** – "**Frontier Gandhi**" / Badshah Khan – among the **Pathans of the NWFP**; pledged to **non-violence** and the freedom struggle; called for **Pathan regional unity and a struggle against colonialism** (not union with Afghanistan, terror or communism).
[3:1] - **23 April 1930:** his arrest led to mass demonstrations in **Peshawar** (Qissa Khwani firing); the city was in the crowds' hands for over a week.
[3:2] - **Peshawar incident:** two platoons of the **Garhwal Rifles** under **Chandra Singh Garhwali** refused to fire on the unarmed crowd – during the **CDM**.
[3:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.192; Spectrum pdf p.407; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.71
## SUB 4 | Women, Martyrs and Notable Participants
[4:0] - CDM = **first nationalist movement with women in large numbers** (picketing liquor and foreign-cloth shops); "maximum women's participation" in keys = **Salt Satyagraha**. **Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay** persuaded Gandhi to admit women.
[4:1] TABLE head: Person | Fact
  [4:1.0] Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay | Led women into the Salt Satyagraha (Bombay Chowpatty)
  [4:1.1] Kasturba Gandhi | Protests; imprisoned
  [4:1.2] Sarojini Naidu | Led the Dharasana raid; first Indian woman INC president (Kanpur 1925)
  [4:1.3] Aruna Asaf Ali | Hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank – Quit India 1942, not CDM
  [4:1.4] Ambabai | Karnataka (Mysore) – picketing at Udipi
  [4:1.5] Prabhavati Devi | Bihar – Patna
  [4:1.6] Rama Devi | Rajasthan – Bijolia peasant movement; jailed in the salt satyagraha (1930) and the 1932 CDM
  [4:1.7] Dr. Radhabai | Arrested 13 June 1932 leading a procession (second CDM)
  [4:1.8] Duvvuri Subbamma | Andhra (East Godavari) woman leader jailed in the CDM
  [4:1.9] Paturi Balasaraswatamma | First woman arrested in Nellore
[4:2] - **Babu Genu** – CDM martyr: crushed by a lorry carrying foreign cloth, **Bombay, 12 Dec 1930**. **Khudiram Bose, Prafulla Chaki** (Muzaffarpur, 1908, target Kingsford) and the **Chapekars** (Rand, Pune, 1897) belong to the earlier revolutionary phase.
[4:3] - **Vinoba Bhave** – first arrested in **1923 (Nagpur Flag Satyagraha)** in his own words; many keys wrongly say the CDM.
[4:4] - Andhra: **"Kattera Udyamam"** raised women's participation.
