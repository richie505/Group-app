## SUB 1 | Jallianwala Bagh Massacre – 13 April 1919
[233:1:0] # The Incident
[233:1:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [233:1:1.0] Place | Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar (Punjab) – an enclosed ground, buildings on all sides, one narrow exit
  [233:1:1.1] Date | 13 April 1919 – Baisakhi day ("black day" of Indian history)
  [233:1:1.2] Context | Rowlatt Satyagraha – protest against the Rowlatt Act (passed March 1919)
  [233:1:1.3] Officer who ordered firing | Brig.-Gen. Reginald Dyer
  [233:1:1.4] Lt. Governor of Punjab | Sir Michael O'Dwyer (1912-19) – backed Dyer
  [233:1:1.5] Viceroy | Lord Chelmsford (1916-21; also Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms / GoI Act 1919)
[233:1:2] - **Why the crowd gathered:** to protest the arrest (**9 April 1919**) of the two Amritsar Congress leaders **Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr Satyapal** and the Rowlatt Act; most of the ~20,000 had come from villages for the **Baisakhi** fair, unaware of Dyer's ban on meetings.
[233:1:3] - 10 April: protestors marching for the leaders' release were fired on → riots, five Englishmen killed, missionary **Marcella Sherwood** assaulted.
[233:1:4] - Dyer **blocked the exit**, gave **no warning**, fired on an **unarmed, non-violent crowd** for 10-15 minutes – **1,650 rounds**, until the ammunition ran out.
[233:1:5] - Casualties: **official 379 dead** (~1,100-1,200 wounded); Congress estimate **about 1,000-1,200 dead**.
[233:1:6] # Martial Law in Punjab
[233:1:7] - Martial law proclaimed in **Punjab** (Amritsar, **Lahore**, Gujranwala …): **public floggings**, **aircraft strafing**.
[233:1:8] - **Crawling order**: Indians made to **crawl on their bellies** down the Amritsar lane where **Marcella Sherwood** had been assaulted.
[233:1:9] # Impact
[233:1:10] - **Faith in British justice declined**; hostility to British rule deepened; a turning point for Gandhi – background to the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)**.
[233:1:11] > Dyer = the officer who fired; O'Dwyer = Lt. Governor; Chelmsford = Viceroy. "General Dyer" PYQs always point to Jallianwala Bagh.

## SUB 2 | Rowlatt Act, 1919
[233:2:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [233:2:0.0] Official name | Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919
  [233:2:0.1] Popular names | Rowlatt Act; "Black Act" (Gandhi); "No Vakil, No Dalil, No Appeal"
  [233:2:0.2] Committee behind it | Sedition Committee (Rowlatt Committee), appointed 1917, headed by Justice Sidney Rowlatt; reported 1918
  [233:2:0.3] Bills | Two bills in the Imperial Legislative Council, 6 February 1919 – one dropped, one passed
  [233:2:0.4] Passed | March 1919 (10 March)
  [233:2:0.5] Basis | Permanent replacement / extension of the Defence of India Act, 1915
  [233:2:0.6] Repealed | Under Viceroy Lord Reading (1921-26), along with the Press Act, 1910
[233:2:1] - **Aim:** to crush **revolutionary nationalists** and curb national / revolutionary activities – a perceived threat from revolutionaries, not press control, the Congress or constitutional reform.
[233:2:2] - **Provisions:** **arrest without warrant**; **detention / imprisonment without trial for up to two years**; in-camera trial by a special court of **three High Court judges**, **no appeal**; evidence not admissible under the Evidence Act; **habeas corpus** suspended.
[233:2:3] - Why it caused popular indignation: it **authorised imprisonment without trial** and **limited individual liberty** – it had nothing to do with religion, education, trade unions or the press as such.
[233:2:4] - Passed despite opposition of **every Indian member** of the Imperial Legislative Council; **Jinnah, Madan Mohan Malaviya, Mazhar-ul-Haq** resigned in protest.
[233:2:5] - **Mohammad Ali and Abul Kalam Azad did not resign from the Council in 1916** – they were interned during the war (UPSC 2003 A-R: A false, R true).
[233:2:6] - **1919 = Gandhi's entry into active all-India politics** (returned from South Africa 1915) – he led the protest against this Act.
[233:2:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History 04 p.135-137; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.352-353 and p.853 (Lord Reading: repeal of the Press Act 1910 and Rowlatt Act); NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 06 p.56; NextGen Modern India L3 Part 7 (src/ap-history/nextgen-modern-l3-07.txt) p.1; old app notes (10 March)

## SUB 4 | Rowlatt Satyagraha
[233:4:0] # 1919 in Dates
[233:4:1] TABLE head: Date (1919) | Event
  [233:4:1.0] 24 February | Satyagraha Sabha formed at Bombay – mostly young radicals of the Home Rule League, plus Pan-Islamists
  [233:4:1.1] March | Rowlatt Act passed
  [233:4:1.2] 6 April | Nationwide hartal – fasting and prayer (first all-India mass strike)
  [233:4:1.3] 8 April | Gandhi at Vijayawada
  [233:4:1.4] 9 April | Kitchlew and Satyapal arrested in Amritsar
  [233:4:1.5] 13 April | Jallianwala Bagh massacre
  [233:4:1.6] 18 April | Satyagraha withdrawn after violence (Punjab, Ahmedabad) – Gandhi's "Himalayan blunder"
  [233:4:1.7] 31 May | Tagore renounced knighthood
  [233:4:1.8] 14 October | Hunter Committee announced
  [233:4:1.9] December | Amritsar Congress session (Motilal Nehru)
[233:4:2] # Nature
[233:4:3] - Gandhi's **first all-India struggle**; he **used the Home Rule League** network; mainly **urban**.
[233:4:4] - The **Sedition Committee** was the basis of the Act; the **Simon Commission (1928)** protests did **not** coincide with it (UPSC 2015: 1 and 2 only).
[233:4:5] - The massacre happened **in the context of the Rowlatt Satyagraha**; the event that immediately preceded it = **enactment of the Rowlatt Act** (and the 6 April hartal).
[233:4:6] - A **no-tax campaign** against the Act was suggested by **Swami Shraddhanand** (Arya Samaj leader who led the Delhi protests).
[233:4:7] # Andhra Response
[233:4:8] - 6 April hartal observed peacefully at **Visakhapatnam, Rajahmundry, Guntur, Vijayawada** (also Nellore).
[233:4:9] - Gandhi's speech at **Vijayawada (8 April 1919)** was translated by **Ayyadevara Kaleswara Rao**.
[233:4:10] - **Andhra Patrika** and **Krishna Patrika** opposed the Act.
[233:4:11] - **Ganjam, Godavari and Krishna** district conferences condemned the massacre; **Prakasam** (Nellore meeting, Sept 1919) demanded fundamental rights.
[233:4:12] - The **Guntur Bar Association** (under **Konda Venkatappayya**) demanded the jailing of General Dyer.

## SUB 5 | Hunter Committee and the Congress Inquiry
[233:5:0] # Hunter Committee (official)
[233:5:1] - Official name **Disorders Inquiry Committee**; announced **14 October 1919** by the Government of India (ordered by Montagu).
[233:5:2] - Chairman **Lord William Hunter** (former Solicitor-General for Scotland).
[233:5:3] - Probed the **Jallianwala Bagh massacre** and the disturbances in **Bombay, Delhi and Punjab**.
[233:5:4] - **Eight members – five British, three Indian** (Rocket Sheets say 7 = 4 + 3): **Chimanlal H. Setalvad**, **Pandit Jagat Narayan**, **Sultan Ahmed Khan**; the Indians wrote a **minority report** more critical of Dyer.
[233:5:5] - Report (March 1920): Dyer's action an **"error of judgement"** / grave error; Dyer **censured and relieved of command**, but **no penal action**; Indemnity Act had already protected officers.
[233:5:6] - Gandhi called the findings a **"whitewash"**; the Amritsar Congress (Dec 1919) resolved to **boycott** it; the **House of Lords** backed Dyer; the Morning Post raised a fund for him.
[233:5:7] # Congress Inquiry Committee (non-official)
[233:5:8] - Congress **Punjab Sub-Committee**: **Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Abbas Tyabji, M.R. Jayakar, Gandhi**.
[233:5:9] - Report ("The Congress Punjab Inquiry", 1920) **drafted by Gandhi**: firing **inhuman**; official toll (379) rejected – about 1,200 dead.
[233:5:10] TABLE head: Point | Hunter Committee | Congress committee
  [233:5:10.0] Nature | Official | Non-official
  [233:5:10.1] Members | Hunter + 3 Indians (Setalvad, Jagat Narayan, Sultan Ahmed) | Motilal, C.R. Das, Tyabji, Jayakar, Gandhi
  [233:5:10.2] Verdict | "Error of judgement" | Firing inhuman
[233:5:11] > Hunter **Committee (1919)** ≠ Hunter **Education Commission (1882)**.

## SUB 6 | Udham Singh
[233:6:0] - Shot **Sir Michael O'Dwyer** (Lt. Governor of Punjab in 1919, who had endorsed Dyer) on **13 March 1940** at **Caxton Hall, London** – **not General Dyer** (Dyer died in 1927).
[233:6:1] - He had witnessed the Amritsar events; alias **Ram Mohammad Singh Azad**; hanged in 1940.
[233:6:2] - Gandhi called the act "an act of insanity".

## SUB 0 | Chauri Chaura and the Withdrawal
[234:0:0] - **1 Feb 1922:** Gandhi's ultimatum – civil disobedience from **Bardoli** (Gujarat); Gandhi was at **Bardoli** when Chauri Chaura happened.
[234:0:1] - **Chauri Chaura, 4 Feb 1922** (Spectrum, PMF: 5 Feb), **Gorakhpur district, United Provinces (UP)** – police beat a volunteer leader campaigning against **liquor sale**, fired on the crowd → crowd **burnt the police station** → **22 policemen** killed.
[234:0:2] - Sequence: police firing → police station burnt → movement suspended (**Bardoli resolution, CWC, 11-12 Feb 1922**) → Gandhi arrested (**10 March 1922**).
[234:0:3] - **Reason for withdrawal: violence at Chauri Chaura** – not arrests, not British concessions, not Jallianwala Bagh (1919).
[234:0:4] - **Defence of the accused:** **Madan Mohan Malaviya with Krishna Kant**; 172 sentenced to death, **19 hanged**.
[234:0:5] - **AICC Delhi, 24 Feb 1922:** **Dr. B.S. Moonje** moved a censure vote against Gandhi.
[234:0:6] - Reactions: **Subhas Bose** – "national calamity"; **Jawaharlal Nehru** – "amazement and consternation"; **M.N. Roy** – weak leadership; Marxists – to stop a radical anti-landlord turn.

## SUB 1 | Khilafat Movement – Aims, Leaders, Gandhi's Role
[234:1:0] # Aim and trigger
[234:1:1] - **Aim:** keep the **Ottoman Sultan as Khalifa** with control of the Muslim holy places, and save the **territorial integrity of the Ottoman (Turkish) Empire** after World War I – not a separate Muslim state, not land-revenue relief.
[234:1:2] - **Trigger:** **Treaty of Sevres (May 1920)** dismembered Turkey; the Khalifa was removed from power.
[234:1:3] - **Khilafat Committee, 1919** (Bombay) – led by the **Ali brothers (Muhammad Ali, Shaukat Ali)** with **Maulana Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Hasrat Mohani**, Dr M.A. Ansari. Started by the Ali brothers.
[234:1:4] - **End:** Mustafa Kemal Pasha stripped the Sultan of power (**Nov 1922**), made Turkey a secular republic (1923) and **abolished the Caliphate in 1924** – the issue lost relevance (CDS 2011: both statements true, II explains I).
[234:1:5] # Leaders
[234:1:6] TABLE head: Leader | Fact
  [234:1:6.0] Ali brothers | Started/led the movement; at Karachi (July 1921) urged Muslims to quit the Army – arrested Sept 1921
  [234:1:6.1] Hakim Ajmal Khan | Gave up the title Haziq-ul-Mulk; acting president, Ahmedabad 1921
  [234:1:6.2] Hasrat Mohani | Coined "Inquilab Zindabad"; demanded complete independence (1921)
  [234:1:6.3] Tej Bahadur Sapru | NOT a Khilafat leader (Liberal)
  [234:1:6.4] Jinnah | Opposed it; warned Gandhi not to encourage the fanaticism of Muslim religious leaders; denounced Gandhi's participation
[234:1:7] # Gandhi's role
[234:1:8] - **All-India Khilafat Conference, Delhi, 23-24 Nov 1919** – **Gandhi elected president**; non-cooperation first proposed there. Gandhi was president of the All India Khilafat Committee.
[234:1:9] - Gandhi saw it as a **"golden opportunity" for Hindu-Muslim unity** "such as would not arise in a hundred years" – to win Muslim support against the British.
[234:1:10] - It arose after the **First** World War (not the Second).
[234:1:11] - **Did not support:** Jinnah, **Madan Mohan Malaviya**; supporters included Jawaharlal Nehru, Swami Shraddhanand.
[234:1:12] > Result: the Khilafat-NCM phase was the **high point of Hindu-Muslim unity** (Hindu-Muslim differences narrowed).

=== NOTES 1:237 | Civil Disobedience Movement – Lahore Session and Purna Swaraj, Dandi March, Spread and Phases
## SUB 0 | Dandi March and the Salt Satyagraha, 1930
[237:0:0] # Prelude
[237:0:1] - **31 Jan 1930:** Gandhi's **eleven demands** to Viceroy **Irwin** (ultimatum to 11 March); the peasant demand **abolition of the salt tax and the government's salt monopoly**. **2 March 1930:** letter informing Irwin of the Salt March. Irwin refused.
[237:0:2] - **Feb 1930:** CWC at Sabarmati gave Gandhi full powers to launch the movement.
[237:0:3] # Why salt
[237:0:4] - Salt = a **daily necessity of rich and poor alike**; the tax (at times 14 times the value) and the **state monopoly** showed the most oppressive face of British rule.
[237:0:5] - Gandhi: the government even **destroyed naturally formed salt** to stop untaxed use – it had no right to destroy what nature produced.
[237:0:6] - **Immediate issue:** salt tax / monopoly; **ultimate goal:** **Purna Swaraj**.
[237:0:7] # The march
[237:0:8] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [237:0:8.0] Start | 12 March 1930 (not 15 March) from Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad
  [237:0:8.1] Marchers | 78 followers (79 with Gandhi; often rounded to "80"); the largest group (about half) from Gujarat
  [237:0:8.2] Only Telugu marcher | Yerneni (Erneni) Subrahmanyam
  [237:0:8.3] Distance / time | About 240 miles (~385-390 km) – not 240 km – in 24 days, entirely on foot
  [237:0:8.4] Destination | Dandi, a coastal village in Navsari district, Gujarat
  [237:0:8.5] Reached Dandi | 5 April 1930 – "I want world sympathy in this battle of Right against Might" (message to the press, 5.4.1930)

## SUB 1 | Lahore Session, 1929 – Purna Swaraj
[237:1:0] # Run-up
[237:1:1] - **Calcutta session (Dec 1928, president Motilal Nehru):** accepted the Nehru Report (Dominion Status) with a **one-year ultimatum** – if Dominion Status was not granted by **31 December 1929**, Congress would adopt **complete independence** and launch civil disobedience.
[237:1:2] - **Irwin's (Deepavali) Declaration, 31 Oct 1929:** Dominion Status as the goal, no timeline; promised a Round Table Conference. The **Delhi Manifesto** (2 Nov 1929) demands were rejected in December 1929.
[237:1:3] # Session facts
[237:1:4] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [237:1:4.0] Session | 44th session, Lahore, 29 Dec 1929 – 1 Jan 1930
  [237:1:4.1] President | Jawaharlal Nehru – his first Congress presidency (backed by Gandhi though most PCCs opposed him); succeeded his father Motilal (1928)
  [237:1:4.2] Viceroy | Lord Irwin (1926-31)
  [237:1:4.3] Resolution passed | Purna Swaraj – at midnight, 31 December 1929 (not Calcutta 1928, not Karachi 1931)
  [237:1:4.4] Flag | Nehru hoisted the newly adopted tricolour at midnight, 31 Dec 1929, on the banks of the Ravi
[237:1:5] - Nehru declared in his presidential address: "We have now an open conspiracy to free this country from foreign rule"; he **declared the aim** and is credited in most keys with **moving** the Purna Swaraj resolution (some accounts say Gandhi moved it).
[237:1:6] # Decisions of the Lahore session
[237:1:7] - **Purna Swaraj (complete independence)** declared the goal; the **Nehru Report / Dominion Status** scheme declared lapsed.
[237:1:8] - **Round Table Conference to be boycotted.**
[237:1:9] - **Congress Working Committee authorised to launch civil disobedience** (including non-payment of taxes); members of legislatures asked to resign.
[237:1:10] - **26 January 1930** fixed as the **first Independence (Purna Swaraj) Day**, to be observed every year.
[237:1:11] - **Not** a Lahore resolution: **abolition of untouchability**; not the session that launched the NCM (1920) or decided to attend the RTC.
[237:1:12] # 26 January 1930
[237:1:13] - **First Independence Day:** flag hoisted and the **Independence Pledge** read out all over India ("the inalienable right of the Indian people ... to have freedom"). Drafting: PMF – **Nehru** (issued by the Working Committee); Spectrum – "supposed to have been drafted by **Gandhi**".
[237:1:14] - **26 January 1950** was chosen for the Constitution because Congress had celebrated **26 January 1930** as the first Independence Day.
[237:1:15] - **Early demand:** **Hasrat Mohani** was the first to demand complete independence from a Congress forum (Ahmedabad, **1921**).
[237:1:16] - **AP angle:** **Bulusu Sambamurthy (East Godavari)** got a Purna Swaraj resolution passed at the district level first.
[237:1:17] > Lahore 1929 = Jawaharlal Nehru = Purna Swaraj = 31 Dec 1929, flag on the Ravi = 26 January 1930. The "19 December 1929" date seen in some keys is wrong – the session met only from 29 December.

## SUB 3 | Khudai Khidmatgars, Peshawar and the Garhwal Rifles
[237:3:0] - **Khudai Khidmatgars ("Servants of God") = "Red Shirts"**, founded by **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** – "**Frontier Gandhi**" / Badshah Khan – among the **Pathans of the NWFP**; pledged to **non-violence** and the freedom struggle; called for **Pathan regional unity and a struggle against colonialism** (not union with Afghanistan, terror or communism).

## SUB 0 | The Dictator and the Organisation in Andhra
[238:0:0] - **Konda Venkatappayya** (had met Gandhi with an Andhra team; "Father of the Andhra State Movement") was appointed **Dictator for Andhra** by the **Andhra Congress Committee** to conduct the **Salt Satyagraha / CDM** – the first leader of the CDM in Andhra (APPSC G2 2000, 2008, 2017) – not Prakasam, Pattabhi, Gadicherla or N.G. Ranga.
[238:0:1] - **Each district had its own dictator, a war council and a military-style camp (sibiram)** – the camps served as salt satyagraha headquarters (idea credited to **Pattabhi Sitaramayya**).

[238:1:0] - **Kakinada Bomb Case:** circle inspector **Mustafa Ali** brutally attacked the satyagrahis of the **Seethanagaram (Sita) Ashram**; in revenge **Prativadi Bhayankarachari**, founder of the revolutionary "**Ujjivana Bharata Samithi / Sammelan**" in the **Godavari district**, threw a bomb at him at Kakinada (failed); he was sentenced (14 years) and sent to the **Andaman Cellular Jail**; he wrote "**Andaman Jeevitam**".
