=== Khilafat Movement – Aims, Leaders, Gandhi's Role
# Aim and trigger
- **Aim:** keep the **Ottoman Sultan as Khalifa** with control of the Muslim holy places, and save the **territorial integrity of the Ottoman (Turkish) Empire** after World War I – not a separate Muslim state, not land-revenue relief.
- **Trigger:** **Treaty of Sevres (May 1920)** dismembered Turkey; the Khalifa was removed from power.
- **Khilafat Committee, 1919** (Bombay) – led by the **Ali brothers (Muhammad Ali, Shaukat Ali)** with **Maulana Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Hasrat Mohani**, Dr M.A. Ansari. Started by the Ali brothers.
- **End:** Mustafa Kemal Pasha stripped the Sultan of power (**Nov 1922**), made Turkey a secular republic (1923) and **abolished the Caliphate in 1924** – the issue lost relevance (CDS 2011: both statements true, II explains I).
# Leaders
| Leader | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Ali brothers** | Started/led the movement; at **Karachi (July 1921)** urged Muslims to **quit the Army** – arrested Sept 1921 |
| **Hakim Ajmal Khan** | Gave up the title **Haziq-ul-Mulk**; acting president, Ahmedabad 1921 |
| **Hasrat Mohani** | Coined **"Inquilab Zindabad"**; demanded complete independence (1921) |
| **Tej Bahadur Sapru** | NOT a Khilafat leader (Liberal) |
| **Jinnah** | Opposed it; **warned Gandhi not to encourage the fanaticism of Muslim religious leaders**; denounced Gandhi's participation |
# Gandhi's role
- **All-India Khilafat Conference, Delhi, 23-24 Nov 1919** – **Gandhi elected president**; non-cooperation first proposed there. Gandhi was president of the All India Khilafat Committee.
- Gandhi saw it as a **"golden opportunity" for Hindu-Muslim unity** "such as would not arise in a hundred years" – to win Muslim support against the British.
- It arose after the **First** World War (not the Second).
- **Did not support:** Jinnah, **Madan Mohan Malaviya**; supporters included Jawaharlal Nehru, Swami Shraddhanand.
> Result: the Khilafat-NCM phase was the **high point of Hindu-Muslim unity** (Hindu-Muslim differences narrowed).

=== Background and Launch of the NCM, 1920
# Causes
- **Khilafat issue**, **Rowlatt Act**, **Jallianwala Bagh (13 April 1919)** and Punjab wrongs, Hunter Committee "eyewash", inadequate **Montagu-Chelmsford reforms**, post-war economic hardship.
- **NOT causes:** the **Salt Law** (1930), Simon Commission (1927).
# Road to launch
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Nov 1919 | Khilafat Conference, Delhi (Gandhi president) |
| Feb 1920 | Joint Hindu-Muslim deputation to the Viceroy failed |
| May 1920 | Treaty of Sevres |
| **June 1920** | **Allahabad** conference of the Khilafat Committee / all parties – approved boycott, **asked Gandhi to lead** |
| **1 Aug 1920** | Gandhi launched the NCM – the day **Tilak died**; returned his **Kaiser-i-Hind** medal |
| 31 Aug 1920 | Khilafat Committee formally started non-cooperation |
| **Sept 1920** | **Calcutta special session (Lala Lajpat Rai)** – resolution **first adopted/passed (1886 to 884)**; C.R. Das opposed |
| **Dec 1920** | **Nagpur session (C. Vijayaraghavachariar)** – **endorsed/ratified**; **C.R. Das moved** the resolution |
- Year of launch = **1920**; slogan **"Swaraj within a year"** belongs to the NCM.
> "Passed" at Calcutta, "ratified" at Nagpur: most keys give **Calcutta** for "first adopted (special session)" and **Nagpur** for "adopted/passed by the Congress".

| Session | President | Point |
|---|---|---|
| Amritsar, Dec 1919 | **Motilal Nehru** | Reforms "inadequate and disappointing" |
| **Calcutta special, Sept 1920** | **Lala Lajpat Rai** | NCM approved |
| **Nagpur, Dec 1920** | **C. Vijayaraghavachariar** | NCM endorsed; new creed |
| **Ahmedabad, Dec 1921** | **C.R. Das** (in jail) – **Hakim Ajmal Khan** acting | **Hasrat Mohani** proposed **Swaraj = complete independence free from all foreign control**; Gandhi made sole authority |
| **Gaya, Dec 1922** | **C.R. Das** | Council entry defeated |
| **Belgaum, Dec 1924** | **Gandhi** | His **only** Congress presidency |
# Nagpur changes
- Creed: **"attainment of Swaraj by all legitimate and peaceful means"** (not "constitutional means"); programme in **stages** – civil disobedience and **non-payment of taxes** only later, if Swaraj did not come within a year and the Government repressed (UPSC 2025: both statements correct).
- **CWC of 15**, **AICC of 350**, **PCCs on linguistic lines**, membership fee **four annas** (not one rupee).
- Congress first declared its **policy towards the Indian (princely) States** – asked the princes to grant full responsible government.
- **Hasrat Mohani** was the first to demand complete independence (1921); Purna Swaraj resolution came only at **Lahore 1929**.

=== NCM Programme – Boycott and Constructive Items
| Boycott (non-constructive) | Constructive |
|---|---|
| **Surrender of titles** and honours | **National schools and colleges** |
| Government schools, colleges, **law courts** (→ **panchayats** for disputes) | **Khadi, charkha**, hand-spinning |
| **Legislatures – Nov 1920 elections boycotted** | **Hindu-Muslim unity**, removal of untouchability |
| **Foreign cloth** (bonfires; imports fell by half), durbars and official functions | **Temperance** – picketing liquor and toddy shops |
| Second stage: civil disobedience, **non-payment of taxes** | **Tilak Swaraj Fund** – **Rs 1 crore**, oversubscribed |
- **Strict non-violence** throughout; Congress volunteer corps.
- **NOT in the programme:** **retention of titles**, **participation in elections**, complying with government orders.
- **Tilak Swaraj Fund (1921)** was raised **to finance the NCM / freedom struggle**, not a memorial.
- **Rabindranath Tagore** called the burning of foreign cloth an **"insensate (ruthless) waste"**.
- In Bihar the government even circulated lists of prominent drinkers to win back excise revenue lost to the anti-liquor drive (BPSC 2019 printed key; not verified).
- Gandhi's methods: **Satyagraha, Swadeshi, non-violence, Hindu-Muslim unity**.

=== Leaders, Lawyers and the Spread of the Movement
# Lawyers who gave up practice
- **C.R. Das** ("**Deshabandhu**", lawyer from East Bengal, defended Aurobindo in the **Alipore Bomb Case**), **Motilal Nehru** (English paper **The Independent**, 1919), Jawaharlal Nehru, **Rajendra Prasad** (Bihar pleader), Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, **Saifuddin Kitchlew** (Punjab), Asaf Ali, **T. Prakasam** – **not** Ambedkar, Malaviya, Sapru or Jinnah.
- **First leader arrested** in the NCM (WBCS 2018 key): **C.R. Das** (Dec 1921); Gandhi only in March 1922.
- Jinnah, Annie Besant, B.C. Pal, G.S. Khaparde **left the Congress**; Jinnah did NOT support the NCM.

=== Chauri Chaura and the Withdrawal
- **1 Feb 1922:** Gandhi's ultimatum – civil disobedience from **Bardoli** (Gujarat); Gandhi was at **Bardoli** when Chauri Chaura happened.
- **Chauri Chaura, 4 Feb 1922** (Spectrum, PMF: 5 Feb), **Gorakhpur district, United Provinces (UP)** – police beat a volunteer leader campaigning against **liquor sale**, fired on the crowd → crowd **burnt the police station** → **22 policemen** killed.
- Sequence: police firing → police station burnt → movement suspended (**Bardoli resolution, CWC, 11-12 Feb 1922**) → Gandhi arrested (**10 March 1922**).
- **Reason for withdrawal: violence at Chauri Chaura** – not arrests, not British concessions, not Jallianwala Bagh (1919).
- **Defence of the accused:** **Madan Mohan Malaviya with Krishna Kant**; 172 sentenced to death, **19 hanged**.
- **AICC Delhi, 24 Feb 1922:** **Dr. B.S. Moonje** moved a censure vote against Gandhi.
- Reactions: **Subhas Bose** – "national calamity"; **Jawaharlal Nehru** – "amazement and consternation"; **M.N. Roy** – weak leadership; Marxists – to stop a radical anti-landlord turn.
- **Centenary:** PM Narendra Modi inaugurated the **Chauri Chaura centenary** on **4 Feb 2021**.

=== Gandhi's Arrest, Outcome and Evaluation
- Arrested **10 March 1922** (Sabarmati); **Great Trial, Ahmedabad, 18 March 1922**, Judge **C.N. Broomfield**; sedition **Sec. 124A IPC** for **Young India** articles ("Tampering with Loyalty"); **pleaded guilty**, asked for the highest penalty; **6 years**; released **1924** on health grounds.
- Earlier, April 1919, Gandhi was detained at **Palwal**. Gandhi's papers: Young India, Navajivan, Harijan, Indian Opinion (The Bengalee = S.N. Banerjea).
# Outcome
- **Hindu-Muslim unity** at its peak; **Congress became a mass movement for the first time**; **fear of British might removed**; nationalism spread from the elite to the masses – but **no British concessions**, Swaraj not won in a year.
- The **Gandhian era** of the freedom movement = **1920-1947**.
