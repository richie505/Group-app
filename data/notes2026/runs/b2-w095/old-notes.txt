=== NOTES 2:95 | Impact of Lpg on Administration – Downsizing, Contractualisation, Npm
## SUB 0 | Contractualisation and Outsourcing
[95:0:0] - **Outsourcing:** hiring outside sources (legal, IT, advertising, security) for regular work once done in-house; an outcome of globalisation, spread by IT – the method that "became popular in the recent past" for staffing jobs in both public and private sectors (APPSC G1).
[95:0:1]   - Distractors: contract appointments (a form of it, narrower), emergency recruitment, on-line recruitment (a channel, not a method of employing).
[95:0:2] - **Contract labour:** **20% (1999) → 40.7%** of manufacturing workforce (2022-23, ASI); such firms **31% lower productivity** (Aug 2025).
[95:0:3] - Weaker direct employer relation → weaker unions; **Industrial Relations Code 2020**: 10% or 100 workers to form a union.
[95:0:4] # AP angle
[95:0:5] - **APCOS** (2020): recruits outsourced staff and **pays directly**, cutting contractor middlemen.
[95:0:6] - **APSRTC:** about 8,000 outsourced staff paid via contractors; wage, PF, ESI irregularities (28 Jul 2026).
[95:0:7] - **Civil Services Board:** curbs political interference in postings.
[95:0:8] - Upper age limit for non-uniform direct recruitment raised **42 → 44** till 30 Sep 2027 (G.O. Ms. No. 187).
[95:0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC Part 3 p.41 (outsourcing); app notes 2:95 (sub 4)
## SUB 1 | Lateral Entry and Performance Management
[95:1:0] - **Normal route:** JS/Director posts by tenure deputation (**Central Staffing Scheme, DoPT**).
[95:1:1] - **Second ARC:** lateral entry at **Joint Secretary** level; performance-based appraisal and promotion; performance incentives.
[95:1:2] - **NITI Aayog Action Agenda:** wider entry at middle/senior levels; longer, stable tenures.
[95:1:3] - First lateral JS joined **2019**; **Aug 2024**: UPSC told to cancel advertisement for **45** posts.
[95:1:4] - **Debate:** domain expertise vs no reservation in single posts, conflict of interest, demotivated career officers.
[95:1:5] # Performance tools
[95:1:6] - **RFD (2009)**, PMES, Cabinet Secretariat: objectives, success indicators, targets = **Key Result Areas**.
[95:1:7] - **Referral Board** reviews adverse ACR remarks; **360-degree** multi-source feedback.
[95:1:8] - **Good Governance Index:** uniform framework for States/UTs. **NeSDA** (2019; 2025 portal June 2026). **GRAI:** **11 indicators, 4 dimensions** on CPGRAMS.
[95:1:9] - India **97th** in UN EGDI 2024.
[95:1:10] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:95 (sub 5, 6)
[95:1:11] - **MBO = Management by Objectives** (Peter Drucker, 1954): manager and subordinate agree objectives and are judged by results – precursor of RFD/KRAs.
## SUB 2 | Downsizing the State and Pay Commissions
[95:2:0] - **Downsizing, disinvestment, privatisation** shrink the State; **FDI promotion** is a growth/external-sector measure, **not** a cut in State size.
[95:2:1] - Keyed set: downsizing, disinvestment, privatising higher education (UPSC 2010).
[95:2:2] - **Disinvestment:** sale of part of PSU equity to the public; need not transfer ownership or management. **Privatisation:** withdrawal or outright sale.
[95:2:3] - **Economic Survey 2025-26:** redefine "Government Company" so the State may hold **less than 51%**.
[95:2:4] # Pay Commissions and staff strength
[95:2:5] TABLE head: Commission | Chair / year | Key change
  [95:2:5.0] Fifth | Justice S. Ratnavel Pandian, 1997 | Staff cut about 30%; freeze on new posts
  [95:2:5.1] Sixth | Justice B.N. Srikrishna, 2008 | Group D merged into Group C; running pay bands; PRIS
  [95:2:5.2] Seventh | Justice A.K. Mathur, 2015 (effective 2016) | Pay matrix replaced pay bands; performance-linked pay
[95:2:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC Part 3 p.41 (disinvestment vs privatisation); app notes 2:95 (sub 0)
## SUB 3 | New Public Management
[95:3:0] - Private-sector techniques in government (Phase 4 of Indian public administration after 1991); term popularised by **Christopher Hood (1991)**.
[95:3:1] - **Hood's doctrines:** hands-on professional management, explicit standards and performance measures, **output controls**, competition.
[95:3:2] - **Features:** performance auditing, **contracting out non-core services**, citizen as customer; it does **not** reject performance measures.
[95:3:3] - **Pioneers:** **UK (Thatcher), New Zealand, Australia, USA**.
[95:3:4] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:95 (sub 1)
## SUB 4 | Reinventing Government (Osborne and Gaebler, 1992)
[95:4:0] - "**Steering, not rowing**." Ten principles:
[95:4:1] TABLE head: Principle | Meaning
  [95:4:1.0] Catalytic | Steer rather than row
  [95:4:1.1] Community-owned | Empower rather than serve
  [95:4:1.2] Competitive | Inject competition
  [95:4:1.3] Mission-driven | Rules → mission
  [95:4:1.4] Results-oriented | Fund outcomes
  [95:4:1.5] Customer-driven | Meet customer needs
  [95:4:1.6] Enterprising | Earn rather than spend
  [95:4:1.7] Anticipatory | Prevent rather than cure
  [95:4:1.8] Decentralised | Hierarchy → participation and teamwork
  [95:4:1.9] Market-oriented | Change through the market
[95:4:2] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:95 (sub 2)
[95:4:3] - **Authors:** David **Osborne** and Ted **Gaebler**, *Reinventing Government* (1992).
## SUB 5 | Critiques of NPM and Citizen as Customer
[95:5:0] TABLE head: Thinker | Work | Idea
  [95:5:0.0] Denhardt & Denhardt | New Public Service (2000) | "Serve, not steer"; citizenship, public interest
  [95:5:0.1] Mark Moore | Creating Public Value (1995) | Value includes legitimacy and trust, not only efficiency
[95:5:1] - Both reject citizen → customer and State → service seller.
[95:5:2] - **Citizen-as-customer instruments:** Citizens' Charters, service standards, grievance redressal.
[95:5:3] - **Criticism:** citizen has **rights, not just purchasing power**; equity and accountability suffer.

=== NOTES 2:96 | Impact of Lpg on Federalism, Welfare and Social Sector Spending
## SUB 0 | Centrally Sponsored Schemes (2015) and Central Control Loosened
[96:0:0] - **1991 reforms loosened the Centre's control over the States** in **industrial licensing, tariffs and foreign investment**: central licensing largely ended, tariffs fell and FDI was opened, so States began competing for investment (APPSC Polytechnic Lecturers 2011). Planning-era control over State plans was not the target.
[96:0:1] - **CSS rationalised** by the **NITI Aayog Sub-Group of Chief Ministers (2015)**.
[96:0:2] TABLE head: Group | Detail
  [96:0:2.0] Core of the core | Social protection and social inclusion schemes – MGNREGA, NSAP, umbrella schemes for SCs, STs, minorities; first charge on funds
  [96:0:2.1] Core | Mostly 60:40; 90:10 for NE and Himalayan states
  [96:0:2.2] Optional | States' choice; 50:50 (80:20 for NE and Himalayan states)
[96:0:3] - **PMMVY, ICDS** now **60:40**. CSS grew **26.5% a year** since 2016-17; state plan allocations fell nearly **30%**.
[96:0:4] > APPSC G1 2017: "core of the core" = social protection and social inclusion schemes (not infrastructure, Digital India or "National Agenda" schemes in general).

## SUB 0 | Disinvestment – DIPAM, Methods and the New PSE Policy
[97:0:0] - **Department of Disinvestment: 10 Dec 1999** (a Ministry of Disinvestment 2001-04) → **DIPAM (14 Apr 2016)**, Ministry of Finance; manages Centre's CPSE equity.
[97:0:1] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [97:0:1.0] Minority stake sale | Govt keeps ≥51% and control; done through IPO / OFS
  [97:0:1.1] Majority | Control passes; minority stake kept
  [97:0:1.2] Strategic sale | Shares (usually 50% or more) + management control to a strategic partner
[97:0:2] - **Methods:** **IPO** (first issue) vs **FPO** (listed PSE); **OFS** (since **2012**); **IPP** (QIBs only); **CPSE ETF, Bharat-22 ETF**.
[97:0:3] - **Minority stake sale method (New PSE Policy):** **Initial Public Offer (IPO) or Offer for Sale (OFS)** on the stock exchange – not full privatisation, transfer to another CPSE or closure (those are strategic-sale / exit options).
[97:0:4] - **History:** 1991: 20% disinvestment announced ("token" disinvestment of 5-10% shares); till 1999-2000 only minority stakes; **1999-2004** strategic sales (13 PSEs); listed PSUs to meet the **25% public shareholding** norm.
[97:0:5] - **LIC IPO (17 May 2022):** biggest issue then.
[97:0:6] - **Trend:** revenue fell every year except **2022-23**; **dividends** grew every year.
[97:0:7] - **Effect on backward states (APPSC G2 2011):** PSUs had built roads, power, townships and other **infrastructure** in backward regions; disinvestment and the private sector's preference for developed areas left backward states short of **infrastructure facilities** (examiner's key; an interpretive question).
[97:0:8] # New PSE Policy 2021 (Atmanirbhar Bharat)
[97:0:9] - Exit **non-strategic** sectors; bare minimum in four strategic groups: **atomic energy, space, defence**; **transport, telecom**; **power, petroleum, coal, minerals**; **banking, insurance, financial services** (not agriculture/textiles/retail).

=== NOTES 2:98 | Constitutional, Statutory, Executive, Regulatory and Quasi-judicial Bodies
## SUB 0 | Constitutional vs Non-constitutional – Odd One Out
[98:0:0] # How to tell them apart
[98:0:1] TABLE head: Type | Basis | Examples
  [98:0:1.0] Constitutional | An Article of the Constitution | FC, CAG, ECI, UPSC/SPSC, NCBC, ISC, SFC, SEC
  [98:0:1.1] Statutory | Act of Parliament; changed by amending that Act | NHRC, NCM, Zonal Councils, UGC, Press Council
  [98:0:1.2] Executive / extra-constitutional | Resolution / executive order; advisory | Planning Commission, NITI Aayog, NDC, Law Commission
  [98:0:1.3] Quasi-judicial | Statute; can summon, sanction; not a court | NHRC, SEBI, CIC, Press Council
[98:0:2] - **Extra-constitutional power/right:** power exercised though it has **no provision in the Constitution** ("not in accordance with" its text) – e.g. the Planning Commission, set up by Cabinet resolution, still exercised power over the Centre and the States.
[98:0:3] - **Most-asked fact:** the **Planning Commission** (resolution, **15 March 1950**) is **not** a constitutional body – the odd one out against **Finance Commission (Art. 280), Election Commission (Art. 324), UPSC / State PSCs (Art. 315), Inter-State Council (Art. 263)** (asked 8 times: APPSC G2 2003, 2008; NTR Univ.; UPPCS 2004, 2005, 2013, 2014).
[98:0:4] - **NITI Aayog** (Cabinet resolution, 1 Jan 2015) = **extra-constitutional**; the odd one among FC, ECI, ISC (UP Lower 2015) and among NCBC, CAG, FC (APPSC 2025).
[98:0:5] - **NITI Aayog, NDC and National Integration Council** = non-constitutional (executive); **Inter-State Council** = constitutional (Art. 263) → answer "A, B & C" (APSLPRB SI 2016).
[98:0:6] - **NDC, Planning Commission, Zonal Councils:** **none** is mentioned in the Constitution (UPSC 2013); Zonal Councils are statutory (States Reorganisation Act 1956, Sec. 15).
[98:0:7] - **Count question (APPSC G2 Mains 2025):** NITI Aayog ✗ (executive), NCM ✗ (statutory 1992), **Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities ✓ (Art. 350B)**, NHRC ✗ (statutory 1993) → **only 1** constitutional.
[98:0:8] - **ECI (Art. 324)** = the commission set up under a definite Article; **UGC, NHRC, CVC** are statutory (UPSC 2006).
[98:0:9] - **Constitution provides for** the Finance Commission, Election Commission **and** UPSC → "All the above".
[98:0:10] - **Inter-State Council:** source = the **Constitution** (Art. 263); set up by Presidential order on **28 May 1990** on Sarkaria advice – not a parliamentary law or Planning Commission recommendation.
[98:0:11] - **Inter-State Commerce Commission:** Art. 307 lets Parliament appoint an authority for Arts. 301-304; treated as constitutionally provided in exam keys.

=== NOTES 2:99 | Financial Regulators – Rbi, Sebi, Irdai, Pfrda, Ibbi, Ifsca – Mandate and Powers
## SUB 0 | IRDAI – Insurance Regulator and Insurance Reforms
[99:0:0] - **IRDAI** = statutory body under the **IRDA Act, 1999**; set up **April 2000**; apex regulator of **insurance and reinsurance** (not SEBI, RBI, GIC or DFHI). HQ **Hyderabad**.
[99:0:1] - Recommended by the **Malhotra Committee** (R.N. Malhotra; set up **1993**, reported **1994**) – private entry into insurance and an independent regulator. Not the Gadgil, Dandekar, Khusro or Rangarajan Committee.
[99:0:2] - **Section 114A, Insurance Act 1938** = IRDAI's regulation-making power.
[99:0:3] - **Feb 2025**: seven-member expert panel under **Dinesh Khara** (ex-SBI chairman) set up to review IRDAI regulations (not Shaktikanta Das, Debasish Panda or Urjit Patel).
[99:0:4] TABLE head: Committee | Subject
  [99:0:4.0] Malhotra (1993-94) | Insurance sector reforms – not civil services
  [99:0:4.1] Hota (2004) | Civil service reforms
  [99:0:4.2] Alagh (2001) | Civil services examination
  [99:0:4.3] Surinder Nath (2003) | Civil services – appraisal and postings
  [99:0:4.4] Dandekar / Khusro | Poverty line / agricultural credit
[99:0:5] # Insurance FDI Cap
[99:0:6] TABLE head: Year | Insurance FDI cap
  [99:0:6.0] 2000 | 26%
  [99:0:6.1] 2015 | 49%
  [99:0:6.2] 2021 | 74%
  [99:0:6.3] 2025-26 | 100% – Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025 (17 Dec 2025); new Sec. 16A of the IRDA Act = Policyholders' Education and Protection Fund
[99:0:7] - **Ind AS** = **Indian Accounting Standards** adopted by Indian companies (converged with IFRS); **Ind AS 117 and Ind AS 109** replace IGAAP for insurers.
[99:0:8] - **D-SIIs, FY 2025-26**: **LIC, GIC Re, New India Assurance**; HDFC Bank is a **D-SIB** (bank), not a D-SII.

## SUB 1 | SEBI and Market Regulation
[99:1:0] - **SEBI** set up **12 April 1988** by resolution (non-statutory); **statutory on 12 April 1992** (SEBI Act, 1992). Replaced the **Controller of Capital Issues**.
[99:1:1] - Regulates the **securities market, stock exchanges, listed companies, intermediaries** (brokers, merchant banks, VC funds) and **mutual funds**.
[99:1:2] - **Quasi-judicial** powers: investigates, summons, orders, penalties. Appeal: SEBI → **Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)** → **Supreme Court** (not NCLAT); IRDAI and PFRDA orders also go to SAT (since 2014).
[99:1:3] - **Forward Markets Commission** (commodities regulator, 1953) **merged into SEBI, Sep 2015** – commodity exchanges now under SEBI.
[99:1:4] - **World's first diamond futures** contracts began on India's **ICEX (Indian Commodity Exchange, Mumbai)** on **28 August 2017** – answer "**India**", not Belgium, South Africa or USA.
[99:1:5] - **Sep 2025**: **SWAGAT-FI** – single-window framework for trusted **FPIs and FVCIs**.
[99:1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy P1 - APPSC 04 p.32; Rocket Indian Economy p.14; app notes 2:99 (SEBI); ICEX diamond futures – app explanation, general knowledge (not checked)
## SUB 2 | IBBI, IBC, PFRDA and the Companies Act 2013
[99:2:0] - **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016**: President's assent **28 May 2016** (not September); **IBBI** established **1 Oct 2016** (Sec. 188).
[99:2:1] - **IBBI Board (Sec. 189)**: Chairperson + **3 ex officio** Central Government officers + **1 RBI nominee** + **5 other** members (at least 3 whole-time) – **not 13 members**.
[99:2:2] - **BIFR** (SICA 1985) dissolved **1 Dec 2016**; cases moved to **NCLT/NCLAT**.
[99:2:3] - **PFRDA**: pension funds and **NPS**; **NPS e-Shramik** for informal workers.
[99:2:4] - **Companies Act, 2013**: **CSR** (Sec. 135), **One Person Company** (new entrepreneurs), e-filing/**MCA21** (IT use); shift from government approval to **self-regulation with disclosure** – so "the Act **regulates** the corporate sector" is the statement keyed as not correct (CDS 2015).

## SUB 5 | FSDC and FSLRC
[99:5:0] - **FSDC** (Financial Stability and Development Council), **2010**: **non-statutory** apex body (executive order), **not an organ of NITI Aayog**; chaired by the **Union Finance Minister** (not RBI Governor); sub-committee chaired by the **RBI Governor**. Proposed by the **Raghuram Rajan Committee (2008)**.
[99:5:1] - Roles: financial stability, **inter-regulatory coordination**, **macro-prudential supervision**, financial literacy and inclusion – not fixing the repo rate.
[99:5:2] - **FSLRC**: **Justice B.N. Srikrishna**; set up 2011, report **2013** – **Indian Financial Code**; **Unified Financial Agency** (SEBI + FMC + IRDA + **PFRDA**); Financial Redress Agency; Public Debt Management Agency; SAT → Financial Sector Appellate Tribunal. No constitutional court for financial disputes.
[99:5:3] - **Economic Survey 2025-26**: RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA moving to **anticipatory regulation**.

## SUB 0 | Competition Commission of India
[100:0:0] - Statutory body under the **Competition Act, 2002**, as amended by the **Competition (Amendment) Act, 2007**; **Ministry of Corporate Affairs**.
[100:0:1] - The Act **replaced the MRTP Act, 1969** and its **MRTP Commission** – shift from curbing monopolies to **promoting competition**.
[100:0:2] - **Established:** notified **14 October 2003**, but legal challenges delayed it; **fully constituted / functional in March 2009** (MCA: "established in March 2009").
[100:0:3] - **Function:** to **ensure fair competition in markets**, prevent practices with adverse effect on competition, protect consumers – not registering competitors or monitoring capital markets.
[100:0:4] - **Composition:** **Chairperson + 2 to 6 other members** appointed by the **Central Government** (original 2002 Act: up to 10) – so "chairperson and five members" is wrong.
[100:0:5] TABLE head: CCI area | What it does
  [100:0:5.0] Anti-competitive agreements | Cartels, bid-rigging
  [100:0:5.1] Abuse of dominant position | e.g. WhatsApp 2021 privacy policy – Meta fined ₹213.14 crore
  [100:0:5.2] Combinations | Mergers and acquisitions; ₹2,000 crore deal-value threshold for start-up "killer acquisitions"
[100:0:6] - Appeals: **NCLAT from 26 May 2017** (earlier **COMPAT**) → Supreme Court. CCI's **Digital Markets Division**; proposed **Digital Competition Bill** for **Systemically Significant Digital Enterprises**.
[100:0:7] - Market forms: many sellers of differentiated brands (e.g. **toothpaste**) = **monopolistic competition**; identical product = perfect competition.
