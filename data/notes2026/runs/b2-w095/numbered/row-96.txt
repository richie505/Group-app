## SUB 0 | Centrally Sponsored Schemes (2015) and Central Control Loosened
[0:0] - **1991 reforms loosened the Centre's control over the States** in **industrial licensing, tariffs and foreign investment**: central licensing largely ended, tariffs fell and FDI was opened, so States began competing for investment (APPSC Polytechnic Lecturers 2011). Planning-era control over State plans was not the target.
[0:1] - **CSS rationalised** by the **NITI Aayog Sub-Group of Chief Ministers (2015)**.
[0:2] TABLE head: Group | Detail
  [0:2.0] Core of the core | Social protection and social inclusion schemes – MGNREGA, NSAP, umbrella schemes for SCs, STs, minorities; first charge on funds
  [0:2.1] Core | Mostly 60:40; 90:10 for NE and Himalayan states
  [0:2.2] Optional | States' choice; 50:50 (80:20 for NE and Himalayan states)
[0:3] - **PMMVY, ICDS** now **60:40**. CSS grew **26.5% a year** since 2016-17; state plan allocations fell nearly **30%**.
[0:4] > APPSC G1 2017: "core of the core" = social protection and social inclusion schemes (not infrastructure, Digital India or "National Agenda" schemes in general).

## SUB 3 | Fiscal Rules, Borrowing and GST
[3:0] - **FRBM Act 2003** (amended 2018): fiscal deficit **≤3%** of GDP; general government debt **60%**, Central debt **40%** of GDP by 2025.
[3:1] - Four statements in Parliament: Medium-term Fiscal Policy, Fiscal Policy Strategy, Macro-economic Framework, Medium-term Expenditure Framework.
[3:2] - **Art. 293(3):** a state indebted to the Centre needs Union consent to borrow.
[3:3] - **State of Kerala v. Union (2024):** **Art. 131** suit against the **Net Borrowing Ceiling** (counts off-budget borrowings).
[3:4] - **16th FC:** state deficit cap **3% of GSDP**; end off-budget borrowing; rationalise subsidies.
[3:5] - **Economic Survey 2025-26:** general government debt down **7.1 points** since 2020; Centre steers state capex through **SASCI**.
[3:6] # GST and States' fiscal autonomy
[3:7] - **GST Council (Art. 279A, 101st CAA 2016):** Centre **one-third** vote, all States **two-thirds**; decisions by **three-fourths** weighted majority.
[3:8] - **Compensation cess:** 5-year compensation ended **June 2022**; extended to repay COVID-era loans; discontinued for most goods from **22 Sept 2025**.
[3:9] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:96 (sub 2, 3); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC Part 4 p.27 (GST, 101st Amendment)
