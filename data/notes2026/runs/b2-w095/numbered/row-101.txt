## SUB 1 | Central Administrative Tribunal; Articles 323A and 323B
[1:0] - **Part XIVA** (Arts. **323A, 323B**) inserted by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**, in force **1 March 1977**.
[1:1] TABLE head: Feature | Article 323A | Article 323B
  [1:1.0] Subject | Public service matters | Taxation, foreign exchange, industrial disputes, land reforms, urban ceiling, elections, foodstuffs, rent
  [1:1.1] Who creates | Parliament only | Parliament and State Legislatures
[1:2] - **CAT**: set up under the **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985** (Art. 323A); original jurisdiction over **recruitment and all service matters** of Central government employees (not just recruitment, promotion or discipline).
[1:3] - **L. Chandra Kumar (1997)**: High Court review under **Arts. 226/227** = basic structure → CAT orders go first to a **High Court Division Bench**, then SC.
[1:4] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Indian Polity 8th ed. part 2 p.73-74 (CAT); app notes 2:101 (Part XIVA, CAT)
## SUB 2 | NCLT, NCLAT, SAT and Other Appellate Tribunals
[2:0] - **NCLT** (Sec. **408**) and **NCLAT** (Sec. **410**) of the **Companies Act, 2013**; both from **1 June 2016**. NCLT = adjudicating authority under the IBC.
[2:1] - Appeal NCLT → NCLAT: **30 days** under IBC Sec. 61; **45 days** under the Companies Act (Sec. 421).
[2:2] TABLE head: Tribunal | Hears appeals from | Further
  [2:2.0] NCLAT | NCLT, IBBI, CCI (from 26 May 2017), NFRA | SC
  [2:2.1] SAT (SEBI Act, 1992) | SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA | SC on questions of law only
  [2:2.2] TDSAT (May 2000) | TRAI, IT Act officers, DPBI | SC
  [2:2.3] APTEL (24 Aug 2004) | CERC, SERCs, PNGRB | SC
[2:3] - SAT = **Securities Appellate Tribunal**; it does not hear CCI appeals. NCLAT does not hear SEBI appeals.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy P1 - APPSC 01 p.54 (NCLAT appeals from NCLT, IBBI, CCI); app notes 2:101 (NCLT, SAT)
## SUB 3 | Consumer Commissions
[3:0] - Three-tier quasi-judicial bodies under the **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**; limits revised by the **Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction) Rules, 2021**.
[3:1] TABLE head: Commission | Limit since 2021
  [3:1.0] District | Up to ₹50 lakh
  [3:1.1] State | Above ₹50 lakh to ₹2 crore
  [3:1.2] National (NCDRC) | Above ₹2 crore
[3:2] - Quasi-judicial bodies are not bound by the CPC, follow **natural justice**, and their decisions are appealable.
[3:3] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:101 (consumer commissions)
## SUB 4 | Tribunals Reforms Act 2021 and Madras Bar Association
[4:0] - **Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021** dissolved several appellate bodies: **Cinematograph Act** appellate tribunal → **High Court**; Airport Appellate Tribunal; Authority for Advance Rulings (Customs); **IPAB** (Trade Marks, Copyright, Patents) → High Court / Commercial Court. The **NGT was not dissolved**.
[4:1] - **19 Nov 2025, Madras Bar Association v. Union of India** (CJI **B.R. Gavai**): key provisions struck down; Centre to set up a **National Tribunals Commission within four months**.
[4:2] - Capacity gaps: NGT has no IT division; Railway Claims Tribunal legal aid at only 9 of 23 benches.
[4:3] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:101 (Tribunals Reforms Act, Madras Bar Association)
