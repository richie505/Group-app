=== Tenth Schedule – 52nd and 91st Amendments, Dates and Firsts
# The 52nd Amendment, 1985
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Law | **52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985** – added the **Tenth Schedule** (anti-defection law); amended **Arts 101, 102, 190 and 191** |
| Bill | Passed by Parliament **30-31 January 1985**; **Presidential assent 15 February 1985** (the date most keys give as "Bill passed") |
| In force | **1 March 1985** |
| Aim | Curb defections "induced by the **lure of office**", ensure party discipline and stable governments; ban **floor crossing** by members elected on a party ticket |
| First | **First constitutional recognition of political parties** (1985) – the term "political party" appears in the Tenth Schedule |
- **Asked as:** "52nd Amendment relates to" → **Defection**; "Tenth Schedule deals with" → **anti-defection / defections** (not official languages = Part XVII/8th Sch., not land reforms = 9th, not Panchayati Raj = 11th); "anti-defection enacted in" → **1985**; "which amendment first introduced anti-defection" → **52nd** (not 44th, 54th, 61st, 86th, 92nd, nor the Representation of the People Act).
- **Applies to:** members of **both Houses of Parliament and the State Legislatures** (MPs and MLAs, incl. **MLCs**) – party, **independent** and **nominated** members.
- **Not applicable to the presidential election:** the Schedule covers voting **in the House**; no binding whip operates in the electoral college.
# Before 1985
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1967 | **"Aaya Ram Gaya Ram"** (Gaya Lal, **Haryana**); **Y.B. Chavan Committee** on defections (1967-69) |
| 1973, 1978 | Anti-defection Bills lapsed |
| 1979 | **Jammu and Kashmir** – first State with its own anti-defection law |
| 1984 | AP: **Nadendla Bhaskara Rao** toppled **N.T. Rama Rao**'s government; NTR restored after a month |
- **⚠ Trap:** the **old Tenth Schedule** (Sikkim's association with India) was added by the **35th Amendment (1974)** and omitted by the **36th (1975)** – not the anti-defection schedule.
# 91st Amendment, 2003
| Change | Provision |
|---|---|
| **One-third split exception deleted** | Para 3 omitted |
| Union ministry ≤ **15% of Lok Sabha** strength | Art. 75(1A) |
| State ministry ≤ **15% of Assembly**, minimum **12** | Art. 164(1A) |
| Disqualified defector barred from **Minister** / remunerative political post until re-elected | Arts **75(1B), 164(1B), 361B** (361B = remunerative political post) |
- 91st Amendment statement test: capped the Union and State Councils of Ministers ✓, deleted the split ✓; it did **not** touch Art. 105 privileges.
> Exam angle: "52nd / 1985 / Tenth Schedule / J&K 1979 / 15 Feb (assent) / 1 March 1985 (in force)" – one cluster asked ~16 times.

=== Grounds of Disqualification, Merger and Split
| Member | Disqualified if | Exception / allowed |
|---|---|---|
| Party member | **Voluntarily gives up** party membership (inferred from conduct); **votes or abstains against the whip** without prior permission (**Para 2(1)(b)**) | Prior permission, or the party **condones within 15 days** |
| Independent | **Joins any political party** after election | May become a Minister without joining a party |
| Nominated | Joins a party **after six months** of taking his seat | May join **within six months**; not joining is no ground |
| Presiding officer | **Joins another party** (Para 5) | May give up party membership on election to the Chair and **rejoin after demitting office** – both are exceptions |
- **Giving up membership ≠ resigning:** conduct such as campaigning for a rival party is enough (Ravi S. Naik 1994).
- **Expelled member:** does **not lose his seat**; he still belongs to the party for Tenth Schedule purposes (G. Viswanathan 1996) and sits as unattached; joining another party then = giving up membership.
- **Merger (Para 4, in force):** no disqualification when the **original party merges** with another and **not less than two-thirds** of its legislature party agree.
- **Split (Para 3, one-third):** **deleted by the 91st Amendment (2003)** – a one-third group that breaks away is now disqualified.
  - Number test: party of **100** MLAs – no break-away faction is protected now; a merger needs **67**. **34** (one-third) was the pre-2003 answer.
- **Statements commonly marked wrong:** "one-third split protects" ✗; "disqualified defector can become a Minister" ✗; "nominated member who fails to join a party in six months is disqualified" ✗; "expelled member loses his seat" ✗; "Speaker not disqualified if he joins another party" ✗.
- **Criticism – en-bloc defections:** it "banned only **retail** defections and legalised **wholesale** defections" – **en-bloc (bulk) defection** via merger is not covered; individual defection, defections in spells and votes against the whip are.
- **Main criticism:** it stifles intra-party **dissent** and members' free expression, yet has not stopped mass defections (the two-thirds merger route can **defend mass defection**).
> Exam angle: "merger fraction" = **two-thirds** (asked ×3); "not covered by the law" = **en-bloc defections**; UPSC 2022 – nominated member six-month statement ✗, no time-frame ✓.

=== Parliamentary Privileges – Articles 105, 194 and 122
| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| **105 / 194** | Powers, privileges and immunities of each House, its members and committees – Parliament / State Legislatures; free speech (105(1)) and immunity for anything said or voted (105(2)) |
| **122 / 212** | Courts cannot inquire into proceedings for **irregularity of procedure** |
- **Not codified** in a single statute; rest on the Constitution, conventions (originally the House of Commons), rules, statutes and court rulings.
- **Notice:** Lok Sabha **Rule 222** / Rajya Sabha **Rule 187** – a member raises breach of privilege or contempt by a minister, member or outsider.
- **Procedure:** Presiding Officer consents → House or **Privileges Committee** examines → the **House decides**. Committee: LS **15** members nominated by the Speaker; RS **10**, usually chaired by the **Deputy Chairman**.
| Collective (of each House) | Individual (of members) |
|---|---|
| Publish its debates and proceedings; **restrain others** from publishing | **Freedom of speech** in the House and committees |
| **Exclude strangers**; hold secret sittings | **Freedom from arrest in civil cases** – during a session and **40 days** before and after; **not** in criminal or preventive detention cases |
| Make its own rules; regulate internal affairs | **Exemption from jury service**; may refuse to **attend as a witness** while the House sits |
| Receive **immediate information of a member's arrest**, detention, conviction or release | |
| **Punish members and outsiders** for breach or contempt | |
| No arrest or legal process within the **precincts** without the presiding officer's permission | |
- **Punishments:** **reprimand, admonition, imprisonment** (only till the session ends), and for members **suspension or expulsion** (seat declared vacant). **No fine**; "six months' imprisonment" ✗.
- **Contempt is wider** than breach: every breach is contempt, but not every contempt (e.g. disobeying a House order) is a breach of a specific privilege.
- "Privileges are only collective" ✗ / "only individual" ✗ – they are **both**.
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| Raja Ram Pal (2007) | Expulsion valid; privilege decisions reviewable only for **gross illegality or unconstitutionality** |
| **Sita Soren (2024)** | 7 judges: **no immunity for bribery** to speak or vote; overruled **P.V. Narasimha Rao (1998)** |
> Exam angle: "courts barred from inquiring" = **Art. 122**; privileges article = **105**; "wrong statement" = arrest immunity "whether in session or not" ✗; punishment = **expulsion**, not fine.

=== Speaker as Deciding Authority and Key Cases
| Basis | Defection | General disqualification |
|---|---|---|
| Provisions | Tenth Schedule; **Arts 102(2), 191(2)** | Arts 102(1), 103 / **191(1)**, 192 |
| Decided by | **Speaker / Chairman** acting as a **tribunal** (Para 6) | **President / Governor** |
| ECI | **Not consulted** | Opinion **binding** (for MPs and State legislators alike) |
- **Final decision** on a Lok Sabha member's defection: the **Speaker** – not the President, Vice-President or ECI. "President decides on the Council of Ministers' advice" ✗ (UPSC 2025).
- **Speaker's own disqualification:** decided by **such member of the House as the House may elect** (Para 6(1)).
- **Petition needed:** the presiding officer acts on a petition by a member – **no suo motu** case (Defection Rules 1985).
- **No time limit** in the Schedule; Speakers stay members of their parties – hence delays.
- **Para 7** (bar on courts) **struck down** in Kihoto: it needed State **ratification under Art. 368(2)**. **Judicial review only after** the decision, on limited grounds – mala fides, perversity, natural justice, constitutional mandate.
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **Kihoto Hollohan (1992)** | Tenth Schedule **valid** (no violation of free speech or basic structure); Para 7 struck down; **3:2** |
| Ravi S. Naik (1994) | Giving up membership ≠ resigning; inferred from conduct |
| G. Viswanathan (1996) | Expelled member **still belongs** to the party |
| Karnataka (2019) | Upheld disqualification of **17** rebel MLAs; Speaker **cannot bar them from contesting** |
| **Keisham Meghachandra (Jan 2020)** | Decide petitions ordinarily within **three months**; Parliament to consider a **permanent independent tribunal** |
| **Subhash Desai (May 2023)** | The **political party** (not the legislature party) appoints the whip and leader; legislators alone cannot bring a "**deemed merger**" |
- **Telangana (BRS) matter:** SC gave the Speaker a final **three weeks** for the last **2 of 10** BRS petitions (Feb 2026, app notes).
- **Jul 2026 (app notes):** Bench of **P.S. Narasimha** and **Alok Aradhe** said gaps in Para 4 are "**matters for Parliament**".
> Exam angle: "who decides" (×3) = **Speaker**; Speaker's own case = **member elected by the House**; Kihoto = validity + review; Keisham = 3 months; Desai = party appoints whip.

=== Working of the Law, AP Angle and Reform Proposals
- **Record:** the law has not stopped defections; its main effect has been to make **presiding officers** the deciders of defection cases (often criticised for partisan delay) – APPSC ASO 2017 key.
- **Resort politics:** legislators sequestered before a floor test to prevent poaching.
- **Resignation route:** MLAs **resign**, lowering the majority mark, and re-contest on the new party's ticket (**Karnataka 2019, MP 2020**).
- **AP 2014-19:** **23 YSRCP MLAs** moved to the TDP; the petitions were **never decided**.
- **June 2019:** **4 of 6 TDP** Rajya Sabha MPs "merged" with the BJP under **Para 4**; the RS Chairman accepted it.
- **Apr 2026 (app notes):** **7 of 10 AAP** Rajya Sabha MPs claimed a merger with the BJP – cited as misuse of the merger exception.
| Body | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990)** | Disqualify only for **voluntarily giving up membership** or defying the whip on a **confidence motion**; decision by President/Governor on EC advice |
| **Law Commission 170th Report (1999)** | Delete **both split and merger** exceptions; treat **pre-poll alliances** as parties |
| **NCRWC (2002)** | Defectors barred from office; **defector's vote invalid** |
| Election Commission | President/Governor to decide on the EC's binding advice |
| **Manish Tewari Bill (2010)** | Limit the law to **confidence and money votes** |
- Chronology of proposals: **Goswami (1990) → Law Commission 170th (1999) → NCRWC (2002) → Tewari Bill (2010)**.
- Limiting the whip to votes that threaten the government lets members **dissent freely** on other matters.
- **Newer:** **fixed timelines** for Speakers and an **independent tribunal** (SC, Keisham 2020).
