=== EVM, VVPAT, NOTA and the Chronology of Reforms
# EVM – firsts and law
| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **19 May 1982** | First EVM use – **Parur (Kerala)** Assembly seat; challenged in **A.C. Jose v Sivan Pillai** |
| **1989** | **Section 61A, RPA 1951** – statutory backing to EVMs (not Art. 328/329) |
| **1998** | Experimental use in selected Assembly seats of **Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Delhi** – **not Gujarat** |
| **1999** | **Goa** – first **entire State** Assembly election on EVMs |
| **2004** | 14th Lok Sabha – first national election held **entirely on EVMs** |
| **4 Sep 2013** | **VVPAT** first used – **Noksen** (Nagaland) Assembly by-election |
- **Makers:** **BEL** (Bengaluru) and **ECIL** (**Hyderabad**).
- **M3** (third-generation) EVM and **VVPAT M3** are in use: up to **2,000 votes**; one control unit takes **24 ballot units = 384 candidates incl. NOTA**; **tamper detection and self-diagnostics**. **M2**: 4 ballot units, **64** candidates.
# VVPAT
- The printed slip (serial no., name, symbol) is visible through a window for **7 seconds** (not 30), then is cut and drops into a **sealed box** – it is **never handed to the voter**; it gives the voter instant feedback that the vote went to the chosen candidate.
- **Subramanian Swamy v ECI (2013):** VVPAT "an indispensable requirement of free and fair elections", to be brought in **in phases**.
- **N. Chandrababu Naidu (2019):** VVPAT-EVM tally raised from **1 to 5 polling stations** per Assembly segment.
- **ADR v ECI (April 2024):** no 100% slip count and **no return to paper ballots**; **symbol-loading units sealed 45 days**; 2nd/3rd-placed candidates may seek a check of **5% of EVMs** within 7 days.
- Slips are counted wherever EVM data and **Form 17C** do not match. Counting order: **postal ballots → penultimate EVM round → final round**.
- **Remote EVM (RVM)** prototype (Jan 2023): lets **domestic migrants** vote for up to **72 constituencies** from one remote polling station. **Totaliser** (Law Commission 255th): pools several machines to **hide booth-wise trends**.
# NOTA and other reforms
- **NOTA** = a voter's choice to **reject all the contesting candidates** while keeping the ballot secret – **PUCL v UoI, 27 Sep 2013** (Art. 19(1)(a)); introduced on EVMs in **2013** (Assembly polls of **Chhattisgarh, Mizoram, Rajasthan, MP, Delhi**); first Lok Sabha use **2014**. Even if NOTA tops the poll, the candidate with the most votes wins.
| Year | Reform |
|---|---|
| 1988-89 | 61st Amendment – voting age 21 → 18 |
| **1993** | **EPIC** (photo identity cards) under CEC **T.N. Seshan** |
| 2010 | Overseas (NRI) electors |
| 2021 | **Election Laws (Amendment) Act** – **Aadhaar-roll linking**, four qualifying dates, "spouse" for "wife", requisition of premises |
| 2023 | **CEC and ECs Act** – selection panel = **PM, a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM, Leader of Opposition**; **CJI dropped** |
| 2025-26 | ECINET; max **1,200 electors per polling station**; SIR of rolls (AP: **Alluri Sitharama Raju** district – 100% digitisation of SIR forms, Jul 2026); ECI–SEC declaration of **26 Feb 2026** under CEC **Gyanesh Kumar** |
- **Chronology:** **EVM (1982) → EPIC (1993) → VVPAT (4 Sep 2013) → NOTA (27 Sep 2013)**; and Sec. 61A (1989) → EPIC (1993) → two-seat cap (1996) → Aadhaar linking (2021).

=== Electoral Bonds and Party Funding
- **Electoral Bond Scheme:** announced in Budget 2017 (**Finance Act, 2017**), notified **2 January 2018**; stated aim – **clean money and transparency** in political funding.
- **Instrument:** a **bearer** banking instrument like a promissory note, without the buyer's or payee's name; sold **only by SBI** branches; denominations **₹1,000, ₹10,000, ₹1 lakh, ₹10 lakh, ₹1 crore**; valid **15 calendar days**; credited to the party's account **the same day**.
- **Buyer:** a **citizen of India** or a body **incorporated or established in India** (alone or jointly).
- **Recipient:** only a party registered under **Sec. 29A, RPA 1951** that secured **not less than 1%** (not 2%) of the votes in the last Lok Sabha or Assembly election; encashed only through its bank account with SBI.
- **ADR v Union of India, 15 Feb 2024:** a **five-judge bench, unanimously, struck the scheme down as unconstitutional** – it violated voters' **right to information, Art. 19(1)(a)**; also struck down the **removal of the corporate donation cap** (Companies Act); **SBI** had to give donor data to the **ECI**. (2024 = bonds struck down, not introduced.)
- **Other funding rules:** anonymous cash donation cap cut **₹20,000 → ₹2,000** (Budget 2017); donations above ₹2,000 only by cheque/bank transfer; parties report contributions above **₹20,000** to the ECI; tax exemption – now **Sec. 12, Income-tax Act 2025** (earlier Sec. 13A, 1961 Act); 2017 removed the **7.5% of net profit** cap on corporate donations.
- **ADR:** about **60% (₹19,083 crore)** of party funds in 2004-23 came from undisclosed sources.
> Asked as: SC ruling (struck down – APPSC 2024-25); statements on buyer, value, 15-day life, 29A party, 1% threshold (AP 2023: all correct; RAS 2018: 2% statement false).

=== Representation of the People Act, 1951 – Key Sections
| Section | Provision |
|---|---|
| 8 | Conviction with **2+ years'** jail → disqualified, plus 6 years after release; **8(4)** struck down (Lily Thomas, 2013) |
| 29A | Registration of parties with the ECI |
| **33(7)** | Contest from **at most two** constituencies (added **1996**) |
| 33A | Disclosure of criminal antecedents |
| 34 | Security deposit: **₹25,000** Lok Sabha, **₹10,000** Assembly; SC/ST half (raised in 2009) |
| **61A** | Use of **EVMs** (inserted 1989) |
| 70 | Vacate one of two seats within **14 days** |
| 77 / 78 | Keep an account of election expenses / file it with the DEO within **30 days** |
| 81 | **Election petition** in the **High Court** within **45 days** |
| **123** | **Corrupt practices** – bribery, undue influence, appeal on grounds of **religion, race, caste, community or language** (123(3)) |
| 126 / **126A** | Campaign silence 48 hours / **ban on exit polls** |
| 135B / 135C | Paid holiday on polling day / **liquor ban** 48 hours before the poll |
| 151A | By-election within **six months** |
- **Deposit forfeited** if a candidate fails to secure **more than one-sixth (1/6) of the valid votes polled**; so if even the **winner** loses his deposit, **a very large number of candidates** contested and votes were split.
- **Abhiram Singh v C.D. Commachen (Jan 2017):** a 7-judge bench read **Sec. 123(3)** widely – votes cannot be sought in the name of religion, race, caste, community or language (of candidate or voter).
- **Two-seat cap:** in **1991 Devi Lal** contested **three** Lok Sabha seats; the **RP (Amendment) Act, 1996** capped it at **two** (Sec. 33(7)). No rule makes a party or candidate bear by-poll costs – that is only an ECI/Law Commission ("one candidate, one seat") proposal.
- **1996 Act (Goswami):** two-seat cap; higher deposits; 10 proposers for independents/unrecognised-party candidates; poll **not countermanded** on a candidate's death (a recognised party may name a substitute within 7 days); paid holiday; liquor ban; by-polls in 6 months; 6-year bar for offences under the **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971**.
- **2003:** Rajya Sabha domicile requirement removed (any elector in India; upheld in **Kuldip Nayar, 2006**) and **open ballot** introduced.
- **1990s:** the **most Lok Sabha and Assembly elections** were held in the **1990s** (Lok Sabha 1991, 1996, 1998, 1999 – frequent changes of government and hung Houses).
> Asked as: deposit (1/6 of valid votes – twice); section interpreted in the religion/caste ruling (123); UPSC 2021 statements (Devi Lal 1991 true; "no law" and "party bears by-poll cost" false); EVM provision (61A).

=== Election Expenditure Limits and Money Power
| Category | Lok Sabha | Assembly |
|---|---|---|
| Larger States/UTs (2022) | **₹95 lakh** (₹70 lakh from 2014; **₹40 lakh** from Feb 2011; ₹25 lakh before) | **₹40 lakh** (earlier ₹28 lakh; ₹16 lakh in 2011) |
| Smaller States/UTs (2022) | **₹75 lakh** (earlier ₹54 lakh) | **₹28 lakh** (earlier ₹20 lakh) |
- **Basis:** **Rule 90, Conduct of Election Rules, 1961**; raised by the **Conduct of Elections (Amendment) Rules, 2022**. **₹1 crore was never the limit.**
- The ceiling applies to **candidates only**, not parties (**Sec. 77** loophole) – there is **no limit on expenditure by political parties**; star campaigners' travel is excluded. Failure to file accounts → ECI can **disqualify for 3 years**.
- **RUPPs:** over **2,800**, only about 750 contested in 2024; the ECI delisted **808** non-compliant ones.

=== State Funding of Elections
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Partial** | The State meets some costs, in cash or **in kind** (airtime, fuel, rallies) |
| **Full** | The State bears all legitimate costs; **private donations banned** |
- **Recommended by:** **Indrajit Gupta (1998)** – partial, in kind, to recognised parties; **Dinesh Goswami (1990)** – government funding of Parliamentary polls; **2nd ARC** – partial.
- **Law Commission 170th Report:** full State funding desirable **only if parties are barred** from taking funds from other sources.
- **Practised in:** Germany, Sweden, Japan, Canada.
- **For:** curbs black money and quid-pro-quo; **level field for resource-poor candidates (Art. 14)**. **Against:** fiscal burden; does not stop off-book cash.
- **State of Karnataka v Prathik Parasrampuria (2026):** eliminating black money from elections is a core duty of the **Election Commission of India**. Idea (Jan 2026): a pooled **National Election Fund**.

=== Criminalisation of Politics – Cases and Steps
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| ADR v UoI | 2002 | Candidates disclose **criminal cases, assets, liabilities, education** |
| PUCL v UoI | 2003 | Voter's right to know (Art. 19(1)(a)); struck down **Sec. 33B** |
| **Lily Thomas v UoI** | July 2013 | Struck down **Sec. 8(4)**; a convicted MP/MLA is **disqualified immediately**, no 3-month appeal shield |
| Krishnamurthy v Sivakumar | 2015 | **Hiding serious cases vitiates the nomination** |
| Public Interest Foundation | 2018 | 5 judges; did **not** disqualify on framing of charges – **left it to Parliament**; parties must publish records |
- **Patna High Court (2004):** jailed persons (no right to vote) cannot contest; SC upheld (2013); Parliament amended the RPA (2013) to let them contest.
- **Tenth Schedule** disqualification is decided by the **Speaker/Chairman**, subject to **judicial review** (Kihoto Hollohan, 1992).
- **ECI rule:** parties fielding candidates with criminal records must publish the records and the **reasons for selecting** them.
- **Data:** **31%** of Lok Sabha MPs elected in 2024 face serious criminal charges (ADR; 14% in 2009); **12 special courts** for MP/MLA cases; "**bahubali culture**" = **muscle power**.

=== One Nation One Election – Simultaneous Polls
- Lok Sabha and Assembly polls were held together **1951-52 to 1967**; backed by the Law Commission **170th (1999)** and its 2018 draft report.
- **High-Level Committee** (set up **2 Sep 2023**), chair **Ram Nath Kovind**; members include **Amit Shah, Ghulam Nabi Azad**, N.K. Singh, Subhash C. Kashyap, Harish Salve, Sanjay Kothari. Lok Sabha + Assemblies first, **local bodies within 100 days**; safeguard idea – the **German constructive vote of no-confidence**.
- **Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024** (Dec 2024, referred to a **Joint Parliamentary Committee**): inserts **Art. 82A** and amends **Art. 83** (duration of Parliament), **Art. 172** (duration of State Legislatures) and **Art. 327** – not Art. 246 or 280.
- **Art. 356** (President's Rule) is also named in the debate (Law Commission 2018 draft listed 83, 85, 172, 174, 356) because a mid-term dissolution breaks the cycle.
> Asked as: which Article needs amendment (83 and 172 – the CGPSC 2024 printed key gave 356).
