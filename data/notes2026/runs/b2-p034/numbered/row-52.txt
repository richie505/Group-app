## SUB 0 | National Emergency (Article 352) – Grounds, Proclamation, Approval
[0:0] # Grounds
[0:1] - Proclaimed by the **President** when the security of India or any part is threatened by **war, external aggression or armed rebellion** – also on **imminent danger**; for the whole of India or a part.
[0:2] - **44th Amendment, 1978** substituted "**armed rebellion**" for "**internal disturbance**" – internal disturbance / threat to internal peace is **no longer a ground**.
[0:3] - War / external aggression = "**External Emergency**"; armed rebellion = "**Internal Emergency**".
[0:4] # Proclamation
[0:5] - Only when the **Union Cabinet's decision is communicated in writing** – **Art. 352(3)**, inserted by the **44th Amendment** – not on the PM's or the Home Minister's advice alone, not suo motu, not on the CJI's advice.
[0:6] - The word "**Cabinet**" (mantrimandal) appears **only once** in the Constitution – in **Art. 352(3)**.
[0:7] - **Judicial review:** the 38th Amendment (1975) made the President's satisfaction **non-justiciable**; the **44th** removed that bar and **Minerva Mills (1980)** held a proclamation **reviewable** (mala fide / irrelevant grounds) – so "it cannot be challenged in any court" is **wrong**.
[0:8] # Approval, duration, revocation
[0:9] TABLE head: Point | Rule
  [0:9.0] Approval | Both Houses within one month (two months till 1978)
  [0:9.1] Majority | Special majority – 2/3 present and voting + majority of total membership, in each House separately (no joint sitting)
  [0:9.2] Duration | 6 months at a time, extendable indefinitely
  [0:9.3] Lok Sabha dissolved | Survives 30 days from the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha if Rajya Sabha has approved
  [0:9.4] Revocation | President any time; must revoke if Lok Sabha disapproves by simple majority
  [0:9.5] Special sitting | 1/10 of total Lok Sabha members give written notice to the Speaker (President if not in session) → sitting within 14 days

## SUB 1 | President's Rule (Article 356) – State or Constitutional Emergency
[1:0] # Grounds and approval
[1:1] - Imposed when the President is satisfied, **on the Governor's report or otherwise**, that the government of a State **cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution** (Art. 356) or the State **fails to comply with Union directions** (Art. **365**).
[1:2] - Called "**State Emergency**" or "**Constitutional Emergency**" – it leads to **President's Rule / Governor's Rule**, not suspension of freedoms, salary cuts or mid-term polls.
[1:3] - **No consent** of the State Legislature; the Governor reports when **he is convinced** the government cannot run per the Constitution.
[1:4] TABLE head: Point | Rule
  [1:4.0] Approval | Both Houses within two months, simple majority
  [1:4.1] Each approval | 6 months
  [1:4.2] Maximum | 3 years
  [1:4.3] Beyond 1 year | Only if a National Emergency is in operation AND the Election Commission certifies elections cannot be held (Art. 356(5))
  [1:4.4] Revocation | President any time – no parliamentary approval
[1:5] # Consequences
[1:6] TABLE head: Organ | Effect
  [1:6.0] Executive | President assumes State functions; Council of Ministers dismissed (necessarily); Governor administers with advisers
  [1:6.1] Legislature | Assembly suspended or dissolved (not necessarily dissolved); Parliament makes laws on State List matters for the State, or delegates to the President (President's Acts)
  [1:6.2] Laws | Laws made during PR continue after it ends until altered by the State Legislature
  [1:6.3] High Court | Unaffected
  [1:6.4] Local bodies | Not dissolved
[1:7] # Proper and improper use (Sarkaria → Bommai)
[1:8] - **Proper:** hung assembly; no party able to form a ministry; **constitutional direction of the Centre disregarded**; internal subversion; physical breakdown.
[1:9] - **Improper:** loss of majority without probing an alternative; no floor test; Lok Sabha defeat of the ruling party; **maladministration or corruption** of the ministry; intra-party problems.
[1:10] # Misuse debate
[1:11] - Used **more than 125 times**, often arbitrarily for political reasons – hence among the **most controversial** provisions.
[1:12] - **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** hoped it would remain a "**dead letter**", used only as a **measure of last resort**.
[1:13] - **AP High Court (Dec 2020)** asked whether it could record a "breakdown of constitutional machinery" – **Art. 356**.
[1:14] > Asked as: Article (356 – 6 PYQs); maximum period (3 years); approval period (2 months); extension beyond 1 year (EC certificate + Emergency); consequences (statements); who reports (Governor); Ambedkar's "dead letter".

## SUB 2 | Scheme of Emergency Provisions – Three Emergencies Compared
[2:0] - **Part XVIII**, Arts **352-360**; source: **Government of India Act, 1935** (also the ordinance power, Arts 123/213); suspension of FRs from the **Weimar Constitution** (Germany).
[2:1] - The Constitution provides **three** emergencies; it has **no "President's Rule at Union level"**, though it does provide martial law (Art. 34) and special provisions for States (Art. 371 series).
[2:2] TABLE head: Feature | National (352) | President's Rule (356) | Financial (360)
  [2:2.0] Ground | War, external aggression, armed rebellion | Failure of constitutional machinery in a State | Threat to financial stability or credit
  [2:2.1] Approval within | 1 month | 2 months | 2 months
  [2:2.2] Majority | Special | Simple | Simple
  [2:2.3] Duration | 6 months, indefinite | 6 months, max 3 years | No maximum
  [2:2.4] Effect on FRs | Yes (358/359) | None | None
  [2:2.5] Used | 3 times (1962, 1971, 1975) | 125+ times; first Punjab 1951 | Never
[2:3] - Federal statement + reason "Art. 352 deals with failure of constitutional machinery" → the reason is **false** (that is Art. 356).
[2:4] > Asked as: Articles 352-360; how many types (three); which emergency for which ground (SSC); what the Constitution does not provide.

## SUB 3 | Effects of National Emergency – Fundamental Rights, Terms, State List
[3:0] # Article 358 vs Article 359
[3:1] TABLE head: Basis | Article 358 | Article 359
  [3:1.0] What | Suspends the six freedoms of Art. 19 | Suspends the right to move courts for FRs named in the order
  [3:1.1] How | Automatic | Needs a Presidential Order
  [3:1.2] Ground | Only war or external aggression | Any National Emergency
  [3:1.3] Limit | Only Emergency-related laws protected | Arts 20 and 21 can never be suspended (44th Amendment)
[3:2] - FRs can be suspended **only during a National Emergency**, by the **President** – not by Parliament's law, a government notification, the PM or the Supreme Court.
[3:3] # Centre-State and terms
[3:4] - **Art. 250:** **Parliament** (not a minister) makes laws on **State List** subjects for any part of India; they lapse **6 months** after the Emergency.
[3:5] - **Lok Sabha term (Art. 83(2)):** Parliament **by law** may extend it **one year at a time**, not beyond **6 months** after the Emergency ends; same for State Assemblies.
[3:6] - **Art. 353** executive directions; **Art. 354** revenue distribution modified.
[3:7] > Asked as: who can suspend FRs (5 PYQs); Art. 359; Lok Sabha extension (2 PYQs); who legislates on the State List.

## SUB 4 | Financial Emergency (Article 360)
[4:0] - Ground: threat to the **financial stability or credit of India** or any part; proclaimed by the **President**.
[4:1] - Approval within **two months**, **simple majority**; once approved it continues **indefinitely – no maximum period** – till revoked.
[4:2] - **Effects:** directions to States on **canons of financial propriety**; **reduction of salaries and allowances** of State employees and of all persons serving the Union **including Supreme Court and High Court judges**; State **Money and Financial Bills reserved** for the President – the Centre gains full control over States' finances.
[4:3] - **Never declared** so far (nearest: the 1991 crisis) – Art. 360 is the emergency "never implemented".
[4:4] > Asked as: statements (2-month approval true; "excluding judges" false – 3 PYQs); effects (all four); never declared; no maximum period.

## SUB 7 | S.R. Bommai v Union of India (1994) and Other Cases
[7:0] - **9-judge bench**; the landmark case on **Art. 356** and **Centre-State relations**.
[7:1] - Proclamation is subject to **judicial review**; majority to be tested on the **floor of the Assembly**; Assembly only **suspended** until Parliament approves; court may **restore** a dismissed government.
[7:2] - **Secularism and federalism** are **basic features**.
