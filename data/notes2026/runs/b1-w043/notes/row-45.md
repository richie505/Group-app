=== Gandhara School – Greek Meets Indian
- **Patron dynasty:** flourished under the **Kushanas** (and Shakas), chiefly **Kanishka** (APPSC ASO; BPSC 1992; CGPSC 2008 pair "Kushans – Gandhara") – not the Mauryas or Satavahanas.
- **Origin:** interaction of Indian craftsmen with **Greeks and Romans** → **Gandhara art** (APPSC 2018 SQT); Mathura is indigenous; "Yavana art" and "Jataka art" are distractors.
- **Synthesis of Indian and Greek art** (UPPCS 1996; RAS 2008) – also called **Greco-Buddhist, Indo-Greek or Greco-Roman** art (CGPSC 2024); not Persian, Chinese or Turk-Afghan.
  - Wider influences: Hellenistic Greek, **Roman**, Persian (also Syrian, Scythian).
- **Medium:** **grey-blue / green schist** (and phyllite) – "invariably used green schist" = **Gandhara** (UPSC 1996); later **stucco** and plaster.
- **Area:** north-west – Peshawar, Taxila, Swat, Afghanistan.
- **A–R (UPSC 1998):** A "Gandhara bears the mark of Hellenistic influence" ✓; R "Hinayana was influenced by that art" ✗ – Gandhara served **Mahayana** → **A true, R false**.
- **Buddha in Gandhara:** realistic, Apollo-like face, **wavy hair with top-knot**, moustache, **heavy toga-like drapery**, spiritual calm; Buddha with Bodhisattvas (Maitreya, Avalokiteshvara).
- **Bamiyan (Afghanistan):** famous for colossal rock-cut **Buddha statues** (destroyed by the Taliban in 2001) (UPPSC Mains 2008 ×2).

=== The Buddha Image, Mudras and Mahayana
- **Mahayana features:** deification of the Buddha, the Bodhisattva path, image worship and rituals → **A, B and C** (UPSC 2019).
- **Before images:** aniconic – throne, footprints, wheel, Bodhi tree, stupa; **Hinayana = symbols, Mahayana = images**.
- **Andhra:** symbol worship (**Chaitika Vada**) was started by **Mahadeva**; Amaravati **ayakapatas** show the Buddha through symbols, not in human form.
- **First human images:** Kushana period (1st c. CE), in **Mathura** and Gandhara – priority is debated.
- **Oldest Buddha sculpture – style:** **Mathura** (IAS 2012 key; PMF: Mathura was the first to make anthropomorphic Buddha and Tirthankara images); Gandhara is the tempting option.
- **Standing statue of the Buddha:** **Kushana period** (UPPCS 1992).
| Mudra | Gesture | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Abhaya** | Raised right palm | Fearlessness; subduing the elephant Nalagiri |
| **Dhyana** | Hands in the lap | Meditation |
| **Dharmachakra** | Hands at chest forming a wheel | **First sermon, Sarnath** |
| **Bhumisparsha** | Right hand touching the earth | Enlightenment; earth called to witness |
| **Varada** | Lowered open palm | Boon-giving |
- **Bhumisparsha symbolises:** the Buddha calling the Earth to **witness his purity and chastity despite Mara's temptations** (UPSC 2012) – not calling the Earth to watch over Mara.
- **Gandharan image of the first sermon:** **Dharmachakra** mudra (UPSC 1994); mudras were first shown in Gandhara statues.
- **Sarnath Buddha (Bhumisparsha) image:** belongs to the **Gupta period** (UPPCS Mains 2009); the famous seated Sarnath Buddha is in Dharmachakra mudra – Gupta either way.
- **Sarnath school (Gupta):** light (Chunar) sandstone, plain drapery over both shoulders; Gupta sculpture continues Mathura.

=== Amaravati School
- **Famous for:** **Buddhist sculpture** (APPSC PYQ) – not Jain, Hindu or Indo-Islamic.
- **Plants and flowers, especially lotuses:** most admirably carved in the **Amaravati** school (APPSC Welfare 2017; APPSC PYQ) – not Gandhara or Mughal.
- **Patrons:** **Satavahanas**, mature phase under the **Ikshvakus**; also called the Andhra or Vengi school; Krishna valley sites – **Amaravati (Dhanyakataka)**, **Nagarjunakonda** (Ikshvaku centre), Jaggayyapeta, Goli, Ghantasala.
- **Style:** narrative reliefs of the Jatakas and the Buddha's life; slender figures in **tribhanga**, movement and emotion; crowded scenes with Yakshas and Gandharvas.
- **Symbols on ayakapatas:** lotus = birth, horse = renunciation, Bodhi tree = enlightenment, wheel = first sermon, stupa = parinirvana; **Purna-kumbha** = AP State emblem.
- **Only school showing all the events of the Buddha's life** in its panels: **Amaravati**.
- **In news (2026):** the Buddha is believed to have first taught the **Kalachakra** at Dhanyakataka (Amaravati); the AP Buddhist circuit links Amaravati, Bavikonda and **Thotlakonda**.
- **Sculptors:** **Selavardhakulu**; first depiction of **Sati** (Ikshvaku phase).
- **Writers:** **James Fergusson** – "Tree and Serpent Worship", "epitome of Indian architecture"; **Percy Brown** – "dynamic beauty".
- **Oldest stupa in AP:** **Bhattiprolu**; sculptures today in the Amaravati museum, Chennai and the **British Museum**.
- **Influence:** Sri Lanka (Anuradhapura), South-East Asia, later South Indian art.

=== Mathura School
- **Medium:** **spotted (white-spotted) red sandstone** (CGPSC 2013) – not marble, slate or granite.
- **Indigenous and multi-religious:** Buddhist, **Jain** (Tirthankaras) and Brahmanical images – early icons of **Shiva, Vishnu, Surya**, Yaksha-Yakshi.
- **Buddha:** fleshy, smiling, shaven head or snail curls, **transparent clinging drapery**, right hand in abhaya, decorated **halo (Jnanachakra)**.
- **Royal statues:** **Mat** near Mathura – the **headless standing Kanishka** in long coat and boots.
- **Period:** began 2nd c. BCE (Shungas), prominent under Kushanas and Guptas; standing Buddhas prominent in the Gupta phase.
| Feature | Gandhara | Mathura | Amaravati |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medium | Grey schist, stucco | Spotted red sandstone | White Palnadu limestone ("marble") |
| Influence | Greco-Roman | Indigenous | Indigenous |
| Religion | Buddhist (Mahayana) | Buddhist, Jain, Hindu | Mainly Buddhist |
| Patron | Kushanas | Kushanas | Satavahanas, later Ikshvakus |
| Figures | Calm, Apollo-like | Fleshy, smiling | Slender, dynamic, crowded |
- **Sthapati (temple tradition):** the **master craftsman-architect** who designs and directs the making of temples and images – "the master who makes sculptures" (APPSC Endowment 2022); the carver under him is the **shilpi**, the builders and carvers under him the **vardhaki** and **takshaka**.
