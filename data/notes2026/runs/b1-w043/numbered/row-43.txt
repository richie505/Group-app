## SUB 0 | Eras and the National Calendar
[0:0] # Eras – starting years
[0:1] TABLE head: Era | Began | Note
  [0:1.0] Kali | 3102 BC | Traditional
  [0:1.1] Vikrama Samvat | 58/57 BC | King of Ujjain (Vikramaditya) expelled the Sakas; also Krit Samvat and Malav Samvat
  [0:1.2] Saka (Shaka) | 78 AD | Exam answer: Kanishka; also equated with Chashtana's accession
  [0:1.3] Kalachuri / Chedi | 248 AD | Kalachuris
  [0:1.4] Gupta | 319-320 AD | Chandragupta I
  [0:1.5] Harsha | 606 AD | Harsha's accession
  [0:1.6] Lakshmana Sena | 1119 AD | Bengal
[0:2] - **Match code (UPSC 1995 ×2):** Vikrama – 58 BC, Saka – 78 AD, Gupta – 320 AD, Kali – 3102 BC; 248 AD (Kalachuri) is the spare.
[0:3] - **Statements (UPPCS 2011 ×2):** Vikrama 58 BC ✓, Saka 78 AD ✓, Gupta 319 AD ✓, era of Muslim rule **1192 AD** (Second Tarain) ✓ → **all four**.
[0:4] - **Malav Samvat = Krit Samvat** = Vikrama Samvat (MPPSC 2021); not Kalachuri, Saka or Gupta Samvat.
[0:5] - **Vikram Samvat started:** **57 BC** (SSC 2020); "58 CE" is a slip – it began BC.
[0:6] - **Who started the Saka era:** **Kanishka in AD 78** (WBCS 2008); Kadphises/Vikramaditya 58 BC and Rudradaman are wrong.
[0:7]   - Settled: 78 CE begins the Shaka era; exams credit **Kanishka**, many scholars tie it to the Kardamaka **Chashtana**, and Kanishka's own accession is now often put at c. 127 CE (Falk); the "Salivahana era" link to Gautamiputra is a later legend.
[0:8] - **Why "Saka" era:** the **Western Kshatrapas (Sakas)** used it on their records and coins; also called **Salivahana Saka**.
[0:9] # Converting dates
[0:10] - **AD = Saka + 78** (not 77): Saka 556 = **634 AD** (UPPSC Mains 2012); **December 2009 = Saka 1931** (UPPSC Mains 2007).
[0:11] - **Vikrama = Saka + 135** – the gap between the two eras is **135 years** (UP UDA 2006); 57 and 78 are the offsets from the AD era.
[0:12] - **Badami inscription of Pulakesin I:** Saka **465** → Vikrama **601** in the key (465 + 78 + 58; strictly 465 + 135 = 600, so 601 is the nearest option) (UPSC 1997).
[0:13] # National calendar
[0:14] - **Indian National Calendar:** based on the **Saka era, which began in 78 AD** (APPSC Royalty Inspector); adopted **22 March 1957**.
[0:15] - **1 Chaitra = 22 March** in a normal year, **21 March** in a leap year (UPSC 2014); first month **Chaitra**.
[0:16] - **Present-day civil calendar:** the **Gregorian** calendar (UP Lower Sub 2004) – not Julian, Jewish or Hijri.
[0:17] - **International Day of Forests:** **21 March** (UN; APPSC FRO) – misfiled GK; 22 March is World Water Day.

## SUB 1 | Western Kshatrapas – Nahapana and the Kshaharata Records
[1:0] # Two lines
[1:1] TABLE head: Line | Kings | Seat
  [1:1.0] Kshaharata | Abhiraka, Bhumaka, Nahapana | Western / upper Deccan – Nasik, Junnar, Karle
  [1:1.1] Kardamaka | Chashtana (founder, c. 78 CE), Rudradaman I | Ujjain, Malwa, Kathiawar
[1:2] - **Nahapana's family:** **Kshatrapa** (Kshaharata Saka) (APPSC G2-A 1991); not Kadamba, Ganga or Vakataka.
[1:3] - **Silver:** Nahapana introduced silver currency in the Nasik area; Western Kshatrapa silver coins are numerous and the **Kardamaka silver coins carry Saka-era dates**.
[1:4] # Satavahana wars
[1:5] - **Gautamiputra Satakarni defeated Nahapana** and **restruck** his silver coins – the **Jogalthembi** hoard (Nasik district) (APPSC PYQ); Nashik, Karle and Ujjain are distractors.
[1:6] - **Nasik Prashasti:** issued by **Gautami Balashri**; calls Gautamiputra destroyer of **Shakas, Yavanas and Pahlavas**.
[1:7] - **Matrimonial alliance:** the Satavahanas allied with **Rudradaman** – his daughter (Rudradamanika) married **Vasishthiputra Satakarni**, a son of Gautamiputra's line (APPSC Tribal Welfare); Nahapana was an enemy, Kanishka and Vasishka Kushanas.
[1:8] - **Chashtana vs Vasishthiputra Pulumavi:** recorded by **Ptolemy**.
[1:9] - **AP angle:** after the Kshatrapa wars the Satavahana centre shifted from **Pratishthana (Paithan)** to **Amaravati (Dhanyakataka)** under Pulumavi.
[1:10] # Kshaharata inscriptions
[1:11] TABLE head: Inscription | Issuer | Content
  [1:11.0] Junnar (Manmodi) | Ayama, Nahapana's minister (amatya) | Gift of a cave / mandapa, year 46
  [1:11.1] Nasik | Ushavadatta (Rishabhadatta), Nahapana's son-in-law | 2,000 karshapanas with the Govardhana weavers' guild at 12%
  [1:11.2] Karle | Ushavadatta | Gift of a village to the Karle monks
[1:12] - **Junnar inscription issuer = Ayama** (G2 Mains 2017; G2 PYQ); **⚠ Trap:** Ushavadatta (Nasik, Karle), Bhumaka and Chashtana are distractors.
[1:13] - **Gold : silver = 1 : 35** in the Shaka-Kshatrapa period – one **suvarna** = 35 silver **karshapanas** (Ushavadatta's Nasik record) (UKPCS 2021).

## SUB 2 | Rudradaman I and the Junagadh Inscription
[2:0] - **Rudradaman I (c. 130-150 CE):** greatest Shaka (Kardamaka) ruler, from **Ujjain**; held Sindh, Gujarat, Konkan, Narmada valley, Malwa, Kathiawar; also defeated the Yaudheyas.
[2:1] - **Junagadh (Girnar) rock inscription:** dated **Saka 72 (150 CE)**; **Sanskrit**, Brahmi script.
[2:2] - **First (long) Sanskrit inscription:** **Junagadh** (APPSC PYQ) – earlier long records were in Prakrit; Nasik, Hathigumpha (Prakrit) and Uttaramerur (Chola) are distractors.
[2:3] - **Satakarni:** Rudradaman twice defeated Satakarni, **lord of Dakshinapatha**, but spared him as a close relative.
[2:4] # Sudarshana lake
[2:5] TABLE head: Work | By
  [2:5.0] Built | Pushyagupta, governor of Chandragupta Maurya
  [2:5.1] Canals | Tushaspha (Yavanaraja) under Ashoka
  [2:5.2] Repaired (150 CE) | Rudradaman I through his Pahlava minister Suvishakha – without forced labour or new taxes
  [2:5.3] Repaired again | Chakrapalita (son of Parnadatta) under Skandagupta
[2:6] - **Gujarat under Chandragupta Maurya:** proved by the **Junagadh rock inscription of Rudradaman** (Pushyagupta's lake) (UP UDA 2010).
[2:7] - **Renovated Sudarshana without forced labour:** **Rudradaman I** (UPPCS 2014).

## SUB 3 | Invader Sequence, Shaka Branches and Parthians
[3:0] - **Chronology:** **Greeks → Sakas → Kushanas** (UPSC 2006 ×2; UPPCS 2023); full order Indo-Greeks → Sakas → Parthians (Pahlavas) → Kushanas.
[3:1]   - Greeks first whether dated from Alexander (326 BC) or Indo-Greek rule (2nd c. BC); Sakas 1st c. BC; Kushanas 1st c. AD.
[3:2] - **First Shaka king:** **Maues (Moga)**, c. 85-80 BC, in Gandhara.
[3:3] TABLE head: Branch | Seat | Rulers
  [3:3.0] North-west | Taxila | Liaka Kusulaka, Patika (Taxila copper plate)
  [3:3.1] Mathura | Mathura | Rajuvula, Sodasa
  [3:3.2] Western Kshatrapas | Ujjain, Kathiawar | Chashtana, Rudradaman I
  [3:3.3] Upper Deccan | Nasik | Bhumaka, Nahapana
[3:4] - **Centres:** Mathura, Ujjain, Girnar; ruled through **Satrapas and Mahasatrapas**; absorbed as **Vratya Kshatriyas**; often at war with the Satavahanas.
[3:5] - **End:** Chandragupta II defeated the last Western Kshatrapa (Rudrasimha III) and issued the silver **Rupaka**.
[3:6] - **Parthians (Pahlavas):** greatest king **Gondophares** – **Takht-i-Bahi** inscription near **Peshawar**; **St Thomas** came to his court.
[3:7] - **Military edge:** better **cavalry** (not elephants).
