[43:0:3] - **Statements (UPPCS 2011 ×2):** Vikrama 58 BC ✓, Saka 78 AD ✓, Gupta 319 AD ✓, era of Muslim rule **1192 AD** (Second Tarain) ✓ → **all four**.
[43:0:4] - **Malav Samvat = Krit Samvat** = Vikrama Samvat (MPPSC 2021); not Kalachuri, Saka or Gupta Samvat.
[43:0:5] - **Vikram Samvat started:** **57 BC** (SSC 2020); "58 CE" is a slip – it began BC.
[43:0:6] - **Who started the Saka era:** **Kanishka in AD 78** (WBCS 2008); Kadphises/Vikramaditya 58 BC and Rudradaman are wrong.
[43:0:7]   - Settled: 78 CE begins the Shaka era; exams credit **Kanishka**, many scholars tie it to the Kardamaka **Chashtana**, and Kanishka's own accession is now often put at c. 127 CE (Falk); the "Salivahana era" link to Gautamiputra is a later legend.
[43:0:8] - **Why "Saka" era:** the **Western Kshatrapas (Sakas)** used it on their records and coins; also called **Salivahana Saka**.
[43:0:9] # Converting dates
[43:0:10] - **AD = Saka + 78** (not 77): Saka 556 = **634 AD** (UPPSC Mains 2012); **December 2009 = Saka 1931** (UPPSC Mains 2007).
[43:0:11] - **Vikrama = Saka + 135** – the gap between the two eras is **135 years** (UP UDA 2006); 57 and 78 are the offsets from the AD era.
[43:0:12] - **Badami inscription of Pulakesin I:** Saka **465** → Vikrama **601** in the key (465 + 78 + 58; strictly 465 + 135 = 600, so 601 is the nearest option) (UPSC 1997).
[43:0:13] # National calendar
[43:0:14] - **Indian National Calendar:** based on the **Saka era, which began in 78 AD** (APPSC Royalty Inspector); adopted **22 March 1957**.
[43:0:15] - **1 Chaitra = 22 March** in a normal year, **21 March** in a leap year (UPSC 2014); first month **Chaitra**.
[43:0:16] - **Present-day civil calendar:** the **Gregorian** calendar (UP Lower Sub 2004) – not Julian, Jewish or Hijri.
[43:0:17] - **International Day of Forests:** **21 March** (UN; APPSC FRO) – misfiled GK; 22 March is World Water Day.

## SUB 1 | Rudradaman I and the Junagadh Inscription
[43:1:0] - **Rudradaman I (c. 130-150 CE):** greatest Shaka (Kardamaka) ruler, from **Ujjain**; held Sindh, Gujarat, Konkan, Narmada valley, Malwa, Kathiawar; also defeated the Yaudheyas.
[43:1:1] - **Junagadh (Girnar) rock inscription:** dated **Saka 72 (150 CE)**; **Sanskrit**, Brahmi script.
[43:1:2] - **First (long) Sanskrit inscription:** **Junagadh** (APPSC PYQ) – earlier long records were in Prakrit; Nasik, Hathigumpha (Prakrit) and Uttaramerur (Chola) are distractors.
[43:1:3] - **Satakarni:** Rudradaman twice defeated Satakarni, **lord of Dakshinapatha**, but spared him as a close relative.
[43:1:4] # Sudarshana lake
[43:1:5] TABLE head: Work | By
  [43:1:5.0] Built | Pushyagupta, governor of Chandragupta Maurya
  [43:1:5.1] Canals | Tushaspha (Yavanaraja) under Ashoka
  [43:1:5.2] Repaired (150 CE) | Rudradaman I through his Pahlava minister Suvishakha – without forced labour or new taxes
  [43:1:5.3] Repaired again | Chakrapalita (son of Parnadatta) under Skandagupta
[43:1:6] - **Gujarat under Chandragupta Maurya:** proved by the **Junagadh rock inscription of Rudradaman** (Pushyagupta's lake) (UP UDA 2010).
[43:1:7] - **Renovated Sudarshana without forced labour:** **Rudradaman I** (UPPCS 2014).

## SUB 2 | Invader Sequence, Shaka Branches and Parthians
[43:2:0] - **Chronology:** **Greeks → Sakas → Kushanas** (UPSC 2006 ×2; UPPCS 2023); full order Indo-Greeks → Sakas → Parthians (Pahlavas) → Kushanas.
[43:2:1]   - Greeks first whether dated from Alexander (326 BC) or Indo-Greek rule (2nd c. BC); Sakas 1st c. BC; Kushanas 1st c. AD.
[43:2:2] - **First Shaka king:** **Maues (Moga)**, c. 85-80 BC, in Gandhara.
[43:2:3] TABLE head: Branch | Seat | Rulers
  [43:2:3.0] North-west | Taxila | Liaka Kusulaka, Patika (Taxila copper plate)
  [43:2:3.1] Mathura | Mathura | Rajuvula, Sodasa
  [43:2:3.2] Western Kshatrapas | Ujjain, Kathiawar | Chashtana, Rudradaman I
  [43:2:3.3] Upper Deccan | Nasik | Bhumaka, Nahapana
[43:2:4] - **Centres:** Mathura, Ujjain, Girnar; ruled through **Satrapas and Mahasatrapas**; absorbed as **Vratya Kshatriyas**; often at war with the Satavahanas.
[43:2:5] - **End:** Chandragupta II defeated the last Western Kshatrapa (Rudrasimha III) and issued the silver **Rupaka**.
[43:2:6] - **Parthians (Pahlavas):** greatest king **Gondophares** – **Takht-i-Bahi** inscription near **Peshawar**; **St Thomas** came to his court.
[43:2:7] - **Military edge:** better **cavalry** (not elephants).

## SUB 3 | Western Kshatrapas – Nahapana and the Kshaharata Records
[43:3:0] # Two lines
[43:3:1] TABLE head: Line | Kings | Seat
  [43:3:1.0] Kshaharata | Abhiraka, Bhumaka, Nahapana | Western / upper Deccan – Nasik, Junnar, Karle
  [43:3:1.1] Kardamaka | Chashtana (founder, c. 78 CE), Rudradaman I | Ujjain, Malwa, Kathiawar
[43:3:2] - **Nahapana's family:** **Kshatrapa** (Kshaharata Saka) (APPSC G2-A 1991); not Kadamba, Ganga or Vakataka.
[43:3:3] - **Silver:** Nahapana introduced silver currency in the Nasik area; Western Kshatrapa silver coins are numerous and the **Kardamaka silver coins carry Saka-era dates**.
[43:3:4] # Satavahana wars
[43:3:5] - **Gautamiputra Satakarni defeated Nahapana** and **restruck** his silver coins – the **Jogalthembi** hoard (Nasik district) (APPSC PYQ); Nashik, Karle and Ujjain are distractors.
[43:3:6] - **Nasik Prashasti:** issued by **Gautami Balashri**; calls Gautamiputra destroyer of **Shakas, Yavanas and Pahlavas**.
[43:3:7] - **Matrimonial alliance:** the Satavahanas allied with **Rudradaman** – his daughter (Rudradamanika) married **Vasishthiputra Satakarni**, a son of Gautamiputra's line (APPSC Tribal Welfare); Nahapana was an enemy, Kanishka and Vasishka Kushanas.
[43:3:8] - **Chashtana vs Vasishthiputra Pulumavi:** recorded by **Ptolemy**.
[43:3:9] - **AP angle:** after the Kshatrapa wars the Satavahana centre shifted from **Pratishthana (Paithan)** to **Amaravati (Dhanyakataka)** under Pulumavi.
[43:3:10] # Kshaharata inscriptions
[43:3:11] TABLE head: Inscription | Issuer | Content
  [43:3:11.0] Junnar (Manmodi) | Ayama, Nahapana's minister (amatya) | Gift of a cave / mandapa, year 46
  [43:3:11.1] Nasik | Ushavadatta (Rishabhadatta), Nahapana's son-in-law | 2,000 karshapanas with the Govardhana weavers' guild at 12%
  [43:3:11.2] Karle | Ushavadatta | Gift of a village to the Karle monks
[43:3:12] - **Junnar inscription issuer = Ayama** (G2 Mains 2017; G2 PYQ); **⚠ Trap:** Ushavadatta (Nasik, Karle), Bhumaka and Chashtana are distractors.
[43:3:13] - **Gold : silver = 1 : 35** in the Shaka-Kshatrapa period – one **suvarna** = 35 silver **karshapanas** (Ushavadatta's Nasik record) (UKPCS 2021).

## SUB 1 | Kushana Rulers, Empire and Kanishka
[44:1:0] # Origin
[44:1:1] - **Kushanas = Yuezhi (Yueh-chi)** of north-west China/Central Asia – one of five Yuezhi clans; Silk Road middlemen between **Han China, Rome and India**; trade fed a second wave of urbanisation (Mathura, Taxila, Ujjain, Shravasti).
[44:1:2] - **Empire beyond India:** the **Kushan** empire ran from the Oxus to the Ganga – Central Asia, Afghanistan, parts of Iran (UK UDA 2006).
[44:1:3] TABLE head: Ruler | Fact
  [44:1:3.0] Kujula Kadphises (Kadphises I) | Founder; united the five Yuezhi clans; took the Kabul valley – the "Kushan ruler" among the options (APPSC G2 2008; SSC 2016)
  [44:1:3.1] Vima Taktu | Between Kujula and Vima (Rabatak)
  [44:1:3.2] Vima Kadphises (Kadphises II) | Extended rule to Mathura and Varanasi; title Sarvalokeshvara ("lord of the whole world") and Maheshvara (RPSC 2024); father of Kanishka
  [44:1:3.3] Kanishka | Zenith; capitals Purushapura (Peshawar) and Mathura
  [44:1:3.4] Huvishka | Kanishka's successor
  [44:1:3.5] Vasudeva I | Last great Kushana; first with an Indian name
[44:1:4] - **Distractors:** Ugrasena, Vikramaditya, Dantidurga, Pushyamitra are not Kushanas; Sakari, Mahakshatrapa, Simha-chandra are not Vima's titles.
[44:1:5] - **Kanishka's titles:** Devaputra (China), Shaonano Shao (Persia), Kaisara (Rome), Maharaja (India).
[44:1:6] # Kanishka
[44:1:7] - **Coronation / era:** **78 AD**, starting the Saka era (UPPSC 1991) – modern estimates put his accession c. 127 CE (Falk).
[44:1:8] - **Rabatak inscription (1993, near Surkh Kotal, Afghanistan):** Bactrian language, Greek script; genealogy Kujula → Vima Taktu → Vima Kadphises → Kanishka; cities **Saketa, Kaushambi, Pataliputra, Sri Champa** – **Shravasti is NOT named** (UPRO 2014).
[44:1:9] - **Era of 78 CE:** called the **Saka era** because the **Western Kshatrapas** used it on their records and coins; also **Salivahana Saka** (a legend credits Gautamiputra Satakarni); it began **135 years after** the Vikrama era (57 BCE); India's national calendar since 1957.
[44:1:10] - **Trade evidence of the period:** the **Periplus of the Erythraean Sea** (text) and the **Arikamedu** excavation – not Inamgaon.
[44:1:11] - **Sarnath image:** Bodhisattva dedicated by Friar **Bala** in year 3 (81 AD).
[44:1:12] - **China:** refused a Han princess, the Kushanas marched against the Chinese general **Pan Chao (Ban Chao)** and were **defeated** (Jharkhand 2003); Ban Yong, Shi Huang Ti, He-Ti are distractors.

## SUB 3 | Central Asian Contributions – Dress, Cavalry, Surkhi and Art
[44:3:0] - **Dress (Shakas and Kushanas):** **turban, tunic, trousers and heavy long coat** (the sherwani descends from the coat); Central Asians also brought **caps, helmets and boots** → all five items, **I, II, III, IV and V** (APPSC G1 PYQ).
[44:3:1]   - **⚠ Settled:** the old app key excluded the turban (II, III, IV, V); PMF and R.S. Sharma list the turban first, so the key is all five.
[44:3:2] - **Cavalry:** better cavalry, wider use of **reins and saddles**, possibly toe-stirrups; not elephants.
[44:3:3] - **Surkhi** (brick-dust lime mortar): evidence first found in **Kushana** levels at Mathura – the key for "introduced by" (UPPCS Mains 2014); the Sultans used it widely later.
[44:3:4] - **Social status:** Manu called foreigners (Shakas, Kushanas) who did not follow priestly custom **"fallen Kshatriyas" (Vratya Kshatriyas)** (UPPSC 2014).
[44:3:5] - **Greatest development of the Kushan period:** **art** (SSC 2005) – Gandhara and Mathura schools under Kushana patronage.
[44:3:6] - **Gandhara art flourished under:** the **Kushans** (BPSC 1992); not Guptas, Akbar or Mauryas.
[44:3:7] - **Early Buddha images (1st c. CE):** Gandhara – standing Buddha in pleated robes; Mathura – seated Buddha with a halo; Amaravati – **white limestone** narrative panels (not red sandstone).
[44:3:8] - **First human statues worshipped in India:** those of the **Buddha** (RAS 2010) – Mahayana image worship.

## SUB 2 | Amaravati School
[45:2:0] - **Famous for:** **Buddhist sculpture** (APPSC PYQ) – not Jain, Hindu or Indo-Islamic.
[45:2:1] - **Plants and flowers, especially lotuses:** most admirably carved in the **Amaravati** school (APPSC Welfare 2017; APPSC PYQ) – not Gandhara or Mughal.
[45:2:2] - **Patrons:** **Satavahanas**, mature phase under the **Ikshvakus**; also called the Andhra or Vengi school; Krishna valley sites – **Amaravati (Dhanyakataka)**, **Nagarjunakonda** (Ikshvaku centre), Jaggayyapeta, Goli, Ghantasala.

[45:2:4] - **Symbols on ayakapatas:** lotus = birth, horse = renunciation, Bodhi tree = enlightenment, wheel = first sermon, stupa = parinirvana; **Purna-kumbha** = AP State emblem.

=== NOTES 1:46 | Satavahanas – Origin, Simuka, Gautamiputra Satakarni, Nasik Prasasti, Vasishthiputra Pulumavi
## SUB 0 | Founder, Firsts and Independence from the Mauryas
[46:0:0] # The firsts
[46:0:1] - **First historical ruling dynasty of the Deccan:** the **Satavahanas** (c. 200 BC – 200 AD), the oldest Deccan dynasty (Sainik Welfare 2012; UP 2001).
[46:0:2]   - Distractors: Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, Cholas, Pallavas – all centuries later.
[46:0:3] - **First dynasty to rule Andhra:** the **Satavahanas** (G1 2010 re-exam; G1 PYQ); not the Cholas, Eastern Chalukyas or Rashtrakutas.
[46:0:4] - **Puranic name:** the Puranas call them **Andhras** or **Andhrabhrityas** ("servants of the Andhras", or Andhras who had been feudatories); the dynasty identified with the Puranic Andhras = Satavahanas.
[46:0:5] - **Caste:** **Brahmins**; the Nasik inscription calls Gautamiputra **"Ekabrahmana"** (APPSC PYQ).
[46:0:6] - **⚠ Trap:** they claimed **Brahmana** status, not Buddhist descent – a statement "Satavahanas claimed Buddhist descent" is false.
[46:0:7] # Founder – Simuka
[46:0:8] - **Founder:** **Simuka** = Simukha = Srimukha = Shimukha (Puranic "Sinthaka"); asked 13 times (G2 2000, 2008; Geologist 2007; SI 2011; UPPSC 2008).
[46:0:9] - **Founder of the administration too:** Simuka (G2 2008); he united several principalities into one kingdom.
[46:0:10] - **Rise:** killed the **Kanva** king **Susarma** and ended the Kanvas; ruled from **Pratishthana**; said to have ruled 23 years.
[46:0:11] - **Progenitor:** per the **Puranas = Srimukha**; per inscriptions, coins and Kathasaritsagara = **Satavahana** (Srimukha's father) – exams answer Simuka.
[46:0:12] - **Successors:** brother **Kanha (Krishna)**, 2nd king; then son **Satakarni I**, 3rd king. Purnotsanga (Vedasri) and Kanha are distractors for "founder".
[46:0:13] # Independence and span
[46:0:14] - **Freedom from the Mauryas:** the Satavahanas began as Mauryan feudatories; the Andhras won freedom from Mauryan rule under them (APPSC PYQ).

[46:2:11] - **Meaning:** usually explained by **polygamy**; shows respect for mothers, but succession stayed **patriarchal**.

## SUB 7 | Sources – Puranas, Literature, Foreign Accounts
[46:7:0] # Puranas
[46:7:1] - **Main Purana:** the **Matsya Purana** – genealogy and reign of each king; Satavahana political history rests on it (Welfare Officers).
[46:7:2] - **Matsya figures:** **30 kings, 456 years** (271 BC – 174 AD); kings 1-23 = early, 24-30 = later Satavahanas.
[46:7:3] - **Compiled under:** **Yajnasri Satakarni** (APPSC PYQ).
[46:7:4] - **Vayu / Vishnu:** 17 kings, 272 years – not treated as standard.
[46:7:5] - **⚠ Trap:** the **Vishnudharmottara Purana** (Chitrasutra) is the "sourcebook of Indian painting", not a Satavahana source (Animal Husbandry GS).
[46:7:6] # Literature
[46:7:7] - **Early Satavahana history:** told in **Kathasaritsagara, Pratishthanapura-kalpa, Dvatrimsatputtalika**.
[46:7:8] - **⚠ Trap:** the **Gathasaptasati** does **not** narrate early Satavahana history (G2 2017; APPSC PYQ).
[46:7:9] # Kharavela
[46:7:10] - **Contemporary:** Kharavela of Kalinga was the contemporary of **Satakarni I** (G2-B 1989); the Hathigumpha inscription and the Chulakalinga Jataka record the contact.
[46:7:11] - **⚠ Sources differ:** NextGen says Kharavela "attacked and defeated" Satakarni I.
[46:7:12]   - Settled: Hathigumpha line 4 says he sent an army west "disregarding Satakarni", to the Kanhabenna; no defeat is recorded (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hathigumpha_inscription).

## SUB 0 | Gathasaptasati and Hala
[47:0:0] - **Compiler:** the Satavahana king **Hala** (17th king, ruled 5 years), himself a poet – the most repeated fact of the topic (G2-A 1984, G2-B 1989, G2-A 1991, G2 2008 ×2; Welfare Officers 2017 ×2; APPSC PYQ ×5).

[47:0:4] - **Women poets:** **Anulakshmi, Anupalabdha (Anupamalabdha), Reva (Revati), Madhavi** – the text "containing writing by women" (G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ); not Brihatkatha, Kamasutra or Lilavati.

## SUB 7 | Land Revenue, Taxes and Tax-free Grants
[47:7:0] - **Bhaga:** land revenue = king's share of **1/6** of the produce (JA NTR UHS) – the chief income.

[47:7:7] - **Spread of cultivation:** mainly in the **Krishna-Godavari** basin; paddy transplantation; chief crops rice and cotton.
