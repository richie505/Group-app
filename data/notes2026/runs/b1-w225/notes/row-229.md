=== Satyagraha – Meaning, Rules and Weapons
- **Satya + agraha** = insistence on (holding to) truth; "**soul force**" / "truth force" in place of physical force.
- Born **1906 in the Transvaal**; early English name **passive resistance** – but Gandhi distinguished them: passive resistance = weapon of the weak, **satyagraha = weapon of the strongest**.
- Gandhi is best known for **using passive resistance (satyagraha) to win Indian independence** – not violence, not an Islamic or anti-Hindu agenda.
- Aim is **not to defeat the enemy** but to **change his heart**; **suffering** is its driving force; a satyagrahi has **no ill feeling** towards the opponent and stays **firm in his belief**.
- **Weapon of satyagraha = ahimsa**; two-fold method for conflicts = **non-violence and satyagraha**.
- Rules: **never insult the opponent**; bear assaults **without retaliation**; **submit to arrest**; obey jail discipline.
- Forms: non-cooperation, boycott, picketing, hartal, strike, civil disobedience, **fasting**.
- **Fasting = the LAST weapon**: "Fasting unto death is the **last and the most potent weapon** in the armoury of Satyagraha" (Harijan, 18 Aug 1946); used only when all other avenues have failed.
| Struggle | Year | Form / "first" |
|---|---|---|
| Champaran | 1917 | First civil disobedience |
| Ahmedabad | 1918 | First hunger strike |
| Kheda | 1918 | First non-cooperation |
| **Rowlatt Satyagraha** | **1919** | **Hartal**; first **all-India mass** movement |
> The old notes' "first step fasting, last step strike" (a printed key) is wrong: in Gandhi's words fasting is the **last** resort.

=== Truth and Ahimsa
- **Truth is the end; non-violence (ahimsa) is the means** – two sides of one coin → non-violence is **a way to attain truth** (not an end in itself).
- Truth = reality; described as **existence, consciousness and bliss** (sat-chit-ananda).
- **"God is Truth"** = earlier formulation; **"Truth is God"** = later.
- Ahimsa in **thought, word and deed**; a quality of the **strong (brave)**, not the weak; between **cowardice and violence**, Gandhi would choose **violence**.
- **Cruellest form of violence = persistence of poverty**.
- **Family planning**: **self-control** (not sterilisation or the loop).
- **2 October** (birth anniversary) = **International Day of Non-Violence** (UN General Assembly, 2007).

=== Means, Ends, Politics and Swaraj
| Quotation | Point |
|---|---|
| "**Wrong means never take us to right ends**" | Means-ends purity (Gandhi) |
| "Means are after all everything. **As the means so the end**" | Means decide the end |
| "What is morally wrong can never be politically right" | Ethics in politics |
| "Politics bereft of religion is a **death-trap**" | Religion = morality of truth and non-violence |
| "Real swaraj … the **capacity of all to resist authority when abused**" | Swaraj and authority |
| "Swaraj will not be a **free gift of the British Parliament**; it will be a declaration of India's full self-expression" | Gandhi (Young India, 1922) |
| **Gandhi's Talisman** | Recall the face of the **poorest and weakest** |
- Politics = **activity for public welfare**; built on **ethics, religion and humanity** – **no place for authority**.
- Key suggestion: **decentralisation through village panchayats**.
- **Swaraj** = not only political freedom: **self-rule, self-control** and the capacity of the masses to govern themselves; linked principles **swadeshi, ahimsa, satyagraha, decentralisation, education for all** – **class dictatorship** is not among them.

=== Gandhism Compared and Influences
| Point | Gandhism | Marxism |
|---|---|---|
| **Final goal** | **Stateless society** | **Stateless society** (the one common agreement – UPSC 2020) |
| Method | **Non-violence, change of heart** | Class struggle, revolution |
| Property | **Trusteeship** | Abolition of private property |
| Basis | Moral / spiritual | Economic determinism |
- **Philosophical anarchist**: "**enlightened anarchy**"; the State = **violence in concentrated form**.
- Preamble **socialism** = blend of Marxism and Gandhism, with a **Gandhian tilt**.
- Not Gandhian: **class dictatorship**; heavy industrialisation (Nehru).
- Influences: **Jainism – Raychandbhai**; Tolstoy (The Kingdom of God is Within You); Ruskin (Unto This Last); **Thoreau (Civil Disobedience)**; **Sermon on the Mount** (essence of Christ's teaching for him).
