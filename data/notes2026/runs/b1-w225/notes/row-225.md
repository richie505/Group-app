=== Simon Commission – Appointment, Boycott and Visits
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Official name | **Indian Statutory Commission**; "White (Men) Commission" |
| Appointed | **8 November 1927** (not 1925/1928/1930), **two years early**, by **Stanley Baldwin's Conservative** government; Secretary of State **Lord Birkenhead** |
| Purpose | **Examine the working of the 1919 Act** (reforms) – statutory 10-year review; "administrative reform" (UPPSC 1990), not education, agriculture, army, Rowlatt Act or princely states |
| Members | **Seven, all British** – chairman **Sir John Simon (Liberal)**; **Clement Attlee** (Labour) a member |
| Why boycotted | **No Indian member** – all were Englishmen (not "appointed before time", not support for the League, not NCM) |
| Exclusion of Indians | On the suggestion of Viceroy **Lord Irwin** (UP Lower Sub 2013 key; general knowledge) |
| Arrival | **Bombay, 3 February 1928** (visit year **1928**) – all-India hartal, black flags, **"Simon Go Back"** |
| Visits | **Two**: 3 Feb – 31 Mar 1928 and **11 Oct 1928 – 13 Apr 1929** (so "second visit Oct 1929 – Apr 1930" ✗) |
- **Boycott decision:** Madras Congress, **Dec 1927, M.A. Ansari** – "at every stage and in every form"; joined by the Hindu Mahasabha and **Jinnah's** League faction; **not** by Shafi's League faction, the Punjab Unionists or the **Justice Party**.
- The Commission **accentuated discontent** in all parties for excluding Indians (not the Welby Commission, Cripps or Cabinet Mission).
- **"Simon Go Back"** slogan coined by **Yusuf Meherally**.
- **Statements (UPPSC 2005):** appointed to enquire into the 1919 Act ✓; headed by Simon ✓; federal form recommended ✓ (as an ultimate goal); opposed by Indian leaders ✓ → **all four**.
- **"Report should be thrown on a heap of rubbish":** **Sir P.S. Sivaswami Iyer** (attribution from exam keys; not in the library).

=== Protests – Lala Lajpat Rai and the Andhra Boycott
# Lahore and Lucknow
- **Lahore, 30 October 1928:** **Lala Lajpat Rai** led the anti-Simon procession, was hit in a **lathi charge** (not Rowlatt, Quit India or 1935 Act protests) and died on **17 Nov 1928** ("last nails in the coffin of British imperialism").
- A-R: protest led by Lajpat Rai at Lahore (A) and "no Indian member" (R) – **both true, R explains A**.
- Officer held responsible: **Saunders** (ASP) – shot by **Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Azad, Dec 1928** (Spectrum: "the police official responsible for the lathicharge"); not Hugh Rose or Nicholson.
- **Lucknow:** Jawaharlal Nehru and G.B. Pant beaten.
- Lajpat Rai had presided over the **first AITUC session (1920)**.
# Andhra
| Place / person | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Madras** | **Tanguturi Prakasam** led the procession, **1928**; after the firing (Parthasarathi killed) he **bared his chest** → title **"Andhra Kesari"** (Simon protest, not Vande Mataram, NCM or Quit India) |
| **Guntur and Ongole** | **Black flags** – places the Commission **visited** in Andhra |
| Vijayawada | **Ayyadevara Kaleswara Rao** handed Simon a "Simon Go Back" letter at the railway station |
| Guntur | Nadimpalli Narasimha Rao organised the movement; Tenali-Guntur protests under G.V. Unnayya Sastri |
| Municipal resolutions | Tirupati, Kurnool, **Vijayawada**, Eluru, Srikakulam backed the boycott |
| Anti-Simon committee | President **S. Satyamurti**; **Bulusu Sambamurthy** toured Andhra |
- Hartal in Andhra towns on **3 Feb 1928**; the Commission reached Madras on 26 Feb 1928 → Simon "visited Andhra" in **1928**.

=== Simon Commission Report (1930)
- Published **May 1930** in two volumes.
- **Abolition of dyarchy** → **responsible (representative) government** and **autonomy in the provinces** (UPSC 2010 correct statement) – not an inter-provincial council, not abolition of the bicameral centre, not an Indian Police Service.
- **Rejected parliamentary responsibility at the Centre**; federation accepted only as a distant goal.
- **Separate communal electorates retained** (and extended); first official use of the term **"Scheduled Castes"**.
- **Sindh** separated from Bombay; **Burma** separated from India; new **Orissa** province – **Andhra's demand ignored**.
- **Baseline of the GoI Act, 1935** (not Cabinet Mission, Cripps or a "Rowlatt Commission").

=== Nehru Report (1928)
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Trigger | **Lord Birkenhead's challenge** to Indians to frame an agreed constitution – the **first Indian draft of a dominion-status constitution**, made in **response to the Simon Commission** |
| Body | Committee of the **All Parties Conference** (Feb 1928, presided by **M.A. Ansari**) |
| Chairman | **Motilal Nehru** (not J.L., R.K. or B.L. Nehru); **Jawaharlal Nehru** = secretary |
| Members | Tej Bahadur Sapru, **Subhas Bose**, Ali Imam, M.S. Aney, Mangal Singh, Shuaib Qureshi, G.R. Pradhan |
| Subject | **Constitutional arrangements** for India |
| Finalised | **August 1928** (Lucknow); adopted at **Calcutta Congress, Dec 1928** |
# Recommendations
- **Dominion status** (not complete independence).
- **Joint electorates with reserved seats** for minorities – **no separate electorates**.
- **Linguistic provinces** (not Mountbatten, Cripps or Cabinet Mission).
- **19 Fundamental Rights**, universal adult suffrage, equal rights for women – first document to advocate FRs (the 1935 Act, August Offer and Cripps did not).
- Responsible government at Centre and provinces; **bicameral** centre; **Supreme Court**; structure broadly **unitary** (residuary powers with the Centre).
# Reactions
- Radicals **Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Bose** formed the **Independence for India League (1928)** in opposition to the report's dominion status.
- **Muslim League** rejected it; **All Parties Muslim Conference**, Delhi, **31 Dec 1928**, presided by the **Aga Khan**; Jinnah's amendments failed at Calcutta (Dec 1928).
- **"First demanded dominion status" (UPPCS 2013 key):** **Tej Bahadur Sapru and Jayakar** (Liberals; printed key, not verified in the library – Motilal Nehru's 1926 call is the other claimant).

=== Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929)
- Proposed by **M.A. Jinnah** after rejection of his amendments to the **Nehru Report**; **Fourteen** points (not 13/15/16).
- Presented at the **Muslim League session, Delhi, March 1929**.
| Key point | Fact |
|---|---|
| Constitution | **Federal**, residuary powers with **provinces**; uniform provincial autonomy |
| Central legislature | Muslim representation **not less than one-third** (not one-fifth) |
| Cabinets | **One-third Muslim ministers** at Centre and provinces |
| Electorates | **Separate electorates** continue |
| Amendment | No change by the Centre **without concurrence of the states** of the federation |
| Religion | **Full religious liberty** for all communities |
| Provinces | Sindh separated from Bombay; reforms in NWFP and Baluchistan |
- **Delhi Proposals (March 1927):** joint electorates with reserved seats, one-third seats at Centre, Sindh separated – vs Fourteen Points: keep separate electorates.

=== Round Table Conferences (1930-32) – Dates, Aim and Outcome
| RTC | Dates | Congress | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First** | **12 Nov 1930 – 19 Jan 1931** | **Absent** (boycotted; CDM) | Opened by George V, chaired by **Ramsay MacDonald**; federation agreed in principle |
| **Second** | **7 Sep – 1 Dec 1931** | **Gandhi – sole representative** | **Failed on the communal (minorities) question** |
| **Third** | **17 Nov – 24 Dec 1932** | **Absent** | Only 46 delegates; White Paper (1933) → GoI Act 1935 |
- All held in **London** (not Delhi, Paris, Moscow); aim: **constitutional reforms** – to **discuss the Simon Commission report** and the future constitution, including **dominion status** (APPSC G1 2019: both).
- Strictly **one conference in three sessions**, not three conferences (UPSC 1996).
- **Gandhi-Irwin Pact, 5 March 1931:** Congress suspended CDM and agreed to attend → paved the way for the **Second RTC** (A-R: Nehru did **not** represent Congress at the 2nd RTC; Congress participation was implicit in the Pact → A false, R true).
- **Karachi Congress (March 1931)** **endorsed** (did not oppose) the Pact.
- **Communal Award: 16 August 1932** (not 1933); Indian Navy Act 1927, CDM 1930, 2nd RTC 1931 ✓.
- **APPSC match (cancelled):** 1st RTC – no Congress; Gandhi-Irwin Pact – Gandhi agreed to attend the next; 2nd RTC – disputes with the League and Ambedkar; 3rd RTC – Congress again absent (A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4).
