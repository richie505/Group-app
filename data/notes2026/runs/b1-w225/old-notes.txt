## SUB 0 | Simon Commission – Appointment, Boycott and Visits
[225:0:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [225:0:0.0] Official name | Indian Statutory Commission; "White (Men) Commission"
  [225:0:0.1] Appointed | 8 November 1927 (not 1925/1928/1930), two years early, by Stanley Baldwin's Conservative government; Secretary of State Lord Birkenhead
  [225:0:0.2] Purpose | Examine the working of the 1919 Act (reforms) – statutory 10-year review; "administrative reform" (UPPSC 1990), not education, agriculture, army, Rowlatt Act or princely states
  [225:0:0.3] Members | Seven, all British – chairman Sir John Simon (Liberal); Clement Attlee (Labour) a member
  [225:0:0.4] Why boycotted | No Indian member – all were Englishmen (not "appointed before time", not support for the League, not NCM)
  [225:0:0.5] Exclusion of Indians | On the suggestion of Viceroy Lord Irwin (UP Lower Sub 2013 key; general knowledge)
  [225:0:0.6] Arrival | Bombay, 3 February 1928 (visit year 1928) – all-India hartal, black flags, "Simon Go Back"
  [225:0:0.7] Visits | Two: 3 Feb – 31 Mar 1928 and 11 Oct 1928 – 13 Apr 1929 (so "second visit Oct 1929 – Apr 1930" ✗)
[225:0:1] - **Boycott decision:** Madras Congress, **Dec 1927, M.A. Ansari** – "at every stage and in every form"; joined by the Hindu Mahasabha and **Jinnah's** League faction; **not** by Shafi's League faction, the Punjab Unionists or the **Justice Party**.
[225:0:2] - The Commission **accentuated discontent** in all parties for excluding Indians (not the Welby Commission, Cripps or Cabinet Mission).
[225:0:3] - **"Simon Go Back"** slogan coined by **Yusuf Meherally**.
[225:0:4] - **Statements (UPPSC 2005):** appointed to enquire into the 1919 Act ✓; headed by Simon ✓; federal form recommended ✓ (as an ultimate goal); opposed by Indian leaders ✓ → **all four**.
[225:0:5] - **"Report should be thrown on a heap of rubbish":** **Sir P.S. Sivaswami Iyer** (attribution from exam keys; not in the library).
[225:0:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum (Ahir) p.389-392; PMF Modern History 04 p.174-175; NextGen AP History Part 19 (src/ap-history/ap-history-appsc-19.txt) p.2; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.58, p.74; Laxmikanth 8th ed. (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt) p.32
[225:0:7] - **Madras session, Dec 1927 (M.A. Ansari):** besides the boycott, it passed Nehru's **complete independence resolution** and objected to Indian troops being sent to **China, Mesopotamia and Persia**.
## SUB 1 | Round Table Conferences (1930-32) – Dates, Aim and Outcome
[225:1:0] TABLE head: RTC | Dates | Congress | Outcome

[225:1:5] - **Communal Award: 16 August 1932** (not 1933); Indian Navy Act 1927, CDM 1930, 2nd RTC 1931 ✓.
[225:1:6] - **APPSC match (cancelled):** 1st RTC – no Congress; Gandhi-Irwin Pact – Gandhi agreed to attend the next; 2nd RTC – disputes with the League and Ambedkar; 3rd RTC – Congress again absent (A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4).

## SUB 2 | Nehru Report (1928)
[225:2:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [225:2:0.0] Trigger | Lord Birkenhead's challenge to Indians to frame an agreed constitution – the first Indian draft of a dominion-status constitution, made in response to the Simon Commission
  [225:2:0.1] Body | Committee of the All Parties Conference (Feb 1928, presided by M.A. Ansari)
  [225:2:0.2] Chairman | Motilal Nehru (not J.L., R.K. or B.L. Nehru); Jawaharlal Nehru = secretary
  [225:2:0.3] Members | Tej Bahadur Sapru, Subhas Bose, Ali Imam, M.S. Aney, Mangal Singh, Shuaib Qureshi, G.R. Pradhan
  [225:2:0.4] Subject | Constitutional arrangements for India
  [225:2:0.5] Finalised | August 1928 (Lucknow); adopted at Calcutta Congress, Dec 1928
[225:2:1] # Recommendations
[225:2:2] - **Dominion status** (not complete independence).
[225:2:3] - **Joint electorates with reserved seats** for minorities – **no separate electorates**.
[225:2:4] - **Linguistic provinces** (not Mountbatten, Cripps or Cabinet Mission).
[225:2:5] - **19 Fundamental Rights**, universal adult suffrage, equal rights for women – first document to advocate FRs (the 1935 Act, August Offer and Cripps did not).
[225:2:6] - Responsible government at Centre and provinces; **bicameral** centre; **Supreme Court**; structure broadly **unitary** (residuary powers with the Centre).
[225:2:7] # Reactions
[225:2:8] - Radicals **Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Bose** formed the **Independence for India League (1928)** in opposition to the report's dominion status.
[225:2:9] - **Muslim League** rejected it; **All Parties Muslim Conference**, Delhi, **31 Dec 1928**, presided by the **Aga Khan**; Jinnah's amendments failed at Calcutta (Dec 1928).
[225:2:10] - **"First demanded dominion status" (UPPCS 2013 key):** **Tej Bahadur Sapru and Jayakar** (Liberals; printed key, not verified in the library – Motilal Nehru's 1926 call is the other claimant).
[225:2:11] ~~ Sources: Spectrum (Ahir) p.393-397; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.58, p.74; PMF Modern History 04 p.178-181
[225:2:12] - **Ultimatum:** Calcutta Congress (Dec 1928) gave the British **one year** to accept dominion status → **Purna Swaraj** resolution, **Lahore, Dec 1929**.

## SUB 5 | Simon Commission Report (1930)
[225:5:0] - Published **May 1930** in two volumes.
[225:5:1] - **Abolition of dyarchy** → **responsible (representative) government** and **autonomy in the provinces** (UPSC 2010 correct statement) – not an inter-provincial council, not abolition of the bicameral centre, not an Indian Police Service.
[225:5:2] - **Rejected parliamentary responsibility at the Centre**; federation accepted only as a distant goal.
[225:5:3] - **Separate communal electorates retained** (and extended); first official use of the term **"Scheduled Castes"**.
[225:5:4] - **Sindh** separated from Bombay; **Burma** separated from India; new **Orissa** province – **Andhra's demand ignored**.
[225:5:5] - **Baseline of the GoI Act, 1935** (not Cabinet Mission, Cripps or a "Rowlatt Commission").
[225:5:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum (Ahir) p.392-393; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.74; NextGen AP History Part 22 (src/ap-history/ap-history-appsc-22.txt) p.26; old app notes 1:224 ("Scheduled Castes")

  [225:6:0.1] Deepavali Declaration | 31 Oct 1929 | Lord Irwin | Dominion status the natural goal of India's constitutional progress; a Round Table Conference after the Simon report

## SUB 7 | Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929)
[225:7:0] - Proposed by **M.A. Jinnah** after rejection of his amendments to the **Nehru Report**; **Fourteen** points (not 13/15/16).
[225:7:1] - Presented at the **Muslim League session, Delhi, March 1929**.
[225:7:2] TABLE head: Key point | Fact
  [225:7:2.0] Constitution | Federal, residuary powers with provinces; uniform provincial autonomy
  [225:7:2.1] Central legislature | Muslim representation not less than one-third (not one-fifth)
  [225:7:2.2] Cabinets | One-third Muslim ministers at Centre and provinces
  [225:7:2.3] Electorates | Separate electorates continue
  [225:7:2.4] Amendment | No change by the Centre without concurrence of the states of the federation
  [225:7:2.5] Religion | Full religious liberty for all communities
  [225:7:2.6] Provinces | Sindh separated from Bombay; reforms in NWFP and Baluchistan
[225:7:3] - **Delhi Proposals (March 1927):** joint electorates with reserved seats, one-third seats at Centre, Sindh separated – vs Fourteen Points: keep separate electorates.
[225:7:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.64-65; Spectrum (Ahir) p.395-396

=== NOTES 1:226 | Government of India Act 1935 – Provisions, Provincial Autonomy, 1937 Elections and Congress Ministries
## SUB 0 | Provisions of the Government of India Act, 1935
[226:0:0] # Federation and the Centre
[226:0:1] TABLE head: Provision | Fact | Implemented?
  [226:0:1.0] Size | Longest British Act to its time – 321 Sections, 10 Schedules | –
  [226:0:1.1] All-India Federation | Provinces + princely states (states only had the option to join by an Instrument of Accession) | No – princes did not join
  [226:0:1.2] Three Lists | Federal 59 – Provincial 54 – Concurrent 36 | Yes (provincial part)
  [226:0:1.3] Residuary powers | Viceroy (Governor-General) – not Federal or Provincial legislature | –
  [226:0:1.4] Dyarchy at the Centre | Federal subjects split into reserved and transferred | Never came into operation
  [226:0:1.5] Federal Court | Interprets the Act, settles inter-province disputes | Set up 1937 (Delhi, October)
  [226:0:1.6] Council of India (of the 1858 Act) | Abolished; Secretary of State got a team of advisors | Yes
  [226:0:1.7] Reserve Bank of India | To control currency and credit | RBI set up 1935 (RBI Act 1934)
  [226:0:1.8] Public Service Commissions | Federal, Provincial and Joint PSCs | Yes
[226:0:2] # Provinces
[226:0:3] - **Abolished provincial dyarchy** (of the 1919 Act) → **provincial autonomy** with **responsible governments** in all **11 provinces**; came into force **1 April 1937**, ended 1939.
[226:0:4] - Governors kept special powers and discretion (law and order, minorities, British business); e.g. the **Section 63** address "in his discretion" → today **Article 175**.
[226:0:5] - **Bicameral legislatures in six provinces:** **Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, UP**.
[226:0:6] - Separate electorates **further extended** to **depressed classes, women and labour**; **women got reserved seats (special representation)** – UPSC 2021: only statement ii true (1919 did not give all women over 21 the vote).
[226:0:7] - Franchise: about **14 per cent** of the population (30.1 million voters).
[226:0:8] - Reorganisation: **Sind** separated from Bombay; **Bihar and Orissa** split; **Burma** separated.
[226:0:9] - **Water** ("water supplies, irrigation and canals") – a **Provincial List** entry (→ State List entry 17). Under 1919, irrigation was already a provincial *reserved* subject (Spectrum).
[226:0:10] - Rocket: **"Constitutional autocracy" = GOI Act 1935**; provincial dyarchy = 1919; central dyarchy = 1935.
[226:0:11] > Federation and central dyarchy never worked; till 1947 the Centre ran largely as under the 1919 Act. India was governed by the **1935 Act (as adapted) until 26 January 1950**.
[226:0:12] # Borrowed into the Constitution
[226:0:13] TABLE head: From the 1935 Act | From the 1919 Act
  [226:0:13.0] Federal scheme, office of Governor, judiciary (Federal Court), Public Service Commissions, emergency provisions, administrative details; Instrument of Instructions → Directive Principles | Provincial dyarchy (not carried over), bicameralism and direct elections at the Centre, separate provincial budget
[226:0:14] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. p.33; PMF Modern History Part 5 p.4-9; Spectrum pdf p.340, p.438; NextGen Rocket Indian History 01 p.97; old app notes (Sec. 63/Art. 175, Instrument of Instructions, 1 April 1937)
[226:0:15] - **RBI:** recommended by the **Hilton Young Commission (1926)**; RBI Act 1934; began **1 April 1935**; nationalised **1 January 1949**.
[226:0:16] - **Why the princes were brought into the Federation:** their nominated bloc in the federal legislature was meant to **counter-balance the nationalist (anti-imperialist) forces**.

[227:0:0] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [227:0:0.0] Session | All-India Muslim League, Lahore, 22-24 March 1940 (Minto/Iqbal Park)
  [227:0:0.1] Adopted | 23 March 1940 – soon called the Pakistan Resolution
  [227:0:0.2] Presided | M.A. Jinnah – leader of the movement for Pakistan
  [227:0:0.3] Drafted | Sikandar Hayat Khan (Unionist Premier of Punjab)
  [227:0:0.4] Moved | A.K. Fazlul Huq, Premier of Bengal
  [227:0:0.5] Seconded | Chaudhry Khaliquzzaman (UP) and others
  [227:0:0.6] Demand | Contiguous Muslim-majority areas (NW and East) grouped into independent states, units autonomous and sovereign; safeguards elsewhere – word "Pakistan" not used

[227:0:5] - Sikandar later (1941) opposed a "Muslim Raj here and Hindu Raj elsewhere".

## SUB 4 | Separate Electorates, 1909-1935
[227:4:0] TABLE head: Act / event | Step
  [227:4:0.0] Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto) | First separate electorates for Muslims + weightage; legalised communalism; Minto = "Father of Communal Electorate"
  [227:4:0.1] Lucknow Pact 1916 | Congress accepted separate electorates + weightage + communal veto (three-fourths rule)
  [227:4:0.2] GoI Act 1919 | Extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans
  [227:4:0.3] Communal Award 1932 | Extended to depressed classes; Poona Pact replaced it with reserved seats in joint electorates
  [227:4:0.4] GoI Act 1935 | Further to SCs, women, labour; Muslims 33% of Federal Legislature seats
[227:4:1] - Separate electorates **institutionalised religion in representation** – the seed of the two-nation theory; after 1935 restricted franchise + separate electorates bred separatism.
[227:4:2] - Jinnah's Point 5 (1929): separate electorates to continue.

=== NOTES 1:229 | Gandhian Philosophy – Truth, Ahimsa, Satyagraha, Means and Ends
## SUB 0 | Satyagraha – Meaning, Rules and Weapons
[229:0:0] - **Satya + agraha** = insistence on (holding to) truth; "**soul force**" / "truth force" in place of physical force.
[229:0:1] - Born **1906 in the Transvaal**; early English name **passive resistance** – but Gandhi distinguished them: passive resistance = weapon of the weak, **satyagraha = weapon of the strongest**.
[229:0:2] - Gandhi is best known for **using passive resistance (satyagraha) to win Indian independence** – not violence, not an Islamic or anti-Hindu agenda.
[229:0:3] - Aim is **not to defeat the enemy** but to **change his heart**; **suffering** is its driving force; a satyagrahi has **no ill feeling** towards the opponent and stays **firm in his belief**.
[229:0:4] - **Weapon of satyagraha = ahimsa**; two-fold method for conflicts = **non-violence and satyagraha**.
[229:0:5] - Rules: **never insult the opponent**; bear assaults **without retaliation**; **submit to arrest**; obey jail discipline.
[229:0:6] - Forms: non-cooperation, boycott, picketing, hartal, strike, civil disobedience, **fasting**.
[229:0:7] - **Fasting = the LAST weapon**: "Fasting unto death is the **last and the most potent weapon** in the armoury of Satyagraha" (Harijan, 18 Aug 1946); used only when all other avenues have failed.
[229:0:8] TABLE head: Struggle | Year | Form / "first"
  [229:0:8.0] Champaran | 1917 | First civil disobedience
  [229:0:8.1] Ahmedabad | 1918 | First hunger strike
  [229:0:8.2] Kheda | 1918 | First non-cooperation
  [229:0:8.3] Rowlatt Satyagraha | 1919 | Hartal; first all-India mass movement
[229:0:9] > The old notes' "first step fasting, last step strike" (a printed key) is wrong: in Gandhi's words fasting is the **last** resort.
[229:0:10] ~~ Sources: mkgandhi.org/voiceoftruth/fasting.php (Harijan 18-8-46, 21-12-47); PMF Modern History Part 4 p.123; Spectrum (Ahir) printed p.315-316; old app notes (rules, Rowlatt hartal)
## SUB 1 | Truth and Ahimsa
[229:1:0] - **Truth is the end; non-violence (ahimsa) is the means** – two sides of one coin → non-violence is **a way to attain truth** (not an end in itself).
[229:1:1] - Truth = reality; described as **existence, consciousness and bliss** (sat-chit-ananda).
[229:1:2] - **"God is Truth"** = earlier formulation; **"Truth is God"** = later.
[229:1:3] - Ahimsa in **thought, word and deed**; a quality of the **strong (brave)**, not the weak; between **cowardice and violence**, Gandhi would choose **violence**.
[229:1:4] - **Cruellest form of violence = persistence of poverty**.
[229:1:5] - **Family planning**: **self-control** (not sterilisation or the loop).
[229:1:6] - **2 October** (birth anniversary) = **International Day of Non-Violence** (UN General Assembly, 2007).
[229:1:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.123; Spectrum (Ahir) printed p.316; PMF Art & Culture Part 6 p.5; old app notes (truth formulations, poverty, self-control)
## SUB 2 | Means, Ends, Politics and Swaraj
[229:2:0] TABLE head: Quotation | Point
  [229:2:0.0] "Wrong means never take us to right ends" | Means-ends purity (Gandhi)
  [229:2:0.1] "Means are after all everything. As the means so the end" | Means decide the end
  [229:2:0.2] "What is morally wrong can never be politically right" | Ethics in politics
  [229:2:0.3] "Politics bereft of religion is a death-trap" | Religion = morality of truth and non-violence
  [229:2:0.4] "Real swaraj … the capacity of all to resist authority when abused" | Swaraj and authority
  [229:2:0.5] "Swaraj will not be a free gift of the British Parliament; it will be a declaration of India's full self-expression" | Gandhi (Young India, 1922)
  [229:2:0.6] Gandhi's Talisman | Recall the face of the poorest and weakest
[229:2:1] - Politics = **activity for public welfare**; built on **ethics, religion and humanity** – **no place for authority**.
[229:2:2] - Key suggestion: **decentralisation through village panchayats**.
[229:2:3] - **Swaraj** = not only political freedom: **self-rule, self-control** and the capacity of the masses to govern themselves; linked principles **swadeshi, ahimsa, satyagraha, decentralisation, education for all** – **class dictatorship** is not among them.

## SUB 3 | Gandhism Compared and Influences
[229:3:0] TABLE head: Point | Gandhism | Marxism
  [229:3:0.0] Final goal | Stateless society | Stateless society (the one common agreement – UPSC 2020)

  [229:3:0.2] Property | Trusteeship | Abolition of private property
  [229:3:0.3] Basis | Moral / spiritual | Economic determinism

[229:3:2] - Preamble **socialism** = blend of Marxism and Gandhism, with a **Gandhian tilt**.

## SUB 1 | Gandhian Economy and Trusteeship
[230:1:0] - **Non-violent economy**: **centralisation leads to exploitation and inequality** → decentralise; **production for need (consumption), not profit**; self-reliance.
[230:1:1] - Priority to **agriculture, village and cottage industries**; against mechanisation that displaces labour in labour-surplus India.
[230:1:2] - A country's economy should suit its **climate, land and the nature of its citizens** → Gandhi **did not oppose mechanisation in the USA** ("did not favour mechanisation in the USA" = the **incorrect** statement).
[230:1:3] - Not against modern industry as such: close to industrialists **G.D. (Ghanshyam Das) Birla** (who favoured healthy capitalism working with Gandhi for a common object) and Ambalal Sarabhai.
[230:1:4] - **Gandhian Innovation** = **produce more from less input for more people** – coined by **C.K. Prahalad and R.A. Mashelkar**.
[230:1:5] - **Ram Rajya** = sovereignty of the people based on **pure moral authority** (Harijan, 1937) – not Hindu Raj.
[230:1:6] # Trusteeship
[230:1:7] - The rich hold wealth **in trust for society**; **class harmony**, not class struggle; capitalists as trustees of workers.
[230:1:8] - Idea linked to **South Africa, 1903** (law office at Johannesburg); roots in the Gita's non-possession and the English law of trusts.
[230:1:9] - Applied to **capitalists and zamindars** (e.g. Ahmedabad mill owners); **Ahmedabad Mill Strike 1918 → 35% wage rise**; **Textile Labour Association (1920)** – arbitration.
[230:1:10] - **Practical Trusteeship Formula**: drafted by **Narhari Parikh and Kishorelal Mashruwala** (Aga Khan Palace), refined by M.L. Dantwala – **minimum living wage and a ceiling on income**.

=== NOTES 1:232 | Champaran, Ahmedabad Mill and Kheda Satyagrahas; Rowlatt Satyagraha and Jallianwala Bagh
## SUB 0 | Champaran Satyagraha (1917) – Gandhi's First Satyagraha in India
[232:0:0] # The Firsts
[232:0:1] - **Champaran (1917), Bihar** – Gandhi's **first satyagraha in India** and his **first successful** satyagraha after returning from South Africa (not Kheda, Ahmedabad, Bardoli, Dandi or Chauri Chaura).
[232:0:2] - Champaran district is in **Bihar** – not Gujarat, Bengal, UP or Punjab (APPSC 2019).
[232:0:3] - Year **1917** (not 1915 or 1918; NextGen's year list wrongly prints "1918 – Satyagrah at Champaran") → **2017 was its centenary** ("exactly 100 years ago" in a 2017 exam).
[232:0:4] - **First civil disobedience** in India: Gandhi **defied the order to leave the district** and preferred punishment – passive resistance / civil disobedience of an unjust order, "a novel method at that time".
[232:0:5] - Also Gandhi's **first peasant (farmers') movement** in India – before Kheda (1918) and Bardoli (1928).
[232:0:6] - Aim: to solve the problems of the **indigo cultivators** – security of tenure and freedom to grow crops of their choice; not Harijan rights or Hindu unity.
[232:0:7] - **Satyagraha** = Satya (truth) + Agraha (insistence); Gandhi (not Patel, Nehru or Vinoba) was the first to use it in the freedom struggle – evolved in **South Africa**, so it was **not formulated at Champaran**.
[232:0:8] - Champaran was **localised** – it was **not** Gandhi's first movement (South Africa came first) nor his first all-India one (**Rowlatt Satyagraha, 1919**). Its "first appearance as a leader of the masses" is accepted (NDA 2009); "second appearance" is wrong (NDA 2011).
[232:0:9] > Firsts: Champaran = first civil disobedience; Ahmedabad = first hunger strike; Kheda = first non-cooperation; Rowlatt = first all-India / mass strike.
[232:0:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 (src/indian-history/pmf-modern-history-04.txt) p.117-118, p.121; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.348-349, p.359; NextGen Rocket Indian History 02 p.54; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.42 (year error), p.72
[232:0:11] - **Before Champaran:** after his return (1915) Gandhi campaigned against the **indentured ("coolie") emigration system** (ended March 1917), so some keys treat that, not Champaran, as his first movement in India.

## SUB 3 | Tinkathia and the Indigo Problem
[232:3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [232:3:0.0] Tinkathia (Teen Kathiya) | Tenants bound to grow indigo on 3/20th of their land for European planters (not 3/19, 3/21, 3/18)
  [232:3:0.1] Sharahbeshi | Enhanced rent
  [232:3:0.2] Tawan | Lump-sum compensation demanded when indigo lost value
[232:3:1] - Place and crop: **Champaran – indigo (neel)**; not Gorakhpur-opium, Begusarai or Burdwan-paddy; not ryotwari or neelkari.
[232:3:2] - After the **Bengal Indigo Revolt (1859–60)** indigo shifted from Bengal to Bihar; Champaran ryots had also protested in 1866–68.
[232:3:3] - **German synthetic dyes** (late 19th c.) made indigo **unprofitable in the world market** – that, not peasant resistance or government control, is why indigo cultivation declined (UPSC 2020).
[232:3:4] - Planters then **raised rents and demanded illegal dues** (they did not reduce taxes) and forced sale at fixed prices – the planters oppressed the **tenants (ryots)**, not the zamindars.

## SUB 4 | Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)
[232:4:0] - Dispute between Ahmedabad **cotton-mill owners and workers** – **industrial workers** (the only one of the three early struggles involving the industrial working class).
[232:4:1] - Cause: the owners **withdrew the "plague bonus"** (of 1917); workers wanted a wage rise for wartime inflation – not hours of work; the owners (led by **Ambalal Sarabhai**) were Indian, not European.
[232:4:2] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [232:4:2.0] Workers' demand / owners' offer | 50% / 20%
  [232:4:2.1] Gandhi advised | 35% – he asked the workers to strike, non-violently
  [232:4:2.2] Gandhi's fast (15 March) | His first hunger strike – owners gave in on the 4th day, settlement 18 March
  [232:4:2.3] Result | Tribunal / arbitrator awarded 35%
  [232:4:2.4] Length | About 21 days (not 35)
[232:4:3] - **Anusuya Sarabhai** (Ambalal's sister) took up the workers' cause and brought Gandhi in; she founded the **Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (Majoor Mahajan Sangh), 25 Feb 1920**, with Gandhi as adviser.
[232:4:4] > First use of the hunger strike as a weapon = Ahmedabad – not NCM, Rowlatt or Bardoli.

## SUB 5 | Champaran Agrarian Committee and Its Results
[232:5:0] - The government appointed the **Champaran Agrarian (Enquiry) Committee, 1917**, with **Gandhi as a member** (representing the ryots) – by **agreement between the Government and Gandhiji**.
[232:5:1] - **Chairman: F.G. Sly**; members included D.J. Reid, L.C. Adami, Raja Harihar Prasad Narayan Singh, G. Rainy, M.E.L. Tainer – **Anugrah Narayan Sinha was not a member**; J.B. Kripalani, Rajendra Prasad and Rajkumar Shukla were not members either.
[232:5:2] - Recommended **abolition of Tinkathia** and the illegal exactions, and a **25% refund** of illegal recoveries → **Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918**; within a decade the planters left.
[232:5:3] - **Significance (UPSC 2018): joining of peasant unrest to India's national movement** – not "drastic decrease in plantation crops", all-India participation or Dalit/tribal involvement.

## SUB 6 | Kheda Satyagraha (1918)
[232:6:0] - **Kheda district, Gujarat**, 1918 – **first non-cooperation**.
