## SUB 0 | Provisions of the Government of India Act, 1935
[0:0] # Federation and the Centre
[0:1] TABLE head: Provision | Fact | Implemented?
  [0:1.0] Size | Longest British Act to its time – 321 Sections, 10 Schedules | –
  [0:1.1] All-India Federation | Provinces + princely states (states only had the option to join by an Instrument of Accession) | No – princes did not join
  [0:1.2] Three Lists | Federal 59 – Provincial 54 – Concurrent 36 | Yes (provincial part)
  [0:1.3] Residuary powers | Viceroy (Governor-General) – not Federal or Provincial legislature | –
  [0:1.4] Dyarchy at the Centre | Federal subjects split into reserved and transferred | Never came into operation
  [0:1.5] Federal Court | Interprets the Act, settles inter-province disputes | Set up 1937 (Delhi, October)
  [0:1.6] Council of India (of the 1858 Act) | Abolished; Secretary of State got a team of advisors | Yes
  [0:1.7] Reserve Bank of India | To control currency and credit | RBI set up 1935 (RBI Act 1934)
  [0:1.8] Public Service Commissions | Federal, Provincial and Joint PSCs | Yes
[0:2] # Provinces
[0:3] - **Abolished provincial dyarchy** (of the 1919 Act) → **provincial autonomy** with **responsible governments** in all **11 provinces**; came into force **1 April 1937**, ended 1939.
[0:4] - Governors kept special powers and discretion (law and order, minorities, British business); e.g. the **Section 63** address "in his discretion" → today **Article 175**.
[0:5] - **Bicameral legislatures in six provinces:** **Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, UP**.
[0:6] - Separate electorates **further extended** to **depressed classes, women and labour**; **women got reserved seats (special representation)** – UPSC 2021: only statement ii true (1919 did not give all women over 21 the vote).
[0:7] - Franchise: about **14 per cent** of the population (30.1 million voters).
[0:8] - Reorganisation: **Sind** separated from Bombay; **Bihar and Orissa** split; **Burma** separated.
[0:9] - **Water** ("water supplies, irrigation and canals") – a **Provincial List** entry (→ State List entry 17). Under 1919, irrigation was already a provincial *reserved* subject (Spectrum).
[0:10] - Rocket: **"Constitutional autocracy" = GOI Act 1935**; provincial dyarchy = 1919; central dyarchy = 1935.
[0:11] > Federation and central dyarchy never worked; till 1947 the Centre ran largely as under the 1919 Act. India was governed by the **1935 Act (as adapted) until 26 January 1950**.
[0:12] # Borrowed into the Constitution
[0:13] TABLE head: From the 1935 Act | From the 1919 Act
  [0:13.0] Federal scheme, office of Governor, judiciary (Federal Court), Public Service Commissions, emergency provisions, administrative details; Instrument of Instructions → Directive Principles | Provincial dyarchy (not carried over), bicameralism and direct elections at the Centre, separate provincial budget

## SUB 1 | 1937 Elections and Congress Ministries
[1:0] - Elections **February 1937** (winter 1936-37) in **11 provinces**; Congress won **716 of 1,161** seats contested.
[1:1] - Largest party everywhere **except Punjab and Sindh**.
[1:2] TABLE head: Count | Provinces
  [1:2.0] Absolute majority – 5 | UP, CP, Bihar, Orissa, Madras
  [1:2.1] No absolute majority | Bombay (85/175 – still formed ministry), Assam (33/108), Bengal, Punjab, Sindh, NWFP
  [1:2.2] Congress ministries 1937 – 7 (no coalition) | UP, CP, Bihar, Orissa, Madras, Bombay, NWFP (Dr Khan Sahib)
  [1:2.3] 8th – Assam, 1938 | Coalition under Bardoloi → 8 of 11
  [1:2.4] No Congress ministry | Punjab – Unionist Party (Sikandar Hayat Khan); Bengal – coalition led by Fazlul Huq's Krishak Praja Party with the League; Sindh – Allah Baksh (Congress-backed)
[1:3] - Premiers: Bombay **B.G. Kher**, UP **G.B. Pant**, Madras **Rajaji**, Orissa **H.K. Mahtab**, CP **Dr Khare**, Bihar **S.K. Sinha**, NWFP **Dr Khan Sahib**.
[1:4] - **Muslim League** did poorly: about **4.4% of Muslim votes** (321,772 of 7.3 million) – **less than 6%**; 2 of 84 Muslim seats in Punjab, 3 of 33 in Sind, none in NWFP → alleged "Hindu Raj", mass-contact drive.
[1:5] - Office acceptance: AICC (March 1937, Rajendra Prasad) – conditional; **CWC Wardha, July 1937** permitted it. Gandhi: hold office "**lightly, not tightly**" – a "crown of thorns".
[1:6] - Record (28 months): prisoners released, press curbs lifted, tenancy laws, **begar** (forced labour) abolished, Bombay **Industrial (Trade) Disputes Act 1938**, Wardha education scheme / Nai Talim, prohibition.
[1:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.11-17; NextGen AP History Part 19 p.5; en.wikipedia.org 1937_Indian_provincial_elections; radhikaranjanmarxist.blogspot.com (4.4%); Spectrum pdf p.441, p.447; old app notes (Gandhi's phrase, begar)
## SUB 2 | Madras Ministry, 1937 – Andhra Angle
[2:0] - Madras polls **15-20 Feb 1937**; Justice Party almost wiped out; Pithapuram Zamindar's **People's Party** lost all seats.
[2:1] - Ministry formed **14 July 1937** – Premier (Chief Minister) **C. Rajagopalachari** (APPSC G2 2011 ×2).
[2:2] TABLE head: Andhra leader | Post
  [2:2.0] Tanguturi Prakasam | Revenue Minister (also chaired the Zamindari abolition committee)
  [2:2.1] Bezawada Gopala Reddy | Local Self-Government
  [2:2.2] V.V. Giri | Labour and Industries
  [2:2.3] Bulusu Sambamurthy | Speaker of the Assembly
[2:3] - Rajaji's **Hindi** in schools → **Periyar's** anti-Hindi agitation (1937–38).
[2:4] - **Sri Bagh Pact, 16 Nov 1937** – at **Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao's** house in Madras: Coastal–Rayalaseema accord (Rayalaseema felt left out of Rajaji's cabinet).
[2:5] > Speaker = **Sambamurthy**, not Prakasam / Tenneti / Kaleswara Rao.

## SUB 3 | Congress Reaction to the Act
[3:0] TABLE head: Who | Verdict
  [3:0.0] Congress | "Totally disappointing"; Act to be "combated inside and outside the legislatures"
  [3:0.1] Jawaharlal Nehru | "Charter of slavery"; "a car, all brakes and no engine"
  [3:0.2] M.A. Jinnah | "Thoroughly rotten, fundamentally bad and totally unacceptable"
  [3:0.3] Lord Linlithgow | Act framed as "the best way of maintaining British influence in India"
[3:1] - Sessions: **Lucknow (April 1936, Nehru)** – **rejected the Act** and **resolved to contest** the elections (office acceptance kept pending); **Faizpur (Dec 1936, Nehru)** – first rural session, agrarian programme, reaffirmed contesting; Haripura (1938, Bose) rejected the federal part.
[3:2] - Constituent Assembly demand: **M.N. Roy first (1934)**; Congress adopted it in 1935.

## SUB 4 | Resignation of the Ministries, 1939
[4:0] - **3 Sept 1939** – Viceroy **Lord Linlithgow** declared India a party to the war with **Germany** without consulting Indian leaders.
[4:1] - Congress ministries (7 provinces) **resigned Oct–Nov 1939** – not over Gandhi's arrest, the left wing, communal riots or failure in other provinces (UPSC 2012: none of a-c; A-R UPSC 2008: A and R true, R explains A).
[4:2] - League's **Day of Deliverance – 22 December 1939**; Lahore Resolution followed on **23 March 1940**.
