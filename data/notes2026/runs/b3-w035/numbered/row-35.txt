## SUB 0 | Informal Sector and Quality of Employment
[0:0] TABLE head: Concept | Meaning
  [0:0.0] Formal / organised sector | Public sector + private establishments with 10 or more hired workers; social security, higher wages
  [0:0.1] Informal / unorganised sector (enterprise) | Unincorporated private enterprises of individuals / households with fewer than 10 workers
  [0:0.2] Informal employment (worker) | Workers without social security, paid leave or dismissal notice – in any sector
[0:1] - **Unemployment and underemployment are concentrated in the unorganised sector**; it runs on **cash (currency transactions)** – the "informal, cash-intensive parts of the economy".
[0:2] - **NCEUS** (National Commission for Enterprises in the Unorganised Sector), set up **2004**, chaired by **Arjun Sengupta**.
[0:3] - Nearly **82%** of the workforce is in the informal sector and nearly **90%** is informally employed (India Employment Report 2024); 2011-12: only about **6%** in the formal sector (NCERT).
[0:4] - **AP:** informal share **62.3% rural, 45.6% urban**; overall about **55%**.
[0:5] - **Casual workers (UPSC 2021):** EPF coverage is **not** for all casual workers (depends on establishment size and wage ceiling); they are entitled to regular working hours and overtime (minimum wage rules); the Government may by notification require an establishment to **pay wages only by cheque or into the bank account** (Payment of Wages Amendment 2017) → **2 and 3 only**.
[0:6] - **India's wage policy** (minimum wages, DA) is linked to the **cost of living**.
[0:7] > APPSC Group-II 2017: NCEUS = Arjun Sengupta; WBCS: unemployment concentrated in the unorganised sector.

## SUB 1 | Jobless Growth and Employment Elasticity
[1:0] - **Jobless growth:** a widening gap between GDP growth and employment growth – more goods and services produced without proportionate jobs; NCERT dates the "disheartening development" to the **late 1990s**; employment growth was near zero again in **2004-05 to 2009-10** (the decade **2001-2010** in the AEE 2016 key).
[1:1] - Term coined by **Nick Perna** (early 1990s), not Arthur Lewis.
[1:2] - **Main reason after 1991:** reforms were **mainly confined to the formal sector** while most jobs are informal; growth led by **capital-intensive** IT and finance, not **labour-intensive manufacturing** ("**missing middle**").
[1:3] - **Post-1991 (UPSC 2020):** rural **non-farm** growth rose and **rural employment growth fell**; productivity rose in rural and urban areas; the rural share of the workforce fell → **III and IV only**.
[1:4] - **Employment elasticity** = % change in employment ÷ % change in output; **low** = output grows with little rise in jobs.
[1:5] TABLE head: Sector (1999-2000 to 2009-10) | Employment elasticity
  [1:5.0] Construction | 1.13
  [1:5.1] Finance and real estate | 0.66
  [1:5.2] Manufacturing | 0.09
  [1:5.3] Agriculture (mixed farming) | 0.04 – very low
[1:6] - About **12 million** enter the job market every year; direct factory employment fell 61% → 47% (2011-2023) as **contract workers rose to 42%** (13 Mar 2026).
[1:7] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 05 p.1; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 03 p.49; 12th Five Year Plan ch.22 (elasticities, as quoted in the PYQ explanation); app notes 3:35 sub 0-1

## SUB 3 | Female Labour Force Participation
[3:0] TABLE head: PLFS round | Female LFPR (15+, usual status)
  [3:0.0] 2017-18 | 23.3%
  [3:0.1] 2020-21 | 32.5%
  [3:0.2] 2022-23 | 37.0%
  [3:0.3] 2023-24 | 41.7%
  [3:0.4] 2025 | about 40% (rural 45.9%, urban 27.7%)
[3:1] - **Earlier decline:** female LFPR fell sharply from 2004-05 to 2017-18; the usual explanation – **rising family income (income effect) and more girls in education** (A and R both true, R explains A in UPPSC 2019; some keys say R does not fully explain A).
[3:2] - **Economic Survey 2017-18:** most women's indicators improved, but **female labour force participation and the fertility rate declined**.
[3:3] - **Rural women = 80%** of the rise in female workers (2017-18 to 2023-24) – subsistence farming, unpaid family work; the **rural-urban gap is widening** (24 Aug 2026).
[3:4] - **U-shaped hypothesis:** female LFPR falls with early income growth and rises later with education and service jobs.
[3:5] - **Feminisation of agriculture:** basic reason = **rural-to-urban migration of men**.
[3:6] - **Barriers:** social norms, unpaid care work, unsafe transport (not the expansion of service jobs).
[3:7] - PLFS 2025: LFPR about **59%**, WPR about **57%**, UR about **3%**.
[3:8] - **AP (PLFS 2023-24):** female LFPR all ages **35.8%** (India **31.7%**), 15+ **44.8%** (India 41.7%); overall LFPR 47.5% (rural 50.1, urban 41.3).
[3:9] ~~ Sources: AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.287-288; OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 04 p.84; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. ch.20 p.61-62; app notes 3:35 sub 5, 8
## SUB 4 | Quarterly Employment Survey and Organised-Sector Jobs
[4:0] - **QES** by the **Labour Bureau**: establishments with **10+ workers** in **9 sectors** – manufacturing, construction, trade, transport, accommodation and restaurants, IT/BPO, education, health, financial services (not agriculture).
[4:1] - **Manufacturing** is the largest QES employer (38.5%, Jan-Mar 2022), then **education** (21.7%).
[4:2] - **28th QES (2015, eight sectors):** only **1.35 lakh** jobs added; **IT/BPO led with about 76,000**, then textiles including apparel (72,000) and metals (37,000). Textiles led only the Oct-Dec 2015 quarter.
[4:3] - **Organised-sector employment growth declined between 1994 and 2007** (Economic Survey 2009-10); reforms reduced formal-sector employment (NCERT).
[4:4] ~~ Sources: millenniumpost.in ("2015 job growth falls to 7-year low: Labour survey", Apr 2016); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. ch.20 p.61; OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 05 p.3; app notes 3:35 sub 4

  [5:0.0] Gig economy | Engagement of independent contractors / freelancers instead of permanent workers
  [5:0.1] NITI Aayog "India's Booming Gig and Platform Economy" (June 2022) | 77 lakh gig workers (2020-21)
  [5:0.2] Projection | 2.35 crore by 2029-30 (about 6.7% of workforce)

[5:3] - **e-Shram** portal (**26 Aug 2021**): national database of **unorganised workers**; each gets a **12-digit Universal Account Number**; about **31.89 crore** registrations (Aug 2026).
