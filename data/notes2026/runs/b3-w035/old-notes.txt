## SUB 1 | Informal Sector and Quality of Employment
[35:1:0] TABLE head: Concept | Meaning
  [35:1:0.0] Formal / organised sector | Public sector + private establishments with 10 or more hired workers; social security, higher wages
  [35:1:0.1] Informal / unorganised sector (enterprise) | Unincorporated private enterprises of individuals / households with fewer than 10 workers
  [35:1:0.2] Informal employment (worker) | Workers without social security, paid leave or dismissal notice – in any sector
[35:1:1] - **Unemployment and underemployment are concentrated in the unorganised sector**; it runs on **cash (currency transactions)** – the "informal, cash-intensive parts of the economy".
[35:1:2] - **NCEUS** (National Commission for Enterprises in the Unorganised Sector), set up **2004**, chaired by **Arjun Sengupta**.
[35:1:3] - Nearly **82%** of the workforce is in the informal sector and nearly **90%** is informally employed (India Employment Report 2024); 2011-12: only about **6%** in the formal sector (NCERT).
[35:1:4] - **AP:** informal share **62.3% rural, 45.6% urban**; overall about **55%**.
[35:1:5] - **Casual workers (UPSC 2021):** EPF coverage is **not** for all casual workers (depends on establishment size and wage ceiling); they are entitled to regular working hours and overtime (minimum wage rules); the Government may by notification require an establishment to **pay wages only by cheque or into the bank account** (Payment of Wages Amendment 2017) → **2 and 3 only**.
[35:1:6] - **India's wage policy** (minimum wages, DA) is linked to the **cost of living**.
[35:1:7] > APPSC Group-II 2017: NCEUS = Arjun Sengupta; WBCS: unemployment concentrated in the unorganised sector.

## SUB 2 | Jobless Growth and Employment Elasticity
[35:2:0] - **Jobless growth:** a widening gap between GDP growth and employment growth – more goods and services produced without proportionate jobs; NCERT dates the "disheartening development" to the **late 1990s**; employment growth was near zero again in **2004-05 to 2009-10** (the decade **2001-2010** in the AEE 2016 key).
[35:2:1] - Term coined by **Nick Perna** (early 1990s), not Arthur Lewis.
[35:2:2] - **Main reason after 1991:** reforms were **mainly confined to the formal sector** while most jobs are informal; growth led by **capital-intensive** IT and finance, not **labour-intensive manufacturing** ("**missing middle**").
[35:2:3] - **Post-1991 (UPSC 2020):** rural **non-farm** growth rose and **rural employment growth fell**; productivity rose in rural and urban areas; the rural share of the workforce fell → **III and IV only**.
[35:2:4] - **Employment elasticity** = % change in employment ÷ % change in output; **low** = output grows with little rise in jobs.
[35:2:5] TABLE head: Sector (1999-2000 to 2009-10) | Employment elasticity
  [35:2:5.0] Construction | 1.13
  [35:2:5.1] Finance and real estate | 0.66
  [35:2:5.2] Manufacturing | 0.09
  [35:2:5.3] Agriculture (mixed farming) | 0.04 – very low
[35:2:6] - About **12 million** enter the job market every year; direct factory employment fell 61% → 47% (2011-2023) as **contract workers rose to 42%** (13 Mar 2026).
[35:2:7] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 05 p.1; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 03 p.49; 12th Five Year Plan ch.22 (elasticities, as quoted in the PYQ explanation); app notes 3:35 sub 0-1

## SUB 2 | MGNREGA – Entitlement, Eligibility and Objectives
[36:2:0] - **Objective:** "**enhancing livelihood security** in rural areas by providing **at least 100 days of guaranteed wage employment in a financial year** to every **household** whose **adult members volunteer to do unskilled manual work**".
[36:2:1] - **Unit = household**, not each adult: "100 days to every adult" is wrong; **150 days** is wrong for the normal guarantee.
[36:2:2] - **Eligible:** adult members of **any rural household** – not only SC/ST, BPL or backward communities.
[36:2:3] - **Unskilled** manual work only – "100 days of **skilled** labour" is not an objective.
[36:2:4] - Other aims: **durable assets** (roads, canals, ponds, wells), rural income, **women's empowerment**, inclusive growth.
[36:2:5] - Work within **5 km**; minimum wages; **legal entitlement** (statutory right, **not a Fundamental Right**); anchored in **Article 41** (DPSP).
[36:2:6] - The Act covers **all rural areas of the country** in notified phases – **not "only selected States"**.
[36:2:7] - **Drought-hit areas:** an extra **50 days** – **150 days** in a year in areas declared drought-affected (as in Feb 2019).
[36:2:8] - **Features of MGNREGS:** guaranteed wage employment, **rights-based**, rural wage employment; **nutritious food is not** a feature.
[36:2:9] - **SDG link:** NITI Aayog's **SDG India Index 2023-24** tracks MGNREGA under **SDG 1 (No Poverty)** – 99.7% of those who demanded work were offered it; Goal 1 score **72**.
[36:2:10] - **World Development Report 2014** (World Bank): "**stellar example of rural development**"; safety net in agrarian distress and **COVID-19**.
[36:2:11] > UPSC 2011 (any household), APPSC Group-II 2017 (household, 100 days), CDS 2020 (skilled labour is not an objective), APPSC Group-I 2023 (nutritious food is not a feature).
[36:2:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.57; NREGA 2005 Act text (prsindia.org PDF); drishtiias.com SDG India Index 2023-24 (via web search); app notes 3:36 sub 2-3

## SUB 0 | Public Distribution System – Aims, Evolution, AAY and Laws
[39:0:0] # Aims
[39:0:1] - **PDS** = distribution of essential commodities to a large number of people through a **network of Fair Price (ration) Shops at subsidised prices**; food procured by FCI goes to the poorer sections.
[39:0:2] - **Main aim:** **food security for the poor** – to **protect the low-income / vulnerable sections against high prices** by guaranteeing a minimum quantity at affordable prices; also checks **hoarding and black-marketing** and stabilises prices ("all of the above").
[39:0:3] - **Procurement at MSP** ensures **stable farm prices, remunerative prices to farmers and food stocks for the PDS** – all three.

## SUB 1 | National Food Security Act, 2013 – Coverage, Entitlements, Grievances
[39:1:0] # Enactment and nature
[39:1:1] - **Dates:** first an **Ordinance (5 July 2013)**; Act notified **10 Sep 2013** with effect from **5 July 2013** – the Act is of **2013** (not 2014, 2019, 2020).
[39:1:2] - **Nature:** welfare → **rights-based** approach; legal right to subsidised grain. Ministry: **Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution** (DFPD).
[39:1:3] - **Objective (long title):** **food and nutritional security** by ensuring access to adequate quality food at **affordable prices** so people can **live a life with dignity**. Distractors: "PDS Act", "FCI Act", "NFSA 2014".
[39:1:4] - **Coverage:** up to **75% of the rural** and **50% of the urban** population = about **two-thirds** of India; about **80-81 crore** people (not "50 crore"). Coverage is NOT limited to BPL families (Priority Households + AAY).
[39:1:5] - **Women:** the **eldest woman aged 18 or above** is head of household for the ration card (else the eldest male).
[39:1:6] # Entitlements
[39:1:7] TABLE head: Category | Entitlement | Issue price
  [39:1:7.0] Priority Household | 5 kg per person per month | ₹3 rice / ₹2 wheat / ₹1 coarse grain
  [39:1:7.1] Antyodaya Anna Yojana | 35 kg per household per month | Same; now free
  [39:1:7.2] Pregnant women and lactating mothers | Free meal during pregnancy and 6 months after + maternity benefit of at least ₹6,000 | –
  [39:1:7.3] Children 6 months-6 years | Take-home ration or hot meal through ICDS/anganwadi | –
  [39:1:7.4] Children 6-14 years | Mid-day meal / PM POSHAN | –

[39:1:9] - **Calorie trap (UPSC 2018):** the "take-home ration of 1,600 calories" for pregnant women is not in the Act (Schedule II norm is 600 kcal) – so only the eldest-woman statement is right.
[39:1:10] - **Issue prices** ₹3/2/1 **not revised since July 2013** while MSP and subsidy keep rising.
[39:1:11] - **Draft NFS (Amendment) Bill 2026** (DFPD, 24 June 2026): AAY → **7 kg per person, capped at 35 kg**; amends the first proviso to Sec 3(1); ends small households getting more grain per head.

## SUB 2 | Food Security – Concept, Dimensions and Entitlements
[39:2:0] # Definition
[39:2:1] - **World Food Summit 1996 / FAO:** food security exists when **all people, at all times**, have **physical and economic access** to **sufficient, safe and nutritious food** for an active and healthy life.
[39:2:2] - Food security does NOT mean access to "**the food of their choice**".
[39:2:3] # Dimensions
[39:2:4] TABLE head: Dimension | Meaning
  [39:2:4.0] Availability | Production + imports + previous years' stock in government granaries
  [39:2:4.1] Accessibility | Food within reach of every person
  [39:2:4.2] Affordability | Enough money to buy sufficient, safe, nutritious food
  [39:2:4.3] Absorption / utilisation | Body's ability to use the food
  [39:2:4.4] Stability | Access at all times
[39:2:5] - **NOT dimensions:** **Accountability**, price, **quantity of exports**.
[39:2:6] # National vs household
[39:2:7] TABLE head: Level | Meaning
  [39:2:7.0] National | Aggregate production + buffer stock (sufficient food stock)
  [39:2:7.1] Household | Each household can actually access and afford food
[39:2:8] - "**National but not household food security**" = **sufficient food stock, but not all households can get it** – hence PDS and NFSA.
[39:2:9] - Food security is needed **even in surplus years**: starvation comes from **unequal distribution and lack of access** (A-R: both true, R explains A).
[39:2:10] # Entitlement approach
[39:2:11] - **Amartya Sen**, *Poverty and Famines* (**1981**): famines come from a **collapse of entitlements** (command over food through income, exchange and transfers), not merely a fall in food availability; Sen added "**access**" to the 1970s "availability" idea.
[39:2:12] - **Per capita norms:** the APPSC 2011 key credits the **440 g/day foodgrain norm to FAO**; Indian nutrition bodies use about 400-420 g (ICMR/NIN) – treat 440 g as an exam figure only.
[39:2:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 03 p.6-7 (availability, accessibility, affordability); OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 05 p.25 (World Food Summit, Sen's access); app notes 3:39 sub 0, 1

## SUB 3 | Rurbanisation – PURA and SPMRM
[40:3:0] # PURA
[40:3:1] - **PURA** = **Provision of Urban Amenities in Rural Areas** – **Dr A.P.J. Abdul Kalam** (explained on the eve of the **54th Republic Day, 2003**); elaborated in ***Target 3 Billion*** (co-author **Srijan Pal Singh**).
[40:3:2] - Aim: **economic upliftment of villages and poverty eradication**; **bridging the rural-urban divide**. Joint AP pilot: Miryalaguda (2004).
[40:3:3] # Shyama Prasad Mukherji Rurban Mission (SPMRM)
[40:3:4] - **Rurban** (not "Rural/Revival") Mission; launched **21 Feb 2016** (not 2015) at **Kurubhat, Rajnandgaon (Chhattisgarh)**; successor of PURA.
[40:3:5] - Aim: **300 Rurban clusters** – "a rural soul with urban amenities"; curb **distress migration**; make rural areas **socially, economically and physically sustainable** regions.
[40:3:6] - Objectives: **stimulate local economic development**, enhance basic services, create well-planned clusters (not literacy, sex ratio or free power).
[40:3:7] - Funding: **convergence** (MGNREGA, PMGSY, SBM, PMAY) + **Critical Gap Funding up to 30%**; Centre:State **60:40**, **90:10** for NE and Himalayan States.
[40:3:8] - Cluster: contiguous villages of **25,000-50,000** population (plain/coastal). **Rurban cluster** = rural areas with rising density, non-farm jobs, growing economic activity.
[40:3:9] - AP: **6 clusters** in the second phase (APPSC key); MoRD classified **359 AP panchayats** under Rurban clusters (Mar 2026).
[40:3:10] - SPMRM does NOT replace Gram Panchayats with urban local bodies.
