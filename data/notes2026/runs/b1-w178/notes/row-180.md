=== Local Self-Government – Madras 1688, Mayo 1870, Ripon 1882
# Milestones
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| **29 Sep 1688** | **Madras** – **first Municipal Corporation** in India (charter 30 Dec 1687); now Greater Chennai Corporation, the oldest |
| **1726** | Corporations at **Bombay and Calcutta** |
| **1817** | **Lottery Committee**, Calcutta – **town planning** with lottery funds (Strand Road, Dalhousie Square) |
| 1864-68 | First local bodies – nominated members, headed by District Magistrates; seen as tax tools |
| **1870** | **Mayo's Resolution** – financial decentralisation |
| **1882** | **Ripon's Resolution (18 May 1882)** – "**Magna Carta**" of LSG |
| 1872 | Bombay Municipal Act – half the Corporation members elected by ratepayers; drafted and backed by citizens led by **Pherozeshah Mehta** |
| 1899 | **Calcutta Corporation (Municipal) Act – Curzon**: **reduced the elected members**, increased nominated ones; **28 members resigned** in protest |
| 1907-09 | Royal Commission on Decentralisation (chairman **C.E.H. Hobhouse**) – stressed **village panchayats**; only recommendations |
| 1919 | LSG a **transferred** subject under dyarchy (finance stayed reserved) |
| **1924** | **Cantonments Act** – passed by the **Central legislature** |
| 1935 | LSG a **provincial** subject |
- **Father of Local Self-Government in India = Lord Ripon** (Viceroy 1880-84) – not Mayo, Lytton, Curzon, Canning, Dalhousie, Cornwallis or Macaulay.
- **Not correctly matched (UPRO 2017):** Local self-government – **Lytton** ✘ (it is Ripon); Doctrine of Lapse – Dalhousie ✔; Permanent Settlement – Cornwallis ✔.
- **APPSC G2 2025 statements:** Cantonments Act 1924 by the Central legislature, Madras corporation 1688, Ripon father of LSG – **all three correct**.
# Mayo vs Ripon
| Mayo (1869-72) | Ripon (1880-84) |
|---|---|
| **Resolution 1870** | **Resolution 1882** |
| **First step to bifurcate Central and Provincial finances**: provinces got fixed grants for police, jails, education, medical services, roads, to run as they liked, and were told to raise **local taxes** | Local boards with **non-official majority**, elected where possible; non-official chairmen; official control only to revise, not dictate |
| "Local interest, supervision and care are necessary" for funds on education, sanitation, medical relief, local works | Local bodies as an instrument of **political and popular education** |
| Also statistical survey and **first census (1872)** | **Father of LSG**; also repealed Vernacular Press Act, Hunter Commission (1882), Ilbert Bill |
- **Mayo's Resolution 1870 statements (State PSC):** first step bifurcating central/provincial finances; provinces empowered to administer certain services; meant to correct the imbalance and meet the actual needs of provinces → **all four correct**.
- **After Ripon:** elected members stayed a **minority** in district boards, franchise narrow, bodies **short of money and under official control** (could be suspended or superseded).
- **Curzon and LSG (UPRO 2023):** Curzon's **Calcutta Municipal Act 1899** is the safe statement; he spoke of British rule as a message "carved in granite" (1904).
> Madras 1688 → Bombay/Calcutta 1726 → Mayo 1870 → Ripon 1882 → Hobhouse 1907-09 → 1919 transferred → 1924 Cantonments → 1935 provincial.

=== ICS Indianisation – First Indians, Age Limits, Commissions, Simultaneous Exam
# Chronology
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1853 | Charter Act – open competition ends Directors' patronage |
| 1854 | Macaulay Committee – exam in **London**; preference for Oxford/Cambridge graduates |
| 1861 | Indian Civil Service Act – posts reserved for covenanted servants; limited Indian entry, mainly **uncovenanted** posts (7 years' residence, local-language exam) |
| **1863/1864** | **Satyendranath Tagore** – **first Indian in the ICS** (qualified 1863, joined 1864; posted to the **Bombay Presidency**) |
| 1869 | **Surendranath Banerjea** selected; disqualified over age, restored by court, then **dismissed** for a minor judicial error |
| 1878 | **Lytton** cut the maximum age to **19** |
| 1878-79 | **Statutory Civil Service** (Lytton) – **one-sixth** of covenanted posts for Indians of high families by nomination; abolished |
| 1885 | INC demands higher age and **simultaneous exams** from its **first session** |
| **1886-87** | **Aitchison Commission** (Dufferin) |
| 1892 | Age raised to **23** (21-23) after Aitchison |
| 1906 | Age limits raised to **22-24** |
| 1912-15 | Islington Commission (Hardinge II) – Indianisation of higher posts |
| 1919 | **Montford Report / GoI Act 1919** – separate ICS exam **in India** |
| **1922** | Exam held simultaneously in India (Allahabad, Feb 1922) and England |
| 1923-24 | **Lee Commission** (Reading) |
| **1 Oct 1926** | Public Service Commission set up (chairman Sir Ross Barker) |
| 1935 | GoI Act 1935 – **Federal PSC** + Provincial PSCs |
| **1 Jan 1941** | **Indians (617) outnumbered Europeans (585)** in the ICS |
| 1947 / 1950 | ICS → **IAS**; UPSC |
- **Age ladder:** **23** (1859) → 22 (1860) → 21 (1866) → **19** (1878) → **23** (1892) → **24** (22-24, 1906). "Raised to 24" = **1906**, not 1892.
- **Dismissed / "unjustly turned out" of the ICS:** **Surendranath Banerjea** – not Satyendranath Tagore, R.C. Dutt or Subhas Bose (Bose resigned in 1921).
- **Sardar Patel** called the civil service the **"Steel Frame"** of India.
# Commissions
| Commission | Year | Viceroy | Main point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aitchison** | 1886 | **Dufferin** | Dropped covenanted/uncovenanted; **Imperial, Provincial, Subordinate** services; strengthened the provincial service; abolished Statutory Civil Service; age 23; **opposed simultaneous exams** |
| Islington | 1912-15 | Hardinge II | **Indianisation** of higher posts (25% reserved) |
| **Lee** | 1923-24 | **Reading** | **50:50 Indian-European parity** in ICS within 15 years; independent **Public Service Commission** |
- **Aitchison suggested (APPSC AEE):** **c** (strengthen provincial services) and **d** (Imperial, Provincial, Subordinate) – not **a** (high-family nominees = Lytton's Statutory Civil Service, which it abolished) or **b** (it rejected simultaneous exams) → **"c and d"**.
- **Formation of the "Civil Service of India" (Imperial/Provincial/Subordinate):** the **Aitchison** Commission – not Whitley (labour), Butler (princely states) or Sapru.
- **Equalisation of Indians and Europeans in the ICS:** **Lee Commission** – not Simon, Cabinet Mission or the Queen's Proclamation.
- **Simultaneous exam recommended by:** the **Montagu-Chelmsford Report** – Aitchison opposed it; Hobhouse was on decentralisation.
> Aitchison = three-tier services; Islington = Indianisation; Lee = 50:50 + PSC. PSC 1926 came after Lee (1924). Simultaneous exam: demanded 1885, provided by 1919 Act, held 1922.

=== Ilbert Bill, 1883 – Indian Judges and European Offenders
- **Viceroy:** **Ripon**; drafted by **Law Member C.P. Ilbert**.
- **Aim:** remove the **disqualification of Indian magistrates** – let Indian **district magistrates and sessions judges** try **European British subjects** in **criminal cases**; Indians and Europeans **on par in criminal jurisdiction**.
- **Not:** restrictions on carrying arms (Arms Act 1878), on vernacular newspapers (Vernacular Press Act 1878), civil service exams in India, or the cotton import duty.
- **Opposition:** fierce European agitation = the **"White Mutiny"**.
- **Outcome (1884):** diluted – a European tried by an Indian judge could demand a **jury of at least half Europeans**.
- **Indian Association** **backed** the Bill; Indians learnt the value of organised all-India agitation.
> Ilbert Bill = racial equality in **criminal** jurisdiction; asked by UPSC 2003 and 2013.

=== Judiciary – Hastings, Cornwallis Code, High Courts
| Governor-General / Act | Reform |
|---|---|
| **Warren Hastings (1772)** | District **Diwani Adalat** (civil, under the **Collector**) and **Faujdari Adalat** (criminal); Sadar Diwani and Sadar Nizamat Adalats |
| Regulating Act 1773 | **Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774)**; first Chief Justice **Sir Elijah Impey** |
| **Cornwallis Code 1793** | **Collector lost judicial powers** (only revenue collector); post of **District Judge**; separation of revenue and justice; 4 provincial courts of appeal at **Calcutta, Dacca, Murshidabad, Patna** |
| Bentinck | **Abolished** provincial courts of appeal and circuit (work to Commissioners); separate Sadar courts at **Allahabad**; vernacular/English replaced **Persian** |
- **Why 1793:** Cornwallis was **alarmed at the power concentrated in the Collector** and kept judicial and revenue work apart (UPSC 2010).
- **Established the judicial organisation in India:** **Lord Cornwallis** (Cornwallis Code) – not Mayo, Curzon or Attlee.
| Code / Act | Year |
|---|---|
| Judicial Committee of the Privy Council | 1833 |
| Code of Civil Procedure | **1859** |
| Indian Penal Code | **1860** |
| Code of Criminal Procedure | **1861** |
| Indian High Courts Act | **1861** – High Courts replaced the Supreme Courts and Sadar Adalats |
| **High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras** | **1862**, by **Letters Patent** under Crown authority (Canning) |
| Allahabad High Court | 1866 |
| Federal Court | GoI Act 1935 (set up 1937) |
- **⚠ Year of the first High Courts:** **1862** (NextGen Rocket, PMF); Spectrum prints **1865** – a PYQ offering only 1853/1856/1858/1865 needs **1862** added.
- **AP angle:** the **Andhra petitions** of the 1850s asked for trained judges, police separate from revenue officials and Indians in higher posts. Jun 2026: AP approved **Rs 130.38 crore** for phase-1 of the **AP Judicial Academy**.
> Hastings = Collector **gets** the civil adalat; Cornwallis = Collector **loses** judicial powers; Bentinck = **abolishes** appeal courts.

=== Madras Presidency Rural Local Boards – Cesses and Taluk Boards
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| **1863** | **Madras Education Cess Act** (Act VI of 1863) – school cess; the **first enactment for rural local purposes** |
| 1864 / 1866 | Village cess (village establishment) / District Roads Cess Act |
| **1871** | **Madras Local Funds Act** – Local Fund Boards first constituted; cess of **one anna per rupee of land revenue** for roads, schools, hospitals; repealed the 1863 and 1866 cesses; two-thirds of the land cess for roads |
| 1878 | Madras Act I of 1878 – local cess raised from one to **two annas** (Malabar) |
| 1884 | Madras Local Boards Act – three tiers: **district board, taluk board, union** |
| 1920 | Madras Local Boards Act + Madras Village Panchayats Act – three tiers kept |
| **1934** | Madras Local Boards (Amendment) – **taluk boards abolished**; reason usually given: **serious financial problems** (too weak beside the district boards); elementary schools went back to district boards |
> AP Panchayat Secretary angle: 1863 education cess → 1871 one anna → 1884 three tiers → 1934 taluk boards abolished.

=== Company-era Civil Service – Cornwallis and Fort William College
- **Lord Cornwallis** (1786-93) = **"Father of Civil Services in India"**: created the **Covenanted Civil Service**; **raised salaries**, banned **private trade and gifts**, promotion by **seniority**.
- **Wellesley** ("Bengal Tiger") set up the **College of Fort William, Calcutta, in 1800** (minute of 10 July 1800; PMF gives 24 Nov 1800) **to train British civilians (Company recruits) for administration** – languages (Persian, Hindustani), law, literature.
  - **UPSC 2020:** purpose = **train British civilians for administrative purposes** – not a Directors' order (they **disapproved**), not oriental revival, not jobs for William Carey (who taught Bengali there).
  - **UPRO 2021:** "founded 10 **June** 1800" ✘; "to teach Indian languages to British officers" ✔ → **only 2**.
- **1806:** replaced by the **East India College, Haileybury** (England) – law, political economy, Indian languages.
- **1833:** Charter Act – Law Commission under **T.B. Macaulay**; codification. **1853:** Charter Act ended Directors' **patronage** (open competition; Trevelyan, Robert Lowe); power passed to the Crown only in 1858.
- **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** (1832, evidence before Parliament) = first Indian to **demand** Indians in higher posts. Bentinck = modern Western education.
- ICS → **IAS** (direct successor).

=== Police and Army
# Police
| Introducer | Reform |
|---|---|
| **Cornwallis (1791-93)** | Took police duties from **zamindars**; **thanas** (circles of **20-30 sq miles**) under an Indian **daroga**; **kotwal** kept in **towns** |
| Munro – Judicial Commission 1814-18 | Police under the **Collector**; minor crimes tried by **village officers** (village munsif) |
| Police Commission 1860 → **Police Act 1861** (Canning) | **Royal Irish Constabulary** model; IG (province), DIG (range), SP (district); meant to crush dissent |
| **Curzon – Police Commission 1902-03** | Chairman **Sir Andrew Fraser**; CID in provinces, Central Intelligence Bureau |
- **Daroga** = the local face of the **"aura and authority of Company Bahadur"** (UPRO 2021); appointed and supervised by the Magistrate; notorious for misuse of power – not the kotwal, amala or zamindar.
- **Thomas Munro:** President of the Judicial Commission (1814-18); **Governor of Madras 1820-27**; extended **Ryotwari**. Madras Presidency governed Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema.
# Army after 1857
| Body | Year | Point |
|---|---|---|
| **Peel Commission** | **1859** | More European troops: **Bengal 1 European : 2 Indians**; **Madras/Bombay 1 : 3** (ratio finally fixed at 2 : 5 there); artillery with Europeans |
| Eden Commission | 1879 (Lytton) | **Amalgamate** the three presidency armies |
| Presidency armies abolished | **1 April 1895** | One Indian Army under four commands |
- **Policy:** "balance and counterpoise", martial-race theory (Sikhs, Gurkhas, Pathans); no Indian officers till 1918.
