## SUB 0 | Railways – First Lines, Purpose and the Guarantee System
[0:0] # Firsts
[0:1] TABLE head: Milestone | Year | Fact
  [0:1.0] First passenger train | 16 April 1853 | Bori Bunder (Bombay) → Thane, 34 km, 14 coaches, 3 locomotives; built by the Great Indian Peninsula Railway Company
  [0:1.1] Dalhousie's Railway Minute | 1853 | Trunk-line network for military, commercial and administrative purposes
  [0:1.2] First line in Eastern India / West Bengal | 1854 | Howrah → Hooghly
  [0:1.3] First line in North India | – | Delhi → Agra
  [0:1.4] First line in South India | 1 July 1856 | Royapuram (Madras) → Wallajah Road (Arcot); Madras Railway Company
  [0:1.5] First railway in Andhra | 1862 | Puttur–Renigunta
  [0:1.6] Passenger trains in England | 1825 | Stockton and Darlington Railway
[0:2] - **Governor-General:** **Lord Dalhousie** – railways laid in his time; he is linked with the first train (not Bentinck, Ripon, Curzon).
[0:3] - **Credit:** railways, post and telegraph = **Dalhousie**; **state railways = Mayo**.
[0:4] # Who built them and why
[0:5] - **Initially built and run by private British companies** under the **guarantee system** – a **5% minimum return** on capital, **paid from Indian revenues** whether or not the lines made a profit; the state built lines itself from **1869**.
[0:6] - **Official reasons:** faster movement, communication, administration, **troop movement**.
[0:7] - **Colonial economic purpose:** carry **raw materials to the ports** and **British goods into Indian markets**; market for British capital goods.
[0:8] - **Unintended effects:** a **national market**, shared travel across castes, faster nationalist mobilisation.
[0:9] - **Karl Marx (1853):** the railway system would become in India "truly the **forerunner of modern industry**".
[0:10] - **Indian Railways** – the **biggest enterprise of the Government of India** today.
[0:11] - Nationalist economic critique: **Indian capitalists** demanded "**Indian domination of Indian markets**".
[0:12] # Railways in Hyderabad State
[0:13] - **Wadi–Secunderabad line** (Nizam's Guaranteed State Railway): built **1871–74**, opened **9 October 1874** (NextGen says 1878; printed key 1872 – both wrong).
[0:14] - **Chanda Railway agitation (1883):** protest against extending the **Nizam's State Railway** to Chanda (Chandrapur) on terms favouring a London company; led by **Dr Aghorenath Chattopadhyaya** (Principal) – deported **20 May 1883**; the first public political agitation in Hyderabad.

## SUB 3 | Press Laws and the Vernacular Press Act, 1878
[3:0] # Press laws in sequence
[3:1] TABLE head: Law | Year | Governor-General | Provision
  [3:1.0] Censorship of Press Act | 1799 | Wellesley | Wartime pre-censorship; papers to print names of printer, editor and proprietor (the first press regulations)
  [3:1.1] Licensing Regulations | 1823 | John Adams (acting) | Press without licence a penal offence; Mirat-ul-Akhbar stopped
  [3:1.2] Press Act (Metcalfe Act) | 1835 | Charles Metcalfe | Repealed 1823 rules – "liberator of the Indian press"
  [3:1.3] Licensing Act | 1857 | Canning | Emergency of the revolt
  [3:1.4] Registration Act | 1867 | John Lawrence | Printer, publisher and place on every book/paper; copy to government
  [3:1.5] Sedition, Sec. 124A IPC | 1870 | Mayo | "Exciting disaffection"
  [3:1.6] Vernacular Press Act | 1878 | Lytton | Gagging Act
  [3:1.7] Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act | 1908 | Minto II | Magistrates could seize presses
  [3:1.8] Indian Press Act | 1910 | Minto II | Revived the worst VPA features – security deposits
[3:2] - **Acts to control the press:** **1835, 1867, 1878, 1908**.
[3:3] - Press censorship was **primarily implemented** by **Wellesley** (1799); Hastings relaxed it in 1818.
[3:4] - 1908 and 1910 Acts are both Minto II era but **not cause and effect**.
[3:5] - Annie Besant's papers had to deposit Rs 2,000, then Rs 10,000 under the Press Act (1916–17).
[3:6] # Vernacular Press Act, 1878
[3:7] - Passed **14 March 1878** under **Lord Lytton**, **modelled on the Irish Press Laws**, to **control Indian-language newspapers** (not to encourage them).
[3:8] - **Background:** criticism of Lytton over the **1876–77 famine** and the costly **Delhi Durbar (1877)**.
[3:9] - **Provisions:** the **district magistrate** (without prior government permission) could make printers/publishers sign a **bond**, demand **forfeitable security**, **seize presses** for repeat offences; **no appeal** to a court; exemption only by **submitting proofs to a government censor**.
[3:10] - **Discriminatory:** applied **only to vernacular papers**, not English ones; about 20 English and 200 vernacular papers in 1878 (textbook figure).
[3:11] - **Evasion:** **Amrita Bazar Patrika** turned into an **English paper overnight**.
[3:12] - Proceedings under it: Som Prakash, Bharat Mihir, Dacca Prakash, Samachar.
[3:13] - **Repeal:** **1882** by **Lord Ripon**; pre-censorship dropped, a **press commissioner** appointed.

## SUB 5 | Telegraph, Post and the Press Today
[5:0] # Telegraph and post
[5:1] - **First telegraph line:** Calcutta → **Diamond Harbour**, **W.B. O'Shaughnessy** (experimental 1850; opened **1851**); Calcutta–Agra 1853.
[5:2] - Telegraph trunk lines linked Calcutta, Bombay, Madras and Peshawar (Dalhousie); **India–Europe telegraph 1865** (John Lawrence, 1864–69).
[5:3] - **Post Office Act 1854 (Dalhousie):** **postage stamps** and a **uniform rate** (half anna a letter) regardless of distance.
[5:4] - **First stamps:** **Scinde Dawk (1852)**, Karachi; the first all-India stamps (1854) were printed at the **Survey of India** (Surveyor General's) office, Calcutta.
[5:5] # Press today (as of 2026)
[5:6] - **Press and Registration of Periodicals Act, 2023** replaced the **1867** Act (in force 1 March 2024); the RNI is now the **Press Registrar General of India**.
[5:7] - **World Press Freedom Index 2026 (RSF, 30 April 2026):** India **157th of 180**.
[5:8] - **Press Council** (Press Council Act, **1978**) has no punitive powers.
