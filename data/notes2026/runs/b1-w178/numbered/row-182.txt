## SUB 0 | Military Grievances – the Sepoys Start the Revolt
[0:0] - The revolt **began as a mutiny of the sepoys** (not zamindars, peasants or plantation workers); **discontent of soldiers** was the first trigger; civilians joined later.
[0:1] TABLE head: Grievance | Detail
  [0:1.0] General Service Enlistment Act 1856 (Canning) | New Bengal Army recruits to serve overseas – crossing the sea meant loss of caste
  [0:1.1] Bhatta | Foreign service allowance stopped for service in Sindh and Punjab
  [0:1.2] Promotion | An Indian could rise no higher than Subedar
  [0:1.3] Pay | Sepoy paid much less than his British counterpart of the same rank
  [0:1.4] Caste marks | Ban on caste and sectarian marks, beards, turbans
  [0:1.5] Post Office Act 1856 | Free postage of sepoys withdrawn
  [0:1.6] Troop ratio | About 2,32,000 sepoys : 45,000 Europeans (about 5:1); Indians about 86% of the army
  [0:1.7] Awadh | "Nursery of the Bengal Army"; the largest number of rebel sepoys came from Oudh (over 75,000)
[0:2] - Sepoys = **"peasants in uniform"** – they carried rural grievances into the army.
[0:3] - European troops had been drawn off for the **Crimean and Persian wars**.
[0:4] - **Not a cause:** the **inefficiency of the officers** (BPSC 2018).
[0:5] - **Earlier mutinies:** Bengal 1764; **Vellore 1806** (new dress code, ban on caste marks and earrings); **Barrackpore Nov 1824** (**47th NI** refused to go to **Burma by sea**); 38th NI 1852; 1844 battalions over batta. The **Santhal revolt 1855-56** was a **civil**, not a sepoy, revolt.

## SUB 1 | Immediate Cause – Enfield Rifle and Greased Cartridges
[1:0] - **Immediate cause:** the new **Enfield rifle**; its cartridge paper, reportedly greased with **cow and pig fat**, had to be **bitten off** – offended Hindus and Muslims alike → suspicion that the British were **interfering in religion** and converting them.
[1:1] - Enfield **replaced the Brown Bess** musket; introduced **December 1856** (app notes; some accounts say late 1856 or Jan 1857); training at **Dum Dum, Ambala and Sialkot**.
[1:2] - **Bone-dust rumour:** the Company's **flour (atta)** was said to be mixed with **bone dust**.
[1:3] - **Chapatis** passed from village to village across **North India**; symbols of the revolt = **lotus and chapati**.
[1:4] TABLE head: Event | Date
  [1:4.0] 19th NI, Berhampur – refused cartridges | Feb 1857
  [1:4.1] Mangal Pandey (34th BNI), Barrackpore | 29 March 1857; hanged 8 April 1857
  [1:4.2] 85 sepoys of the 3rd Native Cavalry, Meerut – 10 years' prison | 9 May 1857
  [1:4.3] Meerut rising | 10 May 1857
  [1:4.4] Rebels at Delhi proclaim Bahadur Shah II Shahenshah-e-Hindustan | 11-12 May 1857
[1:5] - **First martyr:** **Mangal Pandey**.

## SUB 2 | Socio-religious Causes
[2:0] TABLE head: Measure | Year | Note
  [2:0.0] Abolition of Sati | 1829 | William Bentinck (not Dalhousie) – resented by orthodox opinion
  [2:0.1] Religious (Caste) Disabilities Act / Lex Loci | 1850 | A convert to Christianity could inherit ancestral property (Spectrum misprints 1856)
  [2:0.2] Hindu Widow Remarriage Act | 1856 | Orthodoxy resented it
[2:1] - **Christian missionaries** preached under **police protection**; Western education, **railways and the telegraph** fed fears of mass conversion.
[2:2] - After 1858 the Crown promised **religious neutrality**.

## SUB 3 | Economic, Political and Administrative Causes
[3:0] TABLE head: Class / cause | Grievance
  [3:0.0] Peasants | High, rigid land revenue → debt → land lost to moneylenders and traders
  [3:0.1] Taluqdars of Awadh | About 21,000 dispossessed (Summary Settlement 1856)
  [3:0.2] Estate owners | Inam Commission (1852, Bombay) – about 20,000 estates confiscated
  [3:0.3] Artisans | British machine goods duty-free, Indian goods heavily taxed; loss of princely patronage
  [3:0.4] Annexed courts | Officials, retainers and soldiers thrown out of work
  [3:0.5] Administration | Corruption in police and lower courts – courts favoured the rich; Indians excluded from high posts
[3:1] - **"Absentee sovereignty"** and **"drain of wealth"** – transfer of resources to Britain.
[3:2] - Lapse (Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur), Awadh "misgovernance" (Wajid Ali Shah deposed), Mughal title.
