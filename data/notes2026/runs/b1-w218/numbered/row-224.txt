## SUB 0 | Government of India Act, 1919 – Features
[0:0] # Origin
[0:1] - **Montagu Declaration, 20 August 1917:** "progressive realisation of **responsible government** in India as an integral part of the British Empire" – first official promise; pressure mainly from the **Home Rule Leagues**.
[0:2] - **Edwin Montagu** = Secretary of State for India; **Lord Chelmsford** = Viceroy; **Montagu-Chelmsford Report, 1918** → **basis of the GoI Act, 1919** (not 1909, 1935 or 1947). The proposals were **constitutional reforms** (UPSC 2016).
[0:3] - Passed **1919**, in force **1921**; British PM: **David Lloyd George** (1916-22).
[0:4] - **Not** the Morley-Minto Act (that is the **Indian Councils Act, 1909**).
[0:5] # Main features
[0:6] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [0:6.0] Subject lists | Central and provincial subjects separated ("Devolution Rules") – jurisdiction of Centre and provinces clearly defined; delegation of authority to provinces
  [0:6.1] Structure | Still centralised and unitary despite devolution
  [0:6.2] Provinces | Dyarchy (reserved / transferred); provincial budgets separated from the central budget
  [0:6.3] Centre | Bicameral for the first time: Council of State (60) + Legislative Assembly (145, 104 elected)
  [0:6.4] Elections | Direct elections for the first time (1909 = indirect); franchise limited by property, tax, education
  [0:6.5] Electorates | Communal representation extended – separate electorates for Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans (Muslim separate electorates = 1909)
  [0:6.6] Executive Council | 3 of the 6 members (other than C-in-C) Indian
  [0:6.7] High Commissioner | New office of High Commissioner for India, London (Spectrum: tenure six years); first holder Sir William Meyer
  [0:6.8] PSC | Section 96(C) → Public Service Commission; set up 1926 (Lee Commission)
  [0:6.9] Review | Statutory commission after 10 years → Simon Commission (1927, two years early)
  [0:6.10] Princes | Chamber of Princes (Narendra Mandal), 1921
[0:7] - **Statements (CDS 2002):** objectives = **dyarchy in provinces + extension of provincial government**; "power to local government" is not the objective.
[0:8] - **IAS 2012 / CDS 2010:** principal features = dyarchy, **devolution of legislative authority** to provinces, **expanded and reconstituted legislatures**; separate electorates for Muslims are **not** a 1919 feature.
[0:9] - **A-R (UP UDA 1998):** "structure remained unitary and central" – **true** (Laxmikanth, Spectrum); "large part of authority delegated to provinces" – true, but it does not explain A.
[0:10] - **Governor's tenure:** the Act fixed no term; governors (like the Governor-General) were appointed for **five years** by practice.
[0:11] # Reactions
[0:12] - Congress special **Bombay session, Aug 1918 (Hasan Imam)** condemned the scheme; **Amritsar (1919)**: "inadequate, unsatisfactory and disappointing".
[0:13] - Moderates under **Surendranath Banerjee** (with Sapru, Srinivasa Sastri, Jayakar) left and formed the **Indian (National) Liberal Federation** (1919) – not the Swarajya Party.
[0:14] - **C.R. Das + Tilak** rejected; **Gandhi + Malaviya**: give a fair trial; **Krishna Patrika**: "giving with the right hand, taking with the left".
[0:15] - **AP angle:** **Nyapati Subbarao**'s 27-member Andhra delegation met Montagu at Madras for an **Andhra province**; **Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao** presided over the Special Andhra Mahasabha, **Guntur, 17 Aug 1918**.

## SUB 1 | Provincial Dyarchy – Reserved and Transferred Subjects
[1:0] # Meaning
[1:1] - **Dyarchy (diarchy):** "double rule" (Greek *di-arche*) – the **subjects delegated to the provinces** were divided into two categories, **reserved** and **transferred** (UPSC 2017 – not two central houses, not Centre-State, not London-Delhi).
[1:2] - **Introduced by:** the **Government of India Act, 1919** = **Montagu-Chelmsford (Mont-Ford) Reforms** – in the **provinces** (not 1861, 1892, 1909 or 1935; not the Morley-Minto Reforms or the Simon plan).
[1:3] - **Aim:** partial **responsible government** in the provinces – the transferred half was run by ministers answerable to the legislature (so A-R "dyarchy = division of administration in two sections; introduced to bring responsible government in provinces": both true, R explains A).
[1:4] - **Provinces gained power:** the 1919 Act, not the 1892/1909 Acts or the Gandhi-Irwin Pact, gave provincial governments more powers under dyarchy (APPSC G1 2008).
[1:5] # Reserved vs transferred
[1:6] TABLE head: Subjects | Examples | Run by | Answerable to
  [1:6.0] Reserved | Administration of justice, police, land revenue, finance, prisons, irrigation, control of the press | Governor + Executive Council | Not the legislature
  [1:6.1] Transferred | Local self-government, education, public health, agriculture | Governor + Ministers | Provincial legislative council
[1:7] - UPSC 2022 reserved set = **justice, land revenue, police (1, 3, 4)**; **local self-government was transferred**.
[1:8] - Flaw: ministers held transferred subjects but **finance stayed reserved**; the experiment was "largely unsuccessful".
[1:9] - **Dyarchy at the Centre** = the **1935 Act** (never worked); dyarchy in the **provinces** = **1919**; the 1935 Act abolished provincial dyarchy.

## SUB 2 | Rowlatt Act, 1919
[2:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [2:0.0] Official name | Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919
  [2:0.1] Passed | March 1919 (not 1917, 1921, 1923)
  [2:0.2] Basis | Rowlatt (Sedition) Committee under Sir Sidney Rowlatt; replaced the wartime Defence of India Act with a permanent law
  [2:0.3] Provisions | Arrest on suspicion, detention without trial (up to two years), trial without jury
  [2:0.4] Popular names | "Black Act" / "Black Bill" (Gandhi: "Black Law"); "No Vakil, No Appeal, No Daleel"
  [2:0.5] Response | Rowlatt Satyagraha – nationwide hartal 6 April 1919; Jallianwala Bagh, 13 April 1919; satyagraha suspended 18 April 1919
[2:1] - **"A hungry man being offered stones":** NextGen – the **Rowlatt Act** came when Indians expected reward for war help; "this Act was like giving stones to a hungry man".
[2:2] - Not Pitt's India Act, the Indian Arms Act or the Ilbert Bill.
[2:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.56, p.73; Spectrum (Ahir) p.352-353; PMF Modern History 04 p.136; Rocket Indian History 02 p.92
## SUB 3 | Working of the 1919 Act – Elections and Legislatures
[3:0] # Madras, 1920
[3:1] - First elections **1920** under the 1919 Act; **Congress boycotted** (Non-Cooperation).
[3:2] - **Justice Party won 63 seats** (APPSC G2 Mains 2017 ×2) → first ministry under **A. Subbarayalu Reddiar** (Dec 1920), then **Raja of Panagal** (1921).
[3:3] # Central Legislative Assembly
[3:4] - Elections under the 1919 Act: **1923, 1926, 1930, 1934, 1945** – **last in 1945**; **1937** elections (provincial) were under the **1935 Act** (so RAS 2018: 1926 and 1945 only).
[3:5] - **Swaraj Party** won **42 of 104** elected seats (1923); worked with independents like **Jinnah** and Malaviya.
[3:6] - **Vithalbhai Patel** (Swarajist) elected **President (Speaker)** of the Central Legislative Assembly in **August 1925** – first Indian; first President Frederick Whyte, first Deputy President Sachchidananda Sinha.
[3:7] - **AP angle:** **V. Ramadas** elected to the Central Legislative Assembly (1923).
[3:8] ~~ Sources: Spectrum (Ahir) p.376; PMF Modern History 04 p.154; old app notes 1:224 (Justice Party 63, election years, Ramadas)
