## SUB 4 | Munagala Paragana Struggle (1930s)
[218:4:0] - **Location:** **Nandigama taluk, Krishna district** then; now **Suryapet (earlier Nalgonda)**, Telangana.
[218:4:1] - **Zamindar:** **Nayani Venkata Ranga Rao** (Keesaravari family) – literary patron but a harsh landlord.
[218:4:2] - Bodies: Munagala Peasant Association (Batchala Jagannadh Das); Zamin Peasant Association (**Kodati Venkata Narasimha Rao**).
[218:4:3] - **Kalukova Satyagraha (1938)** – **Nanduri Prasada Rao**, over a paddy wage of 80 seers per acre.
[218:4:4] - **Brahmaiah Award (15 Jan 1939)** – by APCC Secretary **Gottipati Brahmaiah**; settlement covering **142 villages**.
[218:4:5] - **Munagala Day: 11 June 1939**; agitation **withdrawn 19 June 1939** after Prakasam and Ranga promised action.
[218:4:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History Part 19 p.11-12; old app notes (142 villages, Munagala Day, withdrawal)

## SUB 1 | Santhal Hul (1855-56)
[219:1:0] # Background – Rajmahal Hills
[219:1:1] - The British **failed to subdue the Paharias** → invited the **Santhals** to settle and cultivate the Rajmahal hills (APPSC G1).
[219:1:2] - **By 1832** a large tract was demarcated as **Damin-i-Koh** – declared the **land of the Santhals**.
[219:1:3] - **Cleveland** (Augustus), Collector of **Bhagalpur** (1780s) – **Hill Assembly Plan**: Paharia hills under hereditary hill chiefs (sardars); fatally wounded by **Tilka Manjhi (1784)**.
[219:1:4] - Rajas of Maheshpur and Pakur leased Santhal villages to zamindars and moneylenders (**dikus**).
[219:1:5] # Course
[219:1:6] - Began at **Bhognadih (Bhaganidihi)** on **30 June 1855** (**Hul Divas**).
[219:1:7] - Leaders: brothers **Sidhu (Siddho) and Kanhu Murmu**, with **Chand and Bhairav** (so "both" pairs) and sisters **Phulo-Jhano**; about **30,000** proclaimed Santhal Raj; area between **Bhagalpur and Rajmahal** declared autonomous.
[219:1:8] - Weapons: **bows, arrows and axes** against British guns.
[219:1:9] - **Major Burrough(s)** defeated near **Bhagalpur**; **Gokko** – leader of **Godda**; **Mahajan Deen Dayal Rai** also figures. Santhals never ruled the Ganga valley near Muzaffarpur.
[219:1:10] - Against **zamindars, moneylenders, police** and the revenue system; crushed in **1856**, over **15,000** killed.
[219:1:11] - **"Kherwari Hul"** = a **Santhal** movement (Kherwar = Santhals' own name); Kharwar/Khairwar movement **1874** (Spectrum: 1870s).
[219:1:12] # After the Hul
[219:1:13] - **Santhal Parganas** carved out (from Bhagalpur and Birbhum) as a separate **non-regulation district** (Act XXXVII of 1855); **land transfer to non-Santhals barred** (UPSC 2018: both).
[219:1:14] - Santhal Parganas Settlement Regulation **1872**; Santhal Parganas Tenancy (Supplementary Provisions) Act **1949**.

=== NOTES 1:223 | Depressed Classes Movement, Communal Award and the Poona Pact 1932
## SUB 0 | Gandhi's Fast and the Poona Pact
[223:0:0] - **Gandhi's fast unto death:** from **20 September 1932**, **Yerwada Jail, Poona**, against the separate electorate for the depressed classes – his **first fast unto death** (not RTC failure, Congress-League rift, or riots).
[223:0:1] - Gandhi: separate electorates would **permanently divide Hindu society**; **Ambedkar** supported the separate electorate.
[223:0:2] - **Poona Pact, 24 September 1932:** **Ambedkar** for the depressed classes, **Madan Mohan Malaviya** for caste Hindus; the government accepted it as an **amendment** to the Award (not withdrawn); Gandhi ended his fast.
[223:0:3] TABLE head: Feature | Communal Award | Poona Pact
  [223:0:3.0] Electorate | Separate (+ double vote) | Joint electorate with reserved seats
  [223:0:3.1] Provincial reserved seats | 71 | 147 (Spectrum) / 148 (most books) – increased
  [223:0:3.2] Centre | – | 18% of general seats
[223:0:4] - The Pact's joint electorate with reserved seats became the **model for SC reservation** (it concerned SCs, not STs).
[223:0:5] - Forerunner: **Rajah-Moonje Pact** (Feb 1932) – **M.C. Rajah** and **B.S. Moonje**.
[223:0:6] - Order: Award (Aug) → fast (20 Sept) → Pact (24 Sept 1932).
[223:0:7] - **Bose on Ambedkar:** "leadership thrust upon him by a benign British Government" – attributed to **Subhas Chandra Bose**.
[223:0:8] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.423-424; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.205-207; app notes 1:223
## SUB 1 | Anti-untouchability and Depressed Classes Bodies
[223:1:0] TABLE head: Body | Year | Founder / leader
  [223:1:0.0] Depressed Classes Mission | 1906 (18 Oct), Bombay | V.R. Shinde
  [223:1:0.1] Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha | 1924 | Ambedkar – "Educate, Agitate, Organise"
  [223:1:0.2] All India Depressed Classes Association | 1920s | M.C. Rajah (president)
  [223:1:0.3] All India Anti-Untouchability League | 30 Sept 1932 | Founded by Gandhi; first president G.D. Birla, secretary Amritlal Thakkar; renamed Harijan Sevak Sangh
  [223:1:0.4] Independent Labour Party | Aug 1936 | Ambedkar – won 15 of 17 seats in 1937
  [223:1:0.5] All India Scheduled Castes Federation | 1942 | Ambedkar
  [223:1:0.6] Adi Hindu Social Service League | Hyderabad | Bhagya Reddy Varma
[223:1:1] - **Bhagya Reddy Varma:** at the **1917 Vijayawada** conference the depressed classes were to be called **Adi-Hindu**; the conference took the name **Adi-Andhra Mahajana Sabha**; worked with **Arige Ramaswamy** among the Malas; title "Varma" from the **Arya Samaj**.
[223:1:2] - **Journals:** *Mooknayak* (31 Jan 1920) and *Bahishkrit Bharat* – Ambedkar; *Harijan* (1933) – Gandhi.
[223:1:3] - **"Scheduled Castes":** first used by the **Simon Commission**, fixed by the **GoI Act 1935**, first list **1936**; SCs are also called **Dalits**.

## SUB 2 | Communal Award, 1932
[223:2:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [223:2:0.0] Date | 16 August 1932
  [223:2:0.1] By | British PM Ramsay MacDonald
  [223:2:0.2] Context | 2nd Round Table Conference failed over minority representation
  [223:2:0.3] Separate electorates | Muslims, Sikhs, Europeans, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, depressed classes (and Marathas for some Bombay seats) – not Buddhists
  [223:2:0.4] Depressed classes | Declared a minority with a separate electorate for the first time; double vote (separate + general); 71 provincial seats
  [223:2:0.5] Women | 3% seats in all provinces except the NWFP
[223:2:1] - Indians were divided into **11 groups**; the Award gave each minority seats elected by **separate electorate**.
[223:2:2] - Muslims had separate electorates from 1909; Europeans, Anglo-Indians, Indian Christians from 1919.

=== NOTES 1:224 | Government of India Act 1919 – Dyarchy, Montagu-chelmsford Reforms; Rowlatt Act
## SUB 0 | Government of India Act, 1919 – Features
[224:0:0] # Origin
[224:0:1] - **Montagu Declaration, 20 August 1917:** "progressive realisation of **responsible government** in India as an integral part of the British Empire" – first official promise; pressure mainly from the **Home Rule Leagues**.
[224:0:2] - **Edwin Montagu** = Secretary of State for India; **Lord Chelmsford** = Viceroy; **Montagu-Chelmsford Report, 1918** → **basis of the GoI Act, 1919** (not 1909, 1935 or 1947). The proposals were **constitutional reforms** (UPSC 2016).
[224:0:3] - Passed **1919**, in force **1921**; British PM: **David Lloyd George** (1916-22).
[224:0:4] - **Not** the Morley-Minto Act (that is the **Indian Councils Act, 1909**).
[224:0:5] # Main features
[224:0:6] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [224:0:6.0] Subject lists | Central and provincial subjects separated ("Devolution Rules") – jurisdiction of Centre and provinces clearly defined; delegation of authority to provinces
  [224:0:6.1] Structure | Still centralised and unitary despite devolution
  [224:0:6.2] Provinces | Dyarchy (reserved / transferred); provincial budgets separated from the central budget
  [224:0:6.3] Centre | Bicameral for the first time: Council of State (60) + Legislative Assembly (145, 104 elected)
  [224:0:6.4] Elections | Direct elections for the first time (1909 = indirect); franchise limited by property, tax, education
  [224:0:6.5] Electorates | Communal representation extended – separate electorates for Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans (Muslim separate electorates = 1909)
  [224:0:6.6] Executive Council | 3 of the 6 members (other than C-in-C) Indian
  [224:0:6.7] High Commissioner | New office of High Commissioner for India, London (Spectrum: tenure six years); first holder Sir William Meyer
  [224:0:6.8] PSC | Section 96(C) → Public Service Commission; set up 1926 (Lee Commission)
  [224:0:6.9] Review | Statutory commission after 10 years → Simon Commission (1927, two years early)
  [224:0:6.10] Princes | Chamber of Princes (Narendra Mandal), 1921
[224:0:7] - **Statements (CDS 2002):** objectives = **dyarchy in provinces + extension of provincial government**; "power to local government" is not the objective.
[224:0:8] - **IAS 2012 / CDS 2010:** principal features = dyarchy, **devolution of legislative authority** to provinces, **expanded and reconstituted legislatures**; separate electorates for Muslims are **not** a 1919 feature.
[224:0:9] - **A-R (UP UDA 1998):** "structure remained unitary and central" – **true** (Laxmikanth, Spectrum); "large part of authority delegated to provinces" – true, but it does not explain A.
[224:0:10] - **Governor's tenure:** the Act fixed no term; governors (like the Governor-General) were appointed for **five years** by practice.
[224:0:11] # Reactions
[224:0:12] - Congress special **Bombay session, Aug 1918 (Hasan Imam)** condemned the scheme; **Amritsar (1919)**: "inadequate, unsatisfactory and disappointing".
[224:0:13] - Moderates under **Surendranath Banerjee** (with Sapru, Srinivasa Sastri, Jayakar) left and formed the **Indian (National) Liberal Federation** (1919) – not the Swarajya Party.
[224:0:14] - **C.R. Das + Tilak** rejected; **Gandhi + Malaviya**: give a fair trial; **Krishna Patrika**: "giving with the right hand, taking with the left".
[224:0:15] - **AP angle:** **Nyapati Subbarao**'s 27-member Andhra delegation met Montagu at Madras for an **Andhra province**; **Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao** presided over the Special Andhra Mahasabha, **Guntur, 17 Aug 1918**.
[224:0:16] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt) p.31-32; Spectrum (Ahir) p.541-542, p.365; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.55; PMF Modern History 04 p.131, p.134; old app notes 1:224 (Montagu Declaration, reactions, AP delegation)
[224:0:17] - **Muddiman Committee (1924):** the British response to the **Swarajist** demand for a review of the 1919 Act.
## SUB 1 | Provincial Dyarchy – Reserved and Transferred Subjects
[224:1:0] # Meaning
[224:1:1] - **Dyarchy (diarchy):** "double rule" (Greek *di-arche*) – the **subjects delegated to the provinces** were divided into two categories, **reserved** and **transferred** (UPSC 2017 – not two central houses, not Centre-State, not London-Delhi).
[224:1:2] - **Introduced by:** the **Government of India Act, 1919** = **Montagu-Chelmsford (Mont-Ford) Reforms** – in the **provinces** (not 1861, 1892, 1909 or 1935; not the Morley-Minto Reforms or the Simon plan).
[224:1:3] - **Aim:** partial **responsible government** in the provinces – the transferred half was run by ministers answerable to the legislature (so A-R "dyarchy = division of administration in two sections; introduced to bring responsible government in provinces": both true, R explains A).
[224:1:4] - **Provinces gained power:** the 1919 Act, not the 1892/1909 Acts or the Gandhi-Irwin Pact, gave provincial governments more powers under dyarchy (APPSC G1 2008).
[224:1:5] # Reserved vs transferred
[224:1:6] TABLE head: Subjects | Examples | Run by | Answerable to
  [224:1:6.0] Reserved | Administration of justice, police, land revenue, finance, prisons, irrigation, control of the press | Governor + Executive Council | Not the legislature
  [224:1:6.1] Transferred | Local self-government, education, public health, agriculture | Governor + Ministers | Provincial legislative council
[224:1:7] - UPSC 2022 reserved set = **justice, land revenue, police (1, 3, 4)**; **local self-government was transferred**.
[224:1:8] - Flaw: ministers held transferred subjects but **finance stayed reserved**; the experiment was "largely unsuccessful".
[224:1:9] - **Dyarchy at the Centre** = the **1935 Act** (never worked); dyarchy in the **provinces** = **1919**; the 1935 Act abolished provincial dyarchy.

[224:3:0] # Madras, 1920
[224:3:1] - First elections **1920** under the 1919 Act; **Congress boycotted** (Non-Cooperation).

## SUB 4 | Rowlatt Act, 1919
[224:4:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [224:4:0.0] Official name | Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919
  [224:4:0.1] Passed | March 1919 (not 1917, 1921, 1923)
  [224:4:0.2] Basis | Rowlatt (Sedition) Committee under Sir Sidney Rowlatt; replaced the wartime Defence of India Act with a permanent law
