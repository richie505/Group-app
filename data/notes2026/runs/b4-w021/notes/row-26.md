=== Coral Reef Places – World and India
# Great Barrier Reef (asked 9 times)
- **World's largest coral reef system** and largest structure built by living organisms: off **Queensland, north-eastern Australia**, in the **Coral Sea (Pacific Ocean)** – not New Zealand, Finland, Greenland or Ireland.
- Length about **2,300 km** (PMF; G.C. Leong gives 1,200 miles) – so "more than 2,500 km" is **false** (UPRO/ARO 2023: only statement 1). Over **2,900 reefs**; dugong and green turtle habitat; **UNESCO World Heritage Site 1981**.
- APPSC Group 1 2004: became the world's biggest protected marine sanctuary (July 2004). Next largest system: **Mesoamerican Barrier Reef**.
# World distribution
- **Most reefs lie in tropical waters** (between the **Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn**, mostly 25°N–25°S, up to 30°); **more than one-third** are in **Australia, Indonesia and the Philippines**; reefs host **far more animal phyla** than tropical rainforests – all three statements of UPSC 2018 (asked 3 times) are **correct**.
- Few reefs on the **west coasts** of tropical continents (cold currents, upwelling).

=== Coral Reefs – Nature, Conditions and Types
# Nature
- Built from the **calcium carbonate** skeletons of tiny colonial **coral polyps** (phylum **Cnidaria**); the CaCO3 is **aragonite**. **Oyster shells** too are **calcium carbonate**.
- **Mutualism with zooxanthellae** (single-celled dinoflagellate algae): the algae photosynthesise and give the coral **food and colour**; the coral gives **shelter and CO2/nutrients**.
- **"Rainforests of the Sea"** – the marine counterpart of **tropical rain forests** – because of exceptional **species richness**: under **1%** of the ocean floor, over **25%** of marine species.
- Reefs **break storm waves** and prevent coastal erosion; also nurseries, fisheries, tourism. APPSC Group 1: both statements true, but wave-breaking does **not explain** the epithet.
- **Biorock (mineral accretion)**: a small electric current on a steel frame precipitates CaCO3 – used to **restore damaged reefs** (ZSI trial, Gulf of Kachchh) (UPSC 2022).
# Growth conditions
| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Temperature | **Not below about 20°C** (20–21°C minimum); optimum **23–25°C**; tropical seas |
| Light | **Photic**, shallow water – under about 50 m (Leong: not over 180 ft; ideal under 100 ft) |
| Water | **Clean, sediment-free**, normal sea salinity (about 3.5%, not 6%); freshwater and very saline water harm growth |
| Avoid | Muddy river mouths, cold currents |
- Astt. Comm. 2017: photic + clean water + 20–21°C = **I, II and IV**; "salinity of 6%" is wrong.
# Reef types
| Type | Feature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Fringing** | Close to shore; shallow lagoon or none; **commonest in India** | Andamans, Gulf of Mannar |
| **Barrier** | Parallel to the coast, separated by a **wide, deep lagoon** | **Great Barrier Reef** |
| **Atoll** | **Ring / horseshoe** reef enclosing a lagoon, no central land | **Lakshadweep** |

=== Marine Life, Ocean Zones and Productivity
# Ocean zones
| Zone | Extent / feature |
|---|---|
| Littoral / intertidal | Between **high- and low-tide marks** |
| **Neritic** | From the high-water line over the **continental shelf to about 200 m**; **richest fishing zone** |
| Photic (euphotic) | Sunlit layer (to about 200 m); photosynthesis |
| **Epipelagic** (sunlit) | **Highest share of marine habitats and life** |
| **Disphotic** (twilight) | Above the aphotic zone; light **too weak for photosynthesis** |
| **Aphotic** | Dark; **no photosynthesis** |
| Pelagic / benthic | Open water column / sea floor |
| Abyssal, hadal | Deep floor and trenches |
- Marine ecosystems cover about **71%** of Earth's surface; **hydrothermal vents** support **chemosynthetic** life. Rivers and freshwater lakes are **freshwater**, not marine, ecosystems.
# Organisms and oxygen
| Group | Feature |
|---|---|
| Plankton | Drift with water: **phytoplankton** (plants) + zooplankton (animals) |
| Nekton | **Swim actively** (fish, squid, marine mammals) |
| Benthos | Live on or in the **bottom** |
- **Phytoplankton** (diatoms, dinoflagellates, cyanobacteria) = **main primary producers** of the sea (not seaweeds or marine angiosperms).
- Marine phytoplankton and photosynthetic bacteria produce **about 50%** of the world's oxygen – **more than rainforests**; air holds **far more oxygen** than even well-oxygenated water (about 25–35 times per litre) – UPSC 2025: **2 only**.
- If phytoplankton were destroyed: the **carbon sink** and **food chains** would suffer; water density would not change (UPSC 2012: I and II).
# Upwelling
- Winds push surface water **offshore** → cold, **nutrient-rich** water rises → plankton boom → rich fisheries. Upwelling raises productivity by bringing **nutrients** to the surface – not decomposers or bottom organisms (UPSC 2011: only II).
| Upwelling zone | Current / fishery |
|---|---|
| Peru–Chile | **Humboldt** – **anchoveta**; suppressed by **El Nino** → fisheries fail |
| Namibia | **Benguela** |
| Somalia | **Findlater** jet, SW monsoon |
| Kerala / SW India | SW monsoon – **oil sardine, mackerel** |
# Algal blooms and red tides
- **Red tide** = bloom of a **pigmented dinoflagellate** that discolours estuarine or coastal water (UPSC 1998).
- **Harmful algal blooms in Indian seas:** **nutrient discharge from estuaries**, **monsoon land run-off** and **upwelling** (also mud banks) – all three (UPSC 2011). Excess N and P → eutrophication → **dead zones** (Gulf of Mexico – **Mississippi** run-off). **INCOIS** issues algal-bloom and coral-bleaching alerts.

=== Coral Bleaching and Ocean Acidification
# Coral bleaching
- **Meaning:** stressed polyps **expel (eject) their zooxanthellae** and lose pigment, turning **white**; a bleached coral is **stressed, not necessarily dead** (it can recover if conditions improve).
- **Most significant cause:** **global warming** – rising sea-surface temperature, **marine heatwaves** (1°C above normal for weeks can trigger it); also UV, turbidity, pollution, salinity change, **extreme low tides**, **ocean acidification**, El Nino – **not** more carbonate, not mining or disease as the main cause.
| Global bleaching event | Period | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **1998** | Strong El Nino |
| 2nd | **2010** | – |
| 3rd | **2014–2017** | **68.2%** of reef areas |
| 4th | **2023 – about mid-2025** | About **84%** of reefs, **83 countries** (NOAA–ICRI) |
- Every strong El Nino since 1998 came with a global bleaching event; recovery takes **more than 15 years**. GCRMN 2026: major disturbances now every **4–6 years**; **87%** of reefs faced heat stress in 2024. **Andaman** mass bleaching linked to about a **2°C** rise in Indian Ocean temperature.
# Ocean acidification
- CO2 + water → **carbonic acid** → bicarbonate + hydrogen (hydronium) ions → **pH falls** (sea water about **8.1**, alkaline) → **carbonate ions fall** → corals, calcareous plankton and shellfish **form shells less easily** (it does not strengthen reefs).
- Oceans absorb about **30–40%** of human CO2; worst in **cold seas** (higher CO2 solubility). Nicknames: **"evil twin of global warming"**, "the other CO2 problem".
