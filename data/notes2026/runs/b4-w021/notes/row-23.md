=== Salinity – Meaning, Factors and Distribution
# Basics
- **Salinity** = total dissolved salts in sea water: grams of salt per **1,000 g (1 kg)** of water, in **‰ / ppt** or PSU. **Average 35‰** (35 g per kg); open ocean 33–37‰; **Indian Ocean average 35‰**, Atlantic about 36‰.
- **Isohaline** = line joining places of equal salinity. Brackish water: about 5–35 ppt (NCERT upper limit 24.7‰).
- **Main source of salts = land** (weathered rocks), **carried by rivers**; volcanoes add a little. Salinity **reduces evaporation**.
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Evaporation** | Raises |
| **Freezing** (salt left behind) | Raises |
| Precipitation | Lowers |
| River inflow | Lowers (Black Sea: Danube, Dnieper, Dniester) |
| Ice melting | Lowers |
| Currents, winds | Redistribute |
# Horizontal distribution
| Region | Salinity | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Equator | Relatively lower | Heavy rain |
| **Subtropics (about 20°–30°/40° N and S)** | **Highest** – near the **Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn** | Strong evaporation, little rain (UPPSC 2023 true statement) |
| **Polar seas** | **Lower** | Low evaporation, ice melt, rivers – so "salinity is high in polar regions" is **false**, though freezing does raise the salinity of the water left (CDS 2009: A false, R true) |
# Seas and lakes
| Water body | Salinity | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Lake Van** (Turkey) | **~330‰** | Closed soda lake |
| **Dead Sea** | **~238‰** (often given as ~340 g/l) | Closed basin, intense evaporation |
| Great Salt Lake (Utah) | ~220‰ | Closed basin (Utah Lake is fresh) |
| **Red Sea** | **~41‰** – **saltiest sea** | **High evaporation**, little rain, few rivers; narrow **Bab-el-Mandeb** link |
| Gulf of California, Persian Gulf, Mediterranean | High | Hot, dry, evaporation |
| **Arabian Sea** | **~36‰** | **High evaporation, low freshwater inflow** |
| **Bay of Bengal** | **~30‰** | Ganga–Brahmaputra and other rivers dilute |
| Arctic | Low (<32‰) | Cold, ice melt |
| Black Sea | 17–18‰ | Rivers pour in |
| **Baltic** | **Lowest** (under ~10‰) | Many rivers, cold |
- Orders asked: **Lake Van > Dead Sea > Great Salt Lake** (APPSC G2); ascending **Baltic < Black Sea < Red Sea < Dead Sea** (APPSC FSO); **Baltic < Arctic < Gulf of California < Red Sea** (CDS 2012); **Arabian Sea saltier than the Bay of Bengal**.
- Most saline among Dead Sea, Caspian, Utah Lake, Red Lake = **Dead Sea** (APPSC G1 2002).

=== Distribution of Earth's Water
| Store | Share of all water | Rank |
|---|---|---|
| **Oceans** | **About 97%** (96.5%) | 1 |
| **Polar ice and glaciers** | About 1.74% (**about 68–70% of fresh water**) | 2 |
| **Groundwater** | About 1.7% (a little over **30% of fresh water**) | 3 |
| Lakes, soil moisture, atmosphere | Tiny | 4 |
| **Rivers** | 0.0002% (smallest) | Last |
- Descending order: **oceans > ice and glaciers > groundwater > rivers** (UPPSC 2025: 1, 3, 4, 2).
- **Largest fresh-water store = glaciers and ice caps**; of the rest, the **largest = groundwater** (UPSC 2013, asked twice); groundwater far exceeds lakes and rivers, and **ice caps hold more than groundwater** (UPSC 2021: statement 2 only).
- Fresh water = about 2.5–3%; usable fresh water under 1%. The **total amount of water on Earth is constant** (water cycle); it is not vanishing and the oceans are not getting saltier because they are drying.
- **2003** = UN International Year of Freshwater.

=== Density, Clines and Water's Anomalous Behaviour
- Water is **densest at 4°C**: a lake freezes at the **top** while the bottom stays liquid at about 4°C (UPSC 2011: density maximum at 4°C).
- **Density of sea water rises** as **temperature falls**, **salinity rises**, and **pressure (depth)** increases; colder and saltier water sinks and drives deep currents.
- **Sea water is denser than fresh water**: swimming is easier in sea water (CDS 2008: A and R true, R explains A); an egg sinks in tap water but **floats in strong salt solution** because the solution is denser than the egg; people float in the Dead Sea.
| Layer | Rapid change with depth in |
|---|---|
| Thermocline | Temperature |
| **Halocline** | **Salinity** (salinity gradient; asked twice) |
| **Pycnocline** | **Density**; drives deep circulation |
| Chemocline | Chemistry; Black Sea (layers rarely mix) |
- Every cline is a **barrier to vertical mixing**.

=== Temperature of Ocean Water
- Oceans are **store-houses** of solar energy: they heat and cool slowly and absorb **over 90%** of the excess heat of global warming, and about a quarter of human CO2 (ocean **pH ~8.1** and falling).
- **Horizontal**: falls from equator to poles (about 0.5°C per degree of latitude); highest just north of the equator; **NH oceans warmer** (more land; averages 19°C vs 16°C).
- **Tropics: western side warmer** (trade winds pile warm water west); **temperate zone: eastern side warmer** (westerlies, North Atlantic Drift) – UPSC 2021: **both statements correct**.
- **Annual range smaller in the Pacific** than the Atlantic (vast) and **greater in the Northern Hemisphere** than the Southern (UPSC 2007: only II).
- **Vertical, three layers**: warm surface layer (about **500 m**, 20–25°C) → **thermocline** (**500–1,000 m**, rapid fall) → cold deep layer (**0–4°C**); about 90% of ocean water lies below the thermocline.
- **Blue colour of sea water** = water absorbs red light and **scatters** blue (molecular scattering, C.V. Raman 1921), not interference.
- **2025**: record ocean heat content; about **90%** of the ocean surface saw marine heatwaves (WMO). **AP**: NIOT's station at **Nellore** runs **OMNI buoys** (temperature, salinity, currents).
