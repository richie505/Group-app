## SUB 0 | Cooperative, Competitive and Sub-federalism
[0:0] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [0:0.0] Federalism | Sharing of power between the Union and the States (not communism, fascism, oligarchy)
  [0:0.1] Cooperative federalism | Centre and States work together on a common platform; NITI's bottom-up version – "marble-cake federalism"
  [0:0.2] Competitive federalism | States compete with each other and with the Centre to draw investment and improve outcomes – e.g. comparing States on ease of doing business
  [0:0.3] Competitive sub-federalism | Local bodies compete with local bodies (districts, cities) – e.g. Aspirational Districts rankings
[0:1] - Cooperative federalism **first recommended by the Sarkaria Commission**; put into practice by **NITI Aayog (2015)**, whose **thrust is to promote cooperative federalism** (not just inclusive growth or correcting the planning process).
[0:2] - NITI planning: credible **village-level plans aggregated upwards**.
[0:3] - Competitive federalism fosters **competition among States to improve social and environmental outcomes** – the link to **sustainable development** (SDG India Index).
[0:4] - **GST Council** = cooperative; **NITI State rankings** = competitive.

  [0:5.1] Bargaining federalism | Morris-Jones
  [0:5.2] Quasi-federal | K.C. Wheare

[1:0] TABLE head: Body | Created by | Year | Status
  [1:0.0] Planning Commission | Cabinet resolution (non-statutory) | 1950 | Extra-constitutional; replaced by NITI

  [1:0.2] NITI Aayog | Union Cabinet resolution | 1 January 2015 (not 2014) | Non-constitutional, non-statutory think-tank; no power to allocate plan grants
[1:1] - **Constitutional** body = directly established by the Constitution (EC, FC, CAG, PSCs); **statutory** = by Act (NHRC, **Lokpal**, NGT); the Planning Commission was **not** statutory.
