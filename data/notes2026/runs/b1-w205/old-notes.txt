=== NOTES 1:205 | Morley-minto Reforms 1909; Formation of the Muslim League 1906; Lucknow Pact 1916
## SUB 0 | Simla Deputation and Founding of the Muslim League, 1906
[205:0:0] # Simla Deputation (1 October 1906)
[205:0:1] - Led by **Aga Khan III**; met **Lord Minto** at **Simla (Shimla)**.
[205:0:2] - Demanded **separate electorates** for Muslims and representation **above their numbers** (weightage), citing their contribution "**to the defence of the Empire**"; also safeguards in government services.
[205:0:3] - **Jinnah**, then a Congressman (secretary to Naoroji at Calcutta, 1906), **opposed separate electorates** in 1906 – the Aga Khan called him the only well-known Muslim to do so.
[205:0:4] # Founding facts
[205:0:5] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [205:0:5.0] Date | 30 December 1906
  [205:0:5.1] Place | Dacca (Dhaka), at the session of the All-India Muhammadan Educational Conference
  [205:0:5.2] Initiative | Nawab Salimullah of Dacca, with Mohsin-ul-Mulk and Viqar-ul-Mulk (who chaired the Dacca meeting)
  [205:0:5.3] First president | Aga Khan III (president 1906/08-1913) – not Jinnah, who joined only in 1913
  [205:0:5.4] First annual session | Karachi, 1907 (constitution adopted); 2nd session Amritsar, 1908
  [205:0:5.5] Headquarters | Lucknow
  [205:0:5.6] London Muslim League | Syed Ameer Ali, 6 May 1908 – exam keys call it the League's "London branch" (PMF: strictly an independent body)
[205:0:6] # Character
[205:0:7] - **Loyalist, communal, conservative**: preached loyalty to the Empire, kept Muslim intelligentsia **away from the Congress**.
[205:0:8] - **Supported the partition of Bengal** and sought safeguards in government services – it did **not** oppose the partition (UPSC 2002 "not correct" statement).
[205:0:9] - **Other figures of the 2002 statement question:** **Hakim Ajmal Khan** – a leader of the militant nationalist **Ahrar movement** (with Mohammad Ali, Hasan Imam, Zafar Ali Khan, Mazharul Haq); **Syed Ahmad Khan** opposed the Congress (United Indian Patriotic Association, 1888); **Barkatullah and Obeidullah Sindhi** – Provisional Government of India, **Kabul (1915)**.
[205:0:10] - **W.W. Hunter** (*The Indian Musalmans*, 1871) – exam keys credit him with "The Muslims, if contented and satisfied, would become the greatest bulwark of British power in India".
[205:0:11] # Chronology and dates
[205:0:12] - **INC 1885 → Partition of Bengal 1905 → Swadeshi (7 Aug 1905) → Muslim League (Dec 1906) → Surat split 1907 → Morley-Minto 1909 → Lucknow Pact 1916**.
[205:0:13] TABLE head: Body | Founded
  [205:0:13.0] Brahmo Samaj | 1828
  [205:0:13.1] Indian National Congress | 1885
  [205:0:13.2] All India Muslim League | 1906
  [205:0:13.3] HSRA | 1928
[205:0:14] > Founded at **Dacca 1906**; first session **Karachi 1907**; first president **Aga Khan**; London League **Ameer Ali 1908**.
[205:0:15] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.308; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.77-79; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.51-52, 72; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian History Part 2 p.58
[205:0:16] - **Delhi Durbar, 12 December 1911:** partition of Bengal annulled; capital shifted from **Calcutta to Delhi**.
[205:0:17] - **1911-13 rift with the British:** annulment of the Bengal partition (Delhi Durbar, **12 December 1911**; capital Calcutta → Delhi), refusal of an Aligarh university and of help to Turkey turned the League towards the Congress.
[205:0:18] - **Aga Khan III** wrote ***India in Transition* (1918)**; M.N. Roy's book of the same name came in 1922.
## SUB 1 | Indian Councils Act, 1909 – Morley-Minto Reforms
[205:1:0] # Names and background
[205:1:1] - **Names:** **Indian Councils Act, 1909** = **Morley-Minto (Minto-Morley) Reforms**; exam options also call it the "Government of India Act, 1909".
[205:1:2] - **Persons:** **John Morley** = Secretary of State for India (Liberal, from end-1905); **Lord Minto II** = Viceroy (1905-10), who succeeded **Curzon** in 1905.
[205:1:3] - **Aim:** "rally the Moderates" (carrot and stick) and win Muslim loyalty – the reforms were meant to **divide the nationalist ranks** and check Indian unity.
[205:1:4] # Provisions
[205:1:5] TABLE head: Area | Provision
  [205:1:5.0] Separate electorates | For the first time, separate electorates for Muslims – Muslim members elected only by Muslim voters = communal representation; "legalised communalism"
  [205:1:5.1] Weightage | Muslims got more seats than their population share, and a lower income qualification for voters
  [205:1:5.2] Elections | Elective principle recognised for non-official members – but indirect, multi-tier (local bodies → electoral college → provincial councils → central council)
  [205:1:5.3] Central Council | Additional members raised to 60 (from 16); official majority kept (69 members: 37 officials, 32 non-officials, 27 elected)
  [205:1:5.4] Provincial Councils | 30-50 additional members; non-official majority (but partly nominated)
  [205:1:5.5] Powers | Ask supplementary questions, move resolutions, vote on separate items of the budget – not on the budget as a whole; army spending non-votable
  [205:1:5.6] Executive Council | One Indian could be appointed: S.P. (Satyendra Prasanna) Sinha, Law Member, 1909 – first Indian on the Viceroy's Executive Council
[205:1:6] - **Before the Act (1907):** Morley put two Indians – K.G. Gupta and Syed Hussain Bilgrami – on the Secretary of State's India Council.
[205:1:7] - **Gokhale** went to England to meet Morley for self-government on colonial lines; Moderates co-operated with the reforms and lost popular support.
[205:1:8] # Verdict
[205:1:9] - **Most short-lived** of Britain's constitutional experiments in India (UPSC 1999) – replaced by the 1919 Act (in force 1921); **Ramsay MacDonald:** an incomplete deal between democracy and bureaucracy.
[205:1:10] - Morley: "if this chapter of reforms led ... to a parliamentary system in India, I would have nothing to do with it." The councils stayed **advisory**.
[205:1:11] > "Which Act introduced separate / communal electorates (communal representation)?" → **Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley-Minto)** – not 1892, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford) or 1935, which only extended it.
[205:1:12] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.308-310; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.66-70; Laxmikanth Part 1 p.31-32; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.52
## SUB 2 | Lucknow Session and the Lucknow Pact, 1916
[205:2:0] # The Lucknow Congress (December 1916)
[205:2:1] - **President:** **Ambika Charan Majumdar** (a Moderate). The League's Lucknow session was presided over by **Jinnah**; **both** bodies met at **Lucknow**.
[205:2:2] - **Reunion** of Moderates and Extremists – Tilak's group readmitted after **9 years** (Surat 1907). Chief architects of the reconciliation: **Annie Besant** and **Tilak**; Pherozeshah Mehta (died 1915) had blocked it.
[205:2:3] - First Congress session Gandhi attended after returning from South Africa; Champaran peasants' problems were put to him here.
[205:2:4] - **Tilak's slogan** "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it" – exam keys (UPPCS) place it at the **Lucknow session, 1916**; strictly it was the slogan of his Home Rule campaign of 1916 (league founded at Poona, April 1916).
[205:2:5] # The Pact
[205:2:6] - **Brokers:** **Tilak and Jinnah**; Besant also helped; Sarojini Naidu called Jinnah the "Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity".
[205:2:7] - **Main concession:** the **Congress accepted separate electorates for Muslims** – the **first time (1916)** the Congress did so; Muslims got a **fixed proportion of seats** (weightage).
[205:2:8] - **Joint demands:** self-government at an early date; expanded councils with **elected majorities**; **half** of the executive councils to be Indians; five-year council term; Secretary of State paid from the British treasury.
[205:2:9] - **Not in the Pact:** no merger (separate sessions continued), no power-sharing deal, no demand for complete independence.
[205:2:10] - **Unity period:** **1916-1922** (Lucknow Pact → Khilafat-Non-Cooperation) = high point of Congress-League unity.
[205:2:11] # Chronology
[205:2:12] - **Lucknow Pact (Dec 1916) → Jallianwala Bagh (13 Apr 1919) → Tilak's death (1 Aug 1920) → Swaraj Party (1 Jan 1923)**.
[205:2:13] > Lucknow 1916: Congress president **A.C. Majumdar**, League president **Jinnah**; Congress **accepted** separate electorates.
[205:2:14] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.332-334, 515; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.93-95; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.54; NextGen Modern India L2 Part 6 p.4
[205:2:15] - **Also in the 1916 scheme:** separation of the executive from the judiciary; elected majorities in the provincial councils.
[205:2:16] - **Order:** Muslim League (1906) → Morley-Minto (1909) → Lucknow Pact (1916) → Jallianwala Bagh (1919).
[205:2:17] - **Communal veto:** no bill affecting a community could pass if **three-fourths** of that community's members opposed it.
## SUB 3 | Separate Electorates – Divide and Rule, Father of the Communal Electorate
[205:3:0] - **Stated reason:** protecting the Muslim minority. **Real reason:** part of the British policy of **divide and rule** – to set Muslims against the Congress and maintain British supremacy.
[205:3:1] - **Effects:** checked India's unification, encouraged separatism, became a potent factor in the **growth of communalism** – the "seeds of discord" that ended in partition.
[205:3:2] - **Lord Minto** = "**Father of the Communal Electorate**" – used separate electorates to **turn Muslims against the Congress**. Exam key (BPSC 2018): **Rajendra Prasad** called **Lord Minto** the "**Father of Pakistan**" (attribution not traced in the sources).
[205:3:3] - **Extension:** 1909 Muslims → **1919** + Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans → **1935** + depressed classes, women, labour.
[205:3:4] > Separate electorates = Minto (1909); Congress accepted them = Lucknow (1916).
[205:3:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.69, 83; Laxmikanth Part 1 p.31-33; Spectrum p.310
## SUB 4 | Acts Compared – 1861 to 1935
[205:4:0] TABLE head: Act | Key feature
  [205:4:0.0] Indian Councils Act, 1861 | Indians associated with legislative councils (nominated)
  [205:4:0.1] Indian Councils Act, 1892 | Discussion of the annual financial statement (budget); questions; indirect (nominated-on-recommendation) members
  [205:4:0.2] Indian Councils Act, 1909 | Separate Muslim electorates (communal representation); elective principle; S.P. Sinha
  [205:4:0.3] GoI Act, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford) | Provincial dyarchy (reserved and transferred subjects); bicameralism and direct elections at the Centre; communal electorates extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans
  [205:4:0.4] GoI Act, 1935 | All-India Federation; provincial autonomy (dyarchy in provinces abolished); dyarchy at the Centre; separate electorates for depressed classes, women, labour
[205:4:1] - **Match (APPSC G2):** separate Muslim representation – 1909; dyarchy in provinces – 1919; federation – 1935; discussion of annual statement – 1892.
[205:4:2] - **1909 vs 1919:** 1909 = separate Muslim electorates; 1919 = dyarchy. No Act brought dyarchy "at the Centre as well as in the provinces".
[205:4:3] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Part 1 p.30-33; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.69

## SUB 0 | Anushilan Samiti and Yugantar – Bengal
[206:0:0] # Anushilan Samiti
[206:0:1] - **Founded 1902, Calcutta**, by **Pramathanath (P.) Mitra** with **Satish Chandra Basu**; named after Bankim's *Anushilan Tattva*; associated with Aurobindo, Barindra Ghosh, Bipin Pal.
[206:0:2] - A **secret revolutionary society** – trained youth for armed struggle; dacoities, bombs, assassinations = **militant nationalism through revolutionary acts** (not social reform, women or labour welfare).
[206:0:3] - **Barindra Kumar Ghosh** (Aurobindo's younger brother) – his activities built its revolutionary core; head of the secret society in the Alipore case.
[206:0:4] - **Dhaka Anushilan (1906):** **Pulin Behari Das** – outgrew the Calcutta parent; organised the **Barrah dacoity (1908)** in Dhaka district = the **first major venture of the revolutionaries in East Bengal**, to raise funds.
[206:0:5] - **Sachindranath Sanyal:** joined Anushilan (1907); exam keys: set up its **Patna branch (1913)**; with Rash Behari Bose in the Hardinge bomb (1912).
[206:0:6] - **Tripura** was drawn into the movement early in the 20th century because **Bengal revolutionaries took shelter there**.
[206:0:7] # Yugantar (Jugantar)
[206:0:8] - **Weekly started April 1906** by an Anushilan inner circle – **Barindra Kumar Ghosh, Bhupendranath Dutta** (and Abinash Bhattacharya).
[206:0:9] - **Bhupendranath Datta** (Vivekananda's brother) stood before the British as **Yugantar's editor** and was jailed for sedition (1907).
[206:0:10] - The western Anushilan under **Jatindranath Mukherjee (Bagha Jatin)** emerged as the **Yugantar party**; German Plot (1915); Jatin died at **Balasore, September 1915**.
[206:0:11] > Anushilan = **P. Mitra 1902**; Dhaka branch = **Pulin Das**; Yugantar paper = **Barindra + Bhupendranath**; Yugantar party = **Bagha Jatin**.

## SUB 0 | Ghadar Party (1913) – Founding, Leaders and the 1915 Rising
[207:0:0] # Basic facts
[207:0:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [207:0:1.0] Founded | 1913, in the USA (North America) by Indian immigrants of the US and Canada; the Hindi Association formed at Portland (Oregon) in May 1913 became the Hindustan Ghadar Party
  [207:0:1.1] Headquarters | Yugantar Ashram, San Francisco (not Berlin, Singapore, Tashkent or Vancouver)
  [207:0:1.2] First president (chairman) | Baba Sohan Singh Bhakna
  [207:0:1.3] General secretary / moving spirit | Lala Har Dayal – the leader and organiser; came to the US in 1911 (lectured at Stanford)
  [207:0:1.4] Treasurer | Pandit Kanshi Ram
  [207:0:1.5] Members | Mainly Punjabi Sikh peasants and ex-soldiers settled on the Pacific coast of North America
  [207:0:1.6] Meaning of "Ghadar" | Revolt / mutiny
  [207:0:1.7] Paper Ghadar | A weekly, for free circulation; first issue 1 November 1913, in Urdu (Gurmukhi edition a month later); masthead "Angrezi Raj ka Dushman"; poems collected as *Ghadar ki Goonj*
[207:0:2] - **Founder:** books credit **Lala Har Dayal** (organiser, secretary) together with **Sohan Singh Bhakna** (founder-president).
[207:0:3] - **Programme:** assassinate officials, publish revolutionary literature, work among Indian troops abroad, procure arms, and bring about a **simultaneous revolt in all British colonies**; ideology strongly **secular**.
[207:0:4] # Leaders – who was and who was not
[207:0:5] - **Ghadarites:** Lala Har Dayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, **Ramchandra**, Bhagwan Singh, **Kartar Singh Sarabha** (sub-editor of *Ghadar*), **Barkatullah**, **Bhai Parmanand**, **Rash Behari Bose** (led the 1915 rising).
[207:0:6] - **NOT Ghadarites:** **Surya Sen** (Chittagong, 1930), **Khudiram Bose** (Muzaffarpur bomb, 1908), Barindra Kumar Ghosh, Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee.
[207:0:7] - **Pre-Ghadar work in North America:** Ramnath Puri, **G.D. Kumar**, **Taraknath Das**, Sohan Singh Bhakna.

  [207:0:8.3] INA / Azad Hind Fauj (1940s) | Singapore, later Rangoon
[207:0:9] # The rising of 1915
[207:0:10] - **Triggers (1914):** Har Dayal's arrest (March 1914), the **Komagata Maru** incident and, above all, the **outbreak of the First World War** – "England's difficulty, India's opportunity".
[207:0:11] - **21 February 1915:** date fixed for an armed revolt in the **Ferozepur, Lahore and Rawalpindi** garrisons; foiled by **treachery**.
[207:0:12] - **Defence of India Act (March 1915)** passed mainly to crush the Ghadar; **45 hanged** (Spectrum; PMF gives 42).
[207:0:13] - **Lahore Conspiracy Case (1915):** against the Ghadarites – **Kartar Singh Sarabha** and **Vishnu Ganesh Pingle** executed (16 Nov 1915); Bhai Parmanand arrested; Sohan Singh Bhakna transported for life.
[207:0:14] - **Singapore mutiny (15 February 1915):** 5th Light Infantry, inspired by the Ghadar.

=== NOTES 1:208 | Home Rule Movement – Tilak and Annie Besant; Impact
## SUB 0 | Annie Besant – Theosophist, Papers and Congress President
[208:0:0] # Life and institutions
[208:0:1] - Irish theosophist; came to India in **1893**; President of the **Theosophical Society** from **1907** (HQ Adyar); called the "**Iron Lady of Ireland**".
[208:0:2] - **Central Hindu College, Varanasi (1898)** – became the core of **Banaras Hindu University**, whose founder is **Madan Mohan Malaviya** (1916) – not Besant or Asutosh Mukherjee.
[208:0:3] - **National College, Madanapalle (1916)** – first principal **J.H. Cousins**; protégé **Jiddu Krishnamurti** (*At the Feet of the Master*).
[208:0:4] # Her papers
[208:0:5] TABLE head: Paper | Fact
  [208:0:5.0] The Commonweal | English weekly, Madras, January 1914 – editor Annie Besant
  [208:0:5.1] New India | She bought the *Madras Standard* (July 1914) and renamed it New India (a daily)
[208:0:6] - **Both papers = Besant** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025); "New Hindu" and "The Aryans" are distractors; New India ≠ Gandhi.
[208:0:7] # Congress President and later work
[208:0:8] TABLE head: President | Session | Fact
  [208:0:8.0] Annie Besant | Calcutta, 1917 | First woman President of the INC
  [208:0:8.1] Sarojini Naidu | Kanpur, 1925 | First Indian woman President
  [208:0:8.2] Nellie Sengupta | Calcutta, 1933 | After president-elect Malaviya was arrested
[208:0:9] - Sucheta Kripalani, Aruna Asaf Ali, Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit, Kasturba – never INC presidents.
[208:0:10] - **1918:** Besant visited **Bihar (Patna)** to strengthen the Home Rule League.
[208:0:11] - Quote: "**I am an Indian Tom-Tom**, whose work is waking up all the sleepers…" – **Annie Besant**.
[208:0:12] - **Chief architect** of the **Moderate–Extremist reunion** at Lucknow (1916), with Tilak.
[208:0:13] # Commonwealth of India Bill (1925)
[208:0:14] - Drafted by the **National Convention** (Sapru); amended by an All Parties Conference sub-committee chaired by **Besant**; introduced in the House of Commons by **George Lansbury** (Labour), **December 1925**; defeated.
[208:0:15] - Proposed **dominion status** ("equal footing with the self-governing dominions") and a **parliamentary system**; its Article 7 listed **seven fundamental rights**.
[208:0:16] - Called the "Dominion of India Bill" in the APPSC key – not the Nehru Report (1928) or the Sapru Report.
[208:0:17] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.328-329, p.643; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.96; NextGen Indian History 06 p.68, p.70, p.77; https://constitutionofindia.net/historical-constitution/the-commonwealth-of-india-bill-national-convention-india-1925; app notes 1:208 (sub 3, 4, 5, 6)
## SUB 1 | The Two Home Rule Leagues (1916)
[208:1:0] # Why and when
[208:1:1] - **Home Rule Movement** = the popular movement of India **during the First World War** (not Swadeshi, Separatist or Swaraj Party); on the lines of the **Irish Home Rule Leagues**; aim **self-government within the British Empire**.
[208:1:2] - Causes: need for popular pressure, disillusion with the **Morley-Minto reforms**, wartime miseries; **Tilak released from Mandalay (June 1914)** after six years.
[208:1:3] - **Two leagues, 1916**, led by **Tilak and Annie Besant** (not Aurobindo, Lajpat Rai or B.C. Pal); they demarcated areas and cooperated.
[208:1:4] # Comparison
[208:1:5] TABLE head: Feature | Tilak's Indian Home Rule League | Besant's All India Home Rule League
  [208:1:5.0] Founded | April 1916 (28 April) – the first league | September 1916
  [208:1:5.1] Place | Launched at the Bombay Provincial Conference, Belgaum; headquarters Poona | Madras (HQ Adyar)
  [208:1:5.2] Area | Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces, Berar | Rest of India, including Bombay city
  [208:1:5.3] Branches | 6 | about 200
  [208:1:5.4] Office-bearers | President Joseph Baptista; Secretary N.C. Kelkar | Organising secretary George Arundale; C.P. Ramaswamy Aiyar, B.P. Wadia
[208:1:6] - **Associated leaders:** **S. Subramania Iyer** (co-leader with Besant), G.S. Khaparde, Joseph Baptista, **Jinnah** (Bombay; also popularised it in UP), Motilal and Jawaharlal Nehru, Madan Mohan Malaviya, Tej Bahadur Sapru, Lajpat Rai.
[208:1:7] - **H.S. Olcott** – co-founder of the **Theosophical Society** (died 1907), **not** a Home Rule leader.
[208:1:8] - **C.R. Das** – the usual answer for "not associated" (he only joined the agitation later; Spectrum p.329 lists him among those who joined).
[208:1:9] - **1915 Bombay Congress:** Besant **failed** to get the Congress to approve her Home Rule League scheme – no Home Rule resolution.
[208:1:10] # Fate
[208:1:11] - Tilak's League later merged with Besant's; **1920:** Gandhi became President of the **All India Home Rule League** and renamed it **Swarajya Sabha**; it soon merged into the Congress.
[208:1:12] - **Year of merger of the two leagues = 1920** (not 1916, 1918 or 1923).
[208:1:13] > Statement trap (APPSC FRO): "Tilak founded the Indian Home Rule League in **1914** and was its **president**" ✗ (April 1916; president Joseph Baptista); "Tilak played a key role in the **Lucknow Pact (1916)** with Jinnah" ✓.
[208:1:14] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.327-331; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.94-97; NextGen Indian History 06 p.53-54, p.72; app notes 1:208 (sub 0, 1)
[208:1:15] - **Order:** Komagata Maru (1914) → Gandhi's return (Jan 1915) → Tilak's League (Apr 1916) → Besant's (Sep 1916) → Lucknow Pact (Dec 1916) → Montagu Declaration (Aug 1917).
[208:1:16] - **Madras Home Rule League:** General Secretary **C.P. Ramaswamy Aiyar**, Treasurer **B.P. Wadia**.
## SUB 2 | Tilak, Lucknow 1916 and the Lucknow Pact
[208:2:0] # Tilak
[208:2:1] - **"Father of Indian Unrest"** – **Valentine Chirol** (*Indian Unrest*); Tilak went to England (Sept 1918) to fight a libel case against him.
[208:2:2] - **"Lokmanya"** ("beloved / respected by the people") – exam keys tie it to the **Home Rule Movement**; Spectrum p.593 says he got it after his **1897 sedition trial**.
[208:2:3] - "**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it**" – Tilak; papers **Kesari (Marathi)** and **Mahratta (English)**.
[208:2:4] - Other quotes of the period: "The British Empire is **rotten to the core**, corrupt in every direction…" – **Sister Nivedita** (not Besant); "First we are Indians, then only Muslims" – **Jinnah**.
[208:2:5] # Lucknow session, December 1916
[208:2:6] - President **A.C. Majumdar**; the **Surat split (1907)** healed – Extremists readmitted; helped by Tilak's and Besant's efforts and the deaths of Gokhale and Pherozeshah Mehta (1915).
[208:2:7] - The session where the **Home Rule supporters demonstrated their political power** (the Home Rule Leagues held a joint meeting of 1,000+ delegates).
[208:2:8] - **Lucknow Pact (Congress–League):** negotiated by **Tilak and Jinnah**; Congress accepted **separate electorates**; "**Better two united fists…**" – Tilak.
[208:2:9] - Tilak proposed at Lucknow a small **Working Committee** to turn the Congress into a body able to lead a sustained movement (created only in 1920).
[208:2:10] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.331-334, p.593; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.93-97; NextGen Indian History 06 p.54; app notes 1:208 (sub 2, 6, 7); Working Committee proposal and Nivedita quote: general knowledge, not checked
## SUB 3 | Impact, Decline and the August Declaration
[208:3:0] # Repression and the British answer
[208:3:1] - **June 1917:** Besant, **B.P. Wadia** and **George Arundale** interned (Ootacamund); S. Subramania Aiyar renounced his knighthood; released **September 1917**.
[208:3:2] - Montagu: "Shiva … cut his wife into fifty-two pieces …" – on the folly of interning Besant.
[208:3:3] - **Montagu (August) Declaration, 20 August 1917:** by **Edwin Montagu**, Secretary of State (not Morley, Minto or Chelmsford) – "**progressive realisation of responsible government**" through self-governing institutions.
[208:3:4] # Why it faded (1918-19)
[208:3:5] - Moderates pacified by the reform promise; Besant's vacillation; the **Montford reforms (July 1918)** split the ranks; Tilak away in England; Gandhi's rise.
[208:3:6] # Why it was a new phase
[208:3:7] - It put the demand for **self-government (Home Rule)** before the nation as a clear programme; shifted the emphasis from the elite to the **masses**; linked town and country; created a generation of nationalists; prepared the ground for Gandhian politics.
[208:3:8] - It **filled the gap** between **Gokhale's death (1915)** and **Gandhi's rise**.
[208:3:9] - Not because Gandhi led it or because of Hindu-Muslim struggle (that is the Lucknow Pact / Khilafat).
[208:3:10] # Papers of the Home Rule era
[208:3:11] TABLE head: Paper | Person
  [208:3:11.0] Young India | Started (1916-19) as a weekly by the Home Rule party (Jamnadas Dwarkadas, Shankarlal Banker of Besant's league); Gandhi's from Oct 1919
  [208:3:11.1] The Leader | Madan Mohan Malaviya, Allahabad – mouthpiece of the Liberals
  [208:3:11.2] New India / Commonweal | Annie Besant
  [208:3:11.3] Free Press Journal | English daily (1930)
[208:3:12] # World-war context
[208:3:13] - **First World War (1914-18):** the **Treaty of Versailles (1919)** humiliated **Germany**.
[208:3:14] - **Second World War (1939-45):** the **deadliest conflict** in history by total casualties.
[208:3:15] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.330-331, p.335; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.96-98; Rocket Indian History Part 1 (newspapers table); app notes 1:208 (sub 4, 8); world-war facts: general knowledge, not checked
[208:3:16] - **Besant released 17 September 1917**, then elected INC President (Calcutta, December 1917).
[208:3:17] - **Congress special session, Bombay (August 1918)**, under **Hasan Imam**, split over the Mont-Ford proposals.
[208:3:18] - **Effect of the internment:** nationwide protest; it **strengthened** the movement and drew in the Moderates (Jinnah, Surendranath Banerjea, Malaviya).

## SUB 0 | Lahore Conspiracy Case (1929-31), the Executions and Jatin Das
[209:0:0] # The case
[209:0:1] - **Trial:** Bhagat Singh, **Sukhdev**, **Rajguru** and others were tried for the **Saunders murder** (and bomb-making) in the **Lahore Conspiracy Case** before a **special tribunal set up by the Governor-General**; the **death sentence came in October 1930**.
[209:0:2] - Bhagat Singh had already got life in the **Assembly Bomb Case**; the sentence waited for the verdict in the Saunders (Lahore) case.
[209:0:3] - The accused turned the **court into a forum of propaganda**; the **defence was organised by Congress leaders**.
[209:0:4] - **"Second" Lahore case:** the **first Lahore Conspiracy Case (1915)** was the **Ghadar** case – the first trial under the **Defence of India Act, 1915** (passed mainly to deal with the Ghadarites).

## SUB 0 | Rampa (Manyam) Rebellion, 1922-24
[210:0:0] # Nature and span
[210:0:1] - **Leader and period:** **Alluri Sitarama Raju** (not Aggi Raju, Gantam Dora, Ambul Reddy, Velu Thampi); **1922-1924** (first raid **22 Aug 1922** – Raju shot **7 May 1924**) – not 1919-20, 1921-22 or 1925-26.
[210:0:2] - **Area:** Agency tracts of East Godavari and Visakhapatnam – the **Gudem hills**; base among the **Koya** tribe (with Konda Doras and Konda Reddis) – not Bhils or Gonds.
[210:0:3] - **Character:** armed tribal guerrilla war, **outside the Congress**; caused the British more trouble than the NCM in the region.
[210:0:4] - **Method:** **raided police stations to seize British arms and used them against the British** – not non-cooperation or open war.
[210:0:5] # Chronology and places
[210:0:6] TABLE head: Date | Event
  [210:0:6.0] 22 Aug 1922 | Chintapalli – the first police station attacked (not Rampachodavaram, Krishnadevipeta, Rajavommangi, Annavaram or Addateegala)
  [210:0:6.1] Aug 1922 | Krishnadevipeta and Rajavommangi stations raided
  [210:0:6.2] Sept 1922 | Damanghat / Panjerighat – the first face-to-face battle with the British army; officers Scott Coward and Heiter killed
  [210:0:6.3] 6 Dec 1922 | Peddagaddapalem – first defeat of Raju's forces, a surprise attack by the Malabar Special Police; Raju escaped
  [210:0:6.4] April 1923 | Rebellion resumed – Annavaram police station attacked
  [210:0:6.5] 18 Sept 1923 | Lieutenant Gam Mallu Dora captured – first major setback (so 1923, not 1924)

## SUB 1 | Guntur No-Tax Campaigns – Pedanandipadu and Palnadu, 1921-22
[210:1:0] # Pedanandipadu no-tax campaign
[210:1:1] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [210:1:1.0] Place | Pedanandipadu firka, Bapatla taluka, Guntur district – "the" no-tax campaign of Andhra (not Chirala, Perala or Nellore)
  [210:1:1.1] Leader | Parvataneni Veeraiah Chowdary – "Andhra Shivaji"; volunteer force Shanti Sena

## SUB 2 | Chirala-Perala Movement and Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya, 1921
[210:2:0] # Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya
[210:2:1] - **Leader** of the **Chirala-Perala movement** (1921) – not Veeraiah Chowdary, Konda Venkatappayya, Prakasam or N.G. Ranga.
[210:2:2] - **Title:** **"Andhra Ratna"** – not Andhra Bhakta, Deshabandhu or Deenabandhu; also called "Ramadasu".
[210:2:3] - **Volunteer corps:** **"Ramadandu"** (1921) – not Shiva Sena, Girisena or Shanti Sena (Shanti Sena = Parvataneni Veeraiah Chowdary, Pedanandipadu).
[210:2:4] - **Life:** 1884-1929, born at **Penuganchiprolu** (Krishna); M.A. (Hons) Economics, **Edinburgh** (1916); met Gandhi in London (1915), introduced by **Ananda K. Coomaraswamy**.
[210:2:5] - **Education work:** founded the **Andhra Vidyapeetha Goshthi** near Chirala (national education); after the NCM an Andhra secretary of the **Swaraj Party**.
[210:2:6] # The movement
[210:2:7] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [210:2:7.0] Place | Chirala and Perala, Bapatla taluk, Guntur district – weavers and small farmers
  [210:2:7.1] Cause | The two villages were made a municipality; yearly tax rose from Rs 4,000 to Rs 40,000
  [210:2:7.2] Method | No-tax campaign (March 1921); Gandhi met Duggirala at the Vijayawada AICC (30-31 March 1921) and visited Chirala on 6 April 1921
  [210:2:7.3] Exodus | Midnight of 25 April 1921: 13,572 of 15,326 residents left their homes
  [210:2:7.4] New township | Ram Nagar (Ramnagar) outside municipal limits, with a parallel all-caste assembly (self-rule); Ramadandu built huts and dug wells; stay lasted 11 months
  [210:2:7.5] Arrest | Duggirala arrested after his speech at the Andhra Mahasabha, Berhampur (Sept-Oct 1921), for breaking a ban on speaking; tried by Rutherford at Srikakulam; one year's rigorous imprisonment
  [210:2:7.6] Failure | Lack of funds (the PCC did not grant even Rs 4,000), rivalry of Konda Venkatappayya and Pattabhi, no leader after his arrest
  [210:2:7.7] Gandhi's view | *Young India*: the municipality meant only more taxes and interference with liberty
  [210:2:7.8] Outcome | Municipality abolished in 1938 by Bezawada Gopala Reddy, minister in the Rajaji ministry
