=== Energy Forms and Conversions – the Physics Behind a Hydro Plant
# Hydro plant
- Stored water (**potential**) → falling water (**kinetic**) → turbine (**mechanical**) → generator (**electrical**, by electromagnetic induction). Net: **potential → electrical** (APPSC 2023).
- The **Mangal turbine** (water-lifting device of Mangal Singh) runs on the **kinetic energy of flowing water**.
# Forms of energy
- **Mechanical energy = kinetic + potential**; under a **conservative force** the **total mechanical energy** stays constant. SI unit of all energy = **joule** (watt = power).
- **Kinetic energy** = ½mv² – depends on **mass and velocity**; a fast car has kinetic energy.
- **Potential energy** = energy of position or shape: a **raised hammer**, a compressed spring or slinky.
- **Chemical energy:** stored in the **bonds of atoms and molecules** (food, fuels, cells). **Radiant energy** = electromagnetic, **transverse** waves; **sound energy** = **longitudinal** (compression–rarefaction) waves.
- **Stored forms:** chemical, nuclear, gravitational and elastic potential. **Electrical energy** is energy in transit, not a stored form (NDA 2016). Thermal energy is not a form of potential energy.
- **Free fall:** loss of potential energy = gain of kinetic energy; after 5 m of a 20 m fall the body has lost **one-fourth of its potential energy**. A body rolling down a hill has **both** KE and PE; a rising swing loses KE and gains PE.
- **Friction** turns **kinetic energy into heat**. **Lightning** carries huge **electrical energy**. **Fireworks:** chemical → heat, light, sound and kinetic energy (all).
- **Photosynthesis:** light energy → **chemical energy**.
| Device | Conversion |
|---|---|
| Dynamo / generator | **Mechanical → electrical** (a converter of energy, not a source of charge) |
| **Electric motor** | **Electrical → mechanical** |
| **Battery / dry cell** | **Chemical → electrical** |
| Photoelectric / solar cell | **Light → electrical** |
| Wind turbine | **Kinetic** energy of wind (only kinetic) → mechanical → electrical |
| Diesel engine | Chemical (fuel) → heat → mechanical (not "light to electrical") |
- **Photoelectric effect** discovered by **Heinrich Hertz (1887)**; explained by Einstein.

=== Pumped Storage Hydropower
- **Two reservoirs** at different heights: surplus/off-peak power **pumps water up**; at peak the water flows down through turbines – a "**giant water battery**".
- Discussed as **long-duration energy storage** (UPSC 2024); the fastest-responding large-scale storage.
- **No ISTS charges** for BESS and PSP **until 2028**; off-stream **closed-loop** PSPs need no CEA concurrence.
- CEA plan to 2035-36: **174 GW** storage = **94 GW PSP + 80 GW BESS**.
| PSP | State | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Tehri PSP | Uttarakhand | 1,000 MW; fully commissioned 12 Apr 2026 |
| Sharavathi PSP | Karnataka | 2,000 MW |

=== Types of Hydro Plants, Small Hydro and Potential
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Run-of-the-river (diversion)** | Little or no storage; natural flow |
| Storage (impoundment) | Dam stores water |
| Pumped storage | Surplus power pumps water up |
- **Small hydro ≤ 25 MW → MNRE**; large hydro > 25 MW → **Ministry of Power**. Up to 3 MW moved to MNRE in **1989**; raised to **25 MW** in **Nov 1999**. Large hydro declared **renewable** in **March 2019**.
- **Small-hydro target: 5 GW** within the 175 GW renewable target for 2022 (100 solar, 60 wind, 10 bio, 5 small hydro); installed small hydro ~5.18 GW (2026).
- Potential **over 145 GW**, about **36%** developed; **Arunachal Pradesh** largest. Small-hydro potential 21,133 MW at **7,133** sites.
- India **3rd** in large dams (**6,628** specified dams); most in **Maharashtra**. East-flowing peninsular rivers suit **irrigation** more than power.

=== Large Dams – Trade-offs, Heights and Construction
- **"Temples of modern India"** – **Jawaharlal Nehru**, of dams such as **Bhakra Nangal**.
- Hydro: cheap and clean once built; weak in **drought and snowpack** variability.
| Trade-off | Detail |
|---|---|
| Displacement | Bhakra, Sardar Sarovar, Tehri |
| Seismicity | Himalayan risk |
| Sedimentation | Cuts reservoir capacity |
| Ecology | **Blocks fish migration** |
- Dam heights in India: **Tehri > Bhakra > Idukki > Koldam**.
- World's highest dam in books: **Rogun (Tajikistan)**, about 335 m when complete (under construction); the tallest completed dam is Jinping-I, China (305 m).
- **Earthen dams:** **rolled-fill** methods = **homogeneous, zoned and diaphragm** embankments; the **hydraulic-fill** dam is a separate method.
