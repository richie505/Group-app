=== NOTES 5:57 | Coal Sector – Reserves, Production, Washing, Commercial Mining, Gasification
## SUB 0 | Coal Types, Carbon Content and Fuel Quality
[57:0:0] TABLE head: Type | Carbon (NextGen/PMF) | Notes
  [57:0:0.0] Anthracite | 80–95% | Highest carbon, best quality; hardest, little smoke, highest calorific value; J&K (Kalakot), small quantity
  [57:0:0.1] Bituminous | 40–80% | Most widely used; includes the coking grade; Jharkhand, Odisha, WB, Chhattisgarh, MP
  [57:0:0.2] Sub-bituminous | – | Between bituminous and lignite; used in power plants
  [57:0:0.3] Lignite (brown coal) | 40–55% | High moisture; Neyveli (TN), Palana (Rajasthan), Lakhimpur (Assam)
  [57:0:0.4] Peat | below 40% | First stage of wood turning into coal
[57:0:1] - Order of carbon: **anthracite > bituminous > sub-bituminous > lignite > peat**.
[57:0:2] - International (ASTM-style) tables give lignite **25–35%**, sub-bituminous 35–45%, bituminous 45–86%, anthracite 86–97% – use these when the options are on that scale.
[57:0:3] - **Calorific value** depends mainly on the **carbon content** (moisture and ash lower it).
[57:0:4] - A good fuel has a **high** calorific value, low moisture, harmless combustion products and controllable burning – "low calorific value" is not a property of a good fuel.
[57:0:5] - **Coal is a natural (fossil) fuel**; petrol and diesel are refined, charcoal is made from wood. **Lignite** contains carbon (tin, silver, iron are metals).
[57:0:6] # Gondwana vs Tertiary coal
[57:0:7] - **Gondwana** (~250 million years): **98% of reserves**, 99% of production; coking and bituminous coal; Damodar, Mahanadi, Son, Godavari, Wardha valleys – hence Gondwana rocks are India's most important rock system.
[57:0:8] - **Damuda series** (Lower Gondwana): India's **metallurgical (coking) coal** and the most productive fields.
[57:0:9] - **Tertiary** (15–60 million years): younger, **low carbon, high moisture and sulphur**; lignite (chief producer **Tamil Nadu**); **North-East India and J&K** (extra-peninsular), about 2% – not "mainly Peninsular". Bituminous coal is a Gondwana coal.

## SUB 2 | Indian Coal Quality, Imports, Washing and Health
[57:2:0] - **Indian coal:** **high ash (35–45%)**, **low calorific value**, **low sulphur (~0.5%)** and a **high ash-fusion temperature (~1500°C)** (UPSC 2013: "1 and 2 only").
[57:2:1] - **Pollution (UPSC 2020, all correct):** coal ash contains **arsenic, lead and mercury**; coal plants emit **SO₂ and NOx**; Indian coal has high ash.
[57:2:2] - **Imports:** **coking coal** mainly from **Australia** (the steel industry imports it, UPSC 2015); non-coking from **Indonesia, South Africa**.
[57:2:3] - **Coal washing (APPSC G1 2024 A–R):** washing raises generation efficiency and cuts costs, but washed-coal use stays limited because it raises the cost of power and hurts plants in **merit-order** dispatch – **A and R true, R explains A**.
[57:2:4] - **Problems:** poor quality, movement bottlenecks, **low washery use**, rising coking-coal imports – not "excess high-quality coking coal".
[57:2:5] - **Pneumoconiosis** (black lung): dust disease of **coal-mine** workers.

## SUB 5 | Gasification, CBM and UCG
[57:5:0] - **Gasification:** coal + oxygen + steam → **syngas (CO + H₂ + CH₄ + CO₂)**; runs **IGCC** plants.
[57:5:1] - Products: SNG, methanol, **ethanol**, **urea**/ammonia, hydrogen, DME – **not nitroglycerine** (UPSC 2025).
[57:5:2] - **CBM** = "**sweet gas**"; free gas in joints, **adsorbed** in micropores.

## SUB 0 | Fuel Gases – LPG, Natural Gas, CNG, LNG and Their Chemistry
[58:0:0] # What each gas is made of
[58:0:1] TABLE head: Fuel | Main constituents | Key facts
  [58:0:1.0] Natural gas | Mostly methane (CH₄), with some ethane, propane, CO₂, N₂ | Cleanest fossil fuel; burnt directly or piped
  [58:0:1.1] CNG – Compressed Natural Gas | Methane (80–90%) | Natural gas compressed to 200–250 kg/cm²; vehicles
  [58:0:1.2] PNG – Piped Natural Gas | 85–95% methane | Supplied to homes through city gas networks
  [58:0:1.3] LNG – Liquefied Natural Gas | Methane | Cooled to about −160°C; volume shrinks about 600 times; carried in special cryogenic tankers; lighter than air
  [58:0:1.4] LPG – Liquefied Petroleum Gas | Propane + butane (roughly 65% butane, 35% propane; a little ethane) | Refinery / gas by-product; liquefied under pressure; heavier than air
[58:0:2] - **LPG is NOT mainly methane** (IAS 2005: A false, R true). When only one gas is asked, **butane** is the chief constituent; the 3-gas option is **ethane, propane and butane**.
[58:0:3] - **LPG is odourless** in its natural state; **ethyl mercaptan (ethanethiol)** is added so that a **leak can be smelt** (the "rotten" stench).
[58:0:4] - **Butane (C₄H₁₀)** is the gas in **cigarette lighters**; it is a **Natural Gas Liquid (NGL)**.
[58:0:5] - **Propane** (an LPG gas) is a clean-burning fuel; coke, petrol and wax are not "clean energy" (NDA 2019).
[58:0:6] - **Methane** is a **greenhouse gas** (ethane, propane, butane are not counted as GHGs); in the air it oxidises to CO₂ in about a decade.
[58:0:7] - **Methane + air** (firedamp, 5–15% methane) causes most **explosions in coal mines**.
[58:0:8] # Other fuel facts asked
[58:0:9] TABLE head: Fuel / term | Fact
  [58:0:9.0] Water gas | CO + H₂ (producer gas = CO + H₂ + N₂)
  [58:0:9.1] Acetylene (ethyne) | Gas welding (oxy-acetylene flame)
  [58:0:9.2] Kerosene | Calorific value about the same as petrol (~45,000 kJ/kg); LPG ~55,000, methane/CNG ~50,000
  [58:0:9.3] Hydrocarbon order by molecular weight | Methane (16) < ethane (30) < propane (44) < butane (58)
  [58:0:9.4] Petroleum | A mixture of many hydrocarbons of different densities; its grade depends on their relative proportion (NDA 2016: both true, II does not explain I)

## SUB 0 | Energy Forms and Conversions – the Physics Behind a Hydro Plant
[60:0:0] # Hydro plant
[60:0:1] - Stored water (**potential**) → falling water (**kinetic**) → turbine (**mechanical**) → generator (**electrical**, by electromagnetic induction). Net: **potential → electrical** (APPSC 2023).
[60:0:2] - The **Mangal turbine** (water-lifting device of Mangal Singh) runs on the **kinetic energy of flowing water**.
[60:0:3] # Forms of energy
[60:0:4] - **Mechanical energy = kinetic + potential**; under a **conservative force** the **total mechanical energy** stays constant. SI unit of all energy = **joule** (watt = power).
[60:0:5] - **Kinetic energy** = ½mv² – depends on **mass and velocity**; a fast car has kinetic energy.
[60:0:6] - **Potential energy** = energy of position or shape: a **raised hammer**, a compressed spring or slinky.
[60:0:7] - **Chemical energy:** stored in the **bonds of atoms and molecules** (food, fuels, cells). **Radiant energy** = electromagnetic, **transverse** waves; **sound energy** = **longitudinal** (compression–rarefaction) waves.
[60:0:8] - **Stored forms:** chemical, nuclear, gravitational and elastic potential. **Electrical energy** is energy in transit, not a stored form (NDA 2016). Thermal energy is not a form of potential energy.
[60:0:9] - **Free fall:** loss of potential energy = gain of kinetic energy; after 5 m of a 20 m fall the body has lost **one-fourth of its potential energy**. A body rolling down a hill has **both** KE and PE; a rising swing loses KE and gains PE.
[60:0:10] - **Friction** turns **kinetic energy into heat**. **Lightning** carries huge **electrical energy**. **Fireworks:** chemical → heat, light, sound and kinetic energy (all).
[60:0:11] - **Photosynthesis:** light energy → **chemical energy**.
[60:0:12] TABLE head: Device | Conversion
  [60:0:12.0] Dynamo / generator | Mechanical → electrical (a converter of energy, not a source of charge)
  [60:0:12.1] Electric motor | Electrical → mechanical
  [60:0:12.2] Battery / dry cell | Chemical → electrical
  [60:0:12.3] Photoelectric / solar cell | Light → electrical
  [60:0:12.4] Wind turbine | Kinetic energy of wind (only kinetic) → mechanical → electrical
  [60:0:12.5] Diesel engine | Chemical (fuel) → heat → mechanical (not "light to electrical")
[60:0:13] - **Photoelectric effect** discovered by **Heinrich Hertz (1887)**; explained by Einstein.
