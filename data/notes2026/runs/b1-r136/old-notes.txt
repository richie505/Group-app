=== NOTES 1:136 | Mughal Society – Nobility, Peasantry, Position of Women, Urban Classes
## SUB 0 | Nobility (umara)
[136:0:0] - **Umara** = top mansabdars; wealthy but **wholly dependent on the emperor's favour**.
[136:0:1] - Cosmopolitan body: **Turanis** (Central Asians), **Iranis** (Persians), **Afghans**, **Hindustanis** (Indian Muslims), **Rajputs**, later **Deccanis**.
[136:0:2] TABLE head: Reign | Shift in nobility
  [136:0:2.0] Babur–Humayun | Turanis and Iranis dominated
  [136:0:2.1] Akbar | Added Rajputs and Hindustanis to balance them
  [136:0:2.2] Aurangzeb | Deccanis and Marathas swelled it after the Deccan wars; Hindu share about one-third (M. Athar Ali)
[136:0:3] Turanis = Central Asians; Iranis = Persians; Hindustanis = Indian Muslims.
## SUB 1 | Peasantry
[136:1:0] TABLE head: Category | Meaning
  [136:1:0.0] Khud-kasht | Resident peasant cultivating land in own village
  [136:1:0.1] Pahi-kasht | Non-resident cultivator of land in another village
  [136:1:0.2] Muzarian | Tenants cultivating land held by others
  [136:1:0.3] Raiyats | Peasants – backbone of the economy; bore the land revenue
## SUB 2 | Court Customs and Festivals
[136:2:0] TABLE head: Custom | Origin / note
  [136:2:0.0] Jharokha darshan | Emperor showed himself daily at a balcony; begun by Akbar, ended by Aurangzeb
  [136:2:0.1] Tuladan | Weighing the emperor against gold and goods on his birthday, given to the poor – Hindu custom adopted by Akbar
  [136:2:0.2] Sijda / paibos | Prostration / kissing the feet – introduced by Akbar (not Shah Jahan)
  [136:2:0.3] Nauroz | Persian New Year; made a court festival by Akbar; abolished by Aurangzeb
[136:2:1] - **AP angle**: Qutb Shahi Golconda shared **Nauroz** and **Muharram (Peerla Panduga)**.
## SUB 3 | Position of Women
[136:3:0] TABLE head: Royal woman | Role
  [136:3:0.0] Gulbadan Begum | Babur's daughter; wrote the Humayun-nama
  [136:3:0.1] Nur Jahan | Married Jahangir 1611; Padshah Begum; name on coins and farmans; built Itimad-ud-Daula's tomb (Agra)
  [136:3:0.2] Jahanara | Shah Jahan's daughter, Padshah Begum; built Jama Masjid, Agra and a sarai at Chandni Chowk
  [136:3:0.3] Zeb-un-Nissa | Aurangzeb's daughter; poet with pen name Makhfi
[136:3:1] Jahanara = Jama Masjid, Agra; Shah Jahan = Jama Masjid, Delhi and Agra Fort Moti Masjid.
[136:3:2] - **Purdah**: Muslim women strictly veiled, carried in covered palanquins; Hindu women in the Deccan moved freely (**John Fryer**, *A New Account of East India and Persia*).
[136:3:3] - **Social evils**: **sati, child marriage, polygamy** (Bernier, Tavernier); Akbar tried to stop **forced sati**.
[136:3:4] - **Property**: Muslim women – **inheritance shares + mehr** under sharia; Hindu women – **stridhana**.
## SUB 4 | Urban Classes
[136:4:0] - Not merchants and soldiers only: **nagarseths, Marwaris, Bohras, Chettis** + **artisans** (guilds, state karkhanas or private).
[136:4:1] - Professionals: **hakims**, teachers, lawyers, painters, musicians.
[136:4:2] - **Bernier**: no real **middle class** in Delhi – very rich or very poor.
[136:4:3] - **Firangi Mahal (Lucknow)** – madrasa of Muslim jurisprudence (UPPSC 2016); **Mulla Nizamuddin** (Sihali) founded the **Dars-e-Nizami**.
## SUB 5 | Slavery and Domestic Servants
[136:5:0] - Akbar **stopped enslavement of prisoners of war (1562)**, yet noble households stayed full of **slaves and large domestic staffs**.
[136:5:1] - **Pelsaert** (Dutch factor) contrasted nobles' luxury with the misery of servants and workmen.

=== NOTES 1:137 | Mughal Architecture – Fatehpur Sikri, Humayun's Tomb, Taj Mahal, Red Fort; Garden and Tomb Traditions
## SUB 0 | Mughal Style – an Overview
[137:0:0] - Zenith of **Indo-Islamic architecture**: a blend of **Persian, Timurid and Indian** elements.
[137:0:1] - Hallmarks: red sandstone + white marble, **charbagh**, **double dome**, **pietra dura**, calligraphy, arabesque, **jali** (lattice) work.
[137:0:2] - **Akbar** built in **red sandstone**; **Shah Jahan** in **white marble** – his reign is the **"Golden Age"** of Mughal architecture.
[137:0:3] Red sandstone (Akbar) → transition (Jahangir, Itimad-ud-Daula) → white marble (Shah Jahan, Taj).
