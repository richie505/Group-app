## SUB 0 | Fatehpur Sikri – Akbar's Capital
[0:0] - **Fatehpur Sikri** ("City of Victory") – Akbar's planned capital **1571–85**, about 37–40 km from Agra, at Sikri where the Chishti saint **Shaikh Salim Chishti** lived (title **Shaikh-ul-Hind**; Prince **Salim** named after him); mainly **red sandstone**; UNESCO 1986. The **finest monuments of Akbar** are here (RAS 1994) – not in the Agra, Lahore or Allahabad forts.
[0:1] TABLE head: Building | Exam fact
  [0:1.0] Buland Darwaza | "Gate of Magnificence", ~41 m; built by Akbar (c. 1575) to commemorate the victory over Gujarat (1572–73) – not Jahangir's birth (A true, R false – UP 1998, UPPCS 2025); southern entrance to the Jama Masjid; red and buff sandstone with marble inlay
  [0:1.1] Jama Masjid | Congregational mosque, among the first buildings of the city; called 'Shan-i-Fatehpur' (UKPCS 2016)
  [0:1.2] Tomb of Salim Chishti | White-marble tomb in the Jama Masjid courtyard (built under Akbar)
  [0:1.3] Panch Mahal | Five-storeyed, entirely columnar pavilion in the style of a Buddhist monastery / vihara (Jharkhand 2003; RPSC 2023); ground floor 84 pillars; Persian badgir (wind-catcher) – the Fatehpur Sikri building in lists with Moti / Rang / Hira Mahal (UPPCS 2004; CDS 2003)
  [0:1.4] Jodha Bai's palace | Largest palace; Hindu trabeate + Islamic arcuate; Gujarati and Rajasthani patterns
  [0:1.5] Maryam's palace | Gilded paintings – the Golden Palace (Sunehra Makan)
  [0:1.6] Diwan-i-Khas | Private audience; single central carved pillar
  [0:1.7] Ibadat Khana | House of Worship (1575) – debates of scholars of different religions
  [0:1.8] Others | Diwan-i-Aam, Birbal ki Kothi, Turkish Sultana's house, Anup Talao, Hiran Minar
[0:2] - **Not at Fatehpur Sikri:** **Akbari Mahal** – it is in **Agra Fort** (UKPCS 2012).
[0:3] - **Built in Akbar's reign (APPSC G1):** Tomb of Salim Chishti, Buland Darwaza, Jami Masjid (Fatehpur Sikri) **and** Humayun's Tomb (1565–72) – **all four**.
[0:4] - **UPSC 2018:** white marble was **not** the material of the Buland Darwaza and the Khanqah (red/buff sandstone), and the Bara Imambara and Rumi Darwaza of Lucknow are **brick and lime plaster**, not red sandstone and marble → **neither** statement correct.
[0:5] - Style: Persian, Indian, Central Asian; introduced the **Tudor (four-centred) arch**, continued at **Jahangir Mahal, Orchha**.

## SUB 1 | Tomb Tradition – Lodi to Bibi ka Maqbara
[1:0] TABLE head: Stage | Tomb | Feature
  [1:0.0] Lodi | Sikandar Lodi's tomb – built by son Ibrahim Lodi | First double dome
  [1:0.1] Sur | Sher Shah's tomb, Sasaram – he built it himself; set in a tank; finished under Islam Shah (c. 1545) | Transition Lodi → Mughal
  [1:0.2] Akbar | Humayun's tomb, Delhi (1565–72) | First garden tomb; first full Persian double dome; red sandstone with white marble; Persian + Indian synthesis (RAS 2003)
  [1:0.3] Jahangir | Itimad-ud-Daula, Agra (1622–28) – built by Nur Jahan for her father Mirza Ghiyas Beg ("Baby Taj") | First tomb wholly of white marble; first pietra dura
  [1:0.4] Shah Jahan | Taj Mahal | Climax
  [1:0.5] Aurangzeb | Bibi ka Maqbara, Aurangabad – tomb of Rabia-ud-Daurani (Dilras Banu), "Taj of the Deccan", "Second Taj" | Last major imitation of the garden tomb
[1:1] - **Humayun's tomb:** built by his widow **Haji Begum (Bega Begum)** – the woman who built her **emperor husband's** mausoleum (UPPSC 2004) – not Mumtaz Mahal or Nur Jahan; architects **Mirak Mirza Ghiyas** and son **Sayyid Muhammad**.
[1:2] - **Bibi ka Maqbara:** built in **Aurangzeb's** reign (keyed "Aurangzeb", RAS 1999) by his son **Azam Shah**; architect **Ata-ullah**, son of Ustad Ahmad Lahori.
[1:3] - **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan's tomb** – **Delhi**, near Humayun's tomb (UPPSC 2010).
[1:4] - **Chronology (UPPSC 2019):** Atala Mosque, Jaunpur (**1408**) → Sher Shah's tomb, Sasaram (c. 1545) → Humayun's tomb (1565–72) → Bibi ka Maqbara (c. 1660) = **IV, II, III, I**.
[1:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Indian History 02 p.15, p.20 (tombs table: Haji Begum, Itmad-ud-Daula 1622–1628, Azam Shah); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 05 p.15 (Humayun's Tomb – Haji Begum); PMF Art & Culture 04 p.3-4, p.8 (double dome, Itmad-ud-Daula first white-marble building, Atala Masjid); app notes 1:137 (sub 2)

## SUB 6 | Mughal Style and Features
[6:0] - Mughal style = **Indo-Islamic + Persian (Timurid) grandeur + Hindu** grace: bulbous domes, slender minarets, vaulted gateways, **charbagh**, **double dome**, **jali**, calligraphy.
[6:1] - **Akbar** built in **red sandstone**; **Shah Jahan** in **white marble** – his reign is the **"Golden Age"** of Mughal architecture.
[6:2] TABLE head: Feature | Meaning | First / best seen
  [6:2.0] Pietra dura | Decorating walls with floral designs of inlaid semi-precious stones | Began under Jahangir; first at Itimad-ud-Daula; peak at the Taj
  [6:2.1] Double dome | Inner and outer shells | Sikandar Lodi's tomb; first full Mughal one – Humayun's tomb
  [6:2.2] Charbagh | Four-quartered garden | Introduced by Babur (Ram Bagh)
  [6:2.3] Jali | Lattice work | Humayun's tomb
  [6:2.4] Sheesh Mahal | Mirror-work chambers | Agra Fort, Red Fort
[6:3] - Contrast: the **Bahmani Jama Masjid, Gulbarga** has a **fully covered courtyard**, unlike Mughal open-court mosques (and not Charminar).
[6:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Art & Culture 04 p.3 (Mughal style); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 04 p.17-18 (pietra dura from Jahangir); app notes 1:137 (sub 0, 7)
