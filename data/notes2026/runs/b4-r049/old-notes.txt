=== NOTES 4:49 | Andhra Pradesh – Climate, Rainfall Regions, Cyclone Vulnerability, Drought-prone Areas
## SUB 0 | Climate and Seasons of Ap
[49:0:0] - Climate: generally **hot and humid**; summer temperatures quite high (IMD report; APPSC 2025 key).
[49:0:1] - Rayalaseema = **semi-arid**; coastal Andhra = humid, cyclone-exposed.
[49:0:2] TABLE head: Season | Period | Note
  [49:0:2.0] Winter | January–February | mostly dry, 13–30 °C; light rain from retreating systems and western disturbances
  [49:0:2.1] Summer | March to mid-June | hot; mango showers in April–May (pre-monsoon, help mangoes ripen)
  [49:0:2.2] SW monsoon | mid-June to September | starts in the second week of June; peaks in July
  [49:0:2.3] Retreating / NE monsoon | October–December | October = transition from rains to winter; withdraws Oct–Nov
[49:0:3] - Time order: **Winter → Summer → SW monsoon → Retreating monsoon**.
[49:0:4] - India-wide: SW monsoon June–September; retreating monsoon October–November; monsoon arrives **first in Kerala**.
[49:0:5] - Summer monsoon blows **SW → NE**; retreating monsoon **NE → SW**.
[49:0:6] # Temperature Extremes → Place
[49:0:7] TABLE head: Extreme | Place
  [49:0:7.0] Hottest place | Rentachintala (Palnadu)
  [49:0:7.1] Other hot spots | Kurnool, Kadapa, Nandyal, Vijayawada, Nandigama (NTR)
  [49:0:7.2] Heat-wave belt | Palnadu, Kurnool, Anantapur (peak May–June, rising)
  [49:0:7.3] Coldest place | Lambasingi (Alluri Sitharama Raju) – "Kashmir of AP", about 2 °C; snow-like frost (APPSC-GS 2019)
[49:0:8] - IMD: Heat Action Plans in **23 heatwave-prone** regions (2026).
[49:0:9] - CMIP6 study (7 Jun 2026): excess SW-monsoon rain for AP and TN; **1 °C-plus** rise in 40 coastal districts by 2040.
## SUB 1 | Rainfall – Two Monsoons
[49:1:0] TABLE head: Basis | SW monsoon | Retreating / NE monsoon
  [49:1:0.0] Share of AP rain | about two-thirds (65–70%) | about one-third
  [49:1:0.1] Peak month | July (wettest) | October
  [49:1:0.2] Key region | most of AP | Rayalaseema, Nellore, Tirupati, Prakasam (south coast)
  [49:1:0.3] Normal (2024-25) | 574.7 mm | 285.3 mm
[49:1:1] - Normal annual rainfall ≈ **860 mm**.
[49:1:2] - Bay of Bengal cyclones bring much of the Oct–Dec rain to southern States.
[49:1:3] - **River floods** come mainly in the **SW monsoon (Jul–Sep)**; most **cyclones** in **Oct–Dec**.
[49:1:4] # AP vs Neighbours (NE-Monsoon Rain)
[49:1:5] - India gets maximum rain from the **SW monsoon (about 75%)**; Jharkhand too.
[49:1:6] - **Tamil Nadu** gets the **maximum NE-monsoon rain** (40–50% of its annual rain); its main rainy months **November–December**; **Chennai** has the lowest SW-monsoon rain.
[49:1:7] - NE-monsoon rain falls on the **Coromandel coast** (Köppen **As**), southern AP, SE Karnataka, SE Kerala – **not the Gujarat coast**.
[49:1:8] - AP lies **in between**: about one-third of its rain in Oct–Dec.
[49:1:9] - **Gujarat** (semi-arid) has annual rainfall variability **above 60%**.
[49:1:10] # IMD Report and 2026 Deficit
[49:1:11] - IMD: **"Observed Rainfall Variability and Changes over Andhra Pradesh State"** – variability **quite high**; **least rainfall = South-western region (Rayalaseema)** (APPSC-GS 2023); summer begins in March.
[49:1:12] - **2026 El Niño year**: SW-monsoon deficit **51.2%** (1 Jun–25 Aug); **all 28 districts** deficient; cause: El Niño subsidence + all seven lows at the head of the Bay.
[49:1:13] - El Niño review (8 Jul 2026): round-the-clock control rooms at State and district levels.

## SUB 4 | Rain-shadow Rayalaseema and Drought
[49:4:0] - Rayalaseema lies in the **rain shadow** of the **Western Ghats + Karnataka–Telangana highlands** → little SW-monsoon rain → depends on the NE monsoon.
[49:4:1] - When the NE monsoon fails → **chronic drought** in Rayalaseema and **southern Prakasam**; falling groundwater worsens it.
[49:4:2] - **Anantapur = lowest-rainfall district**.
[49:4:3] - Rain-shadow areas of India: interior **Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, AP, Tamil Nadu**; western Rajasthan is scanty (Thar).
[49:4:4] - Greatest rural distress: **Prakasam, Anantapur, Kurnool, Kadapa** (rain failure → indebtedness).
[49:4:5] - Areas under **750 mm** a year are classed **chronically drought prone**; 750–1,125 mm = drought prone.
[49:4:6] # Drought-Proofing Projects
[49:4:7] - **Watershed projects** raise groundwater but **yields stay flat** when **crop diversification and soil management** are neglected (APPSC 2025 key).
[49:4:8] - **WDC-PMKSY 2.0**: degraded and rain-fed land via integrated natural-resource management (**Dept of Land Resources**).
[49:4:9] TABLE head: Project | Fact
  [49:4:9.0] Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing | Kurnool, Jul 2026; 92,000 acres; millets, pulses, oilseeds
  [49:4:9.1] Veligonda Phase-I | Krishna water to Prakasam, Nellore, Kadapa
  [49:4:9.2] 100-day water plan | launched at Yadiki (Anantapur), 6 Apr 2026; Water Users' Associations, desilting, check dams
  [49:4:9.3] Gandikota reservoir | on the Pennar, part of Telugu Ganga, YSR Kadapa
## SUB 5 | Drought Management Framework
[49:5:0] - **NDMA Drought Guidelines 2010**; **Manual for Drought Management revised 2016** (Ministry of Agriculture) – **multi-indicator** approach, at least three triggers.
[49:5:1] - **Mandatory** indicators: **rainfall deviation + dry spells**; **impact** indicators: crop sowing, satellite vegetation index (NDVI), soil moisture, hydrology.
[49:5:2] - Meteorological drought trigger: seasonal rain below **75% of normal**.
[49:5:3] TABLE head: Institution | Role
  [49:5:3.0] Crop Weather Watch Group | Ministry of Agriculture; meets weekly in the monsoon; uses meteorological, hydrological and agricultural (not geological) parameters
  [49:5:3.1] NADAMS | satellite monitoring of agricultural drought
  [49:5:3.2] Nodal ministry | Agriculture for drought, pest attack, hailstorm, frost/cold wave; MHA for other disasters
[49:5:4] - **Swaraj Abhiyan v. Union of India (2016)**: SC ordered the drought manual updated and a **National Disaster Mitigation Fund** set up.
## SUB 6 | Cyclone Vulnerability
[49:6:0] - Coastline **1,053.07 km** (MHA remeasurement, Dec 2024); **third-longest** after **Gujarat (2,340.62 km)** and **Tamil Nadu (1,068.69 km)**; old figure **974 km** (then longest on the east coast).
[49:6:1] - Bay cyclones form between **8° N and 14° N**; peak **October–December** (late October typical).
[49:6:2] - **Most vulnerable stretch: Ongole to Machilipatnam** (APPSC-GS 2018).
[49:6:3] - About **44%** of the State is vulnerable to **tropical storms** and related hazards.
[49:6:4] - **Flood Hazard Atlas of AP** (**NRSC**, with West Bengal): built from multi-date **satellite data 2000–2020**.
[49:6:5] - **Most exposed districts: Nellore and Srikakulam**; cyclone-prone: Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Nellore (APPSC 2025 key).
[49:6:6] - **Storm surge** can flood villages up to **10 km inland**.
[49:6:7] - **IMD Cyclone Warning Centre, Visakhapatnam** – one of seven (Chennai, Mumbai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Thiruvananthapuram, Visakhapatnam, Bhubaneswar).
[49:6:8] - IMD warned of the season's first cyclone over the Andaman Sea (19–21 Sep 2026), likely to cross Odisha.

## SUB 8 | Cyclone Mitigation
[49:8:0] - **NCRMP**: **World Bank**-assisted; cyclone shelters, early warning, last-mile connectivity; **Phase I = AP and Odisha** (cost **Rs 2,541.60 crore**).
[49:8:1] - NCRMP-I built **219 multipurpose cyclone shelters (MPCS)** in AP (Odisha 316).
[49:8:2] - **NDMA Cyclone Guidelines 2008**: structural – multipurpose shelters, raised platforms, cattle shelters, evacuation roads, **storm-surge barriers**; nature-based – **mangroves, bio-shields, saline embankments, sand-dune restoration**.
[49:8:3] - Roof: **hipped roof** firmly anchored to walls beats a gable roof; **pyramidal roof** best reduces wind force.
[49:8:4] - Tools: **Doppler radar** network; **SCATSAT-1** (ISRO scatterometer) for cyclone tracking; GIS mapping.
