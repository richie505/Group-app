## SUB 0 | Cyclone Vulnerability and Major Cyclones of AP
[0:0] # Why AP is cyclone-prone
[0:1] - **Coastline:** **1,053.07 km**, **3rd longest** in India after **Gujarat (2,340.62 km)** and **Tamil Nadu (1,068.69 km)** (PIB, 4 Dec 2025); the old figure **974 km ("2nd")** is still quoted in older reports such as the Flood Hazard Atlas 2021.
[0:2] - **Flood Hazard Atlas of AP (2021, NRSC):** AP is **more vulnerable to cyclones than to other disasters**; at risk of **at least one cyclone a year** on average; **moderate to severe cyclones every 2–3 years**; **most cyclones in October and November** – all four statements true.
[0:3] - **Bay cyclones** form between **8° N and 14° N**; on the AP coast they come mostly in the **last week of October** and in **October–December**.
[0:4] - **Month with most storms crossing the AP coast** (IMD count, 1891–2012): **October (about 30 of 73)**, then November (19); most fall in the NE-monsoon season.
[0:5] - About **44%** of the State is vulnerable to **tropical storms** and related hazards (AP Revenue Department).
[0:6] - **Storm surge** can flood villages up to **10 km inland**.
[0:7] - **IMD Cyclone Warning Centre** at **Visakhapatnam**.
[0:8] # Most exposed districts and stretch
[0:9] TABLE head: Item | Answer
  [0:9.0] Most vulnerable stretch | Ongole to Machilipatnam
  [0:9.1] Most exposed districts (long coast, Bay proximity) | Nellore and Srikakulam
  [0:9.2] Cyclone- and flood-prone coastal districts | Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Nellore
  [0:9.3] Drought-prone, not cyclone-prone | Rayalaseema – Anantapur, Kurnool (inland)
[0:10] - **Regional profile:** coastal Andhra = cyclones, storm surge, floods; Godavari–Krishna deltas = river floods; **Rayalaseema = drought** (arid, low rainfall); inland plains = heat waves – so AP needs **multi-hazard** planning.

## SUB 1 | Cyclone Mitigation – NCRMP and Shelters
[1:0] # National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project (NCRMP)
[1:1] - **World Bank**-assisted; run by **NDMA (MHA)**, project HQ **New Delhi**; launched **2011** (Phase I).
[1:2] - Aims: upgrade **forecasting, tracking and warning**, build capacity in **multi-hazard risk management**, and build **multipurpose cyclone shelters** and **embankments**.
[1:3] - **Phase I = Andhra Pradesh and Odisha** only; built **219 multipurpose cyclone shelters (MPCS)** in AP (Odisha 316).
[1:4] TABLE head: NCRMP category | States
  [1:4.0] Category I (higher vulnerability) | Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Odisha, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal
  [1:4.1] Category II (lower vulnerability) | Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Goa, Puducherry, Lakshadweep, Daman & Diu, A&N
[1:5] # Shelters and buildings
[1:6] - Coastal people are best saved by **building cyclone shelters**; in normal times MPCS serve as **community centres** (and schools).
[1:7] - **Roof:** a **pyramidal roof** (four slopes meeting at a point) best reduces wind force; a **hip roof** firmly anchored to the walls beats a **gable** roof; flat and gable roofs fare worst.
[1:8] - **NDMA Cyclone Guidelines 2008:** structural – shelters, raised platforms, cattle shelters, evacuation roads, **storm-surge barriers**; nature-based – **mangroves, bio-shields**, saline embankments, sand-dune restoration.
[1:9] - Tools: **Doppler radars**; **SCATSAT-1** (ISRO) for cyclone tracking; GIS mapping.

## SUB 2 | Climate and Rainfall – Two Monsoons
[2:0] # Climate and seasons
[2:1] - Climate **hot and humid**; summer **March to June**, when the **moisture level is quite high** (IMD report); Rayalaseema is **semi-arid**.
[2:2] TABLE head: Season | Period | Note
  [2:2.0] Winter | January–February | 13–30 °C
  [2:2.1] Summer | March to mid-June | mango showers in April–May (pre-monsoon)
  [2:2.2] SW monsoon | mid-June to September | onset in the 2nd week of June
  [2:2.3] Retreating / NE monsoon | October–December | October = transition from rains to winter
[2:3] - Order: **Winter → Summer → SW monsoon → Retreating monsoon**.
[2:4] - **Coldest place:** **Lambasingi** – "Kashmir of AP" (erstwhile **Visakhapatnam**, now **Alluri Sitharama Raju** district). **Hottest:** Rentachintala (Palnadu); heat-wave belt Palnadu, Kurnool, Anantapur.
[2:5] # Two monsoons
[2:6] TABLE head: Basis | SW monsoon | NE / retreating monsoon
  [2:6.0] Share of AP rain | about two-thirds (IMD 1989–2018: 57%) | about one-third (≈33%)
  [2:6.1] Wettest months | NextGen/Rocket: July; IMD 1989–2018 means: September (146 mm) > August (143) > July (128) > June (96) | October–November
  [2:6.2] Key region | most of AP, esp. north coast | Nellore and southern coastal districts; Rayalaseema depends on it
[2:7] - **"Most of AP's rain falls in August–September"** – the exam key; by IMD means August and September are the two wettest months.
[2:8] - **NE-monsoon maximum district:** **Nellore** (retreating monsoon brings heavy rain to Nellore and the south coast).
[2:9] - Normal annual rainfall about **860 mm** (574.7 SW + 285.3 NE); river floods mainly in the **SW monsoon (Jul–Sep)**, cyclones in Oct–Dec.
[2:10] - India gets about **75%** of its rain from the SW monsoon; the main exception is the **Coromandel coast of Tamil Nadu** (max NE-monsoon rain); the Gujarat coast gets none.
[2:11] - 2026 El Niño year: SW-monsoon deficit **51.2%** (1 Jun–25 Aug), all 28 districts deficient.
[2:12] # Rainfall regions
[2:13] - **IMD "Observed Rainfall Variability and Changes over Andhra Pradesh State" (2020):** least rainfall = **south-western region (Rayalaseema)** – **Anantapur** lowest (558.5 mm a year); highest SW-monsoon rain in north coastal districts.
[2:14] - **Heavy rainfall:** **Sileru and Sabari valleys** (140–152 cm); **highest-rainfall district Visakhapatnam**.
[2:15] - **Low rainfall:** **Hagari valley** (< 51 cm); **lowest-rainfall district Anantapur**.

## SUB 3 | Drought-prone Areas and Drought Management
[3:0] # Drought-prone Rayalaseema
[3:1] - Rayalaseema lies in the **rain shadow** of the Western Ghats and the Karnataka–Telangana highlands → little SW-monsoon rain → depends on the **NE monsoon**; its failure causes **chronic drought** in Rayalaseema and southern Prakasam.
[3:2] - **Most frequently drought-affected district: Anantapur** (lowest rainfall); greatest rural distress in **Prakasam, Anantapur, Kurnool, Kadapa**.
[3:3] - Interior Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, AP and Tamil Nadu are **rain-shadow** areas.
[3:4] - Areas under **750 mm** a year = **chronically drought prone**; meteorological drought when seasonal rain < **75%** of normal.
[3:5] # Drought proofing
[3:6] - **Watersheds in arid areas** = prevention, i.e. stopping a hazard turning into a disaster (relief and assistance are response).
[3:7] - A watershed project that raises groundwater but leaves **yields flat** → the community **ignored crop diversification and soil management**.
[3:8] - **Ralegan Siddhi** (Maharashtra, Anna Hazare) overcame water crisis with **check dams and tanks**.
[3:9] - Drought prevention: **check dams, desilting village tanks, farm ponds**, **artificial recharge of groundwater**; **planting Eucalyptus is not** (water-hungry).
[3:10] - **WDC-PMKSY 2.0**: degraded and rain-fed land via **integrated natural-resource management**.
[3:11] - Projects: Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing (Kurnool, Jul 2026); 100-day water plan launched at **Yadiki (Anantapur)**, 6 Apr 2026; **Gandikota** reservoir on the **Pennar** (Telugu Ganga, YSR Kadapa).
[3:12] # Drought management framework
[3:13] - **NDMA Drought Guidelines 2010**; **Manual for Drought Management revised 2016** – **multi-indicator**; **mandatory: rainfall deviation + dry spells**; impact indicators: crop sowing, NDVI, soil moisture, hydrology.
[3:14] - **Crop Weather Watch Group** (**Ministry of Agriculture**) meets **weekly** in the monsoon; uses **meteorological, hydrological and agricultural** parameters – **not geological**.
[3:15] - Nodal ministry for drought, pest attack, hailstorm, frost: **Agriculture**.
[3:16] - **Swaraj Abhiyan v. Union of India (2016)**: SC ordered the drought manual updated and a **National Disaster Mitigation Fund**.

## SUB 4 | Floods and Disaster Management Institutions
[4:0] # Floods
[4:1] - **Flood season (APSDMA):** with both monsoons, **1 June–31 October** in north coastal AP, Godavari and Krishna; **south of the Krishna it extends to 31 December** – both statements true.
[4:2] - **Rashtriya Barh Ayog (National Flood Commission):** set up **1976**, report **1980**; flood-prone area about **40 million ha**.
[4:3] - **CWC** flood forecasting since **1958**; Model Flood Plain Zoning Bill **1975**; NDMA Urban Flooding guidelines **2010**.
[4:4] - Flood management = **State subject**; Centre helps through **FMBAP**.
[4:5] TABLE head: Measure | Category
  [4:5.0] Dams, embankments, detention basins, channel improvement | Structural (flood)
  [4:5.1] Forecasting, floodplain zoning, insurance | Non-structural (flood)
  [4:5.2] Check dams, farm ponds, irrigation | Structural (drought)
  [4:5.3] Afforestation | Non-structural (landslide)
  [4:5.4] Seismic microzonation | Non-structural (earthquake)
[4:6] - Drainage planning → less urban waterlogging. **City Flood Alert** (Jul 2026): **RTGS + APSDMA + IIT Gandhinagar**, first in **Vijayawada**; **48-hour** rain forecasts, **6-hour** flash-flood warnings.
[4:7] - Godavari Anicut (Arthur Cotton) **1847–1852**; Krishna Anicut, Bezwada **1853–54** (Charles Orr); Polavaram spillway **48** radial gates; KRMB/GRMB advise releases for flood and drought mitigation.
[4:8] # Institutions
[4:9] - **AP State Disaster Management Rules: 2007** (under DM Act 2005).
[4:10] - **APSDMA**: chief nodal agency, chaired by the **Chief Minister**.
[4:11] - **NDMA** = policy and coordination; **NDRF/SDRF** = search, rescue, relief.
[4:12] - **2004 tsunami**: Tamil Nadu worst affected; **INCOIS, Hyderabad** – tsunami warning centre opened **2007**.
[4:13] - **Dial 112 (ERSS)** live in AP from **19 Feb 2019**; control centre at **Mangalagiri**.
