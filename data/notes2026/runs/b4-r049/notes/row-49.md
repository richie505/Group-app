=== Cyclone Vulnerability and Major Cyclones of AP
# Why AP is cyclone-prone
- **Coastline:** **1,053.07 km**, **3rd longest** in India after **Gujarat (2,340.62 km)** and **Tamil Nadu (1,068.69 km)** (PIB, 4 Dec 2025); the old figure **974 km ("2nd")** is still quoted in older reports such as the Flood Hazard Atlas 2021.
- **Flood Hazard Atlas of AP (2021, NRSC):** AP is **more vulnerable to cyclones than to other disasters**; at risk of **at least one cyclone a year** on average; **moderate to severe cyclones every 2–3 years**; **most cyclones in October and November** – all four statements true.
- **Bay cyclones** form between **8° N and 14° N**; on the AP coast they come mostly in the **last week of October** and in **October–December**.
- **Month with most storms crossing the AP coast** (IMD count, 1891–2012): **October (about 30 of 73)**, then November (19); most fall in the NE-monsoon season.
- About **44%** of the State is vulnerable to **tropical storms** and related hazards (AP Revenue Department).
- **Storm surge** can flood villages up to **10 km inland**.
- **IMD Cyclone Warning Centre** at **Visakhapatnam**.
# Most exposed districts and stretch
| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Most vulnerable stretch | **Ongole to Machilipatnam** |
| Most exposed districts (long coast, Bay proximity) | **Nellore and Srikakulam** |
| Cyclone- and flood-prone coastal districts | Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Nellore |
| Drought-prone, not cyclone-prone | Rayalaseema – Anantapur, Kurnool (inland) |
- **Regional profile:** coastal Andhra = cyclones, storm surge, floods; Godavari–Krishna deltas = river floods; **Rayalaseema = drought** (arid, low rainfall); inland plains = heat waves – so AP needs **multi-hazard** planning.

=== Cyclone Mitigation – NCRMP and Shelters
# National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project (NCRMP)
- **World Bank**-assisted; run by **NDMA (MHA)**, project HQ **New Delhi**; launched **2011** (Phase I).
- Aims: upgrade **forecasting, tracking and warning**, build capacity in **multi-hazard risk management**, and build **multipurpose cyclone shelters** and **embankments**.
- **Phase I = Andhra Pradesh and Odisha** only; built **219 multipurpose cyclone shelters (MPCS)** in AP (Odisha 316).
| NCRMP category | States |
|---|---|
| **Category I (higher vulnerability)** | **Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Odisha, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal** |
| Category II (lower vulnerability) | Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Goa, Puducherry, Lakshadweep, Daman & Diu, A&N |
# Shelters and buildings
- Coastal people are best saved by **building cyclone shelters**; in normal times MPCS serve as **community centres** (and schools).
- **Roof:** a **pyramidal roof** (four slopes meeting at a point) best reduces wind force; a **hip roof** firmly anchored to the walls beats a **gable** roof; flat and gable roofs fare worst.
- **NDMA Cyclone Guidelines 2008:** structural – shelters, raised platforms, cattle shelters, evacuation roads, **storm-surge barriers**; nature-based – **mangroves, bio-shields**, saline embankments, sand-dune restoration.
- Tools: **Doppler radars**; **SCATSAT-1** (ISRO) for cyclone tracking; GIS mapping.

=== Climate and Rainfall – Two Monsoons
# Climate and seasons
- Climate **hot and humid**; summer **March to June**, when the **moisture level is quite high** (IMD report); Rayalaseema is **semi-arid**.
| Season | Period | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | January–February | 13–30 °C |
| Summer | March to mid-June | mango showers in April–May (pre-monsoon) |
| SW monsoon | mid-June to September | onset in the 2nd week of June |
| Retreating / NE monsoon | October–December | October = transition from rains to winter |
- Order: **Winter → Summer → SW monsoon → Retreating monsoon**.
- **Coldest place:** **Lambasingi** – "Kashmir of AP" (erstwhile **Visakhapatnam**, now **Alluri Sitharama Raju** district). **Hottest:** Rentachintala (Palnadu); heat-wave belt Palnadu, Kurnool, Anantapur.
# Two monsoons
| Basis | SW monsoon | NE / retreating monsoon |
|---|---|---|
| Share of AP rain | about **two-thirds** (IMD 1989–2018: 57%) | about **one-third (≈33%)** |
| Wettest months | NextGen/Rocket: **July**; IMD 1989–2018 means: **September (146 mm) > August (143) > July (128) > June (96)** | October–November |
| Key region | most of AP, esp. north coast | **Nellore** and southern coastal districts; Rayalaseema depends on it |
- **"Most of AP's rain falls in August–September"** – the exam key; by IMD means August and September are the two wettest months.
- **NE-monsoon maximum district:** **Nellore** (retreating monsoon brings heavy rain to Nellore and the south coast).
- Normal annual rainfall about **860 mm** (574.7 SW + 285.3 NE); river floods mainly in the **SW monsoon (Jul–Sep)**, cyclones in Oct–Dec.
- India gets about **75%** of its rain from the SW monsoon; the main exception is the **Coromandel coast of Tamil Nadu** (max NE-monsoon rain); the Gujarat coast gets none.
- 2026 El Niño year: SW-monsoon deficit **51.2%** (1 Jun–25 Aug), all 28 districts deficient.
# Rainfall regions
- **IMD "Observed Rainfall Variability and Changes over Andhra Pradesh State" (2020):** least rainfall = **south-western region (Rayalaseema)** – **Anantapur** lowest (558.5 mm a year); highest SW-monsoon rain in north coastal districts.
- **Heavy rainfall:** **Sileru and Sabari valleys** (140–152 cm); **highest-rainfall district Visakhapatnam**.
- **Low rainfall:** **Hagari valley** (< 51 cm); **lowest-rainfall district Anantapur**.

=== Drought-prone Areas and Drought Management
# Drought-prone Rayalaseema
- Rayalaseema lies in the **rain shadow** of the Western Ghats and the Karnataka–Telangana highlands → little SW-monsoon rain → depends on the **NE monsoon**; its failure causes **chronic drought** in Rayalaseema and southern Prakasam.
- **Most frequently drought-affected district: Anantapur** (lowest rainfall); greatest rural distress in **Prakasam, Anantapur, Kurnool, Kadapa**.
- Interior Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, AP and Tamil Nadu are **rain-shadow** areas.
- Areas under **750 mm** a year = **chronically drought prone**; meteorological drought when seasonal rain < **75%** of normal.
# Drought proofing
- **Watersheds in arid areas** = prevention, i.e. stopping a hazard turning into a disaster (relief and assistance are response).
- A watershed project that raises groundwater but leaves **yields flat** → the community **ignored crop diversification and soil management**.
- **Ralegan Siddhi** (Maharashtra, Anna Hazare) overcame water crisis with **check dams and tanks**.
- Drought prevention: **check dams, desilting village tanks, farm ponds**, **artificial recharge of groundwater**; **planting Eucalyptus is not** (water-hungry).
- **WDC-PMKSY 2.0**: degraded and rain-fed land via **integrated natural-resource management**.
- Projects: Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing (Kurnool, Jul 2026); 100-day water plan launched at **Yadiki (Anantapur)**, 6 Apr 2026; **Gandikota** reservoir on the **Pennar** (Telugu Ganga, YSR Kadapa).
# Drought management framework
- **NDMA Drought Guidelines 2010**; **Manual for Drought Management revised 2016** – **multi-indicator**; **mandatory: rainfall deviation + dry spells**; impact indicators: crop sowing, NDVI, soil moisture, hydrology.
- **Crop Weather Watch Group** (**Ministry of Agriculture**) meets **weekly** in the monsoon; uses **meteorological, hydrological and agricultural** parameters – **not geological**.
- Nodal ministry for drought, pest attack, hailstorm, frost: **Agriculture**.
- **Swaraj Abhiyan v. Union of India (2016)**: SC ordered the drought manual updated and a **National Disaster Mitigation Fund**.

=== Floods and Disaster Management Institutions
# Floods
- **Flood season (APSDMA):** with both monsoons, **1 June–31 October** in north coastal AP, Godavari and Krishna; **south of the Krishna it extends to 31 December** – both statements true.
- **Rashtriya Barh Ayog (National Flood Commission):** set up **1976**, report **1980**; flood-prone area about **40 million ha**.
- **CWC** flood forecasting since **1958**; Model Flood Plain Zoning Bill **1975**; NDMA Urban Flooding guidelines **2010**.
- Flood management = **State subject**; Centre helps through **FMBAP**.
| Measure | Category |
|---|---|
| Dams, embankments, detention basins, channel improvement | Structural (flood) |
| Forecasting, floodplain zoning, insurance | Non-structural (flood) |
| Check dams, farm ponds, irrigation | Structural (drought) |
| Afforestation | Non-structural (landslide) |
| Seismic microzonation | Non-structural (earthquake) |
- Drainage planning → less urban waterlogging. **City Flood Alert** (Jul 2026): **RTGS + APSDMA + IIT Gandhinagar**, first in **Vijayawada**; **48-hour** rain forecasts, **6-hour** flash-flood warnings.
- Godavari Anicut (Arthur Cotton) **1847–1852**; Krishna Anicut, Bezwada **1853–54** (Charles Orr); Polavaram spillway **48** radial gates; KRMB/GRMB advise releases for flood and drought mitigation.
# Institutions
- **AP State Disaster Management Rules: 2007** (under DM Act 2005).
- **APSDMA**: chief nodal agency, chaired by the **Chief Minister**.
- **NDMA** = policy and coordination; **NDRF/SDRF** = search, rescue, relief.
- **2004 tsunami**: Tamil Nadu worst affected; **INCOIS, Hyderabad** – tsunami warning centre opened **2007**.
- **Dial 112 (ERSS)** live in AP from **19 Feb 2019**; control centre at **Mangalagiri**.
