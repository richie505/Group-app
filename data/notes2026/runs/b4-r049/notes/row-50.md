=== Forest Cover and Recorded Forest Area – ISFR Data
# Recorded (notified) forest area
- **Recorded Forest Area (RFA): 37,258 sq km = 22.86%** of AP – **8th in India** (ISFR 2019, 2021, 2023; AP Forest Department, May 2024). "5th" is the usual wrong option.
- Legal classes: Reserved **85.8%**, Protected **13.6%**, Unclassed **0.6%**; notified forest per APSES 2024-25: 37,221.11 sq km (22.84%); revised 37,421.11 sq km (~22.96%) in 2025-26.
- Land-use statistics show **36.88 lakh ha (22.63%)** of AP as forest land – the figure some keys give as "area covered by forests".
# Forest cover by ISFR edition (AP)
| ISFR | Forest cover | % of AP | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | about 26,006 sq km | ~15.96% | – |
| 2017 | 28,147 sq km | 17.27% | **+2,141** (most improved State) |
| **2019** | **29,137.40 sq km** | **17.88%** | +990.40 |
| **2021** | 29,784 sq km | 18.28% | **+647** |
| **2023** | **30,084.96 sq km** | **18.47%** | +95.59 (10 districts; Vizianagaram +35.35) |
- **ISFR 2023:** tree cover **5,340.02 sq km**; **forest + tree cover 35,424.98 sq km = 21.74%**.
- The often-quoted **38,436.58 sq km (23.59%)** is forest cover **plus 8,351.62 sq km of scrub** – scrub is not forest cover.
- India (ISFR 2023): forest + tree cover **25.17%**; gain 1,445 sq km of which true forest only **156 sq km**; **Madhya Pradesh** has the maximum forest area; India **3rd** in forest-area gain 2010–2020.

# Forest fires
- **2023–24 fire season: AP first in India** in forest area burnt – **5,286.76 sq km**.
- 2026 fire-prone zones: **Chittoor and Annamayya** (Horsley Hills, Mallappa Konda).
- Critique of drives like "Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam": they add **tree cover, not natural forest**.

=== Forest Types and Natural Vegetation
# Types by rainfall
| Forest type | Rainfall | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Moist deciduous** | **125–200 cm** (maximum rain; India: 100–200 cm) | erstwhile **Srikakulam, Visakhapatnam**, **agency of East Godavari**; Papikonda |
| **Dry deciduous** (largest) | **75–100 cm** | erstwhile **Kadapa, Kurnool**; **Seshachalam–Nallamala** belt |
| Short thorn | **< 70 cm** | Kadapa, Kurnool, Chittoor, Anantapur |
| **Tidal / mangrove** ("Koringa forests") | coastal | mouths of the **Godavari and Krishna** – **Coringa**, Krishna WLS; coastal alluvial belt of **Krishna** district |
- **AP forests are mainly tropical deciduous**; most of the natural vegetation is **dry deciduous**.
- **Seshachalam hills = dry deciduous** (not wet evergreen, desert or mangrove); **Papikonda NP = moist deciduous**.
- **ISFR 2019 forest types:** **Southern Dry Mixed Deciduous** is the largest (15,128 sq km), then Dry Deciduous Scrub (12,551); Mangroves and Littoral 418 sq km.
- **Coringa:** mangrove forest where the **Godavari** meets the Bay of Bengal backwaters; tidal forests were taken up on a large scale in the **6th Five-Year Plan**.
- **Biggest forest: Nallamala** (dry deciduous; Gundlakamma forest zone part of it).
- **Biotic provinces:** **Deccan Plateau** is AP's largest (about 53% of forests), then Central Plateau, Eastern Highlands, East Coastal Plains.
- Forest extent: north **Simhachalam hills**; south **Palakonda and Nallamala**; west **Balaghat**.
# Mangroves
- Godavari–Krishna estuaries: called India's **2nd-largest mangrove ecosystem** (APSES) – true only on the **east coast**; by ISFR area AP is behind West Bengal, Gujarat and A&N.
- ISFR 2023: AP mangrove cover **+13.01 sq km**; **MISHTI** launched **5 June 2023**.

=== Tree Species, Forest Produce and Plantations
# Species
- **Red sanders (*Pterocarpus santalinus*)**: **endemic** to AP's **Seshachalam, Palakonda and Veligonda** ranges (southern Eastern Ghats); a **dry deciduous** tree, **not** a tropical rainforest tree; **greatest economic value**; IUCN **Endangered**, WPA **Schedule IV**, **CITES Appendix II**; thermal-imaging drones against smuggling (Feb 2026).
- **Axlewood** = ***Anogeissus latifolia*** (dry deciduous).
- **Eucalyptus** = family **Myrtaceae**; *Cylindrocladium* causes **dieback/blight of *Eucalyptus tereticornis***.
- Teak – dry deciduous; **bamboo, sandalwood** – northern Eastern Ghats moist hill forests.
- **Thimmamma Marrimanu:** giant **banyan** at Kadiri (erstwhile Anantapur, now **Sri Sathya Sai** district), about 70 km from Horsley Hills; **Guinness record 1989** as the **largest banyan (canopy) in the world**; about 550 years old; canopy usually quoted as "about 5–6 acres" (Guinness: about 4.7 acres).
# Revenue and agencies
- **Forest produce revenue:** older AP keys name **bamboo** as the top earner; red sanders auctions have earned more in recent years. Forest revenue ₹32.81 crore (2023-24), ₹19.63 crore (2024-25 to Dec); NTFP over ₹500 crore a year.
- **GCC** (HQ Visakhapatnam) buys minor forest produce; **TRIFED** (1987, Multi-State Co-op Societies Act 1984; Tribal Affairs) – MSP for MFP; **APFDC** – export agent for **red sanders and beedi leaf**; **CAMPA** – compensatory afforestation through an Annual Plan of Operation.
- Wood banks: teak **Kapavaram**, red sanders **Tirupati**, bamboo Rajamahendravaram; craft wood banks (Kondapalli, Etikoppaka, Udayagiri, Bobbili …) give timber to artisans at **no profit**.

- **Coffee** in AP grows mainly in the **agency areas of Visakhapatnam (Araku, Paderu, Chintapalli) and East Godavari (Maredumilli)** – not West Godavari; Srikakulam has only small patches.
# Trees outside forests and programmes
- **TOFI = Trees Outside Forests in India**: **MoEFCC + USAID**, launched **8 Sep 2022**; AP one of **7** States; AP Forest Department launch **Nov 2022**.
- **ISFR 2019 rural TOF in AP:** **mango (*Mangifera indica*)** is the most numerous species (about 28–29%).
- **Vanam-Manam**: green cover outside reserved forests – **5.29 crore** seedlings (2024-25 to Dec).
- Van Adhiniyam 1980: 26,924 ha diverted to 2017-18; 2026 changes allow commercial plantations on degraded forest land (33% goal). **16th FC** counts open forests with density weights. **Rekapalli revolt (1879)** against British forest laws.
