=== NOTES 2:26 | Veto Powers, Ordinance-making Power (art. 123) and Pardoning Power (art. 72)
## SUB 0 | Veto Powers – Types (article 111)
[26:0:0] TABLE head: Veto | Meaning | Applies to | Indian President?
  [26:0:0.0] Absolute | Withholds assent; Bill ends | Private members' Bills; Government Bills when Cabinet resigns before assent | Yes
  [26:0:0.1] Suspensive | Returns once for reconsideration; must assent if re-passed (with or without amendment) | Ordinary Bills only | Yes – not Money Bills
  [26:0:0.2] Pocket | Neither assents, rejects nor returns – keeps the Bill pending | Ordinary Bills | Yes
  [26:0:0.3] Qualified | Overridable only by a higher (2/3) majority | US practice | No
[26:0:1] - **Art. 111** prescribes **no time limit** for the President.
[26:0:2] The **10-day rule** (Bill becomes law if not decided) is a **US** feature, not Indian.
## SUB 1 | Veto by Type of Bill
[26:1:0] TABLE head: Bill | Union legislation | State legislation (reserved)
  [26:1:0.0] Ordinary | Ratify, reject or return once | Ratify, reject or return
  [26:1:0.1] Money | Ratify or reject; cannot return | Ratify or reject; cannot return
  [26:1:0.2] Constitutional Amendment | Must assent (24th Amendment, 1971); cannot reject or return | Not applicable
[26:1:1] - Money Bills are usually brought with the President's **prior recommendation**, so he usually assents.
[26:1:2] - The one-time return power can apply to **Financial Bills of Categories I and II**.
[26:1:3] Money Bill = cannot return | Constitutional Amendment Bill = must assent | Ordinary Bill = return once.
## SUB 2 | Vetoes Actually Used
[26:2:0] TABLE head: Veto | President | Bill
  [26:2:0.0] Absolute | Dr. Rajendra Prasad (on Council of Ministers' advice) | PEPSU Appropriation Bill, 1954
  [26:2:0.1] Pocket | Giani Zail Singh | Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986
[26:2:1] - Pocket veto = taking no action on a Bill; used **only once** at the Union level.
[26:2:2] - **2002**: ordinance on **electoral reforms** returned by the President; the Union Cabinet sent it back unchanged → **assented** under Art. 123.
## SUB 3 | State Bills Reserved for the President (arts. 200, 201)
[26:3:0] - **Art. 200**: Governor may assent, withhold, return (non-money Bill) or **reserve** a Bill for the President.
[26:3:1] - **Art. 201**: for a reserved Bill the President **declares assent or withholds assent**; for a non-money Bill he may direct its return for reconsideration.
[26:3:2] - No time limit is written in Art. 200 or 201.
[26:3:3] - **Governor has no pocket veto**.
[26:3:4] - **8 April 2025 (State of TN v Governor)**: indefinite inaction under Art. 200 held justiciable; ten TN Bills deemed assented.
[26:3:5] - **Nov 2025 (Presidential reference, Art. 143, five judges)**: courts **cannot fix timelines** for the Governor or President; **no deemed assent**; powers cannot be used arbitrarily.
## SUB 4 | Ordinance – Basis and Conditions
[26:4:0] - President: **Art. 123** (Part V); Governor: **Art. 213** (Part VI).
[26:4:1] - Nature: a **legislative power of the Executive** (President is part of Parliament) – not an executive or administrative function.
[26:4:2] - When: only when **either or both Houses are not in session**; **none** while both Houses are sitting.
[26:4:3] - Satisfaction: circumstances need **immediate action**; issued on **ministerial advice**.
[26:4:4] - Force: **same force, effect and limits as an Act** of Parliament / State Legislature.
[26:4:5] - Origin: relic of the **Government of India Act, 1935** (Governor-General's ordinance power).
## SUB 5 | Ordinance – Can vs Cannot
[26:5:0] TABLE head: Can | Cannot
  [26:5:0.0] Amend or repeal ordinary Central Acts | Amend the Constitution (Art. 368)
  [26:5:0.1] Operate retrospectively | Violate FRs – it is "law" under Art. 13(2)
  [26:5:0.2] Introduce or amend tax provisions | Go beyond Parliament's legislative competence
  [26:5:0.3] Be withdrawn any time | –
[26:5:1] - Scope of an ordinance = scope of Parliament's ordinary legislative power.
[26:5:2] "The President cannot withdraw or repeal an ordinance once issued" = FALSE.
## SUB 6 | Life of an Ordinance
[26:6:0] - 1. Promulgated during recess.
[26:6:1] - 2. **Laid** before the House(s) on reassembly.
[26:6:2] - 3. **Lapses six weeks after reassembly** unless approved; Houses meeting on different dates → counted from the **later date**.
[26:6:3] - 4. Ends earlier if both Houses **disapprove** by resolution, or if **withdrawn**.
[26:6:4] - 5. **Maximum life = six months + six weeks** – maximum gap between sessions is six months (**Art. 85** Parliament; **Art. 174** States) + six-week window.
[26:6:5] 6 weeks after reassembly | 6 months maximum gap | 6 months + 6 weeks maximum life
[26:6:6] - Linked time limit: **Art. 101(4)** – MP's unexcused absence for **60 days** can vacate the seat; periods of **prorogation** and **adjournments of more than four consecutive days** are excluded.
## SUB 7 | Satisfaction and Judicial Review
[26:7:0] TABLE head: Year | Event | Effect
  [26:7:0.0] 1975 | 38th Amendment | Satisfaction under Arts. 123, 213, 352 made final / non-justiciable
  [26:7:0.1] 1978 | 44th Amendment | Reversed the 38th
  [26:7:0.2] 1987 | D.C. Wadhwa v State of Bihar | Routine re-promulgation without placing before the legislature = fraud on the Constitution
  [26:7:0.3] 2017 | Krishna Kumar Singh v State of Bihar | Seven judges; re-promulgation unconstitutional; laying is mandatory; satisfaction justiciable
[26:7:1] - Ordinances are **subject to judicial review**.
[26:7:2] - Latest: **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026** (gazette 16 May 2026; judges 33 → 37 excluding CJI) revived debate on ordinance misuse and executive overreach.
[26:7:3] - Criticism: frequent ordinances **bypass parliamentary deliberation**.
[26:7:4] - AP angle: AP used a State ordinance (**Art. 213**) on **organ-transplant committees**.
## SUB 8 | Pardoning Power (article 72)
[26:8:0] # Forms
[26:8:1] TABLE head: Form | Meaning
  [26:8:1.0] Pardon | Removes both sentence and conviction; as if the offence was never committed
  [26:8:1.1] Commutation | Lighter form of punishment (death → rigorous life imprisonment)
  [26:8:1.2] Remission | Shorter period, same character (2 years RI → 1 year RI)
  [26:8:1.3] Respite | Lesser sentence for special circumstances – pregnancy, mental or physical disability
  [26:8:1.4] Reprieve | Temporary stay of execution, especially death, to seek pardon
[26:8:2] Commutation = changes the **form**; Remission = reduces the **period**.
[26:8:3] # Scope and nature
[26:8:4] - Art. 72 covers: (a) sentence by **court-martial**; (b) offence against a **Union law**; (c) **death sentence**.
[26:8:5] - Not State-law sentences in every case; amnesty is outside Art. 72.
[26:8:6] - A **judicial power** of the President, exercised on the **Council of Ministers' advice**.
## SUB 9 | Article 72 vs Article 161 and Cases
[26:9:0] TABLE head: Point | President (Art. 72) | Governor (Art. 161)
  [26:9:0.0] Offences | Against Union laws | Against State laws
  [26:9:0.1] Death sentence | Can pardon | Cannot pardon; can suspend, remit or commute
  [26:9:0.2] Court-martial | Yes | No
  [26:9:0.3] Forms | All five | Same, except as above
[26:9:1] - **Only the President** can pardon a death sentence and a court-martial sentence.
[26:9:2] TABLE head: Case | Holding
  [26:9:2.0] Maru Ram (1980) | Power exercised on Council of Ministers' advice
  [26:9:2.1] Kehar Singh (1989) | President may examine evidence afresh; no right of oral hearing
  [26:9:2.2] Epuru Sudhakar v Govt of AP (2006) | Limited judicial review – mala fide, arbitrary or irrelevant grounds
