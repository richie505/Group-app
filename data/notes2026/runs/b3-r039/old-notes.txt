=== NOTES 3:39 | Food Security – Nfsa 2013, Pds Reform, Buffer Stocks, One Nation One Ration Card, Pmgkay
## SUB 0 | Food Security – Concept and Dimensions
[39:0:0] # Definition
[39:0:1] - **FAO** (HQ **Rome**) / **World Food Summit 1996**: food security exists when **all people, at all times**, have **physical and economic access** to sufficient, safe and nutritious food for an active and healthy life.
[39:0:2] Food security does NOT mean access "to the **food of their choice**" (APPSC G2 2017).
[39:0:3] # Dimensions
[39:0:4] TABLE head: Dimension | Meaning
  [39:0:4.0] Availability | Production + imports + previous stocks
  [39:0:4.1] Accessibility | Food within reach of every person
  [39:0:4.2] Affordability | Enough money for sufficient, safe, nutritious food
  [39:0:4.3] Absorption / utilisation | Body's ability to use the food
  [39:0:4.4] Stability | Access at all times
[39:0:5] **Accountability**, price, quantity of exports are NOT dimensions (UPPSC 2025).
[39:0:6] # National vs household
[39:0:7] TABLE head: Level | Meaning
  [39:0:7.0] National | Aggregate production + buffer stock
  [39:0:7.1] Household | Each household can actually access and afford food
[39:0:8] - "National but not household food security" = sufficient stock, but not all households can get it – hence PDS and NFSA.
[39:0:9] - Food security is needed **even in surplus years**: starvation comes from unequal distribution and lack of access.
[39:0:10] - In India both rich (wrong diet) and poor (poor diet) are malnourished.
## SUB 1 | Entitlement Approach
[39:1:0] - **Amartya Sen**, *Poverty and Famines* (**1981**): famines come from **collapse of entitlements** (command over food via income, exchange, transfers), not merely a fall in availability.
[39:1:1] - UPPSC 2025 A-R: both true, R explains A.
## SUB 2 | Pds to Nfsa – Evolution
[39:2:0] TABLE head: Stage | Year | Fact
  [39:2:0.0] PDS | 1940s | World War II rationing (Bengal famine backdrop)
  [39:2:0.1] Buffer stock | Fourth Plan (1969-74) | Formally introduced
  [39:2:0.2] RPDS | June 1992 | 1,775 blocks
  [39:2:0.3] TPDS | June 1997 (not 1985) | BPL/APL
  [39:2:0.4] NFSA | 2013 | Rights-based
  [39:2:0.5] PMGKAY | 2020 | Extra free grain
[39:2:1] - **PDS** = government shop chain giving basic food and non-food goods at low prices; main aim food security for the poor.
[39:2:2] - **Procurement prices** = prices at which government buys grain for PDS and buffer stock.
[39:2:3] - **Atal Khadyanna Yojana** – Uttarakhand (2011): APL 10 kg wheat + 10 kg rice at ₹4 and ₹6/kg; BPL at ₹2 and ₹3/kg.
[39:2:4] - **National Food Security Mission (NFSM, 2007-08):** Centrally Sponsored Scheme of the **Ministry of Agriculture**, launched in pursuance of the **National Development Council** resolution (29 May 2007); targets set for the end of the **Eleventh Plan**.
## SUB 3 | Nfsa 2013 – Nature and Coverage
[39:3:0] - Passed **5 July 2013** (first as an Ordinance); notified **10 Sep 2013** with backdated effect; **not 2014**.
[39:3:1] - Welfare → **rights-based**; legal, justiciable right. Ministry: Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution (**DFPD**).
[39:3:2] - Objective: **food and nutritional security** at affordable prices for a **life with dignity** (APPSC 2025).
[39:3:3] - Coverage: up to **75% rural** + **50% urban** = about **two-thirds (≈67%)**; ~80-81 crore people (APPSC G2 2017: 75%).
[39:3:4] - Women empowerment: **eldest woman (18+)** = head of household for ration cards.
[39:3:5] NFSA objective distractors: "PDS Act", "FCI Act", "NFSA 2014".

## SUB 8 | Buffer Stocks and Omss
[39:8:0] - **CACP** advises MSP → **FCI** procures wheat and rice → **CCEA** fixes minimum norms **each quarter** (1 Apr, 1 Jul, 1 Oct, 1 Jan) → sold at issue price in deficit areas.
[39:8:1] TABLE head: Quarter | Norm (rice + wheat)
  [39:8:1.0] 1 April | 210.4 lakh tonnes
  [39:8:1.1] 1 July | 411.2 lakh tonnes (highest)
  [39:8:1.2] 1 October | 307.7 lakh tonnes
  [39:8:1.3] 1 January | 214.1 lakh tonnes
[39:8:2] - **Strategic reserve**: **5 million tonnes** above operational stocks (30 LT wheat + 20 LT rice).
[39:8:3] - Stocks: latest ~602 LMT, about **3x** the norms.
[39:8:4] - **OMSS**: FCI sells surplus grain at fixed prices – raises supply, cools prices, clears stock, cuts carrying cost; does **not** replace FCI procurement.
## SUB 9 | Pmgkay, Onorc and Price Interventions
[39:9:0] # PMGKAY
[39:9:1] - Launched **2020** (COVID/Atmanirbhar package): **5 kg free grain per person** on top of NFSA.
[39:9:2] - From **1 Jan 2023** merged into NFSA – whole NFSA allocation **free** ("One Nation–One Price–One Ration").
[39:9:3] - Extended **1 Jan 2024 – 31 Dec 2028**; ~₹11.80 lakh crore by Centre.
[39:9:4] # One Nation One Ration Card
[39:9:5] - Runs on **IM-PDS** (Integrated Management of PDS, **2018**) – nationwide portability and de-duplication.
[39:9:6] - Started **Aug 2019** in 4 States (AP-Telangana, Gujarat-Maharashtra); **Assam** became the **36th** and last State/UT (**June 2022**).
[39:9:7] - **Mera Ration** app (DFPD with NIC) for migrants.
[39:9:8] # Bharat brand
[39:9:9] - **Bharat Rice** (Feb 2024) **₹29/kg**; Bharat Atta and dal – via **NAFED, NCCF, Kendriya Bhandar** outlets and mobile vans to curb inflation.

=== NOTES 3:41 | Rural Credit and Rural Non-farm Sector; Migration and Rural Distress
## SUB 0 | Rural Credit – History and Surveys
[41:0:0] - 1951: **moneylenders ~70%** of rural credit.
[41:0:1] - **All India Rural Credit Survey Committee** (RBI, 1951; report 1954), chair **A.D. Gorwala**: "**Rural credit has failed, but cooperation must succeed**" → **SBI (1955)** from the Imperial Bank.
[41:0:2] - **AIDIS 2019** (NSS 77th round): rural debt **66% institutional, 34% non-institutional**; ~35% rural households indebted.
[41:0:3] - **NAFIS 2021-22** (NABARD): rural household income ₹8,059 → **₹12,698**/month (2016-17 to 2021-22); indebted households 47.4% → **52.0%**.
## SUB 1 | Sources of Rural Credit
[41:1:0] TABLE head: Institutional | Non-institutional
  [41:1:0.0] Commercial banks, RRBs, cooperatives/PACS, Small Finance Banks, SHG-bank linkage | Moneylenders, traders, landlords, relatives
[41:1:1] - **Kisan Credit Card**: valid 5 years; crop, post-harvest, consumption, allied needs; open to **tenants, sharecroppers, SHGs, JLGs** (not only landowners); MISS-KCC ~5.9 crore farmers; Kisan Rin Portal.
[41:1:2] - **Small Finance Banks**: lend to small businesses, small and marginal farmers, rural entrepreneurs (regulators/FSDC members are not borrowers).
[41:1:3] - **NABARD** = apex rural development bank; **SIDBI** = finances MSMEs.
## SUB 2 | Informal Debt and Microfinance
[41:2:0] TABLE head: Debt type | Meaning
  [41:2:0.0] Temporary | Seasonal, repaid after harvest
  [41:2:0.1] Chronic | Long-standing
  [41:2:0.2] Debt trap | New loan to repay old
[41:2:1] - Usurious interest → distress sale of land/gold, bonded-labour-like ties; often the trigger in farmer suicides.
[41:2:2] - Microfinance route (DAY-NRLM): **SHGs, JLGs, BC Sakhis**.
## SUB 3 | Rural Non-farm Sector
[41:3:0] - Components: rural industries, Khadi & Village Industries, handloom/handicrafts, food processing, trade, transport, services.
[41:3:1] TABLE head: Body / scheme | Role
  [41:3:1.0] KVIC | Statutory, KVI Act 1956 (amended 1987, 2006); Ministry of MSME; runs PMEGP; KVI sales ₹1.27 lakh crore (2025-26)
  [41:3:1.1] SVEP | Under DAY-NRLM; funds rural non-farm enterprises
  [41:3:1.2] MSMEs | 8.7 crore units, ~31.1% of GDP, 38.9 crore jobs
  [41:3:1.3] DDU-GKY | Placement-linked skilling; Parliamentary panel flags low wages and drop-outs
## SUB 4 | Rural Distress Indicators
[41:4:0] - Urban wages **1.7-2 times** rural (ES 2025-26).
[41:4:1] - MGNREGA person-days **389.09 crore (2020-21) → 183.77 crore** (2025-26, to Dec 2025); rural unemployment 3.3% → 2.5%.
[41:4:2] - **NREGA** passed 2005, in force 2006, renamed **MGNREGA 2009**: 100 days per household; wages within **15 days** (not 30); one-third women. Now **VB-G RAM G**.
[41:4:3] - MGNREGA results: higher purchasing power, less distress migration (not lower bargaining power).
[41:4:4] UPSC 1996 key: rural incomes lag due to farmers' illiteracy and poor scientific knowledge ("I only").
## SUB 5 | Migration – Push and Pull
[41:5:0] TABLE head: Push (rural) | Pull (urban)
  [41:5:0.0] Few jobs, low wages, landlessness, crop failure, debt | High labour demand, better wages
  [41:5:0.1] Poor health and schools | Education, healthcare
  [41:5:0.2] Unbalanced development | Industry and services
[41:5:1] - **Distress migration**: forced by debt, landlessness, crop loss. **Seasonal**: for a season, then return. **Return migration** also a type.
[41:5:2] - **26.8%** of rural Indians were migrants (2020-21).
[41:5:3] - BPSC 2024: urban demand, unbalanced development, few rural jobs – **all are causes**.
## SUB 6 | Feminisation of Agriculture
[41:6:0] - Meaning: rising share of women among cultivators, labourers, unpaid family workers as men move to non-farm/urban work.
[41:6:1] - Causes: male out-migration; distress; subsistence and unpaid work; COVID return migration.
[41:6:2] - **Claudia Goldin** (2023 Economics Nobel): **U-shaped curve** – high in 1980s, fell 2004-05 to 2012 (machines), rising after 2020.
[41:6:3] - Rural women = **80%** of the rise in female workers (2017-18 to 2023-24).
[41:6:4] Feminisation ≠ land ownership by women; it is participation.
## SUB 7 | Andhra Pradesh
[41:7:0] - **93.2%** agricultural households indebted – highest among major States (NSSO 77th Round).
[41:7:1] - ~**50%** of AP farmers depend on non-institutional credit (NABARD; not 25%).
