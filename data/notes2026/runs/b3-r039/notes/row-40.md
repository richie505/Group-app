=== Community Development and Early Rural Programmes (1948-1980)
# Early community development
- **Etawah Pilot Project:** **September 1948**, Meheva village near Etawah (UP); by **Lt. Col. Albert Mayer** (American town planner); UP Govt + Point-4 programme; the **first rural-development pilot project**.
- **Community Development Programme:** **2 October 1952**; the **first rural development programme**; aim – overall development and **improving the standard of living** of rural people through people's participation; it **set the stage for Panchayati Raj**.
- **National Extension Service:** **1953**; the **first programme of community development** (NextGen); CDP could not cover all India as funds were short.
- **S.K. Dey:** **architect of community development** in India; first Cooperation and Panchayati Raj Minister.
- **Balwantrai Mehta Committee:** reviewed CDP and NES; report **November 1957**; **democratic decentralisation** → Panchayati Raj (Rajasthan, then **Andhra Pradesh, 1959**).
- **Gandhiji's approach:** **Samagra Gramseva** (constructive programme for the whole village).

# Minimum Needs Programme (1974)
- Network of basic services for **rural and urban** people: elementary and adult education, rural health, **rural water supply**, rural roads, rural electrification, rural housing, **environmental improvement of urban slums**, nutrition. **Social forestry is NOT a component.**

=== Bharat Nirman (2005-2009)
- **Launched December 2005** (2005-06) – a **rural infrastructure** programme; targets to be met by the end of **2009**.
- **Six components:** **irrigation, drinking water, electrification (RGGVY), roads (PMGSY), housing (IAY) and rural telephony**.
- **Irrigation component:** Accelerated Irrigation Benefits Programme, **inter-linking of river projects**, **repair, renovation and restoration of water bodies**; rehabilitation of people displaced by irrigation projects was NOT included.
- **Drinking water:** **National Rural Drinking Water Programme** – renamed from **ARWSP (1972-73)** on **1 April 2009**; now **Jal Jeevan Mission** (2019).
- **NOT in Bharat Nirman:** **education / Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan**, **NRLM**, agro-based industries, **rural hospitals**.
- **Rajiv Gandhi Grameen Vidyutikaran Yojana:** **April 2005** – electrify all villages; subsumed in DDUGJY (2015). **Saubhagya** (2017, PM Sahaj Bijli Har Ghar Yojana) – **Ministry of Power**, every household electrified.
- **National Food for Work Programme:** **14 November 2004** in the **150 most backward districts**; merged into NREGA (2006).
> NOT angle: SSA, NRLM, rural hospitals, agro-industries, education are outside the six.
~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. part 05 p.13, p.32 (Bharat Nirman rural infrastructure; roads one of its six components); app notes 3:40 sub 0, 1

=== PMGSY – Rural Roads
- **Launched 25 December 2000** (Ministry of Rural Development) – the earliest of PMGSY, DDU-GKY (2014), SAGY (2014) and DAY (2014-16).
- **Objective:** a **single all-weather road** connecting eligible unconnected habitations to the main road network – **develop the road network to villages** / community life in villages not well connected.
- **Target year:** connect habitations of **1,000+ by 2003** and **500+ by 2007** (end of the Tenth Plan) – the "full connectivity" target year asked = **2007**.
| Area | Population norm |
|---|---|
| **Plains** | **500+** |
| Hill States, NE, deserts, Schedule V/tribal areas | 250+ |
| LWE-affected blocks | 100+ (not 250+) |
| Phase | Focus |
| PMGSY-I (2000) | New roads |
| PMGSY-II (2013) | Upgradation |
| PMGSY-III (2019) | Through routes and major rural links |
- **2016-17:** about **130 km of road built per day** (47,350 km in the year – a record).
- **Meri Sadak** app (2015): citizens' complaints on PMGSY road quality.
- **2014 initiative:** inclusion of **women SHGs in the transect walk** for road planning.
- 99.6% of targeted eligible habitations connected (Aug 2026).

=== Rurbanisation – PURA and SPMRM
# PURA
- **PURA** = **Provision of Urban Amenities in Rural Areas** – **Dr A.P.J. Abdul Kalam** (explained on the eve of the **54th Republic Day, 2003**); elaborated in ***Target 3 Billion*** (co-author **Srijan Pal Singh**).
- Aim: **economic upliftment of villages and poverty eradication**; **bridging the rural-urban divide**. Joint AP pilot: Miryalaguda (2004).
# Shyama Prasad Mukherji Rurban Mission (SPMRM)
- **Rurban** (not "Rural/Revival") Mission; launched **21 Feb 2016** (not 2015) at **Kurubhat, Rajnandgaon (Chhattisgarh)**; successor of PURA.
- Aim: **300 Rurban clusters** – "a rural soul with urban amenities"; curb **distress migration**; make rural areas **socially, economically and physically sustainable** regions.
- Objectives: **stimulate local economic development**, enhance basic services, create well-planned clusters (not literacy, sex ratio or free power).
- Funding: **convergence** (MGNREGA, PMGSY, SBM, PMAY) + **Critical Gap Funding up to 30%**; Centre:State **60:40**, **90:10** for NE and Himalayan States.
- Cluster: contiguous villages of **25,000-50,000** population (plain/coastal). **Rurban cluster** = rural areas with rising density, non-farm jobs, growing economic activity.
- AP: **6 clusters** in the second phase (APPSC key); MoRD classified **359 AP panchayats** under Rurban clusters (Mar 2026).
- SPMRM does NOT replace Gram Panchayats with urban local bodies.

=== Rural Water and Sanitation – JJM and SBM-G
# Sanitation
| Scheme | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Nirmal Gram Puraskar** (Oct 2003) | Award for **open-defecation-free** GPs, blocks, districts, States – boosted the Total Sanitation Campaign |
| Nirmal Bharat Abhiyan (2012) | Renewed **Total Sanitation Campaign** – ODF rural India, WASH approach (not slum toilets or housing) |
| **SBM-G Phase II** | Approved Feb 2020 for **2020-21 to 2024-25**; **ODF Plus** – sustain ODF + solid and liquid waste management; ₹1,40,881 crore |
| **10-Year Rural Sanitation Strategy** | **2019-2029**; DDWS, Jal Shakti, 27 Sep 2019 |
- **ODF sustainability convergence:** MGNREGS, NRDWP, **RuRBAN** and Atal Bhujal (not PMGSY or AMRUT).
- **PMAY-G convergence:** SBM-G, NRDWP, DDUGJY, **Ujjwala** and MGNREGS – all.
- Drinking Water and Sanitation: separate Ministry in **2011** → Department under **Ministry of Jal Shakti** since **June 2019**.
# Jal Jeevan Mission
- **Announced in Budget 2019-20**, launched **15 Aug 2019** (Jal Shakti): **Functional Household Tap Connection (FHTC)** to every rural household + schools, anganwadis, GP buildings, health centres – originally **by 2024**.
- **Census 2011:** only about **31%** of rural households had tap water (30.8%).
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Service level | **55 litres per capita per day** of prescribed quality |
| Har Ghar Jal village | Tap for every household and institution, certified by Gram Sabha resolution |
| Jal Arpan | Handover of schemes to GPs |
| Sujal Gaon ID | Digital ID for every rural piped scheme |
- Target **19.36 crore** households; **15.86 crore (81.94%)** connected (Aug 2026); about 18% still unconnected.
- Deadline **2024** → **December 2028** as **JJM 2.0** (Cabinet, Mar 2026): from infrastructure creation to **service delivery** including O&M; outlay ₹8.69 lakh crore.
| States | Sharing |
|---|---|
| Himalayan/NE States + UTs with legislature | 90:10 |
| Other States | 50:50 |
| UTs without legislature | 100% Centre |

=== Housing – PMAY-G and PMAY-U
- **Indira Awaas Yojana** (1985-86): began under RLEGP, a **sub-scheme of Jawahar Rozgar Yojana** from 1989, independent from **1 Jan 1996**; restructured as **PMAY-G (2016)**.
- **PMAY-G:** unit assistance **₹1.20 lakh (plains)** / **₹1.30 lakh (hilly/difficult)**; beneficiaries from **SECC 2011** housing-deprivation data, verified by the **Gram Sabha**.
- Target **2.95 crore** houses by March 2024; Cabinet (9 Aug 2024) approved **2 crore more** for **2024-25 to 2028-29**; 3.13 crore completed by mid-2026.
- **PMAY-U:** in June 2019 the Housing Ministry **advanced** the 1-crore-house deadline **from 2022 to 2020**; later extended to Dec 2024 and PMAY-U 2.0.

=== Rural Infrastructure Gaps and Andhra Pradesh
| Remaining gaps | Achievements |
|---|---|
| Piped water: ~18% households unconnected | Saubhagya: 100% willing-household electrification |
| O&M of water schemes, housing, last-mile roads | SBM-G: 12.19 crore toilets; 1,288 GOBARdhan plants |

- Rural infrastructure gaps = piped water, O&M, housing, last-mile roads (**not high-speed rail**).
- **16th Finance Commission:** one-time "Urbanisation Premium" for merging peri-urban villages.
# Andhra Pradesh
- **NTR Sujala** (2014): **safe purified drinking water** to every village household (₹2 per 20 litres).
- AP households with piped drinking water **2007-08: 63.9%** (APPSC key).
- **JJM in AP:** FHTC **73%** (70.04 lakh households, 31 Mar 2024); 112 water testing labs; JJM 2.0 goal – every household by 2028. **Madanapalle Water Grid** – drought-prone Rayalaseema.
- **Navaratnalu – Pedalandariki Illu:** about **30.75 lakh house sites** in **17,005** colony layouts.
- **Village and Ward Secretariats:** **2 Oct 2019** at **Karapa (East Godavari)**; ~**1.26 lakh** staff.
