=== Public vs Private Care and Challenges
- **Public system** largely **curative**, with limited preventive, promotive and rehabilitative care (UPSC 2023 Statement-I, true).
- **States are primarily responsible** for organising health services (health is a State subject) – Statement-II, also true but **not the explanation** of Statement-I.
- **SDG 3 challenges:** costly, variable private care; unreliable public care; poor **rural access**; neglected **preventive care** – Centre-State coordination being "compulsory" is not a challenge.
| Indicator | Figure |
|---|---|
| NFHS-3 | Private sector main source of care for **70% of urban** households |
| Private outpatient share | About **70%** |
| Average hospitalisation (Aug 2026) | Rs 50,508 private vs Rs 6,631 government |
| Urban skew | **70% of infrastructure for 30% of population** |
- Concerns: high **out-of-pocket expenditure** (**39.4%** of total health expenditure, NHA 2022-23), "missing middle" of over 40 crore people; private sector dominates secondary–quaternary care in cities.

=== Human Resources
- Doctor-population ratio about **1:834** (NMC-registered allopathic doctors) vs WHO norm 1:1000 – but doctors cluster in cities, so real rural availability is far lower.
- **Specialists** at rural CHCs: only **4,413 of 21,964** (Mar 2023) – ~80% shortfall; **Uttar Pradesh** largest gap.
- Medical colleges **387 (2014) → 819 (2025)**; MBBS seats ~**1.29 lakh** (2025-26).
