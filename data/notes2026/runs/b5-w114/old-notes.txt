## SUB 2 | Mosquito-borne Diseases – Vectors and Pathogens
[114:2:0] # Vector basics
[114:2:1] - **Vector:** an organism (mosquito, flea, sandfly, tick, mite) that carries a pathogen between hosts – insects that transmit disease are called **vectors**.
[114:2:2] - **Only female mosquitoes suck blood** (they need it for eggs) and have **bigger wings** than males; **males feed on plant juices/nectar**.
[114:2:3] - **Culex, Anopheles and Aedes** – all three genera are blood-feeders found throughout the world.
[114:2:4] # Mosquito → disease
[114:2:5] TABLE head: Mosquito | Disease (pathogen type)
  [114:2:5.0] Female Anopheles | Malaria (*Plasmodium*, protozoan)
  [114:2:5.1] Aedes aegypti | Dengue, chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever (all viral)
  [114:2:5.2] Aedes albopictus (Asian tiger / forest mosquito) | Dengue, chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever – NOT Japanese encephalitis (most books)
  [114:2:5.3] Culex (*C. quinquefasciatus*, *C. pipiens*) | Lymphatic filariasis (nematode), Japanese encephalitis (virus)
  [114:2:5.4] Haemagogus | Forest (jungle) yellow fever
[114:2:6] - **NOT mosquito-borne:** kala-azar (sandfly), sleeping sickness (tsetse fly), Chagas (kissing bug), plague (rat flea), scrub typhus (chigger mite), KFD (ticks).
[114:2:7] - Wrong sets often asked: "Aedes – chikungunya – **bacteria**" is wrong (chikungunya is an **alphavirus**); "Tsetse – filariasis" and "bed bug – sleeping sickness" are wrong.
[114:2:8] # Disease details
[114:2:9] TABLE head: Disease | Pathogen | Key fact
  [114:2:9.0] Dengue ("breakbone fever") | Dengue virus (flavivirus), 4 serotypes | Platelets fall sharply → internal bleeding; virus suppresses platelet production in bone marrow, infects endothelial cells and binds platelets (CDS 2016: I, II, III)
  [114:2:9.1] Chikungunya | CHIKV, alphavirus | Severe joint pain
  [114:2:9.2] Zika | Zika virus (flavivirus) | Same mosquito as dengue (Aedes aegypti); also sexual and blood transmission; microcephaly risk
  [114:2:9.3] Yellow fever | Yellow fever virus | Spread by mosquito – not air, water or housefly
  [114:2:9.4] Japanese encephalitis | JEV (flavivirus), a virus | Infects the brain; pigs amplify, pigs + wild birds = reservoirs → keeping pigs away from settlements curbs JE; not water-borne
  [114:2:9.5] Lymphatic filariasis | Nematode *Wuchereria bancrofti* (thread-like roundworm) | Lymph vessels/nodes → fever early, then chronic swelling of lower limbs = elephantiasis; *Culex* is the vector, not the cause
[114:2:10] - Malaria vs dengue: common = fever, mosquito bite, human host; **different = mosquito species** (Anopheles vs Aedes) and pathogen (protozoan vs virus).
[114:2:11] - **Dengue vaccine:** **Qdenga (TAK-003, Takeda)** – live-attenuated tetravalent, age 4–60; CDSCO approval **July 2026** = India's first approved dengue vaccine.

## SUB 4 | Antibiotics and Antimicrobial Resistance
[114:4:0] # Antibiotics
[114:4:1] - **Fleming: penicillin, 1928**; **Waksman** coined "antibiotic", found **streptomycin** (first anti-TB drug).
[114:4:2] - Most antibiotics are **natural or semi-synthetic**, mostly from bacteria (*Streptomyces*); sulfa drugs synthetic. **Prontosil** (Domagk, 1935) → sulfanilamide.
[114:4:3] - **Griseofulvin** treats ringworm; **Acetobacter** → vinegar; chloroquine = antimalarial.
[114:4:4] # How AMR builds
[114:4:5] - Causes: incorrect dose, **incomplete course** (resistant survivors left to multiply), overuse, **self-medication**, livestock use, **mutation**, horizontal gene transfer.
[114:4:6] - Severity: **MDR → XDR → TDR**.
[114:4:7] - **Biofilm:** microbes in a self-made **EPS (extracellular polymeric substance)** matrix on **implants, catheters, tissues, food surfaces**; blocks antibiotics → chronic infection; food spoilage and contamination.
[114:4:8] # Policy and new drugs
[114:4:9] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [114:4:9.0] Nafithromycin (Miqnaf) | First indigenous macrolide; community-acquired pneumonia; Wockhardt + BIRAC (not Sun Pharma); launched 20 Nov 2024 by Dr Jitendra Singh
  [114:4:9.1] NAP-AMR | 2017, One Health based
  [114:4:9.2] Kerala KARSAP | 2018, first State AMR plan
  [114:4:9.3] Red Line campaign | 2016; red line on packs of prescription-only antibiotics (not all OTC medicines)
  [114:4:9.4] WHO Bacterial Priority Pathogens 2024 | Critical: carbapenem-resistant *Acinetobacter baumannii*, Enterobacterales, rifampicin-resistant TB
[114:4:10] ~~ Sources: app notes 5:114 sub 8
[114:4:11] - **Prontosil:** discovered **1932** (Bayer; Domagk), published 1935 – first sulpha drug, a **pro-drug** → sulfanilamide.
[114:4:12] - **Penicillin** comes from the **fungus** *Penicillium notatum*.
[114:4:13] - **WHO 2017 list (12 superbugs):** critical = *Acinetobacter baumannii*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, Enterobacteriaceae (carbapenem-resistant).
[114:4:14] - **Chloramphenicol (chloromycetin):** **broad-spectrum** antibiotic – classic **typhoid** drug.
[114:4:15] - **Sulpha drugs:** sulfanilamide, **Cibazole (sulfathiazole)**, sulfadiazine, sulphaguanidine – synthetic antibacterials.
[114:4:16] - **Antibiotics act only on bacteria** – viruses lack the cell wall and metabolic pathways they target.
[114:4:17] - **Superbug:** bacterium resistant to several antibiotics (MDR), e.g. MRSA.
[114:4:18] - **Antipyretic** = reduces **fever** (paracetamol, aspirin); analgesic = pain relief.
[114:4:19] - **Rifampicin** treats **both TB and leprosy** (leprosy MDT = rifampicin + dapsone + clofazimine); PAS and isoniazid are TB-only.

## SUB 6 | Infection, Immunity and Vaccination
[114:6:0] - **Disease vs symptom:** **cholera** is a disease; fever, sneezing, headache are symptoms. Typhoid is a type of **fever** (enteric fever).
[114:6:1] - **Contagious:** **yawning** is the classic contagious reflex (not snoring, gnashing, vomiting). Infectious diseases (AIDS, chickenpox, mumps, herpes) pass from person to person; AIDS is **not spread by casual contact**.
[114:6:2] - **Measles:** infants **below six months** are protected by **maternal antibodies** (natural passive immunity); MR vaccine at 9-12 and 16-24 months.
[114:6:3] - **No vaccine** controls **diabetes**; polio, whooping cough, smallpox are vaccine-preventable. **Rickets** (vitamin D deficiency) cannot be prevented by any UIP vaccine.
[114:6:4] - **Smallpox:** WHO declared it **eradicated in 1980** (8 May); India free in **1977** – the only human disease eradicated.
[114:6:5] - **Rabies:** spread through the **saliva** of a rabid animal (bite) → nervous system, hydrophobia.
[114:6:6] - **Community transmission (COVID-19):** an infected person in the community, unaware of infection, infects others – the source cannot be traced to travel or a known contact.
[114:6:7] > Exam angle: contagious/infectious (×4), measles immunity, no vaccine (diabetes, rickets), smallpox 1980.
[114:6:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Biology p.24 (rabies saliva, measles), p.32 (Sheet #19 vaccines), p.37 (Sheet #22 terms); PMF General Science Part 5 p.5-9 (contagious diseases, HIV); app notes 5:114 sub 3; yawning contagious – general knowledge, not checked
[114:6:9] - **Pathogen:** any **disease-causing** microorganism (virus, bacterium, fungus, protozoan).
[114:6:10] - **Interferons:** proteins from virus-infected cells that protect healthy cells – **anti-viral**.
[114:6:11] - **Cirrhosis:** scarring of the liver (alcohol, chronic hepatitis) – **non-communicable**, not transmitted person to person.
## SUB 7 | Malaria and Plasmodium
[114:7:0] - **Pathogen:** protozoan ***Plasmodium***; **carrier/vector = female Anopheles mosquito** (not tick, fly or louse).
[114:7:1] - Parasites multiply first in the **liver**, then destroy **red blood cells**; the **spleen** enlarges (organ affected by malaria).
[114:7:2] TABLE head: Species | Fact
  [114:7:2.0] *P. falciparum* | Malignant (cerebral) malaria – most severe
  [114:7:2.1] *P. vivax* | Relapsing malaria (dormant liver hypnozoites); old books: ~65% of India's cases (now ~40%; falciparum ~60% in 2024)
  [114:7:2.2] *P. malariae*, *P. ovale* | Milder
  [114:7:2.3] *P. knowlesi* | Zoonotic (macaques)
[114:7:3] - **Five** Plasmodium species infect humans. Vaccine is hard because malaria is caused by **several species** (with changing antigens) – UPSC 2010.
[114:7:4] - **Drugs:** **chloroquine** (prevention and treatment; *P. vivax*), quinine (from cinchona bark), **artemisinin/arteether** from the seeded plant ***Artemisia annua***; ACTs first-line for falciparum.
[114:7:5] - **Vaccines:** RTS,S/AS01 (Mosquirix) and R21/Matrix-M (WHO-recommended); India's **AdFalciVax** (ICMR-RMRC Bhubaneswar) at preclinical stage (2026).
[114:7:6] > Exam angle: carrier of Plasmodium (×3), malignant malaria (falciparum), 65% share (vivax), organ (spleen), drug (chloroquine, arteether), why vaccine is hard.

## SUB 9 | Air Pollution and Health

=== NOTES 5:115 | Recent Trends in India's Disease Burden – Communicable and Non-communicable Diseases
## SUB 0 | Major NCDs – Heart, Diabetes, Cancer, Lungs and Brain
[115:0:0] # Heart and blood vessels
[115:0:1] - **Coronary artery disease** = result of **plaque buildup** in the arteries supplying the heart (APPSC G2 2018; not "coronary veins disease").
[115:0:2] - **Atherosclerosis** (hardening and narrowing of arteries by plaque) is a disease of the **circulatory system**.
[115:0:3] - **Blood pressure generally increases with age.**
[115:0:4] - Leading cause of death in **India = cardiovascular diseases**; world's single biggest killer (WHO) = **ischaemic heart disease**.
[115:0:5] # Diabetes
[115:0:6] - **Normal fasting blood sugar** (non-diabetic): about **70–100 mg/dL** – nearest option **60–100 mg/dL**.
[115:0:7] - **HbA1c (glycosylated haemoglobin):** average blood glucose over 2–3 months → used to manage **diabetes**.
[115:0:8] - **BGR-34:** CSIR's Ayurvedic anti-**diabetes** drug (NBRI + CIMAP, 2016).
[115:0:9] - **First State diabetes registry:** **Goa** (June 2011).
[115:0:10] TABLE head: ICMR-INDIAB (Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology, June 2023) | Prevalence | People
  [115:0:10.0] Diabetes | 11.4% | ~101 million
  [115:0:10.1] Prediabetes | 15.3% | ~136 million
  [115:0:10.2] Hypertension | 35.5% | ~315 million
[115:0:11] # Cancer
[115:0:12] - **Cancer** = **abnormal (uncontrolled) growth of cells**; non-contagious; can spread (metastasis).
[115:0:13] - **Oncogenes** (mutated growth genes) are associated with **cancer**; first oncogene (SRC) found 1970.
[115:0:14] - **Blood cancer = leukaemia** (abnormal WBC production in bone marrow); haemophilia is a clotting disorder.
[115:0:15] - Cases **1.41 million (2022)** → **2.46 million (2045)**; cancer is **not nationally notifiable**, **17 States** have notified it, **Telangana** latest (Jul 2026).
[115:0:16] - **Free HPV vaccination** campaign for **14-year-old girls** (cervical cancer) launched **28 Feb 2026** from Ajmer.
[115:0:17] # Lungs and brain
[115:0:18] - **COPD = Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease**; World COPD Day in November.
[115:0:19] - **Alzheimer's disease:** degenerative disorder of the **brain**, the leading cause of **dementia** (memory loss, confusion).
[115:0:20] - **PSE = Photosensitive Epileptic Seizures** – triggered by flashing lights or regular patterns.
[115:0:21] - **"Big four" NCDs:** cardiovascular diseases, cancers, chronic respiratory diseases, diabetes.

[115:0:23] - **Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD):** Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis – chronic inflammation of the **gastrointestinal tract** (genetic, e.g. NOD2, and immune factors).
## SUB 1 | Dietary Fats, Cholesterol and Heart Health
[115:1:0] # Good and bad fats
[115:1:1] - **Trans fat:** raises **LDL** and **lowers HDL** ("good cholesterol"), so it is bad for health (UPPSC 2012); excess trans fat raises cardiovascular risk.
[115:1:2] - **Bakery products** made with **hydrogenated fats** contain trans fats, and excess trans fats raise the risk of NCDs – **both statements correct** (APPSC Fisheries 2023).
[115:1:3] - **WHO REPLACE plan:** eliminate industrially produced trans fats from the global food supply **by 2023** (target date; many countries still pending). **FSSAI** caps trans fat at **2%** in fats and oils.
[115:1:4] - **Saturated fat** excess raises blood lipids and cholesterol; **HDL** removes bad cholesterol – high HDL lowers heart-disease risk.
[115:1:5] - **Triglyceride** = ester of glycerol + three fatty acids = a **fat**; the main constituent of body fat.
[115:1:6] - **Most coronary heart disease patients** have elevated **cholesterol** (LDL).
[115:1:7] # Oils and fatty acids
[115:1:8] - Cook in **oil rather than vanaspati ghee**: oils hold **unsaturated fats** (mono- and polyunsaturated) that lower cholesterol (Uttarakhand 2006).
[115:1:9] - **Sunflower oil:** suitable for **heart patients**; good-quality edible oil **because** it is rich in unsaturated fatty acids (A and R both true, R explains A); more vitamin E than other vegetable oils.
[115:1:10] - **Highest PUFA among common oils:** **safflower oil** (about 75% linoleic acid) > sunflower > soybean; **coconut oil** is mostly saturated.
[115:1:11] - **Fish** is healthier than other meat because it contains **polyunsaturated fatty acids** (omega-3) that cut LDL and heart risk.
[115:1:12] - **Eskimos (Inuit):** little heart disease despite a high meat/fat diet – food rich in **omega-3** from fish and marine mammals.
[115:1:13] - **Linseed = flaxseed:** richest source of **alpha-linolenic acid (18-carbon omega-3)**; not oats, moth or mung bean.
[115:1:14] - **Essential fatty acids** cannot be made by the body and must be in the diet; their absence causes deficiency disease – but body cells **can** make most other fatty acids.
[115:1:15] TABLE head: Fatty acid | Source
  [115:1:15.0] α-Linolenic (omega-3) | Linseed oil
  [115:1:15.1] Linoleic | Peanut oil, chicken fat, olive oil
  [115:1:15.2] Arachidonic | Liver, brain, glandular organs
  [115:1:15.3] Oleic | Olive oil, canola oil
  [115:1:15.4] Lauric | Coconut / palm-kernel oil, breast milk
  [115:1:15.5] Palmitic | Butter, palm oil, meat
  [115:1:15.6] Stearic | Meat, cocoa butter
  [115:1:15.7] Butyric | Butter
[115:1:16] - **Obesity:** excess energy intake + low physical activity → obesity and heart disease; NFHS-5 overweight/obese: **24% women, 23% men**.
[115:1:17] > 14 PYQs: the trans-fat/HDL pair, oil vs vanaspati, sunflower/safflower, omega-3 (linseed, fish, Eskimos) are the repeats.
