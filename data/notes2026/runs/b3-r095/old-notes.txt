=== NOTES 3:95 | Direct Tax Reforms – Income Tax Slabs, Corporate Tax Cuts, Faceless Assessment, Dtc Debate
## SUB 0 | Income Tax – Firsts and Nature
[95:0:0] - **Income tax** first introduced **1860** by **James Wilson**, first Finance Member (Act assent **24 July 1860**; income in four schedules).
[95:0:1] - Income tax = a **progressive direct tax**.
[95:0:2] - Budget **1997-98** (FM **P. Chidambaram**, "dream budget"): cut almost every tax; income-tax rates **10, 20, 30%** (from 15, 30, 40%).
[95:0:3] - Budget 2005-06: senior citizens exempt up to **₹1.50 lakh**.
[95:0:4] - **One-by-six scheme**: compulsory return filing on lifestyle signs; telephone connection dropped in Budget 2002-03.
## SUB 1 | Income Tax Regimes and Slabs
[95:1:0] # New Regime (Section 115BAC)
[95:1:1] - **Default, exemption-free**; old regime with deductions stays **optional**.
[95:1:2] - Slabs from **Budget 2025-26**, **unchanged in 2026-27**: 0-4 lakh **nil**; 4-8 lakh **5%** … **20-24 lakh 25%**; **above 24 lakh 30%**.
[95:1:3] - **Section 87A rebate**: income up to **₹12 lakh** tax-free; **₹12.75 lakh** for salaried with **standard deduction ₹75,000**.
[95:1:4] ₹12 lakh = rebate limit; ₹12.75 lakh = salaried, after ₹75,000 standard deduction.
[95:1:5] # Budget by Budget
[95:1:6] TABLE head: Budget | Key change
  [95:1:6.0] 2020-21 (first new optional regime) | 0-2.5 lakh nil; 2.5-5 lakh 5%; 5-7.5 lakh 10% (APPSC 2019); 7.5-10 lakh 15%; 10-12.5 lakh 20%; 12.5-15 lakh 25%; above 15 lakh 30%
  [95:1:6.1] 2023-24 | Up to 3 lakh nil … above 15 lakh 30%; top surcharge (above ₹2 crore) cut 37% → 25% (new regime)
  [95:1:6.2] 2025-26 | Up to ₹12 lakh tax-free
  [95:1:6.3] 2026-27 | No slab change
## SUB 2 | Corporate Tax Cuts and Mat
[95:2:0] # Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019
[95:2:1] - Sept 2019 ordinance → **Act, 2019**: domestic companies **30% → 22%**; **no MAT** for those opting in.
[95:2:2] TABLE head: Section | Who | Base | Effective
  [95:2:2.0] 115BAA | Existing domestic companies forgoing exemptions | 22% | ~25.17%
  [95:2:2.1] 115BAB | New manufacturing companies (incorporated after 1 Oct 2019) | 15% | ~17.01%
[95:2:3] # Minimum Alternate Tax
[95:2:4] - Taxes **"zero-tax companies"** – book profits but little or no regular tax.
[95:2:5] TABLE head: Milestone | Fact
  [95:2:5.0] Introduced | Budget 1987-88 (Finance Act 1987, Sec 115J; effective AY 1988-89); rate 15.75%
  [95:2:5.1] Withdrawn / restarted | Withdrawn 1990; restarted Budget 1996-97
  [95:2:5.2] Before 2026 | 15% of book profits
  [95:2:5.3] Budget 2026-27 | 14%, a final tax; no new MAT credit after 1 April 2026; old credit set off up to 1/4 of liability (new regime only)
[95:2:6] - **Corporate-related**: FBT, MAT, tax on company profit; **capital gains tax** is **not** (levy on investor's gain).
## SUB 3 | Special Direct Taxes – Introduced and Status
[95:3:0] TABLE head: Tax | Introduced | Status
  [95:3:0.0] Estate duty | 1953 | Abolished 1985
  [95:3:0.1] Wealth tax | 1957 (Wealth Tax Act; on the richer people) | Abolished 2015
  [95:3:0.2] Expenditure tax | 1958 – FM T.T. Krishnamachari, on Kaldor's advice (APPSC 2019) | Abolished
  [95:3:0.3] Gift tax | April 1958 | Abolished Oct 1998
  [95:3:0.4] STT | 1 October 2004 | In force
  [95:3:0.5] DDT | – | Abolished from 1 April 2020; dividends taxed in shareholders' hands
  [95:3:0.6] Angel tax | 2012 – on share premium of unlisted start-ups above fair value (APPSC 2019) | Abolished in Budget 2024-25
[95:3:1] # Capital Gains
[95:3:2] - Arises on **transfer** (sale, exchange, relinquishment) of a capital asset; tax on sale of **inherited property** = **capital gains tax**.
[95:3:3] - **Short-term**: held **up to 24 months** (general rule); LTCG rate **12.5%** (Aug 2026).
[95:3:4] - **Indexation**: **Cost Inflation Index**, notified by **CBDT**, base year **2001-02**.
[95:3:5] - **Rural agricultural land** – **not a capital asset**; agricultural income tax-free.
[95:3:6] - **Poultry, wool rearing** income is **not agricultural income** – may be taxed.

## SUB 5 | Black Money Disclosure and Dispute Schemes
[95:5:0] - Main worry from black money: **loss of revenue** to the Exchequer through evasion.
[95:5:1] TABLE head: Scheme | Key fact
  [95:5:1.0] VDIS 1997 | Voluntary disclosure of black income
  [95:5:1.1] IDS 2016 | 1 June – 30 Sept 2016; 45% levy
  [95:5:1.2] PMGKY 2016 | After demonetisation; 50%
  [95:5:1.3] Vivad se Vishwas | Direct Tax Vivad se Vishwas Act, 2020; second scheme 2024 – settle pending disputes
  [95:5:1.4] FAST-DS 2026 | Foreign Assets of Small Taxpayers – Disclosure Scheme (Budget 2026-27); 30% tax + equal amount = 60%; limit ₹1 crore, or ₹5 crore for previously taxed but unreported assets (₹1 lakh fee); file till 31 Dec 2026; immunity under Black Money Act, 2015
[95:5:2] IDS 45% → PMGKY 50% → FAST-DS 60%
## SUB 6 | Retrospective and Cross-border Taxation
[95:6:0] - **Vodafone** (SC, **2012**) → **Finance Act 2012** taxed **indirect transfers retrospectively**.
[95:6:1] - **Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2021** withdrew retrospective demands (**Vodafone, Cairn**).
[95:6:2] - **BEPS** (OECD/G20): **15 actions** against MNC profit shifting to low-tax places.
[95:6:3] - **OECD Pillar Two**: **15% global minimum** corporate tax for **large MNEs** (not all companies).
[95:6:4] - **Equalisation levy**: **6%** on online ads (2016; abolished from 1 Apr 2025); **2%** on e-commerce (2020, dropped 2024).
## SUB 7 | Direct Taxes Code and Income-tax Act, 2025
[95:7:0] - Overhaul of the 1961 Act (effective 1 April 1962) announced July 2024.
[95:7:1] - **Income-tax Act, 2025** replaced it **w.e.f. 1 April 2026** (not 2025).
[95:7:2] - Single 12-month **Tax Year** from 1 April replaces **Assessment Year and Previous Year**; simpler language.
[95:7:3] - **Direct Tax Code** idea: replace the 1961 Act with a single law.
[95:7:4] Enacted name is **Income-tax Act, 2025**, not "2026".
## SUB 8 | Tax Administration
[95:8:0] - **CBDT**: statutory under **Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963** (APPSC 2019); in the **Department of Revenue** (not Expenditure).
[95:8:1] - **Faceless assessment + Taxpayers' Charter**: **13 August 2020** ("Transparent Taxation – Honouring the Honest"); **faceless appeals** Sept 2020.
[95:8:2] - **Project Insight**: launched 2017, working 2019 – AI and data analytics.
[95:8:3] - **AIS**: all reported transactions; pre-filled returns (2021). **e-Sahyog**: online compliance check of mismatches.
[95:8:4] - **PAN** (e.g. AFZPK7190K): 10 characters – 5 letters, 4 numbers, last a letter; **4th character** = holder type (**P** individual, **C** company); **not proof of address**.
[95:8:5] - **TCS on LRS** for education and medical cut **5% → 2%** (2026-27).

=== NOTES 3:96 | Goods and Services Tax – Structure, Slabs, Gst Council, Igst Settlement, Compensation Cess
## SUB 0 | Gst – Basics and Objectives
[96:0:0] - **GST**: an **indirect tax**; **single, comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based** tax on **every value addition**.
[96:0:1] - **Taxable event: supply** of goods and services (not manufacture, not sale).
[96:0:2] - **Destination principle**: revenue accrues where goods or services are **consumed**, **not where produced**.
[96:0:3] - Introduced by the **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016**; in force **1 July 2017**.
[96:0:4] - Biggest **indirect-tax reform since Independence**; monthly GST data is now a macro indicator.
[96:0:5] # Objectives
[96:0:6] - Replace a fragmented structure with one tax – **"one nation, one tax"** (APPSC 2024).
[96:0:7] - **End cascading** ("tax on tax"); **one national market**; transparency and compliance.
[96:0:8] - **Non-inflationary**; cuts production costs.
[96:0:9] - GST replaced **indirect** taxes (excise, service tax, State VAT), **not direct taxes**.
## SUB 1 | Origin, Committees and Timeline

## SUB 2 | Constitutional Basis – 101ST Amendment
[96:2:0] # Articles
[96:2:1] TABLE head: Article | Content
  [96:2:1.0] 246A | Parliament and State legislatures both make GST laws (concurrent, simultaneous power); Parliament alone for inter-State supply
  [96:2:1.1] 269A | IGST on inter-State trade – levied by the Union, apportioned between Centre and States on Council advice
  [96:2:1.2] 279A | GST Council, constituted by the President
  [96:2:1.3] 268A | Service tax – omitted
[96:2:2] - **Ratification**: the Amendment touched the federal distribution of powers → approval by **at least half the State legislatures**.
[96:2:3] Council = **Article 279A**, not 269A (APPSC G2 2025); 269A = IGST.
[96:2:4] # Before GST
[96:2:5] - **VAT** was a **State subject** (Entry 54, State List), not Central; it aimed to end double taxation and cascading of sales tax; levied on value added at each stage.
[96:2:6] - **Central Sales Tax**: on **inter-State trade** and in **Union Territories**; SEZs exempt; now merged into GST.
## SUB 3 | Gst Council
[96:3:0] # Nature and Composition
[96:3:1] - **Constitutional body** under **Article 279A**; **never statutory** (APPSC G1 2024: "statutory then constitutional" is **false**).
[96:3:2] - **Chairperson**: **Union Finance Minister**.
[96:3:3] - **Members**: **Union MoS in charge of Revenue or Finance** (only a member); **Finance/Taxation Minister of each State** and each UT with legislature.
[96:3:4] - **Vice-Chairperson**: chosen by State members from among themselves.
[96:3:5] # Voting
[96:3:6] TABLE head: Rule | Value
  [96:3:6.0] Quorum | One-half of total members
  [96:3:6.1] Centre's weight | 1/3 of votes cast
  [96:3:6.2] All States together | 2/3 of votes cast
  [96:3:6.3] Decision | 3/4 majority of weighted votes of members present and voting
[96:3:7] 1/2 quorum; 1/3 Centre; 2/3 States; 3/4 to decide
[96:3:8] # Powers and Procedures
[96:3:9] - Recommends on **tax rates, exempted goods, threshold limits**, model laws, revenue sharing, special rates, date for petroleum.
[96:3:10] - **Dispute settlement**: Council sets up a mechanism for disputes between Centre and States or among States.
[96:3:11] - Proceedings are **not invalid** due to a vacancy or defect in the Council's constitution.
[96:3:12] - **Union of India v. Mohit Minerals (2022)**: recommendations are **persuasive, not binding**; Parliament and States have **simultaneous power** to legislate; the Council was **not** held statutory.
[96:3:13] - Legislative power stays with **Parliament and State legislatures**; the Council only recommends.

[97:0:2] - **Economic Survey 2025-26**: GST 2.0 = **third pillar** of tax reform (after 2019 corporate tax cut, 2025 personal income-tax reform); rate-cut **volume effects** may cover revenue loss.
[97:0:3] - **Revenue Neutral Rate (RNR):** The **Arvind Subramanian** (CEA) Committee, report **4 Dec 2015**, put the RNR (single rate that keeps revenue at current levels) at **15-15.5%** (15% preferred) and the **standard rate** at **17-18%**.
## SUB 1 | Rate Structure and Gst 2.0
[97:1:0] - 2017 slabs: **5, 12, 18, 28%** + exempt + compensation cess over 28%; four slabs meant as **transitional**.
[97:1:1] - **GST 2.0**: approved at **56th Council meeting (3-4 Sep 2025)**; effective **22 Sep 2025**: **0, 5% (merit), 18% (standard), 40% (demerit)**.
[97:1:2] - ~**99%** of old 12% items → 5%; ~**90%** of old 28% items → 18% (rest 40%).
[97:1:3] - Special: rough diamonds **0.25%**; gold, silver, jewellery **3%**.
[97:1:4] Biggest drawback of multiple rates = **misclassification** and classification disputes (APPSC G1 2017).
[97:1:5] - **40% demerit slab (GST 2.0):** pan masala, tobacco products, aerated sugary drinks, large luxury cars, yachts and private aircraft for personal use, and online money gaming.
## SUB 2 | Impact on Commerce and MSMEs
[97:2:0] - **Common national market**; **ITC** at every stage ends cascading.
[97:2:1] - **E-way bill**: above **₹50,000**; valid **1 day per 200 km**.
[97:2:2] - Inter-State **check-posts removed** → less truck waiting and logistics cost.
[97:2:3] - Formalisation: taxpayers **66.5 lakh (2017) → 1.51 crore (2025)**.
[97:2:4] - **Composition scheme**: turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (₹50 lakh services); **1% goods, 6% services**; **no ITC**.
[97:2:5] - Reform agenda: pre-filled returns, time-bound digital registration, automated exporter refunds.
## SUB 3 | Key Terms

  [97:3:0.0] Inverted Duty Structure | Input tax > output tax → ITC piles up, working capital blocked; refund allowed

  [97:3:0.2] Destination principle | Tax goes to State of consumption
  [97:3:0.3] IGST | Levied by Centre on inter-State supply (Art. 269A); State share to destination State
## SUB 4 | Impact on States
[97:4:0] - Gave up indirect-tax powers (VAT, entry, luxury, entertainment tax, octroi) + weak own revenue → more **dependence on transfers**.
[97:4:1] - **Producing States** feared loss: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka → 5-year compensation.
[97:4:2] - **Compensation**: July 2017 – June 2022 at **14%** growth over **2015-16** base.
[97:4:3] - Cess continued to repay **COVID-19 loans**; ended **22 Sep 2025** except **tobacco** (until loans repaid).
[97:4:4] - Unused balance: **50% Centre, 50% States** in proportion to **SGST revenue**.
## SUB 5 | Unresolved Issues
[97:5:0] - **Petroleum** (crude, petrol, HSD, natural gas, ATF) and **electricity** outside; date set by Council under **Art. 279A(5)**.
[97:5:1] - **Council voting**: Centre 1/3, States 2/3; needs **3/4** → Centre's **effective veto**.
[97:5:2] - **Mohit Minerals (2022)**: recommendations **persuasive, not binding**; "flexible federation"; simultaneous power under Art. 246A.
[97:5:3] - **GSTAT**: Principal Bench **New Delhi** + State benches; launched Sep 2025, hearings Dec 2025. Mid-2025: no functional benches in many States → appeals piled in High Courts.
