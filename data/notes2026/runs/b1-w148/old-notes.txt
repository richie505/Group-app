## SUB 2 | Nathpanthis, Hath Yoga and the Sufis
[148:2:0] - **Nathpanth** traced to **Matsyendranath**; **Gorakhnath** (c. 11th c.) its great yogi. His followers are called **Jogis (Yogis)**, **Gorakhnathi**, **Kanphata** (split-eared) or Nath-panthis.
[148:2:1] - **UPPSC 2019:** **Hath Yoga was practised by the Nathpanthis** ✔ and **its technique was adopted by the Sufis** ✔ → both.
[148:2:2] - 13th-century Sufis met the Kanphata yogis; **Amritakund** (Hath-yoga text) into Arabic and Persian; breath control in all orders except the **Naqshbandi**.
[148:2:3] - **Nizamuddin Auliya** was struck by the yogic division of the body into **Siva** (head to navel) and **Sakti** (below); **Hamiduddin Nagori** equated **Alakh Niranjan** with **Khuda** in Hindi verse. The interaction was not confined to temple ritual.

## SUB 3 | Caste, Women, Sects and Syncretism
[148:3:0] - Low-caste saints: Namdev **tailor**, Kabir **weaver**, Ravidas **tanner**, Sena **barber**, Dhanna **Jat**. **Ramananda** – first medieval reformer to denounce caste outright (disciples Kabir, Raidas, Sena, Dhanna).
[148:3:1] - **Basava** – against caste; widow remarriage, gender equality. Bhakti and Sufism **weakened priestly dominance**.
[148:3:2] - **Andhra line:** **Veerashaivism and Srivaishnavism** before colonial rule; **Brahmanaidu** (Palnadu) – **Chapakudu** inter-dining; **Veerabrahmam** – disciples **Siddayya (Dudekula)**, **Kakkaiah (Madiga)**; **Nasaraiah** – Sayyid reformer among the Madigas of **Vinukonda**.
[148:3:3] - **Women:** Mirabai, Andal, **Lal Ded (Lalla)**, Janabai, Bahinabai, **Molla** (Molla Ramayanam); Dhanna was a man.

## SUB 5 | Limitations of the Movements
[148:5:0] - **Caste persisted**; **purdah** continued; the traditions **hardened into separate sects and panths**; Srivaishnavism and Veerashaivism opposed caste but could not eradicate it.
[148:5:1] - The movements failed in their aims of reforming Hinduism and uniting Hindus and Muslims, though they changed society deeply.

## SUB 0 | Guru Gobind Singh and the Khalsa

## SUB 1 | Maharaja Ranjit Singh
[149:1:0] - Born **1780** (13 Nov; Spectrum gives 2 Nov) at **Gujranwala**; son of **Maha (Mahan) Singh**, chief of the **Sukerchakia misl** – not Bhangi, Ahluwalia, Ramgarhia, Singhpuria or Sandhawalia.
[149:1:1] - Father died when he was a boy (12 per Spectrum; PMF gives 1790, aged 10); called **"Sher-e-Punjab" (Lion of Punjab)**.
[149:1:2] - **Zaman Shah** of Afghanistan invaded Punjab (1798); Ranjit Singh returned his cannons lost in the Jhelum, and in **1799 Zaman Shah made him governor of Lahore with the title "Raja"**.
[149:1:3] - **1799: captured Lahore** → **political capital Lahore**, **religious capital Amritsar** (Amritsar taken 1802/1805) – not Patiala, Kapurthala or Multan.
[149:1:4] - **1801:** proclaimed **Maharaja**; army on European lines (**Allard**, Ventura; **Fauj-i-Khas**); commander **Hari Singh Nalwa**.
[149:1:5] - **Treaty of Amritsar, 25 April 1809** (Lord **Minto**; negotiated by **Charles Metcalfe**): **Sutlej** = boundary; Ranjit Singh gave up the cis-Sutlej states and turned west.
[149:1:6] - **Conquests:** **Multan 1818, Kashmir (Srinagar) 1819**, Peshawar 1834 – his empire included **Srinagar**, not Delhi, Kabul or Makrana (Rajasthan).
[149:1:7] - **1813:** got the **Kohinoor** from **Shah Shuja** (Nadir Shah had taken it from Delhi in 1739); **Tripartite Treaty 1838** (British, Shah Shuja, Ranjit Singh).
[149:1:8] - **Secular ruler:** Hindu and Muslim ministers; blind in one eye – "**God intended me to look upon all religions with one eye, that is why He took away the light from the other**" = **Ranjit Singh**.
[149:1:9] - High court at Lahore = **Adalat-i-Ala**; covered the Harmandir Sahib in gold → "Golden Temple".
[149:1:10] > Sukerchakia → Lahore 1799 (Zaman Shah's "Raja") → Amritsar treaty 1809 (Sutlej) → Kohinoor 1813 → Kashmir 1819 → died 1839.

## SUB 4 | Guru Nanak, the Word "Sikh" and the Ten Gurus
[149:4:0] - **"Sikh"** = **disciple** (from Sanskrit **shishya**, learner) – not soldier, monk or devotee; **Khalsa** = "pure".
[149:4:1] - **Guru Nanak** (**1469-1539**) = **founder of Sikhism**; born at **Talwandi = Nankana (Sahib)** near the Ravi (now Pakistan) – not Amritsar, Nabha, Nanded, Gurdaspur or Lahore.
[149:4:2] - Teachings: one formless God (**Ik Onkar**), **Naam Simran**, **Sangat**, **Langar**, honest householder life; against idol worship and rituals of both faiths.
[149:4:3] - **Successor = Bhai Lehna → Guru Angad** (appointed by Nanak before his death, 1539) – not Amar Das or Ram Das.

## SUB 6 | Banda Bahadur and the Sikh Misls
[149:6:0] - **Original name Lachhman Dev**; Guru Gobind Singh named him **Banda** and sent him north from **Nanded (1708)**.
[149:6:1] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [149:6:1.0] 1709 | Seized Samana
  [149:6:1.1] 1710 | Defeated Wazir Khan at Chappar Chiri; conquered Sirhind → first sovereign Sikh state, capital Lohgarh (Sirhind Fateh Diwas, 12-14 May)

## SUB 7 | Guru Granth Sahib
[149:7:0] - **Adi Granth compiled by Guru Arjan (5th) in 1604**; **final version by Guru Gobind Singh at Damdama Sahib (1705)**; declared the **eternal Guru** (1708).
[149:7:1] - Script **Gurmukhi**; language "Sant Bhasha" (mix of Punjabi, Hindi and dialects).
[149:7:2] - Contains hymns of six Gurus (incl. **116 hymns of Tegh Bahadur**) and of non-Sikh saints: **Kabir**, Sufi **Sheikh (Baba) Farid**, **Namdev**, **Ravidas** (**41 shabdas**), Jaidev.
[149:7:3] - Sikh marriage **Anand Karaj** = four circumambulations of the Granth while **Lavan** hymns are sung.
[149:7:4] > Arjan 1604 (Adi Granth) → Gobind Singh 1705 (final) → eternal Guru 1708.
[149:7:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Art & Culture Part 5 p.70, p.72; old app notes row 149

=== NOTES 1:150 | Age of Discovery and the Sea Route to India – Vasco Da Gama; Motives of European Expansion
## SUB 0 | World History II – Industrial, Russian and Chinese Revolutions; Ancient Firsts
[150:0:0] # Industrial Revolution (Britain first)
[150:0:1] - **Why Britain first:** **coal and iron**, **steam power**, regular **raw materials** (colonies), **navigable rivers/canals**, favourable social-political order, **capitalist** practice and scientific inventions; railways (1825-30) sustained rather than started it.
[150:0:2] - **Cotton inventions:** **Kay's flying shuttle 1733 → Hargreaves' spinning jenny 1764 → Arkwright's water frame 1769 → Crompton's mule 1779**.
[150:0:3] - **Factory system:** production under one roof in towns ruined the **guilds**; **merchant capitalists** gained control (not "production moved to the countryside").
[150:0:4] - **Impact:** cottage industry → **factory system**; **working-class movements**; overcrowded **towns** (not villages).
[150:0:5] # Russian Revolution, 1917
[150:0:6] - **Provisional Government (Feb/March 1917):** first headed by **Prince Lvov**, then by **Alexander Kerensky** (July-Oct 1917); overthrown in the **October (Bolshevik) Revolution**.
[150:0:7] - **Lenin** = father of the Revolution; Russia = first **Marxist** revolution.
[150:0:8] - **Bolsheviks** (Lenin): small, disciplined party; **Mensheviks**: party open to all, as in Germany.
[150:0:9] # Asia and other events
[150:0:10] - **Crimean War 1853-56:** **Russia vs Ottoman Turkey** (Britain, France, Sardinia on the Turkish side).
[150:0:11] - **Boxer Rebellion:** China, 1899-1901, peak **1900**.
[150:0:12] - **1911 Revolution (Xinhai):** ended the Qing; **Republic of China** (1912).
[150:0:13] # Ancient and Renaissance firsts
[150:0:14] - **First proper writing system:** **Sumerian** cuneiform (Mesopotamia), before Egyptian hieroglyphs; Indus script undeciphered.
[150:0:15] - **Mona Lisa** = **Leonardo da Vinci** (Italian Renaissance).

## SUB 1 | World History I – American and French Revolutions, Napoleon
[150:1:0] # American Revolution
[150:1:1] - **Cause:** colonists refused taxes voted by a Parliament where they had no seats – **"No taxation without representation"**; merchants **boycotted British goods**. It was colonists vs Britain, not settlers vs Native Americans.
[150:1:2] - **Stamp Act 1765** (repealed 1766) → **Stamp Act Congress, New York City, October 1765** (9 of the 13 colonies).
[150:1:3] - **Coercive (Intolerable) Acts 1774** made reconciliation virtually impossible; the earlier Stamp Act repeal does not explain that.
[150:1:4] - **First Continental Congress, Philadelphia, Sept 1774:** rejected **Galloway's plan** of union under British authority, drew up a **statement of grievances**, agreed the **Continental Association** (stop trade with England).
[150:1:5] # French Revolution
[150:1:6] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [150:1:6.0] June 1789 | Tennis Court Oath – Third Estate (National Assembly)
  [150:1:6.1] 1791 | Olympe de Gouges' Declaration of the Rights of Woman and the Female Citizen
  [150:1:6.2] 1792 | National Convention meets; monarchy abolished, republic

  [150:1:6.4] 1804 | Napoleon Emperor of the French (first emperor of France); Napoleonic Code
[150:1:7] - **Reign of Terror:** the **Law of Suspects (Sept 1793)** let anyone be arrested, not only the political class; the Jacobins were **anti-clerical** (de-Christianisation), not allies of the clergy.
[150:1:8] - **Olympe de Gouges:** politically active writer; "the nation is the **union of woman and man**"; not on the committee that drafted the Rights of Man (1789); opposed the Jacobins and was guillotined **1793**.

## SUB 4 | Explorers and Motives of European Expansion
[150:4:0] # Why Europe sought a sea route
[150:4:1] - **1453:** Ottoman Sultan **Mehmed II** took **Constantinople**, closing the overland route; the Asian trade was controlled by **Arabs**, and **Venice and Genoa** distributed the goods in Europe.
[150:4:2] - **Motives:** spices (pepper, cinnamon, cardamom, ginger), silk and Indian textiles; profit; breaking the **Arab (Muslim) monopoly**; spreading **Christianity** (Prester John legend); rivalry of Portugal, Spain and later Holland, England, France. "Roman imperial administration" is NOT a motive.
[150:4:3] - **Aids:** **Prince Henry the Navigator** (naval school; papal bull of 1454); the **caravel** (ship) and **astrolabe**.
[150:4:4] - **"Bear in mind that the commerce of India is the commerce of the world"** – **Peter the Great** of Russia, about **18th-century** India (APPSC G2 2017); not da Gama, Clive or Dupleix.

=== NOTES 1:152 | Portuguese in India – Almeida's Blue Water Policy, Albuquerque, Estado Da India, Cartaz System, Decline
## SUB 0 | Viceroys and Governors – Almeida's Blue Water Policy, Albuquerque, da Cunha
[152:0:0] TABLE head: Governor | Years | Title | Key acts
  [152:0:0.0] Francisco de Almeida | 1505-09 | first Governor and Viceroy (appointed by Manuel I) | Blue Water Policy; forts at Anjediva, Cannanore (Cochin); Battle of Diu 1509 – beat Gujarat + Egyptian Mamluks + Zamorin
  [152:0:0.1] Afonso de Albuquerque | 1509-15 | real founder of Portuguese power | took Goa (1510) from Bijapur; Malacca 1511, Hormuz 1515; mixed marriages; abolished sati; enforced the cartaz permit system
  [152:0:0.2] Nino (Nuno) da Cunha | 1529-38 | Governor | HQ Cochin → Goa (1530); Bassein 1534 and Diu from Bahadur Shah; settlers in Bengal (Hooghly)
[152:0:1] - **Blue Water Policy** = **Portuguese**, introduced by **Almeida**: mastery of the Indian Ocean ("As long as you may be powerful at sea you will hold India as yours"), trade not land – not Albuquerque, Cabral or da Cunha.
[152:0:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Albuquerque **reversed** it with territorial conquest; Spectrum calls the Blue Water Policy the "cartaze system", but the pass system is a separate tool (see Cartaz).
[152:0:3] - **Real founder** = **Albuquerque**, not Vasco da Gama, Dias, Almeida or Drake; **first Viceroy** = **Almeida**, not Albuquerque.
[152:0:4] - **Goa (1510)** = "the first bit of Indian territory under the Europeans since Alexander the Great".
[152:0:5] - **Pairs:** Blue Water Policy – Portuguese naval supremacy; Market Policy – **Alauddin Khalji**; Swadeshi – boycott; Home Rule – self-government.

## SUB 1 | Contributions – Crops, Printing, Francis Xavier
[152:1:0] - **New World crops brought by the Portuguese:** **tobacco, potato, chilli, tomato, pineapple, papaya, guava, sapota, cashew, maize, groundnut**, lady's finger.
[152:1:1] - **Telugu soil:** the **tomato** (an American plant); not banana, apple or orange.
[152:1:2] - **Chilli** came with the Portuguese; opium, coffee and betel leaf did not.
[152:1:3] - **NOT Portuguese introductions:** **tea, coffee, rubber**; **wheat** is not a New World crop.
[152:1:4] - **Calico:** cotton cloth of **Calicut**, so called in Europe.
[152:1:5] - **Printing press:** Goa **1556**; Bible printed in Kannada and Malayalam.
[152:1:6] - **St. Francis Xavier:** a **co-founder of the Jesuit Order (1534)**; reached Goa 1542; **died on Shangchuan island, China (1552)** – not in Goa – but his body lies in the **Basilica of Bom Jesus, Goa**; feast **3 December** every year.

## SUB 3 | Hooghly, Piracy and the Decline
[152:3:0] - **Hooghly** was the **Portuguese** base for **piracy** (with Arakanese pirates) and slave-raiding in the **Bay of Bengal**.
[152:3:1] - **1632:** **Shah Jahan** had Bengal governor **Qasim Khan** besiege and take Hooghly (June-Sept 1632).
[152:3:2] - **Shaista Khan** (Aurangzeb's Bengal governor) took **Sondip and Chittagong** and crushed the Arakanese pirates.
[152:3:3] - **Goa Inquisition** (from 1560): state machinery used for conversions and against religious dissent; Hindus too were persecuted.
[152:3:4] - **Causes of decline:** weak, corrupt successors after Albuquerque; **religious intolerance** and forced conversions (Goa Inquisition); piracy and dishonest trade; **Portugal united with Spain (1580)**; discovery of **Brazil**; Dutch and English competition.
[152:3:5] - **Losses:** Surat 1612 (English); **Hormuz 1622**; Hooghly 1632; **Nagapattinam 1658 and Cochin / Malabar forts 1663** (Dutch); Salsette-Bassein 1739 (Marathas).
[152:3:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.63-66; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.27; PMF Modern History Part 1 p.102, p.106
## SUB 4 | First to Come, Last to Leave – Goa 1961 and the Andhra Coast
[152:4:0] - **After 15 August 1947** Goa, Daman and Diu stayed **Portuguese**; the freedom struggle continued against **Portugal**.
[152:4:1] - **Operation Vijay, 19 December 1961** freed them; Goa statehood **30 May 1987**.
[152:4:2] - **Estado da India** = the Portuguese state in Asia (HQ Cochin, then Goa).
[152:4:3] - **Telugu name:** "**Budata Keechu**"; **Petapoli** = **Nizampatnam**; the **1669-70 Machilipatnam factory** allowed by **Abdullah Qutb Shah** was **French**.
[152:4:4] - **Andhra coast:** posts at Masulipatnam, Pulicat, San Thome; Pulicat lost to the Dutch (1610); influence faded by the mid-17th century.
[152:4:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.61; PMF Modern History Part 1 p.107; old app notes row 152 (sub 7-8)
## SUB 5 | Cartaz System – Naval Trade Licence
[152:5:0] - **Cartaz** = a **naval trade licence / pass** that Asian ships had to buy from the Portuguese to sail the Indian Ocean; begun by **Vasco da Gama (1502)**, systematised by **Albuquerque**.
[152:5:1] - A ship **without a cartaz was confiscated** and its crew punished; monopoly goods (pepper, horses, ginger, cinnamon …) could not be loaded.
[152:5:2] - Backed by the **cafila** (convoy of local ships under a Portuguese fleet; older app notes wrote "Caes") and **customs duties**.
[152:5:3] - Monopoly and duties caused constant war with the **Zamorin of Calicut**.
[152:5:4] > Distractors in Polytechnic 2020: "Marcent", "Shellback", "Pollywog" are not Portuguese systems.

## SUB 3 | VOC – Formation, Charter and Trade
[153:3:0] - **VOC (Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie):** **20 March 1602**, the **States-General** merged several companies; powers to **wage war, make treaties, acquire territory, build forts**. ("1592" in NextGen is a misprint.)
[153:3:1] - **Background:** **Philip II**'s ban on Lisbon; **Linschoten**'s *Itinerario*; **Cornelis de Houtman** reached **Sumatra and Bantam (1596)**.
[153:3:2] - **Asian HQ:** **Batavia (Jakarta)**; the spice islands came first, India was a cloth depot.
[153:3:3] - **Trade:** Coromandel textiles for spices; **indigo**, raw **silk**, cotton cloth, **saltpetre (Bihar)**, opium and rice; silk and porcelain imported.
[153:3:4] - **Andhra:** first Europeans in Andhra to **mint coins**; **Koringa** shipbuilding; Pulicat a hub of the **slave trade to Batavia**; network from **Machilipatnam to Bheemunipatnam** (Draksharama, Palakollu, Narsapur, Jagannathapuram).
[153:3:5] - **Industry (old app notes):** **Koringa** – shipbuilding; **Chittavalasa** – jute; Machilipatnam – handloom textiles; Pulicat did not make porcelain.

=== NOTES 1:154 | English East India Company – Charter of 1600, Surat, Madras, Bombay, Calcutta; Farman of 1717
## SUB 0 | Surat – the First Permanent Factory and the Mughal Court
[154:0:0] - **Surat (1613)** = the English Company's **first permanent factory in India** – on a **farman of Jahangir** (not Akbar, Shah Jahan or Aurangzeb); Pune, Goa, Pondicherry, Bangalore were never its first factory.
[154:0:1] - **Masulipatnam (1611)** came earlier but only as a temporary trading post (permanent factory 1616) – so "first factory in India" = **Surat**, "first in South India / Andhra" = **Machilipatnam**.
[154:0:2] - **Captain William Hawkins** – third voyage, landed at **Surat 1608**, at Jahangir's court (Agra) **1609**; first envoy of **James I**; permission withdrawn under Portuguese pressure.
[154:0:3] - **Battle of Swally (1612)**: **Captain Thomas Best** defeated the **Portuguese** off Surat → Jahangir's farman of **1613** (factory under Thomas Aldworth).
[154:0:4] - **Sir Thomas Roe (1615-19)** = ambassador of **King James I** to **Jahangir**; no commercial treaty, but privileges to set up factories at **Agra, Ahmedabad and Broach**; his chaplain was **Edward Terry**.
[154:0:5] - **Ralph Fitch (1583-86)** = first Englishman to visit **Akbar's** court (Agra, Fatehpur Sikri) – before Hawkins and Roe.
[154:0:6] - **Western Presidency**: seat first at **Surat**, shifted to **Bombay in 1687**.
[154:0:7] - **Factories of the 1st quarter of the 17th century**: **Broach** (yes, Roe's privileges); **Chicacole** and **Trichinopoly** – no (UPSC 2021: 1 only).

## SUB 1 | The Charter of 1600 and the Company's Formation
[154:1:0] - **31 December 1600**: **Queen Elizabeth I** granted the charter → the Company was founded in **1600** (not 1601, 1604 or 1607).
[154:1:1] - Name: "**The Governor and Company of Merchants of London Trading into the East Indies**"; promoted by the "**Merchant Adventurers**" (1599).
[154:1:2] - Charter gave only **exclusive trading rights** – a **15-year monopoly** between the **Cape of Good Hope** and the **Straits of Magellan** (made indefinite in 1609); **no territorial governance or legislative power**.
[154:1:3] - **Emperor of India in 1600 = Akbar** (1556-1605), contemporary of **Elizabeth I** (1558-1603).
[154:1:4] - Management: a **Governor + 24-member committee** (later Court of Directors); shareholders = General Court (later Court of Proprietors).
[154:1:5] - **First voyage 1602** under **James Lancaster** (flagship **Red Dragon**, capital £68,000) → factory at **Bantam** (Java), not India.
[154:1:6] - **Levant Company (1592)** traded with the Ottoman lands; the **East India Company** was the first chartered to trade with India; "The English Company trading to the East Indies" was the rival of **1698**.
[154:1:7] TABLE head: Year | Change
  [154:1:7.0] 1657 | Cromwell's charter – permanent joint stock
  [154:1:7.1] Charles II, James II | Coin money, jurisdiction, war and peace with non-Christians
  [154:1:7.2] 1708 | Rival companies merged – United Company of Merchants of England Trading to the East Indies
  [154:1:7.3] 1858 | Company rule ended – Government of India Act, 1858
[154:1:8] - Early objective: **eastern trade** (spices, textiles), paid for with **imported bullion**; secret of later success (UPPCS 1994): absence of nationalism, western-trained army – "all the above".

## SUB 2 | Madras – Francis Day and Fort St. George
[154:2:0] - **1639**: **Francis Day** (with **Andrew Cogan**), a member of the Masulipatnam council, got **Madraspatnam** on lease from the **Chandragiri** ruler **Venkatapati Raya III**, through the **Damerla brothers (Venkatadri, Ayyappa)** – not John Child, Roe or Oxenden.
[154:2:1] - **Fort St. George** at Madraspatnam (near **Mylapore**/San Thome): built from 1 March 1640, completed **23 April 1644** (St George's Day) = **first fort built by the English in India** and the **first fortified English settlement** (not Fort William, Mahim or Aguada).
[154:2:2] - **Chennapatnam** named after **Damerla Chennappa**; Madras = chief of east-coast factories (1641), **presidency 1684**, **first municipality in India (1687-88)**.
[154:2:3] - Main business of the early English settlements in Andhra and Fort St. George = **trade in locally woven piece-goods** (not conquest, spices or agriculture).
[154:2:4] - **Mid-18th century populations** of the fortified English settlements: **Madras about 3 lakh**, Calcutta 2 lakh, Bombay 70,000 → **Madras largest**.

  [154:2:5.0] Madras | Fort St. George
  [154:2:5.1] Bombay | Bombay (replaced Surat, 1687)
  [154:2:5.2] Bengal | Fort William, Calcutta

## SUB 3 | Bengal and Calcutta – Hugli, Job Charnock, Fort William
[154:3:0] - **1651**: **Shah Shuja** (subahdar) let the English trade in Bengal for Rs 3,000 a year → **first English factory in Bengal = Hugli (1651)**, then **Qasim Bazar**, Patna, Rajmahal (earlier Hariharpur and Balasore, 1633, are in **Odisha**).
[154:3:1] - **Job Charnock**, chief of the **Qasim Bazar** factory, returned to **Sutanuti** in **August 1690** → **founder of Calcutta** (not Charles Eyre, Aungier, Norris or John Surman).
[154:3:2] - **1698**: zamindari of **Sutanuti, Gobindapur and Kalikata** bought; **1700**: fortified settlement named **Fort William** – Sir **Charles Eyre** first president.
[154:3:3] - Fort built by the British at Calcutta = **Fort William** (Fort St. David is at Cuddalore; Fort St. George at Madras).
[154:3:4] - Europeans got the best **saltpetre (shora) and opium from Bihar**.
[154:3:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.68, 71-72 (book p.36, 39-40); old app notes row 154
[154:3:6] - **Fort St. David:** English fort at **Devanampatnam (Tegnapatnam) near Cuddalore**, bought in **1690**.
## SUB 4 | Bombay – Dowry, Lease and Gerald Aungier
[154:4:0] - **1661**: Bombay came to **Charles II** as dowry of the **Portuguese** princess **Catherine of Braganza** (married 1662) – taken from the **Portuguese**, not Dutch, French or Danes.
[154:4:1] - **1668**: Charles II gave it to the Company for **£10 a year** (some books 1674/1688 – the lease was 1668).
[154:4:2] - **Gerald Aungier** (President of Surat and Governor of Bombay, 1669-77) made Bombay a major commercial hub – "founding father" of Bombay.
[154:4:3] - Bombay **replaced Surat** as the chief English settlement on the west coast (1687).
[154:4:4] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.70; NextGen Modern India L3 Part 1 p.17; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Aungier; old app notes row 154
[154:4:5] - **Hornby Vellard:** sea wall closing the Worli breach, approved by Governor **William Hornby** (1771–84), completed 1784.
## SUB 5 | Chronology, Child's War and the Farman of 1717
[154:5:0] - Order of English centres: **Surat 1613 → Madras 1639 → Bombay 1668 → Calcutta 1690**.
[154:5:1] - **Child's War (1686-90)**: under **Josiah Child** (London) and **Sir John Child** (in India) the Company fought **Aurangzeb**, lost, was pardoned on paying a fine – Aurangzeb demanded Sir John Child's expulsion.
[154:5:2] - **Shivaji** sacked Surat twice (1664, 1670) and threatened the English factory.
[154:5:3] - **Farman of 1717** (mission of **John Surman**, 1715) by **Farrukhsiyar** = "**Magna Carta**" of the Company.

  [154:5:4.0] Bengal | Duty-free trade for Rs 3,000 a year; dastaks
  [154:5:4.1] Surat | All duties dropped for Rs 10,000 a year
  [154:5:4.2] Bombay | Company coins current in the Mughal empire
[154:5:5] - Misuse of dastaks for private trade drained the Nawab's revenue → Plassey (1757), Buxar (1764) → **Bengal** = first major territorial base.

## SUB 0 | French Settlements – Order of Founding and Pondicherry
[155:0:0] TABLE head: Settlement | Year | Detail
  [155:0:0.0] Surat | 1668 (expedition 1667) | First French factory in India, under François Caron (not Pulicat, Cochin, Calicut, Qasim Bazar)
  [155:0:0.1] Masulipatnam | 1669 | Marcara, patent from the Sultan of Golconda (Abdullah Qutb Shah)
  [155:0:0.2] Pondicherry | 1673-74 | Land from Sher Khan Lodi (Bijapur governor of Valikondapuram) to François Martin; Fort St. Louis
  [155:0:0.3] Chandernagore | 1673 / 1690 | Permission of Shaista Khan (1673); in Bengal, not Malabar
  [155:0:0.4] Yanam | 1723 (re-occupied 1731) | Godavari delta; Nizam confirmed it 1750-51
  [155:0:0.5] Mahe | 1725 | Malabar coast

[155:0:1] - **17th-century** French settlements: Surat, Machilipatnam, Pondicherry, Chandernagore – **Mahe (1725) is not one**.
[155:0:2] - Chronology (APPSC G1): **Surat → Pondicherry → Yanam (1723) → Mahe (1725) → Karaikal (1739)**; the printed key put Yanam last, counting from the Nizam's grant of 1750-51.
[155:0:3] - **Pondicherry**: French control from **1674**; most important settlement and **French HQ**; **Dutch captured it in 1693**, restored by **Treaty of Ryswick (1697)**; taken by the English **1761**, restored by the **Treaty of Paris (1763)** – so the English **did** occupy it.
[155:0:4] - UPSC 2010: the **Portuguese** were the first Europeans at Pondicherry (16th century); the French were not the second; the English occupied it (1761, 1793) → only statement I correct.
[155:0:5] - After 1763 the French kept only enclaves – **Pondicherry, Karaikal, Mahe, Yanam, Chandernagore**; Chandernagore joined India by plebiscite (**1950**); the other four de facto **1954**, de jure **1962** → **UT of Puducherry**.
[155:0:6] - **Yanam** = the part of the UT of Puducherry inside **Andhra Pradesh** (Mahe – Kerala, Karaikal – Tamil Nadu; Daman is not part of Puducherry).

## SUB 1 | French East India Company and Colbert
[155:1:0] - Founded **1664** on the recommendation of **Jean-Baptiste Colbert**, Finance Minister of **Louis XIV** – Colbert is regarded as its founder (not Richelieu, Mazarin or François Martin).
[155:1:1] - Name: **Compagnie des Indes Orientales**; **50-year monopoly** in the Indian and Pacific Oceans + rights over **Madagascar**.
[155:1:2] - **State-controlled**: the king was the largest shareholder; dependent on subsidies → vulnerable; reorganised 1720 as the Perpetual Company of the Indies.
[155:1:3] - French = the **last European company to arrive** in India.
[155:1:4] TABLE head: Governor | Key point
  [155:1:4.0] François Martin | First governor; builder of Pondicherry; died 31 Dec 1706
  [155:1:4.1] Pierre Benoît Dumas (1735-41) | Right to mint rupees at Pondicherry (1736)
  [155:1:4.2] Dupleix (1742-54) | Peak of French power
  [155:1:4.3] Godeheu (1754) | Replaced Dupleix; made peace
  [155:1:4.4] Count de Lally | Lost at Wandiwash (1760) to Eyre Coote
[155:1:5] - French lost for **lack of naval supremacy** and steady state support.

## SUB 2 | Dupleix and His Policy of Intervention
[155:2:0] - **Joseph François Dupleix**, Governor-General of French India (**1742-54**) = **first European to take part in the quarrels of Indian princes to gain territory** (not Clive, Albuquerque or Warren Hastings).
[155:2:1] - First used **trained Indian sepoys** and placed a French army at an Indian court at the prince's cost – the **origin of the subsidiary alliance**; the English later copied his methods.
[155:2:2] - Exploited the **Hyderabad** (Nasir Jang vs **Muzaffar Jang**) and **Carnatic** (Anwaruddin vs **Chanda Sahib**) successions after Nizam-ul-Mulk's death (1748).
[155:2:3] - Rewards: made **Nawab / governor of the Mughal territory south of the Krishna**; jagir **Valapadu**.
[155:2:4] - Recalled **1754** (cost and failures); **Treaty of Pondicherry (1754)** barred interference in princes' quarrels.
[155:2:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.77-80 (book p.45-48); NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 5 p.34; old app notes row 155
