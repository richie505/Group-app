=== French Settlements – Order of Founding and Pondicherry
| Settlement | Year | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Surat** | 1668 (expedition 1667) | **First French factory in India**, under **François Caron** (not Pulicat, Cochin, Calicut, Qasim Bazar) |
| **Masulipatnam** | 1669 | **Marcara**, patent from the Sultan of Golconda (**Abdullah Qutb Shah**) |
| **Pondicherry** | 1673-74 | Land from **Sher Khan Lodi** (Bijapur governor of Valikondapuram) to **François Martin**; **Fort St. Louis** |
| **Chandernagore** | 1673 / 1690 | Permission of **Shaista Khan** (1673); in Bengal, not Malabar |
| **Yanam** | 1723 (re-occupied 1731) | Godavari delta; Nizam confirmed it 1750-51 |
| **Mahe** | 1725 | **Malabar coast** |
| **Karaikal** | 1739 | Coromandel coast – founded last |
- **17th-century** French settlements: Surat, Machilipatnam, Pondicherry, Chandernagore – **Mahe (1725) is not one**.
- Chronology (APPSC G1): **Surat → Pondicherry → Yanam (1723) → Mahe (1725) → Karaikal (1739)**; the printed key put Yanam last, counting from the Nizam's grant of 1750-51.
- **Pondicherry**: French control from **1674**; most important settlement and **French HQ**; **Dutch captured it in 1693**, restored by **Treaty of Ryswick (1697)**; taken by the English **1761**, restored by the **Treaty of Paris (1763)** – so the English **did** occupy it.
- UPSC 2010: the **Portuguese** were the first Europeans at Pondicherry (16th century); the French were not the second; the English occupied it (1761, 1793) → only statement I correct.
- After 1763 the French kept only enclaves – **Pondicherry, Karaikal, Mahe, Yanam, Chandernagore**; Chandernagore joined India by plebiscite (**1950**); the other four de facto **1954**, de jure **1962** → **UT of Puducherry**.
- **Yanam** = the part of the UT of Puducherry inside **Andhra Pradesh** (Mahe – Kerala, Karaikal – Tamil Nadu; Daman is not part of Puducherry).

=== French East India Company and Colbert
- Founded **1664** on the recommendation of **Jean-Baptiste Colbert**, Finance Minister of **Louis XIV** – Colbert is regarded as its founder (not Richelieu, Mazarin or François Martin).
- Name: **Compagnie des Indes Orientales**; **50-year monopoly** in the Indian and Pacific Oceans + rights over **Madagascar**.
- **State-controlled**: the king was the largest shareholder; dependent on subsidies → vulnerable; reorganised 1720 as the Perpetual Company of the Indies.
- French = the **last European company to arrive** in India.
| Governor | Key point |
|---|---|
| **François Martin** | First governor; builder of Pondicherry; died 31 Dec 1706 |
| **Pierre Benoît Dumas** (1735-41) | Right to mint rupees at Pondicherry (1736) |
| **Dupleix** (1742-54) | Peak of French power |
| **Godeheu** (1754) | Replaced Dupleix; made peace |
| **Count de Lally** | Lost at **Wandiwash (1760)** to **Eyre Coote** |
- French lost for **lack of naval supremacy** and steady state support.

=== Dupleix and His Policy of Intervention
- **Joseph François Dupleix**, Governor-General of French India (**1742-54**) = **first European to take part in the quarrels of Indian princes to gain territory** (not Clive, Albuquerque or Warren Hastings).
- First used **trained Indian sepoys** and placed a French army at an Indian court at the prince's cost – the **origin of the subsidiary alliance**; the English later copied his methods.
- Exploited the **Hyderabad** (Nasir Jang vs **Muzaffar Jang**) and **Carnatic** (Anwaruddin vs **Chanda Sahib**) successions after Nizam-ul-Mulk's death (1748).
- Rewards: made **Nawab / governor of the Mughal territory south of the Krishna**; jagir **Valapadu**.
- Recalled **1754** (cost and failures); **Treaty of Pondicherry (1754)** barred interference in princes' quarrels.
