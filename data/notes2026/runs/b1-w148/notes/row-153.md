=== First Dutch Factories and Headquarters – Machilipatnam, Pulicat, Nagapattinam
| Factory | Year | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Machilipatnam (Masulipatnam)** | **1605** | **first Dutch factory in India and in Andhra**; Admiral **Van der Haghen**; permission of **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** (Golconda) |
| Petapuli (Nizampatnam) | 1606 | Krishna-delta weaving centre |
| **Pulicat** | **1609-10** | permission of Queen **Eraivi** (Venkata II of Vijayanagara / Chandragiri); **Fort Geldria (1613)**; HQ of the **Coromandel directorate 1616-1690** |
| Bheemunipatnam / Palakollu | 1641 / 1652 | Dutch cemetery at Bheemili; Godavari weaving centre |
| **Nagapattinam** | **1658** (from the Portuguese) | **HQ of the Dutch in India from 1690** |
- **First Dutch factory:** **Machilipatnam 1605** (asked ×5: APPSC EO 2021, NDA 2010, ACF 2012, AP History PYQ); Pulicat came second.
- **⚠ Trap – "earliest" without Machilipatnam:** among Surat (1616), **Pulicat (1609)**, Cochin (1663), Cassimbazar the answer is **Pulicat** (UPSC 2003).
- **Headquarters:** **Pulicat** till **1689-90** = the Dutch capital / Governor's seat on the Coromandel and in Andhra; **Nagapattinam** after 1690 = HQ of Dutch settlements in India (APPSC G2 2005).
- **Machilipatnam first:** NextGen says the Dutch set up the first European factory there; PMF says the Portuguese were first established at Masulipatnam, then the Dutch, English (1611) and French (1669).

- **Trading centres:** Nagapattinam, Chinsura, Machilipatnam, Surat, Broach, Agra, Cochin, Ahmedabad, Patna, Kasimbazar, Balasore – i.e. "all of the above".
- **Bengal:** **Chinsurah** was Dutch; Bandel and Hooghly were Portuguese, **Serampore (Srirampur)** Danish.
- **Kochi:** the Dutch took it from the Portuguese (**1663**), rebuilt the fort (**Fort Williams**, Fort Kochi); it passed to the **British in 1795** (1814 treaty).

- **Dutch colony among Yanam, Mahe, Karaikal, Bheemili:** **Bheemili** – Yanam and Mahe were French, and Karaikal (Dutch 1645) became French in 1739.

=== VOC – Formation, Charter and Trade
- **VOC (Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie):** **20 March 1602**, the **States-General** merged several companies; powers to **wage war, make treaties, acquire territory, build forts**. ("1592" in NextGen is a misprint.)
- **Background:** **Philip II**'s ban on Lisbon; **Linschoten**'s *Itinerario*; **Cornelis de Houtman** reached **Sumatra and Bantam (1596)**.
- **Asian HQ:** **Batavia (Jakarta)**; the spice islands came first, India was a cloth depot.
- **Trade:** Coromandel textiles for spices; **indigo**, raw **silk**, cotton cloth, **saltpetre (Bihar)**, opium and rice; silk and porcelain imported.
- **Andhra:** first Europeans in Andhra to **mint coins**; **Koringa** shipbuilding; Pulicat a hub of the **slave trade to Batavia**; network from **Machilipatnam to Bheemunipatnam** (Draksharama, Palakollu, Narsapur, Jagannathapuram).
- **Industry (old app notes):** **Koringa** – shipbuilding; **Chittavalasa** – jute; Machilipatnam – handloom textiles; Pulicat did not make porcelain.

=== Battles and Decline – Colachel 1741, Bedara 1759
| Battle | Year | Sides | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Colachel** | **1741** | **Marthanda Varma (Travancore)** vs Dutch (De Lannoy) | Dutch beaten – end of Dutch power in **Kerala/Malabar** |
| **Bedara (Chinsurah / Hooghly)** | **Nov 1759** | English (**Francis Forde**, under Clive) vs Dutch | "a crushing blow to Dutch ambitions in India" |
- **Why the Dutch failed:** **English rivalry** (Amboyna 1623; **1667 compromise** – Dutch withdrew from India for Indonesia), the defeat at Bedara, and their priority on the **Indonesian spice trade**; by **1795** the English had expelled them – "the English forces made them leave India".
- **Anglo-Dutch conferences of 1613 and 1615** failed to end the rivalry.
- **Anglo-Dutch Treaty 1824:** remaining posts (Pulicat, Chinsura) to the British by **1825**; Dutch presence in India **1605-1825**.
