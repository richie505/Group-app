## SUB 0 | Guru Gobind Singh and the Khalsa
[0:0] - **Guru Gobind Singh** (1666-1708) = **tenth and last human Guru** of the Sikhs – not Tegh Bahadur, Har Krishan or Banda Bahadur.
[0:1] - **Born 1666 at Patna** (Bihar) – Takht Sri Patna Sahib marks the birthplace.
[0:2] - **Founded the Khalsa** ("army of the pure") on **Baisakhi, 13 April 1699**, at **Anandpur** (Takht Sri **Keshgarh Sahib**, Rupnagar) – not Amritsar; the **Panj Pyare** (five volunteers) were baptised first with sacred nectar.
[0:3] - **His greatness:** he **turned the Sikhs into a military order** (Khalsa; surnames **Singh / Kaur**) – he did **not** found the Sikh state (that was Banda Bahadur, 1710) and did not defeat Aurangzeb.
[0:4] - **Five Ks:** **Kesh** (hair), **Kangha** (comb), **Kara** (steel bangle), **Kachera** (undergarment), **Kirpan** (sword) – **Kirtan is not** one of them.
[0:5] - Wrote the **Zafarnama** (Persian letter to Aurangzeb accusing the Mughals of betrayal); Dasam Granth at **Paonta Sahib**; the **Chali Mukte** (40 liberated ones) died defending him at **Muktsar** (Maghi).
[0:6] - Fought Aurangzeb from 1699; made peace with **Bahadur Shah**; killed at **Nanded** (1708); declared the **Guru Granth Sahib the eternal Guru** – personal Guruship ended.
[0:7] - **"How many years ago" (MPPSC 2000):** 1699 → about **300 years**.
[0:8] - **350th birth anniversary (Prakash Parv):** PM attended the celebrations at **Patna** (Gandhi Maidan) on **5 January 2017** and released a stamp – not Nanded, Amritsar or Anandpur.
[0:9] > Gobind Singh: Patna 1666 → Khalsa, Anandpur, Baisakhi 1699 → Nanded 1708; 10th and last Guru.
[0:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Art & Culture Part 5 p.70-72; Rocket Indian History 02 (Rocket Sheet #59-60); PMF Modern History Part 1 p.74; pmindia.gov.in release, 5 Jan 2017; old app notes row 149
## SUB 1 | Maharaja Ranjit Singh
[1:0] - Born **1780** (13 Nov; Spectrum gives 2 Nov) at **Gujranwala**; son of **Maha (Mahan) Singh**, chief of the **Sukerchakia misl** – not Bhangi, Ahluwalia, Ramgarhia, Singhpuria or Sandhawalia.
[1:1] - Father died when he was a boy (12 per Spectrum; PMF gives 1790, aged 10); called **"Sher-e-Punjab" (Lion of Punjab)**.
[1:2] - **Zaman Shah** of Afghanistan invaded Punjab (1798); Ranjit Singh returned his cannons lost in the Jhelum, and in **1799 Zaman Shah made him governor of Lahore with the title "Raja"**.
[1:3] - **1799: captured Lahore** → **political capital Lahore**, **religious capital Amritsar** (Amritsar taken 1802/1805) – not Patiala, Kapurthala or Multan.
[1:4] - **1801:** proclaimed **Maharaja**; army on European lines (**Allard**, Ventura; **Fauj-i-Khas**); commander **Hari Singh Nalwa**.
[1:5] - **Treaty of Amritsar, 25 April 1809** (Lord **Minto**; negotiated by **Charles Metcalfe**): **Sutlej** = boundary; Ranjit Singh gave up the cis-Sutlej states and turned west.
[1:6] - **Conquests:** **Multan 1818, Kashmir (Srinagar) 1819**, Peshawar 1834 – his empire included **Srinagar**, not Delhi, Kabul or Makrana (Rajasthan).
[1:7] - **1813:** got the **Kohinoor** from **Shah Shuja** (Nadir Shah had taken it from Delhi in 1739); **Tripartite Treaty 1838** (British, Shah Shuja, Ranjit Singh).
[1:8] - **Secular ruler:** Hindu and Muslim ministers; blind in one eye – "**God intended me to look upon all religions with one eye, that is why He took away the light from the other**" = **Ranjit Singh**.
[1:9] - High court at Lahore = **Adalat-i-Ala**; covered the Harmandir Sahib in gold → "Golden Temple".
[1:10] > Sukerchakia → Lahore 1799 (Zaman Shah's "Raja") → Amritsar treaty 1809 (Sutlej) → Kohinoor 1813 → Kashmir 1819 → died 1839.
[1:11] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 1 p.74-76; Spectrum Modern India p.146-147 (book p.114-115); NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 5 p.17; PMF Modern History Part 2 p.52; old app notes row 149
## SUB 2 | Gurus and the Mughal Emperors – Martyrdoms
[2:0] TABLE head: Guru | Emperor | Relation
  [2:0.0] Ram Das (4th) | Akbar | Friendly – grant of about 500 bighas of land where Ramdaspur (Amritsar) grew
  [2:0.1] Arjan Dev (5th) | Jahangir | Executed 1606 for helping rebel Prince Khusrau – first martyr Guru
  [2:0.2] Hargobind (6th) | Jahangir | Held in Gwalior Fort, freed 1619 (with 52 prisoners – Bandi Chhor Divas) – not executed
  [2:0.3] Har Rai, Har Krishan | Aurangzeb | Conflict, summoned / detained
  [2:0.4] Tegh Bahadur (9th) | Aurangzeb | Publicly executed at Delhi (Chandni Chowk), 1675, for refusing Islam – "Hind-di-Chadar"
  [2:0.5] Gobind Singh (10th) | Aurangzeb, then Bahadur Shah | Wars from 1699; Zafarnama; later peace with Bahadur Shah
[2:1] - **Sikh Guru executed by Aurangzeb = Tegh Bahadur** (not Arjan Dev, Gobind Singh, Ram Das or Angad).
[2:2] - **Gurus put to death by the rulers = Arjan Dev (1606) and Tegh Bahadur (1675)** – Angad was not, and Hargobind was only imprisoned.
[2:3] - **Sis Ganj** = place of Tegh Bahadur's execution; **Rakab Ganj** = his cremation; 350th martyrdom year observed in 2025-26.
[2:4] > Akbar–Ram Das (land); Jahangir–Arjan (1606) and Hargobind (Gwalior); Aurangzeb–Tegh Bahadur (1675).

## SUB 3 | After Ranjit Singh – Kharak Singh to Duleep Singh
[3:0] - Ranjit Singh died **June 1839** → disputed successions: **Kharak (Khadag) Singh (1839, immediate successor)** → Nau Nihal Singh → Sher Singh → **Duleep (Dalip) Singh (1843)** – **Hari Singh Nalwa** was a general, not a successor.
[3:1] - **Duleep Singh** = youngest son, crowned at five (1843) with mother **Rani Jindan (Jind Kaur)** as regent = **last king of the Sikh empire (1843-1849)**.
[3:2] - First Anglo-Sikh War 1845-46 (Treaty of **Lahore**, 9 March 1846; Treaty of **Bhairowal**, Dec 1846); Second 1848-49 → **Dalhousie annexed Punjab (1849)**.
[3:3] - Raised in England, **converted to Christianity (1853)**, **returned to Sikhism (1886)**; **went to Russia** (1887) seeking Tsar Alexander III's help.
[3:4] - **Died in Paris, October 1893** (22 Oct in most references; some books and the UPPCS key give 23 Oct); **buried with Christian rites at Elveden, England** – not cremated at Nasik.
[3:5] > Kharak Singh first, Duleep Singh last; Duleep: Christian 1853 → Sikh 1886 → Russia 1887 → died Paris 1893, buried Elveden.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 2 p.52-55; Spectrum Modern India p.147-148; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duleep_Singh (death 22/23 Oct 1893, Elveden burial, Russia 1887); old app notes row 149
## SUB 4 | Guru Nanak, the Word "Sikh" and the Ten Gurus
[4:0] - **"Sikh"** = **disciple** (from Sanskrit **shishya**, learner) – not soldier, monk or devotee; **Khalsa** = "pure".
[4:1] - **Guru Nanak** (**1469-1539**) = **founder of Sikhism**; born at **Talwandi = Nankana (Sahib)** near the Ravi (now Pakistan) – not Amritsar, Nabha, Nanded, Gurdaspur or Lahore.
[4:2] - Teachings: one formless God (**Ik Onkar**), **Naam Simran**, **Sangat**, **Langar**, honest householder life; against idol worship and rituals of both faiths.
[4:3] - **Successor = Bhai Lehna → Guru Angad** (appointed by Nanak before his death, 1539) – not Amar Das or Ram Das.
[4:4] TABLE head: No. | Guru | Contribution
  [4:4.0] 1 | Nanak Dev | Founder; Ik Onkar; appointed Angad
  [4:4.1] 2 | Angad Dev | Started / standardised Gurmukhi script
  [4:4.2] 3 | Amar Das | 22 Manjis; strengthened langar
  [4:4.3] 4 | Ram Das | Founded Ramdaspur (Amritsar); land from Akbar; holy tank
  [4:4.4] 5 | Arjan Dev (1581-1606) | Compiled Adi Granth (1604); built Harmandir Sahib; first martyr
  [4:4.5] 6 | Hargobind | Militarised the Panth; Miri-Piri; Akal Takht (1609); Gwalior Fort; battle of Kartarpur
  [4:4.6] 7 | Har Rai | Herbal medicine, service
  [4:4.7] 8 | Har Krishan | Child (youngest) Guru
  [4:4.8] 9 | Tegh Bahadur | Born Tyag Mal; founded Anandpur Sahib (Chak Nanki); 116 hymns; executed 1675
  [4:4.9] 10 | Gobind Singh | Born Patna 1666; Khalsa 1699; Five Ks; Guruship to the Granth
[4:5] > Sikh = disciple; Nanak (Talwandi 1469) → Angad (Gurmukhi) → Amar Das (Manjis) → Ram Das (Amritsar) → Arjan (Adi Granth).
[4:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 5 p.3; PMF Modern History Part 1 p.74; PMF Art & Culture Part 5 p.70-71; Rocket Indian History 02 (Rocket Sheet #59-60); old app notes row 149
## SUB 5 | Amritsar, the Golden Temple and Sacred Places
[5:0] TABLE head: Place | Link
  [5:0.0] Amritsar (first Ramdaspur) | City and tank founded by Guru Ram Das on land granted by Akbar
  [5:0.1] Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple) | Built / completed by Guru Arjan Dev (1604), who installed the Adi Granth; gold covering by Ranjit Singh
  [5:0.2] Akal Takht, Amritsar | Guru Hargobind, 1609 – temporal seat
  [5:0.3] Anandpur Sahib (Rupnagar) | Founded by Tegh Bahadur; Khalsa born here 1699 (Keshgarh Sahib); Hola Mohalla
  [5:0.4] Patna Sahib | Birthplace of Gobind Singh
  [5:0.5] Damdama Sahib (Talwandi Sabo, Bathinda) | Final version of the Guru Granth Sahib (1705); Baisakhi fair
  [5:0.6] Hazur Sahib, Nanded (Godavari, Maharashtra) | Death / cremation place of Guru Gobind Singh (1708) – Takht Sachkhand Sri Hazur Abchalnagar Sahib
  [5:0.7] Sri Muktsar Sahib | Maghi Mela (Chali Mukte)
[5:1] - **Amritsar was established by Guru Ram Das**; **Golden Temple completed by Guru Arjan Dev**.
[5:2] - **Nanded is sacred because of Guru Gobind Singh** (stabbed by an Afghan, died there 1708) – not Amar Das, Angad or Arjan.
[5:3] > Ram Das = city; Arjan = temple; Hargobind = Akal Takht; Nanded = Gobind Singh's samadhi.

## SUB 6 | Guru Granth Sahib
[6:0] - **Adi Granth compiled by Guru Arjan (5th) in 1604**; **final version by Guru Gobind Singh at Damdama Sahib (1705)**; declared the **eternal Guru** (1708).
[6:1] - Script **Gurmukhi**; language "Sant Bhasha" (mix of Punjabi, Hindi and dialects).
[6:2] - Contains hymns of six Gurus (incl. **116 hymns of Tegh Bahadur**) and of non-Sikh saints: **Kabir**, Sufi **Sheikh (Baba) Farid**, **Namdev**, **Ravidas** (**41 shabdas**), Jaidev.
[6:3] - Sikh marriage **Anand Karaj** = four circumambulations of the Granth while **Lavan** hymns are sung.
[6:4] > Arjan 1604 (Adi Granth) → Gobind Singh 1705 (final) → eternal Guru 1708.
[6:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Art & Culture Part 5 p.70, p.72; old app notes row 149
## SUB 7 | Banda Bahadur and the Sikh Misls
[7:0] - **Original name Lachhman Dev**; Guru Gobind Singh named him **Banda** and sent him north from **Nanded (1708)**.
[7:1] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [7:1.0] 1709 | Seized Samana
  [7:1.1] 1710 | Defeated Wazir Khan at Chappar Chiri; conquered Sirhind → first sovereign Sikh state, capital Lohgarh (Sirhind Fateh Diwas, 12-14 May)
  [7:1.2] 1715 | Eight-month siege of Gurdas Nangal; captured by Farrukhsiyar's forces
  [7:1.3] 1716 | Executed at Delhi by Farrukhsiyar for refusing to convert
[7:2] - Abolished zamindari, gave farmers ownership of land; **coins in the names of Guru Nanak and Guru Gobind Singh**.
[7:3] - After him: Bandai and Tat Khalsa split (healed 1721, Bhai Mani Singh); **Dal Khalsa** (Kapur Singh) = combined force; Khalsa rule between the **Sutlej and Jamuna**.
[7:4] - **12 misls** (misl = "equal"; democratic military brotherhoods): **Bhangi, Ahluwalia, Ramgarhia, Kanhaiya, Sukerchakia, Phulkian** … – Kanphata were Nath yogis, not a misl.
[7:5] > Banda: Lachhman Dev → Sirhind 1710 (Lohgarh) → executed 1716 by Farrukhsiyar; 12 misls → Ranjit Singh (Sukerchakia).
[7:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Lens May 2026 p.3 (Banda Singh Bahadur, PIB); Spectrum Modern India p.145-146; PMF Modern History Part 1 p.74-75; old app notes row 149
