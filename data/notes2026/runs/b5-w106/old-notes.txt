=== NOTES 5:106 | Environmental Hazards and Disaster Management – Dm Act 2005, Ndma and Ndrf, Sendai Framework, Disaster Types and Mitigation
## SUB 0 | Mitigation, Safety and First Aid
[106:0:0] # Mitigation by hazard
[106:0:1] TABLE head: Hazard | Mitigation
  [106:0:1.0] Earthquake | Earthquake-resistant construction; reframing building codes and enforcing them (IS 1893, NBC)
  [106:0:1.1] Cyclone | Planting trees (shelter belts, mangroves) along the coast; cyclone shelters; IMD warnings
  [106:0:1.2] Flood | Desilting/dredging, afforestation, check dams, diversion levees, decongesting channels, depopulating flood plains – not "deindustrialisation"; basements worsen flood damage
  [106:0:1.3] Landslide | Regulate settlements; retaining walls; hazard zonation
  [106:0:1.4] Drought | Water conservation, check dams
[106:0:2] - **Strict enforcement of building codes** = a mitigation strategy; **tsunamis** are especially hard to mitigate.
[106:0:3] - **CWC flood bulletins**: **Orange** = severe – above danger level but **below HFL** (incl. within 0.5 m of it); **Red** = extreme – HFL reached or crossed.
[106:0:4] # Earthquake-safe buildings
[106:0:5] - Plan: **simple rectangular** (symmetric) – not star, oval or circular; RCC with **small windows and openings away from pillars**; door/window openings **small and centrally located**.
[106:0:6] - Roofs: a **pyramidal (hipped)** roof reduces wind force; heavy roofs are bad.
[106:0:7] - **Quake Safe guidelines** (APPSC key): buildings with **fire-fighting equipment in working order**.
[106:0:8] - After a quake use a **battery torch** (gas leaks – no candles or flames); outdoors stay **in an open field away from buildings**, trees, bridges.
[106:0:9] - **Seismic Vulnerability Assessment of Building Types in India** (NDMA, 2013): building-type catalogue and vulnerability functions – coordinated by **IIT Bombay**.
[106:0:10] - **CFLOWS-Chennai** (Coastal Flood Warning System app, 2019): developed by **IIT Bombay** with NCCR (MoES).
[106:0:11] - **Occupational hazard identification and risk analysis** standards: **Bureau of Indian Standards** (IS 15656).
[106:0:12] - **Stampede-free fair**: proper crowd control with police at various places.
[106:0:13] # First aid
[106:0:14] - Action plan **DRABC** = Danger, Response, **Airway**, Breathing, Circulation; **ABC** of life support: **A = Airway**.
[106:0:15] - Golden rule: **Be calm – don't panic**.
[106:0:16] > Mitigation PYQs are matching "hazard → measure"; earthquake-design items come from APPSC AEE/technical papers.
[106:0:17] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Disaster Management p.4, p.26, p.30-31, p.43; NextGen Rocket Disaster Management p.28 (CWC); web: cwc.gov.in flood-forecasting page (search result); app notes
[106:0:18] - **Seismic zoning** is an earthquake measure, not a cyclone measure; hazard zonation mapping = landslides; Flood Plain Zoning = non-structural.
[106:0:19] - **Aims of first aid (3 Ps):** **preserve life**, **prevent the condition from worsening**, **promote recovery** → "all the above".
[106:0:20] - **Triage colours:** **Red** = immediate (life-threatening), **Yellow** = delayed (serious, not life-threatening), **Green** = minor (walking wounded), **Black** = dead or expectant (pain relief only until death).
[106:0:21] - **Hurricane/cyclone preparedness:** secure outdoor furniture, board up windows, keep a communication plan, move valuables to **higher ground**. Leaving them in a **low-lying area** is the least effective step.
[106:0:22] - **USA:** volcanoes cause the **fewest** annual deaths among natural hazards (tornadoes, heat, winter weather kill more).
[106:0:23] - **Landslide-prone regions:** Himalayas (most), Western Ghats, North-East – not the Deccan or Chambal valley.
## SUB 1 | Global Frameworks – Yokohama, Hyogo, Sendai, UNDRR and DRR Days
[106:1:0] # From IDNDR to Sendai
[106:1:1] TABLE head: Framework / event | Year / period | Key fact
  [106:1:1.0] IDNDR (International Decade for Natural Disaster Reduction) | 1990–1999 | Proclaimed by the UNGA in 1989; first concerted UN effort; prompted India's DM Cell
  [106:1:1.1] Rio Earth Summit | 1992 | Earlier than all the DRR frameworks
  [106:1:1.2] Yokohama Strategy (1st WCDRR – World Conference on Natural Disaster Reduction) | 23–27 May 1994, Yokohama | First global guidelines on prevention, preparedness, mitigation
  [106:1:1.3] UNISDR | 1999 | Secretariat for the International Strategy for Disaster Reduction
  [106:1:1.4] Hyogo Framework for Action (2nd WCDRR, Kobe) | 2005–2015 | 5 priorities; aim: reduce disaster losses in lives and social, economic, environmental assets
  [106:1:1.5] Sendai Framework (3rd WCDRR, Sendai, Japan) | 2015–2030 | Adopted 18 March 2015; 4 priorities, 7 targets; voluntary, non-binding
  [106:1:1.6] Global Platform for DRR | May 2017 | Held at Cancun, Mexico
  [106:1:1.7] UNISDR → UNDRR | 1 May 2019 | Geneva; custodian of Sendai; convenes the Global Platform
[106:1:2] - **Chronology:** Rio (1992) → Yokohama (1994) → Hyogo (2005) → Sendai (2015). **All three WCDRRs were hosted by Japan.**
[106:1:3] - **UNISDR** = **United Nations International Strategy for Disaster Reduction**; **DRR** = **Disaster Risk Reduction**.
[106:1:4] - Statement trap (APPSC FRO 2022): states met at Yokohama in May 1994 – true; "the decade 1994–2004 was declared IDNDR at Yokohama" – **false** (IDNDR = 1990s, proclaimed 1989).
[106:1:5] - **Post-2015 frameworks** with a bearing on India's DRR: **Sendai, SDGs (2015–2030), Paris Agreement** – all three; **Hyogo is NOT** one of them.
[106:1:6] # Sendai – four priorities and seven targets
[106:1:7] - Priorities: **1** Understanding disaster risk; **2** Strengthening disaster risk governance; **3** Investing in DRR for resilience; **4** Enhancing preparedness for effective response and **"Build Back Better"**.
[106:1:8] TABLE head: Target | Aim (by 2030 unless stated)
  [106:1:8.0] A | Reduce global disaster mortality
  [106:1:8.1] B | Reduce number of affected people
  [106:1:8.2] C | Reduce direct economic loss in relation to global GDP
  [106:1:8.3] D | Reduce damage to critical infrastructure and disruption of basic services
  [106:1:8.4] E | Increase countries with national and local DRR strategies – by 2020
  [106:1:8.5] F | Enhance international cooperation to developing countries
  [106:1:8.6] G | Access to multi-hazard early warning systems and risk information
[106:1:9] - Principles: States have the primary role; all-of-society; multi-hazard (natural + man-made); people-centred.

## SUB 2 | Concepts – Hazard, Vulnerability, Risk and Classification
[106:2:0] # Key terms
[106:2:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [106:2:1.0] Hazard | A circumstance/event that poses potential harm or danger
  [106:2:1.1] Disaster | When a hazard meets vulnerability and causes serious harm beyond coping capacity
  [106:2:1.2] Vulnerability | Susceptibility to harm – physical, social, economic, environmental
  [106:2:1.3] Risk | Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability / Capacity – infinite capacity → zero risk
  [106:2:1.4] Vulnerability profile | Assessment of the susceptibility of a community or system to hazards
  [106:2:1.5] Hazard mapping | Mapping areas at risk of various hazards
[106:2:2] - "**All disasters are hazards but all hazards are not disasters**" – taught as **true** (a hazard becomes a disaster only when it strikes a vulnerable community).
[106:2:3] - Disaster = hazard + **exposure** + **vulnerability** (+ location); **social status** alone is not a factor forming a disaster (APPSC key). **Gales** = a **hazard characteristic** (APPSC key).
[106:2:4] - **Disaster Crunch (Pressure-and-Release) Model**: vulnerability is **rooted in socio-economic processes**.
[106:2:5] - **DRR works best when it addresses the underlying causes of vulnerability**; objective of DM = **reduce loss of life and property**.
[106:2:6] - **Environmental vulnerability** = natural-resource depletion and degradation; **environmental degradation** magnifies drought and can trigger natural disasters (it is not itself a natural geological disaster).
[106:2:7] - Rising vulnerability comes from population growth, climate change, pollution – **not "stagnation in S&T"**. Development and disaster deaths are **inversely** related.
[106:2:8] - Most vulnerable: people in an **old city with narrow roads**; women and children.
[106:2:9] - **GIS**: manages large spatial data for **vulnerability and hazard assessment**.
[106:2:10] # Classification
[106:2:11] TABLE head: Class | Examples
  [106:2:11.0] Geophysical / geological | Earthquake, tsunami, landslide, volcano, avalanche
  [106:2:11.1] Hydrological | Flood, flash flood
  [106:2:11.2] Meteorological | Cyclone, tornado, thunderstorm, hailstorm
  [106:2:11.3] Climatological | Drought, heat wave, cold wave
  [106:2:11.4] Biological | Epidemics, pest attack, food poisoning (not hailstorm)
  [106:2:11.5] Man-made | Industrial, chemical, nuclear explosion, fire, transport, war
[106:2:12] - **Natural** disasters arise from environmental/geological phenomena; **man-made** from technological failure or human activity. **Urban flooding** = **mostly man-made**.
[106:2:13] - Floods: melting snow, lack of vegetation, broken dams – a **biological disaster does not cause flooding**.
[106:2:14] - Tsunami causes: undersea earthquake, underwater landslide, underwater volcano (and big explosions) – **underwater mining explosions** are the "not a cause" option.
[106:2:15] - Socio-natural hazards: floods worsened by deforestation; emerging hazards: cyber attacks, space weather.
[106:2:16] > Definition and "NOT a" items dominate; the classification table answers tsunami/landslide (geophysical) and hailstorm (not biological).
[106:2:17] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Disaster Management p.10, p.40, p.47-48, p.57, p.68; PMF Internal Security & DM Part 2 p.42-43; app notes
[106:2:18] - **Mitigation (pre-disaster):** embankments, mangroves, land-use zoning; response = rescue and relief; recovery = rehabilitation and reconstruction.
[106:2:19] - **Resilience:** ability of a community to resist, absorb and recover quickly from a hazard; **capacity** = strengths and resources that reduce risk.
[106:2:20] - **Man-made subtypes:** technological, transport, and **social or complex** (terrorism, riots, war).
[106:2:21] - **Risk assessment dimensions:** hazard, vulnerability, exposure and **capacity** (Sendai Priority 1).
[106:2:22] - **Vulnerable groups:** age-based (children, elderly), gender-based, disability, socio-economic, location-based.
[106:2:23] - **Biological warfare in history:** European colonisers spread **smallpox-laden blankets and kerchiefs** among Native Americans (Fort Pitt, 1763).
[106:2:24] - **Why vulnerability rises:** poverty, **inadequate access to resources**, **low awareness**, **blind faith in beliefs and customs**, unplanned growth. **Spread of irrigation** is not a cause, since it reduces drought risk.
## SUB 3 | DM Act 2005, NDMA, NDRF and the National Set-up
[106:3:0] # The Act and the plans
[106:3:1] - **DM Act 2005**: Presidential assent **23 December 2005**; Act No. 53; **11 chapters, 79 sections**; triggered by the **2004 tsunami** (and Bhuj 2001). Sec 3 NDMA, Sec 8 NEC, Sec 14 SDMA, Sec 25 DDMA, **Sec 42 NIDM**, **Sec 44 NDRF**, Sec 46 NDRF (fund), Sec 47 NDMF, Sec 48 SDRF.
[106:3:2] - **NDMA**: first set up by executive order in **2005**; formally **notified 27 September 2006** under the Act – most keys answer "**2005**" for "when was NDMA formed/constituted".
[106:3:3] - **NDMA functions (Sec 6)**: lays down **policies**, **approves the National Plan**, **approves plans of Union Ministries/Departments**, lays down guidelines for **State Authorities** (so "does not interfere with State Plans" is wrong).
[106:3:4] - **National Plan**: prepared by the **NEC**, **approved by NDMA**. **First NDMP 2016** – document dated **May 2016**, **released by the PM on 1 June 2016**; revised 2019. Its response part lists **18** broad activities.
[106:3:5] - **National Policy on DM (NPDM)**: approved **22 October 2009**; approach **holistic, pro-active**, multi-disaster, technology-driven – **from relief-centric to prevention, mitigation, preparedness**.
[106:3:6] - DM Act gives **PRIs and ULBs** a legal role; the **2025 amendment** adds Urban DM Authorities and statutory status to NCMC and HLC.
[106:3:7] # Who chairs what
[106:3:8] TABLE head: Body | Chair
  [106:3:8.0] NDMA | Prime Minister (ex-officio); up to 9 members
  [106:3:8.1] NEC | Union Home Secretary
  [106:3:8.2] NCMC (National Crisis Management Committee) | Cabinet Secretary
  [106:3:8.3] NPDRR; High Level Committee (fund approvals) | Union Home Minister
  [106:3:8.4] CCS | PM – nuclear, biological, chemical emergencies
  [106:3:8.5] SDMA / State Crisis Management Committee | Chief Minister / Chief Secretary
  [106:3:8.6] DDMA | District Collector / DM
[106:3:9] - **Primary responsibility for disaster management lies with the State Government**; the Centre (MHA) supports with resources, NDRF and funds.
[106:3:10] - **Nodal ministry: Ministry of Home Affairs** (shifted from Agriculture in **2002**, after Bhuj); MHA's DG Fire Services, Civil Defence & Home Guards gives **technical advice on fire protection, prevention and legislation** (fire = State subject).

[106:3:13] # NDRF and other institutions
[106:3:14] - **NDRF**: raised **2006** (Sec 44) with **8 battalions**; **12 by 2017**; **16 now** (as of 2026), drawn from CAPFs; ~1,149 per battalion; motto "Aapada Seva Sadaiv Sarvatra"; Academy **Nagpur**; regional HQ for AP–Telangana–Karnataka **Vijayawada**.
[106:3:15] - **National Civil Defence College**: founded **29 April 1957** at **Nagpur** (as Central Emergency Relief Training Institute); National Fire Service College also at Nagpur.
[106:3:16] - **Mock exercises** are conducted regularly by **NDMA** (with NIDM, NDRF and States). **Aapda Mitra** trains community volunteers as first responders.
[106:3:17] - **First Indian Disaster Management Congress**: **29 November 2006**. **SACHET**: NDMA alert system on the Common Alerting Protocol (C-DOT).
[106:3:18] - **Funds:** SDRF = primary State fund (Centre 75:25 general, 90:10 special category); NDRF fund for severe calamities; NDMF/SDMF for mitigation (after 15th FC). 11th FC → National Calamity Contingency Fund; 13th FC → NDRF/SDRF.
[106:3:19] - **Notified disasters** (SDRF/NDRF aid): **12** – cyclone, drought, earthquake, fire, flood, tsunami, hailstorm, landslide, avalanche, cloudburst, pest attack, frost & cold wave – **14 since Aug 2026** with **heatwave and lightning** (Finance Commission advice, MHA notification); no man-made disaster is notified.
[106:3:20] > NDRF battalions is a moving number: 8 (2006) → 12 (2017) → 16 (now). NCMC = Cabinet Secretary, not Home Minister.

## SUB 8 | Heat Waves, Cold Waves and Community DRR
[106:8:0] - **Heat wave**: March–June; IMD forecasts; NDMA heat-wave action-plan guidelines (updated 2019); Heat Action Plans by States/cities; cool roofs; adjusted working hours = administrative measure.
[106:8:1] - **Cold wave**: November–March; NDMA Guidelines on Cold Wave and Frost **2021**; cold day = max departure −4.5°C to −6.4°C.
[106:8:2] - **Community DRR**: community = first responder; NDMA Disability-inclusive DRR guidelines **2019**; School Safety; gender mainstreaming (10-Point Agenda); 20 Model GPs in PRIs; social media complements, not replaces, official systems.
[106:8:3] - 2004 tsunami: up to **four times as many women as men** died in some areas.
[106:8:4] - Tsunami: Tsunami Ready villages – 24 in Odisha (UNESCO-IOC); ITEWC at INCOIS Hyderabad. ISRO's DMS programme and NDEM support all hazards.
[106:8:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Disaster Management p.68-73; app notes
[106:8:6] - **Social media** complements official warning systems – it does not replace them.

## SUB 1 | Genetics and DNA – Basics and Discoverers
[108:1:0] # DNA, genes and heredity (asked ~20 times)
[108:1:1] - **Genetics** = study of genes, **heredity and variation** (not only Mendel's laws or DNA structure).
[108:1:2] - **Gene** = the **unit of heredity** (a DNA segment coding for a trait); **DNA** controls hereditary characters; **chromosome** and nucleus only carry it.
[108:1:3] - **Nucleic acids (DNA, RNA)**: **storage and expression of genetic information** – not oxygen transport, energy or body temperature.
[108:1:4] - **DNA bases: adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine**; **RNA has uracil in place of thymine** (A and G occur in both).
[108:1:5] - **RNA** is usually **single-stranded** (a "single helix"); DNA is a **double helix**.
[108:1:6] - DNA occurs in the **nucleus** and also in **mitochondria and chloroplasts** (they have their own DNA).
[108:1:7] - **Histones** = basic proteins in the **nucleus**; DNA wraps around them (nucleosomes, chromatin).
[108:1:8] - DNA can store and pass on information because its two strands are **complementary** (A–T, G–C): each strand is a template (UPSC 2001).
[108:1:9] - **Mutation** = change in the **base sequence** of a gene (transversion is only one kind of substitution).
[108:1:10] - **Phenotype** = observable traits; **genotype** = genetic make-up.
[108:1:11] - **Genetic code**: triplet codons for **20 amino acids**; non-overlapping, non-ambiguous, commaless; has **start and stop codons**; "universal" – nearly so (a few mitochondrial exceptions).
[108:1:12] - **Gene mapping** (position of genes on a chromosome) helps trace **livestock pedigree** and breed **disease-resistant animals**; it cannot explain "all human diseases" (UPSC 2011 → I and III).
[108:1:13] # Discoverers

## SUB 3 | DNA Fingerprinting
[108:3:0] # Basis and uses (asked 15 times)
[108:3:1] - **Developed by Alec Jeffreys (1984)**; in India pioneered by **Lalji Singh** (CCMB) – father of Indian DNA fingerprinting.
[108:3:2] - **Basis: DNA polymorphism** – highly variable repetitive regions (**VNTRs**); most DNA is the same in all people. Not the double helix, replication or "errors in base sequence".
[108:3:3] - Tissues of the same person show the **same pattern**; polymorphisms are **inherited** (child gets half from each parent) → **paternity**.
[108:3:4] - **Technique: Southern blotting** of DNA fragments (ELISA, RIA = antigen–antibody tests; Northern blot = RNA).
[108:3:5] - **Uses:** identity, **paternity** (not protein analysis, chromosome counting, gene therapy, cloning or rDNA), **forensic** cases, **rape and assault** (trace hair, saliva, dried semen are enough – A and R true, R explains A), **conservation** – "all of the above"; WWF-India's anti-poaching tool for **rhinos** (RhODIS – rhino DNA index).
[108:3:6] - **Samples:** blood, bone, hair **with root**, saliva, skin, semen – all usable; **burnt bones** lose usable DNA. **Biochips** = a technique, not a sample.
[108:3:7] - **First crime solved:** **England** – the murders of **1983** and 1986 (solved 1987–88).
[108:3:8] - **CDFD** (Centre for DNA Fingerprinting and Diagnostics): **Hyderabad**, under DBT.

## SUB 6 | Bioremediation – Meaning and Methods
[108:6:0] - **Bioremediation**: removal or degradation of pollutants by **organisms or their enzymes**; covers soil, water, air – oil spills, heavy metals, pesticides, solvents; engineered microbes may be used. **Slower but cheaper** than conventional (thermal, chemical) clean-up, no secondary pollution; results vary with pollutant and site.
[108:6:1] - Not bioremediation: burning/incineration (thermal), **biomagnification** (DDT, mercury rising up food chains), **eutrophication** (nutrient-driven algal blooms), biofortification.
[108:6:2] TABLE head: Basis | In-situ | Ex-situ
  [108:6:2.0] Site | At the site, no excavation; passive, cheapest, slow | Excavated, treated elsewhere
  [108:6:2.1] Techniques | Bioventing, biosparging, bioaugmentation | Landfarming, composting, biopiles, bioreactors (bioreactors fastest)
[108:6:3] # Techniques
[108:6:4] TABLE head: Technique | How / target
  [108:6:4.0] Bioaugmentation | Adds chosen degrading microbes – oil spills
  [108:6:4.1] Biostimulation | Adds N, P, oxygen to native microbes – polluted lakes
  [108:6:4.2] Bioventing | Air/nutrients via wells into soil
  [108:6:4.3] Biosparging | Air injected below the water table – groundwater
  [108:6:4.4] Mycoremediation | Fungi – organic pollutants
  [108:6:4.5] Rhizofiltration | Roots absorb/precipitate contaminants from water
  [108:6:4.6] Rhizoremediation | Roots stimulate rhizosphere microbes
[108:6:5] # Phytoremediation and hyperaccumulators
[108:6:6] TABLE head: Sub-type / plant | Fact
  [108:6:6.0] Phytoextraction | Stored in shoots, harvested – water hyacinth (heavy metals)

## SUB 7 | Tools, Omics and Identification Techniques
[108:7:0] TABLE head: Tool | Use
  [108:7:0.0] PCR | Amplifies DNA; key enzyme DNA polymerase; denaturation → annealing → extension
  [108:7:0.1] Gel electrophoresis | Separates DNA by size
  [108:7:0.2] Restriction enzymes | Cut DNA ("molecular scissors") – EcoRI, BamHI, HindIII
  [108:7:0.3] Ligase | Only joins DNA

[108:7:1] - **Genome**: complete genetic information; zygote genome = DNA of **two gametes**; **human genome ≈ 3 billion bases**, over 99% shared.
[108:7:2] - **DNA sequencing** finds the base order (A, G, C, T); genetic information depends on the order of bases (A and R true, R explains A).
[108:7:3] - **Transcriptome**: the **full range of mRNA** molecules expressed by an organism; **varies with conditions**, unlike the genome (UPSC 2016).
[108:7:4] - **Microsatellite DNA** (short tandem repeats): **evolutionary relationships** among species, genetic diversity (UPSC 2023).
[108:7:5] - **DNA barcoding**: **species identification** from a short standard gene region (animals: mitochondrial **COI**) – distinguishes **look-alike species** and detects **undesirable material in processed food**; it **cannot tell age** (UPSC 2022 → B and C).
[108:7:6] - **Serological tests** (antigen–antibody reactions): **identification** – blood groups, pathogens and serotypes (**Widal, ELISA**); also used in serotaxonomy.

[108:7:8] - **Human Genome Project** (1990–2003): **identification and mapping of all human genes** and sequencing of the human genome.

## SUB 10 | Nature and Scope of Biotechnology
[108:10:0] - **Biotechnology** = **industrial use of living organisms**, substances from them or their processes (e.g. yeast for liquor) (IAS 1993); CBD (1992): use of **biological systems** to **make or modify products**.
[108:10:1] - Traditional biotech: **curd**, bread, wine, vinegar; modern: rDNA, PCR, cloning, CRISPR. **Earliest biotechnologists: farmers**.
[108:10:2] - Applications: agriculture (GM crops), medicine (drugs, vaccines, diagnostics, gene therapy), industry (microbial products).
[108:10:3] TABLE head: Colour | Field
  [108:10:3.0] Red | Medical
  [108:10:3.1] Green | Agricultural
  [108:10:3.2] White | Industrial – enzymes, biofuels, bioplastics (NOT marine)
  [108:10:3.3] Blue | Marine
[108:10:4] - **Enzymes** = biological catalysts of reactions in living systems (not bacteria or DNA).
[108:10:5] - **Artificial enzymes (synzymes)** are made by **genetic/protein engineering** and by **synthesis of host molecules such as crown ethers** and cyclodextrins; **denaturing** proteins destroys enzyme activity (AEE 2019 → a and b).
[108:10:6] - **Bionics** = using properties of biological systems in the mechanical world; **bionomics** = organism and environment; **bionomy** = laws of life; **biometry** = statistics of biology.

## SUB 0 | Fertilisers, Plant Nutrients, Manures and Soil Health
[109:0:0] # Plant nutrients
[109:0:1] TABLE head: Group | Elements
  [109:0:1.0] Macronutrients (9) | C, H, O, N, P, K (primary), Ca, Mg, S (secondary)
  [109:0:1.1] Micronutrients (8) | Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo, Cl, Ni – trace amounts (< 1% of dry weight)
[109:0:2] - **Magnesium is a macronutrient** (secondary), not a micronutrient; **manganese and zinc are micronutrients** supplied by the soil.
[109:0:3] - Inorganic fertilisers supply the macronutrients **nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium (NPK)**; desirable soil NPK use ratio in India **4:2:1**.
[109:0:4] - **Zinc deficiency in maize = "white bud" / white tip of young leaves**; **Khaira** in rice is also zinc deficiency.
[109:0:5] - **Excess nitrogen delays maturity** (lush vegetative growth, lodging); phosphorus **hastens** maturity.
[109:0:6] # Fertiliser facts
[109:0:7] TABLE head: Fertiliser | Fact
  [109:0:7.0] Urea CO(NH2)2 | ~46% N – highest of common solid N fertilisers; nitrogen in amide form; 100/46 ≈ 2.2 kg urea gives 1 kg N; most popular for foliar spray (high solubility)
  [109:0:7.1] Ammonium nitrate / CAN / ammonium sulphate | 34% / 26% / 20.6% N
  [109:0:7.2] DAP (NH4)2HPO4 | 18% N, 46% P2O5 – balanced N + P supply
  [109:0:7.3] Superphosphate | Calcium dihydrogen phosphate (+ gypsum); acidic, so continued use lowers soil pH (< 7)
  [109:0:7.4] Potassic (MOP, SOP) | Disease and drought resistance, quality
  [109:0:7.5] Phosphatic | Root development, flowering, fruiting
  [109:0:7.6] Neem-coated urea | Slows dissolution of urea and nitrification → slow N release, higher N-use efficiency
[109:0:8] - Urea → ammoniacal (NH4+) → nitrate in soil; **ammoniacal N is preferred for paddy and potato** (held by soil, less lost in flooded fields).
[109:0:9] - Nitrogen is the main fertiliser for **wheat** (vegetative growth).
[109:0:10] - **Nitrogen-use efficiency** is raised by **split application, nitrification inhibitors and slow-release N fertilisers** (all of these).
[109:0:11] - **Balanced fertilisation** raises production, improves fertiliser-use efficiency and maintains soil productivity (all of these); legumes need little N, so their NPK is P- and K-heavy (exam key **1:2:2**).
[109:0:12] - Excess N: **soil acidification, nitrate leaching, eutrophication**; continuous chemical use: **lower soil organic matter**, fewer microbes, lower nutrient density in food.
[109:0:13] - Broadcasting granular fertiliser loses **50-70%** of the nitrogen; **PM-PRANAM**: States keep **50%** of the subsidy saved, as grants.

[109:0:16] # Organic manures and amendments
[109:0:17] TABLE head: Input | Meaning
  [109:0:17.0] Animal manure (FYM) | Dung, urine and litter of farm animals
  [109:0:17.1] Compost | Decomposed plant and animal waste – raises organic matter and microbial activity
  [109:0:17.2] Crop residue | Remains of harvested crops worked back into the soil
  [109:0:17.3] Green manure | Fresh crops (mostly legumes) ploughed in green – sunhemp (most suitable), dhaincha, sesbania, cowpea
  [109:0:17.4] Vermicompost | Organic waste converted into rich manure by earthworms (Eisenia fetida)

[109:0:20] - **Humus** = dark **organic colloid** from decayed plant and animal matter.
[109:0:21] - AP plans **1,500 MT/day** of manure from **107 waste-processing centres** (Feb 2026); Kharif 2026 green-manure subsidy Rs 62.20 crore, **sunn hemp 50,311 quintals**.

## SUB 1 | Biofertilisers
[109:1:0] - **Biofertilisers** = living microbial inoculants (bacteria, algae, fungi) that **fix nitrogen, solubilise phosphorus** or decompose organic matter; they are **applied to seed/soil** (not only leaves), **do not alter soil chemistry** like chemicals, and **can be used with organic manures**.
[109:1:1] TABLE head: Biofertiliser | Type | Crop / role
  [109:1:1.0] Rhizobium | Symbiotic bacterium | Legumes (pea, pulses) – root nodules fix N2
  [109:1:1.1] Azotobacter | Free-living aerobic bacterium | Cereals – non-symbiotic N fixation
  [109:1:1.2] Azospirillum | Associative bacterium | Cereal roots (rice rhizosphere), no nodules
  [109:1:1.3] Blue-green algae (Anabaena, Nostoc) | Cyanobacteria | Paddy – fix atmospheric N into a form crops absorb (heterocysts)
  [109:1:1.4] Azolla | Aquatic fern with the BGA Anabaena | Water-logged rice fields; 2-3% N; often used with BGA
  [109:1:1.5] PSB | Bacteria | Solubilise phosphate
  [109:1:1.6] VAM / Mycorrhiza | Fungus–root association | Phosphatic biofertiliser (P uptake)
[109:1:2] - **Mycorrhiza (UPSC 2013 – all three):** increases **absorptive area / drought resistance**, helps plants **tolerate extreme pH**, and **resists disease** – hence used to rehabilitate degraded sites.
[109:1:3] - **Legumes** (pea) enrich soil with nitrogen; wheat, coffee, rubber, potato, sorghum do not.
[109:1:4] - Rhizobium, BGA and Azolla are **all** biofertilisers; **alfalfa** is a legume fodder, not a biofertiliser.
[109:1:5] - Not biofertilisers: Salvinia, Marsilea, Pteridium (ferns), Wolffia, Trapa, Lemna (other aquatic plants).
[109:1:6] > Exam angle: BGA → paddy (×5), Azolla = fern + Anabaena (×4), VAM = phosphatic, mycorrhiza statements, biofertiliser statements (CDS 2010: I and IV).

## SUB 2 | Sustainable Agriculture and Environmental Impact of Modern Farming
[109:2:0] - **Sustainable agriculture:** produce food while **protecting the environment and conserving resources for future generations**; maintains soil fertility; may use synthetic inputs **judiciously** (unlike organic farming).
[109:2:1] TABLE head: Sustainable (exam answers) | Not sustainable
  [109:2:1.0] Crop rotation, organic farming | Excessive chemical fertilisers, overuse of pesticides
  [109:2:1.1] Multi-, inter-, mixed and contour cropping, green manure | Intense mono-cropping / monoculture
  [109:2:1.2] IPM, drip irrigation, reduced tillage | Over-irrigation
[109:2:2] - **Eco-friendly technology in agriculture = organic farming** (NextGen PYQ); GM crops, pesticide spraying and chemical fertilisers are not.
[109:2:3] - **Environmental impact (APPSC 2022, 2023, G2 Mains 2025 – I and II):** synthetic fertilisers/pesticides **pollute soil and water**; sustainable farming **prioritises soil health and biodiversity**. Modern agriculture **does** deplete freshwater, and monoculture **harms** ecosystem stability.
[109:2:4] - Most conspicuous effects of modern agriculture: **soil degradation, pollution and ecological imbalance – all of these**; indiscriminate agro-chemicals cause **secondary pest outbreaks, pesticide resistance** and ecosystem contamination.
[109:2:5] - **Rachel Carson, *Silent Spring* (1962):** pesticides (DDT) harming wildlife and the environment → a founding text of **environmental protection**.
[109:2:6] - **GACSA** (Global Alliance for Climate-Smart Agriculture): launched at the UN Climate Summit, New York, **2014**; facilitated by **FAO**; voluntary – **membership creates no binding obligations** (UPSC 2018: 2 only).
[109:2:7] - Agricultural science covers crop and soil cultivation and animal products; **rainwater harvesting** is a water-conservation technique (APPSC ACF key: NOT part of agricultural science technology).
[109:2:8] - **Carbosulfan** – Centre proposed a ban (29 Aug 2026) under the **Insecticides Act, 1968**: breaks down to highly toxic **carbofuran**, harms birds and aquatic life.
[109:2:9] - **Evergreen revolution** (M.S. Swaminathan): ZBNF, organic farming, water conservation, IPM.

## SUB 5 | Natural Farming – ZBNF, APCNF and NMNF
[109:5:0] # Zero Budget Natural Farming
[109:5:1] - **ZBNF** was developed by **Subhash Palekar** (Maharashtra; Padma Shri 2016) in the mid-1990s as an alternative to Green Revolution inputs; spread first through **Karnataka** (farmers' movement; general knowledge); in Union Budget 2019-20.
[109:5:2] - **Chemical fertilisers are not used** – on-farm **cow dung, urine, jaggery** preparations replace them; Palekar **opposes vermicompost**.
[109:5:3] TABLE head: Input / practice | Meaning
  [109:5:3.0] Jeevamrutha | Fermented microbial culture from cow dung and urine
  [109:5:3.1] Beejamrutha | Seed treatment
  [109:5:3.2] Achhadana | Mulching
  [109:5:3.3] Whapasa | Soil aeration and moisture
  [109:5:3.4] Agniastra, Brahmastra, Neemastra | Pest-management preparations
[109:5:4] - ZBNF promoted through **farmer collectives / organic clusters** (village clusters, SHGs).
[109:5:5] - **Natural vs organic:** natural farming uses on-farm inputs, avoids ploughing and bulk manures, is near zero-cost and uncertified; organic farming uses approved (often bought) inputs and is certified (PGS/NPOP).
[109:5:6] - **Permaculture:** Bill Mollison and David Holmgren; **Fukuoka**: *One-Straw Revolution* (1975).

## SUB 6 | Integrated Pest Management, Pests and Plant Diseases
[109:6:0] - **IPM (FAO):** use **all suitable techniques** to keep pest populations **below the economic injury level**; aim is to **optimise, not maximise**, control.
[109:6:1] - IPM = **combination of biological, cultural and mechanical methods**, chemicals only when needed (last resort); key element: **cultural control**.
[109:6:2] TABLE head: Component | Examples

  [109:6:2.3] Chemical | Selective pesticides only when needed
[109:6:3] - **ICAR (2017): pests waste about 30-35% of India's annual crop yield.**
