## SUB 0 | Global Frameworks – Yokohama, Hyogo, Sendai, UNDRR and DRR Days
[0:0] # From IDNDR to Sendai
[0:1] TABLE head: Framework / event | Year / period | Key fact
  [0:1.0] IDNDR (International Decade for Natural Disaster Reduction) | 1990–1999 | Proclaimed by the UNGA in 1989; first concerted UN effort; prompted India's DM Cell
  [0:1.1] Rio Earth Summit | 1992 | Earlier than all the DRR frameworks
  [0:1.2] Yokohama Strategy (1st WCDRR – World Conference on Natural Disaster Reduction) | 23–27 May 1994, Yokohama | First global guidelines on prevention, preparedness, mitigation
  [0:1.3] UNISDR | 1999 | Secretariat for the International Strategy for Disaster Reduction
  [0:1.4] Hyogo Framework for Action (2nd WCDRR, Kobe) | 2005–2015 | 5 priorities; aim: reduce disaster losses in lives and social, economic, environmental assets
  [0:1.5] Sendai Framework (3rd WCDRR, Sendai, Japan) | 2015–2030 | Adopted 18 March 2015; 4 priorities, 7 targets; voluntary, non-binding
  [0:1.6] Global Platform for DRR | May 2017 | Held at Cancun, Mexico
  [0:1.7] UNISDR → UNDRR | 1 May 2019 | Geneva; custodian of Sendai; convenes the Global Platform
[0:2] - **Chronology:** Rio (1992) → Yokohama (1994) → Hyogo (2005) → Sendai (2015). **All three WCDRRs were hosted by Japan.**
[0:3] - **UNISDR** = **United Nations International Strategy for Disaster Reduction**; **DRR** = **Disaster Risk Reduction**.
[0:4] - Statement trap (APPSC FRO 2022): states met at Yokohama in May 1994 – true; "the decade 1994–2004 was declared IDNDR at Yokohama" – **false** (IDNDR = 1990s, proclaimed 1989).
[0:5] - **Post-2015 frameworks** with a bearing on India's DRR: **Sendai, SDGs (2015–2030), Paris Agreement** – all three; **Hyogo is NOT** one of them.
[0:6] # Sendai – four priorities and seven targets
[0:7] - Priorities: **1** Understanding disaster risk; **2** Strengthening disaster risk governance; **3** Investing in DRR for resilience; **4** Enhancing preparedness for effective response and **"Build Back Better"**.
[0:8] TABLE head: Target | Aim (by 2030 unless stated)
  [0:8.0] A | Reduce global disaster mortality
  [0:8.1] B | Reduce number of affected people
  [0:8.2] C | Reduce direct economic loss in relation to global GDP
  [0:8.3] D | Reduce damage to critical infrastructure and disruption of basic services
  [0:8.4] E | Increase countries with national and local DRR strategies – by 2020
  [0:8.5] F | Enhance international cooperation to developing countries
  [0:8.6] G | Access to multi-hazard early warning systems and risk information
[0:9] - Principles: States have the primary role; all-of-society; multi-hazard (natural + man-made); people-centred.

## SUB 1 | DM Act 2005, NDMA, NDRF and the National Set-up
[1:0] # The Act and the plans
[1:1] - **DM Act 2005**: Presidential assent **23 December 2005**; Act No. 53; **11 chapters, 79 sections**; triggered by the **2004 tsunami** (and Bhuj 2001). Sec 3 NDMA, Sec 8 NEC, Sec 14 SDMA, Sec 25 DDMA, **Sec 42 NIDM**, **Sec 44 NDRF**, Sec 46 NDRF (fund), Sec 47 NDMF, Sec 48 SDRF.
[1:2] - **NDMA**: first set up by executive order in **2005**; formally **notified 27 September 2006** under the Act – most keys answer "**2005**" for "when was NDMA formed/constituted".
[1:3] - **NDMA functions (Sec 6)**: lays down **policies**, **approves the National Plan**, **approves plans of Union Ministries/Departments**, lays down guidelines for **State Authorities** (so "does not interfere with State Plans" is wrong).
[1:4] - **National Plan**: prepared by the **NEC**, **approved by NDMA**. **First NDMP 2016** – document dated **May 2016**, **released by the PM on 1 June 2016**; revised 2019. Its response part lists **18** broad activities.
[1:5] - **National Policy on DM (NPDM)**: approved **22 October 2009**; approach **holistic, pro-active**, multi-disaster, technology-driven – **from relief-centric to prevention, mitigation, preparedness**.
[1:6] - DM Act gives **PRIs and ULBs** a legal role; the **2025 amendment** adds Urban DM Authorities and statutory status to NCMC and HLC.
[1:7] # Who chairs what

  [1:8.2] NCMC (National Crisis Management Committee) | Cabinet Secretary
  [1:8.3] NPDRR; High Level Committee (fund approvals) | Union Home Minister
  [1:8.4] CCS | PM – nuclear, biological, chemical emergencies
  [1:8.5] SDMA / State Crisis Management Committee | Chief Minister / Chief Secretary
  [1:8.6] DDMA | District Collector / DM
[1:9] - **Primary responsibility for disaster management lies with the State Government**; the Centre (MHA) supports with resources, NDRF and funds.
[1:10] - **Nodal ministry: Ministry of Home Affairs** (shifted from Agriculture in **2002**, after Bhuj); MHA's DG Fire Services, Civil Defence & Home Guards gives **technical advice on fire protection, prevention and legislation** (fire = State subject).

[1:13] # NDRF and other institutions
[1:14] - **NDRF**: raised **2006** (Sec 44) with **8 battalions**; **12 by 2017**; **16 now** (as of 2026), drawn from CAPFs; ~1,149 per battalion; motto "Aapada Seva Sadaiv Sarvatra"; Academy **Nagpur**; regional HQ for AP–Telangana–Karnataka **Vijayawada**.
[1:15] - **National Civil Defence College**: founded **29 April 1957** at **Nagpur** (as Central Emergency Relief Training Institute); National Fire Service College also at Nagpur.
[1:16] - **Mock exercises** are conducted regularly by **NDMA** (with NIDM, NDRF and States). **Aapda Mitra** trains community volunteers as first responders.
[1:17] - **First Indian Disaster Management Congress**: **29 November 2006**. **SACHET**: NDMA alert system on the Common Alerting Protocol (C-DOT).
[1:18] - **Funds:** SDRF = primary State fund (Centre 75:25 general, 90:10 special category); NDRF fund for severe calamities; NDMF/SDMF for mitigation (after 15th FC). 11th FC → National Calamity Contingency Fund; 13th FC → NDRF/SDRF.
[1:19] - **Notified disasters** (SDRF/NDRF aid): **12** – cyclone, drought, earthquake, fire, flood, tsunami, hailstorm, landslide, avalanche, cloudburst, pest attack, frost & cold wave – **14 since Aug 2026** with **heatwave and lightning** (Finance Commission advice, MHA notification); no man-made disaster is notified.

## SUB 2 | Concepts – Hazard, Vulnerability, Risk and Classification
[2:0] # Key terms
[2:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [2:1.0] Hazard | A circumstance/event that poses potential harm or danger
  [2:1.1] Disaster | When a hazard meets vulnerability and causes serious harm beyond coping capacity
  [2:1.2] Vulnerability | Susceptibility to harm – physical, social, economic, environmental
  [2:1.3] Risk | Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability / Capacity – infinite capacity → zero risk
  [2:1.4] Vulnerability profile | Assessment of the susceptibility of a community or system to hazards
  [2:1.5] Hazard mapping | Mapping areas at risk of various hazards
[2:2] - "**All disasters are hazards but all hazards are not disasters**" – taught as **true** (a hazard becomes a disaster only when it strikes a vulnerable community).
[2:3] - Disaster = hazard + **exposure** + **vulnerability** (+ location); **social status** alone is not a factor forming a disaster (APPSC key). **Gales** = a **hazard characteristic** (APPSC key).
[2:4] - **Disaster Crunch (Pressure-and-Release) Model**: vulnerability is **rooted in socio-economic processes**.
[2:5] - **DRR works best when it addresses the underlying causes of vulnerability**; objective of DM = **reduce loss of life and property**.
[2:6] - **Environmental vulnerability** = natural-resource depletion and degradation; **environmental degradation** magnifies drought and can trigger natural disasters (it is not itself a natural geological disaster).
[2:7] - Rising vulnerability comes from population growth, climate change, pollution – **not "stagnation in S&T"**. Development and disaster deaths are **inversely** related.
[2:8] - Most vulnerable: people in an **old city with narrow roads**; women and children.
[2:9] - **GIS**: manages large spatial data for **vulnerability and hazard assessment**.
[2:10] # Classification
[2:11] TABLE head: Class | Examples
  [2:11.0] Geophysical / geological | Earthquake, tsunami, landslide, volcano, avalanche
  [2:11.1] Hydrological | Flood, flash flood
  [2:11.2] Meteorological | Cyclone, tornado, thunderstorm, hailstorm
  [2:11.3] Climatological | Drought, heat wave, cold wave
  [2:11.4] Biological | Epidemics, pest attack, food poisoning (not hailstorm)
  [2:11.5] Man-made | Industrial, chemical, nuclear explosion, fire, transport, war
[2:12] - **Natural** disasters arise from environmental/geological phenomena; **man-made** from technological failure or human activity. **Urban flooding** = **mostly man-made**.
[2:13] - Floods: melting snow, lack of vegetation, broken dams – a **biological disaster does not cause flooding**.
[2:14] - Tsunami causes: undersea earthquake, underwater landslide, underwater volcano (and big explosions) – **underwater mining explosions** are the "not a cause" option.
[2:15] - Socio-natural hazards: floods worsened by deforestation; emerging hazards: cyber attacks, space weather.
[2:16] > Definition and "NOT a" items dominate; the classification table answers tsunami/landslide (geophysical) and hailstorm (not biological).
[2:17] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Disaster Management p.10, p.40, p.47-48, p.57, p.68; PMF Internal Security & DM Part 2 p.42-43; app notes
## SUB 3 | Mitigation, Safety and First Aid
[3:0] # Mitigation by hazard
[3:1] TABLE head: Hazard | Mitigation
  [3:1.0] Earthquake | Earthquake-resistant construction; reframing building codes and enforcing them (IS 1893, NBC)
  [3:1.1] Cyclone | Planting trees (shelter belts, mangroves) along the coast; cyclone shelters; IMD warnings
  [3:1.2] Flood | Desilting/dredging, afforestation, check dams, diversion levees, decongesting channels, depopulating flood plains – not "deindustrialisation"; basements worsen flood damage
  [3:1.3] Landslide | Regulate settlements; retaining walls; hazard zonation
  [3:1.4] Drought | Water conservation, check dams
[3:2] - **Strict enforcement of building codes** = a mitigation strategy; **tsunamis** are especially hard to mitigate.
[3:3] - **CWC flood bulletins**: **Orange** = severe – above danger level but **below HFL** (incl. within 0.5 m of it); **Red** = extreme – HFL reached or crossed.
[3:4] # Earthquake-safe buildings
[3:5] - Plan: **simple rectangular** (symmetric) – not star, oval or circular; RCC with **small windows and openings away from pillars**; door/window openings **small and centrally located**.
[3:6] - Roofs: a **pyramidal (hipped)** roof reduces wind force; heavy roofs are bad.
[3:7] - **Quake Safe guidelines** (APPSC key): buildings with **fire-fighting equipment in working order**.
[3:8] - After a quake use a **battery torch** (gas leaks – no candles or flames); outdoors stay **in an open field away from buildings**, trees, bridges.
[3:9] - **Seismic Vulnerability Assessment of Building Types in India** (NDMA, 2013): building-type catalogue and vulnerability functions – coordinated by **IIT Bombay**.
[3:10] - **CFLOWS-Chennai** (Coastal Flood Warning System app, 2019): developed by **IIT Bombay** with NCCR (MoES).
[3:11] - **Occupational hazard identification and risk analysis** standards: **Bureau of Indian Standards** (IS 15656).
[3:12] - **Stampede-free fair**: proper crowd control with police at various places.
[3:13] # First aid
[3:14] - Action plan **DRABC** = Danger, Response, **Airway**, Breathing, Circulation; **ABC** of life support: **A = Airway**.
[3:15] - Golden rule: **Be calm – don't panic**.
[3:16] > Mitigation PYQs are matching "hazard → measure"; earthquake-design items come from APPSC AEE/technical papers.

[4:3] - **Rail-accident prevention**: replace over-aged tracks and bridges, fire sensing in coaches, road over/under bridges – **watershed structures are not** a rail measure.
[4:4] - **Nuclear explosion is not a natural disaster** (CBRN; CCS handles NBC emergencies).

## SUB 8 | Heat Waves, Cold Waves and Community DRR
[8:0] - **Heat wave**: March–June; IMD forecasts; NDMA heat-wave action-plan guidelines (updated 2019); Heat Action Plans by States/cities; cool roofs; adjusted working hours = administrative measure.
[8:1] - **Cold wave**: November–March; NDMA Guidelines on Cold Wave and Frost **2021**; cold day = max departure −4.5°C to −6.4°C.
[8:2] - **Community DRR**: community = first responder; NDMA Disability-inclusive DRR guidelines **2019**; School Safety; gender mainstreaming (10-Point Agenda); 20 Model GPs in PRIs; social media complements, not replaces, official systems.
[8:3] - 2004 tsunami: up to **four times as many women as men** died in some areas.
[8:4] - Tsunami: Tsunami Ready villages – 24 in Odisha (UNESCO-IOC); ITEWC at INCOIS Hyderabad. ISRO's DMS programme and NDEM support all hazards.
