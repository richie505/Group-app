=== Fertilisers, Plant Nutrients, Manures and Soil Health
# Plant nutrients
| Group | Elements |
|---|---|
| Macronutrients (9) | C, H, O, **N, P, K** (primary), **Ca, Mg, S** (secondary) |
| Micronutrients (8) | **Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu**, B, Mo, Cl, Ni – trace amounts (< 1% of dry weight) |
- **Magnesium is a macronutrient** (secondary), not a micronutrient; **manganese and zinc are micronutrients** supplied by the soil.
- Inorganic fertilisers supply the macronutrients **nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium (NPK)**; desirable soil NPK use ratio in India **4:2:1**.
- **Zinc deficiency in maize = "white bud" / white tip of young leaves**; **Khaira** in rice is also zinc deficiency.
- **Excess nitrogen delays maturity** (lush vegetative growth, lodging); phosphorus **hastens** maturity.
# Fertiliser facts
| Fertiliser | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Urea** CO(NH2)2 | **~46% N – highest of common solid N fertilisers**; nitrogen in **amide** form; **100/46 ≈ 2.2 kg urea gives 1 kg N**; most popular for **foliar spray** (high solubility) |
| Ammonium nitrate / CAN / ammonium sulphate | 34% / 26% / 20.6% N |
| **DAP** (NH4)2HPO4 | **18% N, 46% P2O5** – balanced N + P supply |
| Superphosphate | **Calcium dihydrogen phosphate** (+ gypsum); acidic, so continued use **lowers soil pH (< 7)** |
| Potassic (MOP, SOP) | Disease and drought resistance, quality |
| Phosphatic | Root development, flowering, fruiting |
| **Neem-coated urea** | **Slows dissolution of urea and nitrification** → slow N release, higher N-use efficiency |
- Urea → ammoniacal (NH4+) → nitrate in soil; **ammoniacal N is preferred for paddy and potato** (held by soil, less lost in flooded fields).
- Nitrogen is the main fertiliser for **wheat** (vegetative growth).
- **Nitrogen-use efficiency** is raised by **split application, nitrification inhibitors and slow-release N fertilisers** (all of these).
- **Balanced fertilisation** raises production, improves fertiliser-use efficiency and maintains soil productivity (all of these); legumes need little N, so their NPK is P- and K-heavy (exam key **1:2:2**).
- Excess N: **soil acidification, nitrate leaching, eutrophication**; continuous chemical use: **lower soil organic matter**, fewer microbes, lower nutrient density in food.
- Broadcasting granular fertiliser loses **50-70%** of the nitrogen; **PM-PRANAM**: States keep **50%** of the subsidy saved, as grants.

# Organic manures and amendments
| Input | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Animal manure** (FYM) | Dung, urine and litter of farm animals |
| **Compost** | Decomposed plant and animal waste – raises organic matter and microbial activity |
| **Crop residue** | Remains of harvested crops worked back into the soil |
| **Green manure** | Fresh crops (mostly **legumes**) ploughed in green – **sunhemp (most suitable)**, dhaincha, sesbania, cowpea |
| **Vermicompost** | Organic waste converted into rich manure by **earthworms** (Eisenia fetida) |
| **Biochar** | Charcoal from **pyrolysis** of biomass (low oxygen) |
- **Biochar (UPSC 2020 – all three):** used in the **growing medium of vertical farming**, **promotes nitrogen-fixing and mycorrhizal microbes**, and **retains water** longer; also cuts leaching and acidity and stores carbon for millennia.
- **Earthworms** improve soil fertility – **"farmers' friends"**; an earthworm has **more than two pairs** of "hearts" (aortic arches – 4 pairs in Pheretima).
- **Humus** = dark **organic colloid** from decayed plant and animal matter.
- AP plans **1,500 MT/day** of manure from **107 waste-processing centres** (Feb 2026); Kharif 2026 green-manure subsidy Rs 62.20 crore, **sunn hemp 50,311 quintals**.
> Exam angle: urea 46% N / amide / 2.2 kg, DAP 18-46, micronutrient pickers (Mn, Zn; Mg is not), biochar statements (UPSC 2020, ×3), sunhemp, organic-amendment match, superphosphate lowers pH, SHC 19 Feb 2015.

=== Biofertilisers
- **Biofertilisers** = living microbial inoculants (bacteria, algae, fungi) that **fix nitrogen, solubilise phosphorus** or decompose organic matter; they are **applied to seed/soil** (not only leaves), **do not alter soil chemistry** like chemicals, and **can be used with organic manures**.
| Biofertiliser | Type | Crop / role |
|---|---|---|
| **Rhizobium** | Symbiotic bacterium | **Legumes** (pea, pulses) – root nodules fix N2 |
| **Azotobacter** | Free-living aerobic bacterium | Cereals – non-symbiotic N fixation |
| Azospirillum | Associative bacterium | Cereal roots (rice rhizosphere), no nodules |
| **Blue-green algae** (Anabaena, Nostoc) | Cyanobacteria | **Paddy** – fix atmospheric N into a form crops absorb (heterocysts) |
| **Azolla** | **Aquatic fern** with the **BGA Anabaena** | **Water-logged rice fields**; 2-3% N; often used with BGA |
| PSB | Bacteria | Solubilise phosphate |
| **VAM / Mycorrhiza** | **Fungus**–root association | **Phosphatic biofertiliser** (P uptake) |
- **Mycorrhiza (UPSC 2013 – all three):** increases **absorptive area / drought resistance**, helps plants **tolerate extreme pH**, and **resists disease** – hence used to rehabilitate degraded sites.
- **Legumes** (pea) enrich soil with nitrogen; wheat, coffee, rubber, potato, sorghum do not.
- Rhizobium, BGA and Azolla are **all** biofertilisers; **alfalfa** is a legume fodder, not a biofertiliser.
- Not biofertilisers: Salvinia, Marsilea, Pteridium (ferns), Wolffia, Trapa, Lemna (other aquatic plants).
> Exam angle: BGA → paddy (×5), Azolla = fern + Anabaena (×4), VAM = phosphatic, mycorrhiza statements, biofertiliser statements (CDS 2010: I and IV).

=== Sustainable Agriculture and Environmental Impact of Modern Farming
- **Sustainable agriculture:** produce food while **protecting the environment and conserving resources for future generations**; maintains soil fertility; may use synthetic inputs **judiciously** (unlike organic farming).
| Sustainable (exam answers) | Not sustainable |
|---|---|
| **Crop rotation**, organic farming | Excessive chemical fertilisers, overuse of pesticides |
| Multi-, inter-, mixed and **contour cropping**, green manure | **Intense mono-cropping / monoculture** |
| IPM, drip irrigation, reduced tillage | **Over-irrigation** |
- **Eco-friendly technology in agriculture = organic farming** (NextGen PYQ); GM crops, pesticide spraying and chemical fertilisers are not.
- **Environmental impact (APPSC 2022, 2023, G2 Mains 2025 – I and II):** synthetic fertilisers/pesticides **pollute soil and water**; sustainable farming **prioritises soil health and biodiversity**. Modern agriculture **does** deplete freshwater, and monoculture **harms** ecosystem stability.
- Most conspicuous effects of modern agriculture: **soil degradation, pollution and ecological imbalance – all of these**; indiscriminate agro-chemicals cause **secondary pest outbreaks, pesticide resistance** and ecosystem contamination.
- **Rachel Carson, *Silent Spring* (1962):** pesticides (DDT) harming wildlife and the environment → a founding text of **environmental protection**.
- **GACSA** (Global Alliance for Climate-Smart Agriculture): launched at the UN Climate Summit, New York, **2014**; facilitated by **FAO**; voluntary – **membership creates no binding obligations** (UPSC 2018: 2 only).
- Agricultural science covers crop and soil cultivation and animal products; **rainwater harvesting** is a water-conservation technique (APPSC ACF key: NOT part of agricultural science technology).
- **Carbosulfan** – Centre proposed a ban (29 Aug 2026) under the **Insecticides Act, 1968**: breaks down to highly toxic **carbofuran**, harms birds and aquatic life.
- **Evergreen revolution** (M.S. Swaminathan): ZBNF, organic farming, water conservation, IPM.
> Exam angle: statement pairs on agri impact (×3, same question), sustainable-practice pickers (×5), eco-friendly technology = organic farming, GACSA, Silent Spring.

=== Organic Farming – Sikkim, Schemes and Certification
- **Organic farming** avoids synthetic fertilisers, pesticides, growth regulators and feed additives; relies on crop rotation, residues, manures, legumes, green manure, biofertilisers and biological pest control.
- Main advantage: **reduced chemical input and improved soil health** – not guaranteed higher yields (yield dip of 2-3 years in transition).
# Sikkim
- **Sikkim = India's first fully organic State (January 2016)** and the **world's first 100% organic State**; **FAO Future Policy (Gold) Award 2018**; Lakshadweep = first organic UT.
- NE States pursuing organic status: **Sikkim, Meghalaya, Nagaland**; **Assam is not one of them**.
# Schemes and certification
| Scheme / body | Fact |
|---|---|
| **PKVY** (Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana) | 2015; organic farming by **cluster approach (50 farmers / 50 acres)** + **PGS certification**; AP's organic initiative among PMFBY/NFSM/RKVY options; portal **jaivikkheti.in** |
| MOVCD-NER | Organic value chains in the **North-East** (NPOP certification, exports) |
| **NPOP** | Since **2001**, under the **Ministry of Commerce and Industry** (not Rural Development); **APEDA** is the secretariat/implementing agency; third-party certification for **exports** |
| **PGS-India** | **Participatory Guarantee System** – locally relevant, **producers and consumers participate**, **outside third-party certification**; for the **domestic** market |
| **NCOF** | National Centre of Organic Farming, **Ghaziabad** (UP) – PGS secretariat |
| Large Area Certification | Under PKVY; Car Nicobar–Nancowry islands certified |
- **Padma Shri 2019** for organic farming: **Venkateswara Rao Yadlapalli** (Andhra Pradesh).
- Rocket CA 2026: India's first **Basmati organic demo farm** – Tanda Bijaisi village, Pilibhit (UP).

=== Natural Farming – ZBNF, APCNF and NMNF
# Zero Budget Natural Farming
- **ZBNF** was developed by **Subhash Palekar** (Maharashtra; Padma Shri 2016) in the mid-1990s as an alternative to Green Revolution inputs; spread first through **Karnataka** (farmers' movement; general knowledge); in Union Budget 2019-20.
- **Chemical fertilisers are not used** – on-farm **cow dung, urine, jaggery** preparations replace them; Palekar **opposes vermicompost**.
| Input / practice | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Jeevamrutha** | Fermented microbial culture from cow dung and urine |
| **Beejamrutha** | Seed treatment |
| **Achhadana** | Mulching |
| **Whapasa** | Soil aeration and moisture |
| Agniastra, Brahmastra, Neemastra | Pest-management preparations |
- ZBNF promoted through **farmer collectives / organic clusters** (village clusters, SHGs).
- **Natural vs organic:** natural farming uses on-farm inputs, avoids ploughing and bulk manures, is near zero-cost and uncertified; organic farming uses approved (often bought) inputs and is certified (PGS/NPOP).
- **Permaculture:** Bill Mollison and David Holmgren; **Fukuoka**: *One-Straw Revolution* (1975).
# Andhra Pradesh – APCNF
- **APCNF = Andhra Pradesh Community-managed Natural Farming** (formerly ZBNF); launched **2016** by **Rythu Sadhikara Samstha (RySS)** under the AP Agriculture Department.
- **2018 declared AP's Year of Natural Farming.**
- Reach (Dec 2024): **9.53 lakh farmers on 3.58 lakh ha**; 14% of farmers, 28% of villages; target **41 lakh farmers, 19 lakh ha by 2028-29**; all gram panchayats by 2026-27; technical support to 12 States.
- Awards: **Gulbenkian Prize for Humanity 2024** (11 July 2024, Lisbon); **Food Planet Prize 2026** (USD 1.5 million, Curt Bergfors Foundation, Sweden).
- Kurnool study: APCNF farmers about **35% less likely to use pesticides**; Kenyan delegation praised the Guntur model (July 2026); **NTR district** QR-based traceability.
# National Mission on Natural Farming (NMNF)
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Approved | **25 Nov 2024**; standalone CSS |
| Outlay | **Rs 2,481 crore** (Centre 1,584 + States 897) |

| Field workers | **Krishi Sakhis** (70,021) |
- **BPKP** (Bharatiya Prakritik Krishi Paddhati, 2020-21): sub-scheme of PKVY for natural farming; natural farming cuts cultivation cost by **20-40%**.

=== Integrated Pest Management, Pests and Plant Diseases
- **IPM (FAO):** use **all suitable techniques** to keep pest populations **below the economic injury level**; aim is to **optimise, not maximise**, control.
- IPM = **combination of biological, cultural and mechanical methods**, chemicals only when needed (last resort); key element: **cultural control**.
| Component | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Cultural** | Crop rotation, resistant varieties, changed irrigation, trap crops |
| Mechanical / physical | Traps, hand collection, mulches |
| Biological | Predators, parasites, pathogens (**green lacewing → aphids**, ladybugs), NPV, pheromone traps |
| Chemical | Selective pesticides only when needed |
- **ICAR (2017): pests waste about 30-35% of India's annual crop yield.**
# Plant diseases and vectors
- Plant pathogens are spread by **birds, dust, rain and wind** (all four).
| Crop | Disease |
|---|---|
| **Bajra** | **Green ear** (downy mildew), **ergot** – "green hair/ear" is **not** a rice disease |
| Pea | Powdery mildew |
| Gram | Wilt |
| **Okra** | **Yellow vein mosaic** – virus spread by **whitefly** |
> Exam angle: IPM aim (below economic injury level), IPM combination, cultural control, ICAR pest loss 30-35%, crop–disease mismatch.

=== Biopesticides – Neem, Bt and Microbial Agents
- **Biopesticides** are biodegradable, low in toxicity and short-lived; a key part of IPM.
| Biopesticide | Source | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Bt** | Bacterium ***Bacillus thuringiensis*** – **microbial/biological insecticide** | Caterpillars, bollworms |
| **Neem** | **Azadirachtin** (antifeedant) | Wide range of insects and mites |
| NPV | Baculovirus | Helicoverpa, Spodoptera |
| Trichoderma | Fungus | Soil-borne wilt, root rot |
| Beauveria bassiana | Entomopathogenic fungus | Borers, whitefly |
- **Neem tree (UPSC 1999):** industrial source of **biopesticide and anti-fertility compound**.
- **Neem statements (UPSC 2014): 1 and 3 only** – neem oil is a pesticide against insects and mites and has pharmaceutical uses; "neem seeds used to make biofuels and hospital detergents" is **not correct** (UPSC official key).
- Other Bacillus species (B. subtilis, B. polymyxa, B. brevis) are not insecticides.
> Exam angle: Bt = biological insecticide (×2), neem statements (×2), neem uses.
