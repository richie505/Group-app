## SUB 0 | Share in GVA, Growth and Jobs – the Mismatch
[0:0] # Share of output
[0:1] - **Services = more than 50% of GVA** (Economic Survey 2025-26) – the **highest** contributor to GDP (WBCS 2017: "more than **50%**").
[0:2] TABLE head: Sector (2021-22) | Share of GDP | Share of workforce
  [0:2.0] Secondary | 24.29% | 22.24%
  [0:2.1] Tertiary | 53.89% | 33.80%
[0:3] - **Employment-output paradox** (**NITI Aayog**): services give over half of output but only about **one-third** of jobs; high-end services (IT, finance) need high skills, so most new jobs are in low-value trade, hospitality, transport.
[0:4] - **Regular wages: 51.5%** of service workers vs about 1% in agriculture.
[0:5] # Share of growth
[0:6] - **Contribution of services to GVA growth, 2011-12 to 2015-16: about 69%** (CGPSC 2017 key); in 2015-16 alone **66.1%** (Economic Survey 2015-16); 2017-18 about 72.5%.
[0:7] - Growth: **9.1%** in FY2025-26 (FAE; new series 8.9%); **10%** in Q1 FY2026-27, led by finance, real estate, IT and professional services (**12.1%**).
[0:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 14 p.4-5; Economic Survey 2015-16 Vol.2 ch.7 (services 66.1% of GVA growth); Economic Survey 2025-26; app notes 3:2 sub 1

## SUB 2 | Services Exports, FDI and Digital Services
[2:0] # Exports
[2:1] - India's share of world services trade: **2% (2005) → 4.3% (2024)**; older target: **4.2% of world services exports by 2022**.
[2:2] - **Digitally delivered services exports:** India's share of the world total about **6% in 2023** (Economic Survey 2023-24).
[2:3] - Rank: **8th**-largest services exporter (WTO, April 2025 data); also 8th in 2014.
[2:4] - 2024-25: exports **USD 387.5 bn** vs imports USD 198.7 bn → surplus; **software and business services = 77.8%** of services exports.
[2:5] - Target: services exports **USD 1 trillion** within the USD 2 trillion export goal by **2030**.
[2:6] # FDI
[2:7] - **1997-2000:** largest FDI share – **services sector** in most books' keys (UPSC 2001; some keys say engineering).
[2:8] - **Last decade (to 2006):** services sector attracted the highest FDI among chemicals, food processing and telecom; April 2000-December 2025 cumulative: **services 16.38%**, computer software and hardware 15.63%, trading, telecom, automobiles.
[2:9] - FY2024-25: **services sector** again the **top** FDI equity recipient (~19%, about $9.35 bn), ahead of computer software and hardware (16%).
[2:10] - Large foreign investment came into **life insurance, banking and automobiles** (UPPCS 2009).
[2:11] ~~ Sources: Economic Survey 2023-24 ch.9 (digitally delivered services 6%); DPIIT FDI Factsheet Dec 2025; app notes 3:2 sub 3
## SUB 3 | Andhra Pradesh and the States
[3:0] - **AP:** services = **AP's largest sector** – about **43%** of GVA (current prices) (APPSC DL 2020).
[3:1] - Yet AP is **basically an agricultural economy** (APPSC Panchayat Secretary): agriculture is its main employer, so the success of its Five Year Plans depended mainly on **agriculture** (APPSC G2 2011).
[3:2] - AP 2025-26: services **Rs 3,59,931 crore (+10.67%)**; **communications** fastest (**15.13%**).
[3:3] - **Villaments** (villa + apartment) = new urban-housing trend in AP real estate (APPSC G2 Mains 2019).
[3:4] - Kerala **64.3%**, **Bihar 58.7%** of GSVA from services; Karnataka, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Telangana = about **40%** of India's services output.
[3:5] - Largest services share in State GDP, 2008-09: **Delhi** > Chandigarh > Kerala > Maharashtra; 2023-24 (NITI Aayog, Oct 2025): Chandigarh 88.8%, Delhi 84.1%.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 (GVA by sector); NITI Aayog "India's Services Sector" (Oct 2025); app notes 3:2 sub 6
## SUB 4 | Service-led Growth and the 1991 Reforms
[4:0] - Boom began in the **mid-1980s**; sped up after the **1990s reforms** – services gained most from globalisation.
[4:1] - The 1991 reforms **did not bring much benefit to agriculture** (public investment in agriculture fell; APPSC ASO 2017) – unlike services, stock markets and power.
[4:2] - **Software** is a major export, but India **lacks a strong hardware base** (UPSC 1999: A true, R false).
[4:3] - **EAC-PM Chairman** (Nov 2025): service-led growth cannot last without a **manufacturing base**; manufacturing creates demand for logistics, IT, finance and steadies GDP cycles.
[4:4] - **Paradox of thrift** – popularised by **Keynes**.
[4:5] ~~ Sources: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development ch.3 (reforms and agriculture); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 14 p.9; app notes 3:2 sub 2
