=== Micro Basics – Demand, Utility, Elasticity and Consumer Choice
# Definitions and laws
- **Lionel Robbins** (1932): economics studies the relationship between **unlimited wants (ends) and scarce means** with alternative uses (scarcity definition).
- **Behavioural economics** applies **psychological insights** into human behaviour to explain economic decisions (Kahneman, Thaler).
- **Saturation point:** total utility is at its maximum and **marginal utility is zero** (beyond it MU turns negative).
- **Consumer equilibrium (two goods):** MUx/Px = MUy/Py – if the MU of the last unit of X is twice that of Y, equilibrium needs the **price of X to be twice the price of Y**.
- **Budget line:** a point **below (inside)** the budget line costs less than income – the consumer is **not spending all income**; a point on the line spends it all.
# Demand
- **Normal goods:** demand rises with income (positive income effect); **inferior goods:** demand falls as income rises.
- The demand curve **slopes downward** and is usually drawn **convex to the origin**.
- **Demand for a good rises** when the **price of a substitute rises**; it falls when the price of a complement rises or (for an inferior good) when income rises; a fall in its own price raises the quantity demanded (UPSC key: **1 and 4 only**).
- **Supply curve of a farmer:** everything is held constant **except the price of the commodity itself** (that is the variable on the curve).
# Elasticity
| Case | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Zero price elasticity | Quantity demanded does **not change at all** whatever the price change (vertical demand curve) |
| Zero cross elasticity | Purchases of one good do not change when the other's price changes – **unrelated (independent) goods** |
| Positive / negative cross elasticity | Substitutes / complements |
| Tangent demand curves | A straight-line and a curved demand curve **tangent at a point have the same elasticity** there |
- **Consumer surplus** (Marshall) – what consumers would pay minus what they pay; used in **tax policy** (burden of commodity taxes), pricing of public utilities.

=== Micro Basics – Production, Costs and Factor Pricing
# Production
- **Law of diminishing returns:** adding more units of a variable factor to a fixed factor → **marginal product eventually diminishes**.
- **Constant returns to scale** need **all inputs** raised in the same proportion; raising **labour alone by 10%** with capital fixed raises output by **less than 10%** (diminishing returns).
- **CES production function:** the **elasticity of substitution** between inputs is **constant** (Cobb-Douglas is the special case with elasticity 1).
# Costs
| Cost | Meaning / example |
|---|---|
| Explicit cost | Money actually paid to buy or hire factors of production |
| Implicit / opportunity cost | Return forgone on own resources – e.g. the **interest he could earn by lending out his own funds** |
| Fixed cost (farm) | Does not vary with output – **land rent**, depreciation, interest on fixed capital (seeds, fertiliser, hired labour are variable) |
| AFC | Falls continuously (rectangular hyperbola) – the **only cost curve that is not U-shaped** (AVC, ATC, MC are U-shaped) |
- **LAC falls as output expands** because of **economies of scale**.
- **Increasing cost industry:** factor prices rise as the industry uses more factors (prices and quantities move in the **same direction**).
- **Cash** = notes, coins, bank balances and cash equivalents; long-term **investments** are not cash.
# Factor pricing
- **Value of marginal product (VMP) of labour** = **marginal product of labour × price** of output.
- **Quasi-rent** (Marshall) = total revenue − total variable cost = fixed cost + profit → **greater than the firm's total profit**.

=== Micro Basics – Market Structures, Oligopoly and Game Theory
# Competition and monopoly
- **Perfect market:** **large number of buyers and sellers**, homogeneous product, free entry and exit, perfect knowledge.
- **Monopolistic competition and monopoly** share a **downward-sloping demand curve**; in long-run equilibrium the monopolistic competitor **never** produces at the lowest point of LAC (tangency on the falling part → excess capacity).
- A pure monopolist keeps **pure profits in the long run** because of **blocked entry**.
- When a competitive industry becomes a monopoly, part of **consumer surplus** goes to the monopolist as **economic profit** (the rest is deadweight loss).
- **Contestable markets** (Baumol): even a few firms behave competitively **if entry is absolutely free and exit entirely costless**.
# Duopoly models
| Model | Each firm assumes the rival keeps … | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Cournot | Its **quantity (output)** constant | Interdependence not recognised; stable solution |
| Bertrand | Its **price** constant | Price war down to competitive price |
| Edgeworth | Its **price** constant | Price oscillates – no stable solution |
# Game theory
- **Zero-sum game:** one player's gain = the other's loss.
- **Maximin:** choose the strategy that **maximises the minimum gain** (minimax = minimise the maximum loss).

=== Micro Basics – Welfare Economics, General Equilibrium and Linear Programming
# Pareto and general equilibrium
- **Pareto's welfare analysis** relates to the production function and **utility** (no one can be made better off without making someone worse off).
- **General equilibrium of production and exchange** (2 persons, 2 goods): **MRT(xy) = MRS(xy) of A = MRS(xy) of B**.
- **Consumption (exchange) contract curve** = locus of general-equilibrium points of exchange.
- **Grand utility-possibility curve** = locus of Pareto optimality in **both production and consumption**.
- **Social welfare function** needs an **ethical (value) judgement** – Pareto optimality alone cannot rank its points.
- **Productive efficiency** = **factor-price (allocative) efficiency + technical efficiency**.
# Shadow prices and LP
- **Shadow (accounting) prices** = imputed values of costs and benefits, used in public-sector project appraisal as a **sound basis for allocation decisions**.
- In **linear programming** the shadow price = **change in the objective function per unit change in a constraint**; LP assumes linearity, i.e. **constant returns** – increasing returns to scale is **not** an assumption.
