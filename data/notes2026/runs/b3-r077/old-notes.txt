=== NOTES 3:77 | India and the Wto - Agreements, Ministerial Conferences, Public Stockholding, Fisheries Subsidies, Appellate Body Crisis
## SUB 0 | Gatt - the Predecessor
[77:0:0] - **Earlier name of the WTO = GATT** (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade).
[77:0:1] - Signed **30 October 1947** at **Geneva** by **23** founding contracting parties; tariff concessions in force by **30 June 1948** through a "**Protocol of Provisional Application**"; GATT office at Geneva, 1948.
[77:0:2] - Focus: cut **tariffs and quantitative restrictions**; **8 rounds** of negotiations. GATT survives as part of the WTO agreements.
[77:0:3] - India: GATT member since **8 July 1948**; founding member of **both GATT and WTO**.
[77:0:4] GATT founders = 23; "110 countries" is later membership.
[77:0:5] TABLE head: Basis | GATT | WTO
  [77:0:5.0] Set up | 1947 (in force 1948) | 1 January 1995 (not 1994)
  [77:0:5.1] Nature | An agreement | An international organisation
  [77:0:5.2] Coverage | Mainly goods | Goods, services, IPR, investment
## SUB 1 | Uruguay Round and Dunkel Draft
[77:1:0] - **8th GATT round**; launched **September 1986** at **Punta del Este**; concluded **1993**; **123** contracting parties.
[77:1:1] - Took rules beyond goods: **GATS** (services), **TRIPS** (IPR), **TRIMS** (investment). Aims: cut farm subsidies, open services, cover investment and IPR.
[77:1:2] - **Arthur Dunkel**: GATT DG (1980-1993) and chair of the Trade Negotiations Committee. **Dunkel Draft** (Draft Final Act) tabled **20 December 1991**; linked to IPR; seen as the last chance for a breakthrough.
[77:1:3] - Per key: acceptance compulsory for India in all sectors; agriculture - cut in farm subsidies; textiles - did not repeat the **Multi-Fibre Arrangement**.
[77:1:4] - **Marrakesh Agreement** (signed April 1994) created the WTO.
## SUB 2 | Wto Basics
[77:2:0] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [77:2:0.0] Established | 1 January 1995
  [77:2:0.1] HQ | Geneva (ILO Geneva; UN New York; FAO Rome)
  [77:2:0.2] Members | 166 (April 2026); 164 from 29 July 2016 (Afghanistan 164th); Nepal 147th (23 April 2004); Serbia = observer only
  [77:2:0.3] DG | Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (Nigeria) - 7th DG, first woman, appointed Feb 2021, took over March 2021
  [77:2:0.4] Earlier DG | Roberto Azevedo (Brazil), replaced Pascal Lamy on 1 Sep 2013; 6th DG; second term 2017
[77:2:1] - WTO = **third pillar** of the world economy, with the **IMF and World Bank**; "security guard" of world trade; largest intergovernmental body regulating trade.
[77:2:2] - Aims: free trade and resource flows, IPR protection, balanced trade (all correct).
[77:2:3] - WTO **does** have a dispute-settlement system.
[77:2:4] - India: **18th** in merchandise exports, 1.8% share (April 2025).
[77:2:5] # Principles
[77:2:6] TABLE head: Principle | Meaning
  [77:2:6.0] MFN | Advantage given to one partner must go to all WTO partners (non-discrimination among partners)
  [77:2:6.1] National treatment | Imported product, once inside, treated like the domestic product (tax, regulation)
[77:2:7] - US unilateral tariffs (2025-26) violate MFN and bound-rate obligations.

## SUB 4 | Doha Round and Country Groups
[77:4:0] - **Doha Round** (launched at the 4th MC, 2001): "development round"; **central issue agriculture**; also NAMA, services; **still unfinished**.
[77:4:1] TABLE head: Group | Members / focus
  [77:4:1.0] G-33 | 2003 (Doha process); 40+ developing countries incl. India; food security, Special Safeguard Mechanism, special products
  [77:4:1.1] NAMA-11 | Developing countries (incl. India) in Non-Agricultural Market Access talks; seek flexibilities to limit opening in industrial goods
  [77:4:1.2] G-90 | African Group + ACP countries + LDCs
[77:4:2] - **Special Safeguard Mechanism**: lets developing countries raise tariffs briefly against import surges or price falls.
## SUB 5 | Agreement on Agriculture
[77:5:0] - Negotiated in the Uruguay Round; in force **1 January 1995**. **Three pillars: domestic support, market access, export competition** - NOT anti-dumping/compensatory duties.
[77:5:1] # Subsidy boxes
[77:5:2] TABLE head: Box | Covers | Treatment
  [77:5:2.0] Green | Research, disease control, infrastructure, food security, decoupled income support | Not trade-distorting; no limits
  [77:5:2.1] Blue | Direct payments under production-limiting programmes | Trade-distorting but exempt from cuts
  [77:5:2.2] Amber | Price/quantity-linked support: MSP, power, fertiliser, seed, irrigation (measured as AMS) | Trade-distorting; to be reduced
[77:5:3] - Traffic-light idea: green permitted, amber reduce, red forbidden - **AoA has NO red box**; Red, Orange, Yellow boxes are distractors.
[77:5:4] - Per key: the difference between MSP and market price paid directly to farmers = **Blue box**.
[77:5:5] # Mechanics
[77:5:6] TABLE head: Term | Rule
  [77:5:6.0] De minimis | Amber support allowed: 5% of production value (developed), 10% (developing)
  [77:5:6.1] Total AMS cut | 20% over 6 years (developed); 13.3% (developing)
  [77:5:6.2] Blue Box conditions | Payments on fixed areas and yields, or on 85% or less of base production, or fixed number of livestock
  [77:5:6.3] Tariffication | Non-tariff barriers → tariffs; cut 36% over 6 years (developed), 24% over 10 years (developing)
  [77:5:6.4] Export subsidy cut | Developed: 36% by value or 21% by volume over 6 years (from 1986-90 levels)
[77:5:7] - India is a developing country, but the claim that it need not reduce subsidies is **false**.
## SUB 6 | Public Stockholding and the Peace Clause
[77:6:0] - **Peace Clause (Bali MC9, 2013)**: shields developing-country **public stockholding (PSH)** for food security from disputes, even if de minimis or AMS limits are breached, until a permanent solution; limited to existing programmes and traditional staples; requires notification and no harm to others' food security.
[77:6:1] - **India first to invoke it** - for **rice** (2020, for marketing year 2018-19).
[77:6:2] - India's complaint: MSP procurement for the **NFSA** counted as Amber Box on an **outdated historical reference price**.
[77:6:3] - India + **African Group** want a **permanent solution**; not achieved at **MC13** or **MC14** (deadlocked).
## SUB 7 | Fisheries Subsidies and E-commerce
[77:7:0] # Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies (AFS)
[77:7:1] - Adopted **MC12 (June 2022)**; **in force September 2025**.
[77:7:2] - Bans subsidies to **IUU fishing**, unregulated high seas fishing, **overfished stocks** (unless rebuilding).
[77:7:3] - Covers **marine wild capture** and at-sea support; **aquaculture and inland fishing exempt**. Developing countries/LDCs get a 2-year EEZ exemption; a **Fish Fund** helps poorer members.
[77:7:4] - **WTO's first agreement with an environmental-sustainability objective**. TFA and AFS = the **only two new agreements in 30 years**.
[77:7:5] - **India = 123rd** member to deposit its Instrument of Acceptance (**July 2026**).
[77:7:6] - **Phase 2** (overcapacity, overfishing): deferred at MC14 to the **General Council, Geneva**.
[77:7:7] - **AP angle**: AFS rules matter for **AP's marine fisher folk**; Indian fisher bodies want a "**fisher-centric**" approach.
[77:7:8] 123 = Uruguay Round contracting parties AND India's AFS acceptance number.
[77:7:9] # E-commerce
[77:7:10] - **Moratorium** on customs duties on electronic transmissions (since **1998**) **lapsed on 31 March 2026** for the first time; developing countries lose tariff revenue.
[77:7:11] - **E-Commerce Agreement** (plurilateral): **66 members** signed separately. India: plurilaterals (IFD, E-Commerce) may **fragment** the WTO by weakening consensus.
## SUB 8 | Other Wto Agreements and India's Compliance
[77:8:0] TABLE head: Agreement | Subject | India's compliance
  [77:8:0.0] TRIPS (1995) | Minimum IPR standards | Patents Act amended 2005 (product patents); GI Act 1999; PPV&FR Act 2001
  [77:8:0.1] TRIMS | Investment measures in goods trade (not services); quantitative limits on imports by foreign investors prohibited | Phase-out of local-content rules
  [77:8:0.2] GATS | Services | Liberalisation commitments
  [77:8:0.3] SPS / TBT | Food safety, plant/animal health / technical standards | Food Safety and Standards Act 2006
  [77:8:0.4] TFA (Bali 2013) | Simplify customs | Ratified 2016
  [77:8:0.5] ITA (1996) | Zero duty on IT products | Signatory
[77:8:1] - **SPS**: WTO uses the **Codex Alimentarius Commission** (FAO and WHO, **1963**) for food-safety standards.
[77:8:2] - **GI of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999** - for TRIPS; GI = an IPR. India relies on TRIPS for GI; **not in the Lisbon Agreement**. Per the official key, **Darjeeling Tea** was named as not protected under the GI Act. **Nizamabad black clay pottery** (Azamgarh, UP) = GI 2015.
[77:8:3] - **Patents Act** in force **1972**, based on the **N. Rajagopala Ayyangar Committee (1957)**; **Patents (Second Amendment) Act 38 of 2002**, in force 20 May 2003.
[77:8:4] - **SCM Agreement** subsidies: **prohibited ("red light")**, actionable (countervailing duty), non-actionable (R&D, regional, environmental). Amber Box is AoA, not SCM.
[77:8:5] - India prohibits GM food imports without approval; India **does** import US apples (six disputes settled, June 2023).
[77:8:6] - US threatened WTO sanctions for not following ILO conventions (true).
[77:8:7] - EXIM Policy 2002-07 liberal; GATT/WTO aided liberalisation (both true, not cause and effect). **BTIA** = India-EU, talks from June 2007, Brussels.
## SUB 9 | Dispute Settlement and Appellate Body Crisis
[77:9:0] - **Appellate Body** non-functional since **December 2019** - **US blocked appointments**.
[77:9:1] - **MPIA** (Multi-Party Interim Appeal Arbitration Arrangement, **EU-led, 2020**) = stop-gap; **India has not joined**.
[77:9:2] - **BRICS New Delhi Declaration (Sep 2026)**: IMF/World Bank quota and voting reform + restoration of the two-tier WTO dispute settlement.

  [77:9:3.0] DS456 (US) | Solar cells - domestic content, National Solar Mission | India lost, 2016

=== NOTES 3:78 | Fdi and Fpi Policy in India - Routes, Sectoral Caps, Trends; Ftas and Cepas Signed by India
## SUB 0 | Fdi - Meaning and What Counts
[78:0:0] - **FDI**: investment for **lasting interest and control over management**; a **largely non-debt-creating** capital flow; longer-term.
[78:0:1] - Legal definition: capital instruments bought by a person resident outside India in an **unlisted** Indian company, or **10% or more** of post-issue paid-up equity (fully diluted) of a **listed** company.
[78:0:2] - Instruments: equity shares, debentures, preference shares, share warrants.
[78:0:3] - Brings capital, **technology**, management skills, competition, jobs, export competitiveness; finances the CAD with stable capital.
[78:0:4] - **Counts as FDI**: subsidiaries of foreign companies, majority foreign equity in Indian companies, companies exclusively financed by foreign companies, a wholly owned subsidiary.
[78:0:5] Portfolio investment is NOT automatically FDI; buying bonds or stocks of a company overseas is NOT FDI.
[78:0:6] TABLE head: Type | Meaning | Example
  [78:0:6.0] Greenfield | New facility | Hyundai, Sriperumbudur
  [78:0:6.1] Brownfield | Acquisition | Vodafone-Hutchison Essar
## SUB 1 | Fdi vs Fpi vs Fii
[78:1:0] TABLE head: Basis | FDI | FPI / FII
  [78:1:0.0] Threshold | ≥10% listed, or any unlisted stake | <10% listed (e.g. a 5% stake)
  [78:1:0.1] Nature | Long-term, stable, control | Liquid, short-term "hot money", more volatile
  [78:1:0.2] Brings | Capital + technology + management | Only financial capital; raises capital availability in general
  [78:1:0.3] Target | Specific sectors/enterprises | Securities (secondary market)
  [78:1:0.4] Regulator | DPIIT + RBI | SEBI (FPI Regulations 2019) + RBI
[78:1:1] - **10% threshold**: **Arvind Mayaram Committee**.
[78:1:2] - **SEBI FPI Regulations, 2019** unified **FIIs, sub-accounts and QFIs**; the term FII has been replaced by FPI. FPI opened to India in **1992**.
[78:1:3] - **Most unstable flows**: **FPI and NRI deposits** (interest-rate driven). Excess portfolio flows caused the **East Asian and Latin American crises**.
