## SUB 0 | Graphene, Fullerene and Carbon Nanotubes
[0:0] # Carbon nanomaterials (all sp², all pure carbon)
[0:1] TABLE head: Material | Structure | Remember
  [0:1.0] Graphene | Single atom-thick layer of graphite; 2D hexagonal (honeycomb) lattice | Made of carbon only – NOT silicon; thinnest and strongest material (~200 times steel; stronger than steel, brass, German silver); excellent electrical and heat conductor; ~97% transparent; flexible
  [0:1.1] Fullerene C60 | Hollow cage ("buckyball", soccer ball): 12 pentagons + 20 hexagons, 60 C atoms | Carbon allotrope – NOT a fluorine compound (Teflon is the fluorine polymer); semiconductor; drug delivery, lubricants, catalysts
  [0:1.2] Carbon nanotubes (CNTs) | Rolled graphene cylinders (1D tubes) | Stronger than steel by weight, light; metallic or semiconducting; drug delivery, biosensors, batteries
[0:2] - **Graphene uses:** **conducting electrodes for touch screens, LCDs and OLEDs**, fast transistors, flexible circuits, sensors, batteries and supercapacitors.
[0:3] - **Discoverers:** graphene isolated **2004** by **Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov** (Manchester) – **Nobel Physics 2010**; fullerene C60 found **1985** by **Kroto (Britain), Curl and Smalley (USA)** – **Nobel Chemistry 1996** (no "Faimen" on the team); named after architect **R. Buckminster Fuller**; **CNTs** – **Sumio Iijima (1991)**.
[0:4] - **Pairs angle (UPPCS):** Dry ice – solid CO2; Teflon – fluorine polymer; Sevin – insecticide; Keratin – protein; Mustard gas – chemical weapon; **Fullerene – carbon allotrope** (the wrongly matched pair).
[0:5] # UPSC 2012 – graphene statements
[0:6] - 1 (2D, good conductor) ✔, 2 (thinnest but strongest) ✔, 3 (**entirely silicon**) ✘, 4 (conducting electrodes for touch screens, LCDs, OLEDs) ✔ → **1, 2 and 4**.
[0:7] # UPSC 2020 – carbon nanotubes statements
[0:8] - 1 drug and antigen carriers ✔; 3 biochemical sensors ✔ (CNT biosensors); 2 artificial blood capillaries and 4 biodegradable are the contested ones.
[0:9] - **UPSC official key: (c) 1, 3 and 4** – CNTs can be degraded by some bacteria, fungi and enzymes; capillaries rejected (CNTs trigger platelet activation and clotting).
[0:10] - ⚠ Books differ: PMF answers (a) 1 and 2 (CNTs "not biodegradable"); many coaching keys gave (d) all four. Learn the official (c).
[0:11] > Exam angle: graphene = carbon, not silicon; fullerene = carbon, not fluorine; Iijima = nanotubes, Geim-Novoselov = graphene.

## SUB 1 | Diamond, Graphite and Other Forms of Carbon
[1:0] TABLE head: Allotrope | Bonding | Key property → use
  [1:0.0] Diamond | sp³; 3D tetrahedral network, each C bonded to four | Hardest natural substance (Mohs 10); least compressible; electrical insulator (no free electrons) but excellent heat conductor; transparent, high refractive index → cutting tools, jewellery
  [1:0.1] Graphite | sp² layers held by weak van der Waals forces | Soft, slippery, opaque, black; good electrical conductor → pencil "lead" (graphite + clay), dry lubricant for heavy machines, electrodes, reactor moderator
  [1:0.2] Amorphous carbon | Non-crystalline | Coal, charcoal, coke, lampblack; charcoal = the amorphous form among diamond/graphite/fullerene; activated charcoal – high surface area
[1:1] - **Diamond, graphite, graphene, fullerene, CNTs are all pure carbon** – so diamond is an allotrope of **carbon**; **methane (CH4) is a compound, NOT an allotrope**.
[1:2] - **Sand** (silica, SiO2) has **no carbon**; graphite, diamond and coal do.
[1:3] - **Lubricant:** **graphite** – not wood, steel, tungsten, bauxite, phosphorus, cuprite, haematite or cryolite.
[1:4] - **Hardest:** diamond (not glass, quartz, platinum). **Wurtzite boron nitride** is believed (by simulation) to be ~18% harder, but it is rare and untested – keys keep **diamond** as the hardest naturally occurring substance.
[1:5] - **Electrical conductor:** **graphite** (free electrons in layers) – diamond, peat and charcoal are poor conductors.

## SUB 2 | Nanoscale – Size, Units and Properties
[2:0] - **1 nm = 10^-9 m** (one billionth of a metre); **1 Å = 10^-10 m**, so **1 nm = 10 Å**.
[2:1] - **Nanoparticle size: 1-100 nm** (APPSC 2018, APPSC G2 2019, UKPSC 2012) – not 0.1-1 nm (atomic scale) or 100-1000 nm; a particle with at least one dimension **below 10^-7 m (100 nm)** is a **nanoparticle**.
[2:2] - **Scale:** human hair ~80,000-100,000 nm; red blood cell ~7,000 nm; virus ~50-100 nm; DNA width ~2 nm.
[2:3] - **Why different:** very high **surface-area-to-volume ratio** (more reactive, catalytic) + **quantum size effect** (discrete energy levels); also superparamagnetism, lower melting point, self-assembly.
[2:4] - **Most important property of nanomaterials (RAS 2013 key): friction** – the huge surface-to-volume ratio makes friction and wear dominant at the nanoscale.
[2:5] - **Device miniaturisation** = **nanotechnology**, the 21st-century technology (UPPCS 2004).
[2:6] - **Nanogold:** gold nanoparticles show **surface plasmon resonance** – look **red, purple (or blue)** instead of yellow, yet stay gold (biocompatible; used in conductive inks and lateral-flow test kits). APPSC key: "**blue gold with properties similar to gold**".
[2:7] # Quantum structures
[2:8] TABLE head: Structure | Nano-confined dimensions | Example
  [2:8.0] Quantum well | One dimension nano, two large (2D) | thin films
  [2:8.1] Quantum wire | Two dimensions nano (1D) | nanowires, nanotubes
  [2:8.2] Quantum dot | All three nano (0D) | semiconductor nanostructure, about 2-10 nm (TSLPRB: ~10 nm)
[2:9] - **Quantum dots:** size-tuned colour – **smaller dots → blue**, larger → red; QLED displays, bio-imaging, solar cells; **Nobel Chemistry 2023 – Bawendi, Brus, Ekimov**.
[2:10] - **Other nanomaterials:** **nanoporous materials – separation and catalysis**; nanofibres – filtration, smart clothing, scaffolds; nanocrystals – LEDs, imaging.

## SUB 3 | Piezoelectric Nanogenerators and Nano Devices
[3:0] - **Piezoelectric effect:** electric charge from **mechanical stress (pressure)** in crystals, ceramics, polymers; reversible (field → deformation).
[3:1] - **NCL Pune nanogenerator:** electricity when **pressure** is applied → **piezoelectric effect** (APPSC G1 2018, 2019) – NOT photoelectric, photovoltaic, thermoelectric, magnetoelectric or induction.
[3:2] - **Quartz crystal in watches** and **gas-stove (electronic) lighters** = **piezoelectric effect** (IAS 1993; APPSC).
[3:3] - **Light nanogenerator (contrast):** **IASST Guwahati** (DST) – graphene oxide + polyaniline device that harvests **light** energy to power wearables.
[3:4] - **Nanoear (2012, LMU Munich):** an **optically trapped gold nanoparticle** – detects sound down to **–60 dB** (not –120 dB; not silica or silver).
[3:5] - **Internet of Nano Things (IoNT):** network of nanodevices connected to the internet.
[3:6] - **Other devices:** gold-nanoparticle lateral-flow kits (malaria, dengue, typhoid in ~15 min); **graphene bendable wearable sensors (IISc + C-DAC)**.
[3:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 1 p.30 (IASST nanogenerator), p.38 (IoNT); PMF S&T Part 7 p.754-755 (piezoelectric materials); app notes 5:113 sub 5; general knowledge (nanoear, gas lighter)

## SUB 7 | Nanoparticles in Nature, Health and Environmental Concerns
[7:0] TABLE head: Origin | Examples
  [7:0.0] Natural | Volcanic ash, sea spray, clay, natural colloids (proteins, viruses)
  [7:0.1] Incidental | Soot, diesel exhaust, welding fumes
  [7:0.2] Engineered | Quantum dots, CNTs, nano-silver, TiO2
[7:1] - **Cosmetics:** **TiO2 and ZnO** nanoparticles in **sunscreens** (UV protection).
[7:2] - **UPSC 2022:** "nanoparticles do not exist in nature" ✘; metallic-oxide nanoparticles in cosmetics ✔; nanoparticles from commercial products entering the environment unsafe ✔ → **B and C**.
[7:3] - **Nanotoxicity:** enter by inhalation, ingestion, skin; cross the blood-brain barrier; **free radicals (ROS) → oxidative stress, inflammation**; persistence, **bioaccumulation**, food-chain entry (UPSC 2014: all three concerns correct).
[7:4] - **Regulation:** **no dedicated nano law**; DGFASLI (occupational), **FSSAI 2022**, **SCOMET** list (dual-use); **Guidelines for Evaluation of Nano-based Agri-input and Food Products (2020)** – **DBT, Agriculture Ministry, FSSAI**. Also cost and the "**nano-divide**".
[7:5] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 7 p.694-695 (origin, UPSC 2022), p.704-707 (TiO2, ZnO, issues, UPSC 2014); app notes 5:113 sub 10
