=== NOTES 2:107 | Marriage – Forms, Rules of Endogamy and Exogamy, Changing Trends and Legislation
## SUB 0 | Forms of Marriage
[107:0:0] # General Forms
[107:0:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [107:0:1.0] Monogamy | one husband, one wife
  [107:0:1.1] Polygyny | one man, many wives; sororal polygyny = marriage to sisters
  [107:0:1.2] Polyandry | one woman, many husbands; fraternal polyandry = marriage to brothers
  [107:0:1.3] Group marriage | several men with several women
  [107:0:1.4] Levirate | widow marries her husband's brother (Gond, Munda)
  [107:0:1.5] Sororate | widower marries his wife's sister
  [107:0:1.6] Child marriage / widow remarriage | below legal age / widow marries again
[107:0:2] Levirate = husband's brother; Sororate = wife's sister.
## SUB 1 | Vedic and Customary Unions
[107:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [107:1:0.0] Anuloma (hypergamy) | higher-varna man + lower-varna woman; tolerated
  [107:1:0.1] Pratiloma (hypogamy) | lower-varna man + higher-varna woman; condemned
  [107:1:0.2] Sambandham | consensual, informal union of Namboothiris and Nairs under Kerala's matrilineal system
  [107:1:0.3] Niyoga | wife lives with a designated man to have a child when the husband is impotent or dead
[107:1:1] - **Later Vedic** changes: child marriage began; **Swayamvara** declined; widow remarriage discouraged; same-gotra marriage banned.
[107:1:2] APPSC 2025: Sambandham and Niyoga asked as meaning-type questions.
## SUB 2 | Eight Forms in Manu Smriti
[107:2:0] - Manu Smriti lists **8** forms: **4 approved (Prashasta)**, **4 disapproved (Aprashasta)**.
[107:2:1] TABLE head: Form | Meaning | Status
  [107:2:1.0] Brahma | daughter given to a learned, virtuous groom; no dowry or bride price | Approved; highest
  [107:2:1.1] Daiva | daughter given to a priest officiating at a sacrifice | Approved
  [107:2:1.2] Arsha | after symbolic gifts (cow and bull) from the groom; sages | Approved
  [107:2:1.3] Prajapatya | duty, family welfare, children | Approved
  [107:2:1.4] Gandharva | love and consent, no parental approval (Shakuntala–Dushyanta) | Disapproved but conditionally accepted
  [107:2:1.5] Asura | purchase of bride; groom's family pays bride price | Disapproved
  [107:2:1.6] Rakshasa | forceful abduction; linked with Kshatriyas; seen in epics | Disapproved
  [107:2:1.7] Paisacha | deceit, intoxication, force, no consent | Most condemned
[107:2:2] Gandharva is listed among the disapproved forms although it is conditionally accepted.
[107:2:3] - **Satyashodhak marriage** (started by **Savitribai Phule**): no dowry, no Brahmin priest.
## SUB 3 | Rules of Mate Selection
[107:3:0] # Endogamy and Exogamy
[107:3:1] TABLE head: Rule | Meaning | Purpose
  [107:3:1.0] Endogamy | within caste, tribe, religion (varna, caste, sub-caste) | keeps caste boundaries, group identity, controls property passing
  [107:3:1.1] Exogamy | outside own group; bars same gotra | –
  [107:3:1.2] Hypergamy / Hypogamy | into a higher / lower caste | –
[107:3:2] - **Ghurye**: endogamy is the **defining feature of caste**.
[107:3:3] - **North India**: caste endogamy + gotra exogamy + **village exogamy**.
[107:3:4] - **Irawati Karve** analysed sapinda and gotra.
[107:3:5] # Sapinda and Prohibited Degrees
[107:3:6] - **HMA Sec. 3(f)** sapinda: **3 generations** on the mother's side, **5** on the father's.
[107:3:7] - **Sec. 5(iv)** bars prohibited degrees; **Sec. 5(v)** bars sapinda – both subject to **custom**.
[107:3:8] - The **Delhi High Court upheld Sec. 5(v)** (sapinda bar), subject to custom.
[107:3:9] Sapinda: 3 (mother's side) / 5 (father's side).
## SUB 4 | Tribal Ways of Acquiring A Mate
[107:4:0] - Ways: probation, capture, trial, purchase, service, exchange, mutual consent, intrusion.
[107:4:1] TABLE head: Way | Tribe / example
  [107:4:1.0] Capture | Naga, Ho, Bhil, Gond
  [107:4:1.1] Service (groom works at bride's home) | Gonds
  [107:4:1.2] Exchange | Atta Satta in Rajasthan; criticised by the Rajasthan High Court
  [107:4:1.3] Purchase (bride price) | parallels Asura form
[107:4:2] # Ho Tribe – Own Terms
[107:4:3] TABLE head: Ho term | Meaning
  [107:4:3.0] Andi | traditional arranged marriage, with bride price (gonong); most esteemed
  [107:4:3.1] Diku Andi | marriage by **negotiation**; later form borrowed from Hindus ("diku" = outsider)
  [107:4:3.2] Oportipi | marriage by capture
  [107:4:3.3] Raji-khushi | elopement by mutual consent (love)
  [107:4:3.4] Anader | intrusion – girl forces herself into the lover's home
[107:4:4] - Ho: **marry outside the paternal clan**; maternal cousin marriage common; **elopement and abduction** traditionally common.

## SUB 7 | Divorce and Marriage Offences
[107:7:0] # Hindu Law
[107:7:1] - **Sec. 13** grounds: adultery, cruelty, desertion, conversion, unsoundness of mind, renunciation, not heard of for **7 years**.
[107:7:2] - **Desertion:** called the **poor man's divorce** (no legal cost); Parsi divorce law dates from **1865** (re-enacted 1936).
[107:7:3] - **Irretrievable breakdown** is **not** an HMA ground; SC grants relief under **Art. 142**.
[107:7:4] # Muslim Law
[107:7:5] - Wife's routes: **khula** (mutual consent); 1939 Act grounds: no maintenance for **2 years**, imprisonment of **7+ years**, cruelty.
[107:7:6] - 2019 Act: subsistence allowance and custody of minor children for the wife.
[107:7:7] # Offences (IPC → BNS)
[107:7:8] - IPC Chapter XX (493–498) = **BNS 81–84**.
[107:7:9] - **Bigamy**: IPC **494** = **BNS 82**.
[107:7:10] - **Assam Prohibition of Polygamy Bill 2025**: cognizable, non-bailable; up to **7 years'** RI.

[107:9:3] - **Nepal**: **first South Asian** nation to register a same-sex marriage.

## SUB 0 | Kinship – Meaning and Types
[108:0:0] - **Kinship**: a system of social relationships based on **blood and marriage**.
[108:0:1] - **Radcliffe-Brown**: kinship = a system of dynamic relations regulated by social usage.
[108:0:2] - **Marriage**: a socially approved union; regulates sex, legitimises children, creates kinship ties.
[108:0:3] TABLE head: Type | Basis | Example
  [108:0:3.0] Consanguineal | blood | parent-child, uncle-nephew
  [108:0:3.1] Affinal | marriage | husband-wife, son-in-law and father-in-law, brothers-in-law
  [108:0:3.2] Fictive / ritual | assumed, not biological | godparenthood, dharam bhai/behen, rakhi ties
[108:0:4] A father-son tie by birth is consanguineal, not fictive.

=== NOTES 2:109 | Caste – Features, Theories of Origin, Varna-jati Distinction
## SUB 0 | Varna and Jati
[109:0:0] - "Caste" from **Portuguese casta** (race, breed).
[109:0:1] TABLE head: Varna | Jati
  [109:0:1.0] theoretical 4-fold scheme of the Vedas (Chaturvarna) | real local groups; thousands of sub-castes
  [109:0:1.1] excludes "untouchables" | regional variations
  [109:0:1.2] almost no mobility | mobility possible, esp. middle castes
[109:0:2] - **1931 Census**: **4,147** castes. Colonial Census enumerated castes from **1871**; 1901 Census (Risley) ranked them by precedence.
## SUB 1 | Theories and Stages of Origin
[109:1:0] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent | Idea
  [109:1:0.0] Traditional / Divine | Purusha Sukta (Rig Veda 10.90) | varnas from head, arms, thighs, feet
  [109:1:0.1] Racial | Herbert Risley | racial difference; Aryans kept apart
  [109:1:0.2] Occupational | Nesfield | hereditary occupations → guilds → castes
  [109:1:0.3] Brahminical / Political | Abbé Dubois | Brahmin device for purity
  [109:1:0.4] Cultural | S.C. Roy | commensality and marriage bars
[109:1:1] - Early Vedic: no rigid varna. **Later Vedic**: four hereditary varnas crystallise. **Post-Maurya/Gupta**: Manu Smriti, rigid caste. British: caste councils' powers go to courts.
## SUB 2 | Features of Caste
[109:2:0] - **Ghurye** (*Caste and Race in India*, 1932), **six features**: segmental division; hierarchy; restrictions on feeding and social intercourse; civil and religious disabilities and privileges; lack of free choice of occupation; restrictions on marriage (endogamy).
[109:2:1] - Other features: ascribed by birth, hereditary occupation, caste panchayat as court.
[109:2:2] - Two dimensions: **vertical** (ranking) and **horizontal** (grouping).
## SUB 3 | Definitions by Scholars
[109:3:0] TABLE head: Scholar | Definition / idea
  [109:3:0.0] S.V. Ketkar | *History of Caste in India* (1909): membership by birth + ban on marrying outside
  [109:3:0.1] Risley | families with common name, claimed mythical ancestor, same hereditary calling
  [109:3:0.2] C. Bouglé | hereditary specialisation, hierarchy, repulsion
  [109:3:0.3] Martindale and Monachesi | birth-fixed duties sanctioned by religion
  [109:3:0.4] A.W. Green | ideally no mobility up or down
## SUB 4 | Purity-pollution and Cultural Traits
[109:4:0] - **Louis Dumont**, *Homo Hierarchicus* (1966): **pure vs impure**; religious status encompasses power.
[109:4:1] - **McKim Marriott**: interactional approach; ranking by exchange of **"coded substances"** (food, water, services).
[109:4:2] - Cultural traits: pollution by touch, untouchability, Sanskritic rituals, commensal taboos (not universal inter-caste commensality).
## SUB 5 | Jajmani System
[109:5:0] - Socio-economic and ritual interdependence: landholding **jajman** gets hereditary services from **kamin** castes.

=== NOTES 2:110 | Caste in Modern India – Dominant Caste, Sanskritisation, Caste Associations, Caste and Politics
## SUB 0 | Sanskritisation and Mobility
[110:0:0] - **Sanskritisation** (M.N. Srinivas): lower caste adopts rituals, vegetarianism, sacred thread of the twice-born; **positional, not structural** change.
[110:0:1] - **Kshatriyaisation** (**Kulke**): Marathas, Jats, Yadavs, Thevars, Nairs claim warrior status.
[110:0:2] - **Census petitions**: the colonial Census of **1871–72** ranked castes → groups formed associations to seek higher classification.
## SUB 1 | Dominant Caste
[110:1:0] - Coined by **M.N. Srinivas** from **Rampura village, Mysore** (1955).
[110:1:1] - Traditional criteria: sizeable **arable land**, **numerical strength**, high **ritual status**.
[110:1:2] - Later additions: Western education, administrative jobs, urban income.
[110:1:3] - Pre-independence **Bihar**: Bhumihar, Rajput, Kayastha (**not Kurmi**).
[110:1:4] Mahuva = I.P. Desai (joint family), not Srinivas.
## SUB 2 | Dominant Castes of Andhra Pradesh
[110:2:0] - **Reddys, Kammas, Velamas, Kapus** = martial, agrarian dominant castes; Kayasthas = administrative elite.
[110:2:1] - Medieval Andhra: these castes **Kshatriyaised** through the **Nayankara** system.
[110:2:2] - **Samayas** = Kakatiya-era caste associations.
[110:2:3] - **Justice Party**: intermediate castes (Kammas, Reddys, Naidus); non-Brahmin movement → **Communal G.O. 1921** (Madras Presidency).
## SUB 3 | Caste Associations
[110:3:0] - Voluntary, membership-based body of a caste acting as a **pressure group**.
[110:3:1] - **Lloyd and Susanne Rudolph**, *The Modernity of Tradition* (**1967**): caste associations = **agents of democratisation** (**Nadars, Vanniyars**); coined **"bullock capitalists"**.
[110:3:2] - Marathas, Gujjars, Patels seek OBC status.
## SUB 4 | Caste and Politics
[110:4:0] - **Rajni Kothari** edited *Caste in Indian Politics* (**1970**).
[110:4:1] - **Minto-Morley Reforms 1909** (separate electorates): root of vote-bank politics.
[110:4:2] - **Jaffrelot**, *India's Silent Revolution*: lower castes rise through politics.
[110:4:3] - Conflict now about **power**, not ritual status.
[110:4:4] RAS/RTS 2013 A-R: castes are politicised and casteism in politics is rising – both true, but one does not explain the other.
## SUB 5 | Forces of Change
[110:5:0] TABLE head: Force | Effect
  [110:5:0.0] Modern education | equality values; fall of Brahmin supremacy
  [110:5:0.1] Urbanisation | food taboos weaken
  [110:5:0.2] Reservation / Mandal | OBCs put at 52%+; identity reinforced
  [110:5:0.3] Land reforms | Gramdan failed in dominant-caste heartlands
[110:5:1] - **Article 17** abolishes untouchability (first constitutional provision against it); only **24** PCR Act cases in 2023.

  [110:6:0.0] M.N. Srinivas | *The Remembered Village*; *Social Change in Modern India*

## SUB 7 | Caste Census Debates
[110:7:0] - First Census **1872** (Lord Mayo, non-synchronous); first synchronous **1881**.
[110:7:1] - **1931**: last full caste count; since **1951** only SCs and STs.
[110:7:2] - **SECC 2011**: **Rural Development Ministry**, outside **Census Act 1948**; ~**46 lakh** caste names; data never released.
