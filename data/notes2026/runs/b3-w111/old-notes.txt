[111:0:7] - **CGS power** split by **five years' consumption** (AP ≈ 46%), not the population ratio.

## SUB 1 | Forests, Protected Areas and Forest Law
[111:1:0] # Forest cover (ISFR, FSI Dehradun, biennial)
[111:1:1] - **ISFR** is **biennial**, published by the **Forest Survey of India, Dehradun**.
[111:1:2] - **ISFR 2021: AP's forest cover rose by 647 sq km over 2019** – the largest increase among States.
[111:1:3] - ISFR 2023: **forest + tree cover 21.74%** of area; forest area incl. scrub 38,436.58 sq km (**23.59%**, includes 8,351.62 sq km scrub); **Recorded Forest Area 37,258 sq km – 8th** (ISFR 2021); forest = canopy **≥10%**.
[111:1:4] - Old districts: most cover **Visakhapatnam**; largest area **YSR Kadapa**; least **Krishna**. National Forest Policy **1988**: 33% (66% in hills).
[111:1:5] # Protected areas and forest types
[111:1:6] - **13 Wildlife Sanctuaries, 1 Tiger Reserve, 3 National Parks**; the tiger reserve = **Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam (NSTR)**, India's largest, in **Nallamala** (AP's largest forest).
[111:1:7] - Mostly **tropical dry deciduous**; **mangroves** (Coringa) at the Godavari and Krishna mouths; moist deciduous in the north-coastal hills.
[111:1:8] - **Red Sanders:** **Chittoor and YSR Kadapa** (Seshachalam, Veligonda, Lankamala, **Nallamala**, Talakona); endemic to the southern Eastern Ghats; IUCN **Endangered** (2021).
[111:1:9] # Laws, schemes and minor forest produce
[111:1:10] TABLE head: Law / scheme | Key point
  [111:1:10.0] AP Forest Act, 1967 | S.3 power to reserve; S.15 final notification; S.28 power to declare a protected forest to cease to be protected
  [111:1:10.1] FCA 1980 | Renamed Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam from 29 Dec 2023
  [111:1:10.2] PESA 1996 / FRA 2006 | Gram Sabha controls MFP / recommends diversion for public utilities
  [111:1:10.3] Green India Mission (2011, NAPCC) | 5 mha new + 5 mha better forest (not 10 mha new)
  [111:1:10.4] Nagar Van Yojana / Nagaravanam | 61 Nagar Vans / 37 city forests
  [111:1:10.5] CAMPA | Spends compensatory levies on afforestation
[111:1:11] - **Minor Forest Produce:** collected and sold mainly by the **Girijan Cooperative Corporation (GCC)**, HQ **Visakhapatnam**; runs MSP for MFP; **beedi leaf nationalised 1971**.
[111:1:12] - **JFM** (Vana Samrakshana Samithis) from **1992**; Community Forest Management from **2002**; **FRA titles 2,27,300** individual (31 Aug 2026).
[111:1:13] - **Neeru-Chettu:** making AP **drought-free** through water conservation and tree planting.

=== NOTES 3:112 | Bifurcation and Ap's State Revenue – Revenue Deficit, Loss of Hyderabad, Resource Gap
## SUB 0 | Resource Gap and Deficits of the Bifurcation Year (2014-15)
[112:0:0] # Dates
[112:0:1] - **Appointed day = 2 June 2014** (both AP and Telangana came into being); the Act got presidential **assent on 1 March 2014** – not "2 March" or "1 April".
[112:0:2] # Resource gap
[112:0:3] - **Resource gap** = **revenue deficit** (revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts) for the **first ten months** (2 June 2014 – 31 March 2015).
[112:0:4] - Method difference: the **Centre** used standardised accounting and **excluded new State schemes** (e.g. farm-loan waiver); **AP** counted its **actual expenditure**.

  [112:0:5.2] State (AP) | ₹16,078 crore
[112:0:6] - **Central assistance to bridge the 2014-15 gap**: **₹3,979.50 crore**, released in instalments from 2 June 2014 up to **2016-17**.
[112:0:7] - Bifurcation-year gap: **more than ₹15,000 crore** on the State's count.
[112:0:8] # Fiscal deficit 2014-15
[112:0:9] - **CAG**: fiscal deficit **₹31,717 crore = 6.10% of GSDP** in 2014-15 – **slightly more than double the FRBM ceiling of 3%**.
[112:0:10] - Before 2014-15 the undivided State ran **revenue surpluses for about eight years** (2006-07 to 2013-14 – printed APPSC key; not checked in the library); bifurcation turned it into a large revenue deficit.

## SUB 1 | Loss of Hyderabad – Revenue Base and Challenges
[112:1:0] - **Hyderabad region (with Rangareddy and Medak)** gave **nearly 50%** of undivided AP's **tax and non-tax revenue**.
[112:1:1] - **Srikrishna Committee (2010)**: **Hyderabad and Rangareddy's share of the State's GDP from modern services** rose from **18% (1999-2000) to about 25% (2005-06)**.
[112:1:2] - Revenue lost: **corporate taxes, stamp duties, commercial taxes** (not agricultural land revenue).
[112:1:3] - **APRA 2014 gives AP no share of Hyderabad's tax revenue** – taxes accrue to the State where the activity is (Telangana); only the 13th FC award was split by population (Sec. 46).
[112:1:4] - The bifurcation's **adverse effect on revenue fell on Andhra Pradesh State** (residual AP), not on Telangana.
[112:1:5] TABLE head: Asset lost | Effect
  [112:1:5.0] HITEC City, Financial District | Loss of IT/ITeS exports

  [112:1:5.2] Loss of Hyderabad as a whole | Structural revenue deficit
[112:1:6] - Residual AP kept its **agricultural base** – that was not lost.
[112:1:7] - Division followed the **population ratio 58.32 : 41.68** (AP : Telangana).
[112:1:8] - **Schedule IX and X institutions:** Schedule IX = government companies and corporations, divided as recommended by the expert committee headed by **Sheela Bhide**; **Schedule X = 142 institutions** (most in Telangana); AP wants assets shared in the population ratio, Telangana by location.
[112:1:9] - **Biggest challenges after 2014 (all of them)**: building **infrastructure for a new capital**, moving the **administration from Hyderabad to Amaravati**, and a **severe financial crisis with scarcity of human resources**.
[112:1:10] > Exam angle: Hyderabad ≈ **50%** of revenue; Srikrishna: Hyderabad + Rangareddy ≈ **25%** of modern-services GDP; **no share** of Hyderabad's taxes for AP.

## SUB 4 | Constitutional and Legal Base
[113:4:0] - **Article 262**: Parliament may make law for adjudicating **inter-State river water disputes** (use, distribution or control of waters).
[113:4:1] - **Article 262(2)**: Parliament may provide that **neither the Supreme Court nor any other court** shall exercise jurisdiction over such disputes.
[113:4:2] - **Inter-State River Water Disputes (ISRWD) Act, 1956** – enacted under Article 262.
[113:4:3]   - **Section 11**: bars the Supreme Court and other courts from disputes that may be referred to a tribunal.
[113:4:4]   - The Supreme Court held (Cauvery case, 1991) that this bar does **not** cover its appeal jurisdiction under **Article 136**.
[113:4:5]   - **Section 5(1)**: used in **October 2023** to give **KWDT-II further terms of reference**.
[113:4:6] - Articles 265 (no tax without law) and 268 (duties levied by the Union, collected by States) are tax articles – not water.

## SUB 4 | Groundwater Regulation and Water Policy
[115:4:0] - **APWALTA, 2002** (AP Water, Land and Trees Act): checks random borewell digging; the authority (Panchayat Secretary in villages) may require **rainwater harvesting structures on premises of 200 sq m or more**.
[115:4:1] - **National Water Policy 2012**: **Water Users Associations** to be given **statutory powers** to collect and retain water charges.
[115:4:2] - **Maharashtra (April 2016)**: banned digging bore wells **deeper than 200 feet** during drought.
[115:4:3] - **APFAMGS**: farmers collect rainfall and well data, prepare **crop-water budgets**, voluntarily limit pumping (FAO-supported).
[115:4:4] - 40,000-50,000 new bore wells a year: area irrigated per well falling.
