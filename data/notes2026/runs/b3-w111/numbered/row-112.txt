## SUB 0 | Resource Gap and Deficits of the Bifurcation Year (2014-15)
[0:0] # Dates
[0:1] - **Appointed day = 2 June 2014** (both AP and Telangana came into being); the Act got presidential **assent on 1 March 2014** – not "2 March" or "1 April".
[0:2] # Resource gap
[0:3] - **Resource gap** = **revenue deficit** (revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts) for the **first ten months** (2 June 2014 – 31 March 2015).
[0:4] - Method difference: the **Centre** used standardised accounting and **excluded new State schemes** (e.g. farm-loan waiver); **AP** counted its **actual expenditure**.
[0:5] TABLE head: Estimate by | Resource gap 2014-15
  [0:5.0] Union Finance Ministry | ₹4,118 crore
  [0:5.1] CAG (2 Jun 2014 – 31 Mar 2015) | ₹13,776 crore
  [0:5.2] State (AP) | ₹16,078 crore
[0:6] - **Central assistance to bridge the 2014-15 gap**: **₹3,979.50 crore**, released in instalments from 2 June 2014 up to **2016-17**.
[0:7] - Bifurcation-year gap: **more than ₹15,000 crore** on the State's count.
[0:8] # Fiscal deficit 2014-15
[0:9] - **CAG**: fiscal deficit **₹31,717 crore = 6.10% of GSDP** in 2014-15 – **slightly more than double the FRBM ceiling of 3%**.
[0:10] - Before 2014-15 the undivided State ran **revenue surpluses for about eight years** (2006-07 to 2013-14 – printed APPSC key; not checked in the library); bifurcation turned it into a large revenue deficit.

## SUB 1 | Loss of Hyderabad – Revenue Base and Challenges
[1:0] - **Hyderabad region (with Rangareddy and Medak)** gave **nearly 50%** of undivided AP's **tax and non-tax revenue**.
[1:1] - **Srikrishna Committee (2010)**: **Hyderabad and Rangareddy's share of the State's GDP from modern services** rose from **18% (1999-2000) to about 25% (2005-06)**.
[1:2] - Revenue lost: **corporate taxes, stamp duties, commercial taxes** (not agricultural land revenue).
[1:3] - **APRA 2014 gives AP no share of Hyderabad's tax revenue** – taxes accrue to the State where the activity is (Telangana); only the 13th FC award was split by population (Sec. 46).
[1:4] - The bifurcation's **adverse effect on revenue fell on Andhra Pradesh State** (residual AP), not on Telangana.
[1:5] TABLE head: Asset lost | Effect
  [1:5.0] HITEC City, Financial District | Loss of IT/ITeS exports
  [1:5.1] Genome Valley pharma cluster; corporate and bank head offices; commercial real estate; MSME supply chains | Narrower tax base (GST, stamp duty, motor-vehicle tax)
  [1:5.2] Loss of Hyderabad as a whole | Structural revenue deficit
[1:6] - Residual AP kept its **agricultural base** – that was not lost.
[1:7] - Division followed the **population ratio 58.32 : 41.68** (AP : Telangana).
[1:8] - **Schedule IX and X institutions:** Schedule IX = government companies and corporations, divided as recommended by the expert committee headed by **Sheela Bhide**; **Schedule X = 142 institutions** (most in Telangana); AP wants assets shared in the population ratio, Telangana by location.
[1:9] - **Biggest challenges after 2014 (all of them)**: building **infrastructure for a new capital**, moving the **administration from Hyderabad to Amaravati**, and a **severe financial crisis with scarcity of human resources**.
[1:10] > Exam angle: Hyderabad ≈ **50%** of revenue; Srikrishna: Hyderabad + Rangareddy ≈ **25%** of modern-services GDP; **no share** of Hyderabad's taxes for AP.
[1:11] ~~ Sources: AP Reorganisation Act 2014 (apra.txt s.46, Schedules IX-X); old app notes 3:112 sub 0; Srikrishna Committee figures as printed in the PYQ explanation (not checked in the library)

## SUB 3 | Power Sector – Reforms, Discoms and Dues
[3:0] - **Department of Expenditure** borrowing window (Budget 2021-22): extra borrowing of **0.5% of GSDP** linked to **power-sector reforms**.
[3:1] - **4 February 2021**: AP became the **second State** to complete the stipulated reforms – **DBT of electricity subsidy to farmers** (Srikakulam pilot from Sep 2020) – and could borrow an extra **₹1,515 crore**.
[3:2] - **UDAY**: AP signed on **24 June 2016** – the **13th State**; State took over about **₹11,000 crore** of DISCOM debt.
[3:3] - CAG on power subsidy: spending at **121% (2023)** and **120% (2024)** of budget estimates.
[3:4] - **Power dues**: under the power-sharing arrangement **Telangana has to pay Andhra Pradesh** for power supplied; on 29 Aug 2022 the Union Power Ministry (Sec. 92, APRA) ordered Telangana to pay about **₹6,756 crore** to APGENCO; the **Telangana High Court quashed** the order (Oct 2023).
[3:5] > Exam angle: AP = **second** State (not first) in DoE power reforms; dues flow **Telangana → AP**.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: The Hans India 4 Feb 2021 and Mercom India (Finance Ministry release: AP 2nd State, ₹1,515 cr, DBT); AP Reorganisation Act 2014 (apra.txt s.92, Twelfth Schedule); old app notes 3:112 sub 7
## SUB 4 | Demands to the Centre – Special Category Status
[4:0] - **N. Chandrababu Naidu** demanded **Special Category Status (SCS)** citing post-bifurcation revenue loss.
[4:1] - SCS for **5 years** was promised on the floor of the **Rajya Sabha (2014)**.
[4:2] - Under SCS the **Centre bears 90%** of CSS costs (ordinary States 60%) plus tax breaks.
[4:3] - Centre's refusal: the **14th FC** ended the general/special category distinction and raised devolution **32% → 42%**.
[4:4] - Chronology: **APRA (2014) → 14th FC award (2015) → TDP leaves NDA over SCS (2018)**.
[4:5] TABLE head: Provision | Demand / grant
  [4:5.0] Sec. 94(1) | Tax incentives
  [4:5.1] Sec. 46 | Backward-area packages (Rayalaseema, North Coastal)
  [4:5.2] Sec. 94(3) | Special support for capital buildings (₹2,500 crore released)
[4:6] - Other demands: revenue deficit grants; full Polavaram funding at revised cost; capital-city grants.
[4:7] ~~ Sources: AP Reorganisation Act 2014 (apra.txt s.46, 94); old app notes 3:112 sub 2
