=== NOTES 1:12 | Ivc Religion – Pashupati Seal, Mother Goddess, Tree and Animal Worship, Fire Altars
## SUB 0 | Nature of Harappan Religion
[12:0:0] - IVC was **predominantly secular**: religion present but not dominant.
[12:0:1] - **No temple** found; worship domestic or open-air. **Great Bath** (Mohenjodaro) = ritual purification.
[12:0:2] - **Idol worship** began in the **Pre-Aryan** period; common in the IVC. People worshipped **both male and female deities**.
[12:0:3] - PYQ key: Indus people believed in **Spirit and Brahma**; they worshipped **Shiva** (Pashupati).
[12:0:4] UPSC 2013: "no palaces, no temples, no horse chariots" – only "worshipped both male and female deities" is correct.
## SUB 1 | Pashupati Seal and Mother Goddess
[12:1:0] TABLE head: Feature | Detail
  [12:1:0.0] Site | Mohenjodaro
  [12:1:0.1] Figure | Three-faced, horned headdress, yogic posture
  [12:1:0.2] Animals | Elephant, tiger, rhinoceros, buffalo; deer below
  [12:1:0.3] Identification | Proto-Shiva / Pashupati (lord of animals) – John Marshall
  [12:1:0.4] Debate | Deity of animals and fertility; script undeciphered
[12:1:1] - **Mother Goddess**: terracotta female figurines → **fertility cult**; Harappa, Kot Diji, Kalibangan, Gujarat sites. Probably the main deity.
[12:1:2] - **Horned deity on a triangular terracotta cake** – **Kalibangan**; other side shows a human pulling a goat by a rope. Clay mask of horned god – Kalibangan and Gujarat.
## SUB 2 | Animal, Tree and Symbol Worship
[12:2:0] - Sacred animals: **humped bull, unicorn** (mythical), elephant, tiger.
[12:2:1] - **Pipal** = most depicted tree on seals, in ritual scenes; motif from **Early Harappan** phase.
[12:2:2] - **Linga and yoni**, round **pierced stones** (Harappa) = fertility objects; early Shaivite elements. **Swastika** = later auspicious symbol.
[12:2:3] - Seals: mostly **steatite** (few terracotta, agate, chert); mostly **square** (few cube, circle, cylindrical).
[12:2:4] - **Lion** remains not found; cow and elephant found; horse disputed.
## SUB 3 | Fire Altars and Burials
[12:3:0] - **Row of seven fire altars** on mud-brick platforms – **Kalibangan**; also **Lothal**. Link to Vedic yajna debated.
[12:3:1] - Amulets show deities in yogic poses. Grave goods (pottery, ornaments, mirrors, beads, food) → **afterlife** belief and status differences.

[12:4:3] - **Script**: **undeciphered**, pictographic; written **right to left** (some boustrophedon); about **400+ signs**; short inscriptions.
[12:4:4] - Toys, masks: terracotta; **dice** at Harappa; **lipstick** at Chanhudaro; mirrors, combs; gold, silver, shell ornaments.

=== NOTES 1:14 | Aryan Question – Original Home Theories and Archaeological Correlates
## SUB 0 | Meaning of 'arya'
[14:0:0] - 'Arya' originally meant **"noble"** / of high birth; later persons of noble lineage and social standing.
[14:0:1] - **Classical Sanskrit** sense: Arya = **"a nobleman"**.
[14:0:2] - It was the Indo-Iranian people's **own name**; Vedic people used it for themselves and for the noble class. The Indo-Iranian group that wrote the **Avesta** and the **Vedas** called themselves Aryans.
[14:0:3] - 'Aryan' = a **speech group** (linguistic-cultural label): speakers of the **Indo-Aryan / Indo-Iranian** branches of the Indo-European family.
[14:0:4] - It is **not an ethnic or racial group**; "superior race" is a rejected colonial misuse.
[14:0:5] - **Max Muller** popularised the 'Aryan' idea.
[14:0:6] Exam options "ethnic group / nomadic people / speech group / superior race" – answer: **speech group**.

=== NOTES 1:16 | Early Vedic Polity – Rajan, Sabha, Samiti, Vidatha, Gana; Tribal Chiefdom
## SUB 0 | Nature of Early Vedic Polity
[16:0:0] - **Tribal polity** (APPSC 2018 key) – not a republic or autocracy; **kinship-based**, **no formal bureaucracy**, **no fixed territory**.
[16:0:1] - **Jana** (tribe) = largest unit; mentioned **275 times** in the Rigveda. The term **Janapada is absent** from the Rigveda.
[16:0:2] - Order (largest → smallest): **Jana → Vis** (clan, under Vispati) **→ Grama** (village, Gramani) **→ Kula** (family, Kulapa).
[16:0:3] - **Rig Veda** = only source for the period.
[16:0:4] # Early vs Later Vedic
[16:0:5] TABLE head: Feature | Early Vedic | Later Vedic
  [16:0:5.0] Unit | Jana (tribe) | Janapada / Rashtra (territory)
  [16:0:5.1] Chief | Elective traces, limited | Hereditary territorial king
  [16:0:5.2] Assemblies | Sabha, Samiti, Vidatha powerful | Vidatha vanished; Sabha, Samiti under chiefs and nobles; women excluded
  [16:0:5.3] Revenue | Bali (voluntary) | Regular taxes; ratnins
  [16:0:5.4] Rituals | Simple | Rajasuya, Vajapeya, Ashvamedha
## SUB 1 | Vedic Assemblies
[16:1:0] TABLE head: Assembly | Composition | Function
  [16:1:0.0] Sabha | Elders, elites, the learned; women (Sabhavati) | Advisory, social, judicial
  [16:1:0.1] Samiti | Common people (folk assembly) | Tribal business, legislation, choice of the chief
  [16:1:0.2] Vidatha | Whole tribe; earliest; women attended | Secular, religious, economic, military
  [16:1:0.3] Gana | Troop of tribesmen | Military and tribal functions
  [16:1:0.4] Assembly | Mentions in Rigveda
  [16:1:0.5] Vidatha | 122 (most)
  [16:1:0.6] Samiti | 9
  [16:1:0.7] Sabha | 8
[16:1:1] - Sabha and Samiti = **checks on the king's autocracy**; early "democratic" elements.
[16:1:2] - **Atharvaveda** calls Sabha and Samiti the **two (twin) daughters of Prajapati** and treats them as separate; it mentions women in the Samiti.
[16:1:3] Judicial assembly = **Sabha**; earliest / most mentioned = **Vidatha**; "daughters of Prajapati" = **Atharvaveda**, not Rigveda.
[16:1:4] # Women in assemblies
[16:1:5] - Women attended the **Sabha** (Sabhavati) and the **Vidatha**; excluded in Later Vedic times.
[16:1:6] Vidatha = earliest assembly and the first to disappear in the Later Vedic period.
## SUB 2 | The Rajan
[16:2:0] - Tribal chief: **protector of the tribe and its cattle**, not ruler of a fixed territory.
[16:2:1] - Titles: **Gopati** (lord of cattle), Gopa, **Gopajanasya**. Queen = **Mahisi**.
[16:2:2] - Selection: tended towards **hereditary** but not strictly; **traces of election by the Samiti**. Older PYQ keys: hereditary monarchy.
[16:2:3] - Duties: protect tribe and cattle, lead in war, keep order, settle disputes – with advice of assemblies and purohita.
[16:2:4] - Kingship **not divine**; rare exception: **Trasadasyu** (Puru king) called **ardhadeva** (demi-god).
## SUB 3 | Officials

  [16:3:0.0] Purohita | Chief priest and adviser
  [16:3:0.1] Senani | Army commander
  [16:3:0.2] Gramani | Village head; military leader of the grama
  [16:3:0.3] Kulapa / Kulapati | Head of the family

  [16:3:0.5] Jivagribha / Ugra | Police officials
  [16:3:0.6] Spasa | Spy
  [16:3:0.7] Madhyamasi | Mediator
[16:3:1] Vrajapati = pasture land, not village; Gramani = village head; Spasa = spy.
## SUB 4 | Army, Warfare and Revenue

  [16:4:0.3] Bali | Voluntary offering; no regular tax

=== NOTES 1:17 | Early Vedic Society and Economy – Pastoralism, Gavishti, Varna Origins, Position of Women
## SUB 0 | Pastoral Economy
[17:0:0] - Economy **mainly pastoral**, agriculture secondary (mixed). **Cattle breeding** = main activity.
[17:0:1] - **Cow** = **most valuable property**, **standard unit of value and exchange**; **Gau** named about **176 times**; a deity in some Suktas.
[17:0:2] - Land not yet main wealth; horses and gold valued. Priests paid in cows, horses, gold.
[17:0:3] - Animals kept: cattle, sheep, goats, horses, dogs, asses (buffalo not prominent). Known: lion, elephant, bear.
[17:0:4] # Cow-derived terms

  [17:0:5.0] Aghanya | "Not to be killed" – the cow
  [17:0:5.1] Gavishti | "Search for cows" – war
  [17:0:5.2] Gopati | Lord of cattle – the chief
  [17:0:5.3] Gomat | Rich man (many cattle)
  [17:0:5.4] Godhuli | Time – dusk, when cattle return
  [17:0:5.5] Gavyuti | Distance
  [17:0:5.6] Duhitri | Daughter – "one who milks"
[17:0:6] Jana 275 times vs Gau 176 times.
## SUB 1 | Agriculture
[17:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [17:1:0.0] Yava | Barley – chief Rigvedic grain
  [17:1:0.1] Vrihi | Rice – not named in the Rigveda; important in Later Vedic texts
  [17:1:0.2] Godhuma | Wheat – chief crop only in Later Vedic period
  [17:1:0.3] Mudga | Mung bean (green gram)
  [17:1:0.4] Ikshu | Sugarcane
[17:1:1] - Rice, wheat, cotton – **not main Rigvedic crops**.
[17:1:2] RAS 2021 key matched **Yava → millet** (barley not an option); standard meaning = barley. Same key: Vrihi = rice, Mudga = mung, Ikshu = sugarcane.

## SUB 3 | Exchange, Trade and Tax
[17:3:0] - Trade limited, by **barter**; **no coinage** in the Rig Vedic age.
[17:3:1] - **Nishka**: Rigveda = **gold neck ornament** ('**Nishka-Griva**'), used in exchange; later = unit of value / **gold coin**.
[17:3:2] - **Panis**: **traders**, rich in cattle, greedy (hid cattle), non-Aryan (MPPSC 2019).
[17:3:3] TABLE head: Phase | Tax
  [17:3:3.0] Rig Vedic | Bali = voluntary gift to the rajan, plus war loot; no regular tax or collector
  [17:3:3.1] Later Vedic | Bali compulsory + bhaga (1/6 of produce) + shulka; collector = Bhagadugha
[17:3:4] - 'Stupa' word first in the Rigveda = "tree stem"; stupa raised by **Varuna** above the forest.
## SUB 4 | Family and Social Units
[17:4:0] - Family: **patriarchal, patrilineal, joint**; sons preferred, daughters treated kindly once born.
[17:4:1] - Compare: in the **Satavahana** period too, family and monarchy were patriarchal (kingship father to son) and women had a respectable position.
[17:4:2] - Ascending order: **Kula** (family) → **Grama** (village) → **Vis** (clan, vispati) → **Jana** (tribe).
[17:4:3] - **Janapada** never mentioned in the Rigveda.
[17:4:4] - Slavery existed (Dasa/Dasyu captives; slave women given to priests) – not the base of the economy.
## SUB 5 | Varna Origins

[17:5:2] - **Vaishya** grew from **vis** (mass of tribespeople).
[17:5:3] - **Arya varna vs Dasa varna**: cultural, linguistic (partly colour) distinction; **not rigid caste**. Broad groups: priests, warriors, common people.
[17:5:4] - **No untouchability**; gotra exogamy = Later Vedic.
## SUB 6 | Position of Women
[17:6:0] - Status **relatively high**: education, composed hymns, joined sacrifices, attended **Sabha and Vidatha**; could own **cattle** (no proof of land ownership).
[17:6:1] - Marriage: mostly **monogamous**; **adult marriage**, choice; **no child marriage**; widow remarriage.
[17:6:2] - **Niyoga** = childless widow's union with her **husband's brother** (levirate).

  [17:6:3.2] Maitreyi | Later women scholar

[17:6:5] - Later Vedic decline: barred from **Upanayana**, rituals, assemblies.
[17:6:6] Gargi is **not** Rigvedic (BPSC 2025 odd-one-out).
