=== Mother Goddess, Pashupati and Male Deities
# Mother Goddess – the chief female deity
- **Mother Goddess (Devi Mata):** terracotta female figurines, heavily jewelled, with fan-shaped head-dresses → **fertility cult**; probably the chief female deity (RAS 1993; SSC 2012: Devi Mata = **Indus Valley Civilisation**).
- **Sites:** Harappa, Mohenjodaro, Kot Diji, Kalibangan, Banawali (with toy plough) and Gujarat sites; not a feature of Ropar (known for the dog burial).
- **Continuity:** the Mother Goddess cult survives in later **Shakti cults**.
- **⚠ Trap:** "Spirit and Brahma", "ritual" and "sacrificial system" are Vedic ideas – the Indus answer is **Mother Goddess**.
# Pashupati seal – the chief male deity
| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Site | **Mohenjodaro** (steatite seal) |
| Figure | **Three-faced**, horned head-dress, seated in a **yogic posture** |
| Animals around | **Elephant, tiger, rhinoceros, buffalo**; **two deer** below the seat – no lion |
| Identification | **Proto-Shiva / Pashupati** ("lord of animals") – **John Marshall** |
| Caution | Script undeciphered, so the reading is an inference from later Shiva worship |
- **Indus people worshipped Shiva** (proto-Shiva, UKPSC 2006) – Indra and Varuna are Rig Vedic gods; Brahma and Vishnu are later.
- **Both male and female deities** were worshipped (Mother Goddess + Pashupati) – the only correct statement in UPSC 2013 (no palaces or temples, no horse-drawn chariots) → **"only 2"**.
# Idol worship
- **Idol worship began in the pre-Aryan (Harappan) period:** no temple has been found, yet idolatry was practised through figurines and seals (UPPCS 1992). The Rig Vedic Aryans had **no idols or temples**.
> Exam angle: "people of the Indus Valley believed in …" → Mother Goddess; "beginning of idol worship" → pre-Aryan; "Shiva / Rudra" → Pashupati.

=== Tree, Animal and Bird Worship; Fertility Symbols
# Sacred trees, animals and birds
- **Pipal:** the tree most often depicted on Indus seals (a deity standing among pipal branches) – MPPSC 2021; the pipal motif goes back to the **Early Harappan** phase and is still worshipped.
- **Pigeon (dove):** listed by NextGen among sacred symbols – the bird "worshipped" by the Harappans (APPSC Group-II 2011); the peacock is only a pottery motif.
- **Sacred animals:** the **unicorn** (mythical one-horned animal, the commonest seal motif), the **humped bull** (next in importance), elephant, tiger, buffalo, rhinoceros; cobras and horned deities also appear.
- **Animals round Pashupati** were probably worshipped too.
# Fertility and phallic symbols
- **Linga and yoni:** conical stones (lingas) and **round pierced stones** (yoni) – fertility objects, possibly early Shaivite elements; found at Harappa.
- **Swastika:** a seal motif, later an auspicious Hindu symbol – it has nothing to do with the Vedic yajna.
- **Seals:** mostly of **steatite**, mostly **square** – some with a hole used as **amulets**.
> Exam angle: "shape of which tree on Indus seals" → **pipal**; "bird worshipped" → **pigeon**; "mythical sacred animal" → **unicorn**.

=== Fire Altars, Great Bath and Ritual Practices
# Fire altars
| Site | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Kalibangan** (Rajasthan) | A row of **seven fire altars** on a mud-brick platform; **triangular terracotta cake** with a **horned deity** on one side and a **human pulling a goat by a rope** (animal sacrifice) on the other |
| **Lothal** (Gujarat) | Fire altar |
| Banawali, Rakhigarhi | Small pits with terracotta cakes regarded as fire altars |
- **Horned deity on a terracotta cake** → **Kalibangan** (State PSC) – not Banawali, Lothal or Surkotada.
- **⚠ Caution:** Harappan fire altars are **not conclusive proof of the later Vedic yajna**.
# Other ritual evidence
- **Great Bath (Mohenjodaro):** probably for **ritual purification** (bathing).
- **Amulets:** holed seals and amulets suggest belief in **ghosts and evil forces**; deities shown in **yogic poses** → practice of yoga and meditation.
- **Burials with grave goods** point to a belief in life after death (details: burial row).

=== Nature of Harappan Religion and Continuities
- **Character:** predominantly **secular** civilisation – religion present but not dominant; nature- and **fertility-oriented** worship.
- **No temple** has been identified anywhere; worship was domestic or open-air (under trees).
- **Undeciphered script:** every reading of Harappan religion (proto-Shiva, Mother Goddess) is inferred from later traditions.
- **Continuities into Hinduism:** Shiva/Pashupati, Shakti (Mother Goddess), linga-yoni, pipal and animal veneration, yoga.
- **Contrast with the Rig Veda:** Vedic religion = **male nature gods and open-air yajnas**, no idols; Harappan = Mother Goddess, idols, animal and tree worship.
- **Significance:** the earliest material evidence of a Shiva-like deity, a Mother Goddess and fertility cults in India.
