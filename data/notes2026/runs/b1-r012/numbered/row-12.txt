## SUB 0 | Mother Goddess, Pashupati and Male Deities
[0:0] # Mother Goddess – the chief female deity
[0:1] - **Mother Goddess (Devi Mata):** terracotta female figurines, heavily jewelled, with fan-shaped head-dresses → **fertility cult**; probably the chief female deity (RAS 1993; SSC 2012: Devi Mata = **Indus Valley Civilisation**).
[0:2] - **Sites:** Harappa, Mohenjodaro, Kot Diji, Kalibangan, Banawali (with toy plough) and Gujarat sites; not a feature of Ropar (known for the dog burial).
[0:3] - **Continuity:** the Mother Goddess cult survives in later **Shakti cults**.
[0:4] - **⚠ Trap:** "Spirit and Brahma", "ritual" and "sacrificial system" are Vedic ideas – the Indus answer is **Mother Goddess**.
[0:5] # Pashupati seal – the chief male deity
[0:6] TABLE head: Feature | Detail
  [0:6.0] Site | Mohenjodaro (steatite seal)
  [0:6.1] Figure | Three-faced, horned head-dress, seated in a yogic posture
  [0:6.2] Animals around | Elephant, tiger, rhinoceros, buffalo; two deer below the seat – no lion
  [0:6.3] Identification | Proto-Shiva / Pashupati ("lord of animals") – John Marshall
  [0:6.4] Caution | Script undeciphered, so the reading is an inference from later Shiva worship
[0:7] - **Indus people worshipped Shiva** (proto-Shiva, UKPSC 2006) – Indra and Varuna are Rig Vedic gods; Brahma and Vishnu are later.
[0:8] - **Both male and female deities** were worshipped (Mother Goddess + Pashupati) – the only correct statement in UPSC 2013 (no palaces or temples, no horse-drawn chariots) → **"only 2"**.
[0:9] # Idol worship
[0:10] - **Idol worship began in the pre-Aryan (Harappan) period:** no temple has been found, yet idolatry was practised through figurines and seals (UPPCS 1992). The Rig Vedic Aryans had **no idols or temples**.
[0:11] > Exam angle: "people of the Indus Valley believed in …" → Mother Goddess; "beginning of idol worship" → pre-Aryan; "Shiva / Rudra" → Pashupati.

## SUB 1 | Tree, Animal and Bird Worship; Fertility Symbols
[1:0] # Sacred trees, animals and birds
[1:1] - **Pipal:** the tree most often depicted on Indus seals (a deity standing among pipal branches) – MPPSC 2021; the pipal motif goes back to the **Early Harappan** phase and is still worshipped.
[1:2] - **Pigeon (dove):** listed by NextGen among sacred symbols – the bird "worshipped" by the Harappans (APPSC Group-II 2011); the peacock is only a pottery motif.
[1:3] - **Sacred animals:** the **unicorn** (mythical one-horned animal, the commonest seal motif), the **humped bull** (next in importance), elephant, tiger, buffalo, rhinoceros; cobras and horned deities also appear.
[1:4] - **Animals round Pashupati** were probably worshipped too.
[1:5] # Fertility and phallic symbols
[1:6] - **Linga and yoni:** conical stones (lingas) and **round pierced stones** (yoni) – fertility objects, possibly early Shaivite elements; found at Harappa.
[1:7] - **Swastika:** a seal motif, later an auspicious Hindu symbol – it has nothing to do with the Vedic yajna.
[1:8] - **Seals:** mostly of **steatite**, mostly **square** – some with a hole used as **amulets**.
[1:9] > Exam angle: "shape of which tree on Indus seals" → **pipal**; "bird worshipped" → **pigeon**; "mythical sacred animal" → **unicorn**.
[1:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.2, p.7; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.100; app notes 1:12 (sub 2)
## SUB 2 | Fire Altars, Great Bath and Ritual Practices
[2:0] # Fire altars
[2:1] TABLE head: Site | Evidence
  [2:1.0] Kalibangan (Rajasthan) | A row of seven fire altars on a mud-brick platform; triangular terracotta cake with a horned deity on one side and a human pulling a goat by a rope (animal sacrifice) on the other
  [2:1.1] Lothal (Gujarat) | Fire altar
  [2:1.2] Banawali, Rakhigarhi | Small pits with terracotta cakes regarded as fire altars
[2:2] - **Horned deity on a terracotta cake** → **Kalibangan** (State PSC) – not Banawali, Lothal or Surkotada.
[2:3] - **⚠ Caution:** Harappan fire altars are **not conclusive proof of the later Vedic yajna**.
[2:4] # Other ritual evidence
[2:5] - **Great Bath (Mohenjodaro):** probably for **ritual purification** (bathing).
[2:6] - **Amulets:** holed seals and amulets suggest belief in **ghosts and evil forces**; deities shown in **yogic poses** → practice of yoga and meditation.
[2:7] - **Burials with grave goods** point to a belief in life after death (details: burial row).
[2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.7, p.12-13; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.101; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.20-21; app notes 1:12 (sub 1, 3)
## SUB 3 | Nature of Harappan Religion and Continuities
[3:0] - **Character:** predominantly **secular** civilisation – religion present but not dominant; nature- and **fertility-oriented** worship.
[3:1] - **No temple** has been identified anywhere; worship was domestic or open-air (under trees).
[3:2] - **Undeciphered script:** every reading of Harappan religion (proto-Shiva, Mother Goddess) is inferred from later traditions.
[3:3] - **Continuities into Hinduism:** Shiva/Pashupati, Shakti (Mother Goddess), linga-yoni, pipal and animal veneration, yoga.
[3:4] - **Contrast with the Rig Veda:** Vedic religion = **male nature gods and open-air yajnas**, no idols; Harappan = Mother Goddess, idols, animal and tree worship.
[3:5] - **Significance:** the earliest material evidence of a Shiva-like deity, a Mother Goddess and fertility cults in India.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.100-101; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.7, p.28; app notes 1:12 (sub 0, 5)
