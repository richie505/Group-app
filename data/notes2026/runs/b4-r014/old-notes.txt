=== NOTES 4:14 | Atmosphere – Structure (layers) and Composition
## SUB 0 | Composition of the Atmosphere
[14:0:0] # Gases by Volume (dry air)
[14:0:1] TABLE head: Gas | Share | Type / role
  [14:0:1.0] Nitrogen | 78.08% (~78%) | Permanent; most abundant
  [14:0:1.1] Oxygen | 20.95% (~21%) | Permanent; respiration, combustion; negligible by ~120 km
  [14:0:1.2] Argon | 0.93% | Permanent, inert; third
  [14:0:1.3] Carbon dioxide | ~0.04% | Variable; photosynthesis, greenhouse; reaches ~90 km
  [14:0:1.4] Water vapour | 0–4% | Variable; most abundant GHG
  [14:0:1.5] Ozone | Trace | Variable; absorbs UV
[14:0:2] - Order: **N₂ > O₂ > Ar > CO₂** (APPSC-G1 2023). N₂ + O₂ = **99%**. N₂ ~78% (APPSC-GS 2025).
[14:0:3] - Trace gases: **neon, helium, xenon, hydrogen, krypton**. Xenon = "**Stranger Gas**" (Greek *xenos*), found by **Ramsay and Travers, 1898**.
[14:0:4] - Weather balloons are filled with **helium** (inert).
[14:0:5] - Greenhouse gases include **methane and nitrous oxide**; **not** hydrogen, krypton, neon, helium, argon.
[14:0:6] - Best CO₂ for natural balance: **0.03%**, kept within the troposphere.
[14:0:7] - **CO₂ 2024: 423.9 ppm**, up 3.5 ppm – largest rise since 1957; ~152% of pre-industrial (WMO Greenhouse Gas Bulletin, Oct 2025).
[14:0:8] Third place = **Argon**, not CO₂.
[14:0:9] # Variable Constituents and Distribution
[14:0:10] - **Water vapour**: up to **4%** in warm wet tropics; **below 1%** in deserts and polar air; falls with height; absorbs sunlight and Earth's heat (**blanket effect**); decides air stability; absorbs most infrared in the lower atmosphere.
[14:0:11] - **Dust**: sea salt, soil, smoke, ash, pollen, meteor dust; mostly in lower layers; **more in subtropical and temperate belts** (dry winds) than equatorial and polar; act as **hygroscopic nuclei** for condensation.
[14:0:12] - Near the surface: mainly nitrogen and oxygen; **hydrogen** (light) goes higher than N₂ and O₂.
[14:0:13] - **Early atmosphere**: **reducing**, little or no free oxygen.
[14:0:14] - Rain cleans a polluted atmosphere by washing out particles.
## SUB 1 | Greenhouse Effect
[14:1:0] - Atmosphere is **transparent to incoming short-wave solar** radiation but **absorbs outgoing long-wave terrestrial** radiation; it is not heated mainly by incoming sunlight.
[14:1:1] - CO₂ and other GHGs are good absorbers of long-wave (infrared) radiation.
## SUB 2 | Layers – Height, Temperature, Features
[14:2:0] - **Basis of division: change of temperature with height**; composition gives homosphere/heterosphere.
[14:2:1] - **99%** of mass lies within **32 km**.
[14:2:2] TABLE head: Layer | Height | Temperature | Key feature
  [14:2:2.0] Troposphere | 0 to 8 km (poles), 18 km (equator); avg 13 km | Falls | Weather; life; water vapour, dust
  [14:2:2.1] Stratosphere | to 50 km | Rises (inversion) | Ozone; calm, dry – jets
  [14:2:2.2] Mesosphere | 50–80 km | Falls to −100°C (coldest) | Meteors burn
  [14:2:2.3] Thermosphere (ionosphere) | from 80 km | Rises sharply | Aurora, airglow, radio-wave reflection
  [14:2:2.4] Exosphere | ~600–10,000 km | — | Outermost; light gases; merges into space; satellites
[14:2:3] - Ascending order: troposphere → stratosphere (15–50 km) → mesosphere → ionosphere (**80–640 km**) → exosphere.
[14:2:4] - Geostationary satellites (~**36,000 km**) lie in the **exosphere** (above 640 km).
## SUB 3 | Temperature Trend and Boundaries
[14:3:0] - Upward: troposphere **falls** → stratosphere **rises** (ozone absorbs UV) → mesosphere **falls** → thermosphere **rises** (high-energy radiation).
[14:3:1] - **Reversal (inversion) layers: stratosphere and thermosphere.**
[14:3:2] TABLE head: Boundary | Height
  [14:3:2.0] Tropopause | 8–18 km
  [14:3:2.1] Stratopause | ~50 km
  [14:3:2.2] Mesopause | ~80 km
## SUB 4 | Troposphere and Tropopause
[14:4:0] - **Normal lapse rate: 6.5°C per km** (**1°C per 165 m**).
[14:4:1] - **Thickest at equator** (16–18 km) – high insolation → strong **convection** lifts the tropopause; ~8 km at poles.
[14:4:2] - Almost all weather, clouds, storms; key for biological activity.
[14:4:3] - **Agra and Darjeeling**: same latitude; Darjeeling colder in January because of **altitude** (lapse rate).
[14:4:4] - **Tropopause**: boundary of troposphere and stratosphere; **higher at equator**, lower at poles; **higher in summer** than winter; caps weather and vertical mixing.
[14:4:5] - Tropopause temperature: **−80°C over equator**, **−45°C over poles**.
## SUB 5 | Homosphere vs Heterosphere
[14:5:0] TABLE head: Region | Height | Layers | Composition
  [14:5:0.0] Homosphere | 0 to ~90 km | Troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere | Gases evenly mixed
  [14:5:0.1] Heterosphere | Above ~90 km | Thermosphere, exosphere | Gases layered by molecular weight: N₂ → atomic O → He → H
[14:5:1] - Ionosphere is **not** in the homosphere.
## SUB 6 | Phenomena → Layer
[14:6:0] TABLE head: Phenomenon | Layer
  [14:6:0.0] Weather, clouds, rain | Troposphere
  [14:6:0.1] Ozone layer | Stratosphere (APPSC 2025)
  [14:6:0.2] Meteors burn | Mesosphere (APPSC-G2 2025)
  [14:6:0.3] Aurora, airglow | Thermosphere / ionosphere
  [14:6:0.4] Radio-wave reflection, wireless signals | Ionosphere
  [14:6:0.5] Jet aircraft | Lower stratosphere – no clouds, very dry
[14:6:1] - Aurora: solar charged particles excite upper-air particles; **Borealis = north**, **Australis = south**.
[14:6:2] Meteors burn in the mesosphere, not the thermosphere; aurora is ionospheric, not mesospheric.
## SUB 7 | Ionosphere – D, E, F Layers
[14:7:0] TABLE head: Layer | Height | Role
  [14:7:0.0] D | 60–90 km | Absorbs medium/long waves; fades at night
  [14:7:0.1] E (Kennelly-Heaviside) | 90–150 km | Reflects medium-frequency waves
  [14:7:0.2] F (Appleton); F1, F2 by day | 150–400 km | Main reflector of short-wave radio
[14:7:1] - Formed when solar UV and X-rays strip electrons; **expands by day, contracts at night**.
[14:7:2] - Solar flares and geomagnetic storms disrupt **GNSS** signals (9 Jul 2026).
## SUB 8 | Ozone Layer
[14:8:0] - O₃ = three oxygen atoms; mainly in the **lower stratosphere** (stratosphere ~10–50 km); main layer **15–30 km**, densest **15–35 km**; exam height also **15–20 km**.
[14:8:1] - About **90%** of ozone is stratospheric, 10% tropospheric.
[14:8:2] - Absorbs harmful **UV-B and UV-C** (APPSC 2025); stratosphere warms because ozone turns UV to heat.
[14:8:3] - **Unit: Dobson Unit (DU)** for column ozone.
[14:8:4] TABLE head: UV band | Wavelength
  [14:8:4.0] UV-A | 315–400 nm
  [14:8:4.1] UV-B | 280–315 nm
  [14:8:4.2] UV-C | 100–280 nm (shortest, most harmful)
[14:8:5] - **Chapman cycle**: O₂ + UV → O + O; O + O₂ → O₃; O₃ absorbs UV and splits.
[14:8:6] - Extra UV causes **cataract, skin cancer, weaker immunity, lower crop yield**.
[14:8:7] - **Ground-level ozone** = secondary pollutant from **NOx + VOCs** in sunlight (photochemical smog).
[14:8:8] Stratospheric ozone | shield | good
Ground-level ozone | pollutant | bad
[14:8:9] - **Heatwaves and ozone:** heatwaves raise **ground-level ozone**; north-west India and the Indo-Gangetic Plain are badly affected.
## SUB 9 | Ozone Depletion and Treaties
[14:9:0] - **Main cause: halocarbons** (CFCs, HCFCs) releasing **chlorine and bromine** (APPSC 2023). Maximum damage: **CFCs**.
[14:9:1] TABLE head: ODS | Use
  [14:9:1.0] CFCs | Refrigeration, ACs, aerosols, foam
  [14:9:1.1] HCFCs | CFC substitute – also deplete ozone
  [14:9:1.2] Halons | Fire extinguishers
  [14:9:1.3] Methyl bromide | Fumigant
  [14:9:1.4] Carbon tetrachloride, methyl chloroform | Solvents
[14:9:2] - **HFCs** spare ozone but have high global-warming potential; CFCs harm ozone and are strong GHGs.
[14:9:3] - **Antarctic ozone hole**: **Aug–Nov** (southern spring), aided by **polar stratospheric clouds**; reported in **1985** by a **British** team (Farman, Gardiner, Shanklin).
[14:9:4] - **2025 hole** peaked at just over **21 million sq km**, closed early; record **29 million sq km (2006)**.
[14:9:5] TABLE head: Treaty | Year | Point
  [14:9:5.0] Vienna Convention | 1985 | Framework: research, monitoring
  [14:9:5.1] Montreal Protocol | 1987 | Legally binding; ends ~100 ODS; 198 parties (197 states + EU) – universally ratified
  [14:9:5.2] Kigali Amendment | 2016 (Rwanda) | HFC phase-down 80–85%; in force 2019
[14:9:6] - India: **first developing country** to phase out **HCFC-141b** in rigid foam; HCFCs cut **44%** vs 35% target by Jan 2020.
[14:9:7] - AP: **Mission SANA** (Red Balloon Aerospace) – India's first home-made **stratospheric super-pressure balloon**, launched from **Vijayawada** (May 2026).
