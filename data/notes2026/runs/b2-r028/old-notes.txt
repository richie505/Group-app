=== NOTES 2:28 | Cabinet System, Cabinet Committees, Collective and Individual Responsibility; Attorney General of India
## SUB 0 | Parliamentary vs Presidential Form
[28:0:0] TABLE head: Parliamentary (India) | Presidential (USA)
  [28:0:0.0] Nominal (President) and real (PM) executives | President = head of State and government
  [28:0:0.1] Majority party rule | Fixed-term executive
  [28:0:0.2] Collective responsibility to Lok Sabha | No responsibility to legislature
  [28:0:0.3] Ministers are MPs (double membership) | Secretaries not members of Congress
  [28:0:0.4] PM's leadership; LS can be dissolved | No dissolution
  [28:0:0.5] Political homogeneity; secrecy | Separation of powers
[28:0:1] - Also: **cabinet/responsible government**, **Westminster model** (Britain, Japan, Canada, India).
[28:0:2] - **Defining feature**: **collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha** – not separation of powers or judicial review.
[28:0:3] - **Necessary features**: majority party rule + collective responsibility + PM as centre of power (complete separation of powers = presidential).
[28:0:4] - **Why chosen**: experience under the **GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935** and to avoid a **personality cult**.
[28:0:5] - **Underlying principle** of Cabinet form: a mechanism of parliamentary democracy ensuring **collective responsibility of the Government to the people**.
## SUB 1 | Council of Ministers vs Cabinet
[28:1:0] TABLE head: Council of Ministers | Cabinet
  [28:1:0.0] Wider body, 60–70 ministers of all ranks | Nucleus – Cabinet Ministers only
  [28:1:0.1] Constitutional – Arts 74–75 | Word only in Art 352(3); rest by convention
  [28:1:0.2] Rarely meets as a body | Meets regularly; takes policy decisions
  [28:1:0.3] Collectively responsible to LS | Directs and supervises the Council
[28:1:1] - Word **"Cabinet"** inserted in **Article 352(3)** by the **44th Amendment, 1978** – Emergency only on the **written advice of the Union Cabinet**; the only place it appears.
## SUB 2 | Collective Responsibility – Article 75(3)
[28:2:0] - Council collectively responsible **only to the Lok Sabha** – not RS, Parliament as a whole or the President. States: **Article 164(2)** – to the Legislative Assembly.
[28:2:1] - **"Sink or swim together"**: whole Council incl. PM resigns if a **no-confidence motion** passes (moved **only in LS**) or it loses majority.
[28:2:2] - **Budget** (treated as a **Money Bill**) defeated in LS → PM submits the Council's resignation.
[28:2:3] - RS ministers also answer to the LS; **defeat in the Rajya Sabha** does not affect survival.
[28:2:4] - **PM's death or resignation** dissolves the whole Council.
[28:2:5] - Cabinet decisions **bind all**; a dissenting minister must defend them or **resign**.
[28:2:6] - **Oath of secrecy** (Third Schedule, given by the President) keeps Cabinet talks secret → public unity.
## SUB 3 | Individual Responsibility – Article 75(2)
[28:3:0] - Ministers hold office during the **President's pleasure**; each answers to Parliament for lapses in his portfolio.
[28:3:1] - In practice the **PM** can ask a minister to resign or advise the President to dismiss him.
[28:3:2] - **Personal statement**: a resigned minister may explain his resignation in the House with the **Speaker's consent**.
[28:3:3] Article 75(3) | Collective | To Lok Sabha
Article 75(2) | Individual | President's pleasure

## SUB 6 | Cabinet Secretariat and Cabinet Secretary
[28:6:0] - **Cabinet Secretariat** works **directly under the PM**; serves Cabinet and committees; administers the Business Rules.
[28:6:1] - **Cabinet Secretary**: **senior-most civil servant**; "first among equals" among Secretaries; **11th** in the Table of Precedence; chairs the Civil Services Board; heads the **National Crisis Management Committee (NCMC)**.
[28:6:2] - Appointed by the **ACC** for **2 years**, extendable up to **4**.
[28:6:3] - **6 Aug 2026**: **T.V. Somanathan** given a one-year extension beyond 30 Aug 2026; Home Secretary **Govind Mohan** extended up to 22.8.2027.
[28:6:4] - State equivalent: **Chief Secretary** – every file goes to the Cabinet through him; **G. Sai Prasad** took charge as **AP Chief Secretary** (Feb 2026).
## SUB 7 | Attorney General – Article 76
[28:7:0] - **Highest law officer**; appointed by the **President**; must be **qualified to be a Supreme Court judge**; remuneration as the **President determines** (Art 76(4)).
[28:7:1] - Advises GoI on legal matters; does duties referred by the **President** (not a Governor).
[28:7:2] - **No fixed term**; holds office at the **President's pleasure**; by convention resigns when the government changes.
[28:7:3] - **Audience in all courts**.
[28:7:4] - **Article 88**: speaks in either House, joint sittings and committees (can be named a member) – **cannot vote**. **Article 105(4)**: MPs' privileges.
[28:7:5] - **Limits**: not in the Cabinet; not a full-time servant – **private practice allowed**, but cannot advise or brief **against GoI**, defend accused in criminal cases or become a company director without permission.
[28:7:6] - First AG: **M.C. Setalvad**. **R. Venkataramani** – AG since 1 Oct 2022 (after K.K. Venugopal); re-appointed for 2 years from **1 Oct 2025**.
[28:7:7] AG's right in Parliament = **Article 88**, not 78.

=== NOTES 2:29 | Types of Legislatures – Unicameral and Bicameral; Arguments for A Second Chamber
## SUB 0 | Unicameral vs Bicameral
[29:0:0] - **Unicameral** = one House; **bicameral** = two Houses.
[29:0:1] - Unicameral: **China** (National People's Congress – **2,977** members, biggest Parliament, a rubber stamp of the Communist Party), **Israel** (Knesset), New Zealand, Sweden, Denmark.
[29:0:2] - Bicameralism at the Centre first by the **GoI Act, 1919** (Council of State + Central Legislative Assembly); **1935 Act** – in some provinces.
[29:0:3] - **Article 79**: Parliament = **President + Council of States + House of the People**; the VP (RS Chairman, a non-member) and State Councils are not part of it.

[29:2:2] - **N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar** (Constituent Assembly): RS as a House of **dignified debate** delaying hasty legislation.
## SUB 3 | Rajya Sabha As Federal Chamber

[29:3:4] Rajya Sabha is neither dissolved nor abolished; Lok Sabha and Assemblies can be dissolved, not abolished; State Councils can be abolished (Art 169), not dissolved.
## SUB 4 | Bicameralism in States
[29:4:0] - **Part VI, Articles 168–212**. Unicameral = Governor + Assembly; bicameral = Governor + Assembly + **Council**.
[29:4:1] - **6 bicameral States**: **Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh**. Largest Council: **UP (100)**.
[29:4:2] - No Council: **Rajasthan**, **Tamil Nadu**, Goa, Mizoram, Sikkim, **Madhya Pradesh** (allowed by the **7th Amendment, 1956**, never created).
[29:4:3] - **Art 169**: Parliament creates/abolishes a Council **by law** (not an Art 368 amendment) after an Assembly resolution by a **majority of total membership + two-thirds present and voting**.
[29:4:4] - **Art 171**: Council not more than **one-third** of Assembly; minimum **40**.
