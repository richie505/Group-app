## SUB 0 | Attorney General of India – Article 76 and Article 88
[0:0] - **Article 76**: office of the **Attorney General (AG) for India** – the **highest (first) law officer** of the country; appointed by the **President** (not by Parliament, the Speaker, the PM or on Parliament's recommendation). Not Arts. 77, 78 or 79.
[0:1] - **Qualification**: a person **qualified to be appointed a Judge of the Supreme Court** – a citizen who has been a High Court judge for 5 years, or a High Court advocate for 10 years, or an eminent jurist in the President's opinion.
[0:2] - **Term**: **not fixed** by the Constitution; holds office **during the pleasure of the President** (not the PM, not "for life", not "six years", not "as long as Parliament has confidence"). No procedure or grounds of removal are laid down, so he is **not removed like a Supreme Court judge**. By convention he **resigns when the government changes**.
[0:3] - **Remuneration**: such as the **President may determine** (Art. 76(4)) – not Parliament.
[0:4] - **Duties** (Art. 76(2)): **advise the Government of India on legal matters** and perform duties of a legal character **referred or assigned by the President** – not a Governor, the CJI or the Law Commission. He appears for the Government in the Supreme Court – the Government's **chief legal adviser and primary lawyer**.
[0:5] - **Right of audience** (Art. 76(3)): in **all courts in the territory of India**.
[0:6] - **Article 88**: though **not a member**, he may **speak and take part** in the proceedings of **either House**, a **joint sitting** and any **committee** of which he is named a member – **without a right to vote**. So he is the non-member who can participate in Lok Sabha proceedings (not the CJI, the Vice-President, the Solicitor General, the CAG or a Governor).
[0:7] - **Article 105(4)**: he enjoys **all the privileges and immunities of an MP** – "not entitled to MP privileges" is false.
[0:8] - **Limits**: not a member of the Cabinet; not a full-time government servant – **private practice allowed**, but he cannot advise or brief **against the Government of India**, defend accused persons in criminal cases without permission, or become a company director without permission.
[0:9] TABLE head: Item | Attorney General | Solicitor General | Advocate General (State)
  [0:9.0] Basis | Art. 76 | Not constitutional (statutory / Law Officers Rules) | Art. 165
  [0:9.1] Appointed by | President | Appointments Committee of the Cabinet | Governor
  [0:9.2] Tenure | President's pleasure | Fixed at appointment (usually 3 years) | Governor's pleasure
  [0:9.3] Rights in the House | Art. 88 (no vote) | None | Art. 177 (no vote)
  [0:9.4] Rank | First law officer | Second law officer – assists the AG | State's legal adviser
  [0:9.5] First holder | M.C. Setalvad | C.K. Daphtary | –
[0:10] - **R. Venkataramani** – AG since **1 October 2022** (after K.K. Venugopal); re-appointed for **two years from 1 October 2025** (as of 2026).
[0:11] - AP Animal Husbandry 2023 statements (SC-judge qualification; advise GoI and duties assigned by the President; audience in all courts) – **all three correct**, so "incorrect" = **None of these**.

## SUB 1 | Collective Responsibility – Article 75(3) and the Parliamentary Principle
[1:0] - **Article 75(3)**: the Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** – not to the Rajya Sabha, Parliament as a whole or the President. States: **Art. 164(2)** (to the Legislative Assembly).
[1:1] - **Collective responsibility** is the feature that marks India as a **parliamentary (cabinet / responsible / Westminster) system** – not separation of powers, rule of law or judicial review.
[1:2] - UPSC 2017: the principle underlying the Cabinet form of government = **a mechanism of parliamentary democracy for ensuring collective responsibility of the Government to the people** (not minimising criticism, speeding up work or strengthening the head of government).
[1:3] - Features of the parliamentary system: **nominal and real executives**, **majority party rule**, **collective responsibility**, **double membership** (ministers are MPs), **PM's leadership**, **dissolution of the Lok Sabha**. "**Election of the whole Parliament every 5 years**" is **not** a feature – the **Rajya Sabha is permanent** (one-third retire every 2 years).
[1:4] TABLE head: Event | Consequence
  [1:4.0] No-confidence motion passed (moved only in the Lok Sabha) | Whole Council, incl. RS ministers, resigns – "sink or swim together"
  [1:4.1] Union Budget / Money Bill defeated in the Lok Sabha | Loss of confidence – the PM submits the resignation of the Council of Ministers (not just the Finance Minister; the Budget is not sent to the RS or re-presented)
  [1:4.2] Government defeated in the Rajya Sabha | None – no resignation, no dissolution, no President's Rule
  [1:4.3] PM dies or resigns | Council of Ministers dissolves
  [1:4.4] A minister disagrees with a Cabinet decision | Must accept and defend it or resign
[1:5] - UPPCS 1993 A-R: "Ministers make policy and civil servants adhere to it" (true) and "parliamentary government works on cabinet collective responsibility" (true), but collective responsibility (ministers to the Lok Sabha) does **not explain** why civil servants carry out policy – **both true, R not the correct explanation**.
[1:6] - **Oath of secrecy** (Third Schedule, administered by the **President**) keeps Cabinet deliberations confidential and the ministry united in public.
[1:7] - India chose the parliamentary form from experience of the **GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935** and to avoid a personality cult.
[1:8] > Exam angle: "responsible to whom?" → Lok Sabha; Budget defeat → whole ministry resigns; RS defeat → nothing.

## SUB 2 | Cabinet and Council of Ministers; Individual Responsibility – Article 75(2)
[2:0] TABLE head: Council of Ministers | Cabinet
  [2:0.0] Wider body – Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers | Nucleus – PM and Cabinet-rank Ministers
  [2:0.1] Constitutional – Arts. 74-75 | Word only in Art. 352(3); rest by convention
  [2:0.2] Rarely meets as a body | Meets regularly; takes policy decisions
  [2:0.3] Collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha | Directs and supervises the Council
[2:1] - The word **"Cabinet"** occurs **only once** – in **Article 352(3)** (National Emergency only on the **written advice of the Union Cabinet**), inserted by the **44th Amendment Act, 1978** (not the 42nd, 43rd or 52nd).
[2:2] - The Council of Ministers does **not include the Cabinet Secretary** – he is a civil servant, not a minister. Ministers without portfolio and Ministers of State are ministers.
[2:3] - The Constitution does not classify ministers into ranks – that is parliamentary practice.
[2:4] - **Article 75(2)** – **individual responsibility**: a Union Minister holds office **during the pleasure of the President** (not the PM, the party president or the Leader of the House). In practice the **PM** can ask a minister to resign or advise the President to dismiss him.
[2:5] - A minister who resigns may explain it in the House by a **personal statement** with the **Speaker's consent**.

## SUB 3 | Cabinet Secretariat, Cabinet Secretary and Chief Secretary
[3:0] - **Cabinet Secretariat** works **directly under the PM**; administers the Business Rules. Functions: **preparation of the agenda for Cabinet meetings**, recording and circulating decisions, **secretarial assistance to Cabinet Committees**, inter-ministerial coordination – **not allocation of financial resources to Ministries** (UPSC 2014 → **1 and 2 only**).
[3:1] - **Cabinet Secretary**: the **senior-most / highest civil servant (bureaucrat)** of the Union – not the Home Secretary, the PM's Secretary, the Lok Sabha Secretary-General, a Chief Secretary or the Attorney General (a law officer, not a civil servant). "First among equals" among Secretaries; **11th** in the Table of Precedence; chairs the **Civil Services Board**.
[3:2] - He heads the **National Crisis Management Committee (NCMC)** for disaster management – not the Home Secretary, Defence Secretary or Central Relief Commissioner.
[3:3] - Appointed by the **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet** for **2 years**, extendable up to a total of **4 years**.
[3:4] - **6 Aug 2026**: Cabinet Secretary **T.V. Somanathan** given a one-year extension beyond 30 Aug 2026; Home Secretary **Govind Mohan** extended up to 22.8.2027.
[3:5] - **State**: the **Chief Secretary** is the State equivalent – in the State Secretariat **every file goes to the Cabinet through the Chief Secretary** (not a Departmental, Deputy or Under Secretary). **G. Sai Prasad** took charge as **AP Chief Secretary** (Feb 2026).

## SUB 5 | Cabinet in Practice – Kitchen Cabinet, Shadow Cabinet, Coalitions
[5:0] - **Shadow cabinet**: a cabinet formed by the **main opposition party**, its members assigned to "shadow" (watch) particular ministries – a **British (UK)** institution; India has no formal counterpart. Not the PM's inner circle or ministers awaiting promotion.
[5:1] - **Kitchen cabinet**: the PM's **informal inner circle** of trusted colleagues and friends (ministers and non-ministers); criticised as an **extra-constitutional power centre** that may bypass the Cabinet.
[5:2] - **Single-party majority** eras → criticism of "**cabinet dictatorship**" over Parliament; **coalition era (1989–2014)** → coordination committees and allies' demands weakened Cabinet cohesion and collective responsibility.
