## SUB 0 | Bicameral States and Legislative Councils – Articles 168, 169, 171
[0:0] - Part VI, **Articles 168–212** – State Legislature. Unicameral = Governor + Assembly; bicameral = **Governor + Legislative Assembly + Legislative Council**.
[0:1] - **Bicameral States now (2026): 6** – **Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh**. Before the **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019** there were **7** (J&K too – its Council of **36** members was abolished in 2019). So APPSC Group-II 2017 "how many bicameral States?" = **7** then, **6** now.
[0:2] - No Council: **West Bengal** (abolished 1969), **Tamil Nadu** (abolished 1986), Rajasthan, Goa, Mizoram, Sikkim and **Madhya Pradesh** – the **7th Amendment, 1956** provided a Council for MP but it was **never created**.
[0:3] - Largest Council: **UP (100)**.
[0:4] - **Art. 168(1)(a)** names the bicameral States (AP and Telangana included); **Art. 169** lets **Parliament create or abolish** a Council **by ordinary law** (not an Art. 368 amendment) after the Assembly passes a resolution by a **majority of total membership + two-thirds present and voting**. The AP Council (abolished 1985) was **revived in 2007** under Art. 169; AP's 2020 resolution to abolish it again was **withdrawn in 2021**.
[0:5] - **Art. 171**: Council not more than **one-third** of the Assembly and **not less than 40**.
[0:6] - Composition: 1/3 by MLAs, 1/3 by local authorities, 1/12 graduates, 1/12 teachers, 1/6 nominated by the Governor.
[0:7] TABLE head: House | Dissolved? | Abolished?
  [0:7.0] Lok Sabha / State Assembly | Yes | No
  [0:7.1] Rajya Sabha | No | No
  [0:7.2] State Legislative Council | No (permanent) | Yes – Art. 169
[0:8] - **Art. 74 / 163** – Union / State Council of Ministers; **Art. 80** – Council of States.
[0:9] > Exam angle: "which State is NOT bicameral" (WB, TN, MP); Art. 169; minimum 40; "cannot be dissolved but can be abolished" = Legislative Council.

## SUB 1 | Unicameral and Bicameral Legislatures – India and the World
[1:0] - **Unicameral** = one House; **bicameral** = two Houses. India's Parliament is **bicameral** – **Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha** (not "federal" or "presidential" legislature).
[1:1] - **Article 79**: Parliament = **President + Council of States + House of the People**; the Vice-President (RS Chairman, a non-member) and State Councils are not part of it.
[1:2] - Bicameralism at the Centre first by the **GoI Act, 1919**; the **1935 Act** brought it to some provinces.
[1:3] TABLE head: Country | Legislature | Type / Houses
  [1:3.0] Japan | Diet (National Diet) | House of Councillors (upper), House of Representatives
  [1:3.1] Israel | Knesset | Unicameral
  [1:3.2] China | National People's Congress | Unicameral; 2,977 members – the biggest; rubber stamp of the Communist Party
  [1:3.3] Sweden | Riksdag | Unicameral
  [1:3.4] Spain | Cortes Generales | Bicameral
  [1:3.5] Poland | Sejm (with Senate) | Bicameral
  [1:3.6] UK | Parliament | House of Lords, House of Commons
  [1:3.7] USA | Congress | Senate, House of Representatives
  [1:3.8] Russia | Federal Assembly | Federation Council (upper), State Duma (lower)

## SUB 2 | Legislatures and Representation of Union Territories
[2:0] - UTs are represented in **both Houses**: Lok Sabha seats for UTs; in the Rajya Sabha only **Delhi (3), Puducherry (1) and Jammu and Kashmir (4)** elect members.
[2:1] TABLE head: UT | Basis | Assembly
  [2:1.0] Delhi | Art. 239AA (69th Amdt, 1991) | 70
  [2:1.1] Puducherry | Art. 239A | 30 elected + up to 3 nominated
  [2:1.2] Jammu and Kashmir | J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019 | 90 elected; 24 seats kept vacant for Pakistan-occupied Kashmir
[2:2] - APPSC Group-I 2003: UTs with their own legislatures = **Pondicherry and Delhi** – not Chandigarh or Daman and Diu (J&K joined them in 2019).
[2:3] - **17 Apr 2026**: **UT Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026** extends women's reservation to the Delhi, Puducherry and J&K legislatures.
