=== Attorney General of India – Article 76 and Article 88
- **Article 76**: office of the **Attorney General (AG) for India** – the **highest (first) law officer** of the country; appointed by the **President** (not by Parliament, the Speaker, the PM or on Parliament's recommendation). Not Arts. 77, 78 or 79.
- **Qualification**: a person **qualified to be appointed a Judge of the Supreme Court** – a citizen who has been a High Court judge for 5 years, or a High Court advocate for 10 years, or an eminent jurist in the President's opinion.
- **Term**: **not fixed** by the Constitution; holds office **during the pleasure of the President** (not the PM, not "for life", not "six years", not "as long as Parliament has confidence"). No procedure or grounds of removal are laid down, so he is **not removed like a Supreme Court judge**. By convention he **resigns when the government changes**.
- **Remuneration**: such as the **President may determine** (Art. 76(4)) – not Parliament.
- **Duties** (Art. 76(2)): **advise the Government of India on legal matters** and perform duties of a legal character **referred or assigned by the President** – not a Governor, the CJI or the Law Commission. He appears for the Government in the Supreme Court – the Government's **chief legal adviser and primary lawyer**.
- **Right of audience** (Art. 76(3)): in **all courts in the territory of India**.
- **Article 88**: though **not a member**, he may **speak and take part** in the proceedings of **either House**, a **joint sitting** and any **committee** of which he is named a member – **without a right to vote**. So he is the non-member who can participate in Lok Sabha proceedings (not the CJI, the Vice-President, the Solicitor General, the CAG or a Governor).
- **Article 105(4)**: he enjoys **all the privileges and immunities of an MP** – "not entitled to MP privileges" is false.
- **Limits**: not a member of the Cabinet; not a full-time government servant – **private practice allowed**, but he cannot advise or brief **against the Government of India**, defend accused persons in criminal cases without permission, or become a company director without permission.

|---|---|---|---|

| Appointed by | **President** | **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet** | **Governor** |
| Tenure | President's pleasure | Fixed at appointment (usually 3 years) | Governor's pleasure |

| Rank | **First law officer** | **Second law officer** – assists the AG | State's legal adviser |

=== Collective Responsibility – Article 75(3) and the Parliamentary Principle
- **Article 75(3)**: the Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** – not to the Rajya Sabha, Parliament as a whole or the President. States: **Art. 164(2)** (to the Legislative Assembly).
- **Collective responsibility** is the feature that marks India as a **parliamentary (cabinet / responsible / Westminster) system** – not separation of powers, rule of law or judicial review.
- UPSC 2017: the principle underlying the Cabinet form of government = **a mechanism of parliamentary democracy for ensuring collective responsibility of the Government to the people** (not minimising criticism, speeding up work or strengthening the head of government).
- Features of the parliamentary system: **nominal and real executives**, **majority party rule**, **collective responsibility**, **double membership** (ministers are MPs), **PM's leadership**, **dissolution of the Lok Sabha**. "**Election of the whole Parliament every 5 years**" is **not** a feature – the **Rajya Sabha is permanent** (one-third retire every 2 years).
| Event | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **No-confidence motion** passed (moved **only in the Lok Sabha**) | Whole Council, incl. RS ministers, resigns – "**sink or swim together**" |
| **Union Budget / Money Bill defeated in the Lok Sabha** | Loss of confidence – the **PM submits the resignation of the Council of Ministers** (not just the Finance Minister; the Budget is not sent to the RS or re-presented) |
| **Government defeated in the Rajya Sabha** | **None** – no resignation, no dissolution, no President's Rule |
| **PM dies or resigns** | Council of Ministers dissolves |
| A minister disagrees with a Cabinet decision | Must **accept and defend it or resign** |
- UPPCS 1993 A-R: "Ministers make policy and civil servants adhere to it" (true) and "parliamentary government works on cabinet collective responsibility" (true), but collective responsibility (ministers to the Lok Sabha) does **not explain** why civil servants carry out policy – **both true, R not the correct explanation**.
- **Oath of secrecy** (Third Schedule, administered by the **President**) keeps Cabinet deliberations confidential and the ministry united in public.
- India chose the parliamentary form from experience of the **GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935** and to avoid a personality cult.
> Exam angle: "responsible to whom?" → Lok Sabha; Budget defeat → whole ministry resigns; RS defeat → nothing.

=== Cabinet and Council of Ministers; Individual Responsibility – Article 75(2)
| Council of Ministers | Cabinet |
|---|---|
| Wider body – Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers | Nucleus – PM and Cabinet-rank Ministers |
| Constitutional – **Arts. 74-75** | Word only in **Art. 352(3)**; rest by convention |
| Rarely meets as a body | Meets regularly; takes policy decisions |
| Collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha | Directs and supervises the Council |
- The word **"Cabinet"** occurs **only once** – in **Article 352(3)** (National Emergency only on the **written advice of the Union Cabinet**), inserted by the **44th Amendment Act, 1978** (not the 42nd, 43rd or 52nd).
- The Council of Ministers does **not include the Cabinet Secretary** – he is a civil servant, not a minister. Ministers without portfolio and Ministers of State are ministers.
- The Constitution does not classify ministers into ranks – that is parliamentary practice.
- **Article 75(2)** – **individual responsibility**: a Union Minister holds office **during the pleasure of the President** (not the PM, the party president or the Leader of the House). In practice the **PM** can ask a minister to resign or advise the President to dismiss him.
- A minister who resigns may explain it in the House by a **personal statement** with the **Speaker's consent**.

=== Cabinet Secretariat, Cabinet Secretary and Chief Secretary
- **Cabinet Secretariat** works **directly under the PM**; administers the Business Rules. Functions: **preparation of the agenda for Cabinet meetings**, recording and circulating decisions, **secretarial assistance to Cabinet Committees**, inter-ministerial coordination – **not allocation of financial resources to Ministries** (UPSC 2014 → **1 and 2 only**).
- **Cabinet Secretary**: the **senior-most / highest civil servant (bureaucrat)** of the Union – not the Home Secretary, the PM's Secretary, the Lok Sabha Secretary-General, a Chief Secretary or the Attorney General (a law officer, not a civil servant). "First among equals" among Secretaries; **11th** in the Table of Precedence; chairs the **Civil Services Board**.
- He heads the **National Crisis Management Committee (NCMC)** for disaster management – not the Home Secretary, Defence Secretary or Central Relief Commissioner.
- Appointed by the **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet** for **2 years**, extendable up to a total of **4 years**.
- **6 Aug 2026**: Cabinet Secretary **T.V. Somanathan** given a one-year extension beyond 30 Aug 2026; Home Secretary **Govind Mohan** extended up to 22.8.2027.
- **State**: the **Chief Secretary** is the State equivalent – in the State Secretariat **every file goes to the Cabinet through the Chief Secretary** (not a Departmental, Deputy or Under Secretary). **G. Sai Prasad** took charge as **AP Chief Secretary** (Feb 2026).

=== Cabinet Committees, Business Rules and Reports on the Machinery of Government
- **Cabinet Committees**: **extra-constitutional**; set up by the **PM** under the **Transaction of Business Rules, 1961** (framed under **Art. 77(3)**); standing or ad hoc; usually chaired by the PM or a senior minister; decisions reviewable by the Cabinet.

=== Cabinet in Practice – Kitchen Cabinet, Shadow Cabinet, Coalitions
- **Shadow cabinet**: a cabinet formed by the **main opposition party**, its members assigned to "shadow" (watch) particular ministries – a **British (UK)** institution; India has no formal counterpart. Not the PM's inner circle or ministers awaiting promotion.
- **Kitchen cabinet**: the PM's **informal inner circle** of trusted colleagues and friends (ministers and non-ministers); criticised as an **extra-constitutional power centre** that may bypass the Cabinet.
- **Single-party majority** eras → criticism of "**cabinet dictatorship**" over Parliament; **coalition era (1989–2014)** → coordination committees and allies' demands weakened Cabinet cohesion and collective responsibility.
