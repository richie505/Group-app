## SUB 0 | GIS – Concept, Components and Uses
[45:0:0] - **GIS = Geographic Information System**: a computer system to **store, manipulate, analyse and present** all types of geographical (**spatial**) data – the most complete system for modelling spatial data and community vulnerability.
[45:0:1] - **Father of GIS:** **Roger Tomlinson** – coined the term for the Canada GIS (1960s).
[45:0:2] - Each theme is a **layer**; **overlay analysis** combines layers; data = spatial (vector, raster) + attribute tables.
[45:0:3] TABLE head: Component | Examples
  [45:0:3.0] Hardware | Computer, digitizer (tablet/table converting analog data to digital), scanner, GPS receiver, plotter
  [45:0:3.1] Software | QGIS – free, open-source, cross-platform desktop GIS
  [45:0:3.2] Data | Spatial (points, lines, polygons; satellite images) + attributes
[45:0:4] - **Remote sensing + GIS:** maps prepared from satellite data or aerial photos are digitised and stored in GIS; manual maps are slow to prepare and hard to update.
[45:0:5] - **Decision Support System:** data systems for planning.
[45:0:6] - **Survey of India** prepares India's **topographical maps**.
[45:0:7] # GIS in disasters
[45:0:8] - **Situational awareness map:** real-time weather, hospitals and shelters, damage imagery; **pre-disaster population density maps** are least useful in the live response.
[45:0:9] - Real-time sources: satellite imagery, weather data, sensor networks – **not news channels**.
[45:0:10] TABLE head: Remote sensing | GIS
  [45:0:10.0] Rainfall estimation during a cyclone; post-disaster damage | Mapping spread of infectious diseases; landslide hazard zonation
[45:0:11] - Earthquake **magnitude and epicentre** come from **seismographs**, not GIS.

## SUB 3 | Remote Sensing Basics and Spectral Indices
[45:3:0] - **Remote sensing:** gathering **images**/information about Earth **without physical contact**, from reflected or emitted radiation; covers dangerous or inaccessible areas.
[45:3:1] TABLE head: Type | Energy | Example
  [45:3:1.0] Active | Sends its own signal | RISAT radar, LiDAR
  [45:3:1.1] Passive | Natural radiation (sunlight) | Resourcesat, Oceansat
[45:3:2] - **Microwave / SAR** sees through cloud and darkness; **soil moisture** best measured by microwave/SAR.
[45:3:3] - **InSAR:** compares the **phase** of radar images from **orbiting** satellites → **ground deformation** (quakes, subsidence, landslides).
[45:3:4] - **NISAR:** radar, **L-band and S-band**; centimetre-scale ground movement.
[45:3:5] - Applications: land cover, vegetation, forests, **surface and groundwater**, snow, wetlands, **minerals and hydrocarbons**, crop area and **crop yield** – "all of the above".
[45:3:6] - Soil nutrient testing by **chemical analysis is NOT remote sensing**.
[45:3:7] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [45:3:7.0] NDVI | Normalized Difference Vegetation Index = (NIR − Red)/(NIR + Red); healthy vegetation reflects near-infrared; −1 to +1; most used for drought
  [45:3:7.1] VCI | From NDVI; drought declaration
  [45:3:7.2] Ecology index | Vegetation, water content, biological quantity – not cost and production
  [45:3:7.3] NH Green Cover Index 2025-26 | NHAI + NRSC; satellite green cover of national highways
[45:3:8] - **National Carbon Project** (ISRO-GBP): **carbon pools, fluxes and net carbon balance** of the terrestrial biosphere – vegetation pools, soil pools, fluxes.

## SUB 0 | Space Situational Awareness – NETRA, IS4OM and Digantara
[46:0:0] # Space Situational Awareness (SSA)
[46:0:1] - **SSA:** the capability to **detect, track, monitor and predict** the movement of objects in space (satellites, debris); used for collision avoidance and space security.
[46:0:2] TABLE head: System | Fact
  [46:0:2.0] Project NETRA (Network for Space Object Tracking and Analysis) | ISRO's indigenous SSA system; aim = safeguard Indian space assets from debris and collisions; 24–72 hours' warning
  [46:0:2.1] SSA Control Centre | foundation stone laid by ISRO in August 2019 at Bengaluru (Peenya, ISTRAC campus); the NETRA control centre
  [46:0:2.2] IS4OM | ISRO System for Safe and Sustainable Space Operations Management; dedicated to the nation in 2022; compiles the annual ISSAR
  [46:0:2.3] Digantara Research and Technologies | private SSA start-up based in Bengaluru; authorised by IN-SPACe on 24 June 2022 to launch its payloads; space-surveillance satellites, debris tracking

[46:0:4] > NETRA is asked twice (APPSC JL 2020, DAO 2020): agency **ISRO** (not RBI, ICSSR, ICHR) and aim **safeguard Indian space assets** (not a space station, Gaganyaan or the Vikram lander search).

## SUB 1 | Space Debris and Re-entry
[46:1:0] # Space debris
[46:1:1] - **Space debris:** all **non-functional man-made objects**, including fragments, in orbit or re-entering (defunct satellites, spent rocket stages, collision and explosion fragments, paint flakes).
[46:1:2] - **Main source = fragmentation** from collisions and in-orbit explosions; **ASAT tests** add debris (China 2007; **Mission Shakti**, DRDO, 27 Mar 2019, Microsat-R at ~300 km).
[46:1:3] - Over **1.2 million objects > 1 cm** (ESA 2025); objects move at ~7–8 km/s in LEO.
[46:1:4] - **Kessler Syndrome** (Donald Kessler, NASA, **1978**): debris density → collisions → more fragments → a self-sustaining cascade that can make orbits unusable.
[46:1:5] - **MMOD** (micrometeoroids and orbital debris) is the top risk in **LEO**, not GEO.
[46:1:6] # Controlled re-entry
[46:1:7] - **POEM-4** (PSLV Orbital Experimental Module of **PSLV-C60 / SpaDeX**): re-entered and impacted the **Indian Ocean on 4 April 2025** (ISRO: controlled re-entry).
[46:1:8] - **Mir** (Russian station, ~140 t): de-orbited in a controlled re-entry over the Pacific on **23 March 2001** after ~15 years, having made **86,331 orbits**.

## SUB 2 | Space Law – Treaties and the Space Activities Bill
[46:2:0] TABLE head: Instrument | Core rule | India
  [46:2:0.0] Outer Space Treaty 1967 | No national sovereignty; Art. VI national responsibility incl. private actors; Art. VII liability; Art. IX due regard | Party (ratified 1982)
  [46:2:0.1] Rescue Agreement 1968 | Return of astronauts | Party
  [46:2:0.2] Liability Convention 1972 | Absolute liability on Earth; fault liability in space | Party
  [46:2:0.3] Registration Convention 1975 | Register space objects with the UN | Party
  [46:2:0.4] Moon Agreement 1979 | Common heritage | Signed, not ratified
[46:2:1] - The 1960s–70s treaties do not cover cumulative debris or mega-constellations.
[46:2:2] # Space Activities Bill (draft, 2017)
[46:2:3] - Aims to **facilitate the overall growth of space activities** in India; a **regulatory body to license** space activities (non-governmental too); protection of **IPR** created through space activity; liability and penalties.
[46:2:4] - It does **not** create a **military wing of the armed forces in outer space** (the incorrect statement, Sachivalayam PYQ).
[46:2:5] - India still has **no Space Activities Act** on liability.

## SUB 3 | Mitigation and Debris-free Missions
[46:3:0] - **Debris Free Space Missions (DFSM) by 2030** – India's pledge at the **2024 IADC** meeting.
[46:3:1] - **25-year rule:** LEO satellites should re-enter within 25 years of mission end (NASA, then IADC and UN guidelines); GEO satellites go to a **graveyard orbit**.
[46:3:2] - **Active Debris Removal:** a servicer with a **robotic arm or net** to capture a defunct Indian rocket body (tested at URSC).
[46:3:3] - **PSLV-C62** (Jan 2026) planned a PS4 de-boost; **2026 re-entry rules:** warn aircraft and ships **at least 45 days** before re-entry.
[46:3:4] - **Gaps:** IADC/UN guidelines are non-binding; no international **duty-of-care** standard; lax licensing regimes attract operators.

## SUB 1 | Formation, Motto, Vision and Mission
[47:1:0] - **Formed 1958** by amalgamating the **Technical Development Establishments (TDEs)** of the Indian Army, the **Directorate of Technical Development & Production (DTDP)** and the **Defence Science Organisation (DSO)** (not AEET Trombay).
[47:1:1] - **DRDO = Defence Research and Development Organisation**; R&D wing of the **Ministry of Defence**; HQ **DRDO Bhawan, New Delhi**.
[47:1:2] - **Motto: "Balasya Mulam Vigyanam"** – the source of strength is science (**not** Tejasvinavadhitamastu, Jagratam Aharnisham or Vayam Rakshamah).
[47:1:3] - **Growth:** started with **10 establishments**; now about **41 laboratories and 5 DRDO Young Scientist Laboratories** (DRDO website; many books still say **about 52** labs, the pre-merger figure).
[47:1:4] - **Vision:** empower the nation with state-of-the-art indigenous **defence and security** technologies (older wording: make India prosperous through a world-class S&T base and give the Services a decisive edge with internationally competitive systems).
[47:1:5] - **Mission (three points):** (1) design, develop and lead to production **state-of-the-art sensors, weapon systems, platforms** and allied equipment; (2) **technological solutions** to optimise **combat effectiveness** and the **well-being of troops**; (3) **infrastructure, quality manpower and a strong indigenous technology base**. "Promote international collaboration" is **not** in the mission.
[47:1:6] - **Achievements:** Agni and Prithvi missiles, LCA **Tejas**, **Pinaka** MBRL, **Akash** air defence, radars and EW systems.

=== NOTES 5:48 | Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme – Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Trishul, Nag
## SUB 0 | Agni Series – Ranges, Tests and Mission Divyastra
[48:0:0] # Agni ("Fire") – India's surface-to-surface ballistic missiles
[48:0:1] - Developed by **DRDO** (not ISRO); **solid-fuelled**; nuclear and conventional warheads; **fully indigenous**; operated by the **Strategic Forces Command**; a **long-range** missile family (~700 km to 5,000+ km).
[48:0:2] - Only **Agni I–III** were developed within IGMDP; Agni-IV, V, P and VI are later projects.
[48:0:3] TABLE head: Missile | Stages / fuel | Range | Key fact
  [48:0:3.0] Agni-I | Single-stage solid | ~700 km (700–1,200 km) | Tested 9 Jan 2003 (Wheeler Island) and 28 Mar 2010 (700 km)
  [48:0:3.1] Agni-II | Two (two-and-a-half) stage solid | ~2,000 km+ (2,000–2,500 km; NextGen 2,500 km) | MRBM, often loosely called an "ICBM" in old keys
  [48:0:3.2] Agni-III | Two-stage solid | ~3,500 km (3,000–3,500 km) | First flight 9 Jul 2006 failed; 2007 and 2008 flights successful; tested Feb 2010
  [48:0:3.3] Agni-IV | Two-stage solid, road-mobile | ~4,000 km (3,500–4,000 km) | Surface-to-surface IRBM (UPSC 2014: not liquid, not 7,500 km)
  [48:0:3.4] Agni-V | Three-stage solid, canister | 5,000+ km | India's ICBM; tested 26 Dec 2016 from Wheeler (now APJ Abdul Kalam) Island; Dr Tessy Thomas
  [48:0:3.5] Agni-P (Prime) | Two-stage solid, canister | 1,000–2,000 km | Rail-based launcher test, 24 Sep 2025
  [48:0:3.6] Agni-VI | – | 8,000–10,000 km | Under development – the longest-range in any list
[48:0:4] - **Mission Divyastra (11 Mar 2024):** first flight test of **Agni-V with MIRV** (Multiple Independently Targetable Re-entry Vehicle) technology.
[48:0:5] - **ICBM threshold 5,500 km** → Agni-V (5,000 km) is formally on the IRBM/ICBM border; Agni-VI = full ICBM.
[48:0:6] > Agni-V "not exclusively indigenous" is the WRONG statement (it is fully indigenous). Agni-II "ICBM with more than 2,000 km" is the old-key wording.

## SUB 2 | Nag Family – Fire-and-Forget Anti-tank Missiles
[48:2:0] - **Nag:** third-generation **fire-and-forget anti-tank** guided missile; **IIR** (imaging infrared) seeker, night capable; 8 kg tandem **HEAT** warhead; range **500 m – 4 km**.
[48:2:1] - **Fire-and-forget:** the missile guides itself after launch, so the operator can take cover. (Astra = air-to-air, Prahaar = tactical SSM, Akash = SAM.)

  [48:2:2.2] MPATGM | Man-portable, ~2.5 km; DRDO flight-tested it at the Kurnool range (2019)
  [48:2:2.3] SANT | Stand-off anti-tank, air-launched, 15–20 km; tested 11 Dec 2021 at Pokhran
[48:2:3] - **Top attack:** climbs, then dives onto the tank's thin top armour.

## SUB 7 | IGMDP – Launch, Objectives, Phases and Closure
[48:7:0] - **Launched 1983** by **DRDO** under **Dr APJ Abdul Kalam**; lead lab **DRDL, Hyderabad** (with RCI and ASL).
[48:7:1] - **Objectives:** **self-sufficiency in missile technology**; a **family of strategic and tactical guided missiles**; competence across technologies, production and deployment. Being "the first country with an international missile market" was **not** an aim.
[48:7:2] - **Five missiles:** **Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Trishul, Nag**.
[48:7:3] TABLE head: Phase | Missiles
  [48:7:3.0] 1984–89 | Prithvi, Trishul
  [48:7:3.1] 1989–92 | Nag, Akash
  [48:7:3.2] 1992–2008 | Agni matured
[48:7:4] - **Closed 2008** after self-reliance was achieved; spin-offs: long-range tracking system, composites, **ring laser gyros**, radomes (not a satellite navigation constellation).
[48:7:5] - **Significance:** turned India from a missile importer into a **missile-exporting** power and laid the base for Agni-V MIRV and BMD.
[48:7:6] - **Aug 2026:** ToT of conventional missiles such as **Nag** to private industry; **Agni stays in state production**.

## SUB 0 | BrahMos – India-Russia Supersonic Cruise Missile
[49:0:0] # Partnership and name
[49:0:1] - **Joint venture of India and Russia:** **DRDO 50.5%** + **NPO Mashinostroyeniya (NPOM), Russia 49.5%** = **BrahMos Aerospace**, created by an Inter-Governmental Agreement signed in **Moscow, 1998** (not USA, France, Israel, Japan, China).
[49:0:2] - **Name:** **Brahmaputra** (India) + **Moskva** (Russia) rivers – "BRAHMOS", not Brahmastra.
[49:0:3] - **Production:** missiles are integrated at the **BrahMos Integration Complex, Kanchanbagh, Hyderabad** (first production unit; later Thiruvananthapuram, and **Lucknow** – BrahMos Integration and Testing Facility, inaugurated **11 May 2025**). Not Bangalore, Mumbai, Ludhiana or Dehradun.
[49:0:4] # Type and technical features
[49:0:5] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [49:0:5.0] Type | Supersonic cruise missile (not ballistic, not ICBM); one of the world's fastest cruise missiles
  [49:0:5.1] Speed | Mach 2.8–3.0 (~3,400–3,700 km/h)
  [49:0:5.2] Role | Essentially anti-ship, also land-attack; not anti-aircraft, not anti-tank
  [49:0:5.3] Propulsion | Solid booster → liquid ramjet
  [49:0:5.4] Navigation | Inertial + satellite; fire-and-forget from a Transport Launch Canister
  [49:0:5.5] Range | 290 km originally → 400–450 km after India joined MTCR (2016); BrahMos-ER 450–800 km. Never 1,200 km
  [49:0:5.6] Platforms | Land, ship, submarine and air (Su-30MKI) – not "only submarines"; in service with Army, Navy and Air Force
[49:0:6] - **Variants:** BrahMos-ER (extended range); **BrahMos-NG** – lighter (~1.5 t vs 2.5 t), for Tejas, smaller warships, submarines, ~Mach 3.5; **BrahMos-II** – planned **hypersonic** (above Mach 5, up to Mach 7–8), scramjet.

  [49:0:8.0] 12 June 2001 | First successful test, Chandipur, Odisha

## SUB 5 | Missile Classes, FOBS and Hypersonic Technology
[49:5:0] # Ballistic vs cruise
[49:5:1] TABLE head: Feature | Ballistic missile | Cruise missile
  [49:5:1.0] Power | Rocket-powered only in the boost phase | Air-breathing (jet), powered throughout
  [49:5:1.1] Path | High parabolic (free-fall) arc | Flies low, constant speed, aerodynamic lift
  [49:5:1.2] Examples | Agni, Prithvi, Pralay, Shaurya | BrahMos, Nirbhay
[49:5:2] - UPSC 2023: "ballistic = jet-propelled subsonic; cruise = rocket only at start" and "Agni-V cruise, BrahMos ICBM" are **both wrong** (neither).
[49:5:3] TABLE head: Class | Speed | Indian example
  [49:5:3.0] Subsonic | Below Mach 1 | Nirbhay
  [49:5:3.1] Supersonic | Mach 1–5 | BrahMos (Mach 2.8–3.0)
  [49:5:3.2] Hypersonic | Above Mach 5 | BrahMos-II (planned), LR-AShM, HSTDV
[49:5:4] # Terms
[49:5:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [49:5:5.0] FOBS (Fractional Orbital Bombardment System) | Warhead put into a (fractional) low Earth orbit, then de-orbits over the target; can come from any direction (e.g. over the South Pole); Soviet Cold War concept
  [49:5:5.1] HGV (Hypersonic Glide Vehicle) | Rocket-boosted, then glides at Mach 5+ and manoeuvres in the upper atmosphere; hard to intercept
  [49:5:5.2] MIRV | One missile, several warheads, different targets; first Indian test on Agni-V in Mission Divyastra (11 March 2024), not K-4
  [49:5:5.3] Scramjet | Supersonic combustion, no rotating parts; uses air, does not carry its oxidiser
  [49:5:5.4] SFDR (Solid Fuel Ducted Ramjet) | Uses atmospheric air; demonstrated from ITR Chandipur; for long-range air-to-air missiles
[49:5:6] # Indian hypersonic systems
[49:5:7] - **HSTDV:** indigenous **scramjet** demonstrator; flew above **Mach 6**; scramjet combustion and thermal protection above **2,000°C**; combustor run over 12 minutes (Jan 2026). Not a submarine-launched missile.
[49:5:8] - **16 November 2024:** first flight-trial of India's first long-range hypersonic missile.
[49:5:9] - **LR-AShM:** hypersonic **glide anti-ship** missile for Navy coastal batteries; **~1,500 km**; **peak Mach 10**, average Mach 5 via "skipping"; at the 77th Republic Day Parade; **AP coast ranges** used for validation rounds (Jan 2026).

## SUB 3 | Nuclear Doctrine, No First Use and Command
[50:3:0] # Timeline
[50:3:1] - **Draft doctrine:** **National Security Advisory Board (NSAB)**, **August 1999**.
[50:3:2] - **No First Use** came from the **Vajpayee government after Pokhran-II** (not Indira Gandhi after Pokhran-I).
[50:3:3] - Formally adopted by the **CCS** on **4 January 2003**.
[50:3:4] # Key tenets
[50:3:5] - **Credible minimum deterrence** backed by an assured **second-strike capability** (survivable SSBNs) – not a bigger arsenal or first use.
[50:3:6] - **No First Use:** only in **retaliation against a nuclear attack on Indian territory or Indian forces**.
[50:3:7] - **Massive retaliation** causing unacceptable damage.
[50:3:8] - **No use against non-nuclear-weapon states**; nuclear option kept against a **major biological or chemical attack**.
[50:3:9] - **Unilateral moratorium** on nuclear tests (since 1998) – so a doctrine question "NFU, moratorium, minimum credible deterrence" = **all of the above**.
[50:3:10] - India kept the bomb option open and would **not open its strategic nuclear plants to inspection** (UPPCS 1994: A true, R "India will not produce any bomb" false).
[50:3:11] # Nuclear Command Authority
[50:3:12] TABLE head: Body | Chaired by | Role
  [50:3:12.0] Political Council | Prime Minister | Only body that can authorise use
  [50:3:12.1] Executive Council | National Security Advisor | Advises; carries out directions
  [50:3:12.2] Strategic Forces Command | Tri-service (set up 2003) | Manages and operates the nuclear arsenal
[50:3:13] - **CCS** oversees civilian political control; it does not manage the forces. Aug 2026: Agni user trials held "under the aegis of SFC".
