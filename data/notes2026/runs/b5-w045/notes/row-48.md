=== Agni Series – Ranges, Tests and Mission Divyastra
# Agni ("Fire") – India's surface-to-surface ballistic missiles
- Developed by **DRDO** (not ISRO); **solid-fuelled**; nuclear and conventional warheads; **fully indigenous**; operated by the **Strategic Forces Command**; a **long-range** missile family (~700 km to 5,000+ km).
- Only **Agni I–III** were developed within IGMDP; Agni-IV, V, P and VI are later projects.
| Missile | Stages / fuel | Range | Key fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Agni-I** | **Single-stage** solid | **~700 km** (700–1,200 km) | Tested **9 Jan 2003** (Wheeler Island) and **28 Mar 2010** (700 km) |
| **Agni-II** | Two (two-and-a-half) stage solid | **~2,000 km+** (2,000–2,500 km; NextGen 2,500 km) | MRBM, often loosely called an "ICBM" in old keys |
| **Agni-III** | Two-stage solid | **~3,500 km** (3,000–3,500 km) | First flight **9 Jul 2006 failed**; **2007 and 2008** flights successful; tested **Feb 2010** |
| **Agni-IV** | Two-stage **solid**, road-mobile | **~4,000 km** (3,500–4,000 km) | Surface-to-surface IRBM (UPSC 2014: not liquid, not 7,500 km) |
| **Agni-V** | Three-stage solid, canister | **5,000+ km** | India's ICBM; tested **26 Dec 2016** from **Wheeler (now APJ Abdul Kalam) Island**; **Dr Tessy Thomas** |
| **Agni-P (Prime)** | Two-stage solid, canister | 1,000–2,000 km | Rail-based launcher test, 24 Sep 2025 |
| **Agni-VI** | – | 8,000–10,000 km | **Under development** – the longest-range in any list |
- **Mission Divyastra (11 Mar 2024):** first flight test of **Agni-V with MIRV** (Multiple Independently Targetable Re-entry Vehicle) technology.
- **ICBM threshold 5,500 km** → Agni-V (5,000 km) is formally on the IRBM/ICBM border; Agni-VI = full ICBM.
> Agni-V "not exclusively indigenous" is the WRONG statement (it is fully indigenous). Agni-II "ICBM with more than 2,000 km" is the old-key wording.

=== The Five IGMDP Missiles – Types
| Missile | Meaning | Type | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prithvi** | Earth | **Surface-to-surface** SRBM (liquid) | 150–350 km (Prithvi-III 750 km) |
| **Agni** | Fire | **Surface-to-surface** ballistic (solid) | ~700 to 5,000+ km |
| **Akash** | Sky | Medium-range **surface-to-air** | ~25–30 km; Mach 2.5; 18 km altitude |
| **Trishul** | Trident | Short-range, low-level **surface-to-air** | ~9 km (NextGen: 12 km) |
| **Nag** | Cobra | 3rd-generation **fire-and-forget anti-tank** | 500 m – 4 km (land version) |
- **Not IGMDP:** **BrahMos** (India-Russia), Astra, Shaurya, Nirbhay, Sagarika, Helina, Prahaar, Barak-8.
- **Not missiles:** **Pinaka** (multi-barrel **rocket** launcher, ARDE), **Arjun** (tank), Vajra (gun), Tejas (aircraft), Lakshya (target drone).
- **SLBM:** **K-4** (~3,500 km) and K-15 Sagarika – submarine-launched; Agni, Prithvi and Akash are land-based.
- Nag is the **shortest-range** of the five; **Prithvi** is the formal SRBM.
> Classic traps: Nag is **not** a SAM; Agni is **not** a sea-launched cruise missile; Trishul and Akash are the SAMs.

=== IGMDP – Launch, Objectives, Phases and Closure
- **Launched 1983** by **DRDO** under **Dr APJ Abdul Kalam**; lead lab **DRDL, Hyderabad** (with RCI and ASL).
- **Objectives:** **self-sufficiency in missile technology**; a **family of strategic and tactical guided missiles**; competence across technologies, production and deployment. Being "the first country with an international missile market" was **not** an aim.
- **Five missiles:** **Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Trishul, Nag**.
| Phase | Missiles |
|---|---|
| 1984–89 | Prithvi, Trishul |
| 1989–92 | Nag, Akash |
| 1992–2008 | Agni matured |
- **Closed 2008** after self-reliance was achieved; spin-offs: long-range tracking system, composites, **ring laser gyros**, radomes (not a satellite navigation constellation).
- **Significance:** turned India from a missile importer into a **missile-exporting** power and laid the base for Agni-V MIRV and BMD.
- **Aug 2026:** ToT of conventional missiles such as **Nag** to private industry; **Agni stays in state production**.

=== Nag Family – Fire-and-Forget Anti-tank Missiles
- **Nag:** third-generation **fire-and-forget anti-tank** guided missile; **IIR** (imaging infrared) seeker, night capable; 8 kg tandem **HEAT** warhead; range **500 m – 4 km**.
- **Fire-and-forget:** the missile guides itself after launch, so the operator can take cover. (Astra = air-to-air, Prahaar = tactical SSM, Akash = SAM.)
| Variant | Role |
|---|---|
| **NAMICA** | Tracked carrier (modified BMP-2) with up to **six** missiles; NAMIS = Nag Mk-2 "tank killer" |
| **HELINA / Dhruvastra** | Helicopter-launched – Army / IAF |
| **MPATGM** | **Man-portable**, ~2.5 km; DRDO flight-tested it at the **Kurnool** range (**2019**) |
| **SANT** | Stand-off anti-tank, air-launched, 15–20 km; tested **11 Dec 2021** at **Pokhran** |
- **Top attack:** climbs, then dives onto the tank's thin top armour.

=== Prithvi and Dhanush
| Variant | Service | Range / fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Prithvi-I** | Army | **150 km**, 1,000 kg; **India's first indigenous ballistic missile** |
| **Prithvi-II** | Air Force | **350 km**, 500–1,000 kg; **nuclear-capable**; first test **27 Jan 1996, Chandipur**; inducted 2003 |
| **Prithvi-III / Dhanush** | **Navy** | 350–750 km; **ship-launched** short-range ballistic missile ("ship to ship / ship to shore") |
- Prithvi = **liquid** fuel (Prithvi-III: solid first stage); three variants, not two.
- **Nov 2006:** a modified Prithvi (**PAD/Pradyumna**) intercepted a **Prithvi-II** target – India's first BMD interception; Prithvi-II itself is **not** an air-defence missile for metros. **PAD (Pradyumna)** = exo-atmospheric interceptor (50–80 km).

=== Akash, Trishul and Their Successors
- **Akash:** medium-range **SAM**, targets up to **30 km**, Mach 2.5, 18 km altitude; Army + IAF; developed by **DRDO** (lead lab DRDL, Hyderabad).
| Variant | Fact |
|---|---|
| Akash Mk-I | ~25 km, Rajendra radar |
| **Akash Prime** | Indigenous **active RF seeker**; first test **27 Sep 2021** |
| Akash-NG | ~70 km, canister-launched |
- **Trishul:** short-range, low-level **SAM**; **programme shut down 27 Feb 2008** (never inducted) – the "shelved" missile; replaced by Spyder (IAF), Barak-1 (Navy), Akash (Army).
- **Akashteer** (BEL + Army) is an **air-defence control system**, not a missile.
- **QRSAM** (Quick Reaction SAM): indigenous successor; test-fired from **Chandipur** (ITR, Odisha) in **June–July 2017**.
