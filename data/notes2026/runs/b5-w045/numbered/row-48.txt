## SUB 0 | Agni Series – Ranges, Tests and Mission Divyastra
[0:0] # Agni ("Fire") – India's surface-to-surface ballistic missiles
[0:1] - Developed by **DRDO** (not ISRO); **solid-fuelled**; nuclear and conventional warheads; **fully indigenous**; operated by the **Strategic Forces Command**; a **long-range** missile family (~700 km to 5,000+ km).
[0:2] - Only **Agni I–III** were developed within IGMDP; Agni-IV, V, P and VI are later projects.
[0:3] TABLE head: Missile | Stages / fuel | Range | Key fact
  [0:3.0] Agni-I | Single-stage solid | ~700 km (700–1,200 km) | Tested 9 Jan 2003 (Wheeler Island) and 28 Mar 2010 (700 km)
  [0:3.1] Agni-II | Two (two-and-a-half) stage solid | ~2,000 km+ (2,000–2,500 km; NextGen 2,500 km) | MRBM, often loosely called an "ICBM" in old keys
  [0:3.2] Agni-III | Two-stage solid | ~3,500 km (3,000–3,500 km) | First flight 9 Jul 2006 failed; 2007 and 2008 flights successful; tested Feb 2010
  [0:3.3] Agni-IV | Two-stage solid, road-mobile | ~4,000 km (3,500–4,000 km) | Surface-to-surface IRBM (UPSC 2014: not liquid, not 7,500 km)
  [0:3.4] Agni-V | Three-stage solid, canister | 5,000+ km | India's ICBM; tested 26 Dec 2016 from Wheeler (now APJ Abdul Kalam) Island; Dr Tessy Thomas
  [0:3.5] Agni-P (Prime) | Two-stage solid, canister | 1,000–2,000 km | Rail-based launcher test, 24 Sep 2025
  [0:3.6] Agni-VI | – | 8,000–10,000 km | Under development – the longest-range in any list
[0:4] - **Mission Divyastra (11 Mar 2024):** first flight test of **Agni-V with MIRV** (Multiple Independently Targetable Re-entry Vehicle) technology.
[0:5] - **ICBM threshold 5,500 km** → Agni-V (5,000 km) is formally on the IRBM/ICBM border; Agni-VI = full ICBM.
[0:6] > Agni-V "not exclusively indigenous" is the WRONG statement (it is fully indigenous). Agni-II "ICBM with more than 2,000 km" is the old-key wording.

## SUB 1 | The Five IGMDP Missiles – Types
[1:0] TABLE head: Missile | Meaning | Type | Range
  [1:0.0] Prithvi | Earth | Surface-to-surface SRBM (liquid) | 150–350 km (Prithvi-III 750 km)
  [1:0.1] Agni | Fire | Surface-to-surface ballistic (solid) | ~700 to 5,000+ km
  [1:0.2] Akash | Sky | Medium-range surface-to-air | ~25–30 km; Mach 2.5; 18 km altitude
  [1:0.3] Trishul | Trident | Short-range, low-level surface-to-air | ~9 km (NextGen: 12 km)
  [1:0.4] Nag | Cobra | 3rd-generation fire-and-forget anti-tank | 500 m – 4 km (land version)
[1:1] - **Not IGMDP:** **BrahMos** (India-Russia), Astra, Shaurya, Nirbhay, Sagarika, Helina, Prahaar, Barak-8.
[1:2] - **Not missiles:** **Pinaka** (multi-barrel **rocket** launcher, ARDE), **Arjun** (tank), Vajra (gun), Tejas (aircraft), Lakshya (target drone).
[1:3] - **SLBM:** **K-4** (~3,500 km) and K-15 Sagarika – submarine-launched; Agni, Prithvi and Akash are land-based.
[1:4] - Nag is the **shortest-range** of the five; **Prithvi** is the formal SRBM.
[1:5] > Classic traps: Nag is **not** a SAM; Agni is **not** a sea-launched cruise missile; Trishul and Akash are the SAMs.

## SUB 2 | IGMDP – Launch, Objectives, Phases and Closure
[2:0] - **Launched 1983** by **DRDO** under **Dr APJ Abdul Kalam**; lead lab **DRDL, Hyderabad** (with RCI and ASL).
[2:1] - **Objectives:** **self-sufficiency in missile technology**; a **family of strategic and tactical guided missiles**; competence across technologies, production and deployment. Being "the first country with an international missile market" was **not** an aim.
[2:2] - **Five missiles:** **Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Trishul, Nag**.
[2:3] TABLE head: Phase | Missiles
  [2:3.0] 1984–89 | Prithvi, Trishul
  [2:3.1] 1989–92 | Nag, Akash
  [2:3.2] 1992–2008 | Agni matured
[2:4] - **Closed 2008** after self-reliance was achieved; spin-offs: long-range tracking system, composites, **ring laser gyros**, radomes (not a satellite navigation constellation).
[2:5] - **Significance:** turned India from a missile importer into a **missile-exporting** power and laid the base for Agni-V MIRV and BMD.
[2:6] - **Aug 2026:** ToT of conventional missiles such as **Nag** to private industry; **Agni stays in state production**.

## SUB 3 | Nag Family – Fire-and-Forget Anti-tank Missiles
[3:0] - **Nag:** third-generation **fire-and-forget anti-tank** guided missile; **IIR** (imaging infrared) seeker, night capable; 8 kg tandem **HEAT** warhead; range **500 m – 4 km**.
[3:1] - **Fire-and-forget:** the missile guides itself after launch, so the operator can take cover. (Astra = air-to-air, Prahaar = tactical SSM, Akash = SAM.)
[3:2] TABLE head: Variant | Role
  [3:2.0] NAMICA | Tracked carrier (modified BMP-2) with up to six missiles; NAMIS = Nag Mk-2 "tank killer"
  [3:2.1] HELINA / Dhruvastra | Helicopter-launched – Army / IAF
  [3:2.2] MPATGM | Man-portable, ~2.5 km; DRDO flight-tested it at the Kurnool range (2019)
  [3:2.3] SANT | Stand-off anti-tank, air-launched, 15–20 km; tested 11 Dec 2021 at Pokhran
[3:3] - **Top attack:** climbs, then dives onto the tank's thin top armour.
[3:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 2 (src/science-tech/nextgen-st-group2-02.pdf, OCR) p.57; PMF S&T Part 6 (src/science-tech/pmf-science-tech-06.txt) p.70; app notes 5:48 sub 5; general knowledge, not checked (SANT 11 Dec 2021 Pokhran; MPATGM Kurnool 2019)
## SUB 4 | Prithvi and Dhanush
[4:0] TABLE head: Variant | Service | Range / fact
  [4:0.0] Prithvi-I | Army | 150 km, 1,000 kg; India's first indigenous ballistic missile
  [4:0.1] Prithvi-II | Air Force | 350 km, 500–1,000 kg; nuclear-capable; first test 27 Jan 1996, Chandipur; inducted 2003
  [4:0.2] Prithvi-III / Dhanush | Navy | 350–750 km; ship-launched short-range ballistic missile ("ship to ship / ship to shore")
[4:1] - Prithvi = **liquid** fuel (Prithvi-III: solid first stage); three variants, not two.
[4:2] - **Nov 2006:** a modified Prithvi (**PAD/Pradyumna**) intercepted a **Prithvi-II** target – India's first BMD interception; Prithvi-II itself is **not** an air-defence missile for metros. **PAD (Pradyumna)** = exo-atmospheric interceptor (50–80 km).

## SUB 5 | Akash, Trishul and Their Successors
[5:0] - **Akash:** medium-range **SAM**, targets up to **30 km**, Mach 2.5, 18 km altitude; Army + IAF; developed by **DRDO** (lead lab DRDL, Hyderabad).
[5:1] TABLE head: Variant | Fact
  [5:1.0] Akash Mk-I | ~25 km, Rajendra radar
  [5:1.1] Akash Prime | Indigenous active RF seeker; first test 27 Sep 2021
  [5:1.2] Akash-NG | ~70 km, canister-launched
[5:2] - **Trishul:** short-range, low-level **SAM**; **programme shut down 27 Feb 2008** (never inducted) – the "shelved" missile; replaced by Spyder (IAF), Barak-1 (Navy), Akash (Army).
[5:3] - **Akashteer** (BEL + Army) is an **air-defence control system**, not a missile.
[5:4] - **QRSAM** (Quick Reaction SAM): indigenous successor; test-fired from **Chandipur** (ITR, Odisha) in **June–July 2017**.
