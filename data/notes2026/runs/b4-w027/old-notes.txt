=== NOTES 4:27 | Hydrological Disasters – Floods, Flash Floods, Cloudbursts, Droughts, Tsunamis, Storm Surges
## SUB 0 | Tsunamis – Meaning, Causes, Behaviour and the 2004 Tsunami
[27:0:0] # Meaning (asked 17 times)
[27:0:1] - **Tsunami** = Japanese word: **"tsu" = harbour + "nami" = wave** ("harbour wave") – **not Latin, Arabic or Hebrew** (UKPSC 2005; CDS 2017 "Latin word" = the wrong statement).
[27:0:2] - A **series of waves** of very long wavelength (over 500 km) and long period (10 minutes to 2 hours) – **not a single wave**; the **first wave is often not the largest**, later waves can be bigger.
[27:0:3] - A **natural disaster**; can strike at **any time of day or night**. **Not a "tidal wave"** – tides (gravity of Moon and Sun) play **no role** in generating it (CDS 2012).
[27:0:4] # Causes
[27:0:5] TABLE head: Cause | Note
  [27:0:5.0] Undersea earthquakes | Main / most common cause – sudden vertical displacement of the sea floor at subduction zones (shocks above about 7.5)
  [27:0:5.1] Submarine or coastal landslides | Displace water
  [27:0:5.2] Volcanic eruptions | E.g. Krakatoa 1883 (about 40 m wave), Anak Krakatau 2018
  [27:0:5.3] Explosions, meteorite (bolide) impacts | Rare
[27:0:6] - **Not caused by hurricanes / cyclones** (they raise **storm surges**) or deserts (CDS 2010, asked twice).
[27:0:7] - Most tsunamis occur in the **Pacific Ocean** (Ring of Fire – Alaska, Japan, Philippines, Indonesia).
[27:0:8] - **Coastal States/UTs** of India are vulnerable to **tsunamis** (not volcanic eruptions).
[27:0:9] # Behaviour
[27:0:10] TABLE head: Deep ocean | Near the coast
  [27:0:10.0] Very long wavelength; height under 1 m ("slight swell" about 30 cm); speed hundreds of km/h (about 700–850 km/h at 4,000–6,000 m depth) | Slows down (speed depends on depth); wavelength shortens, height rises sharply (shoaling) – 15 m or more; on coastal plains it moves at roughly 50 km/h
[27:0:11] - Impact is **less over the open ocean, more near the coast**; ships at sea hardly notice it.
[27:0:12] - **Warning sign: drawdown** – the sea **suddenly recedes** (sea level falls quickly) before the wave hits; also strong quake shaking.
[27:0:13] # 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami
[27:0:14] - **26 December 2004**; **Sumatra-Andaman earthquake, Mw about 9.1** (Indian plate under the Burma plate); speed about 800 km/h, falling to under 70 km/h near shore.
[27:0:15] - Hit **Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, Kerala, Andaman & Nicobar** – **not Gujarat**; **worst-hit state Tamil Nadu**, worst district **Nagapattinam**; India: over **12,000 deaths**; Indira Point submerged.
[27:0:16] - **Mangroves** acted as a safety hedge: their **extensive prop roots** (and pneumatophores) are **not uprooted by storms and tides** and absorb wave energy (UPSC 2011). Sand dunes and coastal cliffs also block waves; **open water bodies give no protection**.
[27:0:17] - **Fukushima (Japan), 11 March 2011**: Mw 9.0 Tohoku earthquake + tsunami → nuclear radiation.
[27:0:18] # Risk zones and warning
[27:0:19] TABLE head: Risk | States/UTs
  [27:0:19.0] Very high | Andaman & Nicobar (near the Sunda subduction zone)
  [27:0:19.1] High | Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, Odisha
  [27:0:19.2] Moderate | Kerala, Karnataka, Goa, Maharashtra, Gujarat
[27:0:20] - Sources for India: **Sunda-Andaman** subduction zone (east) and the **Makran** zone (west coast).
[27:0:21] - **Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC)** at **INCOIS, Hyderabad** (MoES): detects tsunamigenic quakes within **10 minutes** and can warn **25 Indian Ocean countries**; **NIOT (Nellore)** OMNI buoys feed it. International Tsunami Information Centre: **Honolulu**. World Tsunami Awareness Day: **5 November**.
[27:0:22] > Traps: Japanese (not Latin); tides play no role; hurricanes do not cause tsunamis; the wave slows near the shore; the first wave may not be the largest.
[27:0:23] ~~ Sources: Rocket Disaster Management p.10-13; PMF Internal Security & DM 02 p.53-54 (book p.127-128); PMF Physical Geography 02 p.101-103 (book p.192-194); NCERT summary (OnlyIAS Indian Geography 05) p.20-21; app notes 4:27 sub 7
[27:0:24] - The 2004 tsunami struck the south-east coast and islands; **Odisha**, West Bengal and Gujarat were **not affected**.
[27:0:25] - **Ocean-crossing speed:** a deep-ocean tsunami moves at about jet-airliner speed (~800 km/h), so one from Los Angeles could reach Tokyo in about **10 hours – about a flight's time, not 36–48 hours**.
## SUB 1 | Floods – Extent, Causes and Management
[27:1:0] # Nature and extent (asked 18 times)
[27:1:1] - Flood = overflow of water onto normally dry land when rivers or other water bodies cannot contain it; **rapid-onset**; the **most frequent / most common natural disaster in India**; can be **mitigated through early warning**.
[27:1:2] - **Rashtriya Barh Ayog (National Flood Commission)**, set up **1976**, report **1980**: of India's **329 mha**, **more than 40 mha** (about **12%** of the area) is flood-prone; a recent estimate is about **49 mha**. About **10%** of India's area is actually flood-affected.
[27:1:3] - Worst-affected states: **Assam and Bihar**, then **Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal**; **most flood-prone state: Bihar** (UPPCS 2000). Tamil Nadu floods in Nov–Jan (retreating monsoon).
[27:1:4] - About **200 million** people are exposed to recurring floods every year (APPSC Group 1 2012 key).
[27:1:5] - **Flood control is a State subject**; the Centre helps through **FMBAP** (Flood Management and Border Areas Programme, 2021–26).
[27:1:6] # Causes
[27:1:7] TABLE head: Cause | Effect
  [27:1:7.0] Heavy / prolonged rainfall | Primary cause of riverine flooding in low-lying areas
  [27:1:7.1] Cloudburst | Very intense rain over a small area
  [27:1:7.2] Snowmelt | Sudden rise in river flow
  [27:1:7.3] Siltation (from soil erosion) | Reduces channel depth and capacity
  [27:1:7.4] Embankment breach | Water escapes the channel
  [27:1:7.5] Deforestation | Human factor: increases runoff
  [27:1:7.6] Floodplain encroachment | Raises exposure
  [27:1:7.7] Poor drainage | Water accumulates
  [27:1:7.8] Dam failure | Sudden downstream discharge; overtopping = water spills over the crest
[27:1:8] - Major causes in India = **heavy rainfall, poor drainage, deforestation and dam failure** (not high temperature, windstorms or ocean acidification).
[27:1:9] - A landslide can **block a river**: **Pareechu river (Tibet)**, August **2004** landslide lake – flood threat on the **Sutlej** in Himachal Pradesh.
[27:1:10] # Management
[27:1:11] TABLE head: Structural | Non-structural
  [27:1:11.0] Embankments, dams and reservoirs, channel and drainage improvement, diversion channels | Flood-plain zoning, flood insurance, flood-proofing, forecasting and warning, watershed management
[27:1:12] - **Afforestation** of flood-prone areas / catchments = **flood control (mitigation)** – forests **increase infiltration and interception**; wetlands and floodplains store water; mangroves buffer coasts.
[27:1:13] - **Watershed structures** (check dams, percolation tanks) mitigate both **drought and flood** – not tornadoes or fire.
[27:1:14] - **Forecasting**: **Central Water Commission (CWC)** (Jal Shakti) runs flood forecasting – using **GIS, radar** (Doppler) and **remote sensing / satellite** data (not the Richter or Pascal scale or MRI).
[27:1:15] - In flood response, high-resolution satellite imagery and GIS map the **inundated area**, from which the **number of people affected** and damage are estimated.
[27:1:16] - **Odisha Flood Hazard Atlas**: built from satellite images of historic flood inundation **2001–2018** (Rocket: 2000–2018).
[27:1:17] # Committees and programmes
[27:1:18] TABLE head: Body | Year | Role
  [27:1:18.0] National Flood Control Programme / National Flood Policy | 1954 | Launched after the 1954 floods
  [27:1:18.1] Rashtriya Barh Ayog | 1976 (report 1980) | Assessed about 40 mha flood-prone
  [27:1:18.2] Expert Committee for Scientific Assessment of Flood-Prone Areas | July 2012 | Set up by Ministry of Water Resources, under the CWC Chairman
  [27:1:18.3] NDMA Guidelines | 2008 (floods), 2010 (urban floods) | Policy
[27:1:19] ~~ Sources: Rocket Disaster Management p.27-31; PMF Internal Security & DM 02 p.55-56 (book p.130-131); NCERT summary (OnlyIAS Indian Geography 05) p.25-27; CAG Report No.10 of 2017 ch.6 (Expert Committee, July 2012), via web search; app notes 4:27 sub 2, 3
[27:1:20] - **Flood adaptation:** people in **Assam** near rivers and embankments build **houses on stilts** (*chang ghar*) above flood level.
## SUB 2 | Disasters – Meaning, Classification and Onset
[27:2:0] # Meaning (asked 15 times)
[27:2:1] - **Disaster = Hazard × Vulnerability** (with low capacity to cope); French "desastre" = "bad star".
[27:2:2] - Natural disasters cause most damage in **developing countries**; the **Bhopal gas tragedy** was **man-made**; **India is not disaster-free**; **mangroves reduce cyclone impact** → UPPCS 2018 (asked twice): **1, 2 and 4**.
[27:2:3] - Globally **drought (and famine)** are the **deadliest** weather/climate disasters, followed by **floods** (NCERT); **floods** cause the largest economic losses over the long run and affect the most people.
[27:2:4] # Classification
[27:2:5] TABLE head: Class | Examples
  [27:2:5.0] Geological | Earthquakes, tsunamis, landslides, volcanic eruptions, dam burst, mine fire
  [27:2:5.1] Hydrological / hydro-meteorological | Floods, flash floods, cloudbursts, storm surges, cyclones
  [27:2:5.2] Climatological | Droughts, heatwaves, cold waves
  [27:2:5.3] Biological | Epidemics, pest attacks, food poisoning
  [27:2:5.4] Accident-related (HPC 1999) | Urban/forest fires, road/rail/air accidents, building collapse, mine flooding
  [27:2:5.5] Man-made: chemical, industrial, nuclear | Bhopal gas tragedy, Chernobyl
[27:2:6] - **Drought is not geological** (it is climatological).
[27:2:7] - **Mine flooding** = **accident-related** disaster (not geological or water-and-climate).
[27:2:8] - **Avalanche** = classed as a **hydro-geological** disaster (snow + steep slope) in Indian keys; international (EM-DAT) lists it as hydrological.
[27:2:9] - **Socio-natural hazards** = natural + human factors: floods worsened by deforestation, **landslides due to unscientific construction**.
[27:2:10] - **Man-made hazard**: **fires** (not landslides, avalanches or droughts); **not man-made: mud slides** (natural) – deforestation and food poisoning come from human action.
[27:2:11] - **Chennai floods (2015)** = the most human-induced (wetland and lake encroachment); the Chennai tsunami, Anantapur low rainfall and Kedarnath flood had natural triggers.
[27:2:12] # Onset
[27:2:13] TABLE head: Rapid-onset | Slow-onset
  [27:2:13.0] Cyclone, flood, tsunami, earthquake, flash flood, cloudburst | Drought ("creeping emergency / creeping hazard"), pest infestation, environmental degradation
[27:2:14] ~~ Sources: Rocket Disaster Management p.40, 47-48; NCERT summary (OnlyIAS Indian Geography 05) p.15-17; PMF Internal Security & DM 02 p.42-43 (book p.116-117); UNDRR/ScienceDaily 2016 natural-disaster losses study (via web search); app notes 4:27 sub 0
## SUB 3 | Flash Floods, Cloudbursts and GLOFs
[27:3:0] # Flash floods (asked 10 times)
[27:3:1] - A flood that **occurs suddenly, unexpectedly and for a short duration** – from **local precipitation of high intensity** (intense rain, cloudbursts), or dam / glacial-lake / landslide-lake bursts; little warning time; main danger is **debris**; also landslides.
[27:3:2] - Heavy rain, urban settings or blocked drains are only possible causes – not the definition.
[27:3:3] - Vulnerable: **mountains, steep catchments and urban areas**. **IMD** gives flash-flood guidance for India + **Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal**.
[27:3:4] # Cloudburst
[27:3:5] - **Sudden, extremely heavy rainfall for a short period over a small area** (CDS 2012: copious rain lasting a few minutes) – **IMD: at least 100 mm (10 cm) in one hour over about 20–30 sq km**.
[27:3:6] - Localised, with **thunder and lightning**; **mostly in hilly areas** (1,000–2,500 m; orographic lifting); can give **250–300 mm in a couple of hours**; occurs **day or night** → CDS 2017: **I, II and III**.
[27:3:7] - Highly localised → very hard to forecast (Doppler radar, nowcasting). **HKH "monsoon paradox"**: erratic seasonal rain plus more short, intense cloudbursts.
[27:3:8] TABLE head: Region | Areas | Why
  [27:3:8.0] Himalaya | Uttarakhand, HP, J&K, Ladakh, Sikkim | Steep slopes, orographic effect
  [27:3:8.1] Western Ghats | Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra | Intense rain on the windward side
  [27:3:8.2] AP Eastern Ghats agency areas | Narrow valleys | Cloudburst flash floods
[27:3:9] # GLOFs and events
[27:3:10] - **GLOF**: glacier retreat enlarges a **proglacial (moraine-dammed) lake**, whose dam bursts. **NDMA GLOF Guidelines (October 2020)**: population risk highest in **Sikkim and Jammu & Kashmir**.
[27:3:11] TABLE head: Event | Year | Note
  [27:3:11.0] Leh cloudburst | 2010 | Ladakh
  [27:3:11.1] Kedarnath | June 2013 | Cloudburst + GLOF; Chorabari (Chaurabari) Glacier in Uttarakhand, north of the temple; lake Gandhi Sarovar
  [27:3:11.2] Operation Phuktal | 2015 | Army + NDMA blasted a landslide-debris lake on the Phuktal river (Zanskar, Kargil) to prevent flash floods
  [27:3:11.3] Chamoli glacier burst | 2021 | Uttarakhand
  [27:3:11.4] South Lhonak GLOF | Oct 2023 | Sikkim (Teesta III dam)
  [27:3:11.5] HP flash floods | 2023 | Unregulated slope construction
  [27:3:11.6] Mandi (HP) | 2025 | Nearly 20 times normal rain
  [27:3:11.7] Dharali-Harsil (Uttarkashi) | 5 Aug 2025 | Flash flood; ice-patch collapse on the Srikanta glacier (ISRO)
  [27:3:11.8] Nepal GLOF floods | 26 Aug 2026 | 26 GLOFs since the 1970s (UNDP)
[27:3:12] ~~ Sources: Rocket Disaster Management p.45-46; PMF Internal Security & DM 02 p.57-59 (book p.132-134); PMF Indian Physical Geography 01 p.54 (Chorabari, Rudraprayag); NDMA GLOF Guidelines Oct 2020 (nidm.gov.in/PDF/pubs/NDMA/29.pdf, via web search); app notes 4:27 sub 4
## SUB 4 | Droughts
[27:4:0] # Nature and types (asked 8 times)
[27:4:1] - Prolonged deficiency of rainfall or moisture → water scarcity; **slow-onset**; in India drought is **coterminous with the monsoon** (its failure or delay) – **irregular or delayed monsoons** raise drought chances; >80% of rain falls in under 100 days of the SW monsoon.
[27:4:2] TABLE head: Type | Indicator | Time lag
  [27:4:2.0] Meteorological | Prolonged inadequate rainfall, maldistributed over time and space; rain ≥ 25% below LPA (degree of dryness + length of dry period) | Immediate
  [27:4:2.1] Agricultural | Soil-moisture deficit → crop failure | Intermediate
  [27:4:2.2] Hydrological | Fall in reservoirs, rivers, aquifers | Weeks to months
  [27:4:2.3] Socio-economic | Demand exceeds supply; water/food shortage hits livelihoods and markets | Long-term
  [27:4:2.4] Ecological | Ecosystem productivity collapses | —
[27:4:3] - **IMD**: deficiency **26–50%** of LPA = **moderate**; **over 50%** = **severe** drought (no separate "extreme" category).
[27:4:4] # Local names of drought (NCERT)
[27:4:5] TABLE head: Scarcity | Name
  [27:4:5.0] Food grains | Akal
  [27:4:5.1] Fodder | Trinkal
  [27:4:5.2] Water | Jalkal
  [27:4:5.3] All three | Trikal (most devastating)
[27:4:6] # Triggers, consequences, regions
[27:4:7] - Triggers: delayed/erratic monsoon, breaks, heat and evaporation; **El Nino**. Normal SW-monsoon rain **868.6 mm** (1971–2020).
[27:4:8] - Long-term consequences of recurrent drought: **groundwater depletion, land degradation, desertification, distress (rural) migration**, debt, lower farm output.
[27:4:9] - Drought is a **recurrent** hazard that can **regularly affect all regions** of India (APPSC key); 68% of cultivable area is vulnerable.
[27:4:10] - Regions: **Western Rajasthan, Kutch-Saurashtra, Marathwada-Vidarbha, interior Karnataka, Rayalaseema (AP)** – not Lower Assam. **Rayalaseema**: **deep borewells** worsen groundwater loss.
[27:4:11] - Management: NDMA Guidelines 2010; Manual for Drought Management 2016 (Agriculture); nodal ministry **Agriculture**.
[27:4:12] ~~ Sources: Rocket Disaster Management p.22-23; NCERT summary (OnlyIAS Indian Geography 05) p.27-28; PMF Internal Security & DM 02 p.64 (book p.136); NCERT India Physical Environment ch.7 (jalkal, trikal – general knowledge, not checked); app notes 4:27 sub 6
[27:4:13] - **Not a cause of drought:** high soil salt concentration (a soil-salinity problem); droughts come from rainfall deficit, high heat and evaporation, deforestation and over-use of groundwater.

## SUB 6 | Urban Floods and Major Floods
[27:6:0] # Urban flooding
[27:6:1] - Causes: intense rain, cloudbursts, cyclone rain; **encroached drains and lakes**, **wetland destruction**, impervious surfaces, **floodplain construction**, poor storm drains, **high tides**. **Wetland restoration** reduces it. Nodal ministry: **Housing and Urban Affairs**.

## SUB 7 | Storm Surges and the Coastline
[27:7:0] - **Storm surge** = abnormal sea rise pushed by **cyclone winds and low pressure**; a **high tide** raises the baseline; **sea-level rise** worsens surge flooding (more, not less, exposure); heavy coastal rain causes drainage congestion; brings **salinity**.
[27:7:1] - **East coast** gets far more cyclones than the west (Bay of Bengal : Arabian Sea about 4 : 1).
[27:7:2] - Coastal defence: **mangroves, shelter belts, dune stabilisation, seawalls**.
[27:7:3] - **Coastline remeasured (2023–24)** by the **National Hydrographic Office with the Survey of India**, reported Dec 2024: **11,098.81 km**; **Gujarat** 1st (2,340.62 km), Tamil Nadu 2nd, **Andhra Pradesh 3rd (1,053.07 km)**.
[27:7:4] - **AP pattern**: coastal belt – cyclones and storm surges; deltas – floods; **Rayalaseema – drought**.

## SUB 8 | Related Hazards – Landslides, Avalanches, Nodal Ministries and Major Disasters
[27:8:0] # Landslides
[27:8:1] - **Mass movement of rocks, debris or earth down mountain slopes or riverbanks** under gravity; both **geological and hydro-meteorological**; **prolonged rainfall** is the main trigger. About **12.6%** of land landslide-prone.
[27:8:2] - Zones: Himalaya; **Western Ghats** (the other major zone); NE Himalaya (**jhum cultivation**); Eastern Ghats – **Araku region** (AP). Rising frequency: **unregulated construction on unstable slopes**, road widening.
[27:8:3] - **GSI** = nodal agency; **National Landslide Forecasting Centre**; **NDMA Guidelines 2009** (landslides and snow avalanches); **National Landslide Risk Management Strategy 2019**; nodal ministry **Mines**. **Wayanad (Kerala) 2024** = landslide.
[27:8:4] # Avalanches
[27:8:5] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [27:8:5.0] Slab | Large cohesive slab breaks off – most dangerous
  [27:8:5.1] Loose snow (sluff) | Small dry slide
  [27:8:5.2] Ice | Ice masses from glaciers
  [27:8:5.3] Rock/debris | Rock, soil and snow
[27:8:6] - **Rapid flow of snow, ice and debris down a mountain slope**; slopes **30°–45°**; human triggers: **skiing, mountaineering**, explosives. **J&K most affected**; **Lahaul-Spiti, Kinnaur – Himachal Pradesh**.
[27:8:7] - Nodal **Ministry of Defence**; **DGRE** (earlier **SASE**, DRDO) issues **daily winter bulletins**; **BRO** builds roads and protection structures. NDMA guidelines: **structural** (snow nets, bridges, galleries) **and non-structural** (**hazard zonation**) measures.
[27:8:8] - **Mana avalanche (28 Feb 2025)**: BRO camp near **Mana** village (Badrinath); 54 workers, 46 rescued, **8 died**.

=== NOTES 4:28 | Disaster Management Framework – Dm Act 2005, Ndma, Ndrf, Early Warning Systems
## SUB 0 | Disaster Management Cycle – Phases and Measures
[28:0:0] # Phases (asked about 45 times)
[28:0:1] TABLE head: Phase | Stage | Measures
  [28:0:1.0] Prevention | Pre-disaster | Proactive, long-term; stops a hazard becoming a disaster: land-use zoning, regulating deforestation, control of industrial siting
  [28:0:1.1] Mitigation | Pre-disaster | Reduces the damaging effects / risk (lowers the likelihood of a disaster); most cost-effective phase: quake-resistant buildings, building codes, embankments, afforestation, insurance, hazard maps, retrofitting; vulnerability analysis and risk assessment
  [28:0:1.2] Preparedness | Pre-disaster | "First and critical step": early warning (CAP-SACHET), public education on evacuation routes, mock / evacuation drills (practise escape routes), stockpiling food, water and supplies, Aapda Mitra, Emergency Operation Centres, contingency plans
  [28:0:1.3] Response | During / just after | Time-critical: search and rescue (the major post-disaster operation), evacuation, first aid, temporary shelter, food and water, law and order
  [28:0:1.4] Relief | During / post | Providing food, shelter and medical help to victims
  [28:0:1.5] Recovery (rehabilitation + reconstruction) | Post-disaster | Rebuilding infrastructure and restoring normalcy – restore the area to its pre-disaster state, livelihoods, compensation, psycho-social care, Build Back Better
[28:0:2] - **Four phases** of the DM cycle (generally **4**): **Mitigation, Preparedness, Response, Recovery** (Rocket: "Mitigation, Preparation, Response, Recovery"). **Eradication**, rescue-only or risk-assessment lists are not the cycle.
[28:0:3] - **Stages**: **Pre-disaster = prevention + mitigation + preparedness**; **during = response**; **post-disaster = recovery, rehabilitation, reconstruction**. **Recovery / reconstruction are NOT pre-disaster**; **prevention and mitigation are NOT post-disaster**.
[28:0:4] - After a disaster the phases are **sequential and sometimes simultaneous**; the cycle is **continuous** – lessons from recovery feed prevention.
[28:0:5] - Sequences: during – early warning → evacuation → rescue → shelters → emergency medical / water; after – **relief → restoration → reconstruction → resilient rebuilding**.
[28:0:6] - DM needs a **proactive strategy** (not reactive); its main aim is **saving lives and reducing damage**; a contingency plan for people should be in **simple language with basic information and steps**.
[28:0:7] # Mitigation – types and examples
[28:0:8] TABLE head: Strategy | Example / meaning
  [28:0:8.0] Structural mitigation | Seismic-resistant buildings, flood barriers, embankments, cyclone shelters; earthquakes – building codes; cyclones – planting trees along the coast
  [28:0:8.1] Non-structural mitigation | Public awareness campaigns / public education, community awareness, early warning, land-use rules
  [28:0:8.2] Early warning system | Tsunami detection buoys; a crucial component of mitigation because it gives timely information for preparedness and response (A and R true, R explains A)

[28:0:9] - Mitigation **does not** mean evolving a **single strategy for all disasters**; it gives rapid recovery, fewer deaths and lower financial impact. It involves **community action** (CBDM – involving local people in preparedness and response).

[28:0:19] - Phase definitions (FEMA): **mitigation** – minimising the effects of disaster; **preparedness** – planning how to respond; **response** – efforts to minimise the hazards created by a disaster; **recovery** – returning the community to normal.

## SUB 5 | Key Concepts – Hazard, Disaster, Vulnerability, Types
[28:5:0] # Terms (asked about 10 times)
[28:5:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [28:5:1.0] Hazard | Dangerous condition or event with the potential to harm; an earthquake in an uninhabited desert is not a disaster – a hazard / natural event
  [28:5:1.1] Disaster | Occurs when people are unable to cope with the impact of a hazard: Hazard × Vulnerability with low capacity; French "desastre" = bad star
  [28:5:1.2] Vulnerability | Conditions raising susceptibility: limited health care, elderly / disabled residents, poverty, weak housing
  [28:5:1.3] Capacity | Strengths that reduce risk: strong social networks and community cohesion, volunteerism (communities recover better), early warning
  [28:5:1.4] Risk | Likelihood of loss; rises with hazard, exposure, vulnerability; falls with coping capacity
[28:5:2] - **Least** contributor to vulnerability: **strong social networks and community cohesion** (a capacity).
[28:5:3] - Vulnerability types: **physical** (weak houses), **social** (age, gender, caste, disability), **economic** (poverty, daily wage), **environmental** (mangrove loss, deforestation), **systemic/institutional** (weak governance, poor coordination, weak early warning). Community factors: kutcha housing, thin health infrastructure, weak early warning; **high crop-insurance coverage reduces** vulnerability.
[28:5:4] # Types of disasters
[28:5:5] TABLE head: Type | Examples
  [28:5:5.0] Geological | Earthquakes, landslides, tsunamis, volcanoes
  [28:5:5.1] Hydro-meteorological (water and climate) | Cyclones, floods, droughts, cloudbursts, heat waves
  [28:5:5.2] Biological | Epidemics, pest attacks
  [28:5:5.3] Man-made (chemical / industrial, accidents, societal) | Bhopal gas leak (MIC, 2–3 Dec 1984), car accidents, stampedes, fires
[28:5:6] - Four broad emergency types: **natural disasters, environmental emergencies, complex emergencies, pandemic emergencies** – **pandemic emergency** = **sudden onset of contagious disease** that affects health, disrupts services and businesses, with high economic and social cost.
[28:5:7] - Natural disasters cause most damage in **developing countries**; deaths from natural disasters in India 1980–2010: **1,43,039**.
