## SUB 0 | Disaster Management Cycle – Phases and Measures
[0:0] # Phases (asked about 45 times)
[0:1] TABLE head: Phase | Stage | Measures
  [0:1.0] Prevention | Pre-disaster | Proactive, long-term; stops a hazard becoming a disaster: land-use zoning, regulating deforestation, control of industrial siting
  [0:1.1] Mitigation | Pre-disaster | Reduces the damaging effects / risk (lowers the likelihood of a disaster); most cost-effective phase: quake-resistant buildings, building codes, embankments, afforestation, insurance, hazard maps, retrofitting; vulnerability analysis and risk assessment
  [0:1.2] Preparedness | Pre-disaster | "First and critical step": early warning (CAP-SACHET), public education on evacuation routes, mock / evacuation drills (practise escape routes), stockpiling food, water and supplies, Aapda Mitra, Emergency Operation Centres, contingency plans
  [0:1.3] Response | During / just after | Time-critical: search and rescue (the major post-disaster operation), evacuation, first aid, temporary shelter, food and water, law and order
  [0:1.4] Relief | During / post | Providing food, shelter and medical help to victims
  [0:1.5] Recovery (rehabilitation + reconstruction) | Post-disaster | Rebuilding infrastructure and restoring normalcy – restore the area to its pre-disaster state, livelihoods, compensation, psycho-social care, Build Back Better
[0:2] - **Four phases** of the DM cycle (generally **4**): **Mitigation, Preparedness, Response, Recovery** (Rocket: "Mitigation, Preparation, Response, Recovery"). **Eradication**, rescue-only or risk-assessment lists are not the cycle.
[0:3] - **Stages**: **Pre-disaster = prevention + mitigation + preparedness**; **during = response**; **post-disaster = recovery, rehabilitation, reconstruction**. **Recovery / reconstruction are NOT pre-disaster**; **prevention and mitigation are NOT post-disaster**.
[0:4] - After a disaster the phases are **sequential and sometimes simultaneous**; the cycle is **continuous** – lessons from recovery feed prevention.
[0:5] - Sequences: during – early warning → evacuation → rescue → shelters → emergency medical / water; after – **relief → restoration → reconstruction → resilient rebuilding**.
[0:6] - DM needs a **proactive strategy** (not reactive); its main aim is **saving lives and reducing damage**; a contingency plan for people should be in **simple language with basic information and steps**.
[0:7] # Mitigation – types and examples
[0:8] TABLE head: Strategy | Example / meaning
  [0:8.0] Structural mitigation | Seismic-resistant buildings, flood barriers, embankments, cyclone shelters; earthquakes – building codes; cyclones – planting trees along the coast
  [0:8.1] Non-structural mitigation | Public awareness campaigns / public education, community awareness, early warning, land-use rules
  [0:8.2] Early warning system | Tsunami detection buoys; a crucial component of mitigation because it gives timely information for preparedness and response (A and R true, R explains A)
  [0:8.3] Seismic retrofitting | Strengthening existing (old) buildings to withstand earthquakes
  [0:8.4] Floodplain zoning | Regulating land use to restrict construction in flood-prone areas
  [0:8.5] Afforestation | Planting trees to reduce landslide and flood risk
  [0:8.6] Disaster-resistant design | Materials and techniques that minimise damage
[0:9] - Mitigation **does not** mean evolving a **single strategy for all disasters**; it gives rapid recovery, fewer deaths and lower financial impact. It involves **community action** (CBDM – involving local people in preparedness and response).
[0:10] - Cyclone pre-disaster mitigation: revamp drainage, **conserve wetlands**, cyclone shelters – **removing coastal trees/forests is NOT** mitigation.
[0:11] - Educated communities are more likely to **follow land-use rules and early-warning instructions** (education → long-term mitigation).
[0:12] - Types of mitigation: **avoidance, minimization, compensatory**; hazard-specific control activity – e.g. **flood levees**.
[0:13] - Terms: **HAZOP** study = assessment of the **adequacy of safety measures**; **MAT** = **Mitigation Assessment Team**; **hazard map** = identifies **areas at risk for specific disasters**.
[0:14] - **First responder**: the **community**. Urban response = civic bodies + fire brigade + police; the **district administration** has basic responsibility (Collector pivotal).
[0:15] - Search and rescue principles (APPSC key): **Search, Find, Rescue**.
[0:16] > Traps: mitigation, not prevention, is the phase "reducing likelihood/effects" when prevention is not offered; preparedness = drills, stockpiling, evacuation education; response = during the disaster.

## SUB 1 | Disaster Management Act, 2005 and the Authorities
[1:0] # The Act (asked about 25 times)
[1:1] - **Act No. 53 of 2005**; **Presidential assent 23 December 2005** (= "passed / enacted" date in exams); published in the **Gazette of India on 26 December 2005**; in force progressively from 2006; **11 Chapters, 79 Sections**; whole of India.
[1:2] - Enacted after the **2004 Indian Ocean tsunami**; gives the legal and institutional framework at national, state and district levels.
[1:3] - s.2(d) **disaster** = catastrophe, mishap, calamity or grave occurrence from natural or man-made causes, accident or negligence, **beyond the coping capacity of the community of the affected area**. Mitigation = measures to **reduce the risk, impact or effects** of a disaster.

## SUB 5 | NDRF, SDRF and Disaster Funds
[5:0] # National Disaster Response Force (asked about 13 times)
[5:1] - **NDRF = National Disaster Response Force** (not "Relief / Rescue / Reserve Force"); constituted under **s.44** of the DM Act for specialised response to **natural and man-made disasters (incl. CBRN)**; raised / operational **2006**; **Raising Day 19 January**; world's largest dedicated response force.
[5:2] - Under **MHA**; superintendence of **NDMA**; DG from the senior **IPS** cadre; HQ **New Delhi**; motto **"Aapada Seva Sadaiv Sarvatra"**.
[5:3] - Battalions: initial **8** → **12** (2018–19 keys; "as of December 2019" = 12) → **16** now; ~1,149 personnel each; **18** search-and-rescue teams per battalion.
[5:4] - Parent forces (deputation): **BSF, CRPF, CISF, ITBP, SSB, Assam Rifles** (central paramilitary forces).
[5:5] - Skills: **CSSR** (Collapsed Structure Search and Rescue), flood and mountain rescue, medical first response, CBRN; teams placed by **vulnerability profile**.
[5:6] - **AP**: **10th Battalion**; regional HQ for **AP, Telangana and Karnataka = Vijayawada**.
[5:7] - **Budget estimate 2021-22: ₹1,209 crore** (₹1,209.41 crore; RE ₹1,313 crore).
[5:8] - **NDRF Academy**: **Nagpur (2018)**.
[5:9] # State Disaster Response Force
[5:10] - Raised by States/UTs; first responder for the first 24–48 hours; **trained by NDRF/NDRF Academy**; **battalion** model; drawn from **State Police, Armed Police, Fire Services, Home Guards, Civil Defence**. Not yet in all states → strain on NDRF.

## SUB 6 | Key Concepts – Hazard, Disaster, Vulnerability, Types
[6:0] # Terms (asked about 10 times)
[6:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [6:1.0] Hazard | Dangerous condition or event with the potential to harm; an earthquake in an uninhabited desert is not a disaster – a hazard / natural event
  [6:1.1] Disaster | Occurs when people are unable to cope with the impact of a hazard: Hazard × Vulnerability with low capacity; French "desastre" = bad star
  [6:1.2] Vulnerability | Conditions raising susceptibility: limited health care, elderly / disabled residents, poverty, weak housing
  [6:1.3] Capacity | Strengths that reduce risk: strong social networks and community cohesion, volunteerism (communities recover better), early warning
  [6:1.4] Risk | Likelihood of loss; rises with hazard, exposure, vulnerability; falls with coping capacity
[6:2] - **Least** contributor to vulnerability: **strong social networks and community cohesion** (a capacity).
[6:3] - Vulnerability types: **physical** (weak houses), **social** (age, gender, caste, disability), **economic** (poverty, daily wage), **environmental** (mangrove loss, deforestation), **systemic/institutional** (weak governance, poor coordination, weak early warning). Community factors: kutcha housing, thin health infrastructure, weak early warning; **high crop-insurance coverage reduces** vulnerability.
[6:4] # Types of disasters
[6:5] TABLE head: Type | Examples
  [6:5.0] Geological | Earthquakes, landslides, tsunamis, volcanoes
  [6:5.1] Hydro-meteorological (water and climate) | Cyclones, floods, droughts, cloudbursts, heat waves
  [6:5.2] Biological | Epidemics, pest attacks
  [6:5.3] Man-made (chemical / industrial, accidents, societal) | Bhopal gas leak (MIC, 2–3 Dec 1984), car accidents, stampedes, fires
[6:6] - Four broad emergency types: **natural disasters, environmental emergencies, complex emergencies, pandemic emergencies** – **pandemic emergency** = **sudden onset of contagious disease** that affects health, disrupts services and businesses, with high economic and social cost.
[6:7] - Natural disasters cause most damage in **developing countries**; deaths from natural disasters in India 1980–2010: **1,43,039**.

## SUB 7 | Technology – GIS and Remote Sensing
[7:0] # GIS in disaster management
[7:1] - **GIS** stores, analyses and presents **spatial data**; maps hazard zones and vulnerable people; in floods it **analyses flood patterns and topography**; it cannot give details of individual victims.

  [7:2.1] Preparedness | Evacuation route planning, resource mapping

  [7:2.4] Landslide | Susceptibility mapping (GSI NLSM)
  [7:2.5] Drought | NADAMS
  [7:2.6] Tsunami | Inundation modelling (INCOIS)
