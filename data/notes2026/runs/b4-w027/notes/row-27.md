=== Floods – Extent, Causes and Management
# Nature and extent (asked 18 times)
- Flood = overflow of water onto normally dry land when rivers or other water bodies cannot contain it; **rapid-onset**; the **most frequent / most common natural disaster in India**; can be **mitigated through early warning**.
- **Rashtriya Barh Ayog (National Flood Commission)**, set up **1976**, report **1980**: of India's **329 mha**, **more than 40 mha** (about **12%** of the area) is flood-prone; a recent estimate is about **49 mha**. About **10%** of India's area is actually flood-affected.
- Worst-affected states: **Assam and Bihar**, then **Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal**; **most flood-prone state: Bihar** (UPPCS 2000). Tamil Nadu floods in Nov–Jan (retreating monsoon).
- About **200 million** people are exposed to recurring floods every year (APPSC Group 1 2012 key).
- **Flood control is a State subject**; the Centre helps through **FMBAP** (Flood Management and Border Areas Programme, 2021–26).
# Causes
| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Heavy / prolonged rainfall** | **Primary cause of riverine flooding** in low-lying areas |
| Cloudburst | Very intense rain over a small area |
| Snowmelt | Sudden rise in river flow |
| Siltation (from soil erosion) | Reduces channel depth and capacity |
| Embankment breach | Water escapes the channel |
| **Deforestation** | **Human factor**: increases runoff |
| Floodplain encroachment | Raises exposure |
| **Poor drainage** | Water accumulates |
| **Dam failure** | Sudden downstream discharge; **overtopping** = water spills over the crest |
- Major causes in India = **heavy rainfall, poor drainage, deforestation and dam failure** (not high temperature, windstorms or ocean acidification).
- A landslide can **block a river**: **Pareechu river (Tibet)**, August **2004** landslide lake – flood threat on the **Sutlej** in Himachal Pradesh.
# Management
| Structural | Non-structural |
|---|---|
| Embankments, dams and reservoirs, channel and drainage improvement, diversion channels | **Flood-plain zoning**, flood insurance, flood-proofing, **forecasting and warning**, watershed management |
- **Afforestation** of flood-prone areas / catchments = **flood control (mitigation)** – forests **increase infiltration and interception**; wetlands and floodplains store water; mangroves buffer coasts.
- **Watershed structures** (check dams, percolation tanks) mitigate both **drought and flood** – not tornadoes or fire.
- **Forecasting**: **Central Water Commission (CWC)** (Jal Shakti) runs flood forecasting – using **GIS, radar** (Doppler) and **remote sensing / satellite** data (not the Richter or Pascal scale or MRI).
- In flood response, high-resolution satellite imagery and GIS map the **inundated area**, from which the **number of people affected** and damage are estimated.
- **Odisha Flood Hazard Atlas**: built from satellite images of historic flood inundation **2001–2018** (Rocket: 2000–2018).

=== Tsunamis – Meaning, Causes, Behaviour and the 2004 Tsunami
# Meaning (asked 17 times)
- **Tsunami** = Japanese word: **"tsu" = harbour + "nami" = wave** ("harbour wave") – **not Latin, Arabic or Hebrew** (UKPSC 2005; CDS 2017 "Latin word" = the wrong statement).
- A **series of waves** of very long wavelength (over 500 km) and long period (10 minutes to 2 hours) – **not a single wave**; the **first wave is often not the largest**, later waves can be bigger.
- A **natural disaster**; can strike at **any time of day or night**. **Not a "tidal wave"** – tides (gravity of Moon and Sun) play **no role** in generating it (CDS 2012).
# Causes
| Cause | Note |
|---|---|
| **Undersea earthquakes** | **Main / most common cause** – sudden vertical displacement of the sea floor at **subduction zones** (shocks above about 7.5) |
| Submarine or coastal **landslides** | Displace water |
| **Volcanic eruptions** | E.g. Krakatoa 1883 (about 40 m wave), Anak Krakatau 2018 |
| Explosions, meteorite (bolide) impacts | Rare |
- **Not caused by hurricanes / cyclones** (they raise **storm surges**) or deserts (CDS 2010, asked twice).
- Most tsunamis occur in the **Pacific Ocean** (Ring of Fire – Alaska, Japan, Philippines, Indonesia).
- **Coastal States/UTs** of India are vulnerable to **tsunamis** (not volcanic eruptions).
# Behaviour
| Deep ocean | Near the coast |
|---|---|
| Very long wavelength; height under 1 m ("slight swell" about 30 cm); speed **hundreds of km/h** (about **700–850 km/h** at 4,000–6,000 m depth) | **Slows down** (speed depends on depth); wavelength shortens, **height rises sharply** (shoaling) – 15 m or more; on coastal plains it moves at roughly **50 km/h** |
- Impact is **less over the open ocean, more near the coast**; ships at sea hardly notice it.
- **Warning sign: drawdown** – the sea **suddenly recedes** (sea level falls quickly) before the wave hits; also strong quake shaking.
# 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami
- **26 December 2004**; **Sumatra-Andaman earthquake, Mw about 9.1** (Indian plate under the Burma plate); speed about 800 km/h, falling to under 70 km/h near shore.
- Hit **Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, Kerala, Andaman & Nicobar** – **not Gujarat**; **worst-hit state Tamil Nadu**, worst district **Nagapattinam**; India: over **12,000 deaths**; Indira Point submerged.
- **Mangroves** acted as a safety hedge: their **extensive prop roots** (and pneumatophores) are **not uprooted by storms and tides** and absorb wave energy (UPSC 2011). Sand dunes and coastal cliffs also block waves; **open water bodies give no protection**.
- **Fukushima (Japan), 11 March 2011**: Mw 9.0 Tohoku earthquake + tsunami → nuclear radiation.
# Risk zones and warning
| Risk | States/UTs |
|---|---|
| Very high | **Andaman & Nicobar** (near the Sunda subduction zone) |
| High | Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, Odisha |
| Moderate | Kerala, Karnataka, Goa, Maharashtra, Gujarat |
- Sources for India: **Sunda-Andaman** subduction zone (east) and the **Makran** zone (west coast).
- **Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC)** at **INCOIS, Hyderabad** (MoES): detects tsunamigenic quakes within **10 minutes** and can warn **25 Indian Ocean countries**; **NIOT (Nellore)** OMNI buoys feed it. International Tsunami Information Centre: **Honolulu**. World Tsunami Awareness Day: **5 November**.
> Traps: Japanese (not Latin); tides play no role; hurricanes do not cause tsunamis; the wave slows near the shore; the first wave may not be the largest.

=== Disasters – Meaning, Classification and Onset
# Meaning (asked 15 times)
- **Disaster = Hazard × Vulnerability** (with low capacity to cope); French "desastre" = "bad star".
- Natural disasters cause most damage in **developing countries**; the **Bhopal gas tragedy** was **man-made**; **India is not disaster-free**; **mangroves reduce cyclone impact** → UPPCS 2018 (asked twice): **1, 2 and 4**.
- Globally **drought (and famine)** are the **deadliest** weather/climate disasters, followed by **floods** (NCERT); **floods** cause the largest economic losses over the long run and affect the most people.
# Classification
| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| Geological | Earthquakes, tsunamis, landslides, volcanic eruptions, dam burst, mine fire |
| Hydrological / hydro-meteorological | Floods, flash floods, cloudbursts, storm surges, cyclones |
| **Climatological** | **Droughts**, heatwaves, cold waves |
| Biological | Epidemics, pest attacks, food poisoning |
| **Accident-related** (HPC 1999) | Urban/forest **fires**, road/rail/air accidents, building collapse, **mine flooding** |
| Man-made: chemical, industrial, nuclear | **Bhopal gas tragedy**, Chernobyl |
- **Drought is not geological** (it is climatological).
- **Mine flooding** = **accident-related** disaster (not geological or water-and-climate).
- **Avalanche** = classed as a **hydro-geological** disaster (snow + steep slope) in Indian keys; international (EM-DAT) lists it as hydrological.
- **Socio-natural hazards** = natural + human factors: floods worsened by deforestation, **landslides due to unscientific construction**.
- **Man-made hazard**: **fires** (not landslides, avalanches or droughts); **not man-made: mud slides** (natural) – deforestation and food poisoning come from human action.
- **Chennai floods (2015)** = the most human-induced (wetland and lake encroachment); the Chennai tsunami, Anantapur low rainfall and Kedarnath flood had natural triggers.
# Onset
| Rapid-onset | Slow-onset |
|---|---|
| **Cyclone**, flood, tsunami, earthquake, flash flood, cloudburst | **Drought** ("creeping emergency / creeping hazard"), **pest infestation**, environmental degradation |

=== Flash Floods, Cloudbursts and GLOFs
# Flash floods (asked 10 times)
- A flood that **occurs suddenly, unexpectedly and for a short duration** – from **local precipitation of high intensity** (intense rain, cloudbursts), or dam / glacial-lake / landslide-lake bursts; little warning time; main danger is **debris**; also landslides.
- Heavy rain, urban settings or blocked drains are only possible causes – not the definition.
- Vulnerable: **mountains, steep catchments and urban areas**. **IMD** gives flash-flood guidance for India + **Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal**.
# Cloudburst
- **Sudden, extremely heavy rainfall for a short period over a small area** (CDS 2012: copious rain lasting a few minutes) – **IMD: at least 100 mm (10 cm) in one hour over about 20–30 sq km**.
- Localised, with **thunder and lightning**; **mostly in hilly areas** (1,000–2,500 m; orographic lifting); can give **250–300 mm in a couple of hours**; occurs **day or night** → CDS 2017: **I, II and III**.
- Highly localised → very hard to forecast (Doppler radar, nowcasting). **HKH "monsoon paradox"**: erratic seasonal rain plus more short, intense cloudbursts.
| Region | Areas | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Himalaya | Uttarakhand, HP, J&K, Ladakh, Sikkim | Steep slopes, orographic effect |
| Western Ghats | Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra | Intense rain on the **windward** side |
| AP Eastern Ghats agency areas | Narrow valleys | Cloudburst **flash floods** |
# GLOFs and events
- **GLOF**: glacier retreat enlarges a **proglacial (moraine-dammed) lake**, whose dam bursts. **NDMA GLOF Guidelines (October 2020)**: population risk highest in **Sikkim and Jammu & Kashmir**.
| Event | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Leh cloudburst | 2010 | Ladakh |
| **Kedarnath** | June 2013 | Cloudburst + GLOF; **Chorabari (Chaurabari) Glacier** in **Uttarakhand**, **north of the temple**; lake **Gandhi Sarovar** |
| Operation **Phuktal** | 2015 | Army + NDMA blasted a landslide-debris lake on the **Phuktal river** (Zanskar, **Kargil**) to prevent flash floods |
| Chamoli glacier burst | 2021 | Uttarakhand |
| South Lhonak GLOF | Oct 2023 | Sikkim (Teesta III dam) |
| HP flash floods | 2023 | Unregulated slope construction |
| Mandi (HP) | 2025 | Nearly 20 times normal rain |
| **Dharali-Harsil** (Uttarkashi) | 5 Aug 2025 | **Flash flood**; ice-patch collapse on the **Srikanta glacier** (ISRO) |
| Nepal GLOF floods | 26 Aug 2026 | 26 GLOFs since the 1970s (UNDP) |

=== India's Vulnerability Profile
# Hazard exposure (asked 8 times)
| Hazard | Exposure | Region |
|---|---|---|
| Earthquake | **58.6% of landmass** (moderate to very high intensity; PMF rounds to 59%) | Himalaya, Kutch |
| Flood | **>40 mha (about 12%)** | Brahmaputra-Ganga plains |
| Drought | **About 68% of cultivable area** | Rajasthan, Deccan interior |
| Cyclone / tsunami / storm surge | **About 5,700 km** of the about 7,516 km coastline (**about 76%**, not 50%) | East coast |
| Landslide | About 12.6% of land (about 0.42 million sq km) | Himalaya, Western Ghats (not Thar) |
- APPSC DAO: the **incorrect** statement is "50% of the coastline is prone to cyclones and tsunamis" → **only D**.
- **27 of 35** (now 36) States/UTs are **disaster-prone** (NIDM / NDMA); some older books give 25.
- Cyclones affect **13 coastal States/UTs** and **84 coastal districts**.
- **1980–2010**: **1,43,039** people killed by natural disasters in India (APPSC Group 1 key; UNISDR/EM-DAT figures).
- **Odisha** (Bay of Bengal coast) faces the **most natural disasters** – cyclones, floods and droughts (UP Lower 2002).
- India loses about **0.4% of GDP** a year to disasters (1990–2024). **World Risk Index 2025**: India **2nd** most vulnerable, after the **Philippines**.
- **2025**: about 331 days of climate impacts, **over 4,000 deaths**; heaviest toll in **Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand**.
# Displacement data (IDMC)
- **IDMC** (Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre, **Geneva**) publishes the **Global Report on Internal Displacement (GRID)**.
- APPSC key: India **seventh** worst climate-disaster-hit country (2020 to May 2021, IDMC).
- **GRID 2025**: India **5.4 million** disaster displacements in **2024**; over **2015–2024** **32.3 million**, **3rd** after China and the Philippines.

=== Droughts
# Nature and types (asked 8 times)
- Prolonged deficiency of rainfall or moisture → water scarcity; **slow-onset**; in India drought is **coterminous with the monsoon** (its failure or delay) – **irregular or delayed monsoons** raise drought chances; >80% of rain falls in under 100 days of the SW monsoon.
| Type | Indicator | Time lag |
|---|---|---|
| **Meteorological** | **Prolonged inadequate rainfall, maldistributed over time and space**; rain ≥ 25% below LPA (degree of dryness + length of dry period) | Immediate |
| Agricultural | Soil-moisture deficit → crop failure | Intermediate |
| Hydrological | Fall in reservoirs, rivers, aquifers | Weeks to months |
| Socio-economic | Demand exceeds supply; water/food shortage hits livelihoods and markets | Long-term |
| Ecological | Ecosystem productivity collapses | — |
- **IMD**: deficiency **26–50%** of LPA = **moderate**; **over 50%** = **severe** drought (no separate "extreme" category).
# Local names of drought (NCERT)
| Scarcity | Name |
|---|---|
| Food grains | **Akal** |
| Fodder | **Trinkal** |
| Water | **Jalkal** |
| All three | **Trikal** (most devastating) |
# Triggers, consequences, regions
- Triggers: delayed/erratic monsoon, breaks, heat and evaporation; **El Nino**. Normal SW-monsoon rain **868.6 mm** (1971–2020).
- Long-term consequences of recurrent drought: **groundwater depletion, land degradation, desertification, distress (rural) migration**, debt, lower farm output.
- Drought is a **recurrent** hazard that can **regularly affect all regions** of India (APPSC key); 68% of cultivable area is vulnerable.
- Regions: **Western Rajasthan, Kutch-Saurashtra, Marathwada-Vidarbha, interior Karnataka, Rayalaseema (AP)** – not Lower Assam. **Rayalaseema**: **deep borewells** worsen groundwater loss.
- Management: NDMA Guidelines 2010; Manual for Drought Management 2016 (Agriculture); nodal ministry **Agriculture**.

=== Urban Floods and Major Floods
# Urban flooding
- Causes: intense rain, cloudbursts, cyclone rain; **encroached drains and lakes**, **wetland destruction**, impervious surfaces, **floodplain construction**, poor storm drains, **high tides**. **Wetland restoration** reduces it. Nodal ministry: **Housing and Urban Affairs**.
| Flood | Year | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Mumbai | 26 July 2005 | **944 mm** in 24 h; drainage failure |
| Bihar (Kosi) | 2008 | **Embankment breach** |
| Chennai | Dec 2015 | Extreme rain; **wetland destruction** |
| Kerala | Aug 2018 | Heavy rain; **dam releases** |
- **AP**: **Vijayawada – Budameru** rivulet. **AP City Flood Alert (July 2026)**: AI platform (RTGS + APSDMA + IIT Gandhinagar), first in **Vijayawada**.
# Types of flood

|---|---|---|
| **River (riverine)** | Discharge **exceeds channel capacity** | Crop damage, bank erosion |
| Flash | Sudden intense rain, **cloudbursts** | Debris, **landslides** |
| Urban | Rain + poor drainage | Infrastructure collapse |
| **Coastal** | **Storm surge, cyclones, high tides** | Inundation, **salinity intrusion** |
| GLOF | Proglacial lake burst | Downstream flash flood |
| Dam failure | Breach | Downstream flash flooding |

=== Storm Surges and the Coastline
- **Storm surge** = abnormal sea rise pushed by **cyclone winds and low pressure**; a **high tide** raises the baseline; **sea-level rise** worsens surge flooding (more, not less, exposure); heavy coastal rain causes drainage congestion; brings **salinity**.
- **East coast** gets far more cyclones than the west (Bay of Bengal : Arabian Sea about 4 : 1).
- Coastal defence: **mangroves, shelter belts, dune stabilisation, seawalls**.
- **Coastline remeasured (2023–24)** by the **National Hydrographic Office with the Survey of India**, reported Dec 2024: **11,098.81 km**; **Gujarat** 1st (2,340.62 km), Tamil Nadu 2nd, **Andhra Pradesh 3rd (1,053.07 km)**.
- **AP pattern**: coastal belt – cyclones and storm surges; deltas – floods; **Rayalaseema – drought**.

=== Related Hazards – Landslides, Avalanches, Nodal Ministries and Major Disasters
# Landslides
- **Mass movement of rocks, debris or earth down mountain slopes or riverbanks** under gravity; both **geological and hydro-meteorological**; **prolonged rainfall** is the main trigger. About **12.6%** of land landslide-prone.
- Zones: Himalaya; **Western Ghats** (the other major zone); NE Himalaya (**jhum cultivation**); Eastern Ghats – **Araku region** (AP). Rising frequency: **unregulated construction on unstable slopes**, road widening.
- **GSI** = nodal agency; **National Landslide Forecasting Centre**; **NDMA Guidelines 2009** (landslides and snow avalanches); **National Landslide Risk Management Strategy 2019**; nodal ministry **Mines**. **Wayanad (Kerala) 2024** = landslide.
# Avalanches
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Slab** | Large cohesive slab breaks off – **most dangerous** |
| Loose snow (sluff) | Small dry slide |
| Ice | Ice masses from glaciers |
| Rock/debris | Rock, soil and snow |
- **Rapid flow of snow, ice and debris down a mountain slope**; slopes **30°–45°**; human triggers: **skiing, mountaineering**, explosives. **J&K most affected**; **Lahaul-Spiti, Kinnaur – Himachal Pradesh**.
- Nodal **Ministry of Defence**; **DGRE** (earlier **SASE**, DRDO) issues **daily winter bulletins**; **BRO** builds roads and protection structures. NDMA guidelines: **structural** (snow nets, bridges, galleries) **and non-structural** (**hazard zonation**) measures.
- **Mana avalanche (28 Feb 2025)**: BRO camp near **Mana** village (Badrinath); 54 workers, 46 rescued, **8 died**.
