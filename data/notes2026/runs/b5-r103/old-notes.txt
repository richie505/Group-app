=== NOTES 5:103 | Solid Waste, Plastic Waste, E-waste, Biomedical and Construction Waste – Rules and Management
## SUB 0 | Solid Waste – Definitions, Categories and Biodegradability
[103:0:0] # Definitions and Categories
[103:0:1] - **Solid waste**: thrown-away material in **solid, liquid, semi-solid or containerised gaseous** form.
[103:0:2] - **E-waste (ITU)**: discarded electrical and electronic equipment and parts **not meant for reuse**.
[103:0:3] - **Biomedical waste**: waste from **diagnosis, treatment or immunisation** of humans or animals (blood, sharps, cultures, expired medicines).
[103:0:4] TABLE head: Category | What it holds
  [103:0:4.0] Municipal | Household + commercial: organics, plastics, paper, metals
  [103:0:4.1] Industrial / Hazardous / E-waste / Biomedical | See sections 3, 7, 8, 9
  [103:0:4.2] Agricultural | Crop residues, paddy husk, cattle dung (biodegradable; dung → biogas under GOBARdhan)
  [103:0:4.3] Construction & demolition | Mostly inert – concrete, bricks, dirt, rocks, debris
[103:0:5] # Hazardous Waste – Four Criteria
[103:0:6] - **Ignitability, corrosivity, reactivity, toxicity** (NOT biodegradability).
[103:0:7] - **Household hazardous waste**: **paints, batteries**, fluorescent lamps, aerosol cans, tyres, pesticides.
[103:0:8] - Healthcare waste: **~85% general non-hazardous, ~15% hazardous** (infectious, chemical, radioactive) (WHO).
[103:0:9] # Biodegradable vs Non-biodegradable
[103:0:10] - **Biodegradable**: broken into simpler, harmless substances by micro-organisms (mainly **bacteria**). **Aerobic** decomposition → **compost**; **anaerobic** → **methane/biogas**.
[103:0:11] TABLE head: Biodegradable | Non-biodegradable
  [103:0:11.0] Sewage, domestic garbage, urine, agricultural residues, cattle dung | Plastic, DDT, asbestos, insecticides
  [103:0:11.1] Food/kitchen waste, vegetable peels, fruit waste, paddy husk, cow-dung | Tin/tin scrap, aluminium foil, metal scrap, lead
  [103:0:11.2] Paper, wood, plant waste, cellulose, starch, proteins | Glass bottles, plastic cups/bags
  [103:0:11.3] Wool (natural keratin fibre), cotton | Synthetic rubber/tyres (natural rubber is biodegradable)
  [103:0:11.4] Polymers: PHBV, Nylon-2-Nylon-6, polylactic acid, polyglycolic acid | Polythene (polymer of ethylene, C2H4 – carbon + hydrogen), PVC (vinyl chloride), polystyrene, nylon, Teflon
[103:0:12] - Biodegradable polymers are eco-friendly because microbes degrade them.
[103:0:13] - Non-biodegradable plastics persist and break into **microplastics**; polythene bags cannot be destroyed.
[103:0:14] "Which is NOT biodegradable": tin, metal, glass, plastic, PVC, insecticides. "Which IS": wool, peels, sewage, rubber.

## SUB 2 | Waste Hierarchy, 3R/5R and Circular Economy
[103:2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [103:2:0.0] 3R | Reduce, Reuse, Recycle ("Regain" is NOT a 3R – APPSC-GS 2025)
  [103:2:0.1] Top priority | Reduction at source (generating less waste)
  [103:2:0.2] Reuse | Using the item again in the same form
  [103:2:0.3] Recycling | Converting waste into other usable materials (APPSC-GS 2024)
  [103:2:0.4] 5R | Adds Refuse and Repurpose (or Recover)
  [103:2:0.5] Least preferred | Landfilling (after reduction, reuse, recycling, recovery)
  [103:2:0.6] RRR Centre | Permanent in every city under SBM-U 2.0 (Reduce, Reuse, Recover)
[103:2:1] - **Legacy waste**: old MSW in dumpsites; releases methane, pollutes groundwater. **Biomining** sorts it into recyclables, RDF, bio-soil and inert fill → supports **circular economy**.

## SUB 3 | Disposal and Treatment Methods
[103:3:0] TABLE head: Method | Process | Suited to
  [103:3:0.0] Sanitary landfill | Compacted, covered, lined cells | Only residual inert waste; last resort; not for unsegregated biodegradable waste
  [103:3:0.1] Composting (aerobic) – Indore method | Turned at intervals; microbes oxidise organics | Wet/organic waste
  [103:3:0.2] Anaerobic pit – Bangalore method | Alternate layers of MSW and night soil; 4–6 months | Organic waste
  [103:3:0.3] Vermicomposting | Earthworms (biological method) | Household organic waste
  [103:3:0.4] Incineration | Controlled burning in oxygen at high heat; may give steam for power; cuts volume ~90% | Hazardous, biomedical
  [103:3:0.5] Pyrolysis | Heating without oxygen → gas, oil, char | Mixed waste; costly
  [103:3:0.6] Plasma gasification | Electricity + high heat, no combustion → syngas; disposes all organic wastes | –
  [103:3:0.7] RDF | Shredded, dehydrated high-calorific MSW | Fuel in cement kilns and thermal power plants
[103:3:1] - **Compost**: earthworms and microbes (bacteria, fungi) **speed up** decomposition in pits. **Vermiculture** = science of raising earthworms.
[103:3:2] - **Pyrolysis and plasma gasification** = waste-to-energy; compost and vermicompost give **manure, not energy**.
[103:3:3] - Urban India uses composting, incineration and landfilling – **"all the above"** (APPSC-G2 2025); **uncontrolled dumping** is not workable.
[103:3:4] - **Disposal trade-offs:** landfill **cheap but long-term harm**; incineration cuts volume but **emits**; composting gives **useful products**; recycling **saves resources**.
[103:3:5] - **APPCB, March 2025**: mandated **RDF in cement kilns** to cut coal use.
[103:3:6] # Waste → Method (APPSC-G2 2023 match)

  [103:3:7.2] Hazardous / biomedical | Incineration (biomedical at CBMWTF)
  [103:3:7.3] Broken glass | Landfilling
  [103:3:7.4] Non-recyclable high-calorific dry waste | RDF / co-processing in cement kilns

## SUB 4 | Solid Waste Rules and Collection Chain
[103:4:0] TABLE head: Rules | Key points
  [103:4:0.0] MSW (M&H) Rules 2000 | Primary goal: promoting segregation (APPSC-G2 2025); ULBs collect, segregate, store, transport, process, dispose
  [103:4:0.1] SWM Rules 2016 (from 8 Apr 2016) | Beyond municipal areas; 3 streams – biodegradable, dry, domestic hazardous; EPR for producers/brand owners; user fees, spot fines; no untreated waste to landfill; criteria for landfill and waste-processing sites
  [103:4:0.2] SWM Rules 2026 (under EPA 1986; in force 1 Apr 2026) | 4 streams: wet, dry, sanitary, special care; landfills only for inert residue; higher landfill fee for unsegregated waste; legacy dumps mapped with time-bound biomining and quarterly reports
[103:4:1] - 2016: used diapers/napkins wrapped into the **dry** bin; 2026: separate **sanitary** stream. **Special care = domestic hazardous** (batteries, chemicals, paint cans, bulbs). Wet = food scraps, peels, garden waste; paper, plastics, metals, glass → dry.
[103:4:2] - **Bulk Waste Generators**: treat wet waste on site or get an **EBWGR** certificate.
[103:4:3] - **Environmental Compensation** follows the **polluter-pays** principle (not precautionary).
[103:4:4] - Draft "SWM Rules 2024" proposed 1 Oct 2025 – not the notified rules.
[103:4:5] - **Collection chain**: door-to-door → segregated transport → transfer station → **Material Recovery Facility** → processing/recycling → residual landfill.
[103:4:6] - **Supreme Court**: clean environment part of **Art. 21** (Feb 2026); **18 Aug 2026** (Justice S.V.N. Bhatti) – **generators**, not only sanitary workers, answerable for waste at the doorstep.

[103:5:10] - **PIBOs** = Producers, Importers and Brand Owners (packaging outside the SUP ban).

## SUB 6 | E-waste
[103:6:0] # Rules
[103:6:1] - **E-waste (Management) Rules 2022**: notified **Nov 2022**, in force **1 April 2023**; lineage 2011 → 2016 → 2022; **106 EEE items incl. solar PV** waste.
[103:6:2] - Only **registered** producers, manufacturers, refurbishers, recyclers may deal; e-waste not to be mixed with other waste.
[103:6:3] - EPR: annual targets on past sales; register on **CPCB portal**; buy **digital EPR certificates** from registered recyclers.
[103:6:4] - **Battery Waste Management Rules 2022**: replaced 2001 rules; EV, portable, automotive, industrial batteries under EPR.
[103:6:5] # Magnitude
[103:6:6] - India = **3rd-largest** e-waste generator (after **China, USA**); **~2.2 million tonnes (2025)**.
[103:6:7] - **3.54 lakh tonnes** = collected and processed in **FY 2021** (MeitY; APPSC-GS 2021).
[103:6:8] - **Global E-waste Monitor**: **ITU and UNITAR**. GEM 2024: **62 million tonnes** in 2022, only **22.3%** formally recycled; **82 Mt by 2030**.
[103:6:9] # Recycling Chain and Hazards
[103:6:10] - Chain: consumer → collection centre / producer take-back → **authorised dismantler** → **authorised recycler** → recovered materials. Citizens: give devices to authorised recycler after **wiping data** (APPSC-GS 2025).
[103:6:11] - **78%** processed informally. Informal hubs: **Seelampur (Delhi), Moradabad (UP)**.
[103:6:12] - **R2 (Responsible Recycling) Code**: environmentally responsible practices in electronics recycling; promotes reuse (UPSC 2021) – not wastewater.
[103:6:13] - Old computers release **beryllium, cadmium, chromium, mercury, lead**, PCBs, PBBs – NOT **heptachlor** (insecticide) or **plutonium** (UPSC 2013).
[103:6:14] TABLE head: Constituent | Harm
  [103:6:14.0] Mercury | Brain, kidney; bioaccumulates in fish
  [103:6:14.1] Cadmium | Kidney and bone
  [103:6:14.2] PBBs (brominated flame retardants) | Persistent; endocrine effects
[103:6:15] - Improper disposal releases toxics into soil and water → harms ecosystems and biodiversity (APPSC-GS 2025). "Invisible e-waste": vapes, USB cables, LED accessories.
