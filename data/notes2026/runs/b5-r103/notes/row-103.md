=== Biodegradable vs Non-biodegradable; Kinds of Waste
# Definitions
- **Solid waste:** discarded or abandoned material in **solid, liquid, semi-solid or containerised gaseous** form; includes urban, agricultural, biomedical and radioactive wastes.
- **Liquid waste (sewage):** fluids from washing, flushing or manufacturing. **Sullage** = waste water from **kitchens** and bathrooms (grey water), not from toilets (black water/sewage), factories or hospitals.
- **E-waste (ITU):** discarded electrical and electronic equipment and parts **not meant for reuse**. **Biomedical waste:** waste from **diagnosis, treatment or immunisation of humans or animals** (blood, sharps, cultures, discarded medicines).
- **Garbage** = putrescible kitchen/market waste; **rubbish** = household/commercial waste other than garbage and ashes.
# Biodegradable vs non-biodegradable
- **Biodegradable:** decomposed by micro-organisms (mainly **bacteria**) into simpler harmless substances. **Aerobic** decomposition → **compost**; **anaerobic** → **methane (biogas)**.
- **Non-biodegradable:** mainly inorganic; cannot be broken down by microbes, so it accumulates; **recycling** is the ideal option for it.
| Biodegradable | Non-biodegradable |
|---|---|
| Sewage, faecal matter, urine, domestic garbage, food/kitchen waste, **vegetable and fruit peels**, cow dung, paddy husk | **Plastic**, polythene bags, plastic cups, **DDT, insecticides/pesticides**, asbestos |
| **Paper, wood, grass, leaves**, plant waste, cellulose, starch, proteins | **Tin**, aluminium foil, metal scrap, mercury, lead |
| **Wool** (natural keratin fibre), cotton, **leather** | **Glass bottles**, China clay cups, **polystyrene** cups |
| **Natural rubber** | Synthetic rubber/tyres, nylon, Teflon, **PVC** |
| Polymers: **polylactic acid (PLA)**, PHBV, polyglycolic acid, Nylon-2-Nylon-6 | Polyethylene, polystyrene, polycarbonate |
- **Polythene** is a **polymer of ethylene (C2H4)** – carbon and hydrogen only; polythene bags cannot be destroyed by bacteria because they are synthetic **polymers**.
- Biodegradable polymers are eco-friendly because microbes degrade them; non-biodegradable plastics persist and break into **microplastics**.
- **Biodegradable plastics** (e.g. PLA) are a better alternative, but some need much energy and resources to make and can still pollute on disposal – both statements true (TSPSC 2023).
- **Industrial wastes:** **thermal power plants** give fly ash (non-biodegradable) – they produce the most non-biodegradable waste; **gypsum** comes from **fertiliser** plants, not food processing. Food processing, paper, cotton, textile and sugar mills give mostly non-hazardous, biodegradable waste.
| Litter | Time to degrade |
|---|---|
| Paper | 10–30 days |
| Woollen items | about 1 year |
| Tin, aluminium and other metal items | 100–500 years |
| Plastic bags | up to 1 million years |
# Categories
| Category | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Municipal (MSW) | Household + commercial: organics, plastics, paper, metals, street and market waste |
| Agricultural | Crop residues, **paddy husk, cattle dung** (biodegradable; dung → biogas under GOBARdhan) |
| Construction & demolition | Mostly **inert** – concrete, bricks, dirt, rocks, debris |
| Hazardous | Paints, batteries, pesticides, chemicals, fluorescent lamps, aerosol cans |
| Biomedical / E-waste / Radioactive | Separate rules (see later sub-sections) |
- **Hazardous waste – four classic criteria:** **ignitability, corrosivity, reactivity, toxicity** (NOT biodegradability). **Household hazardous waste:** **paints and batteries**, bulbs, aerosol cans, tyres.
- **Healthcare waste (WHO):** **~85% general non-hazardous, ~15% hazardous** (infectious, chemical, radioactive).
- **Land pollution:** its significant cause is the **disposal of solid waste** (not rainfall or planting trees).
> Exam angle: "which is NOT biodegradable" – tin, plastic, PVC, insecticides, glass; "which IS" – wool, peels, leather, natural rubber, PLA, sewage.

=== Disposal and Treatment Methods
| Method | Process | Suited to |
|---|---|---|
| **Open dumping** | Untreated, uncovered, unsegregated waste on land | Not viable – flies, rats, leachate |
| **Landfill** | Most **common and economic** method in India; most urban household waste ends here | Residual waste; last resort |
| **Sanitary landfill** | Waste **compacted** in cells and **covered with earth every day**; **liners** (clay/plastic) + leachate collection protect groundwater; adopted as a **substitute for open-burning dumps** | Only residual inert waste; costly |
| **Composting (aerobic)** – **Indore method** | Microbes (bacteria, **fungi**) oxidise organics; material **turned at intervals**; 2–4 weeks | Wet/organic waste |
| **Bangalore method (anaerobic)** | Pits with alternate layers of MSW and night soil; **4–6 months** | Organic waste |
| **Vermicomposting** | **Earthworms** break waste down (vermiculture) | Household organic waste |
| **Incineration** | Controlled **burning** at high heat in oxygen; cuts volume ~**90%**; steam for power; emits pollutants, toxic ash | Hazardous, infectious/biomedical |
| **Pyrolysis** | Thermo-chemical decomposition (heating) **without oxygen** → gas, oil, char (carbon-based fuels) | Mixed waste; costly |
| **Plasma gasification** | **Extreme thermal** process (electricity + very high temperature), no combustion → **syngas** (H2 + CO); inorganics → slag | All organic wastes |
| **RDF** | Shredded, dehydrated high-calorific dry MSW | Fuel in **cement kilns** and thermal plants |
- **Pyrolysis and plasma gasification = waste-to-energy technologies** (UPSC 2019); compost and vermicompost give manure, not energy.
- **Compost:** biodegradable waste decomposed in pits; rich in organic matter and nutrients; earthworms and microbes **speed up** (not slow down) decomposition. In composting both aerobes and anaerobes degrade organic matter. **Vermiculture** = the science of **raising earthworms**.
- **Biomining** uses bacteria, **fungi** or plants to extract metals from ores and solid wastes; composting also uses fungi.
- Urban India uses **composting, incineration and landfilling – "all the above"**.
- **Better method to reduce the solid-waste problem: recycling** (not burning, sea dumping or compressing). **Composting household waste** cuts methane and helps reduce global warming; **burning household waste** does not reduce GHG emissions.
| Waste | Disposal method |
|---|---|
| Organic | Composting |
| E-waste | Recycling |
| Hazardous / biomedical | Incineration (biomedical at a common treatment facility) |
| Broken glass pieces | Landfilling |
| Non-recyclable high-calorific dry waste | RDF / co-processing in cement kilns |
| Method | Pro / con |
|---|---|
| Landfilling | **Low cost, but long-term environmental impact** |
| Incineration | **Reduces volume, but emits pollutants** |
| Composting | **Useful by-products (manure, biogas), but needs land** |
| Recycling | **Conserves resources, but needs infrastructure** |
> Exam angle: match method → function (incineration = burning; pyrolysis = waste to fuel; plasma = extreme heat; composting = biological decay); "most common method in India" = landfilling.

=== Plastic Waste – Rules, Ban and Microplastics
# Rules timeline and thickness
| Year | Instrument | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| 2011 | Plastic Waste (M&H) Rules | Carry bags **40 microns** |
| 2016 | PWM Rules 2016 | **50 microns**; extended to villages; PWM fee; EPR; plastic roads, waste-to-oil, waste-to-energy |
| 12 Aug 2021 | PWM Amendment Rules 2021 | **75 microns** from 30 Sep 2021; **120 microns** from 31 Dec 2022; phase out identified SUP **by 2022** |
| 16 Feb 2022 | EPR Guidelines on plastic packaging (Schedule II) | In force on notification; widely taught as "from **1 July 2022**" with the SUP ban |
| 1 Jul 2022 | **SUP ban** | 19 identified items banned |
| 2024 | PWM Amendment Rules 2024 | Defined biodegradable plastics; makers need **CPCB** certification |
| 2025-2026 | PWM Amendment Rules 2025 / 2026 | QR/barcode EPR tracking; rigid recycled content **30% → 60%** by 2028-29; shortfalls carried forward up to **3 years** |
- Compostable plastics must meet **IS/ISO 17088**.
# Single-use plastic (SUP) ban – 1 July 2022
- India's measure against plastic pollution: **banning identified single-use plastics** (not all plastic, not all food containers).
- **19 items**: ear buds with plastic sticks, balloon sticks, plastic flags, candy and ice-cream sticks, **thermocol for decoration**, plates, cups, cutlery, straws, cigarette-packet films, PVC banners < 100 microns. NOT banned: **HDPE milk bottles**. The ban covers only **~11%** of plastic waste (CSE).
- Enforcement: **SPCBs/PCCs and local bodies**, with a National Level Task Force and State Special Task Forces.
- **NGT (2019)** fined **25 States/UTs ₹1 crore** each for missing the deadline for plastic waste management action plans.
# EPR for plastic packaging
- **EPR:** the producer's duty for environmentally sound management of its product **till end of life** – collection and recycling after use.
- **PIBOs** = Producers, Importers and Brand Owners; recyclers generate tradable EPR certificates on the **CPCB portal**.
| Category | Example | Recycled content 2025-26 → 2028-29 |
|---|---|---|
| I – Rigid | PET, HDPE bottles | 30% → 60% |
| II – Flexible | Carry bags, chips packets | 10% → 20% |
| III – Multi-layered | Multi-layer packs | 5% → 10% |

- **Bisphenol A (BPA):** key building block of **polycarbonate** (and epoxy resins); leaches from bottles and food containers; endocrine disruptor.

FSSAI found microplastics in all tested sugar and salt.
- **Plastic pollution** = accumulation of non-biodegradable waste in the oceans that threatens marine life.
- **Plastic-eating fungus** *Aspergillus tubingensis*: found at a waste dump in **Islamabad, Pakistan** (2017).
- **IIT Kharagpur** made eco-friendly food packaging from **cucumber peels**. **India's largest charkha** made of used plastic waste was inaugurated in **Noida** (Gandhi 150).

=== Solid Waste Management Rules and Indian Data
| Rules | Key points |
|---|---|
| **MSW (Management and Handling) Rules 2000** | ULBs collect, **segregate**, store, transport, process, dispose; primary goal **promoting segregation** |
| **SWM Rules 2016** (8 Apr 2016) | **Replaced the MSW Rules 2000**; apply **beyond municipal areas** (urban agglomerations, census towns, industrial townships, railways, airports, defence, pilgrimage places); **3 streams** – wet (biodegradable), dry, domestic hazardous; **brand owners with non-biodegradable packaging must set up a collect-back system** (producer responsibility for packaging life cycle); user fees and spot fines; **exact criteria for siting landfills and waste-processing facilities** (landfill 100 m from river, 200 m from pond, 500 m from highways/habitations, 20 km from airports); industries within 100 km of an RDF plant replace 5% fuel with RDF |
| **SWM Rules 2026** (under EPA 1986; in force **1 Apr 2026**; replaced 2016) | **4 streams: wet, dry, sanitary, special care** (green, blue, red, black bins); landfills only for non-recyclable inert residue; higher landfill fee for unsegregated waste; legacy dumps mapped with time-bound biomining; **Environmental Compensation = polluter pays** |
- SWM 2016 did NOT require five categories, did NOT bar moving waste between districts, and was NOT limited to ULBs and industrial townships.
- 2016: used diapers/napkins wrapped and put in the **dry** bin; 2026: separate **sanitary** stream. **Special care** = domestic hazardous (batteries, bulbs, medicines, paint cans). Paper, plastics, metals, glass → **dry**.
- **Bulk Waste Generators** (2026): process wet waste on site or hold an **EBWGR** certificate.
- In 2016 MoEFCC notified **six sets of waste rules** (solid, plastic, e-waste, biomedical, hazardous, C&D); **CPCB** (with SPCBs) is the nodal body for standards and monitoring of waste treatment.
- **Supreme Court:** clean environment part of **Art. 21** (Feb 2026); **18 Aug 2026** – **generators**, not only sanitary workers, answerable for waste at the doorstep. Draft "SWM Rules 2024" (proposed 1 Oct 2025) were not the notified rules.
- **Collection chain:** door-to-door → segregated transport → **transfer station → Material Recovery Facility (MRF) → processing/recycling** → residual landfill.

=== E-waste
# Rules
- **EPR in Indian law was first introduced by the e-Waste (Management and Handling) Rules 2011** (UPSC 2019; not the BMW Rules 1998, Recycled Plastic Rules 1999 or Hazardous Wastes Rules 2016). Rocket Environment p.45 credits the Plastic Waste (M&H) Rules 2011 – a source slip; UPSC's answer is the e-waste rules.
- **E-Waste (Management) Rules 2016:** 21 products; PROs, deposit-refund scheme, e-waste exchange.
- **E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022:** notified by **MoEFCC in November 2022**; in force **1 April 2023** (not Nov 2022); **106 EEE items incl. solar PV** waste; do NOT apply to packaging plastics or radioactive waste (separate rules).
- Collection of e-waste is the **producer's** responsibility (EPR); only **registered** producers, manufacturers, refurbishers and recyclers may deal; producers register on the **CPCB portal** and buy **digital EPR certificates** from registered recyclers; annual targets on past sales.
- **Battery Waste Management Rules 2022** replaced the 2001 rules.
# Magnitude
- India = **3rd-largest** e-waste generator (after **China, USA**); about **2.2 million tonnes (2025)**. **Mumbai** = largest e-waste-producing city (ASSOCHAM); Maharashtra the top State.
- **3.54 lakh tonnes** collected and processed in **FY 2020-21** (of 13.46 lakh t generated); 11.59 lakh t recycled in FY 2024-25.
- **Global E-waste Monitor** (**ITU and UNITAR**) 2024: **62 Mt** in 2022, only **22.3%** formally recycled; 82 Mt by 2030.
# Recycling chain and hazards
- Chain: consumer → collection centre / producer take-back → **authorised dismantler → authorised recycler** → recovered materials. Citizens should **sell or give old electronics to authorised recyclers** (not burn, bin or dump them).
- **78%** processed informally; informal hubs **Seelampur (Delhi), Moradabad (UP)**. "Invisible e-waste": vapes, USB cables, LED accessories.
- **R2 (Responsible Recycling) Code of Practices:** environmentally responsible practices in the **electronics recycling industry** (UPSC 2021).
- Old computers release **beryllium, cadmium, chromium, mercury, lead**, PCBs, PBBs – NOT **heptachlor** (an insecticide) or **plutonium** (radioactive) (UPSC 2013).
| Constituent | Harm |
|---|---|
| Mercury | Brain, kidney; bioaccumulates in fish |
| Cadmium | Kidney and bone |
| Lead | Nervous system and kidney |
| PBBs / BFRs | Persistent; endocrine effects |
> Exam angle: first EPR = e-waste rules 2011; 2022 rules dates; 3.54 lakh t (FY 2021); heptachlor/plutonium are not e-waste; R2 = electronics recycling.

=== 3R, Circular Economy and Industrial By-products
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **3R** | **Reduce, Reuse, Recycle** ("Regain" is NOT one of the 3Rs) |
| Top priority | **Reduction at source** – practising reduce, reuse, recycle cuts waste at source (not disposables, open dumping or street burning) |
| Reuse | Using the item again in the same form |
| **Recycling** | **Converting waste materials into other usable materials** |
| 5R | Adds Refuse and Repurpose (or Recover) |
| Least preferred | Landfilling |
| RRR Centre | Permanent in every city under SBM-U 2.0 |
- **Circular economy:** minimises waste by **reusing and recycling materials** (not using products till they break, landfilling or banning manufacture). **Unplanned waste generation** is NOT sustainable development.
- **Zero waste management:** **segregation at source, separate collection of each kind, community involvement** – all of the above. **Indore** is the model zero-waste city.
- **Legacy waste:** old MSW in dumpsites; **biomining** sorts it into recyclables, RDF, bio-soil and inert fill.
| Waste | Source industry | Reuse |
|---|---|---|
| **Fly ash** | Coal thermal plants | **Bricks**, roads, **replaces part of Portland cement** (up to ~35%); contains toxic elements (arsenic, lead, mercury) – not only SiO2 and CaO; use mandatory within **300 km** |
| **Steel slag** | Steel plants | Road base, agricultural soil, cement (all three – UPSC 2020); Surat steel-slag road |
| Red mud | Alumina refining | Bricks, tiles |
| Phosphogypsum | Phosphoric acid / phosphatic fertiliser plants | Gypsum substitute, soil conditioner |
| Chromium sludge | Tanneries | – (hazardous) |
- **Green rural roads (UPSC 2020):** **copper slag, cold-mix asphalt, geotextiles** are preferred; hot-mix asphalt and Portland cement are not.

=== Biomedical, Hazardous and C&D Waste; Conventions
# Biomedical waste (BMW)
- **BMW Management Rules 2016** replaced the **1998 Rules (10 categories)**; amended 2018, 2019; **4 colour categories**; **barcoding** and GPS tracking; no on-site facility if a common facility is within 75 km.
| Colour | Waste | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow | Anatomical, soiled, expired medicines, chemical | Incineration / plasma pyrolysis / deep burial |
| Red | Contaminated recyclable plastics (tubes, IV sets, catheters, syringes without needles) | Autoclave/microwave + shredding |
| White (translucent) | Sharps – needles, blades | Autoclave, shredding, encapsulation |
| Blue | Glassware, metallic implants | Disinfection, recycling |
- BMW must be treated before disposal **to prevent the spread of infection and protect human health**. **COVID-19:** CPCB guidelines (2020) + **COVID19BWM** app.
- **BMW generated (MoEF 2016): 0.17 million tonnes a year** (about 484 t/day).
# Hazardous waste
| Law | Provision |
|---|---|
| EPA 1986 | Passed after **Bhopal (1984)**; umbrella for hazardous-waste rules |
| MSIHC Rules 1989 | Manufacture, storage, import of hazardous chemicals |
| CAEPPR Rules 1996 | Chemical-accident crisis groups (central, state, district, local) |
