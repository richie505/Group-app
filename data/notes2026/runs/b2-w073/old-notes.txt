=== NOTES 2:73 | National Commission for SCs (338), STs (338A) and Backward Classes (338B); Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities (350B)
## SUB 0 | Articles and Amendments – 338, 338A, 338B, 350B
[73:0:0] TABLE head: Body | Article | Created / changed by
  [73:0:0.0] Special Officer (Commissioner) for SCs and STs | 338 (original) | Constitution, 1950
  [73:0:0.1] Combined National Commission for SCs and STs | 338 | 65th Amendment, 1990 (in force 12 Mar 1992)
  [73:0:0.2] NCSC (separate) | 338 | 89th Amendment, 2003 (bifurcation); from 2004
  [73:0:0.3] NCST | 338A | 89th Amendment, 2003; came into being 19 Feb 2004
  [73:0:0.4] NCBC | 338B | 102nd Amendment, 2018 (statutory from 1993)
  [73:0:0.5] Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities | 350B | 7th Amendment, 1956
[73:0:1] - **Constitutional bodies** "in the sense that they are directly established by the Constitution": NCSC (338), NCST (338A), NCBC (338B, since 2018), Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities (350B). **NCW, NCM, NHRC, NCPCR are statutory.**
[73:0:2]   - So NCST was created by a **constitutional amendment** – not a government order, Cabinet resolution or statute.
[73:0:3] - **Distractors:** Art. **340** = Backward Classes Commission (Kalelkar, Mandal); **341/342** = lists of SCs/STs; **280** = Finance Commission; **332** = Assembly seats for SCs/STs; **350** = representation for grievances; **343/347** = official language.
[73:0:4] - Evolution: 1978 non-statutory Commission for SCs and STs (resolution) → **1987** resolution renamed it the National Commission → **65th Amendment** replaced the single Special Officer with a multi-member constitutional body → **1999** Ministry of Tribal Affairs → **89th** split it.
[73:0:5] - **102nd Amendment** also inserted **Art. 342A** (President notifies SEBCs) and relieved the **NCSC of backward-class work**; the **105th Amendment (2021)** restored States' power to keep their own SEBC lists.
[73:0:6] - **⚠ Dated key:** before 2018 the NCBC was statutory, so old papers answer "NCBC is not a constitutional body"; today all four (NCSC, NCST, NCBC, Special Officer) are constitutional.
[73:0:7] > Asked as: Article for NCSC (338 – 6 PYQs), NCST (338A – 4), Special Officer (350B); amendment that created NCST / split the commission (89th – 3); NCBC status (102nd – APPSC FRO 2022); odd one out "not a constitutional body".

## SUB 1 | Composition, Functions, Powers and Reports
[73:1:0] # Composition
[73:1:1] - NCSC, NCST and the constitutional NCBC each: **Chairperson + Vice-Chairperson + 3 other members**.
[73:1:2] - Appointed by the **President by warrant under his hand and seal**; tenure fixed by the President's Rules (2004) = **3 years**; not more than **two terms**.
[73:1:3] - The statutory NCBC (1993 Act, in force 2 Apr 1993) had **5 members** headed by a **retired judge of the Supreme Court or a High Court**.
[73:1:4] # Functions (Art. 338(5), 338A(5), 338B(5))
[73:1:5] - 1. **Investigate and monitor** the constitutional and legal safeguards and evaluate their working.
[73:1:6] - 2. **Inquire into specific complaints** of deprivation of rights and safeguards.
[73:1:7] - 3. **Participate and advise on the planning process** of socio-economic development and evaluate progress.
[73:1:8] - 4. **Present reports to the President** annually (and when it deems fit) – **not to the Prime Minister**; the President lays them before **Parliament** with an action-taken memorandum; State-related reports go to the **Governor** (NCBC: to the State government).
[73:1:9] - 5. Recommend measures for effective implementation of safeguards.
[73:1:10] - **Not** functions: giving scholarships, raising a protection force, implementing Union welfare schemes, punishing offenders.
[73:1:11] - **Consultation:** the Union and every State must consult the Commission on all major policy matters (Art. 338(9), 338A(9)); proposals for **de-reservation** of posts are examined by the NCSC/NCST/NCBC first (NCSC review of three years' proposals, Aug 2026).
[73:1:12] - **NCST extra (2005 Rules):** ownership of **minor forest produce**, rights over mineral and water resources, rehabilitation of displaced tribals, PESA implementation.
[73:1:13] # Powers – Art. 338(8)
[73:1:14] - While investigating or inquiring it has **all the powers of a civil court trying a suit**: **summon** and examine on oath; **discovery of documents**; **receive evidence on affidavits**; **requisition any public record** from any court or office; **issue summons** for examining witnesses and documents. It **cannot punish**; its advice is recommendatory.
[73:1:15] > Asked as: NCST tenure (3 years) and members besides Chairperson and Vice (3); a function of NCSC (advise on planning); NOT a function (report to the PM – APPSC Group-I); civil-court powers "all of the above" (APPSC Group-I 2023).

## SUB 3 | Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities – Art. 350B
[73:3:0] - **Art. 350B** inserted by the **7th Amendment, 1956** (with **Art. 350A**) on the **States Reorganisation Commission's** recommendation – not the 9th Amendment.
[73:3:1] - **One** Special Officer for the whole country, **appointed by the President** – there is **no** post "for every State".
[73:3:2] - Duty: **investigate all matters relating to the safeguards** for linguistic minorities and **report to the President**, who lays the reports before **each House of Parliament** and sends them to the States.
[73:3:3] - Office created **1957**; designated **Commissioner for Linguistic Minorities (CLM)**; under the **Ministry of Minority Affairs**; headquarters **New Delhi** (moved back from Allahabad, 2015); regional offices **Belgaum, Chennai, Kolkata**.
[73:3:4] - The Constitution does **not** fix his qualifications, tenure, salary or removal.
[73:3:5] - **Art. 350A**: States to provide **instruction in the mother tongue at the primary stage** to linguistic-minority children.
[73:3:6] > Asked as: Article for the Special Officer (350B – APPSC FRO 2022); statements "one officer for every State" and "added by the 9th Amendment" both false (UPPSC 2024).

## SUB 4 | Backward Classes Commissions, Other Bodies and the AP Angle
[73:4:0] # Backward Classes Commissions (Art. 340)
[73:4:1] TABLE head: Commission | Chairman | Year | Government
  [73:4:1.0] First Backward Classes Commission | Kaka Kalelkar | 29 Jan 1953 | Nehru
  [73:4:1.1] Second Backward Classes Commission | B.P. Mandal | 1 Jan 1979 | Janata (Morarji Desai)
[73:4:2] - **Mandal** first recommended **27%** OBC reservation; **Indra Sawhney v UoI (16 Nov 1992)** upheld 27%, the **creamy layer**, and ordered a permanent body → statutory **NCBC (1993 Act)**.
[73:4:3] - First reservation policy: **Kolhapur (Shahu Maharaj), 1902**; Art. 15(4) added by the **1st Amendment, 1951**.
[73:4:4] # Status of other bodies
[73:4:5] - **NCW, NCM, NHRC** = statutory; **NITI Aayog** = executive (**Cabinet resolution**, 1 Jan 2015); **NCSK** = statutory under a **1993 Act** (lapsed 29 Feb 2004), now **non-statutory**, extended to **31 March 2028**.
[73:4:6] # AP angle
[73:4:7] - **AP State Commission for SCs and STs** created by a **2003** State Act; split later – **AP State Commission for Scheduled Tribes Act, 2020** (separate SC and ST commissions).

## SUB 0 | National Commission for Women (NCW)
[74:0:0] # Act and status
[74:0:1] - **Statutory**, not constitutional: **National Commission for Women Act, 1990** (in force 30 Aug 1990); Commission constituted on **31 January 1992**. Nodal ministry: **Women and Child Development**.
[74:0:2] - Recommended earlier by the **Committee on the Status of Women in India (1974)** and the National Perspective Plan for Women (1988).
[74:0:3] # Composition (Sec. 3)
[74:0:4] - **Chairperson + 5 members + a Member-Secretary**, all **nominated by the Central Government** (not the President, not elected).
[74:0:5] - Members: persons with experience in **law or legislation, trade unionism, management of an industry**, **women's voluntary organisations (incl. women activists)**, **administration, economic development, health, education or social welfare**; at least **one SC and one ST** member.
[74:0:6] - Member-Secretary: an expert in management, organisational structure or **sociological movement**, or a civil servant.
[74:0:7] - Term: **3 years** (chairperson and members).
[74:0:8] # Functions (Sec. 10) and powers
[74:0:9] - **Section 10** lists the functions: investigate safeguards for women; report to the **Central Government**; review laws and suggest amendments; **look into complaints and take suo motu notice** of deprivation of women's rights; special studies; **promotional and educational research** to ensure **due representation of women** in all spheres; inspect jails and remand homes; fund litigation.
[74:0:10] - **Powers of a civil court** while investigating; regulates its own procedure; forms committees and co-opts outsiders. It cannot impose or enforce criminal sentences.
[74:0:11] - **Mahila Jan Sunwai** = public hearing of women's grievances; **Parivarik Mahila Lok Adalat** for family disputes.

## SUB 2 | Ombudsman Idea and Recommending Bodies
[75:2:0] # Origin
[75:2:1] - **Ombudsman** first created in **Sweden in 1809** (Scandinavia); spread to Finland 1919, Denmark 1955, Norway 1962; **New Zealand 1962** (first Commonwealth); UK Parliamentary Commissioner 1967.
[75:2:2] - **Indian versions: Lokpal** (Union) and **Lokayukta** (State) – not Lekhpal, Tehsildar or Governor.
[75:2:3] - Other models: French **Administrative Courts**; **Procurator** system in the USSR/China.
[75:2:4] - Term **"Lokpal"** coined by **Dr. L.M. Singhvi** in **1963**.
[75:2:5] - Sweden 1809 = Ombudsman; Sweden **1766** = first right-to-information law.
[75:2:6] # Committees and commissions
[75:2:7] TABLE head: Body | Chair | Recommendation

  [75:2:7.2] Second ARC (2007) | – | Reiterated the Lokpal
[75:2:8] - ARC: Lokpal to be appointed by the President after consulting the CJI, Speaker and RS Chairman; judiciary kept outside (as in New Zealand).
[75:2:9] - **⚠ Trap:** the Rajasthan State Administrative Reforms Committee and the Second ARC are distractors for "first recommended Lokayuktas".

## SUB 3 | Andhra Pradesh Lokayukta
[75:3:0] - Law: **AP Lokayukta and Upa-Lokayukta Act, 1983** (amended 2007, **2024**); **Lokayukta** for higher functionaries, **Upa-Lokayukta** for the rest.
[75:3:1] - **FIRST Lokayukta of integrated AP: Justice A. Sambasiva Rao**.
[75:3:2] - **Chief Minister is outside its purview:** the Act defines "Minister" as a member **other than the Chief Minister** of the Council of Ministers; it covers **Ministers (other than the CM), MLAs** and State public servants.
[75:3:3] - **Time bar (Section 8(2))**: no complaint can be investigated after **6 years** from the date of the action complained against (Lokpal: 7 years).
[75:3:4] - **Vacancy:** when the Lokayukta's office is vacant, the **Upa-Lokayukta** performs its functions as the **Governor** directs (P. Rajani held charge in 2025).
[75:3:5] - Appointment by the **Governor** on the selection committee's recommendation; **5-year term**.
[75:3:6] - **Aug 2026**: Justice (Retd) **T. Mallikarjuna Rao** appointed by Governor **S. Abdul Nazeer** under **Section 3(1)**; **5-year term**.
[75:3:7] - **Seat**: worked from Hyderabad after bifurcation; shifted to **Kurnool** in **2021** (principal seat).
[75:3:8] - Features: **informal procedure, easy access, almost free and prompt service, independence from executive control** – it is **not an interpreter of the Constitution** (that is the judiciary).

## SUB 5 | Lokpal – Composition, Selection, Jurisdiction and People
[75:5:0] # Composition
[75:5:1] - **Chairperson + not more than 8 members**; **50% judicial**; at least **50%** from **SC, ST, OBC, minorities and women**; minimum age **45**.
[75:5:2] - Chairperson: former **CJI**, SC judge, or an eminent person with **25 years'** experience.
[75:5:3] - Term: **5 years or age 70**; **no reappointment**; cannot be an **MP or MLA**; appointed by the **President**.
[75:5:4] # Selection Committee (5)
[75:5:5] TABLE head: Member | Role
  [75:5:5.0] Prime Minister | Chair
  [75:5:5.1] Speaker of the Lok Sabha | Member
  [75:5:5.2] Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha | Member
  [75:5:5.3] CJI or an SC judge nominated by him | Member
  [75:5:5.4] Eminent jurist (nominated by the President on the others' recommendation) | Member
[75:5:6] - **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is NOT a member**; **Search Committee** of **at least 7 persons** (half from SC/ST/OBC/minorities/women).
[75:5:7] # Jurisdiction, powers, time limits
[75:5:8] - Covers the **PM** (except international relations, security, public order, atomic energy, space; inquiry only if **two-thirds** of the full bench approves, in camera), **Ministers, MPs** (not for anything said or votes in Parliament), **Group A–D** central officials (**C and D → CVC**, which reports back), NGOs with foreign contributions **above ₹10 lakh**, public servants **posted abroad**.
[75:5:9] - **Inquiry Wing (Section 11**; preliminary inquiry into **Prevention of Corruption Act** offences) and Prosecution Wing; **superintendence over the CBI only in cases referred by the Lokpal**; attach property **even while prosecution is pending**.

=== NOTES 2:76 | Central Information Commission and State Information Commissions; Cbi and Its Legal Basis
## SUB 0 | CBI – Legal Basis, Director and Units
[76:0:0] # Legal basis
[76:0:1] - **Special Police Establishment (1941)** → **Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) Act, 1946** (it constitutes the special police force = today's **CBI**) → **CBI set up 1 April 1963** by a **Ministry of Home Affairs resolution** → **CVC (1964)**.
[76:0:2] - Set up on the recommendation of the **Santhanam Committee on Prevention of Corruption (1962-64)**.
[76:0:3] - **Neither constitutional nor statutory**: its powers come from the DSPE Act, 1946; attached office under the **Ministry of Personnel (DoPT)**.
[76:0:4] - **Founder Director: D.P. Kohli** (1963-68). Motto: **Industry, Impartiality, Integrity**; mission – to preserve values in public life and ensure the health of the national economy.
[76:0:5] - CBI = "**caged parrot**" (SC, coal allocation case 2013); **Second ARC (2007)**: new law to replace the DSPE Act.
[76:0:6] # Director
[76:0:7] - Appointed by the **Central Government** on the recommendation of a **3-member committee**: **PM, LoP in Lok Sabha (or leader of the largest opposition party), CJI or an SC judge nominated by him** – brought by the **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013**.
[76:0:8] - Minimum tenure **2 years**, extendable **one year at a time up to 5 years** (**2021**; same for the **ED Director**).
[76:0:9] - **Director of Prosecution**: appointed on **CVC recommendation**, **2-year** term; officers of SP rank and above chosen by a **CVC-led** committee.
[76:0:10] TABLE head: Director | Period
  [76:0:10.0] D.P. Kohli | First (1963-68)
  [76:0:10.1] Ranjit Sinha | 2012-14
  [76:0:10.2] Alok Verma | Feb 2017 – Jan 2019 (exam key of 2017-18)
  [76:0:10.3] Praveen Sood | From 25 May 2023; extended to May 2025, May 2026 and again to May 2027 (as of 2026)
[76:0:11] # Vineet Narain (1997) and State consent
[76:0:12] - **Vineet Narain v Union of India (1997)**: quashed the "**Single Directive**"; gave the **CVC superintendence** over the CBI; **2-year tenure** for the Director; **CBI alone decides** whether to investigate.
[76:0:13] - **Section 6, DSPE Act**: CBI needs the State's **consent**, as **police** is **State List Entry 2**; **AP withdrew general consent Nov 2018**, restored June 2019.
[76:0:14] - **Lokpal**: superintendence over the CBI only in cases it refers.
[76:0:15] # Mandate, units and platforms
[76:0:16] - **Corruption, economic and special crimes**, mainly **Central Government and UT employees** and their PSUs; there is **no constitutional bar** on its probing economic offences.

=== NOTES 2:77 | Niti Aayog – Structure, Functions, Initiatives; Contrast with the Planning Commission
## SUB 0 | Planning Commission and the National Development Council
[77:0:0] # Planning Commission
[77:0:1] - Set up **15 March 1950** by a **resolution of the Union Cabinet / Government of India** – **non-constitutional, non-statutory** (not by an amendment or ordinance); the Constitution provides for the Finance Commission (Art. 280), Election Commission (Art. 324) and UPSC (Art. 315), **not** the Planning Commission.
[77:0:2] - **First Chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru** (PM ex officio); first Deputy Chairman **Gulzarilal Nanda**; the Deputy Chairman had **Cabinet Minister** rank.
[77:0:3] - Early Five-Year Plans (from 1951, Mahalanobis model in the 2nd) gave the public sector the commanding heights and kept the **market economy at bay**.
[77:0:4] - Cabinet repealed the 1950 resolution on **13 August 2014** (Modi govt); **no longer in existence** – replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015.
[77:0:5] # National Development Council
[77:0:6] - Set up **6 August 1952** by an **executive order** – **non-constitutional and non-statutory** (calling it statutory is wrong).
[77:0:7] - Members: **PM (chairman)**, **Union Cabinet Ministers**, **Chief Ministers of all States**, CMs/administrators of UTs, members of the Planning Commission – **not** the Finance Commission Chairman or the CJI.
[77:0:8] - **Secretary: the Secretary of the Planning Commission**; meant to meet at least **twice a year**.
[77:0:9] - Role: **coordinator between the Planning Commission and the States**; **final approval of Five-Year Plans**; prescribes **guidelines** for formulating the Plan, **considers/discusses its progress**, and **suggests ways to achieve its targets** – it did not allocate plan money.
[77:0:10] - Restructured into NITI Aayog's **Governing Council**.
[77:0:11] > Angles: Planning Commission basis (Cabinet resolution ×3), first chairman, NDC date/members/secretary/functions (×8), "not in existence now".
[77:0:12] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.56 (laxmikanth-indian-polity-02 p.215-220); Rocket Polity Part 2 p.20-21; Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy 10th ed. ch.5; app notes 2:77 sub 0
## SUB 1 | NITI Aayog – Status, Structure and Hubs
[77:1:0] - Came into force on **1 January 2015** (Cabinet Secretariat notification) by a **Union Cabinet resolution** – **neither constitutional nor statutory** (not an ordinance or Act); a **think-tank**; based on **cooperative federalism**.
[77:1:1] - **Chairperson: the Prime Minister** (ex officio – no full-time chairman); **Vice-Chairperson** (Cabinet rank), appointed by the PM; full-time members (MoS rank); ex-officio Union Ministers; a **CEO**.
[77:1:2] - **First Vice-Chairman: Arvind Panagariya**; then Rajiv Kumar, Suman K. Bery; **Ashok Kumar Lahiri** from April 2026 (as of 2026).
[77:1:3] - Nodal agency for **poverty estimation**.
[77:1:4] TABLE head: Body / hub | Role
  [77:1:4.0] Governing Council | PM, all State CMs, CMs of UTs with legislatures, LGs of other UTs, VC, members (no Governors); 11th meeting 11 June 2026
  [77:1:4.1] Regional Councils | Regional issues; chair PM or his nominee
  [77:1:4.2] Team India Hub | Leads engagement of States with the Centre
  [77:1:4.3] Knowledge and Innovation Hub | Think-tank capacity
  [77:1:4.4] State Support Mission | SETUs → State Vision@2047
[77:1:5] # Planning horizon
[77:1:6] - Five-Year Plans replaced by a **15-year Vision**, **7-year Strategy** and **3-year Action Agenda** – **5 years is not** one of them.
[77:1:7] > Angles: date 1 Jan 2015 (×3), Cabinet resolution, no full-time chairman, first VC, Team India Hub, 3/7/15 years.
[77:1:8] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.56 (laxmikanth-indian-polity-02 p.215-222); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Part 1 p.4-6; Rocket Indian Economy p.33-35; app notes 2:77 sub 1
## SUB 2 | NITI Aayog vs the Planning Commission
[77:2:0] TABLE head: Feature | Planning Commission | NITI Aayog
  [77:2:0.0] Approach | Top-down | Bottom-up, cooperative federalism (Team India)
  [77:2:0.1] Funds | Allocated funds to States | No fund allocation (Finance Ministry / Finance Commission)
  [77:2:0.2] States | Limited role | Equal partners
  [77:2:0.3] Basis | Cabinet resolution 1950 | Cabinet resolution 2015
[77:2:1] - NITI envisages: **empowered States as equal partners**, a **think-tank** role (domain knowledge, strategy), a **catalyst** role – but **not allocation of resources to needy States**.

=== NOTES 2:78 | Centralization vs Decentralization – Concepts, Arguments and Indian Experience
## SUB 0 | Concepts – Centralisation, Decentralisation and Its Forms
[78:0:0] - **Centralisation**: decision-making authority **concentrated at a single central point** (Union, State capital, HQ). **Decentralisation**: authority **distributed across various levels**.
[78:0:1] - Disadvantage of centralisation: **slow response to local issues**; uniform central policies may **hinder regional policy innovation**; centralisation is a **hindrance to liberty and independence** (Laski/Tocqueville view).
[78:0:2] - **Democratic decentralisation** = community participation and local accountability; features: **autonomy, people's participation, empowerment** ("instilling non-confidence" is NOT one).
[78:0:3] - **Local government attributes: statutory status, community participation, power to tax** – **central control is NOT** one.
[78:0:4] - State control over local bodies runs through **legislation, finance and personnel** (administrative control) – **citizens' grievances** is not a field of State control.
[78:0:5] - Local functions: public health, sanitation, water supply – **not** public order or **judicial review** (higher courts).
[78:0:6] TABLE head: Form | What happens | Control
  [78:0:6.0] Deconcentration | Work shifted to field offices of the same government | Centre keeps full control – weakest
  [78:0:6.1] Delegation | Tasks given to semi-autonomous bodies | Can be withdrawn at will
  [78:0:6.2] Devolution | Powers to elected local governments that elect their own officials, raise own revenue, decide independently | Not withdrawable at will – strongest (73rd, 74th)
[78:0:7] - **Dimensions** of decentralisation (India's weakest = **fiscal**):
[78:0:8] TABLE head: Dimension | Transfers
  [78:0:8.0] Political | Power to decide – elected local bodies
  [78:0:8.1] Administrative | Power to implement
  [78:0:8.2] Fiscal | Power to spend and raise funds and taxes
[78:0:9] > Angles: definitions (×4), devolution, attributes of local government, liberty.
[78:0:10] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 (laxmikanth-indian-polity-02 p.96-98); app notes 2:78 sub 0
## SUB 1 | Gandhi, Article 40 and the Indian Experience
[78:1:0] - **Ancient village panchayats**: the **village as a self-sufficient unit of governance** ("little republics"); AP: **12-Ayagar** system of the **Kakatiyas and Vijayanagara**.
[78:1:1] - **Mahatma Gandhi**: strongest advocate of village self-government – **Gram Swaraj**, republic of self-sufficient villages.
[78:1:2] - **Ambedkar (4 Nov 1948)**: village "a **sink of localism, a den of ignorance, narrow-mindedness and communalism**"; unit = **individual**. Nehru: centralised planned state, heavy industry. **Gandhian Plan (1944): Sriman Narayan Agarwal**.
[78:1:3] - **Article 40 (DPSP, Part IV)**: the State shall **organise village panchayats** as units of self-government – the constitutional base of Panchayati Raj (Art. 243 / Part IX / Schedule XI came only in 1992); **non-justiciable**; a compromise between Gandhi and Ambedkar (Art. 39, 41, 42, 33, 48, 50 are distractors).
[78:1:4] - **64th Amendment Bill (1989)** passed Lok Sabha, **failed in the Rajya Sabha**; **73rd Amendment** gave Art. 40 shape (**Articles 243A, 243B, 243G**).
[78:1:5] - **CDP (1952)** top-down; **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee: January 1957**.
[78:1:6] - **First Panchayati Raj: Nagaur, Rajasthan, 2 October 1959** (Nehru); **AP second, 1 November 1959**.
[78:1:7] - Outcome: political power passed, fiscal least – the decentralisation gap.
[78:1:8] > Angles: Article 40 (×3), Gandhi, ancient village, first panchayat (Nagaur).
[78:1:9] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 (laxmikanth-indian-polity-02 p.96-97); Rocket Polity Part 1 p.5; app notes 2:78 sub 2-4
## SUB 2 | Arguments For and Against
[78:2:0] TABLE head: For centralisation | For decentralisation
  [78:2:0.0] Nation-building after Partition, unity, security | Proximity: local bodies know local needs
  [78:2:0.1] Planned heavy industry | Participation
  [78:2:0.2] Weak local capacity | Accountability and speed
  [78:2:0.3] Elite capture by dominant castes | Political education
[78:2:1] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:78 sub 1
[78:2:2] - **Effect on participation:** decentralisation generally **increases public participation** by involving local bodies in decision-making.
## SUB 3 | Subsidiarity and the Three Fs
[78:3:0] - **Subsidiarity**: function at the **lowest competent level**; moves up only if it cannot do it; invoked by the **2nd ARC**.
[78:3:1] - **3 Fs: Funds, Functions, Functionaries**; incomplete devolution keeps PRIs **implementing agencies**, not planning units; without **fiscal decentralisation** elected bodies stay powerless.
[78:3:2] ~~ Sources: Second ARC 6th Report (Local Governance); app notes 2:78 sub 5

=== NOTES 2:79 | Democratic Decentralisation – Meaning, Objectives and Preconditions
## SUB 0 | Meaning and Objectives of Democratic Decentralisation
[79:0:0] # Meaning
[79:0:1] - **Democratic decentralisation:** transfer of power to **elected local bodies** so that people take part in the decisions that affect them – the scheme that "ultimately came to be known as **Panchayati Raj**" (Laxmikanth).
[79:0:2] - **Term coined by:** the **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** (report November 1957), not the Ashok Mehta Committee.
[79:0:3] - **Local self-government / a constitutional provision for local government** = an exercise in **democratic decentralisation** (UPSC 2017; AP Sachivalayam) – not delegation, de-concentration, federalism or a confederation.
[79:0:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [79:0:4.0] Democratic decentralisation | Power passed to elected local bodies
  [79:0:4.1] De-concentration | Field offices of the same government get work; no transfer to elected bodies
  [79:0:4.2] Delegation | Power lent by a superior authority – revocable at will
  [79:0:4.3] Federalism | Union–State division of power
  [79:0:4.4] Confederation | Union of sovereign States
  [79:0:4.5] Direct democracy | Citizens decide without representatives
[79:0:5] # Objectives and purpose of Panchayati Raj
[79:0:6] - **Fundamental objects:** **people's participation in development** and **democratic decentralisation** – code "**1 and 3 only**" (UPSC 2015, WBCS 2019).
[79:0:7] - **Political accountability** and **financial mobilisation** are not its fundamental objects (they may follow from it).
[79:0:8] - **Main purpose:** to enable people to **participate in developmental administration** (UPPSC 2004) – not merely to raise farm output, jobs or political awareness.
[79:0:9] - **Why adopted:** **to decentralise the power of democracy** (UPPSC 2016).
[79:0:10] - **What PRIs brought:** **formal representation of the weaker sections** in village governance (SCs, STs, women through reservation) – not eradication of untouchability, land to depressed classes or spread of education.
[79:0:11] - **Democratic governance:** **participation of the public in power** – power rests with the people and is exercised by them or their representatives.
[79:0:12] - After the **73rd Amendment**, effective PRIs need decentralisation of **decision-making**, of **administrative powers** and of **the system as a whole** – **not judicial powers**.
[79:0:13] > Code trap: a single-answer version of the UPSC 2015 item has two right options (participation + democratic decentralisation) – the full answer is both.
[79:0:14] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.96; PMF Polity Part 2 p.136, Part 3 p.1; Rocket Polity #37 p.94; app notes 2:79
[79:0:15] - **Critique of Indian democracy since 1947:** for most people **sovereignty is reduced to the mere exercise of the franchise** (voting once in five years) – the case for decentralised, participatory democracy.
## SUB 1 | Colonial Roots – Mayo (1870) and Ripon (1882)
[79:1:0] TABLE head: Viceroy | Resolution | Significance
  [79:1:0.0] Lord Mayo | 1870 (14 December) | Financial decentralisation; fixed grants to provinces; aim mainly fiscal relief
  [79:1:0.1] Lord Ripon | 18 May 1882 | Resolution on Local Self-Government – the 'Magna Carta' of local self-government
[79:1:1] - **Ripon = "Father of Local Self-Government in India"** (APPSC G2 2008) – not Canning, Minto, Mayo or Mountbatten.
[79:1:2] # Points of Ripon's resolution
[79:1:3] - Local bodies as instruments of **political and popular education** and better administration.
[79:1:4] - Local affairs run by rural and urban local bodies with **definite duties** and **suitable sources of revenue**.
[79:1:5] - **Non-officials in majority**, **elected** where possible; **non-official chairmen**.
[79:1:6] - **Official interference to the minimum** – to **revise and check, not to dictate** (control from outside); so "**direct State control** over local bodies" is **NOT** part of it.
[79:1:7] - Official sanction still needed for loans, new taxes, alienation of property, big works, bye-laws.
[79:1:8] - Later: Royal Commission on Decentralisation 1907-09 (**Hobhouse**); **GoI Act 1919** – local self-government a **transferred subject**; GoI Act 1935 – provincial subject.
[79:1:9] - India's **municipal model** comes from the **British** pattern (Ripon's boards).
[79:1:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 24 p.9; Spectrum Modern India p.560-561; PMF Modern History Part 3 p.392; Rocket Polity #37 p.94
## SUB 2 | Local Self-Government – Features, Direct Democracy, Imperium in Imperio
[79:2:0] TABLE head: Is a feature | Is NOT a feature
  [79:2:0.0] Elected by and accountable to local people | Sovereignty
  [79:2:0.1] Defined local area and functions | Legislative supremacy
  [79:2:0.2] Own funds / power to tax | Judicial powers (Nyaya Panchayats are separate)
  [79:2:0.3] Autonomy within State law | Central control
[79:2:1] - **Sovereignty and legislative supremacy** belong to **Parliament and State legislatures**, never to local bodies.
[79:2:2] - **Core principle (Rocket):** democratic decentralisation, community participation and **local accountability** – NOT central control or mere administration.
[79:2:3] - **Direct democracy example: the Gram Sabha** – all registered voters of the village decide directly; the **L.M. Singhvi Committee** called the Gram Sabha the **embodiment of direct democracy**.
[79:2:4] - **Imperium in imperio** = "an **empire within an empire**" = a **State within a State** – how panchayats are described as self-governing units inside the State (APPSC G2 2011); not State immunity, liability or division of a State.
[79:2:5] - **Charles Metcalfe** called Indian village communities "**Little Republics**".
[79:2:6] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.100; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 19 p.6; Rocket Polity #37 p.94; app notes 2:79
## SUB 3 | Gram Swaraj, Article 40 and RGSA
[79:3:0] - **Gram Swaraj:** **Mahatma Gandhi**'s self-reliant **village republic** (Hind Swaraj, 1909) – not Vinoba Bhave, JP or Dayananda.
[79:3:1] - **Article 40 (DPSP):** the **constitutional root** of Gram Swaraj – the State shall organise village panchayats as units of self-government.
[79:3:2] - **Rashtriya Gram Swaraj Abhiyan (RGSA):** Ministry of **Panchayati Raj**, from **2018-19**; objective – develop PRIs' governance capabilities so rural local bodies become **self-sustainable, financially stable and more efficient**; **trains PRI members**.
[79:3:3]   - Revamped for **1 Apr 2022 – 31 Mar 2026**; funding **60:40**, **NE and hill States 90:10**.
[79:3:4] ~~ Sources: Rocket Polity #37 p.94; Praveen CA360 May 2026; myscheme.gov.in (RGSA); app notes 2:79
## SUB 4 | Committees – Recommendations and Preconditions
[79:4:0] TABLE head: Committee | Key point / precondition
  [79:4:0.0] Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) | Coined "democratic decentralisation"; three-tier PRI; genuine transfer of power and adequate resources (not constitutional status); NDC accepted it January 1958
  [79:4:0.1] Ashok Mehta (1977-78) | Two-tier; compulsory taxation powers; no supersession (or elections within 6 months); social audit; district the first point of decentralisation
  [79:4:0.2] Dantwala (1978) | Block-level planning
  [79:4:0.3] Hanumantha Rao (1984) | District planning, coordinated by the Collector
  [79:4:0.4] G.V.K. Rao (1985) | Development bureaucratised – "grass without roots"; restore the Zila Parishad's role
  [79:4:0.5] L.M. Singhvi (1986) | Constitutional recognition of PRIs – a new chapter in the Constitution; Gram Sabha = direct democracy
  [79:4:0.6] Committee on Policy and Programmes (1988) | Constitutional status, 5-year term, direct elections, State Finance and Election Commissions
[79:4:1] - **Constitutionalisation of PRIs** was recommended by the **L.M. Singhvi Committee** – realised by the **73rd Amendment (1992)**.
[79:4:2] - **Mehta tiers:** Gram Panchayat (village) – Panchayat Samiti (block) – Zila Parishad (district).
[79:4:3] - **Whose cooperation:** the Mehta Committee held **local public participation** indispensable for development.
[79:4:4] # First rollouts
[79:4:5] - **First State:** **Rajasthan – Nagaur, 2 October 1959**; **second: Andhra Pradesh – 1 November 1959**.
[79:4:6] - AP's **13 Zilla Parishads** are being reorganised into **28** (one per district) after their terms ended on 24 Sep 2026 (as of 2026).
[79:4:7] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.96-100; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 23 p.14-15; Rocket Polity #37 p.94; app notes 2:79
## SUB 5 | Decentralised Planning and Participatory Budgeting
[79:5:0] - **Participatory budgeting:** citizens **deliberate and negotiate** over distribution of public resources, **directly decide** how and where money is spent, and **historically excluded** citizens gain access to decision-making – all three make local bodies transparent and accountable (CDS 2013: I, II and III).
[79:5:1] - **District Planning Committee:** constitutional coordinating body under **Article 243ZD**.
[79:5:2] - **Kerala People's Plan Campaign:** launched **17 August 1996**; about **35-40%** of plan funds devolved to local bodies (not 60-70%); turned 30 in Aug 2026.
[79:5:3] - **T.M. Thomas Isaac** proposed "**People's Plan Campaign 2.0**" – student internships and an online portal (25 Aug 2026).
[79:5:4] - **Economic Survey 2025-26:** PRI own-source revenue, **e-GramSwaraj**, **SabhaSaar** (**AI tool** for writing **Gram Sabha minutes**); low Gram Sabha participation.
[79:5:5] - J&K DDCs' term ended 24 Feb 2026 with no fresh polls.
[79:5:6] ~~ Sources: CDS 2013 key; Economic Survey 2025-26; app notes 2:79 (current affairs 2026)
