## SUB 0 | Meaning and Objectives of Democratic Decentralisation
[0:0] # Meaning
[0:1] - **Democratic decentralisation:** transfer of power to **elected local bodies** so that people take part in the decisions that affect them – the scheme that "ultimately came to be known as **Panchayati Raj**" (Laxmikanth).
[0:2] - **Term coined by:** the **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** (report November 1957), not the Ashok Mehta Committee.
[0:3] - **Local self-government / a constitutional provision for local government** = an exercise in **democratic decentralisation** (UPSC 2017; AP Sachivalayam) – not delegation, de-concentration, federalism or a confederation.
[0:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:4.0] Democratic decentralisation | Power passed to elected local bodies
  [0:4.1] De-concentration | Field offices of the same government get work; no transfer to elected bodies
  [0:4.2] Delegation | Power lent by a superior authority – revocable at will
  [0:4.3] Federalism | Union–State division of power
  [0:4.4] Confederation | Union of sovereign States
  [0:4.5] Direct democracy | Citizens decide without representatives
[0:5] # Objectives and purpose of Panchayati Raj
[0:6] - **Fundamental objects:** **people's participation in development** and **democratic decentralisation** – code "**1 and 3 only**" (UPSC 2015, WBCS 2019).
[0:7] - **Political accountability** and **financial mobilisation** are not its fundamental objects (they may follow from it).
[0:8] - **Main purpose:** to enable people to **participate in developmental administration** (UPPSC 2004) – not merely to raise farm output, jobs or political awareness.
[0:9] - **Why adopted:** **to decentralise the power of democracy** (UPPSC 2016).
[0:10] - **What PRIs brought:** **formal representation of the weaker sections** in village governance (SCs, STs, women through reservation) – not eradication of untouchability, land to depressed classes or spread of education.
[0:11] - **Democratic governance:** **participation of the public in power** – power rests with the people and is exercised by them or their representatives.
[0:12] - After the **73rd Amendment**, effective PRIs need decentralisation of **decision-making**, of **administrative powers** and of **the system as a whole** – **not judicial powers**.
[0:13] > Code trap: a single-answer version of the UPSC 2015 item has two right options (participation + democratic decentralisation) – the full answer is both.
[0:14] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.96; PMF Polity Part 2 p.136, Part 3 p.1; Rocket Polity #37 p.94; app notes 2:79
## SUB 1 | Local Self-Government – Features, Direct Democracy, Imperium in Imperio
[1:0] TABLE head: Is a feature | Is NOT a feature
  [1:0.0] Elected by and accountable to local people | Sovereignty
  [1:0.1] Defined local area and functions | Legislative supremacy
  [1:0.2] Own funds / power to tax | Judicial powers (Nyaya Panchayats are separate)
  [1:0.3] Autonomy within State law | Central control
[1:1] - **Sovereignty and legislative supremacy** belong to **Parliament and State legislatures**, never to local bodies.
[1:2] - **Core principle (Rocket):** democratic decentralisation, community participation and **local accountability** – NOT central control or mere administration.
[1:3] - **Direct democracy example: the Gram Sabha** – all registered voters of the village decide directly; the **L.M. Singhvi Committee** called the Gram Sabha the **embodiment of direct democracy**.
[1:4] - **Imperium in imperio** = "an **empire within an empire**" = a **State within a State** – how panchayats are described as self-governing units inside the State (APPSC G2 2011); not State immunity, liability or division of a State.
[1:5] - **Charles Metcalfe** called Indian village communities "**Little Republics**".
[1:6] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.100; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 19 p.6; Rocket Polity #37 p.94; app notes 2:79
## SUB 2 | Colonial Roots – Mayo (1870) and Ripon (1882)
[2:0] TABLE head: Viceroy | Resolution | Significance
  [2:0.0] Lord Mayo | 1870 (14 December) | Financial decentralisation; fixed grants to provinces; aim mainly fiscal relief
  [2:0.1] Lord Ripon | 18 May 1882 | Resolution on Local Self-Government – the 'Magna Carta' of local self-government
[2:1] - **Ripon = "Father of Local Self-Government in India"** (APPSC G2 2008) – not Canning, Minto, Mayo or Mountbatten.
[2:2] # Points of Ripon's resolution
[2:3] - Local bodies as instruments of **political and popular education** and better administration.
[2:4] - Local affairs run by rural and urban local bodies with **definite duties** and **suitable sources of revenue**.
[2:5] - **Non-officials in majority**, **elected** where possible; **non-official chairmen**.
[2:6] - **Official interference to the minimum** – to **revise and check, not to dictate** (control from outside); so "**direct State control** over local bodies" is **NOT** part of it.
[2:7] - Official sanction still needed for loans, new taxes, alienation of property, big works, bye-laws.
[2:8] - Later: Royal Commission on Decentralisation 1907-09 (**Hobhouse**); **GoI Act 1919** – local self-government a **transferred subject**; GoI Act 1935 – provincial subject.
[2:9] - India's **municipal model** comes from the **British** pattern (Ripon's boards).
[2:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 24 p.9; Spectrum Modern India p.560-561; PMF Modern History Part 3 p.392; Rocket Polity #37 p.94
## SUB 3 | Committees – Recommendations and Preconditions
[3:0] TABLE head: Committee | Key point / precondition
  [3:0.0] Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) | Coined "democratic decentralisation"; three-tier PRI; genuine transfer of power and adequate resources (not constitutional status); NDC accepted it January 1958
  [3:0.1] Ashok Mehta (1977-78) | Two-tier; compulsory taxation powers; no supersession (or elections within 6 months); social audit; district the first point of decentralisation
  [3:0.2] Dantwala (1978) | Block-level planning
  [3:0.3] Hanumantha Rao (1984) | District planning, coordinated by the Collector
  [3:0.4] G.V.K. Rao (1985) | Development bureaucratised – "grass without roots"; restore the Zila Parishad's role
  [3:0.5] L.M. Singhvi (1986) | Constitutional recognition of PRIs – a new chapter in the Constitution; Gram Sabha = direct democracy
  [3:0.6] Committee on Policy and Programmes (1988) | Constitutional status, 5-year term, direct elections, State Finance and Election Commissions
[3:1] - **Constitutionalisation of PRIs** was recommended by the **L.M. Singhvi Committee** – realised by the **73rd Amendment (1992)**.
[3:2] - **Mehta tiers:** Gram Panchayat (village) – Panchayat Samiti (block) – Zila Parishad (district).
[3:3] - **Whose cooperation:** the Mehta Committee held **local public participation** indispensable for development.
[3:4] # First rollouts
[3:5] - **First State:** **Rajasthan – Nagaur, 2 October 1959**; **second: Andhra Pradesh – 1 November 1959**.
[3:6] - AP's **13 Zilla Parishads** are being reorganised into **28** (one per district) after their terms ended on 24 Sep 2026 (as of 2026).
[3:7] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.96-100; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 23 p.14-15; Rocket Polity #37 p.94; app notes 2:79
## SUB 4 | Gram Swaraj, Article 40 and RGSA
[4:0] - **Gram Swaraj:** **Mahatma Gandhi**'s self-reliant **village republic** (Hind Swaraj, 1909) – not Vinoba Bhave, JP or Dayananda.
[4:1] - **Article 40 (DPSP):** the **constitutional root** of Gram Swaraj – the State shall organise village panchayats as units of self-government.
[4:2] - **Rashtriya Gram Swaraj Abhiyan (RGSA):** Ministry of **Panchayati Raj**, from **2018-19**; objective – develop PRIs' governance capabilities so rural local bodies become **self-sustainable, financially stable and more efficient**; **trains PRI members**.
[4:3]   - Revamped for **1 Apr 2022 – 31 Mar 2026**; funding **60:40**, **NE and hill States 90:10**.
[4:4] ~~ Sources: Rocket Polity #37 p.94; Praveen CA360 May 2026; myscheme.gov.in (RGSA); app notes 2:79
## SUB 5 | Decentralised Planning and Participatory Budgeting
[5:0] - **Participatory budgeting:** citizens **deliberate and negotiate** over distribution of public resources, **directly decide** how and where money is spent, and **historically excluded** citizens gain access to decision-making – all three make local bodies transparent and accountable (CDS 2013: I, II and III).
[5:1] - **District Planning Committee:** constitutional coordinating body under **Article 243ZD**.
[5:2] - **Kerala People's Plan Campaign:** launched **17 August 1996**; about **35-40%** of plan funds devolved to local bodies (not 60-70%); turned 30 in Aug 2026.
[5:3] - **T.M. Thomas Isaac** proposed "**People's Plan Campaign 2.0**" – student internships and an online portal (25 Aug 2026).
[5:4] - **Economic Survey 2025-26:** PRI own-source revenue, **e-GramSwaraj**, **SabhaSaar** (**AI tool** for writing **Gram Sabha minutes**); low Gram Sabha participation.
[5:5] - J&K DDCs' term ended 24 Feb 2026 with no fresh polls.
[5:6] ~~ Sources: CDS 2013 key; Economic Survey 2025-26; app notes 2:79 (current affairs 2026)
