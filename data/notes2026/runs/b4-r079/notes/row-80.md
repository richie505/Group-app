=== Plantation Crops – Rubber, Coffee, Cocoa, Tea, Spices
# Rubber
- Source tree: **Hevea brasiliensis** (Amazonian / Para rubber); milky sap = **latex**, collected by **tapping** the bark; latex is about 30-40% rubber.
- **Vulcanisation** (Charles Goodyear, 1839) = heating rubber with **sulphur** to make it strong and elastic.
- **Natural rubber** is **biodegradable**; tyres of natural rubber shed fewer **microplastics** than synthetic ones.
- Brazil, the home of Hevea, lost its plantations to blight; SE Asia (Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand) took over by the 1930s.
# Coffee
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|

| **Robusta** (*Coffea canephora*) | Hardier lowland coffee of **Africa**; also grown in India |
| **Mocha** | Arabica named after the port of **Mocha, Yemen** |
| **Fazendas** | Large **coffee plantations of Brazil** (estancias = Argentine ranches; kolkhoz = Soviet collective farms) |
- **Sri Lanka** abandoned coffee from **1869** because of **leaf rust** (*Hemileia vastatrix*, a fungus) and switched to tea.

=== Commercial Types – Plantation, Grain, Mixed, Mediterranean, Ranching
| Type | Features | Regions |
|---|---|---|
| **Plantation** | Large estates; single cash crop (**monoculture**); capital-intensive; export-oriented; product of colonialism; **humid tropics** | Tea – India, Sri Lanka; coffee – Brazil; rubber – Malaysia; cocoa – Ghana |
| **Extensive commercial grain** | Large mechanised farms; wheat; **monoculture**; low yield per acre, high per person | Prairies, Pampas, Steppes, Velds, Downs, Canterbury |
| **Mixed farming** | **Crops + livestock on the same farm**, equal weight; fodder crops, crop rotation | NW Europe, eastern North America, temperate southern continents |
| Dairy | Milk; refrigeration and transport | Denmark, Netherlands, NZ |
| **Mediterranean** | Highly **specialised commercial** farming: citrus, olives, **viticulture** | Mediterranean Basin, California, Chile, Cape, SW Australia |
| **Livestock ranching** | Commercial grazing on large ranches / open public land | Western USA, Pampas, Australia, NZ |
- Plantations are in the **humid tropics**, but humid tropical soils are **leached and poor**, not highly fertile (NDA 2016).
- **Intensive subsistence** farming is **not commercial**; dairy, grain farming and ranching are.
- **Intensive farming** = high labour and capital per unit of land; **extensive farming** = large areas with low input per unit of land.
- Commercial grain has a **high commodity (marketed) rate** because of **large mechanised farms and low local demand** (wheat dominance is true but not the reason).
- Commercial grain regions do NOT include the Mediterranean Basin; mixed farming is NOT in Monsoon Asia; cocoa is West Africa, not South Asia.
- Mixed farming ≠ mixed cropping (two crops in one field) ≠ cash + food crops.
- **Viticulture** is a Mediterranean speciality: the region gives about three-quarters of the world's wine and **about 85% of its grapes go into winemaking**. Wet paddy is Monsoon Asia, not Mediterranean.
- Cooperative farming pools resources (Denmark, Netherlands); **collective farming = Kolkhoz** (USSR).

=== Sustainable and Modern Farming
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Organic farming** | No synthetic fertilisers, pesticides or GMOs; relies on **crop rotation** and manures – the classic **eco-friendly** practice |
| **Green agriculture** | **IPM, integrated nutrient supply**, scientific water management, appropriate crop varieties |
| Natural farming | No synthetic fertilisers / chemical pesticides; ZBNF by **Subhash Palekar** |
| Permaculture | Self-sustaining designed ecological systems |
| IPM | Bio-pesticides, pheromone traps, predators |
| **Vertical farming** | Crops in stacked layers **year-round**, even in **non-tropical** and harsh environments; more efficient than land farming |
| Hydroponics / aquaponics | Soil-free / hydroponics + **fish rearing** (not agroecological) |
- UPSC 2020: **crop diversification, legume intensification, tensiometer use and vertical farming** – **all four** are eco-friendly practices.
- Shifting cultivation, HYV-chemical farming and glasshouses are not the eco-friendly answer.
- **Sikkim:** India's first fully **organic** State (**2016**) – not Mizoram or Assam.
- **APCNF**: world's largest agro-ecological programme – **10.20 lakh farmers, 4.89 lakh ha** (Jul 2026).
- **National Mission on Natural Farming**: **8.80 lakh ha**, 18.19 lakh farmers (Mar 2026).
- **Horticulture** output (~**362-367 MT**) now exceeds foodgrains (2026).
- **2026** = UN **International Year of the Woman Farmer**.
- 7 Sept 2020: AP MoU with **Netherlands** – food processing, horticulture, aquaculture.

=== Agricultural Regionalisation

|---|---|
| 13 world agricultural regions (1936) | Derwent Whittlesey |
| **Crop combination** (1954) | **J.C. Weaver** (modified by **Doi**, Rafiullah) |

- Sengupta and Sdasyuk (**1968**, Census monograph): India in **4 macro** and **60 micro** agricultural regions.
- The **index of crop diversification** (Gibbs-Martin) ranges between **0 and 1** – 0 = one crop, near 1 = high diversification.
- Oriental intensive (Whittlesey) = Monsoon Asia: wet paddy, high output per hectare, low per worker; Mediterranean = winter rain.
- India has **20 agro-ecological zones**.
