## SUB 1 | Plantation Crops – Rubber, Coffee, Cocoa, Tea, Spices
[1:0] # Rubber
[1:1] - Source tree: **Hevea brasiliensis** (Amazonian / Para rubber); milky sap = **latex**, collected by **tapping** the bark; latex is about 30-40% rubber.
[1:2] - **Vulcanisation** (Charles Goodyear, 1839) = heating rubber with **sulphur** to make it strong and elastic.
[1:3] - **Natural rubber** is **biodegradable**; tyres of natural rubber shed fewer **microplastics** than synthetic ones.
[1:4] - Brazil, the home of Hevea, lost its plantations to blight; SE Asia (Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand) took over by the 1930s.
[1:5] # Coffee
[1:6] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [1:6.0] Arabica | Highland, mild; originated in Ethiopia
  [1:6.1] Robusta (*Coffea canephora*) | Hardier lowland coffee of Africa; also grown in India
  [1:6.2] Mocha | Arabica named after the port of Mocha, Yemen
  [1:6.3] Fazendas | Large coffee plantations of Brazil (estancias = Argentine ranches; kolkhoz = Soviet collective farms)
[1:7] - **Sri Lanka** abandoned coffee from **1869** because of **leaf rust** (*Hemileia vastatrix*, a fungus) and switched to tea.
[1:8] # Plantation pairs

[1:10] - **Pepper**: grown in India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Vietnam; **Singapore** only trades it.

## SUB 4 | Commercial Types – Plantation, Grain, Mixed, Mediterranean, Ranching
[4:0] TABLE head: Type | Features | Regions
  [4:0.0] Plantation | Large estates; single cash crop (monoculture); capital-intensive; export-oriented; product of colonialism; humid tropics | Tea – India, Sri Lanka; coffee – Brazil; rubber – Malaysia; cocoa – Ghana
  [4:0.1] Extensive commercial grain | Large mechanised farms; wheat; monoculture; low yield per acre, high per person | Prairies, Pampas, Steppes, Velds, Downs, Canterbury
  [4:0.2] Mixed farming | Crops + livestock on the same farm, equal weight; fodder crops, crop rotation | NW Europe, eastern North America, temperate southern continents
  [4:0.3] Dairy | Milk; refrigeration and transport | Denmark, Netherlands, NZ
  [4:0.4] Mediterranean | Highly specialised commercial farming: citrus, olives, viticulture | Mediterranean Basin, California, Chile, Cape, SW Australia
  [4:0.5] Livestock ranching | Commercial grazing on large ranches / open public land | Western USA, Pampas, Australia, NZ
[4:1] - Plantations are in the **humid tropics**, but humid tropical soils are **leached and poor**, not highly fertile (NDA 2016).
[4:2] - **Intensive subsistence** farming is **not commercial**; dairy, grain farming and ranching are.
[4:3] - Mixed farming ≠ mixed cropping (two crops in one field) ≠ cash + food crops.
[4:4] - **Viticulture** is a Mediterranean speciality: the region gives about three-quarters of the world's wine and **about 85% of its grapes go into winemaking**. Wet paddy is Monsoon Asia, not Mediterranean.
[4:5] - Cooperative farming pools resources (Denmark, Netherlands); **collective farming = Kolkhoz** (USSR).

## SUB 6 | Sustainable and Modern Farming
[6:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [6:0.0] Organic farming | No synthetic fertilisers, pesticides or GMOs; relies on crop rotation and manures – the classic eco-friendly practice
  [6:0.1] Green agriculture | IPM, integrated nutrient supply, scientific water management, appropriate crop varieties
  [6:0.2] Natural farming | No synthetic fertilisers / chemical pesticides; ZBNF by Subhash Palekar
  [6:0.3] Permaculture | Self-sustaining designed ecological systems
  [6:0.4] IPM | Bio-pesticides, pheromone traps, predators
  [6:0.5] Vertical farming | Crops in stacked layers year-round, even in non-tropical and harsh environments; more efficient than land farming
  [6:0.6] Hydroponics / aquaponics | Soil-free / hydroponics + fish rearing (not agroecological)
[6:1] - UPSC 2020: **crop diversification, legume intensification, tensiometer use and vertical farming** – **all four** are eco-friendly practices.
[6:2] - Shifting cultivation, HYV-chemical farming and glasshouses are not the eco-friendly answer.
[6:3] - **Sikkim:** India's first fully **organic** State (**2016**) – not Mizoram or Assam.
[6:4] - **APCNF**: world's largest agro-ecological programme – **10.20 lakh farmers, 4.89 lakh ha** (Jul 2026).
[6:5] - **National Mission on Natural Farming**: **8.80 lakh ha**, 18.19 lakh farmers (Mar 2026).
[6:6] - **Horticulture** output (~**362-367 MT**) now exceeds foodgrains (2026).
[6:7] - **2026** = UN **International Year of the Woman Farmer**.
[6:8] - 7 Sept 2020: AP MoU with **Netherlands** – food processing, horticulture, aquaculture.
