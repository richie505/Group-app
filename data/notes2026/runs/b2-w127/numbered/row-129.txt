## SUB 0 | Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961
[0:0] - **Enacted 1961; in force 1 July 1961** (not 1955, 1976, 20 May or 1 June). The **1st joint sitting** of Parliament was on the Dowry Prohibition Bill.
[0:1] - **Section 2 – Dowry:** **any property or valuable security** given or agreed to be given, **directly or indirectly**, before, at or any time after marriage, in connection with the marriage.
[0:2] - **Not dowry:** **dower/mahr** (Muslim personal law); **presents given without demand and entered in a list** under the rules (s.3(2)).
[0:3] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [0:3.0] s.3 | Giving/taking/abetting: not less than 5 years + fine ≥ ₹15,000 or dowry value, whichever is more
  [0:3.1] s.4 | Demanding dowry: 6 months to 2 years + fine up to ₹10,000
  [0:3.2] s.8A | Burden of proof on the accused
  [0:3.3] s.8B | Dowry Prohibition Officers appointed by the State Government
[0:4] - Offences are **cognisable, non-bailable and non-compoundable**.
[0:5] - **1986 amendment** brought **dowry death** (IPC 304B, now BNS 80) and its presumption (Evidence Act 113B, now BSA 118); also amended 1984.
[0:6] - **⚠ Trap:** 5 years is the **minimum**; any payment **on demand** (to the groom's father, sister …) is dowry.
[0:7] - Weak enforcement: **poor enforcement, faulty FIRs**, deaths shown as accidents/suicides; about **20 dowry deaths a day**; **UP, Bihar, MP, Rajasthan** lead.
[0:8] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Society p.39, p.99 l.4630; app notes 2:129 sub 1
## SUB 1 | Sexual Harassment at Workplace – Vishaka and POSH Act 2013
[1:0] - **Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997):** SC guidelines against sexual harassment of **working women at the workplace** (Arts 14, 19, 21) → **POSH Act, 2013** evolved from the **Vishaka guidelines** (asked ×4).
[1:1] - Full name: Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (**Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal**) Act, 2013; **in force 9 December 2013**.
[1:2] TABLE head: Committee | Where | Composition
  [1:2.0] Internal Committee (IC) | Every workplace with 10 or more employees | Senior woman employee as Presiding Officer; 2+ employees; 1 NGO member; half women
  [1:2.1] Local Committee (LC) | District; constituted by the District Officer | Workplaces with fewer than 10, or complaint against the employer
[1:3] - **Time limits:**
[1:4] TABLE head: Step | Time limit
  [1:4.0] Complaint | Within 3 months (extendable by 3)
  [1:4.1] Inquiry | Within 90 days
  [1:4.2] Employer action | Within 60 days
[1:5] - Harassment includes **showing pornography**, unwelcome contact, demands for sexual favours.
[1:6] - **SHe-Box:** online complaint system for workplace sexual harassment, **single window for every woman** whatever her work status – organised or unorganised, private or public; **SHe-Box 2.0** (Mission Shakti) also tracks ICs/LCs.
[1:7] - **AP:** **26 LCs**, **10,437 ICs** (AP SES 2025-26).

## SUB 2 | Domestic Violence Act 2005 and Section 498A
[2:0] - **Enacted 2005; in force 26 October 2006**; a **civil law**.
[2:1] - **Section 3:** physical, sexual, **verbal/emotional** and **economic** abuse.
[2:2] - **Protection Officers** appointed by the **State Government**; relief by **Magistrate**. **AP:** Protection Officers = **DWCDA Project Directors and RDOs**.
[2:3] TABLE head: Relief | Section
  [2:3.0] Right to reside | 17
  [2:3.1] Protection order | 18
  [2:3.2] Residence order | 19
  [2:3.3] Monetary relief | 20
  [2:3.4] Custody / compensation | 21 / 22
[2:4] - **Why domestic violence is common:** social acceptance of wife torture, violence in society reflected in the family, emotional instability – a victim's **tolerating** it is a response, not a cause (CSP 1996).
[2:5] - **Rajesh Sharma v. State of UP (2017):** Family Welfare Committees in every district; 498A complaints referred to them; no routine arrest before their report; NRIs also covered (no routine passport impounding).
[2:6] - **⚠ Outdated:** FWC directions **withdrawn** in **Social Action Forum v. UoI (14 Sep 2018)**; 498A is now **BNS 85-86**.
[2:7] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Society p.88, p.99 l.4651; app notes 2:129 sub 2; sci.gov.in (via app explanation)

## SUB 5 | Trafficking – Constitution, Laws and Reports
[5:0] - **Article 23** prohibits **traffic in human beings, begar** and forced labour; **Article 24** bars children below 14 in factories, mines, hazardous work.
[5:1] - **ITPA 1956** primarily addresses **trafficked women** (commercial sexual exploitation); does **not** punish prostitution itself – punishes **brothel-keeping, soliciting, living on earnings**.
[5:2] - **CLA 2013** definition covers all forms **irrespective of consent** (sex trafficking, forced labour, slavery, organ removal).
[5:3] - **UNODC:** high origin – Bangladesh, India, Nepal, Pakistan; high destination – **India and Pakistan**.
[5:4] - **US TIP Report:** **Tier 1** = only tier fully meeting minimum standards; Tier 2, Tier 2 Watch List, Tier 3.
[5:5] - **Anuradha Koirala** (Maiti Nepal): **Padma Shri 2017** for rescuing thousands of **sex-trafficking victims**.
[5:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society Part 3 p.37; Laxmikanth Part 1 l.7645; app notes 2:129 sub 7
