=== Hindu Rate of Growth
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Coined by | **Prof. Raj Krishna** (not V.K.R.V. Rao, Mahalanobis, Amartya Sen) |
| Year | **1978** (some books: 1976) |
| Variable | Growth of **national income / real GDP** – not per capita income, population or money |
| Rate | About **3.5%** a year (3.52%, 1950-1980) – not 5.5% |
| Period | **1950s to early 1980s** (pre-reform planning era) |
| Per capita growth | Only **1.37%** a year – barely above population growth |
- It describes **stagnant / low growth** of the Indian economy in 1950-80 – nothing to do with the Hindu population, faster growth or inequality.
- Broken in the **1980s** (5.1-5.5%); 2026 echo: a **"Bharat rate of growth"** of 8%+ through AI-led "Reforms 3.0" (Jun 2026).
~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Economy p.98 (Hindu rate: Raj Krishna, national income/GDP, 3.5%, 1950s-early 1980s); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.11 (3.52%, per capita 1.37%); app notes 3:6 sub 0

=== The 1991 Reforms – Trigger and Record
- **Economic reforms (LPG / New Economic Policy): July 1991** – opened the economy to foreign and private investment.
- **Trigger: balance-of-payments crisis** – reserves for only about **two weeks** of imports (under $1 bn); fiscal deficit **8.4%** (1990); the **Gulf War (1990-91)** raised oil prices and cut remittances.
- Shortcomings: faster growth did not cut poverty enough, few quality jobs, unbalanced regional development; **rising consumer-goods imports is not usually listed** as a shortcoming.
- 1997-98 slowdown – **East Asian financial crisis**; currency devaluation there was **unfavourable** for Indian exports.
| View | Argument |
|---|---|
| 1980s turn | **Rodrik-Subramanian (2004)**: transition around 1980 from a **pro-business** (not pro-market) shift; **DeLong (2003)**: take-off in the 1980s |
| 1991 turn | 1980s growth was **debt-fuelled** → **1991 crisis** → sustained high growth after reforms |
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.32 (two weeks of imports, reforms 1991); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 13 p.6-11 (LPG); app notes 3:6 subs 7-8

=== Policy Orientation 1947-1991 – Planning and Import Substitution
| Policy / plan | Year | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| First Industrial Policy | 1948 | **IFCI** set up |
| IPR 1956 ("**Economic Constitution**") | 1956 | Schedule A = **17** industries for the State; Schedule B = **12**; rest private under licensing |
| Second Plan (Mahalanobis) | 1956-61 | **Heavy industries**; **import substitution** adopted as the strategy of industrialisation |
| Third Plan | 1961-66 | **Continued** import substitution (did not introduce it) |
| 1980s reforms | 1980s | Pro-business: broad-banding, MRTP limit raised to **Rs 100 crore** |
| 1991 reforms | 1991 | Reserved industries 17 → **8**; delicensing |
- Major focus 1947-1991: **import substitution** with a large public sector – not export promotion, deregulation or less public investment.
- UPSC 2009: Second Plan heavy industry ✓; "Third Plan introduced import substitution" ✗ → **a only**.
- **Bombay Plan (1944)**: industrialists themselves sought **state protection** and planning support.
~~ Sources: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development ch.2 (import substitution, first seven Plans); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.25 (IPR 1956 "Economic Constitution"); app notes 3:6 sub 2

=== Phases and Growth Rates
| Phase | Average growth |
|---|---|
| Before Independence | about **1%** a year; agriculture 0.3% |
| Plan era (Plans 1-11) | about **4.5%**; agriculture 2-3%, industry 6-8% |
| 1950-1980 ("Hindu rate") | ~3.5% |
| 1980-81 to 1989-90 | **5.13-5.18%** |
| Post-reform | ~6.5%; peak **8.5% in 2003-08** |
| Post-Covid (2022-23 series) | 7.2% (2023-24), 7.1% (2024-25), **7.7% (2025-26 PE)** |
- **Expected growth 2012-13** (Economic Survey 2011-12 / Budget 2012-13): **7.6%** (±0.25); 2008-09 expected about **9%** (actual 6.7%).
- UPSC 2011: GDP and per capita growth did **not** rise steadily in 2006-11 (growth fell from 9.6% in 2006-07 to about 6.7% in 2008-09 and then recovered) → **neither**.
- **High growth** depends on savings and their **optimal allocation** (APPSC G1 1990-91 key).
- **Worst years:** **1979-80: -5.2%** (drought, oil shock); **2020-21: -5.8%** (Covid-19).
- RBI outlook 2026-27: **6.6%** (Jun 2026); **Arvind Subramanian**: 2011-17 growth about **4.5%**, not 7%; AP **9.88%** vs India 7.4% (2025-26).
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.11; Economic Survey 2011-12 ch.1 (7.6% for 2012-13); NSO; app notes 3:6 subs 1, 4-5

=== Investment and Growth – ICOR
- **ICOR** = change in capital ÷ change in output; **lower ICOR = more efficient** investment.
- **Harrod-Domar** (First Plan): **g = s/v**; a higher capital-output ratio with the same savings → **lower growth**.
- **Second Plan**: investment 7% → 11% of national income; **heavy, capital-intensive** industry (not light consumer goods) with long gestation → high ICOR.
- GFCF about **30%** of GDP (2025-26).
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 (plans); app notes 3:6 sub 3
