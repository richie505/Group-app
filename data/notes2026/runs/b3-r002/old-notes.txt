## SUB 1 | Share in Gva and Employment – the Mismatch
[2:1:0] - Services = **more than 50%** of GVA (Economic Survey 2025-26) – **highest** contributor to GDP; employment only **about a third**.
[2:1:1] TABLE head: Sector (2021-22) | Share of GDP | Share of workforce
  [2:1:1.0] Secondary | 24.29% | 22.24%
  [2:1:1.1] Tertiary | 53.89% | 33.80%
[2:1:2] - **Employment-output paradox** (NITI Aayog): high-end services (IT, finance) need high skills, so most new jobs are in low-value trade, hospitality, transport.
[2:1:3] - Regular wages: **51.5%** of service workers vs about **1%** in agriculture.
[2:1:4] - Growth: **9.1%** in FY2025-26 (FAE; new series 8.9%); **10%** in Q1 FY2026-27, led by finance, real estate, IT and professional services (**12.1%**).
## SUB 2 | Service-led Growth Debate
[2:2:0] - Boom began in the **mid-1980s**; sped up after the **1990s reforms**.
[2:2:1] - **EAC-PM Chairman** (Nov 2025): service-led growth cannot last without a **manufacturing base**; manufacturing creates demand for logistics, IT, finance and **steadies GDP cycles**.
[2:2:2] - **Software** is a major export, but India lacks a strong **hardware** base.
## SUB 3 | Services Exports and Fdi
[2:3:0] - India's share of world services trade: **2% (2005) → 4.3% (2024)**.
[2:3:1] - Rank: **8th**-largest services exporter (WTO, April 2025 data); also **8th** in 2014.
[2:3:2] - 2024-25: exports **USD 387.5 bn** vs imports USD 198.7 bn → surplus.
[2:3:3] - **Software and business services = 77.8%** of services exports.
[2:3:4] - Targets: share of world services exports **4.2% by 2022** (older target); services exports **USD 1 trillion** within the USD 2 trillion export goal by **2030**.
[2:3:5] - FDI: services had the **largest share of FDI** in 1997-2000; in FY2024-25 the **services sector** was again the **top** FDI equity recipient (~**19%**, about $9.35 bn), ahead of computer software and hardware (16%).

## SUB 0 | Insurance – Milestones and Regulator
[4:0:0] TABLE head: Act / committee | Year | Provision
  [4:0:0.0] LIC Act | 1 September 1956 | LIC formed; life insurance nationalised
  [4:0:0.1] GIC | 1972 | General insurers nationalised
  [4:0:0.2] Malhotra Committee | 1993-94 | Open insurance to private companies + independent regulator
  [4:0:0.3] IRDA Act | 1999 | Set up IRDAI – statutory regulator, HQ Hyderabad (not Mumbai); set up April 2000
  [4:0:0.4] Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act | Passed 17 Dec 2025 | FDI cap 74% → 100%
[4:0:1] - The 2025 Act amends the **Insurance Act 1938, LIC Act 1956, IRDA Act 1999** and creates a **Policyholders' Education and Protection Fund**.
[4:0:2] - IRDAI vision: **"Insurance for All by 2047"**.
[4:0:3] - FY25: premium income **Rs 11.9 lakh crore**; **health** passed **motor** as the top non-life segment.
[4:0:4] - Life and individual health policies GST-exempt from September 2025; IRDAI sub-committee reviewing private health insurance (Apr 2026).
[4:0:5] - **Actuaries** = insurance experts who judge risk and estimate premium.
[4:0:6] IRDAI was **not** set up on the **Gadgil Committee**'s recommendation – it came from the **Malhotra Committee**.
## SUB 1 | Insurance Terms and Principles
[4:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:1:0.0] Insurance penetration | Total premium as % of GDP
  [4:1:0.1] Insurance density | Premium per capita (usually in US dollars)
  [4:1:0.2] Principle of indemnity | Restores the insured to the pre-loss position, no profit; applies to general insurance (fire, marine, motor, health), not life
  [4:1:0.3] Cyber insurance | Data breach, hacking, ransomware, identity theft
  [4:1:0.4] Aviation hull | Physical damage to the aircraft itself
  [4:1:0.5] P&I | Shipowners' third-party liabilities
[4:1:1] - First sovereign-backed P&I cover: **Bharat Maritime Insurance Pool** (Jul 2026).
[4:1:2] Neither penetration nor density = "insured persons per 100 people" → APPSC 2017 answer "none of these".
[4:1:3] Fire and marine insurance are contracts of indemnity; life insurance is not.
## SUB 2 | Para-banking
[4:2:0] - Legal basis: **Section 6, Banking Regulation Act, 1949**, with **RBI** approval.

[4:2:3] - Bank credit-deposit ratio: 62-year high of **82.6%** in Q1 FY27 (Jul 2026).

=== NOTES 3:5 | Occupational Structure of India – Trends and Inter-state Variation
## SUB 0 | Meaning and Theory
[5:0:0] - **Occupational structure** = spread of the working population among different occupations – across **primary, secondary and tertiary** activities.
[5:0:1] - **Clark-Fisher thesis**: as income rises, labour shifts **primary → secondary → tertiary**.
[5:0:2] - **Structural retardation** (India): the occupational structure changed far more slowly than output; India moved from agriculture **directly to services** with no manufacturing-led stage.
[5:0:3] - Reason the structure stayed about the same: **investment directed towards capital-intensive sectors**.
[5:0:4] - **Premature deindustrialisation**: manufacturing fails to absorb enough farm labour.
## SUB 1 | Workforce Share by Sector Over Time
[5:1:0] TABLE head: Year / survey | Agriculture share of workers
  [5:1:0.0] 1951 | 72%
  [5:1:0.1] Census 2001 | 58.2%
  [5:1:0.2] Census 2011 | 54.6%
  [5:1:0.3] PLFS 2023-24 | 46.1%
  [5:1:0.4] PLFS Q2 FY26 | 42.4%
[5:1:1] - **Agriculture** = always the **highest employment share** (falling). At the **eve of Independence**, the main livelihood was agriculture.
[5:1:2] - **NSS 2011-12:** agriculture's share of the workforce about **48.9%** (Census 2001: 58.2%); still the **largest employer**
[5:1:3] - Now (PLFS Q2 FY26): agriculture about **18% of GVA** but **42.4%** of the workforce – the GDP-share vs employment-share gap.
[5:1:4] - 2021-22 workforce: primary **43.96%**, secondary **22.24%**, tertiary **33.80%**.
[5:1:5] - Urban employment: services **62.0%** (Q2 FY26).
[5:1:6] - PLFS Jan-Mar 2026: rural agricultural employment 58.5% → **55.8%**; rural secondary 22.6%, tertiary 21.7%.
[5:1:7] - Workforce size: **61.6 crore** (PLFS 2025); 56.2 crore employed in Q2 FY26.
[5:1:8] - Agricultural households = **57.8%** of rural households (NSSO 70th round, 2013).
[5:1:9] Highest employment share = **agriculture**; an old APPSC-GS 2007 key "cottage industries" is wrong.

## SUB 4 | Rural vs Urban and Women Workers
[5:4:0] - Census 2011: **72.4%** of workers in **rural** areas, against **68.8%** of population (APPSC-GS 2025).
[5:4:1] 72.4% = rural share of **workers**; 68.8% = rural share of **population**.
[5:4:2] # Feminisation of Agriculture
[5:4:3] - Female workers: **55%** agricultural labourers, **24%** cultivators; women hold only **12.8%** of operational holdings.
[5:4:4] - Drivers: **subsistence farming, unpaid family work, distress employment** (not formal manufacturing jobs).
[5:4:5] - Rural women self-employed: 60% (2011-12) → 73% (2023-24).
[5:4:6] - UN: **2026 = International Year of the Woman Farmer**.
[5:4:7] # Female Labour Force Participation (15+)

[5:4:9] - Rural 33.0% → **45.9%**; urban 23.3% → 27.7% (2019-20 to 2025).
[5:4:10] - Rural women = **80%** of the rise in female workers (2017-18 to 2023-24).
[5:4:11] - Women = **32.5%** of employment (2025); share in salaried jobs 16.6% → 18.2%; 40-44% of women outside the labour force cite caregiving.

## SUB 6 | Informal and Unorganised Employment
[5:6:0] # Definitions
[5:6:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [5:6:1.0] Unorganised sector (NCEUS) | Unincorporated private enterprises of individuals/households, proprietary or partnership, fewer than 10 workers
  [5:6:1.1] Unorganised sector (general) | Small, scattered units largely outside government control (GS 2025); low pay, no job security
  [5:6:1.2] Organised manufacturing | Registered factories – 10+ workers with power / 20+ without
  [5:6:1.3] Informal worker (NCEUS) | Unorganised-sector workers + organised-sector workers without social security
  [5:6:1.4] Unorganised workers (Code on Social Security 2020) | Home-based, self-employed or wage workers in the unorganised sector
  [5:6:1.5] Informalisation of formal enterprises | Contract and outsourced workers inside formal firms
  [5:6:1.6] "Formal informality" | New EPFO 'formal' jobs that are short-term, low-wage or contractual
[5:6:2] - **NCEUS**: chaired by **Dr. Arjun Sengupta**; formed **20.09.2004**, term ended **30.04.2009**.

[5:6:5] - Informal employment: **over 80%** of the workforce (Mar 2026); non-farm informal share about **78%** (PLFS 2022-23).

[5:6:7] - **e-Shram**: **31 crore+** unorganised workers registered.
[5:6:8] - **Four Labour Codes** merged **29** laws: Code on Wages **2019**; Industrial Relations, Social Security, OSH **2020**; expected to raise formalisation from 60.4% to 75.5% + 77 lakh jobs by 2029-30.
[5:6:9] - Social-protection coverage: **19% (2015) → 64.3% (2025)** – India **2nd after China**.
[5:6:10] - Direct factory employment **61% → 47%** (2011-2023); **contract workers = 42%** of factory workforce.
[5:6:11] NCEUS cut-off = fewer than **10** workers, not 20.
## SUB 7 | Gig and Platform Economy
[5:7:0] - **Gig worker**: earns outside the **traditional employer-employee relationship**; defined **for the first time** in the **Code on Social Security, 2020** (Section 2(35)).
[5:7:1] - **Platform work**: work outside an employer-employee relationship where organisations/individuals use an **online platform** to reach others or provide services.
[5:7:2] - Gig workers = about **2%** of the 61.6 crore workforce; not yet a separate PLFS category.
## SUB 8 | Jobless Growth and Slow Shift Out of Agriculture
[5:8:0] - **Jobless growth**: output grows without a matching rise in quality employment – **late 1990s**, when employment growth fell to early-plan levels while GDP grew.
[5:8:1] - **Employment elasticity** (job growth per unit of output growth): **negative in agriculture**; **construction** and services most employment-intensive (RBI study, 2014).
[5:8:2] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [5:8:2.0] History | Colonial deindustrialisation pushed artisans onto land
  [5:8:2.1] Land pressure | Agriculture's share fell only slightly in the Plan era
  [5:8:2.2] Capital intensity | Growth failed to absorb surplus farm labour
  [5:8:2.3] Skills | Only 4.2% had formal vocational training
  [5:8:2.4] Job deficit | ~20 million entrants a year vs ~2 million new jobs; about 12 million enter each year
[5:8:3] - Firms prefer flexibility → away from formal employment.

=== NOTES 3:6 | Economic Development Since Independence – Phases, Growth Rates and the Hindu Rate of Growth
## SUB 0 | Hindu Rate of Growth
[6:0:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [6:0:0.0] Coined by | Prof. Raj Krishna
  [6:0:0.1] Year | 1978
  [6:0:0.2] Variable | Growth of national income / real GDP
  [6:0:0.3] Rate | About 3.5% a year (3.52%, 1950-1980) – not 5.5%
  [6:0:0.4] Period | 1950s to early 1980s (pre-reform planning era)
  [6:0:0.5] Per capita growth | Only 1.37% a year – barely above population growth
[6:0:1] - Blamed on inefficient state planning; broken in the **1980s** (5.1-5.5%).
[6:0:2] - 2026 echo: a **"Bharat rate of growth"** of 8%+ through AI-led "Reforms 3.0" (Jun 2026).
[6:0:3] Hindu rate = **stagnant growth**, not faster growth and not inequality (APPSC-G2 2017).
## SUB 1 | Economy at Independence and the Plan Era
[6:1:0] - Main livelihood on the eve of Independence: **agriculture**; growth before Independence about **1%** a year; agricultural growth only **0.3%**.
[6:1:1] - Plan era (Plans 1-11): about **4.5%** a year; agriculture 2-3%, industry **6-8%**.
[6:1:2] - **Bombay Plan (1944)**: industrialists themselves sought **state protection** – the Licence Raj was partly a capitalist demand.
## SUB 2 | Policy Orientation 1947-1991 – Licence Regime
[6:2:0] TABLE head: Policy / plan | Year | Provision
  [6:2:0.0] First Industrial Policy | 1948 | IFCI set up
