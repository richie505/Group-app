## SUB 1 | The 1991 Reforms – Trigger and Record
[1:0] - **Economic reforms (LPG / New Economic Policy): July 1991** – opened the economy to foreign and private investment.
[1:1] - **Trigger: balance-of-payments crisis** – reserves for only about **two weeks** of imports (under $1 bn); fiscal deficit **8.4%** (1990); the **Gulf War (1990-91)** raised oil prices and cut remittances.
[1:2] - Shortcomings: faster growth did not cut poverty enough, few quality jobs, unbalanced regional development; **rising consumer-goods imports is not usually listed** as a shortcoming.
[1:3] - 1997-98 slowdown – **East Asian financial crisis**; currency devaluation there was **unfavourable** for Indian exports.
[1:4] TABLE head: View | Argument
  [1:4.0] 1980s turn | Rodrik-Subramanian (2004): transition around 1980 from a pro-business (not pro-market) shift; DeLong (2003): take-off in the 1980s
  [1:4.1] 1991 turn | 1980s growth was debt-fuelled → 1991 crisis → sustained high growth after reforms
[1:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.32 (two weeks of imports, reforms 1991); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 13 p.6-11 (LPG); app notes 3:6 subs 7-8
## SUB 2 | Policy Orientation 1947-1991 – Planning and Import Substitution
[2:0] TABLE head: Policy / plan | Year | Provision
  [2:0.0] First Industrial Policy | 1948 | IFCI set up
  [2:0.1] IPR 1956 ("Economic Constitution") | 1956 | Schedule A = 17 industries for the State; Schedule B = 12; rest private under licensing
  [2:0.2] Second Plan (Mahalanobis) | 1956-61 | Heavy industries; import substitution adopted as the strategy of industrialisation
  [2:0.3] Third Plan | 1961-66 | Continued import substitution (did not introduce it)
  [2:0.4] 1980s reforms | 1980s | Pro-business: broad-banding, MRTP limit raised to Rs 100 crore
  [2:0.5] 1991 reforms | 1991 | Reserved industries 17 → 8; delicensing
[2:1] - Major focus 1947-1991: **import substitution** with a large public sector – not export promotion, deregulation or less public investment.
[2:2] - UPSC 2009: Second Plan heavy industry ✓; "Third Plan introduced import substitution" ✗ → **a only**.
[2:3] - **Bombay Plan (1944)**: industrialists themselves sought **state protection** and planning support.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development ch.2 (import substitution, first seven Plans); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.25 (IPR 1956 "Economic Constitution"); app notes 3:6 sub 2
## SUB 3 | Phases and Growth Rates
[3:0] TABLE head: Phase | Average growth
  [3:0.0] Before Independence | about 1% a year; agriculture 0.3%
  [3:0.1] Plan era (Plans 1-11) | about 4.5%; agriculture 2-3%, industry 6-8%
  [3:0.2] 1950-1980 ("Hindu rate") | ~3.5%
  [3:0.3] 1980-81 to 1989-90 | 5.13-5.18%
  [3:0.4] Post-reform | ~6.5%; peak 8.5% in 2003-08
  [3:0.5] Post-Covid (2022-23 series) | 7.2% (2023-24), 7.1% (2024-25), 7.7% (2025-26 PE)
[3:1] - **Expected growth 2012-13** (Economic Survey 2011-12 / Budget 2012-13): **7.6%** (±0.25); 2008-09 expected about **9%** (actual 6.7%).
[3:2] - UPSC 2011: GDP and per capita growth did **not** rise steadily in 2006-11 (growth fell from 9.6% in 2006-07 to about 6.7% in 2008-09 and then recovered) → **neither**.
[3:3] - **High growth** depends on savings and their **optimal allocation** (APPSC G1 1990-91 key).
[3:4] - **Worst years:** **1979-80: -5.2%** (drought, oil shock); **2020-21: -5.8%** (Covid-19).
[3:5] - RBI outlook 2026-27: **6.6%** (Jun 2026); **Arvind Subramanian**: 2011-17 growth about **4.5%**, not 7%; AP **9.88%** vs India 7.4% (2025-26).
[3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.11; Economic Survey 2011-12 ch.1 (7.6% for 2012-13); NSO; app notes 3:6 subs 1, 4-5
## SUB 4 | Investment and Growth – ICOR
[4:0] - **ICOR** = change in capital ÷ change in output; **lower ICOR = more efficient** investment.
[4:1] - **Harrod-Domar** (First Plan): **g = s/v**; a higher capital-output ratio with the same savings → **lower growth**.
[4:2] - **Second Plan**: investment 7% → 11% of national income; **heavy, capital-intensive** industry (not light consumer goods) with long gestation → high ICOR.
[4:3] - GFCF about **30%** of GDP (2025-26).
[4:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 (plans); app notes 3:6 sub 3
