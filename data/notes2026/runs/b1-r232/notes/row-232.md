=== Champaran Satyagraha (1917) – Gandhi's First Satyagraha in India
# The Firsts
- **Champaran (1917), Bihar** – Gandhi's **first satyagraha in India** and his **first successful** satyagraha after returning from South Africa (not Kheda, Ahmedabad, Bardoli, Dandi or Chauri Chaura).
- Champaran district is in **Bihar** – not Gujarat, Bengal, UP or Punjab (APPSC 2019).
- Year **1917** (not 1915 or 1918; NextGen's year list wrongly prints "1918 – Satyagrah at Champaran") → **2017 was its centenary** ("exactly 100 years ago" in a 2017 exam).
- **First civil disobedience** in India: Gandhi **defied the order to leave the district** and preferred punishment – passive resistance / civil disobedience of an unjust order, "a novel method at that time".
- Also Gandhi's **first peasant (farmers') movement** in India – before Kheda (1918) and Bardoli (1928).
- Aim: to solve the problems of the **indigo cultivators** – security of tenure and freedom to grow crops of their choice; not Harijan rights or Hindu unity.
- **Satyagraha** = Satya (truth) + Agraha (insistence); Gandhi (not Patel, Nehru or Vinoba) was the first to use it in the freedom struggle – evolved in **South Africa**, so it was **not formulated at Champaran**.
- Champaran was **localised** – it was **not** Gandhi's first movement (South Africa came first) nor his first all-India one (**Rowlatt Satyagraha, 1919**). Its "first appearance as a leader of the masses" is accepted (NDA 2009); "second appearance" is wrong (NDA 2011).
> Firsts: Champaran = first civil disobedience; Ahmedabad = first hunger strike; Kheda = first non-cooperation; Rowlatt = first all-India / mass strike.

=== Leaders and Persons of Champaran
| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| **Rajkumar Shukla** | Local **indigo cultivator** (peasant) who met Gandhi at the **Lucknow Congress, Dec 1916** and **invited / persuaded** him – not Rajendra Prasad |
| **Rajendra Prasad**, Brajkishore Prasad, **Anugrah Narayan Sinha**, Mazhar-ul-Haq | Bihar **lawyers** who joined Gandhi |
| **J.B. Kripalani** | A **teacher** (history professor, Muzaffarpur) – hence "Acharya" |
| Mahadev Desai, Narhari Parikh | Gandhi's associates (Desai joined in 1917) – not planters' leaders |
- Gandhi reached Champaran on **10 April 1917**; with his lawyers he **toured villages, recording the peasants' complaints** against forced indigo cultivation.
- **Not connected:** **Jai Prakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia**, Patel, Nehru, Vinoba, Maniben Patel.
- **"Mahatma Gandhi and Bihar, Some Reminiscences"** – by **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**.
- **"Mahatma"**: popularly said to have been given by **Tagore** and, in exam keys, "added during the Champaran Satyagraha"; in fact the title was used for Gandhi **before 1917** (Pranjivan Mehta's letter 1909; Jetpur address 21 Jan 1915; Tagore, 6 March 1915).
- Exam keys name **N.G. Ranga** as the one who "opposed" Champaran (UPPCS 2007) – no historical source found for this (Ranga, b. 1900, was 16 in 1917).

=== Chronology of Gandhi's Early Movements and the Freedom Struggle
| Order | Event | Date |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Swadeshi Movement (anti-partition of Bengal) | 1905 |
| 2 | **Home Rule Movement** (Tilak at Poona, Annie Besant at Madras – **not Gandhi**; aim **self-government**) | 1916 |
| 3 | **Champaran Satyagraha** | 1917 |
| 4 | **Ahmedabad Mill Strike** | Feb–18 Mar 1918 |
| 5 | **Kheda Satyagraha** | Mar–Jun 1918 (Nadiad meeting 22 Mar) |
| 6 | **Rowlatt Satyagraha** (hartal) | 6 April 1919 |
| 7 | **Jallianwala Bagh** | 13 April 1919 |
| 8 | Amritsar Congress session | Dec 1919 |
| 9 | Hunter Committee report | 1920 |
| 10 | Khilafat and **Non-Cooperation** (adopted at the **Calcutta** special session, Sept 1920) | 1919/1920–22 |
| 11 | Moplah Revolt (Malabar) | 1921 |
| 12 | Civil Disobedience | 1930 |
| 13 | Quit India ("Do or Die", **Bombay**) – **immediate end of British rule** | 1942 |
- **Order to remember:** Champaran → Ahmedabad → Kheda → Rowlatt (2, 4, 3, 1); Home Rule → NCM → CDM → QIM.
- **Rowlatt Satyagraha** = Gandhi's **first venture in all-India politics**; after it **Gandhi emerged as an all-India (national) leader** – the incident that made him a true national leader.
- **Non-Cooperation Movement** = the first **pan-India mass movement**; the Swadeshi Movement "remained confined to the upper and middle classes … failed to reach the masses".
- Gandhi led NCM, CDM and QIM and worked for the "casteless" (Harijans) – all true.
- **1917–1919** (Champaran to Rowlatt) established Gandhi as a **national leader** and Satyagraha as a method of struggle.
- Champaran, Ahmedabad and Kheda all fell under Viceroy **Lord Chelmsford (1916–21)**.

=== Tinkathia and the Indigo Problem
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Tinkathia** (Teen Kathiya) | Tenants bound to grow indigo on **3/20th** of their land for European planters (not 3/19, 3/21, 3/18) |
| Sharahbeshi | Enhanced rent |
| Tawan | Lump-sum compensation demanded when indigo lost value |
- Place and crop: **Champaran – indigo (neel)**; not Gorakhpur-opium, Begusarai or Burdwan-paddy; not ryotwari or neelkari.
- After the **Bengal Indigo Revolt (1859–60)** indigo shifted from Bengal to Bihar; Champaran ryots had also protested in 1866–68.
- **German synthetic dyes** (late 19th c.) made indigo **unprofitable in the world market** – that, not peasant resistance or government control, is why indigo cultivation declined (UPSC 2020).
- Planters then **raised rents and demanded illegal dues** (they did not reduce taxes) and forced sale at fixed prices – the planters oppressed the **tenants (ryots)**, not the zamindars.
~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.117; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.349; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.32, p.72; NextGen Rocket Indian History 01 p.83; old app notes (sharahbeshi, tawan)

=== Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)
- Dispute between Ahmedabad **cotton-mill owners and workers** – **industrial workers** (the only one of the three early struggles involving the industrial working class).
- Cause: the owners **withdrew the "plague bonus"** (of 1917); workers wanted a wage rise for wartime inflation – not hours of work; the owners (led by **Ambalal Sarabhai**) were Indian, not European.
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Workers' demand / owners' offer | **50%** / **20%** |
| Gandhi advised | **35%** – he **asked the workers to strike**, non-violently |
| Gandhi's fast (15 March) | His **first hunger strike** – owners gave in on the 4th day, settlement 18 March |
| Result | Tribunal / arbitrator awarded **35%** |
| Length | About **21 days** (not 35) |
- **Anusuya Sarabhai** (Ambalal's sister) took up the workers' cause and brought Gandhi in; she founded the **Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (Majoor Mahajan Sangh), 25 Feb 1920**, with Gandhi as adviser.
> First use of the hunger strike as a weapon = Ahmedabad – not NCM, Rowlatt or Bardoli.
~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.118-121; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.349-350; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.73; NextGen Rocket Indian History 02 p.54

=== Champaran Agrarian Committee and Its Results
- The government appointed the **Champaran Agrarian (Enquiry) Committee, 1917**, with **Gandhi as a member** (representing the ryots) – by **agreement between the Government and Gandhiji**.
- **Chairman: F.G. Sly**; members included D.J. Reid, L.C. Adami, Raja Harihar Prasad Narayan Singh, G. Rainy, M.E.L. Tainer – **Anugrah Narayan Sinha was not a member**; J.B. Kripalani, Rajendra Prasad and Rajkumar Shukla were not members either.
- Recommended **abolition of Tinkathia** and the illegal exactions, and a **25% refund** of illegal recoveries → **Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918**; within a decade the planters left.
- **Significance (UPSC 2018): joining of peasant unrest to India's national movement** – not "drastic decrease in plantation crops", all-India participation or Dalit/tribal involvement.
~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.118 (UPSC 2018 answer C); Spectrum Modern India pdf p.349, p.873; old app notes (F.G. Sly and members)

=== Kheda Satyagraha (1918)
- **Kheda district, Gujarat**, 1918 – **first non-cooperation**.
- Cause: **crop failure (drought)** – yield below one-fourth, so the Revenue Code entitled peasants to remission, but the administration **did not suspend revenue collection**; no Permanent Settlement was proposed (UPSC 2001: 1 only).
- **Gujarat Sabha** (Gandhi its president) led the peasants' agitation; **Vallabhbhai Patel** (field leader), Indulal Yagnik, Mohanlal Pandya, Narahari Parikh; Gandhi the spiritual head – the start of the **Gandhi–Patel alliance**.
- **Called off** when the government ordered revenue to be **collected only from those able to pay** (APPSC 2023) – revenue **suspended for the poor** and **confiscated property returned**.
- Gandhi took part directly in Kheda, Rajkot and Non-Cooperation, but **not directly in Vaikom (1924–25)**.
~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.120-121; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.259 (Vaikom), p.350-351; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.69, p.72-73

=== Rowlatt Satyagraha and Jallianwala Bagh (1919)
- **Rowlatt Act** (Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, March 1919): detention **without trial up to two years**; Gandhi's **Satyagraha Sabha, Bombay, 24 Feb 1919**; hartal **6 April 1919**.
- **Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar, 13 April 1919 (Baisakhi)**: crowd protesting the arrest of **Kitchlew and Satyapal**; Brig.-Gen. **Dyer** blocked the only exit and opened fire on an **unarmed** crowd **without warning** – about **10 minutes, 1,650 rounds**; official toll **379 dead, about 1,100 wounded**.
- Lt. Governor of Punjab **Michael O'Dwyer**; Viceroy **Chelmsford**; Hunter Committee condemned Dyer, **House of Lords** backed him.
| Protest | By |
|---|---|
| Gave up the title "Sir" (knighthood) | **Rabindranath Tagore** |
| Resigned from the **Viceroy's Executive Council** | **C. Sankaran Nair** |
| Returned Kaiser-i-Hind medal | Gandhi |

=== Gandhi Before Champaran – Background
- **Phoenix Settlement (1904, near Durban)**; **Tolstoy Farm (1910, near Johannesburg)**; journal **Indian Opinion**; later **Young India, Navajivan, Harijan** (**Mathrubhumi** was not his).
- Political mentor **G.K. Gokhale**; autobiography **The Story of My Experiments with Truth**.
- Returned **9 January 1915** → now **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas**; first major speech **BHU, February 1916**.
- Ashrams: **Kochrab Satyagraha Ashram (1915)** → **Sabarmati (1917)** → **Sevagram, Wardha (1936)**.
- **"Father of the Nation"** – first said by **Subhas Chandra Bose**.
~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.117; NextGen Rocket Indian History 02 p.54; old app notes (journals, ashrams, Pravasi Bharatiya Divas)

=== Gandhi's Arrest and the Great Trial (1922)
- Arrested at his ashram on **10 March 1922**; tried for **sedition** at **Ahmedabad** ("The Great Trial", 18 March 1922) for three articles in **Young India**.
- He **pleaded guilty**; Judge **Broomfield** gave him **six years**; printer **Shankerlal Banker** one year.
