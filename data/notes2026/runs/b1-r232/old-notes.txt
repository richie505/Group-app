=== NOTES 1:232 | Champaran, Ahmedabad Mill and Kheda Satyagrahas; Rowlatt Satyagraha and Jallianwala Bagh
## SUB 0 | Gandhi Before 1917 – South Africa and Return
[232:0:0] # South Africa and Return
[232:0:1] - To **South Africa in 1893**; **Phoenix Settlement (1904, near Durban)**; **Tolstoy Farm (1910, near Johannesburg)**; newspaper **Indian Opinion**.
[232:0:2] - Influences: **Tolstoy**, **Ruskin's Unto This Last**, **Thoreau**; political mentor **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**.
[232:0:3] - Returned **9 January 1915** → now **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas**; spent a year touring India without political positions (Gokhale's advice).
[232:0:4] - **First major public speech in India: BHU, February 1916**.
[232:0:5] - **First to start Satyagraha** in the freedom struggle = **Mahatma Gandhi**; **Satyagraha = Satya (truth) + Agraha (insistence)** = "insistence on truth".
[232:0:6] # Journals, Writings and Ashrams
[232:0:7] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [232:0:7.0] Journals | Indian Opinion (South Africa), Young India, Navajivan, Harijan
  [232:0:7.1] Writings | Hind Swaraj, autobiography (The Story of My Experiments with Truth)
  [232:0:7.2] Ashrams in order | Satyagraha Ashram, Kochrab (Ahmedabad), 1915 → Sabarmati (1917) → Sevagram, Wardha (1936)
[232:0:8] **Mathrubhumi** was NOT Gandhi's journal (APPSC 2019).
[232:0:9] # Titles
[232:0:10] - **"Mahatma"** – **Rabindranath Tagore** (prefix added during the Champaran Satyagraha; APPSC 2019 accepted "given in 1914").
[232:0:11] - **"Father of the Nation"** – **Subhas Chandra Bose**.
[232:0:12] - APPSC 2019 also accepted: Gandhi was **absent** at Nehru's **"Tryst with Destiny"** speech.
## SUB 1 | The Three Early Satyagrahas – at A Glance
[232:1:0] TABLE head: Campaign | Year, place | Issue | Result | FIRST
  [232:1:0.0] Champaran | 1917, Bihar | Tinkathia indigo; illegal dues | 25% refund; Tinkathia abolished | First civil disobedience / first satyagraha in India
  [232:1:0.1] Ahmedabad Mill Strike | Feb-Mar 1918, Gujarat | Plague bonus; workers asked 50%, owners offered 20% | Tribunal gave 35% | First hunger strike
  [232:1:0.2] Kheda | 1918 (Mar-Jun), Gujarat | Crop failure; revenue remission refused | Revenue only from those able to pay | First non-cooperation
[232:1:1] Champaran = first satyagraha; Ahmedabad = first fast; Kheda = first non-cooperation.
[232:1:2] - Gandhi **first used a hunger strike as a weapon** in the **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)**.
[232:1:3] - All three took place under **Viceroy Lord Chelmsford (1916-1921)**.
## SUB 2 | Champaran Satyagraha (1917, Bihar)
[232:2:0] # The Indigo System
[232:2:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [232:2:1.0] Tinkathia / Teen Kathiya | Tenants bound to grow indigo on 3/20th of their land for European planters
  [232:2:1.1] Sharahbeshi | Enhanced rent
  [232:2:1.2] Tawan | Lump-sum compensation demanded when indigo lost value
[232:2:2] - Agreements made by European planters from the **early 19th century**.
[232:2:3] - **German synthetic dyes** made indigo unprofitable in the world market by the early 20th century → planters **raised** taxes and imposed **illegal exactions** (they did not reduce them).
[232:2:4] - Struggle was against **forced indigo cultivation and illegal exactions** (not forest tax); for solving the problems of **indigo workers**.
[232:2:5] - Precursor: **Bengal Indigo Revolt of 1859** – Champaran came nearly 60 years later as Gandhi's "launch pad".
[232:2:6] # Sequence of Events
[232:2:7] - 1. **Rajkumar Shukla**, a local **indigo cultivator**, met Gandhi at the **Lucknow Congress, 1916** and persuaded him to come.
[232:2:8] - 2. Gandhi reached Champaran on **10 April 1917** with **Brajkishore Prasad, Rajendra Prasad, Anugrah Narayan Sinha, Acharya Kripalani**.
[232:2:9] - 3. Defied the order to leave the district (**Motihari**).
[232:2:10] - 4. Toured villages with lawyers, **recording peasants' complaints** → government ordered an inquiry.
[232:2:11] - 5. **Champaran Agrarian Committee** appointed; Gandhi a member.
[232:2:12] - 6. **Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918**: **Tinkathia abolished**; **25% refund** of illegal charges.
[232:2:13] # Persons and Professions
[232:2:14] TABLE head: Person | Role / profession
  [232:2:14.0] Rajkumar Shukla | Local indigo cultivator (agriculturist); invited Gandhi
  [232:2:14.1] Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad, Anugrah Narayan Sinha | Lawyers (vakils) of Bihar
  [232:2:14.2] J.B. Kripalani | Teacher – history professor at Muzaffarpur College, hence "Acharya"; met Gandhi in 1917 at Champaran
  [232:2:14.3] Mahadev Desai | Gandhi's secretary; joined the Champaran work in 1917 (not a planters' leader)
  [232:2:14.4] F.G. Sly | Chairman, Champaran Agrarian Committee
[232:2:15] - **"Mahatma Gandhi and Bihar, Some Reminiscences"** – **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**.
[232:2:16] - **N.G. Ranga** opposed the Champaran Satyagraha.
[232:2:17] Inviter = **Rajkumar Shukla**, not Rajendra Prasad. **Ram Manohar Lohia** and **Jai Prakash Narayan** – not connected with Champaran.
[232:2:18] # Champaran Agrarian (Enquiry) Committee, 1917
[232:2:19] - Appointed by the **Bihar and Orissa government** – by **agreement between the Government and Gandhiji**.
[232:2:20] - **Chairman: F.G. Sly**; **members: D.J. Reid, Gandhi (representing the ryots), L.C. Adami, Raja Harihar Prasad Narayan Singh, G. Rainy, M.E.L. Tainer**.
[232:2:21] **Anugrah Narayan Sinha** was NOT a member of the committee.
[232:2:22] # Exam Points
[232:2:23] - Champaran is in **Bihar, not Gujarat** (APPSC 2019); year **1917, not 1915**.
[232:2:24] - First satyagraha on Indian soil; **first farmers' movement**; first successful satyagraha after South Africa.
[232:2:25] - Key feature: **drastic decrease in cultivation of plantation crops** (indigo).
[232:2:26] - Champaran was **not** Gandhi's first national movement – it was localised; first all-India one = **Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919)**.
## SUB 3 | Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)
[232:3:0] - **Feb-Mar 1918**, Gujarat; mill owners **withdrew the "plague bonus"**.
[232:3:1] - Workers demanded **50%** wage rise (wartime inflation); owners offered **20%**.
[232:3:2] - Workers approached **Anusuya Sarabhai** → she appealed to Gandhi; her brother **Ambalal Sarabhai** led the **mill owners**.
[232:3:3] - Gandhi advised **35%**, went on **fast** (first hunger strike in India); strike lasted **21 days (not 35)**; tribunal awarded **35%**.
[232:3:4] - **Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (Majoor Mahajan Sangh)** – founded by **Gandhi with Anusuya Sarabhai**, **25 February 1920**; worked on arbitration and non-violence.
[232:3:5] Anusuya Sarabhai | Workers' side | Co-founded TLA
Ambalal Sarabhai | Owners' side | Her brother
## SUB 4 | Kheda Satyagraha (1918)
[232:4:0] - **Kheda district, Gujarat**, **1918**.
[232:4:1] - Grievance: **crop failure (drought / famine)**; peasants sought **remission / suspension of land revenue**; government refused; also called a struggle **against high taxes**.
[232:4:2] - Peasants refused to pay despite threats of land confiscation.
[232:4:3] - **Gandhi – spiritual head**; organisers **Vallabhbhai Patel** (field commander), **Mohanlal Pandya, Narahari Parikh**.
[232:4:4] - Began the **Gandhi-Patel alliance**.
[232:4:5] - **Called off** when the government agreed to collect revenue **only from those able to pay**, suspend it for poor peasants and return confiscated property (APPSC 2023).
## SUB 5 | Significance
[232:5:0] - **1917-1919** established Gandhi as a **national leader** and Satyagraha as the method of struggle.
[232:5:1] - Connected Gandhi with **peasants** (Champaran, Kheda) and **labour** (Ahmedabad).
[232:5:2] - Recruited leaders: **Rajendra Prasad, Kripalani, Mahadev Desai, Vallabhbhai Patel**.
[232:5:3] - **AP angle**: Champaran (1917) and Kheda (1918) inspired **Andhra peasant movements**.
## SUB 6 | Rowlatt Act and Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919)
[232:6:0] - **Rowlatt Act** = **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919**: political suspects could be held **up to two years without trial**; trials by **three High Court judges** with **no jury and no appeal**; in effect suspended **habeas corpus**.
[232:6:1] - Gandhi organised the **Satyagraha Sabha** (**Bombay, 24 February 1919**); members took an oath to disobey the Act.
[232:6:2] - **6 April 1919**: nationwide **hartal**; peaceful in **Vizag, Rajahmundry, Guntur, Vijayawada, Nellore**.
[232:6:3] - **First venture in all-India politics** – Rowlatt Satyagraha; Champaran, Kheda and Ahmedabad were **localised**.
## SUB 7 | Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919)
[232:7:0] # The Incident
[232:7:1] - **Amritsar, Punjab**; **13 April 1919**; crowd gathered on **Baisakhi**, during Rowlatt protests.
[232:7:2] - Protest against the arrest of **Dr Satyapal** and **Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew** (arrested **9 April 1919**; symbols of Hindu-Muslim unity) and the Rowlatt Act.
[232:7:3] - **10 April**: troops fired on a crowd demanding their release.
[232:7:4] - Lt. Governor **Sir Michael O'Dwyer** handed Amritsar to **Brig.-Gen. Reginald Dyer**, who banned assemblies.
[232:7:5] - Dyer **blocked the only exit**, fired **without warning** for about **10 minutes**, **1,650 rounds**.
[232:7:6] TABLE head: Office | Person
  [232:7:6.0] Viceroy | Lord Chelmsford
  [232:7:6.1] Lt. Governor of Punjab | Michael O'Dwyer
  [232:7:6.2] Ordered firing | Brig.-Gen. Reginald Dyer
[232:7:7] # Casualties and Aftermath
[232:7:8] - **Official: 379 dead, 1,100 injured**; **Congress estimate: over 1,000** dead.
[232:7:9] - Martial law, **crawling order**, floggings.
[232:7:10] - **Hunter Committee** condemned Dyer; the **House of Lords backed him**.
[232:7:11] - **Udham Singh** killed **Sir Michael O'Dwyer** in London.
[232:7:12] # Who Gave Up What
[232:7:13] TABLE head: Protester | Gave up
  [232:7:13.0] Rabindranath Tagore | Knighthood
  [232:7:13.1] Gandhi | Kaiser-i-Hind medal (awarded 1915; surrendered during Non-Cooperation)
  [232:7:13.2] Sir C. Sankaran Nair | Resigned from the Viceroy's Executive Council
[232:7:14] O'Dwyer = Lt. Governor; Dyer = Brigadier-General who fired. Udham Singh killed O'Dwyer, not Dyer.
[232:7:15] - **The ground:** Jallianwala Bagh was a walled garden, closed in by houses and walls, with only one narrow way in and out.
## SUB 8 | First Arrest and Sedition Trial of Gandhi (1922)
[232:8:0] - Arrested at his ashram on **10 March 1922**; tried for **sedition** at **Ahmedabad** on **18 March 1922** – **"The Great Trial"**.
[232:8:1] - Charge based on three **Young India** articles: "Tampering with Loyalty", "The Puzzle and its Solution", "Shaking the Manes".
[232:8:2] - Gandhi **pleaded guilty**; Judge **Broomfield** sentenced him to **six years** (following Tilak's case); printer **Shankerlal Banker** got one year.
