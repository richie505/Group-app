[1:0:3] - **Wrong pairs to spot:** biotin = **B7** (not B12); B1 is not linked to pernicious anaemia (that is **B12**); vitamin E is not about clotting; vitamin K deficiency **prolongs** clotting, it does not shorten it.
[1:0:4] - **Cyanocobalamin** = the synthetic, cobalt-containing form of **B12**.
[1:0:5] # Functions asked
[1:0:6] - **Vitamin D** = the **"sunshine vitamin"** (made in skin under sunlight); needed for **bones and teeth** (calcium absorption).
[1:0:7] - **Vitamin C** = collagen formation (bones, teeth, skin, wound healing), iron absorption, antioxidant; **not stored**; the vitamin most **destroyed by heating**; lost by **oxidation** when cut fruit is exposed to air.
[1:0:8] - **Vitamin K** = blood clotting; made by gut bacteria (with **B12**). Vitamin A is stored in the liver.
[1:0:9] - **Heart disease risk:** high BP, raised glucose, raised lipids – **not** vitamin A deficiency.
[1:0:10] # Sources
[1:0:11] TABLE head: Vitamin | Richest / typical source
  [1:0:11.0] C | Amla (Indian gooseberry) – richest; chilli among vegetables; guava, citrus, papaya; milk is poor in vitamin C
  [1:0:11.1] A | Cod-liver oil (highest per gram), liver; carrot, mango (among fruits), pumpkin; Golden Rice (beta-carotene)
  [1:0:11.2] B1 | Rice bran / husk layer, whole grains, yeast (lost on polishing)
  [1:0:11.3] D | Sunlight, fish-liver oil, egg yolk, fortified milk and orange juice; plain seeds and fruits have almost none
  [1:0:11.4] E | Wheat-germ oil
  [1:0:11.5] K | Green leafy vegetables; also nuts, eggs, animal liver (APPSC 2004 key)
[1:0:12] # Discovery
[1:0:13] - **Casimir Funk** coined "**vitamine**" (1912); **Hopkins** – "accessory food factors"; **James Lind** – citrus cures scurvy; **McCollum and Davis** – vitamin A (1913); McCollum also identified **vitamin D** (1922, anti-rickets factor in cod-liver oil); **Umetaro Suzuki** – rice-bran extract (aberic acid, B1).
[1:0:14] - Vitamins are **organic compounds** needed in small amounts.
[1:0:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Biology p.10, p.41-43, p.46; PMF General Science Part 3 p.14-17; OnlyIAS NCERT S&T Part 11; app notes 5:1
[1:0:16] - **Vitamin B6 and dreams:** a 2018 Univ. of Adelaide study found B6 supplements improved **dream recall** (remembering dreams).
[1:0:17] - **Milk and vitamin A:** books treat milk as **poor in vitamin A** (and C, iron); carrot, mango, papaya are the carotene sources.
[1:0:18] - **Xerophthalmia** (vitamin A deficiency, leading cause of preventable child blindness) is India's priority deficiency – the National Prophylaxis Programme gives children vitamin A doses (UPSC 1996).
[1:0:19] - The body **cannot make most vitamins** (exceptions: D in skin, K and B12 by gut bacteria, niacin from tryptophan) – they must come from food; vitamins **do not kill bacteria**.
[1:0:20] - **Vitamin K** is the **antidote** to warfarin-type **anticoagulant** poisons (rat poisons) – it restores clotting-factor synthesis.
[1:0:21] - **Vitamin B12** is water-soluble yet **stored in the liver for 3–5 years** – the exception to 'water-soluble vitamins are not stored'.
[1:0:22] - **Match drill:** malaria – **mosquito bite** (*Plasmodium* via female *Anopheles*); goitre – **iodine** deficiency; rickets – vitamin D; night blindness – vitamin A.
[1:0:23] - **Milk** has several **B vitamins** (riboflavin B2, B12) and calcium but is poor in **vitamin C** and **iron**.
[1:0:24] - The body **cannot make vitamin A** itself (it comes from food retinol or carotene); proteins, enzymes and hormones are made in the body.
## SUB 1 | Proteins, Amino Acids and Malnutrition
[1:1:0] - **Proteins** = **body-building** nutrients (growth and repair; most important for growing children and athletes building muscle) – ~**4 kcal/g**; **amino acids** are their **building blocks** (20 common, ~9–10 essential; not stored).
[1:1:1] - **Essential amino acids** (from food): histidine, **isoleucine**, leucine, lysine, **methionine**, phenylalanine, **threonine**, **tryptophan**, valine. **Non-essential:** **tyrosine**, alanine, aspartic acid, cysteine, glycine, **proline** (an amino acid).
[1:1:2] - **Egg** = **highest biological value** – contains **all essential amino acids** in the right proportion.
[1:1:3] TABLE head: Protein | Found in
  [1:1:3.0] Casein (~80% of milk protein) | Milk (primary group); whey proteins the rest
  [1:1:3.1] Gluten | Wheat
  [1:1:3.2] Zein | Maize
  [1:1:3.3] Keratin | Hair, nails, horn
  [1:1:3.4] Myosin (with actin) | Muscle
  [1:1:3.5] Albumin | Egg white, blood
  [1:1:3.6] Collagen / fibroin | Connective tissue / silk
[1:1:4] - "**Only proteins**" pair: **keratin and myosin** (fructose, maltose and sucrose are sugars).
[1:1:5] - **Lactoderm** = the sticky **protein film (skin) on heated milk**.
[1:1:6] # Protein-energy malnutrition
[1:1:7] TABLE head: Disease | Cause | Sign
  [1:1:7.0] Kwashiorkor | Protein deficiency (calories adequate), children over 1 | Oedema, swollen belly, skin and hair changes
  [1:1:7.1] Marasmus | Severe lack of both protein and calories, infants | Extreme wasting, no oedema
  [1:1:7.2] Marasmic kwashiorkor | Deficiency of both proteins and calories with oedema | Wasting + oedema
[1:1:8] - Not PEM: scurvy (C), goitre (iodine), mumps (virus), achondroplasia (genetic dwarfism), rickets (D).
[1:1:9] - **Protein per 100 g (increasing):** egg white (~11 g) < fish (~20 g) < Bengal gram dal (~21 g) < groundnut (~25 g) < **skimmed milk powder (~35 g)**; soybean is the **highest-protein grain** (~40 g).
[1:1:10] - Nursing mother needs **65 g** protein a day.
[1:1:11] - **Pepsin** (stomach) breaks proteins into **peptides**; trypsin works later in the small intestine.
[1:1:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Biology p.12, p.42-46; PMF General Science Part 2 p.46, Part 3 p.2-4; protein-content order: general knowledge, not checked; app notes 5:1
[1:1:13] - **Alpha-keratin** – skin (epidermis), hair, nails, horns, wool; **beta-keratin** – feathers, scales.
[1:1:14] - **Protein needs with age:** older adults need **more** protein per kg (1.0–1.2 g/kg, PROT-AGE) than young adults (0.8 g/kg) to prevent muscle loss.
[1:1:15] - **DNA** contains the bases **adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine** (+ sugar, phosphate); **tyrosine** is an amino acid, not part of DNA.
[1:1:16] - **Oxytocin** is a **peptide hormone** (9 amino acids, from the hypothalamus, released by the posterior pituitary), not counted as a protein; keratin, fibroin and collagen are structural proteins.
[1:1:17] - **Hair keratin** has **sulphur (disulphide) bonds**; hair setting/perming breaks and re-forms them; **henna** (lawsone) stains by binding to **proteins and amino acids** of skin and hair.
[1:1:18] - Enzymes are basically **proteins** – e.g. pepsin, trypsin, amylase (not fats, sugars or vitamins).
[1:1:19] - **Enzymes** are biological catalysts and are **mostly proteins** (a few RNA enzymes – ribozymes).
## SUB 2 | Carbohydrates, Sugars, Fats and Food Groups
[1:2:0] TABLE head: Nutrient | Role | Key facts
  [1:2:0.0] Carbohydrate | Main energy-giving food | Rice – highest carbohydrate share; rice's major nutrient = carbohydrate
  [1:2:0.1] Fat | Stored energy | ~9.3 kcal/g (most per gram); built from fatty acids + glycerol
  [1:2:0.2] Protein | Body-building | ~4 kcal/g
  [1:2:0.3] Vitamins, minerals | Regulatory, micronutrients | Protein, carbohydrate and fat = macronutrients (also for livestock)
[1:2:1] - **Starch** = a **polysaccharide** (glucose polymer); the **iodine test** turns starch **blue-black**.
[1:2:2] - **Fructose** = **fruit sugar**, the **sweetest natural sugar**; gives fruits their sweet taste.
[1:2:3] - **Honey:** mainly **fructose** (~38%) > glucose (~31%); sucrose and maltose minor; honey is not a colloid.
[1:2:4] - **Relative sweetness (descending): fructose > sucrose > glucose > maltose** (> lactose).
[1:2:5] - **Invert sugar** = glucose + fructose from **hydrolysis of sucrose**; **sweeter than sucrose** because of its free fructose (the hydrolysis is how it is made, not why it is sweet).
[1:2:6] - **Sucrose** = glucose + fructose (disaccharide); maltose, lactose = disaccharides; glucose, fructose, ribose = monosaccharides.
[1:2:7] - **Soybean** is rich in **all three** – protein (~40%), fat (~20%) and carbohydrate.
[1:2:8] - Plants make the essential foods **carbohydrates, fats and proteins**; **nucleic acids** are genetic material, not a "food".
[1:2:9] - **Oatmeal** = a **cereal-grain** product; sago and arrowroot are starches from stems/roots, soya flour is from a legume.
[1:2:10] # Artificial sweeteners
[1:2:11] TABLE head: Sweetener | Fact
  [1:2:11.0] Saccharin (ortho-sulphobenzimide) | First popular artificial sweetener (~550× sucrose)
  [1:2:11.1] Aspartame | Most widely used; NCERT: "about 100 times as sweet as cane sugar" (dipeptide)
  [1:2:11.2] Alitame | High-potency sweetener (~2000×)
  [1:2:11.3] Sucralose | Modified sugar – trichloro derivative of sucrose (~600×)
  [1:2:11.4] Stevia | Natural sweetener
[1:2:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Chemistry p.49-51; NextGen Rocket Biology p.11, p.42; PMF General Science Part 2 p.54-55; PMF General Science Part 7 p.68; OnlyIAS NCERT S&T Part 11 (food tests); app notes 5:1
[1:2:13] - **Linseed (flaxseed)** oil has the most **α-linolenic acid** (omega-3) among oilseeds.
[1:2:14] - **Balanced (healthy) diet:** an **optimal** mix of carbohydrate, protein and fat with **adequate water and roughage (fibre)**, plus vitamins and minerals.
[1:2:15] - **Taste of starch:** starch itself is almost tasteless; on chewing, **salivary amylase** turns it into **maltose**, so starchy food (and carbohydrates generally) taste **sweet**.
[1:2:16] - **Exercise fuel:** carbohydrate (muscle glycogen, blood glucose) is the main fuel of hard and endurance exercise; in **prolonged low-moderate** exercise **fat** supplies ~50% or more.
[1:2:17] - **ATP** (adenosine triphosphate) = the **energy currency** of the cell; glucose is the fuel, pyruvic acid the product of glycolysis.
[1:2:18] - **Unsaturated fats** have **C=C double bonds**, so they are more reactive (oxidise, go rancid, can be hydrogenated); saturated fats have only single bonds.
[1:2:19] - **Cellulose** gives **no colour with iodine**; starch blue-black – both are plant glucose polymers.
[1:2:20] - **Formulae:** glucose/fructose **C6H12O6**; sucrose C12H22O11; starch (C6H10O5)n.

  [1:3:4.0] Potassium dichromate | Breath analyser – orange dichromate turns green when it oxidises alcohol

[1:3:13] - **Ripe banana:** main acid = **malic acid** (apples too); citric dominates in citrus.
[1:3:14] - **pH ladder:** gastric juice ~1.5 < lemon ~2 < vinegar ~3 < pure water 7 < **blood 7.4** < baking soda ~8.5 < **milk of magnesia ~10** < **NaOH ~14**.
[1:3:15] - **Oxalic acid** – also used in **photography** (ferrous oxalate developer).
[1:3:16] - **Oil of vitriol** = concentrated **sulphuric acid** (king of chemicals); blue vitriol = CuSO4.
[1:3:17] - **Ascorbic acid** has **no –COOH group** (its acidity is from an enol –OH in a lactone ring); citric, lactic and tartaric acids are carboxylic acids.
[1:3:18] - **Sulphuric acid** is usually called the **first mineral acid discovered** (some keys say nitric acid).

[1:3:20] - **Litmus:** a **base** turns **red litmus blue**; an **acid** turns **blue litmus red**; neutral salt changes neither.
[1:3:21] - **Litmus test logic:** red litmus stays red → **not a base** (could be acid or neutral); blue stays blue → not an acid.
[1:3:22] - **Glacial acetic acid:** pure ethanoic acid melts at **290 K (16.6 °C)** – a fairly **high** melting point – so it **freezes in winter** in cold climates.
## SUB 4 | Food Preservation, Ripening, Adulterants and Toxins
[1:4:0] # Preservatives and additives
[1:4:1] TABLE head: Preservative | Used in
  [1:4:1.0] Sodium benzoate / benzoic acid (E211) | Soft drinks, pickles, sauces (acidic foods)
  [1:4:1.1] Sorbic acid / potassium sorbate | Most common against moulds, yeasts, fungi and bacteria – cheese, pickles, cooked foods

[1:4:2] - **Food additives** = added **intentionally** to improve food (preservatives, colours, emulsifiers, sweeteners, flavour enhancers). **Adulteration** = adding inferior/harmful substances; causes poisoning, allergies, nutritional deficiency.
[1:4:3] - **MSG (Ajinomoto)** = flavour enhancer, **umami**. Aspartame and stevia are sweeteners; fructose is a natural sugar, not an artificial sweetener.
[1:4:4] - **Fortification:** iodised salt; rice with iron, folic acid, B12; oil/milk with A and D; FSSAI logo **"+F"**.
[1:4:5] - **Irradiation:** ionising (gamma) radiation kills organisms without much nutrient loss; potato/onion shelf life **8 months** without cold storage. **DAE: 25 irradiators in PPP mode**.
[1:4:6] - **PM Kisan Sampada Yojana** (MoFPI) Cold Chain scheme: 16 irradiation units approved, 9 operational (Aug 2025).
[1:4:7] - **Jul 2026:** food-safety reforms – Food Safety on Wheels, blockchain batch tracing, "One Nation, One Regulator" ends dual **BIS/AGMARK** certification.
[1:4:8] # Storage and freezing
[1:4:9] - **Cold storage** works because low temperature **lowers the rate of respiration** of fruits.
[1:4:10] - **IQF = Individually Quick Frozen** – each piece frozen separately and fast.
[1:4:11] - **Rancidity** = **oxidation** of fats and oils (so check the manufacture date of fried foods); nitrogen-flushed chip packets prevent it.
[1:4:12] - **Blackheart of potato** = **oxygen deficiency** in storage (an abiotic disorder).
[1:4:13] # Ripening
[1:4:14] - **Ethylene** = the gaseous plant hormone for **fruit ripening** (and the gas used for artificial ripening).
[1:4:15] - **Calcium carbide** releases **acetylene**, which acts like ethylene – the chemical (illegally) used to ripen mangoes.

## SUB 6 | Everyday Chemistry – Colloids, Solutions, Polymers and Organic Reactions
