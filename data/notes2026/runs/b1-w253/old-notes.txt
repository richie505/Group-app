## SUB 0 | National Symbols – Flag, Anthem, Song and Emblem
[253:0:0] TABLE head: Symbol | Key facts
  [253:0:0.0] National Flag | Adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 22 July 1947; ratio 3:2; Ashoka Chakra (navy blue, 24 spokes) replaced the charkha
  [253:0:0.1] National Anthem – Jana Gana Mana | Tagore; Bengali, first of five stanzas of a Brahmo hymn; first sung at the Calcutta INC session, 27 Dec 1911; adopted 24 Jan 1950
  [253:0:0.2] National Song – Vande Mataram | Bankim Chandra Chatterji; composed 1875, in Anandamath (1882); first sung by Tagore at the 1896 (Calcutta) INC session; adopted 24 Jan 1950
  [253:0:0.3] State Emblem | Lion Capital of Ashoka, Sarnath; abacus shows bull, horse, elephant, wheel – no camel; motto Satyameva Jayate from the Mundaka Upanishad
  [253:0:0.4] National bird / animal | Peacock / Tiger (Royal Bengal Tiger) – the lion is on the emblem but is not the national animal

[253:0:2] - **Vande Mataram first sung at the 1896 session** – by **Tagore**, not Bankim or Sarojini Naidu; Jana Gana Mana first sung **1911** (Calcutta).
[253:0:3] - The Constituent Assembly adopted the flag on **22 July 1947** – not 14 Aug 1947 or 22 Jan 1947.
[253:0:4] - **Current linkage:** 150 years of Vande Mataram (2025-26); PM's tributes to Pingali Venkayya on his **150th birth anniversary** (PMF Lens, 2 Aug 2026).

## SUB 3 | Origin of the Idea – Demand for a Constituent Assembly
[253:3:0] TABLE head: Year | Development
  [253:3:0.0] 1895 | Swaraj Bill (Constitution of India Bill) – early idea of a constitution; some sources credit Tilak
  [253:3:0.1] 1934 | M.N. Roy first put forward the idea; the Swaraj Party (Ranchi, May 1934) made the first formal demand
  [253:3:0.2] 1935 | Became an official INC demand
  [253:3:0.3] 1936 | Restated at the Lucknow and Faizpur sessions
  [253:3:0.4] 1938 | Nehru: constitution by an Assembly elected on adult franchise
  [253:3:0.5] 1940 | Accepted in principle – August Offer
  [253:3:0.6] 1942 | Cripps proposals conceded an Indian-made constitution
  [253:3:0.7] 1946 | Set up under the Cabinet Mission Plan
[253:3:1] - **First to put forward the idea = M.N. Roy (1934)** (UPSC 2017; APPSC Group-1) – not Nehru, Ambedkar, Tilak or Annie Besant.
[253:3:2] - **"First mooted" among parties = the Swaraj Party** (UP UDA 2001: option "Swaraj Party in 1935"; strictly May 1934) – not the Congress (1936), the League or the 1946 conference.
[253:3:3] > Roy 1934 → INC 1935 → Nehru 1938 → August Offer 1940 → Cabinet Mission 1946.

## SUB 4 | Composition, Members and Criticism
[253:4:0] - Total strength **389** (296 British India + 93 princely states); fell to **299** after the League members from Pakistan areas withdrew.
[253:4:1] - Provincial seats divided among **General, Muslim, Sikh**; elected by each community in the provincial assembly by **proportional representation – single transferable vote**; princely states' members **nominated by the rulers**.
[253:4:2] - July–Aug 1946 elections: **Congress 208**, **League 73**, others 15.
[253:4:3] - **15 women** members (Durgabai Deshmukh, Sarojini Naidu, Hansa Mehta …).
[253:4:4] - **Not members: Gandhi and Jinnah**; also **Jayaprakash Narayan** was not a member (CDS 2009) – Patel, Kripalani and K.M. Munshi were.
[253:4:5] - **Socialist Party** National Executive (1946; **JP, Lohia**) demanded its **dissolution and re-election by universal adult suffrage** (RPSC 2024).
[253:4:6] - **Criticism:** indirectly elected on the **1935 Act's limited franchise** (tax, property, education); "Congress Assembly"; lawyer and Hindu dominance; "Drifting Committee" (Naziruddin Ahmed).
[253:4:7] - **Patel on separate electorates:** "Can you show me one free country where there are separate electorates? … The British element is gone, but they have left **mischief** behind" (UPPCS 2021).
[253:4:8] # Indian Independence Act, 1947
[253:4:9] - Made it a **fully sovereign body**; also the **Dominion Legislature** – chaired by **Rajendra Prasad** as constitution-maker and by **G.V. Mavalankar** as legislature; continued as the **Provisional Parliament till 1952**.
[253:4:10] > Not members: Gandhi, Jinnah, JP. Dissolution demand: Socialist Party. "Mischief" quote: Patel.
[253:4:11] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Indian Polity 8th ed. ch.2 p.39-41, 46; Spectrum Modern India p.645-647; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 08 p.3; old app notes row 253

=== NOTES 1:256 | Wanchoo Committee and the Problems of the New Andhra State
## SUB 0 | Capital, High Court and the Bellary Taluks
[256:0:0] # Capital of Andhra State (1953)
[256:0:1] - **Capital:** **Kurnool** – temporary capital of Andhra State from its formation on **1 October 1953** (APPSC JA 2023: "Kurnool became the capital in **1953**").
[256:0:2] - **How it was chosen:** Rayalaseema legislators wanted the capital in their region as promised by the **Sribagh Pact**; the communists favoured **Vijayawada**; in the vote of the legislators **Kurnool won by majority**, but only with the help of **five non-Andhra members** of the residual Madras Assembly (C. Rajagopalachari had suggested they should not vote).
[256:0:3]   - Counted without the five non-Andhra votes, Vijayawada-Guntur would have had the edge – hence some keys answer "None" to "capital chosen with the support of the majority".
[256:0:4] - **Nehru's condition (25 Mar 1953):** capital to be **inside Andhra**, not Madras; temporary capital to be decided **before 1 July 1953**; some offices could stay in Madras for a while.
[256:0:5] # High Court
[256:0:6] - **Andhra High Court:** set up at **Guntur** in **1954** (first Chief Justice **Koka Subba Rao**) – the compromise for coastal Andhra, while the capital went to Kurnool; not Kurnool, Vijayawada or Anantapur.

## SUB 1 | Wanchoo Committee and the Andhra State Act, 1953
[256:1:0] # Wanchoo Committee
[256:1:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [256:1:1.0] Appointed | December 1952, after Nehru's statement of 19 December 1952 (following Potti Sriramulu's death)
  [256:1:1.1] Member | Justice Kailas Nath (K.N.) Wanchoo, Chief Justice of the Rajasthan High Court – a single-member committee
  [256:1:1.2] Task | To report on the financial and other implications of forming Andhra State – identifying the problems of forming the state (boundaries, finances, division of assets, administration); not the capital of India, not the Nizam or peasants
  [256:1:1.3] Report | 7 February 1953
  [256:1:1.4] Centre's decisions on it | Announced by Nehru on 25 March 1953
[256:1:2] - **NextGen's dates differ:** NextGen gives "appointed 7 Feb 1953, report 25 Mar 1953"; the Lok Sabha record has the report presented on 7 Feb 1953 and the decisions announced on 25 Mar 1953.
[256:1:3] - **On Madras:** Wanchoo suggested the Andhra Government work temporarily from **Madras** (not Kurnool) for **3-5 years**; Nehru rejected it – "no question" of Madras as capital.
[256:1:4] - **Earlier:** **Partition Committee (1949-50)** under **Kumaraswamy Raja** on asset split; **JVP Committee (April 1949)** – Andhra only if Telugus gave up Madras.
[256:1:5] - **Special Officer:** **C.M. Trivedi**, later the first Governor.
[256:1:6] # Finances, assets and services
[256:1:7] - Assets and administration divided between Andhra and **residual Madras**; the Wanchoo apportionment of **assets and liabilities** was largely reproduced in the Act's schedule.
[256:1:8] - The 25 Mar 1953 statement left **services** and **financial implications** to be considered separately later.
[256:1:9] - New state "**administratively and economically orphaned**": no developed capital, no commercial centre.
[256:1:10] # Andhra State Act, 1953
[256:1:11] - **Act 30 of 1953**; **assent of the President: 14 September 1953**; in force **1 October 1953** – not May or June 1953.
[256:1:12] - **2014 parallels:** **Kamalanathan** committee – employees; **Sheela Bhide** committee – Schedule IX institutions; **Section 6** expert committee – new capital.
