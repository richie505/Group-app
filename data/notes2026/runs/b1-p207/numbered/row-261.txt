## SUB 0 | Andhra State 1953 – the First Linguistic State
[0:0] - **First linguistic state of independent India:** **Andhra State**, formed **1 October 1953**, the forerunner of **Andhra Pradesh** (exam keys write "Andhra Pradesh") – not Orissa, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan or Karnataka.
[0:1] - **Carved out of:** **Madras State** (Rayalaseema + Coastal Andhra; Madras city excluded) – not Hyderabad, Mysore or Bombay State.
[0:2] - **Date trap:** **1 October 1953** – not "April 1953" or 1956. **1 Nov 1956** is the birth of **Andhra Pradesh** (Andhra State + Telangana).
[0:3] - **Capital:** **Kurnool** (1953–56), chosen over Vijayawada by **80 to 79** in the Assembly (with five non-Andhra votes) – not Hyderabad, Madras, Kadapa, Nellore, Amaravati or Visakhapatnam. **Hyderabad** became capital only of Andhra Pradesh (1956).
[0:4] - **Potti Sriramulu:** fasted in **Madras** (Bulusu Sambamurthy's house) from **19 Oct 1952**; died **15 Dec 1952** after **58 days** (Laxmikanth: 56-day fast) → unrest → Nehru announced Andhra State on **19 Dec 1952**.

  [0:5.1] Capital | Kurnool | Hyderabad
  [0:5.2] First CM | Tanguturi Prakasam | N. Sanjiva Reddy
  [0:5.3] Basis | Andhra State Act after Sriramulu's death | SR Act 1956 (after SRC)

## SUB 2 | SR Act 1956 and the Formation of Andhra Pradesh
[2:0] - **Key dates:** SRC report (in **four parts**) **30 Sep 1955** → **Gentlemen's Agreement 20 Feb 1956** → **SR Act (Act 37 of 1956) enacted 31 August 1956** → in force, **Andhra Pradesh formed 1 November 1956**.
[2:1] - **Andhra Pradesh = Andhra State + Telugu districts of Hyderabad (Telangana)**, formed under **Section 3** of the Act – the **first state formed under the SR Act** (not Mysore); first CM **N. Sanjiva Reddy**, first Governor **C.M. Trivedi**. Date of AP's formation = **1956** (not 1953).
[2:2] - **Result:** **14 states + 6 Union Territories** from 1 Nov 1956 (SR Act + 7th Amendment); also bilingual Bombay, enlarged Madras, new Mysore, Kerala. **APPSC** was set up in **1956**.
[2:3] # Andhra–Telangana politics, 1955-56
[2:4] - **Andhra:** CM **Bezawada Gopala Reddy's** resolution (Telangana priority, population-based shares) passed **unanimously** (1955). **Communists** consistently backed integrated linguistic states and **Visalandhra**.
[2:5] - **Hyderabad Assembly vote** (174 members): **103 for** Visalandhra, **29 against**, **15 neutral**; CM **Burgula Ramakrishna Rao** worked to allay Telangana's fears on the High Command's direction.
[2:6] - **22 Oct 1955:** the two CMs agreed to merge at **Hyderabad House, New Delhi**. **Nov 1955:** Congress sub-committee **Nehru, G.B. Pant, Maulana Azad** (Azad kept opposing).
[2:7] - **Gentlemen's Agreement (20 Feb 1956, Hyderabad House):** **Telangana Regional Council**, Telangana surplus spent in Telangana, **Mulki rules** retained. The Centre thus **overrode the SRC's wait-till-1961 formula** by political agreement.
[2:8] - **Nehru (1956):** the merger a "**matrimonial alliance**" with a provision for **divorce** – later cited by separate-Telangana advocates.
[2:9] > Exam angle: 31 Aug 1956 (enacted) vs 1 Nov 1956 (in force); 14 + 6.

## SUB 3 | SRC Report 1955 – Principles and Hyderabad/Visalandhra
[3:0] - **Report:** submitted **30 Sep 1955**, made public **9 Oct 1955**; **1,52,250** memorandums; **four parts** – I Conditions of the Problem, II Factors Bearing on Reorganisation, III Proposals for Reorganisation, IV Administrative and Allied Matters (safeguards for linguistic groups).
[3:1] - **Principles:** language **important but not the only factor**; rejected "**one language, one state**" – same-language areas could form more than one state; other factors: **unity and security of India**, **administrative efficiency**, **financial and economic viability**, national plans.
[3:2] - **Recommended 16 states + 3 centrally administered territories**; abolish the **Part A/B/C** classification.
[3:3] TABLE head: Region | SRC recommendation
  [3:3.0] Vidarbha | Separate state
  [3:3.1] Bombay | Bilingual (Gujarati + Marathi)
  [3:3.2] Punjabi Suba | Not accepted
  [3:3.3] Telugu districts of Hyderabad | Separate Hyderabad State (para 386)
[3:4] # Hyderabad (Part III ch. V) and Andhra (ch. VI)
[3:5] - **Division of Hyderabad State:** most regions (Telugu, Kannada, Marathi) wanted it divided; **Kannada areas (Raichur, Gulbarga) → Mysore**; **Marathwada → Bombay** State.
[3:6] - **Para 368:** 45.4% Urdu speakers in Hyderabad–Secunderabad, but **no Union Territory** for the twin cities.
[3:7] - **Para 371 (for Visalandhra):** a state of ~32 million with water, power and minerals; "the **twin cities of Hyderabad and Secunderabad** are very well suited to be the capital of Vishalandhra" – this would **solve Andhra's permanent-capital problem** (not Kurnool).
[3:8] - **Para 386:** Telangana to function initially as **Hyderabad State**; merger **after the 1961 elections** if **two-thirds** of elected Telangana legislators agree.
[3:9] TABLE head: For Visalandhra (paras 369–374) | Against immediate merger
  [3:9.0] Large state, rich in minerals, water, power | Advanced Coastal Andhra would dominate services
  [3:9.1] Hyderabad solves Andhra's capital problem | Mulki rules and local jobs at risk
  [3:9.2] Unified Krishna–Godavari development | Telangana's revenue surplus diverted
[3:10] - **Reactions:** **Tenneti Viswanatham** called the report **self-contradictory**; the departure from para 386 became the core grievance of the Telangana movements (**1969, 2001–14**).
[3:11] > Exam angle: twin-city capital = **Hyderabad and Secunderabad**; "Kurnool as capital solves the problem" = wrong.

## SUB 5 | Linguistic States – Arguments and Background
[5:0] - **For** linguistic states: government and administration in the **people's language** – **administration becomes easier**; cultural identity. **Against**: fear for unity after Partition, regionalism, border and minority disputes (e.g. **Belgaum**, Maharashtra–Karnataka).

  [5:1.0] 1918 | B.N. Sharma moved the linguistic reorganisation resolution in the Imperial Legislative Council
  [5:1.1] 1928 | Tanguturi Prakasam – "Andhra Kesari", bared his chest to police guns in the Simon Commission boycott, Madras
  [5:1.2] 1930 | First Nizam Andhra Mahasabha, Jogipet (Suravaram Pratapa Reddy)
  [5:1.3] 1936 | Orissa = first province on linguistic lines
  [5:1.4] 1937 | Visalandhra idea starts in the Circar districts; Sri Bagh Pact (16 Nov) – irrigation priority, university centres, equal legislative seats for Rayalaseema (no Telangana merger)
  [5:1.5] 1949 | Visalandhra Mahasabha formed (Nov 1949, Vijayawada)

## SUB 6 | Seventh Amendment 1956, Zonal Councils and Later Changes
[6:0] - **7th Constitutional Amendment (1956):** abolished **Part A/B/C/D** → **14 states and 6 UTs**; abolished the **Rajpramukh**; **extended the jurisdiction of High Courts to Union Territories** and allowed a common High Court for two or more states (not the 1st, 3rd or 5th Amendment); inserted **Art 350A** (mother-tongue instruction at primary stage) and **Art 350B** (Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities).
[6:1] # Zonal Councils (SR Act Part III, Sections 15–22)
[6:2] - **Five** statutory councils – Northern, Central, Eastern, Western, Southern; purpose **inter-state cooperation**.
[6:3] - **Chairman:** a Union Minister nominated by the President – in practice the **Union Home Minister**; Vice-Chairman: a member-state CM by annual rotation. Members: **CM + two ministers** of each state; up to two from each UT.
[6:4] - **APRA 2014** amended **Section 15** of the SR Act to read "Andhra Pradesh and Telangana".
[6:5] # Later changes
[6:6] - **1 May 1960:** bilingual Bombay split into **Gujarat** and **Maharashtra**. **Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966** (after the **Shah Commission**): **Haryana** from 1 Nov 1966; hill areas to **Himachal Pradesh**.
