## SUB 0 | Cabinet Mission, 1946
[0:0] # Mission
[0:1] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [0:1.0] Sent by | PM Clement Attlee (Labour); decision 22 Jan, announced in the Commons 19 February 1946
  [0:1.1] Members ("Ministerial Trio") | Lord Pethick-Lawrence (Secretary of State for India, chairman / leader), Sir Stafford Cripps (President of the Board of Trade), A.V. Alexander (First Lord of the Admiralty)
  [0:1.2] Not members | John Simon (Simon Commission), William Wood, Wavell (Viceroy – worked with it), Attlee, Linlithgow
  [0:1.3] Arrived | 24 March 1946 (Delhi)
  [0:1.4] Plan announced | 16 May 1946 ("May 16 Plan")
  [0:1.5] Congress President in the talks | Maulana Abul Kalam Azad
[0:2] - Aim: machinery to frame the constitution + an **interim government**; the **last British attempt to keep India united** – its rejection = the last chance to avoid partition (UPSC 2002).
[0:3] - Quotation angle: "It made its proposals in **May**. It still wanted a **united India** … a **Federal Union** of British provinces" = **Cabinet Mission** (UPSC 1999).
[0:4] # Main points
[0:5] - **Rejected a full Pakistan** – non-Muslims would be **38% in the NW** and **48% in the NE** zone.
[0:6] - **Union of India** (British India + states) dealing only with **Defence, Foreign Affairs, Communications** – a loose **three-tier federation** ("Union of States"); **residuary powers with the provinces**.
[0:7] - **Three tiers:** Union → **groups (sections)** → provinces.
[0:8] TABLE head: Section | Provinces
  [0:8.0] A | Madras, Bombay, UP, Bihar, CP, Orissa (Hindu-majority)
  [0:8.1] B | Punjab, NWFP, Sindh
  [0:8.2] C | Bengal, Assam
[0:9] - **Constituent Assembly:** **389** = **296 British India** (292 provinces + 4 Chief Commissioner's provinces) + **93 princely states**; provincial members elected by the provincial assemblies by **PR-STV**, community-wise (General, Muslim, Sikh); state members **nominated by the rulers** – the Constituent Assembly was **set up under the Cabinet Mission Plan** (not Cripps, Mountbatten or the Independence Act).
[0:10] - **July 1946 elections:** **Congress 208, League 73, others 15**. First meeting **9 December 1946** (League absent).
[0:11] - Demand for a CA: **M.N. Roy 1934** → **Congress 1935** → accepted in the August Offer 1940.
[0:12] - **Interim cabinet of Indians** – yes; **constitution-framing right** – yes; **acceptance of Pakistan – NO** (UPSC 1996).
[0:13] - Not in the plan: enlarging the courts' powers or more Indians in the ICS (UPSC 2015: only "federal government").
[0:14] # Acceptance and withdrawal
[0:15] - **Grouping:** Congress – **optional**; League – **compulsory** (stepping stone to Pakistan).
[0:16] - League accepted **6 June**, Congress **24 June 1946**; **Nehru's statement of 10 July 1946** → League **withdrew its acceptance 29 July 1946** and called for "Direct Action" (16 August). The League later **joined the Interim Government (Oct 1946)** – true, but not the reason (A-R).
[0:17] - **Who backed the plan fully:** keys give **Gandhi** (he called it the best document the British could produce); quiz sites give Patel; Azad claimed it as his own triumph.

## SUB 2 | Wavell Plan and Simla Conference, 1945
[2:0] - **Lord Wavell** (Viceroy 1943-47); plan announced **14 June 1945**; **Simla (Shimla) Conference 25 June – 14 July 1945**.
[2:1] - **Proposals:** Executive Council reconstituted – **all members Indians except the Viceroy and the Commander-in-Chief**; all portfolios **except defence (War)** to Indians; **caste Hindus and Muslims equal** (parity); an interim arrangement under the 1935 Act; constitution talks after the war.
[2:2] - **UPSC 2008:** the body that suggested an all-Indian Executive Council = **Simla Conference (1945)** (strictly the War portfolio stayed with the C-in-C).
[2:3] - **A-R (UPSC 2007):** parity of Hindu and Muslim members – **true**; "Wavell thought it would avoid partition" – **false** (aim: break the deadlock).
[2:4] - **Failure:** **Jinnah insisted the League alone nominate all Muslim members**; Wavell **closed the conference on 14 July 1945** → League got a virtual veto. Congress led by **Azad**.
[2:5] # Wavell's 'Breakdown Plan' (1946)
[2:6] - Put to the Cabinet Mission in **May 1946** by **Viceroy Wavell**: phased British withdrawal to the Muslim provinces of the NW and NE, handing the rest to the Congress; superseded by the Cabinet Mission Plan.
[2:7] ~~ Sources: Spectrum (Ahir) p.487-488, p.507; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.62, p.75; old app notes (14 June, 25 June – 14 July)

## SUB 4 | Precursors – C.R. Formula and Desai-Liaquat Pact
[4:0] - **C.R. (Rajaji) Formula, 1944** – by **C. Rajagopalachari** (not Nehru, C.R. Das, V.P. Menon or Wavell) to end the Congress-League deadlock; tacit acceptance of Pakistan; basis of the **Gandhi-Jinnah talks, Bombay, 9–27 Sept 1944**.
[4:1]   - League to back independence and join a provisional government; after the war a **plebiscite of the whole population** of Muslim-majority NW and NE areas; if partition – joint agreements on defence, commerce, communications.
[4:2]   - **Not in it:** Jinnah's demands – only Muslims to vote, no common centre (he rejected it).
[4:3] - **"The Way Out"** – Rajaji's pamphlet after Quit India: **a solution for the constitutional deadlock** (UPSC 2010).
[4:4] - **Desai-Liaquat Pact (Jan 1945):** **Bhulabhai Desai** + Liaquat Ali Khan – interim government with **equal Congress and League nominees + 20% for minorities**.
[4:5] - League slogan **"Divide and Quit"** – **Karachi session, December 1943**.
[4:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum (Ahir) p.485-486; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.62, p.75; general knowledge, not checked ("The Way Out"); old app notes (Divide and Quit)
