## SUB 0 | First Telugu Papers – Satyadoota and Other Firsts
[0:0] # Satyadoota (Satya Duta) – the first Telugu journal
[0:1] - **Satyadoota:** the **first Telugu newspaper / journal / magazine**, started in **1835** at **Bellary** by the **Christian missionaries (Bellary Christian Society / Association)** to **propagate Christianity** – not Vruttanthini, Vartamana Tarangini, Desabhimani, Tattvabodhini or Andhra Patrika.
[0:2] - **Tattvabodhini (1864, Madras):** published by the **Madras Veda Samaj** for the propagation of Hinduism; supported widow remarriage.
[0:3] # Other "firsts" and "only"

  [0:4.0] First Telugu newspaper printed in Andhra | Hitavadi (1841), Edmund Park

## SUB 2 | Press and the Separate-Andhra Demand
[2:0] - **April 1911 – The Hindu:** a series of **six articles** by **Jonnavittula Gurunatham** (Guntur), "on the present position (condition) of the Telugu people", showed the under-representation of Telugus – not Krishna Patrika, Desabhimani or Indian Patriot.
[2:1]   - "Are Telugu people backward?" (15 Apr 1911): civil services **56 non-Andhras : 8 Andhras**; district judges **19 : 0**.
[2:2] - **Desabhimani (1911):** urged a separate Andhra political conference and Telugu papers writing on Andhra affairs; commented "**Unless there is a separate Government there is no protection for Telugu people's culture**".
[2:3] - **Krishna Patrika:** printed several articles on the need for a **separate Andhra province** (1912 debate with Desamata); 30 Nov 1912 – "strengthening the nation begins with the Andhra movement"; 19 Jul 1913 cartoon – Rayalaseema as an iceberg blocking the "Andhra Movement" ship.
[2:4] - **Janmabhumi (25 Aug 1920):** the Andhra movement strengthens national unity.
[2:5] - **Supporters of a separate Andhra:** Andhra Patrika, Krishna Patrika, Desabhimani, Desamata, Darbar; Andhra Patrika popularised the word "Andhra".
[2:6] - **Significance:** the press spread the national movement and built support for the separate-Andhra demand; newspapers also gave rise to reading rooms and libraries.
[2:7] > Exam angle: 1911 six articles = The Hindu; Desabhimani's "separate Government" remark.

## SUB 4 | Press Against the Government – Grievances and Movements
[4:0] - **Lokaranjani (October 1875):** complained that the ryots were **better off under the East India Company** than under the Crown.
[4:1] - **Home Rule:** **Desamata (Desha Mata)** – Chilakamarti's Rajahmundry paper – strongly supported the **Home Rule movement** in Andhra (APPSC G2 Mains 2017) – not Andhra Mata; **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao** was the Andhra Home Rule leader (secretary).
[4:2] TABLE head: Movement | Paper
  [4:2.0] Vandemataram / Swadeshi | Krishna Patrika, Swarajya, Desamata, Andhra Kesari (Chilukuri Veerabhadra Rao), Navayuga
  [4:2.1] Home Rule | Desamata
  [4:2.2] Peasant / anti-zamindari | Zamin Ryot
  [4:2.3] Socialist / Communist | Navasakthi / Prajasakthi
  [4:2.4] Women's cause | Hindu Sundari (1902), Sri Savitri (1904)
[4:3] - **Vernacular Press Act, 1878** ("Gagging Act") – repealed by **Lord Ripon, 1882**.
[4:4] - **Swarajya sedition case:** Gadicherla (with Bodi Narayana Rao) convicted under **Section 124A IPC** for an article of 26 March 1908; **3 years** – the first Telugu editor in the Presidency imprisoned in the Vandemataram movement.
[4:5] - **"Veerabali":** NextGen says **Krowidi Lingaraju** was jailed for two years for the editorial "Veerabali" (1920s); Madduri Annapurnayya for the playlet "Chichula Pidugu".
