## SUB 0 | Champaran Satyagraha (1917) – Gandhi's First Satyagraha in India
[0:0] # The Firsts
[0:1] - **Champaran (1917), Bihar** – Gandhi's **first satyagraha in India** and his **first successful** satyagraha after returning from South Africa (not Kheda, Ahmedabad, Bardoli, Dandi or Chauri Chaura).
[0:2] - Champaran district is in **Bihar** – not Gujarat, Bengal, UP or Punjab (APPSC 2019).
[0:3] - Year **1917** (not 1915 or 1918; NextGen's year list wrongly prints "1918 – Satyagrah at Champaran") → **2017 was its centenary** ("exactly 100 years ago" in a 2017 exam).
[0:4] - **First civil disobedience** in India: Gandhi **defied the order to leave the district** and preferred punishment – passive resistance / civil disobedience of an unjust order, "a novel method at that time".
[0:5] - Also Gandhi's **first peasant (farmers') movement** in India – before Kheda (1918) and Bardoli (1928).
[0:6] - Aim: to solve the problems of the **indigo cultivators** – security of tenure and freedom to grow crops of their choice; not Harijan rights or Hindu unity.
[0:7] - **Satyagraha** = Satya (truth) + Agraha (insistence); Gandhi (not Patel, Nehru or Vinoba) was the first to use it in the freedom struggle – evolved in **South Africa**, so it was **not formulated at Champaran**.
[0:8] - Champaran was **localised** – it was **not** Gandhi's first movement (South Africa came first) nor his first all-India one (**Rowlatt Satyagraha, 1919**). Its "first appearance as a leader of the masses" is accepted (NDA 2009); "second appearance" is wrong (NDA 2011).
[0:9] > Firsts: Champaran = first civil disobedience; Ahmedabad = first hunger strike; Kheda = first non-cooperation; Rowlatt = first all-India / mass strike.
[0:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 (src/indian-history/pmf-modern-history-04.txt) p.117-118, p.121; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.348-349, p.359; NextGen Rocket Indian History 02 p.54; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.42 (year error), p.72
## SUB 1 | Leaders and Persons of Champaran
[1:0] TABLE head: Person | Role
  [1:0.0] Rajkumar Shukla | Local indigo cultivator (peasant) who met Gandhi at the Lucknow Congress, Dec 1916 and invited / persuaded him – not Rajendra Prasad
  [1:0.1] Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad, Anugrah Narayan Sinha, Mazhar-ul-Haq | Bihar lawyers who joined Gandhi
  [1:0.2] J.B. Kripalani | A teacher (history professor, Muzaffarpur) – hence "Acharya"
  [1:0.3] Mahadev Desai, Narhari Parikh | Gandhi's associates (Desai joined in 1917) – not planters' leaders
[1:1] - Gandhi reached Champaran on **10 April 1917**; with his lawyers he **toured villages, recording the peasants' complaints** against forced indigo cultivation.
[1:2] - **Not connected:** **Jai Prakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia**, Patel, Nehru, Vinoba, Maniben Patel.
[1:3] - **"Mahatma Gandhi and Bihar, Some Reminiscences"** – by **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**.
[1:4] - **"Mahatma"**: popularly said to have been given by **Tagore** and, in exam keys, "added during the Champaran Satyagraha"; in fact the title was used for Gandhi **before 1917** (Pranjivan Mehta's letter 1909; Jetpur address 21 Jan 1915; Tagore, 6 March 1915).
[1:5] - Exam keys name **N.G. Ranga** as the one who "opposed" Champaran (UPPCS 2007) – no historical source found for this (Ranga, b. 1900, was 16 in 1917).
[1:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.118; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.349; NextGen Rocket Indian History 02 p.45, p.54; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.68; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._G._Ranga; old app notes (Kripalani, Prasad's book, 10 April)
## SUB 2 | Chronology of Gandhi's Early Movements and the Freedom Struggle
[2:0] TABLE head: Order | Event | Date
  [2:0.0] 1 | Swadeshi Movement (anti-partition of Bengal) | 1905
  [2:0.1] 2 | Home Rule Movement (Tilak at Poona, Annie Besant at Madras – not Gandhi; aim self-government) | 1916
  [2:0.2] 3 | Champaran Satyagraha | 1917
  [2:0.3] 4 | Ahmedabad Mill Strike | Feb–18 Mar 1918
  [2:0.4] 5 | Kheda Satyagraha | Mar–Jun 1918 (Nadiad meeting 22 Mar)
  [2:0.5] 6 | Rowlatt Satyagraha (hartal) | 6 April 1919
  [2:0.6] 7 | Jallianwala Bagh | 13 April 1919
  [2:0.7] 8 | Amritsar Congress session | Dec 1919
  [2:0.8] 9 | Hunter Committee report | 1920
  [2:0.9] 10 | Khilafat and Non-Cooperation (adopted at the Calcutta special session, Sept 1920) | 1919/1920–22
  [2:0.10] 11 | Moplah Revolt (Malabar) | 1921
  [2:0.11] 12 | Civil Disobedience | 1930
  [2:0.12] 13 | Quit India ("Do or Die", Bombay) – immediate end of British rule | 1942
[2:1] - **Order to remember:** Champaran → Ahmedabad → Kheda → Rowlatt (2, 4, 3, 1); Home Rule → NCM → CDM → QIM.
[2:2] - **Rowlatt Satyagraha** = Gandhi's **first venture in all-India politics**; after it **Gandhi emerged as an all-India (national) leader** – the incident that made him a true national leader.
[2:3] - **Non-Cooperation Movement** = the first **pan-India mass movement**; the Swadeshi Movement "remained confined to the upper and middle classes … failed to reach the masses".
[2:4] - Gandhi led NCM, CDM and QIM and worked for the "casteless" (Harijans) – all true.
[2:5] - **1917–1919** (Champaran to Rowlatt) established Gandhi as a **national leader** and Satyagraha as a method of struggle.
[2:6] - Champaran, Ahmedabad and Kheda all fell under Viceroy **Lord Chelmsford (1916–21)**.
[2:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.117, p.120, p.137-138; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.302, p.350, p.353, p.378; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.5
## SUB 3 | Tinkathia and the Indigo Problem
[3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:0.0] Tinkathia (Teen Kathiya) | Tenants bound to grow indigo on 3/20th of their land for European planters (not 3/19, 3/21, 3/18)
  [3:0.1] Sharahbeshi | Enhanced rent
  [3:0.2] Tawan | Lump-sum compensation demanded when indigo lost value
[3:1] - Place and crop: **Champaran – indigo (neel)**; not Gorakhpur-opium, Begusarai or Burdwan-paddy; not ryotwari or neelkari.
[3:2] - After the **Bengal Indigo Revolt (1859–60)** indigo shifted from Bengal to Bihar; Champaran ryots had also protested in 1866–68.
[3:3] - **German synthetic dyes** (late 19th c.) made indigo **unprofitable in the world market** – that, not peasant resistance or government control, is why indigo cultivation declined (UPSC 2020).
[3:4] - Planters then **raised rents and demanded illegal dues** (they did not reduce taxes) and forced sale at fixed prices – the planters oppressed the **tenants (ryots)**, not the zamindars.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.117; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.349; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.32, p.72; NextGen Rocket Indian History 01 p.83; old app notes (sharahbeshi, tawan)
## SUB 4 | Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)
[4:0] - Dispute between Ahmedabad **cotton-mill owners and workers** – **industrial workers** (the only one of the three early struggles involving the industrial working class).
[4:1] - Cause: the owners **withdrew the "plague bonus"** (of 1917); workers wanted a wage rise for wartime inflation – not hours of work; the owners (led by **Ambalal Sarabhai**) were Indian, not European.
[4:2] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [4:2.0] Workers' demand / owners' offer | 50% / 20%
  [4:2.1] Gandhi advised | 35% – he asked the workers to strike, non-violently
  [4:2.2] Gandhi's fast (15 March) | His first hunger strike – owners gave in on the 4th day, settlement 18 March
  [4:2.3] Result | Tribunal / arbitrator awarded 35%
  [4:2.4] Length | About 21 days (not 35)
[4:3] - **Anusuya Sarabhai** (Ambalal's sister) took up the workers' cause and brought Gandhi in; she founded the **Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (Majoor Mahajan Sangh), 25 Feb 1920**, with Gandhi as adviser.
[4:4] > First use of the hunger strike as a weapon = Ahmedabad – not NCM, Rowlatt or Bardoli.
[4:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.118-121; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.349-350; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.73; NextGen Rocket Indian History 02 p.54
## SUB 5 | Champaran Agrarian Committee and Its Results
[5:0] - The government appointed the **Champaran Agrarian (Enquiry) Committee, 1917**, with **Gandhi as a member** (representing the ryots) – by **agreement between the Government and Gandhiji**.
[5:1] - **Chairman: F.G. Sly**; members included D.J. Reid, L.C. Adami, Raja Harihar Prasad Narayan Singh, G. Rainy, M.E.L. Tainer – **Anugrah Narayan Sinha was not a member**; J.B. Kripalani, Rajendra Prasad and Rajkumar Shukla were not members either.
[5:2] - Recommended **abolition of Tinkathia** and the illegal exactions, and a **25% refund** of illegal recoveries → **Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918**; within a decade the planters left.
[5:3] - **Significance (UPSC 2018): joining of peasant unrest to India's national movement** – not "drastic decrease in plantation crops", all-India participation or Dalit/tribal involvement.
[5:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.118 (UPSC 2018 answer C); Spectrum Modern India pdf p.349, p.873; old app notes (F.G. Sly and members)
## SUB 6 | Kheda Satyagraha (1918)
[6:0] - **Kheda district, Gujarat**, 1918 – **first non-cooperation**.
[6:1] - Cause: **crop failure (drought)** – yield below one-fourth, so the Revenue Code entitled peasants to remission, but the administration **did not suspend revenue collection**; no Permanent Settlement was proposed (UPSC 2001: 1 only).
[6:2] - **Gujarat Sabha** (Gandhi its president) led the peasants' agitation; **Vallabhbhai Patel** (field leader), Indulal Yagnik, Mohanlal Pandya, Narahari Parikh; Gandhi the spiritual head – the start of the **Gandhi–Patel alliance**.
[6:3] - **Called off** when the government ordered revenue to be **collected only from those able to pay** (APPSC 2023) – revenue **suspended for the poor** and **confiscated property returned**.
[6:4] - Gandhi took part directly in Kheda, Rajkot and Non-Cooperation, but **not directly in Vaikom (1924–25)**.
[6:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.120-121; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.259 (Vaikom), p.350-351; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.69, p.72-73
## SUB 7 | Rowlatt Satyagraha and Jallianwala Bagh (1919)
[7:0] - **Rowlatt Act** (Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, March 1919): detention **without trial up to two years**; Gandhi's **Satyagraha Sabha, Bombay, 24 Feb 1919**; hartal **6 April 1919**.
[7:1] - **Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar, 13 April 1919 (Baisakhi)**: crowd protesting the arrest of **Kitchlew and Satyapal**; Brig.-Gen. **Dyer** blocked the only exit and opened fire on an **unarmed** crowd **without warning** – about **10 minutes, 1,650 rounds**; official toll **379 dead, about 1,100 wounded**.
[7:2] - Lt. Governor of Punjab **Michael O'Dwyer**; Viceroy **Chelmsford**; Hunter Committee condemned Dyer, **House of Lords** backed him.
[7:3] TABLE head: Protest | By
  [7:3.0] Gave up the title "Sir" (knighthood) | Rabindranath Tagore
  [7:3.1] Resigned from the Viceroy's Executive Council | C. Sankaran Nair
  [7:3.2] Returned Kaiser-i-Hind medal | Gandhi
[7:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.135-139; Spectrum Modern India pdf p.352-355, p.358; NextGen Rocket Indian History 02 p.92-93; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.73
## SUB 8 | Gandhi Before Champaran – Background
[8:0] - **Phoenix Settlement (1904, near Durban)**; **Tolstoy Farm (1910, near Johannesburg)**; journal **Indian Opinion**; later **Young India, Navajivan, Harijan** (**Mathrubhumi** was not his).
[8:1] - Political mentor **G.K. Gokhale**; autobiography **The Story of My Experiments with Truth**.
[8:2] - Returned **9 January 1915** → now **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas**; first major speech **BHU, February 1916**.
[8:3] - Ashrams: **Kochrab Satyagraha Ashram (1915)** → **Sabarmati (1917)** → **Sevagram, Wardha (1936)**.
[8:4] - **"Father of the Nation"** – first said by **Subhas Chandra Bose**.
[8:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.117; NextGen Rocket Indian History 02 p.54; old app notes (journals, ashrams, Pravasi Bharatiya Divas)
## SUB 9 | Gandhi's Arrest and the Great Trial (1922)
[9:0] - Arrested at his ashram on **10 March 1922**; tried for **sedition** at **Ahmedabad** ("The Great Trial", 18 March 1922) for three articles in **Young India**.
[9:1] - He **pleaded guilty**; Judge **Broomfield** gave him **six years**; printer **Shankerlal Banker** one year.
