## SUB 0 | Mountbatten Plan (3 June Plan), 1947 and Its Acceptance
[0:0] - **Lord Mountbatten** came as Viceroy in **March 1947** (took charge **24 March 1947**); announced his plan on **3 June 1947** – the country "free but not united". It became the **basis of partition** and the platform/formula for the **transfer of power** (sovereignty) to Indian hands.
[0:1] - **Plan Balkan / "Dickie Bird Plan"** (April–May 1947) was the brain-child of **Mountbatten**: power to the separate provinces, which (with princely states) could join India, Pakistan or stay separate; dropped after **Nehru** reacted violently (Balkanisation).
[0:2] - **United Bengal plan** (1947) of **Sarat Chandra Bose and H.S. Suhrawardy** – a sovereign undivided Bengal; rejected by **both Congress and the League**.
[0:3] - **V.P. Menon's** innovation: immediate transfer on the basis of **dominion status**, without waiting for a new constitution.
[0:4] # Provisions
[0:5] - **Punjab and Bengal** Assemblies to meet in **two groups** (Muslim-majority / Hindu-majority districts) and vote; a simple majority of either group for partition = partition → **both voted to divide** (Bengal 20 June, Punjab 23 June).
[0:6] - **Sindh** to decide on its own (joined Pakistan); Baluchistan also went to Pakistan.
[0:7] - **Referendums only in NWFP and Sylhet** (Assam): NWFP chose Pakistan (**Khudai Khidmatgar**/Provincial Congress boycotted); Sylhet joined East Bengal.
[0:8] - **Boundary Commission** for Punjab and Bengal; **two dominions and two Constituent Assemblies** if partition.
[0:9] - **Princely states:** independence ruled out by the plan – join **India or Pakistan** (no third option); Hyderabad's accession to Pakistan ruled out.
[0:10] - Common Governor-General permitted; transfer of power on the basis of the **GoI Act 1935**; armed forces divided.
[0:11] - Date **advanced from June 1948 to 15 August 1947**.
[0:12] # Why it was accepted
[0:13] - **Congress:** to **avoid large-scale communal riots** and civil war, avoid Balkanisation, take power quickly – it **never accepted the Two-Nation Theory** (A true, R false). Kripalani (April 1947): "rather than have a battle, we shall let them have their Pakistan".
[0:14] - **Muslim League:** because it provided for the **creation of Pakistan**.
[0:15] - Patel: "We would not have had one Pakistan but several"; Pant: accepting 3 June was the alternative to "committing suicide".
[0:16] # AICC, New Delhi, 14–15 June 1947
[0:17] TABLE head: Role | Person
  [0:17.0] Presided (Congress President) | Acharya J.B. Kripalani
  [0:17.1] Moved the partition resolution | Govind Ballabh Pant
  [0:17.2] Seconded the resolution | Maulana Abul Kalam Azad
  [0:17.3] Called it "surrender of nationalism in favour of communalism" | Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew (Punjab)
[0:18] > 3 June 1947 = Mountbatten Plan; referendums only NWFP + Sylhet; Pant moved, Azad seconded, Kripalani presided.

## SUB 2 | Indian Independence Act, 1947
[2:0] TABLE head: Stage | Date
  [2:0.0] Bill introduced in the House of Commons (by Attlee) | 4 July 1947
  [2:0.1] Passed by Parliament | July 1947
  [2:0.2] Royal assent (Act enacted) | 18 July 1947
  [2:0.3] Dominions created (appointed day) | Pakistan 14 August, India 15 August 1947
[2:1] - Gave **legal force to the Mountbatten Plan**; created **two independent dominions**, India and Pakistan (the basis of **partition**), with the right to leave the Commonwealth.
[2:2] - **Ended:** office of **Viceroy**, **Secretary of State for India**, title **"Emperor of India"**; paramountcy and treaties with princely states lapsed.
[2:3] - **Princely states:** free to **join India or Pakistan or remain independent** (Laxmikanth); 562/565 states – not forced to merge, not abolished.
[2:4] - **Governor-General** for each dominion appointed by the **British King** on the dominion cabinet's advice – a constitutional head; Britain to have **no control** over the dominions (so British sovereignty did not continue, though the sovereign appointed the GG).
[2:5] - **Constituent Assembly** made **sovereign** – could frame any constitution and repeal any British law, even this Act; it also became the dominion legislature and assumed power at **midnight of 14/15 August 1947**.
[2:6] - Interim law: **GoI Act 1935, as adapted**, till new constitutions.
[2:7] - Most distinctive feature: an **"Act without a constitution"** – transferred power but left constitution-making to Indians.
[2:8] - NOT in the Act: Federal Railway Authority, extension of franchise (GoI Act 1935); declaring India a Republic (1950).
[2:9] > Introduced 4 July → royal assent 18 July → effective 15 August 1947.
[2:10] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Indian Polity Part 1 p.34-35, p.38, p.41; PMF Modern History Part 5 p.101-102; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.64-66; Spectrum Modern India p.527-528
## SUB 3 | Attlee's Statement and Mountbatten's Arrival
[3:0] - **20 February 1947:** British PM **Clement Attlee** (Labour) declared in the **House of Commons** that British rule would end by **30 June 1948**, even without agreement among Indian parties.
[3:1] - Power could go to some central government or to **provincial governments** (hint of Balkanisation); **Mountbatten to replace Wavell**.
[3:2] - Attlee quotes (exam keys): "**Imperialism is dead as dodo**"; "The British Government is **not responsible** for the partition of India".
[3:3] - Mountbatten's instruction: explore unity and division till October 1947 – **keep India united if possible**; he came in **March 1947**, not 1945.
[3:4] - Assertion-reason: Britain left in 1947 because it had become **weak after the Second World War**.
[3:5] > Attlee deadline = 30 June 1948; Mountbatten advanced it to 15 August 1947.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.523-525; PMF Modern History Part 5 p.96-97, p.99; Laxmikanth Indian Polity Part 1 p.34; old app notes row 249
## SUB 4 | Direct Action Day and Gandhi's Last Years, 1946–48
[4:0] - League withdrew its acceptance of the **Cabinet Mission Plan** and on **29 July 1946** called for **"Direct Action"**; slogan "Larke Lenge Pakistan".
[4:1] - **Direct Action Day = 16 August 1946** – riots began in **Calcutta** ("Great Calcutta Killings"; Bengal premier **H.S. Suhrawardy** led the League's rally) → **Noakhali**, **Bihar**, UP, Punjab. The Congress did not endorse it.
[4:2] TABLE head: Date | Gandhi's whereabouts / event
  [4:2.0] Nov 1946 – March 1947 | Noakhali peace mission – he failed to stop the enmity and violence
  [4:2.1] March 1947 | Bihar – to calm anti-Muslim riots
  [4:2.2] 15 August 1947 | Beliaghata, Calcutta – fasting and praying; took part in no Independence Day celebration; absent from the Tryst with Destiny speech
  [4:2.3] 30 January 1948 | Assassinated by Nathuram Godse, New Delhi
[4:3] - Gandhi was **against partition**, but not formally in the Congress: he had left it in **1934** and was **not a member of the Congress** at independence.
[4:4] - **Mountbatten** called Gandhi the **"one-man boundary force"** (50,000 soldiers in Punjab vs one man in Bengal).
[4:5] - Gandhi suggested **winding up the Congress** after independence and turning it into a **Lok Sevak Sangh**.
[4:6] > 29 July 1946 = call; 16 August 1946 = Direct Action Day (Calcutta).
[4:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.93-94, p.104-106; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.7, p.63; old app notes row 249

## SUB 6 | Radcliffe Boundary Commissions
[6:0] - Two commissions – **Punjab** and **Bengal** – set up (30 June 1947) to **demarcate the India–Pakistan boundary**; each = **2 Congress + 2 League** nominees and a chairman.
[6:1] - **Sir Cyril Radcliffe**, a British lawyer with **no knowledge of Indian conditions**, chaired **both**; arrived 8 July, five weeks to decide.
[6:2] - The **Radcliffe Line** award was ready on **12 August** but published on **17 August 1947**, after independence, so Britain would escape the blame.
[6:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.100-101; Spectrum Modern India p.528; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.64
## SUB 7 | Consequences of Partition
[7:0] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [7:0.0] Migration | NCERT: about 80 lakh (8 million) people crossed the new border; Spectrum: nearly 60 lakh refugees came from Pakistan; PMF: some 15 million moved; 5–10 lakh killed
  [7:0.1] Rehabilitation | Ministry of Relief and Rehabilitation; refugee camps, evacuee property
  [7:0.2] Assets | Partition Council divided assets and liabilities; Pakistan took 17.5%
  [7:0.3] Kashmir | UN ceasefire 1 January 1949
  [7:0.4] Goa | Struggle against the Portuguese continued; ended with Operation Vijay (1961)
[7:1] - Criticism: the **hasty advanced date** and the **late Radcliffe award** worsened the violence.
[7:2] - World's first **Partition Museum** – **Town Hall, Amritsar** (2017).
