## SUB 0 | Santhal Hul (1855-56)
[0:0] # Background – Rajmahal Hills
[0:1] - The British **failed to subdue the Paharias** → invited the **Santhals** to settle and cultivate the Rajmahal hills (APPSC G1).
[0:2] - **By 1832** a large tract was demarcated as **Damin-i-Koh** – declared the **land of the Santhals**.
[0:3] - **Cleveland** (Augustus), Collector of **Bhagalpur** (1780s) – **Hill Assembly Plan**: Paharia hills under hereditary hill chiefs (sardars); fatally wounded by **Tilka Manjhi (1784)**.
[0:4] - Rajas of Maheshpur and Pakur leased Santhal villages to zamindars and moneylenders (**dikus**).
[0:5] # Course
[0:6] - Began at **Bhognadih (Bhaganidihi)** on **30 June 1855** (**Hul Divas**).
[0:7] - Leaders: brothers **Sidhu (Siddho) and Kanhu Murmu**, with **Chand and Bhairav** (so "both" pairs) and sisters **Phulo-Jhano**; about **30,000** proclaimed Santhal Raj; area between **Bhagalpur and Rajmahal** declared autonomous.
[0:8] - Weapons: **bows, arrows and axes** against British guns.
[0:9] - **Major Burrough(s)** defeated near **Bhagalpur**; **Gokko** – leader of **Godda**; **Mahajan Deen Dayal Rai** also figures. Santhals never ruled the Ganga valley near Muzaffarpur.
[0:10] - Against **zamindars, moneylenders, police** and the revenue system; crushed in **1856**, over **15,000** killed.
[0:11] - **"Kherwari Hul"** = a **Santhal** movement (Kherwar = Santhals' own name); Kharwar/Khairwar movement **1874** (Spectrum: 1870s).
[0:12] # After the Hul
[0:13] - **Santhal Parganas** carved out (from Bhagalpur and Birbhum) as a separate **non-regulation district** (Act XXXVII of 1855); **land transfer to non-Santhals barred** (UPSC 2018: both).
[0:14] - Santhal Parganas Settlement Regulation **1872**; Santhal Parganas Tenancy (Supplementary Provisions) Act **1949**.
[0:15] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 3 p.108-111; Spectrum (Ahir) p.189, p.191; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.33; old app notes (Hul Divas, Burrough, Gokko, Act XXXVII, 1872/1949)
## SUB 1 | Birsa Munda and the Ulgulan (1899-1900)
[1:0] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [1:0.0] Born | 15 November 1875 – now Janjatiya Gaurav Divas
  [1:0.1] Titles | Dharti Aba ("Father of the Earth"); "Birsa Bhagwan"
  [1:0.2] 1895 | Declared himself a prophet (Chalkad); arrested 24 Aug 1895 – start of his movement
  [1:0.3] Faith | Birsait – one God; against liquor, witchcraft, sorcery, animal sacrifice, spirit worship
  [1:0.4] Golden age | Spoke of a past satyug (age of truth) when Mundas lived well
  [1:0.5] Cause | Destruction of Khuntkatti (common landholding) by jagirdars, thikadars, moneylenders, missionaries
  [1:0.6] Revolt | Ulgulan ("Great Tumult") proclaimed Christmas Eve 1899; about 6,000 Mundas; aim Munda Raj / Abua Raj (self-rule)
  [1:0.7] Area | Ranchi (south of Ranchi), Chotanagpur – present Jharkhand
  [1:0.8] Capture | Jamkopai forest, Chakradharpur, 3 February 1900
  [1:0.9] Death | Ranchi jail, 9 June 1900 (cholera)
[1:1] - **Munda revolt year:** Ulgulan = **1899** (to 1900); Birsa's movement began **1895** – no Munda revolt in 1890 (a printed key).
[1:2] - **Birsa "in favour of" Jharkhand/Chhattisgarh/Uttaranchal?** – **None**: he sought Munda Raj; those states date from 2000.
[1:3] - **Chotanagpur Tenancy Act, 1908** – barred land transfer to non-tribals, recognised Khuntkatti (came **after** the Ulgulan; Tenancy Act 1903 first).
[1:4] - Bhagwan Birsa Munda Memorial Udyan cum Freedom Fighters Museum, **Ranchi (2021)**.

## SUB 4 | Western India – Bhil, Ramosi, Gadkari
[4:0] - **Bhils** (Western Ghats, **Khandesh**, Maharashtra) – revolted **1817-19**; **Govind Giri (Guru)** – **Samp Sabha (1883)**, led Bhils against **bonded labour**; **Bhagat / Lasodia movement** (Mewar, Vagad) with **Surji Bhagat**.
[4:1] - **Mangarh massacre (17 Nov 1913)** – "**Adivasi Jallianwala**"; **Mangarh Dham** a National Monument (**2022**).
[4:2] - **Motilal Tejawat** – **Eki movement (1921-22), Mewar** (second Bhil agitation).
[4:3] - **Ramosis** – hill tribes of the **Western Ghats**; rose after the **fall of the Peshwa** under **Chittur Singh (1822)** around **Satara**, again 1825-26 under **Umaji Naik** and **Bapu Trimbakji Sawant**. **Narayan Rao** is NOT linked (APPSC trap).
[4:4] - **Gadkari revolt (1844)** – **Kolhapur**: disbanded hereditary fort garrisons.

## SUB 5 | Causes, Terms and Colonial Measures
[5:0] - Common factor (UPSC 2011): **complete disruption of the old agrarian order** of the tribes.
[5:1] - **Indian Forest Act, 1878** – forests became **state property** (reserved, protected, unclassified); curbs on podu.
[5:2] - **Paikas** lost rent-free lands after the **1803** conquest of Odisha.
[5:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [5:3.0] Dikus | Outsiders – moneylenders, zamindars, traders

  [5:3.2] Khuntkatti | Joint landholding of the Mundas (not a reform of Birsa)

  [5:3.4] Abua Raj | Self-rule
  [5:3.5] Podu | Shifting cultivation
  [5:3.6] Meriah | Human sacrifice (Khonds)
