## SUB 0 | Lahore (Pakistan) Resolution, 1940
[0:0] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [0:0.0] Session | All-India Muslim League, Lahore, 22-24 March 1940 (Minto/Iqbal Park)
  [0:0.1] Adopted | 23 March 1940 – soon called the Pakistan Resolution
  [0:0.2] Presided | M.A. Jinnah – leader of the movement for Pakistan
  [0:0.3] Drafted | Sikandar Hayat Khan (Unionist Premier of Punjab)
  [0:0.4] Moved | A.K. Fazlul Huq, Premier of Bengal
  [0:0.5] Seconded | Chaudhry Khaliquzzaman (UP) and others
  [0:0.6] Demand | Contiguous Muslim-majority areas (NW and East) grouped into independent states, units autonomous and sovereign; safeguards elsewhere – word "Pakistan" not used
[0:1] - Gave effect to the **two-nation theory**; the League's formal demand for division.
[0:2] - **Pakistan Day** first observed by the League on **23 March 1943**; Pakistan's National Day is 23 March.
[0:3] - Not to confuse: **1930 Allahabad** = Iqbal's address; **Aga Khan / Nawab Salimullah** = 1906 founding; **Liaquat Ali Khan** = first PM of Pakistan; **Rahmat Ali** = coined the name; Iqbal died 1938.
[0:4] - Jinnah presided over the session of the **All-India Muslim League** (no "Hindu-Muslim League"); he stayed out of the Non-Cooperation Movement.
[0:5] - Sikandar later (1941) opposed a "Muslim Raj here and Hindu Raj elsewhere".
[0:6] - Reply in the south: **Periyar** demanded **Dravidistan** (Erode Conference, 1940).
[0:7] > President – Drafter – Mover = **Jinnah – Sikandar Hayat Khan – Fazlul Huq**. Khaliquzzaman only seconded (a printed key wrongly gives him as mover).
[0:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.18, p.50; Spectrum pdf p.485, p.520; NextGen Rocket Indian History 02 p.88; en.wikipedia.org Lahore_Resolution; old app notes (Periyar)
## SUB 1 | Two-nation Theory – Sir Syed, Iqbal, Rahmat Ali
[1:0] TABLE head: Year | Milestone
  [1:0.0] 1880s | Sir Syed Ahmad Khan – first said Hindu and Muslim political interests were different and even divergent (foundation of Muslim communalism); used by Dufferin (1887-88) with Raja Shiva Prasad of Benares; United Indian Patriotic Association (1888)
  [1:0.1] 1906 | Shimla Deputation (Aga Khan) – separate electorates; Muslim League founded (Aga Khan, Salimullah)
  [1:0.2] 1930 | Muhammad Iqbal, presidential address, League's Allahabad session – a "North-West Indian Muslim state" (Punjab, NWFP, Sind, Baluchistan) – first definite expression of a separate Muslim homeland
  [1:0.3] 1933 | Choudhry Rahmat Ali, Cambridge student – coined "Pakistan" in the pamphlet "Now or Never" (also 1935)
  [1:0.4] 1940 | Lahore Resolution – theory formally set out
  [1:0.5] 1946 | Direct Action Day, 16 August – Calcutta killings
[1:1] - **Iqbal:** wrote the patriotic "**Sare Jahan Se Achha**" (1904), later backed Muslim separatism.
[1:2] - Iqbal to Nehru (Lahore, Jan 1938, recorded in *The Discovery of India*): "**He (Jinnah) is a politician and you are a patriot**."
[1:3] - Acronym PAKSTAN: **P**unjab, **A**fghan (NWFP), **K**ashmir, **S**ind, Baluchis**tan**.
[1:4] - **Jinnah** began as an advocate of Hindu-Muslim unity; after 1937 he championed the two-nation theory.
[1:5] > First to articulate = **Sir Syed**; first proposal of a Muslim state = **Iqbal 1930**; name Pakistan / idea of a separate nation-state = **Rahmat Ali 1933**.
[1:6] TABLE head: Book / quote | Author
  [1:6.0] Now or Never | Rahmat Ali
  [1:6.1] Pakistan or the Partition of India | B.R. Ambedkar
  [1:6.2] The Man Who Divided India | Rafiq Zakaria
  [1:6.3] The Indian Musalmans (1871) – Muslims "the greatest bulwark of British power" | W.W. Hunter
[1:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.50-51, p.106; PMF Part 4 p.82; Spectrum pdf p.519; nation.com.pk (Iqbal remark, 2017); old app notes (Dufferin, books)

[2:0] TABLE head: Order | Report | Author / year
  [2:0.0] 1 | Pirpur Report | Raja Syed Mohammad Mehdi of Pirpur, 1938 (Nov)
  [2:0.1] 2 | Sharif (Shareef) Report | S.M. Sharif, on Bihar, March 1939

[2:3] - "**Divide and Quit**" – **Jinnah's** League slogan (Karachi session, 1943-44): divide India, then leave.

## SUB 4 | Separate Electorates, 1909-1935
[4:0] TABLE head: Act / event | Step
  [4:0.0] Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto) | First separate electorates for Muslims + weightage; legalised communalism; Minto = "Father of Communal Electorate"
  [4:0.1] Lucknow Pact 1916 | Congress accepted separate electorates + weightage + communal veto (three-fourths rule)
  [4:0.2] GoI Act 1919 | Extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans
  [4:0.3] Communal Award 1932 | Extended to depressed classes; Poona Pact replaced it with reserved seats in joint electorates
  [4:0.4] GoI Act 1935 | Further to SCs, women, labour; Muslims 33% of Federal Legislature seats
[4:1] - Separate electorates **institutionalised religion in representation** – the seed of the two-nation theory; after 1935 restricted franchise + separate electorates bred separatism.
[4:2] - Jinnah's Point 5 (1929): separate electorates to continue.
[4:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.7, p.18, p.106; Laxmikanth 8th ed. p.32-33; old app notes (Minto title, 33%, three-fourths rule)
