=== NEW NOTES row 239
## SUB 0 | Gandhi-Irwin Pact (Delhi Pact), 1931
[0:0] - **Signed 5 March 1931** at Delhi (talks began 14 February 1931); the CWC, released unconditionally on 25 January 1931, authorised Gandhi to talk to Viceroy **Lord Irwin**.
[0:1] - **Mediators: Tej Bahadur Sapru and M.R. Jayakar** (Liberals; with V.S. Srinivasa Sastri) – not Motilal Nehru, Annie Besant or Chintamani.
[0:2] - **Main purpose:** to bring the **Congress into the Round Table Conference** – the Congress **suspended the CDM** and agreed to attend the **Second RTC**.
[0:3] TABLE head: Irwin agreed | Irwin refused | Congress agreed
  [0:3.0] Release of political prisoners not convicted of violence | Public inquiry into police excesses | Suspend the CDM
  [0:3.1] Remission of fines not yet collected | Commutation of the death sentences of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru | Attend the Second RTC
  [0:3.2] Return of confiscated lands not yet sold to third parties (lands already sold were NOT returned) | – | Discuss federation, Indian responsibility and reservations and safeguards (defence, external affairs, minorities, financial credit of India) – Clause 2
  [0:3.3] Salt-making by coastal villagers for own use (not sale); peaceful picketing; withdrawal of ordinances | – | –
[0:4] - **Not in the Pact:** the **18% reservation for depressed classes** (that is the **Poona Pact**, 1932, in the Central Legislature).
[0:5] - Irwin's biographer **Alan Campbell Johnson** called Gandhi's gains **"Consolation Prizes"** (UPPSC revised key; first key had said S.C. Bose).
[0:6] - **Sarojini Naidu** called Gandhi and Irwin the **"Two Mahatmas"**.
[0:7] - The Pact placed the Congress **on an equal footing** with the government; youth and the left opposed it because Bhagat Singh was not saved.
[0:8] > Delhi Pact = 5 March 1931; Sapru–Jayakar mediated; CDM suspended → Congress to RTC-2; police inquiry and commutation refused.
[0:9] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.196-197, p.201; Spectrum Modern India ch. CDM and RTCs p.411-412 (book p.379-380); NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.58; old app notes row 239
## SUB 1 | Karachi Session, March 1931 – Fundamental Rights Resolution
[1:0] - **29-31 March 1931**, special session to **endorse the Gandhi-Irwin Pact**; **President: Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**.
[1:1] - **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were executed on 23 March 1931** (Lahore Conspiracy Case), six days before the session; Gandhi met **black-flag demonstrations**.
[1:2] - Congress **admired their bravery** but **disapproved of political violence**; **reiterated Purna Swaraj** (= **Complete Independence**); chose Gandhi for RTC-2.
[1:3] - **Resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme drafted by Jawaharlal Nehru** (with **M.N. Roy**'s help; revised by Gandhi) – not Ambedkar, Rajendra Prasad or Patel.
[1:4] TABLE head: Fundamental Rights | Economic Programme
  [1:4.0] Free speech, press, assembly, association | Big cuts in rent and revenue; no rent on uneconomic holdings
  [1:4.1] Equality before law irrespective of caste, creed, sex | Relief from agricultural debt; control of usury
  [1:4.2] Universal adult franchise | Living wage, limited hours, protection of women workers; unions
  [1:4.3] Religious neutrality of the State; free primary education | State ownership of key industries, mines, transport
[1:5] - First time the Congress spelt out what **swaraj meant for the masses**; it **foreshadowed Parts III and IV** of the Constitution.
[1:6] > Karachi 1931: Patel presided, Nehru drafted.
[1:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.164, p.199-200; Spectrum Modern India p.413-414 (book p.381-382); old app notes row 239
## SUB 2 | Round Table Conferences, 1930-32
[2:0] TABLE head: RTC | Dates | Congress | Key facts
  [2:0.0] First | 12 Nov 1930 – 19 Jan 1931, London | Boycotted (CDM on; Lahore 1929 saw no benefit) | Opened by King George V, chaired by Ramsay MacDonald; Ambedkar, Sapru, Jinnah attended; federation agreed in principle
  [2:0.1] Second | 7 Sept – 1 Dec 1931 | Gandhi = sole representative (only RTC he attended) | Viceroy now Willingdon; deadlock on separate electorates (Minorities' Pact); MacDonald threatened a unilateral Communal Award
  [2:0.2] Third | 17 Nov – 24 Dec 1932 | Absent (also the Labour Party) | Only 46 delegates → White Paper (March 1933) → Joint Select Committee → Government of India Act 1935
[2:1] - **Second RTC attendees:** Gandhi, **Sarojini Naidu** (Indian women), **Madan Mohan Malaviya**, Iqbal, Ambedkar, Jinnah, G.D. Birla; **Dr Rajendra Prasad did NOT attend** (nor Nehru).
[2:2] - **V.V. Jogayya Pantulu** went to London to assist Gandhi and handed him the **separate-Andhra resolution** (not Konda Venkatappayya).
[2:3] - Ambedkar, Jayakar, Aga Khan, Sapru, N.M. Joshi attended **all three**.
[2:4] - **Lord Willingdon** (Viceroy 1931-36): "It's a **beautiful world if it wasn't for Gandhi**" (private letter to his sister).
[2:5] > RTC-2 = 1931, Gandhi alone for Congress; Rajendra Prasad absent; Willingdon = "beautiful world" quote.
[2:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.414-419 (book p.382-387); PMF Modern History Part 4 p.200-204; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.59; old app notes row 239
## SUB 3 | Communal Award and Poona Pact, 1932
[3:0] - **Communal Award: announced 16 August 1932 by British PM Ramsay MacDonald** (not Baldwin, Chamberlain or Churchill), after RTC-2 deadlock; based on the Lothian (Franchise) Committee.
[3:1]   - Separate electorates for Muslims, Sikhs, Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans, **depressed classes** (declared a minority, **71 seats**, **double vote** for 20 years), even Marathas (7 seats, Bombay).
[3:2]   - Congress **neither accepted nor rejected** it.
[3:3] - **Gandhi's fast unto death** in **Yerwada Jail, Poona**, from **20 September 1932**.
[3:4] - **Poona Pact: 24 September 1932** – signed by **Ambedkar** (depressed classes) and **Madan Mohan Malaviya** for caste Hindus; Gandhi broke fast on 26 September.
[3:5]   - **Joint electorate** with reserved seats: **148** in provincial legislatures (vs 71), **18%** of the Central Legislature seats.
[3:6]   - Root of reserved seats – **Articles 330 and 332**.
[3:7] - **Harijan Sevak Sangh** (first the Anti-Untouchability League), 30 September 1932.
[3:8] TABLE head: Person | Event
  [3:8.0] MacDonald | Communal Award
  [3:8.1] Linlithgow | August Offer
  [3:8.2] Dalhousie | Doctrine of Lapse
  [3:8.3] Chelmsford | Dyarchy (1919 Act)
[3:9] > Award (16 Aug) → Poona Pact (24 Sept) → RTC-3 (17 Nov 1932).
[3:10] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.421-424 (book p.389-392); PMF Modern History Part 4 p.205-207; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.59 (gives 25 Sept; Spectrum and PMF: 24 Sept); old app notes row 239
## SUB 4 | Resumption and Withdrawal of the CDM, 1932-34
[4:0] TABLE head: Date | Event
  [4:0.0] 29 Dec 1931 | CWC decides to resume CDM after RTC-2 failure
  [4:0.1] 4 Jan 1932 | Gandhi arrested; Congress banned; "civil martial law" under Willingdon
  [4:0.2] May 1933 | Mass movement suspended (individual satyagraha from 14 July 1933)
  [4:0.3] April 1934 | CDM formally withdrawn by Gandhi
  [4:0.4] 1934 | Congress returns to council entry, contests Central Assembly elections
[4:1] - After the CDM Gandhi turned to **constructive programmes**, above all **service to Harijans** – not violence or mere negotiation.
[4:2] > Withdrawn April 1934 (not 1932/1933/1935).
[4:3] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.420-421 (book p.388-389); PMF Modern History Part 4 p.197-198; old app notes row 239
