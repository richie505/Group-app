## SUB 0 | Deltas and Estuaries
[0:0] TABLE head: Basis | Delta | Estuary
  [0:0.0] Process | Deposition dominates | Erosion / tidal flushing dominates
  [0:0.1] Mouth | River splits into many distributaries | Single funnel-shaped (drowned) mouth with free connection to the sea
  [0:0.2] Example | Ganga-Brahmaputra, Godavari, Krishna, Mahanadi | Narmada, Tapi (Tapti), Western Ghats rivers; world's largest = St. Lawrence
[0:1] - **Conditions for a delta:** heavy sediment load, slow flow, shallow sea, **weak tides and currents** (strong tides remove sediment).
[0:2] - **West-flowing Deccan rivers** form **estuaries, not deltas**: **steep gradient** (fast flow), hard rock, short courses, little silt; the Narmada and Tapi also flow through rift valleys.
[0:3] - An estuary is an **ecotone** (river–sea transition), among the most productive habitats.
[0:4] # Largest delta
[0:5] - **World's largest (greatest) delta:** **Ganga-Brahmaputra(-Meghna) = Sundarbans / Bengal delta** (~1 lakh sq km), **arcuate**; about **two-thirds in Bangladesh**; the **world's largest mangrove forest**; also the largest delta of the Indian subcontinent.
[0:6] - In Bangladesh the **Ganga = Padma**; the **Brahmaputra = Jamuna** (so "Ganga is called Jamuna in Bangladesh" is false).
[0:7] - **Moribund delta:** the old western part of the **Bengal delta** (Murshidabad–Nadia) whose distributaries are dead/inactive; the mature and active deltas lie further south-east.
[0:8] TABLE head: Delta type | Shape / condition | Example
  [0:8.0] Arcuate (fan) | Arc-shaped; most common; sediment denser than sea water | Nile, Ganga
  [0:8.1] Bird's foot | Long branching distributaries; sediment lighter, carried far | Mississippi (Gulf of Mexico)
  [0:8.2] Cuspate | Tooth-shaped; densities equal | Tiber (Italy)
  [0:8.3] Estuarine | Drowned funnel mouth | Narmada, Tapi
[0:9] - **Sinking deltas (Apr 2026):** 7 deltas (Ganga-Brahmaputra, Nile, Mekong, Yangtze, Amazon, Irrawaddy, Mississippi) ≈ **57%** of sinking delta area; causes **groundwater extraction + less silt** (app CA).
[0:10] # AP coast
[0:11] - Coastline ~974 km; built by the **Godavari** (delta = **Konaseema**) and **Krishna** deltas; minor deltas: **Vamsadhara** (Srikakulam), **Penna** (Nellore).
[0:12] - **Kolleru**: freshwater lake between the Godavari and Krishna deltas; WLS **308.55 sq km**. **Pulicat**: saltwater lagoon on the **AP–Tamil Nadu border**.
[0:13] > Asked again and again: largest delta = Ganga-Brahmaputra (Sundarbans); bird's foot = Mississippi; Tapti forms an estuary; west-flowing rivers – steep gradient.

## SUB 1 | River Course, Erosional Landforms, Gorge and Canyon
[1:0] TABLE head: Course (stage) | Dominant work | Typical features
  [1:0.0] Upper (youth) | Vertical erosion, steep gradient | V-shaped valleys, gorges, canyons, waterfalls, rapids, potholes
  [1:0.1] Middle (maturity) | Lateral erosion + transport | Wider valleys, meanders, floodplains begin
  [1:0.2] Lower (old age) | Deposition, gentle gradient | Broad floodplains, levees, oxbow lakes, braided channels, deltas/estuaries
[1:1] - **Order downstream (CDS 2014):** falls → meanders → oxbow lake → delta (**II, I, IV, III**). Middle course is not vertical erosion alone.
[1:2] TABLE head: Landform | Formation
  [1:2.0] V-shaped valley | Rills → gullies → valleys by vertical erosion
  [1:2.1] Waterfall | Drop over a resistant rock band or fault step
  [1:2.2] Potholes | Eddy-whirled pebbles drill holes; large one below a waterfall = plunge pool
  [1:2.3] Incised (entrenched) meanders | Uplift rejuvenates the river; meanders cut deep into hard rock
  [1:2.4] River terraces | Stream cuts down into its own floodplain (erosional); paired = same height both banks; unpaired = one bank
[1:3] - River-profile terms:
[1:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:4.0] Graded profile | Smooth concave long profile; erosion and deposition roughly balanced
  [1:4.1] Base level | Lowest level a river can erode to; sea level = ultimate; lakes and dams = local
  [1:4.2] Stream capture (river piracy) | Headward erosion breaches a divide and takes a neighbour's upper waters
  [1:4.3] Peneplain | Near-featureless gentle plain worn by rivers almost to base level (W.M. Davis)
[1:5] - Gorge vs canyon:
[1:6] TABLE head: Basis | Gorge | Canyon
  [1:6.0] Nature | Deep, narrow, steep-sided valley | Very deeply / widely eroded gorge – a variant of a gorge
  [1:6.1] Width | Almost same at top and bottom | Wider at the top, step-like sides
  [1:6.2] Rock | Hard rocks | Horizontally bedded sedimentary rocks
  [1:6.3] Example | Chisapani (Karnali, Nepal) | Grand Canyon (Colorado River, Arizona)
[1:7] - Both gorges and canyons are **V-shaped valleys** (true); "gorges are extended forms of canyons" is **wrong** (APPSC G1).
[1:8] - **Grand Canyon**: a gorge carved by the **Colorado** River in **Arizona, USA**, ~446 km long, over 1.8 km deep.
[1:9] - **Gandikota canyon**: **Pennar (Penna) River**, YSR Kadapa, AP – "**Grand Canyon of India**" (UPSC 2022).
[1:10] - **Dihang gorge**: Brahmaputra enters India (Arunachal); Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon near **Namcha Barwa**.

## SUB 2 | Drainage Patterns
[2:0] TABLE head: Pattern | Control | Example
  [2:0.0] Dendritic | Uniform rock, no structural control; tree-like, tributaries join at acute angles | Ganga plains, Assam Valley, Indus, Mahanadi, Godavari – most rivers of South India
  [2:0.1] Trellis | Folded rocks; primary tributaries parallel, secondary ones join at right angles | Appalachians, Singhbhum, upper Himalayan rivers
  [2:0.2] Radial | Streams flow outward from a central high | Sri Lanka central highlands; Amarkantak (Narmada, Son, Mahanadi)
  [2:0.3] Centripetal | Streams flow into a central lake/depression | Loktak Lake, Manipur
  [2:0.4] Rectangular | Joints and faults – main stream bends at right angles and tributaries join at right angles | Joint-controlled plateaus
  [2:0.5] Annular | Rings on an eroded dome | Structural domes
[2:1] - "Branches of a tree" = **dendritic**, not radial.
[2:2] - **Antecedent drainage:** river older than the uplift, keeps its course by cutting gorges as the land rises – **most Himalayan rivers** (Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra).
[2:3] - Genetic stream types: consequent (follows initial slope), subsequent (along weak strata), obsequent (opposite the dip), **insequent** – **no apparent control**, no clear reason for its course (dendritic on uniform rock).
[2:4] ~~ Sources: NCERT Class 11 India: Physical Environment ch.3 (OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Geography 04 p.2-3, drainage patterns); PMF Indian Physical Geography 02 p.14-20; NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.9-12; app notes 4:12 sub 9
## SUB 3 | Plains and River-plain Terms – Doab, Prayag, Bhabar, Terai, Lacustrine Plains
[3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:0.0] Doab | Land between two confluent rivers (Persian do-ab = two waters); Ganga-Yamuna doab, Bist Doab (Beas–Sutlej)
  [3:0.1] Prayag | A sacred river confluence
  [3:0.2] Bhabar | Porous belt of pebbles and boulders, 8–16 km wide, along the Shiwalik foothills at the break of slope – a piedmont deposit; streams sink underground
  [3:0.3] Terai | Wet, marshy, forested belt south of the Bhabar (15–30 km) where streams re-emerge
  [3:0.4] Bhangar | Older alluvium above flood level; contains kankar
  [3:0.5] Khadar | Newer floodplain alluvium, renewed every flood; more fertile
[3:1] - **Panch Prayag** on the Alaknanda (downstream): **Vishnuprayag** (Dhauliganga), **Nandprayag** (Nandakini), **Karnaprayag** (Pindar), **Rudraprayag** (Mandakini), **Devprayag** (**Bhagirathi + Alaknanda → Ganga**).
[3:2] TABLE head: Plain type | Formed by | Example
  [3:2.0] Alluvial plain | River deposition | Indo-Gangetic plain (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra)
  [3:2.1] Flood plain | Fine silt dropped when the river overflows | Along river valleys
  [3:2.2] Lacustrine plain | Lake filled with sediment, then drained | Kashmir Valley (Karewas), Imphal Basin (Manipur)
  [3:2.3] Peneplain | Long river erosion to near base level | Peninsular plateau (rejuvenated)
[3:3] - **Karewas** of Kashmir = **lacustrine (lake) deposits** (fluvio-glacial clays, sands) – saffron land.
[3:4] - **Ganga Plain** = a **geosyncline** (fore-deep trough filled with sediment) in the classical description. **Patland** (Chotanagpur, central India) = **lava-capped mesas**.

## SUB 4 | Depositional Landforms, Ravines and Badlands
[4:0] TABLE head: Landform | How it forms
  [4:0.0] Alluvial fan | Gently sloping, fan-shaped coarse alluvium dropped by (often braided) streams at the break of slope (hill foot)
  [4:0.1] Natural levee | Raised sandy banks along the channel
  [4:0.2] Floodplain | Fine alluvium dropped when the river overflows
  [4:0.3] Braided channel | Sand bars split the channel into many strands with riverine islands
  [4:0.4] Oxbow lake | Meander loop cut off; stagnant water left
[4:1] - Meander: **outer bend concave, erosional** (cliff); **inner slip-off side convex, depositional**.
[4:2] - **APPSC G1 2023:** depositional work of Himalayan rivers = **braided channels, alluvial fans, natural levees**; **river terraces** are products of vertical **erosion** (NCERT) – so II, III and IV only.
[4:3] - Bajada = coalesced alluvial fans of deserts; diara = riverine sand islands of Bihar.
[4:4] - **Gully erosion → ravines**: rills deepen into gullies; dense gully network = **badland topography** – **Chambal ravines** (central India); **Chos** of Hoshiarpur (Punjab).
[4:5] ~~ Sources: NCERT Class 11 Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.7 (OnlyIAS NCERT Physical Geography 06 p.2-3, terraces then depositional landforms); NextGen Rocket General Geography p.37, p.40 (alluvial fan, levee; Chambal badland); app notes 4:12 sub 6-7
