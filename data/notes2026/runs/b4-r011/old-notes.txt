=== NOTES 4:11 | Weathering, Mass Wasting and Soil Formation; Denudation and the Cycle of Erosion
## SUB 0 | Denudation
[11:0:0] - Order: **weathering** (first stage) → **mass wasting / erosion** → **transportation** → **deposition**.
[11:0:1] - Agents: **water** (most powerful), wind, ice, gravity.
[11:0:2] - Water = strongest cause of soil erosion (**sheet, rill, gully** – Chambal ravines); **wind** leads only in areas like the **Thar**.
## SUB 1 | Weathering Processes
[11:1:0] TABLE head: Process | Nature | What happens / example
  [11:1:0.0] Physical (mechanical) | No chemical change | Frost wedging, heating-cooling (exfoliation), salt crystal growth
  [11:1:0.1] Solution | Chemical | Soluble minerals (rock salt, gypsum) dissolve
  [11:1:0.2] Carbonation | Chemical | CO₂ in rain forms carbonic acid, dissolves limestone; stores carbon as bicarbonate
  [11:1:0.3] Hydration | Chemical | Minerals absorb water and swell: anhydrite → gypsum
  [11:1:0.4] Hydrolysis | Chemical | Water reacts with silicates: feldspar → clay; muscovite → vermiculite (potassium replaced by hydronium) (APPSC 2024)
  [11:1:0.5] Oxidation | Chemical | Iron minerals rust → red or yellow soils
  [11:1:0.6] Biological | Organic | Roots, burrowing animals, lichens, micro-organisms
[11:1:1] - Weathering is **fastest in hot and wet** climates; higher temperature and more rain speed up chemical weathering.
[11:1:2] - Rainwater holds **CO₂ in solution and atmospheric oxygen** – both help chemical weathering.
[11:1:3] - **Freeze-thaw** breaks rocks in the high Himalaya. Glacier-fed Himalayan catchments release CO₂ through **pyrite oxidation** (13 Sep 2026).
## SUB 2 | Weathering Landforms
[11:2:0] TABLE head: Landform | Meaning
  [11:2:0.0] Exfoliation domes | Onion-like peeling of granite (from pressure or temperature change)
  [11:2:0.1] Tors | Residual granite blocks standing above the weathered surface where joints are widely spaced
  [11:2:0.2] Block disintegration | Physical break-up of jointed rock into blocks
## SUB 3 | Mass Wasting
[11:3:0] - Downslope movement of earth material under **gravity** = **landslide** (APPSC 2019).
[11:3:1] TABLE head: Type | Nature
  [11:3:1.0] Rockfall | Free fall of rocks from steep cliffs
  [11:3:1.1] Slide | Whole coherent mass slips along a surface
  [11:3:1.2] Slump | Rotational slip along a curved surface in weak, wet soils
  [11:3:1.3] Debris flow / mudflow | Fast water-soil-rock mix after heavy rain or snowmelt
  [11:3:1.4] Creep | Very slow movement from wetting-drying, heating-cooling
[11:3:2] - Conditions: slopes **above 30 degrees**, weak/fractured rock, bare slopes; clay and silt get saturated (landslide-prone).
[11:3:3] - India's main trigger: **SW monsoon rainfall**, then **earthquakes**. Most at risk: **Himalaya and North-East**.
## SUB 4 | Cycle of Erosion
[11:4:0] TABLE head: Person | Contribution
  [11:4:0.0] W.M. Davis (1899) | Landscape = structure + process + stage (time); youth → maturity → old age; ends in a near-flat peneplain with monadnocks
  [11:4:0.1] Walther Penck | Landform depends on ratio of uplift to erosion acting simultaneously, not time-stages; slopes retreat parallel
  [11:4:0.2] Term | Meaning
  [11:4:0.3] Peneplain | Low, almost flat end-surface of the Davis cycle
  [11:4:0.4] Monadnock | Resistant residual hill on a peneplain
  [11:4:0.5] Inselberg | Residual island-hill of arid/semi-arid regions (wind and sheet-wash erosion)
[11:4:1] - **Peninsular plateau** of India = old peneplain, rejuvenated in places.
## SUB 5 | Soil-forming Factors
[11:5:0] - Five classical factors: **parent material, climate, relief/topography, organisms, time**; **latitude is NOT** one.
[11:5:1] TABLE head: Active factors | Passive factors
  [11:5:1.0] Climate, biological activity (vegetation, micro-organisms) | Parent material, topography (relief), time
[11:5:2] - APPSC G1 2024: active factors = climate and biological activity (**III and IV only**) – they drive weathering and organic processes.
[11:5:3] - **AP**: climate, altitude and **bedrock composition** shape soils; uneven rainfall and excessive heat give AP soils their character; soils mainly a mix of sand, silt and clay.
[11:5:4] - **AP parent materials:** river silt of the **Godavari, Krishna and Pennar** is among the parent materials of Andhra Pradesh soils, along with the bedrock.
## SUB 6 | Soil Texture
[11:6:0] TABLE head: Particle | Diameter
  [11:6:0.0] Sand (fine sand) | 0.05–2 mm
  [11:6:0.1] Silt | 0.002–0.05 mm
  [11:6:0.2] Clay | Less than 0.002 mm
[11:6:1] - Water-holding: **clay > silt > sand**; clay holds **maximum** water (smaller particles); clay pore spaces are **small, not large**; sand drains easily.
[11:6:2] - **Loam** contains all particle types (about **40% sand, 42% silt, 18% clay**).
[11:6:3] - Clay binds organic carbon; sandy soils lose it by leaching (Nov 2025).
[11:6:4] - **Rainforest soils are poor** despite lush forest: heavy rain **leaches** nutrients and dead matter decomposes rapidly.
## SUB 7 | Soil Profile
[11:7:0] TABLE head: Horizon | Character
  [11:7:0.0] O | Surface organic layer of decaying plant matter
  [11:7:0.1] A | Topsoil: minerals + organic matter; most plant roots
  [11:7:0.2] E | Light-coloured leached layer (not in every profile)
  [11:7:0.3] B | Subsoil: leached minerals collect
  [11:7:0.4] C | Weathered parent material, little organic matter
  [11:7:0.5] R | Unweathered bedrock
## SUB 8 | Zonal, Residual and Transported Soils
[11:8:0] TABLE head: Soil | Climate / vegetation
  [11:8:0.0] Podzol | Cold taiga (boreal conifer forests); thin, acidic, leached, humus-poor
  [11:8:0.1] Laterite | Hot, heavy-rain tropics; silica and lime leached, iron and aluminium oxides left; acidic
  [11:8:0.2] Terra rossa | Mediterranean, over limestone; red
  [11:8:0.3] Chernozem | Temperate grasslands (steppes, prairies); black, humus-rich
  [11:8:0.4] Oxisols | Equatorial-tropical surfaces stable for very long (APPSC 2019)
  [11:8:0.5] Type | Formation / region
  [11:8:0.6] In-situ / residual = sedentary (APPSC 2018) | Form where the rock weathered; mainly Peninsular India
  [11:8:0.7] Transported (ex-situ) | Moved by rivers, wind, ice; mainly North India; alluvium ~45.6% of India
[11:8:1] - **AP cultivated land (Jul 2026)**: **red ~60%** (Rayalaseema), **black (regur) ~25%** (one-fourth), deltaic alluvium ~6%, **laterite ~1%**.
[11:8:2] - **FAO Status of the World's Soil Resources 2026** (75 countries, 3 Sep 2026): soil biodiversity loss ranked **4th of seven** threats, after erosion, loss of soil organic carbon and nutrient mismanagement.

  [12:0:0.0] Mountains | Folding, faulting, volcanism, erosion | Himalayas, Andes, Alps, Rockies, Aravallis

  [12:0:0.3] Glacial | Glacial erosion and deposition | Cirques, arêtes, horns, U-shaped and hanging valleys, moraines

## SUB 1 | River Profile – Upper, Middle, Lower Course
[12:1:0] TABLE head: Course (stage) | Dominant work | Typical features
  [12:1:0.0] Upper (youth) | Vertical erosion, steep gradient | V-shaped valleys, gorges, canyons, waterfalls, rapids, potholes
  [12:1:0.1] Middle (maturity) | Lateral erosion + transport | Wider valleys, meanders, floodplains begin
  [12:1:0.2] Lower (old age) | Deposition, gentle gradient | Broad floodplains, levees, oxbow lakes, braided channels, deltas/estuaries
[12:1:1] - Middle course does side erosion and transport too; vertical erosion is not its only work.
[12:1:2] APPSC 2022: A **canyon** forms in the **upper course**; floodplain, alluvial fan and delta do not.
## SUB 2 | Profile Terms – Grade, Base Level, Capture
[12:2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [12:2:0.0] Graded profile | Smooth concave long profile; erosion and deposition roughly balanced
  [12:2:0.1] Base level | Lowest level a river can erode to; sea level = ultimate; lakes and dams = local
  [12:2:0.2] Stream capture (river piracy) | Headward erosion breaks a nearby divide and takes a neighbour's upper waters
  [12:2:0.3] Peneplain | Near-featureless gentle plain worn by rivers almost to base level
## SUB 3 | River-plain Terms and Plain Types
[12:3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [12:3:0.0] Doab | Land between two confluent rivers (Persian "do-ab" = two waters); e.g. Ganga-Yamuna doab, Bist Doab (Beas-Sutlej)
  [12:3:0.1] Bhabar | Narrow pebble belt along the Shiwalik foothills; streams drop load and vanish underground
  [12:3:0.2] Terai | South of Bhabar; water re-emerges, wet marshy belt
  [12:3:0.3] Bhangar | Older alluvium above flood level; contains kankar (lime nodules)
  [12:3:0.4] Khadar | Newer alluvium of the floodplain; renewed every flood, more fertile
  [12:3:0.5] Prayag | A sacred river confluence
[12:3:1] - **Panch Prayag** on the Alaknanda (downstream order): **Vishnuprayag** (Dhauliganga), **Nandprayag** (Nandakini), **Karnaprayag** (Pindar), **Rudraprayag** (Mandakini), **Devprayag** (Bhagirathi; the river is called **Ganga** from here).
[12:3:2] TABLE head: Plain type | Formed by
  [12:3:2.0] Alluvial plain | River deposition of alluvium
  [12:3:2.1] Flood plain | Fine silt dropped when river overflows
  [12:3:2.2] Lacustrine plain | Lake filled with sediment, then drained; e.g. Kashmir Valley (Karewas)
  [12:3:2.3] Peneplain | Long river erosion to near base level
[12:3:3] Bhabar vs Terai = relief and drainage; Bhangar vs Khadar = age of alluvium. Khadar is newer.
## SUB 4 | Erosional Landforms
[12:4:0] TABLE head: Landform | Formation
  [12:4:0.0] V-shaped valley | Rills → gullies → valleys by vertical erosion
  [12:4:0.1] Waterfall | Drop over a resistant rock band or fault step
  [12:4:0.2] Potholes | Eddy-whirled fragments drill circular holes; large one below a waterfall = plunge pool
  [12:4:0.3] Incised (entrenched) meanders | Uplift rejuvenates the river; meanders cut deep into hard rock
  [12:4:0.4] River terraces | Stream cuts down into its own floodplain; paired = same height both banks; unpaired = one bank, slow uplift
## SUB 5 | Gorge vs Canyon
[12:5:0] TABLE head: Basis | Gorge | Canyon
  [12:5:0.0] Nature | Deep, narrow, steep-sided valley | Much deeper gorge
  [12:5:0.1] Width | Almost same at top and bottom | Wider at top
  [12:5:0.2] Rock | Hard rocks | Horizontally bedded sedimentary
  [12:5:0.3] Example | Chisapani Gorge | Grand Canyon (Colorado River)
[12:5:1] - Both are forms of the V-shaped valley; **canyon is a variant of a gorge**, not the reverse.
[12:5:2] - **Grand Canyon**: carved by the **Colorado** in **Arizona, USA**; about **446 km** long, over **1.8 km** deep.
[12:5:3] - **Dihang gorge**: Brahmaputra enters India (Arunachal); Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon near **Namcha Barwa**.
[12:5:4] - **Gandikota canyon**: **Pennar (Penna) River, AP** – "**Grand Canyon of India**".
[12:5:5] APPSC 2018: "Gorges and canyons are V-shaped valleys" = correct; "gorges are extended forms of canyons" = WRONG.
## SUB 6 | Ravines and Badlands
[12:6:0] - Gully = water-worn channel of semi-arid areas; rills merge and deepen into gullies; dense gully network = **badland topography**.
[12:6:1] - India's classic badlands: **Chambal ravines** (central India); also **Chos of Hoshiarpur** (Punjab).
[12:6:2] APPSC 2022: Badland topography = **Chambal basin** (not Rann of Kutch, Godavari delta, western coastal plain).
## SUB 7 | Depositional Landforms
[12:7:0] TABLE head: Landform | How it forms
  [12:7:0.0] Alluvial fan | Coarse load dropped at the break of slope as a fan
  [12:7:0.1] Natural levee | Raised sandy banks along the channel
  [12:7:0.2] Floodplain | Fine alluvium dropped when the river overflows
  [12:7:0.3] Braided channel | Sand bars split the channel into many strands with riverine islands

## SUB 8 | Deltas and Estuaries
[12:8:0] # Delta vs Estuary
[12:8:1] TABLE head: Basis | Delta | Estuary
  [12:8:1.0] Process | Deposition | Erosion / tidal flushing
  [12:8:1.1] Mouth | Many distributaries | Single funnel opening
  [12:8:1.2] Example | Ganga-Brahmaputra, Godavari | Narmada, Tapi, Western Ghats rivers
[12:8:2] - **Estuary**: partly closed river mouth with free connection to the sea; an **ecotone**. **Tapti forms an estuary**.
[12:8:3] - West-flowing Deccan rivers form estuaries: **steep gradient, hard rock, short courses**.
[12:8:4] - Conditions for a delta: slow flow, heavy sediment load, shallow sea, **weak tides and currents**. Strong tides remove sediment.

=== NOTES 4:13 | Major Landforms – Glacial, Arid/aeolian, Karst and Coastal Landforms
## SUB 0 | Agent → Landforms
[13:0:0] TABLE head: Agent | Landforms
  [13:0:0.0] Running water | Delta, V-valley, gorge, canyon, meander, alluvial fan, levee, peneplain
  [13:0:0.1] Glaciers | Cirque, arête, horn, U-valley, hanging valley, moraine, till, esker; fjord (with drowning)
  [13:0:0.2] Wind | Sand dunes, yardangs, mushroom rocks, loess
  [13:0:0.3] Karst (groundwater) | Sinkholes, doline, stalactites, stalagmites, blind valley
  [13:0:0.4] Waves | Cliffs, sea caves, arches, stacks; bars (waves + currents)
  [13:0:0.5] Longshore drift | Spits
  [13:0:0.6] Tectonic faulting | Rift valleys (Death Valley, Narmada), horsts, grabens
[13:0:1] APPSC-G2 2023 match: Delta – running water; Cirque – glaciers; Cliffs – waves and currents; Sand dunes – winds.
[13:0:2] - **Esker** = glacial; yardang, mushroom rock = aeolian; **blind valley** = karst (not tectonic).
## SUB 1 | Weathering, Mass Wasting and Cycle of Erosion
[13:1:0] TABLE head: Type | Examples
  [13:1:0.0] Physical | Exfoliation, frost wedging, block disintegration – no chemical change
  [13:1:0.1] Chemical | Solution, carbonation, oxidation, hydration, hydrolysis
  [13:1:0.2] Mass wasting (gravity) | Soil creep, slump, landslide, mudflow, rockfall
[13:1:1] - Oxidation is chemical weathering, **not** mass wasting.
[13:1:2] - AP: **granite/gneiss → red soil**; **basalt → black soil**.
[13:1:3] - **Cycle of erosion**: proposed by **W.M. Davis**; stages **youth → maturity → old age**; ends in a **peneplain** near sea level; renewed uplift = **rejuvenation**.
[13:1:4] - **Peninsular Plateau** = rejuvenated peneplain.
[13:1:5] - **Biological weathering:** done by living things – growing **plant roots** widen cracks; **burrowing animals** (earthworms, termites, rodents) expose fresh rock; **lichens** release chemicals that break down minerals.
## SUB 2 | Fluvial Landforms and Delta Types
[13:2:0] TABLE head: Stage | Landforms
  [13:2:0.0] Youth (upper) | V-valley, gorge, canyon (APPSC 2022), waterfall, potholes, rapids
  [13:2:0.1] Mature (middle) | Meanders, floodplains, alluvial fans at hill foot
  [13:2:0.2] Old (lower) | Oxbow lakes, natural levees, braided channels, deltas
[13:2:1] - **Canyon**: step-like sides, wider at top, horizontally bedded sedimentary rock; **gorge**: hard rock, equal width.
[13:2:2] - Oxbow lake = meander cut off.
[13:2:3] - River terraces: vertical erosion into own floodplain; paired vs unpaired (slow uplift).
[13:2:4] - Estuary: funnel mouth open to sea (ecotone); **Narmada and Tapi form estuaries**, not deltas.
[13:2:5] TABLE head: Delta type | Condition | Example
  [13:2:5.0] Arcuate | Sediment denser than sea water | Nile, Sundarbans
  [13:2:5.1] Cuspate | Density equal | Tiber
  [13:2:5.2] Bird's foot | Sediment lighter, carried far | Mississippi (Gulf of Mexico)
## SUB 3 | Regional Plain Terms and Plain Types
[13:3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning / region
  [13:3:0.0] Bhabar | Gravel-pebble belt, 8–16 km wide, parallel to Shiwalik foothills; porous
  [13:3:0.1] Terai | Marshy forested belt south of Bhabar; streams reappear
  [13:3:0.2] Bhangar | Older alluvium above flood level, with kankar
  [13:3:0.3] Khadar | New floodplain alluvium, no kankar
  [13:3:0.4] Doab | Land between two rivers (Ganga-Yamuna Doab)

## SUB 4 | Glacial Landforms
[13:4:0] TABLE head: Landform | Meaning
  [13:4:0.0] Cirque (corrie) | Bowl/armchair hollow at valley head

  [13:4:0.3] U-shaped valley | Glacier deepens and widens a river valley
  [13:4:0.4] Hanging valley | Tributary valley left high; joins by waterfall; agent = glacier (UKPSC 2024)
  [13:4:0.5] Fjord | Drowned glacial U-valley (Norway, Greenland, New Zealand)
  [13:4:0.6] Moraine | Unsorted debris (till): lateral, terminal, medial, ground

  [13:4:0.8] Esker | Winding ridge of stratified sediment from a sub-glacial tunnel

## SUB 5 | Arid (aeolian) Landforms
[13:5:0] - Wind transport: deflation, **saltation**, surface creep.
[13:5:1] TABLE head: Landform | Key point
  [13:5:1.0] Barchan | Crescent dune, horns downwind
  [13:5:1.1] Seif | Long narrow dunes where sand is scarce
  [13:5:1.2] Yardang | Ridge parallel to the wind
  [13:5:1.3] Zeugen | Table-like ridge, hard cap over soft rock

  [13:5:1.5] Deflation hollow | Wind removal

## SUB 6 | Karst Landforms
[13:6:0] - **Karst**: groundwater dissolving **limestone/dolomite**; named after the Karst region (Slovenia).
[13:6:1] - Rising size: **sinkhole → doline → uvala → polje**. **Sinkhole (doline)** = commonest karst landform.
[13:6:2] - **Uvala** = joined dolines; **polje** = large hollow from cave collapse; **swallow hole** = stream sinks.
[13:6:3] - **Blind valley**: ends suddenly where the stream sinks (steephead valley).
[13:6:4] - **Stalactites hang from the roof** (APPSC 2022); **stalagmites rise from the floor**; meet → **pillars**. Formed by dissolving and re-depositing calcium carbonate.
[13:6:5] - Longest cave: India **Krem Liat Prah** (Meghalaya); world **Mammoth Cave** (USA).

## SUB 8 | Coastal Erosion
[13:8:0] - **Main agent: waves** (also tides, currents, tsunamis, humans).
[13:8:1] - Processes: hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution (**saltation is wind**).
[13:8:2] - Sequence: **cliff + wave-cut platform → sea cave → arch → stack → stump**.
[13:8:3] - Cave = weak zones eroded; arch = cave cuts a headland; stack = arch collapses.
[13:8:4] - Sediment cut by dams, harbours, sand mining.
[13:8:5] TABLE head: Defence | Methods
  [13:8:5.0] Soft | Beach nourishment, dunes, mangroves
  [13:8:5.1] Hard | Seawalls, groynes (across longshore drift), breakwaters
[13:8:6] - East coast erodes more: soft low alluvium, no rocky cliffs. AP: severe at **Uppada (Kakinada)** and Krishna coast (Jul 2026).
