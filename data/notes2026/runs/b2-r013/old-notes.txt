=== NOTES 2:13 | Cultural and Educational Rights – Articles 29-30; the Right to Property and the 44TH Amendment
## SUB 0 | Cultural and Educational Rights – Articles 29 and 30
[13:0:0] - **Two Articles**: Art. 29 – **protection of interests of minorities**; Art. 30 – minorities' right to **establish and administer educational institutions**.
[13:0:1] # Article 29
[13:0:2] TABLE head: Clause | Provision
  [13:0:2.0] 29(1) | Any section of citizens with a distinct language, script or culture has the right to conserve it (not limited to minorities)
  [13:0:2.1] 29(2) | No citizen denied admission to a State-maintained or State-aided institution on grounds only of religion, race, caste, language
[13:0:3] # Article 30
[13:0:4] TABLE head: Clause | Provision
  [13:0:4.0] 30(1) | All minorities, religious or linguistic, may establish and administer educational institutions of their choice – the "Charter of Education Rights"; madrasas run under it
  [13:0:4.1] 30(1A) (44th Amendment, 1978) | On compulsory acquisition of a minority institution's property, the amount fixed must not restrict or abrogate the Art. 30 right
[13:0:5] - Art. 30 is **not absolute**: the State may regulate **standards**, but may **not take away the minority character**.
[13:0:6] - **Pramati Educational and Cultural Trust v. Union of India (2014)**: the RTE Act's **25% quota does not apply to minority schools**.
[13:0:7] - Critics: misuse of minority status to escape RTE; only **8.76%** disadvantaged enrolments in minority schools (NCPCR, Sep 2025).
[13:0:8] - **Art. 350A**: every State and local authority to provide **instruction in the mother tongue at the primary stage** for children of linguistic minority groups.
## SUB 1 | Who Is A Minority?
[13:1:0] - "Minority" is used in **Arts. 29, 30, 350A, 350B** but **not defined** in the Constitution; **Article 30 does not define it**; the **NCM Act, 1992** does not define it either. "Linguistic minority" is also undefined.
[13:1:1] - A minority = any community **notified by the Centre** (Jul 2026); NCM's definition is mainly religious.
[13:1:2] - Test: **numerical inferiority**, **non-dominant** status, distinct identity; **unit = the State** (**T.M.A. Pai**).
[13:1:3] TABLE head: Basis | Determined by
  [13:1:3.0] Religious minority | NCM, Ministry of Minority Affairs – national notification
  [13:1:3.1] Linguistic minority | State-wise
[13:1:4] - **NCM**: **statutory** body under the NCM Act, 1992.
[13:1:5] - **Six notified minorities**: **Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Parsis, Jains** (Apr 2026). Linguistic minorities are not among these religious categories.
[13:1:6] - National notification brings a group within NCM and central schemes (scholarships, **PMJVK**, **PM VIKAS**).
[13:1:7] - 10 Mar 2026: Kerala **Malayalam Bhasha Bill** (assented Mar 2026) contested as harming the **Kannada** linguistic minority in **Kasaragod**.
## SUB 2 | Minority Institution Cases
[13:2:0] TABLE head: Case | Year | Holding
  [13:2:0.0] In re Kerala Education Bill | 1958 | Presidential reference on State control over aided/recognised institutions' management
  [13:2:0.1] St. Stephen's College | 1992 | Minority-institution autonomy (5-judge bench)

  [13:2:0.3] P.A. Inamdar | 2005 | 7-judge bench; State cannot impose reservation on unaided private (minority or non-minority) colleges → led to the 93rd Amendment
  [13:2:0.4] Pramati | 2014 | RTE 25% quota not for minority schools
## SUB 3 | Right to Property – the Journey
[13:3:0] TABLE head: Stage | Position
  [13:3:0.0] 1950 | FR: Art. 19(1)(f) (acquire, hold, dispose of property) + Art. 31 (no deprivation save by authority of law; acquisition for public purpose with compensation) – the most disputed FR
  [13:3:0.1] 1st Amendment, 1951 | Arts. 31A, 31B and Ninth Schedule to shield land reform laws
  [13:3:0.2] 4th (1955), 17th (1964), 25th (1971) | Narrowed compensation; 17th: land under personal cultivation acquired only on paying market value
  [13:3:0.3] 44th Amendment, 1978 | Deleted 19(1)(f) and 31 from Part III → Art. 300A, Part XII, Chapter IV – a legal (constitutional) right
[13:3:1] - Removed mainly so property rights would not block **land reform**.
[13:3:2] - **FRs: originally seven, now six**.
[13:3:3] # Saving Clauses – 31A, 31B, 31C
[13:3:4] TABLE head: Article | Inserted by | Saves
  [13:3:4.0] 31A | 1st, 1951 | Laws on acquisition of estates etc.
  [13:3:4.1] 31B | 1st, 1951 | Acts in the Ninth Schedule – immune from FR challenge
  [13:3:4.2] 31C | 25th, 1971 | Laws giving effect to Arts. 39(b), 39(c); 42nd extended it to all DPSPs – struck down in Minerva Mills (1980)
[13:3:5] - **I.R. Coelho (2007)**, 9 judges: Ninth Schedule laws inserted **after 24 April 1973** are open to **basic structure** review.
## SUB 4 | Article 300A and Larr Act 2013
[13:4:0] - Text: "**No person shall be deprived of his property save by authority of law**".
[13:4:1] - Available to **all persons** – citizens, non-citizens, **companies**. Enforced via **Art. 226**/ordinary suit, **not Art. 32**.
[13:4:2] - **AP angle**: **GGR Housing v. State** (AP High Court, May 2026) – strict statutory due process in land acquisition under Art. 300A.
[13:4:3] PYQ G1 2019 (State cannot dispossess except by law): key said "Article 300"; correct is Article 300A.
[13:4:4] - **Land Acquisition Act, 1894**: made by British rulers; in effect **1 March 1894**; purpose was **not land reform** but acquisition for public institutions, organisations and companies.
[13:4:5] - **LARR Act, 2013** replaced the 1894 Act; **Social Impact Assessment compulsory** (a feature, not the reason it replaced the 1894 Act); compensation up to **4x** market value (**rural**), **2x** (**urban**); **unused land returned after 5 years**.
## SUB 5 | Fundamental vs Constitutional / Legal Right
[13:5:0] - **Every FR is a constitutional right, but not every constitutional right is an FR.** A **legal right** comes from ordinary law.
[13:5:1] TABLE head: Right | Status | Where
  [13:5:1.0] Property | Legal (constitutional/statutory) right | Art. 300A
  [13:5:1.1] Vote (and to be elected) | Legal/statutory right – not FR | Art. 326 (adult suffrage, 18+); Sec. 62 RPA 1951; registration Sec. 19 RPA 1950
  [13:5:1.2] Information | Legal right (RTI Act, 2005); also read into 19(1)(a) | —
  [13:5:1.3] Work | DPSP | Art. 41
  [13:5:1.4] Education (6-14) | Fundamental Right | Art. 21A
[13:5:2] - **Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India (2009)**: voting is only a **statutory/legal** right.
[13:5:3] - **Voting paradox**: voting is statutory, yet the **right to know candidate details, NOTA and ballot secrecy** are recognised under **Art. 19(1)(a)**.

## SUB 8 | High-probability Mcq Traps
[13:8:0] VERY IMPORTANT
[13:8:1] - 1. Property is now **Art. 300A**, not Art. 300 and not an FR.
[13:8:2] - 2. **44th (1978)** deleted 19(1)(f) and 31 – not the 42nd or 86th.
[13:8:3] - 3. **Right to vote** is a **legal** right, not fundamental (Kuldip Nayar).
[13:8:4] - 4. **Right to work** = DPSP (Art. 41).
[13:8:5] - 5. "Minority" is **not defined** in the Constitution or the NCM Act.
[13:8:6] - 6. Unit for deciding minority status = **State** (T.M.A. Pai).
[13:8:7] - 7. Mother-tongue instruction = **Art. 350A**, not 360A.
[13:8:8] - 8. NCM is **statutory**, not constitutional.
[13:8:9] - 9. 300A is enforced through **Art. 226**, not Art. 32.
