## SUB 2 | National Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas) 2025
[2:0] # The day
[2:1] - Observed on **26 November**; 26 November 2025 marked **76 years** since the adoption of the Constitution.
[2:2] - Declared in **2015**, the **125th birth anniversary year of Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**; earlier observed as **Law Day**; **not a public holiday**.
[2:3] # Constitution: key dates
[2:4] TABLE head: Event | Date
  [2:4.0] First meeting of the Constituent Assembly | 9 December 1946
  [2:4.1] Drafting Committee constituted (Chairman Dr. B.R. Ambedkar) | 29 August 1947
  [2:4.2] Draft Constitution introduced | 4 November 1948
  [2:4.3] Constitution adopted | 26 November 1949 (→ Samvidhan Divas)
  [2:4.4] Constitution came into force | 26 January 1950
[2:5] - Time taken to frame the Constitution: **2 years, 11 months, 18 days**.
[2:6] # Borrowed features (asked with Constitution Day)
[2:7] TABLE head: Source | Features borrowed
  [2:7.0] Government of India Act, 1935 | Federal structure, office of Governor, Public Service Commissions, Emergency provisions
  [2:7.1] USA | Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Impeachment, Vice President
  [2:7.2] Ireland | DPSPs, method of Presidential election, nomination to Rajya Sabha
  [2:7.3] Canada | Strong Centre, residuary powers
  [2:7.4] Australia | Concurrent List, freedom of trade
  [2:7.5] Germany (Weimar) | Suspension of Fundamental Rights during Emergency
  [2:7.6] USSR | Fundamental Duties
  [2:7.7] France | Liberty, Equality, Fraternity
  [2:7.8] South Africa | Amendment procedure, election of Rajya Sabha members
  [2:7.9] Japan | Procedure Established by Law
[2:8] > Drafting Committee 29 Aug 1947 vs adoption 26 Nov 1949 vs enforcement 26 Jan 1950; residuary powers came from Canada, not Ireland.

## SUB 5 | Swadeshi Idea and National Handloom Day
[5:0] # National Handloom Day – 7 August
[5:1] - Observed on **7 August** because the **Swadeshi Movement was launched on 7 August 1905**.
[5:2] # Evolution of swadeshi
[5:3] TABLE head: Period | Person / event | Idea
  [5:3.0] 1822 | Bengali weekly Samachar Chandrika | Use Indian goods for religious/social reasons
  [5:3.1] 19th century | Gopal Hari Deshmukh, Rajnarayan Basu | Locally produced goods
  [5:3.2] Mid-19th century | M.G. Ranade | Prefer domestic goods even if costlier
  [5:3.3] Late 19th century | Dadabhai Naoroji – Drain of Wealth; R.C. Dutt – *The Economic History of India* | Economic nationalism, deindustrialisation
  [5:3.4] 1905–1911 | Swadeshi Movement | Vande Mataram the rallying cry
  [5:3.5] Gandhi | Khadi, charkha | Economic + moral + cultural swadeshi
[5:4] - Gandhi's swadeshi was **not purely economic** – it was economic, moral and cultural.

## SUB 8 | Heritage: ASI, AP Inscriptions and Festivals
[8:0] # Archaeological Survey of India
[8:1] - Under the Ministry of Culture; governed by the **AMASR Act, 1958**; manages 3,650+ monuments of national importance.
[8:2] - Founded **1861**; **Alexander Cunningham** was its **first Director-General** ("Father of Indian Archaeology"); strengthened by Lord Curzon.
[8:3] - PPP successes in conservation: **Elephanta Caves and Dr Bhau Daji Lad Museum, Mumbai**; INTACH and ASI central to heritage management.
[8:4] # Srisailam copper plates (1420 CE)
[8:5] - **Saka 1341 = 28 January 1420 CE** – **15th century** (headlines said 14th); three copper leaves, **Sanskrit and Telugu**.
[8:6] - 'Swarajya Punarudhharana' – first use of 'Swarajya' in a Telugu inscription; ruler **Recharla Lingama Nayaka** reclaimed areas from the Turakas and restored eight agraharas.
[8:7] - Freedom-movement link: **Tilak** – "Swarajya is my birthright"; **Tanguturi Prakasam** started the newspaper 'Swarajya' in three languages.
[8:8] # Vankeswara temple inscription, Durgi (1518 CE)
[8:9] - **Durgi**, **Palnadu district**; 22 November 1518 CE, reign of Sri Krishnadevaraya; commissioned by **Timmapa Nayanim Garu**, a doorkeeper; the inscription is sinking.
[8:10] # Sri Modakondamma Jatara
[8:11] - **Modakondamma Temple, Paderu** (Alluri Sitharama Raju district); May (2025: 11–13 May); Grama Devatha, a form of **Goddess Durga**, revered by Kondhs and Koyas.
