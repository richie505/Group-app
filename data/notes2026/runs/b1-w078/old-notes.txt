=== NOTES 1:78 | Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi – Kubja Vishnuvardhana, Dynastic History, Patronage of Telugu (nannaya)
## SUB 0 | Gunaga Vijayaditya III (848–892) – Greatest King, Titles, Pandaranga, Kandukuru
[78:0:0] # The greatest Eastern Chalukya king
[78:0:1] - **Gunaga Vijayaditya = Vijayaditya III**, the **greatest king** of the Vengi Chalukyas (G2 2011 ×2) – not Danarnava, Chalukya Bhima I or Rajaraja Narendra; ruled **43 years**; "Gunaga" for his skill in mathematics.
[78:0:2] - **Military:** defeated the **Rashtrakuta Krishna II** and the **Kalinga king Devendravarma**; expanded the kingdom; issued grand **copper-plate** records with royal titles (ATWO).
[78:0:3] # Titles
[78:0:4] - **Tripura Martya Maheswara** (G2 2012, Mains 2017; G1, G2 PYQ) – not Vishnuvardhana I/III, Vijayaditya I, Kubja Vishnuvardhana, Rajaraja Narendra or Vijayaditya II.
[78:0:5]   - Assumed to commemorate his victory over **Nellore, Kiranapura and Achalapura** (G2 2012).
[78:0:6]   - **⚠ Settled:** the printed key "Kiranapuram, Achalapuram and **Kandukur**" is wrong – NextGen and Rocket give **Nellore, Kiranapura and Achalapura**.
[78:0:7] - Other titles: **Gunaka-Nallata, Parachakra Rama, Vallabha**, Vira Makaradhwaja, Ranaranga Sudraka, Dakshinadhipati (Sachivalayam: the set = **Vijayaditya III**, not Chalukya Bhima, Vishnuvardhana IV or Amoghavarsha).
[78:0:8] # Pandaranga (Panduranga), his commander
[78:0:9] - **Pandaranga = commander of Gunaga Vijayaditya** (G1 1983-84, 1987; Welfare Officers 2017; APPSC PYQ) – i.e. of the **Vengi (Eastern) Chalukyas**, not the Ananda Gotrikas, Pallavas or Badami Chalukyas.
[78:0:10] - One of **four Brahmin generals** (Pandaranga, Vinayaditya Sarma, Kadiyaraju, Rajaditya); subdued the **Boyas**, built **Pandurangapuram** and a Maheswara temple on **Pulicat lake**.
[78:0:11] - Issued the **Addanki inscription** – the **first Telugu verse (padya) inscription**, in **Taruvoja** metre.
[78:0:12] # Kandukuru "on the lines of Bezawada"
[78:0:13] - **Vijayaditya III (Gunaga Vijayaditya)** developed **Kandukuru** like **Bezawada** (G2 2008 ×2, 2019; Mains; APPSC PYQ) – Pandaranga made it a strong fort after conquering the Boya kingdoms.
[78:0:14]   - **⚠ Settled:** one G2 2008 printed key gave **Vijayaditya II** – wrong; Vijayaditya II is the 108-temples king.

## SUB 1 | Nannaya and Telugu – Nandampudi, Narayana Bhattu and the First Telugu Inscriptions
[78:1:0] # Nannaya
[78:1:1] - **Nannaya Bhattu** (Adi Kavi), **court poet of Rajaraja Narendra** (G2 1993, 2011 ×2) – Rajaraja Narendra **patronised** him, not Bhima II, Vijayaditya, Vishnuvardhana or Ammaraja; Pampa and Nannechoda are distractors.
[78:1:2] - Began the **Andhra Mahabharatamu** (c. 1045–65; **Adi, Sabha** and part of **Aranya** parvas) – the **earliest surviving Telugu literary work**, in **Champu** (verse + prose) style; the court poet, **not the king**, began it.
[78:1:3] - **Telugu literature owes its origin to the (Eastern) Chalukyas** (APPSC JA) – not Cholas, Cheras or Pandyas; the Vengi court turned Telugu into a literary language.
[78:1:4] - Titles: **Adikavi, Vagadheeshwara, Shabda Shasanudu**; grammar **Andhra Shabda Chintamani**.
[78:1:5] - **A–R (ATWO):** A (earliest surviving Telugu work) and R (translated in the 11th century at Rajaraja Narendra's request) are both true; the key takes R as the explanation of A.
[78:1:6] # Narayana Bhattu and the Nandampudi grant
[78:1:7] - **Narayana Bhattu helped Nannaya** (JA 2012); he was a scholar sent to Vengi as the **Kalyani Chalukya** (Someshvara I) representative; title **Ashtabhasha Kavisekhara**.
[78:1:8] - **Evidence: the Nandampudi grant (copper-plate inscription) of Rajaraja Narendra** (G2 Prelims 2019; Mains 2017; G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ ×2) – not Andhra Kavi Tarangini, Kollipaka, Pedda Cherukuru, Maruturu, Kupama or Mangallu.
[78:1:9] - **Donee of Nandampudi village = Narayana Bhattu** (G2 Mains 2017; G2 PYQ); **Nannaya composed** the grant – Nannaya, Jina Vallabha and Pavuluri Mallana were not the donee.
[78:1:10] - **Korumelli** plates of Rajaraja Narendra – same scribe as Nandampudi.

## SUB 2 | Capitals and Cities – Rajamahendravaram, Vengi, Pithapuram, Bezawada
[78:2:0] # Rajamahendravaram (Rajahmundry)
[78:2:1] - **Built by Ammaraja I (Vishnuvardhana VI, 921–927)**, title **Rajamahendra**, after which the city is named (APPSC PYQ ×3; JA 2012; G2 2008); he shifted the capital from Vengi there – "**cultural capital of the Andhras**".
[78:2:2]   - **⚠ Settled:** one G2 2008 printed key gave **Rajaraja Narendra** – he only **ruled from** Rajahmundry and **made it famous**; NextGen and its previous-paper key: "**Ammaraja ruler built Rajahmundry**".
[78:2:3] - **Dynasty:** Rajamahendri was built in the time of the **Eastern Chalukyas** (G2 2001, 2002; unknown 2002 "founded by the Eastern Chalukyas") – not Vishnukundins, Kalingas or Vijayanagara.
[78:2:4] - **Statement (VRO):** A – Rajamahendravaram built by **Ammaraja Vishnuvardhana I** ✓; B – **Rajahmundry district** created in the **Madras Presidency in 1823** ✓ (split into Godavari and Krishna in 1859).

## SUB 3 | Founder, Dynasty Outline and the Chola Connection
[78:3:0] # Founder
[78:3:1] - **Kubja Vishnuvardhana**, **brother of Pulakeshin II**, founded the **Chalukyas of Vengi** (UPPSC 2003) – not Bhanuvardhan, Vijayaditya I or Pulakeshin IV.
[78:3:2] - **Pulakeshin II** took Vengi after defeating the **Vishnukundins** (c. 616); Kubja **viceroy 617**, **independent 624** (JL 2006) – Kopparam plates (**631**) of Pulakeshin II call him **Yuvaraja**; Chipurupalli plates (**Vizianagaram**) of his **18th regnal year**.
[78:3:3] - Titles: **Vishamasiddhi** ("conqueror of difficult places / forts") (APPSC PYQ) – not Jayasimha I, Vijayaditya I/II; also **Makaradhwaja**, Kamadeva, Parama Bhagavata.
[78:3:4] # Identity
[78:3:5] - Branch of the **Badami Chalukyas**; initially **subordinates of Badami**; ruled **624–1075** (over four centuries); emblem **boar (Varaha)**; official language **Sanskrit** (most of ~100 grants); matronymic **Hariti-putra** (like the Satavahanas, Ikshvakus, Kadambas); seal sign manual **Tribhuvanankusa**.
[78:3:6] - **NOT true (ATWO):** they were **not annexed by the Rashtrakutas** – they survived the long Rashtrakuta wars and merged into the **Chola** line.
[78:3:7] - **Contemporaries in the south: the Pallavas** (Jayasimha I clashed with them) – not Ikshvakus (earlier), Mughals or Palas.
[78:3:8] - **Land measures:** **nivartana**, kharidika, kharis = units of **area** (land) (APPSC JA).
[78:3:9] # Rulers and titles in order
[78:3:10] TABLE head: Ruler | Title / event
  [78:3:10.0] Jayasimha I (Vallabha) | Samasta Bhuvanashraya; capital to Vengi; Vipparla
  [78:3:10.1] Kokkili Vikramaditya | An Eastern Chalukya prince; ousted by Vishnuvardhana III, set up a kingdom at Yalamanchili (Welfare Officers)
  [78:3:10.2] Vijayaditya I | Conflict with Dantidurga (Rashtrakuta) begins
  [78:3:10.3] Vishnuvardhana IV | Defeated by Dhruva, who married his daughter Sheela Mahadevi
  [78:3:10.4] Chalukya Bhima I | 360 battles (Pithapuram inscription of Mallappadeva); repelled Krishna II at Niravadyapura and Peruvanguru; court singer and dancer Chellavva, title Gandharva-Vidya-Visarada
  [78:3:10.5] Vijayaditya IV | Kollabhiganda (G2 2012; APPSC PYQ); ruled six months; died at Virajapuri fighting Kalinga – not Vijayaditya II, Gunaga or Chalukya Bhima
  [78:3:10.6] Ammaraja I | Rajamahendra; Chekurru plate
  [78:3:10.7] Danarnava | Magallu inscription; killed by Jatachoda Bhima
  [78:3:10.8] Saktivarma I | Chalukya Chandra; with Chola help beat Bayyalanambi of Satyasraya (1006)
  [78:3:10.9] Vimaladitya | Rajamartanda, Mummadi Bhima; married Kundavai; crowned 1011
  [78:3:10.10] Rajaraja Narendra | Crowned 1022; married Ammanga Devi (Rajendra Chola's daughter); after Koppam (1053) accepted Kalyani suzerainty
  [78:3:10.11] Vijayaditya VII | Last king; ousted Rajendra Chalukya in 1070; died 1075
[78:3:11] - Succession war: **Rajaraja Narendra** (son of Kundavai, backed by **Rajendra I**) vs **Vijayaditya VII** (son of Melama, backed by Kalyani Jayasimha II); **Rajadhiraja I** won at **Dharanikota**, burnt **Kollipaka**, was killed at **Koppam** by **Ahavamalla Someshwara**.
[78:3:12] - After **973** the main rival was the **Kalyani Chalukyas**.
[78:3:13] # Chola connection and the end
[78:3:14] - The **Cholas kept close political and matrimonial ties with the Chalukyas of Vengi** (G1 Re-exam 2010; G1 PYQ) – not the Pandyas or the Badami/Kalyani Chalukyas.
[78:3:15] - **Rajendra Chalukya = Kulottunga I** (married **Madhurantaki**, daughter of **Rajendra II**) united the two lines – first Chalukya-Chola king; the **Chalukya-Chola era / Mandalika Yugam** (1076–c.1210, Velanati Chodas) followed.
[78:3:16] - Andhra order: **Eastern Chalukyas → Kakatiyas → Reddis → Vijayanagara**; **Kesari Samudra** (Kesamudram) lake = Kakatiya **Prola I**.

[78:3:18] - **Rajaraja Narendra's titles:** **Kavyageetha Priyudu** (lover of poetry), Rajarajanarayana.

[78:4:2] - **Vengi school:** Vesara/Chalukya idiom; **local sandstone (not granite)**, raised jagati; leads into the **Chalukya-Chola** style.
[78:4:3] - **Match (APPSC JA):** Bhima – **Draksharama**; Eastern Chalukyas – **Pancharama kshetras**; Rajaraja Narendra – **Chalukya-Chola architecture**; Kakatiyas – **Shaivism almost destroyed Jainism** = A-3, B-4, C-2, D-1.
[78:4:4] # Religion
[78:4:5] - **Shaivism dominant** (Kapalika, Kalamukha, Pasupata sects); mother goddess **Hariti**; tolerant of Jainism and Buddhism.
[78:4:6] # Jain patronage
[78:4:7] - **Ayyana Mahadevi**, queen of **Kubja Vishnuvardhana**, built the Jain **Nedumba (Nadumbi) Vasati / Nedumbasadi** at **Vijayawada** (G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ) – recorded in the **Musinikonda grant**; not Mylama, Upasika Bodhisiri or Devi Naganika; not Gunaga Vijayaditya, Jatachoda Bhima or Jayasimha Vallabha.
[78:4:8] - **Vimaladitya** = the Eastern Chalukya king who **embraced Jainism** (guru **Trikalayogi Siddhanti**; Ramatirtha cave) (JA 2012) – not Gunaga Vijayaditya, Bhima I or Rajaraja Narendra.
[78:4:9] - **Vishnuvardhana III** granted **Mushikonda** to the Jains; **Ammaraja II** issued the **Maliyapundi** grant (Jain temple, Dharmapuri); courtesan **Chamekamba** won the **Kaluchumbarru** grant.
[78:4:10] - **Dynasty that most propagated Jainism in Telugu land = the Vemulawada Chalukyas** (Welfare Officers 2017 ×2) – patrons of **Pampa** and Somadeva Suri; Baddega built the Subhadhama Jinalaya – not the Eastern, Badami or Kalyani Chalukyas.

## SUB 5 | Vijayaditya II (Narendra Mrigaraja) – the 108 Shiva Temples
[78:5:0] - **Title Narendra Mrigaraja ("lion among kings") = Vijayaditya II** (APPSC PYQ; Welfare Officers 2017) – not Vishnuvardhana IV, Gunaga Vijayaditya or Chalukya Bhima; also Chalukya Rama, Vikramadhavala.
[78:5:1] - **108 Shiva temples – the Narendreswara / Narendra Mrigeswara-alayas = Vijayaditya II** (G2 2008 ×2, 2012, 2019; G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ) – built at the sites of his **108 battles in 12 years** with the Rashtrakutas (Govinda III, Amoghavarsha), **as penance**; recorded in the **Sataluru** inscription.
[78:5:2]   - **⚠ Settled:** one G2 printed key gave **Vijayaditya I**; the king is **Vijayaditya II** (when he is missing from the options, the correct answer is added).
[78:5:3]   - Not Kubja Vishnuvardhana, Vishnuvardhana III/IV, Gunaga Vijayaditya or Chalukya Bhima (Bhima I = 360 battles).
[78:5:4] - He overthrew his brother **Bhima Salki** and took the throne (c. 808/818).

## SUB 1 | Religion – Sects, Agamas and the Chalukya Mother Goddess
[79:1:0] # Family goddess
[79:1:1] - **Mother goddess of the early Chalukyas:** **Hariti** – the Chalukyas called themselves **Hariti-putras** of the **Manavya gotra**, nourished by the **Seven Mothers** (Saptamatrikas) (G2 2019).
[79:1:2]   - **⚠ Settled:** the printed key "Cushmandini" is wrong – **Kushmandini** is a **Jain yakshi**; Rocket and the Chalukya prasastis give **Hariti**.
[79:1:3] - **Same goddess, other dynasty:** Hariti was also the tutelary deity of the **Ikshvakus**; the metronymic "Hariti-putra" suggests a southern origin of the Eastern Chalukyas.
[79:1:4] # Shaivism and Vaishnavism
[79:1:5] - **Shaivism dominant:** sects **Pasupata** (founder **Lakulisha**, oldest, peaceful), **Kalamukha** (extremist) and **Kapalika** (Bhairava worship).
[79:1:6] - **Trilinga desa:** Draksharama, Kaleshwaram and Srisailam; Srisailam was called a Kapalika kshetra by Bhavabhuti.
[79:1:7] - **Vaishnava kings:** **Kubja Vishnuvardhana** (Kunti Madhava temple, Pithapuram) and **Danarnava** (Mandavya Narayana temple, Samalkot).
[79:1:8] - **Jayasimha I** performed the **Ashvamedha**; royal prasastis boast of many Ashvamedhas.
[79:1:9] - **Jayasimha II** brought the **Koya Kottalu** (Boya Kottas) into the Brahmanical fold.
[79:1:10] # Agamas and saint traditions
[79:1:11] TABLE head: Tradition | Faith | Point
  [79:1:11.0] Pancharatra | Vaishnava | Common to Ramanuja and Madhva (APPSC Endowment 2022)
  [79:1:11.1] Vaikhanasa | Vaishnava | Founded by Vikhanasa; followed at Srivari temple, Tirumala (APPSC Endowment 2022)
  [79:1:11.2] Nayanars | Saiva | 63 Tamil Shiva saints (Alvars = Vaishnava) (APPSC Endowment 2022)
[79:1:12] - **⚠ Trap:** Pancharatra and Vaikhanasa are **Vaishnava**, not Shaiva or Shakta.
[79:1:13] # Buddhism
[79:1:14] - **Declining:** Buddharamas became Shaiva temples (Dhanyakataka → Amareswara); Hiuen Tsang (639) found Buddhist sites decaying; survived at **Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda**.
[79:1:15] ~~ Sources: Rocket AP History sheet #7; NextGen Eastern Chalukyas p.7, p.27-29; NextGen Post-Kakatiyas p.13; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalukya_dynasty; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaikhanasa; app notes 1:79 (sub 3)
[79:1:16] - **Gramadevata (village goddess) shrines:** belong to the **Shakta (Sakteya)** tradition of mother-goddess worship – not Saiva, Pancharatra or Jaina (APPSC Endowment 2022).
[79:1:17] - **Faiths of the age:** Shaivism, Vaishnavism, Jainism and (declining) Buddhism coexisted under the Eastern Chalukyas; **Judaism** was not among them.

## SUB 3 | Society, Economy and Guilds
[79:3:0] # Society
[79:3:1] - **Varna order** with Brahmin privilege; Brahmins split into **Vaidikas** and **Niyogis** (administration and army).
[79:3:2] - **Agraharas:** tax-free villages for Brahmins, who performed **Hiranyagarbha** and **Bahusuvarna** rituals.
[79:3:3] - **Vaisyas = Komatis**; their guilds = **Nakarams**; Reddy, Kamma and Kapu communities grew strong.
[79:3:4] - **Panchanamvaru:** five artisan groups (blacksmith, goldsmith, brazier, carpenter, sculptor) appear as an organised body.
[79:3:5] - **Panduranga** (general of Gunaga Vijayaditya) **subdued the Boyas**, built Pandurangapuram on Pulicat lake and issued the Addanki verse inscription.

=== NOTES 1:80 | Eastern Chalukya Art and Architecture – Biccavolu, Draksharama and Samalkot Temples
## SUB 0 | Chalukya Bhima I – Pancharamas and the Twin Bhimeswara Temples
[80:0:0] # The builder
[80:0:1] - **Chalukya Bhima I** (892-922): fought **360 battles** (Pithapuram inscription of Mallapadeva) and is said to have built **360 temples**; "the Vengi ruler who built many temples" (APPSC PYQ).
[80:0:2] - **Pancharamas built by:** **Chalukya Bhima I**, i.e. under the **Vengi (Eastern) Chalukyas** (APPSC PYQ ×2) – not Kubja Vishnuvardhana, Pulakesin II, Rajaraja Narendra, Kakatiyas or Vijayanagara.
[80:0:3] - **Draksharama founder:** **Chalukya Bhima I** built the **Bhimeswara** temple (G2 1993); an **Eastern Chalukya** monument, not Pallava or Satavahana.
[80:0:4] - **Twin temples:** **Draksharama Bhimeswara** and **Kumararama Bhimeswara at Samalkot**, the latter in memory of his **son**.

## SUB 1 | Other Temple Builders – Vijayaditya II, Bikkavolu and Rajahmundry

[80:1:1] - **Vijayaditya II (Narendra Mrigaraja):** fought **108 battles in 12 years** against the Rashtrakutas (**Govinda III, Amoghavarsha I**) and their Gangavadi allies.
[80:1:2] - **108 Shiva temples** (**Narendreswara / Narendra Mrigeswara**) at the battle sites – recorded in the **Sataluru** inscription (G2 2008, 2012, 2019; statement a of G2 2025 ✓).

## SUB 2 | Vengi School and the Chalukya-Chola Style
[80:2:0] - **Vengi School:** mainly **Dravida** temples – **Pallava (Kanchi) + Badami Chalukya** influences with **local Andhra** features; local laterite and limestone; concentrated in the **Krishna-Godavari delta**.
[80:2:1] - **Vesara (Chalukya) style:** NextGen defines it as **Dravidian vimana + Buddhist** art; it belongs to the **Badami Chalukyas** and was brought into Andhra by the Vengi Chalukyas.
[80:2:2] - **Navabrahma temples (nine), Alampur:** **Badami Chalukyas**, 7th–8th c. – not Eastern Chalukyas.
[80:2:3] - **Chalukya-Chola model:** the **Draksharama Bhimeswara** temple blends **Pallava + early Chalukya** features into an independent style (G2 Mains 2025).

## SUB 0 | Bahmani–Vijayanagara Rivalry – Raichur Doab, Talikota and the Deccan Sultanates
[82:0:0] # Raichur Doab – the most-asked fact of this row
[82:0:1] - **Where:** the **Raichur Doab (Raichur Antarvedi)** lies between the **Krishna and Tungabhadra** (G2 Mains 2017; APPSC PYQ).
[82:0:2] - **Why fought over:** fertile black soil and a strategic position = **main cause of the Bahmani–Vijayanagara wars** from **Harihara I's** time (G2 2003, G2-B 1989, FRO; APPSC PYQ ×3).
[82:0:3]   - Answer words: "**acquisition of the Raichur Doab**" / "**acquisition of territory**" – not religious discord, tribute or the Krishna-Godavari region.
[82:0:4] - **Main opponents of Vijayanagara:** the **Bahmanis** (G1 2008), later the Deccan Sultanates.
[82:0:5] - **⚠ Trap:** Raichur Doab = Krishna–Tungabhadra; the **Krishna–Godavari** doab is the separate coastal tract contested by Reddis, Velamas, Gajapatis, Vijayanagara and the Bahmanis.

=== NOTES 1:83 | Minor Dynasties of Andhradesa – Chalukya-cholas, Kona Haihayas, Matsyas, Pusapati and Others
## SUB 0 | Palnadu War (Palnati Yuddham), 1182
[83:0:0] - **Where and when:** **Karempudi, 1182 CE** (Rocket); epic **Palnati Veera Charitra** by **Srinatha**; also the folk **Palnati Veerula Katha**.
[83:0:1] - **Rivals:** all sons of the Palnadu Haihaya **Alugaraju (Anugaraju)** – Nalagamaraju's faction vs the Malideva faction.
[83:0:2] TABLE head: Person | Role
  [83:0:2.0] Nalagamaraju | Son of Mailama Devi, daughter of Velanati Gonka II
  [83:0:2.1] Malideva and brothers | Half-brothers – rival faction, led by Brahmanaidu
  [83:0:2.2] Nayakuralu Nagamma | Nalagama's minister; Shaivite; built the Veerabhadra temple, Gurajala; her intrigue exiled Malideva and Brahmanaidu
  [83:0:2.3] Brahmanaidu | Minister of Malideva; Vira Vaishnava reformer
  [83:0:2.4] Balachandra | Brahmanaidu's son; hero killed in the war
[83:0:3] - **Cause of the war:** **Nagamma** – the rivalry of Nayakuralu Nagamma and Brahmanaidu (APPSC PYQ); Balachandra was a hero, not the cause.
[83:0:4] - **Brahmanaidu's faith:** follower of **Ramanujacharya** (Vira Vaishnavism) (APPSC PYQ).
[83:0:5] - **Dalit temple entry:** **Brahma Naidu** opened the **Chennakesava temple, Macherla** to all castes, including Dalits (APPSC PYQ).
[83:0:6] - **Chapa Koodu / Dasa Samaj:** his inter-caste dining; his followers became the **Maladasari** (dasa) community.
[83:0:7] - **Karempudi angle (G2 PYQ):** the **Velamas**, "intruders" into the Reddi-dominated economic life of Palnadu, were driven out in the **Battle of Karempudi**.
[83:0:8] - **Allies:** **Kota Bhimaraju II** joined Kakatiya **Rudradeva** to back Nalagamaraju.

## SUB 1 | Velanati Chodas
[83:1:0] - **Seat:** **Velanadu** (Bapatla, Tenali, Repalle, Guntur); capital **Chandavolu (Dhanadapuram)**.
[83:1:1] - **Line:** progenitor **Mallavarma**; founder **Gonka I** (Samasta Senadhipati of Vengi under the Chalukya-Cholas); greatest **Gonka II** (1132-1161).
[83:1:2] - **Origin:** not Telugu Chodas – ancestors were the **Kirtipura lords of Madhyadesha**; they named sons after their Chalukya-Chola overlords (first: **Rajendra Choda**).
[83:1:3] - **Pithapuram inscription** of Velanati **Prithvisvara** names **Kolleru Lake** (G2 Mains 2017; G2 PYQ) – not Chilka, Pulicat or Pakala.
[83:1:4] - **End:** Kakatiya **Ganapatideva** (1201 campaign) killed **Prithvisvara**, with Nellore Choda Tikkana's help, and later the last member **Mummadi Rajendra Choda**.
[83:1:5] # Marriage alliances
[83:1:6] - **Mailama (Mailamba)**, daughter of **Gonka II**, was married to the Palnadu Haihaya **Anugaraju**, who ruled Palnadu from **Gurajala** with Velanati support.
[83:1:7] - The **Kondapadumatis** were also tied to Velanadu by marriage.
[83:1:8] > Statement angle (NTR Univ. JA): "Rajendra Choda, son of Gonka II, married Mailamba, daughter of Gonkaraja" ✗ (Mailamba was his sister, married to Anugaraju); "Anama, sister of Buddharaja, married the Haihaya chief of Palnadu" – not in the sources → key "neither A nor B".
[83:1:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Kakatiyas (Ganapati's Velanati campaign, Prithvisvara); NextGen AP History Previous Papers p.8; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Palnadu; app notes 1:83 (sub 1, 2)
[83:1:10] - **Durjaya lineage:** the Velanati chiefs claimed descent from the **Durjaya** family, called themselves **Chaturthanvaya** (of the fourth, i.e. Sudra, caste) and were loyal feudatories of the **Chalukya-Cholas** after the Eastern Chalukya decline.
## SUB 2 | Kulottunga I and the Chalukya-Chola Viceroys of Vengi
[83:2:0] - **Kulottunga I (Rajendra Chalukya)**, son of Rajaraja Narendra and Ammanga Devi, = **first Chalukya-Chola king**: Chola throne **1070**, Vengi **1075** after the death of **Vijayaditya VII**, the last Eastern Chalukya → he **united the Chola and Eastern Chalukya crowns** (G1 1983-84).
[83:2:1] - **Married** **Madhurantaki**, daughter of the Chola **Rajendra**.
[83:2:2] - **Deeds:** sent **72 merchants to China**; built **Kulottunga Cholapuram** (Visakhapatnam); conquered **Kalinga by sea** → **Kalingattuparani** by **Jayangondar**.
[83:2:3] # Viceroys
[83:2:4] - **Titles taken:** the Eastern Chalukya **Sarvalokasraya, Sri Vishnuvardhana**; coronation at **Jananathapura (Draksharama)**, the provincial capital of Vengi.
[83:2:5] - **Viceroy crowned at Draksharama:** **Rajaraja Chodaganga** (G2 Mains 2025 key).
[83:2:6]   - The Chellur plates show Kulottunga's son **Vira Choda** also styled Vishnuvardhana; he is not an option.
[83:2:7] - **Vikrama Chola** served after Rajaraja Chodaganga and was recalled in **1118** as his father's **co-regent**; the line ended with **Parantaka Chola**.
[83:2:8] - **After Kulottunga:** Andhra became a web of **nadus** under vassals who accepted **Velanadu**, later **Kakatiya**, overlordship; he raised Velanati, Nellore, Konidena and Pottapi Chodas, Chagis and Kona Haihayas as Mandaleswaras.
[83:2:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Eastern Chalukyas p.21-22; NextGen AP History Previous Papers p.46; Rocket AP History sheet #1; SII Vol 1, Chellur plates of Vira Choda (https://www.whatisindia.com/inscriptions/south_indian_inscriptions/volume_1/vira_choda.html); app notes 1:83 (sub 0)
[83:2:10] - **Battle of Manneru:** **Kulottunga II**, sent to Vengi by Vikrama Chola, with the chiefs of **Velanadu, Giripaschima and Konakandravadi** drove out the **Western (Kalyani) Chalukyas** in the battle of **Manneru**.
