## SUB 0 | Religious Policy – Ibadat Khana, Mahzar, Din-i-Ilahi, Sulh-i-Kul
[129:0:0] # Measures in order
[129:0:1] TABLE head: Year | Measure
  [129:0:1.0] 1563 | Pilgrimage tax abolished – by Akbar (not Bahlul Lodi, Sher Shah or Humayun)
  [129:0:1.1] 1564 | Jizya abolished
  [129:0:1.2] 1575 | Ibadat Khana (House of Worship) built at Fatehpur Sikri – first for Ulemas, Sayyids, Shaikhs
  [129:0:1.3] 1576 | Haj caravans from the state treasury under a Mir Haj – Akbar was the first Indian ruler to organise the Haj at state expense (UPPSC 1998)
  [129:0:1.4] 1578 | Ibadat Khana opened to all religions – Hindus, Jains, Christians, Zoroastrians
  [129:0:1.5] 1579 | Mahzar – an attested statement of leading Ulemas making Akbar supreme arbiter in religious disputes; issued by Akbar, drafted by Shaikh Mubarak
  [129:0:1.6] 1582 | Din-i-Ilahi (Tauhid-i-Ilahi) launched
[129:0:2] - **Chronology:** **jizya 1564 → Ibadat Khana 1575 → Mahzar 1579 → Din-i-Ilahi 1582 = 1, 2, 3, 4** (UP UDA 2006).
[129:0:3] - **Ibadat Khana = the hall where Akbar held discussions with scholars of various religions** (UPSC 2014) – **built by Akbar (1575)**; not a royal mosque, a private prayer chamber or a nobles' room.
[129:0:4] - Guiding principle **Sulh-i-Kul** = "**peace with all**" (universal harmony) – **promulgated by Akbar** (not Nizamuddin Auliya, Zainul Abidin or Nasiruddin Chirag); the result of **political generosity, religious tolerance and a liberal cultural attitude – all three** (UPPCS 1998).
[129:0:5] - Coins with **Rama and Sita** and the Devanagari legend **"Rama-Siya"** – **Akbar** (UPPCS 2011), not Bhoja, Siddharaja or Zain-ul-Abidin.
[129:0:6] - Akbar is called **"an enlightened despot"** (UP Mains 2009).
[129:0:7] - Akbar's reign is known for **annexation, provincial administration, judicial administration – NOT religious intolerance** (1, 2, 3); his popularity rested on **mansabdari, religious policy, land revenue and social reforms – all** (UPPSC 1990).
[129:0:8] - He did **not** forsake Islam – that is the factor NOT conducive to calling him a nationalist (RAS 1994); unity in law, administration and culture and his religious policy were.
[129:0:9] - **Pilgrim tax at Banaras and Allahabad:** a deputation of Banaras pandits led by **Kavindracharya** (Sarasvati) persuaded **Shah Jahan** to end it (UPPCS 2000).

[129:0:12] # Din-i-Ilahi (1582)
[129:0:13] - **Promoted / promulgated by Akbar** (MPPSC 1998; State PSC); **launched 1582** (CGPSC 2011).
[129:0:14] - Features: **one God**, greeting "Allah-hu-Akbar", reverence for **sun and fire**, avoiding meat; piety, prudence, kindness.
[129:0:15] - **Never forced** – a voluntary small circle of about **18**, not a mass religion; **Abul Fazl** its chief exponent; **Birbal the only Hindu**; it **ended with Akbar's death**.
[129:0:16] - **Why it failed (SSC 2009):** not patronised after Akbar, rejected by orthodox Muslims, never projected to the masses – **all of the above**.
[129:0:17] - Debate: an ideology based on **Sufi pantheism**, not a religion; **Mohsin Fani** (Jahangir's time) called it a religion in **Dabistan-i-Mazahib** – none of **Abul Fazl, Badauni, Nizamuddin** (UPPCS 2000: "none of these").
[129:0:18] - Orthodox critics: **Badauni** and **Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi** (Naqshbandi, "Mujaddid Alf-i-Sani").

## SUB 1 | Accession, Bairam Khan's Regency and Conquests
[129:1:0] # Accession and regency
[129:1:1] - Born **15 October 1542** at **Amarkot**; enthroned **14 February 1556** at **Kalanaur (Gurdaspur, Punjab)** on news of Humayun's death (CGPSC 2015) – not Kabul, Lahore or Sirhind – by **Bairam Khan**.
[129:1:2] - **Age at coronation:** books and keys say **fourteen** ("only fourteen years old", NextGen); strictly **13 years 4 months** ("under 14", Rocket).
[129:1:3] - **Panipat II – 5 November 1556**: Hemu hit in the eye by an arrow; Afghan power ended.
[129:1:4] TABLE head: Phase | Years | Who dominated
  [129:1:4.0] Bairam Khan's regency | 1556-60 (JPSC 2010) | Bairam Khan – made Wakil/Wazir and given the title Khan-i-Khanan by Akbar at that time; dismissed 1560; killed 1561 on the way to Mecca
  [129:1:4.1] Petticoat government | 1560-64 | Maham Anaga and her son Adham Khan
[129:1:5] - **Adham Khan** was **personally killed by Akbar (1562)** for murdering the minister **Atagh (Atka) Khan** – not Bairam Khan, Baz Bahadur or Pir Muhammad.
[129:1:6] - Aurangzeb was crowned twice (21 July 1658 Delhi; 5 June 1659); his first coronation was NOT at Kalanaur.

[129:2:4] - Abul Fazl **shaped, represented and articulated the ideas of Akbar's reign**; his qualities made Akbar choose him as **adviser and spokesperson** – statement II explains I (NDA 2019; NCERT Themes II).

## SUB 3 | Rajput Policy, Mewar and Haldighati
[129:3:0] - Twin policy of **conquest and conciliation**: surrender followed by restoration of **autonomy and honour**; high posts, marriages, toleration.
[129:3:1] - **First marriage alliance – 1562**, with the daughter of **Raja Bharmal (Bihari Mal) of Amber – the Kachhwahas** (State PSC; UPRO 2016; UPPCS 2004) – not Rathores, Sisodias, Chauhans or Bundelas; on Akbar's pilgrimage to **Ajmer**.
[129:3:2] - Top Rajput mansabdars: **Man Singh, Bhagwan Das**; also Raja Todar Mal and Raja Birbal.
[129:3:3] - **Contemporaries of Akbar:** Man Singh, Amar Singh, Udai Singh – **Jaswant Singh** of Marwar belongs to Shah Jahan–Aurangzeb (WBCS 2018).
[129:3:4] # Mewar – the holdout
[129:3:5] - **Mewar did not accept Akbar's supremacy willingly** (UP Lower 2004) – Amber, Marwar, Bikaner did.
[129:3:6] - **Haldighati – 18 June 1576** (UKPSC 2010; CGPSC 2016) – **Maharana Pratap** vs Akbar's army under **Man Singh of Amber**; aim: **to subdue Rana Pratap** (RAS 1992).
[129:3:7] - **Hakim Khan Sur** commanded Pratap's army (vanguard of Afghans) – not Amar Singh, Man Singh or Shakti Singh.
[129:3:8] - Rana Pratap employed **Baksariya Muslims** in his army (APPSC G1 2019 key).
[129:3:9] - Mewar submitted only under **Jahangir** – **Amar Singh**'s treaty, **1615**.
[129:3:10] - **Rao Chandrasena of Marwar** never surrendered (Bhadrajun 1571, Siwana; died 1581) – Rai Singh of Bikaner and Bharmal submitted; Amar Singh submitted to Jahangir (RAS 2012).
[129:3:11] - Pairs: Chausa = 1539 (not 1538), Kannauj = 1540 (not 1539), **Haldighati = 1576**, Khajwa = 1659 (not 1657).
[129:3:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History Part 4 p.26-27; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian History Part 1 p.73-74; app notes 1:129 (sub 2)
## SUB 4 | Social and Educational Reforms
[129:4:0] - **Sati** was prohibited / curbed under **Akbar** (WBCS 2018) – widows not to be burnt against their will.
[129:4:1] - **Monogamy order:** a man to marry **only one wife**, a second only if the first was **barren** – **Akbar** (UPPCS 2002).
[129:4:2] - **Marriage age fixed** (16 for boys, 14 for girls) and **consent of bride and groom** required – **both statements true** (UPPSC 2020).
[129:4:3] - Discouraged child marriage, dowries, marriages between close relatives; allowed widow remarriage.
[129:4:4] - **Educational reforms:** **Akbar** (MPPSC 2014) – curriculum of arithmetic, logic, geometry, astronomy, accountancy, agriculture, ethics, medicine, history.
[129:4:5] - **Memoirs:** Babur – Tuzuk-i-Baburi in **Turki**; Jahangir – Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri in **Persian**; Akbar wrote none (Abul Fazl's Akbarnama); Humayun was learned in Persian, Arabic and Turkish.
[129:4:6] - **Akbar was unlettered (illiterate)** but a great patron of learning (APPSC G2 2011) – Babur, Jahangir and Aurangzeb were literate.

## SUB 0 | Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and the Aligarh Movement
[192:0:0] # Sir Syed (1817-1898)
[192:0:1] - In the Company's **judicial service** in 1857; **loyalist**; knighted 1888; **never connected with the Congress** – asked Muslims to keep away from it (Ferozshah Mehta, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Ghaffar Khan were all Congressmen).
[192:0:2] - Aim: modernise Muslims through **English / Western scientific education** and a **rational reading of the Quran**; promoted **Urdu**; opposed purdah and polygamy.
[192:0:3] - Early view: India a bride, Hindus and Muslims "**her two eyes**"; turned after the **Hindi-Urdu controversy (1867)**.

  [192:0:4.0] Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind | 1859 | Sir Syed, "Causes of the Indian Revolt"

## SUB 1 | Faraizi, Taiyuni and Ahmadiyya Movements
[192:1:0] - **Faraizi (Fara'idi) Movement:** founded **1818** by **Haji Shariatullah** in **East Bengal (Faridpur)** – the Faraizis = **followers of Haji Shariatullah** (not Dadu Dayal, Arya Samaj or Muslim League); aim: enforce Islamic duties (*faraiz*), purge un-Islamic practices.
[192:1:1] - **Dudu (Dadu) Miyan**, his son, made it revolutionary from **1840** – peasants against **zamindars and indigo planters**; told followers not to pay rent; arrested 1847, died 1862 → **leader of the Farazi rebellion** (not Wazir Ali, Shamsher Gazi, Aga Muhammad Raza).
[192:1:2] - Founder vs leader trap: *founder* = Haji Shariatullah; *leader / rebellion* = Dudu Miyan.
[192:1:3] - **Taiyuni movement:** **Karamat Ali Jaunpuri** (Bengal) – opposed Faraizi radicalism; pairing it with Rashid Ahmad Gangohi (Deoband) is the **wrong pair**.
[192:1:4] - **Ahmadiyya:** **Mirza Ghulam Ahmad, Qadian (Gurdaspur, Punjab), 1889** (not 1866); claimed to be the Messiah; liberal, spread Western education, **opposed jihad**; rejected by orthodox Muslims.

## SUB 2 | Wahabi Movement – Barelvi, Patna, Anti-British; Hyderabad Angle
[192:2:0] - Inspired by **Abdul Wahab of Arabia** and **Shah Waliullah** (1702-63); popularised by Shah Abdul Aziz; Indian founder **Syed Ahmad Barelvi** (of Rae Bareli).
[192:2:1] - Aim: return to pure Islam; convert **Dar-ul-Harb into Dar-ul-Islam**; fought the **Sikhs** in Punjab, then after 1849 the **British** → character: **anti-British** (APPSC G2 2011, three versions) – not pro-British, anti-Muslim or merely pro-Muslim.
[192:2:2] - **Chief centre: Patna** (base at Sithana in the NW tribal belt) – not Lahore, Amritsar or Pune.
[192:2:3] - Main followers: **Muslim peasants and weavers / artisans**; trials at **Ambala (1864)** and **Patna (1865)**; crushed in the 1870s.
[192:2:4] - **Titu Mir** (Narkelberia, 1831), a disciple of Barelvi, led Muslim peasants of Bengal against zamindars and indigo planters.
[192:2:5] - **Hyderabad:** Wahabi conspiracy **1839** led by **Mubariz-ud-Daula**, the Nizam's brother, who resisted British influence in the Deccan; arrested 1839, died in prison.
[192:2:6] - **Nasaraiah** (Sayyid Muslim, d. 1825): popular among the **Madigas**; preached **one God**, opposed idol worship; **Vinukonda–Narasaraopet** (Guntur).
[192:2:7] - **Osmania University** (Hyderabad, 1918) taught through **Urdu**.

## SUB 0 | Lahore Conspiracy Case (1929-31), the Executions and Jatin Das
[209:0:0] # The case
[209:0:1] - **Trial:** Bhagat Singh, **Sukhdev**, **Rajguru** and others were tried for the **Saunders murder** (and bomb-making) in the **Lahore Conspiracy Case** before a **special tribunal set up by the Governor-General**; the **death sentence came in October 1930**.
[209:0:2] - Bhagat Singh had already got life in the **Assembly Bomb Case**; the sentence waited for the verdict in the Saunders (Lahore) case.
[209:0:3] - The accused turned the **court into a forum of propaganda**; the **defence was organised by Congress leaders**.
[209:0:4] - **"Second" Lahore case:** the **first Lahore Conspiracy Case (1915)** was the **Ghadar** case – the first trial under the **Defence of India Act, 1915** (passed mainly to deal with the Ghadarites).

=== NOTES 1:238 | Andhra in the Civil Disobedience Movement and the Salt Satyagraha
## SUB 0 | The Dictator and the Organisation in Andhra
[238:0:0] - **Konda Venkatappayya** (had met Gandhi with an Andhra team; "Father of the Andhra State Movement") was appointed **Dictator for Andhra** by the **Andhra Congress Committee** to conduct the **Salt Satyagraha / CDM** – the first leader of the CDM in Andhra (APPSC G2 2000, 2008, 2017) – not Prakasam, Pattabhi, Gadicherla or N.G. Ranga.
[238:0:1] - **Each district had its own dictator, a war council and a military-style camp (sibiram)** – the camps served as salt satyagraha headquarters (idea credited to **Pattabhi Sitaramayya**).
[238:0:2] TABLE head: Camp | Place
  [238:0:2.0] Guntur camp | Guntur
  [238:0:2.1] Pallepadu Ashram | Nellore
  [238:0:2.2] Bellary camp | Rayalaseema
  [238:0:2.3] Vedavanam (T. Prakasam's house) | Madras – the centre of Salt Satyagraha activity in Madras (not Krishna district)
  [238:0:2.4] Eluru National School | West Godavari – volunteers joined (May 1930)
[238:0:3] - **Programme:** towns – **picketing foreign-cloth shops**; villages – **making salt, cutting toddy palms, picketing toddy and arrack shops, raiding salt depots**; main target the **salt tax**. Later echo: women's anti-arrack agitation at **Dubagunta (Nellore), 1991**.
[238:0:4] - Spectrum: district salt marches in East and West Godavari, Krishna and Guntur; merchants funded the Congress; but mass support of the NCM level was missing.

## SUB 1 | Kakinada Bomb Case, Repression and End
[238:1:0] - **Kakinada Bomb Case:** circle inspector **Mustafa Ali** brutally attacked the satyagrahis of the **Seethanagaram (Sita) Ashram**; in revenge **Prativadi Bhayankarachari**, founder of the revolutionary "**Ujjivana Bharata Samithi / Sammelan**" in the **Godavari district**, threw a bomb at him at Kakinada (failed); he was sentenced (14 years) and sent to the **Andaman Cellular Jail**; he wrote "**Andaman Jeevitam**". NextGen heads the case "1930" (the year of the ashram raid); the app's MCQ dates the bomb case **1933**.
[238:1:1] - Repression: **Venneti Satyanarayana** lathi-charged at **Rajahmundry**; **15 January 1932** police firing at **Berhampore** (Willingdon's repression).
[238:1:2] - **Viceroys:** **Irwin** – Dandi March 1930, Pact 1931; **Willingdon** – repression of 1932-34.

## SUB 2 | Jai Andhra 1972–73, Six-Point Formula and 32nd Amendment
[263:2:0] # Jai Andhra
[263:2:1] - **1972–73**, Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema – **against the Mulki Rules**; demand: repeal or a **separate Andhra State**.
[263:2:2] - **Main / immediate cause:** the **Supreme Court judgment of October 1972** upholding the Mulki Rules – not taxes, water or administration.
[263:2:3] - Main issue: Andhras treated as "outsiders" in Hyderabad, a capital built with the whole State's revenue.
[263:2:4] - Leaders: **Kakani Venkataratnam**, **B.V. Subba Reddy**, **Tenneti Viswanatham**; students the core.
[263:2:5] - **CM: P.V. Narasimha Rao** (sworn in 30 Sep 1971); resigned **18 Jan 1973**.
[263:2:6] - **Five-Point Formula – 27 Nov 1972** (PM Indira Gandhi): Mulki rules in twin cities till **1977**, Telangana till **1980**; **1 in 3** Secretariat posts to Telangana locals; first two gazetted posts localised.
[263:2:7] - Separate Andhra **flag hoisted 10 Dec 1972**.
[263:2:8] - **President's Rule: 18 Jan – 10 Dec 1973** (~11 months) – the **first** in Andhra Pradesh and the **longest**; imposed because of the Jai Andhra agitation.
[263:2:9] - **Jalagam Vengala Rao** CM from **10 Dec 1973**.
[263:2:10] - **Ended by the Six-Point Formula** (21 Sep 1973); agitation called off **1 Oct 1973** – not by repression, arrests or mere Mulki abolition.

  [263:2:12.0] 1 | Mulki Rules and Telangana Regional Committee abolished | TRC dissolved 1 Jan 1974
