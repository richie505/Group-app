[131:0:2] # Shah Jahan (1628-58)
[131:0:3] - Crowned at **Agra, 1628**; early revolts: Bundela **Jujhar Singh**, **Khan Jahan Lodi**; Portuguese beaten at **Hughli (1632)** for trading abuses.

[131:0:5] - **Poet laureate: Abu Talib Kalim (Kaleem)**; official historian **Abdul Hamid Lahori – Padshahnama**; **Inayat Khan – Shahjahannama**.
[131:0:6] - **Captivity:** deposed 1658 and kept in **Agra Fort** (nursed by Jahanara) till death in **1666** – Aurangzeb imprisoned his father for life.

## SUB 1 | Jahangir (1605-27) – Events of the Reign and Nur Jahan's Junta
[131:1:0] # Jahangir – key events
[131:1:1] - **Accession:** Prince **Salim** (named after **Sheikh Salim Chishti** of Sikri; mother Jodha Bai/Harkha Bai), revolted against Akbar c. 1599-1604; crowned **1605** as **Nur-ud-din Muhammad Jahangir**.
[131:1:2] TABLE head: Event | Fact
  [131:1:2.0] Khusrau's revolt | Eldest son (mother Man Bai = Shah Begum) rebelled 1606; defeated near Lahore, imprisoned, blinded
  [131:1:2.1] Guru Arjan Dev (5th Guru) | Executed 1606 for backing Khusrau
  [131:1:2.2] Mewar – Treaty of Chittor | 1615: Rana Amar Singh submitted; Mewar and Chittor returned; son Karan Singh made a mansabdar
  [131:1:2.3] Deccan | 1616: Khan-i-Khanan beat Ahmadnagar, Bijapur, Golconda with Maratha sardars; Malik Ambar rose
  [131:1:2.4] Kangra | Annexed 1620
  [131:1:2.5] Kandahar | Lost to Persia (Shah Abbas I) in 1622 – NOT annexed by Jahangir
  [131:1:2.6] Khurram's revolt | 1622-26; ordered to Kandahar, refused; Nur Jahan backed Shahryar
  [131:1:2.7] Mahabat Khan's coup | 1626: seized Jahangir on the Jhelum
  [131:1:2.8] English | Hawkins 1608-09, factory at Surat 1613, Roe 1615-19
[131:1:3] - **Rebels against Jahangir:** **Khusrau, Khurram, Mahabat Khan** – NOT **Asaf Khan** (UPPSC 2013).
[131:1:4] - **Events of Jahangir's reign (APPSC G1):** Arjan Dev's execution, Mewar's submission, the English arrival – **"annexation of Kabul and Kandahar" is the trap**.
[131:1:5] - **Shah Begum (Man Bai, Manbhawati Bai)** – Jahangir's Rajput wife, mother of Khusrau – **committed suicide (opium), 1605** over the father-son quarrel (UPPSC 2008).
[131:1:6] - **Orchha:** Bundela **Bir Singh Deo** of Orchha was Salim's ally (killed Abul Fazl, 1602, for him); the **Jahangir Mahal** at Orchha was built for him – the printed key for "fort where Jahangir took shelter".
[131:1:7] - Chain of justice; **Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri**; lived mostly at Lahore; painting at its zenith; pietra dura began.
[131:1:8] # Kandahar not recaptured under Jahangir (CDS 2000)
[131:1:9] - Khurram **refused to go**, an expedition was **hard to organise**, the **cold** of Afghanistan – (printed key II, III, IV).
[131:1:10] # Nur Jahan and the junta
[131:1:11] - **Mehr-un-Nisa** (Mehrunnisa), born **1577** at Kandahar; daughter of **Mirza Ghiyas Beg**; widow of **Sher Afghan**; married Jahangir **1611**; titles **Nur Mahal → Nur Jahan** ("Light of the World"), **Padshah Begum**; pen name **"Makhfi"**.
[131:1:12] - **Childhood name = Mehr-un-Nisa** ("Maharul Nisa" in APPSC's option) – the APPSC key's **"Bahu Begam" is wrong**.
[131:1:13] - **Name on coins and farmans** – the only Mughal consort with this privilege; built the **Tomb of Itimad-ud-Daulah, Agra** for her father.

  [131:1:14.3] Asaf Khan | Brother; Shah Jahan's father-in-law
  [131:1:14.4] Prince Khurram | Married her niece Arjumand Banu Begum (1612)

[131:1:19] - **Religious policy:** Jahangir broadly continued **Akbar's tolerance**; Guru Arjan Dev's execution (1606) was political – for backing Khusrau.
[131:1:20] - **Jahangir's memoirs and bans:** wrote the **Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri in Persian** (Babur's memoirs were in Turki); **banned tobacco** smoking (1617).
[131:1:21] - **Emperor–amir pairs (IAS 2004):** Babur – Tardi Beg; Humayun – Bairam Khan; Aurangzeb – Jaswant Singh; **Sawai Jai Singh** rose under Aurangzeb and the later Mughals – **NOT Jahangir**.

## SUB 4 | Central Asian Policy – Kandahar and Balkh
[131:4:0] TABLE head: Campaign | Commander | Result
  [131:4:0.0] Kandahar recovered, 1638 | Persian governor Ali Mardan Khan surrendered it | Mughal gain (lost 1622 → regained 1638)
  [131:4:0.1] Balkh-Badakhshan, 1646-47 | Murad Bakhsh, then Aurangzeb (not Dara) | Failed, heavy losses
  [131:4:0.2] Kandahar lost, 1649 | – | To Persia (Shah Abbas II)
  [131:4:0.3] Sieges 1649, 1652 | Aurangzeb | Failed
  [131:4:0.4] Siege 1653 | Dara Shikoh | Failed – Kandahar lost for good
[131:4:1] - **Aim of the Balkh campaign:** to set up a **friendly ruler in Balkh and Badakhshan, which bordered Kabul** – not to conquer Samarqand/Farghana or fix the Amu Darya "scientific line" (UPSC 2002).
[131:4:2] - **Kandahar:** frontier fortress on the routes India–Persia–Central Asia – its loss was a blow as a **strategic stronghold** (UPSC 1998).
[131:4:3] - **UPSC 1998 A-R:** Dara led only the **Qandahar** siege (1653); Balkh-Badakhshan were Murad and Aurangzeb; the expeditions **failed** – so R is false.
[131:4:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 04 p.29; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.110; Satish Chandra, Medieval India II (Balkh aim); old app notes 1:131 (sub 5)
## SUB 5 | Deccan Policy of Shah Jahan
[131:5:0] - **1633:** **Daulatabad** fell to Mahabat Khan when **Fath Khan** (son of **Malik Ambar**) surrendered it – Malik Ambar had **died in 1626**; **1636:** Nizam Shahi **Ahmadnagar** extinguished.
[131:5:1] - **1636 ahdnamas** with **Bijapur and Golconda**: Mughal suzerainty, tribute, Mughal arbitration; Golconda's **Inqiyad Nama** (Abdullah Qutb Shah) put Shah Jahan's name in **khutba and coins**.
[131:5:2] - Four Deccan subas: **Khandesh, Berar, Telangana, Daulatabad**.
[131:5:3] - **Aurangzeb – viceroy of the Deccan** for most of the reign: **1636-44** and **1652/53-58**; attacked Golconda in **1656**, urged by **Mir Jumla**.
[131:5:4] TABLE head: Kingdom | Annexed | Last ruler
  [131:5:4.0] Bijapur | 1686 | Sikandar Adil Shah
  [131:5:4.1] Golconda | 1687 | Abul Hasan Tana Shah – exiled to Daulatabad
[131:5:5] - AP angle: Jahangir's envoy to Golconda **Mir Makki**; Tana Shah employed **Madanna and Akkanna** and helped Shivaji.

=== NOTES 1:132 | Aurangzeb – Religious Policy, Deccan Policy and Resistance
## SUB 0 | Religious Policy, Titles and Coronations
[132:0:0] - Regnal title **Alamgir**; epithet **"Zinda Pir"** (living saint) / Darvesh for his simple, pious life.
[132:0:1] - **Double coronation:** 1658 (after Samugarh) and **1659** (after Deorai). **Child's War (1686-90)** – first Mughal war with the English.
[132:0:2] TABLE head: Measure | Detail
  [132:0:2.0] Jizya | Re-imposed 1679 (Akbar had abolished it, 1564)
  [132:0:2.1] Temples | 1669 order – Kashi Vishwanath and Keshava Rai (Mathura) demolished
  [132:0:2.2] Muhtasibs | Officers who saw that people lived by the Sharia
  [132:0:2.3] Court customs | Jharokha darshan, tuladan, Navroz stopped; music banned at court – yet he played the veena
  [132:0:2.4] Zawabit | State-made secular regulations
[132:0:3] - **Jihad** = **holy war** – war to turn **dar-ul-harb** (land of non-believers) into **dar-ul-Islam**; the BPSC 1999 printed key "Dar-ul-Islam" is the aim, not the meaning.
[132:0:4] - Hindu nobles rose to about **31.6% (1679-1707)** (Athar Ali), many of them Marathas.
[132:0:5] - Titles of royal women: **Roshanara = Padshah Begum**; **Jahanara = Sahibat-uz-Zamani**.
[132:0:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 04 p.30-31; old app notes 1:132 (sub 0-1); Oxford Dictionary of Islam "jihad" (holy war)
[132:0:7] - **Jizya exemptions:** women, **children below 14**, slaves, beggars and the unemployed.
## SUB 1 | Resistance – Jats, Satnamis, Rajputs and Sikhs
[132:1:0] TABLE head: Revolt | Leader | Region / fact
  [132:1:0.0] Jat (1669) | Gokula | Mathura (Tilpat); peasant revolt against heavy revenue
  [132:1:0.1] Jat (1680s) | Rajaram | Plundered Akbar's tomb at Sikandra
  [132:1:0.2] Jat (after 1700) | Churaman | Built the power that became Bharatpur
  [132:1:0.3] Satnami (1672) | Satnami sect | Narnaul (Haryana); peasants and artisans – Aurangzeb's reign
[132:1:1] - **Agrarian roots:** the **Satnami and Jat** revolts grew out of peasants' problems – the Rajput, Sikh and Maratha conflicts did not (MPPSC 2010).
[132:1:2] - **Bharatpur state:** built by **Churaman and Badan Singh** (Badan Singh founded it, 1722); **Suraj Mal** (r. 1755-63) – **"the Plato of the Jat tribe"**.
[132:1:3] - Satnamis of 1672 ≠ **Guru Ghasidas** (19th-century Satnampanth, Chhattisgarh); Jat Rajaram ≠ Maratha Rajaram.
[132:1:4] # Rajputs – Marwar and Mewar (1679-81)
[132:1:5] - **Aurangzeb changed the Rajput policy** of Akbar, Jahangir and Shah Jahan (SSC 2019).
[132:1:6] - **Trigger:** **Jaswant Singh** of Marwar died **1678**; Aurangzeb took Marwar under direct control; posthumous son **Ajit Singh** championed by **Durgadas Rathore**; **Rana Raj Singh** of Mewar joined.
[132:1:7] - **Prince Akbar** (Aurangzeb's son; "Akbar II" in UPPSC options) **revolted, joined the Rajputs (1681)** → fled to Sambhaji → Persia – weakened Aurangzeb against the Rajputs.
[132:1:8] - Result: the Rajput alliance – pillar of Akbar's empire – broke.
[132:1:9] # Sikhs and others
[132:1:10] - **Guru Tegh Bahadur** (9th Guru) executed at Delhi, **1675**; **Guru Gobind Singh** wrote the **Zafarnama** after **Chamkaur (1704)**.
[132:1:11] - Afghans: **Yusufzai and Afridi** risings.
[132:1:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 04 p.30-31; old app notes 1:132 (sub 2-4); Satish Chandra, Medieval India II (Jat state, Suraj Mal)
## SUB 2 | Deccan Policy – Bijapur, Golconda and the Marathas
[132:2:0] - **Phases (NextGen):** 1658-68 recover Bijapur's 1636 gains; 1668-84 alliance against the Marathas; **1686-87 outright annexation**; **1687-1707** war with the Marathas.
[132:2:1] - Aurangzeb **spent the last ~25-27 years of his reign in the Deccan** – more than any other Mughal ("Deccan ulcer").
[132:2:2] # Bijapur (1686)
[132:2:3] - Siege 1685-86, Aurangzeb in person; fell **September 1686**; last ruler **Sikandar Adil Shah**.
[132:2:4] # Golconda (1687)
[132:2:5] - Ministers **Madanna-Akkanna** murdered (1686); siege 1687; **Abdur Razzaq Lari** defended; traitor **Abdullah Pani** opened the gate – fort taken with "**keys of gold**" (as Akbar took Asirgarh).
[132:2:6] - Ruler: **Abul Hasan Qutb Shah (Tana Shah)** – exiled to **Daulatabad**, died 1699, buried at **Khuldabad**.
[132:2:7] - AP angle: ended **Qutb Shahi rule over Andhra**; **Bhakta Ramadasu** was the nephew of Madanna-Akkanna.
[132:2:8] - **Greatest extent:** with Bijapur, Golconda and the chase of the Marathas to **Jinji**, the empire reached **Tamil territory** under Aurangzeb; the UPSC 2000 map (Bijapur and Golconda inside) = **Aurangzeb's empire at his death, 1707**.
[132:2:9] # Marathas
[132:2:10] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [132:2:10.0] 1666 | Shivaji at Agra – insulted, house arrest, escaped in fruit baskets
  [132:2:10.1] 1689 | Sambhaji captured at Sangameshwar, executed
  [132:2:10.2] 1689-1700 | Rajaram resisted from Jinji; died at Sinhagad (1700)
  [132:2:10.3] From 1700 | Tarabai led the war as regent for Shivaji II
[132:2:11] # Last years
[132:2:12] - **Died 1707 at Ahmednagar**; simple grave at **Khuldabad** (near Aurangabad).

=== NOTES 1:133 | Mughal Administration – Mansabdari, Jagirdari and the Jagirdari Crisis
## SUB 0 | Mansabdari System – Origin, Purpose and Nature
[133:0:0] - Introduced by **Akbar** (1570s); "Mansab" = **Arabic** for **rank/position**; model **borrowed from Mongolia/Central Asia** (Genghis Khan's decimal army grading) – not Persia, Turkey or Afghanistan.
[133:0:1] - **One grading for army and civil service** – the Mughal **military system** was based on it; **not all mansabdars were military officers** – high civil officials were mansabdars too, graded alike.
[133:0:2] - **Purpose (UPSC 1996 key):** "**effecting clean administration**" – a single graded service (Ghatna Chakra/key); army recruitment is the tempting option.
[133:0:3] - Run by the **Mir Bakhshi** – responsible for its smooth functioning.
[133:0:4] - Ranks **10 to 10,000**; Abul Fazl: **66 grades** in theory, **33** in practice; highest ranks for princes.
[133:0:5] - **Not hereditary** – rank and jagir ended with the holder; appointment, promotion and dismissal at the emperor's will (on merit/ability).
[133:0:6] - Paid in cash (**naqd**) or by a revenue assignment (**jagir**). Mughal state = basically a military state.
[133:0:7] - **UPPSC 2010 statements:** zat (and sawar) ranks granted ✓; **three classes** ✓; hereditary ✗ – printed key "only 1 & 3 true".
[133:0:8] - **Statements (APPSC ASO):** Babur founded the empire at **Panipat 1526**; Akbar's mansabdari; decline after **1707** – but the **Taj Mahal was Shah Jahan's**, not Aurangzeb's.
[133:0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 04 p.32; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.106; old app notes 1:133 (sub 0)
## SUB 1 | Zat, Sawar, Classes and Later Changes
[133:1:0] TABLE head: Term / class | Meaning
  [133:1:0.0] Zat | Personal rank – status in the hierarchy and salary
  [133:1:0.1] Sawar | Number of cavalrymen (horsemen) to be maintained
  [133:1:0.2] First class | Sawar = zat
  [133:1:0.3] Second class | Sawar half or more of zat
  [133:1:0.4] Third class | Sawar less than half of zat
[133:1:1] - **Three classes** of mansabdars; **sawar could NOT exceed zat**.
[133:1:2] - **Chehra** is the descriptive roll, **not a rank** – ranks were only zat and sawar (CDS 2009).
[133:1:3] - Nobles (**umara**): mansabdars of **1,000 zat or above** (NCERT) – a "1,000 sawar" statement is wrong.
[133:1:4] - **Horse rule**: every **10 sawars → 20 horses**.
[133:1:5] TABLE head: Change | Emperor | Effect
  [133:1:5.0] Du-aspa sih-aspa | Jahangir | Part of sawar counted double; zat not raised
  [133:1:5.1] Month-scale | Shah Jahan | Shashmaha = half-yield; sihmaha = quarter-yield
  [133:1:5.2] Reduced obligation | Shah Jahan | Keep 1/5 to 1/3 of sawar
  [133:1:5.3] Mashrut | Aurangzeb | Temporary, conditional rise in sawar for a task
[133:1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 04 p.32-33; NCERT Class 7 Our Pasts II ch.4; old app notes 1:133 (sub 1)

## SUB 3 | Jagirs and the Jagirdari Crisis
[133:3:0] TABLE head: Type | Nature
  [133:3:0.0] Tankhwah | In lieu of salary; transferable
  [133:3:0.1] Watan | Hereditary home of a zamindar/Rajput chief; not transferred
  [133:3:0.2] Mashrut | Conditional
  [133:3:0.3] In'am | Free of service; land to learned and religious men
  [133:3:0.4] Altamgha | Perpetual
  [133:3:0.5] Khalisa | Crown land – income straight to the treasury; NOT a jagir
[133:3:1] - **Jagir** = territorial assignment to mansabdars **in lieu of salary**; **Mauza = village** (rebellious tract = mawas) – Astt. Comm. 2017 wrong pair.
[133:3:2] - **Madad-i-maash:** revenue-free grants to scholars and religious men, under the Sadr.
[133:3:3] - Transfers every **3-4 years** – to stop local roots; **bejagiri** – mansabdars waiting without jagirs.
[133:3:4] - Under **Aurangzeb**: more mansabdars, too little good land (**paibaqi**) → peasants squeezed → core of **Mughal decline**.
[133:3:5] TABLE head: Historian | Explanation
  [133:3:5.0] Satish Chandra | Shortage of jagirs
  [133:3:5.1] Irfan Habib | Agrarian crisis
  [133:3:5.2] Athar Ali | Influx of Deccani and Maratha nobles
[133:3:6] - AP angle: **Asaf Jahi Hyderabad** kept Mughal-style jagirdari.

## SUB 3 | Mansabdar–Jagirdar–Zamindar Linkage
[134:3:0] - 1. **Mansabdar** gets a rank → paid by a **tankhwah jagir**; 2. jagirdar's agents collect the assessed revenue (**jama**) through **zamindars**, who keep a hereditary share (**nankar**); 3. the **peasant** pays about one-third.
[134:3:1] TABLE head: Jagirdar | Zamindar
  [134:3:1.0] Revenue assignment in lieu of salary for state (mainly military) service – no judicial or police duty | Hereditary rights over land/revenue in a locality; also kept order, supplied troops
  [134:3:1.1] Not hereditary; transferred often | Hereditary, saleable rights
[134:3:2] - **UPSC 2019:** both statements wrong – jagirs were for salary, not judicial/police duty, and were **not hereditary**, while zamindari rights **were** – answer **neither A nor B**.
[134:3:3] - Conflicts: short-tenure jagirdars pressed for more → zamindars resisted → peasants fled (local face of the jagirdari crisis).
[134:3:4] TABLE head: Category (S. Nurul Hasan) | Rights and role
  [134:3:4.0] Autonomous chiefs | Rajas ruling their own territories
  [134:3:4.1] Intermediary | Collected revenue from primary zamindars for the state; got a commission
  [134:3:4.2] Primary | Proprietary, hereditary rights over cultivated land
[134:3:5] - **Three-fold classification** of Mughal zamindars (autonomous chiefs, intermediary, primary) – by historian **S. Nurul Hasan**.

## SUB 0 | Position of Women – Royal Women, Purdah and Property
[136:0:0] TABLE head: Royal woman | Exam fact
  [136:0:0.0] Gulbadan Begum | Babur's daughter (Humayun's sister); wrote the Humayun-nama – the woman who wrote a historical record of the period (UPPCS 1998)
  [136:0:0.1] Maham Anga | Akbar's foster-mother; built the Khair-ul-Manazil mosque-madrasa opposite the Purana Qila, Delhi (1561), known as the Madarsa-e-Begum (UPPSC 2012, 2018) – not Gulbadan or Zinat-un-nisa
  [136:0:0.2] Nur Jahan | Married Jahangir 1611; Padshah Begum; the only empress whose name appeared on coins and farmans; built Itimad-ud-Daula's tomb, Agra
  [136:0:0.3] Jahanara | Shah Jahan's daughter, Padshah Begum; built the Jama Masjid, Agra and a sarai at Chandni Chowk
  [136:0:0.4] Zeb-un-Nissa | Aurangzeb's daughter; poet, pen name Makhfi
[136:0:1] - **Status of women (NextGen):** élite women were educated and some (Nur Jahan, Mumtaz Mahal, Jahanara, Chand Bibi, Tarabai) influenced politics; in general **purdah, child marriage, sati, polygamy** and the ban on widow remarriage lowered their status (Bernier, Tavernier report sati, child marriage, polygamy). Akbar tried to stop **forced sati**.
[136:0:2] - **Purdah:** Muslim women strictly veiled, carried in covered palanquins; Hindu women of the Deccan moved freely (**John Fryer**, *A New Account of East India and Persia*).
[136:0:3] - **Property:** Muslim women – inheritance shares + **mehr** (the dower a Muslim bridegroom pays the bride, APPSC 2000) under sharia; Hindu women – **stridhana**.

## SUB 1 | Education and Urban Classes
[136:1:0] - **Firangi Mahal, Lucknow** – the Mughal-period madrasa specialised in **Muslim jurisprudence** (UPPCS 2016 ×2) – not Delhi, Sialkot or Hyderabad; **Mulla Nizamuddin** of Sihali founded the **Dars-e-Nizami** curriculum (Tafsir, Hadith, Fiqh).
[136:1:1] - **Urban society** was not merchants and soldiers only: **nagarseths, Marwaris, Bohras, Chettis**, **artisans** (in guilds, state **karkhanas** or private) and professionals – **hakims**, teachers, lawyers, painters, musicians.
[136:1:2] - **Classes (NextGen):** upper – emperor, royal family, nobles; middle – merchants and officials; lower – labourers and servants in mud houses. **Bernier**: Delhi had no real **middle class** – people were very rich or very poor.
