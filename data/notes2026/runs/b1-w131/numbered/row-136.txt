## SUB 0 | Position of Women – Royal Women, Purdah and Property
[0:0] TABLE head: Royal woman | Exam fact
  [0:0.0] Gulbadan Begum | Babur's daughter (Humayun's sister); wrote the Humayun-nama – the woman who wrote a historical record of the period (UPPCS 1998)
  [0:0.1] Maham Anga | Akbar's foster-mother; built the Khair-ul-Manazil mosque-madrasa opposite the Purana Qila, Delhi (1561), known as the Madarsa-e-Begum (UPPSC 2012, 2018) – not Gulbadan or Zinat-un-nisa
  [0:0.2] Nur Jahan | Married Jahangir 1611; Padshah Begum; the only empress whose name appeared on coins and farmans; built Itimad-ud-Daula's tomb, Agra
  [0:0.3] Jahanara | Shah Jahan's daughter, Padshah Begum; built the Jama Masjid, Agra and a sarai at Chandni Chowk
  [0:0.4] Zeb-un-Nissa | Aurangzeb's daughter; poet, pen name Makhfi
[0:1] - **Status of women (NextGen):** élite women were educated and some (Nur Jahan, Mumtaz Mahal, Jahanara, Chand Bibi, Tarabai) influenced politics; in general **purdah, child marriage, sati, polygamy** and the ban on widow remarriage lowered their status (Bernier, Tavernier report sati, child marriage, polygamy). Akbar tried to stop **forced sati**.
[0:2] - **Purdah:** Muslim women strictly veiled, carried in covered palanquins; Hindu women of the Deccan moved freely (**John Fryer**, *A New Account of East India and Persia*).
[0:3] - **Property:** Muslim women – inheritance shares + **mehr** (the dower a Muslim bridegroom pays the bride, APPSC 2000) under sharia; Hindu women – **stridhana**.

## SUB 1 | Education and Urban Classes
[1:0] - **Firangi Mahal, Lucknow** – the Mughal-period madrasa specialised in **Muslim jurisprudence** (UPPCS 2016 ×2) – not Delhi, Sialkot or Hyderabad; **Mulla Nizamuddin** of Sihali founded the **Dars-e-Nizami** curriculum (Tafsir, Hadith, Fiqh).
[1:1] - **Urban society** was not merchants and soldiers only: **nagarseths, Marwaris, Bohras, Chettis**, **artisans** (in guilds, state **karkhanas** or private) and professionals – **hakims**, teachers, lawyers, painters, musicians.
[1:2] - **Classes (NextGen):** upper – emperor, royal family, nobles; middle – merchants and officials; lower – labourers and servants in mud houses. **Bernier**: Delhi had no real **middle class** – people were very rich or very poor.
[1:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 04 p.4 (social classes); app notes 1:136 (sub 4); UPPCS 2016 explanation (Firangi Mahal, Dars-e-Nizami)
## SUB 2 | Court Customs, Festivals and Honours
[2:0] TABLE head: Custom | Fact
  [2:0.0] Khilat | Robe of honour, a garment once worn by the emperor, given to meritorious men (NDA 2018); sarapa = "head-to-foot" set of tunic, turban and sash; padma murassa = jewelled lotus, only in exceptional cases
  [2:0.1] Nauroz | Persian New Year, a Zoroastrian (Parsi) festival (UPPSC 2001) – not Jewish, Mongol or Turkish; made a court festival by Akbar, abolished by Aurangzeb
  [2:0.2] Jharokha darshan | Emperor showed himself daily at a balcony; begun by Akbar, ended by Aurangzeb
  [2:0.3] Tuladan | Weighing the emperor against gold and goods on his birthday, given to the poor – a Hindu custom adopted by Akbar
  [2:0.4] Sijda / paibos | Prostration / kissing the feet – introduced at the Mughal court by Akbar (not Shah Jahan)
[2:1] - Emperors except Aurangzeb celebrated **Dasara, Dipawali, Nauroz and Id** with special durbars (NextGen).
[2:2] - **AP angle:** Qutb Shahi Golconda shared **Nauroz** and **Muharram (Peerla Panduga)**.
[2:3] ~~ Sources: NCERT Themes in Indian History II, ch. 9 "Kings and Chronicles" (khilat, sarapa, padma murassa); Singhania Art & Culture 05 p.37 (Jamshedi Navroz – Parsi/Zoroastrian New Year); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 04 p.5 (festivals); app notes 1:136 (sub 2)
## SUB 3 | Akbar's Social Reforms and Slavery
[3:0] TABLE head: Year | Akbar's measure
  [3:0.0] 1562 | Stopped enslavement and forced conversion of prisoners of war
  [3:0.1] 1563 | Abolished the pilgrim tax
  [3:0.2] 1564 | Abolished the jizya
  [3:0.3] 1575 | Built the Ibadat Khana
[3:1] - **Social justice (Sachivalayam):** abolition of jizya, public offices open to all on merit (Rajputs and Hindus as mansabdars), discouraging child marriage and allowing widow remarriage, and stopping conversion of prisoners of war – **all four (A, B, C and D)**.
[3:2] - Despite the 1562 measure, noble households stayed full of **slaves and large domestic staffs**; **Pelsaert** (Dutch factor) contrasted nobles' luxury with the misery of servants and workmen.
[3:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.95-96 (pilgrim tax 1563, jizya 1564, sati); app notes 1:136 (sub 5, 7)
## SUB 4 | Nobility (Umara)
[4:0] - **Umara** = top mansabdars; wealthy but **wholly dependent on the emperor's favour**.
[4:1] - Cosmopolitan: **Turanis** (Central Asians), **Iranis** (Persians), **Afghans**, **Hindustanis** (Indian Muslims), **Rajputs**, later **Deccanis**.
[4:2] TABLE head: Reign | Shift in nobility
  [4:2.0] Babur–Humayun | Turanis and Iranis dominated
  [4:2.1] Akbar | Added Rajputs and Hindustanis to balance them
  [4:2.2] Aurangzeb | Deccanis and Marathas swelled it; Hindu share about one-third (M. Athar Ali)
[4:3] ~~ Sources: app notes 1:136 (sub 0)
