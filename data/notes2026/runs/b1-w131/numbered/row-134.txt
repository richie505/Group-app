## SUB 3 | Mansabdar–Jagirdar–Zamindar Linkage
[3:0] - 1. **Mansabdar** gets a rank → paid by a **tankhwah jagir**; 2. jagirdar's agents collect the assessed revenue (**jama**) through **zamindars**, who keep a hereditary share (**nankar**); 3. the **peasant** pays about one-third.
[3:1] TABLE head: Jagirdar | Zamindar
  [3:1.0] Revenue assignment in lieu of salary for state (mainly military) service – no judicial or police duty | Hereditary rights over land/revenue in a locality; also kept order, supplied troops
  [3:1.1] Not hereditary; transferred often | Hereditary, saleable rights
[3:2] - **UPSC 2019:** both statements wrong – jagirs were for salary, not judicial/police duty, and were **not hereditary**, while zamindari rights **were** – answer **neither A nor B**.
[3:3] - Conflicts: short-tenure jagirdars pressed for more → zamindars resisted → peasants fled (local face of the jagirdari crisis).
[3:4] TABLE head: Category (S. Nurul Hasan) | Rights and role
  [3:4.0] Autonomous chiefs | Rajas ruling their own territories
  [3:4.1] Intermediary | Collected revenue from primary zamindars for the state; got a commission
  [3:4.2] Primary | Proprietary, hereditary rights over cultivated land
[3:5] - **Three-fold classification** of Mughal zamindars (autonomous chiefs, intermediary, primary) – by historian **S. Nurul Hasan**.
