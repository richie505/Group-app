## SUB 0 | Resistance – Jats, Satnamis, Rajputs and Sikhs
[0:0] TABLE head: Revolt | Leader | Region / fact
  [0:0.0] Jat (1669) | Gokula | Mathura (Tilpat); peasant revolt against heavy revenue
  [0:0.1] Jat (1680s) | Rajaram | Plundered Akbar's tomb at Sikandra
  [0:0.2] Jat (after 1700) | Churaman | Built the power that became Bharatpur
  [0:0.3] Satnami (1672) | Satnami sect | Narnaul (Haryana); peasants and artisans – Aurangzeb's reign
[0:1] - **Agrarian roots:** the **Satnami and Jat** revolts grew out of peasants' problems – the Rajput, Sikh and Maratha conflicts did not (MPPSC 2010).
[0:2] - **Bharatpur state:** built by **Churaman and Badan Singh** (Badan Singh founded it, 1722); **Suraj Mal** (r. 1755-63) – **"the Plato of the Jat tribe"**.
[0:3] - Satnamis of 1672 ≠ **Guru Ghasidas** (19th-century Satnampanth, Chhattisgarh); Jat Rajaram ≠ Maratha Rajaram.
[0:4] # Rajputs – Marwar and Mewar (1679-81)
[0:5] - **Aurangzeb changed the Rajput policy** of Akbar, Jahangir and Shah Jahan (SSC 2019).
[0:6] - **Trigger:** **Jaswant Singh** of Marwar died **1678**; Aurangzeb took Marwar under direct control; posthumous son **Ajit Singh** championed by **Durgadas Rathore**; **Rana Raj Singh** of Mewar joined.
[0:7] - **Prince Akbar** (Aurangzeb's son; "Akbar II" in UPPSC options) **revolted, joined the Rajputs (1681)** → fled to Sambhaji → Persia – weakened Aurangzeb against the Rajputs.
[0:8] - Result: the Rajput alliance – pillar of Akbar's empire – broke.
[0:9] # Sikhs and others
[0:10] - **Guru Tegh Bahadur** (9th Guru) executed at Delhi, **1675**; **Guru Gobind Singh** wrote the **Zafarnama** after **Chamkaur (1704)**.
[0:11] - Afghans: **Yusufzai and Afridi** risings.
[0:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 04 p.30-31; old app notes 1:132 (sub 2-4); Satish Chandra, Medieval India II (Jat state, Suraj Mal)
## SUB 1 | Deccan Policy – Bijapur, Golconda and the Marathas
[1:0] - **Phases (NextGen):** 1658-68 recover Bijapur's 1636 gains; 1668-84 alliance against the Marathas; **1686-87 outright annexation**; **1687-1707** war with the Marathas.
[1:1] - Aurangzeb **spent the last ~25-27 years of his reign in the Deccan** – more than any other Mughal ("Deccan ulcer").
[1:2] # Bijapur (1686)
[1:3] - Siege 1685-86, Aurangzeb in person; fell **September 1686**; last ruler **Sikandar Adil Shah**.
[1:4] # Golconda (1687)
[1:5] - Ministers **Madanna-Akkanna** murdered (1686); siege 1687; **Abdur Razzaq Lari** defended; traitor **Abdullah Pani** opened the gate – fort taken with "**keys of gold**" (as Akbar took Asirgarh).
[1:6] - Ruler: **Abul Hasan Qutb Shah (Tana Shah)** – exiled to **Daulatabad**, died 1699, buried at **Khuldabad**.
[1:7] - AP angle: ended **Qutb Shahi rule over Andhra**; **Bhakta Ramadasu** was the nephew of Madanna-Akkanna.
[1:8] - **Greatest extent:** with Bijapur, Golconda and the chase of the Marathas to **Jinji**, the empire reached **Tamil territory** under Aurangzeb; the UPSC 2000 map (Bijapur and Golconda inside) = **Aurangzeb's empire at his death, 1707**.
[1:9] # Marathas
[1:10] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [1:10.0] 1666 | Shivaji at Agra – insulted, house arrest, escaped in fruit baskets
  [1:10.1] 1689 | Sambhaji captured at Sangameshwar, executed
  [1:10.2] 1689-1700 | Rajaram resisted from Jinji; died at Sinhagad (1700)
  [1:10.3] From 1700 | Tarabai led the war as regent for Shivaji II
[1:11] # Last years
[1:12] - **Died 1707 at Ahmednagar**; simple grave at **Khuldabad** (near Aurangabad).

## SUB 2 | Religious Policy, Titles and Coronations
[2:0] - Regnal title **Alamgir**; epithet **"Zinda Pir"** (living saint) / Darvesh for his simple, pious life.
[2:1] - **Double coronation:** 1658 (after Samugarh) and **1659** (after Deorai). **Child's War (1686-90)** – first Mughal war with the English.
[2:2] TABLE head: Measure | Detail
  [2:2.0] Jizya | Re-imposed 1679 (Akbar had abolished it, 1564)
  [2:2.1] Temples | 1669 order – Kashi Vishwanath and Keshava Rai (Mathura) demolished
  [2:2.2] Muhtasibs | Officers who saw that people lived by the Sharia
  [2:2.3] Court customs | Jharokha darshan, tuladan, Navroz stopped; music banned at court – yet he played the veena
  [2:2.4] Zawabit | State-made secular regulations
[2:3] - **Jihad** = **holy war** – war to turn **dar-ul-harb** (land of non-believers) into **dar-ul-Islam**; the BPSC 1999 printed key "Dar-ul-Islam" is the aim, not the meaning.
[2:4] - Hindu nobles rose to about **31.6% (1679-1707)** (Athar Ali), many of them Marathas.
[2:5] - Titles of royal women: **Roshanara = Padshah Begum**; **Jahanara = Sahibat-uz-Zamani**.
[2:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 04 p.30-31; old app notes 1:132 (sub 0-1); Oxford Dictionary of Islam "jihad" (holy war)
