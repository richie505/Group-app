=== Resistance – Jats, Satnamis, Rajputs and Sikhs
| Revolt | Leader | Region / fact |
|---|---|---|
| Jat (1669) | **Gokula** | Mathura (Tilpat); **peasant** revolt against heavy revenue |
| Jat (1680s) | **Rajaram** | **Plundered Akbar's tomb at Sikandra** |
| Jat (after 1700) | **Churaman** | Built the power that became **Bharatpur** |
| **Satnami (1672)** | Satnami sect | **Narnaul** (Haryana); peasants and artisans – Aurangzeb's reign |
- **Agrarian roots:** the **Satnami and Jat** revolts grew out of peasants' problems – the Rajput, Sikh and Maratha conflicts did not (MPPSC 2010).
- **Bharatpur state:** built by **Churaman and Badan Singh** (Badan Singh founded it, 1722); **Suraj Mal** (r. 1755-63) – **"the Plato of the Jat tribe"**.
- Satnamis of 1672 ≠ **Guru Ghasidas** (19th-century Satnampanth, Chhattisgarh); Jat Rajaram ≠ Maratha Rajaram.
# Rajputs – Marwar and Mewar (1679-81)
- **Aurangzeb changed the Rajput policy** of Akbar, Jahangir and Shah Jahan (SSC 2019).
- **Trigger:** **Jaswant Singh** of Marwar died **1678**; Aurangzeb took Marwar under direct control; posthumous son **Ajit Singh** championed by **Durgadas Rathore**; **Rana Raj Singh** of Mewar joined.
- **Prince Akbar** (Aurangzeb's son; "Akbar II" in UPPSC options) **revolted, joined the Rajputs (1681)** → fled to Sambhaji → Persia – weakened Aurangzeb against the Rajputs.
- Result: the Rajput alliance – pillar of Akbar's empire – broke.
# Sikhs and others
- **Guru Tegh Bahadur** (9th Guru) executed at Delhi, **1675**; **Guru Gobind Singh** wrote the **Zafarnama** after **Chamkaur (1704)**.
- Afghans: **Yusufzai and Afridi** risings.

=== Deccan Policy – Bijapur, Golconda and the Marathas
- **Phases (NextGen):** 1658-68 recover Bijapur's 1636 gains; 1668-84 alliance against the Marathas; **1686-87 outright annexation**; **1687-1707** war with the Marathas.
- Aurangzeb **spent the last ~25-27 years of his reign in the Deccan** – more than any other Mughal ("Deccan ulcer").
# Bijapur (1686)
- Siege 1685-86, Aurangzeb in person; fell **September 1686**; last ruler **Sikandar Adil Shah**.
# Golconda (1687)
- Ministers **Madanna-Akkanna** murdered (1686); siege 1687; **Abdur Razzaq Lari** defended; traitor **Abdullah Pani** opened the gate – fort taken with "**keys of gold**" (as Akbar took Asirgarh).
- Ruler: **Abul Hasan Qutb Shah (Tana Shah)** – exiled to **Daulatabad**, died 1699, buried at **Khuldabad**.
- AP angle: ended **Qutb Shahi rule over Andhra**; **Bhakta Ramadasu** was the nephew of Madanna-Akkanna.
- **Greatest extent:** with Bijapur, Golconda and the chase of the Marathas to **Jinji**, the empire reached **Tamil territory** under Aurangzeb; the UPSC 2000 map (Bijapur and Golconda inside) = **Aurangzeb's empire at his death, 1707**.
# Marathas
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1666 | Shivaji at Agra – insulted, house arrest, escaped in fruit baskets |
| 1689 | **Sambhaji** captured at **Sangameshwar**, executed |
| 1689-1700 | **Rajaram** resisted from **Jinji**; died at **Sinhagad** (1700) |
| From 1700 | **Tarabai** led the war as regent for Shivaji II |
# Last years
- **Died 1707 at Ahmednagar**; simple grave at **Khuldabad** (near Aurangabad).

=== Religious Policy, Titles and Coronations
- Regnal title **Alamgir**; epithet **"Zinda Pir"** (living saint) / Darvesh for his simple, pious life.
- **Double coronation:** 1658 (after Samugarh) and **1659** (after Deorai). **Child's War (1686-90)** – first Mughal war with the English.
| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Jizya** | **Re-imposed 1679** (Akbar had abolished it, 1564) |
| Temples | 1669 order – Kashi Vishwanath and Keshava Rai (Mathura) demolished |
| **Muhtasibs** | Officers who saw that people lived by the **Sharia** |
| Court customs | Jharokha darshan, tuladan, Navroz stopped; music banned at court – yet he played the **veena** |
| Zawabit | State-made secular regulations |
- **Jihad** = **holy war** – war to turn **dar-ul-harb** (land of non-believers) into **dar-ul-Islam**; the BPSC 1999 printed key "Dar-ul-Islam" is the aim, not the meaning.
- Hindu nobles rose to about **31.6% (1679-1707)** (Athar Ali), many of them Marathas.
- Titles of royal women: **Roshanara = Padshah Begum**; **Jahanara = Sahibat-uz-Zamani**.
