## SUB 0 | Parallel Ranges – Himadri, Himachal, Shiwalik and the Trans-Himalaya
[0:0] # The three parallel (longitudinal) ranges
[0:1] TABLE head: Feature | Himadri (Greater / Inner / Central) | Himachal (Lesser / Middle) | Shiwalik (Outer / foothills)
  [0:1.0] Position | Northernmost of the three | Between Himadri and Shiwalik | Outermost, southernmost
  [0:1.1] Age | Oldest | Intermediate | Youngest (Pliocene, Cenozoic era)
  [0:1.2] Height | Average 6,000 m; loftiest peaks | 3,500–4,500 m (avg ~4,000 m) | 900–1,100 m
  [0:1.3] Width | 160–400 km (NCERT summary; PMF) | ~50 km | 10–50 km, narrowest
  [0:1.4] Make-up | Granite core (central crystallines); asymmetrical folds | Highly compressed and altered rocks; most rugged system | Unconsolidated river sediments; simple Jura-type folds
  [0:1.5] Continuity | Most continuous range | Broken into ranges | Continuous to the Gandak, then broken
  [0:1.6] Known for | Everest, Kanchenjunga, Nanda Devi, Kamet; long glaciers | Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mussoorie, Nag Tibba, Mahabharat (Nepal); Kashmir, Kangra, Kullu valleys; hill stations | Foothills; duns behind them; debris built the plains
[0:2] - **Three parallel ranges** in the longitudinal extent: **Himadri, Himachal, Shiwalik** (NCERT) – a statement "four parallel ranges" is false; "the northernmost range is the Shiwalik" is false (northernmost = **Himadri**).
[0:3] - **Himadri:** also called Greater, Higher, Inner or **Central** Himalaya; highest point **Mount Everest** (~8,850 m; now 8,848.86 m). Dehradun is **not** in the Himadri (it is a dun between Lesser Himalaya and Shiwalik).
[0:4] - **Himachal:** names for the same range – Lesser, Middle, Lower Himalaya; "Himachal stands for the Middle Himalaya".
[0:5] - **Pir Panjal:** the most important Lesser Himalayan range, **~300 km from the Jhelum to the upper Beas** in **J&K and Himachal Pradesh**; Banihal pass crosses it. South-east of the Ravi it continues as the **Dhauladhar**, then the **Mussoorie** range (Uttarakhand) and the **Mahabharat Lekh** in **Nepal**.
[0:6] - **Shiwalik:** formed from riverine deposits of the "Siwalik river" (Pascoe, Pilgrim) about **2–5 million years ago** (Pliocene, **Cenozoic**); local names – **Jammu Hills** (J&K), **Dhang/Dundwa** (Uttarakhand), **Churia Ghat** (Nepal).
[0:7] - **Youngest mountain chain** among Vindhyas, Aravalli, Shiwaliks, Annamalai = **Shiwaliks**; **oldest Himalayan range** = **Greater Himalaya**.
[0:8] - **In Arunachal Pradesh** the Lesser/outer Himalayan hills are the **Dafla, Miri, Abor and Mishmi hills** (PMF lists them as Lesser Himalaya; the Shiwalik is almost absent there).
[0:9] # The Trans-Himalaya (Tibetan Himalaya)
[0:10] - Lies **north of the Himadri**; mostly in Tibet, hence the **Tibet Himalaya**; **formed before** the Himalayas.
[0:11] - Ranges: **Karakoram** (K2), **Ladakh** (continues into Tibet as the **Kailash Range**, not the Zaskar), **Zaskar** (Kashmir Valley separates the Pir Panjal from it), **Kailash** (Mt Kailash 6,714 m; **Lake Manasarovar** lies near it in Tibet).
[0:12] - **Ladakh Plateau:** north-east of the Karakoram; **highest plateau in India**; includes the **Aksai Chin**, Soda Plains, Lingzi Tang, Depsang Plains (Chinese-occupied Aksai Chin is a cold desert).
[0:13] - **Kailash Mansarovar** is in **Chinese Tibet** – Indian pilgrims need a visa.

[3:0] # Purvanchal
[3:1] - Beyond the **Dihang gorge** (near Namcha Barwa) the Himalaya takes a knee-bend and turns south as the **Purvanchal** – "eastern offshoots of the Himalayas", 500–3,000 m; Arunachal, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, eastern Assam.
[3:2] - **Latest in origin** among Ajanta, Palkonda, Kaimur, Patkai = **Patkai** (Tertiary, Himalayan).

## SUB 5 | Dimensions, Origin, Structure and Significance
[5:0] # Dimensions
[5:1] TABLE head: Dimension | Figure
  [5:1.0] Length | ~2,400 km (2,414 km) – Nanga Parbat (west) to Namcha Barwa (east)
  [5:1.1] Width | 500 km (Kashmir) → 200 km (Arunachal) (NextGen); NCERT/PMF: 400 km → 150 km
  [5:1.2] Area | About 5 lakh sq km (not 15 lakh)
  [5:1.3] Eastern limit | The Brahmaputra (Dihang gorge) marks the eastern boundary
[5:2] - The Himalayas form the **northern boundary**, separating the subcontinent from the rest of Asia (not the Aravalli or the Ghats).
[5:3] - **Indian Himalayan Region:** HP, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal, NE States, J&K, Ladakh and hill WB – **not Uttar Pradesh**.
[5:4] # Origin and structure
[5:5] - **Young fold mountains** of tectonic origin, raised from **Tethys (geosynclinal)** sediments in the **Tertiary** (Eocene–Pliocene) as the **Indian plate** pushed into Eurasia (~70 million years ago, still rising).
[5:6] - **Geologically young and unstable** landmass of India.
[5:7] - **Trans-Himalayas formed before** the Himalayas.
[5:8] - Folds of the **Himadri are asymmetrical** with a **granite** core; **steep slope faces the plains**, gentle slope faces Tibet.
[5:9] - **Structural zones (N → S):** Indus–Tsangpo Suture Zone (separates Himalaya from **Tibet**) → Main Central Thrust → Main Boundary Thrust → **Himalayan Frontal Fault** (separates Himalaya from the **plains**, not from Tibet).
[5:10] - **Antecedent drainage:** the Indus and Sutlej are older than the Himalaya and cut gorges through it.
[5:11] - **Fossils (UPPSC 2019 key):** Greater Himalaya sedimentary rocks fossil-less; marine fossils in the Lesser Himalaya; remains of human civilisation in the Shiwaliks.
[5:12] # Significance (IAS 2010: all three true)
[5:13] - Blocks **Siberian cold waves**; shapes the **monsoon** pattern; rivers and Shiwalik debris built the **Indo-Gangetic Plain's alluvium**.
[5:14] - **Plains** are the largest relief division of India (~43% of area).
