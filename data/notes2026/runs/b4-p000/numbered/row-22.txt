## SUB 1 | Relief Divisions of the Ocean Floor and Ocean Deposits
[1:0] # Four major divisions (NCERT)
[1:1] TABLE head: Division | Depth | Key feature
  [1:1.0] Continental shelf | To about 200 m (100-fathom isobath); average width about 80 km | "Submerged prolongation of land"; gradient 1° or less; massive sedimentary deposits → source of fossil fuels (most offshore oil); richest fishing
  [1:1.1] Continental slope | 200–3,000 m | Gradient 2°–5°; canyons and trenches; marks the end of the continent
  [1:1.2] Continental rise | Foot of the slope | Apron of sediment
  [1:1.3] Deep-sea (abyssal) plain | 3,000–6,000 m | Most extensive (over 76% of ocean basins); flattest, smoothest; fine-grained sediments (clay, silt); polymetallic nodules
  [1:1.4] Oceanic deeps / trenches | Deepest | Active volcanoes and strong earthquakes; 3–5 km deeper than the surrounding floor
[1:2] - **APPSC G2 2024 match**: shelf – massive sedimentary deposits (III); slope – canyons and trenches (I); deep-sea plain – fine-grained sediments (IV); deeps – volcanoes and earthquakes (II) = **a-III, b-I, c-IV, d-II**. Wrong pair: deep-sea plain – volcanoes and earthquakes.
[1:3] - Depth order: deeps > deep-sea plains > slope > shelf. The deep sea below 200 m is the **oldest and largest biome**.
[1:4] - **NDA 2012 shelf statements**: it is **submerged** and its average depth is about **100 fathoms** – true; the largest share of shelf is NOT in the Pacific (the **Atlantic** has the most; widest single shelf = **Siberian shelf**, about 1,500 km); the shelf is **narrow, not wide, where high mountains run close and parallel to the coast** (Chile, west Sumatra).
[1:5] - **Marine sediments by volume**: about **90%** lie on the continental margins (shelf, slope, rise), which cover only about a quarter of the floor; the deep-ocean floor holds the least (Britannica). APPSC FSO key (increasing): deep-ocean floor, shelves, rises, slopes.
[1:6] # Ocean deposits (APPSC G1 2024)
[1:7] - **Terrigenous** deposits come **from land** (gravel, sand, silt, **mud**) and lie mainly on **shelves and slopes**; they get **finer (not coarser) away from the shore**; **muds are the finest** terrigenous material; mud **colour depends on chemical content** (blue, red, green muds). Key: **I, III and IV**.
[1:8] TABLE head: Pelagic deposit | Make-up
  [1:8.0] Calcareous ooze | Lime shells (globigerina, pteropods)
  [1:8.1] Siliceous ooze | Silica skeletons (diatoms, radiolarians); colder, deeper water
  [1:8.2] Red clay | Fine inorganic clay + volcanic dust; most widespread deep-sea deposit, largest in the deep Pacific; not from lime shells
[1:9] - **Coastal gravel** is terrigenous, not pelagic.
[1:10] # Continental shelf of India
[1:11] TABLE head: Coast | Shelf
  [1:11.0] West | Wide; broadest off Gujarat (and part of Maharashtra); Mumbai High oilfield on it, about 160 km off Mumbai (ONGC)
  [1:11.1] East | Narrow

[1:13] - **40th Amendment, 1976**: minerals of the shelf and EEZ vest in the **Union**. **2025**: India widened its central Arabian Sea shelf claim by about **10,000 sq km** before the UN **CLCS** (data: NCPOR Goa).

## SUB 2 | Measuring Depth and Ocean Zones
[2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [2:0.0] Bathymetry | Measuring ocean depth and mapping the sea floor; narrower than oceanography
  [2:0.1] Fathom | Unit of depth = 6 feet (about 1.83 m)
  [2:0.2] Nautical mile | 1,852 m exactly (international definition)
  [2:0.3] Isobath | Line joining points of equal depth
  [2:0.4] Hypsometric curve | Share of Earth's surface at each height and depth; shows the bottom topography of the ocean
  [2:0.5] Echo sounding / SONAR | Reflection of ultrasonic waves; depth = speed of sound × half the echo time
[2:1] - SONAR uses: **fish location (fishing)**, submarine detection, depth measurement, sea-bed mapping.
[2:2] TABLE head: Zone | Extent
  [2:2.0] Neritic | Water over the continental shelf
  [2:2.1] Pelagic | Open ocean beyond the shelf
  [2:2.2] Epipelagic | 0–200 m (sunlit)
  [2:2.3] Mesopelagic ("twilight zone") | 200–1,000 m; drives the biological carbon pump
  [2:2.4] Bathypelagic / Abyssopelagic | 1,000–4,000 m / 4,000–6,000 m
  [2:2.5] Hadopelagic | Deep water in the oceanic trenches (below about 6,000 m)
  [2:2.6] Benthic | The sea floor

## SUB 3 | Oceans – Size and Basic Facts
[3:0] - Size order: **Pacific > Atlantic > Indian > Southern > Arctic**; **Arctic = smallest** (asked 3 times), Indian = third largest.
[3:1] - **Pacific** = the largest (about one-third of Earth) and deepest ocean, the **major ocean of the world**; larger than all land combined; has the **most islands** (about 25,000).
[3:2] - Atlantic: S-shaped, highly indented coast; Indian: only ocean named after a country, triangular, lies **south of the Indian subcontinent** (Arabian Sea to the west, Bay of Bengal to the east).
[3:3] - Oceans cover about **70–71%** of Earth's surface.

## SUB 4 | Minor Relief – Seamounts, Guyots, Canyons and Atolls
[4:0] TABLE head: Feature | Description
  [4:0.0] Seamount | Volcanic peak rising from the floor that does not reach the surface; cobalt-rich crusts
  [4:0.1] Guyot | Flat-topped seamount, planed by waves, then sunk
  [4:0.2] Submarine canyon | Deep, steep-sided valley cut across shelf and slope, often off river mouths; Hudson Canyon best known
  [4:0.3] Atoll | Low ring- or horseshoe-shaped coral reef round a central lagoon (Lakshadweep, Maldives)
[4:1] - **Addu Atoll** = southernmost atoll of the **Maldives**, **Indian Ocean**.
[4:2] - **Canyons off Visakhapatnam**: E.C. LaFond (1963) mapped three canyon systems between Visakhapatnam and Bheemunipatnam, named **Andhra** (after Andhra University), **Mahadevan** (after Prof. C. Mahadevan, "father of Indian geology") and **Krishna**; CSIR-**NIO** (RV Sindhu Sankalp, 2017) mapped them and a new canyon system off **Kovvada** (Srikakulam). There is no "Visakha Canyon".
[4:3] - **Raman Seamount** ("Raman Sagar mountain"): about **1,505 m** high, Arabian Sea floor about 455 km WSW of Mumbai, found by Indian oceanographers, named after C.V. Raman.
[4:4] - **Deep Ocean Mission** surveys: about **25 seamounts** in the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal.
