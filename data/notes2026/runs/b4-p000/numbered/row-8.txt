## SUB 0 | Types of Mountains – Fold, Block, Volcanic, Residual, Dome
[0:0] # Five types (asked 15 times)
[0:1] TABLE head: Type | How formed | Features | Examples
  [0:1.0] Young fold | Tertiary (Alpine) compression of geosyncline sediments | Very high, rugged, sharp conical peaks; world's highest peaks | Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies, Atlas
  [0:1.1] Old fold | Caledonian/Hercynian or Precambrian folding + long denudation | Low, rounded, highly eroded | Aravallis (Precambrian), Appalachians, Urals, Pennines
  [0:1.2] Block | Faulting – uplifted block (horst) beside a sunken block (graben) | Steep fault scarps; linked with rift valleys | Sierra Nevada, Vosges (France), Black Forest (Germany), Salt Range
  [0:1.3] Volcanic | Accumulation of lava and ash around a vent | Conical, with a crater | Kilimanjaro, Fujiyama, Etna (Sicily), Mount St. Helens, Cotopaxi
  [0:1.4] Residual | Erosion leaves the resistant core of an old range | Remnants of older surfaces | Aravallis, Nilgiris, Vindhyas, Appalachians, Pennines
  [0:1.5] Dome | Magma pushes the crust up without erupting | Dome-shaped uplift | Black Hills, Bighorns (USA)
[0:2] - **Mountain:** most geologists take a landform rising at least **300 m (1,000 ft)** above its surroundings as a mountain (APPSC FSO).
[0:3] - **World's highest peaks** (Everest) lie in **young fold** mountains; Everest and Mont Blanc are fold, **not volcanic**.
[0:4] - **Young vs old:** **Alps** is the only young fold range among Appalachians, Aravallis, Alps, Urals (APPSC G2); the **Andes** are young, not old (UKPSC 2016); the **Appalachians** are not Tertiary (UPPSC 2014 – Kunlun, Alps, Andes are). Pennines, Appalachians, Aravallis = **old mountains** (State PSC).
[0:5] - **Block:** **Sierra Nevada** is a block mountain, not young fold (MPPSC 2021); **Black Forest = Germany** (Feldberg 1,493 m), Vosges = France, across the Rhine graben.
[0:6] - **Residual mountain of India = Aravallis** (UPPSC 2005; APPSC Asst Director). Match (UP UDA 2013): **Alps – folded, Vosges – block, Vindhya – residual, Fujiyama – volcanic**.
[0:7] - **Volcanic mountain:** **Mount Etna** (APPSC G1); Kilimanjaro, Fuji.
[0:8] - **Highest of Cotopaxi, Kilimanjaro, Pinatubo, Taal:** **Cotopaxi** (~5,897 m, Ecuador) just above Kilimanjaro (5,895 m) (UPPSC 2015). Highest volcano overall: Ojos del Salado (6,893 m).
[0:9] - **Andes** (~7,000 km) = longest range; Himalayas = highest.
[0:10] - **"Island of precipitation":** **dome** mountains like the Black Hills rise abruptly from dry plains and get about twice the plains' rainfall (APPSC G2 2011, printed as "Down mountains").
[0:11] > Trap: Aravallis are both old fold and residual; when "old mountains" and "fold mountains" are both offered, the books' key is "old mountains".

## SUB 1 | The Aravalli – India's Oldest Fold (Residual) Range
[1:0] - **Oldest mountain range of India** (asked 6 times: vs Himalaya, Shiwalik, Vindhya, Satpura, Nilgiri, Western Ghats); Precambrian, from Dharwar sediments; age about **570 million years** (IAS 2001).
[1:1] - **Old fold mountain** (APPSC FSO flashcard: "fold mountain") and a **residual** mountain – "continuously denuded, typical residual hills" 400–600 m.
[1:2] - Runs ~800 km from **Delhi** through **Haryana** and **Rajasthan** to northern **Gujarat** – mainly **Rajasthan** (UPRO 2014).
[1:3] TABLE head: Peak | Height | Note
  [1:3.0] Guru Shikhar | 1,722 m | Highest; Mount Abu, Sirohi district, Rajasthan (UPSC 2007, asked twice)
  [1:3.1] Kumbhalgarh | ~1,224 m | Highest when Guru Shikhar is not offered (RAS 2012)
  [1:3.2] Sajjangarh, Lilagarh, Taragarh | ~938, 874, 873 m | Lower
[1:4] - Guru Shikhar is also the **highest peak of the Central Highlands** of the Peninsular Plateau (APPSC Degree Lecturers 2020); Parasnath and Dalma are Chotanagpur, Kamet is Himalaya.
[1:5] - **Supreme Court (Nov 2025):** an Aravalli **hill** = landform rising at least **100 m** above local relief (not 500 m); an Aravalli **range** = hills lying within **500 m** of each other; new mining leases paused. MoEF&CC banned new leases on **24 Dec 2025**; the SC later stayed its own directions (Dec 2025).

## SUB 2 | Faults, Block Mountains and Rift Valleys
[2:0] # Faults
[2:1] TABLE head: Fault | Force | Movement
  [2:1.0] Normal | Tension | Hanging wall down
  [2:1.1] Reverse | Compression | Hanging wall up
  [2:1.2] Thrust | Compression | Low-angle reverse
  [2:1.3] Strike-slip | Shear | Horizontal
[2:2] - **Horst** = uplifted fault block → **block mountain** (APPSC AEE 2023); **graben** = down-faulted block → **rift valley**.
[2:3] - **Rift valley:** tension → faulting → central block subsides → elongated depression. Examples: **Rhine** (between Vosges and Black Forest), **Great Rift Valley** (East Africa), **Death Valley** (California – UPPSC 2012), **Narmada, Tapi**. Sierra Nevada = horst.
[2:4] # UPSC 2024 (asked twice): mountain – region – type
[2:5] TABLE head: Row | Correct?
  [2:5.0] Central Asia – Vosges – fold | Wrong – Vosges is a block mountain in Europe
  [2:5.1] Europe – Alps – block | Wrong – Alps are fold
  [2:5.2] North America – Appalachians – fold | Correct
  [2:5.3] South America – Andes – fold | Correct
[2:6] - Answer: **only two** rows correct.
[2:7] # Rift valleys of India (asked 6 times)
[2:8] - **Narmada** flows **west** through a **rift valley (fault trough)** between the **Vindhyas (north)** and **Satpuras (south)** → valley of **structural** origin (CDS 2010); forms an **estuary, no delta** (A false, R true – UPPSC 2005).
[2:9] - Why the Narmada flows west: **only because it occupies a rift valley** (UPSC 2015: "Only I"); lying between Vindhya and Satpura is just its location.
[2:10] - **Damodar** = east-flowing river in a **rift valley due to down-warping** (IAS 1998). Tapi also flows in a rift.

## SUB 3 | Young Fold Mountains – Evidence from the Himalaya
[3:0] - **Youngest/most recent range:** the **Himalayas** (CGPSC 2018); within them the **Shiwaliks** are the youngest chain (UPPSC 2010).
[3:1] - **Evidence of youth** (UPSC 2012, asked twice → **all four**): **deep gorges**, **U-turn river courses**, **parallel mountain ranges**, **steep gradients causing landslides**.
[3:2] - Formed by the **northward movement of the Indian Plate** squeezing the **Tethys** sediments; main uplift Eocene–Pliocene.
[3:3] - **Tertiary (Alpine) orogeny:** Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies, Atlas, Kunlun. **Caledonian:** Appalachians (old).
[3:4] ~~ Sources: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment ch.2; PMF Physical Geography 02 p.48 (p.135); NextGen Rocket General Geography p.33; app notes 4:8 sub 1
## SUB 4 | Diastrophism – Endogenic and Exogenic Forces
[4:0] - **Endogenic** (internal heat → mantle dynamics → plate movement): **diastrophism and volcanism**; **earthquakes are endogenic** (APPSC Inspector of Factories). **Exogenic:** weathering, erosion, deposition.
[4:1] - **Diastrophism** = all processes that move, raise or build up the crust: orogeny, epeirogeny, **earthquakes**, **plate tectonics**; **"heterogenic" is NOT** one (MPPSC 2023).
[4:2] TABLE head: Orogenic | Epeirogenic
  [4:2.0] Mountain building; severe folding in narrow belts | Continent building; uplift or warping of large areas
  [4:2.1] Horizontal (tangential) forces → folding (State PSC) | Vertical (radial) forces (UKPSC 2024)
[4:3] - **APPSC G1 – incorrect statement:** "Orogeny is the continental building process" – orogeny is **mountain** building; epeirogeny is continental building.
[4:4] ~~ Sources: NCERT Class 11 Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.5; NextGen Rocket General Geography p.31; app notes 4:8 sub 0
## SUB 5 | Folds
[5:0] - **Anticline** = **upfold** (arch; oldest strata in the core) (NDA 2015); **syncline** = downfold (trough).
[5:1] - Rising pressure: symmetrical → asymmetrical → overturned → **recumbent** (axial plane horizontal; **both limbs horizontal and parallel** – NDA 2019) → **nappe** (recumbent fold broken and thrust far forward).
[5:2] - **Isoclinal** fold = limbs parallel to each other (and to the axial plane).
[5:3] - Shiwaliks: simple anticline–syncline folds (Jura type). Cuddapah sediments lie in **synclinal troughs**.
[5:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 02 p.47-49 (pp.134-136); app notes 4:8 sub 3
## SUB 6 | Isostasy and Geosynclines
[6:0] - **Isostasy:** crustal blocks float in balance on denser material below.
[6:1] TABLE head: Airy | Pratt
  [6:1.0] Uniform density, varying thickness | Varying density, same depth of compensation
  [6:1.1] Mountains have deep roots | Higher blocks are lighter
[6:2] - **Kober's geosynclinal theory:** long shallow sediment-filled troughs (geosynclines) between rigid forelands (**kratogens**) are squeezed into mountains; Tethys → Himalaya. Stages: **lithogenesis** (sedimentation) → **orogenesis** → **gliptogenesis**.
[6:3] ~~ Sources: app notes 4:8 sub 5; PMF Physical Geography 02 p.47 (geosyncline)
