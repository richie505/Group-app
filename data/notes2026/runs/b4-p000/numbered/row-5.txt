## SUB 0 | Layers of the Earth and Their Composition
[0:0] # Crust, mantle, core (asked 11 times)
[0:1] TABLE head: Layer | Depth | State | Density (g/cm³) | Made of
  [0:1.0] Crust – outermost layer | Oceanic ~5 km, continental ~30 km (~70 km under mountains) | Solid | 2.8–3.0 | Continental: granite (Si, Al); oceanic: basalt (Fe, Mg)
  [0:1.1] Mantle – largest layer | Moho → 2,900 km | Solid, flows plastically (convection) | ~4.6 | Silicate minerals of iron and magnesium (upper mantle mostly silicates)
  [0:1.2] Outer core | 2,900–5,150 km (~2,260 km thick) | Liquid iron–nickel | ~10–12 | Fe + Ni
  [0:1.3] Inner core | 5,150–6,371 km (radius ~1,220 km) | Solid – immense pressure; P-waves at 11.23 km/s | 13.3–13.6 | Mostly iron
[0:2] - **Mantle = 83% of volume, 68% of mass** → the **largest** layer; **core = 16% of volume, 32% of mass**.
[0:3] - **Core is mainly iron** (with nickel) – not aluminium, chromium or silicon (UPSC 2009, asked twice). Its **lighter elements: silicon, sulphur, oxygen** – the APPSC G2 answer is **silicon**; not aluminium, magnesium, calcium or cobalt.
[0:4] - **Mantle = "mixed metals and silicates"** (iron–magnesium silicates) (SSC 2013); Sial is only silica + aluminium, Nife only metal.
[0:5] - **Lithosphere = crust + uppermost solid mantle** (~100 km) (NDA 2019).
[0:6] - Continental crust: thicker, older, granitic, lighter; oceanic crust: thinner, younger, **basaltic**, denser.
[0:7] - **Compositional layering:** crust, mantle, core. **Mechanical:** lithosphere, asthenosphere, mesosphere, outer core, inner core.
[0:8] # Suess's chemical layers
[0:9] TABLE head: Suess term | Elements | Feature
  [0:9.0] SIAL | Silica + Aluminium | Granite; forms the continents; density ~2.9
  [0:9.1] SIMA | Silica + Magnesium | Basalt; source of magma and lava
  [0:9.2] NIFE | Nickel + Ferrum (iron) | Core; very dense (~11); explains Earth's magnetism (SSC 2018; State PSC "Nickel and ferrum")
[0:10] - **Eduard Suess** coined SIAL, SIMA and NIFE.
[0:11] - **Whole Earth by mass:** **iron 32.1%** > oxygen 30.1% > **silicon 15.1%** > magnesium 13.9% > sulphur 2.9% > nickel 1.8% (RRB 2005: after iron and oxygen comes **silicon**).
[0:12] > Trap: crust question → oxygen/aluminium; whole-Earth question → iron.

## SUB 1 | Seismic Waves, Shadow Zone and the Seismograph
[1:0] # Seismic waves (asked 7 times)
[1:1] TABLE head: Basis | P (primary) | S (secondary) | Surface (L)
  [1:1.0] Type | Longitudinal, push–pull, compressional; vibration parallel | Transverse, shear; vibration perpendicular | Along the surface (Love, Rayleigh)
  [1:1.1] Speed | Fastest, arrive first | Slower | Slowest
  [1:1.2] Medium | Solid, liquid, gas | Solids only | –
  [1:1.3] Damage | Lower | – | Highest
[1:2] - An earthquake generates **P, S and L (surface) waves** – "P.S.L" (CGPSC 2013). P and S are **body waves**.
[1:3] - **P-waves are sound (acoustic) waves:** compressional like sound, and they become audible sound when they reach the air – so of P, S and L, **P-waves** are the sound waves.
[1:4] - Wave speed changes with density → **reflection and refraction** at boundaries reveal the layers. The **S-wave** behaviour (vanishing in the outer core) gave the key evidence of a **liquid outer core** – the CDS 2012 answer "secondary waves".
[1:5] - **Shadow zone:** P-waves strongly refracted at the mantle–core boundary → no direct P **~105°–145°** (Rocket: 103°–142°); **S-waves absent everywhere beyond ~105°**. The belt **105°–145°** gets **neither P nor S** (APSLPRB 2023).
[1:6] - Each earthquake has its **own** shadow zone, set by its epicentre (CGPSC 2020).
[1:7] # Recording
[1:8] - **Seismograph** records seismic waves (FBO). NCERT Class 8: the seismograph is a **vibrating rod** or pendulum that starts vibrating with the tremors.
[1:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket General Geography p.29-30; NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.1 (p.15); PMF Physical Geography 01 (P-waves "take the form of sound waves" in air); NCERT Class 8 Science ch.12 (seismograph); app notes 4:5 sub 1
## SUB 2 | Earth's Magnetism, Heat and Pressure
[2:0] # Magnetic field (asked 4 times)
[2:1] - **Cause:** electric **currents in the liquid outer core** – convection of molten iron = **geodynamo** (UPPCS 2011) – not a giant magnet at the centre. It shields life from **solar wind and cosmic radiation**; flow changes are linked to **reversals**.
[2:2] - **Magnetic axis ~11.5°** to the geographic axis (not 23½°).
[2:3] - **Magnetic equator:** field lines **horizontal**, dip = 0 (NDA 2017); it passes near **Thumba** (Kerala) – hence TERLS. At the magnetic poles the field is vertical.
[2:4] - **North magnetic pole:** first located (1831) on the **Boothia Peninsula, northern Canada** – UPSC 2005 key; it has since drifted into the Arctic Ocean towards Siberia (~86°N as of 2025, World Magnetic Model 2025).
[2:5] - **Direction of the field** at a place = **declination** (angle from true north) **and inclination/dip** (angle from horizontal) (CDS 2018).
[2:6] - ESA **Swarm** mission: outer-core flow under the Pacific switched from westward to eastward.
[2:7] # Heat, pressure, density
[2:8] - Temperature, pressure and density **all rise with depth**; ~**1°C per 32 m** in the upper crust. Earth's mean density **5.5 g/cm³** (densest planet).
[2:9] - Geothermal sites: **Puga Valley (Ladakh)**, Manikaran (Himachal), Tattapani (Chhattisgarh), Cambay (Gujarat), **Bakreshwar (West Bengal)**.
[2:10] - Diamonds rise from the mantle via **kimberlite pipes**; **Madhya Pradesh** ~90% of India's reserves.
[2:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket General Geography p.31; NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.4 (p.18); NOAA/BGS World Magnetic Model 2025 (north magnetic pole position); app notes 4:5 sub 6
