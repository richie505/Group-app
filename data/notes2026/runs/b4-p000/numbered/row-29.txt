## SUB 0 | Indian Fisheries – Blue Revolution, National Fish, Fisheries Days
[0:0] # Blue Revolution (asked 3 times)
[0:1] - **Blue Revolution = fisheries / fish production / aquaculture** (APPSC Sociology 2002 "Aquaculture"; UPUDA 2010 and UKPSC 2006 "Fisheries / Fishing") – not agriculture, irrigation, dairy (**White**), silk, poultry, bees or iron and steel.
[0:2] TABLE head: Revolution | Sector
  [0:2.0] Green | Food grains
  [0:2.1] White | Milk / dairy
  [0:2.2] Blue | Fish / fisheries / aquaculture
  [0:2.3] Yellow | Oilseeds
  [0:2.4] Golden | Horticulture, honey
  [0:2.5] Black | Petroleum
  [0:2.6] Red | Meat, tomato
[0:3] - Flagship scheme: **PMMSY (2020)** – Ministry of **Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying**; subsidy **40%** general, **60%** women / SC / ST.
[0:4] # National fish and national aquatic animal (asked 2 times)
[0:5] - **National fish = Indian Mackerel** (bangda) – the answer books and APPSC keys give (APPSC Group 2 2019; APPSC Mains 2018) – not Pomfret, Hilsa or Rohu. It was **never formally notified**; Golden Mahseer is sometimes cited but is a **state fish** (Arunachal, Uttarakhand, Himachal …).
[0:6] - **National aquatic animal = Gangetic river dolphin** (declared **2009**).
[0:7] # Fisheries days (asked 2 times)
[0:8] TABLE head: Day | Date | Why
  [0:8.0] World Fisheries Day | 21 November | Global day of fishing communities
  [0:8.1] National Fish Farmers' Day | 10 July | Induced breeding of carps, 10 July 1957, by Dr Hiralal Chaudhuri and Dr K.H. Alikunhi
[0:9] # India's fish economy
[0:10] - India = **2nd** fish producer after **China** (~8–9% of world output); **inland fisheries >75%** of production; seafood exports **₹62,408 crore (FY 2024-25)**, led by **frozen shrimp**.
[0:11] TABLE head: Waters | Rule
  [0:11.0] 0–12 nm | State subject; annual 61-day ban on mechanised fishing
  [0:11.1] 12–200 nm (EEZ) | Centre; EEZ Rules 2025 – Access Pass for vessels ≥24 m via ReALCraft, zero fee; traditional crafts exempt
[0:12] > Blue Revolution is never dairy (White) or oilseeds (Yellow); national fish ≠ national aquatic animal.

## SUB 1 | Blue Economy and SAGAR
[1:0] # What the blue economy is (asked 3 times)
[1:1] - **Blue economy = sustainable use of ocean (marine) resources** – ocean, coastal and inland waters – for growth, livelihoods/jobs and ecosystem conservation (APPSC FSO "Sustainable use of ocean resources"; APPSC AEE ×2 "Ocean Resources") – not water purification, space-based mapping, sky/agricultural resources or "deep-sea mining only".
[1:2] - India: **Draft Blue Economy Policy Framework, 2021 – Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES)**.
[1:3] - **SAGAR** = "**Security and Growth for All in the Region**" – PM, **2015, Mauritius**; expanded as **MAHASAGAR (2025)** to the wider Global South.
[1:4] ~~ Sources: Praveen CA360 June 2026 magazine p.77 (blue economy); PMF Lens July 2026 p.39 (SAGAR → MAHASAGAR); app notes 4:29 sub 5
## SUB 2 | World Fisheries – Grounds, Fishing Nations, Pearls
[2:0] # Fishing nations (asked 1 time)
[2:1] - **Norway** and **Japan** depend most on fishing – hilly/rugged land, harsh winters, little farmland; fish is their main food. Fishing is "particularly important in Britain, **Norway** and British Columbia" (Leong). APPSC Group 2 2011: "fishing is the main occupation of" → **Norway** (not New Zealand – meat and dairy). **Iceland**'s exports lean most heavily on fish.
[2:2] # Pearls (asked 1 time)
[2:3] - **Japan** = the standard answer for **largest producer of (cultured) pearls** (APPSC Group 2 Mains 2019): cultured pearls invented in Japan (**1913**, Mikimoto, **Akoya** oyster). By **volume China** now leads (freshwater pearls).
[2:4] # Fishing grounds
[2:5] TABLE head: Ground | Location
  [2:5.0] Grand Banks (world's largest) | Off Newfoundland, NW Atlantic – Gulf Stream meets Labrador
  [2:5.1] NW Pacific | Off Japan – Kuroshio meets Oyashio
  [2:5.2] Dogger Bank | Shallow sandbank, North Sea, ~100 km off eastern England
[2:6] - Favourable: **wide shallow continental shelves**, **mixing of warm and cold currents**, **upwelling** (Peru), cold waters (plankton), indented coasts.

## SUB 3 | UNCLOS Maritime Zones and India's EEZ
[3:0] # India's EEZ (asked 1 time)
[3:1] - **India's EEZ ≈ 2.37 million sq km** (APPSC Polytechnic Lecturers) – exclusive right to all living and non-living resources; world's **18th largest**; much of it from **Andaman-Nicobar and Lakshadweep**. Coastline **11,098.81 km**.
[3:2] TABLE head: Zone (from baseline) | Limit | Coastal-state rights
  [3:2.0] Territorial sea | 12 nm | Full sovereignty (innocent passage for others)
  [3:2.1] Contiguous zone | 24 nm | Customs, fiscal, immigration, sanitary laws
  [3:2.2] EEZ | 200 nm | Sovereign rights over living and non-living resources only
  [3:2.3] Continental shelf | Up to 350 nm | Seabed and subsoil
  [3:2.4] High seas | Beyond | Freedom of navigation
[3:3] - **UNCLOS** adopted **1982**, in force **1994**; set up the **International Seabed Authority (ISA)**.
[3:4] - Indian law: **Maritime Zones Act, 1976** (Territorial Waters, Continental Shelf, EEZ and Other Maritime Zones).
[3:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Internal Security & DM 02 p.19 (EEZ 2.37 million sq km, coastline); PMF Science & Tech 06; app notes 4:29 sub 0
## SUB 4 | Marine Resources of Andhra Pradesh
[4:0] # Why the coast matters (asked 1 time)
[4:1] - Residuary AP (2014) has little coal and leans on its **974 km coastline (2nd longest)** – **offshore resources** (fish, aquaculture, oil and gas, ports, beach minerals) (APPSC Panchayat Secretary).
[4:2] - ~**30%** of India's fish, **31%** of shrimp exports (**Vannamei**); aquaculture districts: **West Godavari**, Eluru, Krishna, Bapatla, Nellore, Konaseema, Kakinada.
[4:3] - **10 fishing harbours** (₹3,699.09 crore) under **PMMSY, not Sagarmala**.
[4:4] - Oil and gas: **Krishna-Godavari (KG) basin**; beach sand minerals: **Srikakulam–Bhimunipatnam** coast.
[4:5] - **Centre of Excellence on Blue Economy**: **Vignan's University, Vadlamudi (Guntur)**, Aug 2026.
[4:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP Geography 01 p.7 (974 km, 2nd longest); app notes 4:29 sub 6

[5:5] # Deep Ocean Mission and offshore blocks
[5:6] - **Deep Ocean Mission**: **Ministry of Earth Sciences** (not Ports/Shipping); approved **June 2021**; **₹4,077 crore**, 5 years; one of the nine PM-STIAC missions; covers deep-sea mining and a manned submersible, ocean climate services, biodiversity, ocean survey, **energy and freshwater**, marine biology.
[5:7] - **Samudrayaan – Matsya 6000**: indigenous submersible, **3 crew to 6,000 m**.
[5:8] - **OAMDR Act 2002** (amended 2023): first tranche of **13 offshore blocks**, **28 Nov 2024**, composite licence.
