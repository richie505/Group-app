=== Cyclones of India and Andhra Pradesh – Exposure and Major Cyclones
# Exposure (asked 25 times)
- India's coast is exposed to nearly **10%** of the world's tropical cyclones (APPSC-GS 2023; NDMA). Coastline: old **7,516.6 km**; remeasured 2023–24 = **11,098.81 km**; about 5,900 km directly exposed.
- **Bay of Bengal : Arabian Sea ≈ 4 : 1** – the Bay has about **4 to 5 times** more cyclones (75–80% of the total) because it is **warmer (high SST)**, enclosed by land and receives **remnants of Pacific typhoons**; its funnel shape worsens storm surges.
- **Arabian Sea**: fewer cyclones but becoming **more intense**; 2019 Arabian Sea storms = **Vayu, Hikka, Kyarr, Maha, Pavan** (Pabuk, Fani, Bulbul were Bay systems).
- **East coast** (Odisha, AP, WB, TN) is more cyclone-prone than the west coast; **Odisha** faces the most cyclone disasters.
- Season **bimodal**: main peak **Oct–Dec** (retreating monsoon), secondary **Apr–May**; some at monsoon onset (June) and withdrawal (Sept). A May (pre-monsoon) cyclone is linked to **local temperature variation** (strong local heating).
- Bay of Bengal cyclones move **west, north-west or north**, recurving north/north-east later; some cross the peninsula into the Arabian Sea and revive.
- **INCOIS (Hyderabad)** 1995–2019 study: El Nino may **reduce** post-monsoon Bay cyclones (15 in 9 El Nino years vs 33 in 11 La Nina years).
# Andhra Pradesh
- AP coastline **1,053 km** (3rd longest); **cyclones are AP's main hazard** (more than floods, landslides, earthquakes); hit mostly **Oct–Dec** (Hudhud, Titli, Michaung, Montha).
- IMD record 1891–2017: AP coast hit **77 times** by cyclones (34 kt+), **31 of them severe** cyclonic storms.
- **Montha** (Oct 2025, Kakinada): RTGS **AWARE 2.0** auto-evacuations, 423 drones, alerts to 3.60 crore people, only 15 deaths.

=== Measurement Scales and IMD Categories
# Scales (asked 16 times)
| Scale | Measures | Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Saffir-Simpson** Hurricane Wind Scale | **Hurricanes** – wind speed; estimates property damage and coastal flooding | Cat 1–5 |
| **Fujita / Enhanced Fujita** (Ted Fujita) | **Tornadoes** – wind and damage; EF is the current standard (USA, 2007) | **EF0–EF5 (6 categories)** |
| Beaufort | Wind force | 0–12; **gale = force 8** (about 62–74 km/h) |
| Richter (Charles Richter, 1935) | Quake magnitude | Logarithmic |
| Moment magnitude (Mw) | Quake magnitude (energy at source) | Modern standard |
| Modified Mercalli | Quake intensity (effects, damage); mainly USA | I–XII |
| MSK-64 | Quake intensity; India and Europe | I–XII |
| INES | Nuclear events (IAEA, 1990) | 0–7 |
- Saffir-Simpson: Cat 1 **119–153**; Cat 2 154–177; Cat 3 178–208; Cat 4 209–251; Cat 5 **252+** km/h.
- **"Gale force"** winds are a cyclone term.
- Hurricane / typhoon / cyclone threshold: **74 mph (119 km/h, 64 knots)** – above 119 km/h a storm is "hurricane-strength" (old IMD: severe cyclonic storm with a core of hurricane winds; now Very Severe Cyclonic Storm).
- **Super typhoon**: about 240 km/h (strong Cat 4–5); Super Typhoon **Bavi** = 3rd Cat-5 storm of 2026 (10 Jul 2026).
# IMD categories (North Indian Ocean, Indian Cyclone Intensity Scale)
| Category | km/h | Knots |
|---|---|---|
| Low Pressure Area | < 31 | < 17 |
| Depression | 31–49 | 17–27 |
| Deep Depression | 50–61 | 28–33 |
| **Cyclonic Storm** (named from here) | **62–88** | 34–47 |
| Severe Cyclonic Storm | 89–117 | 48–63 |
| Very Severe | 118–166 | 64–89 |
| Extremely Severe (**added 2015**) | 167–221 | 90–119 |
| **Super Cyclonic Storm** (highest) | 222+ | 120+ |
- Tropical cyclone = winds **at or above 62 km/h** (APPSC 2021); Cyclonic Storm = **62–88 km/h** (APPSC AEE 2016; the 2018 option read 63–88).
- From depression upwards the IMD has **7 classes** (6 before 2015). The IMD uses the **Indian Cyclone Intensity Scale**, not Saffir-Simpson.

=== Tropical Cyclones – Formation and Where They Form
# Conditions (asked 10 times)
| Condition | Role |
|---|---|
| **Warm ocean, SST above 26.5–27°C** over a large area | Heat and moisture – most essential |
| Large supply of warm, moist air | **Latent heat of condensation** = energy source |
| Coriolis force | Rotation; too weak within about 5° of the Equator |
| Unstable troposphere, high humidity | Vigorous convection |
| Low vertical wind shear | Keeps the vertical structure organised |
| Pre-existing disturbance; upper-level divergence | Seed low / cyclonic circulation |
- A cyclone is a **violent storm of strong wind and heavy rain**; form **only over warm oceans**, not rivers, lakes or ponds (APPSC-GS 2025).
- Warm sea, high humidity and instability = **formative / initial stage** (NDA 2017).
- **Moving away from the sea its force decreases** because the latent-heat (moisture) supply is cut off – weakens after **landfall** (A–R: both true, R explains A).
# Where they form
- Belt: **between 30°N and 30°S** (APPSC 2022); mainly **5°–30°** N and S, western parts of oceans; **not** in mid-latitudes and **none at the Equator** (no Coriolis).
- **None in the South Atlantic and SE Pacific** – UPSC 2015 key: **ITCZ seldom occurs** there; also **cold currents** (Benguela, Humboldt) keep SST low.
- Leong: typhoons at **6°–20° N and S**, most frequent **July–October**, winds **over 100 mph**, torrential rain with thunder and lightning.
- Marine heatwaves raise cyclone winds by up to 20% and rain by up to 12% (2026 study).

=== Tornadoes, Thunderstorms and Pre-monsoon Local Storms
# Tornadoes (asked 11 times)
- Rotating funnel from a **cumulonimbus/supercell thunderstorm** to the ground; winds **105–322 km/h**; moves up to about 90–100 km/h; lasts **minutes** (rarely over 15–20).
- **Land-based** (hurricanes, typhoons, willy-willies are ocean-born); much **smaller** than hurricanes – both are destructive rotating storms.
- Air spiralling to the centre conserves angular momentum → **angular speed increases** towards the centre.
- **Tornado Alley**: central US plains (**Mississippi plains / Great Plains**) where cold polar air meets warm, moist **Gulf of Mexico** air.
- **India is least vulnerable to tornadoes** among major hazards (rare; pre-monsoon; Belda 1998).
| Feature | Tornado | Hurricane |
|---|---|---|
| Size, life | Small, minutes | Hundreds of km, days |
| Where | Land-based | Ocean-born |
- **Waterspout** = tornado over water; seen over the **Brahmaputra, Barpeta (Assam)**, 26 Aug 2026. An ordinary thunderstorm is a short-lived single-cell Cb; **supercells** spawn tornadoes.
# Thunderstorms
- Thundercloud (Cb, up to 15 km) needs **condensation, high temperature and humidity, orography and vertical winds – all four** (IAS 2002). **Thunder** = air expanding explosively after lightning heats it.
- **Hailstorms**: instability, moisture, updrafts; **synoptic** causes = Western Disturbances and wind discontinuity lines.
- **Damini App**: lightning alerts in 15 languages; 104 detection locations.
# Pre-monsoon local storms of India
| Storm / shower | Season / region |
|---|---|
| **Mango showers** | End of summer (March–April); **Kerala, coastal Karnataka**, nearby TN; early ripening of mangoes |
| Blossom (cherry blossom) showers | Kerala and nearby; **coffee** flowering |
| **Kaal Baisakhi / Nor'westers** | West Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Assam ("**tea showers**" in Assam); approach from NW |
| **Aandhi** (dust storm) | Summer; **Uttar Pradesh**, Punjab – dry plains of NW India |
| Loo | Hot dry wind of the northern plains |
| Cloudburst | Himalaya, June–August; about 100 mm/hour over 20–30 sq km |
- Hindi-medium key: mango showers = "rainfall in March–April in Bihar and Bengal" – strictly Kerala/Karnataka.
- **Blizzard**: severe snowstorm, winds 35 mph (about 56 km/h)+, visibility a quarter mile or less for 3+ hours.

=== Structure, Rotation and Steering of Cyclones
# Structure (asked 8 times)
| Part | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Eye** | Calm, clear, sinking air; **lowest pressure**; **higher temperature** (warm core); 20–50 km across |
| **Eyewall** | Ring of towering cumulonimbus; **strongest winds, heaviest rain** |
| Spiral rainbands | Curved bands of rain and wind – a mature cyclone shows a **spiral pattern** |
- Air moves **inward and rises**; rotation **anticlockwise (counter-clockwise) in NH, clockwise in SH** – spin from the **Coriolis effect**.
- Approach of a cyclone = **falling** barometer.
- Hurricanes usually **grow larger** poleward, and some are **under 150 km** across (Tracy 1974) – CDS 2010: only "spin from Coriolis" is right.
- Steering: **trade winds** push tropical cyclones **west**; mature storms **recurve north-east** under the **westerlies**.
- Threats: high winds, heavy rain, flooding, **storm surge** – abnormal rise of sea level, the **biggest killer**.

=== Warnings, Detection and Naming
# Warnings (asked 4 times)

|---|---|
| Pre-Cyclone Watch | – |
| **Cyclone Alert** | **Yellow** |
| Cyclone Warning | Orange |

=== Cyclones vs Anticyclones; Disturbances at a Glance
# Cyclone vs anticyclone (asked 2 times)
| Basis | Cyclone | Anticyclone |
|---|---|---|
| Centre | **Low pressure**, surrounded by higher | **High pressure**, surrounded by lower |
| Air | Inward, rising | **Outward, sinking** (diverging) |
| Weather | Cloudy, stormy | **Clear, dry, calm, settled** |
| NH rotation | Anticlockwise | **Clockwise** |
| SH rotation | Clockwise | **Anticlockwise** |
- **Continually rising pressure = fine, settled weather** (anticyclone building).
- **Heat dome**: a stalled warm high traps hot air (27 May 2026).
# At a glance
| Disturbance | Nature | Clouds |
|---|---|---|
| Tropical cyclone | Large low over warm tropical oceans | Cumulonimbus, rainbands |
| Temperate cyclone | Large mid-latitude low from air-mass interaction, with fronts | Stratiform and cumuliform |
| Anticyclone | Large high, outward sinking air | Generally clear |
| Thunderstorm | Local violent convective storm with lightning, thunder | Cumulonimbus |
| Tornado | Very local rotating funnel | Cumulonimbus supercell |
| Cloudburst, squall | Very local intense rain / sudden gusty wind | – |
- **Fronts** belong to **temperate cyclones only**.

=== Temperate Cyclones and Western Disturbances
# Temperate vs tropical (asked 1 time)
| Feature | Tropical | Temperate (extra-tropical) |
|---|---|---|
| Latitude | 5–30° | **35–65°** (mid and high) |
| Origin | Only over warm seas | **Land and sea** |
| Fronts | None | **Clear frontal system** (warm, cold; occluded) |
| Size, winds | Smaller, very intense (100–500 km) | **Larger area** (300–2,000 km), gentler winds; long rain |
| Movement | East to west (trades) | **West to east** (westerlies) |
- APPSC G1: "Extra-tropical cyclones **do not have a clear frontal system**" is the **INCORRECT** statement.
- **Western Disturbance**: extratropical low from the **Mediterranean**; reaches **NW India in winter** at the occluded stage; about **10–15 cm** rain for **rabi** crops, snow on the mountains.
