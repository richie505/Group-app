=== Ozone Layer – Location, Formation and UV Shield
# Where the ozone layer lies (asked 17 times)
- **Ozone (O₃) = three oxygen atoms** (APPSC BC Welfare). It lies mainly in the **stratosphere** – the standard answer to "ozone layer is located in / found mainly in / maximum concentration in / rich in ozone" (UPPCS ×8, CGPSC, SSC, CDS 2009, APPSC AEE, APPSC AMVI) – not the troposphere, mesosphere, ionosphere or exosphere.
- **Lower stratosphere:** main layer **~15–30 km**, densest **~15–35 km** (NCERT/Rocket); exam heights "**15–20 km above the surface**" (State PSC) are the accepted option – never "below the surface", never 40–50 or 70–80 km.
- About **90%** of all ozone is **stratospheric**, ~10% tropospheric.
- Formation (**Chapman cycle**): **UV + O₂ → O + O; O + O₂ → O₃**; O₃ absorbs UV and splits again.
- Column ozone is measured in **Dobson Units (DU)**; 1 DU = 0.01 mm of pure ozone at 0°C, 1 atm.
# What it does (asked 13 times)
- **Absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation** of the Sun and acts as **Earth's shield** (APPSC FBO, APPSC ASO; MPPSC 1994 ×2; UKPSC 2002, 2006; UPPCS 2006, 2013; CGPSC 2013) – it does **not** supply oxygen, control Earth's temperature, trap heat, reflect sunlight, cause rain or stop earthquakes/monsoon failure.
- It does **not** absorb cosmic rays, infrared, visible light or microwaves – only **UV**.
| UV band | Wavelength | Ozone layer |
|---|---|---|
| UV-A | 315–400 nm | Mostly reaches the surface |
| UV-B | 280–315 nm | Mostly absorbed, part reaches the ground |
| **UV-C** | 100–280 nm (shortest, most harmful) | **Completely absorbed** (APPSC FSO 2025) |
- Excess UV → **skin (dermal) cancer** (UPPSC 2014), **cataract**, weaker immunity, lower crop yield → ozone is **essential for biotic life** because it screens high-energy radiation (UPPSC Mains 2010: A and R true, R explains A).
- Stratosphere **warms with height** because ozone turns absorbed UV into heat.
| Ozone | Role |
|---|---|
| Stratospheric | Protective shield – good |
| **Ground-level (tropospheric)** | Secondary pollutant from **NOx + VOCs** in sunlight (photochemical smog); heatwaves raise it (north-west India, Indo-Gangetic Plain) – bad |

=== Ozone Depletion, the Ozone Hole and Treaties
# Cause (asked 13 times)
- Depletion happens in the **stratosphere** (APPSC AEE; APPSC Degree Lecturers).
- **Main cause: halocarbons** – **CFCs** (most damaging), HCFCs, halons – that release **chlorine and bromine** (APPSC 2023 Lecturers ×2; APPSC 2025 Welfare Organiser and Sainik Welfare Officer: **CFCs**, not UV rays, heavy metals or reactive oxygen species).
- **Carbon dioxide has no direct role** in ozone depletion (APPSC Sainik Welfare) – nor do CO, nitrogen, acetylene, ethylene, SO₂ or O₂.
| ODS | Use |
|---|---|
| **CFCs** | Refrigeration, ACs, aerosols, foam |
| HCFCs | CFC substitute – **also deplete ozone** |
| **Halons** | Fire extinguishers |
| Methyl bromide | Fumigant |
| **Carbon tetrachloride**, methyl chloroform | Solvents |
- CFCs, halons **and** carbon tetrachloride are all ozone-depleting (UPPSC 2012 → I, II and III).
- **HFCs** spare ozone but have high global-warming potential; CFCs destroy ozone **and** are strong GHGs.
# The Antarctic ozone hole (asked 8 times)
- Maximum over **Antarctica** – the **poles**, not the equator or tropics (MPPSC 2008; CDS 2012); forms in the southern spring **Aug–Nov**.
- Reason (UPSC 2011): **a prominent polar front (polar vortex) and polar stratospheric clouds (PSCs), plus the inflow of CFCs** – PSCs (12–22 km, nitric acid ice) turn inactive chlorine into reactive forms that destroy ozone when sunlight returns.
- First reported in **1985** by a **British team** (British Antarctic Survey: **Farman, Gardiner, Shanklin**) (RAS 2012; UPPSC Mains 2013).
- 2025 hole peaked at just over **21 million sq km** and closed early (record ~29 million sq km, 2006).
| Treaty | Year | Point |
|---|---|---|
| Vienna Convention | 1985 | Framework: research, monitoring |
| **Montreal Protocol** | **1987** | Legally binding; phases out ~100 ODS; universally ratified (first UN treaties ratified by all, with Vienna) |
| Kigali Amendment | 2016 (Kigali, **Rwanda**) | **HFC** phase-down 80–85%; in force 1 Jan 2019 |
- **World Ozone Day: 16 September** (Montreal Protocol signing).
- India: **first developing country** to phase out **HCFC-141b** in rigid foam; HCFCs cut **44%** vs 35% target by Jan 2020.
- AP: **Mission SANA** (Red Balloon Aerospace) – India's first home-made **stratospheric super-pressure balloon**, launched from **Vijayawada** (May 2026).

=== Composition – Gases of the Atmosphere
# Gases of dry air (asked 10 times)
| Gas | Share by volume | Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Nitrogen** | **78.08% (~78%)** – most abundant | Permanent |
| **Oxygen** | 20.95% (~**21%**, rounded "20%" – APPSC G1 2012) | Permanent |
| **Argon** | 0.93% – third, inert | Permanent |
| Carbon dioxide | ~0.04% (0.036% in NCERT) | Variable |
| Water vapour | 0–4% | Variable |
| Neon, helium, krypton, xenon, hydrogen, ozone | Traces | — |
- **N₂ > O₂ > Ar > CO₂** (APPSC G1 2023). **Nitrogen + oxygen = 99%** – the air near the surface is "mainly nitrogen and oxygen" (RAS 1997).
- **Permanent gases** keep a constant proportion: nitrogen, oxygen, argon (and the noble gases); **variable:** CO₂, water vapour, ozone → UPPCS Mains 2017 answer **nitrogen**.
- **Chlorine** is **not** a normal constituent of air (APPSC G2 2008); neon, helium and CO₂ are.
- **Xenon = "Stranger Gas"** (Greek *xenos*), found by **Ramsay and Travers, 1898** (UPSC 2008).
- Weather balloons are filled with **helium** (light, inert) (RAS 1997).
- **0.03% CO₂** = the optimum for natural balance (UPPCS 2017); today it is ~0.04% (**423.9 ppm in 2024**, WMO Greenhouse Gas Bulletin, Oct 2025).
- Air is held to the Earth by **gravity** (SSC 2011).
# Change with height and with time
- **99% of the mass lies within 32 km.** Moving up, **CO₂ and water vapour vanish first (~90 km)**, then **oxygen (negligible by ~120 km)**, while nitrogen continues → order of disappearance **CO₂ → O₂ → N₂** (NDA 2009).
- Light gases rise highest: the **exosphere** is mostly **hydrogen** (with helium) (NDA 2015).
- **Early atmosphere:** **reducing** – methane, ammonia, hydrocarbons, little free oxygen; methane and hydrocarbons were oxidised and replaced by **carbon dioxide** (BPSC 1995); photosynthesis later made it oxygen-rich.

=== Layers – Basis, Sequence and Temperature Trend
# Sequence (asked 10 times)
- Layers are divided on the **vertical profile of air temperature** (APPSC G1) – not wind, humidity or pressure.
| Order | Layer | Height | Temperature | Identity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Troposphere** – lowest (SSC CGL 2019; UKPSC 2016) | 0 to 8 km (poles) / 18 km (equator); avg **13 km** | Falls | Weather |
| 2 | **Stratosphere** – second layer (APPSC AMVI) | to **50 km** | Rises | Ozone |
| 3 | Mesosphere | 50–80 km | Falls to **−100°C – coldest** (Astt. Comm. 2018) | Meteors burn |
| 4 | Thermosphere (ionosphere **80–400/640 km**) | from 80 km | Rises sharply | Ions, radio waves, aurora |
| 5 | Exosphere | outermost | Rarefied | Merges into space |
- Upward order **Troposphere → Stratosphere → Mesosphere → Thermosphere/Ionosphere** (UPSC 1998, CDS 2015, UPPCS 2005, UPPCS 2015).
- The troposphere is the layer of **smallest height** (SSC 2011).
- The **atmosphere** = the thin layer of gases around the Earth (APPSC Jr Asst); it has **no definite upper limit** and **thins gradually** until imperceptible (CDS 2015).
- Temperature trend upward: **falls, rises, falls, rises**; reversal layers: **stratosphere and thermosphere**.
| Boundary | Height |
|---|---|
| Tropopause | 8–18 km |
| Stratopause | ~50 km |
| Mesopause | ~80 km |
# Matching (UPPCS Mains 2002)
- Troposphere – weather; stratosphere – ozone; ionosphere – radio waves reflected to Earth → correct. **Mesosphere – aurorae is wrong** (aurora = ionosphere/thermosphere) → **1, 2 and 3**.

=== Troposphere and Tropopause
# Troposphere (asked 12 times)
- **Average height 13 km**; ~8 km at the poles, ~**18 km at the equator** (State PSC: "average height 13 km" is the true statement; it is not the top layer and its temperature falls with height).
- **Thickest at the equator** because **high insolation and strong convection** carry heat to great heights → UPSC 2004 (A and R true, R explains A; "thickness of the atmosphere" in the stem means the troposphere) and UPSC 2024 (I and II correct, II explains I).
- **Almost all weather** – clouds, rain, fog, hail, storms – happens here (RAS 1999; MPPSC 2023); the **cloud mass of a cyclone** rises only within the troposphere (APPSC AE 2017).
- Contains nearly all **water vapour and dust**; most important for **biological activity**.
- **Normal lapse rate ~6.5°C per km** (≈ **1°C per 165 m**) – temperature **decreases** with height (NDA 2018 option "6.4°C per km" = the approximate normal rate).
- **Agra and Darjeeling** lie on about the same latitude; Darjeeling is much colder in January because temperature falls with **height** (UPPCS 2022: A and R true, R explains A).
# Tropopause
- **Transition zone between troposphere and stratosphere**, lying **below the stratosphere**; its height **varies with season** (higher in summer) and **latitude** (higher at the equator) → APPSC G1 2018: **i, ii and iii all correct**.
- It is a **zone of mixing** (NDA 2012) – not formed by meteoric dust, not 5–10 km thick, and it is ~18 km (not 8 km) over the equator.
- Temperature at the tropopause: about **−80°C over the equator**, **−45°C over the poles**.

=== Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Ionosphere and Exosphere
# Phenomenon → layer (asked 9 times)
| Phenomenon | Layer |
|---|---|
| Jet aircraft fly in the lower **stratosphere** | **No clouds or weather**, dry, stable (JPSC 2010) |
| **Meteors burn up** | **Mesosphere** (APPSC G2) – not thermosphere/exosphere |
| **Radio waves deflected / reflected**; wireless communication; telecommunication | **Ionosphere** (UPSC 1996, UPPCS 2005, 2013, JPSC 2013) |
| **Aurora Borealis** (north) / Australis (south) | **Ionosphere** (the ionised part of the thermosphere) (UPPCS 2010) |
| **Communication satellites** (geostationary ~36,000 km) | **Exosphere** (UKPSC 2005) |
- Ionosphere: **80–400 km** (NextGen; 80–640 km in older books); **electrically charged ions** reflect radio waves back to Earth; expands by day, contracts at night.
- Aurora: solar charged particles excite upper-air atoms near the magnetic poles.
- **MST (Mesosphere–Stratosphere–Troposphere) radar** at the **National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL), Gadanki, near Tirupati** (APPSC Hostel Welfare Officer).

=== Carbon Dioxide, Water Vapour, Dust and the Greenhouse Property
# CO₂ as a greenhouse gas (asked 5 times)
- CO₂ is **transparent to incoming solar (short-wave) radiation** but **opaque to outgoing terrestrial (long-wave, infrared) radiation** – it **absorbs infrared** and re-radiates heat (UPSC 2012; CDS 2012; RAS 1992) – not UV, not all radiation, not water vapour.
- **Greenhouse effect** = heating of the atmosphere by absorption of infrared by CO₂ and other gases; **CO₂** is the gas that contributes most (APPSC FBO) and whose rise enhances it (UPPSC 2015) – not CO, O₂, N₂ or argon.
- Other GHGs: **methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour (most abundant GHG), CFCs**; not hydrogen, krypton, neon, helium or argon.
# Water vapour and dust
- **Water vapour:** up to **4%** in warm wet tropics, **under 1%** in deserts and polar air; decreases with height and from equator to poles; absorbs insolation and Earth's heat (**blanket**); decides air stability.
- **Dust:** sea salt, fine soil, smoke-soot, ash, pollen, meteor dust; mostly in lower layers; **more in subtropical and temperate regions because of dry winds** than in equatorial and polar regions → UPSC 2025: **Statement I correct, Statement II ("less dry winds") wrong**.
- Dust and salt act as **hygroscopic nuclei** for condensation → clouds.

=== Homosphere, Heterosphere and Ionospheric Layers
| Region | Height | Layers | Composition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Homosphere** | 0 to ~90 km | **Three: troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere** (APPSC Degree Lecturers) | Gases evenly mixed |
| Heterosphere | Above ~90 km | Thermosphere (ionosphere), exosphere | Layered by molecular weight: N₂ → atomic O → He → H |
- The **ionosphere is not in the homosphere** (UKPSC 2021).
| Ionospheric layer | Height | Role |
|---|---|---|
| D | 60–90 km | Absorbs medium/long waves; fades at night |
| E (Kennelly-Heaviside) | 90–150 km | Reflects medium-frequency waves |
| F (Appleton) | 150–400 km | Main reflector of short-wave radio |
- Solar flares and geomagnetic storms disturb the ionosphere and disrupt **GNSS** signals.
