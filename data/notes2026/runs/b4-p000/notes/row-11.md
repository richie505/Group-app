=== Weathering – Physical, Chemical and Biological
- **Weathering:** the natural **breaking down and decay of rocks in situ** (at or near the surface) into fragments, clays and dissolved matter; **no transport** is involved. Erosion then carries the loosened material away.
- **Geomorphic processes:** the processes that shape the Earth's surface – weathering, mass wasting, erosion, deposition (endogenic forces build relief, exogenic forces wear it down).
| Type | Process | What happens / example |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical (mechanical)** | **Exfoliation**, frost wedging (freeze-thaw), salt-crystal growth, block disintegration | Breaks rock **without chemical change**; driven mainly by **temperature variation** – typical of hot deserts and cold mountains |
| Chemical | **Solution** | Soluble minerals (rock salt, gypsum) dissolve |
| Chemical | **Carbonation** | CO₂ in rain forms **carbonic acid**, dissolves limestone (karst) and stores carbon as bicarbonate |
| Chemical | **Oxidation** | Oxygen dissolved in water reacts with iron minerals – **rusting** → red/yellow soils |
| Chemical | **Hydration** | Minerals absorb water and **swell**: anhydrite → gypsum |
| Chemical | **Hydrolysis** | Water (H⁺/hydronium) reacts with silicates: feldspar → clay; **muscovite → vermiculite** when interlayer **potassium is replaced by hydronium** (APPSC FRO) |
| **Biological** | Living things | Plant roots widen cracks; burrowing animals (earthworms, termites, rodents); lichens release acids |
- **Climate control:** chemical weathering is **fastest in hot and humid (wet)** climates – heat and abundant water speed up reactions (tropics); it is slowest in cold or dry climates.
- **Rain as a weathering agent (UPSC 2024):** rainwater holds **CO₂ in solution** (→ carbonic acid, carbonation) **and atmospheric oxygen** (→ oxidation); **both** are correct and **both explain** why rainfall weathers rocks.
- **NDA A–R:** "decay and disintegration of rock in situ is weathering" (true) and "mechanical weathering is mainly caused by temperature variation" (true) – but R does **not** explain A.
- Exfoliation is **physical**, not chemical; hydration, oxidation, solution, carbonation, hydrolysis are chemical. Oxidation is weathering, **not mass wasting**.
| Weathering landform | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Exfoliation dome | Onion-like peeling of granite (unloading or heating-cooling) |
| Tor | Residual granite blocks standing above the weathered surface where joints are widely spaced |
| Block disintegration | Physical break-up of jointed rock into blocks |
- **AP:** granite/gneiss weathers to **red soil**; basalt to **black soil**. Glacier-fed Himalayan catchments release CO₂ through **pyrite oxidation** (app CA, Sep 2026). Freeze-thaw breaks rocks in the high Himalaya.
> Most-asked: hot-humid = fastest chemical weathering; rain = CO₂ + O₂; exfoliation = physical; muscovite → vermiculite = hydrolysis.

=== Soil Texture, Water-holding and Soil Properties
| Particle | Diameter (USDA) |
|---|---|
| Sand (fine sand) | **0.05–2 mm** |
| Silt | **0.002–0.05 mm** |
| **Clay** | **Less than 0.002 mm** |
- **Water-holding capacity:** **clay > silt > sand** – the smaller the particle, the more water held; sand drains fastest.
- **Clay** holds the **maximum** water, but its pores are **small, not large** (high total porosity, tiny pores). So in the UPPSC A–R: A true, **R false**.
- **Loam:** contains **all three** particle types in roughly balanced proportions ("equal sand, silt and clay" = loamy soil); a typical loam ≈ **40% sand, 42% silt, 18% clay**; best for farming.
- Clay binds organic carbon; sandy soils lose it by leaching (app CA, Nov 2025).
- **Rainforest soils are poor** despite lush forest: heavy rain **leaches** nutrients and dead matter decomposes rapidly.
- **UPSC 2018 (agricultural soils):** high organic matter **raises** (not reduces) water-holding capacity; soil **does** take part in the **sulphur cycle**; **long irrigation can cause salinisation** – only the third statement is correct.
> Clay < 0.002 mm (asked twice); clay > silt > sand.

=== Soil Formation – Pedology, Factors and Profile
- **Pedology** = the study of soils in their natural setting (soil science); **petrology** = study of rocks; geology = study of the Earth.
- **Five classical factors:** **parent material, climate, relief/topography, organisms (biological activity), time** – **latitude is NOT** one.
| Active factors | Passive factors |
|---|---|
| **Climate**, **biological activity** (vegetation, micro-organisms) – supply energy and drive weathering and organic processes | **Parent material**, **topography (relief)**, **time** |
- **APPSC G1:** active factors = climate and biological activity (**III and IV only**).
- Soils are **surveyed and classified** on the basis of the distinctive characteristics of their **soil horizons** (the soil profile).
| Horizon | Character |
|---|---|
| O | Surface organic layer of decaying plant matter |
| A | Topsoil: minerals + organic matter; most plant roots |
| E | Light-coloured leached layer (**not in every profile**) |
| B | Subsoil: leached minerals collect |
| C | Weathered parent material, little organic matter |
| R | Unweathered bedrock |
- **AP:** climate, altitude and **bedrock** shape the soils; river silt of the **Godavari, Krishna and Pennar** is among AP's parent materials.

=== World Soil Types – Zonal, Intrazonal, Azonal; Residual and Transported
| Group | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Zonal** | Mature soils reflecting climate and vegetation | Podzol, chernozem, laterite, **sierozem** (desert grey) |
| Intrazonal | Local conditions (drainage, salts) dominate | **Solonchak** (saline), bog soils |
| **Azonal** | Young, **no developed profile** | **Lithosol** (thin soil on rock), alluvium, regosol |
- Major zonal and other world soils:
| Soil | Climate / setting |
|---|---|
| Podzol | Cold taiga (boreal conifer); thin, acidic, leached, humus-poor |
| Laterite | Hot, heavy-rain tropics; silica and lime leached, iron and aluminium oxides left; acidic |
| **Terra rossa** | Mediterranean; red clay from the **weathering of limestone** (and dolomite) |
| Chernozem | Temperate grasslands (steppes, prairies); black, humus-rich |
| Oxisols | Equatorial-tropical surfaces stable for very long (APPSC 2019) |
- **In-situ / residual = sedentary** soils (formed where the rock weathered; mainly Peninsular India). **Transported (ex-situ)** by rivers, wind, ice – mainly North India; alluvium ≈ **45.6%** of India.
- **AP cultivated land (Jul 2026):** **red ~60%** (Rayalaseema), **black (regur) ~25%**, deltaic alluvium ~6%, **laterite ~1%**.
- **FAO Status of the World's Soil Resources 2026** (3 Sep 2026): threats led by **soil erosion**; soil biodiversity loss ranked **4th of seven**, after erosion, loss of soil organic carbon and nutrient mismanagement.

=== Denudation and the Cycle of Erosion
- **Denudation** = weathering + mass wasting + erosion + transportation; order: **weathering** (first stage) → mass wasting/erosion → transportation → **deposition**.
- Agents: **running water** (most powerful), wind, ice, waves, gravity. Water = strongest cause of soil erosion (**sheet → rill → gully**; Chambal ravines = gully erosion); **wind** leads in arid areas like the **Thar**.
| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **W.M. Davis** (1899) | **"Father of (American) geomorphology"**; **geographical cycle of erosion**: landscape = **structure + process + stage**; **youth → maturity → old age**; ends in a near-flat **peneplain** with **monadnocks**; renewed uplift = **rejuvenation** |
| Walther Penck | Uplift and erosion act **simultaneously**; landform depends on their ratio; slopes retreat parallel |
- Davis put forward the cycle in **1899**.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Peneplain | Low, almost featureless plain worn by rivers almost to base level (Davis's term) |
| Monadnock | Resistant residual hill on a peneplain |
| Inselberg | Isolated residual hill of arid/semi-arid regions (Uluru) |
- **Peninsular plateau** of India = old peneplain, **rejuvenated** in places. Wegener = continental drift, Dutton = isostasy – not geomorphology's father.

=== Mass Wasting
- Downslope movement of rock and earth material **under gravity** (no transporting medium) = mass movement; the common term in exams = **landslide** (APPSC 2019).
| Type | Nature |
|---|---|
| Rockfall | Free fall of rocks from steep cliffs |
| Slide | Whole coherent mass slips along a surface |
| **Slump** | **Rotational** slip along a curved surface in weak, wet soils |
| Debris flow / mudflow / earthflow | Fast water-soil-rock mix after heavy rain or snowmelt |
| **Creep** | **Very slow** movement from wetting-drying, heating-cooling |
- Conditions: slopes **above ~30 degrees**, weak/fractured rock, bare slopes; saturated clay and silt.
- India's main trigger: **SW monsoon rainfall**, then **earthquakes**. Most at risk: **Himalaya and North-East** (also Western Ghats).
