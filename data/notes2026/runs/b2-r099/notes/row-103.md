=== Pressure Groups – Meaning, Types and Techniques
- **Pressure group**: an **organised group** that seeks to influence public policy **without contesting elections or seeking office**; also called **interest groups, lobby groups**; labelled the **"Anonymous Empire"**.
- Test: seeks to form the government → political party. **Members of a trade union** = pressure group; members of the Lok Sabha, a panchayat or the Cabinet are not (MPPSC 2017). The **Election Commission** is a constitutional authority, not a pressure group.
- **Almond and Powell**: **institutional, associational, non-associational, anomic**.
| Type | Indian examples |
|---|---|
| **Business** – most powerful and largest | **FICCI**, CII, ASSOCHAM – work for **commerce and industry interests** |
| Trade union | AITUC, INTUC, CITU, BMS, HMS |
| **Agrarian** | Bharatiya Kisan Union, All India Kisan Sabha (oldest), **Shetkari Sanghatana** (Maharashtra); **Swabhimani Shetkari Sanghatana** = **farmers' union**, Maharashtra, founder **Raju Shetti** |
| Professional | Indian Medical Association, Bar associations |
| Women / feminist, caste, religious, student | Feminist groups are organised **social (interest) groups** pressing for women's rights |

=== Media, Press Freedom and the Press Council of India
- **Fourth Estate** = **the Press** (media) – watchdog and **agenda-setter**: media sets the public agenda **because** it selectively filters news (A and R true, R explains A).
- **Freedom of the press** is not named in the Constitution; it is read into **Article 19(1)(a)** (Art. 19), not Arts. 14, 21 or 32.
- **Press Council of India**: first set up **1966** (Press Council Act, 1965); now under the **Press Council Act, 1978**; **statutory, quasi-judicial**; **Chairman + 28 members**; **print media only**; can warn, admonish, censure – no punitive powers. Chairman by custom a **retired SC judge**, chosen by the RS Chairman, LS Speaker and a PCI member.
| PCI Chairman | Term |
|---|---|
| **Markandey Katju** | 2011-2014 |
| **Justice C.K. (Chandramauli Kumar) Prasad** | 2014-2022 |
| **Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai** | 17 Jun 2022 – Dec 2025; **again from 24 April 2026** (three years) – current, as of Nov 2026 |
- Problems: paid news, fake news, concentrated ownership. RSF index (May 2026): India **"very serious"**.

=== SHGs and Women's Microfinance – DWCRA, SERP, Velugu
- SHGs in AP are popularly called **DWCRA groups**; a **self-help group** has **10 to 20 members** (NABARD / national norm).
- **Velugu** (AP District Poverty Initiatives Project) was launched in **2000** to organise and federate women's SHGs; run by **SERP** (autonomous society of the Rural Development Dept); renamed **Indira Kranthi Patham** (Velugu + DWCRA merged) under Congress; Velugu name restored after 2014.
- **SAPAP** (1995, UNDP): base for Velugu. **Pavala Vaddi** (2004) for SHGs. **Mana Kirana**: general stores run by DWCRA groups supplying quality goods.
- **MEPMA** (urban): G.O. Ms 414 of 4 Jun 2007, launched **1 Sep 2007**; SHG → Slum Level Federation → Town Level Federation.
| National body | Year | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Rashtriya Mahila Kosh** | **1993** | Society (Societies Registration Act 1860), **Ministry of WCD**; lends to NGOs/MFIs who on-lend to poor women |
| SHG-Bank Linkage | **1992** (NABARD) | Banks lend to SHGs |
| DAY-NRLM | — | **Rural Development**; Pashu, Krishi, Bank, Bima, **Poshan** Sakhis |

=== NGOs, CBOs and Social Service
- NGOs are to be treated as **partners of government in development** (National Policy on the Voluntary Sector, **2007**, Planning Commission: partnership, not subordination). The APPSC key printed "**people's representatives**".
- **Social service** is provided by **both government and private individuals**, profit and non-profit organisations.
- **Community Based Organisations (CBOs)**: non-profit groups that **extend social services locally**.
- Civil society = the **"third sector"** beside State and market; shift from welfare delivery to **rights, advocacy and watchdog** work.
| Legal form | Law |
|---|---|
| Society | Societies Registration Act, 1860 |
| Private trust | Indian Trusts Act, 1882 |
| Public charitable trust | **State laws** (e.g. Bombay Public Trusts Act, 1950) |
| Not-for-profit company | Section 8, Companies Act, 2013 |
- **Bhagavatula Charitable Trust** (Yellamanchili, Visakhapatnam) – founded by **B.V. Parameswara Rao**.
- **Amaravati farmers' agitation**: Dec 2019 – June 2024 (**1,631 days**), led by **Amaravati Parirakshana Samithi**.

=== FCRA Regulation
| Year | Change |
|---|---|
| **1976** | First FCRA |
| 2010 | FCRA 2010 replaced it |
| 2016 | Retrospective: Indian subsidiaries of foreign firms not "foreign source" (after Delhi HC 2014) |
| 2020 | Designated account at **SBI, New Delhi**; admin cap **50% → 20%**; **sub-granting banned** |
| 25 Mar 2026 | Amendment Bill (asset takeover of cancelled NGOs) – deferred |
| **22 Jun 2026** | Amendment Rules notified |
- Administered by the **MHA** (FEMA by RBI and ED). Registration valid **5 years**; **14,538** active (15 Apr 2026). Cancellation grounds: breach, **non-functioning**, defaults – not office location.
- **Public Health Foundation of India**: FCRA licence suspended/cancelled (April 2017) for using foreign funds for **lobbying with parliamentarians** (on tobacco control).
- **Rules 2026**: foreign citizens other than **PIOs** as key functionaries make an NGO ineligible; renewal needs **₹10 lakh** spent over two years.
- Every NGO receiving foreign contributions must register (or get prior permission) under the **FCRA**, besides its registration as a society, trust or Section 8 company.
