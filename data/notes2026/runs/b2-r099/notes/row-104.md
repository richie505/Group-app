=== Meaning of Globalisation and Cultural Change
- **Globalisation**: free flow of **goods, services, capital (investment), technology, ideas and people** across borders – "worldwide interconnectedness"; flows of ideas, capital, commodities and people.
- Intended outcomes: reduced trade barriers, free flow of capital and labour – **not** the flow of **classified military technology**.
- Essence (UPSC Astt. Comm. 2017): the coming together of **worldwide social relations bypassing territories** and **thinking transterritorially about global risks** – not mere inter-state relations or suprastate governance.
- **Global village** (Marshall McLuhan) rests on **transport and communication** development.
- Culture and tradition are said to disappear under **globalisation** (homogenisation), though local culture does not fully end.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Diffusion** | **Spread of cultural traits from one society to another** |
| Acculturation | Mutual exchange; both change, each stays distinct |
| Assimilation | Minority absorbed; loses identity |
| Glocalisation | Global products adapted to local tastes |
| Homogenisation | Convergence to one (Western) pattern |
| Hybridisation | Blend of global and local |
| McDonaldisation | Efficiency, calculability, predictability, control |
- **Economic Survey 2025-26**: "hyper-globalisation" → **"geostrategic globalisation"**.

=== Anti-globalisation Critique and Social Forums
- **Vandana Shiva**: founder of **Navdanya** – seed, food, water, land and knowledge sovereignty; against biopiracy.
- **Seattle, 1999**: protests at the WTO Ministerial. **World Social Forum**: first **Porto Alegre, Brazil, 2001**; **Mumbai 2004**.
- **Asian Social Forum**: **Hyderabad, 2-7 January 2003**. **European Social Forum**: first held in **Florence, November 2002**.
- **Samir Amin** (Egyptian economist) directed the **Third World Forum, Dakar (Senegal)** from 1980; chaired the World Forum for Alternatives.
- **Joseph Stiglitz**, *Globalization and Its Discontents* (**2002**) – criticised **IMF** conditions. **Sharad Joshi** supported globalisation; **Tikait**, **Nanjundaswamy** opposed it.
- Criticism: widens rich-poor gaps; "commercial imperialism"; left and right critiques.

=== Labour and Migration
| Concept | Scholar |
|---|---|
| Informal sector (1971-73) | **Keith Hart** |
| **Footloose labour** – migrant, circular, casual (*Footloose Labour*, 1996) | **Jan Breman** |
- **Gig workers** (NITI Aayog 2022): **77 lakh** (2020-21) → **2.35 crore** by 2029-30.
- **World Migration Report 2026** (**IOM**, biennial since 2000): **304 million** migrants (**3.7%**); **India = largest diaspora**; India-UAE corridor **5th**, India-US **6th**.
- **India = top remittance recipient**, about **$129 billion (2024)**; Gulf labour = backbone of the **remittance economy**; remittances keep the CAD steady despite the **trade deficit**.
- AP: **East and West Godavari, Kadapa** – Gulf emigration; **APNRTS** monitored about **10 lakh** in the Gulf (Mar 2026). **PLFS 2020-21**: migrants **28.9%**; rural migrants 48% of women vs 5.9% of men. **MEA**: Migration and Mobility Partnerships; Pre-Departure Orientation Training.

=== MNCs and Impact on Groups
- Negative effects of MNCs: **large dividend remittances**, **crowding out domestic saving and investment**, **influence in key sectors**, layoffs of artisans and domestic firms. **Fulfilling export obligations** is **not** a negative effect.
| Group | Effect |
|---|---|
| Consumers / youth | More choice, global brands, cultural fusion; risk of mindless aping |
| Farmers | WTO-era debt-linked suicides – **Maharashtra and AP** |
| Small coffee growers | **98%** small; face Brazil, Vietnam |
| Women | Self-employed women nearly **tripled** since 2017-18 |
| Youth | **29%** of tertiary-educated unemployed (2023-24) |
