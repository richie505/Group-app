=== Partition of Bengal, 1905 – Dates, Curzon, Motive, Annulment
# Key facts
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Viceroy | **Lord Curzon** (1899-1905) – not Minto, Hardinge, Lytton or Napier |
| First proposal | December 1903 – Moderate protests 1903-05 |
| Announced | **19 July 1905** (order issued; published 20 July) |
| Took effect | **16 October 1905** – not 15 October |
| New province | **Eastern Bengal and Assam** (Dacca, Chittagong, Rajshahi divisions + Assam), capital **Dacca**, 31 million |
| Rest of Bengal | West Bengal + Bihar + Orissa, 54 million – **18 million Bengalis, 36 million Biharis and Oriyas** (Bengalis a minority) |
| Lt Governor of Bengal | **Sir Andrew Fraser** (1903-08), active in framing the partition |
| Annulled | **12 December 1911**, **Delhi Durbar of King George V** – capital also shifted Calcutta → Delhi |
| Viceroy at annulment | **Lord Hardinge II** |
- **Stated motive:** **administrative convenience** – province of about 80 million "too big"; Curzon called it a readjustment of boundaries.
- **Real motive:** **weaken Bengali nationalism** – "the nerve centre of Indian nationalism" (Curzon) – and **divide Hindus and Muslims** (Curzon at Dacca wooed the Muslims).
- **H.H. Risley:** "**Bengal united is a power; Bengal divided will pull in several different ways.**"
- **Surendranath Banerjea:** the announcement "**fell like a bombshell**"; he and K.K. Mitra **led the anti-partition agitation**.
- **NDA 2013 statement:** Curzon **separated Dacca, Chittagong and Rajshahi divisions from Bengal and joined them to Assam** ✓; he did **not** succeed Canning, introduce the subsidiary alliance (Wellesley) or sympathise with the Congress.
- **Impact:** the partition was the event with the **maximum impact on the freedom movement** of the options usually offered (Prince of Wales 1875, Delhi Durbar 1877, ICS 1861).
- **Durbars:** 1877 (Lytton, Victoria Kaiser-i-Hind), **1903 (Curzon, Edward VII's coronation)**, **1911 (George V – the only one a sovereign attended; annulment)**. Japan's victory over Russia (1904-05) fuelled the anger.
> Traps: annulment **1911** (not 1910, 1912 or 1947); it did not last until WWI, the CDM or 1947. "First partition of Bengal" = 1905 (the second = 1947).

=== Launch of the Swadeshi Movement – Cause, 7 August 1905, Boycott
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 13 July 1905 | **K.K. (Krishna Kumar) Mitra** first suggested **boycott** of British goods in his weekly **Sanjibani** |
| 19/20 July 1905 | Partition announced |
| **7 August 1905** | **Calcutta Town Hall** meeting – **boycott resolution** passed; **formal proclamation of the Swadeshi Movement**; boycott adopted as **national policy** |
| 16 October 1905 | Partition took effect – **day of mourning**, fasting, hartal, Ganga bath, **Raksha Bandhan**, Federation Hall foundation |
- **Immediate cause:** the **partition of Bengal by Curzon** – not Tilak's sentence, the Lajpat Rai–Ajit Singh deportation (1907) or the Chapekar sentence; not Curzon's universities or municipal measures.
- **First use:** **Swadeshi and Boycott** were adopted as methods of struggle for the **first time** in the **anti-partition agitation** – not Home Rule, NCM or the Simon boycott.
- **Movement born of the partition:** the **Swadeshi Movement** (1905-1911) – not NCM, CDM, Quit India or "Purna Swaraj".
- **National Handloom Day = 7 August**, observed since **2015**, commemorates the **Swadeshi Movement** launched on 7 Aug 1905.
- **Phases:** Moderates' petitions and meetings (1903-05: S.N. Banerjea, K.K. Mitra, Prithwishchandra Ray) → **Extremists take over from 1906** (passive resistance, Swaraj).
- **Gandhi** had **no part** in it – he was in South Africa; Khilafat, Individual Satyagraha and Quit India were his movements.
> "Launched / proclaimed" = **7 Aug 1905**; "took effect" = **16 Oct 1905**; "announced" = **19 July 1905** (BPSC key; many books say 20 July).

=== Programme, Samitis, National Education and Swadeshi Enterprises
# Programme
- Programmes against the partition: **Boycott, Swadeshi, National education** (plus passive resistance) – **NOT Non-cooperation** (1920).
- **Boycott:** foreign cloth, salt, sugar; bonfires of foreign cloth; picketing; priests refused foreign sugar, washermen foreign clothes – boycott was the **most successful** form.
- **Samitis:** **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti** of **Barisal** – **Ashwini Kumar Dutt** (school teacher), who led the **Muslim peasants of Barisal** – not Liaquat Hussain, Pulin Das, Surya Sen or Barindra Ghosh.
- **Mass mobilisation:** public meetings, processions, jatras, melas, folk songs, akharas and **lathi clubs**; Tilak's Ganapati and Shivaji festivals.
- **Atmashakti** (self-reliance) – constructive work; the movement was **not "exclusively political"** – it reshaped art, literature, science and industry.
- **Calcutta INC 1906 (Dadabhai Naoroji):** **Swaraj** declared the goal; resolutions on Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education.

# Spread and extent
| Region | Leader |
|---|---|
| Bengal | Aurobindo Ghosh, Bipin Chandra Pal, Ashwini Kumar Dutt |
| **Madras** | **V.O. Chidambaram Pillai** |
| Bombay / Poona | Tilak |
| Punjab | Ajit Singh, Lala Lajpat Rai |
| Delhi | **Syed Haider Raza** |
| Andhra (deltaic) | known as the **Vandemataram Movement** |
- **Participation:** students and **women** took an active part (first time); **some prominent Muslims** joined; the **Muslim peasantry and peasants in general stayed away** (Barisal an exception); it was **not limited to Bengal**.
- **British journalist H.W. Nevinson** covered the Swadeshi Movement and Surat 1907 (The New Spirit in India).
- **Sumit Sarkar** – author of *The Swadeshi Movement in Bengal 1903-1908*.
> UPSC 2002 "not correct": Barisal peasants led by Liaquat Hussain ✗ (Ashwini Kumar Dutt).

=== Swadeshi in Art, Literature and Song
| Field | Person | Work / fact |
|---|---|---|
| Song | **Rabindranath Tagore** | **Amar Sonar Bangla** (1905) – later **national anthem of Bangladesh** |
| Song | Bankim Chandra Chatterjee | **Bande Mataram** (Anandamath) – **theme song / slogan** of the Swadeshi agitation |
| Painting | **Abanindranath Tagore** | **Bharat Mata** (1905) – first painted image of Bharat Mata; Bengal School – **revivalistic** style, wash technique |
| Art society | **Abanindranath** and Gaganendranath Tagore | **Indian Society of Oriental Art**, Calcutta **1907**; Nandalal Bose its first scholar |
| Book | **Sakharam Ganesh Deuskar** | **Desher Katha** (1904) – banned 1910 |
| Other songs | Rajanikanta Sen, Dwijendralal Ray, Mukunda Das | Swadeshi songs |
- **Desher Katha (UPSC 2020):** warned against the colonial **"hypnotic conquest of the mind"** ✓; inspired **swadeshi street plays and folk songs** ✓; "desh" meant **India, not Bengal alone** ✗ → **1 and 2 only**.
- **Tagore:** "**Gurudev**"; first Asian Nobel laureate (1913); preached **Atmashakti**; **critic of the Swadeshi Movement's economic side** (Ghare Baire) and pleaded for **East-West understanding**.
- **Tagore's poetry, in order:** **Manasi (1890) → Sonar Tari (1894) → Gitanjali (1910) → Gitimalya (1914) → Balaka (1916)**.
- **Vande Mataram** was the slogan of the **Swadeshi / anti-partition** movement – not 1857, NCM, Champaran, Rowlatt or Quit India.
> Rabindranath did **not** found the Indian Society of Oriental Art; Nandalal Bose, Asit Haldar and Amrita Sher-Gil are distractors.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| Dec 1904 | INC **Bombay** session (Henry Cotton) |
| Jul-Oct 1905 | Partition announced (19/20 Jul), Swadeshi proclaimed (7 Aug), effective (16 Oct) |
| Dec 1906 | **Muslim League**, Dacca; Calcutta INC – **Swaraj** the goal |
| Dec 1907 | **Surat Split** – first split of the Congress |
| 12 Dec 1911 | Partition annulled; capital to Delhi announced |
| 1916 | Home Rule Leagues |
| 1917 | Champaran Satyagraha |
| 1919 | Jallianwala Bagh |
| 1930 | Chittagong Armoury Raid |
| 1947 | Mountbatten Plan |
- **NDA 2013:** Bombay session → partition → Muslim League → Surat = **I, III, II, IV**.
- **CDS 2007:** partition → Surat → capital to Delhi → Home Rule League = **III, II, I, IV**.
- **Swaraj chronology:** partition decision → Swadeshi declaration → Swaraj goal (1906) → Surat (1907) = **1, 3, 2, 4**.
