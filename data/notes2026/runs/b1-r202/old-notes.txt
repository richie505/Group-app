## SUB 0 | Partition of Bengal, 1905
[202:0:0] # Key Facts
[202:0:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [202:0:1.0] Viceroy | Lord Curzon – called Bengal "the nerve centre of Indian nationalism"
  [202:0:1.1] Announced | 19/20 July 1905
  [202:0:1.2] Took effect | 16 October 1905 (not 15 October)
  [202:0:1.3] New province | Eastern Bengal and Assam – capital Dacca
  [202:0:1.4] Rest of Bengal | Capital Calcutta
  [202:0:1.5] Annulled | 12 December 1911, Delhi Durbar of King George V
  [202:0:1.6] Viceroy at annulment | Lord Hardinge II
  [202:0:1.7] Capital shift | Calcutta → Delhi, announced 1911 at the same Durbar; transfer completed 1912
[202:0:2] # Division and Population
[202:0:3] TABLE head: Part | Population
  [202:0:3.0] Eastern Bengal and Assam | 31 million
  [202:0:3.1] Rest of Bengal | 54 million – 18 million Bengalis, 36 million Biharis and Oriyas → Bengalis a minority in their own province
[202:0:4] # Stated vs Real Motives
[202:0:5] - **Stated**: **administrative convenience** – province "too big"; Curzon called it a redrawing of administrative boundaries.
[202:0:6] - **Real**: **weaken Bengali nationalism**; divide **Hindus from Muslims** – nationalists saw it as **Divide and Rule**.
[202:0:7] - **H.H. Risley** (Home Secretary): "**Bengal united is a power; Bengal divided will pull in several different ways.**"
[202:0:8] - **Surendranath Banerjee**: partition "**fell like a bombshell**".
[202:0:9] - **Japan's victory over Russia (1904-05)** fuelled the anger (Sumit Sarkar).
[202:0:10] The **1903 Durbar** celebrated **Edward VII's coronation**; the **1911 Durbar** (George V) **annulled** the partition (APPSC 2022 A-R). 1911 = the only Durbar a sovereign attended.
[202:0:11] King **George V** was the monarch at annulment; **Curzon** announced the partition; **Hardinge II** was Viceroy in 1911.
## SUB 1 | Launch of the Swadeshi Movement
[202:1:0] - **Immediate cause**: the partition by Lord Curzon.
[202:1:1] - **Moderate campaign (1903-05)**: petitions and meetings by **S.N. Banerjee, K.K. Mitra, Prithwishchandra Ray** – failed.
[202:1:2] - **K.K. (Krishna Kumar) Mitra** – **first suggested boycott**, in weekly **Sanjibani (13 July 1905)**; Brahmo Samaj leader.
[202:1:3] - **7 August 1905** – **boycott resolution at Calcutta Town Hall**; Swadeshi **formally proclaimed**; boycott of British goods adopted as national policy.
[202:1:4] - **16 October 1905** – partition day: **day of mourning, hartal, Ganga bath, Rakhi-bandhan**, Swadeshi vow.
[202:1:5] - **National Handloom Day** = **7 August**, observed since **2015** (APPSC 2019).
[202:1:6] - Leadership passed from **Moderates to Extremists in 1906**.
[202:1:7] Answer **7 August 1905** for "launched / proclaimed"; 16 Oct 1905 = partition took effect.
[202:1:8] 20 July 1905 | partition decision | Curzon
7 Aug 1905 | Swadeshi declared | Town Hall
## SUB 2 | Techniques and Programme
[202:2:0] TABLE head: Technique | Form
  [202:2:0.0] Boycott | Foreign cloth, salt, sugar; bonfires of cloth; extended to courts, schools, government service; priests refused rituals using foreign goods
  [202:2:0.1] Swadeshi / Atmashakti | Self-reliance
  [202:2:0.2] Samitis | Swadesh Bandhab Samiti, Barisal – Ashwini Kumar Dutt (magic lanterns, social work, arbitration courts)
  [202:2:0.3] Mass mobilisation | Jatras, folk songs, melas, volunteer corps, akharas, lathi clubs
  [202:2:0.4] National education | National schools and colleges
[202:2:1] - Programmes against partition: **Boycott, Swadeshi, National education** – **NOT Non-cooperation**.
[202:2:2] - **Barisal peasant agitation** – Ashwini Kumar Dutt, not Liaquat Hussain.
[202:2:3] - **Calcutta INC 1906 (Naoroji)**: Swaraj declared the goal; resolutions on **Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education**.
[202:2:4] - Movement not linked to **Gandhi** (he was in South Africa); period **1905-1911**.
[202:2:5] Swadeshi = **first mass movement**; boycott and Swadeshi **first adopted as methods** here; birth of mass nationalism (UPSC 2016).

## SUB 6 | Muslim League and Surat Split
[202:6:0] # All-India Muslim League
[202:6:1] - Founded **30 December 1906** at **Dacca**, at the All-India Muhammadan Educational Conference.
[202:6:2] - Proposal by **Nawab Salimullah**; **Aga Khan III** first (honorary) president.
[202:6:3] - Context: **Simla Deputation (1906)** → **Morley-Minto Reforms (1909)** gave **separate electorates for Muslims**.
[202:6:4] # Surat Split (December 1907)
[202:6:5] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [202:6:5.0] Moderate nominee / president | Rash Behari Ghosh
  [202:6:5.1] Extremist demand | Session at Nagpur with Tilak or Lajpat Rai as president; keep the Calcutta 1906 resolutions
  [202:6:5.2] Venue | Moved to Surat (fearing Tilak's election)
  [202:6:5.3] Result | Session broke up; Extremists expelled
  [202:6:5.4] Minto | Called it "a great triumph for us"
[202:6:6] - Aftermath (**1908**): **Muzaffarpur bomb**, **Alipore case**, **Newspapers Act**, **Tilak sent to Mandalay**.
[202:6:7] - Reunion: **Lucknow 1916** – Extremists re-entered; **Lucknow Pact** (Tilak, Jinnah).

## SUB 8 | Impact and Assessment
[202:8:0] - **Weaknesses**: few **peasants and Muslims** joined; **faded by 1908** under repression and the Surat Split.
[202:8:1] - Achievements: first mass movement; new methods (boycott, Swadeshi, national education, passive resistance) later used by Gandhi; growth of Indian industry and national culture.
[202:8:2] - **Phase classification**: Swadeshi (1905) and Surat Split (1907) = **Extremist phase**; Morley-Minto (1909) = government response; Home Rule (1916) and Lucknow Pact (1916) = reunion phase before the **Gandhian phase**.
[202:8:3] - **Home Rule Leagues**: Tilak's **April 1916**, Besant's **Sept 1916**; Besant INC president **Calcutta 1917**.
[202:8:4] - **Sumit Sarkar** – author of **The Swadeshi Movement in Bengal**.
[202:8:5] - Tilak born **23 July 1856, Ratnagiri**.

=== NOTES 1:203 | Swadeshi and Vandemataram Movement in Andhra – Bipin Chandra Pal's Tour, Student Participation
## SUB 0 | The Vandemataram Movement – Name, Phase, Trigger
[203:0:0] - In Andhra the Swadeshi Movement was popularly called the **Vandemataram Movement**.
[203:0:1] - **Andhra phase**: **1906-1911**.
[203:0:2] - **Trigger**: **Surendranath Banerjee's arrest (1906)** → protests at **Rajahmundry, Kakinada, Vizianagaram, Gooty**.
[203:0:3] - **Slogan**: "**Vandemataram – Manade Rajyam**" (self-rule is ours).
[203:0:4] - "**Vande Mataram**" became a **greeting**; officials' curbs on it led to the **Rajahmundry and Kakinada incidents**.
[203:0:5] - **Madras, 1906**: an **Industrial Association** (to promote Swadeshi goods) and a **Swadeshi League**.
[203:0:6] - The movement was tied to **national education, Swadeshi stores and student politics** – not limited to elites.
[203:0:7] Significance: **first mass political awakening in Andhra**; national education, Swadeshi stores and student politics all began here.
[203:0:8] # Japan's Influence
[203:0:9] - **Japan's win over Russia (1904-05)** inspired Andhra youth; many went to **Japan for industrial training**.
[203:0:10] - **Munagala Raja** named his sons **Togo and Nogi**.
[203:0:11] - **Adipudi Somanatha Rao** wrote **Japan Charitra** (history of Japan in Telugu), dedicated to the Raja of Munagala; the play **Japaniyam** is by **Sriram Veerabrahmam**.
