=== Ozone Depletion – CFCs, Chlorine and Bromine
# Cause
- **CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons)** are the **main cause** of ozone depletion and of the "ozone hole" – not CO2, CO, methane, N2 or SO2.
- **Full form:** CFC = **Chlorofluorocarbon** – carbon + chlorine + fluorine; entirely **man-made**, non-toxic, non-flammable.
- **Mechanism:** under UV in the stratosphere CFCs release **chlorine**; **chlorine and bromine atoms destroy ozone catalytically** (one Cl atom breaks thousands of O3 molecules before it is locked up again) → more UV reaches the Earth.
- **Bromine** comes from **halons** (fire extinguishers) – about **100 times more potent** than CFCs; **carbon tetrachloride** and **methyl chloroform** also release chlorine.
- Depletion happens in the **stratosphere** (not in the lower atmosphere where humans live, not in the mesosphere).
- **CFCs are also greenhouse gases** – they cause both warming and ozone loss (A-R: both true, but "CFCs are used in fridges, ACs, aerosols" is **not the explanation** of that).
- **A–R (APPSC EO):** "ozone depletion = reduction of stratospheric ozone" ✓; "caused by high chlorine and bromine from CFCs and halons" ✓ → **R explains A**.
- **Wrong statements trap (TSPSC JL):** ozone is in the **stratosphere, not mesosphere**; it is the **chlorine**, not the carbon, of CFCs that splits O3.

=== Ozone Layer, Dobson Unit and the Ozone Hole
# Ozone layer
- **Ozone = O3** (three oxygen atoms); formed when **UV radiation splits O2** (UV + O2 → O3).
- About **90%** of ozone is in the **stratosphere** (**10–50 km**; main layer ~15–30 km) – **protective**, absorbs harmful **UV (UV-B)**; ~10% is in the troposphere, where it is a **pollutant**.
- Ozone protects the biosphere from **ultraviolet rays** – not infra-red, X-rays or gamma rays.
- **Natural regulation:** stratospheric ozone is naturally regulated by **nitrogen oxides – "nitrogen dioxide"** in books and keys (the NO/NO2 cycle; N2O from soils is the source gas).
# Dobson Unit
- **Dobson Unit (DU)** measures the **thickness/concentration of ozone in a column of air from the ground to the top of the atmosphere** – not decibel (sound), angstrom (length), knot, poise or Maxwell.
- **1 DU = 0.01 mm** of pure ozone at **0°C and 1 atm**; an **"ozone hole"** = total column ozone **below 220 DU**.
# Ozone hole
- Most affected region: **Antarctica**; seasonal thinning in the **Southern Hemisphere spring (Aug–Nov)**.
- **Polar stratospheric clouds** in extreme cold turn stable chlorine compounds into **active chlorine**, which destroys ozone when sunlight returns.
- Ozone began to **drop sharply in the 1980s**; the Antarctic hole was **reported in 1985 by the British Antarctic Survey** (NextGen: first noticed 1983).
- **Tibetan ozone low ("halo") 2005:** discovered by **G.W. Kent Moore**, University of Toronto.
- **Recovery:** NASA/WMO report healing of the Antarctic hole, mainly due to the **ban on CFCs** under Montreal.
| Region | Back to 1980 levels |
|---|---|
| Most of the world | **2040** |
| Arctic | **2045** |
| Antarctic | **2066** |

=== Vienna Convention and Montreal Protocol
- **Vienna Convention (1985):** framework treaty for **protection of the ozone layer** – research, monitoring, reporting.
- **Montreal Protocol:** the action treaty under Vienna to **phase out production and consumption of ozone-depleting substances (ODS)** – not GHGs/global warming, acid rain, smog, DDT, petrol or marine life.
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Signed | **16 September 1987**, **Montreal**, Canada |
| In force | **1 January 1989** ("the 1989 treaty to protect the ozone layer") |
| Nature | **Legally binding**; adjustments/amendments: London, Copenhagen, Montreal, Beijing, **Quito Adjustment**, **Kigali (2016)** |
| Coverage | ~100 chemicals: CFCs, halons, CTC, methyl chloroform, HCFCs, methyl bromide |
| Parties | **198** – **first and only UN treaties universally ratified** (Vienna + Montreal) |
| Secretariat | **Ozone Secretariat, Nairobi** (UNEP) |
- Montreal is **not part of the Kyoto Protocol** (Kyoto belongs to UNFCCC) → statements "signed 1987", "phases out ODS", "universally ratified" are correct.
- **UNEP's role:** **monitors and implements** international agreements like the Montreal Protocol (Ozone Secretariat).
- **Results:** **~99% of ODS phased out** (2024); avoids **0.5–1°C** warming by 2050; widely called the most **successful** environmental treaty.
- **Multilateral Fund (1991):** technical and financial aid to developing countries. **MOP37** focused on implementing **Kigali**.
| Treaty | Concern |
|---|---|
| **Montreal Protocol** | **Ozone / ODS** |
| Kyoto Protocol (1997, UNFCCC) | Binding GHG cuts by developed countries |
| Basel (1989) | Hazardous waste |
| Cartagena (2000, CBD) | Biosafety |
| Nagoya (2010, CBD) | Access and benefit sharing |
| Bretton Woods (1944) | IMF/World Bank – economic, not ozone |
> Montreal "related to / focussed on" asked 15 times; signing year **1987** (JL 2020 ×2).

=== ODS vs HFCs – Refrigerants, Foams and Fire Extinguishers
| Substance | ODS? | Main uses |
|---|---|---|
| **CFCs** (CFC-12 in refrigerators) | Yes – main culprit | Refrigerators, ACs, aerosols, foams, solvents |
| **Halons** (Halon-1211, 1301) | Yes – ~100× CFC | **Portable fire extinguishers** |
| **HCFCs** (HCFC-141b) | Yes, weaker – interim substitute | **Foam blowing** (HCFC-141b), ACs |
| CTC, **methyl chloroform** | Yes | Solvents, feedstock |
| **Methyl bromide** | Yes | **Agricultural fumigation and quarantine** |
| **Nitrous oxide (N2O)** | Not controlled under Montreal (books: "not responsible") | Fertilisers, laughing gas; a GHG |
| **HFCs** | **No (non-ODS)** – but **very high GWP** | ODS substitutes in ACs, refrigeration → **Kigali** |
- **HFCs** are entirely **man-made**; used as **aerosol propellants, foam-blowing agents and fire retardants/suppressants (clean agents)** – **not lubricants** → UPSC 2023: **only three**.
# Refrigerants and extinguishers
| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| **Freon** (CFC; DuPont trade name, stable halocarbon) | **Domestic refrigerators** |
| **Mafron** (halogenated hydrocarbon, trade name) | Refrigerator gas (UKPSC 2010 key) |
| **Liquid ammonia** | **Large cold-storage plants** |
| **Carbon dioxide** | **Best extinguisher for electrical fires** (non-conducting) |
| Soda-acid extinguisher | **NaHCO3 + dilute H2SO4 → CO2** |
- **Ammonia** liquefies at ambient temperature only at **high pressure** (not low) → A true, R false (IAS 2000).
- Radon, sodium, fluorine, neon, oxygen are not refrigerants.

=== Kigali Amendment and India's Ozone Action
# Kigali Amendment (2016)
- Adopted **2016, Kigali, Rwanda** (197 nations); **amends the Montreal Protocol** (not Kyoto, Bali or Paris); legally binding; in force **1 January 2019**.
- Phases down **HFCs** – not ODS but potent GHGs (ACs, refrigeration); expected to avoid up to **0.5°C** warming by 2100; Parties cut HFCs **80–85%** from baselines by the late 2040s.
| Group | Baseline | Freeze | Final cut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Developed | 2011–13 | Reductions from 2019 | 85% by 2036 |
| Group 1 (incl. China) | 2020–22 | 2024 | 80% by 2045 |
| **India** (Group 2, with Gulf states, Iran, Iraq, **Pakistan**) | **2024–26** | **2028** | **85% by 2047** |
- India **ratified Kigali in 2021**; MoEFCC: **no environmental clearance** for new/additional HFC production **after 31 December 2027**.
# India's ozone action
- **Ozone Cell (MoEFCC)** = India's **National Ozone Unit**; **ODS (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000** under the **Environment (Protection) Act**.
| Substance | Phased out in India by |
|---|---|
| Halons (except essential use) | **1 Jan 2001** |
| CFCs in manufacturing (except MDIs) | 1 Jan 2003 |
| CTC, methyl chloroform, CFCs in MDIs | 1 Jan 2010 |
| Methyl bromide | **1 Jan 2015** |
| HCFCs | Allowed up to **1 Jan 2040** |
- **HPMP Stage-II:** India **first developing country** to fully phase out **HCFC-141b**, used by **foam manufacturing enterprises** (rigid foam) – the most potent ODS after CFCs.
- **India Cooling Action Plan (MoEFCC, March 2019):** 20-year horizon to **2037–38**; cut **cooling demand 20–25%** and **refrigerant demand 25–30%**; six themes – space cooling in buildings, cold chain, servicing, domestic production, R&D, transport – **not coal-based power**.

=== UV Radiation – Effects of Ozone Loss
| Target | Effect of more UV-B |
|---|---|
| Humans | **Skin cancer, eye cataracts, sunburn**, immune suppression, DNA damage; 1% ozone loss raises harmful UV ~**2%** |
| Crops | Reduced growth and photosynthesis |
| Phytoplankton / marine | Damage to plankton and fish larvae (**not** more phytoplankton) |
| Materials | Degrades plastic, rubber, wood |
- UV bands: UV-A 315–400 nm, **UV-B 280–315 nm**, UV-C 100–280 nm.
| Term | Meaning (match, NTRUHS) |
|---|---|
| Stratospheric ozone | **Protective layer** against UV |
| Ozone depletion | **Allows harmful UV rays** to reach Earth |
| UV-B increase | **Eye damage, skin cancer, sunburn** |
| Tropospheric ozone | **Air pollutant** harmful to health, plants, materials |
- Not effects of UV: respiratory infections, hearing loss, digestive disorders.

=== Chemicals and Waste Conventions – Basel, Rotterdam, Stockholm, Minamata
| Convention | Year (in force) | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Basel** | **1989** (1992) | **Transboundary movement of hazardous wastes** and their disposal |
| **Rotterdam** | 1998 (**2004**) | **Prior Informed Consent (PIC)** for hazardous chemicals and pesticides in trade |
| **Stockholm** | **2001** (**2004**) | **Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)** |
| **Minamata** | **2013** (**2017**) | **Mercury** |
# Basel
- India ratified **1992**; Basel's PIC operates in India under the **Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016**.
- **Ban Amendment:** bans hazardous-waste exports from **OECD/EU + Liechtenstein** to other countries; in force **5 December 2019**. Wrong options: carbon emissions, wetlands, deforestation, biodiversity.
# Rotterdam and Stockholm
- **PIC:** the **importing country must agree** before a listed chemical is exported; Annex III lists **pesticides and industrial chemicals banned or severely restricted**. India **acceded 2005**; DNAs: **Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilisers + Ministry of Agriculture**.
- **BRS:** Basel, Rotterdam, Stockholm share **one joint secretariat** under UNEP; India – MoEFCC **Hazardous Substances Management Division**.
- **POPs:** **toxic, persistent, bioaccumulate, travel long distances**; **POPs Review Committee** assesses candidates. India ratified Stockholm **2006**; **7 more POPs ratified 2020**.
- Initial **12 "dirty dozen"** (DDT, PCBs, dioxins, furans, aldrin…); now **37** listed.
| Annex | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A | Elimination | Aldrin |
| B | Restriction | **DDT** (malaria control) |
| C | Unintentional by-products | **Dioxins, furans** |
- **2025 Annex A additions:** **chlorpyrifos** (neurotoxic insecticide – not in the dirty dozen), **long-chain PFCAs** ("**forever chemicals**"), medium-chain chlorinated paraffins.
# Minamata (mercury)
- Protects from **man-made emissions of mercury**; named after **Minamata, Japan** (factory wastewater mercury poisoned fish-eaters, 1950s).
- **Mercury pollution (UPSC 2023):** **gold mining** ✓, **coal-based thermal power plants** ✓, **no known safe level of exposure** (WHO wording) → **UPSC official key: only two** (statement 3 rejected as absolute; GKToday, Vajiram) – ⚠ PMF Environment notes print "all three". Solar PV panels in normal operation are not a source.
- **Dental amalgam** phase-out by **2034**. "Mercury – Rotterdam" is a wrong pair.

- **Vienna 1985 → Montreal 1987 → Basel 1989 → Kyoto 1997 → Cartagena 2000 → Stockholm 2001 → Minamata 2013 → Kigali 2016.**
