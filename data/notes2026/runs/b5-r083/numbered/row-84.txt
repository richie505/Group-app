## SUB 0 | Meaning, Definition and Levels of Biodiversity
[0:0] - Term **"biodiversity"** coined by **Walter G. Rosen (1985)**; **popularised by E.O. Wilson** – not Tansley (coined "ecosystem"), Darwin or Barrow.
[0:1] - **Biodiversity** = the range/variety of **different species** in an environment; includes **genetic diversity** within populations as well as the diversity of **species and ecosystems** (not atmosphere, lithosphere or "environment").
[0:2] - **CBD (1992) definition:** variability among living organisms from **all sources** (terrestrial, marine, other aquatic) and the **ecological complexes** of which they are part – **within species, between species and of ecosystems**.
[0:3] TABLE head: Level | Meaning / example
  [0:3.0] Genetic | Variation within a species – the basic source; all rice varieties are *Oryza sativa* but differ in size, aroma, nutrients; lets a population adapt and respond to natural selection
  [0:3.1] Species | Variety of species in an area – richness + abundance
  [0:3.2] Ecosystem (ecological) | Variety of habitats/ecosystems in a region; one ecosystem cannot replace another
[0:4] - **Narrow genetic base:** the cultivated **banana** (seedless, vegetatively propagated Cavendish clones) may be wiped out by disease – a classic case for genetic diversity.
[0:5] - **Crop diversity in space and time** = **polyculture** (mixed/inter-cropping and rotation), not monoculture.
[0:6] - Chinese, Americans, Indians and Black Africans = **one species, *Homo sapiens*** (tigers–lions–cats or pigeons–doves–partridges are different species).
[0:7] - **Biomes:** no two biomes are exactly alike because **climate and geographic location** give unique combinations of plants, animals and soils (A and R true, R explains A).

## SUB 1 | Measuring Biodiversity – Alpha, Beta, Gamma
[1:0] TABLE head: Measure | Scale
  [1:0.0] Alpha (α) | Within one habitat/area at a local scale – mean species diversity / species richness there
  [1:0.1] Beta (β) | Comparison between ecosystems – change (turnover) in species composition along a gradient
  [1:0.2] Gamma (γ) | Overall diversity across the ecosystems of a whole region (total geographical area)
  [1:0.3] Endemism | Species restricted to a specific region
  [1:0.4] Species richness / evenness | Number of species / their relative abundance (low evenness = few species dominate)
[1:1] - Terms alpha, beta, gamma by **R.H. Whittaker**; indices: **Shannon-Wiener** and **Simpson**.
[1:2] - Forest A (2 tigers, 6 deer, 7 rabbits) vs B (1, 6, 8): **same richness, A more even**.
[1:3] TABLE head: Measure | Key characteristic (match, NTRUHS)
  [1:3.0] Phylogenetic diversity | Evolutionary relationships between species
  [1:3.1] Ecological diversity | Diversity of habitats/ecosystems in a region
  [1:3.2] Genetic diversity | Variety of genetic traits within species populations
  [1:3.3] Species diversity | Species richness and evenness in a community
[1:4] - **Functional diversity:** range of **ecological roles** performed by organisms.
[1:5] - Field sampling: **mist nets** capture **birds and bats**; dipnets, creel surveys and seines are aquatic methods.
[1:6] > Alpha index asked twice (RAS 2018); two match questions (NTRUHS Mains).

## SUB 2 | Richness Patterns – Latitude, Altitude, Himalaya
[2:0] - **Latitudinal gradient:** biodiversity **increases towards the equator** and falls towards the poles – greatest in **lower latitudes** and, along mountains, in **lower altitudes** (UPSC 2011: both true).
[2:1] - Ants: ~**10 species near the North Pole vs ~2,000 at the equator**.
[2:2] - Richest: **tropical rainforests** (land), **coral reefs** (sea).
[2:3] - Tropics richest: **long undisturbed evolutionary time (no glaciation)**, stable climate, more solar energy → productivity.
[2:4] - **Butterflies:** maximum species in **tropical countries**; books and keys give "butterflies cannot tolerate low temperatures" as the reason (A and R true, R explains A).
[2:5] - **Himalaya** rich because it is a **confluence of different biogeographical zones** (Palearctic + Indo-Malayan) – not rainfall, absence of invasives or low human interference.
[2:6] - Estimates: about **1.5 million** living species described (+300,000 fossil); **Wilson (1992) 10–50 million**; **Robert May ~7 million**. **Over 70%** of recorded species are animals (**insects** richest); plants ~**22%**.
[2:7] - **Species-area relationship** (**Humboldt**, log S = log C + Z log A).
[2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC Part 1.2 p.12-14; NextGen Part 2.1 p.10, p.14; app notes 5:84 sub 2
## SUB 3 | Values of Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services
[3:0] TABLE head: Value | Meaning
  [3:0.0] Consumptive | Used locally, not sold: fuelwood, fodder, wild food
  [3:0.1] Productive | Sold commercially: timber, fibre, medicines, genetic material
  [3:0.2] Ecosystem services | Soil formation, prevention of soil erosion, recycling of waste, pollination, clean air and water, climate
  [3:0.3] Social / cultural | Sacred groves, indigenous knowledge
  [3:0.4] Aesthetic | Beauty, ecotourism
  [3:0.5] Option | Unknown future uses – new drugs, crop genes
[3:1] - **Most significant aspect:** **maintenance of the ecosystem**; role in sustainable development: **supports ecosystem services such as food, water and air quality**.
[3:2] - **Butterfly decline (UPSC 2017):** pollination of some plants affected ✓; fall in populations of **wasps, spiders and birds** that eat them ✓; fungal infections ✗ → **I and III**.
[3:3] - **Himalayan nettle** (*Girardinia diversifolia*): sustainable source of **textile fibre** (UPSC 2019).
[3:4] - **Rivet popper hypothesis** (**Paul Ehrlich**): losing a key rivet (**keystone species**) may collapse the ecosystem.
[3:5] - **TEEB:** **G8+5** initiative, **2007**; hosted by **UNEP** (not IMF/WEF); led by **Pavan Sukhdev**; draws attention to the **economic benefits of biodiversity** and helps decision-makers **recognise, demonstrate and capture** its value → **2 and 3 only**.
[3:6] - **Dasgupta Review (2021):** **Partha Dasgupta** for the **UK Treasury**; nature as an asset.
[3:7] - **International Day for Biological Diversity: 22 May** (22 March = World Water Day). CBD in force **29 Dec 1993**.
[3:8] - **National Biodiversity Congress, Thiruvananthapuram (Feb 2017):** focal theme **"Mainstreaming Biodiversity for Sustainable Development"**.
