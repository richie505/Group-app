## SUB 1 | Federal Tensions and Unifying Institutions
[54:1:0] - Shaping factors: **constitutional provisions**, **planning**, **party politics**, **judicial interpretation**.
[54:1:1] TABLE head: Phase | Features
  [54:1:1.0] 1950s – early 1960s | Congress at Centre and States; calm; States relied on grants
  [54:1:1.1] Mid-1960s onwards | Congress lost many States; autonomy demands grew
  [54:1:1.2] Since 1990s | Coalition era; greater say for States → cooperative federalism
[54:1:2] - Institutions that promote **"unity among diversity"** in Indian federalism: **Inter-State Council and NDC**, **Finance Commission and Regional (Zonal) Councils**, **unitary (single integrated) judiciary and All India Services** → **all of the above**.
[54:1:3] - **Centre-State financial distribution** follows the recommendations of the **Finance Commission** (Art. 280) – not the ISC, Planning Commission or Parliament.
[54:1:4] - **S.R. Bommai (1994):** Art. 356 action is **subject to judicial review**. **2015:** Planning Commission replaced by **NITI Aayog**.
[54:1:5] # Institutions criticised as against federal principles
[54:1:6] TABLE head: Institution | Criticism
  [54:1:6.0] Planning Commission | Extra-constitutional; made States dependent on the Union for aid (Rajamannar)
  [54:1:6.1] Governor | Appointed by the Centre; seen as central interference
  [54:1:6.2] Arts. 256, 257, 356, 365 | Tools of Union overreach
  [54:1:6.3] Cesses and CSS | Cesses are not shared with the States
  [54:1:6.4] All India Services | Restrict State autonomy
[54:1:7] - The Inter-State Council is a forum for consultation, not criticised as anti-federal.
[54:1:8] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.14-15; Uttarakhand PCS 2016 key; app notes 2:54
## SUB 2 | All India Services and State Autonomy
[54:2:0] - **Art. 312:** AIS = **IAS, IPS, Indian Forest Service**; recruited by **UPSC**; **ultimate control with the Centre, immediate control with the States**.
[54:2:1] - New AIS: **Rajya Sabha** resolution by **two-thirds present and voting** – a power **exclusive to the Rajya Sabha** (unlike creating States or changing boundaries, done by Parliament as a whole).
[54:2:2] - **A–R:** AIS are said to **violate the federal principle and State autonomy** because officers are governed by **Central rules** and States lack full control → both true, R explains A.
[54:2:3] - For AIS: high standards, uniformity, **Centre-State coordination**.
[54:2:4] - Abolition of **IAS and IPS** = **Rajamannar**; Sarkaria wanted AIS **strengthened**; Punchhi wanted **new AIS** (health, education, engineering, judiciary).
[54:2:5] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.15 p.238-243 and ch.75; Constitution Art. 249, 312
## SUB 3 | Special Category Status and Article 371
[54:3:0] - SCS: **not in the Constitution**; introduced **1969** (Gadgil formula); **granted by the National Development Council** on Planning Commission advice (not the Union Cabinet or Finance Commission).
[54:3:1] - Criteria (**Gadgil formula**): hilly terrain, low density or tribal share, border location, backwardness, weak finances.
[54:3:2] - Benefit: **90%** Central funding of plan assistance; **11 States** (e.g. Sikkim, Himachal Pradesh, J&K, NE States, Uttarakhand). **Bihar never granted** SCS.
[54:3:3] - **14th FC:** devolution raised to **42%**; no special/general distinction – Centre's main reason to deny SCS.
[54:3:4] - **AP:** PM's assurance **20 Feb 2014**; got a **special assistance package (2016)** instead.
[54:3:5] TABLE head: Article | State
  [54:3:5.0] 371 | Maharashtra, Gujarat
  [54:3:5.1] 371A | Nagaland
  [54:3:5.2] 371B / 371C | Assam / Manipur
  [54:3:5.3] 371D | AP – local cadres, education, jobs
  [54:3:5.4] 371E | Central University in AP
  [54:3:5.5] 371F-J | Sikkim, Mizoram, Arunachal, Goa, Karnataka
[54:3:6] - Ladakh offered **Art. 371** safeguards instead of the **Sixth Schedule** (2026); **FNTA** gives eastern Nagaland autonomy over **46** subjects (2026).
[54:3:7] - **131st Amendment Bill 2026** (Lok Sabha expansion) defeated – sharpened the **North–South** debate (May 2026).

## SUB 6 | GST Council and Fiscal Centralisation
[54:6:0] - **Art. 279A**; vote weight: **Centre one-third**, **States two-thirds**; decision needs **three-fourths** of weighted votes.
[54:6:1] - **Mohit Minerals (May 2022):** GST Council recommendations **not binding**, only persuasive.
[54:6:2] - GST **subsumed State taxes**; cesses and surcharges stay **outside the divisible pool** (~81% of gross tax, 2025-26).
[54:6:3] - **16th FC keeps 41%** (2026-31); **State Finance Commissions** strengthen local devolution.
[54:6:4] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.47 (GST Council); app notes 2:54
## SUB 7 | Governor–State Conflicts and the Inter-State Council
[54:7:0] - **State of Tamil Nadu v Governor (April 2025):** timelines and "deemed assent".
[54:7:1] - **Presidential Reference opinion, 20 Nov 2025:** **no court-imposed timelines** for Governor or President; **deemed assent** alien to the Constitution.

[54:7:3] - **Inter-State Council:** set up **1990** under Art. 263; **PM chairs**; **6 Union Cabinet ministers**; **11th and last meeting 16 July 2016**.

=== NOTES 2:55 | Rajamannar Committee (1969) – Appointment and Recommendations
## SUB 0 | Rajamannar Recommendations
[55:0:0] TABLE head: Subject | Rajamannar recommended
  [55:0:0.0] Inter-State Council | Set up immediately under Art. 263; no decision affecting States without consulting it; every Bill affecting States to be referred to it first
  [55:0:0.1] Article 356 | Complete deletion (with 357 and 365); if kept, only for a complete breakdown
  [55:0:0.2] All-India Services | Abolish IAS, IPS (and IFoS) – to reduce central administrative control
  [55:0:0.3] Planning Commission | Abolish – extra-constitutional; alone decided grants to States; replace with a statutory body
  [55:0:0.4] Finance Commission | Make permanent; revitalise with wider revenue devolution
  [55:0:0.5] Governor | Delete "pleasure of the Governor" clause for the State ministry – not strengthen the Governor system
[55:0:1] - Ending IAS and IPS = **Rajamannar** (UPSC 1993) – not the Dhebar, Kalelkar or Kher commissions, nor Sarkaria.
[55:0:2] - Passage clue: "made a call for **immediate constitution of the Inter-State Council**, every Bill affecting States referred to it first, Art. 356 only in rare cases" = **Rajamannar**.
[55:0:3] - Passage clue: "greater autonomy in legislative and fiscal matters, critical analysis of the '**strong Centre**' concept, States' competence to amend Central laws, mandatory consultation" = **Rajamannar** (Sarkaria **defended** a strong Centre).
[55:0:4] # Findings
[55:0:5] - Despite the federal form, States had become "**administrative units of the Centre**"; causes – special central powers, **one-party rule**, weak State finances, **central planning**.
[55:0:6] - Overreach Articles: **256, 257, 365** (not 280, 282).
[55:0:7] - The Centre's control over States in the **planning process** has been termed "**municipal relations**" (UPSC 1994) – not legislative, administrative or financial control.
[55:0:8] - Relations depend on constitutional provisions, conventions and practices, judicial interpretation and mechanisms for dialogue.
[55:0:9] # Seventh Schedule and residuary powers
[55:0:10] - Transfer subjects (e.g. **stock exchanges, oil fields, trade unions, industrial disputes**, inland waterways) from Union/Concurrent Lists to the **State List** – **not foreign affairs**.
[55:0:11] - **Residuary powers → States** (Sarkaria: residuary taxation with Parliament, other residuary to the Concurrent List).

[57:1:0] - **Fixed tenure of 5 years**; removal not at the sweet will of the Centre (Sarkaria first proposed a secure five-year term).

[59:0:0] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [59:0:0.0] Federalism | Sharing of power between the Union and the States (not communism, fascism, oligarchy)
  [59:0:0.1] Cooperative federalism | Centre and States work together on a common platform; NITI's bottom-up version – "marble-cake federalism"

=== NOTES 2:60 | Devolution of Powers and Finances to Local Levels – Finance Commission and State Finance Commission Transfers
## SUB 0 | State Finance Commission – Article 243I
[60:0:0] # The body
[60:0:1] - **Art. 243I (Panchayats) / 243Y (Municipalities):** the **Governor** constitutes a **Finance Commission** within one year of the 73rd Amendment (1992) and then **every five years** to **review the financial position of the Panchayats** – not the President, Parliament, the Finance Ministry or the Chief Minister.
[60:0:2] - It is a **constitutional body** (Part IX, 73rd Amendment) – not informal, administrative or a "finance committee"/"advisory commission".
[60:0:3] - **243Y:** the **same SFC** also reviews the finances of **Municipalities**.
[60:0:4] # What it recommends to the Governor
[60:0:5] - **Principles governing** (a) the **distribution between the State and the Panchayats** of the net proceeds of State taxes, duties, tolls and fees, and their **allocation among Panchayats at all levels**; (b) the **determination of taxes, duties, tolls and fees that may be assigned to or appropriated by the Panchayats**; (c) **grants-in-aid** from the **Consolidated Fund of the State**.
[60:0:6] - Also **measures to improve the finances** of the Panchayats and any matter the **Governor** refers.
[60:0:7] - The **State Legislature** fixes its composition and members' qualifications; the **Governor lays the report, with an explanatory memorandum (action taken), before the State Legislature**.
[60:0:8] - So the SFC – not the Governor, the CM, the District Planning Committee or the State PR Ministry – is the body for **revenue distribution between the State and its local governments**.

  [60:0:9.0] 243G / 243W | Powers and functions – Eleventh Schedule (29) / Twelfth (18) – State "may" devolve

  [60:0:9.2] 243I / 243Y | State Finance Commission – Governor, every 5 years
  [60:0:9.3] 243J / 243Z | Maintenance and audit of accounts – by State law

## SUB 1 | Fourteenth Finance Commission (2015-20) – Grants to Local Bodies
[60:1:0] # The design

## SUB 0 | Devolution Index – Measuring Devolution to Panchayats
[61:0:0] # The report
[61:0:1] - **Devolution Index / Devolution Report** ("**Status of Devolution to Panchayats in States**"): **commissioned by the Ministry of Panchayati Raj (Central Government)** to assess the States' **policy and legislative framework and its outcomes** for Panchayati Raj – not a "PR Dashboard" or a progress report.
[61:0:2] - It measures the **actual devolution of functions, funds and functionaries** (devolution **in policy and in practice**), not merely expected devolution or funds alone.
[61:0:3] - **Who prepared it:** **IIPA** (earlier editions and the **2024** edition), but the **2014-15 and 2015-16 reports were entrusted to the Tata Institute of Social Sciences (TISS)**, Mumbai – not NIRD or NCAER.
