## SUB 1 | Federal Tensions and Unifying Institutions
[1:0] - Shaping factors: **constitutional provisions**, **planning**, **party politics**, **judicial interpretation**.
[1:1] TABLE head: Phase | Features
  [1:1.0] 1950s – early 1960s | Congress at Centre and States; calm; States relied on grants
  [1:1.1] Mid-1960s onwards | Congress lost many States; autonomy demands grew
  [1:1.2] Since 1990s | Coalition era; greater say for States → cooperative federalism
[1:2] - Institutions that promote **"unity among diversity"** in Indian federalism: **Inter-State Council and NDC**, **Finance Commission and Regional (Zonal) Councils**, **unitary (single integrated) judiciary and All India Services** → **all of the above**.
[1:3] - **Centre-State financial distribution** follows the recommendations of the **Finance Commission** (Art. 280) – not the ISC, Planning Commission or Parliament.
[1:4] - **S.R. Bommai (1994):** Art. 356 action is **subject to judicial review**. **2015:** Planning Commission replaced by **NITI Aayog**.

  [1:6.1] Governor | Appointed by the Centre; seen as central interference
  [1:6.2] Arts. 256, 257, 356, 365 | Tools of Union overreach
  [1:6.3] Cesses and CSS | Cesses are not shared with the States
  [1:6.4] All India Services | Restrict State autonomy
[1:7] - The Inter-State Council is a forum for consultation, not criticised as anti-federal.

## SUB 3 | All India Services and State Autonomy
[3:0] - **Art. 312:** AIS = **IAS, IPS, Indian Forest Service**; recruited by **UPSC**; **ultimate control with the Centre, immediate control with the States**.
[3:1] - New AIS: **Rajya Sabha** resolution by **two-thirds present and voting** – a power **exclusive to the Rajya Sabha** (unlike creating States or changing boundaries, done by Parliament as a whole).
[3:2] - **A–R:** AIS are said to **violate the federal principle and State autonomy** because officers are governed by **Central rules** and States lack full control → both true, R explains A.
[3:3] - For AIS: high standards, uniformity, **Centre-State coordination**.
[3:4] - Abolition of **IAS and IPS** = **Rajamannar**; Sarkaria wanted AIS **strengthened**; Punchhi wanted **new AIS** (health, education, engineering, judiciary).
[3:5] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.15 p.238-243 and ch.75; Constitution Art. 249, 312
## SUB 4 | Special Category Status and Article 371
[4:0] - SCS: **not in the Constitution**; introduced **1969** (Gadgil formula); **granted by the National Development Council** on Planning Commission advice (not the Union Cabinet or Finance Commission).
[4:1] - Criteria (**Gadgil formula**): hilly terrain, low density or tribal share, border location, backwardness, weak finances.
[4:2] - Benefit: **90%** Central funding of plan assistance; **11 States** (e.g. Sikkim, Himachal Pradesh, J&K, NE States, Uttarakhand). **Bihar never granted** SCS.
[4:3] - **14th FC:** devolution raised to **42%**; no special/general distinction – Centre's main reason to deny SCS.
[4:4] - **AP:** PM's assurance **20 Feb 2014**; got a **special assistance package (2016)** instead.

## SUB 6 | GST Council and Fiscal Centralisation
[6:0] - **Art. 279A**; vote weight: **Centre one-third**, **States two-thirds**; decision needs **three-fourths** of weighted votes.
[6:1] - **Mohit Minerals (May 2022):** GST Council recommendations **not binding**, only persuasive.

[6:3] - **16th FC keeps 41%** (2026-31); **State Finance Commissions** strengthen local devolution.
