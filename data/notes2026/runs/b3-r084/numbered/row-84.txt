## SUB 1 | Bank Nationalisation – 1969 and 1980
[1:0] # Two rounds
[1:1] TABLE head: Basis | 1969 | 1980
  [1:1.0] Date | 19 July 1969 | 15 April 1980
  [1:1.1] Banks | 14 | 6
  [1:1.2] Deposit size | ₹50 crore and above | ₹200 crore and above
  [1:1.3] Law | Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, 1969 | Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, 1980
  [1:1.4] PM | Indira Gandhi | Indira Gandhi
[1:2] - **Total nationalised: 14 + 6 = 20** private banks ("how many banks nationalised till now" = 20).
[1:3] - **New Bank of India merged into PNB (September 1993)** → nationalised banks came down to **19**.
[1:4]   - After the 2019-20 mergers there are **12 public sector banks** (SBI + 11 nationalised banks) (as of 2026).
[1:5] - The 14 banks of 1969 held about **85% of bank deposits**; Indira Gandhi's note "**Stray Thoughts on Bank Nationalisation**"; 50th anniversary 19 July 2019.
[1:6] # Which banks
[1:7] - **1969 – 14**: Allahabad (now Indian Bank), Bank of Baroda, Bank of India, Bank of Maharashtra, Central Bank, Canara, **Dena (now BoB)**, Indian Bank, IOB, PNB, **Syndicate (now Canara)**, UCO, Union, United Bank of India (now PNB).
[1:8] - **1980 – 6**: **Andhra Bank**, **Corporation Bank** (both now Union Bank), **Oriental Bank of Commerce** (now PNB), **Punjab & Sind Bank**, **Vijaya Bank** (now BoB), **New Bank of India** (into PNB 1993).
[1:9] - **Andhra Bank = 1980**; **Syndicate Bank = 1969**; **ICICI Bank was never nationalised** (formed 1955 as a DFI by the World Bank, GoI and Indian industry); Corporation, Dena and Vijaya were nationalised (1980/1969/1980).
[1:10] # Reasons and results
[1:11] TABLE head: Factor | Point
  [1:11.0] Reasons | Banks controlled by industrial houses; agriculture, small industry and small business neglected
  [1:11.1] Purpose | Social control of credit – improving credit facilities for neglected sectors (agriculture, small industry)
  [1:11.2] Results | Branch expansion, growth of priority-sector lending, more deposit mobilisation
[1:12] - Allied steps of the social-banking era: **Lead Bank Scheme (1969)**, **RRBs (1975)**, **village/district adoption by bank branches** – all features of the Indian banking system (UPSC 2010).

## SUB 3 | Banking Operations, Instruments and Customer Rules
[3:0] # Assets and liabilities of a commercial bank
[3:1] TABLE head: Liabilities | Assets
  [3:1.0] Capital and reserves | Cash in hand, balances with RBI
  [3:1.1] Deposits (current, savings, term) – owed to customers | Investments (SLR securities)
  [3:1.2] Borrowings from RBI and others | Loans and advances (e.g. credit to farmers), money at call and short notice, fixed assets
[3:2] - **Deposits are NOT an asset** of a bank (UPSC 2019); credit to farmers is an asset.
[3:3] # Instruments and rules
[3:4] TABLE head: Rule | Fact
  [3:4.0] Banker's cheque | = Pay Order (payable locally; a demand draft is payable at another centre)
  [3:4.1] Cheque validity | 3 months from date of issue (since 1 April 2012; earlier 6 months)
  [3:4.2] Savings-bank interest | Deregulated (Oct 2011) – each bank fixes it; never fixed by the Finance Minister, Finance Commission or IBA
  [3:4.3] Para-banking | Credit cards, insurance, factoring, mutual fund distribution – not "credit to paramilitary personnel"
  [3:4.4] Aadhaar | KYC only with the customer's consent
  [3:4.5] ATM cash-out penalty | From 1 Oct 2021: ₹10,000 per ATM out of cash > 10 hours in a month
[3:5] # Banking Ombudsman
[3:6] - Started **14 June 1995** under **Section 35A, BR Act 1949**; quasi-judicial; **appointed by the RBI**; free; NRIs with accounts in India may complain.
[3:7] - Its awards are **not final**: an **appeal** lies with the RBI (Appellate Authority) – so "orders final and binding" is the wrong statement (now Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021).
[3:8] # Seasons, green banking, apex refinance
[3:9] - **Busy season: November–April; slack season: May–October** (RBI's traditional credit-policy seasons; Ramesh Singh gives slack May–September, busy October–April).
[3:10] - **Green banking**: banking that considers **environmental factors in internal processes and in products/services** (not merely paperless banking).
[3:11] - **NABARD** is an **apex refinancing** body – **not a source of direct finance**; RRBs, SBI and other commercial banks lend directly.
[3:12] - **RIDF** (Rural Infrastructure Development Fund) – set up **1995-96** in **NABARD** (nodal agency).
[3:13] - **Scheduled banks in 2013**: 161 (exam figure, not verified).

## SUB 4 | Lead Bank Scheme and Service Area Approach
[4:0] # Origin
[4:1] - Idea first mooted by the **D. R. Gadgil Study Group** (report **October 1969**: "area approach", district as the unit).
[4:2] - **Nariman Committee** (F. K. F. Nariman, bankers' committee appointed by RBI, report **November 1969**) – each public sector bank to act as **Lead Bank** for allotted districts.
[4:3] - **Introduced by the RBI in December 1969** – the same year as the 14-bank nationalisation; an initiative of the **bank-nationalisation** era (not NITI Aayog, XII Plan or demonetisation).
[4:4] - **Priority-sector** idea: traced to RBI's 1967-68 slack-season credit policy; the sector was formally defined in 1972.
[4:5] # Working
[4:6] - **Basic aim**: **individual banks adopt particular districts for intensive development** (not mere office-opening, competition or deposit drives).
[4:7] - **District credit planning** is done **under the Lead Bank**; lead-bank functions are performed by **a bank designated for the purpose** (not automatically SBI or the RBI).
[4:8] - **Service Area Approach (April 1989)** – implemented **under the Lead Bank Scheme**; each rural/semi-urban branch serves a designated group of **15–25 villages**.
[4:9] TABLE head: Forum | Level
  [4:9.0] Lead District Manager / District Consultative Committee | District
  [4:9.1] State Level Bankers' Committee (SLBC) | State
[4:10] - **AP**: lead bank / **SLBC convener = Union Bank of India**; 236th SLBC (16 Sep 2026): CM Naidu asked banks to lend more to MSMEs, tenant farmers, PM Surya Ghar.
[4:11] - RBI draft revision of the scheme (Feb 2026) to strengthen SLBCs and LDM offices.
[4:12] ~~ Sources: RBI, Report of the High Level Committee to Review Lead Bank Scheme (2009), paras 2.1-2.5 (rbi.org.in PublicationDraftReports ID 552); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 07 p.8; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.50; old app notes 3:84 sub 8

## SUB 8 | Small Finance Banks and Payments Banks
[8:0] # Origin
[8:1] - **Nachiket Mor Committee (2013)** → **differentiated (niche) banks**: SFBs and payments banks; licences: universal and differentiated; SFB licences **on tap since 2019**.
[8:2] - Payments banks are licensed under **Section 22, BR Act 1949**; both are public limited companies (Companies Act, 2013).
[8:3] # Comparison
[8:4] TABLE head: Feature | Small finance bank | Payments bank
  [8:4.0] Purpose | Savings vehicles + credit to small business units, small and marginal farmers, micro and small industries, unorganised sector | Small savings accounts, payments/remittances for migrant labour and low-income households
  [8:4.1] Deposits | All types | Demand deposits only; max ₹2 lakh per customer (initially ₹1 lakh)
  [8:4.2] Lending | Yes: 75% of ANBC to PSL; 50% of loans ≤ ₹25 lakh | No lending; no credit cards (debit/ATM cards allowed)
  [8:4.3] Minimum capital | ₹100 crore (2014 guidelines) | ₹100 crore
  [8:4.4] Other | 25% branches in unbanked rural areas; can sell MFs and insurance | Funds in G-Secs/T-bills up to 1 year (75% of demand deposits in SLR)
  [8:4.5] Promoters | Experienced individuals, NBFCs, MFIs, local area banks | Mobile companies, supermarket chains (resident-owned), NBFCs, PPI issuers, corporates, PSUs
[8:5] - UPSC 2017: SFB purpose = **credit to small business units and small/marginal farmers** ("encourage young entrepreneurs in rural areas" is not a stated aim).
[8:6] - UPSC 2016: payments banks – resident-owned **telecoms and supermarket chains can promote** ✓; **cannot issue credit cards** ✗; **cannot lend** ✓ → **1 and 3**.
[8:7] - **SFB → universal bank** (RBI, April 2024): voluntary; **5 years** satisfactory record; net worth **₹1,000 crore**; no change of promoters; **AU SFB** approved (2025).
[8:8] - RBI **cancelled Paytm Payments Bank's** licence (harmful to depositors and public).
[8:9] - **75%** = SFB PSL share = RRB PSL share = payments bank share of demand deposits in SLR securities.

## SUB 10 | Banking Structure and Development Finance Institutions
[10:0] TABLE head: Sector | Who
  [10:0.0] Organised | RBI; commercial banks (public, private, foreign, RRBs, SFBs, payments banks, local area banks); co-operative banks; development banks (NABARD, SIDBI, Exim, NHB, MUDRA)
  [10:0.1] Unorganised | Indigenous bankers, moneylenders
[10:1] - **Scheduled banks**: in the **Second Schedule, RBI Act 1934**; paid-up capital and reserves **≥ ₹5 lakh**; can borrow from the RBI. Five groups: SBI, nationalised banks, RRBs, foreign banks, private banks.
[10:2] - **Co-operative banks**: State Co-operative Societies Acts + RBI under BR Act; **one member, one vote**; no-profit-no-loss.
[10:3] - **NBFCs** cannot take demand deposits or issue cheques; deposits not DICGC-insured.
[10:4] - **DICGC (1978)**: merger of Deposit Insurance Corporation (DIC, 1962) and Credit Guarantee Corporation of India (CGCI).
[10:5] - **Foreign banks**: branches or **wholly owned subsidiaries** with near national treatment (RBI, Nov 2013); WOS minimum capital **₹500 crore**.
[10:6] - **Syndicated loan**: one loan to a **single borrower** funded by a **group of lenders**, arranged by a lead bank.
