## SUB 0 | Start-up India – Launch, Bodies and Programmes
[0:0] # Launch
[0:1] - Launched **16 January 2016** (SSC CGL 2019) – to support entrepreneurs, build a start-up ecosystem and make India a country of **job creators instead of job seekers**; **16 January = National Startup Day**.
[0:2] - Nodal: **DPIIT**, Ministry of Commerce and Industry – also the **nodal agency for start-up recognition**. (Udyam portal = MSME registration.)
[0:3] - **Start-up and Stand-up India** (slogan of Jan 2016) aim to promote innovation and entrepreneurship among **enterprising youth** – Stand-Up targets SC/ST and women.
[0:4] - **Action plan (ES 2015-16):** self-certification, **Startup India Hub**, mobile app and portal, relaxed public procurement, fast-track patents, **faster exit norms**, ₹10,000 crore fund of funds, credit guarantee, 3-year tax exemption.
[0:5] # Bodies and platforms
[0:6] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [0:6.0] Startup India Hub | single online platform / point of contact for all stakeholders; operationalised 2016
  [0:6.1] National Startup Advisory Council | set up (notified) by Ministry of Commerce and Industry on 22 January 2020
  [0:6.2] Startup India Yatra | search for entrepreneurial talent in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities – not villages
  [0:6.3] MAARG | national mentorship platform (all sectors, not only AI) – Mentorship, Advisory, Assistance, Resilience and Growth
  [0:6.4] SETU | Self-Employment and Talent Utilisation – techno-financial, incubation and facilitation programme under Atal Innovation Mission (Budget 2015-16)
[0:7] - **States' Startup Ranking (DPIIT):** promotes **competitive federalism**; categories Best Performers … Emerging; **AP = Best Performer (2022)**.
[0:8] > Most-asked: launch 16 Jan 2016, Yatra = Tier-2/3 cities (asked twice), SETU expansion, NSAC Jan 2020.

## SUB 1 | Stand-Up India Scheme (2016)
[1:0] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [1:0.0] Launch | 5 April 2016
  [1:0.1] Ministry | Ministry of Finance; portal Standupmitra
  [1:0.2] Target groups | SC/ST and women entrepreneurs aged 18+
  [1:0.3] Loan | ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore (composite: term loan + working capital)
  [1:0.4] Enterprise | Greenfield (first-time) only – manufacturing, services, trading, agri-allied; NOT brownfield
  [1:0.5] Branch norm | at least one SC/ST and one woman borrower per bank branch
  [1:0.6] Refinance | SIDBI ₹10,000 crore
[1:1] - **Non-individual enterprises** eligible if at least **51% shareholding and controlling stake** is with an SC/ST or woman entrepreneur.
[1:2] - Loan up to **85%** of project cost; repayment up to **7 years**, moratorium up to **18 months**.
[1:3] - **Working capital up to ₹10 lakh** = **overdraft** (RuPay debit card); above ₹10 lakh = cash credit limit.
[1:4] - **Security:** primary security plus collateral or guarantee of the **Credit Guarantee Fund Scheme for Stand-Up India Loans (CGFSIL)**, as decided by banks.
[1:5] - **PM MUDRA:** micro loans up to **₹20 lakh** (Tarun Plus).
[1:6] > APPSC G1 2024 statements: brownfield ✗; non-individual ✓; working capital ≤₹10 lakh as cash credit ✗ (it is overdraft); CGFSIL ✓ → **II and IV**.
[1:7] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:67 sub 2; Ramesh Singh part 05 p.25 (Start-up India and Stand-up India campaign)
## SUB 2 | Funds and Financing Sources for Start-ups
[2:0] TABLE head: Fund | Key facts
  [2:0.0] Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS) | ₹945 crore (2021); up to ₹20 lakh grant for proof of concept, prototypes, trials + ₹50 lakh investment; given through incubators, not banks
  [2:0.1] Fund of Funds for Startups (FFS) | ₹10,000 crore, managed by SIDBI; contributes to SEBI-registered AIFs (VC funds), not directly
  [2:0.2] FFS 2.0 | another ₹10,000 crore for deep tech, space, AI
  [2:0.3] Credit Guarantee Scheme for Start-ups | Oct 2022; via NCGTC; up to ₹20 crore per borrower; replaced the Credit Guarantee Fund for Startups
[2:1] TABLE head: Source | Feature
  [2:1.0] Bootstrapping | founder's own savings and revenue
  [2:1.1] Angel investors | individuals investing own money at idea/seed stage
  [2:1.2] Venture capital | early-stage equity with active guidance for high-growth firms
  [2:1.3] Crowdfunding | small sums from many people, usually online
  [2:1.4] Incubators, bank loans, government schemes | MUDRA, Stand-Up India, CGSS
[2:2] - Start-ups' needs can be met through angel investors, venture capital and crowdfunding – **all the above** (UPPSC 2017).
[2:3] - **Big deal:** **Walmart** bought a majority stake in **Flipkart** (2018). **Microsoft** launched the **100X100X100** programme for B2B SaaS start-ups.
