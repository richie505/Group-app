## SUB 1 | Income Tax – History, Budgets, Slabs and Deductions
[1:0] - **Income tax** first introduced **1860** by **James Wilson**, the first **Finance Member** (Act assent **24 July 1860**); a **progressive direct tax**; the **Income-tax Act, 1961** took effect **1 April 1962**.
[1:1] - Budget **1997-98** (FM **P. Chidambaram**, "dream budget"): cut almost **every tax**; income-tax rates **10, 20, 30%** (from 15, 30, 40%); corporate tax 40% → 35%.
[1:2] - **One-by-six scheme**: return filing compulsory for anyone showing any one of six lifestyle signs; the **telephone connection** criterion was dropped in Budget **2002-03**.
[1:3] TABLE head: Budget | Exemption / slab fact
  [1:3.0] 2005-06 | Senior citizens exempt up to ₹1.5 lakh
  [1:3.1] 2012-13 | ₹2 lakh general; ₹2.5 lakh senior citizens (60-80); ₹5 lakh very senior (80+)
  [1:3.2] 2020-21 (first new optional regime) | 0-2.5 lakh nil; 2.5-5 lakh 5%; 5-7.5 lakh 10%; 7.5-10 lakh 15%; 10-12.5 lakh 20%; 12.5-15 lakh 25%; above 15 lakh 30%
  [1:3.3] 2023-24 | Up to 3 lakh nil; 3-6 lakh 5%; 6-9 lakh 10%; 9-12 lakh 15%; 12-15 lakh 20%; above 15 lakh 30%; top surcharge (above ₹2 crore) cut 37% → 25%
  [1:3.4] 2025-26 (unchanged in 2026-27) | 0-4 lakh nil; 4-8 lakh 5%; 8-12 lakh 10%; 12-16 lakh 15%; 16-20 lakh 20%; 20-24 lakh 25%; above 24 lakh 30%
[1:4] - **New regime (Section 115BAC)**: **default, exemption-free**; old regime with deductions stays optional. **Section 87A rebate**: income up to **₹12 lakh** tax-free; **₹12.75 lakh** for salaried after the **standard deduction of ₹75,000**.
[1:5] - Old regime: senior citizens ₹3 lakh, very senior ₹5 lakh exemption limit.
[1:6] - **Deductions**: interest on a **housing loan** (Sec 24(b)) is deductible; interest on an **education loan** is also deductible (**Sec 80E**); **Section 80C** covers NSC (and its accrued interest), PPF, EPF, life insurance, tuition fees of **two** children, home-loan **principal**.
[1:7] > Exam angle: budget-specific slab questions – key the slab of the budget asked about; the 12-15 lakh slab was 20% (2023-24) but 12-16 lakh is 15% now.
[1:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.26; Union Budget 2026-27 Speech; app notes 3:95 (budget slabs); general knowledge for Sec 80E / 24(b)
## SUB 2 | Minimum Alternate Tax and Corporate Tax Cuts
[2:0] - **MAT** taxes **"zero-tax companies"** – companies showing **book profits** but paying little or no regular tax because of exemptions and deductions.
[2:1] TABLE head: Milestone | Fact
  [2:1.0] First introduced | Budget 1987-88 (announced 1986-87; Finance Act 1987, Sec 115J; effective AY 1988-89); rate 15.75%
  [2:1.1] Withdrawn / reintroduced | Withdrawn 1990; reintroduced in Budget 1996-97 (Sec 115JA, from AY 1997-98) – the year most books and keys give as "introduced"
  [2:1.2] Before 2026 | 15% of book profits
  [2:1.3] Budget 2026-27 | 14%, a final tax; no new MAT credit after 1 April 2026; old credit set off up to 1/4 of liability (new regime only)
[2:2] - **Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019** (Sept 2019 ordinance): domestic companies **30% → 22%** (Sec **115BAA**, effective ~**25.17%**); new manufacturing companies after 1 Oct 2019 **15%** (Sec **115BAB**, ~**17.01%**); **no MAT** for those opting in.
[2:3] - Taxes on the **corporate sector**: corporation tax on company profit, MAT, Fringe Benefit Tax (2005-09). **Capital gains tax** is the usual "not corporate" answer (a levy on an investor's gain), though companies too pay it.
[2:4] > Exam angle: MAT = zero-tax companies (2 PYQs); "introduced in" → **1996-97** in the options (strictly 1987-88).

  [3:0.2] Expenditure tax | 1958 – FM T.T. Krishnamachari, on Kaldor's advice | Abolished (1962; revived briefly 1964-66)
  [3:0.3] Gift tax | April 1958 | Abolished October 1998
  [3:0.4] STT | 1 October 2004 | In force
  [3:0.5] DDT | – | Abolished from 1 April 2020; dividends taxed in shareholders' hands

[3:1] - **Capital gains**: arise on **transfer** of a capital asset; tax on sale of **inherited property** = **capital gains tax** (no inheritance tax in India).
[3:2] - Short-term asset: held **up to 24 months** (general rule); **LTCG 12.5%** (as of Aug 2026); **indexation** uses the **Cost Inflation Index** (CBDT, base **2001-02**).
[3:3] - **Rural agricultural land** is **not a capital asset**; agricultural income is tax-free (State subject).
[3:4] - **Poll tax** (Community Charge, 1989-90) – the unpopular tax linked to **Margaret Thatcher's** downfall (1990).

  [4:1.1] IDS 2016 | 1 June – 30 Sept 2016; 45% levy

## SUB 5 | Cross-border and Digital Taxation
[5:0] - **BEPS** (OECD/G20): **15 actions** to curb **tax avoidance by multinational companies** shifting profits to low-tax places (UPSC 2016).
[5:1] - **OECD Pillar Two**: **15% global minimum** corporate tax for **large MNEs** (not all companies).
[5:2] - **Equalisation levy**: **6%** on online advertisement services by non-residents (Finance Act **2016**, a **separate levy, not part of the Income Tax Act**, so **no DTAA credit**; abolished from 1 April 2025); **2%** on e-commerce (2020, dropped 2024).
[5:3] - **Vodafone** (SC, 2012) → **Finance Act 2012** taxed **indirect transfers retrospectively**; **Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2021** withdrew such demands (Vodafone, Cairn).

## SUB 6 | Tax Administration and PAN
[6:0] - **CBDT**: statutory under the **Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963**; in the **Department of Revenue** (not Expenditure).
[6:1] - **Faceless assessment + Taxpayers' Charter**: **13 August 2020**; **faceless appeals** September 2020; **Project Insight** (2017, working 2019) – AI and data analytics; **AIS** shows all reported transactions; **e-Sahyog** – online check of mismatches.
[6:2] - **PAN** (e.g. AFZPK7190K): 10 characters – 5 letters, 4 numbers, a letter; **4th character** = holder type (**P = individual/person**, C = company, H = HUF, F = firm); shows name, father's name, DOB – **not proof of address**.
[6:3] - **TCS on LRS** for education and medical cut **5% → 2%** (2026-27).
[6:4] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:95; Union Budget 2026-27 Speech
