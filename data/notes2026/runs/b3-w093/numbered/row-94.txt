## SUB 0 | Direct and Indirect Taxes – Classification and Taxable Events
[0:0] # Direct vs indirect
[0:1] TABLE head: Basis | Direct tax | Indirect tax
  [0:1.0] Burden | Cannot be shifted – impact and incidence on the same person | Shifted to the consumer through prices – impact and incidence on different persons
  [0:1.1] Examples | Income tax, corporation tax, wealth tax, capital gains, property, gift, estate duty, expenditure tax, STT | Customs, excise, sales tax, VAT, service tax, GST, entertainment, octroi, entry tax
  [0:1.2] Nature | Generally progressive | Generally regressive
  [0:1.3] Price effect | Non-inflationary; does not directly raise the price of goods | Inflationary – raises prices
  [0:1.4] Administered by | CBDT – statutory body under the Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963 | CBIC (formerly CBEC)
[0:2] - **Not direct**: customs duty, sales tax, excise, octroi – the usual "odd one out" against income, corporation, wealth and gift taxes.
[0:3] - **Indirect pair**: **customs and excise** (corporation and wealth taxes are direct).
[0:4] - **Income, corporate and wealth taxes – all direct**; "1 and 2 only" for income + corporate tax against service tax and customs.
[0:5] - **Income tax** does not directly increase the price of a commodity – trade/business tax, import and excise (manufacturing) duties do.
[0:6] - **GST** – a comprehensive **indirect** tax on supply, **destination-based**; it is not related to income.
[0:7] - **Service tax** – an **indirect tax levied by the Central Government**, introduced **1994-95** (subsumed in GST 2017).
[0:8] - **STT** – a **direct tax** on **transactions through the stock market** (from **1 October 2004**), though collected by the exchange.
[0:9] - **"Paper taxes"**: wealth, gift and estate duty – never yielded much revenue; **wealth tax** gives the **least** revenue of the major taxes.
[0:10] - **Impact** = on whom the tax is first imposed; **incidence** = who finally bears it; moving the burden forward (consumers) or backward (suppliers) = **tax shifting**. Property tax cannot be shifted through prices.
[0:11] - **Incidence and elasticity**: if demand is **perfectly inelastic (elasticity zero)** the price rises by the full tax and the **consumer bears it all**; with infinite elasticity the price cannot rise.
[0:12] - **Cut in direct taxes** → higher **disposable income** and consumption demand.
[0:13] # Base / taxable event of each tax
[0:14] TABLE head: Tax | Taxable event / base
  [0:14.0] Central excise | Manufacture / production of goods (paid by the manufacturer) – "manufacture only"
  [0:14.1] Customs | Import and export (Customs Act, 1962)
  [0:14.2] Sales tax / VAT | Sale of goods
  [0:14.3] Service tax | Provision of service
  [0:14.4] GST | Supply of goods or services (from 1 July 2017)
  [0:14.5] Corporation tax | Income / net profit of companies – not production, sales or stock
  [0:14.6] Income tax | Income of individuals, HUFs, firms (Income Tax Act, 1961)
  [0:14.7] Capital gains tax | Gain on sale of a capital asset
  [0:14.8] Wealth tax | Net wealth – 1957 to 2015 (abolished)
[0:15] - **Toll** – a levy (tax) for using a road or bridge; tolls are a **State List** subject.
[0:16] > Exam angle: "which is not a direct tax / which is indirect" is the single most repeated tax question (APPSC, UPPSC, MPPSC, Police).

## SUB 1 | Union and State Taxes – Who Levies, Who Shares
[1:0] # Constitutional division
[1:1] TABLE head: Article / entry | Provision
  [1:1.0] Article 265 | No tax levied or collected except by authority of law
  [1:1.1] Taxing entries | Union List 15, State List 20, Concurrent List 3
  [1:1.2] Residuary taxing power | Parliament (gift, wealth, expenditure taxes)
  [1:1.3] Article 276 | Profession tax – a State levy; ceiling ₹2,500 per person per year
  [1:1.4] State List Entry 46 | Agricultural income tax – States only; exempt under Income-tax Act Sec 10(1)
  [1:1.5] Article 271 | Surcharges – retained by the Union, not shared
  [1:1.6] 80th Amendment, 2000 | Net proceeds of all Union taxes (minus cess, surcharge, collection cost) shareable on Finance Commission advice; effective 1 April 1996
[1:2] # Union taxes
[1:3] - **Union alone (Union List)**: **customs duties, corporation tax**; not levied by States.
[1:4] - **Corporation tax** is **imposed by the Central Government**; before 2000 its proceeds were **not shared** with States (Art. 270 then covered only income tax; Union excise under Art. 272) – the basis of old keys "not shared" / "accrues wholly to the Centre". **Since the 80th Amendment it is in the divisible pool.**
[1:5] - **Tax on income other than agricultural income** – the classic tax **levied and collected by the Union but distributed** between Union and States.
[1:6] - Pre-GST pairs that are **not Union taxes**: **tolls and tax on mineral rights**, **taxes on intoxicants (State excise) and entertainment** – State List; customs, estate duty and terminal taxes on goods/passengers by rail, air or sea are Union.
[1:7] - **Income tax on salary (TDS)** is collected and paid into the account of the **Central Government**.
[1:8] - **Agricultural income tax** – **not shared** by the Centre (it is a State tax). Income from **poultry, dairy, wool rearing** is **not** agricultural income (taxable); **nursery saplings** are agricultural income; **rural agricultural land is not a capital asset** under the Income-tax Act, 1961.
[1:9] # State taxes
[1:10] - **Levied by States / local bodies**: **sales tax** (pre-GST), **entertainment tax** (State List, List II), **road / motor vehicle tax**, profession tax, property tax, land revenue, **State excise on liquor**, stamp duty and registration, electricity duty, agricultural income tax.
[1:11] - **Sales tax on toothpaste** (pre-GST) – **imposed and collected by the State Government**.
[1:12] - **Not a State / AP own-revenue source**: **customs duty**, corporation tax ("taxes on companies").
[1:13] - **Most important source of State revenue**: **sales tax** (pre-2017) – now **SGST**.
[1:14] - **States' revenue receipts**: **own tax revenue** is the largest component (~50%, FY25); share in Central taxes second (~32%); CAG average 2014-24: own tax 47%, tax share 28%, grants 17%.
[1:15] - **AP (2024-25 RE)**: **SGST** largest own-tax source – ₹36,296 crore (**38%**) of ₹94,967 crore; **State Excise** ₹21,300 crore (**22%**).
[1:16] - Union Budget 2017-18: farmers who gave land for **Amaravati** (AP capital) under land pooling – exempted from **capital gains tax**.
[1:17] > Exam angle: "levied by the Union but shared" questions were set on the pre-2000 scheme; after the 80th Amendment every Union tax (except cess and surcharge) is shareable.

## SUB 2 | VAT, MODVAT, CENVAT, Sales Tax and Service Tax
[2:0] # Value Added Tax
[2:1] - **VAT** = tax on the **value added at each stage** between production and final sale – a **multi-point**, **destination-based**, **non-cascading** tax on final consumption, borne by the consumer; invoice-credit trail makes it **self-enforcing**; a **harmonised** commodity tax that **improves revenue buoyancy** ("will diminish revenue buoyancy" is **not** VAT's idea).
[2:2] - State VAT was a **State subject** (State List Entry 54) – not "basically a subject of the Central Government".
[2:3] - VAT applies to **commodity taxes – excise duties** (MODVAT/CENVAT) and sales tax; not to income tax, estate duty or agricultural income tax.
[2:4] - Concept first proposed by **Wilhelm von Siemens** (Germany); first implemented by **France (1954)**.
[2:5] - **L.K. Jha Indirect Taxation Enquiry Committee (1976)** – suggested VAT at the manufacturing stage.
[2:6] - **VAT rate for medicines/drugs**: the **4%** slab (pre-GST State VAT).
[2:7] - Some goods (petroleum, alcohol) are still outside GST – **State VAT** continues on them.
[2:8] # Chronology
[2:9] TABLE head: Event | Date
  [2:9.0] MODVAT – on Union excise duties, to end cascading (first indirect tax reform) | 1 March 1986 (Budget 1986-87)
  [2:9.1] Service tax (Chelliah Committee advice; 3 services) | 1994-95
  [2:9.2] Uniform floor rates of sales tax (Standing Committee of State Finance Ministers) | 1 January 2000
  [2:9.3] CENVAT – replaced MODVAT; Union excise (an indirect tax) | 1 April 2000
  [2:9.4] First State VAT – Haryana | 1 April 2003
  [2:9.5] State VAT nationwide (20 States/UTs) | 1 April 2005
  [2:9.6] Last States to join (TN 2007, UP 2008) | by 2008-09
  [2:9.7] GST | 1 July 2017
[2:10] - **Uniform sales tax**: in **January 2000** the Standing Committee of State Finance Ministers recommended **uniform floor rates of sales tax**, implemented from **1 January 2000**.
[2:11] - About **7 States** had not implemented VAT by December 2005 (Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand – 2006; TN 2007; UP 2008).
[2:12] - **Karnataka** – early e-payment of VAT/commercial taxes; **Maharashtra** – the State the **IMF** had advised earlier not to adopt VAT (APPSC key).
[2:13] - **Sales tax** was **not levied in Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep** (question period); GST now covers all India.
[2:14] - **Central Sales Tax (CST)** – levied on **inter-State trade** and in **Union Territories**; **SEZs exempt**; subsumed in GST.
[2:15] # Service tax
[2:16] - **Indirect**, **Central** tax; introduced **1994-95** (Manmohan Singh as FM) on **Raja Chelliah Committee** advice.
[2:17] - Rates: **10% → 12% (Budget 2006-07)**; **12% → 14% (Budget 2015-16)**; from 2012-13, 12% on all services except the **negative list**.
[2:18] - **T.R. Rastogi (Rustagi) Panel** (2006) – Finance Ministry panel to review **service tax** issues and circulars.
[2:19] - **Swachh Bharat Cess** – **0.5%** on **all taxable services** (from 15 Nov 2015).
[2:20] - **Multiple tax rates** – chief drawback: **misclassification** of goods and services (disputes, evasion).

## SUB 4 | Progressive, Regressive and Degressive Taxes; Laffer Effect
[4:0] TABLE head: Type | Feature | Example
  [4:0.0] Progressive | Tax rate rises as income rises – average burden rises with income ("ability to pay") | Income tax
  [4:0.1] Regressive | Takes a higher proportion of income from low incomes than from high | Indirect taxes, GST on essentials
  [4:0.2] Proportional | One rate for all incomes | Flat-rate tax
  [4:0.3] Degressive | Rate rises at first, then becomes flat | Progressive slabs with a top flat rate
[4:1] - **Indirect taxes are regressive** because they are charged at the **same rate for all income groups**.
[4:2] - **Income tax in India**: **progressive and direct** – not collected by States, not proportional; **GST, sales tax, motor vehicles tax** are not progressive.
[4:3] - **India's tax system**: **progressive in nature but overall degressive** (progressive slabs up to a flat top rate; regressive indirect taxes).
[4:4] - **Redistribution** of income is best achieved by **progressive taxation combined with progressive expenditure**.
[4:5] - **Laffer effect**: **reduction in tax rates leads to a more than proportionate increase in tax yield**; Laffer curve – **inverted U**, zero revenue at 0% and 100%; **Arthur Laffer** (1974); term coined by **Jude Wanniski**.
[4:6] - Beyond a point, **higher tax rates may reduce revenue**.
[4:7] - **Widening the tax base** (lower exemption threshold, fewer exemptions) raises revenue without changing rates – the **Chelliah approach: lower rates, broader base**.
[4:8] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Economy p.64; NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 09 p.2; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 02 p.33; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. Part 5 p.204

## SUB 6 | Tax, Fee, Cess and Surcharge
[6:0] TABLE head: Levy | Key feature
  [6:0.0] Tax | Compulsory pecuniary burden imposed by legislative authority to support government; no quid pro quo
  [6:0.1] Fee | Paid for a specific service – quid pro quo
  [6:0.2] Cess | "Tax on tax" for a specific purpose; ends when the purpose is met; not shared
  [6:0.3] Surcharge | Additional charge on the tax payable – classically a levy to raise revenue temporarily (in practice long-running); none if no tax is due; not shared (Art. 271)
[6:1] - **Ad valorem tax** = **% of the value** of a commodity; **specific tax** = fixed amount per unit (weight/quantity).
[6:2] - **Clean Energy Cess** on coal (Finance Act 2010) → renamed **Clean Environment Cess** (Budget **2016-17**).
[6:3] - Budget **2016-17**: income-tax **surcharge 15%** on income **above ₹1 crore** (12% earlier).
[6:4] - **SAT** (Securities Appellate Tribunal) is **not a tax**; **MAT** (Minimum Alternate Tax, 1996-97), GST and VAT are.

## SUB 7 | Tax-GDP Ratio and Tax Buoyancy
[7:0] # Tax-GDP ratio
[7:1] - **Tax-GDP ratio = total tax revenue / nominal GDP** – yardstick of fiscal capacity.
[7:2] - **Low ratio** → large **informal sector** or high **evasion**; **falling ratio** → revenue growing **slower than GDP** (buoyancy < 1), less fiscal space, more borrowing – it does **not** by itself show less equitable distribution.
[7:3] TABLE head: Measure | Value
  [7:3.0] Centre gross tax revenue | 9.9% of GDP in 2019-20 (ES 2021-22); ~11.5% post-pandemic (FY22-FY25, ES 2025-26)
  [7:3.1] Combined Centre + States | ~19.6% (States' own tax ≈ 7% of GSDP)
  [7:3.2] 2008-09 (general government) | 16.39%
  [7:3.3] India average 2001-22 | 16.36% – lowest among emerging economies; below OECD
  [7:3.4] World Bank tipping point | 15%
[7:4] # Buoyancy and elasticity
[7:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [7:5.0] Buoyancy | % change in revenue / % change in GDP, including discretionary changes (rates, base); above 1 = revenue grows faster than GDP
  [7:5.1] Elasticity | Same response excluding discretionary changes
[7:6] - **2026-27 BE**: gross buoyancy **0.8**; **direct 1.1**, **indirect 0.3**.
[7:7] - Buoyant revenues helped bring the fiscal deficit to **4.4% of GDP in FY26** (ES 2025-26).
