=== NOTES 3:93 | Inflation – Types, Causes, Measurement (cpi, Wpi, Core), Effects and Control
## SUB 0 | Measuring Inflation – CPI, WPI and the Headline Index
[93:0:0] # Who compiles what
[93:0:1] TABLE head: Index | Compiled by | Base year (as of 2026) | Main use
  [93:0:1.0] CPI (Rural / Urban / Combined) | NSO, MoSPI (earlier CSO) | 2024 (replaced 2012; first release 12 Feb 2026 for Jan 2026) | Headline inflation; RBI's inflation target
  [93:0:1.1] CPI-IW (Industrial Workers) | Labour Bureau, Ministry of Labour and Employment | 2016 (from Sep 2020) | Dearness allowance of industrial workers and Central Government employees
  [93:0:1.2] CPI-AL / CPI-RL (Agricultural / Rural Labourers) | Labour Bureau | 2019 (from June 2025; earlier 1986-87) | Farm and rural labourers' wages
  [93:0:1.3] CPI-UNME (Urban Non-Manual Employees) | CSO (not the Labour Bureau) | 1984-85 | Discontinued from January 2011 after CPI-Urban began
  [93:0:1.4] WPI | Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce and Industry | 2022-23 (revised 2026; earlier 2011-12, 2004-05) | Wholesale prices; contract escalation
  [93:0:1.5] CFPI (Consumer Food Price Index) | NSO | Same as CPI | Food inflation
  [93:0:1.6] GDP deflator | NSO | National-accounts base | Widest measure – all goods and services produced
[93:0:2] - **CPI-IW base**: **1982 → 2001 → 2016** (w.e.f. September 2020).
[93:0:3] - **Labour Bureau**: set up **1920**, at **Chandigarh**, under the **Ministry of Labour and Employment**.
[93:0:4] - **Odd one out** among CPI-IW, CPI-AL, CPI-RL, CPI-UNME: **CPI-UNME** – the only one compiled by the **CSO** (UPSC 2010).
[93:0:5] - **Dearness allowance (DA)** is fixed on the **Consumer Price Index – CPI-IW** (not national income or per capita income); linking pay to a price index is **indexation** ("fixing of wage compensation").
[93:0:6] - **IIP** measures **industrial output, not prices** (NSO).
[93:0:7] # Headline measure – from WPI to CPI
[93:0:8] - WPI was India's **headline / "primary" / most common** inflation measure **till April 2014**.
[93:0:9] - **April 2014**: RBI adopted **CPI (Combined)** as its key measure (Urjit Patel Committee); **headline inflation is now based on CPI-C**.
[93:0:10] - India still publishes inflation on CPI, WPI and the labour CPIs, but the **policy (headline) measure is CPI-Combined**.
[93:0:11] - Inflation rate = **year-on-year % change in CPI** (current month's CPI over the CPI of the same month last year).
[93:0:12] # WPI
[93:0:13] - Prices of goods traded in bulk (first point); **no services** – CPI covers goods **and services**.
[93:0:14] - **2011-12 series**: **697 items**; **manufactured products = largest weight, 64.23%** (primary articles 22.62%, fuel and power 13.15%; 2004-05 series: manufactured 64.97%).
[93:0:15] - **New WPI (base 2022-23, 2026)**: **957 items**; weights on **Gross Value of Output**; runs with a new **Producer Price Index (PPI)** for **5 years**, then WPI is phased out.
[93:0:16] - **PPI** measures the **average change in prices received by producers** (output PPI: goods leaving production; input PPI: inputs entering it).
[93:0:17] - WPI was released **weekly** (primary articles, fuel) until 2012, so "WPI only monthly" was wrong in 2010; WPI gives **less weight to food** than CPI-IW.
[93:0:18] - WPI series on base **2004-05** was introduced on **14 September 2010**.
[93:0:19] # CPI structure
[93:0:20] - New CPI (base 2024): **12 divisions** (COICOP 2018), **358 items**; **food and beverages 36.75%** (about 46% in the 2012 series); 12 online platforms and rural house rent added.
[93:0:21] - **Food has a bigger weight in CPI than in WPI** (UPSC 2020).
[93:0:22] # Committees on price indices
[93:0:23] TABLE head: Committee | Subject
  [93:0:23.0] Abhijit Sen (2007; report May 2008) | WPI revision – base 2004-05
  [93:0:23.1] Saumitra Chaudhuri (2012) | WPI revision – base 2011-12
  [93:0:23.2] Ramesh Chand (NITI Aayog, 2025) | WPI base 2022-23
  [93:0:23.3] Urjit Patel (2014) | CPI as nominal anchor; flexible inflation targeting
[93:0:24] # GDP deflator
[93:0:25] - **GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100**; the **broadest** measure (all goods and services produced).
[93:0:26] - A **rise in the GDP/GNP deflator** = rising prices → **fall in the purchasing power of money**.
[93:0:27] > Exam angle: "headline / most common measure" now = **CPI-Combined**; older keys (pre-2014) = WPI. DA = CPI-IW (Labour Bureau). WPI = Commerce Ministry, manufactured goods ~64%.
[93:0:28] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.13-14; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.5-6; Rocket Indian Economy p.75-76; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. Part 4 p.126 (WPI weights, CPI-IW, CPI-UNME); PMF Lens 2026-06-07 (new WPI, PPI); Praveen CA360 June 2026 (WPI 2022-23, 957 items); PMF Lens 2026-02-08 (CPI 2024 food 36.75%); old app notes 3:93 sub 5-7; WPI weekly release history general knowledge, not checked
[93:0:29] - **National income and GDP estimates**: **NSO** under MoSPI (earlier CSO).
[93:0:30] - **Retail price index** measures the change in the **average cost of living** of households (a basket of retail prices).
## SUB 1 | Causes and Types of Inflation by Cause
[93:1:0] # By cause
[93:1:1] TABLE head: Type | Cause
  [93:1:1.0] Demand-pull | Aggregate demand > aggregate supply: expansionary fiscal and monetary policy, fiscal stimulus, higher purchasing power, rising money supply – "too much money chasing too few goods"
  [93:1:1.1] Cost-push | Rise in prices of factors of production (wages, raw materials, fuel) → supply falls while demand is constant
  [93:1:1.2] Structural / bottleneck | Deficiencies in the economy – poor farm productivity, supply bottlenecks, inefficient distribution and storage; persists long and is hard for government to tackle
  [93:1:1.3] Export-led | Strong export demand drains domestic supply
[93:1:2] - **Inflationary gap**: excess of aggregate demand (spending) over aggregate supply at **full-employment** output.
[93:1:3] - Demand-pull is **not** caused by **inflation-indexing of wages** (cost side) or **rising interest rates** (they cut demand) – UPSC 2021: 1, 2 and 4 only.
[93:1:4] - A **permanent fall in input costs** reduces cost-push pressure.
[93:1:5] # Causes of price rise in India
[93:1:6] TABLE head: Side | Factors
  [93:1:6.0] Demand | Increase in money supply, rise in non-developmental public expenditure, deficit financing / deficit budget, loose monetary policy, rapid GDP growth, population growth
  [93:1:6.1] Supply | Erratic / less agricultural production, hoarding, higher MSPs, rise in crude oil and fertiliser prices (oil shocks), inefficient storage and marketing, rupee depreciation, cartelisation
[93:1:7] - General price rise may come from **more money supply**, **lower aggregate output** and **higher effective demand** (UPSC 2013: all three).
[93:1:8] - **Food inflation** (UPSC 2011): changing **consumption pattern with rising incomes** and **structural constraints in the food supply chain** – not a 30% fall in foodgrain area.
[93:1:9] - **Unstable currency** works **against** inflation management; stability, sound monetary policy and balanced supply-demand help.
[93:1:10] # Crude oil benchmarks
[93:1:11] - **Brent** (North Sea), **West Texas Intermediate (WTI)** (US) and **Dubai** – grades of **crude oil** used as price benchmarks; oil-price shocks feed cost-push inflation.
[93:1:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.15, p.17-18; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.6; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. Part 4 p.117 (bottleneck, inflationary gap); Rocket Indian Economy p.76; old app notes 3:93 sub 2-3
[93:1:13] - **Better storage and distribution efficiency** eases supply shocks; **supply shocks** (erratic monsoon, El Nino, costlier crude, rupee depreciation) are the **dominant cause** of inflation in India.
[93:1:14] - **Inflation expectations** also feed inflation (wage-price spiral).
[93:1:15] - **Decrease in supply** = supply curve shifts left – the **same quantity is supplied only at a higher price**; extension/contraction are movements along the curve.
## SUB 2 | Meaning of Inflation and Its Kinds by Speed
[93:2:0] # Definition
[93:2:1] - **Inflation**: a sustained **rise in the general price level** and a **fall in the purchasing power (value) of money**; the essential attribute is **increase in prices**.
[93:2:2] - During inflation prices **increase**; when all prices rise, the purchasing power of money **decreases**.
[93:2:3] - A **rise in a commodity's price** = **fall in the value of currency and rise in the value of the commodity** (in money terms).
[93:2:4] - Effects together: prices rise **and** the value of money falls (not "exchange rate improves").
[93:2:5] - **Economic growth** is usually coupled with **(moderate) inflation** – rising income and demand push prices up.
[93:2:6] # Related terms
[93:2:7] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [93:2:7.0] Deflation | Persistent fall in the general price level (negative inflation) – not a currency fall or a fall in the inflation rate
  [93:2:7.1] Disinflation | Fall in the rate of inflation, still positive (e.g. 3% → 2%)
  [93:2:7.2] Reflation | Inflation returning after deflation – sign of recovery
  [93:2:7.3] Stagflation | High inflation + slow growth / stagnant output + high unemployment
  [93:2:7.4] Skewflation | Inflation in some items (e.g. food) while others are stable or falling
[93:2:8] - A **fall in the inflation rate** means only that **prices are rising more slowly** – not that prices fell, food supply rose or industry stagnated.
[93:2:9] # Stagflation factors
[93:2:10] - Inflationary pressure (e.g. from **higher government spending**) **plus** **declining industrial production and sluggish growth** → stagflation.
[93:2:11] - A **fall in aggregate demand** and **currency appreciation** pull prices **down** – they slow growth but do not create the inflation half of stagflation.
[93:2:12] # By speed
[93:2:13] TABLE head: Type | Rate
  [93:2:13.0] Creeping | Gradual, below about 3%
  [93:2:13.1] Walking / trotting | 3-10%
  [93:2:13.2] Galloping | Double or triple digit (20%, 100%, 200% a year)
  [93:2:13.3] Hyperinflation | Large and accelerating; prices shoot up overnight
[93:2:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.11-12, p.15-16, p.20-21; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.7; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. Part 4 p.113; Rocket Indian Economy p.76; old app notes 3:93 sub 0
## SUB 3 | Controlling Inflation
[93:3:0] # Tools
[93:3:1] TABLE head: Type | Measures
  [93:3:1.0] Monetary (RBI) | Decrease money circulation: raise repo, reverse repo, Bank Rate, CRR, SLR; sell government securities (OMO) – reducing interest rates is not an anti-inflation step
  [93:3:1.1] Fiscal (Government) | Cut non-developmental spending, avoid deficit financing, raise direct taxes, cut indirect taxes on essentials
  [93:3:1.2] Supply / administrative | Control demand, rationing, prevent hoarding and black marketing, ban exports of scarce goods, suspend futures trading, buffer stocks
[93:3:2] - Control needs a **combination of monetary, fiscal and exchange-rate policies** – monetary policy alone cannot fix supply-side inflation; controlling inflation is **not** the Government's job only – the **RBI** has the key role.
[93:3:3] - **Dual price policy** (to control prices): **sugar** – a share of output (**levy sugar**) bought at a controlled price for the PDS, the rest sold in the open market (levy abolished 2013).
[93:3:4] - **Price Stabilisation Fund (PSF)**: Central Sector Scheme, **2014-15 (launched March 2015)**; began with **onion and potato**; pulses added later.
[93:3:5] - **Bharat Atta** (subsidised wheat flour) helped contain **wheat inflation**; **Bharat Dal** (chana dal, July 2023) was extended to **moong dal**; sold via NAFED, NCCF, Kendriya Bhandar (APPSC G1 2024: II and IV).
[93:3:6] - OMSS (Domestic): FCI sells **wheat and rice**.
[93:3:7] - **RBI's biggest concern** in its policy statements: **inflation**.
[93:3:8] - Repo rate (as of 7 Oct 2026): **5.50%** (was 5.25% in Aug 2026); repo acts on demand, **cannot directly fix supply shocks** (onion, crude oil).
[93:3:9] # Inflation-Indexed Bonds (IIBs)
[93:3:10] - **Principal and/or interest linked to an inflation index** → protect investors from **uncertainty about inflation** (real return).
[93:3:11] - Government can **reduce coupon rates** on its borrowing (no inflation-risk premium); interest and capital gains are **taxable** (UPSC 2022: A and B only).
[93:3:12] - RBI issued IIBs in **2013**.
[93:3:13] # AP measures
[93:3:14] - **State-level Price Monitoring Committee** headed by the **Chief Secretary**; **DES monitors 8 essential commodities daily** (groundnut and palm oil added Feb 2025).
[93:3:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.18-19; Rocket Indian Economy p.76; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. Part 4 p.162 (PSF); RUNBOOK settled facts (repo 5.50%, RBI MPC 7 Oct 2026); old app notes 3:93 sub 10; dual price sugar general knowledge, not checked
[93:3:16] - **Onion exports**: ban lifted **4 May 2024** with **40% duty** and US$550 MEP; MEP removed and duty cut to **20%** on **13 Sep 2024**; NCCF and NAFED released 47,786 MT of PSF onion (to 8 Oct 2025).
[93:3:17] - **Bharat Chana Dal** sold at **₹60/kg** from July 2023.
## SUB 4 | Effects of Inflation and Deflation
[93:4:0] # Gainers and losers of inflation
[93:4:1] TABLE head: Gain | Lose
  [93:4:1.0] Debtors (borrowers) – repay money of lower real value | Creditors (lenders) – hurt most
  [93:4:1.1] Businessmen, producers, big farmers (profits rise faster than costs) | Fixed-income groups – pensioners, salary and wage earners
  [93:4:1.2] Holders of real assets, equity investors | Bond / debenture holders, savings-account holders
  [93:4:1.3] Exporters (if the rupee depreciates) | Consumers, the poor
[93:4:2] - Rule: **inflation benefits debtors**; it does **not** benefit bond-holders, consumers or fixed-income groups.
[93:4:3] - Price-hike: businessmen gain and consumers must curtail needs (most keys: both true, R not the explanation; strictly not "all" businessmen gain).
[93:4:4] - Other effects: falling saving (MPS falls), shift to gold, rising inequality, wage-price spiral, discourages exports; **food inflation is regressive** (the poor spend more of income on food); **menu costs** of re-pricing.
[93:4:5] # Deflation
[93:4:6] - Purchasing power of money **rises** → **fixed-income groups (pensioners, salary earners)** and **creditors gain**; **debtors lose** (heavier real debt).
[93:4:7] - **Deflationary spiral**: postponed purchases → lower profits → higher unemployment → heavier real debt burden.
[93:4:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.12, p.18-20; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. Part 4 p.122; old app notes 3:93 sub 4
## SUB 5 | Headline, Core and Food Inflation
[93:5:0] TABLE head: Basis | Headline inflation | Core inflation
  [93:5:0.0] Covers | Total inflation, including food and energy (oil, gas) | Excludes volatile food and fuel / energy
  [93:5:0.1] Shows | Raw figure – what consumers feel | Underlying, long-run trend
  [93:5:0.2] Volatility | More volatile | More stable
  [93:5:0.3] Index | Change in CPI-Combined | CPI excluding food and fuel
[93:5:1] - **Headline** = rate of change of the CPI (basket of a typical family); **core** = CPI change after excluding volatile items such as food and fuel – both statements true (RAS 2023).
[93:5:2] - Core does **not** include food and energy; headline does **not** exclude food (RAS 2024: only "headline is more volatile" is correct).
[93:5:3] - Inflation that leaves out commodities with volatile prices = **core inflation**.
[93:5:4] - **Food inflation**: measured by the **CFPI** (10 sub-groups; cereals carry the highest weight).
[93:5:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.16, p.22; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.7-8; Rocket Indian Economy p.75; old app notes 3:93 sub 1
## SUB 6 | Index Numbers and the Business Cycle
[93:6:0] - A **price index** is a **statistical device** to measure changes in the general price level.
[93:6:1] - **Laspeyres** index: **base-year (initial-year) quantities** as weights; **Paasche**: current-year weights; **Fisher's ideal index** = geometric mean of the two.
[93:6:2] - **Irving Fisher** is the economist most associated with **index numbers** (ideal index, tests of index numbers).
[93:6:3] - **Business cycle** phases: **expansion / recovery** (real GDP rising) → **peak / boom** → **recession** (falling) → **trough / depression** (bottom).
[93:6:4] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. Part 4 p.136-137 (boom, recession, recovery); index-number facts general knowledge, not checked

## SUB 8 | Inflation Targeting and the Phillips Curve
[93:8:0] TABLE head: Step | Year | Provision
  [93:8:0.0] Urjit Patel Committee | 2014 | CPI as nominal anchor
  [93:8:0.1] Monetary Policy Framework Agreement | Feb 2015 | Led to RBI Act amendment
  [93:8:0.2] RBI Act 1934 amended | 2016 | Statutory flexible inflation targeting; Sec 45ZA target set by Centre with RBI every 5 years; Sec 45ZB six-member MPC; Sec 45ZN report after 3 consecutive quarters of breach (first invoked 2022)
[93:8:1] - **Target (1 Apr 2026 – 31 Mar 2031)**: **4% CPI-Combined ± 2% (2-6%)**; 6% is only the upper limit.
[93:8:2] - Average CPI fell from 6.8% (2012-16) to about 4.9% under the framework.
[93:8:3] - **Phillips curve** (A.W. Phillips, 1958): **inverse relation between unemployment and (wage) inflation**; Friedman: long-run curve **vertical**; India's curve is flat (about 92% informal workers).

=== NOTES 3:94 | Indian Tax System – Direct and Indirect Taxes, Tax Buoyancy, Tax-gdp Ratio, Progressivity
## SUB 0 | Direct and Indirect Taxes – Classification and Taxable Events
[94:0:0] # Direct vs indirect
[94:0:1] TABLE head: Basis | Direct tax | Indirect tax
  [94:0:1.0] Burden | Cannot be shifted – impact and incidence on the same person | Shifted to the consumer through prices – impact and incidence on different persons
  [94:0:1.1] Examples | Income tax, corporation tax, wealth tax, capital gains, property, gift, estate duty, expenditure tax, STT | Customs, excise, sales tax, VAT, service tax, GST, entertainment, octroi, entry tax
  [94:0:1.2] Nature | Generally progressive | Generally regressive
  [94:0:1.3] Price effect | Non-inflationary; does not directly raise the price of goods | Inflationary – raises prices
  [94:0:1.4] Administered by | CBDT – statutory body under the Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963 | CBIC (formerly CBEC)
[94:0:2] - **Not direct**: customs duty, sales tax, excise, octroi – the usual "odd one out" against income, corporation, wealth and gift taxes.
[94:0:3] - **Indirect pair**: **customs and excise** (corporation and wealth taxes are direct).
[94:0:4] - **Income, corporate and wealth taxes – all direct**; "1 and 2 only" for income + corporate tax against service tax and customs.
[94:0:5] - **Income tax** does not directly increase the price of a commodity – trade/business tax, import and excise (manufacturing) duties do.
[94:0:6] - **GST** – a comprehensive **indirect** tax on supply, **destination-based**; it is not related to income.
[94:0:7] - **Service tax** – an **indirect tax levied by the Central Government**, introduced **1994-95** (subsumed in GST 2017).
[94:0:8] - **STT** – a **direct tax** on **transactions through the stock market** (from **1 October 2004**), though collected by the exchange.
[94:0:9] - **"Paper taxes"**: wealth, gift and estate duty – never yielded much revenue; **wealth tax** gives the **least** revenue of the major taxes.
[94:0:10] - **Impact** = on whom the tax is first imposed; **incidence** = who finally bears it; moving the burden forward (consumers) or backward (suppliers) = **tax shifting**. Property tax cannot be shifted through prices.
[94:0:11] - **Incidence and elasticity**: if demand is **perfectly inelastic (elasticity zero)** the price rises by the full tax and the **consumer bears it all**; with infinite elasticity the price cannot rise.
[94:0:12] - **Cut in direct taxes** → higher **disposable income** and consumption demand.
[94:0:13] # Base / taxable event of each tax
[94:0:14] TABLE head: Tax | Taxable event / base
  [94:0:14.0] Central excise | Manufacture / production of goods (paid by the manufacturer) – "manufacture only"
  [94:0:14.1] Customs | Import and export (Customs Act, 1962)
  [94:0:14.2] Sales tax / VAT | Sale of goods
  [94:0:14.3] Service tax | Provision of service
  [94:0:14.4] GST | Supply of goods or services (from 1 July 2017)
  [94:0:14.5] Corporation tax | Income / net profit of companies – not production, sales or stock
  [94:0:14.6] Income tax | Income of individuals, HUFs, firms (Income Tax Act, 1961)
  [94:0:14.7] Capital gains tax | Gain on sale of a capital asset
  [94:0:14.8] Wealth tax | Net wealth – 1957 to 2015 (abolished)
[94:0:15] - **Toll** – a levy (tax) for using a road or bridge; tolls are a **State List** subject.
[94:0:16] > Exam angle: "which is not a direct tax / which is indirect" is the single most repeated tax question (APPSC, UPPSC, MPPSC, Police).

[94:0:18] - **Differential tax incidence** (comparing two taxes of equal yield): **R.A. Musgrave** (Theory of Public Finance, 1959).
[94:0:19] - **'GAFA tax'** (Google, Apple, Facebook, Amazon): **France's** 3% digital services tax, from **1 January 2019**.

[94:1:2] # Union taxes
[94:1:3] - **Union alone (Union List)**: **customs duties, corporation tax**; not levied by States.
[94:1:4] - **Corporation tax** is **imposed by the Central Government**; before 2000 its proceeds were **not shared** with States (Art. 270 then covered only income tax; Union excise under Art. 272) – the basis of old keys "not shared" / "accrues wholly to the Centre". **Since the 80th Amendment it is in the divisible pool.**

[94:1:7] - **Income tax on salary (TDS)** is collected and paid into the account of the **Central Government**.
[94:1:8] - **Agricultural income tax** – **not shared** by the Centre (it is a State tax). Income from **poultry, dairy, wool rearing** is **not** agricultural income (taxable); **nursery saplings** are agricultural income; **rural agricultural land is not a capital asset** under the Income-tax Act, 1961.
[94:1:9] # State taxes
[94:1:10] - **Levied by States / local bodies**: **sales tax** (pre-GST), **entertainment tax** (State List, List II), **road / motor vehicle tax**, profession tax, property tax, land revenue, **State excise on liquor**, stamp duty and registration, electricity duty, agricultural income tax.
[94:1:11] - **Sales tax on toothpaste** (pre-GST) – **imposed and collected by the State Government**.
[94:1:12] - **Not a State / AP own-revenue source**: **customs duty**, corporation tax ("taxes on companies").
[94:1:13] - **Most important source of State revenue**: **sales tax** (pre-2017) – now **SGST**.
[94:1:14] - **States' revenue receipts**: **own tax revenue** is the largest component (~50%, FY25); share in Central taxes second (~32%); CAG average 2014-24: own tax 47%, tax share 28%, grants 17%.
[94:1:15] - **AP (2024-25 RE)**: **SGST** largest own-tax source – ₹36,296 crore (**38%**) of ₹94,967 crore; **State Excise** ₹21,300 crore (**22%**).
[94:1:16] - Union Budget 2017-18: farmers who gave land for **Amaravati** (AP capital) under land pooling – exempted from **capital gains tax**.

## SUB 2 | VAT, MODVAT, CENVAT, Sales Tax and Service Tax
[94:2:0] # Value Added Tax
[94:2:1] - **VAT** = tax on the **value added at each stage** between production and final sale – a **multi-point**, **destination-based**, **non-cascading** tax on final consumption, borne by the consumer; invoice-credit trail makes it **self-enforcing**; a **harmonised** commodity tax that **improves revenue buoyancy** ("will diminish revenue buoyancy" is **not** VAT's idea).
[94:2:2] - State VAT was a **State subject** (State List Entry 54) – not "basically a subject of the Central Government".
[94:2:3] - VAT applies to **commodity taxes – excise duties** (MODVAT/CENVAT) and sales tax; not to income tax, estate duty or agricultural income tax.
[94:2:4] - Concept first proposed by **Wilhelm von Siemens** (Germany); first implemented by **France (1954)**.
[94:2:5] - **L.K. Jha Indirect Taxation Enquiry Committee (1976)** – suggested VAT at the manufacturing stage.
[94:2:6] - **VAT rate for medicines/drugs**: the **4%** slab (pre-GST State VAT).
[94:2:7] - Some goods (petroleum, alcohol) are still outside GST – **State VAT** continues on them.
[94:2:8] # Chronology
[94:2:9] TABLE head: Event | Date
  [94:2:9.0] MODVAT – on Union excise duties, to end cascading (first indirect tax reform) | 1 March 1986 (Budget 1986-87)
  [94:2:9.1] Service tax (Chelliah Committee advice; 3 services) | 1994-95
  [94:2:9.2] Uniform floor rates of sales tax (Standing Committee of State Finance Ministers) | 1 January 2000
  [94:2:9.3] CENVAT – replaced MODVAT; Union excise (an indirect tax) | 1 April 2000
  [94:2:9.4] First State VAT – Haryana | 1 April 2003
  [94:2:9.5] State VAT nationwide (20 States/UTs) | 1 April 2005
  [94:2:9.6] Last States to join (TN 2007, UP 2008) | by 2008-09
  [94:2:9.7] GST | 1 July 2017
[94:2:10] - **Uniform sales tax**: in **January 2000** the Standing Committee of State Finance Ministers recommended **uniform floor rates of sales tax**, implemented from **1 January 2000**.
[94:2:11] - About **7 States** had not implemented VAT by December 2005 (Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand – 2006; TN 2007; UP 2008).
[94:2:12] - **Karnataka** – early e-payment of VAT/commercial taxes; **Maharashtra** – the State the **IMF** had advised earlier not to adopt VAT (APPSC key).
[94:2:13] - **Sales tax** was **not levied in Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep** (question period); GST now covers all India.
[94:2:14] - **Central Sales Tax (CST)** – levied on **inter-State trade** and in **Union Territories**; **SEZs exempt**; subsumed in GST.
[94:2:15] # Service tax
[94:2:16] - **Indirect**, **Central** tax; introduced **1994-95** (Manmohan Singh as FM) on **Raja Chelliah Committee** advice.
[94:2:17] - Rates: **10% → 12% (Budget 2006-07)**; **12% → 14% (Budget 2015-16)**; from 2012-13, 12% on all services except the **negative list**.
[94:2:18] - **T.R. Rastogi (Rustagi) Panel** (2006) – Finance Ministry panel to review **service tax** issues and circulars.
[94:2:19] - **Swachh Bharat Cess** – **0.5%** on **all taxable services** (from 15 Nov 2015).
[94:2:20] - **Multiple tax rates** – chief drawback: **misclassification** of goods and services (disputes, evasion).

[94:2:22] - **VAT design (2003):** small dealers with annual turnover up to **₹10 lakh** exempt (printed key; the 2005 White Paper used ₹5 lakh, States free up to ₹10 lakh).

## SUB 4 | Progressive, Regressive and Degressive Taxes; Laffer Effect
[94:4:0] TABLE head: Type | Feature | Example
  [94:4:0.0] Progressive | Tax rate rises as income rises – average burden rises with income ("ability to pay") | Income tax
  [94:4:0.1] Regressive | Takes a higher proportion of income from low incomes than from high | Indirect taxes, GST on essentials
  [94:4:0.2] Proportional | One rate for all incomes | Flat-rate tax
  [94:4:0.3] Degressive | Rate rises at first, then becomes flat | Progressive slabs with a top flat rate
[94:4:1] - **Indirect taxes are regressive** because they are charged at the **same rate for all income groups**.
[94:4:2] - **Income tax in India**: **progressive and direct** – not collected by States, not proportional; **GST, sales tax, motor vehicles tax** are not progressive.
[94:4:3] - **India's tax system**: **progressive in nature but overall degressive** (progressive slabs up to a flat top rate; regressive indirect taxes).
[94:4:4] - **Redistribution** of income is best achieved by **progressive taxation combined with progressive expenditure**.
[94:4:5] - **Laffer effect**: **reduction in tax rates leads to a more than proportionate increase in tax yield**; Laffer curve – **inverted U**, zero revenue at 0% and 100%; **Arthur Laffer** (1974); term coined by **Jude Wanniski**.
[94:4:6] - Beyond a point, **higher tax rates may reduce revenue**.
[94:4:7] - **Widening the tax base** (lower exemption threshold, fewer exemptions) raises revenue without changing rates – the **Chelliah approach: lower rates, broader base**.
[94:4:8] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Economy p.64; NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 09 p.2; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 02 p.33; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. Part 5 p.204

## SUB 6 | Tax, Fee, Cess and Surcharge
[94:6:0] TABLE head: Levy | Key feature
  [94:6:0.0] Tax | Compulsory pecuniary burden imposed by legislative authority to support government; no quid pro quo

## SUB 7 | Tax-GDP Ratio and Tax Buoyancy
[94:7:0] # Tax-GDP ratio
[94:7:1] - **Tax-GDP ratio = total tax revenue / nominal GDP** – yardstick of fiscal capacity.
[94:7:2] - **Low ratio** → large **informal sector** or high **evasion**; **falling ratio** → revenue growing **slower than GDP** (buoyancy < 1), less fiscal space, more borrowing – it does **not** by itself show less equitable distribution.
[94:7:3] TABLE head: Measure | Value
  [94:7:3.0] Centre gross tax revenue | 9.9% of GDP in 2019-20 (ES 2021-22); ~11.5% post-pandemic (FY22-FY25, ES 2025-26)
  [94:7:3.1] Combined Centre + States | ~19.6% (States' own tax ≈ 7% of GSDP)
  [94:7:3.2] 2008-09 (general government) | 16.39%
  [94:7:3.3] India average 2001-22 | 16.36% – lowest among emerging economies; below OECD
  [94:7:3.4] World Bank tipping point | 15%
[94:7:4] # Buoyancy and elasticity
[94:7:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [94:7:5.0] Buoyancy | % change in revenue / % change in GDP, including discretionary changes (rates, base); above 1 = revenue grows faster than GDP
  [94:7:5.1] Elasticity | Same response excluding discretionary changes
[94:7:6] - **2026-27 BE**: gross buoyancy **0.8**; **direct 1.1**, **indirect 0.3**.
[94:7:7] - Buoyant revenues helped bring the fiscal deficit to **4.4% of GDP in FY26** (ES 2025-26).

## SUB 1 | GST – Nature and Objectives
[96:1:0] - **GST (Goods and Services Tax)**: an **indirect tax**; **single, comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based** tax levied on **every value addition** – a **value-added consumption tax**, **not a gross value tax**.
[96:1:1] - **Taxable event: supply** of goods and services (not manufacture, not sale).
[96:1:2] - **Destination principle**: tax accrues to the State where goods/services are **consumed**, **not where produced**.
[96:1:3] - Constitutional definition: **Article 366(12A)** – a tax on supply of goods or services except **alcoholic liquor for human consumption**.
[96:1:4] - In force **1 July 2017**; **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016**; biggest indirect-tax reform since Independence – a unified framework for many indirect taxes.
[96:1:5] - Objectives: **"one nation, one tax"**; **end cascading ("tax on tax")** through input tax credit; **one national market** (replaces multiple taxes of multiple authorities); transparency in **indirect** taxes; designed to be **non-inflationary** (A-R: avoids tax-on-tax → correct explanation).
[96:1:6] - Not an advantage: GST does not "drastically cut the current account deficit" or make India "overtake China" (UPSC 2017: only I).
[96:1:7] - GST involves **both Centre and States** (dual GST) – "only at the central level" is false.
[96:1:8] > Exam angle: statements on nature – destination-based (4 PYQs), replaces indirect taxes, not direct taxes, not gross value.
[96:1:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 09 p.4; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy p.19-20; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. Part 5 p.204-206
## SUB 2 | GST Council
[96:2:0] - **Constitutional body** under **Article 279A**; **never statutory** (APPSC G1 2024: "statutory, later constitutional" is false).
[96:2:1] - **Chairperson**: **Union Finance Minister** (first: Arun Jaitley). **Members**: Union **MoS Revenue/Finance** (only a member); Finance/Taxation Minister of each State and UT with legislature; **Vice-Chairperson** chosen by State members.
[96:2:2] TABLE head: Rule | Value
  [96:2:2.0] Quorum | One-half of total members
  [96:2:2.1] Centre's weight | 1/3 of votes cast
  [96:2:2.2] All States together | 2/3 of votes cast – each State has an equal vote
  [96:2:2.3] Decision | 3/4 majority of weighted votes of members present and voting
[96:2:3] - Recommends **tax rates, exempted goods, threshold limits**, model laws, special rates, date for petroleum – the **Council recommends**, the Centre and States **notify** rates (rates are not fixed by Parliament, EC or the Finance Commission).
[96:2:4] - **Disputes**: the Council itself sets up a dispute mechanism (not the PAC); proceedings are **not invalid** due to a vacancy or defect in appointment.
[96:2:5] - **Mohit Minerals (2022)**: recommendations **persuasive, not binding**; simultaneous power under 246A; "flexible federation".

## SUB 3 | Divisible Pool, Cesses and Surcharges
[98:3:0] - **Divisible pool** = all Union taxes **except cesses and surcharges** (and Art. 268/269 taxes), net of collection charges – set up by the **80th Amendment (2000)** following the 10th FC.
[98:3:1] - Cesses and surcharges per ₹100 of Central tax: **₹13.5 (2021-22, decade high)** → ₹11 (2025-26 est.).
[98:3:2] - Headline devolution **41%**; effective only **30-32%** of gross tax revenue.
[98:3:3] - **16th FC "grand bargain"**: States take a **smaller share of a larger pool** if most cesses merge into regular taxes.
