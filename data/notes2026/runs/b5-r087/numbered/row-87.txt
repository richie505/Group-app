## SUB 0 | Biosphere Reserves and the MAB Programme
[0:0] # Concept (most asked)
[0:1] - **Biosphere Reserve (BR):** large terrestrial/coastal/marine area recognised under **UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere (MAB) Programme** (launched **1971**; not IUCN); UNESCO calls BRs "learning places for sustainable development".
[0:2] - **Purpose:** conserve the **genetic diversity** of the whole landscape **together with the traditional life of local/tribal people** – so the "most important strategy for conservation of biodiversity together with traditional human life" is the **biosphere reserve**, not an NP, sanctuary or botanical garden.
[0:3] - Designation **does not change legal status or ownership**; a BR may contain one or more NPs/sanctuaries. **IN-MAB Committee (MoEFCC)** recommends sites; State prepares the management plan; funding **60:40** (**90:10** for NE and three Himalayan States). **Seville Strategy (1995)** added social, cultural and economic needs.
[0:4] - Not a protected-area category under the WPA 1972 (those are NP, sanctuary, conservation reserve, community reserve).
[0:5] TABLE head: Zone | What is allowed
  [0:5.0] Core | Strictly protected (usually NP/WLS); no human activity
  [0:5.1] Buffer | Limited activity: research, education, training, limited recreation
  [0:5.2] Transition | Settlements, farming, forestry, tourism

## SUB 1 | In-situ and Ex-situ Conservation
[1:0] TABLE head: Basis | In-situ (on-site) | Ex-situ (off-site)
  [1:0.0] Where | Within the natural habitat | Outside it, in a controlled setting
  [1:0.1] Examples | National park, wildlife sanctuary, biosphere reserve, reserved/protected forest, nature reserve, sacred groves | Botanical garden, zoo, safari park, seed/gene bank, cryobank (cryopreservation), tissue culture, captive breeding
  [1:0.2] Saves | Whole ecosystem and its genetic diversity | The species or its genes
[1:1] - "Not an in-situ site" = **Botanical Garden** (UPSC 2011, UPPCS 2018). Genetic diversity in **National Parks** is maintained by **in-situ conservation**.
[1:2] - **In-situ is generally most effective:** species keep interacting with their environment; ex-situ helps education, research and reintroduction but needs space and money and cannot save the ecosystem.
[1:3] - **Captive breeding** for species whose habitat is lost, e.g. Gangetic gharials bred and released; 10 captive-bred **Asian Giant Tortoises** released in **Ntangki NP (Nagaland)**.
[1:4] - **Meristem culture** = virus-free propagation. "Biological diversity" coined by **Raymond F. Dasmann**.
[1:5] > Exam angle: "which is NOT in-situ / which is ex-situ" – botanical garden, zoo, seed bank and cryopreservation are ex-situ.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 05 p.7-9 (in-situ, ex-situ, PYQ); NextGen Rocket Environment p.44 (in-situ/ex-situ lists, Dasmann); PMF Environment Part 3 p.41; app notes 5:87 sub 0
## SUB 2 | Protected-Area Categories, Eco-sensitive Zones and Global Coverage
[2:0] TABLE head: Category (WPA 1972) | Key feature
  [2:0.0] National Park | Declared by State (Centre also may); no rights, no grazing, no collection of biomass/forest produce; boundary altered only on NBWL recommendation
  [2:0.1] Wildlife Sanctuary | Some rights allowed; grazing and firewood collection regulated by the Chief Wildlife Warden
  [2:0.2] Conservation Reserve | 2002/2003 amendment; government land, usually adjoining/linking PAs
  [2:0.3] Community Reserve | 2002/2003 amendment; private or community land a community volunteers; declared by the State Government; managed by a Community Reserve Management Committee (not the Chief Wildlife Warden); sanctuary restrictions (no hunting) apply; residents' rights not affected
[2:1] - Local people may **not collect biomass in National Parks** (they may in BR transition zones, Ramsar wetlands and, regulated, in sanctuaries).
[2:2] - **Network (2026):** **1,134 PAs** (981 in 2020): 109 NPs, 573 WLS, 127 conservation, 75 community reserves; ~**5.32%** of India's area; 58 tiger reserves. First community reserve: **Keshopur-Chhamb (Punjab)**. Biosphere reserve is NOT a PA category.
[2:3] # Eco-sensitive Zones (ESZ)
[2:4] - **Purpose:** **"shock absorbers"** / transition zones around NPs and sanctuaries (MoEFCC guidelines), to minimise forest depletion and human–animal conflict.
[2:5] - Notified under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** – **not the WPA 1972**; they **do not ban all activity except agriculture** (only mining, polluting industry, major hydro etc. are prohibited; hotels, tree felling regulated; agriculture, organic farming permitted) → UPSC 2014: **neither statement correct**.
[2:6] - **Wildlife Action Plan 2002-16** / MoEF 2011 guidelines: up to **10 km**; **Supreme Court (June 2022):** minimum **1 km** ESZ, no mining inside PAs or within 1 km.
[2:7] # Global coverage
[2:8] - **2014:** protected areas covered **15.2% of land** and **8.4% of coastal-marine areas** (marine areas under national jurisdiction).
[2:9] - **30x30** = Target 3 of the **Kunming-Montreal GBF** (2022): 30% of land and oceans by 2030; **OECMs** (e.g. Aravalli Biodiversity Park, Gurugram, India's first, 2022) count towards it. **WDPA** is run by **UNEP-WCMC** with IUCN.
[2:10] > Exam angle: NP = no biomass collection; ESZ = shock absorber under EP Act; community reserve statements; 2014 coverage figure.

## SUB 4 | Gene Banks, Seed Vaults and Cryopreservation
[4:0] - **Cryopreservation:** in **liquid nitrogen at −196 °C**; the gas used in cryo-banks is **nitrogen**; metabolism stops. Stores gametes, embryos, seeds, pollen, tissues, DNA – **semen straws / cattle sperm for artificial insemination** are kept in liquid nitrogen.
[4:1] - Seeds keep longest in **cool and dry** conditions (low moisture and temperature slow respiration and fungi).
[4:2] TABLE head: Facility | Location / fact
  [4:2.0] Svalbard Global Seed Vault | Spitsbergen, Norway; world's largest secure seed vault, in permafrost (~−18 °C)
  [4:2.1] Chang La seed vault | High-altitude pass in Ladakh; joint venture of NBPGR and DIHAR (DRDO)
  [4:2.2] National Gene Bank | ICAR-NBPGR, New Delhi
  [4:2.3] International Rice Genebank | IRRI, Los Baños, Philippines
  [4:2.4] Field gene bank | NBRI's Banthara (Lucknow) – preserves endangered plants, identifies useful wild plants, checks biopiracy
  [4:2.5] Millennium Seed Bank | Kew (Wakehurst), UK
[4:3] - APPSC G1: all three seed-vault statements (Svalbard, Chang La, NBPGR+DIHAR) are **correct** → "none incorrect".
[4:4] - ICAR bureaux: NBAGR **Karnal** (livestock), NBFGR **Lucknow** (fish), NBAIM **Mau** (microbes); 2nd National Gene Bank (Budget 2025-26): 10 lakh lines. **LaCONES** (CCMB Hyderabad) = India's frozen zoo.

[6:0] - **Sacred groves:** forest patches preserved by communities on **religious belief** (trees and wildlife venerated) – a **traditional method of conserving tree cover**; an **in-situ** practice, comparable to today's **biosphere reserves** (community-protected nature).
