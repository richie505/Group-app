## SUB 1 | Arab Conquest of Sindh (712 Ce)
[102:1:0] # Commander and Ruler Defeated
[102:1:1] - **First Muslim invaders** of India: the **Arabs**, under **Muhammad bin Qasim**, Sindh, 712.
[102:1:2] - Qasim: nephew and son-in-law of **Al-Hajjaj (Hajjaj bin Yusuf)**, the Umayyad governor; sent by **Caliph al-Walid** at the age of **17**.
[102:1:3] - Removed **Raja Dahir**, capital **Alor (Aror/Arod)**, near modern Karachi.
[102:1:4] - Places taken: **Debal, Nirun, Sehwan, Brahmanabad, Multan** (not Somnath).
[102:1:5] Dahir's capital was Alor (Aror); Brahmanabad was only one of the places taken.
[102:1:6] # Chachnama
[102:1:7] - Arabic chronicle of the conquest of Sindh (680-718); original author unclear.
[102:1:8] - **Persian translation** by **Muhammad Ali bin Abu Bakr Kufi** (BPSC 2024).
[102:1:9] # Causes and Effects
[102:1:10] TABLE head: Factor | Fact
  [102:1:10.0] Causes | Arab maritime interests, expansion, piracy against Arab ships
  [102:1:10.1] Religion | No mass Islamisation; Hindus and Buddhists given dhimmi status
  [102:1:10.2] Limited reach | Confined to Sindh by the Thar and Aravallis, strong Pratiharas, Rashtrakutas, Palas, weak resources
  [102:1:10.3] Knowledge transfer | Indian decimal and place-value maths, astronomy, medicine → Islamic world → Europe
[102:1:11] - **Nagabhata I (Pratihara)** drove back the Arabs, keeping them in Sindh; Arab writers said Pratiharas had the **strongest cavalry** in India.
## SUB 2 | Ghaznavids and Mahmud of Ghazni
[102:2:0] # Founders
[102:2:1] TABLE head: Person | Role
  [102:2:1.0] Alptigin | Founder of the Ghaznavid line; Turkish slave of the Samanid governors
  [102:2:1.1] Sabuktigin | Real founder
  [102:2:1.2] Mahmud (998-1030) | The raider; 17 raids
[102:2:2] Alptigin = founder; Sabuktigin = real founder; Mahmud = the raider.
[102:2:3] # Raids → Target
[102:2:4] TABLE head: Target | Year | Note
  [102:2:4.0] Nagarkot (Kangra) | 1009 | Temple treasure
  [102:2:4.1] Thanesar (Chakraswamin temple) | 1014 | 
  [102:2:4.2] Mathura | 1018 | 
  [102:2:4.3] Kanauj | 1018 | Pratihara king submitted
  [102:2:4.4] Somnath (Gujarat) | 1025-26 | Solanki Bhima I; loot said to be 20 million dinars
[102:2:5] - Raids were repeated, **not systematic conquest**; he did not aim at a permanent empire.
[102:2:6] - Issued **silver coins** with **Arabic and Sanskrit** legends; built the Ghazni minarets.
[102:2:7] - After Mahmud, the Ghaznavids weakened under **Seljuk** pressure.
[102:2:8] # Indian Rulers Who Resisted
[102:2:9] - **Jayapala** and **Anandapala** (Hindu Shahis): first to resist Mahmud.
[102:2:10] - **Chandela Vidyadhara**: faced and **repulsed** Mahmud (1019-20), not defeated (MPPSC 2021). Chandelas ruled Central India, 9th-13th c.; first capital **Khajuraho**.
[102:2:11] - **Bhimadeva I** ruled during the Somnath attack and **reconstructed** the temple.
[102:2:12] # Motives and Effects
[102:2:13] - Motives: more than religion; **temple wealth** to fund the state, political expansion.
[102:2:14] - Effect: exposed India's military weakness and opened the **north-west frontier**.
[102:2:15] - **Somnath Swabhiman Parv** (launched **January 2026**): 1,000 years since Mahmud's attack.

[102:3:1] # Al-Biruni
[102:3:2] - Born **973** in **Khwarizm (Khiva)**; came with Mahmud; "**father of Indian historical writing**".
[102:3:3] - Kitab-ul-Hind: fair account of 11th-century Indian society, religion, science, customs. Translated into English by **Edward Sachau**, Hindi by Rajnikant Sharma.
[102:3:4] - **Kitab Patanjal**: Arabic translation of Patanjali's **Yoga-Sutra**.
[102:3:5] - Not a secular author: no kind word about the Somnath raid.
## SUB 4 | Muhammad Ghori and the Battles of Tarain
[102:4:0] # Route and Conquests
[102:4:1] - Shihabuddin Muhammad Ghori (Ghurid, 1173-1206) entered through the **Gomal Pass**; aimed at **permanent occupation**.
[102:4:2] - Multan (1175) → Peshawar (c. 1179) → Sindh (c. 1182) → Punjab and Lahore (c. 1190).
[102:4:3] # Battles → Opponent + Victor
[102:4:4] TABLE head: Battle | Year | Opponent | Victor
  [102:4:4.0] Gujarat (Kasahrada/Kayadara, near Mount Abu) | 1178 | Mularaja II (Bhimdev II), Chaulukya/Solanki; regent mother Naika Devi | Gujarat; Ghori's first defeat
  [102:4:4.1] First Tarain (near Karnal, Haryana) | 1191 | Prithviraj Chauhan (Prithviraja III, Chahamana of Ajmer) | Prithviraj; Ghori barely escaped
  [102:4:4.2] Second Tarain | 1192 | Prithviraj Chauhan | Ghori; turning point, opened North India
  [102:4:4.3] Chandawar (Firozabad, UP, on the Yamuna near Agra) | 1194 | Jayachandra (Jaichand), Gahadavala of Kannauj | Ghori
[102:4:5] - Cause of Tarain I: **Tabarhinda (Bhatinda)**; Prithviraj took it but left no strong garrison.
[102:4:6] - Aibak (Ghori's slave, Master of Stables) got the **first Iqta** in India (1192); capital **Lahore**; first Sultan of Delhi on Ghori's death (1206). Delhi Sultanate 1206-1526: Mamluk, Khalji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi.
[102:4:7] Somnath = Bhima I (Mahmud); Ghori beaten by Mularaja II (1178).
## SUB 5 | Bakhtiyar Khalji
[102:5:0] - **Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji**: Ghori's slave/general; **real founder of Turk rule in Bihar** (BPSC 2022).
[102:5:1] - Conquered Bihar and Bengal (**1193-1203**); sacked **Nalanda, Vikramashila, Odantapuri**.
[102:5:2] - Entered **Nadiya**, drove **Lakshmana Sena** to East Bengal (1204); ruled from **Lakhnauti**.
## SUB 6 | Causes of Turkish Success
[102:6:0] TABLE head: Factor | Point
  [102:6:0.0] Political | No political unity in North India; rival Rajput states after the Pratihara collapse (UPPSC 2024: A and R true, R explains A)
  [102:6:0.1] Military | Superior war technique: mobile cavalry, mounted archers vs elephant-reliant armies (APPSC key)
  [102:6:0.2] Social | R.S. Sharma: "Indian feudalism", feudal levies, sub-infeudation, closed village economy; caste limited fighting to few groups
[102:6:1] - Debate: historians stress military organisation over religious zeal.
