=== Muhammad Ghori and the Battles of Tarain
| Battle | Year | Opponent | Victor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kayadara / Kasahrada**, near **Mount Abu** (Gujarat campaign) | **1178** | **Mularaja II** (Bhimdev II), Chaulukya/Solanki; regent mother **Naika Devi** | **Gujarat** – Ghori's **first defeat** |
| **First Tarain** (near **Karnal, Haryana**) | **1191** | **Prithviraj Chauhan** (Prithviraja III) | **Prithviraj** – Ghori wounded, barely escaped |
| **Second Tarain** | **1192** | Prithviraj Chauhan | **Ghori** – turning point; foundation of Turkish rule |
| **Chandawar** (near **Firozabad, UP**, on the Yamuna) | **1194** | **Jayachandra (Jaichand)**, Gahadavala of **Kannauj** | **Ghori** (army under Aibak) |
- **Ghori:** Shihabuddin (Muizzuddin) **Muhammad Ghori**, Ghurid, took Ghazni 1173; entered India by the **Gomal Pass**; aimed at **conquest and permanent occupation**, unlike Mahmud.
- **Route:** **Multan (1175, first invasion)** → Peshawar (c. 1179) → Sindh (c. 1182) → Punjab and Lahore (1186, from Khusru Malik, last Ghaznavid).
- **First defeat (1178):** by **Mularaja II / Bhimdev II** of Gujarat (books also write "Bhima II", "Baghel Bhim") – **not Prithviraj**, who beat him only in 1191.
- **Cause of Tarain I:** Ghori's attack on **Tabarhinda (Bhatinda)**; Prithviraj led a confederacy of Rajput princes.
- **Tarain II (1192):** Ghori returned with 1,20,000 men; **Jaichand refused to help** Prithviraj; Turkish **cavalry speed, organisation and mounted archery** beat the larger Rajput army. It **laid the foundation of Muslim (Turkish) rule** – not the Panipat battles.
- **1192:** the Central Asian ruler who **conquered North India in 1192** = **Shihabuddin Muhammad Ghori** – not Mahmud (raider), Jalaluddin Mangbarni or Genghis Khan.
- **Coins:** Ghori's coins bear **Goddess Lakshmi** on one side and the **Kalma in Arabic** on the other.
- **Aibak:** Ghori's slave, **Master of Stables**; his representative in India after 1192; on Ghori's death (1206, near Jhelum) became the **first Sultan**, capital **Lahore** – the Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526).
# Prithviraj Chauhan
- **Prithviraj Chauhan = Prithviraja III** (Rai Pithora), Chahamana of **Ajmer and Delhi** (c. 1177-1192) – not Prithviraja I or II.
- Defeated Chandela **Paramardi**, Chaulukya Bhima II and Gahadavala Jaichand; after 1192 reinstated as a vassal, later executed; son Govindaraja IV moved to **Ranthambore**.
- **"The last Sun of Indian glory"** – Prithviraj Chauhan (B.N. Luniya).

=== Arab Conquest of Sindh (712)
- **First Muslim invaders** of India: the **Arabs**, under **Muhammad bin Qasim**, Sindh, **712** – before the Ghaznavids and Ghurids (not Persians).
- **First successful Muslim invader:** **Muhammad bin Qasim** – not Mahmud of Ghazni, Ghori or Aibak.
- **Year:** the expedition left in 711 and took Debal and Sindh in **712**; so for "674 / 1013 / 711" the answer is **none of these** (712).
- **Qasim:** an **Arab** (not Turk, Mongol or Turk-Afghan); nephew and son-in-law of **Al-Hajjaj bin Yusuf**, the Umayyad governor of Iraq; sent by **Caliph al-Walid** at the age of **17**.
- **Ruler defeated:** **Raja Dahir** (Dahar), killed; capital **Alor (Aror)** – not Brahmanabad, which was only one of the places taken.
- **Places taken:** **Debal, Nirun, Sehwan, Brahmanabad, Multan** – not Somnath (Mahmud, 1025).
- **First contact with Islam:** earlier, through **Arab traders on the Malabar coast** (7th century) – the 712 conquest was the first political contact.
# Chachnama
- Arabic chronicle of the Arab conquest of **Sindh**; original lost; the existing **13th-century Persian translation** is by **Muhammad Ali bin Abu Bakr Kufi** – not Aufi or Shams-i-Siraj.
- Records Dahir's widowed queen fighting on and then immolating herself with the other women.
- Other sources: **Futuh al-Buldan** (Al-Biladuri, 9th c.), Muruj-ul-Zehab (Al-Masudi, 10th c.).
# Causes and Effects
| Factor | Fact |
|---|---|
| Causes | Arab maritime interests, expansion, **piracy against Arab ships** |
| Religion | No mass Islamisation; Hindus and Buddhists given **dhimmi** status |
| Limited reach | Confined to **Sindh and Multan** by the Thar and Aravallis, strong Pratiharas, Rashtrakutas, Palas, weak Arab resources |
| Knowledge transfer | Indian **decimal and place-value** maths, astronomy, medicine → Islamic world → Europe |
- **Nagabhata I (Pratihara)** drove back the Arabs; Arab writers said the **Pratiharas** had the **strongest cavalry** in India.

=== Mahmud of Ghazni and the Ghaznavids
| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| **Alptigin** (Alp-Tegin) | Turkish slave-general of the **Samanids**; seized **Ghazni** (962) – a principality still under the Samanids |
| **Sabuktigin** (977-997) | **Founder of the Ghaznavid dynasty** proper ("real founder") |
| **Mahmud** (998-1030) | The raider: **17 raids**, 1000-1027 |
- **Founder (PYQ):** **Sabuktigin** founded the Ghaznavid dynasty (PMF; Britannica); some guide books key Alptigin, who only seized Ghazni.
- **Motive:** **plunder of temple wealth** to fund his Central Asian wars – he **did not seek a permanent empire** (so for "17 invasions because he wanted a permanent empire": A true, R false).
- **Raids:** Hindu Shahi **Jayapala** beaten 1001; **Battle of Waihind (1008-09)** – **Anandapala** (Hindu Shahi) vs Mahmud; **Nagarkot (Kangra) 1009**; **Thanesar** (Chakraswamin temple) **1014**; **Mathura** and **Kanauj 1018** (the Pratihara king submitted); **Somnath 1025-26**.
- **Somnath (1025):** raided under Solanki **Bhima I (Bhimadeva I)**, who fled to Kanthkot and **reconstructed the temple** after Mahmud left; Kumarapala later rebuilt it in stone.
- **Chandelas:** strongest resistance; **Vidyadhara** faced Mahmud (1019, 1022) and was **not defeated** (peace made) – Dhanga died c. 1002 and **Jayashakti** (9th c.) lived long before Mahmud.
- **Coins:** Mahmud issued **silver coins** with **Arabic** on one side and a **Sanskrit legend (Devanagari)** on the other.
- **Title:** Yamini-ud-Daulah from the Abbasid caliph – hence the **Yamini dynasty**.
- **Effects:** Hindu Shahi barrier destroyed; Punjab and the north-west frontier opened. After Mahmud the Ghaznavids declined under **Seljuk** pressure; Ghori ended the dynasty (1186).
- **Latest (January 2026):** **Somnath Swabhiman Parv** – 1,000 years since Mahmud's attack on Somnath.

=== Scholars of Mahmud's Court – Al-Biruni, Firdausi, Utbi
| Scholar | Work / role |
|---|---|
| **Al-Biruni** | **Kitab-ul-Hind (Tahqiq-i-Hind)**; Kitab Patanjal (Arabic Yoga-Sutra) |
| **Utbi** | **Court historian**; Kitab-ul-Yamini (Tarikh-i-Yamini) |
| **Firdausi** ("Homer of the East") | **Shahnama** |
| **Baihaqi** | Tarikh-i-Subuktigin |
| **Unsuri** | Poet laureate (Malik-ush-Shuara) |
| Uzari, Asjadi, Farrukhi | Persian court poets |
- **Court historian of Mahmud:** **Utbi** – not Hasan Nizami (Aibak; Taj-ul-Maasir), Firdausi (poet) or Chand Bardai (Prithviraj).
- **Shahnama:** by **Firdausi** at **Mahmud Ghaznavi's** court (one PYQ misspells him "Farista").
- **Kitab-ul-Hind / Tahqiq-i-Hind:** **Al-Biruni** – not Hasan Nizami, Minhaj-us-Siraj or Shams-i-Siraj Afif.
- **When he came:** with Mahmud, in the **11th century AD**; an objective account of 11th-century India.
- **Historian who came with Mahmud:** **Al-Biruni** – Ferishta (16th c.), Afif (Firoz Tughlaq) and Ibn Battuta (Muhammad Tughlaq) are later.
# Al-Biruni
- Born **973** in **Khwarizm (Khiva)**; Sanskrit scholar, mathematician (trigonometry), astronomer; "father of Indian historical writing" (Indology).
- Kitab-ul-Hind translated into English by **Edward Sachau**.
- **"Not correct" statement (PYQ):** "He was a **secular** author" – he wrote as a Muslim scholar and has no word of sympathy for the Somnath victims.

=== Causes of Turkish Success; Invasions in Order
| Factor | Point |
|---|---|
| Political | **No political unity** in North India; rival Rajput states after the **Pratihara** collapse |
| Military | **Superior war technique**: mobile cavalry, **mounted archers**, organisation vs elephant-reliant armies |
| Social | R.S. Sharma: "Indian feudalism" – feudal levies, sub-infeudation, closed village economy; caste limited fighting to few groups |
- **A-R (UPPCS 2018, 2024):** A "Turkish invasions were successful" and R "no political unity in North India" – **both true, R explains A**.
- **Why Ghori won (APPSC G1):** **superior war technology / military organisation of the Turks** – not a larger army (Prithviraj's was larger), local support or treachery.
| Order | Invader | Years |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Muhammad bin Qasim | 712 |
| 2 | **Mahmud Ghaznavi** | 1000-1027 |
| 3 | **Muhammad Ghori** | 1175-1206 |
| 4 | **Genghis Khan** | 1221 (Indus, chasing Jalaluddin Mangbarni) |
| 5 | **Timur** | 1398 |
- **Chronology:** Mahmud → Ghori → Genghis Khan → Timur (**2, 3, 1, 4**).

=== Bakhtiyar Khalji – Bihar and Bengal
- **Ikhtiyaruddin Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji:** slave-general of Ghori (under Aibak); **conquered Bihar and Bengal (1193-1203)** – not Aibak, Iltutmish or Yaldauz.
- **Real founder of Turk rule in Bihar.**
- Sacked **Nalanda**, **Vikramashila** and **Odantapuri** – Nalanda was destroyed by Bakhtiyar, not by Mahmud, Aibak or the later Sultans.
- Entered **Nadiya** (1204), drove **Lakshmana Sena** to East Bengal; ruled from **Lakhnauti**; failed Tibet expedition 1205; killed 1206.
