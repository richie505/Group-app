=== NOTES 5:61 | Nuclear Energy – Three-stage Programme, Reactor Types, Phwr/fbr/ahwr, Thorium Strategy
## SUB 0 | Nucleus, Isotopes and Nuclear Fuels
[61:0:0] # Nucleus Basics
[61:0:1] - Nucleus = **protons + neutrons**; **mass number** = protons + neutrons.
[61:0:2] - **Pu-242** (Z = 94) has **148 neutrons**.
[61:0:3] - Hydrogen isotopes: **protium** (no neutron), **deuterium** (1 neutron, stable, heavy hydrogen), **tritium** (2 neutrons). They are isotopes of hydrogen, not carbon.
[61:0:4] - **Tritium**: made by hitting **lithium** with neutrons.
[61:0:5] - **Heavy water (D₂O)**: deuterium takes the place of hydrogen. **H. C. Urey** discovered deuterium and heavy water (Nobel Chemistry **1934**).
[61:0:6] # Isotopes, Isobars, Isotones
[61:0:7] TABLE head: Term | Same | Different | Example
  [61:0:7.0] Isotope | Atomic number (protons) | Mass number (neutrons) | ¹H, ²H, ³H; ¹⁶O, ¹⁷O, ¹⁸O
  [61:0:7.1] Isobar | Mass number | Atomic number | ³H and ³He
  [61:0:7.2] Isotone (isoneutronic) | Number of neutrons | Atomic and mass numbers | ³H and ⁴He (2 each); ¹⁴C, ¹⁵N, ¹⁶O (8 each)
[61:0:8] # Fissile vs Fertile
[61:0:9] TABLE head: Isotope | Type | Key fact
  [61:0:9.0] U-235 | Fissile | Only 0.72% of natural uranium
  [61:0:9.1] U-238 | Fertile | 99.28% of natural uranium; becomes Pu-239
  [61:0:9.2] Pu-239 | Fissile | Not natural; bred from U-238
  [61:0:9.3] Th-232 | Fertile | Th-232 + neutron → fissile U-233 (in FBR blankets)
  [61:0:9.4] U-233 | Fissile | Fuel of Stage III and of KAMINI
[61:0:10] - India's PHWRs use **natural uranium**.
[61:0:11] - **Enrichment** = raising the share of **U-235** above the natural level by **isotope separation** (not U-238). Light water reactors need **3–5%** U-235.
[61:0:12] # What is NOT a Nuclear Fuel
[61:0:13] - Moderators, coolants and absorbers: **graphite, heavy water, sodium, boron, cadmium, zirconium**.
[61:0:14] - Also not fuels: **helium** (inert gas), **lead, calcium**, **chromium** (not linked to atomic power). **Beryllium oxide** = a moderator.
[61:0:15] - **Zirconium (Z = 40)** is not radioactive; elements above Z 80 are mostly radioactive; **technetium (43)** and **promethium (61)** are radioactive.
[61:0:16] - **Uranium** = the metal used to produce electricity. Thorium is usable only by breeding to U-233.
[61:0:17] APPSC 2007 keyed "U-238" as the better fuel; U-238 is fertile, the fissile fuel made from it is Pu-239.
## SUB 1 | Fission vs Fusion and the Bombs
[61:1:0] # Comparison
[61:1:1] TABLE head: Point | Fission | Fusion
  [61:1:1.0] Process | Heavy nucleus splits (U-235 + neutron → barium + krypton + 2–3 neutrons) | Two light nuclei join
  [61:1:1.1] Condition | Neutron hit; critical mass | Very high temperature and pressure (thermonuclear)
  [61:1:1.2] Energy | Large | More per unit mass
  [61:1:1.3] Where | Power reactors, atom bomb | Sun and stars, hydrogen bomb
  [61:1:1.4] Power use | All present-day nuclear electricity | Still at R&D stage
[61:1:2] - **Chain reaction**: released neutrons split more U-235 nuclei; each fission releases more neutrons. **Criticality** = self-sustaining chain reaction.
[61:1:3] - **Critical mass** = minimum fissile mass that sustains a chain reaction.
[61:1:4] - **Reactor**: chain reaction **controlled** (moderators, control rods); **atom bomb**: uncontrolled.
[61:1:5] - Stars: **fusion** (hydrogen → helium), not fission.
[61:1:6] # Atomic vs Hydrogen Bomb
[61:1:7] TABLE head: Feature | Atom bomb | Hydrogen bomb
  [61:1:7.0] Principle | Uncontrolled fission of uranium/plutonium | Uncontrolled fusion of deuterium (and tritium) into helium, triggered by a fission bomb
  [61:1:7.1] Person | Otto Hahn (with F. Strassmann, 1939) – fission principle | Edward Teller – "father of the hydrogen bomb"; first US test November 1952, Eniwetok (Marshall Islands)
  [61:1:7.2] City | Bomb | Material
  [61:1:7.3] Hiroshima | Little Boy | U-235
  [61:1:7.4] Nagasaki (9 August 1945) | Fat Man | Pu-239
[61:1:8] APPSC 2019: "Breaking apart of a nucleus is called fusion" is INCORRECT – that is fission.
## SUB 2 | Radioactivity
[61:2:0] - Discovered by **Henri Becquerel (1896)**; a **nuclear** (not chemical) property; a radioactive substance emits **alpha, beta and gamma**.
[61:2:1] - Measured by the **Geiger (Geiger–Muller) counter**.
[61:2:2] TABLE head: Emission | Nature | Charge | Stopped by
  [61:2:2.0] Alpha | Helium nucleus (2p + 2n), mass ≈ helium nucleus | +2 | Paper or skin
  [61:2:2.1] Beta | Electron (negative beta) or positron (positive beta) | −1 / +1 | Few mm of aluminium
  [61:2:2.2] Gamma | EM radiation | Zero | Lead or concrete
[61:2:3] - Zero charge: **neutrino, neutron**. **Deuteron** is not a sub-atomic particle.
[61:2:4] - **Half-life** = time for half the nuclei to decay. Half-life 5 years → after 20 years **1/16** remains; half-life 10 days → **3/4** decays in 20 days.
[61:2:5] - Natural decay series: **uranium** U-238 → Pb-206; **thorium** Th-232 → Pb-208. **Neptunium series = artificial**.
[61:2:6] - **Xenon-135** = "nuclear poison" that absorbs neutrons.
[61:2:7] - **Actinium series:** a natural decay series that begins with **U-235** and ends in **Pb-207**.
## SUB 3 | Reactor Components and Reactor Types
[61:3:0] # Components
[61:3:1] TABLE head: Part | Function | Substances
  [61:3:1.0] Moderator | Slows neutrons so U-235 can fission | Heavy water (D₂O), light water, graphite, beryllium oxide
  [61:3:1.1] Coolant | Carries heat to make steam | Heavy water (PHWR), light water (LWR), liquid sodium (FBR)
  [61:3:1.2] Control rods | Absorb neutrons, control the rate | Boron, cadmium, silver, indium
[61:3:2] - Without control rods the chain reaction goes **out of bounds**; without a moderator a thermal reactor stops.
[61:3:3] - **Ordinary water** can act as both moderator and coolant.
[61:3:4] # Reactor Types
[61:3:5] TABLE head: Reactor | Fuel | Moderator | Coolant | Indian example
  [61:3:5.0] PHWR | Natural uranium | Heavy water | Heavy water | Stage I fleet
  [61:3:5.1] BWR | Enriched uranium | Light water | Light water (boils in core) | Tarapur
  [61:3:5.2] PWR / VVER | Low-enriched uranium | Light water | Pressurised light water | Kudankulam
  [61:3:5.3] FBR | MOX (Pu-239 + U-238) | None | Liquid sodium | PFBR, Kalpakkam
  [61:3:5.4] AHWR | Th-232 + U-233/Pu | Heavy water | Boiling light water | BARC design
[61:3:6] - **LWRs**: light water as moderator and coolant; **over 85%** of world capacity; India now prioritises LWRs for fast capacity addition.
[61:3:7] - **IAEA safeguards** cover reactors using **imported uranium**, not Indian-supplied ones.
## SUB 4 | Homi Bhabha and the Three-stage Programme
[61:4:0] - **Homi J. Bhabha** – "Father of the Indian nuclear programme"; 1950s plan to use **abundant thorium** against **scarce uranium**; aim: **self-reliance via a closed fuel cycle**.
[61:4:1] TABLE head: Stage | Reactor | Fuel | Output
  [61:4:1.0] I | PHWR (D₂O moderator + coolant) | Natural uranium | Power + Pu-239 from spent fuel
  [61:4:1.1] II | FBR (liquid sodium, no moderator) | MOX: Pu-239 + U-238 | Breeds more Pu-239; Th-232 blanket → U-233
  [61:4:1.2] III | AHWR / thorium reactors | Th-232 + U-233 | Large-scale thorium use
[61:4:2] # Advanced Heavy Water Reactor (AHWR)
[61:4:3] - **300 MWe** (920 MWth), thorium-based; designed by **BARC**; **not yet built or running**.
[61:4:4] - Heavy-water moderated, **boiling light-water** coolant; fuel pins of (Th-U-233) MOX and (Th-Pu) MOX.
[61:4:5] - Main purpose: **large-scale thorium use**; a **bridge from Stage I to Stage III** without passing through Stage II.
[61:4:6] APPSC G2 2025 (A/R): A "Dhruva is India's largest research reactor" – TRUE. On R (AHWR and plutonium-bearing spent fuel) the official key took R as true but NOT the correct explanation of A. The AHWR's goal is thorium use.
[61:4:7] # Latest Developments
[61:4:8] - **6 April 2026**: PFBR first criticality = India enters **Stage II**.
[61:4:9] - **May 2026**: **ANEEL fuel** (thorium + **HALEU**, US firm **Clean Core Thorium Energy**) usable in existing PHWRs; could hasten Stage III.
[61:4:10] - **Stage III hurdles**: thin fissile inventory, 10–12-year builds, protests – not lack of thorium.
## SUB 5 | Fast Breeder Reactors
[61:5:0] # Breeding Principle
[61:5:1] - Core surrounded by a **U-238 blanket**; **fast neutrons** turn it into Pu-239 → **more fissile material than it burns** (electricity by fission).
[61:5:2] - **No moderator**; **liquid sodium** coolant; fuel MOX from reprocessed Stage I spent fuel.
[61:5:3] # PFBR, Kalpakkam (Tamil Nadu)
[61:5:4] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [61:5:4.0] Capacity | 500 MWe (1,250 MWt); sodium-cooled, pool type
  [61:5:4.1] Designed by | IGCAR
  [61:5:4.2] Built and commissioned by | BHAVINI (PSU, 2003, DAE)
  [61:5:4.3] First criticality | 6 April 2026
  [61:5:4.4] Cost | Cost doubled; criticality 16+ years late
[61:5:5] - India = **2nd after Russia** with a commercial-scale FBR; PFBR = **first indigenous FBR**.
[61:5:6] - Next: 2 x 500 MWe FBRs at Kalpakkam.
[61:5:7] # FBTR (Fast Breeder Test Reactor)
[61:5:8] - At **IGCAR, Kalpakkam**; coolant **liquid sodium**; fuel indigenous **plutonium-uranium mixed carbide**.
[61:5:9] - Design based on French reactor **Rapsodie**; critical **18 October 1985**.
[61:5:10] - IGCAR **2005** milestone: reprocessing FBTR's mixed-carbide fuel.
[61:5:11] APPSC G1 2024: PFBR was built by BHAVINI, not IGCAR (IGCAR designed it).

## SUB 7 | Thorium, Monazite and Uranium Mines
[61:7:0] - India: about **25%** of world thorium vs about **2%** of uranium; **3rd-largest** thorium reserve.
[61:7:1] - Thorium vs uranium: more abundant, more energy per unit mass mined, less harmful waste.
[61:7:2] - **Monazite** = principal thorium ore; also **rare earths** (lanthanum, cerium), phosphate, a little uranium; resources **13.15 million tonnes** (DAE).
[61:7:3] - Beach placer sands of **Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh**, Maharashtra, West Bengal – **not the entire coast**.
[61:7:4] - Only government bodies (**IREL**) may process and export monazite.
[61:7:5] TABLE head: Mine | Location | Fact

  [61:7:5.1] Tummalapalle | YSR Kadapa, AP | UCIL mine + processing plant
[61:7:6] - About **80%** of imported uranium comes from **Kazakhstan** (Dec 2025).
## SUB 8 | Fusion Research
[61:8:0] - **ITER** (International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor): **Cadarache, southern France**; **tokamak**; world's largest **magnetic confinement** experiment.
[61:8:1] - Members: **EU + six**: India, China, Japan, South Korea, Russia, USA. **Canada is NOT a member**. India joined **2005**; supplies the **cryostat**.
[61:8:2] - **500 MW out from 50 MW in** (~10 times). Advantage for India: can build fusion power reactors.
[61:8:3] - **Institute for Plasma Research, Gandhinagar** (not BARC): **Aditya** (first Indian tokamak) and **SST-1** (Steady State Superconducting Tokamak).
[61:8:4] - **December 2022**: first fusion **ignition** / net energy gain – **National Ignition Facility**, **Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory**, USA (US DOE/NNSA), using **lasers**.
