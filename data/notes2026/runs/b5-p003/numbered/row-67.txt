## SUB 0 | Bioethanol – Feedstocks, Gasohol, Power Alcohol and Global Producers
[0:0] # Feedstocks and process
[0:1] - **Bioethanol:** made by **fermentation** of sugar or starch by yeast **Saccharomyces cerevisiae** (baker's yeast); can be mixed with petrol to improve combustion and lower emissions.
[0:2] TABLE head: Feedstock | Fact
  [0:2.0] Sugarcane (juice, molasses) | India's main ethanol source; molasses = excellent raw material for power alcohol
  [0:2.1] Maize (corn) | Starch-rich energy crop for ethanol; principal US feedstock
  [0:2.2] Damaged grains, cassava, rotten potatoes, sugar beet | Allowed under the National Policy on Biofuels
  [0:2.3] Jatropha, Pongamia, sunflower | Oilseeds → biodiesel, not ethanol
[0:3] - Potato, rice and wheat are not the classic "ethanol crop" answer; sugarcane (UPSC 2009) and maize (UPSC 2010) are.
[0:4] # Terms
[0:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:5.0] Gasohol | Petrol (gasoline) + ethanol, typically ~10% ethanol
  [0:5.1] Power alcohol | Fuel-grade (anhydrous) ethanol, mainly from molasses (sugar-industry by-product)
  [0:5.2] Rectified spirit | Ethanol with about 5% water (~95% ethanol)
  [0:5.3] Absolute alcohol | ~100% ethanol
[0:6] # Global producers
[0:7] - **USA** (maize/corn) = world's **largest producer, consumer and exporter** of fuel ethanol / gasohol; **Brazil** (sugarcane) second; together ~80% of world ethanol – so in UPSC 2025 Statement I (Brazil > USA) is false, Statement II (corn vs sugarcane) is true.
[0:8] - **Brazil:** **mandatory ethanol blending since 1976** (~25% since 2007); regarded as the world's first sustainable biofuel economy.
[0:9] # Indian technology firsts
[0:10] - **CSIR-NIIST, Thiruvananthapuram** (2017): bioethanol from **discarded cotton stalks** using a wild yeast strain.
[0:11] - **IIT Kharagpur "Soil to Soil"** (May 2017): enzyme-based (no chemical pre-treatment) process giving **biofuel** from crop residues; leftover biomass returns to the soil as bio-fertiliser.
[0:12] - **TVS Apache RTR 200 Fi E100** (2019) = India's first **ethanol-powered motorcycle** (TVS Motor).
[0:13] > Most-asked: ethanol crop (sugarcane / maize), gasohol = petrol + ethanol, largest producer USA, Brazil mandate.

## SUB 1 | Biogas and Biomass Energy
[1:0] # Biomass energy
[1:1] - **Biomass energy** = solar energy stored by plants through **photosynthesis**; burned directly or converted to biogas/biofuels; renewable (unlike coal, natural gas, petroleum).
[1:2] - **Gobar gas = biomass energy** (from cattle dung) – not nuclear, geothermal or hydro.
[1:3] - Biomass from waste: agricultural waste (stubble, husk, manure, dairy waste), **food-processing residues, human sewage, biogenic municipal solid waste** (all three are biomass).
[1:4] - Burning biomass releases CO2 largely balanced by the CO2 it absorbed while growing (near carbon-neutral).
[1:5] # Biogas
[1:6] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [1:6.0] Composition | Methane ~50–75% + CO2 ~25–50% + traces (H2S, moisture); piped natural gas is 85–95% methane
  [1:6.1] Fuel gas | Methane – the burning component (cooking gas / gobar gas); not butane, propane, ethane
  [1:6.2] Process | Anaerobic digestion (biomethanation) / fermentation – in the absence of oxygen
  [1:6.3] Microbes | Methanogens (archaebacteria) produce methane
  [1:6.4] By-product | Slurry = good organic manure
[1:7] - **Composting** is **aerobic** decomposition, unlike biogas.
[1:8] - Pioneer: floating-drum **"Gramalakshmi"** plant of **Jashbhai Patel (1950s)**, spread by **KVIC**.
[1:9] - **CBG (Compressed Biogas)** = purified, compressed biogas, chemically identical to **CNG**.
[1:10] - **India's first Bio-CNG plant from agricultural residue: Pirangut, Pune** (Primove Engineering), inaugurated **2016** by Nitin Gadkari and Manohar Parrikar.
[1:11] - **Bagasse cogeneration:** sugar mills burn bagasse → **steam and power together**.
[1:12] # Gasification and microbial fuel cells
[1:13] - **Biomass gasification:** heating with **limited (controlled) oxygen** → syngas (**CO, H2, CO2**).
[1:14] - **Producer gas** = CO + H2 + CO2 + **methane**.
[1:15] - **Microbial Fuel Cell (MFC):** microbes act as **catalysts**, oxidise organic matter and pass electrons to an electrode; cleans wastewater and makes electricity together (no burning).
[1:16] > Biogas = methane + CO2, anaerobic, methanogens – asked about 10 times.
[1:17] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 10th ed. p.350-351 (Ch22.6); Rocket Environment p.60-62; NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 5 p.32; web: gktoday.in (first Bio-CNG plant, Pune); app notes 5:67 sub 1
## SUB 2 | Geothermal Energy
[2:0] - **Source:** heat stored in the **Earth's interior**, produced mostly by **decay of radioactive** elements (uranium, potassium) and original planetary heat; surface signs: **hot springs, geysers, volcanoes**.
[2:1] - Not ultimately solar: **geothermal and nuclear** energy (biomass, wind, fossil fuels are stored solar energy).
[2:2] - Gives **24x7 baseload** power – independent of sunlight or wind.
[2:3] - **India's potential:** about **10,600 MW**; GSI identified ~340–381 hot springs.
[2:4] - **Provinces:** Himalaya, Naga-Lushai, Andaman-Nicobar; **Cambay**, **Son-Narmada-Tapi (SONATA)**, **West Coast**, Damodar, **Mahanadi**, **Godavari valley** – none in the **Ganga Delta / Indo-Gangetic Plain**.
[2:5] TABLE head: Field | State / UT
  [2:5.0] Puga Valley, Chumathang | Ladakh – most promising / highest potential (GSI)
  [2:5.1] Tattapani | Chhattisgarh
  [2:5.2] Manikaran | Himachal Pradesh
  [2:5.3] Tapovan | Uttarakhand
  [2:5.4] Surajkund | Jharkhand
  [2:5.5] Manuguru (old Khammam district), Godavari valley | Telangana (earlier Andhra Pradesh)
[2:6] - **India's first geothermal power project: Puga, Ladakh** (**ONGC**); described as the world's highest geothermal project.
[2:7] - **World:** first successful tapping of underground heat **Boise, Idaho, USA (1890)** – hot-water pipe network for heating; first geothermal **power** at **Larderello, Italy (1904)**.
[2:8] - **National Policy on Geothermal Energy** – India's first, **MNRE, September 2025**; **five pilot projects**.
[2:9] - **EGS** (Enhanced Geothermal Systems) = artificially stimulated reservoirs. Challenges: high drilling and exploration cost, remote sites, transmission, gas leakage, subsidence.

## SUB 3 | Biodiesel – Jatropha, Pongamia and RUCO
[3:0] TABLE head: Crop | Fact
  [3:0.0] Jatropha curcas (ratanjyot) | The biodiesel plant; non-edible seeds ~40% oil; grows on wasteland, little water
  [3:0.1] Pongamia pinnata (Karanja) | Alternative biodiesel plant; oil rich in oleic acid
  [3:0.2] Mahua, neem, simarouba | Other non-edible biodiesel sources
[3:1] - Not biodiesel plants: guggal, rosha grass, java grass, isabgol, belladonna, senna, cotton, sugarcane, potato.
[3:2] - **Biodiesel** = methyl/ethyl esters of fatty acids by **transesterification** of vegetable oils/animal fats with methanol or ethanol.
[3:3] - **India's first biodiesel plant: Kakinada, Andhra Pradesh** (early 2000s).
[3:4] - **Palm stearin oil** = feedstock of about **94%** of India's biodiesel (mostly imported), not jatropha.
[3:5] - **RUCO** (Repurpose Used Cooking Oil, **FSSAI**) = collection and conversion of **used cooking oil into biodiesel**.
[3:6] - Blend still **below 0.5%**; target **5% (B5) by 2030**.
[3:7] - **April 2023:** Belgian firm **VITO** launched a 2-year biodiesel project at **Ayodhya**.

## SUB 4 | Tidal, Wave, OTEC and Desalination
[4:0] TABLE head: Site | State | Fact
  [4:0.0] Gulf of Khambhat (Cambay) | Gujarat | Tidal range 5–10 m; ~7,000 MW – largest / best tidal site
  [4:0.1] Gulf of Kutch (Hanthal Creek) | Gujarat | Tidal range 5–8 m; major project proposed
  [4:0.2] Sundarbans – Durgaduani Creek | West Bengal | ~5 m; 3 MW pilot
[4:1] - Criterion: tidal range ~**5 m or more** in a narrow gulf/estuary. **Gulf of Mannar** is not a tidal site (offshore wind zone); Gulf of Aden / Panay are not Indian.
[4:2] - **Ocean energy potential (MNRE):** tidal about **12,455 MW** (mainly Khambhat and Kutch); wave about **40,000 MW**.
[4:3] - **First wave plant: Vizhinjam, Kerala (1991)**. **Sindhuja-I** ocean wave energy converter – **IIT Madras** (tested off Tuticorin).
[4:4] - **OTEC = Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion:** warm surface vs cold deep water (~1,000 m), ~**20°C** gap, tropics; sites **Lakshadweep, A&N**.
[4:5] - **NIOT, Chennai (Ministry of Earth Sciences)** = lead ocean-energy body; India's first desalination plant **Kavaratti, Lakshadweep (2005)**, LTTD, **1 lakh L/day**.
[4:6] - **Desalination** (removing salts from seawater) prepares large quantities of drinking water.

## SUB 5 | Aviation Biofuels and Sustainable Aviation Fuel
[5:0] - **First biofuel flight in India:** **SpiceJet**, **Dehradun–Delhi, 27 August 2018**, Bombardier Q-400; one engine 25% bio-jet + 75% ATF; jatropha bio-jet from **CSIR-IIP, Dehradun**.
[5:1] - **SAF** = **drop-in** synthesised hydrocarbons (not direct ethanol), blendable up to **50%** with jet fuel.
[5:2] - **SAF feedstocks:** **agricultural residues, corn grain, wastewater treatment sludge, wood-mill waste** – all four (UPSC 2024); also oilseeds, algae, MSW.
[5:3] - **SAF targets** (international flights): **1% by 2027, 2% by 2028, 5% by 2030**.
[5:4] - **IOCL Panipat** = first Indian ISCC CORSIA-certified SAF producer (Aug 2025). **BioE3 Policy 2024** backs bio-jet fuel.
[5:5] - **Global Biofuels Alliance:** launched **9 September 2023**, **G20 Summit, New Delhi**; core founders **India, USA, Brazil**; Secretariat **New Delhi**.

## SUB 7 | National Policy on Biofuels and Ethanol Blending
[7:0] - **NPB 2018** – aim: cut import dependence; targets **E20 + B5 by 2030**; categories: **basic** (1G bioethanol, biodiesel) and **advanced** (2G ethanol, drop-in fuels from MSW, 3G, **bio-CNG**); does not discourage advanced biofuels.
[7:1] - **Ethanol feedstock widened beyond molasses:** sugarcane juice, B-molasses, **cassava, damaged wheat, rotten potatoes, sugar beet**, maize, surplus FCI rice, agri residues – **not groundnut seeds or horse gram** (UPSC 2020: 1, 2, 5, 6).
[7:2] - **Surplus food grains** need **National Biofuel Coordination Committee (NBCC)** approval; NBCC chaired by the **Minister of Petroleum and Natural Gas** (approved surplus FCI rice Apr 2020, maize Nov 2020).
[7:3] - **NPB 2022 amendment:** E20 advanced to **ESY 2025-26**; more feedstocks; SEZ/EOU production; new NBCC members may be added.
[7:4] - Enablers: **Motor Vehicles Act Section 52** (engine conversion); **100% FDI automatic** for biofuel technology (domestic use).
[7:5] TABLE head: Ethanol Blending Programme (MoPNG) | Date
  [7:5.0] E5 in 9 States + 4 UTs | 1 January 2003
  [7:5.1] E10 achieved | June 2022
  [7:5.2] PM's World Environment Day pledge: E20 by 2025 | 5 June 2021
  [7:5.3] E20 petrol mandatory (min RON 95) | 1 April 2026
  [7:5.4] BIS IS 19850:2026 (E22–E30) | May 2026
  [7:5.5] Next goal E30 | 2030
[7:6] - EBP is run by **MoPNG**; OMC ethanol prices: **maize > molasses > rice**; maize expansion pulled area from **pulses** and oilseeds.

[9:0] - **India's first waste-to-energy plant: Timarpur, Delhi (1987)**; later Okhla, Ghazipur, Narela-Bawana (Delhi).
[9:1] TABLE head: Route | Principle

## SUB 10 | Renewable Classification – What Counts as Renewable
[10:0] - **MNRE renewables:** solar, wind, **bio-power**, **small hydro** and **large hydro** (renewable **since 2019**); geothermal, tidal, OTEC under development.
[10:1] - **Nuclear** = non-fossil but **not renewable** (uranium is exhaustible).
[10:2] - **Renewable resource:** biomass, solar, wind, hydro, tidal; **coal, natural gas, petroleum** are non-renewable.
[10:3] - **Not derived from the Sun:** **nuclear** and **geothermal** (UPPCS 2022 – both are correct; the key gives nuclear); biomass and wind are stored solar energy.
