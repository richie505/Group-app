## SUB 0 | Remote Sensing – Definition, Components and Platforms
[0:0] - **Remote sensing:** gathering information about an object **without physical contact**, by measuring **reflected and emitted electromagnetic radiation** from a distance (satellite, aircraft, drone); it uses **electromagnetic waves**, not sonar or electric waves.
[0:1] # Components (stages)
[0:2] - 1. Energy source / illumination → 2. Radiation through the atmosphere → 3. **Interaction with the target** → 4. Recording by the sensor → 5. **Transmission, reception and processing** → 6. Interpretation and analysis → 7. Application.
[0:3] - "Interaction of Energy with **Minimisation**" is **not** a component (APPSC 2022).
[0:4] # Disaster uses and platforms
[0:5] - Disaster applications: **glacial lakes**, **wildfires**, **wind/cyclone tracking**, floods, landslides – "hills management" is not one.
[0:6] - **UAV (drone)** is best for **detailed, localised** damage assessment; satellites for wide areas.
[0:7] - **Infrared (thermal) cameras** detect people trapped under **rubble** after an earthquake by their body heat.
[0:8] - **GEOSS = Global Earth Observation System of Systems** (built by the Group on Earth Observations, GEO).
[0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 2 p.5; PMF S&T Part 1 p.80-81; app notes 5:39 sub 0
## SUB 1 | Electromagnetic Spectrum, Sensors and Bands
[1:0] # Spectrum order
[1:1] - Wavelength falls: **radio (incl. microwave) → infrared → visible (red … violet) → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays**.
[1:2] - **Colour** of light is set by its **wavelength**; in visible light **red** is longest and **violet** shortest.
[1:3] - **Infrared** is longer than red; gamma rays shortest of all.
[1:4] - **X-rays** strongly darken (expose) **photographic plates and film** – the basis of radiography.
[1:5] # Passive vs active sensors
[1:6] TABLE head: Type | Energy | Examples
  [1:6.0] Passive | Reflected sunlight or emitted heat | Cameras (visible, infrared, multispectral), radiometers, MODIS
  [1:6.1] Active | Sends its own pulse, reads the echo | Radar/SAR (microwave), laser / LiDAR, altimeters
[1:7] - **Laser** is the active sensor in a list of cameras (APPSC 2023).
[1:8] - **LiDAR = Light Detection And Ranging**: pulsed laser measures distances → elevation, 3D terrain, canopy height. **Airborne Laser Terrain Mapping (ALTM)** is airborne LiDAR: a fast, accurate, cost-effective 3D picture of the ground, used mainly for mapping and survey.
[1:9] - Laser uses: LASIK, LiDAR, holography – **MRI is not** a laser use (it uses magnets and radio waves).
[1:10] # Which band for what
[1:11] TABLE head: Band | Use
  [1:11.0] Optical (visible/NIR) | Reflected light; needs daylight and clear sky
  [1:11.1] Thermal infrared | Emitted heat → volcanic eruptions, forest fires, urban heat
  [1:11.2] Microwave (radio region) – SAR | Penetrates clouds and darkness → all-weather imaging of cloud-covered disaster areas
  [1:11.3] Hyperspectral | Hundreds of narrow bands → materials and chemicals
[1:12] > ⚠ Cloud-covered disaster area: the answer is the **radio/microwave** band; visible light cannot pass through cloud (the APPSC 2021 key "Visible" is a factual error).
[1:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 2 p.5; PMF S&T Part 1 p.80-81, 94 (RISAT all-weather), p.69 (active sensors)
## SUB 2 | Resolution and Remote-sensing Techniques
[2:0] - Four resolutions: **spatial** (smallest object seen), **spectral** (number and width of bands), **temporal** (**how often** a satellite revisits the same place), **radiometric** (sensitivity to small energy differences). "**Chemical**" resolution does not exist (APPSC 2025).
[2:1] TABLE head: Technique | Maps
  [2:1.0] InSAR | Ground deformation – subsidence, earthquake/volcano uplift
  [2:1.1] LiDAR | Elevation, 3D terrain, canopy height
  [2:1.2] NDVI | Vegetation health (chlorophyll) from red vs near-infrared reflectance
  [2:1.3] Thermal infrared | Land surface temperature, fires, volcanic heat
  [2:1.4] Hyperspectral | Materials and chemicals
[2:2] - Satellite data estimate **chlorophyll**, **greenhouse-gas emissions from rice paddies** and **land surface temperature** – all three (UPSC 2019).
[2:3] - **MODIS = Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectro-radiometer** (NASA Terra/Aqua): **52,785 forest fires** detected Nov 2020 – Jun 2021.
[2:4] - **Tectonic plate movement** is monitored by **VLBI, SLR and GPS** – not flood or cyclone mapping.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 1 p.106-110; UPSC 2019 key; app notes 5:39 sub 1-2
## SUB 3 | Orbits for Earth Observation and Communication
[3:0] TABLE head: Orbit | Altitude | Use
  [3:0.0] LEO (Low Earth Orbit) | 200–2,000 km | Earth observation, ISS
  [3:0.1] SSPO (sun-synchronous polar) | ~500–900 km | Most EO satellites (IRS, Cartosat, NISAR)
  [3:0.2] MEO | 2,000–35,786 km | Navigation (GPS)
  [3:0.3] GEO (geostationary) | 35,786 km (22,236 miles) over the equator | Communication, weather; appears fixed
  [3:0.4] GSO (geosynchronous, inclined) | Same height | Figure-8 track; NavIC's 4 inclined satellites
[3:1] - **Geostationary** = geosynchronous + **circular** + in the **plane of the equator**; also **Clarke's orbit**; period one sidereal day, **23 h 56 min 4 s**. The "22,236 km" statement is wrong (that figure is in miles) → UPSC 2011 key **1, 2 and 3 only**.
[3:2] - **Sun-synchronous:** passes each place at the **same local solar time** → even lighting.
[3:3] - A satellite does not fall because Earth's gravity **provides the centripetal acceleration** for its orbit (UPSC 2011).
[3:4] - LEO imagers = finer detail, periodic revisit; geosynchronous imagers = coarser detail, near-continuous viewing.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 1 p.65-66 (UPSC 2011 explanation); app notes 5:39 sub 3
## SUB 4 | IRS Programme – Origins and Firsts
[4:0] - IRS began **17 Mar 1988** with **IRS-1A**: India's **first operational remote-sensing satellite**, launched from **Baikonur** (USSR, Kazakhstan) on a Soviet **Vostok** rocket – not Sriharikota.
[4:1] - One of the **world's largest civilian remote-sensing constellations**; data via NRSC and **Bhuvan**.
[4:2] TABLE head: Satellite | Year | Fact
  [4:2.0] Bhaskara I | 1979 | First experimental EO satellite
  [4:2.1] Rohini RS-1 / RS-D1 | 1980 / 31 May 1981 | Satellites launched by SLV-3 (SLV-3 is a launch vehicle, not a satellite)
  [4:2.2] IRS-1A | 1988 | Baikonur, Vostok
  [4:2.3] IRS-1B / 1C | 1991 / 1995 | Baikonur, Soviet/Russian rockets
  [4:2.4] IRS-P2 | 1994 | First IRS launched successfully by PSLV (PSLV-D2)
  [4:2.5] IRS-1D | 1997 | PSLV-C1 – first operational PSLV
[4:3] - **IRS uses:** crop productivity, **groundwater**, **mineral exploration**, land use, forests, floods, drought – **not telecommunications or traffic studies** (UPSC 2015: 1, 2 and 3 only).
[4:4] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 1 p.106-108, 112 (UPSC 2015 explanation); NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 2 p.4, 6-7

## SUB 7 | NISAR and Recent EO Missions
[7:0] - **NISAR** (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar): joint **Earth-observation** mission, partnership signed **2014**; **NASA L-band + ISRO S-band** – world's first dual-frequency radar satellite; **12 m** reflector.
[7:1] - Launched **30 July 2025** on **GSLV-F16** from Sriharikota into a **sun-synchronous LEO** (about 747 km) – not a High Earth Orbit; now operational.
[7:2] > ⚠ Outdated stems: "to be launched in January 2024" / "set to launch in June 2025" – NISAR flew on 30 July 2025.
[7:3] - **EOS-05** (GISAT-1A): GSLV-F17, **4 Sep 2026**, 2,367 kg; visible to long-wave IR; floods, cyclones, fires, crops.

## SUB 8 | NRSC, Bhuvan and EO in the News; Andhra Pradesh
[8:0] - Data chain: ground station → processing at **NRSC, Hyderabad** (nodal) → users. Training: **IIRS** and **CSSTEAP**, both **Dehradun**.
[8:1] - **Bhuvan:** ISRO's (**NRSC**'s) web-based **geoportal** with **3-D imaging**, open to the public (UPSC 2010) – not a satellite or telescope. Other NRSC/ISRO platforms: **Bhoonidhi, MOSDAC, VEDAS**.
[8:2] - Uses: groundwater prospects, wastelands, **FASAL** crop forecasting, drought, NUIS, SIS-DP, **INFFRAS** (Indian Forest Fire Response and Assessment System).
[8:3] TABLE head: In news | Agency / satellite

  [8:3.1] Landslide Atlas of India | NRSC (2023)

  [8:3.3] Glacial lakes | CWC since 2011 with NRSC imagery; Glacial Lake Atlas 2023: 7,570 lakes; Himalayan lake area up ~9% 2011-2025
[8:4] - **AP:** **APSAC** (AP Space Applications Centre) is the state remote-sensing agency; **SDSC-SHAR** (Tirupati district) gives Bay of Bengal cyclone data to **APSDMA**.
