## SUB 0 | Fuel Gases – LPG, Natural Gas, CNG, LNG and Their Chemistry
[0:0] # What each gas is made of
[0:1] TABLE head: Fuel | Main constituents | Key facts
  [0:1.0] Natural gas | Mostly methane (CH₄), with some ethane, propane, CO₂, N₂ | Cleanest fossil fuel; burnt directly or piped
  [0:1.1] CNG – Compressed Natural Gas | Methane (80–90%) | Natural gas compressed to 200–250 kg/cm²; vehicles
  [0:1.2] PNG – Piped Natural Gas | 85–95% methane | Supplied to homes through city gas networks
  [0:1.3] LNG – Liquefied Natural Gas | Methane | Cooled to about −160°C; volume shrinks about 600 times; carried in special cryogenic tankers; lighter than air
  [0:1.4] LPG – Liquefied Petroleum Gas | Propane + butane (roughly 65% butane, 35% propane; a little ethane) | Refinery / gas by-product; liquefied under pressure; heavier than air
[0:2] - **LPG is NOT mainly methane** (IAS 2005: A false, R true). When only one gas is asked, **butane** is the chief constituent; the 3-gas option is **ethane, propane and butane**.
[0:3] - **LPG is odourless** in its natural state; **ethyl mercaptan (ethanethiol)** is added so that a **leak can be smelt** (the "rotten" stench).
[0:4] - **Butane (C₄H₁₀)** is the gas in **cigarette lighters**; it is a **Natural Gas Liquid (NGL)**.
[0:5] - **Propane** (an LPG gas) is a clean-burning fuel; coke, petrol and wax are not "clean energy" (NDA 2019).
[0:6] - **Methane** is a **greenhouse gas** (ethane, propane, butane are not counted as GHGs); in the air it oxidises to CO₂ in about a decade.
[0:7] - **Methane + air** (firedamp, 5–15% methane) causes most **explosions in coal mines**.
[0:8] # Other fuel facts asked
[0:9] TABLE head: Fuel / term | Fact
  [0:9.0] Water gas | CO + H₂ (producer gas = CO + H₂ + N₂)
  [0:9.1] Acetylene (ethyne) | Gas welding (oxy-acetylene flame)
  [0:9.2] Kerosene | Calorific value about the same as petrol (~45,000 kJ/kg); LPG ~55,000, methane/CNG ~50,000
  [0:9.3] Hydrocarbon order by molecular weight | Methane (16) < ethane (30) < propane (44) < butane (58)
  [0:9.4] Petroleum | A mixture of many hydrocarbons of different densities; its grade depends on their relative proportion (NDA 2016: both true, II does not explain I)
[0:10] - **LNG statements (IAS 2005):** only "LNG is liquefied under extreme cold for storage and transport" is right; India's first LNG terminal is **Dahej** (Gujarat, Petronet LNG, 2004), not Hassan; NGLs (ethane, propane, butane, natural gasoline) are separated from **natural gas**, not from LPG.
[0:11] > Exam angle: "main constituent of LPG" is asked a dozen times – butane (with propane); methane is natural gas / CNG.

## SUB 1 | Petroleum Geology, Basins and Oil Fields
[1:0] # Geology
[1:1] - **Coal, petroleum, natural gas** = conventional, **non-renewable** fossil fuels.
[1:2] - Oil is found in **Tertiary** sedimentary rocks; petroleum = 90–95% hydrocarbons.
[1:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:3.0] Source rock | Organic-rich shale where oil forms
  [1:3.1] Reservoir rock | Porous, permeable sandstone or fissured limestone
  [1:3.2] Cap rock | Impervious bed that seals the trap
  [1:3.3] Traps | Anticlines, faults, salt domes; commercial oil at the crest of an anticline
[1:4] - Layers by density from the top: **gas, oil, water**.
[1:5] - **Wet gas** = associated with oil; **dry gas** = non-associated; **sour gas** = rich in **H₂S**.
[1:6] - Conventional gas: permeable sandstone. Unconventional: impermeable sandstone, shale, coal seams – costly technology.

## SUB 2 | Shale Gas, Coal Bed Methane and Gas Hydrates
[2:0] TABLE head: Resource | Where held | India
  [2:0.0] Shale gas / oil | Fine-grained, low-permeability shale below 1,500 m; horizontal drilling + hydraulic fracturing (fracking) | Cambay basin largest; also KG, Cauvery, Gondwana (Damodar), Assam-Arakan, Indo-Gangetic; ONGC estimate 187.5 TCF in five basins
  [2:0.1] Coal bed methane (CBM) | Adsorbed in coal seams; released by pumping water out | Damodar valley – Raniganj (first commercial output), Jharia, Bokaro
  [2:0.2] Gas (methane) hydrates | Methane-water ice crystals under the seafloor and in Arctic tundra / permafrost | KG, Mahanadi, Andaman offshore (not Damodar)
[2:1] - **Hydraulic fracturing** is the technology that stimulates the flow of oil or gas and raises the recoverable volume.
[2:2] - **Guar gum** from the seeds of the **cluster bean** thickens fracking fluid and carries sand into the cracks – hence guar's new importance (UPSC 2014).
[2:3] - **Methane hydrate (UPSC 2019):** warming may release the methane; deposits lie under the seafloor and in Arctic tundra; atmospheric methane oxidises to CO₂ in a decade or two – **all three true**.
