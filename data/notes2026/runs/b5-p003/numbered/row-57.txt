## SUB 0 | Coal Types, Carbon Content and Fuel Quality
[0:0] TABLE head: Type | Carbon (NextGen/PMF) | Notes
  [0:0.0] Anthracite | 80–95% | Highest carbon, best quality; hardest, little smoke, highest calorific value; J&K (Kalakot), small quantity
  [0:0.1] Bituminous | 40–80% | Most widely used; includes the coking grade; Jharkhand, Odisha, WB, Chhattisgarh, MP
  [0:0.2] Sub-bituminous | – | Between bituminous and lignite; used in power plants
  [0:0.3] Lignite (brown coal) | 40–55% | High moisture; Neyveli (TN), Palana (Rajasthan), Lakhimpur (Assam)
  [0:0.4] Peat | below 40% | First stage of wood turning into coal
[0:1] - Order of carbon: **anthracite > bituminous > sub-bituminous > lignite > peat**.
[0:2] - International (ASTM-style) tables give lignite **25–35%**, sub-bituminous 35–45%, bituminous 45–86%, anthracite 86–97% – use these when the options are on that scale.
[0:3] - **Calorific value** depends mainly on the **carbon content** (moisture and ash lower it).
[0:4] - A good fuel has a **high** calorific value, low moisture, harmless combustion products and controllable burning – "low calorific value" is not a property of a good fuel.
[0:5] - **Coal is a natural (fossil) fuel**; petrol and diesel are refined, charcoal is made from wood. **Lignite** contains carbon (tin, silver, iron are metals).
[0:6] # Gondwana vs Tertiary coal
[0:7] - **Gondwana** (~250 million years): **98% of reserves**, 99% of production; coking and bituminous coal; Damodar, Mahanadi, Son, Godavari, Wardha valleys – hence Gondwana rocks are India's most important rock system.
[0:8] - **Damuda series** (Lower Gondwana): India's **metallurgical (coking) coal** and the most productive fields.
[0:9] - **Tertiary** (15–60 million years): younger, **low carbon, high moisture and sulphur**; lignite (chief producer **Tamil Nadu**); **North-East India and J&K** (extra-peninsular), about 2% – not "mainly Peninsular". Bituminous coal is a Gondwana coal.
[0:10] - Some coal lies buried under the **Ganga alluvium and the Deccan Trap**.

## SUB 2 | Indian Coal Quality, Imports, Washing and Health
[2:0] - **Indian coal:** **high ash (35–45%)**, **low calorific value**, **low sulphur (~0.5%)** and a **high ash-fusion temperature (~1500°C)** (UPSC 2013: "1 and 2 only").
[2:1] - **Pollution (UPSC 2020, all correct):** coal ash contains **arsenic, lead and mercury**; coal plants emit **SO₂ and NOx**; Indian coal has high ash.
[2:2] - **Imports:** **coking coal** mainly from **Australia** (the steel industry imports it, UPSC 2015); non-coking from **Indonesia, South Africa**.
[2:3] - **Coal washing (APPSC G1 2024 A–R):** washing raises generation efficiency and cuts costs, but washed-coal use stays limited because it raises the cost of power and hurts plants in **merit-order** dispatch – **A and R true, R explains A**.
[2:4] - **Problems:** poor quality, movement bottlenecks, **low washery use**, rising coking-coal imports – not "excess high-quality coking coal".
[2:5] - **Pneumoconiosis** (black lung): dust disease of **coal-mine** workers.

## SUB 5 | Gasification, CBM and UCG
[5:0] - **Gasification:** coal + oxygen + steam → **syngas (CO + H₂ + CH₄ + CO₂)**; runs **IGCC** plants.
[5:1] - Products: SNG, methanol, **ethanol**, **urea**/ammonia, hydrogen, DME – **not nitroglycerine** (UPSC 2025).
[5:2] - **CBM** = "**sweet gas**"; free gas in joints, **adsorbed** in micropores.
[5:3] - **UCG:** in-situ; the MoEFCC panel **refused to waive the 300 m** minimum depth.
[5:4] - **National Coal Gasification Mission:** **100 MT by 2030**.
[5:5] - **Surface Coal/Lignite Gasification scheme** (13 May 2026): **₹37,500 crore**; up to **20%** of plant cost, cap ₹5,000 crore; no bidders yet – bid windows kept open, not withdrawn.
[5:6] - Commercial blocks: **50% revenue-share rebate** if at least **10%** output is gasified.
[5:7] - **JSPL Angul** (Odisha): only commercial-scale plant; **Talcher**: first gasification fertiliser plant.
