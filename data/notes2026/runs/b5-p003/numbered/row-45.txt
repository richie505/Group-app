## SUB 0 | GIS – Concept, Components and Uses
[0:0] - **GIS = Geographic Information System**: a computer system to **store, manipulate, analyse and present** all types of geographical (**spatial**) data – the most complete system for modelling spatial data and community vulnerability.
[0:1] - **Father of GIS:** **Roger Tomlinson** – coined the term for the Canada GIS (1960s).
[0:2] - Each theme is a **layer**; **overlay analysis** combines layers; data = spatial (vector, raster) + attribute tables.
[0:3] TABLE head: Component | Examples
  [0:3.0] Hardware | Computer, digitizer (tablet/table converting analog data to digital), scanner, GPS receiver, plotter
  [0:3.1] Software | QGIS – free, open-source, cross-platform desktop GIS
  [0:3.2] Data | Spatial (points, lines, polygons; satellite images) + attributes
[0:4] - **Remote sensing + GIS:** maps prepared from satellite data or aerial photos are digitised and stored in GIS; manual maps are slow to prepare and hard to update.
[0:5] - **Decision Support System:** data systems for planning.
[0:6] - **Survey of India** prepares India's **topographical maps**.
[0:7] # GIS in disasters
[0:8] - **Situational awareness map:** real-time weather, hospitals and shelters, damage imagery; **pre-disaster population density maps** are least useful in the live response.
[0:9] - Real-time sources: satellite imagery, weather data, sensor networks – **not news channels**.
[0:10] TABLE head: Remote sensing | GIS
  [0:10.0] Rainfall estimation during a cyclone; post-disaster damage | Mapping spread of infectious diseases; landslide hazard zonation
[0:11] - Earthquake **magnitude and epicentre** come from **seismographs**, not GIS.

[1:2] - Fire hotspots: thermal-infrared **MODIS** (Terra/Aqua) and **VIIRS** (Suomi-NPP), received at **Shadnagar** (NRSC) → **Forest Survey of India** alert system (not IMD) – the modern **fire alert system**.

[1:5] - **Ganga-Brahmaputra basin** = most flood-prone area (NRSC).
[1:6] - **Landslides:** **DInSAR** gives repeat-pass slope-deformation monitoring, before visible failure, without a ground instrument network.
[1:7] - **Global Forest Watch (WRI):** custom maps, forest trends, alerts.

## SUB 2 | Remote Sensing Basics and Spectral Indices
[2:0] - **Remote sensing:** gathering **images**/information about Earth **without physical contact**, from reflected or emitted radiation; covers dangerous or inaccessible areas.
[2:1] TABLE head: Type | Energy | Example
  [2:1.0] Active | Sends its own signal | RISAT radar, LiDAR
  [2:1.1] Passive | Natural radiation (sunlight) | Resourcesat, Oceansat
[2:2] - **Microwave / SAR** sees through cloud and darkness; **soil moisture** best measured by microwave/SAR.
[2:3] - **InSAR:** compares the **phase** of radar images from **orbiting** satellites → **ground deformation** (quakes, subsidence, landslides).
[2:4] - **NISAR:** radar, **L-band and S-band**; centimetre-scale ground movement.
[2:5] - Applications: land cover, vegetation, forests, **surface and groundwater**, snow, wetlands, **minerals and hydrocarbons**, crop area and **crop yield** – "all of the above".
[2:6] - Soil nutrient testing by **chemical analysis is NOT remote sensing**.
[2:7] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [2:7.0] NDVI | Normalized Difference Vegetation Index = (NIR − Red)/(NIR + Red); healthy vegetation reflects near-infrared; −1 to +1; most used for drought
  [2:7.1] VCI | From NDVI; drought declaration
  [2:7.2] Ecology index | Vegetation, water content, biological quantity – not cost and production
  [2:7.3] NH Green Cover Index 2025-26 | NHAI + NRSC; satellite green cover of national highways
[2:8] - **National Carbon Project** (ISRO-GBP): **carbon pools, fluxes and net carbon balance** of the terrestrial biosphere – vegetation pools, soil pools, fluxes.
