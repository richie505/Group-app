## SUB 0 | Environment (Protection) Act 1986
[0:0] # Background and Nature
[0:1] - Trigger: **Bhopal Gas Tragedy (December 1984)**; gives effect to the **Stockholm Conference, 1972**.
[0:2] - Basis: **Article 253**. Purpose: **protect and improve the environment and control pollution** (APPSC 2025).
[0:3] - Called **umbrella legislation** – coordinates central and state authorities under the Water and Air Acts; the **Central Government** is the primary authority. Last amendment mentioned: 1991.
[0:4] # Key Sections and Powers
[0:5] TABLE head: Section | Content
  [0:5.0] 3 | Centre's powers – emission/discharge standards, location of industries, hazardous waste, public health; supports EIA and CRZ notifications
  [0:5.1] 3(3) | Constitute authorities
  [0:5.2] 5 | Directions, including closure
  [0:5.3] 15-21 | Penalties
  [0:5.4] 16 | Environment Protection Fund
[0:6] - Authorities under Section 3(3): **Central Ground Water Authority (January 1997)** – regulates groundwater development; **National Ganga River Basin Authority (2009)**.
[0:7] # Environment Protection Fund
[0:8] - In the **Public Account**; holds fines under the Air, Water and EP Acts; **75% to States/UTs, 25% to Centre**.
[0:9] - **Environmental (Protection) Fund Rules, 2026** notified by MoEFCC.
[0:10] Water Cess → Consolidated Fund of India; EP Fund → Public Account.
[0:11] # Notifications and Rules under EPA
[0:12] TABLE head: Notification | Key point
  [0:12.0] Doon Valley (1989) | No industry burning over 24 MT coal/fuel a day
  [0:12.1] Revdanda Creek (1989) | No industries around the creek
  [0:12.2] CRZ (1991) | Regulates coastal stretches; no ash/waste dumping; CRZ under EPA confirmed (Jul 2026)
  [0:12.3] Dhanu Taluka (1991) | Ecologically fragile; no power plants
  [0:12.4] EIA (1994, revised 2006) | Prior environmental clearance
  [0:12.5] Eco-Sensitive Zones; Plastic Waste Management Rules | Notified under EPA
  [0:12.6] Green Credit Rules (2023) | Voluntary tree planting on degraded land earns Green Credits
[0:13] - Green Credit afforestation can be swapped for compensatory afforestation under Van (S&S) Rules 2023; guidelines 16 Sep 2025.
[0:14] - **UPSC 2019:** EPA empowers the Centre to **lay down emission/discharge standards** – but not to "state the requirement of public participation" (2 only).
[0:15] - **CRZ 2011:** Sundarbans and other ecologically sensitive areas identified under the EPA are **Critical Vulnerable Coastal Areas (CVCA)**, managed **with coastal communities incl. fisherfolk** (both statements true).
[0:16] - **Recognised labs (Section 12):** EPA lets the Centre establish or recognise environmental laboratories; **EPTRI, Hyderabad** (state-government institute) is on the list of recognised government/semi-government labs.

## SUB 2 | CPCB, SPCBs and MoEFCC
[2:0] # CPCB
[2:1] - **Statutory body**, constituted **September 1974** under the **Water Act**; under **MoEFCC**; gives technical services to the Ministry under EPA 1986.
[2:2] - National level; deals with water (1974 Act) and air (1981 Act) pollution; an **enforcement body under the EPA framework**.
[2:3] - Functions: **coordinates SPCBs**, technical assistance, research, training, national monitoring.
[2:4] TABLE head: Item | CPCB | SPCB
  [2:4.0] Level | National | State
  [2:4.1] Chairperson nominated by | Central Government | State Government
  [2:4.2] Functions | Coordination, standards, research | Inspection, sample analysis, granting consents
[2:5] - Monitoring: **NAMP** – 1,646 stations in 603 cities (March 2026); **National AQI 2015**; **SAMEER app** gives real-time air quality (SAFAR is a separate forecasting system); **AUM Photonic** – indigenous real-time remote air monitoring.
[2:6] - **NCAP (2019)**: **40% PM10 cut by 2025-26** over 2019-20 in **130 cities, 24 States/UTs**.
[2:7] # MoEF / MoEFCC
[2:8] - **Department of Environment 1980 → Ministry of Environment and Forests 1985 → MoEFCC 2014**.
[2:9] - Bodies: CPCB (statutory), Central Zoo Authority (1992, under WPA 1972).
[2:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment Part 7 p.2; NextGen Rocket Environment p.11, 37; PMF Environment Part 6 p.59-60
## SUB 3 | Water Act 1974 and Water Cess Act 1977
[3:0] # Water Act 1974
[3:1] - Purpose: **prevent and control water pollution** and **maintain or restore the wholesomeness of water**.
[3:2] - Passed under **Article 252** – applies in States that adopt it by resolution.
[3:3] - Created the **Central Board for the Prevention and Control of Water Pollution (1974)** – renamed **CPCB** by the **1988 amendment** – and the State Boards.
[3:4] - Structure: **8 chapters, 64 sections**; standards for **discharge of effluents**; prior consent of the Board needed before discharging effluent.
[3:5] - **Section 33A**: directions, including **environmental compensation**.
[3:6] - Penalties: **1.5 to 6 years' jail**. Rules: Water Rules 1975.
[3:7] # Water Cess Act 1977
[3:8] - Cess on water consumed by specified industries and local authorities – funds the Boards.
[3:9] - Proceeds go to the **Consolidated Fund of India**; subsumed in **GST in 2017**.
[3:10] - **Water (Amendment) Act 2024:** minor offences decriminalised – penalties Rs 10,000 to 15 lakh; Centre may exempt some plants from consent.
[3:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment Part 6 p.8-10; NextGen Rocket Environment p.37; PMF Environment Part 6 p.60-61
## SUB 4 | Air Act 1981
[4:0] - Purpose: **prevention, control and abatement of air pollution**; maintain **air quality standards** (APPSC G2 2025); Central and State Boards set and enforce standards.
[4:1] - Enacted **29 March 1981**; in force **16 May 1981**; modelled on the Water Act.
[4:2] - Structure: **7 chapters, 54 sections**.
[4:3] - The existing **CPCB got air functions**; SPCBs enforce in States.
[4:4] - **Noise** added to the definition of "air pollutant" by the **1987 amendment** (not 1991).
[4:5] - **Air pollution control areas**: declared by the **State Government** – Chapter IV, Sections 19-31; a special power of the Air Act, not the Water Act.
[4:6] - Emission standards for industry and automobiles. **Section 31A**: directions; Boards can levy compensation.
[4:7] # Water Act vs Air Act
[4:8] TABLE head: Feature | Water Act 1974 | Air Act 1981
  [4:8.0] Aim | Wholesomeness of water | Prevent, control, abate air pollution
  [4:8.1] Structure | 8 chapters, 64 sections | 7 chapters, 54 sections
  [4:8.2] Basis | Article 252 | Modelled on Water Act
  [4:8.3] Boards | Created CPCB and SPCBs | Same Boards given air functions
  [4:8.4] Special power | Effluent discharge standards | Air pollution control areas
  [4:8.5] Amended | 1988 (CPCB name) | 1987 (noise added)
  [4:8.6] Compensation | Section 33A | Section 31A
[4:9] - Common: both require **prior consent** of the Board to establish and operate a polluting unit.
[4:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment Part 6 p.8; NextGen Rocket Environment p.36; PMF Environment Part 6 p.62

## SUB 7 | Penalties and Jan Vishwas Act 2023
[7:0] - EPA original penalty: up to **5 years or Rs 1 lakh**, plus **Rs 5,000 per day** for continuing offence.
[7:1] - **Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023** **decriminalised** the EPA penalty regime – monetary penalties fixed by an **adjudicating officer**; appeals go to the **NGT**. The Water and Air Acts were also amended.
[7:2] - Minor offences decriminalised by amendment in 2024.
[7:3] - **PLI Act 1991** (amended by Jan Vishwas): penalty instead of imprisonment; penalty rates moved to the rules; Environmental Relief Fund money can go to CPCB/SPCBs for restoration; relief for death raised from **Rs 25,000 to Rs 5 lakh**.
[7:4] The 5 years / Rs 1 lakh / Rs 5,000 per day figures belong to the EPA; Water Act jail is 1.5-6 years.
[7:5] - **Economic instruments:** a **pollution tax** (polluter pays) is considered the most effective instrument of pollution control – better than subsidies, moral persuasion or common law.
[7:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment Part 6 p.61-64; app notes 5:76 sub 5
