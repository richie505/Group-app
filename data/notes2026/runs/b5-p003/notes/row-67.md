=== Bioethanol – Feedstocks, Gasohol, Power Alcohol and Global Producers
# Feedstocks and process
- **Bioethanol:** made by **fermentation** of sugar or starch by yeast **Saccharomyces cerevisiae** (baker's yeast); can be mixed with petrol to improve combustion and lower emissions.
| Feedstock | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Sugarcane** (juice, **molasses**) | India's main ethanol source; molasses = excellent raw material for **power alcohol** |
| **Maize (corn)** | Starch-rich energy crop for ethanol; principal US feedstock |
| Damaged grains, cassava, rotten potatoes, sugar beet | Allowed under the National Policy on Biofuels |
| **Jatropha, Pongamia, sunflower** | Oilseeds → **biodiesel**, not ethanol |
- Potato, rice and wheat are not the classic "ethanol crop" answer; sugarcane (UPSC 2009) and maize (UPSC 2010) are.
# Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Gasohol** | **Petrol (gasoline) + ethanol**, typically ~10% ethanol |
| **Power alcohol** | Fuel-grade (anhydrous) ethanol, mainly from **molasses** (sugar-industry by-product) |
| **Rectified spirit** | Ethanol with about **5% water** (~95% ethanol) |
| **Absolute alcohol** | ~100% ethanol |
# Global producers
- **USA** (maize/corn) = world's **largest producer, consumer and exporter** of fuel ethanol / gasohol; **Brazil** (sugarcane) second; together ~80% of world ethanol – so in UPSC 2025 Statement I (Brazil > USA) is false, Statement II (corn vs sugarcane) is true.
- **Brazil:** **mandatory ethanol blending since 1976** (~25% since 2007); regarded as the world's first sustainable biofuel economy.
# Indian technology firsts
- **CSIR-NIIST, Thiruvananthapuram** (2017): bioethanol from **discarded cotton stalks** using a wild yeast strain.
- **IIT Kharagpur "Soil to Soil"** (May 2017): enzyme-based (no chemical pre-treatment) process giving **biofuel** from crop residues; leftover biomass returns to the soil as bio-fertiliser.
- **TVS Apache RTR 200 Fi E100** (2019) = India's first **ethanol-powered motorcycle** (TVS Motor).
> Most-asked: ethanol crop (sugarcane / maize), gasohol = petrol + ethanol, largest producer USA, Brazil mandate.

=== Biogas and Biomass Energy
# Biomass energy
- **Biomass energy** = solar energy stored by plants through **photosynthesis**; burned directly or converted to biogas/biofuels; renewable (unlike coal, natural gas, petroleum).
- **Gobar gas = biomass energy** (from cattle dung) – not nuclear, geothermal or hydro.
- Biomass from waste: agricultural waste (stubble, husk, manure, dairy waste), **food-processing residues, human sewage, biogenic municipal solid waste** (all three are biomass).
- Burning biomass releases CO2 largely balanced by the CO2 it absorbed while growing (near carbon-neutral).
# Biogas
| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Composition | **Methane ~50–75%** + **CO2 ~25–50%** + traces (H2S, moisture); piped natural gas is 85–95% methane |
| Fuel gas | **Methane** – the burning component (cooking gas / gobar gas); not butane, propane, ethane |
| Process | **Anaerobic digestion** (biomethanation) / **fermentation** – in the **absence of oxygen** |
| Microbes | **Methanogens** (archaebacteria) produce methane |
| By-product | Slurry = good organic manure |
- **Composting** is **aerobic** decomposition, unlike biogas.
- Pioneer: floating-drum **"Gramalakshmi"** plant of **Jashbhai Patel (1950s)**, spread by **KVIC**.
- **CBG (Compressed Biogas)** = purified, compressed biogas, chemically identical to **CNG**.
- **India's first Bio-CNG plant from agricultural residue: Pirangut, Pune** (Primove Engineering), inaugurated **2016** by Nitin Gadkari and Manohar Parrikar.
- **Bagasse cogeneration:** sugar mills burn bagasse → **steam and power together**.
# Gasification and microbial fuel cells
- **Biomass gasification:** heating with **limited (controlled) oxygen** → syngas (**CO, H2, CO2**).
- **Producer gas** = CO + H2 + CO2 + **methane**.
- **Microbial Fuel Cell (MFC):** microbes act as **catalysts**, oxidise organic matter and pass electrons to an electrode; cleans wastewater and makes electricity together (no burning).
> Biogas = methane + CO2, anaerobic, methanogens – asked about 10 times.

=== Geothermal Energy
- **Source:** heat stored in the **Earth's interior**, produced mostly by **decay of radioactive** elements (uranium, potassium) and original planetary heat; surface signs: **hot springs, geysers, volcanoes**.
- Not ultimately solar: **geothermal and nuclear** energy (biomass, wind, fossil fuels are stored solar energy).
- Gives **24x7 baseload** power – independent of sunlight or wind.
- **India's potential:** about **10,600 MW**; GSI identified ~340–381 hot springs.
- **Provinces:** Himalaya, Naga-Lushai, Andaman-Nicobar; **Cambay**, **Son-Narmada-Tapi (SONATA)**, **West Coast**, Damodar, **Mahanadi**, **Godavari valley** – none in the **Ganga Delta / Indo-Gangetic Plain**.
| Field | State / UT |
|---|---|
| **Puga Valley**, Chumathang | **Ladakh** – most promising / highest potential (GSI) |
| Tattapani | Chhattisgarh |
| **Manikaran** | **Himachal Pradesh** |
| Tapovan | Uttarakhand |
| Surajkund | Jharkhand |
| **Manuguru (old Khammam district)**, Godavari valley | Telangana (earlier Andhra Pradesh) |
- **India's first geothermal power project: Puga, Ladakh** (**ONGC**); described as the world's highest geothermal project.
- **World:** first successful tapping of underground heat **Boise, Idaho, USA (1890)** – hot-water pipe network for heating; first geothermal **power** at **Larderello, Italy (1904)**.
- **National Policy on Geothermal Energy** – India's first, **MNRE, September 2025**; **five pilot projects**.
- **EGS** (Enhanced Geothermal Systems) = artificially stimulated reservoirs. Challenges: high drilling and exploration cost, remote sites, transmission, gas leakage, subsidence.

=== Biodiesel – Jatropha, Pongamia and RUCO
| Crop | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Jatropha curcas (ratanjyot)** | **The biodiesel plant**; non-edible seeds ~40% oil; grows on wasteland, little water |
| **Pongamia pinnata (Karanja)** | Alternative biodiesel plant; oil rich in oleic acid |
| Mahua, neem, simarouba | Other non-edible biodiesel sources |
- Not biodiesel plants: guggal, rosha grass, java grass, isabgol, belladonna, senna, cotton, sugarcane, potato.
- **Biodiesel** = methyl/ethyl esters of fatty acids by **transesterification** of vegetable oils/animal fats with methanol or ethanol.
- **India's first biodiesel plant: Kakinada, Andhra Pradesh** (early 2000s).
- **Palm stearin oil** = feedstock of about **94%** of India's biodiesel (mostly imported), not jatropha.
- **RUCO** (Repurpose Used Cooking Oil, **FSSAI**) = collection and conversion of **used cooking oil into biodiesel**.
- Blend still **below 0.5%**; target **5% (B5) by 2030**.
- **April 2023:** Belgian firm **VITO** launched a 2-year biodiesel project at **Ayodhya**.

=== Tidal, Wave, OTEC and Desalination
| Site | State | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Gulf of Khambhat (Cambay)** | Gujarat | Tidal range 5–10 m; ~7,000 MW – **largest / best tidal site** |
| Gulf of Kutch (Hanthal Creek) | Gujarat | Tidal range 5–8 m; major project proposed |
| Sundarbans – **Durgaduani Creek** | West Bengal | ~5 m; 3 MW pilot |
- Criterion: tidal range ~**5 m or more** in a narrow gulf/estuary. **Gulf of Mannar** is not a tidal site (offshore wind zone); Gulf of Aden / Panay are not Indian.
- **Ocean energy potential (MNRE):** tidal about **12,455 MW** (mainly Khambhat and Kutch); wave about **40,000 MW**.
- **First wave plant: Vizhinjam, Kerala (1991)**. **Sindhuja-I** ocean wave energy converter – **IIT Madras** (tested off Tuticorin).
- **OTEC = Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion:** warm surface vs cold deep water (~1,000 m), ~**20°C** gap, tropics; sites **Lakshadweep, A&N**.
- **NIOT, Chennai (Ministry of Earth Sciences)** = lead ocean-energy body; India's first desalination plant **Kavaratti, Lakshadweep (2005)**, LTTD, **1 lakh L/day**.
- **Desalination** (removing salts from seawater) prepares large quantities of drinking water.

=== Aviation Biofuels and Sustainable Aviation Fuel
- **First biofuel flight in India:** **SpiceJet**, **Dehradun–Delhi, 27 August 2018**, Bombardier Q-400; one engine 25% bio-jet + 75% ATF; jatropha bio-jet from **CSIR-IIP, Dehradun**.
- **SAF** = **drop-in** synthesised hydrocarbons (not direct ethanol), blendable up to **50%** with jet fuel.
- **SAF feedstocks:** **agricultural residues, corn grain, wastewater treatment sludge, wood-mill waste** – all four (UPSC 2024); also oilseeds, algae, MSW.
- **SAF targets** (international flights): **1% by 2027, 2% by 2028, 5% by 2030**.
- **IOCL Panipat** = first Indian ISCC CORSIA-certified SAF producer (Aug 2025). **BioE3 Policy 2024** backs bio-jet fuel.
- **Global Biofuels Alliance:** launched **9 September 2023**, **G20 Summit, New Delhi**; core founders **India, USA, Brazil**; Secretariat **New Delhi**.

=== Biofuel Generations and Algal Fuels
| Generation | Feedstock | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1G | Food crops: sugarcane, molasses, corn, vegetable oils | Bioethanol, biodiesel |
| 2G | Non-food lignocellulosic residue: rice straw, husk, wood chips, cotton stalks | Cellulosic ethanol |
| **3G** | **Algae / algal biomass** | Algal biodiesel, biobutanol |
| **4G** | **Genetically modified algae**, yeast, cyanobacteria; carbon-capturing crops | Carbon-negative fuels |
- **Biobutanol** (Clostridium acetobutylicum): higher energy content than ethanol.
- **Algal biofuel:** grows in seas **and on land** (ponds, tanks, wastewater) – "seas only" is false.
- **Limitations:** needs **high expertise/technology** and **large-scale facilities** that may raise ecological/social concerns (UPSC 2017: statements 2 and 3); energy-intensive lipid extraction; not cost-effective.

=== National Policy on Biofuels and Ethanol Blending
- **NPB 2018** – aim: cut import dependence; targets **E20 + B5 by 2030**; categories: **basic** (1G bioethanol, biodiesel) and **advanced** (2G ethanol, drop-in fuels from MSW, 3G, **bio-CNG**); does not discourage advanced biofuels.
- **Ethanol feedstock widened beyond molasses:** sugarcane juice, B-molasses, **cassava, damaged wheat, rotten potatoes, sugar beet**, maize, surplus FCI rice, agri residues – **not groundnut seeds or horse gram** (UPSC 2020: 1, 2, 5, 6).
- **Surplus food grains** need **National Biofuel Coordination Committee (NBCC)** approval; NBCC chaired by the **Minister of Petroleum and Natural Gas** (approved surplus FCI rice Apr 2020, maize Nov 2020).
- **NPB 2022 amendment:** E20 advanced to **ESY 2025-26**; more feedstocks; SEZ/EOU production; new NBCC members may be added.
- Enablers: **Motor Vehicles Act Section 52** (engine conversion); **100% FDI automatic** for biofuel technology (domestic use).
| Ethanol Blending Programme (MoPNG) | Date |
|---|---|
| E5 in 9 States + 4 UTs | 1 January 2003 |
| E10 achieved | June 2022 |
| PM's World Environment Day pledge: **E20 by 2025** | 5 June 2021 |
| E20 petrol mandatory (min RON 95) | 1 April 2026 |
| BIS **IS 19850:2026** (E22–E30) | May 2026 |
| Next goal **E30** | 2030 |
- EBP is run by **MoPNG**; OMC ethanol prices: **maize > molasses > rice**; maize expansion pulled area from **pulses** and oilseeds.

=== Waste-to-energy and Small Hydro
- **India's first waste-to-energy plant: Timarpur, Delhi (1987)**; later Okhla, Ghazipur, Narela-Bawana (Delhi).
| Route | Principle |
|---|---|
| Incineration | Direct burning of MSW in excess air |
| **Pyrolysis** | Heating **without oxygen** → char, bio-oil, syngas |
| Gasification | Partial oxidation (controlled oxygen) → syngas |
| Biomethanation | **Microbes** convert wet waste → biogas |
| **RDF** | Refuse-derived fuel = **dry combustible part of MSW** |
# Small hydro
| Small hydro class | Capacity |
|---|---|
| Micro | up to 100 kW |
| **Mini** | **101 kW – 2 MW** |
| Small | 2 – **25 MW** (upper limit) |
- Small hydro sites: hill streams (run-of-river), **canal falls, toes of dams**; ~half the potential in **Arunachal, Himachal, J&K, Uttarakhand**; installed ~5 GW (~**25%** of potential).

=== Renewable Classification – What Counts as Renewable
- **MNRE renewables:** solar, wind, **bio-power**, **small hydro** and **large hydro** (renewable **since 2019**); geothermal, tidal, OTEC under development.
- **Nuclear** = non-fossil but **not renewable** (uranium is exhaustible).
- **Renewable resource:** biomass, solar, wind, hydro, tidal; **coal, natural gas, petroleum** are non-renewable.
- **Not derived from the Sun:** **nuclear** and **geothermal** (UPPCS 2022 – both are correct; the key gives nuclear); biomass and wind are stored solar energy.
