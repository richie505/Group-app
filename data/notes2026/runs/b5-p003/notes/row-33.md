=== First Satellites and Early Experiments (1975-1988)
# Aryabhata – India's first satellite
- **First Indian satellite:** **Aryabhata**, launched **19 April 1975**; with it India **entered the space age** (asked 9 times: "first satellite", "1975", "19 April 1975", "first satellite experiment").
- **Launch:** by the **Soviet Union** on a **Kosmos-3M** rocket from **Kapustin Yar** (USSR), not from Sriharikota; weight **360 kg**.
- **Named after** the 5th-century astronomer Aryabhata; **conceived by Sarabhai**, launched after his death; satellite team led by **U.R. Rao**.
# Early satellites and experiments
| Date | Satellite / experiment | Vehicle / site |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Aug 1975-76 | **SITE** (Satellite Instructional Television Experiment): **first use of space technology for education**; ~2,400 villages, ~5 million people | **NASA's ATS-6** satellite |
| 19 Apr 1975 | **Aryabhata**: first Indian satellite, 360 kg | Soviet **Kosmos-3M**, **Kapustin Yar** |
| 1977-79 | **STEP** (Satellite Telecommunication Experiments Project) | Franco-German **Symphonie** satellite |
| 1979-81 | **Bhaskara I (1979)** and II: experimental earth observation | Soviet Intercosmos rocket, **Baikonur** |
| 18 Jul 1980 | **Rohini (RS-1)**: **first satellite on an Indian-made rocket**; India the **6th** nation to do so | **SLV-3**, Sriharikota (AP) |
| 19 Jun 1981 | **APPLE** (Ariane Passenger Payload Experiment): **first experimental geostationary communication satellite** | **Ariane-1**, **Kourou**, French Guiana |
| 1983 | INSAT-1B (INSAT system operational) | US Space Shuttle **Challenger** |
| 1988 | **IRS-1A**: first operational remote-sensing satellite | Soviet **Vostok**, Baikonur |
- **Chronology of programmes:** **SITE (1975) → STEP (1977) → APPLE (1981) → IGMDP (1983)**: code **D, C, A, B** (UPPCS 1998).
- **Chronology of satellites:** **Aryabhata (1975) → Bhaskara I (1979) → Rohini (1980) → APPLE (1981)**.
> ⚠ Trap: ATS-6 (NASA) served **SITE**, not APPLE; APPLE went up on Ariane-1. Bhaskara, Apple, Rohini, Agni are "first satellite" distractors (Agni is a missile).

=== Vikram Sarabhai and ISRO Chairmen
# Vikram Sarabhai (1919-71)
- **Father of the Indian Space Programme** (not Bhabha, Kalam, Dhawan or C.V. Raman); chaired **INCOSPAR** (1962); set up **TERLS**; **first ISRO Chairman** till his death in **1971**.
- **Padma Bhushan 1966**; **Padma Vibhushan posthumously 1972**; succeeded **Homi Bhabha** at the Atomic Energy Commission (**1966**).
- **COSPAR Vikram Sarabhai Medal** (since 1990): 2026 winner **Annapurni Subramaniam** – 4th Indian, first Indian woman.

=== Institutional Beginnings – INCOSPAR, ISRO, DoS, TERLS
# INCOSPAR, ISRO, Department of Space
| Body | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **INCOSPAR** (Indian National Committee for Space Research) | **1962** | Under the **Department of Atomic Energy**; set up with Homi Bhabha, **led by Vikram Sarabhai** |
| **ISRO** | **15 Aug 1969** | Superseded INCOSPAR (not 1967, 1970 or 1974) |
| **Department of Space + Space Commission** | **1972** (1 June) | ISRO brought under DoS; DoS reports to the PM |
- INCOSPAR (1962) came first; ISRO (1969) replaced it; DoS and Space Commission came in 1972.
# TERLS, Thumba
- **Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station**, Thumba, Thiruvananthapuram (**Kerala**) – not UP, Bihar or Jharkhand; chosen because it lies close to the **magnetic equator** (equatorial electrojet studies); sounding rockets only, no satellites.
- First sounding rocket: **Nike-Apache** (American-built), **21 Nov 1963**.
- **RH-75**: first **Indian-made** sounding rocket, **20 Nov 1967**.
- Satellite Telecommunication Earth Station, **Ahmedabad**: **1 Jan 1967**.
> ⚠ Trap: INCOSPAR was under the **DAE**, not the Department of Space.

=== Key Milestones (1992-2026)
| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1992 | **USA sanctioned ISRO** for two years under the **MTCR** (cryogenic engine deal with Russia's Glavkosmos) |
| **1994** | **PSLV-D2** (15 Oct 1994) – PSLV operational; placed IRS-P2 in a **sun-synchronous** orbit (UPSC 1995: "launching synchronous satellite") |
| **2008** | **Chandrayaan-1** (22 Oct 2008, PSLV-C11): India's first Moon mission – the **first Indian-built spacecraft to leave Earth's gravity** |
| 2008 | Cabinet cleared **Chandrayaan-2** (Indo-Russian plan then) at **Rs 425 crore** |
| 2014 | Mangalyaan: Mars orbit on maiden attempt; first Asian, India 4th country |
| 2017 | 104 satellites on one PSLV |
| 2019 | Mission Shakti (ASAT test); NSIL formed; Chandrayaan-2 |
| 2023 | Chandrayaan-3: first landing near the lunar south pole; Aditya-L1 to the L1 point |
| 30 Dec 2024 / 16 Jan 2025 | **SpaDeX** launched on PSLV-C60 / **docking** done; India **4th** (after US, Russia, China) |
| 24 Dec 2025 | LVM3-M6 lifts BlueBird Block-2 (6,100 kg), heaviest payload from Indian soil |
| 18 Jul 2026 | Skyroot's Vikram-1: first privately developed Indian rocket to reach orbit |
- **SpaDeX** = **Space Docking** technology demonstrator: two small spacecraft; not a Mars landing, space station or asteroid mining (APPSC DL 2023).
- **Moon spacecraft name:** **Chandrayaan-I**; "Chang'e-1" (Change-I) is China's; "Antariksha" is a distractor.
> India is 4th in both: Mars orbit (Mangalyaan) and space docking (SpaDeX).

=== International Cooperation and Space Vision 2047
| Partner | Mission |
|---|---|
| USSR | Aryabhata, Bhaskara; Rakesh Sharma on **Soyuz T-11** (3 Apr 1984) – first Indian in space |
| Indo-US | SITE with NASA (1975-76); **NISAR** launched 2025 |
| Indo-French | APPLE on Ariane-1; **Megha-Tropiques (2011)**; **SARAL (2013)** |
| ISRO-ESA | Ground-tracking support for Gaganyaan |
| Axiom-4 | **Shubhanshu Shukla** to the ISS, June-July 2025 |
- **58th International Astronautical Congress:** **Hyderabad**, Sep 2007, hosted by ISRO; India's earlier IAC was Bangalore 1988.
- **Vision 2047:** **Bharatiya Antariksh Station by 2035**; **Indian on the Moon by 2040**; next missions **Chandrayaan-4, Chandrayaan-5, Venus Orbiter Mission** (Rs **1,236 crore**).
- **Gaganyaan:** crew of **3**, **400 km** orbit, **3 days**; splashdown in Indian waters.
- Space economy: FDI up to **100%** in non-sensitive segments; IN-SPACe chairman **Pawan Kumar Goenka**.
> ⚠ Trap: BAS target is **2035**, Moon landing **2040**; 2047 is only the Vision horizon.
