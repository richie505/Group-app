=== Chandrayaan-3 (2023)
- **Launch:** **14 July 2023**, **LVM3-M4** from SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota; follow-on to Ch-2 to show end-to-end **safe landing and roving**.
- **Modules:** **Propulsion Module** + lander **Vikram** + rover **Pragyan** (names kept from Ch-2) – **no orbiter** (the Ch-2 orbiter relays).
- **Mass:** **3,900 kg** (lander 1,752 kg incl. **26 kg** rover); life **one lunar day (~14 Earth days)**.
- **Landing:** **23 Aug 2023** at **Shiv Shakti Point**, near the **south pole** (~69°S) → **National Space Day, 23 August**.
- **Rank:** India the **4th** to soft-land (after **USA, USSR/Russia, China**) and the **1st near the lunar south pole**; Japan's SLIM later 5th.
| Payload | On | Function |
|---|---|---|
| ChaSTE | Lander | Surface thermal profile |
| ILSA | Lander | Moonquakes (seismic activity) |
| RAMBHA-LP (Langmuir probe) | Lander | Plasma density |
| LRA (NASA) | Lander | Passive laser retroreflector |
| **APXS** | Rover | Elemental composition (**Mg, Al, Si, K, Ca, Ti, Fe**) of soil and rocks |
| **LIBS** | Rover | **Confirmed sulphur** (in-situ, Aug 2023); also Al, Ca, Fe, Cr, Ti, Mn, Si, O |
| SHAPE | Propulsion Module | Studies Earth from lunar orbit |
- APXS found the soil rich in **iron and magnesium**, poor in aluminium.
> Asked 12 times: sulphur (×3), launch date, south pole, names, payload → function, "4th country".

=== Mangalyaan (MOM) and Mars
- **Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM) = Mangalyaan**: India's **first interplanetary mission**; **PSLV-C25**, **5 Nov 2013**; Mars orbit **24 Sep 2014** (highly elliptical).
- **Firsts:** **first country to reach Mars orbit on its maiden attempt**; **first Asian nation**; ISRO the **4th** to reach Mars after **NASA, USSR/Roscosmos, ESA** (not "second after USA"); lowest-cost Mars mission (~Rs 450 crore).
- **5 payloads** incl. **Methane Sensor for Mars**, **Lyman Alpha Photometer**, Mars Colour Camera, MENCA, TIS; designed for **6 months**, worked ~**8 years** (contact lost **2022**).
- Route: Earth orbit-raising, then Trans-Mars Injection – a **Hohmann transfer** (elliptical transfer orbit, two burns).
- Film **Mission Mangal** (2019) on the MOM women scientists: **Vidya Balan** the leading lady (with Akshay Kumar).
- **Methane on Mars:** first measured by **ESA's Mars Express**, also by NASA's **Curiosity**; with **water and gypsum** cited as evidence for possible life – Mars the planet that **may suit life**. (Jupiter's air also has trace methane – UPPCS 2016 accepted both.)
- **Next:** Mangalyaan-2 (Mars landing, targeted ~2030); TSPSC 2023 key names **Japan** as ISRO's partner for a new Mars effort (not confirmed: ISRO-JAXA's joint mission is the lunar LUPEX).
> Angle: launch vehicle and year, "first attempt" statements (UPSC 2016: I and III only).

=== Chandrayaan-2 (2019)
- **Launch:** **22 Jul 2019**, **GSLV Mk III-M1** (LVM3) from **Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh**; Cabinet approval **2008** (PM Manmohan Singh).
- **Orbiter + lander Vikram** (after Vikram Sarabhai) **+ rover Pragyan**; target the **lunar south polar region** – **not the North Pole**. No Moon Impact Probe (that was Ch-1).
- **Originally with Russia:** Roscosmos was to supply the lander; it withdrew, so ISRO built Vikram.
- **Result:** partial success – Vikram **hard-landed** (Sep 2019, **Tiranga Point**); the **orbiter still works**, highest-resolution lunar images (~0.25 m).
- **Orbiter's CLASS** X-ray spectrometer mapped **sodium** abundance on the Moon for the first time (Oct 2022); also mapped chromium and water/hydroxyl.
- **People:** M. Annadurai – Chandrayaan-1 project director and programme director in early Ch-2 (book key); at launch **M. Vanitha** was Project Director and **Ritu Karidhal** Mission Director.

=== Aditya-L1 and Solar Missions
- **Aditya-L1:** India's **first solar mission / space-based solar observatory**; launched **2 Sep 2023**, **PSLV-C57**, from the **Second Launch Pad**, SDSC SHAR (NextGen's "25 Jan 2024" is the magnetometer-boom date, not the launch).
- **Halo orbit** around **Sun-Earth L1**, ~**1.5 million km** from Earth; inserted **6 Jan 2024**; continuous view, no eclipse; life 5 years.
| Type | Payload | Studies |
|---|---|---|
| Remote | VELC (Visible Emission Line Coronagraph, **IIA**) | Corona |
| Remote | SUIT (Solar Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope, **IUCAA**) | Photosphere, chromosphere (near-UV) |
| Remote | SoLEXS | Soft X-ray |
| Remote | HEL1OS | Hard X-ray |
| In-situ | ASPEX | Solar-wind protons, ions |
| In-situ | **PAPA (Plasma Analyser Package for Aditya)** | Solar-wind electrons, ions |
| In-situ | Magnetometer | Magnetic field |
- **7 payloads** = 4 remote sensing + 3 in-situ.
- **Earth's magnetic field** diverts charged solar particles towards the **poles** → aurora.
- **Parker Solar Probe** (NASA, **12 Aug 2018**, on a **Delta IV Heavy** – not "Delta VI"): first craft through the Sun's outer corona; traces energy flow heating the corona and accelerating the solar wind. Solar Orbiter (NASA-ESA, 2020), SOHO (L1, 1995).

=== Chandrayaan-1 (2008)
- India's **first Moon mission** – a **lunar probe** (orbiter + **Moon Impact Probe**), not manned; launched **22 Oct 2008** on **PSLV-C11** from **SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota (Andhra Pradesh)**; 1,380 kg; 100 km polar orbit.
- **11 instruments** (5 Indian, 6 from USA, UK, Germany, Sweden, Bulgaria): TMC, HySI, LLRI, HEX, MIP, **CIXS**, SIR-2, SARA, **Mini-SAR**, **M3**, RADOM. (Lyman Alpha Photometer was on Mangalyaan.)
- NASA's **M3** detected **water molecules** on the surface.
- **MIP** hit the south polar region on **14 Nov 2008** → **Jawahar Point**; India the **4th** to reach the Moon's surface by impact probe, after **USSR, USA and Japan** (Japan's Hiten, 1993, before India).
- 3,400+ orbits; contact lost **29 Aug 2009** (~10 months of 2 planned years).

=== Space Physics – Escape Velocity, Re-entry, Lagrange Points
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Escape velocity** | Minimum speed to escape a body's gravity; **Earth 11.2 km/s** |
| Orbital velocity | ~7.8-7.9 km/s in low Earth orbit |
| Lagrange points | **Five** points (L1-L5) where a small object stays balanced; Sun-Earth L1 ~1.5 million km |
| Soft landing | Controlled low-speed touchdown |
| Hohmann transfer | Fuel-saving elliptical transfer with two burns (used by MOM) |
- An object thrown at **8 km/s** (below 11.2 km/s) **returns to Earth** (book key; strictly a horizontal 8 km/s above the air would orbit).
- **No atmosphere on the Moon:** low gravity, so its escape velocity is **less than the RMS speed** of gas molecules, which escape.
- **Re-entry blackout:** a returning shuttle heats up and **ionises the surrounding air**, so radio contact is lost for a while – A and R both true, R explains A.
