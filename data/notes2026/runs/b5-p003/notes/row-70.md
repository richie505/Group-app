=== Lighting Technologies – LED, CFL, Fluorescent, Sodium, Filament
| Lamp | Features |
|---|---|
| **LED** | **Highest efficacy**, longest life, **no mercury**; semiconductor emitter (**gallium arsenide** family) – the **most energy-saving** lamp |
| **CFL (Compact Fluorescent Lamp)** | Mercury vapour + phosphor; ~75% less energy than bulbs; **shorter life and less efficient than LED** |
| **Fluorescent tube** | Low-pressure **mercury vapour + argon** (sometimes neon, krypton, xenon) |
| **Incandescent / GLS filament** | **Cheapest** for the same wattage; only **~10–15%** of input radiated as light, rest heat; **power factor = unity** (purely resistive) |
| **Sodium vapour** | **Yellow, monochromatic** light that does not split in fog/water droplets → **street lights** |
| **Mercury vapour** | White light |
- **Why mercury in fluorescent lamps (UPSC 2010):** when switched on, **mercury vapour emits ultraviolet**; the **phosphor coating** converts UV to visible light (mercury itself does not convert UV).
- **CFL vs LED (IAS 2011):** CFL uses mercury vapour + phosphor, LED a semiconductor ✓; CFL less efficient than LED ✓; "CFL lasts longer" ✗ → **I and III**.
- **Vacuum filament lamps:** contain **no gas** (gas-filled lamps use argon/nitrogen) → "none of the above".
- **A–R (APPSC 2021 ×2):** government urges replacing bright and CFL bulbs with **LEDs** because LEDs light with **great energy efficiency** → **A and R true, R explains A**.
- **A–R (RAS 2013):** LED life longer than CFL ✓; LED more efficient than CFL ✓; books key "R explains A" (LEDs run cooler); strictly the long life comes from solid-state design (no filament or electrodes to wear).
> Lighting is the heaviest PYQ block of this row (16 PYQs: UPSC 2010, IAS 2011, UPPCS 2000/2006/2007/2015, SSC 2017 ×2, RAS 2013, APPSC 2021, APPSC AEE flashcards).

=== Energy Conservation Act, BEE and Organisations
# Energy Conservation Act, 2001
- Set up the **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE)**; BEE operational from **1 March 2002**; statutory body under the **Ministry of Power**.
- **Designated consumers:** mandatory **energy audits** and norms; **penalties** for non-compliance.
- EE norms for buildings with connected load **>100 kW** or contract demand **>120 kVA**.
# Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022
- **Lok Sabha August 2022 → Rajya Sabha December 2022** – passed by Parliament in **December 2022** (APPSC EO 2023).
| Provision | Detail |
|---|---|
| Carbon Credit Trading Scheme | Legal basis for a national carbon market – **Section 14AA** |
| Non-fossil obligation | Designated consumers to use a minimum share of **non-fossil energy** |
| Building codes | Extended to large **residential** buildings (ECSBC) |
| Vehicles and vessels | Efficiency norms can be set |
# Organisations
| Body | Status / Role |
|---|---|
| BEE | Statutory (EC Act 2001), Ministry of Power; runs PAT, S&L, ECBC, SEEI, CAFE, NECA |
| EESL | JV of Ministry of Power PSUs; runs **UJALA, SLNP**, National Efficient Cooking Programme |
| PCRA | Petroleum Conservation Research Association (MoPNG); runs **SAKSHAM** (not BEE) |
- **NMEEE** = one of the **8 missions of NAPCC (2008)**. India imports ~**80%** of its oil → efficiency = energy security.

=== Standards and Labelling and Building Codes
- **S&L programme:** launched **2006**; **BEE under Ministry of Power**; **1–5 star**; each extra star saves **~10–15%**.
| Mandatory | Voluntary |
|---|---|
| Refrigerators, ACs, **ceiling fans**, TVs, washing machines, **geysers (storage water heaters)**, LED lamps, **tubular fluorescent lamps**, LPG stove, distribution transformers | Industrial motors, computers, microwave ovens |
- **UPSC 2016:** the star label is found on ceiling fans, electric geysers and tubular fluorescent lamps → **1, 2 and 3**.
- **From 1 January 2026:** labels mandatory for fridges, TVs, LPG stoves; makers **penalised if actual use deviates** from the label.
| Code | Year | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| ECBC | 2007 | New **commercial** buildings; 25–40% less energy |
| ECO Niwas Samhita | 2018 | **Residential** buildings |
| ECSBC | 2024 | Adds sustainability after the 2022 amendment |

=== Everyday Conservation and PCRA
- Efficiency **reduces the need for new energy sources** → sustainable development (APPSC FBO 2025).
- Saving practices: **LED lights**, star-rated appliances, switching off idle loads, avoiding AC overuse; **bicycles save fuel** (and cut pollution, noise).
- **Pressure cooker** saves about **20%** cooking gas for rice (PCRA tip).
- **PCRA SAKSHAM** (Sanrakshan Kshamata Mahotsav): annual one-month **fuel conservation mega campaign**, launched **January 2020** edition (APPSC PL 2020); SAKSHAM 2026 at Chandigarh.

=== PAT and the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme
- **PAT:** market mechanism under **NMEEE** – energy-reduction targets for **Designated Consumers**; over-achievers earn **ESCerts**, under-achievers buy them; traded on **power exchanges**.
- **PAT Cycle I (2012-15): 478 units in 8 sectors** – thermal power, iron & steel, cement, aluminium, fertilisers, chlor-alkali, pulp & paper, textiles; **railways** added later.
- After CCTS, PAT keeps 4 sectors: **thermal power, railways, DISCOMs, commercial buildings** (Jun 2026).
| Feature | PAT | CCTS |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate | ESCerts (energy intensity) | CCCs (emission intensity) |
| Start | 2012 | Notified 2023 – first mandatory carbon market |
| Coverage | 478 units, 8 sectors (Cycle I) | 9 heavy industries, 208 units |
| Administrator / Regulator | BEE | BEE / **CERC** |
- CCTS supports **45% cut in GDP emission intensity by 2030** (vs 2005); UK **CBAM** recognised CCTS (from **1 January 2027**).
