=== NOTES 2:14 | Right to Constitutional Remedies – Article 32; the Five Writs and Their Scope
## SUB 0 | Article 32 – Status and Significance
[14:0:0] - **Right to Constitutional Remedies** = **Article 32** (one Article): the right to move the **Supreme Court directly** to enforce Part III – **itself a Fundamental Right** (APPSC GS 2025).
[14:0:1] - **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**: Art. 32 is "**the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it**" – the Article without which the Constitution would be a **nullity**.
[14:0:2] - "A right without a remedy is a **worthless declaration**" – Art. 32 makes Part III effective, not only declaratory.
[14:0:3] - **Part of the basic structure** – cannot be abridged even by constitutional amendment. **Kesavananda Bharati (13 judges)**: judicial review under Arts. **32, 226, 227** is part of the inviolable basic structure.
[14:0:4] G2 Mains 2025 key gave "heart and soul" as Art. 14 – the correct answer is Article 32.
## SUB 1 | Clauses of Article 32
[14:1:0] TABLE head: Clause | Provision
  [14:1:0.0] 32(1) | Right to move the SC by appropriate proceedings to enforce Part III rights
  [14:1:0.1] 32(2) | SC may issue directions, orders or writs – the five named: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto (wide original jurisdiction)
  [14:1:0.2] 32(3) | Parliament may empower any other court to use these powers locally – no such law made yet
  [14:1:0.3] 32(4) | Right not suspended except as the Constitution provides (not by ordinary law)
[14:1:1] - Scope: **only Fundamental Rights**, not statutory or other constitutional rights. Jurisdiction **original but not exclusive** – concurrent with HCs; aggrieved person may choose either.
[14:1:2] - Writs began as **prerogative writs** of **English common law** (the King as "fountain of justice").
[14:1:3] - **Before 1950**, only the **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras** HCs could issue writs; Art. 226 now empowers all HCs.
[14:1:4] - **Article 13**: power of judicial review to SC and HCs. SC has **no exclusive jurisdiction** on validity of Central laws; HCs also have it.
## SUB 2 | Guardian of Fundamental Rights
[14:2:0] - **Guardian of Fundamental Rights = the Judiciary** – the **Supreme Court** is their "**protector and guarantor**" and **Guardian of the Constitution** (APPSC 2021); not the President.
[14:2:1] - Power to enforce FRs: **Supreme Court (Art. 32) and High Courts (Art. 226)**. Only SC and HCs issue writs; **both issue all five**.
## SUB 3 | The Five Writs
[14:3:0] TABLE head: Writ | Literal meaning | Issued against | Purpose
  [14:3:0.0] Habeas Corpus | "To have the body (of)" | Public authorities and private individuals | Release from unlawful detention; detained person produced before court
  [14:3:0.1] Mandamus | "We command" | Public officials, statutory bodies, inferior courts, tribunals, public corporations, govt | Compels performance of a public legal duty refused or neglected
  [14:3:0.2] Prohibition | "To forbid" / to restrain | Judicial and quasi-judicial bodies only | Higher court stops a lower court exceeding jurisdiction – while pending
  [14:3:0.3] Certiorari | "To be certified / informed" | Judicial, quasi-judicial and (since 1991) certain administrative bodies affecting rights | Quashes an order already passed; also transfers a pending case
  [14:3:0.4] Quo Warranto | "By what authority (or warrant)?" | Holder of a substantive public office created by statute or the Constitution | Stops illegal usurpation; tests legality of the claim to office
[14:3:1] - **Mandamus orders action; Prohibition orders inaction.** Mandamus = Judiciary orders the **Executive** to perform its duty.
[14:3:2] - **Certiorari** = "**postmortem**": higher court calls the subordinate court's record for review. Grounds: **lack or excess of jurisdiction**, or **error of law on the face of the record**.
[14:3:3] - Habeas Corpus, Mandamus etc. are writ jurisdiction; **review, advisory opinions and special leave** are other kinds of jurisdiction.
[14:3:4] - **Advisory jurisdiction (Art. 143):** the **President** may seek the Supreme Court's opinion on a question of law or fact of public importance; the opinion is **not binding**. The **US Supreme Court gives no advisory opinions** (it refused to advise President Washington in **1793**) – a key difference from India's Supreme Court.
[14:3:5] # Habeas Corpus
[14:3:6] - The **only writ directly against private individuals** (illegal confinement).
[14:3:7] - "**Bulwark of individual liberty**" – the **greatest safeguard of personal liberty**; quick remedy against arbitrary detention. A **High Court** can also issue it.
[14:3:8] - Not for **lawful detention**, contempt of court/legislature, or detention outside the court's jurisdiction.
[14:3:9] - APPSC 2021: producing a detained person before court = **habeas corpus**.
[14:3:10] # Quo Warranto
[14:3:11] - **Any public-minded citizen** / any interested person may seek it – petitioner **need not be aggrieved** (locus standi relaxed).
[14:3:12] - Not for **ministerial or private** office.
## SUB 4 | Limits on Writs
[14:4:0] # Mandamus Does NOT Lie Against
[14:4:1] - **Private individuals/bodies** without public duties – but it **can** lie against a **government company or statutory corporation** performing a **public function**.
[14:4:2] - **President or Governors** – **Art. 361**: not answerable to any court for official acts.
[14:4:3] - **Discretionary** duty; **legislative** functions; **contractual** obligation; **non-statutory departmental instructions**; Chief Justice of a HC acting judicially.
[14:4:4] - **Law and policy**: mandamus **cannot force the Government to make a law or policy**, but can enforce the terms of a policy **once laid down** (G2 Mains 2025 A-R: both true, R explains A).
[14:4:5] # Other Writs
[14:4:6] - **Prohibition**: not against administrative authorities, legislative bodies, private persons.
[14:4:7] - **Certiorari**: not against **legislative bodies** or **private persons**.
## SUB 5 | Prohibition vs Certiorari
[14:5:0] TABLE head: Point | Prohibition | Certiorari
  [14:5:0.0] Stage | While proceedings are pending | After an order is passed (also to transfer a pending case)
  [14:5:0.1] Nature | Preventive only | Preventive and curative
  [14:5:0.2] Effect | Stops the lower body | Quashes the order
  [14:5:0.3] Nickname | — | "Postmortem"
[14:5:1] - Common: both (traditionally) against judicial/quasi-judicial bodies; both check excess of jurisdiction.
## SUB 6 | Article 32 vs Article 226
[14:6:0] TABLE head: Point | Art. 32 (SC) | Art. 226 (HC)
  [14:6:0.0] Nature | Itself a Fundamental Right – SC cannot refuse | Constitutional, discretionary remedy
  [14:6:0.1] Scope | Only FRs (narrower) | FRs + "any other purpose" (legal rights) – wider
  [14:6:0.2] Territory | Whole of India (wider) | Own territory; beyond it if the cause of action arises inside (15th Amendment, 1963)
  [14:6:0.3] Writs | Five | Five
[14:6:1] - **HC wider in subject matter; SC wider territorially.** High Courts are in a better position to protect rights.
[14:6:2] - **Res judicata**: HC dismisses a writ **on merits** → same plea under Art. 32 barred; remedy is appeal. If not on merits, Art. 32 may be used.
[14:6:3] # Amendments and Cases
[14:6:4] TABLE head: Case / Amendment | Year | Point
  [14:6:4.0] 42nd Amendment | 1976 | Inserted Art. 32A (no challenge to State laws under Art. 32); curbed Art. 226
  [14:6:4.1] 43rd Amendment | 1977 | Omitted Arts. 32A, 131A, 144A, 226A, 228A
  [14:6:4.2] 44th Amendment | 1978 | Also restored Art. 226
  [14:6:4.3] L. Chandra Kumar | 1997 | Judicial review under Arts. 226 and 227 = basic structure
[14:6:5] - 20 Jan 2026: SC (Justices **Dipankar Datta and S.C. Sharma**) agreed to examine whether the **Enforcement Directorate** can invoke writ jurisdiction (appeals by **Kerala and Tamil Nadu** against a Kerala HC ruling).
[14:6:6] - 24 Apr 2026: in the ED-West Bengal matter, Centre defended an Art. 32 petition vs a State's plea for an **Art. 131** suit.
[14:6:7] - 16 Jul 2026: SC studied an **SOP for 24x7 urgent hearings** under Arts. 32 and 226 in threats to life and liberty.
## SUB 7 | Pil and Locus Standi
[14:7:0] - **PIL** loosens **locus standi** (only the directly aggrieved may sue) – any public-spirited person may move the court for those unable to. PIL is **not itself an FR**.
[14:7:1] - **S.P. Gupta (1981)** – key PIL case. **Justices P.N. Bhagwati** (CJ when PIL began) and **V.R. Krishna Iyer** linked with PIL.
[14:7:2] - **Epistolary jurisdiction**: letters/postcards treated as writ petitions – **Sunil Batra** (prisoner's letter).
## SUB 8 | Article 32 During Emergency; Articles 33-35
[14:8:0] - **Art. 359**: during a National Emergency the **President** may by order suspend the right to move courts for enforcement of named FRs – except **Arts. 20 and 21** (44th Amendment).
[14:8:1] - **Art. 33**: **Parliament** (not State legislatures) may restrict or modify FRs of **armed forces, police, intelligence** personnel.
[14:8:2] - **Art. 34**: restrictions on FRs while **martial law** is in force; Parliament may **indemnify** acts done under it.
[14:8:3] - **Art. 35**: **Parliament alone** legislates on specified FR matters (e.g. under Arts. 32(3), 33, 34).
## SUB 9 | Articles 29-30 – Cultural and Educational Rights
[14:9:0] - **Art. 29(1)**: any section of citizens with a distinct **language, script or culture** may **conserve** it; **29(2)**: no denial of admission to State-aided institutions on religion, race, caste, language.
[14:9:1] - **Art. 30(1)**: all minorities, **religious or linguistic**, may establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.
