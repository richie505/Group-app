=== Constitutional Government = Limited Government
- **Constitutional government** by definition = **limited government** – a government **limited by the terms of the Constitution** (UPSC 2020, 2021 ×2) – not "government by the legislature", "popular", "multi-party", "federal structure" or a head with nominal/real powers.
- "**Persons may change but rules should not change**" = principle of **constitutional government** (rule of law, not of persons).
- In a democracy the powers of the government are limited by the **Constitution**.
- **Constitutionalism**: limits + rule of law + checks and balances + judicial review + FRs; a **legislative majority cannot override constitutional limitations**.
- Not an indication of true democracy: "**rule by majority only**" – free and fair elections, respect for the individual and equality before law are.
- **PIL** is **not indispensable** for the rule of law, which existed before PIL through **Arts. 14, 32, 226**.

=== Separation of Powers – Montesquieu and the Indian Degree
- **Montesquieu**: **division of powers** best protects liberty; "**The Spirit of Laws**" (**1748**); drew on the **British** constitution.
- **Best safeguard of liberty in a liberal democracy = separation of powers** (UPSC 2021) – not a committed judiciary, centralisation or an elected government.
- **Presidential** government operates on **separation of powers**; **parliamentary** = **fusion** (ministers drawn from and responsible to the legislature). Division of powers between Centre and States = federalism.
- The organ made up of the **people's elected representatives** = the **legislature** (judges are appointed; the media is not an organ of government).
- India: **Nominal head** President; **real executive** Council of Ministers. **Ambedkar** preferred **responsibility over stability**; reasons: familiarity (1919/1935 Acts), diversity, avoiding American-style deadlock.
- India: **no rigid separation** – implicit, not absolute, **functional**; overlaps → checks and balances (**Ram Jawaya Kapur, 1955**). Separation of powers = basic feature (**Kesavananda, 1973**).
- **Art. 50 (DPSP)**: separate the judiciary from the executive **in the public services of the State**. Judiciary insulated: **Arts. 50, 121, 211, 235**.
- **Munro's Madras Regulations (1816)** reversed the Cornwallis separation – the **Collector** again got **magisterial and police powers**.

=== Legislature Over Executive
- In India the **executive is subordinate to the legislature** – the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
| Device | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Question Hour, Zero Hour | Daily watch |
| No-confidence motion | Removes the government – **Lok Sabha only** |
| Impeachment (**Art. 61**) | Removes the President |
| Collective responsibility | **Art. 75(3)** Lok Sabha; **164(2)** Assembly |

=== Judicial Checks and Overriding a Judgment
- **Judicial review** of laws and executive action: **Arts. 13, 32, 226**; an ultra vires law is **void**.
- **Art. 121**: no discussion in Parliament on an SC/HC judge's conduct except on a removal motion; **Art. 211** for States. **Arts. 122, 212**: courts cannot question legislative proceedings for irregular procedure.
- **Art. 142** complete justice – fills gaps (Vishaka before the POSH Act, 2013).
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| Ram Jawaya Kapur (1955) | No rigid separation |
| Kesavananda (1973) | Separation of powers = basic feature |
| Raj Narain (1975) | Legislature cannot decide an election dispute; **39th** Amendment validation of PM's election struck |
| Minerva Mills (1980) | 42nd Amendment bar on review struck |
| I.R. Coelho (2007) | Post-**24 April 1973** Ninth Schedule entries reviewable |
| Option to override a judgment | Limit |
|---|---|
| Constitutional amendment | Basic structure |
| Validating law | Must **remove the basis**; cannot simply declare the judgment void |
| Ninth Schedule (**76th**, TN 69%) | Coelho review |
| Review **Art. 137**; reference **Art. 143** | Art. 143 advice **not binding** |
| **1st** / **24th** / **26th** / **77th** Amendment | Overrode Champakam / Golaknath / Madhav Rao (privy purses) / Indra Sawhney |
- Parliament overrides a judgment by **changing the law or constitutional basis** – not by a simple resolution.
- **One Nation One Election Bill** (129th Amendment Bill, 2024): amends **Arts. 83, 172, 327** (and adds 82A).

=== Thinkers on the State and Limited Government
| Thinker / school | View |
|---|---|
| **Thomas Hobbes** (Leviathan, 1651) | **Absolute sovereign power** of the monarch |
| **John Locke** | Limited government, natural rights (life, liberty, property) |
| **J.-J. Rousseau** | **The Social Contract (1762)**; general will, popular sovereignty |
| **D.G. Ritchie** | **Customary (historical) rights** – rights grow out of custom |
| **Individualists** | State a **necessary evil** – keep it minimal |
| Anarchists | State an unnecessary evil – abolish it |
