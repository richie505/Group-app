[81:2:1] - Wheat needs **moderate temperature and moderate rainfall**; **macaroni (durum) wheat** suits **rainfed** conditions.
[81:2:2] - **Origins:** rice – **South-East Asia**; red gram (arhar) – **India**; **maize** – the Americas (New World), so it is **not an ancient cereal** of the Old World (rice, wheat, barley are).
[81:2:3] - **Maize** is a **C4** plant (paddy, wheat, soybean are C3) and the best fodder for **silage**.
[81:2:4] - Cereals and millets are mainly **starch**. Wheat, rice, maize, jowar = cereals; **wheat is not a millet**.
[81:2:5] - **Rajma** is the pulse that does **not** fix nitrogen (poor nodulation); **paddy** does not help N-fixation; **black gram (urad)** improves soil fertility.
[81:2:6] - Black soil grows **cotton, wheat, jowar, pulses** (APPSC dropped its "not grown in black soil" item).

[81:2:9] - **Stimulant crops:** **tea**, coffee, tobacco, opium poppy, betel nut.
[81:2:10] - **Subsistence-farmed:** **coarse cereals and rice**.

## SUB 5 | Green Revolution
[81:5:0] TABLE head: Person | Role
  [81:5:0.0] Norman Borlaug | Father of the Green Revolution (world); Mexican dwarf wheat; Nobel Peace 1970
  [81:5:0.1] M.S. Swaminathan | Father of the Green Revolution in India; HYV wheat; Evergreen Revolution
  [81:5:0.2] C. Subramaniam | Union Agriculture Minister who backed it
  [81:5:0.3] William Gaud | Coined the term (1968)
  [81:5:0.4] B.P. Pal | First DG of ICAR
[81:5:1] - **New Agricultural Strategy (mid-1960s, sixties)** after the 1965-67 droughts and **PL-480** imports; **HYV Programme from Kharif 1966**.
[81:5:2] - **Green Revolution = High Yielding Variety programme**; aim: **food-grain output through HYV seeds**, not land redistribution or exports.
[81:5:3] - Components: **HYV seeds**, fertilisers, pesticides, **irrigation**, mechanisation, MSP, **rural electrification, rural roads and marketing** (all four in the code).
[81:5:4] - Main crop: **Mexican dwarf wheat** (Lerma Rojo 64-A, Sonora-63/64, Kalyan Sona). **Wheat** benefited most in **production and productivity** (output about 2.5 times), then **rice**; pulses, oilseeds, millets lagged. Wheat yield touched about **2,000 kg/ha** (3,595 kg/ha in 2024-25).
[81:5:5] - First stage: **Punjab, Haryana, western UP**; eastern belt lagged. Phases: early 1968-81 (growth 2.3%), later 1981-92 (spread east, 3.4%).
[81:5:6] - **Limitations:** **regional disparities** (A and R both true, R not the explanation), **income disparities** (farmers with 10+ ha gained most), **soil degradation** from fertiliser, groundwater depletion; it did **not** raise all crops equally; N:P:K 10.9:4.1:1 vs ideal 4:2:1.
[81:5:7] - Procurement confined to rice, wheat, sugarcane – these use over two-thirds of urea; MSP + free power favour paddy in Punjab.
[81:5:8] - Small and marginal farmers **86.2%** of farmers, 47.3% of area (2015-16).

## SUB 6 | Cropping Systems
[81:6:0] TABLE head: System | Meaning
  [81:6:0.0] Double / multiple cropping | Two crops at different times (in succession) on the same land in a year (paddy then wheat)
  [81:6:0.1] Mixed cropping | Two or more crops simultaneously on the same land, seeds mixed, no row pattern
  [81:6:0.2] Intercropping | Crops together in definite rows (tur + jowar or maize)
  [81:6:0.3] Parallel cropping | Crops of different habit, no competition – wheat + mustard, maize + green gram
  [81:6:0.4] Sequential cropping | Crops in sequence on the same field in a year – no intercrop competition
  [81:6:0.5] Relay cropping | Next crop sown into the standing crop
  [81:6:0.6] Ratoon | Regrowth from stubble
  [81:6:0.7] Crop rotation | Planned sequence; pulse–cereal leaves fixed nitrogen
  [81:6:0.8] Mixed farming | Crops + rearing animals
[81:6:1] - **Rice–wheat** = major cropping system **by area** (Indo-Gangetic plains); UP's largest pattern too. Best cycle for eastern UP: **paddy – maize – wheat**.
[81:6:2] - **Monocropping does not protect soil fertility**; rotation, mixed cropping and fallowing do.
[81:6:3] - **Land Equivalent Ratio (LER)** measures the advantage of **intercropping over sole cropping**; sole cropping = one crop variety.
[81:6:4] - Agro-ecological cell models fit crop cycles to the **length of growing period**, with turn-around times and intercropping levels.
[81:6:5] - **Cereal yield** = output per unit of **harvested area** (kg/ha), not total production.
[81:6:6] - Wheat irrigation: **CRI (crown root initiation) stage**, 20-25 days after sowing, is the most critical – not flowering.
[81:6:7] - AP cropping intensity: **1.21 (2023-24) → 1.88 (2024-25)**; AP diversifying to **fruits, vegetables, spices, oil palm**.
[81:6:8] ~~ Sources: app notes 4:81 (sub 7); NCERT Class 9 definition of mixed cropping, LER, CRI stage: general knowledge, not checked
[81:6:9] - **AP practice pairs (APPSC key):** monocropping – **rice**; mixed cropping – **sorghum**; crop rotation – **cotton**; intercropping – **sugarcane**.
