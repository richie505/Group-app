=== Rolling Plan (1978-80)
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Suggested by | **Gunnar Myrdal**, for backward countries – "Indian Economic Planning in its Broader Setting" |
| Introduced by | **Janata Party government** (ended the Fifth Plan a year early) |
| Deputy Chairman then | **D.T. Lakdawala** – credited with introducing it in India |
| Period | **April 1978 – March 1980** (1978-80) |
| Name | Janata **Sixth Plan (1978-83)** = "the rolling plan" – the **novel idea of the draft Sixth Plan** |
| Outlay | Rs 12,177 crore; stressed **employment** |
| End | Janata fall → 1979-80 annual plan → Congress Sixth Plan from **1 April 1980** |
# Three plans in one (all four book statements are correct)
- 1. A plan for the **current year** (with the annual budget).
- 2. A plan for a fixed number of years (**3, 4 or 5**), **revised every year** as the economy requires and extended by one year.
- 3. A **perspective plan** of **10, 15 or 20 years**.
- Rolling plan **changes both its allocations and its targets every year** → flexibility; criticism: yearly revision **weakened commitment** to targets.
> Myrdal suggested – Janata introduced – Lakdawala Deputy Chairman – 1978-80. Not Indira, Rajiv or the National Front; Madhu Dandavate was Deputy Chairman only in 1990-91.

=== Objectives of Planning
- **Four long-term objectives:** **growth, modernisation, self-reliance, social justice (equity)** – plus employment and removal of poverty; the long-term objective = **all** of high standard of living, social justice and self-reliance.
- **Economic growth = principal objective**; the others are supporting.
- Chief objectives also listed in the Plans: **reduce inequalities of income and wealth**, higher growth, **removal of poverty**, employment. "**To achieve social equality**" is not a stated economic objective of the Plans.
- Major objective of the Five Year Plans (book key): **self-reliance** and less dependence on foreign sources.
- Declared aim (1950 resolution): rapid rise in the **standard of living** through efficient use of resources, more production and **employment for all**.
- **Planning** = a conscious effort of the State with a **set of goals**, a **set of means** and a **specific time period**.
- Initial strategy of the 1950s: **rapid industrialisation** (Second Plan); cornerstone = **central economic planning**.
# Socialistic pattern of society
- Adopted at the **Avadi Congress (1955)** → **Second Plan (1956-61)** and **IPR 1956**.
- Core element: **social gain, not private profit**, as the test of development (not communes or nationalising all firms).
- Planning for socialism aims at a **national minimum standard of living** for all (NPC 1938, Bombay Plan).

=== Imperative vs Indicative Planning
| Feature | Imperative (directive / target) | Indicative |
|---|---|---|
| Market mechanism | **Entirely replaced by a command hierarchy** | Plan **improves** the market |
| Ownership | State / collective | Mixed |
| Targets | Binding quotas, allocation, prices | Guidelines and signals |
| Examples | **USSR (1928, Stalin)**, China | Mixed economies; France |
| India | Public sector in early Plans | From the **Eighth Plan (1992-97)** after 1991 |
- A **Five-Year Plan** is an **indicative plan of action** reflecting the government's intent.
- Soviet collectivisation (Stalin): collective farms = **Kolkhoz** (state farms = sovkhoz).
- **Regional planning** first in the USA (1916+; Tennessee Valley).

=== Perspective Planning and Plan Horizons
| Plan | Horizon |
|---|---|
| Annual plan | Short term – one year |
| **Five Year Plan** | **Medium term** |
| **Perspective plan** | **Long term – 15 to 20 years** or more; five-year and annual plans carved out of it |
- Perspective planning was a **Planning Commission function**.
- **Long-term perspective plan 1985-2000** accompanied the **Seventh Plan**.
- **AP now:** **Swarna Andhra Vision 2047** – GSDP **Rs 308.11 lakh crore by 2047-48**; ten "**Padi Sutralu**"; targets (10 Feb 2026): **15%** growth, GSDP **Rs 31 lakh crore**, PCI Rs 5.15 lakh by 2030.

=== Other Types of Planning
| Pair | Distinction |
|---|---|
| Centralised vs decentralised | One central authority, top-down vs decisions shared with lower governments / private units within a broad policy |
| Financial vs physical | Allocation in **money terms** vs in **real terms** (materials, manpower, output) |
| Direction vs **inducement** | Command (imperative) vs **incentives** guiding private units (indicative) |
| **Democratic** vs totalitarian | Through elected bodies and consent (India) vs state command |

=== Plans – Thrust, Models and Financing
| Plan | Main thrust / model |
|---|---|
| First (from **1 April 1951**) | **Agriculture**, irrigation, power; **Harrod-Domar** (growth = savings rate ÷ capital-output ratio); 3.6% achieved vs 2.1% target |
| Second (1956-61) | **Rapid industrialisation**, socialistic pattern – **Feldman-Mahalanobis** (heavy industry, public sector, import substitution; **not export promotion**); forex crisis cuts **1958** |
| Third (1961-66) | Self-reliance, food self-sufficiency |
| Fourth (1969-74) | **Target-group approach** (SFDA, MFAL, DPAP); reduce dependence on **foreign aid** |
| Fifth (1974-79) | Self-reliance + **raising consumption of the poor**; direct attack on poverty; **Tribal Sub-Plan** |
| Sixth (1980-85) | Poverty alleviation; **Minimum Needs Programme**, IRDP |
| Ninth (1997-2002) | **Seven Basic Minimum Services** |
| Twelfth (2012-17) | Faster, sustainable, more inclusive growth – **last** |
- **Trickle-down** (First–Third Plans; Community Development Programme 1952) → target-group (Fourth) → direct attack (Fifth, Sixth).
- **Wage-goods model:** **C.N. Vakil and P.R. Brahmananda** – alternative to Mahalanobis.
- **Plan financing:** domestic budgetary resources, PSU surpluses, **market borrowing**, **deficit financing (borrowing from the RBI = money creation)**, foreign aid.
- **Eighth Plan (1992-97):** largest source of public sector outlay = **government borrowings** (Rs 2,02,255 crore of Rs 4,34,100 crore, **46.6%**); deficit financing only Rs 20,000 crore.
