=== Animal Husbandry Practice – Breeding, Health, Poultry and Meat
- **Artificial insemination (AI):** semen of **superior bulls can reach every village**, it **avoids contagious (venereal) diseases** and it is **economical** – so "all of the above".
  - **First scientific AI: Lazzaro Spallanzani** (Italy, in a dog, 1780); Ivanoff (Russia) developed it for farm animals.
  - **First AI in India: Palace Dairy Farm, Mysore (1939)**, by Sampath Kumaran; Izzatnagar (UP) is the seat of IVRI.
| Delivery / reproduction term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Calving / Lambing / Kidding / Whelping** | Cow / sheep (ewe) / goat / dog gives birth |
| **Prolapse** | Falling out of part of the womb (uterus or vagina) before or after calving |
| **Pyometra** | **Pus in the uterus** (endometritis = inflammation of its lining) |
| **Oestrus in buffalo** | Lasts about **24-36 hours** (longer than in cows) |
| **Service period (cross-bred cow)** | About **3 months (60-90 days)** after calving → calving interval about 12-13 months |
- **Small ruminants = sheep and goats**; cattle and buffalo are large ruminants; horses, donkeys, pigs and rabbits are not ruminants.
- **Epidural anaesthesia in cows: lignocaine (2%)** – thiopentone and propofol are general anaesthetics, morphine an opioid.
- **Ranikhet (Newcastle) disease** of poultry shows **respiratory, nervous and gastrointestinal signs** – "all of these".
- **Small dog breed: pug** (toy); boxer and Dalmatian are large, bulldog medium.
| Poultry practice | Norm |
|---|---|
| Storing hatching eggs | Best set within **1 week** of laying (cool store) |
| Egg turning in the setter | At least **6-8 times a day** |
| Setter → hatcher transfer | **18th day** (chicken eggs hatch in 21 days) |
| Floor space, layer on deep litter | About **2 sq ft per hen** |
| Meat and slaughter-house norm | Value |
|---|---|
| Thawing meat for feeding zoo animals | **4-6 °C** (in a refrigerator, not at room temperature) |
| Shelf life of canned meat | About **2 years** |
| Floor gradient in a slaughter house | About **2 cm per metre** (1 in 48 to 1 in 60 towards the drains) |
- **Rotational grazing:** livestock moved through a **sequence of paddocks (six or more)** several times a season so each paddock rests and regrows; leader-follower and creep grazing are its variants.

=== Horticulture – Fruits, Mango, Spices and GI
- **Fruit ranks:** India is the **2nd largest producer of fruits and vegetables (China 1st)** – so "India ranks first in fruit production" is false; India is the largest producer of **mango, banana, guava, papaya**, spices, coconut and cashew.
- **Top fruit by output in India: banana** (ahead of mango); **national fruit: mango**.
- **Mango exports:** **Alphonso** (Maharashtra, Konkan) is the most exported and the preferred export variety; Kesar, Banganapalli and Totapuri follow.
| Mango GI | Year / State |
|---|---|
| **Banganapalle** (AP) | **GI in May 2017** |
| **Marathwada Kesar** | GI 30 Nov 2016 (Maharashtra) |
| Laxman Bhog, Himsagar | GI 2008 (West Bengal) |
- All four of these mangoes are now GI-registered; before May 2017 **Banganapalle** was the one not yet registered.
- **GI tag** improves the **marketability** (premium price, protection from imitation) – it is not ornamental.
- **'Kanchan'** is an improved variety of **aonla / amla (Indian gooseberry)**.
- **Turmeric:** India is the **largest producer, consumer and exporter** (over 70% of world output); **30+ varieties**; major States **Maharashtra, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu** (and AP) – all three UPSC 2025 statements correct. **National Turmeric Board – Nizamabad**.
- **Flowers:** the 2004 key said **Karnataka** (old floriculture leader around Bengaluru); now **Tamil Nadu** leads (about 21%), then Karnataka (16%), Madhya Pradesh, West Bengal.
- **ICAR-IIHR** (Indian Institute of Horticultural Research) is at **Hesaraghatta, Bengaluru (Karnataka)**; it held the National Horticultural Fair 2023.
- **FPO (Food Products Order) mark**: mandatory on processed fruit products (jams, squashes, pickles); ISI is for industrial goods, BEE for energy labels, Hallmark for gold.

=== Food Processing – SAMPADA, Mega Food Parks, Operation Greens
- **PM Kisan SAMPADA Yojana (2017)**: umbrella scheme of the **Ministry of Food Processing Industries (MoFPI)**; aim – **supplement agriculture, modernise processing and decrease agri-waste**. Its 'Unit' scheme: **32 projects sanctioned (2020)**.
- **Mega Food Parks:** to **improve infrastructure for food processing**; cluster approach linking farmers, processors and retailers; aims (UPSC 2011): **good infrastructure, more processing of perishables / less wastage, and emerging eco-friendly technologies** – all three.
  - Capital grant **50%** of project cost (75% in difficult / ITDP areas), ceiling ₹50 crore.
  - **Italy's first Mega Food Park in India (June 2021): Fanidhar, Mehsana (Gujarat)**.
- **Operation Greens (2018)**: on the lines of Operation Flood, for **Tomato-Onion-Potato (TOP)** price stabilisation; under **MoFPI**; promotes FPOs, agri-logistics and processing.
- **PMFME**: micro food units under **One District One Product (ODOP)**.
- **APEDA (1985, Ministry of Commerce)** promotes agri exports – e.g. an awareness camp for **potato** exports in **Agra** (Jan 2020).
- **Processed food exports** = about **23%** of agri-food exports (23.4% in 2023-24, up from 13.7% in 2014-15; APPSC 2025 key "around 23").

- **India has the largest livestock population in the world** (not China, Brazil or USA).
- **20th Livestock Census (2019)**: **535.78 million** (+4.6%); cattle 192.49 million; buffalo 109.85 million.
- **21st Livestock Census**: Oct 2024 – Feb 2025; **16 species**, **219** indigenous breeds.

=== Dairy – Operation Flood, Cooperatives and Milk Science
- **White Revolution = increase in milk production**; **Operation Flood** (NDDB) is the programme behind it – related to **milk production**, not floods or irrigation.
- **Operation Flood (1970-1996)**: three phases; world's biggest dairy development programme; built on the **Anand pattern cooperatives (Amul)** – the engine of its success; India passed the USA as the largest milk producer in 1998.
- **NDDB**: set up **1965 at Anand, Gujarat**.
- **Co-operative dairying is most successful in Gujarat** (Anand, Amul, GCMMF) – the pioneer of India's cooperative dairy movement.
- **Milk:** India 1st (about 25% of world), 471 g/day per capita; top States UP, Rajasthan, MP, Gujarat, Maharashtra. Yield **per cow is low in India**; **Denmark** has among the world's highest yields (ahead of the Netherlands and Great Britain).
- **Milk science:** milk is a **colloid (emulsion)**; **skimmed milk has a higher specific gravity than whole milk** (fat, the lightest part, is removed: about 1.035 vs 1.032).
- **HTST pasteurisation: 72 °C for 15 seconds** (batch/LTLT: 63 °C for 30 minutes).
- India also leads world output of **butter and ghee**.

=== Fisheries – Blue Revolution, Production and PMMSY
- **Blue Revolution = fish production** (pisciculture); NFDB (2006, Hyderabad) promotes it.
- **China is the largest fish producer** (capture and aquaculture); **India 2nd** (about 8-9% of world); India **leads the world in inland capture** (2.2 MT, SOFIA 2026).
- Output: 95.79 lakh t (2013-14) → **197.75 lakh t (2024-25)**; fisheries = about 7.43% of agri GVA.
- **Inland fisheries** = raising and capturing fish in **fresh water**; inland = **about 77%** of output (15.16 MT of 19.78 MT, 2024-25), and most of it is **inland aquaculture (culture fisheries)** – capture is only about 2.2 MT. So the most important fish industry today is **culture inland**, not natural inland or deep-sea.
- **PMMSY**: 2020, **₹20,050 crore**, FY 2020-21 to 2024-25, extended to 2025-26; Central Sector 100%; Centrally Sponsored 60:40, 90:10 NE/Himalayan, 100% UTs.
- **PM-MKSSY**: **Central Sector** sub-scheme under PMMSY (formalisation, micro-enterprises).
- **FIDF**: fishing harbours and landing centres; credit guarantee up to ₹12.5 crore; KCC limit for fisheries raised to ₹5 lakh.
- **MPEDA**: Act of Parliament **1972**; **HQ Kochi** (not Kolkata); **Ministry of Commerce and Industry**; seafood exports ₹62,408 crore (2024-25).
- Fish growth rate 2024-25 (APPSC AO 2025 options 5.3 / 10.2 / 15.6 / 20.1): no official figure matching these options was found; production grew about 7.5% in 2024-25.
~~ Sources: PMF Lens July 2026 (2) p.70-71 (India 2nd, 8%, 197.75 lakh t, MPEDA, FIDF); PMF Human & Economic Geography 05 p.98-99 (China largest; inland and aquaculture 70-80%); weareaquaculture.com (Handbook 2025: inland 77%, 15.16 MT); downtoearth.org.in (SOFIA 2026: inland catch 2.2 MT); app notes 3:60 sub 6

=== Sericulture and Apiculture
- **China is the largest silk producer; India 2nd** (with China, the bulk of world silk); India is the **only country producing all five commercial silks** – mulberry, tropical tasar, oak tasar, eri, muga.
- **Mulberry silk** = the bulk of world raw silk (about **90-95%**).
| Silk | Leading State |
|---|---|
| **Mulberry** | **Karnataka** (about 50%); then AP, TN, WB |
| Tasar | Jharkhand |
| Muga, eri | Assam |
- **'Kausheya'** – ancient Sanskrit term for **silk** (from kosha, the cocoon).
- **Honey bees:** the most common (domesticated) Indian species is **Apis indica (Apis cerana indica)**; Apis dorsata = rock bee, A. florea = little bee, A. mellifera = European (Italian) bee.
- **National Beekeeping and Honey Mission (2020)**: **Sweet Revolution** via the **National Bee Board**.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography - APPSC 10 p.10 (five silks, India 2nd after China, Karnataka 50% of mulberry); AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.89 (mulberry States); general knowledge (Kausheya, Apis species, mulberry share)

=== Horticulture Missions – NHM and MIDH
- **National Horticulture Mission (NHM)**: launched **May 2005** (2005-06); covered all States **except the North-Eastern and Himalayan States** (J&K, HP, Arunachal had the separate HMNEH) – so **Uttar Pradesh** is an NHM State.
  - Objectives: **higher growth in horticulture**, **post-harvest management**, **human resource development**; mere "providing financial assistance" is the means, not an objective.
- **MIDH** (Mission for Integrated Development of Horticulture): Centrally Sponsored, **since 2014-15**; sub-schemes **NHM**, **HMNEH** (11 NE and Himalayan States), NHB, CDB, CIH (Nagaland); 60:40, 90:10 NE/Himalayan, 100% for national agencies.
- Indo-Israel Centre of Excellence for Vegetables and Flowers, **Kuppam** (MIDH + MASHAV).
~~ Sources: PMF Lens July 2026 (2) p.72-73 (MIDH 2014-15, sub-schemes, NHM excludes NE/Himalayan); app notes 3:60 sub 2

=== Allied Sectors – Growth and Share
- Farm growth now comes from allied sectors: **livestock (7.1%)** and **fishing and aquaculture (8.8%)** (ES 2025-26) – fisheries faster; ES also says allied sectors grow at a stable 5-6%.
- **Livestock GVA** (2023-24): **₹15.06 lakh crore** = **30.87%** of agri GVA; **12.77%** nominal CAGR (FY15-FY24).
- **Fisheries**: fastest-growing allied sub-sector – **9.08% a year since 2014-15**.
- **Horticulture**: about **one-third (33%)** of agri GVA on under 14% of area; output **367.72 MT (2024-25)** > foodgrains **357.73 MT**.
- Agri exports (2024-25): record **$51.9 billion** = 11.86% of merchandise exports; **processed food ≈ 23%** of agri exports.
