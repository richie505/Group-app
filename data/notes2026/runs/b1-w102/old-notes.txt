## SUB 0 | Muhammad Ghori and the Battles of Tarain
[102:0:0] TABLE head: Battle | Year | Opponent | Victor
  [102:0:0.0] Kayadara / Kasahrada, near Mount Abu (Gujarat campaign) | 1178 | Mularaja II (Bhimdev II), Chaulukya/Solanki; regent mother Naika Devi | Gujarat – Ghori's first defeat
  [102:0:0.1] First Tarain (near Karnal, Haryana) | 1191 | Prithviraj Chauhan (Prithviraja III) | Prithviraj – Ghori wounded, barely escaped
  [102:0:0.2] Second Tarain | 1192 | Prithviraj Chauhan | Ghori – turning point; foundation of Turkish rule
  [102:0:0.3] Chandawar (near Firozabad, UP, on the Yamuna) | 1194 | Jayachandra (Jaichand), Gahadavala of Kannauj | Ghori (army under Aibak)
[102:0:1] - **Ghori:** Shihabuddin (Muizzuddin) **Muhammad Ghori**, Ghurid, took Ghazni 1173; entered India by the **Gomal Pass**; aimed at **conquest and permanent occupation**, unlike Mahmud.
[102:0:2] - **Route:** **Multan (1175, first invasion)** → Peshawar (c. 1179) → Sindh (c. 1182) → Punjab and Lahore (1186, from Khusru Malik, last Ghaznavid).
[102:0:3] - **First defeat (1178):** by **Mularaja II / Bhimdev II** of Gujarat (books also write "Bhima II", "Baghel Bhim") – **not Prithviraj**, who beat him only in 1191.
[102:0:4] - **Cause of Tarain I:** Ghori's attack on **Tabarhinda (Bhatinda)**; Prithviraj led a confederacy of Rajput princes.
[102:0:5] - **Tarain II (1192):** Ghori returned with 1,20,000 men; **Jaichand refused to help** Prithviraj; Turkish **cavalry speed, organisation and mounted archery** beat the larger Rajput army. It **laid the foundation of Muslim (Turkish) rule** – not the Panipat battles.
[102:0:6] - **1192:** the Central Asian ruler who **conquered North India in 1192** = **Shihabuddin Muhammad Ghori** – not Mahmud (raider), Jalaluddin Mangbarni or Genghis Khan.
[102:0:7] - **Coins:** Ghori's coins bear **Goddess Lakshmi** on one side and the **Kalma in Arabic** on the other.
[102:0:8] - **Aibak:** Ghori's slave, **Master of Stables**; his representative in India after 1192; on Ghori's death (1206, near Jhelum) became the **first Sultan**, capital **Lahore** – the Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526).
[102:0:9] # Prithviraj Chauhan
[102:0:10] - **Prithviraj Chauhan = Prithviraja III** (Rai Pithora), Chahamana of **Ajmer and Delhi** (c. 1177-1192) – not Prithviraja I or II.
[102:0:11] - Defeated Chandela **Paramardi**, Chaulukya Bhima II and Gahadavala Jaichand; after 1192 reinstated as a vassal, later executed; son Govindaraja IV moved to **Ranthambore**.
[102:0:12] - **"The last Sun of Indian glory"** – Prithviraj Chauhan (B.N. Luniya).

## SUB 1 | Arab Conquest of Sindh (712)
[102:1:0] - **First Muslim invaders** of India: the **Arabs**, under **Muhammad bin Qasim**, Sindh, **712** – before the Ghaznavids and Ghurids (not Persians).
[102:1:1] - **First successful Muslim invader:** **Muhammad bin Qasim** – not Mahmud of Ghazni, Ghori or Aibak.
[102:1:2] - **Year:** the expedition left in 711 and took Debal and Sindh in **712**; so for "674 / 1013 / 711" the answer is **none of these** (712).
[102:1:3] - **Qasim:** an **Arab** (not Turk, Mongol or Turk-Afghan); nephew and son-in-law of **Al-Hajjaj bin Yusuf**, the Umayyad governor of Iraq; sent by **Caliph al-Walid** at the age of **17**.
[102:1:4] - **Ruler defeated:** **Raja Dahir** (Dahar), killed; capital **Alor (Aror)** – not Brahmanabad, which was only one of the places taken.
[102:1:5] - **Places taken:** **Debal, Nirun, Sehwan, Brahmanabad, Multan** – not Somnath (Mahmud, 1025).
[102:1:6] - **First contact with Islam:** earlier, through **Arab traders on the Malabar coast** (7th century) – the 712 conquest was the first political contact.
[102:1:7] # Chachnama
[102:1:8] - Arabic chronicle of the Arab conquest of **Sindh**; original lost; the existing **13th-century Persian translation** is by **Muhammad Ali bin Abu Bakr Kufi** – not Aufi or Shams-i-Siraj.
[102:1:9] - Records Dahir's widowed queen fighting on and then immolating herself with the other women.
[102:1:10] - Other sources: **Futuh al-Buldan** (Al-Biladuri, 9th c.), Muruj-ul-Zehab (Al-Masudi, 10th c.).
[102:1:11] # Causes and Effects
[102:1:12] TABLE head: Factor | Fact
  [102:1:12.0] Causes | Arab maritime interests, expansion, piracy against Arab ships
  [102:1:12.1] Religion | No mass Islamisation; Hindus and Buddhists given dhimmi status
  [102:1:12.2] Limited reach | Confined to Sindh and Multan by the Thar and Aravallis, strong Pratiharas, Rashtrakutas, Palas, weak Arab resources
  [102:1:12.3] Knowledge transfer | Indian decimal and place-value maths, astronomy, medicine → Islamic world → Europe
[102:1:13] - **Nagabhata I (Pratihara)** drove back the Arabs; Arab writers said the **Pratiharas** had the **strongest cavalry** in India.

## SUB 2 | Mahmud of Ghazni and the Ghaznavids
[102:2:0] TABLE head: Person | Role
  [102:2:0.0] Alptigin (Alp-Tegin) | Turkish slave-general of the Samanids; seized Ghazni (962) – a principality still under the Samanids
  [102:2:0.1] Sabuktigin (977-997) | Founder of the Ghaznavid dynasty proper ("real founder")
  [102:2:0.2] Mahmud (998-1030) | The raider: 17 raids, 1000-1027
[102:2:1] - **Founder (PYQ):** **Sabuktigin** founded the Ghaznavid dynasty (PMF; Britannica); some guide books key Alptigin, who only seized Ghazni.
[102:2:2] - **Motive:** **plunder of temple wealth** to fund his Central Asian wars – he **did not seek a permanent empire** (so for "17 invasions because he wanted a permanent empire": A true, R false).
[102:2:3] - **Raids:** Hindu Shahi **Jayapala** beaten 1001; **Battle of Waihind (1008-09)** – **Anandapala** (Hindu Shahi) vs Mahmud; **Nagarkot (Kangra) 1009**; **Thanesar** (Chakraswamin temple) **1014**; **Mathura** and **Kanauj 1018** (the Pratihara king submitted); **Somnath 1025-26**.
[102:2:4] - **Somnath (1025):** raided under Solanki **Bhima I (Bhimadeva I)**, who fled to Kanthkot and **reconstructed the temple** after Mahmud left; Kumarapala later rebuilt it in stone.
[102:2:5] - **Chandelas:** strongest resistance; **Vidyadhara** faced Mahmud (1019, 1022) and was **not defeated** (peace made) – Dhanga died c. 1002 and **Jayashakti** (9th c.) lived long before Mahmud.
[102:2:6] - **Coins:** Mahmud issued **silver coins** with **Arabic** on one side and a **Sanskrit legend (Devanagari)** on the other.
[102:2:7] - **Title:** Yamini-ud-Daulah from the Abbasid caliph – hence the **Yamini dynasty**.
[102:2:8] - **Effects:** Hindu Shahi barrier destroyed; Punjab and the north-west frontier opened. After Mahmud the Ghaznavids declined under **Seljuk** pressure; Ghori ended the dynasty (1186).
[102:2:9] - **Latest (January 2026):** **Somnath Swabhiman Parv** – 1,000 years since Mahmud's attack on Somnath.
[102:2:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.19-22; Rocket Indian History 01 p.15 (coins), p.78; Britannica "Ghaznavid dynasty"; PMF Lens 2026-01 (Somnath Swabhiman Parv); app notes 1:102 (sub 2)
[102:2:11] - **First to resist Mahmud:** the Hindu Shahi rulers **Jayapala** and **Anandapala**.
## SUB 3 | Scholars of Mahmud's Court – Al-Biruni, Firdausi, Utbi
[102:3:0] TABLE head: Scholar | Work / role
  [102:3:0.0] Al-Biruni | Kitab-ul-Hind (Tahqiq-i-Hind); Kitab Patanjal (Arabic Yoga-Sutra)
  [102:3:0.1] Utbi | Court historian; Kitab-ul-Yamini (Tarikh-i-Yamini)
  [102:3:0.2] Firdausi ("Homer of the East") | Shahnama
  [102:3:0.3] Baihaqi | Tarikh-i-Subuktigin
  [102:3:0.4] Unsuri | Poet laureate (Malik-ush-Shuara)
  [102:3:0.5] Uzari, Asjadi, Farrukhi | Persian court poets
[102:3:1] - **Court historian of Mahmud:** **Utbi** – not Hasan Nizami (Aibak; Taj-ul-Maasir), Firdausi (poet) or Chand Bardai (Prithviraj).
[102:3:2] - **Shahnama:** by **Firdausi** at **Mahmud Ghaznavi's** court (one PYQ misspells him "Farista").
[102:3:3] - **Kitab-ul-Hind / Tahqiq-i-Hind:** **Al-Biruni** – not Hasan Nizami, Minhaj-us-Siraj or Shams-i-Siraj Afif.
[102:3:4] - **When he came:** with Mahmud, in the **11th century AD**; an objective account of 11th-century India.
[102:3:5] - **Ghurid-era chronicles (not Mahmud's court):** **Taj-ul-Maasir** – **Hasan Nizami**; **Tabaqat-i-Nasiri** – **Minhaj-us-Siraj**.
[102:3:6] - **Historian who came with Mahmud:** **Al-Biruni** – Ferishta (16th c.), Afif (Firoz Tughlaq) and Ibn Battuta (Muhammad Tughlaq) are later.
[102:3:7] # Al-Biruni
[102:3:8] - Born **973** in **Khwarizm (Khiva)**; Sanskrit scholar, mathematician (trigonometry), astronomer; "father of Indian historical writing" (Indology).
[102:3:9] - Kitab-ul-Hind translated into English by **Edward Sachau**.
[102:3:10] - **"Not correct" statement (PYQ):** "He was a **secular** author" – he wrote as a Muslim scholar and has no word of sympathy for the Somnath victims.

## SUB 4 | Causes of Turkish Success; Invasions in Order
[102:4:0] TABLE head: Factor | Point
  [102:4:0.0] Political | No political unity in North India; rival Rajput states after the Pratihara collapse
  [102:4:0.1] Military | Superior war technique: mobile cavalry, mounted archers, organisation vs elephant-reliant armies
  [102:4:0.2] Social | R.S. Sharma: "Indian feudalism" – feudal levies, sub-infeudation, closed village economy; caste limited fighting to few groups
[102:4:1] - **A-R (UPPCS 2018, 2024):** A "Turkish invasions were successful" and R "no political unity in North India" – **both true, R explains A**.
[102:4:2] - **Why Ghori won (APPSC G1):** **superior war technology / military organisation of the Turks** – not a larger army (Prithviraj's was larger), local support or treachery.
[102:4:3] TABLE head: Order | Invader | Years
  [102:4:3.0] 1 | Muhammad bin Qasim | 712
  [102:4:3.1] 2 | Mahmud Ghaznavi | 1000-1027
  [102:4:3.2] 3 | Muhammad Ghori | 1175-1206
  [102:4:3.3] 4 | Genghis Khan | 1221 (Indus, chasing Jalaluddin Mangbarni)
  [102:4:3.4] 5 | Timur | 1398
[102:4:4] - **Chronology:** Mahmud → Ghori → Genghis Khan → Timur (**2, 3, 1, 4**).

## SUB 5 | Bakhtiyar Khalji – Bihar and Bengal
[102:5:0] - **Ikhtiyaruddin Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji:** slave-general of Ghori (under Aibak); **conquered Bihar and Bengal (1193-1203)** – not Aibak, Iltutmish or Yaldauz.
[102:5:1] - **Real founder of Turk rule in Bihar.**
[102:5:2] - Sacked **Nalanda**, **Vikramashila** and **Odantapuri** – Nalanda was destroyed by Bakhtiyar, not by Mahmud, Aibak or the later Sultans.
[102:5:3] - Entered **Nadiya** (1204), drove **Lakshmana Sena** to East Bengal; ruled from **Lakhnauti**; failed Tibet expedition 1205; killed 1206.
[102:5:4] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian History 01 p.76; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.24; app notes 1:102 (sub 5)

[105:0:0] - **Name:** born **Bahauddin** (c. 1205); regnal title **Ghiyasuddin Balban** (CGPSC 2004; Chhattisgarh 2004).
[105:0:1] - **Rise:** slave bought by Iltutmish → **water-carrier** → member of **the Forty** → **Amir-i-Shikar** (under Razia) → **naib** (principal minister) of **Nasiruddin Mahmud**, who gave him the title **Ulugh Khan** (IAS 2006).
[105:0:2] - **Imaduddin Raihan:** leader of the **Indian Muslim** group who briefly displaced Balban in Nasiruddin Mahmud's reign (MPPSC 2002).
[105:0:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [105:0:3.0] Zil-i-Ilahi | Sultan = shadow of God
  [105:0:3.1] Sijda | Prostration before the Sultan
  [105:0:3.2] Paibos | Kissing the Sultan's feet
  [105:0:3.3] Nauroz (Nawruz) | Persian festival introduced at court by Balban (IAS 1993; SSC 2006) – not Alauddin, Iltutmish or Firuz
[105:0:4] - **Court** copied the **Iranian kings**; strict etiquette, aloofness.
[105:0:5] - **Blood and iron:** broke **the Forty** by promoting **junior Turks**; efficient spies; harsh justice – amir **Malik Baqbaq** (governor of Badaun) **publicly flogged** for killing a servant (UPPSC 2011).
[105:0:6] - **Army:** created the military department **Diwan-i-Arz**, separating it from **Diwan-i-Wizarat** (finance) (RAS 2018); **outposts and forts** against the Mongols.
[105:0:7] - **⚠ Iltutmish created the Forty; Balban broke it.**
[105:0:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 03 p.56-57; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.33-34; app notes 1:105 (sub 4, 5)
## SUB 1 | Iltutmish – Capital, Coins and the Mongol Threat
[105:1:0] - **Capital:** moved from **Lahore to Delhi** (UPPSC 2016) – first Sultan to make **Delhi** the capital and issue **regular coins** (UPPSC Mains 2014).
[105:1:1] - **Coins:** purely **Arabic** silver **tanka** and copper **jital** – basis of the later rupee.
[105:1:2] - **Chengiz (Genghis) Khan** – original name **Temujin (Temuchin)** (UPPSC 2015) – reached the **Indus (1221)** chasing **Jalaluddin Mangbarni** of Khwarazm; Iltutmish **refused him shelter**, so the Mongols turned back. Mongols seen on the Indus **for the first time** in Iltutmish's reign (UPPSC 1993; CGPSC 2013).
[105:1:3] - **Rivals:** **Tajuddin Yildiz** (beaten at **Tarain, 1216**) and **Nasiruddin Qabacha** (Multan, Uch; fought for Lahore).
[105:1:4] - **Bihar:** after defeating Iwaz Khalji of Bengal, Iltutmish made **Malik (Alauddin) Jani** the **first governor of Bihar** (BPSC 2008).
[105:1:5] - **Investiture ("mansur")** from the **Abbasid Caliph** of Baghdad – **1229**.
[105:1:6] TABLE head: Institution | Meaning
  [105:1:6.0] Turkan-i-Chahalgani | "The Forty" – Turkish military elite (not Persian revenue clerks)
  [105:1:6.1] Iqta | Revenue assignment to officers (muqtis) instead of cash salary
[105:1:7] - **Buildings:** completed the **Qutb Minar** (begun by Aibak; dedicated to **Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki**); **Sultan Ghari** tomb → "**Father of tomb building**".
[105:1:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 03 p.54-56; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.31-32
## SUB 2 | Qutbuddin Aibak (1206-1210)
[105:2:0] - **Origin:** born in **Turkestan**; sold to the **Qazi of Nishapur**, then slave of **Muhammad Ghori**; **Amir-i-Akhur** (Master of Stables) → general; Ghori's representative after **Tarain II (1192)**.
[105:2:1] - **Titles:** **Lakh Bakhsh** ("giver of lakhs"), and **Quran Khwan / "Quran Khan"** (reciter of the Quran) (JPSC 2003) – **Zil-i-Ilahi** was Balban's.
[105:2:2] - **Capital:** **Lahore**.
[105:2:3] - **Buildings:** began the **Qutb Minar**; **Quwwat-ul-Islam** mosque (first mosque in Delhi); **Adhai Din ka Jhonpra** (Ajmer). Patron of **Hasan Nizami**.
[105:2:4] - **Death (1210):** at **Lahore**, **fell from his horse while playing chaugan (polo)** – not stabbed, not killed by Yildiz, not at Kalinjar (UPSC 2003); buried near **Anarkali Bazaar**.
[105:2:5] - **Polo** was brought to India by the **Turks** (UPPSC 2001).
[105:2:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 03 p.53-54; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.31
## SUB 3 | Razia Sultan (1236-1240)
[105:3:0] - **First and only woman ruler** of the Delhi Sultanate – the "first woman ruler of (medieval) India" in exam keys (UPPSC 2004; SSC 2017) – before Chand Bibi, Durgavati, Nur Jahan.
[105:3:1] - **Ways:** discarded **purdah**, wore **male attire** (kuba, kulah), hunted, led the army.
[105:3:2] - **Opposition:** the **Turkish nobles** (the Forty) resented her favour to **Yaqut**, an **Abyssinian** (Amir-i-Akhur) → her fall (UP Lower 2004).
[105:3:3] - **1240:** **Altuniya**, governor of **Bhatinda (Tabarhinda)**, revolted → Yaqut killed, Razia imprisoned; she **married Altuniya**, marched on Delhi and was **killed** (1240).
[105:3:4] - She made **Balban Amir-i-Shikar** (not Diwan-i-Arz).
[105:3:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 03 p.56; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.32-33
## SUB 4 | Mamluk Lines, Rulers and the Turkish Nobility
[105:4:0] - **Mamluk** = Arabic "**owned**" – an owned **military slave**; Slave dynasty ruled **1206-1290**.
[105:4:1] - **Ruling class:** most amirs and Sultans of the Sultanate were **Turks** (Turko-Afghan) (UPPSC 1991).
[105:4:2] TABLE head: Line | Period | Founder
  [105:4:2.0] Qutbi | 1206-1211 | Qutbuddin Aibak
  [105:4:2.1] First Ilbari | 1211-1266 | Iltutmish
  [105:4:2.2] Second Ilbari | 1266-1290 | Balban
[105:4:3] - **Order:** Aibak → Aram Shah → **Iltutmish** → **Ruknuddin Firuz** → **Razia** → Bahram Shah → Masud → **Nasiruddin Mahmud** (1246-66; Tabaqat-i-Nasiri named after him) → **Balban** → **Kaiqubad** → infant **Kaimurs** (last).
[105:4:4] - **End:** **Jalaluddin Firoz Khalji** crowned himself at **Kilokhari (1290)** → **Khalji** dynasty.
[105:4:5] TABLE head: Dynasty | Period
  [105:4:5.0] Mamluk | 1206-1290
  [105:4:5.1] Khalji | 1290-1320
  [105:4:5.2] Tughlaq | 1320-1414
  [105:4:5.3] Sayyid | 1414-1451
  [105:4:5.4] Lodi | 1451-1526
[105:4:6] - **⚠ Odd one out:** Ruknuddin, Masud, Nasiruddin Mahmud **are** Mamluks; **Jalaluddin Khalji is not**.

=== NOTES 1:106 | Khalji Dynasty - Jalaluddin, Alauddin's Conquests, Malik Kafur's Deccan Campaigns
## SUB 0 | Malik Kafur and the Deccan Campaigns
[106:0:0] - **Origin:** a eunuch slave seized at **Cambay** by **Nusrat Khan** in the **Gujarat campaign (1299)** and presented to Alauddin; called **Hazar Dinari** because his first master had **bought him for 1,000 dinars**; rose to **Malik Naib**.
[106:0:1] - **Commander** of all of Alauddin's **Deccan campaigns** (WBCS 2020).
[106:0:2] TABLE head: Year | Kingdom | Ruler | Result
  [106:0:2.0] 1307-08 | Devagiri (Yadava) | Ramachandra | Taken to Delhi; vassal, tribute
  [106:0:2.1] 1309-10 | Warangal (Kakatiya) | Prataparudra II | Treaty and annual tribute; loot on 1,000 camels
  [106:0:2.2] 1310-11 | Dwarasamudra (Hoysala) | Vira Ballala III | Submitted
  [106:0:2.3] 1311 | Madurai (Pandya) | Vira Pandya / Sundara Pandya | Sacked; Kakatiya troops aided Kafur
[106:0:3] - **Order:** **Devagiri → Warangal → Dwarasamudra → Madurai**.

=== NOTES 1:107 | Alauddin Khalji's Market Control, Revenue and Military Reforms
## SUB 0 | Market Control – Aim, Markets and Officials
[107:0:0] - **Chain:** Mongol invasions → **large standing army paid in cash** → **low fixed salaries** → **fixed low prices** of daily goods → **soldiers live comfortably on low pay** (APPSC G2 2017; RRB 2002).
[107:0:1] - **Introduced by Alauddin Khalji** (UPPCS 1992) – the first state price-control and rationing system, called the first "**Public Distribution System**" (UPPCS Mains 2010): state **granaries** released grain in shortage.
[107:0:2] - **⚠ Not** to pay lower wages to palace artisans (UPPCS Mains 2005: A true, R false); not for farmers' returns or direct purchase.
[107:0:3] - **Four-point plan:** **fixed prices**, sure **supply**, controlled **distribution**, **end of monopolies** – enforced by a separate administration.
[107:0:4] TABLE head: Market | Goods
  [107:0:4.0] Mandi | Food grains
  [107:0:4.1] Sarai Adl | Cloth, sugar, dry fruits, butter, oil
  [107:0:4.2] Third market | Horses, slaves, cattle
  [107:0:4.3] Fourth market | Miscellaneous goods
[107:0:5] # Officials and Departments
[107:0:6] TABLE head: Official / department | Role
  [107:0:6.0] Shahna-i-Mandi | Superintendent of each market (e.g. Malik Kabool)
  [107:0:6.1] Diwan-i-Riyasat | Ran the whole market system, under the Naib-i-Riyasat
  [107:0:6.2] Barids | Intelligence officers – daily reports
  [107:0:6.3] Munhiyan | Secret spies – daily reports
  [107:0:6.4] Diwan-i-Mustakhraj | Recovery of revenue arrears from officials
[107:0:7] - **Slave boys** made test purchases; cheating merchants punished; banjaras registered.
[107:0:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 03 p.61-62; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.39-40; app notes 1:107 (sub 0, 1)
## SUB 1 | Revenue Reforms
[107:1:0] - **First Sultan to measure land** – **masahat** (HPPSC 2012) – unit **biswa**; revenue in **cash**.
[107:1:1] - **Statements (UPPCS 1994):** he **measured cultivated land** – true; he **curbed the provincial governors' (muqtis') independent powers** – true in the key; measurement **covered the whole empire – false** (only **Delhi and the Doab**).
[107:1:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [107:1:2.0] Kharaj | Land tax = one-half (50%) of produce (NextGen's "one-fifth" is a slip; PMF: 50%)
  [107:1:2.1] Ghari | House tax
  [107:1:2.2] Charai | Grazing tax on milch cattle
  [107:1:2.3] Khuts, muqaddams, chaudharis | Intermediaries – privileges abolished, taxed like others
  [107:1:2.4] Kismat-i-khuti | Headman's cess – abolished
  [107:1:2.5] Milk, waqf, inam | Grants reclaimed → khalisa (crown land) expanded on a large scale (State PSC)
[107:1:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 03 p.61; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.40; app notes 1:107 (sub 2)
## SUB 2 | Ordinances Against the Nobles
[107:2:0] TABLE head: Cause of revolt (Barani) | Ordinance
  [107:2:0.0] Nobles' wealth | Confiscated property, religious grants and free lands
  [107:2:0.1] Weak spy system | Reorganised spies (barids, munhiyan)
  [107:2:0.2] Drinking | Prohibited liquor, intoxicants and gambling
  [107:2:0.3] Nobles' intermarriage and gatherings | Banquets, festivities and marriage alliances only with the Sultan's permission (SSC 2013)
[107:2:1] - **Severe punishments**; prohibition + market control + severe punishment = **Alauddin** (APPSC G1 2018).
[107:2:2] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 03 p.59, p.62; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.39; app notes 1:107 (sub 4)
## SUB 3 | Effects, Sources and End of the Reforms
[107:3:0] - **Verdict in keys:** market control was **highly successful** (UPPCS Mains 2017) – prices stayed fixed even in famine; Alauddin called a "great political economist".
[107:3:1] - **Other side:** espionage and repression bred discontent; merchants and peasants hurt; controls largely confined to Delhi.
[107:3:2] - **Main source:** **Ziauddin Barani**, **Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi**; also **Khazain-ul-Futuh** (Amir Khusrau).
[107:3:3] - **Reign = "imperial period"** of the Sultanate – conquests, centralisation, imperial ambition.
[107:3:4] - **End:** **Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah (1316-20)** abolished price control and restored confiscated lands.
[107:3:5] TABLE head: Dynasty | Key contribution
  [107:3:5.0] Khaljis | Market control, taxation reforms
  [107:3:5.1] Tughlaqs | Token currency, capital transfer, canals
  [107:3:5.2] Lodis | First Afghan dynasty; Agra founded
[107:3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 03 p.62-63; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.39; app notes 1:107 (sub 7)
## SUB 4 | Military Reforms
[107:4:0] - **First Sultan with a large permanent standing army**, recruited by the Ariz and **paid in cash** from the treasury.
[107:4:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [107:4:1.0] Dagh | Branding of horses
  [107:4:1.1] Huliya / Chehra | Descriptive roll of each soldier
[107:4:2] - **Purpose:** stop fraud and false musters; strict periodic reviews.
[107:4:3] - **Ariz-i-Mamalik:** **army** administration.
[107:4:4] - **Later copy:** **Mahmud Gawan** (Bahmani) brought in dagh, chehra and land measurement.
