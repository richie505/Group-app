## SUB 1 | Firoz Shah Tughlaq – Canals, Irrigation Tax, Towns and Books
[1:0] - **Biggest network of canals among the Delhi Sultans:** **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** (UPSC 1998 ×2) – not Iltutmish, Ghiyasuddin or Sikandar Lodi.
[1:1] - **Haqq-i-Sharb (irrigation tax):** first levied by **Firoz** (UPPSC 2010 ×2), at **one-tenth** of the produce on fields watered by royal canals.
[1:2] - **State-promoted canal irrigation "initiated by":** books and keys give **Firoz Tughlaq** (APPSC G1); strictly **Ghiyasuddin** was the first Sultan to dig canals, Firoz built the most.
[1:3] TABLE head: Canal | Fact
  [1:3.0] Yamuna to Hisar | about 150 miles, the longest in the old notes (PMF: Sutlej–Hansi, about 200 km)
  [1:3.1] Sutlej to Ghaggar | 96 miles
  [1:3.2] Sirmur hills to Hansi | irrigation canal
  [1:3.3] Ghaggar to Firozabad; Yamuna to Firozabad | irrigation
  [1:3.4] Rajabwah | restored under Akbar as the Shihab (Shahab) Canal
[1:4] - **150 wells** for irrigation and travellers.
[1:5] # Towns
[1:6] - **Firozabad** (Firoz Shah Kotla), **Fatehabad** (named after his son **Fateh Khan**; SSC 2019), **Hisar Firoza**, **Firozpur**, **Jaunpur**.
[1:7] - **Jaunpur:** founded by Firoz **in memory of Muhammad bin Tughlaq (Jauna Khan)** (UPPSC 2004).
[1:8] # Pillars and books
[1:9] - **Ashokan pillars** brought to Delhi from **Topra (Ambala/Khizrabad)** and **Meerut**.
[1:10] - Autobiography: **Futuhat-i-Firoz Shahi**.
[1:11] - **Nagarkot (Jawalamukhi) campaign:** about **1,300 Sanskrit manuscripts** (some books: 300 volumes); translated into Persian by **Azizuddin Khalid Khani (Azizuddin Khan)** as **Dalail-i-Firoz Shahi** (UPPSC 2023) – not Talib Amuli, Abdul Baqi or Mirza Muhammad Ali.
[1:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.64, p.67-68; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.48; Rocket Indian History 01 (Haqq-e-Sharb, Azizuddin Khan); app notes 1:108 (sub 7, 10)
## SUB 2 | Muhammad bin Tughlaq – Experiments and Coins
[2:0] TABLE head: Experiment | Year | Cause / aim | Result
  [2:0.0] Doab taxation | c. 1326 | More revenue (land tax raised to one-half) | Coincided with famine; peasant revolts and flight
  [2:0.1] Transfer of capital Delhi → Devagiri (Daulatabad) | 1327 | Central base to control the South | Hardship; court returned to Delhi
  [2:0.2] Token currency of copper/bronze (brass) | 1329-30 | Shortage of silver; Kublai Khan's paper money as model | Mass forgery; withdrawn, redeemed in silver/gold at heavy loss
  [2:0.3] Khurasan expedition | 1332 | Central Asian conquest | Army (3,70,000) disbanded unpaid
  [2:0.4] Qarachil (Kumaon hills) | 1333 | Secure the hill frontier | Army destroyed in the hills
[2:1] - **Capital shifted to Deogiri / Daulatabad:** **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (BC Welfare; UPPCS 2002) – not Ghiyasuddin, Firoz or Nasiruddin; Daulatabad is not a Firoz town.
[2:2] - **First token currency in India:** **MBT** (UPPCS 2004) – not Akbar, Alauddin or Bahlul Lodi.
[2:3] - **Failure of token currency:** the state had **no control over minting** – forgery everywhere (UPPCS 2006: A and R true, R explains A).
[2:4] - **Dinar (A–R, UPSC 2006 ×2):** MBT issued a new **gold coin called Dinar** by Ibn Battuta ✓; the token coins were **copper/bronze, not gold**, and not for West Asian trade ✗ → **A true, R false**.
[2:5] - **Coins (statements):** gold **Dinar** ✓ and silver **Adli** ✓ → **(b) and (c)**; **Adha and Bikh** (copper-silver alloy) were **Firoz's** small coins; the copper **Jital** goes back to **Iltutmish**.
[2:6] - **MBT (Juna / Jauna Khan, Ulugh Khan):** became Sultan in **1325**, not 1300 (G1 2003 NOT-true); suspected of arranging his father's death (pavilion collapse); took **Orugallu (Warangal) 1323**, renamed **Sultanpur**.
[2:7] - Called a "mixture of opposites" (Barani) and "a saint with the heart of a devil".

## SUB 4 | Ibn Battuta and the Historians on Muhammad bin Tughlaq
[4:0] - **Nationality:** **Moroccan ("Moorish")**, born **Tangiers**, 1304-1377 (APPSC G1) – not Egyptian, Nigerian or Ethiopian.
[4:1] - **Came under:** **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (CGPSC 2013; UPPSC 2011) – arrived **1333**, in India to 1342 (left Delhi for **China as envoy, 1342**).
[4:2] - **Post:** MBT made him **Qazi (chief judge) of Delhi** (CDS 2005) – not Balban, Alauddin or Ghiyasuddin.
[4:3] - **Rihla (Kitab-ul-Rihla), in Arabic, completed 1355:** CDS 2013 key = **"an account of the Delhi Sultans from Aibak to Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq"** (its India part retells Sultanate history); it is a travelogue – not a religious text or a Morocco trade account.
[4:4] - Rihla also notes the **postal system** and that **sati needed the Sultan's permission**; Ibn Battuta also described **Harihara I's** Vijayanagara.
[4:5] TABLE head: Historian | Remark
  [4:5.0] Badauni | On MBT's death: "the king was freed from his people and they from their king" (UPSC 1999; MPPSC 1997) – not Balban, Alauddin or Firoz
  [4:5.1] Barani | MBT a "mixture of opposites"; wrote Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi and Fatawa-i-Jahandari

## SUB 5 | Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (1320-25)
[5:0] - **Ghazi Malik:** son of a Turkish slave and a Jat woman; **Governor of Punjab** under Alauddin against the Mongols; killed **Khusrau Shah** and became Sultan in **1320**.
[5:1] - **Revenue:** increase limited to **one-tenth (or one-eleventh)**, raised only gradually (UPPSC 2011) – not Balban, Alauddin or Firoz; remissions in drought; waste land farmed; **first Sultan to dig canals**.
[5:2] - **Khalisa assessment:** dropped Alauddin's **measurement** for **sharing of the crop (batai / hukm-i-hasil)** (RPSC 2008; printed key "Kankut" ✗ – kankut is estimation).
[5:3] - **Warangal:** Juna Khan's first attack (1321-22) failed; fell after a **five-month siege (1323)**, renamed **Sultanpur**; **Prataparudra II** died in the **Narmada** on the way to Delhi.
[5:4] - Built **Tughlaqabad**; died **1325** when a **wooden pavilion collapsed at Afghanpur**; patron of **Amir Khusrau**.
[5:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.64-65; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.43; Moreland, Agrarian System of Moslem India (digi.econbiz.de); app notes 1:108 (sub 1)

## SUB 7 | Firoz Shah Tughlaq – Accession, Revenue, Jizya, Iqta
[7:0] - **Accession:** son of Ghiyasuddin's brother **Rajab** (MBT's **cousin**, not uncle); placed on the throne at **Thatta (1351)**; ruled till **1388**.
[7:1] - **Wazir Khan-i-Jahan Maqbul** (a Telugu Brahmin convert) paid **compensation** to MBT's victims.
[7:2] - **Revenue:** wrote off taqavi loans; **fixed** land revenue; abolished about **24 taxes**; kept the **four Islamic taxes** – **kharaj, jizya, khams, zakat** (haq-i-sharb is extra, his irrigation cess).
[7:3] - **Jizya:** made a **separate tax** and extended to **Brahmins** (ulema's advice).
[7:4] - **Iqtas:** revived and made **hereditary**.
[7:5] - **Campaigns:** two failed **Bengal** expeditions against **Haji Iliyas** (second 1359-60); **Jajnagar** and **Nagarkot** taken; **Jam Babaniya** of Sind agreed to pay tribute.
[7:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.66-68; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.47-48; app notes 1:108 (sub 6)

## SUB 9 | Successors and the End of the Tughlaqs
[9:0] - **Weak successors after 1388:** Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq II, **Abu Bakr**, Nasiruddin Muhammad, then rival Sultans **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah** (Delhi) and **Nusrat Shah** (Firozabad).
[9:1] - **Last Tughlaq ruler:** **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah** (1394-1412) (IAS 2004) – not Firoz, Ghiyasuddin II or Nusrat Shah; in his reign **Timur** invaded (**1398**) and **Khwaja Jahan** founded **Jaunpur** (Sharqi).
[9:2] - Tughlaq period **1320-1414** (longest Sultanate dynasty, about 94 years); **Khizr Khan** took Delhi **1414**.
