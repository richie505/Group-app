## SUB 0 | Market Control – Aim, Markets and Officials
[0:0] - **Chain:** Mongol invasions → **large standing army paid in cash** → **low fixed salaries** → **fixed low prices** of daily goods → **soldiers live comfortably on low pay** (APPSC G2 2017; RRB 2002).
[0:1] - **Introduced by Alauddin Khalji** (UPPCS 1992) – the first state price-control and rationing system, called the first "**Public Distribution System**" (UPPCS Mains 2010): state **granaries** released grain in shortage.
[0:2] - **⚠ Not** to pay lower wages to palace artisans (UPPCS Mains 2005: A true, R false); not for farmers' returns or direct purchase.
[0:3] - **Four-point plan:** **fixed prices**, sure **supply**, controlled **distribution**, **end of monopolies** – enforced by a separate administration.
[0:4] TABLE head: Market | Goods
  [0:4.0] Mandi | Food grains
  [0:4.1] Sarai Adl | Cloth, sugar, dry fruits, butter, oil
  [0:4.2] Third market | Horses, slaves, cattle
  [0:4.3] Fourth market | Miscellaneous goods
[0:5] # Officials and Departments
[0:6] TABLE head: Official / department | Role
  [0:6.0] Shahna-i-Mandi | Superintendent of each market (e.g. Malik Kabool)
  [0:6.1] Diwan-i-Riyasat | Ran the whole market system, under the Naib-i-Riyasat
  [0:6.2] Barids | Intelligence officers – daily reports
  [0:6.3] Munhiyan | Secret spies – daily reports
  [0:6.4] Diwan-i-Mustakhraj | Recovery of revenue arrears from officials
[0:7] - **Slave boys** made test purchases; cheating merchants punished; banjaras registered.

## SUB 1 | Revenue Reforms
[1:0] - **First Sultan to measure land** – **masahat** (HPPSC 2012) – unit **biswa**; revenue in **cash**.
[1:1] - **Statements (UPPCS 1994):** he **measured cultivated land** – true; he **curbed the provincial governors' (muqtis') independent powers** – true in the key; measurement **covered the whole empire – false** (only **Delhi and the Doab**).
[1:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:2.0] Kharaj | Land tax = one-half (50%) of produce (NextGen's "one-fifth" is a slip; PMF: 50%)
  [1:2.1] Ghari | House tax
  [1:2.2] Charai | Grazing tax on milch cattle
  [1:2.3] Khuts, muqaddams, chaudharis | Intermediaries – privileges abolished, taxed like others
  [1:2.4] Kismat-i-khuti | Headman's cess – abolished
  [1:2.5] Milk, waqf, inam | Grants reclaimed → khalisa (crown land) expanded on a large scale (State PSC)
[1:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 03 p.61; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.40; app notes 1:107 (sub 2)
## SUB 2 | Ordinances Against the Nobles
[2:0] TABLE head: Cause of revolt (Barani) | Ordinance
  [2:0.0] Nobles' wealth | Confiscated property, religious grants and free lands
  [2:0.1] Weak spy system | Reorganised spies (barids, munhiyan)
  [2:0.2] Drinking | Prohibited liquor, intoxicants and gambling
  [2:0.3] Nobles' intermarriage and gatherings | Banquets, festivities and marriage alliances only with the Sultan's permission (SSC 2013)
[2:1] - **Severe punishments**; prohibition + market control + severe punishment = **Alauddin** (APPSC G1 2018).

## SUB 3 | Military Reforms
[3:0] - **First Sultan with a large permanent standing army**, recruited by the Ariz and **paid in cash** from the treasury.
[3:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:1.0] Dagh | Branding of horses
  [3:1.1] Huliya / Chehra | Descriptive roll of each soldier
[3:2] - **Purpose:** stop fraud and false musters; strict periodic reviews.
[3:3] - **Ariz-i-Mamalik:** **army** administration.
[3:4] - **Later copy:** **Mahmud Gawan** (Bahmani) brought in dagh, chehra and land measurement.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 03 p.62; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.40; app notes 1:107 (sub 3)
## SUB 4 | Effects, Sources and End of the Reforms
[4:0] - **Verdict in keys:** market control was **highly successful** (UPPCS Mains 2017) – prices stayed fixed even in famine; Alauddin called a "great political economist".
[4:1] - **Other side:** espionage and repression bred discontent; merchants and peasants hurt; controls largely confined to Delhi.
[4:2] - **Main source:** **Ziauddin Barani**, **Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi**; also **Khazain-ul-Futuh** (Amir Khusrau).
[4:3] - **Reign = "imperial period"** of the Sultanate – conquests, centralisation, imperial ambition.
[4:4] - **End:** **Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah (1316-20)** abolished price control and restored confiscated lands.

  [4:5.0] Khaljis | Market control, taxation reforms
  [4:5.1] Tughlaqs | Token currency, capital transfer, canals
  [4:5.2] Lodis | First Afghan dynasty; Agra founded
