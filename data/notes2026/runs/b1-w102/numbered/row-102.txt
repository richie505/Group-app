## SUB 0 | Muhammad Ghori and the Battles of Tarain
[0:0] TABLE head: Battle | Year | Opponent | Victor
  [0:0.0] Kayadara / Kasahrada, near Mount Abu (Gujarat campaign) | 1178 | Mularaja II (Bhimdev II), Chaulukya/Solanki; regent mother Naika Devi | Gujarat – Ghori's first defeat
  [0:0.1] First Tarain (near Karnal, Haryana) | 1191 | Prithviraj Chauhan (Prithviraja III) | Prithviraj – Ghori wounded, barely escaped
  [0:0.2] Second Tarain | 1192 | Prithviraj Chauhan | Ghori – turning point; foundation of Turkish rule
  [0:0.3] Chandawar (near Firozabad, UP, on the Yamuna) | 1194 | Jayachandra (Jaichand), Gahadavala of Kannauj | Ghori (army under Aibak)
[0:1] - **Ghori:** Shihabuddin (Muizzuddin) **Muhammad Ghori**, Ghurid, took Ghazni 1173; entered India by the **Gomal Pass**; aimed at **conquest and permanent occupation**, unlike Mahmud.
[0:2] - **Route:** **Multan (1175, first invasion)** → Peshawar (c. 1179) → Sindh (c. 1182) → Punjab and Lahore (1186, from Khusru Malik, last Ghaznavid).
[0:3] - **First defeat (1178):** by **Mularaja II / Bhimdev II** of Gujarat (books also write "Bhima II", "Baghel Bhim") – **not Prithviraj**, who beat him only in 1191.
[0:4] - **Cause of Tarain I:** Ghori's attack on **Tabarhinda (Bhatinda)**; Prithviraj led a confederacy of Rajput princes.
[0:5] - **Tarain II (1192):** Ghori returned with 1,20,000 men; **Jaichand refused to help** Prithviraj; Turkish **cavalry speed, organisation and mounted archery** beat the larger Rajput army. It **laid the foundation of Muslim (Turkish) rule** – not the Panipat battles.
[0:6] - **1192:** the Central Asian ruler who **conquered North India in 1192** = **Shihabuddin Muhammad Ghori** – not Mahmud (raider), Jalaluddin Mangbarni or Genghis Khan.
[0:7] - **Coins:** Ghori's coins bear **Goddess Lakshmi** on one side and the **Kalma in Arabic** on the other.
[0:8] - **Aibak:** Ghori's slave, **Master of Stables**; his representative in India after 1192; on Ghori's death (1206, near Jhelum) became the **first Sultan**, capital **Lahore** – the Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526).
[0:9] # Prithviraj Chauhan
[0:10] - **Prithviraj Chauhan = Prithviraja III** (Rai Pithora), Chahamana of **Ajmer and Delhi** (c. 1177-1192) – not Prithviraja I or II.
[0:11] - Defeated Chandela **Paramardi**, Chaulukya Bhima II and Gahadavala Jaichand; after 1192 reinstated as a vassal, later executed; son Govindaraja IV moved to **Ranthambore**.
[0:12] - **"The last Sun of Indian glory"** – Prithviraj Chauhan (B.N. Luniya).

## SUB 1 | Arab Conquest of Sindh (712)
[1:0] - **First Muslim invaders** of India: the **Arabs**, under **Muhammad bin Qasim**, Sindh, **712** – before the Ghaznavids and Ghurids (not Persians).
[1:1] - **First successful Muslim invader:** **Muhammad bin Qasim** – not Mahmud of Ghazni, Ghori or Aibak.
[1:2] - **Year:** the expedition left in 711 and took Debal and Sindh in **712**; so for "674 / 1013 / 711" the answer is **none of these** (712).
[1:3] - **Qasim:** an **Arab** (not Turk, Mongol or Turk-Afghan); nephew and son-in-law of **Al-Hajjaj bin Yusuf**, the Umayyad governor of Iraq; sent by **Caliph al-Walid** at the age of **17**.
[1:4] - **Ruler defeated:** **Raja Dahir** (Dahar), killed; capital **Alor (Aror)** – not Brahmanabad, which was only one of the places taken.
[1:5] - **Places taken:** **Debal, Nirun, Sehwan, Brahmanabad, Multan** – not Somnath (Mahmud, 1025).
[1:6] - **First contact with Islam:** earlier, through **Arab traders on the Malabar coast** (7th century) – the 712 conquest was the first political contact.
[1:7] # Chachnama
[1:8] - Arabic chronicle of the Arab conquest of **Sindh**; original lost; the existing **13th-century Persian translation** is by **Muhammad Ali bin Abu Bakr Kufi** – not Aufi or Shams-i-Siraj.
[1:9] - Records Dahir's widowed queen fighting on and then immolating herself with the other women.
[1:10] - Other sources: **Futuh al-Buldan** (Al-Biladuri, 9th c.), Muruj-ul-Zehab (Al-Masudi, 10th c.).
[1:11] # Causes and Effects
[1:12] TABLE head: Factor | Fact
  [1:12.0] Causes | Arab maritime interests, expansion, piracy against Arab ships
  [1:12.1] Religion | No mass Islamisation; Hindus and Buddhists given dhimmi status
  [1:12.2] Limited reach | Confined to Sindh and Multan by the Thar and Aravallis, strong Pratiharas, Rashtrakutas, Palas, weak Arab resources
  [1:12.3] Knowledge transfer | Indian decimal and place-value maths, astronomy, medicine → Islamic world → Europe
[1:13] - **Nagabhata I (Pratihara)** drove back the Arabs; Arab writers said the **Pratiharas** had the **strongest cavalry** in India.
[1:14] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.17-18, p.21-22; PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 (Chachnama); Rocket Indian History 01 p.76, p.78; app notes 1:102 (sub 1)
## SUB 2 | Mahmud of Ghazni and the Ghaznavids
[2:0] TABLE head: Person | Role
  [2:0.0] Alptigin (Alp-Tegin) | Turkish slave-general of the Samanids; seized Ghazni (962) – a principality still under the Samanids
  [2:0.1] Sabuktigin (977-997) | Founder of the Ghaznavid dynasty proper ("real founder")
  [2:0.2] Mahmud (998-1030) | The raider: 17 raids, 1000-1027
[2:1] - **Founder (PYQ):** **Sabuktigin** founded the Ghaznavid dynasty (PMF; Britannica); some guide books key Alptigin, who only seized Ghazni.
[2:2] - **Motive:** **plunder of temple wealth** to fund his Central Asian wars – he **did not seek a permanent empire** (so for "17 invasions because he wanted a permanent empire": A true, R false).
[2:3] - **Raids:** Hindu Shahi **Jayapala** beaten 1001; **Battle of Waihind (1008-09)** – **Anandapala** (Hindu Shahi) vs Mahmud; **Nagarkot (Kangra) 1009**; **Thanesar** (Chakraswamin temple) **1014**; **Mathura** and **Kanauj 1018** (the Pratihara king submitted); **Somnath 1025-26**.
[2:4] - **Somnath (1025):** raided under Solanki **Bhima I (Bhimadeva I)**, who fled to Kanthkot and **reconstructed the temple** after Mahmud left; Kumarapala later rebuilt it in stone.
[2:5] - **Chandelas:** strongest resistance; **Vidyadhara** faced Mahmud (1019, 1022) and was **not defeated** (peace made) – Dhanga died c. 1002 and **Jayashakti** (9th c.) lived long before Mahmud.
[2:6] - **Coins:** Mahmud issued **silver coins** with **Arabic** on one side and a **Sanskrit legend (Devanagari)** on the other.
[2:7] - **Title:** Yamini-ud-Daulah from the Abbasid caliph – hence the **Yamini dynasty**.
[2:8] - **Effects:** Hindu Shahi barrier destroyed; Punjab and the north-west frontier opened. After Mahmud the Ghaznavids declined under **Seljuk** pressure; Ghori ended the dynasty (1186).
[2:9] - **Latest (January 2026):** **Somnath Swabhiman Parv** – 1,000 years since Mahmud's attack on Somnath.
[2:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.19-22; Rocket Indian History 01 p.15 (coins), p.78; Britannica "Ghaznavid dynasty"; PMF Lens 2026-01 (Somnath Swabhiman Parv); app notes 1:102 (sub 2)

  [3:0.0] Al-Biruni | Kitab-ul-Hind (Tahqiq-i-Hind); Kitab Patanjal (Arabic Yoga-Sutra)
  [3:0.1] Utbi | Court historian; Kitab-ul-Yamini (Tarikh-i-Yamini)
  [3:0.2] Firdausi ("Homer of the East") | Shahnama
  [3:0.3] Baihaqi | Tarikh-i-Subuktigin
  [3:0.4] Unsuri | Poet laureate (Malik-ush-Shuara)
  [3:0.5] Uzari, Asjadi, Farrukhi | Persian court poets
[3:1] - **Court historian of Mahmud:** **Utbi** – not Hasan Nizami (Aibak; Taj-ul-Maasir), Firdausi (poet) or Chand Bardai (Prithviraj).
[3:2] - **Shahnama:** by **Firdausi** at **Mahmud Ghaznavi's** court (one PYQ misspells him "Farista").
[3:3] - **Kitab-ul-Hind / Tahqiq-i-Hind:** **Al-Biruni** – not Hasan Nizami, Minhaj-us-Siraj or Shams-i-Siraj Afif.
[3:4] - **When he came:** with Mahmud, in the **11th century AD**; an objective account of 11th-century India.
[3:5] - **Ghurid-era chronicles (not Mahmud's court):** **Taj-ul-Maasir** – **Hasan Nizami**; **Tabaqat-i-Nasiri** – **Minhaj-us-Siraj**.
[3:6] - **Historian who came with Mahmud:** **Al-Biruni** – Ferishta (16th c.), Afif (Firoz Tughlaq) and Ibn Battuta (Muhammad Tughlaq) are later.
[3:7] # Al-Biruni
[3:8] - Born **973** in **Khwarizm (Khiva)**; Sanskrit scholar, mathematician (trigonometry), astronomer; "father of Indian historical writing" (Indology).
[3:9] - Kitab-ul-Hind translated into English by **Edward Sachau**.
[3:10] - **"Not correct" statement (PYQ):** "He was a **secular** author" – he wrote as a Muslim scholar and has no word of sympathy for the Somnath victims.

## SUB 4 | Causes of Turkish Success; Invasions in Order
[4:0] TABLE head: Factor | Point
  [4:0.0] Political | No political unity in North India; rival Rajput states after the Pratihara collapse
  [4:0.1] Military | Superior war technique: mobile cavalry, mounted archers, organisation vs elephant-reliant armies
  [4:0.2] Social | R.S. Sharma: "Indian feudalism" – feudal levies, sub-infeudation, closed village economy; caste limited fighting to few groups
[4:1] - **A-R (UPPCS 2018, 2024):** A "Turkish invasions were successful" and R "no political unity in North India" – **both true, R explains A**.
[4:2] - **Why Ghori won (APPSC G1):** **superior war technology / military organisation of the Turks** – not a larger army (Prithviraj's was larger), local support or treachery.

  [4:3.4] 5 | Timur | 1398

## SUB 5 | Bakhtiyar Khalji – Bihar and Bengal
[5:0] - **Ikhtiyaruddin Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji:** slave-general of Ghori (under Aibak); **conquered Bihar and Bengal (1193-1203)** – not Aibak, Iltutmish or Yaldauz.
[5:1] - **Real founder of Turk rule in Bihar.**
[5:2] - Sacked **Nalanda**, **Vikramashila** and **Odantapuri** – Nalanda was destroyed by Bakhtiyar, not by Mahmud, Aibak or the later Sultans.
[5:3] - Entered **Nadiya** (1204), drove **Lakshmana Sena** to East Bengal; ruled from **Lakhnauti**; failed Tibet expedition 1205; killed 1206.
[5:4] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian History 01 p.76; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.24; app notes 1:102 (sub 5)
