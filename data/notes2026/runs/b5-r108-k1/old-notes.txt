=== NOTES 5:108 | Biotechnology – Nature, Scope, Tools; Applications in Agriculture, Medicine and Industry
## SUB 0 | Nature and Scope of Biotechnology
[108:0:0] - **CBD (1992)**: biotechnology = use of **biological systems** to make or modify products.
[108:0:1] - Also: **industrial use of living organisms**, substances from them or their processes (e.g. **yeast for liquor**).
[108:0:2] - Traditional biotech: curd, bread, wine, vinegar (ancient fermentation). Modern: rDNA, PCR, cloning, CRISPR.
[108:0:3] TABLE head: Colour | Field
  [108:0:3.0] Red | Medical
  [108:0:3.1] Green | Agricultural
  [108:0:3.2] White | Industrial – enzymes, biofuels, bioplastics, precision fermentation, green chemicals (NOT marine)
  [108:0:3.3] Blue | Marine
[108:0:4] - **Genetics** = study of genes, heredity and variation. **Enzymes** speed chemical reactions in living systems.
[108:0:5] - **Bionics** = using biological systems' properties in the mechanical world; **bionomics** = organism and environment; **bionomy** = laws of life; **biometry** = statistical study; **anatomy** = body structures.
## SUB 1 | Tools and Genome Terms
[108:1:0] TABLE head: Tool | Use
  [108:1:0.0] PCR | Key enzyme DNA polymerase; denaturation → annealing → extension
  [108:1:0.1] Gel electrophoresis | Separates DNA by size
  [108:1:0.2] Restriction enzymes | Cut DNA – EcoRI, BamHI, HindIII
  [108:1:0.3] Ligase | Only joins DNA
  [108:1:0.4] Plasmids | Vectors
  [108:1:0.5] Bioreactors | Large-scale culture; fastest ex-situ bioremediation
  [108:1:0.6] CRISPR | Genome editing
[108:1:1] - **Genome**: complete genetic information; zygote genome = DNA of **two gametes**. **Human genome ≈ 3 billion bases; over 99% shared**.
[108:1:2] - **Transcriptome**: all expressed **mRNA**; **varies with environment**, unlike the genome.
[108:1:3] - **DNA sequencing**: finds the base order (A, G, C, T) – genetic information depends on base order.
[108:1:4] - DNA stores and passes information because of **complementarity** of strands (A–T, G–C).
[108:1:5] - **Genetic code**: universal; triplet for 20 amino acids; non-overlapping, non-ambiguous, commaless; start and stop codons.
[108:1:6] - **mRNA** carries the message for protein; **microRNA**: small non-coding RNA – silences target genes by **post-transcriptional regulation** (Ambros and Ruvkun).
[108:1:7] - **Microsatellite DNA** (short tandem repeats): evolutionary relationships, genetic diversity.
[108:1:8] - **DNA barcoding:** identifies species from a **short standard gene region** (animals: mitochondrial **COI**); taxonomy, biodiversity surveys, food-fraud detection.
[108:1:9] - **Serological tests:** **antigen-antibody** reactions identify blood groups, pathogens (**Widal, ELISA**) and species.

## SUB 3 | Dna Fingerprinting
[108:3:0] - **Basis: DNA polymorphism** – highly variable non-coding regions (**VNTRs**/repetitive DNA) differ between individuals.
[108:3:1] - Tissues of the same person show the **same pattern**; polymorphisms are **inherited** (child gets half from each parent).
[108:3:2] - Uses: **identity, paternity**, crime – **rape and assault** (trace hair, saliva, dried semen enough), conservation.
[108:3:3] - Samples: **blood, bone cells, hair, saliva**, skin, sperm – all usable. **Biochips** = a technique, not a sample.
[108:3:4] - First crime solved: **England**, murders of **1983 and 1986**.
[108:3:5] - **CDFD** (Centre for DNA Fingerprinting and Diagnostics): **Hyderabad**, under DBT.

## SUB 9 | Bioremediation – Meaning and Methods
[108:9:0] - **Bioremediation**: removal of pollutants from the biosphere by **biological processes** (APPSC-GS 2019); organisms (bacteria, fungi, algae, plants) or their **enzymes** restore a polluted environment.
[108:9:1] - **Primary objective**: neutralising pollutants in **soil and groundwater** using micro-organisms (APPSC-G2 2025).
[108:9:2] - Bacteria breaking down organic (municipal) waste = bioremediation (APPSC-GS 2022).
[108:9:3] - Scope: soil, water, air; oil spills, heavy metals, pesticides, solvents; engineered microbes may be used.
[108:9:4] TABLE head: Term | Bioremediation?
  [108:9:4.0] Composting, bacterial oil clean-up, phytoremediation, constructed wetlands | Yes
  [108:9:4.1] Burning contaminated soil / incineration (thermal) | No (APPSC-G2 2025)
  [108:9:4.2] Biomagnification – rising DDT, mercury up food chain | No
  [108:9:4.3] Eutrophication – nutrient-driven algal blooms | No
  [108:9:4.4] Desalination, fossilisation, biofortification | No
[108:9:5] # In-situ vs Ex-situ
[108:9:6] TABLE head: Basis | In-situ | Ex-situ
  [108:9:6.0] Site | At original site, no excavation | Excavated, treated elsewhere
  [108:9:6.1] Techniques | Bioventing, biosparging, bioaugmentation | Landfarming, composting, biopiles, bioreactors
  [108:9:6.2] Cost | Cheapest, passive | Bioreactors costliest
  [108:9:6.3] Speed | Slow | Bioreactors fastest
  [108:9:6.4] Limits | Long time; metals complicate | Space, excavation
