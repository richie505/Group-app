=== NOTES 3:83 | Monetary Policy Committee and the Flexible Inflation Targeting Framework
## SUB 0 | Inflation Target – What, Who and How Much
[83:0:0] - **Inflation targeting** = **public announcement of a medium-term numerical target** for inflation by the central bank (APPSC G2).
[83:0:1] - **Target: 4% CPI-Combined, tolerance ±2 percentage points** (band **2–6%**).
[83:0:2] - **Who fixes it:** the **Government of India, in consultation with the RBI** (Sec 45ZA) – not the RBI, MPC, NITI Aayog or Finance Commission (RAS 2018 ×2); **RBI/MPC must achieve it**.
[83:0:3] - Set **once every five years**, notified in the **Official Gazette**.
[83:0:4] TABLE head: Notified | Period | Target
  [83:0:4.0] August 2016 | to 31 March 2021 | 4% ±2% (APPSC G2)
  [83:0:4.1] March 2021 | April 2021 – March 2026 | Retained
  [83:0:4.2] 25 March 2026 | 1 April 2026 – 31 March 2031 | Retained (second time)
[83:0:5] - **Anchor**: headline **CPI-C** compiled by **NSO (MoSPI)**; new series **base 2024** released **12 Feb 2026** (earlier base 2012); not WPI.
[83:0:6] - **Failure (Sec 45ZN):** average CPI inflation **above 6% or below 2% for three consecutive quarters** (NextGen's "3 consecutive months" is wrong) → RBI reports reasons, remedial action and time to return to target; first invoked **2022**.
[83:0:7] - Primary objective under FIT: **price stability while keeping growth in mind**; MPC sets the policy rate to achieve **the inflation target**.

## SUB 2 | Monetary Policy Committee – Composition and Powers
[83:2:0] TABLE head: Member | How
  [83:2:0.0] RBI Governor | Chairperson; casting vote on a tie (no veto)
  [83:2:0.1] Deputy Governor (monetary policy) | Ex officio
  [83:2:0.2] One RBI officer | Nominated by the RBI Central Board
  [83:2:0.3] Three external members | Appointed by the Central Government; 4-year term, no reappointment
[83:2:1] - **6 members** (3 RBI + 3 external) – not 12, not reconstituted every year, **chaired by the RBI Governor, not the Finance Minister** (UPSC 2017: only I).
[83:2:2] - MPC **fixes the benchmark policy (repo) rate** (APPSC G2; APPSC GS 2019) to **control inflation** – it does not dictate fiscal policy, trade or industry (APPSC ASO).
[83:2:3] - Decisions by **majority**, **binding on the RBI**; **quorum 4**; meets **at least 4 times** a year by law (six in practice); first met **October 2016**; constituted under **Sec 45ZB**.
[83:2:4] - RBI publishes a **Monetary Policy Report every six months**.
[83:2:5] - **Governors:** Shaktikanta Das (Dec 2018 – Dec 2024; "present Governor" in the APPSC G4 2022 paper); **Sanjay Malhotra since 11 Dec 2024**.

[84:0:0] # Origin
[84:0:1] TABLE head: Stage | Fact
  [84:0:1.0] Bank of Calcutta | 1806 – first Presidency bank (first bank of the East India Company); renamed Bank of Bengal – the bank SBI was "originally founded as"
  [84:0:1.1] Bank of Bombay, Bank of Madras | 1840, 1843 – the other two Presidency banks
  [84:0:1.2] Imperial Bank of India | 1921 – amalgamation of the three Presidency banks; SBI was "previously known as" Imperial Bank
  [84:0:1.3] State Bank of India | 1 July 1955, SBI Act 1955 – Imperial Bank taken over ("partially nationalised"); first public sector bank; the RBI bought about 92% of the shares (NextGen, Ramesh Singh; many quiz sources say 60%), diluted after the 1993 SBI share issue
  [84:0:1.4] SBI (Subsidiary Banks) Act | 1959 – eight princely-state banks became SBI associates
  [84:0:1.5] RBI stake to Government | June/July 2007 – Government acquired the RBI's 59.7% stake in SBI
[84:0:2] - **SBI "nationalised" in 1955** (exam wording) – earlier than LIC (1956) and the 14 banks (1969), later than the RBI (1949).
[84:0:3] # Size and status
[84:0:4] - **Largest commercial bank** and **largest public sector bank** of India – not the RBI (central bank), NABARD, IFCI, IDBI or Exim Bank (development/apex institutions), not PNB or Bank of Baroda.
[84:0:5] - **Headquarters: Mumbai**; Fortune 500; the **only Indian bank in the global top 100** (S&P: 55th April 2020, **47th April 2024**).
[84:0:6] - SBI is a **commercial bank, not an apex institution** (NABARD, SIDBI, State Co-operative Bank are apex bodies); it is the **odd one out** among UTI, Canbank Financial Services and UP State Financial Corporation (non-bank financial institutions).
[84:0:7] - **Tagline: "With You All The Way"** (also "The Banker to Every Indian").
[84:0:8] - Formed **Farmers' Clubs** (with NABARD) to reach farmers.

[84:0:11] - **1 April 2017**: the five associates (**SBBJ, SBH, SBM, SBP, SBT**) and **Bharatiya Mahila Bank** merged into SBI – **MUDRA Bank was not** (it is a refinancing NBFC under SIDBI).
[84:0:12] # Abroad

## SUB 4 | Lead Bank Scheme and Service Area Approach
[84:4:0] # Origin
[84:4:1] - Idea first mooted by the **D. R. Gadgil Study Group** (report **October 1969**: "area approach", district as the unit).
[84:4:2] - **Nariman Committee** (F. K. F. Nariman, bankers' committee appointed by RBI, report **November 1969**) – each public sector bank to act as **Lead Bank** for allotted districts.
[84:4:3] - **Introduced by the RBI in December 1969** – the same year as the 14-bank nationalisation; an initiative of the **bank-nationalisation** era (not NITI Aayog, XII Plan or demonetisation).
[84:4:4] - **Priority-sector** idea: traced to RBI's 1967-68 slack-season credit policy; the sector was formally defined in 1972.
[84:4:5] # Working
[84:4:6] - **Basic aim**: **individual banks adopt particular districts for intensive development** (not mere office-opening, competition or deposit drives).
[84:4:7] - **District credit planning** is done **under the Lead Bank**; lead-bank functions are performed by **a bank designated for the purpose** (not automatically SBI or the RBI).
[84:4:8] - **Service Area Approach (April 1989)** – implemented **under the Lead Bank Scheme**; each rural/semi-urban branch serves a designated group of **15–25 villages**.
[84:4:9] TABLE head: Forum | Level
  [84:4:9.0] Lead District Manager / District Consultative Committee | District
  [84:4:9.1] State Level Bankers' Committee (SLBC) | State
[84:4:10] - **AP**: lead bank / **SLBC convener = Union Bank of India**; 236th SLBC (16 Sep 2026): CM Naidu asked banks to lend more to MSMEs, tenant farmers, PM Surya Ghar.
[84:4:11] - RBI draft revision of the scheme (Feb 2026) to strengthen SLBCs and LDM offices.

## SUB 3 | Basel Accords and the BIS
[85:3:0] - Basel Accords are framed by the **Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS)** under the **Bank for International Settlements (BIS)**, **Basel**, Switzerland; BCBS set up end-**1974** after the failure of **Bankhaus Herstatt**.
[85:3:1] - **BIS (est. 1930)** = the **world's oldest international financial organisation**; the central banks' bank – **not related to the UN** (MIGA, IFC, ICSID are World Bank Group bodies in the UN system).
[85:3:2] TABLE head: Accord | Year | Core content
  [85:3:2.0] Basel I | 1988 | Credit risk only; five risk buckets (0, 10, 20, 50, 100%); minimum capital 8% of risk-weighted assets; India adopted 1999 (CRAR 9%)
  [85:3:2.1] Basel II | Proposed June 1999; framework June 2004 | International standards for measuring the adequacy of a bank's capital; three pillars: minimum capital requirements, supervisory review, market discipline
  [85:3:2.2] Basel III | 2010 (reply to the 2007-09 crisis); in India from 1 Jan 2013 | Improve banks' ability to deal with financial and economic stress, risk management, transparency; CET1, buffers, leverage ratio, LCR, NSFR
[85:3:3] - **Basel Convention** (**UNEP**, 1989, in force 1992) = **transboundary movement of hazardous wastes** – not bank capital; not civil aviation, cyber crime or doping.
[85:3:4] > Exam angle: Basel II/III "relates to" → capital adequacy / banking stress; BIS is the odd one out in "not related to the UN".

## SUB 4 | Capital Adequacy – CRAR, Tier Capital, RBI vs BCBS
[85:4:0] - **CRAR / CAR** = **capital ÷ risk-weighted assets**: the ratio banks must keep between their **risky assets and capital**; a standard measure of the **strength of banks' balance sheets** (ability to absorb losses). CAR and CRAR are the same ratio; "Capital-to-Risk-weighted Assets Ratio" is the precise name.
[85:4:1] - India introduced CRAR in **1992** under BIS standards; **9%** under Basel I (1999) – stricter than BCBS's 8%.
[85:4:2] TABLE head: Norm | BCBS minimum | RBI norm
  [85:4:2.0] CET1 | 4.5% | 5.5%
  [85:4:2.1] Tier 1 | 6% | 7% (not 8%)
  [85:4:2.2] Total CRAR | 8% | 9%
  [85:4:2.3] Capital conservation buffer (CCB) | 2.5% | 2.5%
  [85:4:2.4] Countercyclical buffer (CCyB) | 0-2.5% | Framework since 2015, not activated
  [85:4:2.5] Leverage ratio | 3% | 4% for D-SIBs, 3.5% for others
[85:4:3] # Capital terms
[85:4:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [85:4:4.0] Tier 1 | Core equity: paid-up capital, reserves, undistributed profits
  [85:4:4.1] Tier 2 | Mixed equity-debt, e.g. subordinated debt
  [85:4:4.2] CCyB | Extra capital in credit booms; main trigger = credit-to-GDP gap
  [85:4:4.3] Leverage ratio | Tier 1 capital ÷ total (non-risk-weighted) exposure
  [85:4:4.4] LCR ≥ 100% | High-quality liquid assets (HQLA) to survive a 30-day stress; Level 1 = cash, G-secs; Level 2 = highly rated corporate bonds
  [85:4:4.5] NSFR | Stable funding over a 1-year horizon
[85:4:5] - **Macroprudential tools:** **LTV caps** (loan limited to a share of collateral value), higher **risk weights** on unsecured consumer credit (Nov 2023). **Oct 2025:** RBI draft proposed **lower Basel III risk weights for MSMEs and real estate**.
[85:4:6] > Exam angle: "Minimum Tier 1 = 8%" is the wrong statement on Basel III in India (it is 7%).

=== NOTES 3:86 | Non-performing Assets – Causes, Twin Balance Sheet Problem, Recognition and Provisioning
## SUB 0 | NPA Definition and Asset Classification
[86:0:0] - **NPA:** a loan or advance whose **instalment of principal or interest** stays **overdue for more than 90 days** – it has stopped producing income for the bank (not idle deposits, unused capital or low-interest loans).
[86:0:1] - **Farm loans:** short-duration crops = overdue **2 crop seasons**; long-duration crops = **1 crop season**.
[86:0:2] TABLE head: Class | Criterion
  [86:0:2.0] Standard | Regular; no overdue beyond 90 days
  [86:0:2.1] SMA-0 / SMA-1 / SMA-2 | Overdue 1-30 / 31-60 / 61-90 days: early warning, still standard
  [86:0:2.2] Sub-standard | NPA for 12 months or less
  [86:0:2.3] Doubtful | NPA for more than 12 months
  [86:0:2.4] Loss | Loss identified by the bank, auditors or RBI inspection, but not wholly written off
[86:0:3] - Sequence: Standard → SMA-0/1/2 → NPA (sub-standard → doubtful → loss).
[86:0:4] - **Pannir Selvam Committee (1998)** gave a detailed report on the **NPAs of public sector banks** (recovery by arbitration/conciliation).
[86:0:5] > Exam angle: 90 days (not 30/60/180); SMA-2 is not an NPA.
[86:0:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 08 p.5; testbook.com Pannir Selvam question page
## SUB 1 | Stress Indicators and Key Terms
[86:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [86:1:0.0] Gross NPA | All NPAs
  [86:1:0.1] Net NPA | Gross NPA − provisions
  [86:1:0.2] Stressed assets | NPAs + restructured loans + written-off assets
  [86:1:0.3] Interest coverage ratio (ICR) | EBIT ÷ interest due; below 1 = cannot cover interest from earnings; shows the present and emerging risk of a borrower a bank is lending to – a higher ICR means a better ability to service debt
  [86:1:0.4] Evergreening | Fresh loans to repay old ones → default stays hidden
[86:1:1] - **Balance sheet:** a statement of a firm's **assets and liabilities** on a date – it shows their **size and composition**, not profitability (that is the profit and loss account) or market share.
[86:1:2] > Exam angle: UPSC 2020 ICR – statements A and B true; "higher ICR = worse" is false.
[86:1:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 08 p.5; app notes 3:86 sub 1
## SUB 2 | Recognition and Provisioning
[86:2:0] - **IRACP** = **Income Recognition, Asset Classification, Provisioning** (prudential norms from 1992).
[86:2:1] - **Income recognition:** interest on an NPA is booked as income **only when actually received**.
[86:2:2] - **Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR)** = provisions held ÷ gross NPAs; higher PCR → more resilient bank.
[86:2:3] - **From 1 April 2027:** **borrower-level tagging** – one NPA loan → all that borrower's loans at the bank become NPAs; **upgrade only after all arrears** on every facility are paid; 90-day test stays; automated identification.
[86:2:4] - **ECL model:** RBI moves from the **incurred-loss** model to a forward-looking **3-stage Expected Credit Loss** model.
[86:2:5] TABLE head: ECL stage | Provision
  [86:2:5.0] Stage 1 (low risk) | 12-month expected loss
  [86:2:5.1] Stage 2 (risk rises) | Lifetime expected loss
  [86:2:5.2] Stage 3 (over 90 days) | Full lifetime provision
[86:2:6] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:86 sub 2
## SUB 3 | Twin Balance Sheet Problem
[86:3:0] - Meaning: **over-leveraged corporates + capital-starved, NPA-laden banks** at the same time.
[86:3:1] - Framed in **Economic Survey 2016-17** (CEA **Arvind Subramanian**), which proposed a public sector asset rehabilitation agency (**PARA**).
[86:3:2] - Loan losses eat bank capital → banks stop new loans → firms cannot invest → **private investment falls**.
[86:3:3] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Part 6 p.77-78; Ramesh Singh Part 5 p.92
## SUB 4 | Causes of NPAs
[86:4:0] - **Aggressive lending (2010-15)** to **infrastructure, steel, power** without strict appraisal → stalled projects.
[86:4:1] - Global shocks: 2008 crisis + weak exports. **Evergreening** hid defaults.
[86:4:2] - Vicious cycle: lending + shocks → NPAs → **provisioning and capital erosion** → weak credit growth.
[86:4:3] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:86 sub 4
## SUB 5 | Asset Quality Review, 4R and the GNPA Trend
[86:5:0] - **AQR by RBI in 2015**: forced recognition; disclosed NPAs jumped, **peaking in March 2018**; exposed the twin balance sheet problem.
[86:5:1] - **4R:** **Recognise, Resolve, Recapitalise, Reform**, with the **IBC**, recapitalisation and **NARCL**.
[86:5:2] TABLE head: Date | SCB GNPA
  [86:5:2.0] Mar 2015 | 4.28% (before AQR)
  [86:5:2.1] Mar 2018 | ~11.2% peak
  [86:5:2.2] Sep 2025 | 2.15%
  [86:5:2.3] Mar 2026 | 1.93%; NNPA 0.39% (decadal low)
[86:5:3] - PSBs: 4.97% → 2.58% (Mar 2015 → Mar 2025). Fall due to 4R, higher provisioning, IBC, NARCL.
[86:5:4] - **AP angle:** **Andhra Pradesh Grameena Bank** FY26 – GNPA **0.91%**, NNPA **nil**.
[86:5:5] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:86 sub 5-6 (RBI FSR figures as carried there)

[87:0:0] TABLE head: Scheme | Year | Feature
  [87:0:0.0] CDR | 2001 | Out-of-court multi-bank restructuring

## SUB 1 | Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016
[87:1:0] - Enacted **28 May 2016** (in force **2016**; CIRP from 1 Dec 2016) on the **T.K. Viswanathan Committee (2015)**; **debtor-in-possession → creditor-in-control**; replaced SICA 1985 / BIFR.
[87:1:1] - **Insolvency** = a **financial state** of not paying debts when due; **bankruptcy** = the **legal declaration / process** (assets liquidated or reorganised) that may follow – so insolvency is the state, bankruptcy the conclusion.
[87:1:2] - **IBBI** (1 Oct 2016) is the **regulator** of insolvency professionals, agencies, information utilities and valuers.
[87:1:3] - **CIRP:** default **₹1 crore**; **Committee of Creditors** votes on the plan; **180 + 90**, outer **330 days**. **Sec 29A** bars wilful defaulters / NPA promoters. **PPIRP for MSMEs (2021): 120 days**.
[87:1:4] - Waterfall: resolution costs, **workmen**, secured creditors – all above government dues. **Lalit Kumar Jain (2021)**: personal guarantors stay liable.
[87:1:5] - **IBC (Amendment) Act 2026:** admit/reject in **14 days**; liquidation **180 + 90**; **CIIRP 150 days**; "may" → "shall" in Sec 7(5)(a) (overturns **Vidarbha Industries**).
[87:1:6] - **Concerns:** deep haircuts, protracted litigation, **NCLT capacity constraints**; deterrent effect = many cases **settled before admission**; largest recovery channel.
[87:1:7] > Exam angle: State PSC statement item – only "IBBI is the regulator" is correct; the definitions of insolvency and bankruptcy are swapped in the trap statements.
[87:1:8] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Part 5 p.93-94; app notes 3:87 sub 4
## SUB 2 | Prompt Corrective Action
[87:2:0] - **PCA = Prompt Corrective Action**: an **RBI** framework (2002; revised Nov 2017 and **Nov 2021**) that puts **weak commercial banks with high NPAs / low capital** under watch – not co-operatives, State borrowing or the fiscal deficit.
[87:2:1] - Triggers (2021): **capital** (CRAR / CET1), **asset quality** (net NPA), **leverage** (Tier 1 leverage ratio). The earlier framework (still in NextGen notes) also used **profitability** (ROA), dropped in 2021.
[87:2:2] - Curbs: lending, branches, **dividends**, management pay; RBI may order capital infusion, management change, merger.
[87:2:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 02 p.25-26; app notes 3:87 sub 7

  [87:3:0.1] NCLAT (Sec 410) | Appeals from NCLT, IBBI, CCI, NFRA; New Delhi + Chennai → Supreme Court
  [87:3:0.2] DRT | Adjudicating authority for individuals and partnership firms
  [87:3:0.3] IBBI | Regulates insolvency professionals, agencies, information utilities, valuers

## SUB 5 | SARFAESI Act 2002 and ARCs
[87:5:0] - Based on the **Narasimham Committee**; lets **secured lenders** enforce security **without court** intervention.
[87:5:1] - **Sec 13(2):** lenders with **60%** of dues (75% before the **2016** amendment) issue a **60-day** notice; **Sec 13(4):** take **possession**, take over management or **sell**; **Sec 13(8):** redeem only by paying the **full dues**; **Sec 17:** appeal to the **DRT within 45 days**.
[87:5:2] - **ARCs** (created under SARFAESI) buy NPAs at a discount against security receipts; registered with and regulated by **RBI (not SEBI)**.
[87:5:3] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Part 5 p.94; Ramesh Singh Part 6 p.100; app notes 3:87 sub 2
## SUB 6 | NARCL and IDRCL
[87:6:0] - **NARCL** ("bad bank", **Budget 2021-22**) aggregates loans **above ₹500 crore** (~₹2 lakh crore); **PSBs hold majority**.
[87:6:1] - Pays **15% cash + 85% security receipts**; Govt guarantee **₹30,600 crore for 5 years**. **IDRCL** (majority private) resolves.
[87:6:2] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:87 sub 6

[87:7:1] - **Fugitive Economic Offenders Act 2018**: cases of **₹100 crore+**.
