=== Narasimham Committees I and II
| Particular | Narasimham-I | Narasimham-II |
|---|---|---|
| Year | Set up **14 Aug 1991**; report in about 3 months (reforms from 1992-93) | Set up **Dec 1997**; report **April 1998** |
| Official title | **Committee on the Financial System (CFS)** | **Committee on Banking Sector Reforms** |
| Chair | **M. Narasimham** (ex-RBI Governor) | M. Narasimham |
| Focus | Financial-sector reform: **lower SLR and CRR**, **phase out directed credit** (priority sector redefined and cut to 10% of credit), interest-rate deregulation, prudential norms, competition, ARCs, end of dual control | Banking-sector reform: merge strong banks and DFIs, close weak banks, **3-tier structure**, **CRAR 10%**, NPA management |
- **What N-I asked to cut:** **SLR** (38.5% → 25%), **CRR** (15% → 3-5%) and **priority-sector (directed) credit** (to 10%) – so "SLR, CRR and priority sector financing" (NextGen), not just "SLR and CRR". N-I did **not** set the 10% CRAR (that was N-II).
- **Three tiers (N-II):** **2-3 international banks**, **8-10 national banks**, many **local banks** – the idea of **fewer and stronger banks** through mergers and closure of weak, unviable banks.
- **Narrow banking:** weak banks to place their funds only in **risk-free assets** (G-secs) with matched maturities – a **Narasimham Committee** idea (Ramesh Singh credits CFS 1991; N-II 1998 recommended it for weak banks). Contrast **universal banking** (S.H. Khan, 1998).
- "Which committee examined financial-sector reforms / banking structure reforms / economic (banking) reforms?" → **Narasimham** (not Abid Hussain = SSI, Chelliah = tax, Bhagwati).
- Name of the 1991 committee: officially the **Committee on the Financial System**; it is popularly the **Narasimham Committee** (APPSC keys accept the official name).
- N-I also recommended **ARCs** for NPAs and **SARFAESI**-type recovery powers; RRBs could stay separate or merge with sponsor banks.
> Exam angle: N-I = Financial **System** (1991); N-II = **Banking Sector** Reforms (1998). Recommendation questions test SLR/CRR/PSL cuts (N-I) vs 3 tiers and 10% CRAR (N-II).

=== Financial Sector Reforms After 1991
- Reforms covered **banking, capital, government debt and forex markets**: **CRR and SLR cuts** (SLR **38.5%** in 1991-92), **deregulation of interest rates**, **entry of private firms in banking and insurance**, prudential norms on NPAs, end of selective credit controls – "all of the above" in such MCQs.
- **Prudential norms** (income recognition, asset classification, provisioning) and **CRAR introduced in 1992**.
- **Private bank entry:** **HDFC, ICICI** (by 1995); **FDI up to 74%** in private banks (**2004**).
- **RBI became the primary regulator**; **dual control** with the Finance Ministry's Banking Division ended.
| Year | Reform |

| 1993 | Market-determined exchange rate |
| 1995 | Banking Ombudsman |
| 1999 | FEMA replaced FERA; IRDA Act opened insurance |
| 2004 | FDI up to 74% in private banks |
- **Not a financial-sector reform:** **FRBM Act, 2003** (fiscal); bank nationalisation (1969, 1980) is pre-reform.

=== Basel Accords and the BIS
- Basel Accords are framed by the **Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS)** under the **Bank for International Settlements (BIS)**, **Basel**, Switzerland; BCBS set up end-**1974** after the failure of **Bankhaus Herstatt**.
- **BIS (est. 1930)** = the **world's oldest international financial organisation**; the central banks' bank – **not related to the UN** (MIGA, IFC, ICSID are World Bank Group bodies in the UN system).
| Accord | Year | Core content |
|---|---|---|
| **Basel I** | 1988 | **Credit risk** only; **five** risk buckets (0, 10, 20, 50, 100%); minimum capital **8%** of risk-weighted assets; India adopted 1999 (CRAR 9%) |
| **Basel II** | Proposed June 1999; framework **June 2004** | **International standards for measuring the adequacy of a bank's capital**; three pillars: **minimum capital requirements, supervisory review, market discipline** |
| **Basel III** | 2010 (reply to the 2007-09 crisis); in India from **1 Jan 2013** | **Improve banks' ability to deal with financial and economic stress, risk management, transparency**; CET1, buffers, **leverage ratio**, LCR, NSFR |
- **Basel Convention** (**UNEP**, 1989, in force 1992) = **transboundary movement of hazardous wastes** – not bank capital; not civil aviation, cyber crime or doping.
> Exam angle: Basel II/III "relates to" → capital adequacy / banking stress; BIS is the odd one out in "not related to the UN".
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 08 p.6-7; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy p.52; app notes 3:85 sub 3

=== Capital Adequacy – CRAR, Tier Capital, RBI vs BCBS
- **CRAR / CAR** = **capital ÷ risk-weighted assets**: the ratio banks must keep between their **risky assets and capital**; a standard measure of the **strength of banks' balance sheets** (ability to absorb losses). CAR and CRAR are the same ratio; "Capital-to-Risk-weighted Assets Ratio" is the precise name.
- India introduced CRAR in **1992** under BIS standards; **9%** under Basel I (1999) – stricter than BCBS's 8%.
| Norm | BCBS minimum | RBI norm |
|---|---|---|
| CET1 | 4.5% | 5.5% |
| Tier 1 | 6% | **7%** (not 8%) |
| Total CRAR | 8% | **9%** |
| Capital conservation buffer (CCB) | 2.5% | 2.5% |
| Countercyclical buffer (CCyB) | 0-2.5% | Framework since 2015, not activated |
| Leverage ratio | 3% | 4% for D-SIBs, 3.5% for others |
# Capital terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Core equity: paid-up capital, reserves, undistributed profits |
| Tier 2 | Mixed equity-debt, e.g. **subordinated debt** |
| CCyB | Extra capital in credit booms; main trigger = **credit-to-GDP gap** |
| Leverage ratio | Tier 1 capital ÷ total (non-risk-weighted) exposure |
| LCR ≥ 100% | High-quality liquid assets (HQLA) to survive a **30-day** stress; Level 1 = cash, G-secs; Level 2 = highly rated corporate bonds |
| NSFR | Stable funding over a **1-year** horizon |
- **Macroprudential tools:** **LTV caps** (loan limited to a share of collateral value), higher **risk weights** on unsecured consumer credit (Nov 2023). **Oct 2025:** RBI draft proposed **lower Basel III risk weights for MSMEs and real estate**.
> Exam angle: "Minimum Tier 1 = 8%" is the wrong statement on Basel III in India (it is 7%).

=== PSB Revival – Indradhanush, Recapitalisation, BBB/FSIB
- **Indradhanush (Aug 2015):** **7-point plan (A-G)** – Appointments, Banks Board Bureau, Capitalisation, De-stressing, Empowerment, Framework of accountability, Governance; **₹70,000 crore** budget capital over 4 years.
- **Recapitalisation (Oct 2017):** **₹2.11 lakh crore** – **₹1.35 lakh crore via recapitalisation bonds** (Govt gives bonds to PSBs, money returns as equity → no immediate cash outgo), ~₹58,000 crore raised by banks.
- **4R:** Recognition, Resolution, Recapitalisation, Reforms.
- **P.J. Nayak (2014, RBI):** weak PSB board governance; proposed a **Bank Investment Company** to hold the Govt stake, stake **below 50%**.
- **Banks Board Bureau (BBB):** proposed by P.J. Nayak, part of Indradhanush; from **1 April 2016**; **selected chairmen / whole-time directors of PSBs** and boards of PSU insurers and FIs.
- **Replaced (1 July 2022) by the Financial Services Institutions Bureau (FSIB)**, which now recommends PSB chairpersons and whole-time directors.

=== EASE Reforms and the EASE Index
- **EASE = Enhanced Access and Service Excellence**: conceptualised 2015, launched **2017**; PSB Reforms Agenda from **Jan 2018**; for **public sector banks only** (not NBFCs).
- Run by the **IBA's EASE Steering Committee** (not a board of heads of premier FIs); PSBs report quarterly against benchmarks.
- **EASE Index**: **measures the performance of PSBs** on reforms (quarterly benchmarking of mid-size and large PSBs) – **not** MSME ease of doing business.
- Themes: digital transformation, **customer service with digital enablement**, risk management, governance. **EASE 8.0 "EASE ₹ise" (FY2026):** Risk and Resilience, Innovation, Socio-economic impact, Excellence.
> Exam angle: APPSC G1 statements – "governed by a board of FI heads" and "for PSBs and NBFCs" are false.

=== DICGC and the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act 2025
- **DICGC**: **DICGC Act, 1961**; set up **1978** by merging the Deposit Insurance Corporation (1962) and the Credit Guarantee Corporation (1971); a **wholly owned RBI subsidiary**.
- Cover **₹5 lakh per depositor per bank** (from 4 Feb 2020; raised from ₹1 lakh in **Budget 2020-21**, after the **PMC Bank** crisis).
- Covers commercial banks (incl. **Indian branches of foreign banks**), RRBs, co-operative banks; excludes **government, inter-bank and NBFC** deposits. **Premium paid by the insured bank**.
- **Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025** (in force **1 Aug 2025**): "substantial interest" ₹5 lakh → **₹2 crore**; co-operative bank directors' tenure 8 → **10 years**.

=== Privatisation Debate
- **Budget 2021-22**: privatisation of **two PSBs and one general insurance company** announced.
- **IDBI Bank**: **LIC** bought a majority stake in **2019** → RBI reclassified it as a **private sector lender**; **60.72%** stake on sale.
- **For:** efficiency, less fiscal burden. **Against:** **social mandate** – unions say it would weaken **priority-sector lending, rural credit and inclusion**.
