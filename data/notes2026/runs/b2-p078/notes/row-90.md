=== Background, Chronology and Enactment
# Chronology
| Year | Landmark |
|---|---|
| **1766** | Sweden – Freedom of the Press Act: world's first RTI law |
| 1948 | UN – UDHR Article 19: right to seek and receive information |
| **1990** | **MKSS** (Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan), Rajasthan – **Aruna Roy**, Nikhil Dey, Shankar Singh; jan sunwais on muster rolls started the RTI movement |
| **1997** | **Tamil Nadu**: first Indian State RTI law (Goa the same year) |
| **2002** | **Freedom of Information Act 2002** – never notified; **repealed by the RTI Act 2005** |
| 2005 | Right to Information Act, 2005 |
- Order: **Sweden → UN → FOI Act → RTI Act**.
# Enactment
| Step | Date |
|---|---|
| Lok Sabha passed | 11 May 2005 |
| Rajya Sabha passed | 12 May 2005 |
| **Presidential assent** (A.P.J. Abdul Kalam) | **15 June 2005** |

=== Preamble, Objectives and Nature of the Right
- Preamble terms: **transparency of information**, **revelation of information**, **informed citizenry**, **paramountcy of the democratic ideal** (all four).
- Objectives: transparency and accountability, **answerability**, **containing corruption**, empowering citizens, making democracy work for the people – **NOT** "empowering women and weaker sections".
- RTI **mandates a timely response** to citizens' requests for government information (not NeGP, Aadhaar or RTE).
- Nature: a **statutory (legal) right** under the 2005 Act; the **right to know** is also read into **Article 19(1)(a)** (State of U.P. v. Raj Narain, 1975) – hence "both" a legal and a fundamental right in most keys.
- **Namit Sharma v. Union of India (2012)** – RTI case on Information Commissions.
- Impact: raised the **sense of responsibility in bureaucracy**, but "miles to go" (both true, R does not explain A); the Act is **misused by both** applicants and public authorities.

=== Key Sections – Who May Ask, What Is Covered
| Section | Subject |
|---|---|
| 2(f) | "Information": records, documents, memos, e-mails, opinions, advices, press releases, circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, reports, samples, models, electronic data |
| 2(h) | "Public authority": bodies under the Constitution, a law or notification; bodies and NGOs **substantially financed** by government |
| **2(j)** | Right to: **inspection of work, documents, records**; notes, extracts, certified copies; **certified samples**; information in **diskettes, floppies, tapes, video cassettes** or electronic form – **not taking files out of the office** |
| **3** | **All citizens** have the right (not "any person" or companies) |
| **4** | **Proactive (suo motu) disclosure**; 4(1)(b) must publish organisation, functions, **procedure followed in decision-making**, norms, budgets, PIOs |
| 5 | PIOs / APIOs designated by each public authority |
| 6 | Request in English, Hindi or the official language of the area; **6(2) no reason** need be given |
| 7 | Disposal – 30 days / 48 hours; **7(5) no fee for BPL** applicants |
| 11 | Third-party information |
| 12 / 15 | Constitute CIC / SIC |
| 18 | Complaints and inquiry by the Commission |
| 19 | Appeals; 19(8)(b) compensation |
| 20 | Penalties on PIOs |
| 22 | Overrides the Official Secrets Act, 1923 |
| 24 | Second Schedule intelligence/security bodies excluded – except **corruption and human-rights** information (CBI added 2011) |
| 25 | Annual report, laid before Parliament/Legislature |
- "Obtaining security information" is **not** part of the right to information.

=== Time Limits
| Stage | Limit |
|---|---|
| Normal reply by PIO (**Sec. 7(1)**) | **30 days** |
| **Life or liberty** | **48 hours** |
| Through APIO | 35 days (30 + 5) |
| Third-party (Sec. 11) | 40 days |
| Human-rights allegations vs Section 24 bodies | 45 days (with Commission approval) |
| **First appeal** | Filed within **30 days**; decided in **30 days, extendable to 45** with reasons recorded (Sec. 19(6)) |
| **Second appeal** to CIC/SIC | Within **90 days** |
- "Normal reply in 45 days" and "second appeal in 180 days" are the standard wrong pairs.

=== Exemptions – Sections 8, 9 and 24
| Clause 8(1) | Exempt information |
|---|---|
| (a) | Sovereignty, security, foreign relations, **incitement of an offence** |
| (b) | Forbidden by a court / **contempt of court** |
| (c) | **Breach of privilege of Parliament** or State Legislature |
| (d) | **Commercial confidence, trade secrets, intellectual property** – **unless larger public interest** warrants disclosure (conditional) |
| (e) | Fiduciary relationship |
| (f) | Information received **in confidence from a foreign Government** |
| (i) | **Cabinet papers** incl. deliberations of the Council of Ministers (decisions made public once complete) |
| (j) | Personal information (rewritten as a blanket exemption by DPDP Act 2023, s.44(3)) |
- **Procedure followed in decision-making** is **not exempt** – Section 4(1)(b) requires its publication.
- **8(2)** public-interest override; **8(3)** most exemptions lapse after **20 years**; **Section 9**: copyright of a person other than the State.
- Information that cannot be denied to Parliament cannot be denied to any person.
- **Second ARC**: maximum information in the public domain; read Section 8 **narrowly**; link RTI with **whistle-blower protection**.

=== Information Commissions and Penalties
| Item | CIC | SIC |
|---|---|---|
| Composition | Chief IC + up to 10 ICs | State Chief IC + up to 10 SICs |
| Appointed by | **President** | **Governor** |
| Committee | **PM** (chair), LoP in Lok Sabha, Union Cabinet Minister nominated by PM | **CM** (chair), **LoP in the Assembly**, **State Cabinet Minister nominated by the CM** |
| Removal for misbehaviour | President, after **Supreme Court** inquiry | Governor, after Supreme Court inquiry |
- **No Chief Justice** (CJI or High Court CJ) on either committee.
- Eligibility: eminence in law, S&T, social service, management, **journalism**, mass media, administration; **cannot be an MP or a member of any State Legislature**, hold office of profit or be linked to a party.
- Statutory bodies with **civil-court powers**; term now **prescribed by the Centre (3 years)** or 65 (RTI Amendment Act 2019; earlier 5 years).
- **Section 20 penalty**: imposed by the **CIC or SIC** (not courts) – **₹250 per day, max ₹25,000**; burden of proof on the PIO; Commission can also recommend disciplinary action.
- **AP SIC**: **Vajji (Vajja) Srinivasa Rao** appointed Chief Information Commissioner (Jan 2026) with four ICs; 2026 inspection of 258 offices found Section 4(1)(b) violations.
- Before 2019: CIC's salary = **Chief Election Commissioner**, IC = Election Commissioner; State CIC = Election Commissioner, State IC = **Chief Secretary**; term was **5 years or 65**.
- Contrast **CVC**: CVC + up to 2 Vigilance Commissioners; **4 years or 65**.

- Issues: **pendency** (over 4 lakh appeals, Jul 2026), **vacancies**, 2019 amendment weakening autonomy, DPDP 8(1)(j) change (Constitution Bench, 2026), attacks on activists; **Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014** not operationalised.
- RTI Online portal (DoPT, 2013); AP HC (Nov 2023) directed an online RTI portal; SIC website sic.ap.gov.in (8 Sep 2025).
- Political parties: inclusion meant to check **corruption, crony capitalism** and illegal **foreign contributions**; the RTI (Amendment) Bill 2013 to exclude them **lapsed**.
- MPLADS works are covered and tracked on **eSAKSHI**; Madras HC's 2014 remark that applicants state reasons was **withdrawn** (Sec. 6(2)).
- DPDP change: notified Nov 2025 → Constitution Bench reference **Feb 2026** → SC to examine the "en bloc embargo" (7 Aug 2026).
- **Economic Survey 2025-26**: re-examine RTI – exempt **confidential service records and draft policy discussions**; **Satark Nagrik Sangathan** 2025 report card found gaps; **more than 100** RTI activists killed (Jul 2026); AP secretariat publishes ATRs on seven sector white papers (22 June 2026).
