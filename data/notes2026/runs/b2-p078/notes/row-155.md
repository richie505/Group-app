=== Security Council
- **15 members**: **5 permanent (P5) – China, France, Russia, UK, USA** + **10 non-permanent**.
- Non-permanent members: elected by the **UNGA**, two-thirds secret ballot, for **2 years**; **no immediate re-election (Art. 23)**.
- **Veto (Art. 27)**: only the P5; a P5 negative vote on a **substantive** matter blocks a decision; **procedural matters need 9 affirmative votes and no veto applies**. The Charter never uses the word "veto" (Art. 27(3) speaks of the "concurring votes of the permanent members").
- **Germany, Japan, India, Canada are NOT** permanent members; India has been an elected (non-permanent) member, never a permanent one.
- Only organ whose decisions **bind** all members (Ch. VII: Art. 41 sanctions, Art. 42 military action).
- **P5 + 1** (Iran nuclear talks, JCPOA 2015): the **+1 = Germany**.
- Resolutions: **2328 (Dec 2016)** – UN monitors for evacuations from **Aleppo** (Syria); **2428 (July 2018)** – arms embargo on **South Sudan**.
- **April 2024**: full UN membership for **Palestine** – **vetoed by the USA**; UK and Switzerland abstained.

=== Charter, Founding, Languages and UN Days
- **Atlantic Charter (1941)** → **Declaration by United Nations (1 Jan 1942)** – name "United Nations" devised by US President **Franklin D. Roosevelt**.
- Charter signed by **50 nations** at the **San Francisco Conference**, **26 June 1945**; **Poland** signed later (ratified 16 Oct 1945) as the **51st original member**.
- In force **24 October 1945** = **UN founded** = **UN Day**.
- Members: **193**.
- **Six official languages**: **Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish**. **Working languages** (Secretariat): **English and French**. **Hindi is NOT** an official UN language.
- **UNGA resolution on multilingualism (June 2022, India co-sponsored)**: mentioned **Hindi** for the first time (also Bangla and Urdu).
- **Amendment** (e.g. UNSC reform): **two-thirds** of members, incl. ratification by **all P5** (Art. 108).
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| Art. 1 | Purposes – peace and security, friendly relations, cooperation, harmonising action |
| **Art. 2** | **Sovereign equality** |
| Art. 18 | UNGA "important questions" = two-thirds majority |
| Art. 23 | No immediate re-election of a retiring non-permanent UNSC member |
| **Art. 27** | Voting in the UNSC; P5 veto |
- **UN Decade of Sustainable Energy for All (2014–2024)**: declared by the **UN General Assembly**.

=== International Court of Justice
- **Principal judicial organ**; seat **The Hague** (Netherlands) – the only principal organ outside New York.
- **15 judges**, **9-year** terms (one-third elected every three years); elected by the **UNGA and UNSC voting separately** (absolute majority in both).
- Indian judges: **B.N. Rau**, **Nagendra Singh** (ICJ President **1985-88**), **R.S. Pathak**, **Dalveer Bhandari**.
- **Dalveer Bhandari**: elected **27 April 2012** (remaining term to Feb 2018); **re-elected** (second time) from 6 Feb 2018 for nine years.
- **Kulbhushan Jadhav case (2019)**: ICJ **stayed his execution**; Pakistan breached the **Vienna Convention on Consular Relations**; review of conviction ordered.
- **July 2025**: unanimous **advisory opinion on climate** obligations.
> Exam angle: 15 = UNSC members and ICJ judges; 9 = ICJ term; 54 = ECOSOC.

=== Principal Organs, General Assembly and ECOSOC
- **Six principal organs**: **General Assembly, Security Council, ECOSOC, Trusteeship Council, ICJ, Secretariat**. Amnesty International is an NGO, not a UN organ.
| Organ | Composition / mandate |
|---|---|
| **General Assembly** | All 193 members, one vote each – the "**World Parliament**"; meets in **annual** regular session |
| Security Council | 15 members; binding decisions |
| **ECOSOC** | **54 members**, **3-year terms**, **elected by the UNGA** (two-thirds majority, Art. 18) |
| Trusteeship Council | Inactive since **1994**, after **Palau** became self-governing |
| ICJ | Judicial organ, The Hague |
| Secretariat | Headed by the Secretary-General |
- **UDHR (10 Dec 1948)** = a **UN General Assembly resolution (217 A)** – not a treaty.
- UNGA: important questions need two-thirds (Art. 18); **Credentials Committee** – **9** members each session.
- **Uniting for Peace (Res. 377), 1950**: lets the UNGA act when a veto blocks the UNSC; first used in the **1956 Suez Crisis** to send peacekeepers.
- Emergency special sessions: the 10th – resolution ES-10/23 (10 May 2024) upgraded Palestine's rights as an observer State.
- **Non-member observer States** (2): **Holy See** (since 1964) and **State of Palestine** (2012).
- **Sweden** (Oct 2014) – first EU member state (while a member) to officially recognise the State of Palestine.
- **Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit** – President of the 8th UNGA (1953), the first woman (and first Indian) to hold the post.
- **81st UNGA President** (2026-27): **Khalilur Rahman** (Bangladesh).
- 22 May 2026 climate resolution: India abstained (141-8-28).

=== UNSC Reform
| Group | Members / demand |
|---|---|
| **G4** | **Brazil, Germany, India, Japan** – support each other's permanent seats, plus seats for Africa (**China is not** in it – already P5) |
| Uniting for Consensus ("Coffee Club") | Led by **Italy**; includes Pakistan; **opposes new permanent seats** |
| L.69 | Developing countries of Asia, Africa, Latin America, Pacific; India a member |
| Ezulwini Consensus (2005) | African Union: **2 permanent seats with veto + 5 non-permanent** |
- G4 model: expand the UNSC from 15 to 25–26.
- **IGN** (Inter-Governmental Negotiations): informal **UNGA** process, set up **2008**; annual "Elements Paper". India demands text-based negotiations (June 2026).
- **China** opposes India's permanent seat.
