=== Eleventh Schedule – 29 Subjects (Article 243G)
- **Eleventh Schedule:** added by the **73rd Amendment**; **29 functional items** of the Panchayats, linked to **Article 243G** – not 18 (that is the **Twelfth Schedule**, Municipalities).
- **Nature:** a menu – the State Legislature "**may**" devolve them; it divides powers between the **State Legislature and the Panchayats** (not Union–State, not Gram Sabha–Panchayat).
- **First item:** **Agriculture**, including agricultural extension. **Last (29th) item:** **Maintenance of community assets**.
| No. | Items (Eleventh Schedule) |
|---|---|
| 1–7 | **Agriculture**; land improvement, land reforms, land consolidation, soil conservation; minor irrigation, watershed; animal husbandry, dairying, poultry; **fisheries**; social and farm forestry; minor forest produce |
| 8–15 | Small-scale industries incl. food processing; Khadi, village and cottage industries; rural housing; drinking water; **fuel and fodder**; **roads**, culverts, bridges, ferries, waterways; **rural electrification**; **non-conventional energy sources** |
| 16–22 | **Poverty alleviation programme**; education incl. **primary and secondary** schools; **technical training and vocational education**; adult and non-formal education; **libraries** (item 20); cultural activities; **markets and fairs** |
| 23–29 | Health and sanitation; family welfare; **women and child development**; social welfare incl. handicapped; welfare of weaker sections, SCs and STs; **public distribution system**; **maintenance of community assets** |
- **NOT in the Eleventh Schedule:** **higher education**, **rural sports**, **public order**, police; and the **Twelfth Schedule** (municipal) items – **slum improvement and upgradation**, **regulation of land use**, **urban planning**, **slaughter houses**, **burials and burial grounds**, **fire services**, cattle pounds.
- **Model Accounting System (MAS) for PRIs:** prescribed by the **C&AG** (with MoPR, 2009); its **major accounting heads follow the 29 Eleventh Schedule functions** – so PRIASoft keeps **29** major heads.
> Most-asked angle: the number (29) – asked 10+ times; then "which is / is NOT an item" (libraries vs land use, slum improvement, higher education, rural sports).

=== The 73rd Amendment and Part IX – Significance and Articles 243 to 243O
- **73rd Amendment Act, 1992:** deals with **Panchayati Raj** (rural local government); the **74th** deals with Municipalities (Nagar Palika). Bill introduced **September 1991** (Narasimha Rao Government), Act of **1992**, in force **24 April 1993**.
- **Third tier of the federal system:** added in **1992** (73rd and 74th Acts; in force 1993).
- Added **Part IX "The Panchayats"** – **Articles 243 to 243O** = **16 Articles** – and the **Eleventh Schedule**.
- Gave a **practical shape to Article 40** (DPSP: organise village panchayats as **units of self-government**) and **constitutional status** to PRIs.
- **Justiciable part:** it brought PRIs under the **justiciable part** of the Constitution – States are under a **constitutional obligation** to adopt the system; formation of panchayats and regular elections no longer depend on the State Government's will.
- **Significance:** a **landmark in the evolution of grassroots democratic institutions**; turns **representative into participatory democracy**; rural local bodies **rejuvenated**.
- It did **not** create **caste panchayats** or khap panchayats – only the **Gram Panchayat** is a body under the Panchayati Raj system.
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| 243 | Definitions – Gram Sabha (243(b)), intermediate level, Panchayat, village |
| **243A** | **Gram Sabha** |
| **243B** | **Constitution of Panchayats** – three tiers in every State |
| **243C** | **Composition** – by State law; direct election of members |
| **243D** | **Reservation of seats** (SC/ST, women, BCs optional) |
| 243E | Duration – 5 years |
| 243F | Disqualifications – minimum age 21 |
| **243G** | **Powers, authority and responsibilities** of Panchayats – Eleventh Schedule |
| **243H** | **Power to impose taxes**; Panchayat funds |
| 243I | State Finance Commission |
| **243J** | **Audit of accounts** of Panchayats |
| **243K** | **State Election Commission** |
| 243L | Application to Union Territories |
| **243M** | Part not to apply to certain areas |
| 243N | Continuance of existing laws |
| **243O** | **Bar to interference by courts** in electoral matters (last Article) |
- **Article 243G:** powers and functions of **Panchayats** (village, intermediate, district) – not finances (243H), not Nyaya Panchayats.
- **Article 243J:** the **State Legislature** may provide for maintenance and **audit of Panchayat accounts**.
- **Pair check (CGPSC 2024):** 243A = Gram Sabha, 243B = Constitution, **243C = Composition** – only the 243C pair is right.
- **Exemption Articles:** **243M** (Part IX) vs **243ZC** (Part IXA); Part IXA = **243P–243ZG**, Twelfth Schedule, 18 subjects, in force **1 June 1993**.
- **Ministry of Panchayati Raj:** created **27 May 2004**; handles Part IX, PESA and District Planning Committees.
> A-R (APPSC G2): "73rd CAA is a landmark for grassroots democracy" (A) because "it brought PRIs under the justiciable part" (R) – both true, R explains A (Laxmikanth's own linkage).

=== Gram Sabha – Article 243(b) and 243A
- **Gram Sabha (Art. 243(b)):** body of **persons registered in the electoral rolls** relating to a village within the Panchayat area – i.e. **all registered voters**, not all residents, all adults, elders or employees.
- **Minimum age:** **18 years** (voting age); the **age to contest** a Panchayat seat is **21**.
- **Article 243A:** Gram Sabha exercises such powers at the village level as the **State Legislature provides by law** – in function it is to the village what the **State Legislature** is to the State.
- **Compulsory:** a Gram Sabha **must be constituted in each village** (or group of villages) – not optional, not abolishable; it is the **electorate** for direct elections (243C).
- **Direct democracy:** the **Gram Sabha** is the best example – voters decide directly; Gram Panchayat, Nagar Panchayat and Zila Panchayat are representative bodies; caste panchayats, opinion polls and people's courts are not.
- **Meetings:** at least **twice a year** (NextGen: April 13 and Oct 3; State Acts fix dates); Gram Sabha meetings may be held in each village (by rotation); Gram Panchayat meetings at the GP headquarters.
# Gram Sabha's statutory roles
- **MGNREGA social audit:** vested in the **Gram Sabha** (MGNREGA s.17).
- **PMAY-Gramin:** the **Gram Sabha verifies the housing-deprivation (SECC) data** for selecting beneficiaries.
# Gram Panchayat Development Plan (GPDP)
- **People's Plan Campaign "Sabki Yojana Sabka Vikas":** from **2 October 2018**; GPDPs prepared through Gram Sabhas.
- **GPDP** converges all village plans – sanitation, MGNREGS labour budget, nutrition, agriculture, school education, water, livelihoods, natural-resource and disaster plans (**all of them**).
- **Vital first step:** **collection of data and situation analysis**, before prioritisation and resource mapping.
- **Viksit Gram Panchayat Plans** are linked to **PM Gati Shakti**.

=== Three-tier Structure, Composition, Term and Qualification
- **Article 243B:** Panchayats at **village, intermediate and district** levels in **every State**; a State with population **not exceeding 20 lakh may not** constitute the intermediate tier – so intermediate Panchayats do **not** exist in all States ("may not", not "shall not").
- **Composition (243C):** made by the **Legislature of the State** by law; a **Gram Panchayat can cover more than one village**.
- **Area:** the intermediate level and villages are specified by the **Governor / State Government** by public notification (e.g. the territory of a Kshetra Panchayat).
| Who | How chosen |
|---|---|
| **Members at all three levels** (incl. Panchayat Samiti) | **Directly elected** from territorial constituencies |
| Intermediate and district chairpersons | **Indirectly**, by and from among elected members |
| Village chairperson (Sarpanch) | As **State law** provides |
| MPs, MLAs, MLCs | May be given representation (voluntary) |
- **Qualification (243F):** minimum age **21 years** (not 25, not 30); other disqualifications by State law.
- **Term (243E):** **5 years from the date of the first meeting**; if dissolved, fresh polls **within six months** (not three); no poll if **less than six months** remain.
- **Reconstituted Panchayat:** serves only the **remainder** of the term – first meeting **10 Feb 2014** → new body ends **10 Feb 2019**.
- **Proxy rule:** a woman Sarpanch **cannot nominate her husband** to attend meetings ("Sarpanch-pati" is not legal); ex-officio MLAs/MPs may send representatives under some State Acts.
- **Nominated members** attend but have no vote (State Acts).
| Intermediate tier name | State |
|---|---|
| **Mandal Parishad** (Mandal Praja Parishad) | **Andhra Pradesh** |
| **Janpad Panchayat** | **Madhya Pradesh** (also Chhattisgarh) |
| **Kshetra Panchayat** | **Uttar Pradesh** (Uttarakhand) |
| **Taluka Panchayat / Taluka Parishad** | **Gujarat** (Taluk Panchayat – Karnataka) |
| **Anchalik Panchayat** | **Assam** |
| Panchayat Samiti | Most States, e.g. Rajasthan, Maharashtra, West Bengal |
- **CGPSC 2019 code** pairs Anchalik Parishad with **West Bengal** (code 3 4 2 1); Anchalik Panchayat is Assam's term.
- **AP tiers:** Gram Panchayat – **Mandal Parishad** – Zilla Parishad.
> Statement traps: "intermediate tier in all States", "minimum age 25/30", "elections within 3 months", "Panchayats have constitutional autonomy" – all wrong.

=== State Election Commission and State Finance Commission
# State Election Commission (Article 243K)
- **SEC:** superintendence, direction and control of **preparing electoral rolls** and **conducting all elections to the Panchayats** – not the Election Commission of India; municipal polls under **243ZA** by the same SEC "referred to in 243K".
- **State Election Commissioner:** appointed by the **Governor** (conditions and tenure also by the Governor); removable only like a **High Court judge**; service conditions cannot be varied to his disadvantage.
- **Law on Panchayat elections:** made by the **State Legislature** (243K(4)).
- **Constitutional authorities:** **SEC (243K)**, **SFC (243I)** and the **District Panchayat (243B)**; the **Chief Electoral Officer** is a statutory officer (RPA 1950, s.13A).
- **Created by the 73rd and 74th Amendments:** **SEC, SFC, District Planning Committee (243ZD, 74th)** – not a property-tax valuation board.
- **Rajasthan SEC:** constituted in **July 1994** (Governor's order 17 June 1994, working from 1 July 1994); its **Secretary is also the Chief Electoral Officer** of the State.
- **Madhya Pradesh SEC:** constituted **1 February 1994**; Panchayat general elections held **five** times by 2020 (1994, 1999-2000, 2004-05, 2009-10, 2014-15).
# State Finance Commission (Article 243I)
- Constituted by the **Governor** within one year of 24 April 1993 and then **every five years**; reviews Panchayat finances (and Municipalities' – **243Y**).
- **Recommends** to the Governor: sharing of **net proceeds of State taxes**, duties, tolls and fees between State and Panchayats; **taxes assigned** to Panchayats; **grants-in-aid**; measures to improve finances.
- Governor lays the report before the **State Legislature** with an **action-taken memorandum**; it does **not** appoint the State Election Commissioner.
- **Article 243H:** the **State Legislature** may authorise Panchayats to **levy, collect and appropriate** taxes, duties, tolls and fees.
- **Own tax domain:** the **Gram Panchayat** has the main own taxes (house/property tax), so it is better placed for own revenue; the intermediate and district tiers depend mostly on grants, tolls and fees.
- **Union FC link:** **Art. 280(3)(bb)** (Panchayats) and **280(3)(c)** (Municipalities) – measures to augment a State's Consolidated Fund on SFC recommendations.
| Basis | Union FC | SFC |
|---|---|---|
| Article | 280 | **243I** (Panchayats), **243Y** (Municipalities) |
| Appointed by | President | **Governor** |
| Divides | Union ↔ States | State ↔ local bodies |
> Code trap: "SFC = Article 243" (it is 243I); "SEC appointed by CEC/President" (it is the Governor).

=== Compulsory vs Voluntary Provisions
| Compulsory | Voluntary (State's choice) |
|---|---|
| Gram Sabha in a village or group of villages | Powers of the Gram Sabha |
| Three tiers (middle optional, 20 lakh or less) | Manner of electing the village chairperson |
| Direct election of all members; indirect election of intermediate/district chairpersons | Representation of MPs, MLAs and chairpersons in higher tiers |
| Minimum age 21 | **Reservation for backward classes** |
| SC/ST seats in proportion to population | **Devolution of the 29 Eleventh Schedule subjects** |
| **One-third seats and chairpersons for women** | Financial powers – taxes, assigned taxes, grants, funds |
| 5-year term; fresh polls within six months | – |
| **State Election Commission**; **SFC every 5 years** | – |
- **Strictly:** only the **constitution** of the SFC is mandatory – its recommendations (transfer of funds) are **advisory**, and **devolution of the 29 subjects** is discretionary; that voluntary devolution is the main cause of **uneven devolution** of funds, functions and functionaries.
- **NOT a provision of the 73rd:** a **two-tier** system, caste panchayats, compulsory BC reservation, "18 functional items".
- **Courts:** the Act **bars court interference** in Panchayat electoral matters (243O) – a correct statement.

=== Reservation – Article 243D
- **Article 243D:** seats for **SCs and STs** in proportion to population at all levels; **not less than one-third** of seats (and of chairpersons, **243D(4)**) for **women** – a floor, not a ceiling, not "50% for SC/ST women".
- **Rotation:** reserved seats are **allotted by rotation** – not fixed for 10/25/50 years.
- **BC reservation:** optional (**243D(6)**).
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **K. Krishna Murthy v. Union of India** | 2010 | Upheld **243D(6)/243T(6)**; political backwardness ≠ social/educational backwardness; vertical reservation normally within 50% |
| **Vikas Kishanrao Gawali v. Maharashtra** | 2021 | **Triple test**: dedicated commission, local-body-wise proportion, **SC+ST+OBC within 50%** |
- **AP:** **34% BC quota** plus SC/ST shares must stay within Gawali's **50% ceiling**.
# Women's 50% and data
- **Bihar** – first to give **50%** to women (2006). By **2009-10**, **Bihar, Himachal Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh** had 50% (Govt statement, 112th Amendment Bill 2009); many States now, including **AP**.
- **MoPR, April 2026:** **12.14 lakh of 24.41 lakh** elected PRI representatives are women (**49.75%**); short of 50%: **Haryana 42.12%**, Gujarat 49.9%, Tripura 45.25%.
- **Proxy problem:** IRMA's **State of Panchayats Report 2008-09** – about **59%** of SC/ST women representatives were proxies.
- **2026:** **Sashakt Panchayat Netri Abhiyan** and **Model Women-Friendly Gram Panchayats** – against token representation.
- **EWS link:** **Gujarat** was the first State to implement the 10% EWS quota (January 2019).
