## SUB 0 | Rural Community and Village Studies
[0:0] # The village as a unit
[0:1] - **Rural sociology:** the study of rural society – its **social structure, institutions, change and conflict** in villages.
[0:2] - **Elements of a rural community:** **territory, we-feeling, relative self-sufficiency, small size, homogeneity**.
[0:3]   - **⚠ Trap:** **cultural diversity** is NOT a rural element; diversity and heterogeneity are urban (UPPSC 2021).
[0:4] - **Charles Metcalfe:** villages as self-reliant **"little republics"**.
[0:5] - **Robert Redfield**, *The Little Community* (1955): the village as a **"little community"** with four traits – **distinctiveness, smallness, homogeneity, self-sufficiency** (TSPSC 2023; CSP 2002). Tonnies = Gemeinschaft-Gesellschaft; Maine = "village communities".
[0:6] - **Myth of self-sufficiency:** **M.N. Srinivas and A.M. Shah** ("The Myth of the Self-Sufficiency of the Indian Village", 1960) – villages depended on outside markets; Srinivas debunked villages as unchanging, self-sufficient "little republics".
[0:7] - **Andre Beteille** (1980): "the village was not merely a place where people lived; it had a **design** in which were reflected the **basic values of Indian civilisation**".
[0:8] - **S.C. Dube:** argued for **systematic study of village communities for better planning**; his Shamirpet study (Osmania University project) was a laboratory for rural development programmes.

## SUB 1 | Village Economy – Jajmani, Peasants and Agrarian Society
[1:0] # Jajmani system
[1:1] - **Jajmani:** a socio-economic and ritual arrangement in which one caste secures the service of another; each works for a family with whom it is **hereditarily linked** – between landholder patron (**jajman**) and service castes (**kamin**).
[1:2] - **Exchange by tradition:** goods and services pass by **custom and tradition**, not law or market value of goods.
[1:3] - **Features:** **functional reciprocity** between castes within the **village social structure**; the **Mahalwari system** is a British land-revenue settlement, not a jajmani feature (CSP 1998).
[1:4] - **First scientific study:** **William Wiser**, *The Hindu Jajmani System* (1936), a UP village.
[1:5] # Peasants and agrarian society
[1:6] - **Peasant (rythu, kisan):** **owns/holds land and cultivates it with the labour of family members** (Thorner's kisan); one who cultivates only through hired labour is a landlord-farmer.
[1:7] - **Agricultural vs industrial society:** differ in **premises, method of production and social dynamics** – all of these.
[1:8] - Forms of exchange (Polanyi): **reciprocity, redistribution, market exchange**; "antagonism" is not a form of exchange.
[1:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society Group 2 Part 3 p.35; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 4 p.5; general knowledge (Wiser 1936, Thorner, Polanyi)
## SUB 2 | Urban Society and Census Data
[2:0] - Urban life: **competition, impersonal relations, anonymity, weak control** – **not informal ties** (JPSC 2021).
[2:1] - **Louis Wirth**, *Urbanism as a Way of Life* (**1938**): **size, density, heterogeneity**.
[2:2] - **Urbanism** = social-cultural side; **urbanisation** = population shift.
[2:3] - **Urban area** = statutory towns (Municipality, Corporation, Cantonment Board, Notified Town Area Committee) + census towns.
[2:4] - **Census town:** **5,000** people; **75%** (not 60%) of male main workers non-farm; **400/sq km**; unchanged since **1981**; kept for **Census 2027** (17 Sep 2026), though MoHUA called it outdated.
[2:5] - Urban share **11% (1901) → 27.81% (2001) → 31.16% (2011)**; centres 1,827 → 7,935.
[2:6] - **2001-11 urban jump:** mainly **reclassification** of villages as census towns (about **2,500** added); urban share 31.1%.
[2:7] - **Largest UAs (2001):** **Greater Mumbai > Kolkata > Delhi**; by 2011 Delhi passed Kolkata.
[2:8] - 2011: **68.8% rural**, **6,40,867 villages**.
[2:9] - **Hidden urbanisation** (DEGURBA): ~**63%** urban (2015).
[2:10] - **16th FC (2026-31):** ULB grant share **36% → 45%**; 41% urban by 2031.
[2:11] > Census criteria angle: "60% of male workers non-agricultural" is the wrong criterion – it is 75%.
[2:12] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:115 sub 2 (Census 2011, ES 2025-26, 16th FC); general knowledge (Wirth)
## SUB 3 | Rural-urban Continuum and Urbanisation
[3:0] - **Redfield:** **folk-urban continuum** (Mexico, **Yucatan**) – a gradual scale, not two boxes.
[3:1] TABLE head: Feature | Village | Town/City
  [3:1.0] Occupation | Agriculture | Non-agricultural
  [3:1.1] Size | Small, sparse | Large, dense
  [3:1.2] Relations | Primary, face-to-face | Secondary, impersonal
  [3:1.3] Control | Informal (caste, custom) | Formal (law, police)
[3:2] - Village-to-town transition begins with **traders and manufacturers** (not wholly dependent on village resources).

  [3:4.2] Moral order (temple centres) | Technical order

## SUB 4 | Urban Problems and Migration
[4:0] - **Industrialisation** drives urbanisation; **density** raises **crime**; maladjustment, rich-poor segregation, alienation.
[4:1] - **ES 2025-26:** informality (slums, informal labour) is **structural** – integrate, not eradicate; cities lack **fiscal autonomy**; **35%** rural households lack healthcare within 5 km.
[4:2] - Caste persists through slums and job networks.
[4:3] - **Jockin (Joachim) Arputham:** Magsaysay awardee (2000); President of the **National Slum Dwellers' Federation**; accompanied **Prince Charles** in **Dharavi** (Mumbai), among Asia's largest slums (5 Nov 2003).
[4:4] # Migration
[4:5] - **Jan Breman**, *Footloose Labour* (**1996**): circular migrants in seasonal, casual, informal jobs.
[4:6] - Sources: **UP, Bihar**; push: monsoon failure, unemployment; urban wages **1.7-2x** rural.
[4:7] - **SPMRM (2016):** "rural soul, urban amenities"; **PMEGP** (KVIC).
[4:8] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:115 sub 4, 6; general knowledge (Arputham)
## SUB 5 | Tribal Society
[5:0] - Features: common **name, dialect**, territory, ancestor, **animism/totemism**, clans, **youth dormitories**, simple economy.
[5:1] TABLE head: Tribe | Peasant
  [5:1.0] Isolated, kinship-based, egalitarian | Part of wider caste-class society
  [5:1.1] Common land and forest | Market-linked; individual land
[5:2] - **N.K. Bose:** Hindu method of tribal absorption; **Surajit Sinha:** **tribe-caste model (continuum)** of tribal amalgamation (CSP 1999); **T.K. Oommen:** tribal autonomy.
[5:3] - 1960s debate: tribes as one end of a continuum with caste-based peasant society vs a wholly distinct type (no purity-pollution).
[5:4] - STs: **89.97% rural**; **705** groups.
