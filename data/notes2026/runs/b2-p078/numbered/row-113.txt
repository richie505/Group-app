## SUB 0 | Culture Contact – Acculturation, Assimilation, Pluralism, Ethnocentrism
[0:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:0.0] Acculturation | Change through sustained contact: a group is influenced by another (dominant) culture and its way of life changes, but it stays distinct
  [0:0.1] Assimilation ("merger") | A group (e.g. immigrants) loses its own cultural identity and is absorbed into the host/dominant culture
  [0:0.2] Integration | Taking part in the wider society while retaining identity
  [0:0.3] Amalgamation | Biological/cultural fusion of groups into a new one ("melting pot")
  [0:0.4] Cultural pluralism / multiculturalism | Peaceful coexistence of distinct groups and cultures in one society
  [0:0.5] Cultural diffusion | Spread of culture (e.g. through media)
  [0:0.6] Cultural relativism | Understanding a culture on its own terms
  [0:0.7] Ethnocentrism | Own group seen as the centre of everything and superior; others rated by it (Sumner)
  [0:0.8] Xenocentrism / Xenophobia | Preferring other cultures / fear of foreigners
  [0:0.9] Ethnic group | Group sharing a common culture
  [0:0.10] Regionalism / Communalism / Casteism | Loyalty to a region / religious group / caste
[0:1] - **⚠ Trap:** pluralism = coexistence; amalgamation = fusion; assimilation = identity lost; acculturation = influenced but distinct.
[0:2] - **Ghurye**, *The Aborigines So-Called and Their Future*: argued for **tribal assimilation** (tribes as "backward Hindus").

## SUB 1 | Sanskritisation and Dominant Caste (M.N. Srinivas)
[1:0] - **Coined by M.N. Srinivas** in *Religion and Society among the Coorgs of South India* (**1952**); developed in *Social Change in Modern India*; first called **"Brahminisation"**.
[1:1] - **Meaning:** a **low caste, tribe or group** adopts the customs, rituals and way of life of a higher, **twice-born** caste (**imitates the upper-caste lifestyle**, observes its rites) to claim a higher rank.
[1:2] - **Reference groups:** Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas or the **local dominant caste**.
[1:3] - A claim to higher status succeeds only when the caste wins **acceptance by others** (group recognition), usually after economic or political gains.
[1:4] - Brings **positional, not structural** change – **upward mobility** for a caste, hierarchy intact; **downward mobility** is no part of it.
[1:5] - **De-sanskritisation:** rejecting Sanskritic norms and asserting own identity (non-Brahmin / Dravidian movements).
[1:6] - **Dominant caste** (Srinivas): concept also his; used to study **stratification in rural India**.
[1:7] - Srinivas's set: **Sanskritisation + Westernisation + Secularisation + Dominant Caste**.
[1:8] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Society p.5, p.13, p.18; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 3 p.13-14; app notes 2:113 sub 3
## SUB 2 | Social Change – Meaning, Sources and Theories
[2:0] # Meaning and sources
[2:1] - **Social change** = **alteration in the social order** (structure and relationships) of a society.
[2:2] TABLE head: Factor | Example
  [2:2.0] Technology | Industrial Revolution (Watt, Hargreaves; term popularised in English by Arnold Toynbee, 1760-1820); internet – 820 million+ users (Dec 2025)
  [2:2.1] Education | Spread of modern education
  [2:2.2] Law | Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955; widow remarriage and sati laws
  [2:2.3] Media | Spreads awareness, forms opinion; cultural diffusion; behaviour-change communication (ES 2025-26)
  [2:2.4] Migration / urbanisation | Industrialisation and urbanisation
  [2:2.5] Planning | Green Revolution; inclusive growth (11th Plan)
[2:3] - **Seasonal migration of nomads** is a routine cycle – **NOT** a source of social change.
[2:4] - New development model for developing societies stresses **non-material indicators** (quality of life, equity, **wellbeing**).
[2:5] - **ILO (2025):** India's social protection coverage **64.3%** (19% in 2015).
[2:6] - Dalit change came through state policy, reform movements, Sanskritisation and Westernisation.
[2:7] # Theories
[2:8] TABLE head: Theory | Proponents
  [2:8.0] Evolutionary (linear) | Comte (positivism, three stages, 1830s-40s), Herbert Spencer (simple → complex; coined "survival of the fittest", 1864), L.H. Morgan, Durkheim (mechanical → organic solidarity, *Division of Labour*, 1893)
  [2:8.1] Cyclical | Spengler (*Decline of the West*), Toynbee (challenge and response), Sorokin (sensate/ideational), Pareto (circulation of elites)
  [2:8.2] Diffusion | Elliot Smith, Perry, Boas
  [2:8.3] Technological | Veblen (*Theory of the Leisure Class*), Ogburn (adaptive culture, cultural lag)
  [2:8.4] Biological / evolutionary struggle | Darwin – survival of the fittest ("worthy living")
[2:9] - **Chronology:** Comte's positivism → Spencer → Durkheim's division of labour.
[2:10] - **W.G. Sumner** (*Folkways*, 1906): Social Darwinist; in-group/out-group antagonism and struggle – usually grouped with the early **conflict** school.
[2:11] TABLE head: Diffusionist school | Scholars | Idea
  [2:11.0] British | Elliot Smith, Perry | Egypt as the source
  [2:11.1] German-Austrian | Graebner, Schmidt | Culture circles
  [2:11.2] American | Boas, Wissler | Culture areas

## SUB 3 | Westernisation, Modernisation and Secularisation
[3:0] # Westernisation (Srinivas)
[3:1] - Changes from **150+ years of British rule** in **technology, institutions, ideology, values**; primary, secondary, tertiary Westernisation.
[3:2] - Source-specific (West/British) and **ethically neutral**; modernisation is **universal** and value-loaded ("progress").
[3:3] - The **English-educated Hindu middle class** was the first to be Westernised.
[3:4] # Modernisation
[3:5] - **Daniel Lerner**, *The Passing of Traditional Society* (**1958**): **urbanisation → literacy → media exposure → participation**; **"empathy"** = **psychic mobility**.
[3:6] - **Yogendra Singh**, *Modernization of Indian Tradition* (**1973**): universalistic, evolutionary cultural response; macro and micro levels.
[3:7] - **Milton Singer**, *When a Great Tradition Modernizes*: tradition and modernity **coexist** (Madras temple culture).
[3:8] - **Indicators:** rationality, science, industrialisation, urbanisation, literacy, mass media, democratic participation – **not hereditary occupation**.
[3:9] - **Zygmunt Bauman – "liquid modernity":** constant **mobility and change in relationships, identities and global economics** in contemporary society.
[3:10] # Secularisation
[3:11] - Decline of religion's hold on social life; differentiation and **rationalisation**; **Max Weber: "disenchantment of the world"**; Bryan Wilson – decline of organised religious participation; in India, **secularisation of caste** (caste as political pressure group).
[3:12] TABLE head: Sociological secularisation | Constitutional secularism
  [3:12.0] Change in society | Duty of the state
  [3:12.1] Beliefs and practices change | State neutrality and equal respect (Arts. 25-28)
[3:13] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 3 p.14-16; Rocket Indian Society p.14; app notes 2:113 sub 4-5, 7; general knowledge (Bauman), not checked
## SUB 4 | Cultural Lag, Great and Little Traditions, Books
[4:0] - **W.F. Ogburn**, *Social Change* (**1922**): **material culture** (technology) changes faster; **non-material culture** trails = **delay in adjustment** = **cultural lag**; "**material culture change lies behind social change**".
[4:1] - Lagging parts: **religion, law, tradition** – **not politics**.
[4:2] - Example: fast digital spread vs slow rules on children's online safety – **DPDP Act, 2023** (parental consent **under 18**).
[4:3] - **Culture** = the sum of human life-ways, behaviour, beliefs, feelings and thought.
[4:4] TABLE head: Concept | Originator
  [4:4.0] Great Tradition and Little Tradition | Robert Redfield; applied to India by Singer and Marriott
  [4:4.1] Universalisation (little → great) and Parochialisation (great → little) | McKim Marriott
  [4:4.2] Sanskritisation, Westernisation, Dominant Caste | M.N. Srinivas
