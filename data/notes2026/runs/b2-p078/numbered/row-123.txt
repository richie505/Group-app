## SUB 0 | MGNREGA and the VB-G RAM G Act
[0:0] TABLE head: Event | Date
  [0:0.0] NREGA passed by Parliament | August 2005 (assent September 2005)
  [0:0.1] In force | 2 February 2006, launched in Anantapur district (AP); all districts from 1 April 2008
  [0:0.2] Renamed MGNREGA | 2 October 2009
  [0:0.3] VB-G RAM G Act, 2025 | Passed 18 Dec 2025; in force 1 July 2026, replacing MGNREGA
[0:1] # Provisions (MGNREGA)
[0:2] - **Legal right** to at least **100 days** of unskilled wage work in a **financial year** per **rural household**; **adult members of any rural household** willing to do unskilled manual work are eligible (not only SC/ST/BPL).
[0:3] - Aim: guarantee the **"Right to Work"**; livelihood security, durable assets, **strengthening the livelihood resource base of the poor**, **proactive social inclusion**, **strengthening PRIs** – not higher education.
[0:4] - Work within **5 km**; no work within **15 days** of demand → **unemployment allowance**.
[0:5] - At least **one-third** of workers must be **women** (not half).
[0:6] - **Gram Sabha:** recommends works, monitors them, holds **social audits**; **Gram Panchayat** registers households, **receives work applications and issues dated receipts**.
[0:7] - Follows the **Directive Principles** (Art. 41); the right to work is **not** in Part III (Fundamental Rights).
[0:8] # VB-G RAM G Act, 2025 (now)
[0:9] - **Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin)**; Ministry of Rural Development.
[0:10] - **125 days** per rural household per year; wage floor **₹300/day** (2026-27); funding **60:40** (NE/Himalayan 90:10).
[0:11] - **AP ranked first** in implementation (2.10 crore person-days in the first 45 days).

## SUB 2 | Social Exclusion
[2:0] - **Meaning:** a **process** that shuts people out of **facilities, benefits and opportunities** that others enjoy; it is **involuntary and systematic** (NCERT).
[2:1] - Term coined by **René Lenoir** (France, **1974**).
[2:2] TABLE head: Basis | Example
  [2:2.0] Caste | Untouchability against SCs
  [2:2.1] Gender | Denial of property, work, mobility
  [2:2.2] Race / tribe | Adivasis in remote areas, racial discrimination
  [2:2.3] Disability | Lack of access to schools, jobs; segregated special schools
  [2:2.4] Religion / region | Minorities; backward, remote areas
[2:3] - **All of caste, gender, race and tribe** are bases of exclusion (TSPSC 2018).
[2:4] - **Practices leading to exclusion:** **untouchability**, **segregation** (e.g. a **special school for the blind** instead of inclusive schooling), denied access to **land, water and jobs**. MGNREGA wage work is a remedy, not a practice of exclusion.
[2:5] - **Results:** hurts **human-capital development**, **widens inequalities**, drives **social unrest and crime** – it does **not** bridge historical divisions (TSPSC 2023).
[2:6] # Constitutional remedies
[2:7] - **Reservations** for SCs, STs, OBCs aim at **social justice and equal opportunity**.
[2:8] - **Art. 46 (DPSP):** promotion of **educational and economic interests** of SCs, STs and other weaker sections.
[2:9] - **10 Aug 2026:** the Union **opposes a creamy layer for SCs/STs**, since income alone does not end caste-based exclusion.
[2:10] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society 02 p.1; NextGen Society G2 Part 3 p.36; NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.66; Laxmikanth 8th ed. (Art. 46); app notes 2:123 sub 7
## SUB 3 | Poverty – Concepts, Causes and Indicators
[3:0] TABLE head: Concept | Meaning | Measured by
  [3:0.0] Absolute poverty | Spending below the minimum for basic needs | A fixed poverty line (MPCE cut-off)
  [3:0.1] Relative poverty | Being poor compared with others in the same society | Inequality / share of income
  [3:0.2] Multidimensional poverty | Deprivation in health, education, living standards | National MPI (NITI Aayog)
[3:1] - India's official poverty line is **consumption-based (MPCE)**, not income-based.
[3:2] - **MPCE 2011-12 (NSS 68th round, MMRP):** about **₹1,430 rural** and **₹2,630 urban** – a gap of about **₹1,200** (urban ~84% higher).
[3:3] - **Causes (NextGen):** **illiteracy and lack of quality education** (main cause for rural and tribal poor), unemployment, disguised unemployment in agriculture, discrimination, health costs, inequality.
[3:4] - **Social indicators:** illiteracy, malnutrition, poor health care, lack of safe drinking water and sanitation; **size of the house is NOT** one.
[3:5] - **Culture of poverty:** **Oscar Lewis (1959)** – fatalism and poverty passed down generations.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.65-66; web: MoSPI NSS 68th round key indicators (corporatelawreporter.com); app notes 2:123 sub 0
