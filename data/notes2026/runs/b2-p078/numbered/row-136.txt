## SUB 0 | Forest Rights Act, 2006
[0:0] # The Act
[0:1] - Full name: **Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006** – aims at **redressal of the historical injustice** done to forest-dwelling STs and other traditional forest dwellers.
[0:2] - **Parliament passed it in December 2006**: Lok Sabha **15 Dec**, Rajya Sabha **18 Dec 2006** (passage complete); President's assent **29 Dec 2006**; in force **31 Dec 2007**; Rules **1 Jan 2008**.
[0:3] - **Nodal ministry: Ministry of Tribal Affairs** (not MoEFCC) (UPSC 2021). The Act **overrides** inconsistent laws.
[0:4] # Who decides
[0:5] - **Sec. 6(1): the Gram Sabha** is the authority to **initiate** the process of determining the nature and extent of individual or community forest rights (UPSC 2013) – not the Forest Department, Collector or Tahsildar.
[0:6] - Claims go up to the six-member **Sub-Divisional** and **District Level Committees**; the **District Level Committee's decision on the record of forest rights is final** (Sec. 6(6)).
[0:7] TABLE head: Category | Eligibility (cut-off 13 Dec 2005)
  [0:7.0] Forest-dwelling STs | Occupying forest land before the cut-off
  [0:7.1] Other Traditional Forest Dwellers | 3 generations (75 years) of residence
[0:8] - Ceiling **4 ha** per family; rights **heritable but not alienable**.
[0:9] TABLE head: Right | Type
  [0:9.0] Habitation, self-cultivation | Individual
  [0:9.1] Nistar, grazing, fishing | Community
  [0:9.2] Ownership of minor forest produce | Community
  [0:9.3] CFR management, PVTG habitat | Community
[0:10] # Bamboo, habitat, CWH
[0:11] - **Bamboo is a minor forest produce** under the FRA, and the FRA gives **ownership of MFP** to forest dwellers. The **Indian Forest (Amendment) Act, 2017** only removed bamboo grown **outside forests** from "tree" – it gave **no right to fell bamboo in forests** (UPSC 2019: 2 and 3 only). **No ownership of minerals.**
[0:12] - **Critical Wildlife Habitat** is defined **only in the FRA**: areas of national parks and sanctuaries to be **kept inviolate**, notified after an **open process of consultation**.
[0:13] - **Habitat rights** for PVTGs are decided under the FRA by the State machinery with the Ministry of **Tribal Affairs** guidelines – **not by MoEFCC**. **First habitat rights in India: the Baiga** (Baigachak, Dindori, Madhya Pradesh, 2015-16); later the **Bharia** (Patalkot, MP, 2022) and Kamar and Baiga (Chhattisgarh). UPSC 2018 key: statements 1 and 2.
[0:14] - **Protecting reserve forests:** allowing **limited rights** (e.g. grazing) to locals works better than keeping them out or giving unlimited rights or privatising.
[0:15] - **Niyamgiri (2013):** Dongria Kondh Gram Sabhas rejected bauxite mining under the FRA.
[0:16] - **Implementation gaps:** focus on individual titles over **CFR**, Gram Sabhas bypassed, MFP transit permits, **weak MoTA capacity**. **MoEF circular (3 Aug 2009):** no diversion until FRA rights are settled and Gram Sabha consent obtained (no public-importance exception).
[0:17] - **Forest Conservation Rules 2022** dropped the consent clause (NCST vs MoEFCC); **Forest (Conservation) Amendment Act 2023** exempts strategic projects within **100 km** of borders. **31 Aug 2026:** MoTA said the FRA has no provision for Gram Sabha consent for Stage-II clearance; MoTA sought CFR funds from **MoEFCC**.
[0:18] - Data (2025-26): 23.92 lakh individual + 1.22 lakh community titles; **AP: 2,26,651** titles.
[0:19] - **RFCTLARR 2013**: SIA **mandatory**; consent **80%** (private), **70%** (PPP); in Scheduled Areas acquisition is a last resort with consent of the **Gram Sabha / Panchayat / Autonomous Council**.

## SUB 1 | Scheduled Tribe Identification and Committees
[1:0] - **Art. 366(25)** defines STs; **Art. 342(1)**: the **President**, after **consultation with the Governor**, notifies STs **State/UT-wise** by public notification; **Art. 342(2)**: later inclusion or exclusion **only by Parliament by law** (amending the Constitution (ST) Order, 1950).
[1:1]   - ST status is **State/UT-specific** – a tribe scheduled in one State does not get ST benefits everywhere; the President cannot alter the list later.
[1:2] - **Procedure:** State proposal → **Registrar General of India** (must agree) → NCST → Union Cabinet → Parliament.
[1:3] - ST status is **religiously neutral** (unlike SCs, limited to Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists).
[1:4] - **Criteria (Lokur Committee, 1965):** **primitive traits, distinctive culture, geographical isolation, shyness of contact, backwardness** – all five (not in the Constitution; no religion criterion).
[1:5] - **September 2022 Cabinet decision:** ST lists revised for **Himachal Pradesh (Hattee), Chhattisgarh, Karnataka (Betta-Kuruba), Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh** – not AP or Bihar.
[1:6] TABLE head: Body | Chair / year | Mandate
  [1:6.0] Dhebar Commission (Art. 339) | U.N. Dhebar, 1960-61 | Scheduled Areas and ST welfare
  [1:6.1] Lokur Committee | 1965 | 5 ST criteria
  [1:6.2] Bhuria Committee | Dilip Singh Bhuria | Basis for PESA
  [1:6.3] High-Level Committee | Virginius Xaxa, 2013 | Socio-economic, health, educational status of tribes
[1:7] - **Art. 244(1)** = Fifth Schedule; **Art. 338A** = NCST (89th CAA, 2003).

## SUB 2 | Tribal Sub-Plan, ITDA and Institutions
[2:0] - **TSP**: Expert Committee **1972** (S.C. Dube); adopted in the **Fifth Plan (1974)**; now **DAPST**; funds at least **in proportion to the ST population**, over and above normal flows. Planning unit = **ITDP/ITDA**, with Panchayat members on its advisory committee – a decentralised, **bottom-up** approach (APPSC key). Grants: **Art. 275(1)** + SCA. Monitoring moved to MoTA in **Jan 2017**.
[2:1] - **SCP**: **1979** → SCSP → DAPSC. **AP SC/TSP Act 2013**: the **first State law** on planning, allocation and utilisation of the SC and Tribal Sub-Plans.
[2:2] - **TADP/ITDA objectives** (all correct): raise farm output with **improved seeds and fertilisers**, plant **fruit trees**, credit and marketing, freedom from **indebtedness**, wean tribals from **shifting cultivation**, and education, health and drinking water.

  [2:3.4] NSKFDC | 1997 | Implements NAMASTE

## SUB 4 | Education and SC/ST Empowerment – EMRS, Sports School, DACE
[4:0] - **Ekalavya (Eklavya) Model Residential Schools**: the model residential schools for **ST students** (also in AP); 1997-98; MoTA Central Sector via **NESTS**; modelled on **Navodaya**; block with **>50% ST and ≥20,000** tribals; target **740**; **Odisha** has the most.
[4:1] - **APTWR Sports School, Araku Valley** (2016): special sports school for tribal (SC/ST) children in AP.
[4:2] - **Dr Ambedkar Centres of Excellence (DACE):** free Civil Services coaching for **SC students** in 31 Central Universities; launched **22 April 2022** from **Banaras Hindu University** by the Social Justice Minister.
[4:3] - **SHRESHTA:** seats for meritorious **SC students** in top **private residential schools** (NETS by NTA).
[4:4] - Scholarships: SC Post-Matric **60:40**; National Overseas **20** awards for STs. **Stand-Up India** (5 April 2016): **greenfield** units, ₹10 lakh-₹1 crore, ≥1 SC/ST and 1 woman per branch. **VCF-SC**: 2014-15, ₹10 lakh-₹15 crore.

## SUB 5 | Tribal Problems, Approaches and Revolts
[5:0] - Colonial **moneylenders, traders and revenue farmers** → **indebtedness, land alienation**; the **common factor** of 19th-century tribal revolts = **complete disruption of the old agrarian order** of tribal communities (UPSC 2011).
[5:1] - **Diku** = outsider-exploiter (moneylender, trader, landlord, contractor); **Marang Buru** = Parasnath Hills revered by Santhals.
[5:2] TABLE head: Approach | Idea | Proponent
  [5:2.0] Isolation | Keep apart ("national park") | Early Verrier Elwin
  [5:2.1] Assimilation | "Backward Hindus", to be absorbed | G.S. Ghurye
  [5:2.2] Integration | Develop while keeping identity | Nehru
[5:3] - **Panchsheel** (Nehru's foreword to Elwin's **A Philosophy for NEFA**): develop along their **own genius**; respect land and forest rights; train tribals for own administration; avoid over-administration; judge by **quality of human character**, not money spent. **T.K. Oommen**: modern tribal movements seek autonomy, identity, control over resources.
[5:4] TABLE head: Revolt | Years | Leader / issue

  [5:4.1] Birsa's Ulgulan | 1899-1900 | Birsa Munda (Dharti Aaba); Abua Raj; against British land-grabbing and dikus → Chotanagpur Tenancy Act 1908
  [5:4.2] Rampa | 1922-24 | Alluri Sitarama Raju; forest curbs on podu
  [5:4.3] Srikakulam Girijan | 1967-70 | Vempatapu Satyanarayana, Adibhatla Kailasam
