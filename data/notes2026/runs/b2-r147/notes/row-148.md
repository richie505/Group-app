=== Prime Ministers and Their Foreign-Policy Landmarks
# PM → term → initiative
| PM | Term | Foreign-policy / landmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Jawaharlal Nehru** | 1947–64 | **Non-alignment** (announced as interim PM, 1946), Panchsheel; held **External Affairs himself** – free India's **first Foreign Minister** |
| Lal Bahadur Shastri | 1964–66 | **Tashkent Declaration** (10 January 1966) |
| **Indira Gandhi** | 1966–77, 1980–84 | Indo-Soviet Treaty, Bangladesh, **Simla Agreement (1972)** with Z.A. Bhutto, Pokhran-I; **MISA 1971**; **Twenty Point Programme (1975)**; **Sarkaria Commission (1983)** |
| **Rajiv Gandhi** | **31 Oct 1984 – 1 Dec 1989** | Sri Lanka Accord, IPKF, SAARC; **1988 action plan for nuclear disarmament**; first brought the Nagarpalika Bill (1989) |
| **P.V. Narasimha Rao** | **21 June 1991 – 16 May 1996** | **Look East Policy** (1991–92), full ties with **Israel (1992)**, economic reforms (FM Manmohan Singh) |
| I.K. Gujral | 1997–98 | **Gujral Doctrine** (as Foreign Minister, 1996) |
| A.B. Vajpayee | 1998–2004 | **Pokhran-II**, NFU, **Lahore bus**, Golden Quadrilateral |
| Manmohan Singh | 2004–14 | Nuclear deal, NSG waiver 2008 |
| **Narendra Modi** | 2014– | Neighbourhood First, Act East, G20, **Voice of Global South**; **Kartarpur Corridor** inaugurated (9 Nov 2019); first PM to visit **Israel** |
- **First PM to visit Israel:** **Narendra Modi**, **4–6 July 2017** – marked **25 years** of full diplomatic ties (set up **1992** under Narasimha Rao); not Vajpayee, Manmohan or Gujral.
- **Look East Policy** formulated by **P.V. Narasimha Rao** (Laxmikanth: first initiated 1992); upgraded to **Act East (2014)**.
- **Half-Lion** (2016) – a book on Narasimha Rao by **Vinay Sitapati**.

=== Six Phases of Indian Foreign Policy
| Phase | Label | Features |
|---|---|---|
| 1946–62 | **Optimistic non-alignment** | Idealism, Panchsheel, NAM, Commonwealth, anti-imperialism; "Hindi-Chini Bhai-Bhai" |
| 1962–71 | Realism and recovery | 1965 war, Tashkent (1966); defence pact with the US (1964); no NPT (1968) |
| 1971–91 | Regional assertion | Indo-Soviet Treaty, Bangladesh, Pokhran-I, IPKF |
| 1991–98 | **Safeguarding strategic autonomy** | USSR collapse, 1991 crisis; Look East, Gujral Doctrine |
| 1998–2013 | Balancing power | Pokhran-II, NFU, Indo-US deal (NSG waiver 2008) |
| 2014 onwards | Energetic engagement | Multi-alignment, "5S" (Samman, Samvad, Sahyog, Shanti, Samriddhi) |
- Idealism → realism; non-alignment → multi-alignment; the constant is **strategic autonomy**.
# Nehruvian era
- NAM, Panchsheel, Commonwealth membership.
- **Korean War**: India headed the **Neutral Nations Repatriation Commission** for POWs – **Gen. K.S. Thimayya**.
- Kashmir taken to the UN (Jan 1948); the **1962 war** with China showed the **limits of idealism**.
# 1971–1991: regional assertion
| Event | Date | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Tashkent Declaration | 10 Jan **1966** | Shastri–Ayub Khan, Soviet mediation |
| Indo-Soviet Treaty of Peace, Friendship and Cooperation | 9 Aug **1971** | 20 years; checked US-China-Pakistan axis |
| Bangladesh liberation | 16 Dec 1971 | Pakistan army surrendered in Dhaka |
| Simla Agreement | 2 July **1972** | India–Pakistan; bilateralism, no third party |
| Pokhran-I | 18 May 1974 | "Peaceful nuclear explosion" |
| Katchatheevu ceded to Sri Lanka | 1974 | — |
| Farakka Accord | **1977** | Ganga water, India–Bangladesh |
| IPKF in Sri Lanka | 1987–90 | India-Sri Lanka Accord 1987 (Rajiv Gandhi–J.R. Jayewardene) |
- **Chronology (UPPSC 2002):** **Tashkent (1966) → Indo-Soviet Treaty (1971) → Simla (1972) → Farakka (1977)** = code **4, 2, 1, 3**; later Lahore (1999) → 123 Agreement (2008).
# Post-Cold War (1991–98)
- **Look East Policy** 1991–92, **P.V. Narasimha Rao**; closer to ASEAN; ties with **Israel**.
- **LPG reforms**: attracting **FDI and markets** became foreign-policy goals; strategic ties with the US in the "unipolar moment".
- **Gujral Doctrine (1996)**: goodwill to neighbours without asking anything in return.

=== Post-1998 and Contemporary Multi-alignment
# Pokhran-II and after
- **Pokhran-II (Operation Shakti), May 1998**, PM **Vajpayee**: India declared itself a **nuclear-weapon state** (not NPT-recognised, not NSG member).
- **No First Use** announced by the **Vajpayee** government; formal doctrine 2003.
- Sanctions: US (**Glenn Amendment**), Japan; followed by **Jaswant Singh–Strobe Talbott** talks (after the tests, not before).
- **Lahore Declaration, Feb 1999** with Delhi–Lahore **bus diplomacy**.
- **Kargil 1999**: first conflict after both had tested nuclear weapons; US pressure made Pakistan withdraw.

# Multi-alignment
- Since 2008 the world moves to multipolarity → **issue-based alignment**: US (security, technology), Russia (defence, energy).
| Platform | Key fact |
|---|---|
| Quad | India, US, Japan, Australia; formed 2007, revived 2017 |
| SCO | Full member 2017 |
| BRICS | Founding member; India chaired 2026: New Delhi Declaration backed UN reform, opposed one-sided sanctions (Sep 2026) |
| Act East | Upgraded from Look East, 2014 |
| Neighbourhood First | 2014 |
| G20 Presidency | Dec 2022–Nov 2023; "One Earth, One Family, One Future"; **African Union** made permanent member |
- **India-UK CETA** signed July 2025 (PM's UK visit). First **Voice of Global South Summit**: **January 2023**.
# Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Strategic autonomy | Judge each issue on merits without military blocs; ≠ neutrality |
| Multi-alignment | Working with rival powers at once (Quad + BRICS/SCO) |
| Issue-based coalitions | Partners by issue, not fixed alliances |
| Global South voice | Reformed multilateralism, climate finance, DPI |
- **Contemporary policy with neighbours** = Neighbourhood First, Look/Act East via Myanmar, SAARC, Panchsheel; **non-alignment** is a Cold War bloc policy, not a neighbourhood policy (CDS 2015).

=== Cold War and World-Order Concepts
- **Cold War**: decades-long US–Soviet rivalry (1947–91) – anti-communist suspicion, arms race, brink of nuclear war (Cuban Missile Crisis 1962); not the "Space Race" or "Missile Crisis" alone.
- **Truman Doctrine (1947)**: US aid to Greece and Turkey to **contain the USSR** (containment) – not farm output or the UN.
- **Hegemony**: the dominant or preponderant position of one state over others (caucus = party meeting; demagogue = rabble-rouser; gerrymander = rigging constituency boundaries).
- **Age of Enlightenment** (18th c.): society can be improved through the **use of reason** (not religion, realism or idealism).
- **Post-Cold War order (after 1991)**: US-led liberal order ("end of history") → today multipolarity, minilaterals (Quad, AUKUS, I2U2), geo-economic fragmentation.
