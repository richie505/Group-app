## SUB 0 | Panchsheel, 1954
[0:0] # The agreement
[0:1] - **Signed 29 April 1954** – the **Agreement on Trade and Intercourse between the Tibet Region of China and India**; the five principles are in its **Preamble**. Signed by **Nehru** and Chinese Premier **Zhou Enlai** (Chou En-lai). Signed **before** the 1962 war, not after it.
[0:2] - Under the same agreement India accepted China's sovereignty over **Tibet**.
[0:3] # The five principles
[0:4] TABLE head: No. | Principle
  [0:4.0] 1 | Mutual respect for each other's territorial integrity and sovereignty
  [0:4.1] 2 | Mutual non-aggression
  [0:4.2] 3 | Mutual non-interference in each other's internal affairs
  [0:4.3] 4 | Equality and mutual benefit
  [0:4.4] 5 | Peaceful co-existence
[0:5] - **NOT Panchsheel:** **non-alignment / NAM** (a separate policy – UPSC 1997, UPPSC 1997); "support each other in global forums"; "mutual protection of the environment / of indigenous population" (NDA 2008).
[0:6] # Spread of Panchsheel
[0:7] - **Burma (Myanmar)** adopted it in **June 1954** (Zhou Enlai–U Nu joint statement) – the third country associated with Panchsheel (APPSC Group 1: "50 years of Panchsheel", 2004). Yugoslavia and Indonesia also adopted it.
[0:8] - **Bandung (April 1955)**: Panchsheel incorporated in the **Ten Principles**; **UNGA 1957**: India's resolution on peaceful co-existence; **Belgrade 1961**: core principle of NAM.
[0:9] - Don't confuse: **Tribal Panchsheel (1952)** – Nehru's tribal policy, drafted with **Verrier Elwin**.
[0:10] - **Sep 2025:** at the Modi–Xi meeting China stressed Panchsheel as the frame for ties.
[0:11] > Exam angle: "Which is NOT a principle of Panchsheel?" → **Non-alignment** (asked 4 times); "Panchsheel after 1962" ✗.

## SUB 1 | Non-Aligned Movement
[1:0] # Origin
[1:1] - **Bandung Conference, April 1955, Bandung (Indonesia)**: **29** Afro-Asian countries; **Ten Principles** of international peace (included Panchsheel) – laid the foundation of NAM.
[1:2] - Founded at the **1st NAM Summit, Belgrade (Yugoslavia), September 1961** (not New Delhi, Cairo or Havana).
[1:3] - **Non-alignment initiated by Nehru** (broadcast of **7 September 1946** as head of the interim government: keep away from power blocs) (UPPSC 2014).
[1:4] - Non-alignment **≠ neutrality**: no military alliance, independent approach, judge each issue on its merits, friendly relations with all.
[1:5] # Founders ("Initiative of Five")
[1:6] TABLE head: Leader | Country
  [1:6.0] Jawaharlal Nehru | India
  [1:6.1] Josip Broz Tito | Yugoslavia (host)
  [1:6.2] Gamal Abdel Nasser | Egypt
  [1:6.3] Kwame Nkrumah | Ghana
  [1:6.4] Sukarno | Indonesia
[1:7] # Summits → venue
[1:8] - 1 Belgrade 1961; 2 **Cairo** 1964; 3 Lusaka 1970; 4 **Algiers 1973**; 5 **Colombo 1976**; 6 Havana 1979; 7 **New Delhi 1983** (India hosted); 8 Harare 1986; 9 Belgrade 1989; 10 Jakarta 1992; 11 Cartagena 1995; 12 Durban 1998; 13 **Kuala Lumpur** 2003; 14 Havana 2006; 15 **Sharm el-Sheikh (Egypt) 2009**; 16 Tehran 2012; 17 Margarita 2016; 18 Baku 2019; 19 **Kampala 2024** (latest).
[1:9] - NAM achievements ("4 Ds"): decolonisation, détente, development, disarmament; today ~120 members.
[1:10] > Trap: 15th NAM = **Egypt (Sharm el-Sheikh)**, not Cuba (14th, Havana 2006).

## SUB 2 | Determinants, Principles and the Constitution
[2:0] # Determinants
[2:1] TABLE head: Determinant | How it shapes policy
  [2:1.0] Geography | Himalayan frontiers; Indian Ocean position
  [2:1.1] History | Anti-colonialism; Nehru's broadcast of 7 September 1946 (away from power blocs)
  [2:1.2] Economy | Aid from both blocs: Bhilai (USSR), Rourkela (West Germany)
  [2:1.3] Politics, leadership | Nehru's idealism, Indira's realism
  [2:1.4] Strategic culture | Autonomy, restraint; 1962 war → realism
[2:2] - Laxmikanth's list: geography, history and tradition, social structure, political organisation, international milieu, economic position, military strength, public opinion and leadership.
[2:3] - Aims: independence, security, **world peace and disarmament**, cooperation across ideologies – **not** alignment with a military bloc.
[2:4] # Principles (Laxmikanth)
[2:5] - Promotion of world peace; **anti-colonialism**; **anti-racialism** (snapped ties with South Africa 1954 over apartheid, restored 1994); non-alignment; Panchsheel; Afro-Asian bias (Asian Relations Conference, New Delhi 1947); links with the Commonwealth; support to the UN; **disarmament** – India rejects the **NPT (1968)** and **CTBT (1996)** as **discriminatory**.
[2:6] - **Reformed multilateralism** – India's demand for UNSC reform; **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam** ("the world is one family") – G20 2023 theme **"One Earth, One Family, One Future"**.
[2:7] # Constitutional basis
[2:8] TABLE head: Provision | Content
  [2:8.0] Article 51 (DPSP) | State shall endeavour to (a) promote international peace and security, (b) maintain just and honourable relations between nations, (c) foster respect for international law and treaty obligations, (d) encourage settlement of international disputes by arbitration – the foreign-policy Article
  [2:8.1] Article 73 | Executive power extends to treaties
  [2:8.2] Union List Entry 10 | Foreign affairs
  [2:8.3] Entries 11–13 | Diplomatic representation, the UN, international conferences
  [2:8.4] Entry 14 | Treaties
  [2:8.5] Entry 15 | War and peace
[2:9] - CDS 2006 statements: promote peace, just relations, arbitration → **all three (I, II and III)** are in Article 51.
[2:10] # Commonwealth
[2:11] - **Nehru**: India's Commonwealth membership is "**an agreement by free will, to be terminated by free will**" (UPPSC 2017) – not Panchsheel or Simla.
[2:12] - 1949 (London Declaration): India stayed a full member as a republic; accepts the Crown only as **symbol/Head of the Commonwealth**; India hosted **CHOGM 1983**.

## SUB 3 | Doctrines, Institutions and Nuclear Doctrine
[3:0] # Doctrines → leader
[3:1] TABLE head: Doctrine | Leader | Core idea
  [3:1.0] Nehruvian idealism | Nehru | NAM, Panchsheel, UN
  [3:1.1] Indira Doctrine | Indira Gandhi | South Asia as India's zone
  [3:1.2] Gujral Doctrine (1996) | I.K. Gujral (Foreign Minister in the Deve Gowda govt) | Non-reciprocal goodwill to smaller neighbours, non-interference, territorial integrity, peaceful bilateral settlement
  [3:1.3] Strategic autonomy | All PMs since 1947 | Today multi-alignment
[3:2] # Institutions
[3:3] - **CCS**: PM + Finance, External Affairs, Home, **Defence** (no Commerce Minister); NSA, Cabinet Secretary and service chiefs attend as **invitees**.
[3:4] - **NSC (1998)**: **National Security Adviser** is its secretary; first NSA **Brajesh Mishra** (1998). **Ajit Doval** – NSA since **2014** (reappointed 2019, 2024).
[3:5] - **Committee on External Affairs**: one of **24 DRSCs**; serviced by the **Lok Sabha**; Chairman appointed by the **Speaker**; term **one year**; scrutinises the **MEA**. **Shashi Tharoor** (MP, **Thiruvananthapuram**) was reappointed Chairman in the **2026** reconstitution.
[3:6] - **ICWA**: 1943, **Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru**, Sapru House; **Vice-President** is ex officio President; **ICWA Act 2001**.
[3:7] # Nuclear doctrine (CCS, 4 January 2003)
[3:8] - **Credible minimum deterrence**; **No First Use** (retaliation only); massive retaliation; no use against non-nuclear-weapon states; nuclear option kept against major biological/chemical attack.
[3:9] - **Nuclear Command Authority** = **Political Council** (chaired by the **PM**; the **sole body** that can authorise use) + **Executive Council** (chaired by the **NSA**).
