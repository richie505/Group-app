## SUB 0 | Article 370 and Jammu and Kashmir
[0:0] # Article 370 – special status
[0:1] - **Article 370** (Part XXI, "temporary provisions") gave **special status to Jammu and Kashmir** – not Art. 365, 367, 369, 371, 372 or 373 (asked 11 times, APPSC 2012–2019).
[0:2] - J&K had its **own Constitution**; only defence, external affairs and communications automatically applied.
[0:3] - **Art. 370(3)**: the President could declare it inoperative only on the **recommendation of the Constituent Assembly of the State**.
[0:4] TABLE head: Event | Date
  [0:4.0] Article 370 operative | 17 November 1952
  [0:4.1] J&K Constituent Assembly adopted its Constitution | 17 November 1956
  [0:4.2] J&K Constitution in force | 26 January 1957
  [0:4.3] Article 370 abrogated (Constitution (Application to J&K) Order, 2019); J&K and Ladakh UTs | 5 August 2019
  [0:4.4] Supreme Court upheld abrogation | December 2023
[0:5] - **Pakistan's response**: downgraded diplomatic ties, suspended bilateral trade and the **Samjhauta Express**, raised it at the UN.
[0:6] - **Gilgit-Baltistan**: India claims it as part of J&K – a political status issue, not a border dispute.
[0:7] > Trap: Art. 371 = special provisions for Maharashtra/Gujarat etc.; Tenth Schedule = defections, not J&K.

## SUB 1 | Kashmir, Wars and Agreements
[1:0] # Accession and the UN
[1:1] - **Instrument of Accession**: **Maharaja Hari Singh**, **26 October 1947**, after the **tribal invasion**; Indian troops airlifted **27 October**.
[1:2] - Ceded only **defence, external affairs and communications**; land revenue stayed with the State.
[1:3] - Sequence: tribal invasion → accession → airlift → **First Indo-Pak war**.
[1:4] - **India (Nehru)** first referred Kashmir to the **UNSC** on **1 January 1948** under **Article 35** of the UN Charter – not Pakistan; **UNSC Resolution 47 (1948)**: plebiscite conditional on Pakistan's withdrawal – never held.
[1:5] # Wars and operations
[1:6] TABLE head: War | Trigger / operation | Outcome
  [1:6.0] 1947–48 (first Kashmir war) | Pakistan-backed tribal invasion | UN ceasefire 1 January 1949; Karachi Agreement 1949
  [1:6.1] 1965 | Pakistan's Operation Gibraltar, Operation Grand Slam | Tashkent Declaration 1966
  [1:6.2] 1971 | Bangladesh crisis; Army chief Gen. Sam Manekshaw | Pakistan surrendered 16 December 1971; Simla 1972
  [1:6.3] 1984 Siachen | India's Operation Meghdoot (April 1984) | Line beyond NJ9842 unmarked
  [1:6.4] 1999 Kargil | Pakistani intrusion; India's Operation Vijay | Pakistan withdrew under US pressure; first war between nuclear-armed states
[1:7] - **Navy Day, 4 December** – marks **Operation Trident**, the Indian Navy's attack on Karachi in the 1971 war.
[1:8] - **2003 LoC ceasefire**, reaffirmed in **2021**. 1965 was NOT the first Kashmir war – that was 1947–48.
[1:9] # Agreements → year and signatories
[1:10] TABLE head: Agreement | Year | Signatories / key point
  [1:10.0] Karachi Agreement | July 1949 | Military representatives under UNCIP; fixed the ceasefire line
  [1:10.1] Tashkent Declaration | 10 January 1966 | Shastri–Ayub Khan; Soviet mediation; Haji Pir Pass returned to Pakistan; Shastri died at Tashkent
  [1:10.2] Simla Agreement | 2 July 1972 | Indira Gandhi–Z.A. Bhutto; bilateral settlement, no third party; ceasefire line → LoC
  [1:10.3] Lahore Declaration | February 1999 | Vajpayee–Nawaz Sharif; nuclear CBMs; bus diplomacy
  [1:10.4] Agra Summit | July 2001 | Vajpayee–Pervez Musharraf; no joint declaration
[1:11] - Tashkent did NOT create the LoC – Simla (1972) did. Simla: signed **2 July 1972** (not June 2; talks began late June).
[1:12] # Composite Dialogue and disputed places
[1:13] - **Composite Dialogue**: agreed **1997** → restarted **2004** → stopped after **26/11 (2008)**.
[1:14] - **Eight baskets**: peace and security/CBMs; J&K; Siachen; **Wullar Barrage/Tulbul**; Sir Creek; terrorism and drugs; economic cooperation; friendly exchanges – **not** nuclear disarmament.
[1:15] TABLE head: Place | Feature
  [1:15.0] Sir Creek | 96-km tidal estuary, Rann of Kutch, between Gujarat and Sindh; Pakistan: 1914 map line; India: mid-channel thalweg rule; unsettled
  [1:15.1] Siachen | Saltoro Ridge held since Operation Meghdoot; line beyond NJ9842 unmarked

## SUB 3 | Indus Waters Treaty 1960
[3:0] - Signed at **Karachi, 19 September 1960** by **Nehru and President Ayub Khan**; broker: **World Bank** (APPSC G2 2017).
[3:1] TABLE head: Rivers | Allotted to
  [3:1.0] Eastern: Ravi, Beas, Sutlej | India
  [3:1.1] Western: Indus, Jhelum, Chenab | Pakistan (India: domestic use, agriculture, run-of-river hydropower, limited storage)
[3:2] - Pakistan gets about **80%** (80.52%) of the waters, India about **20%** (19.48%); no aquifer or drought clause.
[3:3] - **Permanent Indus Commission**: one Commissioner from each country.
[3:4] TABLE head: Article IX level | Handled by
  [3:4.0] "Questions" | PIC
  [3:4.1] "Differences" | Neutral Expert
  [3:4.2] "Disputes" | Court of Arbitration
[3:5] - No hierarchy → World Bank appointed both for **Kishanganga and Ratle**.
[3:6] # Abeyance (2025) and after
[3:7] - After Pahalgam the **CCS** put the Treaty "**in abeyance**" from **23 April 2025** – until Pakistan "**credibly and irrevocably**" abjures cross-border terrorism. First ever suspension; the Treaty has no abeyance clause.
[3:8] - Effects: India stops sharing flow data; storage on western rivers; Chenab projects **Kirthai-II (930 MW)** and **Sawalkote (1,856 MW)** (Jun 2026).
[3:9] - **Jul 2026**: India sent a formal notice to renegotiate. **Aug–Sep 2026**: PCA Court of Arbitration (The Hague), **Ratle** case: Treaty "remains in force"; India **rejected** it – "**no jurisdiction**" (1 Sep 2026).
[3:10] ~~ Sources: PMF IAS International Relations p.18-19; app notes 2:149 sub 8
## SUB 4 | Trade and Connectivity
[4:0] - **Kartarpur Corridor**: agreement (MoU) **October 2019**; inaugurated by **PM Modi**, **9 November 2019**, **Guru Nanak's 550th birth anniversary**; **4.7-km visa-free** corridor from **Dera Baba Nanak (Gurdaspur)** to **Gurdwara Darbar Sahib, Kartarpur (Narowal)**.
[4:1] - Agreement renewed for 5 years in **October 2024**; India closed the corridor in **May 2025** after Operation Sindoor; still closed (Apr 2026).
[4:2] - **MFN**: granted to Pakistan **1996**; withdrawn **February 2019** after Pulwama with **200% customs duty**; Pakistan never reciprocated. After Pahalgam: ban on all imports from Pakistan.
[4:3] - **Samjhauta Express** (Attari–Lahore): started **1976** after Simla; suspended 2019.
[4:4] - **Wagah–Attari**: main land trade route. After Pahalgam India closed the **Attari ICP**, revoked SAARC visa-exemption and cut High Commission strength **55 → 35**.
[4:5] ~~ Sources: PMF IAS International Relations p.19-20 (Kartarpur 4.7 km, MFN, import ban); app notes 2:149 sub 7
