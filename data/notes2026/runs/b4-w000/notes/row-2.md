=== Rotation – Axis, Speed and Effects
# Rotation basics
- **Rotation** = the Earth spinning on its **axis** (line through both poles); **revolution** = moving round the Sun.
- Direction **west to east** – **counter-clockwise** seen from above the North Pole; only **Venus and Uranus** spin the other way.
- **Axis tilted 23½° (23°30') from the perpendicular to the orbital plane** ("from the ecliptic axis"; 66½° with the orbital plane) – so it is **not at 90°** to the orbit (NDA 2009: both statements ✓).
- Angular speed **15° per hour** (1° in 4 min). Linear speed **~1,670 km/h at the Equator – the maximum velocity of rotation** – falling to **zero at the poles**.
| Day | Measured against | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Sidereal** (one true rotation) | A distant star | **23 h 56 min 4.09 s** |
| Solar | The Sun | 24 h (~4 min longer, as the Earth also moves along its orbit) |
- Not felt: motion is uniform and the atmosphere moves along with the Earth.
- **Pole Star** seems fixed because it lies almost **in line with the Earth's axis of rotation**.
# Effects of rotation (Strahler; NDA 2019: all three)
- **Day and night** – the diurnal rhythm of daylight and air temperature.
- **Coriolis deflection** – flow paths of air and water turned sideways.
- **Movement of the tides** – a place is carried through the tidal bulges twice a day.
- Also: time difference between longitudes, centrifugal bulge.
- **Seasons are NOT caused by rotation** (they come from revolution + tilt); day and night are **not** caused by revolution (Jharkhand 2021: both statements false).
- **No rotation** → no sunrise/sunset and no day-night cycle, but seasons would remain (CDS 2015: I and II).
- **Faster rotation** → stronger centrifugal effect → **g decreases at the Equator, unchanged at the poles** (NDA 2018).
# Evidence and pioneers
- **Aryabhata** (5th century) first explained that the **rotation of the Earth** causes the daily rising and setting of the Sun (*Aryabhatiya*; also his *Aryasiddhanta*).
- **Foucault pendulum**, Paris **1851**: the plane of swing slowly turns. Other proofs: Coriolis deflection, equatorial bulge, apparent east-to-west motion of the sky.
# The axis is shifting (UPSC 2025)
- Studies (ETH Zurich, *Nature Geoscience*/*PNAS* 2024) show the rotation axis drifting and the day lengthening; cause = **mass redistribution**: as **polar ice melts, water moves towards the Equator** (also groundwater loss) – day longer by **~1.33 ms per century** (2000–2020).
- **Solar flares and CMEs** do bombard the upper atmosphere with energy (true) but **do not shift the axis** → "both II and III correct, only one (III) explains I".

=== Coriolis and Centrifugal Effects
- **Coriolis force** = an **apparent force** caused by the **Earth's rotation** (not revolution, tilt, pressure, temperature or magnetism); it shapes winds, ocean currents, cyclones and **large-scale weather patterns**.
- **Ferrel's law:** moving air and water are deflected to the **right in the Northern Hemisphere** and to the **left in the Southern Hemisphere** – "left in NH" is the trap (APPSC 2022: I and III only).
- Force = **2Ωv sin(latitude)**: **zero at the Equator**, **maximum at the poles**; grows with **latitude and wind speed**; linked to the **decreasing rotational velocity with increasing latitude** (CDS 2012: all three).
- Cyclones rarely form within **5° of the Equator**; NH cyclones spin **anticlockwise**, SH clockwise.
- AP angle: **Cyclone Montha (Oct 2025)** was steered north-northwest to the **Kakinada** coast.
- **Centrifugal force** of rotation: maximum at the Equator, zero at the poles → **equatorial bulge, polar flattening (oblate spheroid)**; **weight of a body is maximum at the poles**, least at the Equator.

=== Tides
- **Causes (UPSC 2015: a, b and c):** gravitational pull of the **Moon** (main) and the **Sun**, and the **centrifugal force** of the Earth–Moon system (bulge on the far side). Tide-generating force = gravitational attraction − centrifugal force.
- Semi-diurnal tide: **two high and two low tides a day**; successive high tides **~12 h 25 min apart** (≈ **12 h 26 min**, NDA 2010).
- The **lunar day is ~24 h 52 min**, so high tide comes **~52 min later each day**: **8:00 am → 8:52 am** (APPSC G1 2002, asked 3 times).
| Tide | Position | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Spring** (highest) | Sun, Earth, Moon **in a straight line** | **New moon and full moon** – twice a month |
| **Neap** (lowest range) | Sun and Moon at **right angles** | First and third quarter, ~7 days after spring tides – twice a month |
- Highest spring tides: full/new moon at an **equinox**; tides are larger when the Moon is at **perigee**. Ebb = falling tide, flow/flood = rising tide.

=== Day and Night, Day Length, Midnight Sun
- **Day and night** are caused by the **rotation of the Earth on its axis** – not revolution, not tilt alone, not the Moon.
- **Circle of illumination** = terminator = "**grey line**" (APPSC 2019); passes through both poles **only at the equinoxes**.
- **Unequal day and night:** tilt + revolution. The difference is **least near the Equator** (~12 h day all year) and **increases away from it** to the poles (CDS 2014).
- **Poles:** ~6 months of day + 6 months of night (**revolution with axial tilt**). **Midnight Sun** in the **Arctic and Antarctic regions** (beyond the polar circles) near the summer solstice; **Norway** = "Land of the Midnight Sun".
- **Twilight** is shortest at the Equator (Sun rises almost vertically) and longest at high latitudes.
- Equatorial seasons are marked by **wet and dry periods**, not by day length.

=== Revolution, Seasons and Leap Year
- Orbit slightly **elliptical**, Sun at one focus; period **~365¼ days** (365.25; sidereal year 365 d 6 h 9 min 9.5 s); mean speed **29.78 km/s**; the **Sun's gravity** is the centripetal force.
- **Perihelion** early January (~147 million km; **3 Jan in 2026**), **aphelion** early July (~152 million km; **6 July in 2026**) – Earth moves faster at perihelion; the Earth–Sun distance is **not constant**.
- **Seasons = revolution + 23½° tilt + parallelism of the axis**; Sun overhead at the Equator on **21 March and 23 September**, at Cancer on 21 June, at Capricorn on 22 December.
- **Circular orbit instead of elliptical** → the varying distance would vanish, so the **difference between seasons would be reduced** (CDS 2011 key); the effect is small because tilt is the main cause.
- Extra ¼ day a year → **leap year** of 366 days (29 February); a century year only if divisible by **400** (**2000 yes, 1900 no**).
- Rotation gives day and night, time zones and Coriolis; **revolution** gives the year, seasons, solstices, equinoxes and perihelion/aphelion.
