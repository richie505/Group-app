## SUB 1 | Shape and Size of the Earth – Geoid, Gravity, Dimensions
[1:0] # Shape
[1:1] - **Geoid** = "earth-shaped"; the Earth is an **oblate spheroid** – **flattened at the poles, bulging at the Equator**.
[1:2] - **Cause:** **centrifugal force of rotation**, maximum at the Equator (the faster-moving equatorial belt) – not the Sun's pull or stronger sunlight (CDS 2010: "only I").
[1:3] - **Equatorial bulge:** equatorial radius exceeds polar radius by **~21 km** (diameters by ~43 km) (CDS 2008, NDA 2016).
[1:4] - **Gravity and weight:** **g is greater at the poles** than at the Equator; **weight is least at the Equator, greatest at the poles** (poles are ~21 km nearer the centre + no centrifugal force). Moving to **higher latitude, weight increases** (IAS 2003 A-R: A false, R true).
[1:5] - Surface point nearest the Earth's centre: the **poles** (Arctic Ocean floor near the North Pole).
[1:6] - **Proofs of sphericity:** circular shadow of the Earth on the Moon in a **lunar eclipse**; ship's mast seen first; circumnavigation; satellite images.
[1:7] - **First to say the Earth is spherical:** **Pythagoras** (c. 6th century BC) proposed it; **Aristotle** (4th century BC, *On the Heavens*) gave the first proofs – Indian books and keys say **Aristotle** when Pythagoras is not offered (IAS 2001). Not Copernicus (heliocentric model), Ptolemy, Newton (polar flattening) or Columbus.
[1:8] - **Stars rise perpendicular to the horizon** for an observer on the **Equator** (celestial poles lie on the horizon there); at the poles stars circle parallel to the horizon (UPSC 2001, asked twice).
[1:9] # Dimensions

## SUB 2 | The Graticule – Latitude, Longitude, Great Circles
[2:0] # Latitude and longitude
[2:1] - **Latitude** = angular distance of a point **north or south of the Equator**, measured **from the centre of the Earth** (NDA 2011); 0°–90°.
[2:2] - **Parallels of latitude run east–west** (APPSC G1); **meridians run north–south**, pole to pole; longitude 0°–180° E or W.
[2:3] - Parallels at 1°: **179** (89 N + 89 S + Equator; the poles are points). Meridians: **360**.
[2:4] - 1° of latitude ≈ **111 km** everywhere; 1° of longitude: 111 km at the Equator → **55.5 km at 60°** → 0 at the poles.
[2:5] - **Antipode** of (x°N, y°E) = (x°S, (180 − y)°W): 20°N 80°E → **20°S 100°W**.
[2:6] # Great circles
[2:7] - A **great circle** divides the globe into **two equal halves** (its plane passes through the centre): the **Equator** (the only great-circle parallel) and every meridian with its opposite – **0° + 180°** form one (UPPSC 2000).
[2:8] - **60°N latitude is NOT a great circle** (all parallels except the Equator are small circles); 60°E longitude is half of a great circle.
[2:9] - NDA 2009 A-R: Equator is a great circle ✓; R (its plane divides the Earth into two equal halves) ✓ and explains A.
[2:10] # What varies with latitude (CDS 2017: all three true)
[2:11] - **Rainfall, temperature and vegetation** vary with latitude; the **difference between longest and shortest day increases with latitude**.
[2:12] - **Longitude does not affect insolation** (latitude, atmospheric transparency, distance from sea do).
[2:13] # Places by longitude / latitude
[2:14] - **West to east:** **Lisbon (9°W) → London (0°) → Frankfurt (8.7°E) → Beirut (35.5°E)** (MPPSC 2008).
[2:15] - **Nearest the Equator:** **Singapore (1°17'N)** < Jakarta (6°12'S) < Colombo (6°56'N) < Manila (14°35'N) (IAS 2008).
