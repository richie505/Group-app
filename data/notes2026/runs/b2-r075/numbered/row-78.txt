## SUB 0 | Concepts – Centralisation, Decentralisation and Its Forms
[0:0] - **Centralisation**: decision-making authority **concentrated at a single central point** (Union, State capital, HQ). **Decentralisation**: authority **distributed across various levels**.
[0:1] - Disadvantage of centralisation: **slow response to local issues**; uniform central policies may **hinder regional policy innovation**; centralisation is a **hindrance to liberty and independence** (Laski/Tocqueville view).
[0:2] - **Democratic decentralisation** = community participation and local accountability; features: **autonomy, people's participation, empowerment** ("instilling non-confidence" is NOT one).
[0:3] - **Local government attributes: statutory status, community participation, power to tax** – **central control is NOT** one.
[0:4] - State control over local bodies runs through **legislation, finance and personnel** (administrative control) – **citizens' grievances** is not a field of State control.
[0:5] - Local functions: public health, sanitation, water supply – **not** public order or **judicial review** (higher courts).
[0:6] TABLE head: Form | What happens | Control
  [0:6.0] Deconcentration | Work shifted to field offices of the same government | Centre keeps full control – weakest
  [0:6.1] Delegation | Tasks given to semi-autonomous bodies | Can be withdrawn at will
  [0:6.2] Devolution | Powers to elected local governments that elect their own officials, raise own revenue, decide independently | Not withdrawable at will – strongest (73rd, 74th)
  [0:6.3] Dimension | Transfers | 
  [0:6.4] Political | Power to decide – elected local bodies | 
  [0:6.5] Administrative | Power to implement | 
  [0:6.6] Fiscal | Power to spend and raise funds and taxes | 
[0:7] > Angles: definitions (×4), devolution, attributes of local government, liberty.

## SUB 1 | Gandhi, Article 40 and the Indian Experience
[1:0] - **Ancient village panchayats**: the **village as a self-sufficient unit of governance** ("little republics"); AP: **12-Ayagar** system of the **Kakatiyas and Vijayanagara**.
[1:1] - **Mahatma Gandhi**: strongest advocate of village self-government – **Gram Swaraj**, republic of self-sufficient villages.
[1:2] - **Ambedkar (4 Nov 1948)**: village "a **sink of localism, a den of ignorance, narrow-mindedness and communalism**"; unit = **individual**. Nehru: centralised planned state, heavy industry. **Gandhian Plan (1944): Sriman Narayan Agarwal**.
[1:3] - **Article 40 (DPSP, Part IV)**: the State shall **organise village panchayats** as units of self-government – the constitutional base of Panchayati Raj (Art. 243 / Part IX / Schedule XI came only in 1992); **non-justiciable**; a compromise between Gandhi and Ambedkar (Art. 39, 41, 42, 33, 48, 50 are distractors).
[1:4] - **64th Amendment Bill (1989)** passed Lok Sabha, **failed in the Rajya Sabha**; **73rd Amendment** gave Art. 40 shape (**Articles 243A, 243B, 243G**).
[1:5] - **CDP (1952)** top-down; **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee: January 1957**.
[1:6] - **First Panchayati Raj: Nagaur, Rajasthan, 2 October 1959** (Nehru); **AP second, 1 November 1959**.
[1:7] - Outcome: political power passed, fiscal least – the decentralisation gap.
[1:8] > Angles: Article 40 (×3), Gandhi, ancient village, first panchayat (Nagaur).
[1:9] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 (laxmikanth-indian-polity-02 p.96-97); Rocket Polity Part 1 p.5; app notes 2:78 sub 2-4

## SUB 3 | Subsidiarity and the Three Fs
[3:0] - **Subsidiarity**: function at the **lowest competent level**; moves up only if it cannot do it; invoked by the **2nd ARC**.
[3:1] - **3 Fs: Funds, Functions, Functionaries**; incomplete devolution keeps PRIs **implementing agencies**, not planning units; without **fiscal decentralisation** elected bodies stay powerless.
