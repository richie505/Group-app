=== NOTES 2:75 | Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013 – Structure, Jurisdiction, Working; Central Vigilance Commission
## SUB 0 | Ombudsman Idea and Recommending Bodies
[75:0:0] # Origin and Term
[75:0:1] - **Ombudsman** was first set up in **Sweden in 1809**.
[75:0:2] - Indian versions: **Lokpal** (Union) and **Lokayukta** (State).
[75:0:3] - The term **"Lokpal"** was coined by **Dr. L.M. Singhvi** in **1963**.
[75:0:4] Sweden 1809 = Ombudsman; Sweden 1766 = right to information law. Do not mix them.
[75:0:5] # Committees and Commissions
[75:0:6] TABLE head: Body | Chair | Recommendation
  [75:0:6.0] Santhanam Committee (1962-64) | K. Santhanam | CVC and CBI; not the Lokpal
  [75:0:6.1] First ARC (1966-70) | Morarji Desai (later K. Hanumanthaiya) | Two-tier: Lokpal (Union) + Lokayukta (States), 1966
  [75:0:6.2] Second ARC (2007) | – | Reiterated the Lokpal
[75:0:7] - First ARC report "**Problems of Redressal of Citizens' Grievances**" (1966) **first recommended state Lokayuktas**.
## SUB 1 | Lokpal Bill History and the 2013 Act
[75:1:0] # Bills
[75:1:1] - **First Lokpal Bill: 1968**, introduced in the **Lok Sabha** (4th Lok Sabha); **passed by the Lok Sabha in 1969**, but **lapsed** when that House was dissolved before the Rajya Sabha passed it.
[75:1:2] - Re-introduced in **1971, 1977, 1985, 1989, 1996, 1998, 2001, 2005, 2008** – every one lapsed.
[75:1:3] TABLE head: Stage (Lokpal Bill 2011) | Date
  [75:1:3.0] Introduced in the Lok Sabha by V. Narayanasamy, Minister of Parliamentary Affairs | 4 August 2011
  [75:1:3.1] Sent to the Standing Committee on Public Grievances, Law and Justice (chair Abhishek Manu Singhvi); civil society called it "weak" | late 2011
  [75:1:3.2] Passed by the Lok Sabha; Rajya Sabha sent it to a Select Committee | 27 December 2011
[75:1:4] # The Act – Dates
[75:1:5] TABLE head: Event | Date
  [75:1:5.0] Passed by the Rajya Sabha (with amendments) | 17 December 2013
  [75:1:5.1] Passed by the Lok Sabha | 18 December 2013
  [75:1:5.2] President's assent | 1 January 2014
  [75:1:5.3] Came into force | 16 January 2014
  [75:1:5.4] Lokpal began functioning | 27 March 2019
[75:1:6] - **Statutory** body, not constitutional.
[75:1:7] - Also amended the **DSPE Act** (CBI Director committee; Directorate of Prosecution).
[75:1:8] 1 January 2014 = assent; 16 January 2014 = in force (APPSC 2019 and G2 Mains 2025 key).
[75:1:9] # Amendment Act 2016 – Asset Declaration
[75:1:10] - Original Act: public servant declares assets and liabilities of self, spouse and dependent children within **30 days** of entering office.
[75:1:11] - **2016 amendment**: declaration stays, but its **form and manner** are prescribed by the **Central Government**.
## SUB 2 | Civil Society Movement (2011)
[75:2:0] - **India Against Corruption (IAC)**, led by **Anna Hazare**; fasts at **Jantar Mantar (April 2011)** and **Ramlila Maidan (August 2011)** pushed the **Jan Lokpal Bill**.
[75:2:1] - **Joint Drafting Committee (April 2011)**: chair **Pranab Mukherjee**; co-chair **Shanti Bhushan**.
[75:2:2] - Civil society members: **Anna Hazare, Arvind Kejriwal, Shanti Bhushan, Justice N. Santosh Hegde, Prashant Bhushan**.
## SUB 3 | Lokpal – Composition and Selection
[75:3:0] # Composition
[75:3:1] - **Chairperson + not more than 8 members**; minimum age **45**.
[75:3:2] - **50% judicial** members (former SC judges or former HC Chief Justices) **and** at least **50%** from **SC, ST, OBC, minorities and women** – both rules apply together.
[75:3:3] - Chairperson: former **CJI**, a sitting or former **SC judge**, or an **eminent person** with **25 years'** experience (anti-corruption, public administration, vigilance, finance, law, management).
[75:3:4] - Term: **5 years or age 70**, whichever earlier; **no reappointment**; a member may become Chairperson within a 5-year total.
[75:3:5] - Cannot be an **MP or MLA**.
[75:3:6] # Selection Committee (5) and Search Committee
[75:3:7] TABLE head: Member | Role
  [75:3:7.0] Prime Minister | Chair
  [75:3:7.1] Speaker of the Lok Sabha | Member
  [75:3:7.2] Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha | Member
  [75:3:7.3] CJI or an SC judge nominated by him | Member
  [75:3:7.4] Eminent jurist (nominated by the President on the others' recommendation) | Member
[75:3:8] - Appointing authority: the **President**, on the Selection Committee's recommendation.
[75:3:9] - **Search Committee**: set up by the Selection Committee; **at least 7 persons**, at least half from SC, ST, OBC, minorities and women.
[75:3:10] The **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is NOT a member** of the selection committee (UKPSC 2025).
## SUB 4 | Lokpal – Jurisdiction, Powers and Time Limits
[75:4:0] # Who is Covered
[75:4:1] TABLE head: Covered | Detail
  [75:4:1.0] Prime Minister | Except international relations, external and internal security, public order, atomic energy, space; inquiry only if the full bench approves by two-thirds, held in camera
  [75:4:1.1] Union Ministers, MPs | Yes – but not for anything said or any vote in Parliament
  [75:4:1.2] Central officials | Groups A, B, C and D; C and D complaints go to the CVC, which reports back to the Lokpal
  [75:4:1.3] NGOs, trusts, societies | Foreign contribution above ₹10 lakh
  [75:4:1.4] Public servants | In and outside India (posted abroad also covered)
[75:4:2] - Complaint bar: no inquiry into a complaint made after **7 years** from the alleged offence.
[75:4:3] # Powers and Wings
[75:4:4] TABLE head: Power | Detail
  [75:4:4.0] Inquiry Wing (Section 11) | Headed by a Director of Inquiry; preliminary inquiry into PCA offences
  [75:4:4.1] Prosecution Wing | Headed by a Director of Prosecution; files cases before the Special Court
  [75:4:4.2] CBI | Superintendence and direction over the CBI only in cases referred by the Lokpal
  [75:4:4.3] Property | Attach and confiscate property got by corruption, even while prosecution is pending
[75:4:5] # Time Limits
[75:4:6] TABLE head: Stage | Limit
  [75:4:6.0] Preliminary inquiry | 90 days + 90 days (reasons in writing)
  [75:4:6.1] Inquiry Wing report after reference | 60 days
  [75:4:6.2] Investigation | 6 months + 6 months
  [75:4:6.3] Special Court trial | 1 year, extendable up to 2 years

## SUB 7 | Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014
[75:7:0] - Act No. **17 of 2014**; President's assent **9 May 2014**.
[75:7:1] - A **whistle blower** makes a **public interest disclosure** about corruption or wilful misuse of power by a public servant.
[75:7:2] - **Competent authority** for central employees: the **CVC**.
[75:7:3] - Protections: identity of the complainant protected; **anonymous complaints not taken up**; complaints barred after **7 years**; appeal to the **High Court within 60 days**.
## SUB 8 | State Lokayuktas
[75:8:0] # FIRSTs and Chronology
[75:8:1] TABLE head: State | Fact
  [75:8:1.0] Odisha | FIRST to pass a law (1970); institution only in 1983
  [75:8:1.1] Maharashtra | FIRST to set up a Lokayukta – Lokayukta and Upa-Lokayuktas Act, 1971; came into force October 1972
  [75:8:1.2] Bihar | 1973
  [75:8:1.3] Rajasthan | 1973
  [75:8:1.4] Uttar Pradesh | 1975
  [75:8:1.5] Madhya Pradesh | 1982
  [75:8:1.6] Andhra Pradesh | 1983
  [75:8:1.7] Karnataka | 1984
  [75:8:1.8] Assam | 1986
  [75:8:1.9] Uttarakhand | First to pass the Lokayukta Bill in 2011 (1 November 2011)
[75:8:2] - Chronology: Sweden (1809) → first Lokpal Bill (1968) → Maharashtra Lokayukta (1971) → first Lokpal (2019).
[75:8:3] - By 2013: **21 States and 1 UT (Delhi)** had Lokayuktas.
[75:8:4] - **Mandate**: every State to set up a Lokayukta **within one year** of the Act's commencement, by its own law (**Section 63**).
[75:8:5] One PYQ key gives **Orissa (1971)** as first State to create a Lokayukta; Odisha 1970 = law, Maharashtra 1971 = institution.
[75:8:6] # Structure, Appointment, Tenure
[75:8:7] TABLE head: Structure | States
  [75:8:7.0] Lokayukta + Upa-Lokayukta | Rajasthan, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra
  [75:8:7.1] Lokayukta only | Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh
  [75:8:7.2] Officials designated "Lokpal" | Punjab, Odisha
[75:8:8] - Appointed by the **Governor**, after consulting the **Chief Justice of the High Court** and the **Leader of the Opposition** (not the CEC).
[75:8:9] - Yearly **consolidated report to the Governor**; recommendations **advisory** in many States, **binding** in some.
[75:8:10] - **Rajasthan** cut tenure from **8 to 5 years** (APPSC G1 2018); **Narpat Mal Lodha committee** (2014, Rajasthan Advocate General) reviewed its Act.
[75:8:11] # Coverage of CM, Ministers, MLAs
[75:8:12] TABLE head: Office | Included | Excluded
  [75:8:12.0] Chief Minister | HP, AP, MP, Gujarat, Odisha (Odisha Lokayukta Act 2014, Section 14) | Maharashtra, UP, Rajasthan, Bihar
  [75:8:12.1] MLAs | AP, HP, Gujarat, UP, Assam | –
[75:8:13] - Weakness: no uniform law; many lack an investigating wing and rely on **State police / ACB**.
[75:8:14] # Karnataka Model
[75:8:15] - **Karnataka Lokayukta Act, 1984**; own **Lokayukta Police** wing.
[75:8:16] - **Justice N. Santosh Hegde** – **Bellary illegal iron ore mining report (2011)**; led to arrest of former minister **G. Janardhana Reddy**.
## SUB 9 | Ap Lokayukta
[75:9:0] - Law: **AP Lokayukta and Upa-Lokayukta Act, 1983**; amended **2024**.
[75:9:1] - **Lokayukta** for higher functionaries; **Upa-Lokayukta** for the rest; covers the **CM and MLAs**.
[75:9:2] - Features: **informal procedure, easy access, almost free and prompt, independent of the executive**.
[75:9:3] - **FIRST Lokayukta of integrated AP: Justice A. Sambasiva Rao** (APPSC 2018).
[75:9:4] - **Aug 2026**: Justice (Retd) **T. Mallikarjuna Rao** appointed by Governor **S. Abdul Nazeer** under **Section 3(1)** on the selection committee's recommendation; **5-year term** (Section 5). The post was vacant most of 2025; Upa-Lokayukta **P. Rajani** held charge.
[75:9:5] - **Seat**: after bifurcation it worked from Hyderabad; shifted to **Kurnool** in **2021**, now its **principal seat**.
[75:9:6] AP Lokayukta is **not an interpreter of the Constitution** (APPSC 2019).
[75:9:7] TABLE head: Body | Role in AP
  [75:9:7.0] Lokayukta / Upa-Lokayukta | Statutory ombudsman (1983 Act); takes complaints, inquires, recommends; reports to the Governor

  [75:9:7.2] Anti-Corruption Bureau | Police agency for PCA offences (trap and disproportionate-assets cases)

=== NOTES 2:78 | Centralization vs Decentralization – Concepts, Arguments and Indian Experience
## SUB 0 | Concepts
[78:0:0] - **Centralisation**: decisions concentrated at the apex (Union, State capital, department HQ). **Decentralisation**: authority transferred downward.
[78:0:1] - **Democratic decentralisation** = **community participation** and **local accountability**; not central control.
[78:0:2] - Features: **autonomy, people's participation, empowerment**; "instilling non-confidence" is NOT a feature (BPSC 2020).
[78:0:3] - Local government attributes: **statutory status, community participation, power to tax**; central control is NOT one.
[78:0:4] - Local functions: public health, sanitation, water supply, rural development – **not** public order or judicial review (higher courts).
[78:0:5] # Forms – Weakest to Strongest
[78:0:6] TABLE head: Form | What happens | Control
  [78:0:6.0] Deconcentration | Work shifted to field offices of the same government | Centre keeps full control – weakest
  [78:0:6.1] Delegation | Tasks given to semi-autonomous bodies | Can be withdrawn at will
  [78:0:6.2] Devolution | Powers to elected local governments with own mandate and revenue | Not withdrawable at will – strongest (73rd, 74th)
[78:0:7] Power taken back at will = delegation, not devolution.
[78:0:8] # Dimensions
[78:0:9] TABLE head: Dimension | Transfers
  [78:0:9.0] Political | Power to decide – elected local bodies
  [78:0:9.1] Administrative | Power to implement – staff, functions
  [78:0:9.2] Fiscal | Power to spend and raise funds and taxes
## SUB 1 | Arguments
[78:1:0] TABLE head: For centralisation | For decentralisation
  [78:1:0.0] Nation-building after Partition, unity, security | Proximity: local bodies know local needs
  [78:1:0.1] Planned heavy industry, food security | Participation turns subjects into citizens
  [78:1:0.2] Weak local capacity | Accountability and speed
  [78:1:0.3] Elite capture by dominant castes | Political education of leaders
## SUB 2 | Views on the Village
[78:2:0] TABLE head: Person | View
  [78:2:0.0] Gandhi | Gram Swaraj: republic of self-sufficient villages; cottage industry, swadeshi, sarvodaya, trusteeship
  [78:2:0.1] Sriman Narayan Agarwal | Gandhian Plan (1944)
  [78:2:0.2] Nehru | Centralised planned state, heavy industry (National Planning Committee, Bombay Plan)
  [78:2:0.3] Ambedkar (4 Nov 1948) | Village = seat of caste domination, "a sink of localism, a den of ignorance, narrow-mindedness and communalism"; the individual as unit
## SUB 3 | Article 40 and the Amendments
[78:3:0] - **Article 40 (DPSP, Part IV)**: State shall organise **village panchayats** as **units of self-government**; non-justiciable – a **compromise** between Gandhi and Ambedkar.
[78:3:1] - **64th Amendment Bill (1989)**, Rajiv Gandhi: passed Lok Sabha, **failed in the Rajya Sabha** (fear of centralisation).
[78:3:2] - **73rd Amendment** gave Article 40 practical shape; **Articles 243A, 243B, 243G** came then.
[78:3:3] - Article 33 = Parliament's power to modify Fundamental Rights (a distractor).
## SUB 4 | Indian Experience
[78:4:0] - **CDP (1952)** run top-down; **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** appointed **January 1957**.
[78:4:1] - Nehru launched Panchayati Raj at **Nagaur, Rajasthan, 2 October 1959**; **AP followed 1 November 1959**.
[78:4:2] - 1960s: planning more centralised; participation diluted.
[78:4:3] - Outcome: **political** power passed, **some administrative**, **fiscal least** – the decentralisation gap.
[78:4:4] - **AP**: **12-Ayagar** village system of the **Kakatiyas and Vijayanagara**.
## SUB 5 | Subsidiarity and the Three Fs
[78:5:0] - **Subsidiarity**: function at the **lowest competent level**; moves up only if that level cannot do it; invoked by the **2nd ARC**.
[78:5:1] - **3 Fs: Funds, Functions, Functionaries** – incomplete devolution keeps PRIs **implementing agencies**, not planning units.
## SUB 6 | Number Game and Quick Revision

  [78:6:0.0] Gandhian Plan | 1944
  [78:6:0.1] CDP / Mehta Committee | 1952 / 1957

[78:6:2] - First Panchayati Raj: Nagaur, Rajasthan; AP second.
[78:6:3] - Weakest form: deconcentration; strongest: devolution.
