=== Concepts – Centralisation, Decentralisation and Its Forms
- **Centralisation**: decision-making authority **concentrated at a single central point** (Union, State capital, HQ). **Decentralisation**: authority **distributed across various levels**.
- Disadvantage of centralisation: **slow response to local issues**; uniform central policies may **hinder regional policy innovation**; centralisation is a **hindrance to liberty and independence** (Laski/Tocqueville view).
- **Democratic decentralisation** = community participation and local accountability; features: **autonomy, people's participation, empowerment** ("instilling non-confidence" is NOT one).
- **Local government attributes: statutory status, community participation, power to tax** – **central control is NOT** one.
- State control over local bodies runs through **legislation, finance and personnel** (administrative control) – **citizens' grievances** is not a field of State control.
- Local functions: public health, sanitation, water supply – **not** public order or **judicial review** (higher courts).
| Form | What happens | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Deconcentration | Work shifted to field offices of the same government | Centre keeps full control – weakest |
| Delegation | Tasks given to semi-autonomous bodies | **Can be withdrawn at will** |
| **Devolution** | Powers to **elected local governments** that **elect their own officials, raise own revenue, decide independently** | Not withdrawable at will – strongest (73rd, 74th) |
| Dimension | Transfers |
|---|---|
| Political | Power to decide – elected local bodies |
| Administrative | Power to implement |
| Fiscal | Power to spend and raise funds and taxes |
> Angles: definitions (×4), devolution, attributes of local government, liberty.

=== Gandhi, Article 40 and the Indian Experience
- **Ancient village panchayats**: the **village as a self-sufficient unit of governance** ("little republics"); AP: **12-Ayagar** system of the **Kakatiyas and Vijayanagara**.
- **Mahatma Gandhi**: strongest advocate of village self-government – **Gram Swaraj**, republic of self-sufficient villages.
- **Ambedkar (4 Nov 1948)**: village "a **sink of localism, a den of ignorance, narrow-mindedness and communalism**"; unit = **individual**. Nehru: centralised planned state, heavy industry. **Gandhian Plan (1944): Sriman Narayan Agarwal**.
- **Article 40 (DPSP, Part IV)**: the State shall **organise village panchayats** as units of self-government – the constitutional base of Panchayati Raj (Art. 243 / Part IX / Schedule XI came only in 1992); **non-justiciable**; a compromise between Gandhi and Ambedkar (Art. 39, 41, 42, 33, 48, 50 are distractors).
- **64th Amendment Bill (1989)** passed Lok Sabha, **failed in the Rajya Sabha**; **73rd Amendment** gave Art. 40 shape (**Articles 243A, 243B, 243G**).
- **CDP (1952)** top-down; **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee: January 1957**.
- **First Panchayati Raj: Nagaur, Rajasthan, 2 October 1959** (Nehru); **AP second, 1 November 1959**.
- Outcome: political power passed, fiscal least – the decentralisation gap.
> Angles: Article 40 (×3), Gandhi, ancient village, first panchayat (Nagaur).

=== Arguments For and Against
| For centralisation | For decentralisation |
|---|---|
| Nation-building after Partition, unity, security | Proximity: local bodies **know local needs** |
| Planned heavy industry | Participation |
| **Weak local capacity** | Accountability and speed |
| **Elite capture** by dominant castes | Political education |

=== Subsidiarity and the Three Fs
- **Subsidiarity**: function at the **lowest competent level**; moves up only if it cannot do it; invoked by the **2nd ARC**.
- **3 Fs: Funds, Functions, Functionaries**; incomplete devolution keeps PRIs **implementing agencies**, not planning units; without **fiscal decentralisation** elected bodies stay powerless.
