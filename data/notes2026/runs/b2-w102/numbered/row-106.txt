## SUB 0 | Types of Family – Residence, Descent, Authority, Orientation
[0:0] # Family as an institution
[0:1] - **Family** (Latin *familia*, household) = the **basic, primary** social institution and the child's **first school** (language, customs, values).
[0:2] - **Murdock's definition:** a group marked by **common residence, economic co-operation and reproduction**.
[0:3] - **Consanguine family** ("united blood family"): built on **blood ties**; **conjugal** family: built on the marriage bond.
[0:4] - **Family of orientation** = the family one is **born into** and socialised in (parents, siblings); **family of procreation** = set up by one's **marriage** (spouse, children). The **spouse** does not belong to the family of orientation.
[0:5] - **Siblings** = children of the same parents.
[0:6] # Classification
[0:7] TABLE head: Basis | Types
  [0:7.0] Size / structure | nuclear (husband, wife, unmarried children); joint/extended (two or more generations)
  [0:7.1] Residence | patrilocal (husband's family), matrilocal/uxorilocal (wife's family), ambilocal/bilocal (either or both alternately), neolocal (new home), duolocal (each spouse in own natal home), avunculocal (with the husband's maternal uncle, "matula")
  [0:7.2] Descent / inheritance | patrilineal (father to son), matrilineal (mother to daughter)
  [0:7.3] Authority | patriarchal (oldest male; male-dominated), matriarchal (woman holds authority – more theoretical than real)
  [0:7.4] Marriage | monogamous; polygamous (polygyny, polyandry)
  [0:7.5] Centre | affection-centred (filiocentric) family run around the children's wishes
[0:8] - **Neolocal residence** does not support an extended family and speeds the decline of joint families.
[0:9] - **Biological vs social father:** separated in **polyandrous (polygamous)** families, e.g. the Toda.
[0:10] - **Okka** (Coorgs, Srinivas) = **patrilineal, patrilocal joint family**.
[0:11] - **⚠ Trap:** matriliny ≠ matriarchy; duolocal ≠ bilocal.

## SUB 1 | Functions of the Family and Socialisation
[1:0] # Functions
[1:1] - **Primary functions (NextGen):** reproduction and rearing, **culture transmission**, home, **agent of socialisation**, **status ascription**, social control.
[1:2] - **Secondary:** economic (now **consumption** more than production), educational, religious, recreational.
[1:3] - Family **protects** members from social and economic risks; approved sex relations **prevent social disorder**; gives caste, religion, language and **national identity**.
[1:4] - **Inheritance** = passing on property (not consumption of goods).
[1:5] - **Dysfunction** = persistent failure of functions (abuse, alcoholism).
[1:6] TABLE head: Scholar | Number | Functions
  [1:6.0] Murdock | 4 universal | sexual, reproductive (birth of children), economic, educational
  [1:6.1] MacIver | essential / non-essential | essential: sex need, procreation and rearing, home; non-essential: religious, educational, economic, health, recreation
  [1:6.2] Ogburn (and Nimkoff) | 7 | incl. status-giving, affectional; all but affection pass to outside agencies – "loss of functions"
  [1:6.3] Talcott Parsons | 2 irreducible | primary socialisation; stabilisation of adult personality
[1:7] - **Why the family emerged:** sexual, economic and reproductive needs – **all these**.
[1:8] - **Family today** is no longer the main unit of production and has **lost many traditional functions** – both statements correct.
[1:9] - **First and most important agency of socialisation:** the **family** (then school, peer group, community).

## SUB 2 | Joint Family – Features and Scholars
[2:0] # Features
[2:1] - **Joint family:** **co-residence**, one hearth, **joint ownership of property**, **common worship**, pooled income; members are **kin** – private property and "unrelated members" are not features.
[2:2] - **Karve's definition:** people under one roof, one hearth, common property, common worship, related as kindred – an **extended kinship group**; jointness = functional unity and obligations, may hold common property.
[2:3] # Scholars

## SUB 3 | Changing Structure and Urban Families
[3:0] # Causes of change
[3:1] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [3:1.0] Industrialisation and urbanisation | the main factors behind joint-family disintegration; family: production → consumption unit; youth economically independent
  [3:1.1] Migration, housing | rural-urban migration and small housing break up joint families
  [3:1.2] Women's employment | more female-headed households
  [3:1.3] Law, education | Hindu Succession Act, labour laws, rational education
[3:2] - **Modern family:** financial independence, **decline of religious control**, **more freedom for women**; increased religious feeling is **not** a trait.
[3:3] - Urbanisation does **not** eliminate all emotional bonds of joint families.
[3:4] - **T.K. Oommen:** urban families by livelihood, **authority structure**, social milieu, value pattern; **proprietary class** = joint, strong elder-male authority.
[3:5] - **Single-parent / female-headed rise:** widowhood, marital instability, male migration (not joint-family expansion).
[3:6] - **High-risk families:** severe social, economic, health, psychological or environmental challenges; causes economic (poverty, **debt** – economic, not psychological), social (**exclusion**), psychological (stress, addiction); elderly care rests on **filial piety**.

[4:4] # Evolution and critique of the family
[4:5] - **L.H. Morgan** (*Ancient Society*, 1877), five forms in order: **consanguine → punaluan → syndyasmian → patriarchal → monogamian**.
[4:6] - **Engels** (*Origin of the Family*, 1884): the monogamous family arose with private property.
[4:7] - **David Cooper** (*The Death of the Family*, 1971): the family is the **ideal (ideological conditioning) instrument of an exploitative society**.
