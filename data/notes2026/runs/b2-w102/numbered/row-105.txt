## SUB 0 | Founders and Classical Theorists
[0:0] # Founders of sociology
[0:1] TABLE head: Thinker | Key ideas (exam answer words)
  [0:1.0] Auguste Comte (1798–1857) | Father of Sociology; coined "sociology" (1838) after first calling it "social physics"; positivism; motto "Order and Progress"; sociology an encyclopaedic (synthetic) science
  [0:1.1] Herbert Spencer | Social evolution (Social Statics, 1851); society like a biological organism; "survival of the fittest"
  [0:1.2] Emile Durkheim | Father of Modern Sociology; social facts, collective conscience, anomie, sacred-profane; religion builds solidarity
  [0:1.3] Karl Marx | Scientific socialism; historical materialism; surplus value; alienation; class struggle; class fixed by relations of production; Asiatic mode of production (India)
  [0:1.4] Max Weber | Verstehen; ideal type; class, status and power; 3 authorities; 4 social actions; bureaucracy; *Protestant Ethic*, *The Religion of India*
  [0:1.5] C. Wright Mills | *The Sociological Imagination* (1959); power elite (political, military, corporate)
  [0:1.6] Georg Simmel | formal sociology; society exists because individuals interact; studied social interaction and city life; dyad = 2, triad = 3
  [0:1.7] Manuel Castells | network society (1996)
[0:2] - **Comte's three stages:** **theological** (rain = God's will), **metaphysical** (abstract forces of nature), **positive** (rain explained by atmospheric processes); positivism rests on **observation, experiment, evidence**, never on divine forces.
[0:3] - **Durkheim:** **social facts** = laws, customs, religion, traditions existing **outside** the individual and shaping behaviour; **anomie** = normlessness from rapid change (crime, suicide); books *The Division of Labour in Society*, *The Rules of Sociological Method*, *Suicide* ("Collective Conscience" is a concept, not a book).
[0:4] - **Marx:** **historical materialism** = the economic base shapes society, politics, culture; **surplus value** = value workers produce beyond their wages; founder of the **conflict perspective**.
[0:5]   - Alienation from the **product**, from the **production process** (forced, monotonous work), from **fellow humans**, from **oneself** (one's human nature).
[0:6]   - **Communist Manifesto (1848)** – written **jointly by Marx and Engels**; central theme = **class struggle**; *Das Kapital* 1867.
[0:7]   - **Religion:** "the **opium (intoxicant)** of the people", "the **heart of a heartless world**" – Marx (1844).
[0:8]   - **Asiatic mode of production:** Marx's label for India – village communities, no private landed property, despotic state (not Weber).
[0:9] - **Weber's authority:** **traditional** (custom, sanctity of tradition – a tribal chief), **charismatic** (personality), **legal-rational** (rules). Bureaucracy: hierarchy, **impersonality**, written rules, **merit** appointment.
[0:10] - **Weber's social action:** **instrumentally rational** (goals), **value-rational** (values, ethics), **affective** (emotions), **traditional** (customs).
[0:11] - **Mills' sociological imagination:** link **biography** (one's own life and troubles) with **history**; one person's unemployment (trouble) ↔ **economic slowdown** (public issue).
[0:12] - **Malinowski** (functionalist): **strong personal attachments and death** are the main sources of religious belief.

## SUB 1 | Basic Concepts – Society, Community, Culture, Groups and Roles
[1:0] # Society and community
[1:1] - **Main purpose of the sociologist:** to **study society** scientifically; sociology = **the scientific study of society**.
[1:2] - **Human society is unique** because it rests on **culture**; culture (not language alone) separates human from animal society.
[1:3] - **Community (MacIver):** a group sharing "the basic conditions of a common life"; its **bases = locality (territory) + community sentiment (we-feeling, solidarity)**.
[1:4] - **Coercion:** a person or group **forcibly imposes its opinion/will** on another.
[1:5] - **Institutionalisation:** the process by which **conventions and rules develop** and become stable in social forms.
[1:6] - **Reference group:** the group whose standards we **compare ourselves with** and emulate, without belonging to it.
[1:7] - **Social system:** social structure + culture + personality (Parsons).
[1:8] # Culture
[1:9] - **Language** is a product of **culture**; **language, literature, art** = **non-material culture** (tools, buildings = material).
[1:10] - **Not an essential component of culture:** the **economy** (tradition, language, customs are).
[1:11] - **Cultural lag:** non-material culture trails material change (Ogburn).
[1:12] # Beliefs, values, roles
[1:13] - "**Men are stronger than women**" = a **belief** (a proposition held true; "trust" in translation), not a value, oath or approval.
[1:14] - **Independently defined role:** **painter**; father, teacher, officer need a counter-role.
[1:15] - **Sex vs gender:** sex = **biological** difference; gender = **social** role assigned to each sex.
[1:16] # Measuring attitudes
[1:17] - **Social distance scale:** **Emory Bogardus** (1925).
[1:18] - **Equal-appearing interval scale:** **L.L. Thurstone** (1928); Guttman = cumulative scale.

## SUB 2 | Social Psychology and Sociology of Education
[2:0] # Education and society
[2:1] - **Educational sociology** = **application of the principles of sociology to education** (F.J. Brown); sociology of education = sociological analysis of education as an institution.
[2:2] - **Education as a result of social change:** social change creates a **demand for education** (new knowledge and skills).
[2:3] - **Pragmatism** (Dewey): ideas without action are meaningless; truth verified by **experiment**; useful knowledge only. "Develop the individual and society develops automatically" is **not** pragmatist.
[2:4] - **Humanistic realism:** classics studied to **cultivate the intellect** and make man rational and practical.
[2:5] - **Character formation:** best through **good character models** (example), not sermons.
[2:6] - **J. Krishnamurti:** knowledge is **scientific, collective, individual**.
[2:7] # Psychology terms
[2:8] - **Personality** (APPSC key): the **behaviour** of the individual (sum of behaviour patterns); Allport stresses inner systems.
[2:9] - **Leadership:** ability to **influence others' attitudes or behaviour** in a desired way.
[2:10] - **Role play:** best method to **re-examine one's own and others' views, feelings and behaviour**.
[2:11] - **Rorschach inkblot test** (1921): detects **thought disorder**.
[2:12] - *Theories of Motivation* (1959): **K.B. Madsen**; Maslow – *A Theory of Human Motivation* (1943).
[2:13] - **Life stages:** **adolescence** = rapid change, identity search ("storm and stress"); relative **social stability** comes in **adulthood**.

## SUB 3 | Unity in Diversity and National Integration
[3:0] # Diversity
[3:1] - **Salient features:** **multi-ethnic, multi-lingual, multi-class, broadly patriarchal**; matrilineal exceptions **Khasi, Jaintia, Garo**.
[3:2] - **Structural features (UPPSC 2014):** excess village population, **various religions, various castes**; **poverty** is an economic condition, not a feature.
[3:3] - **Languages:** about **122** major languages (Census 2011) vs **22** in the **Eighth Schedule**; **4** language families.
[3:4] - **Religions:** 6 major religions; Hindus a minority in **8** States/UTs.
[3:5]   - **Dakhma** (Tower of Silence) = **Parsi/Zoroastrian** exposure of the dead to vultures.
[3:6]   - **Sallekhana/Santhara** = **Jain** fast unto death; **Rajasthan HC** banned it (2015), SC stayed the ruling.
[3:7]   - **Durkheim:** religion = beliefs and practices about **sacred things**; it strengthens **social solidarity**.
[3:8] - **Festivals:** **Pongal** = harvest festival of **Tamil Nadu** (Sankranti in AP).
[3:9] - **Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Manav Sangrahalaya** (Museum of Mankind, story of human evolution) – **Bhopal**.
[3:10] - **International Mother Language Day: 21 February** (UNESCO, 1999).
[3:11] # Bonds of unity
[3:12] - **Shankaracharya** sent priests from southern temples to the north and vice versa; Onam; Bhakti-Sufi blending. Linguistic homogeneity is **not** a bond.
[3:13] - **Linguistic reorganisation** = a **"safety valve"** accommodating linguistic aspirations, making India a **"State-Nation"**; **Andhra (1953)** began it, the first linguistic State.
[3:14] # National integration
[3:15] - **Dimensions:** **political, economic, social, cultural, psychological** – inter-related.
[3:16] - **National Integration Council:** constituted **1961** (after the National Integration Conference, chaired by **Nehru**/PM; first met **1962**) to fight **communalism, casteism, regionalism**.
[3:17] - **Emotional Integration Committee (1961):** chaired by **Dr Sampurnanand**.
[3:18] - **Ek Bharat Shreshtha Bharat (2015):** pairs States/UTs to promote **national integration**.
[3:19] - **Art. 350-B:** Special Officer for **Linguistic Minorities** (not a Finance Commission).
[3:20] - **Social stability:** society keeps **peace, order, harmony, continuity and effective functioning of institutions** despite change; key feature **inclusive development**; promoted by **family** (primary agent of socialisation), education, **welfare and social justice**, tolerance; threatened by **regionalism**, poverty, extremism. Tolerance does not threaten it.

## SUB 6 | Social Groups – Primary, Secondary, Crowd and Mob
[6:0] TABLE head: Group | Features | Examples
  [6:0.0] Primary (C.H. Cooley) | Small size, face-to-face, physical proximity, intimacy, similarity of background, common interests, person-oriented | Family, village, friends, peer group
  [6:0.1] Secondary | Large size, formal, impersonal, indirect relationships, goal-oriented, voluntary membership (not compulsory) | School, office, hospital, association
  [6:0.2] Community | Personal, intimate, enduring relations | Family, close-knit group
  [6:0.3] Association | Impersonal, formed for a specific interest | Commerce, industry, a sports club
[6:1] - **Not a secondary-group trait:** **physical proximity** (it is a condition of the primary group) and **compulsory membership** (membership is voluntary).
[6:2] - **'Primordial' groups/ties:** concept of **Edward Shils** (1957) – blood bonds of family and kin; "primary group" itself is Cooley's term.
[6:3] - **By duration:** **permanent groups** – family, school, club, gymnasium, sports teams; **temporary groups** – crowd, audience.
[6:4] - **Organisation:** a group of people **organised for a particular need** (purpose).
[6:5] - **In-group / out-group:** a group becomes **more conscious of its unity when another group threatens it**.
[6:6] - **Crowd vs mob:** a **mob** is a crowd that is **excited and engages in, or prepares for, violent action**; an aggregate (passengers at a bus stop) is not a group.

## SUB 7 | Social Control, Socialisation and Social Processes
[7:0] - **Social control:** means by which society brings unruly members back into line. **Formal** – law, **police**, courts, State; **informal** – **family, community**, religion, gossip and ridicule (incl. social media).
[7:1] - **Socialisation:** the process through which **society reproduces itself socially and biologically** – the individual learns the culture of the group.
[7:2] - **Anticipatory socialisation** (R.K. Merton): learning the norms of a group or role one **expects to join** – e.g. parents preparing an unmarried daughter to adjust to her husband's family.
[7:3] - **Social processes:** **conflict** is a **modified (sharpened) form of competition**; competition is impersonal, conflict personal and direct.
[7:4] - **Social capital – three dimensions:** **structural** (network of relationships), **relational** (**trust** in ties), **resource** (benefits gained by virtue of ties and participation) – financial investment is economic, not social, capital.
[7:5] - **Language** is important to society because it **facilitates social relations** (communication, transmission of culture).

## SUB 8 | Social Work – Values, Methods and Scope
[8:0] - **Values of the social work profession:** dignity and worth of the person, **self-determination**, social justice, confidentiality, acceptance – "all the above" items.
[8:1] - **Methods of social work:** primary – **social case work, social group work, community organisation**; secondary – social welfare administration, **social action, social work research**. A **case study** is a research tool, **not** a method.
[8:2] - **Scope:** draws on **sociology, psychology and law** and covers **public health and community development** (both).
