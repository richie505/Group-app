## SUB 3 | Independence and Accountability
[102:3:0] - Safeguards: **fixed tenure**, selection-committee appointment, removal only on statutory grounds; **unrestricted removal** by the parent ministry **weakens** independence.
[102:3:1] - **Securities Markets Code Bill, 2025**: members disclose **direct, indirect and family** interests.
[102:3:2] - Accountability: annual reports and accounts **laid before Parliament**, examined by **Departmentally Related Standing Committees**; sanctions proportional to **intent, scale and harm** (ES 2025-26).

[102:5:1] - **Regulatory Reform Bill, 2013** – drafted by the **Planning Commission** for infrastructure regulators.

=== NOTES 2:103 | Civil Society, Ngos, Shgs and Pressure Groups in Governance; Fcra Regulation
## SUB 0 | Pressure Groups – Meaning, Types and Techniques
[103:0:0] - **Pressure group**: an **organised group** that seeks to influence public policy **without contesting elections or seeking office**; also called **interest groups, lobby groups**; labelled the **"Anonymous Empire"**.
[103:0:1] - Test: seeks to form the government → political party. **Members of a trade union** = pressure group; members of the Lok Sabha, a panchayat or the Cabinet are not (MPPSC 2017). The **Election Commission** is a constitutional authority, not a pressure group.
[103:0:2] - **Almond and Powell**: **institutional, associational, non-associational, anomic**.

  [103:0:3.0] Business – most powerful and largest | FICCI, CII, ASSOCHAM – work for commerce and industry interests

  [103:0:3.3] Professional | Indian Medical Association, Bar associations
  [103:0:3.4] Women / feminist, caste, religious, student | Feminist groups are organised social (interest) groups pressing for women's rights

## SUB 1 | NGOs, CBOs and Social Service
[103:1:0] - NGOs are to be treated as **partners of government in development** (National Policy on the Voluntary Sector, **2007**, Planning Commission: partnership, not subordination). The APPSC key printed "**people's representatives**".
[103:1:1] - **Social service** is provided by **both government and private individuals**, profit and non-profit organisations.
[103:1:2] - **Community Based Organisations (CBOs)**: non-profit groups that **extend social services locally**.
[103:1:3] - Civil society = the **"third sector"** beside State and market; shift from welfare delivery to **rights, advocacy and watchdog** work.

[103:2:1] - **Freedom of the press** is not named in the Constitution; it is read into **Article 19(1)(a)** (Art. 19), not Arts. 14, 21 or 32.

=== NOTES 2:104 | Globalisation and Society – Culture, Migration, Labour, Inequality; the Anti-globalisation Critique
## SUB 0 | Meaning of Globalisation and Cultural Change
[104:0:0] - **Globalisation**: free flow of **goods, services, capital (investment), technology, ideas and people** across borders – "worldwide interconnectedness"; flows of ideas, capital, commodities and people.
[104:0:1] - Intended outcomes: reduced trade barriers, free flow of capital and labour – **not** the flow of **classified military technology**.
[104:0:2] - Essence (UPSC Astt. Comm. 2017): the coming together of **worldwide social relations bypassing territories** and **thinking transterritorially about global risks** – not mere inter-state relations or suprastate governance.
[104:0:3] - **Global village** (Marshall McLuhan) rests on **transport and communication** development.
[104:0:4] - Culture and tradition are said to disappear under **globalisation** (homogenisation), though local culture does not fully end.
[104:0:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [104:0:5.0] Diffusion | Spread of cultural traits from one society to another
  [104:0:5.1] Acculturation | Mutual exchange; both change, each stays distinct
  [104:0:5.2] Assimilation | Minority absorbed; loses identity
  [104:0:5.3] Glocalisation | Global products adapted to local tastes

[105:0:1] TABLE head: Thinker | Key ideas (exam answer words)
  [105:0:1.0] Auguste Comte (1798–1857) | Father of Sociology; coined "sociology" (1838) after first calling it "social physics"; positivism; motto "Order and Progress"; sociology an encyclopaedic (synthetic) science
  [105:0:1.1] Herbert Spencer | Social evolution (Social Statics, 1851); society like a biological organism; "survival of the fittest"
  [105:0:1.2] Emile Durkheim | Father of Modern Sociology; social facts, collective conscience, anomie, sacred-profane; religion builds solidarity
  [105:0:1.3] Karl Marx | Scientific socialism; historical materialism; surplus value; alienation; class struggle; class fixed by relations of production; Asiatic mode of production (India)
  [105:0:1.4] Max Weber | Verstehen; ideal type; class, status and power; 3 authorities; 4 social actions; bureaucracy; *Protestant Ethic*, *The Religion of India*
  [105:0:1.5] C. Wright Mills | *The Sociological Imagination* (1959); power elite (political, military, corporate)
  [105:0:1.6] Georg Simmel | formal sociology; society exists because individuals interact; studied social interaction and city life; dyad = 2, triad = 3
  [105:0:1.7] Manuel Castells | network society (1996)
[105:0:2] - **Comte's three stages:** **theological** (rain = God's will), **metaphysical** (abstract forces of nature), **positive** (rain explained by atmospheric processes); positivism rests on **observation, experiment, evidence**, never on divine forces.
[105:0:3] - **Durkheim:** **social facts** = laws, customs, religion, traditions existing **outside** the individual and shaping behaviour; **anomie** = normlessness from rapid change (crime, suicide); books *The Division of Labour in Society*, *The Rules of Sociological Method*, *Suicide* ("Collective Conscience" is a concept, not a book).
[105:0:4] - **Marx:** **historical materialism** = the economic base shapes society, politics, culture; **surplus value** = value workers produce beyond their wages; founder of the **conflict perspective**.
[105:0:5]   - Alienation from the **product**, from the **production process** (forced, monotonous work), from **fellow humans**, from **oneself** (one's human nature).
[105:0:6]   - **Communist Manifesto (1848)** – written **jointly by Marx and Engels**; central theme = **class struggle**; *Das Kapital* 1867.
[105:0:7]   - **Religion:** "the **opium (intoxicant)** of the people", "the **heart of a heartless world**" – Marx (1844).
[105:0:8]   - **Asiatic mode of production:** Marx's label for India – village communities, no private landed property, despotic state (not Weber).
[105:0:9] - **Weber's authority:** **traditional** (custom, sanctity of tradition – a tribal chief), **charismatic** (personality), **legal-rational** (rules). Bureaucracy: hierarchy, **impersonality**, written rules, **merit** appointment.
[105:0:10] - **Weber's social action:** **instrumentally rational** (goals), **value-rational** (values, ethics), **affective** (emotions), **traditional** (customs).
[105:0:11] - **Mills' sociological imagination:** link **biography** (one's own life and troubles) with **history**; one person's unemployment (trouble) ↔ **economic slowdown** (public issue).
[105:0:12] - **Malinowski** (functionalist): **strong personal attachments and death** are the main sources of religious belief.

## SUB 1 | Basic Concepts – Society, Community, Culture, Groups and Roles
[105:1:0] # Society and community
[105:1:1] - **Main purpose of the sociologist:** to **study society** scientifically; sociology = **the scientific study of society**.
[105:1:2] - **Human society is unique** because it rests on **culture**; culture (not language alone) separates human from animal society.
[105:1:3] - **Community (MacIver):** a group sharing "the basic conditions of a common life"; its **bases = locality (territory) + community sentiment (we-feeling, solidarity)**.
[105:1:4] - **Coercion:** a person or group **forcibly imposes its opinion/will** on another.
[105:1:5] - **Institutionalisation:** the process by which **conventions and rules develop** and become stable in social forms.
[105:1:6] - **Reference group:** the group whose standards we **compare ourselves with** and emulate, without belonging to it.
[105:1:7] - **Social system:** social structure + culture + personality (Parsons).
[105:1:8] # Culture
[105:1:9] - **Language** is a product of **culture**; **language, literature, art** = **non-material culture** (tools, buildings = material).
[105:1:10] - **Not an essential component of culture:** the **economy** (tradition, language, customs are).
[105:1:11] - **Cultural lag:** non-material culture trails material change (Ogburn).
[105:1:12] # Beliefs, values, roles
[105:1:13] - "**Men are stronger than women**" = a **belief** (a proposition held true; "trust" in translation), not a value, oath or approval.
[105:1:14] - **Independently defined role:** **painter**; father, teacher, officer need a counter-role.
[105:1:15] - **Sex vs gender:** sex = **biological** difference; gender = **social** role assigned to each sex.
[105:1:16] # Measuring attitudes
[105:1:17] - **Social distance scale:** **Emory Bogardus** (1925).
[105:1:18] - **Equal-appearing interval scale:** **L.L. Thurstone** (1928); Guttman = cumulative scale.
[105:1:19] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 1 l.464, l.1039; Part 2 l.816, l.1562-1703; web: sociology.institute (MacIver); general knowledge (scales, roles)
[105:1:20] - **Ritual:** the **habitual, prescribed way of functioning** of a community or society (APPSC key) – not oath, value or statute.
[105:1:21] - **Culture vs nature:** culture is man-made, learned and **superorganic** – 'somewhat superior to **nature**' (the given biological environment).
[105:1:22] - **Characteristics of society (APPSC key):** a **network of social relations**, **membership by birth** and an **integral culture** – population density is demographic, not defining.

## SUB 4 | Social Groups – Primary, Secondary, Crowd and Mob
[105:4:0] TABLE head: Group | Features | Examples
  [105:4:0.0] Primary (C.H. Cooley) | Small size, face-to-face, physical proximity, intimacy, similarity of background, common interests, person-oriented | Family, village, friends, peer group
  [105:4:0.1] Secondary | Large size, formal, impersonal, indirect relationships, goal-oriented, voluntary membership (not compulsory) | School, office, hospital, association
  [105:4:0.2] Community | Personal, intimate, enduring relations | Family, close-knit group
  [105:4:0.3] Association | Impersonal, formed for a specific interest | Commerce, industry, a sports club
[105:4:1] - **Not a secondary-group trait:** **physical proximity** (it is a condition of the primary group) and **compulsory membership** (membership is voluntary).
[105:4:2] - **'Primordial' groups/ties:** concept of **Edward Shils** (1957) – blood bonds of family and kin; "primary group" itself is Cooley's term.
[105:4:3] - **By duration:** **permanent groups** – family, school, club, gymnasium, sports teams; **temporary groups** – crowd, audience.
[105:4:4] - **Organisation:** a group of people **organised for a particular need** (purpose).
[105:4:5] - **In-group / out-group:** a group becomes **more conscious of its unity when another group threatens it**.
[105:4:6] - **Crowd vs mob:** a **mob** is a crowd that is **excited and engages in, or prepares for, violent action**; an aggregate (passengers at a bus stop) is not a group.

## SUB 5 | Social Control, Socialisation and Social Processes
[105:5:0] - **Social control:** means by which society brings unruly members back into line. **Formal** – law, **police**, courts, State; **informal** – **family, community**, religion, gossip and ridicule (incl. social media).
[105:5:1] - **Socialisation:** the process through which **society reproduces itself socially and biologically** – the individual learns the culture of the group.
[105:5:2] - **Anticipatory socialisation** (R.K. Merton): learning the norms of a group or role one **expects to join** – e.g. parents preparing an unmarried daughter to adjust to her husband's family.
[105:5:3] - **Social processes:** **conflict** is a **modified (sharpened) form of competition**; competition is impersonal, conflict personal and direct.
[105:5:4] - **Social capital – three dimensions:** **structural** (network of relationships), **relational** (**trust** in ties), **resource** (benefits gained by virtue of ties and participation) – financial investment is economic, not social, capital.
[105:5:5] - **Language** is important to society because it **facilitates social relations** (communication, transmission of culture).

=== NOTES 2:106 | Family – Types, Functions and Changing Structure in India
## SUB 0 | Types of Family – Residence, Descent, Authority, Orientation
[106:0:0] # Family as an institution
[106:0:1] - **Family** (Latin *familia*, household) = the **basic, primary** social institution and the child's **first school** (language, customs, values).
[106:0:2] - **Murdock's definition:** a group marked by **common residence, economic co-operation and reproduction**.
[106:0:3] - **Consanguine family** ("united blood family"): built on **blood ties**; **conjugal** family: built on the marriage bond.
[106:0:4] - **Family of orientation** = the family one is **born into** and socialised in (parents, siblings); **family of procreation** = set up by one's **marriage** (spouse, children). The **spouse** does not belong to the family of orientation.
[106:0:5] - **Siblings** = children of the same parents.
[106:0:6] # Classification
[106:0:7] TABLE head: Basis | Types
  [106:0:7.0] Size / structure | nuclear (husband, wife, unmarried children); joint/extended (two or more generations)
  [106:0:7.1] Residence | patrilocal (husband's family), matrilocal/uxorilocal (wife's family), ambilocal/bilocal (either or both alternately), neolocal (new home), duolocal (each spouse in own natal home), avunculocal (with the husband's maternal uncle, "matula")
  [106:0:7.2] Descent / inheritance | patrilineal (father to son), matrilineal (mother to daughter)
  [106:0:7.3] Authority | patriarchal (oldest male; male-dominated), matriarchal (woman holds authority – more theoretical than real)
  [106:0:7.4] Marriage | monogamous; polygamous (polygyny, polyandry)
  [106:0:7.5] Centre | affection-centred (filiocentric) family run around the children's wishes
[106:0:8] - **Neolocal residence** does not support an extended family and speeds the decline of joint families.
[106:0:9] - **Biological vs social father:** separated in **polyandrous (polygamous)** families, e.g. the Toda.
[106:0:10] - **Okka** (Coorgs, Srinivas) = **patrilineal, patrilocal joint family**.
[106:0:11] - **⚠ Trap:** matriliny ≠ matriarchy; duolocal ≠ bilocal.
[106:0:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.9-10; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 2 p.3; Rocket Indian Society p.18; app notes 2:106 sub 0-1
[106:0:13] - **'Unstable family':** term first used by **Frederic Le Play** (with patriarchal and stem families) – the modern nuclear family that breaks up each generation.
[106:0:14] - **Bases of family classification:** size/structure, residence, descent, authority, marriage – **religion is NOT** a basis.
## SUB 1 | Functions of the Family and Socialisation
[106:1:0] # Functions
[106:1:1] - **Primary functions (NextGen):** reproduction and rearing, **culture transmission**, home, **agent of socialisation**, **status ascription**, social control.
[106:1:2] - **Secondary:** economic (now **consumption** more than production), educational, religious, recreational.
[106:1:3] - Family **protects** members from social and economic risks; approved sex relations **prevent social disorder**; gives caste, religion, language and **national identity**.
[106:1:4] - **Inheritance** = passing on property (not consumption of goods).
[106:1:5] - **Dysfunction** = persistent failure of functions (abuse, alcoholism).
[106:1:6] TABLE head: Scholar | Number | Functions
  [106:1:6.0] Murdock | 4 universal | sexual, reproductive (birth of children), economic, educational
  [106:1:6.1] MacIver | essential / non-essential | essential: sex need, procreation and rearing, home; non-essential: religious, educational, economic, health, recreation
  [106:1:6.2] Ogburn (and Nimkoff) | 7 | incl. status-giving, affectional; all but affection pass to outside agencies – "loss of functions"
  [106:1:6.3] Talcott Parsons | 2 irreducible | primary socialisation; stabilisation of adult personality
[106:1:7] - **Why the family emerged:** sexual, economic and reproductive needs – **all these**.
[106:1:8] - **Family today** is no longer the main unit of production and has **lost many traditional functions** – both statements correct.
[106:1:9] - **First and most important agency of socialisation:** the **family** (then school, peer group, community).
[106:1:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.10; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 2 l.1758; app notes 2:106 sub 0, 2; general knowledge (MacIver)
[106:1:11] - **Husband and wife statuses:** **functional** (reciprocal, complementary) statuses (APPSC key).
[106:1:12] - **James Drever:** "socialisation is a process by which the individual is **adapted to his social environment**".
[106:1:13] - **Erik Erikson:** personality develops through **eight psychosocial stages** (trust vs mistrust … integrity vs despair).
## SUB 2 | Joint Family – Features and Scholars
[106:2:0] # Features
[106:2:1] - **Joint family:** **co-residence**, one hearth, **joint ownership of property**, **common worship**, pooled income; members are **kin** – private property and "unrelated members" are not features.
[106:2:2] - **Karve's definition:** people under one roof, one hearth, common property, common worship, related as kindred – an **extended kinship group**; jointness = functional unity and obligations, may hold common property.
