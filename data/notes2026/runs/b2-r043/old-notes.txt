=== NOTES 2:43 | Supreme Court – Composition, Appointment, Collegium, Njac and Jurisdictions
## SUB 0 | Establishment and Constitutional Basis
[43:0:0] - **Part V, Chapter IV – Articles 124-147**.
[43:0:1] - **Article 124**: establishment and constitution of the Supreme Court (Court of Record is Art. 129, not 124).
[43:0:2] - **Federal Court** set up **1937** (GoI Act, 1935); **Privy Council** appeals abolished **1949**.
[43:0:3] - SC replaced the Federal Court and the Privy Council's Judicial Committee on **26 Jan 1950**; **first sitting 28 January 1950** (two days later), in part of the old Parliament House.
[43:0:4] - Moved to its **Tilak Marg** building in **1958**.
[43:0:5] - **Art. 130**: seat at **Delhi**, or elsewhere as the **CJI** decides with the **President's approval**. No regional benches yet.
[43:0:6] - **Custodian / guardian of the Constitution** through judicial review; final interpreter and guardian of Fundamental Rights.
[43:0:7] - Benches usually 2 or 3 judges; **Constitution Bench ≥ 5** (Art. 145(3)); Art. 145 – SC frames its own rules.
[43:0:8] - **Art. 348(1)(a)**: all SC and HC proceedings in **English**.
[43:0:9] 1937 Federal Court; 1949 Privy Council appeals end; 26 Jan 1950 SC replaces it; 28 Jan 1950 first sitting; 1958 Tilak Marg. The Council of States is the Rajya Sabha, not a court.

## SUB 2 | Appointment, Qualifications, Tenure
[43:2:0] # Appointment – Art. 124(2)
[43:2:1] - **President** appoints judges after consulting the CJI and such judges as he thinks needed; consulting the CJI is **compulsory** for other judges (an autonomy safeguard).
[43:2:2] - **CJI** appointed by the **President** (APPSC-GS 2025 key); by convention the **senior-most** judge; the collegium only recommends.
[43:2:3] - Convention broken: **A.N. Ray (1973)**, **M.H. Beg (1977)**; Second Judges Case later upheld the senior-most convention.
[43:2:4] # Qualifications – Art. 124(3)
[43:2:5] - **Citizen of India** and: **5 years** as HC judge, or **10 years** as HC advocate, or a **distinguished jurist** in the President's opinion – never used in 76 years (Justice Bhuyan, 31 Aug 2026).
[43:2:6] - **L. Nageshwara Rao** – not earlier an HC judge; 7th elevated directly from the Bar.
[43:2:7] # Tenure, Oath, Resignation
[43:2:8] - Retire at **65**; age determined as Parliament provides by law (**Art. 124(2A)**, 15th Amendment 1963).
[43:2:9] - **Oath** before the **President** (form in **Third Schedule**) – to uphold the Constitution and the law. **Resignation** in writing to the **President**.
[43:2:10] - CJI has **no fixed term in years** – serves till 65.
[43:2:11] - **Salaries** fixed by Parliament by law (**Art. 125(1)**); until then, Second Schedule.
[43:2:12] - **Oath (Art. 124(6)):** an SC judge takes the oath before the **President** or a person **appointed by the President** for this purpose.

[43:4:5] - Formal appointment by the **President** – SC judges **Art. 124(2)**, HC judges **Art. 217(1)**.
[43:4:6] - **Zero executive** members; Government (IB checks) may **return a name once** – bound to appoint if reiterated.

  [43:4:7.2] High Court | HC Chief Justice + 2 senior-most HC judges | Initiates HC appointments

[43:4:13] - **Fourth Judges Case**: **5-judge** bench led by **J.S. Khehar**, **4:1**; lone dissent **J. Chelameswar** (upheld NJAC). Ground: executive role hurt **judicial independence – Basic Structure**. India has no NJAC now.

[43:4:18] - **Sep 2026**: Punjab Cabinet objected that the State was bypassed in appointing the Punjab and Haryana HC Chief Justice.
## SUB 5 | Jurisdictions → Article
[43:5:0] TABLE head: Jurisdiction | Article | Key point
  [43:5:0.0] Original | 131 | Centre-State, State-State legal disputes; exclusive
  [43:5:0.1] Writ | 32 | FRs only; original but not exclusive (HC Art. 226)
  [43:5:0.2] Appellate | 132 / 133 / 134 | Constitutional / civil / criminal
  [43:5:0.3] Special leave | 136 | Any court or tribunal except military (136(2))
  [43:5:0.4] Review | 137 | Own judgments; subject to Parliament's law and Art. 145 rules
  [43:5:0.5] Advisory | 143 | Opinion not binding
  [43:5:0.6] President / VP election | 71 | Original, exclusive, final
  [43:5:0.7] Enlarged Union List jurisdiction | 138(1) | By Parliament
[43:5:1] # Original Jurisdiction – Art. 131
[43:5:2] - Only disputes on **legal rights** between Centre and States or between States – SC as a **federal court**; main body for Centre-State fiscal disputes.
[43:5:3] - **Excluded**: inter-State **water disputes (Art. 262)**; **Finance Commission** matters (Art. 280); **pre-Constitution treaties**; political questions; **Art. 290** expense adjustments; **private party** suits for damages against the Union; disputes between private companies; Centre-UT disputes; **election disputes** of Parliament and Legislatures (go to **High Courts** – APPSC-G1 2022 key "II and III").
[43:5:4] # Appellate Jurisdiction
[43:5:5] - **Art. 132** (with **134A** certificate): constitutional interpretation cases.
[43:5:6] - **Art. 133**: civil – substantial question of law of general importance, HC certificate.
[43:5:7] - **Art. 134**: HC reverses acquittal and sentences to **death** – appeal **as of right**, no certificate.
[43:5:8] - **Art. 134(2):** **Parliament** may by law **enlarge** the SC's criminal appellate jurisdiction.
[43:5:9] - **Art. 136 SLP**: discretionary, plenary, residual; needs no HC certificate.
[43:5:10] # Advisory – Art. 143
[43:5:11] - **143(1)**: President refers questions of **law or fact** of public importance; Court **may** give opinion. Only the **President** can seek it.
[43:5:12] - **143(2)**: pre-Constitution treaty matters – Court **must** give opinion.
[43:5:13] - Examples: **Gujarat Assembly elections** delay – reference by **President Abdul Kalam (Aug 2002)**; **16th Presidential Reference (Nov 2025)** on Bill timelines.

[43:6:3] - **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971** (H.N. Sanyal Committee) defines civil and criminal contempt; the Constitution does not define it.
[43:6:4] - **e-SCR** – electronic Supreme Court Reports; free digital database of reported judgments, launched **2023**.
[43:6:5] - **V.V. Giri** appeared in person before the SC as sitting President in a petition challenging his election.
## SUB 7 | Removal of Judges
[43:7:0] - **Art. 124(4)**: by **order of the President** after an **address by Parliament**; grounds **proved misbehaviour or incapacity**.
[43:7:1] - **Special majority** in **each House** in the **same session** – majority of total membership + two-thirds present and voting.
[43:7:2] - **Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968** (Art. 124(5)): motion signed by **100 LS / 50 RS** members; **Speaker / Chairman may admit or reject**; **3-member committee** (SC judge/CJI, an HC Chief Justice, a distinguished jurist).
[43:7:3] - Constitution says "**removal**"; "impeachment" only for the President (**Art. 61**). Neither the CJI nor the President alone can remove a judge.
