=== Jurisdiction – Original (Art. 131), Appellate (Arts. 132-136), Writ (Art. 32)
# Jurisdictions at a glance
| Jurisdiction | Article | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Original** (federal) | **131** | Centre v. State(s), Centre + State(s) v. State(s), **State v. State** – **exclusive**: no other court |
| **Writ** | **32** | Fundamental Rights only; original but **not exclusive** (HCs also issue writs, Art. 226) |
| **Appellate – constitutional** | **132** (certificate under **134A**) | Any case (civil, criminal or other) with a substantial question of constitutional interpretation |
| **Appellate – civil** | **133** | Substantial question of law of general importance, on HC certificate (₹20,000 limit removed by 30th Amendment, 1972) |
| **Appellate – criminal** | **134** | HC reversed acquittal and sentenced to death, or withdrew a case and sentenced to death – **as of right**; otherwise HC certificate |
| **Special leave** | **136** | Discretionary; any court or tribunal **except military** (136(2)) |
| **Review** | **137** | Own judgments / orders |
| **Advisory** | **143** | President's references – opinion not binding |
| **President / VP election disputes** | **71** | Original, exclusive and final |
- The SC has **original, appellate and advisory** jurisdiction (plus writ, review and Court of Record powers) – "all the above" in the PYQs.
# Original jurisdiction – Article 131
- **SC as a federal court**: decides **Centre-State and inter-State** disputes that involve a **legal right** (question of law or fact); **exclusive** – no High Court shares it. It is the main forum for Centre-State **fiscal** disputes too.
- First Art. 131 suit: **West Bengal v. Union (1961)** on the Coal Bearing Areas Act.
- **Excluded from Art. 131**: disputes from **pre-Constitution treaties**, agreements, sanads (proviso – these can go to the President's reference under 143(2)); **inter-State river water disputes (Art. 262)**; matters referred to the **Finance Commission (Art. 280)**; adjustment of expenses and pensions (**Art. 290**); suits by **private persons** against the Union or a State; disputes between **private companies**; **Centre v. Union Territory**; **election disputes** of Parliament and State Legislatures (go to **High Courts**).
> APPSC G1 2023 / UPSC 2012: NOT in Art. 131 = election disputes and Centre-UT disputes; included = Centre-State and State-State. APSLPRB 2019: water disputes and Finance Commission matters are outside it.
# Appellate jurisdiction
- **Highest court of appeal** (replaced the Privy Council); appeals in **constitutional, civil and criminal** cases from HCs.
- **Art. 132 read with Art. 134A**: constitutional cases – HC certifies a substantial question of interpretation of the Constitution.
- **Art. 134(1)**: appeal **as of right** when the HC has **on appeal reversed an acquittal and sentenced the accused to death**, or has withdrawn a case from a subordinate court and convicted and sentenced him to death; otherwise only on HC certificate. No appeal as of right if the HC **reverses a conviction**.
- **Art. 134(2)**: **Parliament may by law enlarge** the criminal appellate jurisdiction – done in **1970** (reversal of acquittal with life or 10 years) – so "Parliament has no power" is **wrong**.
- **Art. 136 – Special Leave to Appeal**: the SC **may in its discretion** grant special leave from **any judgment, decree, order** of **any court or tribunal** (not only HCs), final or interlocutory, in any matter; **not** against **armed forces** courts or tribunals. A plenary, residual power – used "sparingly"; it is **not** about judges' leave or vacations.
# Writ jurisdiction – Article 32
- SC is the **guarantor and defender of Fundamental Rights**; a citizen may go **directly** to it (Art. 32 is itself a Fundamental Right).
- **Not exclusive**: HCs issue writs under **Art. 226** for FRs **and** for other legal rights – so the HC's writ power is **wider**; "SC has exclusive power to issue writs" is **false**.
# Enlarging jurisdiction
- **Art. 138(1)**: **Parliament** may by law confer further jurisdiction on the SC for matters in the **Union List**; Art. 138(2) – other matters by special agreement of Centre and State.
- **Art. 139**: Parliament may confer writ power for other purposes; **Art. 139A** (44th Amendment, 1978): SC may **withdraw** cases from HCs or **transfer** cases from one HC to another – so the **Supreme Court** can transfer a case anywhere in India.
- Parliament **can enlarge but not curtail** the SC's jurisdiction – UPSC 2005 "Parliament cannot enlarge it" is **false**.
> UPSC 2005: both statements wrong – Parliament can enlarge jurisdiction (Art. 138), and HC administrative expenses are charged on the **Consolidated Fund of the State**, not of India.

=== Special Powers – Court of Record, Review, Arts. 141, 142, 144, 145; Guardian of the Constitution
# Articles
| Article | Power |
|---|---|
| **129** | **Court of Record** – records of perpetual evidentiary value; power to **punish contempt** of itself (and, per 1991 ruling, of HCs and lower courts) |
| **137** | **Review** of its own judgments or orders – subject to Parliament's law and Art. 145 rules |
| **141** | **Law declared by the SC binding on all courts** within the territory of India |
| **142** | Decree or order for **complete justice** in any pending cause; enforceable throughout India |
| **144** | **All authorities, civil and judicial**, in India shall **act in aid of the Supreme Court** |
| **145** | Rules of practice and procedure – made **with the approval of the President**; **145(3)**: at least **five judges** for a substantial question of constitutional interpretation and for Art. 143 references |
- **Guardian / custodian of the Constitution** and **final interpreter** of it (judicial review); also guardian of **Fundamental Rights**, a **federal court** and the **highest court of appeal**. The Constitution's enforcement rests on the **Supreme Court** – not the President or Parliament.
- **Not powers of the SC**: issuing **ordinances** (President, Art. 123), advising the President on **all** matters (only on references under Art. 143), striking down **any** law (only unconstitutional ones).
- **Review (Art. 137)**: within **30 days**, before the same bench; grounds – new evidence, error apparent on the record, other sufficient reason. So the SC **can change its own judgment** – neither the President nor the Cabinet can review it.
- **Curative petition**: **Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra (2002)**, after a review is dismissed; rests on **Arts. 129 and 142** (inherent power) – PYQ key **Art. 142**.
- **Art. 142**: "prohibitions or limitations in **ordinary laws** cannot limit" it – the SC is **not constrained by ordinary laws** when doing complete justice. Uses: **ban on liquor shops within 500 m of highways (State of TN v. K. Balu, Dec 2016)**; deemed assent to 10 TN Bills (2025); Right to Safe Travel directives (27 May 2026).
- **Rule-making (Art. 145)** needs the **President's approval** – "complete independence and autonomy to make rules" is **wrong**; it is the SC's own rule-making power, subject to that approval.
- **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971** (H.N. Sanyal Committee) defines civil and criminal contempt; the Constitution does not.
- **V.V. Giri** (1970) – only sitting President to appear **in person** before the SC in a petition challenging his own election (Art. 71).
- **e-SCR** (2023): free digital database of the SC's reported judgments.
> APPSC G1 2019: correct statement = "all civil and judicial authorities shall act in aid of the SC" (Art. 144). Resignation goes to the President, the SC can review its judgments, and rules need the President's approval.

=== Composition, Appointment, Qualifications, Tenure; Acting, Ad Hoc and Retired Judges
# Appointment – Art. 124(2)
- **Every judge is appointed by the President** by warrant under his hand and seal – the collegium only recommends.
- **CJI**: President after consulting such SC and HC judges as he deems necessary; by convention (and Second Judges Case) the **senior-most judge**.
- **Other judges**: consultation with the **CJI is obligatory**; the President **may** consult other SC and HC judges.
- The President acts on the advice of the **Union Council of Ministers headed by the PM** (Art. 74) – the file goes Law Minister → PM → President (Memorandum of Procedure).
- Convention of seniority broken: **A.N. Ray (1973)**, superseding Shelat, Hegde and Grover; **M.H. Beg (1977)**.
# Qualifications – Art. 124(3)
- **Citizen of India** and either **5 years as an HC judge**, or **10 years as an HC advocate**, or a **distinguished jurist** in the **President's** opinion (never used so far). **No minimum age**.
# Oath, salary, tenure, resignation
- **Oath** before the **President** or a person appointed by him (form in the **Third Schedule**).
- **Salaries** determined by **Parliament by law** (Art. 125); not reduced after appointment except in a **Financial Emergency** (Art. 360).
- Holds office till **65**; age question decided as Parliament provides (Art. 124(2A)). **No fixed tenure** in years.
- **Resigns by writing addressed to the President** (Art. 124(2)(a)) – not the CJI, PM or Law Minister.
# Acting, ad hoc and retired judges
| Article | Who | Appointed / requested by |
|---|---|---|
| **126** | **Acting CJI** (office vacant, CJI absent or unable) | **President** appoints one of the other judges |
| **127** | **Ad hoc judge** – a qualified **HC judge** when there is **no quorum** | **CJI** with the President's **previous consent**, after consulting the HC Chief Justice |
| **128** | **Retired SC (or qualified HC) judge** to sit and act | **CJI** with the President's previous consent and the person's consent |
- **No additional judges** in the SC – additional and acting judges exist only in **High Courts (Art. 224)**; ad hoc judges are provided **only for the SC** (Art. 127).
- **High Courts can review their own judgments** as **Courts of Record (Art. 215)** though no specific review power is given to them; the SC has the express power (Art. 137).
> UPSC 2009 (asked again by APPSC): both statements correct – Art. 128 recall of retired judges, and the HC's power to review its own judgments.

=== Collegium, Judges Cases and NJAC
# Judges Cases
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **First – S.P. Gupta** | **1981** | Consultation ≠ concurrence; **executive primacy** |
| **Second – SC Advocates-on-Record Association (SCAORA)** | **1993** | Consultation = **concurrence**; **collegium** born (CJI + 2) |
| **Third – Presidential Reference, Art. 143(1)** | **1998** | Collegium of **CJI + 4 senior-most** judges; plurality of judges |
| **Fourth – SCAORA (NJAC case)** | **16 Oct 2015** | **99th Amendment and NJAC Act struck down** 4:1; collegium revived |
# Collegium
- **CJI + four senior-most SC judges** recommend SC appointments and HC transfers (CJI + 2 for HC appointments; HC collegium = HC CJ + 2 senior-most).
- **Not in the Constitution or any Act** – it derives from **Supreme Court decisions** (Judges Cases); adopted **1993**, present form **1998**.
- Formal appointment by the **President** (Art. 124(2) SC; Art. 217(1) HC); a reiterated name **must** be appointed.
- Criticism: **"imperium in imperio"**, opacity.
# NJAC – 99th Amendment, 2014
- **Constitution (99th Amendment) Act, 2014** + **National Judicial Appointments Commission Act, 2014** – inserted **Art. 124A-124C**; passed LS 13 Aug 2014, ratified by **16 States**, assent **31 Dec 2014**, in force **13 Apr 2015**.
- **Six members**: **CJI (Chairperson)**, **two senior-most SC judges**, **Union Law Minister**, **two eminent persons** nominated by a committee of the **PM, CJI and Leader of Opposition** in the Lok Sabha (or leader of the largest opposition party). AG not a member.
- **Struck down** by a **5-judge Constitution Bench** of the **Supreme Court** (Khehar J led) **4:1** – violated **judicial independence (Basic Structure)**; **J. Chelameswar** the **lone dissent** upholding the NJAC. India has no NJAC now.
# Earlier proposals
- **Constitution (98th Amendment) Bill, 2003** (NDA, Law Minister Arun Jaitley) – a **National Judicial Commission**. (The 98th Amendment **Act** of 2012 is different – Hyderabad-Karnataka, Art. 371J.)
- **NCRWC (Venkatachaliah, report 2002)** and the **Malimath Committee (2003, criminal justice reforms)** proposed a **National Judicial Commission**; Malimath also suggested amending Art. 124 to make **impeachment of judges less difficult** (not Panchayati Raj).
- **Judicial Standards and Accountability Bill, 2010** – passed by Lok Sabha (Mar 2012): a mechanism to **inquire into complaints against judges**; **lapsed** in 2014.

=== Advisory Jurisdiction – Article 143
- **Art. 143(1)**: the **President** may refer a **question of law or fact** of **public importance** (arisen or likely) – the Court **may** give or **refuse** its opinion (e.g. Ram Janmabhoomi, 1993). Only the President can seek it – not the PM, a High Court, or the Court on its own; not limited to Fundamental Rights.
- **Art. 143(2)**: disputes from **pre-Constitution treaties and agreements** (excluded from Art. 131) – the Court **must** give its opinion.
- Opinion is **advisory, not binding on the President**; heard by at least **five judges** (Art. 145(3)).
- Courts treat the law stated in a reference opinion as binding precedent (Art. 141) – the usual key "**not binding on the President, but binding on subordinate courts**".
- **India v. USA**: advisory jurisdiction is the main difference – the **US Supreme Court has none**.

=== Removal of Judges, Independence Safeguards; Supreme Court v. High Court
# Removal – Art. 124(4), (5)
- Removed only by an **order of the President** after an **address by each House** of Parliament in the **same session**, by **special majority** (majority of **total membership** + **two-thirds present and voting**). The President **cannot remove** a judge on his own; nor can the CJI.
- **Grounds**: **proved misbehaviour or incapacity** only – **not defined** in the Constitution; "violation of the Constitution" is the ground for the **President's** impeachment, not a judge's.
- **Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968** gives the procedure: motion signed by **100 LS / 50 RS** members; **Speaker / Chairman may admit or reject** it; a **3-member committee** (CJI or SC judge, an HC Chief Justice, a distinguished jurist) – **not the President** – inquires; debate allowed on the motion.
- **No judge removed so far**: **V. Ramaswami** (SC, 1993) – motion failed in the Lok Sabha; **Soumitra Sen** (Calcutta HC, 2011) – Rajya Sabha passed it, he **resigned (1 Sep 2011)** before the Lok Sabha vote.
- CAG (Art. 148) and CEC (Art. 324(5)) are removed like an SC judge; Art. 218 applies these provisions to HC judges.
> UPSC 2019: correct = 3 and 4 (procedure in the 1968 Act; special majority in each House). Speaker can reject the motion; "misbehaviour" is not defined.
# Independence safeguards
| Safeguard | Provision |
|---|---|
| Mode of appointment | President **must consult the CJI** – curtails executive discretion |
| Security of tenure | Removal only under Art. 124(4) |
| Expenses | Salaries, pensions and administrative expenses **charged on the Consolidated Fund of India** – non-votable |
| Conduct not discussed | In Parliament (Art. 121) / State Legislature (Art. 211), except on a removal motion |
| Ban on practice | Retired SC judge cannot plead **in any court or authority in India** (Art. 124(7)) |
| Staff | **CJI appoints** SC officers and servants **without executive interference** (Art. 146) |
| Jurisdiction | Parliament can enlarge, not curtail it |
> UPSC 2012: safeguards = 1 and 3 (consulting the CJI; salaries on the CFI). Removal is not by the CJI, and staff are appointed by the CJI, not the Government.
# Supreme Court v. High Court
| Point | Supreme Court | High Court |
|---|---|---|
| Strength fixed by | **Parliament** by law (Art. 124(1)) | **President** (Art. 216) |
| Retirement | 65 | **62** |
| Removal | Art. 124(4) | **Same mode** (Art. 218) |
| Retired judge may plead | **Nowhere** in India | Only in the **SC and other HCs** (Art. 220) |
| Salary / pension | CFI / CFI | **Consolidated Fund of the State** / **CFI** |
| Court of Record | Art. 129 | Art. 215 |
| Writs | FRs only (Art. 32) | FRs and other rights (Art. 226); superintendence (Art. 227) |

=== Establishment, Seat and Strength
# Establishment
- **Part V, Chapter IV – Articles 124-147**; **Art. 124** establishes and constitutes the SC (Court of Record is Art. 129).
- **Federal Court** (GoI Act 1935) set up **1937**; Privy Council appeals ended **1949**; the SC replaced both and was **inaugurated on 28 January 1950** (two days after the Republic, 26 Jan **1950**); moved to **Tilak Marg in 1958**.
- **Art. 130 – Seat**: **Delhi**, or elsewhere as the **CJI** decides **with the President's approval**.
- **Art. 348(1)(a)**: all proceedings in the SC and every HC in **English** (Art. 145 only lets the SC frame rules).
# Strength – Art. 124(1)
- **Parliament** fixes and increases the number by **ordinary law** – **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956** – not the President, the CJI, the collegium or the Law Commission.
| Year | Strength incl. CJI |
|---|---|
| 1950 (Constitution) | **8** (CJI + 7) |
| 1956 / 1960 | 11 / 14 |
| 1977 / 1986 | 18 / 26 |
| 2008 Act (2009) | 31 |
| 2019 | **34** (CJI + 33) |
| **16 May 2026** | **38 (CJI + 37)** – Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment **Ordinance, 2026** (Art. 123); replacing Bill introduced in the Lok Sabha (Jul 2026) |
- Benches are usually of 2-3 judges; a **Constitution Bench** has at least **five** (Art. 145(3)); the largest was **13** (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973).
- **Pendency**: the main cause given in PYQs is the **shortage of judges** (vacancies, low judge-population ratio) – the reason for the 2026 increase.
