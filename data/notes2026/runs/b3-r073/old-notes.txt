=== NOTES 3:73 | International Trade - Composition and Direction of India's Exports and Imports; Trade Deficit
## SUB 0 | Basic Terms of Foreign Trade
[73:0:0] - **Exports**: goods and services sold to the rest of the world; **imports**: goods and services bought from it.
[73:0:1] - **Net exports = X − M**: positive = **trade surplus** (exports > imports); negative = **trade deficit** (imports > exports).
[73:0:2] - **Closed economy**: neither exports nor imports; an **open economy** has both.
[73:0:3] - Payment for foreign trade is recorded in the **Balance of Payments**.
[73:0:4] TABLE head: Aspect | Meaning
  [73:0:4.0] Volume | Money value of exports and imports
  [73:0:4.1] Composition | Items traded
  [73:0:4.2] Direction | Country-wise / region-wise pattern
[73:0:5] - **Import substitution** = inward-looking strategy; **export promotion** = outward-looking strategy.
## SUB 1 | Trade Theory and Trade Terms
[73:1:0] # Theory
[73:1:1] TABLE head: Theory | Thinker | Idea
  [73:1:1.0] Absolute advantage | Adam Smith | Produce a good at lower absolute cost than others
  [73:1:1.1] Comparative advantage | David Ricardo | Specialise in the good with lower opportunity cost
[73:1:2] - India's **services surplus vs structural merchandise deficit** = Ricardian comparative advantage in services.
[73:1:3] Adam Smith = absolute advantage; Ricardo = comparative advantage. Do not swap.
[73:1:4] # Types of trade
[73:1:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [73:1:5.0] Intra-industry trade | A country imports and exports the same product (e.g. cars) - asked 2025
  [73:1:5.1] Inter-industry trade | Exchange of different products (comparative advantage)
  [73:1:5.2] Entrepot trade | Import, store/trade and re-export, with or without processing/packaging ("goods imported for export")
  [73:1:5.3] Dumping | Exporting at a price below the home-market price or cost
  [73:1:5.4] Free trade | Little or no tariff and no quantitative restriction ("absence of tariff")
[73:1:6] # Trade barriers and remedies
[73:1:7] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [73:1:7.0] Tariff | Tax on imports
  [73:1:7.1] Import quota | Quantitative restriction on the amount of imports
  [73:1:7.2] Import licensing | Administrative restriction on imports
  [73:1:7.3] Anti-dumping duty | Protects against goods exported below normal / home-market value
  [73:1:7.4] Countervailing duty | Offsets foreign subsidies (e.g. US preliminary CVD of 125.87% on an Indian product, Mar 2026)
  [73:1:7.5] Rules of origin | Decide the country of origin; decide which goods get FTA concessions
[73:1:8] - India used the **most anti-dumping measures in 1995-2005**.
[73:1:9] - **SPS measures:** sanitary and **phytosanitary** rules protecting human, animal and plant health (WTO SPS Agreement) – a **non-tariff barrier**.
## SUB 2 | Terms of Trade and Exchange Rate
[73:2:0] # Terms of trade
[73:2:1] TABLE head: Measure | Formula
  [73:2:1.0] Net (NTT) | Export price index / import price index × 100 (revised base, Mar 2026)
  [73:2:1.1] Gross (GTT) | Import quantity / export quantity
  [73:2:1.2] Income (ITT) | NTT × export quantity index
[73:2:2] # Devaluation and trade
[73:2:3] - **Devaluation/depreciation**: exports become **cheaper abroad**, imports **dearer at home** → exports rise, imports fall.
[73:2:4] - **J-curve**: after depreciation the trade balance **first worsens** (import bills rise at once), then improves as volumes adjust.
[73:2:5] - **Marshall-Lerner condition**: devaluation improves the trade balance only if export + import demand price elasticities **sum to more than 1**.
[73:2:6] - **1991 New Trade Policy**: import substitution and QRs → **price-based regime**; **two-step rupee devaluation (July 1991)**.
[73:2:7] Higher duties on exports would NOT improve the BoP; import substitution, rupee devaluation and higher import tariffs would.
