=== Cause and Chemistry of Ocean Acidification
- **Ocean acidification** = ongoing **fall in ocean pH** caused by the **uptake (dissolution) of atmospheric CO2** – the **primary cause**; not oil spills, sewage, microplastics or acid rain (acid rain matters only locally).
- Called the **"evil twin of global warming"** and **"the other CO2 problem"**.
- Oceans absorb about **one-third** of human CO2 (NextGen: 30-40%), buffering climate change.
# Chemistry
- **Step 1:** CO2 + H2O → **carbonic acid** → H+ + bicarbonate.
- **Step 2:** extra **H+ binds carbonate ions** – carbonate concentration falls.
- **Step 3:** **less carbonate** left for shells and skeletons; the ocean's ability to neutralise CO2 (buffering) falls.
- Surface pH **8.2 (pre-industrial) → ~8.1**: a fall of 0.1 = about **26-30% rise in acidity**; the ocean is **still alkaline**.
# Factors and results (APPSC NTR Health Univ. match)
| Factor | Result |
|---|---|
| CO2 uptake by oceans | **Steady decline in ocean pH** |
| Melting of Arctic ice | Exposes water to air → **more surface CO2**, faster acidification |
| Plankton blooms (eutrophication) | Bloom collapse → **bacterial respiration** adds CO2 |
| Decreased carbonate ion concentration | **Lower carbonate** → less ability to neutralise CO2 |
- Worse in **cold, temperate and Arctic waters**: CO2 is more soluble in cold water.
- **Cement** sector: about 5-8% of global anthropogenic CO2.
> pH falls only 0.1, yet acidity rises ~30% (log scale). The ocean has not turned acidic.

=== Effects on Calcifying Organisms and Marine Life
- **Calcifiers at risk:** corals, molluscs (**snails, oysters**, mussels, clams), **sea urchins**, crustaceans, **calcareous plankton** – all of them; they need carbonate ions to build calcium carbonate shells and skeletons.
| Organism | Effect |
|---|---|
| Pteropods ("**sea butterflies**") | Shells dissolve; **key food for fish** |
| Corals, bivalve larvae | Use **aragonite**, more soluble than calcite → more sensitive |
| Mussels, oysters | Calcification falls at high CO2 |
- **UPSC 2012:** acidification harms **calcareous phytoplankton**, **coral reefs** and animals with **phytoplanktonic larvae** (I, II, III – official key). NextGen also notes less **DMS** from phytoplankton may reduce cloud formation.
- **Saturation horizon** (below which CaCO3 dissolves) is rising towards the surface.
- **Warming oceans:** marine fish are shifting **towards the poles** (cooler water).
> "All of the above" is the usual key for calcifier lists.

=== Coral Reefs and Bleaching
- Reef-building corals secrete **calcium carbonate** skeletons; symbiotic algae **zooxanthellae** give food and colour.
- **Bleaching:** heat-stressed corals lose zooxanthellae; warming raises bleaching, acidification cuts calcifying ability.
- **4th global bleaching event** (declared by **NOAA and ICRI**, Apr 2024): Jan 2023-Mar 2025 hit **84%** of reefs; earlier events **1998, 2010, 2014-17**.
- Coral reefs = "**rainforests of the sea**"; **Great Barrier Reef** (Australia) = world's largest reef system.

=== Related Stressors – Heatwaves and Dead Zones
| Stressor | Meaning / effect |
|---|---|
| Marine heatwave (MHW) | SST 3-4°C above average for **at least 5 days**; number **doubled** since 1982 |
| Deoxygenation | Nutrient runoff → algal blooms → **decay uses oxygen** → hypoxic dead zones |
- Cyclones over MHWs: up to **20% stronger winds**, 12% more rain.
- MHWs = **strongest global driver of coral-cover decline** (GCRMN 2026).
