## SUB 0 | Cause and Chemistry of Ocean Acidification
[0:0] - **Ocean acidification** = ongoing **fall in ocean pH** caused by the **uptake (dissolution) of atmospheric CO2** – the **primary cause**; not oil spills, sewage, microplastics or acid rain (acid rain matters only locally).
[0:1] - Called the **"evil twin of global warming"** and **"the other CO2 problem"**.
[0:2] - Oceans absorb about **one-third** of human CO2 (NextGen: 30-40%), buffering climate change.
[0:3] # Chemistry
[0:4] - **Step 1:** CO2 + H2O → **carbonic acid** → H+ + bicarbonate.
[0:5] - **Step 2:** extra **H+ binds carbonate ions** – carbonate concentration falls.
[0:6] - **Step 3:** **less carbonate** left for shells and skeletons; the ocean's ability to neutralise CO2 (buffering) falls.
[0:7] - Surface pH **8.2 (pre-industrial) → ~8.1**: a fall of 0.1 = about **26-30% rise in acidity**; the ocean is **still alkaline**.
[0:8] # Factors and results (APPSC NTR Health Univ. match)
[0:9] TABLE head: Factor | Result
  [0:9.0] CO2 uptake by oceans | Steady decline in ocean pH
  [0:9.1] Melting of Arctic ice | Exposes water to air → more surface CO2, faster acidification
  [0:9.2] Plankton blooms (eutrophication) | Bloom collapse → bacterial respiration adds CO2
  [0:9.3] Decreased carbonate ion concentration | Lower carbonate → less ability to neutralise CO2
[0:10] - Worse in **cold, temperate and Arctic waters**: CO2 is more soluble in cold water.
[0:11] - **Cement** sector: about 5-8% of global anthropogenic CO2.
[0:12] > pH falls only 0.1, yet acidity rises ~30% (log scale). The ocean has not turned acidic.
[0:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC Part 8.2 p.7-8; Shankar Environment 15 (OCR) p.16-17; PMF Environment Part 4 p.16-18
## SUB 1 | Effects on Calcifying Organisms and Marine Life
[1:0] - **Calcifiers at risk:** corals, molluscs (**snails, oysters**, mussels, clams), **sea urchins**, crustaceans, **calcareous plankton** – all of them; they need carbonate ions to build calcium carbonate shells and skeletons.
[1:1] TABLE head: Organism | Effect
  [1:1.0] Pteropods ("sea butterflies") | Shells dissolve; key food for fish
  [1:1.1] Corals, bivalve larvae | Use aragonite, more soluble than calcite → more sensitive
  [1:1.2] Mussels, oysters | Calcification falls at high CO2
[1:2] - **UPSC 2012:** acidification harms **calcareous phytoplankton**, **coral reefs** and animals with **phytoplanktonic larvae** (I, II, III – official key). NextGen also notes less **DMS** from phytoplankton may reduce cloud formation.
[1:3] - **Saturation horizon** (below which CaCO3 dissolves) is rising towards the surface.
[1:4] - **Warming oceans:** marine fish are shifting **towards the poles** (cooler water).
[1:5] > "All of the above" is the usual key for calcifier lists.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 15 (OCR) p.16-18; NextGen Environment APPSC Part 8.2 p.8; PMF Environment Part 4 p.18
## SUB 2 | Coral Reefs and Bleaching
[2:0] - Reef-building corals secrete **calcium carbonate** skeletons; symbiotic algae **zooxanthellae** give food and colour.
[2:1] - **Bleaching:** heat-stressed corals lose zooxanthellae; warming raises bleaching, acidification cuts calcifying ability.
[2:2] - **4th global bleaching event** (declared by **NOAA and ICRI**, Apr 2024): Jan 2023-Mar 2025 hit **84%** of reefs; earlier events **1998, 2010, 2014-17**.
[2:3] - Coral reefs = "**rainforests of the sea**"; **Great Barrier Reef** (Australia) = world's largest reef system.
[2:4] # Indian reef sites
[2:5] TABLE head: Site | Fact
  [2:5.0] Gulf of Mannar | India's first marine biosphere reserve; Marine National Park 1980
  [2:5.1] Gulf of Kutch | Among the world's most northerly reefs
  [2:5.2] Lakshadweep | Entirely atoll reefs; giant Pavona clavus at Kadmat
  [2:5.3] Andaman & Nicobar | Most diverse reefs in India

[4:0] TABLE head: Stressor | Meaning / effect
