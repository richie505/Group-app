## SUB 0 | Satellite Navigation Systems of the World – Owners and Full Forms
[0:0] # Four global, two regional systems
[0:1] TABLE head: System | Owner | Scope
  [0:1.0] GPS – Global Positioning System | USA (Dept of Defense) | Global
  [0:1.1] GLONASS | Russia (not the UK) | Global
  [0:1.2] Galileo | European Union (not the USA) – live 2016, under civilian control, works with GPS and GLONASS | Global
  [0:1.3] BeiDou | China | Global
  [0:1.4] NavIC (formerly IRNSS) | India | Regional
  [0:1.5] QZSS (Michibiki) | Japan | Regional
[0:2] - **Which country has its own system?** Among Australia, Canada, Israel, Japan only **Japan** (QZSS) does (UPSC 2023).
[0:3] - **Galileo Project** (UPSC 2007): Europe's **multi-satellite navigation** project, not a missile shield.
[0:4] # GPS basics
[0:5] - **GPS** programme begun **1973**, first satellite 1978, fully operational 1995.
[0:6] - Original design: **24 satellites in 6 orbits** at about **20,200 km** (MEO); each circles Earth **twice a day**; about 30 in use (2026).
[0:7] - Signals in **L1** and **L2** bands; a **1 microsecond** clock error ≈ **300 m** position error.
[0:8] - A receiver needs **4 satellites**: 3 for position, 1 to correct its own clock error (trilateration).
[0:9] > Exam angle: "wrong pair" items swap owners – GLONASS–UK and Galileo–USA are the traps (APPSC 2018, 2019).

## SUB 1 | NavIC (IRNSS) – Constellation, Coverage and Services
[1:0] # Names and genesis
[1:1] - **IRNSS = Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System**; operational name **NavIC = Navigation with Indian Constellation**, given by PM **Narendra Modi** (2016).
[1:2] - Genesis: the US denied GPS data to India in the **1999 Kargil War** (**Operation Vijay**).
[1:3] - NavIC is **independent** of GPS: it keeps its own **Indian time** (IRNSS Network Time, from on-board atomic clocks), not GPS time.
[1:4] - Providing PNT since **2018**; India is the **only developing country** with its own independent regional system.
[1:5] # Constellation and coverage
[1:6] - **7 satellites: 3 GEO + 4 inclined GSO** (at **29°**), about 36,000 km up – not 4 GEO + 3 GSO, not 12.
[1:7] - Covers India and **about 1,500 km** beyond its borders (not "5,500 sq km"); **regional, not global** (UPSC 2018: only statement 1 correct).
[1:8] - Signals in **L5 and S bands**; the new **NVS** satellites add a civilian **L1** signal.
[1:9] TABLE head: Service | Users | Accuracy
  [1:9.0] Standard Positioning Service (SPS) | All civilians (open) | Better than 20 m (about 5 m in practice)
  [1:9.1] Restricted Service (RS) | Military / authorised | Encrypted
[1:10] - Nodal body for NavIC and GAGAN: **Satellite Navigation Programme Office**, ISRO.
[1:11] > Exam angle: IRNSS ≠ GAGAN – a statement "IRNSS is also known as GAGAN" is false.
[1:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 2 p.6; PMF S&T Part 1 p.113-118; testbook TSPSC HWO 2018 key (Indian clock)
## SUB 2 | IRNSS and NVS Satellites – Launches and Atomic Clocks
[2:0] # Launches
[2:1] - **IRNSS-1A**, India's first dedicated navigation satellite: **PSLV-C22**, **1 July 2013**, **Sriharikota**; the whole IRNSS-1 series flew on **PSLV**.
[2:2] - **11** navigation satellites launched since 2013, all from Sriharikota.
[2:3] TABLE head: Satellite | Vehicle / date | Note
  [2:3.0] NVS-01 (2nd generation) | GSLV-F12, May 2023 | First indigenous rubidium clock
  [2:3.1] NVS-02 | GSLV-F15, 29 Jan 2025 | 100th Sriharikota launch; valve (pyro connector) glitch → wrong orbit
[2:4] # Atomic clocks
[2:5] - **Caesium** clock is the world's **standard of time** (the SI second); NavIC satellites carry **rubidium** clocks (three each), smaller but slightly less accurate.
[2:6] - First-generation rubidium clocks from Swiss firm **SpectraTime** failed → indigenous clocks by **SAC, Ahmedabad**.
[2:7] - **13 Mar 2026:** last clock on **IRNSS-1F** stopped → only **3** PNT satellites (1B, 1I, NVS-01), below the **4** needed for a fix.
[2:8] # Limitations
[2:9] - **Regional** coverage only; thin **receiver** ecosystem (L5/S rarely in phone chips; L1 on NVS helps); clock failures + NVS-02 failure → NavIC could not fully replace GPS (Mar 2026).
[2:10] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 1 p.114; Uttarakhand PCS 2012 explanation (PSLV-C22); app notes 5:38 sub 2, 5
## SUB 3 | Uses of GPS and NavIC; Space Weather
[3:0] # Applications
[3:1] - **GPS / satellite timing** is used in **mobile phone networks, banking and power grids** – all three (UPSC 2018).
[3:2] - **Tectonic plate movement** is monitored with **GPS** (with VLBI and SLR) – not GIS or weather radar.
[3:3] - NavIC: terrestrial, aerial, marine navigation; **disaster management**; vehicle tracking and **fleet management**; precise timing (IST, power, telecom, banking); mapping and geodesy; **precision farming**; hikers and drivers.
[3:4] - **MoRTH AIS-140:** NavIC/GPS trackers and panic buttons compulsory in **public transport and commercial vehicles** (not all private cars).
[3:5] - **Qualcomm Snapdragon** chipsets support NavIC; reconfigured **IRNSS-1A** and GEMINI devices send cyclone, tsunami and high-wave alerts to **fishermen**.
[3:6] - **Elena Geo Systems:** India's **first NavIC-based device**, for precise directions on infrastructure projects (APPSC 2023).
[3:7] - **Himachal Pradesh** made a **GPS device mandatory for trekkers** (June 2019).
[3:8] - **APSDMA** uses **Satellite Based Mobile Data Voice (SBMDV)** terminals for real-time satellite communication (IRNSS, NavIC, SBAS are navigation, not communication).
[3:9] - Strategic autonomy: forces need not depend on foreign systems in a conflict.
[3:10] # Solar storms
[3:11] - A major **solar storm** can make **GPS fail**, damage **power grids**, cause **auroras** far from the poles, disturb **satellite orbits** and cut **shortwave radio** on polar flights – not tsunamis or forest fires (UPSC 2022).
[3:12] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 1 p.115-116; NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 2 p.6; gktoday / Business Standard (HP trekkers, 2019); UPSC 2018, 2022 keys
## SUB 4 | GAGAN and Other Satellite-Based Augmentation Systems
[4:0] - **GAGAN = GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation**: India's **SBAS**, built jointly by **ISRO + Airports Authority of India** (not DRDO); operational since **2015** (DGCA-certified).
[4:1] - Not an independent system: ground reference stations compute live corrections for orbit, clock and **ionospheric delay**, sent via **GEO** satellites → more **accuracy and integrity**.
[4:2] - Payloads on **GSAT-8, GSAT-10, GSAT-15**; covers the **Indian Flight Information Region**; precision-like approach (APV-I) without ILS.
[4:3] - Benefits go **beyond aviation** – maritime (GEMINI), railways, roads, survey, disaster response – so "only aviation" is false (UPSC 2025: I and II only).
[4:4] - GAGAN is one of the systems ISRO uses for **satellite navigation services** (INSAT, IRS, AstroSat are not).
[4:5] TABLE head: SBAS | Region
  [4:5.0] WAAS | USA
  [4:5.1] EGNOS | Europe
  [4:5.2] MSAS | Japan
  [4:5.3] GAGAN | India
[4:6] > Exam angle: GAGAN augments GPS for aviation; NavIC is an independent regional system.
