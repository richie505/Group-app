=== Supreme Court on District Judges, Waqf and Minority Schools (2025)
# Rejanish K.V. v K. Deepa – district judges (9 October 2025)
- **Ruling:** five-judge Bench led by **CJI B.R. Gavai** – **serving judicial officers can be directly recruited as district judges** (earlier only advocates with 7 years' practice).
- **Overruled:** **Satya Narain Singh (1984)** and **Dheeraj Mor v High Court of Delhi (2020)**; **Justice Sundresh** – an absolute bar violates **Article 14**.
- **Conditions:** **7 years** of advocacy + judicial service combined; uniform minimum age **35 years**.
- **Article 233:** **district judges** are appointed by the **Governor in consultation with the High Court**; other judicial officers (Art 234) – Governor after consulting the **State PSC and High Court** (Arts 233–237, Part VI).
- **Criminal courts:** CJM – offences up to 7 years; JM – up to 3 years; a **death sentence by a Sessions Judge needs High Court confirmation**.
# Seniority in Higher Judicial Services
- **Hearings from 28 October 2025** (five-judge Bench, CJI Gavai): promotees vs direct recruits; **promotees ≈ three-fourths** of the cadre, though direct recruits dominate senior posts.
- **All India Judges Association (2002):** **75% promotion** (50% merit-cum-seniority + 25% LDCE), **25% direct recruitment**; 40-point roster (R.K. Sabarwal, 1995).
- **May 2025 SC framework:** LDCE 25% restored; **qualifying service 5 → 3 years**; **3 years' practice** for Civil Judge entry.
- **District judiciary** handles about **90%** of litigation.
- **NALSA:** **Patron-in-Chief = CJI**; **Executive Chairman = second senior-most SC judge**; Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987; Art 39A.
# Waqf (Amendment) Act, 2025 – interim ruling
| Provision | SC decision |
|---|---|
| 5 years of practising Islam to create a waqf | **Stayed** until a verification mechanism is framed |
| **Section 3C** – officer (Collector) may declare waqf property as government property | **Stayed** ("totally unconstitutional"); title decided by Waqf Tribunal (Sec 83) |
| Central Waqf Council | At most **4 non-Muslims of 22** |
| State Waqf Boards | At most **3 non-Muslims of 11**; CEO preferably Muslim |
| Mandatory registration | Upheld – required since 1923 |
| Protected monuments | Religious practice continues – Sec 5(6), **AMASR Act, 1958** |
- **Overall:** the Act's validity was **upheld overall**; arbitrary provisions stayed.
# Pramati referred to a larger Bench (September 2025)
- **Bench:** Justices **Dipankar Datta and Manmohan** – Pramati (2014) went "too far"; referred to a larger Bench (TET for minority schools); **Art 21A and Art 30(1) must co-exist**.
| Case | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Society for Unaided Private Schools of Rajasthan (2012) | RTE upheld; **unaided minority schools** exempted |
| Pramati (2014), 5 judges | Exempted **aided and unaided minority schools** |
- **RTE Act, 2009:** Art 21A (6–14 years); **Section 12(1)(c)** – private unaided schools reserve **25% of entry-level seats** for disadvantaged groups, State reimburses; bans capitation fees and corporal punishment; TET.
- **Article 30(1):** all minorities (religious and linguistic) may establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.

=== Rights Rulings – DNA, Defamation, Speech, Personality, Internet, NDPS
# DNA evidence – Kattavellai @ Devakar v State of Tamil Nadu
- **Directions:** documented packaging signed by doctor, IO and witnesses; sample to the **FSL within 48 hours**; Chain of Custody Register.
- **Nature:** DNA is **opinion evidence** needing corroboration – **Sec 45 Evidence Act = Sec 39 BSA, 2023**.
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| Anil v Maharashtra (2014) | Reliable only with quality control |
| Manoj v MP (2022) | Rejected – sample from an open area |
| Rahul v Delhi (2022) | Rejected – sample lay in the Malkhana for 2 months |
# Criminal defamation and Section 152 BNS
- **Defamation:** IPC 499, 500 – up to **2 years**, fine or both; **Subramanian Swamy v UoI (2016)** upheld it (reputation part of **Art 21**); **22 September 2025 – Justice M.M. Sundresh** flagged misuse (Foundation for Independent Journalism case); the UK has abolished it.
- **Section 152 BNS:** Assam Police summoned **Siddharth Varadarajan and Karan Thapar** (The Wire) over **Operation Sindoor** coverage; SC protected them; the summons **lacked** the FIR date, offence details and FIR copy; seen as a rebranded **Section 124A (sedition)** (in abeyance since 2022); does **not** need a high standard of proof of intent; Art 19(1)(a).
# High Court rulings
- **AP High Court (Justice N. Harinath):** **SC status ceases immediately on conversion to Christianity**; no SC/ST (PoA) Act, 1989 protection – Pastor Chintada Anand (Guntur); **Constitution (SC) Order, 1950** – only **Hinduism, Sikhism, Buddhism**.
- **Kerala HC (4 Nov 2025):** cultivating any cannabis plant is illegal even without flowering tops.
- **Karnataka HC:** dismissed **X Corp**'s plea; upheld the **Sahyog Portal** (requests under Sec 79(3)(b), IT Act) as "**an instrument of public good**"; Sec 79 differs from Sec 69A (Shreya Singhal, 2015).
- **Delhi HC (September 2025):** protected **Aishwarya Rai Bachchan, Abhishek Bachchan and Karan Johar** against AI deepfakes; personality rights under the **Copyright Act, 1957 (Secs 38A, 38B)**, Trade Marks Act 1999 and Art 21.

# Internet, privacy and dress codes
- **Internet:** **Faheema Shirin v Kerala (Kerala HC, 2019)** – internet access part of **Art 21** (education, privacy); **Anuradha Bhasin v UoI (SC, 10 Jan 2020)** – against indefinite shutdowns, **Art 19**; the **United Nations** declared internet access a human right in **2016**.
- **Privacy:** **Puttaswamy (2017)** – privacy a fundamental right under Art 21.
- **Kerala hijab row** (St. Rita's Public School, Kochi): **Art 25** freedom of religion subject to **public order, morality and health**; school autonomy – **Art 19(1)(g)**; courts apply the **proportionality test**.

=== Constitution (130th Amendment) Bill and Other 2025 Bills
# Constitution (130th Amendment) Bill, 2025
- **Introduced:** in Lok Sabha by the **Union Home Minister** (20 Aug 2025); amends **Article 75** (Ministers hold office during the President's pleasure), with Arts 164 and 239AA.
- **Rule:** the **PM, CMs and Union/State Ministers** held in custody for **30 consecutive days** (without bail) on charges punishable with **at least 5 years** are removed by the President/Governor on advice by the 31st day, or cease automatically.
- **No permanent disqualification** – re-appointment possible after release.
- **Backdrop:** **V. Senthil Balaji case (Tamil Nadu, 2023)**.
- **JPC:** **31 members – 21 Lok Sabha + 10 Rajya Sabha**; a JPC is an **ad hoc** body. By **July 2026** it was **left out of the Monsoon Session agenda, with its JPC deadline extended**.

- **Articles:** 75(1)/164(1) – appointment; **75(3)/164(2) – collective responsibility**.

=== Elections – ECI, SIR, ECINET and the Vice-President
# Election Commission – what it can and cannot do (APPSC PYQ)
- **SVEEP** = **Systematic Voters' Education and Electoral Participation** – the **ECI's flagship voter-awareness programme** (APPSC PYQ answer).
- **Symbols:** the ECI recognises parties and allots symbols under the **Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968** – a power from Art 324 and statute, not one the Constitution names.
- **No power to deregister:** the ECI registers parties under Sec 29A, RPA 1951, but may **not deregister** a party for violating the Act (INC v Institute of Social Welfare, 2002 – only for fraud in registration or similar).
- **Seats per State:** allotment of Lok Sabha seats to States is fixed by **Parliament (Arts 81–82) and the Delimitation Commission**, not the ECI.
- **ECI composition:** three members; 2023 selection committee **chaired by the PM** (LoP and a Cabinet Minister members); 6 years or 65; advises on disqualification of MPs (**Art 103**) and MLAs (Art 192).
# Bihar SIR and pan-India SIR (2025)
- **10 July 2025 SC order:** **did not stay** the Bihar SIR; ordered draft-roll publication; directed the ECI to accept **Aadhaar as the 12th valid document** – proof of identity, **not citizenship** (Justices Surya Kant, Joymalya Bagchi).
- **Pan-India SIR:** announced **27 October 2025**.
| Case / provision | Point |
|---|---|
| **Lal Babu Hussain (1995)** | Burden of proving citizenship on **new registrants** |
| Mohinder Singh Gill (1978) | Art 324 powers plenary but within limits |
| **Sec 16, RPA 1950** | Only citizens can be registered as electors |
# Roll integrity and ECINET
- **Aland (Karnataka, 2023):** fraudulent **Form-7** applications (Form-7 = **objection to inclusion/deletion**) – ECI made **Aadhaar-linked mobile verification (e-sign)** mandatory on ECINet for online registration, deletion and correction.
- **ECINET:** announced **4 May 2025**; first used in the **19 June 2025** bypolls (Kerala, Gujarat, Punjab, West Bengal); turnout uploaded by **presiding officers** before leaving the station.
- **Machine-readable rolls:** rolls shared **as image PDFs without voter photographs**; before 2019 the EC told CEOs to **stop** uploading machine-readable rolls; Kamal Nath v ECI (2018).
# Vice-Presidential election, September 2025
- **Result:** **C.P. Radhakrishnan (NDA)** defeated **B. Sudershan Reddy** (joint opposition); Returning Officer **P.C. Mody** (RS Secretary General).
- **Article 66:** electoral college of **elected and nominated members of both Houses** – State MLAs do **not** vote; **proportional representation by single transferable vote, secret ballot**; minimum age **35**; ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
# Freebies vs welfare
- **S. Subramaniam Balaji v TN (2013):** manifesto promises are **not a "corrupt practice" under Section 123, RPA**.
- **Good welfare example:** **NTR's ₹2-a-kg rice scheme** (AP, 1980s).

=== Citizenship, Immigration and Refugees
# Immigration and Foreigners Act and Orders, 2025
- **Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025** replaced the **Foreigners Act 1946** and related laws.
- **Immigration and Foreigners Order, 2025** (MHA, **1 September 2025**) replaced the **Foreigners (Tribunal) Order, 1964**; gave a **clear legal basis for detention for the first time**; **Foreigners Tribunals exist only in Assam**.
- **Exemption Order, 2025:** exempts Nepal/Bhutan citizens, Tibetans, six minorities, **Sri Lankan Tamils who sheltered on or before 9 January 2015**; it **does not confer citizenship**.
- **IMDT Act, 1983** struck down in **2005** – the 11 IMDTs **became Foreigners Tribunals**.
# Refugee policy
- India is **not a signatory** to the **1951 Refugee Convention or 1967 Protocol**.
- **CAA 2019:** six minorities from Pakistan, Afghanistan, Bangladesh who entered **before 31 December 2014** (not 9 Jan 2015).
