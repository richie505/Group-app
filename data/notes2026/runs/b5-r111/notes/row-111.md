=== rDNA Technology – Tools, Vectors and Steps
# What genetic engineering does
- **Genetic engineering / recombinant DNA (rDNA) technology:** cutting, joining and transferring genes between organisms; **rDNA = DNA formed by combining genetic material from different sources** – also called **chimaeric (chimeric) DNA**.
- **Transgenic organism:** an organism whose DNA carries a **foreign gene from another species** (the "transgene"), inserted artificially for a **desired characteristic or product**.
- **GMO:** any organism whose genome is altered by genetic engineering – genes moved **in ways that would not occur naturally**.
- **Across species:** rDNA moves genes **plant to plant, animal to plant and microbe to higher organism** – all three possible (UPSC 2013).
- **Mouse eight times its size** with a human growth-hormone gene = **genetic engineering** (not hybridisation, mutation breeding or hormonal feeding).
- **Tried with success:** **disease resistance, growth promotion, animal cloning**; **human cloning is NOT** (banned, no fruitful result) → "1, 2 and 3".
# Tools
| Tool | Role |
|---|---|
| **Restriction endonuclease** | **"Molecular scissors" / chemical knives** – **cuts DNA at specific recognition (base) sequences**, not at random |
| **DNA ligase** | **"Molecular glue"** – **joins DNA fragments** (gene + vector); in replication it **joins Okazaki fragments** |
| **Plasmid** | Small, extra-chromosomal, usually **circular double-stranded DNA** of bacteria; replicates independently; the commonest **vector** |
| **pBR322** | Early, widely used **E. coli** cloning vector; p = plasmid, **BR = Bolivar and Rodriguez** |
| **Origin of replication (ori)** | **The specific DNA sequence that initiates replication**; alien DNA linked to it can multiply in the host |
| **PCR** | **Amplifies (copies) a specific DNA segment in a test tube – no living cells needed** (heat-stable Taq polymerase, 20-40 cycles) |
| **RNAi** | Gene silencing |
- **Two bacteria most used:** ***Escherichia coli*** (host for cloning) and ***Agrobacterium tumefaciens*** (plant vector).
- ***Agrobacterium tumefaciens*:** soil bacterium that **causes crown gall disease** (dicots) by transferring **T-DNA** of its **Ti plasmid**; the disarmed Ti plasmid makes it "**Nature's genetic engineer**" → useful for agriculture, crown gall agent and gene-transfer tool = **all of the above**.
  - Crown gall is NOT caused by *Bacillus thuringiensis* (insect pathogen) or *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (biocontrol).
# Steps
- **Isolate DNA → cut at specific sites (restriction enzyme) → join to vector with DNA ligase (rDNA) → insert into host (transformation) → select, multiply, express product.**
- Recombinant plasmid enters a bacterium by **transformation**; bacteria multiply fast and act as factories.
- **Gene cloning:** making many copies of a desired gene or DNA fragment; **genetic library** = a collection of cloned DNA of an organism.
| Field | Biotech use |
|---|---|
| Agriculture | Transgenic / GM crops |
| Medicine | Drugs, vaccines, diagnostics, gene therapy; **human insulin from GE bacteria** |
| Industry | **Microbial production of useful products** |
| Research | **Biochips** – DNA, RNA **and protein** chips for diagnostics and drug discovery |
> Exam angle: molecular scissors = restriction endonuclease (×3); plasmid; E. coli + Agrobacterium; crown gall; ligase joins Okazaki fragments; ori; PCR; chimaeric DNA; human cloning not achieved.

=== Bt Technology and Bt Cotton
# Bt
- **Bt = *Bacillus thuringiensis***: Gram-positive **soil bacterium** – not Bacillus subtilis, amyloliquefaciens or "thermophilus".
- **Cry (delta-endo) toxins:** crystal **protoxin** activated in the larva's **alkaline gut** → pores in midgut → death; trait = **resistance to insects** (bollworms, borers), not to viruses, bacteria or fungi.
- **Insect-resistant cotton = gene from a bacterium** (not virus, insect or plant); A: Bt cotton made by inserting the Bt gene, R: the gene is from a bacterium → **both true, R explains A**.
- **Bt seed is associated with cotton.**
# Bt cotton in India
| Version | Year | Genes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bollgard I** | **2002** (India's first biotech crop technology) | **Cry1Ac** (single gene) |
| **Bollgard II** | **2006** | **Cry1Ac + Cry2Ab** |
- **Bollgard I/II = technologies for developing GM crop plants** (Monsanto) – not clonal propagation, growth substances or biofertilisers (UPSC 2021).
- **Approved March 2002** (for three years): three **MAHYCO** (Monsanto-Mahyco) hybrids against the **American bollworm**; **India's first and only commercial GM crop**; now **~90-95% of the cotton area** (~10.8 m ha).
- Benefits: higher yield; **pesticide use cut ~40-50%** because the plant **makes its own Bt toxin**.
- Problems: **pink bollworm resistance**; rise of **sucking pests (whitefly, mealybug)**; costly seed; Bt proteins **degrade in soil** (no lasting persistence).
- **Maharashtra** = leading Bt cotton State (largest area).
# HTBt cotton (illegal)
- **HTBt** = Bt + tolerance to the herbicide **glyphosate**; **not approved**; spread driven by labour shortage and cheap glyphosate weeding.
- A 2025-26 study: ~**15-18% of AP's cotton area** (27-30% in Telangana); AP **restricted glyphosate** use.
> Exam angle: Bt = Bacillus thuringiensis (×6), bacterium not virus, insect resistance, Bollgard (×2), A-R (×2).

=== Golden Rice and Other GE Crop Traits
# Golden Rice
- **Transgenic rice (*Oryza sativa*) with genes for beta-carotene (carotene)** – **pro-vitamin A**, converted to **vitamin A** in the body; against **vitamin A deficiency** (blindness).
- **Genes:** phytoene synthase from **daffodil** + crtI from the bacterium *Erwinia uredovora*; developed by **Ingo Potrykus and Peter Beyer**; golden colour = beta-carotene.
- Not a wild, Yellow-River or long-stored rice; not higher yield, not all essential amino acids, not vitamin D; not thiamine, ascorbic acid or calciferol.
- **Beta-carotene** is the plant pigment that is a **vitamin A precursor** (not xanthophyll, chlorophyll or anthocyanin).
- A: Golden rice benefits consumers as much as farmers; R: its yellow colour reflects high beta-carotene → **both true, R explains A** (UPPSC 2000 key).
- Status: **not approved** for cultivation in India (IRRI-led; Philippines).
- Other transgenic-plant distractors: buckwheat, macaroni (durum) wheat and **triticale (man-made cereal by hybridisation)** are not transgenic.
# Traits engineered besides pest resistance (UPSC 2012)
- **Drought tolerance, higher nutritive value (biofortification), longer shelf life** (Flavr Savr) → **1, 2 and 4**; growing/photosynthesis in spaceships is not an established GE goal.
- **Biofortification** usually through **conventional breeding** (no foreign gene); GE route = golden rice.
# GM wheat and Indian marker-assisted rice
- **10 May 2004:** **Monsanto** dropped its **Roundup Ready GM wheat**, citing market resistance – while still selling Bt cotton and GM maize.
- **Improved Samba Mahsuri:** gene-pyramided (marker-assisted) bacterial-blight-resistant rice by **ICAR-IIRR (then DRR) and CCMB**, Hyderabad.
> Exam angle: golden rice = beta-carotene / vitamin A (×6), daffodil gene, transgenic plant; UPSC 2012 traits; Monsanto GM wheat (×2).

=== Genome Editing – CRISPR-Cas9 and Genome-edited Rice
# CRISPR-Cas9
- **CRISPR** = **Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats** – a natural **defence system of bacteria and archaea**.
- **Cas9 = the molecular scissors** (nuclease that cuts DNA); **guide RNA** guides Cas9 to the target site.
- Used for **gene (genome) editing** – delete, insert or alter genes; **not** a biosensor, pest-resistance gene, herbicide, plant breeding, radiotherapy or immunisation.
- **Gene editing** is a key application of biotechnology (APPSC FSO Mains).
- Pairs (UPSC 2018): Belle II = particle physics, **blockchain = digital/cryptocurrency** ✓, CRISPR-Cas9 = gene editing → "B only".
- **Nobel Chemistry 2020:** **Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier**.
| Tool / category | Fact |
|---|---|
| **SDN-1** | Small edit, no template, no foreign DNA |
| **SDN-2** | Uses a template, **no foreign DNA** |
| SDN-3 | Inserts foreign DNA (transgenic) |
| **Base editing** | Converts one DNA base into another **without double-strand breaks** |
| **TnpB** | **ICAR's indigenous, smaller alternative to Cas9** |
| CoE-CIT | Centre of Excellence for CRISPR Innovation and Translation – **Bengaluru** |
# India's first genome-edited rice (released 4 May 2025)
| Variety | Developer | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Pusa DST Rice 1** | ICAR-IARI | Edited **MTU1010** (bred at **Maruteru, AP**); SDN-1 knockout of DST gene; **drought and salinity tolerance**; 10-30% more yield under stress |
| **DRR Dhan 100 (Kamala)** | **ICAR-IIRR, Hyderabad** | From **Samba Mahsuri (BPT 5204)**; earlier maturity, higher yield |
- India was the first country to release genome-edited rice (**2025**).
> Exam angle: Cas9 = molecular scissors (×2), CRISPR = gene editing (×2), news pairs.

=== Gene Therapy and Genetic Medicine
- **Gene therapy:** treating disease by correcting the gene defect – **inserting a normal gene to replace/compensate for a defective one** (also inactivating or editing genes); exam options "treating the diseased genes" and "replacement of defective genes" describe the same idea.
- **Genetic medicines** (UPSC 2026): **correct or compensate for faulty genes**; carriers = **engineered viruses (viral vectors) and lipid nanoparticles**; they do **NOT alter the entire DNA sequence** → "1 and 2 only".
- **Vectors:** adenovirus, retrovirus, lentivirus; liposomes, nanoparticles, electroporation.
- **Somatic** gene therapy (only the patient) is accepted; **germline** (inheritable) is generally prohibited in humans.
- CDSCO gene-therapy rules: **2023**; India's first indigenous **CRISPR-based gene therapy** – for **sickle-cell disease** (2026).
- **Pharmacogenomics:** genetics and drug response; **personalised medicine** built on an individual's genome.
- **Mitochondrial replacement therapy = "three-parent baby"**; concern = germline editing, designer babies.
- **Synthetic biology:** insulin, artemisinin; **semaglutide grown in baker's yeast**.
- **Recombinant vaccines:** Hepatitis B, HPV, Novavax.
# Gene drives and GM animals
- **Gene drive:** >90% of GM mosquito offspring inherit the trait (normal 50%); **suppression** (sterile females) vs **modification** (parasite cannot develop); concern = spreads through wild populations with **no simple recall**.
- **AquAdvantage** = GM **salmon**; escapees may out-compete wild stock.
> Exam angle: gene therapy definition; UPSC 2026 genetic medicine statements.

=== Environmental Impact of Transgenics and Terminator Seeds
# Risks to environment
- **Primary environmental concern = cross-pollination / gene flow:** transgenes move by **pollen** into non-GM crops and wild relatives → contamination of local varieties, **superweeds**, loss of endangered relatives (APPSC G2 Mains 2025) – not "reduced pesticide use" or "increased yield" (benefits).
- Other risks: **build-up of insect resistance**, **harm to non-target organisms** (pollinators, soil microbes), effects on biodiversity, **genetic homogenisation** (uniform varieties over large areas).
- Highest risk at a crop's **centre of diversity** – India for **brinjal**.
- Bt insecticidal proteins were suspected to persist but **degrade in soil**.
| Biosafety risk | Case |
|---|---|
| **Allergenicity** | Soybean with a **Brazil-nut** methionine gene – never released |
| **Antibiotic-resistance markers** | Fear of spreading resistance to pathogens |
| **Horizontal gene transfer** | Genes moving between unrelated organisms |
# GURT – terminator technology
- **GURT = Genetic Use Restriction Technology** (not transformation/prohibition/termination technology).
| Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **V-GURT ("terminator")** | **Seeds sterile in the next generation** → farmers must **buy seed every season** |
| **T-GURT ("traitor")** | Seed stays fertile and transgenic; the trait is **switched on/off by a chemical** |
- So terminator technology promotes the sale of transgenic (fertile T-GURT) seed, GM plants and seed sterile in the next generation → "**all of the above**" (UPPSC 2004 key).
- Backed by developed-country firms; seeks **improved seeds that cannot be used as seed for the next crop**.
- India: the **PPV&FR Act, 2001** bars registration of GURT varieties; **National Gene Fund** for benefit sharing.
> Exam angle: cross-pollination = primary concern; GURT full form; terminator = sterile seed (×2).

=== DNA, Genes and Genome Mapping – Basics
- **Gene = the basic physical and functional unit of heredity** (a hereditary unit), made of **DNA** – not a brain cell, digestive protein or energy source.
- Statements on genes: unit of heredity ✓, made of DNA ✓, **most genes are the same in all people, a few differ slightly** ✓; "five copies of each gene" ✗ (two copies, one from each parent) → **A, B and D**.
- **DNA bases:** adenine, guanine, cytosine, **thymine**; **uracil** replaces thymine in **RNA** (so uracil is NOT a component of DNA).
- **Ribozyme = RNA with catalytic activity** (RNA acting as an enzyme).
- **Virus:** contains a **single kind of nucleic acid – either DNA or RNA**.
- **Adenovirus** = **double-stranded DNA** genome (causes some common colds); **retrovirus** = **single-stranded RNA** (HIV causes AIDS) → only statement 2 correct (UPSC 2021).
- Other basics: carbohydrates move through **phloem** (not xylem); programmed cell death = **apoptosis**; rDNA = chimaeric DNA.
- **Genome mapping** = mapping of genes (relative positions of genes / DNA sequences on chromosomes).
- **Uses of gene maps** (UPSC 2011): **livestock pedigree** ✓ and **disease-resistant animal breeds** ✓; "causes of **all** human diseases" ✗ → **I and III**.
- **HTR7** serotonin-receptor gene variant – linked to **alcoholism** (2012 study).
> Exam angle: basic unit of heredity = gene (×2), DNA bases, ribozyme, virus nucleic acid, gene mapping (×2).
