=== GEAC and the Rules, 1989
# GEAC – Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Status | **Apex statutory body** that regulates GMOs and **clears field trials, environmental release and commercial cultivation of GM crops** |
| Law | **Rules, 1989** (Hazardous Micro-organisms / Genetically Engineered Organisms) framed under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** |
| Ministry | **MoEFCC** (Environment and Forests) – not Agriculture, Commerce or Rural Development |
| Chair | **Special / Additional Secretary, MoEFCC**; **co-chair from the Department of Biotechnology** |
| Name | **Genetic Engineering "Approval" Committee renamed "Appraisal" Committee in July 2010** |
- **GEAC (not NGT, DBT, ICAR, FSSAI or CPCB) gives clearance for testing, trials and cultivation of GM crops**; its permission is needed for any GM crop such as Bt cotton.
- Functions also include approving **large-scale use of hazardous microorganisms and recombinants**; fixing seed MRP is not its job.
- **FSSAI** approves GM **food**; **ICAR** only does research and field trials – neither regulates GM cultivation.
- Penalty for an unapproved GM variant: up to **5 years' jail and Rs 1 lakh fine** under the EPA.
# Competent authorities under the Rules, 1989
| Committee | Level | Role |
|---|---|---|
| RDAC | DBT | Recombinant DNA Advisory Committee – policy |
| **IBSC** | Each institution (**first tier**) | Lab-level biosafety |
| **RCGM** | **DBT** | Monitors ongoing GM research, small trials; **advises GEAC** (does not approve release) |
| **GEAC** | **MoEFCC** | Large-scale trials, environmental release, commercial use |
| **SBCC** | State (Chief Secretary) | **Inspect, investigate, punitive action** |
| **DLC** | District (Collector) | Field-level monitoring |
- Approval chain: **IBSC → RCGM → GEAC → SBCC/DLC**; FSSAI is **not** one of the six competent authorities.
- Field trials = **Biosafety Research Level (BRL-I, BRL-II)** trials; since **2010** a **State NOC** is mandatory for field trials.
| Related body | Law |
|---|---|
| **NGT** | **NGT Act, 2010** |
| CGWA | Section 3(3) of the EPA, 1986 |
| CPCB | Water Act, 1974 |
- **Eco-Sensitive Zones:** activities **regulated, not necessarily prohibited**.
> Exam angle: GEAC clears GM trials/regulates GMOs (×2), under MoEFCC, constituted under EPA 1986.

=== GM Crops in India – Approvals, Bt Brinjal and GM Mustard
| GM crop | Trait | Gene / developer | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bt cotton** | Insect (bollworm) resistance | Cry1Ac (+Cry2Ab) from *B. thuringiensis*; Monsanto-Mahyco | **Approved 2002 – the only GM crop commercially cultivated** |
| **Bt brinjal** | **Insect (pest) resistance – fruit and shoot borer** | **Cry1Ac**; Mahyco + UAS Dharwad + TNAU | GEAC recommended **2009**; **moratorium 2010** (Jairam Ramesh) |
| **GM mustard DMH-11** | Hybrid seed, higher yield; herbicide (glufosinate) tolerance via **bar** gene | **Barnase-barstar** from *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens*; **Delhi University CGMCP, Deepak Pental** | GEAC: 2017 recommendation, **environmental release Oct 2022**; SC **split verdict July 2024** |
- **Bt brinjal is a genetically modified brinjal** – developed for **insect/pest resistance**, not taste, drought, shelf-life, bacterial or fungal resistance.
- **DMH-11 = Dhara Mustard Hybrid-11**, a GM hybrid of **mustard** (cross of Varuna × Early Heera-2), the transgenic crop **approved by GEAC and developed by Delhi University**.
- **GM mustard statements (UPSC 2018):** pest-resistance gene ✗ (that is Bt); **genes allowing cross-pollination and hybridisation ✓**; developed by IARI + PAU ✗ (Delhi University) → **"2 (B) only"**.
- **Monsanto's insect-resistant cotton (field trials 2001)** carries the toxin gene of ***Bacillus thuringiensis***.
# Timeline
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2002** | Bt cotton approved (March) |
| 2006 | PIL against GM crops in the Supreme Court |
| 2010 | Bt brinjal moratorium; **State NOC mandatory** for field trials |
| 2013 | SC-appointed Technical Expert Committee: **10-year moratorium** on field trials |
| 2014 | GEAC clears trials of many crops |
| **2022** | DMH-11 environmental release |
| July 2024 | SC split verdict on DMH-11; Centre directed to frame a **national GM crop policy** |
- **Defiance:** farmers in **Maharashtra and Haryana** openly grew **unapproved Bt (HT) cotton and Bt brinjal** to protest regulatory delays.
- Approval in India takes **7-10 years** vs **3-5 years** globally; overlapping DBT, GEAC, CDSCO, FSSAI, NBA.
> Exam angle: Bt cotton only GM crop (2002), Bt brinjal = pest resistance (×4), DMH-11 mustard (×3), Maharashtra-Haryana defiance.

=== The GM Debate – Biodiversity, Health and GM Food Law
- **Reasons for resistance to Bt brinjal (UPSC 2012):** "gene from a soil **fungus**" ✗ (it is a **bacterium**); "terminator seeds" ✗ (Bt brinjal is not GURT); **health apprehension ✓; biodiversity concern ✓** → **3 and 4 only**.
- **Genetic modification** is the technology flagged for **biodiversity** concerns – not solar panels, wind turbines or rainwater harvesting.
| Risk type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Environment | Gene flow, insect resistance, harm to non-target organisms, weediness, genetic homogenisation |
| Health | Toxicity, **allergenicity**, antibiotic resistance |
| Not a GM risk | **Ozone depletion** |
| Socio-economic | Seed monopoly, **biopiracy**, corporate control |
# GM food – import and labelling
- **No GM food may be manufactured, imported or sold without approval of the competent authority (FSSAI)** – Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006; FSS (GM Foods) Regulations 2022.
- From **1 March 2021**, imported major food crops need a **"non-GM cum GM-free" certificate**; draft labelling for foods with **1% or more** GM ingredient.
- **India does import US apples** – after the **June 2023** WTO settlement India removed the extra duties on US apples, walnuts and almonds (UPSC 2024: Statement I wrong, II right).
# Proposed regulators
- **BRAI Bill, 2013:** a single biotech regulator; **lapsed with the 15th Lok Sabha**.
- **BioE3 Policy, 2024:** single-window clearance, regulatory sandboxes.
> Exam angle: Bt brinjal resistance statements, genetic modification and biodiversity, GM food import law.

=== Biosafety, Cartagena Protocol and Bioethics
- **Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety** (to the **CBD**): transboundary movement of **living modified organisms (LMOs)**; adopted **January 2000 in Montreal**, in force **2003**; named after **Cartagena, Colombia**; India a party.
- Global GM area: **USA** first, then Brazil, Argentina, Canada, **India 5th**.
| Biosafety level | Agents |
|---|---|
| BSL-3 | Serious airborne agents (e.g. **TB**) |
| **BSL-4** | Lethal, no cure (e.g. **Ebola**) |
- **BWC** opened 1972, in force **1975**; India's **WMD Act, 2005** criminalises biological weapons; gap = **no unified national biosecurity framework**.
# Bioethics
- **Nuremberg Code (1947):** ethical principles for human experimentation from the **International Military Tribunal** trials of Nazi war criminals – not Hamburg, Leipzig or Stuttgart codes; basis of **informed consent**.
- **National Guidelines for Stem Cell Research, 2017** – **ICMR and DBT**.
- **He Jiankui** (China, **2018**) – "CRISPR babies"; **"designer babies"** = selecting or modifying desired traits in offspring.
> Exam angle: Cartagena in Colombia; Nuremberg Code.

=== IPR, Biopiracy and Farmers' Rights
| IPR | Protects |
|---|---|
| **Patent** | Inventions – a form of **intellectual property** |
| **Copyright** | Literary, artistic, musical works and **cinematograph films** (**Berne Convention, 1886**) |
| Trademark / GI | Brands / origin-linked goods |
- **Intellectual property** = creations of the human mind with commercial value.
| Provision / case | Point |
|---|---|
| Patents Act **Sec 3(j)** | Plants, animals, seeds, essentially biological processes **not patentable**; microbial processes are |
| **Sec 3(d)** | New form of a known substance needs enhanced efficacy – blocks **evergreening**; **Novartis (2013)**: Glivec (imatinib) rejection upheld under 3(d) |
| **PPV&FR Act, 2001** | Farmers may save, use, sow, exchange, share and sell seed – **not branded seed** of protected varieties; bars GURT varieties |
| PepsiCo FL-2027 (FC5) potato | Sued **Gujarat** farmers (2019), withdrew |
# Biopiracy
- **Biopiracy:** commercial exploitation of native biological resources/traditional knowledge without fair compensation.
| Case | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Turmeric** (wound healing, US patent) | Revoked after **CSIR** challenge |
| **Neem** (anti-fungal) | India won at the **European Patent Office** |
| **Basmati** (**RiceTec**, 1997) | CSIR challenge; key claims disallowed |
| **Hoodia** (appetite suppressant) | Benefit-sharing with the **San** (2003) |
- **TKDL (2001):** by **CSIR and the Ministry of AYUSH**; gives patent offices **prior-art** evidence.
- Objection to GURT seeds: **loss of seed-saving**, dependence on companies.
> Exam angle: Berne = copyright; films = copyright; patent = IP (flashcards ×4).

=== Regulating Genome-edited Crops
- **SDN-1 and SDN-2** (no foreign DNA) were **exempted from the GMO rules in 2022**; they need only **IBSC (IBC) approval**, without the GEAC route; **SDN-3** (transgenic) stays under full GM control.
- **Pusa Rice DST1:** drought + salinity tolerance; **DST gene knockout by SDN-1**; regulated **like a conventional crop**.
- Contrast: Bt cotton (Bollgard II, 2006) – no major new GM cotton trait approved since ("Bt technology fatigue").
