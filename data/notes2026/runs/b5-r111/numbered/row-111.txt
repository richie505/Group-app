## SUB 0 | rDNA Technology – Tools, Vectors and Steps
[0:0] # What genetic engineering does
[0:1] - **Genetic engineering / recombinant DNA (rDNA) technology:** cutting, joining and transferring genes between organisms; **rDNA = DNA formed by combining genetic material from different sources** – also called **chimaeric (chimeric) DNA**.
[0:2] - **Transgenic organism:** an organism whose DNA carries a **foreign gene from another species** (the "transgene"), inserted artificially for a **desired characteristic or product**.
[0:3] - **GMO:** any organism whose genome is altered by genetic engineering – genes moved **in ways that would not occur naturally**.
[0:4] - **Across species:** rDNA moves genes **plant to plant, animal to plant and microbe to higher organism** – all three possible (UPSC 2013).
[0:5] - **Mouse eight times its size** with a human growth-hormone gene = **genetic engineering** (not hybridisation, mutation breeding or hormonal feeding).
[0:6] - **Tried with success:** **disease resistance, growth promotion, animal cloning**; **human cloning is NOT** (banned, no fruitful result) → "1, 2 and 3".
[0:7] # Tools
[0:8] TABLE head: Tool | Role
  [0:8.0] Restriction endonuclease | "Molecular scissors" / chemical knives – cuts DNA at specific recognition (base) sequences, not at random
  [0:8.1] DNA ligase | "Molecular glue" – joins DNA fragments (gene + vector); in replication it joins Okazaki fragments
  [0:8.2] Plasmid | Small, extra-chromosomal, usually circular double-stranded DNA of bacteria; replicates independently; the commonest vector
  [0:8.3] pBR322 | Early, widely used E. coli cloning vector; p = plasmid, BR = Bolivar and Rodriguez
  [0:8.4] Origin of replication (ori) | The specific DNA sequence that initiates replication; alien DNA linked to it can multiply in the host
  [0:8.5] PCR | Amplifies (copies) a specific DNA segment in a test tube – no living cells needed (heat-stable Taq polymerase, 20-40 cycles)
  [0:8.6] RNAi | Gene silencing
[0:9] - **Two bacteria most used:** ***Escherichia coli*** (host for cloning) and ***Agrobacterium tumefaciens*** (plant vector).
[0:10] - ***Agrobacterium tumefaciens*:** soil bacterium that **causes crown gall disease** (dicots) by transferring **T-DNA** of its **Ti plasmid**; the disarmed Ti plasmid makes it "**Nature's genetic engineer**" → useful for agriculture, crown gall agent and gene-transfer tool = **all of the above**.
[0:11]   - Crown gall is NOT caused by *Bacillus thuringiensis* (insect pathogen) or *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (biocontrol).
[0:12] # Steps
[0:13] - **Isolate DNA → cut at specific sites (restriction enzyme) → join to vector with DNA ligase (rDNA) → insert into host (transformation) → select, multiply, express product.**
[0:14] - Recombinant plasmid enters a bacterium by **transformation**; bacteria multiply fast and act as factories.
[0:15] - **Gene cloning:** making many copies of a desired gene or DNA fragment; **genetic library** = a collection of cloned DNA of an organism.
[0:16] TABLE head: Field | Biotech use
  [0:16.0] Agriculture | Transgenic / GM crops
  [0:16.1] Medicine | Drugs, vaccines, diagnostics, gene therapy; human insulin from GE bacteria
  [0:16.2] Industry | Microbial production of useful products
  [0:16.3] Research | Biochips – DNA, RNA and protein chips for diagnostics and drug discovery
[0:17] > Exam angle: molecular scissors = restriction endonuclease (×3); plasmid; E. coli + Agrobacterium; crown gall; ligase joins Okazaki fragments; ori; PCR; chimaeric DNA; human cloning not achieved.

## SUB 1 | Bt Technology and Bt Cotton
[1:0] # Bt
[1:1] - **Bt = *Bacillus thuringiensis***: Gram-positive **soil bacterium** – not Bacillus subtilis, amyloliquefaciens or "thermophilus".
[1:2] - **Cry (delta-endo) toxins:** crystal **protoxin** activated in the larva's **alkaline gut** → pores in midgut → death; trait = **resistance to insects** (bollworms, borers), not to viruses, bacteria or fungi.
[1:3] - **Insect-resistant cotton = gene from a bacterium** (not virus, insect or plant); A: Bt cotton made by inserting the Bt gene, R: the gene is from a bacterium → **both true, R explains A**.
[1:4] - **Bt seed is associated with cotton.**
[1:5] # Bt cotton in India
[1:6] TABLE head: Version | Year | Genes
  [1:6.0] Bollgard I | 2002 (India's first biotech crop technology) | Cry1Ac (single gene)
  [1:6.1] Bollgard II | 2006 | Cry1Ac + Cry2Ab
[1:7] - **Bollgard I/II = technologies for developing GM crop plants** (Monsanto) – not clonal propagation, growth substances or biofertilisers (UPSC 2021).
[1:8] - **Approved March 2002** (for three years): three **MAHYCO** (Monsanto-Mahyco) hybrids against the **American bollworm**; **India's first and only commercial GM crop**; now **~90-95% of the cotton area** (~10.8 m ha).
[1:9] - Benefits: higher yield; **pesticide use cut ~40-50%** because the plant **makes its own Bt toxin**.
[1:10] - Problems: **pink bollworm resistance**; rise of **sucking pests (whitefly, mealybug)**; costly seed; Bt proteins **degrade in soil** (no lasting persistence).
[1:11] - **Maharashtra** = leading Bt cotton State (largest area).
[1:12] # HTBt cotton (illegal)
[1:13] - **HTBt** = Bt + tolerance to the herbicide **glyphosate**; **not approved**; spread driven by labour shortage and cheap glyphosate weeding.
[1:14] - A 2025-26 study: ~**15-18% of AP's cotton area** (27-30% in Telangana); AP **restricted glyphosate** use.
[1:15] > Exam angle: Bt = Bacillus thuringiensis (×6), bacterium not virus, insect resistance, Bollgard (×2), A-R (×2).
[1:16] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 12 p.9-11 (Bt cotton March 2002, MAHYCO, American bollworm, A-R PYQ p.12); Rocket Biology p.3-4 (Bollgard-I 2002); PMF S&T Part 3 p.11-13 (Cry toxin, Bollgard I/II genes, UPSC 2021, ~95% area); PMF Environment Part 7 p.31 (pink bollworm); app notes 5:111 sub 3-4
## SUB 2 | Golden Rice and Other GE Crop Traits
[2:0] # Golden Rice
[2:1] - **Transgenic rice (*Oryza sativa*) with genes for beta-carotene (carotene)** – **pro-vitamin A**, converted to **vitamin A** in the body; against **vitamin A deficiency** (blindness).
[2:2] - **Genes:** phytoene synthase from **daffodil** + crtI from the bacterium *Erwinia uredovora*; developed by **Ingo Potrykus and Peter Beyer**; golden colour = beta-carotene.
[2:3] - Not a wild, Yellow-River or long-stored rice; not higher yield, not all essential amino acids, not vitamin D; not thiamine, ascorbic acid or calciferol.
[2:4] - **Beta-carotene** is the plant pigment that is a **vitamin A precursor** (not xanthophyll, chlorophyll or anthocyanin).
[2:5] - A: Golden rice benefits consumers as much as farmers; R: its yellow colour reflects high beta-carotene → **both true, R explains A** (UPPSC 2000 key).
[2:6] - Status: **not approved** for cultivation in India (IRRI-led; Philippines).
[2:7] - Other transgenic-plant distractors: buckwheat, macaroni (durum) wheat and **triticale (man-made cereal by hybridisation)** are not transgenic.
[2:8] # Traits engineered besides pest resistance (UPSC 2012)
[2:9] - **Drought tolerance, higher nutritive value (biofortification), longer shelf life** (Flavr Savr) → **1, 2 and 4**; growing/photosynthesis in spaceships is not an established GE goal.
[2:10] - **Biofortification** usually through **conventional breeding** (no foreign gene); GE route = golden rice.
[2:11] # GM wheat and Indian marker-assisted rice
[2:12] - **10 May 2004:** **Monsanto** dropped its **Roundup Ready GM wheat**, citing market resistance – while still selling Bt cotton and GM maize.
[2:13] - **Improved Samba Mahsuri:** gene-pyramided (marker-assisted) bacterial-blight-resistant rice by **ICAR-IIRR (then DRR) and CCMB**, Hyderabad.
[2:14] > Exam angle: golden rice = beta-carotene / vitamin A (×6), daffodil gene, transgenic plant; UPSC 2012 traits; Monsanto GM wheat (×2).

## SUB 3 | DNA, Genes and Genome Mapping – Basics
[3:0] - **Gene = the basic physical and functional unit of heredity** (a hereditary unit), made of **DNA** – not a brain cell, digestive protein or energy source.
[3:1] - Statements on genes: unit of heredity ✓, made of DNA ✓, **most genes are the same in all people, a few differ slightly** ✓; "five copies of each gene" ✗ (two copies, one from each parent) → **A, B and D**.
[3:2] - **DNA bases:** adenine, guanine, cytosine, **thymine**; **uracil** replaces thymine in **RNA** (so uracil is NOT a component of DNA).
[3:3] - **Ribozyme = RNA with catalytic activity** (RNA acting as an enzyme).
[3:4] - **Virus:** contains a **single kind of nucleic acid – either DNA or RNA**.
[3:5] - **Adenovirus** = **double-stranded DNA** genome (causes some common colds); **retrovirus** = **single-stranded RNA** (HIV causes AIDS) → only statement 2 correct (UPSC 2021).
[3:6] - Other basics: carbohydrates move through **phloem** (not xylem); programmed cell death = **apoptosis**; rDNA = chimaeric DNA.
[3:7] - **Genome mapping** = mapping of genes (relative positions of genes / DNA sequences on chromosomes).
[3:8] - **Uses of gene maps** (UPSC 2011): **livestock pedigree** ✓ and **disease-resistant animal breeds** ✓; "causes of **all** human diseases" ✗ → **I and III**.
[3:9] - **HTR7** serotonin-receptor gene variant – linked to **alcoholism** (2012 study).
[3:10] > Exam angle: basic unit of heredity = gene (×2), DNA bases, ribozyme, virus nucleic acid, gene mapping (×2).

## SUB 5 | Genome Editing – CRISPR-Cas9 and Genome-edited Rice
[5:0] # CRISPR-Cas9
[5:1] - **CRISPR** = **Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats** – a natural **defence system of bacteria and archaea**.
[5:2] - **Cas9 = the molecular scissors** (nuclease that cuts DNA); **guide RNA** guides Cas9 to the target site.
[5:3] - Used for **gene (genome) editing** – delete, insert or alter genes; **not** a biosensor, pest-resistance gene, herbicide, plant breeding, radiotherapy or immunisation.
[5:4] - **Gene editing** is a key application of biotechnology (APPSC FSO Mains).
[5:5] - Pairs (UPSC 2018): Belle II = particle physics, **blockchain = digital/cryptocurrency** ✓, CRISPR-Cas9 = gene editing → "B only".
[5:6] - **Nobel Chemistry 2020:** **Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier**.
[5:7] TABLE head: Tool / category | Fact
  [5:7.0] SDN-1 | Small edit, no template, no foreign DNA
  [5:7.1] SDN-2 | Uses a template, no foreign DNA
  [5:7.2] SDN-3 | Inserts foreign DNA (transgenic)
  [5:7.3] Base editing | Converts one DNA base into another without double-strand breaks
  [5:7.4] TnpB | ICAR's indigenous, smaller alternative to Cas9
  [5:7.5] CoE-CIT | Centre of Excellence for CRISPR Innovation and Translation – Bengaluru

## SUB 6 | Environmental Impact of Transgenics and Terminator Seeds
[6:0] # Risks to environment
[6:1] - **Primary environmental concern = cross-pollination / gene flow:** transgenes move by **pollen** into non-GM crops and wild relatives → contamination of local varieties, **superweeds**, loss of endangered relatives (APPSC G2 Mains 2025) – not "reduced pesticide use" or "increased yield" (benefits).
[6:2] - Other risks: **build-up of insect resistance**, **harm to non-target organisms** (pollinators, soil microbes), effects on biodiversity, **genetic homogenisation** (uniform varieties over large areas).
[6:3] - Highest risk at a crop's **centre of diversity** – India for **brinjal**.
[6:4] - Bt insecticidal proteins were suspected to persist but **degrade in soil**.
[6:5] TABLE head: Biosafety risk | Case
  [6:5.0] Allergenicity | Soybean with a Brazil-nut methionine gene – never released
  [6:5.1] Antibiotic-resistance markers | Fear of spreading resistance to pathogens
  [6:5.2] Horizontal gene transfer | Genes moving between unrelated organisms
[6:6] # GURT – terminator technology
[6:7] - **GURT = Genetic Use Restriction Technology** (not transformation/prohibition/termination technology).
[6:8] TABLE head: Type | Mechanism
  [6:8.0] V-GURT ("terminator") | Seeds sterile in the next generation → farmers must buy seed every season
  [6:8.1] T-GURT ("traitor") | Seed stays fertile and transgenic; the trait is switched on/off by a chemical
[6:9] - So terminator technology promotes the sale of transgenic (fertile T-GURT) seed, GM plants and seed sterile in the next generation → "**all of the above**" (UPPSC 2004 key).
[6:10] - Backed by developed-country firms; seeks **improved seeds that cannot be used as seed for the next crop**.
[6:11] - India: the **PPV&FR Act, 2001** bars registration of GURT varieties; **National Gene Fund** for benefit sharing.
[6:12] > Exam angle: cross-pollination = primary concern; GURT full form; terminator = sterile seed (×2).

## SUB 7 | Gene Therapy and Genetic Medicine
[7:0] - **Gene therapy:** treating disease by correcting the gene defect – **inserting a normal gene to replace/compensate for a defective one** (also inactivating or editing genes); exam options "treating the diseased genes" and "replacement of defective genes" describe the same idea.
[7:1] - **Genetic medicines** (UPSC 2026): **correct or compensate for faulty genes**; carriers = **engineered viruses (viral vectors) and lipid nanoparticles**; they do **NOT alter the entire DNA sequence** → "1 and 2 only".
[7:2] - **Vectors:** adenovirus, retrovirus, lentivirus; liposomes, nanoparticles, electroporation.
[7:3] - **Somatic** gene therapy (only the patient) is accepted; **germline** (inheritable) is generally prohibited in humans.
[7:4] - CDSCO gene-therapy rules: **2023**; India's first indigenous **CRISPR-based gene therapy** – for **sickle-cell disease** (2026).
[7:5] - **Pharmacogenomics:** genetics and drug response; **personalised medicine** built on an individual's genome.
[7:6] - **Mitochondrial replacement therapy = "three-parent baby"**; concern = germline editing, designer babies.
[7:7] - **Synthetic biology:** insulin, artemisinin; **semaglutide grown in baker's yeast**.
[7:8] - **Recombinant vaccines:** Hepatitis B, HPV, Novavax.
[7:9] # Gene drives and GM animals
[7:10] - **Gene drive:** >90% of GM mosquito offspring inherit the trait (normal 50%); **suppression** (sterile females) vs **modification** (parasite cannot develop); concern = spreads through wild populations with **no simple recall**.
[7:11] - **AquAdvantage** = GM **salmon**; escapees may out-compete wild stock.
[7:12] > Exam angle: gene therapy definition; UPSC 2026 genetic medicine statements.
