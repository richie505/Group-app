=== NOTES 5:111 | Genetic Engineering – rDNA, Gm Crops, Gene Editing and Environmental Impact of Transgenics
## SUB 0 | Basics – Gmo, Transgenic, Applications
[111:0:0] - **GMO**: genome altered by genetic engineering; genes moved in ways that **would not occur naturally**.
[111:0:1] - **Transgenic organism**: carries a **foreign gene from another species** for a desired characteristic or product.
[111:0:2] TABLE head: Field | Uses
  [111:0:2.0] Agriculture | GM crops; drought tolerance, higher nutritive value
  [111:0:2.1] Medicine | Drugs, vaccines, genetic diagnostics, gene therapy
  [111:0:2.2] Industry | Microbial production of useful products
  [111:0:2.3] Research | Genetic library; biochips (DNA, RNA, protein) for drug discovery
[111:0:3] - Achieved by genetic engineering: disease resistance, growth promotion, animal cloning; **NOT human cloning** (banned).
[111:0:4] - Mouse grown **eight times** its size with a human growth hormone gene = genetic engineering.
[111:0:5] - **Human Genome Project**: mapping human genes; **paternity** = **DNA fingerprinting**.
[111:0:6] - **Gene cloning:** making many copies of (amplifying) a desired gene or DNA fragment; whole-animal cloning copies a complete organism.
[111:0:7] - **Genetic library:** a collection of genetic material (cloned DNA fragments of an organism), used in research.
## SUB 1 | rDNA Technology – Steps and Tools
[111:1:0] - 1. Isolate DNA
[111:1:1] - 2. Cut at specific sites by **restriction endonucleases**
[111:1:2] - 3. Amplify fragment (**PCR**)
[111:1:3] - 4. Join to vector with **DNA ligase** (rDNA)
[111:1:4] - 5. Insert into host (**E. coli**)
[111:1:5] - 6. Extract product → downstream processing
[111:1:6] TABLE head: Tool | Fact
  [111:1:6.0] Restriction enzyme | Molecular scissors; cuts at specific recognition sites, not at random
  [111:1:6.1] DNA ligase | Molecular glue / stitcher; joins fragments
  [111:1:6.2] Plasmid | Extranuclear, usually circular double-stranded DNA; replicates independently
  [111:1:6.3] pBR322 | Early E. coli vector; p = plasmid, BR = Bolivar and Rodriguez
  [111:1:6.4] *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* | "Nature's genetic engineer"; disarmed Ti plasmid carries T-DNA
  [111:1:6.5] PCR | Amplifies DNA; for diagnostics, research
  [111:1:6.6] RNAi | Gene silencing
[111:1:7] - Recombinant plasmid enters bacteria by **transformation**; bacteria multiply to give product.
[111:1:8] - **E. coli and Agrobacterium** = two most useful bacteria in genetic engineering.
[111:1:9] - **Stem cells**: somatic and embryonic; **not only from embryos** or mammals; used to screen new drugs.

## SUB 3 | Bt Technology and Bt Cotton
[111:3:0] - Gene from ***Bacillus thuringiensis***: **Gram-positive soil bacterium**; microbial insecticide sprayed since **1920**; used in organic farming.
[111:3:1] - Crystal **delta-endotoxins (Cry)**: protoxin activated in the larva's **alkaline gut** → binds midgut → **pores** → death. Trait = **insect resistance**.
[111:3:2] TABLE head: Version | Year | Genes
  [111:3:2.0] Bollgard I | 2002 | Cry1Ac (single gene)
  [111:3:2.1] Bollgard II | 2006 | Cry1Ac + Cry2Ab
[111:3:3] - Bt cotton approved **March 2002**: three **MAHYCO** hybrids; against **American bollworm**; ~**90%** of cotton area (~10.8 m ha); **first and only** commercial GM crop.
[111:3:4] - Yield **190 → 480 kg/ha** (2002-2023); pesticide use cut **40-50%**.
[111:3:5] - Problems: **pink bollworm resistance**; rise of sucking pests (**whitefly, mealybug**).
[111:3:6] - **Maharashtra** = largest Bt cotton producer (APPSC G2 2017).
[111:3:7] - Bt proteins **degrade in soil** (lasting persistence not proven).

[111:4:3] - Monsanto dropped **Roundup Ready wheat** (**10 May 2004**), citing market resistance.
## SUB 5 | Genome Editing – Crispr
[111:5:0] - **CRISPR** = Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats; a natural **bacterial/archaeal defence**.
[111:5:1] - **Cas9** = molecular scissors; **guide RNA** = navigator.
[111:5:2] - **Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier** (2012) → **Nobel Chemistry 2020**.
[111:5:3] TABLE head: Category | Nature | India (2022)

  [111:5:3.1] SDN-2 | Template, no foreign DNA | Exempt

  [111:5:3.3] Tool | Fact

  [111:5:3.5] Base editing | One base → another, no double-strand break
  [111:5:3.6] CoE-CIT | CRISPR centre, Bengaluru

## SUB 6 | Genetic Medicine
[111:6:0] - **Gene therapy**: alter, replace or correct defective genes; CDSCO rules **2023**.
[111:6:1] - **Genetic medicines**: carriers = **engineered viruses, lipid nanoparticles**.
[111:6:2] - **Personalised medicine**: built on genetic make-up = **CRISPR**; **pharmacogenomics** = genes and drug response.
[111:6:3] - **Mitochondrial replacement therapy**: **"three-parent baby"**; concern = germline editing, designer babies.
[111:6:4] - **Recombinant vaccines**: Hepatitis B, HPV, COVID-19.
[111:6:5] - **Synthetic biology**: insulin, artemisinin; **semaglutide in baker's yeast**.
## SUB 7 | Golden Rice, Biofortification, Transgenic Animals
[111:7:0] - **Golden Rice**: **beta-carotene** (pro-vitamin A) against **Vitamin A deficiency** (APPSC 2025); **phytoene synthase from daffodil** + **crtI from *Erwinia uredovora***; **Ingo Potrykus and Peter Beyer**.
[111:7:1] Nutrition: Biofortification | Conventional breeding | No foreign gene
GM crop | Genetic engineering | Foreign gene
[111:7:2] - ICAR: **34 biofortified** varieties in one year.
[111:7:3] - **Mutation breeding** (non-GMO): **71 Trombay** varieties.
[111:7:4] - **GM mosquitoes / gene drives**: >90% offspring inherit (normal 50%); **suppression** (sterile females) vs **modification** (parasite cannot develop); **no simple recall**.
[111:7:5] - **GM fish**: AquAdvantage salmon; escapees may out-compete wild stock.
## SUB 8 | Global Gm Status and Cartagena
[111:8:0] - Top GM area: **USA**, Brazil, Argentina, Canada, **India (5th)** – top five >90% of world area.
[111:8:1] - **Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety**: adopted **2000** (Montreal), in force **2003**; transboundary movement of **LMOs**; India a party.
## SUB 9 | Environmental and Biosafety Risks
[111:9:0] - **Gene flow** = **primary environmental concern**: transgenes move by **pollen** into non-GM crops and wild relatives → **superweeds**, altered gene pools, risk to endangered relatives.
[111:9:1] - Highest risk at a crop's **centre of origin/diversity**: India = centre of diversity of **brinjal**.
[111:9:2] - **Non-target harm**: pollinators, natural enemies, soil nematodes/microbes, aquatic life.
[111:9:3] - **Genetic homogenisation**: uniform varieties → less agro-biodiversity, more vulnerability.
[111:9:4] - **Genetic modification** = technology flagged for **biodiversity impact** (NOT solar, wind, rainwater harvesting).
[111:9:5] TABLE head: Biosafety risk | Case
  [111:9:5.0] Allergenicity | Soybean with Brazil-nut methionine gene – never released
  [111:9:5.1] Horizontal gene transfer | Genes between unrelated organisms
  [111:9:5.2] Antibiotic-resistance markers | Fear of spreading resistance to pathogens
  [111:9:5.3] Mon 863 | Monsanto pest-resistant maize (corn rootworm); feeding-study debate
[111:9:6] - Claimed benefits: less pesticide; drought/stress tolerance.
[111:9:7] # GURT and Safeguards
[111:9:8] TABLE head: Type | Mechanism | Status
  [111:9:8.0] V-GURT (Terminator) | Sterile seeds | Banned under PPV&FR Act, 2001
  [111:9:8.1] T-GURT (Traitor) | Trait switched by a chemical | Research stage
[111:9:9] - Terminator ends **seed saving** → farmers must **buy seed each season**.
[111:9:10] - **PPV&FR Act** protects landraces; **National Gene Fund** for benefit sharing.

[112:0:0] - **Apex statutory body regulating GMOs** in India (APPSC G2 2023) – **NOT FSSAI, CPCB or ICAR**.
