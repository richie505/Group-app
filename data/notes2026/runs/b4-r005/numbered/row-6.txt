[0:4] # Minerals (asked 7 times)
[0:5] - **Feldspar:** Si and O common to all feldspars; Na, K, Ca, Al in specific varieties; feldspar makes up about **one-half** of the crust (NCERT) – "one-fourth" is **wrong** (APPSC G1 2024 → **Statement I only**).
[0:6] - **Quartz** = SiO₂ (silicon dioxide); hard, resists weathering; Mohs 7; major part of granite and sandstone.
[0:7] TABLE head: Silicate class | Example
  [0:7.0] Inosilicate – single chain | Augite (pyroxene)
  [0:7.1] Inosilicate – double chain | Hornblende (amphibole)
  [0:7.2] Phyllosilicate (sheet) | Muscovite, biotite (mica) (APPSC FRO)
  [0:7.3] Tectosilicate (framework) | Albite, orthoclase (feldspar), quartz
[0:8] - **Bowen's reaction series** = order in which minerals crystallise from magma (APPSC FRO): **olivine first** → pyroxene → amphibole → biotite → K-feldspar → muscovite → **quartz last** (most stable).
[0:9] - **Mohs scale** measures **hardness of minerals** (APPSC G1 2002, repeated): talc 1, gypsum 2, calcite 3, fluorite 4, apatite 5, orthoclase 6, quartz 7, **topaz 8**, corundum 9, diamond 10. (Mercalli/Rossi-Forel = earthquake intensity; Beaufort = wind.)
[0:10] - **Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃):** limestone, **chalk and marble** (NDA 2020); **granite is not** a form of CaCO₃ (APPSC ASO 2017).

## SUB 1 | Igneous Rocks
[1:0] - **Formed by cooling and solidification of magma or lava** (State PSC); called **primary rocks**; **no layers, no fossils**; both **crystalline and non-crystalline** (State PSC: the true statement).
[1:1] TABLE head: Type | Formation | Examples
  [1:1.0] Intrusive (plutonic) | Cools slowly at depth → large grains / crystals | Granite (APPSC G2 2024), gabbro, diorite, pegmatite
  [1:1.1] Extrusive (volcanic) | Lava cools fast at the surface → fine grains | Basalt (APPSC FBO), rhyolite, andesite
[1:2] - **By silica:** rhyolite = felsic extrusive; granite = felsic intrusive; **diorite = intermediate intrusive**; basalt = mafic extrusive.
[1:3] - **Felsic:** silica-rich, light-coloured. **Mafic (basic):** rich in Fe, Mg, Ca; dark, dense.
[1:4] - **Not igneous:** **quartzite** (metamorphic, APSLPRB 2023), **dolomite** (sedimentary carbonate, NDA 2017). Granite and basalt = igneous (NDA 2007).
[1:5] - **Granite has no fossils** – formed from magma (JPSC 2013).
[1:6] # Deccan Trap basalt
[1:7] - The **Deccan Plateau / Deccan Traps** are made mainly of **basalt** – igneous lava flows (asked 4 times by APPSC).
[1:8] - Fissure eruptions at the end of the Cretaceous, **~65–66 million years ago** (APPSC Tech. Asst: nearest 65), as India passed over the Réunion hotspot.
[1:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.6 (p.20); NextGen Rocket General Geography p.32; NextGen AP Geography 02 p.4 (Deccan Trap: Cretaceous fissure lava, basalt); app notes 4:6 sub 2, 9
## SUB 2 | Sedimentary Rocks, Fossils and Geological Time
[2:0] - Formed at the Earth's surface by the **hydrological system**, from **weathering of pre-existing rocks**; sediments are transported, deposited, compacted and **lithified** in **layers** (strata) → "stratified rocks"; they **contain fossils** – all four UPSC 2001 statements are correct.
[2:1] - Cover about **75% of the Earth's surface**; hold most fossils and **coal, petroleum, natural gas**.
[2:2] TABLE head: Mode | Examples
  [2:2.0] Mechanically (clastic) | Sandstone, conglomerate, shale, loess
  [2:2.1] Chemically | Rock salt, gypsum, halite, chert, limestone
  [2:2.2] Organically | Coal (Uttarakhand 2006), chalk, coral limestone
[2:3] - **Sandstone** forms **under water** (MPPSC 1993); the **Red Fort and Agra Fort** are built of red **sandstone** – sedimentary.
[2:4] - **Himalayan rocks** are mainly **sedimentary** (folded Tethys sediments) (AP surveyor 2017; Sachivalayam 2020).
[2:5] - **Limestone** occurs white as well as black; igneous rocks have no layers; fossils are **not** found in igneous rocks.
[2:6] # Porosity vs permeability (UPSC 2025)
[2:7] TABLE head: Term | Rocks
  [2:7.0] Porous and permeable | Chalk (very permeable, aquifers), sandstone
  [2:7.1] Porous but impermeable | Clay – very fine pores, poorly connected
  [2:7.2] Neither | Granite
[2:8] - UPSC 2025: Statement II (chalk porous) correct and explains I; Statement III ("clay not at all porous") **wrong** → "Only one of II and III is correct and that explains I".
[2:9] # Fossils and time
[2:10] - **Fossils** = preserved remains, impressions or traces of ancient organisms (SSC 2018).
[2:11] - **Dinosaurs: Mesozoic era** (Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous); died out ~66 million years ago.
[2:12] - **Age of the Earth:** **uranium (radiometric) dating**; carbon dating only for young organic remains.
[2:13] - Giovanni Arduino (1760) made the first geological time scale.

## SUB 3 | Metamorphic Rocks
[3:0] - Igneous **or** sedimentary rocks recrystallise under **heat and pressure** ("change of form") – they come from **both** (JPSC 2011).
[3:1] TABLE head: Parent rock | Metamorphic rock
  [3:1.0] Limestone (and chalk, dolomite) | Marble (UPPCS 1991; APPSC Panchayat Secretary)
  [3:1.1] Shale / clay | Slate (then schist)
  [3:1.2] Sandstone | Quartzite (UPSC 2001, asked twice) – not limestone, obsidian or shale
  [3:1.3] Granite | Gneiss
  [3:1.4] Basalt | Amphibolite, schist
  [3:1.5] Coal | Graphite (via anthracite)
[3:2] - Typical metamorphic minerals: **garnet** (UP 2010), kyanite, sillimanite, staurolite; **halite is not**.
[3:3] - Odd one out: **gneiss** (metamorphic) among sandstone, limestone, shale (sedimentary); **granite** (igneous) among marble, quartzite, slate (metamorphic); limestone among marble, slate, quartzite.
[3:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.6 (p.20); app notes 4:6 sub 5
## SUB 4 | Petrology and the Rock Cycle
[4:0] - **Petrology** = science (study) of rocks, a branch of geology (APPSC G2 2019). Rocks: igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic.
[4:1] - **Rock cycle:** magma → (cooling) igneous → (weathering, erosion, deposition, **lithification**) sedimentary → (heat, pressure) metamorphic → (melting) magma; **any rock type can become another**; exposed metamorphic rock weathers into sediment.
[4:2] - Rainwater with dissolved **CO₂** forms **carbonic acid**, which weathers rocks.
[4:3] - **Enhanced rock weathering:** crushed **basalt** spread on farmland locks CO₂ as bicarbonate.
[4:4] - **Lithology** = physical character of a rock unit – texture, grain size, composition.
[4:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.1 (p.15); app notes 4:6 sub 1, 7-8
