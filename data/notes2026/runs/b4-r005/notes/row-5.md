=== Layers of the Earth and Their Composition
# Crust, mantle, core (asked 11 times)
| Layer | Depth | State | Density (g/cm³) | Made of |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Crust** – outermost layer | Oceanic ~5 km, continental ~30 km (~70 km under mountains) | Solid | 2.8–3.0 | Continental: granite (Si, Al); oceanic: **basalt** (Fe, Mg) |
| **Mantle** – largest layer | Moho → **2,900 km** | Solid, flows plastically (convection) | ~4.6 | **Silicate minerals** of iron and magnesium (upper mantle mostly silicates) |
| **Outer core** | 2,900–5,150 km (~2,260 km thick) | **Liquid** iron–nickel | ~10–12 | Fe + Ni |
| **Inner core** | 5,150–6,371 km (radius ~1,220 km) | **Solid** – immense pressure; P-waves at 11.23 km/s | 13.3–13.6 | Mostly **iron** |
- **Mantle = 83% of volume, 68% of mass** → the **largest** layer; **core = 16% of volume, 32% of mass**.
- **Core is mainly iron** (with nickel) – not aluminium, chromium or silicon (UPSC 2009, asked twice). Its **lighter elements: silicon, sulphur, oxygen** – the APPSC G2 answer is **silicon**; not aluminium, magnesium, calcium or cobalt.
- **Mantle = "mixed metals and silicates"** (iron–magnesium silicates) (SSC 2013); Sial is only silica + aluminium, Nife only metal.
- **Lithosphere = crust + uppermost solid mantle** (~100 km) (NDA 2019).
- Continental crust: thicker, older, granitic, lighter; oceanic crust: thinner, younger, **basaltic**, denser.
- **Compositional layering:** crust, mantle, core. **Mechanical:** lithosphere, asthenosphere, mesosphere, outer core, inner core.
# Suess's chemical layers
| Suess term | Elements | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **SIAL** | **Si**lica + **Al**uminium | Granite; forms the continents; density ~2.9 |
| **SIMA** | **Si**lica + **Ma**gnesium | Basalt; source of magma and lava |
| **NIFE** | **Ni**ckel + **Fe**rrum (iron) | Core; very dense (~11); explains Earth's magnetism (SSC 2018; State PSC "Nickel and ferrum") |
- **Eduard Suess** coined SIAL, SIMA and NIFE.
- **Whole Earth by mass:** **iron 32.1%** > oxygen 30.1% > **silicon 15.1%** > magnesium 13.9% > sulphur 2.9% > nickel 1.8% (RRB 2005: after iron and oxygen comes **silicon**).
> Trap: crust question → oxygen/aluminium; whole-Earth question → iron.

=== Seismic Waves, Shadow Zone and the Seismograph
# Seismic waves (asked 7 times)
| Basis | P (primary) | S (secondary) | Surface (L) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type | **Longitudinal**, push–pull, compressional; vibration parallel | Transverse, shear; vibration perpendicular | Along the surface (Love, Rayleigh) |
| Speed | **Fastest**, arrive first | Slower | Slowest |
| Medium | Solid, liquid, gas | **Solids only** | – |
| Damage | Lower | – | **Highest** |
- An earthquake generates **P, S and L (surface) waves** – "P.S.L" (CGPSC 2013). P and S are **body waves**.
- **P-waves are sound (acoustic) waves:** compressional like sound, and they become audible sound when they reach the air – so of P, S and L, **P-waves** are the sound waves.
- Wave speed changes with density → **reflection and refraction** at boundaries reveal the layers. The **S-wave** behaviour (vanishing in the outer core) gave the key evidence of a **liquid outer core** – the CDS 2012 answer "secondary waves".
- **Shadow zone:** P-waves strongly refracted at the mantle–core boundary → no direct P **~105°–145°** (Rocket: 103°–142°); **S-waves absent everywhere beyond ~105°**. The belt **105°–145°** gets **neither P nor S** (APSLPRB 2023).
- Each earthquake has its **own** shadow zone, set by its epicentre (CGPSC 2020).
# Recording
- **Seismograph** records seismic waves (FBO). NCERT Class 8: the seismograph is a **vibrating rod** or pendulum that starts vibrating with the tremors.

=== Earth's Magnetism, Heat and Pressure
# Magnetic field (asked 4 times)
- **Cause:** electric **currents in the liquid outer core** – convection of molten iron = **geodynamo** (UPPCS 2011) – not a giant magnet at the centre. It shields life from **solar wind and cosmic radiation**; flow changes are linked to **reversals**.
- **Magnetic axis ~11.5°** to the geographic axis (not 23½°).
- **Magnetic equator:** field lines **horizontal**, dip = 0 (NDA 2017); it passes near **Thumba** (Kerala) – hence TERLS. At the magnetic poles the field is vertical.
- **North magnetic pole:** first located (1831) on the **Boothia Peninsula, northern Canada** – UPSC 2005 key; it has since drifted into the Arctic Ocean towards Siberia (~86°N as of 2025, World Magnetic Model 2025).
- **Direction of the field** at a place = **declination** (angle from true north) **and inclination/dip** (angle from horizontal) (CDS 2018).
- ESA **Swarm** mission: outer-core flow under the Pacific switched from westward to eastward.
# Heat, pressure, density
- Temperature, pressure and density **all rise with depth**; ~**1°C per 32 m** in the upper crust. Earth's mean density **5.5 g/cm³** (densest planet).
- Geothermal sites: **Puga Valley (Ladakh)**, Manikaran (Himachal), Tattapani (Chhattisgarh), Cambay (Gujarat), **Bakreshwar (West Bengal)**.
- Diamonds rise from the mantle via **kimberlite pipes**; **Madhya Pradesh** ~90% of India's reserves.

=== Discontinuities, Lithosphere and Asthenosphere
| Discontinuity | Separates | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Conrad | Upper and lower crust (SIAL–SIMA) | 1st from the surface |
| **Mohorovicic (Moho)** | **Crust and mantle** (APPSC G1) | P 6.9 → **8.1 km/s**; **1909**, **Andrija Mohorovicic** (Croatian) |
| Repetti | Upper and lower mantle | ~700–1,000 km |
| **Gutenberg** (Weichert–Gutenberg) | Mantle and outer core | ~2,900 km; S-waves vanish |
| **Lehmann** | Outer and inner core | ~5,150 km |
- 410 km and 660 km: mantle mineral **phase changes**. IUGG mantle zones: Moho–200, 200–700, 700–2,900 km.
| Basis | Lithosphere | Asthenosphere |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Rigid; crust + uppermost mantle** (~100 km) | Weak, plastic, partly molten (~100–200 km), in the **upper mantle** |
| Role | Broken into plates | Plates glide over it; **main source of magma** |
- Sequence: lithosphere → asthenosphere → mesosphere → **barysphere** (core).
- Indian plate under Tibet: dense lower layer **delaminating** into the mantle at ~**100 km** (94 seismic stations).
