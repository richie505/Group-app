=== Composition of the Crust and Minerals
# Elements of the crust (asked 8 times)
| Rank | Element | % by weight of crust |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Oxygen** | **46.6** – most abundant element (in oxides and silicates) |
| 2 | **Silicon** | 27.7 – second; not a metal; bulk of rocks and minerals as silica/silicates |
| 3 | **Aluminium** | 8.1 – **most abundant metal** |
| 4 | **Iron** | 5.0 – **second-most abundant metal** |
| 5–8 | Calcium, sodium, potassium, magnesium | 3.6, 2.8, 2.6, 2.1 |
- Highest and lowest among O, Si, Ca, Na, Mg: **oxygen and magnesium** (CDS 2015).
- "Element present in large amounts in rocks and minerals" = **silicon** (APPSC G1 2012).
# Minerals (asked 7 times)
- **Feldspar:** Si and O common to all feldspars; Na, K, Ca, Al in specific varieties; feldspar makes up about **one-half** of the crust (NCERT) – "one-fourth" is **wrong** (APPSC G1 2024 → **Statement I only**).
- **Quartz** = SiO₂ (silicon dioxide); hard, resists weathering; Mohs 7; major part of granite and sandstone.
| Silicate class | Example |
|---|---|
| Inosilicate – single chain | Augite (pyroxene) |
| Inosilicate – double chain | Hornblende (amphibole) |
| **Phyllosilicate (sheet)** | **Muscovite**, biotite (mica) (APPSC FRO) |
| Tectosilicate (framework) | Albite, orthoclase (feldspar), quartz |
- **Bowen's reaction series** = order in which minerals crystallise from magma (APPSC FRO): **olivine first** → pyroxene → amphibole → biotite → K-feldspar → muscovite → **quartz last** (most stable).
- **Mohs scale** measures **hardness of minerals** (APPSC G1 2002, repeated): talc 1, gypsum 2, calcite 3, fluorite 4, apatite 5, orthoclase 6, quartz 7, **topaz 8**, corundum 9, diamond 10. (Mercalli/Rossi-Forel = earthquake intensity; Beaufort = wind.)
- **Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃):** limestone, **chalk and marble** (NDA 2020); **granite is not** a form of CaCO₃ (APPSC ASO 2017).

=== Igneous Rocks
- **Formed by cooling and solidification of magma or lava** (State PSC); called **primary rocks**; **no layers, no fossils**; both **crystalline and non-crystalline** (State PSC: the true statement).
| Type | Formation | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Intrusive (plutonic)** | Cools **slowly at depth** → **large grains / crystals** | **Granite** (APPSC G2 2024), gabbro, diorite, pegmatite |
| **Extrusive (volcanic)** | Lava cools fast at the surface → fine grains | **Basalt** (APPSC FBO), rhyolite, andesite |
- **By silica:** rhyolite = felsic extrusive; granite = felsic intrusive; **diorite = intermediate intrusive**; basalt = mafic extrusive.
- **Felsic:** silica-rich, light-coloured. **Mafic (basic):** rich in Fe, Mg, Ca; dark, dense.
- **Not igneous:** **quartzite** (metamorphic, APSLPRB 2023), **dolomite** (sedimentary carbonate, NDA 2017). Granite and basalt = igneous (NDA 2007).
- **Granite has no fossils** – formed from magma (JPSC 2013).
# Deccan Trap basalt
- The **Deccan Plateau / Deccan Traps** are made mainly of **basalt** – igneous lava flows (asked 4 times by APPSC).
- Fissure eruptions at the end of the Cretaceous, **~65–66 million years ago** (APPSC Tech. Asst: nearest 65), as India passed over the Réunion hotspot.

=== Sedimentary Rocks, Fossils and Geological Time
- Formed at the Earth's surface by the **hydrological system**, from **weathering of pre-existing rocks**; sediments are transported, deposited, compacted and **lithified** in **layers** (strata) → "stratified rocks"; they **contain fossils** – all four UPSC 2001 statements are correct.
- Cover about **75% of the Earth's surface**; hold most fossils and **coal, petroleum, natural gas**.
| Mode | Examples |
|---|---|
| Mechanically (clastic) | Sandstone, conglomerate, shale, loess |
| Chemically | Rock salt, gypsum, halite, chert, limestone |
| Organically | **Coal** (Uttarakhand 2006), chalk, coral limestone |
- **Sandstone** forms **under water** (MPPSC 1993); the **Red Fort and Agra Fort** are built of red **sandstone** – sedimentary.
- **Himalayan rocks** are mainly **sedimentary** (folded Tethys sediments) (AP surveyor 2017; Sachivalayam 2020).
- **Limestone** occurs white as well as black; igneous rocks have no layers; fossils are **not** found in igneous rocks.
# Porosity vs permeability (UPSC 2025)
| Term | Rocks |
|---|---|
| Porous and permeable | **Chalk** (very permeable, aquifers), sandstone |
| **Porous but impermeable** | **Clay** – very fine pores, poorly connected |
| Neither | Granite |
- UPSC 2025: Statement II (chalk porous) correct and explains I; Statement III ("clay not at all porous") **wrong** → "Only one of II and III is correct and that explains I".
# Fossils and time
- **Fossils** = preserved remains, impressions or traces of ancient organisms (SSC 2018).
- **Dinosaurs: Mesozoic era** (Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous); died out ~66 million years ago.
- **Age of the Earth:** **uranium (radiometric) dating**; carbon dating only for young organic remains.
- Giovanni Arduino (1760) made the first geological time scale.

=== Metamorphic Rocks
- Igneous **or** sedimentary rocks recrystallise under **heat and pressure** ("change of form") – they come from **both** (JPSC 2011).
| Parent rock | Metamorphic rock |
|---|---|
| **Limestone** (and chalk, dolomite) | **Marble** (UPPCS 1991; APPSC Panchayat Secretary) |
| Shale / clay | **Slate** (then schist) |
| **Sandstone** | **Quartzite** (UPSC 2001, asked twice) – not limestone, obsidian or shale |
| Granite | **Gneiss** |
| Basalt | Amphibolite, schist |
| Coal | Graphite (via anthracite) |
- Typical metamorphic minerals: **garnet** (UP 2010), kyanite, sillimanite, staurolite; **halite is not**.
- Odd one out: **gneiss** (metamorphic) among sandstone, limestone, shale (sedimentary); **granite** (igneous) among marble, quartzite, slate (metamorphic); limestone among marble, slate, quartzite.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.6 (p.20); app notes 4:6 sub 5

=== Petrology and the Rock Cycle
- **Petrology** = science (study) of rocks, a branch of geology (APPSC G2 2019). Rocks: igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic.
- **Rock cycle:** magma → (cooling) igneous → (weathering, erosion, deposition, **lithification**) sedimentary → (heat, pressure) metamorphic → (melting) magma; **any rock type can become another**; exposed metamorphic rock weathers into sediment.
- Rainwater with dissolved **CO₂** forms **carbonic acid**, which weathers rocks.
- **Enhanced rock weathering:** crushed **basalt** spread on farmland locks CO₂ as bicarbonate.
- **Lithology** = physical character of a rock unit – texture, grain size, composition.
