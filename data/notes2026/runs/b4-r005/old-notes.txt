=== NOTES 4:5 | Interior of the Earth – Crust, Mantle, Core; Discontinuities and Seismic Evidence
## SUB 0 | Sources of Information
[5:0:0] TABLE head: Direct | Indirect
  [5:0:0.0] Surface rock from mining (APPSC-G1 2017) | Seismic waves (most important)
  [5:0:0.1] Magma and lava | Gravity anomalies, magnetic surveys
  [5:0:0.2] Kola (USSR, 1970s): ~12 km – deepest hole | Meteorites
  [5:0:0.3] Mponeng, TauTona (South Africa): 3.9 km – deepest mines | Rise of temperature and pressure with depth
## SUB 1 | Seismic Waves and Shadow Zone
[5:1:0] TABLE head: Basis | P (primary) | S (secondary) | Surface (L)
  [5:1:0.0] Type | Longitudinal, push-pull; vibration parallel | Transverse, shear; vibration perpendicular | Along surface
  [5:1:0.1] Speed | Fastest, arrive first | Slower | Slowest
  [5:1:0.2] Medium | Solid, liquid, gas | Solids only | –
  [5:1:0.3] Damage | Lower | – | Highest
[5:1:1] - P and S are **body waves**. Recorded by the **seismograph**. Speed changes with density → reflection and refraction reveal boundaries.
[5:1:2] - **P-wave shadow zone: ~105°–145°** from the epicentre; P-waves strongly refracted at the mantle–core boundary.
[5:1:3] - **S-waves absent beyond 105°** → bigger shadow; S-waves vanish in the outer core → **outer core is liquid**.
[5:1:4] - Each earthquake has its **own** shadow zone, set by its epicentre (CGPSC 2020).
[5:1:5] - **Surface waves:** also called **L-waves** (long waves); they move along the surface, are the slowest and cause the most damage.
## SUB 2 | Layers
[5:2:0] TABLE head: Layer | Depth | State | Density (g/cm³)
  [5:2:0.0] Crust | Oceanic ~5 km, continental ~30 km (~70 under mountains) | Solid | 2.8–3.0
  [5:2:0.1] Mantle | Moho → 2,900 km | Solid, plastic flow (convection) | 4.6
  [5:2:0.2] Outer core | 2,900–5,150 km (~2,260 km thick) | Liquid iron-nickel | ~10–12
  [5:2:0.3] Inner core | 5,150–6,371 km (radius ~1,220 km) | Solid (immense pressure); P at 11.23 km/s | 13.3–13.6
[5:2:1] - **Mantle 83% volume, 68% mass**; **core 16% volume, 32% mass**.
[5:2:2] - Continental crust: thicker, older, granitic (Si, Al). Oceanic: thinner, younger, **basaltic** (Fe, Mg).
[5:2:3] - Compositional: crust, mantle, core. Mechanical: lithosphere, asthenosphere, mesosphere, outer core, inner core.
[5:2:4] - The Moon's core is **solid**.
## SUB 3 | Discontinuities
[5:3:0] TABLE head: Discontinuity | Separates | Note
  [5:3:0.0] Conrad | Upper and lower crust (SIAL–SIMA) | –
  [5:3:0.1] Mohorovicic (Moho) | Crust and mantle (APPSC-G1 2019) | P 6.9 → 8.1 km/s; 1909, Croatian Andrija Mohorovicic
  [5:3:0.2] Repetti | Upper and lower mantle | ~700–1,000 km
  [5:3:0.3] Gutenberg | Mantle and outer core | ~2,900 km; S-waves vanish
  [5:3:0.4] Lehmann | Outer and inner core | ~5,150 km
[5:3:1] - 410 km and 660 km: mantle mineral phase changes. IUGG mantle zones: Moho–200, 200–700, 700–2,900 km.
## SUB 4 | Lithosphere and Asthenosphere
[5:4:0] TABLE head: Basis | Lithosphere | Asthenosphere
  [5:4:0.0] Nature | Rigid; crust + uppermost mantle (~100 km) | Weak, plastic, partly molten (~100–200 km)
  [5:4:0.1] Role | Broken into plates | Main source of magma; plates glide over it
[5:4:1] - Sequence: lithosphere → asthenosphere → mesosphere → **barysphere** (core).
[5:4:2] - Indian plate under Tibet **delaminating** at ~100 km (94 stations, helium isotopes). Stanford: first global map of continental mantle earthquakes (80+ km below Moho).
[5:4:3] - **Asthenosphere:** lies in the **upper mantle**, about 100–200 km deep, just below the lithosphere.
## SUB 5 | Composition – Sial, Sima, Nife
[5:5:0] TABLE head: Suess term | Elements | Feature
  [5:5:0.0] SIAL | Silica + Aluminium | Granite; continents
  [5:5:0.1] SIMA | Silica + Magnesium | Basalt; magma source
  [5:5:0.2] NIFE | Nickel + Ferrum (iron) (BPSC 2022) | Core; magnetism; density ~11
  [5:5:0.3] Crust element | % weight
  [5:5:0.4] Oxygen | 46.60 (most abundant)
  [5:5:0.5] Silicon | 27.72 (not a metal)
  [5:5:0.6] Aluminium | 8.13 (most abundant metal)
  [5:5:0.7] Iron | 5.00
  [5:5:0.8] Calcium, Na, K, Mg | 3.63, 2.83, 2.59, 2.09
[5:5:1] - Core's lighter elements: **silicon** (APPSC G2 2017), sulphur, oxygen – not aluminium, sodium, potassium.
[5:5:2] - **Eduard Suess:** gave the terms SIAL, SIMA and NIFE, naming the Earth's layers after their main elements.
## SUB 6 | Magnetism, Heat and Pressure
[5:6:0] - **Geodynamo**: convection of molten iron in the outer core makes the magnetic field, shielding life from solar wind and cosmic rays; flow changes linked to reversals (some up to ~70,000 years). ESA **Swarm**: outer-core flow under the Pacific switched westward to eastward, weakening ~2020.
[5:6:1] - Magnetic axis ~**11.5°** to geographic axis.
[5:6:2] - Temperature, pressure, density **all rise with depth**; ~**1°C per 32 m** in the upper crust. Earth's density **5.5 g/cm³** (densest planet).
[5:6:3] - Geothermal: **Puga Valley** (Ladakh, highest), Manikaran (HP), Tattapani (Chhattisgarh), **Cambay** (Gujarat), **Bakreshwar** (WB).
[5:6:4] - Diamonds rise via **kimberlite pipes**; **MP** has ~90% of reserves.

=== NOTES 4:6 | Rocks and Minerals – Igneous, Sedimentary, Metamorphic; the Rock Cycle
## SUB 0 | Composition of the Earth's Crust
[6:0:0] # Crustal Elements by Rank
[6:0:1] TABLE head: Rank | Element | % by weight of crust
  [6:0:1.0] 1 | Oxygen | 46.6 (mostly in oxides and silicates – SiO₂, Al₂O₃)
  [6:0:1.1] 2 | Silicon | 27.7
  [6:0:1.2] 3 | Aluminium | 8.1 – most abundant metal
  [6:0:1.3] 4 | Iron | 5.0 – second-most abundant metal
[6:0:2] - Whole Earth by mass: **iron 32.1%** > oxygen 30.1% > silicon 15.1% > magnesium 13.9%.
[6:0:3] - Suess: **SIAL** – granitic, continental; **SIMA** – basaltic; **NIFE** – nickel-iron core.
[6:0:4] Most abundant metal in the crust = aluminium (not iron); whole-Earth question = iron.
## SUB 1 | Rock Types and Petrology
[6:1:0] - **Petrology** = study of rocks (branch of geology). Three types: igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic.
[6:1:1] TABLE head: Rock | Examples
  [6:1:1.0] Igneous ("primary", APPSC 2019) | Granite, basalt, gabbro, dolerite
  [6:1:1.1] Sedimentary | Sandstone, limestone, shale, conglomerate, coal, chalk
  [6:1:1.2] Metamorphic | Marble, slate, quartzite, gneiss, schist
[6:1:2] - **Granite is igneous**, not metamorphic; **coal is sedimentary** (organic); **gneiss is metamorphic** (usually from granite).
[6:1:3] - **Weathering** breaks rocks into sediments, clays, soils. Rain wears rocks because rainwater holds dissolved **CO₂ and oxygen** (CO₂ → carbonic acid).
[6:1:4] - **Terra rossa** (red clay soil) forms on **limestone** (and dolomite).
## SUB 2 | Igneous Rocks
[6:2:0] - Rock from cooling of lava = igneous (intrusive and extrusive types); igneous rocks are both **crystalline and non-crystalline**.
[6:2:1] TABLE head: Type | Formation | Examples
  [6:2:1.0] Intrusive (plutonic) | Slow cooling at depth → coarse grains | Granite (APPSC G2 2024), gabbro
  [6:2:1.1] Extrusive (volcanic) | Lava cools fast at surface → fine grains | Basalt, rhyolite, andesite
  [6:2:1.2] Rock | Type
  [6:2:1.3] Rhyolite | Felsic extrusive
  [6:2:1.4] Granite | Felsic intrusive
  [6:2:1.5] Diorite | Intermediate intrusive
  [6:2:1.6] Basalt | Mafic extrusive
[6:2:2] - **Felsic**: silica-rich, light-coloured, fairly light.
[6:2:3] - **Mafic (basic)**: rich in **Fe, Mg, Ca**; dark, dense; olivine, pyroxene.
[6:2:4] APPSC 2024 A-R: "Felsic rocks contain high iron, magnesium and calcium" is FALSE – that describes mafic rocks.
## SUB 3 | Sedimentary Rocks
[6:3:0] - Formed at Earth's surface by the **hydrological system**; need weathering of pre-existing rocks. Sediments are transported, deposited, compacted and **lithified** in layers (strata) → **"stratified rocks"**.
[6:3:1] - Cover **75% of Earth's surface**; hold most **fossils** and mineral oil.
[6:3:2] TABLE head: Mode | Examples
  [6:3:2.0] Mechanically (clastic) | Sandstone, conglomerate, shale, loess
  [6:3:2.1] Chemically | Rock salt, gypsum, halite, chert, limestone
  [6:3:2.2] Organically | Coal, chalk, coral limestone, geyserite
[6:3:3] - Sandstone is flaky because it forms **underwater**.
[6:3:4] # Porosity vs Permeability
[6:3:5] TABLE head: Term | Rocks
  [6:3:5.0] Porous and permeable | Chalk (very permeable, aquifers), sandstone
  [6:3:5.1] Porous but impermeable | Clay (very fine particles; least permeable)
  [6:3:5.2] Neither | Granite
## SUB 4 | Fossils and Fossil Fuels by Rock Type
[6:4:0] - Sedimentary rocks = main store of fossils; hold **coal, petroleum, natural gas**. Conglomerate, sandstone, shale have fossils.
[6:4:1] - **Granite unlikely to have fossils** (plutonic). Archaean rocks formed **before life** → fossil-free.
[6:4:2] - **Dravidian** (Palaeozoic): many marine fossils.
## SUB 5 | Metamorphic Rocks
[6:5:0] - Igneous or sedimentary rocks recrystallise under **heat and pressure** (full melting not needed – that gives magma). Come from **both** igneous and sedimentary rocks ("change of form").
[6:5:1] TABLE head: Parent rock | Metamorphic rock
  [6:5:1.0] Limestone | Marble
  [6:5:1.1] Shale / clay | Slate (then schist)
  [6:5:1.2] Sandstone | Quartzite (quartz-rich, non-foliated)
  [6:5:1.3] Granite | Gneiss
  [6:5:1.4] Basalt | Amphibolite, schist
  [6:5:1.5] Coal | Graphite (via anthracite)
[6:5:2] - Typical metamorphic minerals: **garnet**, kyanite, sillimanite, staurolite; **halite is not** (chemical sedimentary).
[6:5:3] Odd one out: marble, slate, quartzite are metamorphic; limestone is sedimentary (parent of marble).
## SUB 6 | Minerals – Classes, Crystallisation, Hardness
[6:6:0] - **Feldspar**: about **one-half** of the crust ("one-fourth" is WRONG – APPSC G1 2024: statement I only).
[6:6:1] - **Quartz** = SiO₂; hard, weathering-resistant; major part of granite and sandstone.
[6:6:2] TABLE head: Silicate class | Example
  [6:6:2.0] Inosilicate – single chain | Augite (pyroxene)
  [6:6:2.1] Inosilicate – double chain | Hornblende (amphibole)
  [6:6:2.2] Phyllosilicate (sheet) | Muscovite, biotite (mica) – APPSC 2024
  [6:6:2.3] Tectosilicate (framework) | Albite, orthoclase (feldspar), quartz
[6:6:3] - **Bowen's reaction series** = order of crystallisation from cooling magma (APPSC 2024): olivine (first) → pyroxene → amphibole → biotite → K-feldspar → muscovite → **quartz last** (most stable). Mafic minerals form first at highest temperature.
[6:6:4] - **Mohs scale**: talc 1, gypsum 2, calcite 3, fluorite 4, **apatite 5**, **orthoclase 6**, **quartz 7**, **topaz 8**, corundum 9, diamond 10.
## SUB 7 | The Rock Cycle
[6:7:0] - Magma → (cooling) igneous → (weathering, erosion, deposition, lithification) sedimentary → (heat, pressure) metamorphic → (melting) magma.
[6:7:1] - **Any rock type can become another**; exposed metamorphic rock can weather into sediment.
[6:7:2] - **Enhanced rock weathering**: crushed basalt spread on farmland locks CO₂ as **bicarbonate**.

=== NOTES 4:7 | Earth Movements – Continental Drift, Sea-floor Spreading, Plate Tectonics and Plate Boundaries
## SUB 0 | Theories in Order
[7:0:0] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent / year | Mechanism
  [7:0:0.0] Continental drift | Alfred Wegener (German), 1912 (APPSC 2018) | Pole-fleeing force (Earth's rotation) + tidal force (Sun and Moon)
  [7:0:0.1] Convection current | Arthur Holmes, 1930s | Radioactive decay heat in mantle → currents; rising limbs split crust (ridges), sinking limbs pull plates (trenches)
  [7:0:0.2] Sea-floor spreading | Harry Hess, early 1960s (1961) | Magma rises at mid-ocean ridges → new crust spreads → destroyed at trenches
  [7:0:0.3] Palaeomagnetic stripes | Vine and Matthews, 1963 | Symmetrical normal/reversed bands either side of ridge
  [7:0:0.4] Plate tectonics | 1967, McKenzie and Parker; separately Morgan | Rigid lithosphere plates on semi-fluid asthenosphere; driven by mantle convection
[7:0:1] - Wegener's forces were **too weak** → theory rejected. Holmes gave drift the **driving force** Wegener lacked.
[7:0:2] - Plate tectonics is **based on sea-floor spreading**; it was **not** given by H.H. Hess alone in 1967 – developed in the 1960s by Wilson, Morgan, Parker, McKenzie, Hess and others.
[7:0:3] 1912 Wegener → 1930s Holmes → 1961 Hess → 1967 plate tectonics.
## SUB 1 | Pangaea, Laurasia and Gondwanaland
[7:1:0] - Original single landmass: **Pangaea** (Carboniferous), surrounded by mega-ocean **Panthalassa** (named by Wegener).
[7:1:1] - Split into **Laurasia** (north) and **Gondwanaland** (south), separated by the **Tethys Sea**.
[7:1:2] TABLE head: Name | Present landmasses
  [7:1:2.0] Laurasia | North America, Greenland, Europe, Asia (minus India)
  [7:1:2.1] Gondwanaland | South America, Africa, India, Australia, Antarctica, Madagascar, Arabia
[7:1:3] India = Gondwanaland (APPSC 2018), not Laurasia. North America is NOT part of Gondwanaland.
## SUB 2 | Evidences of Continental Drift
[7:2:0] TABLE head: Evidence | Example
  [7:2:0.0] Jig-saw fit | South America and Africa (best at continental-shelf edge); Bullard's 1964 computer fit
  [7:2:0.1] Rock belts | ~2,000-million-year belt of Brazil coast matches West Africa
  [7:2:0.2] Placer deposits | Ghana coast gold has no local source; source rock in Brazil plateau
  [7:2:0.3] Tillite | Glacial deposits of India have counterparts in southern continents
  [7:2:0.4] Fossils | Mesosaurus (South Africa and Brazil – not India and Australia); Glossopteris; lemurs ("Lemuria")
[7:2:1] - UPSC 2025: rock belts, placer gold and the third evidence – **all correct**.
## SUB 3 | Sea-floor Spreading
[7:3:0] - Rocks record the magnetic field of their time (**palaeomagnetism**).
[7:3:1] - Away from the ridge: **age, density, thickness** of oceanic crust and sediment cover **increase** (APPSC 2019).
[7:3:2] - Ocean-floor rocks much younger than continental; oldest ~**200 million years**.
[7:3:3] - Spreading always **adds** new crust; subduction **destroys** old crust – the two roughly balance.
[7:3:4] - First direct observation of a full spreading episode: seafloor sank 4 m, spread over 1 m, 160 million cubic metres of lava in 16 days → spreading comes in **bursts**, not steadily.
## SUB 4 | Plates – Major and Minor
[7:4:0] - **Seven major plates** (APPSC 2018): Pacific, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian, Antarctic.
[7:4:1] TABLE head: Minor plate | Location
  [7:4:1.0] Cocos | Between Central America and Pacific plate
  [7:4:1.1] Nazca | Between South America and Pacific plate
  [7:4:1.2] Caroline | Between Philippine and Indian plates, north of New Guinea
  [7:4:1.3] Philippine | Between Asiatic and Pacific plates
  [7:4:1.4] Arabian | Mostly Saudi Arabian landmass
  [7:4:1.5] Caribbean | Borders South American plate; Venezuela on this boundary (Jul 2026)
[7:4:2] - Plate movement is monitored with **GPS** data (APPSC 2021); GSI runs 34 GNSS stations building strain models.
## SUB 5 | Plate Boundaries
[7:5:0] TABLE head: Boundary | Motion | Examples
  [7:5:0.0] Divergent (constructive) | Apart; new crust | Mid-Atlantic Ridge; Red Sea, East African Rift (Nubian, Somalian, Arabian plates near Afar triple junction)
  [7:5:0.1] Convergent (destructive) | Collision / subduction | Himalayas (continent-continent); Andes (Nazca under South American); Mariana Trench (ocean-ocean)
  [7:5:0.2] Transform (conservative) | Slide past horizontally | San Andreas (not divergent); Sagaing Fault (Myanmar, Indian–Eurasian)
[7:5:1] - Convergent: earthquakes from **subduction friction** (Pacific Ring of Fire), mountain building.
[7:5:2] - **Owen Fracture Zone** separates Arabian and Indian plates.
[7:5:3] - **Ladakh Magmatic Arc**: between Indus and Shyok suture zones; northward subduction of **Neo-Tethys** plate.
## SUB 6 | The Indian Plate
[7:6:0] - 1. Gondwanaland broke up ~**225 million years** ago.
[7:6:1] - 2. Palaeomagnetic latitudes show India moving **north** from south of the equator; Peninsular Plateau part of African landmass until the Cretaceous.
[7:6:2] - 3. **Deccan Traps** erupted as India passed near the equator over the **Reunion hotspot**.
[7:6:3] - 4. Collision with Eurasia began ~**40–50 million years** ago (4–5 crore); Tethys closed; sediments folded into the Himalayas.
[7:6:4] - 5. India moves north at ~**5 cm/year** → Himalayas still rising (about **5 mm/year**).
[7:6:5] - India underthrust Asia 45–20 million years ago, lifting Tibet; pushed deeper under western Tibet → west more rugged.
[7:6:6] - **Delamination**: denser lower layer peels off and sinks into the mantle at ~**100 km** beneath southern Tibet (94 seismic stations).
[7:6:7] - Collision explains the Himalayas, **Indo-Gangetic foreland basin** and India's earthquake risk.
## SUB 7 | Diastrophism
[7:7:0] TABLE head: Movement | Force | Result
  [7:7:0.0] Orogenic | Horizontal (tangential) | Mountain building by folding/faulting – e.g. Himalayas
  [7:7:0.1] Epeirogenic | Vertical | Uplift or warping of continents and plateaus
[7:7:1] - Sudden movements (earthquakes, volcanoes) are treated separately.
## SUB 8 | Folding and Fold Mountains
[7:8:0] - Compression bends layers: **anticline** = up-arch; **syncline** = down-trough.
[7:8:1] - Rising pressure: symmetrical → asymmetrical → **overfold** → recumbent → **nappe** (recumbent fold broken by thrust, pushed far forward).
[7:8:2] - Siwaliks: simple **"Jura" type** folds; **youngest** Himalayan range. Himalayas began ~70 million years ago.
[7:8:3] TABLE head: Young fold (Tertiary / Alpine) | Old fold (worn down)
  [7:8:3.0] Himalayas, Alps, Andes, Rockies | Aravallis (among India's oldest), Appalachians, Urals
[7:8:4] - **Eastern Ghats** (AP): old, eroded, discontinuous hills of the Gondwana shield – not young fold mountains.
## SUB 9 | Faulting
[7:9:0] TABLE head: Fault | Force | Movement / example
  [7:9:0.0] Normal | Tension | Hanging wall down; East African Rift, Rio Grande
  [7:9:0.1] Reverse | Compression | Hanging wall up
  [7:9:0.2] Thrust | Compression | Low-angle reverse; Himalayan MCT, MBT, HFF
  [7:9:0.3] Strike-slip | Shear | Horizontal; San Andreas, Sagaing
[7:9:1] - **Horst** = uplifted block (block mountain); **graben** = sunken block (rift valley). Vosges and **Rhine Valley** (APPSC 2022: horsts = block mountains).
[7:9:2] - **Satpura** = block mountain between rift valleys of **Narmada and Tapi**.
[7:9:3] - Indo-Gangetic Plain: Himalayan Frontal Fault (north) + normal fault (south).
[7:9:4] - East African Rift: chain of mostly freshwater lakes; **Lake Malawi** at southern end; **Lake Victoria is not a rift lake** (APPSC G1 2024). Red Sea is also a rift.
