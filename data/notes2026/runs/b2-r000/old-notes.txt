=== NOTES 2:0 | Constitutional Evolution – Regulating Act 1773 to the Government of India Act 1935
## SUB 0 | Before 1773 and the Regulating Act, 1773
[0:0:0] # Background
[0:0:1] - **Act of 1767**: Company to pay the British Government **£400,000 a year** (APPSC G2 2017).
[0:0:2] - **1772**: famine, falling revenue and the Company's financial crisis (loan of about **£1 million** sought) → Parliament intervened in Bengal.
[0:0:3] - **Lord North** committee report → Regulating Act.
[0:0:4] # Regulating Act, 1773
[0:0:5] - **First** step by Parliament to **control the EIC**.
[0:0:6] - **Governor of Bengal → Governor-General of Bengal**: **Warren Hastings** (first) with a **4-member Executive Council**.
[0:0:7] - **Bombay and Madras** made subordinate to Bengal.
[0:0:8] - **Supreme Court at Calcutta** (Fort William), set up **1774**: **1 Chief Justice + 3 judges**; first CJ **Sir Elijah Impey**.
[0:0:9] - Banned **private trade and bribes** by Company officials.
[0:0:10] - Directors to send important letters on revenue, civil and military affairs to the British Government.
[0:0:11] - Did **NOT abolish the Court of Directors** (it lasted till **1858**).
[0:0:12] - **Amending Act 1781 (Act of Settlement)**: separated judicial and executive functions.
[0:0:13] "Regulating Act abolished the Court of Directors" = the incorrect statement (APPSC GS 2019).
## SUB 1 | Pitt's India Act 1784 and the Act of 1786
[0:1:0] - **Dual Control / double government**: **Board of Control** (political) + **Court of Directors** (commercial).
[0:1:1] - Board of Control: **6 members**, incl. a Secretary of State and the Chancellor of the Exchequer.
[0:1:2] - Company territories = **"British possessions in India"**; Company a subordinate department of the State.
[0:1:3] - GG's council **cut to 3**.
[0:1:4] - **Act of 1786**: GG may **override his Council** and may also act as **Commander-in-Chief** (two separate powers); first used by **Lord Cornwallis**.
[0:1:5] Board of Control was set up by Pitt's India Act 1784 (UPPSC 2015).
## SUB 2 | Charter Acts 1793–1853
[0:2:0] # Act-wise Provisions
[0:2:1] TABLE head: Act | Key provisions
  [0:2:1.0] 1793 | Monopoly extended 20 years; override power extended to all future GGs and Presidency Governors; more control over Bombay and Madras; Board of Control salaries from Indian revenue; C-in-C not ordinarily a council member
  [0:2:1.1] 1813 | Trade monopoly ended except tea and China trade; Christian missionaries allowed (Charles Grant, Wilberforce); Rs 1 lakh a year for education – first such provision; asserted Crown sovereignty over Company territories
  [0:2:1.2] 1833 (Saint Helena Act) | Governor-General of India created (William Bentinck, first; also last GG of Bengal); monopoly completely ended – Company purely administrative; Law Member added (council to 4) – Macaulay; Law Commission under Macaulay (origin of Law Ministry); legislation centralised in GG-in-Council; Section 87: no Indian debarred by religion, birth, descent or colour; open competition only attempted
  [0:2:1.3] 1853 (last Charter Act) | Legislative and executive functions of the GG's council separated; 6 legislative members added (Indian Legislative Council – mini-parliament); open competition put into operation (Macaulay Committee 1854); territories held "in trust for Her Majesty", no fixed term (APPSC GS 2018)
[0:2:2] - Order: Regulating Act 1773 → Pitt's India Act 1784 → Charter Act 1793 → Charter Act 1813.
[0:2:3] - English education: 1813 grant → General Committee of Public Instruction (1823) → Orientalist–Anglicist controversy.
[0:2:4] 1813 kept tea and China trade; 1833 ended all trade. Calling the 1813 Act the "Magna Carta" is the wrong statement.
[0:2:5] 1833 Act did NOT provide that an Indian be Law Member; first Law Member = Macaulay, not Impey.
## SUB 3 | Crown Rule – Government of India Act, 1858
[0:3:0] - Rule transferred from the **Company to the Crown**.
[0:3:1] - Governor-General also styled **Viceroy**: **Lord Canning** first; last Viceroy **Mountbatten**.
[0:3:2] - **Board of Control and Court of Directors abolished**.
[0:3:3] - **Secretary of State for India** created, with a **15-member Council of India**.
## SUB 4 | Indian Councils Acts 1861 and 1892
[0:4:0] # Indian Councils Act, 1861 (passed 1 August 1861)
[0:4:1] - Restored legislative powers of **Bombay and Madras** (decentralisation).
[0:4:2] - Viceroy nominated Indians as non-officials: **Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao** (by Canning) → beginning of representative institutions.
[0:4:3] - **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan** criticised lack of Indians (lesson of 1857) – he was NOT a nominee.
[0:4:4] - **Portfolio system**: begun by **Canning (1859)**, statutory recognition in 1861.
[0:4:5] - Viceroy's **ordinance** power – at most **six months**.
[0:4:6] - New provincial councils: Bengal (1862), Punjab (1897).
[0:4:7] - **Indian High Courts Act 1861** → High Courts of **Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1862)**.
[0:4:8] # Indian Councils Act, 1892
[0:4:9] - **Indirect election** began: nomination on recommendation of **universities, municipalities, chambers of commerce** (APPSC GS 2019).
[0:4:10] - **Budget discussion** (annual financial statement), **no vote**.
[0:4:11] - Questions with **6 days' notice**; **no supplementary** questions.
[0:4:12] # 1861 vs 1892
[0:4:13] TABLE head: Feature | 1861 | 1892
  [0:4:13.0] Indians | Nominated non-officials | More non-officials
  [0:4:13.1] Election | None | Indirect begins
  [0:4:13.2] Budget | No discussion | Discussion, no vote
  [0:4:13.3] Other | Portfolio, ordinance | Right to ask questions
## SUB 5 | Indian Councils Act, 1909 – Morley-minto Reforms
[0:5:0] - **John Morley** = Secretary of State; **Lord Minto** = Viceroy, **"Father of Communal Electorate"**.
[0:5:1] - **Separate electorate for Muslims**; formal elected representation (2018 key: Act allowing elected representation = 1909).
[0:5:2] - Indians first in Viceroy's and Governors' Executive Councils: **S.P. Sinha**, first Indian, as **Law Member**.
[0:5:3] - Central council: additional members to **60** (total **69**: 37 officials, 32 non-officials); **official majority** at Centre, non-official majority in provinces; still **unicameral**.
[0:5:4] - **Supplementary questions**, budget discussion and resolutions allowed.
[0:5:5] - No dyarchy, no provincial autonomy; most **short-lived** experiment (about a decade); sowed seeds of separatism.
## SUB 6 | Government of India Act, 1919 – Montagu-chelmsford
[0:6:0] # Basis
[0:6:1] - **Montagu's declaration, 20 August 1917**: gradual responsible government; **Montagu-Chelmsford Report (1918)**.
[0:6:2] - **Edwin Montagu** = Secretary of State; **Chelmsford** = Viceroy.
[0:6:3] # Provisions
[0:6:4] - **Provincial dyarchy** (Greek "di-arche", double rule) – first time.
[0:6:5] - **Bicameral Centre** first time: **Council of State** (upper, **60**) + **Legislative Assembly** (lower); **direct elections**, limited franchise (property, tax, education).
[0:6:6] - Term **"responsible government"** first used; central and provincial **budgets separated**; Central and Provincial lists defined.
[0:6:7] - **Communal representation extended** to **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans**.
[0:6:8] - 3 of 6 Viceroy's Executive Council members Indian; **High Commissioner for India in London**; separate **ICS examination in India**.
[0:6:9] - **Chamber of Princes**: **120** members; George V's Proclamation **23 Dec 1919**; first Chancellor **Ganga Singh of Bikaner**.
[0:6:10] - **Public Accounts Committee** 1921; **PSC** provided, set up **1 October 1926** (Chairman + 4), became Federal PSC under 1935.
[0:6:11] - **Statutory commission after 10 years** → **Simon Commission** appointed **1927** (two years early), 7 members, all British, chaired by **Sir John Simon**; boycotted ("Simon Go Back").
[0:6:12] - Last Indian Legislature election under this Act: **1945**.
[0:6:13] # Reserved vs Transferred Subjects
[0:6:14] TABLE head: Reserved (Governor + Executive Council) | Transferred (Governor + Indian Ministers)
  [0:6:14.0] Law and order, police, administration of justice, land revenue, irrigation, finance | Local self-government, education, public health, agriculture, public works
## SUB 7 | Government of India Act, 1935
[0:7:0] # Basis and Size
[0:7:1] - Based on **Third Round Table Conference** and the **White Paper**; **321 sections, 14 parts, 10 schedules**.
[0:7:2] # Federation and Centre
[0:7:3] - **All-India Federation** ("Federation of India") of provinces + princely states – joining **optional**, no state joined → **never formed**.
[0:7:4] - Princes included to counter-balance nationalist leaders.
[0:7:5] - **Dyarchy at the Centre** proposed – **never worked**; **defence and foreign affairs** = reserved under the **GG**.
[0:7:6] - Bicameral Centre: **Federal Assembly + Council of States**.
[0:7:7] - **Three lists** (Federal, Provincial, Concurrent); **residuary powers with the Governor-General**.
[0:7:8] - **Section 42 → Art. 123** (ordinance); **Section 63 → Art. 175** (Governor's address; not mandatory, 2026 row in Opposition States).
[0:7:9] - **Council of India abolished**; Secretary of State given advisers.
[0:7:10] # Provinces
[0:7:11] - **Provincial dyarchy abolished** → **provincial autonomy**, responsible ministries from **April 1937** (APPSC GS 2025).
[0:7:12] - **Bicameral in 6**: **Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, United Provinces** – NOT Punjab.
[0:7:13] - Communal representation extended to **depressed classes, women, labour**.
[0:7:14] - **Burma** separated; **Sindh** from Bombay; **Orissa** from Bihar.
[0:7:15] - Franchise extended from about **3%** to about **14%** of population (about **3.5 crore** voters).
[0:7:16] - Congress did not form a ministry in **Punjab** (1937).
[0:7:17] # Institutions
[0:7:18] - **Federal Court**, Delhi, inaugurated **1 October 1937**; first CJ **Sir Maurice Gwyer**; replaced by the Supreme Court in **1950**.
[0:7:19] - **RBI** formed **1 April 1935** under the **RBI Act 1934**; Federal, Provincial and Joint **PSCs**.
[0:7:20] - Carried into the Constitution: federal scheme, Governor, judiciary, PSCs, emergency provisions, administrative details; main source of Centre–State distribution.
[0:7:21] UPSC 2024: "All-India Federation" correct; "Defence and Foreign Affairs under the federal legislature" WRONG.
