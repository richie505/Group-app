## SUB 0 | Mosquito-borne Diseases – Vectors and Pathogens
[0:0] # Vector basics
[0:1] - **Vector:** an organism (mosquito, flea, sandfly, tick, mite) that carries a pathogen between hosts – insects that transmit disease are called **vectors**.
[0:2] - **Only female mosquitoes suck blood** (they need it for eggs) and have **bigger wings** than males; **males feed on plant juices/nectar**.
[0:3] - **Culex, Anopheles and Aedes** – all three genera are blood-feeders found throughout the world.
[0:4] # Mosquito → disease
[0:5] TABLE head: Mosquito | Disease (pathogen type)
  [0:5.0] Female Anopheles | Malaria (*Plasmodium*, protozoan)
  [0:5.1] Aedes aegypti | Dengue, chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever (all viral)
  [0:5.2] Aedes albopictus (Asian tiger / forest mosquito) | Dengue, chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever – NOT Japanese encephalitis (most books)
  [0:5.3] Culex (*C. quinquefasciatus*, *C. pipiens*) | Lymphatic filariasis (nematode), Japanese encephalitis (virus)
  [0:5.4] Haemagogus | Forest (jungle) yellow fever
[0:6] - **NOT mosquito-borne:** kala-azar (sandfly), sleeping sickness (tsetse fly), Chagas (kissing bug), plague (rat flea), scrub typhus (chigger mite), KFD (ticks).
[0:7] - Wrong sets often asked: "Aedes – chikungunya – **bacteria**" is wrong (chikungunya is an **alphavirus**); "Tsetse – filariasis" and "bed bug – sleeping sickness" are wrong.
[0:8] # Disease details
[0:9] TABLE head: Disease | Pathogen | Key fact
  [0:9.0] Dengue ("breakbone fever") | Dengue virus (flavivirus), 4 serotypes | Platelets fall sharply → internal bleeding; virus suppresses platelet production in bone marrow, infects endothelial cells and binds platelets (CDS 2016: I, II, III)
  [0:9.1] Chikungunya | CHIKV, alphavirus | Severe joint pain
  [0:9.2] Zika | Zika virus (flavivirus) | Same mosquito as dengue (Aedes aegypti); also sexual and blood transmission; microcephaly risk
  [0:9.3] Yellow fever | Yellow fever virus | Spread by mosquito – not air, water or housefly
  [0:9.4] Japanese encephalitis | JEV (flavivirus), a virus | Infects the brain; pigs amplify, pigs + wild birds = reservoirs → keeping pigs away from settlements curbs JE; not water-borne
  [0:9.5] Lymphatic filariasis | Nematode *Wuchereria bancrofti* (thread-like roundworm) | Lymph vessels/nodes → fever early, then chronic swelling of lower limbs = elephantiasis; *Culex* is the vector, not the cause
[0:10] - Malaria vs dengue: common = fever, mosquito bite, human host; **different = mosquito species** (Anopheles vs Aedes) and pathogen (protozoan vs virus).
[0:11] - **Dengue vaccine:** **Qdenga (TAK-003, Takeda)** – live-attenuated tetravalent, age 4–60; CDSCO approval **July 2026** = India's first approved dengue vaccine.

## SUB 1 | Malaria and Plasmodium
[1:0] - **Pathogen:** protozoan ***Plasmodium***; **carrier/vector = female Anopheles mosquito** (not tick, fly or louse).
[1:1] - Parasites multiply first in the **liver**, then destroy **red blood cells**; the **spleen** enlarges (organ affected by malaria).
[1:2] TABLE head: Species | Fact
  [1:2.0] *P. falciparum* | Malignant (cerebral) malaria – most severe
  [1:2.1] *P. vivax* | Relapsing malaria (dormant liver hypnozoites); old books: ~65% of India's cases (now ~40%; falciparum ~60% in 2024)
  [1:2.2] *P. malariae*, *P. ovale* | Milder
  [1:2.3] *P. knowlesi* | Zoonotic (macaques)
[1:3] - **Five** Plasmodium species infect humans. Vaccine is hard because malaria is caused by **several species** (with changing antigens) – UPSC 2010.
[1:4] - **Drugs:** **chloroquine** (prevention and treatment; *P. vivax*), quinine (from cinchona bark), **artemisinin/arteether** from the seeded plant ***Artemisia annua***; ACTs first-line for falciparum.
[1:5] - **Vaccines:** RTS,S/AS01 (Mosquirix) and R21/Matrix-M (WHO-recommended); India's **AdFalciVax** (ICMR-RMRC Bhubaneswar) at preclinical stage (2026).
[1:6] > Exam angle: carrier of Plasmodium (×3), malignant malaria (falciparum), 65% share (vivax), organ (spleen), drug (chloroquine, arteether), why vaccine is hard.
[1:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Biology p.25-26 (malaria: liver → RBC, spleen), p.29 (Sheet #17: falciparum malignant, vivax recurring/major share), p.33 (Sheet #20 drugs); PMF General Science Part 5 p.28 (five species, chloroquine for vivax, ACTs, vaccines); Rocket CA 2026 Part 02 p.111-112 (AdFalciVax); orfonline.org "More tests, fewer cases" (falciparum 60% of 2024 cases)

## SUB 3 | Water-borne and Faeco-oral Diseases
[3:0] - **Faeco-oral (contaminated water/food) group:** **cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A and E, acute diarrhoea, dysentery, polio**; also amoebiasis, ascariasis.
[3:1] TABLE head: Disease | Pathogen | Fact
  [3:1.0] Typhoid (enteric fever) | *Salmonella Typhi* (bacterium) | Widal test; intestine
  [3:1.1] Cholera | *Vibrio cholerae* (bacterium) | Contaminated food/water → severe diarrhoea, dehydration; ORS
  [3:1.2] Dysentery | Bacillary = *Shigella* (bacterial); amoebic = *Entamoeba* (protozoan) | Blood in stool
  [3:1.3] Hepatitis A, E | Viruses | Inflammation of the liver → jaundice; spread by polluted water
  [3:1.4] Polio | Poliovirus | Enters through polluted food and water; spinal cord
  [3:1.5] Acute diarrhoea | Rotavirus, *E. coli*, *Shigella* | Leading child killer; acute diarrhoeal diseases caused the most water-borne deaths in India (2012-2017)
  [3:1.6] Food poisoning | *Salmonella* (not Anopheles, Culex, Aedes) | Contaminated food
[3:2] - **Unsafe water + poor sanitation** (developing countries): **malaria, acute diarrhoea, schistosomiasis**; ~**5,000 children die daily** worldwide. **Kwashiorkor** (protein deficiency) is NOT water-borne.
[3:3] - **Floods** spread cholera, typhoid, hepatitis, **leptospirosis**.
[3:4] - **Shallow hand-pump** water → cholera, typhoid, jaundice – **NOT fluorosis** (fluoride, deep groundwater).
[3:5] - **GI diseases:** symptoms are diarrhoea, vomiting, abdominal pain – **kidney failure is not** a GI symptom. **Inflammatory bowel disease** = gastrointestinal tract.
[3:6] # Look-alikes that are NOT water-borne

  [3:7.1] Jaundice | A symptom of liver disease (bilirubin), not a disease of one microbe – not so contagious
  [3:7.2] TB, pneumonia | Airborne bacterial

## SUB 7 | Infection, Immunity and Vaccination
[7:0] - **Disease vs symptom:** **cholera** is a disease; fever, sneezing, headache are symptoms. Typhoid is a type of **fever** (enteric fever).
[7:1] - **Contagious:** **yawning** is the classic contagious reflex (not snoring, gnashing, vomiting). Infectious diseases (AIDS, chickenpox, mumps, herpes) pass from person to person; AIDS is **not spread by casual contact**.
[7:2] - **Measles:** infants **below six months** are protected by **maternal antibodies** (natural passive immunity); MR vaccine at 9-12 and 16-24 months.
[7:3] - **No vaccine** controls **diabetes**; polio, whooping cough, smallpox are vaccine-preventable. **Rickets** (vitamin D deficiency) cannot be prevented by any UIP vaccine.
[7:4] - **Smallpox:** WHO declared it **eradicated in 1980** (8 May); India free in **1977** – the only human disease eradicated.
[7:5] - **Rabies:** spread through the **saliva** of a rabid animal (bite) → nervous system, hydrophobia.
[7:6] - **Community transmission (COVID-19):** an infected person in the community, unaware of infection, infects others – the source cannot be traced to travel or a known contact.
[7:7] > Exam angle: contagious/infectious (×4), measles immunity, no vaccine (diabetes, rickets), smallpox 1980.

## SUB 9 | Vector Control, Insecticide Resistance and Elimination
[9:0] - **DDT resistance** in mosquitoes = **natural selection**: resistant individuals survive and breed, so resistance spreads.
[9:1] TABLE head: Bio-control | Mechanism
  [9:1.0] *Gambusia affinis* (mosquito fish), guppy | Larvivorous fish – eat larvae
  [9:1.1] Bti | Bacterial bio-larvicide
  [9:1.2] Wolbachia | Bacterium; controls viral diseases spread by mosquitoes (dengue, chikungunya, Zika) through cytoplasmic incompatibility + pathogen blocking; no GM, no pesticide

## SUB 10 | Air Pollution and Health
[10:0] - Main target of air pollution: **respiratory system**; also heart disease, stroke, lung cancer.
[10:1] - **PM2.5 raises heart disease** because these fine particles **penetrate deep into the lungs and enter the blood** (A and R true, R explains A).
[10:2] - Air pollution comes from **natural sources too** (volcanoes, dust, forest fires) – "only human activities" is false; **planting trees reduces** it (only Statement II true).
[10:3] - **WHO:** **4.2 million** premature deaths from **ambient** air pollution (**2019**). State of Global Air 2020: 116,000+ Indian infants died within a month of birth (2019).
[10:4] - **IQAir 2025:** India **6th** most polluted country (PM2.5 **48.9 µg/m³**); only 13 countries met the WHO annual guideline of **5 µg/m³**.
[10:5] - **DALY = YLL + YLD.** Noise: hearing loss, hypertension, anxiety, sleep disorders.

## SUB 12 | Antibiotics and Antimicrobial Resistance
[12:0] # Antibiotics
[12:1] - **Fleming: penicillin, 1928**; **Waksman** coined "antibiotic", found **streptomycin** (first anti-TB drug).
[12:2] - Most antibiotics are **natural or semi-synthetic**, mostly from bacteria (*Streptomyces*); sulfa drugs synthetic. **Prontosil** (Domagk, 1935) → sulfanilamide.
[12:3] - **Griseofulvin** treats ringworm; **Acetobacter** → vinegar; chloroquine = antimalarial.
[12:4] # How AMR builds
[12:5] - Causes: incorrect dose, **incomplete course** (resistant survivors left to multiply), overuse, **self-medication**, livestock use, **mutation**, horizontal gene transfer.
[12:6] - Severity: **MDR → XDR → TDR**.
[12:7] - **Biofilm:** microbes in a self-made **EPS (extracellular polymeric substance)** matrix on **implants, catheters, tissues, food surfaces**; blocks antibiotics → chronic infection; food spoilage and contamination.
