=== NOTES 3:19 | Environmental Degradation As A Development Challenge; Green Accounting and Green Gdp
## SUB 0 | Functions of the Environment
[19:0:0] TABLE head: Function | Meaning
  [19:0:0.0] Supplies resources (first function) | Both renewable (trees, fish) and non-renewable (fossil fuels)
  [19:0:0.1] Assimilates waste | Absorbs and neutralises wastes
  [19:0:0.2] Sustains life | Genetic and biodiversity
  [19:0:0.3] Aesthetic services | Scenery and amenity
[19:0:1] - **First function** of the environment = **supply / production of raw materials** (APPSC-GS 2023 key).
[19:0:2] - **Absorptive capacity** = power to take in degradation = maximum pollutants an ecosystem can process and neutralise without harm (APPSC-GS 2024 key).
[19:0:3] - **Crisis** comes when extraction > regeneration and waste > absorptive capacity; the environment then **cannot sustain life**. The world is at the threshold of an environmental crisis.
[19:0:4] "Supplies renewable resources only" is wrong. **Recycling** is NOT a function of the environment; nor is "it disorganizes waste that benefits human life".
## SUB 1 | Resource Depletion and Its Causes

=== NOTES 3:20 | Sustainable Development – Definition, Brundtland Report, Sdgs and India's Progress
## SUB 0 | Meaning of Sustainable Development
[20:0:0] - **Sustainable development** = meeting present needs **without reducing the options of future generations**.
[20:0:1] - Core goal = **intergenerational equity** within Earth's **carrying capacity**.
[20:0:2] - Simple meaning: **balancing the need to use resources and conserve them for the future**.
[20:0:3] - Base of sustainable development = **environmental approach**.
[20:0:4] - Why it matters: conventional growth uses resources faster than nature can renew them; sustainable development asks that today's use leave the stock of natural capital intact for those who come later.
[20:0:5] - The idea links three things: **needs** (especially of the poor), **limits** set by the environment's ability to meet present and future needs, and **equity** between and within generations.
[20:0:6] # Basic goal vs NOT goals (odd-one-out)
[20:0:7] TABLE head: Is a goal of sustainable development | NOT a goal
  [20:0:7.0] Meet present needs while keeping choices for future generations | Growth at any cost
  [20:0:7.1] Stay within Earth's carrying capacity | Exploiting resources for present gains
  [20:0:7.2] Balance economy, society and environment | Profit maximisation
  [20:0:7.3] Intergenerational equity | Prioritise individual wealth accumulation
  [20:0:7.4]  | Unrestricted resource exploitation
## SUB 1 | Milestones – from Stockholm to Agenda 2030
[20:1:0] # Timeline
[20:1:1] TABLE head: Year | Milestone | Key fact
  [20:1:1.0] 1968 (Apr) | Club of Rome founded | Meeting of 30 people from 10 countries at the Accademia dei Lincei, Rome
  [20:1:1.1] 1972 | Stockholm – UN Conference on the Human Environment (5-16 June) | First put environment on the global agenda; created UNEP
  [20:1:1.2] 1972 | "Limits to Growth" | Club of Rome; Earth's finite carrying capacity
  [20:1:1.3] 1980 | World Conservation Strategy (IUCN, UNEP, WWF) | Term "sustainable development" introduced
  [20:1:1.4] 1987 | "Our Common Future" – Brundtland Report | First formal definition
  [20:1:1.5] 1992 | UNCED, Rio (Earth Summit) | Rio Declaration, Agenda 21, UNFCCC
  [20:1:1.6] 2002 | Johannesburg – World Summit on Sustainable Development ("Rio+10") | Johannesburg Plan of Implementation
  [20:1:1.7] 2012 | Rio+20 – UN Conference on Sustainable Development | Decision to develop the SDGs
  [20:1:1.8] 2015 | Agenda 2030 adopted, 25 September 2015, New York | 17 SDGs
[20:1:2] APPSC 2025: Sustainable development was first defined by the **Brundtland Report** – not Paris, Montreal or Kyoto.
[20:1:3] The concept was **popularised** by the Brundtland Commission Report **1987**, not the Rio Declaration 1992. The Brundtland Report is NOT "The Limits to Growth".
[20:1:4] - **Stockholm 1972** was the first UN conference on the environment; **Rio 1992** shifted the focus to **environment and development** together; **Johannesburg 2002** reviewed progress ten years after Rio; **Rio+20 (2012)** returned to Rio and launched the process that produced the SDGs.
[20:1:5] - The common thread from Stockholm to Rio+20 is the growth of **global public awareness and concern** about the environment.
[20:1:6] - **Agenda 21** (Rio 1992) was the first global plan of action for sustainable development; **Agenda 2030** is its successor in spirit.

## SUB 6 | Agenda 2030 – Roots and Principles

[20:6:1] - Two roots: **Millennium Development Goals (MDGs)** + the **Rio+20 outcome**.
[20:6:2] - **MDGs**: **8 goals**, period **2000-2015**.

[20:6:4] - Agenda 2030 is grounded in the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights**, **international human rights treaties**, the **Millennium Declaration** and the **2005 World Summit Outcome**.
[20:6:5] # Principles
[20:6:6] - **Universal**: applies to **all countries** – developed, developing and least developed alike.
[20:6:7] - **Leave no one behind**: progress is measured by the poorest and most excluded groups, not by averages.
[20:6:8] - **Integrated and indivisible**: the 17 goals are to be pursued together; progress on one (for example clean energy, SDG 7) supports others (climate action, SDG 13).
[20:6:9] - Agenda 2030 is also informed by the **Declaration on the Right to Development**.
[20:6:10] - Specific focus group: **ending all discrimination and violence against women and girls** (APPSC-GS 2025 key).
