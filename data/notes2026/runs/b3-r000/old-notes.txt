=== NOTES 3:0 | Basic Characteristics of the Indian Economy – Features of A Developing Economy
## SUB 0 | Types of Economies
[0:0:0] # Comparison
[0:0:1] TABLE head: Type | Core feature | Example / proponent
  [0:0:1.0] Capitalist | Private ownership, profit motive, market prices; inequality is a feature | USA
  [0:0:1.1] Socialist | Collective ownership, equitable distribution, State welfare | Socialist elements in Sweden, Norway
  [0:0:1.2] Command / controlled (planned) | Central plan replaces the market; government decides what, how and for whom | Former USSR, North Korea
  [0:0:1.3] Gandhian | Decentralised, self-contained villages, cottage industries | Gandhian Plan 1944 – S.N. Agarwal
  [0:0:1.4] Traditional | Custom and tradition decide what, how and for whom | –
  [0:0:1.5] Mixed | Coexistence of public and private sectors | India, UK, Germany
  [0:0:1.6] Dual | Modern sector + traditional agrarian sector | Lewis model
[0:0:2] # Mixed Economy vs Joint Sector
[0:0:3] - **Mixed economy**: public and private sectors go side by side; the State **regulates the private sector through taxes, subsidies and credit**; planning treats both as complementary; combines capitalism and socialism.
[0:0:4] - **Joint sector**: **one enterprise jointly owned** by government and private investors.
[0:0:5] - **Public sector**: government ownership or control (e.g. **Bhilai Steel Plant**); **private sector**: private ownership or control.
[0:0:6] - **"Commanding heights"** held by the State (heavy industry, infrastructure, finance); private sector in consumer goods under regulation.
[0:0:7] # Why India Is Mixed
[0:0:8] - A **pragmatic middle path** between liberal-capitalist, Soviet and Gandhian models; aim – **growth by democratic means** with a social order based on **justice**.
[0:0:9] - The Indian model protects the interest of **both State and Person**.
[0:0:10] UPPSC 2024: India is an underdeveloped economy AND a mixed economy.
## SUB 1 | Nature of the Economy – Labels
[0:1:0] TABLE head: Label | Defining feature
  [0:1:0.0] Developing | Low per capita income; low capital formation (needs a high savings rate); high public investment in infrastructure is a precondition for private growth
  [0:1:0.1] Underdeveloped | Low PCI, low capital formation, predominance of agriculture, disguised unemployment, high dependency ratio
  [0:1:0.2] Dual | Modern industrial/urban sector coexists with a traditional, subsistence agrarian sector
  [0:1:0.3] Labour-surplus | Surplus farm labour with marginal productivity near zero – the Lewis setting
  [0:1:0.4] Mixed | Public-private coexistence (this is NOT a feature of underdevelopment)
[0:1:1] - India is a **developing** economy that still shows **underdeveloped** features.
## SUB 2 | Features of an Underdeveloped Economy
[0:2:0] - **Low per capita income**; **inequitable income distribution**; slower increase in national income.
[0:2:1] - **Low capital formation** – depends on savings and good financial institutions.
[0:2:2] - **Heavy dependence on agriculture**; **high population growth**; **high dependency ratio**.
[0:2:3] - **Disguised unemployment**: more workers than needed; **marginal productivity zero**; removing extra workers would not cut output; mainly **agriculture/primary** and the unorganised sector.
[0:2:4] - India: predominance of agriculture, low PCI, massive unemployment. **Industrial dominance** marks a developed economy, not India.
[0:2:5] "Lack of natural resources" is NOT a characteristic of the Indian economy.
## SUB 3 | India at Independence and Causes of Underdevelopment
[0:3:0] # Features at Independence
[0:3:1] - **First National Income Committee (1951)**: 1948-49 national income **₹8,710 crore**; per capita **₹225**.
[0:3:2] - Agriculture: **72%** of the workforce (**1951**).
[0:3:3] - Weak industrial base: capital goods only **one-fourth** of industrial output.
[0:3:4] - Literacy below **16%**; life expectancy **32 years** (1947); thin social overhead capital.
[0:3:5] - Colonial legacy (from **1757, Battle of Plassey**): India turned from textile exporter into a **raw-material supplier and captive market** – NOT a supplier of manufactured goods; economy stagnated.
[0:3:6] - **Angus Maddison**: India's share of world GDP **22.6% (1700) → 3.8% (1952)**.
[0:3:7] # Causes
[0:3:8] TABLE head: Cause | Note
  [0:3:8.0] Colonial legacy | Deindustrialisation
  [0:3:8.1] Capital deficiency | Low savings
  [0:3:8.2] Population pressure | High dependency ratio; surplus workers in farming
  [0:3:8.3] Low productivity | Disguised unemployment
  [0:3:8.4] Weak human capital | Low literacy, low life expectancy
[0:3:9] - **Most important cause**: **co-existence of under-utilised manpower and under-utilised natural resources** (APPSC key) – NOT high density, lack of capital or low PCI alone.
[0:3:10] - Recorded real causes: rapid population growth with high dependency, low human development, high poverty.
[0:3:11] - **Not** a main cause: **working efficiency of the population** (APPSC G2 2017).
[0:3:12] - Ending poverty needs **all of**: population control, higher savings and more productive investment.
[0:3:13] # Economists
[0:3:14] TABLE head: Economist | Contribution
  [0:3:14.0] Adam Smith (Scottish) | Father of (Modern) Economics / Capitalism – not Keynes
  [0:3:14.1] J.M. Keynes | Modern macroeconomic thought
  [0:3:14.2] Ragnar Nurkse | Vicious circle of poverty: low income → low saving → low investment → low productivity → low income
  [0:3:14.3] W. Arthur Lewis | Dual-sector model: surplus labour moves from subsistence to modern sector
  [0:3:14.4] Angus Maddison | World GDP share history
  [0:3:14.5] Joan Robinson | 'The Accumulation of Capital' (1956)
[0:3:15] - **Paradox of thrift (J.M. Keynes):** if everyone tries to save more at the same time, spending, income and output fall, so total saving may not rise.
## SUB 4 | Sectors by Activity
[0:4:0] # Primary, Secondary, Tertiary
[0:4:1] TABLE head: Sector | Nature | Examples
  [0:4:1.0] Primary | Uses/extracts natural resources | Agriculture, forestry, fishing, dairy farm, mining and quarrying, mineral exploration
  [0:4:1.1] Secondary | Converts raw materials into goods | Manufacturing, textile mills, weaving cloth, construction, electricity, gas, water supply
  [0:4:1.2] Tertiary (service sector) | Services, not physical goods | Trade, transport, storage (incl. storage of farm produce), communication, banking, real estate, marketing, health, education, social work, personal services
[0:4:2] - Eight industrial divisions: agriculture; mining and quarrying; manufacturing; electricity, gas and water supply; construction; trade; transport and storage; services.
[0:4:3] - Mining is primary but grouped under "industry" in the three-way agriculture/industry/services split.

[0:5:3] - **Gross domestic savings** (descending): **household > private corporate > public corporations > government**.
[0:5:4] - Private corporate investment: **12.3% of GDP (FY23) → 11.2% (FY24)**; its share of GFCF at a decadal low of **33%** (Aug 2026).

## SUB 7 | Dual Economy – Organised vs Unorganised, Rural vs Urban
[0:7:0] - **Organised** = formal, regulated, social security; **unorganised** = informal.
[0:7:1] - Unorganised sector: **over 80% of workers**, about **45% of output**.
[0:7:2] - Organised-sector employment growth **declined** in 1994-2007 (Economic Survey 2009-10).
[0:7:3] - Census 2011: **68.8% rural, 31.2% urban**; rural share of workforce **72.4%**.
[0:7:4] - **Work participation rate 39.8%** (Census 2011) – higher in less developed States (AP, Chhattisgarh, HP, Karnataka, NE) as subsistence work needs manual labour.
[0:7:5] - Regional imbalance: per capita income gaps, poverty ratio, sectoral mix; inter-state Gini of PCI **0.230 (1994-95) → 0.261 (2025-26)**; Telangana's PCI = **1.35 times** AP's (2024-25).
## SUB 8 | Demographic Features
[0:8:0] - Census 2011: **121.09 crore**; sex ratio **940** (933 in 2001); females **48.46%** (provisional; final 48.53%).
[0:8:1] - **TFR**: 2.7 (NFHS-3) → 2.2 (NFHS-4) → 2.0 (NFHS-5) → **1.9** now, below the **replacement level 2.1**.
[0:8:2] - Median age: 24 (2011) → about 29; about **36 by 2036**.
[0:8:3] - Dependency ratio expected to fall from 65% to 54% over a decade; **demographic dividend peaks around 2030**.
