[50:0:0] # Recorded (notified) forest area
[50:0:1] - **Recorded Forest Area (RFA): 37,258 sq km = 22.86%** of AP – **8th in India** (ISFR 2019, 2021, 2023; AP Forest Department, May 2024). "5th" is the usual wrong option.
[50:0:2] - Legal classes: Reserved **85.8%**, Protected **13.6%**, Unclassed **0.6%**; notified forest per APSES 2024-25: 37,221.11 sq km (22.84%); revised 37,421.11 sq km (~22.96%) in 2025-26.
[50:0:3] - Land-use statistics show **36.88 lakh ha (22.63%)** of AP as forest land – the figure some keys give as "area covered by forests".

## SUB 1 | Tree Species, Forest Produce and Plantations
[50:1:0] # Species
[50:1:1] - **Red sanders (*Pterocarpus santalinus*)**: **endemic** to AP's **Seshachalam, Palakonda and Veligonda** ranges (southern Eastern Ghats); a **dry deciduous** tree, **not** a tropical rainforest tree; **greatest economic value**; IUCN **Endangered**, WPA **Schedule IV**, **CITES Appendix II**; thermal-imaging drones against smuggling (Feb 2026).
[50:1:2] - **Axlewood** = ***Anogeissus latifolia*** (dry deciduous).
[50:1:3] - **Eucalyptus** = family **Myrtaceae**; *Cylindrocladium* causes **dieback/blight of *Eucalyptus tereticornis***.
[50:1:4] - Teak – dry deciduous; **bamboo, sandalwood** – northern Eastern Ghats moist hill forests.
[50:1:5] - **Thimmamma Marrimanu:** giant **banyan** at Kadiri (erstwhile Anantapur, now **Sri Sathya Sai** district), about 70 km from Horsley Hills; **Guinness record 1989** as the **largest banyan (canopy) in the world**; about 550 years old; canopy usually quoted as "about 5–6 acres" (Guinness: about 4.7 acres).
[50:1:6] # Revenue and agencies
[50:1:7] - **Forest produce revenue:** older AP keys name **bamboo** as the top earner; red sanders auctions have earned more in recent years. Forest revenue ₹32.81 crore (2023-24), ₹19.63 crore (2024-25 to Dec); NTFP over ₹500 crore a year.
[50:1:8] - **GCC** (HQ Visakhapatnam) buys minor forest produce; **TRIFED** (1987, Multi-State Co-op Societies Act 1984; Tribal Affairs) – MSP for MFP; **APFDC** – export agent for **red sanders and beedi leaf**; **CAMPA** – compensatory afforestation through an Annual Plan of Operation.
[50:1:9] - Wood banks: teak **Kapavaram**, red sanders **Tirupati**, bamboo Rajamahendravaram; craft wood banks (Kondapalli, Etikoppaka, Udayagiri, Bobbili …) give timber to artisans at **no profit**.
[50:1:10] # APFDC plantations (to 2024-25)

  [50:1:11.0] Eucalyptus (clonal) | 33,584 – largest; APFDC is the largest Eucalyptus grower among PSUs
  [50:1:11.1] Cashew | 5,664 (coastal districts, not only agency)
  [50:1:11.2] Coffee | 4,010 – Visakhapatnam and East Godavari
  [50:1:11.3] Bamboo | 2,359
[50:1:12] - **Coffee** in AP grows mainly in the **agency areas of Visakhapatnam (Araku, Paderu, Chintapalli) and East Godavari (Maredumilli)** – not West Godavari; Srikakulam has only small patches.
[50:1:13] # Trees outside forests and programmes
[50:1:14] - **TOFI = Trees Outside Forests in India**: **MoEFCC + USAID**, launched **8 Sep 2022**; AP one of **7** States; AP Forest Department launch **Nov 2022**.
[50:1:15] - **ISFR 2019 rural TOF in AP:** **mango (*Mangifera indica*)** is the most numerous species (about 28–29%).
[50:1:16] - **Vanam-Manam**: green cover outside reserved forests – **5.29 crore** seedlings (2024-25 to Dec).
[50:1:17] - Van Adhiniyam 1980: 26,924 ha diverted to 2017-18; 2026 changes allow commercial plantations on degraded forest land (33% goal). **16th FC** counts open forests with density weights. **Rekapalli revolt (1879)** against British forest laws.
[50:1:18] ~~ Sources: AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.80, p.85 (APFDC, coffee, red sanders export); NextGen AP Geography 02 p.12; Testbook solution to APPSC Group-I 2024 coffee PYQ; The Hindu via inkl (ISFR rural TOF mango ~28%, via web search); app notes 4:50 (sub 6–9)
[50:1:19] - **Rosewood** occurs in the dry deciduous forests of **Kadapa, Chittoor and Nellore** (Seshachalam–Palakonda–Veligonda belt).
[50:1:20] - **State tree of AP:** **Neem** (Vepa) – red sanders is endemic but is not the State tree.
## SUB 2 | Forest Types and Natural Vegetation
[50:2:0] # Types by rainfall
[50:2:1] TABLE head: Forest type | Rainfall | Where
  [50:2:1.0] Moist deciduous | 125–200 cm (maximum rain; India: 100–200 cm) | erstwhile Srikakulam, Visakhapatnam, agency of East Godavari; Papikonda
  [50:2:1.1] Dry deciduous (largest) | 75–100 cm | erstwhile Kadapa, Kurnool; Seshachalam–Nallamala belt
  [50:2:1.2] Short thorn | < 70 cm | Kadapa, Kurnool, Chittoor, Anantapur
  [50:2:1.3] Tidal / mangrove ("Koringa forests") | coastal | mouths of the Godavari and Krishna – Coringa, Krishna WLS; coastal alluvial belt of Krishna district
[50:2:2] - **AP forests are mainly tropical deciduous**; most of the natural vegetation is **dry deciduous**.
[50:2:3] - **Seshachalam hills = dry deciduous** (not wet evergreen, desert or mangrove); **Papikonda NP = moist deciduous**.
[50:2:4] - **ISFR 2019 forest types:** **Southern Dry Mixed Deciduous** is the largest (15,128 sq km), then Dry Deciduous Scrub (12,551); Mangroves and Littoral 418 sq km.
[50:2:5] - **Coringa:** mangrove forest where the **Godavari** meets the Bay of Bengal backwaters; tidal forests were taken up on a large scale in the **6th Five-Year Plan**.
[50:2:6] - **Biggest forest: Nallamala** (dry deciduous; Gundlakamma forest zone part of it).
[50:2:7] - **Biotic provinces:** **Deccan Plateau** is AP's largest (about 53% of forests), then Central Plateau, Eastern Highlands, East Coastal Plains.
[50:2:8] - Forest extent: north **Simhachalam hills**; south **Palakonda and Nallamala**; west **Balaghat**.
[50:2:9] # Mangroves
[50:2:10] - Godavari–Krishna estuaries: called India's **2nd-largest mangrove ecosystem** (APSES) – true only on the **east coast**; by ISFR area AP is behind West Bengal, Gujarat and A&N.
[50:2:11] - ISFR 2023: AP mangrove cover **+13.01 sq km**; **MISHTI** launched **5 June 2023**.
[50:2:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP Geography 02 p.9-12; Rocket AP Geography p.29, p.41-42; AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.80; app notes 4:50 (sub 0, 1)
[50:2:13] - **Dry deciduous species:** teak, *Terminalia*, red sanders and *Anogeissus*; moist deciduous hills give bamboo and sandalwood.
[50:2:14] - **Vegetation pattern:** dense tropical forests in the **Eastern Ghats**; **shrub (scrub) vegetation** is common on the **Deccan plateau** (Rayalaseema); red sanders is endemic to AP.

## SUB 1 | Black Soils (Regur) – Names, Properties, Cotton
[52:1:0] # Names
[52:1:1] TABLE head: Name | Meaning
  [52:1:1.0] Regur / Regadi (Telugu *Reguda*) | black cotton soil
  [52:1:1.1] Vertisols | black soils (USDA order)

[52:1:2] - **⚠ Dispute:** books and keys put black cotton soil in the **Chernozem** group; strictly it is a **Vertisol** – true chernozems are humus-rich grassland soils (Mollisols).
[52:1:3] # Properties
[52:1:4] - **Very fine, clayey**; **very high water (moisture) retention** – the **best soil for retaining moisture**, so it **needs little irrigation** (rain-fed crops last through the dry season).
[52:1:5] - **Swells when wet, develops deep cracks when dry** → "**self-ploughing**".
[52:1:6] - **Rich** in **calcium (carbonate), potash, magnesium (carbonate)**, lime, iron; **poor** in **nitrogen, phosphorus** and humus – so "regur is not rich in **phosphorus**".
[52:1:7] - Colour from **iron and titanium** compounds; neutral to strongly alkaline.
[52:1:8] - AP black soils come from **Deccan Trap basalt** (Prakasam, Palnadu, Guntur) and from **crystalline schists and basic gneiss**; two kinds – formed in place and transported.
[52:1:9] # Where
[52:1:10] - **Deep black soils: Adoni and Aluru (Kurnool)**; unclassified black soils: West Godavari, Krishna, Guntur, mid-Kurnool, NW Kadapa, **Tadipatri**.
[52:1:11] - **Kunderu basin (YSR Kadapa)**: black soils suited to **cash crops**.
[52:1:12] - **North-western Deccan Plateau** = the home of (very deep) black soil; India: Maharashtra (most), MP, Gujarat, Karnataka; Malwa = black lava soil.
[52:1:13] # Crops
[52:1:14] - **Cotton** = the crop best suited to black soil (India and Rayalaseema black soils), because of the **very high water retention**.
[52:1:15] - Also chillies, tobacco, turmeric, jowar, maize, bajra, **black gram**, Bengal gram.
[52:1:16] - "High fertility, cotton, groundnut, pulses" – of the options, **black soil** (red soil grows groundnut and pulses but is poor).

## SUB 2 | Deltaic Alluvium – the Most Fertile Soil
[52:2:0] - Laid as **river-borne silt** (**river deposition**) by the **Godavari and Krishna** (also Penna, Vamsadhara); **renewed with silt in each flood**.
[52:2:1] - **Deltaic alluvial soils** of the Krishna–Godavari deltas = the **most fertile** and **most important soils of AP for agricultural production** (not red, black or laterite).
[52:2:2] - **Konaseema** (Godavari delta) = AP's fertile delta region; Godavari delta around **Rajahmundry–Kakinada**, Krishna delta around **Vijayawada–Machilipatnam**.
[52:2:3] - A–R: the Godavari forms a fertile delta below **Rajahmundry** because it **splits into two streams** (Gautami and Vasishta, at Dowleswaram) – R explains A.
[52:2:4] - Nature: porous, loamy, high moisture retention; **rich in potash and lime**; **poor in nitrogen and humus** – so "alluvial soils are rich in nitrogen" is **not true**; easy to plough, highly fertile.
[52:2:5] - Fertile because they carry **mineral nutrients plants can absorb**; the deltas are **canal-irrigated** for rice and sugarcane – it is **black soil**, not alluvium, that needs little irrigation.
[52:2:6] - India: alluvium covers Punjab, Haryana, UP, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam and the Mahanadi–Godavari–Krishna–Cauvery deltas; suits sugarcane, jute, maize, oilseeds, fruits and vegetables.
[52:2:7] - **Alluvium** = material deposited by flowing water; **Bhangar (Bangar) = old alluvium**, Khadar = new alluvium.
[52:2:8] - **Coastal sands** line the shore; **cashew** (and casuarina, coconut) grow on coastal sandy soils.
[52:2:9] - Crops: **rice and sugarcane**, tobacco, maize, vegetables.

## SUB 3 | Red Soils of Andhra Pradesh
[52:3:0] # Formation and properties
[52:3:1] - Formed by weathering of **old crystalline igneous and metamorphic rocks** – **granite, gneiss, Dharwar schists, mica schists, greenstones** (basalt gives black soil instead).
[52:3:2] - Red colour = **iron oxides (ferric oxide)** – red soils are **rich in iron**.
[52:3:3] - **Poor** in lime, phosphates, **nitrogen**, humus / **organic matter**; fairly rich in **potash**; siliceous, well drained, **poor moisture-holding capacity**.
[52:3:4] - **Rayalaseema** red soils: **shallow**, low moisture retention, low organic matter; red soils are found **primarily in Rayalaseema** and make up **~66% of cultivated regions**.
[52:3:5] # Six sub-groups
[52:3:6] - NextGen: **six sub-categories** – sandy loams with clay in the sub-soil, deep loam sand, silty loams, clay/sand-loam mixtures, sandy loams known as **chalkas**, and **dubba** soils.

[52:3:9] - Local names: **Chalka** = red sandy (loamy) soils; **Dubba** = red loamy sands.
[52:3:10] # Where and crops

## SUB 4 | Laterite and Skeletal Soils
[52:4:0] # Laterite
[52:4:1] - Forms under **high temperature and heavy rainfall** with alternate wet and dry seasons – **intense leaching** removes silica and lime and leaves **hydrated iron and aluminium oxides**; laterite is the "end product of weathering".
[52:4:2] - "Laterites form in semi-arid, low-rainfall regions" = the **incorrect** statement (APPSC Group-II 2024).
[52:4:3] - **~1% of AP**; **acidic** (the most acidic of red, black, alluvial and laterite), **poor fertility**, well drained; red to yellowish red; poor in nitrogen.
[52:4:4] - AP pockets: coastal – **Ellavaram, Peddapuram, Kavali, Kovvur, Gudur, Sullurpeta**; elevated – **Satyavedu, Puttur**; Nellore, Srikakulam, East Godavari, Visakhapatnam.
[52:4:5] - Crops: **mango, cashew, tamarind, coconut** (horticulture); can produce **oilseeds and some legumes**.
[52:4:6] - North Coastal agro-climatic zone is typed by laterite; India: Western and Eastern Ghats, Malabar; not the Thar.
[52:4:7] # Skeletal soils
[52:4:8] - **Infertile**, no nutritive value; on **highly eroded hill slopes and foothills**.
[52:4:9] - Found on the **Nallamala slopes of Badvel (Kadapa), Giddalur (Prakasam)** and a patch at **Jammalamadugu (Kadapa)**.
[52:4:10] > Statements item (APPSC 2022): skeletal-soil places – true; laterite suits horticulture, pulses and oilseeds – true; "most of Rayalaseema is black soil" – false (red), so **I and II only**.
