## SUB 1 | Black Soils (Regur) – Names, Properties, Cotton
[1:0] # Names
[1:1] TABLE head: Name | Meaning
  [1:1.0] Regur / Regadi (Telugu *Reguda*) | black cotton soil
  [1:1.1] Vertisols | black soils (USDA order)
  [1:1.2] Tropical chernozem / tropical black earth | black soil – the "Chernozem group" in exam keys
[1:2] - **⚠ Dispute:** books and keys put black cotton soil in the **Chernozem** group; strictly it is a **Vertisol** – true chernozems are humus-rich grassland soils (Mollisols).
[1:3] # Properties
[1:4] - **Very fine, clayey**; **very high water (moisture) retention** – the **best soil for retaining moisture**, so it **needs little irrigation** (rain-fed crops last through the dry season).
[1:5] - **Swells when wet, develops deep cracks when dry** → "**self-ploughing**".
[1:6] - **Rich** in **calcium (carbonate), potash, magnesium (carbonate)**, lime, iron; **poor** in **nitrogen, phosphorus** and humus – so "regur is not rich in **phosphorus**".
[1:7] - Colour from **iron and titanium** compounds; neutral to strongly alkaline.
[1:8] - AP black soils come from **Deccan Trap basalt** (Prakasam, Palnadu, Guntur) and from **crystalline schists and basic gneiss**; two kinds – formed in place and transported.
[1:9] # Where
[1:10] - **Deep black soils: Adoni and Aluru (Kurnool)**; unclassified black soils: West Godavari, Krishna, Guntur, mid-Kurnool, NW Kadapa, **Tadipatri**.
[1:11] - **Kunderu basin (YSR Kadapa)**: black soils suited to **cash crops**.
[1:12] - **North-western Deccan Plateau** = the home of (very deep) black soil; India: Maharashtra (most), MP, Gujarat, Karnataka; Malwa = black lava soil.
[1:13] # Crops
[1:14] - **Cotton** = the crop best suited to black soil (India and Rayalaseema black soils), because of the **very high water retention**.
[1:15] - Also chillies, tobacco, turmeric, jowar, maize, bajra, **black gram**, Bengal gram.
[1:16] - "High fertility, cotton, groundnut, pulses" – of the options, **black soil** (red soil grows groundnut and pulses but is poor).

## SUB 2 | Red Soils of Andhra Pradesh
[2:0] # Formation and properties
[2:1] - Formed by weathering of **old crystalline igneous and metamorphic rocks** – **granite, gneiss, Dharwar schists, mica schists, greenstones** (basalt gives black soil instead).
[2:2] - Red colour = **iron oxides (ferric oxide)** – red soils are **rich in iron**.
[2:3] - **Poor** in lime, phosphates, **nitrogen**, humus / **organic matter**; fairly rich in **potash**; siliceous, well drained, **poor moisture-holding capacity**.
[2:4] - **Rayalaseema** red soils: **shallow**, low moisture retention, low organic matter; red soils are found **primarily in Rayalaseema** and make up **~66% of cultivated regions**.
[2:5] # Six sub-groups
[2:6] - NextGen: **six sub-categories** – sandy loams with clay in the sub-soil, deep loam sand, silty loams, clay/sand-loam mixtures, sandy loams known as **chalkas**, and **dubba** soils.
[2:7] TABLE head: Sub-type | Share of AP (APPSC FRO key)
  [2:7.0] Red earths with loamy sub-soil | 30% (largest)
  [2:7.1] Red soils with clay base | 12%
  [2:7.2] Red loamy | 9%
  [2:7.3] Red sandy (chalka) | 8%
  [2:7.4] Deep red loamy | 3%
[2:8] - **⚠ Dispute:** the FRO statement "red **sandy** soil covers most of the state" was keyed true (all four statements), though the sub-type shares make **loamy-sub-soil red earth (30%)** the largest.
[2:9] - Local names: **Chalka** = red sandy (loamy) soils; **Dubba** = red loamy sands.
[2:10] # Where and crops
[2:11] - Places: Rayalaseema plateau; Krishna–Palnadu uplands (**Nuziveedu, Vinukonda**); **Visakhapatnam** district – **red loamy** soils.
[2:12] - Crops: **groundnut and pulses** (red, green, horse gram), castor, millets – not cotton (black) or rice (alluvium).

## SUB 3 | Deltaic Alluvium – the Most Fertile Soil
[3:0] - Laid as **river-borne silt** (**river deposition**) by the **Godavari and Krishna** (also Penna, Vamsadhara); **renewed with silt in each flood**.
[3:1] - **Deltaic alluvial soils** of the Krishna–Godavari deltas = the **most fertile** and **most important soils of AP for agricultural production** (not red, black or laterite).
[3:2] - **Konaseema** (Godavari delta) = AP's fertile delta region; Godavari delta around **Rajahmundry–Kakinada**, Krishna delta around **Vijayawada–Machilipatnam**.
[3:3] - A–R: the Godavari forms a fertile delta below **Rajahmundry** because it **splits into two streams** (Gautami and Vasishta, at Dowleswaram) – R explains A.
[3:4] - Nature: porous, loamy, high moisture retention; **rich in potash and lime**; **poor in nitrogen and humus** – so "alluvial soils are rich in nitrogen" is **not true**; easy to plough, highly fertile.
[3:5] - Fertile because they carry **mineral nutrients plants can absorb**; the deltas are **canal-irrigated** for rice and sugarcane – it is **black soil**, not alluvium, that needs little irrigation.
[3:6] - India: alluvium covers Punjab, Haryana, UP, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam and the Mahanadi–Godavari–Krishna–Cauvery deltas; suits sugarcane, jute, maize, oilseeds, fruits and vegetables.
[3:7] - **Alluvium** = material deposited by flowing water; **Bhangar (Bangar) = old alluvium**, Khadar = new alluvium.
[3:8] - **Coastal sands** line the shore; **cashew** (and casuarina, coconut) grow on coastal sandy soils.
[3:9] - Crops: **rice and sugarcane**, tobacco, maize, vegetables.
[3:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP Geography 02 p.7 (book p.34); NextGen AP Geography 03 p.22 (book p.76); NextGen AP Geography 01 p.23 (PYQ key: Bangar); app notes 4:52
## SUB 4 | Laterite and Skeletal Soils
[4:0] # Laterite
[4:1] - Forms under **high temperature and heavy rainfall** with alternate wet and dry seasons – **intense leaching** removes silica and lime and leaves **hydrated iron and aluminium oxides**; laterite is the "end product of weathering".
[4:2] - "Laterites form in semi-arid, low-rainfall regions" = the **incorrect** statement (APPSC Group-II 2024).
[4:3] - **~1% of AP**; **acidic** (the most acidic of red, black, alluvial and laterite), **poor fertility**, well drained; red to yellowish red; poor in nitrogen.
[4:4] - AP pockets: coastal – **Ellavaram, Peddapuram, Kavali, Kovvur, Gudur, Sullurpeta**; elevated – **Satyavedu, Puttur**; Nellore, Srikakulam, East Godavari, Visakhapatnam.
[4:5] - Crops: **mango, cashew, tamarind, coconut** (horticulture); can produce **oilseeds and some legumes**.
[4:6] - North Coastal agro-climatic zone is typed by laterite; India: Western and Eastern Ghats, Malabar; not the Thar.
[4:7] # Skeletal soils
[4:8] - **Infertile**, no nutritive value; on **highly eroded hill slopes and foothills**.
[4:9] - Found on the **Nallamala slopes of Badvel (Kadapa), Giddalur (Prakasam)** and a patch at **Jammalamadugu (Kadapa)**.
[4:10] > Statements item (APPSC 2022): skeletal-soil places – true; laterite suits horticulture, pulses and oilseeds – true; "most of Rayalaseema is black soil" – false (red), so **I and II only**.
[4:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP Geography 03 p.23 (book p.77); NextGen AP Geography 02 p.8 (book p.35); app notes 4:52

[5:5] # Problem soils
[5:6] - **Acid soils → lime**; **alkaline soils → gypsum or sulphur** (they **decrease alkalinity**); **saline soils → drainage and leaching**.
[5:7] - Deltas: salinity and waterlogging; Rayalaseema plateau: saline soils high in lime and salt – **unfit for cropping**; laterite tracts acidic; black-soil tracts alkaline.
[5:8] - **Salinity** lowers yields and breaks soil structure; **alkalinity** (high pH) cuts nutrient supply.
[5:9] - **39 watersheds (896 villages)** classed saline (2025-26); India: Gujarat largest saline area, UP most alkali land; halophytes grow on saline soils.
[5:10] - Coastal erosion at **Uppada (Kakinada)** from sea-level rise and waves.
[5:11] # Degradation and remedies
[5:12] - Anantapur, Sri Sathya Sai: drought, desertification, wind erosion; **Podu** strips topsoil in **ASR and Parvathipuram Manyam**; heavy chemicals in the Guntur chilli and Krishna paddy belts; overgrazing, deforestation. Fresh delta silt is **not** a cause of degradation.
[5:13] - **APCNF** natural farming restores soil organic carbon; **Rainfed Area Development**: **9 rainfed districts**, **Rs 30,000 per farm family**.
[5:14] - **Soil Health Card**: launched **19 Feb 2015, Suratgarh**; since **2022-23** "Soil Health & Fertility" under **RKVY**; soil-sample analysis and village-wise nutrient mapping; AP **1,51,61,346 cards** (about **1.52 crore**, 30 Jan 2025).
[5:15] - Soil terms: **edaphic** = soil factor; ideal pH **6–7**; arid soil has low moisture retention.
