=== Jallianwala Bagh Massacre – 13 April 1919
# The Incident
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Place | **Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar (Punjab)** – an enclosed ground, buildings on all sides, one narrow exit |
| Date | **13 April 1919** – **Baisakhi** day ("black day" of Indian history) |
| Context | **Rowlatt Satyagraha** – protest against the **Rowlatt Act** (passed March 1919) |
| Officer who ordered firing | **Brig.-Gen. Reginald Dyer** |
| Lt. Governor of Punjab | **Sir Michael O'Dwyer** (1912-19) – backed Dyer |
| Viceroy | **Lord Chelmsford** (1916-21; also Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms / GoI Act 1919) |
- **Why the crowd gathered:** to protest the arrest (**9 April 1919**) of the two Amritsar Congress leaders **Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr Satyapal** and the Rowlatt Act; most of the ~20,000 had come from villages for the **Baisakhi** fair, unaware of Dyer's ban on meetings.
- 10 April: protestors marching for the leaders' release were fired on → riots, five Englishmen killed, missionary **Marcella Sherwood** assaulted.
- Dyer **blocked the exit**, gave **no warning**, fired on an **unarmed, non-violent crowd** for 10-15 minutes – **1,650 rounds**, until the ammunition ran out.
- Casualties: **official 379 dead** (~1,100-1,200 wounded); Congress estimate **about 1,000-1,200 dead**.
# Martial Law in Punjab
- Martial law proclaimed in **Punjab** (Amritsar, **Lahore**, Gujranwala …): **public floggings**, **aircraft strafing**.
- **Crawling order**: Indians made to **crawl on their bellies** down the Amritsar lane where **Marcella Sherwood** had been assaulted.
# Impact
- **Faith in British justice declined**; hostility to British rule deepened; a turning point for Gandhi – background to the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)**.
> Dyer = the officer who fired; O'Dwyer = Lt. Governor; Chelmsford = Viceroy. "General Dyer" PYQs always point to Jallianwala Bagh.

=== Rowlatt Act, 1919
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Official name | **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919** |
| Popular names | **Rowlatt Act**; **"Black Act"** (Gandhi); "**No Vakil, No Dalil, No Appeal**" |
| Committee behind it | **Sedition Committee** (Rowlatt Committee), appointed **1917**, headed by **Justice Sidney Rowlatt**; reported 1918 |
| Bills | Two bills in the Imperial Legislative Council, **6 February 1919** – one dropped, one passed |
| Passed | **March 1919** (10 March) |
| Basis | Permanent replacement / extension of the **Defence of India Act, 1915** |
| Repealed | Under Viceroy **Lord Reading** (1921-26), along with the **Press Act, 1910** |
- **Aim:** to crush **revolutionary nationalists** and curb national / revolutionary activities – a perceived threat from revolutionaries, not press control, the Congress or constitutional reform.
- **Provisions:** **arrest without warrant**; **detention / imprisonment without trial for up to two years**; in-camera trial by a special court of **three High Court judges**, **no appeal**; evidence not admissible under the Evidence Act; **habeas corpus** suspended.
- Why it caused popular indignation: it **authorised imprisonment without trial** and **limited individual liberty** – it had nothing to do with religion, education, trade unions or the press as such.
- Passed despite opposition of **every Indian member** of the Imperial Legislative Council; **Jinnah, Madan Mohan Malaviya, Mazhar-ul-Haq** resigned in protest.
- **Mohammad Ali and Abul Kalam Azad did not resign from the Council in 1916** – they were interned during the war (UPSC 2003 A-R: A false, R true).
- **1919 = Gandhi's entry into active all-India politics** (returned from South Africa 1915) – he led the protest against this Act.

=== Rowlatt Satyagraha
# 1919 in Dates
| Date (1919) | Event |
|---|---|
| 24 February | **Satyagraha Sabha** formed at **Bombay** – mostly young radicals of the **Home Rule League**, plus Pan-Islamists |
| March | Rowlatt Act passed |
| **6 April** | Nationwide **hartal** – fasting and prayer (first all-India mass strike) |
| 8 April | Gandhi at Vijayawada |
| **9 April** | **Kitchlew and Satyapal arrested** in Amritsar |
| **13 April** | **Jallianwala Bagh massacre** |
| **18 April** | Satyagraha **withdrawn** after violence (Punjab, Ahmedabad) – Gandhi's "**Himalayan blunder**" |
| 31 May | Tagore renounced knighthood |
| 14 October | Hunter Committee announced |
| December | **Amritsar Congress session** (Motilal Nehru) |
# Nature
- Gandhi's **first all-India struggle**; he **used the Home Rule League** network; mainly **urban**.
- The **Sedition Committee** was the basis of the Act; the **Simon Commission (1928)** protests did **not** coincide with it (UPSC 2015: 1 and 2 only).
- The massacre happened **in the context of the Rowlatt Satyagraha**; the event that immediately preceded it = **enactment of the Rowlatt Act** (and the 6 April hartal).
- A **no-tax campaign** against the Act was suggested by **Swami Shraddhanand** (Arya Samaj leader who led the Delhi protests).
# Andhra Response
- 6 April hartal observed peacefully at **Visakhapatnam, Rajahmundry, Guntur, Vijayawada** (also Nellore).
- Gandhi's speech at **Vijayawada (8 April 1919)** was translated by **Ayyadevara Kaleswara Rao**.
- **Andhra Patrika** and **Krishna Patrika** opposed the Act.
- **Ganjam, Godavari and Krishna** district conferences condemned the massacre; **Prakasam** (Nellore meeting, Sept 1919) demanded fundamental rights.
- The **Guntur Bar Association** (under **Konda Venkatappayya**) demanded the jailing of General Dyer.

=== Hunter Committee and the Congress Inquiry
# Hunter Committee (official)
- Official name **Disorders Inquiry Committee**; announced **14 October 1919** by the Government of India (ordered by Montagu).
- Chairman **Lord William Hunter** (former Solicitor-General for Scotland).
- Probed the **Jallianwala Bagh massacre** and the disturbances in **Bombay, Delhi and Punjab**.
- **Eight members – five British, three Indian** (Rocket Sheets say 7 = 4 + 3): **Chimanlal H. Setalvad**, **Pandit Jagat Narayan**, **Sultan Ahmed Khan**; the Indians wrote a **minority report** more critical of Dyer.
- Report (March 1920): Dyer's action an **"error of judgement"** / grave error; Dyer **censured and relieved of command**, but **no penal action**; Indemnity Act had already protected officers.
- Gandhi called the findings a **"whitewash"**; the Amritsar Congress (Dec 1919) resolved to **boycott** it; the **House of Lords** backed Dyer; the Morning Post raised a fund for him.
# Congress Inquiry Committee (non-official)
- Congress **Punjab Sub-Committee**: **Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Abbas Tyabji, M.R. Jayakar, Gandhi**.
- Report ("The Congress Punjab Inquiry", 1920) **drafted by Gandhi**: firing **inhuman**; official toll (379) rejected – about 1,200 dead.
| Point | Hunter Committee | Congress committee |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Official | Non-official |
| Members | Hunter + 3 Indians (Setalvad, Jagat Narayan, Sultan Ahmed) | Motilal, C.R. Das, Tyabji, Jayakar, Gandhi |
| Verdict | "Error of judgement" | Firing inhuman |
> Hunter **Committee (1919)** ≠ Hunter **Education Commission (1882)**.

=== Udham Singh
- Shot **Sir Michael O'Dwyer** (Lt. Governor of Punjab in 1919, who had endorsed Dyer) on **13 March 1940** at **Caxton Hall, London** – **not General Dyer** (Dyer died in 1927).
- He had witnessed the Amritsar events; alias **Ram Mohammad Singh Azad**; hanged in 1940.
- Gandhi called the act "an act of insanity".
