=== NOTES 1:233 | Rowlatt Satyagraha and Jallianwala Bagh; Hunter Committee
## SUB 0 | Rowlatt Act, 1919
[233:0:0] # Name and Origin
[233:0:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [233:0:1.0] Official name | Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919
  [233:0:1.1] Popular names | "Black Act" / "Black Law" (Gandhi's term)
  [233:0:1.2] Committee behind it | Sedition Committee (Rowlatt Committee), appointed 1917, headed by Justice Sidney Rowlatt
  [233:0:1.3] Bills presented | Two bills in the Imperial Legislative Council, 6 February 1919 – one dropped, one passed
  [233:0:1.4] Passed | 10 March 1919
  [233:0:1.5] Basis | Extension of the Defence of India Act, 1915
  [233:0:1.6] Repealed | Under Viceroy Lord Reading, along with the Press Act, 1910
[233:0:2] # Provisions Opposed
[233:0:3] - **Arrest without warrant** and **detention without trial** up to **two years**; summary procedures.
[233:0:4] - **In-camera (secret) trials** by a **three-judge High Court panel**, **no appeal**; special tribunals.
[233:0:5] - Aim: curb national and revolutionary activities; punish dissent.
[233:0:6] - Slogan: **"No Vakil, No Dalil, No Appeal"** – an Act without lawyer, plea or appeal.
[233:0:7] - Passed despite opposition of **all Indian members** of the Imperial Legislative Council.
[233:0:8] - **Jinnah, Madan Mohan Malaviya, Mazhar-ul-Haq** resigned from the Council in protest (they did not draft the Hunter report).
[233:0:9] The claim that Mohammad Ali and Abul Kalam Azad resigned from the Council in 1916 is FALSE.
## SUB 1 | Rowlatt Satyagraha
[233:1:0] # 1919 in Dates
[233:1:1] TABLE head: Date (1919) | Event
  [233:1:1.0] 24 February | Gandhi warned; Satyagraha Sabha formed at Bombay (using Home Rule League networks)
  [233:1:1.1] 10 March | Rowlatt Act passed
  [233:1:1.2] 6 April | Nationwide hartal – fasting, prayer, "national humiliation"
  [233:1:1.3] 8 April | Gandhi at Vijayawada
  [233:1:1.4] 9 April | Kitchlew and Satyapal arrested (not 13 April)
  [233:1:1.5] 13 April | Jallianwala Bagh massacre; martial law in Amritsar and Lahore
  [233:1:1.6] 18 April | Satyagraha withdrawn – violence in Punjab and Ahmedabad; "Himalayan blunder"
  [233:1:1.7] 31 May | Tagore renounced knighthood
  [233:1:1.8] 14 October | Hunter Committee set up
  [233:1:1.9] 26-30 December | Amritsar Congress session
[233:1:2] # Nature of the Struggle
[233:1:3] - Gandhi's **first all-India campaign**; **first all-India mass strike**; **urban-centred** (not limited to rural Punjab).
[233:1:4] - Gandhi tried to use the **Home Rule League** for it.
[233:1:5] - A **no-tax campaign** against the Act was suggested by **Swami Shraddhanand**.
[233:1:6] Event just before the massacre = enactment of the Rowlatt Act (March 1919) and the country-wide strike of 6 April 1919.
[233:1:7] # Andhra Response
[233:1:8] - Peaceful hartals at **Vizag, Rajahmundry, Guntur, Vijayawada, Nellore**.
[233:1:9] - Gandhi's speech at **Vijayawada (8 April 1919)** translated by **Ayyadevara Kaleswara Rao**.
[233:1:10] - **Andhra Patrika** and **Krishna Patrika** opposed the Act.
[233:1:11] - **Ganjam, Godavari and Krishna** district conferences condemned the massacre; **Prakasam** (Nellore meeting, Sept 1919) demanded fundamental rights.
[233:1:12] - The **Guntur Bar Association** (under **Konda Venkatappayya**) demanded the jailing of General Dyer.
## SUB 2 | Jallianwala Bagh Massacre – 13 April 1919
[233:2:0] # The Incident
[233:2:1] - Place: **Amritsar, Punjab**; date: **13 April 1919**; occasion: **Baisakhi**.
[233:2:2] - Reason for the gathering: to protest the arrest of **Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew** and **Dr Satyapal** (Punjab leaders, arrested **9 April 1919**) and the Rowlatt Act; also to celebrate Baisakhi.
[233:2:3] - **Brig.-Gen. Reginald Dyer** gave **no warning**, **blocked the only exit**, fired for about **10 minutes**, **1,650 rounds**.
[233:2:4] TABLE head: Office | Person
  [233:2:4.0] Viceroy | Lord Chelmsford (his tenure also saw the Government of India Act, 1919 – dyarchy)
  [233:2:4.1] Lt. Governor of Punjab | Sir Michael O'Dwyer
  [233:2:4.2] Officer who ordered firing | Brig.-Gen. Reginald Dyer
[233:2:5] - Casualties: **official 379 dead, 1,100 injured**; Congress estimated more.
[233:2:6] - Happened in the **context of the Rowlatt Satyagraha**.
[233:2:7] O'Dwyer = Lt. Governor; Dyer = officer who fired.
## SUB 3 | Martial Law in Punjab and the Crawling Order
[233:3:0] - Martial law in **Amritsar and Lahore** under **O'Dwyer's** regime.
[233:3:1] - **Public floggings** and **aircraft strafing** of protestors.
[233:3:2] - **Crawling order**: Indians had to **crawl on their bellies** through the Amritsar lane where English missionary **Marcella Sherwood** had been assaulted.
## SUB 4 | Protests, Resignations and Renunciations
[233:4:0] TABLE head: Person | Protest
  [233:4:0.0] Rabindranath Tagore | Gave up Knighthood ("Sir"), returned on 31 May 1919
  [233:4:0.1] Sir C. Sankaran Nair | Resigned from the Viceroy's Executive Council – the only Indian there
  [233:4:0.2] Gandhi | Returned the Kaiser-i-Hind medal
  [233:4:0.3] Montagu | Called the action "Preventive Murder"
[233:4:1] - Tagore gave up knighthood **in protest at the massacre**, not to join the Non-Cooperation Movement.
[233:4:2] - Sankaran Nair later **lost O'Dwyer's libel suit (11-1)**.
[233:4:3] - **Apr 2025**: PM recalled Sir C. Sankaran Nair's resignation on the **106th anniversary**.
[233:4:4] - **17 Jul 2026**: **K. Kamaraj** (birth anniversary) joined the freedom struggle after Jallianwala Bagh.
[233:4:5] Of the usual list, **only Sankaran Nair resigned from the Viceroy's Executive Council**.
## SUB 5 | Hunter Committee (official Inquiry)
[233:5:0] - Also called the **Disorders Inquiry Committee**; set up **14 October 1919** by the government.
[233:5:1] - Probed the **Jallianwala Bagh massacre** and disturbances in **Bombay, Delhi and Punjab**.
[233:5:2] - Chairman: **Lord William Hunter**; **8 members – 5 British, 3 Indian**.
[233:5:3] - **British members: Lord Hunter, Justice G.C. Rankin, W.F. Rice, Maj.-Gen. George Barrow, Thomas Smith**; the five British members wrote the **majority report**.
[233:5:4] - **Indian members: Chimanlal H. Setalvad, Pandit Jagat Narayan, Sultan Ahmed Khan** – wrote a **minority report** more critical of Dyer.
[233:5:5] - Called Dyer's action **"an error of judgement"**; Dyer censured and **relieved of command**, but no real punishment.
[233:5:6] - **House of Lords backed Dyer**; Gandhi called the findings a **"whitewash"**.
[233:5:7] "Error of judgement" = Hunter Committee; "Preventive murder" = Montagu.

## SUB 7 | Udham Singh
[233:7:0] - Shot **Sir Michael O'Dwyer** (former Lt. Governor of Punjab) on **13 March 1940** at **Caxton Hall, London**.
[233:7:1] - He had witnessed the Amritsar massacre and was wounded there.
[233:7:2] Udham Singh's target was **O'Dwyer, not Dyer**.
## SUB 8 | Impact
[233:8:0] - Faith in British justice declined; hostility to British rule deepened.
[233:8:1] - **Turning point** in Gandhi's career; background to the **Khilafat-Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)**.
