=== NOTES 1:154 | English East India Company – Charter of 1600, Surat, Madras, Bombay, Calcutta; Farman of 1717
## SUB 0 | Foundation and the Charter of 1600
[154:0:0] # The Charter
[154:0:1] - Established **1600** as an English **joint-stock trading company**.
[154:0:2] - Royal charter granted by **Queen Elizabeth I** on **31 December 1600** (APPSC-G2 2025: EIC founded 1600).
[154:0:3] - Official name: "**The Governor and Company of Merchants of London Trading into the East Indies**".
[154:0:4] - Formed by the group "**Merchant Adventurers**" (association c.1599).
[154:0:5] - **15-year monopoly** of East Indies trade, later made **perpetual** – between the **Cape of Good Hope** and the **Magellan Straits**.
[154:0:6] - Gave **trading rights only**, not territorial power.
[154:0:7] APPSC-GS 2024 key: the 1600 Charter gave **exclusive trading rights** – not territorial governance or legislative power.
[154:0:8] # Mughal Emperor in 1600
[154:0:9] - Emperor of India when the Company was formed = **Akbar** (reigned **1556-1605**), a contemporary of **Elizabeth I** (1558-1603).
[154:0:10] The Company was formed under Akbar, not Jahangir; but its first factories (1611, 1613) came under **Jahangir**.
[154:0:11] # Organisation and Powers
[154:0:12] TABLE head: Body | Role
  [154:0:12.0] Governor + 24-member committee | Later the Court of Directors (elected annually)
  [154:0:12.1] Shareholders = General Court | Later the Court of Proprietors
  [154:0:12.2] Nature | Private joint-stock company vs state-run Portuguese and French ventures
[154:0:13] # Later Charters (1600-1708)
[154:0:14] TABLE head: Year / ruler | Change
  [154:0:14.0] 1657 – Cromwell | Charter for a permanent joint stock
  [154:0:14.1] Charles II, James II | Delegated sovereignty: coin money, jurisdiction, war and peace with non-Christians
  [154:0:14.2] 1708 | Rival English companies merged (United Company)
[154:0:15] - **First voyage**: **1602** under **James Lancaster** (capital **£68,000**) → factory at **Bantam**.
[154:0:16] - **Levant Company (1592)**: first British company chartered to trade with the **Ottoman Empire** and eastern areas incl. India (silk, spices).
[154:0:17] - Early objective: **eastern trade**, especially **spices and textiles**.
[154:0:18] # Significance
[154:0:19] - A **trading company (1600)** turned into a **ruling power** after **Plassey (1757)** and **Buxar (1764)**.
[154:0:20] - Territorial rule over **Bengal** came only after 1757; Madras and Bombay stayed trading settlements before that.
[154:0:21] - Its rule ended with the **Government of India Act, 1858**, when the Crown took over.
[154:0:22] - The **Andhra coast** (Machilipatnam 1611, Armagaon 1626, Madras 1639 on land from the Chandragiri ruler) was its first home in the south.

## SUB 4 | The English on the Andhra Coast
[154:4:0] # Machilipatnam Arrival
[154:4:1] - **1611**: ship "**Globe**" under **Captain Anthony Hippon** reached Machilipatnam → **first English factory in Andhra** (APPSC G2 2025 key).
[154:4:2] - Permission from **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** of Golconda (ruled **1580-1612**) – fits the date.
[154:4:3] APPSC-G2 2025 A-R: "Globe under Captain **Hawkins**" – A is false; it was **Hippon** (⚠ APPSC key (2025) gave both A and R true, R explains A – standard sources name Hippon). APPSC G2 2008 key named Ibrahim Qutb Shah, but Ibrahim died in **1580** – correct answer is Muhammad Quli.
[154:4:4] # Moves Along the Coast
[154:4:5] - 1. **Dutch rivalry** + harassment by Golconda officials.
[154:4:6] - 2. **Pulicat (1621)**, Chandragiri king – abandoned due to **Dutch hostility**.
[154:4:7] - 3. **Armagaon (1626)** – sole shelter on the east coast for a time.
[154:4:8] - 4. Back to Machilipatnam after the **Golden Farman**.
[154:4:9] - 5. Founded **Madras (1639)**.
[154:4:10] TABLE head: Year | Andhra settlement
  [154:4:10.0] 1611 | Machilipatnam (1st)
  [154:4:10.1] 1621 | Pulicat (2nd)
  [154:4:10.2] 1626 | Armagaon (3rd) – Dugarajapatnam, now Tirupati district; 35 miles north of Pulicat
  [154:4:10.3] 1682 | Visakhapatnam factory
[154:4:11] - Other factories: **Nizampatnam, Narsapuram, Veeravasaram, Bheemunipatnam**.
[154:4:12] - **British factory in Nellore district** = **1626** (Armagaon).
[154:4:13] - Andhra coast = EIC's **first home in the south**.
[154:4:14] # Golden Farman
[154:4:15] - From the **Sultan of Golconda** (**Abdullah Qutb Shah**), **1632**.
[154:4:16] - Privilege: **free trade in Golconda ports** for a fixed **500 pagodas a year**.
[154:4:17] - Brought the English **back to Machilipatnam**.
## SUB 5 | Madras – Fort St. George
[154:5:0] - Founded **1639** by **Francis Day** (with **Andrew Cogan**) at **Madraspatnam**.
[154:5:1] - Grant from the **Chandragiri** ruler **Venkatapati Raya III**, with efforts of the **Damerla brothers** (**Venkatadri** and **Ayyappa**).
[154:5:2] - **Fort St. George** = **first fort built by the British in India** (1639-40); completed on **St George's Day, 23 April 1644**.
[154:5:3] - Seat of the **Madras Presidency**; some books call Madras the Company's HQ in India.
[154:5:4] - **Madras = India's first municipality (1687)**; **Bhimili = Andhra's first (1861)**.
[154:5:5] Madras | 1639 | Francis Day
Calcutta | 1690 | Job Charnock
## SUB 6 | Bombay – Acquisition from the Portuguese
[154:6:0] - 1. **1661**: **dowry** of **Catherine of Braganza** (Portuguese princess) to **Charles II**.
[154:6:1] - 2. **1668**: Charles II **leased** Bombay to the Company for **£10 a year**.
[154:6:2] - 3. Bombay **replaced Surat** as the chief west-coast settlement.
[154:6:3] Bombay transfer to the Company = **1668** (not 1688).
## SUB 7 | Calcutta – Job Charnock and the Three Villages
[154:7:0] - **Job Charnock**, chief of the **Qasim Bazar** factory, chose **Sutanuti** over Hooghly; arrived **1690** → **founder of Calcutta**.
[154:7:1] - **1691** imperial order: trade in Bengal for **Rs 3,000 a year** in lieu of dues.
[154:7:2] - **1698**: Company bought the zamindari of **Sutanuti, Gobindapur and Kalikata**.
[154:7:3] - **1700**: **Fort William** – nucleus of Calcutta; **Presidency of Fort William in Bengal**.
[154:7:4] - **Sutanuti** = English trading centre and Calcutta base; **Batavia** was the Dutch eastern centre.
[154:7:5] TABLE head: Presidency | Seat
  [154:7:5.0] Madras | Fort St. George
  [154:7:5.1] Bombay | Bombay (replaced Surat)
  [154:7:5.2] Bengal | Fort William, Calcutta
## SUB 8 | Farman of 1717 – Farrukhsiyar
[154:8:0] - Issued by Emperor **Farrukhsiyar**; mission led by **John Surman**.
[154:8:1] - Called the "**Magna Carta**" of the Company's trade; increased trading privileges.
[154:8:2] TABLE head: Region | Privilege
  [154:8:2.0] Bengal | Duty-free trade for Rs 3,000 a year; right to issue dastaks
  [154:8:2.1] Surat | All duties dropped for Rs 10,000 a year
[154:8:3] # From Dastak Misuse to Rule
[154:8:4] - 1. **Dastaks** misused by Company servants for **private trade**.
[154:8:5] - 2. Nawab of Bengal's **revenue drained** – a deeper cause of **Plassey**.
[154:8:6] - 3. **Plassey (1757)** + **Diwani (1765)** after **Buxar (1764)** → **Bengal** = first major territorial base.
[154:8:7] - 4. **Regulating Act 1773**; Company rule ended by the **GoI Act 1858**.
[154:8:8] Madras and Bombay were trading settlements; **Bengal** became the first major territorial base, only after 1757.

=== NOTES 1:155 | French in India – Colbert, Compagnie Des Indes, Pondicherry, Dupleix and His Policy
## SUB 0 | French East India Company
[155:0:0] - Founded **1664** on the recommendation of **Jean-Baptiste Colbert**, **Finance Minister** of **Louis XIV**.
[155:0:1] - Name: **Compagnie des Indes Orientales** ("Compagnie Française pour le Commerce des Indes Orientales").
[155:0:2] - Charter: **50-year monopoly** in the **Indian and Pacific Oceans** + rights over **Madagascar**.
[155:0:3] - **State-owned**: the French state was the largest shareholder and paid most costs; dependent on **subsidies** → vulnerable.
[155:0:4] - **Last European company to arrive** in India.
[155:0:5] Founder = **Colbert** (minister), not Louis XIV himself and not Dupleix.
## SUB 1 | French Settlements – Order of Founding
[155:1:0] TABLE head: Settlement | Year | Detail
  [155:1:0.0] Surat | 1668 | First (earliest) French factory, under François Caron
  [155:1:0.1] Masulipatnam | 1669 | Caron + Marcara; permission of Abdullah Qutb Shah
  [155:1:0.2] Pondicherry | 1673-74 | François Martin
  [155:1:0.3] Chandernagore | 1673 / 1690 | Permission of Shaista Khan (1673); settled 1690
  [155:1:0.4] Yanam | 1723 | Godavari delta; re-occupied 1731; sovereignty confirmed 1750
  [155:1:0.5] Mahe | 1725 | Malabar coast
  [155:1:0.6] Karaikal | 1739 | Coromandel coast
[155:1:1] APPSC-G1 key order: **Surat → Pondicherry → Mahe → Karaikal → Yanam** (Yanam formally ceded c.1750).
[155:1:2] **Mahe** is on the Malabar coast; **Chandernagore** is in Bengal, not Malabar.
## SUB 2 | Pondicherry
[155:2:0] # Founding
[155:2:1] - **1673**: land grant by **Sher Khan Lodi** (Bijapur governor of **Valikondapuram**) to **François Martin** (director at Masulipatnam).
[155:2:2] - **1674**: Pondicherry founded; **Fort St. Louis** built – French **headquarters** in India; became the most important French settlement.
[155:2:3] # Changes of Control
[155:2:4] TABLE head: Year | Change
  [155:2:4.0] 1693 | Captured by the Dutch (with English support)
  [155:2:4.1] 1697 | Restored by the Treaty of Ryswick (APPSC-G1 2018 key); Dutch stayed two more years
  [155:2:4.2] 1761 | Taken by the British after Wandiwash
  [155:2:4.3] 1763 | Restored by the Treaty of Paris
[155:2:5] Ryswick 1697 | restored from Dutch | Pondicherry
Paris 1763 | restored from British | Pondicherry
## SUB 3 | French Governors in Order

  [155:3:0.0] François Martin | First French governor; builder of Pondicherry; died 31 Dec 1706

  [155:3:0.3] Godeheu (1754) | Replaced Dupleix; recalled at the start of the Third Carnatic War
  [155:3:0.4] Count de Lally | Lost at Wandiwash (1760) to Eyre Coote
## SUB 4 | Dupleix and His Policy of Intervention
[155:4:0] - **Governor-General of French India (1742-54)**; **first European to interfere in Indian princes' quarrels** to gain territory.
[155:4:1] - First used **trained Indian sepoys** and strategic alliances.
[155:4:2] - After **Nizam-ul-Mulk's death (1748)** he used two succession disputes:
[155:4:3] TABLE head: Region | Dispute
  [155:4:3.0] Hyderabad | Nasir Jang vs Muzaffar Jang
  [155:4:3.1] Carnatic | Anwaruddin vs Chanda Sahib
[155:4:4] - **Battle of Ambur (1749)**: Dupleix + Muzaffar Jang + Chanda Sahib killed **Anwaruddin** → Muzaffar Jang **Subedar**, Chanda Sahib **Nawab**.
[155:4:5] - Titles and jagir: Dupleix made **Nawab of the Mughal territory south of the Krishna**; jagir **Valapadu**.
[155:4:6] - **Treaty of Pondicherry (1754)**: neither side to meddle in princes' quarrels → **Dupleix recalled**.
[155:4:7] # Bussy
[155:4:8] - Escorted **Muzaffar Jang**; after his murder at **Rayachoti**, made **Salabat Jang** Subedar of the Deccan; stayed seven years in Hyderabad.
## SUB 5 | French in Andhra
[155:5:0] # Machilipatnam and Yanam
[155:5:1] - **First French trade centre in Andhra** = **Machilipatnam (1669)**, permission of **Abdullah Qutb Shah** (APPSC G2 2002; APPSC-GS 2025).
[155:5:2] - **Yanam** = **only French territory in Andhra** (APPSC-G2 2025); under French control until **1954**; now part of the **UT of Puducherry**.
[155:5:3] # Northern Circars Grant (1753)
[155:5:4] - Given by **Salabat Jang** to pay for **Bussy's troops** – high point of French influence.
[155:5:5] - Circars: **Mustafanagar (Kondapalli), Eluru, Rajahmundry, Chicacole (Srikakulam)**.
[155:5:6] **Nellore** was NOT among the Circars given to the French.
[155:5:7] # Battles in Andhra
[155:5:8] TABLE head: Battle | Alignment
  [155:5:8.0] Tumnapalem (1756) | Bussy + Vizianagaram's Vijayaramaraju vs Jafar Ali
  [155:5:8.1] Bobbili (24 Jan 1757) | Vijayaramaraju + French (Bussy) vs Bobbili Ranga Rao – Ranga Rao killed
[155:5:9] - **Tandra Papayya** (Ranga Rao's brother-in-law) later killed Vijayaramaraju.
[155:5:10] APPSC G2 2019: at Bobbili, Vijayaramaraju took French help.
[155:5:11] # Loss of the Circars
[155:5:12] - **1758-59**: after **Chendurthi** and the fall of Machilipatnam to **Colonel Forde (1759)**, Salabat Jang switched to the British.
[155:5:13] - Gave them **Machilipatnam, Nizampatnam, Vakal Mannar** and part of Kondapalli; **1759 treaty = first British-Hyderabad agreement**.
[155:5:14] - Ended French power north of the Krishna, except in **Guntur**.
[155:5:15] - Other Circars British in **1768**; **Guntur in 1788**.
## SUB 6 | Later Possessions and Merger with India

=== NOTES 1:156 | Danish and Other Minor European Settlements (tranquebar, Serampore)

[156:0:0] # Order of Arrival
[156:0:1] - Order in India: **Portuguese → Dutch → British → Danish → French**.
[156:0:2] - Portuguese came **first (1498)** and left **last (1961)**; the **French** came **last (1664)**.

[156:3:0] # Missionaries and Contributions
[156:3:1] TABLE head: Missionary | Contribution
  [156:3:1.0] Bartholomaeus Ziegenbalg (1682-1719) | German pietist; founded the Danish-Halle Mission (1706); worked in Tamil; translated the Bible (New Testament) into Tamil

[156:3:6] APPSC G2 2019: Telugu Bible, 1746-47 = **Benjamin Schulz**.

=== NOTES 1:157 | Advent of Europeans in Andhra – Portuguese, Dutch, French and English
## SUB 0 | Why Europeans Came to Andhra
[157:0:0] - **Machilipatnam (Masulipatnam)** on the Krishna delta coast was the chief port of the **Qutb Shahi** kingdom of Golconda.
[157:0:1] - Its **chintz** (painted/printed cotton) and **diamond** trade drew all four powers: Portuguese, Dutch, English and French.
[157:0:2] - **Hyderabad** was founded in **1591** by **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah**; under him the Dutch (1605) and the English (1611) got their Machilipatnam factories.
[157:0:3] - Early travellers: **Marco Polo** visited **Motupalli**; **Tavernier**, a **17th-century** trader, visited the Andhra mines in search of the best diamonds and precious stones.
[157:0:4] - **Madras (1639)** became the English base that later controlled Andhra.
[157:0:5] # Order of Arrival in India
[157:0:6] - Sequence: **Portuguese (1498) → Dutch (1605) → English (1608) → French**.
[157:0:7] - The **French** were the **last Europeans to come to India as traders**.
[157:0:8] Portuguese came first to India (1498), but their Machilipatnam factory came last (1670).

## SUB 2 | The English in Andhra
[157:2:0] # The Ship Globe (1611)
[157:2:1] - The English ship **Globe** reached Machilipatnam in **1611**, under **Captain Hippon**, with **Peter Floris** (a Dutchman in English service) as merchant.
[157:2:2] - The English set up their **first factory on the east coast** at Machilipatnam in **1611**, with permission from **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah**.
[157:2:3] - The Masulipatnam factory (1611) marks the start of the East India Company's expansion in the south.

[157:2:7] # English Trade Practice
[157:2:8] - English used **Dadni** advances to weavers through **dubashes** (local intermediaries).
[157:2:9] - English **paid weavers less** while demanding **high quality**, so weavers avoided them.
