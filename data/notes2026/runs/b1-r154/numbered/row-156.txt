## SUB 0 | Missionaries, the Telugu Bible and the First Telugu Book
[0:0] - **Benjamin Schulz (Schultze)**, German Lutheran of the **Danish-Halle (Tranquebar) Mission**, = **first to translate the Bible into Telugu** – not C.P. Brown, A.D. Campbell or Robert Caldwell.
[0:1] - Learnt Telugu from 1719; translation begun 1727, completed about **1732**; printed at **Halle (Germany)** in **1746-47** – not at Serampore.
[0:2] - **First book printed in Telugu = the Bible** (not Rajasekhara Charitra, Kalapurnodayam or Satya Harischandra).
[0:3] - **Subbarayudu (Anandarao)** helped Schulz and ran a mission school at Visakhapatnam; wrote **Vedanta Rasayanam**.
[0:4] TABLE head: Missionary | Work
  [0:4.0] Bartholomaeus Ziegenbalg | German pietist; founded the Danish-Halle Mission, Tranquebar (1706); New Testament in Tamil
  [0:4.1] Benjamin Schulz | Telugu Bible (Halle, 1746-47)
  [0:4.2] Serampore Trio – William Carey, Joshua Marshman, William Ward | Baptists; worked from Danish Serampore to bypass Company bans; first Telugu Bible printed in India (1807-10); Serampore College 1818
[0:5] - Danish-Halle Mission = earliest lasting **Protestant** mission in South India; later Andhra missions: **Lutheran – Guntur**, **Baptist – Ongole**; **Jesuits** were linked with the **Portuguese**.
[0:6] - Danish legacy = **educational and linguistic** (start of printed **Telugu prose**), trade impact negligible.

## SUB 1 | Danish East India Company – Tranquebar and Serampore
[1:0] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [1:0.0] 1616 | Danish East India Company founded
  [1:0.1] 1620 | Tranquebar (Tharangambadi), near Tanjore, Tamil Nadu – first Danish settlement
  [1:0.2] 1636 | Danish factory at Balasore (Odisha)
  [1:0.3] 1699 | Serampore (near Calcutta) – centre of Danish activity in Bengal
  [1:0.4] 1845 | Serampore and Tranquebar sold to the English (British) under Governor-General Hardinge I – Danes left India
[1:1] - Order of arrival in India: **Portuguese → Dutch → English → Danes → French**; Portuguese came first (1498) and left last (1961, Goa, Daman, Diu).
[1:2] TABLE head: Settlement | Power
  [1:2.0] Tranquebar, Serampore | Danish
  [1:2.1] Chinsura (Chinsurah), Sadras, Pulicat | Dutch
  [1:2.2] Pondicherry, Chandernagore | French
[1:3] - **Pulicat (1621)**: the English briefly joined the **Dutch** there (Treaty of Defence, 1619) – abandoned due to Dutch hostility.
