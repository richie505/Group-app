## SUB 0 | French Settlements – Order of Founding and Pondicherry
[0:0] TABLE head: Settlement | Year | Detail
  [0:0.0] Surat | 1668 (expedition 1667) | First French factory in India, under François Caron (not Pulicat, Cochin, Calicut, Qasim Bazar)
  [0:0.1] Masulipatnam | 1669 | Marcara, patent from the Sultan of Golconda (Abdullah Qutb Shah)
  [0:0.2] Pondicherry | 1673-74 | Land from Sher Khan Lodi (Bijapur governor of Valikondapuram) to François Martin; Fort St. Louis
  [0:0.3] Chandernagore | 1673 / 1690 | Permission of Shaista Khan (1673); in Bengal, not Malabar
  [0:0.4] Yanam | 1723 (re-occupied 1731) | Godavari delta; Nizam confirmed it 1750-51
  [0:0.5] Mahe | 1725 | Malabar coast
  [0:0.6] Karaikal | 1739 | Coromandel coast – founded last
[0:1] - **17th-century** French settlements: Surat, Machilipatnam, Pondicherry, Chandernagore – **Mahe (1725) is not one**.
[0:2] - Chronology (APPSC G1): **Surat → Pondicherry → Yanam (1723) → Mahe (1725) → Karaikal (1739)**; the printed key put Yanam last, counting from the Nizam's grant of 1750-51.
[0:3] - **Pondicherry**: French control from **1674**; most important settlement and **French HQ**; **Dutch captured it in 1693**, restored by **Treaty of Ryswick (1697)**; taken by the English **1761**, restored by the **Treaty of Paris (1763)** – so the English **did** occupy it.
[0:4] - UPSC 2010: the **Portuguese** were the first Europeans at Pondicherry (16th century); the French were not the second; the English occupied it (1761, 1793) → only statement I correct.
[0:5] - After 1763 the French kept only enclaves – **Pondicherry, Karaikal, Mahe, Yanam, Chandernagore**; Chandernagore joined India by plebiscite (**1950**); the other four de facto **1954**, de jure **1962** → **UT of Puducherry**.
[0:6] - **Yanam** = the part of the UT of Puducherry inside **Andhra Pradesh** (Mahe – Kerala, Karaikal – Tamil Nadu; Daman is not part of Puducherry).

## SUB 1 | French East India Company and Colbert
[1:0] - Founded **1664** on the recommendation of **Jean-Baptiste Colbert**, Finance Minister of **Louis XIV** – Colbert is regarded as its founder (not Richelieu, Mazarin or François Martin).
[1:1] - Name: **Compagnie des Indes Orientales**; **50-year monopoly** in the Indian and Pacific Oceans + rights over **Madagascar**.
[1:2] - **State-controlled**: the king was the largest shareholder; dependent on subsidies → vulnerable; reorganised 1720 as the Perpetual Company of the Indies.
[1:3] - French = the **last European company to arrive** in India.
[1:4] TABLE head: Governor | Key point
  [1:4.0] François Martin | First governor; builder of Pondicherry; died 31 Dec 1706
  [1:4.1] Pierre Benoît Dumas (1735-41) | Right to mint rupees at Pondicherry (1736)
  [1:4.2] Dupleix (1742-54) | Peak of French power
  [1:4.3] Godeheu (1754) | Replaced Dupleix; made peace
  [1:4.4] Count de Lally | Lost at Wandiwash (1760) to Eyre Coote
[1:5] - French lost for **lack of naval supremacy** and steady state support.

## SUB 2 | Dupleix and His Policy of Intervention
[2:0] - **Joseph François Dupleix**, Governor-General of French India (**1742-54**) = **first European to take part in the quarrels of Indian princes to gain territory** (not Clive, Albuquerque or Warren Hastings).
[2:1] - First used **trained Indian sepoys** and placed a French army at an Indian court at the prince's cost – the **origin of the subsidiary alliance**; the English later copied his methods.
[2:2] - Exploited the **Hyderabad** (Nasir Jang vs **Muzaffar Jang**) and **Carnatic** (Anwaruddin vs **Chanda Sahib**) successions after Nizam-ul-Mulk's death (1748).
[2:3] - Rewards: made **Nawab / governor of the Mughal territory south of the Krishna**; jagir **Valapadu**.
[2:4] - Recalled **1754** (cost and failures); **Treaty of Pondicherry (1754)** barred interference in princes' quarrels.
