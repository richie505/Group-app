## SUB 0 | Machilipatnam – the Great Port of the Qutb Shahis
[0:0] - **Machilipatnam (Masulipatnam)** = the **most famous port town for (handloom) textile trade in the 16th-17th centuries** and the **chief trading centre of Andhra with the world before the 19th century** – not Motupalli, Visakhapatnam, Narsapur, Korangi or Bheemunipatnam.
[0:1] - Under **Golconda** in the 1600s it was famous for **textiles and diamonds** (also rubies) – not silver, gold or platinum.
[0:2] - Principal maritime gateway of the **Qutb Shahis**; passed to the Mughals when **Aurangzeb annexed Golconda (1687)**; **Komatis** were the key merchant community.
[0:3] - Ptolemy's **Maisolia (Mysolia)**, the "great market" = Machilipatnam (Kantakossyla = Ghantasala).
[0:4] - **First English factory in South India = Masulipatam, 1611** (Vizagapatam came in 1682).
[0:5] TABLE head: Export | Centre
  [0:5.0] Chintz (glazed, brighter after washing), palampores, kalamkari | Machilipatnam
  [0:5.1] Saltpetre | Machilipatnam, Narsapur, Pulicat
  [0:5.2] Diamonds, rubies | Golconda mines; Koh-i-Noor from Kollur (Guntur)
  [0:5.3] Silk and cotton piece-goods | Bengal
  [0:5.4] Indigo | Nagulavancha, Nalgonda
  [0:5.5] Salt | Pedaganjam, Chinaganjam
  [0:5.6] Cheap calico | Narasapuram
  [0:5.7] Taanis | Injaram
  [0:5.8] Rumaals (kerchiefs) | Nellore
[0:6] - Textiles = dominant industry, second only to diamonds in the Qutb Shahi economy; **pintado** = printed textile; English purchases were financed with **bullion**.
[0:7] - Imports: **horses** from Ormuz/Persia, **elephants** from Ceylon, **camphor** from Punjab – credited to the Reddi-period merchant **Avachi Tippaya Setti**.

## SUB 1 | Shipbuilding – Narasapuram
[1:0] - **Narasapuram / Narsapur** (West Godavari, Godavari delta) = the **chief shipbuilding centre of coastal Andhra in the 16th-17th centuries** – not Machilipatnam, Kakinada (Cocanada), Nagapattinam, Quilon or Madras.
[1:1] - **Methold** described large, strongly built ships there under the Qutb Shahis; good timber and a sheltered river mouth.
[1:2] - **Corangi** = a Company-era shipbuilding site; **odaberam** = sea-going ships (Shuka Saptati).
[1:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History - APPSC Part 15 (ships and boats: Narasapuram, Machilipatnam); old app notes row 158

## SUB 3 | Ports Before the Europeans – Motupalli and Ghantasala
[3:0] - **Motupalli (Desiyakondapattanam)**, now in Bapatla district = the **most important seaport of the Kakatiya kingdom** – not Machilipatnam (Qutb Shahi port), Kakinada, Nellore or Dharanikota.
[3:1] - **Marco Polo** visited it in **1287** under **Rudrama Devi**; **Ganapatideva's Abhaya Sasanam** protected foreign traders; Reddi minister **Somaya** issued another; **Anavota Reddi** lifted customs duties.
[3:2] - **Haravilasam** of **Srinatha**, dedicated to **Avachi Tippaya Setti**, describes the **sea trade of the Reddi period** – not Kashikhandam, Harivamsa or Bhimeswara Purana.
[3:3] - **Roman coins** in Andhra: **Bhattiprolu** (NextGen previous-paper key), Nagarjunakonda, Vinukonda, Vijayawada, Attirala, Salihundam; **Ghantasala** (Ptolemy's **Kantakossyla**) and **Koddura** were the Satavahana ports of Indo-Roman trade.
[3:4] - **Penugonda** = central trade centre of medieval Andhra.
