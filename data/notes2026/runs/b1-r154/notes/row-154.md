=== Surat – the First Permanent Factory and the Mughal Court
- **Surat (1613)** = the English Company's **first permanent factory in India** – on a **farman of Jahangir** (not Akbar, Shah Jahan or Aurangzeb); Pune, Goa, Pondicherry, Bangalore were never its first factory.
- **Masulipatnam (1611)** came earlier but only as a temporary trading post (permanent factory 1616) – so "first factory in India" = **Surat**, "first in South India / Andhra" = **Machilipatnam**.
- **Captain William Hawkins** – third voyage, landed at **Surat 1608**, at Jahangir's court (Agra) **1609**; first envoy of **James I**; permission withdrawn under Portuguese pressure.
- **Battle of Swally (1612)**: **Captain Thomas Best** defeated the **Portuguese** off Surat → Jahangir's farman of **1613** (factory under Thomas Aldworth).
- **Sir Thomas Roe (1615-19)** = ambassador of **King James I** to **Jahangir**; no commercial treaty, but privileges to set up factories at **Agra, Ahmedabad and Broach**; his chaplain was **Edward Terry**.
- **Ralph Fitch (1583-86)** = first Englishman to visit **Akbar's** court (Agra, Fatehpur Sikri) – before Hawkins and Roe.
- **Western Presidency**: seat first at **Surat**, shifted to **Bombay in 1687**.
- **Factories of the 1st quarter of the 17th century**: **Broach** (yes, Roe's privileges); **Chicacole** and **Trichinopoly** – no (UPSC 2021: 1 only).

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ralph Fitch** | 1583-86 | Akbar |
| 2 | **William Hawkins** | 1608-11 | Jahangir |
| 3 | **Thomas Best** (Swally) | 1612 | Jahangir |
| 4 | **Sir Thomas Roe** | 1615-19 | Jahangir |
> Company formed under **Akbar** (1600), first factory under **Jahangir** (1613, Surat); Roe was sent by **James I**.

=== The Charter of 1600 and the Company's Formation
- **31 December 1600**: **Queen Elizabeth I** granted the charter → the Company was founded in **1600** (not 1601, 1604 or 1607).
- Name: "**The Governor and Company of Merchants of London Trading into the East Indies**"; promoted by the "**Merchant Adventurers**" (1599).
- Charter gave only **exclusive trading rights** – a **15-year monopoly** between the **Cape of Good Hope** and the **Straits of Magellan** (made indefinite in 1609); **no territorial governance or legislative power**.
- **Emperor of India in 1600 = Akbar** (1556-1605), contemporary of **Elizabeth I** (1558-1603).
- Management: a **Governor + 24-member committee** (later Court of Directors); shareholders = General Court (later Court of Proprietors).
- **First voyage 1602** under **James Lancaster** (flagship **Red Dragon**, capital £68,000) → factory at **Bantam** (Java), not India.
- **Levant Company (1592)** traded with the Ottoman lands; the **East India Company** was the first chartered to trade with India; "The English Company trading to the East Indies" was the rival of **1698**.
| Year | Change |
|---|---|
| 1657 | **Cromwell's** charter – permanent joint stock |
| Charles II, James II | Coin money, jurisdiction, **war and peace with non-Christians** |
| 1708 | Rival companies merged – **United Company of Merchants of England Trading to the East Indies** |
| 1858 | Company rule ended – **Government of India Act, 1858** |
- Early objective: **eastern trade** (spices, textiles), paid for with **imported bullion**; secret of later success (UPPCS 1994): absence of nationalism, western-trained army – "all the above".

=== Madras – Francis Day and Fort St. George
- **1639**: **Francis Day** (with **Andrew Cogan**), a member of the Masulipatnam council, got **Madraspatnam** on lease from the **Chandragiri** ruler **Venkatapati Raya III**, through the **Damerla brothers (Venkatadri, Ayyappa)** – not John Child, Roe or Oxenden.
- **Fort St. George** at Madraspatnam (near **Mylapore**/San Thome): built from 1 March 1640, completed **23 April 1644** (St George's Day) = **first fort built by the English in India** and the **first fortified English settlement** (not Fort William, Mahim or Aguada).
- **Chennapatnam** named after **Damerla Chennappa**; Madras = chief of east-coast factories (1641), **presidency 1684**, **first municipality in India (1687-88)**.
- Main business of the early English settlements in Andhra and Fort St. George = **trade in locally woven piece-goods** (not conquest, spices or agriculture).
- **Mid-18th century populations** of the fortified English settlements: **Madras about 3 lakh**, Calcutta 2 lakh, Bombay 70,000 → **Madras largest**.

|---|---|

| Bombay | Bombay (replaced Surat, 1687) |

=== Bengal and Calcutta – Hugli, Job Charnock, Fort William
- **1651**: **Shah Shuja** (subahdar) let the English trade in Bengal for Rs 3,000 a year → **first English factory in Bengal = Hugli (1651)**, then **Qasim Bazar**, Patna, Rajmahal (earlier Hariharpur and Balasore, 1633, are in **Odisha**).
- **Job Charnock**, chief of the **Qasim Bazar** factory, returned to **Sutanuti** in **August 1690** → **founder of Calcutta** (not Charles Eyre, Aungier, Norris or John Surman).
- **1698**: zamindari of **Sutanuti, Gobindapur and Kalikata** bought; **1700**: fortified settlement named **Fort William** – Sir **Charles Eyre** first president.
- Fort built by the British at Calcutta = **Fort William** (Fort St. David is at Cuddalore; Fort St. George at Madras).
- Europeans got the best **saltpetre (shora) and opium from Bihar**.

=== Bombay – Dowry, Lease and Gerald Aungier
- **1661**: Bombay came to **Charles II** as dowry of the **Portuguese** princess **Catherine of Braganza** (married 1662) – taken from the **Portuguese**, not Dutch, French or Danes.
- **1668**: Charles II gave it to the Company for **£10 a year** (some books 1674/1688 – the lease was 1668).
- **Gerald Aungier** (President of Surat and Governor of Bombay, 1669-77) made Bombay a major commercial hub – "founding father" of Bombay.
- Bombay **replaced Surat** as the chief English settlement on the west coast (1687).

=== Chronology, Child's War and the Farman of 1717
- Order of English centres: **Surat 1613 → Madras 1639 → Bombay 1668 → Calcutta 1690**.
- **Child's War (1686-90)**: under **Josiah Child** (London) and **Sir John Child** (in India) the Company fought **Aurangzeb**, lost, was pardoned on paying a fine – Aurangzeb demanded Sir John Child's expulsion.
- **Shivaji** sacked Surat twice (1664, 1670) and threatened the English factory.
- **Farman of 1717** (mission of **John Surman**, 1715) by **Farrukhsiyar** = "**Magna Carta**" of the Company.
| Region | Privilege (1717) |
|---|---|
| Bengal | Duty-free trade for **Rs 3,000** a year; **dastaks** |
| Surat | All duties dropped for **Rs 10,000** a year |
| Bombay | Company coins current in the Mughal empire |
- Misuse of dastaks for private trade drained the Nawab's revenue → Plassey (1757), Buxar (1764) → **Bengal** = first major territorial base.
