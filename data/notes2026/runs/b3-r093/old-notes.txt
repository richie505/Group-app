=== NOTES 3:93 | Inflation – Types, Causes, Measurement (cpi, Wpi, Core), Effects and Control
## SUB 0 | Inflation – Basic Terms
[93:0:0] - **Inflation**: a rise in the **general price level**; it **decreases the purchasing power of money** (APPSC 2025).
[93:0:1] - **Deflation**: a lasting **fall in the general price level** (negative inflation); linked with lower consumer spending, falling demand and slowdown.
[93:0:2] - **Disinflation**: a **fall in the rate** of inflation that **stays positive** (e.g. 3% to 2%), not negative inflation.
[93:0:3] - **Reflation**: inflation coming back after deflation – a sign of **recovery**.
[93:0:4] - A fall in the inflation rate surely means only that **prices are rising more slowly** than before, not that prices fell.
[93:0:5] - **Economic growth** usually comes with **moderate inflation** – rising demand pushes prices up.
[93:0:6] Disinflation = rate falls but stays positive; Deflation = prices fall.
[93:0:7] # By Speed (Ascending)
[93:0:8] TABLE head: Type | Rate
  [93:0:8.0] Creeping | Below 3%
  [93:0:8.1] Walking / trotting | About 3-10% a year
  [93:0:8.2] Galloping | Above walking; comes before hyperinflation
  [93:0:8.3] Hyperinflation | Most severe
## SUB 1 | Headline, Core and Food Inflation
[93:1:0] # Headline vs Core
[93:1:1] TABLE head: Basis | Headline inflation | Core inflation
  [93:1:1.0] Represents | Total inflation | Underlying trend (long run, ignores short-term swings)
  [93:1:1.1] Food | Includes | Excludes volatile food
  [93:1:1.2] Fuel and energy | Includes | Excludes fuel and energy (and electricity)
  [93:1:1.3] Stability | More volatile | More stable
  [93:1:1.4] Shows | What consumers feel | Demand-side trend
[93:1:2] - **Headline inflation** = rate of change in the **CPI number** (standard basket of a typical family).
[93:1:3] - Core does not include food and energy; headline does **not** exclude food.
[93:1:4] - **Food inflation**: from monsoon and supply shocks; measured by the **Consumer Food Price Index (CFPI)** with **10 sub-groups** (cereals, meat and fish, egg, milk, oils, fruits, vegetables, pulses, sugar, spices).
[93:1:5] - Policy debate: target headline or core? Core is stickier and responds more to policy.
## SUB 2 | Types of Inflation by Cause
[93:2:0] TABLE head: Type | Cause / meaning
  [93:2:0.0] Demand-pull | Aggregate demand > aggregate supply (full-employment output); expansionary policy, fiscal stimulus, public spending, loose money, higher purchasing power – "too much money chasing too few goods" (UPSC 2021)
  [93:2:0.1] Cost-push | Rising input costs – wages, raw materials, fuel, rupee fall → supply falls; e.g. West Asia oil shock 2026
  [93:2:0.2] Bottleneck | Supply falls sharply while demand stays same; structural shortages (farm productivity, storage, distribution); hard for government to tackle
  [93:2:0.3] Stagflation | High inflation + slow growth + unemployment
  [93:2:0.4] Export-led | Strong export demand drains domestic supply
  [93:2:0.5] Skewflation | Prices rise sharply in a few items (e.g. food) while overall inflation stays low
  [93:2:0.6] Reflation | Inflation returning after deflation
[93:2:1] Demand-pull is NOT caused by inflation-indexing of wages or rising interest rates.
[93:2:2] - **Cost-push pressure falls** with a **permanent fall in input costs**.
## SUB 3 | Causes of Inflation in India
[93:3:0] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [93:3:0.0] Supply shocks (dominant) | Erratic monsoon, El Nino (vegetables), costlier crude oil, rupee depreciation; West Asia conflict raised fuel and input costs in 2026
  [93:3:0.1] Demand side | Public expenditure, loose monetary policy, higher incomes
  [93:3:0.2] Other | MSP hikes, hoarding, inflation expectations
[93:3:1] - Better **storage and distribution efficiency** eases supply shocks.
[93:3:2] - **Stagflation factors**: inflation from **higher government spending** + **fall in industrial production** and slow growth (APPSC G2 2025: a and b only; an older question took all four listed factors).
[93:3:3] - Crude benchmarks: **Brent** (North Sea), **West Texas Intermediate (WTI)** and **Dubai** – three main benchmarks.
## SUB 4 | Effects of Inflation and Deflation
[93:4:0] # Gainers and Losers
[93:4:1] TABLE head: Group | Who
  [93:4:1.0] Gainers | Debtors (repay money of lower real value), producers, businessmen, real-asset holders
  [93:4:1.1] Losers | Creditors (hurt most), fixed-income groups (pensioners, salaried without DA), savers, bond-holders
[93:4:2] - Rule: **debtors gain, creditors lose**.
[93:4:3] - Fixed-income groups are exposed unless incomes are adjusted – **not automatically protected**.
[93:4:4] - **Food inflation is regressive** – the poor spend a bigger share on food; food and beverages ≈ **37%** of the CPI basket.
[93:4:5] - Price-hike: businessmen profit and consumers cut needs – both true, but the second does not explain the first.
[93:4:6] - Other costs: **menu costs** (repeated re-pricing), uncertainty that hurts saving and investment.
[93:4:7] # Effects of Deflation
[93:4:8] - Purchasing power **rises**.
[93:4:9] - **Deflationary spiral**: delayed purchases → lower profits → **higher unemployment** → heavier **real debt burden**.
## SUB 5 | Price Indices – Publisher, Base Year, Weights
[93:5:0] # Who Publishes What
[93:5:1] TABLE head: Index | Compiled by | Base year | Use
  [93:5:1.0] CPI-Combined (Rural, Urban, Combined) | NSO, MoSPI | 2024 (replaced 2012 series; first release 12 Feb 2026 for Jan 2026) | Headline inflation; monetary policy target
  [93:5:1.1] CPI-IW | Labour Bureau (not NSO) | 2016 (from Sep 2020) | DA of industrial workers and Central Government employees; urban poverty
  [93:5:1.2] CPI-AL | Labour Bureau | 2019 (from June 2025; earlier 1986-87) | Farm labourers' wages; rural poverty
  [93:5:1.3] CPI-RL | Labour Bureau | 2019 (from June 2025; earlier 1986-87) | Rural labourers' wages
  [93:5:1.4] WPI | Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce and Industry | 2011-12 (revised from 2004-05 in 2017); new series 2022-23 | Contract escalation, industrial analysis
  [93:5:1.5] CFPI | NSO | As CPI | Food inflation
  [93:5:1.6] GDP deflator | NSO | Series base 2022-23 | Widest measure
[93:5:2] - **CPI-IW base**: **1982 → 2001 → 2016** (w.e.f. September 2020).
[93:5:3] - **Labour Bureau**: est. **1920**, **Chandigarh**, **Ministry of Labour and Employment**; also collects data on industrial disputes, closures, retrenchments, lay-offs.
[93:5:4] - **Odd one out** among CPI-IW, CPI-AL, CPI-RL, CPI-UNME: **CPI-UNME** (Urban Non-Manual Employees – not a Labour Bureau series).
[93:5:5] - National income and GDP estimates – **NSO** under MoSPI (earlier **CSO**).
[93:5:6] - **IIP** measures **industrial output, not prices**; NSO, monthly on the 28th, 14 source agencies.
[93:5:7] CPI = NSO; CPI-IW/AL/RL = Labour Bureau; WPI = Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT.
[93:5:8] # New CPI (Base 2024) Structure
[93:5:9] - **12 divisions** under **COICOP 2018** (earlier 6 groups).
[93:5:10] - **358 items** (308 goods, 50 services).
[93:5:11] - Weights: **food and beverages 36.75%**, housing 17.67% (old 2012 series food ~46%).
[93:5:12] - Added rural house rent and **12 online platforms** (Feb 2026).
[93:5:13] - RBI now uses **CPI (Combined), base 2024 = 100**.
[93:5:14] # WPI Structure
[93:5:15] - **2011-12 series**: **697 items** – primary 117, **manufactured 564**, fuel and power 16; **no services**.
[93:5:16] - **Manufactured products** = largest weight, **64.23%** (~64%).
[93:5:17] - **New WPI (base 2022-23)**: **957 items**; weights on **Gross Value of Output**; runs with a new **Producer Price Index (PPI)** for 5 years, then WPI phased out.
[93:5:18] - **PPI**: average change in prices of produced goods (output PPI: leaving production; input PPI: entering it).
[93:5:19] - New WPI series on base 2004-05 started on **14 September 2010**.
[93:5:20] - WPI was long called India's primary inflation measure.
## SUB 6 | Cpi vs Wpi vs Gdp Deflator
[93:6:0] TABLE head: Basis | CPI | WPI | GDP deflator
  [93:6:0.0] Measures | Retail prices | Wholesale (first-point bulk) prices | All goods and services produced
  [93:6:0.1] Services | Included | Excluded | Included
  [93:6:0.2] Publisher | NSO | Office of the Economic Adviser | NSO
  [93:6:0.3] Biggest weight | Food (more than WPI – UPSC 2020) | Manufactured goods | –
[93:6:1] - **GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100**; the **broadest** measure.
[93:6:2] - A **rise in the GDP deflator** → **fall in purchasing power** of money (APPSC G2 2025).
[93:6:3] - GDP deflator is **not** used for industrial workers' DA (that is CPI-IW).
[93:6:4] - **Dearness allowance basis**: **CPI-IW**.
[93:6:5] - India measures inflation on CPI, WPI and the labour cost-of-living indices; RBI now uses CPI (Combined).
## SUB 7 | Headline Measure, Inflation Targeting and Committees
[93:7:0] # From WPI to CPI
[93:7:1] - Earlier **WPI** = headline measure, the main index **till April 2014**.
[93:7:2] - **April 2014**: RBI adopted **CPI (Combined)** – **Urjit Patel Committee** framework.
[93:7:3] - Present headline inflation = **CPI-Combined (CPI-C)**; the most common measure in India.
[93:7:4] # Flexible Inflation Targeting
[93:7:5] TABLE head: Act / step | Year | Provision
  [93:7:5.0] Urjit Patel Committee | 2014 | CPI as nominal anchor; flexible inflation targeting
  [93:7:5.1] Monetary Policy Framework Agreement | Feb 2015 | Led to RBI Act amendment
  [93:7:5.2] RBI Act, 1934 amended | 2016 | MPC; statutory flexible inflation targeting
  [93:7:5.3] Section 45ZA | – | Target-setting by the Centre with RBI every 5 years
  [93:7:5.4] Section 45ZB | – | Six-member MPC
  [93:7:5.5] Section 45ZN | First invoked 2022 | RBI reports reasons to Government when band is breached for 3 consecutive quarters
[93:7:6] - **Target (1 Apr 2026 – 31 Mar 2031)**: **4% CPI-Combined, ±2% (2-6%)**, kept by Gazette notification.
[93:7:7] - **6%** is the **upper limit**, not the target; **WPI and core CPI are not the target index**.
[93:7:8] - After flexible inflation targeting, average CPI fell from **6.8% (2012-16)** to about **4.9%**.
[93:7:9] # Committees on Price Indices
[93:7:10] TABLE head: Committee | Subject
  [93:7:10.0] Urjit Patel (2014) | Monetary policy framework; CPI as anchor
  [93:7:10.1] Abhijit Sen | Working group on WPI revision (2007; report May 2008); base 2004-05
  [93:7:10.2] Saumitra Chaudhuri | Working group on WPI revision (set up March 2012); base 2011-12
  [93:7:10.3] Ramesh Chand (NITI Aayog) | Working group for WPI base 2022-23 (2025)
[93:7:11] Abhijit Sen = 2004-05 WPI base; Saumitra Chaudhuri = 2011-12 base; Urjit Patel = CPI anchor, not WPI.
## SUB 8 | Phillips Curve
[93:8:0] TABLE head: Person | Contribution
  [93:8:0.0] A.W. Phillips (1958) | Inverse relation between unemployment and (wage) inflation – lower unemployment comes with higher inflation
  [93:8:0.1] Friedman | Long-run Phillips curve is vertical; no lasting trade-off
[93:8:1] - **India's curve is fairly flat** (2012-2026 IIP-CPI data): about **92% of workers informal** → weak wage bargaining.
[93:8:2] - **Stagflation risk**: rate hikes cut output and jobs without lowering supply-driven inflation much.

## SUB 10 | Control Measures and Inflation-indexed Bonds
[93:10:0] # Tools
[93:10:1] TABLE head: Type | Tools

[93:10:11] - **Principal and/or interest linked to an inflation index**; protects the **real return**.

[93:10:14] - Government: cheaper borrowing when credible on inflation; signals commitment to low inflation.
