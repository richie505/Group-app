=== Longitude and Local Time – Calculations
# The rule (asked most: 23 PYQs)
- Earth turns **360° in 24 h**, **west to east** → **15° = 1 hour**, **1° = 4 minutes**, **1' of arc = 4 seconds**.
| Longitude gap | Time gap |
|---|---|
| 1° | **4 minutes** |
| 15° | **1 hour (60 min)** |
| 90° | 6 hours |
| 180° | **12 hours** (720 min) |
| 1' of arc | 4 seconds |
- **East is ahead (add), west is behind (subtract)**; moving westward local time **falls behind**. A place 30° west of Greenwich is **2 hours behind** GMT; the 15°E meridian is **1 hour fast**.
- **Same side** of Greenwich → subtract the longitudes; **opposite sides** → add them (25°E and 35°W = 60° = **4 h**; 6°W and 9°E = 15° = 1 h).
- **Same meridian = same local time**, however far apart north–south (NDA 2008: four places 500 km apart on one meridian all show **12:00 noon**).
- Journey/message: arrival local time = departure + travel time ± longitude time. Flight 10:00 a.m., 3 h, 15° east → **2:00 p.m.**; telegram from Greenwich at noon, 12 min in transit, arrives 6:00 p.m. → 5 h 48 min = 348 min ÷ 4 = **87°E**.
# Worked PYQs
| Given | Answer |
|---|---|
| A 6°W at 10 a.m. → B 9°E (APPSC G1 2023) | 15° = 1 h → **11 a.m.** |
| A 60°W at 10 a.m. → B 90°E (APPSC G1 2019) | 150° = 10 h → **8 p.m.** |
| 3:40 p.m. GMT → 10°W, West Africa (APPSC FSO) | 40 min behind → **3:00 p.m.** |
| Noon GMT, place at 6 a.m. (UPSC 1998) | **90°W** |
| Noon at IST meridian, place at 6 a.m. (IAS 1998) | 82°30'E − 90° = **7°30'W** |
| Noon at 82°30'E, place at 6:30 a.m. (IAS 1994) | 5½ h = 82°30' west → **0°** |
| Midnight IST, place at 6 a.m. (UPPCS 2002) | 82°30' + 90° = **172°30'E** |
| Noon IST → 120°E (UPPCS 2001) | 37°30' × 4 = 150 min → **14:30** |
| 10:00 a.m. IST → Shillong 92°E (IAS 1999) | 9.5° × 4 = 38 min → **10:38 a.m.** |
| Greenwich noon → 5 p.m. local (UPPCS 2008) | **75°E** |
| GMT 3 a.m., local 6 a.m. | **45°E** |
| Cairo 2 h ahead of Greenwich | **30°E** |
| Thimphu 90°E at Greenwich noon | **6:00 p.m.** |
| Sunrise Tirap (Arunachal) 5 a.m. → Kandla (Gujarat, ~70°E) | ~25° ≈ 1 h 40 min → **about 7:00 a.m.** (nearest option) |
- **Ship** sailing west at the speed of rotation, 10:00 Monday at 90°W: it keeps pace with the Sun, so at the Date Line it is still **10:00 Monday** (UPPCS 2000).
- **Rotation reversed** (UPSC 1997, asked twice): normally IST is 5½ h ahead of GMT and the 180°W side 12 h behind; reversed, IST would be 17½ h behind the Date Line → noon there = **18:30 hrs IST**.
> Calculation angle: find the gap, × 4 min, then add (east) or subtract (west). Check the side of Greenwich first.

=== Indian Standard Time and the Standard Meridian
# IST (asked 21 times)
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Standard meridian** | **82°30'E (82.5°E)** – India's central meridian |
| IST vs GMT | **GMT + 5 h 30 min** (82.5 × 4 = 330 min), i.e. **5½ h ahead**; 0000 UTC = **5:30 a.m. IST** |
| Reference town | **Mirzapur, UP** (clock tower); the meridian passes just east of **Prayagraj (Allahabad – Naini)** – the town given in the keys |
| City closest (MPPSC 2008) | **Rewa** (MP, ~81°18'E) |
| States crossed, W→E | **Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh** – **5**; **not West Bengal** (APPSC); last state = **Andhra Pradesh** (Kakinada district) |
| Meets the Tropic of Cancer | **Koriya district, Chhattisgarh**, on the **Baghelkhand Plateau** |
| Keeper | **CSIR-National Physical Laboratory, New Delhi** |
- **History:** India's time zones were set in **1884** – **Bombay Time** (GMT + 4 h 51 min) and **Calcutta Time** (GMT + 5 h 53 min 20 s); a single IST was adopted after Independence (**1947**; Calcutta Time lasted till 1948, Bombay Time till 1955). Calling Calcutta Time "4 h 51 min" and Bombay "5 h 30 min" is the false statement (APPSC flashcard).
- **Basis of standard time:** a country's standard time is the local time of a **chosen longitude** (usually its central meridian), counted from Greenwich.
- **City longitudes:** Delhi ~77°12'E – closest among the options is **Bengaluru** (~77°35'E); Hyderabad ~78°29'E, Nagpur ~79°05'E, Pune ~73°51'E (UPSC 2018).
# India's span and the two-time-zone debate
- India: **68°7'E (Guhar Moti, Kutch)** to **97°25'E** (Arunachal; easternmost between 97°25' and 97°30'E) → ~29° ≈ 116 min → **about 2 hours** between Arunachal and Gujarat (not 2½) (NDA 2017; Jharkhand 2023).
- Latitudes **8°4'N** (mainland, Kanyakumari) to **37°6'N** (Indira Col); both extents **about 30°**. Older texts quote **8°4'28"N to 37°17'53"N**.
- North-East loses daylight hours; proposals: advance IST by **30 min**, or a second zone ~1 h ahead. Assam tea gardens kept an informal **"Bagan time" (Chaibagaan time)**, 1 h ahead of IST – not official.
- Andhra Pradesh: **12°41'N – 19°07'N**, ~77°E – 84°40'E.
# IST in 2026 (current linkage)
- **Jul 2026:** **White Rabbit** IST distribution network at the Regional Reference Standards Laboratory, **Jakkur (Bengaluru)** – sub-nanosecond sync over fibre, independent of GPS; the protocol was developed at **CERN** (not ISRO); "One Nation, One Time" (NPL–ISRO). It provides synchronised high-precision IST time-stamps for banking, payments, railways, airports, power grids and telecom, and guards against GPS spoofing.
- **27 Aug 2026:** Consumer Affairs Ministry notified the **Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026** – IST the single legal reference; in force **180 days** after Gazette publication (not immediately).
> Trap: IST line misses **West Bengal**; 8 states on the Tropic vs 5 on the IST line, **Chhattisgarh** is on both.

=== Tropic of Cancer and India
# Tropic of Cancer (asked 18 times)
- At **23°26'N** (written **23½°N / 23°30'N**); divides India into **almost two equal halves** (not the international boundary).
- Crosses **8 states**, W→E: **Gujarat** (1st), **Rajasthan**, **Madhya Pradesh**, **Chhattisgarh**, **Jharkhand**, **West Bengal**, **Tripura**, **Mizoram** (last).
- **Not** crossed: **Odisha** (asked 4 times), **Uttar Pradesh**, **Bihar**, Himachal, J&K, Assam, AP. Old keys write "Bihar (Jharkhand)" – Jharkhand was then part of Bihar.
- Wholly **north**: UP, Bihar, Punjab, Haryana, Himachal, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Assam, Meghalaya, Arunachal, Nagaland, **Manipur**. **Andhra Pradesh lies wholly south**.
- The line joining India's **easternmost and westernmost points lies north** of the Tropic; the maximum east–west extent is **not** along the Tropic (Jharkhand 2023: I and IV).
| City | Latitude |
|---|---|
| **Gandhinagar** (nearest, UPPCS 1999) | 23°13'N |
| **Jabalpur** (nearest, UPPCS 2017) | 23°11'N |
| Ujjain | 23°9'N |
| Ahmedabad | 23°2'N |
| Agartala | 23°50'N |
| **Kolkata** (nearest of Delhi, Jodhpur, Nagpur – IAS 2003) | 22°34'N |
- Other cities near it: Bhopal and Ujjain (MP), **Ranchi (Jharkhand)**, Agartala (Tripura), Aizawl (Mizoram).
- **Mahi** crosses it **twice**: rises in MP, flows through Rajasthan and Gujarat into the **Gulf of Khambhat**.
- World: crosses **17 countries** (Mexico, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Oman, India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, China …) – **not Iran**. **Equator** passes Kenya, Colombia, **Indonesia**.
- Globe: **179 parallels** and **360 meridians** at 1° interval.
> NOT-angle: "Tropic does not pass through" → Odisha / UP; "lies north of the Tropic" → Manipur.

=== Time Zones, GMT and UTC
# Time terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **GMT** | Mean solar time at the Royal Observatory, **Greenwich (0°)**; Prime Meridian adopted at the International Meridian Conference, **1884** |
| **UTC** | World time kept by **caesium atomic clocks**; kept in step with the slowing Earth by **leap seconds** |
| **Leap second** | Added (e.g. **31 Dec 2016**, just before midnight) because the **Earth's rotation is slowing** |
| **Daylight Saving Time** | Clocks advanced **1 h in summer** to use evening light; **India does not use it** |
| Natural units | Day (rotation), **month (Moon's revolution)**, year (Earth's revolution) |
- **NavIC** (7 satellites: 3 GEO + 4 GSO) times its signals with **rubidium** atomic clocks.
# Zones
- **24 theoretical zones** of 15° each; more in practice (half- and quarter-hour offsets).
| Country / city | Fact |
|---|---|
| **France** (with overseas territories) | **12 zones – most** |
| Russia | **11** |
| **UK 9, Australia 9, Denmark 5, New Zealand 5** (with territories) | **More than four** (UPSC 2025: only four of five) |
| **Brazil** | Exactly **4** |
| **China** | **One** zone despite its width |
| **Colombo** (Sri Lanka) | Same clock as Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai (UTC+5:30) |
| **Nepal** | **UTC+5:45** |
| **Accra, Dublin, Lisbon, London** | GMT; **not Madrid** (CET) or **Addis Ababa** (UTC+3) |
- Ahead → behind GMT (UPSC 1995, asked twice): **Japan (+9) > Iraq (+3) > Greece (+2) > Cuba (−5) > Costa Rica (−6)**.

=== International Date Line
- Imaginary **pole-to-pole** line roughly along **180°** in the **middle of the Pacific Ocean** (APPSC, State PSC); **12 hours** (720 min) from Greenwich because it is the 180° meridian (A and R true, R explains A).
- Nearest strait: **Bering Strait** (UPSC 2008, asked twice) – between the **Chukchi** (Russia) and **Seward** (Alaska) peninsulas.
- **Zigzags** where **land is in the way**: Chukotka, Aleutians, Kiribati, Fiji, Tonga, Samoa.
| Crossing | Date change |
|---|---|
| **Eastward** (Asia → America) | **Subtract a day** (gain a day) |
| **Westward** (America → Asia) | **Add a day** (lose a day) |
- **Big Diomede (Russia) and Little Diomede (USA)** are neighbours on opposite sides, a day apart.
- **Samoa** moved to the Asian side in **2011**; **Kiribati's Line Islands** (UTC+14) are first to see a new day.
- Old UPPCS 1992 key: a ship's least-time path is "along the meridian" – strictly the shortest route is a **great-circle** route (meridians are great circles).
