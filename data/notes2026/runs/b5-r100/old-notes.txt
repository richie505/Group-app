=== NOTES 5:100 | Bharat Stage Emission Norms – Bs-i to Bs-vi
## SUB 0 | Origins and Authority
[100:0:0] - First vehicle norms: **1991 (petrol), 1992 (diesel)**.
[100:0:1] - **Mashelkar Committee → National Auto Fuel Policy (2003) → Euro norms**.
[100:0:2] - Euro-based **Bharat Stage** name from **2000** (APPSC 2019); BS-I also called **"India 2000"**.
[100:0:3] - **Euro I** in Europe: **1992**; India lagged Europe by **~5 years**.
[100:0:4] - Notified by **MoRTH** under **CMV Rules, 1989** on CPCB/MoEFCC advice – not MoEFCC.
## SUB 1 | Stage → Year

  [100:1:0.0] BS-I | Euro I | – | 1 April 2000
  [100:1:0.1] BS-II | Euro II | Delhi, 1 April 2000 | 2005
  [100:1:0.2] BS-III | Euro III | NCR + 10 cities (incl. Hyderabad-Secunderabad), 1 April 2005 | 2010
  [100:1:0.3] BS-IV | Euro IV | 13 cities, 2010 | April 2017
  [100:1:0.4] BS-V | Euro 5 | Skipped | –
  [100:1:0.5] BS-VI | Euro 6 | – | 1 April 2020
  [100:1:0.6] BS-VI Phase 2 | – | – | April 2023 (Real Driving Emissions)
  [100:1:0.7] BS-VII | Euro 7 | Planned 2026-27 | –
## SUB 2 | Why Bs-v Was Skipped
[100:2:0] - Decision **2016**: BS-IV straight to BS-VI.
[100:2:1] - Reasons: urgent **Delhi-NCR** air pollution; close the gap with Europe in **one step** instead of two changeovers.
[100:2:2] - **Supreme Court (2018)**: no **sale or registration of BS-IV** vehicles from **1 April 2020** (APPSC 2018).
[100:2:3] BS-V is the only stage India skipped.
## SUB 3 | Pollutants and Limits
[100:3:0] TABLE head: Stage | Fuel sulphur
  [100:3:0.0] BS-II | 500 ppm
  [100:3:0.1] BS-III | 350 ppm
  [100:3:0.2] BS-IV | 50 ppm
  [100:3:0.3] BS-VI | 10 ppm (−80% vs BS-IV)
[100:3:1] - Euro II ultra-low sulphur diesel: **0.05%** or less = 500 ppm (UPPSC 2013).
[100:3:2] - Euro norms limit **CO** (RPSC 1999); cover **CO, hydrocarbons and lead** → "all of the above" (UPPSC 2013).
[100:3:3] - BS-VI limits: NOx **60 mg/km petrol, 80 mg/km diesel**; diesel PM **4.5 mg/km**. Expected NOx cut: diesel cars ~70%, petrol ~25%.
## SUB 4 | Vehicular Pollutants → Health Effect
[100:4:0] TABLE head: Pollutant | Effect
  [100:4:0.0] CO | Forms carboxyhaemoglobin; cuts oxygen supply
  [100:4:0.1] NOx | Respiratory illness, children most at risk; forms ground-level ozone and acid rain
  [100:4:0.2] PM2.5 | Deep lungs/bloodstream; heart and lung disease
  [100:4:0.3] Benzene | Carcinogen – leukaemia
  [100:4:0.4] Lead | Neurotoxin; leaded petrol phased out by 2000
[100:4:1] - **Photochemical smog**: NOx + hydrocarbons + **sunlight**. Acid rain: SO2 + NOx, pH < 5.6.
[100:4:2] - **Chernobyl** = nuclear (radioactive) pollution: hereditary changes, cancer. **Nanoparticles** pollute water, soil, food chain.
[100:4:3] - **Bioremediation** uses microorganisms; heavy metals (**cadmium, lead**) limit it by killing microbes.
## SUB 5 | Control Technologies
[100:5:0] TABLE head: Technology | Function
  [100:5:0.0] Catalytic converter (BS-I era) | CO → CO2
  [100:5:0.1] Multi-point fuel injection (BS-II) | Replaced carburettors
  [100:5:0.2] SCR | Diesel NOx → N2 + water using urea AdBlue
  [100:5:0.3] DPF | Traps soot, oxidises it
  [100:5:0.4] OBD-II | Monitors emissions, alerts driver
  [100:5:0.5] Electronic Fuel Injection | Mandated for two-wheelers
## SUB 6 | Implications and Allied Measures
[100:6:0] - Refineries upgraded to supply **10 ppm** fuel nationwide; sulphur cut 500 → 10 ppm in ~15 years.
[100:6:1] - **CAFE III** third draft (**16 July 2026**): cars from **April 2027**; credits for ethanol, CBG, biofuels; targets **~21% relaxed** (not stricter) vs Sept 2025 proposal; average weight 1,229 kg.
[100:6:2] - **E20**: notified 17 Feb 2026; all States/UTs from **1 April 2026**, min **RON 95** (not 91); replaces 2021 E10 notification.
[100:6:3] - **Vehicle Scrappage Policy (2021)**: Automated Testing Stations + Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facilities.
[100:6:4] - **PUC** mandatory under CMV Rules 1989; officers trained at **ARAI Pune** and IIP Dehradun.

=== NOTES 5:101 | Water Pollution – Sources, Eutrophication, Namami Gange, Sewage Treatment, Groundwater Contamination
## SUB 0 | Sources of Water Pollution
[101:0:0] TABLE head: Type | Examples
  [101:0:0.0] Point | Sewage outfalls, industrial effluent pipes
  [101:0:0.1] Non-point | Agricultural runoff, urban runoff
  [101:0:0.2] Sand mining | Raises turbidity, degrades river beds
[101:0:1] - Fertilisers pollute soil, water and air; **nitrates and phosphates** cause eutrophication.
[101:0:2] - Ritual offerings and **dairy effluent** can have BOD far above sewage → deplete DO fast (15 Apr 2026).
[101:0:3] - AP's main water polluters: **Pharma City (Vizag), tanneries, paper mills, aquaculture** (not coal mines or cement).
## SUB 1 | Water Quality Parameters
[101:1:0] # Oxygen Demand
[101:1:1] TABLE head: Parameter | Measures | Clean-water value
  [101:1:1.0] DO | Oxygen dissolved in water | > 8.0 mg/L
  [101:1:1.1] BOD | Oxygen bacteria need to decompose organic matter | < 4.0 mg/L
  [101:1:1.2] COD | Oxygen to oxidise all organic matter (biodegradable + non-biodegradable) | < 5.0 mg/L
[101:1:2] - River pollution is measured by its **dissolved oxygen**: more pollution → **less DO**.
[101:1:3] - **BOD** = standard criterion for pollution assay in aquatic ecosystems (mg/L). **High BOD** = sewage contamination; low BOD = good water.
[101:1:4] # Other Terms
[101:1:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [101:1:5.0] Coliform count | Faecal contamination (MPN/100 ml)
  [101:1:5.1] Suspended solids | Retained on a filter: silt, clay (APPSC 2023)
  [101:1:5.2] Dissolved solids / TDS | Salts passing the filter (sodium salts, nitrate)
  [101:1:5.3] Turbidity | Measured in NTU
[101:1:6] Silt is a suspended solid; sodium salts and nitrate are dissolved.

## SUB 3 | Eutrophication
[101:3:0] - **Nutrient (N, P) enrichment** of a water body; the **natural ageing of a lake** by nutrient build-up.
[101:3:1] - Triggers: fertiliser runoff, sewage, **detergents**, aquaculture effluent, airborne nitrogen (not a reduction of atmospheric nitrogen).
[101:3:2] - Chain: **enrichment → algal bloom → algae decay → decomposition uses up DO → hypoxia, fish kills**.
[101:3:3] - Stages: **oligotrophic → mesotrophic → eutrophic → hypereutrophic**.
[101:3:4] - Harmful algal blooms in Indian seas: nutrient discharge from estuaries, monsoon land run-off, **upwelling** (all three).
[101:3:5] - Blooms of pigmented **dinoflagellates** in estuaries = **red tides**.
## SUB 4 | Pollution-linked Diseases
[101:4:0] TABLE head: Disease | Contaminant | Key fact
  [101:4:0.0] Minamata | Methyl mercury | Japan, 1956; biomagnified through fish; neurological damage
  [101:4:0.1] Itai-itai ("ouch-ouch") | Cadmium | Japan; mining wastewater used for irrigation (APPSC 2024); bone, joint pain; first reported 1912, mass case 1950, Toyama
  [101:4:0.2] Blue baby syndrome | Nitrate | Methaemoglobinaemia in infants – blood cannot carry oxygen
  [101:4:0.3] Fluorosis | Fluoride | Dental (children under 8), skeletal, non-skeletal
  [101:4:0.4] Knock-knee (genu valgum) | Fluoride | Skeletal fluorosis; above 0.5 ppm over 5–10 years
  [101:4:0.5] Arsenicosis / blackfoot | Arsenic | Skin lesions, skin and lung cancer
[101:4:1] - Fluorosis chemistry: fluoride replaces hydroxyl in **hydroxyapatite → fluorapatite**.
