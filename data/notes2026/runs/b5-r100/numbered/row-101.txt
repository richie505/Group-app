## SUB 0 | Water-borne Pollutant Diseases
[0:0] TABLE head: Disease | Contaminant | Key fact
  [0:0.0] Minamata | Methyl mercury | Japan, 1956; bacteria turn mercury into methyl mercury, biomagnified through fish; neurological damage
  [0:0.1] Itai-itai ("ouch-ouch") | Cadmium | Japan (Toyama, mass case 1950); mining wastewater used to irrigate rice; painful bones and joints
  [0:0.2] Blue baby syndrome | Nitrate in drinking water | Methaemoglobinaemia – nitrate → nitrite → methaemoglobin in the blood, which cannot carry oxygen; infants
  [0:0.3] Fluorosis | Fluoride | Dental mottling (children under 8), skeletal fluorosis, stiff painful joints
  [0:0.4] Knock-knee (genu valgum) | Fluoride | Skeletal fluorosis – outward bending of legs from the knees
  [0:0.5] Arsenicosis / blackfoot disease | Arsenic | Skin lesions, skin and lung cancer
  [0:0.6] Contact dermatitis | Nickel | Allergic skin rash (SSC CGL 2017)
[0:1] - **Mercury → Minamata; nitrate → blue baby** (IAS 2017). Fluoride is **not** matched with "indigestion" (UPPSC 2017 key), though NextGen/Shankar list gastro-intestinal problems among fluorosis effects.
[0:2] - "In the blood" = **methaemoglobin**; "in drinking water" = **nitrate**.
[0:3] - **Metal-toxicity diseases:** Minamata and Itai-itai; **dermatitis** is the usual "NOT metal toxicity" answer (SSC 2018), though nickel allergy causes it.
[0:4] - **DDT** at high concentration disturbs **calcium metabolism in birds** (thin eggshells); **nitrates + phosphates → eutrophication**.
[0:5] TABLE head: Pollutant | Main source (APPSC NTRUHS)
  [0:5.0] Arsenic | Inorganic compounds in agriculture, mining, manufacturing; fly ash
  [0:5.1] Mercury | Coal-fired power plants, incinerators, industry, historic gold mining
  [0:5.2] Fluoride | Minerals – apatite, micas, hornblende – released from rocks
  [0:5.3] Chromium | Tannery / leather wastewater
[0:6] - Fluorosis chemistry: fluoride replaces hydroxyl in **hydroxyapatite → fluorapatite**.
[0:7] > Most-asked angle (~22 PYQs): match the disease to the metal – Itai-itai = cadmium (×7), blue baby = nitrate (×5), knock-knee = fluoride (×3).

## SUB 1 | Measuring Water Pollution – DO, BOD, Coliform, Solids
[1:0] TABLE head: Parameter | Measures | Unpolluted water
  [1:0.0] DO | Oxygen dissolved in water | > 8–9 mg/L
  [1:0.1] BOD | Oxygen bacteria need to decompose organic matter | < 4 mg/L
  [1:0.2] COD | Oxygen to oxidise all organic matter (biodegradable + non-biodegradable) | < 5 mg/L
[1:1] - River pollution is measured by its **dissolved oxygen**: more pollution → **less DO**.
[1:2] - **BOD = standard criterion for pollution assay in aquatic ecosystems** (UPSC 2017), i.e. organic pollution in **water** – not soil or air. **High BOD = heavy organic/microbial pollution, e.g. sewage**; low BOD = good water.
[1:3] - **Coliform count** (MPN/100 ml) = **faecal contamination by human wastes** – the predominant human-body pollutant of the **Ganga**; higher total coliform = mixing of human faeces.
[1:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:4.0] Suspended solids | Retained on a filter – silt, clay (APPSC 2023)
  [1:4.1] Dissolved solids / TDS | Salts passing the filter (sodium salts, nitrate)
  [1:4.2] Turbidity | Cloudiness from suspended solids, measured in NTU
[1:5] - Water-changing agents: **effluents** alter physico-chemical characteristics (UPSC 1996). **Sulphur dioxide** is an **air** pollutant, not a water pollutant (zinc, copper, nickel are).
[1:6] - **Aquatic organisms respire faster** because **water holds far less dissolved oxygen than air**.
[1:7] > Asked ~15 times: what BOD/DO/coliform indicates; "dissolved oxygen" is the river-pollution answer.
[1:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.10, p.13-14; PMF Environment Part 04 p.64-65, p.71; app notes 5:101 sub 1

## SUB 3 | Eutrophication and Algal Blooms
[3:0] - **Eutrophication = nutrient (N, P) enrichment of a water body** – the **natural ageing of a lake by nutrient (biological) enrichment** of its water; human-accelerated = **cultural eutrophication**.
[3:1] - Triggers: fertiliser runoff (**nitrates, phosphates**), sewage, detergents, aquaculture effluent – not idol immersion as such and not a fall in nitrogen inputs.
[3:2] - Chain: **enrichment → algal bloom → algae decay → decomposition uses up DO → hypoxia, fish kills**. So eutrophication **increases** algae and **decreases** DO (both statements true, UPPSC 2019/2020).
[3:3] - **Algal bloom:** excessive growth of planktonic (free-floating) algae – deteriorates water quality and causes **fish mortality**.
[3:4] - **Hypoxia:** reduction of oxygen in water (or air) threatening aerobic organisms → **dead zones**.
[3:5] - Stages: **oligotrophic → mesotrophic → eutrophic → hypereutrophic**.
[3:6] - **Harmful algal blooms in Indian seas** (UPSC 2011): nutrient discharge from **estuaries**, monsoon **land run-off** and **upwelling** – all three.
[3:7] - Blooms of pigmented **dinoflagellates** in estuaries = **red tides**.
[3:8] > Asked ~14 times: definition ("over-supply of nutrients", "natural ageing of a lake"), its effect on DO and algae.
[3:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.12-13; PMF Environment Part 04 p.73-74, p.77; Rocket Environment p.22; app notes 5:101 sub 3
## SUB 4 | Sources of Water Pollution and Sewage Treatment
[4:0] - **Point sources:** municipal and industrial discharge pipes; **non-point:** agricultural and urban runoff.
[4:1] - **Largest contributor to water pollution in India: untreated sewage**; only about **28%** of urban sewage is treated (2026).
[4:2] - Agricultural water polluters: **chemical fertilisers and excessive pesticides**.
[4:3] - **Sand mining** in river beds: raises **turbidity**, **pollutes groundwater** and **lowers the water table** (UPSC 2018) – it does not reduce salinity.
[4:4] - **Dairy effluent** and ritual offerings can have BOD far above sewage.
[4:5] TABLE head: Technology | Function
  [4:5.0] Activated sludge | Conventional STP process; settled sludge is digested to biogas
  [4:5.1] SBR (sequencing batch reactor) | Treats in batches in one tank
  [4:5.2] MBR (membrane bioreactor) | Activated sludge + membrane filtration – wastewater treatment (UPSC 2024)
  [4:5.3] Ozonation | Ozone kills micro-organisms; leaves no residue (ozone is not radioactive, but that is not why it disinfects)
[4:6] - **Treat domestic sewage before release** – the healthy practice (burning plastics/leaves is not).
[4:7] - **Rain water** collected after 30–40 minutes of rain is clean but **deficient in minerals**.
[4:8] - **CETP** = shared plant for clusters of **small-scale industries**; **India's first CETP: Jeedimetla (near Hyderabad, then Andhra Pradesh), 1989**. AP runs **9 CETPs**; **Zero Liquid Discharge** example: **Tiruppur** (Tamil Nadu).
[4:9] - **HAM** for Namami Gange approved **2016**; first HAM STP **Haridwar**. **AMRUT 2.0: 20% reuse** of treated wastewater; thermal plants within **50 km** of an STP must use treated wastewater. BOD discharge limit relaxed **10 → 30 mg/L** (criticised: more organic pollution allowed).
[4:10] - **IISER Pune** (Jan 2023) reported a new material that removes pollutants from water.
[4:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.10-11; PMF Environment Part 04 p.64, p.81-84; app notes 5:101 sub 0, 6
## SUB 5 | Groundwater Contamination
[5:0] TABLE head: Contaminant | Where
  [5:0.0] Arsenic | Ganga-Brahmaputra plains – West Bengal (maximum, Bengal basin, Kolkata), Bihar, Jharkhand, UP, Assam; extends into Bangladesh
  [5:0.1] Fluoride | Rajasthan highest (CGWB 2024), Haryana, Karnataka; Nalgonda (Telangana) is the classic fluorosis district; AP also hit
  [5:0.2] Nitrate | About 56% of districts exceed 45 mg/L; mainly fertiliser
  [5:0.3] Uranium | Malwa (Punjab), Rajasthan; > 30 ppb also in AP, Telangana
[5:1] - **Arsenic** is the **non-biotic** (chemical) pollutant of groundwater; bacteria, algae, viruses are biotic.
[5:2] - Drinking-water pollutants in parts of India (IAS 2013): **arsenic, fluoride, uranium** – not sorbitol or formaldehyde.
[5:3] - **Nalgonda technique** (fluoride removal): **alum + lime + bleaching powder**.
[5:4] - **UP Ground Water (Management and Regulation) Act, 2019 (UP cabinet, 2020):** registration of submersible pumps mandatory; rainwater harvesting mandatory in private schools/colleges; it **does penalise** polluting groundwater through boring pipes.
