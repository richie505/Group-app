=== Water-borne Pollutant Diseases
| Disease | Contaminant | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Minamata** | **Methyl mercury** | Japan, **1956**; bacteria turn mercury into methyl mercury, biomagnified through fish; neurological damage |
| **Itai-itai ("ouch-ouch")** | **Cadmium** | Japan (**Toyama**, mass case 1950); **mining wastewater used to irrigate rice**; painful bones and joints |
| **Blue baby syndrome** | **Nitrate** in drinking water | **Methaemoglobinaemia** – nitrate → nitrite → **methaemoglobin** in the blood, which cannot carry oxygen; infants |
| **Fluorosis** | **Fluoride** | **Dental mottling** (children under 8), skeletal fluorosis, stiff painful joints |
| **Knock-knee (genu valgum)** | **Fluoride** | Skeletal fluorosis – outward bending of legs from the knees |
| **Arsenicosis / blackfoot disease** | **Arsenic** | Skin lesions, **skin and lung cancer** |
| Contact **dermatitis** | **Nickel** | Allergic skin rash (SSC CGL 2017) |
- **Mercury → Minamata; nitrate → blue baby** (IAS 2017). Fluoride is **not** matched with "indigestion" (UPPSC 2017 key), though NextGen/Shankar list gastro-intestinal problems among fluorosis effects.
- "In the blood" = **methaemoglobin**; "in drinking water" = **nitrate**.
- **Metal-toxicity diseases:** Minamata and Itai-itai; **dermatitis** is the usual "NOT metal toxicity" answer (SSC 2018), though nickel allergy causes it.
- **DDT** at high concentration disturbs **calcium metabolism in birds** (thin eggshells); **nitrates + phosphates → eutrophication**.
| Pollutant | Main source (APPSC NTRUHS) |
|---|---|
| Arsenic | Inorganic compounds in agriculture, mining, manufacturing; **fly ash** |
| Mercury | **Coal-fired power plants, incinerators**, industry, historic gold mining |
| Fluoride | Minerals – **apatite, micas, hornblende** – released from rocks |
| Chromium | **Tannery** / leather wastewater |
- Fluorosis chemistry: fluoride replaces hydroxyl in **hydroxyapatite → fluorapatite**.
> Most-asked angle (~22 PYQs): match the disease to the metal – Itai-itai = cadmium (×7), blue baby = nitrate (×5), knock-knee = fluoride (×3).
~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.12-13; PMF Environment Part 04 p.67-68, p.73; Shankar Environment Part 06 p.18; app notes 5:101 sub 4

=== Measuring Water Pollution – DO, BOD, Coliform, Solids
| Parameter | Measures | Unpolluted water |
|---|---|---|
| **DO** | Oxygen dissolved in water | > 8–9 mg/L |
| **BOD** | Oxygen **bacteria need to decompose organic matter** | < 4 mg/L |
| **COD** | Oxygen to oxidise **all** organic matter (biodegradable + non-biodegradable) | < 5 mg/L |
- River pollution is measured by its **dissolved oxygen**: more pollution → **less DO**.
- **BOD = standard criterion for pollution assay in aquatic ecosystems** (UPSC 2017), i.e. organic pollution in **water** – not soil or air. **High BOD = heavy organic/microbial pollution, e.g. sewage**; low BOD = good water.
- **Coliform count** (MPN/100 ml) = **faecal contamination by human wastes** – the predominant human-body pollutant of the **Ganga**; higher total coliform = mixing of human faeces.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Suspended solids** | Retained on a filter – **silt**, clay (APPSC 2023) |
| Dissolved solids / TDS | Salts passing the filter (sodium salts, nitrate) |
| **Turbidity** | Cloudiness from suspended solids, measured in **NTU** |
- Water-changing agents: **effluents** alter physico-chemical characteristics (UPSC 1996). **Sulphur dioxide** is an **air** pollutant, not a water pollutant (zinc, copper, nickel are).
- **Aquatic organisms respire faster** because **water holds far less dissolved oxygen than air**.
> Asked ~15 times: what BOD/DO/coliform indicates; "dissolved oxygen" is the river-pollution answer.
~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.10, p.13-14; PMF Environment Part 04 p.64-65, p.71; app notes 5:101 sub 1

| Year | Body / plan | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **1985** | **Central Ganga Authority** | PM chair; renamed National River Conservation Authority, Sept 1995 |
| **1985** | **Ganga Action Plan-I** | First river action plan (MoEF); Rajiv Gandhi, **Varanasi**; 25 main-stem towns |

- **GAP launch date:** most books and keys give **1985** (approved, Central Ganga Authority set up); the formal launch by Rajiv Gandhi was in **June 1986** (14 June 1986, Varanasi).
- Five tiers: **National Ganga Council (PM) → Empowered Task Force (Jal Shakti Minister) → NMCG → State Ganga Committees → District Ganga Committees**; legal base: **River Ganga Authorities Order, 2016**.

- **Eight main pillars:** Sewerage Treatment Infrastructure, River-Front Development, River-Surface Cleaning, Bio-Diversity, Afforestation, Public Awareness, **Industrial Effluent Monitoring**, **Ganga Gram**. **Not pillars:** River Aquifer Management, Boat and Ship Effluent Monitoring, uranium mining.
- Namami Gange is a key Indian initiative for **SDG 6 (clean water and sanitation)**; UN listed it among the top 10 **World Restoration Flagships**.
- Most sewerage infrastructure projects (31 Dec 2021, APPSC key): **Bihar**.
- **Feb 2020 MoU** (NMCG + SPMG-Uttarakhand + Indorama Charitable Trust): **ghat at Badrinath, ghats and crematoria at Gangotri**.

| JALAJ | Livelihood centres (NMCG + **WII**) |
| Ganga Praharis | Trained local volunteers for river biodiversity |
| Ganga Utsav | Annual river festival |
- Results: first **dolphin estimate 6,327** (March 2025). **Global River Cities Alliance:** launched at **COP28, Dubai (Dec 2023)**, **led by NMCG**; members – river cities worldwide plus partner countries and agencies.

=== Eutrophication and Algal Blooms
- **Eutrophication = nutrient (N, P) enrichment of a water body** – the **natural ageing of a lake by nutrient (biological) enrichment** of its water; human-accelerated = **cultural eutrophication**.
- Triggers: fertiliser runoff (**nitrates, phosphates**), sewage, detergents, aquaculture effluent – not idol immersion as such and not a fall in nitrogen inputs.
- Chain: **enrichment → algal bloom → algae decay → decomposition uses up DO → hypoxia, fish kills**. So eutrophication **increases** algae and **decreases** DO (both statements true, UPPSC 2019/2020).
- **Algal bloom:** excessive growth of planktonic (free-floating) algae – deteriorates water quality and causes **fish mortality**.
- **Hypoxia:** reduction of oxygen in water (or air) threatening aerobic organisms → **dead zones**.
- Stages: **oligotrophic → mesotrophic → eutrophic → hypereutrophic**.
- **Harmful algal blooms in Indian seas** (UPSC 2011): nutrient discharge from **estuaries**, monsoon **land run-off** and **upwelling** – all three.
- Blooms of pigmented **dinoflagellates** in estuaries = **red tides**.
> Asked ~14 times: definition ("over-supply of nutrients", "natural ageing of a lake"), its effect on DO and algae.
~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.12-13; PMF Environment Part 04 p.73-74, p.77; Rocket Environment p.22; app notes 5:101 sub 3

=== Sources of Water Pollution and Sewage Treatment
- **Point sources:** municipal and industrial discharge pipes; **non-point:** agricultural and urban runoff.
- **Largest contributor to water pollution in India: untreated sewage**; only about **28%** of urban sewage is treated (2026).
- Agricultural water polluters: **chemical fertilisers and excessive pesticides**.
- **Sand mining** in river beds: raises **turbidity**, **pollutes groundwater** and **lowers the water table** (UPSC 2018) – it does not reduce salinity.
- **Dairy effluent** and ritual offerings can have BOD far above sewage.
| Technology | Function |
|---|---|
| Activated sludge | Conventional STP process; settled **sludge** is digested to **biogas** |
| SBR (sequencing batch reactor) | Treats in batches in one tank |
| **MBR (membrane bioreactor)** | Activated sludge + membrane filtration – **wastewater treatment** (UPSC 2024) |
| **Ozonation** | **Ozone kills micro-organisms**; leaves no residue (ozone is not radioactive, but that is not why it disinfects) |
- **Treat domestic sewage before release** – the healthy practice (burning plastics/leaves is not).
- **Rain water** collected after 30–40 minutes of rain is clean but **deficient in minerals**.
- **CETP** = shared plant for clusters of **small-scale industries**; **India's first CETP: Jeedimetla (near Hyderabad, then Andhra Pradesh), 1989**. AP runs **9 CETPs**; **Zero Liquid Discharge** example: **Tiruppur** (Tamil Nadu).
- **HAM** for Namami Gange approved **2016**; first HAM STP **Haridwar**. **AMRUT 2.0: 20% reuse** of treated wastewater; thermal plants within **50 km** of an STP must use treated wastewater. BOD discharge limit relaxed **10 → 30 mg/L** (criticised: more organic pollution allowed).
- **IISER Pune** (Jan 2023) reported a new material that removes pollutants from water.
~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.10-11; PMF Environment Part 04 p.64, p.81-84; app notes 5:101 sub 0, 6

=== Groundwater Contamination
| Contaminant | Where |
|---|---|
| **Arsenic** | **Ganga-Brahmaputra plains – West Bengal (maximum, Bengal basin, Kolkata)**, Bihar, Jharkhand, UP, Assam; extends into **Bangladesh** |
| **Fluoride** | Rajasthan highest (CGWB 2024), Haryana, Karnataka; **Nalgonda** (Telangana) is the classic fluorosis district; AP also hit |
| Nitrate | About **56%** of districts exceed 45 mg/L; mainly **fertiliser** |
| Uranium | **Malwa** (Punjab), Rajasthan; > 30 ppb also in AP, Telangana |
- **Arsenic** is the **non-biotic** (chemical) pollutant of groundwater; bacteria, algae, viruses are biotic.
- Drinking-water pollutants in parts of India (IAS 2013): **arsenic, fluoride, uranium** – not sorbitol or formaldehyde.
- **Nalgonda technique** (fluoride removal): **alum + lime + bleaching powder**.
- **UP Ground Water (Management and Regulation) Act, 2019 (UP cabinet, 2020):** registration of submersible pumps mandatory; rainwater harvesting mandatory in private schools/colleges; it **does penalise** polluting groundwater through boring pipes.
