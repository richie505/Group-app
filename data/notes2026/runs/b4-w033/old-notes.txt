## SUB 6 | Tropic of Cancer and the Standard Meridian
[33:6:0] - **Tropic of Cancer (23°30′ N)** passes through **8 States**, west to east: **Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram**; it roughly halves India. **Andhra Pradesh lies wholly south** of it; it does not touch Odisha or Bihar.
[33:6:1] - **Indian Standard Meridian: 82°30′ E** (a multiple of 7°30′), reference place **Mirzapur (UP)**; **IST = GMT/UTC + 5:30**.
[33:6:2] - It passes through **5 States:** **Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh** (not Telangana). **Chhattisgarh** lies on both the Tropic and the meridian; **Odisha** only on the meridian.
[33:6:3] - **Time spread:** ~30° of longitude = **~2 hours** (1° = 4 min); the sun rises in the North-East about 2 hours before Jaisalmer.
[33:6:4] - **Separate time zone demand:** **Arunachal Pradesh** CM Pema Khandu (2017) asked for a North-East time zone; India keeps a single zone.
[33:6:5] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Geography p.116, p.118-120; PMF Indian Physical Geography 01 p.13-14; app notes 4:33 sub 1-2
[33:6:6] - In **Andhra Pradesh** the 82°30′ E meridian passes near **Kakinada** (Kakinada district).

=== NOTES 4:34 | India – Physiographic Division: the Himalayas (ranges, Regional Divisions, Passes)
## SUB 0 | Parallel Ranges – Himadri, Himachal, Shiwalik and the Trans-Himalaya
[34:0:0] # The three parallel (longitudinal) ranges
[34:0:1] TABLE head: Feature | Himadri (Greater / Inner / Central) | Himachal (Lesser / Middle) | Shiwalik (Outer / foothills)
  [34:0:1.0] Position | Northernmost of the three | Between Himadri and Shiwalik | Outermost, southernmost
  [34:0:1.1] Age | Oldest | Intermediate | Youngest (Pliocene, Cenozoic era)
  [34:0:1.2] Height | Average 6,000 m; loftiest peaks | 3,500–4,500 m (avg ~4,000 m) | 900–1,100 m
  [34:0:1.3] Width | 160–400 km (NCERT summary; PMF) | ~50 km | 10–50 km, narrowest
  [34:0:1.4] Make-up | Granite core (central crystallines); asymmetrical folds | Highly compressed and altered rocks; most rugged system | Unconsolidated river sediments; simple Jura-type folds
  [34:0:1.5] Continuity | Most continuous range | Broken into ranges | Continuous to the Gandak, then broken
  [34:0:1.6] Known for | Everest, Kanchenjunga, Nanda Devi, Kamet; long glaciers | Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mussoorie, Nag Tibba, Mahabharat (Nepal); Kashmir, Kangra, Kullu valleys; hill stations | Foothills; duns behind them; debris built the plains
[34:0:2] - **Three parallel ranges** in the longitudinal extent: **Himadri, Himachal, Shiwalik** (NCERT) – a statement "four parallel ranges" is false; "the northernmost range is the Shiwalik" is false (northernmost = **Himadri**).
[34:0:3] - **Himadri:** also called Greater, Higher, Inner or **Central** Himalaya; highest point **Mount Everest** (~8,850 m; now 8,848.86 m). Dehradun is **not** in the Himadri (it is a dun between Lesser Himalaya and Shiwalik).
[34:0:4] - **Himachal:** names for the same range – Lesser, Middle, Lower Himalaya; "Himachal stands for the Middle Himalaya".
[34:0:5] - **Pir Panjal:** the most important Lesser Himalayan range, **~300 km from the Jhelum to the upper Beas** in **J&K and Himachal Pradesh**; Banihal pass crosses it. South-east of the Ravi it continues as the **Dhauladhar**, then the **Mussoorie** range (Uttarakhand) and the **Mahabharat Lekh** in **Nepal**.
[34:0:6] - **Shiwalik:** formed from riverine deposits of the "Siwalik river" (Pascoe, Pilgrim) about **2–5 million years ago** (Pliocene, **Cenozoic**); local names – **Jammu Hills** (J&K), **Dhang/Dundwa** (Uttarakhand), **Churia Ghat** (Nepal).
[34:0:7] - **Youngest mountain chain** among Vindhyas, Aravalli, Shiwaliks, Annamalai = **Shiwaliks**; **oldest Himalayan range** = **Greater Himalaya**.
[34:0:8] - **In Arunachal Pradesh** the Lesser/outer Himalayan hills are the **Dafla, Miri, Abor and Mishmi hills** (PMF lists them as Lesser Himalaya; the Shiwalik is almost absent there).
[34:0:9] # The Trans-Himalaya (Tibetan Himalaya)
[34:0:10] - Lies **north of the Himadri**; mostly in Tibet, hence the **Tibet Himalaya**; **formed before** the Himalayas.
[34:0:11] - Ranges: **Karakoram** (K2), **Ladakh** (continues into Tibet as the **Kailash Range**, not the Zaskar), **Zaskar** (Kashmir Valley separates the Pir Panjal from it), **Kailash** (Mt Kailash 6,714 m; **Lake Manasarovar** lies near it in Tibet).
[34:0:12] - **Ladakh Plateau:** north-east of the Karakoram; **highest plateau in India**; includes the **Aksai Chin**, Soda Plains, Lingzi Tang, Depsang Plains (Chinese-occupied Aksai Chin is a cold desert).
[34:0:13] - **Kailash Mansarovar** is in **Chinese Tibet** – Indian pilgrims need a visa.
[34:0:14] # Sequence questions (north ↔ south)
[34:0:15] TABLE head: Asked | Answer
  [34:0:15.0] N → S (all) | Karakoram – Ladakh – Zaskar – Pir Panjal – Shiwalik
  [34:0:15.1] N → S (four) | Karakoram – Ladakh – Zanskar – Shiwalik
  [34:0:15.2] N → S (main) | Karakoram – Himadri – Himachal – Shiwalik
  [34:0:15.3] S → N (west) | Shiwalik – Lesser Himalaya – Great Himalaya
  [34:0:15.4] Northward | Pir Panjal – Zanskar – Ladakh – Karakoram
  [34:0:15.5] S → N | Dhauladhar – Zanskar – Ladakh – Karakoram
  [34:0:15.6] Hills S → N | Satmala (Maharashtra) – Kaimur (MP–UP–Bihar) – Naga (Nagaland) – Pir Panjal (J&K–HP)
[34:0:16] - The **Lesser Himalaya lies between the Shiwalik and the Great Himalaya**.
[34:0:17] > Statement trap (APPSC 2023): "Shiwaliks are composed of highly compressed and altered rocks" ✗ (that is the Himachal); "folds of the Great Himalaya are symmetrical" ✗; "Himadri is the most continuous range, average 6,000 m" ✓.

## SUB 5 | Dimensions, Origin, Structure and Significance
[34:5:0] # Dimensions
[34:5:1] TABLE head: Dimension | Figure
  [34:5:1.0] Length | ~2,400 km (2,414 km) – Nanga Parbat (west) to Namcha Barwa (east)
  [34:5:1.1] Width | 500 km (Kashmir) → 200 km (Arunachal) (NextGen); NCERT/PMF: 400 km → 150 km
  [34:5:1.2] Area | About 5 lakh sq km (not 15 lakh)
  [34:5:1.3] Eastern limit | The Brahmaputra (Dihang gorge) marks the eastern boundary
[34:5:2] - The Himalayas form the **northern boundary**, separating the subcontinent from the rest of Asia (not the Aravalli or the Ghats).
[34:5:3] - **Indian Himalayan Region:** HP, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal, NE States, J&K, Ladakh and hill WB – **not Uttar Pradesh**.
[34:5:4] # Origin and structure
[34:5:5] - **Young fold mountains** of tectonic origin, raised from **Tethys (geosynclinal)** sediments in the **Tertiary** (Eocene–Pliocene) as the **Indian plate** pushed into Eurasia (~70 million years ago, still rising).
[34:5:6] - **Geologically young and unstable** landmass of India.
[34:5:7] - **Trans-Himalayas formed before** the Himalayas.
[34:5:8] - Folds of the **Himadri are asymmetrical** with a **granite** core; **steep slope faces the plains**, gentle slope faces Tibet.
[34:5:9] - **Structural zones (N → S):** Indus–Tsangpo Suture Zone (separates Himalaya from **Tibet**) → Main Central Thrust → Main Boundary Thrust → **Himalayan Frontal Fault** (separates Himalaya from the **plains**, not from Tibet).
[34:5:10] - **Antecedent drainage:** the Indus and Sutlej are older than the Himalaya and cut gorges through it.
[34:5:11] - **Fossils (UPPSC 2019 key):** Greater Himalaya sedimentary rocks fossil-less; marine fossils in the Lesser Himalaya; remains of human civilisation in the Shiwaliks.
[34:5:12] # Significance (IAS 2010: all three true)
[34:5:13] - Blocks **Siberian cold waves**; shapes the **monsoon** pattern; rivers and Shiwalik debris built the **Indo-Gangetic Plain's alluvium**.
[34:5:14] - **Plains** are the largest relief division of India (~43% of area).
[34:5:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography - APPSC Exams 04 p.8; PMF Indian Physical Geography 01 p.38, p.70; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Geography 03 p.13-14; app notes 4:34 (sub 0)
[34:5:16] - **East v. west:** in the east the Himalaya rises **almost abruptly from the plains** over a narrow belt, so **Everest and Kanchenjunga** stand not far from the plains; in the west the ranges rise gradually across a wide belt.

## SUB 7 | Peninsular Plateau – Structure, Plateaus and the Deccan Traps
[35:7:0] # Structure
[35:7:1] - **Oldest and largest** physiographic division; a **Precambrian shield**, part of **Gondwana land**; one of the most **stable** blocks; **not** of volcanic origin overall; rich in **metallic minerals**.

## SUB 5 | Character of Himalayan Rivers – Antecedent, Perennial, Inland Drainage
[36:5:0] # Four groups of Indian rivers
[36:5:1] TABLE head: Group | Example
  [36:5:1.0] Himalayan | Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra
  [36:5:1.1] Deccan (Peninsular) | Godavari, Krishna, Mahanadi – seasonal, not perennial
  [36:5:1.2] Coastal | Short west-coast streams
  [36:5:1.3] Inland drainage | Western Rajasthan – Ghaggar, Luni (into the Rann; the river of the "Great (Thar) Desert"), Rupen
[36:5:2] - **Inland drainage:** the river does not reach the sea – e.g. **Ghaggar** (not the Ghaghara, a Ganga tributary).
[36:5:3] - **River Khari (Rajasthan):** the main **Khari** is a **Banas tributary** (Rajsamand → Deoli) → Banas → Chambal → Yamuna → Ganga → **Bay of Bengal**; other streams named Khari feed the Luni (Arabian Sea) and Sambhar lake (inland).
[36:5:4] # Antecedent rivers
[36:5:5] - **Definition:** a river that **existed before the Himalayas rose** and kept its course by **cutting deep gorges** as the land rose.
[36:5:6] - Marks: **valley-in-valley**, **paired terraces**, **entrenched (ingrown) meanders**.
[36:5:7] - Examples: **Indus** (gorge at Nanga Parbat – the river most cited for **gorges**), **Sutlej**, **Brahmaputra** (Dihang gorge) – the **Brahmaputra is "older than the Himalayas"**; the Yamuna, Kosi and Ganga are not the answer to that question.
[36:5:8] - The **Ganga** is usually listed as an **antecedent** river in exam keys; it rises at Gangotri **within** the Himalayas, unlike the Indus, Sutlej and Brahmaputra which rise **beyond** them in Tibet (**Trans-Himalayan**).
[36:5:9] # Perennial character and work in the plains
[36:5:10] - **Perennial:** fed by **rain + snowmelt** (rainfall and melting ice); Himalayan rivers are **not dry in winter**.
[36:5:11] - In the plains: gentle slope → **meanders, shifting channels, ox-bow lakes, floodplains**, braided channels and riverine islands.
[36:5:12] - Himalayan rivers show **all three stages** of the erosion cycle (youth, maturity, old age); **Peninsular rivers have reached maturity**.
[36:5:13] - The Northern Plain (not the "Western plain") is formed by the interplay of the **Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra**.
[36:5:14] > A–R pattern: "Himalayan rivers are perennial" + "Kosi changes its course" → both true, R does **not** explain A; + "Himalayas get rain from the SW monsoon" → both true, R does not explain A (the snowmelt part is missing).
[36:5:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 07 p.7, p.13; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Geography 04 p.2-6; rajras.in (Khari, rivers of Rajasthan); app notes 4:36 (sub 0)
[36:5:16] - **Two major groups** of Indian rivers (NCERT): **Himalayan** and **Peninsular**; the four-way split adds coastal and inland-drainage rivers.
