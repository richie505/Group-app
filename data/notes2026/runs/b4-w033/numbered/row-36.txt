## SUB 0 | Ganga Tributaries – Yamuna, Son, Damodar, Kosi and the Left Bank
[0:0] # Left bank vs right bank
[0:1] TABLE head: Bank | Tributaries
  [0:1.0] Left (Himalayan) | Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Mahananda
  [0:1.1] Right (mostly Peninsular) | Yamuna (longest), Son, Tons, Punpun; Damodar joins the Hugli (right bank)
[0:2] - **Yamuna** = **westernmost and longest** tributary of the Ganga; **Son** = the largest right-bank tributary from the Peninsula.
[0:3] - **Mahananda** = **last, easternmost** left-bank tributary; joins in **West Bengal**.
[0:4] - **Son** is the main right-bank tributary that **flows northwards** (from Amarkantak) and **joins the Ganga directly** near **Maner (Patna)**; Gomti, Ghaghara, Kosi join from the **left**.
[0:5] - **Chambal, Betwa, Ken, Sindh** join the **Yamuna**, not the Ganga; the Chambal therefore lies in the **Ganga basin** (not Narmada/Sabarmati/Godavari).
[0:6] # Order of confluence below Prayagraj (west → east)
[0:7] - **Gomti** (near Ghazipur, Kaithi) → **Ghaghara** (near **Chapra**, Revelganj, Saran) → **Son** (R, near Maner) → **Gandak** (Hajipur/**Sonpur**, opposite Patna) → **Kosi** (Kursela, Katihar, below Bhagalpur) → Mahananda.
[0:8] > UPSC 2024 (asked twice): Himalayan rivers joining below Prayagraj, west → east = **Gomti – Ghaghara – Gandak – Kosi**.
[0:9] # Left-bank (Himalayan) tributaries

  [0:10.3] Kosi | "Sorrow of Bihar"; maximum shifting of course; seven headstreams – Arun most important (Sun Kosi, Tamur Kosi); changed course in Aug 2008 (Kusaha breach) → Bihar flood calamity
  [0:10.4] Gomti | Rises at Gomat Taal (Fulhar lake), Pilibhit – the only Ganga tributary rising in the plains; Lucknow
  [0:10.5] Mahananda | Easternmost/last left-bank tributary

[0:15] # Yamuna and its tributaries
[0:16] - Rises at **Yamunotri glacier**, western slopes of **Bandarpunch (Banderpunch)**; joins the Ganga at **Prayagraj**.
[0:17] TABLE head: Bank | Tributaries of the Yamuna

[0:18] - **Chambal:** rises at **Janapav hills near Mhow** (Dr Ambedkar Nagar), Malwa; **deep ravines** (badlands); tributaries **Banas** (left), **Kali Sindh, Parvati, Kshipra** (right) – the **Betwa does not join the Chambal**; Gandhi Sagar, Rana Pratap Sagar, Kota.
[0:19] - **Betwa** rises in the Vindhyas (Bhopal plateau) and joins the **Yamuna** at Hamirpur.
[0:20] - **Yamuna between Delhi and Agra** is almost a "dead river" for much of the year because of heavy abstraction and pollution – but the Yamuna itself is a **perennial** Himalayan river.
[0:21] # Son, Damodar and the Chota Nagpur rivers
[0:22] - **Son:** Amarkantak → north through Baghelkhand → Ganga near **Maner (Patna)**; tributaries Johilla, Banas, Gopad, **Rihand**, Kanhar, **North Koel**.
[0:23] - **Kaimur range** = water divide between the **Yamuna (via the Tons) and the Son** (MPPSC).
[0:24] - **North Koel:** rises on the **Ranchi plateau**, flows through the **north of Betla NP** (Palamu), joins the **Son** → part of the **Ganga basin**; **Mandal dam** on it. **South Koel and Sankh** go to the **Brahmani** (not Ganga).
[0:25] - **Damodar:** Khamarpat hill/Tori, Chota Nagpur; flows in a **rift (fault) valley**; "**Sorrow of Bengal**" (before DVC); joins the **Hugli (Hooghly)** south of Kolkata; main tributary **Barakar** (also Konar, Jamunia, Barki).
[0:26] - **Rajarappa** (Ramgarh, Jharkhand) – confluence of **Damodar and Bhera (Bhairavi)**; Chhinnamasta temple.
[0:27] - **Bansagar Accord, 1973** on the Son: 7.75 MAF; the **2026 Bihar–Jharkhand MoU** gives **Bihar 5.75 MAF, Jharkhand 2 MAF** (as of 2026).

## SUB 1 | Indus System
[1:0] # Course of the Indus
[1:1] - Rises at **Singge Khabab** (Bokhar Chu glacier) on the **northern slopes of the Kailash range** near Mansarovar (5,182 m); in Tibet = **Singi Khamban** ("Lion's mouth").
[1:2] - Flows west between the **Ladakh and Zaskar** ranges; **Leh** lies on its **right bank** (UT of Ladakh since 31 Oct 2019); **Zanskar** joins at **Nimu**.
[1:3] - Takes a southerly course at **Nanga Parbat** through a deep **antecedent gorge** → Pakistan → **Arabian Sea near Karachi**; flows through **north-western India and Pakistan**.
[1:4] - Length **2,880 km** (NCERT; NextGen 2,480 km), only **709 km** in India. Himalayan tributaries: **Shyok**, Gilgit, Zaskar, Shigar, Dras, Hunza.
[1:5] - **Panjnad:** the five Punjab rivers join the Indus a little **above Mithankot**: Jhelum and Ravi → Chenab; Beas → Sutlej; Sutlej + Chenab = Panjnad.
[1:6] > UPSC 2021 (asked twice): the river that "joins the Indus direct" = **Sutlej** (UPSC key, per PMF); strictly the Sutlej and Chenab merge as the Panjnad.
[1:7] # The five Punjab rivers
[1:8] TABLE head: River | Greek / Vedic | Source and facts
  [1:8.0] Jhelum | Hydaspes / Vitasta | Spring at Verinag, foot of the Pir Panjal (NextGen: Sheshnag); flows through Srinagar and Wular lake; main tributary Kishanganga (= Neelum in Pakistan); Tulbul project
  [1:8.1] Chenab | Acesines / Asikni | Chandra + Bhaga (rise near Baralacha La, flow in Lahaul) meet at Tandi (HP); largest tributary of the Indus; Salal, Baglihar, Dul Hasti; Tawi is a Chenab tributary
  [1:8.2] Ravi | Hydraotes / Parushni | Kalu hills/Rohtang, HP; joins the Chenab in Pakistan; Harappa lies on the Ravi; Upper Bari Doab canal
  [1:8.3] Beas | Hyphasis / Vipasa | Beas Kund near Rohtang; joins the Sutlej at Harike; whole course in India; Indus river dolphin (Beas Conservation Reserve, Punjab – the only Indian population)
  [1:8.4] Sutlej | Zaradros / Shutudri | Rakas Tal (Rakshastal), Kailash range, Tibet (Langchen Khambab); enters India at Shipki La; antecedent, Trans-Himalayan – cuts across all the Himalayan ranges; tributaries Spiti (joins at Khab), Baspa, Beas; Bhakra-Nangal
[1:9] - **North → south (also west → east):** **Jhelum → Chenab → Ravi → Beas → Sutlej**.
[1:10] - **North → south, upper Indus streams:** **Shyok – Zaskar – Spiti – Sutlej** (UPSC 2006).
[1:11] - **Ravi** is not a tributary of the Sutlej directly (it joins the Chenab); **Mahanadi** is a Peninsular river, not an Indus tributary.
[1:12] - **Doabs:** **Bist (Doaba) = Beas–Sutlej**; Bari = Beas–Ravi; Rechna = Ravi–Chenab; Chaj = Chenab–Jhelum; Sind Sagar = Jhelum/Chenab–Indus. "Panjnad/Punjab" = land of five rivers.
[1:13] - **Rivers of Himachal Pradesh:** **Beas, Chenab, Ravi, Sutlej and Yamuna**.
[1:14] - **Sutlej identification (UPSC 2026):** antecedent, flows through **three countries** (China, India, Pakistan), rises on the Tibetan plateau, key for irrigation, forms **no distributaries** (it ends in the Panjnad).
[1:15] # Indus Waters Treaty, 1960

## SUB 2 | Ganga – Headwaters, Panchprayag, Course and Delta

[2:3] - The two meet at **Devprayag** (Tehri Garhwal); the river is called the **Ganga** from Devprayag.

[2:8] # Course
[2:9] - Rises in the western Himalayas of **Uttarakhand**, enters the plains at **Haridwar**; navigable up to **Prayagraj**; towns: **Kanpur (largest city on the Ganga)**, Prayagraj, Varanasi, **Patna**, Bhagalpur.
[2:10] - In Bihar the Ganga touches **12 districts**; **Patna** district has its longest stretch (about 99 km).
[2:11] - At **Farakka** (WB): left arm **Bhagirathi–Hooghly**; right arm **Padma** enters Bangladesh – in Bangladesh the **Ganga = Padma**, the **Brahmaputra = Jamuna**.
[2:12] - Padma + Jamuna (at **Goalundo**) + Meghna → enters the **Bay of Bengal as the Meghna**; the delta ends in the **Sundarbans**.
[2:13] - **Length:** **2,525 km** – the **longest river in India** (NextGen gives 2,465 km, 2,071 km in India); basin ≈ **26%** of India's area, about 40% of its population.
[2:14] # Bengal delta
[2:15] - **Moribund delta** (old, inactive, Nadia–Murshidabad), **mature delta**, and **active delta** (still growing, **North and South 24 Parganas**, Sundarbans) – "moribund" is a sub-division of the **Bengal delta**.
[2:16] > Statement trap: "Burhi Dihing and Dhansiri are the Ganga's left-bank tributaries" and "it finally merges with the Padma" are NCERT lines about the **Brahmaputra**, so both are false for the Ganga.

## SUB 3 | Character of Himalayan Rivers – Antecedent, Perennial, Inland Drainage
[3:0] # Four groups of Indian rivers
[3:1] TABLE head: Group | Example
  [3:1.0] Himalayan | Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra
  [3:1.1] Deccan (Peninsular) | Godavari, Krishna, Mahanadi – seasonal, not perennial
  [3:1.2] Coastal | Short west-coast streams
  [3:1.3] Inland drainage | Western Rajasthan – Ghaggar, Luni (into the Rann; the river of the "Great (Thar) Desert"), Rupen
[3:2] - **Inland drainage:** the river does not reach the sea – e.g. **Ghaggar** (not the Ghaghara, a Ganga tributary).
[3:3] - **River Khari (Rajasthan):** the main **Khari** is a **Banas tributary** (Rajsamand → Deoli) → Banas → Chambal → Yamuna → Ganga → **Bay of Bengal**; other streams named Khari feed the Luni (Arabian Sea) and Sambhar lake (inland).
[3:4] # Antecedent rivers
[3:5] - **Definition:** a river that **existed before the Himalayas rose** and kept its course by **cutting deep gorges** as the land rose.
[3:6] - Marks: **valley-in-valley**, **paired terraces**, **entrenched (ingrown) meanders**.
[3:7] - Examples: **Indus** (gorge at Nanga Parbat – the river most cited for **gorges**), **Sutlej**, **Brahmaputra** (Dihang gorge) – the **Brahmaputra is "older than the Himalayas"**; the Yamuna, Kosi and Ganga are not the answer to that question.
[3:8] - The **Ganga** is usually listed as an **antecedent** river in exam keys; it rises at Gangotri **within** the Himalayas, unlike the Indus, Sutlej and Brahmaputra which rise **beyond** them in Tibet (**Trans-Himalayan**).
[3:9] # Perennial character and work in the plains
[3:10] - **Perennial:** fed by **rain + snowmelt** (rainfall and melting ice); Himalayan rivers are **not dry in winter**.
[3:11] - In the plains: gentle slope → **meanders, shifting channels, ox-bow lakes, floodplains**, braided channels and riverine islands.
[3:12] - Himalayan rivers show **all three stages** of the erosion cycle (youth, maturity, old age); **Peninsular rivers have reached maturity**.
[3:13] - The Northern Plain (not the "Western plain") is formed by the interplay of the **Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra**.
[3:14] > A–R pattern: "Himalayan rivers are perennial" + "Kosi changes its course" → both true, R does **not** explain A; + "Himalayas get rain from the SW monsoon" → both true, R does not explain A (the snowmelt part is missing).
[3:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 07 p.7, p.13; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Geography 04 p.2-6; rajras.in (Khari, rivers of Rajasthan); app notes 4:36 (sub 0)
## SUB 4 | Brahmaputra System
[4:0] # Names and course
[4:1] TABLE head: Region | Name
  [4:1.0] Tibet | Tsangpo / Yarlung Zangbo / Sanpu
  [4:1.1] Arunachal Pradesh | Dihang / Siang
  [4:1.2] Assam | Brahmaputra (after the Dibang and Lohit join near Sadiya)
  [4:1.3] Bangladesh | Jamuna (below Dhubri); joins the Padma at Goalundo
[4:2] - Rises at **Chemayungdung glacier**, east of Mansarovar (Kailash range); also traced to the Angsi glacier.
[4:3] - Flows east, then makes a **U-turn at Namcha Barwa** and enters India through the deep **Dihang gorge** – "**born before the Himalayas**" (antecedent).
[4:4] - Length **2,900 km**, only **885 km** in India; catchment **Tibet (China), Bhutan, India, Bangladesh**; navigable **1,280 km up to Dibrugarh**.
[4:5] - **"Sorrow of Assam"** – floods; **Majuli** (upper Assam) = **largest riverine island** in the world.
[4:6] - **China's dams:** **Zangmu** (construction from **Nov 2010**) on the Yarlung Zangbo, Tibet.
[4:7] # Tributaries
[4:8] TABLE head: Right bank (north, Himalayan) | Left bank (south)
  [4:8.0] Subansiri, Kameng (Jia Bharali), Manas, Sankosh, Teesta | Burhi Dihing, Dhansiri, Dikhu, Kopili
[4:9] - Also: **Dibang, Lohit** (headstreams in Arunachal). **Lohit and Subansiri** flow through **Arunachal Pradesh**; the **Barak** does not.
[4:10] - **Sankosh** – rises in northern Bhutan; forms the **Assam–West Bengal** boundary.
[4:11] - **Teesta** – rises at **Tso Lhamo** lake, North Sikkim (**not** the Brahmaputra's source); largest tributary **Rangeet** (rises in Sikkim); joins the **Brahmaputra in Bangladesh**, not the Bay of Bengal.
[4:12] # Barak (Meghna) system
[4:13] - **Barak** rises in the **Manipur hills**; the **important river of Manipur and Mizoram**; headstream of the **Meghna**.
[4:14] - Tributaries of the Barak: **Katakhal, Jiri, Chiri, Madhura (Modhura)**, Sonai, Dhaleswari.
