## SUB 0 | Western and Eastern Ghats and the Southern Hills
[0:0] # Western Ghats vs Eastern Ghats
[0:1] TABLE head: Feature | Western Ghats (Sahyadri) | Eastern Ghats
  [0:1.0] Extent | Tapti valley to near Kanyakumari, about 1,600 km | Mahanadi (Odisha) to the Vaigai (Tamil Nadu), about 1,750 km
  [0:1.1] Continuity | Continuous; steep west face, gentle east slope; watershed of the Peninsula | Discontinuous, eroded groups of hills; cut by the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri (not the Narmada)
  [0:1.2] Height | 900–1,600 m; rises from north to south (higher in the south) | Average about 600 m (610 m)
  [0:1.3] Rocks | Deccan lava in the north (horizontal sheets → stepped, "landing stair" look) | Khondalite and charnockite
  [0:1.4] Highest | Anamudi 2,695 m (Anaimalai, Kerala) = highest in South India and in the Peninsula | Jindhagada 1,690 m (PMF, Rocket) / Arma Konda 1,680 m = highest peak of Andhra Pradesh (Rocket); Mahendragiri ~1,501 m (Odisha)
  [0:1.5] Passes / gaps | Thal Ghat (Mumbai–Nashik), Bhor Ghat (Mumbai–Pune), Pal Ghat | River gaps
[0:2] - **"Ghat"** in Hindi means both **river-landing steps (stairs)** and a **mountain pass**; the WBCS 2017 key gives **"gap or pass"**.
[0:3] - **Local names, north → south:** **Sahyadri** (Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka) → **Nilgiri** → **Anaimalai** → Palani → **Cardamom (Yela Mala)**.
[0:4] - Sahyadri peaks: **Kalsubai (1,646 m, Maharashtra)**, Salher, Mahabaleshwar; **Mullayanagiri 1,892 m = highest of Karnataka** (Baba Budan hills).
[0:5] - A statement "the Western Ghats are higher in their northern region" is **false**; the Narmada and Tapi valleys are old rift valleys and the Tapi lies south of the Satpura.
[0:6] - **Vandaravu (Palani), Perumal Malai (Palani), Velliangiri (Coimbatore) and Anamudi** are peaks/hills of the **Western Ghats**.
[0:7] - Goran Ghat is **not** a Western Ghats pass.
[0:8] # Nilgiris – where the two Ghats meet
[0:9] - The **Eastern and Western Ghats meet at the Nilgiri Hills** (Gudalur), the **tri-junction of Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu**; "Blue Mountains".
[0:10] - **Doddabetta (2,637 m)** = highest of the Nilgiris, second in South India; **Ooty** lies in the Nilgiris; home of the **Toda** tribe.
[0:11] - **Pal Ghat (Palakkad gap)** at the southern end of the Nilgiris, **between the Nilgiris (north) and the Anaimalai (south)**; 144 m, about 24–30 km wide; misfit river Ponnani; Kerala–TN rail and road link.
[0:12] # Hills of the far south
[0:13] TABLE head: Hills | Place | Fact
  [0:13.0] Anaimalai | Kerala–TN | Anamudi; three ranges radiate from it
  [0:13.1] Palani (Palni) | Dindigul, TN | Kodaikanal
  [0:13.2] Cardamom (Yela Mala) | Kerala–Tamil Nadu border | Southernmost range; closest to Kanyakumari; Agastyamalai; tea at Kanan Devan (Munnar)
  [0:13.3] Shevaroy (Servarayan) | Salem, Tamil Nadu (Eastern Ghats) | Yercaud
  [0:13.4] Javadi, Biligirirangan | TN / Karnataka | Eastern Ghats remnants
  [0:13.5] Seshachalam | Southern Andhra Pradesh (Eastern Ghats, Tirupati) | Not part of the Western Ghats or Nilgiri/Palani
[0:14] - **Tea** grows in Kanan Devan, the Nilgiris and Darjeeling; **Girnar** (Gujarat) has **no tea**.
[0:15] - **UPSC 2023:** none of the three statements is correct – Amarkantak joins the **Vindhya and Satpura** (not Sahyadri); Biligirirangan is in **Karnataka** (Eastern Ghats, not Satpura); Seshachalam is in **Andhra Pradesh** (Eastern Ghats, not the southernmost Western Ghats).
[0:16] - **Eastern Ghats in AP:** Nallamala (Chenchus; **Srisailam Tiger Reserve**), Erramala (Krishna–Penna divide), Seshachalam (**red sanders**), Velikonda, Palkonda (Tirupati), **Papikondalu** (Godavari gorge); Araku Valley = "**Ooty of Andhra Pradesh**".
[0:17] > Exam angle: the Nilgiri "meeting point" is asked again and again (UPSC 2008, UPPSC, CGPSC 2024, MPPSC 2025); Anamudi vs Doddabetta is the classic trap.

## SUB 1 | Peninsular Plateau – Structure, Plateaus and the Deccan Traps
[1:0] # Structure
[1:1] - **Oldest and largest** physiographic division; a **Precambrian shield**, part of **Gondwana land**; one of the most **stable** blocks; **not** of volcanic origin overall; rich in **metallic minerals**.
[1:2] - About **16 lakh sq km**; **triangular**; outer edge: Delhi ridge (NW), **Rajmahal hills (E)**, Gir (W), Cardamom hills (S); extensions: **Meghalaya / Karbi Anglong plateau** and Kutch-Kathiawar.
[1:3] - **Tilts east** (most rivers flow east); Narmada and Tapi flow west in rift valleys.
[1:4] - NCERT: three broad groups – **Deccan Plateau, Central Highlands, North-Eastern Plateau** (Kutch is not one).
[1:5] - **S.P. Chatterjee** divides the Peninsula into 8 parts; the **Mahanadi basin** falls in the **Northern Deccan Plateau** (old app key).
[1:6] # Plateaus
[1:7] TABLE head: Plateau | Where | Facts
  [1:7.0] Malwa | Western MP and SE Rajasthan | Between the Aravalli and the Vindhya (Vindhya to its south); Bundelkhand to its east; lava, black (lava) soil; two drainage systems – to the Arabian Sea (Narmada, Mahi) and to the Bay of Bengal (Chambal, Betwa → Yamuna)
  [1:7.1] Chota Nagpur | Mainly Jharkhand; also Chhattisgarh, Odisha, WB, Bihar | Storehouse of minerals, "Ruhr of India", biggest mineral deposits; series of plateaus (patlands: Ranchi, Hazaribagh, Palamu) – "a front sloping" plateau; source of Damodar (Khamarpat hill), Koel, Subarnarekha; Rajmahal hills on its north-eastern edge (Jharkhand)
  [1:7.2] Deccan | South of the Narmada; ~7 lakh sq km | Largest plateau; northern boundary Satpura, Mahadeo, Maikal; Western and Eastern Ghats on the sides; Andhra Pradesh lies on the Deccan plateau; Vidarbha is the eastern region of Maharashtra
  [1:7.3] Meghalaya (Shillong) | Meghalaya | Part of the Peninsular Plateau, cut off by the Malda (Garo–Rajmahal) gap; Garo, Khasi, Jaintia hills (Bhuban hills of Assam are not part); living root bridges of the Khasis in Meghalaya
  [1:7.4] Dandakaranya | Chhattisgarh and Odisha, also Telangana, Maharashtra, AP | ~89,078 sq km; central-eastern highland
  [1:7.5] Bhander | MP | Southern part of the Vindhyan scarpland
  [1:7.6] Karnataka (Mysore) | Karnataka | Malnad = hilly forested western Karnataka (not AP)
[1:8] - **Lowest-lying region of India:** **Kuttanad** (Kerala), about 2 m below sea level.
[1:9] # Deccan Traps
[1:10] - **Fissure eruptions** of basaltic lava → **solidification of lava**; at the **end of the Cretaceous** (Cretaceous–Eocene), in the **north-west** of the Peninsula.
[1:11] - 'Trap' = Swedish for **step**; once ~10 lakh sq km, now ~5 lakh sq km; thickness 3,000 m (west) → 150 m (east); weathers into **regur** (black cotton soil).
[1:12] TABLE head: Formation | Age (UPSC 1997)
  [1:12.0] Deccan Traps | Cretaceous–Eocene
  [1:12.1] Western Ghats | Late Cenozoic
  [1:12.2] Aravalli | Pre-Cambrian
  [1:12.3] Narmada–Tapti alluvium | Pleistocene
[1:13] - **Structural statements:** Meghalaya plateau = extension of the Deccan/Peninsular plateau; Kashmir valley formed in a **synclinorium**; the Gangetic plain in a **foredeep** (all true).
[1:14] > Exam angle: oldest region = Peninsular Plateau (CGPSC ×2); Chota Nagpur as the mineral storehouse (×4).

## SUB 4 | Indian Desert and the Rann of Kutch
[4:0] # Thar (Great Indian Desert)
[4:1] - Lies **north-west / west of the Aravallis**; undulating sandy plain with **barchans and longitudinal (seif) dunes**; rainfall **below 150 mm**; **Marusthali** (true desert, west) and **Bagar** (semi-arid strip along the Aravallis).
[4:2] - Order of observation (APPSC key): **semi-arid/arid weather → sand dunes → thorny bushes → seasonal streams** that appear only with rain.
[4:3] - Extent: from the edge of the **Rann of Kutch** across the **Luni** to the Aravalli face (APPSC key).
[4:4] - **Luni** = the only large river of the region; rises in the **Aravalli near Ajmer**; **"Indian desert" and "Thar desert" are the same desert**; it is **lost in the Rann of Kutch** – does not reach the sea or the Gulf of Kutch.
[4:5] - Under the sea in the Mesozoic (Akal wood fossils, Jaisalmer); salt lakes – **Sambhar** largest.
[4:6] # Rann of Kutch
[4:7] - **Seasonal salt marsh** (Great Rann north, Little Rann south), Gujarat–Sindh; once a shallow arm of the Arabian Sea / Kutch an island; **repeated falls in sea level** left the present marshland (UPSC 2023).
[4:8] - **Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary** – Little Rann, **Gujarat**; **Banni grasslands** at the edge of the salt marsh – **Gujarat**.
[4:9] - **Agarias** = traditional **salt-pan workers** of the Little Rann, Gujarat (UPSC 2009).

## SUB 5 | Physiographic Divisions of India
[5:0] - **Six divisions** (NCERT): **Himalayas, Northern Plains, Peninsular Plateau, Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, Islands**.
[5:1] - **Plains** cover the largest share of land (about **43%**), mountains ~30%, plateaus ~27%.
[5:2] - **Oldest** = Peninsular Plateau; **youngest** = Northern Plains (NextGen p.13).
[5:3] - Statements: the **Sahara** is **not** in the subcontinent; the **Eastern Ghats** run along the east coast; the **Deccan** is a **basaltic plateau**, not fertile lowland.
[5:4] TABLE head: Feature | Description
  [5:4.0] Himalaya | World's highest range, peaks over 29,000 ft
  [5:4.1] Thar | Arid region of sand dunes and extreme temperatures
  [5:4.2] Deccan | High plateau covering much of southern India
  [5:4.3] Ganga–Brahmaputra delta | Vast alluvial plain where two great rivers meet
  [5:4.4] Chota Nagpur | Central/eastern India, rich in minerals
  [5:4.5] Malwa | MP and Rajasthan
  [5:4.6] Karnataka plateau | Karnataka, TN, Telangana parts
  [5:4.7] Deccan plateau (match) | Southern India, between the Eastern and Western Ghats

## SUB 6 | Northern Plains
[6:0] - **Aggradational** plain built by Himalayan rivers (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra); ~6.5–7 lakh sq km; 2,400–3,200 km long; northern edge = **Himalayan Frontal Fault**; geologically **youngest** division.
[6:1] - Very gentle slope: **about 200 m drop over ~1,600 km** from the Yamuna at Delhi to the Bay of Bengal (average plain height ~200 m).
[6:2] TABLE head: Belt | Feature
  [6:2.0] Bhabar | Pebbles at the Shiwalik foot; streams sink
  [6:2.1] Terai | Swampy, marshy, forested belt where streams re-emerge
  [6:2.2] Bhangar | Older alluvium, kankar
  [6:2.3] Khadar | Newer floodplain alluvium
  [6:2.4] Bhur | Undulating aeolian sandy deposit in the upper Ganga–Yamuna doab
[6:3] - Punjab plain = doabs of Indus tributaries; Ganga plain divisions: Ganga–Yamuna doab, Rohilkhand, Avadh, Bihar, Bengal (not Malwa); **Kosi** = "Sorrow of Bihar", a mega-fan; Ganga–Brahmaputra delta = deltaic plain; Chambal = ravines/badlands; loess plains = wind-blown silt.
