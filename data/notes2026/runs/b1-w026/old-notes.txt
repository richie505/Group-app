=== NOTES 1:26 | Jainism – Tirthankaras, Mahavira, Triratna, Panchamahavratas, Anekantavada and Syadvada
## SUB 0 | Tirthankaras – First, 23rd, Last and Their Symbols
[26:0:0] # The 24 Tirthankaras
[26:0:1] - **Tirthankara** = "ford-maker", one who shows the path across the cycle of rebirth – a **Jain** term (UPPCS 1993). **Jina** = conqueror (of passions).
[26:0:2] - **Historicity:** only the last two, **Parshvanatha (23rd)** and **Mahavira (24th)**, are confirmed by Jain and Buddhist texts; the first 22 are uncertain.
[26:0:3] - **First:** **Rishabhadeva (Adinatha / Rishabhanatha)**, born at **Ayodhya** to Nabhi and Marudevi (APPSC G1; CGPSC 2013).
[26:0:4] - **Last (24th):** **Vardhamana Mahavira** (APPSC FBO; IAS 1993; WBPCS 2008) – a **reformer** of an older faith, not its founder (CDS 2011).
[26:0:5] - **Originator of Jainism:** Jain tradition → **Rishabhadeva** (UPPSC 2010).
[26:0:6]   - **⚠ Disputed:** historians treat Parshvanatha as the first historical teacher and some books call Mahavira the founder.
[26:0:7] - **20th:** **Munisuvrata (Munisuvrat Swami)** (APPSC Extension Officer).
[26:0:8] TABLE head: No. | Tirthankara | Symbol | Nirvana / place
  [26:0:8.0] 1 | Rishabhadeva | Bull | Mount Kailash (Ashtapada); born Ayodhya
  [26:0:8.1] 2 | Ajitanatha | Elephant | Sammed Shikhar
  [26:0:8.2] 12 | Vasupujya | Buffalo | Champapuri (not Pavapuri)
  [26:0:8.3] 13 | Vimalanatha | Boar (not buffalo) | Sammed Shikhar
  [26:0:8.4] 20 | Munisuvrata | Tortoise | Sammed Shikhar
  [26:0:8.5] 21 | Naminatha | Blue lotus | Sammed Shikhar
  [26:0:8.6] 22 | Neminatha (Arishtanemi) | Conch | Girnar (Urjayanta)
  [26:0:8.7] 23 | Parshvanatha | Serpent | Sammed Shikhar
  [26:0:8.8] 24 | Mahavira | Lion | Pavapuri
[26:0:9] - **Incorrect pair (SI 2023):** Vimalanatha – Buffalo ✗ (boar).
[26:0:10] - **Not Tirthankaras:** **Nathamuni** (Sri Vaishnava acharya; UPPCS 2004) and **Bahubali (Gomateshwara)**, son of Rishabhadeva – so "Gomateshwara represents the last Tirthankara" is the wrong statement (UPSC). Chandraprabha (8th), Sambhava (3rd) and Nemi (22nd) are Tirthankaras.
[26:0:11] # Parshvanatha (23rd)
[26:0:12] - **Born at Varanasi** to **King Asvasena and Queen Vama** – associated mainly with **Varanasi** (UPPSC 2016; IAS 2003).
[26:0:13] - Renounced at **30**; enlightenment after **84 days** of penance; died at **100**, about **250 years before Mahavira**.
[26:0:14] - **Nirvana at Sammed Shikhar** (Parasnath hill, **Jharkhand**) → a Jain **Siddha Kshetra** for him (UPPCS 2002 ×2); Pava = Mahavira, Champa = Vasupujya, Urjayanta = Neminatha.
[26:0:15] - Taught **four vows (chaturyama)**; believed in the eternity of matter; his followers wore **white**.
[26:0:16] > Exam angle: first → **Rishabha**; 23rd → **Parshvanatha (Varanasi, Sammed Shikhar)**; last → **Mahavira**; Bahubali is **not** a Tirthankara.

## SUB 1 | Life of Mahavira
[26:1:0] TABLE head: Aspect | Detail
  [26:1:0.0] Birth | Kundagrama (Kundalpur / Basukunda) near Vaishali; 599 BCE (traditional) or 540 BCE (NextGen, PMF)
  [26:1:0.1] Father | Siddhartha, chief of the Jnatrika Kshatriya clan
  [26:1:0.2] Mother | Trishala, Lichchhavi princess, sister of Chetaka
  [26:1:0.3] Varna | Kshatriya (APPSC G1)
  [26:1:0.4] Wife, daughter | Yashoda; daughter Anojja (Priyadarshana) – Svetambara view; Digambaras say he never married
  [26:1:0.5] Renunciation | At 30; one garment for 13 months, then a naked monk; 12 years of austerity
  [26:1:0.6] Kaivalya | At 42, under a sal tree at Jrimbhikagrama on the Rijupalika (UP RO 2017)
  [26:1:0.7] Death | Pava (Pavapuri) near Rajgriha, aged 72; 527 BCE (traditional) or 468 BCE (NextGen)
[26:1:1] - **Died at:** **Pavapuri** – not Rajgir (the nearby town), Sanchi, Ranchi or Samastipur (BPSC 2002; State PSC ×2).
[26:1:2] - **Birthplace:** **Kundagrama / Kundalpur** (UPPCS 2004; State PSC) – not Vaishali itself, Pataliputra or Magadha.
[26:1:3] - **"Not true" (CGPSC 2022):** "He did not marry" ✗ – he married Yashoda; the 24th Tirthankara ✓, Trishala Chetaka's sister ✓, died 527 BCE at Pavapuri ✓.
[26:1:4] - **Titles:** Mahavira (great hero), **Jina** (conqueror), **Nirgrantha** (free from bonds), Kevalin, Arhat – **Tathagata** is the Buddha's.
[26:1:5] - **Preaching:** 30 years in Kosala, Magadha and further east; first sermon at **Rajgriha**; visited the courts of Bimbisara and Ajatashatru.
[26:1:6] # Family and Lichchhavi links
[26:1:7] - **Chetaka:** chief of the **Lichchhavis** of Vaishali (RAS 2013: Chetak–Lichchhavi ✓); Mahavira's **maternal uncle**; his daughter **Chellana** married Bimbisara.
[26:1:8] - **IAS 2003:** "Mahavira's mother was the **daughter** of Chetaka" ✗ (she was his **sister**); Buddha's mother was a **Koliya** princess ✓; Parshvanatha of **Banaras** ✓ → **II and III**.
[26:1:9]   - **⚠ Disputed:** the Digambara tradition makes Trishala Chetaka's daughter; the UPSC key follows "sister".
[26:1:10] - **Lichchhavi republic:** first to recognise Jainism.
[26:1:11] # Disciples
[26:1:12] - **Jamali**, his nephew and son-in-law: **first disciple / follower** (UPPCS 2008; SSC 2013) – not Yashoda (wife), Anojja (daughter) or Trishala (mother).
[26:1:13] - **11 Ganadharas**; only **Arya Sudharma** survived him and became the **first Thera (head of the Sangha)** (MPPSC 2009); **Bhadrabahu** was the 6th Thera.

## SUB 2 | Anekantavada, Syadvada and Their Exponents
[26:2:0] # Doctrines
[26:2:1] TABLE head: Doctrine | Meaning
  [26:2:1.0] Anekantavada | Reality is many-sided; no single view holds the whole truth – basis of tolerance and pluralism
  [26:2:1.1] Syadvada | Relativity of knowledge: every statement is prefixed with syat ("maybe", "in some respect") – conditioned predication
  [26:2:1.2] Saptabhanginaya | The seven-fold predication of Syadvada
  [26:2:1.3] Nayavada | Theory of partial standpoints
[26:2:2] - **Two main doctrines of Jain philosophy:** Anekantavada and Syadvada ✓; every thing has many properties, the permanent one being its **attribute (guna)** ✓ (CGPSC 2023 → **both**). Everything that exists has **substance (dravya), quality (guna) and mode (paryaya)**.
[26:2:3] - **Core theory of Jainism:** **Anekantavada** (UPSC 2009; JPSC 2011); **Syadvada** is Jain (UKPSC 2005; APPSC G1 1987).
[26:2:4] - **Jain vs Buddhist doctrines (RAS 2021):** Anekantavada, Syadvada = **Jain**; **Sarvastivada, Sunyavada = Buddhist**; niyati = Ajivika; materialism = Charvaka.
[26:2:5] # Exponents
[26:2:6] TABLE head: Person | Fact
  [26:2:6.0] Kundakundacharya | Digambara monk; propounded / preached Syadvada (APPSC 2008 ×2) and Anekantavada; wrote Samayasara (APPSC G2 2008 ×2), Pravachanasara, Panchastikayasara, Niyamasara; ashram at Konakondla (Anantapur); first Jain monk of Andhra
  [26:2:6.1] Somadeva Suri | Title "Syadvadachala Simha" (APPSC G2 2011 ×3); wrote Yasastilaka Champu, Nitivakyamrita
  [26:2:6.2] Kalakasuri | Jain monk honoured at the Satavahana court (Pratishthana)
[26:2:7] - **⚠ Trap:** Kundakunda **preached** Syadvada; Somadeva Suri only **held the title**. Nagarjuna and Bavari are Buddhists.
[26:2:8] - **Yogasara** is not Kundakunda's – APPSC keys it to **Nagarjuna** (Buddhist).
[26:2:9] > Exam angle: "preached Syadvada" / "Samayasara" → **Kundakundacharya**; "Syadvadachala Simha" → **Somadeva Suri**.

## SUB 3 | Triratna, Vows and Jain Practices
[26:3:0] # Triratna (Ratnatraya) – the path to moksha
[26:3:1] - **Right faith (belief), right knowledge, right conduct (action)** – the Jain three jewels (UPPCS 2004; State PSC; CGPSC 2017 ×2).
[26:3:2] - **⚠ Not a Jain jewel:** **right view** – the first step of the Buddhist Eightfold Path (SSC 2011).
[26:3:3] - **⚠ Disputed:** Buddhism also has a Triratna (**Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha** – the three refuges); with Jainism offered, keys give **Jainism**. Nyaya and Yoga have none.
[26:3:4] - **Match (APPSC G2):** Jainism – **Triratna**; Buddhism – **Ashtanga Marga**; Saivism – **Linga**; Animism – **Nature worship** → a-iii, b-ii, c-iv, d-i.
[26:3:5] # Five vows
[26:3:6] TABLE head: Vow | Meaning | Given by
  [26:3:6.0] Ahimsa | Non-violence | Parshvanatha
  [26:3:6.1] Satya | Truth | Parshvanatha
  [26:3:6.2] Asteya | Non-stealing | Parshvanatha
  [26:3:6.3] Aparigraha | Non-possession | Parshvanatha
  [26:3:6.4] Brahmacharya | Celibacy (chastity) | Added by Mahavira
[26:3:7] - **Mahavratas** = great vows of monks; **Anuvratas** = small vows of householders – a **Jain** concept (UPSC 1995 ×2; CGPSC 2013).
[26:3:8] - **Householders** also gave cooked food to the needy daily; strict followers avoided **agriculture**.
[26:3:9] # Practices
[26:3:10] TABLE head: Practice | Meaning
  [26:3:10.0] Sallekhana / Santhara / Samadhi Marana | Ritual fast unto death at life's end – Jain (CGPSC 2015 ×2); Chandragupta Maurya at Shravanabelagola
  [26:3:10.1] Paryushana | Festival; last day Samvatsari = forgiveness day
  [26:3:10.2] Pratikramana | Self-review
  [26:3:10.3] Padmasana, Kayotsarga | Postures of Tirthankara images
[26:3:11] - **Digambara** = sky-clad; the **Agamas** are in **Ardhamagadhi**.

## SUB 4 | Soul, Karma and the Uncreated Universe
[26:4:0] - **Souls everywhere:** all objects, even **rocks, stones and running water**, have a soul and feel injury → extreme ahimsa (UPSC 2023 ×2: **Jainism**).
[26:4:1] - **No creator:** the world is **not created** by a supreme being; it runs by an **eternal law** of decay and development → "**Universal Law**" (UPSC 2011). The universe is **eternal**, with **no final annihilation** (UPPCS 2014: Jainism); Jainism is non-theistic.
[26:4:2] - **Four forms of existence:** **Deva** (gods), **Manushya** (humans), **Naraki** (hell beings), **Tiryancha** (animals and plants) – **not Yaksha** (UPSC 2026).
[26:4:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [26:4:3.0] Jiva / Ajiva | Soul / non-conscious matter
  [26:4:3.1] Karma | Material bondage of the soul, cause of rebirth
  [26:4:3.2] Samvara | Stopping the inflow of karma
  [26:4:3.3] Nirjara | Shedding existing karma by penance
  [26:4:3.4] Kaivalya (Kevala jnana) | Complete knowledge, omniscience (IAS 1993)
  [26:4:3.5] Siddha | Fully liberated soul
  [26:4:3.6] Tattva | Origination, decay, permanence
[26:4:4] - **Salvation:** only through **monastic life** – householders cannot attain it; the Vedas and Brahmana supremacy are rejected.
[26:4:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.44, p.46; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.69-72; app notes 1:26 (sub 4)
## SUB 5 | Jainism and Buddhism Compared
[26:5:0] TABLE head: Point | Jainism | Buddhism
  [26:5:0.0] Penance | Extreme path, self-mortification | Middle Way – avoids extremes
  [26:5:0.1] Soul | Eternal jiva in all things | Anatta (no-self)
  [26:5:0.2] Ahimsa | Stricter, even in thought | Important, less strict
  [26:5:0.3] Goal | Kevala jnana, moksha | Nirvana
  [26:5:0.4] Spread | Mostly within India | Widely abroad
[26:5:1] - **Common to both:** **karma and transmigration** (UPPCS 1996), **indifference to the Vedas**, **denial of rituals**, **non-injury to animals**, both Kshatriya-led shramana movements.
[26:5:2] - **Not common:** **self-mortification** (BPSC 2001; State PSC) and **avoiding the extremes** of penance and enjoyment (Buddhist only) → UPSC 2012: **II and III**; UPSC 1996: **II, III and IV**.
[26:5:3] - **⚠ Trap:** "both men and women could attain salvation" and "salvation only after death" are not shared beliefs.
[26:5:4] - **Decline of Jainism:** severe austerities, loss of royal patronage, the Digambara–Svetambara split.
[26:5:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.70, p.84; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.44, p.52; app notes 1:26 (sub 8)

## SUB 9 | Pratityasamutpada – Dependent Origination
[28:9:0] - **Pratityasamutpada (Paticca-samuppada):** every phenomenon arises from a cause; a **12-link** chain (**12 nidanas**, bhavachakra); removing ignorance ends the chain.
[28:9:1] TABLE head: Order | Links
  [28:9:1.0] 1–4 | Avidya (ignorance) → Samskara (impressions) → Vijnana (consciousness) → Namarupa
  [28:9:1.1] 5–8 | Shadayatana (six senses) → Sparsha (contact) → Vedana (feeling) → Trishna (craving)
  [28:9:1.2] 9–12 | Upadana (clinging) → Bhava (becoming) → Jati (birth) → Jara-marana (old age–death)
[28:9:2] - **Cause of Samskara = Avidya** (CGPSC 2024) – the first link and root of suffering; Vijnana comes after Samskara.
