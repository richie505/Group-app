[0:6] - Mandal **mandate**: identify the socially and educationally backward; dealt with **reservation in government employment** → basis of the **27% OBC quota**.
[0:7] - **Rohini findings:** ~**97%** of benefits taken by **25%** of sub-castes; ~**1,000** communities with zero representation; 14 extensions. It examined **OBCs**, not STs.

[2:1] - **"Backward Classes" is NOT defined** in the Constitution (APPSC G2: the "defined" statement is the wrong one).
[2:2] - Correct term: **Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBC)** – not "Socially and Economically".
[2:3] - **Art. 16(4)** backward classes are **mainly social**; they need not be both socially and educationally backward (Indra Sawhney) – identified mainly by **caste and social backwardness**.
[2:4] - **Art. 338(10)** (before 2018): references to SCs included such backward classes as the **President** specified – BC complaints went to the SC Commission.
[2:5] - For the CEI Act, OBCs are the classes "so determined by the **Central Government**".
[2:6] - **Protective discrimination** = e.g. **reservation of posts for STs**; SHG aid or minority scholarships are welfare schemes; one-region-only jobs would be plain discrimination.
[2:7] - **First reservation in India:** **Kolhapur State, 1902**, under **Shahu Maharaj** (for non-Brahmins and backward classes).
[2:8] > Number game: Art. 340 = commission (Kalelkar, Mandal, Rohini all under it); 338B = NCBC; 342A = SEBC list; NHRC is statutory, not constitutional.

## SUB 3 | Landmark Cases – Indra Sawhney to Janhit Abhiyan
[3:0] # Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992, "Mandal case")
[3:1] - **Caste** can be the main criterion of backwardness (overruled Balaji on this); **upheld 27%** OBC quota.
[3:2] - Total reservation **not above 50%** except in **extraordinary cases**; applied **year by year**; **carry-forward** allowed within 50%.
[3:3] - **Creamy layer** excluded from OBCs (the concept originates here).
[3:4] - Reservation only at **initial appointment, not promotion** → answered by the **77th** Amendment.
[3:5] - Directed a **permanent statutory body** for OBC lists → **NCBC** (1993).
[3:6] TABLE head: Case | Year | Issue decided
  [3:6.0] State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan | 1951 | Caste-based quota struck down → 1st Amendment, Art. 15(4)
  [3:6.1] M.R. Balaji v. State of Mysore | 1962 | Reservation generally within 50%; caste not the sole test
  [3:6.2] P.A. Inamdar | 2005 | No quota in private unaided institutions → undone by 93rd
  [3:6.3] M. Nagaraj | 2006 | Upheld 77th/81st/82nd/85th; promotion quota needs data on backwardness, inadequacy and efficiency
  [3:6.4] Ashoka Kumar Thakur v. UoI | 2008 | Upheld the 93rd Amendment and 27% OBC quota in central educational institutions (CEI Act); creamy layer applies to OBCs
  [3:6.5] Ram Singh v. UoI | 2015 | Jats struck out of the Central OBC list
  [3:6.6] Jarnail Singh | 2018 | Creamy layer can apply to SC/ST promotions
  [3:6.7] Jaishri Patil (Maratha case) | 5 May 2021 | Maratha SEBC quota struck down (breach of 50%); only Centre identifies SEBCs → reversed by 105th
  [3:6.8] Janhit Abhiyan | 7 Nov 2022 | EWS upheld
  [3:6.9] Union of India v. Rohith Nathan | 11 March 2026 | Parental salary alone cannot place a candidate in the creamy layer
[3:7] - **OBC quota timeline:** 27% central jobs from **1993**; **CEI (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006** – 27% OBC seats, in force **2007**.

## SUB 4 | Creamy Layer and EWS Reservation
[4:0] # Creamy layer
[4:1] - **Status-based exclusions**: children of constitutional post-holders, **Class-I officers**, parents **both Class-II** officers, senior armed-forces officers, large landowners, high earners – **not all OBC children** are excluded.
[4:2] TABLE head: Year | Income limit (per annum)
  [4:2.0] 1993 | ₹1 lakh
  [4:2.1] 2004 | ₹2.5 lakh
  [4:2.2] 2008 | ₹4.5 lakh
  [4:2.3] 2013 | ₹6 lakh
  [4:2.4] 2017 | ₹8 lakh (still current)
[4:3] - **SC/ST creamy layer debate:** the Union Government opposed it (August 2026) – SC/ST status flows from **Arts. 341 and 342**.
[4:4] # EWS – 103rd Amendment (2019)
[4:5] - **Up to 10%** for economically weaker sections **not covered** by SC/ST/OBC quotas; **over and above** existing reservation (outside the 50% cap).
[4:6] - In jobs and education, including **private** institutions; **enabling, not mandatory** for States.
[4:7] - Criteria: family income **below ₹8 lakh**; agricultural land below **5 acres**; flat below **1,000 sq ft**.
[4:8] - **First State to implement** the 10% EWS quota: **Gujarat**, from **14 January 2019**.
[4:9] - **Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022):** upheld **3:2** by a **five-judge** bench; dissent: **CJI U.U. Lalit** and **Justice S. Ravindra Bhat**.
