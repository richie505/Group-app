=== NOTES 2:135 | Reservation – Constitutional Basis, Indra Sawhney, Creamy Layer, Ews, Promotion
## SUB 0 | Backward Classes in the Constitution
[135:0:0] - **"Backward Classes" is NOT defined** in the Constitution (APPSC G2 2017: the "defined" statement is the wrong one).
[135:0:1] - **Article 340**: the **President** appoints a commission to investigate the conditions of socially and educationally backward classes and recommend remedies. Kalelkar, Mandal and Rohini commissions were set up under it.
[135:0:2] - Correct term: **Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBC)** – not "Socially and Economically".
[135:0:3] - Under Art. 16(4), backward classes are identified mainly by **caste and social backwardness**, not economic criteria alone (Indra Sawhney).
[135:0:4] - For the CEI Act, OBCs are classes "so determined by the **Central Government**".
[135:0:5] SEBC = Socially and **Educationally** Backward Classes. "Economically" is the distractor.

## SUB 4 | Landmark Cases
[135:4:0] # Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992, Mandal case)
[135:4:1] - **Caste** can be the main criterion of backwardness (overruled Balaji on this).
[135:4:2] - Total reservation **not above 50%** except in **extraordinary cases**; applied **year by year**; carry-forward allowed within 50%.
[135:4:3] - **Creamy layer** excluded from OBCs (the concept originates here).
[135:4:4] - Reservation only at **initial appointment, not promotion** → answered by the **77th** Amendment.
[135:4:5] - Directed a **permanent statutory body** for OBC lists → **NCBC**.
[135:4:6] # Case Table
[135:4:7] TABLE head: Case | Year | Issue decided
  [135:4:7.0] State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan | 1951 | Caste-based quota struck down → 1st Amendment, Art. 15(4)
  [135:4:7.1] M.R. Balaji v. State of Mysore | 1962 | Reservation should be within 50%; caste not the sole test
  [135:4:7.2] Indra Sawhney | 1992 | 50% ceiling, creamy layer, no promotion quota
  [135:4:7.3] P.A. Inamdar | 2005 | No quota in private unaided institutions → undone by 93rd
  [135:4:7.4] M. Nagaraj | 2006 | Upheld 77th/81st/82nd/85th; promotion quota needs data on backwardness, inadequacy and efficiency; creamy layer and 50% ceiling not to be breached
  [135:4:7.5] Ashoka Kumar Thakur | 2008 | Upheld 93rd Amendment and 27% OBC quota (CEI Act); creamy layer applies to OBCs, not SCs/STs
  [135:4:7.6] Jarnail Singh | 2018 | Nagaraj wrong on needing quantifiable data on SC/ST backwardness; creamy layer can apply to SC/ST promotions; no 7-judge reference

## SUB 5 | Creamy Layer
[135:5:0] - **Status-based exclusions**: children of constitutional post-holders, **Class-I officers**, parents **both Class-II** officers, senior armed-forces officers, large landowners/property owners, high earners. Not all OBC children are excluded.

## SUB 6 | Ews Reservation – 103RD Amendment
[135:6:0] - **10%** (up to 10%) for economically weaker sections **not covered** by SC/ST/OBC quotas; **over and above** existing reservation.
[135:6:1] - In jobs and education, including **private** institutions; **enabling, not mandatory** for States.
[135:6:2] - Criteria: family income **below ₹8 lakh**; agricultural land below **5 acres**; flat below **1,000 sq ft**.
[135:6:3] - **Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022)**: upheld **3:2** by a **five-judge** bench; dissent: **CJI U.U. Lalit** and **Justice S. Ravindra Bhat**. The 50% cap applies to backward-class quotas; EWS stands outside it.
## SUB 7 | Obc Quota in Central Educational Institutions
[135:7:0] - Central jobs: **27%** OBC quota from **1993**.
[135:7:1] - 93rd Amendment (Art. 15(5)) is the base of the **CEI (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006**: **27%** OBC seats; in force **January 2007** (GS 2007 key: 93rd Amendment).
[135:7:2] - 40-50% of reserved central posts lie vacant.
[135:7:3] 27% = Mandal quota (implemented 1990, posts 1993) = CEI seats (2006).
