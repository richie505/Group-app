## SUB 0 | South Indian Roots - Alvars and Nayanars
[145:0:0] # Alvars vs Nayanars
[145:0:1] TABLE head: Feature | Alvars | Nayanars (Nayanmars)
  [145:0:1.0] Sect / deity | Vaishnava (Vishnu) | Shaiva (Shiva)
  [145:0:1.1] Traditional number | 12 | 63
  [145:0:1.2] Woman saint | Andal - the only female Alvar | Karaikkal Ammaiyar (extreme asceticism)
  [145:0:1.3] Key saints | Nammalvar, Tirumangai, Andal, Periyalvar, Poigai, Pudam | Appar, Sundarar, Tirugnana Sambandar, Manikkavachakar
  [145:0:1.4] Canon | Nalayira Divya Prabandham | Tevaram - earliest, most sacred part of the Tirumurai
[145:0:2] - Period c. **6th-9th centuries**; hymns in **Tamil**. Alvars were the **first Vaishnavite devotees** of South India.
[145:0:3] - Their hymns marked sacred sites that grew into temple and pilgrimage centres; they challenged caste and Brahmanical dominance (Nayanmars included Dalits, hunters and women).
[145:0:4] - **Tirumurai**: **12-volume** Tamil Shaiva canon, called the **"Fifth Veda"**; with the Vedas and Saiva agamas the base of **Saiva Siddhanta**.
[145:0:5] - Chola patronage: **Parantaka I** installed statues of Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar; **Rajaraja I** consecrated bronzes of all **63 Nayanmars at Chidambaram**.
[145:0:6] Alvars = 12 = Vishnu; Nayanars = 63 = Shiva. Karaikkal Ammaiyar is a Nayanar, not a female Alvar. "Tirugyan" was not an Alvar.

## SUB 2 | Virashaiva (lingayat) Movement and Its Spread Into Andhra
[145:2:0] # Persons
[145:2:1] TABLE head: Person | Contribution
  [145:2:1.0] Basavanna (Basaveshwara) | Founder, 12th century; minister of Kalachuri ruler Bijjala II; led the Sharana movement; Anubhava Mantapa
  [145:2:1.1] Akka Mahadevi | Woman saint, 12th-century Karnataka, Vachana tradition
  [145:2:1.2] Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya | Brought it into Andhra; Sivatattvasaram (~400 kanda poems); founder of Aradhya Saivism; originator of the Sataka form in Telugu
  [145:2:1.3] Palkuriki Somanatha (Kakatiya period) | Basava Puranam in simple Telugu Dwipada (APHQ 2019); Panditaradhya Charitra, Basavana Ragada, Vrushadhipa Satakam
[145:2:2] - Pandita reformers (Mallikarjuna, Manchana and others) spread it in Andhra; initial impact was limited.
[145:2:3] - Somanatha taught equality, devotion to Shiva, **dignity of labour**, opposition to caste.
[145:2:4] # Terms
[145:2:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [145:2:5.0] Anubhava Mantapa | Early "religious parliament" at Kalyana
  [145:2:5.1] Vachanas | Kannada prose-poems
  [145:2:5.2] Shunya Sampadane | Later compilation of Anubhava Mantapa discourses
  [145:2:5.3] Kayaka | Honest labour
  [145:2:5.4] Dasoha | Sharing surplus
  [145:2:5.5] Ishtalinga | Linga worn on the body; worship of Shiva outside temples
  [145:2:5.6] Aradhya Saivism | Moderate Andhra Shaiva sect; kept Vedic learning and varna while preaching Shiva bhakti
[145:2:6] - Virashaivas rejected caste, untouchability and ritualism, condemned **child marriage**, supported **widow remarriage**, and **questioned rebirth** (UPSC 2016).
[145:2:7] Sivatattvasaram = Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya, not Palkuriki Somanatha. Kayaka = labour; Dasoha = sharing.
[145:2:8] - **Panchacharyas:** Veerashaiva tradition names five founding acharyas - **Revanaradhya (Renukacharya)**, **Marularadhya**, **Ekoramaradhya**, **Panditaradhya** and **Vishwaradhya**; their five peethas are at Rambhapuri (Balehonnur), Ujjaini, Kedar, Srisailam and Kashi.
## SUB 3 | Annamacharya (1408-1503)
[145:3:0] - Born at **Tallapaka**; contemporary of **Saluva Narasimha** (APPSC-G2 2011).
[145:3:1] - About **32,000 sankirtanas** to **Lord Venkateswara of Tirumala**; about **12,000 survive on copper plates** at Tirumala.
[145:3:2] - **Veturi Prabhakara Sastry** recovered and edited the copper-plate songs.
[145:3:3] - Titles: **Padakavita Pitamaha**, **Sankirtanacharya**, **Harikirtanacharya**; father of the Telugu keertana; pioneer of the **pada / kriti** form of Carnatic music; preceded and influenced Tyagaraja.
[145:3:4] - Other works: **Sringara Manjari** (258 dvipadas), **Sri Venkateswara Satakam** (first Telugu Shringara Satakam), Dvipada Ramayanam, Alivelumangamma Satakam; Sanskrit **Sankirtana Lakshanam**, Venkatachala Mahatyam.
[145:3:5] - **Arudra** called him **"Incarnated Telugu"**. **Annamayya district** is named after him.
[145:3:6] Sri Venkateswara Satakam is Annamacharya's, not his son Peda Tirumalacharya's.

## SUB 5 | Bammera Potana (c. 1450-1510)
[145:5:0] - Native of **Bammera** (Janagama district, Telangana); contemporary of Srinatha.
[145:5:1] - **Birthplace debate:** most say **Bammera**; one tradition places Potana at **Vontimitta (Ontimitta), Kadapa**
[145:5:2] - Titles: **Sahaja Panditya (Sahaja Panditudu)**, **Nigarva Chudamani**.
[145:5:3] TABLE head: Work | Fact
  [145:5:3.0] Veerabhadra Vijayam | His first work, at the request of his guru Ivaturi Somanaradhya
  [145:5:3.1] Andhra Maha Bhagavatamu | Telugu Bhagavata Purana, dedicated to Lord Rama; Potana wrote 8 of 12 skandhas; printed by C.P. Brown, 1840
  [145:5:3.2] Bhogini Dandakam | First independent Telugu Dandakam; on Sarvajna Singabhupala and Bhogini
[145:5:4] - Other skandhas: Bopparaju Gangana (5th), Erchuri Singana (6th), Veligandala Narayana (11th-12th).
[145:5:5] - **Patronage**: vowed never to dedicate his work to a human king; **refused Sarvajna Singabhupala of Rachakonda**; offered the Bhagavatam to Sri Rama.
[145:5:6] Veerabhadra Vijayam is Potana's; the APPSC 2011 key gave it to Srinatha.
## SUB 6 | Srinatha (c. 1385-1475)
[145:6:0] # Courts and patrons
[145:6:1] TABLE head: Phase | Patron / place | What happened
  [145:6:1.0] Kondaveedu | Pedakomati Vema Reddy | Vidyadhikari (education minister); wrote the king's Sanskrit inscriptions
  [145:6:1.1] Rajahmundry (after Kondaveedu fell, 1424) | Veerabhadra Reddy, via Bendapudi Anantamatya | Bhimakhandam, Kasikhandam
  [145:6:1.2] Vijayanagara | Devaraya II | Defeated Gauda Dindima Bhatta; Kanakabhishekam; title Kavi Sarvabhauma
  [145:6:1.3] After Rajahmundry fell (1448) | Palnadu, then Srisailam | Poverty; refuge with Santhalingayya of the Bhikshavritti matha
[145:6:2] - Titles: **Kavi Sarvabhauma**, Andhra Bhasha Naishadhabhava, Sarvavidya Parangata, Brahmadatta Varaprasad.
[145:6:3] - Pioneer of the **Prabandha** style; his age = **Kavya Yuga**; first reference to **paper** as writing material in Andhra (APPSC 2012).
[145:6:4] # Works
[145:6:5] TABLE head: Work | Dedication / point
  [145:6:5.0] Haravilasamu | To Avachi Tippaya Setti; Reddi-era sea trade, imports and exports (APPSC 2019)
  [145:6:5.1] Sringara Naishadhamu | Telugu Naishadham of Sriharsha; to minister Mamidi (Madiki) Singana
  [145:6:5.2] Bhimakhandamu (Bhimeswara Puranam) | Rajahmundry court of Veerabhadra Reddy
  [145:6:5.3] Kasikhandamu | Rajahmundry Reddi period
  [145:6:5.4] Sivaratri Mahatmyam | Story of Sukumara; to Mummadi Santayya
  [145:6:5.5] Madanavilasa Bhanam | Bhana-style work; NOT Srinatha's (G2 2025 key)
  [145:6:5.6] Maruttaratcharitra, Salivahana Saptasati | Youth works; also Sanskrit Sringaradipika
[145:6:6] - **Palnati Veera Charitra**: dvipada ballad of the **Palnadu war (1182)**; credited to **Srinatha**. The **APPSC 2025 key** answer to "which was NOT written by Srinatha" = **Madanavilasa Bhanam** (others: Haravilasam, Sivaratri Mahatmyam, Palnati Veera Charitra).
[145:6:7] # Palnadu in literature
[145:6:8] - Srinatha's **chatu** verses from Palnadu picture its hard life and coarse food.
[145:6:9] - Palnadu social life: houses painted with Rama and Krishna avatars; 1,500 painters' houses at Orugallu.

## SUB 8 | Later Andhra Composers and Temples
[145:8:0] # Bhakta Ramadasu (Kancherla Gopanna)
[145:8:1] - Golconda revenue official (Bhadrachalam tahsildar); used state revenue to rebuild the **Bhadrachalam Rama temple**.
[145:8:2] - Jailed **12 years in Golconda fort** by **Abul Hasan Tana Shah**.
[145:8:3] - Works: **Dasarathi Satakam** (in prison) and **Ramadasu kirtanas**; Dasarathi Satakam translated into English by **Dr. Bhanumathi Ramakrishna** (APPSC-G2 2001).
[145:8:4] - **Ramadasu's satakam:** **Dasarathi Satakam**; ⚠ APPSC key (2002) gave **Sumati Satakam**, which is **Baddena's**.

## SUB 10 | Social Impact of Bhakti
[145:10:0] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [145:10:0.0] Caste critique | Alvars, Nayanars, Basava, Vemana, Veerabrahmam; some promoted common dining
  [145:10:0.1] Vernaculars | Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam replaced Sanskrit
  [145:10:0.2] Forms | Satakam and dwipada carried bhakti to common people

=== NOTES 1:146 | Sufism - Doctrines, Khanqah, Sama; Silsilas - Chishti, Suhrawardi, Qadiri, Naqshbandi
## SUB 0 | Meaning, Origin and Firsts
[146:0:0] - **Sufism** = Islamic mysticism. "Sufi" from **suf** (coarse wool); also **safa** (purity) or Greek **sophia**.
[146:0:1] - Sufis accepted the **Quran and the Prophet** but absorbed **Vedanta, Yoga and Neo-Platonism**. Sufism gave Islam a tolerant, vernacular face; khanqahs were welfare centres.
[146:0:2] - **Ulema** = scholars of Islamic religious law; not part of Sufism and generally did not sanction music.
[146:0:3] - First prominent Sufi in India: **Ali Hujwiri (Data Ganj Bakhsh)**, author of **Kashf-ul-Mahjub** (love and knowledge of God, fana).
[146:0:4] - Most popular order: **Chishti**. Last great Chishti of Delhi: **Nasiruddin Chiragh-i-Dehlavi**.
## SUB 1 | Sufi Terms
[146:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [146:1:0.0] Khanqah | Sufi hospice where disciples lived and trained; gave food and shelter to the poor
  [146:1:0.1] Sama | Musical-spiritual assembly to produce ecstasy; raqs = ritual dance
  [146:1:0.2] Pir / Murshid / Shaikh | Master, authorised guide
  [146:1:0.3] Murid | Disciple in full submission; the bond = Piri-Muridi
  [146:1:0.4] Wali / Auliya | Friend(s) of God, saint(s); Auliya is a title, not an order
  [146:1:0.5] Dargah | Shrine at a saint's tomb
  [146:1:0.6] Urs | Saint's death anniversary = union with God (e.g. Urs of Moinuddin Chishti at Ajmer)
  [146:1:0.7] Fana / Baqa | Annihilation of the self in God / subsistence in God
  [146:1:0.8] Tariqa / Silsila | Path / order
  [146:1:0.9] Malfuzat / Maktubat / Tazkiras | Conversations / letters / biographies
[146:1:1] - **SufiKalam** (devotional music) is typical of **Kashmir**.
[146:1:2] Malfuzat = conversations; Maktubat = letters; Tazkiras = biographies (not anti-Shia tracts).
## SUB 2 | Doctrines and Stages
[146:2:0] TABLE head: Doctrine | Meaning | Linked to
  [146:2:0.0] Wahdat-ul-Wujud | Unity of Being: Haq (Creator) and Khalq (creation) are one | Ibn Arabi; Qadiris and Chishtis
  [146:2:0.1] Wahdat-ush-Shuhud | Unity of Appearance/Witness: creation only reflects God | Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi (Naqshbandi)
[146:2:1] - **10 maqamat (stages)**: first **Tauba** (repentance) → Wara, Zuhd, Faqr, Sabr, Shukr, Khauf, Raja, Tawakkul → last **Riza** (submission).
## SUB 3 | Practices and Yogic Borrowings
[146:3:0] - **Zikr** (remembrance of God), meditation, fasting, asceticism, sama, solitude or retreat (**chilla**).
[146:3:1] - **Amritakund**, a Hatha-yogic text, translated into Arabic and Persian (13th c.); after it **breath control** became key in almost every order **except the Naqshbandis**.
[146:3:2] - **Alberuni** compared Sufi ideas with **Patanjali's Yoga Sutra** and the **Gita**.
[146:3:3] - **Alberuni's book:** **Kitab-ul-Hind** (also called **Tahqiq-i-Hind**), completed about **1030 CE**, describes Indian religion, philosophy and science; translated into English by **E.C. Sachau** as *Alberuni's India*.
## SUB 4 | Ba-shara vs Be-shara
[146:4:0] Ba-shara | Follow Sharia | Chishti, Suhrawardi, Qadiri, Naqshbandi, Firdausi
Be-shara | Outside Sharia | Wandering Qalandars, Madaris
## SUB 5 | Silsilas - Founders, Saints, Regions
[146:5:0] TABLE head: Silsila | Founder / in India | Major saints | Region | Stance
  [146:5:0.0] Chishti | Abu Ishaq Shami (of Syria) with disciple Abu Ahmad Abdal at Chisht (Afghanistan); in India Moinuddin Chishti | Bakhtiyar Kaki, Baba Farid, Nizamuddin, Nasiruddin, Burhanuddin Gharib, Gesudaraz, Hamiduddin of Nagaur | Delhi-Ajmer; later Deccan | Liberal; aloof from court; sama, qawwali
  [146:5:0.1] Suhrawardi | Shihabuddin Suhrawardi (Baghdad); in India Bahauddin Zakariya | Sadruddin Arif, Ruknuddin Abul Fath, Jalaluddin Surkh, Jalaluddin Tabrizi, Qazi Hamiduddin Nagori | Sindh, Multan, Punjab | Close to rulers; royal service
  [146:5:0.2] Firdausi | Branch of the Suhrawardi order; Sharfuddin Yahya Maneri | — | Bihar (founded there) | Ba-shara
  [146:5:0.3] Qadiri | Abdul Qadir Jilani (Baghdad) | Miyan Mir, Muhibullah Shah, Dara Shikoh | Punjab, Sindh | Backed Wahdat-ul-Wujud

## SUB 4 | Caste and Social Reform
[148:4:0] - Weakened priestly dominance; **khanqahs** fed all groups. Low-caste saints: Namdev **tailor**, Kabir **weaver**, Ravidas **tanner**, Sena **barber**, Dhanna **Jat cultivator**.
[148:4:1] - **Ramananda** - first medieval reformer to **denounce caste** outright.
[148:4:2] - **Basava** - against caste, **child marriage**; backed **widow remarriage**, gender equality.
[148:4:3] - **Brahmanaidu** (Palnadu) - **Chapakudu** inter-dining; commander **Karnamadasu**, an untouchable.
[148:4:4] - **Veerabrahmam** - disciples **Siddayya (Dudekula)**, **Kakkaiah (Madiga)**; admitted women (granddaughter **Eswaramma**).
[148:4:5] - **Nasaraiah** - Sayyid reformer (to 1825), Madigas of **Vinukonda**; opposed untouchability.
[148:4:6] - **Andhra reform line:** Before colonial rule, social reform in Andhra came through **Veerashaivism** and **Srivaishnavism**, and then Brahmanaidu, Vemana, Veerabrahmam and Nasaraiah; they opposed caste, but caste inequalities could not be removed.
