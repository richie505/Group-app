## SUB 0 | Common Principles of Bhakti and Sufism
[0:0] TABLE head: Principle | Bhakti | Sufi | Common?
  [0:0.0] Monotheism | One God, many names (Kabir) | Tawhid; Wahdat-ul-Wujud | Yes
  [0:0.1] Asceticism, guide–disciple bond | Guru; renunciation of ritual | Pir–murid; austerity | Yes
  [0:0.2] Religious tolerance | Kabir, Nanak criticised both orthodoxies | Unity of humanity | Yes
  [0:0.3] Devotion over ritual | Over Vedic ritual and priests | Over formal Shariat | Yes
  [0:0.4] Idol worship | Nirguna saints rejected it | Rejected | No
[0:1] - **APPSC G1 2023:** common principles = **I, II and III only** (monotheism, asceticism, tolerance); idol worship is the trap.
[0:2] - **Sufism is closest to Bhakti (devotion)** (CDS 2003) – not karma, kalpa or gyana.
[0:3] - **Common feature of all Bhakti saints = verses in the languages of their followers** (BPSC 2005); rejecting priests, idol worship and women in temples were not shared by all.
[0:4] - Bhakti stressed devotion over Vedic ritual; Sufism valued inner experience over formal law. Kabir and Nanak criticised the orthodoxy of **both Hinduism and Islam**; the meeting of the two produced a **composite Indo-Islamic culture**.

## SUB 1 | Nathpanthis, Hath Yoga and the Sufis
[1:0] - **Nathpanth** traced to **Matsyendranath**; **Gorakhnath** (c. 11th c.) its great yogi. His followers are called **Jogis (Yogis)**, **Gorakhnathi**, **Kanphata** (split-eared) or Nath-panthis.
[1:1] - **UPPSC 2019:** **Hath Yoga was practised by the Nathpanthis** ✔ and **its technique was adopted by the Sufis** ✔ → both.
[1:2] - 13th-century Sufis met the Kanphata yogis; **Amritakund** (Hath-yoga text) into Arabic and Persian; breath control in all orders except the **Naqshbandi**.
[1:3] - **Nizamuddin Auliya** was struck by the yogic division of the body into **Siva** (head to navel) and **Sakti** (below); **Hamiduddin Nagori** equated **Alakh Niranjan** with **Khuda** in Hindi verse. The interaction was not confined to temple ritual.
[1:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 5 p.8 (src/indian-history/nextgen-indian-history-appsc-05.txt); Singhania App.2 / Pala section on Gorakhnath (src/indian-history/singhania-art-culture-05.txt: "followers are called Yogis, Gorakhnathi, Darshani or Kanphatas")
## SUB 2 | Music – Sama, Qawwali and the Classical Streams
[2:0] TABLE head: Tradition | Fact
  [2:0.0] Sama / Qawwali | Chishti (and Suhrawardi) assemblies; Amir Khusrau introduced the Qaul; Naqshbandis opposed sama
  [2:0.1] Sanskrit → Persian | Many Sanskrit music works were translated into Persian through rulers' interest: Ghunyat-ul-Munya (1375), Lahjat-i-Sikandar Shahi (Sikandar Lodi)
  [2:0.2] Hindustani | Shaped largely by Arabo-Persian music brought by Muslim rulers and Sufis
  [2:0.3] Carnatic | Stayed tied to temple Bhakti; Telugu composers
[2:1] - **UPPCS 2018 A-R:** A (Sanskrit music works translated into Persian) ✔; R (early Chishtis fond of sama) ✔; **R does not explain A** – the translations came from royal interest.
[2:2] - **UPSC 1999 match:** **V.D. Paluskar – music for "Vande Mataram"**; **Venkatamakhin – raga classification (Melakarta)**; **Shyama Shastri – Carnatic music**; **Amir Khusrau – Khayal** → I-D, II-A, III-B, IV-C.

## SUB 4 | Caste, Women, Sects and Syncretism
[4:0] - Low-caste saints: Namdev **tailor**, Kabir **weaver**, Ravidas **tanner**, Sena **barber**, Dhanna **Jat**. **Ramananda** – first medieval reformer to denounce caste outright (disciples Kabir, Raidas, Sena, Dhanna).
[4:1] - **Basava** – against caste; widow remarriage, gender equality. Bhakti and Sufism **weakened priestly dominance**.
[4:2] - **Andhra line:** **Veerashaivism and Srivaishnavism** before colonial rule; **Brahmanaidu** (Palnadu) – **Chapakudu** inter-dining; **Veerabrahmam** – disciples **Siddayya (Dudekula)**, **Kakkaiah (Madiga)**; **Nasaraiah** – Sayyid reformer among the Madigas of **Vinukonda**.
[4:3] - **Women:** Mirabai, Andal, **Lal Ded (Lalla)**, Janabai, Bahinabai, **Molla** (Molla Ramayanam); Dhanna was a man.
[4:4] TABLE head: Sect | Founder / region
  [4:4.0] Satnami (Chhattisgarh) | Guru Ghasidas (c. 1820s); an earlier Satnami sect began at Narnaul (1657)
  [4:4.1] Dadupanth | Dadu, Rajasthan; Sant tradition with Kabir, Nanak, Ravidas
  [4:4.2] Mahanubhava | Chakradhar Swami, Maharashtra
  [4:4.3] Rishi movement | Sheikh Nuruddin (Nund Rishi), Kashmir; inspired by Lal Ded; universal love, service to all castes, vegetarianism
  [4:4.4] Lingayat | Basava (Shaiva)
[4:5] - **Akbar**: Sulh-i-kul, **Din-i-Ilahi (1582)**; **Dara Shikoh** (Qadiri) carried the syncretic line; Sirhindi opposed it.
[4:6] - Maratha awakening: **Tukaram, Ramdas** → **Shivaji**.

## SUB 5 | Limitations of the Movements
[5:0] - **Caste persisted**; **purdah** continued; the traditions **hardened into separate sects and panths**; Srivaishnavism and Veerashaivism opposed caste but could not eradicate it.
[5:1] - The movements failed in their aims of reforming Hinduism and uniting Hindus and Muslims, though they changed society deeply.
[5:2] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 5 p.3; old app notes 1:148 sub 7
