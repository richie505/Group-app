=== Common Principles of Bhakti and Sufism
| Principle | Bhakti | Sufi | Common? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monotheism** | One God, many names (Kabir) | Tawhid; Wahdat-ul-Wujud | Yes |
| **Asceticism**, guide–disciple bond | Guru; renunciation of ritual | Pir–murid; austerity | Yes |
| **Religious tolerance** | Kabir, Nanak criticised both orthodoxies | Unity of humanity | Yes |
| Devotion over ritual | Over Vedic ritual and priests | Over formal Shariat | Yes |
| **Idol worship** | Nirguna saints rejected it | Rejected | **No** |
- **APPSC G1 2023:** common principles = **I, II and III only** (monotheism, asceticism, tolerance); idol worship is the trap.
- **Sufism is closest to Bhakti (devotion)** (CDS 2003) – not karma, kalpa or gyana.
- **Common feature of all Bhakti saints = verses in the languages of their followers** (BPSC 2005); rejecting priests, idol worship and women in temples were not shared by all.
- Bhakti stressed devotion over Vedic ritual; Sufism valued inner experience over formal law. Kabir and Nanak criticised the orthodoxy of **both Hinduism and Islam**; the meeting of the two produced a **composite Indo-Islamic culture**.

=== Nathpanthis, Hath Yoga and the Sufis
- **Nathpanth** traced to **Matsyendranath**; **Gorakhnath** (c. 11th c.) its great yogi. His followers are called **Jogis (Yogis)**, **Gorakhnathi**, **Kanphata** (split-eared) or Nath-panthis.
- **UPPSC 2019:** **Hath Yoga was practised by the Nathpanthis** ✔ and **its technique was adopted by the Sufis** ✔ → both.
- 13th-century Sufis met the Kanphata yogis; **Amritakund** (Hath-yoga text) into Arabic and Persian; breath control in all orders except the **Naqshbandi**.
- **Nizamuddin Auliya** was struck by the yogic division of the body into **Siva** (head to navel) and **Sakti** (below); **Hamiduddin Nagori** equated **Alakh Niranjan** with **Khuda** in Hindi verse. The interaction was not confined to temple ritual.

=== Music – Sama, Qawwali and the Classical Streams
| Tradition | Fact |
|---|---|
| Sama / Qawwali | Chishti (and Suhrawardi) assemblies; **Amir Khusrau** introduced the **Qaul**; Naqshbandis opposed sama |
| Sanskrit → Persian | **Many Sanskrit music works were translated into Persian** through rulers' interest: Ghunyat-ul-Munya (**1375**), **Lahjat-i-Sikandar Shahi** (Sikandar Lodi) |
| Hindustani | Shaped largely by **Arabo-Persian** music brought by Muslim rulers and Sufis |
| Carnatic | Stayed tied to temple Bhakti; Telugu composers |
- **UPPCS 2018 A-R:** A (Sanskrit music works translated into Persian) ✔; R (early Chishtis fond of sama) ✔; **R does not explain A** – the translations came from royal interest.
- **UPSC 1999 match:** **V.D. Paluskar – music for "Vande Mataram"**; **Venkatamakhin – raga classification (Melakarta)**; **Shyama Shastri – Carnatic music**; **Amir Khusrau – Khayal** → I-D, II-A, III-B, IV-C.

=== Caste, Women, Sects and Syncretism
- Low-caste saints: Namdev **tailor**, Kabir **weaver**, Ravidas **tanner**, Sena **barber**, Dhanna **Jat**. **Ramananda** – first medieval reformer to denounce caste outright (disciples Kabir, Raidas, Sena, Dhanna).
- **Basava** – against caste; widow remarriage, gender equality. Bhakti and Sufism **weakened priestly dominance**.
- **Andhra line:** **Veerashaivism and Srivaishnavism** before colonial rule; **Brahmanaidu** (Palnadu) – **Chapakudu** inter-dining; **Veerabrahmam** – disciples **Siddayya (Dudekula)**, **Kakkaiah (Madiga)**; **Nasaraiah** – Sayyid reformer among the Madigas of **Vinukonda**.
- **Women:** Mirabai, Andal, **Lal Ded (Lalla)**, Janabai, Bahinabai, **Molla** (Molla Ramayanam); Dhanna was a man.
| Sect | Founder / region |
|---|---|
| **Satnami** (Chhattisgarh) | **Guru Ghasidas** (c. 1820s); an earlier Satnami sect began at Narnaul (1657) |
| Dadupanth | Dadu, **Rajasthan**; Sant tradition with Kabir, Nanak, Ravidas |
| Mahanubhava | **Chakradhar Swami**, Maharashtra |
| **Rishi movement** | **Sheikh Nuruddin (Nund Rishi)**, **Kashmir**; inspired by Lal Ded; universal love, service to all castes, **vegetarianism** |
| Lingayat | Basava (Shaiva) |
- **Akbar**: Sulh-i-kul, **Din-i-Ilahi (1582)**; **Dara Shikoh** (Qadiri) carried the syncretic line; Sirhindi opposed it.
- Maratha awakening: **Tukaram, Ramdas** → **Shivaji**.

=== Limitations of the Movements
- **Caste persisted**; **purdah** continued; the traditions **hardened into separate sects and panths**; Srivaishnavism and Veerashaivism opposed caste but could not eradicate it.
- The movements failed in their aims of reforming Hinduism and uniting Hindus and Muslims, though they changed society deeply.
