## SUB 0 | Capitals of Magadha – Rajgriha, Pataliputra, Vaishali
[0:0] # Capitals in order
[0:1] TABLE head: Capital | Ruler | Fact
  [0:1.0] Girivraja / Rajgriha (modern Rajgir) | Brihadratha line (Jarasandha); Bimbisara, Ajatashatru | First capital; ringed by five hills, walled – impregnable
  [0:1.1] Pataliputra (fort) | Ajatashatru | Fortified the village Pataligrama and built the city as a base against the Vajjis
  [0:1.2] Pataliputra (capital) | Udayin | Shifted the capital from Rajgriha to Pataliputra
  [0:1.3] Vaishali | Shishunaga | Capital moved there temporarily
  [0:1.4] Pataliputra again | Kalashoka | Capital moved back; it stayed the capital of the Mauryas and Guptas
[0:2] - **First / earliest / initial capital:** **Girivraja (Rajgriha, Rajgir)** – asked 6 times (State PSC ×3, BPSC 2004, NDA 2001). Not Pataliputra, Vaishali, Champa (capital of **Anga**) or Varanasi.
[0:3] - **⚠ Option missing:** a State PSC paper asked the first capital with only Pataliputra, Vaishali, Champa and Anga – none is right; the answer is **Rajgriha**.
[0:4] - **Vaishali:** capital of the **Lichchhavi (Vajji) republic**, named after King **Vishal**; near **Mahavira's birthplace**; venue of the **Second** (not First) Buddhist Council.
[0:5] - **Not a Magadhan capital:** **Kaushambi** – capital of **Vatsa** (CGPSC 2011).
[0:6] # Who founded Pataliputra
[0:7] - **Ajatashatru** fortified Pataligrama and **built the city of Pataliputra** – the answer when Udayin is not offered (APPSC G1 2002 and G1 PYQ: Ajatashatru; Dhanyakataka, Nalanda, Lumbini are distractors).
[0:8] - **Udayin** made it the **capital**; Jain texts call him the **founder of Pataliputra** – the answer when Ajatashatru is not offered ("who founded Pataliputra": Udayin, not Ashoka, Bimbisara, Mahapadma).
[0:9] - **⚠ Disputed:** with both offered, most books and keys give **Ajatashatru** for the city and **Udayin** for the capital.
[0:10] - **"First chose Pataliputra as capital":** **Udayin** (not Ajatashatru, Kalashoka or Kanishka).
[0:11] # Pataliputra – site and remains
[0:12] - **Site:** modern **Patna**, at the **confluence of the Ganga and the Son**; surrounded by rivers – a **water-fort (jaladurga)**.
[0:13] - **Excavated remains:** **Kumrahar** (Mauryan pillared hall) and **Bulandibagh** (wooden palisade) – both = Pataliputra, not Vaishali, Kapilavastu, Shravasti or Varanasi.
[0:14] - **Decline:** Hiuen-Tsang (7th c. CE) found it in ruins.
[0:15] # Rajgir today
[0:16] - **Vishwa Shanti Stupa:** the World Peace Pagoda on **Ratnagiri hill, Rajgir** – billed as the highest in the world (BPSC 2008).
[0:17] - **Rajgriha** was also the place of **Mahavira's first sermon** and of the **First Buddhist Council** (Saptaparni cave).
[0:18] > Exam angle: "first capital" → **Rajgriha**; "built Pataliputra / Pataligrama fort" → **Ajatashatru**; "made it the capital" → **Udayin**; Kumrahar, Bulandibagh → **Pataliputra**.
[0:19] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.100-101, p.103; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.40-41; app notes 1:25 (sub 0, 6); nalanda.nic.in (Vishwa Shanti Stupa)
## SUB 1 | Haryankas – Bimbisara, Ajatashatru, Udayin
[1:0] # Bimbisara (c. 545–493 BCE)
[1:1] - **Founder of the Haryanka dynasty** (NextGen Rocket; APPSC; SSC CGL 2019) – PMF adds that many scholars give the founding to his grandfather, but exams key **Bimbisara**. Brihadratha belongs to the earlier legendary line.
[1:2] - **Real founder of the Magadhan empire**; also called **Seniya / Shrenika** (WBCS 2017).
[1:3] - **Contemporary of the Buddha** and of Mahavira (APPSC GS 2018; FRO 2020; Sachivalayam).
[1:4]   - **⚠ Disputed:** Ajatashatru was also a contemporary – the Buddha died in his reign – but keys give **Bimbisara** when both are offered.
[1:5] - **Conquest:** defeated Brahmadatta of **Anga** and put **Kunika (Ajatashatru)** as viceroy at **Champa**.
[1:6] - **Diplomacy:** friendship with **Pradyota of Avanti**; when Pradyota had jaundice, Bimbisara sent his royal physician **Jivaka** to Ujjain.
[1:7] - **Jivaka:** physician of Bimbisara's court (UP UDA 2006; State PSC); son of the Rajgriha courtesan **Salavati** (MPPSC 2009) – not Amrapali (Vaishali).
[1:8] TABLE head: Queen | Clan / kingdom | Note
  [1:8.0] Kosaladevi (Mahakosala) | Kosala, sister of Prasenajit | Brought Kashi as dowry
  [1:8.1] Chellana | Lichchhavi of Vaishali | Mother of Ajatashatru (not Udayin)
  [1:8.2] Khema | Madra (Punjab) | –
[1:9] - **Death:** imprisoned and starved to death by his son **Ajatashatru**.
[1:10] # Ajatashatru (c. 493–462 BCE)
[1:11] - **Parricide:** killed his father Bimbisara (UPPCS 2011) – Jain name **Kunika**, "Pitruhanta"; he was in turn **killed by his son Udayin** (UPPCS 2007; UPPSC 2007).
[1:12] - **Patricide chain (Buddhist texts):** **Bimbisara → Ajatashatru → Udayin**.
[1:13] - **Kosala:** war with **Prasenajit** over Kashi; Prasenajit gave his daughter **Vajira** with Kashi as dowry; later annexed Kosala.
[1:14] - **Vajji:** annexed after a **16-year** war; minister **Vassakara** split the Lichchhavi chiefs.
[1:15] - **War engines:** **mahashilakantaka** (stone-throwing catapult) and **rathamusala** (chariot fitted with a mace).
[1:16] - **Buddhism:** met the Buddha (Bharhut relief); hosted the **First Buddhist Council** at Rajgriha.
[1:17] # Udayin (c. 462–444 BCE)
[1:18] - **Shifted the capital to Pataliputra**; a devout Jain in Jain tradition. Not to be confused with **Udayana of Vatsa**.
[1:19] - Probably killed at the urging of **Palaka of Avanti**; his weak successors ended with **Nagadasaka**.
[1:20] # Contemporaries
[1:21] - **Milinda (Menander)**, the 2nd-century-BCE Indo-Greek, was **not** a contemporary of Bimbisara, the Buddha and **Prasenajit (Kosala)** (IAS 2005).
[1:22] > Exam angle: "founder of Haryanka" → **Bimbisara**; "killed his father and was killed by his son" → **Ajatashatru**; "Jivaka" → **Bimbisara's court**.
[1:23] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.99-101; NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.29-30; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.40; britannica.com/biography/Bimbisara; app notes 1:25 (sub 3, 8)
## SUB 2 | Shishunagas and Nandas
[2:0] # Shishunaga dynasty (c. 413–345 BCE)
[2:1] - **Shishunaga:** a viceroy at Banaras / amatya **chosen by the people** after Udayin's weak successors; moved the capital to **Vaishali**.
[2:2] - **Malwa:** Shishunaga **destroyed Avanti** (capital **Ujjayini**) and annexed Avanti and Vatsa – the expansion over Malwa (UP Lower 2008 ×2).
[2:3] - **Kalashoka:** moved the capital **back to Pataliputra**; **Second Buddhist Council** at Vaishali (383 BCE).
[2:4] - **End:** the last Shishunaga, **Mahanandin**, was killed by **Mahapadma Nanda**.
[2:5] # Conquests in order
[2:6] TABLE head: Ruler | Annexed
  [2:6.0] Bimbisara | Anga
  [2:6.1] Ajatashatru | Kosala, Vajji
  [2:6.2] Shishunaga | Avanti, Vatsa
  [2:6.3] Mahapadma Nanda | Kalinga, Kshatriya kingdoms of the north
[2:7] # Nanda dynasty (c. 345–321 BCE)
[2:8] - **Sequence:** Haryanka → **Shishunaga → Nanda → Maurya** (State PSC; BPSC 2005).
[2:9] - **Mahapadma Nanda:** founder; **first non-Kshatriya dynasty**; **first empire-builder** of India; titles **Ekarat** (sole sovereign) and **Sarvakshatrantaka** (destroyer of Kshatriyas) → hence **Aparoparashurama**, "a second Parashurama" (UPUDA 2010).
[2:10] - **Hathigumpha inscription** of Kharavela: a **Nanda king dug a canal in Kalinga** and carried off a Jina image (UPPCS 1999).
[2:11] - **Dhana Nanda:** **last Nanda** (RRB 2001); Greek **Agrammes / Xandrames**; ruled at **Alexander's invasion** (326 BCE) – the dynasty ruling North India then was the **Nanda** (UPSC 2000).
[2:12] - **Fall:** Chandragupta Maurya, with Chanakya, overthrew Dhana Nanda in **c. 321 BCE**.
[2:13] - **Army (Greek accounts):** about 200,000 infantry, 60,000 cavalry, 3,000–6,000 elephants.
[2:14] > Exam angle: "after the Shishunagas" → **Nandas**; "after the Nandas" → **Mauryas**; "Malwa added" → **Shishunaga**; "Aparoparashurama" → **Mahapadma Nanda**.
[2:15] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.101-102, p.105-106; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.40-41; app notes 1:25 (sub 4, 5)
## SUB 3 | Causes of Magadhan Supremacy
[3:0] - **Most powerful and richest Mahajanapada** (6th–4th c. BCE); it became the base of the **Mauryan Empire**. Puranas: the first dynasty was the **Brihadratha** (Jarasandha ruled from **Girivraj**).
[3:1] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [3:1.0] Iron mines nearby (Jharkhand) | Weapons and tools; forest clearing
  [3:1.1] Alluvial soil, ample rainfall | Agricultural surplus, taxes
  [3:1.2] Ganga and Son (navigable) | Cheap transport and trade
  [3:1.3] Elephants from the eastern forests | First large-scale use of war elephants
  [3:1.4] Naturally fortified capitals | Rajgriha (five hills), Pataliputra (water-fort)
  [3:1.5] Unorthodox society | Little Vedic hold; ambitious rulers and ministers
[3:2] - **⚠ Not a cause:** control of western sea ports, gold mines, superior cavalry or a republican system.
[3:3] - **Buddhist councils linked to Magadhan kings:** First – **Ajatashatru** (Rajgriha); Second – **Kalashoka** (Vaishali); Third – **Ashoka** (Pataliputra).
[3:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.103-104; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.67; app notes 1:25 (sub 0, 2, 7)
