## SUB 0 | Lorenz Curve and Gini Coefficient
[0:0] TABLE head: Person | Measure | Year
  [0:0.0] Max O. Lorenz (American) | Lorenz curve – graphical distribution of income / wealth | 1905 ("Methods of measuring the concentration of wealth")
  [0:0.1] Corrado Gini (Italian statistician) | Gini coefficient ("Variability and Mutability") | 1912
  [0:0.2] Simon Kuznets | Kuznets curve (inverted U) | 1955
[0:1] - **Lorenz curve:** X-axis = cumulative % of population (poorest first); Y-axis = cumulative % of income; **45° line = perfect equality**; the farther the curve bows away, the **greater the inequality**. It is a graph, not a number – it **measures inequality of income / distribution of income**.
[0:2] - **Gini coefficient:** **derived from the Lorenz curve** = area between the Lorenz curve and the equality line ÷ whole area under the equality line; measures **income inequality**.
[0:3] - Range **0 (perfect equality) to 1 (one person has all income)**; Gini index = coefficient × 100 (0.40 → 40); **higher Gini = more inequality** (so "higher Gini, lower inequality" is wrong).
[0:4] - **Relative poverty** within India is measured with the **Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient**; absolute poverty with the **poverty line**.
[0:5] - India: consumption Gini (World Bank, Spring 2025) **28.8 (2011-12) → 25.5 (2022-23)**; income Gini (World Inequality Database) 52 (2004) → 62 (2023).
[0:6] TABLE head: Curve | Shows
  [0:6.0] Lorenz | Distribution of income (inequality)
  [0:6.1] Phillips | Inflation vs unemployment
  [0:6.2] Laffer | Tax rate vs tax revenue
  [0:6.3] Marshall (demand curve) | Price vs quantity demanded
  [0:6.4] Kuznets | Inequality vs per capita income

## SUB 2 | Measuring Inequality in India – Consumption, Income and Wealth
[2:0] - **NSSO/NSO indicator of household economic inequality = consumption** (**MPCE**, monthly per capita consumption expenditure) – not income, wealth or social status; poverty too is measured from household consumption expenditure.
[2:1] TABLE head: Inequality | Data source | Finding
  [2:1.0] Consumption | NSS/HCES (MoSPI), every five years | Looks lower; may understate top-end incomes
  [2:1.1] Income | No official survey: PLFS wages, tax data, WID | Top 10% median about 13 times bottom 10% (2023-24)
  [2:1.2] Wealth | AIDIS (All India Debt and Investment Survey) | Top 10% hold about 77%
[2:2] - **Oxfam, "An economy for the 99%" (Davos, Jan 2017):** India's **richest 1% hold 58%** of total wealth; 57 billionaires = wealth of the bottom 70%; the top 10% share (about 77-80%) is the figure asked.
[2:3] - **McKinsey Global Institute, "Poorer than their parents?" (2016):** **65-70% of households** in 25 advanced economies had flat or falling real incomes, 2005-2014.
[2:4] - **Sen's poverty index** combines the headcount ratio, the income-gap ratio and the **Gini coefficient of income among the poor** – i.e. it brings in **income inequality**.
[2:5] - **Inequality** (of income and assets) is the underlying cause of poverty within a society.
