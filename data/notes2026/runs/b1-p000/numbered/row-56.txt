## SUB 0 | Undavalli and Mogalrajapuram – Which Dynasty
[0:0] - **Undavalli, Mogalrajapuram and Vijayawada (Indrakeeladri) cave temples** = **Vishnukundins** (G2 2019; G2 Mains 2025; Welfare Officers 2017 ×2; APPSC PYQ ×3) – not Ikshvakus, Salankayanas, Pallavas, Rashtrakutas, Cholas, Chalukyas, Kakatiyas or Reddi kings.
[0:1] - **Brahmanical (Hindu) caves** of Mogalrajapuram and Undavalli are assigned to the Vishnukundins; **Madhava Varma I** is credited with Undavalli, Bhairavakonda and Mogalrajapuram.
[0:2] - **Vishnukundins** = **first Hindu cave temples in the Telugu states**; **Guptas** = earliest Hindu cave temples in India (**Udayagiri**, Madhya Pradesh).

  [0:3.1] Mogalrajapuram | Vishnukundins | Trimurti, Durga, Nataraja; mainly Shaiva | Vijayawada (NTR district)
  [0:3.2] Bhairavakonda | Vishnukundin (MV I) or Pallava | Shiva, Kapalika sect | Nallamala hills, Nellore–Prakasam border
  [0:3.3] Akkanna-Madanna | Vishnukundins | Shaiva | Indrakeeladri, Vijayawada
  [0:3.4] Bojjannakonda | – | Buddhist | Visakhapatnam

## SUB 1 | Undavalli Caves
[1:0] - **District:** **Guntur** (Endowment EO 2022) – not Krishna, NTR or Prakasam; the caves face **Vijayawada** across the Krishna (Boilers 2019: "near Vijayawada").
[1:1] - **Four storeys**, **three main caves**; carved **top-down** – upper levels finely finished, ground floor **crude and unfinished**.
[1:2] - Centrepiece: **monolithic reclining Vishnu** (**Ananta Padmanabha Swamy**), about 5 m; a Buddha in padmasana was later turned into Vishnu; **Purnakumbha** motif.
[1:3] - **Changing use:** **Jain abode → Buddhist monks → Hindu temple**.
[1:4] - **⚠ Trap:** on the **Krishna**, not the Godavari; "built by the Cholas as a Hindu temple, later Buddhist" is false.
[1:5] - **In news:** AP's two-day **Telugu Mahotsavam** at Undavalli Caves, **25–26 July 2026**.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Vishnukundis p.15; app notes 1:56 (sub 1)

## SUB 3 | Bhairavakonda Caves
[3:0] - **Eight rock-cut caves**, all dedicated to **Shiva** (G2 2012) – not 4, 9 or 12; a centre of **Shaivism** (Kapalika sect).
[3:1] - **Location:** listed under **Nellore** district in NextGen/Rocket and the G2 2012 stem; on the ground in the **Nallamala hills** on the Nellore–Prakasam border.
[3:2] - **Attribution debate:** Madhava Varma I (Vishnukundin) vs **Pallava period (7th–8th c.)**; closely resembles the early Pallava caves of **Mahabalipuram**.
[3:3] - Pillars: **lion-based** with **kumbha (pot) capitals**.
[3:4] > Number angle: 4 storeys = Undavalli | 5 caves = Mogalrajapuram | 8 caves = Bhairavakonda.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Vishnukundis p.9, p.15; Rocket AP History sheet #4, "Art" (Bhairavakonda – eight caves); NextGen Previous Papers p.28; app notes 1:56 (sub 3)
## SUB 4 | Features and the Pallava Comparison
[4:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:0.0] Pillared mandapa | Hall in front of rock-cut garbhagriha cells – the usual Vishnukundin plan
  [4:0.1] Kumbha capitals | Pot capitals on lion-based pillars (Bhairavakonda)
  [4:0.2] Kapota | Decorated eave above capitals (Mogalrajapuram)
  [4:0.3] Dwarapalas | Door guardians
[4:1] - Style: mix of **Ikshvaku and Vakataka** traditions with **Pallava** borrowings; dated **4th–6th c. CE**.
[4:2] - **Brick structural temples** with garbhagriha, antarala-mandapa, mukhamandapa at **Keesaragutta**.
[4:3] - **Lost-wax (cire perdue):** early multi-armed metal images credited to the **Vishnukundin** age.
[4:4] - **Pallava caves** of **Mahendravarman I** (7th c. CE) – lion-based pillars – later taken up by the **Telugu Chodas and Eastern Chalukyas**.
[4:5] - **⚠ Trap:** the storeyed cave is **Undavalli**, not Bhairavakonda.
[4:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Vishnukundis p.8, p.15; app notes 1:56 (sub 4, 5)
