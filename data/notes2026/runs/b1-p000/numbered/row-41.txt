## SUB 1 | Pushyamitra Sunga – Coup, Ashvamedhas and Buddhism
[1:0] - **Mauryan commander-in-chief (senani)**, a **Brahmin**; killed Brihadratha while showing him the army (Harshacharita) – the last Mauryan king was killed by **Pushyamitra Shunga** (APPSC 2023), not Dattamitra, Vibhuti or Indrabhattarika.
[1:1] - **Ancestors of the Sungas came from Ujjain** (MPPSC 2018); heartland **Magadha, Malwa–Vidisha, Narmada valley**.
[1:2] - **Two Ashvamedhas** – recorded in the **Ayodhya inscription of Dhanadeva** (UPPCS 2018) – not Sarnath, Besnagar or Hathigumpha; not three.
[1:3] - **Wars:** beat the Indo-Greeks (Demetrius), fought the Deccan powers and **Kharavela** of Kalinga.
[1:4] - **Buddhism:** Pushyamitra **did not contribute to the expansion of Buddhism** (CGPSC 2018) – unlike Ashoka, Kanishka and Harsha; the **Divyavadana** and **Taranatha** call him a persecutor, yet Bharhut and Sanchi were renovated under him (Romila Thapar, Lamotte: exaggerated). Hostility was probably aimed at monasteries allied with the **Indo-Greeks**.
[1:5] - **Bodhi Tree:** tradition links its destruction to Pushyamitra and later **Shashanka of Gauda**.
[1:6] - **Society:** feudatories rose – **Bhojas, Maharathis, Rastrikas**; classical **Sanskrit** revived; **Manusmriti** took shape, classing foreigners as **Vratya Kshatriyas**; Brahmanical revival with the rise of Bhagavatism and stone Buddhist art.
[1:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.4-5; Rocket Indian History 01 p.7; app notes 1:41 (sub 1, 5)
## SUB 2 | Literary Sources – Malavikagnimitram, Mahabhashya, Gargi Samhita
[2:0] TABLE head: Source | Author | What it gives
  [2:0.0] Gargi Samhita (Yuga Purana) | Garga | Yavana march on Saketa, Panchala, Mathura, Pataliputra
  [2:0.1] Mahabhashya | Patanjali | Yavana siege of Saketa and Madhyamika; sacrifices for Pushyamitra
  [2:0.2] Malavikagnimitram | Kalidasa | Love story of Agnimitra (son of Pushyamitra) and Malavika; Vasumitra beats the Yavanas on the Sindhu; war with Vidarbha
  [2:0.3] Divyavadana | Buddhist text | Pushyamitra as persecutor
  [2:0.4] Harshacharita | Banabhatta | Brihadratha killed at the army review
[2:1] - **Yavana invasion under Pushyamitra** is in Gargi Samhita, Mahabhashya and Malavikagnimitram – **NOT Visakhadatta's Mudrarakshasa**, which tells **Chandragupta Maurya's** rise (APPSC G1 2023).
[2:2] - **Malavikagnimitram:** the book with the love story of the **son of the Sunga founder** (UPSC 2016) – protagonist **Agnimitra** (UPPCS 1998), not Pushyamitra; Kalidasa's **first play**, **5 acts**; not Swapnavasavadatta, Meghaduta or Ratnavali. Mrichchhakatikam is by **Sudraka**.
[2:3] - **Patanjali** (Mahabhashya) was the **contemporary** and, it is believed, **royal priest** of **Pushyamitra Sunga** (UPPCS 2011) – not of Chandragupta Maurya, Ashoka or Chandragupta I; he explained **Panini's Ashtadhyayi** through **Katyayana's Varttikas**. Panini is earlier (not Pushyamitra's).

## SUB 3 | Besnagar (Heliodorus) Pillar
[3:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [3:0.0] Donor | Heliodorus, a Yavana from Taxila, calling himself a Bhagavata
  [3:0.1] Envoy of | Indo-Greek king Antialcidas
  [3:0.2] At the court of | Sunga king Bhagabhadra
  [3:0.3] Site | Besnagar (Vidisha), MP
  [3:0.4] Nature | Garuda-dhvaja of Vasudeva only (IAS 1998) – not Sankarshana or Pradyumna
  [3:0.5] Language/script | Prakrit / Brahmi
[3:1] - **Earliest inscriptional evidence of the Bhagavata religion** (UPPSC Mains 2008) – earlier than the Allahabad, Bhitari or Mehrauli inscriptions; shows a foreigner absorbed into an Indian religion.
[3:2] - **Not Gupta:** the Besnagar pillar (2nd c. BCE) is the item NOT related to Gupta art, unlike the Sarnath Buddha images, Deogarh temple and the Nalanda copper Buddha.
[3:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.5; PMF Art & Culture 04 p.29; app notes 1:41 (sub 6)
## SUB 4 | Sunga Art – Bharhut and Sanchi
[4:0] TABLE head: Site | Feature
  [4:0.0] Bharhut (Satna, MP) | Fine example of Sunga architecture (RPSC 2023); railings and gateways of early narrative art; inscriptions name guilds (ivory workers, stone carvers) as donors
  [4:0.1] Sanchi | Ashoka's brick stupa enlarged and cased in stone; railings (vedika) and toranas; gateway shows Ashoka's pilgrimage to the Bodhi tree with his two queens (MPPSC 2022)
  [4:0.2] Bodh Gaya | Early stone railing
  [4:0.3] Mathura | The Mathura school of sculpture began under the Sungas
[4:1] - **Transition** from wood/ivory to stone; Sanchi's south torana carries an inscription of **Ananda** linking it to **Satakarni II**, so the gateways are often dated Satavahana.
[4:2] - **Gupta temple architecture did not begin under the Sungas.**
