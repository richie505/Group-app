## SUB 0 | Gathasaptasati and Hala
[0:0] - **Compiler:** the Satavahana king **Hala** (17th king, ruled 5 years), himself a poet – the most repeated fact of the topic (G2-A 1984, G2-B 1989, G2-A 1991, G2 2008 ×2; Welfare Officers 2017 ×2; APPSC PYQ ×5).
[0:1] - **Distractors:** Gunadhya, Sarvavarma, Kuntala Satakarni, Shudraka, Pulomavi, Lilavati, Rudramadevi, Yajnasri.
[0:2] - **Work:** **Gathasaptasati** (Sattasai) – **700** Prakrit gathas, in **Maharashtri Prakrit**.
[0:3] - **Themes:** **love and romance**, rural life and nature (G2 PYQ statement ✓); first work to cover all the **Navarasas**.
[0:4] - **Women poets:** **Anulakshmi, Anupalabdha (Anupamalabdha), Reva (Revati), Madhavi** – the text "containing writing by women" (G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ); not Brihatkatha, Kamasutra or Lilavati.
[0:5] - **Religion in it:** opens with a prayer to **Shiva**, ends with a Gauri stotra; names Gauri, Indra, Ganesha; Radha-Krishna stories.
[0:6] - **Social picture:** sugar and jaggery, hanging of criminals, sati, no child marriage, prostitution, group dances, plight of wives of seafaring merchants.
[0:7] - **Desi (Telugu) words:** atta, addam, potta, pilla, padi, pandi, tuppu – earliest traces of Telugu.
[0:8] - **⚠ Trap:** a source for **society**, not for early **political** history (G2 2017).
[0:9] # Hala
[0:10] - **Title:** **Kavivatsala**; honoured poets **Kumarila** and **Sripalita**; married the Sri Lankan princess **Lilavati**.
[0:11] - **Golden age of Prakrit:** Hala's reign. **⚠ Trap:** NextGen p.23 prints "golden age of Sanskrit" – its own p.9 says Prakrit; Sanskrit was Kuntala Satakarni's cause.
[0:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.1-2, p.9, p.23; Rocket AP History p.9, p.52; app notes 1:47 (sub 10)
## SUB 1 | Guilds (Srenis) and Guild Banking
[1:0] - **Shreni:** guild of traders or artisans of one craft; fixed work rules, **quality and prices**, controlled its members' conduct; head = **Shreshthi (Sethi / Setti)**.
[1:1] - **⚠ Trap:** guilds existed in **north and south** India – "prevalent only in northern India" is the untrue statement (State PSC).
[1:2] - **Number:** **18** guilds in Satavahana records; they later hardened into castes.
[1:3] TABLE head: Guild | Members
  [1:3.0] Kolika | Weavers (strongest guild; Padmashalis) (G2 2019 ×2)
  [1:3.1] Padhika (Vadhaka) | Carpenters (G2 2012; APPSC PYQ)
  [1:3.2] Kularika | Potters (JA Panchayat Raj 2012)
  [1:3.3] Gandhika | Perfumers – noted donors to Buddhist causes (JA NTR UHS)
  [1:3.4] Tilapishaka | Oil pressers
  [1:3.5] Udayantrika | Makers / operators of water machines
  [1:3.6] Halika | Farmers
  [1:3.7] Malakara | Florists
  [1:3.8] Kasakara / Kamara / Suvarnakara | Brass workers / blacksmiths / goldsmiths
  [1:3.9] Chammakara | Leather workers
[1:4] - **⚠ Trap:** butchers = Katika, oil pressers = Ganugavaru, weavers = Salevaru – distractors for Padhika.
[1:5] # Guilds as banks
[1:6] - **Interest:** guilds took deposits and lent at **12% a year** (1% a month) (G2 2008 ×2; G2 2019 ×2).
[1:7] - **Rishabhadatta (Ushavadatta):** Nahapana's son-in-law; his **Nasik inscription** records **2,000 karshapanas** with the **Govardhana weavers' guild** at 12% for monks' robes (G2 2017; G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ).
[1:8]   - A second deposit: 1,000 karshapanas at 3/4% a month. NextGen says "borrowed" – the inscription records a permanent deposit.
[1:9] - **⚠ Trap:** Sopara, Kanheri and Pratishthana inscriptions are distractors.
[1:10] - **Not Satavahana guilds:** **Chitrameli** = guild of **agriculturists** in medieval Deccan and South India (G2 2019); **Nigamalu** = Ikshvaku trade guilds.
[1:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.17-18; Rocket AP History p.41, p.48; app notes 1:47 (sub 4)

## SUB 3 | Administration – Units and Officers
[3:0] # Units
[3:1] - **Largest administrative division:** the **Ahara** (APPSC PYQ; APSLPRB 2023) – not Janapada, Vishaya, Nadu, Sima or Sthala.
[3:2] - **Hierarchy:** kingdom → **Ahara** → Nigamas (towns) / Vishaya → **Grama** (village); Janapadas = vassal territories.
[3:3] - **Head of an Ahara:** the **Amatya**, usually a blood relative or son of the king (Sachivalayam 2019 ×2; APPSC PYQ); distant aharas under **Mahasenadhipatis**.
[3:4] - **⚠ Trap:** not Yuvaraja, Rajuka, Mahamatra, Kumaramatya or Vishayapati.
[3:5] - **'Amatya' meant:** **minister** (APPSC PYQ); four grades – Vishva-amatya (inner adviser), Raja-amatya (executes orders), Maha-amatya (special/finance), Amatya.
[3:6] - **Mantri Parishad:** council that helped the king.
[3:7] # Town and village
[3:8] - **Nigama Sabha:** ran **cities/towns**, not villages (G2 2008; APPSC PYQ); members = **Gahapatis**; named in the **Bhattiprolu** inscription.
[3:9] - **Gramika:** **village headman** (APPSC PYQ); Gramani – over 5 or 10 villages; Gahapati – farmers' representative.
[3:10] - **Gaulmika (Gulmika):** head of a **gulma** (cluster of villages / military outpost) – controlled the **military bases** (APPSC PYQ); also border-defence commander (Myakadoni).
[3:11] - **⚠ Trap:** NextGen calls the Gulmika the village head; the exam answer for "village headman" is **Gramika**.
[3:12] # Officers
[3:13] TABLE head: Officer | Role
  [3:13.0] Heranika (Hiranika) | Treasurer – cash revenue (Welfare Officers 2017; Asst Social Welfare)
  [3:13.1] Bhandagarika | Stores; revenue in kind
  [3:13.2] Nibandhanakara | Keeper of state documents = director of archives (Welfare Officers 2017)
  [3:13.3] Lekhaka | King's secretary; drafted documents and edicts
  [3:13.4] Pratihara | Defender of the fortress
  [3:13.5] Mahatalavara | Commander of border provinces
  [3:13.6] Akshapatala | Secretariat; kept the king's orders
  [3:13.7] Mahadharmika / Mahaaryaka | Religious-educational / legal disputes
  [3:13.8] Visvamaatyulu / Rajaamatyas | Consulted on important decisions / advisers
  [3:13.9] Rajjugahaka / Pradeshika | Land surveyor / police
  [3:13.10] Akshapatakas | Kept records of the king's orders
[3:14] - **⚠ Trap:** Mahataraka, Dhutaka, Pratihari, Patlika-palaka are distractors for treasurer and archivist.
[3:15] - **Most prominent Ahara:** **Govardhana** (Nasik inscription); others – Satavahani, Sopara, Mamala, Kodura, Vaijayanti.
[3:16] - **Pattikapala:** drafted the land-grant inscriptions.

## SUB 4 | Official Language – Prakrit
[4:0] - **Official, royal and inscription language:** **Prakrit** (G2 2011; Port Officer 2011; GS common paper; APPSC PYQ ×3); script **Brahmi**.
[4:1] - **Which Prakrit:** **Maharashtri Prakrit** (Welfare Officers).
[4:2] - **⚠ Trap:** not Sanskrit, Pali, Telugu, Tamil, Marathi or Aramaic.
[4:3] - **Early Ikshvaku inscriptions:** also **Prakrit** (Gazetted 2006); later Ikshvaku records turned to Sanskrit.
[4:4] - **Later shift:** Salankayanas – Sanskrit replaced Prakrit (5 of 8 inscriptions); Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi – Telugu.
[4:5] - **Sanskrit grew alongside:** **Kuntala Satakarni** made Sanskrit the royal language; Mahayana texts were written in Sanskrit.
[4:6] > A-R angle (UP RO 2017): "Sanskrit developed along with Prakrit and folk languages" ✓ because "kings promoted Sanskrit and folk languages in literature" ✓ – R explains A.
[4:7] > Statement angle (UPPCS 2021): "Satavahanas were nurturers of Prakrit" ✓; "public aspect of the arts got more encouragement" ✓ → both.
[4:8] - **Firsts:** first complete Sanskrit inscription = **Junagadh** (Rudradaman); first Telugu word **"Nagabu"** (Amaravati); "Vepuru" in Myakadoni.
[4:9] - **Desi bhasha:** language of the common people, of the **Dravidian** family.
[4:10] - **"Italian of the East":** Telugu (its words end in vowels) – phrase from Niccolo de Conti, used by the British.
[4:11] - **Telugu script:** developed from **Brahmi**, via the Bhattiprolu and Telugu-Kannada scripts.
[4:12] - **Official language of AP:** Telugu from **1966** (AP Official Languages Act), not 1956.
[4:13] - **Gundaram inscriptions:** 11 Satavahana-era Prakrit-Brahmi records decoded by the ASI in the **Gundaram reserve forest, Peddapalli district** (Telangana) in May 2025.
[4:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.9, p.23-24; Rocket AP History p.16; app notes 1:47 (sub 8)
## SUB 5 | Coinage
[5:0] - **Currency:** the **karshapana** (APPSC PYQ); 1 suvarna (gold unit) = **35 karshapanas**; Sathamana, Rama Raya, Venkataraya are later coins.
[5:1] - **Metals:** **lead** (most common), copper, bronze, potin, silver – **nickel was NOT used** (ACF 2022).
[5:2] - **⚠ Sources differ:** NextGen lists gold among Satavahana coin metals — settled: they minted base metals, lead dominant; gold was not a mint metal (https://iaspoint.com/satavahana-coinage/).
[5:3] - **⚠ Trap:** **iron coins with a thin copper coating** = **Ikshvakus** (APPSC key; G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ) – not Satavahanas, Salankayanas or Vishnukundins.
[5:4] - **Ship-motif coins:** **Vasishthiputra Pulumavi** and **Yajnasri Satakarni** – proof of **sea trade** (G2-A 1989).
[5:5] - **Who minted ship coins:** standard answer **Yajnasri Satakarni** – anchored-ship mark (JL 2004).
[5:6] - **Typical symbol:** the **ship** (Asst Social Welfare) – not Lakshmi, Garuda or arrow; no later ruler issued ship-motif coins.
[5:7] - **Simuka's coins:** mostly at **Kotilingala** (Karimnagar), legend "Siri Chimuka Sata"; also Kondapur (Medak) (Asst Social Welfare); Dhulikatta is a distractor.
[5:8] > A-R angle (Welfare Officers): "Coins are a crucial source" ✓; "Satakarni's coins show only religious symbols, no portraits" ✗ – they carry elephant, horse, lion and the Ujjain symbol, and portrait coins began with Gautamiputra.
[5:9] - **Jogalthambi:** Nahapana's silver coins restruck by Gautamiputra.
[5:10] - **Portrait coins:** an indigenous innovation, beginning with Gautamiputra.
[5:11] - **Bilingual silver:** Vasishthiputra Satakarni – Prakrit + a Dravidian language.
[5:12] - **Karshapana:** the **silver** coin was called a karshapana (copper and lead karshapanas also circulated).
[5:13] - **Ujjain symbol:** a **cross with four circles** on the reverse; obverse motifs – elephant, horse, lion, camel, chaitya.
[5:14] - **Coin legends:** in **Prakrit**, **Brahmi** script – not Kharosthi.
[5:15] - **⚠ Trap:** lead, copper and bronze coins with **bull and lion** symbols = **Pallavas**, not Satavahanas.
[5:16] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.10, p.19-21; Rocket AP History p.14, p.19; app notes 1:47 (sub 7); https://iaspoint.com/satavahana-coinage/

[6:0] TABLE head: Coast | Ports (Satavahana period)

  [6:0.1] East | Maisolia (Machilipatnam), Kantakossyla (Ghantasala), Koddura (Gudur, Krishna), Arikamedu (Puducherry)

[6:3] - **⚠ Sources differ:** NextGen puts Kalyan in Karnataka — settled: Kalyan is in Thane district, **Maharashtra**; the Periplus records it (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalyan).
[6:4] - **East-coast ports:** Ghantasala and Masulipatnam (Welfare Officers: "2 and 4"); Barygaza and Kalyan are western; Koddura is eastern too.
[6:5] - **Ptolemy's "great market":** **Maisolia (Machilipatnam)** (APPSC PYQ); not Nizampatnam, Kalingapatnam, Kotilingala.
[6:6] - **⚠ Trap:** Motupalli is an east-coast port that rose under the Kakatiyas — not a western Satavahana port (Rocket Sheets misprint); "port not used in Satavahana times" = **Motupalli** (APPSC PYQ). Today in Bapatla district.
[6:7] - **Koddura:** the Periplus' only east-coast port of the Satavahanas.
[6:8] # Evidence of sea trade
[6:9] - **All three testify:** the **Periplus**, **Roman coins** found in Andhra-Telangana, and the **ship coins** of Pulomavi and Yajnasri (Welfare Officers Mains).
[6:10] - **Roman objects found most in India:** **coins** (IAS 1999) – Bhattiprolu, Nagarjunakonda, Attirala, Salihundam, Vijayawada, Vinukonda, Nellore.
[6:11] - **Pliny:** Roman gold drained to India for fine cloth (mallasella, muslin) – about 12.5 million denarii a year.
[6:12] - **Arikamedu:** the Periplus' "market street of the Romans" (near Puducherry).
[6:13] - **Exports / imports:** muslin, spices, pearls, diamonds, ivory out; gold (Malaya, Sumatra), wine (Italy, Arabia), silk and camphor (China) in.
[6:14] - **Inland route:** Ujjain → Bharuch via **Paithan and Tagara**; the Satavahanas were famed road builders ("Mahapatha nirmatalu").
[6:15] - **Reach:** Rome, and the eastern islands – Siam, Java, Borneo.
[6:16] > A-R angle (G2 PYQ): "Trade with Rome flourished after the Satavahanas" ✗ (it peaked under them and declined); "no ruler issued ship-motif coins after them" ✓.
[6:17] - **Products of Andhra centres:** Palnadu – diamonds; Guduru – fine cotton; Vinukonda – metal; **Guntupalli – copper and iron** (not gold).
[6:18] - **Kalyan and the Sakas:** per the Periplus, Kalyan was ruined by the Sakas (Kshatrapas) – the answer for "port destroyed by the Sakas".
[6:19] - **West coast into the Deccan:** Kalyan → **Naneghat pass** → Junnar → Paithan.
[6:20] - **Vengipuram:** = **Pedavegi** (near Eluru).
[6:21] - **Constantine gold coin:** found at **Kudali Sangameswaram** (Alampur) – of a later period.
[6:22] - **Egypt (Feb 2026):** about **30 Indian inscriptions** of the Indo-Roman phase (Tamil-Brahmi, Prakrit, Sanskrit) reported from tombs of the Theban necropolis, Luxor.
[6:23] - **Significance:** land grants, guild banking and Roman trade laid the base of **Deccan urbanisation**.

## SUB 7 | Land Revenue, Taxes and Tax-free Grants
[7:0] - **Bhaga:** land revenue = king's share of **1/6** of the produce (JA NTR UHS) – the chief income.
[7:1] - **First tax-free land grants:** to **Brahmanas and Buddhist monks** – by the **Satavahanas** (Deputy screening 2023; Welfare Officers) – not Parthians, Kushanas, Shakas, Guptas, Mauryas or Ikshvakus.
[7:2] - **Satakarni I:** gave tax-free land, cows and gold to Brahmanas (Naneghat); granted land could not be entered by royal officers.
[7:3] - **Other dues:** Karukara (artisans' and professionals' tax), Kara, Sulka (water tax), Deya-meya (king's share in kind).
[7:4] - **Vetti:** forced unpaid labour; with hereditary village headships it fed the growth of **feudalism**; also the term for the exploitation of village servant castes by **feudal lords in Telangana** (Social Structure 2016).
[7:5] - **⚠ Trap:** **Moturpha** was a **British** tax on trades and occupations (looms) in Andhra, not Satavahana; and the British salt monopoly did apply in Andhra (A ✓, R ✗; Welfare Officers).
[7:6] - **Nivartana:** land unit, about 1.5 acres.
[7:7] - **Spread of cultivation:** mainly in the **Krishna-Godavari** basin; paddy transplantation; chief crops rice and cotton.
[7:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.7, p.16-17; Rocket AP History p.48; app notes 1:47 (sub 3)

## SUB 9 | Feudatories and Military Camps
[9:0] - **Not strongly centralised:** many vassal janapadas run by local kings, supervised by **Mahabhojas** and **Maharathas**.
[9:1] > A-R angle (Welfare Officers): "Semi-autonomous Bhojas and Maharathis made the polity decentralised" ✓; "the centre discouraged local leaders and ruled all areas directly" ✗.
[9:2] TABLE head: Feudatory | Feature
  [9:2.0] Maharathas | Hereditary; collected (and could remit) taxes
  [9:2.1] Mahabhojas | Hereditary; inscriptions omit the emperor → near-independent
  [9:2.2] Mahasenapatis / Mahasenadhipatis, Mahatalavaras | Commanders of border provinces against the Sakas
[9:3] - **⚠ Trap:** **Mahajanapadas** were NOT Satavahana feudal chiefs (Social & Cultural History).
[9:4] - **Kataka:** permanent garrison; **Skandhavara:** temporary camp.
[9:5] - **Meaning together:** **military camps and settlements** that served as **administrative centres** when the king was present (G1 2024; JA NTR UHS); not monks' tax-free villages, rock-cut areas or paddy lands.
[9:6] - **Army:** chaturanga bala (Hathigumpha); Amaravati sculptures show infantry in front, cavalry and elephants on the flanks, archers at the rear.
[9:7] - **Highest feudatory:** the **Raja**, who could issue coins; grading Raja > Mahabhoja > Senapati.
[9:8] - **Maharathis:** hereditary tax-collecting feudatories, mainly of the **western Deccan**.
[9:9] - **Mahabhojas:** powerful local chiefs of the **Konkan** – not Eastern Bengal.
[9:10] - **Samanta rajyas:** Chutus – Mysore; Kuras – Kolhapur; Ikshvakus – Vijayapuri; they paid tribute and sent troops.
[9:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.12-14; app notes 1:47 (sub 2)
