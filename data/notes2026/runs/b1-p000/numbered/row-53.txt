## SUB 1 | Language of the Inscriptions
[1:0] - **Prakrit** = court language and language of the Ikshvaku inscriptions, including the Nagarjunakonda records of gifts to the great monastery (NTR Univ. JA; APPSC PYQ) – not Sanskrit, Telugu, Tamil or Paisachi.
[1:1] - **Ehuvala Santamula** brought **Sanskrit** into inscriptions; Sanskrit replaced Prakrit from the **4th c. CE**.
[1:2] TABLE head: Inscription | Language
  [1:2.0] Swarna Devalayam (Golden Temple), Nagarjunakonda, Ehuvala's 11th regnal year | First Sanskrit inscription of Andhra and South India
  [1:2.1] Elisri's Sarvadeva inscription | Sanskrit
  [1:2.2] Ehuvala's Gummadidurru record (Guntur) | Prakrit
[1:3] - Telugu words in the records: **Bapi, Remmanaka, Adavi, Ehuvala, Anikki** (not Dhanyakataka).
[1:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Ikshvakus p.5, p.9, p.14, p.19; Rocket AP History sheet #11 (Prakrit – Ikshvaku); app notes 1:53 (sub 3)
## SUB 2 | Sculpture – the Amaravati School at Nagarjunakonda
[2:0] - Nagarjunakonda = **main centre** of the **Amaravati School** in its **final, most mature phase**; **Mahayana** themes – Buddha's life and **Jataka** stories.
[2:1] - Early Ikshvaku carving (low relief) = the "**last flicker**" of the Satavahana Amaravati tradition.
[2:2] TABLE head: Sculpture / feature | Significance
  [2:2.0] Mandhata (Chakravarti Mandhata), Jaggayyapeta | Most important Ikshvaku sculpture; first depiction of a Chakravarti
  [2:2.1] Sati panel (Nagarjunakonda) | First depiction of sati in Indian art
  [2:2.2] Sculptors' names carved | Start of the "Sculptor's Style" – e.g. Badantacharya at the Mahavihara
[2:3] - **Largest stupa in AP** = **Amaravati**; oldest stupa in Andhra = **Bhattiprolu**.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Ikshvakus p.3, p.7, p.18; Rocket AP History sheet "Art" (Mandhata – Jaggayyapet); app notes 1:53 (sub 0)
## SUB 3 | Stupas, Temple Plans and Secular Buildings
[3:0] TABLE head: Stupa type | Built over
  [3:0.0] Dhatugarbha | Bodily relics (e.g. the Maha Chaitya)
  [3:0.1] Paribhogika | Objects used by monks (alms bowls)
  [3:0.2] Uddeshika | Nothing – purely commemorative
[3:1] - Major stupas: **wheel-shaped (spoked) plan** + **ayaka platforms** in the four directions, **five pillars** each; ayaka platforms belong to **Buddhist stupas**.
[3:2] - Temples follow the **Agamic** plan: garbhagudi, antarala, mantapa, dhvajastambha, prakara; the Ikshvakus laid the base of **Dravida** temple architecture.
[3:3] TABLE head: Structure | Purpose
  [3:3.0] Rajaprasadam and prakaras | Palace complex; compound walls
  [3:3.1] Kreeda Vedi (amphitheatre) | Sports arena, thought built with Roman help; no parallel elsewhere in India
  [3:3.2] Acoustic building | Built on rules of sound; the site's most unique find
  [3:3.3] Kummakundam | Water tanks / bathing pools
  [3:3.4] Yeleswaram | Town credited to commander Elisri, who also built the Sarvadevalaya
[3:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Ikshvakus p.18-19; app notes 1:53 (sub 1, 2)
