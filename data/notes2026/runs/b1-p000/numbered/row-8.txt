## SUB 0 | Metals and Raw Materials → Source
[0:0] - **Copper = the first metal used by man** (not iron, bronze, gold or silver); chalcolithic cultures began with it (APPSC G2 2011, RAS 2012).
[0:1] - Harappans used **copper, bronze, gold, silver, lead**; **iron NOT found** at any Harappan site (CGPSC 2011) – a Bronze Age culture.
[0:2] - **Bronze = copper + tin**; tools, weapons and the **Dancing Girl** (lost-wax casting). Pure copper objects far outnumber bronze ones.
[0:3] - Harappans **pioneered silver** use; silver imported (Afghanistan).
[0:4] TABLE head: Material | Source
  [0:4.0] Copper | Khetri mines (Rajasthan); also Oman (Magan)
  [0:4.1] Tin | Afghanistan, Iran
  [0:4.2] Gold | Karnataka (Kolar), Afghanistan
  [0:4.3] Lapis lazuli | Badakhshan via Shortughai (Afghanistan)
  [0:4.4] Carnelian | Bharuch / Saurashtra (Gujarat)
  [0:4.5] Steatite | South Rajasthan, north Gujarat
  [0:4.6] Chert, limestone (blades) | Sukkur and Rohri hills (Sindh) – mass-produced blades for Sindh (CDS 2020); not Khetri
  [0:4.7] Shells | Saurashtra coast, Nageshwar, Balakot

## SUB 1 | Earliest Agriculture – Mehrgarh and the First Cereal
[1:0] TABLE head: First | Site / fact
  [1:0.0] Earliest agriculture and animal domestication in the subcontinent | Mehrgarh (Kachhi plain, Baluchistan), c. 7000 BC – not Lothal, Harappa, Mundigak, Koldihwa, Kili Gul Muhammad
  [1:0.1] First farmers' crops | Mainly barley, also wheat; sheep, goats, cattle
  [1:0.2] First cereal used by man | Barley (c. 8000 BC)
  [1:0.3] Early Baluchistan farming villages | Kili Gul Muhammad (c. 5000–4000 BC), Damb Sadaat, Anjira, Rana Ghundai
  [1:0.4] First agriculture per latest research; oldest paddy | Lahuradeva (Sant Kabir Nagar, UP)
  [1:0.5] Once thought oldest paddy | Koldihwa (Belan valley, UP)
  [1:0.6] Rice and cattle pens (not barley) | Mahagara
  [1:0.7] Earliest iron in India | Atranjikhera (Kali river); dug by R.C. Gaur (1962)
[1:1] - Mehrgarh (7th–3rd millennium BCE) is the neolithic root of the Harappan civilisation; similar villages at Mundigak (Afghanistan).
[1:2] - Iron spread in North India 1000–600 BC (Later Vedic).

## SUB 2 | Agriculture, Crops and Cotton
[2:0] - **Main occupation = agriculture** (not trade or hunting); surplus stored in granaries fed the cities.
[2:1] TABLE head: Crop | Site of evidence
  [2:1.0] Wheat (largest finds) | Harappa; Mehrgarh
  [2:1.1] Barley | Banawali
  [2:1.2] Rice husk (earliest in South Asia) | Lothal, Rangpur
  [2:1.3] Millets and rice as staple | Rangpur, Surkotada
  [2:1.4] Cotton | Mohenjodaro (woven cotton piece); Mehrgarh
  [2:1.5] Garlic (earliest) | Balu (Haryana)
  [2:1.6] Indigo | Rojdi
[2:2] - Also: peas, lentil, chickpea, **sesame**, **mustard**, dates, melon, grapes, henna.
[2:3] - **Cotton:** the Harappans were the **first to grow cotton** and make cotton cloth → "India" and "cotton" are the answers (UPPSC 2006, SSC 2001); Greeks called it **Sindon** (from Sindh). Not coins – the IVC had no coins.
[2:4] - **Maize** came with the Portuguese. Rig Vedic economy was pastoral.

## SUB 4 | Crafts, Industries and Standardisation
[4:0] TABLE head: Craft | Centre
  [4:0.0] Bead-making (carnelian, steatite, lapis) | Chanhudaro, Lothal
  [4:0.1] Shell work | Nageshwar, Balakot (specialised), Lothal, Chanhudaro
  [4:0.2] Black terracotta bangles | Kalibangan
  [4:0.3] Spinning, weaving | Mohenjodaro; Chanhudaro = "Lancashire of India"; Lothal = "Manchester of the Harappan civilisation"
  [4:0.4] Ivory, copper, bead workshops | Kuntasi
  [4:0.5] Salt | Kerala-no-dhoro
  [4:0.6] Earliest furnace | Kot Bala
[4:1] - **Chanhudaro** was almost wholly a craft town: beads, shell, metal, seals, weights.
[4:2] - **Spindle-whorls** common in houses; **no spinning wheel** found (UPSC 2026).
[4:3] - **Boat models:** Mohenjodaro (boat on seals) and Lothal (clay boats, terracotta ship).
[4:4] - **Kenoyer:** the amazing **standardisation** of crafts (using non-local raw materials and complex technology) was possibly due to **state control** (NDA 2015); others add hereditary craft groups and internal trade.

## SUB 5 | External Trade – Meluhha and Mesopotamia
[5:0] TABLE head: Mesopotamian name | Region
  [5:0.0] Meluhha | Indus (Harappan) region
  [5:0.1] Dilmun | Bahrain
  [5:0.2] Magan | Oman (Makran coast)
[5:1] - **Closest external contact = Mesopotamia** (not Egypt): Harappan seals at **Susa, Ur, Nippur**, Failaka, Bahrain; three Mesopotamian cylinder seals at Mohenjodaro; Dilmun weights followed the Harappan standard.
[5:2] - **Sargon of Akkad** (c. 2300 BCE) records ships from Meluhha.
[5:3] - **Exports:** carnelian beads, lapis lazuli, ivory, timber, cotton, copper, gold. **Imports:** silver, tin, woollens.
[5:4] - Contacts also with Altyn-depe (Turkmenistan), Shahr-i-Sokhta and Tepe Yahya (Iran); network across the Arabian Sea and Persian Gulf.
[5:5] - **Nov 2025:** a second **Dilmun temple** (c. 1900–1800 BCE) found on **Failaka**, Kuwait.
[5:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.90-92; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.3, p.6; old app notes row 8
## SUB 6 | Internal Trade and Transport
[6:0] - Trade by **barter**, no metal money; **bullock carts** (solid wheels) and **boats**. Reach: South India, UP, Maharashtra, Bihar, Rajasthan.
[6:1] - **Harappa** = inland city on the Ravi, not a port; Harappa and Mohenjodaro had warehouses.
[6:2] TABLE head: Port / outpost | Location
  [6:2.0] Lothal | Gulf of Khambhat, Gujarat – tidal dock
  [6:2.1] Sutkagendor | Makran coast, Baluchistan; westernmost
  [6:2.2] Balakot | Sindh coast; shells
  [6:2.3] Kuntasi, Bhagatrav, Prabhas Patan | Gujarat coast
  [6:2.4] Shortughai | Afghanistan – trading post for lapis
[6:3] - Farm surplus + trade + crafts + standard weights → **urbanisation**.
