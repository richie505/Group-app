## SUB 0 | Revenue – Bhaga, Gupta Taxes and Vishti
[0:0] # The king's share
[0:1] - **Prescribed rate:** the law books (Dharmashastras / "Hindu law") fix the king's share (**bhaga**) at **one-sixth** of the produce (UPPCS 1992; State PSC ×2). In practice the Gupta king took **one-fourth to one-sixth**.
[0:2]   - ⚠ NextGen Indian History 03 p.8 gives "about 1/7" – an outlier; the same file (p.6) and PMF give 1/4–1/6, and exam keys give **1/6**.
[0:3] - **Tax trend:** under the Guptas **land taxes rose** while **trade and commerce taxes fell**.
[0:4] - **Bhaga and Bali:** **sources of revenue** (UPPCS 1996) – bhaga = king's share of produce; bali = once a voluntary offering, later compulsory. Not military departments, rituals or divisions.
[0:5] # Revenue terms
[0:6] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:6.0] Bhaga | King's share of produce – main land revenue (1/6)
  [0:6.1] Bhoga | Periodic supplies in kind – fruits, firewood, flowers
  [0:6.2] Kara | Periodic general tax
  [0:6.3] Udranga | New Gupta tax: land revenue in the inscriptions (nature unclear – perhaps water or police tax)
  [0:6.4] Uparikara | New Gupta tax, probably on temporary tenants – an additional farm tax
  [0:6.5] Udakabhaga | Irrigation (water) tax (UPPCS Mains 2009)
  [0:6.6] Halivakara | Plough tax paid by every cultivator owning a plough (SSC CGL 2019)
  [0:6.7] Hiranya | Tax paid in cash (gold) instead of produce
  [0:6.8] Sulka (Shulka) | Customs duty and tolls on merchandise
  [0:6.9] Sita | Revenue from crown land
  [0:6.10] Vata-bhuta | Cess for rites to winds and spirits
  [0:6.11] Uparnika / Klipta / Upaklipta / Valiam | Tax on border farmers / taxes on land sale and purchase / commodity tax
[0:7] # Vishti – forced labour
[0:8] - **Vishti:** unpaid **forced labour** for the army, officials, the state or the donee (Telugu **vetti**); it was considered **a source of income for the State, a sort of tax paid by the people** (UPSC 2019).
[0:9]   - It was imposed in **central and western India** (Malwa, Kathiawar – Junagadh inscription), so "totally absent in MP and Kathiawar" is wrong; it was **unpaid**, so no weekly wages; not confined to the eldest son.
[0:10] - **Peasants:** had to feed the royal army on the march and supply officials on tour; they sank to a **semi-servile** status.
[0:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 03 p.6-8; PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.66-67; app notes 1:63 (sub 5, 6)
## SUB 1 | Land Grants, Feudalism and the Agrarian Structure
[1:0] # Land grants
[1:1] TABLE head: Grant | Grantee | Rights
  [1:1.0] Agrahara | Brahmanas (religious functionaries) | Tax-free, hereditary; right to collect dues from peasants
  [1:1.1] Brahmadeya | Brahmanas, singly or jointly | Tax exemption + administrative rights
  [1:1.2] Devagrahara / Devadana | Temples and monasteries | Revenue for worship
  [1:1.3] Secular grants | Officials, samantas | Revenue in lieu of salary
[1:2] - **Tax-free agrarian land** went to the **Brahmins** (MPPSC 2014) – not to military or civil officials or court scholars.
[1:3] - **Most villages donated to temples and Brahmins:** the **Guptas** (State PSC key).
[1:4] - **Earliest land grants:** the **Satavahanas** (Deccan, early centuries CE); the practice became common under the Guptas.
[1:5] - **Effects:** royal officials barred from entering granted villages; grantees could punish criminals; **sub-infeudation**; grants in **tribal frontiers** brought virgin land under cultivation and turned tribes into **sub-castes**.
[1:6] # Post-Gupta agrarian structure (AP recruitment papers)
[1:7] - **TRUE:** land grants in tribal frontiers brought **virgin land under cultivation**; post-Gupta **copper-plate charters describe various types of land**; the **Smritis** speak of graded rights in the same land – **Mahipati** (king), **Kshetrasvamin** (owner), **Karshaka** (cultivator) and **sub-tenant**.
[1:8] - **FALSE:** "Pallava records specify 10 to 15 kinds of taxes" – the Pallava grants mention **18 types of taxes** exempted for donated villages.
[1:9] - **Early medieval features:** hierarchical **land intermediaries** with feudal titles and military obligations, prevalence of **vishti**, and a relatively **closed village economy** – all three.
[1:10] # The feudalism debate
[1:11] - **Essential elements (UPSC 2015):** an administrative structure based on **control and possession of land**, and a **lord–vassal** relationship – a **strong central authority** with weak local power is **not** feudal (it is the opposite).
[1:12] - **Post-Gupta feudalism (UPPCS Mains 2004 A–R):** the main feature of the post-Gupta agrarian structure was **feudalism** **because** a **landowning intermediate class** and a **dependent peasantry** arose – A and R true, R explains A.
[1:13] TABLE head: Historian | Position
  [1:13.0] R.S. Sharma | Proponent – land grants, sub-infeudation, vishti, decline of trade, urban decay, fewer gold coins
  [1:13.1] D.C. Sircar | Critic – grants transferred revenue rights, not ownership
  [1:13.2] Harbans Mukhia | Critic – "Was there feudalism in Indian history?"; free peasantry, no serfdom
  [1:13.3] B.D. Chattopadhyaya | Integrative model – grants spread state society into new areas
[1:14] - **Evidence cited (7th c.):** decline of long-distance trade and **gold coinage**, shrinking towns.
[1:15] - **Satavahana land measure:** **nivartana** ≈ **1.5 acres** (100 nivartanas granted to monks).

## SUB 3 | Kingship, Officials and Local Bodies
[3:0] # Kingship
[3:1] - **Monarchy:** Gupta rule was **kingship** (CDS 2008) – hereditary, with divine claims, not republican or democratic; **primogeniture was not firm**.
[3:2] - **Titles:** **Maharajadhiraja, Parameshvara, Paramabhattaraka**; kings likened to **Vishnu**; **Samudragupta** equated with **Kubera (Dhanada), Varuna, Indra and Yama (Antaka)**.
[3:3] - **Decentralised:** unlike the Mauryas, much of the empire was held by **feudatories (samantas)** – **Samudragupta** reinstated defeated kings; officials often paid in **land or kind**.
[3:4] # Officials
[3:5] TABLE head: Official | Function
  [3:5.0] Kumaramatya | Most important officers, appointed by the king in the home provinces; ministers and commanders chosen from them
  [3:5.1] Sandhivigrahika | Minister of peace and war
  [3:5.2] Mahadandanayaka | Chief judicial / military officer
  [3:5.3] Mahabaladhikrita | Commander-in-chief
  [3:5.4] Dandapashika | District police officer
  [3:5.5] Akshapataladhikrita | Keeper of royal records
  [3:5.6] Pustapala | Keeper of land-transfer records
  [3:5.7] Dutaka | Spy / messenger
[3:6] # Units
[3:7] TABLE head: Unit | Level | Head
  [3:7.0] Bhukti (desha) | Province | Uparika
  [3:7.1] Vishaya | District | Vishayapati
  [3:7.2] Vithi (eastern India) | Group of villages | Ayuktaka
  [3:7.3] Grama | Village | Gramika / Gramadhyaksha with mahattaras
[3:8] - **Village headman (CDS 2012):** he became **more important** in Gupta times, and **no land transaction could be effected without his consent** – so "lost importance" is wrong; "Kumaramatyas were the most important officers appointed by the king in home provinces" is right → **only I**.
[3:9] - **Mahattara and Pattakila:** designations of the **village headman** (UPSC 2014) – not military officers, Vedic ritualists or guild chiefs.
[3:10] # Town board and courts
[3:11] - **Adhikarana (Kotivarsha, Damodarpur plates):** **Nagarashreshthi** (chair, chief banker), **Sarthavaha** (chief caravan trader), **Prathama kulika** (chief artisan), **Prathama kayastha** (chief scribe); a **district council** helped the district officer.
[3:12] - **Law:** civil and criminal law first clearly demarcated – **property disputes = civil**, theft and adultery = criminal; **Narada smriti** (survives complete, civil law), Brihaspati and Katyayana (fragments).
