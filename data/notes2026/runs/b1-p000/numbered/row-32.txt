## SUB 0 | Alexander's Campaign – Route, Years, Hydaspes, Retreat
[0:0] TABLE head: Event | Year
  [0:0.0] Crossed the Hindu Kush (start of the invasion) | 327 BC (APPSC G2 2008 key)
  [0:0.1] Entered through the Khyber Pass, crossed the Indus | 326 BC
  [0:0.2] Battle of the Hydaspes vs Porus | 326 BC
  [0:0.3] Halt at the Beas (Hyphasis) | 326 BC
  [0:0.4] Left India from Patala | 325 BC (about 19 months in India)
  [0:0.5] Died at Babylon | 323 BC, aged 32
[0:1] - **"When did Alexander invade?"** – **327 BC** when offered (APPSC); **326 BC** in UPPSC 2006 (Indus crossing, Hydaspes).
[0:2] - **Hydaspes** = Greek name of the **Jhelum** (Sanskrit **Vitasta**); Alexander defeated **Porus** there (UPRO 2013; APPSC G1 2002). Hyphasis = Beas, Acesines = Chenab, Hydraotes = Ravi.
[0:3] - **Porus** was **not executed** – asked how to be treated, he said "**Like a king**"; Alexander **restored and enlarged** his kingdom and made him ally/satrap.
[0:4] - **Bucephala** (after his horse) and **Nicaea** were founded on the Jhelum.
[0:5] - **Pushkalavati:** its Ashtaka raja **Astes (Hasti)** resisted and was killed – no alliance.
[0:6] - **Satrapies:** PMF – the land from the Indus to the Beas went into **three parts under Greek governors**; Telugu Akademi texts count **six satrapies** (hence the "a, b & d" key).
[0:7] - **Retreat:** army refused to cross the **Beas** (not the Ravi) – **war-weariness, homesickness, disease, fear of the Nanda army**; **12 stone altars** on its northern bank; **Nearchus** led the fleet down the Indus.
[0:8] - **A–R (UPPCS 1998):** Alexander left in 325 BC ✓; "defeated by Chandragupta Maurya" ✗.
[0:9] > Hydaspes = Jhelum = Vitasta; Hyphasis = Beas (halt point).

## SUB 1 | India on the Eve of Alexander – Nandas and North-west Rulers
[1:0] - **Magadha:** **Nanda** dynasty ruled North India (IAS 2000); king **Dhana Nanda** (Greek **Agrammes**), the **last Nanda** and Alexander's contemporary – not Mahapadma Nanda or Chandragupta; commander **Bhaddasala**; huge army.
[1:1] - **North-west:** small independent states (Taxila, Porus's kingdom, Gandhara) – no united front.

## SUB 2 | Achaemenid (Persian) Invasion and Its Influence
[2:0] - **Cyrus II** (c. 558–530 BC): first Achaemenid to campaign into the north-west – captured **Gandhara and Kamboja**.
[2:1] - **Darius I** (**522–486 BC**): usual answer for the **first Persian ruler to capture/annex Indian land** (RAS 1994) – Sindh and Punjab west of the Indus = **20th satrapy** (Hindush).
[2:2] - **Herodotus:** the most fertile satrapy; tribute of **360 talents of gold dust** (about one-third of the revenue).
[2:3] - **Influence:** **Kharosthi** from Aramaic, written **right to left**; Ashoka's **Kandahar** edict partly in **Aramaic**; words **lipi / dipi**; third-person preamble (Darius model); polished pillars; **satrapy** system.
[2:4] > First to campaign = Cyrus; first to annex (20th satrapy) = Darius I.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.94; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 2 p.53; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.76; Rocket Part 2 p.113; old app notes 1:32
## SUB 3 | Greek Writers and Effects of the Invasion
[3:0] TABLE head: Writer | Status
  [3:0.0] Nearchus (admiral), Onesicritus, Aristobulus | Came with Alexander; first-hand accounts
  [3:0.1] Arrian, Curtius, Plutarch | Later writers
  [3:0.2] Deimachus | Later envoy of Antiochus I to Bindusara – did not come with Alexander (UPPSC 2015)
[3:1] - **Effects:** direct contact and **new land and sea routes** between Greece and India; small NW states weakened, clearing the way for **Chandragupta Maurya**, who expelled the Greek satraps with **Porus (Parvataka)** as ally.
[3:2] - **Sandrokottas** = Chandragupta; the invasion is the **first dated event** of Indian history.
[3:3] - **Little lasting political mark:** the earlier Persian rule left the deeper influence.
