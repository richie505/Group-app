## SUB 0 | Allahabad Prasasti – Harisena, the Pillar and Its Inscriptions
[0:0] # The prashasti
[0:1] - **Allahabad Pillar inscription (Prayaga Prashasti)** = the chief source for **Samudragupta**: his conquests and personality (UPSC 2006 ×2; UP Lower 2004; SSC 2019; APPSC Draughtsman).
[0:2] - **Composer:** **Harisena**, court poet, who was at once **Kumaramatya, Sandhivigrahika** (minister of peace and war) and **Mahadandanayaka** (SI Mains 2017) – not Ravikirti (Aihole, Pulakesin II).
[0:3] - **Form:** classical **Sanskrit**, **champu** style (prose + verse), Gupta **Brahmi** script, **33 lines**.
[0:4] - Harisena of the prashasti is **not** the later Jain Harisena of the **Brihatkatha Kosha**.
[0:5] # The pillar
[0:6] - An **Ashokan** pillar first set up at **Kaushambi**; **Akbar** moved it to the Allahabad (Prayagraj) fort.
[0:7] TABLE head: Inscription on the pillar | Fact
  [0:7.0] Ashoka's edicts | Brahmi; dhamma
  [0:7.1] Queen's Edict | Queen Karuvaki
  [0:7.2] Samudragupta's prashasti | By Harisena; glorifies war
  [0:7.3] Jahangir's record | Persian
[0:8] - **Not on the pillar:** **Kumaragupta** (and no Shahjahan, Aurangzeb or Dara Shikoh).
[0:9] - **Mehrauli Iron Pillar** = **Chandragupta II** ("Chandra"), not Samudragupta.
[0:10] # Reading the prashasti (CAPF 2017)
[0:11] - Upinder Singh: the prashasti presents the Gupta empire as the **epicentre of a complex web of varied political relationships** – the line-number options in that PYQ are misquoted.
[0:12] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval India Part 3 p.55, p.57-58; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 3 p.3; Upinder Singh, A History of Ancient and Early Medieval India; app notes 1:61 (sub 0, 5)
## SUB 1 | Samudragupta's Southern Campaign – Andhra Kings
[1:0] # Policy
[1:1] TABLE head: Group | Treatment | Regions
  [1:1.0] North (Aryavarta) | Rulers slain, kingdoms annexed | Ganga–Yamuna region
  [1:1.1] Atavika rajyas | Subdued | Forest kingdoms of central India
  [1:1.2] Frontier kings | Paid tribute | Assam, Nepal, Bengal
  [1:1.3] South (Dakshinapatha) | Captured and released – reinstated as tributaries | 12 kings from Kosala to Kanchi
  [1:1.4] Distant kings | Homage | Kushanas, Sakas, Sri Lanka
[1:2] - Southern policy = **grahana-moksha-anugraha** → **no permanent annexation** in the south (APPSC G1 statement II true).

[1:5] - **Ruler of Vengi** during the campaign = **Hastivarman** (SI 2019; APPSC PYQ) – not Damana, Mahendra or Vishnugopa.
[1:6] - **Pallava king defeated** = **Vishnugopa** of Kanchi (G2 1993) – not Vishnugupta, Nandivarman or Kubja Vishnuvardhana.
[1:7] - **Kosala's king** = **Mahendra**; Pishtapura's = **Mahendragiri**.
[1:8]   - **⚠ Settled:** Devarashtra = **Yelamanchili** (Visakhapatnam) per NextGen; the old app note "S. Kota, Vizianagaram" is dropped. **Kaurala** is disputed (Bhandarkar: Kolleru lay inside Vengi; others Ganjam or Korada), but among the options Kolleru is the accepted answer.
[1:9] # AP angle
[1:10] - The campaign hit **coastal Andhra**: Pishtapura, **Vengi, Avamukta, Devarashtra**, Erandapalle, Palakka – the Allahabad Pillar is the **chief source for 4th-century Andhra** kings.
[1:11] - A Samudragupta-period record is found at **Sankaram** (Visakhapatnam).
[1:12] - Empire stretched from Nepal and Punjab to Kanchi; influence reached **Simhala** – not confined to Magadha.

## SUB 3 | Chandragupta I and the Early Guptas
[3:0] # Origin and order
[3:1] - **Sri Gupta** = **founder of the dynasty** (SSC 2015); ruled a small area of **North Bengal and South Bihar**; succeeded by **Ghatotkacha**.
[3:2] - **Origin** debated: Vaishya, later treated as Kshatriya; a Brahmana origin argued from the **Dharana gotra** of Prabhavatigupta – the Junagadh inscription does not settle it.
[3:3] - **Early territory:** Magadha, **Prayag**, eastern UP (**Vayu Purana**).
[3:4] - **Order:** Sri Gupta → Ghatotkacha → Chandragupta I → Samudragupta → **Ramagupta** → Chandragupta II → Kumaragupta I → Skandagupta.
[3:5] # Chandragupta I (c. 319–335 CE)
[3:6] - **Real founder of the empire**; capital **Pataliputra**.
[3:7] - **Gupta era (Gupta Samvat):** **319–320 CE** = his accession (MPPCS 1991; SSC 2006; APPSC G1) – not Sri Gupta.
[3:8] - **Maharajadhiraja:** the first Gupta to take it (SSC 2018).
[3:9] - **Marriage:** **Kumaradevi**, a **Lichchhavi** princess – gave legitimacy and power.
[3:10] - **First Gupta coins:** gold **king-queen type** (Chandragupta I with Kumaradevi, legend "Lichchhavayah") (UPPCS 2010) – no coins of Sri Gupta or Ghatotkacha are known.
[3:11]   - **⚠ Settled:** one NextGen page says Chandragupta I "defeated Saka satraps" and "first minted silver coins" – wrong: both belong to **Chandragupta II** (PMF, NextGen p.4, p.7); Chandragupta I did **not** defeat the Sakas (APPSC G1 statement I false).
[3:12] # Gupta coins
[3:13] - Mostly **gold** (dinaras): show rulers, deities and rituals. **Samudragupta's types:** **lyrist (veena)**, **Ashvamedha** (to celebrate his conquests after the horse sacrifice), **archer**; the **king-queen** type is Chandragupta I's.
[3:14] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval India Part 3 p.55-56, p.60; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 3 p.2, p.4, p.7, p.17; app notes 1:61 (sub 1, 2, 3, 6)
