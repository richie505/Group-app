=== Disaster Management Cycle – Phases and Measures
# Phases (asked about 45 times)
| Phase | Stage | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Prevention** | Pre-disaster | Proactive, long-term; stops a hazard becoming a disaster: land-use zoning, **regulating deforestation**, control of industrial siting |
| **Mitigation** | Pre-disaster | **Reduces the damaging effects / risk** (lowers the likelihood of a disaster); **most cost-effective** phase: quake-resistant buildings, building codes, embankments, afforestation, insurance, **hazard maps**, **retrofitting**; **vulnerability analysis and risk assessment** |
| **Preparedness** | Pre-disaster | "**First and critical step**": early warning (CAP-SACHET), **public education on evacuation routes**, **mock / evacuation drills** (practise escape routes), **stockpiling food, water and supplies**, Aapda Mitra, Emergency Operation Centres, **contingency plans** |
| **Response** | **During / just after** | **Time-critical**: **search and rescue** (the major post-disaster operation), evacuation, first aid, temporary shelter, food and water, law and order |
| **Relief** | During / post | **Providing food, shelter and medical help** to victims |
| **Recovery** (rehabilitation + reconstruction) | **Post-disaster** | **Rebuilding infrastructure and restoring normalcy** – restore the area to its **pre-disaster state**, livelihoods, compensation, psycho-social care, **Build Back Better** |
- **Four phases** of the DM cycle (generally **4**): **Mitigation, Preparedness, Response, Recovery** (Rocket: "Mitigation, Preparation, Response, Recovery"). **Eradication**, rescue-only or risk-assessment lists are not the cycle.
- **Stages**: **Pre-disaster = prevention + mitigation + preparedness**; **during = response**; **post-disaster = recovery, rehabilitation, reconstruction**. **Recovery / reconstruction are NOT pre-disaster**; **prevention and mitigation are NOT post-disaster**.
- After a disaster the phases are **sequential and sometimes simultaneous**; the cycle is **continuous** – lessons from recovery feed prevention.
- Sequences: during – early warning → evacuation → rescue → shelters → emergency medical / water; after – **relief → restoration → reconstruction → resilient rebuilding**.
- DM needs a **proactive strategy** (not reactive); its main aim is **saving lives and reducing damage**; a contingency plan for people should be in **simple language with basic information and steps**.
# Mitigation – types and examples
| Strategy | Example / meaning |
|---|---|
| **Structural mitigation** | **Seismic-resistant buildings**, flood barriers, embankments, cyclone shelters; earthquakes – **building codes**; cyclones – **planting trees along the coast** |
| **Non-structural mitigation** | **Public awareness campaigns / public education**, community awareness, early warning, land-use rules |
| **Early warning system** | **Tsunami detection buoys**; a **crucial component of mitigation** because it gives **timely information** for preparedness and response (A and R true, R explains A) |
| **Seismic retrofitting** | **Strengthening existing (old) buildings** to withstand earthquakes |
| **Floodplain zoning** | Regulating land use to restrict construction in flood-prone areas |
| **Afforestation** | Planting trees to reduce landslide and flood risk |
| **Disaster-resistant design** | Materials and techniques that minimise damage |
- Mitigation **does not** mean evolving a **single strategy for all disasters**; it gives rapid recovery, fewer deaths and lower financial impact. It involves **community action** (CBDM – involving local people in preparedness and response).
- Cyclone pre-disaster mitigation: revamp drainage, **conserve wetlands**, cyclone shelters – **removing coastal trees/forests is NOT** mitigation.
- Educated communities are more likely to **follow land-use rules and early-warning instructions** (education → long-term mitigation).
- Types of mitigation: **avoidance, minimization, compensatory**; hazard-specific control activity – e.g. **flood levees**.
- Terms: **HAZOP** study = assessment of the **adequacy of safety measures**; **MAT** = **Mitigation Assessment Team**; **hazard map** = identifies **areas at risk for specific disasters**.
- **First responder**: the **community**. Urban response = civic bodies + fire brigade + police; the **district administration** has basic responsibility (Collector pivotal).
- Search and rescue principles (APPSC key): **Search, Find, Rescue**.
> Traps: mitigation, not prevention, is the phase "reducing likelihood/effects" when prevention is not offered; preparedness = drills, stockpiling, evacuation education; response = during the disaster.

=== Disaster Management Act, 2005 and the Authorities
# The Act (asked about 25 times)
- **Act No. 53 of 2005**; **Presidential assent 23 December 2005** (= "passed / enacted" date in exams); published in the **Gazette of India on 26 December 2005**; in force progressively from 2006; **11 Chapters, 79 Sections**; whole of India.
- Enacted after the **2004 Indian Ocean tsunami**; gives the legal and institutional framework at national, state and district levels.
- s.2(d) **disaster** = catastrophe, mishap, calamity or grave occurrence from natural or man-made causes, accident or negligence, **beyond the coping capacity of the community of the affected area**. Mitigation = measures to **reduce the risk, impact or effects** of a disaster.

=== Nodal Ministries, Early Warning Agencies and Relief Responsibility
# Who is responsible (asked about 17 times)

- **Central Relief Commissioner** (MHA) = nodal officer coordinating relief for natural disasters.

# High Powered Committee (1999)
- Constituted **August 1999**; first chairman **J.C. Pant**; reported **2001**; theme **"Building a Culture of Prevention"**; first natural disasters only, later man-made too.
- Identified **31 types of disasters** in **five broad categories**; recommended a **separate Department of DM** in GoI.
- Levels: **L0 = normal times (normalcy)**, **L1 = district level**, **L2 = State level**, **L3 = national level** (used in NDMP 2016).
# National Policy (NPDM 2009) and NDMP
- **NPDM**: approved by the Union Cabinet **22 October 2009** (exam answer for "approved / enforced"); prepared by NDMA under the DM Act; vision **"safe and disaster-resilient India"**; mainstreaming DRR into development, partnership with the **media**, identification and monitoring of risks. **NOT an objective**: "preparedness only after a disaster occurs". Primary responsibility: **State Government**.
- **NDMP 2016**: India's first national plan, all phases of the cycle, aligned with Sendai; time frames **short 5, medium 10, long 15 years**; revised 2019. Mainstreaming DRR is part of the **PM's 10-Point Agenda** (2016).

=== NDRF, SDRF and Disaster Funds
# National Disaster Response Force (asked about 13 times)
- **NDRF = National Disaster Response Force** (not "Relief / Rescue / Reserve Force"); constituted under **s.44** of the DM Act for specialised response to **natural and man-made disasters (incl. CBRN)**; raised / operational **2006**; **Raising Day 19 January**; world's largest dedicated response force.
- Under **MHA**; superintendence of **NDMA**; DG from the senior **IPS** cadre; HQ **New Delhi**; motto **"Aapada Seva Sadaiv Sarvatra"**.
- Battalions: initial **8** → **12** (2018–19 keys; "as of December 2019" = 12) → **16** now; ~1,149 personnel each; **18** search-and-rescue teams per battalion.
- Parent forces (deputation): **BSF, CRPF, CISF, ITBP, SSB, Assam Rifles** (central paramilitary forces).
- Skills: **CSSR** (Collapsed Structure Search and Rescue), flood and mountain rescue, medical first response, CBRN; teams placed by **vulnerability profile**.
- **AP**: **10th Battalion**; regional HQ for **AP, Telangana and Karnataka = Vijayawada**.
- **Budget estimate 2021-22: ₹1,209 crore** (₹1,209.41 crore; RE ₹1,313 crore).
- **NDRF Academy**: **Nagpur (2018)**.
# State Disaster Response Force
- Raised by States/UTs; first responder for the first 24–48 hours; **trained by NDRF/NDRF Academy**; **battalion** model; drawn from **State Police, Armed Police, Fire Services, Home Guards, Civil Defence**. Not yet in all states → strain on NDRF.
# Funds
| Fund | Section | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **State Disaster Response Fund** | 48 | First fund for **immediate relief** to victims of notified disasters; run by SEC; not for compensation or long-term rebuilding |
| National Disaster Response Fund | 46 | Central fund that **tops up SDRF** in severe calamities |
| NDMF / SDMF | 47 | **Mitigation** (long-term risk reduction), operationalised on the **15th Finance Commission**'s recommendations; ₹4,645 crore approved for 9 states incl. AP |

- **14th Finance Commission**: SDRF for **Andhra Pradesh, 2015-20 = ₹2,429 crore** (440 + 462 + 485 + 509 + 534).

=== Key Concepts – Hazard, Disaster, Vulnerability, Types
# Terms (asked about 10 times)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Hazard** | Dangerous condition or event with the **potential** to harm; an **earthquake in an uninhabited desert** is **not a disaster** – a hazard / **natural event** |
| **Disaster** | Occurs **when people are unable to cope** with the impact of a hazard: Hazard × Vulnerability with low capacity; French "desastre" = bad star |
| **Vulnerability** | Conditions raising susceptibility: limited health care, **elderly / disabled residents**, poverty, weak housing |
| **Capacity** | Strengths that reduce risk: **strong social networks and community cohesion**, volunteerism (communities **recover better**), early warning |
| Risk | Likelihood of loss; rises with hazard, exposure, vulnerability; **falls with coping capacity** |
- **Least** contributor to vulnerability: **strong social networks and community cohesion** (a capacity).
- Vulnerability types: **physical** (weak houses), **social** (age, gender, caste, disability), **economic** (poverty, daily wage), **environmental** (mangrove loss, deforestation), **systemic/institutional** (weak governance, poor coordination, weak early warning). Community factors: kutcha housing, thin health infrastructure, weak early warning; **high crop-insurance coverage reduces** vulnerability.
# Types of disasters
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Geological** | **Earthquakes**, landslides, tsunamis, volcanoes |
| Hydro-meteorological (water and climate) | Cyclones, floods, droughts, cloudbursts, heat waves |
| Biological | Epidemics, pest attacks |
| **Man-made** (chemical / industrial, accidents, societal) | **Bhopal gas leak (MIC, 2–3 Dec 1984)**, **car accidents**, **stampedes**, fires |
- Four broad emergency types: **natural disasters, environmental emergencies, complex emergencies, pandemic emergencies** – **pandemic emergency** = **sudden onset of contagious disease** that affects health, disrupts services and businesses, with high economic and social cost.
- Natural disasters cause most damage in **developing countries**; deaths from natural disasters in India 1980–2010: **1,43,039**.

=== Technology – GIS and Remote Sensing
# GIS in disaster management
- **GIS** stores, analyses and presents **spatial data**; maps hazard zones and vulnerable people; in floods it **analyses flood patterns and topography**; it cannot give details of individual victims.
| Phase / hazard | GIS use |
|---|---|
| Prevention / mitigation | Hazard zonation, vulnerability and risk mapping |
| Preparedness | **Evacuation route planning**, resource mapping |
| Response / recovery | Real-time damage assessment; Build Back Better |
| Cyclone | Track monitoring, **storm surge modelling** |
| Landslide | Susceptibility mapping (GSI NLSM) |
| Drought | **NADAMS** |
| Tsunami | Inundation modelling (INCOIS) |
| Tools | **INSAT-3D/3DR** track cyclones; **NDEM** (ISRO) GIS repository; **Google Earth Engine** – rapid flood mapping from large satellite archives |
- **Landslide Hazard Zonation**: divides hill areas into homogeneous zones by **degree of susceptibility, hazard or risk**.
