=== Local Winds of the World and India
# Local winds – region and nature (asked 18 times)
- Local winds: **short-lived, predictable, region-specific**; caused by local temperature and pressure differences and relief.
| Wind | Region | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **Foehn** | **Alps** (Switzerland) | Warm, dry, downslope; melts snow |
| **Chinook** | East slopes of the **Rockies** (USA, Canada) | Warm, dry "**snow eater**"; blows in **winter** and early spring, **raises winter temperature** of the plains; benefits grazing – brings **no rain** |
| **Zonda** | **Argentina** (east slope of the Andes) | Foehn-type, warm, dry |
| **Santa Ana** | Southern **California** | Hot, dry; spreads wildfires |
| **Samun (Simoom)** | **Kurdistan**, Iran–Iraq | Hot, dry desert wind |
| **Sirocco** | Sahara → Mediterranean (**Italy, Sicily**) | Hot, dusty; red dust causes "**blood rain**" in **Italy** |
| **Harmattan** | West Africa – blows **from the north-east and east to the west** of the Sahara | Dry, dusty; "**the doctor**" |
| **Khamsin** | **Egypt** | Hot, dry, dusty |
| **Haboob** | **Sudan** | Dust storm |
| **Shamal** | **Mesopotamia** – Iraq, Persian Gulf | Hot, dry, dusty |
| **Leveche** | Spain | Hot, dry |
| **Brickfielder** | South-east Australia | Hot, dusty |
| **Mistral** | **Rhone valley, France** – not Australia | **Cold**, dry |
| **Bora** | **Eastern shore of the Adriatic** (Croatia, Slovenia, NE Italy) – **not Siberia** | Cold, dry, down from the mountains |
| **Purga** | **Siberia** (tundra) | Cold, snow-laden |
| **Yamo** | **Japan** | Warm, dry |
| **Blizzard** | Polar lands, Siberia, Canada, USA – **not Chile** | Snowstorm with strong winds |
- **Cold** local winds: **Mistral**, Bora, Purga, Blizzard; Santa Ana, Chinook, Loo are hot/warm (NDA 2020).

=== Pressure Belts – Doldrums, Horse Latitudes, ITCZ
# The belts (asked 11 times)
| Belt | Latitude | Other name / nature |
|---|---|---|
| **Equatorial low** | 0–5° N and S, **on both sides of the Equator** | **Doldrums** – **calm**, rising air; NE and SE trades converge (**ITCZ**) |
| **Subtropical high** | **30–35° N and S** | **Horse latitudes** – calm, sinking dry air, hot deserts; **dynamically induced** |
| **Subpolar low** | **60–65°** | Westerlies meet polar easterlies; storms; **dynamically induced** |
| Polar high | Poles (90°) | Cold, dense, sinking air; thermally induced |
- **ITCZ** = low-pressure zone **at / near the Equator** where the **north-east and south-east trades converge** (APPSC Asst. Chemist; MPPCS 2018; CDS 2007) – not at the Tropics or poles.
- **Doldrums** = the **equatorial low-pressure belt** (NDA 2007) with calm winds – **not** strong winds (APPSC Group-1: "doldrums are strong winds" and "NH trades blow from NW" are both wrong → **neither**).
- **Horse latitudes** ~**30° (30–35°)** – sub-tropical high, **dynamically induced** by sinking air of the Hadley cell, calm (NDA 2012); sailors threw horses overboard when becalmed.
- **Hadley cell**: air rises at the ITCZ and **descends at the subtropical high-pressure cells** (~30°) (CDS 2020).
- Subpolar low at 60–65° instead of a high (IAS 2002): converging westerlies and polar easterlies; the lows are semi-permanent **over oceans** (Aleutian, Icelandic) – both true, but R does not explain A.
- Belts **shift 5–10°** with the overhead Sun – **north in the NH summer**, south in winter.
~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 05 p.5-7; NCERT Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.10; app notes 4:16 subs 2-3

=== Atmospheric Pressure – Causes, Gradient and Instruments
# Pressure and weather (asked 10 times)
- Atmospheric pressure = **weight of the air column**, held by **gravitational pull** (APPSC Panchayat Secretary).
- Heated air **expands → density falls → low pressure**; cooled air contracts → high pressure.
- Pressure **falls with altitude** → at high altitudes some people get **nose bleeding** (low pressure) (APPSC AE).
- **Standard sea-level pressure 1013.25 mb (hPa)**; **isobars** join places of equal pressure.
- **Pressure gradient** decides **wind velocity** (APPSC Group-II 2011): closely spaced isobars = steep gradient = strong wind. Air moves high → low.
- **Sudden fall of the barometer** (mercury) = **stormy weather** (UKPSC 2002; IAS 2001); rising = fair weather.
- A **low-pressure area** with converging winds = **cyclone**; high = anticyclone.
# Instruments
| Instrument | Measures |
|---|---|
| **Barometer** (mercury / aneroid), barograph | Atmospheric **pressure** (APPSC; NDA 2018) |
| **Aneroid barometer** | **No liquid** – an evacuated metal box |
| **Anemometer** | Wind **speed** |
| Wind vane | Wind **direction** |

=== Jet Streams and the Polar Vortex
# Jet streams (asked 8 times)
- Narrow bands of **high-velocity westerly winds** in the **upper troposphere near the tropopause** (8–15 km; generally **10–12 km**) (NDA 2011; CDS 2017).
- Found in **both hemispheres** – not the NH only (UPSC 2020); NH jets stronger.
- **Sub-tropical jet** forms at about **10–16 km** (option "10–20 km", APPSC AD 2017); polar front jet lower (6–9 km).
- **Polar front jet is strongest in winter**, not summer (CDS 2017) – greatest temperature contrast.
- Associated with the **monsoon** (APSLPRB SI 2023, 2018): they regulate onset and withdrawal.
| Jet | Position / season | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Sub-Tropical Westerly Jet** | **South of the Himalayas in winter**, north in summer | Brings **western disturbances**; leaves before the monsoon "burst"; returns ~October |
| **Tropical Easterly Jet** | Summer, 5–20°N (South China Sea → East Africa) | Strengthens the **SW monsoon** |
| Somali (Findlater) Jet | Low-level, off East Africa | Boosts monsoon flow |
- NCERT: in winter the westerly jet blows across Asia **north of the Himalayas, parallel to the Tibetan highlands**; **Tibet splits it into two branches**; the **southern branch** (south of the Himalayas) controls India's winter weather – not the northern branch → "1 and 2" (State PSC).
- First identified by **Wasaburo Ooishi** (Japan, 1920s); widely known in **World War II**.
# Polar vortex
- Cold **low** around the poles, **anticlockwise** (NH), strongest in **winter**. A **strong vortex keeps cold air up north** (APPSC 2018); when weakened (Rossby waves, sudden stratospheric warming) cold spells reach mid-latitudes.

=== Planetary Winds and Wind Controls
# Planetary winds (asked 7 times)
| Wind | From → to | NH | SH |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trade winds** (easterlies) | **Horse latitudes (subtropical high) → equatorial low** | **North-east** (blow towards the south-west) | South-east |
| **Westerlies** | Subtropical high → subpolar low (**~35–60°**) | South-west | North-west |
| Polar easterlies | Polar high → subpolar low | North-east | South-east |
- **Trade winds = planetary winds**; monsoon is periodic; Chinook and Mistral are local (IAS 2009).
- Order from Equator to pole: **doldrums → trade winds → westerlies → polar winds** (NDA 2014).
- Westerlies blow between about 35° and 60° in **each** hemisphere – not "30°N to 60°S"; the **western disturbances** that give NW India winter rain come with the westerlies (UPSC 2015 → only II).
- **NW European ports** stay ice-free all year because of the warm **North Atlantic Drift**; the south-westerlies do blow there all year, but are not the reason (UPPCS 2002: both true, R not the explanation).
- Trades push tropical cyclones **west onto east coasts**; westerlies turn them north-east.
# Wind controls (asked 4 times)
- Surface horizontal wind = **pressure gradient + friction + Coriolis force** (CDS 2015).
- **Coriolis force**: deflects **right in NH, left in SH** (**Ferrel's law**); **zero at the Equator, maximum at the poles**; grows with wind speed; depends on latitude, **not on hemisphere**.
- **Geostrophic wind**: upper air, parallel to isobars, when **pressure gradient force = Coriolis force** (UKPSC 2024).
- **SW monsoon** direction: SE trades crossing the Equator are deflected to the **right by the Coriolis force** (NDA 2011).
- **Friction** slows surface winds, strongest over land, negligible aloft. No cyclones within ~5° of the Equator (Coriolis negligible).
- **Walker circulation** = east–west Pacific cell; Hadley cell = meridional.
~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 05 p.11-14; NextGen Indian Geography 03 p.9 (Coriolis); NCERT Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.10; app notes 4:16 subs 1, 4

=== Roaring Forties, Furious Fifties, Shrieking Sixties
# The southern westerlies (asked 7 times)
| Name | Latitude |
|---|---|
| **Roaring Forties** | **40–50° S** (strong ocean westerlies, "40–60° S" option) |
| Furious Fifties | 50–60° S |
| Shrieking Sixties | Around 60° S |
- They are the **westerlies of the Southern Hemisphere** (MPPCS 2015; State PSC) – stronger and steadier because the SH has **less landmass** (open ocean, little friction) (IAS 2011 → only I; Coriolis is not higher in the SH).
- UPSC 2000: they blow with **great strength and constancy**, generally **from north-west to east (west to east)** in the SH, with **overcast skies, rain and raw weather** → **2, 3 and 4**; they do **not** blow uninterrupted in the NH (land breaks them).
- NDA 2018: strong westerlies of the SH oceans (true); they blow **west to east**, not east to west, and there is **no** abundance of landmasses → **only I**.
- Region: the southern oceans, incl. the **Indian Ocean** (of the options given).

=== Monsoon and Periodic Winds
# Monsoon (asked 6 times)
- Word from Arabic **"mausim" = season** (MPPCS 2013; APPSC ASO 2017).
- **Seasonal reversal of winds** due to **differential heating of land and sea** (State PSC).
- Summer: **high temperature and low pressure** over the subcontinent draw air from the Indian Ocean → **south-west monsoon** (UPSC 1996), flowing **from south-west to north-east** (MPPCS 2012).
- Modern theory (1950s): ITCZ shift + jet streams + Tibet Plateau + ENSO. Halley (1686): giant land–sea breeze.
- Mediterranean (UPPCS 1992 key "monsoonal"): in winter air from the surrounding high-pressure lands flows towards the warmer sea, reversing in summer; its winter rain itself comes with the westerlies.
- **El Niño** weakens the monsoon; **positive IOD** can offset it.
- AP: SW monsoon 2026 entered via **Rayalaseema** (6 June 2026); IMD expected **below-normal** AP rain under El Niño. AP coastline **~1,053 km – third longest** (AP Socio-Economic Survey 2025-26).
# Breezes
| Breeze | Time | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Sea breeze | Day | Sea → land (land heats faster, lower pressure) |
| **Land breeze** | **Night** | **Land → sea** |
| Valley (anabatic) | Day | Uphill |
| Mountain (katabatic) | Night | Downhill; cold air in valleys → inversion, frost |
- Land and sea breezes are **daily**; they moderate **AP coastal** temperatures.
