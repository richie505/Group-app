=== NOTES 4:15 | Insolation and Heat Budget; Distribution of Temperature, Inversion
## SUB 0 | Heating and Cooling of the Atmosphere
[15:0:0] TABLE head: Process | Mechanism
  [15:0:0.0] Insolation | Incoming solar radiation; direct heating by short waves
  [15:0:0.1] Conduction | Heats lower layers of air in contact with warm ground
  [15:0:0.2] Convection | Vertical transfer by rising warm air (troposphere)
  [15:0:0.3] Advection | Horizontal transfer by wind (e.g. the Loo)
  [15:0:0.4] Terrestrial radiation | Long-wave heat from the earth = main heater of air
[15:0:1] APPSC-G2 2023 match: Advection – horizontal; Convection – vertical; Insolation – direct; Conduction – lower layers.
[15:0:2] - Air is **heated from below**, not directly by the sun. Earth re-emits long-wave infra-red (8,000–25,000 nm), absorbed by CO₂ and water vapour.
[15:0:3] UPSC 2024: "Atmosphere is heated more by incoming solar radiation" = WRONG; "greenhouse gases absorb long waves" = right.
## SUB 1 | Insolation – Factors and Distribution
[15:1:0] - Factors: **angle of sun's rays**, **length of day**, **transparency** (clouds, dust, vapour), **earth-sun distance**, land-sea distribution, slope and aspect.
[15:1:1] - **Perihelion ~3 Jan, 147 million km**; **aphelion ~4 Jul, 152 million km**.
[15:1:2] - Equator gets direct rays; poles oblique rays. Insolation falls poleward; **higher over continents than oceans** at the same latitude.
[15:1:3] - **Maximum insolation and highest temperatures: subtropical deserts (20–30° N)**, not the equator – clear skies, dry air.
[15:1:4] - Heat **surplus in tropics**, deficit at poles; winds and currents carry heat poleward.
## SUB 2 | Albedo and Clouds
[15:2:0] - **Albedo** = share of incoming radiation a surface reflects.
[15:2:1] - Order: **fresh snow (highest, APPSC-G1 2019) > sand > grass > forest > water**.
[15:2:2] TABLE head: Surface | Albedo (approx.)
  [15:2:2.0] Fresh snow | 75–95%
  [15:2:2.1] Dry sand | 20–40%
  [15:2:2.2] Grass | 20–25%
  [15:2:2.3] Crop land | 15–25%
  [15:2:2.4] Water (sun high) | below 10%
[15:2:3] - **High, thin clouds trap heat** (warming); **low, thick clouds reflect sunlight** (cooling).
[15:2:4] - **Ice-albedo feedback** (positive): melting ice exposes darker ocean → more absorption. **Arctic** warms **3–4 times** the global average, at least 2°C (Apr 2025).
## SUB 3 | Earth's Heat Budget (100 Units)
[15:3:0] TABLE head: Of 100 units | Units
  [15:3:0.0] Reflected to space (clouds 27, scattering 6, snow/ice 2) = planetary albedo | 35
  [15:3:0.1] Absorbed by atmosphere | 14
  [15:3:0.2] Absorbed by surface | 51
[15:3:1] - Surface radiates all 51: **17 to space**, **34 to atmosphere** (6 direct, 9 convection/turbulence, 19 latent heat).
[15:3:2] - Atmosphere sends **48** (14 + 34) to space → balance; earth neither warms nor cools.
## SUB 4 | Greenhouse Effect
[15:4:0] - Atmosphere **transparent to short-wave** sunlight, **opaque to long-wave** terrestrial radiation; re-radiates part back.
[15:4:1] - GHGs **CO₂, CH₄, water vapour, N₂O** trap heat and raise temperature (APPSC-GS 2025).
[15:4:2] - **Without atmosphere: about −18°C** instead of **+15°C** (UPSC 2025).
[15:4:3] TABLE head: Gas | Share of effect | Source
  [15:4:3.0] CO₂ | ~60% | Fossil fuels, deforestation, cement
  [15:4:3.1] Methane ("marsh gas") | ~20% | Paddy, wetlands, livestock
  [15:4:3.2] CFCs | ~15% | Refrigeration, aerosols
  [15:4:3.3] N₂O | ~5% | Fertilisers
[15:4:4] - **Water vapour** = commonest GHG, a climate feedback.
[15:4:5] - **GWP (100 yr)**: CO₂ 1; methane **28–36**; N₂O **265–298**.
[15:4:6] - Global land temperature up **1.59°C** since pre-industrial (UNEP Emissions Gap 2025). Oceans took a record **+23 ZJ** in 2025 (~90% of excess heat).
## SUB 5 | Vertical Distribution
[15:5:0] - **Normal lapse rate 6.5°C per km**.
[15:5:1] - Why: ground heats air from below; higher air is **thinner, with less water vapour and dust**.
[15:5:2] - **Himalayas** are cool despite low latitude – **altitude**.
## SUB 6 | Horizontal Distribution – Isotherms and Thermal Equator
[15:6:0] - **Isotherms** join places of equal temperature; run east-west; **more regular in Southern Hemisphere** (mostly ocean).
[15:6:1] TABLE head: Month | Over land | Over oceans
  [15:6:1.0] January (NH) | Bend equatorward | Bend poleward (oceans warmer; land cools faster)
  [15:6:1.1] July | Bend poleward | Reverse
[15:6:2] - **Thermal equator**: line of highest mean temperature; **mostly north of the Equator** (more land); moves with the sun.
[15:6:3] - India, winter: **20°C isotherm** runs **Tapi mouth → Mahanadi delta**; temperature rises north to south.
[15:6:4] - **January 18°C isotherm** splits India into tropical (south) and sub-tropical (north).
[15:6:5] - Controls: latitude, altitude, sea (equable coasts), **ocean currents** (cold currents lower coast temperatures, help west-coast deserts), relief.
## SUB 7 | Range of Temperature
[15:7:0] - **Seasonal range maximum in high latitudes** (APPSC-G1 2023); largest annual range in **high-latitude continental interiors (Siberia)**; **minimum at the equator**.
[15:7:1] - **Interior** = larger annual and daily range (continentality); **coasts** = equable, small range.
[15:7:2] - **Cloudy nights warmer** – clouds send back Earth's heat (blanket); **no dew** on cloudy nights. **Clear nights cooler** – frost.
[15:7:3] - India: highest winter daily range in **NW India (14–18°C)**; highest daily range in **Rajasthan deserts**.
[15:7:4] - **Chennai**: annual mean 26°C, rainfall ~63 cm, annual range **9°C**.
[15:7:5] - AP: **Rayalaseema** high daily range; coast low (**Visakhapatnam, Kakinada** sea breezes).
## SUB 8 | Temperature Inversion
[15:8:0] - Temperature **rises with height** (normal lapse reversed).
[15:8:1] - Conditions: **long winter nights, clear skies, calm dry air, snow cover**.
[15:8:2] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [15:8:2.0] Ground (radiation) | Surface night cooling
  [15:8:2.1] Valley (air drainage) | Cold air drains into valleys → frost pockets on floor; slopes warmer → orchards on upper slopes
  [15:8:2.2] Permanent | Polar areas; stratosphere = permanent inversion layer
[15:8:3] - Effects: **fog, frost, smog**; pollutants trapped in the **planetary boundary layer**. North Indian winter mornings: inversion + moisture → fog.
[15:8:4] - **Frontal inversion:** forms along a front, where warm air rides over a wedge of cold air, so temperature rises with height across the frontal boundary.

=== NOTES 4:16 | Atmospheric Pressure – Pressure Belts; Planetary, Periodic and Local Winds; Jet Streams
## SUB 0 | Pressure – Basics and Instruments
[16:0:0] - Heated air **expands, becomes lighter → low pressure**; cooled air contracts → high pressure.
[16:0:1] - Pressure **falls with altitude**.
[16:0:2] - **Standard sea-level pressure: 1013.25 mb (hPa)**.
[16:0:3] - **Isobars** join places of equal pressure (reduced to sea level).
[16:0:4] - **Pressure gradient** = fall of pressure per unit distance; **sets wind speed** (APPSC-G2 2011). Air moves **high → low**.
[16:0:5] - **Closely spaced isobars** = steep gradient = **strong winds**.
[16:0:6] TABLE head: Barometer | Weather
  [16:0:6.0] Sudden / steady fall | Low pressure → cloud, rain, storm
  [16:0:6.1] Rising | Fair, settled
  [16:0:6.2] Instrument | Measures
  [16:0:6.3] Barometer (mercury, aneroid) / barograph | Pressure (mb, hPa)
  [16:0:6.4] Anemometer | Wind speed (km/h, knots)
  [16:0:6.5] Wind vane | Wind direction
## SUB 1 | Wind Controls – Coriolis, Friction, Geostrophic Wind
[16:1:0] - **Coriolis force**: due to Earth's rotation; deflects **right in NH, left in SH** (**Ferrel's law**, APPSC-G1 2019).
[16:1:1] - Coriolis is **zero at the equator, maximum at poles**; **grows with wind speed**.
[16:1:2] - **No cyclones within ~5°** of the equator (Coriolis negligible); tropical cyclones stay between 30°N and 30°S.
[16:1:3] - **Friction** slows surface winds, stronger over land; negligible aloft.
[16:1:4] - **Geostrophic wind**: upper air, **parallel to isobars**; **pressure gradient force = Coriolis force**, no friction.
[16:1:5] - Coriolis decides direction; friction affects speed.
## SUB 2 | Pressure Belts
[16:2:0] TABLE head: Belt | Latitude | Other name / nature
  [16:2:0.0] Equatorial low | 0–5° N and S | Doldrums, ITCZ; calm rising air; trades converge
  [16:2:0.1] Subtropical high | 30–35° N and S | Horse latitudes; calm, sinking dry air, hot deserts
  [16:2:0.2] Subpolar low | 60–65° | Westerlies meet polar easterlies; storms
  [16:2:0.3] Polar high | Poles (90°) | Cold, dense, sinking air
[16:2:1] - Belts **shift 5–10°** with the overhead sun (**north in NH summer**, south in winter).
[16:2:2] - **Horse latitudes** and ITCZ asked in APPSC 2019.
[16:2:3] APPSC-G1 2024: "Doldrums are strong winds" and "NH trades blow from NW" are both WRONG → **neither**. Doldrums = **calm**; NH trades = **north-east**.
## SUB 3 | Itcz and the Monsoon
[16:3:0] - **ITCZ**: low-pressure trough near the equator where **NE and SE trades meet**; shifts with the sun.
[16:3:1] - NH summer: ITCZ moves **north over the Indian plains** (monsoon trough) – this **helps** the monsoon (a result, not cause).
[16:3:2] - Winter: ITCZ moves **south** of India; highs over **Tibet and Siberia** → **north-east monsoon**; most of India dry under **NE trades**.
[16:3:3] - Southward shift of doldrums → heavy rain in southern states.
[16:3:4] - Winter **anticyclonic** conditions: high pressure, low temperature. **Western disturbances** give North India winter rain – they do not cause the anticyclone.
[16:3:5] - Himalayas = **climatic divide**, giving India its distinct monsoon climate.
## SUB 4 | Planetary Winds
[16:4:0] TABLE head: Wind | From → to | NH | SH
  [16:4:0.0] Trade winds (easterlies) | Subtropical high → equatorial low | NE | SE
  [16:4:0.1] Westerlies | Subtropical high → subpolar low (35–60°) | SW | NW
  [16:4:0.2] Polar easterlies | Polar high → subpolar low | NE | SE
[16:4:1] - Trade winds push tropical cyclones **west onto east coasts**; westerlies turn them **north-east** (Cyclone Montha, Kakinada, Nov 2025).
[16:4:2] - **Hadley cell**: rises at equator, sinks near **30°**, returns as trades (meridional). **Walker circulation**: **east–west** Pacific cell.
[16:4:3] - NW Europe ports open all year; SW winds blow there all year (true, but not the reason).
## SUB 5 | Roaring Forties, Furious Fifties, Shrieking Sixties
[16:5:0] TABLE head: Name | Latitude
  [16:5:0.0] Roaring Forties | 40–50° S
  [16:5:0.1] Furious Fifties | 50–60° S
  [16:5:0.2] Shrieking Sixties | Around 60° S
[16:5:1] - **SH westerlies** are stronger and steadier – open ocean, little land friction; NH westerlies less steady (landmasses).
[16:5:2] - Roaring Forties: strong, constant, blow west to east; over the **Indian Ocean**.
## SUB 6 | Jet Streams and Polar Vortex
[16:6:0] - **Jet streams**: narrow, fast upper winds at **10–12 km**; regulate monsoon onset and withdrawal.
[16:6:1] - **First identified by Wasaburo Ooishi (Japan, 1920s)**; widely known in **World War II** (bomber pilots).
[16:6:2] TABLE head: Jet | Position / season | Role
  [16:6:2.0] Sub-Tropical Westerly Jet | South of Himalayas in winter; north in summer | Carries western disturbances from the Mediterranean; leaves before monsoon "burst"; returns October
  [16:6:2.1] Tropical Easterly Jet | Summer, 5–20°N, June–early Oct (South China Sea → East Africa) | Upper anticyclone; strengthens SW monsoon
  [16:6:2.2] Somali (Findlater) Jet | Low-level, off East Africa | With Somali Current boosts monsoon
[16:6:3] - STWJ-carried western disturbances worsened 2026 monsoon extremes; may have fed flash floods in Nepal and Tibet (Aug 2026).
[16:6:4] - **El Niño** weakens the monsoon; **positive IOD** (warmer western Indian Ocean) can offset it. UK Met Office expects the **biggest El Niño in over a century** (Aug 2026).
[16:6:5] - **Polar vortex**: year-round **cold low** around the poles; **anticlockwise**; strongest in **winter**; stratospheric part 15–50 km.
[16:6:6] - **Strong vortex keeps cold air up north** (APPSC 2018). **Rossby waves / sudden stratospheric warming** weaken it → winds reverse, vortex splits → cold spells in mid-latitudes.
## SUB 7 | Periodic Winds – Breezes and Monsoon
[16:7:0] TABLE head: Breeze | Time | Direction
  [16:7:0.0] Sea breeze | Day | Sea → land (land heats faster, lower pressure)
  [16:7:0.1] Land breeze | Night | Land → sea
  [16:7:0.2] Valley (anabatic) | Day | Uphill
  [16:7:0.3] Mountain (katabatic) | Night | Downhill; cold air in valleys → inversion, frost
[16:7:1] - Land and sea breezes are **daily**; monsoon reverses **seasonally**. They moderate **AP coastal** temperatures.
[16:7:2] - **Monsoon**: from Arabic **"mausim" = season**; **seasonal reversal of winds** from **differential heating of land and sea**; summer sea → land, winter land → sea; clear wet and dry seasons.
[16:7:3] TABLE head: Theory | Year | Idea
  [16:7:3.0] Halley | 1686 | Giant land-sea breeze
  [16:7:3.1] Modern theory | 1957 | ITCZ shift + jet streams + Tibet Plateau + ENSO
[16:7:4] - **AP**: SW monsoon 2026 reached AP via **Rayalaseema on 6 June 2026** (two days late; normal 4 June). IMD expected **below-normal** AP rain under El Niño (Jun 2026).
[16:7:5] - AP coastline **1,053.07 km**, **third longest** in India (AP Socio-Economic Survey 2025-26).
