## SUB 1 | Widow Remarriage, Girls' Schools and Reform Bodies
[1:0] # Milestones
[1:1] TABLE head: Year | Step
  [1:1.0] 1874 | first girls' school in Andhra – Dowlaiswaram (Dhavaleswaram), near Rajahmundry; first principal Malladi Achchanna Sastri
  [1:1.1] 1878 | Rajahmundry Social Reform Association – first organised reform body in Andhra; for widow remarriage, against nautch girls
  [1:1.2] 3 Aug 1879 | lecture at Rajahmundry: widow remarriage valid by Hindu scripture
  [1:1.3] 1880 | Widow Remarriage Association (Stree Punarvivaha Sangham), with Challapalli Bapayya and Basavaraju Gavarraju
  [1:1.4] 11 Dec 1881 | first widow remarriage in Andhra, Rajahmundry
  [1:1.5] 15 Dec 1881 | second remarriage – Ratnamma and Racharla Ramachandraiah
  [1:1.6] 1881 | second girls' school, Innispeta, Rajahmundry
  [1:1.7] 1897 / 16 Jan 1905 | widow homes at Madras / Rajahmundry (Vitantu Saranalayam)
  [1:1.8] 1906 | Hitakarini Samajam, Rajahmundry – he gave it all his property; Veeresalingam its founder president
[1:2] - **First widow remarriage:** **11 December 1881**, at the **Ananda Udyana Mandapam** behind his house; groom **Gogulapati Sriramulu**, bride **Seethamma (Gauramma)**; money from **Paidah (Pyda) Ramakrishnayya**, Kakinada.
[1:3] - By 1892 he had held about **20** widow remarriages.
[1:4] - Bengal comparison: **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act 1856** (Act XV, **Lord Canning**); first Bengal remarriage **7 Dec 1856** (Vidyasagar).
[1:5] - **Hitakarini date:** **1906**, registered **2 May 1908** (Hans India); ⚠ NextGen gives 1905.
[1:6] - **Rajahmundry Social Reform Association:** founded **1878** (RAS 2021 stem says 1871 – still Veeresalingam); main aim = **widow remarriages**.
[1:7] - **Kakinada:** Paidah Ramakrishnayya, as municipal president, funded remarriages and built houses for the grooms.
[1:8] - **Opponents:** **Kokkonda Venkataratnam** (Andhra Bhasha Sanjeevini); **Vedam Venkataraya Sastri** – **Stree Punarvivaha Durvada Nirvaranam (1883)**, first major anti-remarriage book; **Kasibhatta Brahmayya Sastri** – **Aryananda Brundavani Sabha**, Kakinada; A.P. Parthasarathi Naidu also opposed him – **Uma Ranganayakulu** was his ally (general knowledge, not checked for Parthasarathi Naidu).
[1:9] - **Mrs Manning** (UK) willed **£50** to his widow home.
[1:10] - Also first in Andhra: a **night school**, a **Theosophical High School**, **co-education**.
[1:11] - **Evils fought:** ban on widow remarriage, child marriage, **dowry, bride-price**, caste, superstition, **nautch/Bhogam**, **official corruption**; scientific education was an aim, not an evil.
[1:12] - **Methods:** journals and satire, lectures, schools, remarriages at his own cost, widow homes, prayer without idols – the **press, not direct politics**.
[1:13] > 1874 girls' school (Dowlaiswaram) → 1878 Social Reform Association → 1880 Widow Remarriage Association → 11 Dec 1881 first remarriage → 1906 Hitakarini.

## SUB 4 | Brahmo and Prarthana Samaj in Andhra
[4:0] - **First Prarthana (Brahmo) Samaj in Andhra:** **Rajahmundry, 1878**, by **Kandukuri Veeresalingam** – not Sivanatha Sastri, Venkataratnam Naidu or Mokkapati Subbarayudu.
[4:1] - Founded **under the influence of Mannava Butchaiah Pantulu** – the **first Andhra Brahmo**, editor of the **Hindu Reformer**.
[4:2] - **Founder of the Brahmo Samaj in (united) Andhra = Veeresalingam**; **R. Venkataratnam Naidu spread it**.
[4:3] - **Brahmo Trinity (Brahmatraya):** **Veeresalingam, Raghupathi Venkataratnam Naidu, Desiraju Peda Bapayya** – not Chilakamarthi or Mannava.
[4:4] - **Desiraju Peda Bapayya (Bapatla):** first practising (**Anushthana**) Brahmo; helped Veeresalingam become an Anushthana Brahmo (giving up the sacred thread); paper **Voice of Truth**; **Young Men's Prayer Union**, **Temperance and Social Purity Association**, Rajahmundry.
[4:5] - **Chembeti Sridharalu Naidu** (Madras): renamed the Veda Samaj the **Dakshina Bharata Brahmo Samaj**; **translated Brahmo books into Tamil and Telugu**.
[4:6] - **Centres:** Rajahmundry (Veeresalingam), **Kakinada** (Venkataratnam's Brahma Mandir), **Pithapuram** (Raja Surya Rao, patron) – "all are correct".
[4:7] TABLE head: Year | Samaj | Founder
  [4:7.0] 1878 | Prarthana Samaj, Rajahmundry | Veeresalingam
  [4:7.1] 1882 | Brahmo Samaj, Machilipatnam | R. Venkataratnam Naidu
  [4:7.2] 1887 | Brahmo Samaj (Brahmo Mandir), Rajahmundry | –
  [4:7.3] 1888 | Kakinada Samaj | Tayi Surya Prakasarao Naidu
  [4:7.4] 1898 | Guntur Samaj | Pillarisetty Sitaramayya
[4:8] - **K. Seethamma** organised its women's wing.
[4:9] - Prarthana Samaj did **not** reject idol worship or caste outright; the Brahmo Samaj did.
[4:10] - **Pithapuram Raja** gave **Rs 1 lakh** to the Brahma Dharma Prachara Nidhi – to train preachers and translate Bengali Brahmo books.
[4:11] - Decline: splits after Venkataratnam Naidu joined the **Justice Party**.

## SUB 5 | Raghupathi Venkataratnam Naidu – Social Purity and Anti-Nautch
[5:0] - **Titles:** **Brahmarshi** (1932), Kulapati, Abhinava Socrates, **Knighthood (1924)** – first non-official educator knighted; the Telugu "Uttama Purusha".
[5:1] - **Papers:** **Brahma Prakasika**, Fellow Worker, People's Friend.
[5:2] - **Social Purity Association (Samajika Shuddhi Samajam):** **1891**, Bandar (Machilipatnam) – against **Bhogam melas** (nautch).
[5:3] - **Bhogam (devadasi) caste** disappeared from Andhra society mainly through **his** efforts – not Veeresalingam, Gurajada or Unnava.
[5:4] - **Career:** Noble College lecturer; principal, **Mahbub College, Secunderabad** (Hyderabad) and **Pithapuram Raja's College, Kakinada (1905-18)**; **Vice-Chancellor, Madras University (1919-22)** – first Telugu VC.
[5:5] - **Brahmo work:** inspired by **Pandit Sivanath Sastri**; Brahmo Samaj Machilipatnam (1882); **Brahma Mandir at Kakinada**; condemned idol worship.
[5:6] - Kept away from the **national movement**; did not join the Theosophical (Divyajnana) Society.
[5:7] > Venkataratnam Naidu = Brahmarshi; Social Purity Association 1891; anti-Bhogam; VC Madras.

## SUB 6 | Gurajada, Gidugu, Tripuraneni and the Language Movement
[6:0] # Gurajada Appa Rao (1861-1915)
[6:1] - **Kanyasulkam** – social play on **bride-price** and child marriage, in spoken (vyavaharika) Telugu, **Srikakulam dialect**; written 1890, **first staged 13 Aug 1892** (Jagannatha Vilasa Nataka Sangham, Vizianagaram; NextGen: 1893), **published 1897** (Vavilla), 2nd edition 1909 dedicated to **Ananda Gajapati**.
[6:2] - Characters: **Madhuravani** – reformed courtesan; **Girisham** – educated fraud; Ramappa Pantulu – hypocrite; Buchamma – widow; **Soujanya Rao** – reformer.
[6:3] - Kanyasulkam is NOT by Kommaraju Lakshmana Rao, Veeresalingam or Panuganti.
[6:4] - Other works: **Mutyala Saralu** (1910, new metre), **Diddubatu** (first modern Telugu short story), **Purnamma** (girl married off for money), Kanyaka; Saranga Dhara (1883) – English poem with brother Shyamala Rao.
[6:5] - Song **Desamunu Preminchumanna** – "Desamante matti kadoyi, desamante manushuloyi" (a country is its people, not its soil).
[6:6] - Patron court: **Vizianagaram** (Ananda Gajapati); titles Navayuga Vaitalikudu.
[6:7] # Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy (1863-1940)
[6:8] - Born **29 Aug 1863, Ponduru (Srikakulam)**; Telugu teacher at **Parlakimidi**; **Telugu Language Day = 29 August** (his birthday).
[6:9] - **Father of the Vyavaharika (spoken Telugu) movement**, against the Sanskritised **granthika** style; title Vyavaharika Bhashodyama Pitamaha; **Kaisar-e-Hind (1934)**.
[6:10] - **Savara:** script, Savara-Telugu dictionaries, **Savara grammar/manual in English (1931)**, Savara-English dictionary (1936).
[6:11] - Works: **A Memorandum on Modern Telugu**, Bala Kavi Saranyam; monthly **Telugu (1919)**.
[6:12] - **Vartamana Vyavaharikandhra Bhasha Parivartaka Samajam:** **founded by Veeresalingam** (president), **Gidugu secretary** – not founded by Gidugu.
[6:13] - Vyavaharika Bhashodyama Samstha (1910, after a Visakhapatnam meeting); inspired by **J.A. Yates**.
[6:14] TABLE head: Side | Leaders
