## SUB 0 | Origin of Andhra Social Reform
[195:0:0] - Began in **Rajahmundry** (Godavari district, today East Godavari) = Andhra's **first Renaissance centre**; led by the **English-educated middle class**.
[195:0:1] - **Atmuri Lakshmi Narasimham** – first guru of the early Andhra reform movement.
[195:0:2] - Peak **1870-1935**; main centres **Rajahmundry and Kakinada**; then Machilipatnam, Bapatla, Guntur.
[195:0:3] - Rajahmundry = centre of Telugu social reform, **modern prose, journalism and the novel** (19th century).
[195:0:4] - Cradle of the **language movement** = **North Coastal Andhra** (Vizianagaram, Srikakulam, Parlakimidi).

## SUB 1 | Kandukuri Veeresalingam (1848-1919)
[195:1:0] # Life
[195:1:1] - Born **1848**, Rajahmundry; died **27 May 1919**, Madras. Wife **Rajyalakshmamma (Bapamma)**, married 1860.
[195:1:2] - Method: the **press, not direct political activity**.
[195:1:3] - **Career:** first a teacher at **Rajahmundry** (1871); then head teacher at **Korangi** (1872) and **Dowlaiswaram**.

## SUB 2 | Widow Remarriage
[195:2:0] - **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856** (Act XV) – Vidyasagar's campaign; passed under **Lord Canning**; first Bengal remarriage **7 Dec 1856**.
[195:2:1] - **First Andhra remarriage**: **11 Dec 1881**, Rajahmundry, **Ananda Udyana Mandapam** behind his house; groom **Gogulapati Sriramulu**, bride **Seethamma** (Gauramma).
[195:2:2] - **Widow Remarriage Association (1880)**: with **Challapalli Bapayya** and **Basavaraju Gavarraju**; **Mrs Manning** willed 50 pounds to it.
[195:2:3] - **Kakinada**: **Paidah Ramakrishnayya** (municipal president) funded remarriages and built houses for grooms → Kakinada Widow Remarriage Association.
[195:2:4] - Opponents: **Vedam Venkataraya Sastri** – **Stree Punarvivaha Durvada Nirvaranam (1883)**, first major anti-remarriage book; **Kasibhatta Brahmayya Sastri** – **Aryananda Brundavani Sabha**, Kakinada.
[195:2:5] - Related laws: **1829** Bentinck – Sati Regulation; **1872** – Native Marriage Act; **1891** Lansdowne – Age of Consent Act.
[195:2:6] # Social Evils and Methods
[195:2:7] - Evils fought: ban on widow remarriage, **child marriage, dowry, caste, bride-price**, superstition, **nautch/Bhogam**, **official corruption**. Scientific education was an aim, not an evil.
[195:2:8] - Methods: journals and satire, lectures, schools and co-education, remarriages at his own cost, widow homes, Prarthana Samaj worship without idols.

## SUB 5 | Brahmo and Prarthana Samaj in Andhra
[195:5:0] - Parent: **Brahmo Samaj**, Raja Ram Mohan Roy, **1828**, Calcutta (Atmiya Sabha 1814; sati ban 1829).
[195:5:1] - Madras root: **Veda Samajam** → renamed **Dakshina Bharata Brahmo Samaj** by **Chembeti Sridharulu Naidu**, who translated Brahmo texts into **Tamil and Telugu**.
[195:5:2] - Rajahmundry Prarthana Samaj founded under the influence of **Mannava Buchchayya Pantulu**.
[195:5:3] - **Hindu Reformer:** edited by **Mannava Buchchayya Pantulu**, pioneer Andhra Brahmo, to promote **social reform**.
[195:5:4] TABLE head: Year | Samaj | Founder
  [195:5:4.0] 1878 | Prarthana Samaj, Rajahmundry (first in Andhra) | Veeresalingam
  [195:5:4.1] 1882 | Brahmo Samaj, Machilipatnam | R. Venkataratnam Naidu
  [195:5:4.2] 1887 | Brahmo Samaj, Rajahmundry | –
  [195:5:4.3] 1888 | Kakinada Samaj | Tayi Surya Prakasarao Naidu
  [195:5:4.4] 1898 | Guntur Samaj | Pillarisetty Sitaramayya
[195:5:5] - Order: **Rajahmundry (1878) → Machilipatnam (1882) → Kakinada (1888) → Guntur (1898)**.
[195:5:6] - Founder of the Brahmo Samaj in (united) Andhra: **Veeresalingam**, with **Venkataratnam Naidu** who spread it.
[195:5:7] - **Brahmo Trinity**: Veeresalingam, Venkataratnam Naidu, **Desiraju Pedda Bapayya**.
[195:5:8] - Prarthana Samaj did **not** reject idol worship or caste outright; Brahmo Samaj did.
[195:5:9] - Decline: splits after Venkataratnam Naidu joined the **Justice Party**.
[195:5:10] - **Pithapuram Raja** gave **Rs 1 lakh** to the Brahma Dharma Prachara Nidhi to train preachers and **translate Bengali Brahmo books**.
[195:5:11] 1878 = Prarthana Samaj, Rajahmundry; 1887 = Brahmo Samaj, Rajahmundry.
## SUB 6 | Raghupathi Venkataratnam Naidu (1862-1939)
[195:6:0] - Joined the Brahmo Samaj inspired by **Pandit Sivanath Sastri**; centre **Kakinada** (Brahma Mandir); took reform to **North Coastal Andhra**.
[195:6:1] - **Social Purity Association** (Samajika Shuddhi Samajam), **1891**, against **Bhogam melas**.
[195:6:2] - Led the **anti-nautch (Bhogam / Devadasi)** campaign (Madras) – main force behind ending the **Bhogam caste** custom (APPSC 2019).
[195:6:3] - Journals: **Brahma Prakasika**, Fellow Worker, People's Friend.
[195:6:4] - Offices: Principal, **Pithapuram Raja's College, Kakinada (1905-18)**; **first Telugu Vice-Chancellor of Madras University**.
[195:6:5] - Titles: **Brahmarshi** (1932, from students); **knighthood 1924** (first non-official educator knighted); Rao Bahadur, Diwan Bahadur, Kulapati, Abhinava Socrates.
[195:6:6] # Desiraju Pedda Bapayya (Bapatla)
[195:6:7] - **First practising (Anushtanika) Brahmo**; dropped the sacred thread; helped Veeresalingam become an Anushthana Brahma.
[195:6:8] - Founded **Temperance and Social Purity Sangham, Rajahmundry** (not Veeresalingam) → boycott of Bhogam melas.
[195:6:9] Veeresalingam | Prarthana Samaj | widow remarriage
Venkataratnam Naidu | Brahmo Samaj | anti-nautch, social purity
## SUB 7 | Gurajada Apparao (1861-1915)
[195:7:0] - Worked alongside Veeresalingam in the **literary and cultural renaissance**; with Gidugu, champion of **vyavaharika** Telugu; patron court **Vizianagaram** (Ananda Gajapati).
[195:7:1] TABLE head: Work | Genre | Theme / fact
  [195:7:1.0] Kanyasulkam | Social play in spoken Telugu | Bride-price, child marriage, plight of widows
  [195:7:1.1] Mutyala Saralu | Poetry (1910) | Introduced a new metre of the same name
  [195:7:1.2] Diddubatu | Short story | First modern Telugu short story
  [195:7:1.3] Purnamma | Poem | Girl married off for money
  [195:7:1.4] Kanyaka | Poem | –
  [195:7:1.5] Saranga Dhara (1883) | English poem with brother Shyamala Rao | In Indian Leisure Hour; not a Telugu novel
[195:7:2] - Other works: Kalinga Desa Charitra, Neelagiri Paatalu.
[195:7:3] # Kanyasulkam
[195:7:4] - **Written 1890 → first staged 13 Aug 1892** (Jagannatha Vilasa Nataka Sangham, Vizianagaram) **→ published 1897** (Vavilla) **→ 2nd edn 1909**, dedicated to **Ananda Gajapati**.
[195:7:5] - First **vyavaharika play**; **Srikakulam dialect**.
[195:7:6] - Characters: **Madhuravani** – reformed courtesan; **Girisham** – educated fraud; **Ramappa Pantulu** – hypocrite; **Buchamma** – widow; **Soujanya Rao** – reformer.
[195:7:7] APPSC-GS 2024: "Kanyasulkam written by Kommaraju Lakshmana Rao" – **false**; "a satirical play on social evils" – **true**.
[195:7:8] # Famous Line
[195:7:9] - **"Desamante matti kadoyi, desamante manushuloyi"** – a country is its people, not its soil.
[195:7:10] - From the patriotic song **Desamunu Preminchumanna** (Desabhakti section of **Mutyala Saralu**); the **APPSC 2025 key** accepted **Kanyaka** as the book.
## SUB 8 | Gidugu Ramamurthy and the Vyavaharika Movement
[195:8:0] # Gidugu (1863-1940)
[195:8:1] - Born **29 Aug 1863, Ponduru (Srikakulam)**; died 22 Jan 1940; Telugu teacher at **Parlakimidi**.
[195:8:2] - **Savara** (Ganjam Agency): created a script, **Savara-Telugu dictionaries**, **A Manual of Savara Language**.
[195:8:3] - Works: **A Memorandum of Modern Telugu Prose**, Bala Kavi Saranyam.
[195:8:4] - Titles: **Vyavaharika Bhashodyama Pitamaha**, Abhinava Vaganushasanudu, **Rao Saheb**, **Kaisar-e-Hind (1934)**.
[195:8:5] - **Telugu Language Day = 29 August** (his birthday). Telugu got **classical language** status in **2008**.
[195:8:6] # Granthika vs Vyavaharika
[195:8:7] TABLE head: Feature | Granthika | Vyavaharika
  [195:8:7.0] Nature | Sanskritised classical | Spoken Telugu
  [195:8:7.1] Aim | Only "pure" literary medium | Close gap between written and spoken
  [195:8:7.2] Supporters | Kokkonda Venkataratnam, Jayanti Ramaiah Pantulu (Andhra Sahitya Parishad and its magazine), orthodox pandits | Gidugu, Gurajada, Tapi Dharma Rao, Navya Sahitya Parishat
[195:8:8] - **J.A. Yates** inspired the movement.
[195:8:9] - Bodies: **Vyavaharika Bhashodyama Samstha (1910)** after a Visakhapatnam meeting; **Varthamana Vyavaharikandhra Bhasha Parivartaka Samaj** – president **Veeresalingam**, secretary **Gidugu** (not founded by Gidugu).
[195:8:10] - 1912: Gidugu defended modern Telugu after a Telugu Pandits' conference at Madras.
[195:8:11] - **Madras University controversy:** in **April 1913** the University set up a **Telugu Composition Committee** (ten members, incl. **P.T. Srinivasa Iyengar**) to fix the style of Telugu composition for the **Intermediate** course.
[195:8:12] - **Jayanti Ramayya Pantulu** – **A Defence of Literary Telugu (1913)**; urged the University not to back the "new school".
[195:8:13] - Outcome: the Sub-Committee majority (**Vedam Venkataraya Sastri**, K.V. Lakshmana Rao) kept the **literary (classical) dialect**; **Gurajada** wrote a **Minute of Dissent (1914)** for the polite spoken dialect.
[195:8:14] - Grammars: Malladi Suryanarayana Sastri, Vadlamudi Gopalakrishnayya, Vajjala Venkateswarlu.
[195:8:15] - Outcome: Andhra Mahasabha endorsed colloquial Telugu.
[195:8:16] Andhra Sahitya Parishad = **classical** side, not Gidugu's.
