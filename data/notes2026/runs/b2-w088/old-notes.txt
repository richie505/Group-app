## SUB 4 | E-governance – Meaning, Models and Types of Interaction
[88:4:0] # Meaning and significance
[88:4:1] - E-governance = use of ICT to deliver government services, information and transactions; aim **"faceless, paperless, cashless"**.
[88:4:2] - Significance: **citizen engagement**, better **service delivery**, fewer face-to-face contacts, **audit trails** (e-governance **strengthens** them – "removal of audit trails" is not a benefit).
[88:4:3] # Arie Halachmi – five models
[88:4:4] TABLE head: Model | Meaning
  [88:4:4.0] Broadcasting / wider dissemination | Government information put in the public domain
  [88:4:4.1] Critical flow | Critical information to a targeted audience (e.g. corruption data → ombudsman)
  [88:4:4.2] Comparative analysis | Learning from best practices elsewhere
  [88:4:4.3] E-advocacy / mobilisation and lobbying | Online civil-society networks influence decisions
  [88:4:4.4] Interactive service | Two-way G2C services (filing, payments, feedback)
[88:4:5] # Types of interaction
[88:4:6] TABLE head: Type | Meaning | Examples
  [88:4:6.0] G2C | Government to Citizen | Online certificates; UMANG, MeeSeva, eSeva
  [88:4:6.1] G2B | Government to Business | MCA portal, GSTN, e-Procurement
  [88:4:6.2] G2G | Between departments/governments | NIC solutions, SWAN, e-Office
  [88:4:6.3] G2E | Government to Employees | HR and administrative tools
[88:4:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 3 p.18-19; app's current notes 2:88 sub 0-2
[88:4:8] - **First State to adopt an e-governance policy:** **Maharashtra** (APPSC key).
## SUB 5 | NeGP, e-Kranti and Digital India
[88:5:0] # NeGP (2006)
[88:5:1] - Formulated by **DeitY and DARPG**; approved **18 May 2006** with **27 Mission Mode Projects** and 10 components.
[88:5:2] - **2011**: 4 MMPs added – **Health, Education, PDS, Posts** → **31**.
[88:5:3] - Core infrastructure: **SWAN**, **State Data Centres**, **CSCs** (UMANG is not core NeGP infrastructure).
[88:5:4] - MMPs: **Central, State, Integrated**.
[88:5:5] # e-Kranti (NeGP 2.0)
[88:5:6] - Approved by the Union Cabinet **25 March 2015**; a **pillar of Digital India**; vision **"Transforming e-Governance for Transforming Governance"**; **44 MMPs** = **15 Central, 17 State, 12 Integrated**.
[88:5:7] # Digital India
[88:5:8] - Launched **1 July 2015**; ministry **MeitY**; **nine pillars**: Broadband Highways; Universal Access to Mobile Connectivity; Public Internet Access Programme; e-Governance; **e-Kranti**; Information for All; Electronics Manufacturing; IT for Jobs; Early Harvest Programmes (a satellite launch programme is not a pillar).
[88:5:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 3 (src/science-tech/nextgen-st-group2-03.txt) p.18-19 (NeGP 27 MMPs, 18 May 2006, SWAN, CSC); PMF Lens 2026-05-02 p.5 (NeGP 2006, DARPG); app's current notes 2:88 sub 3-4

=== NOTES 2:89 | Citizen-centric Administration – Citizens' Charter, Sevottam, Grievance Redress (cpgrams), Social Audit
## SUB 0 | Social Audit – MGNREGA and AP's SSAAT
[89:0:0] - **MGNREGA 2005, Section 17**: the **Gram Sabha** conducts the **social audit** of all works – the process used to **enhance accountability** in MGNREGA (not financial, political or legal audit).
[89:0:1] - **AP SSAAT** (**Society for Social Audit, Accountability and Transparency**): set up **2009** as an independent society; looks after social audit of Panchayat Raj / rural development programmes in AP (not SERP, Streenidhi or DWACRA).
[89:0:2] - **CAG** found that the Social Audit Unit **lacks functional independence**.
[89:0:3] - **Village Social Auditors**: short training of about **two days** at the mandal level before the audit.
[89:0:4] - **AP pioneered State-wide MGNREGA social audits**; **Meghalaya (2017)** passed the first law making social audit of government programmes mandatory.
[89:0:5] - **Second ARC (12th Report)**: social audits to be **institutionalised in all major welfare schemes**.
[89:0:6] > Angles: MGNREGA accountability process (×2), who conducts it (Gram Sabha), SSAAT, CAG finding, training days.

## SUB 2 | Citizens' Charter and Ombudsman
[89:2:0] # Citizens' Charter
[89:2:1] - A **written commitment** on service standards; main purpose = **time-bound service delivery and grievance redress**.
[89:2:2] - Origin **UK, 1991** (John Major); India adopted **1997**; **DARPG** coordinates; charters are **non-justiciable**.
[89:2:3] - **Six principles**: quality, choice, standards, value, accountability, transparency (**participation is not** one).
[89:2:4] - **Basic elements (India)**: vision and mission; services provided and their standards; information on how to obtain them; **grievance redress**; **expectations from citizens** – **inspection of the agency's work is NOT** an element.
[89:2:5] - **Citizens Charter Bill, 2011** lapsed with the Lok Sabha in **2014**; **Second ARC** wanted statutory backing.
[89:2:6] # Ombudsman
[89:2:7] - Ombudsman = **Sweden, 1809**; banks, insurance and tax departments use an **Ombudsman** as the alternate grievance redress system for complaints (Banking/Insurance/Tax Ombudsman).
[89:2:8] - Classic ombudsman = **maladministration**; Lokpal/Lokayukta = **corruption** (Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act **2013**); terms coined by **L.M. Singhvi**; first Lokayukta **Maharashtra 1971**.
[89:2:9] - **First ARC** (1966-70, **Morarji Desai**): recommended a **Lokpal at the Union** and **Lokayuktas in the States**.
[89:2:10] ~~ Sources: app's current notes 2:89 sub 0-1, 8
[89:2:11] - **Filtering** (communication barrier): the sender **manipulates information so that the receiver sees it more favourably**.
## SUB 3 | Right to Services Acts
[89:3:0] - Idea grew out of the **Citizens' Charter** in **Great Britain**.
[89:3:1] - First law: **Madhya Pradesh Lok Sewa Guarantee Act, 2010**; **Bihar** 2011; among Punjab, Assam, Gujarat, Maharashtra the earliest is **Punjab**.
[89:3:2] - Features: notified services in a **fixed time**, appeals, **penalties**; they do **not** abolish other grievance mechanisms.
[89:3:3] - **AP Public Services Delivery Guarantee Act, 2017** – in force **18 Oct 2017**.
[89:3:4] - **Project Saksham** (railways) = time-bound skill upgradation of railway employees (a CA item, not a Right to Services law).
[89:3:5] ~~ Sources: app's current notes 2:89 sub 4
## SUB 4 | Sevottam, CPGRAMS and the Second ARC 12th Report
[89:4:0] # Sevottam
[89:4:1] - **Seva + Uttam**; suggested in the **Second ARC's 12th Report**; promoted by **DARPG** (framework for improving service delivery); from **2009**.
[89:4:2] - **Three modules**: Citizens' Charter; Public Grievance Redress; Service Delivery Capability; certification **IS 15700:2005**.
[89:4:3] # CPGRAMS
[89:4:4] - **Centralised Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System**; launched **2007** by **DARPG** (Ministry of **Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions**, not Home Affairs); 24×7 via web, app and **UMANG**.
[89:4:5] - DARPG circular **23 Aug 2024**: maximum disposal time **30 → 21 days**; **GRAI**: 11 indicators, 4 dimensions (efficiency, feedback, domain, organisational commitment); pensioners: **CPENGRAMS**.
[89:4:6] # Second ARC 12th Report (2009)
[89:4:7] - **"Citizen Centric Administration – The Heart of Governance"**; Second ARC (2005), chairman **Veerappa Moily**; recommended Sevottam, statutory charters, social audits, right to public services laws.
[89:4:8] - **Punchhi Commission (2010)** volume on good governance argued for treating good governance / corruption-free delivery of services as a **right** of citizens.

## SUB 1 | Preamble, Objectives and Nature of the Right
[90:1:0] - Preamble terms: **transparency of information**, **revelation of information**, **informed citizenry**, **paramountcy of the democratic ideal** (all four).
[90:1:1] - Objectives: transparency and accountability, **answerability**, **containing corruption**, empowering citizens, making democracy work for the people – **NOT** "empowering women and weaker sections".
[90:1:2] - RTI **mandates a timely response** to citizens' requests for government information (not NeGP, Aadhaar or RTE).
[90:1:3] - Nature: a **statutory (legal) right** under the 2005 Act; the **right to know** is also read into **Article 19(1)(a)** (State of U.P. v. Raj Narain, 1975) – hence "both" a legal and a fundamental right in most keys.
[90:1:4] - **Namit Sharma v. Union of India (2012)** – RTI case on Information Commissions.
[90:1:5] - Impact: raised the **sense of responsibility in bureaucracy**, but "miles to go" (both true, R does not explain A); the Act is **misused by both** applicants and public authorities.
[90:1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Polity Part 1 p.54 (legal right; Namit Sharma 2012); app's current notes 2:90 sub 1, 11
[90:1:7] - **Union of India v. Association for Democratic Reforms (2002):** SC held that the **right to information** (voters' right to know candidates' antecedents) is a **fundamental right under Art. 19(1)(a)**.

## SUB 0 | Money Laundering, Benami Property and Offshore Leaks
[92:0:0] # PMLA 2002
[92:0:1] - **Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002**: India's principal anti-money-laundering law; **in force 1 July 2005** (with its Rules).
[92:0:2] - **Section 3:** money laundering = concealing, possessing, acquiring, using, projecting or claiming **"proceeds of crime"** as untainted property; punishment **3 to 7 years** RI (up to 10 in some cases) + fine.
[92:0:3] - **Director of the Enforcement Directorate** can **attach property** believed to be proceeds of crime (APPSC Group 1).
[92:0:4] - Machinery: **Adjudicating Authority**, **Appellate Tribunal** and **Special Courts** for the trial of money-laundering offences.
[92:0:5] - **Schedule:** **Part A** = listed offences (IPC/BNS, NDPS, etc.) with no money limit; Part B = Customs false declaration; **Part C = offences with cross-border implications** (so "Part A = trans-border crimes" is **wrong**).
[92:0:6] - **"Proceeds of crime" definition (Sec 2(1)(u)) amended by the Finance Act, 2015** (w.e.f. 14 May 2015): added property taken or held **outside the country** → equivalent value within India (APPSC AEE 2016); widened again in **2018** and **2019** (Explanation).
[92:0:7] - **Vijay Madanlal Choudhary (2022):** SC upheld the main PMLA provisions; ED works under the **Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance** (ED set up 1956).

[93:0:3] # Capital account convertibility
[93:0:4] - **Meaning:** freedom to convert rupees into any major currency (and back) for **trading financial assets** – investment, loans, portfolio flows (UPSC 1998). Conversion for trade in goods and services = **current account** convertibility.
[93:0:5] - **Current account:** fully convertible since **August 1994** (IMF Article VIII obligation).
[93:0:6] - **Capital account:** never fully convertible; India allows **partial** CAC with limits (e.g. individuals up to **$2,50,000 a year** under LRS).
[93:0:7] - **Tarapore I (1997) preconditions:** fiscal consolidation – Centre's gross fiscal deficit cut from **4.5% to 3.5% of GDP** over three years (by 1999-2000); a mandated inflation target; a strong financial system; a phased move over three years from 1997-98.
[93:0:8] - **Capital account convertibility was not part of the 1991 LPG package.**
[93:0:9] > Recall angle: "Tarapore" → capital account convertibility (asked as "framework for full CAC", "fuller CAC" and "3.5% fiscal deficit precondition").

## SUB 1 | The 1991 Crisis and the New Economic Policy
[93:1:0] - **Trigger:** **flight of NRI deposits**; forex reserves fell to about **$975 million (June 1991)** = about **two weeks of imports**.
[93:1:1] - **Gold:** **67 tonnes** pledged with the **Bank of England and UBS**.
[93:1:2] - **IMF:** approached for about **$7 billion**, with **structural-adjustment conditions**.
[93:1:3] - **Other 1991 numbers:** fiscal deficit about **9.4%** of GDP, inflation about **12%**, GDP growth only about **1.3%**.
[93:1:4] - **NEP (LPG) announced:** the New Industrial Policy on **24 July 1991** – so economic reforms were formally introduced in **July 1991**.
[93:1:5] - **Leaders:** PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**, FM **Dr. Manmohan Singh** (UPPSC 2013); the earlier Janata (1977), Indira (1980) and Rajiv (1985) governments did not launch it.
[93:1:6] - **Components:** **Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation**, with **disinvestment** as the main form of privatisation; **nationalisation** is the opposite of the NEP (APPSC 2018).

=== NOTES 2:94 | Impact of Lpg on the Role of the State – Provider to Regulator and Enabler
## SUB 0 | The 1991 Shift – Provider to Regulator and Enabler
[94:0:0] - **Trigger:** 1991 crisis + LPG (PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**, FM **Dr. Manmohan Singh**): **state-controlled → market-oriented** economy.
[94:0:1] - **Socialism** = a **state-controlled economy** in which the State owns and runs production (NDA 2010); not "liquidation of the bourgeoisie" or military rule.
[94:0:2] - **Government to Governance:** the State became **facilitator, regulator and catalyst**; it did **not fully withdraw**. Privatisation asks the government to move from "**owner-manager**" to "**controller / regulator**" and concentrate on education, administration and infrastructure.
[94:0:3] # Models of the State
[94:0:4] TABLE head: Model | Role | Indian marker
  [94:0:4.0] Welfare / provider | Owns and runs production; "commanding heights" | IPR 1956
  [94:0:4.1] Regulatory | Sets rules; independent regulators watch markets | SEBI, TRAI, CERC, IRDA
  [94:0:4.2] Facilitator / enabler | Delivery through private or civil-society actors | PPPs, DBT, GeM, UMANG
[94:0:5] - **RBI** after 1991: regulator → **facilitator**; freer bank decisions, entry of private and foreign banks; still regulates.
[94:0:6] - **Housing:** State moved from provider → enabler of private real estate (22 Apr 2026).
[94:0:7] - **AP – Deepam-2:** 3 free LPG cylinders a year to BPL families via **DBT** = provider → enabler (Jan 2026).

  [94:3:0.0] Market failure → planning-era State | Public goods, externalities, monopoly, information asymmetry
  [94:3:0.1] Government failure → 1991 | PSU low efficiency, losses, political interference, no autonomy, labour problems, project delays
[94:3:1] - **Public choice theory (James Buchanan)** = basis of the government-failure case.
[94:3:2] - **Critique:** PSU assets **undervalued**; proceeds plugged revenue gaps; agri public investment fell; social spending curbed; overlap (TRAI vs MIB on TV ratings).
[94:3:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Part 8.2 p.9-11 (PSU problems; arguments for and against privatisation); app notes 2:94 (sub 4)
## SUB 4 | Washington Consensus and Minimum Government
[94:4:0] - **John Williamson, 1989**; IMF + World Bank + US Treasury (**not WTO**).
[94:4:1] - Prescriptions include fiscal discipline, reordering public spending, tax reform, **market-determined interest rates**, competitive exchange rate, trade and FDI liberalisation, privatisation, deregulation, property rights.
[94:4:2] - **India 1991:** interest deregulation (**Narasimham**), tax cuts (**Chelliah**), market-determined exchange rate (**1993**).
[94:4:3] - **Financial liberalisation:** in the Washington Consensus, **market-determined interest rates** are the financial-liberalisation prescription; a **competitive exchange rate** is a separate exchange-rate prescription.
[94:4:4] # Minimum Government, Maximum Governance
[94:4:5] - Slogan from **2014**: a leaner State that governs better through **rules, technology, accountability**.
[94:4:6] - E-governance cuts discretion and middlemen: **Aadhaar, DBT, GeM**, UMANG (not Navratna status); RTI Act 2005.
[94:4:7] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:94 (sub 3, 5); Rocket Indian Economy p.95 (Narasimham, Chelliah)
