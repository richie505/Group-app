# Capital account convertibility
- **Meaning:** freedom to convert rupees into any major currency (and back) for **trading financial assets** – investment, loans, portfolio flows (UPSC 1998). Conversion for trade in goods and services = **current account** convertibility.
- **Current account:** fully convertible since **August 1994** (IMF Article VIII obligation).
- **Capital account:** never fully convertible; India allows **partial** CAC with limits (e.g. individuals up to **$2,50,000 a year** under LRS).
- **Tarapore I (1997) preconditions:** fiscal consolidation – Centre's gross fiscal deficit cut from **4.5% to 3.5% of GDP** over three years (by 1999-2000); a mandated inflation target; a strong financial system; a phased move over three years from 1997-98.
- **Capital account convertibility was not part of the 1991 LPG package.**
> Recall angle: "Tarapore" → capital account convertibility (asked as "framework for full CAC", "fuller CAC" and "3.5% fiscal deficit precondition").

=== The 1991 Crisis and the New Economic Policy
- **Trigger:** **flight of NRI deposits**; forex reserves fell to about **$975 million (June 1991)** = about **two weeks of imports**.
- **Gold:** **67 tonnes** pledged with the **Bank of England and UBS**.
- **IMF:** approached for about **$7 billion**, with **structural-adjustment conditions**.
- **Other 1991 numbers:** fiscal deficit about **9.4%** of GDP, inflation about **12%**, GDP growth only about **1.3%**.
- **NEP (LPG) announced:** the New Industrial Policy on **24 July 1991** – so economic reforms were formally introduced in **July 1991**.
- **Leaders:** PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**, FM **Dr. Manmohan Singh** (UPPSC 2013); the earlier Janata (1977), Indira (1980) and Rajiv (1985) governments did not launch it.
- **Components:** **Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation**, with **disinvestment** as the main form of privatisation; **nationalisation** is the opposite of the NEP (APPSC 2018).
# Stabilisation vs structural adjustment
| Aspect | Stabilisation | Structural adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Time frame | Short-term, demand side | Medium/long-term, supply side |
| Aim | BoP, inflation, fiscal deficit | Efficiency, competitiveness |
| Measures | **Devaluation**, deficit cuts | **Delicensing**, trade, financial, tax, PSU reforms |
> Trap: devaluation = stabilisation; delicensing = structural adjustment.

=== 1991 Measures – Delicensing, De-reservation, FDI and the Rupee
| Measure | Part of 1991 LPG? |
|---|---|
| Industrial de-licensing | Yes – licensing first kept for 18 industries, now only **5** (alcohol, cigarettes, hazardous chemicals, industrial explosives, electronic aerospace and defence; drugs since delicensed) |
| Public-sector reservation | Cut **17 → 8 → 3** (atomic energy, railways, specified minerals); now only **atomic energy and railway operations** |
| FDI automatic route | Yes – up to **51% in 34 priority industries**; later up to **100%** in hotels, infrastructure, software |
| PSU autonomy | **MoUs**; Navratnas (1997) |
| Current account convertibility | Yes, **1994** |
| Capital account convertibility | **No**; never fully adopted (Tarapore) |
| FERA → FEMA | **1999**, not 1991 |
| Foreign Investment Promotion Board | Set up to promote FDI |
- **Defence opened to the private sector:** **May 2001** (Press Note 4 of 2001, 21 May 2001) – Indian private firms allowed to make **arms and ammunition** (100% Indian private, FDI up to 26%), under licence (APPSC G2 Mains).
- **Rupee:** devalued about **18-20% on 1 and 3 July 1991**; LERMS dual rate 1992, unified **market-determined** rate from **March 1993** – today the rupee's rate is **market determined** (managed float), not pegged to the dollar, the euro or a basket.
- **Financial sector:** RBI's role moved from **regulator to facilitator**; private Indian and foreign banks allowed.

=== LPG Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Liberalisation | Removing controls: licences, quotas, entry curbs; freedom to fix prices, expand, import |
| Privatisation | Government leaves ownership/management – withdrawal or **outright sale** of PSUs; narrow sense = private ownership in a PSU, broad sense = bigger role for the private sector |
| Disinvestment | Sale of **part of PSU equity** to the public; above **51%** private stake transfers control |
| Globalisation | Joining the world economy; the **outcome** (not precondition) of L and P |
- **Navratnas:** nine PSUs given greater autonomy in **1997** (NextGen NCERT summary says 1996 – the scheme was launched July 1997); **Miniratnas: 97**; Maharatna from 2010.
- **New PSE Policy for Atmanirbhar Bharat (2021):** favours **strategic disinvestment**.
- **Private sector** = engine of growth after 1991 (before: size of the public sector measured "socialisation").

=== Industrial Policies, Licence Raj and MRTP
| Year | Thrust |
|---|---|
| 1948 IPR | Mixed economy; four categories (6 April 1948, S.P. Mukherjee) |
| 1956 IPR | Schedule A (**17** public), B (**12** mixed), C (private); "economic constitution of India" |
| 1977 IPS (Janata) | Small/cottage industries; **District Industries Centres** |
| 1980 IPS | Technology upgrading, export orientation, backward areas; MRTP firms 25% expansion |
| 1991 IPS | De-licensing, MRTP restructured, FDI, PSU autonomy via MoUs; did **not** expand licensing |
- **MRTP Act 1969** (Dutt Committee recommended it); relaxed 1980; in 1991 asset limits and pre-entry curbs on expansion, mergers and acquisitions **abolished**.
- Replaced by the **Competition Act, 2002 (CCI)**.

=== Trade Reforms, Sequencing and the Washington Consensus
- Import licensing abolished except hazardous/environmentally sensitive items; peak customs duty cut from about **130% to 30%**.
| Generation | Content |
|---|---|
| First (1991-2000) | Trade and industrial delicensing; tariffs ~125% → ~30%; FDI; financial reform |
| Second (2000s) | Factor markets (labour, land, power), fiscal rules, disinvestment, governance |
- **Washington Consensus:** **John Williamson, 1989**; 10 prescriptions (fiscal discipline, tax reform, trade and FDI liberalisation, privatisation, deregulation, property rights).
- **Foreign Trade Policy 2023:** announced 31 March 2023, in force from **1 April 2023**; unlike the earlier five-year EXIM/trade policies it has **no end date** and is updated when needed; target **$2 trillion** exports by 2030.
> Trap: factor-market reforms are **second-generation**; globalisation is the outcome, not the first step.
