## SUB 0 | The 1991 Shift – Provider to Regulator and Enabler
[0:0] - **Trigger:** 1991 crisis + LPG (PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**, FM **Dr. Manmohan Singh**): **state-controlled → market-oriented** economy.
[0:1] - **Socialism** = a **state-controlled economy** in which the State owns and runs production (NDA 2010); not "liquidation of the bourgeoisie" or military rule.
[0:2] - **Government to Governance:** the State became **facilitator, regulator and catalyst**; it did **not fully withdraw**. Privatisation asks the government to move from "**owner-manager**" to "**controller / regulator**" and concentrate on education, administration and infrastructure.
[0:3] # Models of the State
[0:4] TABLE head: Model | Role | Indian marker
  [0:4.0] Welfare / provider | Owns and runs production; "commanding heights" | IPR 1956
  [0:4.1] Regulatory | Sets rules; independent regulators watch markets | SEBI, TRAI, CERC, IRDA
  [0:4.2] Facilitator / enabler | Delivery through private or civil-society actors | PPPs, DBT, GeM, UMANG
[0:5] - **RBI** after 1991: regulator → **facilitator**; freer bank decisions, entry of private and foreign banks; still regulates.
[0:6] - **Housing:** State moved from provider → enabler of private real estate (22 Apr 2026).
[0:7] - **AP – Deepam-2:** 3 free LPG cylinders a year to BPL families via **DBT** = provider → enabler (Jan 2026).

## SUB 1 | Rolling Back the State
[1:0] - Licensing abolished except alcohol, cigarettes, hazardous chemicals, industrial explosives, electronic aerospace and defence (drugs since delicensed).
[1:1] - **Public-sector reserved list:** **17 (Schedule A, 1956) → 8 (1991) → 3 → now 2**: **atomic energy and railway operations**.
[1:2] - **Defence production opened to private sector in 2001** (May 2001); last SSI items de-reserved **2015**.
[1:3] - **PSE autonomy:** **MoUs** and **Navratna / Maharatna** status.
[1:4] - **Disinvestment** began **1991-92** (target **Rs 2,500 crore**): first to bridge the fiscal deficit, later for efficiency.
[1:5] - **LIC:** SEBI deadline **16 May 2027** for 10% minimum public shareholding (4 Aug 2026).
[1:6] - Ending **MRTP asset limits** removed curbs on concentration, entry, expansion, mergers and acquisitions.

## SUB 3 | Market Failure vs Government Failure
[3:0] TABLE head: Case | Basis
  [3:0.0] Market failure → planning-era State | Public goods, externalities, monopoly, information asymmetry
  [3:0.1] Government failure → 1991 | PSU low efficiency, losses, political interference, no autonomy, labour problems, project delays
[3:1] - **Public choice theory (James Buchanan)** = basis of the government-failure case.
[3:2] - **Critique:** PSU assets **undervalued**; proceeds plugged revenue gaps; agri public investment fell; social spending curbed; overlap (TRAI vs MIB on TV ratings).
[3:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Part 8.2 p.9-11 (PSU problems; arguments for and against privatisation); app notes 2:94 (sub 4)
## SUB 4 | Washington Consensus and Minimum Government
[4:0] - **John Williamson, 1989**; IMF + World Bank + US Treasury (**not WTO**).
[4:1] - Prescriptions include fiscal discipline, reordering public spending, tax reform, **market-determined interest rates**, competitive exchange rate, trade and FDI liberalisation, privatisation, deregulation, property rights.
[4:2] - **India 1991:** interest deregulation (**Narasimham**), tax cuts (**Chelliah**), market-determined exchange rate (**1993**).
[4:3] - **Financial liberalisation:** in the Washington Consensus, **market-determined interest rates** are the financial-liberalisation prescription; a **competitive exchange rate** is a separate exchange-rate prescription.
[4:4] # Minimum Government, Maximum Governance
[4:5] - Slogan from **2014**: a leaner State that governs better through **rules, technology, accountability**.
[4:6] - E-governance cuts discretion and middlemen: **Aadhaar, DBT, GeM**, UMANG (not Navratna status); RTI Act 2005.
[4:7] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:94 (sub 3, 5); Rocket Indian Economy p.95 (Narasimham, Chelliah)
