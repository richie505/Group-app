## SUB 0 | Money Laundering, Benami Property and Offshore Leaks
[0:0] # PMLA 2002
[0:1] - **Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002**: India's principal anti-money-laundering law; **in force 1 July 2005** (with its Rules).
[0:2] - **Section 3:** money laundering = concealing, possessing, acquiring, using, projecting or claiming **"proceeds of crime"** as untainted property; punishment **3 to 7 years** RI (up to 10 in some cases) + fine.
[0:3] - **Director of the Enforcement Directorate** can **attach property** believed to be proceeds of crime (APPSC Group 1).
[0:4] - Machinery: **Adjudicating Authority**, **Appellate Tribunal** and **Special Courts** for the trial of money-laundering offences.
[0:5] - **Schedule:** **Part A** = listed offences (IPC/BNS, NDPS, etc.) with no money limit; Part B = Customs false declaration; **Part C = offences with cross-border implications** (so "Part A = trans-border crimes" is **wrong**).
[0:6] - **"Proceeds of crime" definition (Sec 2(1)(u)) amended by the Finance Act, 2015** (w.e.f. 14 May 2015): added property taken or held **outside the country** → equivalent value within India (APPSC AEE 2016); widened again in **2018** and **2019** (Explanation).
[0:7] - **Vijay Madanlal Choudhary (2022):** SC upheld the main PMLA provisions; ED works under the **Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance** (ED set up 1956).
[0:8] # Benami and fugitive offenders
[0:9] TABLE head: Law | Key point
  [0:9.0] Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Act, 1988 | Sec 3(2): up to 3 years' imprisonment, or fine, or both for entering into a benami transaction
  [0:9.1] Benami (Amendment) Act, 2016 | Prohibition of Benami Property Transactions Act; bans proxy holdings; confiscation + RI 1 to 7 years (not ten)
  [0:9.2] Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018 | Warrant + offence of ₹100 crore or more + flight from India; Special Court (on ED application) may confiscate even before conviction; e.g. Vijay Mallya, Nirav Modi
[0:10] - **Ganpati Dealcom (23 Aug 2022):** SC held **Sec 3(2)** (3-year punishment) **unconstitutional** and the 2016 Act **prospective** (APSLPRB SI 2023). **⚠ Recalled in Oct 2024** when the SC allowed the Union's review petition.
[0:11] # Offshore leaks
[0:12] - **Paradise Papers (5 Nov 2017, ICIJ):** leaked records mainly of offshore law firm **Appleby**; **714 Indians** named. (Panama Papers = the earlier 2016 leak.)

## SUB 3 | Prevention of Corruption Act and Sanction for Prosecution
[3:0] - **PCA 1988**: consolidated anti-bribery law after the **Santhanam Committee**'s push.
[3:1] - **Public servant (Sec 2(c))**: any government or **local-authority** employee, any **Judge**, anyone whose office requires a **public duty** – not only permanent Union employees.
[3:2] # PC (Amendment) Act 2018
[3:3] TABLE head: Change | Detail
  [3:3.0] Purpose | Align with UNCAC
  [3:3.1] Bribe-giving | Made a direct offence, up to 7 years; a coerced bribe-giver is protected if he reports within 7 days (not 30)
  [3:3.2] Commercial organisations | Liable for bribes offered on their behalf; offence framed around "undue advantage"
  [3:3.3] Punishment | Bribe-taking public servant: 3 to 7 years + fine
  [3:3.4] Trial | Within 2 years, extendable to max 4 years
[3:4] # Sanction stages
[3:5] TABLE head: Stage | Provision
  [3:5.0] Before inquiry/investigation | Sec 17A PCA – government approval (official decisions)
  [3:5.1] Before court takes cognizance | Sec 19 PCA – prior sanction; covers retired public servants since 2018
  [3:5.2] General protection | Sec 218 BNSS (earlier Sec 197 CrPC; BNSS from 1 July 2024)
[3:6] - **Subramanian Swamy v Manmohan Singh (31 Jan 2012)**: SC upheld a **private citizen's right to seek sanction** for prosecuting a public servant (A. Raja, 2G) and faulted delay by the PMO (APPSC JAO/AE).
[3:7] - **Vineet Narain (1997)** (Single Directive) and **Subramanian Swamy v Director CBI (2014)** (Sec 6A DSPE) struck down prior-sanction rules.
[3:8] - **CPIL v UoI (Jan 2026)**: **split verdict** on 17A – **Justice Nagarathna** struck it down (Art. 14); **Justice Viswanathan** upheld it with approval vested in an independent body (**Lokpal/Lokayukta**); pending before a larger Bench.

## SUB 4 | Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014
[4:0] - **Whistle-blower**: **any public servant or other person, including an NGO**, making a **public interest disclosure** on corruption, wilful misuse of power or a criminal offence to the **Competent Authority** (CVC by default).
[4:1] TABLE head: Complained against | Competent authority
  [4:1.0] Central employees | CVC
  [4:1.1] State employees | State Vigilance Commission
  [4:1.2] Union Minister | Prime Minister
  [4:1.3] State Minister | Chief Minister
  [4:1.4] MPs | Rajya Sabha Chairman / Lok Sabha Speaker
  [4:1.5] MLAs | Presiding officer of the House
[4:2] - **Identity** kept **confidential**; protection against **victimisation**; **no anonymous** complaints; complaint within **7 years**.
[4:3] - Penalties: revealing identity = up to **3 years + ₹50,000**; knowingly false complaint = up to **2 years + ₹30,000**.
[4:4] - **SPG** excluded. Bill passed the Lok Sabha in 2011; **Act of 2014**, still **not brought into force** (CVC uses the 2004 PIDPI resolution).
[4:5] - **Amendment Bill 2015** (Lok Sabha, May 2015) would bar disclosures in **RTI Sec 8(1)-type** categories; **lapsed in 2019** with the 16th Lok Sabha.
[4:6] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. Part 2 p.262; PMF Polity Part 4 p.24 (Act not in force); PMF LENS Jul 2026 Part 1 p.17; app notes 2:92 sub 3

## SUB 6 | Lokpal and Lokayuktas
[6:0] - **Lokpal (2013 Act)**: **Chairperson + up to 8 members**; **50% judicial**; at least 50% from **SC, ST, OBC, minorities, women**.
[6:1] - **Selection committee**: **PM** (chair), **Speaker**, **LoP**, **CJI** or nominated SC judge, an **eminent jurist** (not the Cabinet Secretary).
[6:2] - **First Lokpal**: **Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose** (March 2019); **Justice A.M. Khanwilkar** chairperson from **Feb 2024**.
[6:3] - The Act obliged every State to set up a **Lokayukta within one year**.
[6:4] - **AP Lokayukta and Upa-Lokayukta Act, 1983**; Lokayukta = **higher functionaries**, Upa-Lokayukta = the rest; **2024 amendment**: retired HC CJ or Judge, chosen by a committee **chaired by the CM**, term **5 years**.
[6:5] - **Aug 2026**: **Justice Tallapragada Mallikarjuna Rao** selected and sworn in (25 Aug) as AP Lokayukta; oath by Governor **S. Abdul Nazeer**.
[6:6] ~~ Sources: Rocket Polity Part 1 p.5 (Lokpal); Praveen CA-360 Aug 2026 p.37 (AP Lokayukta); app notes 2:92 sub 5
