## SUB 0 | Turbines, Generators and Dynamos
[66:0:0] - **Wind turbine chain:** **kinetic energy** of wind → **mechanical** (rotation) → **electrical** energy in the generator.
[66:0:1] - **Generator / dynamo:** converts **mechanical energy into electrical energy** (asked ×5); a dynamo gives DC through a commutator.
[66:0:2] - Turbines plus dynamos convert **mechanical** energy, not chemical, solar or magnetic energy, into electricity.
[66:0:3] - Reverse device: **electric motor** = electrical → mechanical; **transformer** only steps voltage up or down.
[66:0:4] - Rotating generators give **AC**; stationary solar panels give **DC**.
[66:0:5] - Power varies with the **cube of wind speed**; **Betz limit 16/27 (~59.3%)**.
[66:0:6] TABLE head: Feature | HAWT | VAWT
  [66:0:6.0] Axis | Parallel to the wind | Vertical
  [66:0:6.1] Designs | 3-blade, yaw control | Darrieus, Savonius
  [66:0:6.2] Efficiency | Higher – standard utility turbine | Lower; takes wind from any direction
[66:0:7] - **CUF** = actual output ÷ output at full rated power; wind **36-40%**, solar ~21%.
[66:0:8] - **IEC classes:** Class I high-wind, **Class III low-wind** sites.
[66:0:9] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment Part 7 p.73 (rotating → AC); app notes 5:66 sub 0

## SUB 7 | Offshore Wind Policy
[66:7:0] - **National Offshore Wind Energy Policy, 2015:** farms in India's **EEZ**; nodal ministry **MNRE**, nodal agency **NIWE**.
[66:7:1] - Potential ~140 GW by 2050: ~36 GW off **Gujarat**, ~35 GW off **Tamil Nadu** (Gulf of Mannar).
[66:7:2] - **VGF scheme (2024): ₹7,453 crore** for the first **1 GW** – 500 MW each off **Gujarat and Tamil Nadu**.
[66:7:3] - Partners: **UK** Offshore Wind Taskforce (Feb 2026); Denmark.

## SUB 8 | Repowering and Other Support
[66:8:0] - **Repowering** = replacing ageing turbines with **larger, modern turbines at the same site**.
[66:8:1] - **National Repowering Policy, 2016** → **Repowering and Life Extension Policy, 2023**.
[66:8:2] - Supports: accelerated depreciation, **GBI**, competitive bidding, **Wind RPO trajectory to 2030**.
[66:8:3] > Order: Offshore 2015 → Repowering 2016 → Hybrid 2018 → Repowering and Life Extension 2023 → Offshore VGF 2024.

## SUB 0 | Biogas and Biomass Energy
[67:0:0] # Biomass energy
[67:0:1] - **Biomass energy** = solar energy stored by plants through **photosynthesis**; burned directly or converted to biogas/biofuels; renewable (unlike coal, natural gas, petroleum).
[67:0:2] - **Gobar gas = biomass energy** (from cattle dung) – not nuclear, geothermal or hydro.
[67:0:3] - Biomass from waste: agricultural waste (stubble, husk, manure, dairy waste), **food-processing residues, human sewage, biogenic municipal solid waste** (all three are biomass).
[67:0:4] - Burning biomass releases CO2 largely balanced by the CO2 it absorbed while growing (near carbon-neutral).
[67:0:5] # Biogas
[67:0:6] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [67:0:6.0] Composition | Methane ~50–75% + CO2 ~25–50% + traces (H2S, moisture); piped natural gas is 85–95% methane
  [67:0:6.1] Fuel gas | Methane – the burning component (cooking gas / gobar gas); not butane, propane, ethane
  [67:0:6.2] Process | Anaerobic digestion (biomethanation) / fermentation – in the absence of oxygen
  [67:0:6.3] Microbes | Methanogens (archaebacteria) produce methane
  [67:0:6.4] By-product | Slurry = good organic manure
[67:0:7] - **Composting** is **aerobic** decomposition, unlike biogas.
[67:0:8] - Pioneer: floating-drum **"Gramalakshmi"** plant of **Jashbhai Patel (1950s)**, spread by **KVIC**.
[67:0:9] - **CBG (Compressed Biogas)** = purified, compressed biogas, chemically identical to **CNG**.
[67:0:10] - **India's first Bio-CNG plant from agricultural residue: Pirangut, Pune** (Primove Engineering), inaugurated **2016** by Nitin Gadkari and Manohar Parrikar.
[67:0:11] - **Bagasse cogeneration:** sugar mills burn bagasse → **steam and power together**.
[67:0:12] # Gasification and microbial fuel cells
[67:0:13] - **Biomass gasification:** heating with **limited (controlled) oxygen** → syngas (**CO, H2, CO2**).
[67:0:14] - **Producer gas** = CO + H2 + CO2 + **methane**.
[67:0:15] - **Microbial Fuel Cell (MFC):** microbes act as **catalysts**, oxidise organic matter and pass electrons to an electrode; cleans wastewater and makes electricity together (no burning).

## SUB 2 | Geothermal Energy
[67:2:0] - **Source:** heat stored in the **Earth's interior**, produced mostly by **decay of radioactive** elements (uranium, potassium) and original planetary heat; surface signs: **hot springs, geysers, volcanoes**.
[67:2:1] - Not ultimately solar: **geothermal and nuclear** energy (biomass, wind, fossil fuels are stored solar energy).
[67:2:2] - Gives **24x7 baseload** power – independent of sunlight or wind.

=== NOTES 5:68 | Green Hydrogen – National Green Hydrogen Mission, Electrolysers, Applications
## SUB 0 | Hydrogen as a Fuel – Basic Facts
[68:0:0] - **Cleanest fuel:** burning hydrogen gives **only water** – least / minimum air pollution (coal, kerosene, diesel, petrol give CO2 and harmful gases; CNG and power alcohol still give CO2).
[68:0:1] - **Highest fuel (calorific) value:** about **150,000 kJ/kg** – above natural gas, gasoline, charcoal.
[68:0:2] - Burns in air at about **4% to 75%** by volume.
[68:0:3] - **Rocket fuel:** liquid hydrogen (**dihydrogen**, H2) with liquid oxygen in cryogenic stages – not hydrogen peroxide, heavy water or CFCs.
[68:0:4] - **Storage for vehicles:** hydrogen absorbed at low temperature by a **metal hydride** (e.g. palladium hydride) and released by exhaust heat.
[68:0:5] - Hydrogen is also the **most abundant element in the Universe** (~3/4 of matter) and the fuel of the Sun's fusion reactions.
[68:0:6] > Least pollution / highest fuel value / burning gives water – the classic hydrogen-fuel PYQ.
[68:0:7] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 10th ed. p.354 (Ch22.10 fuel cells); app notes 5:68 sub 0, sub 6
## SUB 1 | Applications of Green Hydrogen
[68:1:0] - **Uses of green hydrogen (UPSC 2023 – all three correct):** **directly as a fuel in internal combustion** engines (H2-ICE), **blended with natural gas** for heat or power, and in **hydrogen fuel cells** to run vehicles.
[68:1:1] - **Decarbonises fertiliser plants (ammonia), oil refineries and steel plants** (UPSC 2023 – all three) – **hard-to-abate** sectors where direct electrification is difficult.
[68:1:2] - **Industry:** refining (**desulphurisation**), **ammonia for fertilisers**, methanol, **steel by DRI** (hydrogen replaces **coking coal**).
[68:1:3] - **Fuel uses:** fuel-cell vehicles (buses, trucks, trains, ships), rocket fuel, **H-CNG** (hydrogen blended with CNG).
[68:1:4] - **Green ammonia** = nitrogen + green hydrogen; fertiliser, fuel, marine fuel.
[68:1:5] - **India's first green hydrogen blending project:** **NTPC + Gujarat Gas Ltd**, **Kawas, Surat** (Jan 2023), blending into the PNG network; PNGRB allowed 5% blending at start, scaling to 20%.
[68:1:6] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 10th ed. p.355 (Ch22.10.1); PMF Environment Part 7 p.101 (UPSC 2023 solved); web: pib.gov.in PRID=1888334 (NTPC–Gujarat Gas blending, 3 Jan 2023); app notes 5:68 sub 7

## SUB 4 | Fuel Cells
[68:4:0] - **Fuel cell** = electrochemical device: **H2 + O2 → electricity (DC) + water + heat**; no combustion; efficiency up to ~55%.
[68:4:1] - With pure hydrogen the only by-products are **heat and water**; FCEV exhaust = **water vapour** (and warm air) – not ammonia, nitrogen, oxygen or hydrogen peroxide.
[68:4:2] - Fuel cells power buildings **and** small devices (laptops); they give **DC, not AC** (UPSC 2015: only statement 1 correct).
[68:4:3] - A fuel cell combines hydrogen with **oxygen**, not carbon dioxide.
[68:4:4] # PEM fuel cell steps
[68:4:5] - 1. **Anode:** H2 → 2H+ + 2e-; electrons flow through the external circuit.
[68:4:6] - 2. **Protons cross the membrane**; **cathode:** 1/2 O2 + 2H+ + 2e- → H2O + heat.
[68:4:7] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 10th ed. p.354 (Ch22.10); app notes 5:68 sub 5

=== NOTES 5:69 | Energy Storage – Battery Technologies, Pli for Acc, Grid Storage
## SUB 0 | Cells and Batteries – Basics and Redox
[69:0:0] # How a cell works
[69:0:1] - **Cell = chemical energy → electrical energy** (spontaneous **redox** reaction; a galvanic cell); not a motor, generator, heater or fan.
[69:0:2] - **Battery:** **two or more cells** joined by opposite terminals (series); everyday word for any cell pack.
[69:0:3] - **Oxidation = loss of electrons** (at the **anode**); **reduction = gain of electrons** (at the **cathode**) during discharge.
[69:0:4] - **Electrolyte** lets **ions** flow between electrodes and keeps them apart.
[69:0:5] - A dry cell works on a **spontaneous oxidation–reduction** reaction; **electrolysis** is the reverse (needs outside energy).
[69:0:6] - **Charging needs DC:** AC is **not preferable to charge a storage battery** (a rectifier converts AC → DC); AC is fine for motors, toasters and transmission.
[69:0:7] - **Battery acid** (dilute H2SO4) has a pH **near 0–1** – the most acidic of pure water, saline water, milk.
[69:0:8] > Recall angle (RAS 2013, RRB 2018, SSC 2017 ×2, APPSC 2018): device converting chemical → electrical = **cell / battery**; oxidation = **loss of electrons**.
[69:0:9] ~~ Sources: Rocket Physics p.33, p.40; Rocket Chemistry p.21; PMF S&T Part 8 p.61; app notes 5:69 sub 0

## SUB 2 | Lithium-ion and New Battery Chemistries
[69:2:0] - **Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2019:** **development of lithium-ion batteries** (Goodenough, Whittingham, Yoshino) – APPSC JL 2020.
[69:2:1] - **Li-ion:** Li⁺ ions move **anode → cathode on discharge**; **anode = graphite**; cathode = lithium metal oxide (**LiCoO2, LiFePO4/LFP, NMC**); lithium salt in organic solvent; efficiency ~90–95%.
[69:2:2] - **Mobile phone batteries:** **lithium** (CDS 2015) – not copper, zinc, nickel.
[69:2:3] - **EV cathodes (UPSC 2025 ×2):** **cobalt, lithium, nickel** = cathode; **graphite = anode** → **only three**.
[69:2:4] - **ISRO Li-ion technology:** ISRO developed space-grade **lithium-ion cells** and offered the technology for transfer to industry (APPSC 2017 question).
[69:2:5] TABLE head: Chemistry | Key point
  [69:2:5.0] LFP (Li-ion) | Safe, long life
  [69:2:5.1] Sodium-ion | Abundant, cheaper, no cobalt/nickel, safer; lower energy density than Li-ion/LFP; grid storage
  [69:2:5.2] Solid-state | Solid electrolyte; higher density, safer; emerging EVs
  [69:2:5.3] Flow (vanadium redox) | Liquid electrolytes in external tanks; energy and power scale separately; grid-scale, long duration

## SUB 0 | Lighting Technologies – LED, CFL, Fluorescent, Sodium, Filament
[70:0:0] TABLE head: Lamp | Features
  [70:0:0.0] LED | Highest efficacy, longest life, no mercury; semiconductor emitter (gallium arsenide family) – the most energy-saving lamp
  [70:0:0.1] CFL (Compact Fluorescent Lamp) | Mercury vapour + phosphor; ~75% less energy than bulbs; shorter life and less efficient than LED
  [70:0:0.2] Fluorescent tube | Low-pressure mercury vapour + argon (sometimes neon, krypton, xenon)
  [70:0:0.3] Incandescent / GLS filament | Cheapest for the same wattage; only ~10–15% of input radiated as light, rest heat; power factor = unity (purely resistive)
  [70:0:0.4] Sodium vapour | Yellow, monochromatic light that does not split in fog/water droplets → street lights
  [70:0:0.5] Mercury vapour | White light
[70:0:1] - **Why mercury in fluorescent lamps (UPSC 2010):** when switched on, **mercury vapour emits ultraviolet**; the **phosphor coating** converts UV to visible light (mercury itself does not convert UV).
[70:0:2] - **CFL vs LED (IAS 2011):** CFL uses mercury vapour + phosphor, LED a semiconductor ✓; CFL less efficient than LED ✓; "CFL lasts longer" ✗ → **I and III**.
[70:0:3] - **Vacuum filament lamps:** contain **no gas** (gas-filled lamps use argon/nitrogen) → "none of the above".
[70:0:4] - **A–R (APPSC 2021 ×2):** government urges replacing bright and CFL bulbs with **LEDs** because LEDs light with **great energy efficiency** → **A and R true, R explains A**.
[70:0:5] - **A–R (RAS 2013):** LED life longer than CFL ✓; LED more efficient than CFL ✓; books key "R explains A" (LEDs run cooler); strictly the long life comes from solid-state design (no filament or electrodes to wear).
