## SUB 1 | Red Soils
[52:1:0] - Formed by weathering of **old crystalline igneous and metamorphic rocks** – **granite, gneiss, Dharwar schists, mica schists, greenstones** (basalt gives black soil).
[52:1:1] - Red colour = **iron oxides, mainly ferric oxide** (oxidised in well-drained soil).
[52:1:2] - **Poor** in lime, phosphates, **nitrogen**, humus/organic matter; fairly rich in **potash**; mainly **siliceous**; well drained; **low moisture retention**.
[52:1:3] - **Rayalaseema** red soils: **shallow**, low moisture retention, low organic matter. About 66% of AP's farmed regions.
[52:1:4] - India: Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, AP, Telangana, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand; western TN, NE AP, southern Maharashtra, eastern MP.
[52:1:5] # Red-Soil Sub-types
[52:1:6] TABLE head: Sub-type | Share of AP
  [52:1:6.0] Red earths with loamy subsoil | 30% (largest)
  [52:1:6.1] Red soils with clay base | 12%
  [52:1:6.2] Red loamy | 9%
  [52:1:6.3] Red sandy (chalka) | 8%
[52:1:7] - APPSC-GS 2024 accepted all four statements: red soils over 60%, **six sub-groups**, loamy 9%, clay base 12%.
[52:1:8] Largest red sub-type = **loamy-subsoil red earth (30%)**, not red sandy.
## SUB 2 | Black Soils
[52:2:0] - AP black soils come from **Deccan Trap basalt** (Prakasam, Palnadu, Guntur, East Godavari) and **crystalline schists and basic gneiss**.
[52:2:1] - Fine, **clayey**; **neutral to strongly alkaline**; colour from **iron and titanium** compounds.
[52:2:2] - **Rich** in **calcium, potash, magnesium** (also alumina, iron, lime, magnesium carbonate); **poor** in **nitrogen, phosphorus, humus**.
[52:2:3] - **Very high water retention**; swells when wet, **deep cracks** when dry → **"self-ploughing"**; needs little irrigation.
[52:2:4] - **Deep black** at **Adoni and Alur (Aluru)**; unclassified black in West Godavari, Krishna, Guntur, mid-Kurnool, NW Kadapa, Tadipatri; **Kunderu basin** (Kadapa).
[52:2:5] - **Very deep black soils: north-western Deccan Plateau** (APPSC-G2 2025); thinner towards east and south.
[52:2:6] - India: Maharashtra (most widespread), MP, Gujarat, Karnataka; **Malwa Plateau** = black lava soil; **not in the Himalaya**.

## SUB 4 | Deltaic Alluvium and Coastal Sands
[52:4:0] - Laid as **river-borne silt** by the **Godavari and Krishna**; **renewed with silt each flood** (fresh silt does not harm the soil).
[52:4:1] - **Most fertile** and most important soils of AP for production.
[52:4:2] - Porous, loamy, high moisture retention; **rich in potash and lime** (and phosphoric acid); **poor in nitrogen and humus**.
[52:4:3] - **Krishna delta**: around **Vijayawada–Machilipatnam**; **Godavari delta**: around **Rajahmundry and Kakinada**.
[52:4:4] - Also along the **Vamsadhara and Penna**; Krishna–Godavari–Penna–Vamsadhara delta alluvium → **rice and sugarcane**.
[52:4:5] - A **belt of coastal sands** lines the shore. **Alluvial sand** has the **minimum water retention**.
[52:4:6] - India: northern plains (Punjab, Haryana, UP, Bihar, WB, Assam), Mahanadi–Godavari–Krishna–Cauvery deltas, Narmada–Tapi valleys; sand content falls west to east.
[52:4:7] Most fertile soil of the K-G deltas = **deltaic alluvial**.
## SUB 5 | Laterite Soils
[52:5:0] - Forms under **high temperature and heavy rainfall** with alternate wet and dry seasons.
[52:5:1] - **Intense leaching** removes **silica and lime**; leaves **hydrated iron and aluminium oxides**.
[52:5:2] - **Acidic**, low fertility, poor in **nitrogen**, organic matter, phosphorus; red to yellowish-red; well drained; not moisture-retentive; made infertile by extra iron.
[52:5:3] - AP extent ~**1%**: coastal – **Ellavaram, Peddapuram (Kakinada), Kavali, Kovvur, Gudur, Sullurpeta**; elevated – **Satyavedu, Puttur**; pockets in Nellore, Srikakulam, East Godavari, Anakapalli, Visakhapatnam.
[52:5:4] - North Coastal zone is typed by laterite. India: Western and Eastern Ghats, Maharashtra, **Malabar** coast; **not the Thar**.
[52:5:5] "Laterite forms in semi-arid, low-rainfall regions" = **incorrect** (APPSC 2023/2024).

## SUB 8 | Problem Soils and Reclamation
[52:8:0] TABLE head: Area | Problem
  [52:8:0.0] Deltas | salinity, waterlogging (poor drainage, faulty irrigation)
  [52:8:0.1] Coast | saline water intrusion; Uppada (Kakinada) coastal erosion from sea-level rise and waves
  [52:8:0.2] Rayalaseema plateau | saline soils high in lime and salt – unfit for cropping
  [52:8:0.3] Laterite tracts | acidic soils
  [52:8:0.4] Black soil tracts | alkaline tendency
[52:8:1] - **Salinity** lowers yields and breaks soil structure; **alkalinity** (high pH from limestone/basalt or alkaline water) cuts nutrient supply.
[52:8:2] - **Reclamation**: **alkaline → gypsum**; **saline → drainage and leaching**.
[52:8:3] - **39 watersheds (896 villages)** classed saline (2025-26); **SMWSIPDCL** (Salinity Mitigation & Water Security) company targets it.
[52:8:4] - India: **Gujarat** = largest saline area; **UP** = most alkali land; desert soil (Thar, Gujarat) sandy, low humus, often saline; western Rajasthan soils high in calcium.
[52:8:5] - AP's red lateritic and black soils have low fertility and salinity problems (true, but not the cause in assertion-reason items).
[52:8:6] - **Halophytes** = saline soils; cryophytes – cold; lithophytes – rocks; xerophytes – deserts; hygrophytes – waterlogged; hydrophytes – aquatic.
## SUB 9 | Erosion and Degradation in Rayalaseema
[52:9:0] - **Anantapur, Sri Sathya Sai**: drought-induced degradation, **desertification**, wind erosion of topsoil.
[52:9:1] - Heavily eroded tracts → **skeletal soils**: Nallamala slopes of **Badvel (Kadapa), Giddalur (Prakasam)**, patch at **Jammalamadugu**.
[52:9:2] - Causes: overgrazing, deforestation, erratic rain; heavy chemicals in Guntur chilli and Krishna paddy belts; **Podu** strips topsoil in ASR and Parvathipuram Manyam.
[52:9:3] - Sandy soils and steep slopes lose **soil organic carbon**; clay soils hold more.
[52:9:4] - Responses: **APCNF** natural farming restores SOC; **Rainfed Area Development** – **9 rainfed districts** (8 Rayalaseema + Prakasam), **Rs 30,000 per farm family**; Kharif 2026 green-manure drive **₹62.20 crore**, 91,918 quintals of sunn hemp, dhaincha, green gram seed.
[52:9:5] - Green manures by nitrogen: **cow pea 0.49%** > sunhemp 0.43% > dhaincha 0.42% > guar 0.34%.

## SUB 11 | Soil Science Terms
[52:11:0] - **Edaphic** = non-living factor linked to soil.
[52:11:1] - **Capillary action** best in **clayey** soil (small pores), least in sandy; **clay** holds the most plant-available water.
[52:11:2] - Ideal pH for common crops **6–7**; below 7 acidic, above 7 alkaline.
[52:11:3] - **Podzol** = coniferous (taiga) belt near 60° N. **Soil geography** studies formation, types, fertility, distribution and use.
[52:11:4] - Net sown area: West Bengal ~60%, AP ~40%; most of AP is not laterite.

## SUB 1 | Trewartha and Thornthwaite
[54:1:0] # Trewartha (modified Koeppen)
[54:1:1] - Same **temperature-rainfall** logic, simpler for India.
[54:1:2] TABLE head: Trewartha | Type | Area
  [54:1:2.0] Am | Tropical rainforest | West coast, North-East
  [54:1:2.1] Aw | Tropical savanna | Most of the Peninsula
  [54:1:2.2] BS/BW | Steppe and desert | Rajasthan, rain-shadow Deccan
  [54:1:2.3] Caw | Humid subtropical, dry winter | Ganga plain
[54:1:3] Trewartha Caw | = Koeppen Cwg | Ganga plain
Trewartha H | = Koeppen Dfc/E | Himalaya
[54:1:4] # Thornthwaite (moisture-based)
[54:1:5] - Basis: **moisture index** (precipitation effectiveness) and **potential evapotranspiration** (thermal efficiency).
[54:1:6] - Order: perhumid (A) > humid (B) > sub-humid (C) > semi-arid (D) > arid (E).
[54:1:7] - Perhumid: Western Ghats-west coast, North-East; semi-arid: rain-shadow interior Deccan; arid: western Rajasthan (Thar) and Kutch.
## SUB 2 | Climate of Andhra Pradesh
[54:2:0] - **Tropical Monsoon** (APPSC-GS 2018: "Tropical"); Koeppen **Aw**; latitude **12°41'N to 19°07'N**.
