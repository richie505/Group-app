=== NOTES 2:47 | Centre-state Legislative Relations – Articles 245-255, Repugnancy, Parliament on the State List
## SUB 0 | Parliament on the State List – Articles 249, 250, 252, 253 and 356
[47:0:0] # The five situations
[47:0:1] TABLE head: Article | Trigger | Area / duration
  [47:0:1.0] 249 | Rajya Sabha resolution, 2/3 of members present and voting, national interest | Resolution in force up to 1 year, renewable 1 year at a time; law ceases 6 months after the resolution lapses
  [47:0:1.1] 250 | National Emergency (Proclamation under Art. 352 in operation) | Whole or any part of India; law ceases 6 months after the Emergency ends
  [47:0:1.2] 252 | Resolutions of the legislatures of two or more States | Only the consenting States (others may adopt later); only Parliament can amend or repeal
  [47:0:1.3] 253 | To implement international treaties, agreements, conventions | Whole or any part of India; no State's consent needed
  [47:0:1.4] 356/357 | President's Rule in a State | Parliament (or its delegate) makes the State's laws; they continue after PR ends until the State Legislature alters, repeals or re-enacts them
[47:0:2] - **Normal rule:** Parliament cannot legislate on the State List except under **Arts 249, 250, 252, 253 and 356** – "on its own in the national interest" is **wrong** (needs the Rajya Sabha resolution).
[47:0:3] - **Articles are the grant, emergencies the occasion:** the power during a National Emergency is given by **Art. 250** (Art. 352 only proclaims the Emergency).
[47:0:4] # Article 249 – Rajya Sabha resolution
[47:0:5] - **Majority:** **two-thirds of members present and voting** – not total membership, not the Lok Sabha (the Lok Sabha has **no role**).
[47:0:6] - **Duration:** resolution in force for a period **not exceeding one year** (Art. 249(2)); can be renewed any number of times.
[47:0:7] - A **special power of the Rajya Sabha** (with Art. 312 – new All India Services).
[47:0:8] - The State **may still legislate**, but the **Parliamentary law prevails** in a conflict (**Art. 251**).
[47:0:9] # Article 250 – Emergency
[47:0:10] - During a Proclamation of Emergency **Parliament** (not the President or a minister) legislates on State List matters; State power is **not suspended**, Union law prevails on conflict.
[47:0:11] - Laws cease **6 months after the Proclamation ceases** – they do **not** continue indefinitely.
[47:0:12] # Article 252 – consent of States
[47:0:13] TABLE head: Act passed under Article 252 | Year
  [47:0:13.0] Prize Competition Act | 1955
  [47:0:13.1] Wild Life (Protection) Act | 1972
  [47:0:13.2] Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act | 1974
  [47:0:13.3] Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) Act | 1976
  [47:0:13.4] Transplantation of Human Organs Act | 1994
[47:0:14] - A request by **one State** is not enough; the requesting States **lose the power to amend or repeal** the law.
[47:0:15] - **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** is **not** an Art. 252 law (made under Art. 253 after the Stockholm Conference).
[47:0:16] # Article 253 – international agreements
[47:0:17] - Parliament can make **any law for the whole or any part of India** to implement treaties, agreements, conventions or decisions of international bodies – **even on State List subjects** and **without the consent of any State** (UPSC 2013; UPPCS 2006, 2016).
[47:0:18] - So "the Union can legislate on State List subjects to implement an international agreement" is the statement that reflects India's federal design.
[47:0:19] > Statement angle: State List law possible on – request of 2+ States ✓, Rajya Sabha 2/3 resolution ✓, international agreements ✓, Emergency ✓, President's Rule ✓; a **Lok Sabha** resolution ✗.
[47:0:20] > Wrong-answer angle: "Both Union and States have absolute freedom on the Concurrent List" ✗ – Union law prevails in conflict.

## SUB 1 | Residuary Powers – Article 248
[47:1:0] # The rule
[47:1:1] - **Article 248** (Part XI) + **Entry 97 of the Union List**: matters in **no list** belong **exclusively to Parliament** – Parliament **only**, not the States, the President, the Supreme Court or "both jointly".
[47:1:2] - Art. 248 itself says Parliament has **exclusive power** to make any law on any matter **not enumerated in the Concurrent or State List** – so both "residuary powers are in Art. 248" and "Parliament has exclusive power over items not in the State or Concurrent List" are correct.
[47:1:3] - Includes **residuary taxation** (e.g. gift tax, wealth tax, expenditure tax); examples: **space research**, **cyber law**.
[47:1:4] TABLE head: Country | Residuary power with
  [47:1:4.0] India, Canada | Centre (Union / Parliament)
  [47:1:4.1] USA, Australia | States
[47:1:5] - **Sarkaria Commission (1988):** residuary powers **except taxation** to the **Concurrent List**; residuary taxation to stay with Parliament.
[47:1:6] - **Rajamannar Committee (1971):** residuary powers (legislation and taxation) to the **States**.
[47:1:7] # Jammu and Kashmir (till 5 August 2019)
[47:1:8] - Under Art. 370 and the 1954 Presidential Order, residuary power for J&K lay with the **State Legislature** (except a few matters such as terrorism) – not Parliament (APPSC 2001, DAO 2011).
[47:1:9] - Other J&K facts asked: J&K was part of the territory of India (Art. 1, First Schedule), but not all provisions on States applied to it; by accession India got **Defence, External Affairs and Communications**.
[47:1:10] - **⚠ Now:** Art. 370 provisions ceased in Aug 2019; the **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019** made J&K and Ladakh **Union Territories** (31 Oct 2019), so residuary power now rests with **Parliament** (upheld by the SC, Dec 2023).
[47:1:11] > Trap: "Like other States, J&K's residuary powers vest in Parliament" was the WRONG statement (pre-2019).

[47:2:2] - Distribution of legislative powers is regulated by **Articles 245 and 246** (with the **Seventh Schedule**); scheme borrowed from **Canada**, plus a third **Concurrent List** (from the GoI Act, 1935).

[47:3:0] # Article 246 scheme
[47:3:1] TABLE head: Clause | Who | List

  [47:3:1.3] 246(4) | Parliament for areas not in a State (UTs) | All three Lists
[47:3:2] - **Art. 246 / Seventh Schedule** divide all subject areas (incl. environment-related subjects) into the three Lists, and is also the source of the power to impose **taxes and fees** (tax entries are in the Lists).
[47:3:3] - **Order of predominance:** **Union List > Concurrent List > State List** – in a Concurrent-State conflict the **Concurrent List prevails**; no reference to the President or the Speaker.
[47:3:4] - **Limits on Parliament:** it legislates subject to the **distribution of powers** (and the Constitution) – not the UN Charter, conventions or public opinion.
[47:3:5] - **Art. 246A (GST)** breaks the strict division: **simultaneous** power; **only Parliament** for **inter-State supply (IGST)**.

## SUB 4 | Territorial Extent – Article 245 and Territorial Nexus
[47:4:0] TABLE head: Legislature | Extent
  [47:4:0.0] Parliament | Whole or any part of India; alone can make extra-territorial laws
  [47:4:0.1] State Legislature | Own State only; outside reach only with a real territorial nexus
[47:4:1] - **Art. 245(2):** no law of Parliament is invalid because it has **extra-territorial operation** – so the power of extra-territorial legislation = **Parliament only** (not a House alone, the President, the State or any UN approval).
[47:4:2] - Parliament's laws apply to persons and property in India and to **Indian citizens and their property anywhere** in the world.
[47:4:3] - **Doctrine of territorial nexus:** decides the **scope of a State Legislature's extra-territorial power** – a State law with extra-territorial operation is valid if there is a **sufficient, real (not illusory) nexus** between the State and the object (Wallace 1948; R.M.D. Chamarbaugwalla 1957; TISCO 1958).
[47:4:4] - **⚠ Do not confuse:** "what cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly" = **colourable legislation**, not territorial nexus.
[47:4:5] # Exceptions to Parliament's reach
[47:4:6] - **Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu (Art. 240):** **President's regulations** have the force of Acts of Parliament.
[47:4:7] - **Scheduled Areas:** the **Governor** may **bar or modify** Acts of Parliament; Governor of **Assam** likewise for tribal areas.

## SUB 5 | Repugnancy, Paramountcy and Central Control
[47:5:0] # Article 254
[47:5:1] - Applies to a Central vs State law on a **Concurrent** subject.
[47:5:2] - **254(1):** **Union law prevails**; the State law is **void to the extent of repugnancy** – the **doctrine of paramountcy** (reconciles conflicting laws where both levels can legislate on the same matter).
[47:5:3] - **254(2):** a State law **reserved for and assented to by the President** prevails **only in that State**.
[47:5:4] - **Proviso:** Parliament can later **override** it by a fresh law.
[47:5:5] - **Art. 251** (not 254) covers conflict with laws made under **Arts 249/250**; Art. 250 is **not** the repugnancy article.
[47:5:6] # Central control over State legislation
[47:5:7] - **Governor may reserve** State Bills for the President (Art. 200/201); Art. 254(2) reservation is an exception to aid and advice.
[47:5:8] - **State Bill** with **reasonable restrictions on inter-State trade** in the public interest needs the **President's previous sanction** (Part XIII, Art. 304(b)).
[47:5:9] - **Arts 301-303:** Parliament cannot **prefer or discriminate** between States in trade laws, except in **scarcity**.
[47:5:10] - **Nov 2025 SC advisory opinion (Presidential Reference):** **rejected "deemed assent"** and **judicially fixed timelines** for Governors and the President.

=== NOTES 2:48 | Distribution of Executive Powers Between the Union and the States
## SUB 0 | Union Executive – Article 53 and the President
[48:0:0] # Article 53
[48:0:1] - **Art. 52:** there shall be a President of India.
[48:0:2] - **Art. 53(1):** the **executive power of the Union is vested in the President** and is exercised by him **either directly or through officers subordinate to him** (Ministers included) in accordance with the Constitution – "only through officers" is wrong.
[48:0:3] - **Art. 53(2):** **Supreme Command** of the Defence Forces vests in the President, its exercise **regulated by law**.
[48:0:4] - **Art. 53(3)(b):** nothing in Art. 53 **prevents Parliament from conferring by law functions on authorities other than the President**.
[48:0:5] - **Appointing the PM** (Art. 75) is an **executive power** of the President.
[48:0:6] # Nominal vs real executive
[48:0:7] - President = **nominal / constitutional head**; **Head of State**, not Head of Government; **First Citizen**; **Rank 1** in the Order of Precedence; part of Parliament (**Art. 79**).
[48:0:8] - **Real executive:** the **Council of Ministers** headed by the **PM** (**Art. 74(1)**), collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha – the feature that shows this is **parliamentary democracy** (not federalism or adult franchise).
[48:0:9] - **Art. 77:** all executive action is taken **in the President's name**.
[48:0:10] - **Art. 299:** all **contracts** made in exercise of the Union's executive power are **expressed to be made by the President** (for a State, by the Governor).
[48:0:11] > A-R angle: "President is the executive power of the Union" (A) is explained by Art. 53(1) (R) – both true, R explains A.

## SUB 1 | Union Territories – Administration, Delhi and Lieutenant Governors
[48:1:0] # Article 239 and Delhi
[48:1:1] - **Art. 239:** every UT is administered by the **President**, acting through an **Administrator / Lieutenant Governor** appointed by him – so "UTs are administered by the President".
[48:1:2] - **Delhi** is a **Union Territory** in the First Schedule; the **69th Amendment, 1991** made it the **National Capital Territory** – **Art. 239AA**: Legislative Assembly + Council of Ministers.
[48:1:3] - **Outside the Delhi Assembly's power:** **public order, police, land** (State List entries 1, 2, 18) – not public health or industries.
[48:1:4] - **Police Complaints Authorities:** in **March 2010** the **MHA** ordered PCAs for the **Union Territories** (following Prakash Singh, 2006); States set up their own.

=== NOTES 2:49 | Centre-state Administrative Relations – Articles 256-263, All India Services, Directions to States
## SUB 0 | All India Services – Article 312
[49:0:0] # Creation
[49:0:1] - **Art. 312:** Parliament may create a new AIS if the **Rajya Sabha** declares it necessary in the national interest by a resolution supported by **2/3 of members present and voting** – not "2/3 of total members". It expressly mentions an **All India Judicial Service**.
[49:0:2] - **Council of States' exclusive powers:** recommend **creation of a new AIS (Art. 312)**; authorise Parliament to legislate on a **State List** subject (**Art. 249**).
[49:0:3] - **IAS and IPS** are **deemed to be services created by Parliament** (Art. 312(2)); governed by the **All India Services Act, 1951**; created by **Parliament**, not the UPSC, Home Ministry or courts.
[49:0:4] - Existing AIS = **IAS, IPS, Indian Forest Service (IFoS, 1966)** – all three.
[49:0:5] - **ICS → IAS** (1947); **Sardar Patel** – "**Father of All India Services**", called them the "**Steel Frame**".
[49:0:6] # Recruitment and control
[49:0:7] - Recruited by the **UPSC** on an all-India basis; trained by the Centre; serve in **State cadres**.
[49:0:8] - **Appointment** by the **President** (on UPSC recommendation) – not Governors, the UPSC itself or SPSCs.
[49:0:9] - **Dismissal / removal only by the Union Government**, even while serving a State; officers can be **transferred** and serve both Union and States.
[49:0:10] - **Uniform service** across India = a device of **national integration** (not caste or religious associations).
[49:0:11] # Classify the service
[49:0:12] TABLE head: Service | Category | Nature
  [49:0:12.0] IAS, IPS, IFoS (Forest) | All India Services | Common to Union and States
  [49:0:12.1] IFS (Foreign), IRS, IA&AS, Indian Economic Service, Indian Statistical Service | Central Services | Union subjects only
  [49:0:12.2] SPSC-recruited services | State Services | State subjects
[49:0:13] - **⚠ Trap:** **IPS is an AIS, not a Central Service**; Indian Forest Service (AIS) ≠ Indian Foreign Service (Central).
[49:0:14] # Federal critique and commissions
[49:0:15] - **Main criticism:** AIS are **incompatible with the federal system** / State autonomy – Central rules govern them and States lack full control.
[49:0:16] - **Sarkaria Commission:** the AIS should be **further strengthened and more such services created** (e.g. medicine, engineering, education).
[49:0:17] - **Rajamannar Committee:** **abolish** the AIS (IAS, IPS, IFoS).
[49:0:18] - **IAS (Cadre) Rules, 1954** amendment proposal (2022): **central deputation** without the consent of the **State or the officer**.

## SUB 0 | Distribution of Tax Revenues – Articles 268 to 271 and State Taxes
[50:0:0] # Who levies, who collects, who keeps (present position, after the 80th and 101st Amendments)
[50:0:1] TABLE head: Article | Levied by | Collected / kept by | Taxes
  [50:0:1.0] 268 | Union | States collect and appropriate | Stamp duties on bills of exchange, cheques, promissory notes, insurance policies, share transfers; proceeds not part of the CFI
  [50:0:1.1] 269 | Union | Union collects, assigned to the States | Taxes on inter-State sale or purchase of goods (not newspapers) and on inter-State consignment; net proceeds do not form part of the CFI
  [50:0:1.2] 269A | Union | Shared as Parliament provides on GST Council advice | IGST on inter-State supplies (101st Amendment)
  [50:0:1.3] 270 | Union | Shared with States on Finance Commission advice (divisible pool) | All Union List taxes – income tax (non-agricultural), corporation tax, customs, CGST … – except 268, 269, 269A taxes, surcharges (271) and cesses
  [50:0:1.4] 271 | Union | Union only – States get no share | Surcharges on 269 and 270 taxes (not on GST)
  [50:0:1.5] 268A (omitted) | Union | Union + States | Service tax – inserted by 88th (2003), omitted by 101st (2016)
  [50:0:1.6] 272 (omitted) | Union | Shared by law | Union excise – omitted by the 80th (2000)
[50:0:2] - **Income tax:** the Union **levies, collects and distributes** it between itself and the States (Art. 270) – it is neither kept wholly by the Union nor given wholly to the States; only the **surcharge** stays with the Union.
[50:0:3] - **Before 2000** (old Art. 269) the Union levied and collected but **assigned to the States** taxes on **railway fares and freights**, **stock-exchange and futures-market transactions** (other than stamp duty), estate and succession duty, newspapers and their advertisements; the **80th Amendment** moved these into the **Art. 270 pool**. Only **income tax** was shared from 1950; **corporation tax and customs** became shareable only from **1996** (80th).
[50:0:4] - **Art. 268 before GST** also covered **excise on medicinal and toilet preparations containing alcohol** – levied by the Union, **collected and kept by the States**; the 101st Amendment removed it (now under GST).
[50:0:5] # Taxes levied, collected and retained by the States (State List, 18 heads)
[50:0:6] - **Land revenue**, **agricultural income tax** (Entry 46 – the **Constitution**, not the Finance Commission, assigns it), duties on succession to agricultural land, taxes on **lands and buildings**, mineral rights.
[50:0:7] - **State excise on alcoholic liquor** for human consumption, opium and narcotics; taxes on **sale of petroleum crude, diesel, petrol, natural gas, ATF and liquor** (outside GST – levied **by the States**, not the Centre).
[50:0:8] - Electricity, goods and passengers by road/inland waterways, **vehicles**, animals and boats, **tolls**, **professions** (Art. 276), capitation, **entertainment tax by local bodies**, stamp duty on other documents.
[50:0:9] - **Profession tax (Art. 276(2)):** the **only tax whose total per person is capped by the Constitution** – **₹2,500 a year** (₹250 till the **60th Amendment, 1988**).
[50:0:10] # Local bodies
[50:0:11] - **Art. 243H / 243X:** the State Legislature may let Panchayats/Municipalities **levy, collect and appropriate** taxes, duties, tolls and fees, and may **assign** to them taxes collected by the State.
[50:0:12] - **Assigned revenues:** taxes **assigned to local bodies by statute but collected by the State** and passed on to the Panchayats.
[50:0:13] - **Gram Panchayat taxes:** house tax, **tax/fee on fairs and markets ("fair tax")**, water and lighting rates, vehicle and animal fees – **not** sales tax, customs or excise.

## SUB 1 | Finance Commission and Devolution – Article 280
[50:1:0] # The body
[50:1:1] - **Art. 280:** a **quasi-judicial** body constituted by the **President** every **fifth year** or earlier; chairman + 4 members (Finance Commission Act, 1951); **Art. 281:** its report and the action taken are laid before Parliament.
[50:1:2] - Called the **"balancing wheel of fiscal federalism"**; its advice is **not binding**.
[50:1:3] # What it recommends (Art. 280(3))
[50:1:4] - **Distribution of the net proceeds of taxes** between the Union and the States (**vertical**) and **among the States inter se** (**horizontal distribution**).
[50:1:5] - **Principles governing grants-in-aid** to the States out of the **Consolidated Fund of India** (Art. 275).
[50:1:6] - Measures to augment a State's Consolidated Fund for **Panchayats and Municipalities** (73rd/74th Amendments), and any matter referred by the President.
[50:1:7] - It does **not** attract foreign capital, distribute funds among PSUs or audit financial administration (UPSC 2011 – "none of these").
[50:1:8] - Tax sharing is **not** fixed by the Finance Ministry, RBI, NABARD, NITI Aayog, the Inter-State Council, NDC or the GST Council; the **CAG certifies the net proceeds** (Art. 279).
[50:1:9] # Shares
[50:1:10] TABLE head: FC | Period | States' share of divisible pool
  [50:1:10.0] 10th | 1995-2000 | Alternative Scheme of Devolution – 29% of gross Union taxes (80th Amendment)
  [50:1:10.1] 14th (Y.V. Reddy) | 2015-20 | 42% – "compositional shift" from grants to devolution
  [50:1:10.2] 15th (N.K. Singh) | 2020-26 | 41%
  [50:1:10.3] 16th (Arvind Panagariya) | 2026-31 | 41% retained; State fiscal deficit 3% of GSDP; end off-budget borrowing
[50:1:11] - **Compositional shift:** more tax devolution and fewer conditional grants → **increase in the share of unconditional (untied) transfers** to States and more fiscal autonomy (APPSC Group-I 2017).
[50:1:12] - **Cesses and surcharges** (~18% of gross tax) stay outside the pool, so effective devolution is about **31-32%**; southern States' **"Chalo Delhi" (2024)** opposed population-based criteria. Tax devolution 2024-25: **₹12.87 lakh crore**.

[50:3:2] - **Subsumed:** Central excise, service tax, CVD/SAD, **State VAT/sales tax**, **entry tax and octroi**, **entertainment tax (except by local bodies)**, luxury, purchase and lottery taxes.
[50:3:3] - **Not subsumed:** **land revenue** (not a tax on supply at all), **State excise on alcohol for human consumption** (liquor is outside GST), stamp duty, electricity duty, basic customs; petroleum products zero-rated till the Council decides.
[50:3:4] - **Art. 246A:** Parliament and States both make GST laws; **only Parliament** for **inter-State supply**; **Art. 269A:** IGST, revenue to the **destination State**.
[50:3:5] # GST Council – Art. 279A
[50:3:6] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [50:3:6.0] Chair | Union Finance Minister
  [50:3:6.1] Members | Union MoS (Revenue/Finance) + every State's Finance Minister
  [50:3:6.2] Votes | Centre 1/3, all States together 2/3; decision by 3/4 of weighted votes
  [50:3:6.3] Status | Mohit Minerals (2022): recommendations persuasive, not binding
[50:3:7] - **53rd GST Council (22 June 2024):** monetary limits for departmental appeals – **₹20 lakh GSTAT, ₹1 crore High Court, ₹2 crore Supreme Court** (CBIC Circular 207/1/2024-GST).
[50:3:8] - **GST and federalism:** "**pooled sovereignty**" – Centre and States decide **jointly** in the Council as partners (cooperative federalism); critics note the Centre's effective veto. Compensation cess on luxury/sin goods for **5 years (2017-22)**; **GST 2.0** from **September 2025**.

=== NOTES 2:52 | Emergency Provisions – National Emergency (352), President's Rule (356), Financial Emergency (360); S.r. Bommai Case
## SUB 0 | National Emergency (Article 352) – Grounds, Proclamation, Approval
[52:0:0] # Grounds
[52:0:1] - Proclaimed by the **President** when the security of India or any part is threatened by **war, external aggression or armed rebellion** – also on **imminent danger**; for the whole of India or a part.
[52:0:2] - **44th Amendment, 1978** substituted "**armed rebellion**" for "**internal disturbance**" – internal disturbance / threat to internal peace is **no longer a ground**.
[52:0:3] - War / external aggression = "**External Emergency**"; armed rebellion = "**Internal Emergency**".
[52:0:4] # Proclamation
[52:0:5] - Only when the **Union Cabinet's decision is communicated in writing** – **Art. 352(3)**, inserted by the **44th Amendment** – not on the PM's or the Home Minister's advice alone, not suo motu, not on the CJI's advice.
[52:0:6] - The word "**Cabinet**" (mantrimandal) appears **only once** in the Constitution – in **Art. 352(3)**.
[52:0:7] - **Judicial review:** the 38th Amendment (1975) made the President's satisfaction **non-justiciable**; the **44th** removed that bar and **Minerva Mills (1980)** held a proclamation **reviewable** (mala fide / irrelevant grounds) – so "it cannot be challenged in any court" is **wrong**.
[52:0:8] # Approval, duration, revocation
[52:0:9] TABLE head: Point | Rule
  [52:0:9.0] Approval | Both Houses within one month (two months till 1978)
  [52:0:9.1] Majority | Special majority – 2/3 present and voting + majority of total membership, in each House separately (no joint sitting)
  [52:0:9.2] Duration | 6 months at a time, extendable indefinitely
  [52:0:9.3] Lok Sabha dissolved | Survives 30 days from the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha if Rajya Sabha has approved

## SUB 1 | President's Rule (Article 356) – State or Constitutional Emergency
[52:1:0] # Grounds and approval
[52:1:1] - Imposed when the President is satisfied, **on the Governor's report or otherwise**, that the government of a State **cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution** (Art. 356) or the State **fails to comply with Union directions** (Art. **365**).
[52:1:2] - Called "**State Emergency**" or "**Constitutional Emergency**" – it leads to **President's Rule / Governor's Rule**, not suspension of freedoms, salary cuts or mid-term polls.
[52:1:3] - **No consent** of the State Legislature; the Governor reports when **he is convinced** the government cannot run per the Constitution.
[52:1:4] TABLE head: Point | Rule
  [52:1:4.0] Approval | Both Houses within two months, simple majority
  [52:1:4.1] Each approval | 6 months
  [52:1:4.2] Maximum | 3 years
  [52:1:4.3] Beyond 1 year | Only if a National Emergency is in operation AND the Election Commission certifies elections cannot be held (Art. 356(5))
  [52:1:4.4] Revocation | President any time – no parliamentary approval
[52:1:5] # Consequences
[52:1:6] TABLE head: Organ | Effect
  [52:1:6.0] Executive | President assumes State functions; Council of Ministers dismissed (necessarily); Governor administers with advisers
  [52:1:6.1] Legislature | Assembly suspended or dissolved (not necessarily dissolved); Parliament makes laws on State List matters for the State, or delegates to the President (President's Acts)
  [52:1:6.2] Laws | Laws made during PR continue after it ends until altered by the State Legislature
  [52:1:6.3] High Court | Unaffected
  [52:1:6.4] Local bodies | Not dissolved
[52:1:7] # Proper and improper use (Sarkaria → Bommai)
[52:1:8] - **Proper:** hung assembly; no party able to form a ministry; **constitutional direction of the Centre disregarded**; internal subversion; physical breakdown.
[52:1:9] - **Improper:** loss of majority without probing an alternative; no floor test; Lok Sabha defeat of the ruling party; **maladministration or corruption** of the ministry; intra-party problems.
[52:1:10] # Misuse debate
[52:1:11] - Used **more than 125 times**, often arbitrarily for political reasons – hence among the **most controversial** provisions.
[52:1:12] - **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** hoped it would remain a "**dead letter**", used only as a **measure of last resort**.
[52:1:13] - **AP High Court (Dec 2020)** asked whether it could record a "breakdown of constitutional machinery" – **Art. 356**.

## SUB 2 | Financial Emergency (Article 360)
[52:2:0] - Ground: threat to the **financial stability or credit of India** or any part; proclaimed by the **President**.

=== NOTES 2:53 | Inter-state Relations – Inter-state Council, Zonal Councils, River-water Disputes (article 262), Full Faith and Credit
## SUB 0 | Inter-State Council – Article 263
[53:0:0] # Article 263 and who sets it up
[53:0:1] - **Article 263** = provision for an **Inter-State Council** to effect co-ordination **between the States** and **between the Centre and the States** (not Arts. 260, 261, 262, 264, 360, 365 or 368). Art. 262 is river-water disputes; Art. 261 is full faith and credit.
[53:0:2] - Set up by **the President** by order, when public interest would be served (not Parliament, the PM, the Council of Ministers, the Speaker, the NDC or a Zonal Council); the President also defines its duties, organisation and procedure.
[53:0:3] - Art. 263 duties: (a) **enquire into and advise upon disputes** between States; (b) **investigate and discuss subjects of common interest**; (c) make **recommendations for better co-ordination of policy and action**.
[53:0:4] - Other councils set up under Art. 263: **Central Council of Health** (and Family Welfare), **Central Council of Local Self-Government** (1954), **Transport Development Council**, Regional Councils for Sales Tax.
[53:0:5] # Establishment (1990)
[53:0:6] - Set up by **Presidential order of 28 May 1990** (V.P. Singh government) on the recommendation of the **Sarkaria Commission**, which made a strong case for a permanent council called the "**Inter-Governmental Council**".
[53:0:7] - Earlier backers: **First ARC (1966-69)** and the **Rajamannar Committee (1969)** – but neither got it set up.
[53:0:8] - Sarkaria also proposed constitutional status under **Art. 263** for the NDC renamed as the **National Economic and Development Council**.
[53:0:9] - **Nature:** a **constitutional, recommendatory** body for consultation – advice **not binding**, not a court (Art. 131 disputes go to the Supreme Court).
[53:0:10] - **Meetings:** at least **three a year** (on paper), held **in camera**; all questions decided by **consensus** (not by simple, two-thirds or three-fourths majority). Only **11 meetings** so far, the last on **16 July 2016**.
[53:0:11] # Composition

  [53:0:12.0] Chairman | Prime Minister (not the President)
  [53:0:12.1] Members | Chief Ministers of all States and of UTs with legislatures

  [53:0:12.3] Members (only then) | Governors of States under President's Rule – not permanent members for all States

  [53:0:12.5] Permanent invitees | Five Cabinet ministers / MoS (independent charge) nominated by the PM

## SUB 1 | Zonal Councils and the North Eastern Council
[53:1:0] # Zonal Councils
[53:1:1] - **Statutory, not constitutional** bodies – set up by **Parliament** under the **States Reorganisation Act, 1956** (Sections 15-22); idea of **PM Jawaharlal Nehru**. "Regional councils are constituted by **Parliamentary laws**."
[53:1:2] - States and UTs grouped with an advisory council each = **Zonal Council** (not State, Regional or Joint Council).
[53:1:3] - **Five** zonal councils: **Northern, Central, Eastern, Western, Southern**. **Chandigarh**, though not a State, is in the Northern council.
[53:1:4] - **Chairman:** the **Union Home Minister** – common chairman of all five (not the PM). **Vice-Chairman:** a **CM by annual rotation**.
[53:1:5] - Members: Home Minister, **CMs of the zone's States**, **two other ministers from each State**, **Administrators of UTs**; advisers (no vote): a NITI Aayog nominee, Chief Secretaries, Development Commissioners.
[53:1:6] - Only **deliberative and advisory** bodies – recommendations **not binding**; described as **sub-federal links** that promote **co-operation and integration** among States (both statements true).
[53:1:7] - Objectives: **emotional integration**, check **regionalism, linguism**, remove after-effects of reorganisation, co-operate on development projects, **political equilibrium** between regions.

## SUB 3 | Full Faith and Credit (Art. 261) and Inter-State Trade (Arts. 301-307)
[53:3:0] # Article 261
[53:3:1] - **261(1):** **full faith and credit** throughout India to **public acts, records and judicial proceedings** of the **Union and every State** (not Arts. 133, 142, 256, 263, 266 or 324).

[53:3:3] - **261(3):** **final judgments or orders of civil courts** can be **executed anywhere** in India (civil only, not criminal).

## SUB 4 | River Water Disputes – Article 262 and Tribunals
[53:4:0] - **Art. 262(1):** Parliament may provide for **adjudication** of disputes on the **use, distribution or control of waters of inter-State rivers** (not Arts. 56, 105 or 352).
[53:4:1] - **Art. 262(2):** Parliament may bar the **Supreme Court and all other courts**; so **Art. 131** (SC original jurisdiction over Centre-State disputes) does not cover water disputes.
[53:4:2] - **Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956:** Centre sets up an **ad hoc tribunal**; a State complains to the **Central Government** (Section 3); award final and binding.
