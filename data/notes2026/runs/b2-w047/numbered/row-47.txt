## SUB 0 | Parliament on the State List – Articles 249, 250, 252, 253 and 356
[0:0] # The five situations
[0:1] TABLE head: Article | Trigger | Area / duration
  [0:1.0] 249 | Rajya Sabha resolution, 2/3 of members present and voting, national interest | Resolution in force up to 1 year, renewable 1 year at a time; law ceases 6 months after the resolution lapses
  [0:1.1] 250 | National Emergency (Proclamation under Art. 352 in operation) | Whole or any part of India; law ceases 6 months after the Emergency ends
  [0:1.2] 252 | Resolutions of the legislatures of two or more States | Only the consenting States (others may adopt later); only Parliament can amend or repeal
  [0:1.3] 253 | To implement international treaties, agreements, conventions | Whole or any part of India; no State's consent needed
  [0:1.4] 356/357 | President's Rule in a State | Parliament (or its delegate) makes the State's laws; they continue after PR ends until the State Legislature alters, repeals or re-enacts them
[0:2] - **Normal rule:** Parliament cannot legislate on the State List except under **Arts 249, 250, 252, 253 and 356** – "on its own in the national interest" is **wrong** (needs the Rajya Sabha resolution).
[0:3] - **Articles are the grant, emergencies the occasion:** the power during a National Emergency is given by **Art. 250** (Art. 352 only proclaims the Emergency).
[0:4] # Article 249 – Rajya Sabha resolution
[0:5] - **Majority:** **two-thirds of members present and voting** – not total membership, not the Lok Sabha (the Lok Sabha has **no role**).
[0:6] - **Duration:** resolution in force for a period **not exceeding one year** (Art. 249(2)); can be renewed any number of times.
[0:7] - A **special power of the Rajya Sabha** (with Art. 312 – new All India Services).
[0:8] - The State **may still legislate**, but the **Parliamentary law prevails** in a conflict (**Art. 251**).
[0:9] # Article 250 – Emergency
[0:10] - During a Proclamation of Emergency **Parliament** (not the President or a minister) legislates on State List matters; State power is **not suspended**, Union law prevails on conflict.
[0:11] - Laws cease **6 months after the Proclamation ceases** – they do **not** continue indefinitely.
[0:12] # Article 252 – consent of States
[0:13] TABLE head: Act passed under Article 252 | Year
  [0:13.0] Prize Competition Act | 1955
  [0:13.1] Wild Life (Protection) Act | 1972
  [0:13.2] Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act | 1974
  [0:13.3] Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) Act | 1976
  [0:13.4] Transplantation of Human Organs Act | 1994
[0:14] - A request by **one State** is not enough; the requesting States **lose the power to amend or repeal** the law.
[0:15] - **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** is **not** an Art. 252 law (made under Art. 253 after the Stockholm Conference).
[0:16] # Article 253 – international agreements
[0:17] - Parliament can make **any law for the whole or any part of India** to implement treaties, agreements, conventions or decisions of international bodies – **even on State List subjects** and **without the consent of any State** (UPSC 2013; UPPCS 2006, 2016).
[0:18] - So "the Union can legislate on State List subjects to implement an international agreement" is the statement that reflects India's federal design.
[0:19] > Statement angle: State List law possible on – request of 2+ States ✓, Rajya Sabha 2/3 resolution ✓, international agreements ✓, Emergency ✓, President's Rule ✓; a **Lok Sabha** resolution ✗.
[0:20] > Wrong-answer angle: "Both Union and States have absolute freedom on the Concurrent List" ✗ – Union law prevails in conflict.

## SUB 1 | Residuary Powers – Article 248
[1:0] # The rule
[1:1] - **Article 248** (Part XI) + **Entry 97 of the Union List**: matters in **no list** belong **exclusively to Parliament** – Parliament **only**, not the States, the President, the Supreme Court or "both jointly".
[1:2] - Art. 248 itself says Parliament has **exclusive power** to make any law on any matter **not enumerated in the Concurrent or State List** – so both "residuary powers are in Art. 248" and "Parliament has exclusive power over items not in the State or Concurrent List" are correct.
[1:3] - Includes **residuary taxation** (e.g. gift tax, wealth tax, expenditure tax); examples: **space research**, **cyber law**.
[1:4] TABLE head: Country | Residuary power with
  [1:4.0] India, Canada | Centre (Union / Parliament)
  [1:4.1] USA, Australia | States
[1:5] - **Sarkaria Commission (1988):** residuary powers **except taxation** to the **Concurrent List**; residuary taxation to stay with Parliament.
[1:6] - **Rajamannar Committee (1971):** residuary powers (legislation and taxation) to the **States**.
[1:7] # Jammu and Kashmir (till 5 August 2019)
[1:8] - Under Art. 370 and the 1954 Presidential Order, residuary power for J&K lay with the **State Legislature** (except a few matters such as terrorism) – not Parliament (APPSC 2001, DAO 2011).
[1:9] - Other J&K facts asked: J&K was part of the territory of India (Art. 1, First Schedule), but not all provisions on States applied to it; by accession India got **Defence, External Affairs and Communications**.
[1:10] - **⚠ Now:** Art. 370 provisions ceased in Aug 2019; the **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019** made J&K and Ladakh **Union Territories** (31 Oct 2019), so residuary power now rests with **Parliament** (upheld by the SC, Dec 2023).
[1:11] > Trap: "Like other States, J&K's residuary powers vest in Parliament" was the WRONG statement (pre-2019).

[3:2] - **Art. 246 / Seventh Schedule** divide all subject areas (incl. environment-related subjects) into the three Lists, and is also the source of the power to impose **taxes and fees** (tax entries are in the Lists).
[3:3] - **Order of predominance:** **Union List > Concurrent List > State List** – in a Concurrent-State conflict the **Concurrent List prevails**; no reference to the President or the Speaker.
[3:4] - **Limits on Parliament:** it legislates subject to the **distribution of powers** (and the Constitution) – not the UN Charter, conventions or public opinion.
[3:5] - **Art. 246A (GST)** breaks the strict division: **simultaneous** power; **only Parliament** for **inter-State supply (IGST)**.

## SUB 4 | Territorial Extent – Article 245 and Territorial Nexus
[4:0] TABLE head: Legislature | Extent
  [4:0.0] Parliament | Whole or any part of India; alone can make extra-territorial laws
  [4:0.1] State Legislature | Own State only; outside reach only with a real territorial nexus
[4:1] - **Art. 245(2):** no law of Parliament is invalid because it has **extra-territorial operation** – so the power of extra-territorial legislation = **Parliament only** (not a House alone, the President, the State or any UN approval).
[4:2] - Parliament's laws apply to persons and property in India and to **Indian citizens and their property anywhere** in the world.
[4:3] - **Doctrine of territorial nexus:** decides the **scope of a State Legislature's extra-territorial power** – a State law with extra-territorial operation is valid if there is a **sufficient, real (not illusory) nexus** between the State and the object (Wallace 1948; R.M.D. Chamarbaugwalla 1957; TISCO 1958).
[4:4] - **⚠ Do not confuse:** "what cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly" = **colourable legislation**, not territorial nexus.
[4:5] # Exceptions to Parliament's reach
[4:6] - **Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu (Art. 240):** **President's regulations** have the force of Acts of Parliament.
[4:7] - **Scheduled Areas:** the **Governor** may **bar or modify** Acts of Parliament; Governor of **Assam** likewise for tribal areas.

## SUB 5 | Repugnancy, Paramountcy and Central Control
[5:0] # Article 254
[5:1] - Applies to a Central vs State law on a **Concurrent** subject.
[5:2] - **254(1):** **Union law prevails**; the State law is **void to the extent of repugnancy** – the **doctrine of paramountcy** (reconciles conflicting laws where both levels can legislate on the same matter).
[5:3] - **254(2):** a State law **reserved for and assented to by the President** prevails **only in that State**.
[5:4] - **Proviso:** Parliament can later **override** it by a fresh law.
[5:5] - **Art. 251** (not 254) covers conflict with laws made under **Arts 249/250**; Art. 250 is **not** the repugnancy article.
[5:6] # Central control over State legislation
[5:7] - **Governor may reserve** State Bills for the President (Art. 200/201); Art. 254(2) reservation is an exception to aid and advice.
[5:8] - **State Bill** with **reasonable restrictions on inter-State trade** in the public interest needs the **President's previous sanction** (Part XIII, Art. 304(b)).
[5:9] - **Arts 301-303:** Parliament cannot **prefer or discriminate** between States in trade laws, except in **scarcity**.
[5:10] - **Nov 2025 SC advisory opinion (Presidential Reference):** **rejected "deemed assent"** and **judicially fixed timelines** for Governors and the President.
