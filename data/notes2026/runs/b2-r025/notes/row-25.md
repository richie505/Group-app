=== Election – Electoral College, Value of Votes, Disputes (Arts. 54, 55, 71)
| Votes in the Presidential election | Does NOT vote |
|---|---|
| **Elected members of both Houses of Parliament** (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha) | **Nominated members** of Parliament and of State Assemblies |
| **Elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies** | **All members of State Legislative Councils** (elected or nominated) – a CM who sits in the Council also cannot vote |
| **Elected members of the Assemblies of NCT Delhi and Puducherry** (added by the **70th Amendment, 1992**) | Governors, Lt. Governors; UTs without Assemblies (e.g. Chandigarh, Daman and Diu) |
- **Art. 54** = the electoral college; **Art. 55** = manner of election; Arts. 54 and 55 together = **election of the President** (not Arts. 49, 56, 62, 74, 78, 103).
- **Keyword – "elected" members**: "all the members of the two Houses" is **wrong** (nominated MPs are out); the correct code is **elected MPs + elected MLAs** (States, Delhi, Puducherry).
- **Indirect election**: the President is chosen by the elected representatives, not directly by the people, and not by Parliament or the Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha alone.
- **Art. 55(3)**: **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote**, by **secret ballot** – method borrowed from **Ireland**.
- PR-STV is also used for the **Vice-President**, **Rajya Sabha** members and **Legislative Council** members; **Lok Sabha** and State Assembly members are elected directly (first-past-the-post).
| Elector | Value of vote | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| MLA | **Population of the State ÷ elected MLAs × 1/1000** | **Varies from State to State** (uniformity among States, Art. 55(1)) |
| MP | **Total value of all MLAs' votes ÷ total elected MPs** | **Same for every MP**, Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha (parity between the Union and the States) |
- **Population base: 1971 Census** – frozen by the 42nd Amendment (till 2000) and then by the **84th Amendment (2001)** till the first Census after **2026**.
- **Vote value depends on the population–seats ratio, not on the number of seats**: Puducherry MLA **16** > Arunachal Pradesh MLA **8**; Kerala MLA **152** > Madhya Pradesh MLA **131** (2022 values). So "more Assembly seats → higher MLA vote value" is **wrong**.
- **Nomination**: at least **50 electors as proposers and 50 as seconders** (since 1997; **10 + 10** from 1974 to 1997); deposit ₹15,000. Vice-President: **20 + 20**.
- **Quota**: more than half of valid vote value; lowest candidate eliminated and votes transferred by preference (V.V. Giri, 1969, won on **second-preference** votes).
- **Art. 71**: all doubts and disputes about the election are decided **only by the Supreme Court**, whose decision is **final** (not the Election Commission or the RS Chairman).
- **Acts done before the election is declared void remain valid** ("not invalidated").
- **Art. 71(4)**: the election **cannot be challenged or postponed** because of **vacancies in the electoral college** – members of a **dissolved Assembly** simply do not vote.
- **Election vs impeachment**: **elected MLAs vote in the election but take no part in impeachment** (Parliament alone impeaches); **nominated MPs** do the reverse. **Legislative Councils** do neither.
> UPSC 1996 (twice) and State PSCs: "part of the electoral college but not of impeachment" = **State Legislative Assemblies**.
- **Electoral colleges compared**: President = elected MPs + elected MLAs; Vice-President = all MPs; Rajya Sabha = elected MLAs; Lok Sabha = the people – so a **Lok Sabha member** shares no electoral college with the others.

=== Qualifications, Term, Re-election, Oath, Resignation, Emoluments (Arts. 56–60)
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **56** | **Term: 5 years from the date he enters upon his office**; resignation in writing **to the Vice-President**; continues until the successor enters office |
| **57** | **Eligibility for re-election** – **any number of times**, no upper limit |
| **58** | **Qualifications**: citizen of India; **35 years** completed; **qualified for election to the Lok Sabha**; no office of profit |
| **59** | Conditions of office: not a member of any legislature; no office of profit; **rent-free residence**; emoluments cannot be diminished |
| **60** | **Oath** before the **CJI** (in his absence the senior-most SC judge) |
- **Art. 58 – what is NOT required**: **education**, citizenship **by birth** (any citizen), a **maximum age** (there is none). Qualified for the **Lok Sabha** (House of the People), not the Rajya Sabha or a State legislature.
- **Office of profit**: a person holding an office of profit under the Union, a State or a local authority is **not eligible**; the offices of President, VP, Governor and Union/State Minister are not offices of profit.
- **Art. 59**: an MP or MLA may contest, but his **seat is deemed vacated on the date he enters upon his office** as President (no prior resignation needed).
- **Resignation**: addressed to the **Vice-President**, who **communicates it to the Speaker of the Lok Sabha** (not to the CJI, PM, RS Chairman or the electoral college).
- **Oath (Art. 60)**: to **faithfully execute the office**, **preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law**, and devote himself to the **service and well-being of the people of India** – administered by the **CJI**, not the Vice-President.
- **Emoluments**: fixed by **Parliament by law**, originally listed in the **Second Schedule**; charged on the Consolidated Fund; **cannot be diminished during the term**. Salary **₹5 lakh per month** (Finance Act, 2018, w.e.f. 1 January 2016).
- **Term continues**: if the successor has not entered office when the 5 years end, the **outgoing President continues** – the VP or the CJI does not step in.
- No term limit in India (USA: two terms, 22nd Amendment); only **Rajendra Prasad** has been elected twice (1952, 1957).

=== Powers of the President
- **Art. 53**: the **executive power of the Union is vested in the President**, exercised directly or through subordinate officers **in accordance with the Constitution** – so his power is **not unlimited** (Art. 74: aid and advice).
- **Art. 53(2)**: **Supreme Command of the Defence Forces** is vested in the **President** (regulated by law) – not the Defence Minister, Army Chief or a Field Marshal.
- **Union executive**: **President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers and Attorney General** (Part V, Chapter I) – not the Speaker or the RS Deputy Chairman.
| Power | Examples |
|---|---|
| Executive | Appoints the **PM** and on his advice other Ministers; **Governors (Art. 155)**, **CAG (Art. 148)**, **AG (Art. 76)**, **Election Commissioners (Art. 324)**, UPSC, Finance Commission; **Ambassadors and High Commissioners**; administers UTs; **Art. 240** regulations for A&N, Lakshadweep, DNH and Daman & Diu, and **Puducherry when its Assembly is suspended or dissolved** |
| Legislative | **Integral part of Parliament (Art. 79: President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha)**; **summons and prorogues** the Houses, **dissolves the Lok Sabha** (Art. 85); **summons a joint sitting** (Art. 108 – presided over by the **Speaker**); addresses and sends messages (Art. 86); special address (Art. 87); **nominates 12** to the Rajya Sabha; assent to Bills; ordinances |
| Financial | Budget laid in his name; **Money Bills** and demands for grants only on his **recommendation**; advances from the **Contingency Fund**; constitutes the Finance Commission |
| Judicial | Appoints SC/HC judges (Third Judges Case 1998: **CJI + four senior-most judges** collegium); **Art. 143** advisory opinion – **not binding**; **Art. 72** pardon |
| Diplomatic and military | Treaties in his name; sends and receives envoys; declares war and concludes peace **on the Council's advice** |
| Emergency | **Art. 352** National, **Art. 356** President's Rule, **Art. 360** Financial |
- **Prior recommendation of the President** is needed for: **Money Bills (Art. 117)**, **Art. 3 Bills** (new States, boundaries), Bills involving expenditure from the Consolidated Fund, State Bills restricting trade (Art. 304) – not for Constitution Amendment Bills or every private member's Bill.
- **Reports laid before Parliament by the President**: **Finance Commission** recommendations (Art. 281), **CAG** reports (Art. 151), **National Commission for SCs** (Art. 338), UPSC, Linguistic Minorities – but **not the PAC** report (a committee of Parliament itself).
- **Art. 78 (PM's duties)**: communicate **all decisions of the Council of Ministers** on administration and **proposals for legislation**; furnish information the President calls for; place before the Council a matter **decided by a single Minister** if the President so requires. With **Art. 86** (address and messages) – all four CDS 2013 statements are correct.
- **Address to Parliament (Art. 87)**: at the **first session after each general election** and the **first session of each year**; the speech is **prepared by the Union Cabinet** (Council of Ministers).
- **When the Lok Sabha is dissolved** the Council of Ministers continues (Art. 75 needs a Council at all times), and the President still **acts on its advice**.
- **First citizen** of India; "**represents the nation but does not lead the nation**" (the PM leads).

=== Vacancy and Acting President (Arts. 62, 65)
- **Causes of vacancy**: expiry of term, **death**, **resignation**, **removal by impeachment**, disqualification, election declared void.
- **Art. 62(1) – expiry**: the election must be **completed before the term expires**; if delayed, the **outgoing President continues** (no vacancy, so the VP does **not** act).
- **Art. 62(2) – death, resignation, removal or otherwise**: election **within six months** of the vacancy (the office can stay vacant at most 6 months); the new President gets a **full five-year term**, not the remainder.
- **Art. 65(1)**: in a vacancy by **death, resignation or removal**, the **Vice-President acts as President** until the new President enters office; **Art. 65(2)**: the VP also **discharges the functions** when the President is absent, ill or otherwise unable.
- While acting, the VP has all the powers, immunities and emoluments of the President and does **not** act as RS Chairman – the **Deputy Chairman presides** over the Rajya Sabha.
- **Both offices vacant**: the **Chief Justice of India**, and in his absence the **senior-most SC judge**, discharges the President's functions – **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969** – not the PM, Speaker, AG or CEC.
| Acting President | When | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **V.V. Giri** (VP) | 3 May – 20 July **1969** | **Zakir Husain died** in office |
| **M. Hidayatullah** (CJI) | **20 July – 24 August 1969** | Giri resigned as VP to contest; **both offices vacant** |
| **B.D. Jatti** (VP) | February – July **1977** | **Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed died** in office |
| **M. Hidayatullah** (VP) | **6–31 October 1982** | Discharged the functions while **Zail Singh** was abroad for surgery |
- **M. Hidayatullah** – the **only CJI to act as President** (first and only, 1969); the only person who has been **CJI, acting President and Vice-President (1979–84)**; he performed the President's functions **twice** (1969 as CJI, 1982 as VP).
- **Not CJIs who acted**: P.N. Bhagwati, P.B. Gajendragadkar.

=== Impeachment (Art. 61)
- **Art. 61** = procedure for **impeachment** (removal) of the President (not Arts. 53, 60, 63, 71, 72, 74, 78); Art. 56(1)(b) refers to it.
- **Only ground: "violation of the Constitution"** – not corruption, misbehaviour, criminal offence, moral degradation, unsoundness of mind or professional misconduct (the Constitution does not define the term).
- **Who removes him**: **Parliament** (the two Houses) – not the PM, the CJI, the party in power, a court, or the Lok Sabha alone.
- **Initiated in either House of Parliament** (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha) – not only the Lok Sabha and not a joint sitting.
| Step | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Notice | **14 days'** written notice, resolution signed by **one-fourth** of the total members of the initiating House |
| Initiating House | Passes the charge by **two-thirds of its total membership** |
| Other House | **Investigates** the charge (President may appear or be represented) and sustains it by **two-thirds of its total membership** → removed from that date |
- **Quasi-judicial procedure**; borrowed from the **USA**. **No President has ever been impeached.**
- **Nominated MPs take part**; **elected MLAs (States, Delhi, Puducherry) do not** – the President is removed by the national Parliament alone, keeping removal free from narrow regional considerations.
- "Impeachment" is used **only for the President**; judges and the Vice-President are "removed". **VP removal**: Rajya Sabha resolution by a majority of all its then members, agreed to by the Lok Sabha; **no ground** is specified.

=== Constitutional Position, Article 74 and Discretion
- **Nominal / de jure** executive and **constitutional head of the State**; the **PM and Council of Ministers** are the **real / de facto** executive. A-R: "the Constitution vests executive power in the President" (A) because "he is the constitutional Head of the State" (R) – both true, R explains A.
- **Art. 74(1)**: a Council of Ministers headed by the PM to aid and advise the President. **42nd Amendment (1976)**: advice made **binding** ("shall act"). **44th Amendment (1978)**: he may **return the advice once for reconsideration**, then must act on it.
- So the President's influence on ministers = he **may seek reconsideration once** before putting his seal on a matter.
- **Art. 74(2)**: whether and what advice was tendered **cannot be inquired into by any court**.
- **Art. 361**: President/Governor not answerable to any court for official acts; no criminal proceedings during the term; **civil proceedings only after two months' written notice**; no court can compel a Governor to perform a duty.
- **Situational discretion** (no constitutional discretion): appointing a PM when no party has a clear majority; dismissing a Ministry that has lost confidence; dissolving the Lok Sabha if the Ministry lost its majority; day-to-day decisions under a **caretaker government**; **suspensive veto** (returning a Bill), **pocket veto** (keeping a Bill pending); asking the Council to **reconsider** advice.
- **Not discretionary**: **summoning and proroguing Parliament**, appointing Ministers, appointing the majority leader as PM (a convention), assent to Bills.
- **PM** = "keystone of the Cabinet arch" – the President is only the formal head.

=== Articles 52–78 and President vs Vice-President
- **Arts. 52–78 = Part V, Chapter I – the Union Executive** (Chapter II = Parliament; Part VI = the States).
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| 52 / 53 | There shall be a President / executive power of the Union vested in him |
| 54 / 55 | Election (electoral college) / manner of election |
| 56 / 57 / 58 | Term / re-election / qualifications |
| 59 / 60 / 61 / 62 | Conditions of office / oath / impeachment / time of election to fill vacancy |
| 63 / 64 | There shall be a Vice-President / VP ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha |
| 65 | **Vice-President to act as President** |
| 66 / 67 / 68 | Election of VP / term and removal of VP / time of election to fill VP vacancy |
| 71 | Election disputes (Supreme Court) |
| 72 | Pardon |
| 74 / 75 | Council of Ministers to aid and advise / other provisions – **75(1A)** Council not above **15%** of Lok Sabha (**91st Amendment, 2003**) |
| 76 / 77 / 78 | Attorney General / conduct of business / **PM's duty to keep the President informed** |
- **President vs Vice-President** – the most-tested differences:
| Basis | President | Vice-President |
|---|---|---|
| Electors | Elected MPs + elected MLAs | **All MPs, elected and nominated** (no MLAs) |
| Qualified for | Lok Sabha | **Rajya Sabha** |
| Nomination | 50 + 50 | **20 + 20** |
| Removal | Impeachment (Art. 61) | **Rajya Sabha resolution agreed to by the Lok Sabha** |
| Resigns to | Vice-President | **President** |
| Part of Parliament | Yes | No |
| Vacancy | Election within 6 months | **No six-month deadline** (Art. 68) |

- **2022 VP election**: electoral college of **788** members.

=== Veto, Ordinance and Pardon – Summary (details in row 26)
| Veto | Meaning | Indian President? |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute | Withholds assent – private members' Bills; Government Bills after the Cabinet resigns | Yes |
| Suspensive | Returns once; must assent if re-passed – **not for Money Bills** | Yes |
| Pocket | Keeps the Bill pending – the Constitution fixes **no time limit** | Yes |
| Qualified | Overridable by a higher majority (USA) | **No** |
- **Constitution Amendment Bill**: must give assent (**24th Amendment, 1971**). The **2025 Presidential Reference** (opinion of 20 November 2025) held courts cannot fix timelines or create "deemed assent".
- **Ordinance (Art. 123)**: only when Parliament (either House) is not in session; must be laid and **lapses six weeks after reassembly**; may be **withdrawn at any time**; subject to **judicial review**. **D.C. Wadhwa (1987)**: re-promulgation in Bihar a fraud on the Constitution; **Krishna Kumar Singh (2017)**: **seven judges**, laying is mandatory.
- **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026** (President Droupadi Murmu): SC judges **33 → 37, excluding the CJI** (38 with the CJI).
| Pardon term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pardon | Removes both sentence and conviction |
| Commutation | Lighter form (death → rigorous life imprisonment) |
| Remission | Shorter period, same character |
| Respite | Lesser sentence for special reasons (pregnancy, disability) |
| Reprieve | Temporary stay of execution |
- **Art. 72** (President): **court-martial**, offences against **Union law**, **all death sentences**; **Art. 161** (Governor): State laws; the Governor **cannot pardon a death sentence or a court-martial** sentence (he may suspend, remit or commute a death sentence).
- **Cases**: **Maru Ram (1980)** – exercised on the Council's advice; **Kehar Singh (1989)** – may examine evidence afresh, **no right of oral hearing**; **Epuru Sudhakar (2006)** – **limited judicial review** on mala fide, arbitrary or irrelevant grounds.
