=== NOTES 2:32 | Parliamentary Devices – Question Hour, Zero Hour, Motions (adjournment, No-confidence, Censure, Calling Attention)
## SUB 0 | Devices of Parliamentary Control
[32:0:0] - Parliamentary hours, motions and committees are the main means of **executive accountability** – controlling the Council of Ministers (flows from **Art 75(3)**).
[32:0:1] - **Accountability triangle**: **Question Hour + Supplementary Questions + Adjournment Motions**.
[32:0:2] - Tools: Question Hour, Zero Hour, half-an-hour and short-duration discussions; calling attention, adjournment, censure, no-confidence motions; committees (Government Assurances, Petitions, Subordinate Legislation, PAC, Estimates); Budget approval; **Motion of Thanks**.
[32:0:3] - **Not a tool**: **judicial review** – it is the judiciary's job.
[32:0:4] - Parliament controls administration mainly through **Parliamentary Committees**.
[32:0:5] - State Legislatures use the same devices plus estimates and public accounts committees.
[32:0:6] TABLE head: Most effective device | Why
  [32:0:6.0] No-confidence motion | Strongest / most powerful weapon – whole CoM resigns (APPSC-G2 2025 key)
  [32:0:6.1] Question Hour | Most effective day-to-day vigil – Ministers must answer
[32:0:7] # Indian innovations
[32:0:8] TABLE head: Device | Year
  [32:0:8.0] Short-duration discussion (Rule 193) | 1953
  [32:0:8.1] Calling attention motion | 1954
  [32:0:8.2] Zero Hour | 1962
[32:0:9] 1953 – 1954 – 1962 = short-duration – calling attention – Zero Hour. Neither Zero Hour nor calling attention is in the Constitution.
## SUB 1 | Question Hour and Zero Hour
[32:1:0] TABLE head: Basis | Question Hour | Zero Hour
  [32:1:0.0] Lok Sabha | 11 AM – 12 Noon (first hour) | 12 Noon – 1 PM (right after QH)
  [32:1:0.1] Rajya Sabha (since 2014) | 12 Noon – 1 PM | 11 AM – 12 Noon
  [32:1:0.2] Nature | Formal, in the Rules of Procedure | Informal Indian innovation (1962); not in the Rules
  [32:1:0.3] Notice | 15 clear days (ordinary questions) | No prior notice – member intimates the Presiding Officer the same day, who decides whom to allow
  [32:1:0.4] Reply | Ministers must answer | Ministers not bound to reply
  [32:1:0.5] Duration | One hour | No time fixed in the Rules; at most one hour
[32:1:1] - Question Hour aim: **accountability of the Council of Ministers**; maximum attendance of members.
[32:1:2] - Zero Hour: urgent matters of public importance; in political jargon "matters that cannot be delayed". Chair's directions go through the **Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs** to the ministry concerned.
[32:1:3] - **Interpellation**: formal right of asking questions of the Executive.
[32:1:4] APPSC-GS 2025; G2 2023, 2025: "time after Question Hour when members raise matters without prior notice" = **Zero Hour**.
[32:1:5] UPPCS 2021 key called Zero Hour "the first hour of the Session" – in the LS it follows Question Hour.

## SUB 3 | Rules of Procedure – Devices and Rule Numbers
[32:3:0] - LS work runs mainly by the **Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business**; the Constitution gives only the framework. **No-confidence motion, Zero Hour, Rule 377** are **not in the Constitution**.
[32:3:1] TABLE head: Rule | Device
  [32:3:1.0] Rule 184 (LS) | Discussion with a formal motion and vote – governments dislike it, losing may cost power
  [32:3:1.1] Rule 193 (LS) | Short-duration discussion, no vote, max 2 hours
  [32:3:1.2] Rule 377 (LS) | Matters not raisable under any other device; written notice to Secretary-General + Speaker's consent; 20 → 30 a day from 27 July 2015
  [32:3:1.3] Rule 180A–E (RS) | Special Mentions
  [32:3:1.4] Rule 198 (LS) | No-confidence motion
  [32:3:1.5] Rule 222 (LS) / Rule 187 (RS) | Privilege motion
  [32:3:1.6] Rules 200–203 (LS) | Removal of Speaker
[32:3:2] - **Half-an-hour discussion**: matter already debated enough but needing **factual elucidation**; no formal motion or vote.
## SUB 4 | Calling Attention vs Adjournment Motion
[32:4:0] TABLE head: Basis | Calling Attention | Adjournment Motion
  [32:4:0.0] Origin | Indian innovation (1954), in the Rules | Traditional device
  [32:4:0.1] House | Either House | Lok Sabha only
  [32:4:0.2] Requirement | Presiding Officer's permission | Presiding Officer's consent + 50 members
  [32:4:0.3] Objective | Authoritative statement from a Minister on an urgent matter | Discuss a definite matter of urgent public importance; sets aside business
  [32:4:0.4] Business | Not disrupted | Suspended; debate at least 2 h 30 min
  [32:4:0.5] Censure | No censure | Involves censure of Government
[32:4:1] - Adjournment motion conditions: single, definite, specific matter of **recent occurrence**; **no privilege question**; **one per sitting**; only when other tools (e.g. short-duration discussion) would be **too late**.
[32:4:2] - **Calling attention notices narrowed the scope of the Adjournment Motion** – not of Question Hour or Zero Hour.
[32:4:3] Adjournment and no-confidence: both **LS only**, both need **50 members**.
## SUB 5 | No-confidence, Confidence and Censure Motions
[32:5:0] # No-confidence motion (Rule 198)
[32:5:1] - Moved by the **Opposition**, **Lok Sabha only**, against the **entire Council of Ministers** – not one minister.
[32:5:2] - Written notice to the **Secretary-General**; **at least 50 members** for leave; **no reasons** needed.
[32:5:3] - **Not in the Constitution** – flows from **Art 75(3)** (collective responsibility to LS).
[32:5:4] - If passed, the **whole CoM resigns**.
[32:5:5] - **Speaker's role**: decides **admissibility** – discretion to admit or reject (APPSC-GS 2025 key); does not resign or dissolve the House.
[32:5:6] - A PM from the RS can take part in LS work but **cannot vote** there, even on a no-confidence motion.
[32:5:7] # Comparison
[32:5:8] TABLE head: Motion | Moved by / where | Reasons | Against | Effect if passed
  [32:5:8.0] No-confidence | Opposition; LS; 50 members | Not needed | Entire CoM | Must resign
  [32:5:8.1] Confidence | Government, to prove majority; LS only | – | – | If defeated, Government falls
  [32:5:8.2] Censure | LS | Must be stated | A minister, a group or the CoM | Not bound to resign – disapproval of policy
[32:5:9] - Censure differs from no-confidence in aim and procedure; no separate "leave of the House".
[32:5:10] # Instances
[32:5:11] - **First no-confidence motion**: **J.B. Kripalani** against **Nehru** (**1963**).
[32:5:12] - **AP angle – Andhra State, Nov 1954**: **Gouthu Latchanna's** no-confidence motion passed **69–68** – **Prakasam ministry fell by one vote** after 13 months; President's Rule 16 Nov 1954 – 27 Mar 1955.
[32:5:13] - **G.V. Mavalankar** – first Speaker to face a removal motion.
## SUB 6 | Privilege and Cut Motions
[32:6:0] - **Privilege motion**: against a Minister **withholding or distorting facts**; **either House**; Presiding Officer's consent; referred to the **Committee of Privileges**; does not force resignation.
[32:6:1] - **Cut motions** (LS, on **demands for grants** – Union Budget):
[32:6:2] TABLE head: Cut | Amount | Purpose
  [32:6:2.0] Policy cut | Reduce to Re 1 | Disapproval of policy
  [32:6:2.1] Economy cut | By a specified amount | Economy
  [32:6:2.2] Token cut | By Rs 100 | Specific grievance
## SUB 7 | Resolutions and the Whip
[32:7:0] # Resolutions
[32:7:1] - Self-contained proposal; **always a substantive motion**; **must be put to vote**; mover cannot withdraw without **leave of the House**; same subject **barred for one year**.
[32:7:2] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [32:7:2.0] Private member's | Alternate Fridays, afternoon
  [32:7:2.1] Government | Moved by a minister – e.g. approving treaties
  [32:7:2.2] Statutory | Under a constitutional/statutory provision
[32:7:3] # Office of Whip
[32:7:4] - **Not in the Constitution, the Rules or any statute** – rests on **parliamentary conventions** (APPSC-G1 2024 key).
[32:7:5] - Each party appoints a whip/chief whip to secure attendance and party-line voting; the **Minister of Parliamentary Affairs** is the government's Chief Whip and keeps contact with Chief Whips in both Houses.
[32:7:6] - Defying the whip on a vote → **Tenth Schedule** disqualification.
[32:7:7] TABLE head: Whip | Meaning
  [32:7:7.0] One-line | Informative
  [32:7:7.1] Two-line | Direction to attend
  [32:7:7.2] Three-line | Strict voting direction
## SUB 8 | Closures, Point of Order, Yielding the Floor
[32:8:0] TABLE head: Device | Meaning
  [32:8:0.0] Closure motion | Cuts short debate and puts the matter to vote – four kinds
  [32:8:0.1] Simple closure | "Matter sufficiently discussed; be put to vote"
  [32:8:0.2] Closure by compartments | Clauses grouped; debate and vote part by part
  [32:8:0.3] Kangaroo closure | Only important clauses debated; others taken as passed
  [32:8:0.4] Guillotine | Allotted time ends – all remaining clauses/demands, even undiscussed, put to vote (LS)
  [32:8:0.5] Point of order | Raised when proceedings depart from rules/Constitution; no debate; Presiding Officer rules
  [32:8:0.6] Yielding the floor | Member speaking makes way for another member or the Chair; the Speaker can direct it (convention)
[32:8:1] Kangaroo = only important clauses debated; guillotine = undiscussed clauses voted when time ends.
