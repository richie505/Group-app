=== Zero Hour and Question Hour – Timing, Nature, Duration
| Basis | Question Hour | Zero Hour |
|---|---|---|
| Lok Sabha timing | **11 AM – 12 Noon** – the **first hour** of every sitting | **12 Noon onwards** – **immediately after Question Hour**, till the regular agenda (business) of the day is taken up |
| Rajya Sabha (since 2014) | 12 Noon – 1 PM | **11 AM** – the RS day begins with Zero Hour |
| Basis | Formal – in the **Rules of Procedure** | **Informal** – **not in the Rules of Procedure** and not in the Constitution |
| Origin | Traditional (Westminster) device | **Indian innovation**, in use **since 1962** – not borrowed from the UK |
| Notice | **15 clear days** for an ordinary question | No advance notice like a question; member **intimates the Presiding Officer the same day** (LS notices balloted on the day) |
| Reply | Ministers must answer | Ministers **not bound to reply** |
| Duration | One hour | **Not fixed (unspecified)** – lasts until the agenda is taken up; in practice about an hour |
- **Zero Hour – definition**: the **time gap between the Question Hour and the agenda** (regular business); members raise **matters of urgent public importance** – "members are free to raise any matter that they think is important". Not "the first hour", not "the hour before Question Hour", not "when no members are present", not a lunch break.
- **Formally prescribed?** Zero Hour is the device **not formally prescribed** in the Rules – Calling Attention, Half-an-hour and Short-duration discussions are all in the Rules.
- **Indian contribution** to parliamentary procedure: **Zero Hour** (also Calling Attention, 1954) – not cut motion, adjournment motion or guillotine (British devices).
- **Ballot**: in the Lok Sabha **20 matters a day**, in order of their priority in the ballot, may be raised in Zero Hour (Lok Sabha Bulletin Part II).
- Directions given by the Chair in Zero Hour reach the ministry concerned through the **Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs**.
- **Question Hour aim**: **accountability of the Council of Ministers**; maximum attendance; asking and answering questions addressed to Ministers. **Interpellation** = the formal right of members to ask questions of the Executive.
- APPSC G1 2003 / 2008, G2, UPPCS 2021, SSC 2019: Zero Hour = **the time immediately following Question Hour**.
> Exam angle: "maximum duration of Zero Hour" = **unspecified / indefinite** (no time fixed in the Rules; some keys say "one hour"); statements – "in the Rules ✗, from UK ✗, starts after QH till agenda ✓".

=== No-confidence, Confidence and Censure Motions
# No-confidence motion (Rule 198)
- **Not mentioned in the Constitution** – flows from **Art. 75(3)** (Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha**). The Constitution mentions **neither** a confidence nor a no-confidence motion.
- Moved **only in the Lok Sabha** (not the Rajya Sabha, not "both Houses", not a committee) – an **exclusive power of the Lok Sabha** (UPSC 2022: Emergency approval and impeachment need both Houses).
- Against the **entire Council of Ministers / the Government** – not the PM alone, an individual minister or the Speaker.
- Leave needs the support of **at least 50 members**; written notice to the **Secretary-General**; **no reasons** need be stated.
- **Speaker** decides admissibility – **discretion to admit or reject**; he does not resign or dissolve the House.
- If passed, the **whole Council of Ministers must resign**. "Six months must lapse between two motions" and "100 members" are **false**.
- **Most powerful weapon** for executive accountability = the **no-confidence motion** (NextGen); the power to dismiss the government is real only when coalitions break (post-1989).
- A PM who is a Rajya Sabha member **cannot vote** in the Lok Sabha, even on a no-confidence motion against his own government (UPSC 1997).
- **First** no-confidence motion: **J.B. Kripalani** against **Nehru** (**1963**).
- **AP angle**: Andhra State, Nov 1954 – **Gouthu Latchanna's** no-confidence motion carried **69–68**; **Prakasam ministry fell by one vote**; President's Rule from **November 1954**.
# Comparison
| Motion | Moved by / where | Reasons | Against | If passed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **No-confidence** | Opposition; **Lok Sabha only**; 50 members | **Not needed** | **Entire CoM** | CoM **must resign** |
| **Confidence** | **Government (Council of Ministers)**, to prove majority; Lok Sabha | – | – | If **defeated**, the Government **falls** |
| **Censure** | Lok Sabha | **Must be stated** | A minister, a group or the whole CoM | **Not bound to resign** – disapproval of policies |
- **Censure motion is different from the no-confidence motion** (aim, reasons, effect).
- The motion the **Council of Ministers can move** = **Confidence Motion** (no-confidence, censure and adjournment come from members).

=== Instruments of Parliamentary Control over the Executive
- NextGen lists four instruments: **(1) deliberation and discussion, (2) approval or refusal of laws, (3) financial control, (4) no-confidence motion**. A **walkout** is a protest, **not** an instrument.
- Deliberation includes **Question Hour, Zero Hour, half-an-hour discussion, adjournment motion** etc.
- **Accountability triangle**: **Question Hour + Supplementary Questions + Adjournment Motions** – UPSC 2017: all three (1, 2 and 3) are ways Parliament controls the Council of Ministers.
- **Most effective day-to-day vigil**: **Question Hour** – "perhaps the most effective method of keeping vigil on the executive" (NextGen).
- UPSC 2001: Parliament controls the administration **through Parliamentary Committees** – not Consultative Committees (set up by the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs), periodic reports or writs (a court's power).
- **Not a tool**: **dissolution of the House** (an executive power), **judicial review** (judiciary), **walkout**; the **Budget session** is only a period of sitting, and bills are formulated by the executive.
- Other tools: calling attention, censure motion, half-an-hour and short-duration discussions, **Motion of Thanks**, resolutions; State Legislatures also use **estimates and public accounts committees**.

=== Adjournment Motion and Calling Attention Motion
| Basis | Adjournment Motion | Calling Attention Motion |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Draw attention of the House to a **definite matter of urgent public importance** | Call the attention of a **Minister** to an urgent matter and seek an **authoritative statement** |
| Origin | Traditional device | **Indian innovation**, **since 1954**; in the Rules |
| House | **Lok Sabha only** (RS cannot use it) | **Either House** |
| Support | Presiding Officer's consent + **50 members** | Presiding Officer's permission |
| Business | **Interrupts** normal business – "extraordinary device"; debate **at least 2 h 30 min** | Does not disrupt business |
| Censure | **Element of censure** of the Government | **No censure** |
- **Adjournment motion conditions**: definite, factual, urgent, of public importance; **one matter** only; **specific matter of recent occurrence**; **must not raise a question of privilege**; not revive a matter discussed in the session; not sub judice; **not more than one such motion at a sitting**; admissible only when other tools (e.g. short-duration discussion) would be too late. RAS 2018 (Rajasthan Assembly) → **I, III and IV** (privilege statement wrong).
- It is **not** a motion to adjourn the session or the day, to collect information or to cut a demand.
- **Calling Attention notices restricted the scope of the Adjournment Motion** – not of Question Hour or Zero Hour.
- Calling attention rules: introduced **1954**; notices in writing **by 10 AM**; a member may give **not more than two** notices for a sitting – so "not more than one" (MPPSC 2015 statement III) is **wrong**.
- **Peculiar to Indian Legislatures**: **Calling Attention Motion** (with Zero Hour).

=== Closures, Guillotine, Point of Order and Yielding the Floor
| Device | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Closure motion** | Cuts short the debate – **stoppage of debate on a motion** – and puts the matter to vote; four kinds |
| Simple closure | "Matter sufficiently discussed; be now put to vote" |
| Closure by compartments | Clauses grouped into parts; debate and vote part by part |
| **Kangaroo closure** | Only **important clauses** debated and voted; intervening clauses taken as passed |
| **Guillotine** | Allotted time over – **all remaining clauses / demands, discussed or not, put to vote** (in the Budget the Speaker puts all remaining demands to vote without discussion) |
| **Point of order** | Raised when proceedings **depart from the rules** (written or unwritten) or the articles regulating business; **no debate**; Presiding Officer rules |
| **Yielding the floor** | The Speaker asks a member to **stop speaking and let another member speak** (member gives way to another member or the Chair) – not decorum, crossing the floor or interpellation |
- **Guillotine** in loose exam wording = "approving bills / demands **without any discussion**" – not a walkout or a budget cut.
- **Crossing the floor** = changing sides (party); **decorum** = dignified conduct.

| Basis | Starred | Unstarred | Short Notice | To a private member |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mark | **Asterisk** | None | – | – |
| Answer | **Oral** | **Written** (laid on the Table) | **Oral** | As for a minister |

=== Rules of Procedure – Discussions and Rule Numbers
- Day-to-day work of the Lok Sabha runs on the **Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha**; the Constitution gives only the framework. **No-confidence motion, Zero Hour, Rule 377** are **not in the Constitution**.
| Rule | Device |
|---|---|
| **Rule 184** (LS) | Discussion with a **formal motion and voting** – governments dislike it, losing may embarrass or topple them (UPPCS A-R: A and R true, R explains A) |
| **Rule 193** (LS) | **Short-duration (two-hour) discussion** – **no formal motion, no voting**; max **2 hours**; since **1953** |
| **Rule 377** (LS) | **Special mention** – matters that are not points of order and cannot be raised under questions, calling attention or motions; written notice to the Secretary-General + **Speaker's consent** |
| Rules 180A–E (RS) | **Special Mentions** in the Rajya Sabha |
| **Rule 198** (LS) | No-confidence motion |
| Rule 222 (LS) / Rule 187 (RS) | Privilege motion |
- **Rule 377 – matters a day**: **20** in most books and keys (RAS 2018 key); NextGen Rocket says it was raised to **30** a day in 2015.
- **Half-an-hour discussion**: a matter already sufficiently debated whose answer needs **factual elucidation**; Speaker can allot 3 days a week; no formal motion or vote.

=== Motions, Privilege Motion, Resolutions and the Whip
- **Motion** = a **formal proposal by a member** for the decision of the House (to do something permissible); no discussion on a matter of public importance except on a motion with the Presiding Officer's consent.
- Kinds: **substantive** (self-contained, e.g. impeachment), **substitute**, **subsidiary** (ancillary, superseding, amendment).
- **Privilege motion**: moved when a **Minister withholds facts of a case or gives wrong / distorted facts** – breach of privilege; purpose: **censure the minister**; **either House**; referred to the **Committee of Privileges**; does **not** force the Government to resign.
# Resolutions
- Always a **substantive motion**; mover cannot withdraw it except by **leave of the House**; same subject barred for **one year**.
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| Private member's | **Alternate Fridays**, afternoon sitting |
| Government | Moved by a minister – treaties, policies, committee recommendations |
| **Statutory** | Moved under a **constitutional or statutory provision** |
# The Whip
- Office of Whip is **not in the Constitution, the Rules or a statute** – rests on **parliamentary conventions**. The **1998 Act** (Leaders and Chief Whips of Recognised Parties and Groups in Parliament (Facilities) Act) only gives **facilities** – it does not put the office into the Rules.
- **Every party** – ruling or opposition – has its own whips (APPSC G1 2024: **only statement III** valid).
- Government Chief Whip in the Lok Sabha = the (Cabinet) **Minister of Parliamentary Affairs**, who keeps contact with the Chief Whips of parties in both Houses.
- Defying the whip → disqualification under the **Tenth Schedule**.
| Whip | Meaning |
|---|---|
| One-line | Informative |
| **Two-line** | **Direction to attend** |
| **Three-line** | **Vote strictly as the party directs** |
