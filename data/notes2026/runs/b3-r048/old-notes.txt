=== NOTES 3:48 | Agricultural Inputs – Seeds, Fertilisers and Subsidy Regime, Pesticides, Mechanisation, Extension Services
## SUB 0 | Green Revolution – Technology and Timeline
[48:0:0] # Scientific Core and Origins
[48:0:1] - Scientific core: **HYV seeds** (APPSC 2024); package also fertilisers, irrigation, pesticides, mechanisation = **seed-fertiliser-water technology** (late 1960s).
[48:0:2] - **Wheat**: Borlaug's **Mexican dwarf wheats** → **Kalyan Sona, Sonalika**; 1966: **18,000 t** Mexican wheat imported and crossed with local varieties in Punjab; wheat output rose 5 → 17 MT.
[48:0:3] - **Rice**: **IR-8** from **IRRI, Philippines**.
[48:0:4] - **Punjab** = "Granary of India", pioneer State.
[48:0:5] # Timeline
[48:0:6] - **IADP 1960-61**.
[48:0:7] - Droughts **1965-66 and 1966-67**: foodgrains fell to **72.3 and 74.2 MT**; dependence on **PL-480**.
[48:0:8] - HYV programme full-fledged from **1966**, during Annual Plans (1966-69) → agri growth 4.16%.
[48:0:9] - First KVK **1974**; **MSSRF 1988**.
[48:0:10] - APPSC 2023 order: **IADP → end of first Green Revolution period → MSSRF** (II, I, III).
[48:0:11] TABLE head: Phase | Years
  [48:0:11.0] Pre-Green Revolution | 1951-68
  [48:0:11.1] Early Green Revolution | 1968-81
  [48:0:11.2] Later Green Revolution | 1987-92
[48:0:12] # Second Green Revolution
[48:0:13] - Focus: **pulses, oilseeds, millets (Shree Anna)**, precision farming (drones, AI), **micro-irrigation**, integrated nutrient and pest management, climate-resilient varieties; **not more chemical pesticides**.

## SUB 2 | Fertiliser Subsidy Regime
[48:2:0] # Urea and Subsidy Size
[48:2:1] - **Urea** = most subsidised; **statutory price control**; kept cheap → nitrogen overuse; **46% nitrogen**, dissolves easily.
[48:2:2] - Fertiliser subsidy = **second largest after food**.
[48:2:3] - Budget 2026-27: **Rs 1.71 lakh crore** (RE 2025-26: Rs 1.86 lakh crore); may reach ~**Rs 2.41 lakh crore** as the **West Asia crisis** doubled urea prices (Dec 2025-Apr 2026).
[48:2:4] - India: **2nd largest consumer, 3rd largest producer** of fertilisers.
[48:2:5] - National Investment Policy for Urea-2026: adds **10 MT** urea capacity.
[48:2:6] # Reforms
[48:2:7] TABLE head: Reform | Year | Provision
  [48:2:7.0] NBS | 2010 | Fixed per-kg subsidy on N, P, K and S announced yearly; 22 grades of decontrolled P&K; makers set MRPs
  [48:2:7.1] Neem-coated urea | Notified May 2015; all domestic urea from 1 Sep 2015, imported from 1 Dec 2015 | Slows N release; stops diversion of farm urea to industry
  [48:2:7.2] DBT in fertilisers | Pilot October 2016 | Subsidy to companies after sale at PoS devices
  [48:2:7.3] PM-PRANAM | 28 June 2023 | States keep 50% of subsidy saved by cutting chemical fertiliser use
[48:2:8] # Nano Fertilisers
[48:2:9] - **IFFCO Nano Urea**: **500 ml bottle = one 45 kg bag**; foliar spray; ~**80%** absorption.
[48:2:10] - **Nano DAP**: 8% N, 16% P; IFFCO; April 2023.
[48:2:11] # Nutrient Balance
[48:2:12] - **N:P:K = 10.9 : 4.1 : 1** (2023-24) vs ideal **4:2:1** – urea-centred subsidy distorts the ratio; ES suggests acre-based support.
[48:2:13] NBS covers P&K (and S) with fixed subsidy; urea stays under price control.
[48:2:14] - **Largest consuming State:** **Uttar Pradesh** uses the most fertiliser in total quantity (about **51.7 lakh t** of N+P+K nutrients, 2021-22, RBI); Punjab is high per hectare.
## SUB 3 | Soil Health Card Scheme
[48:3:0] - Launched **19 February 2015** at **Suratgarh (Rajasthan)**; part of **NMSA** (Soil Health Management).
[48:3:1] - Card issued **free every two years**; gives soil nutrient status and fertiliser advice.
[48:3:2] - Impact: fertiliser use cut **8-10%**; yields up **5-6%**.
