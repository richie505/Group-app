[50:5:2] # High and Low Productivity States
[50:5:3] - **Punjab**: **highest rice yield** (canal + tubewell irrigation despite low rainfall) and **highest wheat yield** (about 50 q/ha).

[50:5:5] - **Odisha, UP, Bihar** (eastern rice states): below the national average.
[50:5:6] - **Pulses yields**: highest in **MP and Gujarat** (black/semi-arid soils, high SRR).
[50:5:7] - **Highest average cotton yield**: **West Bengal**. **Black (Regur) soil** is best for cotton; covers most of the Deccan Plateau.
## SUB 6 | Yield Concepts, Causes of Gaps and Pathways
[50:6:0] # Terms
[50:6:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [50:6:1.0] Cereal yield | kg per hectare of harvested land under cereals
  [50:6:1.1] Yield gap vs world | Cereals, maize, soybean, pulses below global averages; exception groundnut (semi-arid, better varieties)
  [50:6:1.2] TFP | Output growth not explained by growth in inputs (land, labour, capital, fertiliser)
[50:6:2] # Causes and Determinants
[50:6:3] TABLE head: Factor | Effect / figure
  [50:6:3.0] Irrigation | Rice ~67% irrigated vs pulses ~26%
  [50:6:3.1] Inputs | Skewed NPK, low seed replacement (SRR), low mechanisation
  [50:6:3.2] Post-harvest | ~30% cold-chain losses
  [50:6:3.3] Holding size | Small holdings limit mechanisation
  [50:6:3.4] Climate | ~8% yield fall per 1°C (or 20% rainfall deficit); wheat 6–10%; 1°C wipes ~4 years of rice/wheat gains
  [50:6:3.5] El Niño (pulses) | Acreage −2 to −9%; yield −5 to −25%; output −6 to −30%
[50:6:4] Universal cold-chain access is NOT a cause of the yield gap; the lack of it is.
[50:6:5] # Pathways
[50:6:6] - **Seed**: SRR/VRR and **genome editing**.
[50:6:7] - **Nutrition**: **Soil Health Card**.
[50:6:8] - **Mechanisation**: **Custom Hiring Centres (CHCs)**.
## SUB 7 | Total Factor Productivity
[50:7:0] - TFP = output growth beyond input growth.
[50:7:1] - **Drivers**: research and new varieties, irrigation, rural roads and markets, extension, education.
[50:7:2] - Debate: growth comes **more from area expansion than yield**; growth now led by livestock/fisheries while crop growth (3.5%) lags.
## SUB 8 | Area Trends – Stagnant or Declining Crops
[50:8:0] - **Cotton**: area down **18 lakh ha**; output **290.91 lakh bales (2025–26)** from about 350; Punjab cotton area down **37.5% to 80,000 ha** (2026); India became a **net importer of raw cotton**.
[50:8:1] - **Pulses and oilseeds**: stagnant yields; **soybean area in MP fell 10%** (2023 to 2024).
[50:8:2] - **Telangana**: cultivated area **1.31 crore acres (2014) → 2.2 crore acres (FY23)** via Kaleshwaram and Mission Kakatiya.

=== NOTES 3:53 | Land Reforms – Abolition of Intermediaries, Tenancy Reform, Ceiling Laws, Consolidation; Outcomes
## SUB 0 | Components and Aims of Land Reforms
[53:0:0] TABLE head: Measure | Meaning / outcome
  [53:0:0.0] Abolition of intermediaries | Removing middlemen – the actual cultivator has direct contact with the State
  [53:0:0.1] Tenancy reform | Rent cap 1/4 to 1/6 of produce; security of tenure; ownership to tenants; failed through "voluntary surrenders" and evictions
  [53:0:0.2] Land ceiling | Legal maximum land an individual/family may hold; surplus redistributed to the landless
  [53:0:0.3] Consolidation | Pooling scattered plots
[53:0:1] - Steps for a helpful agrarian structure: abolition of intermediaries, tenancy reforms, ceiling on holdings.
[53:0:2] - Aims: reduce **inequality** (ceilings free surplus land for the landless), agrarian restructuring, agricultural growth; major aim – agricultural land to **all the landless**.
[53:0:3] - First intermediary-abolition Act: **Madras Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act, 1948**.
[53:0:4] **Multiple cropping** is NOT a part of land reforms.
## SUB 1 | Land Ceiling Laws
[53:1:0] TABLE head: Feature | Pre-1972 laws | 1972 national guidelines
  [53:1:0.0] Unit | Individual (fuelled benami transfers to relatives) | Family
  [53:1:0.1] Ceiling | High, varied by State | 10–18 acres two-crop irrigated; 27 single-crop; 54 dry
[53:1:1] - Two rounds: **1950s and early 1970s**; about 21 States had ceiling laws (2020).
[53:1:2] - **Outcome**: **less than 2%** of cultivable area redistributed – due to exemptions for **plantations, orchards, "mechanised" farms**.
## SUB 2 | Tenancy Reform and Operation Barga
[53:2:0] - **Operation Barga** (**West Bengal**, **Left Front**, **1978**): **recording names of sharecroppers (bargadars)** (APPSC 2019); heritable rights and **75%** share of produce.
[53:2:1] - Legal backing: Bengal Land Holding Revenue Act, 1979.
[53:2:2] Rent cap 1/4 to 1/6 of produce; Bhoodan appeal 1/6th of land; bargadar share 75%.
## SUB 3 | Consolidation, Bhoodan and Cooperative Farming
[53:3:0] # Consolidation
[53:3:1] - Succeeded in **Punjab, Haryana, western UP**; little progress in southern and eastern States.
[53:3:2] # Bhoodan-Gramdan
[53:3:3] - **Acharya Vinoba Bhave**, **Pochampally** (Nalgonda, Telangana), **18 April 1951**, amid the **Telangana armed struggle**; led to **Gramdan**.
[53:3:4] - Appeal to donate **1/6th** of land; over **40 lakh acres** pledged, much barren; called the "**Bloodless Revolution**".
[53:3:5] # Cooperative and Joint Farming
[53:3:6] - **Kumarappa Committee (1949)** recommended cooperative farming → **Nagpur Resolution (1959)** made it a party goal; **failed** – captured by rich peasants to evade ceilings.
[53:3:7] - **Charan Singh** did not approve cooperative farming.
[53:3:8] - **Joint cooperative farming features**: land **pooled voluntarily**; produce shared per land/labour contributed.
[53:3:9] **Compulsory membership** is NOT a feature (APPSC G2 2017).
