=== NOTES 3:55 | Agricultural Credit – Institutional Structure, Nabard, Kcc, Priority Sector Lending, Interest Subvention
## SUB 0 | Institutional Structure of Agricultural Credit
[55:0:0] - Agricultural credit flows through **commercial banks, cooperatives and RRBs** (multi-agency approach).
[55:0:1] - Share of **non-institutional** (moneylender) credit in rural credit: about **90% (1950)** to **23.4% (2021-22)**.
[55:0:2] - **Credit by duration:** short-term up to **15 months**; medium-term **15 months to 5 years**; long-term **over 5 years** ⚠ APPSC key (2010) gave medium-term as less than 3 months.
[55:0:3] # Tiers and Who Lends Directly
[55:0:4] TABLE head: Tier / body | Level | Credit
  [55:0:4.0] PACS | Village | Short-term, direct to farmers
  [55:0:4.1] DCCB | District | Short-term, finances PACS
  [55:0:4.2] StCB (e.g. APCOB) | State apex | Short-term, refinanced by NABARD
  [55:0:4.3] Land Development Banks (SCARDB/PCARDB) | State/primary | Long-term (land development, wells, machinery)
  [55:0:4.4] RRBs | District/region | Direct to small farmers, labourers, artisans
  [55:0:4.5] Scheduled commercial banks | – | Direct lending; rural branches 41,464 (2014) to 56,193 (July 2025)
[55:0:5] - Short-term cooperative chain: **StCB (state) – DCCB (district) – PACS (village)**.
[55:0:6] - Cooperative credit structure: State Cooperative Banks, Cooperative Land Development Banks, PACS.
[55:0:7] - **Land Development Banks** (now **Cooperative Agriculture and Rural Development Banks**): part of the **long-term** cooperative structure; quasi-commercial; first at **Jhang, Punjab, 1920**.
[55:0:8] - **Direct credit** to rural households: RRBs and Land Development Banks; **NABARD only refinances**.
[55:0:9] - **SIDBI** = main institution for **MSMEs**, not agriculture.
[55:0:10] - 11 small finance banks operate.
[55:0:11] Not a source of direct finance to farmers = NABARD (refinance). In the exam key, "not a source of direct finance" was **State Bank of India** in one option set; read options carefully.
## SUB 1 | Regional Rural Banks
[55:1:0] - First RRB: **Prathama Grameen Bank, Moradabad (UP)**, **2 October 1975**; **5 RRBs** in 1975.
[55:1:1] - Originating committee: **Working Group on Rural Banks (1975), M. Narasimham**; RRBs part of the multi-agency approach.
[55:1:2] - Sponsored by **Scheduled Commercial Banks**, with **Central and State Government** contributions; **regulated by NABARD**, supported by the sponsor bank.
[55:1:3] - Serve rural and semi-urban areas; mobilise rural resources; lend mainly to **small and marginal farmers, agricultural labourers, rural artisans** (not large industry); supplement commercial banks.
[55:1:4] - RRBs **do not take over agricultural refinance** functions.
[55:1:5] - **Bhandari Committee (1994)**: RRB restructuring; identified **49 RRBs**; more powers to RRB Boards.
[55:1:6] - **One State, One RRB**: **26 RRBs** amalgamated, effective **1 May 2025** (fourth phase); **28 RRBs** remain.
[55:1:7] - RRBs and SFBs face a higher PSL target (**75%**) than commercial banks.
[55:1:8] - **RRB share capital:** Centre 50%, sponsor bank 35%, State Government 15% (50:35:15).

=== NOTES 3:56 | Agricultural Marketing – Apmc, E-nam, Contract Farming, Model Acts, Fpos, Warehousing
## SUB 0 | Apmc System
[56:0:0] - Agricultural markets = **State List**; **APMC Acts are enacted by States**.
[56:0:1] - AP law: **AP (Agricultural Produce and Livestock) Markets Act, 1966** (Act 16 of 1966) – still the operative framework.
[56:0:2] - Set-up order: Government **notifies the area** → **Market Committee** formed → **market yard** set up → run by the committee.
[56:0:3] - Regulated markets: **open auction, standard weights, recorded prices, licensed buyers**; ~**2,477** regulated markets + 4,843 sub-yards.
[56:0:4] - Market fee **1%**; AP AMCs collected ₹429.77 crore in 2025-26 (to Dec 2025).
## SUB 1 | Marketing Problems and Price Spread
[56:1:0] TABLE head: Problem | Effect
  [56:1:0.0] Intermediaries | Raise consumer prices (onion Rs 62/kg in Delhi blamed on hoarding and middlemen)
  [56:1:0.1] APMC charges | Market and licence fees, commission charges, cartels of licensed traders, too few mandis
  [56:1:0.2] Poor storage | ~40% of storage idle → distress sale
  [56:1:0.3] Market density | Swaminathan (NCF): a regulated market within about 5 km
[56:1:1] - **Price spread** = consumer price minus farmer's price (marketing margins and middlemen's share).
[56:1:2] - **Farmer's share in consumer rupee** (RBI studies, Oct 2024): tomato ~**33%**, onion ~36%, potato ~37%; pulses higher – gram ~75%, moong ~70%, tur ~65%. Perishables give farmers a lower share.
## SUB 2 | Grading and Standardisation – Agmark
[56:2:0] - **AGMARK** = quality mark under the **Agricultural Produce (Grading and Marking) Act, 1937** (trap: not "Grading and Marketing").
[56:2:1] - Certifier: **Directorate of Marketing and Inspection (DMI)**, Ministry of Agriculture – **not FCI**.

  [56:3:0.0] Model APMC Act, 2003 | Direct sale to contract sponsors; private markets; farmer-consumer markets
