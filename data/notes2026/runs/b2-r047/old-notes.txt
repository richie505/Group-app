## SUB 0 | Constitutional Framework – Part Xi
[47:0:0] - **Part XI** (Relations between the Union and the States) = **Articles 245-263** (APPSC-GS 2023).
[47:0:1] - Distribution of powers regulated by **Articles 245 and 246**; borrowed from **Canada** (strong Centre) + a third **Concurrent List** (GoI Act 1935 tradition).
[47:0:2] - Power to impose **taxes and fees** also flows from the Seventh Schedule via Art. 246.

## SUB 2 | Territorial Extent – Article 245
[47:2:0] TABLE head: Legislature | Extent
  [47:2:0.0] Parliament | Whole or any part of India; alone can make extra-territorial laws
  [47:2:0.1] State legislature | Own State only; outside reach only with a real territorial nexus
[47:2:1] - **Art. 245(2):** no Parliament law is invalid for **extra-territorial operation** – this is **not a limit**.
[47:2:2] - Parliament makes laws for the Union of India, the Territory of India, and **Indian subjects and their property anywhere** in the world.
[47:2:3] - **Exceptions:**
[47:2:4]   - **Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep:** **President's regulations** have the force of Acts of Parliament.
[47:2:5]   - **Scheduled Areas:** the **Governor** may **bar or modify** Acts of Parliament.
[47:2:6]   - Governor of **Assam** for Assam tribal areas.
[47:2:7] Extra-territorial laws = Parliament only; a State needs territorial nexus.

## SUB 5 | Residuary Powers – Article 248
[47:5:0] - **Article 248** (**Part XI**) + **Entry 97, Union List**: subjects in **no list** – vested **exclusively in Parliament**.
[47:5:1] - Examples: **space research**, **cyber law**, **residuary taxation** (APPSC-G2 2025 key: only the Art. 248 statement correct; the broad "exclusive powers over any item" wording rejected).
[47:5:2] TABLE head: Country | Residuary power with
  [47:5:2.0] India, Canada | Centre
  [47:5:2.1] USA, Australia | States
[47:5:3] - **Sarkaria Commission (1988):** move all residuary powers **except taxation** to the **Concurrent List**.
## SUB 6 | Parliament on the State List – Five Situations
[47:6:0] TABLE head: Article | Trigger | Duration / effect
  [47:6:0.0] 249 | Rajya Sabha resolution, 2/3 of members present and voting, national interest | Resolution up to 1 year, renewable; law ceases 6 months after it lapses
  [47:6:0.1] 250 | National Emergency (Art. 352) | Whole or any part of India; lapses 6 months after Emergency ends
  [47:6:0.2] 252 | Resolutions of two or more States (not one) | Only consenting States; only Parliament amends or repeals
  [47:6:0.3] 253 | International treaties, agreements, conventions | Whole or part of India; no State consent
  [47:6:0.4] 356 | President's Rule | Law continues until the State Legislature alters, repeals or re-enacts it
[47:6:1] # Article 249 – Resolution
[47:6:2] - Majority: **two-thirds of members present and voting** (APPSC-GS 2025); a **special power** of the **Rajya Sabha**.
[47:6:3] - In force **not exceeding one year** (Art. 249(2)); renewable any number of times, **1 year at a time**.
[47:6:4] - State **may still legislate**, but **Parliament's law prevails** in conflict (Art. 251).
[47:6:5] # Article 250 – Emergency
[47:6:6] - During a Proclamation of Emergency, **Parliament** (not a minister) legislates on State List matters for **any part of India** (APPSC-GS 2023).
[47:6:7] - State power **not suspended**; Union law prevails on conflict.
[47:6:8] "Only consenting States" is Article 252, not 250.
[47:6:9] # Article 252 – Consent of States
[47:6:10] TABLE head: Act under Article 252 | Year
  [47:6:10.0] Prize Competition Act | 1955
  [47:6:10.1] Wild Life (Protection) Act | 1972
  [47:6:10.2] Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act | 1974
  [47:6:10.3] Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) Act | 1976
  [47:6:10.4] Transplantation of Human Organs Act | 1994
[47:6:11] - **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** is **not** an Art. 252 law.
[47:6:12] - A request by **one State** is invalid; requesting States lose the power to amend.
[47:6:13] # Article 253 – International Agreements
[47:6:14] - Parliament implements treaties for **whole or any part of India**, even on **State List** subjects, **without the consent of any State** (APPSC-GS 2018).
## SUB 7 | Limits on Parliament and Central Control Over State Laws
[47:7:0] - **State List:** Parliament cannot legislate except under **Arts 249, 250, 252, 253, 356**.
[47:7:1] - **Arts 301-303:** Parliament cannot **prefer or discriminate** between States in trade laws, except in **scarcity**.
[47:7:2] - **State Bill** with **reasonable restrictions on inter-State trade** in public interest needs the **President's previous sanction** (Part XIII, Arts 301-305).
[47:7:3] - **Art. 254(2):** Governor may **reserve** a State Bill for the President (an exception to aid and advice).
[47:7:4] - **Nov 2025 SC advisory opinion:** **rejected deemed assent** and **fixed timelines** for Governors and the President.
## SUB 8 | Repugnancy – Article 254
[47:8:0] - 1. Central vs State law on a **Concurrent** subject.
[47:8:1] - 2. **254(1):** **Union law prevails**; State law **void to the extent of repugnancy**.
[47:8:2] - 3. **254(2):** State law **reserved and assented by the President** prevails **only in that State**.
[47:8:3] - 4. **Proviso:** Parliament can later **override** it by a fresh law – States become "practically powerless".
[47:8:4] Conflict on Concurrent subjects = Article 254 (not 250); Art. 251 covers laws under 249/250.
## SUB 9 | Doctrines of Interpretation
[47:9:0] Most doctrines are **judicially evolved**, not written in the Constitution.
[47:9:1] TABLE head: Doctrine | Meaning | Case
  [47:9:1.0] Pith and substance | True nature of a law decides; incidental encroachment on another List is fine (Art. 246) | Prafulla Kumar v Bank of Commerce (1947, Privy Council); State of Bombay v F.N. Balsara (1951) – prohibition law valid under State List; State of Rajasthan v G. Chawla (1959)
  [47:9:1.1] Colourable legislation | "What cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly" | Kameshwar Singh v State of Bihar (1952); K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo v State of Orissa (1953)
  [47:9:1.2] Harmonious construction | Overlapping entries read together so both operate | —
  [47:9:1.3] Severability | Only the unconstitutional part void, if separable (Art. 13) | A.K. Gopalan (1950); F.N. Balsara (1951)
  [47:9:1.4] Eclipse | Pre-Constitution law conflicting with an FR is dormant; revives if the right is amended | Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v State of M.P. (1955)
  [47:9:1.5] Basic Structure | Parliament cannot destroy basic features | Kesavananda Bharati (1973)
  [47:9:1.6] Judicial Review | Courts test legislative acts and executive actions (Arts 13, 32, 226) | Marbury v Madison (1803, USA)
[47:9:2] Pith and substance = doctrine for Union-State List encroachment.

=== NOTES 2:48 | Distribution of Executive Powers Between the Union and the States
## SUB 0 | Union Executive – President
[48:0:0] - **Art. 52:** there shall be a President of India.
[48:0:1] - **Art. 53(1):** executive power of the Union **vests in the President**, exercised **directly or through subordinate officers**, per the Constitution (so **not unlimited**).
[48:0:2] - **Art. 53(2):** **Supreme Commander** of the armed forces, **regulated by law**.
[48:0:3] - President = **nominal / constitutional head**; **Head of State**, not of Government; **First Citizen**; **Rank 1** in Order of Precedence; part of Parliament (**Art. 79**).
[48:0:4] - Real executive: **Council of Ministers** under the **PM** (**Art. 74(1)**), collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
[48:0:5] - **Art. 77:** all executive action is taken **in the President's name**.
## SUB 1 | Extent of Executive Power
[48:1:0] TABLE head: Article | Level | Provision
  [48:1:0.0] 73 | Union | Matters on which Parliament can legislate + treaty/agreement rights
  [48:1:0.1] 154 | State | Vests in the Governor
  [48:1:0.2] 162 | State | Matters within State Legislature's competence, within the State – heading "Extension of Executive Power of State"
  [48:1:0.3] 163(1) | State | CoM advises the Governor, except discretionary matters
  [48:1:0.4] 371A / 371H | State | Governor's special responsibility for law and order: Nagaland / Arunachal Pradesh
[48:1:1] Art. 154 vests power in the Governor, though real power lies with the Council of Ministers.
[48:1:2] - **Art. 161 remission:** Governor bound by **State Cabinet advice** (SC, Apr 2026).
## SUB 2 | Concurrent List – Who Executes?
[48:2:0] - **Default:** executive power stays with the **States**.
[48:2:1] - **Exception:** Centre executes where the **Constitution or a Parliamentary law** expressly says (proviso to **Arts 73 and 162**).
## SUB 3 | Entrustment and Compliance
[48:3:0] TABLE head: Article | Direction | Condition
  [48:3:0.0] 258(1) | Union → State | President, with the State's consent
  [48:3:0.1] 258(2) | Union → State | Parliament by law, no consent needed
  [48:3:0.2] 258A | State → Union | State entrusts functions with the Union's consent (APPSC-GS 2023)
  [48:3:0.3] 256 / 257(1) | States | Comply with Union laws; must not impede Union executive power
  [48:3:0.4] 365 | Non-compliance | Breakdown of machinery → opens Art. 356
[48:3:1] 258A is NOT about river valley agreements.
## SUB 4 | Emergencies and Executive Relations
[48:4:0] TABLE head: Emergency | Effect
  [48:4:0.0] National (352, via 353) | Union directs any State on any matter; State governments not suspended
  [48:4:0.1] President's Rule (356) | State CoM dismissed; High Court untouched
  [48:4:0.2] Financial (360) | Financial propriety; Money and Financial Bills reserved; salary cuts of State employees
[48:4:1] - Financial Emergency **never used** so far.
[48:4:2] - Criticism: **258(2)** and **365** let the Union act without State consent.
## SUB 5 | Union Territories
[48:5:0] - **Art. 239:** UTs run by the **President** through an **Administrator / Lieutenant Governor**.
[48:5:1] - **69th Amendment, 1991:** Delhi made **National Capital Territory**; **Art. 239AA** – Assembly + Council of Ministers.
[48:5:2] - Outside Delhi Assembly's power: **public order, police, land** (not public health).
[48:5:3] - **Ladakh (2026):** **Art. 371-like safeguards** instead of the Sixth Schedule (Jul); **sui generis** elected body (Sep).
[48:5:4] - **Delhi LG (Mar 2026):** Former US ambassador **Taranjit Singh Sandhu** became Lieutenant Governor of Delhi, replacing **V.K. Saxena**, who moved to **Ladakh** as LG.
[48:5:5] - **Puducherry LG:** **Kiran Bedi**, India's first woman IPS officer, was LG of Puducherry from **May 2016** to **February 2021**.

## SUB 2 | All India Services – Article 312
[49:2:0] # Creation and Existing Services
[49:2:1] - **Art. 312:** Parliament may create a new AIS after a **Rajya Sabha** resolution by **2/3 of members present and voting**; it expressly mentions an **All India Judicial Service**.
[49:2:2] - Existing AIS: **IAS, IPS, IFoS** (**Indian Forest Service, 1966**); governed by the **All India Services Act, 1951**.
[49:2:3] - **ICS → IAS** in 1947; **Sardar Patel** called AIS the "**Steel Frame**" of India.
[49:2:4] - **UPSC** replaced the **Federal PSC** in **1950**.
[49:2:5] - Council of States' exclusive powers: create a new AIS (**Art. 312**); authorise State List laws (**Art. 249**).
[49:2:6] # Recruitment and Control
[49:2:7] - Recruited by **UPSC** on an all-India basis; **trained by the Centre**; serve in **State cadres**.
[49:2:8] - **Appointment** by the **President**, on UPSC recommendation.
[49:2:9] - **Dismissal/removal only by the Union Government** (PYQ 2017, 2019; APPSC-G1 2019).
[49:2:10] # Federal Critique
[49:2:11] - AIS seen as against the **federal principle** and **State autonomy** – Central rules govern them, States lack full control (APPSC-G2 2025: A and R both true, R explains A).
[49:2:12] - **IAS (Cadre) Rules, 1954** amendments: **central deputation** without the consent of the **State or the officer**; Centre's order final after a set time; hurts State bureaucracy's morale.
[49:2:13] # Classify the Service
