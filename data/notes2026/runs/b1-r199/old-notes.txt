## SUB 0 | Causes of the Rise of Nationalism
[199:0:0] - **Western education**, the **press**, a **new middle class**, **socio-religious reform** and **economic exploitation** (India = raw-material supplier + captive market).
[199:0:1] - NOT a cause: hereditary feudal privileges.
[199:0:2] - **Lytton (1876-80)**: lavish **Delhi Durbar 1877** amid the famine; **Vernacular Press Act 1878**; **Arms Act 1878**; **Second Afghan War (1878-80)**.
[199:0:3] - **Ilbert Bill (1883, Ripon)**: let Indian judges try Europeans → European **"White Mutiny"** forced dilution → exposed racial discrimination (APPSC 2018).
[199:0:4] Lytton = Press Act + Arms Act (1878); **Ripon = Ilbert Bill (1883)**.

## SUB 2 | A.o. Hume and the British Founders
[199:2:0] - **Retired ICS officer**; clashed with Lytton → demoted 1879, retired 1882, settled at **Shimla**.
[199:2:1] - Took the idea of local self-government from **Ripon**; closer to **Bombay-Poona** groups than Calcutta leaders.
[199:2:2] - Formed the **Indian (National) Union**; became the INC's **first General Secretary**.
[199:2:3] - Title: **"Father of Indian Ornithology"**.
[199:2:4] - Other British founders: **William Wedderburn, Justice John Jardine**.
## SUB 3 | Founding of the Inc, 1885
[199:3:0] # Origins and Viceroy
[199:3:1] - Private meeting of **17 people** after the **Theosophical Convention at Madras (Dec 1884)**.
[199:3:2] - **Viceroy: Lord Dufferin (1884-88)** (UPPSC 2025) – not Lytton.
[199:3:3] - **Dufferin's attitude**: at first backed Hume's annual-forum idea → soon cooled → mocked the Congress as a **"microscopic minority" (1888)**. No proof he proposed the INC.
[199:3:4] - Name **"Indian National Congress"** credited to **Dadabhai Naoroji**.
[199:3:5] # First Session
[199:3:6] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [199:3:6.0] Dates | 28-31 December 1885
  [199:3:6.1] Venue | Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay
  [199:3:6.2] Venue shift | From Poona, due to cholera (not plague)
  [199:3:6.3] Delegates | 72 – 54 Hindus, 2 Muslims, plus Jains and Parsis; mainly lawyers, journalists, teachers
  [199:3:6.4] President | W.C. Bonnerjee
  [199:3:6.5] General Secretary | A.O. Hume
[199:3:7] # Aims and Demands
[199:3:8] - Aims: **national unity**, ending **caste, sectarian and regional biases**, framing public demands.
[199:3:9] - **Social issues** were kept off the Congress platform at first.
[199:3:10] - Four demands: **simultaneous ICS exams** in England and India; **elected councils**; **against the annexation of Upper Burma**; **cut in military spending**. NOT complete provincial autonomy.
[199:3:11] - Early demand: **separation of Executive and Judiciary**.
[199:3:12] - **2nd session, Calcutta 1886**: **436 delegates** (some notes give 408, with 21 from Andhra); Provincial Congress Committees set up.
[199:3:13] 72 delegates (1885) vs 436 (1886)
## SUB 4 | Safety-valve Theory
[199:4:0] - **Claim**: Hume and Dufferin created the INC as a safe outlet for educated Indians' anger, to prevent another **1857**.
[199:4:1] - Basis: **seven volumes of secret reports** Hume read at Simla in **1878**.
[199:4:2] TABLE head: Person | View
  [199:4:2.0] William Wedderburn | First mention – biography of Hume (1913)
  [199:4:2.1] Lala Lajpat Rai | First user – Young India (1916), against the Moderates
  [199:4:2.2] R.P. Dutt (Marxist) | "Conspiracy" theory – INC made to head off a popular uprising
  [199:4:2.3] Bipan Chandra | Critic – a "colonial myth"; Hume a "lightning conductor"; wrote India's Struggle for Independence
  [199:4:2.4] G.K. Gokhale | No Indian could have started it (lightning-conductor view also credited to him)
[199:4:3] - **Lightning conductor**: Indian leaders used a Briton (Hume) to shield the infant body from official hostility.
[199:4:4] Wedderburn (1913) = first mention; Lajpat Rai (1916) = first user; Bipan Chandra = lightning conductor.
