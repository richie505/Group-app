[0:1:5] # Rudradaman – Junagadh (Girnar)
[0:1:6] - **Junagadh rock inscription of Rudradaman I** (c. 150 CE): **first long inscription in chaste Sanskrit**, **earliest specimen of Kavya style**; Brahmi; records his **achievements**, his conflict with the Satavahanas and his **repair of the Sudarshana lake without forced labour**.
[0:1:7] - **Sudarshana lake:** built under **Chandragupta Maurya** by governor **Pushyagupta**, canals under **Ashoka** (Tushaspha) → the inscription **names both Chandragupta and Ashoka** and **proves Chandragupta's sway over western India**.
[0:1:8] - **Same rock** also carries **Ashoka's 14 major rock edicts** (boulder near Uparkot fort) and a **Skandagupta** record (Parnadatta repaired the lake).
[0:1:9] - **⚠ Trap:** Rudradaman = **Junagadh**, not Nanaghat (Naganika).
[0:1:10] # Ashoka's edicts – what they mention
[0:1:11] - **Maski** (Karnataka): first edict naming "**Asoka**" (minor rock edict).
[0:1:12] - **Kalinga war:** told in **Major Rock Edict XIII** (not at Maski); Hathigumpha records Kharavela's own wars, not Ashoka's victory.
[0:1:13] - **Southern kingdoms named (RE II, XIII):** **Chola, Pandya, Satiyaputra, Keralaputra** – the **Satavahanas are NOT mentioned** (they rose after Ashoka).
[0:1:14] - **13th Major Rock Edict:** lists the **Andhras** among peoples inside the Mauryan empire.
[0:1:15] - **Yerragudi** (Kurnool): proves Mauryan rule in Andhra.

## SUB 7 | Types of Sources and Dating Methods
[0:7:0] - Three groups: **archaeological** (inscriptions, coins, monuments, sites), **literary** (religious and secular texts) and **foreign accounts**.
[0:7:1] - **Inscriptions** are the **most reliable** source; foreign accounts only **supplement** Indian sources.
[0:7:2] - **Earliest land-grant records:** **Satavahana**.
[0:7:3] TABLE head: Method | Principle
  [0:7:3.0] Stratigraphy | Superposition: lower layer is older
  [0:7:3.1] Radiocarbon (C-14) | Decay of C-14 in organic remains; half-life about 5,730 years
  [0:7:3.2] Dendrochronology | Counting annual tree rings
  [0:7:3.3] Thermoluminescence | Light given on heating pottery/burnt material

=== NOTES 1:1 | Sources for the History of Andhra – Inscriptions, Coins, Literature and Foreign Accounts
## SUB 0 | Satavahana Inscriptions – Language, Script and Issuers
[1:0:0] # Language and script – the most-asked fact
[1:0:1] - **All Satavahana inscriptions:** **Prakrit language, Brahmi script** (G2 2002; APPSC PYQ ×2) – not "Sanskrit language, Prakrit script" or "Brahmi language".
[1:0:2] - **Royal language:** **Prakrit** – the administrative, business, written and royal language (G2 2011); Sanskrit and Telugu are distractors.
[1:0:3] - **Script:** **Brahmi** (not Kharosthi, Greek or Aramaic).

## SUB 1 | Mauryan and Pre-Satavahana Records – Erragudi, Bhattiprolu, Hathigumpha
[1:1:0] # Earliest records of Andhra
[1:1:1] - **Earliest inscriptions on Andhra history:** Ashoka's edicts at **Yerragudi (Erragudi)** and **Rajula Mandagiri** (Kurnool) – **Prakrit** language, Brahmi script (G1 1984, 1987).
[1:1:2] - **Proof of Mauryan rule in Andhra:** **Yerragudi** inscription (not Maski, which is in Karnataka).
[1:1:3] - **Erragudi:** Ashoka tells **teachers to inculcate Dhamma in pupils** (Welfare Officers flashcard).
[1:1:4] # Bhattiprolu (Guntur)
[1:1:5] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [1:1:5.0] Date | 3rd c. BC, relic caskets of the oldest stupa in AP
  [1:1:5.1] King | Raja Kubhiraka (Kubheraka / Kuberaka), a local pre-Satavahana ruler
  [1:1:5.2] Body named | Nigama-sabha (town council / guild assembly) (G2 2011 ×2)
  [1:1:5.3] Other | Among the oldest Prakrit inscriptions of Andhra; Roman coins found
[1:1:6] - **⚠ Trap:** Nanaghat, Erragudi and Amaravati do not mention the Nigama-sabha; Ashoka, Mahadeva and Sudarshana did not rule Bhattiprolu.
[1:1:7] # Kharavela and the Krishna
[1:1:8] - **Hathigumpha inscription:** in his 2nd year Kharavela sent an army west, ignoring **Satakarni**, to the **Kannabenna** river (= **Krishna**) and attacked the Mushika city.

## SUB 5 | Copper-plate Grants and Land Grants
[1:5:0] - **Donor** = king; **donee** = Brahmins; gift of a **village/land**, often with **tax exemptions**.
[1:5:1] - **Earliest surviving land grant with fiscal exemptions:** the **Satavahanas** (Naneghat; Gautamiputra's Nasik grants) – not the Guptas (Asst. Tribal Welfare Officer PYQ).
[1:5:2] - **Vishnukundin** grants measured land in **nivartanas**.
[1:5:3] - Examples: Mayidavolu (Pallava), Kondamudi (Brihatphalayana).

=== NOTES 1:2 | Stone Age Cultures – Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic, Chalcolithic Sites of India
## SUB 0 | Neolithic – First Farming, Pit Dwellings, Dog Burials, Ash Mounds
[2:0:0] # First farming
[2:0:1] - **Cultivation of cereals began in the Neolithic.**
[2:0:2] - **Mehrgarh** (Baluchistan, c. **7000 BCE**): **earliest agriculture** (wheat, barley) and settled life in the subcontinent; of dated options "7000 BC" is the start of farming.
[2:0:3] - **Rice:** **Lahuradeva** (UP, c. **6500 BCE**, oldest paddy); **Koldihwa** (Belan valley, UP) – earlier thought oldest; sequence **Neolithic–Chalcolithic–Iron**.
[2:0:4] # Site table
[2:0:5] TABLE head: Site | Region | Distinctive find
  [2:0:5.0] Burzahom | Kashmir (16 km NW of Srinagar) | Pit dwellings; dog buried with master
  [2:0:5.1] Gufkral | Kashmir | Pit dwellings (with Burzahom)
  [2:0:5.2] Chirand | Bihar | Bone and antler tools
  [2:0:5.3] Koldihwa | Belan valley, UP | Early rice
  [2:0:5.4] Sanganakallu | Ballari, Karnataka | Ash mound (also Utnur, Piklihal, Kupgal, Budihal)
  [2:0:5.5] Tekkalakota | Ballari, Karnataka | Hill-dwellers; excavated by M. S. Nagaraja Rao
  [2:0:5.6] Brahmagiri, Maski | Karnataka | Neolithic–megalithic sites
[2:0:6] - **Ash mounds:** burnt dung of cattle pens = seasonal camps of Neolithic herdsmen.
[2:0:7] - **Eastern Neolithic** (Birbhanpur, Chirand, Kuchai, Sankarjang): **pointed-butt celts** and **shouldered axes**.
[2:0:8] - **Pit dwellings** are NOT at Mehrgarh (mud-brick houses), Pallavaram (Palaeolithic tools) or Balakot (Harappan).

## SUB 1 | Mesolithic – Domestication, Burials and Ganga–Vindhya Sites
[2:1:0] # Most-asked facts
[2:1:1] - **Animal domestication began in the Mesolithic:** earliest evidence at **Adamgarh** (Narmadapuram, MP) and **Bagor** (Bhilwara, Rajasthan) – not Langhnaj, Birbhanpur or Chopani Mando.
[2:1:2] - **Mahadaha** (Pratapgarh, UP): **bone and antler ornaments** (earrings, necklaces), bone implements, double burials.
[2:1:3] - **Damdama** (Pratapgarh): 41 graves, five double burials and one **triple burial** (three skeletons in one grave).
[2:1:4] - **Sarai Nahar Rai** (Pratapgarh): a grave with **four** skeletons.
[2:1:5] - **Lekhahia** (Mirzapur, Vindhyas): **17 human skeletons** from rock shelter 1 – the most from any Vindhyan rock shelter.
[2:1:6] # Sites and regions
[2:1:7] TABLE head: Site | Region | Note
  [2:1:7.0] Bagor | Bhilwara, Rajasthan | Rock shelters, microliths, early domestication
  [2:1:7.1] Adamgarh | Narmadapuram, MP | Rock shelter, early domestication
  [2:1:7.2] Langhnaj | Gujarat | Microliths, burials
  [2:1:7.3] Sarai Nahar Rai, Mahadaha, Damdama | Ganga plain, Pratapgarh (UP) | Lake-side camps, burials
  [2:1:7.4] Lekhahia, Baghai Khor, Morahana Pahar, Chopani Mando | Vindhyas / Belan valley (Mirzapur, UP) | Rock shelters; Chopani Mando – huts, microliths
  [2:1:7.5] Paisra | Bihar | –
  [2:1:7.6] Birbhanpur | West Bengal | –
  [2:1:7.7] Teri sites | Thoothukudi (TN) and Visakhapatnam coast | Coastal red-sand dunes
[2:1:8] - **West to east:** **Mahadaha → Lekhahia → Paisra → Birbhanpur**.
[2:1:9] > Mesolithic = microlithic stage; tools made by **flaking and chipping**.

## SUB 2 | Bhimbetka and Prehistoric Rock Art
[2:2:0] - **Bhimbetka:** foothills of the Vindhyas, **Raisen** district near Bhopal (MP); discovered by **V. S. Wakankar in 1957-58**; **UNESCO World Heritage Site 2003**.
[2:2:1] - **Most rock paintings in India** (up to 20 layers in places); Upper Palaeolithic art in about **300** caves and shelters; continuous use from Lower Palaeolithic to medieval times.
[2:2:2] - Paintings are **Upper Palaeolithic and Mesolithic** – hunting scenes, animals, geometric patterns; Ajanta, Ellora, Bagh are historic-period cave art, not prehistoric.
[2:2:3] - Most commonly painted animal: **deer**. A **petroglyph** is **carved** into rock; a painting is applied on the surface.
[2:2:4] - 12 May 2026 study: after contact with the Chalcolithic farmers of **Malwa**, the Bhimbetka style became **static**.
[2:2:5] - H. D. Sankalia, S. S. Nigam, R. Pandey, M. S. Vats, V. N. Misra are distractors – the discoverer is **Wakankar**.
[2:2:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.59-61; app notes 1:2 (sub 6)
## SUB 3 | Chalcolithic Cultures – Ahar, Kayatha, Malwa, Jorwe
[2:3:0] - **Chalcolithic = Copper Age** (copper + stone tools); **first metal used = copper**.
[2:3:1] - The **Chalcolithic** people are credited with the first wide use of **painted pottery** (a few Neolithic sites had some).
[2:3:2] TABLE head: Culture | Sites | Region | Date | Pottery / feature
  [2:3:2.0] Ahar-Banas | Ahar, Gilund, Balathal | Mewar, Banas valley (Rajasthan) | c. 2800/2100–1500 BCE | White-painted black-and-red ware; copper smelting
  [2:3:2.1] Kayatha | Kayatha (Chambal) | MP | 2450–1700 BCE | Chocolate-slipped ware
  [2:3:2.2] Malwa | Navdatoli (Narmada), Eran | Madhya Pradesh | 1900–1400 BCE | Malwa ware; Navdatoli dug by H. D. Sankalia
  [2:3:2.3] Jorwe | Jorwe, Nevasa, Daimabad, Inamgaon | Maharashtra (nuclear zone Pravara–Godavari) | 1400–700 BCE | Painted red ware (black-on-red)
[2:3:3] - **Contemporaries of the Harappans:** **Ahar-Banas** and Kayatha; Malwa and Jorwe are later. Order of start: Ahar → Kayatha → Malwa → Jorwe.
[2:3:4] - **Burials in Maharashtra:** dead buried in urns **under the house floor, north to south**.
[2:3:5] - **Daimabad** (Pravara): largest Jorwe site; **bronze chariot**, buffalo, elephant, rhinoceros; **symbolic burial**; southernmost Late Harappan site.
[2:3:6] - **Jorwe end:** most sites abandoned gradually after c. 1000 BCE (drought); Inamgaon, Prakash continued (Late Jorwe) – "**suddenly deserted**" is the wrong statement (CDS 2012).
[2:3:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.63-74; examrobot CDS-I 2012 key; app notes 1:2 (sub 5)
## SUB 4 | Stone Age Framework – Phases, Proto-history, Early Man
[2:4:0] - **Order:** Lower → Middle → Upper Palaeolithic → Mesolithic → Neolithic → Chalcolithic → Iron Age (Megalithic).
[2:4:1] - **Phases are defined by tool type and technology**; tools varied by region and cultures did **not** evolve uniformly or unilinearly.
[2:4:2] - **Three-Age system** (Stone, Bronze, Iron): **C. J. Thomsen** (Copenhagen museum); "Palaeolithic/Neolithic" coined by Lubbock.
[2:4:3] - **Proto-history:** writing exists but is undeciphered – the **Indus civilisation** is **proto-historic**.
[2:4:4] - **Early man:** **Narmada Man** skull found by **Arun Sonakia, 1982** at **Hathnora** (Narmada valley, MP) – earliest human fossil of India; Bori (Maharashtra) artefacts c. 1.38 million years.
[2:4:5] - **Cro-Magnon** = anatomically modern humans of the Upper Palaeolithic – later than Neanderthals and Heidelberg man.
[2:4:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.26-27, p.45-47; Britannica (Three-Age system, Cro-Magnon)
[2:4:7] - **Wheel:** first invented c. **3500 BC** (Mesopotamia); in India solid-wheeled carts and toy carts appear with the Harappans.
[2:4:8] - **Why so many Stone Age sites in the South:** the Deccan and Andhra hills had **abundant stone (quartzite) for tools** and rocky terrain that preserves sites, while thick Gangetic alluvium buries them.
## SUB 5 | Megaliths – Nature, Iron Age and New Finds
[2:5:0] - **Megaliths:** graves with weapons, tools and pottery, marked by **big stones** (mega = large, lith = stone) – **burial sites** away from homes; not microliths, chalcoliths or monoliths.
[2:5:1] - Belong to the **Iron Age**, with **black-and-red ware**; c. 1000 BCE – 2nd century BCE (PMF).
[2:5:2] - Earliest AMS-dated iron: **Mayiladumparai** (Krishnagiri, TN) – **2172 BCE**.
[2:5:3] - **Bihar:** first documentation of a megalithic site (July 2019) in the **Kaimur hills** (Hurmeta menhirs).
[2:5:4] - **Kerala:** rock-cut burial chamber at **Panayal** (Kasaragod).
[2:5:5] - 21 Jul 2026: **Karivalamvanthanallur** (Tenkasi, TN) passed straight from **Microlithic to Iron/Early Historic**, with no Neolithic or Chalcolithic.
[2:5:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.27-28; rohtas.nic.in (megalithic tombs); app notes 1:2 (sub 8-9)
## SUB 6 | Palaeolithic – Tool Techniques and Sites
[2:6:0] TABLE head: Stage | Technique / tools | Sites
  [2:6:0.0] Lower | Acheulian = handaxe-cleaver; Soanian = chopper-chopping | Attirampakkam (TN), Hunsgi and Isampur (Karnataka), Didwana (Rajasthan), Sohan valley (Pakistan Punjab), Belan valley
  [2:6:0.1] Middle | Flake tools; prepared-core Levallois technique; scrapers, borers, points | Nevasa, Bhimbetka, Narmada valley; Sembur (TN)
  [2:6:0.2] Upper | Blades, burins, bone tools | Kurnool caves, Jwalapuram, Patne, Baghor
[2:6:1] - **Attirampakkam** is an **Old Stone Age** (Lower Palaeolithic, Acheulian) site – not "Middle Stone Age" (Mesolithic).
[2:6:2] - **Levallois** = Middle Palaeolithic prepared-core flake technique (Sembur); "Lacoid/Placoid/Hollois" are not techniques. No Neanderthal fossil has been found in India.
[2:6:3] - Middle Palaeolithic = **flake** stage, not the microlithic stage.
[2:6:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.47-50; app notes 1:2 (sub 1)

=== NOTES 1:3 | Pre-historic Cultures of Andhra – Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic and Megalithic Sites
## SUB 0 | Palaeolithic Andhra – Billa Surgam, Kurnool Caves and Sites
[3:0:0] # Billa Surgam and the Kurnool caves (most asked)
[3:0:1] - **Billa Surgam caves:** K. K. Kottala village, **Betamcherla** mandal, **Kurnool** (now Nandyal) district – **not Anantapuramu**; limestone caves; **Old Stone Age** site.
[3:0:2] - Explored by **Robert Bruce Foote** (1884) – the "Father of Indian prehistory", who also found the first Palaeolithic tool at **Pallavaram** (Chennai); **stone and rare bone tools** with Pleistocene animal fossils.
[3:0:3] - **Kurnool caves** and **Muchatla Chintamani Gavi**: the **only Indian sites with bone tools in an Upper Palaeolithic context**.
[3:0:4] - **Belum Caves** (Kolimigundla, Nandyal): **second-longest** cave system in India.
[3:0:5] - **Betamcherla** = famous for **prehistoric caves** (not temples or a dam).
[3:0:6] - Distractors in the G2 2017 question: **Keesarapalli** (Chalcolithic), **Utnur** (Neolithic ash mound), **Revarala** (megalithic menhir).
[3:0:7] # Sites by district
[3:0:8] TABLE head: District | Palaeolithic sites
  [3:0:8.0] Kurnool | Billa Surgam, Betamcherla, Veerapuram, Sangameswaram, Jwalapuram (Upper Palaeolithic)
  [3:0:8.1] Prakasam | Giddaluru, Dornala, Yerragondapalem (well-made quartzite blades), Nandikanuma
  [3:0:8.2] Guntur / Krishna valley | Nagarjunakonda, Karampudi, Chejarla
  [3:0:8.3] Chittoor | Renigunta (Upper Palaeolithic blade-and-burin industry), Gudimallam
  [3:0:8.4] Anantapur | Guntakal (bone implements), Lattavaram, Velpulamadugu
  [3:0:8.5] Nellore, Kadapa | Pennar valley (Foote: 28 quartzite tools), Sagileru
[3:0:9] - **Kurnool** is the Palaeolithic area of AP (Bagor = Rajasthan, Sarai Nahar Rai and Belan valley = UP).
[3:0:10] - **Nagarjunakonda:** Lower Palaeolithic tools in river gravels studied by K. V. Soundara Rajan, which record **alternating dry and wet climatic phases** (APPSC key: three cycles); also microliths and an Iron Age–Megalithic centre.
[3:0:11] - Mar 2026: Upper Palaeolithic **quartz tools** and **petroglyphs** at **Manchirevula** near Hyderabad.

## SUB 1 | Megalithic (Iron Age) Andhra – Rakshasa Gullu and Burial Types
[3:1:0] - Andhra megaliths = **Rakshasa Gullu** ("large stones"), of the **Iron Age**, with **black-and-red ware** (absent only at **Visakhapatnam**).
[3:1:1] - Local names: **Pandava Gullu** (Kurnool, Kadapa), **Kurambara Gullu** (Chittoor), **Morivara Gullu** (Anantapur).
[3:1:2] - About **12 types** in AP (studied by **N. R. Banerji**); grave goods: pottery, beads, **iron implements**, gold ornaments – not glass mirrors; **dolmens for elites, pits for commoners**.
[3:1:3] - Sites: **Nagarjunakonda** (main habitation centre), Brahmagiri, Gudivada, **Jonnawada** (Nellore), Boodigapalli, Naidupalli; a coffin with a male and a female body in one grave near **Yeleswaram**.
[3:1:4] - Jun 2026: **Mayiladumparai** (TN) AMS dating puts the South Indian Iron Age at c. **2100 BCE** (2172 BCE), earlier than the Gangetic Iron Age.
[3:1:5] - H. D. Sankalia: **rice** was first harvested and eaten in Andhra in this **Iron/Megalithic** age.
[3:1:6] TABLE head: Type | Form | AP area
  [3:1:6.0] Cist | Stone-slab box | NextGen: found everywhere except Visakhapatnam (the G2 key treated "many in Visakhapatnam" as correct)
  [3:1:6.1] Dolmen | Table-like chamber of slabs | Krishna, Guntur, East and West Godavari; also Chittoor
  [3:1:6.2] Sarcophagus | Terracotta coffin | Sankhavaram (Kurnool) – ram-shaped; Nagarjunakonda; NOT a Chittoor–Anantapuramu cluster
  [3:1:6.3] Menhir | Upright stone | Revarala (Krishna)
  [3:1:6.4] Anthropomorphic | Human-shaped stone | Tirupati – India's largest collection
[3:1:7] > G2 PYQ: the incorrect statement (official key) = "sarcophagus burials are more in Chittoor and Anantapuramu".
[3:1:8] - **Warangal** region: famous for **iron implements** and steel (wootz) – the iron tradition of the Telangana–Andhra region.

## SUB 2 | Neolithic–Chalcolithic Andhra – Patapadu, Keesarapalli, Utnur
[3:2:0] TABLE head: Site | Location | Feature
  [3:2:0.0] Patapadu | Kurnool | Chalcolithic – painted pottery (Copper Age evidence of Andhra)
  [3:2:0.1] Keesarapalli | Krishna | Chalcolithic – copper tools
  [3:2:0.2] Utnur | Mahbubnagar, Telangana | Neolithic ash mound ("mounds of burning soot")
  [3:2:0.3] Palavoy | Anantapur | Neolithic settlement, four ash mounds
  [3:2:0.4] Veerapuram | Kurnool, right bank of the Krishna | Neolithic–Chalcolithic to Early Historic sequence
  [3:2:0.5] Ramapuram | Kurnool | Neolithic–Chalcolithic
  [3:2:0.6] Sanganakallu, Tekkalakota | Ballari, Karnataka | Neolithic settlements – not Andhra
  [3:2:0.7] Brahmagiri | Chitradurga, Karnataka | Copper objects; megalithic graves
[3:2:1] - First Neolithic tool in South India: **Meadows Taylor, 1842**, at **Lingasugur** (Raichur) – not Jwalapuram.
[3:2:2] - Neolithic weapons of AP mostly of **dolomite**; sandstone axes near **Vaddamanu** (Amaravati).
[3:2:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP HISTORY Part 1 p.4-5; app notes 1:3 (sub 4)
[3:2:4] - **Disposal of the dead (Neolithic–Chalcolithic Andhra):** adults in **extended burials**, often with pots; children in urns – not cremation or mound burials.
[3:2:5] - **Utnur ash mounds:** formed by repeatedly **burning cow dung inside wooden cattle stockades** rebuilt many times (Allchin) → proof of Neolithic **animal domestication**.
## SUB 3 | Mesolithic Andhra – Giddaluru and the Microlithic Centres
[3:3:0] - **Giddaluru (Prakasam)** = the **microlithic implement centre** of Andhra identified by archaeologists (Nellore, Kurnool, Guntur are distractors).
[3:3:1] - Microliths also at **Nagarjunakonda** and **Kondapur**; tool centres discovered by **Robert Bruce Foote**.
[3:3:2] - Mesolithic = **microlithic**; tools by **flaking and chipping**; Andhra microliths resemble those of **South India and the Caspian** region.
[3:3:3] - Microlithic implements of c. 5000 BCE at **Giddaluru and Khammam**.
[3:3:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP HISTORY Part 1 p.3-4
[3:3:5] - **Raw material:** South Indian Mesolithic microliths were mostly of **milky quartz**; found at **Nagarjunakonda** and **Renigunta**, among others.
## SUB 4 | Rock Art and Caves of Andhra
[3:4:0] - **Borra Caves** (Ananthagiri hills, Araku, **Visakhapatnam**): karstic limestone caves; discovered **1807** by William King (GSI); Mesolithic example.
[3:4:1] - **Ketavaram** (Kurnool): Mesolithic rock paintings; most painted animal in AP rock art = **deer**.
[3:4:2] - Neolithic paintings: **Chintagunta** (man with bow), **Velpulamadugu** (humped bulls facing each other), **Thenagala** (dog and deer in red) – Anantapur.
[3:4:3] - **Petroglyphs** are **carved**, not painted.
[3:4:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP HISTORY Part 1 p.4-5

=== NOTES 1:4 | Pre-harappan and Early Harappan Cultures – Mehrgarh, Amri, Kot Diji, Kalibangan-i
## SUB 0 | Mehrgarh – Earliest Farming and Settled Life
[4:0:0] - **Mehrgarh** (Kachhi plain, Baluchistan, near the **Bolan Pass**; c. **7000 BCE**): **earliest agriculture** (wheat, barley), domestication and **settled life** in the subcontinent; excavated by **J.-F. Jarrige** (1974).
[4:0:1] - Seven periods (I–VII) give remains **from the Stone Age (Neolithic) to the Harappan** period – unlike Amri, Kot Diji or Kalibangan.
[4:0:2] TABLE head: Period | Evidence
  [4:0:2.0] I (c. 7000 BCE) | Aceramic Neolithic; mud-brick houses; barley, wheat; sheep, goat, cattle
  [4:0:2.1] II | Pottery and cotton (c. 5000 BCE, oldest cotton) begin
  [4:0:2.2] III onward | Copper working, wheel-made painted pottery, beads
[4:0:3] - **Burials:** under or near the houses, flexed, with grave goods, sometimes with **goats**.
[4:0:4] - Neolithic Baluchistan and Indus plains: crops **wheat, barley, dates, cotton**; main animals **sheep and goats**.
[4:0:5] TABLE head: Early village | Area | Excavator
  [4:0:5.0] Kili Gul Muhammad | Quetta valley | W. A. Fairservis
  [4:0:5.1] Damb Sadaat | Quetta valley (Early Harappan phase) | W. A. Fairservis
  [4:0:5.2] Rana Ghundai | Zhob-Loralai valley | E. J. Ross
[4:0:6] > Mehrgarh is a **Neolithic** site, not a Harappan city; "earliest settled life" and "earliest agriculture" = Mehrgarh (not Kili Gul Muhammad, Quetta or Kalat).
[4:0:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.53-56; NextGen Indian History Part 1 p.1; app notes 1:4
## SUB 1 | Early Harappan Cultures – Amri, Kot Diji, Sothi, Kalibangan-I
[4:1:0] - Early (Pre-) Harappan phase **3300–2600 BCE** = **Regionalisation Era** (Hakra phase); copper, the wheel and the **plough** were already known.
[4:1:1] TABLE head: Culture / site | Area | Feature | Excavator
  [4:1:1.0] Amri | Sindh | Transitional pre- to Mature culture; rhinoceros remains; later Jhangar | N. G. Majumdar (1929), later J.-M. Casal
  [4:1:1.1] Kot Diji | Sindh | Fortified, destroyed by fire; many stone implements | Ghurye; F. A. Khan (1955)
  [4:1:1.2] Sothi-Siswal | Rajasthan–Haryana (Ghaggar) | Pre-Harappan pottery | A. Ghosh identified Sothi
  [4:1:1.3] Hakra | Cholistan (Ghaggar-Hakra) – not Gujarat | – | –
  [4:1:1.4] Banawali | Fatehabad, Haryana | Pre- and Mature phases | R. S. Bisht (1974)
  [4:1:1.5] Mitathal | Bhiwani, Haryana | Pre-, Mature and Late phases | Suraj Bhan (1968)
[4:1:2] - **Amalananda Ghosh** (1953) was the **first to see the likeness between a pre-Harappan culture (Sothi) and the Mature Harappan**.
[4:1:3] - Pottery was regional (Amri, Kot Diji, Sothi-Siswal, Hakra wares), not uniform; Early Harappans used the potter's wheel.
[4:1:4] # Kalibangan-I
[4:1:5] - **Earliest ploughed field** with **criss-cross furrows** = **mixed cropping**; mud-brick fortification; pottery in **six fabrics**.
[4:1:6] - Excavated **1961–69** by **B. B. Lal and B. K. Thapar**. The ploughed field is pre-Harappan; the fire altars belong to the Mature phase.
[4:1:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History Part 1 p.1, p.13-15; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.72-73; Wikipedia "Amalananda Ghosh"; app notes 1:4
## SUB 2 | Origin Debate and Excavators
[4:2:0] - **W. A. Fairservis** wrote "**The Roots of Ancient India**" (1971) on the prehistoric village cultures of Baluchistan.
[4:2:1] TABLE head: View on Harappan origin | Scholar
  [4:2:1.0] Indigenous growth | John Marshall
  [4:2:1.1] Migration from Sumer | E. J. H. Mackay
  [4:2:1.2] Diffusion of ideas from West Asia | Mortimer Wheeler
  [4:2:1.3] Local evolution from the Mehrgarh Neolithic | Current consensus
[4:2:2] - Kalibangan and Kot Diji foreshadow Mature buildings and pottery → urbanisation was **gradual and local**, not imported from Mesopotamia.
[4:2:3] - Unsolved: the **gap between Neolithic Mehrgarh and the urban phase**.
[4:2:4] - 23 Jun 2026: **Rakhigarhi** (~550 ha) shows unbroken Early to Mature occupation; Haryana protected **Tighrana and Mitathal** (**Bhiwani**).
