## SUB 0 | Mehrgarh – Earliest Farming and Settled Life
[0:0] - **Mehrgarh** (Kachhi plain, Baluchistan, near the **Bolan Pass**; c. **7000 BCE**): **earliest agriculture** (wheat, barley), domestication and **settled life** in the subcontinent; excavated by **J.-F. Jarrige** (1974).
[0:1] - Seven periods (I–VII) give remains **from the Stone Age (Neolithic) to the Harappan** period – unlike Amri, Kot Diji or Kalibangan.
[0:2] TABLE head: Period | Evidence
  [0:2.0] I (c. 7000 BCE) | Aceramic Neolithic; mud-brick houses; barley, wheat; sheep, goat, cattle
  [0:2.1] II | Pottery and cotton (c. 5000 BCE, oldest cotton) begin
  [0:2.2] III onward | Copper working, wheel-made painted pottery, beads
[0:3] - **Burials:** under or near the houses, flexed, with grave goods, sometimes with **goats**.
[0:4] - Neolithic Baluchistan and Indus plains: crops **wheat, barley, dates, cotton**; main animals **sheep and goats**.
[0:5] TABLE head: Early village | Area | Excavator
  [0:5.0] Kili Gul Muhammad | Quetta valley | W. A. Fairservis
  [0:5.1] Damb Sadaat | Quetta valley (Early Harappan phase) | W. A. Fairservis
  [0:5.2] Rana Ghundai | Zhob-Loralai valley | E. J. Ross
[0:6] > Mehrgarh is a **Neolithic** site, not a Harappan city; "earliest settled life" and "earliest agriculture" = Mehrgarh (not Kili Gul Muhammad, Quetta or Kalat).
[0:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.53-56; NextGen Indian History Part 1 p.1; app notes 1:4
## SUB 1 | Early Harappan Cultures – Amri, Kot Diji, Sothi, Kalibangan-I
[1:0] - Early (Pre-) Harappan phase **3300–2600 BCE** = **Regionalisation Era** (Hakra phase); copper, the wheel and the **plough** were already known.
[1:1] TABLE head: Culture / site | Area | Feature | Excavator
  [1:1.0] Amri | Sindh | Transitional pre- to Mature culture; rhinoceros remains; later Jhangar | N. G. Majumdar (1929), later J.-M. Casal
  [1:1.1] Kot Diji | Sindh | Fortified, destroyed by fire; many stone implements | Ghurye; F. A. Khan (1955)
  [1:1.2] Sothi-Siswal | Rajasthan–Haryana (Ghaggar) | Pre-Harappan pottery | A. Ghosh identified Sothi
  [1:1.3] Hakra | Cholistan (Ghaggar-Hakra) – not Gujarat | – | –
  [1:1.4] Banawali | Fatehabad, Haryana | Pre- and Mature phases | R. S. Bisht (1974)
  [1:1.5] Mitathal | Bhiwani, Haryana | Pre-, Mature and Late phases | Suraj Bhan (1968)
[1:2] - **Amalananda Ghosh** (1953) was the **first to see the likeness between a pre-Harappan culture (Sothi) and the Mature Harappan**.
[1:3] - Pottery was regional (Amri, Kot Diji, Sothi-Siswal, Hakra wares), not uniform; Early Harappans used the potter's wheel.
[1:4] # Kalibangan-I
[1:5] - **Earliest ploughed field** with **criss-cross furrows** = **mixed cropping**; mud-brick fortification; pottery in **six fabrics**.
[1:6] - Excavated **1961–69** by **B. B. Lal and B. K. Thapar**. The ploughed field is pre-Harappan; the fire altars belong to the Mature phase.
[1:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History Part 1 p.1, p.13-15; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.72-73; Wikipedia "Amalananda Ghosh"; app notes 1:4
## SUB 2 | Origin Debate and Excavators
[2:0] - **W. A. Fairservis** wrote "**The Roots of Ancient India**" (1971) on the prehistoric village cultures of Baluchistan.
[2:1] TABLE head: View on Harappan origin | Scholar
  [2:1.0] Indigenous growth | John Marshall
  [2:1.1] Migration from Sumer | E. J. H. Mackay
  [2:1.2] Diffusion of ideas from West Asia | Mortimer Wheeler
  [2:1.3] Local evolution from the Mehrgarh Neolithic | Current consensus
[2:2] - Kalibangan and Kot Diji foreshadow Mature buildings and pottery → urbanisation was **gradual and local**, not imported from Mesopotamia.
[2:3] - Unsolved: the **gap between Neolithic Mehrgarh and the urban phase**.
[2:4] - 23 Jun 2026: **Rakhigarhi** (~550 ha) shows unbroken Early to Mature occupation; Haryana protected **Tighrana and Mitathal** (**Bhiwani**).
