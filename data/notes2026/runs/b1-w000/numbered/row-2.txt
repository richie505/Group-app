## SUB 0 | Neolithic – First Farming, Pit Dwellings, Dog Burials, Ash Mounds
[0:0] # First farming
[0:1] - **Cultivation of cereals began in the Neolithic.**
[0:2] - **Mehrgarh** (Baluchistan, c. **7000 BCE**): **earliest agriculture** (wheat, barley) and settled life in the subcontinent; of dated options "7000 BC" is the start of farming.
[0:3] - **Rice:** **Lahuradeva** (UP, c. **6500 BCE**, oldest paddy); **Koldihwa** (Belan valley, UP) – earlier thought oldest; sequence **Neolithic–Chalcolithic–Iron**.
[0:4] # Site table
[0:5] TABLE head: Site | Region | Distinctive find
  [0:5.0] Burzahom | Kashmir (16 km NW of Srinagar) | Pit dwellings; dog buried with master
  [0:5.1] Gufkral | Kashmir | Pit dwellings (with Burzahom)
  [0:5.2] Chirand | Bihar | Bone and antler tools
  [0:5.3] Koldihwa | Belan valley, UP | Early rice
  [0:5.4] Sanganakallu | Ballari, Karnataka | Ash mound (also Utnur, Piklihal, Kupgal, Budihal)
  [0:5.5] Tekkalakota | Ballari, Karnataka | Hill-dwellers; excavated by M. S. Nagaraja Rao
  [0:5.6] Brahmagiri, Maski | Karnataka | Neolithic–megalithic sites
[0:6] - **Ash mounds:** burnt dung of cattle pens = seasonal camps of Neolithic herdsmen.
[0:7] - **Eastern Neolithic** (Birbhanpur, Chirand, Kuchai, Sankarjang): **pointed-butt celts** and **shouldered axes**.
[0:8] - **Pit dwellings** are NOT at Mehrgarh (mud-brick houses), Pallavaram (Palaeolithic tools) or Balakot (Harappan).

## SUB 1 | Mesolithic – Domestication, Burials and Ganga–Vindhya Sites
[1:0] # Most-asked facts
[1:1] - **Animal domestication began in the Mesolithic:** earliest evidence at **Adamgarh** (Narmadapuram, MP) and **Bagor** (Bhilwara, Rajasthan) – not Langhnaj, Birbhanpur or Chopani Mando.
[1:2] - **Mahadaha** (Pratapgarh, UP): **bone and antler ornaments** (earrings, necklaces), bone implements, double burials.
[1:3] - **Damdama** (Pratapgarh): 41 graves, five double burials and one **triple burial** (three skeletons in one grave).
[1:4] - **Sarai Nahar Rai** (Pratapgarh): a grave with **four** skeletons.
[1:5] - **Lekhahia** (Mirzapur, Vindhyas): **17 human skeletons** from rock shelter 1 – the most from any Vindhyan rock shelter.

  [1:7.0] Bagor | Bhilwara, Rajasthan | Rock shelters, microliths, early domestication
  [1:7.1] Adamgarh | Narmadapuram, MP | Rock shelter, early domestication
  [1:7.2] Langhnaj | Gujarat | Microliths, burials
  [1:7.3] Sarai Nahar Rai, Mahadaha, Damdama | Ganga plain, Pratapgarh (UP) | Lake-side camps, burials
  [1:7.4] Lekhahia, Baghai Khor, Morahana Pahar, Chopani Mando | Vindhyas / Belan valley (Mirzapur, UP) | Rock shelters; Chopani Mando – huts, microliths
  [1:7.5] Paisra | Bihar | –
  [1:7.6] Birbhanpur | West Bengal | –

[1:9] > Mesolithic = microlithic stage; tools made by **flaking and chipping**.

## SUB 2 | Bhimbetka and Prehistoric Rock Art
[2:0] - **Bhimbetka:** foothills of the Vindhyas, **Raisen** district near Bhopal (MP); discovered by **V. S. Wakankar in 1957-58**; **UNESCO World Heritage Site 2003**.
[2:1] - **Most rock paintings in India** (up to 20 layers in places); Upper Palaeolithic art in about **300** caves and shelters; continuous use from Lower Palaeolithic to medieval times.
[2:2] - Paintings are **Upper Palaeolithic and Mesolithic** – hunting scenes, animals, geometric patterns; Ajanta, Ellora, Bagh are historic-period cave art, not prehistoric.
[2:3] - Most commonly painted animal: **deer**. A **petroglyph** is **carved** into rock; a painting is applied on the surface.
[2:4] - 12 May 2026 study: after contact with the Chalcolithic farmers of **Malwa**, the Bhimbetka style became **static**.
[2:5] - H. D. Sankalia, S. S. Nigam, R. Pandey, M. S. Vats, V. N. Misra are distractors – the discoverer is **Wakankar**.
[2:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.59-61; app notes 1:2 (sub 6)
## SUB 3 | Chalcolithic Cultures – Ahar, Kayatha, Malwa, Jorwe
[3:0] - **Chalcolithic = Copper Age** (copper + stone tools); **first metal used = copper**.
[3:1] - The **Chalcolithic** people are credited with the first wide use of **painted pottery** (a few Neolithic sites had some).
[3:2] TABLE head: Culture | Sites | Region | Date | Pottery / feature
  [3:2.0] Ahar-Banas | Ahar, Gilund, Balathal | Mewar, Banas valley (Rajasthan) | c. 2800/2100–1500 BCE | White-painted black-and-red ware; copper smelting
  [3:2.1] Kayatha | Kayatha (Chambal) | MP | 2450–1700 BCE | Chocolate-slipped ware
  [3:2.2] Malwa | Navdatoli (Narmada), Eran | Madhya Pradesh | 1900–1400 BCE | Malwa ware; Navdatoli dug by H. D. Sankalia
  [3:2.3] Jorwe | Jorwe, Nevasa, Daimabad, Inamgaon | Maharashtra (nuclear zone Pravara–Godavari) | 1400–700 BCE | Painted red ware (black-on-red)
[3:3] - **Contemporaries of the Harappans:** **Ahar-Banas** and Kayatha; Malwa and Jorwe are later. Order of start: Ahar → Kayatha → Malwa → Jorwe.
[3:4] - **Burials in Maharashtra:** dead buried in urns **under the house floor, north to south**.
[3:5] - **Daimabad** (Pravara): largest Jorwe site; **bronze chariot**, buffalo, elephant, rhinoceros; **symbolic burial**; southernmost Late Harappan site.
[3:6] - **Jorwe end:** most sites abandoned gradually after c. 1000 BCE (drought); Inamgaon, Prakash continued (Late Jorwe) – "**suddenly deserted**" is the wrong statement (CDS 2012).
[3:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.63-74; examrobot CDS-I 2012 key; app notes 1:2 (sub 5)
## SUB 4 | Stone Age Framework – Phases, Proto-history, Early Man
[4:0] - **Order:** Lower → Middle → Upper Palaeolithic → Mesolithic → Neolithic → Chalcolithic → Iron Age (Megalithic).
[4:1] - **Phases are defined by tool type and technology**; tools varied by region and cultures did **not** evolve uniformly or unilinearly.
[4:2] - **Three-Age system** (Stone, Bronze, Iron): **C. J. Thomsen** (Copenhagen museum); "Palaeolithic/Neolithic" coined by Lubbock.
[4:3] - **Proto-history:** writing exists but is undeciphered – the **Indus civilisation** is **proto-historic**.
[4:4] - **Early man:** **Narmada Man** skull found by **Arun Sonakia, 1982** at **Hathnora** (Narmada valley, MP) – earliest human fossil of India; Bori (Maharashtra) artefacts c. 1.38 million years.
[4:5] - **Cro-Magnon** = anatomically modern humans of the Upper Palaeolithic – later than Neanderthals and Heidelberg man.
[4:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.26-27, p.45-47; Britannica (Three-Age system, Cro-Magnon)
## SUB 5 | Palaeolithic – Tool Techniques and Sites
[5:0] TABLE head: Stage | Technique / tools | Sites
  [5:0.0] Lower | Acheulian = handaxe-cleaver; Soanian = chopper-chopping | Attirampakkam (TN), Hunsgi and Isampur (Karnataka), Didwana (Rajasthan), Sohan valley (Pakistan Punjab), Belan valley
  [5:0.1] Middle | Flake tools; prepared-core Levallois technique; scrapers, borers, points | Nevasa, Bhimbetka, Narmada valley; Sembur (TN)
  [5:0.2] Upper | Blades, burins, bone tools | Kurnool caves, Jwalapuram, Patne, Baghor
[5:1] - **Attirampakkam** is an **Old Stone Age** (Lower Palaeolithic, Acheulian) site – not "Middle Stone Age" (Mesolithic).
[5:2] - **Levallois** = Middle Palaeolithic prepared-core flake technique (Sembur); "Lacoid/Placoid/Hollois" are not techniques. No Neanderthal fossil has been found in India.
[5:3] - Middle Palaeolithic = **flake** stage, not the microlithic stage.
[5:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.47-50; app notes 1:2 (sub 1)
## SUB 6 | Megaliths – Nature, Iron Age and New Finds
[6:0] - **Megaliths:** graves with weapons, tools and pottery, marked by **big stones** (mega = large, lith = stone) – **burial sites** away from homes; not microliths, chalcoliths or monoliths.
[6:1] - Belong to the **Iron Age**, with **black-and-red ware**; c. 1000 BCE – 2nd century BCE (PMF).
[6:2] - Earliest AMS-dated iron: **Mayiladumparai** (Krishnagiri, TN) – **2172 BCE**.
[6:3] - **Bihar:** first documentation of a megalithic site (July 2019) in the **Kaimur hills** (Hurmeta menhirs).
[6:4] - **Kerala:** rock-cut burial chamber at **Panayal** (Kasaragod).
[6:5] - 21 Jul 2026: **Karivalamvanthanallur** (Tenkasi, TN) passed straight from **Microlithic to Iron/Early Historic**, with no Neolithic or Chalcolithic.
[6:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.27-28; rohtas.nic.in (megalithic tombs); app notes 1:2 (sub 8-9)
