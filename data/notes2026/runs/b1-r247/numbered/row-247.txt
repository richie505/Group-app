## SUB 0 | Operation Polo – the Hyderabad Police Action (1948)
[0:0] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [0:0.0] Code name | Operation Polo ("Police Action"; also Operation Caterpillar)
  [0:0.1] Dates | 13 September 1948 (troops entered) → Nizam's ceasefire 17 September, Hyderabad army surrendered 18 September 1948 – about five days (four to five days, "108 hours")
  [0:0.2] Ended | 17 September 1948 in exam keys (Nizam's surrender; Hyderabad "Liberation Day"); PMF and Wikipedia give the formal surrender as 18 September
  [0:0.3] Indian commander | Maj. Gen. J.N. (Jayanto Nath) Chaudhuri led the main thrust and took the surrender; overall command Lt. Gen. Rajendrasinhji
  [0:0.4] Nizam's Commander-in-Chief | Maj. Gen. Syed Ahmed El Edroos ("L. Edroos / Edrus / Edrews" in papers) – Hyderabad State Forces
  [0:0.5] Not commanders | Sidney Cotton = Australian pilot who ran arms into Hyderabad; Walter Monckton = the Nizam's British legal adviser
  [0:0.6] Result | Hyderabad freed from Nizam rule in 1948; Chaudhuri became Military Governor (17 Sept 1948 – 1 Dec 1949)
[0:1] - **Why:** the Nizam would not accede; **Razakar** violence, arms imports and breach of the Standstill Agreement.
[0:2] - **Hyderabad outside India:** 15 Aug 1947 → 17 Sept 1948 = about **13 months**.

  [0:3.1] Vijay | Goa, Daman and Diu | December 1961 – Maj. Gen. K.P. Candeth
  [0:3.2] Blue Star / Meghdoot / Pawan | Golden Temple 1984 / Siachen 1984 / Sri Lanka (IPKF) 1987 | – (distractors)

## SUB 1 | Hyderabad State – Nizam, Standstill Agreement, Razakars, UN Appeal
[1:0] # The state and its ruler
[1:1] - **Ruling dynasty in 1947-48:** **Asaf Jahi (Nizams)** – founded by **Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I (1724)**; not the Qutb Shahis (Golconda) or Adil Shahis.
[1:2] - **Last Nizam:** **Mir Osman Ali Khan, the 7th Nizam (Asaf Jah VII)**; Muslim ruler over a Hindu majority; sought **independence** (or ties with Pakistan), not accession to India.
[1:3] - **Largest and richest** princely state.
[1:4] # Standstill Agreement
[1:5] - Signed **29 November 1947** by the **Nizam** and **Lord Mountbatten** (Government of India) – **Hyderabad** was the party; existing arrangements kept for **one year** during talks.
[1:6] # Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen and the Razakars
[1:7] - **Majlis-e-Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen (MIM):** emerged **1927**.
[1:8] - **Razakars:** its militant volunteer force, led by **Kasim (Qasim) Razvi** – not Laik Ali.

## SUB 3 | Accession of the States – Legal Frame, Patel-Menon, Junagadh, Kashmir, Manipur
[3:0] # Legal frame
[3:1] - **Indian Independence Act 1947:** British paramountcy **lapsed 15 August 1947**; a state could **join India, join Pakistan or (in theory) stay independent**.
[3:2] - **Instrument of Accession (IoA):** ceded only **Defence, External Affairs, Communications**; **internal administration stayed with the rulers**.
[3:3] - **Hold-outs on 15 August 1947:** **Junagadh, Hyderabad, Kashmir** – ruler's religion differed from the majority's; **Bhopal** is the odd one out (it acceded).
[3:4] # Machinery
[3:5] - **States Department (July 1947):** **Sardar Patel** (head) with **V.P. Menon** (Secretary, chief negotiator); Patel's appeal of **5 July 1947**; bait: **privy purses**, rulers as Rajpramukhs.
[3:6] - **V.P. Menon** wrote **"The Story of the Integration of the Indian States"** (not B.N. Rau, Rajaji or Krishna Menon).
[3:7] - **Phases (Menon):** 1 **extinction** (**216** small states merged into provinces) → 2 **consolidation into unions** (Kathiawar → **Saurashtra**, Rajasthan, PEPSU, Madhya Bharat) → 3 **democratisation** → 4 privy purses ended (1971).
[3:8] - **1950 classes:** Part A = former Governor's provinces; **Part B = princely states/unions under a Rajpramukh**; Part C = Chief Commissioner's provinces; **Part D = Andaman and Nicobar**.
[3:9] # The hard cases
[3:10] TABLE head: State | Ruler / figure | Outcome
  [3:10.0] Junagadh | Nawab Mahabat Khanji III; Dewan Shah Nawaz Bhutto | Nawab announced accession to Pakistan (Sept 1947) though most people wanted India; blockade, Arzi Hukumat; Nawab fled; plebiscite 20 Feb 1948 – 91% for India (the princely state where a plebiscite was held)
  [3:10.1] Jammu and Kashmir | Maharaja Hari Singh | Tribal invasion from Pakistan → IoA 26 October 1947; army airlifted 27 October; India to the UN (31 Dec 1947); ceasefire 1 January 1949; Sheikh Abdullah PM (March 1948); Delhi Agreement 1952
  [3:10.2] Travancore | Dewan C.P. Ramaswami Iyer | Declared independence (monazite/thorium); his autocratic rule was opposed by the State Congress; joined 30 July 1947
  [3:10.3] Jodhpur | Maharaja Hanwant Singh | Tempted by Jinnah; joined India
  [3:10.4] Bhopal | Nawab Hamidullah Khan | Delayed; merged 1949
  [3:10.5] Manipur | Maharaja Bodhachandra Singh | IoA before independence; elections June 1948 under the Manipur State Constitution Act 1947 – first part of India to hold elections on universal adult franchise; merger Sept 1949 (effective Oct 1949, with Tripura)
[3:11] - **J&K at independence** was governed by Hari Singh's **Jammu and Kashmir Constitution Act, 1939** (7 Sept 1939; Act XIV of Samvat 1996); **1934** = first Praja Sabha elections.

## SUB 5 | After Integration – Part B Hyderabad, Rajpramukhs, Privy Purses
[5:0] - **Hyderabad State (of India) existed 1948–1956:** Military Governor Chaudhuri → CM **M.K. Vellodi (Dec 1949)** → Part B state from **26 Jan 1950**, **Nizam as Rajpramukh** → elected CM **Burgula (1952)**.
[5:1] - Dissolved **1 November 1956:** Telugu districts → **Andhra Pradesh**; **Marathwada → Bombay**; **Raichur, Gulbarga → Mysore**.
[5:2] TABLE head: Term / Act | Fact
  [5:2.0] Privy purse | Annual payment to rulers for accession
  [5:2.1] 7th Amendment, 1956 | Ended Part B states and the office of Rajpramukh
  [5:2.2] 26th Amendment, 1971 | Privy purses and princely privileges abolished; Articles 291 and 362 omitted, Art. 363A added

## SUB 6 | French and Portuguese Possessions
[6:0] - **French:** Pondicherry, Karikal, Mahe, **Yanam** – **de facto 1 November 1954** (after the **Kizhur** referendum, Oct 1954), **de jure 16 August 1962**; Yanam (Godavari delta, inside AP) is part of **Puducherry UT**; **Chandernagore** – plebiscite, de facto 1950, merged into **West Bengal** (1954).
[6:1] - **Portuguese:** **Operation Vijay (18-19 December 1961)**, Maj. Gen. **K.P. Candeth** – freed **Goa, Daman and Diu**; end of European colonial rule in India; Goa a state in 1987. **Dadra and Nagar Haveli** were freed earlier (1954) by volunteers, not by Vijay.
