=== Operation Polo – the Hyderabad Police Action (1948)
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Code name | **Operation Polo** ("Police Action"; also Operation Caterpillar) |
| Dates | **13 September 1948** (troops entered) → Nizam's ceasefire **17 September**, Hyderabad army surrendered **18 September 1948** – about **five days** (four to five days, "108 hours") |
| Ended | **17 September 1948** in exam keys (Nizam's surrender; Hyderabad "Liberation Day"); PMF and Wikipedia give the formal surrender as 18 September |
| Indian commander | **Maj. Gen. J.N. (Jayanto Nath) Chaudhuri** led the main thrust and took the surrender; overall command Lt. Gen. Rajendrasinhji |
| Nizam's Commander-in-Chief | **Maj. Gen. Syed Ahmed El Edroos** ("L. Edroos / Edrus / Edrews" in papers) – Hyderabad State Forces |
| Not commanders | **Sidney Cotton** = Australian pilot who ran arms into Hyderabad; **Walter Monckton** = the Nizam's British legal adviser |
| Result | Hyderabad freed from Nizam rule in **1948**; Chaudhuri became **Military Governor** (17 Sept 1948 – 1 Dec 1949) |
- **Why:** the Nizam would not accede; **Razakar** violence, arms imports and breach of the Standstill Agreement.
- **Hyderabad outside India:** 15 Aug 1947 → 17 Sept 1948 = about **13 months**.
| Operation | Place | Year – commander |
|---|---|---|
| **Polo** | Hyderabad | **1948** – J.N. Chaudhuri |
| **Vijay** | Goa, Daman and Diu | **December 1961** – Maj. Gen. K.P. Candeth |
| Blue Star / Meghdoot / Pawan | Golden Temple 1984 / Siachen 1984 / Sri Lanka (IPKF) 1987 | – (distractors) |
- **Year Hyderabad joined India:** exam keys say **1948** (Polo). Strictly the Nizam's **firman of 24 November 1949** accepted the Indian Constitution (Spectrum: "Hyderabad acceded in November 1949") and Hyderabad became a **Part B state of the Republic on 26 January 1950**.
> Polo = Hyderabad, not Junagadh (plebiscite) or Kashmir (IoA + war).

=== Hyderabad State – Nizam, Standstill Agreement, Razakars, UN Appeal
# The state and its ruler
- **Ruling dynasty in 1947-48:** **Asaf Jahi (Nizams)** – founded by **Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I (1724)**; not the Qutb Shahis (Golconda) or Adil Shahis.
- **Last Nizam:** **Mir Osman Ali Khan, the 7th Nizam (Asaf Jah VII)**; Muslim ruler over a Hindu majority; sought **independence** (or ties with Pakistan), not accession to India.
- **Largest and richest** princely state.
# Standstill Agreement
- Signed **29 November 1947** by the **Nizam** and **Lord Mountbatten** (Government of India) – **Hyderabad** was the party; existing arrangements kept for **one year** during talks.
# Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen and the Razakars
- **Majlis-e-Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen (MIM):** emerged **1927**.
- **Razakars:** its militant volunteer force, led by **Kasim (Qasim) Razvi** – not Laik Ali.

=== Accession of the States – Legal Frame, Patel-Menon, Junagadh, Kashmir, Manipur
# Legal frame
- **Indian Independence Act 1947:** British paramountcy **lapsed 15 August 1947**; a state could **join India, join Pakistan or (in theory) stay independent**.
- **Instrument of Accession (IoA):** ceded only **Defence, External Affairs, Communications**; **internal administration stayed with the rulers**.
- **Hold-outs on 15 August 1947:** **Junagadh, Hyderabad, Kashmir** – ruler's religion differed from the majority's; **Bhopal** is the odd one out (it acceded).
# Machinery
- **States Department (July 1947):** **Sardar Patel** (head) with **V.P. Menon** (Secretary, chief negotiator); Patel's appeal of **5 July 1947**; bait: **privy purses**, rulers as Rajpramukhs.
- **V.P. Menon** wrote **"The Story of the Integration of the Indian States"** (not B.N. Rau, Rajaji or Krishna Menon).
- **Phases (Menon):** 1 **extinction** (**216** small states merged into provinces) → 2 **consolidation into unions** (Kathiawar → **Saurashtra**, Rajasthan, PEPSU, Madhya Bharat) → 3 **democratisation** → 4 privy purses ended (1971).
- **1950 classes:** Part A = former Governor's provinces; **Part B = princely states/unions under a Rajpramukh**; Part C = Chief Commissioner's provinces; **Part D = Andaman and Nicobar**.
# The hard cases
| State | Ruler / figure | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Junagadh** | Nawab **Mahabat Khanji III**; Dewan **Shah Nawaz Bhutto** | Nawab announced accession to **Pakistan** (Sept 1947) though most people wanted India; blockade, **Arzi Hukumat**; Nawab fled; **plebiscite 20 Feb 1948** – **91% for India** (the princely state where a plebiscite was held) |
| **Jammu and Kashmir** | **Maharaja Hari Singh** | Tribal invasion from Pakistan → **IoA 26 October 1947**; army airlifted **27 October**; India to the UN (31 Dec 1947); **ceasefire 1 January 1949**; Sheikh Abdullah PM (March 1948); Delhi Agreement 1952 |
| **Travancore** | Dewan **C.P. Ramaswami Iyer** | Declared independence (monazite/thorium); his autocratic rule was opposed by the State Congress; joined **30 July 1947** |
| Jodhpur | Maharaja Hanwant Singh | Tempted by Jinnah; joined India |
| **Bhopal** | Nawab Hamidullah Khan | Delayed; merged **1949** |
| **Manipur** | Maharaja **Bodhachandra Singh** | IoA before independence; **elections June 1948** under the Manipur State Constitution Act 1947 – **first part of India to hold elections on universal adult franchise**; merger **Sept 1949** (effective Oct 1949, with Tripura) |
- **J&K at independence** was governed by Hari Singh's **Jammu and Kashmir Constitution Act, 1939** (7 Sept 1939; Act XIV of Samvat 1996); **1934** = first Praja Sabha elections.

=== After Integration – Part B Hyderabad, Rajpramukhs, Privy Purses
- **Hyderabad State (of India) existed 1948–1956:** Military Governor Chaudhuri → CM **M.K. Vellodi (Dec 1949)** → Part B state from **26 Jan 1950**, **Nizam as Rajpramukh** → elected CM **Burgula (1952)**.
- Dissolved **1 November 1956:** Telugu districts → **Andhra Pradesh**; **Marathwada → Bombay**; **Raichur, Gulbarga → Mysore**.
| Term / Act | Fact |
|---|---|
| Privy purse | Annual payment to rulers for accession |
| **7th Amendment, 1956** | Ended Part B states and the office of **Rajpramukh** |
| **26th Amendment, 1971** | **Privy purses and princely privileges abolished**; Articles **291 and 362** omitted, Art. 363A added |

=== French and Portuguese Possessions
- **French:** Pondicherry, Karikal, Mahe, **Yanam** – **de facto 1 November 1954** (after the **Kizhur** referendum, Oct 1954), **de jure 16 August 1962**; Yanam (Godavari delta, inside AP) is part of **Puducherry UT**; **Chandernagore** – plebiscite, de facto 1950, merged into **West Bengal** (1954).
- **Portuguese:** **Operation Vijay (18-19 December 1961)**, Maj. Gen. **K.P. Candeth** – freed **Goa, Daman and Diu**; end of European colonial rule in India; Goa a state in 1987. **Dadra and Nagar Haveli** were freed earlier (1954) by volunteers, not by Vijay.
