=== NOTES 1:247 | Integration of Princely States – Instrument of Accession, Junagadh, Hyderabad (operation Polo), Kashmir
## SUB 0 | Legal Frame and Instrument of Accession
[247:0:0] # Indian Independence Act, 1947
[247:0:1] - Lapse of British paramountcy: a princely state could **join India, join Pakistan or remain independent**.
[247:0:2] - **Hold-outs on 15 August 1947:** all states except **Kashmir, Hyderabad and Junagadh** had signed (Travancore is sometimes listed as a fourth, but it joined before 15 August).
[247:0:3] # Instrument of Accession (IoA)
[247:0:4] - Ceded only **three subjects** to the Dominion: **Defence, External Affairs, Communications**.
[247:0:5] - **Internal administration stayed with the rulers**; in return rulers got **privy purses** and internal autonomy.
[247:0:6] IoA = Defence + External Affairs + Communications. Nothing else was surrendered at first.
[247:0:7] # Three Hard Cases, Three Methods
[247:0:8] TABLE head: State | Resolved by
  [247:0:8.0] Junagadh | Plebiscite (February 1948)
  [247:0:8.1] Hyderabad | Police action (Operation Polo, September 1948)
  [247:0:8.2] Kashmir | Instrument of Accession, followed by war
[247:0:9] States that had not acceded by 15 August 1947 and where the ruler's religion differed from the majority: **Junagadh, Kashmir, Hyderabad**. **Bhopal** is the odd one out (it delayed but is not in this group).
## SUB 1 | Integration Machinery – States Department, Patel and V.p. Menon
[247:1:0] # Who Did What
[247:1:1] TABLE head: Person | Role

  [247:1:1.1] V.P. Menon | Secretary and chief negotiator, States Department
[247:1:2] - Method: **persuasion**, patriotic appeals, threat of popular uprisings or force; **privy purses + internal autonomy** offered.
[247:1:3] # Menon's Account and Four Phases
[247:1:4] - Book: **"The Story of the Integration of the Indian States"** – **V.P. Menon**.
[247:1:5] - 1. **Extinction** – **216 small states** merged into provinces.
[247:1:6] - 2. **Consolidation into unions** – **Saurashtra** (Kathiawar states), **Rajasthan Union**, **PEPSU**, **Madhya Bharat**.
[247:1:7] - 3. **Democratisation**.
[247:1:8] - 4. **Abolition of privy purses** (1971).

## SUB 2 | States' People's Movement and Congress Policy
[247:2:0] # All India States Peoples' Conference (AISPC)
[247:2:1] - Founded **1927**, **Bombay** (December 1927); organisers **Balwantrai Mehta**, **Maniklal Kothari**, **G.R. Abhyankar**; first session presided by **Diwan Bahadur Ramachandra Rao**.
[247:2:2] - Aim: coordinate people's movements in princely states against autocratic rule.
[247:2:3] - Presidents: **Jawaharlal Nehru** (from **1939**, till 1946); **Pattabhi Sitaramayya** later.
[247:2:4] - Dissolved on **25 April 1948**; its units merged into the Congress.
[247:2:5] - **Praja Mandal movement** – the parallel movement in the princely states (Kashmir, Hyderabad, Travancore); also called the **State Peoples Conference**.

## SUB 5 | Hyderabad State – Dynasty, Nizam and Defiance
[247:5:0] # The State
[247:5:1] - Ruling dynasty: **Asaf Jahi (Nizams)**, founded by **Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I, 1724**.
[247:5:2] - **Largest and richest** princely state; **first in population** (1,63,38,534 in 1951).
[247:5:3] - Last Nizam: **Mir Osman Ali Khan**, the **7th Nizam**; wanted **independence or accession to Pakistan**; sought an independent "Dominion of Hyderabad".
[247:5:4] Asaf Jahi (Nizams) ≠ Qutb Shahi (Golconda).
[247:5:5] # Standstill Agreement
[247:5:6] - Signed **29 November 1947** by **Nizam Mir Osman Ali Khan** and **Lord Mountbatten**, for **one year**; kept existing arrangements during talks.
[247:5:7] - Nizam's defiance continued: banned Indian currency, imported arms from Pakistan; India suspected secret deals with Pakistan.
[247:5:8] - Hyderabad stayed outside India about **13 months** (15 Aug 1947 – 17 Sept 1948).
[247:5:9] # Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen and the Razakars
[247:5:10] - **Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen (MIM)** emerged in **1927**.
[247:5:11] - **Razakars** – militant volunteer force of the **Majlis-e-Ittehadul Muslimeen**, led by **Kasim (Qasim) Razvi**.
[247:5:12] - Their violence against the population forced military action; they also targeted the Telangana armed struggle.

## SUB 6 | Operation Polo – the Police Action
[247:6:0] # Road to Polo
[247:6:1] - 1. Standstill Agreement, 29 November 1947 →
[247:6:2] - 2. Nizam bans Indian currency, imports arms →
[247:6:3] - 3. Razakar violence →
[247:6:4] - 4. **13–17 September 1948:** Operation Polo; **Nizam surrenders and accedes** →
[247:6:5] - 5. **Military Governor J.N. Chaudhuri**; Part B state with the Nizam as **Rajpramukh**.

  [247:6:7.3] Indian commander | Maj. Gen. J.N. Chaudhuri (later Military Governor)

## SUB 7 | Hyderabad's Un Appeal, the "join Indian Union" Movement and Accounts
[247:7:0] - **'Join Indian Union' movement (1947)** – **Swami Ramananda Tirtha** (Theertha), through the **Hyderabad State Congress**.
[247:7:1] - **'The Saga of Hyderabad Struggle in India's Freedom Movement'** – Swami Ramananda Tirtha; released by **Vice-President Krishna Kant, May 2002**.
[247:7:2] - Nizam's appeal to the **UN Security Council (1948)** – delegation led by **Nawab Moin Nawaz Jung**.
[247:7:3] - **"Hyderabad in Retrospect" (1949)** – **Nawab Ali Yavar Jung**; first published **anonymously** ("an ex-official of Hyderabad") as articles in the **Times of India**.
[247:7:4] - **K.M. Munshi:** India's **Agent-General** in Hyderabad; wrote ***The End of an Era***.
[247:7:5] "Hyderabad in Retrospect" = **Ali Yavar Jung**; the APPSC (Group 2, 2012) key gave **Laik Ali**, the last PM.
## SUB 8 | Telangana Armed Struggle (1946–51)
[247:8:0] - Peasant revolt led by the **Communists** and the **Andhra Mahasabha** against the Nizam, the **deshmukhs** and later the Razakars.
[247:8:1] - Trigger: killing of **Doddi Komaraiah (1946)**.
[247:8:2] - Link to integration: unrest + Razakar violence → Operation Polo (1948); the Indian Army then put down the armed squads; struggle **withdrawn October 1951**.

[247:10:3] - **Yanam** – French-origin enclave in AP's **Godavari delta**; part of **Puducherry UT**.

## SUB 1 | Cripps Mission, 1942
[248:1:0] - **Sir Stafford Cripps** – a **Labour** politician, member of the War Cabinet.
[248:1:1] - Offered **post-war Dominion Status** and a constitution-making body; provinces could opt out.
[248:1:2] - **Gandhi:** "a **post-dated cheque** on a crashing bank".
[248:1:3] - **Congress Working Committee** rejected it on **7 April 1942** – no immediate self-government, no control over defence; League rejected it as it gave no clear Pakistan.
[248:1:4] - Failure led to **Quit India (August 1942)**.
## SUB 2 | League Demands, C.r. Formula and Desai-liaquat Pact (1943–45)
[248:2:0] TABLE head: Demand / formula | Author / year | Proposal
  [248:2:0.0] "Divide and Quit" | Muslim League, Karachi session, December 1943 | Slogan answering "Quit India"
  [248:2:0.1] C.R. Formula | C. Rajagopalachari, 1944 (not Wavell) | See below
  [248:2:0.2] Desai-Liaquat Pact | Bhulabhai Desai (Congress leader, Central Legislative Assembly) + Liaquat Ali Khan, January 1945 | Interim government with equal Congress and League nominees + 20% for minorities; never formally accepted
[248:2:1] # C.R. Formula – Provisions
[248:2:2] - League to back the independence demand and join a provisional government.
[248:2:3] - After the war, a **plebiscite in contiguous Muslim-majority areas** (NW and NE) to decide on separation.
