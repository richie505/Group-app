=== NOTES 2:39 | Governor – Appointment, Powers, Discretion, Article 200/201 and Article 356
## SUB 0 | Office of the Governor – Articles 153-159
[39:0:0] - **Nominal head**; the **CM and Council of Ministers** are the real executive.
[39:0:1] - **Article 168**: the Governor is an integral part of the State Legislature without being a member.
[39:0:2] # Articles on the Office
[39:0:3] TABLE head: Article | Subject
  [39:0:3.0] 153 | A Governor for each State; 7th Amendment, 1956 – same person for two or more States
  [39:0:3.1] 154(1) | Executive power of the State vested in the Governor
  [39:0:3.2] 155 | Appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal (neither directly nor indirectly elected)
  [39:0:3.3] 156 | Term – holds office during the pleasure of the President
  [39:0:3.4] 157 | Qualifications – citizen of India, completed 35 years
  [39:0:3.5] 158 | Conditions – not an MP or MLA (must vacate seat), no office of profit; emoluments fixed by Parliament by law, cannot be reduced during term
  [39:0:3.6] 159 | Oath by the Chief Justice of the High Court (in his absence, the senior-most judge)
  [39:0:3.7] 202(3) | Emoluments charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State
[39:0:4] - A Governor of two or more States: salary shared among the States in the **ratio fixed by the President**.
[39:0:5] - **Conventions:** an **outsider** to the State; the State CM is consulted (not compulsory).
[39:0:6] # Term, Resignation, Removal
[39:0:7] - Normal term **5 years**, but continues **until his successor enters office** (Art. 156(3)); may be removed or transferred earlier and reappointed.
[39:0:8] - **Resigns to the President**; answers to the President (Art. 156(1)); the **State Legislature cannot remove** him.
[39:0:9] - **No procedure or grounds for removal** in the Constitution.
[39:0:10] - Not answerable to either House of Parliament, the State Legislature or the State Council of Ministers.
[39:0:11] APPSC-G1 2019: "The Governor quits office immediately after his term expires" is WRONG – he stays until the successor takes office.
[39:0:12] # B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010)
[39:0:13] - Pleasure doctrine is **not unfettered**; reasons need not be given, but removal **must not be arbitrary**.
[39:0:14] - Cannot be removed merely because the **Centre's government changes** or he is out of tune with the Centre; **limited judicial review**.
[39:0:15] # Immunity – Article 361
[39:0:16] - **Art. 361(1)**: not answerable to any court for acts done in office.
[39:0:17] - **Art. 361(2)**: **no criminal proceedings** started or continued, and no arrest, during the term – even with a court's permission.
[39:0:18] - **Art. 361(4)**: civil proceedings for personal acts can be started only after **2 months' notice** (so he can be sued).

## SUB 3 | Discretionary Powers – Article 163
[39:3:0] - **163(1)**: Council headed by CM aids and advises, **except where he acts in his discretion** (discretion explicitly mentioned); courts cannot review acts done in his own discretion.
[39:3:1] - **163(2)**: Governor's decision on whether a matter is discretionary is **final** – but not complete immunity from judicial review.
[39:3:2] - **163(3)**: courts **cannot inquire** into advice tendered by ministers.
[39:3:3] - Governor is **not bound in all matters**: he can reserve a Bill **against ministerial advice**. His discretion is **wider than the President's**.

## SUB 4 | Bills – Article 200 and Article 201
[39:4:0] # Governor's Options – Art. 200
[39:4:1] - 1. **Assent**, 2. **withhold** assent, 3. **return once** for reconsideration (**not a Money Bill**), 4. **reserve** for the President.
[39:4:2] - **Re-passed** returned Bill – Governor **must assent**; cannot refuse again.
[39:4:3] - A Bill may be reserved **only once**; again only if materially altered.
[39:4:4] - **Mandatory reservation**: only a Bill that **derogates from the powers of the High Court**.
[39:4:5] - Constitution sets **no time limit** for returning an ordinary Bill.
[39:4:6] # President's Options – Art. 201
[39:4:7] - Assent, withhold, or (non-Money Bill) direct the Governor to return it; Legislature reconsiders within **6 months**; President **not bound** to assent afterwards – **absolute veto**.
[39:4:8] - **No fixed time limit** for the President. Apr 2025: President withheld assent to TN's anti-NEET Bill.

## SUB 7 | President's Rule – Article 356
[39:7:0] # Provisions
[39:7:1] - When the government of a State **cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution** – on the **Governor's report or otherwise** (suo motu).
[39:7:2] - Grounds: **Art. 356** and **Art. 365** (State fails to follow Union directions).
[39:7:3] - Parliamentary approval; **6 months** at a time; **maximum 3 years** (APPSC 2019 key).
[39:7:4] - **Beyond 1 year** only if a **National Emergency** is in force and the **Election Commission certifies** elections cannot be held (**44th Amendment**).
[39:7:5] - State Legislature's powers exercised by or under Parliament (**Art. 357**); Assembly **suspended or dissolved**. Central laws reach the State through an **Art. 357(1)(a)** resolution – e.g. Water Amendment Act extended to Manipur (Dec 2025).
[39:7:6] # FIRSTs and Frequency

[39:7:8] - **Former J&K**: 6 months of **Governor's Rule under Section 92** of the J&K Constitution, then Art. 356 (e.g. 2018); after 2019, President's Rule under the Reorganisation Act until Oct 2024.
[39:7:9] - **Ayodhya (Dec 1992)**: BJP governments of **UP, MP, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh** dismissed; **Bihar not** (Bihar had President's Rule in 1995).
