=== Office of the Governor – Articles 153-161, Term, Removal, Immunity
# Articles on the office
| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| **153** | **A Governor for each State**; proviso added by the **7th Amendment, 1956** – the **same person** may be Governor of **two or more States** |
| **154** | **Executive power** of the State vested in the Governor, exercised directly or through subordinate officers |
| **155** | **Appointed by the President** by warrant under his hand and seal – neither directly nor indirectly elected |
| **156** | **Term** – holds office **during the pleasure of the President**; resigns to the President; normal term **5 years** (156(3)) |
| **157** | **Qualifications** – **citizen of India** (not necessarily by birth) and **completed 35 years** |
| **158** | **Conditions** – not a member of Parliament or a State Legislature (seat vacated on appointment), no **office of profit**; emoluments fixed by **Parliament by law**, not reduced during the term |
| **159** | **Oath** before the **Chief Justice of the High Court** (in his absence, the senior-most judge) |
| **160** | President provides for the Governor's functions in an unforeseen contingency |
| **161** | Pardoning power (offences against State laws) |
- **Nominal / constitutional head** of the State and its **executive head**; the **Chief Minister and Council of Ministers** are the **real executive**.
- **Article 168**: the Governor is an **integral part of the State Legislature**, though not a member.
- Emoluments **charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State** (Art. 202(3)); a Governor of two or more States has them shared in the **proportion the President fixes** (Art. 158(3A)).
- **Conventions:** usually an **outsider** to the State; the State **CM is consulted** (not compulsory).
# Term and removal
- Normal term **5 years**, but he **continues until his successor enters office** (Art. 156(3)) – "quits immediately after his term expires" is **wrong**.
- The five years are **not secure**: he serves at the President's pleasure (= Union Council of Ministers) and can be **removed or transferred at any time**, and reappointed.
- **No procedure or grounds for removal** are laid down in the Constitution; he is **not impeached** and **the State Legislature cannot remove** him (President, Election Commissioner and HC judges are removed by Parliament; the Governor is not).
- He is **responsible to the President**; **not answerable** to either House of Parliament, either House of the State Legislature or the State Council of Ministers.
- **B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010)**: the pleasure doctrine is **not unfettered** – removal must not be **arbitrary** or merely because the **Government at the Centre changed**; limited judicial review.
# Immunity – Article 361
- **361(1)**: not answerable to **any court** for acts done in the exercise of his powers and duties.
- **361(2)**: **no criminal proceedings** instituted or continued during the term – not even with a court's permission; **361(3)**: no arrest or imprisonment.
- **361(4)**: **civil proceedings** for **personal acts** only after **two months' written notice** – so "a Governor cannot be sued for any act" is **false**.
- Members of a State Legislature are separately immune for anything said or any vote in the House (**Art. 194(2)**).
> UPSC 2013: "No procedure has been laid down in the Constitution for the removal of a Governor" = correct. UPSC 2025: Art. 361(1), 361(2) and 194(2) all correct.

=== Powers of the Governor – Executive, Legislative, Financial, Judicial
# Executive powers
| Power | Article |
|---|---|
| All executive action of the State expressed in the **Governor's name** | **166** |
| Appoints the **CM** (not the President) and other Ministers on the CM's advice | **164(1)** |
| Appoints the **Advocate-General**, fixes his pay | **165** |
| Appoints Chairman and members of the **SPSC** – only the **President removes** them | **316/317** |
| Appoints the **State Election Commissioner** | **243K** |
| Constitutes the **State Finance Commission** (Panchayat/municipal finances) every 5 years | **243I / 243Y** |
| Appoints **District Judges** in consultation with the **High Court** | **233** |
| Report recommending **President's Rule** (not Art. 360) | **356** |
| Seeks information from the CM | **167** |
- **Executive head** of the State; represents the Centre in the State and by convention sends **fortnightly reports to the President** on the State's affairs.
- **Chancellor** of State universities under the State university laws (not Central or private ones); appoints their VCs.
- Does **not** appoint **High Court judges** (the President does; the Governor is consulted).
- Places the **CAG** report on the State, the **SPSC** report and the **State Finance Commission** report before the Legislature.
# Legislative powers
| Power | Article |
|---|---|
| **Summons, prorogues** (174(2)(a)) the Houses and **dissolves** the Assembly; gap between sessions **≤ 6 months** | **174** |
| Optional address / messages at any time | **175** |
| **Mandatory special address** – first session after each general election and **first session of each year** | **176** |
| Nominates **one-sixth** of the Legislative Council | **171** |
| Decides disqualification of MLAs (not anti-defection) on the **Election Commission's opinion** | **192** |
| **Ordinance** during recess | **213** |
- Dissolves the Assembly on the CM / Council of Ministers' advice (Speaker, CM or Chief Justice cannot).
- Under **Nabam Rebia (2016)** the Art. 174 and Art. 176 functions are on aid and advice; the address must reflect **Cabinet policy**. **January 2026**: Governors in Opposition-ruled States walked out of / skipped the Art. 176 address.
# Financial powers
- **Money Bill** introduced in the Assembly only on the **Governor's recommendation** (Art. 207); no demand for grants except on his recommendation.
- Causes the **annual financial statement (budget)** to be laid (Art. 202).
- **Contingency Fund of the State** is placed **at the disposal of the Governor** (Art. 267(2)).
# Ordinance and pardon – Governor vs President
| Basis | President | Governor |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinance | **Art. 123**; Union + Concurrent List; co-extensive with Parliament | **Art. 213**; State + Concurrent List |
| Approval | **Parliament** | **State Legislature** (not the President) |
| Prior Presidential instructions | Not applicable | Needed when a Bill with the same provisions would need **prior sanction** or **reservation** |
| Lapse | 6 weeks after Parliament reassembles | **6 weeks after the Legislature reassembles** |
| Withdrawal | Any time – 123(2)(b) | Any time – 213(2)(b) |
| Pardon | **Art. 72** – all cases, incl. court-martial and death sentence | **Art. 161** – offences against **State laws** only; cannot pardon a death sentence (can suspend, remit, commute) |
| Discretion | Very limited | **Comparatively wider** |
- A Governor's ordinance has the **same force as an Act** of the State Legislature.
- **Perarivalan (2022)**: the Governor is **bound by Cabinet advice** on remission under Art. 161.
- **Art. 333** nomination of an Anglo-Indian to the Assembly **ended on 25 January 2020** (104th Amendment).

=== Discretionary Powers – Article 163
# Article 163
- **163(1)**: a Council of Ministers headed by the CM **aids and advises** the Governor **except where he is required to act in his discretion** – the Constitution **explicitly mentions** the Governor's discretion (it gives the President none).
- **163(2)**: the Governor's decision on **whether a matter is within his discretion is final** – his discretionary acts cannot be called in question (not complete immunity after case law).
- **163(3)**: courts **cannot inquire** into the advice tendered by Ministers to the Governor.
- So the Governor is **not bound by advice in all matters**; he can **reserve a Bill even against ministerial advice**. His discretion is **wider than the President's**.
- Of four categories of the Governor's orders (executive action, orders on aid and advice, pardon, discretion), the one **not subject to judicial review** is where he **acts in his own discretion** without aid and advice.
- The President cannot **suo motu** reserve a State Bill; only the Governor reserves it.
# Constitutional vs situational discretion
| Constitutional (express) | Situational |
|---|---|
| **Reserving a Bill** for the President (Art. 200) | Appointing a CM in a **hung Assembly** |
| **Report recommending President's Rule** (Art. 356) | **Dismissing** a ministry that lost majority (Art. 164 pleasure clause) |
| Seeking information from the CM (Art. 167) | Dissolving the Assembly when the ministry lost majority |
| Administrator of an adjoining UT (Art. 239(2)) | Asking the Government to **prove majority** |
| Sixth Schedule royalty share; special responsibilities under Arts. 371-371J | – |
- **Not discretion**: **appointing Ministers** (on the CM's advice) and **making rules of business** (Art. 166(3)); consulting the High Court is not a Governor's power at all.
- **Art. 239(2)**: as administrator of an adjoining UT the Governor acts **independently of his State Council of Ministers**.
- Special responsibility for law and order: **371A Nagaland**, **371H Arunachal Pradesh**.
- **Fifth Schedule**: Governor **reports to the President** on Scheduled Areas; makes regulations for **peace and good government** after consulting the **Tribes Advisory Council** (not more than **20** members, about three-fourths ST MLAs). **Sixth Schedule**: Autonomous District Councils.
- A CM who loses majority **must resign or may be dismissed** – e.g. **West Bengal, May 2026**: Mamata Banerjee refused to resign; the Art. 164 **pleasure clause** was cited.
# Hung Assembly – order of invitation (Sarkaria / Punchhi)
- 1. **Pre-poll alliance** → 2. largest single party with support → 3. post-poll coalition → 4. post-poll alliance with outside support – then an **early floor test**.
- **Tamil Nadu, May 2026**: Governor invited **C. Joseph Vijay** on **120 letters of support** in the 234-member House with an early floor test (Sarkaria-Punchhi order).
- **Justice Kurian Joseph Committee (TN, 2026)**: proposes a **new Schedule** codifying the Governor's discretion, appointment from a State panel and removal by Assembly resolution.

=== President's Rule – Article 356
# Provisions
- Imposed when the government of a State **cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution** – on the **Governor's report** or **otherwise** (suo motu).
- Second ground: **Art. 365** – a State **fails to comply with Union directions** (Art. 355 = Centre's duty to ensure States run per the Constitution).
- Approval by both Houses within **2 months** (simple majority); **6 months** at a time; **maximum 3 years**.
- **Beyond 1 year** only if a **National Emergency** is in force and the **Election Commission certifies** elections cannot be held (**44th Amendment**).
- Effects: the President takes over the State executive, administered by the **Governor on his behalf**; the Legislature's powers go to **Parliament** (**Art. 357**) – **Parliament**, not the Union Cabinet, passes State Bills and budget; the Assembly is **suspended or dissolved** (not automatically dissolved); **Art. 19 is not suspended**; the High Court is untouched.
- Under President's Rule there is **no State Council of Ministers** to advise the Governor (it exists even after dissolution or the CM's resignation, as caretaker).
# Firsts and frequency
| Fact | Answer |
|---|---|
| **First imposition** | **Punjab, 20 June 1951**; PEPSU 1953 was second |
| First dismissal of an elected (non-Congress) government | **Kerala, 1959** |
| Never imposed | **Telangana, Chhattisgarh** |
| Most often | **Manipur**; latest spell from **13 Feb 2025**, revoked **Feb 2026** (Y. Khemchand Singh CM) |
| Times used | Over 125 |
- **Ayodhya (Dec 1992)**: BJP governments of **UP, MP, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh** dismissed and Assemblies dissolved; **Bihar's was not** (Bihar had a brief spell in 1995).
- **Former J&K**: 6 months of **Governor's Rule under Sec. 92** of the J&K Constitution (a provision **no other State had**), then Art. 356 (e.g. Dec 2018); **Art. 360** did not apply and the **right to property** stayed a FR there. Since **31 Oct 2019** J&K is a UT (Reorganisation Act 2019) – none of this survives.
# Judicial review – S.R. Bommai (1994, 9 judges)
- Proclamation **judicially reviewable**; Centre must disclose **relevant material**.
- Majority tested on the **floor of the Assembly**, not in Raj Bhavan.
- Assembly **not dissolved before Parliament approves**; a dismissed ministry **can be restored**.
- Upheld dismissal of MP, Rajasthan, HP governments on **secularism**.
| Proper use | Improper use |
|---|---|
| Hung House with no alternative | Intra-party disputes |
| **Flagrant defiance** of constitutional directions | Maladministration alone |
| Physical breakdown | Ordinary law and order |
- **Rameshwar Prasad (2006)**: Bihar dissolution on unproven fears of horse-trading struck down.
- **AP High Court (2020)**: "constitutional breakdown" observation – **stayed by the Supreme Court**.

=== Bills – Articles 200 and 201; Assent Cases
# Governor's options – Art. 200
- 1. **Assent**, 2. **withhold** assent, 3. **return** for reconsideration (**not a Money Bill**), 4. **reserve** for the President – **Art. 200**, not 201.
- A returned Bill **re-passed** with or without amendment – the Governor **must assent**.
- Constitution fixes **no time limit** ("as soon as possible") for returning a Bill.
- **Must reserve**: a Bill that **derogates from the powers of the High Court**. **May reserve**: ultra vires Bills, against DPSP or national interest, compulsory acquisition (Art. 31A).
- Reservation of State Bills is an example of **central control over the States**.
# Reserved to get validity or protection
| Article | Bill |
|---|---|
| **288(2)** | State tax on **water / electricity** of an inter-State river authority – **has no effect unless reserved and assented** |
| 31A / 31C | Compulsory acquisition; DPSP laws – reservation only for protection |
| **254(2)** | Concurrent List law **repugnant** to a Union law – prevails only if reserved and assented |
| **304(b)** | Restrictions on inter-State trade – **previous sanction** of the President before **introduction** |
| 360 | Money Bills during a Financial Emergency |
# President's options – Art. 201
- Assent, withhold, or (non-Money Bill) direct the Governor to **return** it; the Legislature reconsiders **within 6 months**; the re-passed Bill goes **back to the President**, who is **not bound** to assent.
- **No fixed time limit** for the President. A Bill reserved once can be reserved again only if **materially altered**.
# Assent time-limit cases
| Date | Case | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | **State of Punjab v. Principal Secy to Governor** | Act **as soon as possible**; cannot keep Bills pending indefinitely |
| **8 Apr 2025** | **State of TN v. Governor of TN** | Timelines **1 month / 3 months / 1 month** on re-passage; referral of 10 re-enacted Bills unconstitutional; **deemed assent** under **Art. 142** |
| **Nov 2025** | **16th Presidential Reference** (Art. 143) | **No court-fixed timelines**; deemed assent rejected; only limited mandamus for prolonged delay; review limited to mala fides and procedure; Governor **not bound by advice** in choosing among Art. 200 options; Governor = "**guide, philosopher and friend**" |
