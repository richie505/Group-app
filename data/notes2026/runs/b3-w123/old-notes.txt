[123:0:0] TABLE head: Year | Programme | Fact
  [123:0:0.0] Sep 1982 | DWCRA | Development of Women and Children in Rural Areas; Centre:State 75:25
  [123:0:0.1] 2000 | Velugu | Women's groups against poverty; World Bank-aided; SERP registered 2000
  [123:0:0.2] 2004 | Pavala Vaddi | Interest subsidy for SHGs (3% effective)
  [123:0:0.3] 2004-05 | Indira Kranthi Patham (IKP) | Velugu + DWCRA merged – all anti-poverty programmes in one integrated scheme; flagship of SERP
  [123:0:0.4] 2011 | Stree Nidhi | Credit cooperative federation promoted by Mandal Samakhyas + GoAP
[123:0:1] - **SERP** = Society for Elimination of Rural Poverty (rural); **MEPMA** = Mission for Elimination of Poverty in Municipal Areas (**urban** SHGs).
[123:0:2] - **SHG registration:** **no registration is mandatory** (informal groups of 10-20).
[123:0:3] - **NRLM "Institutions of the Poor":** **SHGs and their federations** (VOs, MS) – not PACS or RRBs.
[123:0:4] - **AP leads India** in SHG-bank linkage: **36%** national share, **99.66%** recovery (2024-25). Members lack individual **CIBIL** histories.
[123:0:5] - **MEPMA limits:** SHG bank linkage up to **Rs 20 lakh**; individual loans **Rs 50,000-2 lakh**.
[123:0:6] # Stree Nidhi
[123:0:7] - **Streenidhi Credit Cooperative Federation Ltd** (2011): **banking institution promoted by SHG women**; a **community-based micro-finance** body of the Mandal Samakhyas with GoAP; registered under the AP Cooperative Societies Act, 1964.
[123:0:8] - **Purpose:** **timely and affordable credit** to SHG members through VOs/SLFs and MS/TLFs; also deposits, loan insurance, Business Correspondent.
[123:0:9] - **Rate:** **13%** at launch (2011); **11%** now (livelihood loans up to Rs 1 lakh, APSES 2024-25). Loans Rs 21,123.71 crore (2011-2024).
[123:0:10] - Stree Nidhi describes itself as a "**historic necessity**" – SHG women needed their own credit body.
[123:0:11] - **PS-SBG** (Pattabhi Sitaramayya-Self Business Group) for rural women entrepreneurs: launched by **Andhra Bank**.
[123:0:12] > Traps: MEPMA urban, SERP rural; IKP merged Velugu and DWCRA (not Indira Prabha – that is land development); SHGs need no registration.

## SUB 2 | NABARD – Establishment and Functions
[123:2:0] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [123:2:0.0] Recommended by | CRAFICARD – Committee to Review Arrangements for Institutional Credit for Agriculture and Rural Development, chaired by B. Sivaraman
  [123:2:0.1] Act / set up | NABARD Act 1981; set up 12 July 1982
  [123:2:0.2] Took over | RBI's ACD and RPCC and the whole of ARDC
  [123:2:0.3] Capital / HQ | Rs 100 crore; Mumbai; fully Government-owned since 2019
[123:2:1] - **Role:** apex **refinancing** institution for rural credit – **term loans/refinance to State Cooperative Banks**, RRBs and commercial banks; **long-term loans to State Governments** to contribute to the **share capital** of cooperative credit societies.
[123:2:2] - **Does NOT lend short-term credit directly** to farmers.
[123:2:3] - **Supervision:** inspects **RRBs, State and District Central Cooperative Banks** – **not PACS** (inspected by the Registrar/DCCB).
[123:2:4] - **SHG-Bank Linkage** (1992-93); **KCC** model scheme (**1998**, R.V. Gupta Committee). Same year as NABARD: **EXIM Bank** (1982).
[123:2:5] - Order: RRBs (1975) → NABARD (1982) → SHG linkage (1992-93) → KCC (1998).
[123:2:6] > Trap: NABARD does not inspect primary cooperative societies.

[123:2:8] - **SIDBI:** set up **2 April 1990**; apex bank for MSME finance (HQ Lucknow).

## SUB 4 | Sources of Agricultural Credit and RRBs in AP
[123:4:0] - **Commercial banks** = **major source** of institutional farm credit in AP (as in India: SCBs over 70% of formal farm credit).
[123:4:1] - **2007-08:** **cooperative credit societies** gave about **one-fifth** of AP's crop loans; commercial banks most of the rest.
[123:4:2] - Institutional: cooperatives, commercial banks, RRBs, Government. **Non-institutional:** moneylenders.
[123:4:3] - Exclusion: **tenant, tribal, women and oral-lessee** farmers – no **land records**.
[123:4:4] TABLE head: Loan term | Period
  [123:4:4.0] Short-term | Up to 1 year, maximum 15 months
  [123:4:4.1] Medium-term | 15 months to 3 (or 5) years
  [123:4:4.2] Long-term | Above 3 (or 5) years
[123:4:5] # RRBs
[123:4:6] - Set up **2 October 1975** on the **Narasimham Working Group** (1975) recommendations; first = **Prathama Bank** (Moradabad); RRB Act 1976; shares Centre 50 : State 15 : sponsor bank 35.
[123:4:7] - **One State, One RRB:** AP's four RRBs merged into **Andhra Pradesh Grameena Bank** from **1 May 2025**; sponsor **Union Bank of India**; RRBs in India 43 → **28**.
[123:4:8] - **Andhra Bank:** founded **1923**, Machilipatnam, by **Bhogaraju Pattabhi Sitaramayya**; merged into **Union Bank of India** (1 April 2020).
[123:4:9] - **Narasimham Committee I (1991):** began banking reforms (different from the 1975 Working Group).

=== NOTES 3:124 | Agricultural Marketing in Andhra Pradesh – Market Yards, Rythu Bazaars and E-marketing
## SUB 0 | Rythu Bazars
[124:0:0] - **Concept:** **direct marketing between farmers and consumers**, no middlemen → **better price to farmers and fair price to consumers**.
[124:0:1] - **Started 1999**; first Rythu Bazars **functioning from January 1999**.
[124:0:2] - **Ryot Bazar idea first in Punjab (Apni Mandi)**; **AP was the second** State.
[124:0:3] - Run day-to-day by an **Estate Officer**; district coordinating officer = **Joint Collector** (not the District Collector); outside the purview of Agricultural Market Committees.
[124:0:4] - **Pricing:** about **25% above wholesale** and **25% below local retail** prices.
[124:0:5] - **Number:** 124 functioning, 28 under construction (2024-25); 129 (2025-26).
[124:0:6] - **Price intervention:** the State's quickest channel – government procures **perishables** through them when prices swing.
[124:0:7] TABLE head: Feature | Regulated market yard | Rythu Bazar
  [124:0:7.0] Basis | AP (AP&L) Markets Act, 1966 | Executive scheme, 1999
  [124:0:7.1] Managed by | Agricultural Market Committee | Estate Officer
  [124:0:7.2] Seller | Farmers sell to licensed traders | Farmer sells directly to consumer
[124:0:8] > Trap (APPSC): "District Collector is the coordinating officer" is the wrong statement – it is the Joint Collector.
[124:0:9] ~~ Sources: AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.95 (Rythu Bazars 124, perishables); OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 05 p.21 (direct farmer-to-consumer); app notes 3:124 sub 3
[124:0:10] - **Department:** Rythu Bazars come under the **Revenue Department** (Estate Officer; Joint Collector coordinates).
[124:0:11] - **AP Budget 2019-20:** **17 new Rythu Bazars** proposed for direct sale by farmers.
## SUB 1 | Cooperative Marketing – MARKFED and Price Stabilisation
[124:1:0] - **AP MARKFED** (AP State Cooperative Marketing Federation Ltd) = **apex organisation of the Cooperative Marketing Societies**; supplies inputs, cattle feed, fertilisers; works through RSKs and PACS.
[124:1:1] - **13 DCMSs** procure produce at minimum administered prices, also on behalf of MARKFED.
[124:1:2] - **Price Stabilisation Fund:** **Rs 3,000 crore** (2019-20); **MARKFED** played the key role; protects MSP for **turmeric, minor millets, sweet orange, banana, onion**; 2019 – groundnut farmers of 7 districts compensated.
[124:1:3] ~~ Sources: AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.64-65; app notes 3:124 sub 5
[124:1:4] - **Purpose of the PSF:** intervention in **agricultural and horticultural crops** to secure farmers a just price.
## SUB 2 | Pledge Loans, Storage and Cold Storage
[124:2:0] - **Rythu Bandhu Pathakam** (Raitu Bandhu): **pledge loan** scheme – farmer stores produce in **market-committee godowns** and borrows against it (hypothecation); avoids distress sale.
[124:2:1] - **Capital Intensive Subsidy Scheme for cold storages:** introduced **July 1999**.
[124:2:2] - **APSWC:** set up **August 1958**; share capital **50:50 GoAP and CWC**.
[124:2:3] TABLE head: Market committee godowns | 2024-25 | 2025-26
  [124:2:3.0] Total | 1,052; 9.65 lakh MT | 1,052
  [124:2:3.1] Operational | 626 | 643
[124:2:4] - Gap: limited operational storage, need for **cold storage**. **AIF**: farm-gate infrastructure at **RBKs/RSKs**.
[124:2:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP Economy Static-1 01 p.14 (cold storage July 1999); app notes 3:124 sub 6
## SUB 3 | Market Yards, Market Committees and the 1966 Act
[124:3:0] - Farmers selling alone face **bargaining imbalance**: forced sales, false weights, unrecorded prices, middlemen margins.
[124:3:1] TABLE head: Act / rule | Year | Provision
  [124:3:1.0] AP (Agricultural Produce and Livestock) Markets Act | 1966 | Act No. 16 of 1966; crops, livestock and products
  [124:3:1.1] AP (AP&L) Markets Rules | 1969 | Under Section 33 of the Act; licensing
  [124:3:1.2] Three central farm laws | 2020 | Repealed Nov 2021 → 1966 framework remains operative
  [124:3:1.3] Model APMC Act | 2003 | Single market fee, registration in place of licensing
[124:3:2] - **1966 Act mechanisms:** open auction, standard weights, recorded prices, **licensed buyers**.
[124:3:3] - **Setting up:** notified area → Market Committee → notified market (yard). District supervision: **DATMO**.
[124:3:4] - **Market fee: 1%**; Rs 433.39 crore (2024-25 to Dec 2024). AMCs pay **25%** of income (10% before **Oct 2015**) into the **Central Market Fund**.
[124:3:5] - **Adoni** = second largest market yard; **Guntur Mirchi Yard** = Asia's largest chilli market; **Agri Export Zone for red chillies** in Guntur.
[124:3:6] - **Pulpwood trading (Feb 2016):** **Subabul, Eucalyptus and Casuarina** sold to paper mills through market committees – **not bamboo**.
[124:3:7] ~~ Sources: AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.95 (market fee); app notes 3:124 sub 1-2
## SUB 4 | e-Marketing – e-NAM and eFarmarket
[124:4:0] - **e-NAM:** 1,656 mandis in 23 States and 4 UTs; **AP 33 markets**; each AP mandi got free software + **Rs 30 lakh**; 39-52 FPOs registered.
[124:4:1] - **Mostly intra-mandi:** most e-NAM trades are within one mandi; AP: only **11 mandis** in inter-mandi trade (Rs 13.98 crore, Dec 2024). Helped **price discovery**, not yet payments or inter-mandi trade.
[124:4:2] - **Model APLM Act 2017:** makes the **whole State a single market**, private markets, e-trading, unified licence – i.e. pushes **inter-mandi and inter-State** trade, not intra-mandi.
[124:4:3] - **eFarmarket:** AP = **first State** with an e-marketing platform linking its farmers to traders across India (APFEVCL SPV, 2021). **CM APP** (Continuous Monitoring of Agricultural Prices and Procurement): farm-gate prices collected **daily from every RBK** – AP the only State.

## SUB 5 | FPOs and Collective Marketing

[124:5:1] - **Farmer Producer Companies** get financial assistance from **NABARD and SFAC** (equity grant, credit guarantee).
