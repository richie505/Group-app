[0:0] # Core facts
[0:1] TABLE head: Item | Fact

  [0:1.1] Created by | Marrakesh Agreement (signed April 1994) that ended the Uruguay Round
  [0:1.2] Predecessor | GATT – WTO is "the new name of GATT"; the WTO replaced the GATT as an organisation
  [0:1.3] HQ | Geneva, Switzerland (also ILO Geneva; UN New York; FAO Rome)
  [0:1.4] India | Member since 1 January 1995 (founder member of both GATT and WTO)

  [0:1.6] Director-General | Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (Nigeria) – 7th DG and first woman, appointed Feb 2021, took office 1 March 2021
  [0:1.7] Earlier DG | Roberto Azevedo (Brazil) – 6th DG, replaced Pascal Lamy on 1 September 2013 (second term 2017)
[0:2] - **Nature:** an **international organisation** (GATT was only an agreement) with a **broader multilateral framework** – goods, **services, IPR and investment measures**.
[0:3] - **"Third pillar" of the world economy** with the **IMF and World Bank**; the "**main security guard of international trade**"; promotes **multilateral trade** (not financial support or unilateral trade).

## SUB 1 | GATT, Uruguay Round and the Dunkel Draft
[1:0] # GATT
[1:1] - **GATT = General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade**; signed **30 October 1947** at **Geneva** by **23** founding contracting parties (not Marrakesh); in force **1948**; GATT office set up at **Geneva, 1948**.
[1:2] - **India**: GATT member since **8 July 1948**.
[1:3] - Focus: cut **tariffs and quantitative restrictions** in goods; **8 rounds** of negotiations; GATT survives as part of the WTO agreements.
[1:4] TABLE head: Basis | GATT | WTO
  [1:4.0] Set up | 1947 (in force 1948) | 1 January 1995
  [1:4.1] Nature | An agreement | An international organisation
  [1:4.2] Coverage | Mainly goods | Goods, services, IPR, investment measures
[1:5] # Uruguay Round (1986-1993/94)
[1:6] - **8th GATT round**; launched **September 1986** at **Punta del Este**; concluded **1993** (December), Final Act signed **1994**; **123** contracting parties.
[1:7] - It "pertains to" **GATT** and **created the WTO** (not UNICEF, IMF, UNESCO).
[1:8] - Extended rules beyond goods: **GATS** (services), **TRIPS** (IPR), **TRIMS** (investment). Aims: cut farm subsidies, lift investment restrictions, open services (banking, insurance), protect IPR.
[1:9] # Dunkel Draft
[1:10] - **Arthur Dunkel**: **Director-General of GATT** (1980-1993), chair of the Trade Negotiations Committee.
[1:11] - **Dunkel Draft** (Draft Final Act of the Uruguay Round) tabled **20 December 1991**; concerned with **trade**; best known for **intellectual property** rules (RAS 1992).
[1:12] - **UPSC 1994 statements:** acceptance was **all-or-nothing** (single undertaking – "mandatory to accept all proposals") and agriculture proposal = **cut farm subsidies** → correct; it **phased out** (did not reiterate) the **Multi-Fibre Arrangement**, and Parliament never "accepted" it → answer "**1 and 2**".
[1:13] - **CDS 2018 chronology:** Uruguay Round concluded 1994 ✔, WTO 1995 ✔, Nasdaq 1971 ✔ – **SWIFT** began in **1973** (live 1977), **not 1985**.
[1:14] > Repeats: Dunkel = GATT (×3), Uruguay Round → WTO (×3), GATT office Geneva 1948. NextGen p.55 prints "20 October 1991" and "110 countries" for GATT – the Draft Final Act is dated 20 December 1991, and 23 were the founders (110 is later membership).

## SUB 2 | Agreement on Agriculture – Pillars and Subsidy Boxes
[2:0] # Pillars
[2:1] - AoA negotiated in the Uruguay Round, in force **1 January 1995**. **Three pillars: market access, domestic support, export competition** – **anti-dumping and countervailing duties are NOT a pillar**.
[2:2] - "Agreement on Agriculture", "SPS Agreement" and "**Peace Clause**" = **WTO** terms (UPSC 2015), as are **amber, blue and green boxes** (UPSC 2016).
[2:3] # Subsidy boxes
[2:4] TABLE head: Box | Covers | Treatment
  [2:4.0] Green | Research, pest/disease control, infrastructure, public stockholding for food security, decoupled income support, environment, regional development | Minimal or no trade distortion; no limits
  [2:4.1] Blue | "Amber box with conditions" – direct payments under production-limiting programmes | Trade-distorting but exempt from reduction; no spending limit
  [2:4.2] Amber | Price- or quantity-linked support: MSP, power, fertiliser, seed, irrigation (measured as AMS) | Trade-distorting; to be reduced to de minimis
[2:5] - **Blue Box = "production-limiting subsidies that distort trade"** (APPSC Group 2).
[2:6] - **No Red Box** in the AoA (traffic-light "red = forbidden" belongs to the SCM Agreement); **White, Pink, Yellow** are not boxes either.
[2:7] - **S&D box**: special and differential support for developing countries.
[2:8] - **India + China proposal (July 2017):** eliminate developed countries' **Amber Box (AMS)** support beyond de minimis first.
[2:9] # Mechanics
[2:10] TABLE head: Term | Rule
  [2:10.0] De minimis | Amber support allowed up to 5% of production value (developed), 10% (developing)
  [2:10.1] Total AMS cut | 20% over 6 years (developed); 13.3% over 10 years (developing)
  [2:10.2] Blue Box conditions | Payments on fixed areas and yields, or on 85% or less of base production, or a fixed number of livestock
  [2:10.3] Tariffication | Non-tariff barriers converted into tariffs; cuts 36% over 6 years (developed), 24% over 10 years (developing)
  [2:10.4] Export subsidy cut | Developed: 36% by value or 21% by volume over 6 years; developing 24% / 14% over 10 years
[2:11] - **A-R (UPPSC 1998):** "India need not reduce subsidies under WTO" is **false** although India is a developing country (R true) – developing countries also had reduction commitments.
[2:12] - **Price-gap payment (MSP minus market price) paid directly to farmers:** keyed **Blue Box** by UPPSC 2016 (direct payment); strictly, such deficiency payments are Amber unless tied to production limits.
[2:13] > Most asked: boxes (×9) – blue box definition, no red box, MSP = amber, Amber in the India-China proposal. Trap: "Red box" and "Blue box is subject to cuts".

## SUB 3 | TRIPS, TRIMS, SPS and India's Compliance Laws
[3:0] # Agreements
[3:1] TABLE head: Agreement | Subject | India
  [3:1.0] TRIPS (1995) | Minimum IPR standards (patents, GI, designs, copyright) | Patents Act amended 2005 (product patents); GI Act 1999; Designs Act 2000 (in force 11 May 2001); PPV&FR Act 2001
  [3:1.1] TRIMS – Trade-Related Investment Measures | Investment measures affecting trade in goods only | Phase-out of local-content and trade-balancing rules
  [3:1.2] GATS | Trade in services | Liberalisation commitments
  [3:1.3] SPS / TBT | Food safety, plant and animal health / technical standards | Food Safety and Standards Act 2006
  [3:1.4] TFA (Bali 2013) | Simplify customs | Ratified April 2016
  [3:1.5] ITA (1996) | Zero duty on IT products | Signatory
[3:2] - **TRIMS (UPSC 2020):** **quantitative restrictions on imports by foreign investors are prohibited** ✔; apply to **goods, not services** ✘; **not concerned with regulating foreign investment** itself ✔ → "**1 and 3 only**". TRIPS and TRIMS = **WTO**.
[3:3] - **SPS:** for food-safety standards in dispute settlement the WTO uses the **Codex Alimentarius Commission** (set up by **FAO and WHO, 1963**).
[3:4] - **Trade Facilitation Agreement (UPSC 2017):** India ratified it ✔, part of the **Bali package (MC9, 2013)** ✔; it entered into force **22 February 2017**, not January 2016 → "**I and II only**".
[3:5] # India's IPR laws
[3:6] - **GI of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999** (in force 15 September 2003) – enacted to comply with **WTO (TRIPS)** obligations, not ILO/IMF/UNCTAD. GI = an IPR; India relies on **TRIPS**, not the Lisbon Agreement.
[3:7] - GI examples: **Darjeeling Tea – India's first GI (2004-05)**; **Allahabad Surkha guava (2007-08)**; **Lucknow Chikan craft (2008-09)**; **Banaras brocades and sarees (2009-10)**; Nizamabad black pottery (2015-16). So all four in the UPPSC 2011 question are protected.
[3:8] - **Patents Act 1970** came into force **20 April 1972**, on the **N. Rajagopala Ayyangar Committee (1957)** report; **Patents (Second Amendment) Act, 2002** (Act 38 of 2002, passed 2002); product patents from 2005.
[3:9] - **Industrial designs:** protection since **11 May 2001** under the **Designs Act, 2000**, enacted for **TRIPS**; it adopted the **Locarno classification** of designs.
[3:10] # Subsidies (SCM Agreement)
[3:11] - **Prohibited ("red light")**, **actionable** (countervailing duty), **non-actionable** (R&D, regional development, environmental). Amber Box is AoA, not SCM.
[3:12] # India's trade policy and the WTO
[3:13] - **A-R (UPSC 2003):** EXIM Policy 2002-07 was liberal and market-oriented ✔; GATT/WTO aided liberalisation ✔ – both true, **R not the explanation**.
[3:14] - **A-R (UPSC 1998):** the **USA threatened** WTO sanctions against developing countries for not observing **ILO conventions** ✔; but the USA **has not itself ratified** most of those conventions ✘.
[3:15] - India prohibits GM food imports without approval; six India-US disputes settled (June 2023), India imports US apples.

## SUB 5 | Public Stockholding and the Peace Clause
[5:0] - **Peace Clause (Bali MC9, 2013):** no country is legally barred from **food-security public stockholding (PSH)** even if the subsidy **breaches de minimis or AMS limits**, until a permanent solution is found.
[5:1] - Conditions: **existing programmes and traditional staples only**; **notification** required; no harm to others' food security. It was **not made permanent** at MC13 or MC14.
[5:2] - The Peace Clause concept sits in the **Agreement on Agriculture** (original "due restraint" clause, Art. 13).
[5:3] - **India first to invoke it** – for **rice** (2020, for marketing year 2018-19).
[5:4] - India's complaint: MSP procurement for the **NFSA** is counted as Amber Box on an **outdated historical reference price** (1986-88).
[5:5] - India with the **African Group** seeks a **permanent solution**; not achieved at **MC13** or **MC14**.

## SUB 6 | Fisheries Subsidies and E-commerce
[6:0] # Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies (AFS)
[6:1] - Adopted at **MC12 (Geneva, June 2022)**; **in force September 2025**.
[6:2] - Bans subsidies to **IUU fishing**, fishing on the **unregulated high seas**, and **overfished stocks** (unless rebuilding).
[6:3] - Covers **marine wild capture**; **aquaculture and inland fishing are exempt**. A **Fish Fund** helps poorer members.
[6:4] - **First WTO agreement with an environmental-sustainability objective**; TFA and AFS = the **only two new multilateral agreements in 30 years**.
[6:5] - **India = 123rd** member to deposit its Instrument of Acceptance (**July 2026**).
[6:6] - **Phase 2** (overcapacity, overfishing): **deferred at MC14 to the General Council, Geneva**.
[6:7] - **AP angle:** Indian fisher bodies, incl. **AP's marine fisher folk**, want a "**fisher-centric**" approach.
[6:8] # E-commerce
[6:9] - **Moratorium** on customs duties on electronic transmissions (since **MC2, 1998**) **lapsed on 31 March 2026**.
[6:10] - **E-Commerce Agreement** (plurilateral): **66 members** signed separately. India's concern: plurilaterals (IFD, E-commerce) may **fragment the WTO** by moving away from consensus.
