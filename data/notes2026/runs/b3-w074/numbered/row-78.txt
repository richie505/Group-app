## SUB 0 | FDI, FPI and FII – Meaning and What Counts
[0:0] # FDI
[0:1] - **FDI**: investment for **lasting interest and management control**; a **largely non-debt-creating** capital flow (UPSC 2020) – not debt-servicing, not FII buying G-secs; longer-term and **stable**.
[0:2] - Legal line: capital instruments bought by a person resident outside India in an **unlisted** Indian company (any stake), or **10% or more** of post-issue paid-up equity of a **listed** company; 10% threshold from the **Arvind Mayaram Committee**.
[0:3] - Instruments: equity shares, debentures, preference shares, share warrants.
[0:4] - **Counts as FDI (UPSC 2012):** **subsidiaries of foreign companies**, **majority foreign equity** in Indian companies, **companies exclusively financed by foreign companies** – **portfolio investment is NOT** → "**1, 2 and 3 only**".
[0:5] - **Can be included in FDI (UPSC 2021):** **FCCBs**, **FII investment with certain conditions**, **GDRs** – **NRE deposits are not** → "**1, 2 and 3**".
[0:6] - **Not FDI:** buying **bonds or stocks** of a company overseas (portfolio). FDI = acquiring a mill abroad, a wholly owned firm abroad, building a new plant abroad.
[0:7] TABLE head: Type | Meaning | Example
  [0:7.0] Greenfield | New facility | Hyundai, Sriperumbudur
  [0:7.1] Brownfield | Acquisition | Vodafone-Hutchison Essar
[0:8] - Brings capital, **technology**, management skills, competition, jobs; finances the CAD with stable capital. **Gross FDI is not all fresh capital** (includes reinvested earnings, accounting changes).
[0:9] - **MNCs** locate near markets (A) because they seek **more profit** (R) – both true, R explains A.
[0:10] # FDI vs FPI / FII
[0:11] TABLE head: Basis | FDI | FPI / FII
  [0:11.0] Threshold | ≥10% listed, or any unlisted stake | <10% of a listed company
  [0:11.1] Nature | Long-term, stable, control | Liquid, short-term "hot money", more volatile
  [0:11.2] Brings | Capital + technology + management | Only financial capital – raises capital availability in general
  [0:11.3] Target | Specific sectors / enterprises | Securities (secondary market)
  [0:11.4] Regulator | DPIIT + RBI | SEBI (registration) + RBI
[0:12] - **Key difference (UPSC 2011):** "**FII helps in increasing capital availability in general, while FDI only targets specific sectors**".
[0:13] - **FII** = portfolio investment by foreign institutional investors. **SEBI FPI Regulations, 2019** unified **FIIs, sub-accounts and QFIs** as FPIs. FPI opened to India in **1992**.
[0:14] - **Most volatile flows:** **FPI and NRI deposits** (interest-rate driven) – "Both". Excess portfolio flows drove the **East Asian and Latin American crises** → **FDI** is the good inflow for a host country (UPSC 2002).
[0:15] - **SEBI relaxing norms for sovereign wealth funds** (CDS 2011) – to **attract more foreign investment**.
[0:16] > Most asked (×18): what counts as FDI, non-debt character, FII vs FDI difference, P-Notes. Trap: "FII is more stable than FDI".

## SUB 1 | Forms of Foreign Capital – ADR/GDR, P-Notes, ECB, NRI Deposits
[1:0] - Foreign capital includes **FDI, trade credit, NRI deposits and ECBs**.
[1:1] TABLE head: Form | Classification
  [1:1.0] FDI | Non-debt, long-term, control (≥10%)
  [1:1.1] FPI / FII | Non-debt, portfolio (<10%)
  [1:1.2] ECB, trade credit, FCCB | Debt
  [1:1.3] NRI deposits (FCNR(B), NRE) | Banking capital (debt)
  [1:1.4] ADR / GDR | Equity raised abroad = Foreign Equity Investment; counted under portfolio investment in BoP
  [1:1.5] Participatory Notes | Offshore derivative instruments
  [1:1.6] Masala bond | Rupee-denominated bond issued abroad
[1:2] - **P-Notes**: issued by **registered FIIs/FPIs** to overseas investors who want exposure to Indian stocks **without registering with SEBI** (UPSC 2007, 2019).
[1:3] - **FCNR(B)** replaced FCNR(A) on **15 May 1993** – term deposit in foreign currency.
[1:4] - **ECBs** are regulated by the **RBI under FEMA**.
[1:5] - **Old data PYQs:** largest forex inflow in **2005-06 = portfolio investment**; FII investment in **calendar 2005 ≈ US $10.7 bn**.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Sheet Indian Economy p.29 (P-Notes issued by registered FII/FPI), p.72 (P-Notes vs FDI); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. part 05 p.145 (Participatory Notes); app notes 3:78 sub 2

[2:11] # Prohibited sectors
[2:12] - **Lottery**; gambling, betting, casinos; **chit funds**; **Nidhi companies**; trading in **TDRs**; real estate business and farmhouses; **cigars and cigarettes** of tobacco; **atomic energy**; **railway operations** (except permitted activities).
[2:13] - **Railway infrastructure is NOT prohibited** (100% automatic); petroleum refining, cable networks, newspapers, defence, health care allow FDI.

## SUB 4 | FPI Limits and Flows
[4:0] - **G-sec limits (General Route):** **6%** of Central G-secs, **2%** of State securities; also VRR and FAR routes. Limits for 2024-25 were kept **unchanged**.
[4:1] - **Corporate bonds:** FPI limit raised to **15% (from 9%)** in **Union Budget 2020-21**.
[4:2] - **2024-25 (APPSC Group 2 Mains 2025):** rupee moved in a **narrow range** despite FPI outflows; RBI set FPI limits for debt and **Credit Default Swaps**; G-sec and corporate bond limits **unchanged**; RBI **framework (Nov 2024)** to **reclassify FPI holdings above 10% as FDI** → "**All the above**".
[4:3] - **JP Morgan EM bond index:** India's target weight **10%**.
[4:4] - Drivers: **US Fed rates and global risk appetite**. FY26 outflows about **₹2.2 lakh crore**.

## SUB 5 | Press Note 3 – Land-border Countries
[5:0] - **April 2020**: all FDI from a country sharing a **land border** with India needs the **Government route**.
[5:1] - Covers China, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Myanmar, Bhutan, Afghanistan – **Sri Lanka not covered**.
[5:2] - **Amended March 2026, effective 1 May 2026:** firms with up to **10%** Chinese/Hong Kong beneficial ownership may use the **automatic route**; beneficial owner as per **PML Rules, 2005**.
