=== WTO Basics – Establishment, HQ, Members, DG, Principles
# Core facts
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Established | **1 January 1995** (not 1994 – 1994 is the Marrakesh Agreement) |
| Created by | **Marrakesh Agreement** (signed **April 1994**) that ended the Uruguay Round |
| Predecessor | **GATT** – WTO is "the new name of GATT"; the WTO **replaced the GATT** as an organisation |
| HQ | **Geneva**, Switzerland (also ILO Geneva; UN New York; FAO Rome) |
| India | Member since **1 January 1995** (founder member of both GATT and WTO) |
| Members | **166** (as of April 2026); **164** from 29 July 2016 (Afghanistan 164th); **Nepal 147th** (23 April 2004); **Serbia** = observer, not a member |
| Director-General | **Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala** (Nigeria) – **7th DG and first woman**, appointed Feb 2021, took office 1 March 2021 |
| Earlier DG | **Roberto Azevedo** (Brazil) – **6th DG**, replaced **Pascal Lamy** on 1 September 2013 (second term 2017) |
- **Nature:** an **international organisation** (GATT was only an agreement) with a **broader multilateral framework** – goods, **services, IPR and investment measures**.
- **"Third pillar" of the world economy** with the **IMF and World Bank**; the "**main security guard of international trade**"; promotes **multilateral trade** (not financial support or unilateral trade).
- **Aims (UPSC 1996):** free trade and resource flows, protection of IPR, managed/balanced trade and bringing former East-bloc economies into the system – answer "**1, 2, 3 and 4**".
- **Has a dispute-settlement system** (Dispute Settlement Body) – "it has no way to solve trade disputes" is NOT a valid criticism. Valid criticisms: puppet of MNCs, reduces sovereignty, favours free trade over product quality.
# Principles
| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN)** | An advantage given to one partner must go to **all** WTO partners – **non-discrimination between nations** ("most favour to all countries") |
| **National treatment** | Imported product, once inside, treated **like the domestic product** for internal tax and regulation |
- US unilateral tariffs (2025-26) are criticised as violating MFN and bound-rate obligations.
> Most asked: HQ Geneva (×5), year 1995 (×4), earlier name GATT (×4), DGs (×4), MFN (×3). Trap: "1994" (Marrakesh signing) and "most favour to some countries".

=== GATT, Uruguay Round and the Dunkel Draft
# GATT
- **GATT = General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade**; signed **30 October 1947** at **Geneva** by **23** founding contracting parties (not Marrakesh); in force **1948**; GATT office set up at **Geneva, 1948**.
- **India**: GATT member since **8 July 1948**.
- Focus: cut **tariffs and quantitative restrictions** in goods; **8 rounds** of negotiations; GATT survives as part of the WTO agreements.
| Basis | GATT | WTO |
|---|---|---|
| Set up | 1947 (in force 1948) | 1 January 1995 |
| Nature | An agreement | An international organisation |
| Coverage | Mainly goods | Goods, services, IPR, investment measures |
# Uruguay Round (1986-1993/94)
- **8th GATT round**; launched **September 1986** at **Punta del Este**; concluded **1993** (December), Final Act signed **1994**; **123** contracting parties.
- It "pertains to" **GATT** and **created the WTO** (not UNICEF, IMF, UNESCO).
- Extended rules beyond goods: **GATS** (services), **TRIPS** (IPR), **TRIMS** (investment). Aims: cut farm subsidies, lift investment restrictions, open services (banking, insurance), protect IPR.
# Dunkel Draft
- **Arthur Dunkel**: **Director-General of GATT** (1980-1993), chair of the Trade Negotiations Committee.
- **Dunkel Draft** (Draft Final Act of the Uruguay Round) tabled **20 December 1991**; concerned with **trade**; best known for **intellectual property** rules (RAS 1992).
- **UPSC 1994 statements:** acceptance was **all-or-nothing** (single undertaking – "mandatory to accept all proposals") and agriculture proposal = **cut farm subsidies** → correct; it **phased out** (did not reiterate) the **Multi-Fibre Arrangement**, and Parliament never "accepted" it → answer "**1 and 2**".
- **CDS 2018 chronology:** Uruguay Round concluded 1994 ✔, WTO 1995 ✔, Nasdaq 1971 ✔ – **SWIFT** began in **1973** (live 1977), **not 1985**.
> Repeats: Dunkel = GATT (×3), Uruguay Round → WTO (×3), GATT office Geneva 1948. NextGen p.55 prints "20 October 1991" and "110 countries" for GATT – the Draft Final Act is dated 20 December 1991, and 23 were the founders (110 is later membership).

=== Agreement on Agriculture – Pillars and Subsidy Boxes
# Pillars
- AoA negotiated in the Uruguay Round, in force **1 January 1995**. **Three pillars: market access, domestic support, export competition** – **anti-dumping and countervailing duties are NOT a pillar**.
- "Agreement on Agriculture", "SPS Agreement" and "**Peace Clause**" = **WTO** terms (UPSC 2015), as are **amber, blue and green boxes** (UPSC 2016).
# Subsidy boxes
| Box | Covers | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** | Research, pest/disease control, infrastructure, public stockholding for food security, decoupled income support, environment, regional development | Minimal or no trade distortion; **no limits** |
| **Blue** | "Amber box with conditions" – **direct payments under production-limiting programmes** | Trade-distorting but **exempt from reduction**; no spending limit |
| **Amber** | Price- or quantity-linked support: **MSP**, power, fertiliser, seed, irrigation (measured as **AMS**) | Trade-distorting; **to be reduced** to de minimis |
- **Blue Box = "production-limiting subsidies that distort trade"** (APPSC Group 2).
- **No Red Box** in the AoA (traffic-light "red = forbidden" belongs to the SCM Agreement); **White, Pink, Yellow** are not boxes either.
- **S&D box**: special and differential support for developing countries.
- **India + China proposal (July 2017):** eliminate developed countries' **Amber Box (AMS)** support beyond de minimis first.
# Mechanics
| Term | Rule |
|---|---|
| De minimis | Amber support allowed up to **5%** of production value (developed), **10%** (developing) |
| Total AMS cut | **20% over 6 years** (developed); **13.3% over 10 years** (developing) |
| Blue Box conditions | Payments on fixed areas and yields, or on **85% or less** of base production, or a fixed number of livestock |
| Tariffication | Non-tariff barriers converted into tariffs; cuts **36% over 6 years** (developed), **24% over 10 years** (developing) |
| Export subsidy cut | Developed: **36% by value or 21% by volume** over 6 years; developing 24% / 14% over 10 years |
- **A-R (UPPSC 1998):** "India need not reduce subsidies under WTO" is **false** although India is a developing country (R true) – developing countries also had reduction commitments.
- **Price-gap payment (MSP minus market price) paid directly to farmers:** keyed **Blue Box** by UPPSC 2016 (direct payment); strictly, such deficiency payments are Amber unless tied to production limits.
> Most asked: boxes (×9) – blue box definition, no red box, MSP = amber, Amber in the India-China proposal. Trap: "Red box" and "Blue box is subject to cuts".

=== TRIPS, TRIMS, SPS and India's Compliance Laws
# Agreements
| Agreement | Subject | India |
|---|---|---|
| **TRIPS** (1995) | Minimum IPR standards (patents, GI, designs, copyright) | Patents Act amended 2005 (product patents); **GI Act 1999**; **Designs Act 2000** (in force 11 May 2001); PPV&FR Act 2001 |
| **TRIMS** – Trade-Related **Investment** Measures | Investment measures affecting **trade in goods only** | Phase-out of local-content and trade-balancing rules |
| GATS | Trade in services | Liberalisation commitments |
| **SPS** / TBT | Food safety, plant and animal health / technical standards | Food Safety and Standards Act 2006 |
| TFA (Bali 2013) | Simplify customs | **Ratified April 2016** |
| ITA (1996) | **Zero duty on IT products** | Signatory |
- **TRIMS (UPSC 2020):** **quantitative restrictions on imports by foreign investors are prohibited** ✔; apply to **goods, not services** ✘; **not concerned with regulating foreign investment** itself ✔ → "**1 and 3 only**". TRIPS and TRIMS = **WTO**.
- **SPS:** for food-safety standards in dispute settlement the WTO uses the **Codex Alimentarius Commission** (set up by **FAO and WHO, 1963**).
- **Trade Facilitation Agreement (UPSC 2017):** India ratified it ✔, part of the **Bali package (MC9, 2013)** ✔; it entered into force **22 February 2017**, not January 2016 → "**I and II only**".
# India's IPR laws
- **GI of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999** (in force 15 September 2003) – enacted to comply with **WTO (TRIPS)** obligations, not ILO/IMF/UNCTAD. GI = an IPR; India relies on **TRIPS**, not the Lisbon Agreement.
- GI examples: **Darjeeling Tea – India's first GI (2004-05)**; **Allahabad Surkha guava (2007-08)**; **Lucknow Chikan craft (2008-09)**; **Banaras brocades and sarees (2009-10)**; Nizamabad black pottery (2015-16). So all four in the UPPSC 2011 question are protected.
- **Patents Act 1970** came into force **20 April 1972**, on the **N. Rajagopala Ayyangar Committee (1957)** report; **Patents (Second Amendment) Act, 2002** (Act 38 of 2002, passed 2002); product patents from 2005.
- **Industrial designs:** protection since **11 May 2001** under the **Designs Act, 2000**, enacted for **TRIPS**; it adopted the **Locarno classification** of designs.
# Subsidies (SCM Agreement)
- **Prohibited ("red light")**, **actionable** (countervailing duty), **non-actionable** (R&D, regional development, environmental). Amber Box is AoA, not SCM.
# India's trade policy and the WTO
- **A-R (UPSC 2003):** EXIM Policy 2002-07 was liberal and market-oriented ✔; GATT/WTO aided liberalisation ✔ – both true, **R not the explanation**.
- **A-R (UPSC 1998):** the **USA threatened** WTO sanctions against developing countries for not observing **ILO conventions** ✔; but the USA **has not itself ratified** most of those conventions ✘.
- India prohibits GM food imports without approval; six India-US disputes settled (June 2023), India imports US apples.

=== Ministerial Conferences, Doha Round and Country Groups
# Ministerial Conference
- **Highest decision-making body** (trade ministers); meets **at least once every two years**.
| MC | Venue, year | Key outcome |
|---|---|---|
| MC1 | **Singapore, 1996** | First MC; "**Singapore issues**" (investment, competition policy, transparency in government procurement, trade facilitation); ITA |
| MC2 | Geneva, 1998 | **E-commerce customs-duty moratorium** begun |
| MC3 | **Seattle, 1999** | Collapsed amid protests |
| MC4 | **Doha, 2001** | **Doha Development Agenda** launched; TRIPS-public health declaration |
| MC5 | Cancun, 2003 | Collapsed; G-33 formed |
| MC6 | Hong Kong, 2005 | Farm export subsidies to end; **duty-free quota-free access for LDCs** |
| MC7-8 | Geneva, 2009, 2011 | Stock-taking |
| MC9 | **Bali, 2013** | **Bali Package: Trade Facilitation Agreement + interim Peace Clause** |
| MC10 | **Nairobi, 2015** | **Elimination of agricultural export subsidies**; first MC in Africa |
| MC11 | **Buenos Aires, 2017** | No declaration; fisheries work programme |
| MC12 | **Geneva, June 2022** | **Fisheries Subsidies Agreement** (Phase 1); **COVID-19 TRIPS waiver**; moratorium extended |
| MC13 | **Abu Dhabi**, UAE, 26 Feb-2 Mar 2024 | Moratorium extended to 31 Mar 2026; no PSH solution |
| MC14 | **Yaounde, Cameroon**, 26-29 Mar 2026 | **No declaration** (only a draft "Yaounde package"); e-commerce moratorium lapsed; seen as a failure |
- **MC14:** **India blocked** adding the **Investment Facilitation for Development (IFD)** plurilateral to **Annex 4**, though 129 of 166 members backed it.
# Doha Round and groups
- **Doha Round** (MC4, 2001): "development round"; **central issue = agriculture**; also NAMA, services; **still unfinished**.
| Group | Members / focus |
|---|---|
| **G-33** | 2003, 40+ developing countries incl. India; **food security, Special Safeguard Mechanism, special products** |
| **NAMA-11** | **Developing countries** (incl. India) in Non-Agricultural Market Access talks |
| G-90 | **African Group + ACP countries + LDCs** |
- **Special Safeguard Mechanism (SSM)** = a **WTO** tool letting developing countries **raise tariffs for a short time** against import surges or price falls (UPSC 2010).

=== Public Stockholding and the Peace Clause
- **Peace Clause (Bali MC9, 2013):** no country is legally barred from **food-security public stockholding (PSH)** even if the subsidy **breaches de minimis or AMS limits**, until a permanent solution is found.
- Conditions: **existing programmes and traditional staples only**; **notification** required; no harm to others' food security. It was **not made permanent** at MC13 or MC14.
- The Peace Clause concept sits in the **Agreement on Agriculture** (original "due restraint" clause, Art. 13).
- **India first to invoke it** – for **rice** (2020, for marketing year 2018-19).
- India's complaint: MSP procurement for the **NFSA** is counted as Amber Box on an **outdated historical reference price** (1986-88).
- India with the **African Group** seeks a **permanent solution**; not achieved at **MC13** or **MC14**.

=== Fisheries Subsidies and E-commerce
# Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies (AFS)
- Adopted at **MC12 (Geneva, June 2022)**; **in force September 2025**.
- Bans subsidies to **IUU fishing**, fishing on the **unregulated high seas**, and **overfished stocks** (unless rebuilding).
- Covers **marine wild capture**; **aquaculture and inland fishing are exempt**. A **Fish Fund** helps poorer members.
- **First WTO agreement with an environmental-sustainability objective**; TFA and AFS = the **only two new multilateral agreements in 30 years**.
- **India = 123rd** member to deposit its Instrument of Acceptance (**July 2026**).
- **Phase 2** (overcapacity, overfishing): **deferred at MC14 to the General Council, Geneva**.
- **AP angle:** Indian fisher bodies, incl. **AP's marine fisher folk**, want a "**fisher-centric**" approach.
# E-commerce
- **Moratorium** on customs duties on electronic transmissions (since **MC2, 1998**) **lapsed on 31 March 2026**.
- **E-Commerce Agreement** (plurilateral): **66 members** signed separately. India's concern: plurilaterals (IFD, E-commerce) may **fragment the WTO** by moving away from consensus.

=== Dispute Settlement and the Appellate Body Crisis
- **Appellate Body** non-functional since **December 2019** – the **USA blocked appointments**.
- **MPIA** (Multi-Party Interim Appeal Arbitration Arrangement, **EU-led, 2020**) = stop-gap; **India has not joined**.
- **BRICS New Delhi Declaration (September 2026):** IMF/World Bank quota and voting reform + **restoration of the WTO Appellate Body** (two-tier dispute settlement).
| Dispute | Subject | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **DS456** (US) | **Solar cells** – domestic content, National Solar Mission | India lost, 2016 |
| **DS541** (US) | Export subsidies: **MEIS, SEZ, EOU** | Against India 2019; appealed into the void |
| Sugar (**Brazil, Australia, Guatemala**) | **Sugarcane prices, export subsidies** | Against India 2021; appealed |
| ICT tariffs (**EU, Japan, Taiwan**) | Duties on phones and ICT goods | Against India 2023 |
