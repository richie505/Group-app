=== NOTES 3:74 | Foreign Trade Policy - Objectives, Incentives, Eous; Trade Facilitation and Logistics Costs
## SUB 0 | Trade Promotion and Export Credit Bodies
[74:0:0] TABLE head: Body | Year / role
  [74:0:0.0] ECGC (Export Credit Guarantee Corporation, now ECGC Ltd) | 1957, wholly government-owned; export credit-risk insurance – the body that insures exporters
  [74:0:0.1] EXIM Bank | Act 1981, set up 1982, Mumbai; finance for foreign trade – lines of credit, buyer's/seller's credit, project exports; not an insurer
  [74:0:0.2] MMTC | 1963; minerals, metals, precious metals; largest international trading organisation / trading government company; was the largest non-oil importer
  [74:0:0.3] STC (State Trading Corporation) | 1956; canalising agency for bulk goods; foreign trade, not insurance
  [74:0:0.4] IBEF (India Brand Equity Fund/Foundation) | 1996, trust of the Department of Commerce; promotes the "Made in India" brand abroad
  [74:0:0.5] PEC (Project and Equipment Corporation) | Under the Ministry of Commerce, not Industry
  [74:0:0.6] DGFT | Attached office of the Department of Commerce, Vanijya Bhavan, New Delhi; issues FTP authorisations
[74:0:1] - **Insurance of export risk = ECGC only** (not EXIM Bank, GIC, STC or RBI); "export financing and insurance" = ECGC.
[74:0:2] - **Pre-shipment inspection / quality control** = **Export Inspection Council**, NOT an ECGC objective (UPSC 2002: only statement 1 – MMTC – correct).
[74:0:3] - **NIRVIK** (Niryat Rin Vikas Yojana, **Budget 2020-21**): ECGC scheme – higher insurance cover, lower premium for **exporters**.
[74:0:4] - Channelising (canalising) exports and imports – UPPSC 1999 key: **MMTC, EXIM Bank, STC and FCI (all four)**. Promote foreign trade: **ECGC, MMTC, STC (all)**.
[74:0:5] - **Neelachal Ispat Nigam Ltd (NINL)** was set up by **MMTC jointly with the Government of Odisha** (Odisha PSUs); sold to Tata Steel in 2022.
[74:0:6] - Agri goods exporters: **NAFED, STC, MMTC**; **IFFCO** (fertilisers) is NOT one.
[74:0:7] - Status holders: earlier **Export House → Trading House → Star Trading House → Super Star Trading House** (high, sustained export performance = Star Trading Houses); now **One-Star to Five-Star Export Houses**.

## SUB 1 | Export Incentive Schemes and Towns of Export Excellence
[74:1:0] TABLE head: Scheme | Fact
  [74:1:0.0] EPCG | Export Promotion Capital Goods: zero-duty import of capital goods against an export obligation
  [74:1:0.1] Duty drawback | Refund of import (customs) and excise duties on inputs used in exports – not income tax, not export duty
  [74:1:0.2] Advance Authorisation | Duty-free import of inputs for export production
  [74:1:0.3] MEIS (FTP 2015-20) | Goods exports reward; replaced five schemes – Focus Product, Focus Market, Market Linked Focus Product, VKGUY, Agri Infrastructure Incentive Scrip
  [74:1:0.4] SEIS | Services exports reward (FTP 2015-20)
  [74:1:0.5] RoDTEP | Replaced the WTO-challenged MEIS; refunds unrebated central, state and local taxes; run online by CBIC
  [74:1:0.6] RoSCTL | Replaced RoSL; apparel and made-ups
  [74:1:0.7] Vishesh Krishi (and Gram) Upaj Yojana (2004) | Promoted exports of fruits, vegetables, flowers, minor forest produce and their value-added products
  [74:1:0.8] Gold Card Scheme (RBI, EXIM Policy 2003-04) | Quicker, cheaper credit for creditworthy exporters
  [74:1:0.9] Niryat Bandhu | DGFT training/outreach – mentoring new and first-generation entrepreneurs in export-import
[74:1:1] - **Towns of Export Excellence (TEE):** **Tirupur** (hosiery), **Ludhiana** (woollen knitwear), **Panipat** (woollen blankets) – 2003; **Madurai** (handlooms) is also a TEE – so "Madurai was not a TEE" is wrong; Visakhapatnam and Bhimavaram (seafood) are AP's TEEs.
[74:1:2] - **Export Promotion Mission:** ₹25,060 crore, FY 2025-26 to 2030-31, run by **DGFT**; merges **Interest Equalisation + Market Access Initiative**; for **MSMEs and first-time exporters**.
[74:1:3] - **RELIEF Scheme 2026:** ₹497 crore via **ECGC** for **war-risk and logistics costs in West Asia**.

[74:2:0] TABLE head: Policy | Fact
  [74:2:0.0] First EXIM Policy | 1985-88

## SUB 3 | Export-Import Procedure, Documents and Rules of Origin
[74:3:0] TABLE head: Document | Purpose
  [74:3:0.0] Indent | Importer's order to buy goods – the import process begins with the indent
  [74:3:0.1] Letter of credit | Produced by the importer (through its issuing bank), promising payment to the exporter (beneficiary)
  [74:3:0.2] Shipping bill | Customs document for clearing export goods
  [74:3:0.3] Mate's receipt | Ship's mate acknowledges goods loaded on board
  [74:3:0.4] Bill of lading | Shipping company's receipt and document of title
[74:3:1] - **Rules of origin:** fix a product's **national source**; tell customs which **duties, regulations and fees** apply; used for **trade statistics**; matter because duties and restrictions depend on the **source of imports** – **all four statements correct**.
[74:3:2] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Economy p.80; app notes 3:74 sub 8
[74:3:3] - **2017: 10% import duty on wheat and tur dal** – to **check falling domestic prices** after bumper harvests and cheap imports.

## SUB 5 | Trade Policy Instruments and Economic Integration
[74:5:0] - **Import substitution industrialisation (ISI):** inward-looking; produce substitutes at home to **reduce import dependence** (India's policy for about four decades) – not open trade, FDI or liberalisation.
[74:5:1] - **Import quota** = quantitative restriction; tariffs, countervailing and anti-dumping duties = price-based.
[74:5:2] TABLE head: Stage | Feature
  [74:5:2.0] FTA | Free internal trade; members keep own external tariffs
  [74:5:2.1] Customs union | Free internal trade + common external tariff
  [74:5:2.2] Common market | Customs union + free movement of factors
  [74:5:2.3] Economic union | Common market + coordinated economic policies
[74:5:3] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Economy p.80; app notes 3:74 sub 0
## SUB 6 | EOUs, EHTPs, STPs and SEZs
[74:6:0] TABLE head: Unit | Covers / nature
  [74:6:0.0] SEZ | Duty-free enclave treated as foreign territory; policy 1 April 2000, Act 2005, Act + Rules in force 10 Feb 2006; concept from EXIM Policy 1997-2002; 100% FDI automatic for most manufacturing
  [74:6:0.1] EOU | Goods generally
  [74:6:0.2] EHTP | Electronics hardware
  [74:6:0.3] STP | Software
[74:6:1] - EOU/EHTP/STP: **export all output**, can be **located anywhere** (need not be in an SEZ), duty-free inputs.
[74:6:2] - SEZ aims: exports, investment, employment, infrastructure; internationally competitive, hassle-free, **simplified business-friendly** environment.
[74:6:3] - First EPZ: **Kandla, 1965** (Asia's first).
[74:6:4] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Economy p.26, p.79; app notes 3:74 sub 6

=== NOTES 3:75 | Balance of Payments - Structure, Current and Capital Account, Convertibility, Forex Reserves
## SUB 0 | Meaning of BoP – BoP vs Balance of Trade
[75:0:0] # Definition
[75:0:1] - **Balance of Payments (BoP):** a **systematic and complete record of all economic transactions between the residents of a country and the rest of the world** during a period, **normally one year**.
[75:0:2] - BoP has **two main accounts – the current account and the capital account** (NextGen); it is not "only capital" or "only current" or "only exports and imports".
[75:0:3] - **IMF BPM6** uses **three accounts – current, capital and financial**; the **RBI generally combines capital and financial** under "Capital Account".
[75:0:4] - BoP includes **visible trade, invisible trade and borrowings** – "all of the above"; the term is used in relation to **exports and imports** (foreign trade), not factory sales or tax collection.
[75:0:5] - UPSC 2013 wording: BoP = systematic record of "**all import and export transactions** of a country during a given period, normally a year" – the official key, the closest fit (goods exports only, government-to-government deals or capital movements alone are parts).
[75:0:6] # Balance of Trade vs Balance of Payments
[75:0:7] TABLE head: Basis | Balance of Trade (BoT) | Balance of Payments (BoP)
  [75:0:7.0] Coverage | Goods only (visible) | All transactions: goods, services, transfers, income, capital
  [75:0:7.1] Formula | Goods exports − goods imports | Whole external account, double-entry
  [75:0:7.2] Place | Part of the current account | Current + capital/financial account
  [75:0:7.3] Picture | Not the complete record | Better, complete picture
[75:0:8] - **A-R (UPSC 2006):** BoP gives a better picture than BoT **because** it covers both **visible and invisible** items – A and R both true, R explains A.
[75:0:9] - **BoT surplus**: goods exports > goods imports; **BoT deficit**: goods imports > goods exports. "Exports minus imports" is the BoT, **not** the BoP.
[75:0:10] - **Favourable BoP** – achieved by **stepping up exports and reducing imports** (both).
[75:0:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.15-17 (BoP definition, two accounts, BoT); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.49 (UPSC 2013 wording); Rocket Indian Economy p.36 (BoT vs BoP table); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.21 (IMF three parts); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.19 (BPM6)
[75:0:12] - Among CRR, SLR, demonetisation and forex reserves, only **foreign currency reserves** relate to the BoP – its overall balance shows up as a change in reserves.

[75:2:0] - **CAD**: payments exceed receipts from **goods, services, transfers and net income** (excess of total imports over total exports). A CAD is **not** caused by exporting more.
[75:2:1] - India runs a **persistent trade deficit** in goods; services and remittances only partly offset it.

  [75:2:2.2] FY13 | Peak 4.8% of GDP (taper tantrum) – higher than 1990-91
  [75:2:2.3] 2013-14 | Trade deficit, net invisibles positive, capital account in surplus (financed the CAD)
  [75:2:2.4] Q4 2023-24 | Surplus $5.7 bn (0.6%) – net services receipts $42.7 bn (software, travel, business services)

## SUB 3 | Structure – Current Account vs Capital Account
[75:3:0] TABLE head: Current account | Capital (and financial) account
  [75:3:0.0] Merchandise exports and imports (visible trade; trade balance) | Foreign loans, FDI, portfolio investment (FPI/FII), ADRs/GDRs
  [75:3:0.1] Invisibles – services: software, travel, transport, banking, insurance | ECB, external assistance
  [75:3:0.2] Invisibles – primary income: interest, dividends, profits, wages | NRI deposits (FCNR(B)), banking capital
  [75:3:0.3] Secondary income: private remittances, gifts, grants | Short-term trade credit; change in forex reserves
[75:3:1] - **Current account** = goods + services + income + transfers; it **includes the Balance of Trade**. **Capital receipts and payments are NOT** part of it (CDS 2014).
[75:3:2] - **Invisibles** = services + income + transfers; **invisible export = export of services**. Services = **non-factor** (shipping, banking, tourism, software) + factor income.
[75:3:3]   - "Invisible items" in the classic sense = **service transactions**; goods are the visible items.
[75:3:4] - **Capital account** (UPSC 2013): **foreign loans, FDI and portfolio investment** – **private remittances are current account** (so codes with remittances are wrong). **FDI is NOT current account.**
[75:3:5] - **Current-account balance** = exports − imports of goods and services + net primary income + net secondary income.
[75:3:6] # Credit and debit
[75:3:7] - Every **inflow of foreign exchange = credit side**; every outflow = debit (double-entry book-keeping).
[75:3:8] - **Gift to foreigners = debit** in the current account; gift from foreigners = credit; exports of goods/services = credit.
[75:3:9] - Residents **buying foreign assets = capital-account debit**; **sale of Indian assets to foreigners = credit**.
[75:3:10] TABLE head: Item | BoP placement
  [75:3:10.0] Software services exports | Current account (services)
  [75:3:10.1] Foreign loans, FDI, FPI | Capital account
  [75:3:10.2] Private remittances | Current account – secondary income (transfers)
  [75:3:10.3] Crude oil imports | Merchandise trade
[75:3:11] # The identity
[75:3:12] - **Current + Capital + Financial account + Net Errors & Omissions = 0** – BoP **always balances** in accounting terms; a surplus in one account = deficit in another. **BoT need not balance.**
[75:3:13] - **Autonomous** transactions ("above the line") happen for their own sake; **accommodating** ones ("below the line") fill the gap – **official reserve changes, official borrowing**.
[75:3:14] - Overall BoP **positive → adds to reserves; negative → draws them down**; monetary authority = ultimate financier.

## SUB 5 | Correcting BoP Disequilibrium
[75:5:0] TABLE head: Expenditure-switching | Expenditure-reducing / financing

## SUB 0 | Effects of Devaluation
[76:0:0] - Devaluation makes **exports cheaper abroad** (their foreign-currency price falls) and **imports dearer at home** → it **promotes exports**; it is an **expenditure-switching** policy (spending switches to home-produced goods) used to **correct the balance of trade / BoP**.
[76:0:1] - **A-R pattern:** "Devaluation may promote exports" (A) is explained by "the price of the country's products in the international market falls" (R) – **A true, R true, R explains A**. A reason saying devaluation **raises** export prices is **false**.
[76:0:2] - Devaluation does **not necessarily** improve the trade balance or raise the currency's foreign value: it **necessarily** only improves export competitiveness (UPSC 2021: "1 only"). Limits: **imported inflation** (it **raises domestic prices**), inelastic oil imports, costlier ECB repayment.
[76:0:3] - It **raises the cost of imports** and does **not curb inflation** – so "to fix the balance of trade" is the purpose, not "reduce import costs" or "curb inflation".
[76:0:4] - **When does it work?** When exports actually **become cheaper to importers** abroad, i.e. export prices fall in proportion to the devaluation and domestic prices do not rise to cancel it (CDS 2017; APPSC 2011 key: "exports change proportionately").
[76:0:5] - **1991:** the rupee was devalued to **make exports cheaper**; export growth rose after 1991 and the devaluation is credited for it (UPSC 2000 key: A and R true, R explains A).
[76:0:6] - **Trade imbalance and controls:** import duties, exchange controls and devaluation are used to **wipe out** the trade imbalance and keep the rate stable (APPSC 2011 key).
[76:0:7] - BoP correction methods (NextGen): monetary policy, **exchange depreciation, devaluation, exchange control**, import duties and quotas, export promotion, foreign loans and investment.

[76:1:0] - **Exchange rate:** the price / value of one currency in terms of another (₹50 for $1 = ₹50 per dollar). **Forex market** participants: commercial banks, brokers, authorised dealers, monetary authorities.
[76:1:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [76:1:1.0] Direct quote | Units of domestic currency per one unit of foreign currency (₹95 = $1)
  [76:1:1.1] Indirect quote | Units of foreign currency per one unit of domestic currency
  [76:1:1.2] Spot rate | Immediate delivery – settled within two days
  [76:1:1.3] Forward rate | Agreed now for future delivery; an importer's forward contract covers exchange-rate risk
  [76:1:1.4] Hedging / speculation | Covering exchange risk (forwards) / taking an open position on expected moves
  [76:1:1.5] Arbitrage | Buying a currency where it is cheaper and simultaneously selling it where it is dearer
  [76:1:1.6] Hot currency (money) | Foreign currency that migrates quickly between markets for the highest short-term return
  [76:1:1.7] Tobin tax | Tax on foreign exchange transactions (James Tobin)
  [76:1:1.8] Currency war | Countries competitively devaluing their currencies to gain export advantage
  [76:1:1.9] Hawala | Illegal transactions of foreign exchange (informal transfer outside banking channels)
[76:1:2] - **Money laundering** turns the **proceeds of crime** (drugs, smuggling, corruption) into apparently legal money – hence "drugs and crime"; hawala is a common channel.
[76:1:3] - **Import cover:** the **number of months of imports that could be paid for by the country's reserves** – about **11 months** with $701.4 bn (16 Jan 2026, Economic Survey 2025-26).
[76:1:4] - **Most valuable currency (in ₹): Kuwaiti dinar**; of pound, euro and US dollar the costliest is the **pound sterling** (₹125.18 vs euro ₹109.05, $ ₹93.00 on 17 Apr 2026).
[76:1:5] - **International liquidity problem** = shortage of **dollars and other hard currencies** (internationally accepted reserve assets).
[76:1:6] - **RBI** (through authorised dealers, under FEMA) sanctions foreign exchange for imports.

## SUB 3 | Devaluation, Depreciation, Appreciation and Revaluation
[76:3:0] - **Devaluation:** an **official reduction in the value of a currency vis-a-vis major internationally traded currencies**, made by the government / monetary authority under a **fixed** exchange-rate system (more rupees per dollar) – not a general "decline in values" or a rise in inflation.
[76:3:1] TABLE head: Term | System | Meaning | Done by
  [76:3:1.0] Devaluation | Fixed | Official reduction of the currency's value | Government / monetary authority
  [76:3:1.1] Revaluation | Fixed | Official rise in the currency's value | Government / monetary authority
  [76:3:1.2] Appreciation | Flexible | Market-driven rise in value – fewer rupees per $ | Demand and supply
  [76:3:1.3] Depreciation | Flexible | Market-driven fall in value – more rupees per $ | Demand and supply
[76:3:2] # Reading a rate change
[76:3:3] - **$1 = ₹60 → ₹58:** the **rupee appreciated** and the **dollar depreciated**.
[76:3:4] - **$1 = ₹60 → ₹65 (or ₹70) by market forces:** the **rupee depreciated** (not "devalued" – that word is for an official change). ₹96 → ₹94 per $ = rupee appreciated.
[76:3:5] - **"Rate of exchange falls"** (foreign currency per rupee falls = rupee cheaper), ceteris paribus: **exports rise and imports fall**.
[76:3:6] > Exam angle: official change → devaluation / revaluation; market change → depreciation / appreciation. Wrong-pair MCQs swap these.
[76:3:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.19-20; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy p.69-70

## SUB 5 | Exchange Rate Regimes and Determinants
[76:5:0] TABLE head: Regime | Meaning
  [76:5:0.0] Fixed / pegged | Government fixes the rate (to gold, a currency or a basket); the central bank buys / sells forex to hold it, so private demand and supply need not come into balance
  [76:5:0.1] Exchange control | Rate fixed and held by rationing out the available foreign exchange
  [76:5:0.2] Flexible / floating | Set by demand and supply of foreign currency in the market – not by the central bank
  [76:5:0.3] Managed float (dirty float) | Market-set, with central-bank intervention to curb volatility – India's market-determined system
[76:5:1] - **Post-Bretton Woods classes:** **fixed** – currency unions, dollarised regimes, **currency boards (Argentina 1991 – Jan 2002)**, pegs; **intermediate** – bands, crawling pegs; **flexible** – managed and independent floats.
[76:5:2] - **Floating rates:** traders face the risk of an **exchange-rate loss**; price differences between markets give scope for **arbitrage** (APPSC 2019 key).
[76:5:3] - **Determinants:** demand for the country's **goods and services**, **stability of its government**, economic potential – the **World Bank does not decide a currency's price** (UPSC 2012 key: II and III).
[76:5:4] - **Raghuram Rajan:** **high inflation, high fiscal deficit and high crude oil prices** all weaken the rupee's exchange value.
[76:5:5] - **PPP theory:** equality of purchasing power (the **inflation differential**) decides the long-run **rate of exchange**. Long-run anchors (ES 2025-26): PPP, savings-investment balance, current account.
[76:5:6] - **Capital flows:** large inflows → **appreciation** (Dutch disease); higher imports can coexist with a stable rupee if backed by **net investment inflows**; a repo hike attracts **carry-trade** capital; deliberate undervaluation (alleged of China) = **currency manipulation**.
[76:5:7] - **Impossible trinity (Mundell-Fleming trilemma):** no country can have a **fixed exchange rate, free capital flows and an independent monetary policy** at once.

## SUB 6 | FERA to FEMA and Rupee Convertibility
[76:6:0] - **FERA 1973** (restrictive, to prevent outflow of currency) → replaced by **FEMA 1999**: proposed in the **1997-98 Budget**; **in force 1 June 2000**, with a **two-year sunset clause** for FERA cases (ended 31 May 2002 – "finally implemented 2002" in the UPPSC key). **FEMA regulates the flow of foreign exchange**; no "Foreign Exchange Control Act" ever replaced FERA.
[76:6:1] - **Convertibility:** **freely permitting the conversion of the rupee to other currencies and vice versa** – not conversion into gold, nor fixing by market forces.
[76:6:2] - **Full convertibility** may mean the rupee's **free float**, its **direct exchange anywhere** inside and outside India, and its acting **like any other international currency** (UPSC 2002: 1, 2 and 3).
[76:6:3] - India: **full current account** convertibility (Aug 1994), only **partial capital account** convertibility.

  [76:6:4.0] Tarapore I | 1997 – roadmap to capital account convertibility
  [76:6:4.1] Tarapore II | Appointed 2006 (report 31 July 2006) – fuller capital account convertibility

## SUB 7 | SDR, Reserve Tranche and Paper Gold
[76:7:0] - **SDR (Special Drawing Rights)** – relates to the **IMF**; an **artificial currency / international reserve asset** created in **1969** (agreed 1967) to **supplement members' official reserves**; called **"paper gold"**; exists only as **book-keeping entries**. ADR / GDR are depository receipts, not currency.
[76:7:1] - **Basket (since 1 Oct 2016):** US dollar, euro, **renminbi** (the currency proposed / added in 2015-16), yen, pound sterling – **no rupee**. Largest allocation **about $650 bn, August 2021**.
[76:7:2] - **Reserve (gold) tranche:** the part of a member's quota paid in reserve assets (historically gold); a **credit facility of the IMF to its members** drawable **without conditions**; the **smallest** component of India's reserves (FCA > gold > SDR > RTP).
[76:7:3] - **Reserves:** **RBI is the custodian**; reserves rose to **$785.71 bn on 15 Sep 2026 – India 4th**, overtaking Russia; rising **gold share** = diversification; **India-Japan bilateral swap** renewed from 28 Feb 2026 (two-way, local currencies against US dollars).

## SUB 8 | Bretton Woods, IMF and India's Exchange-Rate History
[76:8:0] - **Gold standard (1870-1914):** the epitome of the fixed-rate system.
[76:8:1] - **Bretton Woods Conference** = **United Nations Monetary and Financial Conference**, **1-22 July 1944**, **New Hampshire (USA)**, **44 nations**: set up the **IMF and IBRD** (not the UN or WTO) and fixed rates centred on the **dollar** (two-tier convertibility – the origin of **currency convertibility**).
[76:8:2] - **IMF:** formally created **27 December 1945**; **India a founding member (27 Dec 1945)**; **191 members** (Liechtenstein 191st, Oct 2024); India's quota **2.75%** of SDRs, votes 2.63% (March 2026). Its purposes are monetary cooperation, trade growth, exchange **stability** and multilateral payments – **not** promoting flexible exchange rates.
[76:8:3] TABLE head: Year | India's exchange-rate step
  [76:8:3.0] 1947-1971 | Par value system under the IMF (rupee linked to sterling)
  [76:8:3.1] 1971 | Smithsonian Agreement – ±2.25% band (NextGen: 2.5%), lasted 14 months
  [76:8:3.2] Sept 1975 | Rupee delinked from pound sterling, pegged to a basket of currencies – the end of the strict fixed system (Ramesh Singh: "between fixed and floating")
  [76:8:3.3] 1992-93 | LERMS (Budget 1992-93): dual rate – 40% official, 60% market; partial convertibility
  [76:8:3.4] 1 March 1993 | Rates unified; market-determined
  [76:8:3.5] August 1994 | IMF Article VIII – current account convertibility
[76:8:4] - **Official exchange rate of the rupee:** associated with a **basket of selected currencies** (NextGen; the 1975 basket peg); since 1993 it is market-determined, with the US dollar as the RBI's reference and intervention currency.
[76:8:5] - **India's current policy:** **market-determined exchange rate** (managed float).

## SUB 9 | Rupee Movement and RBI Tools
[76:9:0] - **RBI aim:** curb **excessive volatility**, not defend a fixed level. If the rupee falls fast, the RBI **sells dollars**; if US / EU rates fall and inflows surge, it **buys dollars**; to fight high inflation it **sells** (not buys) government securities (UPSC 2022: B and C).
[76:9:1] TABLE head: Tool | Example
  [76:9:1.0] Spot intervention | Selling dollars from reserves
  [76:9:1.1] Forex swap | Buy/sell swap: banks sell dollars to the RBI and buy them back later at a premium
  [76:9:1.2] NDF curbs | Restricting domestic banks in offshore Non-Deliverable Forward markets; Net Open Position limits
  [76:9:1.3] FCNR(B) support | RBI bore the full hedging cost on fresh FCNR(B) deposits till 30 Sep 2026
[76:9:2] - **NOT RBI tools:** **fiscal policy adjustments** (APPSC 2025); **import curbs and export promotion** are Government structural measures.
[76:9:3] - **To stop a slide:** curb non-essential imports, promote exports, **masala bonds**, ease **ECB** norms – but **not an expansionary monetary policy** (UPSC 2019).
[76:9:4] - **Recent:** FY 2025-26 (to Jan 2026) rupee **depreciated about 5.4%**; crossed **₹96.2/$ on 19 May 2026** (Brent near $110, FPI outflows over ₹2 lakh crore). In 2002-07 the rupee **gained against the US dollar** (₹48.4 → ₹44.3).

## SUB 10 | NEER and REER
[76:10:0] - **NEER:** weighted average of the rupee's bilateral **nominal** rates against a **trade-weighted basket**; RBI indices of **40 and 6 currencies**, **base 2015-16 = 100**. **A rise in NEER = rupee appreciation.**
[76:10:1] - **REER** = NEER **adjusted for relative inflation** differentials – the better competitiveness measure. A **rise in REER = loss of competitiveness** (overvalued rupee), not an improvement.
[76:10:2] - Higher domestic inflation than abroad **widens the divergence between NEER and REER** (UPSC 2022: statements A and C correct).

[77:2:2] - **India**: GATT member since **8 July 1948**.
[77:2:3] - Focus: cut **tariffs and quantitative restrictions** in goods; **8 rounds** of negotiations; GATT survives as part of the WTO agreements.

[77:2:5] # Uruguay Round (1986-1993/94)

## SUB 5 | Public Stockholding and the Peace Clause
[77:5:0] - **Peace Clause (Bali MC9, 2013):** no country is legally barred from **food-security public stockholding (PSH)** even if the subsidy **breaches de minimis or AMS limits**, until a permanent solution is found.

## SUB 7 | Fisheries Subsidies and E-commerce
[77:7:0] # Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies (AFS)
[77:7:1] - Adopted at **MC12 (Geneva, June 2022)**; **in force September 2025**.
[77:7:2] - Bans subsidies to **IUU fishing**, fishing on the **unregulated high seas**, and **overfished stocks** (unless rebuilding).
[77:7:3] - Covers **marine wild capture**; **aquaculture and inland fishing are exempt**. A **Fish Fund** helps poorer members.
[77:7:4] - **First WTO agreement with an environmental-sustainability objective**; TFA and AFS = the **only two new multilateral agreements in 30 years**.
[77:7:5] - **India = 123rd** member to deposit its Instrument of Acceptance (**July 2026**).
[77:7:6] - **Phase 2** (overcapacity, overfishing): **deferred at MC14 to the General Council, Geneva**.
[77:7:7] - **AP angle:** Indian fisher bodies, incl. **AP's marine fisher folk**, want a "**fisher-centric**" approach.
[77:7:8] # E-commerce
[77:7:9] - **Moratorium** on customs duties on electronic transmissions (since **MC2, 1998**) **lapsed on 31 March 2026**.
[77:7:10] - **E-Commerce Agreement** (plurilateral): **66 members** signed separately. India's concern: plurilaterals (IFD, E-commerce) may **fragment the WTO** by moving away from consensus.

## SUB 1 | FDI, FPI and FII – Meaning and What Counts
[78:1:0] # FDI
[78:1:1] - **FDI**: investment for **lasting interest and management control**; a **largely non-debt-creating** capital flow (UPSC 2020) – not debt-servicing, not FII buying G-secs; longer-term and **stable**.
[78:1:2] - Legal line: capital instruments bought by a person resident outside India in an **unlisted** Indian company (any stake), or **10% or more** of post-issue paid-up equity of a **listed** company; 10% threshold from the **Arvind Mayaram Committee**.
[78:1:3] - Instruments: equity shares, debentures, preference shares, share warrants.
[78:1:4] - **Counts as FDI (UPSC 2012):** **subsidiaries of foreign companies**, **majority foreign equity** in Indian companies, **companies exclusively financed by foreign companies** – **portfolio investment is NOT** → "**1, 2 and 3 only**".
[78:1:5] - **Can be included in FDI (UPSC 2021):** **FCCBs**, **FII investment with certain conditions**, **GDRs** – **NRE deposits are not** → "**1, 2 and 3**".
[78:1:6] - **Not FDI:** buying **bonds or stocks** of a company overseas (portfolio). FDI = acquiring a mill abroad, a wholly owned firm abroad, building a new plant abroad.
[78:1:7] TABLE head: Type | Meaning | Example
  [78:1:7.0] Greenfield | New facility | Hyundai, Sriperumbudur
  [78:1:7.1] Brownfield | Acquisition | Vodafone-Hutchison Essar
[78:1:8] - Brings capital, **technology**, management skills, competition, jobs; finances the CAD with stable capital. **Gross FDI is not all fresh capital** (includes reinvested earnings, accounting changes).
[78:1:9] - **MNCs** locate near markets (A) because they seek **more profit** (R) – both true, R explains A.
[78:1:10] # FDI vs FPI / FII
[78:1:11] TABLE head: Basis | FDI | FPI / FII
  [78:1:11.0] Threshold | ≥10% listed, or any unlisted stake | <10% of a listed company
  [78:1:11.1] Nature | Long-term, stable, control | Liquid, short-term "hot money", more volatile
  [78:1:11.2] Brings | Capital + technology + management | Only financial capital – raises capital availability in general
  [78:1:11.3] Target | Specific sectors / enterprises | Securities (secondary market)
  [78:1:11.4] Regulator | DPIIT + RBI | SEBI (registration) + RBI
[78:1:12] - **Key difference (UPSC 2011):** "**FII helps in increasing capital availability in general, while FDI only targets specific sectors**".
[78:1:13] - **FII** = portfolio investment by foreign institutional investors. **SEBI FPI Regulations, 2019** unified **FIIs, sub-accounts and QFIs** as FPIs. FPI opened to India in **1992**.
[78:1:14] - **Most volatile flows:** **FPI and NRI deposits** (interest-rate driven) – "Both". Excess portfolio flows drove the **East Asian and Latin American crises** → **FDI** is the good inflow for a host country (UPSC 2002).
[78:1:15] - **SEBI relaxing norms for sovereign wealth funds** (CDS 2011) – to **attract more foreign investment**.
