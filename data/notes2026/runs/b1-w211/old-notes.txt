## SUB 3 | Gurajada Apparao
[211:3:0] - **Titles:** **"Abhyudaya Kavitha Pitamahudu"** (father of advanced / progressive poetry), **Kavisekhara**; NextGen: Navayuga Vaitalikudu, Bhava Kavita Vaitalikudu.
[211:3:1] - **Works:** ***Kanyasulkam*** (play on bride-price; written 1892, first staged at **Vizianagaram**), ***Mutyala Saralu*** (1910 poems in his own colloquial metre), *Puttadibomma Purnamma*, *Diddubatu*, *Desabhakti Geyam*; English poem ***Sarangadhara***.
[211:3:2] - Champion of **vyavaharika bhasha** (spoken Telugu) as a literary medium; *Mutyala Saralu* opened modern Telugu poetry.
[211:3:3] - Born at Rayavaram (Visakhapatnam); in the court of Ananda Gajapati of Vizianagaram.
[211:3:4] > "Desamante matti kadoi" is from *Mutyala Saralu* – not *Kanyasulkam*, *Manishi* or *Puttadi Bomma Purnamma*.
[211:3:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Modern India L2 Part 3 p.9; NextGen AP History Part 7 p.26; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurajada_Apparao; app notes 1:211 (sub 2)

## SUB 6 | Rayaprolu Subbarao and Bhava Kavitvam
[211:6:0] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [211:6:0.0] Titles | "Abhinava Nannaya" (Encyclopedia of Indian Literature); Father of Telugu Romanticism
  [211:6:0.1] Contribution | Brought Western (English lyric – Shelley and Keats) romanticism – Bhava Kavitvam – into Telugu; left translations of Sanskrit epics for original, emotive verse
  [211:6:0.2] Works | *Trunakankanamu* (first independent poem), *Andhravali*, *Swatantrya Rathamu*, *Janmabhoomi*, *Misra Manjari* (Sahitya Akademi 1965)

[211:6:2] - Progressive writers such as **Sri Sri** called it **"escapist"**.
[211:6:3] > Abhinava Nannaya = **Rayaprolu** (not Viswanatha, Boyi Bhimanna or Sri Sri); Kavi Samrat = Viswanatha.

=== NOTES 1:212 | Revolutionary Literature in Telugu – Sri Sri, Abhyudaya Movement
## SUB 0 | Gorky's Mother and Leftist Literature in Andhra
[212:0:0] - **Maxim Gorky's *Mother*** was translated into Telugu as ***Amma*** by **Krovvidi (K.) Lingaraju** in **1934** – the work most responsible for **sowing socialism among Andhra youth** (with Sri Sri's *Mahaprasthanam*); not M.S. Ram, Gopichand or C. Rajeswara Rao.
[212:0:1] - Background: failure of the CDM and Nehru's socialist writings turned Andhra youth to the left (Communist Party, Congress Socialist Party, M.N. Roy's Radical Humanists).
[212:0:2] - **Andhra Socialist Party:** June 1934, Vijayawada – N.G. Ranga president, Madduri Annapurnayya secretary; members included K. Lingaraju, P. Sundarayya, C. Rajeswara Rao.
[212:0:3] - Leftist journals: *Udayini* (Kompella Janardhana Rao), *Pratibha* (Siva Sankara Sastri); Rayalaseema – *Navya Sahityamala*.
[212:0:4] - Publishers: **Adarsha Grantha Mandali – Gadde Lingayya**; **Viswa Sahityamala – Mahidhara brothers**.
[212:0:5] - **Tenali** radicals (M.N. Roy): **T. Gopichand** – ***Asamarthuni Jeevitha Yatra*** (1947), first psychological novel in Telugu (not N.G. Ranga, Sri Sri or K. Lingaraju); *Pandita Parameswara Sastri Veelunama* – Sahitya Akademi; first Director of Information, Andhra State (1953).
[212:0:6] > Amma (1934) = Krovvidi Lingaraju; *Parivarthana*, *Mundadugu*, *Maa Bhoomi* are distractors.

  [212:1:0.2] Preface | Gudipati Venkata Chalam – "Krishna Sastri's pain is the pain of the world; the pain of the world is Sri Sri's pain"

  [212:1:0.4] Other works | *Khadga Srushti*, *Maro Prasthanam* (Viplava phase), *Siprali* (Sirisirimuvva satakam and limericks), autobiography *Anantham*; *Gajinche Russia*
  [212:1:0.5] Organisations | Sahiti Samiti (Vizag, with Puripanda Appalaswamy); founding President of Virasam (1970)
  [212:1:0.6] Award | National Award for Best Lyricist – "Telugu Veera Levara" (*Alluri Seetharama Raju*, 1974)

## SUB 3 | Arasam, AIPWA and Virasam
[212:3:0] TABLE head: Body | Year / place | Note
  [212:3:0.0] AIPWA (All India Progressive Writers' Association) | 1936, Lucknow | Founding session presided by Premchand; president in 2011: Namvar Singh (exam key)
  [212:3:0.1] Arasam (Abhyudaya Rachayitala Sangham) | 1943; first conference Tenali | Telugu counterpart of the AIPWA; first conference presided by Tapi Dharma Rao
  [212:3:0.2] Virasam (Viplava Rachayitala Sangham) | July 1970, Hyderabad | Revolutionary Writers' Association; founding President Sri Sri; Vice-President Kodavatiganti Kutumba Rao
[212:3:1] - Path: **nationalist poetry → Abhyudaya (progressive, Arasam) → Viplava (revolutionary, Virasam, after Srikakulam)**.
[212:3:2] - Virasam's spark: **Sri Sri's Shashtipurti, Vizag, Feb 1970**; its publications ***Le*** and ***March*** were banned.
[212:3:3] - Virasam is **not** the "Progressive Writers' Association" – that is Arasam.

## SUB 4 | Praja Natya Mandali and Progressive Theatre
[212:4:0] - **Praja Natya Mandali (1943):** communist cultural front, **Andhra wing of IPTA**; spread ideas through drama, songs and folk forms – main form **Burrakatha**; workshops by Garikapati Rajarao and Koduri Atchaiah; suspended by 1948.
[212:4:1] - Play ***Maa Bhoomi*** – **Sunkara Satyanarayana and Vasireddy Bhaskara Rao**.
[212:4:2] - **Jana Natya Mandali (1971):** grew from the Art Lovers (1970) of **Gaddar and B. Narsinga Rao** – Viplava phase.

## SUB 0 | Viplava Parivartanamu and Plays Banned by the British
[213:0:0] # The banned NCM drama
[213:0:1] - **Viplava Parivartanamu:** published and **banned during the Non-Cooperation Movement**; it pushed **Swadeshi goods** and khaddar.
[213:0:2] - **Author: Kodali Anjaneyulu** (NextGen; APPSC 2018 and G2 2019) – not **Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri** (Swami Sitaram), the usual trap.
[213:0:3]   - Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri wrote the NCM song **"Kallu Monadoi"** and supported the **NCM boycott of elections (1920)**.
[213:0:4] - **Why troupes were watched:** a drama company could put a political idea before **thousands of illiterate people in one evening**.
[213:0:5] - Drama companies were the **main mass entertainment** of the time, so they carried **social-reform** themes first and **nationalist** ones later.
[213:0:6] - **Jan 2022:** the AP government banned staging of ***Chintamani*** (over the character Subbi Setty); the ban was challenged in the High Court.
[213:0:7] > Trap: Viplava Parivartanamu = Kodali Anjaneyulu, NCM; Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri = "Kallu Monadoi".

[214:1:0] - **Gandhi's 1921 coastal tour** (Kakinada, Rajahmundry, Eluru, Vijayawada, Chirala, Nellore) **drew women into the struggle**; he collected the **Tilak Swaraj Fund**.

[214:1:3] - **Women leaders of the NCM:** Duvvuri Subbamma, Ponaka Kanakamma, Unnava Lakshmibayamma – orators and organisers.

=== NOTES 1:216 | Peasant Movements – Indigo, Deccan Riots, Moplah, Bardoli, Tebhaga, Telangana Armed Struggle
## SUB 0 | Indigo Revolt, Nil Darpan, Pabna and the Deccan Riots
[216:0:0] # Indigo Revolt (1859-60), Bengal
[216:0:1] - European planters forced ryots to grow **indigo instead of paying crops like rice**, through **advances and fraudulent contracts**, at prices that did not pay; intimidation, flogging, seizure of cattle.
[216:0:2] - Leaders **Digambar and Bishnu Biswas** of **Nadia**; ryots refused to sow, went on rent strike and used the courts.
[216:0:3] - **Supported by the Bengali intelligentsia** – press (**Harish Chandra Mukherjee's *Hindoo Patriot***), meetings, legal aid; missionaries too.
[216:0:4] - **Indigo Commission (March 1860):** found planters coercive; ryots must **fulfil existing contracts** but **could refuse future ones**; Nov 1860 notification – no compulsion. It did **not** find indigo profitable to ryots.
[216:0:5] - Came **immediately after 1857** (Sanyasi 1763-1800 and Santhal 1855-56 earlier; Pabna 1873-76 later).
[216:0:6] # Nil Darpan
[216:0:7] TABLE head: Role | Person
  [216:0:7.0] Written (Bengali play, 1860) | Dinabandhu Mitra
  [216:0:7.1] English translation | Michael Madhusudan Dutt
  [216:0:7.2] Published | Rev. James Long
[216:0:8] - Nil Darpan (1860) is earlier than Satyendranath Tagore's ICS (1863), Arya Samaj (1875) and Anandamath (1882).
[216:0:9] - **Tinkathia** (indigo on **3/20** of land) belongs to **Champaran (Bihar, 1917)**, ended by the Champaran Agrarian Act 1918.
[216:0:10] # Pabna and Deccan
[216:0:11] - **Pabna (1873-76):** agrarian league of **Ishan Chandra Roy and Shambhu Pal** against **rent hikes** by zamindars; legal, peaceful → **Bengal Tenancy Act 1885**.
[216:0:12] - **Deccan Riots (1875):** **Poona and Ahmednagar**; ryotwari peasants vs **Marwari and Gujarati moneylenders**; debt bonds burnt; cause incl. the **Limitation Law of 1859** → **Deccan Agriculturists' Relief Act 1879** (protection from **moneylenders**).
[216:0:13] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.607-609; PMF Modern History 03 p.120-121; app notes 1:216 (sub 1, 2)
[216:0:14] - **Ryoti system:** indigo contract farming – the ryot took an advance and signed a contract to grow indigo on **his own land**; under **nij** the planter grew it on his own land with hired labour.

## SUB 3 | Telangana Armed Struggle (1946-51)
[216:3:0] - Against the **Nizam's feudal order** – deshmukhs, jagirdars, **vetti** (forced labour); led by the **CPI** through the **Andhra Mahasabha** – the biggest peasant guerrilla war (3,000 villages).
[216:3:1] - Land redistributed; **village soviets** set up; **called off October 1951**.
[216:3:2] - **Doddi Komaraiah:** **first martyr** – shot at **Kadavendi** (Jangaon taluka, Nalgonda) on **4 July 1946** by the **Visnur deshmukh's** men while **defending a poor washerwoman's land** (**Chakali Ailamma**).
[216:3:3] - Leaders: **P. Sundarayya, Chandra Rajeswara Rao**; woman fighter **Mallu Swarajyam**; Andhra Mahasabha president 1941-46 **M. Suryanarayana**; **Ravi Narayana Reddy** presided Chilkur (1941); 11th session **Bhongir (1944)** communist-dominated.
[216:3:4] TABLE head: Book | Author
  [216:3:4.0] *Veera Telangana – Naa Anubhavalu, Gnapakalu* (Heroic Telangana) | Ravi Narayana Reddy – not Mallu Swarajyam
  [216:3:4.1] *History of the People's Armed Struggle of Telangana (1946-51)* | Devulapalli Venkateswara Rao (NextGen/APPSC line: "Devulapalli Ramanuja Rao")
  [216:3:4.2] *Telangana People's Struggle and its Lessons* | P. Sundarayya
[216:3:5] - **Vetti vs bonded labour:** vetti = caste-bound forced service of village servant castes; bonded labour = debt-based, regardless of caste (the statements swap them).

## SUB 4 | Moplah Revolt (1921) and Bardoli (1928)
[216:4:0] - **Moplah (Mappila):** **Muslim tenants of Malabar** against **Hindu landlords (jenmis)** and the British – no security of tenure, high rents, renewal fees; merged with the **Khilafat** agitation; arrest of **Ali Musliyar** (Aug 1921); leader **Variyankunnath Kunjahammad Haji**.
[216:4:1] - **Wagon Tragedy, November 1921:** Moplah prisoners suffocated in a closed railway wagon.

## SUB 2 | Prakasam Committee and the 1948 Abolition Act
[217:2:0] - **1937:** Rajaji's Madras ministry appointed a **zamindari committee** chaired by **Revenue Minister T. Prakasam**; report **7 Nov 1938**: **the land belongs to the tiller; the zamindar is only a tax collector**.
[217:2:1] TABLE head: Step | Date
  [217:2:1.0] Bill introduced by Revenue Minister Kala Venkata Rao | 11 Nov 1947
  [217:2:1.1] Passed – Madras Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act | 1948
  [217:2:1.2] Assent of Governor-General C. Rajagopalachari | 2 April 1949 (called the first law of independent India with the GG's assent)
  [217:2:1.3] In force | 7 Sept 1949
[217:2:2] - Abolished zamindari and inam estates; tenants became **direct ryots of the State**; zamindars compensated.
[217:2:3] - Shielded by the **1st (1951)** and **4th (1955)** Amendments; one of the **first abolition laws** of independent India.
[217:2:4] - **Madras Estates Land Act 1908:** **regulated** landholder–ryot relations, **did not abolish** estates; 3rd Amendment (1936) – occupancy rights for those in possession on **30 June 1934**.
