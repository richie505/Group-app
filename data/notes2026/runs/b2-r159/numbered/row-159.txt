## SUB 0 | European Union and Council of Europe
[0:0] # Evolution
[0:1] TABLE head: Year | Step
  [0:1.0] 1951 | Treaty of Paris – European Coal and Steel Community (6 states)
  [0:1.1] 1957 | Treaty of Rome – EEC (forerunner of the EU) and Euratom
  [0:1.2] 1986 | Single European Act
  [0:1.3] 1992 | Maastricht Treaty (signed 7 Feb 1992, in force 1993) – created the EU
  [0:1.4] 1999 | Euro launched (1 January 1999) only as a unit of account; notes and coins in circulation from 1 January 2002
  [0:1.5] 2007 | Bulgaria and Romania join – 27 members
  [0:1.6] 2009 | Lisbon Treaty in force (Article 50 exit clause)
  [0:1.7] 2013 | Croatia joins (1 July 2013) – latest member; 28
  [0:1.8] 2020 | UK leaves, 31 January 2020 – back to 27
[0:2] - The EU grew out of the **European Community**; EU citizens hold EU citizenship in addition to national citizenship; **Switzerland is not** a member.
[0:3] - The EU has its **own flag, anthem and currency** but **no Constitution** (the 2004 Constitutional Treaty failed); its combined armed forces are the second largest in the world (NCERT).
[0:4] - The **Preamble of the proposed EU Constitution** was drafted by **Valéry Giscard d'Estaing**, chairman of the European Convention (2002–03).
[0:5] # Brexit
[0:6] - Referendum **23 June 2016** (51.9% Leave) → David **Cameron** resigned; **Theresa May** (the Home Secretary) became Conservative leader and PM – the Conservatives stayed in power (the opposition did **not** come to power).
[0:7] - Exit under **Article 50 TEU**; UK left **31 January 2020**.
[0:8] - **Windsor Framework** (February 2023) – Northern Ireland, UK and EU.
[0:9] # Other EU facts
[0:10] - **Greek referendum (July 2015)** – whether to accept the **bailout conditions** of the EU and IMF (not on leaving the EU).
[0:11] - **Ukraine and Moldova** granted candidate status – European Council, Brussels, **23 June 2022**.
[0:12] - **Roberta Metsola** (Malta) – elected **President of the European Parliament**, January 2022.
[0:13] - **Digital Single Market Strategy** – EU, adopted **6 May 2015**. **Stability and Growth Pact** – limits budget deficits.
[0:14] - **India-EU FTA (2026):** EU commitments in **144 services subsectors**; social security agreements with all EU members within 5 years; post-study work framework for Indian students.
[0:15] # Council of Europe
[0:16] - Founded **1949**, HQ **Strasbourg**; upholds **human rights, democracy and rule of law** in Europe (European Convention on Human Rights) – **not** an EU body.
[0:17] - **47 members** until **Russia was expelled (March 2022)** → now **46**.

## SUB 1 | SAARC and BBIN
[1:0] # Basics
[1:1] - **Charter signed at Dhaka, 8 December 1985** (1st Summit) – SAARC founded **1985**, not 1984 or 1986.
[1:2] - **Secretariat: Kathmandu** (Nepal) – not Dhaka (Charter venue) or New Delhi.
[1:3] - **Seven founders:** India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Maldives, Bhutan. **Afghanistan** joined at the **14th Summit, April 2007** → **8 members** (not a founder).
[1:4] - **Members (8):** Afghanistan, Bangladesh, **Bhutan**, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka.
[1:5] - **Not members:** **Myanmar** (an observer), China (observer), Tibet (not a state), Malaysia, Iran. SAARC includes every land neighbour of India **except China and Myanmar**.
[1:6] - **Observers (9):** EU, Australia, China, Iran, Japan, Republic of Korea, Mauritius, Myanmar, USA – China is **not** the only observer; **Russia is not** an observer.
[1:7] # Secretariat and Secretary General
[1:8] - The **Secretary General** heads the Secretariat; appointed by the **Council of Ministers** from member states in **alphabetical rotation** for a **three-year** term; assisted by **eight directors** on deputation from member states (CDS 2014: all three statements correct).
[1:9] - **31st SAARC Council of Ministers** (February 2009) – **Colombo, Sri Lanka**.
[1:10] # Summits
[1:11] TABLE head: Summit | Venue / date
  [1:11.0] 1st | Dhaka, December 1985 (Charter)
  [1:11.1] 12th | Islamabad, January 2004 – SAFTA signed

[1:12] # Institutions and India's initiatives
[1:13] - **SAPTA** 1995 → **SAFTA:** signed **2004**, in force **1 January 2006**; zero duty on eligible goods meeting Rules of Origin (DRI busted an areca-nut origin fraud, Aug 2026).
[1:14] - **South Asian University** – **New Delhi** (2010). **SAARC Development Fund** – **Thimphu**. SAARC Disaster Management Centre – India.
[1:15] - India's proposals: **South Asia Satellite (2017)**, **SAARC COVID-19 Emergency Fund (2020)**. SAARC Visa Exemption Scheme (1992) cancelled for Pakistanis after **Pahalgam (2025)**.
[1:16] - Not all South Asian states are democracies (e.g., Taliban-ruled Afghanistan since 2021) – the wrong statement in CDS 2011.
[1:17] - Jun 2026: SAARC and BIMSTEC both constrained by India's tensions with Islamabad and Dhaka.
[1:18] # BBIN Motor Vehicles Agreement
[1:19] - **Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal**; signed at **Thimphu, 2015**; **Bhutan has not ratified** it.

## SUB 2 | ASEAN, East Asia Summit and RCEP
[2:0] # ASEAN
[2:1] - Founded by the **Bangkok Declaration, 8 August 1967** – five founders: Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand. **Secretariat: Jakarta**.
[2:2] - **11 members** with **Timor-Leste (2025)**: Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam, Timor-Leste.
[2:3] - **Not members:** **China**, **India**, **South Korea**, Japan (dialogue partners). **Myanmar** joined ASEAN and BIMSTEC in **1997**; it is the **only ASEAN country with a land border with India**.
[2:4] - **Timor-Leste (East Timor):** a **Portuguese** colony until 1975, then occupied by Indonesia (1975–99) and run by the UN (1999–2002); independent **20 May 2002**.
[2:5] - Geostrategic importance of Southeast Asia: its **location between the Pacific and Indian Oceans** and its maritime character (UPSC 2011).
[2:6] # India and ASEAN

[2:8] - **ASEAN Regional Forum** – **1994**. **AIFTA (goods)** signed 2009, in force **2010**; India sought early completion of the **AITIGA review** (32nd ARF, Kuala Lumpur, July 2025).

[2:11] # East Asia Summit
[2:12] - **First EAS: 14 December 2005, Kuala Lumpur**; 16 countries (10 ASEAN + India, Japan, China, South Korea, Australia, NZ).
[2:13] - Now **19**: 11 ASEAN + India, China, Japan, US, Russia, Australia, NZ, South Korea. India is a member of the **EAS**, but **not** of ASEAN or **APEC** (UPSC 2015). **20th EAS (Kuala Lumpur, 2025)** – Kuala Lumpur Declaration.
[2:14] # RCEP
[2:15] - **ASEAN-led** FTA: ten ASEAN + Australia, China, Japan, NZ, Korea; signed **15 November 2020**; about **30% of world population**; **India stayed out (November 2019)**.
[2:16] - Seen as a counter to the US-led **Trans-Pacific Partnership**; often described as China-backed though it is ASEAN-centred.
[2:17] # Mekong Ganga Cooperation
[2:18] - Launched **10 November 2000, Vientiane**; **6 members**: India, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Thailand, Vietnam; areas: **tourism, culture, education, transport and communications** (no defence).

## SUB 3 | BIMSTEC
[3:0] # Name, members, Secretariat
[3:1] - **Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation** – set up by the **Bangkok Declaration, 1997** as **BIST-EC** (Bangladesh, India, Sri Lanka, Thailand); Myanmar joined 1997; **Nepal and Bhutan 2004**.
[3:2] - **7 members:** Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Myanmar, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Thailand – **Pakistan is not** a member. **Secretariat: Dhaka**.
[3:3] - A **bridge between South Asia (SAARC) and Southeast Asia (ASEAN)**. It was **never** called the "Bangkok Agreement" (that is the 1975 Asia-Pacific Trade Agreement, APTA) and was **not** renamed "BISTEC" in 2014.
[3:4] - Sectors **cut from 14 to 7** (not expanded); each has a lead country. India leads **security** (counter-terrorism, disaster management, energy). Under the old 14 sectors **Myanmar** led **energy** (and agriculture).
[3:5] # Summits
[3:6] TABLE head: Summit | Venue / date | Outcome
  [3:6.0] 1st | Bangkok, 31 July 2004 | –
  [3:6.1] 4th | Kathmandu, 31 Aug 2018 | Grid Interconnection MoU signed
  [3:6.2] 5th | 30 March 2022, virtual, hosted by Sri Lanka (Colombo); addressed by PM Modi | BIMSTEC Charter adopted; Sri Lanka chair (India did not chair it)
  [3:6.3] 6th | Bangkok, 4 April 2025 (Thailand chair; PM Modi attended) | Bangkok Vision 2030 ("Prosperous, Resilient and Open"); Maritime Transport Cooperation Agreement; India's 21-point Action Plan
[3:7] - Current chair: **Bangladesh** (from April 2025).
[3:8] # Energy, security and India's initiatives
[3:9] - **Grid Interconnection MoU:** Union Cabinet approved signing on **12 April 2017** (**power sector** – cross-border electricity trade); signed at the 4th Summit (2018); set up the **BIMSTEC Grid Interconnection Coordination Committee (BGICC)**.
[3:10] - **First BIMSTEC Disaster Management Exercise (DMEx)** – **October 2017, hosted by India** (New Delhi/NCR).
[3:11] - **MILEX 2018, Pune** – first BIMSTEC army exercise.
[3:12] - **BIMSTEC leaders** were invited to PM Modi's **oath-taking, 30 May 2019**.
[3:13] - **First BIMSTEC Conference on Combating Drug Trafficking** – **13–14 February 2020, New Delhi**, hosted by **India** (NCB).
[3:14] - **1st BIMSTEC Youth Summit** – Gandhinagar (Feb 2025); **Cancer Care programme** at Tata Memorial (July 2025); **BODHI** training initiative (2025).

## SUB 5 | SCO, GCC, IORA and African Union
[5:0] # Shanghai Cooperation Organisation
[5:1] - **Shanghai Five (1996)** → **SCO 2001**; Secretariat **Beijing**; **RATS** (Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure) at **Tashkent**.
[5:2] - **10 members:** China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, **India and Pakistan (2017, Astana)**, Iran (2023), **Belarus (2024 – newest)**.
[5:3] TABLE head: SCO summit | Venue
  [5:3.0] May 2003 | Moscow
  [5:3.1] 15–16 Sep 2022 | Samarkand, Uzbekistan
  [5:3.2] July 2024 | Astana – Belarus joined
  [5:3.3] 2025 (25th) | Tianjin, China
[5:4] # Gulf Cooperation Council
[5:5] - **1981**, HQ **Riyadh**; **6 members:** Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE – **Iran is not** a member.
[5:6] - **June 2017:** Saudi Arabia, UAE, Bahrain and Egypt (with others) cut ties with **Qatar**, citing its **support for terrorism and ties with Iran**; ended by the **Al-Ula Declaration (January 2021)**.
[5:7] # IORA
[5:8] - **Indian Ocean Rim Association** (1997, as IOR-ARC), Secretariat **Ebene, Mauritius**, **23** members; **India is a member**; **Nelson Mandela** was among the leaders behind its creation.
[5:9] - Not "very recent" and not set up against piracy or oil spills; **not a maritime-security-only** alliance.
[5:10] - **Priority areas:** maritime safety and security; trade and investment facilitation; fisheries management; disaster risk management; academic and scientific cooperation; tourism and cultural exchanges. Cross-cutting: blue economy, women's economic empowerment.
[5:11] - **India is chair (2025-27)**, taking over from Sri Lanka in Nov 2025; **summit 2027**, first since **Jakarta 2017** (20th anniversary).
[5:12] # African Union and AP angle
[5:13] - **African Union** (2002) replaced the **OAU** (1963); HQ **Addis Ababa**; G20 permanent member since 2023.
[5:14] - AP: **NACIN Palasamudram** (Rayalaseema) – a **World Customs Organization** regional training centre for officials of SAARC, ASEAN and African nations.
[5:15] > Exam angle: "not a GCC member" → **Iran**; RATS → SCO; IORA Secretariat Ebene.
