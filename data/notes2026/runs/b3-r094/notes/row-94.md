=== Direct and Indirect Taxes – Classification and Taxable Events
# Direct vs indirect
| Basis | Direct tax | Indirect tax |
|---|---|---|
| Burden | **Cannot be shifted** – impact and incidence on the **same person** | **Shifted** to the consumer through prices – impact and incidence on different persons |
| Examples | **Income tax, corporation tax, wealth tax**, capital gains, property, gift, estate duty, expenditure tax, **STT** | **Customs, excise, sales tax, VAT, service tax, GST**, entertainment, octroi, entry tax |
| Nature | Generally **progressive** | Generally **regressive** |
| Price effect | **Non-inflationary**; does **not directly raise the price** of goods | **Inflationary** – raises prices |
| Administered by | **CBDT** – statutory body under the **Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963** | **CBIC** (formerly CBEC) |
- **Not direct**: customs duty, sales tax, excise, octroi – the usual "odd one out" against income, corporation, wealth and gift taxes.
- **Indirect pair**: **customs and excise** (corporation and wealth taxes are direct).
- **Income, corporate and wealth taxes – all direct**; "1 and 2 only" for income + corporate tax against service tax and customs.
- **Income tax** does not directly increase the price of a commodity – trade/business tax, import and excise (manufacturing) duties do.
- **GST** – a comprehensive **indirect** tax on supply, **destination-based**; it is not related to income.
- **Service tax** – an **indirect tax levied by the Central Government**, introduced **1994-95** (subsumed in GST 2017).
- **STT** – a **direct tax** on **transactions through the stock market** (from **1 October 2004**), though collected by the exchange.
- **"Paper taxes"**: wealth, gift and estate duty – never yielded much revenue; **wealth tax** gives the **least** revenue of the major taxes.
- **Impact** = on whom the tax is first imposed; **incidence** = who finally bears it; moving the burden forward (consumers) or backward (suppliers) = **tax shifting**. Property tax cannot be shifted through prices.
- **Incidence and elasticity**: if demand is **perfectly inelastic (elasticity zero)** the price rises by the full tax and the **consumer bears it all**; with infinite elasticity the price cannot rise.
- **Cut in direct taxes** → higher **disposable income** and consumption demand.
# Base / taxable event of each tax
| Tax | Taxable event / base |
|---|---|
| **Central excise** | **Manufacture / production** of goods (paid by the manufacturer) – "manufacture only" |
| **Customs** | **Import and export** (Customs Act, 1962) |
| Sales tax / VAT | **Sale** of goods |
| Service tax | Provision of service |
| GST | **Supply** of goods or services (from 1 July 2017) |
| **Corporation tax** | **Income / net profit of companies** – not production, sales or stock |
| Income tax | Income of individuals, HUFs, firms (Income Tax Act, 1961) |
| Capital gains tax | Gain on sale of a capital asset |
| Wealth tax | Net wealth – 1957 to 2015 (abolished) |
- **Toll** – a levy (tax) for using a road or bridge; tolls are a **State List** subject.
> Exam angle: "which is not a direct tax / which is indirect" is the single most repeated tax question (APPSC, UPPSC, MPPSC, Police).

=== Union and State Taxes – Who Levies, Who Shares
# Constitutional division
| Article / entry | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Article 265** | No tax levied or collected **except by authority of law** |
| Taxing entries | **Union List 15**, **State List 20**, Concurrent List 3 |
| Residuary taxing power | **Parliament** (gift, wealth, expenditure taxes) |
| **Article 276** | **Profession tax** – a **State** levy; ceiling **₹2,500 per person per year** |
| **State List Entry 46** | **Agricultural income tax** – States only; exempt under Income-tax Act **Sec 10(1)** |
| **Article 271** | **Surcharges** – retained by the Union, **not shared** |
| **80th Amendment, 2000** | Net proceeds of **all Union taxes** (minus cess, surcharge, collection cost) shareable on Finance Commission advice; **effective 1 April 1996** |
# Union taxes
- **Union alone (Union List)**: **customs duties, corporation tax**; not levied by States.
- **Corporation tax** is **imposed by the Central Government**; before 2000 its proceeds were **not shared** with States (Art. 270 then covered only income tax; Union excise under Art. 272) – the basis of old keys "not shared" / "accrues wholly to the Centre". **Since the 80th Amendment it is in the divisible pool.**
- **Tax on income other than agricultural income** – the classic tax **levied and collected by the Union but distributed** between Union and States.
- Pre-GST pairs that are **not Union taxes**: **tolls and tax on mineral rights**, **taxes on intoxicants (State excise) and entertainment** – State List; customs, estate duty and terminal taxes on goods/passengers by rail, air or sea are Union.
- **Income tax on salary (TDS)** is collected and paid into the account of the **Central Government**.
- **Agricultural income tax** – **not shared** by the Centre (it is a State tax). Income from **poultry, dairy, wool rearing** is **not** agricultural income (taxable); **nursery saplings** are agricultural income; **rural agricultural land is not a capital asset** under the Income-tax Act, 1961.
# State taxes
- **Levied by States / local bodies**: **sales tax** (pre-GST), **entertainment tax** (State List, List II), **road / motor vehicle tax**, profession tax, property tax, land revenue, **State excise on liquor**, stamp duty and registration, electricity duty, agricultural income tax.
- **Sales tax on toothpaste** (pre-GST) – **imposed and collected by the State Government**.
- **Not a State / AP own-revenue source**: **customs duty**, corporation tax ("taxes on companies").
- **Most important source of State revenue**: **sales tax** (pre-2017) – now **SGST**.
- **States' revenue receipts**: **own tax revenue** is the largest component (~50%, FY25); share in Central taxes second (~32%); CAG average 2014-24: own tax 47%, tax share 28%, grants 17%.
- **AP (2024-25 RE)**: **SGST** largest own-tax source – ₹36,296 crore (**38%**) of ₹94,967 crore; **State Excise** ₹21,300 crore (**22%**).
- Union Budget 2017-18: farmers who gave land for **Amaravati** (AP capital) under land pooling – exempted from **capital gains tax**.
> Exam angle: "levied by the Union but shared" questions were set on the pre-2000 scheme; after the 80th Amendment every Union tax (except cess and surcharge) is shareable.

=== VAT, MODVAT, CENVAT, Sales Tax and Service Tax
# Value Added Tax
- **VAT** = tax on the **value added at each stage** between production and final sale – a **multi-point**, **destination-based**, **non-cascading** tax on final consumption, borne by the consumer; invoice-credit trail makes it **self-enforcing**; a **harmonised** commodity tax that **improves revenue buoyancy** ("will diminish revenue buoyancy" is **not** VAT's idea).
- State VAT was a **State subject** (State List Entry 54) – not "basically a subject of the Central Government".
- VAT applies to **commodity taxes – excise duties** (MODVAT/CENVAT) and sales tax; not to income tax, estate duty or agricultural income tax.
- Concept first proposed by **Wilhelm von Siemens** (Germany); first implemented by **France (1954)**.
- **L.K. Jha Indirect Taxation Enquiry Committee (1976)** – suggested VAT at the manufacturing stage.
- **VAT rate for medicines/drugs**: the **4%** slab (pre-GST State VAT).
- Some goods (petroleum, alcohol) are still outside GST – **State VAT** continues on them.
# Chronology
| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| **MODVAT** – on **Union excise** duties, to end cascading (first indirect tax reform) | **1 March 1986** (Budget 1986-87) |
| **Service tax** (Chelliah Committee advice; 3 services) | **1994-95** |
| **Uniform floor rates of sales tax** (Standing Committee of State Finance Ministers) | **1 January 2000** |
| **CENVAT** – replaced MODVAT; **Union excise** (an **indirect** tax) | **1 April 2000** |
| **First State VAT – Haryana** | **1 April 2003** |
| State VAT nationwide (20 States/UTs) | **1 April 2005** |
| Last States to join (TN 2007, UP 2008) | by 2008-09 |
| GST | 1 July 2017 |
- **Uniform sales tax**: in **January 2000** the Standing Committee of State Finance Ministers recommended **uniform floor rates of sales tax**, implemented from **1 January 2000**.
- About **7 States** had not implemented VAT by December 2005 (Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand – 2006; TN 2007; UP 2008).
- **Karnataka** – early e-payment of VAT/commercial taxes; **Maharashtra** – the State the **IMF** had advised earlier not to adopt VAT (APPSC key).
- **Sales tax** was **not levied in Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep** (question period); GST now covers all India.
- **Central Sales Tax (CST)** – levied on **inter-State trade** and in **Union Territories**; **SEZs exempt**; subsumed in GST.
# Service tax
- **Indirect**, **Central** tax; introduced **1994-95** (Manmohan Singh as FM) on **Raja Chelliah Committee** advice.
- Rates: **10% → 12% (Budget 2006-07)**; **12% → 14% (Budget 2015-16)**; from 2012-13, 12% on all services except the **negative list**.
- **T.R. Rastogi (Rustagi) Panel** (2006) – Finance Ministry panel to review **service tax** issues and circulars.
- **Swachh Bharat Cess** – **0.5%** on **all taxable services** (from 15 Nov 2015).
- **Multiple tax rates** – chief drawback: **misclassification** of goods and services (disputes, evasion).

=== Progressive, Regressive and Degressive Taxes; Laffer Effect
| Type | Feature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Progressive** | **Tax rate rises as income rises** – average burden rises with income ("ability to pay") | **Income tax** |
| **Regressive** | Takes a **higher proportion of income from low incomes** than from high | Indirect taxes, GST on essentials |
| **Proportional** | **One rate for all** incomes | Flat-rate tax |
| **Degressive** | Rate rises at first, then becomes flat | Progressive slabs with a top flat rate |
- **Indirect taxes are regressive** because they are charged at the **same rate for all income groups**.
- **Income tax in India**: **progressive and direct** – not collected by States, not proportional; **GST, sales tax, motor vehicles tax** are not progressive.
- **India's tax system**: **progressive in nature but overall degressive** (progressive slabs up to a flat top rate; regressive indirect taxes).
- **Redistribution** of income is best achieved by **progressive taxation combined with progressive expenditure**.
- **Laffer effect**: **reduction in tax rates leads to a more than proportionate increase in tax yield**; Laffer curve – **inverted U**, zero revenue at 0% and 100%; **Arthur Laffer** (1974); term coined by **Jude Wanniski**.
- Beyond a point, **higher tax rates may reduce revenue**.
- **Widening the tax base** (lower exemption threshold, fewer exemptions) raises revenue without changing rates – the **Chelliah approach: lower rates, broader base**.

=== Tax, Fee, Cess and Surcharge
| Levy | Key feature |
|---|---|
| **Tax** | **Compulsory pecuniary burden** imposed by legislative authority to support government; **no quid pro quo** |
| **Fee** | Paid for a **specific service** – quid pro quo |
| **Cess** | "Tax on tax" for a **specific purpose**; ends when the purpose is met; not shared |
| **Surcharge** | Additional charge on the tax payable – classically a levy to **raise revenue temporarily** (in practice long-running); none if no tax is due; **not shared (Art. 271)** |
- **Ad valorem tax** = **% of the value** of a commodity; **specific tax** = fixed amount per unit (weight/quantity).
- **Clean Energy Cess** on coal (Finance Act 2010) → renamed **Clean Environment Cess** (Budget **2016-17**).
- Budget **2016-17**: income-tax **surcharge 15%** on income **above ₹1 crore** (12% earlier).
- **SAT** (Securities Appellate Tribunal) is **not a tax**; **MAT** (Minimum Alternate Tax, 1996-97), GST and VAT are.

=== Tax-GDP Ratio and Tax Buoyancy
# Tax-GDP ratio
- **Tax-GDP ratio = total tax revenue / nominal GDP** – yardstick of fiscal capacity.
- **Low ratio** → large **informal sector** or high **evasion**; **falling ratio** → revenue growing **slower than GDP** (buoyancy < 1), less fiscal space, more borrowing – it does **not** by itself show less equitable distribution.
| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Centre gross tax revenue | **9.9% of GDP in 2019-20** (ES 2021-22); ~**11.5%** post-pandemic (FY22-FY25, ES 2025-26) |
| Combined Centre + States | ~**19.6%** (States' own tax ≈ 7% of GSDP) |
| **2008-09** (general government) | **16.39%** |
| India average 2001-22 | **16.36%** – lowest among emerging economies; below OECD |
| World Bank tipping point | **15%** |
# Buoyancy and elasticity
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Buoyancy** | % change in revenue / % change in GDP, **including discretionary changes** (rates, base); above 1 = revenue grows faster than GDP |
| **Elasticity** | Same response **excluding** discretionary changes |
- **2026-27 BE**: gross buoyancy **0.8**; **direct 1.1**, **indirect 0.3**.
- Buoyant revenues helped bring the fiscal deficit to **4.4% of GDP in FY26** (ES 2025-26).
