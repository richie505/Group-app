=== NOTES 3:94 | Indian Tax System – Direct and Indirect Taxes, Tax Buoyancy, Tax-gdp Ratio, Progressivity
## SUB 0 | Tax, Fee, Cess and Surcharge
[94:0:0] TABLE head: Levy | Key feature
  [94:0:0.0] Tax | Compulsory money burden imposed by legislative authority to support government; no quid pro quo (APPSC 2023)
  [94:0:0.1] Fee | Paid for a specific service – involves quid pro quo
  [94:0:0.2] Cess | "Tax on tax" for a specific purpose; ends when purpose is met; not shared with States
  [94:0:0.3] Surcharge | Extra charge on tax payable (% of tax); usually permanent; none if no tax is due; not shared (Article 271)
[94:0:1] - Cesses and surcharges are **outside the divisible pool** – a federal grievance of States.
[94:0:2] - Example cess: **Clean Energy Cess** on coal (Finance Act 2010) → renamed **Clean Environment Cess** (Budget 2016-17).
[94:0:3] - Budget 2016-17: **15% surcharge** on income above **₹1 crore**.
[94:0:4] - **Levied by States / local bodies**: profession tax, road tax, entertainment tax, property tax; **Union**: customs, corporation tax.
[94:0:5] "Pecuniary burden imposed by legislative authority to support government" = **Tax**, not fee or cess.
[94:0:6] - **Ad valorem vs specific:** ad valorem tax = **% of value** of the commodity; specific tax = **fixed amount per unit** (weight/quantity).
## SUB 1 | Constitutional Basis of Taxation
[94:1:0] TABLE head: Article / entry | Provision
  [94:1:0.0] Article 265 | No tax levied or collected except by authority of law
  [94:1:0.1] Taxing entries | Union List 15, State List 20, Concurrent List 3
  [94:1:0.2] Residuary taxing power | Parliament (gift, wealth, expenditure taxes)
  [94:1:0.3] 80th Amendment, 2000 | Net proceeds of all Union taxes (minus cess, surcharge, collection cost) shareable on FC advice; effective 1 April 1996
  [94:1:0.4] Article 276 | Profession tax – State levy; ceiling ₹2,500 a year – not a Union source (APPSC 2018)
  [94:1:0.5] State List Entry 46 | Agricultural income tax – States only
[94:1:1] - **Union taxes**: income tax (non-agricultural), corporation tax, customs, Union excise, CGST/IGST.
[94:1:2] - **Union Government alone**: **customs duties, corporation tax** (Union List).
[94:1:3] - **Corporation tax** – levied and collected by the **Union**; **not levied by States**; now **shared** with States (per FC).
[94:1:4] - **Shared with States**: income tax, corporate tax, customs duty.
[94:1:5] - **Agricultural income tax** – **not shared** by the Centre; a **State subject**; exempt under Income-tax Act **Sec 10(1)**; nursery saplings = agricultural income; poultry and dairy are not.
[94:1:6] - **Entertainment tax** – **not levied by the Union**; **State List (List II)**.
[94:1:7] - **Road tax** – State governments.
[94:1:8] - **Sales tax on toothpaste** (pre-GST) – imposed and collected by the **State Government**.
## SUB 2 | Direct vs Indirect Taxes – Classification
[94:2:0] # Comparison
[94:2:1] TABLE head: Basis | Direct tax | Indirect tax
  [94:2:1.0] Burden | Cannot be shifted; impact and incidence on the same person | Shifted to consumer through prices
  [94:2:1.1] Examples | Income, corporation, wealth, capital gains, property, gift, estate, dividend tax, STT | Customs, excise, service tax, sales tax, VAT, GST, entertainment, luxury, entry taxes
  [94:2:1.2] Administered by | CBDT (Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963) | CBIC
  [94:2:1.3] Price effect | Generally non-inflationary | Inflationary
  [94:2:1.4] Nature | Usually progressive | May be regressive
[94:2:2] - **Income tax** – direct; does **not directly increase the price** of a commodity.
[94:2:3] - **Income, corporate and wealth taxes** – **all direct** (APPSC 2021).
[94:2:4] - **Sales tax** – **not a direct tax**.
[94:2:5] - **STT** – a **direct tax** though collected by the exchange (from **1 Oct 2004**).
[94:2:6] - **"Paper taxes"**: wealth, gift, estate duty – never yielded much revenue.
[94:2:7] - **Impact** = on whom the tax is **first imposed**; **incidence** = who **finally bears** it. **Shifting** moves burden **forward** (consumers) or **backward** (suppliers).
[94:2:8] # Base of Each Tax
[94:2:9] TABLE head: Tax | Type | Base
  [94:2:9.0] Income tax | Direct | Income of individuals, HUFs, firms, trusts
  [94:2:9.1] Corporation tax | Direct | Net profit of companies (22% base, effective ~25.17%)
  [94:2:9.2] Capital gains tax | Direct | Gain on sale of capital asset
  [94:2:9.3] Wealth tax | Direct | Net wealth – 1957 to 2015 (abolished)
  [94:2:9.4] Customs duty | Indirect | Import / export (Customs Act, 1962)
  [94:2:9.5] Excise duty | Indirect | Production, sale or licence of goods; paid by manufacturer
  [94:2:9.6] Service tax | Indirect | Services (subsumed in GST)
  [94:2:9.7] VAT | Indirect | Value added at each stage
  [94:2:9.8] GST | Indirect | Supply of goods and services
[94:2:10] - **Incidence and elasticity:** if demand is **perfectly inelastic**, buyers do not cut purchases, so the price rises by the full tax and the **consumer bears the whole** indirect tax.
## SUB 3 | Taxable Event for Each Tax
[94:3:0] TABLE head: Tax | Taxable event
  [94:3:0.0] Central excise | Manufacture of goods
  [94:3:0.1] Customs | Import or export
  [94:3:0.2] Sales tax / VAT | Sale of goods
  [94:3:0.3] Service tax | Provision of service
  [94:3:0.4] GST | Supply of goods or services (from 1 July 2017)
  [94:3:0.5] Corporation tax | Company income / profit
[94:3:1] GST = supply; excise = manufacture; customs = import/export; VAT = sale.
## SUB 4 | Progressive, Proportional, Regressive Taxes
[94:4:0] TABLE head: Type | Feature | Indian example
  [94:4:0.0] Progressive | Average tax burden rises with income; higher marginal rates on higher slabs ("ability to pay") | Income tax
  [94:4:0.1] Regressive | Poor pay a higher share of income | Excise, customs, GST on essentials
  [94:4:0.2] Proportional | One rate for all | Sales tax (flat rate)
  [94:4:0.3] Degressive | Rate rises at first, then becomes flat | Capped slabs
[94:4:1] - **Indirect taxes are regressive** – charged at the **same rates for all income groups**.
[94:4:2] - **Income tax** – **progressive and direct** (not collected by States, not proportional).
[94:4:3] - **India's tax system** – **progressive in nature but overall degressive**: progressive direct taxes + regressive indirect taxes; becoming more progressive as the direct share rises.
[94:4:4] - Income is best spread through **progressive taxation plus progressive expenditure** (welfare, subsidies, social schemes).
## SUB 5 | Composition of Tax Revenue – Then and Now
[94:5:0] # Historical Composition
[94:5:1] - **First four decades of planning**: main Central revenue from **indirect taxes** (excise, customs, sales tax).
[94:5:2] - Two largest Central tax sources (1995 question): **customs duty and income tax**.
[94:5:3] - Largest revenue source: **Union excise** in 2004-05 and 2005-06; **corporation tax** in 2005-06, 2007-08 and 2013-14 (as per those years' questions).
[94:5:4] - **Excise share** of gross tax revenue fell sharply: **30.4% (2005-06) → 20.8% (2007-08)** (ES 2009-10).
[94:5:5] - Indirect share fell from 1990 to 2011; direct share in GNP (2004 question): about **5%**.
[94:5:6] # Now
[94:5:7] - **Direct tax share** in gross tax revenue: **61.2% (2026-27 BE)** vs indirect **38.8%**; 58.8% in FY25; 55.5% average FY22-FY25.
[94:5:8] - 2026-27 BE: direct taxes **6.9% of GDP**, indirect **4.3%** (2024-25 actuals 6.7% / 4.8%).
[94:5:9] - **Income tax** = **single largest tax source** (2025-26 and 2026-27: 21% of the rupee).
[94:5:10] Subsidy is **not public revenue**; it is expenditure. Income tax, borrowings and VAT are revenue.

## SUB 7 | Tax-gdp Ratio
[94:7:0] - **Tax-GDP ratio = total tax revenue / nominal GDP** – yardstick of fiscal capacity.
[94:7:1] - **Low ratio** → large **informal sector** or high **evasion** (~4.3% of tax revenue lost yearly).
[94:7:2] - **Falling ratio** → revenue growing **slower than GDP** (buoyancy < 1) → less fiscal space, more borrowing.
[94:7:3] TABLE head: Measure | Value
  [94:7:3.0] Centre gross tax revenue | 9.9% of GDP in 2019-20 (ES 2021-22; APPSC 2021); ~11.5% post-pandemic (FY22-FY25, ES 2025-26)
  [94:7:3.1] States | Own tax ≈ 7% of GSDP
  [94:7:3.2] Combined | ~19.6%
  [94:7:3.3] 2008-09 | 16.39% (Centre + States about 16.5%)
  [94:7:3.4] India average (2001-22) | 16.36% – lowest among emerging economies; below OECD
  [94:7:3.5] World Bank tipping point | 15%
## SUB 8 | Tax Buoyancy, Elasticity and Effects of Tax Changes
[94:8:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [94:8:0.0] Buoyancy | % change in revenue / % change in GDP, including discretionary changes (rates, base); above 1 = revenue grows faster than GDP
  [94:8:0.1] Elasticity | Same response excluding discretionary changes
  [94:8:0.2] Laffer curve | Inverted-U; revenue zero at 0% and 100% rates; Arthur Laffer (1974); term by Jude Wanniski
  [94:8:0.3] Base widening | More taxpayers, fewer exemptions rather than rate hikes – Chelliah approach
[94:8:1] - **Recent buoyancy (2026-27 BE)**: gross **0.8**; **direct 1.1** vs **indirect 0.3** (GST not keeping pace).
[94:8:2] - Buoyant revenues helped the fiscal deficit fall to **4.4% of GDP in FY26** (ES 2025-26).
[94:8:3] - **Cut in direct taxes** → higher **disposable income** and **consumption demand**; may also help savings and investment (APPSC 2018). E.g. income up to **₹12 lakh** tax-free under the new regime.
[94:8:4] - Caution: personal income tax growth in 2025-26 only 0.037% after rate cuts; Centre's GTR grew only 3.7% in Q1 2026-27.
[94:8:5] - **Simpler tax system** raises **productivity** by removing **deadweight costs** and resource-allocation distortions (APPSC G1 2024).
[94:8:6] - **Direct Tax Code** – to replace the **Income Tax Act, 1961** with a single law.

[94:9:2] # Chronology
[94:9:3] TABLE head: Event | Date

  [94:9:3.1] Service tax (Chelliah advice) | 1994-95

  [94:9:3.5] GST | 1 July 2017
[94:9:4] - **Service tax rates**: 10% → 12% (Budget 2006-07); 12% → 14% (Budget 2015-16); from 2012-13, 12% on all services except the **negative list**.
[94:9:5] - **VAT**: uniform, **non-cascading**; taxes value added at **all stages** between production and final sale; **self-enforcing** (invoice-credit trail); improves revenue buoyancy; now merged into GST but still applies to goods outside GST. "Will diminish revenue buoyancy" does **not** explain VAT's idea.
[94:9:6] - **Sales tax was not levied in Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep** (question period); GST covers all India.
[94:9:7] - **Project Saksham** – new indirect tax network of CBEC (now CBIC); approved by CCEA, September 2016.
