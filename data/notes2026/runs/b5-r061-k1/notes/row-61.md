=== Fission vs Fusion – Chain Reaction, Reactor vs Bomb, Energy of the Sun
| Point | Fission | Fusion |
|---|---|---|
| Process | **Heavy nucleus splits** (U-235 + slow neutron → barium + krypton + **2–3 neutrons** + energy) | **Two light nuclei join** (deuterium + tritium → helium + neutron) |
| Trigger / condition | **Neutron** hit on a fissile nucleus; **critical mass** | Very high temperature (10⁷–10⁸ K) and pressure – "thermonuclear" |
| Energy | Large | **More per unit mass** (3–4 times fission) |
| Where | **Nuclear reactors** (controlled), **atom bomb** (uncontrolled) | **Sun and stars**, **hydrogen bomb**; fusion power still at R&D stage |
- **Breaking apart of a nucleus = fission**, not fusion – the "NOT correct" statement in APPSC/UPPSC items.
- All present-day nuclear electricity comes from **fission**.
# Chain reaction and criticality
- Each fission releases **more neutrons** (2–3), which split more nuclei → **chain reaction**; fast neutrons are **slowed by a moderator (graphite, heavy water)** so that U-235 captures them. Not every neutron causes a fresh fission, and neutrons do not all react "immediately" (UPSC 2001: only statements I and III).
- **Critical mass** = minimum fissile mass that sustains a chain reaction; **criticality** = self-sustaining chain reaction – every operating reactor is "critical".
- **Reactor vs atom bomb:** both use a chain reaction; in a **reactor it is controlled** (moderator, control rods), in a **bomb it is uncontrolled**. Reactors do use moderators (CDS 2017: only II and III correct).
# Atom bomb and hydrogen bomb
| Feature | Atom (fission) bomb | Hydrogen (thermonuclear) bomb |
|---|---|---|
| Principle | **Uncontrolled fission** of U-235 / Pu-239 | **Uncontrolled fusion** of **deuterium and tritium** into helium, **triggered by a fission bomb** |
| Person | **Otto Hahn** (with F. Strassmann, 1938-39) – discovered nuclear fission | **Edward Teller** – "father of the hydrogen bomb"; first US test Nov 1952, Eniwetok |
| Power | Large | Far more powerful |
| Hiroshima, 6 Aug 1945 (first) | **Little Boy** | **U-235** |
| Nagasaki, 9 Aug 1945 | **Fat Man** | **Plutonium-239** |
- Only **Hiroshima and Nagasaki** were atom-bombed (not Tokyo, Sendai, Miyazaki).
- **North Korea** claimed a successful **hydrogen bomb** test (January 2016; again September 2017).
- India's **Shakti 1998** (Pokhran-II) series included a thermonuclear device.
# Energy of the Sun and stars
- Sun and stars shine by **nuclear fusion** – hydrogen nuclei fuse into **helium** in the core (about 15 million K); not fission, chemical reaction, photoelectric effect or gravity. "Energy generation in stars" is the statement **not** associated with fission.
> Exam angle: about 35 PYQs – "principle of the atom bomb / hydrogen bomb", "energy of the Sun", "difference between reactor and bomb = controlled chain reaction". Uncontrolled fusion = H-bomb; controlled fission = reactor.

=== Atomic Structure – Nucleus, Isotopes, Isobars and Isotones
| Particle | Charge | Mass | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Proton** | **+1** (equal to the charge of a **positron**) | ≈ 1 amu | Nucleus |
| **Neutron** | **Zero** | ≈ 1 amu (slightly more than proton) | Nucleus (does **not** revolve around it) |
| Electron | −1 | ≈ 1/1837 of proton | Shells around the nucleus |
- **Nucleus = protons + neutrons** (nucleons); it holds **almost all the mass** of the atom. Rutherford's **gold-foil (α-scattering)** experiment showed a small, dense, **positively charged nucleus** at the centre.
- Electrons are held to the nucleus by the **electric (electrostatic)** force; electrons in the outermost (highest) shell are **valence electrons**. Atoms of different elements have **different atomic numbers and different numbers of valence electrons**.
- Discoverers: electron – J.J. Thomson (1897); nucleus – Rutherford (1911); neutron – **James Chadwick (1932)**.
# Atomic number, mass number, atomic weight
- **Atomic number (Z) = number of protons** (= electrons in a neutral atom); fixes the element.
- **Mass number (A) = protons + neutrons** – a whole number; neutrons = A − Z.
| Example | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| ⁹⁴Pu²⁴² | 242 − 94 | **148 neutrons** |
| Carbon, 6 protons, A = 12 | 12 − 6 | **6 neutrons** |
| 18 electrons, 20 neutrons | 18 + 20 | **A = 38** |
| 7 electrons in M-shell, 18 neutrons | 2 + 8 + 7 = 17 protons; 17 + 18 | **A = 35** |
| Atomic weight W, atomic number N | protons = Z | **N** |
- **Atomic weight** is the average mass of an element's isotopes, so it **can be a fraction** (mass number cannot).
- **Uranium (Z = 92)** is the **heaviest naturally occurring** element.
# Isotopes, isobars, isotones
| Term | Same | Different | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Isotope** | **Atomic number** (protons) | **Mass number** (neutrons) | ¹H, ²H, ³H; ¹⁶O, ¹⁷O, ¹⁸O |
| **Isobar** | **Mass number** | Atomic number | ³H and ³He |
| **Isotone** (isoneutronic) | **Number of neutrons** (A − Z) | Atomic and mass numbers | ³H and ⁴He (2 each); **¹⁴C, ¹⁵N, ¹⁶O** (8 each) |
- Isotopes have the **same chemical properties** (same electrons) but different physical properties (mass).
- Hydrogen isotopes: **protium** (no neutron), **deuterium** (1 neutron, "heavy hydrogen"), **tritium** (2 neutrons, radioactive, made by hitting **lithium** with neutrons) – isotopes of **hydrogen**, not carbon.
- **Heavy water (D₂O):** **deuterium** (an isotope of **hydrogen**) takes the place of ordinary hydrogen; discovered by **H.C. Urey** (Nobel Chemistry 1934).
- **Helium nucleus** = **2 protons + 2 neutrons** (= the alpha particle).
> Exam angle: NDA/SSC/state PSC recall items – mass number = p + n, isotope = same Z, isobar = same A, isotone = same neutrons; isotopes/isobars "both reversed" → neither statement correct.

=== Nuclear Fuels – Fissile vs Fertile, Enrichment, What Is Not a Fuel
| Isotope | Type | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **U-235** | **Fissile** | Only **0.7%** of natural uranium; the isotope that powers reactors and fuels bombs |
| U-238 | **Fertile** | 99.3% of natural uranium; absorbs a neutron → **Pu-239** |
| **Pu-239** | **Fissile** | Not natural; bred from U-238 – a "good nuclear fuel" |
| Th-232 | **Fertile** | Th-232 + neutron → fissile **U-233** |
| **U-233** | Fissile | Fuel of Stage III and of KAMINI |
- **Uranium** is the main fuel of nuclear reactors (the metal "used for producing electricity"); **thorium** is usable only after breeding into U-233.
- India's PHWRs burn **natural uranium** (unenriched; mostly U-238 with 0.7% U-235).
- **Enrichment** = raising the share of **U-235** (not U-238) by **isotope separation**; most common method: **gas centrifuge** (uranium as UF₆; earlier gaseous diffusion).
| Use | U-235 level |
|---|---|
| Light-water power reactors | **3–5%** |
| Research / some naval reactors | Up to 20% |
| Weapons-grade | About **90%** (far above 20%) |
# Not a nuclear fuel
- **Moderators, coolants, absorbers:** graphite, heavy water, sodium, **boron, cadmium**, beryllium oxide (moderator).
- **Zirconium** (alloy cladding of fuel rods – corrosion-resistant, absorbs very few neutrons) is "essential for the construction of reactors"; it is **not radioactive**.
- Also not fuels: **helium** (inert gas), **lead**, **calcium**, **chromium** (not linked to atomic power), radium (radioactive but not a reactor fuel). **Monazite** (thorium ore), thorium and **beryllium** are linked to atomic power.
- "Good nuclear fuel" among U-238, Pu-239, neptunium, "thorium-236": **Pu-239** (fissile); U-238 is only fertile (APPSC 2007).
> Exam angle: "Which is NOT a fuel?" – cadmium, lead, calcium, helium, chromium; "uranium isotope used in power plants" – U-235; "thorium + neutrons" – U-233 (APPSC Group 1 2002, repeated).

=== Radioactivity – Alpha, Beta, Gamma and Half-life
- Discovered by **Henri Becquerel (1896)** in uranium; Marie and Pierre Curie found polonium and radium (1898). Radioactivity is a **nuclear** (not chemical) property; a radioactive substance emits **alpha, beta and gamma** rays. Measured by the **Geiger (Geiger–Müller) counter**.
| Emission | Nature | Charge | Stopped by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alpha** | **Helium nucleus** (2p + 2n); mass ≈ helium nucleus/atom | **+2** (positive) | Paper or skin – least penetrating |
| **Beta** | Electron (β⁻) or positron (β⁺) **emitted by the nucleus** | **−1** (negative) / +1 | Few mm of aluminium |
| **Gamma** | Electromagnetic radiation (photons) | Zero | Lead or concrete – most penetrating |
- Penetrating power (same energy): **α < β < γ**.
- **β⁻ emission:** a neutron turns into a proton + electron → the atom gains **one proton** (Z rises by 1).
- **Zero charge:** neutron, **neutrino**, gamma. **Deuteron** (a proton + neutron nucleus) is not a sub-atomic particle in the usual list.
- **Half-life** = time for half the nuclei to decay; a substance never decays "completely".
| Half-life | After | Remaining | Decayed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 days | 20 days (2 half-lives) | 1/4 | **3/4** |
| 5 years | 20 years (4 half-lives) | **1/16** | 15/16 |
- Natural decay series: **uranium** (U-238 → Pb-206), **thorium** (Th-232 → **Pb-208**), **actinium** (U-235 → Pb-207); **neptunium series = artificial**; there is **no "carbon series"**. Lead is the stable **end product of uranium decay**.
- Elements above Z = 82–83 are radioactive (plus technetium 43, promethium 61); **astatine, francium, tritium** are radioactive, **zirconium (Z = 40) is not**.
- **Xenon-135** = "nuclear poison" – a fission product that absorbs neutrons.
> Exam angle: charge of α/β/γ, half-life arithmetic (RAS 2007 repeated), "not radioactive" (zirconium, IAS 2001 and 2007).

=== Reactor Components and Reactor Types
| Part | Function | Substances |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel | Fissile material | U-235, Pu-239, U-233 |
| **Moderator** | **Slows fast neutrons to thermal speed** so U-235 can fission (does not absorb them or stop the reaction) | **Heavy water (D₂O)**, **graphite**, **ordinary (light) water**, beryllium oxide |
| **Control rods** | **Absorb neutrons**, control the rate | **Cadmium, boron**, hafnium, silver-indium |
| **Coolant** | **Transfers heat from the core** to make steam | Heavy water, light water, **CO₂**, helium, **liquid sodium** |
| Reactor core | Where **fuel, moderator and control rods** are located | – |
- Without control rods the chain reaction goes **out of control**; without a moderator a thermal reactor stops.
- **Heavy water** is chiefly a **moderator**; in PHWRs it is **both moderator and coolant**. **Ordinary water** is also both in PWRs/BWRs.
| Reactor | Fuel | Moderator | Coolant | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PHWR** | Natural uranium | Heavy water | Heavy water | Rajasthan (first), Kaiga, Kakrapar, Narora – Stage I |
| **BWR** | Enriched uranium | Light water | Light water (boils in core) | **Tarapur** units 1-2 (India's first, 1969) |
| **PWR / VVER** | Low-enriched uranium | Light water | Pressurised light water | **Kudankulam** |
| **Gas-cooled (Magnox)** | Natural uranium | **Graphite** | **CO₂** | UK |
| **FBR** | MOX (Pu-239 + U-238) | **None** | **Liquid sodium** | FBTR, PFBR – Kalpakkam |
| **AHWR** | Th-232 + U-233 / Pu | Heavy water | Boiling light water | BARC design |
- **Light-water reactors** (light water as moderator and coolant) make up **over 85%** of world capacity; PWR alone about 70% of reactors.
- **May 2017:** Union Cabinet approved **10 indigenous 700 MWe PHWRs** in "fleet mode".
> Exam angle: "function of heavy water / moderator" (UPSC 2011 ×2, IAS 2011) = slow down neutrons; "if control rods are not used" = chain reaction goes out of control (RAS 1992 ×2).

=== Fast Breeder Reactors – FBTR, PFBR, IGCAR and BHAVINI
- **Breeder reactor:** produces **more fissile material than it burns**; electricity still comes from **fission**. Fast neutrons (no moderator) turn the **U-238 blanket** into **Pu-239** (and Th-232 into U-233); **Pu-239 fissions** to give energy.
- **No moderator; liquid sodium coolant**; fuel MOX from reprocessed Stage I spent fuel. FBRs are **Stage II** (not Stage I).
# FBTR (Fast Breeder Test Reactor)
- At **IGCAR, Kalpakkam (Tamil Nadu)**; critical **18 October 1985**; design based on French **Rapsodie**.
- Coolant **liquid sodium**; fuel indigenous **plutonium-uranium mixed carbide** (plutonium-rich).
- **2005** milestone: IGCAR reprocessed FBTR's mixed-carbide fuel.
# PFBR, Kalpakkam
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Capacity | **500 MWe** (1,250 MWt); sodium-cooled, pool type |
| Designed by | **IGCAR** (Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research, Kalpakkam, est. 1971 as Reactor Research Centre) |
| Built and operated by | **BHAVINI** (Bharatiya Nabhikiya Vidyut Nigam Ltd, PSU of DAE, 2003, HQ Kalpakkam) |
| First criticality | **6 April 2026** → India enters **Stage II** |
| Delay | Cost doubled; about 16 years late |
- PFBR = India's **first indigenous FBR**; India the **2nd country after Russia** to run a commercial-scale fast breeder. Next: FBR-600 / 2 × 500 MWe units at Kalpakkam.
> ⚠ "Who developed / built PFBR": APPSC keys **BHAVINI** (builder, operator); IGCAR is the designer – read the verb.

=== Three-stage Programme, Thorium and the AHWR
- **Homi J. Bhabha** – "father of the Indian nuclear programme"; 1950s plan to use **abundant thorium** against **scarce uranium**; aim: self-reliance through a **closed fuel cycle**.
| Stage | Reactor | Fuel | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **PHWR** (D₂O moderator + coolant) | **Natural uranium** | Power + **Pu-239** in spent fuel |
| II | **FBR** (liquid sodium, no moderator) | MOX: **Pu-239 + U-238** | Breeds more Pu-239; Th-232 blanket → U-233 |
| III | **AHWR** / thorium reactors | **Th-232 + U-233** | Large-scale thorium use |
- **Thorium:** India holds about **25%** of world thorium (3rd largest) but only about **2%** of uranium – so thorium is the element with **large reserves in India**.
- **Thorium over uranium** (UPSC 2012): far **more abundant**, **more energy per unit mass mined**, **less harmful (long-lived) waste** – all three.
- **Monazite** = principal thorium ore (also rare earths – lanthanum, cerium); resources about **13.15 million tonnes** (DAE); beach sands of **Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh** – not the entire coast; only **IREL** may process and export it.
# AHWR (Advanced Heavy Water Reactor)
- **300 MWe**, designed by **BARC**; **heavy-water moderated, boiling light-water cooled**; fuel (Th-U-233) MOX and (Th-Pu) MOX – so it also burns **plutonium-bearing** fuel; **not yet built**.
- A **bridge from Stage I to Stage III** for large-scale thorium use.
- **ANEEL fuel** (thorium + **HALEU**, US firm Clean Core Thorium Energy, May 2026) is usable in existing PHWRs.
- Stage III hurdles: thin fissile inventory, long builds – **not** lack of thorium.

=== Fusion Research – Tokamak, ITER and IPR
- **Fusion reactors** work on the **fusion of light nuclei** (not fission of heavy nuclei), usually in a **tokamak** design, at **very high temperature** (UPPSC RO/ARO 2016: ii and iii).
- Plasma confinement: **magnetic** (tokamak) or **inertial** (lasers on fuel pellets).
- **ITER:** **Cadarache, southern France**; tokamak, world's largest magnetic-confinement experiment; members **EU + India, China, Japan, South Korea, Russia, USA** – **Canada is not a member**; India joined **2005** and supplies the **cryostat**; about **500 MW out from 50 MW in**.
- **Institute for Plasma Research, Gandhinagar** (not BARC): **Aditya** (first Indian tokamak) and **SST-1**.
- **December 2022:** first fusion ignition with net energy gain – **National Ignition Facility, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, USA**, using lasers.
