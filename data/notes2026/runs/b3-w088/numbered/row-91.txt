  [0:0.8] Role | Main regulator of the capital and securities (stock) market
[0:1] - **"SEBI was established in …"**: most books say **1988** (non-statutory); the statutory body, as SEBI itself puts it, was established in **1992** – read the options and the exam key (APPRB 2011 key: 1992).
[0:2] - **Parent law:** the **SEBI Act, 1992**, not the SCRA, 1956.
[0:3] - **SEBI Act Preamble:** **investor protection**, **development** and **regulation** of the securities market; bank lending rates are the RBI's job.
[0:4] - **Why statutory powers:** to **prevent (recurrence of) scams** in the capital market (Harshad Mehta, 1992) – the Government gave regulatory powers to **SEBI**, not RBI, SBI or ICICI.
[0:5] - **Board (SEBI Act, Sec. 4):** **Chairman** + **2** members from the **Finance and Law** ministries + **1** from the **RBI** + **5** other members, of whom **at least 3 are whole-time**.
[0:6] - **Functions:** registering intermediaries, levying fees, **investor education**, inspection and audit of exchanges and intermediaries.

  [0:7.2] NSE set up / recognised | Nov 1992 / April 1993
  [0:7.3] SAT set up | 1995
  [0:7.4] FMC merged into SEBI | 2015
[0:8] - ⚠ The Chakravarty Committee (monetary system), Chelliah Committee (tax reform) and Tendulkar Committee (poverty) had nothing to do with SEBI.

## SUB 2 | Commodity Regulation – Forward Markets Commission
[2:0] - **Forward Markets Commission (FMC):** statutory regulator of **commodity futures** trading, set up **1953** under the **Forward Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1952**; HQ Mumbai; moved from the Consumer Affairs Ministry to the **Ministry of Finance (2014)**.
[2:1] - In 2010 (UPSC) the FMC regulated **commodities futures trading** – not currency, equity or financial futures (those were SEBI's).
[2:2] - **Merged with SEBI on 28 September 2015**; SEBI now regulates commodity derivatives (now the answer to "who regulates commodity futures" = **SEBI**).
[2:3] - After the merger: brokers, NSE/BSE and FPIs allowed into commodity derivatives; **Category III AIFs** allowed.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.51; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #11 p.14; Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy 05 p.135-136 (FMC, merger Sep 2015); app notes 3:91 sub 3

## SUB 5 | Recent SEBI Reforms – T+0, BRSR, Finfluencers, Securities Markets Code
[5:0] - **T+0 (same-day) settlement:** **optional**, alongside **T+1**. Goals (APPSC G2 2025): **enhance liquidity, reduce settlement risk, minimise counterparty risk, market efficiency, less impact of volatility** – **not** higher operational costs → **I, II and III**.
[5:1] - **BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report):** prescribed by **SEBI (not RBI)** for the **top 1,000 listed companies** (not all); disclosures are **largely non-financial** (ESG); **replaced the BRR**; **BRSR Core** needs **reasonable assurance**.
[5:2] - **Finfluencers:** stock tips or return claims need **SEBI registration**; verified "tick mark" for broker apps with **Google**.
[5:3] - **SME IPOs** surged **87%** (FY23-25); **AP:** AP MSME Development Corporation + NSE SME IPO awareness programme in **Vijayawada**.
[5:4] - **Securities Markets Code Bill, 2025** (Lok Sabha, Dec 2025): merges the **SEBI Act 1992, SCRA 1956, Depositories Act 1996**; SEBI board up to **15**; **MIIs** get statutory status.
[5:5] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:91 sub 8 (T+0, BRSR, finfluencers, SMC Bill)
## SUB 6 | Debt Market and Infrastructure Financing
[6:0] - **REIT and InvIT Regulations** (SEBI, **2014**); **municipal bonds** – SEBI frames the issue and listing regulations.
[6:1] - **Large corporate framework (2018):** large listed companies must raise **25%** of fresh borrowing through **debt securities** (bonds).
[6:2] - SEBI **lowered** (did not raise) the **bond ticket (face value) size** to draw retail investors.
[6:3] - **RBI Retail Direct Scheme:** retail investors buy **G-Secs** directly from the **RBI** – an RBI scheme, not a SEBI-RBI one.
[6:4] > APPSC G1 2024: only the 25%-debt framework statement is valid → **Only II** (statement I's "exempted" is wrong – SEBI eased the InvIT/municipal bond rules; III and IV false).
