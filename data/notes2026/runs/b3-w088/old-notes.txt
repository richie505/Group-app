## SUB 0 | Financial Inclusion – Meaning, Objectives and Committees
[88:0:0] # Meaning
[88:0:1] - **Financial inclusion**: basic banking and financial services for **everybody, whatever their income or savings** – it can be encouraged by banking services and financial services for low-income people ("**all of the above**").
[88:0:2] - **Rangarajan definition**: ensuring **access to financial services** and **timely and adequate credit** for **vulnerable groups** (weaker sections, low-income groups) at an **affordable cost**.
[88:0:3] - **RBI view** covers consumer protection for newly included customers, easy and speedy grievance redressal and wider financial literacy – "**All of the above**".
[88:0:4] TABLE head: Objective | Fact
  [88:0:4.0] Extend financial services | Poor population
  [88:0:4.1] Expand banking infrastructure | Underserved and rural areas (not metros)
  [88:0:4.2] Credit access | Weaker sections – unlock their growth potential
  [88:0:4.3] Financial literacy | Awareness + informed use
  [88:0:4.4] Payments / remittances, insurance | Formal system; raises financial security
[88:0:5] - **Shrinking of banking infrastructure** is NOT an objective; "reducing financial security" is not an outcome.
[88:0:6] - **Hindrances**: **low income, illiteracy, lack of bank branches** – "all of the above".
[88:0:7] TABLE head: Financial inclusion | Financial exclusion
  [88:0:7.0] Bank account, credit, insurance | No / limited banking or insurance access
  [88:0:7.1] Payments and remittances | Limited payment facilities
  [88:0:7.2] Financial literacy | Lack of awareness

## SUB 1 | Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY)

[88:1:2] - Aim: **financial inclusion** – universal access to banking, **a bank account for every household** (not housing loans, SHGs, credit to businessmen or bank income).
[88:1:3] - Slogan: "**Mera Khata – Bhagya Vidhata**".
[88:1:4] - Tenets: **banking the unbanked, securing the unsecured, funding the unfunded**.

[88:1:6] - **SI 2016 statements**: launched 28 Aug 2014 ✔, accounts + insurance + debit card ✔, Ministry of Finance in charge ✔; it does **not** grant business loans → "**a, b & c**".

## SUB 4 | Microfinance and SHG-Bank Linkage
[88:4:0] # Microfinance
[88:4:1] - **Microfinance** = financial services for low-income people: **microcredit, savings, microinsurance, payments / fund transfer** → UPSC 2011 answer "**1, 2, 3 and 4**".
[88:4:2] - Modern microfinance roots: **Grameen Bank, Bangladesh (1976)** – **Muhammad Yunus**, pioneer of microfinance (Nobel Peace Prize 2006).
[88:4:3] - UN **International Year of Microcredit: 2005**.
[88:4:4] - **AP microfinance crisis (2010)** began with **SKS Microfinance** (IPO, coercive recoveries, borrower suicides) → AP MFI (Regulation of Money Lending) **Ordinance, 15 October 2010**: MFIs had to **register with a state-appointed authority**; replaced by an Act (Dec 2010).
[88:4:5] - The crisis **reshaped national regulation** → RBI **Y.H. Malegam** sub-committee (Oct 2010) → **NBFC-MFI** category created **2 December 2011**.
[88:4:6] - **MFIN**: self-regulatory national body of RBI-regulated MFIs (2009).
[88:4:7] # SHG-Bank Linkage
[88:4:8] TABLE head: Body / programme | Year | Fact
  [88:4:8.0] SEWA | 1970s | Genesis of SHGs in India
  [88:4:8.1] MYRADA | Mid-1980s | Pioneered linkage
  [88:4:8.2] NABARD SHG-Bank Linkage pilot | 1992 (1992-93) | Started by NABARD (not SBI); world's largest microfinance project
  [88:4:8.3] DAY-NRLM | 2011 | Ministry of Rural Development; rural livelihoods through SHGs
[88:4:9] - In an SHG **all members take responsibility** for a member's loan (joint liability); **RRBs, commercial and co-operative banks** lend to SHGs. UPSC 2023: only statements 2 and 3 correct → "**Only two**".
[88:4:10] - **Implementers**: commercial banks, RRBs, co-operative banks; **NABARD** promotes and refinances – keyed as the body that does **not** implement (UPPSC 2015).
[88:4:11] # Andhra Pradesh model
[88:4:12] - **DWCRA** (1982-83, sub-scheme of IRDP; AP with UNICEF) – early women's group model; **SERP** runs the SHG model.
[88:4:13] - **AP ranks 1st** in SHG-bank linkage: about **36% national share**, **99.66% recovery**.
[88:4:14] > Most asked: microfinance services 1-2-3-4 (×2), SKS and the AP crisis, Malegam, NABARD 1992. Trap: "SHG programme started by SBI".
[88:4:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 02 p.37-38 (NABARD SHG-BLP 1992, world's largest microfinance project, SEWA, microfinance services, Yunus, DWCRA 1982-83); NextGen Rocket Sheet Indian Economy p.23 (SHG-BLP 1992-93); app notes 3:88 sub 4-5
[88:4:16] - **SHGs** combine the advantages of the **traditional moneylender** (reach, flexibility, no collateral) and the **commercial bank** (low-cost formal finance).
[88:4:17] - After the **2010 AP MFI crisis**, moneylenders met about **60%** of the cut-off borrowers' credit needs (at 36-120% interest; MicroSave studies).
## SUB 5 | Digital Payments Policy, Schemes and RBI Indices
[88:5:0] # Policy and schemes
[88:5:1] - **Lucky Grahak Yojana** (consumers) and **Digi-Dhan Vyapar Yojana** (merchants) – NITI Aayog, 25 Dec 2016: cash prizes to promote digital payments; under Digi-Dhan **merchants get prizes** for digital transactions.
[88:5:2] - **MDR (Merchant Discount Rate)**: the **charge a bank levies on a merchant** for accepting card / digital payments; shared by issuing bank, acquiring bank and network. **Zero MDR** on **RuPay debit and BHIM-UPI** since **1 Jan 2020** (Sec 10A, PSS Act) – cost burden on **payment system providers**.
[88:5:3] - **RuPay-BHIM UPI incentive scheme**: **0.15%** on P2M payments up to **₹2,000** to small merchants; **NPCI** nodal agency.
[88:5:4] - **Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026**: UPI stays free for consumers; MDR may apply to **high-value** payments.
[88:5:5] - **Data localisation (RBI, April 2018)**: entire payment-system data must be **stored only in India** – no public-sector ownership or CAG audit condition → "**Only I**".
[88:5:6] - **Kerala**: first state with **digital banking in all eligible accounts** (January 2023).

## SUB 6 | UPI – Features, New Products and UPI Abroad
[88:6:0] - **UPI = Unified Payments Interface**; NPCI, launched **April 2016** with **21 banks**; instant account-to-account P2P / P2M via a VPA; runs on the **IMPS** framework (not NEFT).
[88:6:1] - Consequence (UPSC 2017): **mobile wallets are not necessary** for online payments – money moves bank-to-bank.
[88:6:2] - **BHIM** lets a user pay anyone with a UPI-enabled bank account; the "card has four factors, BHIM two" claim is wrong → "**Only I**".

=== NOTES 3:89 | Financial Markets – Money Market Instruments and Capital Market Segments
## SUB 0 | Government Securities and the Gilt-edged Market
[89:0:0] # Gilt-edged market
[89:0:1] - **Gilt-edged market** = market for **government securities** (and those **guaranteed by government**) – "of the best quality"; **no default risk**, highly liquid; **RBI** is the sole supplier.
[89:0:2] - Two parts: **Treasury Bill market** and **Government bond market** – not bullion, shares, MFs, derivatives, corporate bonds or bank CDs.
[89:0:3] - **Gilt-edged securities = Government securities**, not company shares or MF units.
[89:0:4] # Types
[89:0:5] TABLE head: Security | Issued by | Maturity
  [89:0:5.0] Treasury Bills | Central Government only, sold by RBI auction | 91, 182, 364 days
  [89:0:5.1] Dated G-Secs | Central Government | One year or more; pay coupon
  [89:0:5.2] State Development Loans | State Governments | Dated securities
  [89:0:5.3] Cash Management Bills | Centre | Under 91 days; temporary cash mismatches
[89:0:6] - **G-Sec** (Government Securities Act, 2006): tradeable instrument acknowledging debt of the **Central or a State Government**; RBI manages and services **both** Central and State securities.
[89:0:7] # Treasury Bills
[89:0:8] - Short-term **promissory notes issued by RBI on behalf of the Government** for its short-term borrowing; **no State Government T-bills**.
[89:0:9] - **Zero-coupon**: issued at a **discount**, redeemed at **face value**; first issued in India in **1917**. (14-day intermediate T-bills go only to State Govts / central banks.)
[89:0:10] - **UPSC 2018**: A false (RBI manages State securities too), B true, C true → "**B and C only**".
[89:0:11] # Market players
[89:0:12] - **Primary dealers**: **SBI DFHI = largest**. **RBI Retail Direct**: individuals via a **Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) account**.
[89:0:13] - **OMO**: RBI **sells** G-Secs → **absorbs** liquidity; buys → injects.
[89:0:14] - **FAR** (June 2026) now covers new 15-, 30-, 40-year G-Secs and **sovereign green bonds**.
[89:0:15] - **AP**: SDL borrowings of **₹73,383 crore** planned for 2026-27.
[89:0:16] > Most asked: gilt-edged = government securities (×7), T-bill tenors 91/182/364 (×2, APPSC Group 1 2019). Trap: "14 and 91 days".

## SUB 1 | Money Market vs Capital Market
[89:1:0] - Indian financial system = **money market + capital market**.
[89:1:1] TABLE head: Basis | Money market | Capital market
  [89:1:1.0] Meaning | Market for short-term funds / loans – where loans of money are obtained | Market for medium- and long-term funds – where bonds, shares and securities are bought and sold
  [89:1:1.1] Tenure | Up to 1 year (overnight to 364 days) | More than 1 year
  [89:1:1.2] Regulator | RBI | SEBI
  [89:1:1.3] Instruments | T-bills, CP, CD, call money, CBLO/TREPS, commercial bills | Equities, preference shares, debentures, bonds
  [89:1:1.4] Sub-markets | Call Money Market, Treasury Bill Market | Government Bond Market, Stock Market
  [89:1:1.5] Participants | RBI, commercial banks, FIs, MFs, corporates – individuals do not take part | Individuals and institutions
[89:1:2] - **Capital market** in the common sense = **share market** (not commodity or money market).
[89:1:3] - **UPSC 2023**: of G-bond, call money, T-bill and stock markets, only **G-bond and stock markets** are capital markets → "**Only two**".
[89:1:4] - **Difference** (money vs securities market): the former deals in **short-term debt obligations**, the latter in **long-term securities**.
[89:1:5] - **RBI's place**: NextGen lists the RBI among the **major participants** (and controlling authority) of the money market; the UPPCS 1999 key treats **RBI** as the one that is "not a participant" (it regulates) – IDBI, ICICI and MMMFs do operate in it.
[89:1:6] - Global firms (UPSC 2009): JP Morgan Chase – financial services; WL Ross and Warburg Pincus – private equity; **Roche Holding AG is pharmaceuticals**, not financial services.

[89:2:0] # Structure and instruments
[89:2:1] - Capital market: gilt-edged market; **industrial securities market** (**primary + secondary**); DFIs (IFCI, ICICI, IDBI, SIDBI, UTI); intermediaries (merchant banks, MFs, leasing, venture capital companies).

  [89:3:0.1] Controlled and coordinated by RBI | Not controlled by RBI

[89:3:4] - **Co-operative credit institutions** sit between the two.
[89:3:5] - **Call money market** = the **most volatile / unstable part** of the organised money market (repayable on demand; overnight to 14 days).

## SUB 0 | Global Financial Crisis 2007-09 – Sub-prime, Lehman, Teaser Loans
[92:0:0] - **Origin:** the **US sub-prime mortgage** market (surfaced July 2007) – loans to borrowers with poor credit records, caused by the **US housing (real-estate) bust**, **not by oil prices**.
[92:0:1] - **Chain:** 1. loans to **weak borrowers** → 2. sold on and **securitised** into **mortgage-backed securities** sold worldwide → 3. rates rise, **home-loan defaults** → 4. **failure of US banks** → 5. FPI outflows and a **crash in the Indian stock market**.
[92:0:2] - UPPSC 2010 statements: caused by oil prices ✗; home-loan defaults ✓; failure of US banks ✓; crash in Indian market ✓ → **2, 3 and 4 only**.
[92:0:3] - **Lehman Brothers** (US investment bank) filed for **Chapter 11 bankruptcy on 15 Sep 2008**; sub-prime lending was a main cause of the crisis → A and R both true, **R explains A**.
[92:0:4] - **Teaser loans:** **low fixed rate in the first years, higher later** (e.g. SBI home loans, 2008-09) – an aspect of **sub-prime lending**; banks risk **future defaults**. They were home loans, **not** loans to inexperienced entrepreneurs for factories or exports → **A only** (UPSC 2011).
[92:0:5] - **Credit Default Swap:** insurance-like derivative on bonds; seller pays on default; **counterparty risk**.
[92:0:6] - **Response in India:** FSDC set up **Dec 2010** (G-20 initiative after the crisis); shadow banking came under scrutiny worldwide.

## SUB 2 | Shadow Banking, NBFCs and the 2018 Shadow-Lender Crisis
[92:2:0] - **Shadow banking** = credit creation by **financial institutions outside the regular banking regulatory framework** (hedge funds, NBFCs; also unregulated activities of regulated entities) – they escape CRR/SLR and capital rules.

[92:2:2] - **Asset-liability mismatch:** NBFCs **borrow short** (CPs, liquid mutual funds) and **lend long** (infrastructure, housing).
[92:2:3] - **NBFCs:** registered under the **Companies Act**; **cannot take demand deposits, issue cheques or join the payment system**; deposits **not DICGC-covered**.

## SUB 3 | East Asian Crisis 1997 and the Taper Tantrum 2013
[92:3:0] - **East Asian crisis (1997):** hit **Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, South Korea**; began with the **fall of the Thai baht** (devalued 2 July 1997).
