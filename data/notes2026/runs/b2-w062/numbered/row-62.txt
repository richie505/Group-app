## SUB 1 | Appointment, Tenure, Removal and Status of the CEC and ECs
[1:0] # Appointment
[1:1] - **Art. 324(2):** the CEC and other ECs are appointed by the **President** (subject to any law of Parliament) – **not** elected by Parliament, not appointed by the PM, the Home Minister or the CJI.
[1:2] - **Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2 March 2023, Constitution Bench):** until Parliament made a law, appointment on the advice of **PM + Leader of Opposition (or leader of the largest opposition party) in Lok Sabha + CJI**; it also said it is **desirable** that **other ECs** be removable only on the grounds of an SC judge – it said nothing about **Regional Commissioners**.
[1:3] - **CEC and Other ECs (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023** (Act 49 of 2023; replaced the **1991 Act**):
[1:4] TABLE head: Stage | Composition / rule
  [1:4.0] Search Committee | Headed by the Law Minister + two Secretary-rank members; shortlists five names
  [1:4.1] Selection Committee | PM (chair), LoP / largest opposition party leader in Lok Sabha, a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM (replaces the CJI); may pick names outside the shortlist
  [1:4.2] Appointment | President, on the Selection Committee's recommendation
[1:5] - Criticism: gives the government a **2:1** majority; challenged by ADR and **Jaya Thakur (Jaya Thakur v. Union of India)** – pending as of 2026.
[1:6] # Committees that recommended a three-member ECI
[1:7] - **Dinesh Goswami Committee on Electoral Reforms (1990)** – appointed by the V.P. Singh Government, chaired by Law Minister Dinesh Goswami; first main recommendation: **"Election Commission to be a three-member body"**, CEC appointed in consultation with the **CJI and LoP**.
[1:8] - **Tarkunde Committee (1974-75)** – **unofficial**, set up by **Jayaprakash Narayan**; also proposed a multi-member Commission chosen by a PM-LoP-CJI panel. **Rajamannar** and **Sarkaria** dealt with Centre-State relations.
[1:9] # Tenure, salary and status
[1:10] TABLE head: Item | Rule
  [1:10.0] Term | 6 years or age 65, whichever is earlier (1991 Act; 2023 Act s.9 – no reappointment); may resign by writing to the President
  [1:10.1] Powers and pay | CEC and ECs have equal powers and equal salary = a Judge of the Supreme Court; differences decided by majority
  [1:10.2] Status | The CEC's status is that of a Supreme Court judge (pay, removal) – not that of a Governor or a Speaker
  [1:10.3] Service conditions | Cannot be varied to the CEC's disadvantage after appointment (Art. 324(5))
[1:11] - **Not** "5 years or 62" and **not** "equal powers but unequal salaries" (both wrong – UPSC 2002).
[1:12] # Removal (Art. 324(5))
[1:13] TABLE head: Office | How removed
  [1:13.0] CEC | Only in like manner and on like grounds as a Supreme Court judge – by the President on an address of both Houses passed by special majority on proved misbehaviour or incapacity; motion needs 100 Lok Sabha / 50 Rajya Sabha signatures (Judges Inquiry Act)
  [1:13.1] Election Commissioners and Regional Commissioners | By the President on the recommendation of the CEC – so the CEC's security of tenure is not available to them
  [1:13.2] State Election Commissioner | Like a High Court judge (Art. 243K(2))
[1:14] # Gaps the Constitution leaves
[1:15] - **No qualifications** and **no term** prescribed for members; **retiring ECs are not debarred** from further government appointment (contrast **CAG – Art. 148(4)**, **UPSC Chairman – Art. 319**). (NextGen's "ineligible for office of profit after retirement" is **not** a constitutional bar.)
[1:16] - ECI expenses are **not charged** on the Consolidated Fund (a long-standing demand); it depends on the Law, Home and DoPT Ministries.

## SUB 2 | Constitutional Status and Composition – Single-Member to Multi-Member
[2:0] # Status
[2:1] - A **permanent, independent constitutional body** set up directly by the Constitution – **Part XV ("Elections"), Articles 324-329**; basis **Art. 324**; an **all-India body common to the Centre and the States**.
[2:2] - Art. 324 is a **"reservoir of power"** (Mohinder Singh Gill, 1978) – used only where **no law holds the field**.
[2:3] - Seat **Nirvachan Sadan**, New Delhi; training arm **IIIDEM**, New Delhi.
[2:4] # Composition (Art. 324(2)-(4))
[2:5] - **CEC + such number of ECs as the President fixes**; today **CEC + 2 ECs = a three-member body** (APPSC G2 2025 – "three member body" and "constitutional body" are true; "appointed by the CJI" is false).
[2:6] - The CEC acts as **chairman**; the President may appoint **Regional Commissioners** after **consulting the Election Commission**.
[2:7] - All members have **equal powers**; decisions by **majority** – upheld in **T.N. Seshan v. Union of India (1995)**.
[2:8] # Single-member to multi-member
[2:9] TABLE head: Period | Status
  [2:9.0] 1950 – 15 Oct 1989 | Single-member – CEC only (originally one)
  [2:9.1] 16 Oct 1989 | Two ECs added – first three-member Commission (work grew after the 61st Amendment cut the voting age 21 → 18)
  [2:9.2] Jan 1990 | Posts abolished – single-member again
  [2:9.3] 1 Oct 1993 onwards | Multi-member (CEC + 2 ECs) continuously
[2:10] - So the ECI was **not** multi-member "since inception"; "made multi-member / three-member" in a 1987-1990 option set = **1989**.

## SUB 3 | Powers and Functions of the ECI
[3:0] # Three kinds – administrative, advisory, quasi-judicial
[3:1] - Superintendence, direction and control of **electoral rolls** and **conduct of elections** to **Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President** (Art. 324(1)); decides **every aspect from the announcement of elections to the declaration of results** (APPSC G1 2023).
[3:2] - **Officers on election duty work under the ECI's control**, not the government's; staff preparing rolls are deemed on **deputation** to the ECI. It can issue guidelines and order **transfers/suspension** – it does **not** order the government to **dismiss** officials.
[3:3] - Delimits constituencies on the basis of the **Delimitation Commission Acts**; prepares and revises **electoral rolls** – **Special Intensive Revision** under **Art. 324 + Section 21, RPA 1950**.
[3:4] - Notifies **dates and schedules** (the Home Ministry has no such role), scrutinises nominations, fixes **expenditure limits**, can **cancel polls** for booth capturing or rigging.
[3:5] - **Recognises parties and allots symbols** (**Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968**) and acts as a court on **splits/mergers and symbol disputes** – not the Speaker, PM or President.
[3:6] - **Model Code of Conduct** – not a statute; rests on **Art. 324**; applies **from the announcement of the schedule** (**Harbans Singh Jalal, 1997**); **Part VII** = party in power.
[3:7] - Advises the **President on MPs' disqualification (Art. 103)** – the **President decides after obtaining the ECI's opinion and acts on it** – and the **Governor on MLAs' (Art. 192)**.
[3:8] - Advises the President whether elections can be held in a State under **President's Rule** (for extension beyond one year) – it does **not recommend** President's Rule.
[3:9] # What it does NOT do
[3:10] - **Election disputes:** decided by **courts** – election petitions under **RPA 1951** go to the **High Court** (appeal to SC); President/VP disputes to the **Supreme Court (Art. 71)**. The ECI **cannot** give the final verdict on disputes and does not try petitions.
[3:11] - **Anti-defection (Tenth Schedule)** → **Speaker/Chairman**.
[3:12] - Delimitation of J&K (2022) was by a Commission under **Justice Ranjana Desai**; **Assam** was delimited by the **ECI itself** (effective Aug 2023).

## SUB 7 | Election Disputes and Polling-Day Situations
[7:0] - **Art. 329(a):** laws on delimitation and allotment of seats **cannot be questioned in court**.
[7:1] - **Art. 329(b):** an election to Parliament/State Legislature can be challenged **only by an election petition** under **RPA 1951** – tried by the **High Court** (original jurisdiction), appeal to the **Supreme Court**.
[7:2] - **Art. 71:** President/VP election disputes → **Supreme Court**. The ECI **cannot review a declared result**.
[7:3] TABLE head: RPA 1951 section | Situation | Action
  [7:3.0] 57 | Riot, open violence, natural calamity | Presiding or Returning Officer adjourns; resumes with ECI approval; only those who had not voted may vote
  [7:3.1] 58 | EVM/ballot box unlawfully taken, destroyed or tampered | ECI declares the poll void, fixes a fresh poll
  [7:3.2] 58A | Booth capturing | Adjournment or countermand
