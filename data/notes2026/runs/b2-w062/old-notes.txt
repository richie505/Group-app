=== NOTES 2:62 | Election Commission of India – Composition, Appointment (2023 Act), Powers and Functions
## SUB 0 | Appointment, Tenure, Removal and Status of the CEC and ECs
[62:0:0] # Appointment
[62:0:1] - **Art. 324(2):** the CEC and other ECs are appointed by the **President** (subject to any law of Parliament) – **not** elected by Parliament, not appointed by the PM, the Home Minister or the CJI.
[62:0:2] - **Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2 March 2023, Constitution Bench):** until Parliament made a law, appointment on the advice of **PM + Leader of Opposition (or leader of the largest opposition party) in Lok Sabha + CJI**; it also said it is **desirable** that **other ECs** be removable only on the grounds of an SC judge – it said nothing about **Regional Commissioners**.
[62:0:3] - **CEC and Other ECs (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023** (Act 49 of 2023; replaced the **1991 Act**):
[62:0:4] TABLE head: Stage | Composition / rule
  [62:0:4.0] Search Committee | Headed by the Law Minister + two Secretary-rank members; shortlists five names
  [62:0:4.1] Selection Committee | PM (chair), LoP / largest opposition party leader in Lok Sabha, a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM (replaces the CJI); may pick names outside the shortlist
  [62:0:4.2] Appointment | President, on the Selection Committee's recommendation
[62:0:5] - Criticism: gives the government a **2:1** majority; challenged by ADR and **Jaya Thakur (Jaya Thakur v. Union of India)** – pending as of 2026.
[62:0:6] # Committees that recommended a three-member ECI
[62:0:7] - **Dinesh Goswami Committee on Electoral Reforms (1990)** – appointed by the V.P. Singh Government, chaired by Law Minister Dinesh Goswami; first main recommendation: **"Election Commission to be a three-member body"**, CEC appointed in consultation with the **CJI and LoP**.
[62:0:8] - **Tarkunde Committee (1974-75)** – **unofficial**, set up by **Jayaprakash Narayan**; also proposed a multi-member Commission chosen by a PM-LoP-CJI panel. **Rajamannar** and **Sarkaria** dealt with Centre-State relations.
[62:0:9] # Tenure, salary and status
[62:0:10] TABLE head: Item | Rule
  [62:0:10.0] Term | 6 years or age 65, whichever is earlier (1991 Act; 2023 Act s.9 – no reappointment); may resign by writing to the President
  [62:0:10.1] Powers and pay | CEC and ECs have equal powers and equal salary = a Judge of the Supreme Court; differences decided by majority
  [62:0:10.2] Status | The CEC's status is that of a Supreme Court judge (pay, removal) – not that of a Governor or a Speaker
  [62:0:10.3] Service conditions | Cannot be varied to the CEC's disadvantage after appointment (Art. 324(5))
[62:0:11] - **Not** "5 years or 62" and **not** "equal powers but unequal salaries" (both wrong – UPSC 2002).
[62:0:12] # Removal (Art. 324(5))
[62:0:13] TABLE head: Office | How removed
  [62:0:13.0] CEC | Only in like manner and on like grounds as a Supreme Court judge – by the President on an address of both Houses passed by special majority on proved misbehaviour or incapacity; motion needs 100 Lok Sabha / 50 Rajya Sabha signatures (Judges Inquiry Act)
  [62:0:13.1] Election Commissioners and Regional Commissioners | By the President on the recommendation of the CEC – so the CEC's security of tenure is not available to them
  [62:0:13.2] State Election Commissioner | Like a High Court judge (Art. 243K(2))
[62:0:14] # Gaps the Constitution leaves
[62:0:15] - **No qualifications** and **no term** prescribed for members; **retiring ECs are not debarred** from further government appointment (contrast **CAG – Art. 148(4)**, **UPSC Chairman – Art. 319**). (NextGen's "ineligible for office of profit after retirement" is **not** a constitutional bar.)
[62:0:16] - ECI expenses are **not charged** on the Consolidated Fund (a long-standing demand); it depends on the Law, Home and DoPT Ministries.

## SUB 2 | Constitutional Status and Composition – Single-Member to Multi-Member
[62:2:0] # Status
[62:2:1] - A **permanent, independent constitutional body** set up directly by the Constitution – **Part XV ("Elections"), Articles 324-329**; basis **Art. 324**; an **all-India body common to the Centre and the States**.
[62:2:2] - Art. 324 is a **"reservoir of power"** (Mohinder Singh Gill, 1978) – used only where **no law holds the field**.
[62:2:3] - Seat **Nirvachan Sadan**, New Delhi; training arm **IIIDEM**, New Delhi.
[62:2:4] # Composition (Art. 324(2)-(4))
[62:2:5] - **CEC + such number of ECs as the President fixes**; today **CEC + 2 ECs = a three-member body** (APPSC G2 2025 – "three member body" and "constitutional body" are true; "appointed by the CJI" is false).
[62:2:6] - The CEC acts as **chairman**; the President may appoint **Regional Commissioners** after **consulting the Election Commission**.
[62:2:7] - All members have **equal powers**; decisions by **majority** – upheld in **T.N. Seshan v. Union of India (1995)**.
[62:2:8] # Single-member to multi-member
[62:2:9] TABLE head: Period | Status
  [62:2:9.0] 1950 – 15 Oct 1989 | Single-member – CEC only (originally one)
  [62:2:9.1] 16 Oct 1989 | Two ECs added – first three-member Commission (work grew after the 61st Amendment cut the voting age 21 → 18)
  [62:2:9.2] Jan 1990 | Posts abolished – single-member again
  [62:2:9.3] 1 Oct 1993 onwards | Multi-member (CEC + 2 ECs) continuously
[62:2:10] - So the ECI was **not** multi-member "since inception"; "made multi-member / three-member" in a 1987-1990 option set = **1989**.

## SUB 3 | Powers and Functions of the ECI
[62:3:0] # Three kinds – administrative, advisory, quasi-judicial
[62:3:1] - Superintendence, direction and control of **electoral rolls** and **conduct of elections** to **Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President** (Art. 324(1)); decides **every aspect from the announcement of elections to the declaration of results** (APPSC G1 2023).
[62:3:2] - **Officers on election duty work under the ECI's control**, not the government's; staff preparing rolls are deemed on **deputation** to the ECI. It can issue guidelines and order **transfers/suspension** – it does **not** order the government to **dismiss** officials.
[62:3:3] - Delimits constituencies on the basis of the **Delimitation Commission Acts**; prepares and revises **electoral rolls** – **Special Intensive Revision** under **Art. 324 + Section 21, RPA 1950**.
[62:3:4] - Notifies **dates and schedules** (the Home Ministry has no such role), scrutinises nominations, fixes **expenditure limits**, can **cancel polls** for booth capturing or rigging.
[62:3:5] - **Recognises parties and allots symbols** (**Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968**) and acts as a court on **splits/mergers and symbol disputes** – not the Speaker, PM or President.
[62:3:6] - **Model Code of Conduct** – not a statute; rests on **Art. 324**; applies **from the announcement of the schedule** (**Harbans Singh Jalal, 1997**); **Part VII** = party in power.
[62:3:7] - Advises the **President on MPs' disqualification (Art. 103)** – the **President decides after obtaining the ECI's opinion and acts on it** – and the **Governor on MLAs' (Art. 192)**.
[62:3:8] - Advises the President whether elections can be held in a State under **President's Rule** (for extension beyond one year) – it does **not recommend** President's Rule.
[62:3:9] # What it does NOT do
[62:3:10] - **Election disputes:** decided by **courts** – election petitions under **RPA 1951** go to the **High Court** (appeal to SC); President/VP disputes to the **Supreme Court (Art. 71)**. The ECI **cannot** give the final verdict on disputes and does not try petitions.
[62:3:11] - **Anti-defection (Tenth Schedule)** → **Speaker/Chairman**.
[62:3:12] - Delimitation of J&K (2022) was by a Commission under **Justice Ranjana Desai**; **Assam** was delimited by the **ECI itself** (effective Aug 2023).

## SUB 7 | Election Disputes and Polling-Day Situations
[62:7:0] - **Art. 329(a):** laws on delimitation and allotment of seats **cannot be questioned in court**.
[62:7:1] - **Art. 329(b):** an election to Parliament/State Legislature can be challenged **only by an election petition** under **RPA 1951** – tried by the **High Court** (original jurisdiction), appeal to the **Supreme Court**.
[62:7:2] - **Art. 71:** President/VP election disputes → **Supreme Court**. The ECI **cannot review a declared result**.
[62:7:3] TABLE head: RPA 1951 section | Situation | Action
  [62:7:3.0] 57 | Riot, open violence, natural calamity | Presiding or Returning Officer adjourns; resumes with ECI approval; only those who had not voted may vote
  [62:7:3.1] 58 | EVM/ballot box unlawfully taken, destroyed or tampered | ECI declares the poll void, fixes a fresh poll
  [62:7:3.2] 58A | Booth capturing | Adjournment or countermand

## SUB 0 | Part XV and the Right to Vote – Articles 324-329, Adult Suffrage
[63:0:0] # Part XV – Elections (Articles 324-329)
[63:0:1] TABLE head: Article | Content
  [63:0:1.0] 324 | Election Commission: superintendence, direction and control of electoral rolls and of all elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President; 324(2): CEC + other ECs as the President fixes
  [63:0:1.1] 325 | One general electoral roll for every territorial constituency; no one excluded on grounds only of religion, race, caste or sex
  [63:0:1.2] 326 | Elections to the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies on the basis of adult suffrage (universal franchise)
  [63:0:1.3] 327 / 328 | Parliament / State Legislature may make laws on elections
  [63:0:1.4] 329 | Bar on courts: delimitation laws cannot be questioned; an election can be challenged only by an election petition
[63:0:2] - Elections are in **Part XV** (not XIV, XVI or XVII). Correct pair: **ECI – Art. 324** (Supreme Court = 124, UPSC = 315, Attorney General = 76); Art. 280 = Finance Commission; **Art. 171** = Legislative Councils, not elections.
[63:0:3] - ECI set up on **25 January 1950** – permanent constitutional body; its institute **IIIDEM** trains election managers. **Mohinder Singh Gill v CEC (1978)**: Art. 324 is a "**reservoir of power**" where Parliament has not legislated.
[63:0:4] - **ECI** conducts Lok Sabha, Assembly, Rajya Sabha, Council, **President and Vice-President** polls and prepares **electoral rolls / registers voters**; **State Election Commissions** (Arts. **243K, 243ZA**) run panchayat and municipal polls.
[63:0:5] - Qualifications/disqualifications: **Art. 84 / 102** (Parliament), **173 / 191** (State Legislatures). Simultaneous polls would need amending **Arts. 83** (duration of Houses), **85, 172, 174, 356**.
[63:0:6] # Adult suffrage
[63:0:7] - **Art. 326**: every citizen aged **18+** may vote unless disqualified by law for **non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime, corrupt or illegal practice** – universal franchise "subject to limitations by statute".
[63:0:8] - Voting age **21 → 18**: **61st Amendment Act, 1988** (in force **28 March 1989**); 18-year-olds first voted in the **1989** Lok Sabha election (not 59th, 60th, 63rd, 69th or 73rd).
[63:0:9] - Principles for Lok Sabha/Assembly elections (UPPSC 2011): adult suffrage, **one roll per territorial constituency**, no exclusion on religion, race, sex (Arts. 325-326); the UPPSC key also accepted "parties free to have their own norms" (All four).
[63:0:10] - Democracy needs **political parties, an electorate, an independent judiciary and universal adult suffrage** (UPSC 1994: all four).
[63:0:11] - Right to vote and to contest = **statutory rights** (**Kuldip Nayar v UoI, 2006**), not Fundamental Rights; with the right to petition they are **political rights** – the **right to life** (Art. 21) is a civil right.
[63:0:12] - **Who cannot vote:** a person **confined in prison** (Sec. 62(5) RPA 1951); **preventive detenus** vote by **postal ballot**. **Overseas electors** (Sec. **20A** RPA 1950, 2010): Indian **citizen**, enrolled at the **passport address**, must vote **in person** in India.

## SUB 3 | Delimitation – Commission, Freeze and Amendments
[63:3:0] # Meaning and basis
[63:3:1] - **Delimitation** = fixing the **number and boundaries** of Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies, and the **SC/ST reserved seats**, on the latest **census** – "**one person, one vote, one value**".
[63:3:2] - The body that decides the number and size of constituencies = **Delimitation Commission** (not the ECI, NITI Aayog or MHA); purpose = revising **constituency** boundaries, **not State boundaries** (Parliament, Art. 3).
[63:3:3] - Basis: **Art. 82** (Lok Sabha), **Art. 170** (Assemblies) – readjustment after each census; seats go to States on **population, not area**.
[63:3:4] # Delimitation Commission

[63:3:7] - Orders have the **force of law**: **cannot be questioned in any court** and **cannot be modified** when laid before the Lok Sabha/Assembly (UPSC 2012: both correct).

## SUB 4 | Electoral Systems and Types of Elections
[63:4:0] # Who is elected how
[63:4:1] TABLE head: Office / House | Electorate | Method
  [63:4:1.0] Lok Sabha / Legislative Assembly | Citizens directly (adult suffrage) | First past the post, single-member territorial constituencies
  [63:4:1.1] Rajya Sabha | Elected MLAs; seats allotted to States by population | PR – single transferable vote
  [63:4:1.2] President | Elected MPs + elected MLAs (incl. Delhi, Puducherry – 70th Amendment) | PR-STV

  [63:4:1.4] Legislative Council | MLAs, local bodies, graduates, teachers + Governor's nominees | PR-STV
[63:4:2] - India uses **direct election on adult suffrage** and **PR by single transferable vote**; the **list system** and **cumulative vote** are not used.
[63:4:3] # First past the post vs proportional representation
[63:4:4] - **FPTP** (plurality): the candidate with **more votes than any other** wins – no 50% needed. Vote-seat distortion, e.g. **Karnataka 1994**: Janata Dal 116 seats on 36% votes.
[63:4:5] - **PR** gives seats in proportion to votes → **representation of minorities**; not needed where a **two-party system** has developed (UK). Rejected for the Lok Sabha: complex, fragmentation, **unstable governments**, no by-elections.
[63:4:6] # Types of elections and special constituencies

  [63:4:7.2] By-election | A single vacancy; within 6 months (Sec. 151A) – not if the remaining term is under one year
[63:4:8] - **Floating constituency**: no fixed geographical boundary – **Sangha (Sikkim)**: only registered **Buddhist monks** vote and contest (both statements correct).
[63:4:9] - **Security deposit** is forfeited if a candidate does not get **more than one-sixth** of the valid votes (Sec. 158).

## SUB 6 | EVM-VVPAT, NOTA and Indelible Ink
[63:6:0] # EVM and VVPAT
[63:6:1] - EVM = electronic vote-recording and counting device (not an electoral system). **First used: Parur (Kerala), 1982**; older EVMs recorded up to **3,840** votes – current ECI EVMs up to **2,000**.
[63:6:2] - **VVPAT first used: Noksen Assembly by-poll, Nagaland, 4 September 2013** (not LS 2014, Goa 2017).
[63:6:3] - **N. Chandrababu Naidu v UoI (2019)**: VVPAT slips tallied in **5 polling stations per Assembly segment**. **ADR v ECI (2024)**: SC rejected **100% VVPAT** counting; 2nd/3rd-placed candidates may seek checks of 5% of EVMs.
[63:6:4] # NOTA
[63:6:5] - **PUCL v Union of India, 27 September 2013** – SC ordered a NOTA button on all EVMs (secret negative vote, **Art. 19(1)(a)**). NOTA introduced in **2013** – first used in the **Nov-Dec 2013 Assembly polls**; first Lok Sabha use **2014 = 16th Lok Sabha** (1.1% votes). The 12th-15th Lok Sabha polls had no NOTA, and it was not used in 2009.
[63:6:6] - Symbol: ballot paper with a **black cross**, designed by **NID Ahmedabad**. NOTA votes are **not counted as valid votes** for result/deposit; even if NOTA tops, the leading candidate **wins** – no fresh poll; using NOTA is **voting, not abstention**.
[63:6:7] - **Not** for **Rajya Sabha / Legislative Councils** (SC struck it down, **2018**) – only direct elections.
[63:6:8] - Old **Rule 49-O**: voter refused to vote before the Presiding Officer, noted in **Form 17-A** – broke secrecy.

[64:1:4] # VVPAT
[64:1:5] - The printed slip (serial no., name, symbol) is visible through a window for **7 seconds** (not 30), then is cut and drops into a **sealed box** – it is **never handed to the voter**; it gives the voter instant feedback that the vote went to the chosen candidate.
[64:1:6] - **Subramanian Swamy v ECI (2013):** VVPAT "an indispensable requirement of free and fair elections", to be brought in **in phases**.
[64:1:7] - **N. Chandrababu Naidu (2019):** VVPAT-EVM tally raised from **1 to 5 polling stations** per Assembly segment.
[64:1:8] - **ADR v ECI (April 2024):** no 100% slip count and **no return to paper ballots**; **symbol-loading units sealed 45 days**; 2nd/3rd-placed candidates may seek a check of **5% of EVMs** within 7 days.
[64:1:9] - Slips are counted wherever EVM data and **Form 17C** do not match. Counting order: **postal ballots → penultimate EVM round → final round**.
[64:1:10] - **Remote EVM (RVM)** prototype (Jan 2023): lets **domestic migrants** vote for up to **72 constituencies** from one remote polling station. **Totaliser** (Law Commission 255th): pools several machines to **hide booth-wise trends**.
[64:1:11] # NOTA and other reforms
[64:1:12] - **NOTA** = a voter's choice to **reject all the contesting candidates** while keeping the ballot secret – **PUCL v UoI, 27 Sep 2013** (Art. 19(1)(a)); introduced on EVMs in **2013** (Assembly polls of **Chhattisgarh, Mizoram, Rajasthan, MP, Delhi**); first Lok Sabha use **2014**. Even if NOTA tops the poll, the candidate with the most votes wins.

[64:2:1] - **Deposit forfeited** if a candidate fails to secure **more than one-sixth (1/6) of the valid votes polled**; so if even the **winner** loses his deposit, **a very large number of candidates** contested and votes were split.
[64:2:2] - **Abhiram Singh v C.D. Commachen (Jan 2017):** a 7-judge bench read **Sec. 123(3)** widely – votes cannot be sought in the name of religion, race, caste, community or language (of candidate or voter).
[64:2:3] - **Two-seat cap:** in **1991 Devi Lal** contested **three** Lok Sabha seats; the **RP (Amendment) Act, 1996** capped it at **two** (Sec. 33(7)). No rule makes a party or candidate bear by-poll costs – that is only an ECI/Law Commission ("one candidate, one seat") proposal.

## SUB 4 | Recognition, Symbols and Legal Framework
[65:4:0] - **Registration:** by the **ECI** under **Sec. 29A, RPA 1951** – apply **within 30 days** of formation; pledge allegiance to the Constitution and to **socialism, secularism and democracy**, and India's sovereignty, unity and integrity.
[65:4:1] - **Recognition** (national/State) under the **Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968** – an ECI order, **not a constitutional provision**; based on poll performance, **not** on the number of States contested.
[65:4:2] - **National party** (any one): 6% votes in 4+ States + 4 Lok Sabha seats; or 2% of Lok Sabha seats from 3 States; or State party in 4 States.
[65:4:3] - **State party** routes include 6% votes + 2 Assembly seats; 6% + 1 Lok Sabha seat; 3% of Assembly seats or 3 seats; 1 Lok Sabha seat per 25; **8% of votes alone** (not 10%).
[65:4:4] - **National parties (2025): six** – **BJP, INC, BSP, CPI(M), AAP, NPP** (AAP got status in 2023; CPI, NCP, AITC lost it). **NPP** = first national party from the **North-East**.
[65:4:5] - **Symbols:** recognised parties → **reserved symbols**; RUPPs and independents → **free symbols**. **BSP – elephant** (the elephant is also the US **Republican** Party's symbol). **Bicycle**: TDP, SP, J&K Panthers Party; **RJD – hurricane lantern**.
[65:4:6] - **Split disputes** over name and symbol are decided by the **ECI** (para 15, Symbols Order) – **not the High Court**.
[65:4:7] - **Parties in the Constitution:** not in the original text; entered via the **Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985)**. Art. 19(1)(c) protects forming **associations** in general.
[65:4:8] - **INC v Institute of Social Welfare (2002):** the ECI **cannot ordinarily deregister** a party.
[65:4:9] - **Funding:** donors above **₹20,000** disclosed yearly; donations above **₹2,000** only by cheque/bank transfer; **ADR v UoI (15 Feb 2024)** struck down electoral bonds.
