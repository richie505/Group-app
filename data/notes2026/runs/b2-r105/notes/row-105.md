=== Founders and Classical Theorists
# Founders of sociology
| Thinker | Key ideas (exam answer words) |
|---|---|
| **Auguste Comte** (1798–1857) | **Father of Sociology**; coined "sociology" (1838) after first calling it **"social physics"**; **positivism**; motto **"Order and Progress"**; sociology an **encyclopaedic** (synthetic) science |
| **Herbert Spencer** | **Social evolution** (Social Statics, 1851); society like a **biological organism**; "survival of the fittest" |
| **Emile Durkheim** | **Father of Modern Sociology**; **social facts**, **collective conscience**, **anomie**, **sacred-profane**; religion builds **solidarity** |
| **Karl Marx** | **Scientific socialism**; **historical materialism**; **surplus value**; **alienation**; **class struggle**; class fixed by **relations of production**; **Asiatic mode of production** (India) |
| **Max Weber** | **Verstehen**; **ideal type**; **class, status and power**; 3 authorities; 4 social actions; bureaucracy; *Protestant Ethic*, *The Religion of India* |
| **C. Wright Mills** | *The Sociological Imagination* (1959); **power elite** (political, military, corporate) |
| **Georg Simmel** | formal sociology; society exists because **individuals interact**; studied **social interaction and city life**; **dyad = 2**, **triad = 3** |
| **Manuel Castells** | **network society** (1996) |
- **Comte's three stages:** **theological** (rain = God's will), **metaphysical** (abstract forces of nature), **positive** (rain explained by atmospheric processes); positivism rests on **observation, experiment, evidence**, never on divine forces.
- **Durkheim:** **social facts** = laws, customs, religion, traditions existing **outside** the individual and shaping behaviour; **anomie** = normlessness from rapid change (crime, suicide); books *The Division of Labour in Society*, *The Rules of Sociological Method*, *Suicide* ("Collective Conscience" is a concept, not a book).
- **Marx:** **historical materialism** = the economic base shapes society, politics, culture; **surplus value** = value workers produce beyond their wages; founder of the **conflict perspective**.
  - Alienation from the **product**, from the **production process** (forced, monotonous work), from **fellow humans**, from **oneself** (one's human nature).
  - **Communist Manifesto (1848)** – written **jointly by Marx and Engels**; central theme = **class struggle**; *Das Kapital* 1867.
  - **Religion:** "the **opium (intoxicant)** of the people", "the **heart of a heartless world**" – Marx (1844).
  - **Asiatic mode of production:** Marx's label for India – village communities, no private landed property, despotic state (not Weber).
- **Weber's authority:** **traditional** (custom, sanctity of tradition – a tribal chief), **charismatic** (personality), **legal-rational** (rules). Bureaucracy: hierarchy, **impersonality**, written rules, **merit** appointment.
- **Weber's social action:** **instrumentally rational** (goals), **value-rational** (values, ethics), **affective** (emotions), **traditional** (customs).
- **Mills' sociological imagination:** link **biography** (one's own life and troubles) with **history**; one person's unemployment (trouble) ↔ **economic slowdown** (public issue).
- **Malinowski** (functionalist): **strong personal attachments and death** are the main sources of religious belief.

=== Basic Concepts – Society, Community, Culture, Groups and Roles
# Society and community
- **Main purpose of the sociologist:** to **study society** scientifically; sociology = **the scientific study of society**.
- **Human society is unique** because it rests on **culture**; culture (not language alone) separates human from animal society.
- **Community (MacIver):** a group sharing "the basic conditions of a common life"; its **bases = locality (territory) + community sentiment (we-feeling, solidarity)**.
- **Coercion:** a person or group **forcibly imposes its opinion/will** on another.
- **Institutionalisation:** the process by which **conventions and rules develop** and become stable in social forms.
- **Reference group:** the group whose standards we **compare ourselves with** and emulate, without belonging to it.
- **Social system:** social structure + culture + personality (Parsons).
# Culture
- **Language** is a product of **culture**; **language, literature, art** = **non-material culture** (tools, buildings = material).
- **Not an essential component of culture:** the **economy** (tradition, language, customs are).
- **Cultural lag:** non-material culture trails material change (Ogburn).
# Beliefs, values, roles
- "**Men are stronger than women**" = a **belief** (a proposition held true; "trust" in translation), not a value, oath or approval.
- **Independently defined role:** **painter**; father, teacher, officer need a counter-role.
- **Sex vs gender:** sex = **biological** difference; gender = **social** role assigned to each sex.
# Measuring attitudes
- **Social distance scale:** **Emory Bogardus** (1925).
- **Equal-appearing interval scale:** **L.L. Thurstone** (1928); Guttman = cumulative scale.

=== Social Psychology and Sociology of Education
# Education and society
- **Educational sociology** = **application of the principles of sociology to education** (F.J. Brown); sociology of education = sociological analysis of education as an institution.
- **Education as a result of social change:** social change creates a **demand for education** (new knowledge and skills).
- **Pragmatism** (Dewey): ideas without action are meaningless; truth verified by **experiment**; useful knowledge only. "Develop the individual and society develops automatically" is **not** pragmatist.
- **Humanistic realism:** classics studied to **cultivate the intellect** and make man rational and practical.
- **Character formation:** best through **good character models** (example), not sermons.
- **J. Krishnamurti:** knowledge is **scientific, collective, individual**.
# Psychology terms
- **Personality** (APPSC key): the **behaviour** of the individual (sum of behaviour patterns); Allport stresses inner systems.
- **Leadership:** ability to **influence others' attitudes or behaviour** in a desired way.
- **Role play:** best method to **re-examine one's own and others' views, feelings and behaviour**.
- **Rorschach inkblot test** (1921): detects **thought disorder**.
- *Theories of Motivation* (1959): **K.B. Madsen**; Maslow – *A Theory of Human Motivation* (1943).
- **Life stages:** **adolescence** = rapid change, identity search ("storm and stress"); relative **social stability** comes in **adulthood**.

=== Unity in Diversity and National Integration
# Diversity
- **Salient features:** **multi-ethnic, multi-lingual, multi-class, broadly patriarchal**; matrilineal exceptions **Khasi, Jaintia, Garo**.
- **Structural features (UPPSC 2014):** excess village population, **various religions, various castes**; **poverty** is an economic condition, not a feature.
- **Languages:** about **122** major languages (Census 2011) vs **22** in the **Eighth Schedule**; **4** language families.
- **Religions:** 6 major religions; Hindus a minority in **8** States/UTs.
  - **Dakhma** (Tower of Silence) = **Parsi/Zoroastrian** exposure of the dead to vultures.
  - **Sallekhana/Santhara** = **Jain** fast unto death; **Rajasthan HC** banned it (2015), SC stayed the ruling.
  - **Durkheim:** religion = beliefs and practices about **sacred things**; it strengthens **social solidarity**.
- **Festivals:** **Pongal** = harvest festival of **Tamil Nadu** (Sankranti in AP).
- **Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Manav Sangrahalaya** (Museum of Mankind, story of human evolution) – **Bhopal**.
- **International Mother Language Day: 21 February** (UNESCO, 1999).
# Bonds of unity
- **Shankaracharya** sent priests from southern temples to the north and vice versa; Onam; Bhakti-Sufi blending. Linguistic homogeneity is **not** a bond.
- **Linguistic reorganisation** = a **"safety valve"** accommodating linguistic aspirations, making India a **"State-Nation"**; **Andhra (1953)** began it, the first linguistic State.
# National integration
- **Dimensions:** **political, economic, social, cultural, psychological** – inter-related.
- **National Integration Council:** constituted **1961** (after the National Integration Conference, chaired by **Nehru**/PM; first met **1962**) to fight **communalism, casteism, regionalism**.
- **Emotional Integration Committee (1961):** chaired by **Dr Sampurnanand**.
- **Ek Bharat Shreshtha Bharat (2015):** pairs States/UTs to promote **national integration**.
- **Art. 350-B:** Special Officer for **Linguistic Minorities** (not a Finance Commission).
- **Social stability:** society keeps **peace, order, harmony, continuity and effective functioning of institutions** despite change; key feature **inclusive development**; promoted by **family** (primary agent of socialisation), education, **welfare and social justice**, tolerance; threatened by **regionalism**, poverty, extremism. Tolerance does not threaten it.

=== Continuity and Change
# Forces of change
| Force | Effect |
|---|---|
| Education | individualism, rights; questions joint-family customs |
| Urbanisation | nuclear households; family a **consumption unit**; more **individualism, competition and social distance** |
| Law | **Caste Disabilities Removal Act 1850** (no loss of inheritance on conversion); Art. 17; Hindu Code; **RTE Act 2009** (in force 2010, Art. **21A**) |
| Technology | digital citizenship; **DPDP Act 2023** |
- **Nuclear households** do not reject the **joint-family ideal**; continuity survives structural change.
- **A.M. Shah:** the joint family **changes in structure but continues in function**.
- **Harappan continuity** is limited: the **script is undeciphered**.
- **Feb 2026:** a Mother Language Day report proposed a **National Mission for Mother-Tongue-Based Multilingual Education**.
# Terms of change
| Term | Originator | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Great / Little Tradition | **Robert Redfield**; applied to India by **Milton Singer** (*When a Great Tradition Modernizes*, Madras) and **McKim Marriott** | elite-Sanskritic vs local-folk |
| Universalisation | **McKim Marriott** (*Village India*, 1955) | little → great (upward) |
| Parochialisation | McKim Marriott | great → localised (downward) |
| Cultural lag | **W.F. Ogburn** (*Social Change*, 1922) | non-material lags behind material |
| Orthogenetic / heterogenetic | **Yogendra Singh** | internal / external sources of change |
