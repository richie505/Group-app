=== NOTES 2:105 | Indian Society – Salient Features, Unity in Diversity, Continuity and Change
## SUB 0 | Salient Features
[105:0:0] - **Multi-ethnic, multi-lingual, multi-class, patriarchal**.
[105:0:1] - Matrilineal exceptions: **Khasi, Jaintia, Garo**.
[105:0:2] - **Caste** = birth-based, closed, **cumulative inequality**; **class** = open, mobile, **dispersed inequality** (**Beteille**).
[105:0:3] - Nuclear households do not reject the joint-family ideal.
[105:0:4] - Structural features: village population, religions, castes; **poverty** is not one.
[105:0:5] - **Religion** = beliefs and practices about sacred things (**Durkheim**); builds **solidarity**.
[105:0:6] - **Dakhma** (Tower of Silence) = **Parsi/Zoroastrian** exposure of the dead to vultures; **Sallekhana/Santhara** = Jain fast unto death (SC stayed Rajasthan HC's 2015 ban).
[105:0:7] - Hindus a minority in **8** States/UTs; 6 major religions.
## SUB 1 | Unity in Diversity and National Integration
[105:1:0] - Languages: about **122** (Census 2011) vs **22** in the Constitution; **4** language families.
[105:1:1] - Bonds: **Shankaracharya** swapped priests between southern and northern temples; **Onam**; Bhakti-Sufi blending, Khanqahs.
[105:1:2] - Threats: communalism, casteism, **regionalism**, linguism.
[105:1:3] - Remedies: **National Integration Council** (1962, chaired by PM), SRA 1956, Zonal Councils, **Art. 350-B** (Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities).
[105:1:4] - **Andhra (1953)** = first linguistic State; reorganisation a **"safety valve"** making India a **"State-Nation"**.
[105:1:5] - Integration: political, economic, social, cultural, psychological dimensions.
[105:1:6] - **Social stability**: order and continuity despite change; features: cohesion, political stability, **inclusive development**; promoted by family, education, welfare, tolerance; threatened by poverty, extremism.
[105:1:7] - **Family and stability:** the family is the **primary agent of socialisation**; by teaching values and norms it promotes social stability.
[105:1:8] - **Social stability (meaning):** society keeps **peace, order, harmony, continuity** and the **effective functioning of its institutions** despite social change.
## SUB 2 | Indology and Indian Sociologists
[105:2:0] - **Indological approach**: texts (Smritis, Puranas, epics), historical, continuity, cultural interpretation; surveys = field-view.

  [105:2:1.1] M.N. Srinivas | Father of Modern Indian Sociology; Sanskritisation, Westernisation, dominant caste; joint family modified; *Coorgs*

[105:2:3] - **Andre Beteille:** wrote ***Caste, Class and Power*** (1965), a study of Sripuram village in Tamil Nadu.
[105:2:4] - **A.R. Desai and D.P. Mukerji:** Desai traced Indian **nationalism** to **socio-economic changes under colonial rule**; D.P. Mukerji held that **tradition** is essential for understanding Indian society.
## SUB 3 | Continuity and Change
[105:3:0] TABLE head: Force | Effect
  [105:3:0.0] Education | individualism, rights; questions joint-family customs

[105:3:3] - **Urbanisation and social life:** city (metropolitan) life increases **individualism**, **competition** and **social distance** between people.

## SUB 5 | Founders of Sociology
[105:5:0] TABLE head: Thinker | Key ideas
  [105:5:0.0] Auguste Comte (1798–1857) | Father of Sociology; coined the word; positivism; "Order and Progress"; Three Stages: theological (God's will), metaphysical (nature's force), positive
  [105:5:0.1] Emile Durkheim | Father of Modern Sociology; social facts, collective conscience, anomie, sacred-profane
  [105:5:0.2] Karl Marx | Scientific socialism; historical materialism; surplus value; alienation; *Communist Manifesto* 1848 (with Engels), *Das Kapital* 1867
  [105:5:0.3] Max Weber | Verstehen; ideal type; bureaucracy (hierarchy, specialisation, impersonality, merit); traditional/charismatic/legal authority; 4 social actions; *Protestant Ethic*, *Religion of India*
  [105:5:0.4] C. Wright Mills | *Sociological Imagination* (1959): trouble ↔ issue; power elite (political, military, corporate)
  [105:5:0.5] Georg Simmel | formal sociology; dyad, triad
  [105:5:0.6] Manuel Castells | network society (1996)
[105:5:1] - **Malinowski** (functionalist): religion springs from **personal attachments and death**, helping people cope with crises; Durkheim: religion = society's sacred expression.
[105:5:2] - **Georg Simmel:** German sociologist who studied **social interaction and city life**; for him society exists because **individuals interact**. In his formal sociology a **dyad** is a group of **two** and a **triad** a group of **three**.
[105:5:3] - **Max Weber (1864–1920):** Father of **Interpretative Sociology**; **Verstehen** = interpretive understanding of social action; **class, status and power** as dimensions of stratification. Four social actions: **instrumentally rational** (goals), **value-rational** (values and ethics), **affective** (emotions), **traditional** (customs and traditions).
[105:5:4] - **Weber's authority and bureaucracy:** **traditional** authority rests on custom; **charismatic** on personality and leadership; **legal-rational** on rules. An **ideal type** is an analytical model to understand social reality. In bureaucracy, official conduct follows **written rules**, and the **merit system** bases appointment and promotion on qualifications.
[105:5:5] - **Marx's key ideas:** **historical materialism** – the economic base shapes society, politics and culture; **surplus value** – the value workers produce beyond the wages they get; Marx is a founder of the **conflict perspective** (class struggle).
[105:5:6] - **Marx's alienation:** from the **product** (goods are owned and sold by the capitalist), from the **production process** (forced, monotonous work), from **fellow humans** (who become competitors) and from **oneself** (one's own human nature).
[105:5:7] - **Comte's positivism:** knowledge must rest on **observation, experimentation and scientific evidence**, not on divine or supernatural forces. In the **positive** stage, rain is explained by atmospheric processes.
[105:5:8] - **Durkheim's concepts:** **social facts** – external forces such as laws, customs, religion and traditions that exist outside the individual and shape behaviour; **collective conscience** – shared beliefs and values that unite society; **anomie** – normlessness caused by rapid social change, seen in crime and suicide. Books: *The Division of Labour in Society*, *The Rules of Sociological Method*, *Suicide*.
[105:5:9] - **C. Wright Mills (1916–1962):** the **sociological imagination** is the ability to connect personal experience (**biography** – one's own life and troubles) with wider social forces and **history**; e.g. one person's unemployment (personal trouble) is linked to an **economic slowdown** (public issue).
