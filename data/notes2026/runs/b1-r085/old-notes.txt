=== NOTES 1:85 | Imperial Cholas – Vijayalaya, Rajaraja I, Rajendra I; Gangaikonda Title and Naval Expeditions
## SUB 0 | Origin and Core Region
[85:0:0] - Imperial Cholas flourished c. **850–1279 CE** (9th to 13th c.); rose from the **Kaveri delta**.
[85:0:1] - **Coromandel** comes from **Cholamandalam** (Tondaimandalam was the **Pallava** country).
[85:0:2] - Empire covered the Coromandel Coast and parts of the Deccan (Tungabhadra to Krishna).
[85:0:3] - Chola kings recorded achievements in inscriptions → most voluminous inscriptions in South India.
[85:0:4] - Cholas were famous for elaborate **local self-government** and excellent village administration (Uttaramerur inscriptions of Parantaka I).
[85:0:5] - Kings named their **successor during their own rule** (co-regency).
[85:0:6] # Founder
[85:0:7] - **Vijayalaya**, a **Pallava feudatory at Uraiyur**, captured **Thanjavur** from the **Muttaraiyars** in **850 CE** → founder of the imperial line.
[85:0:8] - Successor: **Aditya I** (not Parantaka I).
[85:0:9] Founder = Vijayalaya, not Parantaka I or Rajendra I. Aditya I, not Vijayalaya, followed the founder.

## SUB 4 | Naval Expeditions and the "chola Lake"
[85:4:0] # Srivijaya Expedition (1025 CE)
[85:4:1] - By **Rajendra I** against the revived **Srivijaya** empire – a sea empire under the **Sailendra** dynasty (Malay Peninsula, **Sumatra**, **Java** and nearby islands).
[85:4:2] - Defeated the **Sailendra king**; took **Kadaram (Kedah)** in Malaya and parts of Sumatra (**APPSC 2000, 2003**; UPSC 2025).
[85:4:3] - Cause: once-cordial Chola-Sailendra ties soured; Srivijaya **blocked Indian traders on the China route**; the expedition widened Chola trade with China.
[85:4:4] - Rajaraja I had earlier sent a naval expedition against the Sailendras.
[85:4:5] # Significance
[85:4:6] - **K.A. Nilakanta Sastri**: Cholas turned the **Bay of Bengal into a "Chola Lake"**.
[85:4:7] - Cholas = **first Indian power with overseas naval campaigns**; spread Dravidian art to South-East Asia (Angkor Wat, Borobudur).

## SUB 9 | Kulottunga I and the Later Cholas
[85:9:0] # Kulottunga I
[85:9:1] - Born **Rajendra Chalukya**, son of Rajaraja Narendra + Ammanga Devi.
[85:9:2] - **1070**: ousted from Vengi by uncle Vijayaditya VII; Virarajendra and Adhirajendra died → took the Chola throne (1070).
[85:9:3] - **First king of the Chalukya-Chola line** (APPSC G2 2011).
[85:9:4] - Sent **72 merchants to China** (**1077**, goodwill mission; UPPSC 1992).
[85:9:5] - Conquered **Kalinga**; built Kulottunga Cholapatnam (Visakhapatnam); **abolished tolls**.
[85:9:6] # Sri Lanka Relations and Decline
[85:9:7] - **Vijayabahu I** expelled the Cholas from Sri Lanka around **1070**.
[85:9:8] - Kulottunga I **gave freedom to Sri Lanka** (Vijayabahu declared independence) and married his daughter to Sinhala prince **Virapperumal**.
[85:9:9] - Decline after Kulottunga I: **weakening central bureaucracy**, growth of **local chieftains**, then the **rise of the Pandyas**; Rajendra III gone by **1279**.
[85:9:10] # Latest Developments
[85:9:11] - **1 Feb 2026**: Grand Chola Museum, Thanjavur – 125-ft Rajaraja Chola statue planned; 52 acres; Rs 56.41 crore.
[85:9:12] - **16 May 2026**: Netherlands returned the **Anaimangalam (Leiden) copper plates** at The Hague – ordered by Rajaraja I, engraved under Rajendra I, extended by Kulottunga I.
[85:9:13] - **8 Sep 2026**: Tamil Nadu began the third phase of underwater exploration off **Poompuhar** (early Chola capital).

=== NOTES 1:86 | Chola Local Self-government – Uttaramerur Inscriptions, Ur, Sabha, Nagaram, Variyams
## SUB 0 | Village Autonomy – the Chola Hallmark
[86:0:0] - The **Cholas** are the dynasty known for **village autonomy** and elaborate **local self-government** (MPPSC 2021, BPSC 2020).
[86:0:1] - **K.A. Nilakanta Sastri**: a remarkable example of **"grassroots democracy"**; often called an early **Panchayati Raj** system (village governments run by self-governing elected panchayats, developed by the Dravidian Cholas).
[86:0:2] - Every village had its own committee – the **Sabha**; special feature of the Chola era = autonomy of village administration.
[86:0:3] - We know more about the Cholas than their predecessors because **Rajaraja I** and **Rajendra I** had long **inscriptions on temple walls**.
[86:0:4] # Dynasty Comparison
[86:0:5] TABLE head: Dynasty | Local government
  [86:0:5.0] Cholas | Culmination: elected assemblies, committees, lottery
  [86:0:5.1] Pallavas | Sabha and ur assemblies already appear; Cholas built on this base
  [86:0:5.2] Badami Chalukyas | Highly centralised
  [86:0:5.3] Rashtrakutas | Village assemblies had a significant role in administration and disputes
[86:0:6] - Distractors: Chalukya, Pallava, Pandya, Chera, Solanki, Parmar.
[86:0:7] Pallava, Chola and Rashtrakuta records show active village assemblies; the Badami Chalukya polity was highly centralised.
## SUB 1 | Territorial Units and Nadu Assembly
[86:1:0] - **Ur** = village; **Nadu** = group of villages.
[86:1:1] - **Nattar** = assembly of the nadu, made up of leading **vellala** (peasant) landholders; handled **revenue collection** and **disputes above village level**.
[86:1:2] - The nadu's **nagaram** (market town) worked alongside it.
[86:1:3] - Nattar is a Chola body, **not** a 73rd Amendment body.
## SUB 2 | Village Assemblies → Type
[86:2:0] TABLE head: Assembly | Where | Composition
  [86:2:0.0] Ur | Ordinary, non-brahmadeya (vellanvagai) villages | General assembly of local residents/landholders; land sale, gift, tax exemptions; generally fewer than ten members
  [86:2:0.1] Sabha / Mahasabha | Brahmadeya villages (agraharas, rent-free) | Brahmans only; criteria: property, family, learning, good conduct
  [86:2:0.2] Nagaram | Market and commercial towns | Merchants
  [86:2:0.3] Variyams | Within the sabha | Village committees
[86:2:1] Ur = non-brahmadeya; Sabha = brahmadeya (Brahmans only); Nagaram = merchants.
## SUB 3 | Uttaramerur Inscriptions
[86:3:0] - Two inscriptions of **Parantaka I**, dated **919 and 921 CE**.
[86:3:1] - Place: **Uttaramerur**, **Kanchipuram district** (Tamil Nadu).
[86:3:2] - **Most detailed record of village administration in ancient India**; main source for Chola village government (**APPSC G2 2000, 2003**).
[86:3:3] - Contents: the sabha, qualifications and disqualifications, the **kudavolai** system and the **variyams**.
[86:3:4] Source is Uttaramerur – not Kudimiamalai, Gangaikondacholapuram, Silappadikaram, Tolkappiyam or Periya Puranam. The Thanjavur inscription of Rajaraja I is about military feats (Chera navy at Kandalur), not village government.
[86:3:5] # Qualifications and Disqualifications
[86:3:6] TABLE head: Qualifications | Disqualifications
  [86:3:6.0] Own at least 1/4 veli of land | Failed to submit accounts
  [86:3:6.1] Own house (residence) | Committed sins
  [86:3:6.2] Age 35–70 | Stolen property
  [86:3:6.3] Knowledge of the Vedas | Served on a committee in the past three years
## SUB 4 | Election Procedure – Kudavolai
[86:4:0] - 1. Village divided into **30 wards (kudumbus)**.
[86:4:1] - 2. Names of eligible persons written on **palm leaves**.
[86:4:2] - 3. Leaves put in a **pot (kudam)**.
[86:4:3] - 4. A young boy/girl **drew the names by lot**.
[86:4:4] - 5. Chosen members served a term of **360 days**; removed at once if found guilty of an offence.
[86:4:5] - Those who had served in the **last three years** were barred.
[86:4:6] - **Aug 2025**: PM Modi cited the Uttaramerur **"ballot pot"** (kudavolai) as proof of India's early democratic traditions.
## SUB 5 | Terms → Meaning (appsc Match)
[86:5:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [86:5:0.0] Variyam | Committee system / working committee overseeing village work
  [86:5:0.1] Kudumbu / Kudumba | Ward
  [86:5:0.2] Kudavolai | Lottery (palm-leaf ballots in a pot)
  [86:5:0.3] Perumakkal | Members of the village assembly
  [86:5:0.4] Variyapperumakkal | Elected committee members
[86:5:1] APPSC G1 2022, 2023 match: Variyam = committee; Kudumbu = ward; Kudavolai = lottery; Perumakkal = assembly member.
## SUB 6 | Variyams → Function
[86:6:0] TABLE head: Variyam | Function
  [86:6:0.0] Samvatsara variyam | Annual committee – annual works; also justice
  [86:6:0.1] Eri variyam | Tanks; distribution of water to fields
  [86:6:0.2] Pon variyam | Gold – finance, currency checking
  [86:6:0.3] Thotta variyam | Gardens / orchards
  [86:6:0.4] Panchavara variyam | Standing committee (supervision)
[86:6:1] - Uttaramerur committees covered irrigation, gardens, justice and finance; others handled law and order.
## SUB 7 | Powers of Assemblies
[86:7:0] - **Land**: assembly was absolute proprietor of village lands and newly acquired lands.
[86:7:1] - **Finance**: collected **royal dues in cash, kind or both**; levied taxes; **raised loans**; **auctioned defaulters' land**.
[86:7:2] - **Tanks and water**: through the eri variyam.
[86:7:3] - **Justice**: law and order and disputes through committees.
[86:7:4] - **Temples**: Rajaraja I directed the **Cholamandalam sabhas** to serve the **Brihadeshvara** temple.
## SUB 8 | Chola Assembly vs 73RD Amendment
[86:8:0] TABLE head: Point | Chola village assembly | 73rd Amendment PRIs
  [86:8:0.0] Selection | Lottery (kudavolai) among qualified | Direct election (Art. 243C)
  [86:8:0.1] Eligibility | Property, age 35–70, Vedic learning | Universal adult franchise; age 21
  [86:8:0.2] Term | 360 days; 3-year bar on re-entry | 5 years (Art. 243E); election within 6 months of dissolution
  [86:8:0.3] Committees | Variyams | Standing committees under state law
  [86:8:0.4] Women | No quota | At least 1/3 seats and chairperson posts (Art. 243D); SC/ST reservation
