## SUB 0 | Village Autonomy – the Chola Hallmark
[0:0] - **Special feature of Chola polity:** **autonomy of village administration** / autonomous village panchayats (UPPSC 1995; CDS 2000) – not mandalam divisions, monarchy, ministers or tax methods.
[0:1] - **Famous for:** **village assemblies** (RAS 1993); **excellent village administration** (UPPSC 1991); **elaborate local self-government** (State PSC) – the **Cholas**, not the Chalukyas, Cheras, Solankis or Parmars.
[0:2] - **Early Panchayati Raj:** "Chola village administration was an early example of the Panchayati Raj system" – autonomous elected village bodies evolved by the **Dravidians (Cholas)** (RAS 1997).
[0:3] - **Real powers:** the sabha, ur and nagaram were **elected bodies with real powers** over revenue, irrigation, temples and justice – not symbolic councils.
[0:4] - **Nature of the gram sabhas (Degree Lecturers 2011):** fully **autonomous**, **helped the public** (tanks, roads, justice) and acted as **bankers and trustees** (raised loans, held endowments) → **all of the above**.
[0:5] - **Historians:** K.A. **Nilakanta Sastri** – a remarkable "grassroots democracy"; **Burton Stein** – a **segmentary state**.
[0:6] TABLE head: Dynasty | Local government
  [0:6.0] Cholas | Culmination: elected assemblies, committees, lottery
  [0:6.1] Pallavas | Sabha and ur already appear – the base the Cholas built on
  [0:6.2] Badami Chalukyas | Highly centralised
  [0:6.3] Rashtrakutas | Village assemblies active in disputes

## SUB 1 | Uttaramerur Inscriptions
[1:0] - **What:** two inscriptions on the temple walls at **Uttaramerur**, a **brahmadeya** village in **Kanchipuram district**, Tamil Nadu.
[1:1] - **Date and king:** **919 and 921 CE** (c. 920), reign of **Parantaka I** – not Rajaraja I.
[1:2] - **Source of Chola local government:** Uttaramerur (APPSC G2 2000, 2003; APPSC PYQ ×3; IAS 1993; APPSC G1) – not Kudumiyanmalai, Gangaikondacholapuram, Kanchipuram, Thanjavur, Uraiyur, Silappadikaram, Tolkappiyam or Periya Puranam.
[1:3] - **Contents:** constitution of the sabha, **qualifications**, **disqualifications**, mode of election (**kudavolai**), committees, functions and the **right to recall**.
[1:4] - **⚠ Trap:** the **Thanjavur** inscription of Rajaraja I records military feats (Chera navy at Kandalur), not village government.
[1:5] # Qualifications and disqualifications
[1:6] TABLE head: Qualifications | Disqualifications
  [1:6.0] Own at least 1/4 veli of tax-paying land | Failed to submit accounts
  [1:6.1] Own a house (residence) on own site | Committed sins / crimes, alcoholism
  [1:6.2] Age 35–70 | Debt defaulters; close relatives of members
  [1:6.3] Knowledge of the Vedas or administration | Served on a committee in the past three years
[1:7] - **Aug 2025:** the PM cited the Uttaramerur **"ballot pot"** as proof of India's early democratic traditions.

## SUB 2 | Kudavolai, Terms and Variyams
[2:0] # Kudavolai (pot-ballot)
[2:1] - 1. Village divided into **30 wards (kudumbus)**; 2. names of eligible persons written on **palm leaves**; 3. put in a **pot (kudam)**; 4. a **young boy** drew the lots in public.
[2:2] - **Term:** **360 days** (one year); removed at once for an offence; a person who served in the last three years was barred.
[2:3] - **⚠ Sources differ:** NextGen says committee members "served three-year terms" – the inscription fixes a **one-year** term and a **three-year bar** on re-selection (PMF).
[2:4] # Terms → meaning (APPSC G1 match)

  [2:5.3] Perumakkal | Member of the village assembly

## SUB 3 | Assemblies – Ur, Sabha, Nagaram, Nattar
[3:0] TABLE head: Assembly | Where | Members
  [3:0.0] Ur | Non-brahmadeya (vellanvagai) villages | Landholders (urar); land sale, gifts, tax exemptions
  [3:0.1] Sabha / Mahasabha | Brahmadeya (agrahara) villages | Brahmins with property, family, learning (Vedic) and good conduct
  [3:0.2] Nagaram | Merchant (market) towns | Merchants (nagarattar)
  [3:0.3] Nattar | The nadu (group of villages) | Leading vellala landholders
[3:1] - **Nadu** = a group of villages; the **nattar** handled **revenue collection and disputes above the village** (a Chola body, not created by the 73rd Amendment).
[3:2] - **Powers:** absolute proprietor of village lands; collected royal dues in **cash, kind or both**; levied taxes; **raised loans**; **auctioned defaulters' land**.
[3:3] - **Temples:** Rajaraja I directed the **Cholamandalam sabhas** to serve the **Brihadeshvara** temple.
[3:4] - **AP parallel:** village **mahajana** assemblies in **Kakatiya and Velanati** inscriptions – a weaker parallel.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval India p.407-408; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 3 p.204; app notes 1:86 (sub 1, 2, 7)
## SUB 4 | Chola Assembly vs 73rd Amendment
[4:0] TABLE head: Point | Chola village assembly | 73rd Amendment
  [4:0.0] Selection | Lottery among the qualified | Direct election
  [4:0.1] Eligibility | Property, age 35–70, Vedic learning | Adult franchise
  [4:0.2] Term | 360 days | 5 years (Art. 243E)
  [4:0.3] Women | No quota (women, labourers, landless excluded) | At least 1/3 seats (Art. 243D)
[4:1] - **Gram Sabha:** **Art. 243A**; the intermediate tier may be omitted in a State with population **not above 20 lakh** (Art. 243B).
