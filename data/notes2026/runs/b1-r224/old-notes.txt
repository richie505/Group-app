=== NOTES 1:224 | Government of India Act 1919 – Dyarchy, Montagu-chelmsford Reforms; Rowlatt Act
## SUB 0 | Overview and Road to the 1919 Act
[224:0:0] # Montagu Declaration, 1917
[224:0:1] - Date: **20 August 1917**, made in the British **Parliament**.
[224:0:2] - Text: "Gradual development of self-governing institutions with a view to the **progressive realisation of responsible government** in India as an integral part of the British Empire."
[224:0:3] - **First official recognition** of self-government as an aim; pressure mainly from the **Home Rule Leagues**.
[224:0:4] - **Dec 1917**: Montagu and Chelmsford toured India; **July 1918**: Report announced.
[224:0:5] # Names Behind the Reform
[224:0:6] TABLE head: Person | Post
  [224:0:6.0] Edwin Montagu | Secretary of State for India
  [224:0:6.1] Lord Chelmsford | Viceroy
[224:0:7] GoI Act 1919 = **Montagu-Chelmsford** Reforms; **Morley-Minto** Reforms = Indian Councils Act **1909**.
[224:0:8] # Moderates' Exit
[224:0:9] - Congress special **Bombay session, August 1918** (President **Hasan Imam**) condemned the reforms.
[224:0:10] - Moderates under **Surendranath Banerjee** accepted the reforms, left Congress and formed the **Indian National Liberal Federation (1918)** (also called Indian Liberal Federation).
[224:0:11] - **Amritsar Congress (1919, Motilal Nehru)**: reforms "**inadequate, unsatisfactory and disappointing**".
[224:0:12] TABLE head: Person / body | Reaction
  [224:0:12.0] C.R. Das + Tilak | Rejection (Amritsar, 1919)
  [224:0:12.1] Gandhi + Malaviya | Give the reforms a fair trial
  [224:0:12.2] Krishna Patrika | "Giving with the right hand, taking with the left"
[224:0:13] - AP angle: **Nyapati Subbarao** led a **27-member Andhra delegation** that met Montagu at **Madras** to demand a separate **Andhra province**; **Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao** presided over the **Special Andhra Mahasabha, Guntur, 17 Aug 1918**.
## SUB 1 | Provincial Dyarchy – Reserved and Transferred Subjects
[224:1:0] # Meaning
[224:1:1] - **Dyarchy** = **dual government**: subjects given to the provinces split into **reserved** and **transferred**.
[224:1:2] - Subjects first divided into **central** and **provincial** lists; **provincial budgets separated** from the central budget.
[224:1:3] - The Act delegated a large part of authority to the provinces; governance did not stay unitary; it **clearly defined central and provincial jurisdiction**.
[224:1:4] # Reserved vs Transferred
[224:1:5] TABLE head: Subjects | Examples | Run by | Answerable to
  [224:1:5.0] Reserved | Administration of Justice, Land revenue, Finance, Law and order, Police | Governor + Executive Council | Not the legislature
  [224:1:5.1] Transferred | Education, Health, Local self-government | Governor + Ministers from the elected legislature | Legislature
[224:1:6] - Flaw: ministers had transferred subjects but **no finance** (finance stayed reserved).
[224:1:7] **Local self-government** was transferred, not reserved.
[224:1:8] APPSC 2019: "dyarchy = single government" is FALSE; dyarchy = dual government.
[224:1:9] Dyarchy (1919) was in the **provinces**; **provincial autonomy** came only with the **1935 Act**.
## SUB 2 | Centre, Electorate and Direct Elections
[224:2:0] # Central Legislature (bicameral)
[224:2:1] TABLE head: House | Members
  [224:2:1.0] Council of State (upper) | 60
  [224:2:1.1] Legislative Assembly (lower) | 145 (104 elected)
[224:2:2] - **Viceroy's Executive Council**: **6** members besides the Commander-in-Chief, **3 Indians**.
[224:2:3] - First **President** of the Assembly: **Frederick Whyte (1921)**; first **Deputy President**: **Sachchidananda Sinha**.
[224:2:4] # Direct Elections and Franchise
[224:2:5] - **GoI Act 1919** = **first Act to introduce direct elections**.
[224:2:6] - Franchise was **limited**: based on **property, tax and education**.
[224:2:7] - The **1909 Act** had elected non-officials, largely via **indirect** and **separate** electorates.
[224:2:8] - Communal (separate) electorates were extended in 1919.
[224:2:9] The APPSC GS 2018 key gave 1909. Rule: mark **1919**; if 1919 is not among the options, mark 1909.
## SUB 3 | Bodies Created and Statutory Review
[224:3:0] TABLE head: Body / office | Fact
  [224:3:0.0] Public Service Commission | Section 96(C), GoI Act 1919 (APPSC GS 2022); set up 1 October 1926 after the Lee Commission
  [224:3:0.1] Chamber of Princes (Narendra Mandal) | 1921, by royal proclamation; consultative body of rulers
  [224:3:0.2] High Commissioner for India (London) | Provided by the 1919 Act; first holder Sir William Meyer (1920); looked after Indian trade in Europe
  [224:3:0.3] Legislature officers | President + Deputy President
[224:3:1] # Statutory Review Clause
[224:3:2] - The Act required a **statutory commission after 10 years** to review its working.
[224:3:3] - The **Simon Commission** was appointed in **1927**, **two years early**.
[224:3:4] - **Muddiman Committee (1924)**: British response to the Swarajist demand to review the 1919 Act.
[224:3:5] 96(C) = PSC section; 1926 = PSC set up; 1921 = Chamber of Princes; 10 years = review clause.
## SUB 4 | Working of the Act – Elections and Ministries
[224:4:0] # Madras and Provinces
[224:4:1] - **First elections: 1920**; **Congress boycotted** (Non-Cooperation).
[224:4:2] - **Madras, 1920**: **Justice Party** won **63 seats**; **A. Subbarayalu Reddiar** = first Chief Minister (Premier), **Dec 1920**; then **Raja of Panagal (1921)**. Justice Party held office most of 1920-37.
[224:4:3] # Central Legislative Assembly
[224:4:4] - Elections under the 1919 Act: **1923, 1926, 1930, 1934, 1945** (last in **1945**).
[224:4:5] - **Swaraj Party** (C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru) contested **1923** to obstruct from within; won **42 of 104** elected seats (Nov 1923); backed by independents like **Jinnah** and **Malaviya**.
[224:4:6] - **Vithalbhai Patel**: **first Indian President (Speaker)** of the Assembly, **1925**; co-founder of the Swaraj Party.
[224:4:7] - AP angle: **V. Ramadas** elected to the Central Legislative Assembly (1923).
## SUB 5 | Rowlatt Act, 1919
[224:5:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [224:5:0.0] Official name | Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919
  [224:5:0.1] Passed | March 1919

## SUB 7 | Simon Commission
[224:7:0] # Appointment and Composition
[224:7:1] - Appointed **8 November 1927** by **Stanley Baldwin's Conservative** government, to study the working of the 1919 Act.
[224:7:2] - Other names: **Indian Statutory Commission**, "White Commission".
[224:7:3] - **7 members, all British**: chairman **Sir John Simon** (Liberal) + 6 others, including **Clement Attlee**.
[224:7:4] - **No Indian member** → boycott.
[224:7:5] # Visits and Boycott
[224:7:6] - Arrived at **Bombay, 3 February 1928** (APPSC GS 2021: 1928) → all-India hartal, black flags.
[224:7:7] - Two visits: **3 Feb–31 Mar 1928**; **11 Oct 1928–13 Apr 1929**.
[224:7:8] - **Madras Congress (Dec 1927, M.A. Ansari)**: boycott "at every stage and in every form".
[224:7:9] - Slogan **"Simon Go Back"** – coined by **Yusuf Meherally** (APPSC GS 2018).
[224:7:10] TABLE head: Joined boycott | Did not join
  [224:7:10.0] Congress, Jinnah's League faction, Hindu Mahasabha | Shafi's League faction, Punjab Unionists, Justice Party
[224:7:11] # Places of Protest
[224:7:12] TABLE head: Place | Leader
  [224:7:12.0] Lahore | Lala Lajpat Rai – lathi charge 30 Oct 1928; died of injuries (Nov 1928)
  [224:7:12.1] Madras | Tanguturi Prakasam – led the procession, bared his chest before police guns → "Andhra Kesari"
  [224:7:12.2] Lucknow | Jawaharlal Nehru, G.B. Pant
  [224:7:12.3] Tenali / Guntur | G.V. Unnayya Sastri; complete bandh in Guntur
[224:7:13] - Andhra route: Tenali – Guntur – Vijayawada – Ongole – Madras; anti-Simon committee president **S. Satyamurti**; **Bulusu Sambamurthy** toured Andhra.
[224:7:14] - **Saunders** shot by **Bhagat Singh** and **Rajguru** (Dec 1928) in revenge for Lajpat Rai.
[224:7:15] # Report (1930)
[224:7:16] - Abolish provincial dyarchy → **responsible government / provincial autonomy** in provinces.
[224:7:17] - Keep **separate electorates**; first used the term "**Scheduled Castes**".
[224:7:18] - Its report became the **base of the GoI Act 1935**.

## SUB 9 | Government of India Act 1935
[224:9:0] # Structure
[224:9:1] - **321 sections and 10 schedules** – the **longest** British Act on India; Royal assent **2 August 1935**.
[224:9:2] - Basis: **Simon Report** + three RTCs + White Paper.
[224:9:3] # Key Provisions
[224:9:4] - **All-India Federation** (provinces + princely states; optional for states) – **never formed**; princes were to counter-balance nationalists.
[224:9:5] - **Dyarchy at the Centre** – never operated.
[224:9:6] - **Provincial autonomy** – provincial dyarchy abolished; began **April 1937**.
[224:9:7] - **Bicameral legislatures** in some provinces; **India Council** abolished.
[224:9:8] - **Federal Court** at **Delhi (1937)**; **Federal PSC** and **Provincial PSCs**.
[224:9:9] - Local self-government became a provincial subject.
[224:9:10] - **RBI**: RBI Act **1934** (Hilton Young Commission, 1926); started **1 April 1935**; nationalised **1 Jan 1949**.
[224:9:11] # Elections and Congress Ministries
[224:9:12] - **1937 provincial elections** under the 1935 Act: Congress won; Madras premier **C. Rajagopalachari** (first Congress ministry).
[224:9:13] - Centre continued under the 1919 structure.
[224:9:14] - Congress ministries **resigned Oct–Nov 1939** – India dragged into **WWII** without consent.
[224:9:15] - Muslim League "**Day of Deliverance**" – **22 Dec 1939**; Ambedkar joined.
[224:9:16] 1919 Act elections | Central Legislative Assembly 1923-1945 | provincial councils 1920
1935 Act elections | Provincial legislatures 1937 | Congress ministries

[225:3:11] - **Joint electorates + reserved seats** for minorities – not separate electorates.
[225:3:12] - **Universal adult suffrage**; **fundamental rights**.

[225:3:15] - **All Parties Muslim Conference** (Aga Khan) rejected it and demanded separate electorates.
