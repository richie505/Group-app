## SUB 0 | Simon Commission – Appointment, Boycott and Visits
[0:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [0:0.0] Official name | Indian Statutory Commission; "White (Men) Commission"
  [0:0.1] Appointed | 8 November 1927 (not 1925/1928/1930), two years early, by Stanley Baldwin's Conservative government; Secretary of State Lord Birkenhead
  [0:0.2] Purpose | Examine the working of the 1919 Act (reforms) – statutory 10-year review; "administrative reform" (UPPSC 1990), not education, agriculture, army, Rowlatt Act or princely states
  [0:0.3] Members | Seven, all British – chairman Sir John Simon (Liberal); Clement Attlee (Labour) a member
  [0:0.4] Why boycotted | No Indian member – all were Englishmen (not "appointed before time", not support for the League, not NCM)
  [0:0.5] Exclusion of Indians | On the suggestion of Viceroy Lord Irwin (UP Lower Sub 2013 key; general knowledge)
  [0:0.6] Arrival | Bombay, 3 February 1928 (visit year 1928) – all-India hartal, black flags, "Simon Go Back"
  [0:0.7] Visits | Two: 3 Feb – 31 Mar 1928 and 11 Oct 1928 – 13 Apr 1929 (so "second visit Oct 1929 – Apr 1930" ✗)
[0:1] - **Boycott decision:** Madras Congress, **Dec 1927, M.A. Ansari** – "at every stage and in every form"; joined by the Hindu Mahasabha and **Jinnah's** League faction; **not** by Shafi's League faction, the Punjab Unionists or the **Justice Party**.
[0:2] - The Commission **accentuated discontent** in all parties for excluding Indians (not the Welby Commission, Cripps or Cabinet Mission).
[0:3] - **"Simon Go Back"** slogan coined by **Yusuf Meherally**.
[0:4] - **Statements (UPPSC 2005):** appointed to enquire into the 1919 Act ✓; headed by Simon ✓; federal form recommended ✓ (as an ultimate goal); opposed by Indian leaders ✓ → **all four**.
[0:5] - **"Report should be thrown on a heap of rubbish":** **Sir P.S. Sivaswami Iyer** (attribution from exam keys; not in the library).

## SUB 1 | Protests – Lala Lajpat Rai and the Andhra Boycott
[1:0] # Lahore and Lucknow
[1:1] - **Lahore, 30 October 1928:** **Lala Lajpat Rai** led the anti-Simon procession, was hit in a **lathi charge** (not Rowlatt, Quit India or 1935 Act protests) and died on **17 Nov 1928** ("last nails in the coffin of British imperialism").
[1:2] - A-R: protest led by Lajpat Rai at Lahore (A) and "no Indian member" (R) – **both true, R explains A**.
[1:3] - Officer held responsible: **Saunders** (ASP) – shot by **Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Azad, Dec 1928** (Spectrum: "the police official responsible for the lathicharge"); not Hugh Rose or Nicholson.
[1:4] - **Lucknow:** Jawaharlal Nehru and G.B. Pant beaten.
[1:5] - Lajpat Rai had presided over the **first AITUC session (1920)**.
[1:6] # Andhra
[1:7] TABLE head: Place / person | Fact
  [1:7.0] Madras | Tanguturi Prakasam led the procession, 1928; after the firing (Parthasarathi killed) he bared his chest → title "Andhra Kesari" (Simon protest, not Vande Mataram, NCM or Quit India)
  [1:7.1] Guntur and Ongole | Black flags – places the Commission visited in Andhra
  [1:7.2] Vijayawada | Ayyadevara Kaleswara Rao handed Simon a "Simon Go Back" letter at the railway station
  [1:7.3] Guntur | Nadimpalli Narasimha Rao organised the movement; Tenali-Guntur protests under G.V. Unnayya Sastri
  [1:7.4] Municipal resolutions | Tirupati, Kurnool, Vijayawada, Eluru, Srikakulam backed the boycott
  [1:7.5] Anti-Simon committee | President S. Satyamurti; Bulusu Sambamurthy toured Andhra
[1:8] - Hartal in Andhra towns on **3 Feb 1928**; the Commission reached Madras on 26 Feb 1928 → Simon "visited Andhra" in **1928**.

## SUB 2 | Simon Commission Report (1930)
[2:0] - Published **May 1930** in two volumes.
[2:1] - **Abolition of dyarchy** → **responsible (representative) government** and **autonomy in the provinces** (UPSC 2010 correct statement) – not an inter-provincial council, not abolition of the bicameral centre, not an Indian Police Service.
[2:2] - **Rejected parliamentary responsibility at the Centre**; federation accepted only as a distant goal.
[2:3] - **Separate communal electorates retained** (and extended); first official use of the term **"Scheduled Castes"**.
[2:4] - **Sindh** separated from Bombay; **Burma** separated from India; new **Orissa** province – **Andhra's demand ignored**.
[2:5] - **Baseline of the GoI Act, 1935** (not Cabinet Mission, Cripps or a "Rowlatt Commission").
[2:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum (Ahir) p.392-393; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.74; NextGen AP History Part 22 (src/ap-history/ap-history-appsc-22.txt) p.26; old app notes 1:224 ("Scheduled Castes")
## SUB 3 | Nehru Report (1928)
[3:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [3:0.0] Trigger | Lord Birkenhead's challenge to Indians to frame an agreed constitution – the first Indian draft of a dominion-status constitution, made in response to the Simon Commission
  [3:0.1] Body | Committee of the All Parties Conference (Feb 1928, presided by M.A. Ansari)
  [3:0.2] Chairman | Motilal Nehru (not J.L., R.K. or B.L. Nehru); Jawaharlal Nehru = secretary
  [3:0.3] Members | Tej Bahadur Sapru, Subhas Bose, Ali Imam, M.S. Aney, Mangal Singh, Shuaib Qureshi, G.R. Pradhan
  [3:0.4] Subject | Constitutional arrangements for India
  [3:0.5] Finalised | August 1928 (Lucknow); adopted at Calcutta Congress, Dec 1928
[3:1] # Recommendations
[3:2] - **Dominion status** (not complete independence).
[3:3] - **Joint electorates with reserved seats** for minorities – **no separate electorates**.
[3:4] - **Linguistic provinces** (not Mountbatten, Cripps or Cabinet Mission).
[3:5] - **19 Fundamental Rights**, universal adult suffrage, equal rights for women – first document to advocate FRs (the 1935 Act, August Offer and Cripps did not).
[3:6] - Responsible government at Centre and provinces; **bicameral** centre; **Supreme Court**; structure broadly **unitary** (residuary powers with the Centre).
[3:7] # Reactions
[3:8] - Radicals **Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Bose** formed the **Independence for India League (1928)** in opposition to the report's dominion status.
[3:9] - **Muslim League** rejected it; **All Parties Muslim Conference**, Delhi, **31 Dec 1928**, presided by the **Aga Khan**; Jinnah's amendments failed at Calcutta (Dec 1928).
[3:10] - **"First demanded dominion status" (UPPCS 2013 key):** **Tej Bahadur Sapru and Jayakar** (Liberals; printed key, not verified in the library – Motilal Nehru's 1926 call is the other claimant).
[3:11] ~~ Sources: Spectrum (Ahir) p.393-397; NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.58, p.74; PMF Modern History 04 p.178-181
## SUB 4 | Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929)
[4:0] - Proposed by **M.A. Jinnah** after rejection of his amendments to the **Nehru Report**; **Fourteen** points (not 13/15/16).
[4:1] - Presented at the **Muslim League session, Delhi, March 1929**.
[4:2] TABLE head: Key point | Fact
  [4:2.0] Constitution | Federal, residuary powers with provinces; uniform provincial autonomy
  [4:2.1] Central legislature | Muslim representation not less than one-third (not one-fifth)
  [4:2.2] Cabinets | One-third Muslim ministers at Centre and provinces
  [4:2.3] Electorates | Separate electorates continue
  [4:2.4] Amendment | No change by the Centre without concurrence of the states of the federation
  [4:2.5] Religion | Full religious liberty for all communities
  [4:2.6] Provinces | Sindh separated from Bombay; reforms in NWFP and Baluchistan
[4:3] - **Delhi Proposals (March 1927):** joint electorates with reserved seats, one-third seats at Centre, Sindh separated – vs Fourteen Points: keep separate electorates.
[4:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.64-65; Spectrum (Ahir) p.395-396

## SUB 6 | Round Table Conferences (1930-32) – Dates, Aim and Outcome
[6:0] TABLE head: RTC | Dates | Congress | Outcome
  [6:0.0] First | 12 Nov 1930 – 19 Jan 1931 | Absent (boycotted; CDM) | Opened by George V, chaired by Ramsay MacDonald; federation agreed in principle
  [6:0.1] Second | 7 Sep – 1 Dec 1931 | Gandhi – sole representative | Failed on the communal (minorities) question
  [6:0.2] Third | 17 Nov – 24 Dec 1932 | Absent | Only 46 delegates; White Paper (1933) → GoI Act 1935
[6:1] - All held in **London** (not Delhi, Paris, Moscow); aim: **constitutional reforms** – to **discuss the Simon Commission report** and the future constitution, including **dominion status** (APPSC G1 2019: both).
[6:2] - Strictly **one conference in three sessions**, not three conferences (UPSC 1996).
[6:3] - **Gandhi-Irwin Pact, 5 March 1931:** Congress suspended CDM and agreed to attend → paved the way for the **Second RTC** (A-R: Nehru did **not** represent Congress at the 2nd RTC; Congress participation was implicit in the Pact → A false, R true).
[6:4] - **Karachi Congress (March 1931)** **endorsed** (did not oppose) the Pact.
[6:5] - **Communal Award: 16 August 1932** (not 1933); Indian Navy Act 1927, CDM 1930, 2nd RTC 1931 ✓.
[6:6] - **APPSC match (cancelled):** 1st RTC – no Congress; Gandhi-Irwin Pact – Gandhi agreed to attend the next; 2nd RTC – disputes with the League and Ambedkar; 3rd RTC – Congress again absent (A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4).
