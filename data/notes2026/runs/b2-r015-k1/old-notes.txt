=== NOTES 2:15 | Directive Principles of State Policy – Articles 36-51, Classification, Implementation
## SUB 0 | Basics of Dpsp
[15:0:0] - Located in **Part IV** of the Constitution – **Articles 36 to 51** (16 articles).
[15:0:1] - **Article 36**: "State" has the **same meaning as in Part III** (Article 12).
[15:0:2] - **Article 37**: DPSPs "shall **not be enforceable by any court**", but are "**fundamental in the governance** of the country"; it is the **duty of the State to apply them in making laws**.
[15:0:3] - Aim: **social and economic democracy** and a **welfare state** (political democracy is the aim of FRs).
[15:0:4] - Called the **"Instrument of Instructions"**; with FRs, the **"conscience of the Constitution"** (Granville Austin).
[15:0:5] - Welfare state idea is found in **both the Preamble and the DPSPs**; **Economic Justice** is in both (e.g. Article 38).
[15:0:6] - Welfare State aims at caring for the welfare of **weaker sections**; it is a secular, democratic state – a **Religious State is NOT an objective**.
[15:0:7] - Welfare state description: "The State is everywhere: it leaves hardly a gap."
[15:0:8] DPSPs do not impose a legal duty on the State; their sanction is moral and political.
## SUB 1 | Source and Historical Antecedents
[15:1:0] # Foreign Source
[15:1:1] - Borrowed from the **Irish Constitution of 1937** ("Directive Principles of Social Policy").
[15:1:2] - Ireland had itself copied the idea from the **Spanish Constitution**.
[15:1:3] # Indian Precursors
[15:1:4] TABLE head: Antecedent | Contribution
  [15:1:4.0] Nehru Report (1928) | Early charter of rights and welfare goals
  [15:1:4.1] Karachi Resolution (1931) | Socialist-nationalist economic programme of the freedom struggle
  [15:1:4.2] Instrument of Instructions (GoI Act, 1935) | Instructions to Governors – reborn as DPSPs
  [15:1:4.3] Sapru Committee (1945) | Split rights into justiciable and non-justiciable
  [15:1:4.4] Gandhian ideals | Village self-sufficiency, cottage industries
[15:1:5] Source country is Ireland, not the USSR (USSR = Fundamental Duties) and not the USA (USA = FRs, judicial review).
[15:1:6] - **Jammu and Kashmir:** under the **Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 1954**, **Part IV (DPSP)** did not apply to J&K. The Presidential order **C.O. 272 of 5 August 2019** superseded the 1954 Order and applied **all provisions** of the Constitution to J&K.
## SUB 2 | Article 37 – Justiciability and the Fr–dpsp Balance
[15:2:0] # FR vs DPSP
[15:2:1] TABLE head: Basis | Fundamental Rights | Directive Principles
  [15:2:1.0] Courts | Justiciable | Non-justiciable
  [15:2:1.1] Aim | Political democracy | Social and economic democracy
  [15:2:1.2] Sanction | Legal | Moral and political
  [15:2:1.3] Obligation | Mostly negative (restrain State) | Mostly positive (State must act)
  [15:2:1.4] In a conflict | Generally prevail – except 39(b) and 39(c) in some cases | Subordinate, with exceptions
[15:2:2] # Courts' View
[15:2:3] - **Minerva Mills (1980)**: the **balance between Part III and Part IV** is part of the **Basic Structure**.
[15:2:4] - Courts use DPSPs as **interpretive aids** (harmonious construction); many welfare principles are **read into Article 21**.
[15:2:5] - DPSPs are **guideposts for legislation**.
[15:2:6] APPSC G2 Mains 2025 A-R: A – DPSPs are not independently enforceable (true); R – they act as a guidepost for legislation (true). Official key: **both true and R is the correct explanation of A**.
[15:2:7] Non-enforceability flows from **Article 37's clear bar**, not merely from their role as a guide to law-making.

[15:3:2] - **Cow slaughter:** Art. 48 is only a directive; power to legislate lies with the **States** (**State List Entry 15**, livestock).
[15:3:3] # What is NOT a DPSP
[15:3:4] - **Free education up to 14 years** (now FR, Article 21A); free education up to **16 years**.
[15:3:5] - **Adult education** – DPSPs are silent on it.
[15:3:6] - **Right to Information** – a legal right under the **RTI Act, 2005**.
[15:3:7] - **Abolition of untouchability** (Article 17), prohibition of **child labour** (Article 24) and **forced labour** (Article 23) – all FRs.
[15:3:8] - **Mother-tongue education at primary level** – **Article 350A**, outside Part IV.
[15:3:9] - **Right to work** is NOT a Fundamental Right – it is a DPSP (Article 41).
[15:3:10] Article 44 (UCC) is a DPSP with no legal force; nutrition is Article 47, not 44.

  [15:4:0.1] 39(b) | Ownership and control of material resources of the community distributed to subserve the common good
  [15:4:0.2] 39(c) | Economic system not to result in concentration of wealth and means of production to the common detriment
  [15:4:0.3] 39(d) | Equal pay for equal work for both men and women

[15:4:2] - APPSC G2 Mains 2025: both the 39(b) and 39(c) statements **are** in the Constitution.
[15:4:3] - Present wording through the 42nd Amendment; Article 39 is a DPSP (Articles **24 and 21A are FRs**).

## SUB 5 | Classification – Socialist, Gandhian, Liberal-intellectual
[15:5:0] - The **Constitution does not classify** DPSPs; the three-fold scheme is an **academic grouping** for study.
[15:5:1] TABLE head: Group | Articles | Key ideas
  [15:5:1.0] Socialistic | 38, 39, 39A, 41, 42, 43, 43A, 47 | Welfare, equal pay, legal aid, right to work, living wage, workers in management
  [15:5:1.1] Gandhian (6) | 40, 43, 43B, 46, 47, 48 | Panchayats, cottage industries, co-operatives, weaker sections, prohibition, cow slaughter ban
  [15:5:1.2] Liberal-intellectual (7) | 44, 45, 48, 48A, 49, 50, 51 | UCC, ECCE, modern agriculture, environment, monuments, separation of judiciary, international peace
[15:5:2] - **43B** is Gandhian (38, 39A, 47 are socialist).
[15:5:3] - Gandhian = village panchayats, cottage industries; **NOT Gandhian** = UCC (liberal-intellectual), right to work (socialist).
[15:5:4] Overlaps: **43** and **47** sit in both Socialist and Gandhian lists; **48** in both Gandhian and Liberal-intellectual.
