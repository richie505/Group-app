## SUB 0 | Soilless and Protected Cultivation – Hydroponics, Aeroponics, Phytotron
[0:0] TABLE head: Technique | Meaning
  [0:0.0] Hydroponics | Growing plants without soil, roots in mineral nutrient solution (sand, gravel or water may support them); still needs water, light, nutrients; ~90% less water
  [0:0.1] Aeroponics | Roots suspended in air and misted – no soil medium
  [0:0.2] Aquaponics | Fish culture + hydroponics; fish waste feeds plants
  [0:0.3] Vertical farming | Stacked indoor layers, year-round output where land is scarce
  [0:0.4] Greenhouse / polyhouse | Controlled microclimate; protection from weather, pests and diseases
  [0:0.5] Phytotron | Enclosed research greenhouse to grow plants under controlled conditions
[0:1] - **Aeroponics limits disease transmission** (less plant-to-plant contact, oxygen-rich roots) – so "disease transmission is its major disadvantage" is **incorrect**; aeroponic plants have **high dry-weight biomass** and need less nutrient input than hydroponics.
[0:2] - "Hydrophonics" in old papers = hydroponics, i.e. growth **without soil**, not without water.

## SUB 1 | Micro-irrigation and Fertigation
[1:0] - **Localised (micro) irrigation:** water piped under low pressure and given in small discharges at each plant – **drip, micro-sprinkler, bubbler**; **conserves water** and **reduces water consumption** (vs flood, canal or open-field watering).
[1:1] TABLE head: Method | Efficiency | How water is applied
  [1:1.0] Drip | ~90% (highest) | To the root zone through emitters
  [1:1.1] Sprinkler | 70-80% | Through nozzles like rain; loses more to wind and evaporation
  [1:1.2] Flood / furrow | 30-50% | Surface methods
[1:2] - Drip limit = **higher initial cost**; Evergreen Revolution ("more output at lower ecological cost") – **M.S. Swaminathan**.
[1:3] # Fertigation
[1:4] - **Fertigation = applying fertiliser through irrigation water** (through the drip line); not chemigation (pesticides) or "microgation".
[1:5] - Advantages (**UPSC 2020: I, III and IV**): **controls alkalinity** of irrigation water, **more nutrients available**, **less leaching**. Efficient application of **rock phosphate** is **not** possible (it is insoluble).
[1:6] > Exam angle: micro-irrigation conserves water (×2), fertigation definition and advantages.

## SUB 2 | Resource-conserving Technologies and Crop Practices
[2:0] TABLE head: Technology | Benefit
  [2:0.0] Zero tillage | New crop sown into previous residue; soil carbon sequestration, more earthworms, less runoff
  [2:0.1] Zero/reduced tillage + crop residue on field | Conserves water (UPSC 2017: 1 and 3); gypsum before irrigation does not
  [2:0.2] Contour bunding | Soil conservation on slopes
  [2:0.3] Relay cropping | Next crop sown before the standing crop is harvested
  [2:0.4] Laser land levelling | Uniform field; saves 20-25% irrigation water
  [2:0.5] SRI (alternate wetting and drying) | Less seed, water and methane
  [2:0.6] Direct-seeded rice | No nursery or transplanting; promoted with zero-till wheat (NICRA)
  [2:0.7] Sustainable Sugarcane Initiative | Single-bud chips / bud-chip settlings
[2:1] - **Carbon sequestration (UPSC 2012):** of contour bunding, relay cropping and zero tillage, **only zero tillage** (most keys).
[2:2] - **Sugarcane (UPSC 2020: 1 and 4):** **bud-chip settlings** raised in a nursery save seed material; **tissue-culture settlings** can be used. Single-budded setts germinate worse and survive worse than multi-bud setts in bad weather.
[2:3] - **Onion** is grown by **transplanting** nursery seedlings (maize, sorghum, soyabean are sown directly).
[2:4] - **Bio-drainage:** fast-transpiring trees such as **Eucalyptus** pump out excess water from waterlogged land.
[2:5] > Exam angle: UPSC statement questions on zero tillage, water conservation, sugarcane; onion transplanting; bio-drainage plant.

## SUB 3 | Irrigation Schemes – PMKSY, Per Drop More Crop and APMIP
[3:0] - **PMKSY** (Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana): launched **1 July 2015 (2015-16)**; motto **"Har Khet Ko Pani"** + **"Per Drop More Crop"**; aims to **enhance physical access of water on farm**, **expand assured irrigation**, **reduce wastage and raise water-use efficiency**, and achieve **convergence of investment in irrigation at field level**; creates protected irrigation by **harnessing rainwater at micro level** (watershed component).
[3:1] TABLE head: Component | Fact
  [3:1.0] AIBP, Har Khet Ko Pani | Ministry of Jal Shakti (DoWR)
  [3:1.1] Per Drop More Crop (PDMC) | Agriculture Ministry; drip and sprinkler; under PMKSY 2015-16 to 2021-22, under RKVY from 2022-23
  [3:1.2] Watershed Development (WDC-PMKSY 2.0) | Department of Land Resources (Rural Development)
  [3:1.3] Micro Irrigation Fund | Rs 10,000 crore corpus with NABARD; operational 2019-20
[3:2] - PDMC: **102 lakh ha** under micro-irrigation (2015-16 to 2025-26); priority to rainfed, water-stressed and critical groundwater blocks; NMSA subsidy up to 55%, capped at 5 ha.
[3:3] - Gross irrigated area **41.7% (2001-02) → 55.8%** of GCA (2022-23); Soil Health Cards issued: **25.55 crore** (Nov 2025).
[3:4] # Andhra Pradesh Micro Irrigation Project (APMIP)
[3:5] - AP Micro Irrigation programme introduced **2003**; APMIP now implemented State-wide under **PMKSY**; **AP ranked 1st** in micro-irrigation (2024-25), led by **Ananthapuramu**.
[3:6] TABLE head: Farmer category (drip) | Subsidy
  [3:6.0] SC/ST small and marginal | 100%
  [3:6.1] Other small and marginal | 90%
  [3:6.2] 5-10 acres | 70%
  [3:6.3] Above 10 acres | 50%
[3:7] - **7 Jan 2026**: automation-based micro-irrigation at **Governorpet, Vijayawada** – Minister **K. Atchannaidu**; APSES 2024-25 focus: precision farming, drones, IoT.

## SUB 4 | Precision Agriculture, Remote Sensing and Crop Monitoring
[4:0] - **Precision farming:** data-driven use of **GIS, GPS, remote sensing**, sensors and drones to apply water, fertiliser and pesticide where and when needed; tools include fertigation, greenhouse, mulching, micropropagation, high-density planting, drip.
[4:1] TABLE head: Tool | Use
  [4:1.0] Mahalanobis National Crop Forecast Centre (2012, Agriculture Ministry) | Institutionalises ISRO's crop forecasting (FASAL) and drought/disaster methodology
  [4:1.1] KISAN project (Oct 2015) | Space tech for crop-cutting experiments under PMFBY
  [4:1.2] GIS | Soil and Land Use Survey of India maps soils
  [4:1.3] Variable-rate technology | Inputs at varying rates within a field by sensor/GIS maps
  [4:1.4] GPS, drones, AI, sensors | Second Green Revolution / sustainable intensification
[4:2] - **Crop logging** = **plant (tissue) analysis** to assess the crop's nutrient requirement during growth (developed for sugarcane), not soil testing.
[4:3] > Exam angle: MNCFC (ISRO methodology), crop logging definition.
