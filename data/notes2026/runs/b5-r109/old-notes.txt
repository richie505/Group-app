=== NOTES 5:109 | Eco-friendly Technologies in Agriculture – Ipm, Biofertilisers, Biopesticides, Organic and Natural Farming
## SUB 0 | Sustainable Agriculture – Meaning and Practices
[109:0:0] - **Sustainable agriculture**: produce food while **protecting the environment** and **conserving resources for future generations**.
[109:0:1] - It **prioritises soil health and biodiversity**.
[109:0:2] - Unlike organic farming, sustainable agriculture **may use synthetic inputs judiciously** (IPM, precision farming, conservation tillage).
[109:0:3] - Shift from the Green Revolution to the **"evergreen revolution"**: ZBNF, organic farming, water conservation, IPM.
[109:0:4] # Sustainable vs Harmful Practices
[109:0:5] TABLE head: Sustainable (exam answers) | Harmful
  [109:0:5.0] Crop rotation, organic farming | Excessive chemical fertilisers
  [109:0:5.1] Mixed / inter-cropping, green manuring, cover crops | Overuse of pesticides
  [109:0:5.2] Reduced tillage, drip irrigation, IPM | Monoculture, over-irrigation
[109:0:6] - Eco-friendly technology (PYQ answer): **organic farming** – avoids synthetic fertilisers and pesticides.
[109:0:7] IPM is a sustainable practice; monoculture and over-irrigation are harmful, even though they raise short-term output.
## SUB 1 | Agrochemicals – Environmental Impact
[109:1:0] # Fertiliser Impacts
[109:1:1] - **Synthetic fertilisers and pesticides** cause **soil and water pollution / contamination**.
[109:1:2] - **Excess nitrogen**: **soil acidification** (sours soil), **nitrate leaching** into groundwater, **eutrophication**.
[109:1:3] - **Continuous chemical fertiliser use**: decline in **soil organic matter**, fewer microbes, **lower nutrient density** in food.
[109:1:4] - **Broadcasting** granular fertiliser loses **50-70%** of applied nitrogen (as ammonia or nitrate leaching into aquifers).
[109:1:5] - Careless fertiliser use causes nitrogen loss, soil and land degradation and water pollution (**all** of these).
[109:1:6] - Careless synthetic fertilisers/pesticides cause **secondary pest outbreaks**, **pesticide resistance** and ecosystem contamination.
[109:1:7] - Pesticide residues **bioaccumulate** in the food chain.
[109:1:8] # Remedies
[109:1:9] - Organic manure, vermicompost, biofertilisers.
[109:1:10] - **Neem-coated urea**: slows **dissolution of urea and nitrification**, so nitrogen is released slowly → better **nitrogen-use efficiency**.
[109:1:11] - Nitrogen-use efficiency is raised by **split application**, **nitrogen inhibitors** and **slow-release N fertilisers** (all).
[109:1:12] - **PM-PRANAM**: States keep **50%** of the fertiliser subsidy saved, as **grants**.
[109:1:13] # Restricted Pesticide in News
[109:1:14] TABLE head: Pesticide | Fact
  [109:1:14.0] Carbosulfan | Centre proposed a ban (29 Aug 2026): breaks down to highly toxic Carbofuran; harms birds and aquatic life; recommended by the Registration Committee under the Insecticides Act, 1968
[109:1:15] Carbosulfan is the pesticide proposed for ban; Carbofuran is its toxic breakdown product.
## SUB 2 | Fertilisers, Manures and Soil Amendments
[109:2:0] # Fertiliser → Effect
[109:2:1] TABLE head: Fertiliser | Effect / fact
  [109:2:1.0] Urea | Vegetative growth (wheat and most crops); about 46% N → about 2.2 kg urea for 1 kg N
  [109:2:1.1] Phosphatic | Root development, flowering, fruiting
  [109:2:1.2] Potassic | Disease resistance, drought tolerance, quality
  [109:2:1.3] DAP | Balanced N and P supply; (NH4)2HPO4; 18% N, 46% P2O5
  [109:2:1.4] Neem-coated urea | Slow release, higher nitrogen-use efficiency
[109:2:2] # Organic Manures and Amendments
[109:2:3] TABLE head: Input | Composition | Benefit
  [109:2:3.0] Vermicompost | Organic waste digested by earthworms | Restores soil microbes and organic carbon
  [109:2:3.1] Farmyard manure | Dung, urine and litter of farm animals | Slow-release nutrients, better soil structure
  [109:2:3.2] Compost | Decomposed crop residues and organic wastes | Recycles nutrients
  [109:2:3.3] Green manure | Legumes ploughed in green – sunn hemp, dhaincha | Adds nitrogen and organic matter
  [109:2:3.4] Biochar | Charcoal from pyrolysis of biomass | Long-term carbon sequestration; water and nutrient retention
[109:2:4] - **Earthworms** improve soil fertility – called **"farmer's friends"**.
[109:2:5] - **Humus**: an **organic colloid**; dark material from decayed plant and animal matter.
[109:2:6] - **Biochar** is also part of the growing medium in **vertical farming** and helps nitrogen-fixing microorganisms.
[109:2:7] - AP plans **1,500 MT/day** of manure from **107 waste-processing centres** (Feb 2026).
[109:2:8] # Green Manure
[109:2:9] - A crop **grown and ploughed in while green** (or just after flowering); **legumes preferred** (fix nitrogen in **root nodules**) – clover, vetch, **sesbania**, sunn hemp, dhaincha.
[109:2:10] - Green manure = green **undecomposed** material used as manure: grown green-manure crops, or **green leaf** from wastelands, bunds and forests.
[109:2:11] - Adds organic matter (**humus**), fixes nitrogen → better soil structure, water retention, microbial activity.
[109:2:12] - **Sunhemp** = most suitable green manuring crop.
[109:2:13] - **Cowpea** and **green gram** = pulse, fodder and green manure (legumes).
## SUB 3 | Biofertilisers
[109:3:0] - Living microbes that add or mobilise nutrients for crops.
[109:3:1] TABLE head: Biofertiliser | Organism type | Crop / function
  [109:3:1.0] Rhizobium | Symbiotic bacterium | Legumes; root nodules fix atmospheric nitrogen
  [109:3:1.1] Azotobacter | Free-living (non-symbiotic) bacterium | Fixes nitrogen for non-legume crops
  [109:3:1.2] Azospirillum | Associative bacterium | Cereal roots; fixes nitrogen in rice rhizosphere
  [109:3:1.3] Azolla | Water fern with cyanobacterium Anabaena | Nitrogen for paddy
  [109:3:1.4] Blue-green algae | Cyanobacteria | Fix nitrogen in paddy fields
  [109:3:1.5] PSB (phosphobacteria) | Bacteria | Solubilise phosphate for crops
  [109:3:1.6] Mycorrhiza | Fungus | The fungal biofertiliser (phosphorus uptake by roots)
[109:3:2] Mycorrhiza is a fungus, not a bacterium; Azolla is a fern that works with Anabaena.
## SUB 4 | Biopesticides
[109:4:0] - **Biodegradable, low toxicity, short persistence (short-lived)** – a key IPM component.
[109:4:1] TABLE head: Biopesticide | Source | Target
  [109:4:1.0] Bt | Bacterium *Bacillus thuringiensis* | Caterpillars, bollworms
  [109:4:1.1] Trichoderma | Fungus (antagonist) | Soil-borne fungal diseases – wilt, root rot
  [109:4:1.2] NPV | Baculovirus | Helicoverpa, Spodoptera caterpillars
  [109:4:1.3] Neem | Azadirachtin | Wide range of insects (antifeedant)
  [109:4:1.4] Beauveria bassiana | Entomopathogenic fungus | Borers, whitefly, other insects
[109:4:2] Bt = bacterium; Trichoderma and Beauveria = fungi; NPV = virus.
## SUB 5 | Integrated Pest Management (ipm)
[109:5:0] - **IPM (FAO)**: use all suitable methods to keep pests **below economic injury levels**.
[109:5:1] - Aim = **optimise, not maximise**, control.
[109:5:2] # Components
[109:5:3] TABLE head: Component | Examples
  [109:5:3.0] Cultural | Crop rotation, change of irrigation
  [109:5:3.1] Mechanical | Traps, mulches
  [109:5:3.2] Biological | Natural enemies – green lacewings against aphids; pheromone traps
  [109:5:3.3] Chemical | Only when needed
[109:5:4] In IPM, chemical control is the last resort, not the first or usual line of defence.

## SUB 7 | Organic Farming – Milestones, Schemes, Certification
[109:7:0] # Sikkim
[109:7:1] - **Sikkim**: India's **first fully organic State** and the world's **first 100% organic State** (**2016**; process began **2003**).
[109:7:2] - Won the **Future Policy Gold Award, 2018 (FAO)**.
[109:7:3] - PM cited Sikkim's model at its **50th Statehood** celebrations (May 2026).
[109:7:4] - NE organic-farming states: **Sikkim, Meghalaya, Nagaland**; **Assam not among them**.
[109:7:5] # Schemes and Certification
[109:7:6] TABLE head: Scheme / system | Launch | Key facts
  [109:7:6.0] PKVY (Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana) | 2015-16 | First comprehensive CSS for organic farming; clusters of 50 farmers / 50 acres; 15.08 lakh ha, 25.38 lakh farmers, Rs 2,524.68 crore released (to 31 Jul 2025); jaivikkheti.in portal
  [109:7:6.1] MOVCDNER | 2015-16 | Organic value chains in the North-Eastern Region; PKVY covers the rest
  [109:7:6.2] PGS-India | – | Participatory, farmer-group certification for the domestic market
  [109:7:6.3] NPOP | 2001 | Third-party certification, mainly for exports; run by Ministry of Commerce and Industry; implemented by APEDA
[109:7:7] Certification: PGS-India | Farmer groups | Domestic market
NPOP | Third-party | Exports (APEDA)
[109:7:8] NPOP is under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, not Rural Development or Agriculture.
## SUB 8 | Natural Farming and Permaculture
[109:8:0] - **Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF)**: developed by **Subhash Palekar**.
[109:8:1] - **Permaculture**: coined by **Bill Mollison and David Holmgren** (Australia).
[109:8:2] - Natural farming **cuts cultivation cost by 20-40%**; it is **knowledge-intensive** and yields may **dip during transition**.
[109:8:3] # Inputs and Practices
[109:8:4] TABLE head: Input / practice | Meaning
  [109:8:4.0] Jeevamrutha | Microbial culture from cow dung and urine
  [109:8:4.1] Beejamrutha | Seed treatment
  [109:8:4.2] Achhadana | Mulching
  [109:8:4.3] Whapasa | Soil aeration and moisture
[109:8:5] # National Mission on Natural Farming (NMNF)
[109:8:6] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [109:8:6.0] Approved | 25 Nov 2024
  [109:8:6.1] Outlay | Rs 2,481 crore (Centre 1,584 + States 897) till March 2026
  [109:8:6.2] Coverage | 8.80 lakh ha, 18.19 lakh farmers, 17,632 clusters (by March 2026)
  [109:8:6.3] Field workers | 70,021 Krishi Sakhis
[109:8:7] - **BPKP** (Bharatiya Prakritik Krishi Paddhati) also promoted natural farming.
[109:8:8] # Organic vs Natural Farming
[109:8:9] TABLE head: Feature | Organic farming | Natural farming
  [109:8:9.0] Inputs | Approved organic inputs (may be bought) | On-farm, cow-based inputs
  [109:8:9.1] Certification | Certified (PGS / NPOP) | No same certification
  [109:8:9.2] Example scheme | PKVY, MOVCDNER | NMNF, APCNF
## SUB 9 | Andhra Pradesh – Community-managed Natural Farming (apcnf)
[109:9:0] - **APCNF**: formerly **Zero Budget Natural Farming**; bio-inputs **jeevamrutham, ghanajeevamrutham**; **over 7 lakh farmers** (Feb 2026).
[109:9:1] - Implementing agency: **Rythu Sadhikara Samstha (RySS)**, a not-for-profit body under the AP Department of Agriculture (named implementing agency by a 2016 government order).
[109:9:2] - **2018** = AP's **Year of Natural Farming**; promoted ZBNF.
[109:9:3] - Pesticide reduction: a **Kurnool** study found APCNF farmers about **35% less likely to use pesticides** than conventional farmers, though about half still used some.
[109:9:4] - Global recognition: **Gulbenkian Prize for Humanity, 2024**.
[109:9:5] - **8 Jul 2026**: **Kenyan delegation** praised APCNF as a climate-resilient model after visiting **Guntur** farms (20+ crop plots; trap crops **marigold, castor**).
[109:9:6] - **NTR district**: natural-farming model with **QR-based traceability** for organic produce.
[109:9:7] - Feb 2026: **200 new certified PKVY organic clusters** in tribal **Visakhapatnam**; PKVY is AP's initiative on sustainable and organic farming.
[109:9:8] - **Kharif 2026 green manure**: Rs 62.20 crore subsidy; 91,918 quintals of seed, of which **sunn hemp (Jilledu) 50,311** quintals.

## SUB 0 | Micro-irrigation – Drip vs Sprinkler
[110:0:0] - **Evergreen Revolution**: "more output at **lower ecological cost**" – **M.S. Swaminathan**.
[110:0:1] - **Localised irrigation**: low-pressure piped water given in small amounts near each plant – drip, micro-sprinkler, bubbler.
[110:0:2] TABLE head: Method | Efficiency | How water is applied
  [110:0:2.0] Drip | ~90% (highest) | To the root zone through emitters on lateral pipes
  [110:0:2.1] Sprinkler | 70-80% | Pressurised water through nozzles, like simulated rainfall
  [110:0:2.2] Flood / furrow | 30-50% | Surface methods
[110:0:3] - Drip: most efficient, cuts evaporation and runoff; limit = **higher initial cost**.
[110:0:4] - Sprinkler: suits uneven terrain; loses more to **wind and evaporation** (water travels through the air).
[110:0:5] - **Drip irrigation = best for water-scarce regions** (APPSC G2 2025); micro-irrigation **conserves water** in agriculture.
[110:0:6] - Savings of water, labour, power and fertiliser.
[110:0:7] # Fertigation
[110:0:8] - **Fertiliser applied through irrigation water** (through the drip line).
[110:0:9] - Advantages (UPSC 2020): **controls alkalinity** of water, **more nutrients available**, **less leaching**.
[110:0:10] Efficient application of rock phosphate is NOT an advantage of fertigation.
