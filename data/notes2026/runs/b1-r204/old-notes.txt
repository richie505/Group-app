=== NOTES 1:204 | Extremists – Lal-bal-pal and Aurobindo; Ideology; Surat Split 1907
## SUB 0 | Moderates vs Extremists
[204:0:0] - **Extremists** = **Assertive Nationalists**, the aggressive wing of the Congress; influence grew after **1906**.
[204:0:1] - Goal: **Swaraj** (complete freedom), more immediate than the Moderates' gradual self-government.
[204:0:2] - They rejected the **"politics of prayer, petition and protest"**; Tilak blamed the Congress for it, and the prayer-and-petition policy ended under him.
[204:0:3] # Comparison
[204:0:4] TABLE head: Basis | Moderates | Extremists
  [204:0:4.0] Period | 1885–1905 | 1905–1916 (Assertive nationalism)
  [204:0:4.1] Goal | Self-government within the Empire, gradual reform | Swaraj: complete freedom
  [204:0:4.2] Method | 3 Ps: prayers, petitions, protests; constitutional agitation | 3 Ss: self-reliance, self-sacrifice, strong will; mass agitation
  [204:0:4.3] View of British | Faith in British justice and liberalism | British rule exploitative; no faith in British generosity
  [204:0:4.4] Swadeshi–Boycott | Unwilling to extend it | Wanted it wider, all-India
  [204:0:4.5] Social reform | Pro-reform | Comparatively orthodox (Tilak opposed the Age of Consent Bill)
[204:0:5] 3 Ps = Moderates; 3 Ss = Extremists.
[204:0:6] # Classification of leaders
[204:0:7] - **Moderates:** Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Gokhale, S.N. Banerjee, Ranade, W.C. Bonnerjee, R.C. Dutt, Anand Mohan Bose, G.V. Joshi.
[204:0:8] - **Extremists:** Tilak, Lajpat Rai, Bipin Chandra Pal, Aurobindo Ghosh (the **four pillars**: Lal, Bal, Pal, Ghose).
[204:0:9] TABLE head: Regional group | Leader
  [204:0:9.0] Maharashtra | Bal Gangadhar Tilak
  [204:0:9.1] Bengal | Bipin Chandra Pal (and Aurobindo)
  [204:0:9.2] Punjab | Lala Lajpat Rai
[204:0:10] - **Swadeshi in Delhi:** Syed Haidar Raja.
[204:0:11] APPSC 2018: the militant leader is **Tilak**; Ranade, Gokhale and G.V. Joshi were Moderates. Lajpat Rai and Tilak were **not** Moderates.
## SUB 1 | Extremist Ideology
[204:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [204:1:0.0] Swaraj | Complete freedom from British control, not colonial self-government
  [204:1:0.1] Passive resistance | Boycott, Swadeshi, national education, mass agitation, self-sacrifice; doctrine set out by Aurobindo in *Bande Mataram*
  [204:1:0.2] Spiritual nationalism | Nation as mother goddess (Bande Mataram, *Bhavani Mandir*); Hindu festivals for mass contact; pride in India's past
  [204:1:0.3] Atmashakti | Self-reliance; constructive work such as cooperatives
[204:1:1] - Ideology: self-government by **aggressive means** instead of petitions and constitutional ways.
[204:1:2] - **Contribution:** made nationalism a **mass movement** and Swaraj the goal.
[204:1:3] - Word **Swaraj** was first used by **Dayanand Saraswati**; as a political demand it is linked with **Tilak** ("Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it").
[204:1:4] - **Criticism:** the Extremists' Hindu religious symbols (Bhavani worship, Ganesh and Shivaji festivals) kept many Muslims away, and their social outlook was comparatively orthodox.

## SUB 3 | Bal Gangadhar Tilak
[204:3:0] - Born **23 July 1856, Ratnagiri**; joined Congress **1890**; **never INC President**.
[204:3:1] - With **Agarkar**: New English School, **Deccan Education Society** → **Fergusson College**.
[204:3:2] - **Kesari** (Marathi, vernacular) and **Mahratta** (English), both **1881**; Agarkar an early editor.
[204:3:3] - Revived festivals: public **Ganesh Utsav (1893)**, **Shivaji Jayanti (1895)**.
[204:3:4] - **June 1897:** **Chapekar brothers** killed **Rand** (Plague Committee, Poona) and **Lt Ayerst**; Tilak accused of conspiracy.
[204:3:5] # Sedition trials
[204:3:6] TABLE head: Year | Outcome
  [204:3:6.0] 1897 | 18 months; Max Muller pleaded for him ("my interest in Tilak is that of a Sanskrit scholar")
  [204:3:6.1] 1908 | 6 years in Mandalay (Burma); released 16 June 1914
  [204:3:6.2] 1916 | Acquitted
[204:3:7] - Books: **Gita Rahasya** (Marathi, written in **Mandalay jail**); **The Arctic Home in the Vedas** (Arctic/North Pole as Aryan homeland).
[204:3:8] - Quote: "If God were to tolerate untouchability, I would not recognise him as God."
[204:3:9] - **Home Rule League:** **28 April 1916**; president Joseph Baptista; Besant's League **September 1916**.
[204:3:10] - Backed Non-Cooperation but died **1 August 1920**; bier lifted by **Gandhi, Shaukat Ali, Saifuddin Kitchlew**; elegy by **Hasrat Mohani**.
## SUB 4 | Lajpat Rai and Bipin Chandra Pal
[204:4:0] # Lala Lajpat Rai (1865–1928)
[204:4:1] - **Arya Samaj** leader; political guru **Mazzini**; translated *The Duty of Man* into Urdu.
[204:4:2] - **Deported to Mandalay, 1907**, with **Ajit Singh**.
[204:4:3] - Went to the **USA** after 1908; **Young India** (USA, 1916); **Indian Home Rule League of America (1917)**.
[204:4:4] - Works: biographies of **Mazzini, Garibaldi, Shivaji, Shri Krishna, Dayanand**; *The Story of My Deportation* (1908); *Arya Samaj* (1915); *The United States of America: A Hindu's Impression*; **Unhappy India** (India vs USA and Britain).
[204:4:5] - Lahore 1920: Urdu daily **Bande Mataram**, English weekly **The People**; also **The Punjabee**.
[204:4:6] - **Only one of Lal-Bal-Pal to be INC President** – special session, **Calcutta 1920**; elected to the Central Assembly.
[204:4:7] - First used the **safety-valve theory** (in *Young India*) to attack Moderates: Congress was **Dufferin's** idea.
[204:4:8] - Called Congress sessions the "**Annual National Festival of Educated Indians**"; said Congress "was neither inspired by the people, nor devised or planned by them".
[204:4:9] - Slogan "**Go back Simon**"; lathi-charge at **Lahore**; died **17 November 1928**.
[204:4:10] # Bipin Chandra Pal (1858–1932)
[204:4:11] - **Brahmo Samaj** member; called Congress a "**Begging Institute**".
[204:4:12] - Weekly **New India (1901)**; *The Soul of India*; co-editor of *Bande Mataram*.
[204:4:13] - **April 1907: toured Andhra**, promoting the Vande Mataram movement.
[204:4:14] - **Arrested 1907** for refusing to testify in the *Bande Mataram* case; **not deported to Mandalay**.
[204:4:15] - Lived in **England 1908–1911**.
[204:4:16] Abroad / away: Lajpat Rai = **USA**; Pal = **England**; Aurobindo = **Pondicherry**; Tilak = **Mandalay**.
## SUB 5 | Aurobindo Ghosh (15 Aug 1872 – 5 Dec 1950)
[204:5:0] - **New Lamps for Old** (**1893–94**): nine articles in **Indu Prakash** (Bombay); attacked Moderates; Congress cut off from the proletariat.
[204:5:1] - Editor of **Bande Mataram** (with Pal); criticised the partition there; first principal of **Bengal National College**.
[204:5:2] - **1908:** arrested after the **Maniktalla Garden** raid; about a year as undertrial in the **Alipore Bomb Case**; **acquitted 1909**; retired to **Pondicherry 1910**.
[204:5:3] - Political career **1901–1910**; "Political freedom is the life-breath of a nation."
[204:5:4] - Works: *Bhavani Mandir*, *Savitri*, **The Life Divine**, *Essays on the Gita*, *The Synthesis of Yoga*, *The Ideal of Human Unity*, *The Foundation of Indian Culture*.

## SUB 7 | Calcutta 1906 and Surat Split 1907
[204:7:0] # Calcutta 1906
[204:7:1] - President **Dadabhai Naoroji**; split averted by compromise.
[204:7:2] - **Four resolutions:** **Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education**.
[204:7:3] **Annulment of the partition** was NOT one of the four Calcutta resolutions.
[204:7:4] # Surat 1907 (December) – first split of the INC
[204:7:5] - President: **Rash Behari Ghosh** (Moderates' choice).
[204:7:6] TABLE head: Side | Leaders | Demand
  [204:7:6.0] Extremists (Garam Dal) | Tilak | Tilak or Lajpat Rai as president; keep the 1906 resolutions; boycott all-India
  [204:7:6.1] Moderates (Naram Dal) | Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta | Drop the 1906 resolutions; keep the movement to Bengal
[204:7:7] - **Immediate cause:** quarrel over the four Calcutta resolutions.
[204:7:8] - **Basic cause (UPSC 2016):** Extremists' **lack of faith in Moderates' ability to bargain**.
[204:7:9] - Outcome: Moderates (majority) pushed Extremists out; scuffle; session suspended. By **1910** Moderates controlled Congress (Tilak jailed, Aurobindo retired, Pal arrested, Lajpat Rai away).
[204:7:10] - **Reunion: Lucknow 1916.**
[204:7:11] - The **Swadeshi Movement** (reaction to Curzon's partition) helped cause the split; partition did not end the Moderates' role.
[204:7:12] Calcutta 1906 = Naoroji; Surat 1907 = Rash Behari Ghosh; Benaras 1905 = Gokhale.
