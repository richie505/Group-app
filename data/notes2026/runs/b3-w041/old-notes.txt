=== NOTES 3:41 | Rural Credit and Rural Non-farm Sector; Migration and Rural Distress
## SUB 0 | MGNREGA, Migration and Rural Distress
[41:0:0] # MGNREGA and distress
[41:0:1] - **NREGA** passed 2005, in force 2006, renamed **MGNREGA 2009**: **100 days** per household; wages within **15 days** (not 30); **one-third** women. Now replaced by **VB-G RAM G**.
[41:0:2] - **Positive results:** **higher purchasing power of the rural poor** and **less distress migration** to cities; it **raised** (not reduced) labourers' bargaining power.
[41:0:3] - Person-days **389.09 crore (2020-21) → 183.77 crore** (2025-26, to Dec 2025); urban wages **1.7-2 times** rural (ES 2025-26).
[41:0:4] - AP: **125 days** wage work in distressed pockets under VB-G RAM G (Jun 2026).
[41:0:5] # Migration
[41:0:6] TABLE head: Push (rural) | Pull (urban)
  [41:0:6.0] Few jobs, low wages, landlessness, crop failure, debt | High labour demand, better wages
  [41:0:6.1] Poor health and schools | Education, healthcare
  [41:0:6.2] Unbalanced rural-urban development | Industry and services
[41:0:7] - Causes of rural-urban migration: urban labour demand, unbalanced development, few rural jobs – **all of the above**.
[41:0:8] - **Distress migration:** forced by debt, landlessness, crop loss. **Seasonal:** for a season, then return. **26.8%** of rural Indians were migrants (2020-21).
[41:0:9] - **AP:** distress belts **Rayalaseema, Srikakulam**; Gulf migration from **YSR Kadapa, Godavari/Konaseema**; **93.2%** agricultural households indebted (highest, NSSO 77th round); ~**50%** of AP farmers depend on non-institutional credit (NABARD; not 25%).
[41:0:10] # Rural connectivity gap
[41:0:11] - **TRAI:** urban teledensity is about **three times** rural teledensity (APPSC key; e.g. urban ~155 vs rural ~57 in 2018).
[41:0:12] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 10th ed. part 04 p.165 (MGNREGA 100 days for food security); app notes 3:41 sub 4, 5, 7
## SUB 1 | Rural Credit – History, Surveys and Sources
[41:1:0] # History and surveys
[41:1:1] - **1951:** **moneylenders ~70%** of rural credit; institutional (government, cooperatives, banks) only about **5-7%** – the "modern lending institutions" share asked in APPSC = **about 5%**.
[41:1:2] - **All India Rural Credit Survey Committee** (RBI, 1951; report 1954), chair **A.D. Gorwala**: "**Rural credit has failed, but cooperation must succeed**" → **SBI (1 July 1955)** from the **Imperial Bank of India**.
[41:1:3] - **1969:** social banking and multi-agency approach; **NABARD (1982)** coordinates rural finance – the **apex rural development bank**.
[41:1:4] - **AIDIS 2019** (NSS **77th round**): rural debt **66% institutional**, 34% non-institutional.
[41:1:5] - **NAFIS 2021-22** (NABARD): rural household income **₹12,698**/month; indebted households 47.4% → **52.0%**.
[41:1:6] # Sources
[41:1:7] TABLE head: Institutional | Non-institutional
  [41:1:7.0] Commercial banks, RRBs, cooperatives/PACS, Small Finance Banks, SHG-bank linkage | Moneylenders, traders, landlords, relatives
[41:1:8] - **Kisan Credit Card:** valid **5 years**; open to **tenants, sharecroppers, SHGs, JLGs** (not only landowners).
[41:1:9] - **Small Finance Banks:** lend to small businesses, small and marginal farmers, rural entrepreneurs (not to financial-sector regulators).
[41:1:10] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 05 p.20 (moneylenders and debt trap, 1969 social banking, NABARD 1982); Rocket Indian Economy p.3 (SBI 1 July 1955 from Imperial Bank, NABARD 1982); app notes 3:41 sub 0, 1
[41:1:11] - **AIDIS (NSS 70th round, debt as on 30 June 2012):** average debt of a rural household **₹32,522** (urban ₹84,625) – nearest option ₹25,000.
## SUB 2 | Rural Non-farm Sector and Feminisation of Agriculture
[41:2:0] # Non-farm sector
[41:2:1] - Components: rural industries, Khadi & Village Industries, handloom/handicrafts, food processing, trade, transport, services (**not crop cultivation**).
[41:2:2] TABLE head: Body / scheme | Role
  [41:2:2.0] KVIC | Statutory, KVI Act 1956; Ministry of MSME; implements PMEGP
  [41:2:2.1] SVEP | Under DAY-NRLM; funds rural non-farm enterprises
  [41:2:2.2] MSMEs | 8.7 crore units, ~31.1% of GDP
  [41:2:2.3] DDU-GKY | Placement-linked skilling; parliamentary panel flags low wages and drop-outs
[41:2:3] # Feminisation of agriculture
[41:2:4] - Rising share of women among cultivators, labourers and unpaid family workers as **men move to non-farm/urban work** (male out-migration); participation, not land ownership.
[41:2:5] - **Claudia Goldin** (2023 Nobel): **U-shaped curve** – high in the 1980s, fell 2004-05 to 2012, rising after 2020.
[41:2:6] - Rural women = **80%** of the rise in female workers (2017-18 to 2023-24).

## SUB 3 | Informal Debt, Debt Trap and Microfinance
[41:3:0] TABLE head: Debt type | Meaning
  [41:3:0.0] Temporary | Seasonal, repaid after harvest
  [41:3:0.1] Chronic | Long-standing
  [41:3:0.2] Debt trap | Taking a new loan (debt) to repay an old one – for a household or a country
[41:3:1] - Usurious interest → distress sale of land/gold, bonded-labour-like ties; often the trigger in farmer suicides.
[41:3:2] - **Microfinance route (DAY-NRLM):** **SHGs, JLGs, BC Sakhis** (not moneylenders); Kerala's **Kudumbashree** (1995 thrift and credit society).

## SUB 6 | Causes and Social Effects of Urbanisation
[42:6:0] - Main component: **migration** (natural increase similar in rural and urban areas).
[42:6:1] - **Push**: monsoon failure, rural unemployment, **lack of rural jobs**, non-growing farm land, **rural-urban imbalance**.
[42:6:2] - **Pull**: **magnetic features of cities** – jobs, public facilities (health, education), transport, industrial growth.
[42:6:3] - **Philip M. Hauser** – "**Population Implosion**" = rural-urban concentration of people. "**New Population Bomb**" = rapidly growing **Third World urban population**.
[42:6:4] - **Crime rises with urbanisation and industrialisation** – industrial-urban life causes family and cultural disorganisation (UPPSC A-R: R explains A).
[42:6:5] > Statement sets mixing push and pull factors are usually **all correct**.

## SUB 4 | JNNURM and Earlier Urban Schemes
[43:4:0] - Announced in Budget 2005-06; launched **3 December 2005**; a **7-year** **urban renewal programme**.

## SUB 5 | Learning Theories, Learning Curve and Behaviour Therapy
[44:5:0] - **Gagne's hierarchy of learning:** signal (conditioned-reflex) → stimulus-response → chaining → verbal association → discrimination → concept → rule → **problem-solving (highest)**.
[44:5:1] - **Guthrie's contiguity theory:** we forget **not by disuse but by other (new) learning** – interference.
[44:5:2] - **Plateau** in the learning curve: a period of no visible progress when the learner is **consolidating previous learning and preparing for further learning** – not the final stage.
[44:5:3] - **Bandura** – social (observational) learning theory, **Bobo doll** experiment (1961); rats/pigeons = Skinner, dog = Pavlov.
[44:5:4] - **Assimilated learning:** a group learns something and **its behaviour and attitudes change** as a result (printed key).
[44:5:5] - **Behaviour therapy:** counselling based on **learning theories and principles** (conditioning).
[44:5:6] - **Motives** are not observed directly – they are **inferences from behaviour**.
[44:5:7] - **Classification / sorting** by similarities and differences develops **cognitive** skills (Piaget).
[44:5:8] - **Word mapping errors:** **under-extension** = defining a word too narrowly ('dog' only for the family pet); **over-extension** = using it too broadly.

## SUB 8 | Human Capital Formation – Meaning, Means and Indicators
[44:8:0] - **Human capital** = the **stock of skill and productive knowledge** (and health) embodied in people; population becomes human capital when there is investment in **education, training and medical care**.
[44:8:1] - **Human capital formation** = a process of **increasing the knowledge, skill levels and capacities** of the people – **intangible wealth** (UPSC 2018 key: "2 and 4"); it is not accumulation of tangible wealth or of more (physical) capital.
[44:8:2] - **Means:** investment in **education, health, on-the-job training, migration and information**. **Skill development** programmes enhance **human capital** (not physical, working or fixed capital). **Purchase of machinery** = physical capital; **irrigation** has no direct impact on human capital.
[44:8:3] - **Returns:** investment in human capital **yields returns in the future** because **education and health make people more productive** (UPPSC 2025 A-R: both true, R explains A). India's IT revolution shows human capital ranking above plant and machinery.
[44:8:4] - **Kerala:** low **infant mortality rate** because of **adequate provision of basic health and educational facilities** (APPSC 2025).
[44:8:5] - **Economic Survey 2025-26** (ch. 11): shift from **access to quality**, learning outcomes and **preventive care**; new concerns obesity and digital addiction.
[44:8:6] - Indicators cited: **MMR 88** per lakh live births; **stunting 29.3%**, wasting 19%.
[44:8:7] > Human capital = people (education, health, skills); physical capital = machines, buildings. Statement items test "intangible" and "process of raising knowledge and skills".

=== NOTES 3:45 | Indian Agriculture – Importance, Land Use Classification, Cropping Seasons and Cropping Pattern
## SUB 0 | Cropping Seasons – Kharif, Rabi and Zaid
[45:0:0] # Three crop seasons
[45:0:1] TABLE head: Season | Sowing | Harvest | Major crops
  [45:0:1.0] Kharif | June-July (SW monsoon onset) | Sep-Oct / Oct-Nov | Rice (paddy), maize, jowar, bajra, cotton, groundnut, soybean, jute, sugarcane, tur/arhar
  [45:0:1.1] Rabi | Oct-Dec (after monsoon) | March-April | Wheat (the most important rabi crop), barley, gram, mustard, peas, linseed, potato
  [45:0:1.2] Zaid | March-June (summer) | June-July | Watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder
[45:0:2] - **Crop seasons:** Kharif, Rabi, Zaid-Kharif and Zaid-Rabi together denote the **crop seasons** of India; besides Rabi and Kharif the **third season is Zaid** ("Track", Barsati, Sharad, Jhum are not seasons).
[45:0:3] - **Rabi** depends on winter rain from **western disturbances** + irrigation; mustard is sown Oct-Nov and harvested Feb-Mar.
[45:0:4] - **Zaid:** short, irrigation-dependent summer window **between rabi and kharif**; products are mainly **vegetables and fruits** (watermelon). Sub-seasons: Zaid Kharif (Aug-Sep sowing), Zaid Rabi (Feb-Mar sowing).
[45:0:5] - **Sugarcane:** annual/long-duration crop, placed on the **Kharif** side in exam keys (barley, gram, wheat are Rabi).
[45:0:6] - **Jowar** is mainly a kharif crop (also some rabi jowar in the Deccan) – the usual "NOT a major rabi crop" answer against linseed, gram, wheat.
[45:0:7] - **Pulses:** **tur/arhar = kharif**; **gram/chana = rabi**. Millets mainly kharif.
[45:0:8] - **Kharif set (UPSC 2004):** cotton, groundnut, maize = kharif; mustard = rabi.
[45:0:9] - **Monsoon season:** the SW monsoon season runs from about **mid-June to mid-September** (kharif sowing window).
[45:0:10] # Crops grown in both seasons (AP)
[45:0:11] - AP: **paddy, maize, jowar, bajra and pulses** are grown in both kharif and rabi; AP 2024-25 paddy: 14.04 lakh ha kharif + 6.93 lakh ha rabi.
[45:0:12] > Match pattern: Rabi – wheat; Kharif – paddy; Zaid – watermelon; sunshine = a natural resource. Jute and cotton are Kharif, never Rabi.
[45:0:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 20 p.8 (Zaid Kharif / Zaid Rabi); OnlyIAS NCERT Human Geography 05 p.1; app notes 3:45
[45:0:14] - India is the world's **second-largest wheat producer** (after China).

## SUB 5 | Crop Classification – Food, Cash and Mixed Farming
[45:5:0] TABLE head: Group | Crops

  [45:5:0.1] Oilseeds | Groundnut, mustard, soybean, sunflower, sesame
  [45:5:0.2] Cash / commercial | Cotton, jute, sugarcane, tobacco, rubber, plantation crops, groundnut
[45:5:1] - **Cash crop:** grown for sale, not self-consumption – **cotton** (textiles), jute, groundnut, sugarcane, tobacco, rubber. **Jowar** is a food crop, not a cash crop; in the UPPSC key **soyabean** is also treated as "not a cash crop" (oilseed/pulse-type food crop).
[45:5:2] - Rice, maize, wheat = **food crops**.
[45:5:3] - AP traditional food crops: paddy, jowar, bajra, ragi, pulses; traditional commercial: **groundnut, cotton, tobacco, sugarcane**.
[45:5:4] - **Commercialisation of agriculture:** producing crops **for sale** (market) rather than for **own consumption**; shown when non-food crop area grows faster than food-crop area.
[45:5:5] - **Mixed farming:** **cultivation of crops together with rearing of animals** (milch cattle); raising several crops together is mixed/multiple cropping, not mixed farming.
[45:5:6] - **GM mustard** (DMH-11) was cleared by GEAC as safe (2017 recommendation; environmental release nod 2022, matter in the Supreme Court); **Bt cotton (2002)** is the only commercial GM crop; Bt brinjal under moratorium since 2010.

[46:0:5] - **Tanks** matter in **AP, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu** – undulating **hard-rock**, impervious terrain with poor groundwater, where wells fail; hence tank and well maps are **negatively related**.

## SUB 1 | PMKSY and Watershed Development
[46:1:0] # PMKSY
[46:1:1] - Launched **1 July 2015**; umbrella irrigation scheme; motto: **"Har Khet Ko Pani"** and **"More crop per drop"** – raise **water-use efficiency**.
[46:1:2] - Components: **AIBP**, **Har Khet Ko Pani** (assured irrigation; CAD and water management), **Per Drop More Crop** (micro-irrigation; now under PM-RKVY), **Watershed Development**. **National Water Grid is NOT** a component.
[46:1:3] - Objectives: **convergence of investment** in irrigation at field level, **expand cultivable area under irrigation**, **improve on-farm water-use efficiency**; works: renovate water bodies, rainwater-harvesting structures, **recharge of aquifers**. **Not** an objective: protecting farmers against crop failure (that is crop insurance – PMFBY); "construction of wells" is the item keyed as incorrect.
[46:1:4] - **PMKSY planning:** District Irrigation Plans → State Irrigation Plans; **State Level Sanctioning Committee chaired by the Chief Secretary**.
[46:1:5] - **Micro Irrigation Fund**: corpus **Rs 5,000 crore** with **NABARD**.
[46:1:6] # Watershed
[46:1:7] - **Hariyali** (launched January 2003; guidelines from **1 April 2003**): watershed / rainwater-harvesting scheme = **water management**.
[46:1:8] - Hariyali, **IWDP** (Integrated Wasteland Development Programme, 1989-90 nationally; 100% central assistance) and NWDPRA merged into **IWMP** (2008); IWMP subsumed in PMKSY as **WDC-PMKSY** (2015); funding **90:10 → 60:40**; **WDC 2.0** adds springsheds.
[46:1:9] - **Neeranchal National Watershed Project**: **World Bank**; **nine States incl. AP and Telangana**.
[46:1:10] - **Watershed Development Fund:** NABARD, 1999-2000, corpus Rs 200 crore.
[46:1:11] - **Tarun Bharat Sangh**: **Rajasthan** NGO of **Dr Rajendra Singh** ("Waterman", johads).
[46:1:12] - **Four Waters Concept** (T. Hanumantha Rao): **rain water, surface water, ground water and soil moisture** – "river water" is not one of the four.
[46:1:13] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 07 p.51 (Har Khet Ko Pani, Per Drop More Crop); AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.128; app notes 3:46 sub 3; general knowledge (IWDP, four waters)
[46:1:14] - **Mukhyamantri Jal Swavlamban Abhiyan** (January 2016): **Rajasthan** – water self-reliant villages through water harvesting.
[46:1:15] - **PMKSY funding:** Centre-State **60:40** (90:10 for North-Eastern and Himalayan States).

## SUB 3 | Groundwater – CGWB, Aquifers and Atal Bhujal
[46:3:0] - **Aquifers:** clay holds water but its pores are tiny and poorly connected → **poor aquifer**; sand and gravel are good aquifers.
[46:3:1] - **CGWA** was constituted under **Section 3(3) of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.
[46:3:2] - CGWB categories (stage of extraction): **Safe, Semi-critical, Critical, Over-exploited**, + Saline.
[46:3:3] - India stage: **60.63% (2025)**; high utilisation in **Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu**, Delhi; low in high-rainfall Odisha.
[46:3:4] - **Uttar Pradesh** has the largest **replenishable groundwater** resource (Ganga alluvium).
[46:3:5] - **GRACE** satellite studies: severe depletion in **north-west India** – Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan and **Delhi** (epicentre named in the APPSC 2019 key).
[46:3:6] - **Atal Bhujal Yojana**: **December 2019**; **World Bank**-aided **Central Sector**; **Ministry of Jal Shakti**; 7 States – Gujarat, Haryana, Karnataka, MP, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, UP; **AP NOT included**; **demand-side** community management via GP Water Security Plans.
[46:3:7] - **Piezometers** (digital water-level recorders with telemetry) give real-time groundwater levels (AP: 1,810 piezometers).
[46:3:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 07 p.54 (CGWB mandate); AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.132 (piezometers); app notes 3:46 sub 4
[46:3:9] - **Groundwater recharge:** infiltrated water passes through the **unsaturated (vadose) zone** and joins the **water table**.
[46:3:10] - **Atal Bhujal Yojana outlay: Rs 6,000 crore** (50% World Bank loan), 2020-21 to 2024-25.

  [46:4:1.2] C | Solarisation of grid-connected pumps, incl. feeder-level

[46:4:3] - **Drip irrigation:** water straight to the root zone; fertiliser through the same lines (fertigation).
[46:4:4] - **APMIP** (since **2003**): AP ranks **1st** in micro-irrigation; **Rayalaseema 64%** of area; AP has **converged MGNREGA with the AP Micro Irrigation Project**.

## SUB 0 | Water-use Efficiency and Conservation
[47:0:0] TABLE head: Method | Efficiency
  [47:0:0.0] Drip | ~90% (highest)
  [47:0:0.1] Sprinkler | ~70-80%
  [47:0:0.2] Flood/basin/canal surface | 30-50% (lowest)
[47:0:1] - **PMKSY** aims at enhancing **water-use efficiency** ("More crop per drop"), not organic, hybrid or GM seeds.
[47:0:2] - **Micro-irrigation**: water straight to the root zone; cuts evaporation and runoff. **DSR** and **SRI**: save up to 50% water.
[47:0:3] - **Zero / minimum tillage**: lower evaporation, better soil moisture. **Crop-residue mulch** keeps soil moisture.
[47:0:4] - **Conservation Agriculture** (FAO): minimum soil disturbance, **permanent soil cover (at least 30%)**, crop diversification.
[47:0:5] - **Bench terracing**: most widely adopted mechanical soil and water conservation measure on **hill slopes**.
[47:0:6] - **Virtual water** (Tony Allan): water **consumed in producing a product**; exporting rice exports virtual water.
[47:0:7] - **Flow duration curve:** plots discharge against the **percentage of time it is equalled or exceeded**.
[47:0:8] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:47 sub 1; general knowledge (bench terracing, flow duration curve)
[47:0:9] - **Khadin** = runoff-harvesting earthen embankment of the **Jaisalmer** region (Rajasthan, since the 15th century, Paliwal Brahmins); the moist bed is cropped. Kund = covered pit; johad = small check dam.
[47:0:10] - **Bioswales:** shallow vegetated channels that collect, slow and filter **stormwater (rainwater) runoff**.
