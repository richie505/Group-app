## SUB 0 | Cropping Seasons – Kharif, Rabi and Zaid
[0:0] # Three crop seasons
[0:1] TABLE head: Season | Sowing | Harvest | Major crops
  [0:1.0] Kharif | June-July (SW monsoon onset) | Sep-Oct / Oct-Nov | Rice (paddy), maize, jowar, bajra, cotton, groundnut, soybean, jute, sugarcane, tur/arhar
  [0:1.1] Rabi | Oct-Dec (after monsoon) | March-April | Wheat (the most important rabi crop), barley, gram, mustard, peas, linseed, potato
  [0:1.2] Zaid | March-June (summer) | June-July | Watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder
[0:2] - **Crop seasons:** Kharif, Rabi, Zaid-Kharif and Zaid-Rabi together denote the **crop seasons** of India; besides Rabi and Kharif the **third season is Zaid** ("Track", Barsati, Sharad, Jhum are not seasons).
[0:3] - **Rabi** depends on winter rain from **western disturbances** + irrigation; mustard is sown Oct-Nov and harvested Feb-Mar.
[0:4] - **Zaid:** short, irrigation-dependent summer window **between rabi and kharif**; products are mainly **vegetables and fruits** (watermelon). Sub-seasons: Zaid Kharif (Aug-Sep sowing), Zaid Rabi (Feb-Mar sowing).
[0:5] - **Sugarcane:** annual/long-duration crop, placed on the **Kharif** side in exam keys (barley, gram, wheat are Rabi).
[0:6] - **Jowar** is mainly a kharif crop (also some rabi jowar in the Deccan) – the usual "NOT a major rabi crop" answer against linseed, gram, wheat.
[0:7] - **Pulses:** **tur/arhar = kharif**; **gram/chana = rabi**. Millets mainly kharif.
[0:8] - **Kharif set (UPSC 2004):** cotton, groundnut, maize = kharif; mustard = rabi.
[0:9] - **Monsoon season:** the SW monsoon season runs from about **mid-June to mid-September** (kharif sowing window).
[0:10] # Crops grown in both seasons (AP)
[0:11] - AP: **paddy, maize, jowar, bajra and pulses** are grown in both kharif and rabi; AP 2024-25 paddy: 14.04 lakh ha kharif + 6.93 lakh ha rabi.
[0:12] > Match pattern: Rabi – wheat; Kharif – paddy; Zaid – watermelon; sunshine = a natural resource. Jute and cotton are Kharif, never Rabi.
[0:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 20 p.8 (Zaid Kharif / Zaid Rabi); OnlyIAS NCERT Human Geography 05 p.1; app notes 3:45
## SUB 1 | Crop Classification – Food, Cash and Mixed Farming
[1:0] TABLE head: Group | Crops
  [1:0.0] Foodgrains = cereals + pulses | Rice, wheat, coarse cereals/millets (Shree Anna), pulses
  [1:0.1] Oilseeds | Groundnut, mustard, soybean, sunflower, sesame
  [1:0.2] Cash / commercial | Cotton, jute, sugarcane, tobacco, rubber, plantation crops, groundnut
[1:1] - **Cash crop:** grown for sale, not self-consumption – **cotton** (textiles), jute, groundnut, sugarcane, tobacco, rubber. **Jowar** is a food crop, not a cash crop; in the UPPSC key **soyabean** is also treated as "not a cash crop" (oilseed/pulse-type food crop).
[1:2] - Rice, maize, wheat = **food crops**.
[1:3] - AP traditional food crops: paddy, jowar, bajra, ragi, pulses; traditional commercial: **groundnut, cotton, tobacco, sugarcane**.
[1:4] - **Commercialisation of agriculture:** producing crops **for sale** (market) rather than for **own consumption**; shown when non-food crop area grows faster than food-crop area.
[1:5] - **Mixed farming:** **cultivation of crops together with rearing of animals** (milch cattle); raising several crops together is mixed/multiple cropping, not mixed farming.
[1:6] - **GM mustard** (DMH-11) was cleared by GEAC as safe (2017 recommendation; environmental release nod 2022, matter in the Supreme Court); **Bt cotton (2002)** is the only commercial GM crop; Bt brinjal under moratorium since 2010.
[1:7] ~~ Sources: G.C. Leong (src/geography/gc-leong-geography-18.2) p.2 (mixed farming); app notes 3:45

[2:2] - Size of an operational holding = **owned land + leased-in land – leased-out land**.
[2:3] - Agriculture Census uses **five size classes**:

## SUB 5 | Cropping Pattern – India and Andhra Pradesh
[5:0] # India
[5:1] - Long-run shift: **food crops → commercial and high-value crops**.
[5:2] - **Factors of cropping pattern:** natural (soil, rainfall), historical, social, economic and **government policy** (MSP, procurement, subsidies).
[5:3] - **Rice: largest cropped area** in India (~51-52 Mha); pulses and oilseeds: stagnant yields.
[5:4] - Rice-wheat expansion (2015-2025): Punjab, Telangana, MP – assured **MSP procurement**; policy to shift **~20% of rice area to pulses** to cut urea use and save groundwater.
[5:5] - **Soyabean:** largest area **Madhya Pradesh** (5.35 Mha, 2009-10), then Maharashtra.
[5:6] # Andhra Pradesh
[5:7] - **Rice** = largest crop (about **one-third of GCA** in 2004-05); 2024-25: **paddy 20.97 of 37.51 lakh ha** of foodgrains.
[5:8] - **West Godavari** = largest area under food crops (rice bowl, Godavari delta).
[5:9] TABLE head: AP crop | Main region
  [5:9.0] Rice | Krishna-Godavari delta
  [5:9.1] Maize | Guntur (Godavari-Krishna belt)
  [5:9.2] Sorghum (jowar) | Kurnool
  [5:9.3] Groundnut | Anantapuramu
[5:10] - **Cotton = "white gold"** of AP, expanding; groundnut shrinking (11.36 → 3.46 lakh ha, 2011-12 → 2024-25); oilseeds 15.46 → 5.35 lakh ha.
[5:11] - Expanding: maize (poultry feed), horticulture, **oil palm (East Godavari, Eluru, West Godavari)**, aquaculture; shrinking: ragi, jowar.
[5:12] TABLE head: AP foodgrains area (lakh ha) | 2021-22 | 2023-24 | 2024-25 | 2025-26 (2nd AE)
  [5:12.0] Area | 41.44 | 33.24 | 37.51 | 42.74
[5:13] - 2024-25: pulses 11.66 lakh ha, maize 3.20 lakh ha; production 161.86 lakh t. 2025-26 (2nd AE): paddy 24.68 lakh ha.
[5:14] - **Kharif 2026** (El Niño): AP rainfall **51% below normal** (1 June-25 Aug); sown 20.35 vs 25.63 lakh ha; shift to pulses and drought-resilient crops (as of 2026).
[5:15] - AP problems: monsoon dependence (~50% NSA rainfed), holdings below 1 ha, soil-fertility loss.
