## SUB 0 | Rural Credit – History, Surveys and Sources
[0:0] # History and surveys
[0:1] - **1951:** **moneylenders ~70%** of rural credit; institutional (government, cooperatives, banks) only about **5-7%** – the "modern lending institutions" share asked in APPSC = **about 5%**.
[0:2] - **All India Rural Credit Survey Committee** (RBI, 1951; report 1954), chair **A.D. Gorwala**: "**Rural credit has failed, but cooperation must succeed**" → **SBI (1 July 1955)** from the **Imperial Bank of India**.
[0:3] - **1969:** social banking and multi-agency approach; **NABARD (1982)** coordinates rural finance – the **apex rural development bank**.
[0:4] - **AIDIS 2019** (NSS **77th round**): rural debt **66% institutional**, 34% non-institutional.
[0:5] - **NAFIS 2021-22** (NABARD): rural household income **₹12,698**/month; indebted households 47.4% → **52.0%**.
[0:6] # Sources
[0:7] TABLE head: Institutional | Non-institutional
  [0:7.0] Commercial banks, RRBs, cooperatives/PACS, Small Finance Banks, SHG-bank linkage | Moneylenders, traders, landlords, relatives
[0:8] - **Kisan Credit Card:** valid **5 years**; open to **tenants, sharecroppers, SHGs, JLGs** (not only landowners).
[0:9] - **Small Finance Banks:** lend to small businesses, small and marginal farmers, rural entrepreneurs (not to financial-sector regulators).

  [1:0.0] Temporary | Seasonal, repaid after harvest
  [1:0.1] Chronic | Long-standing
  [1:0.2] Debt trap | Taking a new loan (debt) to repay an old one – for a household or a country

## SUB 2 | MGNREGA, Migration and Rural Distress
[2:0] # MGNREGA and distress
[2:1] - **NREGA** passed 2005, in force 2006, renamed **MGNREGA 2009**: **100 days** per household; wages within **15 days** (not 30); **one-third** women. Now replaced by **VB-G RAM G**.
[2:2] - **Positive results:** **higher purchasing power of the rural poor** and **less distress migration** to cities; it **raised** (not reduced) labourers' bargaining power.
[2:3] - Person-days **389.09 crore (2020-21) → 183.77 crore** (2025-26, to Dec 2025); urban wages **1.7-2 times** rural (ES 2025-26).
[2:4] - AP: **125 days** wage work in distressed pockets under VB-G RAM G (Jun 2026).
[2:5] # Migration
[2:6] TABLE head: Push (rural) | Pull (urban)
  [2:6.0] Few jobs, low wages, landlessness, crop failure, debt | High labour demand, better wages
  [2:6.1] Poor health and schools | Education, healthcare
  [2:6.2] Unbalanced rural-urban development | Industry and services
[2:7] - Causes of rural-urban migration: urban labour demand, unbalanced development, few rural jobs – **all of the above**.
[2:8] - **Distress migration:** forced by debt, landlessness, crop loss. **Seasonal:** for a season, then return. **26.8%** of rural Indians were migrants (2020-21).
[2:9] - **AP:** distress belts **Rayalaseema, Srikakulam**; Gulf migration from **YSR Kadapa, Godavari/Konaseema**; **93.2%** agricultural households indebted (highest, NSSO 77th round); ~**50%** of AP farmers depend on non-institutional credit (NABARD; not 25%).
[2:10] # Rural connectivity gap
[2:11] - **TRAI:** urban teledensity is about **three times** rural teledensity (APPSC key; e.g. urban ~155 vs rural ~57 in 2018).

## SUB 3 | Rural Non-farm Sector and Feminisation of Agriculture
[3:0] # Non-farm sector
[3:1] - Components: rural industries, Khadi & Village Industries, handloom/handicrafts, food processing, trade, transport, services (**not crop cultivation**).
[3:2] TABLE head: Body / scheme | Role
  [3:2.0] KVIC | Statutory, KVI Act 1956; Ministry of MSME; implements PMEGP
  [3:2.1] SVEP | Under DAY-NRLM; funds rural non-farm enterprises
  [3:2.2] MSMEs | 8.7 crore units, ~31.1% of GDP
  [3:2.3] DDU-GKY | Placement-linked skilling; parliamentary panel flags low wages and drop-outs
[3:3] # Feminisation of agriculture
[3:4] - Rising share of women among cultivators, labourers and unpaid family workers as **men move to non-farm/urban work** (male out-migration); participation, not land ownership.
[3:5] - **Claudia Goldin** (2023 Nobel): **U-shaped curve** – high in the 1980s, fell 2004-05 to 2012, rising after 2020.
[3:6] - Rural women = **80%** of the rise in female workers (2017-18 to 2023-24).
