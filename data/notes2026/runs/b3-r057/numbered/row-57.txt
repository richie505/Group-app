## SUB 0 | MSP – Meaning, CACP and Who Fixes It
[0:0] - **Minimum Support Price (MSP):** a form of **market intervention** by the Government of India to insure farmers against a **sharp fall in farm prices** – a guarantee (floor) price announced **at the beginning of the sowing season**, before the crop reaches the market.
[0:1] - **Purpose:** save farmers from **distress sales** and **procure foodgrains for public distribution** (PDS buffer stocks → food security); it **ensures a minimum fixed price** and supports food security, but it is **not** a promise of "immense profit", and farmers sell to the government only when the **market price falls below MSP**.
[0:2] - **Mechanism:** if the market price falls below MSP (bumper crop, glut), **government agencies purchase the stock offered** by farmers at MSP.
[0:3] - **Who:** **CACP recommends → Government of India (Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs, CCEA) approves/fixes**. Exam answer for "MSP is determined/decided/recommended by": **CACP** – not ICAR, IARI, NABARD, Planning Commission, RBI, President, Finance Commission, State Governments or FCI.
[0:4] - **Legal status:** an **administrative decision**, not backed by a statute; not a legal right.
[0:5] - Government's tools to improve agricultural marketing: **PDS, MSP and buffer stocks** – all three ("more than one of the above").
[0:6] TABLE head: CACP fact | Detail
  [0:6.0] Origin | Agricultural Prices Commission (APC), January 1965 (same year as FCI)
  [0:6.1] Renamed | CACP in 1985
  [0:6.2] Status | Attached office of the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare; a decentralised agency, not statutory
  [0:6.3] Composition | Chairman + Member Secretary + 1 official member + 2 non-official members
  [0:6.4] Main objective | Recommend MSPs to incentivise farmers to adopt modern technology, raise productivity and output
  [0:6.5] Mandated objectives (UPSC 1995) | Stabilise agricultural prices; ensure meaningful real income to farmers; protect consumers via PDS – not "maximum price for the farmer"
[0:7] - **Ashok Gulati** (CACP Chairman 2011-14): long-term food security needs agricultural growth of **more than 4% a year**.
[0:8] > Exam angle: "Who determines MSP" – more than a dozen PYQs; the key is always CACP (strictly it recommends, CCEA decides).

## SUB 1 | CACP Factors, Cost Concepts and the MSP Formula
[1:0] - **Factors CACP considers:** **cost of production**, demand and supply, **price trends in domestic and international markets**, **inter-crop price parity**, terms of trade between agriculture and non-agriculture, a minimum 50% margin over cost, and the likely **effect on consumers / the economy**.
[1:1] - **Not a factor:** a **cost of living index** as such (CDS 2019) – CACP only weighs the *effect* of MSP on cost of living.
[1:2] TABLE head: Cost concept | Meaning
  [1:2.0] A2 | Actual paid-out costs (seed, fertiliser, hired labour, fuel, irrigation)
  [1:2.1] FL | Imputed value of family labour
  [1:2.2] A2+FL | Paid-out cost + family labour (leaves out rent on owned land and interest on fixed capital)
  [1:2.3] C2 | A2 + FL + land rent + interest on fixed capital – the fullest cost
[1:3] - **Present formula (since 2018-19):** MSP at least **1.5 × (A2+FL)** – a 50% margin over A2+FL, **not over C2**; in short, the support price is **"cost plus"** a margin (cost plus benefit).
[1:4] - **Swaminathan Commission (National Commission on Farmers, 2004-06):** MSP = **C2 + 50%**; farmer unions (SKM) say paddy at C2+50% should be ₹3,243/q for 2026-27 vs ₹2,441 fixed.
[1:5] - **Farm running (working) capital** = seed, fertiliser, irrigation water, hired labour; **land revenue** is a fixed charge, **not** running capital.

## SUB 3 | Sugarcane – FRP, SMP and SAP
[3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:0.0] FRP (Fair and Remunerative Price) | Minimum price sugar mills must pay; approved by the CCEA on CACP's recommendation; linked to the sugar recovery rate
  [3:0.1] SMP (Statutory Minimum Price) | Replaced by FRP from 2009-10 (Sugarcane (Control) Order amended 22 Oct 2009)
  [3:0.2] SAP (State Advised Price) | Announced by some State Governments (UP, Punjab, Haryana), usually above FRP; not by CACP
[3:1] - **Legal basis:** **Sugarcane (Control) Order, 1966** under the **Essential Commodities Act, 1955**; sugar and sugarcane are essential commodities.
[3:2] - "FRP" relates to **sugarcane** only; CACP **recommends**, it does **not approve**.

## SUB 4 | Procurement at MSP and Its Price Effects
[4:0] TABLE head: Crop | Procurement
  [4:0.0] Wheat, paddy | FCI + State agencies – effectively open-ended
  [4:0.1] Coarse grains | State agencies with prior GoI approval (not exclusively FCI)
  [4:0.2] Pulses, oilseeds, copra | Price Support Scheme – capped at 25% of production
  [4:0.3] Cotton | CCI
[4:1] - **Neither** statement of UPSC 2020 holds: procurement is **not unlimited** for all cereals, pulses and oilseeds, and MSP is **not** set at a level the market price "will never rise" above.
[4:2] - **Rice prices** in the recent past were affected by **all four**: MSP, government trading, government stockpiling and consumer subsidies (UPSC 2020).
[4:3] - **Decentralised Procurement (DCP), 1997:** States procure, store and distribute; Centre pays economic cost minus CIP.
[4:4] - States **expedite kharif procurement** so farmers get **timely payment and protection from market fluctuations**.
[4:5] - **AP:** State agencies procure paddy for the Central Pool; **DCMS** societies buy at MSP; APMARKFED apex cooperative; payment by DBT within 48 hours.
[4:6] - Wheat procurement RMS 2026-27 above **35 MT**.

## SUB 5 | PM-AASHA, PSF, MIS and Other Price Support
[5:0] - **PM-AASHA** = **Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay SanraksHan Abhiyan**, approved by the Union Cabinet **September 2018**, Ministry of Agriculture; beneficiaries: **farmers** – aims that MSP benefits reach farmers, including small and marginal ones.
[5:1] - **Outlay ₹35,000 crore** for the 15th Finance Commission cycle **up to 2025-26** (APPSC 2023).
[5:2] TABLE head: Component | Nature
  [5:2.0] PSS | Physical procurement of pulses, oilseeds, copra by NAFED, NCCF; cap 25%
  [5:2.1] PDPS | Pays MSP minus market price without buying; mainly oilseeds
  [5:2.2] PPSS | 2018 pilot: private stockists procured oilseeds at MSP
  [5:2.3] MIS | Perishables not under MSP; trigger ≥ 10% price fall or output rise; loss shared 50:50 (Centre : NE States 75:25)
[5:3] - PSS 2024-25: 61.87 LMT bought, over ₹39,000 crore, 26.56 lakh farmers. **Pulses mission:** 100% MSP buying of **tur, urad, masoor** for **4 years**.
[5:4] TABLE head: Price Stabilisation Fund | Fact
  [5:4.0] PSF 2003 (Ministry of Commerce) | For growers of tea, coffee, natural rubber and tobacco (plantation crops); 2003-2013 – the PSF of the UPPCS 2006 question
  [5:4.1] PSF 2015 (agriculture, then Consumer Affairs) | Buffers of onion, potato, later pulses
[5:5] - **Kerala:** first State with **floor prices (MSP) for 16 vegetables/fruits, November 2020**.
[5:6] - **Kisan Kranti Yatra** (Bharatiya Kisan Union, October 2018): demands included **MSP, a blanket loan waiver and the Swaminathan Committee report** – all three.
[5:7] - AP maize sold at ₹1,400-1,600 against ₹2,400 MSP (May 2026).

## SUB 6 | Price Floors, Ceilings, Phases and WTO
[6:0] TABLE head: Term | Example
  [6:0.0] Price floor (minimum) | MSP (e.g. for jowar, cane), minimum wages
  [6:0.1] Price ceiling (maximum) | MRP printed on a biscuit packet
[6:1] - A ration-shop price is a subsidised issue price and a fertiliser subsidy is a subsidy – neither is a price floor.
[6:2] TABLE head: Phase | Hallmark
  [6:2.0] Pre-Green Revolution (1950-65) | Land reforms, community development, irrigation
  [6:2.1] Green Revolution (mid-1960s) | HYV wheat, rice; APC and FCI 1965
  [6:2.2] Third phase: price-support | Price policy based on MSP and procurement, FCI buffer stocks, PDS
  [6:2.3] Post-1991 | Liberalisation, WTO 1995, e-NAM, FPOs, PM-KISAN
[6:3] - **WTO Agreement on Agriculture (1995):** pillars – **market access, domestic support, export subsidies** (not IPR). **Aggregate Measure of Support (AMS)** = **quantum of (trade-distorting) subsidy to agriculture**.
[6:4] - **Amber box** (MSP price support, input subsidies) capped by **de minimis 10%** of production value for developing countries (5% developed); **green box** (research, infrastructure) unlimited; **blue box** = production-limiting payments.
[6:5] - **Peace clause (Bali, 2013)** protects public stockholding for food security. **Bt cotton** approved **2002**.

## SUB 7 | Criticism and the Legal-Guarantee Debate
[7:0] TABLE head: Issue | Effect
  [7:0.0] Crop bias | Procurement mostly paddy and wheat
  [7:0.1] Regional bias | Punjab, Haryana, Telangana, AP, Chhattisgarh gain; eastern and North-Eastern farmers gain little
  [7:0.2] Shanta Kumar HLC (2015) | Only about 6% of farmers sell at MSP
[7:1] - **Ineffective MSP** is one cause of agrarian distress (with unviable farming, indebtedness).
[7:2] - **Legal MSP:** farmer unions want a law **binding agencies to buy eligible produce at MSP**; economists fear fiscal cost, trade distortion, monoculture and WTO problems and prefer **PDPS-type deficiency payments**.
[7:3] - SC notice on plea for MSP ≥ weighted average cost (14 Apr 2026). **Economic Survey 2025-26:** use procurement savings for **voluntary crop diversification** away from water-guzzling rice and wheat.
