=== NOTES 5:80 | Sustainable Development – Meaning, Nature, Scope and Components
## SUB 0 | Meaning and Definition
[80:0:0] # Core Meaning
[80:0:1] - Sustainable development = using resources **today** without compromising **future generations'** ability to meet their needs.
[80:0:2] - Balance of **economic, social and environmental** concerns – not growth alone.
[80:0:3] # Brundtland Commission (1987)
[80:0:4] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [80:0:4.0] Body | World Commission on Environment and Development (WCED)
  [80:0:4.1] Chairperson | Gro Harlem Brundtland (Prime Minister of Norway)
  [80:0:4.2] Report | "Our Common Future" (Brundtland Report)
  [80:0:4.3] Year | 1987
  [80:0:4.4] Contribution | First definition; coined/popularised "Sustainable Development"
[80:0:5] - Definition: "Development that **meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs**."
[80:0:6] - Our Common Future **linked economic development with environmental stability**; it laid the foundation for Rio 1992.
[80:0:7] First definition of sustainable development = **Brundtland Report, 1987**.

[80:2:1] # Rio 1992 Details
[80:2:2] - **Agenda 21**: comprehensive action plan for SD at **local, national and global** levels; emerged from UNCED 1992.
[80:2:3] - **UNFCCC**: drawn and opened for signature at UNCED, Rio, 1992.
[80:2:4] - **Forest Principles**: a **non-binding** statement (not a treaty).
[80:2:5] - **UN Commission on Sustainable Development (CSD)**: reviews implementation of Agenda 21 and the Rio Declaration.
[80:2:6] - Rio 1992 = **first Earth Summit**.
[80:2:7] # Rio+20 Details
[80:2:8] - Rio+20 = **20-year follow-up to 1992** and **10-year follow-up to 2002**; the **third** international conference on SD, hosted by **Brazil**.
[80:2:9] - "The Future We Want" is a **2012** outcome, **not 1992**.
[80:2:10] - **SDGs were born at Rio+20**; part of Agenda 2030 since 2015.
[80:2:11] - **PAGE (Partnership for Action on Green Economy)**: emerged at UNCSD 2012; five agencies – **UNEP, UNDP, ILO, UNIDO, UNITAR**.

## SUB 4 | Three Pillars and Triple Bottom Line
[80:4:0] # Pillars
[80:4:1] TABLE head: Pillar | Alternate phrasing (2024–25)
  [80:4:1.0] Economic development | Economic growth
  [80:4:1.1] Social equity | Social inclusion
  [80:4:1.2] Environmental protection | Environmental protection
[80:4:2] - UN's three pillars: **economic growth, environmental protection, social equity** – interlinked.
[80:4:3] - **Triple bottom line**: **People, Planet, Profit** (social, environmental, economic) – business version of the three pillars.
[80:4:4] Odd-one-out: **political, military (security) or cultural** dimensions are NOT among the three classic pillars.
[80:4:5] # Related Terms
[80:4:6] - **Economic sustainability** does NOT mean maximising profits without considering social or environmental factors.
[80:4:7] - **Poverty alleviation** is NOT an environmental-protection issue; that list = climate change, pollution, environmental degradation, resource depletion.
## SUB 5 | Goals and Principles
[80:5:0] TABLE head: Principle | Meaning
  [80:5:0.0] Intergenerational equity | Present must not deplete resources at the cost of future generations
  [80:5:0.1] Precautionary principle | Lack of full scientific certainty is no reason to postpone cost-effective measures against environmental harm
  [80:5:0.2] Polluter pays | Polluter bears the cost of preventing and controlling pollution
  [80:5:0.3] CBDR – Common but Differentiated Responsibilities | Shared duty; developed nations carry more (capacity, historical contribution)
  [80:5:0.4] Carrying capacity | Human use within what ecosystems can support (finite Earth)
[80:5:1] - Principles endorsed at **Rio 1992**; underpin international environmental law.
[80:5:2] # How the Principles Work Together
[80:5:3] - **Intergenerational equity** is the heart of the Brundtland definition: today's users hold resources in trust for future users.
[80:5:4] - **Precautionary principle** acts **before harm** happens, even when science is not fully certain.
[80:5:5] - **Polluter pays** acts **after or during** pollution: the cost falls on the polluter, not on the public.
[80:5:6] - **CBDR** applies equity **between nations**: all share the duty, but rich nations do more because of their capacity and past emissions.
[80:5:7] - **Carrying capacity** sets the outer limit: no use beyond what ecosystems can support.
[80:5:8] Odd-one-out as a goal: **"maximising economic growth at any cost"** – SD needs balance.
## SUB 6 | Nature and Scope
[80:6:0] # Includes and Excludes
[80:6:1] TABLE head: Includes | Excludes
  [80:6:1.0] Meeting present needs (development not rejected) | Growth that runs down natural capital
  [80:6:1.1] Equity within and across generations | Treating environment and development as opposites
  [80:6:1.2] Inclusion – "leave no one behind" | Profit without social/environmental concern
[80:6:2] - Assertion-reason logic: the **present generation may use resources**, but not in a way that compromises future generations' ability to meet their needs.
[80:6:3] # Natural Capital
[80:6:4] - **Natural capital** = renewable and non-renewable resources created by nature: **air, water, land, minerals, forests**.
[80:6:5] - **Roads** are NOT natural capital (man-made).
[80:6:6] # National Environment Policy, 2006
[80:6:7] - Policy framework for environmental conservation and SD.
[80:6:8] - **Conservation and livelihood security are linked**, not opposed.
[80:6:9] - **Wetlands (Conservation & Management) Rules, 2010**: framed on the recommendation of an **Expert Panel** under NEP 2006.
## SUB 7 | Measures of Sustainability
[80:7:0] TABLE head: Measure | Meaning
  [80:7:0.0] Green GDP | GDP adjusted for natural-resource depletion and environmental damage
  [80:7:0.1] Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI) | Adds unpaid work; subtracts pollution, crime, resource loss
  [80:7:0.2] Ecological footprint | Productive land and sea needed to meet consumption and absorb wastes; in global hectares
  [80:7:0.3] Earth Overshoot Day | Date when humanity's demand exceeds what Earth can regenerate that year
[80:7:1] - **Earth Overshoot Day**: related to **environmental degradation**; conceived by **Andrew Simms** (New Economics Foundation, UK); first campaign **2006** with **Global Footprint Network**.
[80:7:2] - Why new measures: ordinary **GDP counts output only**; it treats loss of forests, water or minerals as income and ignores pollution. Green GDP and GPI correct this, while ecological footprint and Earth Overshoot Day show demand against nature's supply.
