=== Meaning and the Brundtland Definition
# Brundtland Commission (1987)
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Formal name | **World Commission on Environment and Development (WCED)** – UN-mandated, set up in the early 1980s |
| Chair | **Gro Harlem Brundtland**, then Prime Minister of **Norway** ("Mother of Sustainable Development") |
| Report | **"Our Common Future"** = the **Brundtland Report** |
| Year | **1987** |
| Contribution | First widely accepted (official) definition; **popularised** the term and linked economic development with environmental stability |
- **Definition:** development that "**meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs**" – given by the **WCED (1987)**, not UNEP, the World Bank, the IMF, UNCED 1992 or the Tbilisi Declaration (1977).
- **Our Common Future on poverty:** "**Poverty is a major cause and effect of global environmental problems**" – so environment cannot be tackled without tackling poverty and inequality.
- **Concepts in one line:** sustainable development = the definition above; Our Common Future = the 1987 report; **intergenerational equity** = fair sharing of resources between present and future generations.
- Exam shorthand: "**improving the conditions of the present generation without affecting the needs of future generations**" = sustainable development exactly.
> Most-asked (×20): who defined it / which report / which year → **Brundtland, Our Common Future, 1987**. Rio 1992 and Stockholm 1972 are the tempting wrong options.

=== Rio Earth Summit 1992 and Agenda 21
# UNCED – the first Earth Summit
- **UNCED = United Nations Conference on Environment and Development** ("Earth Summit"), **Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, 3-14 June 1992**; 20th anniversary of Stockholm 1972; about **178-179 countries**.
- **Outcomes:** **Agenda 21**, **Rio Declaration** (**27 principles**), **Forest Principles** (non-binding); legally binding conventions opened for signature: **UNFCCC** and **CBD** (UNCCD followed in 1994).
- **UNFCCC** was negotiated in New York and **drawn/opened for signature at UNCED, Rio 1992** – not at Stockholm 1972, WSSD 2002 or Copenhagen 2009.
# Agenda 21
| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| What | **Comprehensive (non-binding) global action plan for sustainable development** – globally, nationally, locally, wherever humans affect the environment |
| Origin | **UNCED, Rio 1992** – NOT the Johannesburg WSSD 2002 (UPSC 2016: statement 1 only) |
| "21" | **21st century** |
| Key objective | **Every local government should draw its own Local Agenda 21 (LA21)** |
| Sample programme area | Ch.2: "**Promoting sustainable development through trade**"; Ch.4 is *changing* (not maintaining) consumption patterns |
| Aim | Combat environmental damage, poverty and disease through global co-operation |
- Agenda 21 is not a law that each country must enact, and not a "20th-century only" target.
> Rio 1992 is the "first Earth Summit" (not Washington, Geneva or Buenos Aires); MPPSC 1992 printed "UN Conference on Sustainable Development" for Rio – formally the 1992 meeting was UNCED.

=== Follow-up Summits – Rio+5, WSSD, Rio+20, 2030 Agenda
| Year | Formal name (popular name) | Venue | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1972 | UN Conference on the Human Environment (UNCHE) | **Stockholm** | First world environment conference; **UNEP** (HQ Nairobi) |
| 1992 | UNCED – Earth Summit | Rio de Janeiro | Agenda 21, Rio Declaration, UNFCCC, CBD |
| 1997 | Earth Summit +5 (**Rio+5**), UNGA special session | **New York** | Reviewed Agenda 21 |
| 1997 | Kyoto COP3 | Kyoto | Kyoto Protocol (climate) |
| 2000 | Millennium Summit | New York | **MDGs (2000-2015)** |
| **2002** | **World Summit on Sustainable Development (WSSD) – Rio+10** | **Johannesburg** | Johannesburg Declaration; **Johannesburg Plan of Implementation (JPOI)** |
| **June 2012** | **UN Conference on Sustainable Development (UNCSD) – Rio+20** | **Rio de Janeiro** | "**The Future We Want**"; green economy; **SDGs born here**; PAGE |
| **25 Sept 2015** | UN Sustainable Development Summit (UNGA 70) | **New York** | "**Transforming our World: the 2030 Agenda**" – 17 SDGs |
- **JPOI:** states to formulate **national strategies for sustainable development** and **begin their implementation by 2005**.
- **Rio+20:** 20-year follow-up to 1992, 10-year follow-up to 2002; third SD conference, hosted by Brazil. The SDGs were **born at Rio+20, 2012** (not 2010).
- **PAGE (Partnership for Action on Green Economy):** **emerged at Rio+20 (2012)** as a response to The Future We Want (launched 2013); agencies **UNEP, UNDP, ILO, UNIDO, UNITAR**.
- **2015** also saw the **COP21 Paris Agreement** – the SDGs coincided with it.
# UN Commission on Sustainable Development (CSD)
- **Established by the UN General Assembly in December 1992** (Res. 47/191) to **review implementation of Agenda 21 and the Rio Declaration**.
- **Replaced in 2013** by the **High-level Political Forum on Sustainable Development (HLPF)** (under ECOSOC/UNGA), as decided at Rio+20.

=== 2030 Agenda and SDGs – Basics
- **17 SDGs, 169 targets, 232 indicators**; adopted by **all 193 UN member states** in **September 2015**; in force **1 Jan 2016**; target year **2030**.
- **Not legally binding** and not a single development model; they **replaced the MDGs (2000-2015)**.
- **Five Ps:** **People, Planet, Prosperity, Peace, Partnership** (not Patriotism).
- **Dimensions:** **economic, social, environmental**.
- **Goal 1:** "**End poverty in all its forms everywhere**" – no 50%/75% target.
- Aim: get rid of poverty, protect the environment and ensure peace and prosperity for all; scope covers goals like life below water, decent work and cultural diversity (target 4.7) – **not political alliances**.
- Biggest hurdle for poverty eradication: **economic inequality and unequal distribution of resources**.
- **UNDP** is the UN development agency that leads SDG support work in countries; in India **NITI Aayog** coordinates.
- **Andhra Pradesh** adopted **all 17 SDGs** with **212 indicators** to monitor progress to 2030; SDG targets are built into **Swarna Andhra @2047**.

=== Three Pillars and Types of Sustainability
| Pillar | Aim |
|---|---|
| **Economic** | Growth that lasts – NOT profit maximisation that ignores social or environmental impact |
| **Social** | **Social equity / equality**, inclusion, community development |
| **Environmental** | Reduce waste, conserve natural resources, protect ecosystems |
- UN wording: **economic growth, environmental protection, social equity** – interlinked; SD's key goal is to ensure all three together.
- **Triple bottom line:** **People, Planet, Profit**.
- **NOT a pillar:** political, military, cultural, technological, legal or financial.
- "Social sustainability focuses exclusively on growth and jobs" ✗; "economic sustainability = maximising profits" ✗ (A false, R true).
- **Sustainability** broadly = programmes and actions to preserve a particular resource; SD is achieved by social and economic improvement that protects the environment and supports equality (A/R: both true, R not the explanation).

=== Principles, Nature and Scope
| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Intergenerational equity** | Present must not deplete (natural) resources at the cost of future generations – "inter-generational sensibility" in the use of **natural resources** |
| **Carrying capacity** | Human use within what ecosystems can support – the Brundtland idea is **intertwined with carrying capacity** (UPSC 2010) |
| **Precautionary principle** | Lack of full scientific certainty is no reason to postpone measures against harm |
| **Polluter pays** | The **polluter (person or company) bears the cost** of preventing or remedying the damage – not the government or public |
| CBDR | Shared duty; developed nations carry more |
| Integration | Environment and economy are integrated, not opposites |
- **NOT a principle:** over-exploitation of resources, growth at the cost of environment, more pollution for industry.
- **NCERT principles:** respect all life, improve quality of life, **conserve the earth's vitality and diversity**, **minimise depletion of natural resources**, change attitudes, let communities care for their own environment.
- **Measures:** renewables, recycling, lower and more efficient energy use; all pollution minimised; consumption must **not exceed** the rate of regeneration.
- **Challenges:** **over-consumption**; **increased use of fossil fuels** is a negative impact of technology.
- **Education** helps by **promoting environmental awareness**; communication for SD aims at **transformative learning**.
- **Environmental sustainability needs participatory, open and democratic decision-making**; **environmental protection** maintains (and restores) a healthy natural environment and **is not a luxury** at today's level of development.
- SD includes a **constant or rising stock of capital assets**; natural **climatic change over time** is not part of the concept.
- **Scope includes** meeting present needs, **equity within and across generations**, inclusion ("leave no one behind"); **excludes** growth that runs down natural capital and treating environment and development as opposites.
- **Poverty alleviation** is printed as "not related to environmental protection" (UP 2013 key).

=== Precursors and Thinkers
| Year | Milestone | Point |
|---|---|---|
| **1962** | **Silent Spring – Rachel Carson** | Set off the modern environmental movement |
| **1972** | **The Limits to Growth – Club of Rome** (MIT team, Donella and Dennis Meadows) | Computer model: growth cannot go on forever on finite resources |
| **1980** | **World Conservation Strategy** – IUCN with UNEP and WWF | Subtitle "Living Resource Conservation for **Sustainable Development**" – first prominent use of the term |
| 1987 | Our Common Future | First accepted definition |
- **Green Development** (1990; "Environment and Sustainability in the Third World/Developing World") – **W.M. (Bill) Adams**, Cambridge geographer.
- **When did the term come in?** Printed key **1980** (World Conservation Strategy); many books say **1987** (Brundtland) because the report popularised and defined it.
- The Club of Rome did NOT propose the SDGs (UPSC 2016).

=== Measuring Sustainability – Natural Capital and Footprints
| Measure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Natural capital** | Resources created by nature – **air, water, land, minerals, forests**; **roads are NOT** (man-made) |
| **Ecological footprint** | **Biologically productive land and sea area needed to supply a person's/population's consumption and absorb its wastes**; unit **global hectare** |
| **Earth Overshoot Day** | Date when humanity's yearly demand exceeds what Earth can regenerate – a sign of **environmental degradation**; calculated by **Global Footprint Network**; conceived by **Andrew Simms** (New Economics Foundation), first campaign **2006** |
| **Green GDP** | GDP – net natural capital consumption (depletion + degradation) |
| Genuine Progress Indicator | Adds unpaid work; subtracts pollution, crime, resource loss |
- Humanity's footprint ≈ **1.5 Earths**; India ≈ 0.9 (sustainable lifestyle).
- Biota, biome and niche are not area measures.
