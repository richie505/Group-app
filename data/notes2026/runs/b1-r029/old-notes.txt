  [29:0:0.0] First | Rajagriha (Rajgir), Saptaparni Cave; 483 BCE, soon after the Buddha's death | Ajatashatru (Haryanka) | Mahakassapa | Vinaya (recited by Upali) and Sutta (by Ananda) compiled
  [29:0:0.1] Second | Vaishali; 383 BCE, 100 years after the Buddha's death | Kalashoka (Shishunaga) | Sabakami | Dispute on monastic rules; split into Sthaviravadins and Mahasanghikas

  [29:0:0.3] Fourth | Kundalavana, Kashmir; c. 72 CE (1st c. CE) | Kanishka (Kushana) | Vasumitra (Ashvaghosha deputy) | In Sanskrit; Mahavibhasa; division into Mahayana and Hinayana

## SUB 3 | Sects – Hinayana, Mahayana, Vajrayana
[29:3:0] TABLE head: Sect | Ideal / features | Buddha | Texts / spread
  [29:3:0.0] Hinayana / Theravada | Arhat – individual liberation | Symbols, no idols | Pali canon; Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand
  [29:3:0.1] Mahayana (after 4th council) | Bodhisattva – postpones nirvana for all beings; Sunyata | Idol worship | Sanskrit – Prajnaparamita, Lotus Sutra; China, Japan, Korea, Tibet
  [29:3:0.2] Vajrayana | Salvation through magical power (vajra); mantras, mudras, mandalas | Taras, wrathful deities | Guhyasamaja, Manjushrimulakalpa; Bengal–Bihar, Tibet, Nepal, Bhutan
[29:3:1] - **Vajrayana**: emerged in the **7th century AD**, flourished from the **8th c.**; most prominent in the Sultanate period; linked with **Padmasambhava**; centres **Vikramashila**, later **Nalanda**.
[29:3:2] - **Six paramitas** (perfections) of the Bodhisattva: **dana, sila, kshanti, virya, dhyana, prajna**.
[29:3:3] - **Avalokitesvara** = **Padmapani** ("Lotus-Bearer"), Bodhisattva of compassion.
[29:3:4] - **Sthaviravadins** = Hinayana; **Lokottaravadins** = offshoot of the Mahasanghikas; Mahasanghika **deification of the Buddha** fostered Mahayana.
[29:3:5] - **Tibetan** Buddhism merges Mahayana and Vajrayana; canon = **Kangyur and Tengyur**.
[29:3:6] Arhat = Hinayana; Bodhisattva = Mahayana; vajra = Vajrayana.

## SUB 5 | Nagarjuna
[29:5:0] - Founder of **Madhyamika (Sunyavada)** school of Mahayana; doctrine of **Shunyata** (void).
[29:5:1] - Works: **Mulamadhyamakakarika (Madhyamika Karika)**, **Suhrlekha**, **Ratnavali**, Yogasara, Rasaratnakaram.
[29:5:2] - **Prajnaparamita:** Nagarjuna is linked with the **Prajnaparamita** (Perfection of Wisdom) texts, basis of his Sunyata doctrine.
[29:5:3] - **Ratnavali (Rajaparikatha)**: **welfare state** theory, addressed to a Satavahana king.
[29:5:4] - **Suhrlekha**: letter to a friend (Satavahana king); **I-tsing** saw students memorising it.
[29:5:5] - Title **"Einstein of India"** – idea of relativity.
[29:5:6] - Royal friend **Yajna Sri Satakarni** (Satavahana), who built the **1,500-room Maha Vihara (Parvata Vihara)** at **Sriparvata**.
[29:5:7] - Placed at the 4th council by tradition; credited with 32 new sects in Andhradesha.
[29:5:8] **Siddha Nagarjuna** (founded **Vajrayana** at Sriparvata; Ramireddypalli inscription calls him a Telugu native) is a different person.

[29:7:1] - **Sanchi** (Raisen, MP): built by **Ashoka** (3rd c. BCE); encased in stone under the **Shungas**; **four toranas** by the **Satavahanas**; **UNESCO 1989**.

[29:7:7] - **Dhamek** (**Sarnath**, UP): marks the place of the **first sermon**; present structure of the **Gupta** period.
[29:7:8] - **Aniconic art**: early Buddhism showed the Buddha by symbols – **empty throne**, **footprints**, **Bodhi tree**, **wheel**, **stupa** – not in human form.

## SUB 0 | Stupa, Chaitya and Vihara – Basics
[30:0:0] - **Stupa, Chaitya and Vihara** are all related to **Buddhism** (Jains also cut caves and viharas).
[30:0:1] - **Stupa** = solid relic mound for worship by circumambulation.
[30:0:2] - **Chaitya** = **place of worship** (prayer hall, usually with a stupa at the apsidal end).
[30:0:3] - **Vihara** = **residence of monks** (monastery).
[30:0:4] - **Arama** = a complete complex of **stupa + vihara + chaitya + school**, e.g. **Nandaluru**.
[30:0:5] TABLE head: Feature | Stupa | Chaitya | Vihara
  [30:0:5.0] Nature | Relic mound | Prayer hall | Monastery
  [30:0:5.1] Use | Worship, circumambulation | Congregational worship | Living quarters of monks
  [30:0:5.2] Example | Sanchi, Amaravati | Karla, Guntupalli | Ajanta viharas, Nalanda
[30:0:6] # Parts of a Stupa
[30:0:7] TABLE head: Part | Meaning
  [30:0:7.0] Anda | Hemispherical mound / dome (the cosmos)
  [30:0:7.1] Medhi | Raised drum or terrace below the dome, used for circumambulation
  [30:0:7.2] Harmika | Square railing on top of the anda (abode of the gods)
  [30:0:7.3] Yashti | Central mast rising from the harmika
  [30:0:7.4] Chhatra / Chhatri | Tiered umbrellas on the yashti (honour, protection)
  [30:0:7.5] Vedika | Railing separating the sacred from the secular
  [30:0:7.6] Torana | Ornamental gateways, usually at the four directions
  [30:0:7.7] Pradakshina patha | Path for clockwise circumambulation
  [30:0:7.8] Ayaka pillars / Ayakapata | Special feature of Andhra stupas; Purna Kumbha among linked symbols
[30:0:8] Ayaka pillars are an Andhra-only feature; they are not part of the Sanchi model.
[30:0:9] # Types of Stupa (Nagarjunakonda classification)

  [30:0:10.0] Dhatugarbha | Bodily relics of the Buddha
  [30:0:10.1] Paribhogika | Objects used by the Buddha, e.g. alms bowl
  [30:0:10.2] Uddeshika | Memorial only (no relics)

=== NOTES 1:31 | Other Heterodox Sects – Ajivikas (makkhali Gosala), Charvaka / Lokayata Materialism
## SUB 0 | Six Heterodox Teachers of the Buddha's Time
[31:0:0] TABLE head: Teacher | Doctrine
  [31:0:0.0] Makkhali Gosala | Niyativada – fatalism (Ajivika)
  [31:0:0.1] Purana Kassapa | Akriyavada – actions bring neither merit nor demerit
  [31:0:0.2] Ajita Kesakambalin | Materialist – nothing survives death
  [31:0:0.3] Pakudha Kaccayana | Seven eternal, uncreated elements

  [31:0:0.5] Nigantha Nataputta | = Mahavira (Jain)
[31:0:1] Niyativada = Gosala; Akriyavada = Purana Kassapa; seven elements = Pakudha; Pratityasamutpada = Buddhist.
## SUB 1 | Ajivikas – Doctrine and Life
[31:1:0] - **Makkhali Gosala** – usual exam answer for **founder**; most important leader and populariser (**third chief**) (UKPSC 2024).
[31:1:1] - **Niyati** (fate): all thoughts and actions **fixed before birth**; **no free will**; **karma cannot change destiny**; order of rebirth cannot be changed.
[31:1:2] - "Fate assigns everything, human is incapable" = Ajivikas.
[31:1:3] - **Atomic theory** of matter – influenced the later **Vaisheshika**.
[31:1:4] - **Extreme austerities**; ascetics called **Sudra sannyasins**; **no caste discrimination**; appealed to warrior, industrial and mercantile classes.
## SUB 2 | Ajivikas, Mahavira and the Buddha
[31:2:0] - Gosala was **Mahavira's companion for about six years** before breaking away; Jain texts make him Mahavira's disciple.
[31:2:1] - **Majjhima Nikaya** records the Buddha's debates with Jains and Ajivikas.
## SUB 3 | Ajivikas and Rulers
[31:3:0] TABLE head: Ruler / record | Ajivika link
  [31:3:0.0] Bindusara | Ajivikas popular under him
  [31:3:0.1] Ashoka | Donated the Barabar caves (Bihar) – among the earliest rock-cut caves in India
  [31:3:0.2] 7th Pillar Edict (Delhi-Topra) | Names Ajivikas with Brahmanas, the Sangha and Nirgranthas
[31:3:1] Barabar caves were given by Ashoka, not Chandragupta (UPSC 2013). "Ashoka was an Ajivika" is false – he was Buddhist.
## SUB 4 | Charvaka / Lokayata
[31:4:0] - Other names: **Lokayata** (APPSC GS 2025), **Barhaspatya** (after legendary founder **Brihaspati**).
[31:4:1] - **Complete materialism**; highest value = **Kama** (material pleasure).
[31:4:2] - **Dehatmavada**: "**The conscious body is the soul**" – no separate soul survives death (CGPSC 2024).
[31:4:3] - Only pramana = **perception (pratyaksha)**; inference and testimony (Vedas) rejected; rejects Vedic ritualism and supernaturalism.
[31:4:4] - Famous aphorism: "**Yavaj jivet sukham jivet, rinam kritva ghritam pibet**" – live happily while you live; drink ghee even on debt.
[31:4:5] Charvaka | Perception only | 1 pramana
Nyaya | Perception, inference, comparison, testimony | 4 pramanas

## SUB 6 | Decline
[31:6:0] - Ajivikas survived nearly **2,000 years**, to the **14th c. CE**, in **Karnataka and Tamil Nadu**, then disappeared.
[31:6:1] - Causes: **Puranic-Bhakti revival** (incl. under the **Pallavas**) and **loss of royal patronage**.
