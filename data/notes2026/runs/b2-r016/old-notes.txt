=== NOTES 2:16 | Conflict and Harmony Between Fundamental Rights and Dpsp
## SUB 0 | Fr vs Dpsp – Basic Differences
[16:0:0] TABLE head: Point | Fundamental Rights | DPSP
  [16:0:0.0] Courts | Justiciable | Non-justiciable (Art. 37)
  [16:0:0.1] Nature | Mostly negative – limit the State | Positive directions to the State
  [16:0:0.2] Aim | Political democracy | Social and economic democracy
  [16:0:0.3] Violation | Law void under Article 13 (to the extent of inconsistency) | Law cannot be struck down merely for breaking a DPSP
[16:0:1] - Main difference: DPSPs are **not enforceable in courts**, but are **fundamental in the governance** of the country; the State must apply them in making laws.
[16:0:2] - **"FRs are the means and DPSPs are the ends"** – complementary, not competing.
[16:0:3] - The **Preamble** may be used to decide the scope of FRs and DPSPs.
[16:0:4] - **Parliament may amend FRs to implement DPSPs** (within the basic structure).
[16:0:5] The **44th** Amendment did NOT give DPSPs primacy over Arts 14 and 19 – the attempt was the **42nd** (struck down in Minerva Mills).
## SUB 1 | Landmark Cases – Position and Order
[16:1:0] TABLE head: Case | Year | Position
  [16:1:0.0] A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras | 1950 | Narrow reading of Article 21 (personal liberty, not property)
  [16:1:0.1] Champakam Dorairajan | 1951 | FRs prevail over DPSPs; communal order violating Art. 29(2) struck down – first FR-DPSP conflict case
  [16:1:0.2] In re Kerala Education Bill | 1958 | Harmonious construction; FRs still prevail in direct conflict
  [16:1:0.3] Golaknath v. State of Punjab | 1967 | Parliament cannot amend FRs; FRs "transcendental"; prospective overruling; first time SC limited the amending power
  [16:1:0.4] Kesavananda Bharati | 1973 | FRs amendable but not the Basic Structure (doctrine first given)
  [16:1:0.5] Maneka Gandhi | 1978 | Widened Art. 21 – procedure must be fair, just and reasonable
  [16:1:0.6] Minerva Mills | 1980 | Balance between Parts III and IV is part of Basic Structure
  [16:1:0.7] Sanjeev Coke | 1983 | 39(b) includes privately owned resources; doubted Minerva's 31C ruling (obiter)
[16:1:1] - Chronology: **Gopalan → Champakam → Golaknath → Kesavananda → Minerva Mills → Sanjeev Coke**.
[16:1:2] - Bench size: **Kesavananda – 13 judges (largest)**; **Golaknath – 11 judges** (second largest).
[16:1:3] - Golaknath, Kesavananda, Minerva Mills concerned **property rights and amending power**.
[16:1:4] Champakam did NOT adopt harmonious construction – that was Kerala Education Bill (1958).
## SUB 2 | Constitutional Response to Champakam
[16:2:0] - **1st Amendment, 1951** inserted **Article 15(4)** – special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes, SCs, STs.
[16:2:1] - Right changed: **Article 15**, overriding the **Article 29(2)** bar applied in the case.
[16:2:2] 15(4) answered Champakam; 31A, 31B and the Ninth Schedule answered the land-reform judgments.
## SUB 3 | Amendments Responding to Courts

  [16:3:0.1] 4th | 1955 | – | Adequacy of compensation made non-justiciable

## SUB 4 | Article 31C – Step by Step
[16:4:0] TABLE head: Stage | What happened
  [16:4:0.0] 25th Amendment, 1971 | (1) Laws for 39(b) and (c) not void for violating Arts 14, 19 (and 31); (2) a declaration that a law gives effect to that policy cannot be questioned in court
  [16:4:0.1] Kesavananda, 1973 | First part upheld; second part (bar on judicial review) struck down
  [16:4:0.2] 42nd Amendment, 1976 | Extended protection to laws implementing any DPSP
  [16:4:0.3] Minerva Mills, 1980 | Struck down the extension; 31C back to 39(b), (c) only
  [16:4:0.4] Property Owners Association, 2024 | Original 31C (as upheld in Kesavananda) continues in force
[16:4:1] - Minerva Mills also held that an **Emergency proclamation is subject to judicial review**.
[16:4:2] - Scope today: narrow DPSP primacy, only for **39(b)-(c)** laws.
[16:4:3] APPSC G1 2024: "the first provision of 31C was invalidated in Kesavananda" is WRONG – it was the **second**.
## SUB 5 | Harmonious Construction and Basic Structure
[16:5:0] - Meaning: read **Part III and Part IV together** so that both operate as far as possible.
[16:5:1] - Cases: **Kerala Education Bill (1958)**, **Minerva Mills (1980)**.
[16:5:2] - Minerva Mills: FR-DPSP **balance is part of the Basic Structure**; absolute primacy to either destroys it.
[16:5:3] - Justice Chandrachud: the two together are the **conscience of the Constitution**.
[16:5:4] - DPSPs strengthen FRs: **48A + 21** = right to healthy environment; **45 + 21** → **21A** (86th Amendment, 2002).
[16:5:5] - Non-justiciability does not stop DPSPs from being used in interpretation.
## SUB 6 | Ninth Schedule and Article 31B
[16:6:0] - Added by the **1st Amendment, 1951**, under PM **Jawaharlal Nehru**, to protect **agrarian reforms** in certain States.
[16:6:1] - Grew to **over 280 entries**; not limited to land reforms.
[16:6:2] TABLE head: Case | Year | Rule
  [16:6:2.0] Waman Rao | 1981 | Entries after 24 April 1973 (Kesavananda date) open to challenge
  [16:6:2.1] I.R. Coelho | 2007 | No blanket immunity; test = Basic Structure + Arts 14, 19, 21 ("golden triangle")
[16:6:3] - Reservation Acts placed in the Ninth Schedule are also open to judicial review.
## SUB 7 | Article 39(b) – Material Resources of the Community
[16:7:0] TABLE head: Case | Year | View
  [16:7:0.0] Ranganatha Reddy | 1977 | Krishna Iyer (minority): includes all privately owned resources
  [16:7:0.1] Sanjeev Coke | 1983 | Followed Krishna Iyer view
  [16:7:0.2] Property Owners Association v. State of Maharashtra | 5 Nov 2024 | 9-judge Bench, 7:2, CJI D.Y. Chandrachud: not every private resource is a community material resource
[16:7:1] - Test: the resource's **nature, scarcity, effect on community well-being** and consequences of **concentration** in private hands.
[16:7:2] - Rejected the broad Krishna Iyer view.

=== NOTES 2:17 | Fundamental Duties – Article 51A, Swaran Singh Committee, Enforceability
## SUB 0 | Basics – Part Iva and Article 51A
[17:0:0] - Located in **Part IVA** (not Part VI-A) – contains a **single article, Article 51A**, placed after Part IV (DPSP).
[17:0:1] - Opening words: "It shall be the duty of **every citizen of India**."
[17:0:2] - Added by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** ("**Mini Constitution**"); in force **3 January 1977**; added a **new chapter** – duties are **not part of Fundamental Rights**.
[17:0:3] - The original 10 duties are called the **"10 commandments"**.
[17:0:4] - Recommended by the **Swaran Singh Committee (1976)**.
[17:0:5] - Number: originally **10**; presently **11** (11th by the **86th Amendment, 2002**).
[17:0:6] - Part IVA is one of **four Parts added after 1950**: **IVA, IXA, IXB, XIVA**.
[17:0:7] - Other Parts: **Part VI = The States (Articles 152–237)**; Part XI is NOT The Municipalities (that is IXA).
[17:0:8] 8 – 10 – 11 = suggested by Swaran Singh – added in 1976 – total after 2002

## SUB 2 | What Is Not A Fundamental Duty
[17:2:0] - **To protect the elderly, differently abled and children** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025, asked twice); the closest is 51A(k).
[17:2:1] - **To vote** in public/general elections.
[17:2:2] - **To pay taxes**; **family planning**.
[17:2:3] - To **protect monuments** and places of national importance – DPSP, **Article 49**.
[17:2:4] - To **protect weaker sections** from social injustice – DPSP, **Article 46**.
[17:2:5] - To strive towards **abolition of untouchability** – FR, **Article 17**.
[17:2:6] - To **organise village panchayats** – DPSP, **Article 40**.
[17:2:7] Real duties include scientific temper 51A(h), safeguarding public property 51A(i), abiding by the Constitution 51A(a). Check the list word for word.

## SUB 6 | Enforceability and Justiciability
[17:6:0] # Legal Status
[17:6:1] - **Non-justiciable**: courts **cannot directly enforce** or punish; **cannot be enforced by writs**; no legal sanction in the Constitution.
[17:6:2] - **Moral obligations** of citizens; called a "**code of moral precepts**"; critics call them **superfluous**.
[17:6:3] - **Parliament can enforce** them by **legislation**.
[17:6:4] - Courts use them to **interpret ambiguous laws**, to test **constitutionality**, and to judge the **reasonableness** of a law restricting an FR.
[17:6:5] - They do **not** give a ground for **suspending Fundamental Rights**.

## SUB 7 | Rights and Duties – Judicial Interpretation
[17:7:0] # Correlative Relationship
[17:7:1] - **Rights are correlative with duties** – every right has a corresponding duty.
[17:7:2] - **Holland**: others holding back (from interfering) is called a duty.
[17:7:3] - Example: **Article 19(1)(a)** (free speech) goes with **51A(c)** (sovereignty, unity, integrity).
[17:7:4] - Rights must be exercised with restraint and respect for others' rights.

## SUB 9 | Criticism and Significance
[17:9:0] - **Not exhaustive**: omits **voting, paying taxes, family planning**.
[17:9:1] - **Vague**: "noble ideals", "scientific temper", "composite culture".
[17:9:2] - **Non-justiciable**: a code of moral precepts.

[17:9:4] - Significance: remind citizens of obligations; help courts on reasonableness; back action against **anti-national activities**; promote civic virtue and patriotism.
