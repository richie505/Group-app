## SUB 0 | Tsunami – Warning Centres, 2004 Tsunami and International Centres
[0:0] # Tsunami basics
[0:1] - **Tsunami:** large waves from a sudden movement of the **ocean floor** – mostly an **undersea earthquake** (also submarine landslides, volcanic eruptions); Japanese "harbour wave". A **natural** disaster, not man-made.
[0:2] - **26 December 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami** (Sumatra–Andaman earthquake): hit Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Puducherry, Kerala and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands; **Indira Point** (southern tip of Great Nicobar) went **under the sea**.
[0:3] - Most exposed: the **east coast** and the **Andaman & Nicobar Islands**.
[0:4] # Indian warning system
[0:5] TABLE head: Question | Answer
  [0:5.0] Tsunami Warning Centre of India / ITEWC | Hyderabad, at INCOIS
  [0:5.1] INCOIS | Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services, Hyderabad; autonomous body (1999) under the Ministry of Earth Sciences (ESSO unit)
  [0:5.2] ITEWC set up | After the 2004 tsunami; inaugurated 15 Oct 2007, working 24x7 since 2007
  [0:5.3] Detection | Tsunamigenic earthquakes detected within ~10 minutes; seismic network, buoys, tide gauges
  [0:5.4] Other INCOIS tools | Real-time coastal risk / wave-height / vulnerable-building forecast (first in the world), SAMUDRA app, storm-surge and high-wave warnings
[0:6] - **Not** IMD Delhi, **not** NIO Goa, **not** NIOT Chennai – the tsunami warning system is run by **INCOIS, Hyderabad**.
[0:7] - The **IOC (UNESCO)-coordinated Indian Ocean Tsunami Warning System** was set up after 2004; India (ITEWC) is one of its regional tsunami service providers (with Australia, Indonesia).
[0:8] - **Mock drills:** IOC-UNESCO's **IOWave 18** (Sept 2018, India + 23 Indian Ocean nations); later IOWave-23, IOWave-25.

## SUB 1 | Landslides and Avalanches – Causes, Prone Areas, Zonation, Mitigation
[1:0] # Where and why
[1:1] - **About 12.6% of India's area (~0.42 million sq km)** is landslide-prone (GSI 12.6–15%): **Himalayas, North-East, Western Ghats (incl. Nilgiris)**; parts of the Eastern Ghats.
[1:2] - Landslides occur **mostly in hilly terrain** on **steep slopes of rocky mountains** – not in mud valleys, seashores or plateaus.
[1:3] - **Natural causes:** heavy rain and cloudbursts, earthquakes, fragile young fold mountains, steep slopes.
[1:4] - **Man-made causes:** **deforestation**, **road building**, **shifting cultivation**, mining and quarrying, changes in **land-use pattern**, hill cutting. **Increased forestation is NOT a cause** – it is a remedy.
[1:5] - India's vulnerability = **deforestation + heavy rainfall in mountains** (India has little volcanic activity).
[1:6] - **Himalayas:** landslide frequency has **increased**, with large-scale **mining**, roads and dams (A and R both true, R explains A in the keys).
[1:7] - Western Ghats/Nilgiris: steep escarpments and orographic rain (not permafrost).
[1:8] # Major events
[1:9] TABLE head: Event | Type | Year
  [1:9.0] Malpa (Pithoragarh, Uttarakhand) | Landslide – Kailash-Mansarovar pilgrims killed | 18 Aug 1998
  [1:9.1] Wayanad (Kerala) | Landslides after very heavy rain | July 2024
  [1:9.2] Badrinath (Mana), Chamoli | Snow avalanche hit a BRO labour camp | 28 Feb 2025
  [1:9.3] Kedarnath | Cloudburst + flash flood | June 2013
  [1:9.4] Chamoli | Glacier (rock-ice) flood | 2021
[1:10] - **Avalanches** = rapid flow of snow and ice down steep (30°–45°) slopes – mainly in **high Himalayan hilly terrain**; nodal ministry: Defence (DGRE).
[1:11] - Hanging-glacier collapse in high seismic zones can trigger **GLOFs**.
[1:12] # Landslide hazard zonation
[1:13] - **Landslide (hazard) zoning:** division of hill areas into **homogeneous spatial units** by degree of **actual or potential** landslide susceptibility, hazard or risk; maps are **probabilistic** (likelihood, not exact time/place).
[1:14] - Preparing a full zonation map needs **huge data** on many variables over large slope areas, and **aerial photographs, satellite images and remote sensing** help collect it (both conclusions follow).
[1:15] TABLE head: Rock falls (number/annum/km of cliff or rock cut) | Frequency zone
  [1:15.0] > 10 | Very high
  [1:15.1] 1 to 10 | High
  [1:15.2] 0.1 to 1.0 | Moderate
  [1:15.3] 0.01 to 0.1 | Low
  [1:15.4] < 0.01 | Very low
[1:16] - NDMA's **National Landslide Risk Management Strategy (2019)**; GSI national susceptibility mapping; GSI **LANDSLIP** regional early warning system.
[1:17] # Mitigation
[1:18] - **Cheapest and most effective:** **increasing vegetative cover** (afforestation); walls and engineered works cost more.
[1:19] - **NCERT list:** **area-specific measures**, **limiting agriculture to valleys and moderate slopes**, **large-scale afforestation and bunds** to reduce water flow (all three).
[1:20] - **Structural:** retaining walls, rock bolting, wire mesh, drainage, slope grading. **Non-structural:** hazard zonation mapping, early warning, land-use regulation. **Regulatory:** restrict construction in high-risk zones.

## SUB 3 | Seismic Zoning Map of India
[3:0] - Prepared by **BIS** (**IS 1893**); **four zones – II, III, IV, V** (Zone V = very high damage risk, MSK IX+).
[3:1] - **Killari (Latur) 1993** quake hit a "low-risk" area → **2002** revision merged **Zone I into Zone II**.
[3:2] TABLE head: Zone | Share of area | Places
  [3:2.0] V | ~11% | Entire North-East (e.g. Meghalaya), J&K, Ladakh, HP, Uttarakhand, Kutch, North Bihar, Andaman & Nicobar; Guwahati, Aizawl
  [3:2.1] IV | ~18% | Delhi, parts of J&K/HP; Srinagar (IV–V)
  [3:2.2] III | ~30% | Indo-Gangetic plains, coasts, Kerala, Goa; Amaravati region
  [3:2.3] II | ~41% (largest single zone) | Most of the stable peninsula – Hyderabad (least prone)
[3:3] - Karnataka, Maharashtra and Kerala are **not** Zone V. **Andhra Pradesh: Zones II and III only** (no Zone V).
[3:4] - **Withdrawn revision:** a BIS probabilistic (PSHA) map adding a **Zone VI** was **rolled back in March 2026** – the four-zone map still applies.
[3:5] - Codes: **IS 1893** = seismic design criteria; **IS 13920** = ductile detailing. GSI tracks crustal strain through **34 GNSS stations**.
[3:6] TABLE head: Earthquake | Type / year
  [3:6.0] Koyna (Maharashtra) | Reservoir-induced, 1967
  [3:6.1] Killari (Latur) | Intraplate, 1993
  [3:6.2] Bhuj (Kutch) | 26 Jan 2001 → DM Act 2005
[3:7] - AP: reservoir-induced tremors not ruled out near **Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Polavaram**. **Liquefaction** hazard: Indo-Gangetic plain.
[3:8] > "Majority of India's area" – official key Zone III; by the MoES shares Zone II (~41%) is the largest single zone.

## SUB 4 | Cyclone-prone Coasts
[4:0] - **Cyclone = low-pressure area**; Indian cyclones start from low-pressure areas in the **Bay of Bengal near the Andaman Sea / Andaman & Nicobar Islands** (8–14°N).
[4:1] - Bay of Bengal : Arabian Sea ≈ **4 : 1** → the **east coast** is hit far more (Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu).
[4:2] - **Most cyclone-prone coast:** **Odisha (Orissa) coast** in most books (most landfalls over a century); **Andhra Pradesh** was hit most in 1995–2020 (NDMI tally) and is the APPSC GS 2025 key's "most cyclone-prone State".
[4:3] - **West coast:** **Gujarat** is the most vulnerable (Kandla 1998, Tauktae 2021, Biparjoy 2023).
[4:4] - **Mangroves** shield coasts; their clearing in the **Mahanadi delta** added to Odisha's cyclone risk.
[4:5] - Exposure: **13 coastal States/UTs**, **84 coastal districts**. AP's most vulnerable stretch: **Ongole–Machilipatnam**; deadliest AP cyclone: **1977** cyclone.
[4:6] - IMD cyclone warning centres (7): Chennai, Mumbai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Thiruvananthapuram, **Visakhapatnam**, Bhubaneswar – **not Hyderabad**. ISRO runs **NDEM**; GIS and remote sensing map coastal vulnerability.
[4:7] ~~ Sources: Rocket Disaster Mgmt p.30, p.34-36; PMF Internal Security & DM 02 p.55; Down To Earth (cyclones account for nearly half of climate-disaster deaths; AP hit most 1995-2020); app notes 4:45 (sub 5)
