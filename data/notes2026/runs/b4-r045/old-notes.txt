## SUB 0 | India's Hazard and Vulnerability Profile
[45:0:0] - **Disaster = Hazard x Vulnerability.**
[45:0:1] TABLE head: Hazard | Exposure
  [45:0:1.0] Earthquake | ~59% of landmass prone to moderate-to-high earthquakes (NDMA, 2023)
  [45:0:1.1] Flood | ~40 million ha, about 12% of land (not 40%)
  [45:0:1.2] Drought | ~68% of cultivable area vulnerable
  [45:0:1.3] Cyclone / tsunami | ~5,700 km of the ~7,516 km coastline
  [45:0:1.4] Landslide | 12.6–15% of area, about 0.42 million sq km (GSI)
[45:0:2] - Under the withdrawn 2025 map, more than **60%** of land would have been moderate-to-high earthquake risk.
[45:0:3] - **Tsunami** is a **natural**, not man-made, disaster.
[45:0:4] - **DM Act 2005**: shifted focus from post-disaster **relief → prevention, mitigation, preparedness**.
## SUB 1 | Seismic Zoning Map
[45:1:0] # The Official Map
[45:1:1] - Prepared by **BIS** under **IS 1893**; **four zones: II, III, IV, V** (Zone V = highest risk).
[45:1:2] - **2002 revision**: after the **1993 Killari** quake struck a **low-risk** area, old **Zone I was merged into Zone II**.
[45:1:3] # The Withdrawn Revision (Zone VI)
[45:1:4] - BIS revised map used **probabilistic seismic hazard assessment (PSHA)**; added a **Zone VI**; drew on **69,519 events** (2600 BC–2019) and **36 seismogenic source zones** (IIT Madras study, 22 Mar 2026).
[45:1:5] - **Rolled back in March 2026** (8 and 12 Mar 2026) after criticism over method and cost → the **four-zone map still applies**.
[45:1:6] Zones merged = I into II (2002). Zone VI = proposed, then withdrawn (Mar 2026).
[45:1:7] # Zones → Places
[45:1:8] TABLE head: Zone | Places
  [45:1:8.0] Zone V | Kutch (Rann of Kutch), North-East, Himalayan belt (J&K, Ladakh, HP, Uttarakhand), North Bihar, Andaman & Nicobar
  [45:1:8.1] Zone IV | Delhi
  [45:1:8.2] Zone III | Amaravati (core capital region, Vijayawada, Guntur)
  [45:1:8.3] Zones II–III | Most of the stable southern peninsula
[45:1:9] - **Andhra Pradesh has no area in Zone V.**
[45:1:10] - **Crustal strain**: GSI monitors through **34 GNSS stations**.

## SUB 4 | Landslide-prone Areas
[45:4:0] # Regions
[45:4:1] TABLE head: Region | Why prone
  [45:4:1.0] Himalaya (J&K, Ladakh, HP, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal) | Young, fragile geology, active tectonics
  [45:4:1.1] North-East | Steep slopes, heavy rain
  [45:4:1.2] Western Ghats incl. Nilgiris | Steep escarpments, orographic rain (not permafrost)
  [45:4:1.3] Eastern Ghats, Vindhyas | Parts of AP, Odisha
[45:4:2] - Landslides occur **mostly in hilly areas** (PYQ).
[45:4:3] # Landslide Hazard Zonation
[45:4:4] - **Landslide Hazard Zonation (landslide zoning)**: divides hilly areas into **homogeneous units** by degree of **actual or potential** landslide susceptibility, hazard or risk (PYQ).
[45:4:5] - Zonation maps are **probabilistic** – they show likelihood, not exact forecasts.
[45:4:6] Zonation does not predict the exact time or place of a landslide.
[45:4:7] # Mitigation
[45:4:8] - **Structural**: retaining walls, rock bolting, wire mesh, drainage improvement, slope grading.
[45:4:9] - **Non-structural**: Landslide Hazard Zonation mapping, early warning, land-use regulation, awareness.
[45:4:10] - **Regulatory**: restrict construction in high-risk zones; enforce **mountain-specific (hill) building codes**; regulate settlements.
[45:4:11] - **LANDSLIP** project: GSI building a **regional Landslide Early Warning System**.
## SUB 5 | Cyclone-prone Coasts
[45:5:0] - Cyclones come from both the **Bay of Bengal** and the **Arabian Sea**; the Bay of Bengal generates about **four times** as many → **east coast** hit far more.
[45:5:1] - Exposure: **13 coastal states/UTs** and **84 coastal districts** cyclone-affected.
[45:5:2] - **Most cyclone-prone state: Andhra Pradesh** (APPSC GS 2025), then **Odisha, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal**.
[45:5:3] TABLE head: IMD cyclone warning centres (7) | Chennai, Mumbai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Thiruvananthapuram, Visakhapatnam, Bhubaneswar
[45:5:4] - **Hyderabad is NOT** an IMD cyclone warning centre.
[45:5:5] - **ISRO DMS**: maps coastal vulnerability; runs the **NDEM** database. Remote sensing, **GIS** (spatial data) and satellites address coastal disaster vulnerability.
## SUB 6 | Tsunami-prone Coasts and Warning Centres
[45:6:0] - Most exposed: **east coast and Andaman & Nicobar Islands** (as in the 2004 tsunami).
[45:6:1] - **Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC)**: at **INCOIS, Hyderabad**, set up after the **26 Dec 2004** tsunami, operating since **2007**; seismic monitoring, ocean buoys, sirens.
[45:6:2] - **INCOIS**: autonomous body since **1999** under the **Ministry of Earth Sciences**; unit of the Earth System Science Organisation.
[45:6:3] - **Indian Ocean Tsunami Warning System**: coordinated by **IOC**, set up after 2004; regional centres in India, Indonesia, Australia and others.
[45:6:4] Tsunami warning = INCOIS, Hyderabad (not IMD).
