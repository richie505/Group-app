=== Suicide – Durkheim's Types and NCRB Data
# Durkheim's types (*Le Suicide*, 1897)
| Type | Cause | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Egoistic | **Too little integration** | Isolated individual |
| Altruistic | **Too much integration** – the group demands the death | **Sati (obligatory altruistic)**, hara-kiri |
| Anomic | **Too little regulation** (crisis, sudden change, normlessness) | Economic crash |
| Fatalistic | **Too much regulation** | Slaves, harshly ruled persons |
- **Sati = altruistic suicide**, not anomic; "passive" and "self-respect" suicide are not Durkheim's types (APPSC Sociology 2000: answer "None of the above" when altruistic is not printed).
- **Durkheim's *Le Suicide*:** published in **1897**; anomie = normlessness caused by rapid social change.
# NCRB ADSI data
- **ADSI 2015 – farmer suicides:** **bankruptcy/indebtedness** first (**38.7%**), then **farming-related issues** (19.5%) (**APPSC G1 2017**).
- **ADSI 2024** (NCRB, released May 2026): **1,70,746 suicides**; **daily wagers ~31%**; homemakers 22,113; unemployed 14,778; students 14,488; **drug-overdose deaths 978 (up 50%)**.
- **Top States (ADSI 2024):** **Maharashtra** (**22,174**, about 13%), then **Tamil Nadu** (19,965) and **Madhya Pradesh** (15,491).

=== Attempt to Suicide in Law
| Law / case | Provision |
|---|---|
| Sec. 309 IPC | Attempt punishable with simple imprisonment **up to one year** and/or fine |
| P. Rathinam (1994) | Held Sec. 309 **invalid** (later overruled in Gian Kaur, 1996) |
| Law Commission, **210th Report** | Recommended **deleting** Sec. 309 |
| Mental Healthcare Act 2017, **Sec. 115** | **Presumes severe stress** → no trial or punishment; State must provide care |
| BNS 2023 (in force 1 July 2024) | No general attempted-suicide offence; **Sec. 226** = attempt to suicide to coerce a public servant |
- **Suicide** is the crime whose **attempt** was punishable but whose **completion** cannot be punished (the culprit is dead); murder, theft, rape are punishable either way (MPPSC 1991).
- **Tele-MANAS:** tele-mental-health helpline, launched **10 Oct 2022**, toll-free **14416**.

=== Human Trafficking
- **Meaning:** trade in humans for sexual slavery, forced labour or commercial sexual exploitation – a form of **modern slavery**.
- **Article 23 (Fundamental Right – Right against Exploitation)** prohibits **traffic in human beings, begar and forced labour** – it is **NOT a Directive Principle** (UPSC 2008).
- **Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956** (SITA 1956, renamed ITPA in 1986) is the main law on sex trafficking; **BNS Sec. 143** defines trafficking; also POCSO 2012, Bonded Labour (Abolition) Act 1976; India signed the **Palermo Protocol**.
- **Most cases – West Bengal:** NCRB *Crime in India 2016*: **West Bengal 3,579 cases (~44%)**, then Rajasthan (1,422).
- **Anti Human Trafficking Units (AHTUs):** **696** across States/UTs (NCRB *Crime in India 2020*); ~2,183 trafficking cases in 2023 (NCRB).
- UNODC: India is both a **high-origin and high-destination** country.

=== Beggary
- **West Bengal** tops the Social Justice Ministry's Census 2011 list of beggars and vagrants (**81,224**), then **UP** (65,835), **AP** (30,218), **Bihar** (29,723); India total **4,13,670** (BPSC 2017).
- **Bombay Prevention of Begging Act, 1959**: punitive model copied by many States.
- **Delhi HC (2018, Harsh Mander)**: **decriminalised begging** in Delhi.
- **SC (2021)**: refused to ban begging; a **socio-economic problem**.
- **SMILE (MoSJE, 2022)**: sub-scheme for **comprehensive rehabilitation of persons engaged in begging** ("Bhiksha Vritti Mukt Bharat"); rights-based, not punitive; 216 cities, target 295 by 2030-31.
- **Indore**: **first officially beggar-free city (2025)**.

=== Alcoholism
- **Art. 47 (DPSP)**: State to prohibit **intoxicating drinks** and drugs injurious to health **except for medicinal purposes**.
- **Health effect:** chronic alcohol and drug use → **liver cirrhosis** and **nervous-system damage** (not leukaemia/lymphoma).
- **Anti-Arrack Movement**: began **1992**, **Dubagunta** village, **Nellore** district (not Nizamabad); neo-literate women of the literacy campaign; led by **Dubagunta Rosamma**.
- Precedent: **R. Venkataratnam Naidu's Social Purity Association (1891)**, a temperance campaign.

=== Jogini (Devadasi) System
- **Jogini/Devadasi/Basavi:** dedication of girls (mostly Dalit) to a village deity, leading to sexual exploitation – a form of trafficking and social evil.
- **Law:** **AP Devadasis (Prohibition of Dedication) Act, 1988/89**; AP rehabilitates Jogin women through APSCCFC economic-support schemes.
- **Most prevalent (Telangana):** old **Mahbubnagar** district (about **2,000 Joginis**); also Nizamabad, Medak, Adilabad; only marginal in Karimnagar, Warangal, Khammam.

=== Social Defence and the AP Angle
- **Scope:** prevention and rehabilitation for drug and alcohol abuse, beggary, destitute elderly, transgender persons.
- **National Institute of Social Defence (NISD)**: under **MoSJE**; implements NMBA; trains officials.
- Shift from **punishment** (Sec. 309, begging laws) to **health and rehabilitation** (MHCA, SMILE, NMBA).
