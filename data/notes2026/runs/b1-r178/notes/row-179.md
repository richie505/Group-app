=== Railways – First Lines, Purpose and the Guarantee System
# Firsts
| Milestone | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **First passenger train** | **16 April 1853** | **Bori Bunder (Bombay) → Thane**, **34 km**, 14 coaches, 3 locomotives; built by the **Great Indian Peninsula Railway Company** |
| Dalhousie's **Railway Minute** | 1853 | Trunk-line network for **military, commercial and administrative** purposes |
| First line in **Eastern India / West Bengal** | 1854 | **Howrah → Hooghly** |
| First line in North India | – | **Delhi → Agra** |
| First line in South India | 1 July 1856 | Royapuram (Madras) → **Wallajah Road** (Arcot); **Madras Railway Company** |
| First railway in Andhra | 1862 | **Puttur–Renigunta** |
| Passenger trains in England | **1825** | Stockton and Darlington Railway |
- **Governor-General:** **Lord Dalhousie** – railways laid in his time; he is linked with the first train (not Bentinck, Ripon, Curzon).
- **Credit:** railways, post and telegraph = **Dalhousie**; **state railways = Mayo**.
# Who built them and why
- **Initially built and run by private British companies** under the **guarantee system** – a **5% minimum return** on capital, **paid from Indian revenues** whether or not the lines made a profit; the state built lines itself from **1869**.
- **Official reasons:** faster movement, communication, administration, **troop movement**.
- **Colonial economic purpose:** carry **raw materials to the ports** and **British goods into Indian markets**; market for British capital goods.
- **Unintended effects:** a **national market**, shared travel across castes, faster nationalist mobilisation.
- **Karl Marx (1853):** the railway system would become in India "truly the **forerunner of modern industry**".
- **Indian Railways** – the **biggest enterprise of the Government of India** today.
- Nationalist economic critique: **Indian capitalists** demanded "**Indian domination of Indian markets**".
# Railways in Hyderabad State
- **Wadi–Secunderabad line** (Nizam's Guaranteed State Railway): built **1871–74**, opened **9 October 1874** (NextGen says 1878; printed key 1872 – both wrong).
- **Chanda Railway agitation (1883):** protest against extending the **Nizam's State Railway** to Chanda (Chandrapur) on terms favouring a London company; led by **Dr Aghorenath Chattopadhyaya** (Principal) – deported **20 May 1883**; the first public political agitation in Hyderabad.

=== Press Laws and the Vernacular Press Act, 1878
# Press laws in sequence
| Law | Year | Governor-General | Provision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Censorship of Press Act** | **1799** | **Wellesley** | Wartime **pre-censorship**; papers to print names of **printer, editor and proprietor** (the **first press regulations**) |
| Licensing Regulations | 1823 | **John Adams** (acting) | Press without licence a penal offence; Mirat-ul-Akhbar stopped |
| **Press Act (Metcalfe Act)** | **1835** | Charles **Metcalfe** | Repealed 1823 rules – "**liberator of the Indian press**" |
| Licensing Act | 1857 | Canning | Emergency of the revolt |
| **Registration Act** | **1867** | John Lawrence | Printer, publisher and place on every book/paper; copy to government |
| Sedition, Sec. 124A IPC | 1870 | Mayo | "Exciting disaffection" |
| **Vernacular Press Act** | **1878** | **Lytton** | Gagging Act |
| **Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act** | **1908** | Minto II | Magistrates could seize presses |
| Indian Press Act | 1910 | Minto II | Revived the worst VPA features – security deposits |
- **Acts to control the press:** **1835, 1867, 1878, 1908**.
- Press censorship was **primarily implemented** by **Wellesley** (1799); Hastings relaxed it in 1818.
- 1908 and 1910 Acts are both Minto II era but **not cause and effect**.
- Annie Besant's papers had to deposit Rs 2,000, then Rs 10,000 under the Press Act (1916–17).
# Vernacular Press Act, 1878
- Passed **14 March 1878** under **Lord Lytton**, **modelled on the Irish Press Laws**, to **control Indian-language newspapers** (not to encourage them).
- **Background:** criticism of Lytton over the **1876–77 famine** and the costly **Delhi Durbar (1877)**.
- **Provisions:** the **district magistrate** (without prior government permission) could make printers/publishers sign a **bond**, demand **forfeitable security**, **seize presses** for repeat offences; **no appeal** to a court; exemption only by **submitting proofs to a government censor**.
- **Discriminatory:** applied **only to vernacular papers**, not English ones; about 20 English and 200 vernacular papers in 1878 (textbook figure).
- **Evasion:** **Amrita Bazar Patrika** turned into an **English paper overnight**.
- Proceedings under it: Som Prakash, Bharat Mihir, Dacca Prakash, Samachar.
- **Repeal:** **1882** by **Lord Ripon**; pre-censorship dropped, a **press commissioner** appointed.

=== Telegraph, Post and the Press Today
# Telegraph and post
- **First telegraph line:** Calcutta → **Diamond Harbour**, **W.B. O'Shaughnessy** (experimental 1850; opened **1851**); Calcutta–Agra 1853.
- Telegraph trunk lines linked Calcutta, Bombay, Madras and Peshawar (Dalhousie); **India–Europe telegraph 1865** (John Lawrence, 1864–69).
- **Post Office Act 1854 (Dalhousie):** **postage stamps** and a **uniform rate** (half anna a letter) regardless of distance.
- **First stamps:** **Scinde Dawk (1852)**, Karachi; the first all-India stamps (1854) were printed at the **Survey of India** (Surveyor General's) office, Calcutta.
# Press today (as of 2026)
- **Press and Registration of Periodicals Act, 2023** replaced the **1867** Act (in force 1 March 2024); the RNI is now the **Press Registrar General of India**.
- **World Press Freedom Index 2026 (RSF, 30 April 2026):** India **157th of 180**.
- **Press Council** (Press Council Act, **1978**) has no punitive powers.
