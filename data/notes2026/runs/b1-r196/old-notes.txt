## SUB 1 | Colonial Scholars of Telugu
[196:1:0] # Scholar → Contribution
[196:1:1] TABLE head: Scholar | Contribution
  [196:1:1.0] Benjamin Schultze | German Lutheran, Danish-Halle Mission; came 1719; Telugu grammar; returned 1743; Telugu books printed at Halle
  [196:1:1.1] Colin Mackenzie (1754-1821) | First Surveyor General (1815); collected Kaifiyats with the Kavali brothers (Borraiah, Venkataramaiah, Lakshmaiah); reached the Amaravati Stupa in 1797
  [196:1:1.2] William Carey | Serampore Mission; complete Telugu Bible (1812-19); Telugu typefaces
  [196:1:1.3] A.D. Campbell | First systematic Telugu Grammar (1816) and Telugu-English dictionary
  [196:1:1.4] Thomas Munro | Kept Telugu as an administrative language
  [196:1:1.5] C.P. Brown (1798-1884) | Collector of Cuddapah; learnt Telugu from Kodandarama Pantulu; Grammar 1840; dictionaries 1853; ~2,000 Vemana poems, Verses of Vemana (1829)
[196:1:2] # C.P. Brown
[196:1:3] - Edited **Nannaya, Tikkana, Pothana**; rescued palm-leaf texts through missionary presses.
[196:1:4] - Preserved Mackenzie's **Kaifiyats** in the Madras Library.
[196:1:5] - Called Telugu the **"Italian of the East"** – phrase first used by **Nicolo de Conti**.
[196:1:6] - **Gentoo:** European name for **Telugu** (and Coromandel Hindus), from Portuguese **gentio** (gentile).
[196:1:7] - **C.P. Brown Memorial Trust Library = Kadapa (Cuddapah)** (APPSC G2 2001, 2005).
[196:1:8] First Telugu grammar: Schultze (early) vs **A.D. Campbell** (first systematic, 1816); Brown's Grammar 1840, dictionaries 1853.

## SUB 5 | "fathers" and Key Persons
[196:5:0] - **Kandukuri Veeresalingam** – **Father of the Telugu Renaissance**; his **Vivekavardhini** (from 1874) attacked Kokkonda's **Sanjeevani**.
[196:5:1] - **Kokkonda Venkataratnam** – orthodox; Andhra Bhasha Johnson; Mahamahopadhyaya 1907.
[196:5:2] - **Prolaya Nayaka** – led the post-Kakatiya liberation confederacy.
[196:5:3] - **Bhakti Movement** – Saint **Ramanuja**; devotion to Vishnu and social equality.

=== NOTES 1:198 | Growth of Nationalism – Factors; Pre-congress Political Associations
## SUB 0 | Factors of Indian Nationalism
[198:0:0] # Unintended Results of British Rule
[198:0:1] - **Political and administrative unification**: railways, telegraph, post and **uniform laws** tied India together.
[198:0:2] - **Western education**: liberal ideas (liberty, equality, democracy); **English as a link language** across regions.
[198:0:3] - Rise of a **new educated middle class** – lawyers, journalists, teachers – who led the early associations.
[198:0:4] - **Press and literature** spread political ideas; history-writing revived pride in India's past.
[198:0:5] - Inspiration from the **American, French and Italian** freedom struggles.
[198:0:6] # Reactions to British Rule
