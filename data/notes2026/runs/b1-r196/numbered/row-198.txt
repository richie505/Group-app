[0:1] - **All the above were pre-Congress bodies** (INC 1885) – the Indian Association (1876) included.
[0:2] - **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** sent the **1875 petition to the House of Commons** demanding India's **direct representation in the British Parliament**.
[0:3] - **Debendranath Tagore** was a founder (secretary) of the **British Indian Association**; he reorganised, but did not found, the Brahmo Samaj (Rammohan Roy, 1828).
[0:4] - **G.V. Joshi at the Delhi Durbar (1877):** asked the Queen to give Indians the **same political and social status as British subjects**.
[0:5] - **Indian Association:** fought the ICS age cut (21 → 19), the Arms and Vernacular Press Acts; held the all-India conference of **Dec 1883**.
[0:6] - **Chronology codes:** Bangabhasha 1836 → Landholders 1838 → BBIS 1843 → Indian League 1875; Bombay Assn 1852 → Indian League 1875 → Indian Assn 1876 → Madras Mahajana Sabha 1884; East India Assn 1866 → PSS 1870 → MMS 1884 → Bombay Presidency Assn 1885.
[0:7] - **Nature:** early bodies were **regional**, led by **landlords or the urban elite**, worked by **petitions and memorials**; after 1858 the **educated middle class** led them, and they grew into all-India coordination → **INC 1885**.

## SUB 1 | Nationalist Literature – Anandamath, Vande Mataram, Nil Darpan
[1:0] TABLE head: Work | Author | Fact
  [1:0.0] Nil Darpan (Neeldarpan) | Dinabandhu Mitra | Written 1858-59, published 1860; cruelty of indigo planters – earliest of Neeldarpan / Satyendranath Tagore's ICS (1863-64) / Arya Samaj (1875) / Anandamath (1882)
  [1:0.1] Anandamath | Bankim Chandra Chatterjee | 1882; set in the Sannyasi Rebellion (1763-1800); contains Vande Mataram
  [1:0.2] Durgeshnandini | Bankim Chandra Chatterjee | His first novel (1865)
  [1:0.3] Rajasimha, Rajani, Indira | Bankim Chandra Chatterjee | Other novels
[1:1] - **Anandamath = "Bible of Bengali Patriotism"** – the book associated with the rise of the national movement (not Gitanjali, Gora, Satyarth Prakash, Gita Rahasya).
[1:2] - **Bankim made the Sannyasi Rebellion famous** – not the Bhil, Rangpur or Kuka revolts.
[1:3] - **Vande Mataram:** composed by **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee** – India's **National Song** (adopted 24 Jan 1950); **first sung at the INC by Rabindranath Tagore at the Calcutta session, 1896** (Tagore sang it, he did not compose it).
[1:4] - **Telugu translation** of Vande Mataram: **Y. Doraiswamayya and M. Ramarao**; "**The Cry of Mother India**" (Krishna Patrika, **15 Dec 1905**) – first nationalist song of Andhra.
[1:5] - **Nationalist poets of Bengal:** **Rangalal Banerjee, Hem Chandra Banerjee, Nabin Chandra Sen** – **Shiv Kumar Batalvi** is a post-independence Punjabi poet, not one of them.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 3 p.7, p.102, p.123; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.18; Spectrum p.547; app notes 1:198 (sub 6)
## SUB 2 | Madras and Andhra Associations
[2:0] # Madras Native Association (26 February 1852)
[2:1] - **Founder and first president:** **Gazula (Gajula) Lakshminarasu Chetty** – first person from Andhra in the national movement; also ran **The Crescent** (1844); secretary **Somasundaram Chettiyar**.
[2:2] - **First political body of South India**; began as a branch of the British Indian Association, then independent.
[2:3] - **Petitions:** 1852 (before the **Charter renewal of 1853**), **1859** and **1861** – against ryotwari over-assessment, torture by revenue men, the salt monopoly and **moturpha**, a **tax on trades and occupations** (looms).
[2:4] - Its agitation helped bring the **Torture Commission (1854)**.
[2:5] # Madras Mahajana Sabha (16 May 1884)
[2:6] - **President: P. Rangaiah Naidu**; **secretary: P. Anandacharlu** (NextGen); founders with M. Viraraghavachariar and G. Subramania Iyer.
[2:7] - Demanded larger councils and more Indian representation.
[2:8] # District associations
[2:9] - **Krishna District Association (1892):** organised by **Ramaswamy Gupta**; **first district association in the Madras Presidency**; conferences at **Guntur (July 1892)** and Machilipatnam (1893).
[2:10] - **Inspiration:** the **formation of the INC (1885)** led to the district associations.
[2:11] - **Angalur conference (1900)** of the Association demanded **agricultural banks**; its 1899 resolution led to **Krishna Patrika (1902)**.
[2:12] - **Kakinada (Coconada) Literary Association:** memorandums (1884-85) for a **higher ICS age limit** and **simultaneous examinations**.
[2:13] > Pairs: Madras Native Association **1852** ✓, Madras Mahajana Sabha **1884** ✓, Krishna District Association **1892** (1900 = Angalur conference ✗).

## SUB 3 | Factors of Nationalism; Lytton and the Ilbert Bill
[3:0] # Factors
[3:1] - **Political and administrative unification** – railways, telegraph, post, uniform laws; **K.M. Panikkar**: "the most notable achievement of British rule was the **unification of India**".
[3:2] - **Western education** – liberal ideas; **English as a link language**; rise of the **educated middle class**.
[3:3] - **Press and literature**; **rediscovery of India's past**; socio-religious reform.
[3:4] - **Contemporary world movements** – South American, Greek, **Italian** and Irish struggles; **German unification** – **Bismarck**'s "**blood and iron**" speech (1862).
[3:5] - **Economic critique** – drain of wealth, ruin of artisans.
[3:6] - Early associations trained the educated class in **petitions, memorials and all-India coordination** → INC 1885.
[3:7] # Lytton's resented measures (1876-80)
[3:8] TABLE head: Measure | Year | Why resented
  [3:8.0] ICS maximum age cut | 1876 | 21 → 19 (Lytton's period; ordered by Secretary of State Salisbury)
  [3:8.1] Delhi Durbar | 1877 | Proclaimed Queen Victoria Empress of India during famine
  [3:8.2] Vernacular Press Act | 1878 | Gagged the Indian-language press; Amrita Bazar Patrika (1868) turned English overnight in 1878
  [3:8.3] Arms Act | 1878 | Disarmed Indians but spared Europeans
  [3:8.4] Second Afghan War | 1878-80 | Burden on Indian revenues
[3:9] # Ilbert Bill (1883)
[3:10] - **Ripon**'s government, drafted by **Sir Courtenay Ilbert (Law Member)**: Indian magistrates and judges could **try European British subjects**; European agitation forced a compromise – a European accused could claim a jury of **at least half Europeans**; passed **25 January 1884**. **Not a measure of Lytton.**

## SUB 4 | Surendranath Banerjee
[4:0] - **ICS:** passed **1869**, **dismissed in 1874** on a minor irregularity – the leader the British dismissed from the ICS.
[4:1] - Founded the **Indian Association (1876)** with A.M. Bose; toured India against the ICS age cut (**21 → 19**).
[4:2] - Convened the **Indian National Conference (1883, 1885)**, which **merged with the INC in 1886**.
[4:3] - Title **"Rashtraguru"**; paper **The Bengalee** (from 1879); autobiography **A Nation in Making**.
[4:4] - INC president at **Poona 1895** and **Ahmedabad 1902** – not Surat 1907.
[4:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.277, p.279; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.43; app notes 1:198 (sub 5)
