## SUB 0 | Ramsar Convention
[42:0:0] # The Convention
[42:0:1] - Adopted in **1971** at **Ramsar, Iran**; purpose: **conservation and wise use of wetlands**. India joined in **1982**.
[42:0:2] - **World Wetlands Day: 2 February** (marks the adoption of the Convention).
[42:0:3] - Other days (distractors): 22 March World Water Day; 22 April Earth Day; 5 June World Environment Day.
[42:0:4] # Wetland Definition
[42:0:5] - Marsh, fen, peatland or water; **natural or artificial**; fresh, brackish or salt; includes **marine water up to 6 m deep at low tide**.
[42:0:6] # Montreux Record
[42:0:7] - Register of Ramsar sites where **changes in ecological character** have occurred, are occurring or are likely to occur (technology, pollution, other human interference).
[42:0:8] - India's entries: **Keoladeo** and **Loktak**.
[42:0:9] # Court Ruling
[42:0:10] - Supreme Court: Ramsar status needs heightened scrutiny; **no mining within 10 km** of a Ramsar site without approval of **NBWL** (National Board for Wildlife) and **MoEFCC**; extended in 2026 to all wetland conservation reserves.

## SUB 2 | Tiger Reserves
[42:2:0] # Project Tiger and NTCA
[42:2:1] - **Project Tiger** launched **1 April 1973** with **9 reserves**; formally launched at **Corbett** – **first tiger reserve (1973)**.
[42:2:2] - **NTCA** (National Tiger Conservation Authority) set up in **2006** under the amended Wildlife (Protection) Act – a **statutory body**.
[42:2:3] - **M-STrIPES**: app-based patrolling and monitoring tool used in tiger reserves.
[42:2:4] - **All India Tiger Estimation 2022**: average **3,682 tigers** (2006 baseline: 1,411).
[42:2:5] - Growing share of tigers live **outside** notified reserves → more human–tiger conflict.

## SUB 3 | Elephant Reserves
[42:3:0] - **Project Elephant: February 1992**; **33 elephant reserves in 14 States**.
[42:3:1] - ERs **overlap** tiger reserves, sanctuaries and reserved forests (not limited to areas outside TRs).
[42:3:2] - **Most ERs: Assam and Tamil Nadu (5 each)**; Odisha has 3 – Mayurbhanj, Mahanadi, Sambalpur.
[42:3:3] - DNA-based census (Oct 2025): **22,446** wild elephants; Karnataka has the largest population.
## SUB 4 | Important Bird Areas and Bird Deaths
[42:4:0] - **IBAs**: named by **BirdLife International**; Indian partner **BNHS** (Bombay Natural History Society).
[42:4:1] - **Telineelapuram and Telukunchi** (Srikakulam, AP): IBAs for breeding **painted storks** and **spot-billed pelicans**.
[42:4:2] - **Rushikulya mouth** (Odisha): IBA hosting **olive ridley arribada**.
[42:4:3] - **Sambhar Lake** (Rajasthan), **Nov 2019**: mass death of migratory birds from **avian botulism**.

## SUB 7 | Migratory Birds and Flyways
[42:7:0] - **Central Asian Flyway**: Arctic/Siberia → Indian Ocean; India = key wintering ground. India hosted **CMS COP13** at **Gandhinagar (2020)**.
[42:7:1] - **Bar-headed goose**: breeds on high Central Asian lakes; **crosses the Himalaya** to winter in India; one of the **highest-flying** birds.
[42:7:2] - **Amur falcon**: **longest raptor migration**; SE Siberia/N China → stopover in **Nagaland** (Doyang, Pangti; Oct–Nov) → winters in **southern Africa**.
