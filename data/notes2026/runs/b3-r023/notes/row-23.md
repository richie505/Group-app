=== The Three Methods – Names and Non-methods
| Method | Also called | What is summed |
|---|---|---|
| **Product / production** | Output method, **value added method**, net product method, inventory method | Value added by every producing unit (output − intermediate consumption) = total **final product** |
| **Income** | Factor income / factor payment / **distributed share** method | Compensation of employees + rent + interest + profit + mixed income |
| **Expenditure** | Consumption-and-investment method, **final expenditure** method, outlay method | **C + I + G + (X − M)** – spending on final goods and services |
- **Only three methods:** product, income, expenditure (NextGen: "National income is calculated by three methods").
- **NOT methods (frequent distractors):** **Export-Import method** (AEE 2012; Deputy Jailors 2012), **Savings approach** (SSC 2013), **Diminishing cost method** (State PSC), **Gross Value method** (Civil Asst Surgeon), **Cost and Accounting method** (G2 2025); balance of trade, net exports, foreign reserves are not methods either.
- **"Net product method"** = the product method (G1 2007).
- **"Final product method":** standard texts (NCERT/NIOS, NextGen) place it under the **product method** – value of final goods produced (the other variant being value added); one APPSC flashcard key gave "expenditure method".
- All three give the **same total** (product = income = expenditure).
> Most asked angle: "Which is NOT a method?" – 7 PYQs. Answer = the odd one (export-import, savings, diminishing cost, gross value).

=== Income Method – Factor Incomes, Inclusions and Exclusions
- **National income = income of factors of production** (Sachivalayam Cat-II) – rent + wages + interest + profit; not government income, PSU profits as such, or a State budget.
- **Factor income** = income accruing to a factor of production in return for services rendered to the production unit (Degree Lecturers 2023).
- Economic Survey formula: **GDP = compensation of employees + consumption of fixed capital + (taxes on production − subsidies on production) + gross operating surplus**.
- **Transfer income** = income **not produced by any production process** – received without any service in return (SSC 2012); pensions, scholarships, gifts, old-age allowances.
| Item | In NI? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Rent, wages, interest on productive loans, dividends, **undistributed profits**, **mixed income** | Yes | Factor incomes |
| **Profits of public enterprises** | Yes | Factor income (profit) of the State's enterprises |
| Imputed rent of owner-occupied houses; income from agriculture; self-consumed farm output | Yes | Production / imputed service |
| Change in inventories (production − sales) | Yes | Part of output |
| **Pension**, scholarships, gifts | No | Transfer payments (SSC 2011) |
| **Tax revenue** | No | Transfer to government, not a factor income (G1 2023) |
| Interest on public debt | No | Treated as a transfer |
| **Housewife's services** | No | Not marketed (HWO 2017) |
| Second-hand sales | No | Only the dealer's / broker's commission counts |
| Shares and bonds | No | Financial transaction – no new production |
| Transfer earnings | No | Not payments for current factor services |
> Imputed rent and PSU profits = IN; tax revenue, pension, interest on public debt, housewives' work = OUT.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.5-6 (income method: rent + wages + interest + profit; Economic Survey formula); NextGen Rocket Sheets Economy p.40; OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 03 p.71; app notes 3:23 sub 0, 3

=== Expenditure Method – C + I + G + (X − M) and Capital
- **GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)** – consumption + investment + government purchases + **net exports** (G2 Mains 2019; Town Planning 2012 open economy: **Y = C + I + G + (X − M)**; "C + I + G + X" omits imports).
| Component | Fact |
|---|---|
| Private consumption (C) | **Largest** – about **59% of GDP**; 11th Plan (2007-12) average about **59%** (APPSC flashcard); keeps India less exposed to global swings |
| Government spending (G) | Second largest |
| Business investment (I) | **Most volatile** component |
| Net exports (X − M) | Effect of foreign trade |
- **Q1 2026-27**: GFCF **34.3% of GDP** (from 31.4%); real GFCF grew **11.9%**; private consumption grew **7.1%** (Sep 2026).
| Fixed capital | Working capital |
|---|---|
| Lasts many production cycles – **farmer's plough, computer**, machines, buildings | Used up in one cycle – **yarn** used by the weaver, **petrol**, raw materials |
- **IAS Prelims 2024** pairs: plough–working ✗, computer–fixed ✓, yarn–fixed ✗, petrol–working ✓ → **only two** correct.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.6-7 (C + I + G + (X−M); consumer spending about 59%, investment most volatile); OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 03 p.70-71 (GDP = C + I + G + X − M); NCERT Class XI Indian Economic Development (fixed vs working capital); MoSPI Q1 2026-27 GDP release; app notes 3:23 sub 0

=== Product (Value Added) Method and Double Counting
- **Value added = value of output − intermediate consumption**; summing value added at every stage = value of the final product.
- **Double counting:** adding sales at every stage (cotton farmer → weaver → tailor) counts the cotton again and again. Remedy: count **only final goods** or the **value added** at each stage; **intermediate goods are excluded**. Both routes give the same total.
- **Gross value added − depreciation = net value added**.
- **Sum of net value added at factor cost** of all sectors within the domestic territory = **Net Domestic Product at factor cost (domestic income)**; + NFIA = NNP at FC = national income.
- **Change in inventories = production − sales**; treated as investment.
- **Double deflation** (2022-23 series): **real GVA = real output − real intermediate consumption**, output and inputs deflated separately.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.4-5 (value added = production − intermediate goods; GVA − depreciation = NVA); OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 03 p.69 (value added, GVA/NVA, inventories); NIOS Economics 318 L25 (sum of NVA at FC = NDP at FC); app notes 3:23 sub 1

=== Methods by Sector in India; State Income
- India uses a **combination of the production and income methods** (NextGen).
| Method | Sectors |
|---|---|
| Production (value added) | Agriculture and allied, mining, manufacturing |
| Income | Services – trade, transport, banking, real estate, public administration |
| Expenditure (commodity flow) | In part for construction; PFCE data as a cross-check |
- **2026**: new 2022-23 series integrates **Supply and Use Tables** (cuts the gap between production- and expenditure-side GDP) and uses **double deflation + volume extrapolation**.
| State income term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Income originating** | Value of goods and services produced **within the State's boundaries** (= GSDP) (G2 2011) |
| **Income accruing** | Adds income of the State's residents earned **outside** the State (G2 2011) |
| GSDP (market prices) | GVA at basic prices + product taxes − product subsidies |
| NSDP | GSDP − depreciation; AP per capita income is based on NSDP |
| Production taxes / subsidies | Stamp duties, property taxes / apprentice and interest subsidies |
- **DES, AP** compiles AP's GSDP (NSO compiles national accounts); States use the **production (income-originating) approach**.
- AP GSDP in **9 sectors**: agriculture-forestry-fishing; mining; manufacturing; electricity-gas-water; construction; trade-hotels; transport-storage-communication; finance-real estate; community and social services (no separate tourism sector).
- APSES 2024-25 figures at **2011-12** prices; manufacturing GVA was allocated to States by **ASI shares** (now GST data too); **May 2026**: MoSPI uniform guideline for **GSVA on base 2022-23**.

=== Circular Flow, Output = Income and Equilibrium
| Flow | Direction |
|---|---|
| Real flow – factor services | Households → firms |
| Real flow – goods and services | Firms → households |
| Money flow – factor payments | Firms → households |
| Money flow – consumption spending | Households → firms |
# Leakages and injections
| Leakages | Injections |
|---|---|
| Saving (S) | Investment (I) |
| Taxes (T) | Government spending (G) |
| Imports (M) | Exports (X) |
- **Two-sector equilibrium: planned Savings = planned Investment (S = I)** (G2 2008) – the same as aggregate income = aggregate expenditure (Y = C + I).
- **Open economy: S + T + M = I + G + X** (leakages = injections).
- **Output = income:** production pays out factor incomes, so national product = national income; **J.R. Hicks** (*The Social Framework*): "**Net Social Product and Social Income are one and the same thing**" (G1 PYQ).

=== Definitions of National Income by Economists
| Economist | Core of the definition |
|---|---|
| **Alfred Marshall** | "National dividend" – net aggregate of commodities and services produced annually by labour and capital acting on natural resources (net of depreciation, plus income from abroad) |
| **A.C. Pigou** | Part of community income, incl. income from abroad, **measurable in money** – suits **developed countries** |
| **Irving Fisher** | **Consumption**-based – services received by ultimate consumers |
| **Simon Kuznets** | Net output flowing to ultimate consumers or into **net additions to capital stock** |
~~ Sources: app notes 3:23 sub 4 (standard textbook definitions, e.g. M.L. Jhingan, Macroeconomic Theory)

=== Difficulties in Measuring National Income in India
| Difficulty | Example |
|---|---|
| **Non-monetised economy** | Subsistence output, farm produce kept for home use |
| Large **informal sector** | Unorganised units without proper accounts |
| **No occupational specialisation** | One person both farmer and weaver |
| Black money / parallel economy | Unrecorded income |
| Valuation problems; data gaps | Depreciation, inventories; missing records |
| Double counting risk | Intermediate goods counted again |
- **IMF review (Dec 2025)**: persistent **gaps between production- and expenditure-side GDP** show only **partial capture of informal activity**; the new series uses **Supply and Use Tables** to reduce the discrepancy.
