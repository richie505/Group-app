## SUB 0 | The Three Methods – Names and Non-methods
[0:0] TABLE head: Method | Also called | What is summed
  [0:0.0] Product / production | Output method, value added method, net product method, inventory method | Value added by every producing unit (output − intermediate consumption) = total final product
  [0:0.1] Income | Factor income / factor payment / distributed share method | Compensation of employees + rent + interest + profit + mixed income
  [0:0.2] Expenditure | Consumption-and-investment method, final expenditure method, outlay method | C + I + G + (X − M) – spending on final goods and services
[0:1] - **Only three methods:** product, income, expenditure (NextGen: "National income is calculated by three methods").
[0:2] - **NOT methods (frequent distractors):** **Export-Import method** (AEE 2012; Deputy Jailors 2012), **Savings approach** (SSC 2013), **Diminishing cost method** (State PSC), **Gross Value method** (Civil Asst Surgeon), **Cost and Accounting method** (G2 2025); balance of trade, net exports, foreign reserves are not methods either.
[0:3] - **"Net product method"** = the product method (G1 2007).
[0:4] - **"Final product method":** standard texts (NCERT/NIOS, NextGen) place it under the **product method** – value of final goods produced (the other variant being value added); one APPSC flashcard key gave "expenditure method".
[0:5] - All three give the **same total** (product = income = expenditure).
[0:6] > Most asked angle: "Which is NOT a method?" – 5 PYQs. Answer = the odd one (export-import, savings, diminishing cost, gross value).

## SUB 1 | Income Method – Factor Incomes, Inclusions and Exclusions
[1:0] - **National income = income of factors of production** (Sachivalayam Cat-II) – rent + wages + interest + profit; not government income, PSU profits as such, or a State budget.
[1:1] - **Factor income** = income accruing to a factor of production in return for services rendered to the production unit (Degree Lecturers 2023).
[1:2] - Economic Survey formula: **GDP = compensation of employees + consumption of fixed capital + (taxes on production − subsidies on production) + gross operating surplus**.
[1:3] - **Transfer income** = income **not produced by any production process** – received without any service in return (SSC 2012); pensions, scholarships, gifts, old-age allowances.
[1:4] TABLE head: Item | In NI? | Reason
  [1:4.0] Rent, wages, interest on productive loans, dividends, undistributed profits, mixed income | Yes | Factor incomes
  [1:4.1] Profits of public enterprises | Yes | Factor income (profit) of the State's enterprises
  [1:4.2] Imputed rent of owner-occupied houses; income from agriculture; self-consumed farm output | Yes | Production / imputed service
  [1:4.3] Change in inventories (production − sales) | Yes | Part of output
  [1:4.4] Pension, scholarships, gifts | No | Transfer payments (SSC 2011)
  [1:4.5] Tax revenue | No | Transfer to government, not a factor income (G1 2023)
  [1:4.6] Interest on public debt | No | Treated as a transfer
  [1:4.7] Housewife's services | No | Not marketed (HWO 2017)
  [1:4.8] Second-hand sales | No | Only the dealer's / broker's commission counts
  [1:4.9] Shares and bonds | No | Financial transaction – no new production
  [1:4.10] Transfer earnings | No | Not payments for current factor services
[1:5] > Imputed rent and PSU profits = IN; tax revenue, pension, interest on public debt, housewives' work = OUT.

## SUB 3 | Product (Value Added) Method and Double Counting
[3:0] - **Value added = value of output − intermediate consumption**; summing value added at every stage = value of the final product.
[3:1] - **Double counting:** adding sales at every stage (cotton farmer → weaver → tailor) counts the cotton again and again. Remedy: count **only final goods** or the **value added** at each stage; **intermediate goods are excluded**. Both routes give the same total.
[3:2] - **Gross value added − depreciation = net value added**.
[3:3] - **Sum of net value added at factor cost** of all sectors within the domestic territory = **Net Domestic Product at factor cost (domestic income)**; + NFIA = NNP at FC = national income.
[3:4] - **Change in inventories = production − sales**; treated as investment.
[3:5] - **Double deflation** (2022-23 series): **real GVA = real output − real intermediate consumption**, output and inputs deflated separately.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.4-5 (value added = production − intermediate goods; GVA − depreciation = NVA); OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 03 p.69 (value added, GVA/NVA, inventories); NIOS Economics 318 L25 (sum of NVA at FC = NDP at FC); app notes 3:23 sub 1

## SUB 5 | Circular Flow, Output = Income and Equilibrium
[5:0] TABLE head: Flow | Direction
  [5:0.0] Real flow – factor services | Households → firms
  [5:0.1] Real flow – goods and services | Firms → households
  [5:0.2] Money flow – factor payments | Firms → households
  [5:0.3] Money flow – consumption spending | Households → firms
[5:1] # Leakages and injections
[5:2] TABLE head: Leakages | Injections
  [5:2.0] Saving (S) | Investment (I)
  [5:2.1] Taxes (T) | Government spending (G)
  [5:2.2] Imports (M) | Exports (X)
[5:3] - **Two-sector equilibrium: planned Savings = planned Investment (S = I)** (G2 2008) – the same as aggregate income = aggregate expenditure (Y = C + I).
[5:4] - **Open economy: S + T + M = I + G + X** (leakages = injections).
[5:5] - **Output = income:** production pays out factor incomes, so national product = national income; **J.R. Hicks** (*The Social Framework*): "**Net Social Product and Social Income are one and the same thing**" (G1 PYQ).

## SUB 6 | Definitions of National Income by Economists
