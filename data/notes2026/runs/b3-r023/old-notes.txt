=== NOTES 3:23 | Methods of Measuring National Income – Product, Income and Expenditure Methods; Difficulties
## SUB 0 | Three Methods of Measurement
[23:0:0] TABLE head: Method | Also called | What is summed
  [23:0:0.0] Production / Product | Value added method; output method; net product / final product method | Sum of value added by every producing unit (output − intermediate inputs) = total final product = GDP
  [23:0:0.1] Income | Factor income / factor payment / distributed share method | Compensation of employees + operating surplus (rent, interest, profit) + mixed income
  [23:0:0.2] Expenditure | Consumption-and-investment method; final expenditure; consumption / outlay method | C + I + G + (X − M) – final spending on output
[23:0:1] "**Cost and Accounting method**" is NOT a method of measuring national income (APPSC-G2 2025). "**Diminishing cost method**" is NOT a method to calculate GDP.
[23:0:2] # Product (value added) method – steps
[23:0:3] - **Value added = value of output − intermediate consumption**.
[23:0:4] - Add value added at every stage; the sum equals the value of the final product.
[23:0:5] - **Change in inventories = production − sales**; included (unsold production counts as output).
[23:0:6] # Income method – formula
[23:0:7] - Economic Survey formula: **GDP = Compensation of employees + Consumption of fixed capital + (Taxes on production − subsidies on production) + Gross operating surplus**.
[23:0:8] - **Factor income** = income earned by a factor of production for services rendered to the production unit.
[23:0:9] # Expenditure method – components
[23:0:10] TABLE head: Component | Fact
  [23:0:10.0] Private consumption (C) | Largest share – about 59% of GDP; 11th Plan (2007-12) average = 59% (APPSC-GS 2019); makes India less exposed to global swings
  [23:0:10.1] Business investment (I) | Most volatile component
  [23:0:10.2] Government spending (G) | Final spending of government
  [23:0:10.3] Net exports (X − M) | Last; the trade effect
[23:0:11] - **Q1 2026-27**: GFCF **34.3% of GDP** (from 31.4%); real GFCF grew **11.9%**; private consumption grew **7.1%** (1 and 17 Sep 2026).
[23:0:12] # Fixed vs working capital
[23:0:13] TABLE head: Fixed capital | Working capital
  [23:0:13.0] Long-term assets used over many years – farmer's plough, computer | Used up in one cycle – yarn, petrol
## SUB 1 | Double Counting and Value Added
[23:1:0] - **Double counting** = counting the same value more than once by adding sales at each stage.
[23:1:1] - Example: **cotton farmer -> weaver -> tailor**; counting all three sales counts cotton three times (cotton -> cloth -> garment).
[23:1:2] - Solution: count **only final goods**, or add the **value added at each stage**.
[23:1:3] - **Intermediate goods** are excluded.
[23:1:4] - **Double deflation** (2022-23 series): **Real GVA = Real output − Real intermediate consumption**; output and input prices deflated **separately**.
[23:1:5] Final product method and value added method give the same total – both avoid double counting.
## SUB 2 | Method -> Sectors in India
[23:2:0] - India uses a **combination of the production and income methods**.
[23:2:1] TABLE head: Method | Sectors
  [23:2:1.0] Production (value added) | Agriculture and allied, mining, manufacturing
  [23:2:1.1] Income | Services: trade, transport, banking, real estate, public administration
  [23:2:1.2] Expenditure (commodity flow) | In part for construction; expenditure data (PFCE) serves as a cross-check
[23:2:2] - **NSO** compiles national accounts.
[23:2:3] - **2026**: New 2022-23 series aligns **Supply and Use Tables** with national accounts -> cuts discrepancies between production- and expenditure-side GDP; improves real GVA with **double deflation + volume extrapolation**.

## SUB 4 | Definitions of National Income by Economists
[23:4:0] TABLE head: Economist | Definition's core
  [23:4:0.0] Alfred Marshall | "National dividend" – net aggregate of commodities (material and immaterial) and services produced each year by labour and capital acting on natural resources
  [23:4:0.1] A.C. Pigou | The part of the community's income, including income from abroad, that can be measured in money
  [23:4:0.2] Irving Fisher | Consumption-based – only services received by ultimate consumers
  [23:4:0.3] Simon Kuznets | Net output of commodities and services flowing to ultimate consumers or into net additions to capital stock
[23:4:1] - Marshall's "net" = after deducting depreciation of machines; income from abroad is added.
[23:4:2] - Pigou's definition suits mainly **developed countries**, where nearly everything is bought for money.
[23:4:3] # Official wording
[23:4:4] - **GDP** = value of all final goods and services produced within the country in a year.
[23:4:5] - **GSDP / SDP** = value of all goods and services produced within the State's boundaries, counted without duplication, during a period.
## SUB 5 | Circular Flow of Income
[23:5:0] # Two-sector flows
[23:5:1] TABLE head: Flow | Direction
  [23:5:1.0] Real flow – factor services | Households -> firms
  [23:5:1.1] Real flow – goods and services | Firms -> households
  [23:5:1.2] Money flow – factor payments | Firms -> households
  [23:5:1.3] Money flow – consumption spending | Households -> firms
[23:5:2] # Leakages vs injections
[23:5:3] TABLE head: Leakages (withdrawals) | Injections
  [23:5:3.0] Saving (S) | Investment (I)
  [23:5:3.1] Taxes (T) | Government spending (G)
  [23:5:3.2] Imports (M) | Exports (X)
[23:5:4] - **Open-economy equilibrium: S + T + M = I + G + X** (total leakages = total injections).
[23:5:5] - Open-economy expenditure identity: **Y = C + I + G + (X − M)**.
[23:5:6] Investment, government spending, exports = **injections**; saving, taxes, imports = **leakages**.
[23:5:7] - **Output = income:** production pays factor incomes, so national product equals national income; **J.R. Hicks** (*The Social Framework*): **Net Social Product = Social Income**.
## SUB 6 | Difficulties in Measuring National Income in India
[23:6:0] # Structural difficulties

  [23:6:1.2] No occupational specialisation | One person as both farmer and weaver -> double counting risk
  [23:6:1.3] Black money / parallel economy | Unrecorded income
  [23:6:1.4] Valuation problems | Depreciation, inventories
  [23:6:1.5] Data gaps | Missing records
[23:6:2] # Current issues
[23:6:3] - **IMF review (Dec 2025)**: persistent **gaps between production- and expenditure-side GDP** show only **partial capture of informal activity**.
[23:6:4] - Fix: new series uses **Supply and Use Tables** to reduce the discrepancy.
[23:6:5] - **Mar 2026**: household-sector GVA is extrapolated using indicators after the benchmark year; private corporate segment depends on the **MCA database**.
