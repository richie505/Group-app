=== Child Labour – Article 24 and the 1986 Act as Amended in 2016
# Constitution and age limits
| Provision | Content |
|---|---|
| **Article 24** | No child **below 14 years** to be employed in any **factory, mine** or other hazardous employment (Right against Exploitation) – the Article on **child labour** (not Arts. 23, 26 or 29) |
| Article 21A | Free and compulsory education, **6–14** years (RTE Act, 2009) |
| **Article 39(e)** | Tender age of children not abused |
| **Article 39(f)** | Children protected against exploitation and against moral and material abandonment |
| Factories Act, 1948 | No child **below 14** in a factory |
| Mines Act, 1952 | No person **below 18** in a mine (not 16) |
- **National Policy for Children, 2013**: every person **below 18** is a child; best interests first. The **Draft National Policy on Child Protection** asks for a **zero-tolerance code of conduct** in all organisations.
# Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986 and the 2016 Amendment
- Enacted **1986** (in force **23 December 1986**) – the first comprehensive child labour law; child = a person who has not completed **14 years**. Followed the **Gurupadaswamy Committee (1979)**, Ministry of Labour: poverty the root cause; **dual approach** – ban hazardous work, regulate the rest → 1986 Act and **National Policy on Child Labour, 1987**.
- **2016 Amendment** (in force **1 September 2016**) renamed it the **Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act**.
| Feature | 1986 Act | 2016 Amendment |
|---|---|---|
| Below 14 | Banned in **18 occupations and 65 processes**; rest regulated | **Banned in all occupations and processes** (linked to RTE age) |
| 14–18 | — | New **"adolescent"** – banned in **hazardous** occupations only (mines, inflammable substances/explosives, Factories Act hazardous processes; earlier list had **83** entries) |
| Exceptions | — | Child **helping the family enterprise after school hours**; **child artist** in audio-visual entertainment (**not circus**) |
| Penalty | 3 months–1 year and/or fine ₹10,000–20,000 | **Cognizable**; **6 months–2 years** and/or fine **₹20,000–50,000** |
- **Child and Adolescent Labour Rehabilitation Fund** set up; enforcement through the **District Magistrate**.
- **Penalty PYQ (APPSC):** the key is "**2 years imprisonment and fine up to ₹20,000**"; strictly the 2016 law gives 6 months–2 years and/or ₹20,000–50,000.
- **Bonded labour** = work without freedom to leave, against a debt – e.g. a **child in a brick kiln not allowed to leave** (not unpaid help in a family shop or occasional work). **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976**; debt bondage can run across generations.
> Exam angle: Art. 24 = below 14; Act year = 1986; "adolescent" exists only since 2016.

=== Child Labour – Magnitude, Enforcement, ILO Conventions and Cases
# Magnitude
- **Census 2011 (5–14 years)**: about **10.1 million** child workers (incl. marginal; **4.35 million main workers**) – about **5.6 million boys and 4.5 million girls (≈55:45)**; most in rural areas, rising in urban areas.
- **Uttar Pradesh** had the largest number (about **21.8 lakh**, **one-fifth**), followed by Bihar, Rajasthan, MP, Maharashtra. India has the **largest number** of child labourers in the world.
- Causes: **poverty**, demand for cheap labour, family breakdown, illiteracy, debt bondage, migration, weak enforcement.
# Enforcement
| Mechanism | Fact |
|---|---|
| **PENCIL (2017)** | **Platform for Effective Enforcement for No Child Labour**; **Ministry of Labour and Employment**; for enforcing the amended Child and Adolescent Labour Act; inter-agency tracking of child labour cases; components: Child Tracking System, Complaint Corner, State Government, NCLP, Convergence (not a National Adoption Registry). Wrong options: SPARC, SRIJAN, YUKTI |
| **NCLP (1988)** | National Child Labour Project, **Ministry of Labour and Employment** (not Social Justice); withdraws children from labour; **Special Training Centres for 9–14**: bridge education, vocational training, mid-day meal, stipend |
| **Operation Smile / Operation Muskaan** | **MHA campaign-mode drives to trace, rescue and rehabilitate missing children** (2015) |
| CHILDLINE **1098** | Integrated with ERSS-112 |
- Trafficking: widened by the **Criminal Law Amendment Act, 2013** (sexual exploitation, slavery, servitude, forced labour, organ removal) – now **Section 143, BNS 2023**.
# ILO conventions and cases
| ILO Convention | Subject | India |
|---|---|---|
| **138** (1973) | **Minimum age** for admission to employment | Ratified **13 June 2017** |
| **182** (1999) | **Worst forms of child labour** | Ratified **13 June 2017** |
- Both relate to **child labour** (UPSC 2018) and are among the **eight fundamental** ILO conventions. **World Day Against Child Labour: 12 June**.
| Case | Ruling |
|---|---|

| Bachpan Bachao Andolan (2011) | Trafficking, child begging, circus children; oversight of child care institutions |
| Independent Thought (2017) | Marital-rape exception (Sec. 375 IPC) struck down for wives **below 18** |

=== Juvenile Justice Act 2015 and Earlier Juvenile Laws
# Earlier laws
| Law | Note |
|---|---|
| Children Act 1960 | For **Union Territories** |
| **Juvenile Justice Act 1986** | First uniform juvenile law; juvenile = boys **16**, girls **18** |
| JJ (Care and Protection of Children) Act **2000** | Uniform age **18**; homes: **Observation Homes, Special Homes, Children's Homes, Shelter Homes** – a **"Protective Home" is not one of them** (that is under the Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act) |
| **JJ Act 2015** | In force **15 January 2016**; response to the **Delhi gang rape case (2012)** |
# Key features of the 2015 Act
- Replaced "**juvenile**" with "**child**" / "child in conflict with law"; two categories – children **in conflict with law** and children **in need of care and protection**.
- A **delinquent** = a **minor** (below 18) who commits an offence – a "juvenile in conflict with law".
- **Heinous offence** = minimum punishment **7 years or more**. A child of **16–18** accused of a heinous offence: **JJB preliminary assessment** (not a finding of guilt) → may transfer to the **Children's Court** (a **Sessions Court**) to be tried as an adult.
- **CARA (Central Adoption Resource Authority)** given **statutory** status – the **nodal body for adoption**, under the **Ministry of Women and Child Development**; regulates **in-country and inter-country adoption**.
- **Compulsory registration** of all **Child Care Institutions**; **working children** = children in need of care and protection.
| Body | Deals with / composition |
|---|---|
| **Juvenile Justice Board** | **Children in conflict with law**; Principal Magistrate + **2 social workers**, at least one a woman |
| **Child Welfare Committee** | **Children in need of care and protection**; Chairperson + **4** members |
| Children's Court | Transferred 16–18 heinous cases; a Sessions Court |
- Every district has a JJB and a CWC. Juvenile delinquents are dealt with by **juvenile courts / Juvenile Justice Boards**, which put the **well-being and best interests of the child** first – child-friendly, rehabilitation-oriented justice (not punishment, humiliation or precedent).
- **2021 Amendment** (in force **1 September 2022**): **District Magistrate** (and ADM) = focal point; DMs issue **adoption orders** instead of courts.
- **Private Member's Bill (Dec 2025)**: lower the heinous-offence age threshold from **16 to 14**.
- **AP**: follows the JJ Act 2015 + **Probation of Offenders Act, 1958**.

=== POCSO Act 2012
# Basics
- **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012** (not "social offences") – addresses **child sexual abuse**; drafted by **MWCD**.
- In force **14 November 2012** (Children's Day) – the year to remember is **2012**.
- Child = **below 18**; gender-neutral; consent below 18 **legally irrelevant**. After the **BNS** (from **1 July 2024**) POCSO continues as a **separate special law**.
# Offences and procedure
- Offences: penetrative and non-penetrative sexual assault, their **aggravated** forms (by a person in **trust or authority**), sexual harassment, child pornography. **Child marriage is not** a POCSO offence.
| Section | Provision |
|---|---|
| 19 | **Mandatory reporting**; failure – up to **6 months** and/or fine |
| 22 | **False complaints** punishable (except good faith) |
| 29–30 | Presumption – **burden of proof on the accused** |
- Child-friendly procedure: statement at the child's residence by a **woman officer**, not in uniform; **no child kept at the police station at night**; **Special Courts**, **in-camera** trial; investigation in **2 months**, trial in **1 year**. Monitored by NCPCR/SCPCRs; **POCSO e-Box (2016, NCPCR)** – confidential online reporting.
# Amendments
| Year | Change |
|---|---|
| **Ordinance, 21 April 2018** | Criminal Law (Amendment) Ordinance – harsher punishment (up to death) for **rape of girls below 12**; the POCSO amendment cleared by the Union Cabinet in **2018–19** |
| **2019 Amendment** (16 Aug 2019) | Penetrative assault min **10 years**; aggravated penetrative assault min **20 years**, up to life or **death**; child pornography defined; punished **giving chemicals to hasten sexual maturity** |

=== Age of Consent and the Juvenile-Age Debate
| Stage | Age of consent |
|---|---|
| IPC 1860 | 10 |
| **Age of Consent Act 1891** | **12** |
| **IPC amendment 1925** | **14** |
| POCSO 2012 / CLA 2013 | 18 |
| BNS **Sec. 63** | Keeps **18** |
- Marriage age (distinct from consent): **18 women, 21 men**.
| Body | Stand |
|---|---|
| **Justice Verma Committee** (2013) | Wanted **16** |
| **Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs** (Anand Sharma, **March 2021**, report on crimes against women and children) | Asked the Government to consider **reducing the POCSO age from 18 to 16**, reasoning that minor sexual offenders left uncorrected may commit graver crimes (later reports: the panel did not press it) |
| **Law Commission, 283rd Report (2023)** | Against lowering – it would make POCSO a "**paper law**" |
- **Madhya Pradesh High Court** (2023) asked the Centre to lower the consent age to **16**, citing **early puberty due to social-media/internet exposure**.
- **State of U.P. v. Anurudh** (SC, Jan 2026): flagged POCSO misuse in adolescent relationships; urged a "**Romeo-Juliet**" clause; SC set aside the **Calcutta HC** ruling that had excluded consensual adolescent relationships.

=== Child Abuse – Forms and Data
| Form | Examples |
|---|---|
| Physical | Commission (beating, burning) or **omission** (**medical/nutritional neglect**) |
| Emotional | Verbal battering, educational neglect, encouraging delinquency |
| **Social** | **Infanticide, child marriage, child prostitution, beggary** |
| Online (CSAM) | **4.5 lakh+** reports (2023); **IT Act 2000**; **IT Rules 2021** – large platforms detect CSAM automatically |
- **Causes** (e.g. poverty, family breakdown, **parental alcoholism**) vs consequences (trauma, delinquency, dropout).
- **Study on Child Abuse: India 2007 (MWCD)**: 13 States; **two out of three** children faced physical abuse; **53.22%** reported sexual abuse; **over 50%** of abusers known to the child or in trust.
- **NCRB 2021**: **97.1%** of child sexual-abuse offenders known to the child. **NCRB 2023**: **Madhya Pradesh** highest number of crimes against children; **Assam** highest rate.
- Women are **more likely to be sexually abused at home** (by known persons) than outside; **dress is not a cause** of sexual abuse.
