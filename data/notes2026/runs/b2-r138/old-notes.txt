## SUB 2 | Juvenile Justice Act 2015
[138:2:0] # Earlier Juvenile Laws
[138:2:1] TABLE head: Law | Note
  [138:2:1.0] Children Act 1960 | For Union Territories
  [138:2:1.1] Juvenile Justice Act 1986 | First uniform juvenile law; boys 16, girls 18
  [138:2:1.2] JJ (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2000 | Uniform age 18; amended 2006
  [138:2:1.3] JJ Act 2015 | Response to the Delhi gang rape case (2012); "transfer system" for 16–18
[138:2:2] # Key Features
[138:2:3] - Replaced "**juvenile**" with "**child**"; two categories.
[138:2:4] - **Heinous offence** = minimum punishment **7 years or more**.
[138:2:5] - 16–18 + heinous offence: **JJB preliminary assessment** (not a finding of guilt) → possible transfer to **Children's Court**.
[138:2:6] - **CARA** given **statutory** status; **compulsory registration** of all Child Care Institutions.
[138:2:7] - **Working children** = children in need of care and protection.
[138:2:8] - Guiding principle: **best interests of the child**; child-friendly, rehabilitation-oriented (APPSC 2019, 2024). Juvenile delinquents are dealt with by **juvenile courts/boards** (APPSC 2019).
[138:2:9] TABLE head: Category / body | Handled by / composition
  [138:2:9.0] Child in Conflict with Law (CCL) | Juvenile Justice Board – Principal Magistrate (MM or JMFC) + 2 social workers, at least one woman
  [138:2:9.1] Child in Need of Care and Protection (CNCP) | Child Welfare Committee – Chairperson + 4 members
  [138:2:9.2] Transferred 16–18 heinous cases | Children's Court (a Sessions Court)
[138:2:10] - Every district has a JJB and a CWC, each with at least one woman member.
[138:2:11] # 2021 Amendment
[138:2:12] - **District Magistrate** (and ADM) = focal point; DMs issue **adoption orders** instead of courts; CWC member eligibility conditions; Adoption Regulations 2022.
[138:2:13] - **Private Member's Bill (Dec 2025)**: lower heinous-offence age threshold from **16 to 14**.
## SUB 3 | Pocso Act 2012
[138:3:0] # Basics
[138:3:1] - **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012**; drafted by **MWCD**.
[138:3:2] - Child = **below 18**, gender-neutral for child and offender; consent below 18 **legally irrelevant**.
[138:3:3] - After **BNS** (from **1 July 2024**), POCSO stays a separate special law.
[138:3:4] # Offences and Procedure
[138:3:5] - Offences: **penetrative** and **non-penetrative** sexual assault, their **aggravated** forms, **sexual harassment**, **child pornography**. **Child marriage is NOT** a POCSO offence.
[138:3:6] - **Aggravated** = by a person in trust/authority (police, armed forces, public servant).
[138:3:7] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [138:3:7.0] 19 | Mandatory reporting to SJPU or local police; failure: up to 6 months and/or fine
  [138:3:7.1] 22 | False complaints punishable (except good faith)
  [138:3:7.2] 29–30 | Presumption / burden of proof on the accused
[138:3:8] - Statement at child's residence or chosen place, by a **woman officer (at least Sub-Inspector)**, **not in uniform**; no child kept at police station at night; no contact with accused.
[138:3:9] - **Special Courts**: **in-camera** trial; investigation in **2 months**; trial in **1 year**; compensation and rehabilitation. Monitored by NCPCR and SCPCRs.
[138:3:10] # Amendments
[138:3:11] TABLE head: Year | Change
  [138:3:11.0] Ordinance, 21 April 2018 | Harsher punishment (up to death) for rape of girls below 12
  [138:3:11.1] 2019 Amendment | Penetrative assault min 10 years; aggravated min 20 years, up to life or death
  [138:3:11.2] 2019 Amendment | Child pornography defined; storing without deleting/reporting – fine at least ₹5,000 (repeat ₹10,000); punished giving chemicals to hasten sexual maturity
  [138:3:11.3] POCSO Rules 2020 | Replaced 2012 Rules

## SUB 6 | Child Labour Law – 1986 and 2016
[138:6:0] TABLE head: Feature | 1986 Act | 2016 Amendment
  [138:6:0.0] Below 14 | Banned in 18 occupations, 65 processes; rest regulated | Banned in all occupations and processes (linked to RTE age)
  [138:6:0.1] 14–18 | — | New "adolescent" – banned in hazardous work only
  [138:6:0.2] Exceptions | — | Family enterprise after school; child artist in audio-visual entertainment (excluding circus)
  [138:6:0.3] Penalty | — | Cognizable; 6 months–2 years and/or ₹20,000–50,000
[138:6:1] - Hazardous list trimmed to mines, inflammable substances/explosives and Factories Act hazardous processes (earlier 83 entries). **Child and Adolescent Labour Rehabilitation Fund**. Enforcement: **District Magistrate**.
[138:6:2] - 1986 Act = first comprehensive child labour law; clashed with RTE 2009.
[138:6:3] - **Gurupadaswamy Committee (1979)**, Ministry of Labour: poverty = root cause; **dual approach** – ban hazardous, regulate rest → **1986 Act** and **National Policy on Child Labour, 1987**.
[138:6:4] - **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976**; debt bondage across generations.
## SUB 7 | Magnitude and Causes of Child Labour
[138:7:0] - **Census 2011 (5–14)**: **10.1 million** (incl. marginal); **4.35 million** main workers.
[138:7:1] - India has the **largest number** of child labourers in the world.
[138:7:2] - Sectors: brick kilns, pesticide farm work, toxic chemicals, tea stalls, begging.
[138:7:3] - Causes: **poverty**, **cheap labour** demand, family disorganisation, illiteracy, debt bondage, migrant families, kidnapping, weak enforcement.
[138:7:4] - **Top State (Census 2011):** **Uttar Pradesh** had the largest number of child labourers (5–14), about **21.8 lakh** – about **one in five** of India's child workers.
