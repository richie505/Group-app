=== Carbon Sinks, Blue Carbon, REDD+ and Carbon Monitoring
# Sinks
- **Carbon sink:** a reservoir that accumulates and stores carbon. **Natural sinks: forests, soil, oceans** (chlor-alkali plants are NOT sinks). Forests act as sinks when they grow in density or area.
- **Blue carbon:** carbon captured by **oceans and coastal ecosystems** – **mangroves, seagrass, salt marshes** (all three are blue-carbon ecosystems). Forest/soil carbon is not "blue"; fossil carbon is in petroleum and gas.
- **Best sink per unit area = blue carbon ecosystems:** they store several times more carbon per area than tropical rainforests (seagrass ~3 times). Glaciers are not sinks.
- **Tropical peatlands:** most carbon **per hectare** of any terrestrial ecosystem.
- **Carbon fertilisation:** **increased plant growth** because more atmospheric CO2 raises photosynthesis (~**70%** of Earth's recent greening). Not warming, not ocean acidity.
- **Bhutan** = carbon-negative country. Net zero = emissions balanced by removals.

# IPCC carbon pools (5)
- Above-ground biomass, below-ground biomass, dead wood, litter, soil organic carbon. **Atmospheric oxygen is NOT a pool.**
# REDD+ and forest-carbon finance
- **REDD+** = Reducing Emissions from **Deforestation and Forest Degradation** (+ conservation, sustainable management, enhancement of stocks).
- **UN-REDD** (2008; **UNDP, FAO, UNEP**): proper design contributes to **biodiversity protection, forest resilience and poverty reduction – all three** (UPSC 2016).
- **Forest Carbon Partnership Facility (World Bank):** global partnership of governments, businesses, civil society and indigenous peoples; **helps countries with REDD+** finance and technical aid. It does **not** fund universities/scientists for forestry research (UPSC 2015: I and III).
- **Khasi Hills Community REDD+ Project** (East Khasi Hills, **Meghalaya**): **India's first community REDD+ project, Plan Vivo certified**; run by Khasi **himas**; ~27,000 ha (UPSC 2026).

=== Carbon Sequestration, CCS and Carbon Removal
- **Carbon sequestration:** capturing and long-term storing of atmospheric CO2 – in plants (afforestation), soil, oceans or geological formations. **Afforestation = capturing and storing CO2.**
- **CCS (Carbon Capture and Storage), "also known as carbon sequestration":** capture at a stationary point source (power plant, cement plant) → transport → storage (**3 steps**); store only. APPSC key for "technology that captures and stores CO2 from industrial processes" = **carbon sequestration**.
- **CCUS:** capture, then **use or store**; supports coal gasification and the hydrogen economy.
| Method | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Geological storage | **Abandoned/uneconomic coal seams, depleted oil and gas reservoirs, deep saline formations** – all three (UPSC 2017) |
| **Enhanced rock weathering** | Finely ground **basalt** spread on farmland binds CO2 as carbonate |
| **Ocean alkalinity enhancement** | Adding **lime**/alkaline minerals to seawater |
| Mine storage | Industrial CO2 pumped into abandoned mines as carbonated water |
| **Direct Air Capture (DAC)** | CO2 straight from **ambient air** anywhere; a sequestration method; CO2 used in **plastics, food/beverage processing**; with **hydrogen** gives **synthetic aviation fuel** (UPSC 2025: all three) |
| **BECCS** | Bioenergy + CCS → net-negative emissions |
- UPSC 2023: basalt on farms, lime in oceans, CO2 into abandoned mines – **all three** are discussed for carbon capture and sequestration.
- **Blue carbon (mangroves) is a sink, not geological storage.**
- **NET Power** (La Porte, Texas): **Allam-Fetvedt cycle** – burns gas in oxygen with **supercritical CO2** as working fluid; claims power as cheap as a gas combined-cycle plant while **capturing nearly all CO2**.
- **DST:** five **CCU testbeds** in the **cement** sector – a first-of-its-kind research cluster.

=== Carbon Credits, Markets and Carbon Taxes
# Credits
- **1 carbon credit = 1 tonne (1,000 kg) of CO2 / CO2e** avoided or removed – not of carbon.
- **Kyoto Protocol (1997):** caps on GHG emissions; credit system ratified with Kyoto; credits go to countries/groups that cut emissions below quota; goal = limit emissions. **CDM** issues **CERs** (Certified Emission Reductions); JI issues ERUs.
- **Prices set by supply and demand** on exchanges – **NOT fixed by UNEP** or any body (UPSC 2011 "not correct" option).
- **ERPA (May 2011):** World Bank (for IBRD/BioCarbon) and **Himachal Pradesh** government – CERs from the Mid-Himalayan watershed (forestry) project.
| Voluntary standard | Run by |
|---|---|
| Verified Carbon Standard (VCS) | **Verra** |
| Gold Standard | Founded by **WWF** |
# India's compliance market
- **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS):** notified **June 2023** under **Sec. 14AA, Energy Conservation Act 2001** (NOT EP Act); run by **BEE** (Ministry of Power); trades **Indian Carbon Credits**; India's **first mandatory compliance** market; 7 sectors, ~477 Mt CO2e.
- **Oct 2025:** emission-intensity targets under CCTS for **aluminium, cement, chlor-alkali, pulp & paper** (282 entities). Carbon pricing covers **29%** of global GHGs.
- **Net Zero Portal** (**MoEFCC**, 16 Sep 2026): voluntary registration/disclosure of net-zero commitments; launched with the NAPCC Dashboard.
- **Budget 2026-27 carbon credit programme:** follows the DST roadmap – industrial, not farmer-only. India–Japan **Joint Crediting Mechanism** (Paris Art. 6.2).
# Carbon taxes and border levies
- **New Zealand:** first to **propose** a tax on agricultural (livestock) emissions ("burp tax", 2022, later scrapped) (APPSC G1); also credited (UPSC 2006) as first to propose a carbon tax for its people.
- **Denmark:** first to **levy** a livestock carbon tax, **from 2030**.
- **EU CBAM:** levy on imports to stop **carbon leakage**; transitional 2023, **definitive 1 Jan 2026**; covers iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen, electricity (not textiles). **Article 9**: carbon price already paid (e.g. CCTS) is deductible.
- **UK CBAM:** from **1 Jan 2027**; Sep 2026 UK recognised CCTS as a qualifying carbon price.

=== Footprints and Measurement Tools
| Footprint | Measures |
|---|---|
| **Carbon footprint** | Total GHGs produced, **directly and indirectly, to support human activities** (t CO2/yr; individual, organisation, product) |
| **Ecological footprint** | **Land and water** needed to produce what we consume **and absorb our waste**; unit **global hectare (gha)** |
| Water footprint | Freshwater used – blue, green, grey water |
| Scope | Emissions |
| Scope 1 | Direct, from owned sources (e.g. diesel generator) |
| Scope 2 | Purchased electricity or heat |
| Scope 3 | Rest of value chain (manufacture, transport of inputs) |
- **Common Carbon Metric** (supported by **UNEP**): measures energy use and GHG emissions of **building operations** (UPSC 2021).
- Carrying capacity = population an area can support; not a footprint.
> Exam angle: definition match (carbon vs ecological footprint), unit = global hectare, Common Carbon Metric (×2).

=== Carbon Emissions – Emitters, Sources and Targets
- **Biggest CO2 emitter: China** (~30-32%) > USA (~13%) > **India (3rd, ~8%)**; India has only ~3% of historical emissions (Global Carbon Budget 2025).
- India per capita: about **2 tonnes** CO2 vs world ~4.7 t – **lowest in the G20**.
- Sources: **fossil-fuel burning** – petrol and diesel in automobiles; **coal** in thermal power (CO2, NOx, SO2).
| India sector | Share of GHG emissions |
|---|---|
| Cement | ~6% |
| Aluminium | ~2.8% |
| Waste | 2.56% (2020, BUR-4) |
- **Coal thermal power – steps:** **supercritical technology** (higher efficiency, less coal/CO2 per unit); **Dec 2015 MoEFCC emission norms** (new limits for PM, **SO2, NOx, mercury**; water use). No freeze on coal capacity or gas conversion was ordered.
- **Biomass co-firing:** 5% (FY 2024-25) → **7% (FY 2025-26)**, watched by the **SAMARTH** mission.
- **NITI Aayog** decarbonisation roadmaps (cement, aluminium, MSMEs): round-the-clock RE, nuclear, CCUS.
- **Carbon bombs:** fossil projects able to emit **over 1 Gt CO2** in their lifetime (LINGO list, 425 worldwide); India: **Gevra (Chhattisgarh)**, **Rajmahal (Jharkhand)** coal mines.
- **2035 NDC:** **47%** emission-intensity cut vs 2005; **60%** non-fossil power capacity; net zero 2070.
> Exam angle: biggest emitter, "recent step" for coal power plants.

- Founded **1961**; HQ **Gland (Vaud canton), Switzerland**; symbol giant panda; works for **wildlife and biodiversity conservation** (protects species and forests).
- Renamed World Wide Fund for Nature in 1986 (silver jubilee); **WWF-India** set up 1969, renamed **World Wide Fund for Nature-India in 1987**.
- Helped launch Project Tiger (1973); TRAFFIC is a WWF–IUCN alliance; founded the Gold Standard.
