=== NOTES 5:104 | Carbon Emissions, Carbon Footprint, Carbon Sequestration, Carbon Credits and Carbon Trading
## SUB 0 | Carbon Emissions – Sources and Emitters
[104:0:0] - Sources: **fossil-fuel burning** – petrol and diesel in automobiles; **coal** in thermal power (gives CO2, NOx, SO2). Photosynthesis removes CO2; respiration and decay release it.
[104:0:1] - Top emitters (fossil CO2, Global Carbon Budget 2025): **China (~31.8%) > USA (~12.7%) > India (~8.3%, 3rd)**.
[104:0:2] - India per capita: about **2 tonnes** CO2 vs world ~4.7 t – **lowest in the G20**.
[104:0:3] TABLE head: India sector | Share of GHG emissions
  [104:0:3.0] Cement | ~6%
  [104:0:3.1] Aluminium | ~2.8%
  [104:0:3.2] Waste | 2.56% (2020, BUR-4)
[104:0:4] - **NITI Aayog** decarbonisation roadmaps (cement, aluminium, MSMEs): round-the-clock RE, nuclear, CCUS.
[104:0:5] - Coal plants co-fire **biomass 5% (FY 2024-25) → 7% (FY 2025-26)**, watched by **SAMARTH** mission.
[104:0:6] - **Carbon bombs**: fossil projects that could emit **over 1 Gt CO2** in their lifetime; India: **Gevra (Chhattisgarh)**, **Rajmahal (Jharkhand)** coal mines.
[104:0:7] - **2035 NDC**: **47%** emission-intensity cut vs 2005; **60%** non-fossil power capacity; extra sink of 3.5–4.0 bn t CO2e.
## SUB 1 | Footprint Concepts and Scopes
[104:1:0] TABLE head: Footprint | Measures
  [104:1:0.0] Carbon footprint | Total GHGs produced, directly and indirectly, to support human activities (t CO2e/yr) (APPSC-GS 2025)
  [104:1:0.1] Ecological footprint | Land and water needed to produce what we consume and absorb our waste (APPSC-GS 2018)
  [104:1:0.2] Water footprint | Freshwater used – blue, green, grey water
  [104:1:0.3] Scope | Emissions
  [104:1:0.4] Scope 1 | Direct, from owned sources (e.g. diesel generator)
  [104:1:0.5] Scope 2 | Purchased electricity or heat
  [104:1:0.6] Scope 3 | Rest of value chain (manufacture, transport of inputs)
[104:1:1] - **Common Carbon Metric** (supported by **UNEP**): carbon footprint of **building operations**.
## SUB 2 | Carbon Sinks and Sequestration
[104:2:0] - **Natural sinks**: forests, soil, oceans (chlor-alkali plants are NOT).
[104:2:1] - **Blue carbon**: carbon captured by oceans and coastal ecosystems – **mangroves, seagrass, salt marshes**.
[104:2:2] - **Tropical peatlands**: most carbon **per hectare** of any terrestrial ecosystem.
[104:2:3] - **Carbon fertilisation**: more plant growth because higher CO2 raises photosynthesis; ~**70%** of Earth's recent greening.
[104:2:4] - **Bhutan**: **carbon-negative** country. Net zero = emissions balanced by removals.
[104:2:5] - Forest carbon stock (**ISFR 2023**, Dec 2024): **7,285.5 million tonnes**, up 81.5 Mt over 2021; total sink ~30.43 bn t CO2e, 2.29 bn t above 2005.
[104:2:6] # REDD+
[104:2:7] - **REDD+** = Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation; India takes part; forest conservation can earn tradable credits.
[104:2:8] - **UN-REDD** (started **2008**; UNDP, FAO, UNEP) contributes to biodiversity protection, forest resilience and poverty reduction (all three).
[104:2:9] # IPCC Carbon Pools (5)
[104:2:10] - 1. Above-ground biomass
[104:2:11] - 2. Below-ground biomass
[104:2:12] - 3. Dead wood
[104:2:13] - 4. Litter
[104:2:14] - 5. Soil organic carbon
[104:2:15] Atmospheric oxygen is NOT a carbon pool.
[104:2:16] - **Khasi Hills Community REDD+ Project** (East Khasi Hills, **Meghalaya**): **India's first community-based REDD+ project**, certified under **Plan Vivo**; run by a federation of Khasi **himas**; protects about 27,000 ha of forest.
## SUB 3 | Carbon Capture and Removal
[104:3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [104:3:0.0] CCS | Capture at stationary point source → transport → storage (3 steps); store only
  [104:3:0.1] CCUS | Capture, then use or store; supports coal gasification, hydrogen
  [104:3:0.2] Carbon sequestration | Capturing and storing CO2 – geological (saline aquifers, depleted oil/gas), ocean, terrestrial (forests, soil)
  [104:3:0.3] Direct Air Capture | CO2 straight from ambient air, anywhere (unlike point source); a sequestration method; CO2 useful for plastics, food processing
  [104:3:0.4] Enhanced rock weathering | Crushed silicate rock (basalt) on farmland binds CO2 as carbonate
  [104:3:0.5] Ocean alkalinity enhancement | Adding lime/alkaline minerals to seawater
  [104:3:0.6] BECCS | Bioenergy + CCS → net-negative emissions
[104:3:1] - **Geological storage sites**: abandoned/uneconomic **coal seams**, **depleted oil and gas reservoirs**, **deep saline formations** – all three.
[104:3:2] - CO2-removal activities: basalt on farmland, lime in ocean, pumping industrial CO2 underground – **all three**.
[104:3:3] - **APPSC-GS 2024**: "captures and stores CO2 from industrial processes" = **carbon sequestration** (carbon capture also accepted).
[104:3:4] - **DST**: five **CCU testbeds** in the **cement** sector – first-of-its-kind research cluster.
[104:3:5] - **NET Power** (La Porte, Texas) **Allam-Fetvedt cycle**: burns gas in oxygen with **supercritical CO2** as working fluid; captures nearly all CO2.
## SUB 4 | Carbon Monitoring

[104:4:1] - Goals: assess **carbon pools, fluxes and net carbon balance** of India's terrestrial biomes; remote-sensing spatial databases. NOT space technology for sequestration.
[104:4:2] TABLE head: CO2-observing satellite | Agency
  [104:4:2.0] OCO-2 | NASA
  [104:4:2.1] GOSAT | Japan
## SUB 5 | Carbon Credits and Markets
[104:5:0] - **1 carbon credit = 1 tonne CO2e** avoided or removed.
[104:5:1] - **Kyoto Protocol (1997)**: set **caps on GHG emissions**; countries set internal quotas for 'operators'; **CDM** issues **CERs** (Certified Emission Reductions).
[104:5:2] - Credit prices set by **supply and demand** – NOT fixed by UNEP or any international body.
[104:5:3] TABLE head: Voluntary standard | Run by
  [104:5:3.0] Verified Carbon Standard (VCS) | Verra
  [104:5:3.1] Gold Standard | Founded by WWF
[104:5:4] # Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS)
[104:5:5] - Notified **June 2023** under **Sec. 14AA, Energy Conservation Act 2001** (NOT EP Act); run by **BEE**; trades **Indian Carbon Credits**; India's **first mandatory compliance** market.
[104:5:6] - **Oct 2025**: intensity targets for **aluminium, cement, chlor-alkali, pulp & paper**; 282 entities.
[104:5:7] - 7 sectors, ~477 Mt CO2e. Carbon pricing covers **29%** of global GHGs.
[104:5:8] - **Net Zero Portal** (MoEFCC, Sep 2026): voluntary net-zero disclosure.
[104:5:9] - **Budget 2026-27 carbon credit programme**: follows DST roadmap – industrial, not farmer-only.
## SUB 6 | Carbon Pricing – Tax and Cbam
[104:6:0] - **New Zealand**: first to **propose** a tax on agricultural (livestock) emissions (APPSC-G1 2024); also cited as first to propose a carbon tax for its people (Sweden, Finland, Netherlands, Norway already had carbon tax).
[104:6:1] - **Denmark**: first to **levy** a livestock carbon tax, **from 2030**.
[104:6:2] - **EU CBAM**: carbon levy on imports to stop **carbon leakage** (emissions moving abroad). Transitional **2023**; **definitive 1 Jan 2026**. Covers iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen, electricity. Certificates priced on **EU ETS**; revenue stays in Europe.
[104:6:3] - **Article 9**: carbon price already paid (e.g. CCTS) can be deducted.
[104:6:4] - **UK CBAM**: from **1 Jan 2027**; Sep 2026 UK recognised **CCTS** as qualifying carbon price → lower liability for Indian exports.
