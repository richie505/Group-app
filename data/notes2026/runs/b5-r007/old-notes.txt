=== NOTES 5:7 | Programmes Promoting S&t and Innovation – Inspire, Fellowships, Atal Innovation Mission
## SUB 0 | Inspire – Dst Flagship (2008)
[7:0:0] - **INSPIRE** is the **DST** flagship scheme, launched **2008**, to attract talent to science.
[7:0:1] # INSPIRE Components
[7:0:2] TABLE head: Component | Full form | Target
  [7:0:2.0] SEATS | Scheme for Early Attraction of Talents for Science | School students
  [7:0:2.1] MANAK | Million Minds Augmenting National Aspiration and Knowledge | Classes VI–X, aged 10–15; ₹10,000 each
  [7:0:2.2] SHE | Scholarship for Higher Education | Top-ranked UG/PG students in natural and basic sciences
  [7:0:2.3] AORC | Assured Opportunity for Research Careers – INSPIRE Fellowship (PhD) + INSPIRE Faculty | Doctoral and post-doctoral researchers
[7:0:3] - MANAK 2025: **8.54 lakh** ideas; **46,926** students given ₹10,000 each.
[7:0:4] - INSPIRE Fellowship 2025: **653** doctoral fellowships, **65% female**, about 31% former SHE scholars.
[7:0:5] SHE = Scholarship for Higher Education, NOT "Support for Her Education" – it is not a women's scheme.
## SUB 1 | Fellowships and Brain-gain Schemes
[7:1:0] # Scheme → Body → Target
[7:1:1] TABLE head: Scheme | Awarding body | Target
  [7:1:1.0] VAJRA (Visiting Advanced Joint Research Faculty) | SERB (now ANRF) | Overseas scientists incl. NRIs/OCIs, short spells in India
  [7:1:1.1] Ramanujan Fellowship | SERB, now ANRF | All Indian scientists abroad returning
  [7:1:1.2] Ramalingaswami Re-entry | DBT (not DST) | Life-science/biotech returnees only
  [7:1:1.3] J.C. Bose Fellowship (now JC Bose Grant) | SERB/ANRF | Active senior scientists, for excellence
  [7:1:1.4] Swarnajayanti | DST, 1997 (50 years of Independence) | Young scientists with a proven record
  [7:1:1.5] PMRF | Ministry of Education (2018) | PhD scholars in IITs, IISc, IISERs (not a career-break scheme)
[7:1:2] - **Brain drain → brain gain**: **VAJRA, Ramanujan Fellowship and INSPIRE**.
[7:1:3] - ANRF schemes: PMECRG (PM Early Career Research Grant), Prime Minister Professorship, National Science Chair, N-PDF.

## SUB 7 | Atal Innovation Mission (aim)
[7:7:0] - Flagship of **NITI Aayog**, set up **2016** (announced in Budget 2015); builds innovation and start-up culture at school, university and other levels.
[7:7:1] TABLE head: Component | Focus
  [7:7:1.0] Atal Tinkering Labs (ATLs) | Schools, grades 6–12: IoT, 3D printing, robotics; 10,000 ATLs in 700+ districts
  [7:7:1.1] Atal Incubation Centres (AICs) | Start-ups in universities/institutions
  [7:7:1.2] ACICs (Atal Community Innovation Centres) | Rural and under-served areas
  [7:7:1.3] ARISE (Atal Research and Innovation for Small Enterprises) | MSMEs
  [7:7:1.4] Atal New India Challenges | Product innovation for national challenges
[7:7:2] # AIM 2.0 and Latest
[7:7:3] - **AIM 2.0 (2024)**: **₹2,750 crore**, till **March 2028**; shift from building infrastructure to filling ecosystem gaps and scaling.
[7:7:4] - **LIPI**: 30 vernacular innovation centres in 22 scheduled languages; 10 Atal Sectoral Innovation Launchpads; 2,500 new ATLs.
[7:7:5] - **Feb 2026**: Union Budget 2026-27 raised ATL funding nearly six-fold, **₹500 crore → ₹3,200 crore**; Generative-AI assistants added to ATLs.
[7:7:6] - **Jun 2026**: **Tripura** – first **State Innovation Mission** under AIM 2.0; **India-France ATL Bridge** launched at **Nice** (Bharat Innovates 2026).
## SUB 8 | Andhra Pradesh Innovation Ecosystem
[7:8:0] - AP has **590 ATLs** (2024-25).
[7:8:1] - **Andhra Pradesh Innovation Society (APIS)**: state body for start-ups; set up the **Ratan Tata Innovation Hub** as a Section-8 company.
[7:8:2] - **Ratan Tata Innovation Hub (RTIH)**: inaugurated **20 Aug 2025** by CM N. Chandrababu Naidu; central hub at **Mangalagiri** (Amaravati) with five spokes – Visakhapatnam, Rajahmundry, Vijayawada-Guntur, Tirupati, Anantapur.
[7:8:3] - **Amaravati Quantum Valley** – Andhra Pradesh; IISER Tirupati hosts IITTNiF.
[7:8:4] - **AP Startup Policy:** Andhra Pradesh was the **first State** to launch an **Innovation and Startup Policy (2014-2020)**. **APIS**, headed by a **CEO** under the **ITE&C Department**, is the State's **nodal agency** for start-up initiatives.

=== NOTES 5:8 | National S&t Missions – Quantum, Supercomputing, Deep Ocean, BIO-E3, Anrf
## SUB 0 | Indian Supercomputer History
[8:0:0] - India's supercomputing programme began in the **late 1980s**; India's supercomputer family is **PARAM**, designed and assembled by **C-DAC, Pune**.
[8:0:1] - **PARAM 8000 (1991)** – **India's first** (first indigenously developed) supercomputer, C-DAC under **Vijay Bhatkar**, the **father of Indian supercomputing**.
[8:0:2] # Developer → Machine

## SUB 1 | National Supercomputing Mission (nsm, 2015)
[8:1:0] - Run jointly by **DST and MeitY**; implementing agencies **C-DAC Pune + IISc Bengaluru**.
[8:1:1] - Machines linked on the **National Supercomputing Grid** over the **National Knowledge Network**.
[8:1:2] - Plan: **40 systems (>64 PF)**; 37 commissioned (39 PF); 3 more on the indigenous RUDRA server (~27 PF, incl. a 20 PF National Facility).
[8:1:3] - Indigenous stack: **Rudra server board** + **Trinetra** interconnect + open-source (AlmaLinux) software.
[8:1:4] # PARAM Systems → Host
[8:1:5] TABLE head: System | Host
  [8:1:5.0] PARAM Shivay (2019) | IIT (BHU) Varanasi – first NSM machine (first indigenously assembled)
  [8:1:5.1] PARAM Porul | NIT Tiruchirappalli (May 2022)
  [8:1:5.2] PARAM Pravega | IISc Bengaluru
  [8:1:5.3] PARAM Ananta | IIT Gandhinagar
  [8:1:5.4] PARAM Kamrupa | IIT Guwahati – 14th machine, opened by President Droupadi Murmu, 13 Oct 2022
  [8:1:5.5] PARAM Rudra | 3 systems: Pune (GMRT), Delhi (IUAC), Kolkata (S.N. Bose Centre)
  [8:1:5.6] PARAM Shakti | IIT Madras – 3.1 PF, Rudra board, 40% capacity for outside researchers (Jan 2026)
  [8:1:5.7] AIRAWAT (AI supercomputer) | C-DAC Pune – 75th in Top500, ISC 2023 (Germany, May 2023)
[8:1:6] Rudra = a server board the Rudra machines are built on (not an operating system); AUM = C-DAC's separate HPC chip programme.
[8:1:7] PARAM Shivay is at IIT (BHU), NOT IIT Guwahati.
## SUB 2 | National Quantum Mission (nqm)
[8:2:0] - **Qubit**: basic unit of quantum computing; through **superposition** it can be 0 and 1 together.
[8:2:1] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [8:2:1.0] Approved | Union Cabinet, 19 April 2023
  [8:2:1.1] Outlay | ₹6,003.65 crore
  [8:2:1.2] Period | 2023-24 to 2030-31 (8 years)
  [8:2:1.3] Nodal | DST
[8:2:2] # Targets
[8:2:3] - Computing: intermediate-scale computers of **50–1000 physical qubits** in 8 years (superconducting, photonic).
[8:2:4] - Communication: **satellite QKD over 2000 km** within India; inter-city QKD over 2000 km.
[8:2:5] - Sensing: **magnetometers** and **atomic clocks**.

## SUB 3 | Deep Ocean Mission
[8:3:0] - Nodal: **Ministry of Earth Sciences**; Cabinet **June 2021**, **₹4,077 crore** for five years; six components.
[8:3:1] - **Samudrayaan** is a sub-project of the Deep Ocean Mission – India's first crewed deep-ocean mission.
[8:3:2] - **Matsya 6000**: indigenous crewed submersible by **NIOT Chennai**; **3 people** to **6,000 m**; 12 hours normal, 96 hours emergency endurance.
[8:3:3] - **Six components:** (1) deep-sea mining technologies and **manned submersible**; (2) **ocean climate change advisory services**; (3) deep-sea **biodiversity** exploration and conservation; (4) **deep ocean survey** and exploration; (5) **energy and freshwater from the ocean** (OTEC-powered offshore desalination); (6) **Advanced Marine Station for Ocean Biology**.
## SUB 4 | BIO-E3 Policy (2024)
[8:4:0] - **Bio-E3 = Biotechnology for Economy, Environment and Employment**; Cabinet **24 Aug 2024**; steered by **DBT**.
[8:4:1] - Core aim: a **bio-manufacturing ecosystem**; India a global hub of high-performance biomanufacturing.
[8:4:2] - Six thematic sectors: bio-based chemicals and enzymes; smart proteins; precision biotherapeutics; climate-resilient agriculture; carbon capture; marine and space research.
[8:4:3] - Infrastructure: **Biomanufacturing hubs**, **Bio-AI hubs** and **biofoundries**.
[8:4:4] - India's bioeconomy (2024): **over US$165 billion**.
[8:4:5] - Budget 2026-27: **Biopharma SHAKTI**, ₹10,000 crore over 5 years (biologics, biosimilars).

## SUB 1 | C.v. Raman
[9:1:0] - Born **7 November 1888**. Worked at **IACS** outside office hours while in the Accountant General's Office, Calcutta (from 1907), on invitation of Amrit Lal Sircar.
[9:1:1] - **Raman Effect** (change in wavelength of scattered light): **28 February 1928**, IACS Kolkata → **National Science Day, 28 Feb**.
[9:1:2] - First to study the harmonic nature of **tabla and mridangam**; first Indian director of **IISc**.
[9:1:3] - Founded **Indian Academy of Sciences (1934)** and **Raman Research Institute, Bengaluru (1948)**.
[9:1:4] - Honours: knighted **1929**, **Nobel Physics 1930**, **Bharat Ratna 1954** – the scientist with both Nobel and Bharat Ratna; India's first science Nobel; first Asian and first non-White science Nobel.
## SUB 2 | J.c. Bose
[9:2:0] - Showed **plants have life** and respond to stimuli like animals; invented the **Crescograph** (measures tiny plant growth).
[9:2:1] - Pioneer of **microwave/millimetre-wave radio** – **coherer** and **galena (semiconductor) detector**; research in both biology and physics.
[9:2:2] - Founded **Bose Institute, Kolkata, 1917**. Knighted 1917; **FRS 1920**.
[9:2:3] - Books: **Response in the Living and Non-Living (1902)**, **The Nervous Mechanism of Plants**, Bengali sci-fi **Niruddesher Kahini**.
## SUB 3 | S.n. Bose
[9:3:0] - Paper **"Planck's Law and the Hypothesis of Light Quanta" (1924)**, from a Dhaka University lecture, sent to **Einstein**, who translated it into German (Zeitschrift fur Physik) → **Bose-Einstein Statistics**.
[9:3:1] - Particle class **"bosons"** named by **Paul Dirac**.
[9:3:2] - **Padma Vibhushan 1954**; **FRS 1958**; National Professor. Tagore's only science book **Visva-Parichay (1937)** is dedicated to him.
## SUB 4 | Srinivasa Ramanujan
[9:4:0] - Born **22 December 1887, Erode** (TN); died 1920; collaborator **G.H. Hardy** at Cambridge.
[9:4:1] - Works: number theory, partitions, infinite series, continued fractions, **mock theta functions**; **1729** = Hardy-Ramanujan number.
[9:4:2] - **22 December = National Mathematics Day**; **2012 = National Mathematical Year** (125th birth anniversary).
[9:4:3] - Biopic **"The Man Who Knew Infinity"** (2015 British film, from Robert Kanigel's 1991 book; Dev Patel as Ramanujan).
## SUB 5 | Meghnad Saha
[9:5:0] - From Dacca district (now Bangladesh). **Saha (ionisation) equation** from his theory of **thermal ionisation** (papers 1920-21) – gives temperature and ionisation state of stars; explained the Sun's and stellar spectra.
[9:5:1] - Devised an instrument to measure the weight and pressure of solar rays.
[9:5:2] - **Chief architect of river planning** – original plan of the **Damodar Valley Project**; elected to **Lok Sabha 1952**.
[9:5:3] - Founded **NASI (1930)** and **Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics**, Kolkata; **FRS 1927**.
## SUB 6 | Homi Bhabha
[9:6:0] - **Father of India's Atomic Energy / Nuclear Programme**; first chairman of the **Atomic Energy Commission (1948)**.
[9:6:1] - Conceived the **thorium-based three-stage** nuclear power programme; chaired the **first UN Conference on Peaceful Uses of Atomic Energy, Geneva 1955**.
[9:6:2] - Founded **TIFR (1945, Mumbai)** and **AEET Trombay (1954)** – renamed **BARC (1967)** after his death in the Air India crash on Mont Blanc (**1966**).
[9:6:3] - **Bhabha scattering** (electron-positron); cascade theory of cosmic-ray showers.
[9:6:4] - **Adams Prize 1942**, **FRS 1941**, **Padma Bhushan 1954**. **Homi Bhabha Award** is for nuclear energy.
[9:6:5] BARC was NOT founded as BARC in 1945; TIFR = 1945, AEET = 1954, BARC name = 1967.
