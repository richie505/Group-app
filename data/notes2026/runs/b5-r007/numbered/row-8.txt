## SUB 0 | Indian Supercomputers and Computing Firsts
[0:0] - India's supercomputer family is **PARAM**, designed and assembled by **C-DAC, Pune** (C-DAC set up 1988) – not Shubham, SX-2, Tejas or EKA.
[0:1] - **PARAM 8000 (1991)** = India's **first** (first indigenously developed) supercomputer; C-DAC under **Vijay Bhatkar**, the **father of Indian supercomputing**.
[0:2] TABLE head: Machine | Developer / site
  [0:2.0] PARAM 8000 | C-DAC (1991)
  [0:2.1] Anupam | BARC – from 1991, parallel processing
  [0:2.2] Flosolver | NAL Bengaluru
  [0:2.3] PARAM Padma | C-DAC (2003) – first Indian system on the TOP500 list (June 2003, rank 171)
  [0:2.4] SAGA-220 | ISRO (2011) – about 220 TFLOPS
  [0:2.5] SahasraT (Cray XC40) | SERC, IISc – India's fastest in 2015-16 (ahead of Aaditya, IITM Pune)
  [0:2.6] PARAM Siddhi-AI | C-DAC (2020) – ranked 62nd/63rd on TOP500 (Nov 2020), India's highest then
  [0:2.7] Pratyush / Mihir (weather) | IITM Pune / NCMRWF Noida
[0:3] - **Computing firsts:** first analog computer in India designed by **Samarendra Kumar Mitra** (ISI Kolkata, 1953); **TIFRAC** (1960) = first indigenous digital computer; exam keys name **"Siddharth"** as "the first computer made in India".

## SUB 1 | National Supercomputing Mission (NSM, 2015) – Systems and Hosts
[1:0] - Run jointly by **DST and MeitY** (2015); implemented by **C-DAC Pune + IISc Bengaluru**; machines linked as the **National Supercomputing Grid** over the **National Knowledge Network**; **40 systems** planned.
[1:1] - Indigenous stack: **Rudra** server board + **Trinetra** interconnect + open-source (AlmaLinux) software.

## SUB 2 | National Quantum Mission (NQM)
[2:0] - **Qubit** = basic unit of **quantum information**; by **superposition** it can be 0 and 1 together (bit/byte are classical).
[2:1] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [2:1.0] Approved | Union Cabinet, 19 April 2023
  [2:1.1] Outlay | ₹6,003.65 crore
  [2:1.2] Period | 2023-24 to 2030-31 (8 years)
  [2:1.3] Nodal | DST
[2:2] - Targets: computers of **50–1000 physical qubits**; satellite QKD over **2000 km**; magnetometers and atomic clocks.
[2:3] TABLE head: T-Hub | Host

  [2:3.1] Quantum Communication | IIT Madras (with C-DOT)
  [2:3.2] Quantum Sensing and Metrology | IIT Bombay
  [2:3.3] Quantum Materials and Devices | IIT Delhi
[2:4] - Start-ups backed: **QNu Labs** (QKD), QpiAI. **14 April = World Quantum Day**.

## SUB 3 | Deep Ocean Mission
[3:0] - Nodal **Ministry of Earth Sciences**; Cabinet **June 2021**, **₹4,077 crore** for five years; six components.
[3:1] - **Samudrayaan** = sub-project (India's first crewed deep-ocean mission); **Matsya 6000** = crewed submersible built by **NIOT Chennai**, **3 people to 6,000 m**; **Varaha** = deep-sea mining machine trialled at 5,270 m.
[3:2] - **Components:** deep-sea mining and manned submersible; ocean climate change advisory services; deep-sea biodiversity; deep ocean survey; **energy and freshwater from the ocean** (OTEC-powered desalination); **Advanced Marine Station for Ocean Biology**.
[3:3] ~~ Sources: app notes 5:8 sub 3
## SUB 4 | BIO-E3, ANRF and Other Missions
[4:0] - **Bio-E3 = Biotechnology for Economy, Environment and Employment**; Cabinet **24 Aug 2024**; steered by **DBT**; biomanufacturing hubs, Bio-AI hubs, biofoundries; bioeconomy **over US$165 billion** (2024); Budget 2026-27 **Biopharma SHAKTI** ₹10,000 crore over 5 years.

## SUB 5 | Andhra Pradesh Quantum Push – Amaravati Quantum Valley
[5:0] - **Andhra Pradesh** = **first State** with its own **quantum computing policy**; foundation stone at **Uddandarayunipalem**.
[5:1] - Incentive: **100% exemption from stamp duty and registration fees** for tech firms; plan to train 4.5 million people in AI and quantum.
[5:2] - **14 April 2026** (World Quantum Day): India's first **Quantum Reference Facility** test beds – **1S at SRM University-AP**, **1Q at Medha Towers, Gannavaram**.
[5:3] - MoUs (Feb 2026): **IBM to train 1 lakh youth**; **NIELIT Quantum and AI campus** classes from Sept 2026 at **Acharya Nagarjuna University, Guntur**.
[5:4] - PMF: AP announced India's first **133-qubit** quantum computer at Amaravati (April 2026).
[5:5] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 7 p.76; app notes 5:8 sub 7
