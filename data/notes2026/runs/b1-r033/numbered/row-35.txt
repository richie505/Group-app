## SUB 0 | Dhamma – Content, Welfare and the Dhamma Mahamatras
[0:0] # What Dhamma was
[0:1] - **A moral code and social ethic**, not a new religion – discipline, harmony among sects, welfare; Rajdharma on **longanimity, liberty and compassion** = **Ashoka** (UPPCS 1993) – not Akbar, Ranjit Singh or Shivaji.
[0:2] - **Central point:** **peace and non-violence** (RAS 2013) – RE I bans slaughter and sacrifice; toleration and respect for elders are parts, not the core.
[0:3] - **Source of the definition:** the Buddhist **Rahulovada Sutta** (spirit also in the Lakkhana Sutta and Dhammapada).
[0:4] - **Virtues:** kindness, **charity**, truthfulness, purity, respect for elders and teachers, regard for **Brahmanas and Shramanas**, courtesy to slaves and servants. **Vices:** violence, cruelty, anger, pride, jealousy. **Avoid:** wasteful festive gatherings (**samajas**).
[0:5] - **PE II** defines Dhamma as "**few faults, many good deeds**". It names no Four Noble Truths or Nibbana.
[0:6] # Officials and welfare
[0:7] TABLE head: Measure | Edict
  [0:7.0] Dhamma Mahamatras – for all sects, women, prisoners | RE V, 13 years after consecration = 14th regnal year
  [0:7.1] Quinquennial tours of rajukas, pradeshikas, yuktas | RE III (12th year)
  [0:7.2] Pativedakas may report at any time | RE VI
  [0:7.3] Rajukas' independent judicial power; 3-day respite for the condemned | PE IV (26th year)
  [0:7.4] No branding of cattle; prisoner releases | PE V
  [0:7.5] Hospitals for men and animals, medicinal herbs, wells, roadside trees – also in the Chola, Pandya, Satyaputra, Keralaputra lands | RE II
[0:8] - **UPPCS 2024 statements:** Dhamma defined from the **Rahulovada Sutta** ✓; Dhamma Mahamatras in the **14th year of his reign** ✓ → **both 1 and 2**.
[0:9] - **India's first hospitals and herbal gardens:** **Ashoka** (UP Lower Mains 2015) – not Chandragupta, Mahavira or Dhanvantari.
[0:10] - **Devanampriya and Hinduism:** the epithet "**beloved of the gods**" is read as proof that Ashoka, though a Buddhist, kept faith in the old gods (RAS 1999).
[0:11] > Exam angle: Dhamma = **ethics, not religion**; Mahamatras = **RE V, 14th year**; hospitals = **RE II**.

## SUB 1 | Kalinga War and the Change of Policy
[1:0] - **Date:** **c. 261 BCE**, **8th regnal year** (RE XIII). **Cause:** Kalinga had broken free in Bindusara's last years and controlled the **land and sea routes to the south**.
[1:1] - **UP Lower 2002 A–R:** A (Ashoka annexed Kalinga) ✓, R (Kalinga controlled land and sea routes to South India) ✓ and R explains A → **(a)**.
[1:2] - **Toll (RE XIII):** about **1,00,000 killed**, **1,50,000 deported/captive**, many more died later.
[1:3] - **Profound change in policy:** the **Kalinga War** (SSC 2009) – remorse led to **Dhammavijaya**; Bherighosha (war drum) → **Dhammaghosha**; no more wars of conquest. Buddhism, the Third Council and the Ceylon mission came later.
[1:4] - **Kalinga under the Mauryas:** province with capital **Tosali**; the Separate Kalinga Edicts at **Dhauli and Jaugada** omit RE XIII.
[1:5] > Exam angle: "event that changed his policy" → **Kalinga War**; killed / deported = **1 lakh / 1.5 lakh**.

## SUB 3 | Ashoka and Buddhism – Pilgrimage, Council and Missions
[3:0] - **Initiation:** by **Nigrodha** (Sri Lankan tradition, 4th regnal year); by **Upagupta** (Divyavadana).
[3:1] - **Bhabru edict:** faith in the Buddha, Dhamma and Sangha.
[3:2] - **Dhammayatras:** began in the **11th regnal year** with **Bodh Gaya** (Sambodhi); **Lumbini** in the 21st year (tax cut to one-eighth). Kanchipuram was not on his route.
[3:3] - **Pilgrimage order (Divyavadana):** **Lumbini → Kapilavastu → Gaya → Sarnath → Kushinagar → Shravasti** = codes **4, 2, 1, 5, 3, 6** (UPPCS 1999).
[3:4] - **Third Buddhist Council:** **Pataliputra**, under **Moggaliputta Tissa**; missions followed.
[3:5] TABLE head: Missionary | Region
  [3:5.0] Mahinda (son), Sanghamitta (daughter, with a Bodhi branch) | Sri Lanka – king Devanampiya Tissa
  [3:5.1] Majjhantika | Kashmir and Gandhara
  [3:5.2] Mahadeva | Mahishamandala (Andhra tradition links him with the Amaravati stupa, which Ashoka enlarged)
  [3:5.3] Rakkhita | Vanavasi
  [3:5.4] Yona Dhammarakkhita | Aparantaka
  [3:5.5] Mahadhammarakkhita | Maharattha
  [3:5.6] Maharakkhita | Yona (Greek) country
  [3:5.7] Majjhima | Himalayan region
  [3:5.8] Sona and Uttara | Suvarnabhumi
[3:6] - **Dasaratha**, Ashoka's grandson, is taken as a **Buddhist** though he gave the Nagarjuni caves to the Ajivikas.
[3:7] > Exam angle: Maharattha = **Mahadhammarakkhita**, Yona = **Maharakkhita**; Suvarnabhumi = **Sona and Uttara**.

## SUB 4 | Titles, Accession and Early Career
[4:0] TABLE head: Title / name | Where / meaning
  [4:0.0] Devanampiya | "Beloved of the gods"
  [4:0.1] Piyadasi | "Of pleasing looks"
  [4:0.2] Buddhashakya Ashoka | Maski
  [4:0.3] Dharmasoka | Sarnath inscription (CDS 2003) – not Maski, Junagadh or the Allahabad pillar
[4:1] - **Before accession:** viceroy of **Taxila** (sent to crush a revolt – Divyavadana) and **Ujjain**; won Avanti in Bindusara's time per the **Samantapasadika** of **Buddhaghosa**.
[4:2] - **Succession:** struggle with **Susima**; the Mahavamsa says he killed **99 brothers**; four-year gap – accession **c. 273/272 BCE**, **coronation 269 BCE**.
[4:3] - **Legends:** Ashokavadana/Divyavadana – Upagupta, Chandashoka → **Dharmashoka**; Mahavamsa/Dipavamsa – Nigrodha, 99 brothers, Mahinda.
[4:4] - **Prakrit inscriptions:** he was the first Indian king to address his people directly, through inscriptions written in **Prakrit**.
[4:5] - **Out-of-row facts asked with Ashoka:** Agrammes = Dhanananda; Patanjali – Pushyamitra Shunga; Kanishka's court – Ashvaghosha, Vasumitra, Charaka (not Banabhatta, who was Harsha's poet).
[4:6] > Exam angle: Dharmasoka = **Sarnath**; Buddhashakya = **Maski**.
