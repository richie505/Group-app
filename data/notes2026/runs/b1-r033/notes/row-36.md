=== The Name 'Ashoka', His Titles and the Decipherment
# Titles in the edicts
- **Usual title:** **Devanampiya Piyadasi** – Devanampiya (Devanampriya) = "**Beloved of the Gods**"; Piyadasi (**Priyadarshi**) = "of pleasing appearance" (IAS 1995; MPPSC 2015; APPSC G1) – not Chakravarti, Dharmadeva or Dharmakirti; not a title of Samudragupta, Kanishka or Harsha.
- **Other names:** **Buddhashakya** – Maski; **Dharmasoka** – Sarnath inscription; **Ashokavardhana** – Puranas.
- **Bhabru (Bairat):** calls himself "**Piyadasi Raja**" of **Magadha** – the only edict where he declares himself king of Magadha (State PSC; it is a minor rock edict, though the paper called it a "pillar"); Maski, Rummindei and the Queen's edict are distractors.
# Edicts that name "Ashoka"
| Edict | Location | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| **Maski** (found 1915) | Raichur, Karnataka | "Devanampiya Asoka" – the edict that proved Piyadasi = Ashoka |
| **Gujarra** | **Datia district, Madhya Pradesh** (on the Ujjain–Bharuch road) | "Devanampiya Piyadasi Asoka" |
| **Nittur, Udegolam** | Ballari, Karnataka | Also carry the name |
| **Kanaganahalli** | Near Sannati, Kalaburagi, Karnataka, on the **Bhima** | Stone portrait of Ashoka with his queens, label "**Ranyo Asoko**" (King Ashoka) – the only inscribed portrait |
- **Personal name asked:** **Maski** (UPSC 1997 ×2; RAS 2007) and **Gujarra** (UPPCS 2015). Kalsi, Rummindei, the Special Kalinga Edict, Bhabru, RE XIII, Ahraura, Brahmagiri and Sarnath use only the titles.
- **Ranyo Ashoka with his portrait:** **Kanaganahalli** (UPSC 2019 ×2) – Ashoka is depicted at Sanchi and Amaravati but not named; Shahbazgarhi and Sohgaura are distractors.
# Decipherment and firsts
- **James Prinsep** (British, "Princep") **deciphered Brahmi and Kharosthi in 1837** – the first scholar to read the Ashokan edicts and the first Briton to study them (UPPCS 2010; CGPSC 2003; IAS 2016). Not Buhler, Max Muller, William Jones, Cunningham.
- **First edicts read:** the **Delhi-Topra** pillar edicts. George Turnour later linked "Devanampiya Piyadasi" with Ashoka from the Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa.
- **First Indian ruler to speak directly to his people through inscriptions** – on **natural rocks and polished pillars**, in **Prakrit**: **Ashoka** (APPSC; AP Hostel Welfare Officer) – not Chandragupta Maurya, Kharavela, Gautamiputra Satakarni or the Guptas.
- **Form borrowed from Persia:** the third-person opening and the word "**lipi/dipi**" follow the inscriptions of **Darius**. Ashoka called his edicts **Dhammalipi**.
> Exam angle: "name Ashoka" → **Maski / Gujarra**; "Ranyo Ashoka + portrait" → **Kanaganahalli**; "first deciphered" → **James Prinsep, 1837**.

=== Major Rock Edicts – Number and Subject
| RE | Subject |
|---|---|
| **I** | **Ban on animal sacrifice** and on festive gatherings (**samaja**); killing in the royal kitchen cut down |
| **II** | **Medical care for men and animals**, herbs and trees; names the **Cholas, Pandyas, Satiyaputtas, Keralaputtas, Tamraparni** (Sri Lanka) and the Yavana king **Antiyoka** |
| **III** | **Quinquennial (five-yearly) tours** of rajukas, pradeshikas and yuktas (12th year) |
| **IV** | **Dhammaghosha** in place of Bherighosha |
| **V** | **Dhamma Mahamattas** appointed, 13 years after consecration; mentions his brothers and sisters |
| **VI** | **Pativedakas** may report to the king at all times |
| **VIII** | **Dhammayatras** replace royal pleasure tours; the first to Bodh Gaya |
| **XII** | **Religious tolerance** – respect for all sects, restraint in praising one's own sect |
| **XIII** | **Kalinga War**, remorse, **Dhammavijaya**; five Greek kings; the **Andhras** named |
| **XIV** | Summary – why and how the edicts were engraved |
- **RE I:** bans animal sacrifice at festive gatherings (RAS 2013) – not PE V, RE IX or XI.
- **RE II:** names the **South Indian kingdoms** (UPPCS Mains 2016). **Sangam kingdoms: RE II and XIII** (UPSC 1998 ×2).
- **RE XII:** "Whosoever praises his religious sect or blames other sects … injures his own sect very severely" = **Ashoka** (UPSC 2020 ×2); the edict of **religious tolerance / synthesis** (UP Lower 2008; UPPCS 2022) – not RE XIII, PE VII or Bhabru.
- **RE XIII:** the **account of the Kalinga War**, its victory and casualties (UPPSC 2016; IAS 1998) – not Rummindei, Minor RE I or Hiuen Tsang.
- **Andhras:** the **definite mention of the Andhras** is in **RE XIII** (APPSC Asst. BC & Tribal Welfare Officer 2017 ×2) – listed (with the Palidas) among peoples within Ashoka's domain who follow his Dhamma. There is no 15th RE (only 14).
- **Prisoners:** released on **every anniversary of his coronation (abhisheka)** – 25 releases in 26 years (PE V) (APPSC G1) – not his birth, Kalinga or conversion.
- **Sri Lanka (Tamraparni)** was a neighbour named in RE II, **not part of the empire** (State PSC); Afghanistan, Bihar and Kalinga were.
- **What the inscriptions tell:** his life (Kalinga), internal policy (officials, Dhamma) and foreign policy (Greek kings, southern kingdoms) – **all of these** (SSC 2018).
> Exam angle: **I** sacrifice, **II** medical care + south, **V** Mahamattas, **XII** tolerance, **XIII** Kalinga + Greeks + Andhras.

=== Scripts and Languages
| Script | Language | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahmi** (left to right) | **Prakrit** | **Most edicts** – Ganga plain, Deccan, Andhra (Erragudi, Rajula Mandagiri), Girnar, Kalsi |
| **Kharosthi** (**right to left**) | Prakrit | North-west only: **Shahbazgarhi**, **Mansehra** (Pakistan) |
| **Greek** | Greek | Kandahar (Afghanistan) |
| **Aramaic** | Aramaic | **Taxila**, Laghman, Kandahar |
| Greek + Aramaic (bilingual) | – | **Kandahar (Shar-i-Kuna)** |
- **Script of most edicts:** **Brahmi** (APPSC FRO) – not Devanagari, Kharosthi or Sharada. **Language:** **Prakrit** (State PSC) – not Sanskrit, Pali or Hindi.
- **Kharosthi:** written **right to left** (UPSC 1997), derived from **Aramaic**; the Kharosthi edict among Kalsi, Girnar, Shahbazgarhi, Meerut is **Shahbazgarhi** (CGPSC 2011).
- **APPSC G1 2023 statements:** I. Brahmi, Kharosthi, Greek, Aramaic ✓; II. name Ashoka only at Maski and Gujarra ✓ (the key's view); III. RE XIII names five Greek rulers ✓; IV. the war of succession in RE IV and V ✗ (RE V only mentions his brothers' families) → **I, II and III**.
  - **⚠ Disputed:** strictly the name also occurs at **Nittur and Udegolam** (PMF), so II is loose; the key still takes it as correct.
- **Brahmi** is the source of the **Telugu** script (it was not used only for Telugu). No edict names the Four Noble Truths or Nibbana; the edicts never mention varna or sati.
- Inscriptions fall into three classes: **rock, pillar and cave** (Barabar) inscriptions.
> Exam angle: Kharosthi = **right to left, NW, Shahbazgarhi/Mansehra**; most edicts = **Prakrit in Brahmi**.
~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.3; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.122, p.125-126; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.43; app notes 1:36 (sub 1)

=== Pillar Edicts and the Pillars
| Pillar | Content |
|---|---|
| **Rummindei (Lumbini)**, Nepal | Visit 20 years after consecration; marks the **Buddha's birthplace**; Lumbini freed from **bali**, **bhaga cut from 1/6 to 1/8** |
| **Nigali Sagar**, Nepal | Kanakamuni stupa enlarged (14th year) |
| **Sarnath** | Schism edict; **lion capital** = national emblem |
| **Allahabad-Kosam** | PE I–VI, Schism and **Queen's edict** (gifts of **Karuvaki**); later Samudragupta's prashasti and Jahangir's inscription |
| **Delhi-Topra** | The only pillar with **PE VII** – rajukas, Ajivikas, Nirgranthas; Dhamma spreads better by **persuasion than by law** |
| **Lauriya Nandangarh** | West Champaran, Bihar; single lion capital; PE I–VI |
- **Lumbini tax cut to 1/8:** **Rummindei pillar** (APPSC G2 2024) – not Maski, Kalsi or Vaishali.
- **Confirms the Buddha was born at Lumbini:** **Rummindei** (UPUDA 2010) – not Basarh, Nigali Sagar, Rampurwa; so the dynasty whose inscription marks Lumbini = **Mauryan** (UPUDA 2006).
- **Sarnath pillar:** built by **Ashoka** (UP Lower 2008) – not Harsha, the Buddha or Kanishka.
- **Topra and Meerut pillars** were brought to Delhi by **Firuz Shah Tughluq** (UPPCS 1996); the Kosam pillar went to the Allahabad fort traditionally under **Akbar**.
- **PE II** defines Dhamma ("few faults, many good deeds"); **PE IV** (26th year) – rajukas' judicial power, 3-day respite; **PE V** – no cattle branding, prisoner releases.
- **Schism edict:** dissenting monks and nuns expelled – **Sarnath, Sanchi, Kaushambi**.
- Pillars cluster in the **Ganga plain** – river transport for the heavy Chunar sandstone monoliths.
> Exam angle: Rummindei = **pillar in Nepal, Lumbini, 1/8**; Firuz Shah = **Topra + Meerut**.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.43, p.45; NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.3-4; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.126-127; app notes 1:36 (sub 5)

=== Ashokan Edicts in Andhra and the Minor Rock Edicts
| Site | Location | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Erragudi (Yerragudi / Jonnagiri)** | Pattikonda, **Kurnool** district, AP | 14 Major RE + Minor RE; **teachers must instil Dhamma in their pupils** |
| **Rajula Mandagiri** | **Kurnool** district, AP | Minor RE only; guru–disciple relationship; proof of Mauryan rule in Andhra |
| **Maski** | Raichur, Karnataka | Name "Ashoka" |
| Brahmagiri, Siddapura, Jatinga-Rameshwara | Chitradurga, Karnataka | Minor RE I and II – southern limit |
| **Bhabru (Bairat)** | Rajasthan | Faith in the **Buddha, Dhamma and Sangha**; recommends Buddhist texts |
- **Teachers to inculcate religious thought in disciples:** **Erragudi** (APPSC G2 2008; AP History PYQ) – Bhattiprolu and Dhanyakataka are not Ashokan sites.
  - **⚠ Disputed:** most books and keys give **Erragudi**; strictly **Rajula Mandagiri** carries the same Minor RE II text on gurus and disciples (AP History APPSC 01).
- **Ashoka's inscriptions in AP:** **Erragudi** (AP History PYQ) – not Nagarjuna Hill, Amaravati or Bhattiprolu.
- **Earliest inscriptions in Andhra:** **Ashoka's** (AP History PYQ) – in **Prakrit, Brahmi**; they show Mauryan rule reached Rayalaseema. Nashik, Hathigumpha and the Rakshasa caves are later.
- **Erragudi's district:** **Kurnool** (Pattikonda mandal; ASI site on the Gooty–Pattikonda road) – not Nandyal.
- **Connection with Buddhism:** **Minor Rock Edict I** – Ashoka calls himself an **upasaka** who drew closer to the Sangha (NDA 2017); RE XIII, RE VI and PE IV are distractors. Minor edicts are the earliest Ashokan records and are densest in the Andhra–Karnataka area.
> Exam angle: AP edict sites = **Erragudi and Rajula Mandagiri (Kurnool)**; Bhattiprolu = Kubiraka casket, not Ashoka.

=== Major Rock Edict Sites
| Site | Present location |
|---|---|
| **Kalsi** | **Dehradun, Uttarakhand** |
| **Girnar** | **Junagadh, Gujarat** – same rock bears Rudradaman's inscription |
| Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra | Pakistan (Kharosthi) |
| **Dhauli** | Near Bhubaneswar, **Odisha** – Separate Kalinga Edicts |
| **Jaugada** | **Ganjam, Odisha** – Separate Kalinga Edicts |
| Sopara | Maharashtra |
| **Erragudi** | **Andhra Pradesh** |
| Sannati | Karnataka |
| Kandahar | Afghanistan (Greek) |
- **Kalsi:** famous for Ashoka's rock edicts (UP Lower 2008); the Ashokan edict of **Uttarakhand** (UKPSC 2005; UK UDA 2007) – not Devprayag, Kedarnath, Rishikesh, Nainital, Pauri or Tehri.
- **UPSC 2022 pairs:** Dhauli–Odisha ✓, Erragudi–AP ✓, Jaugada–MP ✗ (Odisha), Kalsi–Karnataka ✗ (Uttarakhand) → **only two pairs**.
- **Separate Kalinga Edicts** (Dhauli, Jaugada) replace RE XI–XIII there; addressed to officers of **Tosali and Samapa**; "**All men are my children**".
- **Not Ashokan:** **Besnagar** – Heliodorus pillar. Rummindei is a pillar, not a Major RE site. The spread of edict sites is the main evidence for the **extent of the empire**; Kashmir comes through Kalhana's Rajatarangini (son **Jalauka**).
> Exam angle: Kalsi = **Uttarakhand**; Jaugada and Dhauli = **Odisha**; Girnar = **Gujarat**.
