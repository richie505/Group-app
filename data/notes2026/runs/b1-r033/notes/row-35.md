=== Dhamma – Content, Welfare and the Dhamma Mahamatras
# What Dhamma was
- **A moral code and social ethic**, not a new religion – discipline, harmony among sects, welfare; Rajdharma on **longanimity, liberty and compassion** = **Ashoka** (UPPCS 1993) – not Akbar, Ranjit Singh or Shivaji.
- **Central point:** **peace and non-violence** (RAS 2013) – RE I bans slaughter and sacrifice; toleration and respect for elders are parts, not the core.
- **Source of the definition:** the Buddhist **Rahulovada Sutta** (spirit also in the Lakkhana Sutta and Dhammapada).
- **Virtues:** kindness, **charity**, truthfulness, purity, respect for elders and teachers, regard for **Brahmanas and Shramanas**, courtesy to slaves and servants. **Vices:** violence, cruelty, anger, pride, jealousy. **Avoid:** wasteful festive gatherings (**samajas**).
- **PE II** defines Dhamma as "**few faults, many good deeds**". It names no Four Noble Truths or Nibbana.
# Officials and welfare
| Measure | Edict |
|---|---|
| **Dhamma Mahamatras** – for all sects, women, prisoners | **RE V**, 13 years after consecration = **14th regnal year** |
| Quinquennial tours of rajukas, pradeshikas, yuktas | RE III (12th year) |
| **Pativedakas** may report at any time | RE VI |
| Rajukas' independent judicial power; **3-day respite** for the condemned | **PE IV** (26th year) |
| No branding of cattle; prisoner releases | **PE V** |
| **Hospitals for men and animals**, medicinal herbs, wells, roadside trees – also in the Chola, Pandya, Satyaputra, Keralaputra lands | **RE II** |
- **UPPCS 2024 statements:** Dhamma defined from the **Rahulovada Sutta** ✓; Dhamma Mahamatras in the **14th year of his reign** ✓ → **both 1 and 2**.
- **India's first hospitals and herbal gardens:** **Ashoka** (UP Lower Mains 2015) – not Chandragupta, Mahavira or Dhanvantari.
- **Devanampriya and Hinduism:** the epithet "**beloved of the gods**" is read as proof that Ashoka, though a Buddhist, kept faith in the old gods (RAS 1999).
> Exam angle: Dhamma = **ethics, not religion**; Mahamatras = **RE V, 14th year**; hospitals = **RE II**.

=== Kalinga War and the Change of Policy
- **Date:** **c. 261 BCE**, **8th regnal year** (RE XIII). **Cause:** Kalinga had broken free in Bindusara's last years and controlled the **land and sea routes to the south**.
- **UP Lower 2002 A–R:** A (Ashoka annexed Kalinga) ✓, R (Kalinga controlled land and sea routes to South India) ✓ and R explains A → **(a)**.
- **Toll (RE XIII):** about **1,00,000 killed**, **1,50,000 deported/captive**, many more died later.
- **Profound change in policy:** the **Kalinga War** (SSC 2009) – remorse led to **Dhammavijaya**; Bherighosha (war drum) → **Dhammaghosha**; no more wars of conquest. Buddhism, the Third Council and the Ceylon mission came later.
- **Kalinga under the Mauryas:** province with capital **Tosali**; the Separate Kalinga Edicts at **Dhauli and Jaugada** omit RE XIII.
> Exam angle: "event that changed his policy" → **Kalinga War**; killed / deported = **1 lakh / 1.5 lakh**.

=== Ashoka and Buddhism – Pilgrimage, Council and Missions
- **Initiation:** by **Nigrodha** (Sri Lankan tradition, 4th regnal year); by **Upagupta** (Divyavadana).
- **Bhabru edict:** faith in the Buddha, Dhamma and Sangha.
- **Dhammayatras:** began in the **11th regnal year** with **Bodh Gaya** (Sambodhi); **Lumbini** in the 21st year (tax cut to one-eighth). Kanchipuram was not on his route.
- **Pilgrimage order (Divyavadana):** **Lumbini → Kapilavastu → Gaya → Sarnath → Kushinagar → Shravasti** = codes **4, 2, 1, 5, 3, 6** (UPPCS 1999).
- **Third Buddhist Council:** **Pataliputra**, under **Moggaliputta Tissa**; missions followed.

|---|---|
| **Mahinda** (son), **Sanghamitta** (daughter, with a Bodhi branch) | **Sri Lanka** – king **Devanampiya Tissa** |
| **Majjhantika** | **Kashmir and Gandhara** |
| **Mahadeva** | **Mahishamandala** (Andhra tradition links him with the **Amaravati** stupa, which Ashoka enlarged) |
| Rakkhita | Vanavasi |
| Yona Dhammarakkhita | Aparantaka |
| **Mahadhammarakkhita** | **Maharattha** |

| Majjhima | Himalayan region |

- **Dasaratha**, Ashoka's grandson, is taken as a **Buddhist** though he gave the Nagarjuni caves to the Ajivikas.
> Exam angle: Maharattha = **Mahadhammarakkhita**, Yona = **Maharakkhita**; Suvarnabhumi = **Sona and Uttara**.

=== Titles, Accession and Early Career
| Title / name | Where / meaning |
|---|---|
| **Devanampiya** | "Beloved of the gods" |
| **Piyadasi** | "Of pleasing looks" |
| **Buddhashakya Ashoka** | Maski |
| **Dharmasoka** | **Sarnath inscription** (CDS 2003) – not Maski, Junagadh or the Allahabad pillar |
- **Before accession:** viceroy of **Taxila** (sent to crush a revolt – Divyavadana) and **Ujjain**; won Avanti in Bindusara's time per the **Samantapasadika** of **Buddhaghosa**.
- **Succession:** struggle with **Susima**; the Mahavamsa says he killed **99 brothers**; four-year gap – accession **c. 273/272 BCE**, **coronation 269 BCE**.
- **Legends:** Ashokavadana/Divyavadana – Upagupta, Chandashoka → **Dharmashoka**; Mahavamsa/Dipavamsa – Nigrodha, 99 brothers, Mahinda.
- **Prakrit inscriptions:** he was the first Indian king to address his people directly, through inscriptions written in **Prakrit**.
- **Out-of-row facts asked with Ashoka:** Agrammes = Dhanananda; Patanjali – Pushyamitra Shunga; Kanishka's court – Ashvaghosha, Vasumitra, Charaka (not Banabhatta, who was Harsha's poet).
> Exam angle: Dharmasoka = **Sarnath**; Buddhashakya = **Maski**.
