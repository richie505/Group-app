## SUB 2 | Indica of Megasthenes
[33:2:0] - **Megasthenes** (Greek) – envoy of **Seleucus I Nicator** to the court of **Chandragupta Maurya** at **Pataliputra** (c. 302 BCE).
[33:2:1] - Original **lost**; fragments in **Diodorus, Strabo, Pliny, Arrian** (Strabo and Arrian only kept parts).
[33:2:2] - **Deimachus** – a later envoy of **Antiochus I** to **Bindusara** (not Megasthenes).
[33:2:3] # What Indica Says
[33:2:4] TABLE head: Topic | Account
  [33:2:4.0] Society | Seven classes, not the fourfold varna; noted endogamy and hereditary occupations
  [33:2:4.1] Slavery | Claimed no slavery in India – wrong; Arthashastra and Ashokan edicts mention dasas
  [33:2:4.2] Pataliputra | Palibothra (also Polibrotha) = Patna (UKPSC 2025); palace; wooden palisade
  [33:2:4.3] City administration | Six committees of five members each; town officials Astynomoi
  [33:2:4.4] Andhras | First foreign work to mention the Andhras: 30 fortified towns, 1,00,000 infantry, 2,000 cavalry, 1,000 elephants
[33:2:5] APPSC G2 2002: first to mention forts in Andhra = Megasthenes (Indika, 4th c. BC).
[33:2:6] Andhra forts = 30, not 80. The 80 belongs to the 80-pillared hall at Kumrahar.
[33:2:7] 7 classes | 6 committees x 5 members | 30 Andhra forts | 1,00,000 infantry | 2,000 cavalry | 1,000 elephants
[33:2:8] - **Errors in Indica:** besides the wrong "no slavery" claim, Indica has uncritical tales such as gold-digging ants; Strabo, following Megasthenes, wrote that Indians did not know writing.

## SUB 1 | Chandragupta Maurya – Origin and Rise
[34:1:0] # Origin Debate
[34:1:1] TABLE head: Tradition | Origin
  [34:1:1.0] Brahmanical | "Maurya" from a Shudra woman Mura
  [34:1:1.1] Buddhist and Jain | Kshatriya of the Moriya clan
  [34:1:1.2] Mudrarakshasa (commentary by Dhundiraja) | A descendant of the Nanda house
[34:1:2] - Reign **324/321–297 BCE**; founder of the **Maurya** dynasty (321–185 BC) – the oldest among Maurya, Kushan, Gupta and Vardhana.
[34:1:3] - **First pan-Indian empire** = Mauryas; first Indian empire = Chandragupta Maurya (BPSC 2020, 2024) – uniting most of India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and parts of Afghanistan.
[34:1:4] Even if the Nandas are called empire-builders, the answer for "first (pan-Indian) empire" is Chandragupta Maurya.
[34:1:5] # Overthrow of the Nandas
[34:1:6] - 1. **Chanakya** guided him (linked with **Taxila**).
[34:1:7] - 2. Army: **6,00,000 infantry, 30,000 cavalry**; **Justin** (Roman historian) called it a "**gang of dacoits**".
[34:1:8] - 3. Eliminated the **Greek satraps**, with Porus (Parvataka) as ally.
[34:1:9] - 4. Took the Magadhan throne in **321 BC** from **Dhana Nanda**.
[34:1:10] - **Takshashila (Taxila)** – in **Gandhara**; centre of learning linked with Chanakya, Chandragupta, **Charaka** and **Jivaka**.
[34:1:11] - **Pataliputra** – founded by **Ajatashatru** (Haryanka); **Udayin** moved the capital there from Rajgriha.
## SUB 2 | Chanakya (kautilya)
[34:2:0] - **Office:** **prime minister / chief minister** and guide of Chandragupta; taught at Taxila.
[34:2:1] - The Arthashastra names its author **Kautilya**; one verse calls him **Vishnugupta**.
[34:2:2] - **Taranath** and **Jain tradition**: continued as **Bindusara's minister**.
[34:2:3] - **Mudrarakshasa** (Vishakhadatta): court intrigues of Chandragupta's time – **Chanakya versus Rakshasa**, minister of the Nandas.
[34:2:4] - Other traditions: **Devi Chandraguptam**; **Vamsatthapakasini** (10th c.) on the Chanakya legend.

## SUB 6 | Kalinga War
[36:6:0] - Date: **261 BC**, **8th regnal year**. Cause: Kalinga controlled east-coast land and sea routes.
[36:6:1] - RE XIII: about **1,00,000 killed**, **1,50,000 captive**; many more died of famine and disease.
[36:6:2] - Result: remorse, conversion to Buddhism, **no more wars of conquest**, **Dhammavijaya** replaces military conquest; **Bherighosha → Dhammaghosha**.
[36:6:3] - **Separate Kalinga Edicts** (Dhauli, Jaugada) **replace RE XI–XIII** (RE XIII is omitted there); addressed to officers of **Tosali and Samapa** against unjust jailing and torture; "all men are my children".
## SUB 7 | Ashoka's Dhamma
[36:7:0] - Virtues: kindness, **charity**, truthfulness, gratitude, respect for elders, compassion to humans and animals.
[36:7:1] - Vices: **violence, cruelty, anger, pride, jealousy**.
[36:7:2] - Agents: **Dhamma Mahamattas** (RE V); **Dhammayatras** (RE VIII, from the 11th regnal year).
[36:7:3] - Dhamma is a moral code, not Buddhist doctrine – no Noble Truths or Nibbana named.
