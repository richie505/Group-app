=== NOTES 1:63 | Gupta Administration – Feudalism Debate, Land Grants, Agraharas, Revenue and Local Bodies
## SUB 0 | Revenue – Bhaga, Gupta Taxes and Vishti
[63:0:0] # The king's share
[63:0:1] - **Prescribed rate:** the law books (Dharmashastras / "Hindu law") fix the king's share (**bhaga**) at **one-sixth** of the produce (UPPCS 1992; State PSC ×2). In practice the Gupta king took **one-fourth to one-sixth**.
[63:0:2]   - ⚠ NextGen Indian History 03 p.8 gives "about 1/7" – an outlier; the same file (p.6) and PMF give 1/4–1/6, and exam keys give **1/6**.
[63:0:3] - **Tax trend:** under the Guptas **land taxes rose** while **trade and commerce taxes fell**.
[63:0:4] - **Bhaga and Bali:** **sources of revenue** (UPPCS 1996) – bhaga = king's share of produce; bali = once a voluntary offering, later compulsory. Not military departments, rituals or divisions.
[63:0:5] # Revenue terms
[63:0:6] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [63:0:6.0] Bhaga | King's share of produce – main land revenue (1/6)
  [63:0:6.1] Bhoga | Periodic supplies in kind – fruits, firewood, flowers
  [63:0:6.2] Kara | Periodic general tax
  [63:0:6.3] Udranga | New Gupta tax: land revenue in the inscriptions (nature unclear – perhaps water or police tax)
  [63:0:6.4] Uparikara | New Gupta tax, probably on temporary tenants – an additional farm tax
  [63:0:6.5] Udakabhaga | Irrigation (water) tax (UPPCS Mains 2009)
  [63:0:6.6] Halivakara | Plough tax paid by every cultivator owning a plough (SSC CGL 2019)
  [63:0:6.7] Hiranya | Tax paid in cash (gold) instead of produce
  [63:0:6.8] Sulka (Shulka) | Customs duty and tolls on merchandise
  [63:0:6.9] Sita | Revenue from crown land
  [63:0:6.10] Vata-bhuta | Cess for rites to winds and spirits
  [63:0:6.11] Uparnika / Klipta / Upaklipta / Valiam | Tax on border farmers / taxes on land sale and purchase / commodity tax
[63:0:7] # Vishti – forced labour
[63:0:8] - **Vishti:** unpaid **forced labour** for the army, officials, the state or the donee (Telugu **vetti**); it was considered **a source of income for the State, a sort of tax paid by the people** (UPSC 2019).
[63:0:9]   - It was imposed in **central and western India** (Malwa, Kathiawar – Junagadh inscription), so "totally absent in MP and Kathiawar" is wrong; it was **unpaid**, so no weekly wages; not confined to the eldest son.
[63:0:10] - **Peasants:** had to feed the royal army on the march and supply officials on tour; they sank to a **semi-servile** status.
[63:0:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 03 p.6-8; PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.66-67; app notes 1:63 (sub 5, 6)
## SUB 1 | Land Grants, Feudalism and the Agrarian Structure
[63:1:0] # Land grants
[63:1:1] TABLE head: Grant | Grantee | Rights
  [63:1:1.0] Agrahara | Brahmanas (religious functionaries) | Tax-free, hereditary; right to collect dues from peasants
  [63:1:1.1] Brahmadeya | Brahmanas, singly or jointly | Tax exemption + administrative rights
  [63:1:1.2] Devagrahara / Devadana | Temples and monasteries | Revenue for worship
  [63:1:1.3] Secular grants | Officials, samantas | Revenue in lieu of salary
[63:1:2] - **Tax-free agrarian land** went to the **Brahmins** (MPPSC 2014) – not to military or civil officials or court scholars.
[63:1:3] - **Most villages donated to temples and Brahmins:** the **Guptas** (State PSC key).
[63:1:4] - **Earliest land grants:** the **Satavahanas** (Deccan, early centuries CE); the practice became common under the Guptas.
[63:1:5] - **Effects:** royal officials barred from entering granted villages; grantees could punish criminals; **sub-infeudation**; grants in **tribal frontiers** brought virgin land under cultivation and turned tribes into **sub-castes**.
[63:1:6] # Post-Gupta agrarian structure (AP recruitment papers)
[63:1:7] - **TRUE:** land grants in tribal frontiers brought **virgin land under cultivation**; post-Gupta **copper-plate charters describe various types of land**; the **Smritis** speak of graded rights in the same land – **Mahipati** (king), **Kshetrasvamin** (owner), **Karshaka** (cultivator) and **sub-tenant**.
[63:1:8] - **FALSE:** "Pallava records specify 10 to 15 kinds of taxes" – the Pallava grants mention **18 types of taxes** exempted for donated villages.
[63:1:9] - **Early medieval features:** hierarchical **land intermediaries** with feudal titles and military obligations, prevalence of **vishti**, and a relatively **closed village economy** – all three.
[63:1:10] # The feudalism debate
[63:1:11] - **Essential elements (UPSC 2015):** an administrative structure based on **control and possession of land**, and a **lord–vassal** relationship – a **strong central authority** with weak local power is **not** feudal (it is the opposite).
[63:1:12] - **Post-Gupta feudalism (UPPCS Mains 2004 A–R):** the main feature of the post-Gupta agrarian structure was **feudalism** **because** a **landowning intermediate class** and a **dependent peasantry** arose – A and R true, R explains A.
[63:1:13] TABLE head: Historian | Position
  [63:1:13.0] R.S. Sharma | Proponent – land grants, sub-infeudation, vishti, decline of trade, urban decay, fewer gold coins
  [63:1:13.1] D.C. Sircar | Critic – grants transferred revenue rights, not ownership
  [63:1:13.2] Harbans Mukhia | Critic – "Was there feudalism in Indian history?"; free peasantry, no serfdom
  [63:1:13.3] B.D. Chattopadhyaya | Integrative model – grants spread state society into new areas
[63:1:14] - **Evidence cited (7th c.):** decline of long-distance trade and **gold coinage**, shrinking towns.
[63:1:15] - **Satavahana land measure:** **nivartana** ≈ **1.5 acres** (100 nivartanas granted to monks).
[63:1:16] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.66-67; NextGen Ikshvakus p.16 (Pallava – 18 taxes exempted); NextGen Indian History APPSC 03 p.8; app notes 1:63 (sub 8, 9)
[63:1:17] - **Ardhika (arthika):** a **sharecropper** who tilled another's land for **half the crop**; a rent-free Brahmin village = agrahara / brahmadeya.

[63:2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [63:2:0.0] Kshetra | Cultivated land
  [63:2:0.1] Khila | Untilled / uncultivable waste land
  [63:2:0.2] Aprahata | Uncultivated forest (jungle) land (BPSC 2017)
  [63:2:0.3] Vastu (Vasti) | Habitable / house-site land
  [63:2:0.4] Gopata Sarah | Pasture land
  [63:2:0.5] Kulyavapa, Dronavapa | Units of land measurement in Bengal inscriptions – area sown with one kulya / one drona of seed (UPSC 2020)
  [63:2:0.6] Nivartana | Land measure used in other regions
[63:2:1] - **Aprahata:** keyed as "uncultivated forest land", not dense forest, irrigated or cultivated land; NextGen 03 p.15 glosses it "cultivable wasteland" – same idea of land not yet tilled.
[63:2:2] - **Kulyavapa / dronavapa** are **not** coins, urban land classes or rituals.

## SUB 3 | Kingship, Officials and Local Bodies
[63:3:0] # Kingship
[63:3:1] - **Monarchy:** Gupta rule was **kingship** (CDS 2008) – hereditary, with divine claims, not republican or democratic; **primogeniture was not firm**.
[63:3:2] - **Titles:** **Maharajadhiraja, Parameshvara, Paramabhattaraka**; kings likened to **Vishnu**; **Samudragupta** equated with **Kubera (Dhanada), Varuna, Indra and Yama (Antaka)**.
[63:3:3] - **Decentralised:** unlike the Mauryas, much of the empire was held by **feudatories (samantas)** – **Samudragupta** reinstated defeated kings; officials often paid in **land or kind**.
[63:3:4] # Officials
[63:3:5] TABLE head: Official | Function
  [63:3:5.0] Kumaramatya | Most important officers, appointed by the king in the home provinces; ministers and commanders chosen from them
  [63:3:5.1] Sandhivigrahika | Minister of peace and war
  [63:3:5.2] Mahadandanayaka | Chief judicial / military officer
  [63:3:5.3] Mahabaladhikrita | Commander-in-chief
  [63:3:5.4] Dandapashika | District police officer
  [63:3:5.5] Akshapataladhikrita | Keeper of royal records
  [63:3:5.6] Pustapala | Keeper of land-transfer records
  [63:3:5.7] Dutaka | Spy / messenger
[63:3:6] # Units
[63:3:7] TABLE head: Unit | Level | Head
  [63:3:7.0] Bhukti (desha) | Province | Uparika
  [63:3:7.1] Vishaya | District | Vishayapati
  [63:3:7.2] Vithi (eastern India) | Group of villages | Ayuktaka
  [63:3:7.3] Grama | Village | Gramika / Gramadhyaksha with mahattaras
[63:3:8] - **Village headman (CDS 2012):** he became **more important** in Gupta times, and **no land transaction could be effected without his consent** – so "lost importance" is wrong; "Kumaramatyas were the most important officers appointed by the king in home provinces" is right → **only I**.
[63:3:9] - **Mahattara and Pattakila:** designations of the **village headman** (UPSC 2014) – not military officers, Vedic ritualists or guild chiefs.
[63:3:10] # Town board and courts
[63:3:11] - **Adhikarana (Kotivarsha, Damodarpur plates):** **Nagarashreshthi** (chair, chief banker), **Sarthavaha** (chief caravan trader), **Prathama kulika** (chief artisan), **Prathama kayastha** (chief scribe); a **district council** helped the district officer.
[63:3:12] - **Law:** civil and criminal law first clearly demarcated – **property disputes = civil**, theft and adultery = criminal; **Narada smriti** (survives complete, civil law), Brihaspati and Katyayana (fragments).
[63:3:13] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.64-67; NextGen Indian History APPSC 03 p.7, p.14-15; app notes 1:63 (sub 0-4)

## SUB 1 | Trade Decline and Urban Decay
[64:1:0] # Causes of trade decline
[64:1:1] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [64:1:1.0] Fall of the West Roman Empire (476 CE) | The standard cause of the decline of North Indian trade, 7th–10th c. (APPSC G2 2017) – not the collapse of China or SE Asia or loss of artisan skills
  [64:1:1.1] Eastern Roman (Byzantine) trade | Silk and spices till c. 550 CE; fell when the Romans learnt to rear silkworms
  [64:1:1.2] Hunas | Blocked the north-western route; loss of western India cut the inflow of gold and silver
  [64:1:1.3] Sassanid decline | After the rise of Islam – hurt overland trade
[64:1:2] - **New direction:** as the Roman market was lost, Indian merchants relied more on **South-East Asian trade** (UPSC 1999) – it did not revive the northern economy.
[64:1:3] - **Silk weavers:** a guild left Lata (Gujarat) for **Mandsaur** (mid-5th c.) and took up other professions – falling silk demand.
[64:1:4] # Urban decay
[64:1:5] - **Gradual decline of towns:** a feature of the **Gupta period** (State PSC; BPSC 1995) – not the Pratihara, Rashtrakuta or Satavahana eras; **Pataliputra** had become a village when Hiuen-Tsang came.
[64:1:6] - **Post-Gupta decline of trade (AP recruitment):** **TRUE** – towns such as **Sanghol, Hastinapur, Mathura, Shravasti, Kaushambi** declined, and there is a **paucity of coins**; **FALSE** – that it "became more pronounced only by the middle of the 9th century" (it was marked from the 6th–7th c.).
[64:1:7] - **R.S. Sharma's view:** shrinking towns, self-reliant villages, few gold coins – the basis of the **urban decay and feudalism** thesis; critics note coins still appear in texts and inscriptions.
[64:1:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.68, p.82; NextGen Indian History APPSC 03 p.6-7; app notes 1:64 (sub 8)
## SUB 2 | Varna, Jati, Women and Post-Gupta Society
[64:2:0] # Caste
[64:2:1] - **Proliferation of jatis:** castes multiplied into sub-castes by absorbing **foreigners** (Hunas → Rajput clans) and **tribes** (through land grants).
[64:2:2] - **Post-Gupta caste (AP recruitment):** the **Abhira** tribe split into Abhira Brahmanas, Kshatriyas and Shudras; **peasants** were placed in the **Shudra** varna; **Kayasthas** worked as record-keepers and scribes – all true; but Brahmanas **were** divided into many sub-castes, so (b) is false → **a, c, d**.
[64:2:3] - **Kayastha = scribes** (APPSC G2 2012): a new caste of scribes and accountants from the land-grant bureaucracy; **prathama kayastha** sat on town boards.
[64:2:4] - **Jajmani system:** hereditary service castes serving landowning patrons (**jajmans**) for a share of produce – became prevalent in the **post-Gupta** period (APPSC G1 2010).
[64:2:5] - **Medhatithi:** the post-Gupta commentator on Manu who declared that **Shudras were not slaves by nature** (MPPSC 2010) – not Vijnaneshvara, Narada or Jimutavahana.
[64:2:6] - **Shudras and untouchables:** Shudras' position improved (seen as agriculturists by the 7th c.; could hear the epics), but **untouchability** grew – **chandalas** lived outside villages; **Narada Smriti** lists **15 types of slaves**.
[64:2:7] # Women
[64:2:8] - **Marriage:** pre-puberty marriage advised; **svayamvara** gave way to marriages arranged by parents; widows to stay celibate.
[64:2:9] - **Property:** no general property rights, but control over **stridhana** (marriage gifts); Katyayana let women sell their stridhana.
[64:2:10] - **Earliest sati record:** **Eran, 510 CE** – wife of **Goparaja**, a general/friend of Bhanugupta; sati was still rare.
[64:2:11] - **Land:** Amarasimha's **Amarakosha** lists **12 types of land** (urvara = fertile, usara = barren, maru = desert).
[64:2:12] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.69-70; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.10 (Kayasthas); NextGen Indian History APPSC 03 p.8; app notes 1:64 (sub 1, 2, 3)
[64:2:13] - **Equality with men:** women enjoyed it in **no** age (Mauryan, Gupta or Chola) – in the Gupta age their status fell (early marriage, no property rights).
## SUB 3 | Guilds and the Dashpur (Mandsaur) Inscription
[64:3:0] # Guilds (shrenis)
[64:3:1] - **Autonomous bodies** of artisans and merchants with their own rules, seals and **judicial powers**; some maintained their **own armed bodies**; they took deposits and lent money (**banks**).
[64:3:2] - **NOT a feature (APPSC G1 2023):** "**non-autonomous bodies**" – the guilds were autonomous.
[64:3:3] - **Head:** **Sreshthi / Setti**; caravan trader = **Sarthavaha**.
[64:3:4] # Dashpur (Mandsaur) inscription
[64:3:5] - **Silk weavers' guild:** the **Dashpur inscription** of Kumaragupta I and his governor **Bandhuvarman** records the guild of **silk weavers** (from Lata) who built a **Sun temple** (436–37 CE) and repaired it (473 CE) (MPPSC 2019) – not the Prayag, Eran or Hathigumpha records.
[64:3:6] - **"First advertisement":** its praise of silk cloth makes it the **oldest example of advertisement** in India / "the world's first advertisement" (MPPSC 2021).
[64:3:7] - **Composer:** **Vatsabhatti** – NOT **Bhavabhuti** (MPPSC 2024); it calls Dashpur **Pashchimapur**.
[64:3:8] TABLE head: Record | Guild fact
  [64:3:8.0] Dashpur / Mandsaur (Kumaragupta I) | Silk weavers; Sun temple
  [64:3:8.1] Indore copper plate (Skandagupta) | Guild of oil-men – lamp in a Sun temple
  [64:3:8.2] Damodarpur copper plates (Kumaragupta I) | Guilds as banks; town board
[64:3:9] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.66-67; Rocket Indian History 01 p.10, p.85, p.100; app notes 1:64 (sub 5)
## SUB 4 | Fa-hien's Account
[64:4:0] - **Mission:** to **visit Buddhist institutions and collect copies of Buddhist manuscripts** (CDS 2009) – not to study administration, women or peasants; he never named the king.
[64:4:1] - **Hospital:** a **free hospital at Pataliputra**, endowed by citizens – poor patients got doctors, food and medicine (CGPSC 2019, 2020); **Tamralipti** was only his port of departure.
[64:4:2] - **Dates and route:** **c. 399–412 CE** (Chandragupta II); came overland via Central Asia and Gandhara, returned by sea via Tamralipti and **Sri Lanka**; account = **Fo-kwo-ki** ("Record of Buddhist Kingdoms").
[64:4:3] - **What he saw:** mild administration, no spy system, safe roads, capital punishment rare; **20 viharas at Mathura**; decay at Kapilavastu, Gaya, Kushinagar; **chandalas** struck a piece of wood to announce their approach; **cowries** for daily exchange.

=== NOTES 1:65 | Gupta Religion – Bhagavatism, Vaishnavism, Saivism; Beginnings of Temple Architecture (deogarh, Bhitargaon)
## SUB 0 | Bhagavatism and Vaishnavism
[65:0:0] # Origin
[65:0:1] - **First worshippers of Vasudeva-Krishna:** the **Bhagavatas** (Vasudevakas) (UPPCS 1997) – not Vedic Aryans, Tamils or Abhiras; Vasudeva worship is known from **Panini's** age.
[65:0:2] - **Originator:** **Krishna (Vasudeva)** of the **Vrishni (Satvata)** clan, son of Devaki, pupil of Ghora Angirasa (Chhandogya Upanishad) (RAS 1993) – not Janaka, Yajnavalkya or Surdas.
[65:0:3] - **Earliest epigraphic evidence:** the **Garuda pillar at Besnagar** (Vidisha, c. 2nd c. BCE), set up to Vasudeva by **Heliodorus**, envoy of the Indo-Greek **Antialcidas** at the court of the Sunga **Bhagabhadra** (UKPSC 2012 ×2) – the Prayag prashasti, Nasik and Ayodhya records are later.
[65:0:4] - **Doctrine:** **bhakti**, **ahimsa**, the **avatara** theory – **ten incarnations**; open to women, Vaishyas, Shudras and foreigners.
[65:0:5] # Gupta patronage
[65:0:6] - **Greatest contribution to the Bhagavata sect:** the **Guptas** (State PSC) – virtually the state religion; rulers styled **Parama Bhagavata** (first: **Samudragupta**); dynastic emblem **Garuda**.
[65:0:7] - **Udayagiri (MP):** colossal **Varaha** panel carved under **Chandragupta II**.
[65:0:8] # Avatars and attributes
[65:0:9] - **Varaha (boar):** the incarnation that **saved the Earth from the sea**, lifting her on his tusks after killing **Hiranyaksha** (UP UDA/LDA 2010) – not Matsya, Kurma or Narasimha.
[65:0:10] - **Dashavatara:** Matsya, Kurma, Varaha, Narasimha, Vamana, Parashurama, Rama, Krishna, Buddha, Kalki.
[65:0:11] - **Kaustubha (Kausthubham):** the gem on **Vishnu's chest**, from the churning of the ocean (APPSC Endowment 2022).
[65:0:12] - **Vaishnava symbols:** chakra, shankha, Garuda; **AP angle:** Salankayanas were Vaishnavas (title Parama Mahesvara); Pedavegi inscription of Nandivarman II – first AP record of Vishnu temples.
[65:0:13] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.71-72; NextGen Indian History APPSC 03 p.9; app notes 1:65 (sub 1)
[65:0:14] - **Avatar by yuga:** Satya – Matsya, Kurma, Varaha, Narasimha; Treta – Vamana, Parashurama, Rama; **Dvapara – Krishna** (and Balarama); Kali – Kalki.

[65:1:0] # Shaiva sects

  [65:1:1.0] Pashupata | Oldest Shaiva sect; founder Lakulisha
  [65:1:1.1] Kalamukha | Followers of Kalanana (disciple of Lakulisha); extremist; Simha Parishat in Andhra
  [65:1:1.2] Kapalika | Skull-bearing Vamacharas; Bhairava worship; Srisailam a Kapalika kshetra; Bhairavakonda caves
  [65:1:1.3] Mattamayura | A Shaiva sect known from Chedi (Kalachuri) inscriptions (IAS 1996)
  [65:1:1.4] Nathpanth / Lingayats | Matsyendranath, Gorakhnath / Basava
[65:1:2] - **Kapalikas emerged from Misra Saivam** (APPSC G2) – NextGen's APPSC fact list; NextGen's AP history also calls them the Vamacharas of the Kalamukhas. Not Pashupata Saivam, Vaishnavam or Shuddha Saivam in the APPSC key.

[65:1:8] - **Mother goddesses:** Durga, Amba, Kali, Chandi; **Tantrism** spread from the **5th century**, open to women and Shudras.

[65:1:11] - **18 Mahapuranas**; took their final shape in the **Gupta period**; five topics (**pancha-lakshana**): creation, re-creation, genealogies, manvantaras, dynastic history.

[65:2:0] - **Earliest free-standing Hindu temples** survive from the **Gupta** age: square **garbhagriha**, pillared mandapa, raised **jagati**.

## SUB 0 | Ajanta Caves – Phases, Patrons and Paintings
[66:0:0] # Basics
[66:0:1] - **Ajanta** = **Buddhist** rock-cut caves in **Aurangabad (Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar) district, Maharashtra**, cut in a horseshoe-shaped gorge of the **Waghora river** (UPSC 2021) in the Sahyadri ranges – not Sanchi (Betwa side), Pandu-lena (Nashik) or Amaravati (Krishna).
[66:0:2] - **30 caves** (29 numbered + 15A): **5 chaityas** (9, 10, 19, 26, 29), the rest viharas.
[66:0:3] - Rediscovered in **1819** by British officer **John Smith** on a tiger hunt; **UNESCO World Heritage Site 1983** (with Ellora).
[66:0:4] - Ajanta is famous for **Buddhist cave temples** (Uttarakhand 2006) – Elephanta is Shaiva, Khajuraho Hindu-Jain, Nalanda a monastery-university.
[66:0:5] # Two phases
[66:0:6] TABLE head: Phase | Patron | Caves | Note
  [66:0:6.0] Early (2nd–1st c. BCE) | Satavahanas | 9, 10 (also 8, 12, 13, 15A) | Earliest paintings of historical India – Amaravati style
  [66:0:6.1] Later (5th c. CE) | Vakatakas – King Harishena, minister Varahadeva | 1, 2, 16, 17, 19, 26 … | Most paintings and sculpture
[66:0:7] - Ajanta's art was **mainly patronised by the Vakatakas** (Jharkhand 2013) – not Chalukyas, Pallavas or Gangas; the Vakataka phase is contemporary with the **Gupta** age, so "Ajanta built in the Gupta period" (SSC 2014) is the accepted answer.
[66:0:8] - **Earliest paintings** (APPSC AP History PYQ) = **Satavahana** period, Caves 9 and 10 (prehistoric rock art such as Bhimbetka aside).

[66:0:14] - Themes: **Jataka tales** (Buddha's previous births) and the **life of Gautama Buddha** (SSC 2019; APSC 2017; MPPSC 2012) – not the epics, Panchatantra, Vikramaditya or Mahavira; also court and secular life.

[67:1:2] - Left **Changan** (Tang capital) in **629**; emperor **Taizong** had banned foreign travel, so he went **on his own** by the Silk Route – **no one sent him as an emissary** (UPPCS 2007); back in China in **645**, about **16 years** later.
[67:1:3] - At Harsha's court **631**; studied at **Nalanda** under **Silabhadra** (Yogashastra) and taught there.
[67:1:4] - His record = **Si-Yu-Ki** ("Records of the Western World") (UPPCS 2016) – the main reason he is remembered (RAS 1992). Not Fa-Hien, Al-Biruni or Megasthenes.
[67:1:5] - Praised **Harsha**, **Pulakesin II** and Pallava **Narasimhavarman I**.

## SUB 2 | Harsha – Dynasty, Family, Rivals and Capital
[67:2:0] # Pushyabhuti (Vardhana) dynasty
[67:2:1] - Founded at **Thanesar (Sthanvishvara)** by **Pushyabhuti** per Bana's Harshacharita (State PSC); NextGen names **Naravardhana** as the first historical king.
[67:2:2] - **Prabhakaravardhana** (title Maharajadhiraja), Harsha's father; mother **Yashomati** became sati in 605.
[67:2:3] - Order: **Adityavardhana → Prabhakaravardhana → Rajyavardhana → Harshavardhana**.
[67:2:4] - **Rajyavardhana**, the **elder** brother, was killed by **Shashanka**; Harsha then took the throne (SSC 2019).

=== NOTES 1:68 | Tripartite Struggle for Kannauj – Palas, Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas
## SUB 0 | Kannauj – the Prize and the Three Contenders
[68:0:0] # Why Kannauj?
[68:0:1] - **Old names:** **Mahodaya**, **Mahodaya Shri** ("Nagar Mahodaya Shri" in Mihira Bhoja's time), Kanyakubja (UP Lower 2015; UPPSC 2012) – not Mahoba, Kampilya, Mathura or Allahabad.
[68:0:2] - **Post-Gupta business centre:** Harsha made Kannauj his capital; the chief commercial and political centre shifted from Ujjain to **Kannauj** on the Ganga (RAS 1993).
[68:0:3] - **Symbol of imperial sovereignty:** its holder claimed to be **Samrat of North India** (like Magadha in the Gupta period).
[68:0:4] - **Economic pull:** **Gangetic-Doab** farm wealth; **Arabian Sea trade via Gujarat**, bypassing the Arab-held **Indus delta**.
[68:0:5] - **Period:** **8th–9th century CE** (c. 780 onwards). The prize was **Kannauj, not Ujjain**.
[68:0:6] - **Objective of the Palas and Pratiharas:** to **establish supremacy over Kannauj** – not merely to control the Ganga trade route, make it a feudatory, or keep the Rashtrakutas out (Sachivalayam).
[68:0:7] # The three contenders
[68:0:8] TABLE head: Dynasty | Base | Rulers in the struggle
  [68:0:8.0] Gurjara-Pratiharas | Rajasthan-Gujarat / Avanti (Malwa), later Kannauj | Vatsaraja, Nagabhata II, Mihira Bhoja, Mahendrapala I
  [68:0:8.1] Palas | Bengal and Bihar | Dharmapala, Devapala, Mahipala I
  [68:0:8.2] Rashtrakutas | Deccan, capital Manyakheta (Malkhed) | Dhruva, Govinda III, Amoghavarsha, Indra III
[68:0:9] - **Rashtrakutas fought whom for Kannauj?** The **Pratiharas and the Palas** (G2 2003; APPSC PYQ).
[68:0:10] - **Not part of it:** **Cholas** (UPPSC 2015; UP Lower 2002: answer "Pala, Gurjara, Rashtrakuta"), Pallavas, Kalyani Chalukyas, Chauhans, Gahadavalas.
[68:0:11] - **Statements (APPSC):** Rashtrakuta **Dhruva** jumped into the fight ✓; "tripartite" = struggle among three powers ✓; the Gurjara-Pratiharas finally kept Kannauj ✓ → **all three correct**.
[68:0:12] - **Chronology (UPSC 2020):** Pallava Mahendravarman I → Pala Gopala → Pratihara Bhoja → Chola Parantaka I.
[68:0:13] > Exam angle: "Mahodaya" = **Kannauj**; "not a party" = **Cholas**.

## SUB 1 | Outcome and the Rajput Successors
[68:1:0] # Outcome
[68:1:1] - Rashtrakuta raids gave **loot and prestige**, no lasting land.
[68:1:2] - The **Pratiharas** finally held Kannauj (Bhoja, Mahendrapala I), till Mahmud of Ghazni (1018).
[68:1:3] - All three were exhausted; power shifted to **regional kingdoms** – rise of Rajput states such as the **Chandelas** and Chauhans.

[68:2:1] TABLE head: Phase | Events
  [68:2:1.0] I (c. 780–800) | Vatsaraja takes Kannauj, defeats Dharmapala; Dhruva defeats both, takes loot and returns south
  [68:2:1.1] II (early 9th c.) | Dharmapala removes Pratihara nominee Indrayudha, installs his puppet Chakrayudha; holds a darbar as overlord of the north
  [68:2:1.2] III | Nagabhata II ousts Chakrayudha and defeats Dharmapala at Monghyr (Munger); Govinda III defeats both, then retreats
