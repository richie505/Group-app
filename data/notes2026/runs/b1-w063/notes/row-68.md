=== Kannauj – the Prize and the Three Contenders
# Why Kannauj?
- **Old names:** **Mahodaya**, **Mahodaya Shri** ("Nagar Mahodaya Shri" in Mihira Bhoja's time), Kanyakubja (UP Lower 2015; UPPSC 2012) – not Mahoba, Kampilya, Mathura or Allahabad.
- **Post-Gupta business centre:** Harsha made Kannauj his capital; the chief commercial and political centre shifted from Ujjain to **Kannauj** on the Ganga (RAS 1993).
- **Symbol of imperial sovereignty:** its holder claimed to be **Samrat of North India** (like Magadha in the Gupta period).
- **Economic pull:** **Gangetic-Doab** farm wealth; **Arabian Sea trade via Gujarat**, bypassing the Arab-held **Indus delta**.
- **Period:** **8th–9th century CE** (c. 780 onwards). The prize was **Kannauj, not Ujjain**.
- **Objective of the Palas and Pratiharas:** to **establish supremacy over Kannauj** – not merely to control the Ganga trade route, make it a feudatory, or keep the Rashtrakutas out (Sachivalayam).
# The three contenders
| Dynasty | Base | Rulers in the struggle |
|---|---|---|
| **Gurjara-Pratiharas** | Rajasthan-Gujarat / Avanti (Malwa), later Kannauj | Vatsaraja, Nagabhata II, Mihira Bhoja, Mahendrapala I |
| **Palas** | Bengal and Bihar | Dharmapala, Devapala, Mahipala I |
| **Rashtrakutas** | Deccan, capital **Manyakheta (Malkhed)** | Dhruva, Govinda III, Amoghavarsha, Indra III |
- **Rashtrakutas fought whom for Kannauj?** The **Pratiharas and the Palas** (G2 2003; APPSC PYQ).
- **Not part of it:** **Cholas** (UPPSC 2015; UP Lower 2002: answer "Pala, Gurjara, Rashtrakuta"), Pallavas, Kalyani Chalukyas, Chauhans, Gahadavalas.
- **Statements (APPSC):** Rashtrakuta **Dhruva** jumped into the fight ✓; "tripartite" = struggle among three powers ✓; the Gurjara-Pratiharas finally kept Kannauj ✓ → **all three correct**.
- **Chronology (UPSC 2020):** Pallava Mahendravarman I → Pala Gopala → Pratihara Bhoja → Chola Parantaka I.
> Exam angle: "Mahodaya" = **Kannauj**; "not a party" = **Cholas**.

=== Phases and Battles
# The four phases
| Phase | Events |
|---|---|
| I (c. 780–800) | **Vatsaraja** takes Kannauj, defeats Dharmapala; **Dhruva** defeats both, takes loot and returns south |
| II (early 9th c.) | **Dharmapala** removes Pratihara nominee **Indrayudha**, installs his puppet **Chakrayudha**; holds a darbar as overlord of the north |
| III | **Nagabhata II** ousts Chakrayudha and defeats Dharmapala at **Monghyr (Munger)**; **Govinda III** defeats both, then retreats |
| IV (late 9th c.) | **Mihira Bhoja**, Mahendrapala I hold Kannauj – empire from the Himalayas to the Narmada |
| c. 916 | **Indra III** sacks Kannauj, defeating Pratihara **Mahipala I** |
- **Nagabhata II was a Pratihara**, not a Pala; Indrayudha = removed, Chakrayudha = Dharmapala's puppet.
# Rashtrakutas in the north
| Rashtrakuta ruler | Defeated |
|---|---|
| **Dhruva** (780–793) | **Vatsaraja** and Dharmapala (also Vengi's **Vishnuvardhana IV**) |
| **Govinda III** (Dhruva's successor) | **Nagabhata II** and Dharmapala – Himalayan campaign |
| **Indra III** | **Mahipala I** (c. 916) |
- **Govinda III beat Nagabhata II** (Uttarakhand 2021) – not Dhruva, Indra III or Krishna III.

=== Outcome and the Rajput Successors
# Outcome
- Rashtrakuta raids gave **loot and prestige**, no lasting land.
- The **Pratiharas** finally held Kannauj (Bhoja, Mahendrapala I), till Mahmud of Ghazni (1018).
- All three were exhausted; power shifted to **regional kingdoms** – rise of Rajput states such as the **Chandelas** and Chauhans.
