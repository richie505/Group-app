## SUB 0 | Kannauj – the Prize and the Three Contenders
[0:0] # Why Kannauj?
[0:1] - **Old names:** **Mahodaya**, **Mahodaya Shri** ("Nagar Mahodaya Shri" in Mihira Bhoja's time), Kanyakubja (UP Lower 2015; UPPSC 2012) – not Mahoba, Kampilya, Mathura or Allahabad.
[0:2] - **Post-Gupta business centre:** Harsha made Kannauj his capital; the chief commercial and political centre shifted from Ujjain to **Kannauj** on the Ganga (RAS 1993).
[0:3] - **Symbol of imperial sovereignty:** its holder claimed to be **Samrat of North India** (like Magadha in the Gupta period).
[0:4] - **Economic pull:** **Gangetic-Doab** farm wealth; **Arabian Sea trade via Gujarat**, bypassing the Arab-held **Indus delta**.
[0:5] - **Period:** **8th–9th century CE** (c. 780 onwards). The prize was **Kannauj, not Ujjain**.
[0:6] - **Objective of the Palas and Pratiharas:** to **establish supremacy over Kannauj** – not merely to control the Ganga trade route, make it a feudatory, or keep the Rashtrakutas out (Sachivalayam).
[0:7] # The three contenders
[0:8] TABLE head: Dynasty | Base | Rulers in the struggle
  [0:8.0] Gurjara-Pratiharas | Rajasthan-Gujarat / Avanti (Malwa), later Kannauj | Vatsaraja, Nagabhata II, Mihira Bhoja, Mahendrapala I
  [0:8.1] Palas | Bengal and Bihar | Dharmapala, Devapala, Mahipala I
  [0:8.2] Rashtrakutas | Deccan, capital Manyakheta (Malkhed) | Dhruva, Govinda III, Amoghavarsha, Indra III
[0:9] - **Rashtrakutas fought whom for Kannauj?** The **Pratiharas and the Palas** (G2 2003; APPSC PYQ).
[0:10] - **Not part of it:** **Cholas** (UPPSC 2015; UP Lower 2002: answer "Pala, Gurjara, Rashtrakuta"), Pallavas, Kalyani Chalukyas, Chauhans, Gahadavalas.
[0:11] - **Statements (APPSC):** Rashtrakuta **Dhruva** jumped into the fight ✓; "tripartite" = struggle among three powers ✓; the Gurjara-Pratiharas finally kept Kannauj ✓ → **all three correct**.
[0:12] - **Chronology (UPSC 2020):** Pallava Mahendravarman I → Pala Gopala → Pratihara Bhoja → Chola Parantaka I.
[0:13] > Exam angle: "Mahodaya" = **Kannauj**; "not a party" = **Cholas**.

## SUB 1 | Phases and Battles
[1:0] # The four phases
[1:1] TABLE head: Phase | Events
  [1:1.0] I (c. 780–800) | Vatsaraja takes Kannauj, defeats Dharmapala; Dhruva defeats both, takes loot and returns south
  [1:1.1] II (early 9th c.) | Dharmapala removes Pratihara nominee Indrayudha, installs his puppet Chakrayudha; holds a darbar as overlord of the north
  [1:1.2] III | Nagabhata II ousts Chakrayudha and defeats Dharmapala at Monghyr (Munger); Govinda III defeats both, then retreats
  [1:1.3] IV (late 9th c.) | Mihira Bhoja, Mahendrapala I hold Kannauj – empire from the Himalayas to the Narmada
  [1:1.4] c. 916 | Indra III sacks Kannauj, defeating Pratihara Mahipala I
[1:2] - **Nagabhata II was a Pratihara**, not a Pala; Indrayudha = removed, Chakrayudha = Dharmapala's puppet.
[1:3] # Rashtrakutas in the north
[1:4] TABLE head: Rashtrakuta ruler | Defeated
  [1:4.0] Dhruva (780–793) | Vatsaraja and Dharmapala (also Vengi's Vishnuvardhana IV)
  [1:4.1] Govinda III (Dhruva's successor) | Nagabhata II and Dharmapala – Himalayan campaign
  [1:4.2] Indra III | Mahipala I (c. 916)
[1:5] - **Govinda III beat Nagabhata II** (Uttarakhand 2021) – not Dhruva, Indra III or Krishna III.

## SUB 2 | Outcome and the Rajput Successors
[2:0] # Outcome
[2:1] - Rashtrakuta raids gave **loot and prestige**, no lasting land.
[2:2] - The **Pratiharas** finally held Kannauj (Bhoja, Mahendrapala I), till Mahmud of Ghazni (1018).
[2:3] - All three were exhausted; power shifted to **regional kingdoms** – rise of Rajput states such as the **Chandelas** and Chauhans.
