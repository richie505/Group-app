## SUB 0 | Renati Cholas – the First Telugu Inscriptions
[0:0] - **First Telugu inscription:** **Kalamalla** (Kadapa), c. **575 CE**, of **Dhanunjaya Varma** (Erikal Mutturaju) (G2 2012; APPSC PYQ).
[0:1]   - **⚠ Trap:** Chejarla = Kubja Vishnuvardhana (over half Telugu words); Addanki = Panduranga's first verse inscription; Vipparla = Jayasimha I's first full Telugu record of the Vengi Chalukyas.
[0:2] - **Issuer of the first Telugu inscription:** the **Renati Cholas** – not the Satavahanas, Vishnukundins or Vengi Chalukyas (APPSC PYQ).
[0:3] - **Credit for establishing Telugu in Andhradesa:** **Renati Cholas** (G2 PYQ ×2) – they made Telugu the **royal and legislative language**, replacing **Prakrit and Sanskrit**, about **five centuries before Nannaya**.
[0:4]   - Dignaga and Prithvimula are distractors; the **Eastern Chalukyas** later took up the practice.
[0:5] TABLE head: Inscription | Ruler | Point
  [0:5.0] Kalamalla | Dhanunjaya Varma | c. 575 CE; first Telugu inscription; prose, incomplete
  [0:5.1] Erragudipadu | Erikal Mutturaju / Punyakumara | Late 6th–7th c.; first fully available Telugu record (NextGen)
  [0:5.2] Potladurthi | Punyakumara | Telugu
  [0:5.3] Malepadu plates | Punyakumara | Sanskrit prose; Madras Museum
[0:6] - **33** Renati Telugu inscriptions, mostly gift records, around **Jammalamadugu and Proddatur**.
[0:7] - **Before 575 CE** Telugu appears only in **names**; **575–848 CE** in **full prose sentences**.
[0:8] - **⚠ Trap:** "Erragudipadu, 575 CE, first Telugu inscription" merges two records – the 575 CE first record is **Kalamalla**.
[0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Andhra Cholas p.6-7; NextGen Eastern Chalukyas p.30, p.32; NextGen AP History Previous Papers p.28; Rocket AP History sheet #1; app notes 1:81 (sub 2)
## SUB 1 | Renati Cholas – Territory and Rulers
[1:0] - **Descent claimed:** **Suryavamsa Kshatriyas**, descendants of **Karikala Chola**.
[1:1] - **Renadu "Saptasahasra"** (**7,000** villages) – where **Kadapa, Kurnool and Chittoor** meet, upper **Pennar** basin.
[1:2] - **Capital:** **Peddachapalli** (Kamalapuram taluk, Kadapa); Dhanunjaya Varma ruled from **Chimbapuram (Chimamburu)**.
[1:3] - **Overlords:** first the **Pallavas**, later the **Badami Chalukyas**; successors **Banas and Vaidumbas** (Vaidumbas: Telugu **hero-stone** inscriptions).
[1:4] TABLE head: Ruler | Detail
  [1:4.0] Nandivarma | Progenitor
  [1:4.1] Dhanunjaya Varma | Founder; Kalamalla inscription
  [1:4.2] Mahendra Vikramavarman | Title Muditha Silakshara Navarama
  [1:4.3] Punyakumara (625–650) | Greatest; wife Vasantapori; titles Pormukharama, Purushashardula, Uttamottama, Prithvivallabha
  [1:4.4] Satyaditya | Title Chola Maharaja; decline after him
  [1:4.5] Elachola Maharaja | Last ruler
[1:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Andhra Cholas p.6-7; app notes 1:81 (sub 0, 1)
## SUB 2 | Nellore Chodas and Other Telugu Choda Families
[2:0] # Nellore (Vikrama Simhapuri) Chodas – the foremost clan
[2:1] TABLE head: Person | Role
  [2:1.0] (Tenungu) Bijjana | Progenitor / first important chief; vassal of Kalyani Someshvara I
  [2:1.1] Manumasiddhi I | First king at Nellore (Taruvariyur inscription)
  [2:1.2] Tikkana Choda (Tikkabhupati) | Son of Manumasiddhi I; put Rajendra Chola on the Chola throne → Chola Sthapanacharya; helped Ganapati kill Velanati Prithvisvara
  [2:1.3] Manumasiddhi II | Lost his throne to Vijayaganda Gopala and cousins; restored by Kakatiya Ganapatideva
  [2:1.4] Kavi-Brahma Tikkana | Minister and court poet of Manumasiddhi II; envoy to Ganapati
[2:2] - **First important chief:** **Chola Bijjana** (Sachivalayam PYQ) – not Tikka or Someshwara.
[2:3] - **Restoration:** Ganapati's general **Samantabhoja** won at **Palaiyuru** and reinstated Manumasiddhi II, who gave **Motupalli** to Ganapati.
[2:4] - **Decline:** under **Manumasiddhi II** the Nellore Chodas **started to decline**; he was killed by Jatavarman Sundara Pandya at **Muttukur (1263)**.
[2:5] > A-R angle (G2 Mains 2025): "Ganapatideva reinstated Manumasiddhi II" ✓; "Nellore Choda power at its zenith under Manumasiddhi II" ✗ → A true, R false.
[2:6] - **⚠ Trap:** Tikkana Choda (king) ≠ Kavi-Brahma Tikkana (poet).
