=== NOTES 1:79 | Eastern Chalukya Administration, Society and Religious Conditions
## SUB 0 | Administration – Structure and Offices
[79:0:0] - The kingdom followed the **Saptanga** idea of the state (seven limbs).
[79:0:1] - **Ashtadasa Tirthas** = **eighteen administrative offices**.
[79:0:2] - **Official language: Sanskrit**; Jainism and Buddhism were patronised alongside Hinduism.
[79:0:3] # Administrative Terms → Meaning
[79:0:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [79:0:4.0] Ashtadasa Tirthas | 18 offices
  [79:0:4.1] Tirthulu | Council of ministers; Samaharta = its chief member
  [79:0:4.2] Vishaya / Mandala | Province (state unit) under a Vishayapati / Mandaleswara
  [79:0:4.3] Kottam | Another territorial subdivision found in records
  [79:0:4.4] Grameyaka / Gramani / Urgavunda | Village head
  [79:0:4.5] Vanneyus | Toll collectors
  [79:0:4.6] Sthanadhipati | Officer managing temples and their land endowments
[79:0:5] - **Pradvivaka:** king's adviser on **judicial** matters (chief judge); disputes settled with local elders; written verdicts = **jayapatras**.
[79:0:6] - **Udranga:** in Chalukya grants read as a tax on **water**; in Gupta records usually the land tax.
[79:0:7] # Nadus → Region
[79:0:8] TABLE head: Nadu | Region
  [79:0:8.0] Venginadu | Parts of Krishna and Godavari
  [79:0:8.1] Kammanadu | Bapatla, Ongole, Narasaraopet
  [79:0:8.2] Pakanadu | Nellore
[79:0:9] - **Pithapuram and Vengi** were the Eastern Chalukya capitals (later Rajamahendravaram).
[79:0:10] Pakanadu = **Nellore**, not Krishna; Kammanadu = Bapatla–Ongole–Narasaraopet area.
## SUB 1 | Society
[79:1:0] - Social order: **Varna-based**, with **Brahmin privilege**.
[79:1:1] - **Agraharas**: **tax-free villages** given to **Brahmins**, who performed **Hiranyagarbha** and **Bahusuvarna** rituals.
[79:1:2] - Inscriptions record merchants such as a **Setti Shreshthi** (chief merchant) in Chalukya Bhima I's Kakumanu inscription.
[79:1:3] - **Panchanamvaru:** five artisan groups (blacksmith, goldsmith, brazier, carpenter, sculptor) appear as an organised body from the **Eastern Chalukya** period.
[79:1:4] # Tribal Assimilation
[79:1:5] - **Jayasimha II** brought the **Koya Kottalu** and others into the **Brahmanical fold**.
[79:1:6] - **Panduranga** (commander of Gunaga Vijayaditya III) **subdued the Boyas**.
## SUB 2 | Economy – Grants, Guilds and Overseas Trade
[79:2:0] - Land grants: tax-free **agraharas** to Brahmins; land endowments to temples from kings and vassals; village grants to Jains.
[79:2:1] - Tolls collected by **Vanneyus**.
[79:2:2] - **Penugonda** = **headquarters of the merchant guilds** in medieval Andhra.
[79:2:3] # Overseas Trade
[79:2:4] TABLE head: Fact | Ruler
  [79:2:4.0] Coins found in Arakan (Burma) | Saktivarma I
  [79:2:4.1] Trade with the eastern islands | Rajaraja Narendra
  [79:2:4.2] 72 merchants sent to China | Kulothunga I
## SUB 3 | Religious Conditions – Sects and Patronage
[79:3:0] # Vedic Hinduism and Shaivism
[79:3:1] - **Jayasimha I** performed the **Ashvamedha**; royal **prashastis** (praise-poems) boast of several Ashvamedhas.
[79:3:2] - **Shaivism was dominant**; sects: **Kapalika, Kalamukha, Pasupata**.
[79:3:3] - **Chalukya Bhima I** built the **Pancharamas**.
[79:3:4] # Vaishnavism
[79:3:5] - **Kubja Vishnuvardhana** – Vaishnava; built the **Kunti Madhava temple, Pithapuram**.
[79:3:6] - **Danarnava** – Vaishnava; **Mandavya Narayana temple, Samalkot**.
[79:3:7] - **Pancharatra and Vaikhanasa** = **Vaishnava** traditions (not Shaiva).
[79:3:8] # Jainism and Buddhism
[79:3:9] - **Potlacheruvu** = the leading **Jain** centre of the period (G2 2017 key) – **not Buddhist**.
[79:3:10] - **Vimaladitya** = the **only Vengi Chalukya king who became a Jain**.
[79:3:11] - Under Brahmanical pressure many Jains **migrated to Karnataka**, e.g. the family of **Basaveshwara**.
[79:3:12] - **Buddhism**: declining but surviving at **Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda**.
[79:3:13] Jain persecution at Potlacheruvu by **Kalamukhas and Virashaivas** came **later, in the Kakatiya age**. The Eastern Chalukyas supported **Jainism, Buddhism and Hinduism** – a sign of religious tolerance.
[79:3:14] # Eastern Chalukya Age vs Kakatiya Age – Religion
[79:3:15] TABLE head: Feature | Eastern Chalukya age | Kakatiya age
  [79:3:15.0] Royal policy | Tolerant – Hinduism, Jainism and Buddhism all patronised | Shaiva dominance
  [79:3:15.1] Jainism | Royal and queenly grants (Vijayawada, Ramatirtham, Mushikonda, Kaluchumbarru) | Persecution – Potlacheruvu attacked
  [79:3:15.2] Sects active | Kapalika, Kalamukha, Pasupata; Pancharatra, Vaikhanasa | Kalamukha and Virashaiva pressure on Jains
  [79:3:15.3] Buddhism | Declining; survives at Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda | Further decline
  [79:3:15.4] Official language | Sanskrit | Sanskrit

## SUB 5 | Education – Ghatikas and Agraharas
[79:5:0] - **Ghatikas** = **advanced Sanskrit schools** attached to **agraharas or temples**; settlements of Brahmin scholars studying scriptures, philosophy and Vedic learning.
[79:5:1] - Heads of Ghatikas = **Ghatika Samanyulu**, e.g. **Mandasharma** at **Asanapura Ghatika**, Kammanadu.
[79:5:2] - Subjects: **Vedas, Vedangas, Dharmashastras, Tarka, Vyakarana, Arthashastra, Dhanurveda, Ayurveda**.
[79:5:3] - **Jain pupils** began studies with **"Om Namah Siddham"**.
[79:5:4] **Hallisaka** (a dance) was **not** a Ghatika subject.
## SUB 6 | Temple Institutions, Music and Dance
[79:6:0] - Temples received land endowments from kings and vassals, managed by the **Sthanadhipati**.
[79:6:1] - **Musicians and dancers** were attached to temples and courts, e.g. **Chellavva**.
[79:6:2] - Popular dance: **Hallisaka (= Kolatam)**.
[79:6:3] - Sculptures of musical instruments at **Bhairavakona**.
[79:6:4] - **Panchadarla** (Dharapalem, **Anakapalli** district) – **Sri Uma Dharmalingeswara** temple; pillars carry **Eastern Chalukya and 15th-century** inscriptions; **ASI recorded 17 inscriptions in 1899** (South Indian Inscriptions Vol. VI); in news **Aug 2026**.
[79:6:5] - **Bhairavakona:** in the **Nallamala hills (Prakasam district)**; **eight rock-cut cave temples of Shiva**, cut in one rock face, c. **7th–8th century CE**; Shiva is worshipped here as **Kala Bhairava**.

## SUB 0 | The Vengi School – Style and Influences
[80:0:0] - The Eastern Chalukyas mainly followed the **Dravida** style – a mix of **Pallava (Kanchi)** and **Badami Chalukya** influences with **local Andhra** features = the **Vengi School**.
[80:0:1] - Also described as **Vesara (Chalukya) style** – **Dravidian vimana + Buddhist art** influence.
[80:0:2] - Materials: local **laterite and limestone**; most temples stand in the **Krishna-Godavari delta**.
[80:0:3] - Comparative note: the **Badami Chalukyas** developed **Vesara** in Karnataka (in AP: **Navabrahma temples – nine – at Alampur**, 7th–8th c. CE); the **Vengi line** carried a **Dravida-based** regional style into the **Chalukya-Chola** phase.

=== NOTES 1:81 | Andhra Cholas / Telugu Cholas – Renati Cholas and Their Inscriptions
## SUB 0 | Renati Cholas – Ancestry, Territory and Overlords
[81:0:0] - Claimed **Suryavamsa Kshatriya** descent from **Karikala Chola**; culture and administration in **Telugu**.
[81:0:1] - Territory: **Renadu "Saptasahasra"** (7,000 villages), also called Renati Cholanadi – where **Kadapa, Kurnool and Chittoor** meet, upper **Pennar** basin.
[81:0:2] - **Principal capital: Peddachapalli** (Kamalapuram taluk, **Kadapa**); Dhanunjaya Varma's capital: **Chimbapuram (Chimamburu)**.
[81:0:3] - Overlords: first feudatories of the **Pallavas**, later of the **Badami Chalukyas**; their Telugu inscription style was later copied by the **Eastern Chalukyas**. Renadu art shows **Pallava** influence.
[81:0:4] - Successors: **Banas and Vaidumbas**; Vaidumbas issued Telugu **hero-stone (veerakal)** inscriptions.

## SUB 0 | Kulottunga I and the Chalukya-chola Merger
[83:0:0] - **Kulottunga I (Rajendra Chalukya)** = **first Chalukya-Chola king** (APPSC 2011).
[83:0:1] - Married **Madhurantaki**, daughter of the Chola **Rajendra**.
[83:0:2] - Took the **Chola throne in 1070**; took **Vengi in 1075** after the death of **Vijayaditya VII**, the last Eastern Chalukya.
[83:0:3] - Sent **72 merchants to China**; built **Kulottunga Cholapuram** (Visakhapatnam).
[83:0:4] - Conquered **Kalinga by sea** → recorded in **Kalingattuparani** by **Jayangondar**.
[83:0:5] - After him, Andhra became a web of **nadus** under vassals who accepted **Velanadu**, later **Kakatiya**, overlordship.
[83:0:6] # Viceroys of Vengi and Andhra Feudatories
[83:0:7] - Chalukya-Chola **viceroys** of Vengi took the Eastern Chalukya titles **Sarvalokasraya, Sri Vishnuvardhana**.
[83:0:8] - Crowning place: **Jananathapura (Draksharama)** – APPSC 2025 key names **Rajaraja Chodaganga** (title **Sarvalokasraya Vishnuvardhana**) as the viceroy crowned there.
[83:0:9] - Last of the line: **Parantaka Chola**.
[83:0:10] - **Vikrama Chola** (son of Kulottunga I) served as viceroy of Vengi after **Rajaraja Chodaganga**; recalled in **1118** to be his father's **co-regent**.
[83:0:11] - **Velanati Gonka I** = **Samasta Senadhipati of Vengi** (fought off Kalyani Chalukyas and Kalingas).
[83:0:12] - **Mandaleswaras raised by Kulottunga I**: Velanati, Nellore, Telugu, Konidena and Pottapi Chodas; Giri Paschima Durjayas; Natavadis; **Chagis**; Kona Haihayas.

=== NOTES 1:84 | Kalyani (western) Chalukyas – Tailapa Ii, Vikramaditya Vi, Bilhana, Mitakshara
## SUB 0 | Dynasty at A Glance
[84:0:0] - Also called the **Western Chalukyas**; ruled c. **973–1189 CE** (543–753 CE is **Badami**, not Kalyani).
[84:0:1] - Capital: **Kalyani** = present **Basavakalyan** (Karnataka); made capital under Someshvara I (some books credit Tailapa II).
[84:0:2] - Branches in order: **Badami** (6th–8th c.) → **Vengi** (7th–11th c.) → **Kalyani** (late 10th–12th c.).
[84:0:3] - Significance: **checked Chola expansion northwards**; Mitakshara shaped Hindu inheritance law for about a millennium.
## SUB 1 | Rulers → Period and Achievement
[84:1:0] Order: **Tailapa II → Satyasraya → Someshvara I → Vikramaditya VI**.

  [84:1:1.3] Vikramaditya VI (1076–1126) | Greatest; ruled 50 years; title Tribhuvanamalla; started Chalukya-Vikrama era; controlled the Vengi feudatories; court of Bilhana and Vijnaneshwara
