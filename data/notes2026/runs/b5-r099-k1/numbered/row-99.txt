## SUB 0 | Carbon Monoxide and Other Pollutants – Health Effects
[0:0] # Carbon Monoxide (CO) – the most-asked pollutant
[0:1] - **Source:** **incomplete combustion** of carbon fuels – **vehicle exhaust** (main pollutant gas in exhaust), **cigarette smoke**, coal/wood burning. CO2 comes from **complete** combustion.
[0:2] - **Nature:** **colourless, odourless**, poisonous; "odourless poisonous gas in coal smoke" = CO.
[0:3] - **Effect:** binds **haemoglobin** → **carboxyhaemoglobin**; affinity **200–250 times** that of oxygen → cuts the blood's **oxygen-carrying capacity**; can affect the **bloodstream leading to death** → usually called the "**most dangerous**" air pollutant.
[0:4] - **Not** converted to CO2 in the body, does **not** destroy haemoglobin's structure, not mainly a digestive/liver/kidney poison (UPSC 2010, MPPSC 2012).
[0:5] - "Forms an **irreversible** complex with haemoglobin" (UPPCS 1996) – books answer **CO**; strictly the bond is reversible but very stable (CO is released slowly).
[0:6] - Flashcard: CO breathing is harmful because **it competes with oxygen in the blood**.
[0:7] TABLE head: Pollutant | Main source | Part / effect
  [0:7.0] Carbon monoxide | Incomplete combustion, vehicles, cigarettes | Blood stream – carboxyhaemoglobin, hypoxia, death
  [0:7.1] Asbestos dust | Asbestos mining, cement sheets | Lung – asbestosis
  [0:7.2] Lead | Leaded petrol (old), smelters, batteries, paints | Brain / nervous system; blood pressure, kidney
  [0:7.3] Mercury | Coal burning, industry | Stomach (UPPSC 2012 match key) / nervous system
  [0:7.4] SO2 | Coal/oil burning, power plants, smelting | Eye, throat and lung irritation, asthma; acid rain
  [0:7.5] NOx | High-temperature combustion – vehicles, power plants, lightning | Eye/airway irritation; smog, acid rain
  [0:7.6] H2S | Decaying vegetation and animal matter (chief), sewage, refineries | Rotten-egg smell; toxic
  [0:7.7] Fly ash | Burning coal in thermal power plants | Lung disease; captured by ESP
  [0:7.8] PM2.5 / PM10 | Combustion, road and construction dust, biomass | Heart/lung disease; PM2.5 enters blood, crosses placenta
  [0:7.9] Ground-level O3 | Secondary (photochemical) | Asthma, chest pain, crop damage
[0:8] - **UPPSC 2012 match:** Asbestos – Lung, Lead – Brain, Mercury – Stomach, CO – Blood stream → **3 1 2 4**.
[0:9] - **Lead** from **petrol** combustion (anti-knock **tetraethyl lead, TEL**) – the metal polluting city air with many vehicles; leaded petrol is **phased out in India (2000)**.
[0:10] - **Most injurious exhaust components:** **CO and lead** (old leaded-petrol era).
[0:11] - **Cigarette smoke:** **CO and benzene** (also nicotine, HCN, arsenic, formaldehyde).
[0:12] - **Inorganic gaseous pollutants:** HCN, H2S, CO, CO2, SO2, NOx (methane and phosgene are carbon compounds usually grouped as organic).
[0:13] - **Asthma** is caused / aggravated by **air pollution**; **rickets** is **not** caused by smog directly (smog cuts UV → less vitamin D; rickets is a deficiency disease).
[0:14] - **Bio-gas / gobar gas** = mainly **methane** (50–65%) + CO2 (35–50%), traces of H2, N2, H2S.
[0:15] - Gases: **ozone = O3**; **CFCs** deplete the stratospheric ozone layer.
[0:16] - Other links: **ammonia** (fertilisers + livestock, 77–80%) drives secondary particles; **ammonium sulphate** (from SO2 + NH3) ≈ one-third of India's PM2.5; **magnetite** particles (combustion, brake wear) found in brain tissue – possible Alzheimer's link; **cadmium** from metal smelting, PAHs from incomplete burning; PM2.5 crosses the placenta (AIIMS, 3 June 2026).
[0:17] - Burden: WHO ~**7 million** deaths a year; fossil-fuel air pollution ~**1.72 million** premature deaths in India (2022); NCR winter PM2.5 ~35 times the WHO limit; IIT Delhi: curbing power-plant SO2 could prevent ~1.24 lakh deaths a year.
[0:18] > Exam angle: ≈20 PYQs ask the same fact – CO + haemoglobin. Pick CO whenever the stem says "blood", "oxygen-carrying", "colourless gas from exhaust and cigarettes", "incomplete combustion" or "most dangerous".

## SUB 1 | Sources of Air Pollution – Natural, Vehicles, Industry
[1:0] TABLE head: Basis | Examples
  [1:0.0] Natural | Forest fires, volcanic eruptions (SOx, ash), dust storms, natural decay (methane, H2S), lightning (NOx), pollen, spores
  [1:0.1] Anthropogenic | Combustion of coal, petrol, diesel (basic source of air pollution), vehicles, industries, thermal plants, fireworks
  [1:0.2] Point / line / area / diffuse | Chimneys of industries / automobile exhaust along roads / industrial estates, mining / sprayed fertilisers and pesticides
[1:1] - **Main source in Indian cities: automobile (vehicular) emissions** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025). Main pollutants of metros (MPPSC 2018 key): **CO and SO2**.
[1:2] - **Composition of air:** N2 78%, O2 21%, CO2 0.04% – **N2, O2, helium are not pollutants**; CO, NO2, SO2, ground-level O3, lead dust, smoke are.
[1:3] - **CO2** is the usual "**not an air pollutant**" answer (greenhouse gas, quantitative pollutant; not in NAQI).
[1:4] - **Vehicles:** CO, NOx, unburnt hydrocarbons, PM, (old) lead. **Diesel engine:** much **CO at low temperature** (incomplete burning), much **NOx at high temperature**.
[1:5] - **Steel industry:** oxides of **sulphur, nitrogen, CO and CO2 – all four** (UPSC 2014).
[1:6] - **Largest SO2 source (US EPA, UPSC 2024): power plants using fossil fuels** (~73%), then other industry; not ships, locomotives or ore extraction.
[1:7] - **SO2 + fossil fuel:** SO2 is the air-pollutant gas released by burning fossil fuel (H2, N2, O2 are not).
[1:8] - **Delhi PM (IIT Kanpur study, 2016):** **road dust** (incl. construction dust) is the largest PM2.5/PM10 source annually (~38% PM2.5); in **winter** secondary particles, vehicles and biomass burning lead.
[1:9] - **Delhi winter peak:** A true, but "vehicle combustion increases in winter" is false – winter peaks come from **temperature inversion, calm winds, stubble smoke**.
[1:10] - **Stubble burning in Punjab** (and Haryana) contributes more to Delhi's air than Rajasthan or firewood use.
[1:11] - **Transport and technology:** more travel → more pollution; transport releases lead, NOx, CO (ozone forms from them – secondary); burning fuel harms health.
[1:12] - Blackened vessel bottom while cooking = **fuel not burning completely**.
[1:13] - **Sita Ashok** tree = prevention of dust pollution (UPPCS 2007).
[1:14] - **Eutrophication** = water pollution, not air (smog, acid rain, asbestosis are air-related).
[1:15] - **PM2.5 main source in India** (printed key): **vehicular emissions**.
[1:16] - **Silver oxide** is not a major primary air pollutant (SOx, CO, NOx are).
[1:17] - NCR: **transport** is the largest primary source, ~**23%** of the winter load (CAQM synthesis report, Jan 2026).
[1:18] - Fuels: **coal** most polluting; diesel gives more PM than petrol; CNG/LPG still release **CO2**; **LPG** burns without smoke; biogas mainly CO2 + traces of H2S; **hydrogen** gives only water (no CO, SO2, PM); wood and cow-dung give smoke, CO, PM.
[1:19] > Exam angle: "natural source" = forest fires/volcanoes; "urban source" = vehicles; "steel / crop residue releases which" = all of them.

## SUB 2 | Acid Rain – Causes, Chemistry, pH and Effects
[2:0] - **Definition:** precipitation (rain, snow, fog, mist) with **pH below 5.6**; normal rain ≈ 5.6 (carbonic acid from CO2). Term coined **1872** by **Robert Angus Smith** (Manchester coal burning).
[2:1] - **pH scale** = **acidity / basicity**; 0–14; **7 neutral**, below 7 acidic; each unit = **tenfold** change. **Human blood ≈ 7.4** (slightly basic).
[2:2] - **Causing gases: SO2 and NOx** (oxides of **sulphur and nitrogen**) – **not** CO, CO2, O3, CH4, CFCs, H2S, HCl or "oxides of hydrogen". Main single factor: **SO2**.
[2:3] - **Ozone** only speeds up the oxidation (photo-oxidant) – it is not a cause (UPSC 2022: **2 and 4 only**).
[2:4] - **Chemistry:** SO2 / NOx + water + O2 + photo-oxidants → **H2SO4 (~two-thirds)** + **HNO3 (~one-third)** → both **sulphuric and nitric acid** are components; acid falls from atmosphere to the ground.
[2:5] - Acid rain occurs due to the **reaction between clouds (water) and sulphur dioxide** (UPPCS 2001).
[2:6] TABLE head: Gas | Anthropogenic | Natural
  [2:6.0] SOx | Coal power plants, smelting of sulphide ores, H2SO4 plants, refineries, industries | Volcanoes, oceans, decay
  [2:6.1] NOx | Vehicles, power plants, biomass burning | Lightning, forest fires
[2:7] - **Deposition:** wet (rain, snow, fog) and **dry** – about **half** of atmospheric acidity returns dry.
[2:8] - **Effects:** acidifies lakes (fish die, aluminium released), damages forests and soils (leaches Ca, Mg), corrodes limestone/marble (**Taj Mahal "marble cancer"** – SO2, Mathura refinery), harms health (**respiratory ailments**).
[2:9] - **Taj Trapezium Zone** ~10,400 sq km (Agra, Fatehpur Sikri, Agra Fort; not Sanchi); **M.C. Mehta v. UoI (1996)** barred coal/coke.
[2:10] - **Transboundary:** Scandinavia receives acid rain from UK/central Europe; **India = largest SO2 emitter (2022)**.
[2:11] - **Control:** low-sulphur fuel, **FGD scrubbers** (trap SO2), low-NOx burners, catalytic converters; **liming** = remedial (not preventive).
[2:12] TABLE head: Instrument | Year | Focus
  [2:12.0] LRTAP (UNECE) | 1979 | First binding treaty on transboundary air pollution
  [2:12.1] Helsinki Protocol | 1985 | 30% sulphur cut
  [2:12.2] Sofia Protocol | 1988 | Freeze NOx
  [2:12.3] Gothenburg Protocol | 1999 | SO2, NOx, NH3, VOCs
[2:13] > Exam angle: ~17 PYQs; the answer is almost always "SO2 and NOx" / "pH below 5.6" / "sulphuric and nitric acid".

## SUB 4 | Smog, Primary and Secondary Pollutants
[4:0] TABLE head: Type | Formed from | Conditions
  [4:0.0] Classical / London smog (sulphurous, 1952) | Smoke + fog + SO2 | Cold, humid; grey/black
  [4:0.1] Photochemical / Los Angeles / summer smog | NO2/NOx + hydrocarbons (VOCs) + sunlight → ozone, PAN (oxidising agents) | Warm, sunny, traffic; brownish – "brown air"
[4:1] - **Smog** = smoke + fog; essentially caused by **oxides of nitrogen and sulphur**; photochemical smog **always contains ozone**; ozone is **produced** during its formation (hydrocarbons, NOx are inputs, not products).
[4:2] - TSPSC 2023 pairs: classical smog = smoke, fog, SO2 ✔; photochemical smog = high concentration of **oxidising agents** ✔ → **both**.
[4:3] - **Primary** pollutants (emitted directly): CO, CO2, SO2, NOx, fly ash, lead. **Secondary** (formed in air): **O3, PAN, smog**, ammonium sulphate → "**not a primary pollutant**" = **ozone**; "secondary pollutant" = **smog**.
[4:4] - **Tropospheric ozone precursors:** NOx, CO, methane/VOCs – **water vapour is not** one.
[4:5] - Stratospheric ozone is **not** an air pollutant (shields UV); ground-level ozone is.
[4:6] - Smog effects: breathing problems, bronchitis; heavy smog cuts UV → less vitamin D (rickets rise) – but **rickets is not a smog disease** (SSC 2018).
[4:7] - **Asian Brown Cloud (UNEP, 2002):** layer of soot, black carbon, sulphate aerosols over **South Asia** (Indo-Gangetic plain) and the Indian Ocean, **January–March** (dry season), from biomass and fossil-fuel burning.
[4:8] > Exam angle: "brown air", "always contains", "produced during", "secondary" – answer ozone / photochemical smog.

## SUB 5 | National Clean Air Programme (NCAP), PRANA and Swachh Vayu Survekshan
[5:0] - **Launched 10 January 2019** by **MoEFCC** – PAN-India **time-bound national strategy** for prevention, control and abatement of air pollution at **regional and urban scales**, plus augmenting the monitoring network; also the answer to "GoI initiative to control industrial emissions" (APPSC G2 Mains 2025).
[5:1] - **Original target: 20–30% cut in PM10/PM2.5 by 2024**, base **2017** (so "by 2025" is wrong).
[5:2] - **Revised (September 2022): up to 40% PM10 reduction (or meeting NAAQS) by 2025-26** (the year asked = **2026**).
[5:3] - Coverage: **130 cities in 24 States/UTs** (non-attainment = failed NAAQS for five years; earlier 131/102); coordinated by **CPCB with SPCBs**; legal base **Air Act 1981**; City Action Plans; performance-linked grant ₹16,425 crore.
[5:4] - **PRANA** = Portal for Regulation of Air-pollution in Non-Attainment cities – **monitors NCAP implementation**; launched **7 September 2021**.
[5:5] - **Highest % PM10 cut, 2017-18 → 2023-24: Varanasi (230 → 73 µg/m³, 68%)**, then Bareilly (61%), Firozabad (59%) (MoEFCC/PIB).
[5:6] - **Swachh Vayu Survekshan**: annual city ranking under NCAP since 2022-23; awards on **Swachh Vayu Diwas, 7 September** (5th SVS awards, 7 Sep 2026). Indore first among million-plus cities in SVS 2023 and 2025; Surat in SVS 2024.
[5:7] - Results: 2024-25 vs 2017-18 – 103 cities cut PM10; only 22 of 130 met PM10 NAAQS. NGT (May 2026): 86% of spend went to road dust.
[5:8] - Programme match: MoEFCC – NCAP; MoES – SAFAR; Jal Shakti – Namami Gange (NRCP, NLCP are water programmes).

## SUB 7 | Bioindicators and Indoor Air Pollution
[7:0] - **Lichens** (symbiotic **algae + fungi**) are the best **bioindicators of air pollution**, especially **SO2** – they **do not grow in polluted areas** (so "lichens grow well in polluted areas" is false). **Mosses** indicate heavy-metal deposition.

[7:2] - Biological indoor pollutants: pollen, mites, pet hair, fungi → allergies, asthma.
[7:3] - Intercity buses: cabin air 2–3 times WHO limits (IntrCity study, 15 Mar 2026).
