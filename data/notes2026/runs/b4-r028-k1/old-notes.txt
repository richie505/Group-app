=== NOTES 4:28 | Disaster Management Framework – Dm Act 2005, Ndma, Ndrf, Early Warning Systems
## SUB 0 | Key Terms – Hazard, Disaster, Vulnerability, Risk
[28:0:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [28:0:0.0] Hazard | Potentially damaging event (e.g. an earthquake in an empty desert = hazard, not disaster)
  [28:0:0.1] Disaster | Hazard meeting a vulnerable population: Hazard × Vulnerability; Sec. 2(d) DM Act – catastrophe causing loss beyond the affected community's coping capacity (not the State Government's)
  [28:0:0.2] Risk | Likelihood of loss; rises with hazard, exposure, vulnerability; falls with coping capacity
  [28:0:0.3] Mitigation (DM Act) | Measures to reduce the risk, impact or effects of a disaster or threatening disaster situation
[28:0:1] # Types of Vulnerability
[28:0:2] TABLE head: Type | Example
  [28:0:2.0] Physical | Weak houses, floodplain settlements
  [28:0:2.1] Social | Age, gender, caste, disability discrimination
  [28:0:2.2] Economic | Poverty, daily-wage workers, small farmers
  [28:0:2.3] Environmental | Mangrove loss (raises cyclone impact), deforestation → landslides
  [28:0:2.4] Systemic / institutional | Weak governance, poor coordination, weak early warning
[28:0:3] - **Community vulnerability factors**: kutcha housing, farm-labour dependence, thin health infrastructure, weak early warning; **high crop-insurance coverage reduces** vulnerability.
[28:0:4] - Hazard types: **geological** (earthquakes, landslides, volcanoes, tsunamis); **hydro-meteorological** (cyclones, floods, droughts, cloudbursts, heat waves); **biological** (epidemics, pest attacks).
[28:0:5] - **Natural vs man-made**: natural disasters cause most damage in **developing countries**; **Bhopal gas tragedy (2–3 December 1984, Methyl Isocyanate)** was **man-made**; mangroves reduce cyclone impact. People killed by natural disasters in India, 1980–2010: **1,43,039**.

## SUB 2 | Disaster Management Act, 2005
[28:2:0] - **Act No. 53 of 2005**; Presidential assent **23 December 2005**; in force progressively from 2006; **whole of India**; **11 Chapters, 79 Sections**.
[28:2:1] - Enacted after the **2004 tsunami** exposed gaps; legal and institutional framework at national, state and district levels.
[28:2:2] - **MHA** = nodal ministry (except drought, pest attack, epidemics); **primary responsibility lies with State Governments**; Centre supports.

[28:2:9] - **June 2026 – "Resilient India 2047"**: NDMA asks Union ministries for hazard-specific plans (**s.35**) and Ministry DM Plans (**s.37**).

[28:4:14] - Not yet set up in all states → strain on NDRF in simultaneous disasters.

## SUB 7 | Disaster Management Cycle
[28:7:0] TABLE head: Phase | Stage | Measures
  [28:7:0.0] Prevention | Pre | Proactive, long-term: land-use zoning, regulating deforestation, control of industrial siting, GIS hazard zonation
  [28:7:0.1] Mitigation | Pre | Most cost-effective; lowers likelihood of disaster: quake-resistant buildings, building codes, embankments, afforestation, hazard maps, retrofitting, insurance; vulnerability analysis and risk assessment
  [28:7:0.2] Preparedness | Pre | "First critical step": early warning (CAP-SACHET), mock drills, stockpiling, Aapda Mitra, Emergency Operation Centres, contingency plans, evacuation routes
  [28:7:0.3] Response | During | Time-critical: search and rescue, evacuation, first aid, shelter, food, restoring critical services
  [28:7:0.4] Recovery / Reconstruction | Post | Restore normal life; damage assessment, compensation, psycho-social care, livelihoods, Build Back Better
[28:7:1] - The cycle is **continuous** – lessons from recovery feed prevention; phases overlap.
[28:7:2] - During: early warning → evacuation → rescue → shelters → emergency medical/water. After: **relief → restoration → reconstruction → resilient rebuilding**.
[28:7:3] - **Structural mitigation** for earthquakes = building codes; cyclones = **coastal tree planting**. **Non-structural mitigation** = **community awareness and education** (public education).
[28:7:4] - **Early warning** is a crucial component of mitigation (timely information).
[28:7:5] - Terms: **HAZOP** study – checks adequacy of safety measures; **MAT** – Mitigation Assessment Team; **hazard mapping** – mapping areas at risk.
[28:7:6] - **First responder**: the **community**. Urban response is shared by civic bodies, fire brigade, police; **district administration** in charge (Collector pivotal).
[28:7:7] "Planning Mitigation" is NOT a type of mitigation; "Non planning required" is NOT a hazard-specific control activity; **recovery and reconstruction are NOT pre-disaster**.
[28:7:8] - **Types of mitigation:** **avoidance**, **minimization** and **compensatory** mitigation.
[28:7:9] - **Hazard-specific control activity:** a measure aimed at one particular hazard, e.g. building **flood levees** against floods.

## SUB 10 | Technology – Gis and Remote Sensing
[28:10:0] - **GIS** stores, analyses, overlays and presents **spatial data**; maps disaster-prone areas and vulnerable people; plans camps, routes, resources; maps flood damage.
[28:10:1] - **APPSC 2025**: in floods, GIS **analyses flood patterns and topography** for proactive action (it builds nothing, pays nothing).
[28:10:2] - GIS **cannot** give details of individual victims and exact locations, nor fully resolve social issues.
