=== Tirthankaras – First, 23rd, Last and Their Symbols
# The 24 Tirthankaras
- **Tirthankara** = "ford-maker", one who shows the path across the cycle of rebirth – a **Jain** term (UPPCS 1993). **Jina** = conqueror (of passions).
- **Historicity:** only the last two, **Parshvanatha (23rd)** and **Mahavira (24th)**, are confirmed by Jain and Buddhist texts; the first 22 are uncertain.
- **First:** **Rishabhadeva (Adinatha / Rishabhanatha)**, born at **Ayodhya** to Nabhi and Marudevi (APPSC G1; CGPSC 2013).
- **Last (24th):** **Vardhamana Mahavira** (APPSC FBO; IAS 1993; WBPCS 2008) – a **reformer** of an older faith, not its founder (CDS 2011).
- **Originator of Jainism:** Jain tradition → **Rishabhadeva** (UPPSC 2010).
  - **⚠ Disputed:** historians treat Parshvanatha as the first historical teacher and some books call Mahavira the founder.
- **20th:** **Munisuvrata (Munisuvrat Swami)** (APPSC Extension Officer).
| No. | Tirthankara | Symbol | Nirvana / place |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rishabhadeva** | **Bull** | Mount Kailash (Ashtapada); born Ayodhya |
| 2 | Ajitanatha | **Elephant** | Sammed Shikhar |
| 12 | Vasupujya | **Buffalo** | **Champapuri** (not Pavapuri) |
| 13 | **Vimalanatha** | **Boar** (not buffalo) | Sammed Shikhar |
| 20 | **Munisuvrata** | **Tortoise** | Sammed Shikhar |
| 21 | Naminatha | **Blue lotus** | Sammed Shikhar |
| 22 | **Neminatha (Arishtanemi)** | **Conch** | **Girnar (Urjayanta)** |
| 23 | **Parshvanatha** | **Serpent** | **Sammed Shikhar** |
| 24 | **Mahavira** | **Lion** | **Pavapuri** |
- **Incorrect pair (SI 2023):** Vimalanatha – Buffalo ✗ (boar).
- **Not Tirthankaras:** **Nathamuni** (Sri Vaishnava acharya; UPPCS 2004) and **Bahubali (Gomateshwara)**, son of Rishabhadeva – so "Gomateshwara represents the last Tirthankara" is the wrong statement (UPSC). Chandraprabha (8th), Sambhava (3rd) and Nemi (22nd) are Tirthankaras.
# Parshvanatha (23rd)
- **Born at Varanasi** to **King Asvasena and Queen Vama** – associated mainly with **Varanasi** (UPPSC 2016; IAS 2003).
- Renounced at **30**; enlightenment after **84 days** of penance; died at **100**, about **250 years before Mahavira**.
- **Nirvana at Sammed Shikhar** (Parasnath hill, **Jharkhand**) → a Jain **Siddha Kshetra** for him (UPPCS 2002 ×2); Pava = Mahavira, Champa = Vasupujya, Urjayanta = Neminatha.
- Taught **four vows (chaturyama)**; believed in the eternity of matter; his followers wore **white**.
> Exam angle: first → **Rishabha**; 23rd → **Parshvanatha (Varanasi, Sammed Shikhar)**; last → **Mahavira**; Bahubali is **not** a Tirthankara.

=== Life of Mahavira
| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Birth | **Kundagrama (Kundalpur / Basukunda)** near **Vaishali**; 599 BCE (traditional) or 540 BCE (NextGen, PMF) |
| Father | **Siddhartha**, chief of the **Jnatrika** Kshatriya clan |
| Mother | **Trishala**, Lichchhavi princess, **sister of Chetaka** |
| Varna | **Kshatriya** (APPSC G1) |
| Wife, daughter | **Yashoda**; daughter **Anojja (Priyadarshana)** – Svetambara view; Digambaras say he never married |
| Renunciation | At **30**; one garment for 13 months, then a naked monk; **12 years** of austerity |
| Kaivalya | At **42**, under a **sal tree** at **Jrimbhikagrama** on the **Rijupalika** (UP RO 2017) |
| Death | **Pava (Pavapuri)** near Rajgriha, aged **72**; 527 BCE (traditional) or 468 BCE (NextGen) |
- **Died at:** **Pavapuri** – not Rajgir (the nearby town), Sanchi, Ranchi or Samastipur (BPSC 2002; State PSC ×2).
- **Birthplace:** **Kundagrama / Kundalpur** (UPPCS 2004; State PSC) – not Vaishali itself, Pataliputra or Magadha.
- **"Not true" (CGPSC 2022):** "He did not marry" ✗ – he married Yashoda; the 24th Tirthankara ✓, Trishala Chetaka's sister ✓, died 527 BCE at Pavapuri ✓.
- **Titles:** Mahavira (great hero), **Jina** (conqueror), **Nirgrantha** (free from bonds), Kevalin, Arhat – **Tathagata** is the Buddha's.
- **Preaching:** 30 years in Kosala, Magadha and further east; first sermon at **Rajgriha**; visited the courts of Bimbisara and Ajatashatru.
# Family and Lichchhavi links
- **Chetaka:** chief of the **Lichchhavis** of Vaishali (RAS 2013: Chetak–Lichchhavi ✓); Mahavira's **maternal uncle**; his daughter **Chellana** married Bimbisara.
- **IAS 2003:** "Mahavira's mother was the **daughter** of Chetaka" ✗ (she was his **sister**); Buddha's mother was a **Koliya** princess ✓; Parshvanatha of **Banaras** ✓ → **II and III**.
  - **⚠ Disputed:** the Digambara tradition makes Trishala Chetaka's daughter; the UPSC key follows "sister".
- **Lichchhavi republic:** first to recognise Jainism.
# Disciples
- **Jamali**, his nephew and son-in-law: **first disciple / follower** (UPPCS 2008; SSC 2013) – not Yashoda (wife), Anojja (daughter) or Trishala (mother).
- **11 Ganadharas**; only **Arya Sudharma** survived him and became the **first Thera (head of the Sangha)** (MPPSC 2009); **Bhadrabahu** was the 6th Thera.

=== Anekantavada, Syadvada and Their Exponents
# Doctrines
| Doctrine | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Anekantavada** | Reality is **many-sided**; no single view holds the whole truth – basis of **tolerance and pluralism** |
| **Syadvada** | Relativity of knowledge: every statement is prefixed with **syat** ("maybe", "in some respect") – **conditioned predication** |
| **Saptabhanginaya** | The **seven-fold predication** of Syadvada |
| **Nayavada** | Theory of **partial standpoints** |
- **Two main doctrines of Jain philosophy:** Anekantavada and Syadvada ✓; every thing has many properties, the permanent one being its **attribute (guna)** ✓ (CGPSC 2023 → **both**). Everything that exists has **substance (dravya), quality (guna) and mode (paryaya)**.
- **Core theory of Jainism:** **Anekantavada** (UPSC 2009; JPSC 2011); **Syadvada** is Jain (UKPSC 2005; APPSC G1 1987).
- **Jain vs Buddhist doctrines (RAS 2021):** Anekantavada, Syadvada = **Jain**; **Sarvastivada, Sunyavada = Buddhist**; niyati = Ajivika; materialism = Charvaka.
# Exponents
| Person | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Kundakundacharya** | Digambara monk; **propounded / preached Syadvada** (APPSC 2008 ×2) and Anekantavada; wrote **Samayasara** (APPSC G2 2008 ×2), Pravachanasara, Panchastikayasara, Niyamasara; ashram at **Konakondla** (Anantapur); first Jain monk of Andhra |
| **Somadeva Suri** | Title **"Syadvadachala Simha"** (APPSC G2 2011 ×3); wrote Yasastilaka Champu, Nitivakyamrita |
| Kalakasuri | Jain monk honoured at the Satavahana court (Pratishthana) |
- **⚠ Trap:** Kundakunda **preached** Syadvada; Somadeva Suri only **held the title**. Nagarjuna and Bavari are Buddhists.
- **Yogasara** is not Kundakunda's – APPSC keys it to **Nagarjuna** (Buddhist).
> Exam angle: "preached Syadvada" / "Samayasara" → **Kundakundacharya**; "Syadvadachala Simha" → **Somadeva Suri**.

=== Triratna, Vows and Jain Practices
# Triratna (Ratnatraya) – the path to moksha
- **Right faith (belief), right knowledge, right conduct (action)** – the Jain three jewels (UPPCS 2004; State PSC; CGPSC 2017 ×2).
- **⚠ Not a Jain jewel:** **right view** – the first step of the Buddhist Eightfold Path (SSC 2011).
- **⚠ Disputed:** Buddhism also has a Triratna (**Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha** – the three refuges); with Jainism offered, keys give **Jainism**. Nyaya and Yoga have none.
- **Match (APPSC G2):** Jainism – **Triratna**; Buddhism – **Ashtanga Marga**; Saivism – **Linga**; Animism – **Nature worship** → a-iii, b-ii, c-iv, d-i.
# Five vows
| Vow | Meaning | Given by |
|---|---|---|
| Ahimsa | Non-violence | Parshvanatha |
| Satya | Truth | Parshvanatha |
| **Asteya** | Non-stealing | Parshvanatha |
| Aparigraha | Non-possession | Parshvanatha |
| **Brahmacharya** | Celibacy (chastity) | **Added by Mahavira** |
- **Mahavratas** = great vows of monks; **Anuvratas** = small vows of householders – a **Jain** concept (UPSC 1995 ×2; CGPSC 2013).
- **Householders** also gave cooked food to the needy daily; strict followers avoided **agriculture**.
# Practices
| Practice | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sallekhana / Santhara / Samadhi Marana** | Ritual fast unto death at life's end – **Jain** (CGPSC 2015 ×2); Chandragupta Maurya at Shravanabelagola |
| Paryushana | Festival; last day **Samvatsari** = forgiveness day |
| Pratikramana | Self-review |
| Padmasana, Kayotsarga | Postures of Tirthankara images |
- **Digambara** = sky-clad; the **Agamas** are in **Ardhamagadhi**.

=== Soul, Karma and the Uncreated Universe
- **Souls everywhere:** all objects, even **rocks, stones and running water**, have a soul and feel injury → extreme ahimsa (UPSC 2023 ×2: **Jainism**).
- **No creator:** the world is **not created** by a supreme being; it runs by an **eternal law** of decay and development → "**Universal Law**" (UPSC 2011). The universe is **eternal**, with **no final annihilation** (UPPCS 2014: Jainism); Jainism is non-theistic.
- **Four forms of existence:** **Deva** (gods), **Manushya** (humans), **Naraki** (hell beings), **Tiryancha** (animals and plants) – **not Yaksha** (UPSC 2026).
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Jiva / **Ajiva** | Soul / non-conscious matter |
| Karma | Material bondage of the soul, cause of rebirth |
| **Samvara** | Stopping the inflow of karma |
| **Nirjara** | Shedding existing karma by penance |
| **Kaivalya (Kevala jnana)** | **Complete knowledge**, omniscience (IAS 1993) |
| **Siddha** | Fully liberated soul |
| Tattva | Origination, decay, permanence |
- **Salvation:** only through **monastic life** – householders cannot attain it; the Vedas and Brahmana supremacy are rejected.

=== Jainism and Buddhism Compared
| Point | Jainism | Buddhism |
|---|---|---|
| Penance | **Extreme path**, self-mortification | **Middle Way** – avoids extremes |
| Soul | Eternal jiva in all things | **Anatta** (no-self) |
| Ahimsa | Stricter, even in thought | Important, less strict |
| Goal | Kevala jnana, moksha | Nirvana |
| Spread | Mostly within India | Widely abroad |
- **Common to both:** **karma and transmigration** (UPPCS 1996), **indifference to the Vedas**, **denial of rituals**, **non-injury to animals**, both Kshatriya-led shramana movements.
- **Not common:** **self-mortification** (BPSC 2001; State PSC) and **avoiding the extremes** of penance and enjoyment (Buddhist only) → UPSC 2012: **II and III**; UPSC 1996: **II, III and IV**.
- **⚠ Trap:** "both men and women could attain salvation" and "salvation only after death" are not shared beliefs.
- **Decline of Jainism:** severe austerities, loss of royal patronage, the Digambara–Svetambara split.
