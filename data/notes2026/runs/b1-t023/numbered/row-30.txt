## SUB 2 | Stupa, Chaitya and Vihara – Concept and Parts
[2:0] - **Stupa** = solid relic mound worshipped by circumambulation; the **concept is pre-Buddhist** (burial mounds; the word occurs in Vedic texts).
[2:1] - **Buddhist stupas** enshrine **relics**, are built as **objects of devotion** by monasteries and **commemorate events** of the Buddha's life – all four statements true (APPSC AEE flashcard). UPSC 2023: "Buddhist in origin" ✗, relic repository ✓, votive and commemorative ✓ → **only two**.
[2:2] - **Chaitya** (chaitya-shala / chaitya-griha) = **place of worship**, prayer hall with a stupa at the apsidal end – the Buddhist "temple" (**chaitya stupas**).
[2:3] - **Vihara** = **residence of monks**; **Arama** = complex of **stupa + vihara + chaitya + school**, e.g. Nandaluru.
[2:4] TABLE head: Part | Meaning
  [2:4.0] Anda | Hemispherical dome (the cosmos)
  [2:4.1] Medhi | Raised drum / terrace round the base of the dome, for circumambulation
  [2:4.2] Harmika | Square railing on top of the anda
  [2:4.3] Yashti | Central mast rising from the harmika
  [2:4.4] Chhatra / Chhatri | Umbrella(s) on the yashti
  [2:4.5] Vedika | Stone railing round the stupa
  [2:4.6] Torana | Ornamental gateways at the four directions
  [2:4.7] Pradakshinapatha | Path for clockwise circumambulation
  [2:4.8] Ayaka pillars / Ayakapata | Special feature of Andhra stupas
[2:5] - **Anda, harmika and chhatri** = parts of a **stupa** (APPSC G2 2024).
[2:6] TABLE head: Stupa type | Content
  [2:6.0] Dhatugarbha | Bodily relics (Amaravati, Bhattiprolu)
  [2:6.1] Paribhogika | Objects used by the Buddha
  [2:6.2] Uddeshika | Commemorative, no relics
  [2:6.3] Aniconic symbol | Event
  [2:6.4] Lotus and bull | Birth
  [2:6.5] Horse (Kanthaka) | Renunciation
  [2:6.6] Empty seat under the Bodhi tree | Enlightenment
  [2:6.7] Dharmachakra (wheel) | First sermon
  [2:6.8] Stupa / footprints | Parinirvana
[2:7] - **Hinayana** showed the Buddha by **symbols**; **Mahayana** adopted images; first human images – **1st c. CE, Gandhara and Mathura**.

[3:2] - **Sect centres:** **Purvasaila – Dhanyakataka (Amaravati)**; **Aparasaila – Sriparvata (Nagarjunakonda)**; **Rajagirika – Guntupalli**; **Siddharthaka Chaitya – Gudivada** (APPSC G2 2008).
[3:3] - **Wrong pair (APPSC G2 2011): "Guntupalli – Siddharthaka Chaitya"** (Guntupalli = Rajagirika). ⚠ Some scholars place the Siddharthikas at Guntupalli.
[3:4] - **Mahasanghika** sect was born at **Dhanyakataka**; **9 of the 18** early sects existed in Andhra; **Andhakas** attended the 3rd council (Kathavatthu); Hinayana – Amaravati, Mahayana – Sriparvata, Vajrayana – Salihundam.
[3:5] - **N. Venkataramanayya** identified "**Sriparvata Swamy**" with the Buddha (APPSC G2).
[3:6] - **Why monasteries near cities** (e.g. Amaravati near Guntur): monks lived on **daily alms**, so viharas stood close enough to towns for the alms round (merchant donations also flowed).
[3:7] - **Latest:** AP plans **2 Buddhist circuits** round its **40** monuments (2024–25); Feb 2026 "**Discover Andhra**" campaign – Amaravati, Bavikonda, Thotlakonda.
[3:8] - **Distractors:** Kondaveedu – **Reddy** kingdom; Srinatha's **Haravilasam** – Reddi sea trade.

## SUB 5 | Spread, Patrons and Decline of Buddhism
[5:0] - **Causes of spread:** simple doctrine, appeal to the lower orders, **missionary** zeal and the people's **language** (Pali/Prakrit) – I, II, III, IV (UPPSC 2009); not "soundness of the Vedic spirit".
[5:1] - **Mauryan followers:** **Ashoka** and **Dasharatha** (CGPSC 2013); Chandragupta became a Jain, Bindusara an Ajivika.
[5:2] - **Sites (IAS 1998):** **Lothal – dockyard**, **Sarnath – first sermon**, **Nalanda – learning** ✓; Rajgir – Lion Capital ✗ (the capital is from Sarnath).
[5:3] TABLE head: Ashoka's missionary | Region
  [5:3.0] Mahinda + Sanghamitta | Sri Lanka
  [5:3.1] Sona + Uttara | Suvarnabhumi
  [5:3.2] Majjhantika | Kashmir and Gandhara
  [5:3.3] Mahadeva | Mahishamandala (Mysore)
  [5:3.4] Rakkhita | Vanavasi
  [5:3.5] Maharakkhita | Yona country
[5:4] - Buddhism reached Central Asia and China by the Kushan-era **Silk Route**.
[5:5] - **Mahavamsa:** **Mahadeva Bhikshu** took Buddhism **from Andhra to Sri Lanka** (not the reverse).
[5:6] TABLE head: Andhra patron | Contribution
  [5:6.0] Satavahanas | Vedic kings but lavish donors; Sanchi toranas
  [5:6.1] Veerapurushadatta (Ikshvaku) | Nagarjunakonda Maha Chaitya; royal women donors
  [5:6.2] Upasika Bodhisri | Service in Ehuvala Chantamula's reign
  [5:6.3] Vishnukundins | Last dynasty to support Buddhism in Andhra; Indrapalanagara vihara
[5:7] - Greatest royal patronage in Andhra: **Ikshvakus** of Vijayapuri. **Lay patronage** – queens, merchants, guilds; 2,000 karshapanas with a **weavers'** guild for robes.
[5:8] - **Decline:** loss of royal patronage; **Brahmanical (Bhakti) revival**; Vajrayana ritualism; **Sanskrit replaced Pali**; hostile rulers **Pushyamitra Shunga, Shashanka, Mihirakula**; Turkish raids (Nalanda sacked by **Bakhtiyar Khalji**). **Hiuen Tsang** found Sriparvata **without patronage**.
