=== Largest Items and Trends of Union Expenditure
- **Largest item of Union revenue expenditure = interest payments** – larger than defence, subsidies, pensions or grants to States; it was also the **largest non-plan item** (1991-92 onwards). BE 2026-27: **₹14,03,972 crore** = about **26% of total spending** and **40% of revenue receipts** (37% in 2013-14; 42% in 2020-21).
- **Revenue expenditure** is the largest block of the Budget (bigger than plan, non-plan or capital expenditure): **88% of total (2014-15) → about 77%** (2026-27).
- **Centre + States** spending ≈ **28% of GDP** (Economic Survey 2016-17).
- Total Union spending BE 2026-27 **₹53,47,315 crore = 13.6% of GDP** (+7.7%); revenue expenditure ₹41.25 lakh crore (10.5% of GDP).
- **Committed expenditure** (salaries, pensions, interest) = **65.3%** of revenue receipts.
- **Why spending grows:** urbanisation, interest payments, subsidies and defence – all of them.
| Rupee goes to | 2023-24 BE | 2026-27 BE |
|---|---|---|
| States' share of taxes | 18% | 22% |
| Interest | 20% | 20% |
| Central Sector Schemes | 17% | 17% |
| Defence | 8% | 11% |
| Major subsidies | **7%** (not 9%) | 6% |
- Then CSS 8%, FC transfers 7%, pensions 2% (2026-27).
> Exam angle: "largest item of (revenue / non-plan / total) expenditure" = **interest payments** – asked by UPPCS, CGPSC and APPSC almost every year.

=== Revenue vs Capital Expenditure
| Revenue (no asset) | Capital (asset / liability change) |
|---|---|
| Salaries, pensions, **interest** | Roads, airports, irrigation, buildings |
| **Subsidies** (food, fertiliser) | **Defence equipment** (capital outlay) |
| **Grants to States** (even if assets are made) | **Loans and advances to States**, PSUs |
| Maintenance of existing assets | **Repayment of loans** |
- **Test:** changes the government's **assets or liabilities** = capital; the rest = revenue. Capital expenditure buys, creates, upgrades or **extends the life** of assets.
- **Revenue deficit** = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts.
- **Golden Rule:** borrow only for **asset creation** (capital expenditure).
- Firms too: **acquiring new technology = capital expenditure**; debt vs equity is a way of **financing**, not a type of expenditure.
- **Plan expenditure** had **both revenue and capital** parts.
- **Budget terms:** the **per capita method** of estimation fixes a minimum amount per head; **budget control** keeps spending within the sanctioned budget and the laid-down rules.

=== Plan vs Non-Plan Expenditure
- Plan/non-plan split **ended with Budget 2017-18** (end of 12th Plan), announced in the Budget Speech 2016-17; replaced by **revenue vs capital**. **Andhra Pradesh** also dropped it from its **2017-18** Budget.
- **C. Rangarajan High Level Expert Committee on Efficient Management of Public Expenditure (Planning Commission, 2011):** the split was **"dysfunctional"**, an obstacle to **outcome-based budgeting** → remove it.
- **Non-plan items:** **interest payments (largest)**, defence, **subsidies** (food, fertiliser), salaries, pensions, loans to States/UTs/PSUs.
- Plan = asset-creating, productive; non-plan = consumptive, committed. **Maintenance of completed Plan assets** was shifted to the **non-plan (non-developmental)** side.

|---|---|
| Education, sports, art, culture | **Defence**, border roads |
| Medical and public health | **Interest payments** |
| Irrigation and flood control | Pensions |
| Rural development, power, railways | Administrative and fiscal services, **law and order (police)** |

=== Subsidies
- **2026-27:** **₹4,54,773 crore**; **food ₹2,27,629 crore (largest) + fertiliser ₹1,70,799 crore = 87%**.
- Major subsidies (food, fertiliser, petroleum) **₹4.11 lakh crore = 1.0% of GDP**; Q1 2026-27 up 37.4% (crude, West Asia).
- **2002-03:** Central subsidies about **1.6% of GDP** (never above 2% since 1991).
| Explicit | Hidden (implicit) |
|---|---|
| Budget line: food, fertiliser, LPG, export incentives, **interest subvention** | Under-pricing of public services: **higher education in public universities**, health, **irrigation water** |
- **Oil Pool Account** under the **Administered Pricing Mechanism** closed **1 April 2002** (FM **Yashwant Sinha**); under-recoveries later paid through **oil bonds**.
- **NBS:** from **1 April 2010**; fixed subsidy per kg of **N, P, K, S**; **22 grades** of decontrolled P&K; **urea outside NBS**.
- **16th FC:** well-targeted subsidies, **exclusion criteria, sunset clauses**, no off-budget financing.
- **Rationalising subsidies** and cutting revenue expenditure reduce the deficit.

=== Capital Expenditure Push
- Capex 2026-27: **₹12,21,821 crore** (+11.5%, **3.1% of GDP**) = capital outlay **₹9,43,042 crore** + loans **₹2,78,780 crore**.
- **Effective capex** = capex + **grants-in-aid for capital assets**: **₹17.15 lakh crore (4.4% of GDP)**; grants ₹4.93 lakh crore.
- **SASCI:** **50-year interest-free** loans to States for capex; **₹1.85 lakh crore** (2026-27).

=== Laws of Expenditure Growth
| Law | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Wagner's law** (Adolph Wagner) | Spending rises faster than national income as an economy industrialises = **law of increasing state activity** |
| **Peacock-Wiseman** (**1961**, **UK** data) | Spending rises in steps; wars/crises raise the tolerable tax level = **displacement effect** |

=== State and Andhra Pradesh Expenditure
- **RBI "State Finances: A Study of Budgets" (2018):** States' capital outlay was highest on **rural development**, then water supply and sanitation, urban development, education.
- States 2014-24: spending **16.15-17.49% of GSDP**; revenue expenditure **80-87%** of total; 9 of 18 large States budget revenue deficits for 2026-27.
- **AP 2026-27 BE:** **Revenue expenditure > Capital outlay > Loans and advances**; revenue ≈ **83%**; capital outlay **₹48,698 crore (+47%)**; revenue ₹2,56,143 crore. Revenue items: rice and power subsidy, welfare pensions, housing.
