## SUB 0 | External Debt
[0:0] - **Size:** end-Dec 2025 **US$ 765.5 bn = 20.4% of GDP**. History: US$ 98.2 bn (Mar 2000) → 100.2 bn (Mar 2001, rise from **commercial borrowings and NRI deposits**); about **US$ 300 bn** around end-2010 (297.5 bn Dec 2010, 305.9 bn Mar 2011); ratio ~**21%** of GDP (Mar 2002), **23.7%** (Mar 2016).
[0:1] - **Who owes it:** mostly **non-sovereign** (companies, banks); government external debt ~2.6% of GDP.
[0:2] - **Largest component:** **commercial borrowings** (about 38% till 2017; still largest end-Dec 2025). **ADB** = largest multilateral lender (32.8%).
[0:3] - **Currency (Dec 2025):** **US dollar 54.8%**, rupee 30.1%, yen 6.3% – not all in dollars.
[0:4] - **ECBs** are regulated by the **RBI** (FEMA).
[0:5] - **World Bank:** India a **less indebted** country. **GDF 2010** most indebted: **Russia, China, Turkey, Brazil, India**; around 2000 **Brazil** was the top developing-country debtor; India ranked about **10th** of the top fifteen (GDF, ES 2001-02).
[0:6] - **Grants 2005-06:** the largest source was the **United Kingdom** (DFID).
[0:7] - "India's External Debt: A Status Report" – **DEA, Ministry of Finance**; RBI compiles quarterly data.
[0:8] > Exam angle: UPSC 2019 – "most debt is governmental" and "all in dollars" are both false.

## SUB 1 | Components of Public Debt and Government Funds
[1:0] - **Art. 292:** the Union borrows on the security of the **Consolidated Fund of India**. Internal debt = **96.7%** of public debt; **G-Secs** over 90% of Central debt. Internal vs external is by **source** of borrowing.
[1:1] TABLE head: Head | Items
  [1:1.0] Internal – marketable | Market loans (dated G-Secs), Treasury Bills (91/182/364-day), Cash Management Bills
  [1:1.1] Internal – non-marketable | Special securities to NSSF and RBI, 14-day intermediate T-Bills, securities to international institutions
  [1:1.2] Other liabilities (Public Account) | Small savings (NSC, post-office deposits), provident funds, reserve funds
  [1:1.3] External | Loans from foreign governments and multilaterals
[1:2] - **Consolidated Fund (Art. 266)** = main account: **all revenues, loans raised and loan repayments**. **Public Account** = money held **in trust** (State Provident Fund credited here). **Contingency Fund** = urgent needs.
[1:3] - **Provident fund = contractual savings.** **Life insurance policies** are insurers' liabilities – **not** national (government) debt; NSCs and PF are.
[1:4] - **T-Bills** = short-term debt instruments; dated securities at market-related rates in auctions = the largest part of internal debt.
[1:5] - **Non-financial(-sector) debt** covers households (**housing loans, credit-card dues**), non-financial firms and government (**Treasury Bills**).
[1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.22; app notes 3:103 sub 0
## SUB 2 | Debt Sustainability and Debt Trap
[2:0] - **Debt trap:** a country has to **borrow to pay interest** on its outstanding loans.
[2:1] - **Zero primary deficit** = fresh borrowing only for interest payments.
[2:2] - **r − g:** if g > r the debt-GDP ratio falls; if **r > g a primary surplus** is needed.
[2:3] - Interest ≈ **40% of revenue receipts** → high interest **squeezes other spending**; a **rising debt-GDP ratio hampers** good fiscal administration.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.54; app notes 3:103 sub 3
## SUB 3 | Market Borrowings and Savings
[3:0] - **Market borrowings = largest source of capital receipts**; borrowings gave the maximum resources for plan expenditure.

## SUB 5 | State Debt
[5:0] - **Art. 293:** States borrow **only within India** on the security of their Consolidated Fund, via **SDLs**; a State **owing the Centre** needs its **consent** to borrow; the Centre may lend to States (Art. 293(2)). External loans to States are **routed through the Centre** since **2005-06**.
[5:1] - **Net borrowing ceiling:** **4% of GSDP in 2021-22** (+0.5% for power reforms); **16th FC: 3% of GSDP** (2026-31). Key ratios: debt-GSDP, interest/revenue receipts.

## SUB 6 | Off-budget Borrowing and Debt Management
[6:0] - Off-budget: PSU/SPV loans **serviced from the budget but kept outside the deficit** → hides true debt (makes it look lower), hurts ratings.
[6:1] - **FCI borrowing from NSSF** for food subsidy – ended in **Budget 2021-22**. FRBM: new Central guarantees ≤ **0.5% of GDP** a year.
[6:2] - **AP** off-budget **₹1.28 lakh crore** (9.7% of GSDP, March 2023). **16th FC:** stop it and bring existing debt **on-budget**.
[6:3] - **RBI** manages government debt (RBI Act 1934). **PDMC** (2016) is in the **DEA**, not the RBI. A statutory **PDMA** was proposed (2015-16) but **not created**.
