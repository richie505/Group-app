=== NOTES 3:99 | Finance Commissions – Mandate and Key Recommendations of the 13TH, 14TH, 15TH and 16TH
## SUB 0 | Mandate (article 280)
[99:0:0] - Constituted by the **President** every five years; **Chairman + 4 members**.
[99:0:1] - **Art. 280(3)** duties: (a) sharing **net proceeds** of taxes Centre-States and among States; (b) principles of **grants-in-aid** (Art. 275); (bb)/(c) measures to augment State Consolidated Funds for **panchayats and municipalities** (added **1992**, based on **State Finance Commission** reports).
[99:0:2] - Recommendations are **advisory**; Art. 282 discretionary grants are **not** an FC role.
[99:0:3] - **FC (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951**: Chairman = experience in **public affairs**; members from judiciary, finance/accounts, administration, economics.

[100:3:0] - Pool = Art. 270 taxes (corporation tax, income tax, CGST, Centre's IGST share); **excludes cesses, surcharges, cost of collection**.

=== NOTES 3:101 | Fiscal Decentralisation to Local Bodies – State Finance Commissions and Local Revenue
## SUB 0 | Constitutional Provisions
[101:0:0] TABLE head: Article | Body | Provision
  [101:0:0.0] 243H | Panchayats | State Legislature may authorise taxes, tolls, fees; assign State taxes; give grants
  [101:0:0.1] 243I | Panchayats | Governor constitutes SFC every five years
  [101:0:0.2] 243X | Municipalities | Same enabling power
  [101:0:0.3] 243Y | Municipalities | Same SFC (243I) reviews municipal finances; no separate SFC
  [101:0:0.4] 280(3)(bb), (c) | Both | Central FC augments State fund on SFC recommendations
[101:0:1] - "May" = **enabling**; State not forced to impose taxes.
[101:0:2] - **Eleventh Schedule** (Art. 243G): **29** subjects; **Twelfth Schedule** (Art. 243W): **18** subjects; both 1992. Lists are enabling → **funds, functions, functionaries (3Fs)** rarely move together.
## SUB 1 | Own Revenue of Local Bodies
[101:1:0] TABLE head: Type | Sources
  [101:1:0.0] ULB tax | Property tax (most important), professional tax (ceiling ₹2,500), advertisement, entertainment, water, market taxes; octroi (traditional)
  [101:1:0.1] ULB non-tax | Rent, fees, fines, user charges (water, sewerage)
  [101:1:0.2] ULB other | Grants, SFC devolution, loans; municipal bonds in a few large cities
[101:1:1] - Weak municipalities = **under-assessment and under-collection** of property tax.
[101:1:2] - **ES 2016-17**: **satellite imagery** estimated property-tax gap in **Bengaluru and Jaipur** (APPSC G1 2017).
[101:1:3] - **SVAMITVA** drone maps → panchayat property-tax baseline; **AMRUT 2.0** rewards property-tax reform and bonds.
## SUB 2 | Panchayat Revenue
[101:2:0] - **Property tax** = major own revenue of Panchayats; levy only **State-authorised** taxes: local fairs, house tax, fees, tolls.
[101:2:1] - Sources: **FC grants**, **Central Cooperative Banks**, **CSS** allocations, **SFC** allocations.
[101:2:2] **NABARD** is NOT a direct panchayat source; **sales tax, land revenue** are State levies.
[101:2:3] - Gram sabhas spend only **4%** of time on revenue (13% on local issues).
[101:2:4] - **Grant dependence:** panchayats have limited powers to tax, so they depend substantially on **grants** (FC, SFC and CSS funds).

=== NOTES 3:102 | Public Expenditure – Revenue and Capital, Developmental and Non-developmental, Trends; Subsidies
## SUB 0 | Plan vs Non-plan Expenditure
[102:0:0] - Plan/non-plan split **ended with Budget 2017-18** (end of 12th Plan); announced in Budget Speech 2016-17; replaced by **revenue vs capital**.
[102:0:1] - **C. Rangarajan Committee (2011)**: split was "dysfunctional", an obstacle to **outcome-based budgeting**.
[102:0:2] - Non-plan items: defence, interest, loans to States/UTs/PSUs.
[102:0:3] - Non-plan expenditure also included **subsidies** (food, fertiliser) and pensions.
## SUB 1 | Developmental vs Non-developmental
[102:1:0] TABLE head: Developmental | Non-developmental
  [102:1:0.0] Education, sports, art, culture | Defence, border roads
  [102:1:0.1] Medical and public health | Interest payments
  [102:1:0.2] Irrigation and flood control | Pensions
  [102:1:0.3] Rural development, power, railways | Administrative and fiscal services
## SUB 2 | Revenue vs Capital Expenditure
[102:2:0] TABLE head: Revenue (no asset) | Capital (asset / liability change)
  [102:2:0.0] Salaries, pensions, interest | Roads, airports, irrigation, buildings
  [102:2:0.1] Subsidies (food, fertiliser) | Defence equipment (capital outlay)
  [102:2:0.2] Grants to States (even if assets made) | Loans and advances to States, PSUs
  [102:2:0.3] Maintenance of existing assets | Repayment of loans
[102:2:1] - Test: changes govt's **assets or liabilities** = capital; rest = revenue.
[102:2:2] - Capital expenditure buys, creates, upgrades or **extends life** of assets.
[102:2:3] - **Revenue deficit** = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts.
[102:2:4] - **Golden Rule**: borrow only for asset creation.
[102:2:5] Grants to States = revenue; loans to States = capital. Food and fertiliser subsidies = revenue (APPSC G2 2008).
## SUB 3 | Laws of Expenditure Growth
[102:3:0] TABLE head: Law | Statement
  [102:3:0.0] Wagner's law (Adolph Wagner) | Spending rises faster than national income as economy industrialises = law of increasing state activity
  [102:3:0.1] Peacock-Wiseman (1961, UK data) | Rises in steps; wars/crises raise tolerable tax = displacement effect; no fall-back
## SUB 4 | Union Expenditure Trends (be 2026-27)
[102:4:0] - **Centre + States** spending ≈ **28% of GDP** (ES 2016-17; APPSC G2 2017).
[102:4:1] - Total **₹53,47,315 crore = 13.6% of GDP** (+7.7%); revenue exp. ₹41.25 lakh crore (10.5%).
[102:4:2] - Revenue share in total: **88% (2014-15) → ~77%**.
[102:4:3] - **Largest item of Union revenue expenditure = interest**: **₹14,03,972 crore** = 26% of total, **40% of revenue receipts** (37% in 2013-14; 42% in 2020-21).
[102:4:4] - **Committed expenditure** (salaries, pensions, interest) = **65.3%** of revenue receipts.

=== NOTES 3:103 | Public Debt - Internal and External, Debt-gdp Ratio, Sustainability, State Debt and Off-budget Borrowing
## SUB 0 | Centre's Liabilities
[103:0:0] - **Art. 292**: Union borrows on the security of the **Consolidated Fund of India**. Internal debt = **96.7%** of public debt; **G-Secs** over 90% of Central debt.
[103:0:1] TABLE head: Head | Items
  [103:0:1.0] Internal - marketable | Market loans (G-Secs), T-Bills (91/182/364-day), Cash Management Bills
  [103:0:1.1] Internal - non-marketable | NSSF special securities, 14-day intermediate T-Bills, compensation/saving bonds
  [103:0:1.2] Other liabilities (Public Account) | Small savings, post-office deposits, provident funds, reserve funds
  [103:0:1.3] External | Loans from foreign governments, multilaterals
[103:0:2] - Funds: Consolidated Fund = main account, receives loans and loan recoveries; Public Account = money held in trust (State Provident Fund credited here); Contingency Fund = urgent needs; NIF = permanent corpus.
[103:0:3] Market loans, T-Bills, special securities to RBI = internal debt; small savings, PF = other liabilities. Provident Fund = **contractual savings**.
[103:0:4] - **Internal vs external debt:** classified by the **source** of borrowing. Internal debt is borrowed within the country (domestic economy); external debt comes from foreign sources, and foreign-currency obligations belong to it.
## SUB 1 | Market Borrowings
[103:1:0] - **Market borrowings = largest source of capital receipts**; borrowings give maximum resources for plan expenditure.
[103:1:1] - 2026-27: gross ₹**17.20 lakh crore**, net ₹11.73 lakh crore.
[103:1:2] - Household savings reach government via bank deposits (**SLR**), small savings/NSSF, insurance/PF.
## SUB 2 | External Debt
[103:2:0] - End-Dec 2025: **USD 765.5 bn = 20.4%** of GDP.
[103:2:1] - Mostly **non-sovereign** (companies, banks); government external debt ~2.6% of GDP; only 3.3% of Centre's liabilities.
[103:2:2] - Largest component: **commercial borrowings** (about 38% till 2017; still largest end-Dec 2025). ADB largest multilateral lender (32.8%).
[103:2:3] - Currency (Dec 2025): **US dollar 54.8%**, rupee 30.1%, yen 6.3% - not all in dollars.
[103:2:4] - World Bank classed India **less indebted**. GDF 2010 most indebted: Russia, China, Turkey, Brazil, India. Rise 2000-01: commercial borrowings, NRI deposits; crossed USD 300 bn end-2010.
[103:2:5] - "India's External Debt: A Status Report" - **DEA, Ministry of Finance**; RBI compiles quarterly data.
## SUB 3 | Debt Sustainability
[103:3:0] - **Debt trap**: borrowing to pay interest on past debt. **Zero primary deficit** = borrowing only for interest.
[103:3:1] - **r - g**: g > r, debt-GDP falls; **r > g, primary surplus needed**.
[103:3:2] - Interest ≈ **40% of revenue receipts**. Rising debt-GDP hurts fiscal administration.
