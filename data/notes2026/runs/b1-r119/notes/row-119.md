=== Founding of Vijayanagara (1336) – Harihara I and Bukka I
- **Founders:** brothers **Harihara I and Bukka I (Bukkaraya I)**, sons of **Sangama** → **Sangama dynasty** (APPSC FBO, G2; UKPSC; Uttarakhand PCS 2004, 2024) – not Krishnadevaraya, Rama Raya, Achyuta Raya, Devaraya I/II, Pushyamitra or Bhadrabahu.
- **Year:** **1336** (RPSC 2010; APPSC 2019) – not 1326, 1332, 1346 or 1035; **1346** = Sringeri visit after the Hoysala victory.
- **First ruler:** **Harihara I** (1336-56) – Bukka I succeeded him (1356-77).
- **River:** on the **southern bank of the Tungabhadra**, a tributary of the **Krishna** (APPSC ASO 2011; UPPSC 2011) – not the Krishna, Kaveri, Penna or Wainganga; ruins at **Hampi**.
- **Agent of the deity (UPSC 2015 ×2):** **Harihara I** founded the new city and ruled as the agent of **Virupaksha**, to whom all land **south of the Krishna** belonged – not Amoghavarsha I, Ballala II or Prataparudra II.
- **Story in brief:** subordinates at **Kampili** (under the Hoysalas of Dwarasamudra) → **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** took Kampili, captured the brothers, converted them in **Delhi** and sent them back → **Vidyaranya** of **Sringeri** reconverted them → independence declared at the **Virupaksha temple, Hampi**; crowned **18 April 1336**.
- **"Treasurers of Warangal":** one tradition makes the brothers treasury officers of **Prataparudra (Kakatiya)**; the place they founded = **Vijayanagara** (APPSC Medical Officer 2021; dropped from the key for a wording error) – not Kadapa, Hyderabad or Tirupati.
- **Capital:** first **Anegondi**, then the new city of **Vijayanagara** (completed **c. 1343**, under Bukka I's oversight).
- **Name:** Vijayanagara = **"City of Victory"**; legend of a **hare turning on the hunting dogs** marked the site; emblem **Varaha (boar)**; Sangamas were **Shaiva**, the later dynasties **Vaishnava**.
- **Largest Hindu kingdom** of medieval South India (APPSC) – not Rashtrakuta, Kakatiya or Satavahana.

=== The Four Dynasties of Vijayanagara
| Order | Dynasty | Span | Founder | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sangama** | 1336-1485 | Harihara I and Bukka I | Andhra / Kannada (disputed) |
| 2 | **Saluva** | 1485-1505 | Saluva Narasimha – **first usurpation** | Kalyana (Karnataka); "Telugu people" (NextGen) |
| 3 | **Tuluva** | 1505-1570 | **Vira Narasimha** (son of Tuluva Narasa Nayaka) – **second usurpation** | Tuluva nadu |
| 4 | **Aravidu** | 1570-1646 | **Tirumala Raya** | Araviti (Kurnool), Andhra |
- **First dynasty:** **Sangama** (UKPSC 2011; Polytechnic 2020; APPSC) – not Hoysala, Saluva, Tuluva or Aravidu.
- **Order:** **Sangama → Saluva → Tuluva → Aravidu** = C, A, B, D / C, A, D, B (Sachivalayam papers) – S-S-T-A.
- **Last Hindu dynasty of Vijayanagara:** **Aravidu** (APPSC 2019 ×2).
- **Ruled under Vijayanagara:** Sangamas, Saluvas, Tuluvas and Aravidus (Asst. Comm. 2017) – **Hoysalas, Yadavas of Devagiri and Poligars** were never Vijayanagara dynasties.
- **Odd one out:** **Vishnukundins** (an older Andhra dynasty) against Saluva, Sangama, Tuluva (APPSC G1 ×2); **Kadamba** = the dynasty that never ruled Vijayanagara (APPSC 2019).
- **Odd one (APPSC G2 2001):** **Kings of Vijayanagara** – Yadagirigutta, Simhachalam and Vemulawada are temple towns.
- **Devaraya II** (1426 in the stem; reign 1422/26-46) belonged to the **Sangama** dynasty.
- **Sangama:** longest-ruling dynasty; greatest king **Devaraya II**; last king **Praudha Raya** (removed 1485 by **Saluva Narasimha**, who succeeded him).
- **Tuluva:** **Vira Narasimha** crowned **1505** after killing **Immadi Narasimha**; then **Krishnadevaraya** and **Achyutaraya** (Tuluvas).
- **Aravidu:** capitals **Penukonda, Chandragiri, Vellore**; after Talikota the capital moved **Hampi → Penukonda → Chandragiri**; Anegondi was never an Aravidu capital.
- **Andhra in origin:** only **Sangama and Aravidu** (app notes); the Kakatiyas were not a Vijayanagara dynasty.
| Ruler | Dynasty / fact |
|---|---|
| Krishnadevaraya (1509-29) | Tuluva; wrote **Amuktamalyada**; queens **Tirumala Devi, Chinna Devi**; founded **Nagalapuram** for his mother **Nagala Devi**; credited with the **Vithala** temple; contemporary of **Purandara Dasa** |
| Sadasivaraya | Tuluva; regent Rama Raya **remitted the barbers' tax** (at Kandoja's request) |

=== Devaraya II (1422/26-46) – the Greatest Sangama King
- **Greatest king of the Sangama dynasty**; also called **Praudha Devaraya**.
- **Title Gajabetekara** (Gajabetegara, "**hunter of elephants**") (RPSC 2010; APPSC) – it is a **title of Devaraya II**, not a coin, a king's name or a Jain monk; coins bearing it are his **Gajabetekara coins** – not Devaraya I, Bukka I or Mallikarjuna. **Andhra Bhoja** and **Dakshinapatha-swami** are Krishnadevaraya's titles.
- **Army:** enlisted about **10,000 Muslim** soldiers (mounted archers) (APPSC G2 2011 ×3) – not Vira Narasimha, Bukka I or Virupaksha; gave them **jagirs** and a mosque; kept a **Quran before his throne** so they could salute it.
- **First to enlist Muslims at all:** **Devaraya I** (NextGen; APPSC) – Devaraya II enlarged it to 10,000.
- **Ceylon:** his general **Lakkanna** (Dakshina Samudradhipati) invaded **Sri Lanka** and collected **tribute** (IAS 2003) – not Harihara I/II or Bukka II.
- **Abdur Razzaq**, Persian envoy of **Shah Rukh**, came in **1443** in **Devaraya II's** reign (BPSC 2018; APPSC JL 2004; UDA/LDA 2010) – not Devaraya I, Krishnadevaraya, Achyuta Raya or Vira Vijaya; described diamond bazaars, about **300 ports**, taxed **prostitution**.
- **Literature:** wrote the Sanskrit **Mahanataka Sudhanidhi** (APPSC AE 2011) – not Devaraya I, Krishnadevaraya or Peddana; his literary hall = **Mutyala Sala** (APPSC G1) – **Bhuvana Vijayam** is Krishnadevaraya's.
- **Srinatha** adorned his court (CGPSC 2019, 2020): **kanakabhishekam** after defeating **Dindima Bhatta** – not Harihara II, Devaraya I or Krishnadevaraya.
- Other: took **Kondaveedu (1424)**; **Draksharama inscription (1444)** – pushed back the **Gajapatis**; Nicolo Conti also spent time at his court.
~~ Sources: NextGen Vijayanagara Kingdom p.3, p.11-12; NextGen AP History - APPSC 13 p.6; NextGen previous papers p.36, p.42; app notes 1:119 (sub 5)

=== Bukka I (1356-77) – Madurai, Madhura Vijayam, Jains and Vaishnavas, China
- **Kumara Kampana** (Kamparaya), son of Bukka I, conquered **Madurai** – destroyed the **Madurai (Ma'bar) Sultanate** (c. 1371) after overthrowing **Sambhuvaraya** (APPSC; G2-A 1989) – not Harihara II, Devaraya, Marappa or Sankula Nayaka.
- **Madhura Vijayam** (Veera Kamparaya Charitam), Sanskrit, by his wife **Gangadevi (Gangamba)** (UPPCS 2000; APPSC G1 2010; G2 2000; G2-B 1989) – not Molla, Tirumalamba, Ramabhadramba, Madhuravani, Bharati or Polamamba.
- **Gangadevi's gurus: Agastya and Vidyanatha** → "disciple of **Agastya**" (APPSC G2 2012) – not Vidyaranya, Sayana, Rameswara Pandita, Tikkana or Potana.
- **Jains and Vaishnavas:** **Bukka I** settled their dispute in **1368** (**Shravanabelagola** inscription) and protected the Jains (APPSC G1 1994, G2 2003; ×4 in all) – not Harihara I, Devaraya I/II, Saluva Narasimha or Krishnadevaraya.
- **China:** **Bukka I** sent an embassy to the **Ming** emperor (**1374**) (CGPSC 2008) → the south Indian power that sent an envoy to the Ming in the late 14th century = **Vijayanagara** (APPSC JA) – not Kakatiya, Pandya or Bahmani.
- **Nachana Somana** (Uttara Harivamsam) = court poet of **Bukka I**, the first Telugu poet of the Vijayanagara court (APPSC 2011); granted **Penchikaladinne**.
- **Succession:** Bukka I → his son **Harihara II** (APPSC 2019) – not Harihara I, Bukka II or Devaraya I.
- Completed the capital; title **Vedamarga Pratishthapaka**; took **Srisailam** and **Dupadu** (near Markapur); general **Saluva Mangu** killed the Madurai Sultan → title **"Parapakshi Saluva"**.
~~ Sources: NextGen AP History - APPSC 13 p.5; NextGen Vijayanagara Kingdom p.2, p.9-10; NextGen previous papers p.28 ("Gangadevi was the student of Agastya"), p.43 ("Bukka-I resolved the conflict between the Jainas and Vaishnavas"); app notes 1:119 (sub 4, 6)

=== Origin Theories and Sources – Sewell's 'A Forgotten Empire'
- **'A Forgotten Empire (Vijayanagar)'** (1900) = **Robert Sewell** (APPSC G2 2011 ×2, G2 Mains 2017, JA 2012) – not Ferishta, B.A. Saletore, Burton Stein, James Fergusson, Father Heras, H.G. Wells, N. Venkataramanayya or K.A.N. Sastri; it carries the English translation of **Domingo Paes** and **Fernao Nuniz**, and covers 1336-1565.
- Sewell recorded **seven** local traditions of the founding.

|---|---|---|

| Kannada / Hoysala | Officers of **Veera Ballala III** | Father Heras, S.K. Ayyangar, H. Krishna Sastri, P.B. Desai |

- **Delhi legend:** fall of Warangal → conversion in Delhi → sent against Deccan rebels → defeat → met **Vidyaranya** near **Pampa** → return to Hinduism → Anegondi in the name of Virupaksha.

=== Harihara II, Sayana and Devaraya I
- **Sayana** (Vedic commentator, **Vedartha Prakasha**), brother of **Vidyaranya**, minister of **Harihara II** → "patronised by / flourished under the **Vijayanagara** rulers" (UPPCS 2008; Uttarakhand 2002) – not Paramaras, Satavahanas, Vakatakas, Cholas or Guptas.
- **Vedic commentaries of Sayana and Madhava:** written in **Harihara II's** period (NextGen; APPSC) – the work was begun on **Bukka I's** order and finished under Harihara II.
- **Harihara II (1377-1404):** longest-reigning Sangama king; titles **Rajavyasa, Rajavalmiki**; first Vijayanagara ruler to **attack Sri Lanka**; his general **Madhava** took **Goa** (with Belgaum, **1380**) – title **Bhuvanaikavira** → first ruler to capture Goa from the Bahmanis = **Harihara II** (BPSC 1995); minister **Irugappa** built the **Ganigitti Jain temple**, wrote **Navaratnamala**; rule over Kanara, Mysore, Kanchi, Trichinopoly; gave a daughter to **Kataya Vema's** son.
- **Tungabhadra dam:** **Devaraya I** (c. **1410**) built a large dam and a canal-cum-aqueduct about **24 km** long to the capital (UPSC 2023 ×2) – not Mallikarjuna, Vira Vijaya or Virupaksha; in the Sachivalayam version the only option naming him is "**Bukka I and Devaraya I**".
- **Devaraya I (1406-22):** first to **enlist Muslims** in the army; defeated at **Mudgal** in the **war of the goldsmith's daughter** (Nehal) with **Firuz Shah Bahmani** – gave his daughter and **Bankapur**; **Nicolo Conti** visited; fought the **Kandukuru Reddis**, allied with Kataya Vema and the Velamas; **Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam**; coronation copper plates with a **Vamana** seal (unveiled in Bengaluru, Apr 2025).

=== Vijayanagara–Bahmani Conflict
- **Main rival in the south:** the **Bahmani Sultans** (APPSC G1) – not Hoysala, Warangal or Madurai.
- **Bone of contention:** the **Raichur Doab (Raichur Antarvedi)**, between the **Krishna and Tungabhadra** (APPSC G2 2000) – not Mathura, Warangal or Malabar.
- **Conflict began under Harihara I:** the Bahmani kingdom was founded by **Hasan Gangu** at **Gulbarga in 1347**, in **Harihara I's** reign (Police Constable 2019; APPSC) – Mallikarjuna, Devaraya I and Virupaksha are later.
- **First war:** under **Bukka I** against **Muhammad Shah I** (1362-67; Mudgal, Kautalam, Adoni) – where both Harihara I and Bukka I are offered for "the **war** started", key **Bukka I** (APPSC); treaty to spare civilians.
| War | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Mudgal (1367) | Bukka I; called the first Indian war with artillery |
| Goa, Belgaum (1380) | Taken by Harihara II's general Madhava |
| Goldsmith's daughter | Devaraya I vs Firuz Shah; Bankapur as dowry |
| Devaraya II | Two wars: Mudgal, Raichur, Bijapur |
| Goa lost (**1472**) | To the Bahmanis |
| Raichur (**1520**), Gulbarga (1523) | Krishnadevaraya |
| Talikota (1565) | Deccan Sultanates defeated Rama Raya |
- Order: Mudgal 1367 → Goa lost 1472 → Gulbarga 1523 → Talikota 1565.

=== Saluva Narasimha and the Sangama Campaigns in Andhra
- **Saluva Narasimha:** lord of **Chandragiri**; clan from Kalyana; removed **Praudha Raya** (1485) with **Tuluva Narasa Nayaka** – **first usurpation**.
- **Court:** *Saluvabhyudayam* – **Rajanatha Dindima**; **Annamacharya** (contemporary, supported); **Pillalamarri Pina Veerabhadrudu** – *Jaimini Bharatam*, *Sringara Sakuntalam*.
- **Vinukonda Vallabharaya**, governor of Vinukonda under Devaraya II, wrote ***Kridabhiramam*** – describes **Kakatiya Warangal**.
- **Gangadevi** wrote Madhura Vijayam, **not** Jaimini Bharatam.
