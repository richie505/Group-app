=== NOTES 1:119 | Foundation of Vijayanagara – Harihara and Bukka, Vidyaranya; the Sangama Dynasty
## SUB 0 | Founding of Vijayanagara
[119:0:0] - Founded **1336** by brothers **Harihara I** and **Bukka I**, sons of **Sangama** → **Sangama dynasty** (family claimed **Yadava** (lunar) descent; also placed in the Kuruba clan).
[119:0:1] - **Vijayanagara = "City of Victory"**, on the **southern bank of the Tungabhadra** (tributary of the Krishna); ruins at **Hampi**.
[119:0:2] - **Coronation**: Harihara I crowned **18 April 1336** at the **Virupaksha** temple, after **Vidyaranya** of Sringeri reconverted the brothers to Hinduism.
[119:0:3] - **Founding legend**: a **hare turned on the royal hunting dogs** → site chosen as a place of valour.
[119:0:4] - Harihara I ruled as agent of the deity (Virupaksha) owning all land south of the Krishna.
[119:0:5] - **Emblem: Varaha (boar)**; Sangamas **Shaiva**, the later three dynasties **Vaishnava**.
[119:0:6] - Largest Hindu kingdom of medieval South India.
[119:0:7] # Founding FIRSTs
[119:0:8] TABLE head: FIRST | Fact
  [119:0:8.0] First seat | Anegondi – set up "in the name of Sri Virupaksha"
  [119:0:8.1] First record calling Harihara I king | Nellore inscription – dates his reign 1336–1355
  [119:0:8.2] City completed | 1343
  [119:0:8.3] First foreign traveller | Ibn Battuta (Harihara I's reign)
[119:0:9] - **Harihara I shifted the capital** from **Anegondi → Vijayanagara**.
[119:0:10] 1336 = founding + coronation (18 April); 1343 = city completed; 1346 = Sringeri visit after Hoysala victory
## SUB 1 | Origin Theories of Harihara and Bukka
[119:1:0] - **Robert Sewell** recorded **seven** local founding traditions.
[119:1:1] TABLE head: Theory | What it says | Backed by
  [119:1:1.0] Kakatiya service | Sangama a Kakatiya chief; brothers served Prataparudra of Warangal (treasurers); after 1323 taken to Delhi and converted | Local tradition
  [119:1:1.1] Kampili | Governed Kampili; captured by Muhammad bin Tughlaq, converted in Delhi, sent back as Sultanate governors | –
  [119:1:1.2] Delhi service | Sent by Tughlaq to crush Deccan rebels (Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka, Prolaya Vema); defeated, they met Vidyaranya | –
  [119:1:1.3] Kannada / Hoysala | Officers of Veera Ballala III | Father Heras, Rice, S.K. Ayyangar, H. Krishna Sastri, P.B. Desai
  [119:1:1.4] Andhra | Brothers were Andhras | N. Venkataramanaiah, Sewell, Nilakanta Sastri
[119:1:2] # The Delhi Legend – Sequence
[119:1:3] Fall of Warangal → conversion in Delhi → sent against Deccan rebels → defeat → met **Vidyaranya** near **Pampa** → return to Hinduism → founded Anegondi in the name of Virupaksha.
[119:1:4] APPSC G2 2005 key calls **Sangama** a **Yadava** (lunar line, as the inscriptions claim); the Kakatiya link is only one origin theory.
[119:1:5] *Kampili and Vijayanagara* is by **Nelaturi Venkataramanaiah**, not Sewell.

## SUB 3 | Four Dynasties of Vijayanagara
[119:3:0] TABLE head: Order | Dynasty | Span | Founder | Origin
  [119:3:0.0] 1 | Sangama | 1336–1485 | Harihara I and Bukka I | Andhra
  [119:3:0.1] 2 | Saluva | 1485–1505 | Saluva Narasimha – first usurpation | Karnataka (Kalyani)
  [119:3:0.2] 3 | Tuluva | 1505–1570 | Vira (Veera) Narasimha (APPSC G2 2002 key) | Karnataka (Tuluva)
  [119:3:0.3] 4 | Aravidu | after 1570 | Tirumala Raya | Andhra (Araviti, Kurnool)
[119:3:1] - **Sangama** = **longest-ruling** dynasty; greatest ruler **Devaraya II**; last ruler **Praudha Raya**.
[119:3:2] - Aravidu capitals: **Penukonda, Chandragiri, Vellore**.
[119:3:3] - **Tuluva Narasa Nayaka** = father of Vira Narasimha; Vira Narasimha crowned **1505** after executing **Immadi Narasimha**.
[119:3:4] - After Talikota: **Hampi → Penukonda → Chandragiri**.
[119:3:5] Order S-S-T-A = Sangama – Saluva – Tuluva – Aravidu; Andhra in origin: only **Sangama and Aravidu**.
[119:3:6] Odd one out: **Vishnukundins** – an older Andhra dynasty, not Vijayanagara.

## SUB 4 | Sangama Rulers – Harihara I to Devaraya I
[119:4:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Achievements
  [119:4:0.0] Harihara I (1336–56) | Annexed Hoysala (1346), Kadambas (1347); title Purva-Paschima Samudradhipati
  [119:4:0.1] Bukka I (1356–77) | Took Udayagiri (1343); completed the city; Madurai conquest; reconciled Jains and Vaishnavas (1368) (Shravanabelagola inscription; APPSC G2 2003); embassy to Ming China (1374); title Vedamarga Pratishthapaka
  [119:4:0.2] Harihara II (1377–1404) | Longest-reigning Sangama king; titles Rajavyasa, Rajavalmiki; first to attack Sri Lanka; took Goa and Belgaum (1380); ruled Kanara, Mysore, Kanchi, Trichinopoly; minister Irugappa built Ganigitti Jain temple, wrote *Navaratnamala*
  [119:4:0.3] Devaraya I (1406–22) | Tungabhadra dam, canals; first to appoint Muslims in the army (APPSC key); Nicolo Conti visited; Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam
[119:4:1] - **Apr 2025**: Devaraya I's **coronation copper plates** (Sanskrit, Kannada, Nagari) unveiled in **Bengaluru** – **Vamana seal**, not the usual Varaha.
[119:4:2] Harihara II = longest-reigning king; Sangama = longest-ruling dynasty; Devaraya II = greatest.
[119:4:3] # Bukka I – Southern Conquest and Embassy
[119:4:4] - **Kumara Kampana** (Kamparaya), son of Bukka I, overthrew **Sambhuvaraya** and destroyed the **Madurai Sultanate** (completed c. 1371).
[119:4:5] - General **Saluva Mangu** killed the Sultan → title **"Parapakshi Saluva"**.
[119:4:6] - Literary record: ***Madhura Vijayam*** (Sanskrit) by **Gangadevi**, Kampana's wife. ⚠ APPSC key (2010) gave **Madhuravani**.
[119:4:7] - Telugu poet **Nachana Somana** (*Uttara Harivamsam*) – patronised by the **first two Sangama kings**; granted Penchikaladinne agrahara.
## SUB 5 | Devaraya II (1422–46)
[119:5:0] - **Army**: hired **10,000 Muslim soldiers** (APPSC G2 2011); gave them **jagirs and a mosque**; kept a **Quran before his throne** so they could salute it; improved **cavalry and archery**.
[119:5:1] - **Titles**: **Gajabetekara** (elephant hunter; Gajabetekara coins), **Praudha Devaraya**, Ilanti Raikonda.
[119:5:2] - **Deeds**: took **Kondaveedu (1424)**; **Draksharama inscription (1444)** – pushed back the **Gajapatis**, sent Mallappa Odayar to help Rajahmundry.
[119:5:3] - **Culture**: wrote ***Mahanataka Sudhanidhi*** (Sanskrit); literary hall **Mutyala Sala**; honoured **Srinatha** (**kanakabhishekam** after he defeated **Dindima Bhatta**).
[119:5:4] - **Visitor**: **Abdur Razzaq**, Persian envoy of **Shah Rukh**, **1443** – called Vijayanagara rich and grand; open **diamond bazaars**, about **300 ports**, **taxation of prostitution**.
[119:5:5] Gajabetekara | Devaraya II | elephant hunter
Purva-Paschima Samudradhipati | Harihara I | lord of eastern and western seas
[119:5:6] **Mutyala Sala** = Devaraya II; **Bhuvana Vijayam** = Krishnadevaraya. Dakshina Samudradhipati = Lakkanna Dandanayaka.

## SUB 7 | Saluva Narasimha and Andhra Campaigns
[119:7:0] - **Lord of Chandragiri**; clan from Kalyana; ancestor Saluva Mangu.
[119:7:1] - **1485**: removed the weak **Praudha Raya** with help of **Tuluva Narasa Nayaka** → **first usurpation**; ruled about five years.
[119:7:2] - **Annamacharya (1408–1503)** – his contemporary, supported in his court (APPSC 2011).

[119:7:4] - **Pillalamarri Pinaveerabhadrudu:** also wrote **Sringara Sakuntalam** (Saluva era)
[119:7:5] # Sangama Campaigns in Andhra
[119:7:6] - **Bukka I**: **Srisailam** and **Dupadu** (near Markapur); Udayagiri (1343).
[119:7:7] - **Harihara II**: pushed to the Krishna with the **Reddis**; gave a daughter to Kataya Vema's son.
[119:7:8] - **Devaraya I**: fought the **Kandukuru Reddis**; allied with the **Velamas** and Kataya Vema; took southern Kondaveedu with **Motupalli**.
[119:7:9] - **Devaraya II**: Kondaveedu (1424).
[119:7:10] - **Vinukonda Vallabharaya**, governor of Vinukonda under Devaraya II, wrote ***Kridabhiramam*** – describes **Kakatiya Warangal**; character **Machaladevi** (courtesan).
## SUB 8 | Vijayanagara-bahmani Conflicts
[119:8:0] - **Raichur Doab (Raichur Antarvedi)** – fertile land between **Krishna and Tungabhadra** – **main cause** (APPSC G2 2000, 2003); aim = territory.
[119:8:1] - Bahmani kingdom founded **1347** in Harihara I's reign; clashes over the Doab began under **Harihara I**, the first major war (**1367**) under **Bukka I**; wars lasted two centuries.
[119:8:2] TABLE head: War | Outcome
  [119:8:2.0] Mudgal (1367) | Taken by Bukka I; first Indian war with artillery
  [119:8:2.1] Goa, Belgaum (1380) | Taken by Harihara II's forces
  [119:8:2.2] War of the Goldsmith's Daughter | Devaraya I vs Firuz Shah, over Nehal of Mudgal; Devaraya gave his daughter and Bankapur as dowry
  [119:8:2.3] Devaraya II | Two wars over Mudgal, Raichur, Bijapur
  [119:8:2.4] Goa lost (1472) | To the Bahmanis
  [119:8:2.5] Raichur (1520) | Retaken by Krishnadevaraya (Gobburi war) with Portuguese help; Gulbarga 1523
  [119:8:2.6] Talikota (1565) | Deccan sultanates defeated Rama Raya
