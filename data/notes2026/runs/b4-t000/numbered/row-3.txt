## SUB 0 | Longitude and Local Time – Calculations
[0:0] # The rule (asked most: 23 PYQs)
[0:1] - Earth turns **360° in 24 h**, **west to east** → **15° = 1 hour**, **1° = 4 minutes**, **1' of arc = 4 seconds**.
[0:2] TABLE head: Longitude gap | Time gap
  [0:2.0] 1° | 4 minutes
  [0:2.1] 15° | 1 hour (60 min)
  [0:2.2] 90° | 6 hours
  [0:2.3] 180° | 12 hours (720 min)
  [0:2.4] 1' of arc | 4 seconds
[0:3] - **East is ahead (add), west is behind (subtract)**; moving westward local time **falls behind**. A place 30° west of Greenwich is **2 hours behind** GMT; the 15°E meridian is **1 hour fast**.
[0:4] - **Same side** of Greenwich → subtract the longitudes; **opposite sides** → add them (25°E and 35°W = 60° = **4 h**; 6°W and 9°E = 15° = 1 h).
[0:5] - **Same meridian = same local time**, however far apart north–south (NDA 2008: four places 500 km apart on one meridian all show **12:00 noon**).
[0:6] - Journey/message: arrival local time = departure + travel time ± longitude time. Flight 10:00 a.m., 3 h, 15° east → **2:00 p.m.**; telegram from Greenwich at noon, 12 min in transit, arrives 6:00 p.m. → 5 h 48 min = 348 min ÷ 4 = **87°E**.
[0:7] # Worked PYQs
[0:8] TABLE head: Given | Answer
  [0:8.0] A 6°W at 10 a.m. → B 9°E (APPSC G1 2023) | 15° = 1 h → 11 a.m.
  [0:8.1] A 60°W at 10 a.m. → B 90°E (APPSC G1 2019) | 150° = 10 h → 8 p.m.
  [0:8.2] 3:40 p.m. GMT → 10°W, West Africa (APPSC FSO) | 40 min behind → 3:00 p.m.
  [0:8.3] Noon GMT, place at 6 a.m. (UPSC 1998) | 90°W
  [0:8.4] Noon at IST meridian, place at 6 a.m. (IAS 1998) | 82°30'E − 90° = 7°30'W
  [0:8.5] Noon at 82°30'E, place at 6:30 a.m. (IAS 1994) | 5½ h = 82°30' west → 0°
  [0:8.6] Midnight IST, place at 6 a.m. (UPPCS 2002) | 82°30' + 90° = 172°30'E
  [0:8.7] Noon IST → 120°E (UPPCS 2001) | 37°30' × 4 = 150 min → 14:30
  [0:8.8] 10:00 a.m. IST → Shillong 92°E (IAS 1999) | 9.5° × 4 = 38 min → 10:38 a.m.
  [0:8.9] Greenwich noon → 5 p.m. local (UPPCS 2008) | 75°E
  [0:8.10] GMT 3 a.m., local 6 a.m. | 45°E
  [0:8.11] Cairo 2 h ahead of Greenwich | 30°E
  [0:8.12] Thimphu 90°E at Greenwich noon | 6:00 p.m.
  [0:8.13] Sunrise Tirap (Arunachal) 5 a.m. → Kandla (Gujarat, ~70°E) | ~25° ≈ 1 h 40 min → about 7:00 a.m. (nearest option)
[0:9] - **Ship** sailing west at the speed of rotation, 10:00 Monday at 90°W: it keeps pace with the Sun, so at the Date Line it is still **10:00 Monday** (UPPCS 2000).
[0:10] - **Rotation reversed** (UPSC 1997, asked twice): normally IST is 5½ h ahead of GMT and the 180°W side 12 h behind; reversed, IST would be 17½ h behind the Date Line → noon there = **18:30 hrs IST**.
[0:11] > Calculation angle: find the gap, × 4 min, then add (east) or subtract (west). Check the side of Greenwich first.

## SUB 1 | Indian Standard Time and the Standard Meridian
[1:0] # IST (asked 21 times)
[1:1] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [1:1.0] Standard meridian | 82°30'E (82.5°E) – India's central meridian
  [1:1.1] IST vs GMT | GMT + 5 h 30 min (82.5 × 4 = 330 min), i.e. 5½ h ahead; 0000 UTC = 5:30 a.m. IST
  [1:1.2] Reference town | Mirzapur, UP (clock tower); the meridian passes just east of Prayagraj (Allahabad – Naini) – the town given in the keys
  [1:1.3] City closest (MPPSC 2008) | Rewa (MP, ~81°18'E)
  [1:1.4] States crossed, W→E | Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh – 5; not West Bengal (APPSC); last state = Andhra Pradesh (Kakinada district)
  [1:1.5] Meets the Tropic of Cancer | Koriya district, Chhattisgarh, on the Baghelkhand Plateau
  [1:1.6] Keeper | CSIR-National Physical Laboratory, New Delhi
[1:2] - **History:** India's time zones were set in **1884** – **Bombay Time** (GMT + 4 h 51 min) and **Calcutta Time** (GMT + 5 h 53 min 20 s); a single IST was adopted after Independence (**1947**; Calcutta Time lasted till 1948, Bombay Time till 1955). Calling Calcutta Time "4 h 51 min" and Bombay "5 h 30 min" is the false statement (APPSC flashcard).
[1:3] - **Basis of standard time:** a country's standard time is the local time of a **chosen longitude** (usually its central meridian), counted from Greenwich.
[1:4] - **City longitudes:** Delhi ~77°12'E – closest among the options is **Bengaluru** (~77°35'E); Hyderabad ~78°29'E, Nagpur ~79°05'E, Pune ~73°51'E (UPSC 2018).
[1:5] # India's span and the two-time-zone debate
[1:6] - India: **68°7'E (Guhar Moti, Kutch)** to **97°25'E** (Arunachal; easternmost between 97°25' and 97°30'E) → ~29° ≈ 116 min → **about 2 hours** between Arunachal and Gujarat (not 2½) (NDA 2017; Jharkhand 2023).
[1:7] - Latitudes **8°4'N** (mainland, Kanyakumari) to **37°6'N** (Indira Col); both extents **about 30°**. Older texts quote **8°4'28"N to 37°17'53"N**.
[1:8] - North-East loses daylight hours; proposals: advance IST by **30 min**, or a second zone ~1 h ahead. Assam tea gardens kept an informal **"Bagan time" (Chaibagaan time)**, 1 h ahead of IST – not official.
[1:9] - Andhra Pradesh: **12°41'N – 19°07'N**, ~77°E – 84°40'E.
[1:10] # IST in 2026 (current linkage)
[1:11] - **Jul 2026:** **White Rabbit** IST distribution network at the Regional Reference Standards Laboratory, **Jakkur (Bengaluru)** – sub-nanosecond sync over fibre, independent of GPS; the protocol was developed at **CERN** (not ISRO); "One Nation, One Time" (NPL–ISRO). It provides synchronised high-precision IST time-stamps for banking, payments, railways, airports, power grids and telecom, and guards against GPS spoofing.
[1:12] - **27 Aug 2026:** Consumer Affairs Ministry notified the **Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026** – IST the single legal reference; in force **180 days** after Gazette publication (not immediately).
[1:13] > Trap: IST line misses **West Bengal**; 8 states on the Tropic vs 5 on the IST line, **Chhattisgarh** is on both.

## SUB 2 | Tropic of Cancer and India
[2:0] # Tropic of Cancer (asked 18 times)
[2:1] - At **23°26'N** (written **23½°N / 23°30'N**); divides India into **almost two equal halves** (not the international boundary).
[2:2] - Crosses **8 states**, W→E: **Gujarat** (1st), **Rajasthan**, **Madhya Pradesh**, **Chhattisgarh**, **Jharkhand**, **West Bengal**, **Tripura**, **Mizoram** (last).
[2:3] - **Not** crossed: **Odisha** (asked 4 times), **Uttar Pradesh**, **Bihar**, Himachal, J&K, Assam, AP. Old keys write "Bihar (Jharkhand)" – Jharkhand was then part of Bihar.
[2:4] - Wholly **north**: UP, Bihar, Punjab, Haryana, Himachal, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Assam, Meghalaya, Arunachal, Nagaland, **Manipur**. **Andhra Pradesh lies wholly south**.
[2:5] - The line joining India's **easternmost and westernmost points lies north** of the Tropic; the maximum east–west extent is **not** along the Tropic (Jharkhand 2023: I and IV).
[2:6] TABLE head: City | Latitude
  [2:6.0] Gandhinagar (nearest, UPPCS 1999) | 23°13'N
  [2:6.1] Jabalpur (nearest, UPPCS 2017) | 23°11'N
  [2:6.2] Ujjain | 23°9'N
  [2:6.3] Ahmedabad | 23°2'N
  [2:6.4] Agartala | 23°50'N
  [2:6.5] Kolkata (nearest of Delhi, Jodhpur, Nagpur – IAS 2003) | 22°34'N
[2:7] - Other cities near it: Bhopal and Ujjain (MP), **Ranchi (Jharkhand)**, Agartala (Tripura), Aizawl (Mizoram).
[2:8] - **Mahi** crosses it **twice**: rises in MP, flows through Rajasthan and Gujarat into the **Gulf of Khambhat**.
[2:9] - World: crosses **17 countries** (Mexico, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Oman, India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, China …) – **not Iran**. **Equator** passes Kenya, Colombia, **Indonesia**.
[2:10] - Globe: **179 parallels** and **360 meridians** at 1° interval.
[2:11] > NOT-angle: "Tropic does not pass through" → Odisha / UP; "lies north of the Tropic" → Manipur.

## SUB 3 | Time Zones, GMT and UTC
[3:0] # Time terms
[3:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:1.0] GMT | Mean solar time at the Royal Observatory, Greenwich (0°); Prime Meridian adopted at the International Meridian Conference, 1884
  [3:1.1] UTC | World time kept by caesium atomic clocks; kept in step with the slowing Earth by leap seconds
  [3:1.2] Leap second | Added (e.g. 31 Dec 2016, just before midnight) because the Earth's rotation is slowing
  [3:1.3] Daylight Saving Time | Clocks advanced 1 h in summer to use evening light; India does not use it
  [3:1.4] Natural units | Day (rotation), month (Moon's revolution), year (Earth's revolution)
[3:2] - **NavIC** (7 satellites: 3 GEO + 4 GSO) times its signals with **rubidium** atomic clocks.
[3:3] # Zones
[3:4] - **24 theoretical zones** of 15° each; more in practice (half- and quarter-hour offsets).
[3:5] TABLE head: Country / city | Fact
  [3:5.0] France (with overseas territories) | 12 zones – most
  [3:5.1] Russia | 11
  [3:5.2] UK 9, Australia 9, Denmark 5, New Zealand 5 (with territories) | More than four (UPSC 2025: only four of five)
  [3:5.3] Brazil | Exactly 4
  [3:5.4] China | One zone despite its width
  [3:5.5] Colombo (Sri Lanka) | Same clock as Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai (UTC+5:30)
  [3:5.6] Nepal | UTC+5:45
  [3:5.7] Accra, Dublin, Lisbon, London | GMT; not Madrid (CET) or Addis Ababa (UTC+3)
[3:6] - Ahead → behind GMT (UPSC 1995, asked twice): **Japan (+9) > Iraq (+3) > Greece (+2) > Cuba (−5) > Costa Rica (−6)**.

## SUB 4 | International Date Line
[4:0] - Imaginary **pole-to-pole** line roughly along **180°** in the **middle of the Pacific Ocean** (APPSC, State PSC); **12 hours** (720 min) from Greenwich because it is the 180° meridian (A and R true, R explains A).
[4:1] - Nearest strait: **Bering Strait** (UPSC 2008, asked twice) – between the **Chukchi** (Russia) and **Seward** (Alaska) peninsulas.
[4:2] - **Zigzags** where **land is in the way**: Chukotka, Aleutians, Kiribati, Fiji, Tonga, Samoa.
[4:3] TABLE head: Crossing | Date change
  [4:3.0] Eastward (Asia → America) | Subtract a day (gain a day)
  [4:3.1] Westward (America → Asia) | Add a day (lose a day)
[4:4] - **Big Diomede (Russia) and Little Diomede (USA)** are neighbours on opposite sides, a day apart.
[4:5] - **Samoa** moved to the Asian side in **2011**; **Kiribati's Line Islands** (UTC+14) are first to see a new day.
[4:6] - Old UPPCS 1992 key: a ship's least-time path is "along the meridian" – strictly the shortest route is a **great-circle** route (meridians are great circles).
[4:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Indian Geography #47 p.119; PMF Physical Geography 03 p.50-52; app notes 4:3 sub 5
