## SUB 0 | Hydrogen as a Fuel – Basic Facts
[0:0] - **Cleanest fuel:** burning hydrogen gives **only water** – least / minimum air pollution (coal, kerosene, diesel, petrol give CO2 and harmful gases; CNG and power alcohol still give CO2).
[0:1] - **Highest fuel (calorific) value:** about **150,000 kJ/kg** – above natural gas, gasoline, charcoal.
[0:2] - Burns in air at about **4% to 75%** by volume.
[0:3] - **Rocket fuel:** liquid hydrogen (**dihydrogen**, H2) with liquid oxygen in cryogenic stages – not hydrogen peroxide, heavy water or CFCs.
[0:4] - **Storage for vehicles:** hydrogen absorbed at low temperature by a **metal hydride** (e.g. palladium hydride) and released by exhaust heat.
[0:5] - Hydrogen is also the **most abundant element in the Universe** (~3/4 of matter) and the fuel of the Sun's fusion reactions.
[0:6] > Least pollution / highest fuel value / burning gives water – the classic hydrogen-fuel PYQ.
[0:7] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 10th ed. p.354 (Ch22.10 fuel cells); app notes 5:68 sub 0, sub 6
## SUB 1 | Applications of Green Hydrogen
[1:0] - **Uses of green hydrogen (UPSC 2023 – all three correct):** **directly as a fuel in internal combustion** engines (H2-ICE), **blended with natural gas** for heat or power, and in **hydrogen fuel cells** to run vehicles.
[1:1] - **Decarbonises fertiliser plants (ammonia), oil refineries and steel plants** (UPSC 2023 – all three) – **hard-to-abate** sectors where direct electrification is difficult.
[1:2] - **Industry:** refining (**desulphurisation**), **ammonia for fertilisers**, methanol, **steel by DRI** (hydrogen replaces **coking coal**).
[1:3] - **Fuel uses:** fuel-cell vehicles (buses, trucks, trains, ships), rocket fuel, **H-CNG** (hydrogen blended with CNG).
[1:4] - **Green ammonia** = nitrogen + green hydrogen; fertiliser, fuel, marine fuel.
[1:5] - **India's first green hydrogen blending project:** **NTPC + Gujarat Gas Ltd**, **Kawas, Surat** (Jan 2023), blending into the PNG network; PNGRB allowed 5% blending at start, scaling to 20%.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 10th ed. p.355 (Ch22.10.1); PMF Environment Part 7 p.100-103; app notes 5:68 sub 7
## SUB 2 | Colours of Hydrogen
[2:0] TABLE head: Colour | Production route | CO2
  [2:0.0] Green | Electrolysis of water with renewable power | Zero – only zero-carbon colour
  [2:0.1] Grey | Steam methane reforming (SMR) of natural gas | High; ~95% of production; cheapest
  [2:0.2] Blue | SMR + carbon capture and storage (CCS) | Low
  [2:0.3] Pink / purple | Electrolysis with nuclear power | Near-zero
  [2:0.4] Turquoise | Methane pyrolysis → solid carbon | Near-zero
  [2:0.5] Brown / black | Coal gasification without CCS | Very high
  [2:0.6] White | Naturally occurring geological hydrogen | –
[2:1] - **SMR:** methane + steam at **700–1000°C**, **nickel catalyst** → hydrogen + CO.
[2:2] - Cost (Jul 2026): grey **$1.5–2/kg** vs green **~$4/kg**.
[2:3] - "Green" means the **production route (renewable energy)** – not sea water, not coal, not a green-coloured gas.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 10th ed. p.356 (Ch22.10.2); app notes 5:68 sub 1
## SUB 3 | Fuel Cells
[3:0] - **Fuel cell** = electrochemical device: **H2 + O2 → electricity (DC) + water + heat**; no combustion; efficiency up to ~55%.
[3:1] - With pure hydrogen the only by-products are **heat and water**; FCEV exhaust = **water vapour** (and warm air) – not ammonia, nitrogen, oxygen or hydrogen peroxide.
[3:2] - Fuel cells power buildings **and** small devices (laptops); they give **DC, not AC** (UPSC 2015: only statement 1 correct).
[3:3] - A fuel cell combines hydrogen with **oxygen**, not carbon dioxide.
[3:4] # PEM fuel cell steps
[3:5] - 1. **Anode:** H2 → 2H+ + 2e-; electrons flow through the external circuit.
[3:6] - 2. **Protons cross the membrane**; **cathode:** 1/2 O2 + 2H+ + 2e- → H2O + heat.
[3:7] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 10th ed. p.354 (Ch22.10); app notes 5:68 sub 5

[5:0] - Approved by **Union Cabinet, 4 January 2023**; nodal ministry **MNRE**; outlay **₹19,744 crore** up to **2029-30**.
[5:1] TABLE head: Component | Role | Outlay
  [5:1.0] SIGHT | Incentives for electrolyser manufacturing + green H2 production | ₹17,490 cr
  [5:1.1] Pilot projects | Steel, mobility, shipping, storage, biomass hydrogen | ₹1,466 cr
  [5:1.2] SHIP (Strategic Hydrogen Innovation Partnership) | PPP for R&D | ₹400 cr
  [5:1.3] Green Hydrogen Hubs | Production-use-export clusters; 4 Hydrogen Valley Innovation Clusters | –
[5:2] - **2030 targets:** **5 MMT/year** green hydrogen; **125 GW** added RE; **60–100 GW electrolysers** (not 200 GW); **6 lakh jobs**; ₹8 lakh crore investment; **50 MMT/yr GHG** cut.
[5:3] - Goals: **energy independence 2047**, **net zero 2070**.
[5:4] - Progress: **15 companies**, 3,000 MW electrolyser manufacturing; green ammonia 7,24,000 TPA at **₹53.27/kg** (SECI); **Japanese** firms signed green ammonia/methanol export deals (Jul 2026).

## SUB 6 | Electrolysers, Standard, Certification and Storage
[6:0] - **Electrolyser** = uses **electricity to split water** into hydrogen and oxygen.
[6:1] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [6:1.0] Alkaline | Liquid KOH/NaOH electrolyte; oldest, mature
  [6:1.1] PEM | Solid polymer membrane; compact; quick response suits variable RE
  [6:1.2] Solid oxide | High-temperature steam; most efficient
  [6:1.3] AEM | Can use non-platinum catalysts (nickel, cobalt, iron)
[6:2] - Concern: **Chinese dominance** of electrolyser supply; dependence on imported electrolysers.
[6:3] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [6:3.0] Green Hydrogen Standard (MNRE, Aug 2023) | Well-to-gate ≤ 2 kg CO2-eq per kg H2 (12-month average)
  [6:3.1] GHCI certification scheme | April 2025
  [6:3.2] Certification nodal authority | Bureau of Energy Efficiency
  [6:3.3] Storage method | Fact
  [6:3.4] Compression | High-pressure composite cylinders
  [6:3.5] Liquefaction | −253°C (cryogenic)
  [6:3.6] Metal hydrides | Solid-state storage; absorbed in alloys, released on heating
  [6:3.7] Chemical carriers | Ammonia, methanol, LOHC (not CO2)
[6:4] - Water need: **~9 litres** purified water per kg hydrogen.

## SUB 7 | Andhra Pradesh – Green Hydrogen Policy and Valley
[7:0] - **AP Green Hydrogen and Green Ammonia Policy (2023):** target **0.5 MTPA green hydrogen or 2.0 MTPA green ammonia** in five years (not 1 MTPA); nodal agency **NREDCAP**; RE may be sourced via open access, captive, **third-party sale and power exchange** (allowed, not prohibited); 100% SGST reimbursement for 5 years.
