=== NOTES 5:67 | Other Renewables – Biomass, Biofuels, Small Hydro, Geothermal, Tidal, Waste-to-energy
## SUB 0 | Mnre Classification of Renewable Sources
[67:0:0] - **MNRE renewables:** solar, wind, **bio-power** (biomass, bagasse cogeneration, waste-to-energy), **small hydro** and **large hydro** (counted as renewable **since 2019**).
[67:0:1] - Under development: **geothermal, tidal, OTEC**.
[67:0:2] - **Nuclear** = non-fossil but **not renewable**; counted separately (8.78 GW).
[67:0:3] - Significance: geothermal gives **24x7 baseload** (underground heat does not depend on sunlight or wind); bioenergy links waste management, rural income and energy security.
[67:0:4] Large hydro is renewable (since 2019); nuclear is non-fossil but NOT renewable.
## SUB 1 | Biomass and Biogas
[67:1:0] # Biomass Energy
[67:1:1] - **Biomass energy** = solar energy stored by plants through **photosynthesis**; burned directly or converted to biogas/biofuels.
[67:1:2] - **Gobar gas = biomass energy** (from cattle dung) (APPSC 2019).
[67:1:3] - Biomass from waste: agricultural waste (stubble, paddy husk, manure, dairy waste), **food-processing residues, human sewage, biogenic municipal solid waste** (all three are biomass).
[67:1:4] # Biogas
[67:1:5] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [67:1:5.0] Composition | Methane (~50–75%) + CO2 + traces (piped natural gas is 85–95% methane)
  [67:1:5.1] Process | Anaerobic digestion / fermentation of organic matter
  [67:1:5.2] Microbes | Methanogens (methanogenic archaea/bacteria), in absence of oxygen
  [67:1:5.3] By-product | Slurry = good organic manure
  [67:1:5.4] Gobar gas / cooking gas | Biogas from cattle dung; main component methane
[67:1:6] - Pioneer: floating-drum **"Gramalakshmi"** plant of **Jashbhai Patel (1950s)**, spread by **KVIC**.
[67:1:7] - **CBG (Compressed Biogas)** = purified, compressed biogas, chemically identical to **CNG**; renewable, carbon-neutral.
[67:1:8] - **Bagasse cogeneration:** sugar mills burn bagasse → steam + power together.
[67:1:9] # Gasification and Microbial Fuel Cells
[67:1:10] - **Biomass gasification:** heating with **limited (controlled) oxygen** → syngas (**CO, H2, CO2**) for engines/power.
[67:1:11] - **Producer gas** = CO + H2 + CO2 + methane.
[67:1:12] - **Microbial Fuel Cell (MFC):** microbes act as **catalysts**, oxidise organic matter and pass electrons to an electrode; **cleans wastewater and makes electricity together**.
## SUB 2 | Biofuel Generations and Algal Fuels
[67:2:0] TABLE head: Generation | Feedstock | Example
  [67:2:0.0] 1G | Food crops: sugarcane, molasses, corn, cassava, vegetable oils | Bioethanol (yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae), biodiesel
  [67:2:0.1] 2G | Non-food lignocellulosic residue: rice straw, husk, wood chips, bamboo, non-edible oils | Cellulosic ethanol
  [67:2:0.2] 3G | Algae / algal biomass (APPSC 2023) | Algal biodiesel, biobutanol
  [67:2:0.3] 4G | Genetically modified algae, yeast, fungi, cyanobacteria | Carbon-negative fuels
[67:2:1] - **Biobutanol** (Clostridium acetobutylicum): higher energy density than ethanol.
[67:2:2] - 1G = food-fuel conflict; 2G avoids it but needs complex conversion.
[67:2:3] - **Algal biofuel advantages:** high yield per area; uses wastewater and CO2; grows on land unsuitable for agriculture and also on land (ponds, lakes, tanks), not only seas.
[67:2:4] - **Limitations:** high cultivation/harvest cost, **energy-intensive lipid extraction**, contamination, scale-up; needs high expertise, capital and large-scale facilities (a drawback for developing countries); not cost-effective.
[67:2:5] 3G = algae; 4G = genetically modified algae/microbes.
## SUB 3 | Biodiesel, Gasohol and Power Alcohol
[67:3:0] # Terms
[67:3:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [67:3:1.0] Biodiesel | Methyl/ethyl esters of fatty acids by transesterification of vegetable oils/animal fats with methanol or ethanol
  [67:3:1.1] Gasohol | Petrol (gasoline) + ethanol, typically ~10% ethanol
  [67:3:1.2] Power alcohol | Fuel-grade (anhydrous) ethanol, mainly from molasses (sugar-industry by-product)
  [67:3:1.3] Bioethanol | Fermentation of sugar/starch by yeast
[67:3:2] # Feedstock Plants
[67:3:3] TABLE head: Crop | Fact
  [67:3:3.0] Jatropha curcas (ratanjyot) | Biodiesel plant (APPSC 2019); seeds ~40% oil; non-edible; grows in harsh conditions
  [67:3:3.1] Pongamia pinnata (Karanja) | Biodiesel plant; non-edible oil rich in oleic acid
  [67:3:3.2] Mahua, neem, sunflower | Other biofuel sources
  [67:3:3.3] Sugarcane | Ethanol from fermented juice and molasses
  [67:3:3.4] Maize | Starch-rich energy crop for ethanol
[67:3:4] - India has **400+** tree species with non-edible oilseeds.
[67:3:5] - **Palm stearin oil** = feedstock of about **94%** of India's biodiesel (mostly imported), not jatropha.
[67:3:6] - **RUCO** (FSSAI) = Repurpose Used Cooking Oil for biodiesel.
[67:3:7] - Others: rapeseed, soybean, animal tallow, acid oil, algae.

## SUB 4 | National Policy on Biofuels
[67:4:0] # NPB 2018
[67:4:1] - Aim: cut import dependence, domestic feedstock, energy security, jobs; does **not** discourage advanced biofuels.
[67:4:2] - Targets: **E20 + B5 by 2030**.
[67:4:3] TABLE head: Category | Fuels
  [67:4:3.0] Basic biofuels | 1G bioethanol, biodiesel
  [67:4:3.1] Advanced biofuels | 2G ethanol, drop-in fuels from MSW, 3G biofuels, bio-CNG
[67:4:4] - Ethanol feedstock widened beyond molasses: **sugarcane juice, B-molasses, surplus FCI rice, maize, damaged foodgrains (wheat), cassava, rotten potatoes, sugar beet**, grasses, agri residues (rice straw, cotton stalk, corn cobs, saw dust, bagasse).
[67:4:5] - Biodiesel feedstock: non-food oilseeds (jatropha, pongamia) + algae.
[67:4:6] - **Surplus food grains** for ethanol only with **National Biofuel Coordination Committee (NBCC)** approval (APPSC-G2 2025).
[67:4:7] - **NBCC** is chaired by the **Minister of Petroleum and Natural Gas**; it approved surplus FCI rice (April 2020) and surplus maize (November 2020).
[67:4:8] # NPB 2022 Amendment
[67:4:9] TABLE head: Change | Detail
  [67:4:9.0] E20 target | Advanced from 2030 to ESY 2025-26
  [67:4:9.1] Feedstock | More feedstocks allowed
  [67:4:9.2] Advanced fuels | Boost to 2G/advanced biofuels
  [67:4:9.3] NBCC | New members may be added
[67:4:10] # Enablers
[67:4:11] - **Motor Vehicles Act, Section 52**: engine conversion to biofuels.
[67:4:12] - **100% FDI (automatic)** for biofuel technology for domestic use; plantations barred.
[67:4:13] - VGF, tax credits, differential pricing for advanced biofuels.
[67:4:14] - **NPB 2022 amendment – more changes:** it also allowed biofuel production in **SEZs and EOUs** under **Make in India**, and allowed **export of biofuels** in specific cases.
## SUB 5 | Ethanol Blending Programme
[67:5:0] # Milestones
[67:5:1] TABLE head: Milestone | Date
  [67:5:1.0] Pilot | 2001
  [67:5:1.1] E5 in 9 States + 4 UTs | 1 January 2003
  [67:5:1.2] E10 achieved | June 2022 (ahead of Nov 2022 target)
  [67:5:1.3] E20 mandatory (minimum RON 95) | 1 April 2026
  [67:5:1.4] BIS IS 19850:2026 (E22, E25, E27, E30) | May 2026
  [67:5:1.5] Next goal E30 | 2030
[67:5:2] # Pricing and Gains
[67:5:3] - EBP run by **MoPNG**; OMCs pay feedstock-wise prices: **maize > molasses > rice**; maize price CAGR 11.7% (FY22-25).
[67:5:4] - Sugar mills may now make ethanol directly from cane juice; **Ethanol Interest Subvention Scheme** funds distillery capacity.
[67:5:5] - Debates: food vs fuel (sugar prices, Sep 2026); maize-ethanol pulled land from **pulses and oilseeds**; 1–2% mileage loss with E20 (NITI roadmap 2021).
[67:5:6] # Global Producers
[67:5:7] - **USA** (maize) = world's **largest producer and exporter** of fuel ethanol; **Brazil** (sugarcane) second; together ~80% of world ethanol (UPSC 2025).
[67:5:8] - **Brazil:** mandatory blending since **1976**; ~25% ethanol since 2007.
## SUB 6 | Aviation Biofuels and Global Biofuels Alliance
[67:6:0] - **First biofuel flight in India:** **SpiceJet**, **Dehradun–Delhi, 27 August 2018** (Bombardier Q-400), jatropha biojet from **CSIR-IIP**.
[67:6:1] - **SAF targets** (international flights): **1% by 2027, 2% by 2028, 5% by 2030**; aligned with ICAO net zero 2050.
[67:6:2] - SAF = **drop-in** synthesised hydrocarbons (not direct ethanol), blendable up to **50%** with jet fuel.
[67:6:3] - SAF feedstocks: **agri residues, corn grain, wastewater sludge, wood-mill waste** (all four, UPSC 2024).
[67:6:4] - **IOCL Panipat** = first Indian ISCC CORSIA-certified SAF producer (Aug 2025); Air India-IOCL SAF MoU.
[67:6:5] - **BioE3 Policy 2024** backs bio-jet fuel.
[67:6:6] - **Global Biofuels Alliance (GBA):** launched **9 September 2023** at the **G20 Summit, New Delhi**; core founders **India, USA, Brazil**; Secretariat in **New Delhi** (host agreement Oct 2024).

## SUB 9 | Geothermal Energy
[67:9:0] - Source: **Earth's internal heat**, largely from **radioactive decay**; surface signs: hot springs, geysers, steam, **volcanoes** (APPSC 2018).
[67:9:1] - Potential: **~10,600 MW from 381 hot springs** (GSI).
[67:9:2] - Provinces: **Himalaya, Godavari valley, Cambay, SONATA, Mahanadi, West Coast, Andaman-Nicobar** (not Indo-Gangetic Plain / Ganga Delta).
[67:9:3] TABLE head: Field | State / UT
  [67:9:3.0] Puga Valley, Chumathang | Ladakh – highest potential
  [67:9:3.1] Tattapani | Chhattisgarh
  [67:9:3.2] Manikaran | Himachal Pradesh
  [67:9:3.3] Tapovan | Uttarakhand
  [67:9:3.4] Surajkund | Jharkhand
  [67:9:3.5] Manuguru (old Khammam), Godavari valley | Telangana (earlier AP)
[67:9:4] - **India's first geothermal project: Puga, Ladakh** (ONGC; APPSC 2021); world's highest (~14,000 ft); ONGC MoU extended 5 years (26 May 2026).
[67:9:5] - **World's first geothermal power: Larderello, Italy (1904)**.
[67:9:6] - **National Policy on Geothermal Energy** – India's first, MNRE, **15 September 2025**; **five pilot projects**; Net Zero 2070.
[67:9:7] - **EGS** = artificially stimulated reservoirs. Challenges: uneven reservoirs, high drilling cost, subsidence, water pollution.

=== NOTES 5:68 | Green Hydrogen – National Green Hydrogen Mission, Electrolysers, Applications
## SUB 0 | Hydrogen – Basic Facts
[68:0:0] - **Hydrogen** = most common element in the Universe (about **3/4 of matter**; helium most of the rest).
[68:0:1] - **Highest fuel value** (calorific value, kJ/kg); fuel with **minimum air pollution** – burning gives **only water** (coal, kerosene, diesel give CO2 and harmful gases).
[68:0:2] - Burns in air at about **4% to 75%** by volume.
[68:0:3] - **Ammonia (NH3)** = nitrogen + hydrogen.
[68:0:4] - Exam-key facts: chemical elements on Earth ~**250**; diamond = purest carbon; sand = silicon + oxygen; marble = calcium, carbon, oxygen; sugar = carbon, hydrogen, oxygen.
## SUB 1 | Colours of Hydrogen
[68:1:0] TABLE head: Colour | Production route | CO2
  [68:1:0.0] Green | Electrolysis of water with renewable power (solar, wind, hydro) | Zero – only zero-carbon colour
  [68:1:0.1] Grey | Steam methane reforming (SMR) of natural gas | High; ~95% of production; cheapest
  [68:1:0.2] Blue | SMR + carbon capture and storage (CCS) | Low; transitional
  [68:1:0.3] Pink / purple | Electrolysis with nuclear power | Near-zero
  [68:1:0.4] Turquoise | Methane pyrolysis (no oxygen) → solid carbon | Near-zero
  [68:1:0.5] Brown / black | Coal gasification without CCS | Very high – dirtiest
  [68:1:0.6] White | Naturally occurring geological hydrogen | –
[68:1:1] - **SMR:** methane + steam at **700–1000°C**, **nickel catalyst** → hydrogen + CO (syngas).
[68:1:2] - Cost (Jul 2026): grey **$1.5–2/kg** vs green **~$4/kg**.

## SUB 4 | Electrolysers
[68:4:0] - **Electrolyser** = device that uses **electricity to split water** into hydrogen and oxygen.
[68:4:1] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [68:4:1.0] Alkaline | Liquid electrolyte of concentrated KOH/NaOH; oldest, mature route
  [68:4:1.1] PEM | Solid polymer membrane; compact; quick response → suits variable RE
  [68:4:1.2] Solid oxide | High-temperature steam; most efficient
  [68:4:1.3] AEM (anion exchange membrane) | Pure water or dilute alkali; can use non-platinum catalysts (nickel, cobalt, iron)
[68:4:2] - Concern: **Chinese dominance** of electrolyser supply.
## SUB 5 | Fuel Cells
[68:5:0] - **Fuel cell** = electrochemical device: **H2 + O2 → electricity + water**; high efficiency; **zero emissions** at point of use; hydrogen is not burnt.
[68:5:1] # PEM Fuel Cell Steps
[68:5:2] - 1. **Anode:** H2 → 2H+ + 2e-.
[68:5:3] - 2. Electrons flow through the **external circuit** → electricity.
[68:5:4] - 3. **Protons cross the membrane**.
[68:5:5] - 4. **Cathode:** 1/2 O2 + 2H+ + 2e- → H2O + heat.
[68:5:6] - **Exhaust:** only **water vapour and warm air**.
## SUB 6 | Storage and Transport
[68:6:0] TABLE head: Method | Fact
  [68:6:0.0] Compression | High-pressure Type-IV composite cylinders (roof-mounted on hydrogen train)
  [68:6:0.1] Liquefaction | −253°C (cryogenic); density 0.071 g/ml → bulky tanks
  [68:6:0.2] Metal hydrides | Hydrogen absorbed in alloys, released on heating
  [68:6:0.3] Chemical carriers | Ammonia, methanol, LOHC (not CO2)
[68:6:1] - Water need: **~9 litres purified water per kg** – limit in dry solar states.
## SUB 7 | Applications

[68:7:1] - **Fuel uses:** fuel-cell vehicles (buses, trucks, trains, ships), **rocket fuel**, **H-CNG** (blending in CNG / city gas).
[68:7:2] - **Power:** fuel cells, backup, long-duration storage, grid balancing.
[68:7:3] - **Green ammonia** = nitrogen + green hydrogen; fertiliser, fuel, marine fuel; leads adoption.
[68:7:4] - Green hydrogen uses: **direct IC fuel, blending with natural gas, fuel cells** (all three).
[68:7:5] - Decarbonises **fertilizer plants, oil refineries, steel plants** (all three) – **hard-to-abate** sectors where direct electrification is difficult.
