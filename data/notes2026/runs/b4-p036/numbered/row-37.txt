## SUB 0 | East-flowing vs West-flowing Rivers – Deltas, Estuaries and Direction
[0:0] # Why most Peninsular rivers flow east
[0:1] - The **Western Ghats** are the main water divide; the Deccan Plateau is **higher in the west and slopes east** to the Bay of Bengal, so most large rivers flow **east**.
[0:2] - The Peninsular block was **tilted north-west → south-east** (Himalayan upheaval), giving the whole drainage a Bay of Bengal orientation.
[0:3] - **Narmada and Tapi** are the only major Peninsular rivers flowing **west** – they occupy **trough faults (rift valleys)**.
[0:4] # Comparison
[0:5] TABLE head: Feature | East-flowing (Bay of Bengal) | West-flowing (Arabian Sea)
  [0:5.0] Rivers | Subarnarekha, Brahmani, Baitarani, Mahanadi, Vamsadhara, Nagavali, Godavari, Krishna, Pennar, Palar, Kaveri, Vaigai, Tamraparni | Narmada, Tapi, Mahi, Sabarmati, Vaitarna, Mandovi, Kali, Sharavati, Netravati, Periyar, Bharathapuzha, Pamba
  [0:5.1] Size | Long and large, dendritic tributaries | Short and swift (except Narmada, Tapi)
  [0:5.2] Mouth | Extensive deltas | No deltas – long, deep estuaries (natural harbours)
  [0:5.3] Use | Irrigation | Hydropower
[0:6] - **Why no deltas in the west:** steep Western Ghats slope (rapid flow), **short courses**, **hard rocky terrain**; Narmada and Tapi filled their fault troughs, so there is little alluvium – sediment is carried away by waves.
[0:7] - **Mahanadi** (delta near **Paradeep**, Odisha), Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri build deltas; **Narmada, Tapi, Mandovi** end in **estuaries** (Narmada estuary ~27 km, south of **Bharuch**).
[0:8] - **Subarnarekha** forms an **estuary** between the Ganga and Mahanadi deltas.
[0:9] - About **600** short, swift rivers drain the west coast.
[0:10] # Classic odd-ones
[0:11] - **Kali (Kalinadi)** of Karnataka is **west-flowing** (Diggi, Uttara Kannada → Arabian Sea at Karwar) – not east-flowing.
[0:12] - **Periyar** – west-flowing, reaches the Arabian Sea via **Vembanad**; **Pennar and Palar** flow east to the Bay of Bengal.
[0:13] - **Krishna** rises ~64 km from the Arabian Sea but flows ~1,400 km east to the **Bay of Bengal**.
[0:14] - **Tamraparni** flows east into the Gulf of Mannar – not west-flowing; **Brahmani** is east-flowing.
[0:15] - **Betwa** is a Yamuna tributary (flows north-east); **Rapti** is a Ghaghara tributary (NW → SE) – neither is west-flowing.
[0:16] - **Tungabhadra, Penganga** flow east (Krishna / Godavari systems).
[0:17] - **Kerala:** all major rivers flow west, but the **Kabini** (Wayanad) flows **east** to the Kaveri; **MP:** most rivers flow east but **Narmada and Tapi** flow **west** – so "no east-flowing river in Kerala" and "no west-flowing river in MP" are both **false**.
[0:18] > A–R (UPSC 2004): "West-flowing rivers have no deltas" / "they do not carry any alluvial sediments" → key (a), both true and R explains A; PMF calls this debatable because "do not carry any" is too absolute.
[0:19] > A–R (CDS 2010): "East-flowing Deccan rivers have deltas" is true, but "because they flow through soft rocks" is false – the Deccan is hard rock.
[0:20] > A–R (UPPSC 2014): "Western Ghats rivers do not form deltas" / "short courses, high speed over hard rocks" → both true, R explains A.

## SUB 1 | Krishna System
[1:0] # Course
[1:1] - Rises near **Mahabaleshwar** (Sahyadri, Maharashtra; ~1,337 m) → Maharashtra (**Sangli**), Karnataka, Telangana, AP → Bay of Bengal at **Hamsaladeevi**.
[1:2] - **~1,400 km**; **second-longest Peninsular river** and second-largest east-flowing river; basin ~**8%** of India; one of India's largest deltas.
[1:3] - Splits into two branches at **Puligadda**, **64 km below Vijayawada**; **Diviseema** lies between them.
[1:4] - Cities: **Sangli, Vijayawada, Srisailam, Kurnool (Tungabhadra), Hyderabad (Musi)**.
[1:5] # Tributaries
[1:6] TABLE head: Bank | Tributaries
  [1:6.0] Right | Tungabhadra (largest), Ghataprabha, Malaprabha, Koyna, Yerla, Varna (Warna), Panchganga, Dudhganga
  [1:6.1] Left | Bhima (largest left-bank; Chandrabhaga at Pandharpur), Dindi, Musi (Anantagiri hills; Hyderabad), Munneru
[1:7] - **Tungabhadra** = **Tunga + Bhadra**, both rising at **Varaha Parvata (Varaha hills), Western Ghats**; 531 km; joins the Krishna at **Sangameswaram** (Kurnool); **Hampi** on its bank; **Hagari (Vedavathi)** is its biggest tributary.
[1:8] - **Raichur Doab** = wedge between the **Krishna and Tungabhadra**.
[1:9] - **Not Krishna tributaries:** **Tel** (Mahanadi), **Gomati** (Ganga). **Panchganga** and **Koyna** belong to the Krishna, not the Godavari; Mand, Warna, Tungabhadra are Krishna tributaries.
[1:10] # Dams and projects
[1:11] TABLE head: Project | Place / note
  [1:11.0] Nagarjuna Sagar | On the Krishna near Nandikonda; multipurpose; then in undivided AP – now on the Telangana (Nalgonda)–AP (Palnadu) border
  [1:11.1] Srisailam | On the Krishna (not Tungabhadra); HEP; heart of the Nagarjuna Sagar–Srisailam Tiger Reserve
  [1:11.2] Almatti (Lal Bahadur Shastri Dam) | Krishna, Bijapur/Vijayapura, Karnataka; main reservoir of the Upper Krishna Project
  [1:11.3] Tungabhadra (Pampa Sagar) | Near Hosapete
[1:12] - **Krishna Raja Sagara (KRS)** is on the **Kaveri**, not the Krishna, despite the name.
[1:13] > "Rises in Maharashtra, flows through Karnataka and AP, meets the Bay of Bengal on the Andhra coast" (also "passes Sangli, meets the sea at Hamsaladeevi") = **Krishna**; the Godavari does not enter Karnataka.

## SUB 3 | Mahanadi, Kaveri, Pennar and Other East-flowing Rivers
[3:0] # Mahanadi
[3:1] - Rises at **Sihawa** (Chhattisgarh) → Chhattisgarh and **Odisha** (does **not** pass through AP); **851 km**; basin **1,41,589 sq km** (~4.3% of India); **Cuttack**; **delta in Odisha near Paradeep**; second to the Godavari in water potential and flood capacity.
[3:2] TABLE head: Bank | Tributaries
  [3:2.0] Left | Seonath (Shivnath), Hasdeo (largest), Mand, Ib
  [3:2.1] Right | Ong, Tel, Jonk
[3:3] - **Hirakud Dam** on the Mahanadi, ~15 km from **Sambalpur** (Odisha) – longest earthen dam (main dam 4.8 km). **Kuakhai** (Bhubaneswar) is a delta branch.
[3:4] - **Tel** = right-bank tributary of the **Mahanadi** (not of the Krishna or any Bihar river).
[3:5] # Kaveri (Cauvery)
[3:6] - **Talakaveri, Brahmagiri hills, Kodagu (Karnataka)** → Karnataka, **Kerala** (Kabini catchment), **Tamil Nadu**, Puducherry → Bay of Bengal near **Poompuhar**; ~800 km; basin 81,155 sq km.
[3:7] - Called **"Dakshina Ganga" / "Ganga of the South"** (the Godavari is also called Dakshin/Vriddha Ganga).
[3:8] - **Cauvery delta = "South India's granary"** (Thanjavur).
[3:9] TABLE head: Bank | Tributaries
  [3:9.0] Left | Harangi, Hemavathi, Shimsha, Arkavathi
  [3:9.1] Right | Lakshmantirtha, Kabini (Kapila), Suvarnavati, Bhavani, Noyyal, Amaravati
[3:10] - **Kabini** – Wayanad (Kerala), flows east, joins at **Tirumakudalu Narsipur**.
[3:11] - Forks thrice: islands **Srirangapatna, Sivasamudram, Srirangam**; dams **KRS (Kannambadi)**, **Mettur**; most exploited river (highest share abstracted for irrigation).
[3:12] # Pennar and smaller rivers
[3:13] - **Pennar (Uttara Pinakini)** – Nandi hills (Karnataka) → AP (Anantapur, Kadapa – **Gandikota gorge**, Nellore) → Bay of Bengal; **lifeline of Rayalaseema**; tributaries **Jayamangali**, Kunderu, Sagileru (left), Chitravathi, **Papagni**, Cheyyeru (right).
[3:14] - **Palar** – Nandi hills → Karnataka, AP (Chittoor), Tamil Nadu. **Vaigai** – Madurai → Palk Bay.
[3:15] - **Brahmani** (South Koel + Sankh) and **Baitarani** – common delta at **Bhitarkanika** (Odisha). **Subarnarekha** – Ranchi plateau; estuary.
[3:16] - **Machkund** – Duduma falls; **Machkund HEP** (AP–Odisha) is on the **Machkund** river.
[3:17] # Hilsa
[3:18] - **Hilsa (Indian shad)** migrates up the east-coast rivers; in AP the **Godavari's "Pulasa"** (Jul–Sep floods) is the famous catch; the **Narmada estuary (Bharuch)** now lands the largest hilsa catch on the west coast; Hooghly and Mahanadi also have hilsa.

## SUB 4 | Godavari System
[4:0] - **Dakshin Ganga / Vriddha Ganga** – called so for its **length and the area it covers** and its many tributaries; **longest Peninsular river** (1,465 km), second longest in India; **largest Peninsular basin** (3,12,812 sq km, ~9.5% of India).
[4:1] - Rises at **Trimbakeshwar (Nashik)** → Maharashtra (Nanded), Telangana, AP → Bay of Bengal; basin also in Chhattisgarh, Odisha, MP, Karnataka, Puducherry (Yanam).
[4:2] - **Widest/extensive delta** of the Deccan (**Konaseema**); after Rajahmundry splits into **Gautami** (east) and **Vasishta** (west), which divide into **7 branches** (Sapta Godavari).
[4:3] TABLE head: Branch | Mouth
  [4:3.0] Gautami | Yanam
  [4:3.1] Vasishta | Antarvedi
  [4:3.2] Vainateya | Komaragiripatnam
  [4:3.3] Tulya, Bharadwaja, Kaushika, Athreya | Bendamoorlanka / Coringa area
[4:4] - **2.7 km** wide at Rajahmundry; **Sir Arthur Cotton's anicut** at **Dowleswaram** (1852).
[4:5] # Tributaries
[4:6] TABLE head: Bank | Tributaries
  [4:6.0] Left | Purna, Pranhita (Penganga + Wardha + Wainganga), Indravati, Sabari, Kinnerasani
  [4:6.1] Right | Pravara, Manjira (Manjeera), Maner (Manair)
[4:7] - **Pranhita** = **largest tributary** (biggest flow), joins near **Kaleshwaram**; Penganga reaches the Godavari **via the Wardha–Pranhita**, not the Ganga.
[4:8] - **Indravati** rises in **Kalahandi (Odisha)**, flows through Bastar (Jagdalpur; Chitrakote falls).
[4:9] - **Sabari** (= **Kolab** in Odisha; Sinkaram hills) is the **last major tributary**, joining near **Kunavaram** above the delta; its tributary **Sileru** (upper reaches **Machkund**) joins it at the **AP–Chhattisgarh–Odisha** tri-junction.
[4:10] - **Nizam Sagar** is on the **Manjira** (Kamareddy, Telangana).
[4:11] - **Not Godavari tributaries:** **Koyna, Ghataprabha, Panchganga, Bhima** (Krishna), **Vamsadhara, Pennar**, **Amaravati** (Kaveri).
[4:12] > The Godavari is **not** perennial everywhere: like other Peninsular rivers it is rain-fed and seasonal in its upper Maharashtra course; Narmada and Tapi (rift-valley rivers) have more sustained flow.

## SUB 5 | Structure and Sequence – Older Drainage, Rift Valleys, Consequent and Superimposed Rivers
[5:0] - Peninsular drainage is **older** than the Himalayan – **broad, graded, shallow valleys**, **fixed courses**, no meanders, **non-perennial** flow.
[5:1] - **Consequent rivers** follow the general slope – **most Peninsular rivers**, e.g. **Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri** (Western Ghats → Bay of Bengal). **Subsequent:** Chambal, Sind, Ken, Betwa, Son meet the Yamuna/Ganga at right angles.
[5:2] - **Superimposed** rivers keep a course developed on a former cover rock – **Chambal**, Damodar, Subarnarekha, Banas.
[5:3] # Rift valleys
[5:4] TABLE head: River | Rift between / note
  [5:4.0] Narmada | Vindhya (north) and Satpura (south) – divides the Deccan Tableland from northern India; west-flowing
  [5:4.1] Tapi | Satpura (north) and Ajanta (south)
  [5:4.2] Damodar | Chota Nagpur – east-flowing rift valley due to down-warping (Gondwana trough); called a "biological desert" because of heavy industrial pollution
[5:5] # Sequences
[5:6] - East-flowing, **north → south:** **Subarnarekha, Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Pennar, Kaveri, Vaigai** (UPSC 2002) – so south → north = Kaveri, Pennar, Krishna, Godavari, Mahanadi.
[5:7] - **North → south:** Godavari, Pennar, Kaveri, **Periyar**; **Penganga, Godavari, Bhima, Tungabhadra**.
[5:8] - Whole India N → S: **Indus – Ganga – Godavari – Krishna** (Ganga – Mahanadi – Godavari – Krishna is also N → S).
[5:9] - Main systems: **Himalayan** = Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra (the Indus rises on the northern slopes of the **Kailash range near Mansarovar**); **Peninsular** = Narmada, Tapi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi.

## SUB 6 | Narmada, Tapi and the West-coast Rivers
[6:0] # Narmada
[6:1] - Amarkantak (Maikal) → MP, Maharashtra (border), Gujarat → **Gulf of Khambhat** south of Bharuch; **1,312 km** – **longest west-flowing**; **Jabalpur, Bhedaghat** (marble rocks, **Dhuandhar**), Kapildhara near Amarkantak.
[6:2] - Tributaries: **Tawa** (longest; Mahadeo hills; joins at Bandra Bhan), Burhner, **Banjar**, Sher, Shakkar, **Dudhi**, Ganjal (left); **Hiran**, Kolar, Orsang (right). **Purna** is **not** a Narmada tributary.
[6:3] - Projects: **Sardar Sarovar** (Gujarat), **Indira Sagar** (Khandwa, MP – **largest reservoir in India by volume**; Nagarjuna Sagar 2nd), **Omkareshwar** (Khandwa), **Bargi**, Maheshwar. The Narmada Valley plan has **30 major dams** – **29 major projects in MP** plus Sardar Sarovar in Gujarat.
[6:4] - Riverine islands: **Aliabet** (largest), **Sarfuddin, Dashan, Mehgam, Vegni**.
[6:5] - Basin: MP, Gujarat, Maharashtra and **Chhattisgarh** (Kabirdham/**Rajnandgaon** area of the Maikal).
[6:6] # Tapi (Tapti)
[6:7] - **Multai, Betul (MP)** → MP, Maharashtra, Gujarat → Arabian Sea at **Suvali near Surat**; **724 km**, second-longest west-flowing; "twin / handmaid of the Narmada"; **Purna** (largest tributary), Girna; **Ukai** dam.

## SUB 8 | Luni and Inland Drainage
[8:0] - **Luni** – rises in the Aravallis south-west of **Ajmer** (near Pushkar; called **Sagarmati**), flows west then south-west after **Telwara**, and is **lost in the marshy Rann of Kutch** – does not reach the sea (not the Gulf of Khambhat or Kutch); **endorheic / land-bound**; largest river of the Thar; ~495–511 km.
[8:1] - Its water is **fresh in the upper course and saline in the lower course** (salt-bearing desert soils).
[8:2] - Left-bank tributaries **Jawai, Sukri**, Guhiya, Bandi; right bank Jojari.
[8:3] - Inland drainage: **Luni, Ghaggar**. **Ghaggar** water is **not** used by the Indira Gandhi Canal (which draws on the Sutlej–Beas at Harike).

## SUB 9 | River Linking, Water Disputes and Watershed
[9:0] - After bifurcation (2014), **Telangana and AP** fight over the **Krishna and Godavari** waters (KRMB, GRMB; Polavaram–Banakacherla / Nallamala Sagar link opposed by **Telangana**).
[9:1] - **River linking:** first proposed by **Sir Arthur Cotton** (19th c.); **National Perspective Plan 1980** (NWDA) – **30 links = 14 Himalayan + 16 Peninsular**. First link completed: **Godavari–Krishna (Pattiseema), 2015**. **Ken-Betwa** (MP–UP; Daudhan dam in **Panna Tiger Reserve**) is the flagship link; **Par-Tapi-Narmada** – Gujarat and Maharashtra; **Tapti Basin Mega Recharge** – MP–Maharashtra MoU (May 2025). AP priorities: Vamsadhara-Penna intra-linking, Godavari-Krishna.
[9:2] - **Disputes:** **Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956** under **Article 262**; water = **Entry 17, State List**, subject to **Entry 56, Union List**.
