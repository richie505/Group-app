## SUB 4 | Green Revolution
[4:0] TABLE head: Person | Role
  [4:0.0] Norman Borlaug | Father of the Green Revolution (world); Mexican dwarf wheat; Nobel Peace 1970
  [4:0.1] M.S. Swaminathan | Father of the Green Revolution in India; HYV wheat; Evergreen Revolution
  [4:0.2] C. Subramaniam | Union Agriculture Minister who backed it
  [4:0.3] William Gaud | Coined the term (1968)
  [4:0.4] B.P. Pal | First DG of ICAR
[4:1] - **New Agricultural Strategy (mid-1960s, sixties)** after the 1965-67 droughts and **PL-480** imports; **HYV Programme from Kharif 1966**.
[4:2] - **Green Revolution = High Yielding Variety programme**; aim: **food-grain output through HYV seeds**, not land redistribution or exports.
[4:3] - Components: **HYV seeds**, fertilisers, pesticides, **irrigation**, mechanisation, MSP, **rural electrification, rural roads and marketing** (all four in the code).
[4:4] - Main crop: **Mexican dwarf wheat** (Lerma Rojo 64-A, Sonora-63/64, Kalyan Sona). **Wheat** benefited most in **production and productivity** (output about 2.5 times), then **rice**; pulses, oilseeds, millets lagged. Wheat yield touched about **2,000 kg/ha** (3,595 kg/ha in 2024-25).
[4:5] - First stage: **Punjab, Haryana, western UP**; eastern belt lagged. Phases: early 1968-81 (growth 2.3%), later 1981-92 (spread east, 3.4%).
[4:6] - **Limitations:** **regional disparities** (A and R both true, R not the explanation), **income disparities** (farmers with 10+ ha gained most), **soil degradation** from fertiliser, groundwater depletion; it did **not** raise all crops equally; N:P:K 10.9:4.1:1 vs ideal 4:2:1.
[4:7] - Procurement confined to rice, wheat, sugarcane – these use over two-thirds of urea; MSP + free power favour paddy in Punjab.
[4:8] - Small and marginal farmers **86.2%** of farmers, 47.3% of area (2015-16).
[4:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy 20 p.5 (PL 480, 1965-67 droughts, New Agricultural Strategy, HYV seeds, fertilisers), p.6 (HYV from 1966; wheat gained maximum; farmers with 10+ ha; phases 2.3% / 3.4%; eastern region lagged); app notes 4:81 (sub 4)
## SUB 5 | Cropping Systems
[5:0] TABLE head: System | Meaning
  [5:0.0] Double / multiple cropping | Two crops at different times (in succession) on the same land in a year (paddy then wheat)
  [5:0.1] Mixed cropping | Two or more crops simultaneously on the same land, seeds mixed, no row pattern
  [5:0.2] Intercropping | Crops together in definite rows (tur + jowar or maize)
  [5:0.3] Parallel cropping | Crops of different habit, no competition – wheat + mustard, maize + green gram
  [5:0.4] Sequential cropping | Crops in sequence on the same field in a year – no intercrop competition
  [5:0.5] Relay cropping | Next crop sown into the standing crop
  [5:0.6] Ratoon | Regrowth from stubble
  [5:0.7] Crop rotation | Planned sequence; pulse–cereal leaves fixed nitrogen
  [5:0.8] Mixed farming | Crops + rearing animals
[5:1] - **Rice–wheat** = major cropping system **by area** (Indo-Gangetic plains); UP's largest pattern too. Best cycle for eastern UP: **paddy – maize – wheat**.
[5:2] - **Monocropping does not protect soil fertility**; rotation, mixed cropping and fallowing do.
[5:3] - **Land Equivalent Ratio (LER)** measures the advantage of **intercropping over sole cropping**; sole cropping = one crop variety.
[5:4] - Agro-ecological cell models fit crop cycles to the **length of growing period**, with turn-around times and intercropping levels.
[5:5] - **Cereal yield** = output per unit of **harvested area** (kg/ha), not total production.
[5:6] - Wheat irrigation: **CRI (crown root initiation) stage**, 20-25 days after sowing, is the most critical – not flowering.
[5:7] - AP cropping intensity: **1.21 (2023-24) → 1.88 (2024-25)**; AP diversifying to **fruits, vegetables, spices, oil palm**.
