## SUB 0 | Population Theories – Malthus, Ehrlich and Others
[0:0] # Malthusian theory
[0:1] - **Thomas Robert Malthus**, *An Essay on the Principle of Population* (**1798**): population grows in **geometric progression** (1, 2, 4, 8), food only in **arithmetic progression** (1, 2, 3, 4).
[0:2] - Unchecked growth outruns food and ends in **scarcity, famine, disease and war** – the classic **overpopulation** theory (APPSC: the scientist who said population is too large with severe consequences = **Malthus**, not Darwin, Ehrlich or Einstein).
[0:3] TABLE head: Check | Acts on | Examples
  [0:3.0] Preventive | Lowers births | Moral restraint, late marriage, celibacy
  [0:3.1] Positive | Raises deaths | Famine, disease, war
[0:4] - **Criticism:** food production and living standards rose faster than he predicted – **technology, innovation and sustainable resource management**; Marxists and liberals say poverty comes from unequal distribution, not numbers.
[0:5] # Neo-Malthusian works
[0:6] TABLE head: Book / report | Author | Year
  [0:6.0] An Essay on the Principle of Population | Thomas Malthus | 1798
  [0:6.1] The Population Bomb | Paul R. Ehrlich | 1968
  [0:6.2] The Limits to Growth (World3 computer model) | Donella Meadows and others, for the Club of Rome | 1972
  [0:6.3] The Population Explosion | Paul and Anne Ehrlich | 1990
[0:7] - **"The battle to feed all of humanity is over"** is the opening line of **The Population Bomb (1968)** – not Malthus's Essay or the 1990 sequel.
[0:8] - **"New Population Bomb"** = rapid **urban growth in the Third World**.
[0:9] # Other theories
[0:10] TABLE head: Theory | Thinker | Idea
  [0:10.0] Demographic transition | Warren Thompson (1929) first; Frank Notestein named and formalised it (1945, three stages) | Death rates fall first with development, birth rates later
  [0:10.1] Optimum population | Edwin Cannan | The size giving the highest output (income) per head for given resources and technology
  [0:10.2] Marxian (anti-Malthusian) | Karl Marx | Hunger and "surplus population" come from exploitation / capitalism, not nature

## SUB 1 | Distribution of World Population and Its Factors
[1:0] # Pattern
[1:1] - **90%** of the world's people live on about **10%** of its land; most live in the **Northern Hemisphere mid-latitudes and the tropics of Asia**.
[1:2] - Crowded regions: **East Asia, South and South-east Asia, Europe, north-eastern North America**; two great clusters = **East Asia** and **South Asia**; the **Ganges–Brahmaputra delta** is among the densest areas on Earth.
[1:3] - Sparse: high latitudes, tropical deserts, high mountains, equatorial forests.
[1:4] # Factors
[1:5] TABLE head: Factor | Effect | Example
  [1:5.0] Water | River valleys densest | Nile valley
  [1:5.1] Landform | Plains favoured, mountains sparse | Ganga plains
  [1:5.2] Climate | Moderate climates attract; hot and cold deserts repel | Sahara, polar lands
  [1:5.3] Soil | Fertile loamy alluvium supports dense farming | Java, eastern China
  [1:5.4] Minerals | Mining draws workers | Katanga–Zambia copper belt
  [1:5.5] Industry, urbanisation | Jobs concentrate people | Kobe–Osaka
  [1:5.6] Social-cultural | Religious centres attract; unrest repels | Varanasi, Vatican
[1:6] # The odd one out
[1:7] - **North-eastern USA** (New York, Philadelphia, Boston, Pittsburgh) is dense because of **industry and urbanisation** – it does **not depend directly on agriculture**, unlike the lower Nile valley, Java and the eastern plains of China (MPPSC 2021).
[1:8] # Religion
[1:9] - **China** has the world's **largest Buddhist population** (about half of all Buddhists); Thailand is second, then Myanmar and Japan.
