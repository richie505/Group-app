## SUB 0 | Population Density – Measures and Census 2011 Pattern
[0:0] # Measures
[0:1] TABLE head: Measure | Formula
  [0:1.0] Arithmetic (crude) density | Total population ÷ total area (persons per sq km)
  [0:1.1] Physiological density | Total population ÷ net cultivated (arable) area
  [0:1.2] Agricultural density | Agricultural population ÷ net cultivable area
[0:2] - Physiological density is the **better index** of pressure on land because it uses arable land, while arithmetic density uses total area – so in the CDS 2009 A–R both are true and **R explains A**.
[0:3] - Density **rises** with natality and immigration; **falls** with mortality and emigration.
[0:4] # India
[0:5] - **117 (1951) → 382 per sq km (2011)**; 325 in 2001. India is among the densest large countries (only Bangladesh and Japan higher among big ones).

## SUB 3 | Factors, Regions of High Density and BIMARU
[3:0] # Factors
[3:1] TABLE head: Factor | Dense | Sparse
  [3:1.0] Water | River valleys | Thar desert (lack of water)
  [3:1.1] Soil | Loamy alluvium | Thin, rocky soils
  [3:1.2] Relief | Plains | Hills, mountains (except valleys like Kashmir, parts of Himachal)
[3:2] # High-density regions
[3:3] - **Ganga plain and Ganges–Brahmaputra delta**; **Godavari–Krishna deltas** (intensive **rice farming**); Kerala coast.
[3:4] - **Dhanbad** is the densest district of the **Chotanagpur plateau** because of the **development of mining industries and industrialisation** (coal; BCCL); its red soil is poor for farming, and mere availability of minerals is not the reason (UPPSC 2007).
[3:5] - Growing **population pressure** hinders government efforts to improve the quality of life (APPSC Group II 2011).
[3:6] # Population–resource regions
[3:7] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [3:7.0] Progressive | Resource-developed: Ganga–Yamuna Doab, Tamil Nadu
  [3:7.1] Problem | Population pressure on limited or degraded resources
  [3:7.2] Deficit | Resources exceed their use
[3:8] # BIMARU
[3:9] - **BI**har, **MA**dhya Pradesh, **R**ajasthan, **U**ttar Pradesh ("bimar" = sick); coined by demographer **Ashish Bose** in **1986** (not Odisha).
[3:10] - High growth and weak indicators; their faster growth than the southern States feeds the **delimitation debate**.

## SUB 4 | Census Town Criteria
[4:0] - A **Census Town** needs population **at least 5,000**, density **at least 400 per sq km**, and **at least 75% of male workers** in non-farm work – unchanged since **1981**.
