## SUB 0 | Soil Conservation – Methods and Practices
[0:0] # Meaning
[0:1] - **Soil conservation:** protecting soil from harm – **controlling erosion and keeping fertility high** (NCERT); it prevents **soil erosion**, not fertility or farming.
[0:2] - **Best single method to prevent erosion:** **afforestation**; deforestation, overgrazing and heavy rain cause erosion.
[0:3] # Methods
[0:4] TABLE head: Method | How it works / where
  [0:4.0] Contour ploughing / contour bunding | Ploughing and bunds along contour lines on hilly, sloping, rainfed land slow runoff – reduces erosion by water flow (not up-and-down ploughing)
  [0:4.1] Terrace cultivation | Steps on steep slopes and mountain areas – the best check on steep slopes; a conservation measure, not a cause of erosion
  [0:4.2] Shelter belts | Rows of trees that cut wind speed and protect soil (arid W India, coasts); CAZRI dune fixing in Rajasthan
  [0:4.3] Check dams, gully plugging | Trap sediment and stop gully growth
  [0:4.4] Strip cropping | Alternate erosion-resistant strips (grass) with row-crop strips
  [0:4.5] Mulching | Cover of residue/straw on the soil – retains soil moisture, reduces erosion
  [0:4.6] Crop rotation | Different crops in sequence on the same land – increases soil fertility, cuts pests, prevents degradation; an example of sustainable agriculture
  [0:4.7] Afforestation, regulated forestry, controlled grazing, cover cropping, mixed farming | NCERT remedial measures
[0:5] - **Government-promoted measures to prevent erosion:** **contour bunding and afforestation** (SI 2017).
[0:6] - **Soil fertility is improved by** crop rotation, mixed cropping, multiple cropping, manuring, green manuring – **seed modification does not** improve fertility.
[0:7] - **Contour bunding is NOT for** desert margins (wind), low flat flood plains or weedy scrublands – so "none of the above" (UPSC 2013).
[0:8] # Conservation agriculture (FAO)
[0:9] - Three principles: **minimum / zero tillage**, **permanent soil cover with crop residues**, **crop rotation / diversification** – so 2, 4 and 5 (UPSC 2018); "avoiding monoculture" and "avoiding plantation crops" are not its defined components.
[0:10] - **Zero tillage:** new crop sown into the previous crop's residue without seed-bed preparation – raises soil organic matter, cuts runoff; of contour bunding, relay cropping and zero tillage, **only zero tillage** is counted as storing carbon in the soil (UPSC 2012).
[0:11] - **Relay cropping:** next crop sown into the standing crop just before its harvest (e.g. paddy–lucerne).
[0:12] - Other: agroforestry, biochar (raises water holding, cuts soil N2O), China's **straw checkerboards** (stabilise dunes).
[0:13] - **Kurnool (Jul 2026):** Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing with **Jeevamrutham** to conserve soil moisture (target 92,000 acres).
[0:14] # Schemes
[0:15] - **Soil Health Card:** launched **19 Feb 2015** at **Suratgarh, Rajasthan**; slogan "**Swasth Dharaa, Khet Haraa**"; card **every two years**; **12 parameters** – N, P, K, S, Zn, Fe, Cu, Mn, B, pH, EC, organic carbon (micronutrients: **Zn, Fe, Cu, Mn, B**); **25.79 crore** cards by Feb 2026, most in **Uttar Pradesh**.
[0:16] - Most Indian soils are deficient in **nitrogen, phosphorus and humus** – the reason for soil testing.
[0:17] - **IWMP** (2009-10) merged in **2015-16** into the Watershed Development Component of **PMKSY**.
[0:18] > Problem → remedy pairs: deforestation → afforestation; soil erosion → soil stabilisation; habitat destruction → restoration.
[0:19] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Geography 06 p.6-8 (book p.71-73); Shankar Environment 20 (zero tillage, relay cropping); getmarks.app UPSC 2012 PYQ page; app notes 4:43 (sub 7, 9)
## SUB 1 | Alluvial Soil, Khadar–Bhangar and the Soil-Type Ranking
[1:0] # Ranking by area
[1:1] TABLE head: Soil | Area | Note
  [1:1.0] Alluvial | 15 lakh sq km, 45.6% (NCERT: ~40%) | Largest / most widespread and most productive group
  [1:1.1] Black (regur) | 5.46–5.47 lakh sq km, 16.6% | Deccan Plateau
  [1:1.2] Red (and yellow) | 5.18 lakh sq km (NextGen); 3.5 lakh sq km, 10.6% (PMF) | Often called the "second largest" group – see the dispute below
  [1:1.3] Laterite | 2.48 lakh sq km | Ghats summits
  [1:1.4] Forest / mountain | 2.85 lakh sq km, 8.67% | Himalaya, Ghats
  [1:1.5] Arid / desert | 1.42 lakh sq km, ~4.3% | W Rajasthan
[1:2] - **Eight** major soil types (ICAR): alluvial, black, red and yellow, laterite, arid, saline and alkaline, peaty and marshy, forest and mountain.
[1:3] - **Residual (in situ)** soils dominate **Peninsular India**; **drifted (ex situ)** soils dominate **North India**.
[1:4] # Alluvial soil
[1:5] - **Deposited by rivers** (transported/azonal); Indo-Gangetic–Brahmaputra plains, river valleys, **deltas** of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery, Narmada–Tapi valleys, coasts.
[1:6] - **Sand content decreases from west to east**; becomes more loamy and clayey towards the Brahmaputra valley.
[1:7] - NCERT: **rich in potash but poor in phosphorus**; poor in **nitrogen** and humus (PMF: potash, phosphate and lime adequate) – so alluvial soils are **deficient in nitrogen**.
[1:8] - Most fertile / **most productive** soil; needs the **least chemical fertiliser**; crops rice, wheat, sugarcane, jute, cotton, oilseeds.
[1:9] - **Very little alluvium:** **Madhya Pradesh** (Deccan Trap black soil) – not Bihar, Punjab or Tamil Nadu (deltas).
[1:10] - **Depth:** average **1,000–2,000 m** in the plains, maximum **about 6,100 m** – exams answer "about 6,000 m".
[1:11] - **Alluvial sand** has the **minimum water retention**; **clay** retains the most (tiny pores).
[1:12] # Khadar, Bhangar, Bhabar, Tarai
[1:13] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:13.0] Khadar | New alluvium, renewed by flood silt every year; lighter, sandy; fertile, ideal for intensive agriculture
  [1:13.1] Bhangar | Old alluvium on higher ground beyond flood reach; kankar (calcareous concretions); "old Kachhari clay"; pale reddish brown
  [1:13.2] Bhabar | Coarse, pebbly piedmont belt below the Shiwaliks; streams disappear
  [1:13.3] Tarai | Marshy lowland south of Bhabar; now largely reclaimed
[1:14] - Khadar and Bhangar are both **alluvial** soils – not regur, kankar or Bhabar.
[1:15] - **⚠ Trap:** "Khadar contains kankar" is **false** – kankar belongs to Bhangar (NCERT notes kankar can occur in both, but the exam reason is false).
[1:16] # Karewa soils
[1:17] - **Lacustrine (old lake-bed) deposits** of glacial clay and moraines on plateaus above the Jhelum in the **Kashmir Himalayas** (and Bhadarwah).
[1:18] - Famous for **saffron (zafran)** at Pampore, also almonds and walnuts.
[1:19] > Dispute: by area in PMF and NextGen, black soil (5.46–5.47 lakh sq km) is larger than red soil, but most books and keys call red (and yellow) soil the second-largest group.
[1:20] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 08 p.3-4 (book p.101-102); OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Geography 06 p.3 (book p.68); PMF Indian Physical Geography 03 p.82-84, p.86; PMF Indian Physical Geography 01 p.73 (alluvium depth); Rocket Indian Geography p.56-58; app notes 4:43 (sub 1, 2)
## SUB 2 | Black (Regur) Soil
[2:0] # Formation and properties
[2:1] - Formed by weathering of **Deccan Trap basalt** – **fissure volcanic rock** (lava); in Tamil Nadu from gneiss and schist. Also called **regur**, **black cotton soil**, tropical chernozem; Krebs called regur essentially a mature soil.
[2:2] - **Clay above 62%**: **highest moisture retention** – **needs little irrigation**, suits dry farming.
[2:3] - **Sticky when wet**, shrinks with **deep cracks** when dry – "**self-ploughing**" soil.
[2:4] - Colour from **titaniferous magnetite / iron** and dark parent rock – **not from humus**.
[2:5] - **Rich in** lime, iron, magnesia, alumina, potash (calcium, potassium, magnesium); **poor in nitrogen, phosphorus and humus** – so "rich in humus / nitrogen and organic matter" is a **false reason**.
[2:6] - Neutral to alkaline; in USDA terms = **Vertisols** (= Grumusols; semi-arid; crack in summer; **not humus-rich**).
[2:7] # Distribution
[2:8] - **5.46 lakh sq km (16.6%)** – Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh (**Malwa Plateau**), western AP, north Karnataka, Telangana, Tamil Nadu.
[2:9] - **Largest area / most widespread: Maharashtra** – not Assam, Bihar, Punjab, Tamil Nadu or Jharkhand. **Not** found in the Himalayan region.
[2:10] - **Very deep black soils:** **north-western Deccan Plateau** and upper Godavari–Krishna valleys.
[2:11] # Crops
[2:12] - **Cotton** (most suitable soil), jowar, wheat, linseed, tobacco, chillies, oilseeds, sunflower.
[2:13] TABLE head: AP black-soil areas (Rocket) | Crops
  [2:13.0] Deep black – Adoni, Aluru; unclassified black – West Godavari, Krishna, Guntur, mid-Kurnool | Maize, bajra, tobacco, turmeric, chillies
[2:14] > Key note: the app's old notes record an APPSC Group-I 2002 key of **Assam** for the largest black-soil State; the verified answer is **Maharashtra**.
[2:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 08 p.6 (book p.104); OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Geography 06 p.3-4 (book p.68-69); PMF Indian Physical Geography 03 p.84-85 (UPSC 2021 PYQ); Rocket Indian Geography p.58-59; Rocket AP Geography p.44; app notes 4:43 (sub 1, 4)
## SUB 3 | Laterite and Red Soils
[3:0] # Laterite
[3:1] - Latin **"later" = brick**; formed by **intense leaching** under **high temperature and heavy rainfall (> 200 cm)** with alternate wet and dry seasons; lime and silica leached, **iron and aluminium oxides** left.
[3:2] - **Red** in colour (iron oxide); **acidic**, **low fertility** – **excess iron** makes it infertile; **poor in nitrogen, phosphate, calcium (lime)**, organic matter; NCERT: iron oxide and **potash** in excess (PMF: poor in potash).
[3:3] - **Not** moisture-retentive with a large clay factor – it is porous and well drained (that describes black soil).
[3:4] - **Building material:** cut into bricks that harden in air.
[3:5] - Crops with manure: **tea, coffee, rubber, cashew, tapioca**, coconut, arecanut.
[3:6] - **Where:** summits of Western and Eastern Ghats, **Malabar coast (Kerala)**, Vindhya–Satpura, Rajmahal hills, Malwa, south **Maharashtra**, Karnataka, Odisha, Assam, Meghalaya; **Gangani (West Medinipur, West Bengal)**; **not** Rajasthan, UP, HP or Punjab.
[3:7] # Red (and yellow) soil
[3:8] - From **ancient crystalline granite, gneiss and schist** under low rainfall; red colour from **ferric oxide** (wide diffusion of iron), **yellow when hydrated**.
[3:9] - Porous, light, **low moisture retention**; poor in lime, humus, nitrogen, phosphates; fairly rich in potash.
[3:10] - **Where:** Peninsular India – almost all of **Tamil Nadu**, **Andhra Pradesh**, Karnataka, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Chota Nagpur; Kanyakumari–Jhansi and Kutch–Rajmahal; surrounds the black soil on the south, east and north.
[3:11] - Crops: ragi, groundnut, tobacco, millets, pulses.
[3:12] # Andhra Pradesh
[3:13] - Cultivated area: **red ~60%**, black ~25%, deltaic alluvium 5–6%, laterite ~1%; 22 soil categories; **six agro-climatic zones**.
[3:14] - Red soils (Rayalaseema): red gram, green gram, horse gram, groundnut, castor; AP laterite is poor in nitrogen.
[3:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 08 p.7 (book p.105); OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Geography 06 p.4 (book p.69); PMF Indian Physical Geography 03 p.85-88 (UPSC 2010, 2013 PYQs); Rocket Indian Geography p.59-60; Rocket AP Geography p.43-44; app notes 4:43 (sub 1, 3, 9)
## SUB 4 | Soil Erosion – Types, Agents and Causes
[4:0] # Types
[4:1] TABLE head: Type | Process | Example
  [4:1.0] Splash | Raindrop impact breaks crumbs – first stage | Bare soil
  [4:1.1] Sheet | Thin uniform topsoil layer removed by overland flow; widespread, least noticed | Gentle slopes
  [4:1.2] Rill | Small, shallow channels on the soil surface | Bare fields
  [4:1.3] Gully | Deep trenches; badlands and ravines; final stage | Chambal ravines
  [4:1.4] Wind | Deflation, saltation, creep | Thar Desert
  [4:1.5] Mass (landslide) | Gravity on slopes | Himalayan monsoon slopes
  [4:1.6] Tunnel | Subsurface removal | Parts of Australia, Africa
[4:2] - **Increasing soil loss:** **splash → sheet → rill → gully** (II, IV, III, I).
[4:3] - **Ravines:** Chambal–Yamuna basin on the **periphery of the Gangetic plain** (MP, Rajasthan, UP); also Tamil Nadu, West Bengal; India loses ~8,000 ha a year to gullies.
[4:4] # Causes and rate
[4:5] - **Deforestation** is the main human cause (removes root binding and humus); also overgrazing, shifting cultivation, faulty farming. **Terrace cultivation is a remedy**, and tropical climate is not counted a cause – "only deforestation" (UPSC 2014).
[4:6] - **Deforestation generally decreases rainfall** (and increases erosion, drought, warming).
[4:7] - Erosion rate depends on **all** of: rainfall intensity and duration, slope, vegetation cover, soil texture, land use.
[4:8] - **Rainfall intensity matters more than total annual rainfall**; higher intensity → more runoff → **effective rainfall reduces**.
[4:9] - Wind erosion worst on **bare, dry, fine-textured soil** with strong winds; **water is the most powerful agent**.
[4:10] - Widely spaced row crops leave soil bare between rows – **sorghum** is the exam answer for fastest erosion (dense clover and wheat protect the soil).
[4:11] - **RUSLE** model predicts long-term water erosion on slopes (ICAR C- and P-factor maps).
[4:12] - Intense bursts exceed **infiltration capacity** → runoff, so soils can end up drier (2026 studies).
[4:13] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Geography 06 p.6-7 (book p.71-72); PMF Indian Physical Geography 03 p.81 (Chambal ravines); gktoday.in UPSC 2014 PYQ page; app notes 4:43 (sub 6)
## SUB 5 | Problem Soils – Salinity, Alkalinity, Acidity and Degradation
[5:0] # Saline and alkaline soils
[5:1] - Local names **reh, kallar, usar**, thur, rakar, chopan; caused by **unscientific irrigation and poor drainage**; capillary rise leaves salts on top.
[5:2] - **Salinisation** of irrigated land: salts left by evaporating water **make some soils impermeable** – it does not raise yields.
[5:3] - **Largest saline area: Gujarat** (Rann of Kutch, Gulf of Khambhat); **maximum alkali area: Uttar Pradesh**.
[5:4] - Affected: Rajasthan, Haryana, Punjab, UP, Bihar; Green Revolution canal areas of Punjab–Haryana.
[5:5] # Amendments
[5:6] TABLE head: Problem | Remedy
  [5:6.0] Alkaline / sodic (and saline-alkali) | Gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O) – replaces sodium with calcium; long-term solution, with leaching water
  [5:6.1] Excess soluble salts | Leaching – removing salts by applying water
  [5:6.2] Alkalinity | Acid-forming chemicals (sulphur etc.) neutralise it
  [5:6.3] Acidic | Lime – decreases soil acidity
  [5:6.4] Poor fertility | Green manuring – boosts microbes and fertility
[5:7] # Degradation
[5:8] - **Soil impoverishment** = soil becoming very deficient in plant nutrients.
[5:9] - **Green Revolution reduced soil fertility**: chemical fertilisers without manure, over-irrigation, salinity.
[5:10] TABLE head: Cause | States
  [5:10.0] Overgrazing | Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Maharashtra
  [5:10.1] Over-irrigation | Punjab, Haryana, western UP
  [5:10.2] Mining | Jharkhand, Odisha, MP, Chhattisgarh
[5:11] - **Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas** (ISRO **SAC, Ahmedabad**, June 2021): **97.85 mha (29.7%)** degraded in 2018-19 (28.76% in 2003-05); rose in **28 of 31** States/UTs; **water erosion** the largest driver.
[5:12] - **FAO Status of the World's Soil Resources 2026:** soil-biodiversity loss **4th of 7** threats (after erosion, SOC loss, nutrient mismanagement).

## SUB 6 | Other Soils, Rain-Forest Soils and the ICAR/USDA Orders
[6:0] # Desert, peaty, forest soils
[6:1] - **Arid / desert:** sandy, saline; poor in nitrogen and humus, **phosphate normal/high**; **calcium increases downward** (kankar) – **western Rajasthan soils are rich in calcium**.
[6:2] - **Peaty / marshy:** areas of **heavy rainfall and high humidity with good vegetation**; organic matter 40–50%; black, heavy, highly acidic (NCERT: alkaline in places); Kottayam–Alappuzha, Sundarbans, coastal Odisha–TN, north Bihar, Almora. "Semi-arid with salinity" = saline soil.
[6:3] - **Forest / mountain:** humus-rich in forests and lower valleys (NextGen, PMF), but **acidic with low humus** on snow-clad upper slopes (NCERT); poor in potash and phosphorus.
[6:4] - **Podzol:** acidic leached soil of **coniferous (taiga)** forests (about 60°N).
[6:5] # Tropical rain-forest soils
[6:6] - **Poor in nutrients**: heat and moisture **decompose organic matter quickly** (true), but nutrients go straight into vegetation or are **leached** (UPSC 2023: I false, II true).
[6:7] - Cleared rain forest **cannot** support intensive farming for years without fertilisers, though its **primary productivity is very high** (UPSC 2003: A false, R true).
[6:8] # ICAR / USDA soil orders
[6:9] TABLE head: Order | Share of India
  [6:9.0] Inceptisols (largest) | 39.74%
  [6:9.1] Entisols | 28.08%
  [6:9.2] Alfisols | 13.55%
  [6:9.3] Vertisols (black soils) | 8.52%
  [6:9.4] Aridisols | 4.28%
  [6:9.5] Ultisols | 2.51%
  [6:9.6] Mollisols (smallest) | 0.40%
[6:10] - Decreasing order asked: **Entisols > Alfisols > Aridisols > Ultisols**.
[6:11] - **Histosols** = organic (peat/bog) soils with very high organic matter; **Gelisols** frozen; **Spodosols** acidic leached (podzols); **Vertisols** cracking clays.

## SUB 7 | Soil Formation, Profile, Water and Nutrients
[7:0] # Formation
[7:1] - Five factors: **parent material, climate, relief, organisms, time** (soil colour is a property, not a factor); limestone → calcium-rich soil.
[7:2] - **Tors:** rounded granite boulders left by weathering and erosion of granite – common in Andhra Pradesh (e.g. Rayalaseema).
[7:3] # Profile
[7:4] - **Soil profile** = the **vertical arrangement of horizons** down to the parent rock (a single layer is a horizon).
[7:5] TABLE head: Horizon | Meaning
  [7:5.0] O | Organic litter
  [7:5.1] A | Topsoil, humus-rich
  [7:5.2] E | Leached layer
  [7:5.3] B | Subsoil – minerals washed down accumulate
  [7:5.4] C | Weathered parent material
  [7:5.5] R | Bedrock
[7:6] - Order from the surface: **O → A → B → C → R**.
[7:7] # Composition and water
[7:8] - Good soil by volume: **mineral 45%, organic matter 5%, water 25%, air 25%**.
[7:9] - **Soil organic carbon** binds particles into aggregates, raises infiltration, feeds microbes; India's average SOC reportedly fell from **1% to 0.3%** in 70 years.
[7:10] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [7:10.0] Infiltration | Entry of water at the surface
  [7:10.1] Percolation | Movement down through the soil column
  [7:10.2] Permeability | Ease of water movement
  [7:10.3] Saturated flow | All soil pores filled with water
[7:11] - **Clay** retains the most water because of its many **small** pores (not large ones).
[7:12] # Nutrients
[7:13] TABLE head: Nutrient | Main source in soil
  [7:13.0] Nitrogen | Organic matter
  [7:13.1] Iron | Pyrites
  [7:13.2] Calcium | Gypsum
  [7:13.3] Boron | Tourmaline
[7:14] - **Zinc** is the micronutrient most widely deficient in Indian soils.
[7:15] - Crops remove large amounts of **potassium** – among Ca, K, Zn and P the most depleted after harvest.
