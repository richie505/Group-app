## SUB 0 | Education Policy and School Schemes
[0:0] # Policies
[0:1] - **NPE 1986** launched **Operation Blackboard (1987)** – at least **two rooms and two teachers**, teaching aids, for primary schools; also stressed ST education. **Ramamurti Committee (1990)** reviewed NPE; NPE **revised 1992** (Programme of Action) gave more weight to tribal education.
[0:2] - **NEP 2020:** replaced NPE 1986 after **34 years**; **5+3+3+4** – Foundational **3-8**, Preparatory 8-11, Middle 11-14, Secondary 14-18.
[0:3] - NEP targets: **100% school GER by 2030**; **50% higher-education GER by 2035**; **100% youth and adult literacy by 2030**.
[0:4] - **NIPUN Bharat:** foundational literacy by **Grade 3**; deadline moved to **2026-27**.
[0:5] # Schemes

  [0:6.0] Samagra Shiksha (2018) | Subsumes SSA, RMSA, Teacher Education
  [0:6.1] Unnat Bharat Abhiyan | Higher education – institutions adopt villages
  [0:6.2] SEMIS | Secondary Education MIS – nodal agency NUEPA, New Delhi
  [0:6.3] Mana Badi Nadu-Nedu (AP) | 14 Nov 2019; English labs (not Operation Blackboard)
[0:7] # Learning and enrolment data
[0:8] - **FLN Index** (Institute for Competitiveness for **EAC-PM**): 2nd edition – **Punjab** highest (64.19) among **small States**; Jharkhand was **not** the lowest small State in the web summaries. 2021 edition – West Bengal top among large States.
[0:9] - **AISHE 2023-24:** GER about **30**, female GER **31.2**, GPI **1.08**; female GER 28.5 → **30.2** (2021-23), above male; gender parity at all school levels.
[0:10] - **UDISE+ 2024-25** dropout: preparatory **2.3%**, middle **3.5%**, secondary **8.2%** (from 3.7, 5.2, **10.9**).
[0:11] - NFHS-5: women with **10+ years** of schooling **41%** (NFHS-4 35.7%).

## SUB 2 | Adult Literacy Programmes and Literacy Days
[2:0] TABLE head: Programme | Year | Note
  [2:0.0] Farmers' Functional Literacy Programme | 1967-68 | Early adult education
  [2:0.1] NAEP | 2 Oct 1978 | Age 15-35
  [2:0.2] National Literacy Mission | 5 May 1988 | Total Literacy Campaign = its main mass-campaign strategy
  [2:0.3] Total Literacy Campaign | 1989-90 | Ernakulam – first totally literate district (1990); Kottayam – first fully literate town (1989)
  [2:0.4] Saakshar Bharat | 8 Sep 2009 (International Literacy Day) | Adults 15+, focus on women; Centre : State 75 : 25 (NE 90 : 10); planned up to March 2012, extended to 31 March 2018, then wound up
  [2:0.5] ULLAS (Nav Bharat Saaksharta Karyakram / New India Literacy Programme) | 2022 | FY 2022-23 to 2026-27; Ministry of Education; non-literates 15+
[2:1] - ULLAS = **Understanding of Lifelong Learning for All in Society**; five components – FLN, critical life skills, basic education, vocational skills, continuing education.
[2:2] - Fully literate (ULLAS): **Ladakh** (first UT, 2024) → **Mizoram** (first State, 2025) → Goa, Tripura, Himachal Pradesh, Sikkim → **Uttarakhand 6th (Jul 2026)**.
[2:3] - **Velland (Kerala)** is the exam answer for India's first fully **computer-literate panchayat**; Chamravattom (Malappuram) was declared the first 100% computer-literate village (2003).
[2:4] - **International Literacy Day: 8 September** (UNESCO, declared 1966, first observed 1967). **2017 theme: "Literacy in a digital world"**; 2026: "Literacy for people, the planet and prosperity".
[2:5] - **Information literacy:** the skill of defining, locating, accessing and using information.
[2:6] > NAEP = 15-35; Saakshar Bharat and ULLAS = 15+.

## SUB 3 | Census 2011 – India, Gender Gap and States
[3:0] # Definition
[3:1] - **Literate** = aged **7 and above**, can **both read and write with understanding** in any language; reading only = **not literate**.
[3:2] - Censuses 1951-1971 used age **5+**; **7+ from 1981**. PLFS uses the same 7+ definition but a **sample** – not directly comparable.
[3:3] - Full literacy (ULLAS) = **95%+** among persons aged **15+**. **Census 2027** adds a **digital literacy** question.
[3:4] # National figures
[3:5] TABLE head: Census | 1951 | 1961 | 1971 | 1981 | 1991 | 2001 | 2011
  [3:5.0] Literacy % | 18.33 | 28.30 | 34.45 | 43.57 | 52.21 | 64.83 | 74.04
[3:6] # Census 2011 – provisional vs final
[3:7] TABLE head: India 2011 | Total | Male | Female
  [3:7.0] Provisional | 74.04% | 82.14% | 65.46%
  [3:7.1] Final | 73.0% (72.98%) | 80.9% (80.88%) | 64.6% (64.63%)
[3:8] - **Largest decadal gain: 1990s**. Males about 27% → 82% in 60 years; women from less than 1 in 10 (1951) to 2 in 3.
[3:9] - **Gender gap 2011:** about **16-17 points** (82.14 − 65.46 = 16.68 provisional); widest in **Rajasthan (about 28 points)**; under **5 points** in **Meghalaya** (about 76 vs 73).
[3:10] - Rural / urban (provisional): **68.9% / 85.0%**; largest deficit: **rural women**. Causes of the gap: distance and safety, early marriage, dowry, son preference, poverty.
[3:11] - SC literacy **66.1%**, ST **59.0%**; low literacy is one of the **four PVTG criteria**.
