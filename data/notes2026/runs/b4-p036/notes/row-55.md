=== Population Theories – Malthus, Ehrlich and Others
# Malthusian theory
- **Thomas Robert Malthus**, *An Essay on the Principle of Population* (**1798**): population grows in **geometric progression** (1, 2, 4, 8), food only in **arithmetic progression** (1, 2, 3, 4).
- Unchecked growth outruns food and ends in **scarcity, famine, disease and war** – the classic **overpopulation** theory (APPSC: the scientist who said population is too large with severe consequences = **Malthus**, not Darwin, Ehrlich or Einstein).
| Check | Acts on | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Preventive** | Lowers births | Moral restraint, **late marriage**, celibacy |
| **Positive** | Raises deaths | **Famine, disease**, war |
- **Criticism:** food production and living standards rose faster than he predicted – **technology, innovation and sustainable resource management**; Marxists and liberals say poverty comes from unequal distribution, not numbers.
# Neo-Malthusian works
| Book / report | Author | Year |
|---|---|---|
| An Essay on the Principle of Population | Thomas Malthus | 1798 |
| **The Population Bomb** | **Paul R. Ehrlich** | **1968** |
| The Limits to Growth (World3 computer model) | Donella Meadows and others, for the **Club of Rome** | 1972 |
| The Population Explosion | Paul and Anne Ehrlich | 1990 |
- **"The battle to feed all of humanity is over"** is the opening line of **The Population Bomb (1968)** – not Malthus's Essay or the 1990 sequel.
- **"New Population Bomb"** = rapid **urban growth in the Third World**.
# Other theories
| Theory | Thinker | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| Demographic transition | **Warren Thompson (1929)** first; **Frank Notestein** named and formalised it (1945, three stages) | Death rates fall first with development, birth rates later |
| **Optimum population** | **Edwin Cannan** | The size giving the **highest output (income) per head** for given resources and technology |
| Marxian (anti-Malthusian) | Karl Marx | Hunger and "surplus population" come from **exploitation / capitalism**, not nature |
> Exam angle: book–author and quote–book pairs; preventive vs positive check; geometric (population) vs arithmetic (food).

=== Distribution of World Population and Its Factors
# Pattern
- **90%** of the world's people live on about **10%** of its land; most live in the **Northern Hemisphere mid-latitudes and the tropics of Asia**.
- Crowded regions: **East Asia, South and South-east Asia, Europe, north-eastern North America**; two great clusters = **East Asia** and **South Asia**; the **Ganges–Brahmaputra delta** is among the densest areas on Earth.
- Sparse: high latitudes, tropical deserts, high mountains, equatorial forests.
# Factors
| Factor | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Water | River valleys densest | Nile valley |
| Landform | Plains favoured, mountains sparse | Ganga plains |
| Climate | Moderate climates attract; **hot and cold deserts repel** | Sahara, polar lands |
| Soil | Fertile loamy alluvium supports dense farming | Java, eastern China |
| Minerals | Mining draws workers | **Katanga–Zambia copper belt** |
| Industry, urbanisation | Jobs concentrate people | **Kobe–Osaka** |
| Social-cultural | Religious centres attract; unrest repels | Varanasi, Vatican |
# The odd one out
- **North-eastern USA** (New York, Philadelphia, Boston, Pittsburgh) is dense because of **industry and urbanisation** – it does **not depend directly on agriculture**, unlike the lower Nile valley, Java and the eastern plains of China (MPPSC 2021).
# Religion
- **China** has the world's **largest Buddhist population** (about half of all Buddhists); Thailand is second, then Myanmar and Japan.

| 1 | **India** (1,428.6 mn, 17.76%) | 481 |

- **India overtook China in April 2023** (UN); until then India was **second**. India: **1.46 billion** (UNFPA SOWP 2025).

- **Latin America:** **Puerto Rico** (small densely settled island) has the highest density among Puerto Rico, Brazil, Panama and Mexico.
