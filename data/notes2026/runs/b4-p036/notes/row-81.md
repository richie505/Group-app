=== Cropping Seasons – Kharif, Rabi, Zaid
| Season | Sown | Harvested | Crops |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kharif** | **June-July** (SW monsoon) | Sep-Oct / Oct-Nov | **Rice, jowar, maize, bajra, ragi, tur, moong, cotton, soybean, groundnut, sugarcane, jute, red chilli** |
| **Rabi** | **October-November** | March-April | **Wheat, gram, peas, mustard, lentil (masoor), linseed, lathyrus, French bean, barley** |
| **Zaid** | Short summer **between rabi and kharif** (April-June) | – | **Watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, bitter gourd, vegetables, fodder**, sunflower; irrigated **moong and urad** |
- **Not kharif:** gram, masoor, mustard. **Not rabi:** lady's finger (summer/kharif vegetable), moong. Carrot, radish, pea are rabi.
- **Maize** – grown **throughout the year** (mainly kharif, also rabi in Bihar); "**Queen of Cereals**".
- **Rice in West Bengal:** three crops – **aus, aman, boro** (all **paddy**). **Aman** sown **June-July**, harvested Nov-Dec.
- **Pulses in both seasons:** kharif (tur, moong, urad) and rabi (gram, masoor, pea); **black gram** grows as kharif and rabi.
- **Transplanted crops:** **rice** (less seed, more labour) and **onion** (nursery seedlings).
- Rabi in **Punjab, Haryana** (north-west) depends on winter rain from **western disturbances** – **temperate (extra-tropical) cyclones** from the Mediterranean, 10-15 cm of rain.
> Exam angle: cotton, groundnut, rice = kharif; wheat = rabi (UPSC 2013).

=== Food Crops – Growth Conditions and Crop Types
| Crop | Season / rank | Temperature | Rainfall | Soil / area |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rice** | Kharif; **chief food crop**, 1st cereal | **Above 25°C**, high humidity | **Above 100 cm** (100 cm isohyet ≈ western limit) | Clayey/loamy deltaic and coastal alluvium |
| **Wheat** | Rabi; **2nd cereal** | Cool growing season, 10-15°C winter; bright warm sun at ripening | **50-75 cm** (moderate) | **Ganga-Satluj plains**; **black soil of the Deccan also suits wheat** |
| Maize | Year-round | Warm | Moderate | Semi-arid, inferior soils |
| Jowar | Kharif/rabi | Warm, drought tolerant | Rainfed | Peninsular, semi-arid |
| Bajra | Kharif | Hot, dry | Driest | Sandy |
| Ragi | Kharif | Warm, dry | Low | Red soils |
| Pulses | Both | – | Rainfed | **Fix atmospheric nitrogen** – improve soil |
- Wheat needs **moderate temperature and moderate rainfall**; **macaroni (durum) wheat** suits **rainfed** conditions.
- **Origins:** rice – **South-East Asia**; red gram (arhar) – **India**; **maize** – the Americas (New World), so it is **not an ancient cereal** of the Old World (rice, wheat, barley are).
- **Maize** is a **C4** plant (paddy, wheat, soybean are C3) and the best fodder for **silage**.
- Cereals and millets are mainly **starch**. Wheat, rice, maize, jowar = cereals; **wheat is not a millet**.
- **Rajma** is the pulse that does **not** fix nitrogen (poor nodulation); **paddy** does not help N-fixation; **black gram (urad)** improves soil fertility.
- Black soil grows **cotton, wheat, jowar, pulses** (APPSC dropped its "not grown in black soil" item).

- **Subsistence-farmed:** **coarse cereals and rice**.

=== Green Revolution
| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| Norman Borlaug | Father of the Green Revolution (world); Mexican dwarf wheat; Nobel Peace 1970 |
| **M.S. Swaminathan** | **Father of the Green Revolution in India**; HYV wheat; Evergreen Revolution |
| C. Subramaniam | Union Agriculture Minister who backed it |
| William Gaud | Coined the term (1968) |
| B.P. Pal | First DG of ICAR |
- **New Agricultural Strategy (mid-1960s, sixties)** after the 1965-67 droughts and **PL-480** imports; **HYV Programme from Kharif 1966**.
- **Green Revolution = High Yielding Variety programme**; aim: **food-grain output through HYV seeds**, not land redistribution or exports.
- Components: **HYV seeds**, fertilisers, pesticides, **irrigation**, mechanisation, MSP, **rural electrification, rural roads and marketing** (all four in the code).
- Main crop: **Mexican dwarf wheat** (Lerma Rojo 64-A, Sonora-63/64, Kalyan Sona). **Wheat** benefited most in **production and productivity** (output about 2.5 times), then **rice**; pulses, oilseeds, millets lagged. Wheat yield touched about **2,000 kg/ha** (3,595 kg/ha in 2024-25).
- First stage: **Punjab, Haryana, western UP**; eastern belt lagged. Phases: early 1968-81 (growth 2.3%), later 1981-92 (spread east, 3.4%).
- **Limitations:** **regional disparities** (A and R both true, R not the explanation), **income disparities** (farmers with 10+ ha gained most), **soil degradation** from fertiliser, groundwater depletion; it did **not** raise all crops equally; N:P:K 10.9:4.1:1 vs ideal 4:2:1.
- Procurement confined to rice, wheat, sugarcane – these use over two-thirds of urea; MSP + free power favour paddy in Punjab.
- Small and marginal farmers **86.2%** of farmers, 47.3% of area (2015-16).
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy 20 p.5 (PL 480, 1965-67 droughts, New Agricultural Strategy, HYV seeds, fertilisers), p.6 (HYV from 1966; wheat gained maximum; farmers with 10+ ha; phases 2.3% / 3.4%; eastern region lagged); app notes 4:81 (sub 4)

=== Cropping Systems
| System | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Double / multiple cropping** | **Two crops at different times** (in succession) on the same land in a year (paddy then wheat) |
| **Mixed cropping** | Two or more crops **simultaneously** on the same land, seeds mixed, no row pattern |
| Intercropping | Crops together in **definite rows** (tur + jowar or maize) |
| **Parallel cropping** | Crops of different habit, no competition – **wheat + mustard**, maize + green gram |
| Sequential cropping | Crops in sequence on the same field in a year – no intercrop competition |
| Relay cropping | Next crop sown into the standing crop |
| Ratoon | Regrowth from stubble |
| Crop rotation | Planned sequence; pulse–cereal leaves fixed nitrogen |
| Mixed farming | Crops + rearing animals |
- **Rice–wheat** = major cropping system **by area** (Indo-Gangetic plains); UP's largest pattern too. Best cycle for eastern UP: **paddy – maize – wheat**.
- **Monocropping does not protect soil fertility**; rotation, mixed cropping and fallowing do.
- **Land Equivalent Ratio (LER)** measures the advantage of **intercropping over sole cropping**; sole cropping = one crop variety.
- Agro-ecological cell models fit crop cycles to the **length of growing period**, with turn-around times and intercropping levels.
- **Cereal yield** = output per unit of **harvested area** (kg/ha), not total production.
- Wheat irrigation: **CRI (crown root initiation) stage**, 20-25 days after sowing, is the most critical – not flowering.
- AP cropping intensity: **1.21 (2023-24) → 1.88 (2024-25)**; AP diversifying to **fruits, vegetables, spices, oil palm**.

=== Millets and Dryland Farming
| Group | Millets |
|---|---|
| Major | **Bajra** (pearl; ~60% of millet output), **jowar (sorghum)**, **ragi** (finger) |
| Minor | Foxtail (kangni), little (kutki), **kodo**, proso (chena) |
- Millet group: **sorghum, kodo, kangani, ragi** – not maize or moong; tur and gram are pulses.
- **2023 = UN International Year of Millets** (proposed by India); brand **Shree Anna**.
- **Dryland farming: annual rainfall below 75 cm** (NCERT; NDA 2020) – wetland farming in high-rainfall areas.
- Best dryland crop: **groundnut** (50-125 cm; needs gypsum). Predominantly **rainfed: groundnut, sesamum, pearl millet** (UPSC 2012).
- Sorghum and pearl-millet based cropping systems: keyed **Andhra Pradesh** (APPSC Panchayat Secretary).
- Millets are mainly **kharif** crops of dryland and rainfed tracts; rainfed areas grow mostly **millets, pulses and oilseeds**.

=== Varieties, Diseases and Biofertilisers
| Crop | Disease | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Wheat | Black rust; loose smut; Karnal bunt | *Puccinia*; *Ustilago*; *Tilletia indica* |
| Rice | Tungro; **Khaira** | Virus; **zinc deficiency** |
