=== Forestry Practice – Seeds, Logging, Timber and Forest Fires
# Forest fires
- **Types:** **ground** fire (burns humus/peat below the litter), **surface** fire (litter and undergrowth – grassland and scrub fires), **crown** fire (runs through the canopy; fastest-spreading and most dangerous in **coniferous** forests, whose resinous needles burn readily).
- **Counter fire (back-firing)** = deliberately burning a strip of fuel ahead of the advancing fire, from a control line – the **traditional** method of fighting forest fires; fire lines, alert systems and mobile squads are aids.
# Logging and timber
- **Salvage logging** = removing trees killed or damaged by fire, wind, insects or disease to recover their timber value.
- **Traditional logging tools:** axe, hand (cross-cut) saw, wedges, mauls – the **chainsaw** is a modern powered machine.
- **Wood transport:** **rafting** = logs tied together and floated as one unit; **floating** = loose logs one by one; rope-ways and tramways run overland.
- **Timber groups (macrostructure):** **hardwood** (broad-leaved angiosperms, e.g. teak, sal) and **softwood** (conifers, e.g. pine, deodar). **Wood anatomy** (cells, grain, density, pores) shows the best way to **process and use** a timber.
- **Forest inventory** = systematic collection of forest data (area, stock, growth) for assessment and planning.
# Seeds, planting and site
- **Recalcitrant seed** (cannot be dried/stored) – **sal (Shorea robusta)**; **orthodox seed** (stores dry) – **babul (Vachellia nilotica)**; **vivipary** (germinates on the tree) – mangrove **Rhizophora mucronata**; **stump planting** – **teak (Tectona grandis)**.
- **Timber line (tree line)** = upper altitude or high-latitude limit beyond which trees do not grow.
- **Degraded, heavily eroded zone (NW India):** hardy dry species **Acacia modesta, Capparis aphylla (kair), Salvadora oleoides (pilu)** survive there.
