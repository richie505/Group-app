## SUB 0 | Vice-President – Office, Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, Election (Arts. 63, 64, 66, 71, 79, 89)
[0:0] # Office
[0:1] - **Article 63**: "There shall be a Vice-President of India" – the **second-highest office** (second highest dignitary) after the President; modelled on the **American Vice-President**, but unlike the US VP he does **not succeed** to the Presidency for the rest of the term – he only **acts** until a new President enters office.
[0:2] - **Articles 64 and 89(1)**: the VP is the **ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Council of States)** – the VP **presides over the sessions of the Rajya Sabha**. Not the President, PM, Speaker, Deputy Speaker, CJI, CEC or Leader of Opposition; the Chairman is **not separately elected, appointed or nominated**.
[0:3] - The VP is **not a member** of either House or of any State Legislature (a member elected VP vacates the seat) – so "VP is a member of the Rajya Sabha" is **false**.
[0:4] - As Chairman he does **not vote in the first instance**; he has a **casting vote** in a tie. He does not preside while a resolution for **his own removal** is under consideration.
[0:5] - He has a **formal function** – chairing the Rajya Sabha (so "no formal function attached to his office" is **false**). Ex officio **President of the Indian Council of World Affairs (ICWA)**.
[0:6] - **Article 79**: Parliament = **the President + the Council of States + the House of the People** – the VP, the PM and the Council of Ministers are **not** parts of Parliament.
[0:7] # Election (Article 66)
[0:8] - **Elected, not appointed** – the only one of VP / PM / Governor / CEC who is **not appointed by the President**.
[0:9] - Electoral college = **all members of both Houses of Parliament** – **elected and nominated** MPs (**788** when full). **State Legislative Assemblies and Councils have no role** – so the VP's electoral college is **not** "similar to the President's".
[0:10] - **Proportional representation by single transferable vote**, **secret ballot**; each MP's vote has equal value; **20 proposers + 20 seconders**; governed by the **Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952**.
[0:11] TABLE head: Point | President | Vice-President
  [0:11.0] Electoral college | Elected MPs + elected MLAs (States, Delhi, Puducherry) | All MPs (elected + nominated); no MLAs
  [0:11.1] Qualified for election as member of | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha
  [0:11.2] Proposers / seconders | 50 / 50 | 20 / 20
[0:12] - **Qualifications**: citizen; **35 years**; qualified for election as a member of the **Rajya Sabha** (not the Lok Sabha); no office of profit.
[0:13] - **Article 71**: all doubts and disputes about the VP's election are decided by the **Supreme Court** – final (not the Speaker, President or Parliament); acts done before the election is declared void stay valid. **39th Amendment (1975)** removed the SC's power over President/VP election disputes; **44th Amendment (1978)** restored it.

## SUB 1 | Vice-President – Term, Oath, Resignation, Removal, Vacancy; Acting President (Arts. 65, 67-70)
[1:0] # Term, oath, resignation (Articles 67, 69)
[1:1] - Term **5 years**; continues **until his successor enters upon office** (Art. 67(c)); **re-election any number of times**.
[1:2] - **Oath** administered by the **President** (or a person appointed by him) – Art. 69.
[1:3] - **Resigns** by writing to the **President** (Art. 67(a)) – not the PM or Parliament.
[1:4] # Removal (Article 67(b))
[1:5] - Resolution moved **only in the Rajya Sabha** (not the Lok Sabha, either House or a joint sitting), passed by a **majority of all the then members** of the Rajya Sabha (effective majority) and **agreed to by the Lok Sabha** (simple majority); **14 days' notice**; **no grounds** stated; not impeachment.
[1:6] - "Removed by a two-thirds majority in the Rajya Sabha" – **false** (two-thirds is the President's impeachment).
[1:7] # Vacancy (Article 68)
[1:8] - Vacancy by expiry: election before the term ends. Casual vacancy (death, resignation, removal): filled **as soon as possible** – **no six-month deadline**; the new VP gets a **full 5-year term**.
[1:9] - The Constitution is **silent** on who performs the VP's duties during a vacancy; the **Deputy Chairman** only performs the duties of the **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** – he does **not** "act as Vice-President".
[1:10] # Acting President (Article 65)
[1:11] - On a **vacancy** in the President's office (death, resignation, removal) the VP **acts as President until a new President is elected** and enters office – a **maximum of six months**, because **Art. 62** requires the new President to be elected **within six months** of the vacancy.
[1:12] - When the President is **absent, ill or unable**, the VP **discharges his functions** until he resumes.
[1:13] - While acting: he gets the **powers, immunities and emoluments of the President**; he does **not perform the duties of Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** (the **Deputy Chairman** presides) and draws no Chairman's salary.
[1:14] - If both offices are vacant (e.g. the acting VP resigns), the **Chief Justice of India** (then the senior-most SC judge) discharges the President's functions – **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969**.
[1:15] TABLE head: Point | President | Vice-President
  [1:15.0] Resigns to | Vice-President (Art. 56(1)(a)) | President (Art. 67(a))
  [1:15.1] Removal | Impeachment (Art. 61), two-thirds of total membership of each House | RS resolution (majority of all members) + LS agrees
  [1:15.2] Vacancy filled | Within 6 months (Art. 62) | As soon as possible (Art. 68)
[1:16] - On the President's resignation, the VP immediately informs the **Speaker of the Lok Sabha** (Art. 56(2)).
[1:17] > Exam angle: removal starts only in the RS; while acting as President he does not chair the RS but gets the President's emoluments.

## SUB 3 | Prime Minister – Appointment, Membership, Tenure (Art. 75)
[3:0] # Appointment (Article 75(1))
[3:1] - **"The Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President"** – **Art. 75(1)** (not Arts. 32, 70, 74, 79, 356, 348); **other Ministers** are appointed by the President **on the advice of the PM**. The PM is **not elected** by Parliament or the Lok Sabha and is not merely "nominated by the majority party".
[3:2] - By convention the President appoints the **leader of the majority party / coalition in the Lok Sabha**; in a hung House he uses **discretion** and may ask for a floor test later.
[3:3] - The PM has **full discretion** in choosing his Ministers (any person, MP or not) – the President does not counsel him and has no discretion here. The President **cannot appoint a Minister not recommended by the PM**.
[3:4] TABLE head: Article | Content
  [3:4.0] 54 | Election of the President
  [3:4.1] 75 | Appointment of the PM and Council of Ministers
  [3:4.2] 155 | Appointment of the Governor
  [3:4.3] 164 | Appointment of the CM and State Council of Ministers
[3:5] # Membership
[3:6] - The PM **need not be an MP when appointed** but must become a member of **either House within six months** (not "of the Lok Sabha" only).
[3:7] - May belong to **either House** (UK: Commons by convention); **generally a member of the Lok Sabha**. A PM from the **Rajya Sabha cannot vote in the Lok Sabha** (e.g. on a no-confidence motion) but can speak there.
[3:8] # Tenure, salary, death
[3:9] - **No fixed tenure**; formally holds office during the **President's pleasure** (not Parliament's); in practice while he has the **Lok Sabha's confidence**. No limit on terms.
[3:10] - Salary and allowances fixed by **Parliament** (not the President).
[3:11] - **Death or resignation of the PM automatically dissolves the Council of Ministers**; a Minister's death only creates a vacancy.
[3:12] - **Convention, not text**: the **PM resigns on losing the majority in the Lok Sabha** (the Constitution does not say so). The Finance Minister's House, regional representation and the Speaker acting as President are not rules at all.
[3:13] - The **Prime Minister is the most important political institution in the country** (NCERT Class 9, "Working of Institutions").
[3:14] > Exam angle: "appointed by the President" (×7) and "within six months, either House".

## SUB 5 | Council of Ministers – Responsibility, Non-member Ministers, Rights in Parliament (Arts. 75, 88)
[5:0] # Responsibility
[5:1] - **Article 75(3)**: the Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the House of the People (Lok Sabha)** – a **constitutional obligation**; **not to both Houses / "the Parliament"**, even if the PM or Ministers sit in the Rajya Sabha.
[5:2] - **Article 75(2)**: Ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the President** (not of the PM) – so the **President determines the term and membership** of the Council, on the PM's advice.
[5:3] - Members of **both** Houses can be Ministers – true, but that is not why the Council answers to the Lok Sabha.
[5:4] # Non-member Ministers (Article 75(5))
[5:5] - A person who is not an MP **can be appointed a Minister** (including PM), but a Minister who for **six consecutive months** is not a member of either House **ceases to be a Minister** – **Art. 75(5)** (75(2) = pleasure, 75(3) = collective responsibility, 75(4) = oath).
[5:6] - **S.R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001)**: a non-member cannot be **re-appointed repeatedly** without getting elected.
[5:7] # Ministers in Parliament (Article 88)
[5:8] - Every Minister has the **right to speak and take part** in the proceedings of **either House**, any **joint sitting** and any committee of which he is named a member – but **votes only in the House of which he is a member**. So a **Rajya Sabha Minister can take part in Lok Sabha deliberations** (unlike a nominated member, the Deputy Chairman or the Leader of the House in the RS) but **cannot vote** there.
[5:9] - **Article 75(1B)**: a member disqualified for **defection** cannot be a Minister until **re-elected**.
[5:10] - **July 2026**: a Minister of State's resignation was accepted under **Article 75(2)** after his RS term ended.
[5:11] > Exam angle: "responsible to the Lok Sabha" + "pleasure of the President" statement pairs; six consecutive months (×5).

## SUB 6 | Size of the Council of Ministers – 91st Amendment, 2003
[6:0] - Before 2003 the Constitution set **no limit**; the **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003** inserted **Art. 75(1A)**: total Ministers **including the PM** shall **not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha** (543 → max **81**) – not 10%, 12% or 20%.
[6:1] - **Art. 164(1A)** (States): **15% of the Assembly**, including the CM, **minimum 12**; 294-member Assembly → max **44**.
[6:2] - Aim: curb jumbo ministries and **strengthen the anti-defection law** (also inserted 75(1B), 164(1B)).

## SUB 7 | Categories of Ministers; Cabinet; Deputy PM and Other Non-constitutional Posts
[7:0] - **Three categories**: **Cabinet Ministers**, **Ministers of State** (incl. **independent charge**), **Deputy Ministers** – the Constitution **does not classify** them; the ranking rests on **convention and the PM's choice**.
[7:1] - Hierarchy (top down): **Cabinet Minister → Minister of State → Deputy Minister → Parliamentary Secretary** (Parliamentary Secretaries are **not members** of the Council of Ministers).
[7:2] TABLE head: Basis | Council of Ministers | Union Cabinet

  [7:2.1] Composition | Cabinet Ministers, MoS (independent charge), MoS, Deputy Ministers | PM + Cabinet-rank Ministers only
  [7:2.2] Role | Arts. 74–75 body | Core / supreme decision-making body
[7:3] - The word **"Cabinet"** was inserted (Art. 352(3)) by the **44th Amendment, 1978** – so the **Cabinet, Council of Ministers and Speaker are mentioned** in the Constitution; the **Deputy Prime Minister is not**.
