## SUB 1 | Microfinance – Grameen Model, AP Crisis and Malegam Committee
[79:1:0] # Origin
[79:1:1] - **Microfinance pioneer:** **Prof. Muhammad Yunus** (Bangladesh), founder of the **Grameen Bank** (Nobel Peace Prize 2006) – the Grameen model was developed in **Bangladesh**.
[79:1:2] # AP crisis to regulation
[79:1:3] - 1. **AP crisis 2010:** multiple lending, over-indebtedness, coercive recovery, borrower suicides; the AP MFI loan book then **collapsed** (did not expand).
[79:1:4] - 2. **AP MFI Ordinance, October 2010** (Act 2011): MFIs to register with **district authorities**; doorstep recovery curbed, interest capped.
[79:1:5] - 3. **Malegam Committee** (RBI sub-committee, **October 2010**, Y.H. Malegam) – set up **after the AP crisis**; created the **NBFC-MFI** category (framework **2 December 2011**); margin and interest caps; no more than **2 MFIs per borrower**. (Y.H. Malegam also chaired SEBI's 1995 committee on primary-market disclosure – hence "Malegam = capital market reforms" against Kelkar (tax), Naresh Chandra (corporate governance), Swaminathan.)
[79:1:6] - 4. **MFIN** (**2009**, association of NBFC-MFIs) and **Sa-Dhan**: RBI-recognised **SROs (2014)**.
[79:1:7] # Framework and issues
[79:1:8] - **NBFC-MFI:** non-deposit-taking; at least **75%** of assets in microfinance loans; minimum NOF **₹10 crore** (new registrations from 1 Oct 2022; earlier ₹5 crore, ₹2 crore in the North East).
[79:1:9] - **Microfinance loan (RBI, March 2022):** **collateral-free**; household annual income up to **₹3 lakh**; repayments ≤ **50%** of income.
[79:1:10] - **Funding:** MFIs borrow mainly from **banks and FIs** (they cannot take deposits) – **not government subsidies**.
[79:1:11] - **Impact:** unregulated lending can cause **over-indebtedness**; **technology** cuts costs; regulation (lending caps, margin and interest ceilings) **affects profitability**.
[79:1:12] - **Borrowers:** **95% women**, 80% rural; portfolio share (Mar 2025): **NBFC-MFIs 39%**, banks 32%, SFBs 16%, NBFCs 12%; banks' MFI book fell 40% (Q3 FY26); NCGTC guarantee scheme to June 2026.
[79:1:13] > Statements (APPSC ASO): over-indebtedness ✓, government subsidies the main source ✗, technology cuts costs ✓ → **1 and 3**.
[79:1:14] ~~ Sources: RBI Consultative Document on Regulation of Microfinance, 2021 (rbi.org.in PublicationsView id=20377: AP crisis 2010, Malegam Committee, NBFC-MFI framework 2 Dec 2011, NOF ₹5 crore / ₹2 crore NE, NBFC-MFIs borrow); scconline.com (6 Apr 2022: NOF ₹10 crore for NBFC-MFIs from 1 Oct 2022, glide path to March 2027); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 07 p.10 (SHGs and microfinance); app notes 3:79 sub 9

[79:4:9] - **Public sector dominance (IAS 1993):** commercial banking (nationalised banks) – the keyed answer; but in 1993 the organised term-lending FIs (IFCI, IDBI, SIDBI, IIBI) were also wholly public sector, ICICI excepted.

## SUB 6 | NBFCs – Definition, Regulators, Categories and Scale-Based Regulation
[79:6:0] # What an NBFC is
[79:6:1] - A company registered under the **Companies Act** engaged in **loans and advances, acquisition of shares, stocks, bonds, debentures and government securities**, leasing, hire-purchase – banking services **without a banking licence**.
[79:6:2] - **Principal business (50-50) test:** financial assets > 50% of total assets **and** financial income > 50% of gross income.
[79:6:3] - **100% FDI** through the automatic route; **FDI is prohibited in Nidhi companies** (and chit funds).
[79:6:4] - **Liquid debt mutual funds** = main source of short-term funds for NBFCs.
[79:6:5] # NBFC vs bank
[79:6:6] TABLE head: Basis | Bank | NBFC
  [79:6:6.0] Demand deposits | Yes | No (deposit-taking NBFCs take term deposits only)
  [79:6:6.1] Cheques drawn on itself | Yes | Cannot issue
  [79:6:6.2] Payment and settlement system | Part of it | Not part
  [79:6:6.3] Deposit insurance (DICGC) | Covered | Not covered
  [79:6:6.4] CRR / SLR | Both | No CRR; deposit-taking NBFCs keep liquid assets
  [79:6:6.5] RBI LAF (repo) access | Yes | No
[79:6:7] - **Not all NBFCs register with the RBI:** venture capital, merchant banking, stock broking → **SEBI**; insurance → **IRDAI**; HFCs → NHB (supervision; regulation now RBI); **Nidhi** → **Ministry of Corporate Affairs**; **chit funds → State Governments**.
[79:6:8] - **UPSC 2010:** "cannot acquire government securities" ✗; "cannot accept demand deposits" ✓ → **B only**. **UPSC 2026:** no demand deposits ✓, all must register with RBI ✗, part of payment system / issue cheques ✗, no DICGC cover ✓ → **1 and 4**.
[79:6:9] - **Payments banks cannot lend**; 11 Small Finance Banks (differentiated banks, Nachiket Mor Committee 2013).
[79:6:10] # Categories and capital
[79:6:11] - Categories: **AFC, Loan Company, Investment Company, IFC, NBFC-MFI, CIC, HFC, IDF, NBFC-P2P** – "Regional Rural Banking Company" is NOT one.
[79:6:12] - **NBFC-P2P (2017):** RBI-regulated online platforms for **peer-to-peer lending** – **individuals lend to individuals through online services**.
[79:6:13] TABLE head: Category | Minimum Net Owned Fund
  [79:6:13.0] NBFC-MFI | ₹10 crore (new from 1 Oct 2022; existing ₹7 crore by March 2025, ₹10 crore by March 2027)
  [79:6:13.1] NBFC-ICC | ₹10 crore (raised from ₹2 crore; glide path to March 2027)
  [79:6:13.2] HFC | ₹20 crore
[79:6:14] # Scale-Based Regulation (Oct 2021)
[79:6:15] TABLE head: Layer | Who / what applies
  [79:6:15.0] Base | Smaller non-deposit NBFCs
  [79:6:15.1] Middle | All deposit-taking NBFCs; HFCs, IFCs, CICs

  [79:6:15.3] Top | Kept empty; only for a sharp rise in systemic risk
[79:6:16] - NBFC-UL list 2026-27: **17** companies incl. **Tata Sons** (15 in April 2026); RBI rejected Tata Sons' bid to surrender its licence and asked it to list (13 Sep 2026); proposed single ₹1 lakh crore asset test for UL.
[79:6:17] - **NBFC Ombudsman Scheme 2018** → merged into the **RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2021**.

## SUB 7 | Organised vs Unorganised Financial System
[79:7:0] TABLE head: Organised (RBI-regulated) | Unorganised
  [79:7:0.0] Commercial banks, cooperative banks, foreign banks, DFHI, provident funds, STCI, mutual funds | Indigenous bankers, moneylenders, unregulated finance/loan companies, chit funds, nidhis
  [79:7:0.1] Instruments: call money, Treasury bills, commercial bills, certificates of deposit, CPs, repo, MMMFs | Serves farmers, artisans, small traders
[79:7:1] - **Finance companies are NOT part of the organised money market** (APPSC G2 2017); commercial banks, DFHI and cooperative banks are inside it.
[79:7:2] - **Unregulated non-bank intermediaries:** indigenous bankers, moneylenders, loan companies, chit funds – **commercial and trade bills** are organised money-market **instruments**, not intermediaries.
[79:7:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [79:7:3.0] Indigenous banker | Accepts deposits and lends, combining banking with trade or commission business; deals in hundis (Shroffs, Seths, Mahajans, Multanis, Chettiars)
  [79:7:3.1] Moneylenders | Professional or agricultural; covered by state money-lending laws
  [79:7:3.2] Chit funds | Regulated by the State Government (Chit Funds Act 1982)
  [79:7:3.3] Shadow banking | Bank-like credit intermediation outside full bank regulation; term coined by Paul McCulley, 2007
  [79:7:3.4] Regulatory arbitrage | Using gaps in regulatory treatment to work outside the stricter banking framework
[79:7:4] - One who "**lends only in kind**" is **NOT** an indigenous banker.

## SUB 1 | Central Banking Functions – Banker to Government and Banker's Bank
[80:1:0] # The central bank
[80:1:1] - The **RBI performs the central banking functions** in India; it is the **Central Bank** (not SBI, NABARD, SBH or Central Bank of India, which are banks/DFIs). It is the regulator of the entire banking system.
[80:1:2] TABLE head: Function | Legal basis / content
  [80:1:2.0] Bank of issue | Sec 22: sole right to issue notes except the ₹1 note
  [80:1:2.1] Banker, agent and adviser to Government | Sec 20 (Centre), Sec 21/21A (States, by agreement); payments, receipts, public debt management, borrowing programme, Ways and Means Advances, overdrafts
  [80:1:2.2] Banker's bank | Holds SCBs' deposits and reserves (CRR); lender of last resort; guides and advises banks
  [80:1:2.3] Monetary authority | Price stability with growth in mind; MPC sets the repo rate
  [80:1:2.4] Custodian of forex | Manages forex reserves under FEMA 1999; stabilises the rupee's external value
  [80:1:2.5] Controller of credit | Controls money and credit (quantitative and qualitative measures)
  [80:1:2.6] Payments | Regulates and upgrades payment systems (PSS Act 2007)
  [80:1:2.7] Regulator | Banks, NBFCs (Chapter IIIB), AIFIs, HFCs
[80:1:3] # Banker to Government
[80:1:4] - Manages **public debt of Centre and States** and handles the **Government of India's borrowing programme** (not the Department of Expenditure – UPSC 2004); maintains Government accounts; **Public Debt Office** functions under it.
[80:1:5] - Banker to **all State Governments except Sikkim** (Sikkim: limited agreement, public debt only).
[80:1:6] - **Fiscal agent and adviser** to Government in monetary and financial matters; **represents India at the IMF** (Government's agent for India's IMF membership) and World Bank.
[80:1:7] - **UPSC 2001:** banker to Centre, formulates monetary policy, agent for IMF membership, handles GoI borrowing – **all four correct**.
[80:1:8] # Banker's bank and lender of last resort
[80:1:9] - **Banker's bank** (IAS 2012): (i) other banks **keep deposits/reserves with the RBI**, (ii) the RBI **lends to banks in need**, (iii) it **advises banks on monetary matters** – **all three**.
[80:1:10] - **Lender of last resort (LoLR)** – exclusive RBI function: **emergency liquidity to banks facing a temporary crisis** (UPSC 2021: **2 only**); it is **not** lending to trade/industry or financing budget deficits.
[80:1:11] - **Bank Rate** = rate at which the **RBI lends to / rediscounts bills of commercial banks** (also called the discount rate) – not the rate banks charge borrowers or pay depositors.
[80:1:12] - **FEMA A-R (APPSC):** RBI administers FEMA 1999 "to facilitate external trade and payments and promote orderly development of the forex market"; it also upgrades **payment systems** – both true, **R does not explain A**.

## SUB 2 | Monetary Authority, Credit Control and What RBI Does Not Do
[80:2:0] # Monetary policy and credit control
[80:2:1] - **Monetary (and credit) policy is formulated and implemented by the RBI** (MPC since 2016; six members) – the RBI **regulates and announces the repo and reverse repo rates**, not the Finance Ministry, PM, President, CAG or SBI.
[80:2:2] - **Price stability / controlling inflation** = **RBI** (flexible inflation targeting; target set by the **Finance Ministry in consultation with the RBI**) – not the FSDC, Dept of Consumer Affairs or Expenditure Management Commission.
[80:2:3] - RBI **controls "money and credit"** and **controls credit creation by commercial banks** through quantitative (Bank Rate, repo, CRR, SLR, OMO) and qualitative (selective credit control, margins, moral suasion) tools.
[80:2:4] - Other than market forces, the **RBI intervenes (buys/sells dollars) to manage the exchange rate**.
[80:2:5] - **A-R (APPSC 2024):** institutions ensure stability through monetary policy **because** central banks use repo and CRR to manage liquidity and inflation – **R explains A**.
[80:2:6] - **Regulating and controlling banks** = an activity of the **RBI** (powers under the **Banking Regulation Act 1949**).
[80:2:7] - 2016 framework (MPC + inflation-targeting law) gave **operational independence** on policy rates.
[80:2:8] # What the RBI does NOT do
[80:2:9] - **Credit creation** – done by **commercial banks**; the RBI only **controls** it.
[80:2:10] - **Regulation of foreign trade** (Commerce Ministry/DGFT) – the RBI regulates **currency, credit and forex reserves**.
[80:2:11] - **Fiscal policy** – formulated by the **Ministry of Finance** (not RBI, Finance Commission or Planning Commission).
[80:2:12] - **Granting corporate deeds**; lending to the public; minting coins; issuing the ₹1 note; income-tax collection.
[80:2:13] - **Savings bank interest rate** in nationalised banks: fixed by the **RBI until it was deregulated on 25 Oct 2011** – now each bank fixes it; never the Finance Ministry, Finance Commission or IBA (UPSC 2010: **None of the above**).
[80:2:14] - Does not regulate securities (SEBI), insurance (IRDAI) or pensions (PFRDA).

=== NOTES 3:81 | Money Supply – M0 to M4, High-powered Money, Money Multiplier, Velocity
## SUB 0 | Money – Functions, Kinds and Key Terms
[81:0:0] TABLE head: Class | Functions of money
  [81:0:0.0] Primary | Medium of exchange; measure of value (unit of account)
  [81:0:0.1] Secondary | Store of value; standard of deferred payments; transfer of value
  [81:0:0.2] Contingent | Basis of credit; distribution of national income; giving liquidity
[81:0:1] - **Not a function:** **production of goods** (APPSC).
[81:0:2] # Kinds of money
[81:0:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [81:0:3.0] Fiat money | Notes and coins with no intrinsic value; money by government order; also legal tender
  [81:0:3.1] Legal tender | Money a creditor is under compulsion to accept in settlement of claims (UPSC 2018); RBI notes unlimited, coins limited legal tender
  [81:0:3.2] Commodity money | Money with intrinsic value (gold, silver); a paper note is not commodity money
  [81:0:3.3] Near money | Liquid assets that cannot directly pay: bills of exchange, time deposits, T-bills, bonds near redemption, savings certificates
  [81:0:3.4] Plastic money | Credit cards (and debit/prepaid cards), mainly issued by banks; used in place of cash but not legal tender
  [81:0:3.5] Cheque | Bank (credit) money – an alternative to cash; can be refused, so not legal tender
  [81:0:3.6] Hard / soft money | Hard = backed by gold or credible assets; soft = paper backed by government bonds
  [81:0:3.7] Bank money / cash | Bank money = secondary money; cash = high-powered money
  [81:0:3.8] Token coin | Face value higher than metal value (rupee coin)
[81:0:4] - **Digital Rupee** = legal tender under the RBI Act, amended by the **Finance Act 2022**; not every highly liquid asset is legal tender.
[81:0:5] # Key terms
[81:0:6] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [81:0:6.0] Seigniorage | Face value minus cost of producing currency = issuer's (central bank's) profit from issuing notes; a ₹500 note costs about ₹4 – not a tax
  [81:0:6.1] Gresham's Law | Bad money drives good money out: good (full-value) coins are hoarded, melted and exported; applies to metallic coins
  [81:0:6.2] Velocity | Average number of times a unit of money changes hands in a period; ₹10 used twice = ₹20 of transactions
  [81:0:6.3] Required Reserve Ratio | Fixed percentage of deposits every bank must keep as reserves
[81:0:7] - **Money supply** = **currency + deposit money** (deposits of the public with banks).
[81:0:8] - **Small coins** are phased out when their **production cost exceeds face value**.
[81:0:9] - **Currency expansion** → value of money falls → **higher prices**.
[81:0:10] - **Money supply is NOT part of a country's wealth** (mines, dams, livestock are).
[81:0:11] > Traps: plastic money = credit card, not a coated note; seigniorage = profit, not a tax; Gresham = good money hoarded, melted AND exported.

[81:0:13] - **Desirable attributes of money:** general acceptability, legal-tender status, portability, durability, stable value – a **fall in purchasing power is not** desirable.

## SUB 2 | Monetary Aggregates M1 to M4 and Liquidity Aggregates
[81:2:0] TABLE head: Aggregate | Components | Type
  [81:2:0.0] M1 | Currency with public + demand deposits with banks + other deposits with RBI (NCERT: CU + DD) | Narrow money, most liquid
  [81:2:0.1] M2 | M1 + post office savings bank deposits | Narrow
  [81:2:0.2] M3 | M1 + time deposits with banks | Broad money ("aggregate monetary resources")
  [81:2:0.3] M4 | M3 + all post office deposits (excluding NSCs) | Broad, least liquid
[81:2:1] - **Second Working Group on Money Supply (1977)** → RBI publishes M1–M4 besides Reserve Money.
[81:2:2] - **M3** = most commonly used broad money = **currency with public + demand deposits + time deposits + other deposits with RBI** (all four).
[81:2:3] - **M3 − M1 = time deposits**; time deposits are **not in M1**; M3 = M1 (not M2) + time deposits.
[81:2:4] - **Liquidity order:** M1 > M2 > M3 > M4; assets: **currency > demand deposits > savings deposits > time deposits** (4-1-3-2).
[81:2:5] - **Highly liquid asset** = converted into cash in a very short time with little loss of value.
[81:2:6] - **Banks' liabilities:** deposits are the main liability; **time deposits** = the largest component, far above demand deposits.
[81:2:7] # New aggregates (1998)
[81:2:8] - **Third Working Group** (1998), chaired by **Y. V. Reddy**: residency-based **NM0–NM3** (separate short-term and long-term deposits).
[81:2:9] - **L1, L2, L3** = **liquidity aggregates**: add post-office deposits, term money and CDs of FIs, NBFC public deposits.
[81:2:10] > APPSC repeats: L1-L3 = liquidity aggregates; M1 = narrow money; M3 components; liquidity order.
[81:2:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 04 p.11-12; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.20, 04 p.3-4, p.40; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #38 p.56-57; OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 03 p.82-83; Ramesh Singh 10th ed. ch.12 p.12.30-12.31; app notes 3:81 sub 5
[81:2:12] - **Demand deposits** can be withdrawn instantly; **time deposits** are locked for a fixed period and are the least liquid.
[81:2:13] - **US money supply, June 1933 (Depression trough) to end-1939:** rose steadily (gold inflows after the 1933 devaluation) – keyed "**increased by about 90%**".
## SUB 3 | Money Multiplier and Credit Creation
[81:3:0] - **Money multiplier = money supply ÷ reserve money (M3 ÷ M0)**; m = (1 + cdr) / (cdr + rdr).
[81:3:1] - 10% reserves, no leakages: m = 1/0.10 = **10**; ₹100 supports about **₹1,000** of deposits.
[81:3:2] - **Credit creation:** banks lend beyond their cash; **loans exceeding deposits = credit creation**; capacity depends on **cash reserves** (strictly, a bank lends out its **excess reserves**).
[81:3:3] - **Interbank transfer** to a bank brings it **reserves**, raising its lending potential.
[81:3:4] TABLE head: Money creator | Creates
  [81:3:4.0] RBI | Reserve money (notes)
  [81:3:4.1] Government | Coins and ₹1 note
  [81:3:4.2] Commercial banks | Deposit money by lending beyond CRR/SLR
[81:3:5] # What moves the multiplier and money supply
[81:3:6] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [81:3:6.0] Higher CRR, SLR or cash preference (cdr) | Lower multiplier, less credit creation
  [81:3:6.1] Stronger banking habit | Higher multiplier (UPSC 2019, 2021)
  [81:3:6.2] RBI buys G-secs from the public; Government borrows from RBI | Money supply rises (UPSC 2012: 1 and 3)
  [81:3:6.3] RBI sells G-secs; public deposits currency in banks | Falls / no rise
  [81:3:6.4] Cash withdrawal ₹1 lakh from a demand deposit | Unchanged (DD −₹1 lakh, currency +₹1 lakh) (UPSC 2020)
[81:3:7] - **Raising SLR** locks more funds in liquid assets (kept by the bank itself; CRR is kept with RBI) → fewer loanable funds; an SLR cut frees funds.
[81:3:8] - **CRR increase** → banks have less to lend.

## SUB 6 | High-powered Money (Reserve Money, M0)
[81:6:0] - Names: **reserve money, monetary base, base money, central bank money** = **M0** – the "most important indicator"; **basis of credit creation**.
[81:6:1] - **H = currency held by the public + cash reserves of banks (with themselves and with RBI) + other deposits with RBI**; no time deposits, no post-office deposits, no forex held by persons.
[81:6:2] TABLE head: Side | Components
  [81:6:2.0] Uses side | Currency with public + banks' cash in hand + bankers' deposits with RBI (legal reserves) + other deposits with RBI

[81:6:3] - **Currency in circulation** = notes + rupee coins + small coins (held by public + banks' cash in hand).
[81:6:4] - **"Other" deposits with RBI**: quasi-government and financial institutions, foreign central banks and governments, international agencies such as the **IMF**.
[81:6:5] > High-powered money = M0, not M2 (an old APPSC key gave M2).

=== NOTES 3:82 | Monetary Policy – Objectives, Instruments (crr, Slr, Repo, Reverse Repo, Msf, Omo, Laf), Transmission
## SUB 0 | CRR and SLR – Reserve Ratios
[82:0:0] # Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)
[82:0:1] - **CRR = Cash Reserve Ratio:** the share of a bank's **Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL)** that it must keep as **cash balances with the RBI** (CDS 2020; APPSC AEE 2008).
[82:0:2] - **"Required reserve ratio"** = the **certain percentage of deposits every bank must keep as reserves** = **CRR** (APPSC Degree Lecturers 2023).
[82:0:3] - CRR is **fixed by the RBI (the central bank)**, not the Finance Ministry or the market (APPSC G2 2011); governed by **Section 42 of the RBI Act, 1934**; **no floor or ceiling** since 2006.
[82:0:4] - Banks get **no interest** on CRR balances; CRR is in **cash only**.
[82:0:5] - **Raising CRR:** commercial banks have **less money to lend** (not the RBI or the Union Government) → **liquidity falls**, **credit creation falls** → helps control inflation (UPSC 2010 ×4; UPPSC 2004, 2010; CGPSC 2017).
[82:0:6]   - APPSC G1: a CRR hike causes **fall in money circulation** and **low demand for goods and services** (I and II); it does **NOT** raise purchasing power or the banks' lending capacity.
[82:0:7] - **Lowering CRR:** **credit creation increases** (APPSC G2 2017); banks get **higher leverage to liquidity** and the economy **may see increased investment** (NextGen: lower CRR "helps banks provide loans … for investment purposes" and "boosts the growth rate").
[82:0:8] - **Temporary I-CRR (Aug 2023):** **10%** on the increase in NDTL (19 May–28 Jul 2023), announced **10 Aug 2023**, effective fortnight from 12 Aug; **impounded about ₹1.1 lakh crore**; released in phases (9 Sep, 23 Sep, 7 Oct 2023) (APPSC G2 Mains 2025: both statements correct).
[82:0:9] - CRR applies to the **whole NDTL**; the I-CRR applied only to the **incremental deposit base** (not to "new borrowers") (APPSC G1 2024).
[82:0:10] # Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)
[82:0:11] - **SLR:** minimum share of NDTL a bank keeps **with itself** in liquid assets **before lending** – **cash, gold and unencumbered approved/Government securities (G-secs, T-bills, SDLs)**; **not priority-sector loans** (APPSC G1 2023: I, II and III).
[82:0:12] - **SLR = mechanism by which commercial banks provide credit to the Government** (they buy G-secs to meet it) (UPSC 2010 ×3).
[82:0:13] - Older wording "ratio between cash in hand and total assets" = **SLR** (UPSC 1998; UPPSC 2007) – strictly SLR is liquid assets to NDTL.
[82:0:14] - Governed by **Section 24, Banking Regulation Act, 1949**; range **0%–40% of NDTL**; the **25% floor** was removed by the **BR (Amendment) Act, 2007**; **MSS securities count** towards SLR.
[82:0:15] - **Shortfall penalty:** **bank rate + 3%**, then **bank rate + 5%** if it continues.
[82:0:16] - **SLR cut of 50 bps** → more lendable funds → **scheduled commercial banks may cut lending rates** (UPSC 2015 ×2) – not "GDP up drastically" or "liquidity down".
[82:0:17] - **Raise SLR to fight inflation; cut SLR for growth.**
[82:0:18] # Trend

  [82:0:19.0] 1991-92 | 38.5% | 15%
  [82:0:19.1] Since reforms | Both reduced (Narasimham Committee) | Both reduced
  [82:0:19.2] 5 Jan 2019 | 19.25% (Dec 2018 review: −25 bps every quarter) | 4%

  [82:0:19.4] 2026 (now) | 18% | 3% (cut 4% → 3% in 2025, ~₹2.5 lakh crore released)
[82:0:20] # CRR vs SLR
[82:0:21] TABLE head: Basis | CRR | SLR
  [82:0:21.0] Kept | With RBI | By the bank itself
  [82:0:21.1] Form | Cash only | Cash, gold, approved securities
  [82:0:21.2] Return | No interest | Earns interest on G-secs
  [82:0:21.3] Law | Sec 42, RBI Act 1934 | Sec 24, BR Act 1949
[82:0:22] > Trap: SLR "19.25%" (APPSC G2, 2019 review) is now 18%; "required reserve ratio" = CRR, not SLR.

## SUB 1 | Policy Rates – Repo, Reverse Repo, Bank Rate, MSF and LAF
[82:1:0] # Definitions
[82:1:1] TABLE head: Rate | Who lends to whom | Key point
  [82:1:1.0] Repo | RBI lends to banks short term (overnight) against G-secs (repurchase agreement) | Principal policy rate; injects liquidity, regulates money supply
  [82:1:1.1] Reverse repo | Banks lend to / park funds with RBI | Absorbs surplus liquidity; always below repo
  [82:1:1.2] Bank Rate | RBI's official / discount rate for long-term loans; rediscounts bills of exchange | Penal rates linked to it; signal rate
  [82:1:1.3] MSF | Banks borrow overnight from RBI in emergency, dipping into SLR | Contains volatility in the overnight inter-bank rate; ceiling of corridor
  [82:1:1.4] SDF | Banks park surplus with RBI without collateral | Floor of corridor (since April 2022)
[82:1:2] - **Repo** = "Repurchase Option": bank sells G-secs to RBI and buys them back later; the difference is the interest – it is **not the cost of collateral** (CDS 2020) and has **nothing to do with the rupee's exchange value** (UPPSC 1998: "None of the above").
[82:1:3] - **Rate at which RBI lends short-term to banks / at which banks borrow from RBI = Repo rate** (UPPSC 2005, 2008, 2013; SI 2019; APPSC G2; AP recruitment); comes under **monetary policy**; used **to regulate liquidity** (APPSC G2 2017).
[82:1:4] - **Rate at which banks lend to / park funds with RBI = Reverse repo** (UPPSC 2008 ×2).
[82:1:5] - **Bank Rate** = rate at which **RBI discounts / rediscounts bills of exchange** (UPSC 1995; Degree Lecturers 2017); = **official rate of interest charged by the central bank** (State PSC).
[82:1:6] - **BPSC 2015 trap:** "Bank Rate = short-term" and "Repo = long-term" are **both wrong** (swapped): Bank Rate is long-term, repo short-term.
[82:1:7] - **LAF (June 2000):** the window through which banks **borrow via repos** and **lend via reverse repos** – "borrowing through repurchase agreements" = **LAF** (APPSC G2).
[82:1:8] - **MSF (May 2011):** began at repo + **100 bps**, up to **1% of NDTL**; now repo + **25 bps**, up to **2%** of NDTL; aligned with Bank Rate in **Feb 2012**; minimum ₹1 crore.
[82:1:9] - **MSF rate and NDTL** are terms of **banking operations** (UPSC 2014 ×2).
[82:1:10] - **LAF access:** scheduled commercial banks and **standalone Primary Dealers** (registered as NBFCs); other NBFCs (HFCs, MFIs, asset-finance cos) cannot use it.
[82:1:11] - **Repo − reverse repo gap:** **1.00 percentage point** in June 2010 – June 2011 (from Sep 2010); from **May 2011** only repo is fixed and reverse repo set **100 bps below** it.
[82:1:12] # The corridor
[82:1:13] TABLE head: Date | SDF (floor) | Repo | MSF = Bank Rate (ceiling) | Stance
  [82:1:13.0] 5 Aug 2026 | 5.00% | 5.25% (unanimous hold) | 5.50% | Neutral
  [82:1:13.1] 7 Oct 2026 | 5.25% | 5.50% (+25 bps, unanimous) | 5.75% | Calibrated tightening
[82:1:14] - Corridor width **50 bps**: SDF = repo − 25 bps; MSF = Bank Rate = repo + 25 bps. **Fixed-rate reverse repo 3.35%** (May 2026), used at RBI's discretion since SDF.
[82:1:15] - **SDF** (proposed 2015, enabled by **Budget 2018-19**, began **April 2022**): absorbs surplus liquidity **without collateral** – the only collateral-free absorption tool (APPSC G2).
[82:1:16] # Stances
[82:1:17] TABLE head: Stance | Meaning
  [82:1:17.0] Accommodative | Supports growth; cuts likely
  [82:1:17.1] Neutral | Either way, data-dependent
  [82:1:17.2] Calibrated tightening | No cuts; hikes measured, not at every meeting
  [82:1:17.3] Hawkish / Dovish | Worried about inflation, tightens / worried about growth, easing bias
[82:1:18] - Path: neutral → accommodative (Apr 2025) → **neutral (June 2025)**, kept through Aug 2026 → **calibrated tightening (Oct 2026)**.

## SUB 2 | OMO, Sterilisation and Liquidity Management
[82:2:0] # Open Market Operations
[82:2:1] - **OMO** = **purchase and sale of Government securities (and T-bills) by the RBI** (UPSC 2013; UPPSC 2010 ×2; NDA 2012) – "trading in securities", not forex auctions or gold.
[82:2:2] - OMO relates to **both sale and purchase** of G-secs (APPSC G2 2011); RBI deals **through banks, not directly with the public**.
[82:2:3] - **Sell G-secs → absorbs liquidity** (fights inflation); **buy G-secs → injects liquidity**, money supply up, **interest rates fall** (OPSC 2018: "to lower interest rates, purchase securities").
[82:2:4] - OMO is a **quantitative** tool and "**a part of credit policy**"; main tool for **durable** liquidity.
[82:2:5] - **Money supply rises** with **central-bank purchase of G-secs from the public** and **Government borrowing from the central bank** (UPSC 2012: I and III); depositing currency in banks only shifts money; sale of G-secs reduces it.
[82:2:6] # Sterilisation
[82:2:7] - **Sterilisation** = offsetting the rupee liquidity created by forex inflows (RBI buying dollars) by **selling G-secs through OMO / MSS** or VRRR – part of sterilisation = **conducting OMOs** (UPSC 2023 ×2).
[82:2:8] - **MSS (April 2004):** T-bills/dated securities issued to withdraw excess liquidity; proceeds in a **separate account the Government cannot spend**.
[82:2:9] # Other liquidity tools
[82:2:10] TABLE head: Tool | Feature
  [82:2:10.0] VRR / VRRR | Auction-based repo / reverse repo; VRRR absorbs for longer than overnight SDF
  [82:2:10.1] Fine-tuning operations | Short VRR/VRRR auctions to keep liquidity in line with the monetary policy stance – 49 in FY24 (APPSC G2 Mains 2025)
  [82:2:10.2] LTRO (2020) | 1–3 year funds at repo against G-secs
  [82:2:10.3] USD/INR buy-sell swap | Injects rupees without changing RBI's balance-sheet size ($5 bn, 3-year, Dec 2025)
[82:2:11] - **Surplus liquidity** pulls money-market rates **below repo** and speeds transmission; 4 Sep 2026: RBI absorbed **₹6.02 lakh crore** through two 3-day VRRRs.
[82:2:12] - **G-sec yields** are influenced by **US Fed actions, RBI actions and domestic inflation and short-term rates** – all three (UPSC 2021).
[82:2:13] > UPSC 2024 (official key: all three): NBFCs via Primary Dealers can use LAF; **FIIs/FPIs can hold G-secs**; **stock exchanges can run separate debt platforms**.

## SUB 3 | Quantitative vs Qualitative Instruments
[82:3:0] TABLE head: Basis | Quantitative (general) | Qualitative (selective)
  [82:3:0.0] Aim | Volume and cost of total credit / money supply | Direction of credit among borrowers and sectors
  [82:3:0.1] Other name | Traditional methods | Selective credit control
  [82:3:0.2] Tools | Bank rate, repo, reverse repo, OMO, CRR, SLR (variable reserve ratios) | Margin requirements, moral suasion, rationing of credit, consumer-credit regulation, direct action, differential interest rates, guidelines
[82:3:1] - **Not selective / not qualitative:** **variable (cash) reserve ratios** (UPSC 1995 ×2; NDA 2014) and **Bank Rate policy** (APPSC G1).
[82:3:2] - **Not quantitative:** **rationing of credit** (UPPSC 2017). **OMO** = quantitative (UPPSC 2017).
[82:3:3] - **Selective controls of RBI:** **margin requirements and moral suasion** – not CRR/SLR (CDS 2017).
[82:3:4] - **Margin** = value of security − loan; a **higher margin = smaller loan** against the same security.
[82:3:5] - **Moral suasion:** persuasion – closed-door meetings, appeals, warnings – not force.
[82:3:6] - **Not a credit-control method:** **devaluation of the rupee** (exchange-rate step).
[82:3:7] - **Components of monetary policy:** **Bank rate and OMO** – **public debt and public revenue are fiscal** (UPSC 2015; CDS 2017).
[82:3:8] - **Variable reserve ratios and OMO** are means of **monetary policy** (UPSC 1993).

## SUB 4 | Tool → Effect: Dear vs Cheap Money
[82:4:0] TABLE head: Tool | Control inflation (dear, tight, contractionary) | Boost growth (cheap, easy, expansionary)
  [82:4:0.0] CRR, SLR | Raise | Cut
  [82:4:0.1] Repo, reverse repo, Bank Rate, MSF | Raise | Cut
  [82:4:0.2] OMO | Sell G-secs | Buy G-secs
[82:4:1] - **'Dear money'** = **high rate of interest**, money hard to borrow (APPSC AEE 2011); cheap money = low rates.
[82:4:2] - **Inflation rising steadily** → central bank **increases the repo rate** (SSC 2020).
[82:4:3] - **Anti-inflation set:** **raise Bank Rate, CRR and SLR** – **buying** G-secs is expansionary (UPPSC RO 2016: 1, 2 and 3).
[82:4:4] - **Lowering Bank Rate** → **more liquidity** (UPSC 2011); it is **not a remedy for inflation** (APSLPRB 2012) – so CPI inflation **rises if Bank Rate is decreased** (RAS 2016 key).
[82:4:5] - **Increase in Bank Rate** = **tight (dear) money policy** (UPSC 2013).
[82:4:6] - **Expansionist policy would NOT increase the MSF rate**; it would cut SLR, Bank Rate and repo (UPSC 2020: only II).
[82:4:7] - **Interest-rate cut** → **higher investment expenditure** (UPSC 2014 ×2) – not lower consumption, more tax or more savings.
[82:4:8] - CRR/SLR cut → more lendable funds and a higher **money multiplier**; policy rates regulate the **capacity for credit creation**.
[82:4:9] - Monetary tools act on **demand**; supply-driven food inflation needs **buffer stocks, duty cuts, farm reform**.

## SUB 5 | Objectives, Authority and Post-1991 Shifts
[82:5:0] - **Monetary policy** = macro policy on **money supply**, **framed by the RBI** (APPSC Welfare Organiser; UPPSC 2006) under the **RBI Act, 1934**; the **MPC** fixes the repo rate since 2016.
[82:5:1] - **Primary objective:** **price stability while keeping in mind growth** (MPPSC 2025; APPSC flashcard); also financial stability, exchange-rate stability.
[82:5:2] - **Not an objective:** **equitable distribution of income and assets** – a fiscal goal (UPPSC 2011).
[82:5:3] - **Fiscal vs monetary:** fiscal = Government, **taxes, spending, public debt**, direct but slow (legislative lags); monetary = RBI/MPC, indirect, quick (meets every two months). Fiscal deficit is fiscal policy.
[82:5:4] - **Post-1991 policy change:** **deregulation of interest rates** (AP recruitment).
[82:5:5] - **Post-1991 challenge:** **volatility in exchange rates** from open capital flows (not high reserves, low inflation or revenue surplus); ad hoc T-bills ended 1997 → **Ways and Means Advances**.
[82:5:6] TABLE head: Phase | Framework
  [82:5:6.0] 1985–1998 | Monetary targeting with feedback (M3; Chakravarty 1985)
  [82:5:6.1] 1998–2014/16 | Multiple indicator approach (LAF 2000, MSS 2004, MSF 2011) – dropped as opaque
  [82:5:6.2] 2016 onwards | Flexible inflation targeting (Urjit Patel Committee 2014)
[82:5:7] - Transmission obstacles: administered **small-savings rates** (Rakesh Mohan Committee 2004: align with G-sec yields), NPAs, informal credit outside RBI's reach, heavy Government borrowing (**fiscal dominance**).
[82:5:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 06 p.4-5 (credit control, monetary policy, inflation and growth in mind); Rocket Indian Economy p.10 (RBI conducts monetary policy); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 04 p.132 (Chakravarty Committee 1985); app notes 3:82 sub 0, 1, 9
