## SUB 0 | Folk Arts – Basics and Firsts
[125:0:0] - **Folk arts (Janapada Kalalu)** = arts of the **Janapadam** (village).
[125:0:1] - **Biruduraju Ramaraju** wrote the **first book on the theory of folk knowledge** in Telugu.
[125:0:2] - **World Folk Day: 22 August**.
[125:0:3] - **1857–1956** = "golden era" of dance.
[125:0:4] - **Gathasaptasati** records **couple and choir dances** of rural folk.

## SUB 3 | Kuchipudi – Origin and Distinction
[125:3:0] - Classical dance of AP; named after **Kuchipudi (Kuchelapuram) village, Krishna district**; shaped by **Siddhendra Yogi**; one of India's **eight classical** forms; "global ambassador of Indian culture".
[125:3:1] APPSC GS 2024: "Kuchipudi originated in Krishna district" = correct (2019 option: "Krishna and Guntur").
[125:3:2] TABLE head: Kuchipudi | Bharatanatyam
  [125:3:2.0] Andhra; dance-drama from Bhagavata Mela (Brahmin male troupes) | Tamil Nadu; solo temple dance
  [125:3:2.1] Dancers speak dialogue/sing | No dialogue; geometric solo repertoire
  [125:3:2.2] Tarangam – dancing on the rim of a brass plate | —
[125:3:3] - Growth: **Bhagavata Mela** dance-drama of hereditary Brahmin families → Siddhendra Yogi → **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (last Qutb Shahi) gifted **Kuchipudi village** to the performers.
[125:3:4] - **Lakumadevi**, court dancer of **Kumaragiri Reddy**, was famed for **Bharatanatyam**, not Kuchipudi.

=== NOTES 1:126 | Battle of Talikota (1565), the Aravidu Dynasty and the Legacy of Vijayanagara
## SUB 0 | Battle of Talikota – 1565
[126:0:0] - **23 January 1565**: Vijayanagara **defeated**; Hampi **looted about six months** (severe, not total damage).
[126:0:1] - Names: **Talikota** (Sewell), **Rakshasi-Tangadi / Rakshasa Tangadi** (Heras), **Bannihatti** (Sherwani).
[126:0:2] - King: **Sadasiva Raya** (nominal, last Tuluva); real power with regent **Aliya Rama Raya** (son-in-law of Krishnadevaraya).
[126:0:3] - Rama Raya was beheaded by **Hussain Nizam Shah**; Muslim contingents deserted.
[126:0:4] # The Confederacy (full list)
[126:0:5] TABLE head: Sultanate | Ruler
  [126:0:5.0] Bijapur | Ali Adil Shah
  [126:0:5.1] Ahmadnagar | Hussain Nizam Shah
  [126:0:5.2] Golconda | Ibrahim Qutb Shah (main organiser)
  [126:0:5.3] Bidar | Ali Barid Shah
[126:0:6] **Berar (Imad Shahi)** stayed out. The enemy was the Deccan Sultanates, NOT the Bahmani kingdom (already broken up), Mughals or Gajapatis.
[126:0:7] # Rama Raya's Policy and Causes
[126:0:8] TABLE head: Cause | Fact
  [126:0:8.0] Divide and rule | 1546-47 backed Ahmadnagar vs Bijapur and Bidar, took the Raichur doab; 1557-63 backed Bijapur and razed Ahmadnagar
  [126:0:8.1] Marriage links | Chand Bibi – Ali Adil Shah; Bibi Jamal – Ibrahim Qutb Shah
  [126:0:8.2] Shelter to rebel | Jagadeva Rao of Golconda
[126:0:9] # Results
[126:0:10] - Sadasiva and **Tirumala** fled to **Penukonda**; centre moved to the **Telugu country**; Nayakas became autonomous.
[126:0:11] - Sewell called the city a **"graveyard"** – a view that may be exaggerated.
## SUB 1 | Aravidu Dynasty (1570-1680)
[126:1:0] - **Fourth** Vijayanagara dynasty; **7 rulers**; named after **Araviti (Aravidu)** village, **Kurnool** district.
[126:1:1] # Rulers in Order
[126:1:2] TABLE head: Ruler | Key fact
  [126:1:2.0] Tirumala Raya (1570-72) | Founder, Rama Raya's brother; removed Sadasiva; crowned at Penukonda, 1570
  [126:1:2.1] Sriranga I (1572-85) | Eldest son; lost Kondaveedu to Ibrahim Qutb Shah
  [126:1:2.2] Venkatapati II (1585-1614) | Greatest, longest (29 years)
  [126:1:2.3] Sriranga II (1614) | Killed by Jagga Raya
  [126:1:2.4] Ramadeva (1616-30) | Battle of Toppur (1616) = "second Talikota"; Jagga Raya killed
  [126:1:2.5] Venkatapati III (1630-43) | Grant of Madras
  [126:1:2.6] Sriranga III (1643-80) | Last; defeated at Vandavasi (1642) by Mir Jumla of Golconda
[126:1:3] - Toppur was won by **Velugoti Yachama Nayaka** and **Raghunatha of Tanjore**.
[126:1:4] # Capital Shifts
[126:1:5] Hampi (till 1565) → **Penukonda** (1570, Tirumala) → **Chandragiri** (Venkata II) → **Vellore** (1600).
## SUB 2 | Venkatapati Raya II (1585-1614)
[126:2:0] - Titles: **"Second Krishnadevaraya"**, **"Second Andhra Bhoja"**.
[126:2:1] - **Vilapakam inscription (1601)**: genealogy of Tuluva and Aravidu; **Siddhavatam fort (Kadapa) inscription (1605)** also his.
[126:2:2] - Allowed **Dutch** trade (factory) at **Pulicat**.
[126:2:3] - Allowed **Jesuit churches at Nellore and Chandragiri** (APPSC 2022).
[126:2:4] - Court: **Appayya Dikshita** (Shaivarka Mani Dipika).
## SUB 3 | Grant of Madras – 1639
[126:3:0] TABLE head: Point | Fact
  [126:3:0.0] Reign | Venkatapati Raya III

  [126:3:0.2] Grantee | Francis Day, English East India Company, Machilipatnam council
[126:3:1] - **Damarla Nayaks:** Velama feudatories of the Aravidus, ruling from **Srikalahasti** (and Wandiwash); **Venkatadri** and brother **Ayyappa** granted the Madras site.

=== NOTES 1:127 | Mughal Empire – Babur: Panipat, Khanwa, Chanderi, Ghaghra; Tuzuk-i-baburi
## SUB 0 | Babur – Background and Career
[127:0:0] - Given name: **Zahir-ud-din Muhammad**; "Babur" = **tiger** (a nickname/title).
[127:0:1] - Born **1483** in **Farghana** (Central Asia).
[127:0:2] - Descent: **Timur** on father's side, **Chingiz Khan** on mother's side.
[127:0:3] - Mughals were **Chagatai Turks** – named after Chagatai, **Genghis Khan's second son**, ruler of Central Asia and Turkistan.
[127:0:4] - Babur's arrival set up the **Timurid dynasty** in India.
[127:0:5] # Career Timeline

  [127:0:6.0] 1483 | Born, Farghana
  [127:0:6.1] 1501 | Took Samarkand (held only 8 months)

  [127:0:6.3] 1526 | First Battle of Panipat – Lodis displaced
  [127:0:6.4] 1527 | Khanwa – Rajput power broken
  [127:0:6.5] 1528 | Chanderi – Medini Rai
  [127:0:6.6] 1529 | Ghaghra – Afghans of Bihar and Bengal
  [127:0:6.7] 1530 | Died at Agra; buried at Kabul (Bagh-e-Babur)
[127:0:7] # Invited to Invade India

## SUB 2 | First Battle of Panipat – 1526
[127:2:0] - Babur defeated **Ibrahim Lodi**, the **last Delhi Sultan** (Lodis = last Sultanate dynasty).
[127:2:1] - Ended the Delhi Sultanate; began Mughal rule.
[127:2:2] - Numbers: Babur's **12,000** vs about **1,00,000** of Ibrahim's troops.
[127:2:3] - Gains: **Delhi and Agra** (APPSC 2023) + the **Lodi treasure**.
[127:2:4] - He shared the treasure lavishly → nickname **"Qalandar"**; treasury left depleted for Humayun.
[127:2:5] Babur's side used **no war elephants** at Panipat. The Rumi carts were a barrier for guns, not elephants.
## SUB 3 | Military Tactics and Technology
[127:3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning | Origin
  [127:3:0.0] Tulughma | Flanking units wheel round to encircle the enemy | Uzbek
  [127:3:0.1] Rumi method / araba | Carts chained as a barrier for matchlockmen and guns | Ottoman field defence
  [127:3:0.2] Artillery | Commanded by Ustad Ali and Mustafa | Ottoman gunners
[127:3:1] # Gunpowder Debate
[127:3:2] - Panipat I = **first large-scale use of field artillery** in North Indian warfare.
[127:3:3] - Gunpowder and firearms were **not wholly unknown** in India before Babur (used to break fort walls).
[127:3:4] - Babur is credited with its use in **cannons and guns**.
[127:3:5] Babur did NOT introduce the arch and dome; those came with the Delhi Sultanate.
## SUB 4 | Battle of Khanwa – 1527
[127:4:0] - Coalition: **Rana Sanga's** Rajput confederacy + Afghan **Mahmud Lodi** + **Hasan Khan Mewati**.
[127:4:1] - Tactics: **trenches and barricades** against Sanga's elephants; artillery decisive again.
[127:4:2] - Religious appeal before battle: declared **jihad**, **renounced wine**, remitted the **tamgha** (trade tax) for Muslims.
[127:4:3] - Title after victory: **"Ghazi"**.
[127:4:4] - **Significance:** Khanwa, more than Panipat, **firmly established** Mughal power in India.
## SUB 5 | Empire at Babur's Death – Scope and Limits
[127:5:0] - Extent: **Kabul, Badakhshan → Punjab, Delhi, Agra → borders of Bihar** (after Ghaghra).
[127:5:1] - Included Kabul, Punjab and modern Uttar Pradesh – **not modern Rajasthan**.
[127:5:2] - Ruled through **jagirs** and **paraganas**; **Badakhshan** was given to Humayun.
[127:5:3] - Humayun took the throne on **29 December 1530**.
[127:5:4] # Limits of the 4-year Rule
[127:5:5] - Mughal authority had **shallow roots**; Bengal unconquered; Afghans still claimed Delhi.
[127:5:6] - Gujarat and Malwa united under **Bahadur Shah**.
[127:5:7] - **No administrative system** was set up; treasury empty.
[127:5:8] **Mansabdari** system = **Akbar's**, not Babur's.
[127:5:9] - AP / South angle: Babur was a contemporary of **Krishnadevaraya** and Golconda's **Sultan Quli**; Mughals did not interfere in the Deccan in his time.
## SUB 6 | Tuzuk-i-baburi (baburnama)
[127:6:0] - Babur's memoirs, written in **Chagatai Turki** (Turkish).
[127:6:1] - Translated into **Persian** (1589) under Akbar by **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan**; into English first by **Leyden and Erskine**, later by **Annette Beveridge** (from the Turki original).
[127:6:2] - On India: **five Muslim and two Hindu powers**; Hindu ones = **Vijayanagara and Mewar**.
[127:6:3] - Praised **Krishnadevaraya** as the most powerful ruler in India.
[127:6:4] Babur wrote in Turki, but the **Mughal court language was Persian** (UPPSC 2025: assertion true, reason false).
