=== Battle of Talikota (1565) – Rakshasa Tangadi
- **Date:** **23 January 1565** (some books 26 January) – year **1565** (APPSC FRO 2022, 2023; JL 2004; UPPCS 1993; MPPCS 1997) – not 1526 (Panipat I), 1545, 1555, 1560, 1564 or 1576.
- **Names:** **Talikota / Tallikota** (Sewell, *A Forgotten Empire*); **Rakshasa-Tangadi / Rakshasi-Tangadi** (NextGen: Nilakanta Sastri; app notes: Fr. Heras); **Bannihatti** (H.K. Sherwani); fought near Tallikota between the **Krishna** and its tributary.
- **King:** **Sadasiva Raya** (nominal, last Tuluva) – real power with regent **Aliya Rama Raya**, **son-in-law of Krishnadevaraya** (APPSC JL 2006) – not Achyuta Raya, Tirumala Raya or Venkata.
- **Rama Raya's policy:** the "adventurous ruler" who **played one Sultan against another** until they united (APPSC 2019 Social & Cultural History) – not Krishnadevaraya, Harihara I/II.
| Sultanate | Ruler at Talikota |
|---|---|
| **Bijapur** (Adil Shahi) | **Ali Adil Shah** |
| **Ahmadnagar** (Nizam Shahi) | **Hussain Nizam Shah** – beheaded Rama Raya |
| **Golconda** (Qutb Shahi) | **Ibrahim Qutb Shah** (key organiser) |
| **Bidar** (Barid Shahi) | **Ali Barid Shah** |
- **Berar (Imad Shahi)** stayed out → enemy = **the Deccan Sultanates / combined forces of Bijapur, Ahmadnagar and Golconda** (APPSC G1; UPPSC 2014) – **not the Bahmani kingdom** (already broken up), the Mughals or the Marathas.
- **"Who fought Rakshasi-Tangadi?" (APPSC G1):** **the Adil Shahis of Bijapur and Ramaraya** (Rocket Sheet wording) – Berar's Imad Shahis stayed out; Veeranarasimha is a Tuluva of 1505; "Ramaraya and Nizam Shah" names only the sultan who killed him.
- **Causes:** 1546-47 Rama Raya backed Ahmadnagar against Bijapur and Bidar and took the **Raichur doab**; 1557-63 backed Bijapur and razed Ahmadnagar; sheltered **Jagadeva Rao**, a rebel of Golconda; marriage links – **Chand Bibi–Ali Adil Shah**, Bibi Jamal–Ibrahim Qutb Shah.
- **Results:** Hampi **looted for about six months**; **Sewell** called it a "graveyard" (possibly exaggerated); Sadasiva and **Tirumala** fled to **Penukonda**; the empire's centre moved to the **Telugu country**; Nayakas grew autonomous.

=== Aravidu Dynasty (1570-1646/80) – Penukonda, Chandragiri, Vellore
- **Fourth** Vijayanagara dynasty; named after **Araviti (Aravidu)** village, **Kurnool** district.
- **Capital after 1565 / of the Aravidus:** **Penukonda (Penugonda)** (APPSC G1 1991; G2 2000; AP History PYQ ×3) – not Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Kampili, Kondavidu, Addanki, Vizianagaram, Anantapur or Chandragiri.
- **Capital shifts:** Hampi → **Penukonda** (1570, Tirumala) → **Chandragiri** (Venkatapati II) → **Vellore** (c. 1600).
| Ruler | Key fact |
|---|---|
| **Tirumala Raya** (1570-72) | Founder, Rama Raya's brother; removed Sadasiva; crowned at Penukonda |
| Sriranga I (1572-85) | Lost **Kondaveedu** to Ibrahim Qutb Shah |
| **Venkatapati Raya II** (1585-1614) | Greatest and longest; "**Second Krishnadevaraya**", "**Second Andhra Bhoja**"; moved to Chandragiri; literary contests at **Venkatapati Vilasam**; **Vilapakam (1601)** and **Siddhavatam (1605)** inscriptions; Dutch at **Pulicat**; court scholar **Appayya Dikshita** |
| Sriranga II (1614) | Killed by **Jagga Raya** |
| Ramadeva (1616-30) | Battle of **Toppur (1616)** – "second Talikota", won by **Velugoti Yachama Nayaka** and **Raghunatha of Tanjore** |
| **Venkatapati III** (1630-43) | **Madras** granted to **Francis Day** (1639) by **Damarla Venkatadri Nayaka** (Srikalahasti) |
| Sriranga III (1643-) | Last; defeated by **Bijapur** and Golconda (Vandavasi; Mir Jumla) |
- **Jesuits:** **Venkatapati Raya II** allowed Jesuit churches at **Nellore and Chandragiri** (APPSC G1 2023; G1 PYQ) – not Krishnadevaraya, Achyuta Raya, Sadasiva, Devaraya II or Tirumala.
- **Mysore:** **Raja Wodeyar** took **Srirangapatna (1610)** and founded the Mysore kingdom under **Venkata II** (IAS 2006 ×2) – not Sadasiva, Tirumala or Ranga II.
~~ Sources: NextGen Vijayanagara Kingdom p.5-6 (Tirumalaraya at Penukonda; Venkatapatiraya II – Chandragiri; Venkatapatiraya III – Madras); NextGen previous papers p.40 (capital of Vijayanagara after the Tallikota battle – Penugonda); NextGen AP Rocket Sheets (src/ap-history/rocket-ap-history.txt); app notes 1:126 (sub 1, 2, 3)

=== Nayaka Kingdoms, Kshetrayya and Court Literature
| Kingdom | Founder | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Madurai | **Vishwanatha Nayaka** (after capturing his rebel father **Nagama**) | **Krishnappa** made it independent c. 1565; **Tirumala Nayaka** (1623-59) built the **Tirumala Rayala Mahal** |
| Tanjore | **Sevappa Nayaka** (under Achyutaraya) | Raghunatha; last king **Vijayaraghava Nayaka** |
| Gingee | **Krishnappa Nayaka** | – |
| Ikkeri (Keladi) | **Chowdappa Nayaka** | – |
| Mysore | **Raja Wodeyar** (1610) | under Venkata II |
- **Kshetrayya** (original name Varadayya; "Pada Kavita Pitamaha"): born at **Movva (Muvva), Krishna district**; padams with the mudra **Muvva Gopala**; sang for **Tirumala Nayaka of Madurai**, **Vijayaraghava Nayaka of Tanjore** and **Abdullah Qutb Shah of Golconda** (1500 padams dedicated to him) → **"All the above"** (APPSC G1 2003).
- **Govinda Dikshita** – minister of the **Tanjore Nayaks** (Achyutappa, Raghunatha); wrote ***Sahitya Sudha*** – not Ayyappa Dikshita, Vedanta Desika or Vyasaraya.
- **Appayya Dikshita** – *Shaivarka Mani Dipika*; **Ramaraja Bhushanudu** – *Vasucharitra*.
- **Amara-nayakas** → hereditary **Palegars** → forerunners of zamindars (Kalahasti, Chittoor); **Mir Jumla** melted temple idols into cannon at **Gandikota (1657)**; Raya gopurams and kalyana mandapas spread through the Nayaka courts.
~~ Sources: NextGen Qutb Shahis p.8 (Kshetrayya – Mowva; 1500 poems to Abdullah Qutub Shah); NextGen AP History - APPSC 15 p.16 (same); app notes 1:126 (sub 4, 5)

=== Rediscovery of Hampi
- **Colin Mackenzie** – surveyed and mapped the ruins of **Hampi in 1800**; later the **first Surveyor General of India** (NDA 2019; APPSC 2019) – not James Prinsep (Brahmi), James Rennell, Charles Metcalfe, Charles Harvey or Lord Hastings.
- **Caesar Frederick** (Venetian) saw the ruined city in **1567**; **Robert Sewell** – *A Forgotten Empire* (1900).
