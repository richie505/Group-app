=== NOTES 1:268 | Development of Telugu Literature After 1956 – Digambara, Viplava, Dalit, Feminist and Muslim Minority Streams
## SUB 0 | Dalit Literature – Gurram Jashuva and Gabbilam
[268:0:0] # Gurram Jashuva (1895-1971)
[268:0:1] - **First modern Telugu Dalit poet**; wrote against untouchability; called "Madhurakavi" by Viswanatha Satyanarayana.
[268:0:2] - **Works:** **Gabbilam (1941)**, **Firdausi**, **Kandiseekudu (The Refugee)**, **Kreesthu Charitra** – "Seven Seasons" is not his work (Social & Cultural History of AP).
[268:0:3] - **Gabbilam author:** **Gurram Jashuva** (AP History PYQ; Asst. Social Welfare Officer) – not Darsi Chenchaiah or Didla Pullaiah.
[268:0:4] # Gabbilam (The Bat)
[268:0:5] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [268:0:5.0] Messenger | A bat carries a hungry, poor Dalit's message to Lord Shiva
  [268:0:5.1] Meaning | The bat, kept outside the temple in darkness = the untouchable barred from the temple
  [268:0:5.2] Model | Kalidasa's Meghaduta (cloud-messenger)
  [268:0:5.3] Purpose | Public awareness against untouchability
[268:0:6] - **Messenger in Jashuva's famous work:** the **bat** (AP History PYQ; G2 PYQ) – not squirrel, crow, sparrow or koel.
[268:0:7] - **"Dalit Manifesto":** **Gabbilam** is the work the exam key treats as the Dalit manifesto (Asst. BC Welfare Officers 2017) – Malapalli (Unnava Lakshminarayana, 1922) is a novel on Dalit life, Paleru a play.
[268:0:8] - **Firdausi:** on the Persian poet cheated of his promised reward by Sultan Mahmud – poet as rebel against patronage.
[268:0:9] - **Award:** Gabbilam did **not** win the Sahitya Akademi Award (that was Kreesthu Charitra, 1964).
[268:0:10] > Exam angle: Gabbilam = **Jashuva, bat, Meghaduta, 1941**.

## SUB 4 | Viplava Sahityam – Virasam and the Digambara Kavulu
[268:4:0] # The streams in order
[268:4:1] - Post-1956 streams moved Telugu literature from **classical/romantic themes** to **caste, class, gender and minority identity**; base: **Gidugu–Gurajada's spoken-Telugu revolution**.
[268:4:2] TABLE head: Stream / body | Year | Key fact
  [268:4:2.0] Digambara kavulu | 1965 | Six "naked poets"; three volumes of Digambara Kavitvam (1965-68)
  [268:4:2.1] Virasam (Viplava Rachayitala Sangham) | 4 July 1970 | First president Sri Sri (not Gaddar)
  [268:4:2.2] Dalit literature | Roots in Jashuva; surge after Karamchedu (1985) | Dalit Mahasabhas
  [268:4:2.3] Feminist poetry | Neeli Meghalu (1993) | Volga and others
  [268:4:2.4] Muslim minority poetry | 1990s | Jaljala
[268:4:3] # Digambara kavulu
[268:4:4] TABLE head: Pen name | Real name
  [268:4:4.0] Cherabandaraju | Baddam Bhaskara Reddy
  [268:4:4.1] Mahaswapna | Kammisetti Venkateswara Rao
[268:4:5] - Six **anti-establishment** poets of the mid-1960s; **Cherabandaraju, Jwalamukhi, Nikhileswar** moved to **Virasam in 1970**.
[268:4:6] # Virasam
[268:4:7] - Context: **Srikakulam peasant struggle (1969)** under **Vempatapu Satyam** spread Naxalism in Andhra.
[268:4:8] - Trigger: **Visakhapatnam** students' pamphlet **"Writers, where do you stand?"** (1970).
[268:4:9] - **Sri Sri:** **Maha Prasthanam** broke Telugu **prosody** – a call to the oppressed; also the **first National Award for a Telugu lyricist** ("Teluguveera Levara", film **Alluri Seetharamaraju**).
[268:4:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History Part 6 p.13 (Srikakulam struggle 1968-70); app notes 1:268 (sub 0, 1, 2)
## SUB 5 | Feminist and Muslim Minority Literature
[268:5:0] - **Neeli Meghalu (1993):** feminist anthology edited by **Volga** and others (not Gogu Syamala).
[268:5:1] - **Feminist poets:** **Jayaprabha, Vimala, Kondepudi Nirmala** (Khadar Mohiuddin is not one).
[268:5:2] - **Bhumika** magazine: edited by **Kondaveeti Satyavathi**.
[268:5:3] - **Jaljala:** anthology of Telugu **Muslim minority poetry of the 1990s**.
[268:5:4] - **Puttumacha** ("Birthmark"): long poem on **Muslim identity** by **Khadar Mohiuddin**.

## SUB 4 | Nagarjunakonda and Buddhist Sites Dug after 1956
[270:4:0] # Nagarjunakonda – salvage archaeology
[270:4:1] - Found **1926** by **A. Ranganatha Saraswati**; dug by **Longhurst (1927)**, later Ramachandran, **R. Subrahmanyam**.
[270:4:2] - Finds: acoustic building, **amphitheatre**, **Santamula's Ashvamedha altar**, **Sati sculpture**, **30+ stupas**.
[270:4:3] - **Nagarjunasagar Dam:** site submerged **after 1960**; ASI rebuilt monuments on higher ground at **Anupu** and on the island = **"Transplanted Monuments"**.
[270:4:4] - Island museum on the hilltop opened **1966** (ASI).

## SUB 5 | Land Reforms – Phases, Types and Aims
[271:5:0] # Background and phases
[271:5:1] - **Prakasam Committee (1938):** advised **abolition of intermediaries**.
[271:5:2] - **Kumarappa Committee (1949):** set Congress's land reform agenda.
[271:5:3] - **Phase I (1947-70)** → **Phase II (1970 onwards)**.
[271:5:4] - **First Constitutional Amendment** shielded zamindari abolition from legal challenge.
[271:5:5] - **Raj Krishna's** classification: **liberative, distributive, organisational, developmental**.
[271:5:6] # The four measures
[271:5:7] TABLE head: Measure | Key fact
  [271:5:7.0] Abolition of intermediaries | Most successful; about 2 crore tenants in direct contact with the State; ~40% of cultivated area
  [271:5:7.1] Tenancy reform | Aim: security and ownership rights for the tiller tenant; rent capped at 1/4 to 1/6 of produce; defeated by "voluntary surrenders"; exception Operation Barga (1977), West Bengal
  [271:5:7.2] Ceiling | Two rounds (1950s, early 1970s); family made the unit from 1972; benami transfers
  [271:5:7.3] Consolidation | Succeeded in Punjab, Haryana, western UP
[271:5:8] - **Aim of post-independence tenancy laws:** **transfer of ownership rights to tiller tenants** (Asst. Director of Fisheries) – not protecting owners or only fixing returns.
[271:5:9] - Failure on ceilings and tenancy helped cause the **Naxalbari revolt (1967)**; abolition only changed the revenue hierarchy.
[271:5:10] - **National Council for Land Reforms:** set up **9 January 2008**, **chaired by the Prime Minister**.
[271:5:11] - First State to abolish zamindari = **UP (1950)**, though Madras's 1948 Act was earlier.
