=== Dalit Literature – Gurram Jashuva and Gabbilam
# Gurram Jashuva (1895-1971)
- **First modern Telugu Dalit poet**; wrote against untouchability; called "Madhurakavi" by Viswanatha Satyanarayana.
- **Works:** **Gabbilam (1941)**, **Firdausi**, **Kandiseekudu (The Refugee)**, **Kreesthu Charitra** – "Seven Seasons" is not his work (Social & Cultural History of AP).
- **Gabbilam author:** **Gurram Jashuva** (AP History PYQ; Asst. Social Welfare Officer) – not Darsi Chenchaiah or Didla Pullaiah.
# Gabbilam (The Bat)
| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Messenger | A **bat** carries a hungry, poor Dalit's message to **Lord Shiva** |
| Meaning | The bat, kept outside the temple in darkness = the untouchable barred from the temple |
| Model | **Kalidasa's Meghaduta** (cloud-messenger) |
| Purpose | Public awareness against untouchability |
- **Messenger in Jashuva's famous work:** the **bat** (AP History PYQ; G2 PYQ) – not squirrel, crow, sparrow or koel.
- **"Dalit Manifesto":** **Gabbilam** is the work the exam key treats as the Dalit manifesto (Asst. BC Welfare Officers 2017) – Malapalli (Unnava Lakshminarayana, 1922) is a novel on Dalit life, Paleru a play.
- **Firdausi:** on the Persian poet cheated of his promised reward by Sultan Mahmud – poet as rebel against patronage.
- **Award:** Gabbilam did **not** win the Sahitya Akademi Award (that was Kreesthu Charitra, 1964).
> Exam angle: Gabbilam = **Jashuva, bat, Meghaduta, 1941**.

=== Telugu Jnanpith Awardees and Classic Works
| Awardee | Work | Year |
|---|---|---|
| **Viswanatha Satyanarayana** (first; "**Kavi Samrat**", "Abhinava Nannaya") | **Ramayana Kalpavrukshamu** | **1970** |
| **C. Narayana Reddy** | **Vishwambhara** | **1988** |
| **Ravuri Bharadwaja** | **Paakudu Raallu** | **2012** |
- **First Telugu Jnanpith:** **Viswanatha Satyanarayana** for **Ramayana Kalpavrukshamu** (G2 Mains 2025; Grama Sachivalayam) – not C. Narayana Reddy, Ravuri Bharadwaja or Sri Sri.
- **"Kavi Samrat":** Viswanatha Satyanarayana (AP History PYQ); he wrote **over 100 works**.
- **Vishwambhara:** **Dr C. Narayana Reddy** (AP History PYQ).
- **Not a Telugu Jnanpith winner:** **Chandrashekhara Kambara** (Kannada, 2010) (Divisional Accounts Officer).
- **Veyi Padagalu** (Viswanatha) was translated into Hindi as **Sahasraphan** by **P.V. Narasimha Rao** (FRO 2022) – Tulasidalam, Mahaprasthanam, Vennello Aadapilla are distractors.
- Telugu: one of India's **six classical languages**, **10 crore+** speakers (Jan 2026).
> Exam angle: Viswanatha's Jnanpith work = **Ramayana Kalpavrukshamu**, not Veyi Padagalu.
~~ Sources: NextGen AP History Part 7 p.21; CDI AP History G2 PYQs (Viswanatha Q45); app notes 1:268 (sub 7, 8)

=== Viplava Sahityam – Virasam and the Digambara Kavulu
# The streams in order
- Post-1956 streams moved Telugu literature from **classical/romantic themes** to **caste, class, gender and minority identity**; base: **Gidugu–Gurajada's spoken-Telugu revolution**.
| Stream / body | Year | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Digambara kavulu** | **1965** | Six "naked poets"; three volumes of Digambara Kavitvam (1965-68) |
| **Virasam** (Viplava Rachayitala Sangham) | **4 July 1970** | First president **Sri Sri** (not Gaddar) |
| Dalit literature | Roots in Jashuva; surge after **Karamchedu (1985)** | Dalit Mahasabhas |
| Feminist poetry | Neeli Meghalu (1993) | Volga and others |
| Muslim minority poetry | 1990s | Jaljala |
# Digambara kavulu
| Pen name | Real name |
|---|---|
| **Cherabandaraju** | **Baddam Bhaskara Reddy** |
| **Mahaswapna** | **Kammisetti Venkateswara Rao** |
- Six **anti-establishment** poets of the mid-1960s; **Cherabandaraju, Jwalamukhi, Nikhileswar** moved to **Virasam in 1970**.
# Virasam
- Context: **Srikakulam peasant struggle (1969)** under **Vempatapu Satyam** spread Naxalism in Andhra.
- Trigger: **Visakhapatnam** students' pamphlet **"Writers, where do you stand?"** (1970).
- **Sri Sri:** **Maha Prasthanam** broke Telugu **prosody** – a call to the oppressed; also the **first National Award for a Telugu lyricist** ("Teluguveera Levara", film **Alluri Seetharamaraju**).
~~ Sources: NextGen AP History Part 6 p.13 (Srikakulam struggle 1968-70); app notes 1:268 (sub 0, 1, 2)

=== Feminist and Muslim Minority Literature
- **Neeli Meghalu (1993):** feminist anthology edited by **Volga** and others (not Gogu Syamala).
- **Feminist poets:** **Jayaprabha, Vimala, Kondepudi Nirmala** (Khadar Mohiuddin is not one).
- **Bhumika** magazine: edited by **Kondaveeti Satyavathi**.
- **Jaljala:** anthology of Telugu **Muslim minority poetry of the 1990s**.
- **Puttumacha** ("Birthmark"): long poem on **Muslim identity** by **Khadar Mohiuddin**.
