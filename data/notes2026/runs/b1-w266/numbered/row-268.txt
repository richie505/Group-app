## SUB 0 | Dalit Literature – Gurram Jashuva and Gabbilam
[0:0] # Gurram Jashuva (1895-1971)
[0:1] - **First modern Telugu Dalit poet**; wrote against untouchability; called "Madhurakavi" by Viswanatha Satyanarayana.
[0:2] - **Works:** **Gabbilam (1941)**, **Firdausi**, **Kandiseekudu (The Refugee)**, **Kreesthu Charitra** – "Seven Seasons" is not his work (Social & Cultural History of AP).
[0:3] - **Gabbilam author:** **Gurram Jashuva** (AP History PYQ; Asst. Social Welfare Officer) – not Darsi Chenchaiah or Didla Pullaiah.
[0:4] # Gabbilam (The Bat)
[0:5] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [0:5.0] Messenger | A bat carries a hungry, poor Dalit's message to Lord Shiva
  [0:5.1] Meaning | The bat, kept outside the temple in darkness = the untouchable barred from the temple
  [0:5.2] Model | Kalidasa's Meghaduta (cloud-messenger)
  [0:5.3] Purpose | Public awareness against untouchability
[0:6] - **Messenger in Jashuva's famous work:** the **bat** (AP History PYQ; G2 PYQ) – not squirrel, crow, sparrow or koel.
[0:7] - **"Dalit Manifesto":** **Gabbilam** is the work the exam key treats as the Dalit manifesto (Asst. BC Welfare Officers 2017) – Malapalli (Unnava Lakshminarayana, 1922) is a novel on Dalit life, Paleru a play.
[0:8] - **Firdausi:** on the Persian poet cheated of his promised reward by Sultan Mahmud – poet as rebel against patronage.
[0:9] - **Award:** Gabbilam did **not** win the Sahitya Akademi Award (that was Kreesthu Charitra, 1964).
[0:10] > Exam angle: Gabbilam = **Jashuva, bat, Meghaduta, 1941**.
[0:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History Part 6 p.8, p.14; NextGen Previous Papers p.6, p.13; app notes 1:268 (sub 3, 4); "Dalit manifesto" and Malapalli: printed key / general knowledge, not checked

## SUB 2 | Telugu Jnanpith Awardees and Classic Works
[2:0] TABLE head: Awardee | Work | Year
  [2:0.0] Viswanatha Satyanarayana (first; "Kavi Samrat", "Abhinava Nannaya") | Ramayana Kalpavrukshamu | 1970
  [2:0.1] C. Narayana Reddy | Vishwambhara | 1988
  [2:0.2] Ravuri Bharadwaja | Paakudu Raallu | 2012
[2:1] - **First Telugu Jnanpith:** **Viswanatha Satyanarayana** for **Ramayana Kalpavrukshamu** (G2 Mains 2025; Grama Sachivalayam) – not C. Narayana Reddy, Ravuri Bharadwaja or Sri Sri.
[2:2] - **"Kavi Samrat":** Viswanatha Satyanarayana (AP History PYQ); he wrote **over 100 works**.
[2:3] - **Vishwambhara:** **Dr C. Narayana Reddy** (AP History PYQ).
[2:4] - **Not a Telugu Jnanpith winner:** **Chandrashekhara Kambara** (Kannada, 2010) (Divisional Accounts Officer).
[2:5] - **Veyi Padagalu** (Viswanatha) was translated into Hindi as **Sahasraphan** by **P.V. Narasimha Rao** (FRO 2022) – Tulasidalam, Mahaprasthanam, Vennello Aadapilla are distractors.
[2:6] - Telugu: one of India's **six classical languages**, **10 crore+** speakers (Jan 2026).
[2:7] > Exam angle: Viswanatha's Jnanpith work = **Ramayana Kalpavrukshamu**, not Veyi Padagalu.
[2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History Part 7 p.21; CDI AP History G2 PYQs (Viswanatha Q45); app notes 1:268 (sub 7, 8)

## SUB 4 | Viplava Sahityam – Virasam and the Digambara Kavulu
[4:0] # The streams in order
[4:1] - Post-1956 streams moved Telugu literature from **classical/romantic themes** to **caste, class, gender and minority identity**; base: **Gidugu–Gurajada's spoken-Telugu revolution**.
[4:2] TABLE head: Stream / body | Year | Key fact
  [4:2.0] Digambara kavulu | 1965 | Six "naked poets"; three volumes of Digambara Kavitvam (1965-68)
  [4:2.1] Virasam (Viplava Rachayitala Sangham) | 4 July 1970 | First president Sri Sri (not Gaddar)
  [4:2.2] Dalit literature | Roots in Jashuva; surge after Karamchedu (1985) | Dalit Mahasabhas
  [4:2.3] Feminist poetry | Neeli Meghalu (1993) | Volga and others
  [4:2.4] Muslim minority poetry | 1990s | Jaljala
[4:3] # Digambara kavulu
[4:4] TABLE head: Pen name | Real name
  [4:4.0] Cherabandaraju | Baddam Bhaskara Reddy
  [4:4.1] Mahaswapna | Kammisetti Venkateswara Rao
[4:5] - Six **anti-establishment** poets of the mid-1960s; **Cherabandaraju, Jwalamukhi, Nikhileswar** moved to **Virasam in 1970**.
[4:6] # Virasam
[4:7] - Context: **Srikakulam peasant struggle (1969)** under **Vempatapu Satyam** spread Naxalism in Andhra.
[4:8] - Trigger: **Visakhapatnam** students' pamphlet **"Writers, where do you stand?"** (1970).
[4:9] - **Sri Sri:** **Maha Prasthanam** broke Telugu **prosody** – a call to the oppressed; also the **first National Award for a Telugu lyricist** ("Teluguveera Levara", film **Alluri Seetharamaraju**).
[4:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History Part 6 p.13 (Srikakulam struggle 1968-70); app notes 1:268 (sub 0, 1, 2)
