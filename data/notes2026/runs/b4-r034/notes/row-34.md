=== Parallel Ranges – Himadri, Himachal, Shiwalik and the Trans-Himalaya
# The three parallel (longitudinal) ranges
| Feature | Himadri (Greater / Inner / Central) | Himachal (Lesser / Middle) | Shiwalik (Outer / foothills) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position | **Northernmost** of the three | Between Himadri and Shiwalik | **Outermost, southernmost** |
| Age | **Oldest** | Intermediate | **Youngest** (Pliocene, **Cenozoic** era) |
| Height | Average **6,000 m**; loftiest peaks | 3,500–4,500 m (avg ~4,000 m) | **900–1,100 m** |
| Width | 160–400 km (NCERT summary; PMF) | ~50 km | 10–50 km, **narrowest** |
| Make-up | **Granite** core (central crystallines); **asymmetrical** folds | Highly compressed and altered rocks; **most rugged** system | Unconsolidated river sediments; simple **Jura-type** folds |
| Continuity | **Most continuous** range | Broken into ranges | Continuous to the Gandak, then broken |
| Known for | Everest, Kanchenjunga, Nanda Devi, Kamet; long glaciers | Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mussoorie, Nag Tibba, **Mahabharat (Nepal)**; Kashmir, Kangra, Kullu valleys; hill stations | Foothills; **duns** behind them; debris built the plains |
- **Three parallel ranges** in the longitudinal extent: **Himadri, Himachal, Shiwalik** (NCERT) – a statement "four parallel ranges" is false; "the northernmost range is the Shiwalik" is false (northernmost = **Himadri**).
- **Himadri:** also called Greater, Higher, Inner or **Central** Himalaya; highest point **Mount Everest** (~8,850 m; now 8,848.86 m). Dehradun is **not** in the Himadri (it is a dun between Lesser Himalaya and Shiwalik).
- **Himachal:** names for the same range – Lesser, Middle, Lower Himalaya; "Himachal stands for the Middle Himalaya".
- **Pir Panjal:** the most important Lesser Himalayan range, **~300 km from the Jhelum to the upper Beas** in **J&K and Himachal Pradesh**; Banihal pass crosses it. South-east of the Ravi it continues as the **Dhauladhar**, then the **Mussoorie** range (Uttarakhand) and the **Mahabharat Lekh** in **Nepal**.
- **Shiwalik:** formed from riverine deposits of the "Siwalik river" (Pascoe, Pilgrim) about **2–5 million years ago** (Pliocene, **Cenozoic**); local names – **Jammu Hills** (J&K), **Dhang/Dundwa** (Uttarakhand), **Churia Ghat** (Nepal).
- **Youngest mountain chain** among Vindhyas, Aravalli, Shiwaliks, Annamalai = **Shiwaliks**; **oldest Himalayan range** = **Greater Himalaya**.
- **In Arunachal Pradesh** the Lesser/outer Himalayan hills are the **Dafla, Miri, Abor and Mishmi hills** (PMF lists them as Lesser Himalaya; the Shiwalik is almost absent there).
# The Trans-Himalaya (Tibetan Himalaya)
- Lies **north of the Himadri**; mostly in Tibet, hence the **Tibet Himalaya**; **formed before** the Himalayas.
- Ranges: **Karakoram** (K2), **Ladakh** (continues into Tibet as the **Kailash Range**, not the Zaskar), **Zaskar** (Kashmir Valley separates the Pir Panjal from it), **Kailash** (Mt Kailash 6,714 m; **Lake Manasarovar** lies near it in Tibet).
- **Ladakh Plateau:** north-east of the Karakoram; **highest plateau in India**; includes the **Aksai Chin**, Soda Plains, Lingzi Tang, Depsang Plains (Chinese-occupied Aksai Chin is a cold desert).
- **Kailash Mansarovar** is in **Chinese Tibet** – Indian pilgrims need a visa.

=== Purvanchal and the North-eastern Hills
# Purvanchal
- Beyond the **Dihang gorge** (near Namcha Barwa) the Himalaya takes a knee-bend and turns south as the **Purvanchal** – "eastern offshoots of the Himalayas", 500–3,000 m; Arunachal, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, eastern Assam.
- **Latest in origin** among Ajanta, Palkonda, Kaimur, Patkai = **Patkai** (Tertiary, Himalayan).
| Hills | State / fact |
|---|---|
| **Patkai Bum** | **Arunachal Pradesh** – Myanmar border; with the Naga Hills forms the India–Myanmar watershed; touches Arunachal, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram – **not Tripura** |
| **Naga Hills** | Nagaland; **Saramati** 3,826 m |
| Manipur Hills | Manipur; **Loktak** lake; Manipur is mostly **mountainous** |
| **Barail Range** | Assam; **separates Manipur from Nagaland**, links Naga and Mizo hills; highest range of Assam |
| **Mizo (Lushai) Hills** | Mizoram; Blue Mountain; run north–south; **Molasse basin** (soft, unconsolidated deposits) |
| **Garo, Khasi, Jaintia** | **Meghalaya** (Shillong Plateau) – part of the Purvanchal/eastern hills group; **Nokrek** 1,412 m in the Garo Hills |
| Bhuban Hills | Assam (Barak valley) – **not** in the Meghalaya Plateau |
| Miri Hills | **Arunachal Pradesh** – not Meghalaya |
| **Arakan Yoma** | **Myanmar** – southern extension of the Himalaya/Purvanchal; highest Natma Taung (Mt Victoria) |
- **West → east:** **Garo – Khasi – Jaintia – Naga**.
- **Shillong** and **Cherrapunji (Sohra)** are on the **Khasi Hills**; Shillong = "Scotland of the East".
- **India–Myanmar boundary ranges:** Patkai, Naga, Mizo (Lushai) hills – a "Khasi, Patkai and Arakan Yoma" option is wrong (Khasi is in Meghalaya, Arakan wholly in Myanmar).
- **Mountains on India's eastern border with Myanmar:** **Patkai**.

# Duns
- **Duns (Doons):** **longitudinal valleys between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwalik** (NCERT) – not between Greater and Lesser; called **duars** in the east.
- Examples: **Dehra Dun (largest)**, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun, Nalagarh Dun – "Nalagarh is the largest dun" is false.
- "The valleys in the Siwalik ranges are called" = **Duns**.
# Karewas
- **Karewas:** thick lake (lacustrine) and glacial-clay deposits forming flat-topped terraces in the **Kashmir Himalaya**; soils used for **zafran (saffron)**.
- The **Kashmir valley** produces the largest quantity of saffron in India.
# People
- **Dogras** live mainly from the **southern Pir Panjal region to the Punjab plains** (Jammu region).

=== Dimensions, Origin, Structure and Significance
# Dimensions
| Dimension | Figure |
|---|---|
| Length | **~2,400 km** (2,414 km) – **Nanga Parbat** (west) to **Namcha Barwa** (east) |
| Width | **500 km (Kashmir) → 200 km (Arunachal)** (NextGen); NCERT/PMF: **400 km → 150 km** |
| Area | About **5 lakh sq km** (not 15 lakh) |
| Eastern limit | The **Brahmaputra** (Dihang gorge) marks the eastern boundary |
- The Himalayas form the **northern boundary**, separating the subcontinent from the rest of Asia (not the Aravalli or the Ghats).
- **Indian Himalayan Region:** HP, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal, NE States, J&K, Ladakh and hill WB – **not Uttar Pradesh**.
# Origin and structure
- **Young fold mountains** of tectonic origin, raised from **Tethys (geosynclinal)** sediments in the **Tertiary** (Eocene–Pliocene) as the **Indian plate** pushed into Eurasia (~70 million years ago, still rising).
- **Geologically young and unstable** landmass of India.
- **Trans-Himalayas formed before** the Himalayas.
- Folds of the **Himadri are asymmetrical** with a **granite** core; **steep slope faces the plains**, gentle slope faces Tibet.
- **Structural zones (N → S):** Indus–Tsangpo Suture Zone (separates Himalaya from **Tibet**) → Main Central Thrust → Main Boundary Thrust → **Himalayan Frontal Fault** (separates Himalaya from the **plains**, not from Tibet).
- **Antecedent drainage:** the Indus and Sutlej are older than the Himalaya and cut gorges through it.
- **Fossils (UPPSC 2019 key):** Greater Himalaya sedimentary rocks fossil-less; marine fossils in the Lesser Himalaya; remains of human civilisation in the Shiwaliks.
# Significance (IAS 2010: all three true)
- Blocks **Siberian cold waves**; shapes the **monsoon** pattern; rivers and Shiwalik debris built the **Indo-Gangetic Plain's alluvium**.
- **Plains** are the largest relief division of India (~43% of area).
