## SUB 0 | Dynasty, Capital, Rulers and the Kakatiya Link
[0:0] # The dynasty
[0:1] - **Name:** **Seuna (Sevuna) Yadavas** of Devagiri; first **feudatories of the Western (Kalyani) Chalukyas** (and earlier the Rashtrakutas); ruled the western Deccan (Maharashtra) c. **1187–1317**.
[0:2] - **Mother tongue:** **Marathi** (APPSC G2 2000; APPSC PYQ) – not Telugu, Kannada or Urdu; the Yadavas **encouraged Marathi**; **Sanskrit** = language of scholarship (not Telugu at court).
[0:3] - **Founder of the independent kingdom:** **Bhillama V** threw off Kalyani Chalukya rule and built **Devagiri** (c. **1187**) as capital (APPSC G2 2000) – not Prola I, Prola II or Ganapati (Kakatiyas), not Shankaradeva.
[0:4] - **Daulatabad:** name given to Devagiri by **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** when he shifted his capital there (**1327**).
[0:5] # Rulers
[0:6] TABLE head: Ruler | Exam fact
  [0:6.0] Bhillama V | Founder; built Devagiri; beaten by Hoysala Veera Ballala II
  [0:6.1] Jaitugi (Jaitrapala) | Killed Kakatiya Rudradeva; held young Ganapatideva captive, released him (1199) and married his daughter Somaladevi to him
  [0:6.2] Singhana II (c. 1210–1246) | Greatest Yadava; title Teluguraya Sthapanacharya; defeated the Hoysalas; son Sarangapanideva later served at Rudramadevi's court
  [0:6.3] Krishna (1246–1261) | Fought the Hoysalas
  [0:6.4] Mahadeva (1261–1271) | His attack on the Kakatiyas was repulsed by Rudramadevi
  [0:6.5] Ramachandra (1271–1311) | Ruler at Alauddin Khalji's 1296 raid; later a vassal
  [0:6.6] Shankaradeva (1311–1313) | Died in Malik Kafur's invasion
  [0:6.7] Harapaladeva (1313–1317) | Last ruler; killed in Mubarak Khalji's invasion
[0:7] - **Rudradeva's killer:** **Jaitugi** (APPSC PYQ) – recorded by Hemadri and confirmed by the **Paithan copper plate** of Ramachandra; not Narasimha Deva or Namadeva.
[0:8] - **Why Ganapati was freed (1199):** as a counterweight to Hoysala **Veera Ballala II's** push into the **Krishna–Tungabhadra** region.
[0:9] - **Rudrama's victory over Mahadeva:** recorded in the **Bidar fort inscription** (APPSC PYQ) – not Bayyaram, Chandupatla or Hanumakonda.

## SUB 1 | Scholars – Hemadri, Sarngadeva, Changadeva; the Hemadpanti Style
[1:0] # Hemadri
[1:1] - **Chaturvarga Chintamani:** Sanskrit encyclopaedia by **Hemadri** (APPSC G2 2000 ×2; APPSC PYQ) – on **dharma, rituals, charity, pilgrimage and vratas**; not Vidyanatha, Jayapa Senani or Palkuriki Somanatha.
[1:2] - **Office:** **Shrikaranadhipa** (head of secretariat and records) under **Mahadeva and Ramachandra**.
[1:3] - **On Kakatiya Rudra:** Hemadri claimed **Jaitugi captured and sacrificed Rudra**.
[1:4] - **Hemadpanti style:** credited to Hemadri – local **black basalt**, dressed stones **interlocked without mortar** (**dry masonry**).
[1:5] # Other scholars and works
[1:6] TABLE head: Person | Work / role
  [1:6.0] Sarngadeva | Court musician of Singhana II; Sangita Ratnakara – base text of both Hindustani and Carnatic music (not Hemadri)
  [1:6.1] Changadeva | Court astronomer of Singhana II; grandson of Bhaskaracharya; school at Patan to teach Bhaskara's works
  [1:6.2] Bhaskaracharya (Bhaskara II) | Lilavati, the mathematics treatise (Uttarakhand PCS 2010) – not Ramanuja, Kautilya or Amartya Sen
  [1:6.3] Dnyaneshwar | Dnyaneshwari – Marathi commentary on the Bhagavad Gita, 1290, Nevasa, in Ramachandra's reign
  [1:6.4] Chakradhar Swami | Founder of the Mahanubhava sect (not Dnyaneshwar)
  [1:6.5] Mhaimbhat | Lilacharitra = first Marathi prose
[1:7] - Later Bhakti saints Namdev, Tukaram and Ramdas carried Upanishad and Gita ideas into Marathi.

## SUB 2 | Fall of Devagiri to Delhi
[2:0] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [2:0.0] 1296 | First Muslim invasion of the South: Alauddin Khalji, then governor of Kara under Sultan Jalaluddin, raids Devagiri and defeats Ramachandra; promised the yearly revenue of Ellichpur but did not send it
  [2:0.1] 1307–08 | Malik Kafur: Ramachandra submits and is taken to Delhi, then restored as a vassal
  [2:0.2] 1313 | Shankaradeva dies in Kafur's invasion
  [2:0.3] 1317–18 | Harapaladeva killed by Mubarak Khalji; kingdom annexed
  [2:0.4] After 1323 | Malik Burhanuddin of Devagiri given charge of Andhra; Devagiri = the Sultanate's Deccan base
[2:1] - **Significance:** Devagiri was the **first southern kingdom to fall** to Delhi and opened the road to Warangal, Dwarasamudra and Madurai.
[2:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Kafur's 1307–08 campaign brought the submission of **Ramachandra**, not Harapaladeva.
