=== Dynasty, Capital, Rulers and the Kakatiya Link
# The dynasty
- **Name:** **Seuna (Sevuna) Yadavas** of Devagiri; first **feudatories of the Western (Kalyani) Chalukyas** (and earlier the Rashtrakutas); ruled the western Deccan (Maharashtra) c. **1187–1317**.
- **Mother tongue:** **Marathi** (APPSC G2 2000; APPSC PYQ) – not Telugu, Kannada or Urdu; the Yadavas **encouraged Marathi**; **Sanskrit** = language of scholarship (not Telugu at court).
- **Founder of the independent kingdom:** **Bhillama V** threw off Kalyani Chalukya rule and built **Devagiri** (c. **1187**) as capital (APPSC G2 2000) – not Prola I, Prola II or Ganapati (Kakatiyas), not Shankaradeva.
- **Daulatabad:** name given to Devagiri by **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** when he shifted his capital there (**1327**).
# Rulers
| Ruler | Exam fact |
|---|---|
| **Bhillama V** | Founder; built Devagiri; beaten by Hoysala Veera Ballala II |
| **Jaitugi (Jaitrapala)** | **Killed Kakatiya Rudradeva**; held young Ganapatideva captive, released him (1199) and married his daughter **Somaladevi** to him |
| **Singhana II** (c. 1210–1246) | **Greatest Yadava**; title **Teluguraya Sthapanacharya**; defeated the Hoysalas; son **Sarangapanideva** later served at Rudramadevi's court |
| Krishna (1246–1261) | Fought the Hoysalas |
| **Mahadeva** (1261–1271) | His attack on the Kakatiyas was **repulsed by Rudramadevi** |
| **Ramachandra** (1271–1311) | Ruler at **Alauddin Khalji's 1296 raid**; later a vassal |
| Shankaradeva (1311–1313) | Died in Malik Kafur's invasion |
| **Harapaladeva** (1313–1317) | **Last ruler**; killed in **Mubarak Khalji's** invasion |
- **Rudradeva's killer:** **Jaitugi** (APPSC PYQ) – recorded by Hemadri and confirmed by the **Paithan copper plate** of Ramachandra; not Narasimha Deva or Namadeva.
- **Why Ganapati was freed (1199):** as a counterweight to Hoysala **Veera Ballala II's** push into the **Krishna–Tungabhadra** region.
- **Rudrama's victory over Mahadeva:** recorded in the **Bidar fort inscription** (APPSC PYQ) – not Bayyaram, Chandupatla or Hanumakonda.

=== Scholars – Hemadri, Sarngadeva, Changadeva; the Hemadpanti Style
# Hemadri
- **Chaturvarga Chintamani:** Sanskrit encyclopaedia by **Hemadri** (APPSC G2 2000 ×2; APPSC PYQ) – on **dharma, rituals, charity, pilgrimage and vratas**; not Vidyanatha, Jayapa Senani or Palkuriki Somanatha.
- **Office:** **Shrikaranadhipa** (head of secretariat and records) under **Mahadeva and Ramachandra**.
- **On Kakatiya Rudra:** Hemadri claimed **Jaitugi captured and sacrificed Rudra**.
- **Hemadpanti style:** credited to Hemadri – local **black basalt**, dressed stones **interlocked without mortar** (**dry masonry**).
# Other scholars and works
| Person | Work / role |
|---|---|
| **Sarngadeva** | Court musician of **Singhana II**; **Sangita Ratnakara** – base text of both Hindustani and Carnatic music (not Hemadri) |
| **Changadeva** | Court astronomer of Singhana II; **grandson of Bhaskaracharya**; school at **Patan** to teach Bhaskara's works |
| **Bhaskaracharya** (Bhaskara II) | **Lilavati**, the mathematics treatise (Uttarakhand PCS 2010) – not Ramanuja, Kautilya or Amartya Sen |
| **Dnyaneshwar** | **Dnyaneshwari** – Marathi commentary on the **Bhagavad Gita**, **1290**, Nevasa, in **Ramachandra's** reign |
| **Chakradhar Swami** | Founder of the **Mahanubhava** sect (not Dnyaneshwar) |
| **Mhaimbhat** | **Lilacharitra** = first Marathi prose |
- Later Bhakti saints Namdev, Tukaram and Ramdas carried Upanishad and Gita ideas into Marathi.

=== Fall of Devagiri to Delhi
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1296** | **First Muslim invasion of the South**: Alauddin Khalji, then governor of Kara under Sultan Jalaluddin, raids Devagiri and defeats **Ramachandra**; promised the yearly revenue of **Ellichpur** but did not send it |
| 1307–08 | **Malik Kafur**: Ramachandra submits and is taken to Delhi, then restored as a vassal |
| 1313 | Shankaradeva dies in Kafur's invasion |
| 1317–18 | **Harapaladeva** killed by **Mubarak Khalji**; kingdom annexed |
| After 1323 | **Malik Burhanuddin** of Devagiri given charge of Andhra; Devagiri = the Sultanate's Deccan base |
- **Significance:** Devagiri was the **first southern kingdom to fall** to Delhi and opened the road to Warangal, Dwarasamudra and Madurai.
- **⚠ Trap:** Kafur's 1307–08 campaign brought the submission of **Ramachandra**, not Harapaladeva.
