=== Prataparudra Yasobhushanam and Kakatiya Court Literature
- **Prataparudra Yasobhushanam:** Sanskrit work on **alankara (poetics)** praising **Prataparudra II**, by his court poet **Vidyanatha** (APPSC G2 2000, 2011; Police Constable 2016; GS common paper; APPSC PYQ ×5) – not Prataparudra I or II themselves, Jayapa, Ambadeva, Sarangadhara, Rudradeva, Tikkana, Ketana, Baddena or Palkuriki Somanatha.
- **Vidyanatha = Agastya** (G2 2012): historians identify the two; Agastya also wrote **Nalakirtikaumudi**, **Balabharata** and **Krishnacharita**.
- **"What Bana was to Harsha, Vidyanatha was to Prataparudradeva."**
- **Gangadevi**, author of **Madhura Vijayam** (Vijayanagara), hailed **Agastya** as her guru; Madhura Vijayam also describes the hardship after the conquest.

=== Rudrama Devi (1262–1289)
# Family and accession
- **Daughter of Ganapatideva** and **Kupama Devi** (APPSC PYQ); Ganapati had **no son**, so she was raised as a son (**putrika**).
- **Kakatiya** queen (Uttarakhand PCS 2021) – not Eastern Ganga, Hoysala or Western Chalukya.
- **Co-regent from 1258** under the male name **Rudradeva Maharaja**; ruled to **1289**.
- **First woman ruler of the Andhra region** (Rudramba; APPSC PYQ); **second great woman ruler of medieval India after Razia Sultana**.
- **4th sovereign Kakatiya** (Rudradeva → Mahadeva → Ganapatideva → Rudrama).
- **Husband:** Chalukya prince **Veerabhadra** (Nidadavolu).
| Daughter | Married / son |
|---|---|
| **Mummadamma** | Mahadeva; mother of **Prataparudra II** |
| Rudramma | Yadava prince **Ellanadeva** |
| Ruyyamma | Induluri Annaya |
- **Children:** **three daughters**, no son (APPSC Drug Inspector 2011).
# Wars and title
- **Statements (UPRO 2023):** "4th independent ruler" ✓ and "defeated the Pandyas, Eastern Gangas of Odisha and Seunas of Devagiri" ✓ → **both**.
- **Rayagajakesari:** title taken for her victory over the **rulers of Devagiri** (Yadava **Mahadeva**, chased back to Devagiri) (APPSC G2 PYQ ×3) – not the Pandyas, Kalinga rulers or Vaidumbas.
- **Internal revolt:** half-brothers **Harihara and Murari**, crushed with Recherla Prasaditya and others.
- **Vengi:** occupied by Ganga **Narasimhadeva** (1262); regained **1278** when **Poti Nayaka and Prolinayaka** beat Virabhanudeva.
- **Death, 1289:** killed fighting the Kayastha rebel **Ambadeva** (made head of Kadapa in 1272) with general **Mallikarjuna Nayaka**.
- **Chandupatla inscription** (**Nalgonda**), set up by her servant **Puvvula Mummadi**, records her death (APPSC Asst. Welfare Officers) – not Tripurantakam (Ambadeva's victories), Gurjala or Induluru.
~~ Sources: NextGen AP History - Kakatiyas p.15-17, p.51-53; NextGen previous papers p.14; app notes 1:93 (sub 0, 1, 2)

=== Marco Polo's Account
- **Who:** **Marco Polo**, the **Venetian (Italian)** traveller; work **Il Milione / The Travels**.
- **When:** visited in **Rudramadevi's** reign (DAO 2011; G2 2011 ×2; Group-II-A 1984) – reached the Coromandel coast c. **1292–93**, "towards the end of her reign" (most books; older books ended her reign c. 1295); strictly she died in **Nov 1289** (Chandupatla inscription), so his "queen" account came just after her death, early in Prataparudra II's reign.
- **Where:** landed at the Kakatiya port **Motupalli ("Mutfili")** (Group-I 1990-91; G1 1994; APPSC PYQ) – not Ibn Battuta, Abdur Razzaq, Nicolo Conti, Domingo Paes, Jordanus, Hiuen Tsang or Columbus.
| Theme | Observation |
|---|---|
| Ruler | Praised the queen's strict and just rule and the country's prosperity |
| Trade | Flourishing sea trade; port duties at Motupalli; merchants **not** heavily taxed |
| Wealth | **Diamond** mines of the Golconda region (APPSC PYQ); fine textiles |
| Climate, food | Hot climate; **rice** the only local grain |

=== Fall of Warangal (1323) and the Captivity of Prataparudra
- **Captor:** **Ulugh Khan** (Juna Khan, later **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**), under Sultan **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** (APPSC G2 2019; G2 PYQ; Group-II-A 1989) – not Malik Kafur, Alauddin Khalji or Malik Chajju.
- **Year:** **1323** – the end of the Kakatiya kingdom (Group-II-B 1989); final siege about **five months**; **Bobbareddi's** withdrawal weakened the defence.
- **Death:** Prataparudra **drowned himself in the Narmada** on the way to Delhi (**Vilasa** copper plate of Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka; one account says the Kaveri).
- **Renaming:** Orugallu → **Sultanpur** (APPSC PYQ).
- **After 1323:** Andhra under **Malik Burhanuddin**; **Malik Maqbul** (converted Brahmin Jagannatha Pandit) at Warangal; survivors **Prolaya Nayaka, Racherla Singama, Prolaya Vema** led the liberation → Musunuri and Reddi states.
- **Causes:** southern disunity, weak cavalry, betrayal by chiefs, exhaustion from repeated raids – **not** a European sea blockade.
~~ Sources: NextGen AP History - Kakatiyas p.19, p.57-59; NextGen previous papers p.14, p.58; app notes 1:93 (sub 6)

=== Prataparudra II – Campaigns and the Delhi Invasions
- **Prataparudra II (1289–1323):** grandson of Rudrama through **Mummadamma**; title **Kumara Rudradeva**; last Kakatiya.
- **Campaigns:** commanders beat **Ambadeva** at **Tripurantakam** (1291); **Ranganatha** of Nellore (Pandya ally) replaced by **Manuma Gandapalu**; Gonavithala pushed to Devagiri.
- **Nayankara:** kingdom divided into **77 Nayankaras** held by military officers (**Nayakas**); each held a **fort**, kept troops and paid a **fixed tribute**; about **one-fourth** of the land; continued as Vijayanagara **Amaranayakas** → later **Poligars**.
| No. | Year | Commander | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **1303** | Alauddin's **Malik Fakhruddin Juna** | **Repulsed at Upparapalli** by **Pothuganti Aili and Recherla Venna** – first attack on Orugallu |
| 2 | 1309–10 | **Malik Kafur** | Siege; tribute and Delhi overlordship |
| 3 | 1318 | **Mubarak Khalji**'s **Khusrau Khan** | Tribute renewed |
| 4 | 1321–22 | Ulugh Khan | Rumour of the Sultan's death; army chased back |
| 5 | **1323** | **Ulugh Khan** | Warangal fell |
- **1311:** Kakatiya troops under **Muppidi Nayaka** joined Kafur's march on Madurai; **1316:** tribute stopped after Alauddin's death.
- **⚠ Trap:** the 1323 conquest was by Ulugh Khan, not Khusrau Khan.
~~ Sources: NextGen AP History - Kakatiyas p.17-19, p.53-57; app notes 1:93 (sub 4, 5)
