## SUB 1 | Six Phases of Indian Foreign Policy
[1:0] TABLE head: Phase | Label | Features
  [1:0.0] 1946–62 | Optimistic non-alignment | Idealism, Panchsheel, NAM, Commonwealth, anti-imperialism; "Hindi-Chini Bhai-Bhai"
  [1:0.1] 1962–71 | Realism and recovery | 1965 war, Tashkent (1966); defence pact with the US (1964); no NPT (1968)
  [1:0.2] 1971–91 | Regional assertion | Indo-Soviet Treaty, Bangladesh, Pokhran-I, IPKF
  [1:0.3] 1991–98 | Safeguarding strategic autonomy | USSR collapse, 1991 crisis; Look East, Gujral Doctrine
  [1:0.4] 1998–2013 | Balancing power | Pokhran-II, NFU, Indo-US deal (NSG waiver 2008)
  [1:0.5] 2014 onwards | Energetic engagement | Multi-alignment, "5S" (Samman, Samvad, Sahyog, Shanti, Samriddhi)
[1:1] - Idealism → realism; non-alignment → multi-alignment; the constant is **strategic autonomy**.
[1:2] # Nehruvian era
[1:3] - NAM, Panchsheel, Commonwealth membership.
[1:4] - **Korean War**: India headed the **Neutral Nations Repatriation Commission** for POWs – **Gen. K.S. Thimayya**.
[1:5] - Kashmir taken to the UN (Jan 1948); the **1962 war** with China showed the **limits of idealism**.

  [1:7.2] Bangladesh liberation | 16 Dec 1971 | Pakistan army surrendered in Dhaka

[1:10] - **Look East Policy** 1991–92, **P.V. Narasimha Rao**; closer to ASEAN; ties with **Israel**.
[1:11] - **LPG reforms**: attracting **FDI and markets** became foreign-policy goals; strategic ties with the US in the "unipolar moment".
[1:12] - **Gujral Doctrine (1996)**: goodwill to neighbours without asking anything in return.

## SUB 2 | Post-1998 and Contemporary Multi-alignment
[2:0] # Pokhran-II and after
[2:1] - **Pokhran-II (Operation Shakti), May 1998**, PM **Vajpayee**: India declared itself a **nuclear-weapon state** (not NPT-recognised, not NSG member).
[2:2] - **No First Use** announced by the **Vajpayee** government; formal doctrine 2003.
[2:3] - Sanctions: US (**Glenn Amendment**), Japan; followed by **Jaswant Singh–Strobe Talbott** talks (after the tests, not before).
[2:4] - **Lahore Declaration, Feb 1999** with Delhi–Lahore **bus diplomacy**.
[2:5] - **Kargil 1999**: first conflict after both had tested nuclear weapons; US pressure made Pakistan withdraw.

## SUB 3 | Cold War and World-Order Concepts
[3:0] - **Cold War**: decades-long US–Soviet rivalry (1947–91) – anti-communist suspicion, arms race, brink of nuclear war (Cuban Missile Crisis 1962); not the "Space Race" or "Missile Crisis" alone.
[3:1] - **Truman Doctrine (1947)**: US aid to Greece and Turkey to **contain the USSR** (containment) – not farm output or the UN.
[3:2] - **Hegemony**: the dominant or preponderant position of one state over others (caucus = party meeting; demagogue = rabble-rouser; gerrymander = rigging constituency boundaries).
[3:3] - **Age of Enlightenment** (18th c.): society can be improved through the **use of reason** (not religion, realism or idealism).
[3:4] - **Post-Cold War order (after 1991)**: US-led liberal order ("end of history") → today multipolarity, minilaterals (Quad, AUKUS, I2U2), geo-economic fragmentation.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Ch89 p.860 (cold war blocs); PMF IAS International Relations p.4-6 (world order, minilaterals); hegemony, Truman Doctrine and Enlightenment: general knowledge, not checked
