## SUB 0 | Articles 29 and 30 and the Linguistic Minority Provisions
[0:0] # Cultural and Educational Rights (Part III)
[0:1] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [0:1.0] 29(1) | Any section of citizens having a distinct language, script or culture shall have the right to conserve it – the Fundamental Right protecting cultural rights (not Arts. 14, 25, 30, 350, 350A, 351)
  [0:1.1] 29(2) | No citizen shall be denied admission into any educational institution maintained by the State or receiving State aid on grounds only of religion, race, caste, language or any of them (no "sex" or "place of birth")
  [0:1.2] 30(1) | All minorities, religious or linguistic, have the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice – the Article on the educational rights of minorities (not Arts. 29, 31, 31A, 21A, 44)
  [0:1.3] 30(1A) | 44th Amendment (1978): compensation to be fixed so as not to abridge the right when a minority institution's property is compulsorily acquired
  [0:1.4] 30(2) | State shall not discriminate in granting aid on the ground that an institution is under minority management (religion or language)
[0:2] - **Art. 29 protects both religious and linguistic minorities**, but the Supreme Court held it is not restricted to minorities – it covers any section of citizens (even the majority). **Art. 30 = only minorities.** Both apply to citizens.
[0:3] - Related in match questions: **Art. 16(2)** – no discrimination in **public employment** on religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth; **old Art. 31(1)** – no deprivation of **property** save by authority of law (now Art. 300A); Art. 19 – freedoms; Art. 26 – manage religious affairs.
[0:4] - A political promise to conserve a section's language is **not a "corrupt practice"** under the RPA, 1951.
[0:5] # Linguistic minority Articles (Part XVII)
[0:6] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [0:6.0] 347 | President may recognise a language spoken by a substantial part of a State's population
  [0:6.1] 350 | Representation for redress of grievances in any language used in the Union/State
  [0:6.2] 350A | Instruction in the mother tongue at the primary stage
  [0:6.3] 350B | Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities, appointed by the President; reports to the President, laid before each House of Parliament and sent to the States – safeguards linguistic minorities (not ethnic or aboriginal)
[0:7] - **350A and 350B** inserted by the **7th Amendment, 1956** (States Reorganisation Commission's advice). The Special Officer is designated **Commissioner for Linguistic Minorities (CLM)**; office created **1957**; HQ **New Delhi** (moved to Allahabad/Prayagraj for a time, **back to New Delhi in 2015**); regional offices Belgaum, Chennai, Kolkata; under the Ministry of Minority Affairs.
[0:8] - **Linguistic minorities are identified State-wise** (mother tongue differs from the State majority).

## SUB 1 | Meaning of Minority and the Notified Communities
[1:0] # Meaning
[1:1] - **"Minority" is not defined** in the Constitution; the word appears in **Articles 29, 30, 350A and 350B**. The Constitution recognises only **religious and linguistic minorities** – not ethnic minorities.
[1:2] - **NCM Act 1992, Section 2(c):** minority = a community **notified as such by the Central Government** (not the President, Parliament, a State or the NCM); critics say it gives the Centre "unbridled power".
[1:3] - **The State is the unit** for minority status (**T.M.A. Pai, 2002**, 11 judges) – status depends on the community's population in the State concerned. States may name their own minorities; **11 States** had declared Jains a minority before 2014.
[1:4] - **Minority rights are not absolute**: the State may regulate standards (and attach reasonable conditions to aid) but cannot take away management. They aim at equality, not privilege.
[1:5] # Notified minorities
[1:6] TABLE head: Community | Notified | Census 2011 share
  [1:6.0] Muslims | 1993 | 14.2% (largest)
  [1:6.1] Christians | 1993 | 2.3% (second largest)
  [1:6.2] Sikhs | 1993 | 1.7%
  [1:6.3] Buddhists | 1993 | 0.7%
  [1:6.4] Parsis (Zoroastrians) | 1993 | smallest notified minority
  [1:6.5] Jains | Cabinet 20 Jan 2014; notified 27 January 2014 – the sixth | 0.4%
[1:7] - The first five were notified in **1993** (then Ministry of Welfare); six together ≈ **19%** of India. **Jews are not notified** – their holy book is the **Torah** (first five books of the Hebrew Bible).
[1:8] - **Art. 25, Explanation II:** "Hindus" include **Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists** (not Christians, Parsis or Jews).
[1:9] - **Muslims** have their own uncodified **personal law** (Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937) – the community usually keyed for "has its own personal law".

[2:2] # Composition and powers
[2:3] - **Chairperson + Vice-Chairperson + 5 Members = 7 members**; **nominated by the Central (Union) Government** (not the President, PM or Minister); **five incl. the Chairperson** must be from minority communities; tenure **3 years**; removal by the Central Government.
[2:4] - **Statutory, not constitutional** (it is not under Art. 350B – that is the Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities).
[2:5] - Functions: evaluate development of minorities, monitor safeguards, **recommend effective implementation of safeguards by the Central or State Governments**, look into complaints, studies (not conducting elections to minority institutions). Powers of a **civil court** while inquiring.
[2:6] - **July 2026:** **Harjit Singh Grewal** Chairperson; Munawari Begum Vice-Chairperson; only 4 of 7 posts filled.
[2:7] # State Minorities Commissions
[2:8] - Set up by **State Governments** (under State laws or orders); offices in State capitals.
[2:9] - Count: NextGen lists **15** States (AP, Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Delhi, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Maharashtra, MP, Manipur, Rajasthan, TN, Uttarakhand, UP, WB); by **2017 the Ministry gave 18** (adding Kerala, Punjab, Telangana); **19 States/UTs** as of 2026.
[2:10] - **NCSK** (National Commission for Safai Karamcharis): NCSK Act 1993 **lapsed in 2004**; continues as a non-statutory body.

## SUB 3 | Minority Educational Institutions – NCMEI and Court Cases
[3:0] # NCMEI Act, 2004
[3:1] - **National Commission for Minority Educational Institutions**: **Chairperson + 3 Members** (two until the 2010 amendment), nominated by the **Central Government**; the Chairperson must be from a **minority community** (a retired High Court judge); **5-year term**; removal by the Central Government – **no Parliamentary approval** needed.
[3:2] - Decides **minority status** of institutions, affiliation disputes, issues minority certificates; civil court powers.
[3:3] - **Section 10 – No Objection Certificate:** a person wishing to set up a minority educational institution applies to the **competent authority of the State Government** (its Department of Education); the authority must grant or reject within **90 days** (no discretion over the time limit) and **communicate a rejection**; if it does not, the NOC is **deemed granted**.
[3:4] - **Reservation:** minority educational institutions are **exempt** from SC/ST/OBC reservation – **Art. 15(5)** (93rd Amendment) and Art. 15(6) (EWS) exclude them → SC quota in a minority institution = **nil**.
[3:5] # Cases
[3:6] TABLE head: Case | Year | Held
  [3:6.0] In re Kerala Education Bill | 1958 | Art. 143 Presidential reference; Art. 30 not absolute; reasonable conditions for aid
  [3:6.1] St. Xavier's College v. Gujarat | 1974 | Right to administer protected; regulation only for standards and excellence
  [3:6.2] St. Stephen's College v. Delhi University | 1992 | Aided minority college may prefer its own community (up to about 50%)
  [3:6.3] T.M.A. Pai Foundation | 2002 | 11 judges; State is the unit; regulation, not control
  [3:6.4] Bal Patil v. Union of India | 2005 | Declined to direct the Centre to notify Jains; left to States
  [3:6.5] P.A. Inamdar | 2005 | 7 judges; no State quota in unaided institutions → Art. 15(5)

## SUB 7 | Waqf Administration
[7:0] - Order: **Waqf Act 1954 → Waqf Act 1995 → Amendment 2013 → Amendment 2025**. The **Waqf Act, 1995** governs the **administration of waqf properties** through State Waqf Boards, the Central Waqf Council and Waqf Tribunals.
[7:1] - **Central Waqf Council:** statutory, **1964**; advises the Centre; the **2013 Amendment** let it **direct State Boards**.
[7:2] - **Waqf (Amendment) Bill 2024:** introduced in Lok Sabha **8 August 2024**, passed **April 2025** – focus **transparency, better governance and accountability** (not privatisation, abolition or nationalisation).
[7:3] - **2025 Act** (assent 5 April 2025; 1995 Act renamed **UMEED Act**): waqf by user only for properties registered before the law; at least **two non-Muslim members** per Board; central portal within **6 months** – **UMEED portal** (June 2025); Limitation Act applies; appeal to HC within **90 days**.
[7:4] - **SC interim order (Sep 2025):** stayed the 5-years-practising-Islam condition; capped non-Muslims at **3** (State Board) / 4 (Central Council); accepted the Limitation Act change.
