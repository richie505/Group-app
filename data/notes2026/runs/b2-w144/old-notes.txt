=== NOTES 2:144 | Minority Rights – Articles 29 and 30, Ncm, Sachar Committee, Schemes
## SUB 0 | Articles 29 and 30 and the Linguistic Minority Provisions
[144:0:0] # Cultural and Educational Rights (Part III)
[144:0:1] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [144:0:1.0] 29(1) | Any section of citizens having a distinct language, script or culture shall have the right to conserve it – the Fundamental Right protecting cultural rights (not Arts. 14, 25, 30, 350, 350A, 351)
  [144:0:1.1] 29(2) | No citizen shall be denied admission into any educational institution maintained by the State or receiving State aid on grounds only of religion, race, caste, language or any of them (no "sex" or "place of birth")
  [144:0:1.2] 30(1) | All minorities, religious or linguistic, have the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice – the Article on the educational rights of minorities (not Arts. 29, 31, 31A, 21A, 44)
  [144:0:1.3] 30(1A) | 44th Amendment (1978): compensation to be fixed so as not to abridge the right when a minority institution's property is compulsorily acquired
  [144:0:1.4] 30(2) | State shall not discriminate in granting aid on the ground that an institution is under minority management (religion or language)
[144:0:2] - **Art. 29 protects both religious and linguistic minorities**, but the Supreme Court held it is not restricted to minorities – it covers any section of citizens (even the majority). **Art. 30 = only minorities.** Both apply to citizens.
[144:0:3] - Related in match questions: **Art. 16(2)** – no discrimination in **public employment** on religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth; **old Art. 31(1)** – no deprivation of **property** save by authority of law (now Art. 300A); Art. 19 – freedoms; Art. 26 – manage religious affairs.
[144:0:4] - A political promise to conserve a section's language is **not a "corrupt practice"** under the RPA, 1951.
[144:0:5] # Linguistic minority Articles (Part XVII)
[144:0:6] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [144:0:6.0] 347 | President may recognise a language spoken by a substantial part of a State's population
  [144:0:6.1] 350 | Representation for redress of grievances in any language used in the Union/State
  [144:0:6.2] 350A | Instruction in the mother tongue at the primary stage
  [144:0:6.3] 350B | Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities, appointed by the President; reports to the President, laid before each House of Parliament and sent to the States – safeguards linguistic minorities (not ethnic or aboriginal)
[144:0:7] - **350A and 350B** inserted by the **7th Amendment, 1956** (States Reorganisation Commission's advice). The Special Officer is designated **Commissioner for Linguistic Minorities (CLM)**; office created **1957**; HQ **New Delhi** (moved to Allahabad/Prayagraj for a time, **back to New Delhi in 2015**); regional offices Belgaum, Chennai, Kolkata; under the Ministry of Minority Affairs.
[144:0:8] - **Linguistic minorities are identified State-wise** (mother tongue differs from the State majority).
[144:0:9] > Exam angle: "conserve language, script or culture" = Art. 29(1); "no denial of admission" = Art. 29(2); "establish and administer" = Art. 30(1); linguistic minorities officer = Art. 350B.

## SUB 1 | Meaning of Minority and the Notified Communities
[144:1:0] # Meaning
[144:1:1] - **"Minority" is not defined** in the Constitution; the word appears in **Articles 29, 30, 350A and 350B**. The Constitution recognises only **religious and linguistic minorities** – not ethnic minorities.
[144:1:2] - **NCM Act 1992, Section 2(c):** minority = a community **notified as such by the Central Government** (not the President, Parliament, a State or the NCM); critics say it gives the Centre "unbridled power".
[144:1:3] - **The State is the unit** for minority status (**T.M.A. Pai, 2002**, 11 judges) – status depends on the community's population in the State concerned. States may name their own minorities; **11 States** had declared Jains a minority before 2014.
[144:1:4] - **Minority rights are not absolute**: the State may regulate standards (and attach reasonable conditions to aid) but cannot take away management. They aim at equality, not privilege.
[144:1:5] # Notified minorities
[144:1:6] TABLE head: Community | Notified | Census 2011 share
  [144:1:6.0] Muslims | 1993 | 14.2% (largest)
  [144:1:6.1] Christians | 1993 | 2.3% (second largest)
  [144:1:6.2] Sikhs | 1993 | 1.7%
  [144:1:6.3] Buddhists | 1993 | 0.7%
  [144:1:6.4] Parsis (Zoroastrians) | 1993 | smallest notified minority
  [144:1:6.5] Jains | Cabinet 20 Jan 2014; notified 27 January 2014 – the sixth | 0.4%
[144:1:7] - The first five were notified in **1993** (then Ministry of Welfare); six together ≈ **19%** of India. **Jews are not notified** – their holy book is the **Torah** (first five books of the Hebrew Bible).
[144:1:8] - **Art. 25, Explanation II:** "Hindus" include **Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists** (not Christians, Parsis or Jews).
[144:1:9] - **Muslims** have their own uncodified **personal law** (Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937) – the community usually keyed for "has its own personal law".

## SUB 2 | National Commission for Minorities and State Minorities Commissions
[144:2:0] # Evolution
[144:2:1] TABLE head: Year | Milestone
  [144:2:1.0] 1978 | Non-statutory Minorities Commission (MHA resolution of 12 Jan 1978)
  [144:2:1.1] 1992 | NCM Act, 1992 (in force 17 May 1993) – statutory; renamed NCM
  [144:2:1.2] 1993 | First statutory Commission; first chairman Justice Mohammed Sardar Ali Khan
  [144:2:1.3] 1995 | Amendment added the Vice-Chairperson
  [144:2:1.4] 2006 | Ministry of Minority Affairs carved out of MoSJE (Haj added 2016)
[144:2:2] # Composition and powers
[144:2:3] - **Chairperson + Vice-Chairperson + 5 Members = 7 members**; **nominated by the Central (Union) Government** (not the President, PM or Minister); **five incl. the Chairperson** must be from minority communities; tenure **3 years**; removal by the Central Government.
[144:2:4] - **Statutory, not constitutional** (it is not under Art. 350B – that is the Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities).
[144:2:5] - Functions: evaluate development of minorities, monitor safeguards, **recommend effective implementation of safeguards by the Central or State Governments**, look into complaints, studies (not conducting elections to minority institutions). Powers of a **civil court** while inquiring.
[144:2:6] - **July 2026:** **Harjit Singh Grewal** Chairperson; Munawari Begum Vice-Chairperson; only 4 of 7 posts filled.
[144:2:7] # State Minorities Commissions
[144:2:8] - Set up by **State Governments** (under State laws or orders); offices in State capitals.
[144:2:9] - Count: NextGen lists **15** States (AP, Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Delhi, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Maharashtra, MP, Manipur, Rajasthan, TN, Uttarakhand, UP, WB); by **2017 the Ministry gave 18** (adding Kerala, Punjab, Telangana); **19 States/UTs** as of 2026.
[144:2:10] - **NCSK** (National Commission for Safai Karamcharis): NCSK Act 1993 **lapsed in 2004**; continues as a non-statutory body.

## SUB 3 | Minority Educational Institutions – NCMEI and Court Cases
[144:3:0] # NCMEI Act, 2004
[144:3:1] - **National Commission for Minority Educational Institutions**: **Chairperson + 3 Members** (two until the 2010 amendment), nominated by the **Central Government**; the Chairperson must be from a **minority community** (a retired High Court judge); **5-year term**; removal by the Central Government – **no Parliamentary approval** needed.
[144:3:2] - Decides **minority status** of institutions, affiliation disputes, issues minority certificates; civil court powers.
[144:3:3] - **Section 10 – No Objection Certificate:** a person wishing to set up a minority educational institution applies to the **competent authority of the State Government** (its Department of Education); the authority must grant or reject within **90 days** (no discretion over the time limit) and **communicate a rejection**; if it does not, the NOC is **deemed granted**.
[144:3:4] - **Reservation:** minority educational institutions are **exempt** from SC/ST/OBC reservation – **Art. 15(5)** (93rd Amendment) and Art. 15(6) (EWS) exclude them → SC quota in a minority institution = **nil**.
[144:3:5] # Cases
[144:3:6] TABLE head: Case | Year | Held
  [144:3:6.0] In re Kerala Education Bill | 1958 | Art. 143 Presidential reference; Art. 30 not absolute; reasonable conditions for aid
  [144:3:6.1] St. Xavier's College v. Gujarat | 1974 | Right to administer protected; regulation only for standards and excellence
  [144:3:6.2] St. Stephen's College v. Delhi University | 1992 | Aided minority college may prefer its own community (up to about 50%)
  [144:3:6.3] T.M.A. Pai Foundation | 2002 | 11 judges; State is the unit; regulation, not control
  [144:3:6.4] Bal Patil v. Union of India | 2005 | Declined to direct the Centre to notify Jains; left to States
  [144:3:6.5] P.A. Inamdar | 2005 | 7 judges; no State quota in unaided institutions → Art. 15(5)

## SUB 8 | Waqf Administration
[144:8:0] - Order: **Waqf Act 1954 → Waqf Act 1995 → Amendment 2013 → Amendment 2025**. The **Waqf Act, 1995** governs the **administration of waqf properties** through State Waqf Boards, the Central Waqf Council and Waqf Tribunals.
[144:8:1] - **Central Waqf Council:** statutory, **1964**; advises the Centre; the **2013 Amendment** let it **direct State Boards**.
[144:8:2] - **Waqf (Amendment) Bill 2024:** introduced in Lok Sabha **8 August 2024**, passed **April 2025** – focus **transparency, better governance and accountability** (not privatisation, abolition or nationalisation).
[144:8:3] - **2025 Act** (assent 5 April 2025; 1995 Act renamed **UMEED Act**): waqf by user only for properties registered before the law; at least **two non-Muslim members** per Board; central portal within **6 months** – **UMEED portal** (June 2025); Limitation Act applies; appeal to HC within **90 days**.
[144:8:4] - **SC interim order (Sep 2025):** stayed the 5-years-practising-Islam condition; capped non-Muslims at **3** (State Board) / 4 (Central Council); accepted the Limitation Act change.

## SUB 3 | Unorganised Sector – 2008 Act, NCEUS, e-Shram and Shram Suvidha
[145:3:0] # Unorganised Workers' Social Security Act, 2008
[145:3:1] - Set up the **National Social Security Board**; schemes in **Schedule I** – IGNOAPS, National Family Benefit Scheme, **Janani Suraksha Yojana**, Janashree Bima Yojana, AABY, RSBY (APY is not in it). [145:3:3] - **NCEUS** (2004, **Arjun Sengupta**): **77%** living on **under Rs 20 a day**. **Over 90%** of India's workers are informal.
[145:3:4] # e-Shram
[145:3:5] - Launched **26 August 2021** (Ministry of Labour); India's first **Aadhaar-seeded National Database of Unorganised Workers**; age **16-59**; not income-tax payers, not EPFO/ESIC members; **12-digit UAN**.

[145:3:8] - **LIN (Labour Identification Number)** is issued to **establishments** (not individual workers) on the **Shram Suvidha Portal** (2014) for unified labour compliance.

## SUB 2 | Consumer Protection Act 2019 – Rights, Consumer and Complaints
[146:2:0] - **Replaced** the Consumer Protection Act, **1986** (2019 Act: assent 9 August 2019). Like the old Act, a **three-tier** consumer dispute redressal machinery.
[146:2:1] - **Six rights:** safety, information, choice, to be heard, redressal, **consumer awareness** (no "right to private ownership").
[146:2:2] - **Consumer** includes **online/electronic** purchases for personal use; a buyer for **resale or commercial purpose** is excluded. **IMA v. V.P. Shantha (1995)**: paid medical services are a **"service"**.
[146:2:3] - New in 2019: **CCPA**, **product liability**, e-commerce rules, **mediation cells** (attached to District, State and National Commissions, not High Courts), e-filing.
[146:2:4] - File where the complainant **lives or works**; admissibility decided within **21 days** or deemed admitted.
[146:2:5] - **Fees:** **nil up to Rs 5 lakh**; a small fee above that – so "no fee is required in any Consumer Forum" is **wrong**.
[146:2:6] - Consumers may get **food samples tested**; the **legal heir** of a deceased consumer may complain (UPSC 2012: statements 1 and 3 correct).
[146:2:7] > Exam angle: statement questions on fee, samples, legal heir; the resale buyer is not a consumer.

=== NOTES 2:147 | Determinants and Principles of India's Foreign Policy; Panchsheel and Non-alignment
## SUB 0 | Panchsheel, 1954
[147:0:0] # The agreement
[147:0:1] - **Signed 29 April 1954** – the **Agreement on Trade and Intercourse between the Tibet Region of China and India**; the five principles are in its **Preamble**. Signed by **Nehru** and Chinese Premier **Zhou Enlai** (Chou En-lai). Signed **before** the 1962 war, not after it.
[147:0:2] - Under the same agreement India accepted China's sovereignty over **Tibet**.
[147:0:3] # The five principles
[147:0:4] TABLE head: No. | Principle
  [147:0:4.0] 1 | Mutual respect for each other's territorial integrity and sovereignty
  [147:0:4.1] 2 | Mutual non-aggression
  [147:0:4.2] 3 | Mutual non-interference in each other's internal affairs
  [147:0:4.3] 4 | Equality and mutual benefit
  [147:0:4.4] 5 | Peaceful co-existence
[147:0:5] - **NOT Panchsheel:** **non-alignment / NAM** (a separate policy – UPSC 1997, UPPSC 1997); "support each other in global forums"; "mutual protection of the environment / of indigenous population" (NDA 2008).
[147:0:6] # Spread of Panchsheel
[147:0:7] - **Burma (Myanmar)** adopted it in **June 1954** (Zhou Enlai–U Nu joint statement) – the third country associated with Panchsheel (APPSC Group 1: "50 years of Panchsheel", 2004). Yugoslavia and Indonesia also adopted it.
[147:0:8] - **Bandung (April 1955)**: Panchsheel incorporated in the **Ten Principles**; **UNGA 1957**: India's resolution on peaceful co-existence; **Belgrade 1961**: core principle of NAM.
[147:0:9] - Don't confuse: **Tribal Panchsheel (1952)** – Nehru's tribal policy, drafted with **Verrier Elwin**.
[147:0:10] - **Sep 2025:** at the Modi–Xi meeting China stressed Panchsheel as the frame for ties.
[147:0:11] > Exam angle: "Which is NOT a principle of Panchsheel?" → **Non-alignment** (asked 4 times); "Panchsheel after 1962" ✗.

## SUB 1 | Non-Aligned Movement
[147:1:0] # Origin
[147:1:1] - **Bandung Conference, April 1955, Bandung (Indonesia)**: **29** Afro-Asian countries; **Ten Principles** of international peace (included Panchsheel) – laid the foundation of NAM.
[147:1:2] - Founded at the **1st NAM Summit, Belgrade (Yugoslavia), September 1961** (not New Delhi, Cairo or Havana).
[147:1:3] - **Non-alignment initiated by Nehru** (broadcast of **7 September 1946** as head of the interim government: keep away from power blocs) (UPPSC 2014).
[147:1:4] - Non-alignment **≠ neutrality**: no military alliance, independent approach, judge each issue on its merits, friendly relations with all.

[147:2:0] # Determinants
[147:2:1] TABLE head: Determinant | How it shapes policy
  [147:2:1.0] Geography | Himalayan frontiers; Indian Ocean position
  [147:2:1.1] History | Anti-colonialism; Nehru's broadcast of 7 September 1946 (away from power blocs)

[147:2:4] # Principles (Laxmikanth)
[147:2:5] - Promotion of world peace; **anti-colonialism**; **anti-racialism** (snapped ties with South Africa 1954 over apartheid, restored 1994); non-alignment; Panchsheel; Afro-Asian bias (Asian Relations Conference, New Delhi 1947); links with the Commonwealth; support to the UN; **disarmament** – India rejects the **NPT (1968)** and **CTBT (1996)** as **discriminatory**.

[147:2:10] # Commonwealth
[147:2:11] - **Nehru**: India's Commonwealth membership is "**an agreement by free will, to be terminated by free will**" (UPPSC 2017) – not Panchsheel or Simla.
[147:2:12] - 1949 (London Declaration): India stayed a full member as a republic; accepts the Crown only as **symbol/Head of the Commonwealth**; India hosted **CHOGM 1983**.

[147:3:2] # Institutions

[147:3:8] - **Credible minimum deterrence**; **No First Use** (retaliation only); massive retaliation; no use against non-nuclear-weapon states; nuclear option kept against major biological/chemical attack.

## SUB 1 | Cold War and World-Order Concepts
[148:1:0] - **Cold War**: decades-long US–Soviet rivalry (1947–91) – anti-communist suspicion, arms race, brink of nuclear war (Cuban Missile Crisis 1962); not the "Space Race" or "Missile Crisis" alone.
[148:1:1] - **Truman Doctrine (1947)**: US aid to Greece and Turkey to **contain the USSR** (containment) – not farm output or the UN.
[148:1:2] - **Hegemony**: the dominant or preponderant position of one state over others (caucus = party meeting; demagogue = rabble-rouser; gerrymander = rigging constituency boundaries).
[148:1:3] - **Age of Enlightenment** (18th c.): society can be improved through the **use of reason** (not religion, realism or idealism).
[148:1:4] - **Post-Cold War order (after 1991)**: US-led liberal order ("end of history") → today multipolarity, minilaterals (Quad, AUKUS, I2U2), geo-economic fragmentation.
[148:1:5] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Ch89 p.860 (cold war blocs); PMF IAS International Relations p.4-6 (world order, minilaterals); hegemony, Truman Doctrine and Enlightenment: general knowledge, not checked
## SUB 2 | Six Phases of Indian Foreign Policy
[148:2:0] TABLE head: Phase | Label | Features
  [148:2:0.0] 1946–62 | Optimistic non-alignment | Idealism, Panchsheel, NAM, Commonwealth, anti-imperialism; "Hindi-Chini Bhai-Bhai"
  [148:2:0.1] 1962–71 | Realism and recovery | 1965 war, Tashkent (1966); defence pact with the US (1964); no NPT (1968)
  [148:2:0.2] 1971–91 | Regional assertion | Indo-Soviet Treaty, Bangladesh, Pokhran-I, IPKF
  [148:2:0.3] 1991–98 | Safeguarding strategic autonomy | USSR collapse, 1991 crisis; Look East, Gujral Doctrine
  [148:2:0.4] 1998–2013 | Balancing power | Pokhran-II, NFU, Indo-US deal (NSG waiver 2008)
  [148:2:0.5] 2014 onwards | Energetic engagement | Multi-alignment, "5S" (Samman, Samvad, Sahyog, Shanti, Samriddhi)
[148:2:1] - Idealism → realism; non-alignment → multi-alignment; the constant is **strategic autonomy**.
[148:2:2] # Nehruvian era
[148:2:3] - NAM, Panchsheel, Commonwealth membership.
[148:2:4] - **Korean War**: India headed the **Neutral Nations Repatriation Commission** for POWs – **Gen. K.S. Thimayya**.
[148:2:5] - Kashmir taken to the UN (Jan 1948); the **1962 war** with China showed the **limits of idealism**.

## SUB 3 | Post-1998 and Contemporary Multi-alignment
[148:3:0] # Pokhran-II and after
[148:3:1] - **Pokhran-II (Operation Shakti), May 1998**, PM **Vajpayee**: India declared itself a **nuclear-weapon state** (not NPT-recognised, not NSG member).
[148:3:2] - **No First Use** announced by the **Vajpayee** government; formal doctrine 2003.
[148:3:3] - Sanctions: US (**Glenn Amendment**), Japan; followed by **Jaswant Singh–Strobe Talbott** talks (after the tests, not before).
[148:3:4] - **Lahore Declaration, Feb 1999** with Delhi–Lahore **bus diplomacy**.
[148:3:5] - **Kargil 1999**: first conflict after both had tested nuclear weapons; US pressure made Pakistan withdraw.

  [148:3:6.0] Hyde Act | 2006
  [148:3:6.1] NSG waiver | 6 Sept 2008
  [148:3:6.2] 123 Agreement signed | Oct 2008
[148:3:7] # Multi-alignment
[148:3:8] - Since 2008 the world moves to multipolarity → **issue-based alignment**: US (security, technology), Russia (defence, energy).
[148:3:9] TABLE head: Platform | Key fact
  [148:3:9.0] Quad | India, US, Japan, Australia; formed 2007, revived 2017
  [148:3:9.1] SCO | Full member 2017
  [148:3:9.2] BRICS | Founding member; India chaired 2026: New Delhi Declaration backed UN reform, opposed one-sided sanctions (Sep 2026)
  [148:3:9.3] Act East | Upgraded from Look East, 2014
  [148:3:9.4] Neighbourhood First | 2014
  [148:3:9.5] G20 Presidency | Dec 2022–Nov 2023; "One Earth, One Family, One Future"; African Union made permanent member
[148:3:10] - **India-UK CETA** signed July 2025 (PM's UK visit). First **Voice of Global South Summit**: **January 2023**.
[148:3:11] # Terms
[148:3:12] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [148:3:12.0] Strategic autonomy | Judge each issue on merits without military blocs; ≠ neutrality
  [148:3:12.1] Multi-alignment | Working with rival powers at once (Quad + BRICS/SCO)
  [148:3:12.2] Issue-based coalitions | Partners by issue, not fixed alliances
  [148:3:12.3] Global South voice | Reformed multilateralism, climate finance, DPI
[148:3:13] - **Contemporary policy with neighbours** = Neighbourhood First, Look/Act East via Myanmar, SAARC, Panchsheel; **non-alignment** is a Cold War bloc policy, not a neighbourhood policy (CDS 2015).

## SUB 4 | Indus Waters Treaty 1960
[149:4:0] - Signed at **Karachi, 19 September 1960** by **Nehru and President Ayub Khan**; broker: **World Bank** (APPSC G2 2017).

[149:4:7] - After Pahalgam the **CCS** put the Treaty "**in abeyance**" from **23 April 2025** – until Pakistan "**credibly and irrevocably**" abjures cross-border terrorism. First ever suspension; the Treaty has no abeyance clause.
