=== Article 370 and Jammu and Kashmir
# Article 370 – special status
- **Article 370** (Part XXI, "temporary provisions") gave **special status to Jammu and Kashmir** – not Art. 365, 367, 369, 371, 372 or 373 (asked 11 times, APPSC 2012–2019).
- J&K had its **own Constitution**; only defence, external affairs and communications automatically applied.
- **Art. 370(3)**: the President could declare it inoperative only on the **recommendation of the Constituent Assembly of the State**.
| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Article 370 operative | 17 November 1952 |
| J&K Constituent Assembly adopted its Constitution | 17 November 1956 |
| **J&K Constitution in force** | **26 January 1957** |
| Article 370 abrogated (Constitution (Application to J&K) Order, 2019); J&K and Ladakh UTs | **5 August 2019** |
| Supreme Court upheld abrogation | December 2023 |
- **Pakistan's response**: downgraded diplomatic ties, suspended bilateral trade and the **Samjhauta Express**, raised it at the UN.
- **Gilgit-Baltistan**: India claims it as part of J&K – a political status issue, not a border dispute.
> Trap: Art. 371 = special provisions for Maharashtra/Gujarat etc.; Tenth Schedule = defections, not J&K.

=== Kashmir, Wars and Agreements
# Accession and the UN
- **Instrument of Accession**: **Maharaja Hari Singh**, **26 October 1947**, after the **tribal invasion**; Indian troops airlifted **27 October**.
- Ceded only **defence, external affairs and communications**; land revenue stayed with the State.
- Sequence: tribal invasion → accession → airlift → **First Indo-Pak war**.
- **India (Nehru)** first referred Kashmir to the **UNSC** on **1 January 1948** under **Article 35** of the UN Charter – not Pakistan; **UNSC Resolution 47 (1948)**: plebiscite conditional on Pakistan's withdrawal – never held.
# Wars and operations
| War | Trigger / operation | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1947–48 (first Kashmir war) | Pakistan-backed tribal invasion | UN ceasefire 1 January 1949; Karachi Agreement 1949 |
| 1965 | Pakistan's Operation Gibraltar, Operation Grand Slam | Tashkent Declaration 1966 |
| 1971 | Bangladesh crisis; Army chief **Gen. Sam Manekshaw** | Pakistan surrendered 16 December 1971; Simla 1972 |
| 1984 Siachen | India's Operation Meghdoot (April 1984) | Line beyond NJ9842 unmarked |
| 1999 Kargil | Pakistani intrusion; India's Operation Vijay | Pakistan withdrew under US pressure; first war between nuclear-armed states |
- **Navy Day, 4 December** – marks **Operation Trident**, the Indian Navy's attack on Karachi in the 1971 war.
- **2003 LoC ceasefire**, reaffirmed in **2021**. 1965 was NOT the first Kashmir war – that was 1947–48.
# Agreements → year and signatories
| Agreement | Year | Signatories / key point |
|---|---|---|
| Karachi Agreement | July 1949 | Military representatives under UNCIP; fixed the ceasefire line |
| Tashkent Declaration | 10 January 1966 | Shastri–Ayub Khan; Soviet mediation; Haji Pir Pass returned to Pakistan; Shastri died at Tashkent |
| **Simla Agreement** | **2 July 1972** | **Indira Gandhi–Z.A. Bhutto**; bilateral settlement, no third party; ceasefire line → **LoC** |
| Lahore Declaration | February 1999 | Vajpayee–Nawaz Sharif; nuclear CBMs; bus diplomacy |
| Agra Summit | July 2001 | Vajpayee–Pervez Musharraf; no joint declaration |
- Tashkent did NOT create the LoC – Simla (1972) did. Simla: signed **2 July 1972** (not June 2; talks began late June).
# Composite Dialogue and disputed places
- **Composite Dialogue**: agreed **1997** → restarted **2004** → stopped after **26/11 (2008)**.
- **Eight baskets**: peace and security/CBMs; J&K; Siachen; **Wullar Barrage/Tulbul**; Sir Creek; terrorism and drugs; economic cooperation; friendly exchanges – **not** nuclear disarmament.
| Place | Feature |
|---|---|
| Sir Creek | 96-km tidal estuary, Rann of Kutch, between Gujarat and Sindh; Pakistan: 1914 map line; India: mid-channel thalweg rule; unsettled |
| Siachen | Saltoro Ridge held since Operation Meghdoot; line beyond NJ9842 unmarked |

=== Indus Waters Treaty 1960
- Signed at **Karachi, 19 September 1960** by **Nehru and President Ayub Khan**; broker: **World Bank** (APPSC G2 2017).
| Rivers | Allotted to |
|---|---|
| Eastern: **Ravi, Beas, Sutlej** | India |
| Western: **Indus, Jhelum, Chenab** | Pakistan (India: domestic use, agriculture, run-of-river hydropower, limited storage) |
- Pakistan gets about **80%** (80.52%) of the waters, India about **20%** (19.48%); no aquifer or drought clause.
- **Permanent Indus Commission**: one Commissioner from each country.
| Article IX level | Handled by |
|---|---|
| "Questions" | PIC |
| "Differences" | Neutral Expert |
| "Disputes" | Court of Arbitration |
- No hierarchy → World Bank appointed both for **Kishanganga and Ratle**.
# Abeyance (2025) and after
- After Pahalgam the **CCS** put the Treaty "**in abeyance**" from **23 April 2025** – until Pakistan "**credibly and irrevocably**" abjures cross-border terrorism. First ever suspension; the Treaty has no abeyance clause.
- Effects: India stops sharing flow data; storage on western rivers; Chenab projects **Kirthai-II (930 MW)** and **Sawalkote (1,856 MW)** (Jun 2026).
- **Jul 2026**: India sent a formal notice to renegotiate. **Aug–Sep 2026**: PCA Court of Arbitration (The Hague), **Ratle** case: Treaty "remains in force"; India **rejected** it – "**no jurisdiction**" (1 Sep 2026).

=== Trade and Connectivity
- **Kartarpur Corridor**: agreement (MoU) **October 2019**; inaugurated by **PM Modi**, **9 November 2019**, **Guru Nanak's 550th birth anniversary**; **4.7-km visa-free** corridor from **Dera Baba Nanak (Gurdaspur)** to **Gurdwara Darbar Sahib, Kartarpur (Narowal)**.
- Agreement renewed for 5 years in **October 2024**; India closed the corridor in **May 2025** after Operation Sindoor; still closed (Apr 2026).
- **MFN**: granted to Pakistan **1996**; withdrawn **February 2019** after Pulwama with **200% customs duty**; Pakistan never reciprocated. After Pahalgam: ban on all imports from Pakistan.
- **Samjhauta Express** (Attari–Lahore): started **1976** after Simla; suspended 2019.
- **Wagah–Attari**: main land trade route. After Pahalgam India closed the **Attari ICP**, revoked SAARC visa-exemption and cut High Commission strength **55 → 35**.
