=== Articles 29 and 30 and the Linguistic Minority Provisions
# Cultural and Educational Rights (Part III)
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **29(1)** | Any section of citizens having a **distinct language, script or culture** shall have the right to **conserve** it – the Fundamental Right protecting **cultural rights** (not Arts. 14, 25, 30, 350, 350A, 351) |
| **29(2)** | No citizen shall be **denied admission** into any educational institution maintained by the State or receiving State aid on grounds only of **religion, race, caste, language** or any of them (no "sex" or "place of birth") |
| **30(1)** | **All minorities, religious or linguistic**, have the right to **establish and administer educational institutions of their choice** – the Article on the educational rights of minorities (not Arts. 29, 31, 31A, 21A, 44) |
| 30(1A) | **44th Amendment (1978)**: compensation to be fixed so as not to abridge the right when a minority institution's property is compulsorily acquired |
| **30(2)** | State shall not **discriminate in granting aid** on the ground that an institution is under minority management (religion or language) |
- **Art. 29 protects both religious and linguistic minorities**, but the Supreme Court held it is not restricted to minorities – it covers any section of citizens (even the majority). **Art. 30 = only minorities.** Both apply to citizens.
- Related in match questions: **Art. 16(2)** – no discrimination in **public employment** on religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth; **old Art. 31(1)** – no deprivation of **property** save by authority of law (now Art. 300A); Art. 19 – freedoms; Art. 26 – manage religious affairs.
- A political promise to conserve a section's language is **not a "corrupt practice"** under the RPA, 1951.
# Linguistic minority Articles (Part XVII)
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| 347 | President may recognise a language spoken by a substantial part of a State's population |
| 350 | Representation for redress of grievances in **any language** used in the Union/State |
| **350A** | Instruction in the **mother tongue at the primary stage** |
| **350B** | **Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities**, appointed by the **President**; reports to the President, laid before each House of Parliament and sent to the States – safeguards **linguistic** minorities (not ethnic or aboriginal) |
- **350A and 350B** inserted by the **7th Amendment, 1956** (States Reorganisation Commission's advice). The Special Officer is designated **Commissioner for Linguistic Minorities (CLM)**; office created **1957**; HQ **New Delhi** (moved to Allahabad/Prayagraj for a time, **back to New Delhi in 2015**); regional offices Belgaum, Chennai, Kolkata; under the Ministry of Minority Affairs.
- **Linguistic minorities are identified State-wise** (mother tongue differs from the State majority).
> Exam angle: "conserve language, script or culture" = Art. 29(1); "no denial of admission" = Art. 29(2); "establish and administer" = Art. 30(1); linguistic minorities officer = Art. 350B.

=== Meaning of Minority and the Notified Communities
# Meaning
- **"Minority" is not defined** in the Constitution; the word appears in **Articles 29, 30, 350A and 350B**. The Constitution recognises only **religious and linguistic minorities** – not ethnic minorities.
- **NCM Act 1992, Section 2(c):** minority = a community **notified as such by the Central Government** (not the President, Parliament, a State or the NCM); critics say it gives the Centre "unbridled power".
- **The State is the unit** for minority status (**T.M.A. Pai, 2002**, 11 judges) – status depends on the community's population in the State concerned. States may name their own minorities; **11 States** had declared Jains a minority before 2014.
- **Minority rights are not absolute**: the State may regulate standards (and attach reasonable conditions to aid) but cannot take away management. They aim at equality, not privilege.

|---|---|---|

| **Christians** | 1993 | 2.3% (second largest) |
| Sikhs | 1993 | 1.7% |

- The first five were notified in **1993** (then Ministry of Welfare); six together ≈ **19%** of India. **Jews are not notified** – their holy book is the **Torah** (first five books of the Hebrew Bible).
- **Art. 25, Explanation II:** "Hindus" include **Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists** (not Christians, Parsis or Jews).
- **Muslims** have their own uncodified **personal law** (Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937) – the community usually keyed for "has its own personal law".

=== National Commission for Minorities and State Minorities Commissions
# Evolution
| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|

# Composition and powers
- **Chairperson + Vice-Chairperson + 5 Members = 7 members**; **nominated by the Central (Union) Government** (not the President, PM or Minister); **five incl. the Chairperson** must be from minority communities; tenure **3 years**; removal by the Central Government.
- **Statutory, not constitutional** (it is not under Art. 350B – that is the Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities).
- Functions: evaluate development of minorities, monitor safeguards, **recommend effective implementation of safeguards by the Central or State Governments**, look into complaints, studies (not conducting elections to minority institutions). Powers of a **civil court** while inquiring.
- **July 2026:** **Harjit Singh Grewal** Chairperson; Munawari Begum Vice-Chairperson; only 4 of 7 posts filled.
# State Minorities Commissions
- Set up by **State Governments** (under State laws or orders); offices in State capitals.
- Count: NextGen lists **15** States (AP, Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Delhi, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Maharashtra, MP, Manipur, Rajasthan, TN, Uttarakhand, UP, WB); by **2017 the Ministry gave 18** (adding Kerala, Punjab, Telangana); **19 States/UTs** as of 2026.
- **NCSK** (National Commission for Safai Karamcharis): NCSK Act 1993 **lapsed in 2004**; continues as a non-statutory body.
> Exam angle: NCM = 1992 Act, 7 members, nominated by the Centre, statutory; "five communities notified to date" is wrong (six since 2014).

=== Minority Educational Institutions – NCMEI and Court Cases
# NCMEI Act, 2004
- **National Commission for Minority Educational Institutions**: **Chairperson + 3 Members** (two until the 2010 amendment), nominated by the **Central Government**; the Chairperson must be from a **minority community** (a retired High Court judge); **5-year term**; removal by the Central Government – **no Parliamentary approval** needed.
- Decides **minority status** of institutions, affiliation disputes, issues minority certificates; civil court powers.
- **Section 10 – No Objection Certificate:** a person wishing to set up a minority educational institution applies to the **competent authority of the State Government** (its Department of Education); the authority must grant or reject within **90 days** (no discretion over the time limit) and **communicate a rejection**; if it does not, the NOC is **deemed granted**.
- **Reservation:** minority educational institutions are **exempt** from SC/ST/OBC reservation – **Art. 15(5)** (93rd Amendment) and Art. 15(6) (EWS) exclude them → SC quota in a minority institution = **nil**.

=== Waqf Administration
- Order: **Waqf Act 1954 → Waqf Act 1995 → Amendment 2013 → Amendment 2025**. The **Waqf Act, 1995** governs the **administration of waqf properties** through State Waqf Boards, the Central Waqf Council and Waqf Tribunals.
- **Central Waqf Council:** statutory, **1964**; advises the Centre; the **2013 Amendment** let it **direct State Boards**.
- **Waqf (Amendment) Bill 2024:** introduced in Lok Sabha **8 August 2024**, passed **April 2025** – focus **transparency, better governance and accountability** (not privatisation, abolition or nationalisation).
- **2025 Act** (assent 5 April 2025; 1995 Act renamed **UMEED Act**): waqf by user only for properties registered before the law; at least **two non-Muslim members** per Board; central portal within **6 months** – **UMEED portal** (June 2025); Limitation Act applies; appeal to HC within **90 days**.
- **SC interim order (Sep 2025):** stayed the 5-years-practising-Islam condition; capped non-Muslims at **3** (State Board) / 4 (Central Council); accepted the Limitation Act change.
