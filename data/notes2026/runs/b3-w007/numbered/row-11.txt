## SUB 0 | Achievements, Failures and Critics of Planning
[0:0] TABLE head: Achievements | Failures
  [0:0.0] Rise in national income, savings, investment, per-capita income (Plans I-XI averaged ~4.5% growth vs ~1% before Independence) | High rural poverty; rising unemployment – root cause of poverty
  [0:0.1] Self-sufficiency in basic, capital and consumer-goods industries | Income inequality not reduced; widened after reforms
  [0:0.2] Foodgrain self-sufficiency – Green Revolution (1966-67), FCI (1965) | Land reforms poorly implemented; unequal land ownership persists
  [0:0.3] Infrastructure – transport, irrigation, power, telecom | Regional disparities persist (a failure, not an achievement)
  [0:0.4] Education, scientific manpower, IT, space; IITs | Neglect of agriculture, lop-sided (capital-intensive) employment strategy, excessive emphasis on PSUs
[0:1] # Drawbacks of the planning process
[0:2] - Drawbacks named by the books: **implementation failure** (the UN expert team called implementation and enforcement "the principal shortcomings"), a **wrong / absent financial strategy** (Ramesh Singh), **misplaced faith in investment** alone, centralisation, politicisation.
[0:3] - APPSC Group 2 Mains 2017 asked for "the chief drawback", and **"All the given answers"** (financial strategy, faith in investment, implementation failure) was keyed. When only one of these is offered, the key is **implementation failure**.
[0:4] # Origin and critics
[0:5] - The idea of planning came from **Soviet Russia** (USSR five-year plans from 1928).
[0:6] TABLE head: Book | Author
  [0:6.0] Planning and the Poor (1974) | B.S. Minhas (also asked as "Planning for the Poor")
  [0:6.1] India: Planning for Industrialization (1970) | Jagdish Bhagwati and Padma Desai
[0:7] - **Padma Desai**: critic of India's planned economy; **first Asian woman** to get a **PhD in Economics from Harvard (1960)**.
[0:8] > Distractors for Minhas: Y.K. Alagh, Jagdish Bhagwati, L.G. Patel, D.R. Gadgil, Charan Singh, Rudra Dutt, Manmohan Singh.

## SUB 1 | Planning After 1991 and the End of the Plan Era
[1:0] - After 1991 planning moved from **"directed" to "indicative"**: the plan shows **priority sectors**; the private sector invests.
[1:1] - Plans were kept after 1991 because they give a **long-term perspective** to a market economy; planning is **not a constitutional requirement**.
[1:2] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [1:2.0] Last plan | Twelfth (2012-17), ended 31 March 2017
  [1:2.1] Planning Commission | Scrapping decided August 2014 (Cabinet 13 Aug; PM's Independence Day speech 15 Aug); last Deputy Chairman M.S. Ahluwalia
  [1:2.2] Successor | NITI Aayog, 1 January 2015, by Union Cabinet resolution
  [1:2.3] NDC (1952) | Inactive; its work passed to the NITI Governing Council; formal abolition pending
[1:3] - Abolition ended **discretionary plan grants**; NITI has **no financial powers** – the Governing Council coordinates policy, it does not redistribute funds.

## SUB 2 | Plan Record – First Plan, Third Plan Failure, Plan Holiday, Fourth Plan
[2:0] - **First Plan** launched **1 April 1951**; focus **agriculture and irrigation**.
[2:1] - **Third Plan** grew only **2.2%** vs **5.6%** target – **Sino-Indian War (1962)**, **Indo-Pak War (1965)**, **1965-66 droughts**.
[2:2] - **Plan Holiday 1966-69** (three Annual Plans) followed the **failure of the Third Plan**.
[2:3] - **Fourth Plan (1969-74)**, **Gadgil strategy**: **growth with stability** + progress towards **self-reliance**; targets agriculture **5%** a year, exports **~7%** a year; achieved **3.3%** vs **5.7%** (droughts, 1971 war); **14 banks nationalised (1969)**.
[2:4] TABLE head: Formula for central plan assistance to States | Fact
  [2:4.0] Gadgil formula | 1969 (Fourth Plan)
  [2:4.1] Gadgil-Mukherjee formula | 1991; highest weight to population (1971 Census) – 60%; per capita income 25%; fiscal performance 7.5%; special problems 7.5%
  [2:4.2] Special Category States | 90% grant : 10% loan; General Category 30 : 70
