## SUB 0 | Rolling Plan (1978-80)
[0:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [0:0.0] Suggested by | Gunnar Myrdal, for backward countries – "Indian Economic Planning in its Broader Setting"
  [0:0.1] Introduced by | Janata Party government (ended the Fifth Plan a year early)
  [0:0.2] Deputy Chairman then | D.T. Lakdawala – credited with introducing it in India
  [0:0.3] Period | April 1978 – March 1980 (1978-80)
  [0:0.4] Name | Janata Sixth Plan (1978-83) = "the rolling plan" – the novel idea of the draft Sixth Plan
  [0:0.5] Outlay | Rs 12,177 crore; stressed employment
  [0:0.6] End | Janata fall → 1979-80 annual plan → Congress Sixth Plan from 1 April 1980
[0:1] # Three plans in one (all four book statements are correct)
[0:2] - 1. A plan for the **current year** (with the annual budget).
[0:3] - 2. A plan for a fixed number of years (**3, 4 or 5**), **revised every year** as the economy requires and extended by one year.
[0:4] - 3. A **perspective plan** of **10, 15 or 20 years**.
[0:5] - Rolling plan **changes both its allocations and its targets every year** → flexibility; criticism: yearly revision **weakened commitment** to targets.
[0:6] > Myrdal suggested – Janata introduced – Lakdawala Deputy Chairman – 1978-80. Not Indira, Rajiv or the National Front; Madhu Dandavate was Deputy Chairman only in 1990-91.

## SUB 1 | Objectives of Planning
[1:0] - **Four long-term objectives:** **growth, modernisation, self-reliance, social justice (equity)** – plus employment and removal of poverty; the long-term objective = **all** of high standard of living, social justice and self-reliance.
[1:1] - **Economic growth = principal objective**; the others are supporting.
[1:2] - Chief objectives also listed in the Plans: **reduce inequalities of income and wealth**, higher growth, **removal of poverty**, employment. "**To achieve social equality**" is not a stated economic objective of the Plans.
[1:3] - Major objective of the Five Year Plans (book key): **self-reliance** and less dependence on foreign sources.
[1:4] - Declared aim (1950 resolution): rapid rise in the **standard of living** through efficient use of resources, more production and **employment for all**.
[1:5] - **Planning** = a conscious effort of the State with a **set of goals**, a **set of means** and a **specific time period**.
[1:6] - Initial strategy of the 1950s: **rapid industrialisation** (Second Plan); cornerstone = **central economic planning**.
[1:7] # Socialistic pattern of society
[1:8] - Adopted at the **Avadi Congress (1955)** → **Second Plan (1956-61)** and **IPR 1956**.
[1:9] - Core element: **social gain, not private profit**, as the test of development (not communes or nationalising all firms).
[1:10] - Planning for socialism aims at a **national minimum standard of living** for all (NPC 1938, Bombay Plan).
[1:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.4-6; NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development ch.2 (goals of Five Year Plans); Second Five Year Plan ch.2 (socialist pattern – social gain, reduction of inequalities); app notes 3:8 sub 2
## SUB 2 | Imperative vs Indicative Planning
[2:0] TABLE head: Feature | Imperative (directive / target) | Indicative
  [2:0.0] Market mechanism | Entirely replaced by a command hierarchy | Plan improves the market
  [2:0.1] Ownership | State / collective | Mixed
  [2:0.2] Targets | Binding quotas, allocation, prices | Guidelines and signals
  [2:0.3] Examples | USSR (1928, Stalin), China | Mixed economies; France
  [2:0.4] India | Public sector in early Plans | From the Eighth Plan (1992-97) after 1991
[2:1] - A **Five-Year Plan** is an **indicative plan of action** reflecting the government's intent.
[2:2] - Soviet collectivisation (Stalin): collective farms = **Kolkhoz** (state farms = sovkhoz).
[2:3] - **Regional planning** first in the USA (1916+; Tennessee Valley).
[2:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.28, p.58 (imperative vs indicative); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.6; Britannica "kolkhoz"; app notes 3:8 sub 5

## SUB 4 | Other Types of Planning
[4:0] TABLE head: Pair | Distinction
  [4:0.0] Centralised vs decentralised | One central authority, top-down vs decisions shared with lower governments / private units within a broad policy
  [4:0.1] Financial vs physical | Allocation in money terms vs in real terms (materials, manpower, output)
  [4:0.2] Direction vs inducement | Command (imperative) vs incentives guiding private units (indicative)
  [4:0.3] Democratic vs totalitarian | Through elected bodies and consent (India) vs state command

## SUB 5 | Plans – Thrust, Models and Financing
[5:0] TABLE head: Plan | Main thrust / model
  [5:0.0] First (from 1 April 1951) | Agriculture, irrigation, power; Harrod-Domar (growth = savings rate ÷ capital-output ratio); 3.6% achieved vs 2.1% target
  [5:0.1] Second (1956-61) | Rapid industrialisation, socialistic pattern – Feldman-Mahalanobis (heavy industry, public sector, import substitution; not export promotion); forex crisis cuts 1958
  [5:0.2] Third (1961-66) | Self-reliance, food self-sufficiency
  [5:0.3] Fourth (1969-74) | Target-group approach (SFDA, MFAL, DPAP); reduce dependence on foreign aid
  [5:0.4] Fifth (1974-79) | Self-reliance + raising consumption of the poor; direct attack on poverty; Tribal Sub-Plan
  [5:0.5] Sixth (1980-85) | Poverty alleviation; Minimum Needs Programme, IRDP
  [5:0.6] Ninth (1997-2002) | Seven Basic Minimum Services
  [5:0.7] Twelfth (2012-17) | Faster, sustainable, more inclusive growth – last
[5:1] - **Trickle-down** (First–Third Plans; Community Development Programme 1952) → target-group (Fourth) → direct attack (Fifth, Sixth).
[5:2] - **Wage-goods model:** **C.N. Vakil and P.R. Brahmananda** – alternative to Mahalanobis.
[5:3] - **Plan financing:** domestic budgetary resources, PSU surpluses, **market borrowing**, **deficit financing (borrowing from the RBI = money creation)**, foreign aid.
[5:4] - **Eighth Plan (1992-97):** largest source of public sector outlay = **government borrowings** (Rs 2,02,255 crore of Rs 4,34,100 crore, **46.6%**); deficit financing only Rs 20,000 crore.
[5:5] - **PURA** (Providing Urban Amenities in Rural Areas, 2003): idea of **A.P.J. Abdul Kalam**, announced by PM Vajpayee.
[5:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.6-24; Eighth Five Year Plan Vol.1 ch.4 (financing of public sector outlay, 1991-92 prices) via Economic Survey 2000-01 Appendix 2.11; app notes 3:8 sub 3, 4, 10
