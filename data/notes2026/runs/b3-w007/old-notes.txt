=== NOTES 3:7 | Economic Planning in India – Origins, Planning Commission, National Development Council
## SUB 0 | Planning Commission – Set-up, Status, Chairman
[7:0:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [7:0:0.0] Set up | 15 March 1950 (began work 28 March 1950), with the Budget of 1950-51
  [7:0:0.1] How | Cabinet (executive) resolution of the Government of India – "a resolution of the Government of India"
  [7:0:0.2] Recommended by | Advisory Planning Board (1946), chaired by K.C. Neogy – not the Congress National Planning Committee
  [7:0:0.3] Basis | In line with Article 39 (DPSP) objectives
  [7:0:0.4] Status | Non-constitutional, non-statutory, advisory body (Rocket: also "quasi-political")
  [7:0:0.5] Chairman | Prime Minister, ex officio; first – Jawaharlal Nehru (as first PM)
  [7:0:0.6] Model | Broadly the Soviet Gosplan
  [7:0:0.7] Replaced by | NITI Aayog, 1 January 2015 (also a Cabinet resolution); last Plan = Twelfth (2012-17)
[7:0:1] - Body set up after Independence to prepare a **common plan** for the country = **Planning Commission**.
[7:0:2] - **Five-Year Plan idea in India: Jawaharlal Nehru** (as PM and first Chairman); planned development began with the **First Plan from 1 April 1951**.
[7:0:3] - **Functions:** assess material, capital and human resources; **formulate** plans; fix stages and priorities; determine resources; **appraise progress**; recommend on policy and administration; **perspective planning**.
[7:0:4] - **Execution is NOT its function** – plans were executed by ministries and States.
[7:0:5] - No mention in the Constitution; **no fixed tenure or qualifications** for the Deputy Chairman and members.
[7:0:6] - Was the **nodal agency for poverty estimation** at national and state level (now NITI Aayog, its successor).
[7:0:7] - **UIDAI** (Unique Identification Authority, 2009) began as an **attached office of the Planning Commission**; statutory since the Aadhaar Act 2016, under **MeitY**.
[7:0:8] - **National Planning Council (1965):** expert advisory body attached to the Commission.
[7:0:9] > 1950 = Planning Commission; 1952 = NDC; 2015 = NITI Aayog. "Advisory body" is the keyed status.
[7:0:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.4-5; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.28; Rocket Indian Economy #23 p.33; app notes 3:7 sub 1, 3:8 sub 1
## SUB 1 | National Development Council (NDC)
[7:1:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [7:1:0.0] Set up | 6 August 1952 – executive resolution, on the recommendation of the draft outline of the First Plan
  [7:1:0.1] Status | Neither constitutional nor statutory; extra-constitutional; advice not binding; to meet at least twice a year
  [7:1:0.2] Chairman | Prime Minister (presides over meetings)
  [7:1:0.3] Members | PM, all Union Cabinet Ministers (since 1967), Chief Ministers of all States, UT administrators, members of the Planning Commission
  [7:1:0.4] Secretary | Secretary of the Planning Commission
  [7:1:0.5] NOT members | President, Vice-President, Governors, Chairman of the Finance Commission, Secretary of the Ministry of Planning and Programme Implementation
[7:1:1] - **Objectives:** **chief – secure cooperation of the States in executing the plan** (NextGen); strengthen and mobilise the nation's efforts and resources for the plan; promote common economic policies; ensure **balanced and rapid development** of all regions.
[7:1:2] - **Functions:** prescribe **guidelines for the national plan, including assessment of resources**; consider and **approve the draft Five-Year Plan** – the **apex / final authority** that approves plans (can modify the draft).
[7:1:3] - **Sarkaria Commission:** constitutional status under **Article 263**, rename it "**National Economic and Development Council**".
[7:1:4] - Now inactive; its role passed to **NITI Aayog's Governing Council** (formal abolition pending).
[7:1:5] - **Chronology (UPPCS 2003):** India joins IMF (**Dec 1945**) → first devaluation (**Sept 1949**) → First Plan submitted to Parliament (**draft outline July 1951**) → NDC (**Aug 1952**).
[7:1:6] > NDC is NOT 1950 and NOT statutory; "guidelines incl. assessment of resources" = NDC, "assess resources and draft" = Planning Commission.
[7:1:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC 20 (NDC: composition, objectives); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.28, p.53 (NDC finally approves plans); app notes 3:7 sub 2
## SUB 2 | Origins – Pre-independence Plans
[7:2:0] TABLE head: Year | Effort | Person / idea
  [7:2:0.0] 1931 | Karachi Resolution (INC) | Economic freedom; state ownership / control of key industries
  [7:2:0.1] 1934 | Planned Economy for India | M. Visvesvaraya – first blueprint; ten-year plan to double national income; "Father of Indian Planning"; Bharat Ratna 1955
  [7:2:0.2] 1934 | FICCI | Called for national planning (Depression, New Deal, Soviet experiment)
  [7:2:0.3] 1938 | National Planning Committee (Congress) | Initiated by Subhas Chandra Bose (Haripura); chaired by Jawaharlal Nehru; final report 1949
  [7:2:0.4] 1944 | Bombay Plan | Eight industrialists (J.R.D. Tata, G.D. Birla, Kasturbhai Lalbhai, Purshottamdas Thakurdas, Ardeshir Dalal, John Mathai) – state-led industrialisation with protection
  [7:2:0.5] 1944 | Gandhian Plan | Shriman Narayan Agarwal (not Kripalani) – decentralised, self-contained villages
  [7:2:0.6] 1944 | Planning and Development Department | Ardeshir Dalal; abolished 1946
  [7:2:0.7] 1945 | People's Plan | M.N. Roy (Indian Federation of Labour) – Marxist; priority to agriculture
  [7:2:0.8] 1946 | Advisory Planning Board | K.C. Neogy – recommended the Planning Commission
  [7:2:0.9] 1947 | Economic Programme Committee | Jawaharlal Nehru
  [7:2:0.10] 1950 | Sarvodaya Plan | Jayaprakash Narayan (inspired by Gandhi and Vinoba)
[7:2:1] - **First Five-Year Plan in the world: USSR (1928, Stalin)**; India from 1951.
[7:2:2] - Indian planning drew on **Soviet planning** and the **Fabian socialism of Sidney and Beatrice Webb** (Nehru) – not Lenin's NEP.
[7:2:3] - **Mahatma Gandhi opposed centralised planned development** – self-reliance meant simple living, local resources and local production.
[7:2:4] - Supporters of planning: industrialists (Bombay Plan) and socialists; Gandhian and Sarvodaya plans = **decentralised alternative**.
[7:2:5] > Bose initiated, Nehru chaired the NPC; M.N. Roy = People's Plan, not a Bombay Plan signatory.

## SUB 3 | Which Body Did What – PC, NDC, Finance Commission, Finance Ministry
[7:3:0] TABLE head: Task | Body
  [7:3:0.0] Assess resources; prepare the draft plan | Planning Commission
  [7:3:0.1] Guidelines incl. assessment of resources; final approval of the plan | NDC
  [7:3:0.2] Plan becomes operative | Union Cabinet, then Parliament
  [7:3:0.3] Share of States in taxes; principles of distribution of net tax proceeds; grants | Finance Commission (Article 280, constitutional)
  [7:3:0.4] Suggestions on transfer and allocation of financial resources to Union and States | Finance Commission
  [7:3:0.5] Control of government expenditure; budgetary allocation | Finance Ministry
  [7:3:0.6] Allocation of central funds to States now (after the Planning Commission) | Union Finance Ministry (devolution on Finance Commission advice)
[7:3:1] - **Flow:** Commission drafts → **NDC approves** → Cabinet → Parliament. So the bodies "associated with planning" = **NDC and Parliament** (UPSC 2014), not the Finance Commission or the line ministries.
[7:3:2] TABLE head: Basis | Planning Commission | Finance Commission
  [7:3:2.0] Nature | Non-constitutional, permanent | Constitutional (Art. 280), periodic
  [7:3:2.1] Dealt with | Plan resources, planning | Tax devolution, grants (old non-Plan)
[7:3:3] - Finance Commission decides the **method and formula** for sharing tax proceeds between Centre and States.
[7:3:4] - **State plans:** State Planning Board (**CM as Chairman**, Vice-Chairman, 6-9 members) drafts; the **Chief Minister** sends the State draft plan to the Planning Commission.

## SUB 5 | Planning, the Constitution and Decentralised Planning
[7:5:0] - "**Economic and social planning**" = **Concurrent List (List III), Entry 20**, Seventh Schedule.
[7:5:1] - **Article 39** (DPSP) – source of plan objectives; **Article 40** – village panchayats, the village as the primary unit.
[7:5:2] - **Not mentioned in the Constitution:** **NDC, Planning Commission, Zonal Councils** (all three; Zonal Councils are **statutory** – States Reorganisation Act 1956); NITI Governing Council is not statutory.
[7:5:3] - **Bottom-up planning** is a goal not yet achieved; the village is accepted as a suitable unit (UPPSC 1998 key: A and R true, R explains A).
[7:5:4] TABLE head: Body | Article | Rule
  [7:5:4.0] District Planning Committee | 243ZD | Four-fifths elected from elected members of district panchayat and municipalities; chairperson sends the draft district plan to the State
  [7:5:4.1] Metropolitan Planning Committee | 243ZE | Metro areas of 10 lakh+; two-thirds elected
[7:5:5] - **74th Amendment (1992)** inserted **Part IXA** (municipalities), which holds the DPC and MPC.
[7:5:6] - **Balwantrai Mehta (1957):** planning to PRIs (accepted Jan 1958); **JP Narayan Committee (1961):** flagged centralisation; **Thungon (1988):** **Zila Parishad** the pivot of planning.
[7:5:7] - **SFDA, DPAP, ITDP, IADP** ran **outside the panchayats**; **73rd and 74th Amendments** restored local bodies' role.
[7:5:8] - Levels: Centre (Planning Commission → NITI Aayog) → State Planning Boards → DPC / MPC → Mandal plans, **GPDP**.
[7:5:9] - **AP:** State Planning Commission in the **Fifth Plan**; first district-level planning in the **Sixth Plan – Rs 1 crore to each of 23 districts**; Economic Regions – **Visakhapatnam 10 districts** (NITI-assisted, launched 13 Nov 2025), **Amaravati 9**, Tirupati 10.
[7:5:10] - **Kerala People's Plan Campaign:** 17 Aug 1996; 35-40% of plan outlay to local bodies.
[7:5:11] - **NITI State Support Mission** (2026) helps States set up **State Institutions for Transformation**.
[7:5:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.4 (JP Committee, 73rd/74th); Constitution of India (List III Entry 20; Arts 40, 243ZD, 243ZE); app notes 3:7 sub 4, 6, 7, 3:8 sub 9
## SUB 6 | Criticism and the Shift to NITI Aayog
[7:6:0] - Epithets: "**super cabinet**", "economic cabinet", "parallel cabinet", "**fifth wheel of the coach**" – for **centralising decisions** and eroding **State autonomy**.
[7:6:1] - **Nehru (1958)** admitted the Commission had become a kind of "super-cabinet"; the Advisory Planning Board's "**common plan**" idea bred lasting Centre-State tension.
[7:6:2] - **NITI Aayog:** think tank; **bottom-up**, **cooperative (and competitive) federalism**; **no financial powers** – not a fund allocator.
[7:6:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.5; Rocket Indian Economy #24 p.34; app notes 3:7 sub 5

=== NOTES 3:8 | Objectives of Indian Planning; Types of Planning (imperative, Indicative, Perspective, Rolling)
## SUB 0 | Rolling Plan (1978-80)
[8:0:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [8:0:0.0] Suggested by | Gunnar Myrdal, for backward countries – "Indian Economic Planning in its Broader Setting"
  [8:0:0.1] Introduced by | Janata Party government (ended the Fifth Plan a year early)
  [8:0:0.2] Deputy Chairman then | D.T. Lakdawala – credited with introducing it in India
  [8:0:0.3] Period | April 1978 – March 1980 (1978-80)
  [8:0:0.4] Name | Janata Sixth Plan (1978-83) = "the rolling plan" – the novel idea of the draft Sixth Plan
  [8:0:0.5] Outlay | Rs 12,177 crore; stressed employment
  [8:0:0.6] End | Janata fall → 1979-80 annual plan → Congress Sixth Plan from 1 April 1980
[8:0:1] # Three plans in one (all four book statements are correct)
[8:0:2] - 1. A plan for the **current year** (with the annual budget).
[8:0:3] - 2. A plan for a fixed number of years (**3, 4 or 5**), **revised every year** as the economy requires and extended by one year.
[8:0:4] - 3. A **perspective plan** of **10, 15 or 20 years**.
[8:0:5] - Rolling plan **changes both its allocations and its targets every year** → flexibility; criticism: yearly revision **weakened commitment** to targets.
[8:0:6] > Myrdal suggested – Janata introduced – Lakdawala Deputy Chairman – 1978-80. Not Indira, Rajiv or the National Front; Madhu Dandavate was Deputy Chairman only in 1990-91.
[8:0:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.16 (Rolling Plan 1978-80); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.38 (Myrdal; April 1978 – April 1980); app notes 3:8 sub 8
## SUB 1 | Objectives of Planning
[8:1:0] - **Four long-term objectives:** **growth, modernisation, self-reliance, social justice (equity)** – plus employment and removal of poverty; the long-term objective = **all** of high standard of living, social justice and self-reliance.
[8:1:1] - **Economic growth = principal objective**; the others are supporting.
[8:1:2] - Chief objectives also listed in the Plans: **reduce inequalities of income and wealth**, higher growth, **removal of poverty**, employment. "**To achieve social equality**" is not a stated economic objective of the Plans.
[8:1:3] - Major objective of the Five Year Plans (book key): **self-reliance** and less dependence on foreign sources.
[8:1:4] - Declared aim (1950 resolution): rapid rise in the **standard of living** through efficient use of resources, more production and **employment for all**.
[8:1:5] - **Planning** = a conscious effort of the State with a **set of goals**, a **set of means** and a **specific time period**.
[8:1:6] - Initial strategy of the 1950s: **rapid industrialisation** (Second Plan); cornerstone = **central economic planning**.
[8:1:7] # Socialistic pattern of society
[8:1:8] - Adopted at the **Avadi Congress (1955)** → **Second Plan (1956-61)** and **IPR 1956**.
[8:1:9] - Core element: **social gain, not private profit**, as the test of development (not communes or nationalising all firms).
[8:1:10] - Planning for socialism aims at a **national minimum standard of living** for all (NPC 1938, Bombay Plan).
[8:1:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.4-6; NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development ch.2 (goals of Five Year Plans); Second Five Year Plan ch.2 (socialist pattern – social gain, reduction of inequalities); app notes 3:8 sub 2
## SUB 2 | Perspective Planning and Plan Horizons
[8:2:0] TABLE head: Plan | Horizon
  [8:2:0.0] Annual plan | Short term – one year
  [8:2:0.1] Five Year Plan | Medium term
  [8:2:0.2] Perspective plan | Long term – 15 to 20 years or more; five-year and annual plans carved out of it
[8:2:1] - Perspective planning was a **Planning Commission function**.
[8:2:2] - **Long-term perspective plan 1985-2000** accompanied the **Seventh Plan**.
[8:2:3] - **AP now:** **Swarna Andhra Vision 2047** – GSDP **Rs 308.11 lakh crore by 2047-48**; ten "**Padi Sutralu**"; targets (10 Feb 2026): **15%** growth, GSDP **Rs 31 lakh crore**, PCI Rs 5.15 lakh by 2030.
[8:2:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.5, p.20 (15-year perspective 1985-2000); app notes 3:8 sub 7
[8:2:5] - **Plan horizon** = the **time frame** of a plan (5 years for a Five Year Plan, 15-20 years for a perspective plan) – not its size, outlay or growth target.
## SUB 3 | Plans – Thrust, Models and Financing
[8:3:0] TABLE head: Plan | Main thrust / model
  [8:3:0.0] First (from 1 April 1951) | Agriculture, irrigation, power; Harrod-Domar (growth = savings rate ÷ capital-output ratio); 3.6% achieved vs 2.1% target
  [8:3:0.1] Second (1956-61) | Rapid industrialisation, socialistic pattern – Feldman-Mahalanobis (heavy industry, public sector, import substitution; not export promotion); forex crisis cuts 1958
  [8:3:0.2] Third (1961-66) | Self-reliance, food self-sufficiency
  [8:3:0.3] Fourth (1969-74) | Target-group approach (SFDA, MFAL, DPAP); reduce dependence on foreign aid
  [8:3:0.4] Fifth (1974-79) | Self-reliance + raising consumption of the poor; direct attack on poverty; Tribal Sub-Plan
  [8:3:0.5] Sixth (1980-85) | Poverty alleviation; Minimum Needs Programme, IRDP
  [8:3:0.6] Ninth (1997-2002) | Seven Basic Minimum Services
  [8:3:0.7] Twelfth (2012-17) | Faster, sustainable, more inclusive growth – last
[8:3:1] - **Trickle-down** (First–Third Plans; Community Development Programme 1952) → target-group (Fourth) → direct attack (Fifth, Sixth).
[8:3:2] - **Wage-goods model:** **C.N. Vakil and P.R. Brahmananda** – alternative to Mahalanobis.
[8:3:3] - **Plan financing:** domestic budgetary resources, PSU surpluses, **market borrowing**, **deficit financing (borrowing from the RBI = money creation)**, foreign aid.
[8:3:4] - **Eighth Plan (1992-97):** largest source of public sector outlay = **government borrowings** (Rs 2,02,255 crore of Rs 4,34,100 crore, **46.6%**); deficit financing only Rs 20,000 crore.
[8:3:5] - **PURA** (Providing Urban Amenities in Rural Areas, 2003): idea of **A.P.J. Abdul Kalam**, announced by PM Vajpayee.
[8:3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.6-24; Eighth Five Year Plan Vol.1 ch.4 (financing of public sector outlay, 1991-92 prices) via Economic Survey 2000-01 Appendix 2.11; app notes 3:8 sub 3, 4, 10
## SUB 4 | Imperative vs Indicative Planning
[8:4:0] TABLE head: Feature | Imperative (directive / target) | Indicative
  [8:4:0.0] Market mechanism | Entirely replaced by a command hierarchy | Plan improves the market
  [8:4:0.1] Ownership | State / collective | Mixed
  [8:4:0.2] Targets | Binding quotas, allocation, prices | Guidelines and signals
  [8:4:0.3] Examples | USSR (1928, Stalin), China | Mixed economies; France
  [8:4:0.4] India | Public sector in early Plans | From the Eighth Plan (1992-97) after 1991
[8:4:1] - A **Five-Year Plan** is an **indicative plan of action** reflecting the government's intent.
[8:4:2] - Soviet collectivisation (Stalin): collective farms = **Kolkhoz** (state farms = sovkhoz).
[8:4:3] - **Regional planning** first in the USA (1916+; Tennessee Valley).
[8:4:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.28, p.58 (imperative vs indicative); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.6; Britannica "kolkhoz"; app notes 3:8 sub 5
## SUB 5 | Other Types of Planning
[8:5:0] TABLE head: Pair | Distinction
  [8:5:0.0] Centralised vs decentralised | One central authority, top-down vs decisions shared with lower governments / private units within a broad policy
  [8:5:0.1] Financial vs physical | Allocation in money terms vs in real terms (materials, manpower, output)
  [8:5:0.2] Direction vs inducement | Command (imperative) vs incentives guiding private units (indicative)
  [8:5:0.3] Democratic vs totalitarian | Through elected bodies and consent (India) vs state command
[8:5:1] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.58; app notes 3:8 sub 6
[8:5:2] - Indian planning is an **open process** – drafts are published and debated by the public, the NDC and Parliament.

## SUB 2 | Mahalanobis Model
[9:2:0] # Strategy and sectors
[9:2:1] - **Heavy sector strategy** / **unbalanced growth**: priority to **heavy capital goods industries** (steel, machinery) – most public-sector investment went there.
[9:2:2] - Originally conceived by **G.A. Feldman** (USSR, **1928**); Mahalanobis (**1953**) developed it independently – so the **Feldman-Mahalanobis** model, based on the **Russian (Soviet)** model.
[9:2:3] - **Four sectors:** (1) capital goods, (2) factory consumer goods, (3) household / small-scale consumer goods, (4) services.
[9:2:4] - **Stalin's USSR** began five-year plans in **1928** – **imperative** planning; India follows **indicative** planning.
[9:2:5] TABLE head: Included / emphasised | Excluded / neglected
  [9:2:5.0] Industrialisation through heavy industries | Foreign trade – assumed a closed economy
  [9:2:5.1] Infrastructure, basic and key industries | Agriculture (not the priority)
  [9:2:5.2] Dominant public sector (socialistic pattern, IPR 1956), self-reliance, import substitution | Exports / export promotion
  [9:2:5.3] Investment from 7% to 11% of national income | Village and cottage industries were not top priority (left consumer goods to them)
[9:2:6] # Criticisms
[9:2:7] - **Neglect of agriculture and small-scale industries**, **over-emphasis on heavy industry**, **neglect of social objectives** (employment, equity) – food and capital shortages, inflation in the late 1950s.
[9:2:8] - The emphasis on **growth** itself was the plan's aim, **not** the criticism (APPSC G2 Mains 2017).
[9:2:9] # Second Plan outcome
[9:2:10] - Growth: target **4.5%**, achieved **4.27%** (most books; NextGen prints 4.21%).
[9:2:11] - Steel plants **Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela**; **Chittaranjan** – locomotives; **Perambur** – coaches; Atomic Energy Commission.
[9:2:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.8-9; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.46 (unbalanced growth); app notes 3:9 sub 3, 8
[9:2:13] - **Closed economy:** for nearly four decades after independence (till the 1991 reforms) India was a largely **closed**, inward-looking economy.
[9:2:14] - **Nehru-Mahalanobis strategy** also sought to turn **dependence on metropolitan capitalism** into **independent development** and to move from **semi-feudal agriculture to capitalist farming** – both are its components.
## SUB 3 | Harrod-Domar Model and Capital-Output Ratio
[9:3:0] - **g = s / k** (g = growth rate, s = **savings** rate, k = **capital-output ratio**): growth **rises with savings**, **falls with a higher COR** – so COR fixes growth **along with savings**.
[9:3:1] - **Capital-output ratio** = capital needed to raise output by **one unit** per annum.
[9:3:2] TABLE head: Basis | High COR | Low COR
  [9:3:2.0] Meaning | More capital per unit of output; investment gives less output | Less capital per unit of output
  [9:3:2.1] Capital productivity | Lower | Higher
  [9:3:2.2] Growth | Slower, even with high savings | Higher potential
[9:3:3] - ★ Low growth despite high savings and capital formation = **high capital-output ratio** (UPSC 1995, 2018).
[9:3:4] - ★ Slow per-capita income growth = **high COR + high population growth** (UPSC 1993: "1 and 2").
[9:3:5] # First Plan
[9:3:6] - Started **1 April 1951**; priority **agriculture**, irrigation and power; investment target 7% of national income.
[9:3:7] - Faced refugees, food shortage, inflation; succeeded mainly due to **good harvests in its last two years**.
[9:3:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.56 (high COR); NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy p.71 (COR); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.6-7; app notes 3:9 sub 2
[9:3:9] - **W.A. Lewis:** development means raising saving and investment from about **4-5% to 12-15%** of national income (Rostow's take-off needs investment above 10%).

## SUB 6 | Gandhian Economics and the Janata Strategy
[9:6:0] - Gandhian economy rests on **Trusteeship**: the wealthy act as trustees of wealth.
[9:6:1] - "**India's Economic Policy: The Gandhian Blue Print**" – **Charan Singh**; "**Gandhian Constitution for Free India**" – **Shriman Narayan Agarwal** (not M.N. Roy).
[9:6:2] - **Ram Rajya:** rule on moral authority and justice for the poorest; **not a Hindu state**.
[9:6:3] - **Janata alternative strategy (1977-80):** ended the Fifth Plan a year early (1978); **Rolling Plan 1978-80** (Gunnar **Myrdal's** concept); stressed **agriculture, rural development, employment, cottage and village industries, minimum needs** – **not** heavy industry, and **SEZs** (a 2000s idea) had no place in it.

## SUB 7 | Wage-goods Model
[9:7:0] - Authors: **C.N. Vakil and P.R. Brahmananda** – "**Planning for an Expanding Economy**" (**1956**).
[9:7:1] - Real constraint = supply of **wage goods** (food, essential consumer goods), **not capital goods**.
[9:7:2] - More wage goods → **disguised unemployed** put to productive work; stresses **employment**.
[9:7:3] - Priority: **agriculture and wage goods** vs Mahalanobis's heavy industry.

=== NOTES 3:11 | Overall Achievements and Failures of Planning in India; End of the Plan Era (2017)
## SUB 0 | Achievements, Failures and Critics of Planning
[11:0:0] TABLE head: Achievements | Failures
  [11:0:0.0] Rise in national income, savings, investment, per-capita income (Plans I-XI averaged ~4.5% growth vs ~1% before Independence) | High rural poverty; rising unemployment – root cause of poverty
  [11:0:0.1] Self-sufficiency in basic, capital and consumer-goods industries | Income inequality not reduced; widened after reforms
  [11:0:0.2] Foodgrain self-sufficiency – Green Revolution (1966-67), FCI (1965) | Land reforms poorly implemented; unequal land ownership persists
  [11:0:0.3] Infrastructure – transport, irrigation, power, telecom | Regional disparities persist (a failure, not an achievement)
  [11:0:0.4] Education, scientific manpower, IT, space; IITs | Neglect of agriculture, lop-sided (capital-intensive) employment strategy, excessive emphasis on PSUs
[11:0:1] # Drawbacks of the planning process
[11:0:2] - Drawbacks named by the books: **implementation failure** (the UN expert team called implementation and enforcement "the principal shortcomings"), a **wrong / absent financial strategy** (Ramesh Singh), **misplaced faith in investment** alone, centralisation, politicisation.
[11:0:3] - APPSC Group 2 Mains 2017 asked for "the chief drawback", and **"All the given answers"** (financial strategy, faith in investment, implementation failure) was keyed. When only one of these is offered, the key is **implementation failure**.
[11:0:4] # Origin and critics
[11:0:5] - The idea of planning came from **Soviet Russia** (USSR five-year plans from 1928).
[11:0:6] TABLE head: Book | Author
  [11:0:6.0] Planning and the Poor (1974) | B.S. Minhas (also asked as "Planning for the Poor")
  [11:0:6.1] India: Planning for Industrialization (1970) | Jagdish Bhagwati and Padma Desai
[11:0:7] - **Padma Desai**: critic of India's planned economy; **first Asian woman** to get a **PhD in Economics from Harvard (1960)**.
[11:0:8] > Distractors for Minhas: Y.K. Alagh, Jagdish Bhagwati, L.G. Patel, D.R. Gadgil, Charan Singh, Rudra Dutt, Manmohan Singh.
[11:0:9] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy 10th ed. ch.5 p.5.36-5.38 (criticism of planning; wrong financial strategy); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 03 p.3-5 (regional disparity, inequality); app notes 3:11 sub 3-4
[11:0:10] - **Per capita availability** of cloth, foodgrains and electricity **increased** over the planning period.
## SUB 1 | Planning After 1991 and the End of the Plan Era
[11:1:0] - After 1991 planning moved from **"directed" to "indicative"**: the plan shows **priority sectors**; the private sector invests.
[11:1:1] - Plans were kept after 1991 because they give a **long-term perspective** to a market economy; planning is **not a constitutional requirement**.
[11:1:2] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [11:1:2.0] Last plan | Twelfth (2012-17), ended 31 March 2017
  [11:1:2.1] Planning Commission | Scrapping decided August 2014 (Cabinet 13 Aug; PM's Independence Day speech 15 Aug); last Deputy Chairman M.S. Ahluwalia
  [11:1:2.2] Successor | NITI Aayog, 1 January 2015, by Union Cabinet resolution
  [11:1:2.3] NDC (1952) | Inactive; its work passed to the NITI Governing Council; formal abolition pending
[11:1:3] - Abolition ended **discretionary plan grants**; NITI has **no financial powers** – the Governing Council coordinates policy, it does not redistribute funds.
[11:1:4] # Documents that replaced the Five Year Plans
[11:1:5] TABLE head: Document | Period
  [11:1:5.0] 15-year Vision | Up to 2030
  [11:1:5.1] 7-year Strategy | 2017-18 to 2023-24
  [11:1:5.2] Three Year Action Agenda | 2017-18 to 2019-20 (released 1 Aug 2017)
[11:1:6] - Action Agenda on jobs: "unemployment is the lesser of India's problems; the more serious problem is **severe underemployment**" (one job done by two or more workers).
[11:1:7] - **11th Governing Council** (11 June 2026): PM asked States to estimate **district-level GDP**.

## SUB 2 | Plan Record – First Plan, Third Plan Failure, Plan Holiday, Fourth Plan
[11:2:0] - **First Plan** launched **1 April 1951**; focus **agriculture and irrigation**.
[11:2:1] - **Third Plan** grew only **2.2%** vs **5.6%** target – **Sino-Indian War (1962)**, **Indo-Pak War (1965)**, **1965-66 droughts**.
[11:2:2] - **Plan Holiday 1966-69** (three Annual Plans) followed the **failure of the Third Plan**.
[11:2:3] - **Fourth Plan (1969-74)**, **Gadgil strategy**: **growth with stability** + progress towards **self-reliance**; targets agriculture **5%** a year, exports **~7%** a year; achieved **3.3%** vs **5.7%** (droughts, 1971 war); **14 banks nationalised (1969)**.

## SUB 2 | Approach, Objectives and Functions
[12:2:0] - Guiding principle: **Cooperative federalism** (not competitive, collateral or compulsive) – also called **"marble-cake" federalism** (shared roles, vs layered "layer-cake").
[12:2:1] - **Bottom-up** approach (vs Planning Commission's **top-down, one-size-fits-all**); bottom-up and top-down are strategies of **information processing**.
[12:2:2] - State rankings (SDG India Index etc.) → **competitive federalism**; competition among States and cities = **competitive sub-federalism**.
[12:2:3] # Objectives in the Cabinet resolution
[12:2:4] - **Shared vision** of national priorities with the States; structured support to States (strong States make a strong nation).
[12:2:5] - **Credible plans at the village level, aggregated progressively at higher levels** of government.
[12:2:6] - **Antyodaya**, inclusive and participatory development; focus on **small enterprises** (not large ones); monitoring and evaluation; knowledge and innovation hub.
[12:2:7] - **Four mandate areas:** knowledge and innovation; NITI for States; driving transformational change; monitoring and evaluation.
[12:2:8] # NOT a function
[12:2:9] - **Allocating central funds to States** / **finalising financial resource allocation** – that was the Planning Commission's role (now Finance Ministry / Finance Commission).
[12:2:10] > Assertion-Reason (APPSC G2 Mains 2025): A (non-constitutional advisory body) and R (village-level plans aggregated upwards) are **both true, but R does not explain A**.
[12:2:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 03 p.8-10; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #24 p.34 (cooperative federalism + bottom-up); NITI Aayog Cabinet Resolution 1 Jan 2015; app notes 3:12 sub 3
[12:2:12] - NITI's overall aim (as keyed): **sustainable development** – it coordinates the **SDGs** (SDG India Index) and fosters cooperative federalism.
