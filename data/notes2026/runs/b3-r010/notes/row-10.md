=== Plans I to V and the Plan Holiday – Objectives and Firsts
| Plan | Period | Model | Main objective / tagline |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | 1951-56 (launched **1 April 1951**) | Harrod-Domar | **Agriculture and irrigation** (with power, transport); food, refugee rehabilitation, price stability |
| Second | 1956-61 ("**Mahalanobis Plan**") | Nehru-Mahalanobis | **Rapid industrialisation – heavy and basic industries**; **socialistic pattern of society** |
| Third | 1961-66 ("Gadgil Yojana") | Sandy-Chakravarty | **Self-reliant and self-generating economy** ("make the economy independent"); self-sustaining growth; agriculture priority |
| Plan Holiday | 1 April 1966 – 31 March 1969 | – | **Three Annual Plans**; food problem, Green Revolution (1966-67); agriculture and industry equal weight |
| Fourth | 1969-74 (finally launched **1969**) | Manne-Rudra (Gadgil strategy) | Twin objectives: **growth with stability** and **progressive achievement of self-reliance**; 14 banks nationalised 1969 |
| Fifth | 1974-79 (ended 1978) | Dhar (final draft by **D.P. Dhar**) | Twin objectives: **removal of poverty (Garibi Hatao)** and **self-reliance**; control of inflation; **zero net foreign aid** |
# First Plan (1951-56)
- **Draft outline** presented by the Planning Commission in **July 1951**; final plan December 1952.
- Prepared to face **Partition (refugees)**, **inflation** and **food shortage**; priority **agriculture and irrigation** – about **one-third** of outlay (highest agriculture share of any plan); **Bhakra Nangal, Hirakud**.
- **Land reform:** the First Plan first proposed **co-operative village management** (village land managed co-operatively) – not the Second Plan.
- Succeeded mainly due to **good harvests** in its last two years.
# Second Plan (1956-61)
- **Mahalanobis** two- and four-sector model; **heavy industry-led development**; **maximum emphasis on the industrial sector**; the **first major industrial plan**; primary responsibility of development shifted to the **public sector** ("public sector plan").
- From the Second Plan a **determined thrust towards substitution (import substitution) of basic and capital goods** industries.
- Goal of **socialistic pattern of society** (with IPR 1956).
- **Targets pruned** because of an **acute shortage of foreign exchange** (1957-58 crisis) and rising prices.
# Third, Fourth and Fifth Plans
- **Third:** self-reliant, self-generating economy – self-reliance / self-sustaining growth first made the objective; hit by **two wars (1962, 1965)** and the 1965-66 drought.
- **Fourth:** "growth with stability and progressive achievement of self-reliance"; **correcting the earlier trend of increased concentration of wealth and economic power**; **exports to grow about 7 per cent a year** for balance on foreign account; **Growth Centre approach** for regional disparities; target-group programmes.
- **Fifth:** **removal of poverty for the first time** the basic objective; "**Garibi Hatao**" – Indira Gandhi's slogan of the **1971 elections**; **anti-poverty programmes** and serious concern for **unemployment** start here; high priority to **bring inflation under control** and achieve stability.
- **Financial sector** was **not** first made an integral part of the plan in the Fifth (false statement, UPSC 2019).
> Twin objectives (APPSC): Fourth = growth with stability + progressive self-reliance; Fifth = poverty removal + self-reliance. Garibi Hatao = Fifth, not Fourth or Sixth.

# Sixth to Ninth
- **Sixth:** first plan to focus on **gender issues** and inter/intra-State inequalities.
- **Eighth:** shift from heavy industry to **infrastructure** (UPSC 2010); **generation of productive employment** = central element of its strategy; growth "on two legs" with a safety net.
- **Ninth:** agriculture growth **decelerated**; seven Basic Minimum Services; Women's Component Plan.
# Tenth
- **Monitorable targets** for 11 indicators, set for the Centre **and for each State** in consultation with the States (state-wise targets – unique feature).
- Agriculture the prime moving force; Governance as a factor of development.
# Eleventh and Twelfth
- **Eleventh:** top priority to **agriculture** (NDC Approach Paper); social sector first, education on top.
- **Twelfth:** growth strategy depends crucially on **productivity gains**; three scenarios – "Strong Inclusive Growth", "Insufficient Action", "**Policy Logjam**" (Scenario 3); households with electricity **56% (2001) → 67% (2011)**.
- **Twelfth targets:** Approach Paper **9%** GDP → NDC (Dec 2012) **8%**; agriculture **4%**; **manufacturing 10%**; **50 million** non-farm jobs; core aims – health, education, skills, PPP in infrastructure, fiscal correction (a "gene revolution" is **not** among them).
> Eleventh = faster and **more inclusive**; Twelfth adds **sustainable**; Ninth = growth with social justice; Eighth = human development.

=== Growth: Target vs Achieved, Outlays
| Plan | Target (%) | Achieved (%) |
|---|---|---|

- **Plans that beat targets:** **I, V, VI, VII, VIII**.
- **Eighth Plan:** growth **6.7-6.8%** against 5.6% – a higher-than-expected rate and the plan-period peak; annual growth averaged ~7.5% in its last three years (1994-97); agriculture 3.6%, industry 8.5%; per capita output 3.9%.
- **Ninth Plan:** services grew about **7.8%** a year; share of the public sector in GDCF fell from 44.6% (Sixth) to about **27.3%**.
- **Eleventh Plan:** agriculture **3.3%** (Approach Paper / mid-term estimate; final **3.6-3.7%**) against 4% target.
- **Fifth Plan outlay:** **industry and minerals ~25%** (Rs 10,201 cr of Rs 39,303 cr revised), power ~19%, agriculture + irrigation ~21%.
- **Eighth Plan State Plans:** States' own contribution at 1993-94 prices **(-) 4%** (negative balance from current revenues).
- **Highest GDP and per-capita growth:** **Eleventh Plan** (per capita GDP ~**7.6%** a year); Twelfth: GDP ~**6.7%**, per capita NNI ~**5.3%**.
- **1950-51:** ~3.6%; lowest year since 1961 (excluding Covid) **1979, −5.2%**; 2020-21 −5.8%; 1990-91 1.1%; 2002-03 ~4%, 2003-04 **8.2%**.
- **Highest average period:** **2004-05 to 2012-13**; plan-era average (I–XI) **~4.5%** vs ~1% before Independence.

=== Plans Cut Short, Plan Holiday and the Rolling Plan
| Plan | Cause | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Third | **1962 and 1965 wars** + 1965-66 drought | **Plan Holiday (1966-69)** declared after the Third Plan |
| Fifth | **Emergency**, new elections, **Janata Party** elected; Janata government did not want to continue it | **Terminated a year early (1978)**; Rolling Plan (1978-80) |
| Eighth | 1990-91 political instability + BoP crisis | Postponed two years; Annual Plans 1990-92 |
- **Rolling Plan** = the Janata government's **Sixth Plan (1978-83)**, advocated by Gunnar Myrdal; emphasis on **employment**; ended 1980; Congress launched a new Sixth Plan. Not introduced in any of the Third, Fourth or Fifth Plans.
- Rolling Plan structure: a plan for **one year**, a plan for **3, 4 or 5 years** renewed every year, and a **perspective plan** for 10, 15 or 20 years.
- **Two wars in one plan:** the Third (1962 China, 1965 Pakistan).

=== Second Plan Outlay; Eleventh and Twelfth Plan Targets
- **Second Plan:** planned public outlay **Rs 4,800 crore** (private Rs 3,100 crore); actual **Rs 4,600 crore** (Rs 3,650 crore investment + Rs 950 crore current).
- **Why pruned:** food and capital shortages, **inflation**, closed-economy assumption, **1957-58 foreign-exchange crisis** (not falling inflation).
| Criticism of the Nehru-Mahalanobis strategy | Detail |
|---|---|
| Agriculture | Neglected → food shortages |
| Employment | **Capital-intensive** industry → few jobs |
| Prices | High inflation by the late 1950s |
# Eleventh vs Twelfth Plan
| Feature | Eleventh (2007-12) | Twelfth (2012-17) |
|---|---|---|
| GDP target | 9% (10% by end) | 9% → 8% |
| Agriculture | 4% | 4% |
| Health | IMR 28; MMR 1 per 1,000 live births; TFR 2.1 | IMR 25 |
| Targets | **27** national monitorable (13 State-level) | **25** monitorable |
| Outlay | Rs 36,44,718 crore | **Rs 76,69,807 crore** |
- **Eleventh inclusive growth:** continued reform and a competitive private sector (**not** withdrawal of the private sector); agriculture, health, education, skills, infrastructure; **7 crore** new jobs; poverty ratio down **10 points**.
- **Twelfth:** fewer CSS with a **flexi-fund**; social-sector PPPs; plan/non-plan distinction reviewed by the **Rangarajan Committee**; largest allocation to **social services (~34.7%)**.
