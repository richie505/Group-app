=== CPSE Profiles, Ownership and Performance
# What is (and is not) a public enterprise
- **CPSE** = a company in which the Central Government or other CPSEs hold **51%+** equity; the **Public Enterprises Survey** (DPE, on the **Estimates Committee's** advice) covers CPSEs with **over 50% GoI equity** and leaves out departmental undertakings, banks and insurers.
- **Number of CPSEs:** **5** at the start of the First Plan (1951) → **244 by 1990** (end of the VII Plan) → **291 operating in 2024-25** (**226 profit-making, 63 loss-making**).
- **Central PSUs (UPSC 2008):** **Balmer Lawrie and Co.**, **Dredging Corporation of India** and **EdCIL (Educational Consultants of India Ltd)** – all three.
- **NOT public enterprises:** **TISCO / Tata Steel** (private, Jamshedpur 1907), **Essar Oil** (now Nayara Energy, private), **Indian Aluminium Co.** (private), Bharti Airtel, Renuka Sugars.
- **BSNL** = **100% Government-owned**; NTPC, BHEL and SBI have public shareholders.

|---|---|
| **ONGC** | Oil and Natural Gas **Commission set up 14 Aug 1956** (statutory 1959); traditionally the **top profit-maker** |
| **Oil India Ltd (OIL)** | **Oil exploration** and production (crude, gas), not refining or marketing |
| **NTPC** | **Largest power utility** in India |
| **NHPC** | **Electricity (hydropower) generation**; Navratna |

- **UPSC 2002:** NTPC largest power utility ✔; Ordnance Factories largest departmental undertaking ✔; ONGC does **not** make half of India's LPG and **IOC does not run all refineries** → **1 and 4**.
- **UPSC 2005 (wrong pair):** Integrated steel plant at **Jajpur – SAIL** is wrong (Jajpur/Kalinganagar plants are Tata and others); Jamnagar – Essar Power, Nabinagar – Indian Railways (with NTPC, BRBCL), Kayamkulam – NTPC are right.
# Profit and loss
- **Loss-makers:** **Hindustan Fertilizers and Chemicals Corporation** (chronic, 2004 exam); **BSNL** (2013-14, 2014-15 and still); ONGC, NTPC, IOC, NMDC, Coal India make profits.
- **Largest share of CPSE turnover:** **Petroleum (refinery and marketing)**.
- FY22-25: rising CPSE **dividends** lifted non-tax revenue; disinvestment receipts fell.
# Logos and industry bodies
- **Eye logo** = **Doordarshan** (not BHEL, SAIL or BSNL).
- **CII (Confederation of Indian Industry):** HQ **New Delhi**.
- **Quality Council of India (QCI):** set up **jointly by the Government and Indian industry** (ASSOCHAM, CII, FICCI); its **Chairman is appointed by the PM on industry's recommendation** – both true (UPSC 2017).

=== CPSE Categories – Maharatna, Navratna, Miniratna
| Category | Introduced | Key criteria | Invest without Govt approval |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Maharatna** | **2010** (Cabinet Dec 2009; w.e.f. 19 May 2010) | Already **Navratna**; **listed**; 3-year average **turnover > ₹25,000 cr**, **net worth > ₹15,000 cr**, **net profit > ₹5,000 cr**; global presence | **₹5,000 cr** per project |
| **Navratna** | **1997, first 9 CPSEs** | Miniratna-I + Schedule 'A'; Excellent/Very Good MoU in 3 of 5 years; **60+** marks on six parameters | **₹1,000 cr or 15% of net worth** |
| **Miniratna-I** | 1997 | Profit 3 years running; **pre-tax profit ₹30 cr+** in one year; positive net worth | **₹500 cr** or net worth |
| Miniratna-II | 1997 | Profit 3 years running; positive net worth | **₹300 cr** or 50% of net worth |
- All tiers: **no budgetary support or Government guarantee**; no default on Govt loans.
- Concept: **"Navratna" = selected Public Sector Enterprises** (not export units or manpower); basis **turnover, net worth, net profit**; purpose = **autonomy to compete globally**; granted by the **Department of Public Enterprises (DPE)**.
- Memory: **5,000 – 1,000 – 500 – 300** = Maharatna – Navratna – Miniratna-I – Miniratna-II.

=== Early Industry, Textiles and Major Industry Facts
# Industrial firsts
| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **1818** | **First cotton mill**, **Fort Gloster (Ghusuri) near Kolkata** – failed |
| **1854** | First successful mill, **Bombay** – Cowasjee Nanabhoy Davar (Bombay Spinning and Weaving Co.) |
| 1855 | First jute mill, **Rishra** (Bengal) |
| June 1906 | Munagala Raja's textile unit, Vijayawada – first in Madras Presidency |
| **1907** | **TISCO**, Jamshedpur (private) |
| 1932 | Tariff protection sets off the sugar industry |
| **3 April 1970** | **Managing agency system abolished** (Companies (Amendment) Act 1969) |
- Early **cotton mills** were mostly **Indian-owned**; **jute mills** mostly **foreign (British)-owned**.
- **Textile firms by founding year:** **Bombay Dyeing (1879) → Lakshmi Mills, Coimbatore (1910) → Raymond (1925) → Grasim (1947-48)** = B, A, D, C.
# Textiles
- **Cotton textiles = oldest large-scale industry**; **largest textile centre = Mumbai** (also Ahmedabad).
- **Textiles = largest agro-based industry**; sugar is next.
- **Largest employer after agriculture** (and the industry with most workers): **textiles** – about **4.5 crore direct**.
- **Largest cottage industry = handloom**; **powerlooms** make most cloth (mill, powerloom, handloom segments).
- **National Handloom Development Programme:** concessional credit, marketing assistance, block-level clusters; benefits include loan waivers, concessional loans, fairs, loom upgradation, training – **both statements true**.
# Sugar, cement, steel and others
- **Sugar:** largest agro-based after textiles; about 5 lakh direct workers – **most employment among iron & steel, cement, sugar and petroleum**; **dual pricing** (levy + free-sale sugar); **cooperatives most successful** (Maharashtra, Gujarat).
- **Sugar decontrol committee (2012):** **C. Rangarajan**.
- **Cement:** about **87%** in a few States (Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh …), **near limestone** (not ports); India is **2nd**, not 1st, in world output.
- **Steel:** **China > one-third** (about half) of world crude steel; India **2nd – 149.4 MT (2024)**; main imported input = **coking coal**.
- **Nepanagar (MP):** **newsprint** (NEPA Ltd).
- **HINDALCO, Renukoot:** sited for **abundant power** (Rihand hydel).
- **Engineering industry** = largest segment of industry, about **3% of GDP** (Economic Survey 2011-12).

=== Index of Industrial Production and Core Industries
# IIP
- Measures **short-term changes in the volume** of industrial production against a base period; **monthly**, by **NSO (MoSPI)** (earlier CSO).
- Sectors: **mining, manufacturing, electricity**. **NOT included: construction**, agriculture, services.
- **Base 2011-12 → 2022-23** (effective **1 June 2026**); basket **1,042 products** (old 809 items / 407 groups).
- New weights (GVA share 2022-23): manufacturing **76.1%**, mining **11.1%**, electricity and gas **10.9%**; new: gas, water supply, sewerage, waste management.
- Use-based (2011-12): **primary goods highest (~34%)**, **capital goods lowest (8.22%)**.
- **Highest growth** in the early 2000s: **manufacturing** (2002-03); in **2000-01** among cement, coal, electricity, steel: **steel**.
- FY 2023: capital goods and infrastructure goods grew fastest; FY 2024-25 IIP **4.0%**; **Jun 2026 IIP 7.3%** (23-month high).
- **Index of Services Production:** base 2024-25; first release **14 July 2026**.
# Core industries (ICI)
- **Core sector** in planning = **selected basic industries**; ICI compiled monthly by the **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT**.
- **Eight:** coal, crude oil, natural gas, **refinery products**, **fertilisers**, steel, **cement**, **electricity**. NOT core: **transport, chemicals, textiles**.
| Feature | 2004-05 base | 2011-12 base | 2022-23 base (July 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number | 8 | 8 | **9 – iron ore added** |
| Weight in IIP | **37.90%** | 40.27% | **32.88%** |
| Highest | **Electricity** | **Refinery products 28.04%** (electricity 19.85% 2nd) | **Electricity 30.93%** |
| Lowest | Fertilisers | Fertilisers 2.63% | Fertilisers 2.73% |
- Under the 2011-12 "new IIP", **refinery products** displaced electricity and steel as the dominant core industry.
- New order: Electricity 30.93 > Refinery 22.57 > Steel 17.58 > Crude oil 7.43 > Coal 5.59 > **Iron ore 4.90** > Cement 4.41 > Natural gas 3.84 > Fertilisers 2.73; counts **only raw coal**, uses **gross steel output**.
> Among coal, electricity, fertiliser and steel, **electricity** has the highest weight in every series.

=== Growth Phases, Structure and Problems
| Phase | Character | Growth (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| 1951-65 | Public-sector-led heavy industrialisation | 7-8% |
| **1965-80** | **Industrial deceleration**: wars, droughts, **infrastructure bottlenecks**, licensing | about **4%** |
| 1980s | Recovery with partial liberalisation | 7-8% |
| 1991 onward | Liberalisation; services outgrow industry | – |
- **Second Plan (1956-61):** priority to **basic and heavy industries** (Nehru-Mahalanobis model).
- **IPR 1956** reserved **17** industries for the public sector; **1991** cut this to **8**.
- **Commanding heights:** public sector took **capital-heavy, long-gestation** industries; early base – Hindustan Aircraft, **Sindri Fertilizers, Chittaranjan Locomotives, Integral Coach Factory, HMT**, ITI, Hindustan Cables.
- **Premature deindustrialisation (Dani Rodrik):** manufacturing share peaks at a lower income level.
# Since 1991
- **Services, not industry,** gained the largest GDP share; **many PSEs disinvested/privatised**; **IT-software became a major growth contributor** → **only II and III** (APPSC).
- Industry about **25.3% of GVA**; manufacturing real share **17-18%** (nominal fell only on relative prices); public sector about **13-14% of GDP**; medium/high-tech manufacturing **46.3%** (ES 2025-26).
# Problems
- **Capacity under-utilisation:** main cause = **deficient basic infrastructure** (power, transport, raw materials).
- **Regional concentration:** Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka.
- **Capital intensity** (reliance on machinery over labour): highest in manufacturing in **2017-18 = Madhya Pradesh**.
- **Jobless growth:** directly employed workers 61% → 47% (2011-2023); contract workers about **42%**.

=== Public Sector Administration and Reform – DPE, MoU, PSE Policy 2021
- **Department of Public Enterprises (DPE):** places CPSEs in **four schedules**, monitors **MoUs**, grants **Ratna status**, brings out the **Public Enterprises Survey**.
- **Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises** consisted of the **Department of Heavy Industry + Department of Public Enterprises** (CDS 2020); **since July 2021 DPE is under the Ministry of Finance** and the parent is now the **Ministry of Heavy Industries**.
- **PESB** (Public Enterprises Selection Board) selects **board-level executives** of CPSEs.
| Committee / body | Role |
|---|---|
| **Arjun Sengupta (1984)** | Autonomy → **MoU system** |
| **Rangarajan (1993)** | **Disinvestment** |
| **BRPSE (2004)** | Revival of sick CPSEs |
- **MoU:** annual targets for **all CPSEs, including subsidiaries**, through a High Power Committee.
- **New PSE Policy 2021:** four **strategic** sector groups (atomic energy, space, defence; transport, telecom; power, petroleum, coal, minerals; banking, insurance, finance) keep **1 to 4** PSEs; **non-strategic** PSEs privatised or closed.
- **MSMED (Amendment) Bill 2026:** every CPSE to settle MSME invoices on **TReDS**.
