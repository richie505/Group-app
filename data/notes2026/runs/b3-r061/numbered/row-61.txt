## SUB 0 | CPSE Profiles, Ownership and Performance
[0:0] # What is (and is not) a public enterprise
[0:1] - **CPSE** = a company in which the Central Government or other CPSEs hold **51%+** equity; the **Public Enterprises Survey** (DPE, on the **Estimates Committee's** advice) covers CPSEs with **over 50% GoI equity** and leaves out departmental undertakings, banks and insurers.
[0:2] - **Number of CPSEs:** **5** at the start of the First Plan (1951) → **244 by 1990** (end of the VII Plan) → **291 operating in 2024-25** (**226 profit-making, 63 loss-making**).
[0:3] - **Central PSUs (UPSC 2008):** **Balmer Lawrie and Co.**, **Dredging Corporation of India** and **EdCIL (Educational Consultants of India Ltd)** – all three.
[0:4] - **NOT public enterprises:** **TISCO / Tata Steel** (private, Jamshedpur 1907), **Essar Oil** (now Nayara Energy, private), **Indian Aluminium Co.** (private), Bharti Airtel, Renuka Sugars.
[0:5] - **BSNL** = **100% Government-owned**; NTPC, BHEL and SBI have public shareholders.

[3:4] # Textiles
[3:5] - **Cotton textiles = oldest large-scale industry**; **largest textile centre = Mumbai** (also Ahmedabad).
[3:6] - **Textiles = largest agro-based industry**; sugar is next.
[3:7] - **Largest employer after agriculture** (and the industry with most workers): **textiles** – about **4.5 crore direct**.
[3:8] - **Largest cottage industry = handloom**; **powerlooms** make most cloth (mill, powerloom, handloom segments).
[3:9] - **National Handloom Development Programme:** concessional credit, marketing assistance, block-level clusters; benefits include loan waivers, concessional loans, fairs, loom upgradation, training – **both statements true**.
[3:10] # Sugar, cement, steel and others
[3:11] - **Sugar:** largest agro-based after textiles; about 5 lakh direct workers – **most employment among iron & steel, cement, sugar and petroleum**; **dual pricing** (levy + free-sale sugar); **cooperatives most successful** (Maharashtra, Gujarat).
[3:12] - **Sugar decontrol committee (2012):** **C. Rangarajan**.
[3:13] - **Cement:** about **87%** in a few States (Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh …), **near limestone** (not ports); India is **2nd**, not 1st, in world output.
[3:14] - **Steel:** **China > one-third** (about half) of world crude steel; India **2nd – 149.4 MT (2024)**; main imported input = **coking coal**.
[3:15] - **Nepanagar (MP):** **newsprint** (NEPA Ltd).
[3:16] - **HINDALCO, Renukoot:** sited for **abundant power** (Rihand hydel).

## SUB 4 | Index of Industrial Production and Core Industries
[4:0] # IIP
[4:1] - Measures **short-term changes in the volume** of industrial production against a base period; **monthly**, by **NSO (MoSPI)** (earlier CSO).
[4:2] - Sectors: **mining, manufacturing, electricity**. **NOT included: construction**, agriculture, services.
[4:3] - **Base 2011-12 → 2022-23** (effective **1 June 2026**); basket **1,042 products** (old 809 items / 407 groups).
[4:4] - New weights (GVA share 2022-23): manufacturing **76.1%**, mining **11.1%**, electricity and gas **10.9%**; new: gas, water supply, sewerage, waste management.
[4:5] - Use-based (2011-12): **primary goods highest (~34%)**, **capital goods lowest (8.22%)**.
[4:6] - **Highest growth** in the early 2000s: **manufacturing** (2002-03); in **2000-01** among cement, coal, electricity, steel: **steel**.
[4:7] - FY 2023: capital goods and infrastructure goods grew fastest; FY 2024-25 IIP **4.0%**; **Jun 2026 IIP 7.3%** (23-month high).
[4:8] - **Index of Services Production:** base 2024-25; first release **14 July 2026**.
[4:9] # Core industries (ICI)
[4:10] - **Core sector** in planning = **selected basic industries**; ICI compiled monthly by the **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT**.
[4:11] - **Eight:** coal, crude oil, natural gas, **refinery products**, **fertilisers**, steel, **cement**, **electricity**. NOT core: **transport, chemicals, textiles**.
[4:12] TABLE head: Feature | 2004-05 base | 2011-12 base | 2022-23 base (July 2026)
  [4:12.0] Number | 8 | 8 | 9 – iron ore added
  [4:12.1] Weight in IIP | 37.90% | 40.27% | 32.88%
  [4:12.2] Highest | Electricity | Refinery products 28.04% (electricity 19.85% 2nd) | Electricity 30.93%
  [4:12.3] Lowest | Fertilisers | Fertilisers 2.63% | Fertilisers 2.73%
[4:13] - Under the 2011-12 "new IIP", **refinery products** displaced electricity and steel as the dominant core industry.
[4:14] - New order: Electricity 30.93 > Refinery 22.57 > Steel 17.58 > Crude oil 7.43 > Coal 5.59 > **Iron ore 4.90** > Cement 4.41 > Natural gas 3.84 > Fertilisers 2.73; counts **only raw coal**, uses **gross steel output**.
[4:15] > Among coal, electricity, fertiliser and steel, **electricity** has the highest weight in every series.

## SUB 5 | Growth Phases, Structure and Problems
[5:0] TABLE head: Phase | Character | Growth (approx.)
  [5:0.0] 1951-65 | Public-sector-led heavy industrialisation | 7-8%
  [5:0.1] 1965-80 | Industrial deceleration: wars, droughts, infrastructure bottlenecks, licensing | about 4%
  [5:0.2] 1980s | Recovery with partial liberalisation | 7-8%
  [5:0.3] 1991 onward | Liberalisation; services outgrow industry | –
[5:1] - **Second Plan (1956-61):** priority to **basic and heavy industries** (Nehru-Mahalanobis model).
[5:2] - **IPR 1956** reserved **17** industries for the public sector; **1991** cut this to **8**.
[5:3] - **Commanding heights:** public sector took **capital-heavy, long-gestation** industries; early base – Hindustan Aircraft, **Sindri Fertilizers, Chittaranjan Locomotives, Integral Coach Factory, HMT**, ITI, Hindustan Cables.
[5:4] - **Premature deindustrialisation (Dani Rodrik):** manufacturing share peaks at a lower income level.
[5:5] # Since 1991
[5:6] - **Services, not industry,** gained the largest GDP share; **many PSEs disinvested/privatised**; **IT-software became a major growth contributor** → **only II and III** (APPSC).
[5:7] - Industry about **25.3% of GVA**; manufacturing real share **17-18%** (nominal fell only on relative prices); public sector about **13-14% of GDP**; medium/high-tech manufacturing **46.3%** (ES 2025-26).
[5:8] # Problems
[5:9] - **Capacity under-utilisation:** main cause = **deficient basic infrastructure** (power, transport, raw materials).
[5:10] - **Regional concentration:** Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka.
[5:11] - **Capital intensity** (reliance on machinery over labour): highest in manufacturing in **2017-18 = Madhya Pradesh**.
[5:12] - **Jobless growth:** directly employed workers 61% → 47% (2011-2023); contract workers about **42%**.

## SUB 6 | Public Sector Administration and Reform – DPE, MoU, PSE Policy 2021
[6:0] - **Department of Public Enterprises (DPE):** places CPSEs in **four schedules**, monitors **MoUs**, grants **Ratna status**, brings out the **Public Enterprises Survey**.
[6:1] - **Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises** consisted of the **Department of Heavy Industry + Department of Public Enterprises** (CDS 2020); **since July 2021 DPE is under the Ministry of Finance** and the parent is now the **Ministry of Heavy Industries**.
[6:2] - **PESB** (Public Enterprises Selection Board) selects **board-level executives** of CPSEs.
[6:3] TABLE head: Committee / body | Role
  [6:3.0] Arjun Sengupta (1984) | Autonomy → MoU system
  [6:3.1] Rangarajan (1993) | Disinvestment
  [6:3.2] BRPSE (2004) | Revival of sick CPSEs
[6:4] - **MoU:** annual targets for **all CPSEs, including subsidiaries**, through a High Power Committee.
[6:5] - **New PSE Policy 2021:** four **strategic** sector groups (atomic energy, space, defence; transport, telecom; power, petroleum, coal, minerals; banking, insurance, finance) keep **1 to 4** PSEs; **non-strategic** PSEs privatised or closed.
[6:6] - **MSMED (Amendment) Bill 2026:** every CPSE to settle MSME invoices on **TReDS**.
