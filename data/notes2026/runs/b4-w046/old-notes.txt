## SUB 2 | Regions, Physiography and Rocks
[46:2:0] TABLE head: Region (2022 set of 26) | Districts
  [46:2:0.0] Uttarandhra ("Kalinga Andhra", 6) | ASR, Anakapalli, Parvathipuram Manyam, Visakhapatnam, Vizianagaram, Srikakulam
  [46:2:0.1] Rest of Coastal Andhra (12) | Kakinada, Konaseema, East and West Godavari, Eluru, NTR, Krishna, Guntur, Palnadu, Bapatla, Prakasam, SPSR Nellore
  [46:2:0.2] Rayalaseema (8) – inland south/south-west | Kurnool, Nandyal, Anantapur, Sri Sathya Sai, YSR Kadapa, Annamayya, Tirupati, Chittoor
[46:2:1] - Coastal Andhra: deltas, most farm output, highest population density. Rayalaseema: Deccan plateau, drought-prone, very low density; borders TN, Karnataka, Telangana. Palnadu, Bapatla and NTR are coastal.
[46:2:2] - **Physiographic divisions:** coastal plains (~950 km long, "Rice Bowl"), Eastern Ghats hill region, western (Rayalaseema) plateau.
[46:2:3] - Western (Rayalaseema) plateau lies mostly at **150–600 m**; Mahendragiri belongs to the Eastern Ghats hill region (Srikakulam), not this plateau.
[46:2:4] - **Rayalaseema plateau** is made of **gneissic** (granite-gneiss) Archaean rock; red soils.
[46:2:5] - **Oldest rocks:** Archaean **Dharwar** rocks (>2,500 million years) of Anantapur, Chittoor, Kadapa – e.g. the schist belt round **Kuppam**.
[46:2:6] TABLE head: Landmark | Fact
  [46:2:6.0] Borra Caves | Ananthagiri hills, ASR; limestone karst; Gosthani river; found by William King, 1807
  [46:2:6.1] Belum Caves | Nandyal (earlier Kurnool); 2nd-longest cave of the subcontinent
  [46:2:6.2] Billa Surgam | Kurnool, near Betamcherla; prehistoric
  [46:2:6.3] Gandikota | YSR Kadapa – Penna gorge, "Grand Canyon of India"
[46:2:7] - AP called the **"Kohinoor of India"** (Kohinoor diamond from Kollur, Krishna valley). Firsts: first linguistic State; Asia's first rubber dam (**Janjavathi**, Nagavali tributary); first private gold mine **Jonnagiri** (Kurnool).
[46:2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP Geography 01 p.13-14; Rocket AP Geography p.2, p.53-54; NextGen AP History 07 p.10 (Kohinoor, Kollur); app notes 4:46 (subs 3, 4, 6, 9)
[46:2:9] - **Geography and history/economy:** Eastern Ghats – natural defence and a barrier to inland trade; **Krishna–Godavari deltas** – rice, agrarian wealth and dynasties; **Bay of Bengal coast** – maritime trade with South-East Asia; **Rayalaseema** – water scarcity, resilient folk culture; **Visakhapatnam port** – colonial trade and urban growth.
[46:2:10] - **Landlocked:** the **Rayalaseema** districts (e.g. **Kurnool**) have no coastline; Srikakulam and Nellore are coastal.

## SUB 2 | Eastern Ghats – Character, Sections, Rocks and Limits
[47:2:0] # Character
[47:2:1] - Lie between the **western side of the Coastal Plain** and the **eastern side of the Deccan Plateau** – the **major hill system of AP**; AP = eastern part of the **Deccan plateau** + a large part of the **Eastern Ghats**.
[47:2:2] - **Discontinuous, eroded, residual hills** – not a continuous range, not young fold or volcanic mountains, no snow; cut by the **Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri**; **older and lower** than the Western Ghats (the Sahyadri are the Western Ghats).
[47:2:3] - In AP the **Godavari and Krishna** split the Ghats into two sections:
[47:2:4] TABLE head: Section | Name
  [47:2:4.0] North AP | Eastern Ranges
  [47:2:4.1] South AP | Kadapa Ranges
[47:2:5] - Geologically the old residual hills form the **Eastern Ghats Mobile Belt**.
[47:2:6] - Rocks: **khondalite and charnockite** (Archaean); **khondalite hills = highest bauxite-bearing mountains** → AP's **bauxite** lies in the **Eastern Ghats** (erstwhile Visakhapatnam and East Godavari: Ananthagiri, Araku, Galikonda, Chintapalli belt).
[47:2:7] - Rivers rising in the Eastern Ghats: **Vamsadhara** (biggest), **Nagavali**, Sabari, Machkund (UPSC 2021: Nagavali and Vamsadhara – not Brahmani or Subarnarekha).
[47:2:8] # Limits beyond AP
[47:2:9] - The Eastern Ghats run from the **Mahanadi valley** (Odisha) **to the Nilgiris**, where they **join the Western Ghats**; Mahendragiri is only the northern start in AP.
[47:2:10] - Eastern Ghats hills outside AP: **Garhjat** (Odisha), **Javadi** (Vellore–Tiruvannamalai, TN), **Shevaroy** (Yercaud, **Salem, Tamil Nadu**), **Kalvarayan** (TN), Kolli Malai (TN), Biligirirangan (Karnataka). **Tirumala** hills are also Eastern Ghats.
[47:2:11] - **North → south:** **Nallamalai – Javadi – Nilgiri – Anaimalai** (UPSC 2005); Kaimur – Nallamalai – Seshachalam – Biligirirangan.
[47:2:12] - **Salher** (Maharashtra) is a **Western Ghats** peak, not Eastern Ghats.
[47:2:13] > Most asked: "discontinuous and eroded"; Eastern Ranges (north) / Kadapa Ranges (south); Mahanadi to Nilgiris.

## SUB 1 | Cyclone Mitigation – NCRMP and Shelters
[49:1:0] # National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project (NCRMP)
[49:1:1] - **World Bank**-assisted; run by **NDMA (MHA)**, project HQ **New Delhi**; launched **2011** (Phase I).
[49:1:2] - Aims: upgrade **forecasting, tracking and warning**, build capacity in **multi-hazard risk management**, and build **multipurpose cyclone shelters** and **embankments**.
[49:1:3] - **Phase I = Andhra Pradesh and Odisha** only; built **219 multipurpose cyclone shelters (MPCS)** in AP (Odisha 316).
[49:1:4] TABLE head: NCRMP category | States
  [49:1:4.0] Category I (higher vulnerability) | Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Odisha, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal
  [49:1:4.1] Category II (lower vulnerability) | Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Goa, Puducherry, Lakshadweep, Daman & Diu, A&N
[49:1:5] # Shelters and buildings
[49:1:6] - Coastal people are best saved by **building cyclone shelters**; in normal times MPCS serve as **community centres** (and schools).
[49:1:7] - **Roof:** a **pyramidal roof** (four slopes meeting at a point) best reduces wind force; a **hip roof** firmly anchored to the walls beats a **gable** roof; flat and gable roofs fare worst.
[49:1:8] - **NDMA Cyclone Guidelines 2008:** structural – shelters, raised platforms, cattle shelters, evacuation roads, **storm-surge barriers**; nature-based – **mangroves, bio-shields**, saline embankments, sand-dune restoration.

## SUB 3 | Drought-prone Areas and Drought Management
[49:3:0] # Drought-prone Rayalaseema
[49:3:1] - Rayalaseema lies in the **rain shadow** of the Western Ghats and the Karnataka–Telangana highlands → little SW-monsoon rain → depends on the **NE monsoon**; its failure causes **chronic drought** in Rayalaseema and southern Prakasam.
[49:3:2] - **Most frequently drought-affected district: Anantapur** (lowest rainfall); greatest rural distress in **Prakasam, Anantapur, Kurnool, Kadapa**.
[49:3:3] - Interior Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, AP and Tamil Nadu are **rain-shadow** areas.
[49:3:4] - Areas under **750 mm** a year = **chronically drought prone**; meteorological drought when seasonal rain < **75%** of normal.
[49:3:5] # Drought proofing
[49:3:6] - **Watersheds in arid areas** = prevention, i.e. stopping a hazard turning into a disaster (relief and assistance are response).
[49:3:7] - A watershed project that raises groundwater but leaves **yields flat** → the community **ignored crop diversification and soil management**.
[49:3:8] - **Ralegan Siddhi** (Maharashtra, Anna Hazare) overcame water crisis with **check dams and tanks**.
[49:3:9] - Drought prevention: **check dams, desilting village tanks, farm ponds**, **artificial recharge of groundwater**; **planting Eucalyptus is not** (water-hungry).
[49:3:10] - **WDC-PMKSY 2.0**: degraded and rain-fed land via **integrated natural-resource management**.
[49:3:11] - Projects: Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing (Kurnool, Jul 2026); 100-day water plan launched at **Yadiki (Anantapur)**, 6 Apr 2026; **Gandikota** reservoir on the **Pennar** (Telugu Ganga, YSR Kadapa).
[49:3:12] # Drought management framework
[49:3:13] - **NDMA Drought Guidelines 2010**; **Manual for Drought Management revised 2016** – **multi-indicator**; **mandatory: rainfall deviation + dry spells**; impact indicators: crop sowing, NDVI, soil moisture, hydrology.
[49:3:14] - **Crop Weather Watch Group** (**Ministry of Agriculture**) meets **weekly** in the monsoon; uses **meteorological, hydrological and agricultural** parameters – **not geological**.
[49:3:15] - Nodal ministry for drought, pest attack, hailstorm, frost: **Agriculture**.
[49:3:16] - **Swaraj Abhiyan v. Union of India (2016)**: SC ordered the drought manual updated and a **National Disaster Mitigation Fund**.
[49:3:17] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP Geography 02 p.6; Rocket AP Geography p.29, p.53; IMD rainfall report (2020) p.7; app notes 4:49 (sub 4, 5)
