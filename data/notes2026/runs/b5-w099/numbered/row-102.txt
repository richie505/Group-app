## SUB 0 | Noise Pollution – Units, Sound Levels and Sources
[0:0] # Units
[0:1] TABLE head: Quantity | Unit
  [0:1.0] Sound / noise level (intensity, loudness level) | Decibel (dB) – logarithmic; dB(A) is weighted to human hearing
  [0:1.1] Perceived loudness | Sone (phon = loudness level)
  [0:1.2] Frequency (pitch) | Hertz (Hz)
  [0:1.3] Hearing loss | Decibels (dB HL on an audiogram)
[0:2] - **Noise pollution is measured in decibels**, not Celsius, Daltons, cusecs, magnitude, nanometres or hertz.
[0:3] - **Every 10 dB rise ≈ doubles the loudness** (NextGen); WHO: indoor sound **below 30 dB** is safe.
[0:4] # Sound levels
[0:5] TABLE head: Sound / limit | Level
  [0:5.0] Rustling leaves | ~20 dB (Rocket)
  [0:5.1] WHO safe indoors | < 30 dB
  [0:5.2] Normal conversation | ~60 dB
  [0:5.3] Tolerable limit for humans (exam answer) | ~80 dB; 100–125 dB very uncomfortable
  [0:5.4] Prolonged exposure → hearing loss | > 75 dB (NextGen, Shankar); permanent damage commonly quoted > 85 dB
  [0:5.5] Heavy traffic | ~90 dB
  [0:5.6] Rocket engine | ~180 dB (Rocket)
[0:6] - Among heavy traffic, election meetings, rock music and **jet flight**, the **maximum noise** comes from **jet flight**.
[0:7] # Sources and effects
[0:8] - Noise pollution is **completely man-made**: construction, vehicle horns, industries, defence equipment, space rockets, domestic gadgets, loudspeakers, music concerts, **fire-crackers**.
[0:9] - Effects: annoyance, raised pulse and blood pressure, **constriction of blood vessels**, digestive spasms, **impaired night vision**, sleeplessness, loss of hearing; infrasonic/ultrasonic waves can damage buildings.
[0:10] > Exam angle: unit of noise / hearing loss = decibel (×8), sone vs decibel, rustling leaves 20 dB, tolerable 80 dB, maximum noise = jet flight.

## SUB 1 | Noise Rules 2000, Fire-cracker Standards and Noise Control
[1:0] # Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000
[1:1] - Framed under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**; noise is also an "air pollutant" under the Air Act, 1981; SC links freedom from noise to **Art. 21**.
[1:2] TABLE head: Zone | Day dB(A) Leq | Night dB(A) Leq
  [1:2.0] Industrial | 75 | 70
  [1:2.1] Commercial | 65 | 55
  [1:2.2] Residential | 55 | 45
  [1:2.3] Silence zone | 50 | 40
[1:3] - **Day = 6 am–10 pm; night = 10 pm–6 am.** **Loudspeakers / public address systems are banned 10 pm–6 am** except in closed premises (auditoria, banquet halls) – a rule of the **Noise Rules**, not the IPC or RP Act.
[1:4] - **Silence zone:** not less than **100 m** around hospitals, educational institutions, courts (and religious places so declared).
[1:5] - **NANMN** (Real Time National Ambient Noise Monitoring Network, CPCB, launched 2011): **70 stations in 7 metros** – Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Lucknow.
[1:6] # Fire-crackers
[1:7] - Noise standard (EP (Second Amendment) Rules 1999, amended **2006**): manufacture, sale or use of crackers exceeding **125 dB(AI) or 145 dB(C)pk at 4 m** from the point of bursting is prohibited; for series crackers the limit falls by 5 log10(N).
[1:8] - Supreme Court (2017–18): banned **heavy metals** (lead, mercury, arsenic, antimony, lithium) and **barium salts** in crackers; only **green crackers** (CSIR-NEERI) allowed; series crackers banned.
[1:9] # Control
[1:10] - At source (silencers, machine maintenance, loudspeaker limits), in the path (**barriers, green belts**, building design), at receiver (ear protection, job rotation).
[1:11] - **Green muffler:** **4–5 rows of trees/shrubs along roads** and near noisy places that **absorb noise** (also some gases and dust) – a **noise-pollution** control, not air, water or soil.
[1:12] - Trees also absorb noise, but "absorbing excess noise from small industries and irrigation" is **not** listed as a benefit of tree planting for poor communities (APPSC AEE 2023 key); shade, soil quality and income are.
[1:13] > Exam angle: residential day 55 dB, 10 pm–6 am loudspeaker ban (Noise Rules), cracker 125 dB(AI)/145 dB(C)pk at 4 m, green muffler = noise (×4).
[1:14] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment Part 7 p.2-3 (zone table, silence zone, NANMN, control); PMF Environment Part 4 p.2 (Noise Rules under EPA, Art. 21); NextGen Rocket Environment p.22, p.70 (Green Muffler – noise); Noise Standards for Fire-crackers (npcb.nagaland.gov.in PDF)
## SUB 2 | Radioactive Pollution – Sources, Effects and Units
[2:0] # Sources
[2:1] TABLE head: Source | Examples
  [2:1.0] Natural | Cosmic rays; radionuclides in the crust – radium-224, uranium-235/238, thorium-232, radon-222, potassium-40
  [2:1.1] Building materials | Thorium (exam answer; granite, monazite, fly-ash bricks also carry uranium and K-40); its decay gas radon collects indoors
  [2:1.2] Medical / isotopes | X-rays, carbon-14, iodine-125, phosphorus-32
  [2:1.3] Man-made nuclear | Reactor waste, nuclear fuel, mining and refining of uranium/thorium/plutonium, weapon tests (Sr-90, Cs-137, I-131); accidents – Chernobyl 1986 (Ukraine), Fukushima 2011
[2:2] - **Radon:** colourless, odourless radioactive **noble gas** from uranium/radium decay in rocks and soil; builds up in poorly ventilated houses; **major cause of lung cancer after smoking**; used in **cancer treatment**.
[2:3] - **Chernobyl** = **radioactive** pollution (reactor accident), not oil spill, acid rain or CO2.
[2:4] # Effects
[2:5] - Depend on intensity, rate of diffusion, exposure time and **half-life**.
[2:6] - Short range: burns, hair loss, impaired metabolism, changes in blood corpuscles, death of cells.
[2:7] - Long range: **genetic mutations (hereditary changes)**, **cancers (carcinogenesis)**, tumours, embryo changes; **Sr-90 → bone cancer**; I-131 → blood, bone marrow, thyroid.
[2:8] - Rocket: "Radioactive pollution → **hereditary changes + carcinogenesis**" (not soil-mineral imbalance or hindered blood circulation).
[2:9] - **Ionising** (X-rays, gamma, cosmic) vs **non-ionising** (UV, cell towers).
[2:10] # Units
[2:11] TABLE head: Quantity | Units
  [2:11.0] Activity | Becquerel, curie
  [2:11.1] Absorbed dose | Gray, rad
  [2:11.2] Biological (equivalent) dose | Sievert, rem
[2:12] > Exam angle: building material (thorium / radon), statement code on effects (UPPSC 2009), Chernobyl, units.

## SUB 3 | Radiological Emergencies – NDMA, MRDS, AERB and Tummalapalle
[3:0] - **MRDS = Mobile Radiation Detection Systems**: NDMA plan to give States/UTs MRDS for metros, State capitals and big cities to detect **unclaimed (orphan) radioactive material** (APPSC 2021, asked twice).
[3:1] - Precedent: **Mayapuri, Delhi (2010)** – scrap-market **cobalt-60** exposure.
[3:2] - **NDMA guidelines** on nuclear and radiological emergencies: **2009**; **District Collector** = Incident Commander off-site; NDRF CBRN teams respond.
[3:3] - **AERB** (1983, under the **Atomic Energy Act, 1962**): independent regulator; approves radioactive waste disposal. **DAE** = nodal technical agency with the **Crisis Management Group**; Environmental Survey Laboratories only monitor.
[3:4] - **AP: Tummalapalle** uranium mine and mill (**UCIL**), **YSR Kadapa** district (not Kurnool). Villagers blamed seepage from the **tailings pond** for uranium in groundwater; **APPCB show-cause notice to UCIL (Aug 2019)**; Centre/UCIL (BARC isotope study) say the uranium comes from the **natural ore body**.

## SUB 4 | Soil Pollution – Causes, Heavy Metals and Control
[4:0] - **Definition:** build-up of toxic compounds, chemicals, salts, **radioactive materials** or pathogens in soil that harms plants, animals and humans.
[4:1] - **Natural causes:** floods with contaminated water, **acid rain**, minerals from weathering of rocks. **Man-made:** industrial waste, agricultural runoff (**pesticides, fertilisers**), urban waste, landfill **leachate**, mining, e-waste, **coal ash**, oil spills.
[4:2] TABLE head: Source | Soil pollutants
  [4:2.0] Industrial waste | Lead, mercury, copper, zinc, cadmium, cyanides, chromates, acids, alkalis
  [4:2.1] Pesticides | Insecticides (DDT, aldrin), herbicides, fungicides, rodenticides
  [4:2.2] Fertilisers | NPK, urea – cadmium from phosphate fertilisers
  [4:2.3] Discarded materials | Concrete, plastics, glass, cans, carcasses
  [4:2.4] Radioactive wastes | Uranium, thorium, radium
[4:3] # Heavy metals
[4:4] TABLE head: Heavy metal | Main source | Disease
  [4:4.0] Mercury | Chlor-alkali effluent, fungicides | Minamata (biomagnifies as methyl mercury)
  [4:4.1] Cadmium | Phosphate fertilisers, batteries, mining | Itai-itai
  [4:4.2] Chromates | Tanneries | –
  [4:4.3] Copper, zinc, lead | Smelting, industrial waste | –
  [4:4.4] Lead (Pb) | Old petrol, paints, printing ink on newspapers | Brain and kidney damage
[4:5] - **DDT** is a POP under the **Stockholm Convention**; DDT and mercury **biomagnify**.
[4:6] - **Fly ash** must be used in bricks, cement, roads within **300 km** of thermal plants.
[4:7] - **Contaminated Sites Rules** (EPA 1986): test against **189 hazardous chemicals**; **Polluter Pays**; exempt radioactive waste, mining, marine oil spills, dumpsites. **EP Fund Rules:** Centre keeps **25%**.
[4:8] # Control
[4:9] - **Treat waste before dumping**, **minimise pesticides** and chemical fertilisers, **crop rotation**, biofertilisers, biopesticides, **IPM**, organic farming, the 4 Rs, afforestation, solid-waste treatment.
[4:10] - **Setting up watersheds is NOT a soil-pollution control** (it is soil and water conservation) – APPSC key.
[4:11] > Exam angle: NOT-a-control picker (watersheds), heavy-metal pairs, lead in newspapers.

## SUB 5 | Bioremediation and Remediation of Contaminated Soil
[5:0] - **Bioremediation:** microorganisms (bacteria, fungi) degrade contaminants into less toxic forms; its **primary application is soil contamination** (oil, pesticides, solvents, heavy metals); microbes can be **genetically engineered**; **heavy metals (Cd, Pb) cannot be fully removed** by it (Rocket "heavy-metal limitation").
[5:1] TABLE head: Type | Agent / method
  [5:1.0] Bioaugmentation | Adds pollutant-degrading microbes
  [5:1.1] Biostimulation / bioventing | Adds nutrients (or air) to boost native microbes
  [5:1.2] Biosparging | Air injected below the water table
  [5:1.3] Phytoremediation | Plants take up heavy metals (hyperaccumulators; water hyacinth)
  [5:1.4] Mycoremediation | Fungi (oyster mushroom, Pleurotus)
  [5:1.5] Landfarming, bioreactors, composting | Ex-situ methods
[5:2] - **Oil Zapper (and Oilivorous-S):** **TERI** bacterial consortium for oil spills and oily sludge – not a chemical dispersant.
[5:3] - **Heavy-metal removal from soil:** **immobilisation**, **soil washing**, **phytoremediation**; **keeping land fallow does NOT remove** heavy metals.
[5:4] - Alternatives to conventional control: **green technology, bioremediation, carbon capture and storage**; **incineration** is a conventional method, not an alternative.
[5:5] > Exam angle: primary application (soil), NOT-a-removal-method (fallow), types.
[5:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 13 p.2-4, p.6; NextGen Rocket Environment p.63, p.70; PMF Environment Part 4 p.58 (phytoremediation immobilises contaminants)
## SUB 6 | Marine Pollution – Microplastics, Oil Spills and GloLitter
[6:0] - Oceans are the **ultimate sink**: rivers, sewage, garbage, oil, grease, detergents, **radioactive wastes**, offshore oil mining, **oil spills**, plastics. Marine pollution harms **humans too** (seafood toxins, tourism, fisheries) and **plastic is not its only form** – so "only marine life" and "only plastic" are both false.
[6:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [6:1.0] Microplastics | Plastic particles smaller than 5 mm; microbeads in cosmetics
  [6:1.1] Ghost nets | Abandoned, lost or discarded fishing gear
  [6:1.2] Dead zones | Hypoxic waters from nutrient runoff (Gulf of Mexico)
  [6:1.3] Blue Flag | Beach eco-label of the Foundation for Environmental Education (Denmark); AP: Rushikonda, Visakhapatnam (2020)
[6:2] # Conventions and programmes

## SUB 7 | Thermal Pollution
[7:0] - **Definition:** heat becoming a pollutant – rise (or fall) in temperature of a natural water body by human activity; when heat energy is the pollutant it is **thermal pollution**.
[7:1] - **Main source:** **thermal and nuclear power plants** using water as **coolant** and returning hot water; so **harnessing nuclear energy often causes thermal pollution**. Also industries, deforestation of shorelines, release of cold reservoir water.
[7:2] - **Effects:** **warm water holds less dissolved oxygen**; higher metabolic rate, fish kills, algal blooms, loss of biodiversity.
[7:3] - **Control:** **cooling towers, cooling ponds**, cogeneration, retaining shoreline vegetation.
[7:4] > Exam angle: definition (flashcard), nuclear power → thermal pollution.
[7:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.9, p.11; Shankar Environment Part 6 p.17, p.19-20
## SUB 8 | Light Pollution and Dark Sky Places
[8:0] - **Light pollution:** excessive, misdirected artificial light that causes **skyglow**, hides stars and disturbs ecosystems and circadian rhythms.
[8:1] - **Dark sky place / reserve:** public or private land with an exceptional starry night, protected by responsible lighting; certified by **DarkSky International** (formerly International Dark-Sky Association), a **US-based non-profit** (Tucson).
[8:2] TABLE head: FIRST | Place
  [8:2.0] World's first Dark Sky Reserve | Mont-Mégantic, Quebec, Canada (2007) – not Russia
  [8:2.1] India's first Dark Sky Reserve | Hanle, Ladakh (2022), in Changthang WLS – not Himachal Pradesh
  [8:2.2] India's first Dark Sky Park | Pench (Maharashtra), 2024
[8:3] - APPSC G1 2023: statements I and III are correct, II and IV wrong; as no option offers "I and III only", the key is **"None of the above"**.
