=== NOTES 5:99 | Air Pollution – Sources, Pollutants, Naaqs, Aqi, Ncap, Stubble Burning and Acid Rain
## SUB 0 | Acid Rain – Causes, Chemistry, pH and Effects
[99:0:0] - **Definition:** precipitation (rain, snow, fog, mist) with **pH below 5.6**; normal rain ≈ 5.6 (carbonic acid from CO2). Term coined **1872** by **Robert Angus Smith** (Manchester coal burning).
[99:0:1] - **pH scale** = **acidity / basicity**; 0–14; **7 neutral**, below 7 acidic; each unit = **tenfold** change. **Human blood ≈ 7.4** (slightly basic).
[99:0:2] - **Causing gases: SO2 and NOx** (oxides of **sulphur and nitrogen**) – **not** CO, CO2, O3, CH4, CFCs, H2S, HCl or "oxides of hydrogen". Main single factor: **SO2**.
[99:0:3] - **Ozone** only speeds up the oxidation (photo-oxidant) – it is not a cause (UPSC 2022: **2 and 4 only**).
[99:0:4] - **Chemistry:** SO2 / NOx + water + O2 + photo-oxidants → **H2SO4 (~two-thirds)** + **HNO3 (~one-third)** → both **sulphuric and nitric acid** are components; acid falls from atmosphere to the ground.
[99:0:5] - Acid rain occurs due to the **reaction between clouds (water) and sulphur dioxide** (UPPCS 2001).
[99:0:6] TABLE head: Gas | Anthropogenic | Natural
  [99:0:6.0] SOx | Coal power plants, smelting of sulphide ores, H2SO4 plants, refineries, industries | Volcanoes, oceans, decay
  [99:0:6.1] NOx | Vehicles, power plants, biomass burning | Lightning, forest fires
[99:0:7] - **Deposition:** wet (rain, snow, fog) and **dry** – about **half** of atmospheric acidity returns dry.
[99:0:8] - **Effects:** acidifies lakes (fish die, aluminium released), damages forests and soils (leaches Ca, Mg), corrodes limestone/marble (**Taj Mahal "marble cancer"** – SO2, Mathura refinery), harms health (**respiratory ailments**).
[99:0:9] - **Taj Trapezium Zone** ~10,400 sq km (Agra, Fatehpur Sikri, Agra Fort; not Sanchi); **M.C. Mehta v. UoI (1996)** barred coal/coke.
[99:0:10] - **Transboundary:** Scandinavia receives acid rain from UK/central Europe; **India = largest SO2 emitter (2022)**.
[99:0:11] - **Control:** low-sulphur fuel, **FGD scrubbers** (trap SO2), low-NOx burners, catalytic converters; **liming** = remedial (not preventive).
[99:0:12] TABLE head: Instrument | Year | Focus
  [99:0:12.0] LRTAP (UNECE) | 1979 | First binding treaty on transboundary air pollution
  [99:0:12.1] Helsinki Protocol | 1985 | 30% sulphur cut
  [99:0:12.2] Sofia Protocol | 1988 | Freeze NOx
  [99:0:12.3] Gothenburg Protocol | 1999 | SO2, NOx, NH3, VOCs
[99:0:13] > Exam angle: ~17 PYQs; the answer is almost always "SO2 and NOx" / "pH below 5.6" / "sulphuric and nitric acid".
[99:0:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 14 p.4-5; NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.7; Rocket Environment p.21, p.63; Rocket Chemistry p.21; PMF Environment Part 04 p.11, p.13-14, p.48-49; app notes 5:99 sub 9-10
[99:0:15] - **Acid-rain countries (books):** **Norway** (most among China/Japan/Norway/USA) and **Canada** – classic transboundary receivers (UPPCS 2000, 2011); strictly East Asia/China now has heavy acid rain, Germany's Black Forest was also hit.
[99:0:16] - **Maximum acid in acid rain:** **sulphuric acid (H2SO4)**, then nitric acid; HCl and carbonic acid are minor.
[99:0:17] - **A-R (IAS 2003):** coal thermal plants cause acid rain through **SO2** (sulphur in coal); oxides of carbon are emitted too but do not explain it → **A and R true, R not the explanation**.
## SUB 1 | Carbon Monoxide and Other Pollutants – Health Effects
[99:1:0] # Carbon Monoxide (CO) – the most-asked pollutant
[99:1:1] - **Source:** **incomplete combustion** of carbon fuels – **vehicle exhaust** (main pollutant gas in exhaust), **cigarette smoke**, coal/wood burning. CO2 comes from **complete** combustion.
[99:1:2] - **Nature:** **colourless, odourless**, poisonous; "odourless poisonous gas in coal smoke" = CO.
[99:1:3] - **Effect:** binds **haemoglobin** → **carboxyhaemoglobin**; affinity **200–250 times** that of oxygen → cuts the blood's **oxygen-carrying capacity**; can affect the **bloodstream leading to death** → usually called the "**most dangerous**" air pollutant.
[99:1:4] - **Not** converted to CO2 in the body, does **not** destroy haemoglobin's structure, not mainly a digestive/liver/kidney poison (UPSC 2010, MPPSC 2012).
[99:1:5] - "Forms an **irreversible** complex with haemoglobin" (UPPCS 1996) – books answer **CO**; strictly the bond is reversible but very stable (CO is released slowly).
[99:1:6] - Flashcard: CO breathing is harmful because **it competes with oxygen in the blood**.
[99:1:7] TABLE head: Pollutant | Main source | Part / effect
  [99:1:7.0] Carbon monoxide | Incomplete combustion, vehicles, cigarettes | Blood stream – carboxyhaemoglobin, hypoxia, death
  [99:1:7.1] Asbestos dust | Asbestos mining, cement sheets | Lung – asbestosis
  [99:1:7.2] Lead | Leaded petrol (old), smelters, batteries, paints | Brain / nervous system; blood pressure, kidney
  [99:1:7.3] Mercury | Coal burning, industry | Stomach (UPPSC 2012 match key) / nervous system
  [99:1:7.4] SO2 | Coal/oil burning, power plants, smelting | Eye, throat and lung irritation, asthma; acid rain
  [99:1:7.5] NOx | High-temperature combustion – vehicles, power plants, lightning | Eye/airway irritation; smog, acid rain
  [99:1:7.6] H2S | Decaying vegetation and animal matter (chief), sewage, refineries | Rotten-egg smell; toxic
  [99:1:7.7] Fly ash | Burning coal in thermal power plants | Lung disease; captured by ESP
  [99:1:7.8] PM2.5 / PM10 | Combustion, road and construction dust, biomass | Heart/lung disease; PM2.5 enters blood, crosses placenta
  [99:1:7.9] Ground-level O3 | Secondary (photochemical) | Asthma, chest pain, crop damage
[99:1:8] - **UPPSC 2012 match:** Asbestos – Lung, Lead – Brain, Mercury – Stomach, CO – Blood stream → **3 1 2 4**.
[99:1:9] - **Lead** from **petrol** combustion (anti-knock **tetraethyl lead, TEL**) – the metal polluting city air with many vehicles; leaded petrol is **phased out in India (2000)**.
[99:1:10] - **Most injurious exhaust components:** **CO and lead** (old leaded-petrol era).
[99:1:11] - **Cigarette smoke:** **CO and benzene** (also nicotine, HCN, arsenic, formaldehyde).
[99:1:12] - **Inorganic gaseous pollutants:** HCN, H2S, CO, CO2, SO2, NOx (methane and phosgene are carbon compounds usually grouped as organic).
[99:1:13] - **Asthma** is caused / aggravated by **air pollution**; **rickets** is **not** caused by smog directly (smog cuts UV → less vitamin D; rickets is a deficiency disease).
[99:1:14] - **Bio-gas / gobar gas** = mainly **methane** (50–65%) + CO2 (35–50%), traces of H2, N2, H2S.
[99:1:15] - Gases: **ozone = O3**; **CFCs** deplete the stratospheric ozone layer.
[99:1:16] - Other links: **ammonia** (fertilisers + livestock, 77–80%) drives secondary particles; **ammonium sulphate** (from SO2 + NH3) ≈ one-third of India's PM2.5; **magnetite** particles (combustion, brake wear) found in brain tissue – possible Alzheimer's link; **cadmium** from metal smelting, PAHs from incomplete burning; PM2.5 crosses the placenta (AIIMS, 3 June 2026).
[99:1:17] - Burden: WHO ~**7 million** deaths a year; fossil-fuel air pollution ~**1.72 million** premature deaths in India (2022); NCR winter PM2.5 ~35 times the WHO limit; IIT Delhi: curbing power-plant SO2 could prevent ~1.24 lakh deaths a year.
[99:1:18] > Exam angle: ≈20 PYQs ask the same fact – CO + haemoglobin. Pick CO whenever the stem says "blood", "oxygen-carrying", "colourless gas from exhaust and cigarettes", "incomplete combustion" or "most dangerous".

## SUB 2 | Sources of Air Pollution – Natural, Vehicles, Industry
[99:2:0] TABLE head: Basis | Examples
  [99:2:0.0] Natural | Forest fires, volcanic eruptions (SOx, ash), dust storms, natural decay (methane, H2S), lightning (NOx), pollen, spores
  [99:2:0.1] Anthropogenic | Combustion of coal, petrol, diesel (basic source of air pollution), vehicles, industries, thermal plants, fireworks
  [99:2:0.2] Point / line / area / diffuse | Chimneys of industries / automobile exhaust along roads / industrial estates, mining / sprayed fertilisers and pesticides
[99:2:1] - **Main source in Indian cities: automobile (vehicular) emissions** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025). Main pollutants of metros (MPPSC 2018 key): **CO and SO2**.
[99:2:2] - **Composition of air:** N2 78%, O2 21%, CO2 0.04% – **N2, O2, helium are not pollutants**; CO, NO2, SO2, ground-level O3, lead dust, smoke are.
[99:2:3] - **CO2** is the usual "**not an air pollutant**" answer (greenhouse gas, quantitative pollutant; not in NAQI).
[99:2:4] - **Vehicles:** CO, NOx, unburnt hydrocarbons, PM, (old) lead. **Diesel engine:** much **CO at low temperature** (incomplete burning), much **NOx at high temperature**.
[99:2:5] - **Steel industry:** oxides of **sulphur, nitrogen, CO and CO2 – all four** (UPSC 2014).
[99:2:6] - **Largest SO2 source (US EPA, UPSC 2024): power plants using fossil fuels** (~73%), then other industry; not ships, locomotives or ore extraction.
[99:2:7] - **SO2 + fossil fuel:** SO2 is the air-pollutant gas released by burning fossil fuel (H2, N2, O2 are not).
[99:2:8] - **Delhi PM (IIT Kanpur study, 2016):** **road dust** (incl. construction dust) is the largest PM2.5/PM10 source annually (~38% PM2.5); in **winter** secondary particles, vehicles and biomass burning lead.
[99:2:9] - **Delhi winter peak:** A true, but "vehicle combustion increases in winter" is false – winter peaks come from **temperature inversion, calm winds, stubble smoke**.
[99:2:10] - **Stubble burning in Punjab** (and Haryana) contributes more to Delhi's air than Rajasthan or firewood use.
[99:2:11] - **Transport and technology:** more travel → more pollution; transport releases lead, NOx, CO (ozone forms from them – secondary); burning fuel harms health.
[99:2:12] - Blackened vessel bottom while cooking = **fuel not burning completely**.
[99:2:13] - **Sita Ashok** tree = prevention of dust pollution (UPPCS 2007).
[99:2:14] - **Eutrophication** = water pollution, not air (smog, acid rain, asbestosis are air-related).
[99:2:15] - **PM2.5 main source in India** (printed key): **vehicular emissions**.
[99:2:16] - **Silver oxide** is not a major primary air pollutant (SOx, CO, NOx are).
[99:2:17] - NCR: **transport** is the largest primary source, ~**23%** of the winter load (CAQM synthesis report, Jan 2026).
[99:2:18] - Fuels: **coal** most polluting; diesel gives more PM than petrol; CNG/LPG still release **CO2**; **LPG** burns without smoke; biogas mainly CO2 + traces of H2S; **hydrogen** gives only water (no CO, SO2, PM); wood and cow-dung give smoke, CO, PM.
[99:2:19] > Exam angle: "natural source" = forest fires/volcanoes; "urban source" = vehicles; "steel / crop residue releases which" = all of them.
[99:2:20] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 09 p.14; NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.1-3; Rocket Environment p.21, p.35; PMF Environment Part 04 p.3, p.9-10; Shankar Environment Part 06 p.5-6; IIT Kanpur study summary (governancenow.com)
## SUB 3 | AQI, NAAQS, WHO Guidelines and Monitoring Networks
[99:3:0] # National Air Quality Index (NAQI)
[99:3:1] - Launched **April 2015** under **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan** (MoEFCC/CPCB; developed with IIT Kanpur); first for **10 cities**; motto "**One Number – One Colour – One Description**".
[99:3:2] - **8 pollutants** (up to 24-hour averaging): **PM10, PM2.5, NO2, SO2, CO, O3, NH3, Pb**. **Not included: CO2, methane, benzene**. UPSC 2016: of CO2, CO, NO2, SO2, methane → **CO, NO2, SO2 (2, 3 and 4 only)**.
[99:3:3] TABLE head: AQI | Category | Colour
  [99:3:3.0] 0–50 | Good | Dark green
  [99:3:3.1] 51–100 | Satisfactory | Light green
  [99:3:3.2] 101–200 | Moderately polluted | Yellow
  [99:3:3.3] 201–300 | Poor | Orange
  [99:3:3.4] 301–400 | Very poor | Red
  [99:3:3.5] 401–500 | Severe | Maroon
[99:3:4] # NAAQS (CPCB, 2009)
[99:3:5] - **12 pollutants**: the 8 above + **benzene, benzo(a)pyrene, arsenic, nickel**; **ozone** added for the first time in 2009.
[99:3:6] TABLE head: Pollutant | NAAQS annual | NAAQS 24-hour | WHO AQG 2021
  [99:3:6.0] PM2.5 | 40 µg/m³ | 60 µg/m³ | 5 annual; 15 (24-hr)
  [99:3:6.1] PM10 | 60 µg/m³ | 100 µg/m³ | 15 annual; 45 (24-hr)
  [99:3:6.2] SO2 | 50 (20 in eco-sensitive areas) | 80 µg/m³ | 40 (24-hr)
  [99:3:6.3] NO2 | 40 µg/m³ | 80 µg/m³ | 10 annual
[99:3:7] - **WHO AQG 2021 (UPSC 2022 → 1 and 4 only):** ozone peaks in **sunny** weather, not inclement weather; **PM2.5** (not PM10) crosses the lung barrier into blood; **excess ozone triggers asthma**.
[99:3:8] - **RSPM:** CPCB treats RSPM as **PM10 (< 10 µm)**; old books/UPPCS 2017 key: "**less than 5 µm**".
[99:3:9] # Monitoring networks
[99:3:10] TABLE head: Network | Feature

  [99:3:10.5] Precipitation chemistry | IMD, under WMO GAW / BAPMoN
[99:3:11] - NAMP size: **1,646 stations in 603 cities** (March 2026).

## SUB 4 | Smog, Primary and Secondary Pollutants
[99:4:0] TABLE head: Type | Formed from | Conditions
  [99:4:0.0] Classical / London smog (sulphurous, 1952) | Smoke + fog + SO2 | Cold, humid; grey/black
  [99:4:0.1] Photochemical / Los Angeles / summer smog | NO2/NOx + hydrocarbons (VOCs) + sunlight → ozone, PAN (oxidising agents) | Warm, sunny, traffic; brownish – "brown air"
[99:4:1] - **Smog** = smoke + fog; essentially caused by **oxides of nitrogen and sulphur**; photochemical smog **always contains ozone**; ozone is **produced** during its formation (hydrocarbons, NOx are inputs, not products).
[99:4:2] - TSPSC 2023 pairs: classical smog = smoke, fog, SO2 ✔; photochemical smog = high concentration of **oxidising agents** ✔ → **both**.
[99:4:3] - **Primary** pollutants (emitted directly): CO, CO2, SO2, NOx, fly ash, lead. **Secondary** (formed in air): **O3, PAN, smog**, ammonium sulphate → "**not a primary pollutant**" = **ozone**; "secondary pollutant" = **smog**.
[99:4:4] - **Tropospheric ozone precursors:** NOx, CO, methane/VOCs – **water vapour is not** one.
[99:4:5] - Stratospheric ozone is **not** an air pollutant (shields UV); ground-level ozone is.
[99:4:6] - Smog effects: breathing problems, bronchitis; heavy smog cuts UV → less vitamin D (rickets rise) – but **rickets is not a smog disease** (SSC 2018).
[99:4:7] - **Asian Brown Cloud (UNEP, 2002):** layer of soot, black carbon, sulphate aerosols over **South Asia** (Indo-Gangetic plain) and the Indian Ocean, **January–March** (dry season), from biomass and fossil-fuel burning.
[99:4:8] > Exam angle: "brown air", "always contains", "produced during", "secondary" – answer ozone / photochemical smog.
[99:4:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 09 p.12-13; NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.3-4; Rocket Environment p.22, p.63; PMF Environment Part 04 p.8-11; Shankar Environment Part 06 p.5-7
## SUB 5 | Control Devices and Cleaner Fuels
[99:5:0] TABLE head: Device | Removes
  [99:5:0.0] Electrostatic precipitator (ESP) | Particulate matter (fly ash) from industrial smoke
  [99:5:0.1] Bag filter (baghouse) | Dust by fabric filtration
  [99:5:0.2] Cyclone separator / collector | Coarse particles by centrifugal force ("cyclone divider" is not a device)
  [99:5:0.3] Wet / venturi scrubber | Gases (SO2) and particles
  [99:5:0.4] FGD | Up to 95% SO2 from flue gas
[99:5:1] - **Catalytic converter:** **platinum, palladium, rhodium** catalysts turn CO → CO2, HC → CO2 + H2O, NOx → N2; needs **unleaded petrol**.
[99:5:2] - **Fuel efficiency (IAS 2000 key):** radial tyres, multipoint fuel injection, catalytic converter → **1, 3 and 4**; strictly a catalytic converter cuts emissions, and a streamlined body cuts drag.
[99:5:3] - Fuel injection promotes **complete combustion**.
[99:5:4] - **CNG = mainly methane (CH4)**; cleaner because it gives very little SOx/NOx, less pollution and fewer GHGs – all three true.
[99:5:5] - Fuels by pollution: coal > diesel > petrol > CNG/LPG; **hydrogen** cleanest (only water).
[99:5:6] - Other measures: BS-VI nationwide 1 April 2020 (BS-V skipped), PUC, RFID pollution charge on Delhi-bound trucks, FAME-II, SATAT (compressed biogas), E20 ethanol blending, Methanol Economy (NITI Aayog), vehicle scrappage policy (2021), OCEMS for red-category industries in Delhi-NCR, ban on pet coke and furnace oil.
[99:5:7] - BS-III from 1 April 2005 in NCR + 10 cities. **FGD** mandated 2015 for coal plants; only **39 of 537** had it when the mandate was relaxed (2025).
[99:5:8] - **WAYU** (Wind Augmentation PurifYing Unit) – CSIR-NEERI with IIT Bombay; roadside purifier installed in **Delhi** (ITO, Mukarba Chowk) in September 2018.
[99:5:9] > Exam angle: ESP = particulates; converter = Pt/Pd/Rh; CNG = methane.
[99:5:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment Part 04 p.34-40, p.63; Shankar Environment Part 06 p.8, p.11; NextGen Environment APPSC 12 p.2, p.7; Rocket Environment p.22; Rocket Chemistry p.35, p.68; app notes 5:99 sub 7
[99:5:11] - **Natural cleansing:** **rain** washes particles and soluble gases out of the air (wet deposition / scavenging), so it cleans a polluted atmosphere.
[99:5:12] - **Policy measures:** cheaper, faster, better-connected **public transport** and rules for **pooling private vehicles** cut vehicle emissions; sound-proofing hospitals controls noise, not air pollution.
## SUB 6 | National Clean Air Programme (NCAP), PRANA and Swachh Vayu Survekshan
[99:6:0] - **Launched 10 January 2019** by **MoEFCC** – PAN-India **time-bound national strategy** for prevention, control and abatement of air pollution at **regional and urban scales**, plus augmenting the monitoring network; also the answer to "GoI initiative to control industrial emissions" (APPSC G2 Mains 2025).
[99:6:1] - **Original target: 20–30% cut in PM10/PM2.5 by 2024**, base **2017** (so "by 2025" is wrong).
[99:6:2] - **Revised (September 2022): up to 40% PM10 reduction (or meeting NAAQS) by 2025-26** (the year asked = **2026**).
[99:6:3] - Coverage: **130 cities in 24 States/UTs** (non-attainment = failed NAAQS for five years; earlier 131/102); coordinated by **CPCB with SPCBs**; legal base **Air Act 1981**; City Action Plans; performance-linked grant ₹16,425 crore.
[99:6:4] - **PRANA** = Portal for Regulation of Air-pollution in Non-Attainment cities – **monitors NCAP implementation**; launched **7 September 2021**.
[99:6:5] - **Highest % PM10 cut, 2017-18 → 2023-24: Varanasi (230 → 73 µg/m³, 68%)**, then Bareilly (61%), Firozabad (59%) (MoEFCC/PIB).
[99:6:6] - **Swachh Vayu Survekshan**: annual city ranking under NCAP since 2022-23; awards on **Swachh Vayu Diwas, 7 September** (5th SVS awards, 7 Sep 2026). Indore first among million-plus cities in SVS 2023 and 2025; Surat in SVS 2024.
[99:6:7] - Results: 2024-25 vs 2017-18 – 103 cities cut PM10; only 22 of 130 met PM10 NAAQS. NGT (May 2026): 86% of spend went to road dust.
[99:6:8] - Programme match: MoEFCC – NCAP; MoES – SAFAR; Jal Shakti – Namami Gange (NRCP, NLCP are water programmes).
[99:6:9] > Exam angle: year = **2019**; revised target = **40% by 2026**; PRANA = monitoring NCAP.

## SUB 7 | Bioindicators and Indoor Air Pollution
[99:7:0] - **Lichens** (symbiotic **algae + fungi**) are the best **bioindicators of air pollution**, especially **SO2** – they **do not grow in polluted areas** (so "lichens grow well in polluted areas" is false). **Mosses** indicate heavy-metal deposition.
[99:7:1] - **Indoor pollution:** solid fuels (wood, cow-dung, coal), tobacco smoke, VOCs, radon, CO, mosquito coils; **maximum indoor chemical pollution: burning coal** (Jharkhand 2021); worst hit **women and children**; the four most serious pollutants in biofuel smoke: particulates, CO, polycyclic organic matter, formaldehyde.
[99:7:2] - Biological indoor pollutants: pollen, mites, pet hair, fungi → allergies, asthma.
[99:7:3] - Intercity buses: cabin air 2–3 times WHO limits (IntrCity study, 15 Mar 2026).
[99:7:4] > Exam angle: "bioindicator / indicator of SO2 / do not grow in polluted area" = lichen.

=== NOTES 5:100 | Bharat Stage Emission Norms – Bs-i to Bs-vi
## SUB 0 | Bharat Stage Timeline and Supreme Court Deadlines
[100:0:0] - **First vehicle emission norms:** **1991 (petrol), 1992 (diesel)** – before the Euro-based system.
[100:0:1] - **Mashelkar Committee → National Auto Fuel Policy (2003) → Euro norms** (roadmap for BS stages).
[100:0:2] - **Euro-based norms under the Bharat Stage name from 2000** (four-wheelers, APPSC Asst. Chemist); BS-I was also called **"India 2000"**.
[100:0:3] - **Euro I** came into force in Europe in **1992**; India generally lagged Europe by about **5 years**.
[100:0:4] - **Who notifies:** tailpipe standards notified under the **Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989** by **MoRTH**; CPCB/MoEFCC set the pollution side (Shankar credits CPCB under MoEFCC with implementation).
[100:0:5] TABLE head: Stage | Euro equivalent | First applied | Nationwide
  [100:0:5.0] BS-I ("India 2000") | Euro I | – | 2000
  [100:0:5.1] BS-II | Euro II | Delhi, 1 April 2000 | 2005
  [100:0:5.2] BS-III | Euro III | NCR + 10 cities incl. Hyderabad-Secunderabad, 1 April 2005 | 2010
  [100:0:5.3] BS-IV | Euro IV | 13 cities, 2010 | 1 April 2017
  [100:0:5.4] BS-V | Euro 5 | Skipped | –
  [100:0:5.5] BS-VI | Euro 6 | – | 1 April 2020
  [100:0:5.6] BS-VI Phase 2 | – | Real Driving Emissions (RDE) + OBD Stage II-A | 1 April 2023 (OBD II-B from 1 April 2025)
  [100:0:5.7] BS-VII | Euro 7 | Planned 2026-27 | –
[100:0:6] - **Supreme Court, 29 March 2017 (M.C. Mehta case):** **no sale or registration of BS-III (and below) vehicles from 1 April 2017**.
[100:0:7] - **Supreme Court, 2018:** **no sale or registration of BS-IV vehicles from 1 April 2020** (APPSC Polytechnic Lecturers).
[100:0:8] - **Leapfrog decision 2016:** BS-IV straight to BS-VI – urgent **Delhi-NCR** air pollution, close the gap with Europe in **one step**; BS-V is the only stage skipped.
[100:0:9] - **BS-VI statements (APPSC NTRUHS):** BS-IV and BS-VI are based on **Euro 4 and Euro 6**; **RDE introduced for the first time**; **On-Board Diagnostics (OBD) is mandatory** – "OBD not necessary" is the **INCORRECT** statement.

## SUB 1 | Measuring Water Pollution – DO, BOD, Coliform, Solids
[101:1:0] TABLE head: Parameter | Measures | Unpolluted water
  [101:1:0.0] DO | Oxygen dissolved in water | > 8–9 mg/L
  [101:1:0.1] BOD | Oxygen bacteria need to decompose organic matter | < 4 mg/L
  [101:1:0.2] COD | Oxygen to oxidise all organic matter (biodegradable + non-biodegradable) | < 5 mg/L
[101:1:1] - River pollution is measured by its **dissolved oxygen**: more pollution → **less DO**.
[101:1:2] - **BOD = standard criterion for pollution assay in aquatic ecosystems** (UPSC 2017), i.e. organic pollution in **water** – not soil or air. **High BOD = heavy organic/microbial pollution, e.g. sewage**; low BOD = good water.
[101:1:3] - **Coliform count** (MPN/100 ml) = **faecal contamination by human wastes** – the predominant human-body pollutant of the **Ganga**; higher total coliform = mixing of human faeces.
[101:1:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [101:1:4.0] Suspended solids | Retained on a filter – silt, clay (APPSC 2023)
  [101:1:4.1] Dissolved solids / TDS | Salts passing the filter (sodium salts, nitrate)
  [101:1:4.2] Turbidity | Cloudiness from suspended solids, measured in NTU
[101:1:5] - Water-changing agents: **effluents** alter physico-chemical characteristics (UPSC 1996). **Sulphur dioxide** is an **air** pollutant, not a water pollutant (zinc, copper, nickel are).
[101:1:6] - **Aquatic organisms respire faster** because **water holds far less dissolved oxygen than air**.
[101:1:7] > Asked ~15 times: what BOD/DO/coliform indicates; "dissolved oxygen" is the river-pollution answer.

## SUB 2 | Eutrophication and Algal Blooms
[101:2:0] - **Eutrophication = nutrient (N, P) enrichment of a water body** – the **natural ageing of a lake by nutrient (biological) enrichment** of its water; human-accelerated = **cultural eutrophication**.
[101:2:1] - Triggers: fertiliser runoff (**nitrates, phosphates**), sewage, detergents, aquaculture effluent – not idol immersion as such and not a fall in nitrogen inputs.
[101:2:2] - Chain: **enrichment → algal bloom → algae decay → decomposition uses up DO → hypoxia, fish kills**. So eutrophication **increases** algae and **decreases** DO (both statements true, UPPSC 2019/2020).
[101:2:3] - **Algal bloom:** excessive growth of planktonic (free-floating) algae – deteriorates water quality and causes **fish mortality**.
[101:2:4] - **Hypoxia:** reduction of oxygen in water (or air) threatening aerobic organisms → **dead zones**.
[101:2:5] - Stages: **oligotrophic → mesotrophic → eutrophic → hypereutrophic**.
[101:2:6] - **Harmful algal blooms in Indian seas** (UPSC 2011): nutrient discharge from **estuaries**, monsoon **land run-off** and **upwelling** – all three.
[101:2:7] - Blooms of pigmented **dinoflagellates** in estuaries = **red tides**.
[101:2:8] > Asked ~14 times: definition ("over-supply of nutrients", "natural ageing of a lake"), its effect on DO and algae.
[101:2:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.12-13; PMF Environment Part 04 p.73-74, p.77; Rocket Environment p.22; app notes 5:101 sub 3

## SUB 4 | Sources of Water Pollution and Sewage Treatment
[101:4:0] - **Point sources:** municipal and industrial discharge pipes; **non-point:** agricultural and urban runoff.
[101:4:1] - **Largest contributor to water pollution in India: untreated sewage**; only about **28%** of urban sewage is treated (2026).
[101:4:2] - Agricultural water polluters: **chemical fertilisers and excessive pesticides**.
[101:4:3] - **Sand mining** in river beds: raises **turbidity**, **pollutes groundwater** and **lowers the water table** (UPSC 2018) – it does not reduce salinity.
[101:4:4] - **AP's main water polluters:** Pharma City (Vizag), tanneries, paper mills, aquaculture.
[101:4:5] - **Dairy effluent** and ritual offerings can have BOD far above sewage.
[101:4:6] TABLE head: Technology | Function
  [101:4:6.0] Activated sludge | Conventional STP process; settled sludge is digested to biogas
  [101:4:6.1] SBR (sequencing batch reactor) | Treats in batches in one tank
  [101:4:6.2] MBR (membrane bioreactor) | Activated sludge + membrane filtration – wastewater treatment (UPSC 2024)
  [101:4:6.3] Ozonation | Ozone kills micro-organisms; leaves no residue (ozone is not radioactive, but that is not why it disinfects)
[101:4:7] - **Treat domestic sewage before release** – the healthy practice (burning plastics/leaves is not).
[101:4:8] - **Rain water** collected after 30–40 minutes of rain is clean but **deficient in minerals**.
[101:4:9] - **CETP** = shared plant for clusters of **small-scale industries**; **India's first CETP: Jeedimetla (near Hyderabad, then Andhra Pradesh), 1989**. AP runs **9 CETPs**; **Zero Liquid Discharge** example: **Tiruppur** (Tamil Nadu).
[101:4:10] - **HAM** for Namami Gange approved **2016**; first HAM STP **Haridwar**. **AMRUT 2.0: 20% reuse** of treated wastewater; thermal plants within **50 km** of an STP must use treated wastewater. BOD discharge limit relaxed **10 → 30 mg/L** (criticised: more organic pollution allowed).
[101:4:11] - **IISER Pune** (Jan 2023) reported a new material that removes pollutants from water.

=== NOTES 5:102 | Soil, Noise, Thermal, Marine and Radioactive Pollution
## SUB 0 | Noise Pollution – Units, Sound Levels and Sources
[102:0:0] # Units
[102:0:1] TABLE head: Quantity | Unit
  [102:0:1.0] Sound / noise level (intensity, loudness level) | Decibel (dB) – logarithmic; dB(A) is weighted to human hearing
  [102:0:1.1] Perceived loudness | Sone (phon = loudness level)
  [102:0:1.2] Frequency (pitch) | Hertz (Hz)
  [102:0:1.3] Hearing loss | Decibels (dB HL on an audiogram)
[102:0:2] - **Noise pollution is measured in decibels**, not Celsius, Daltons, cusecs, magnitude, nanometres or hertz.
[102:0:3] - **Every 10 dB rise ≈ doubles the loudness** (NextGen); WHO: indoor sound **below 30 dB** is safe.
[102:0:4] # Sound levels
[102:0:5] TABLE head: Sound / limit | Level
  [102:0:5.0] Rustling leaves | ~20 dB (Rocket)
  [102:0:5.1] WHO safe indoors | < 30 dB
  [102:0:5.2] Normal conversation | ~60 dB
  [102:0:5.3] Tolerable limit for humans (exam answer) | ~80 dB; 100–125 dB very uncomfortable
  [102:0:5.4] Prolonged exposure → hearing loss | > 75 dB (NextGen, Shankar); permanent damage commonly quoted > 85 dB
  [102:0:5.5] Heavy traffic | ~90 dB
  [102:0:5.6] Rocket engine | ~180 dB (Rocket)
[102:0:6] - Among heavy traffic, election meetings, rock music and **jet flight**, the **maximum noise** comes from **jet flight**.
[102:0:7] # Sources and effects
[102:0:8] - Noise pollution is **completely man-made**: construction, vehicle horns, industries, defence equipment, space rockets, domestic gadgets, loudspeakers, music concerts, **fire-crackers**.
[102:0:9] - Effects: annoyance, raised pulse and blood pressure, **constriction of blood vessels**, digestive spasms, **impaired night vision**, sleeplessness, loss of hearing; infrasonic/ultrasonic waves can damage buildings.
[102:0:10] > Exam angle: unit of noise / hearing loss = decibel (×8), sone vs decibel, rustling leaves 20 dB, tolerable 80 dB, maximum noise = jet flight.
[102:0:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 11 p.2-4; NextGen Rocket Environment p.22; Shankar Environment Part 7 p.2-3; PMF Environment Part 4 p.2
## SUB 1 | Noise Rules 2000, Fire-cracker Standards and Noise Control
[102:1:0] # Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000
[102:1:1] - Framed under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**; noise is also an "air pollutant" under the Air Act, 1981; SC links freedom from noise to **Art. 21**.
[102:1:2] TABLE head: Zone | Day dB(A) Leq | Night dB(A) Leq
  [102:1:2.0] Industrial | 75 | 70
  [102:1:2.1] Commercial | 65 | 55
  [102:1:2.2] Residential | 55 | 45
  [102:1:2.3] Silence zone | 50 | 40
[102:1:3] - **Day = 6 am–10 pm; night = 10 pm–6 am.** **Loudspeakers / public address systems are banned 10 pm–6 am** except in closed premises (auditoria, banquet halls) – a rule of the **Noise Rules**, not the IPC or RP Act.
[102:1:4] - **Silence zone:** not less than **100 m** around hospitals, educational institutions, courts (and religious places so declared).
[102:1:5] - **NANMN** (Real Time National Ambient Noise Monitoring Network, CPCB, launched 2011): **70 stations in 7 metros** – Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Lucknow.
[102:1:6] # Fire-crackers
[102:1:7] - Noise standard (EP (Second Amendment) Rules 1999, amended **2006**): manufacture, sale or use of crackers exceeding **125 dB(AI) or 145 dB(C)pk at 4 m** from the point of bursting is prohibited; for series crackers the limit falls by 5 log10(N).
[102:1:8] - Supreme Court (2017–18): banned **heavy metals** (lead, mercury, arsenic, antimony, lithium) and **barium salts** in crackers; only **green crackers** (CSIR-NEERI) allowed; series crackers banned.
[102:1:9] # Control
[102:1:10] - At source (silencers, machine maintenance, loudspeaker limits), in the path (**barriers, green belts**, building design), at receiver (ear protection, job rotation).
[102:1:11] - **Green muffler:** **4–5 rows of trees/shrubs along roads** and near noisy places that **absorb noise** (also some gases and dust) – a **noise-pollution** control, not air, water or soil.
[102:1:12] - Trees also absorb noise, but "absorbing excess noise from small industries and irrigation" is **not** listed as a benefit of tree planting for poor communities (APPSC AEE 2023 key); shade, soil quality and income are.
[102:1:13] > Exam angle: residential day 55 dB, 10 pm–6 am loudspeaker ban (Noise Rules), cracker 125 dB(AI)/145 dB(C)pk at 4 m, green muffler = noise (×4).
[102:1:14] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment Part 7 p.2-3 (zone table, silence zone, NANMN, control); PMF Environment Part 4 p.2 (Noise Rules under EPA, Art. 21); NextGen Rocket Environment p.22, p.70 (Green Muffler – noise); Noise Standards for Fire-crackers (npcb.nagaland.gov.in PDF)

## SUB 3 | Radioactive Pollution – Sources, Effects and Units
[102:3:0] # Sources
[102:3:1] TABLE head: Source | Examples
  [102:3:1.0] Natural | Cosmic rays; radionuclides in the crust – radium-224, uranium-235/238, thorium-232, radon-222, potassium-40
  [102:3:1.1] Building materials | Thorium (exam answer; granite, monazite, fly-ash bricks also carry uranium and K-40); its decay gas radon collects indoors
  [102:3:1.2] Medical / isotopes | X-rays, carbon-14, iodine-125, phosphorus-32
  [102:3:1.3] Man-made nuclear | Reactor waste, nuclear fuel, mining and refining of uranium/thorium/plutonium, weapon tests (Sr-90, Cs-137, I-131); accidents – Chernobyl 1986 (Ukraine), Fukushima 2011
[102:3:2] - **Radon:** colourless, odourless radioactive **noble gas** from uranium/radium decay in rocks and soil; builds up in poorly ventilated houses; **major cause of lung cancer after smoking**; used in **cancer treatment**.
[102:3:3] - **Chernobyl** = **radioactive** pollution (reactor accident), not oil spill, acid rain or CO2.
[102:3:4] # Effects
[102:3:5] - Depend on intensity, rate of diffusion, exposure time and **half-life**.
[102:3:6] - Short range: burns, hair loss, impaired metabolism, changes in blood corpuscles, death of cells.
[102:3:7] - Long range: **genetic mutations (hereditary changes)**, **cancers (carcinogenesis)**, tumours, embryo changes; **Sr-90 → bone cancer**; I-131 → blood, bone marrow, thyroid.
[102:3:8] - Rocket: "Radioactive pollution → **hereditary changes + carcinogenesis**" (not soil-mineral imbalance or hindered blood circulation).
[102:3:9] - **Ionising** (X-rays, gamma, cosmic) vs **non-ionising** (UV, cell towers).
[102:3:10] # Units
[102:3:11] TABLE head: Quantity | Units
  [102:3:11.0] Activity | Becquerel, curie
  [102:3:11.1] Absorbed dose | Gray, rad
  [102:3:11.2] Biological (equivalent) dose | Sievert, rem

## SUB 5 | Soil Pollution – Causes, Heavy Metals and Control
[102:5:0] - **Definition:** build-up of toxic compounds, chemicals, salts, **radioactive materials** or pathogens in soil that harms plants, animals and humans.
[102:5:1] - **Natural causes:** floods with contaminated water, **acid rain**, minerals from weathering of rocks. **Man-made:** industrial waste, agricultural runoff (**pesticides, fertilisers**), urban waste, landfill **leachate**, mining, e-waste, **coal ash**, oil spills.
[102:5:2] TABLE head: Source | Soil pollutants
  [102:5:2.0] Industrial waste | Lead, mercury, copper, zinc, cadmium, cyanides, chromates, acids, alkalis
  [102:5:2.1] Pesticides | Insecticides (DDT, aldrin), herbicides, fungicides, rodenticides
  [102:5:2.2] Fertilisers | NPK, urea – cadmium from phosphate fertilisers
  [102:5:2.3] Discarded materials | Concrete, plastics, glass, cans, carcasses
  [102:5:2.4] Radioactive wastes | Uranium, thorium, radium
[102:5:3] # Heavy metals
[102:5:4] TABLE head: Heavy metal | Main source | Disease
  [102:5:4.0] Mercury | Chlor-alkali effluent, fungicides | Minamata (biomagnifies as methyl mercury)
  [102:5:4.1] Cadmium | Phosphate fertilisers, batteries, mining | Itai-itai
  [102:5:4.2] Chromates | Tanneries | –
  [102:5:4.3] Copper, zinc, lead | Smelting, industrial waste | –
  [102:5:4.4] Lead (Pb) | Old petrol, paints, printing ink on newspapers | Brain and kidney damage
[102:5:5] - **DDT** is a POP under the **Stockholm Convention**; DDT and mercury **biomagnify**.
[102:5:6] - **Fly ash** must be used in bricks, cement, roads within **300 km** of thermal plants.
[102:5:7] - **Contaminated Sites Rules** (EPA 1986): test against **189 hazardous chemicals**; **Polluter Pays**; exempt radioactive waste, mining, marine oil spills, dumpsites. **EP Fund Rules:** Centre keeps **25%**.
[102:5:8] # Control
[102:5:9] - **Treat waste before dumping**, **minimise pesticides** and chemical fertilisers, **crop rotation**, biofertilisers, biopesticides, **IPM**, organic farming, the 4 Rs, afforestation, solid-waste treatment.
[102:5:10] - **Setting up watersheds is NOT a soil-pollution control** (it is soil and water conservation) – APPSC key.

## SUB 6 | Bioremediation and Remediation of Contaminated Soil
[102:6:0] - **Bioremediation:** microorganisms (bacteria, fungi) degrade contaminants into less toxic forms; its **primary application is soil contamination** (oil, pesticides, solvents, heavy metals); microbes can be **genetically engineered**; **heavy metals (Cd, Pb) cannot be fully removed** by it (Rocket "heavy-metal limitation").
[102:6:1] TABLE head: Type | Agent / method
  [102:6:1.0] Bioaugmentation | Adds pollutant-degrading microbes
  [102:6:1.1] Biostimulation / bioventing | Adds nutrients (or air) to boost native microbes
  [102:6:1.2] Biosparging | Air injected below the water table
  [102:6:1.3] Phytoremediation | Plants take up heavy metals (hyperaccumulators; water hyacinth)
  [102:6:1.4] Mycoremediation | Fungi (oyster mushroom, Pleurotus)
  [102:6:1.5] Landfarming, bioreactors, composting | Ex-situ methods
[102:6:2] - **Oil Zapper (and Oilivorous-S):** **TERI** bacterial consortium for oil spills and oily sludge – not a chemical dispersant.
[102:6:3] - **Heavy-metal removal from soil:** **immobilisation**, **soil washing**, **phytoremediation**; **keeping land fallow does NOT remove** heavy metals.
[102:6:4] - Alternatives to conventional control: **green technology, bioremediation, carbon capture and storage**; **incineration** is a conventional method, not an alternative.
[102:6:5] > Exam angle: primary application (soil), NOT-a-removal-method (fallow), types.

## SUB 7 | Thermal Pollution
[102:7:0] - **Definition:** heat becoming a pollutant – rise (or fall) in temperature of a natural water body by human activity; when heat energy is the pollutant it is **thermal pollution**.
[102:7:1] - **Main source:** **thermal and nuclear power plants** using water as **coolant** and returning hot water; so **harnessing nuclear energy often causes thermal pollution**. Also industries, deforestation of shorelines, release of cold reservoir water.
[102:7:2] - **Effects:** **warm water holds less dissolved oxygen**; higher metabolic rate, fish kills, algal blooms, loss of biodiversity.
[102:7:3] - **Control:** **cooling towers, cooling ponds**, cogeneration, retaining shoreline vegetation.
[102:7:4] > Exam angle: definition (flashcard), nuclear power → thermal pollution.
[102:7:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.9, p.11; Shankar Environment Part 6 p.17, p.19-20
