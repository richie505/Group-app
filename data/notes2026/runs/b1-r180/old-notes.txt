## SUB 0 | Civil Services – Company Era
[180:0:0] # Cornwallis and the Covenanted Service
[180:0:1] - **Lord Cornwallis** (Governor-General 1786-1793) = **"Father of Civil Services in India"**.
[180:0:2] - Created the **Covenanted Civil Service** (later the ICS), dominated by Europeans.
[180:0:3] - Banned **private trade and gifts**; raised salaries; promotion by **seniority**; separated revenue from justice.
[180:0:4] # Training and Recruitment
[180:0:5] TABLE head: Year | Step | By / Under
  [180:0:5.0] 1800 | College of Fort William, Calcutta – training of Company recruits | Wellesley
  [180:0:5.1] 1806 | Replaced by East India College, Haileybury (England) – law, political economy, Indian languages | Company
  [180:0:5.2] 1833 | Charter Act – Law Commission under T.B. Macaulay; codification | –
  [180:0:5.3] 1853 | Charter Act – ended Directors' patronage; open competitive examination (backed by Trevelyan and Robert Lowe) | –
  [180:0:5.4] 1854 | Macaulay Committee – exam in London; preference for Oxford/Cambridge graduates | –
[180:0:6] - Till 1853 appointments were by **patronage of the Company's Directors** (APPSC 2018).
[180:0:7] - **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** (1832, evidence before Parliament) = first Indian to **demand** Indians in higher posts.
[180:0:8] - Wellesley = the "Bengal Tiger"; Bentinck = modern Western education.

[180:5:2] - Madras Presidency governed **Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema**.

## SUB 7 | Ilbert Bill, 1883
[180:7:0] - Viceroy **Ripon**; drafted by **Law Member C.P. Ilbert**.
[180:7:1] - **Aim:** remove disqualifications of Indian magistrates – let Indian **district magistrates and sessions judges** try **European British subjects** in criminal cases; put Indians and Europeans on par.
[180:7:2] - **Opposition:** fierce European agitation = **"White Mutiny"**.
[180:7:3] - **Outcome (1884):** diluted – a European tried by an Indian judge could demand a **jury of at least half Europeans**.
[180:7:4] - **Indian Association** **backed** the Bill; it exposed British racism and taught the value of organised agitation.
[180:7:5] The Indian Association supported (not opposed) the Ilbert Bill.

[180:8:2] # Mayo vs Ripon
[180:8:3] TABLE head: Mayo (1869-72) | Ripon (1880-84)
  [180:8:3.0] Resolution 1870 | Resolution 1882
  [180:8:3.1] First split of Central and Provincial finances; provinces given police, jails, education, medical with fixed grants; told to raise local taxes | Local boards with elected non-official majority; local autonomy
  [180:8:3.2] Also statistical survey and first census (1872) | "Father of Local Self-Government in India"
[180:8:4] - After Ripon, bodies stayed **short of money and controlled by officials**.
[180:8:5] APPSC 2025: Cantonments Act 1924, Madras corporation 1688, Ripon father of LSG – all three correct.
[180:8:6] - **Bombay Municipal Act, 1872:** drafted and backed by citizens led by **Pherozeshah Mehta**; set up a Corporation in which **half the members were elected** by ratepayers.
[180:8:7] - **Calcutta Corporation Act, 1899 (Curzon):** **reduced the elected members** and increased nominated ones; **28 members resigned** in protest.
