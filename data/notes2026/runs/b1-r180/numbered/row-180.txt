## SUB 0 | Local Self-Government – Madras 1688, Mayo 1870, Ripon 1882
[0:0] # Milestones
[0:1] TABLE head: Year | Step
  [0:1.0] 29 Sep 1688 | Madras – first Municipal Corporation in India (charter 30 Dec 1687); now Greater Chennai Corporation, the oldest
  [0:1.1] 1726 | Corporations at Bombay and Calcutta
  [0:1.2] 1817 | Lottery Committee, Calcutta – town planning with lottery funds (Strand Road, Dalhousie Square)
  [0:1.3] 1864-68 | First local bodies – nominated members, headed by District Magistrates; seen as tax tools
  [0:1.4] 1870 | Mayo's Resolution – financial decentralisation
  [0:1.5] 1882 | Ripon's Resolution (18 May 1882) – "Magna Carta" of LSG
  [0:1.6] 1872 | Bombay Municipal Act – half the Corporation members elected by ratepayers; drafted and backed by citizens led by Pherozeshah Mehta
  [0:1.7] 1899 | Calcutta Corporation (Municipal) Act – Curzon: reduced the elected members, increased nominated ones; 28 members resigned in protest
  [0:1.8] 1907-09 | Royal Commission on Decentralisation (chairman C.E.H. Hobhouse) – stressed village panchayats; only recommendations
  [0:1.9] 1919 | LSG a transferred subject under dyarchy (finance stayed reserved)
  [0:1.10] 1924 | Cantonments Act – passed by the Central legislature
  [0:1.11] 1935 | LSG a provincial subject
[0:2] - **Father of Local Self-Government in India = Lord Ripon** (Viceroy 1880-84) – not Mayo, Lytton, Curzon, Canning, Dalhousie, Cornwallis or Macaulay.
[0:3] - **Not correctly matched (UPRO 2017):** Local self-government – **Lytton** ✘ (it is Ripon); Doctrine of Lapse – Dalhousie ✔; Permanent Settlement – Cornwallis ✔.
[0:4] - **APPSC G2 2025 statements:** Cantonments Act 1924 by the Central legislature, Madras corporation 1688, Ripon father of LSG – **all three correct**.
[0:5] # Mayo vs Ripon
[0:6] TABLE head: Mayo (1869-72) | Ripon (1880-84)
  [0:6.0] Resolution 1870 | Resolution 1882
  [0:6.1] First step to bifurcate Central and Provincial finances: provinces got fixed grants for police, jails, education, medical services, roads, to run as they liked, and were told to raise local taxes | Local boards with non-official majority, elected where possible; non-official chairmen; official control only to revise, not dictate
  [0:6.2] "Local interest, supervision and care are necessary" for funds on education, sanitation, medical relief, local works | Local bodies as an instrument of political and popular education
  [0:6.3] Also statistical survey and first census (1872) | Father of LSG; also repealed Vernacular Press Act, Hunter Commission (1882), Ilbert Bill
[0:7] - **Mayo's Resolution 1870 statements (State PSC):** first step bifurcating central/provincial finances; provinces empowered to administer certain services; meant to correct the imbalance and meet the actual needs of provinces → **all four correct**.
[0:8] - **After Ripon:** elected members stayed a **minority** in district boards, franchise narrow, bodies **short of money and under official control** (could be suspended or superseded).
[0:9] - **Curzon and LSG (UPRO 2023):** Curzon's **Calcutta Municipal Act 1899** is the safe statement; he spoke of British rule as a message "carved in granite" (1904).
[0:10] > Madras 1688 → Bombay/Calcutta 1726 → Mayo 1870 → Ripon 1882 → Hobhouse 1907-09 → 1919 transferred → 1924 Cantonments → 1935 provincial.

## SUB 2 | Ilbert Bill, 1883 – Indian Judges and European Offenders
[2:0] - **Viceroy:** **Ripon**; drafted by **Law Member C.P. Ilbert**.
[2:1] - **Aim:** remove the **disqualification of Indian magistrates** – let Indian **district magistrates and sessions judges** try **European British subjects** in **criminal cases**; Indians and Europeans **on par in criminal jurisdiction**.
[2:2] - **Not:** restrictions on carrying arms (Arms Act 1878), on vernacular newspapers (Vernacular Press Act 1878), civil service exams in India, or the cotton import duty.
[2:3] - **Opposition:** fierce European agitation = the **"White Mutiny"**.
[2:4] - **Outcome (1884):** diluted – a European tried by an Indian judge could demand a **jury of at least half Europeans**.
[2:5] - **Indian Association** **backed** the Bill; Indians learnt the value of organised all-India agitation.
[2:6] > Ilbert Bill = racial equality in **criminal** jurisdiction; asked by UPSC 2003 and 2013.
[2:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.19; Spectrum Modern India p.275, p.278; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 06 p.4, p.43; old app notes row 180
## SUB 3 | Judiciary – Hastings, Cornwallis Code, High Courts
[3:0] TABLE head: Governor-General / Act | Reform
  [3:0.0] Warren Hastings (1772) | District Diwani Adalat (civil, under the Collector) and Faujdari Adalat (criminal); Sadar Diwani and Sadar Nizamat Adalats
  [3:0.1] Regulating Act 1773 | Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774); first Chief Justice Sir Elijah Impey
  [3:0.2] Cornwallis Code 1793 | Collector lost judicial powers (only revenue collector); post of District Judge; separation of revenue and justice; 4 provincial courts of appeal at Calcutta, Dacca, Murshidabad, Patna

[3:1] - **Why 1793:** Cornwallis was **alarmed at the power concentrated in the Collector** and kept judicial and revenue work apart (UPSC 2010).
[3:2] - **Established the judicial organisation in India:** **Lord Cornwallis** (Cornwallis Code) – not Mayo, Curzon or Attlee.

## SUB 5 | Company-era Civil Service – Cornwallis and Fort William College
[5:0] - **Lord Cornwallis** (1786-93) = **"Father of Civil Services in India"**: created the **Covenanted Civil Service**; **raised salaries**, banned **private trade and gifts**, promotion by **seniority**.
[5:1] - **Wellesley** ("Bengal Tiger") set up the **College of Fort William, Calcutta, in 1800** (minute of 10 July 1800; PMF gives 24 Nov 1800) **to train British civilians (Company recruits) for administration** – languages (Persian, Hindustani), law, literature.
[5:2]   - **UPSC 2020:** purpose = **train British civilians for administrative purposes** – not a Directors' order (they **disapproved**), not oriental revival, not jobs for William Carey (who taught Bengali there).
[5:3]   - **UPRO 2021:** "founded 10 **June** 1800" ✘; "to teach Indian languages to British officers" ✔ → **only 2**.
[5:4] - **1806:** replaced by the **East India College, Haileybury** (England) – law, political economy, Indian languages.
[5:5] - **1833:** Charter Act – Law Commission under **T.B. Macaulay**; codification. **1853:** Charter Act ended Directors' **patronage** (open competition; Trevelyan, Robert Lowe); power passed to the Crown only in 1858.
[5:6] - **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** (1832, evidence before Parliament) = first Indian to **demand** Indians in higher posts. Bentinck = modern Western education.
[5:7] - ICS → **IAS** (direct successor).
[5:8] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.545-546, p.595; PMF Modern History Part 3 p.4, p.29; Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.66; old app notes row 180
## SUB 6 | Police and Army
[6:0] # Police
[6:1] TABLE head: Introducer | Reform
  [6:1.0] Cornwallis (1791-93) | Took police duties from zamindars; thanas (circles of 20-30 sq miles) under an Indian daroga; kotwal kept in towns
  [6:1.1] Munro – Judicial Commission 1814-18 | Police under the Collector; minor crimes tried by village officers (village munsif)
  [6:1.2] Police Commission 1860 → Police Act 1861 (Canning) | Royal Irish Constabulary model; IG (province), DIG (range), SP (district); meant to crush dissent
  [6:1.3] Curzon – Police Commission 1902-03 | Chairman Sir Andrew Fraser; CID in provinces, Central Intelligence Bureau
[6:2] - **Daroga** = the local face of the **"aura and authority of Company Bahadur"** (UPRO 2021); appointed and supervised by the Magistrate; notorious for misuse of power – not the kotwal, amala or zamindar.
[6:3] - **Thomas Munro:** President of the Judicial Commission (1814-18); **Governor of Madras 1820-27**; extended **Ryotwari**. Madras Presidency governed Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema.
[6:4] # Army after 1857

  [6:5.0] Peel Commission | 1859 | More European troops: Bengal 1 European : 2 Indians; Madras/Bombay 1 : 3 (ratio finally fixed at 2 : 5 there); artillery with Europeans
  [6:5.1] Eden Commission | 1879 (Lytton) | Amalgamate the three presidency armies
  [6:5.2] Presidency armies abolished | 1 April 1895 | One Indian Army under four commands
[6:6] - **Policy:** "balance and counterpoise", martial-race theory (Sikhs, Gurkhas, Pathans); no Indian officers till 1918.
