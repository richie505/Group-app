=== Subsidiary Alliance – Wellesley, Terms, Aims and the Order of States
# Author and aims
- **Lord Wellesley (Governor-General 1798-1805)** used it to build the empire; he did **not invent** it – Dupleix first hired out troops, Awadh's **1765** treaty "anticipated" it – but made **cession of territory for the subsidy** the general rule (its final shape).
- **Aims:** keep the **French** out (fear of **Napoleon**); bring the states into dependence = **British paramountcy** without direct rule; a large army kept **at the states' expense**. A **fixed income** for the Company was **not** an aim (UPSC 2018 ×2: "does not apply" = fixed income).
- **Rajput states (1818):** the Company's aim = to **establish British sovereignty / paramountcy** (RAS 1992); the Rajputs themselves sought protection from the **Marathas and Pindaris**; treaties negotiated by **Charles Metcalfe** under Lord Hastings.
# Terms
- 1. Permanent **subsidiary force** in the state, paid by a subsidy – or, if unpaid, by **ceding territory**.
- 2. A **British Resident** at the court.
- 3. **No other Europeans** in service without the Company's consent.
- 4. **No war or negotiation** with other states without the Governor-General = **foreign affairs surrendered**.
- 5. In return the Company **defends the ruler** and promises **non-interference** in internal affairs.
- **Effects:** rulers lost independence and revenue, became weak and irresponsible, subjects exploited; unpaid subsidies → cessions (the **Nizam's Ceded Districts**).

- **First state (asked ×9):** **Hyderabad, 1798** – APPSC G2 2002, 2008 ×2, JL 2011, IAS 1994, AP History PYQ; **⚠ APPSC Inspector of Factories key gave Awadh** – Awadh's 1765 defence treaty only anticipated the system and Awadh joined Wellesley's system in **1801**.

- **Not accepted under Wellesley:** **Holkar** of Indore resisted (signed only in 1818 after defeat); **Tipu Sultan** refused → Fourth Mysore War (1799).
- **Rayalaseema** came to the British **through the Subsidiary Alliance** – ceded by the Nizam on **12 Oct 1800** in lieu of the subsidy.
# Policies towards the states
| Policy | Author |
|---|---|
| **Ring-fence** – defend neighbours' frontiers (Awadh as buffer shielding Bengal from Marathas and Afghans) | **Warren Hastings** |
| **Subsidiary Alliance** | **Wellesley** (1798) |
| **Subordinate isolation** | **Lord Hastings** (1813-23) |
| **Doctrine of Lapse** – annex states without a natural male heir | **Dalhousie** |
| **Subordinate union** – princes as "breakwaters to the storm"; **right of adoption** recognised | Crown, after 1858 |
- Other "policy" pairs used as distractors: Dhamma – Ashoka; Blood and Iron – Balban; Market control – Alauddin Khalji; Blue Water – Almeida.
- **Grounds for intervention** in states: succession disputes, misgovernment, and the need to fulfil their **international commitments**.

=== Asaf Jahi Nizams – Reigns and Firsts
| Nizam | Reign | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan, Nizam-ul-Mulk, Asaf Jah I** | from **1724** | independent after defeating **Mubariz Khan** at **Shakar Kheda**; founder (Chin Qilich Khan) |
| **Nizam Ali Khan (Asaf Jah II)** | 1762-1803 | capital **Aurangabad → Hyderabad, 1763**; Circars leased (1766); Guntur given (1788); **first Subsidiary Alliance (1798)**; Ceded Districts (1800) |
| **Sikandar Jah (Asaf Jah III)** | 1803-29 | **Secunderabad** cantonment (near Hussain Sagar) named after him |
| **Nasir-ud-Daula (Asaf Jah IV)** | 1829-57 | Wahabi conspiracy **1839** (Mubariz-ud-Daula arrested); **Berar Treaty 1853** (Berar, Raichur, Dharashiv/Osmanabad); **Salar Jung I made Diwan (1853)**; died **16 May 1857** |
| **Afzal-ud-Daula (Asaf Jah V)** | 1857-69 | **Nizam during the 1857 revolt in Hyderabad**; loyal → title **"Star of India"** |
| **Mir Mahbub Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VI)** | 1869-1911 | **youngest Asaf Jahi ruler** – Nizam as an infant (turned 18 only in 1884); Salar Jung I regent |
| **Mir Osman Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VII)** | 1911-48 | last Nizam; **Razakar** struggle for an independent Hyderabad → Police Action (Sept 1948) |
- **1763 shift (asked ×2):** **Nizam Ali Khan** moved the Deccan capital from **Aurangabad to Hyderabad** (not Guntur; not 1756/1758/1760).
- **1857:** the Meerut outbreak (10 May) fell under Nasir-ud-Daula, but the **Hyderabad events** (Aurangabad, June; Residency attack, **17 July 1857**) came under **Afzal-ud-Daula** – the answer for "Nizam during 1857" (APPSC G2 2001, ATWO).
- **Residency attack:** **Turrebaz (Tura Baz) Khan**, a Rohilla jamedar, with ~5,000 men, to free **Chida Khan**; repulsed by **Col. Davidson**; the Nizam and Salar Jung stayed loyal.
> Trap: Sikandar Jah = Secunderabad; Mahbub Ali Khan = youngest; Afzal-ud-Daula = 1857 + Star of India; Osman Ali Khan = Razakars.

=== Salar Jung I and the Hyderabad Reforms (1853-1883)
- **Mir Turab Ali Khan = Salar Jung I**, made **Diwan on 31 May 1853** (after Siraj-ul-Mulk) by **Nasir-ud-Daula** → the **Salar Jung family rose under Nasir-ud-Daula**; served **three Nizams** (Nasir-ud-Daula, Afzal-ud-Daula, Mahbub Ali Khan) till his death in **1883**.
- **Revenue:** curbed the daftardars; **paid talukdars** replaced the **auctioning of revenue to contractors** = he **abolished revenue farming**; **Zillabandi (1865)** – districts under talukdars; Halisikka rupee, central mint at Hyderabad.
- **Post-1857 offices (ATWO A-R):** more work in the Minister's **Dar-ul-Insha** → a **Munshi Khana** for correspondence with the **taluqdars**, beside the offices for the restored districts (**Azla-i-Mustarida**).

- **Police (1865 on):** Mahkama-i-Kotwali; force = Nizamat; SP = Muhtamim, inspector = Amin, station = chauki.
- **Education:** Darul-ulum (1855), City High School (1870), Chaderghat (1872), Madrasa-e-Aliya (1873); **Nizam College 1887**.
- **Railways:** Hyderabad-Wadi line completed **1878** (NextGen; old app notes said 1874).
- **Chanda Railway Scheme (1883):** the elite under **Dr Aghornath Chattopadhyaya** demanded its details; he was deported on **20 May 1883** → the **beginning of public political awakening** in Hyderabad.

=== Northern Circars Lease, Kandregula Jogipantulu and Guntur
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 1759 | **Salabat Jang** gave the British **Machilipatnam, Nizampatnam, Vakal Mannar, part of Kondapalli** |
| 1765 | Mughal **Shah Alam II**'s farman, received by **Clive**, granted the five Circars – **Nizam Ali refused** it at first |
| **12 Nov 1766** | Treaty of Hyderabad (envoy **Calliaud**): Circars leased – ₹9 lakh a year or military aid |
| 1768 | **Treaty of Masulipatnam**: Nizam accepted Shah Alam's grant and resigned the Circars |
| **18 Sep 1788** | **Guntur** handed over by **Nizam Ali Khan** for ₹7 lakh, after **Basalat Jang**'s death (1782) |
- **Negotiator (asked ×2):** **Kandregula Jogipantulu** – mediator between Nizam Ali and the Company's men at **Machilipatnam**; used the **Gollapudi pay mutiny** to propose a lease at **₹15 lakh** (1766 treaty fixed ₹9 lakh); Nizam made him **Majumdar of Rajamahendravaram**, the British "**Raja Bahadur**". **Col. Forde** took Machilipatnam from the French (1759) – not the negotiator.
- **Circars of 1766:** Chicacole, Rajahmundry, Ellore, **Mustafanagar (Kondapalli)** – **not Kurnool** (Ceded District, 1800); **Guntur (Murtazanagar / Kondaveedu) = the fifth Circar**, excepted as Basalat Jang's estate.
- **"Main point of difference between Nizam Ali and the English" (G2 2011 ×2):** **the Northern Sarkars** – the 1765 grant over his head, the 1766-68 treaties (NextGen previous papers). Guntur was the later, narrower dispute (1779 lease from Basalat Jang, handover 1788).

=== Road to 1798 – Kharda, Raymond and the Hyderabad Residency
- Nizam beaten by the Marathas at **Kharda (1795)** → blamed **Sir John Shore's neutrality** → relied on **French battalions under Raymond** → **Wellesley** forced the alliance (1 Sep 1798).
- **Resident James Kirkpatrick** reported the French battalions; built the Residency ("**Koti**"). The Resident controlled the Nizam's **foreign relations** and the subsidiary force.
- **Russell Brigade** – reformed Hyderabad force raised by **Resident Henry Russell** (c. 1813).

=== Ceded Districts and Andhra Consequences
- **12 Oct 1800:** the Nizam ceded the lands he had gained from Mysore – **Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary** – in lieu of the subsidy = **Dattamandalalu (Ceded Districts)**.
- Name **Rayalaseema** (1928) proposed by **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao** at the **Nandyal Andhra Mahasabha**, presided by **Dr S. Radhakrishnan**.
- **1801:** Nellore and Chittoor from the Carnatic Nawab → the whole of Andhra under the Company; **Telangana stayed under the Nizam**.
- **Padmanabham (10 July 1794):** the Company defeated and killed **Chinna Vijayaramaraju** of Vizianagaram.
