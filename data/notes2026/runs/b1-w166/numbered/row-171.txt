## SUB 0 | William Bentinck (1828-35) – Sati, Thugs and Reforms
[0:0] - **Sati declared illegal** by **Regulation XVII of 4 Dec 1829** (the **Bengal Sati Regulation**) – **William Bentinck**, backed by **Raja Rammohan Roy** (a reformer, not the lawmaker); extended to Madras and Bombay in 1830.
[0:1] - **Thuggee suppressed (from 1830):** **Captain William Henry Sleeman** (appointed **1835**); Thugs (Hindu and Muslim) worshipped **Kali / Durga / Bhavani**.
[0:2] - **Female infanticide** suppressed (earlier regulations 1795, 1802 under Wellesley).
[0:3] - **Annexed:** **Mysore taken over (1831) for misgovernance**; **Coorg** and Central Cachar (1834).
[0:4] - **Ranjit Singh** received with great honour at **Ropar (1831)** – treaty of "perpetual friendship".
[0:5] - **Charter Act 1833:** Bentinck = **last GG of Bengal, first GG of India**; Company's trade ended, Europeans free to settle and hold land – he **eased the entry of British investors and traders** (APPSC key); the Act asked for slavery to be ended.
[0:6] - **Education:** Macaulay's Minute and the **English Education Act (1835)**; English the official language – "**Father of Modern Western Education in India**". Revised the **Mahalwari** settlement (Regulation IX, 1833) in the North-Western Provinces.
[0:7] TABLE head: Bentinck did NOT | Who did
  [0:7.0] Abolish slavery (Act V of 1843) | Ellenborough
  [0:7.1] Free the press (1835) | Metcalfe
  [0:7.2] Set up the first universities (1857) | Canning
  [0:7.3] Remove disabilities on change of religion (1850) | Dalhousie

## SUB 1 | Warren Hastings (1772-85)
[1:0] - **Governor of Bengal from 1772**; **first Governor-General of Bengal** under the **Regulating Act, 1773** (in office 1773/74-85).
[1:1] - **1772:** ended Clive's **Dual Government**, moved the treasury to Calcutta; **introduced the District Collector** (Diwani Adalats under the collector); **District Diwani and Fauzdari Adalats**; auctioned revenue-collection rights (five-year farming) – the **earliest land revenue settlement for Bengal**.
[1:2] - **Wars:** **Rohilla War (1774)** – he **helped Awadh against the Rohillas** (did not encourage them); **First Anglo-Maratha War** (Salbai 1782); **Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780-84)**; **Ring Fence** policy (Awadh as buffer).
[1:3] - **Culture:** **Calcutta Madrasa (1781)** – first Company educational institution; **Asiatic Society of Bengal (1784, William Jones)**; **A Code of Gentoo Laws (1776)** – Halhed's English translation of the Manu-based digest *Vivadarnavasetu*; stopped Shah Alam's **Rs 26 lakh** tribute; Supreme Court, Calcutta (1774); Pitt's India Act (1784).
[1:4] - **Impeachment:** begun **1787** by **Edmund Burke** in Parliament; trial **1788-95** → **acquitted**. Charges: Rohilla War, **Chait Singh** of Benares, **Begums of Awadh**, judicial murder of **Nand Kumar (1775)**. The **only Governor-General impeached**.

## SUB 2 | Cornwallis (1786-93) – Permanent Settlement and Civil Service
[2:0] - **Permanent Settlement (Istamarari) of Bengal, 1793** – zamindars owners; revenue fixed for ever.
[2:1] - **Cornwallis Code (1793):** separated **revenue administration from justice**; created the post of **District Judge**; collector left only revenue.
[2:2] - **Civil service:** Europeanised the administration, raised salaries, banned private trade – created the **Covenanted Civil Service** (later **ICS**) – "**Father of Civil Services in India**"; Warren Hastings had laid its foundation.
[2:3] - **Third Anglo-Mysore War** (Treaty of Seringapatam, 1792).
[2:4] - **Second term:** from 30 July 1805; **died 5 Oct 1805 at Ghazipur** (tomb there).
[2:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.39; Spectrum Modern India p.848; PMF Modern History Part 3 p.15; old app notes row 171
## SUB 3 | Dalhousie (1848-56) – the Maker of Modern India
[3:0] - **Railways:** **Railway Minute (1853)**; first line **Bombay-Thane (16 April 1853)** – he advocated rapid railway building.
[3:1] - **Telegraph** (Calcutta to Bombay, Madras, Peshawar) and the **Post Office Act (1854)**; **Public Works Department** (1854) – before him public works were under the Military Board; **Ganges Canal** (1854).
[3:2] - **Education:** **Wood's Despatch (1854)** – so "educational reforms" **were** his; the **Factories Act (1881)** was **Ripon's**, not his.
[3:3] - **Social:** Caste Disabilities Removal Act (**1850**); **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (1856)** – listed under Dalhousie by NextGen and Spectrum, though passed on 26 July 1856 after **Canning** took over.
[3:4] - **Causes of 1857 from his measures:** Doctrine of Lapse, annexation of Awadh, **abolition of titles and pensions** – railways and telegraph alarmed people too; educational reform was not one of them.

## SUB 5 | Wellesley, Metcalfe and Ellenborough – Other Measures
[5:0] - **Wellesley (1798-1805):** called himself the **"Bengal Tiger"**; Subsidiary Alliance; College of Fort William; took over Tanjore (1799), Surat (1800), Carnatic (1801); **Ceded Districts (1800)**. His administration was dominated by wars; it was **not** confined to Company affairs (statement-type trap).
[5:1] - **Charles Metcalfe (1835-36):** **Press Act of 1835 (Metcalfe Act)** repealed the 1823 licensing rules – the "**liberator of the Indian press**" (dated **1835**, at the end of the Bentinck period).
[5:2] - **Ellenborough (1842-44):** **ended the First Afghan War (1842)**; **Act V of 1843 abolished slavery** (the Charter Act of 1833 had asked for it); Sindh annexed.
[5:3] - **Hardinge I (1844-48):** Anti-Human-Sacrifice Act (**1845**); preference for English education.
[5:4] > **Press freed = Metcalfe (1835)**; Vernacular Press Act = Lytton (1878); its repeal = Ripon (1882).
