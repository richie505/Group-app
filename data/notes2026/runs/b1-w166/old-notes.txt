  [166:0:0.0] First | 1767-69 | Haidar Ali | Harry Verelst (Governor of Bengal) | Haidar before Madras → Treaty of Madras, 4 April 1769 (mutual restitution, help against third party)

  [166:0:0.2] Third | 1790-92 (PMF 1789-92) | Tipu | Lord Cornwallis (led in person) | Treaty of Seringapatam (1792) with Tipu – ended Tipu's supremacy in the south

## SUB 1 | Tipu Sultan – Capital, Embassies, Jacobin Club
[166:1:0] - **Capital: Srirangapatna (Seringapatam)**; born 1750; **"Tiger of Mysore"**; also **Padshah** (1787) and **"Citizen Tipu"** – "Lion of Mysore" is not his title.
[166:1:1] - **Embassies on modern lines:** **France** (three ambassadors in Paris, 1788), **Ottoman Empire**, Oman, Persia, **Afghanistan (Zaman Shah)**, Arabia, Mauritius – not Haidar, Mir Qasim or Shah Alam II.
[166:1:2] - **Navy:** Board of Admiralty (1796), dockyards at Mangalore, Wajedabad, Molidabad.
[166:1:3] - **Jacobin Club** at Seringapatam (**1797**); he became **Citizen Tipu** and **planted the Tree of Liberty**.
[166:1:4] - **Reforms:** new calendar, coinage, weights and measures; sericulture; **war rockets** (pioneer – he did **not** discontinue them).
[166:1:5] TABLE head: Monument (Srirangapatna) | Feature
  [166:1:5.0] Gumbaz | Mausoleum of Haidar Ali and Tipu
  [166:1:5.1] Daria Daulat Bagh | Summer palace with war murals
[166:1:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.130-131; PMF Modern History Part 1 p.79; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.17; old app notes row 166
## SUB 2 | Second War Battles – Pollilur and Porto Novo

  [166:2:0.1] Pollilur | 10 Sept 1780 | Haidar Ali and Tipu destroyed Col. William Baillie's British force (Spectrum misprints 1781)
  [166:2:0.2] Porto Novo | 1 July 1781 | Sir Eyre Coote defeated Haidar – reversed Pollilur, saved Madras

## SUB 3 | Haidar Ali (1761-82)
[166:3:0] - Rose in the **Wodeyar** army under ministers **Nanjaraj** (sarvadhikari) and **Devaraj** (dalwai), who had made **Chikka Krishnaraja Wodeyar II** a puppet.
[166:3:1] - **Faujdar of Dindigul (1755)** under **Nanjaraja**; set up a **modern arsenal at Dindigul with French help**; trained troops on Western lines; de facto ruler **1761**.
[166:3:2] - **First war diplomacy:** paid the **Marathas** to stay neutral and **won over the Nizam**; appeared before Madras → humiliating **Treaty of Madras (1769)** – the **first South Indian ruler to defeat British armies**.
[166:3:3] - Defeated by Peshwa **Madhav Rao** (1764, 1766, 1771); the English did not help him in 1771 → Second war.
[166:3:4] - **Velu Nachiyar** of Sivaganga recovered her kingdom with Haidar's help – the first queen to fight the British.
[166:3:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.126-128; PMF Modern History Part 1 p.78; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.17, 36; old app notes row 166
## SUB 4 | Third War and the Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)
[166:4:0] - Cause: Tipu attacked **Travancore** (1789/90), a Company ally; Tipu beat **General Meadows (1790)**; **Cornwallis** took **Bangalore (March 1791)** via Ambur and Vellore.
[166:4:1] - **Both the Nizam and the Marathas** joined the British (the grand alliance) – only in the **Third** war.
[166:4:2] - **Treaty of Seringapatam** – with **Tipu Sultan** (not Haidar): about **half his territory** ceded, indemnity **Rs 3 crore** (330 lakh), **two sons as hostages**.
[166:4:3] TABLE head: Gainer | Share
  [166:4:3.0] British | Malabar, Coorg (protectorate), Dindigul, Bara Mahal – Ryotwari began here (Read and Munro)
  [166:4:3.1] Nizam | Krishna to beyond the Pennar – Bellary, Gurramkonda, Gandikota, Kambham, Kadapa
  [166:4:3.2] Marathas | Around the Tungabhadra
[166:4:4] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.129-130; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.19, Part 6 p.18; PMF Modern History Part 2 p.39; old app notes row 166
[166:4:5] - **Why Travancore mattered:** a **British ally** and the Company's **only pepper supplier** – Tipu's attack (1789) precipitated the Third war.
[166:4:6] - **Spice factor:** British conflicts with Mysore were driven partly by Mysore's control of Malabar **pepper and cardamom**, its threat to Madras and its French ties.
## SUB 5 | Fourth War (1799) and the Settlement of Mysore
[166:5:0] - Tipu refused the **Subsidiary Alliance**; **Wellesley** declared war (17 April – 4 May 1799); led by **General George Harris**, General Stuart and **Arthur Wellesley**.
[166:5:1] - **Nizam** fought as a subsidiary ally (since 1798); the **Marathas** are listed as allies by NextGen, PMF and Spectrum, but in fact gave **no active help** and refused their share.
[166:5:2] - Wodeyars restored: boy ruler **Krishnaraja Wodeyar III** under a subsidiary alliance; Nizam got **Gooty and Gurramkonda**.
[166:5:3] - The Nizam's Mysore lands were handed to the Company in **1800** = **Ceded Districts** (**Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary**).
[166:5:4] - Removed the French-backed threat; brought Mysore and Rayalaseema under the Company; Bentinck took over Mysore 1831, Ripon restored it 1881.
[166:5:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.130-132; PMF Modern History Part 2 p.40-41; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.18; old app notes row 166
[166:5:6] - **British fear behind the Fourth war:** **Lord Wellesley** feared Tipu's alliance with **Napoleon** (then in Egypt) and the French.

## SUB 0 | Subsidiary Alliance – Wellesley, Terms, Aims and the Order of States
[167:0:0] # Author and aims
[167:0:1] - **Lord Wellesley (Governor-General 1798-1805)** used it to build the empire; he did **not invent** it – Dupleix first hired out troops, Awadh's **1765** treaty "anticipated" it – but made **cession of territory for the subsidy** the general rule (its final shape).
[167:0:2] - **Aims:** keep the **French** out (fear of **Napoleon**); bring the states into dependence = **British paramountcy** without direct rule; a large army kept **at the states' expense**. A **fixed income** for the Company was **not** an aim (UPSC 2018 ×2: "does not apply" = fixed income).
[167:0:3] - **Rajput states (1818):** the Company's aim = to **establish British sovereignty / paramountcy** (RAS 1992); the Rajputs themselves sought protection from the **Marathas and Pindaris**; treaties negotiated by **Charles Metcalfe** under Lord Hastings.
[167:0:4] # Terms
[167:0:5] - 1. Permanent **subsidiary force** in the state, paid by a subsidy – or, if unpaid, by **ceding territory**.
[167:0:6] - 2. A **British Resident** at the court.
[167:0:7] - 3. **No other Europeans** in service without the Company's consent.
[167:0:8] - 4. **No war or negotiation** with other states without the Governor-General = **foreign affairs surrendered**.
[167:0:9] - 5. In return the Company **defends the ruler** and promises **non-interference** in internal affairs.
[167:0:10] - **Effects:** rulers lost independence and revenue, became weak and irresponsible, subjects exploited; unpaid subsidies → cessions (the **Nizam's Ceded Districts**).

## SUB 0 | Revenue Systems and Taxes – Moturpha, Peshkash, Zamindari and Ryotwari
[168:0:0] # Revenue terms
[168:0:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [168:0:1.0] Moturpha | tax on looms – a tax on trades and occupations (weavers, shops, artisans); not a salt tax; attacked in the Madras Native Association's 1861 petition; abolished 1861
  [168:0:1.1] Peshkash / Peshcush | the share of revenue (land tax / tribute) paid by zamindars to the British
  [168:0:1.2] Korunu | revenue the zamindars collected from the farmers
  [168:0:1.3] Haveli lands | lands under direct British (government) control
  [168:0:1.4] Sunkams / Sayer | land customs, inland transit duties
  [168:0:1.5] Abkari | excise on liquor and toddy
[168:0:2] - **Moturpha (asked ×7):** "tax on looms" (APPSC G2 2011 ×2, AP History PYQ, Asst Social Welfare, BC Welfare); **G2 Mains 2025 statements:** Moturpha a salt tax – **false**; Peshcush = revenue from the zamindars – **true** → "(A) false, (B) true".
[168:0:3] # Three methods of land revenue (both statements true)
[168:0:4] TABLE head: System | Where | Settled with
  [168:0:4.0] Zamindari (Permanent Settlement) | coastal Northern Circars (Uttara Sarkarlu) – Vizianagaram, Bobbili, Pithapuram | zamindar pays peshkash
  [168:0:4.1] Ryotwari | Ceded Districts / Rayalaseema (Datta mandalalu) first (from 1802), later most of Madras (1820, Munro) | the ryot directly
  [168:0:4.2] Mahalwari | Nellore | village headmen
[168:0:5] - **Permanent Settlement:** Cornwallis **1793** ended the auctions and fixed the demand; applied to Andhra in **1802** (Vizianagaram peshcush fixed at ₹6 lakh); zamindars kept 1/10, government 9/10.
[168:0:6] - **Effects:** zamindari → absentee landlords, rack-rented tenants; ryotwari → over-assessment, cash payment, moneylender debt, land alienation.
[168:0:7] - **Zamindari abolished:** Madras Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act **1948**, in force **19 April 1949**.

## SUB 0 | Dalhousie (1848-56) – Doctrine of Lapse and Annexations
[170:0:0] - **Doctrine of Lapse:** an **adopted son** could inherit the ruler's private property but **not the state**; a dependent state without a natural heir **lapsed** to the Company. Applied by **Lord Dalhousie** (not its originator – Kittur 1824 is an earlier case).

[170:0:5] - **Awadh (1856)** came before the Indigo Revolt (1859-60), the Second Afghan War (1878-80) and the Ilbert Bill (1883).

## SUB 2 | Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley, 1798-1805)
[170:2:0] - **Introduced as a system by Lord Wellesley** (Governor-General **1798-1805**); not invented by him – **Dupleix** first hired out troops; **Awadh (1765)** was the first state in the protection trap; Cornwallis (Carnatic, 1787) first barred foreign relations.
[170:2:1] - **Terms:** British force permanently stationed in the state, paid for by a **subsidy or ceded territory**; a **British Resident** at the court; no Europeans in service without consent; no war or negotiation without the Governor-General; in return protection and (nominal) non-interference.
[170:2:2] - **Order of acceptance:** **Hyderabad (Sept 1798, 1800)** → **Mysore (1799)** → Tanjore (1799) → **Awadh (Nov 1801)** → **Peshwa (Bassein, Dec 1802)** → Bhonsle (Dec 1803) → **Sindhia (Feb 1804)** → Rajput states, Holkar (1818). Hyderabad was the **first**.
[170:2:3] - **Wellesley against Napoleon:** while Napoleon broke the empires of Europe, Wellesley's forward policy (Mysore 1799, Marathas 1803-05, Tanjore, Surat, Carnatic) **kept the British flag flying high** – "converted the British Empire in India into the British Empire of India".
[170:2:4] - **Effects:** states lost independence and revenue; rulers became irresponsible; the Company spread its troops at strategic points and kept the French out.
[170:2:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.151-154; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.39; Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.42; old app notes row 170
## SUB 3 | Anglo-Nepal War and Treaty of Sugauli; Burmese and Afghan Wars
[170:3:0] - **Anglo-Nepal War (1814-16):** under **Lord Hastings** (Governor-General 1813-23); Gorkhas had taken **Butwal and Sheoraj**; **Ochterlony** forced terms after Almora (1815).

## SUB 4 | Conquest of Sindh (1843)
[170:4:0] - **Governor-General: Lord Ellenborough (1842-44)**; general **Sir Charles Napier** defeated the **Talpur Amirs** at **Miani** and **Dabo** (1843); **Sindh annexed 1843**, Napier its first governor.
[170:4:1] - **Steps:** Treaty of "eternal friendship" (1809, Minto) → **Pottinger's treaty (1832, Bentinck)** – Indus trade → **protectorate (1838, Auckland)** → **subsidiary alliance (Feb 1839)** – force at Shikarpur and Bukkar → **Outram**'s new treaty → war and annexation.
[170:4:2] - **Motives:** recover prestige after the **First Afghan War**, counter **Russia**, control the **Indus** – not the Doctrine of Lapse. Called the **"Tail of the Afghan Storm"**.
[170:4:3] - **Napier:** "We have no right to seize Sindh, yet we shall do so ... a very advantageous, useful, humane piece of rascality".

## SUB 5 | Paramountcy – Lord Hastings and the Rajput States
[170:5:0] - **Lord Hastings (1813-23)** turned subsidiary alliances into **paramountcy**: ended non-intervention, crushed the **Pindaris** (Amir Khan, Karim Khan, Chitu) and the Marathas, imposed humiliating treaties on the Peshwa and Sindhia.
[170:5:1] - **Rajputana (1817-18):** Udaipur, Jaipur, Jodhpur, Bundi and others accepted **subordinate alliances**, negotiated by **Charles Metcalfe** – British protection in return for giving up **external relations**.
[170:5:2] - States kept **internal administration** but had **no international life**; the British intervened **to give effect to their international commitments** (and on **misgovernance** – Mysore 1831, Coorg 1834, Awadh 1856).

## SUB 0 | William Bentinck (1828-35) – Sati, Thugs and Reforms
[171:0:0] - **Sati declared illegal** by **Regulation XVII of 4 Dec 1829** (the **Bengal Sati Regulation**) – **William Bentinck**, backed by **Raja Rammohan Roy** (a reformer, not the lawmaker); extended to Madras and Bombay in 1830.
[171:0:1] - **Thuggee suppressed (from 1830):** **Captain William Henry Sleeman** (appointed **1835**); Thugs (Hindu and Muslim) worshipped **Kali / Durga / Bhavani**.
[171:0:2] - **Female infanticide** suppressed (earlier regulations 1795, 1802 under Wellesley).
[171:0:3] - **Annexed:** **Mysore taken over (1831) for misgovernance**; **Coorg** and Central Cachar (1834).
[171:0:4] - **Ranjit Singh** received with great honour at **Ropar (1831)** – treaty of "perpetual friendship".
[171:0:5] - **Charter Act 1833:** Bentinck = **last GG of Bengal, first GG of India**; Company's trade ended, Europeans free to settle and hold land – he **eased the entry of British investors and traders** (APPSC key); the Act asked for slavery to be ended.
[171:0:6] - **Education:** Macaulay's Minute and the **English Education Act (1835)**; English the official language – "**Father of Modern Western Education in India**". Revised the **Mahalwari** settlement (Regulation IX, 1833) in the North-Western Provinces.

## SUB 1 | Warren Hastings (1772-85)
[171:1:0] - **Governor of Bengal from 1772**; **first Governor-General of Bengal** under the **Regulating Act, 1773** (in office 1773/74-85).
[171:1:1] - **1772:** ended Clive's **Dual Government**, moved the treasury to Calcutta; **introduced the District Collector** (Diwani Adalats under the collector); **District Diwani and Fauzdari Adalats**; auctioned revenue-collection rights (five-year farming) – the **earliest land revenue settlement for Bengal**.
[171:1:2] - **Wars:** **Rohilla War (1774)** – he **helped Awadh against the Rohillas** (did not encourage them); **First Anglo-Maratha War** (Salbai 1782); **Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780-84)**; **Ring Fence** policy (Awadh as buffer).
[171:1:3] - **Culture:** **Calcutta Madrasa (1781)** – first Company educational institution; **Asiatic Society of Bengal (1784, William Jones)**; **A Code of Gentoo Laws (1776)** – Halhed's English translation of the Manu-based digest *Vivadarnavasetu*; stopped Shah Alam's **Rs 26 lakh** tribute; Supreme Court, Calcutta (1774); Pitt's India Act (1784).
[171:1:4] - **Impeachment:** begun **1787** by **Edmund Burke** in Parliament; trial **1788-95** → **acquitted**. Charges: Rohilla War, **Chait Singh** of Benares, **Begums of Awadh**, judicial murder of **Nand Kumar (1775)**. The **only Governor-General impeached**.
[171:1:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.39; Spectrum Modern India p.553, p.595, p.848; PMF Modern History Part 2 p.98, p.102-103, Part 3 p.10, p.18, p.54; old app notes row 171
## SUB 2 | Cornwallis (1786-93) – Permanent Settlement and Civil Service
[171:2:0] - **Permanent Settlement (Istamarari) of Bengal, 1793** – zamindars owners; revenue fixed for ever.
[171:2:1] - **Cornwallis Code (1793):** separated **revenue administration from justice**; created the post of **District Judge**; collector left only revenue.
[171:2:2] - **Civil service:** Europeanised the administration, raised salaries, banned private trade – created the **Covenanted Civil Service** (later **ICS**) – "**Father of Civil Services in India**"; Warren Hastings had laid its foundation.
[171:2:3] - **Third Anglo-Mysore War** (Treaty of Seringapatam, 1792).
[171:2:4] - **Second term:** from 30 July 1805; **died 5 Oct 1805 at Ghazipur** (tomb there).

## SUB 4 | Wellesley, Metcalfe and Ellenborough – Other Measures
[171:4:0] - **Wellesley (1798-1805):** called himself the **"Bengal Tiger"**; Subsidiary Alliance; College of Fort William; took over Tanjore (1799), Surat (1800), Carnatic (1801); **Ceded Districts (1800)**. ## SUB 5 | Dalhousie (1848-56) – the Maker of Modern India
[171:5:0] - **Railways:** **Railway Minute (1853)**; first line **Bombay-Thane (16 April 1853)** – he advocated rapid railway building.
[171:5:1] - **Telegraph** (Calcutta to Bombay, Madras, Peshawar) and the **Post Office Act (1854)**; **Public Works Department** (1854) – before him public works were under the Military Board; **Ganges Canal** (1854).
[171:5:2] - **Education:** **Wood's Despatch (1854)** – so "educational reforms" **were** his; the **Factories Act (1881)** was **Ripon's**, not his.
[171:5:3] - **Social:** Caste Disabilities Removal Act (**1850**); **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (1856)** – listed under Dalhousie by NextGen and Spectrum, though passed on 26 July 1856 after **Canning** took over.
[171:5:4] - **Causes of 1857 from his measures:** Doctrine of Lapse, annexation of Awadh, **abolition of titles and pensions** – railways and telegraph alarmed people too; educational reform was not one of them.
