## SUB 0 | The 73RD Amendment – What It Added and Why It Matters
[81:0:0] # 73rd vs 74th Amendment
[81:0:1] TABLE head: Item | 73rd Amendment Act, 1992 (rural) | 74th Amendment Act, 1992 (urban)
  [81:0:1.0] Bodies | Rural Panchayats | Urban local bodies (Municipalities)
  [81:0:1.1] Part | Part IX "The Panchayats" | Part IXA "The Municipalities"
  [81:0:1.2] Articles | 243 to 243O (16 Articles) | 243P to 243ZG (18 Articles)
  [81:0:1.3] Schedule | Eleventh Schedule – 29 subjects | Twelfth Schedule – 18 subjects
  [81:0:1.4] In force | 24 April 1993 (National Panchayati Raj Day) | 1 June 1993
  [81:0:1.5] Exemption Article | 243M | 243ZC
[81:0:2] - Gave **constitutional status** to PRIs and put **Article 40** (DPSP – organise village panchayats) into effect.
[81:0:3] - Other DPSP distractors: 39(b) material resources, 44 Uniform Civil Code, 48 agriculture and animal husbandry.
[81:0:4] # Significance
[81:0:5] - Brought PRIs into the **justiciable part** of the Constitution – key features became legally enforceable.
[81:0:6] - States are now **constitutionally obliged** to hold elections; not free to choose.
[81:0:7] - Shift from **representative to participatory** (grassroots) democracy; rejuvenated rural local bodies and reduced political interference in them.
[81:0:8] - Clearest achievements: **regular elections** and near-**50% women** representation.
[81:0:9] APPSC 2025 Assertion-Reason: "73rd Amendment is a landmark for grassroots democracy" (A) because "it brought PRIs under the justiciable part" (R) – both true, R explains A.
[81:0:10] - Keyed answer (APPSC): 73rd gives regular elections (243K) and women's reservation of at least one-third at all levels (243D); the key also added mandatory transfer of funds suggested by the SFC.
[81:0:11] - **Ministry of Panchayati Raj** – created **27 May 2004**; handles Part IX, PESA and District Planning Committees.

## SUB 3 | Three-tier Structure, Membership and Tenure
[81:3:0] # Tiers and elections (Articles 243B, 243C)
[81:3:1] - Tiers: **village – intermediate – district**. A Gram Panchayat can cover **more than one village**; the **Gram Panchayat** is the elected executive body.
[81:3:2] TABLE head: Who | How elected
  [81:3:2.0] Members at all three levels | Directly elected from territorial constituencies
  [81:3:2.1] Intermediate and district chairpersons | Indirectly, by and from among elected members
  [81:3:2.2] Village chairperson | As State law provides
[81:3:3] Follow the Article as enacted: only members are directly elected; intermediate/district chairpersons are indirect.
[81:3:4] # Qualification (Article 243F)
[81:3:5] - Minimum age **21 years** (not 25, not 30); other disqualifications as State law provides.
[81:3:6] # Term and dissolution (Article 243E)
[81:3:7] - Term **5 years from the date of its first meeting**.
[81:3:8] - After dissolution: elections within **six months** (not three); none needed if **less than six months** of term remain.
[81:3:9] - Panchayat formed after early dissolution serves only the **remainder** – e.g. first meeting 10 February 2014 → term ends **10 February 2019**.
[81:3:10] - Gram Sabha meetings are held in each village (by rotation); Gram Panchayat meetings at the GP headquarters.
[81:3:11] # Names of tiers across States

  [81:3:12.2] Kshetra Panchayat (area decided by State Government) | Uttar Pradesh

  [81:3:12.6] Taluka Parishad / Anchalik Parishad (PYQ pairing) | Gujarat / West Bengal

[81:3:13] - **AP tiers:** Gram Panchayat – **Mandal (Praja) Parishad** – Zilla Parishad.
## SUB 4 | Gram Sabha and Pesa
[81:4:0] # Definition and powers
[81:4:1] - **Article 243(b):** body of all persons **registered as voters** in the electoral rolls of a village within the Panchayat area.
[81:4:2] - **Article 243A:** exercises such powers as the **State Legislature** provides by law.
[81:4:3] - Example of **direct democracy**; approves plans and budgets.
[81:4:4] # Planning campaigns
[81:4:5] - **People's Plan Campaign "Sabki Yojana Sabka Vikas"** – from **2 October 2018**; prepares **Gram Panchayat Development Plans (GPDP)** through Gram Sabhas.
[81:4:6] - GPDP – GP-level plan for economic development and social justice.
[81:4:7] - **Viksit Gram Panchayat Plans** – linked to **PM Gati Shakti**.
[81:4:8] # PESA Act, 1996
[81:4:9] - From the **Bhuria Committee (1995)**; extends Part IX with exceptions to **Fifth Schedule Areas** (not Sixth) under **Article 243M(4)(b)**, in **ten States including AP**.
[81:4:10] - Gram Sabha is supreme, not merely advisory: owns **minor forest produce**, controls **village markets and liquor**, is **consulted before land acquisition**, approves plans and identifies beneficiaries.
[81:4:11] - **2026:** 30 years of PESA marked.

## SUB 6 | Reservation – Article 243D
[81:6:0] # Rules
[81:6:1] - SC/ST seats in proportion to population; **at least one-third** seats and chairperson posts for **women** (243D(4)) – a floor, not a ceiling; reserved seats go by **rotation**.
[81:6:2] - BC reservation – optional (243D(6)).
[81:6:3] # Women's 50% States and data
[81:6:4] - **Bihar** – first to give **50%** to women (**2006**). By 2010: Bihar, Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh. Many States now give 50%, including **AP**.
[81:6:5] - **MoPR, April 2026:** **12.14 lakh of 24.41 lakh** elected PRI representatives are women (**49.75%**).
[81:6:6] TABLE head: Meet 50% exactly | Short of 50%
  [81:6:6.0] AP, Himachal Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Sikkim | Gujarat 49.9%, Haryana 42.12%, Tripura 45.25%, DNH & DD 31.97%
[81:6:7] - **Jun 2026:** **Sashakt Panchayat Netri Abhiyan** (training women representatives) and **Model Women-Friendly Gram Panchayats** (one per district) – against token representation.
[81:6:8] # OBC reservation cases
[81:6:9] TABLE head: Case | Year | Holding
  [81:6:9.0] K. Krishna Murthy v. Union of India | 2010 | Upheld 243D(6)/243T(6); political backwardness ≠ social/educational; total vertical reservation normally within 50%
  [81:6:9.1] Vikas Kishanrao Gawali v. State of Maharashtra | 2021 | Triple test; no automatic OBC quota
[81:6:10] - Triple test: (1) dedicated commission with empirical inquiry; (2) local-body-wise proportion on its findings; (3) **SC+ST+OBC within 50%**.
[81:6:11] - AP's **34% BC quota** plus SC/ST shares must pass Gawali's 50% ceiling.

## SUB 8 | State Election Commission and State Finance Commission
[81:8:0] # State Election Commission (Article 243K)
[81:8:1] - Superintendence of electoral rolls and conduct of **all Panchayat elections** (municipal elections under 243ZA).
[81:8:2] - **State Election Commissioner** – appointed by the **Governor**; removed only like a **High Court judge**; conditions of service cannot be varied to his disadvantage.
[81:8:3] - SFC does **not** appoint the State Election Commissioner.
[81:8:4] - **AP SEC:** formed **17 June 1994** under the AP Panchayat Raj Act, 1994; Commissioner must hold or have held a post not below **Principal Secretary**. A **2020 ordinance** cutting the term from **five to three years** was struck down by the High Court.
[81:8:5] - **2026:** AP local body polls under SEC **Anil Chandra Punetha**; ULB elections for 100 of 123 ULBs (Arts 243K, 243ZA).
[81:8:6] # State Finance Commission (Article 243I) vs Union FC
[81:8:7] TABLE head: Basis | Union Finance Commission | State Finance Commission
  [81:8:7.0] Article | 280 (Part XII) | 243I (Panchayats) and 243Y (Municipalities) – same Commission
  [81:8:7.1] Appointed by | President | Governor
  [81:8:7.2] Periodicity | Every 5 years or earlier | Every 5 years; first within one year of 24 April 1993
  [81:8:7.3] Divides | Union ↔ States | State ↔ local bodies
  [81:8:7.4] Report | President, then Parliament (Art. 281) | Governor, then State Legislature with action-taken memorandum
[81:8:8] - SFC is a **constitutional** body; recommendations **advisory**; State Legislature decides its composition.
[81:8:9] - SFC recommends: sharing **net proceeds of State taxes**, duties, tolls and fees; taxes assigned to Panchayats; **grants-in-aid** from State Consolidated Fund; measures to improve finances; any matter the Governor refers.
[81:8:10] - **Article 280(3)(bb)** (Panchayats, 73rd) and **280(3)(c)** (Municipalities, 74th): Union FC suggests measures to augment a **State's Consolidated Fund** on SFC recommendations.
[81:8:11] - Central tax share goes to States; Panchayats get **FC grants** routed through the State. Panchayat funds: taxes, grants, **loans from State Government** (valid source).
[81:8:12] - Union FC also handles any matter the President refers; it does not settle Article 131 disputes (Supreme Court does).
[81:8:13] "Union FC has no role in local body assistance" is FALSE (UPPSC 2011).
[81:8:14] # AP State Finance Commissions
[81:8:15] - **Part VI** of the AP Panchayat Raj Act, 1994 gives effect to Article 243I.
[81:8:16] - **4th SFC** report – October 2019. **5th SFC** constituted **20 March 2023** after a PIL over delay; report for **2025–30** submitted 31 December 2024 (chair Prof. S. Rathna Kumari).
[81:8:17] - **Aug 2026:** 16th Finance Commission grants require duly constituted local bodies; AP stands to lose **₹12,150 crore** if polls are not completed by October 2026.
[81:8:18] - **SECs of other States:** **Madhya Pradesh** SEC constituted **1 February 1994** (first Commissioner N.B. Lohani from 15 February 1994); **Rajasthan** SEC constituted in **July 1994** under Article 243K.
[81:8:19] - **Own tax domain:** under State Panchayat Acts the **Gram Panchayat** has the main own taxes, such as **property (house) tax**; the intermediate and district tiers depend mostly on grants, tolls and fees.
