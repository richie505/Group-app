## SUB 0 | Dynasty, Capital and Mother Goddess
[0:0] - **Badami (Vatapi) Chalukyas:** **c. 543–753 CE**; ruled the **Deccan and the South**, **NOT North India** (Uttarakhand 2010: "dynasty that did not rule North India" = Chalukyas).
[0:1] - **Capital = Vatapi** (UPPSC 1991 ×2) = modern **Badami, Bagalkot district, Karnataka** – not Shravasti, **Kanchi** (Pallava) or Kannauj; sandstone hills around Agastya lake. Before Vatapi, **Aihole (Ayyavole)** was the seat.
[0:2] - **Vatapi fortified by Pulakesin I** – **Badami cliff inscription, 543 CE**.
[0:3] - **Mother goddess:** the early Chalukyas called themselves **Hariti-putras** and worshipped **Hariti** (G2 Mains 2019; APPSC PYQ) – not Kushmandini, Padmakshi or Kanakadurga.
[0:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.121-122; NextGen AP History Part 7 p.1; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.107; app notes 1:73 (sub 0)
## SUB 1 | Pulakesin II – the Greatest Chalukya
[1:0] # Greatest ruler
[1:1] - **Pulakesin II (610–642)** = the greatest ruler of the **Chalukyas of Vatapi** (UPPSC 1991; SSC 2013) – not Kalyani Chalukyas, Pallavas or Cholas; not Vikramaditya, Mangalesha or Pulakesin I.
[1:2] - **Hiuen Tsang** visited his kingdom and praised him (APPSC FRO 2012) – not Pulakesin I or III; Madhava Varma was a Vishnukundin.
[1:3] # Events
[1:4] TABLE head: Year (CE) | Event
  [1:4.0] 617 | Satara inscription: brother Kubja Vishnuvardhana viceroy of Vengi
  [1:4.1] 618–19 | Stopped Harsha on the Narmada
  [1:4.2] 624 | Vengi independent = Eastern Chalukyas (capital Pithapuram)
  [1:4.3] c. 626 | Embassy with Persian king Khusrau II
  [1:4.4] 634–35 | Aihole inscription
  [1:4.5] 642 | Pallava Narasimhavarman I destroyed Vatapi → title Vatapikonda
[1:5] - **Eastern campaign:** **Kosala & Kalinga** → Vengi → defeated the **Durjayas** → then the Pallava **Mahendravarman I**.
[1:6] # Titles and foreign accounts
[1:7] TABLE head: Ruler | Title
  [1:7.0] Kushanas | Devaputra
  [1:7.1] Pulakesin II | Dakshinapatheshwara
  [1:7.2] Harshavardhana | Sakalottarapathanatha
  [1:7.3] Samudragupta | Kaviraja
[1:8] - Match (UPRO 2023): **A-i, B-iii, C-ii, D-iv**.
[1:9] - **Paramesvara** after beating **Harsha**; also Satyashraya, Prithvivallabha.
[1:10] - China–India relations under the Chalukyas: **Ma Twan-lin** (Matwalin). **Lohnara inscription (630 CE)** does NOT list Harsha; Aihole does.
[1:11] - First **gold coins** of South India credited to Pulakesin II.

## SUB 2 | Aihole Inscription of Ravikirti
[2:0] TABLE head: Point | Fact
  [2:0.0] Author | Ravikirti, Jain court poet of Pulakesin II
  [2:0.1] Language / script | Sanskrit prasasti, Southern Brahmi
  [2:0.2] Date | 634–35 CE (Saka era)
  [2:0.3] Place | Meguti Jain temple, Aihole
  [2:0.4] Poets named | Kalidasa and Bharavi (Ravikirti claimed fame like theirs)
[2:1] - **Kalidasa is mentioned in** the **Aihole inscription of Pulakesin II** (IAS 1994; UPPSC 2013) – not the Allahabad pillar, Alapadu grant, Hanumakonda, Gwalior, Karamdanda or Mathura pillar inscriptions.
[2:2] - **Victories:** **Harsha** (Narmada), **Kadambas** (Banavasi), Gangas, Alupas, Mauryas of Konkan, Latas, Malavas, Gurjaras, **Kosala, Kalinga, Pishtapura** (**Pithapuram**), **Pallavas**.
[2:3] - Kirtivarman I = "**night of doom**" to the **Nalas, Mauryas and Kadambas**.

## SUB 4 | Rulers and Succession
[4:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Reign | Key fact
  [4:0.0] Pulakesin I | 543–566 | Real founder; Ashvamedha; walled Vatapi
  [4:0.1] Kirtivarman I | 566–597 | Took Kadambas, Gangas, Mauryas of Konkan; Bahusuvarna-Agnishtoma (Mahakuta pillar)
  [4:0.2] Mangalesha | 597–609 | Regent; earliest rock-cut caves at Badami
  [4:0.3] Pulakesin II | 610–642 | Zenith
  [4:0.4] Vikramaditya I | 655–680 | Revived power after the Pallava occupation of Vatapi
  [4:0.5] Vikramaditya II | 733–746 | Took Kanchi thrice but spared it; queen Lokamahadevi built the Virupaksha temple, Pattadakal
  [4:0.6] Kirtivarman II | 746–753 | Last; overthrown by Rashtrakuta Dantidurga
[4:1] - Other sacrifices: **Vajapeya, Hiranyagarbha** – claim to **Kshatriya** status.
[4:2] - **Succession war:** Kirtivarman I left minor sons → brother **Mangalesha** ruled as **regent**; Pulakesin II killed him at **Elapattu-Simbige** (Bana territory, c. 609–10).
[4:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.121; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.107-108; app notes 1:73 (sub 1, 2)
