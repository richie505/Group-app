## SUB 1 | Mihira Bhoja
[1:0] - **Greatest Pratihara king** (State PSC) – not Dharmapala (Pala), Harsha or Mahendrapala.
[1:1] - **Son of Ramabhadra** and queen **Appa Devi**; **grandson (not son)** of Nagabhata II (CGPSC 2022: statement "son of Nagabhata II" is the false one).
[1:2] - **Titles:** **Adivaraha** and **Prabhasa** (UPRO 2017); a **Vaishnava** – Adivaraha (Varaha avatar) legend on his **coins**, not Shaiva.
[1:3] - **Capital:** recovered **Kannauj c. 836 AD** and made it the capital for about a century.
[1:4] - Early defeats by Palas, Rashtrakutas, Kalachuris, then a comeback; checked by **Sankaravarman of Kashmir**, Rashtrakuta **Krishna II** and Pala **Devapala**.
[1:5] - **Gwalior prashasti** composed by court poet **Baladitya**.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.147; NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.119; app notes 1:70 (sub 3)

## SUB 4 | Name, Origin and Capitals
[4:0] - **"Pratihara" = door-keeper**: claimed descent from **Lakshmana**, Rama's door-keeper brother.
[4:1] - **Agnikula legend** (**Prithviraj Raso**): **Pratiharas, Chaulukyas, Paramaras, Chauhans** born from the fire-pit at **Mount Abu**.
[4:2] - **"Gurjara":** link with the Gurjars, first mentioned in the **Aihole inscription** of Pulakesin II and Banabhatta's Harshacharita.
[4:3] TABLE head: Stage | Capital
  [4:3.0] Early Gurjara seat | Bhinmal (Rajasthan)
  [4:3.1] Nagabhata I | Ujjain (Avanti)
  [4:3.2] From Nagabhata II; held firmly by Mihira Bhoja (c. 836) | Kannauj (Mahodaya)
[4:4] - **Barah copper plate** of Bhoja mentions a camp (skandhavara) at **Mahodaya**.
[4:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.147, p.151; NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.119; app notes 1:70 (sub 0, 1)
## SUB 5 | Resistance to the Arabs and Decline
[5:0] # Resistance to Arab expansion
[5:1] - **Muhammad bin Qasim** took **Sindh in 712**, defeating and killing **Raja Dahir**.
[5:2] - The **Gwalior inscription** of Mihira Bhoja says **Nagabhata I** crushed the **mleccha** (Arab) army.
[5:3] - Result: Arab rule stayed **confined to Sindh** and Multan; the Pratiharas were the main barrier.
[5:4] # Decline
[5:5] - **Indra III's** raid on Kannauj (c. 916) and loss of Gujarat; Krishna III's invasion (c. 963).
[5:6] - **Mahmud of Ghazni** attacked Kannauj in **1018**.
[5:7] - Breakaway feudatories: **Chandelas** (Khajuraho), Chauhans, Paramaras, Chaulukyas.
[5:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.147, p.151; app notes 1:70 (sub 5, 7)
