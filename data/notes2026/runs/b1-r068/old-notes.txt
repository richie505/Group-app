=== NOTES 1:68 | Tripartite Struggle for Kannauj – Palas, Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas
## SUB 0 | Why Kannauj?
[68:0:0] - Old names: **Mahodaya**, **Mahodaya Shri** ("Nagar Mahodaya Shri", in Mihira Bhoja's time), Kanyakubja.
[68:0:1] - After Harsha, Kannauj = **symbol of imperial sovereignty**: its holder claimed to be **Samrat of North India** (like Magadha in the Gupta period).
[68:0:2] - Economic: **Gangetic-Doab** farm wealth; **Arabian Sea trade via Gujarat**, avoiding the Arab-held Indus delta.
[68:0:3] - Period: **8th–9th century CE**. The struggle was for Kannauj, **not Ujjain**.
## SUB 1 | The Three Contenders
[68:1:0] TABLE head: Dynasty | Base | Rulers in the struggle
  [68:1:0.0] Gurjara-Pratiharas | Rajasthan-Gujarat / Avanti, later Kannauj | Vatsaraja, Nagabhata II, Mihira Bhoja, Mahendrapala I
  [68:1:0.1] Palas | Bengal and Bihar | Dharmapala, Devapala, Mahipala I
  [68:1:0.2] Rashtrakutas | Deccan, capital Manyakheta (Malkhed) | Dhruva, Govinda III, Amoghavarsha, Indra III
[68:1:1] NOT part of it: **Cholas**, Pallavas, Kalyani Chalukyas, Chauhans, Gahadavalas.
[68:1:2] - Chronology: Pallava Mahendravarman I → Pala Gopala → Pratihara Bhoja → Chola Parantaka I.
## SUB 2 | Phases and Battles
[68:2:0] TABLE head: Phase | Events
  [68:2:0.0] I (c. 780–800) | Vatsaraja takes Kannauj, defeats Dharmapala; Dhruva defeats both, takes loot and returns south
  [68:2:0.1] II (early 9th c.) | Dharmapala removes Pratihara nominee Indrayudha, installs puppet Chakrayudha
  [68:2:0.2] III | Nagabhata II ousts Chakrayudha and defeats Dharmapala at Monghyr; Govinda III defeats both, then retreats
  [68:2:0.3] IV (late 9th c.) | Mihira Bhoja, Mahendrapala I hold Kannauj; Himalayas to Narmada
  [68:2:0.4] c. 916 | Indra III sacks Kannauj, defeating Pratihara Mahipala I
[68:2:1] Indrayudha = removed; Chakrayudha = Dharmapala's puppet. Nagabhata II was a Pratihara, not a Pala.

=== NOTES 1:69 | Palas of Bengal – Gopala, Dharmapala, Devapala; Nalanda and Vikramashila, Pala Art
## SUB 0 | Gopala – Founder
[69:0:0] - **Elected** by notables (mid-8th c.) to end **matsyanyaya** (anarchy, law of the jungle); born near **Pundravardhana** (per Taranatha).
[69:0:1] - Source: **Khalimpur inscription of Dharmapala** records his election.
[69:0:2] - Replaced the later Guptas of Magadha and the **Khadgas** of east Bengal; built **Odantapuri**.
[69:0:3] Kings chosen by the people: **Gopala** and **Nandivarman II Pallavamalla**. Dharmapala, Devapala, Narasimhavarman I, Mahendravarman I, Harsha, Yashovarman inherited.
## SUB 1 | Later Pala Rulers
[69:1:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Achievement
  [69:1:0.0] Dharmapala (c. 770–810) | Founded Vikramashila and Somapura; installed Chakrayudha at Kannauj; defeated by Dhruva and by Nagabhata II near Monghyr
  [69:1:0.1] Devapala | Gave five villages for the Nalanda monastery of Balaputradeva (Nalanda copper plate)
  [69:1:0.2] Mahipala I (c. 977–1027) | Revived Pala power; defeated by Rajendra Chola
  [69:1:0.3] Ramapala (c. 1072–1126) | Last strong ruler; Kaivarta revolt; Kamarupa, Kalinga; Jagaddala
[69:1:1] - Palas = **last great royal patrons of Buddhism** in India; took it to Tibet and SE Asia.
## SUB 2 | Mahaviharas – Founder and Location
[69:2:0] TABLE head: Mahavihara | Founder | Present location
  [69:2:0.0] Nalanda | Kumaragupta I (Gupta, 5th c. AD) | Nalanda, Bihar (ancient Magadha); Pala patronage
  [69:2:0.1] Odantapuri | Gopala | Bihar Sharif, Bihar; ~12,000 monks; model for Tibetan monasteries
  [69:2:0.2] Vikramashila | Dharmapala | Antichak, Bhagalpur district, Bihar, on the Ganga
  [69:2:0.3] Somapura | Dharmapala | Paharpur, Naogaon, Bangladesh
  [69:2:0.4] Jagaddala | Ramapala | Varendra, Naogaon, Bangladesh
[69:2:1] - Together = **Panchamahavihara** (Tibetan sources).
[69:2:2] - Vikramashila: centre of **Tantric/Vajrayana** Buddhism and logic; trained monks for Tibet.
[69:2:3] - Nalanda texts compiled under Dharmapala; Jagaddala translated books into **Tibetan**.
[69:2:4] - Destroyed by **Bakhtiyar Khilji**, c. 1193–1200 (1194 for Nalanda in one tradition); Jagaddala looted early 13th c.
[69:2:5] - **Nava Nalanda Mahavihara**: **Bhikshu Jagadish Kashyap**; foundation **20 November 1951**.
[69:2:6] Vikramashila is in **Bhagalpur** (not Banka) and was founded by **Dharmapala** (not Gopala).
## SUB 3 | Odd-one-out – Centres of Learning
[69:3:0] - Early medieval (Pala) centres: Nalanda, Vikramashila, Odantapuri, Somapura.
[69:3:1] - **Taxila** = odd one out: early Buddhist centre, capital of **Gandhara** (Rawalpindi district, Pakistan); students – Chanakya, Chandragupta Maurya, Charaka, Jivaka.
[69:3:2] - **Valabhi** = **Hinayana** centre of Harsha's time; Buddhist-period centres: Nalanda, Vikramashila, Valabhi.

## SUB 0 | Name and Origin
[70:0:0] - **"Pratihara" = door-keeper**: claimed descent from **Lakshmana**, Rama's door-keeper brother.
[70:0:1] - **Agnikula legend** (**Prithviraj Raso**): **Pratiharas, Chaulukyas, Paramaras, Chauhans** born from the fire-pit at **Mount Abu**.
[70:0:2] - "Gurjara" from their link with the **Gurjars**, first mentioned in the **Aihole inscription** of Pulakesin II and Banabhatta's **Harshacharita**.
[70:0:3] - Ruled much of north India from the mid-8th to the 11th century.

=== NOTES 1:74 | Chalukya-harsha and Chalukya-pallava Conflicts; Chalukya Administration
## SUB 0 | Chalukya-harsha Conflict – Battle of the Narmada
[74:0:0] - Victor: **Pulakesin II** (Badami Chalukya); Harsha's southward march stopped on the **Narmada**.
[74:0:1] - Date: **618 CE**; it must predate **634 CE**, when the **Aihole inscription** (Ravikirti) recorded it.
[74:0:2] - Sources: **Aihole inscription + Hiuen Tsang** (NOT the Kailasanatha inscription).
[74:0:3] - Titles: **Paramesvara** (after Harsha) and **Dakshinapatheshwara** ("lord of the south") vs Harsha's **Uttarapatheshwara**.
[74:0:4] Paramesvara after defeating Harsha = Pulakesin II, not Pulakesin I (⚠ APPSC key (2010) gave Pulakesin I).
## SUB 1 | Chalukya-pallava Rivalry – Four Rounds
[74:1:0] TABLE head: Round | Chalukya | Pallava | Result
  [74:1:0.0] 1 | Pulakesin II | Mahendravarman I | Advanced to Kanchi; won Krishna-Godavari land (Maruturu, Kopparam inscriptions)
  [74:1:0.1] 2 | Pulakesin II | Narasimhavarman I | Pallava victory; Vatapi taken (642), Pulakesin II killed
  [74:1:0.2] 3 | Vikramaditya I | Narasimhavarman I / Paramesvaravarman I | Vatapi recovered (c. 655)
  [74:1:0.3] 4 | Vikramaditya II | Nandivarman II | Kanchi taken three times, spared
[74:1:1] - Pallava occupation of Vatapi: **642 to c. 655** (about **13 years**).
[74:1:2] - End: Badami Chalukyas replaced by the **Rashtrakutas (Dantidurga)**, NOT the Cholas; Nandivarman II did not overthrow the Rashtrakutas.

## SUB 5 | Chalukya Administration
[74:5:0] - Units: **rashtra** (province) → **vishaya** (district, under the **vishayapati**) → **grama** (village, under the gramakuta).
[74:5:1] - **Vatapi and Aihole** = urban and trade centres.
[74:5:2] - **Agraharas**: Brahmin land grants run by **mahajanas**.
[74:5:3] - Feudatories: **Rashtrakutas**, Alupas, Gangas, Sendrakas; **Renati Chodas** (Rayalaseema) – feudatories of the early Pallavas, then the Badami Chalukyas.
[74:5:4] - **AP angle**: wars → rise in Rayalaseema of **Renati Chodas, Banas, Vaidumbas**; **Pitikayagulla** inscription (8th c., early Telugu script) shows post-Badami Chalukya influence.
