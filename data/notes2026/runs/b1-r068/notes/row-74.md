=== Chalukya–Harsha Conflict – Battle of the Narmada
- **Victor:** **Pulakesin II** (Badami Chalukya) stopped **Harsha's** southward march on the **Narmada** (UPSC 2003 ×2; UPPSC 2016) – not Pulakesin I, Vikramaditya I/II or Kirtivarman II.
- **Title Paramesvara** after defeating Harsha = **Pulakesin II** (APPSC G1) – not Pulakesin I or the Pallava Mahendravarmans.
- **Date:** **618 CE** (some books 630–634); before the **Aihole inscription** (634) recorded it.
- **Sources:** **Aihole inscription + Hiuen Tsang** (NOT the Kailasanatha inscription).
- **Titles:** Paramesvara and **Dakshinapatheshwara** ("lord of the south") vs Harsha's **Uttarapatheshwara**; Harsha accepted the Narmada as his southern border.

=== Chalukya–Pallava Rivalry
# Four rounds
| Round | Chalukya | Pallava | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pulakesin II** | **Mahendravarman I** | Advanced to **Kanchi**; won the Krishna–Godavari land (Maruturu, Kopparam) |
| 2 | Pulakesin II | **Narasimhavarman I** | Pallava victory; Vatapi taken (**642**), Pulakesin II killed |
| 3 | **Vikramaditya I** | Narasimhavarman I / Paramesvaravarman I | Vatapi recovered (c. 655) |
| 4 | **Vikramaditya II** | **Nandivarman II** | Kanchi taken three times, spared |
- Pallava occupation of Vatapi: **642 to c. 655** (about **13 years**).
- **End:** Badami Chalukyas replaced by the **Rashtrakutas (Dantidurga)**, NOT the Cholas; Nandivarman II did not overthrow the Rashtrakutas.
# Battles
| Battle | Victor |
|---|---|
| **Pullalur** (near **Kanchi**, c. 618–19) | **Pulakesin II** over **Mahendravarman I** (Chalukya records; Pallava records claim it) |
| **Manimangala** (c. 642) | **Narasimhavarman I** over Pulakesin II (also Pariyala, Suramara) |
| Vatapi (642) | Narasimhavarman I; sacked by general **Paranjoti** |
- **Battle where Pulakesin II conquered Mahendravarman I = Pullalur** (SI 2016) – not Vatapi, Kolleru or Aihole.
# Titles
| Title | Meaning | Ruler |
|---|---|---|
| Paramesvara, Dakshinapatheshwara | Lord of the south | Pulakesin II |
| **Vatapikonda** | Conqueror of Vatapi | **Narasimhavarman I** (Pallava; also Mamalla) |
| **Kanchiyamkonda** | Who took Kanchi | **Vikramaditya II** (Chalukya) |
# Chronology (SI 2023)
- **Pulakesin I (543–566) → Kirtivarman I (566–597) → Pallava Mahendravarman I (600–630) → Rashtrakuta Krishna I (756–773)** = iv, ii, iii, i.

=== Women in Chalukya Administration
- **Dynasty that often gave women high administrative posts = Chalukyas** (UPPSC 2007) – not Cholas, Palas or Senas.
| Woman | Role |
|---|---|
| **Vijayabhattarika (Vijaya-mahadevi)** | Queen of **Chandraditya** (son of Pulakesin II); two copper plates / land grants in her own name; poetess; **administered the kingdom** after his death |
| **Kumkumdevi** | Sister of Vikramaditya I; village granted to a **Jain monastery** |
| **Lokamahadevi, Trailokyamahadevi** | Queens of Vikramaditya II; **Virupaksha** and **Mallikarjuna** temples, Pattadakal |
- Comparison: Kalyani Chalukya princess **Akkadevi** governed **Kisukad**; Eastern Chalukya queens (Ayyana Mahadevi, Chamakamba) = temple builders, not governors.
~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.121; app notes 1:74 (sub 6)

=== Chalukya Occupation of Kanchi
- **Vikramaditya II** entered Kanchi **c. 740 CE** (**Nandivarman II's** reign); **no plunder**.
- Gave gold to the **Rajasimheshvara (Kailasanatha)** temple; left a **Kannada inscription** on a pillar.
- **Lokamahadevi's Virupaksha temple, Pattadakal** copies the **Kailasanatha** of Kanchi.
- **Kanchi** – Pallava capital, temple city and seat of learning, the "**Kashi (Benares) of the South**".
~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.108, p.135; app notes 1:74 (sub 4)

=== Chalukya Administration and the Andhra Angle
- Units: **rashtra** (province) → **vishaya** (district, under the **vishayapati**) → **grama** (village, under the gramakuta).
- **Vatapi and Aihole** = urban and trade centres.
- **Agraharas:** Brahmin land grants run by **mahajanas**.
- Feudatories: **Rashtrakutas**, Alupas, Gangas, Sendrakas; **Renati Chodas** (Rayalaseema) – feudatories of the early Pallavas, then the **Badami Chalukyas**.
- **AP angle:** the wars led to the rise in Rayalaseema of the **Renati Chodas, Banas, Vaidumbas**; the **Pitikayagulla** inscription (8th c., early Telugu script) shows **post-Badami Chalukya influence**.
