## SUB 0 | Annual Financial Statement (Art. 112) – Budget Basics, Presentation and Firsts
[0:0] # Article 112 – the "Budget"
[0:1] - **Constitutional name:** the Budget = **Annual Financial Statement (AFS)**, **Article 112** – a statement of the **estimated receipts and expenditure of the Government of India for that year** (not the Economic Survey, not last year's surplus).
[0:2] - **"Budget" not in the Constitution:** the word **Budget** nowhere appears; Annual Financial Statement, Appropriation Bill and Consolidated Fund of India do. (Budget ← French **"bougette"** = little bag.)
[0:3] - **Laid by:** the **President** "causes" the AFS to be laid before **both Houses** every financial year; the **Finance Minister** presents it in the **Lok Sabha** (Union Budget 2023-24: **Nirmala Sitharaman**).
[0:4] - **Financial year:** **1 April – 31 March**; Budget shown as **Revenue Budget + Capital Budget**; it must show **charged** and **voted** expenditure separately (Art. 112(2)).
[0:5] - **Prepared by:** the **Budget Division, Department of Economic Affairs (DEA)**, Ministry of Finance – not Revenue, Expenditure or Financial Services; process starts with the **Budget Circular** (usually **September**).
[0:6] - **Halwa ceremony** (North Block) marks the start of the secrecy/printing of Budget documents.
[0:7] - **Economic Survey:** prepared by the Finance Ministry (DEA), presented **before** the Budget (a day or a few days earlier).
[0:8] # Budget articles in order
[0:9] TABLE head: Article | Subject
  [0:9.0] 112 | Annual Financial Statement; 112(3) charged items
  [0:9.1] 113 | Estimates as Demands for Grants; only LS votes; Art. 113(3) President's recommendation
  [0:9.2] 114 | Appropriation Bill; 114(3) no withdrawal except under appropriation by law
  [0:9.3] 115 | Supplementary, additional, excess grants
  [0:9.4] 116 | Vote on account, vote of credit, exceptional grants
  [0:9.5] 117 | Financial Bills (President's recommendation)
  [0:9.6] 265 | No tax except by authority of law
  [0:9.7] 266 / 267 | Consolidated Fund & Public Account / Contingency Fund

## SUB 1 | Consolidated Fund, Public Account and Contingency Fund (Arts. 266-267)
[1:0] TABLE head: Basis | Consolidated Fund of India | Public Account of India | Contingency Fund of India
  [1:0.0] Article (Union / States) | 266(1) / 266(1) | 266(2) / 266(2) | 267(1) / 267(2)
  [1:0.1] Receipts | All revenues (taxes and other receipts), all loans (Treasury Bills, ways and means advances), loan repayments | Money held in trust: provident funds, small savings, judicial deposits, savings bank deposits, remittances | Imprest for unforeseen expenditure
  [1:0.2] Withdrawal | Only under appropriation made by law – Parliament's authorisation (Art. 266(3), 114) | Executive action, no vote of Parliament (banking transactions) | Advance by the President, before Parliament's approval
  [1:0.3] Controlled by | Parliament | Executive | President; held by the Finance Secretary, DEA (not Expenditure)
[1:1] - **All revenues** of the Union by taxes and other receipts → **Consolidated Fund of India** (not Public Account or Contingency Fund).
[1:2] - **Withdrawal from the CFI:** authorisation must come from **Parliament** (an Appropriation Act) – not the President, PM, Finance Minister or CAG. In India the CAG has **no "comptroller" power** over issue of money; the Ministries draw under the Appropriation Act.
[1:3] - **Article for "no money withdrawn from the CFI without Parliament's sanction":** **Art. 266(3)** (appropriation "in accordance with law") read with **Art. 114(3)**.
[1:4] - **Contingency Fund of India:** created by **Parliament by law** – **Contingency Fund of India Act, 1950**; at the **President's disposal**, who **advances** money for **unforeseen** expenditure (also additional/excess needs) **pending authorisation** by Parliament – spent **before** Parliament's approval. Corpus **Rs 30,000 crore** from **2021-22** (Rs 500 crore from 2005; Rs 50 crore earlier).
[1:5] - **State Contingency Fund:** established by the **State Legislature** by law (**Art. 267(2)**); placed at the disposal of the **Governor**, who operates it (not the CM or State FM).
[1:6] - **CAG** audits all three funds of the Union and the States.
[1:7] > Exam angle: "Contingency Fund – Article?" → 267; "State Contingency Fund – who operates?" → Governor (asked ×4); "authorisation for CFI withdrawal" → Parliament (×3).

## SUB 2 | Charged Expenditure on the Consolidated Fund (Art. 112(3))
[2:0] - **Charged expenditure** = expenditure from the CFI that the Constitution makes **non-votable** – **not submitted to the vote** of Parliament but **can be discussed** in either House (Art. 113(1)); it still needs an Appropriation Act (Art. 114).
[2:1] # Charged on the Consolidated Fund of India – Art. 112(3)
[2:2] - **President** – emoluments, allowances, office expenses (Art. 59(3)).
[2:3] - **Chairman and Deputy Chairman** of the RS; **Speaker and Deputy Speaker** of the LS – salaries and allowances (Art. 97).
[2:4] - **Supreme Court judges** – salaries, allowances, **pensions** (Art. 125); SC administrative expenses.
[2:5] - **High Court judges** – **only pensions** (Art. 112(3)(d)(iii)); their **salaries** are charged on the **Consolidated Fund of the State** (Art. 202(3)(d); salaries fixed under Art. 221).
[2:6] - **CAG** – salary, allowances, pension and office expenses (Art. 148).
[2:7] - **UPSC** chairman and members – salaries, allowances, pensions (Art. 322); a **State PSC's expenses** are charged on the **Consolidated Fund of the State** (Art. 322).
[2:8] - **Debt charges** of the Government of India; sums to satisfy a **judgment, decree or arbitral award**; anything Parliament declares charged.
[2:9] # Not charged (voted)
[2:10] - **Prime Minister and Ministers** – salaries fixed by Parliament (Art. 75(6)); **voted**, not charged.
[2:11] - **Election Commission** – salaries and expenses are **voted**, not charged.
[2:12] - **Vice-President:** no salary **as VP**; he draws it **as Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** – that salary is **charged on the CFI** (not paid from the Public Account).
[2:13] - **Governor:** emoluments charged on the **Consolidated Fund of the State** (Art. 158(3)), not the CFI; one Governor for two or more States → shared in the proportion the **President** fixes by order (**Art. 158(3A)**). Governors' pensions are not in Art. 112(3).
[2:14] - **Second Schedule** – emoluments, allowances and privileges; **Third Schedule** – forms of oaths.
[2:15] > Exam angle: "NOT charged on the CFI" → PM / Ministers, Election Commission, (VP as such); "HC judges' salaries" → State Consolidated Fund, pensions → CFI.

## SUB 3 | Stages of the Budget, Demands for Grants, Guillotine and Cut Motions
[3:0] # Six stages (Laxmikanth)
[3:1] - **1. Presentation** of the Budget (no discussion that day).
[3:2] - **2. General discussion** – both Houses, 3-4 days; no cut motion, no vote.
[3:3] - **3. Scrutiny by the Departmentally Related Standing Committees** (House adjourned 3-4 weeks).
[3:4] - **4. Voting on Demands for Grants** (Art. 113) – **Lok Sabha only**; estimates of expenditure are submitted as **Demands for Grants**; it may pass, reject or **reduce**, never increase; a **demand becomes a grant after it is voted**.
[3:5] - **5. Passing of the Appropriation Bill** (Art. 114).
[3:6] - **6. Passing of the Finance Bill** – last stage; gives effect to the tax proposals.
[3:7] - **Sequence:** Presentation → DRSC scrutiny → **Appropriation Bill → Finance Bill**. The **vote on account** is passed **after the general discussion** (before the demands are voted).
[3:8] - **Not stages** of normal financial legislation: vote on account, vote of credit, exceptional grants.
[3:9] - **Guillotine:** on the last allotted day the **Speaker** puts **all remaining demands** to vote – those **not discussed for want of time** – at once.
[3:10] # Cut motions – motions to reduce a demand
[3:11] TABLE head: Cut motion | Reduces the demand | Purpose
  [3:11.0] Policy cut | To Re 1 | Disapproval of the policy behind the demand
  [3:11.1] Economy cut | By a specified amount | Economy in expenditure
  [3:11.2] Token cut | By Rs 100 | Air a specific grievance within the Government's responsibility
[3:12] - **Object:** to **move a proposal to reduce expenditure** in the Budget (a demand for grant) and draw the House's attention to a matter.
[3:13] - Moved **only in the Lok Sabha**, at the **voting on Demands for Grants**; **admitted at the discretion of the Speaker**; cannot relate to charged expenditure.
[3:14] - **Passage** = loss of confidence → the Government is expected to **resign**.
[3:15] > Exam angle: guillotine = undiscussed demands; cut motion admitted by the Speaker; order "I, II, IV, III" (Appropriation before Finance).

## SUB 4 | Vote on Account and Other Special Grants (Arts. 115-116)
[4:0] TABLE head: Grant | Article | Meaning
  [4:0.0] Supplementary | 115 | Sanctioned amount insufficient for the year
  [4:0.1] Additional | 115 | New service not in the Budget
  [4:0.2] Excess | 115 | Spent more than granted; voted after the year, PAC approval first
  [4:0.3] Vote on account | 116 | Advance grant for part of the year pending passage of the Budget (≈2 months, one-sixth; 3-5 months in election years)
  [4:0.4] Vote of credit | 116 | Blank cheque for an unexpected/indefinite demand
  [4:0.5] Exceptional | 116 | Not part of the current service of any year
  [4:0.6] Token grant | – | New service met by re-appropriation; token sum voted by the LS
[4:1] - **Vote on account:** needed **when the regular Budget will not be passed in time** (e.g. an election year); it lets the Government **withdraw money from the CFI** before the Appropriation Act.
[4:2] - **Since the 1 February Budget (2017)** a vote on account is normally needed only in **election years** (with an Interim Budget) – it has not become ineffectual.
[4:3] - **AP angle:** the **February 2019 AP Budget** (election year) was a **vote-on-account budget**.
[4:4] - **Latest:** the LS approved the **second batch of Supplementary Demands for Grants 2025-26** (Art. 115).

## SUB 5 | Appropriation Bill, Finance Bill and Parliamentary Control over Finance
[5:0] - **Appropriation Bill:** authorises withdrawal from the CFI for **voted grants + charged expenditure**; must be **passed by both Houses** (as a Money Bill) and assented to; **Art. 114(3)**: no withdrawal except under it – even charged expenditure needs it.
[5:1] - **No amendment** may vary the amount or alter the destination of a voted grant, or vary charged expenditure.
[5:2] - **Automatic repeal clause:** since the **Appropriation Act, 2016** (after the Appropriation Acts (Repeal) Act, 2016 repealed 758 old Acts) Appropriation Acts carry an **automatic repeal (sunset) clause**.
[5:3] - **Finance Bill:** gives effect to the tax proposals; amendments to **reduce or abolish** a tax **can** be moved, **not** to increase it; must be enacted within **75 days** (Provisional Collection of Taxes Act, 1931). Changes in existing tax rates also need law (Art. 265) – usually via the Finance Bill.
[5:4] - **Parliament's control over public finance:** placing the AFS; withdrawal only after the Appropriation Act; supplementary grants and vote on account; the Finance Bill. India has **no Parliamentary Budget Office** (so UPSC 2012 answer = 1, 2, 3 and 5).
[5:5] - **Department of Economic Affairs** (not Revenue) prepares the Budget; **Public Account** disbursements need **no** Parliament authorisation (UPSC 2015 → only "no withdrawal from the CFI without Parliament").
[5:6] - **Money Bill (Art. 110(1)):** covers custody of the CFI/Contingency Fund and appropriation out of the **CFI** – not "appropriation out of the Contingency Fund".
