## SUB 1 | Powers of the Speaker – Money Bill, Casting Vote, Joint Sitting
[31:1:0] TABLE head: Power | Article / basis
  [31:1:0.0] Decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill – decision final | Art. 110(3)
  [31:1:0.1] Endorses his certificate on a Money Bill sent to the RS and to the President | Art. 110(4)
  [31:1:0.2] Casting vote only in a tie (no vote in the first instance) | Art. 100(1)
  [31:1:0.3] Presides over a joint sitting of both Houses (summoned by the President, Art. 108) | Art. 118(4)
  [31:1:0.4] Adjourns / suspends the House for want of quorum (one-tenth) | Art. 100(3)
  [31:1:0.5] Anti-defection decisions | Tenth Schedule – subject to judicial review (Kihoto Hollohan, 1992)
  [31:1:0.6] Recognises the Leader of the Opposition (party with one-tenth of the House); LoP statutory since the 1977 Act | Speaker's direction
  [31:1:0.7] Decides admissibility of questions, motions and resolutions; adjourns the House sine die | Rules of Procedure
[31:1:1] - The Speaker's Money Bill decision cannot be questioned by the **President**, the **Rajya Sabha**, the **Finance Minister**, a JPC or the **Supreme Court**.
[31:1:2] - **Casting vote**: only "in case of a tie when votes are equally divided" – not to save the Government, not for Constitutional Amendments or emergencies; the President, PM and CJI have no vote in the Lok Sabha. The **RS Chairman** likewise has a vote **only in a tie**.
[31:1:3] - **Joint sitting**: presided by the **Speaker**; in his absence the **Deputy Speaker**; if he too is absent, the **Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**; then a person chosen by the members present. Never the RS Chairman / VP, the President, the PM or a minister.
[31:1:4] - A **minister who resigns** may make a personal explanation in the House only with the **Speaker's permission** (consent), not the PM's or President's.
[31:1:5] - Speaker is ex officio **President of the Indian Parliamentary Group** and chairman of the Conference of Presiding Officers; appoints chairmen of LS committees; chairs the Business Advisory, Rules and General Purposes Committees.
[31:1:6] - Powers list (Raj PCS 2024): presides over joint sitting ✔, Money Bill certificate ✔, does not vote except in a tie ✔; "neither presides nor takes part during his own removal" ✘ (he may take part) → **(i), (ii), (iv)**.
[31:1:7] - **Panel of Chairpersons**: nominated by the **Speaker** (up to **ten**); a member presides when **both** Speaker and Deputy Speaker are **absent** (not when the Speaker's office is vacant). If none is present, the House determines who presides (Art. 95(2)).
[31:1:8] - **Indian Parliamentary Group (IPG)**: an **autonomous body** (1949); all MPs may be members, **former MPs** may become **associate members**; **Speaker** = ex officio President; **Deputy Speaker** and **Deputy Chairman** = ex officio Vice-Presidents; Secretary-General of the LS = its Secretary-General.

## SUB 2 | Speaker of the Lok Sabha – Election, Oath, Resignation, Removal (Articles 93-97)
[31:2:0] - **Article 93**: the Lok Sabha **chooses (elects)** two of **its own members** as Speaker and Deputy Speaker – the Speaker is **not nominated or appointed by the President**, not elected by the public or by both Houses, but by **all members of the Lok Sabha**. Speaker's provisions: **Arts. 93-96**, 97 (salary), **100** (casting vote), **110** (Money Bill), **118** (joint sitting).
[31:2:1] - Must already be a **member of the House** when elected – the "six months to become a member" grace belongs to **Ministers** (Art. 75(5)), not to the Speaker.
[31:2:2] - Date of the Speaker's election is fixed by the **President** (Rule 7); the Deputy Speaker's by the **Speaker** (Rule 8).
[31:2:3] - **No separate oath of office** – the Speaker (and Deputy Speaker) take only the **oath as an MP**; the President (Art. 60), VP (Art. 69), PM / ministers (Art. 75(4)) and judges take oaths of office.
[31:2:4] - **Tenure**: not at the pleasure of the President – he holds office **so long as he is a member**; vacates on ceasing to be a member (**Art. 94(a)**).
[31:2:5] - **Resignation (Art. 94(b))**: Speaker → **Deputy Speaker**; Deputy Speaker → **Speaker** (not the President, PM, CJI or RS Chairman). In a State: Speaker → **Deputy Speaker of the Assembly** (Art. 179).
[31:2:6] - **Removal (Art. 94(c))** – of the Speaker **or the Deputy Speaker**: resolution of the **Lok Sabha** passed by a **majority of all the then members** (effective majority) after **at least 14 days' notice** – not two-thirds, not by the President, not by both Houses.
[31:2:7] - **Dissolution**: the Speaker **does not vacate** office – continues **until immediately before the first meeting** of the new Lok Sabha (second proviso to Art. 94); the Deputy Speaker and members go at once. Same for a State Speaker (**Art. 179**); State Speaker and Deputy Speaker are provided by **Art. 178**.
[31:2:8] - No constitutional duty to **resign from his party** on becoming Speaker (UPSC 2025: only I and III correct) – that is a British convention, not Indian law.
[31:2:9] - **Article 96** – while a resolution for **his own removal** is under consideration:
[31:2:10] TABLE head: Situation | Presides | Speaks / takes part | Votes in first instance | Casting vote
  [31:2:10.0] Normal sitting | Yes | Yes | No | Yes (Art. 100(1))
  [31:2:10.1] Own removal resolution | No, even if present | Yes | Yes | No
[31:2:11] - **Chairman (RS)** during his own removal resolution: cannot preside and **cannot vote** (not a member), but may speak.
[31:2:12] - **Art. 97**: salaries of the Speaker and Deputy Speaker are **charged on the Consolidated Fund of India** (not voted).
[31:2:13] - **14 days' notice** is needed to remove the **President, Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Deputy Chairman**; a **Governor** is removed by the President at will without notice.
[31:2:14] - **Warrant of Precedence**: 1 President, 2 VP, 3 PM, **4 Governor within his State**, 5 former Presidents (5A Deputy PM), **6 CJI and Speaker of the Lok Sabha**, 7 Cabinet Ministers – so a Governor (in his State) ranks above the Speaker, and the Speaker ranks above all Cabinet Ministers except the PM.
[31:2:15] - The Speaker is **not appointed by the President** – the CJI and the service chiefs are.
[31:2:16] > Exam angle: "chosen/elected by the Lok Sabha", "resigns to the Deputy Speaker", "majority of all the then members + 14 days", "continues after dissolution", "no oath of office".

## SUB 3 | Sessions, Adjournment, Prorogation, Dissolution; President's Address
[31:3:0] - **Art. 85(1)**: the **President summons** each House (separately or together) at such time and place as he thinks fit, but **six months shall not intervene** between the **last sitting of one session** and the **first sitting of the next** → Parliament must meet **at least twice a year**.
[31:3:1] - **Three sessions** – **Budget** (Feb-May, **longest**), **Monsoon** (Jul-Sep), **Winter** (Nov-Dec, **shortest**) – a **convention**, not in the Constitution; no fixed number of sessions or minimum sitting days is laid down. States: **Art. 174** (same six-month rule); State Assemblies averaged only **24 sittings in 2025**.
[31:3:2] - **Session**: first sitting → prorogation/dissolution. **Recess**: the period between **prorogation** and the reassembly in a new session.
[31:3:3] - **Quorum**: **one-tenth** of the total members of the House, **including the presiding officer** – **Art. 100(3)**.
[31:3:4] TABLE head: Device | By whom | Ends | Effect on pending business
  [31:3:4.0] Adjournment | Presiding officer | A sitting, for hours/days/weeks | Session continues
  [31:3:4.1] Adjournment sine die | Presiding officer (Speaker / Chairman) | A sitting for an indefinite period (no date fixed) | Bills continue
  [31:3:4.2] Prorogation – Art. 85(2)(a) | President on the Council of Ministers' advice (Governor in States) | The session | Bills do not lapse; notices lapse (except to introduce bills)
  [31:3:4.3] Dissolution – Art. 85(2)(b) | President – Lok Sabha only | The life of the House | Pending business lapses (subject to Art. 107); the RS is permanent
[31:3:5] - Sequence: adjournment **sine die** → President's notification of **prorogation** → recess → new session. After prorogation, **only the President** can summon the House.
[31:3:6] - **Automatic dissolution**: the LS term is **five years from the date appointed for its first meeting** (Art. 83(2)); at its end it dissolves by efflux of time even without a formal order (UPSC 2004: a, b and c all correct).
[31:3:7] - APPSC G1 2018: "adjournment sine die = terminating a sitting for an indefinite period" (key); prorogation ends the session; dissolution applies only to the LS; adjournment **never** ends a session.
[31:3:8] - **Art. 86**: President may **address** either or both Houses, **require attendance** of members and send **messages** on pending Bills. **Art. 87**: **special address** at the start of the **first session after each general election** and the **first session of each year**.
[31:3:9] - The address is **prepared by the Union Cabinet** (it states the Government's policy) and is debated on a **Motion of Thanks**; defeat of the Motion of Thanks = defeat of the **Government**, which must **resign**.
[31:3:10] - **Gap between two sessions** – "shall not exceed **six months**" (flashcard).
[31:3:11] > Exam angle: "minimum meetings a year" → twice; "how is six months counted" → last sitting to first sitting; prorogation by the President; recess.

## SUB 4 | Chairman and Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha
[31:4:0] - The **Vice-President** is the **ex officio Chairman** of the Rajya Sabha (**Arts. 64, 89(1)**) – not the President, Speaker or Deputy Speaker.
[31:4:1] - The Chairman is **not a member** of the Rajya Sabha – so the **Rajya Sabha** is the House presided over by a **non-member** (the Speaker of the LS / Vidhan Sabha and the Chairman of a Vidhan Parishad are members of their Houses).
[31:4:2] - Vote: a **casting vote only in a tie** – not "like other members", not for Amendment Bills.
[31:4:3] - Functions: presides and conducts business, protects members' privileges, is the House's spokesperson; he **cannot dissolve the Rajya Sabha** – it is a **permanent House**, never dissolved.
[31:4:4] - Removal: only by **removal as Vice-President** (Art. 67(b)); during that resolution he cannot preside or vote but may speak.
[31:4:5] - **Art. 91**: when the VP **acts as President** (or the Chairman's office is vacant), the **Deputy Chairman** performs the Chairman's duties; the VP then draws the **President's** salary. **Jan 2026 (Justice Varma case)**: SC upheld the Deputy Chairman's Art. 91 power to decide a judge-removal motion while the Chairman's office was vacant.
[31:4:6] - **Deputy Chairman**: elected by the RS from its members (Art. 89(2)); resigns to the **Chairman**; removed by an RS effective majority after 14 days' notice (**Art. 90(c)**). **Panel of Vice-Chairpersons** nominated by the Chairman.

## SUB 5 | Parliament Secretariat – Article 98
[31:5:0] - **Art. 98(1)**: **each House** has a **separate secretarial staff**, but **posts common to both Houses** may be created.
[31:5:1] - **Art. 98(2)**: **Parliament** may by law regulate recruitment and service conditions of the staff of either House.
[31:5:2] - **Art. 98(3)**: until such a law, the **President**, **after consultation with the Speaker** (or the Chairman for the RS), makes the rules; individual appointment orders are issued by the Speaker / Secretary-General.
[31:5:3] - **Independent of the Government (executive)**: the **Lok Sabha Secretariat** works under the **direct control and supervision of the Speaker** (the RS Secretariat under the Chairman) – not the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs, Home Ministry, PM or President. It is a permanent body, not a five-year one.
[31:5:4] - Head: **Secretary-General** of each House – appointed by the **presiding officer**; rank of the **Cabinet Secretary**. The idea of a separate legislative secretariat was raised in **1926** by **Vithalbhai Patel**.
[31:5:5] - **Presidential election returning officer**: the **Secretary-General of the Lok Sabha or of the Rajya Sabha, by rotation** (appointed by the ECI).
[31:5:6] > Exam angle: "LS Secretariat under whom?" (×4) → Speaker; "separate secretariat but common posts" (Art. 98).

## SUB 6 | Deputy Speaker and Pro-Tem Speaker
[31:6:0] - **Deputy Speaker**: elected by the LS from its members (Art. 93) on a date fixed by the **Speaker** (Rule 8), communicated to members by the **Secretary-General**.
[31:6:1] - That the Deputy Speaker comes **from the Opposition** is only a **convention** – no constitutional or mandatory provision (UPSC 2017, 2022). Likewise no rule that the Speaker comes from the majority party. (The winner of an LS/Assembly election needs only a **plurality**, not 50% of votes.)
[31:6:2] - **Art. 95**: performs the Speaker's duties when the office is **vacant** and presides when the Speaker is **absent**; when presiding he has the **same powers as the Speaker** and **no appeal lies to the Speaker** against his rulings – he is **not subordinate** to the Speaker.
[31:6:3] - Out of the Chair he is an ordinary member – may debate, move motions, **vote in the first instance**. On being appointed to a committee he becomes its **chairman automatically**.
[31:6:4] - Own removal resolution: cannot preside; may speak and vote in the first instance; no casting vote.
[31:6:5] - UPSC 2022: date fixed by the Speaker ✔; Deputy Speaker has the Speaker's powers in the Chair, no appeal ✔; "mandatory opposition/ruling party" ✘; "moved by Speaker, seconded by PM" ✘ → **A and C only**.
[31:6:6] - **Vacancy**: the LS Deputy Speaker's post has been **vacant since June 2019** – through the whole 17th and the 18th Lok Sabha (as of 2026); in the States, **Jharkhand**'s post was vacant for **21 years** (June 2026). Last LS Deputy Speaker: **M. Thambidurai** (2014-19).
[31:6:7] - **Pro-Tem Speaker (Art. 95(1))**: **appointed and sworn in by the President**; usually **one of the senior-most members** of the newly elected House (list prepared by the Legislative section, sent to the **Minister of Parliamentary Affairs**, who seeks the President's approval).
[31:6:8]   - Functions: **administers the oath** to new members and presides over the **election of the Speaker**; tenure ends when the regular Speaker is elected. Not elected by the House; not the outgoing Speaker, Deputy Speaker or LoP.
[31:6:9] > Exam angle: "Deputy Speaker from the Opposition – Constitution?" → only convention; "function of the Pro-Tem Speaker" → oath to members.

## SUB 1 | No-confidence, Confidence and Censure Motions
[32:1:0] # No-confidence motion (Rule 198)
[32:1:1] - **Not mentioned in the Constitution** – flows from **Art. 75(3)** (Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha**). The Constitution mentions **neither** a confidence nor a no-confidence motion.
[32:1:2] - Moved **only in the Lok Sabha** (not the Rajya Sabha, not "both Houses", not a committee) – an **exclusive power of the Lok Sabha** (UPSC 2022: Emergency approval and impeachment need both Houses).
[32:1:3] - Against the **entire Council of Ministers / the Government** – not the PM alone, an individual minister or the Speaker.
[32:1:4] - Leave needs the support of **at least 50 members**; written notice to the **Secretary-General**; **no reasons** need be stated.
[32:1:5] - **Speaker** decides admissibility – **discretion to admit or reject**; he does not resign or dissolve the House.
[32:1:6] - If passed, the **whole Council of Ministers must resign**. "Six months must lapse between two motions" and "100 members" are **false**.
[32:1:7] - **Most powerful weapon** for executive accountability = the **no-confidence motion** (NextGen); the power to dismiss the government is real only when coalitions break (post-1989).
[32:1:8] - A PM who is a Rajya Sabha member **cannot vote** in the Lok Sabha, even on a no-confidence motion against his own government (UPSC 1997).
[32:1:9] - **First** no-confidence motion: **J.B. Kripalani** against **Nehru** (**1963**).
[32:1:10] - **AP angle**: Andhra State, Nov 1954 – **Gouthu Latchanna's** no-confidence motion carried **69–68**; **Prakasam ministry fell by one vote**; President's Rule from **November 1954**.
[32:1:11] # Comparison
[32:1:12] TABLE head: Motion | Moved by / where | Reasons | Against | If passed
  [32:1:12.0] No-confidence | Opposition; Lok Sabha only; 50 members | Not needed | Entire CoM | CoM must resign
  [32:1:12.1] Confidence | Government (Council of Ministers), to prove majority; Lok Sabha | – | – | If defeated, the Government falls
  [32:1:12.2] Censure | Lok Sabha | Must be stated | A minister, a group or the whole CoM | Not bound to resign – disapproval of policies
[32:1:13] - **Censure motion is different from the no-confidence motion** (aim, reasons, effect).
[32:1:14] - The motion the **Council of Ministers can move** = **Confidence Motion** (no-confidence, censure and adjournment come from members).

## SUB 2 | Closures, Guillotine, Point of Order and Yielding the Floor
[32:2:0] TABLE head: Device | Meaning
  [32:2:0.0] Closure motion | Cuts short the debate – stoppage of debate on a motion – and puts the matter to vote; four kinds
  [32:2:0.1] Simple closure | "Matter sufficiently discussed; be now put to vote"
  [32:2:0.2] Closure by compartments | Clauses grouped into parts; debate and vote part by part
  [32:2:0.3] Kangaroo closure | Only important clauses debated and voted; intervening clauses taken as passed
  [32:2:0.4] Guillotine | Allotted time over – all remaining clauses / demands, discussed or not, put to vote (in the Budget the Speaker puts all remaining demands to vote without discussion)

## SUB 3 | Instruments of Parliamentary Control over the Executive
[32:3:0] - NextGen lists four instruments: **(1) deliberation and discussion, (2) approval or refusal of laws, (3) financial control, (4) no-confidence motion**. A **walkout** is a protest, **not** an instrument.
[32:3:1] - Deliberation includes **Question Hour, Zero Hour, half-an-hour discussion, adjournment motion** etc.
[32:3:2] - **Accountability triangle**: **Question Hour + Supplementary Questions + Adjournment Motions** – UPSC 2017: all three (1, 2 and 3) are ways Parliament controls the Council of Ministers.
[32:3:3] - **Most effective day-to-day vigil**: **Question Hour** – "perhaps the most effective method of keeping vigil on the executive" (NextGen).
[32:3:4] - UPSC 2001: Parliament controls the administration **through Parliamentary Committees** – not Consultative Committees (set up by the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs), periodic reports or writs (a court's power).
[32:3:5] - **Not a tool**: **dissolution of the House** (an executive power), **judicial review** (judiciary), **walkout**; the **Budget session** is only a period of sitting, and bills are formulated by the executive.
[32:3:6] - Other tools: calling attention, censure motion, half-an-hour and short-duration discussions, **Motion of Thanks**, resolutions; State Legislatures also use **estimates and public accounts committees**.

## SUB 7 | Motions, Privilege Motion, Resolutions and the Whip
[32:7:0] - **Motion** = a **formal proposal by a member** for the decision of the House (to do something permissible); no discussion on a matter of public importance except on a motion with the Presiding Officer's consent.
[32:7:1] - Kinds: **substantive** (self-contained, e.g. impeachment), **substitute**, **subsidiary** (ancillary, superseding, amendment).
[32:7:2] - **Privilege motion**: moved when a **Minister withholds facts of a case or gives wrong / distorted facts** – breach of privilege; purpose: **censure the minister**; **either House**; referred to the **Committee of Privileges**; does **not** force the Government to resign.
[32:7:3] # Resolutions
[32:7:4] - Always a **substantive motion**; mover cannot withdraw it except by **leave of the House**; same subject barred for **one year**.
[32:7:5] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [32:7:5.0] Private member's | Alternate Fridays, afternoon sitting
  [32:7:5.1] Government | Moved by a minister – treaties, policies, committee recommendations
  [32:7:5.2] Statutory | Moved under a constitutional or statutory provision
[32:7:6] # The Whip
[32:7:7] - Office of Whip is **not in the Constitution, the Rules or a statute** – rests on **parliamentary conventions**. The **1998 Act** (Leaders and Chief Whips of Recognised Parties and Groups in Parliament (Facilities) Act) only gives **facilities** – it does not put the office into the Rules.
[32:7:8] - **Every party** – ruling or opposition – has its own whips (APPSC G1 2024: **only statement III** valid).
[32:7:9] - Government Chief Whip in the Lok Sabha = the (Cabinet) **Minister of Parliamentary Affairs**, who keeps contact with the Chief Whips of parties in both Houses.
[32:7:10] - Defying the whip → disqualification under the **Tenth Schedule**.

=== NOTES 2:33 | Legislative Procedure – Ordinary, Money, Financial and Constitutional Amendment Bills; Joint Sitting
## SUB 0 | Money Bill – Articles 109 and 110
[33:0:0] # Definition – Art. 110(1)
[33:0:1] - A Bill is a Money Bill if it contains **only** provisions on: (a) imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any **tax**; (b) regulation of **borrowing** or the **giving of any guarantee** by the Government of India; (c) **custody** of the Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund, payments into or withdrawals from them; (d) **appropriation** out of the CFI; (e) declaring expenditure **charged** on the CFI; (f) receipt, custody or issue of money of the CFI or Public Account, or the **audit of accounts of the Union or of a State**; (g) incidental matters.
[33:0:2] - **Not** Money Bills merely because they provide for (Art. 110(2)): **fines/penalties**, **fees for licences or services**, **taxes by local bodies** for local purposes.
[33:0:3] - **Speaker** decides finally whether a Bill is a Money Bill and **certifies** it (Art. 110(3)); the decision cannot be questioned in any court, in either House or by the President.
[33:0:4] # Procedure – Art. 109
[33:0:5] - Introduced **only in the Lok Sabha**, **only by a minister**, **only on the President's recommendation** (not the PM's). Never in the Rajya Sabha or a joint sitting.
[33:0:6] - **Rajya Sabha**: **cannot amend or reject** it; can only make **recommendations** and must return it within **14 days** (two weeks) – the maximum it can delay it.
[33:0:7] - **Lok Sabha may accept or reject** all or any of the RS recommendations – it is **not bound** to accept them, and no 14-day limit applies to the LS.
[33:0:8] - RS amends it substantially → the LS **may still proceed**, accepting or not accepting the recommendations. Not returned within 14 days → **deemed passed by both Houses** in the form passed by the LS, and sent to the President.
[33:0:9] - So **RS approval is not necessary** to pass a Money Bill; RS "has no power" over it beyond delay of 14 days.
[33:0:10] - **President**: may **give or withhold assent** but **cannot return** a Money Bill for reconsideration (normally assents, as it came on his recommendation).
[33:0:11] - **No joint sitting** for a Money Bill.
[33:0:12] - **Aadhaar Bill, 2016** was introduced and passed as a **Money Bill**; upheld in **K.S. Puttaswamy (2018)**; the Money Bill question went to a 7-judge bench in **Rojer Mathew (2019)**.
[33:0:13] - **Appropriation Bill** is a Money Bill (Art. 114).
[33:0:14] # Rajya Sabha in financial matters
[33:0:15] TABLE head: Rajya Sabha can | Rajya Sabha cannot
  [33:0:15.0] Discuss the Annual Financial Statement (budget) | Vote on the Demands for Grants (only LS votes, Art. 113)
  [33:0:15.1] Recommend changes to a Money Bill within 14 days | Amend or reject a Money Bill
  [33:0:15.2] Amend or reject Financial Bills I and II | Have a Money Bill or the Annual Financial Statement introduced first in it

## SUB 1 | Joint Sitting of the Two Houses – Article 108
[33:1:0] # When it is held
[33:1:1] - **Article 108** = joint sitting; **Art. 109** = Money Bill procedure; **Art. 110** = definition of a Money Bill; **Art. 118(4)** = Speaker presides. "Art. 109 provides for the joint sitting" is **false**.
[33:1:2] - **Deadlock grounds** (after one House passes a Bill and sends it to the other): (1) the other House **rejects** it; (2) the Houses **finally disagree on amendments**; (3) more than **six months** pass without the other House passing it (periods of prorogation or adjournment for more than **4 consecutive days** are not counted).
[33:1:3] - "Both Houses agree to a joint sitting for speedy legislation" is **not** a ground – a joint sitting is only a deadlock-breaking device.
[33:1:4] - **Applies to:** **ordinary Bills** and **Financial Bills (I and II)**. **Not to:** **Money Bills** (the Lok Sabha has **overriding powers**, so no deadlock can arise) and **Constitution Amendment Bills** (each House must pass them separately by special majority).
[33:1:5] - A joint sitting is **not** used to elect the President or Vice-President.
[33:1:6] # Procedure
[33:1:7] TABLE head: Point | Fact
  [33:1:7.0] Who summons | President – he "notifies his intention" to summon the Houses (Art. 108(1)); not the Speaker, PM or Chairman
  [33:1:7.1] Who presides | Speaker of the Lok Sabha (Art. 118(4)) → Deputy Speaker → Deputy Chairman of RS → person chosen by members present
  [33:1:7.2] Chairman of RS (Vice-President) | Never presides – he is not a member of either House
  [33:1:7.3] Quorum | One-tenth of total members of both Houses
  [33:1:7.4] Majority | Simple majority of members of both Houses present and voting (Art. 108(4))
  [33:1:7.5] Rules followed | Rules of Procedure of the Lok Sabha
  [33:1:7.6] New amendments | Only those causing final disagreement or made necessary by delay
[33:1:8] - After the President notifies his intention, **neither House can proceed further** with the Bill.
[33:1:9] - If the President **does not summon** the joint sitting, the **Bill comes to an end** (APPSC G2).
[33:1:10] - Bill already **lapsed** on dissolution → no joint sitting. But if the **Lok Sabha is dissolved after** the President has notified the joint sitting, the Bill does not lapse and the **joint sitting is still held**.
[33:1:11] - The Lok Sabha, with greater numbers, normally wins at a joint sitting.

## SUB 2 | Ordinary Bills – Kinds, Stages and the President's Assent
[33:2:0] # Public vs Private Bills
[33:2:1] - All legislative proposals come before Parliament as **Bills**; a Bill becomes an **Act** only after the **President's assent** (Art. 111).
[33:2:2] TABLE head: Public (Government) Bill | Private Member's Bill
  [33:2:2.0] Introduced by a minister (of either House) | By any MP who is not a minister (not necessarily nominated)
  [33:2:2.1] Reflects government policy | Does not reflect government policy
  [33:2:2.2] 7 days' notice | 1 month's notice
  [33:2:2.3] Drafted by the department with the Law Department | Drafted by the member
  [33:2:2.4] Rejection = loss of confidence in government | Rejection has no such implication
[33:2:3] - Both are governed by the **same general procedure** and pass through the **same stages**.
[33:2:4] - Only **14** Private Members' Bills have become law, the last in **1970** – none "recently for the first time".
[33:2:5] - **9 Dec 2022:** CPI(M) MP **Bikash Ranjan Bhattacharyya** introduced the private member's **National Judicial Commission Bill, 2022** in the Rajya Sabha.
[33:2:6] # Stages – three readings in each House
[33:2:7] TABLE head: Stage | What happens
  [33:2:7.0] First reading | Leave to introduce; Bill introduced and published in the Gazette
  [33:2:7.1] Second reading | General discussion, committee stage, clause-by-clause consideration with amendments
  [33:2:7.2] Third reading | Bill accepted or rejected as a whole – the formal vote/division
  [33:2:7.3] Second House | Same three readings; may pass, amend, reject, or keep pending (6 months → deadlock)
  [33:2:7.4] Assent | President: assent, withhold, or return once for reconsideration (suspensive veto)
[33:2:8] - An ordinary ("legislative") Bill can be introduced in **either House**.
[33:2:9] - **Defamation Bill, 1988**: passed by the Lok Sabha but **withdrawn** before the Rajya Sabha took it up, after press protests.
[33:2:10] # President's options on Bills (Art. 111)
[33:2:11] TABLE head: Bill | Assent | Withhold | Return
  [33:2:11.0] Ordinary / Financial I and II | Yes | Yes | Yes (once)
  [33:2:11.1] Money Bill | Yes | Yes | No
  [33:2:11.2] Constitution Amendment Bill | Must assent (24th Amendment, 1971) | No | No
[33:2:12] - President's powers also include summoning a joint sitting and **proroguing** the Houses and **dissolving the Lok Sabha** – he **cannot dissolve "Parliament"** (the Rajya Sabha is permanent).
[33:2:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams (src/polity/nextgen-polity-full.txt) p.50-52, p.356-358, p.360; Laxmikanth, Indian Polity 8th ed. (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt) p.355-358; Deccan Chronicle / LiveLaw 9 Dec 2022 (NJC Bill); ThePrint (Defamation Bill 1988)
[33:2:14] - **Art. 312**: a new All-India Service is created only after a **Rajya Sabha** resolution (two-thirds present and voting) – a special power of the RS, not a Bill.
[33:2:15] - The LS **Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions** also classifies such Bills and examines private members' **Constitution Amendment Bills** before introduction.
[33:2:16] - **Pocket veto** is possible because the Constitution fixes **no time limit** for the President's decision on a Bill (e.g. Zail Singh, Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986); India has no qualified veto.
[33:2:17] - **Time for Private Members' Bills**: in the **Lok Sabha** the **Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions** allots it; the **Rajya Sabha** has no such committee – its **Business Advisory Committee** does.
## SUB 3 | Financial Bills and the President's Prior Recommendation
[33:3:0] # Three kinds of financial bills (Art. 117)
[33:3:1] TABLE head: Feature | Money Bill (Art. 110) | Financial Bill I (Art. 117(1)) | Financial Bill II (Art. 117(3))
  [33:3:1.0] Content | Only Art. 110 matters | Art. 110 matters + general legislation | Expenditure from CFI, no Art. 110 matter
  [33:3:1.1] Where introduced | LS only | LS only | Either House
  [33:3:1.2] President's recommendation | At introduction | At introduction | Only before consideration
  [33:3:1.3] Rajya Sabha | Recommends in 14 days | Can amend/reject | Can amend/reject
  [33:3:1.4] Joint sitting | No | Yes | Yes
  [33:3:1.5] President can return | No | Yes | Yes
[33:3:2] - **Every Money Bill is a Financial Bill, but not every Financial Bill is a Money Bill.**
[33:3:3] - **Article 117** = "special provisions as to Financial Bills"; Art. 117(1): no Bill **imposing a tax** can be introduced except on the **President's recommendation**, and not in the RS. An amendment **reducing or abolishing** a tax needs no recommendation.
[33:3:4] - A Bill that merely **involves expenditure** (no Art. 110 matter) can be **initiated in either House**.
[33:3:5] - **Finance Bill** = gives effect to the Budget's tax proposals, i.e. how the government collects revenue for the year; usually certified as a Money Bill – so exam keys treat "Finance Bill" as a Money Bill (14 days; President must sign, cannot return).
[33:3:6] # Bills needing the President's prior recommendation/sanction
[33:3:7] TABLE head: Bill | Article
  [33:3:7.0] Money Bill; Financial Bill I | Arts. 109, 117(1)
  [33:3:7.1] New State, alteration of areas, boundaries, names | Art. 3 (either House; simple majority; not an Art. 368 amendment)
  [33:3:7.2] Bill affecting taxation in which States are interested | Art. 274
  [33:3:7.3] State Bill restricting inter-State trade | Art. 304(b)
[33:3:8] - **Ordinary Bills** need **no** prior recommendation (except the listed cases); nor do Constitution Amendment Bills.
[33:3:9] - A Bill for a **new State** can be introduced in **either House** – "must be first introduced in the Rajya Sabha" is false.

[33:3:11] - A **Financial Bill I** needs **no Speaker's certificate** (only Money Bills are certified), though, like a Money Bill, it starts only in the LS, by a minister, on the President's recommendation.

=== NOTES 2:34 | Budget in Parliament – Stages, Cut Motions, Appropriation and Finance Bills, Funds
## SUB 0 | Annual Financial Statement (Art. 112) – Budget Basics, Presentation and Firsts
[34:0:0] # Article 112 – the "Budget"
[34:0:1] - **Constitutional name:** the Budget = **Annual Financial Statement (AFS)**, **Article 112** – a statement of the **estimated receipts and expenditure of the Government of India for that year** (not the Economic Survey, not last year's surplus).
[34:0:2] - **"Budget" not in the Constitution:** the word **Budget** nowhere appears; Annual Financial Statement, Appropriation Bill and Consolidated Fund of India do. (Budget ← French **"bougette"** = little bag.)
[34:0:3] - **Laid by:** the **President** "causes" the AFS to be laid before **both Houses** every financial year; the **Finance Minister** presents it in the **Lok Sabha** (Union Budget 2023-24: **Nirmala Sitharaman**).
[34:0:4] - **Financial year:** **1 April – 31 March**; Budget shown as **Revenue Budget + Capital Budget**; it must show **charged** and **voted** expenditure separately (Art. 112(2)).
[34:0:5] - **Prepared by:** the **Budget Division, Department of Economic Affairs (DEA)**, Ministry of Finance – not Revenue, Expenditure or Financial Services; process starts with the **Budget Circular** (usually **September**).
[34:0:6] - **Halwa ceremony** (North Block) marks the start of the secrecy/printing of Budget documents.
[34:0:7] - **Economic Survey:** prepared by the Finance Ministry (DEA), presented **before** the Budget (a day or a few days earlier).

## SUB 1 | Consolidated Fund, Public Account and Contingency Fund (Arts. 266-267)
[34:1:0] TABLE head: Basis | Consolidated Fund of India | Public Account of India | Contingency Fund of India
  [34:1:0.0] Article (Union / States) | 266(1) / 266(1) | 266(2) / 266(2) | 267(1) / 267(2)
  [34:1:0.1] Receipts | All revenues (taxes and other receipts), all loans (Treasury Bills, ways and means advances), loan repayments | Money held in trust: provident funds, small savings, judicial deposits, savings bank deposits, remittances | Imprest for unforeseen expenditure
  [34:1:0.2] Withdrawal | Only under appropriation made by law – Parliament's authorisation (Art. 266(3), 114) | Executive action, no vote of Parliament (banking transactions) | Advance by the President, before Parliament's approval

[34:1:1] - **All revenues** of the Union by taxes and other receipts → **Consolidated Fund of India** (not Public Account or Contingency Fund).
[34:1:2] - **Withdrawal from the CFI:** authorisation must come from **Parliament** (an Appropriation Act) – not the President, PM, Finance Minister or CAG. In India the CAG has **no "comptroller" power** over issue of money; the Ministries draw under the Appropriation Act.
[34:1:3] - **Article for "no money withdrawn from the CFI without Parliament's sanction":** **Art. 266(3)** (appropriation "in accordance with law") read with **Art. 114(3)**.
[34:1:4] - **Contingency Fund of India:** created by **Parliament by law** – **Contingency Fund of India Act, 1950**; at the **President's disposal**, who **advances** money for **unforeseen** expenditure (also additional/excess needs) **pending authorisation** by Parliament – spent **before** Parliament's approval. Corpus **Rs 30,000 crore** from **2021-22** (Rs 500 crore from 2005; Rs 50 crore earlier).
[34:1:5] - **State Contingency Fund:** established by the **State Legislature** by law (**Art. 267(2)**); placed at the disposal of the **Governor**, who operates it (not the CM or State FM).
[34:1:6] - **CAG** audits all three funds of the Union and the States.

## SUB 2 | Charged Expenditure on the Consolidated Fund (Art. 112(3))
[34:2:0] - **Charged expenditure** = expenditure from the CFI that the Constitution makes **non-votable** – **not submitted to the vote** of Parliament but **can be discussed** in either House (Art. 113(1)); it still needs an Appropriation Act (Art. 114).
[34:2:1] # Charged on the Consolidated Fund of India – Art. 112(3)
[34:2:2] - **President** – emoluments, allowances, office expenses (Art. 59(3)).
[34:2:3] - **Chairman and Deputy Chairman** of the RS; **Speaker and Deputy Speaker** of the LS – salaries and allowances (Art. 97).
[34:2:4] - **Supreme Court judges** – salaries, allowances, **pensions** (Art. 125); SC administrative expenses.
[34:2:5] - **High Court judges** – **only pensions** (Art. 112(3)(d)(iii)); their **salaries** are charged on the **Consolidated Fund of the State** (Art. 202(3)(d); salaries fixed under Art. 221).
[34:2:6] - **CAG** – salary, allowances, pension and office expenses (Art. 148).
[34:2:7] - **UPSC** chairman and members – salaries, allowances, pensions (Art. 322); a **State PSC's expenses** are charged on the **Consolidated Fund of the State** (Art. 322).
[34:2:8] - **Debt charges** of the Government of India; sums to satisfy a **judgment, decree or arbitral award**; anything Parliament declares charged.
[34:2:9] # Not charged (voted)
[34:2:10] - **Prime Minister and Ministers** – salaries fixed by Parliament (Art. 75(6)); **voted**, not charged.
[34:2:11] - **Election Commission** – salaries and expenses are **voted**, not charged.
[34:2:12] - **Vice-President:** no salary **as VP**; he draws it **as Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** – that salary is **charged on the CFI** (not paid from the Public Account).
[34:2:13] - **Governor:** emoluments charged on the **Consolidated Fund of the State** (Art. 158(3)), not the CFI; one Governor for two or more States → shared in the proportion the **President** fixes by order (**Art. 158(3A)**). Governors' pensions are not in Art. 112(3).
[34:2:14] - **Second Schedule** – emoluments, allowances and privileges; **Third Schedule** – forms of oaths.

## SUB 3 | Stages of the Budget, Demands for Grants, Guillotine and Cut Motions
[34:3:0] # Six stages (Laxmikanth)
[34:3:1] - **1. Presentation** of the Budget (no discussion that day).
[34:3:2] - **2. General discussion** – both Houses, 3-4 days; no cut motion, no vote.
[34:3:3] - **3. Scrutiny by the Departmentally Related Standing Committees** (House adjourned 3-4 weeks).
[34:3:4] - **4. Voting on Demands for Grants** (Art. 113) – **Lok Sabha only**; estimates of expenditure are submitted as **Demands for Grants**; it may pass, reject or **reduce**, never increase; a **demand becomes a grant after it is voted**.
[34:3:5] - **5. Passing of the Appropriation Bill** (Art. 114).
[34:3:6] - **6. Passing of the Finance Bill** – last stage; gives effect to the tax proposals.
[34:3:7] - **Sequence:** Presentation → DRSC scrutiny → **Appropriation Bill → Finance Bill**. The **vote on account** is passed **after the general discussion** (before the demands are voted).
[34:3:8] - **Not stages** of normal financial legislation: vote on account, vote of credit, exceptional grants.
[34:3:9] - **Guillotine:** on the last allotted day the **Speaker** puts **all remaining demands** to vote – those **not discussed for want of time** – at once.
[34:3:10] # Cut motions – motions to reduce a demand
[34:3:11] TABLE head: Cut motion | Reduces the demand | Purpose
  [34:3:11.0] Policy cut | To Re 1 | Disapproval of the policy behind the demand
  [34:3:11.1] Economy cut | By a specified amount | Economy in expenditure
  [34:3:11.2] Token cut | By Rs 100 | Air a specific grievance within the Government's responsibility
[34:3:12] - **Object:** to **move a proposal to reduce expenditure** in the Budget (a demand for grant) and draw the House's attention to a matter.
[34:3:13] - Moved **only in the Lok Sabha**, at the **voting on Demands for Grants**; **admitted at the discretion of the Speaker**; cannot relate to charged expenditure.
[34:3:14] - **Passage** = loss of confidence → the Government is expected to **resign**.
[34:3:15] > Exam angle: guillotine = undiscussed demands; cut motion admitted by the Speaker; order "I, II, IV, III" (Appropriation before Finance).

## SUB 4 | Appropriation Bill, Finance Bill and Parliamentary Control over Finance
[34:4:0] - **Appropriation Bill:** authorises withdrawal from the CFI for **voted grants + charged expenditure**; must be **passed by both Houses** (as a Money Bill) and assented to; **Art. 114(3)**: no withdrawal except under it – even charged expenditure needs it.
[34:4:1] - **No amendment** may vary the amount or alter the destination of a voted grant, or vary charged expenditure.
[34:4:2] - **Automatic repeal clause:** since the **Appropriation Act, 2016** (after the Appropriation Acts (Repeal) Act, 2016 repealed 758 old Acts) Appropriation Acts carry an **automatic repeal (sunset) clause**.
[34:4:3] - **Finance Bill:** gives effect to the tax proposals; amendments to **reduce or abolish** a tax **can** be moved, **not** to increase it; must be enacted within **75 days** (Provisional Collection of Taxes Act, 1931). Changes in existing tax rates also need law (Art. 265) – usually via the Finance Bill.
[34:4:4] - **Parliament's control over public finance:** placing the AFS; withdrawal only after the Appropriation Act; supplementary grants and vote on account; the Finance Bill. India has **no Parliamentary Budget Office** (so UPSC 2012 answer = 1, 2, 3 and 5).
[34:4:5] - **Department of Economic Affairs** (not Revenue) prepares the Budget; **Public Account** disbursements need **no** Parliament authorisation (UPSC 2015 → only "no withdrawal from the CFI without Parliament").
[34:4:6] - **Money Bill (Art. 110(1)):** covers custody of the CFI/Contingency Fund and appropriation out of the **CFI** – not "appropriation out of the Contingency Fund".

  [34:5:0.4] Vote of credit | 116 | Blank cheque for an unexpected/indefinite demand
  [34:5:0.5] Exceptional | 116 | Not part of the current service of any year
  [34:5:0.6] Token grant | – | New service met by re-appropriation; token sum voted by the LS
[34:5:1] - **Vote on account:** needed **when the regular Budget will not be passed in time** (e.g. an election year); it lets the Government **withdraw money from the CFI** before the Appropriation Act.
[34:5:2] - **Since the 1 February Budget (2017)** a vote on account is normally needed only in **election years** (with an Interim Budget) – it has not become ineffectual.
