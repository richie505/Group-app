## SUB 1 | Basic Facts – Bronze Age, Area, Rivers, Non-Aryan Character
[5:1:0] - **Proto-historic** (script undeciphered), **Bronze Age**; India's **first urbanisation**.
[5:1:1] - Area about **1.3 million sq km** – larger than Egypt and Mesopotamia; population ~5 million; matured in **Sindh and Punjab** and spread **south and east**.
[5:1:2] - Highest concentration of sites on the **Ghaggar–Hakra** system (about 250 sites, 6 cities).
[5:1:3] - **Rivers:** Sindhu, **Chenab, Jhelum**, Ravi, Sutlej, Ghaggar – up to the **Hindon** (Alamgirpur); **not the Ganga**.
[5:1:4] - **Main sites:** Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Ropar, Kalibangan; roads, houses and drains were planned.
[5:1:5] - **Metals:** copper, bronze, gold, **silver** (first use of silver in India), lead, tin – but **no iron**.
[5:1:6] - **Non-Aryan:** Harappan culture was **urban**; the Rigvedic Aryans were **pastoral/rural**.
[5:1:7] TABLE head: Period | Economy
  [5:1:7.0] Indus Valley | Urban
  [5:1:7.1] Rigvedic | Pastoral
  [5:1:7.2] Later Vedic | Agrarian
[5:1:8] - **Not Harappan:** **Sohgaura** (Gorakhpur – Mauryan copper plate), **Pataliputra** (Magadha capital), Mehrgarh (Neolithic), Burzahom (Neolithic pit dwellings).
[5:1:9] - Assertion–reason: Mohenjodaro and Harappa are "dead" cities **and** were found by excavation – both true, but R does not explain A.
[5:1:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History Part 1 p.4-5, p.7; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.26-27, p.79; app notes 1:5 (sub 0, 3, 6)

## SUB 3 | Discovery and Excavators
[5:3:0] - **1826**: **Charles Masson** described the Harappa ruins (1826-38). **1853, 1856**: **Alexander Cunningham** saw pottery, tools and a seal but missed their value.
[5:3:1] - **1857**: Harappan bricks used as ballast for the **Karachi–Lahore** railway. **1912**: **J. Fleet** published Harappan seals.
[5:3:2] - **Sir John Marshall** (DG, ASI 1902–28) directed the 1921-22 campaign; announced the civilisation in 1924; coined "Indus Valley Civilisation".
[5:3:3] - The two Indian discoverers: **Daya Ram Sahni** and **Rakhaldas Banerji**.

## SUB 4 | Largest Sites, UNESCO and Recent Developments
[5:4:0] - **Rakhigarhi** (Hisar, Haryana; ~550 ha) = **largest Harappan site in India**; **Dholavira** second; **Mohenjodaro** largest in Pakistan. Five largest: Harappa, Mohenjodaro, Ganweriwala, Rakhigarhi, Dholavira.
[5:4:1] - **Dholavira:** UNESCO WHS **2021** – India's **40th**, Gujarat's 4th, the **first IVC site in India**; absence of encroachment helped.
[5:4:2] - **Mohenjodaro:** UNESCO WHS since **1980**. **Rakhigarhi:** on the UNESCO **Tentative List** (Jun 2026).
[5:4:3] - **Union Budget 2020-21:** **five** iconic archaeological sites – **Rakhigarhi, Hastinapur, Shivsagar, Dholavira, Adichanallur**.
[5:4:4] - Jun 2026: Haryana protected **Tighrana and Mitathal** (Bhiwani) under the **Haryana Ancient and Historical Monuments Act, 1964**.
[5:4:5] - 23 Jun 2026: ASI transferred Rakhigarhi's **56 skeletons** to the **Anthropological Survey of India**; a 4,500-year-old skeleton gave the ancient DNA study.
[5:4:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History Part 1 p.16-17; NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.22; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.80; app notes 1:5 (sub 8)

[5:6:0] TABLE head: Phase | Dates | Features
  [5:6:0.0] Early Harappan | 3300–2600 BCE | Kot Diji, Amri, Sothi; villages
  [5:6:0.1] Mature Harappan | 2600–1900 BCE | Planned cities, 1:2:4 bricks, script

=== NOTES 1:6 | Ivc Town Planning – Grid Layout, Citadel and Lower Town, Drainage, Great Bath, Granaries
## SUB 0 | Hallmarks of the IVC – Town Planning, Burnt Bricks, Bronze Age
[6:0:0] # What the exams ask
[6:0:1] - **Hallmark / specialisation:** **town planning** (urban centres, grid, drains) is the single most distinctive feature of the IVC (APPSC G2 2011) – not "all of these".
[6:0:2] - **Most significant feature:** **burnt-brick buildings** – large-scale use of burnt bricks in almost all constructions (BPSC 2018, JPSC 2023). Egypt used mainly dried bricks; no "first true arches", no buildings of worship.
[6:0:3] - **Known for (UK UDA 2003):** town planning, Mohenjodaro and Harappa, agriculture and craft industry (bead, shell, metal factories) → "all of these".
[6:0:4] - **No temples or palaces** clearly found, unlike Mesopotamia and Egypt.
[6:0:5] - Standard bricks, grid, streets and drains point to a **civic authority**; the absence of palaces or temples does **not** disprove it.
[6:0:6] - **Not known:** the **true arch** (only corbelled), and **iron** – IVC is a **Bronze Age** culture; iron came c. 1000 BC (Later Vedic). Pillars (Mohenjodaro assembly hall, 20 pillars), wells, drains and bows-and-arrows were all known.
[6:0:7] # IVC vs Vedic / Aryan
[6:0:8] TABLE head: Point | IVC (Harappan) | Vedic
  [6:0:8.0] Life | Urban, planned cities | Rural, pastoral
  [6:0:8.1] Script | Pictographic (undeciphered) | No script
  [6:0:8.2] Metal | Copper, bronze; no iron | Iron in Later Vedic age
  [6:0:8.3] Pottery | Red ware painted in black | Painted Grey Ware
[6:0:9] - **Why IVC is placed before the Aryans (UPPCS 1990):** **pottery** – the stratified pottery sequence (from Mehrgarh to the black-on-red Harappan ware) lies below the Painted Grey Ware of the Vedic people.
[6:0:10] - **IVC different from Vedic (UPPCS 2004):** city amenities + pictographic script + no iron → "all of the above".
[6:0:11] > Trap: "the IVC knew iron" is always the false statement.
[6:0:12] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.83-86, p.101, p.107; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.5, p.7; old app notes row 6
## SUB 1 | Wells, Drainage and Water Management
[6:1:0] TABLE head: Site | Feature
  [6:1:0.0] Mohenjodaro | About 700 brick wells, many inside houses → most wells (UPPCS 2004)
  [6:1:0.1] Dholavira | Water management: 16 reservoirs, dams, embankments, channels; some rock-cut (UPSC 2021, CDS 2012, UPPCS 2010)
  [6:1:0.2] Kalibangan | Many houses had their own wells; wooden drains
  [6:1:0.3] Banawali | No systematic drainage
  [6:1:0.4] Allahdino (Sindh) | Well irrigation
[6:1:1] - **Drainage** = the most advanced IVC feature: house drains → sump/cesspit → **covered** street drains (bricks or stone slabs) with **manholes** → **soak pits** or main channels; streets sloped for flow; better than contemporary Mesopotamia and Egypt. Lothal: mud-brick houses but burnt-brick drains.
[6:1:2] - **Dholavira reservoirs:** water from the **Manhar and Mansar** rivulets; huge rock-cut reservoir about **73–79 m long, 7 m deep**; stepwells cut in sandstone = Harappan **rock-cut architecture** (UPPSC 2006).
[6:1:3] > Trap: Dholavira = reservoirs (water harvesting); Mohenjodaro = wells.
[6:1:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.85-86, p.102-103; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.14; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.21-22; old app notes row 6

## SUB 3 | Bricks, Streets and Houses
[6:3:0] # Bricks
[6:3:1] - **Burnt bricks** common; **mud bricks** for fillings and platforms and at **Kalibangan**. Both baked and raw bricks used.
[6:3:2] - Standard ratio **1:2:4** (thickness : width : length) = 4:2:1.
[6:3:3] - **Same ratio, different sizes:** house bricks **7.5×15×30 cm** (≈7×14×28), city-wall bricks **10×20×40 cm** → "the same size of brick for all constructions" is **false**.
[6:3:4] - **Dholavira**: **sandstone and limestone** blocks, not baked brick.
[6:3:5] - **Ornamental bricks** = Kalibangan; **curved bricks** = Chanhudaro.
[6:3:6] # Streets
[6:3:7] - **Grid layout**: streets ran north-south and east-west, crossing at **right angles**, splitting the city into blocks → **broad and straight** (not narrow, zig-zag).
[6:3:8] - Main streets about **10 m (30 feet)** wide (Rocket: main road of Mohenjodaro ≈9.15 m). **Banawali** = only city with **radial streets**.
[6:3:9] # Houses
[6:3:10] - Rooms round a **central courtyard**; brick-paved **bathrooms** and toilets; no ground-level windows (privacy).
[6:3:11] - Doors and windows opened on **side lanes**, not main streets (Lothal is the exception – entrances on the main street).
[6:3:12] - **Storeys:** most houses single-storeyed but **staircases** show many had an upper storey. CGPSC 2022 key treats "mostly all houses single storey" as untrue along with "same size of bricks" → **(i) and (ii)**; "main roads ~10 m" is true.
[6:3:13] - **Arch:** Harappans used only the corbelled arch, never the true arch (MPPSC 2009).
[6:3:14] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.85-86; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.5; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.19; old app notes row 6

## SUB 1 | Lothal – Port Town, Dockyard and the Maritime Heritage Complex
[7:1:0] - **Lothal** (Ahmedabad, Gujarat) on the **Bhogava** (tributary of the Sabarmati), near the **Gulf of Khambhat** = **the port city** of the IVC (UPPCS 1999, BPSC 2011, SI 2019) – not Harappa, Kalibangan, Mohenjodaro or Ropar.
[7:1:1] - **Dockyard:** brick-lined basin **218 m × 37 m** with sluice gates – the world's earliest tidal dock; warehouse on a raised platform with seals and sealings.
[7:1:2] - **Discovered 1954, excavated 1955–60 by S.R. Rao** (not Wheeler, not Daya Ram Sahni).
[7:1:3] - **Other finds:** rice husk, fire altars, bead-making factory, Persian Gulf (button) seal, ship on a seal, terracotta ship, copper dog, chess-like game set, three double burials, painted jar (cunning fox story), ivory scale.
[7:1:4] - Entrance of houses on the **main street** (other cities: side lanes). Called "Mini Harappa".
[7:1:5] - **Other ports:** Rangpur, Somnath (Prabhas Patan), Balakot, Kuntasi, Khirasara, Sutkagendor.
[7:1:6] # National Maritime Heritage Complex (NMHC)
[7:1:7] - **NMHC** is being built at **Lothal**, Gujarat (not Kozhikode, Visakhapatnam, Sindhudurg, Kanyakumari, Puri or Panjim) – Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways; approved by the **Union Cabinet, October 2024**.
[7:1:8] - **As of Sep 2026:** Phase 1A about **82%** complete, target **31 Oct 2026**; Phase 1B to add eight galleries and a lighthouse museum.

## SUB 0 | Metals and Raw Materials → Source
[8:0:0] - **Copper = the first metal used by man** (not iron, bronze, gold or silver); chalcolithic cultures began with it (APPSC G2 2011, RAS 2012).
[8:0:1] - Harappans used **copper, bronze, gold, silver, lead**; **iron NOT found** at any Harappan site (CGPSC 2011) – a Bronze Age culture.
[8:0:2] - **Bronze = copper + tin**; tools, weapons and the **Dancing Girl** (lost-wax casting). Pure copper objects far outnumber bronze ones.
[8:0:3] - Harappans **pioneered silver** use; silver imported (Afghanistan).

  [8:0:4.0] Copper | Khetri mines (Rajasthan); also Oman (Magan)
  [8:0:4.1] Tin | Afghanistan, Iran
  [8:0:4.2] Gold | Karnataka (Kolar), Afghanistan
  [8:0:4.3] Lapis lazuli | Badakhshan via Shortughai (Afghanistan)
  [8:0:4.4] Carnelian | Bharuch / Saurashtra (Gujarat)
  [8:0:4.5] Steatite | South Rajasthan, north Gujarat

## SUB 0 | Seals and Sealings – Uses and Value As A Source
[9:0:0] - The seal is the **most distinctive artefact** of the IVC (APPSC FRO; APPSC GS 2024) – not megaliths, DNA or royal palaces.
[9:0:1] - With the script unread, **seals** are the **main source** of knowledge about the Harappan people (RAS 1996); broadly, the culture is known only from **archaeological excavations** (UPPSC 1993) – no inscriptions read, no religious books.
[9:0:2] - A **sealing** is the clay impression of a seal pressed on the knot of a package; an intact sealing proved the goods were untouched and named the sender.
[9:0:3] - **Uses:** facilitating **long-distance communication** and trade (APPSC G1 → **seals and sealings**), authentication, **ownership**, amulets – **not coins**.
[9:0:4] - **Uniform seals and weights** = main proof of **commercial and economic development** and regulated trade (SSC CPO 2002).

=== NOTES 1:10 | Ivc Society and Polity – Stratification, Burials, Absence of Temples and Palaces
## SUB 0 | Burials – Types and Sites
[10:0:0] - **Common burial system (HPPSC 2012):** **extended earth burial with the head generally to the north**, and **grave goods** (pots, ornaments, copper mirrors) buried with the dead → "both a and b". Sitting-posture burial is not Harappan.
[10:0:1] - **Three forms:** complete (extended) burials, fractional burials, post-cremation burials.
[10:0:2] TABLE head: Burial practice | Site
  [10:0:2.0] Extended burials with goods; coffin burial | R-37, Harappa (head generally to the north)
  [10:0:2.1] Post-cremation / fractional, Late Harappan | Cemetery H, Harappa
  [10:0:2.2] Fractional (bones buried after exposure of the body) | Mohenjodaro, Harappa
  [10:0:2.3] Joint / double burial | Lothal (UPPSC 2016)
  [10:0:2.4] Dog buried with human, oval pit burials | Ropar
  [10:0:2.5] Pot burials; megalith-type grave with a big rock | Surkotada
  [10:0:2.6] Large cemetery of human burials (125 graves, N–S) | Sanauli (Sinauli), Baghpat, UP (UP Lower 2004)
  [10:0:2.7] Double burials; Mound 7 cemetery; women with jasper, agate beads, shell bangles | Rakhigarhi
[10:0:3] - **Kalibangan – three types:** extended burials in rectangular pits; pot burials; circular pits with grave goods but **no skeleton**.
