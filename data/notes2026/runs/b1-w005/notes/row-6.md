=== Hallmarks of the IVC – Town Planning, Burnt Bricks, Bronze Age
# What the exams ask
- **Hallmark / specialisation:** **town planning** (urban centres, grid, drains) is the single most distinctive feature of the IVC (APPSC G2 2011) – not "all of these".
- **Most significant feature:** **burnt-brick buildings** – large-scale use of burnt bricks in almost all constructions (BPSC 2018, JPSC 2023). Egypt used mainly dried bricks; no "first true arches", no buildings of worship.
- **Known for (UK UDA 2003):** town planning, Mohenjodaro and Harappa, agriculture and craft industry (bead, shell, metal factories) → "all of these".
- **No temples or palaces** clearly found, unlike Mesopotamia and Egypt.
- Standard bricks, grid, streets and drains point to a **civic authority**; the absence of palaces or temples does **not** disprove it.
- **Not known:** the **true arch** (only corbelled), and **iron** – IVC is a **Bronze Age** culture; iron came c. 1000 BC (Later Vedic). Pillars (Mohenjodaro assembly hall, 20 pillars), wells, drains and bows-and-arrows were all known.
# IVC vs Vedic / Aryan
| Point | IVC (Harappan) | Vedic |
|---|---|---|
| Life | **Urban**, planned cities | Rural, pastoral |
| Script | **Pictographic** (undeciphered) | No script |
| Metal | Copper, bronze; **no iron** | Iron in Later Vedic age |
| Pottery | **Red ware painted in black** | **Painted Grey Ware** |
- **Why IVC is placed before the Aryans (UPPCS 1990):** **pottery** – the stratified pottery sequence (from Mehrgarh to the black-on-red Harappan ware) lies below the Painted Grey Ware of the Vedic people.
- **IVC different from Vedic (UPPCS 2004):** city amenities + pictographic script + no iron → "all of the above".
> Trap: "the IVC knew iron" is always the false statement.

=== Wells, Drainage and Water Management
| Site | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Mohenjodaro** | **About 700 brick wells**, many inside houses → **most wells** (UPPCS 2004) |
| **Dholavira** | **Water management**: 16 reservoirs, dams, embankments, channels; some **rock-cut** (UPSC 2021, CDS 2012, UPPCS 2010) |
| Kalibangan | Many houses had their own wells; **wooden drains** |
| Banawali | **No systematic drainage** |
| Allahdino (Sindh) | Well irrigation |
- **Drainage** = the most advanced IVC feature: house drains → sump/cesspit → **covered** street drains (bricks or stone slabs) with **manholes** → **soak pits** or main channels; streets sloped for flow; better than contemporary Mesopotamia and Egypt. Lothal: mud-brick houses but burnt-brick drains.
- **Dholavira reservoirs:** water from the **Manhar and Mansar** rivulets; huge rock-cut reservoir about **73–79 m long, 7 m deep**; stepwells cut in sandstone = Harappan **rock-cut architecture** (UPPSC 2006).
> Trap: Dholavira = reservoirs (water harvesting); Mohenjodaro = wells.

=== Citadel and Lower Town – Dholavira's Three Parts
| Site | Plan |
|---|---|
| Mohenjodaro, Harappa | **Citadel on the west** (smaller, higher, on a mud-brick platform, elite and public buildings) + **lower town on the east** (larger, commoners) |
| Kalibangan | Citadel + lower town, **both fortified** |
| **Dholavira** | **Only city in three parts: citadel, middle town, lower town** |
| Chanhudaro | **No citadel** (only Harappan city without one) |
| Lothal, Surkotada | Citadel not separately walled; inside one fortified enclosure |
| Sutkagendor | Citadel + lower city |
- **Great Bath, granary, college of priests, assembly hall** stood on the **citadel** of Mohenjodaro → both "citadel on the west" and "Great Bath in the citadel" are correct (AP Sachivalayam).
- Pataliputra is NOT an IVC site; Kalibangan, Ropar and Lothal are.

=== Bricks, Streets and Houses
# Bricks
- **Burnt bricks** common; **mud bricks** for fillings and platforms and at **Kalibangan**. Both baked and raw bricks used.
- Standard ratio **1:2:4** (thickness : width : length) = 4:2:1.
- **Same ratio, different sizes:** house bricks **7.5×15×30 cm** (≈7×14×28), city-wall bricks **10×20×40 cm** → "the same size of brick for all constructions" is **false**.
- **Dholavira**: **sandstone and limestone** blocks, not baked brick.
- **Ornamental bricks** = Kalibangan; **curved bricks** = Chanhudaro.
# Streets
- **Grid layout**: streets ran north-south and east-west, crossing at **right angles**, splitting the city into blocks → **broad and straight** (not narrow, zig-zag).
- Main streets about **10 m (30 feet)** wide (Rocket: main road of Mohenjodaro ≈9.15 m). **Banawali** = only city with **radial streets**.
# Houses
- Rooms round a **central courtyard**; brick-paved **bathrooms** and toilets; no ground-level windows (privacy).
- Doors and windows opened on **side lanes**, not main streets (Lothal is the exception – entrances on the main street).
- **Storeys:** most houses single-storeyed but **staircases** show many had an upper storey. CGPSC 2022 key treats "mostly all houses single storey" as untrue along with "same size of bricks" → **(i) and (ii)**; "main roads ~10 m" is true.
- **Arch:** Harappans used only the corbelled arch, never the true arch (MPPSC 2009).

=== The Great Bath
| Angle | Fact |
|---|---|
| Site | **Mohenjodaro**, on the **citadel** mound, Sindh – not Harappa, Lothal, Ropar or Kalibangan |
| Size | **11.88 × 7.01 m, 2.43 m deep**; two flights of steps on north and south |
| Material | Burnt bricks set in **gypsum mortar**; **bitumen** (natural tar) waterproofing |
| Water | From a large well in a side room; drain in one corner |
| Purpose | **Ritual bath** (most view); public bath ruled out as houses had bathrooms |

=== Dholavira – Plan, Stadium and Signboard
- **Kachchh** (Khadir island), Gujarat; rectangular plan; one of the **five largest** cities (with Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Ganweriwala, Rakhigarhi).
- **Stadium** (~283 × 47 m) between citadel and middle town; special cemetery structures; **ten-sign signboard** near the **North Gate** = largest Harappan signs.
- Settled in seven stages, **c. 3000–1500 BCE** (APPSC 2018 marked 3000–1800 BCE).
- **UNESCO 2021**: India's **40th** WHS; **first IVC site in India** on the list.
