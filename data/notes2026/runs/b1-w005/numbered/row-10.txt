## SUB 0 | Burials – Types and Sites
[0:0] - **Common burial system (HPPSC 2012):** **extended earth burial with the head generally to the north**, and **grave goods** (pots, ornaments, copper mirrors) buried with the dead → "both a and b". Sitting-posture burial is not Harappan.
[0:1] - **Three forms:** complete (extended) burials, fractional burials, post-cremation burials.
[0:2] TABLE head: Burial practice | Site
  [0:2.0] Extended burials with goods; coffin burial | R-37, Harappa (head generally to the north)
  [0:2.1] Post-cremation / fractional, Late Harappan | Cemetery H, Harappa
  [0:2.2] Fractional (bones buried after exposure of the body) | Mohenjodaro, Harappa
  [0:2.3] Joint / double burial | Lothal (UPPSC 2016)
  [0:2.4] Dog buried with human, oval pit burials | Ropar
  [0:2.5] Pot burials; megalith-type grave with a big rock | Surkotada
  [0:2.6] Large cemetery of human burials (125 graves, N–S) | Sanauli (Sinauli), Baghpat, UP (UP Lower 2004)
  [0:2.7] Double burials; Mound 7 cemetery; women with jasper, agate beads, shell bangles | Rakhigarhi
[0:3] - **Kalibangan – three types:** extended burials in rectangular pits; pot burials; circular pits with grave goods but **no skeleton**.
[0:4] - Grave goods → belief in an **afterlife**; a copper mirror with cotton thread was found in a Harappa grave. Overall the Harappans did not bury precious things.
[0:5] - **Rakhigarhi Mound 7:** 56 skeletons incl. a ~4,600-year-old woman.
[0:6] - **Huddled skeletons** at Mohenjodaro (16 with ornaments) – once read as a "massacre", now as **multiple burials**.
[0:7] > Double burial = Lothal; dog with human = Ropar; pot burial = Surkotada; R-37 = extended; Cemetery H = Late Harappan.

## SUB 1 | Social Stratification – Evidence
[1:0] TABLE head: Evidence | What it shows
  [1:0.0] Town layout | Citadel (west) – elite and public buildings vs lower town (east) – commoners
  [1:0.1] Houses | One to twelve rooms; single-roomed barracks at Harappa
  [1:0.2] Burial goods | Variation in goods and ornament quality = best sign of social differentiation
[1:1] - **Uniform bricks** prove organisation and authority, **not status**.
[1:2] - **No proof** of a rigid caste system; caste became fixed in the **Later Vedic** period. On the whole the Harappans seem relatively **egalitarian**.
[1:3] - Ranks and polity are rebuilt only from **material remains**; the script gives no rulers' names.
[1:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.100; old app notes row 10
## SUB 2 | Polity and Authority
[2:0] - Uniform pottery, seals, **weights**, **1:2:4 bricks** and **grid towns** over ~**8,00,000 sq km** show complex decisions made and carried out → a significant authority.
[2:1] TABLE head: View | Based on
  [2:1.0] Priestly rule | Citadels, Great Bath, steatite "Priest-King" (not conclusive – no temples)
  [2:1.1] Merchant rule | Trade-driven uniformity, few weapons
  [2:1.2] Several rulers / city-states | Several large cities, no single capital
  [2:1.3] Single state | Most plausible per PMF
[2:2] - Political structure **not clear**; it **seems corporate**; municipal-type civic administration (NextGen). Later Vedic = hereditary monarchy, weakening Sabha and Samiti.
[2:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.101; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.7; old app notes row 10
## SUB 3 | No Palaces, No Temples
[3:0] - **No royal palaces or temples** found. "Possessed great palaces and temples" = **WRONG** (UPSC 2013).
[3:1] - Worship was probably **domestic or open-air** (trees, animals); power **not personalised** in a king.
[3:2] - Public buildings were mainly **civic**: Great Bath, granary, pillared hall.
[3:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.101; old app notes row 10
## SUB 4 | Dress, Ornaments, Pastimes and Weapons
[4:0] - **Cotton and wool**; two garments (upper and lower); **Priest-King wears a shawl** with trefoil pattern; **no proof of stitching**.
[4:1] - **Ornaments:** gold, silver, ivory, copper, lapis lazuli; Allahdino gold and silver necklaces.
[4:2] - **Lipstick** = **Chanhudaro**; also bronze mirrors, ivory combs, collyrium sticks.
[4:3] - **Toy factory** = Chanhudaro; terracotta carts, animals, marbles, rattles, whistles (Karanpura); **dice** at Harappa and Mohenjodaro; board games (Lothal chess-like set).
[4:4] - **Weapons:** maces, axes, spears, bows and arrows of copper, bronze and stone; few and simple → relatively **peaceful**; **no horse-drawn chariots** (UPSC 2013).
