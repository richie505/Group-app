## SUB 0 | Processes and Causes of Land Degradation
[0:0] - Main cause of desertification in semi-arid regions: **overgrazing and deforestation** (APPSC 2025).
[0:1] TABLE head: Process | How it degrades land
  [0:1.0] Overgrazing | Strips vegetation cover
  [0:1.1] Deforestation | Exposes soil to wind and water erosion
  [0:1.2] Water erosion | Removes topsoil; silts rivers and reservoirs
  [0:1.3] Wind erosion | Blows topsoil away in dry areas (Anantapur)
  [0:1.4] Salinisation, waterlogging | Poor drainage and over-irrigation (AP delta areas); by 2050, 50% of world arable land could be affected
[0:2] # Agriculture-driven Degradation
[0:3] - Effects of **modern (intensive) agriculture**: soil degradation, pollution, ecological imbalance – **all of these** (APPSC 2018).
[0:4] - Fertilisers and pesticides contaminate soil and water; **excess urea** upset the NPK ratio in **Punjab and Haryana**.
[0:5] - Resource depletion → cause: groundwater – over-irrigation; topsoil – erosion from overgrazing and deforestation; habitats – deforestation; land – salinity and waterlogging.
[0:6] - **Punjab, Haryana, western UP:** **over-irrigation** → waterlogging, salinity and alkalinity is the main cause of land degradation (NCERT X); overgrazing = Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Maharashtra; mining = Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, MP, Odisha.
[0:7] - **Linked with desertification:** deforestation, drought and loss of vegetation – all of these.
[0:8] - **Heavy sand mining in riverbeds (UPSC 2018):** **pollutes groundwater** and **lowers the water table**; it does not decrease river salinity.
[0:9] - **Borrow pit:** a pit dug to take earth, sand or gravel for use as fill elsewhere (roads, embankments) – a land-degrading practice.
[0:10] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Human Geography Part 10 (NCERT X Resources and Development); app notes 5:75 sub 1
## SUB 1 | Key Terms
[1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:0.0] Desertification (UNCCD) | Land degradation in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid areas from climatic variation and human activity – NOT just the spread of existing deserts
  [1:0.1] Environmental degradation | Depletion of air, water, soil; destruction of ecosystems; extinction of wildlife
  [1:0.2] Tragedy of the Commons | Over-use of a shared pasture lowers land quality – Garrett Hardin, 1968 (APPSC 2018)
  [1:0.3] LDN | Land Degradation Neutrality – no net loss of land health by 2030 (SDG 15.3)
  [1:0.4] Soil Organic Carbon | Indian soils fell from 1% to 0.3% in 70 years
  [1:0.5] Ridge to valley | Watershed approach of the PMKSY Watershed Development Component
[1:1] - **Controlled grazing:** regulating the grazing **time and consumption level** of a pasture (rotational grazing is one method).

## SUB 3 | Desertification Geography and Data
[3:0] - **Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas 2021**: by **ISRO's Space Applications Centre (SAC), Ahmedabad**.
[3:1] - **97.85 Mha = 29.77%** of India degraded; main contributors: **water erosion + chemical degradation**.
[3:2] - About **one-fourth** of degraded land faces desertification; **Rajasthan** has over **23%** of desertified land.
[3:3] - FAO: **one-third** of world soils degraded; Global Land Outlook: degradation costs over 10% of global GDP.
[3:4] - Worst-hit continents: **Africa (Sahel) and Asia**.
[3:5] - **Sand and dust storms** hit NW India (Rajasthan), West Asia and the Sahel.
[3:6] - **Dust storm season:** In north-west India (Rajasthan), dust storms are most common in the hot, dry pre-monsoon summer months (April to June).
[3:7] - **Least affected continent:** **Europe** (no large hot desert; smallest dryland share).
[3:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment Part 5 p.42-47; app notes 5:75 sub 4

[7:0] - Impacts: faster erosion, falling fertility, desertification; **loss of CO₂ sink adds to greenhouse effect**.
[7:1] - Highest loss: **tropical forests** – Amazon, Congo Basin, Southeast Asia.
[7:2] TABLE head: FAO FRA 2025 (Oct 2025) | Fact
  [7:2.0] Forest share of land | About 32%
  [7:2.1] Five countries with over 54% of forests | Russia, Brazil, Canada, USA, China
  [7:2.2] Net loss | 10.7 Mha/yr (1990s) → 4.12 Mha/yr (2015-2025)
  [7:2.3] Only region with net gain | Asia, led by China and India
  [7:2.4] India | 9th in forest area (10th in 2020); 3rd in annual gain
[7:3] - Older FRA (APPSC 2018): net loss **8.3 Mha/yr (1990s) → 5.2 Mha/yr (2000-2010)**.
[7:4] - Forest Declaration Assessment 2025: 8.1 Mha lost in 2024, about **94%** in tropical forests.

## SUB 8 | Urban Heat Islands
[8:0] - **UHI**: cities markedly hotter than surroundings. Causes: **low-albedo concrete/asphalt** and **loss of vegetation** (separate causes).
[8:1] - **Cool roofs**: white/reflective coatings – absorb less heat, lower cooling load.
[8:2] - **AP Cool Surface Policy 2023** objectives: affordable urban heat solutions, lower heat and energy demand, reflective materials.
[8:3] APPSC 2023: "rural heat" is NOT an objective of the AP Cool Surface Policy.
[8:4] - First Heat Action Plan: **Ahmedabad, 2013**; **NDMA** heat action plan guidelines **2016**.
[8:5] - **Namo Van–Nagar Van Scheme**: urban forests against UHI (Jul 2026).
[8:6] ~~ Sources: app notes 5:75 sub 8 (AP Cool Surface Policy 2023); NextGen Environment Part 8.2 p.4
