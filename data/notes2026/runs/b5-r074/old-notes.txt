## SUB 2 | Biogeochemical Cycles – Basics and Types
[74:2:0] - Nutrients are recycled indefinitely between organisms, air and the Earth's crust; driven by energy flow; **all cycles are linked to the water cycle**.
[74:2:1] - Organisms need at least **27 elements** (15 metals). Macronutrients: potassium, sodium, magnesium, calcium, sulphur, phosphorus, chlorine.
[74:2:2] TABLE head: Type | Cycles | Main reservoir
  [74:2:2.0] Gaseous | Carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, water | Atmosphere / ocean
  [74:2:2.1] Sedimentary | Phosphorus, sulphur, calcium, potassium, iron | Earth's crust – soil and rocks
[74:2:3] Hydrogen (H₂) is NOT a gaseous cycle in exam lists.
[74:2:4] APPSC 2025: Removing decomposers blocks mineral movement – nutrients locked in dead matter never return to the soil.
[74:2:5] # Phosphorus Cycle
[74:2:6] - **No atmospheric phase**; natural reservoir = **rock (phosphates)**; weathering of rocks is the main source; much ends in **ocean sediments**.
[74:2:7] - P is used in membranes, nucleic acids, energy transfer, shells, bones, teeth.
## SUB 3 | Carbon Cycle
[74:3:0] TABLE head: Adds CO₂ | Removes CO₂
  [74:3:0.0] Respiration, decomposition, fossil-fuel combustion, volcanic activity, deforestation | Photosynthesis (plants, algae, some bacteria), dissolving in oceans, carbonate sedimentation, silicate weathering
[74:3:1] - Reservoirs and sinks: atmosphere, oceans, forests, soils; long-term – carbonate rocks and fossil fuels. Forests = vital carbon sinks.
[74:3:2] Photosynthesis does NOT add CO₂ – it removes it.
## SUB 4 | Nitrogen Cycle
[74:4:0] # Reservoir and Processes
[74:4:1] - Main reservoir = **atmosphere (N₂ about 78% of air)**, NOT soil (APPSC G2 2025).
[74:4:2] - Plants take nitrogen as **nitrate**; atmospheric N₂ is not directly usable.
[74:4:3] TABLE head: Process | What happens | Agents
  [74:4:3.0] Fixation | N₂ → ammonia/nitrate (enzyme nitrogenase) | Rhizobium, Azotobacter, cyanobacteria (Anabaena, Nostoc), lightning, industrial Haber process
  [74:4:3.1] Ammonification | Organic N in dead matter → ammonia | Decomposer bacteria and fungi
  [74:4:3.2] Nitrification | Ammonia → nitrite → nitrate | Nitrosomonas (to nitrite), Nitrobacter (to nitrate)
  [74:4:3.3] Denitrification | Nitrate → N₂ gas, returned to air | Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus
[74:4:4] - Fixation, ammonification and nitrification **add** usable N to soil; denitrification **removes** it.
[74:4:5] # Legumes and Root Nodules
[74:4:6] - **Rhizobium** – symbiotic, in **legume root nodules** (pulses, pea); **Rhizobium japonicum** – soybean.
[74:4:7] - **Leghaemoglobin**: **pink/red** pigment in nodules (alfalfa, soybean); binds oxygen to keep it low, since **nitrogenase is destroyed by oxygen**.
[74:4:8] - **Boron** is necessary for nodule formation.
[74:4:9] - Pea family (Fabaceae/Leguminosae): groundnut, horse-gram, soybean; N-fixing plants: alfalfa, chickpea, clover.
[74:4:10] # Biofertilisers
[74:4:11] TABLE head: Organism | Nature | Crop / role
  [74:4:11.0] Rhizobium | Symbiotic, legume nodules | Pulses, pea
  [74:4:11.1] Azotobacter, Azospirillum | Free-living N fixers | Cereals, millets, non-leguminous upland crops
  [74:4:11.2] Blue-green algae (cyanobacteria) | Photosynthetic N fixers; heterocysts | Rice / paddy
  [74:4:11.3] Azolla–Anabaena | Water fern Azolla + Anabaena in its leaves | Water-logged rice fields (Azolla pinnata)
  [74:4:11.4] Frankia | Actinomycete, non-legumes | Alnus, Casuarina
  [74:4:11.5] VAM (mycorrhiza) | Fungus–root association | Phosphorus and water uptake
  [74:4:11.6] PSB | Bacillus megaterium, Pseudomonas striata | Solubilise phosphate
[74:4:12] Azotobacter is NOT partnered with Anabaena; symbiotic fixation in legume nodules is by Rhizobium, not cyanobacteria; VAM does not denitrify.
## SUB 5 | Water Cycle Regulation – Wetlands and Forests
[74:5:0] - Water carries elements through the ecosystem; cycles would stop if water did not cycle.
[74:5:1] - **Wetlands as sponges**: store floodwater, recharge aquifers, keep dry-season flow; also control erosion, retain sediment, support nutrient cycling and biodiversity.
[74:5:2] - **Wetlands as "kidneys" of the Earth**: aquatic plants absorb heavy metals and excess substances.
[74:5:3] - **Microclimate regulators**: stored water evaporates and holds heat, steadying local temperature and humidity.
[74:5:4] - **Forests**: regulate water flow, conserve soil and moisture, recharge groundwater, moderate floods.
## SUB 6 | Human Disruption of Cycles
[74:6:0] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [74:6:0.0] Fossil fuels + deforestation | Raise atmospheric CO₂ → climate change
  [74:6:0.1] Chemical pesticides | Alter soils and microbial networks; may weaken resilience (not always increase it)
  [74:6:0.2] Nitrogen overload | Human activity has at least doubled nitrogen available to plants
  [74:6:0.3] Fertiliser runoff | Algal blooms; decay uses up dissolved oxygen → dead zones
  [74:6:0.4] N fertiliser in soil | Microbes release nitrous oxide, a greenhouse gas
[74:6:1] - **Prosopis juliflora** (invasive) called an "ecological first responder" – a pioneer spreading where hydrology and soil chemistry have changed (7 May 2026).
## SUB 7 | Ecological Succession – Terms and Types
[74:7:0] # Terms
[74:7:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [74:7:1.0] Succession | Gradual, orderly replacement of one community by another in an area over time
  [74:7:1.1] Pioneer species | First hardy colonisers – lichens, mosses
  [74:7:1.2] Sere / seral stage | Whole sequence = sere; each step = seral stage (seral communities in between)
  [74:7:1.3] Climax community | Final stable community in balance with climate (forest, grassland, coral reef)
  [74:7:1.4] Seasonal succession | Cyclic, driven by environmental rhythms; NOT caused by disturbance
[74:7:2] # Primary vs Secondary
[74:7:3] TABLE head: Primary | Secondary
  [74:7:3.0] Area that never had life – lava, bare rock, volcanic island; no soil | After fire, flood, logging; soil, seeds, roots remain
  [74:7:3.1] Pioneers: lichens and mosses | Faster than primary
[74:7:4] - On a surface without soil, **lichens and mosses** survive; **ferns need soil**.
[74:7:5] - Grasslands do not turn into forest because of **water limits and dry-season fire**.
[74:7:6] # Seres by Starting Medium
[74:7:7] TABLE head: Sere | Starts on
  [74:7:7.0] Hydrosere | Water (pond, lake)
  [74:7:7.1] Xerosere | Dry land
  [74:7:7.2] Lithosere | Bare rock (sub-type of xerosere)
  [74:7:7.3] Psammosere | Sand (sub-type of xerosere)
  [74:7:7.4] Halosere | Saline soil or water
## SUB 8 | Succession – Phases, Sequences and Theories
[74:8:0] # Clements' Phases (1916)
[74:8:1] TABLE head: Phase | What happens
  [74:8:1.0] 1. Nudation | Bare area forms (erosion, landslide, volcano, fire)
  [74:8:1.1] 2. Invasion | Seeds/spores arrive; ecesis (successful establishment), aggregation
  [74:8:1.2] 3. Competition and co-action | Unfit species eliminated
  [74:8:1.3] 4. Reaction | Organisms modify soil, water, light, temperature – most important phase
  [74:8:1.4] 5. Stabilisation | Climax community
[74:8:2] Invasion comes right after nudation; ecesis belongs to the invasion phase.
[74:8:3] # Sequences
[74:8:4] - **Xerosere**: crustose lichens → foliose lichens → mosses → herbs and grasses → shrubs → forest. **Lichens** (algae + fungi) start succession on bare rock.
[74:8:5] - Pioneer trees (birch, oak) need high light; shade-tolerant species form the climax.
[74:8:6] - **Hydrosere**: phytoplankton → submerged rooted (Chara) → floating (Lemna, Pistia) → reed-swamp → sedge-meadow → woodland → forest; ends in wet woodland (**fen carr**).
[74:8:7] - Hydroseres are better studied – **lake sediments keep a permanent record**.
[74:8:8] # Ecologists and Climax Theories
[74:8:9] TABLE head: Person | Contribution
  [74:8:9.0] F.E. Clements | Phases of succession (1916); monoclimax – one climax per region, set by climate
  [74:8:9.1] Tansley | Polyclimax; coined "ecosystem"
  [74:8:9.2] Whittaker | Climax-pattern
  [74:8:9.3] E.P. Odum (1971) | Succession = orderly community change within a unit area
  [74:8:9.4] H.C. Cowles | Succession on sand dunes
  [74:8:9.5] Elton / Lindeman | Food chains, pyramid of numbers / 10% law

=== NOTES 5:76 | Environmental Legislation in India – Water Act 1974, Air Act 1981, Environment (protection) Act 1986
## SUB 0 | Chronology of Environmental Acts

  [76:0:0.2] 1977 | Water (P&CP) Cess Act | Cess on water used by industries and local bodies; Cess Rules 1978
  [76:0:0.3] 1980 | Forest (Conservation) Act | Central approval for diverting forest land
  [76:0:0.4] 1981 | Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act | Followed the Water Act framework
  [76:0:0.5] 1986 | Environment (Protection) Act | Umbrella law
  [76:0:0.6] 1991 | Public Liability Insurance Act | Relief for hazardous-substance accidents
  [76:0:0.7] 2002 | Biological Diversity Act | –
  [76:0:0.8] 2010 | National Green Tribunal Act | Specialised tribunal; latest in the chain

## SUB 2 | Water Act 1974 and Water Cess Act 1977
[76:2:0] # Water Act 1974
[76:2:1] - Purpose: **prevent and control water pollution** and **maintain or restore the wholesomeness of water**.
[76:2:2] - Passed under **Article 252** – applies in States that adopt it by resolution.
[76:2:3] - Created the **Central Board for the Prevention and Control of Water Pollution (1974)** – renamed **CPCB** by the **1988 amendment** – and the State Boards.
[76:2:4] - Structure: **8 chapters, 64 sections**; standards for **discharge of effluents**; prior consent of the Board needed before discharging effluent.
[76:2:5] - **Section 33A**: directions, including **environmental compensation**.
[76:2:6] - Penalties: **1.5 to 6 years' jail**. Rules: Water Rules 1975.
[76:2:7] # Water Cess Act 1977
[76:2:8] - Cess on water consumed by specified industries and local authorities – funds the Boards.
[76:2:9] - Proceeds go to the **Consolidated Fund of India**; subsumed in **GST in 2017**.
## SUB 3 | Air Act 1981
[76:3:0] - Purpose: **prevention, control and abatement of air pollution**; maintain **air quality standards** (APPSC G2 2025); Central and State Boards set and enforce standards.
[76:3:1] - Enacted **29 March 1981**; in force **16 May 1981**; modelled on the Water Act.
[76:3:2] - Structure: **7 chapters, 54 sections**.
[76:3:3] - The existing **CPCB got air functions**; SPCBs enforce in States.
[76:3:4] - **Noise** added to the definition of "air pollutant" by the **1987 amendment** (not 1991).
[76:3:5] - **Air pollution control areas**: declared by the **State Government** – Chapter IV, Sections 19-31; a special power of the Air Act, not the Water Act.
[76:3:6] - Emission standards for industry and automobiles. **Section 31A**: directions; Boards can levy compensation.
[76:3:7] # Water Act vs Air Act
[76:3:8] TABLE head: Feature | Water Act 1974 | Air Act 1981
  [76:3:8.0] Aim | Wholesomeness of water | Prevent, control, abate air pollution
  [76:3:8.1] Structure | 8 chapters, 64 sections | 7 chapters, 54 sections
  [76:3:8.2] Basis | Article 252 | Modelled on Water Act
  [76:3:8.3] Boards | Created CPCB and SPCBs | Same Boards given air functions
  [76:3:8.4] Special power | Effluent discharge standards | Air pollution control areas
  [76:3:8.5] Amended | 1988 (CPCB name) | 1987 (noise added)
  [76:3:8.6] Compensation | Section 33A | Section 31A
[76:3:9] - Common: both require **prior consent** of the Board to establish and operate a polluting unit.
## SUB 4 | Environment (protection) Act 1986
[76:4:0] # Background and Nature
[76:4:1] - Trigger: **Bhopal Gas Tragedy (December 1984)**; gives effect to the **Stockholm Conference, 1972**.
[76:4:2] - Basis: **Article 253**. Purpose: **protect and improve the environment and control pollution** (APPSC 2025).
[76:4:3] - Called **umbrella legislation** – coordinates central and state authorities under the Water and Air Acts; the **Central Government** is the primary authority. Last amendment mentioned: 1991.
[76:4:4] # Key Sections and Powers
[76:4:5] TABLE head: Section | Content
  [76:4:5.0] 3 | Centre's powers – emission/discharge standards, location of industries, hazardous waste, public health; supports EIA and CRZ notifications
  [76:4:5.1] 3(3) | Constitute authorities
  [76:4:5.2] 5 | Directions, including closure
  [76:4:5.3] 15-21 | Penalties
  [76:4:5.4] 16 | Environment Protection Fund
[76:4:6] - Authorities under Section 3(3): **Central Ground Water Authority (January 1997)** – regulates groundwater development; **National Ganga River Basin Authority (2009)**.
[76:4:7] # Environment Protection Fund
[76:4:8] - In the **Public Account**; holds fines under the Air, Water and EP Acts; **75% to States/UTs, 25% to Centre**.
[76:4:9] - **Environmental (Protection) Fund Rules, 2026** notified by MoEFCC.
[76:4:10] Water Cess → Consolidated Fund of India; EP Fund → Public Account.
[76:4:11] # Notifications and Rules under EPA
[76:4:12] TABLE head: Notification | Key point
  [76:4:12.0] Doon Valley (1989) | No industry burning over 24 MT coal/fuel a day
  [76:4:12.1] Revdanda Creek (1989) | No industries around the creek
  [76:4:12.2] CRZ (1991) | Regulates coastal stretches; no ash/waste dumping; CRZ under EPA confirmed (Jul 2026)
  [76:4:12.3] Dhanu Taluka (1991) | Ecologically fragile; no power plants
  [76:4:12.4] EIA (1994, revised 2006) | Prior environmental clearance
