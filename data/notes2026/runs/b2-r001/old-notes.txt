=== NOTES 2:1 | Indian Independence Act 1947; the Constituent Assembly – Demand, Composition, Functioning
## SUB 0 | Demand for A Constituent Assembly, 1934–1946
[1:0:0] # Timeline
[1:0:1] TABLE head: Year | Step
  [1:0:1.0] 1895 | Constitution of India Bill – first explicit demand for Fundamental Rights
  [1:0:1.1] 1934 | M.N. Roy first put forward the idea
  [1:0:1.2] May 1934 | Swaraj Party (parliamentary wing of Congress) – first formal demand, Ranchi conference; claimed self-determination; Congress adopted it at Patna (AICC, May 1934); Congress Working Committee's resolution of June 1934 = Congress's first official demand
  [1:0:1.3] 1938 | Nehru – clear statement of support
  [1:0:1.4] 1940 | August Offer – British accepted the principle
  [1:0:1.5] 1942 | Cripps Mission – proposal for an elected Constituent Assembly
  [1:0:1.6] 1946 | Constituted under the Cabinet Mission Plan
[1:0:2] # Cabinet Mission (arrived 24 March 1946)
[1:0:3] - **Pethick-Lawrence** (Secretary for India), **Stafford Cripps** (Board of Trade), **A.V. Alexander** (Navy) – NOT William Wood.
[1:0:4] First idea = M.N. Roy; first formal demand = Swaraj Party, Ranchi, May 1934.
## SUB 1 | Composition
[1:1:0] # Seats
[1:1:1] TABLE head: Item | Figure
  [1:1:1.0] Total planned | 389
  [1:1:1.1] British India | 296 = 292 Governor's provinces + 4 Chief Commissioners' provinces
  [1:1:1.2] Princely States | 93
  [1:1:1.3] Ratio | 1 member per 10 lakh population
  [1:1:1.4] After partition | 299 (members from Pakistan areas left)
[1:1:2] - Provincial seats split among **Muslims, Sikhs and General** by population.
[1:1:3] # Method of Election (July–August 1946)
[1:1:4] - **Indirectly elected** by members of the **Provincial Legislative Assemblies** (set up under the 1935 Act; provincial elections January 1946).
[1:1:5] - **Proportional representation by single transferable vote**.
[1:1:6] - **NOT adult franchise**: restricted franchise of the 1935 Act (tax, education, property).
[1:1:7] - Princely representatives **nominated by rulers**; 93 seats at first unfilled; states joined from the third session (**28 April 1947**: Baroda, Bikaner, Jaipur, Patiala, Rewa, Udaipur).
[1:1:8] - Nature: **partly elected, partly nominated**.
[1:1:9] - Results: **Congress 208, Muslim League 73, others 15**.
[1:1:10] - **15 women** members: Ammu Swaminathan, Annie Mascarene, Sarojini Naidu, Dakshayani Velayudhan, Durgabai Deshmukh, Hansa Mehta, Kamala Choudhury, Leela Roy, Malati Choudhury and others.
[1:1:11] - **Ambedkar**: first elected from **East Bengal**; later from the Bombay (Pune) seat vacated by **M.R. Jayakar**.
[1:1:12] Elections were July–August 1946, not June–July. Not direct, not adult franchise.

## SUB 4 | Linguistic Provinces – Dar Commission and Jvp
[1:4:0] - **Linguistic Provinces Commission** (**17 June 1948**): **S.K. Dar**, retired Allahabad HC judge (APPSC GS 2025).
[1:4:1] - **Dar Commission and JVP Committee** (Jawaharlal, Vallabhbhai, Pattabhi) both **rejected linguistic states** for the time being; JVP left room for **Andhra**.

## SUB 7 | Other Functions – Symbols, Language and Legislature
[1:7:0] # Symbols
[1:7:1] - **Flag**: wheel from the **Ashoka Chakra (Dharmachakra)**, **24 spokes**, wheel of law (justice).
[1:7:2] - **Jana Gana Mana**: adopted 24 Jan 1950 by Prasad's statement, not a vote; full rendition **52 seconds**.
[1:7:3] - **Vande Mataram** (Bankimchandra): **Sanskrit**, in **Bengali script**; declared national song, **honoured equally**.
[1:7:4] - **Art. 51A(a)** names the **Flag and Anthem**, NOT the Song; 2026 amendment of the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act gives the Song equal statutory protection (cited 23 Aug 2026); **Bijoe Emmanuel v State of Kerala (1986)**.
[1:7:5] # Language
[1:7:6] - **Munshi-Ayyangar formula**: **Hindi in Devanagari**; **15-year** transition with English continued; **14** other languages in the Eighth Schedule – NOT immediate end of English.

## SUB 9 | Indian Independence Act, 1947
[1:9:0] # Mountbatten Plan and the Bill
[1:9:1] - **3 June 1947 Plan** (Mountbatten): accepted by Congress and League; basis of the Act.
[1:9:2] - Bill introduced in the Commons **4 July 1947**; **royal assent 18 July 1947**.
[1:9:3] - **Radcliffe** chaired the **Punjab** and **Bengal** Boundary Commissions; award published **17 August 1947**.
[1:9:4] # Provisions
[1:9:5] - Ended **British sovereignty**; two Dominions **India and Pakistan** from **15 August 1947**.
[1:9:6] - **Governor-General** (common or separate) to act on Dominion ministers' advice; British monarch nominal head; **Crown lost its veto**.
[1:9:7] - **Constituent Assembly** = sovereign constitution-maker + Dominion legislature; **Central Legislative Assembly ended**.
[1:9:8] - **Paramountcy lapsed**: about **565** princely states theoretically free; expected to join India or Pakistan.
[1:9:9] - Dominion status ended with the Republic, **26 Jan 1950**.
[1:9:10] # Princely States
[1:9:11] TABLE head: Instrument | Provision
  [1:9:11.0] Instrument of Accession | Ceded defence, external affairs, communications (NOT finance)
  [1:9:11.1] Standstill Agreement | Existing arrangements continued; Hyderabad signed one in November 1947
[1:9:12] - **Kashmir** acceded **26 October 1947** after the tribal invasion; UN ceasefire **1 January 1949**.
[1:9:13] # Governors-General
[1:9:14] - **Mountbatten**: last Viceroy and first GG of independent India.
[1:9:15] - **C. Rajagopalachari**: last and only Indian GG (June 1948-Jan 1950); assented to the **Madras Estates (Abolition) Act** on 2 April 1949.
[1:9:16] - Significance: sovereignty came with partition; Congress accepted dominion status for a quick, peaceful transfer.
## SUB 10 | Remarks and Criticisms
[1:10:0] TABLE head: Statement | Speaker
  [1:10:0.0] Constitutional morality "is not a natural sentiment. It has to be cultivated" (borrowed from George Grote) | Ambedkar, 4 Nov 1948
  [1:10:0.1] Civil disobedience = "grammar of anarchy"; "a life of contradictions" | Ambedkar, closing speech 25 Nov 1949
  [1:10:0.2] Constitution a sacred document | Ambedkar
  [1:10:0.3] "A Constitution, like a machine, is a lifeless thing…" | Rajendra Prasad, 26 Nov 1949
  [1:10:0.4] "A one-party body in an essentially one-party country"; "Assembly was Congress and Congress was India" | Granville Austin
  [1:10:0.5] "A body of Hindus"; lawyer-politician dominated | Viscount Simon
  [1:10:0.6] "Drifting Committee" | Naziruddin Ahmed
  [1:10:0.7] Represented "only one major community" | Winston Churchill
  [1:10:0.8] Delay adult franchise by 15 years | Maulana Azad (opposed by Prasad, Nehru)
[1:10:1] - Criticisms: **not representative** (no adult franchise); **not sovereign** (British creation); **time-consuming** (about 3 years vs about 4 months for the USA); **Congress-dominated**; **"borrowed"/patchwork**.
[1:10:2] - Defence: multiparty body; worked through committees.
[1:10:3] - **Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas)**: 26 November, since **2015**, Ambedkar's **125th birth anniversary** year (14 April 2016; Rs 10 and Rs 125 coins).
