=== NOTES 3:14 | Economic Growth vs Economic Development; Measures of Development
## SUB 0 | Growth vs Development
[14:0:0] TABLE head: Basis | Economic growth | Economic development
  [14:0:0.0] Meaning | Sustained rise in actual (real) output | Growth + better living standards, capabilities, distribution (normative)
  [14:0:0.1] Nature | Quantitative | Qualitative + quantitative
  [14:0:0.2] Scope | Narrow | Broad; includes growth
  [14:0:0.3] Measure | Real GDP/GNP, per capita income | HDI, poverty ratio, literacy, life expectancy, inequality
  [14:0:0.4] Distribution | Not a concern | Central concern
  [14:0:0.5] Relation | Means; necessary, not sufficient | End
[14:0:1] - One-liner: growth = increase in output; development = increase in **wellbeing**. GDP alone cannot measure development.
[14:0:2] - Rise in absolute or **per capita real GNP** is NOT higher development if **poverty and unemployment rise**.
[14:0:3] - Growth without development: **jobless growth** or unequal gains. Development needs a **multi-dimensional** approach.
[14:0:4] - Economic growth usually comes with **inflation** (rising income raises demand). Government focuses on both micro and macro issues.
[14:0:5] - Kerala: **2nd in HDI** among States but **7th in per capita NSDP**.
## SUB 1 | Models and the "new View"
[14:1:0] TABLE head: Model / view | Emphasis
  [14:1:0.0] Western / modernisation model (e.g. Rostow) | Key factor = economic growth (GDP, industrialisation) (APPSC 2018)
  [14:1:0.1] Gandhian model | Decentralisation, village industry
  [14:1:0.2] Kerala model | High human development at modest income
  [14:1:0.3] Trickle-down | Growth benefits reach the poor automatically (early Indian plans)
  [14:1:0.4] Capability approach | Broader than GDP
  [14:1:0.5] Directive Principles | Adequate livelihood, reduced inequality – not just national income
[14:1:1] - **New view (1970s)**: framed by **Dudley Seers**, *The Meaning of Development* (**1969**) – reduce **poverty, unemployment, inequality**.
[14:1:2] New view does NOT stress the **simple growth rate** (APPSC-G2 2017).
## SUB 2 | Indicators of Development
[14:2:0] - Valid: **institutional change**, **structural transformation** (agrarian → industry and services), **inclusive growth**, human resource development, rising capital formation.
[14:2:1] - Best indicator of structural transformation: rising share of **manufactured products in exports**.
[14:2:2] NOT an indicator: a **larger GDP share from the primary sector** (APPSC-G2 2008).

## SUB 7 | Determinants of Growth; Capital-output Ratio
[14:7:0] TABLE head: Economic | Non-economic
  [14:7:0.0] Capital formation, natural resources, technical progress, human capital, labour | Institutions, rule of law, social behaviour, political stability, population quality
[14:7:1] - **Capital formation** = most important growth factor (Planning Commission). Higher saving/investment → higher growth; rising investment reduces poverty.
[14:7:2] - **Higher ICOR** = more capital per extra output → **lower growth**.
[14:7:3] - Capital formation may fail to raise output: idle/misused capital, missing inputs, weak demand (**all of these**); capital-intensive investment adds few jobs.
[14:7:4] - **Corruption** = a barrier (governance factor), not a contributor. Technocrats and bureaucrats are NOT a major factor.
[14:7:5] - Development and population growth affect **each other**. Slow public-sector jobs: reassessment of the State's role (NEP 1991).
## SUB 8 | Sustainable Development and Ap Data
[14:8:0] - **Brundtland** (*Our Common Future*, **1987**): meets present needs **without compromising future generations**; does not ban resource use.
[14:8:1] - Sustainable growth = policy for **economic and social well-being** (APPSC 2018 key). **Neemrana** (Rajasthan) = model.
[14:8:2] - **Green GDP** = GDP minus natural resource depletion and environmental degradation costs.
[14:8:3] - **Green economy (UNEP)**: low-carbon, resource-efficient, socially inclusive; not only agriculture.
[14:8:4] - **AP**: GSDP 2025-26 **₹17.62 lakh crore** (+10.75%); per capita **₹2,94,507** vs India ₹2,19,575. **Swarna Andhra @2047**: 15%+ growth, ₹308 lakh crore GSDP, ₹55 lakh per capita, **651 KPIs**. AP = first State with **monthly economic reports**. Claim that **AP Economic Development Board** has 6 divisions is incorrect. Vision 2020 for India: **A.P.J. Abdul Kalam**.
[14:8:5] - **AP Economic Development Board (APEDB):** set up to speed up inclusive economic growth and bring investment into Andhra Pradesh.
