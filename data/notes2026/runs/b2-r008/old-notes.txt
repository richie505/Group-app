=== NOTES 2:8 | Fundamental Rights – Article 12, Article 13 and Doctrines
## SUB 0 | Part III – Overview and Number Game
[8:0:0] - Location: **Part III, Articles 12–35** (24 Articles); FRs are **granted by the Constitution**.
[8:0:1] - Called the **"Magna Carta of India"**.
[8:0:2] - Borrowed mainly from the **Bill of Rights of the USA** (not the Irish Constitution).
[8:0:3] - Originally **seven**, now **six** (APPSC 2018).
[8:0:4] - **Right to Property** removed by the **44th Amendment (1978)** – Art. 19(1)(f) and Art. 31 deleted; now a **constitutional/legal right under Art. 300A (Part XII)**.
[8:0:5] - Map: **Art. 12** defines State; **Art. 13** voids conflicting laws; **Arts. 33–35** limits and legislation.
[8:0:6] - Neighbours: **Part IV (Arts. 36–51)** DPSP; **Part IVA (Art. 51A)** Fundamental Duties.
[8:0:7] # Six Groups
[8:0:8] TABLE head: Group | Articles
  [8:0:8.0] Right to Equality | 14–18
  [8:0:8.1] Right to Freedom | 19–22
  [8:0:8.2] Right against Exploitation | 23–24
  [8:0:8.3] Right to Freedom of Religion | 25–28
  [8:0:8.4] Cultural and Educational Rights | 29–30
  [8:0:8.5] Right to Constitutional Remedies | Article 32 alone (not 30–32)
[8:0:9] - **Art. 15** (no discrimination on religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth) and **Art. 17** (abolition of untouchability) fall under **Right to Equality**.
[8:0:10] Right to Protest and Right to Die are NOT among the Fundamental Rights.

## SUB 1 | Features of Part III
[8:1:0] # Justiciable and Qualified
[8:1:1] - **Justiciable**: aggrieved person can move the **SC directly (Art. 32)**; **HCs (Art. 226)** enforce FRs and other legal rights; the judiciary is the **guardian** of FRs.
[8:1:2] - **Not absolute**: FRs are **qualified**, subject to **reasonable restrictions** imposed by law only on grounds the Constitution names.
[8:1:3] - **Courts judge reasonableness** (judicial review, Arts. 13, 32, 226).
[8:1:4] - **Art. 19** lists **11 restriction grounds** in all.
[8:1:5] - FRs are **claims of citizens against the State**; they **limit the State – even Parliament** (Art. 13).
[8:1:6] - **Both natural and legal**; protect individuals and minorities (APPSC 2023: both correct).
[8:1:7] - **Britain** has **no written FRs**; protection rests on conventions and the rule of law.
[8:1:8] APPSC GS 2019: "FRs are absolute" and "all FRs are suspended in an emergency" – both FALSE (answer: Neither).
[8:1:9] # Negative vs Positive Rights
[8:1:10] TABLE head: Nature | Articles
  [8:1:10.0] Negative (limits on the State) | 14, 15(1), 16(2), 18(1), 20, 22(1), 28(1)
  [8:1:10.1] Positive (confer privileges) | 21A, 29, 30
  [8:1:10.2] Absolute limits on legislative power | 15, 17, 18, 20, 24
  [8:1:10.3] Also bind private persons | 15(2), 17, 23, 24
## SUB 2 | Citizens Only vs All Persons
[8:2:0] TABLE head: Citizens only | All persons (citizens + foreigners)
  [8:2:0.0] Art. 15 – no discrimination | Art. 14 – equality before law
  [8:2:0.1] Art. 16 – public employment | Art. 20 – protection in conviction
  [8:2:0.2] Art. 19 – six freedoms | Art. 21 / 21A – life, education
  [8:2:0.3] Art. 29 – minorities' culture | Art. 22 – arrest safeguards (not enemy aliens)
  [8:2:0.4] Art. 30 – minorities' institutions | Arts. 23–24 – exploitation
  [8:2:0.5]  | Arts. 25–28 – religion
  [8:2:0.6]  | Art. 32 – constitutional remedies
[8:2:1] - **Denied to aliens**: **Arts. 29–30, 19, 16** (APPSC G1 2022 key: I, II and IV) – **not 23–24**.
[8:2:2] - **Enemy aliens** lose even **Art. 22** safeguards (**Art. 22(3)**).
[8:2:3] - **Companies** can claim all-persons rights, but **not Art. 19**.
[8:2:4] - A **British citizen** in India does not get **freedom of trade and profession**; foreigners do not get **freedom of expression, free movement, residence**.
[8:2:5] - Citizens only: right against discrimination, **move freely [Art. 19(1)(d)]**, **contest elections**.
[8:2:6] Five citizens-only Articles: 15, 16, 19, 29, 30.
## SUB 3 | Article 12 – Meaning of "state"
[8:3:0] # Definition
[8:3:1] - **Government and Parliament of India**.
[8:3:2] - **Government and legislature of each State**.
[8:3:3] - **All local authorities**.
[8:3:4] - **Other authorities** in India or under the control of the Government of India.
[8:3:5] - **Private bodies**: writs do not ordinarily lie against them just because they do a public activity, unless the **State acts in collusion**.
[8:3:6] # Instrumentality Cases
[8:3:7] TABLE head: Case | Year | Holding
  [8:3:7.0] Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal | 1967 | Statutory bodies are "other authorities"
  [8:3:7.1] R.D. Shetty v. International Airport Authority | 1979 | Laid down tests for an instrumentality or agency of the State
  [8:3:7.2] Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib | 1981 | Society running a Regional Engineering College = State; test is creation and control, not legal form – govt share capital, financial aid, deep and pervasive control, monopoly, public function
  [8:3:7.3] Pradeep Kumar Biswas | 2002 | State if financially, functionally and administratively dominated by govt
## SUB 4 | Article 13 – Meaning of "law" and Supremacy
[8:4:0] # Clauses
[8:4:1] TABLE head: Clause | Provision
  [8:4:1.0] 13(1) | Pre-constitution laws inconsistent with FRs are void to the extent of inconsistency
  [8:4:1.1] 13(2) | State shall not make laws abridging FRs; such post-constitution laws void to that extent
  [8:4:1.2] 13(3)(a) | "Law" includes ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage having force of law
  [8:4:1.3] 13(4) | Art. 13 does not apply to Art. 368 amendments (24th Amendment, 1971)
[8:4:2] - Basis of **judicial review**; makes FRs **supreme over ordinary law** (Constitution ranks above laws).
[8:4:3] 13(1) refers to laws in force before the **commencement of the Constitution** – not "before Independence".
[8:4:4] # Are Amendments "Law"?
[8:4:5] TABLE head: Case / Amendment | Year | Position
  [8:4:5.0] Shankari Prasad | 1951 | Amendment is not "law" under Art. 13; Parliament can amend FRs
  [8:4:5.1] Golaknath | 1967 | FRs have a transcendental position; Parliament cannot abridge them
  [8:4:5.2] 24th Amendment | 1971 | Added Art. 13(4) in reply
  [8:4:5.3] Kesavananda Bharati | 1973 | Amendable, but basic structure cannot be destroyed
[8:4:6] 1967 twice: Rajasthan SEB (Art. 12) and Golaknath (Art. 13).
## SUB 5 | Doctrines – Meaning and Case
[8:5:0] TABLE head: Doctrine | Meaning | Case
  [8:5:0.0] Severability | Only the inconsistent part is void; the rest survives if separable | A.K. Gopalan (1950); R.M.D.C. (1957); Motor General Traders v. State of AP (1984)
  [8:5:0.1] Eclipse | Inconsistent law is dormant, not dead; revives when the conflict is removed; mainly pre-constitution laws | Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v. State of MP (1955) – revived after the 1st Amendment; State of Gujarat v. Ambica Mills (1974) – extended to post-constitution laws for non-citizens
  [8:5:0.2] Waiver | A person cannot waive an FR; only statutory/contractual rights can be given up | Basheshar Nath v. CIT (1959)
  [8:5:0.3] Prospective overruling | Ruling operates only for the future | Golaknath (1967)
  [8:5:0.4] Progressive interpretation | Constitution as a living document | Maneka Gandhi (1978) widened Art. 21; trauma care under Art. 21 – SaveLIFE Foundation v. UoI (2026)
  [8:5:0.5] Absolute liability | Hazardous industry liable without strict-liability exceptions | M.C. Mehta (1987) – Oleum gas leak
  [8:5:0.6] Territorial nexus | State law valid outside State if sufficient connection | State of Bombay v. R.M.D.C. (1957)
  [8:5:0.7] Pith and substance | Union–State List encroachment: looks at true nature and character of the law | Federal doctrine
[8:5:1] - Other federal doctrines: **aspect doctrine**, **colourable legislation**, **incidental and ancillary powers**, **repugnancy**.
[8:5:2] APPSC GS 2018: "Void only to the extent of inconsistency, rest valid" = Doctrine of Severability.
## SUB 6 | Who Can Limit FRs – Articles 33, 34, 35 and 359
[8:6:0] # Amendment, not Ordinary Law
[8:6:1] - FRs can be changed **only by a constitutional amendment**, subject to the **basic structure (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973)**; an ordinary law cannot.
[8:6:2] # Articles 33–35
[8:6:3] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [8:6:3.0] 33 | Parliament alone may restrict FRs of armed forces, paramilitary, police, intelligence and their telecom staff – even non-combatants (cooks, barbers)
  [8:6:3.1] 34 | Restrictions while martial law is in force; Parliament may pass an indemnity Act; "martial law" not defined
  [8:6:3.2] 35 | Only Parliament (not States) legislates on 16(3), 32(3), 33, 34 and punishments for Part III offences – for uniformity
[8:6:4] # Suspension – Article 359
[8:6:5] - During a **National Emergency**, the **President** may by order suspend the right to move courts for **specified FRs**.
[8:6:6] - **Never Arts. 20 and 21** (**44th Amendment, 1978**).
[8:6:7] - Arts. 14, 19, 22 and Art. 32 for other rights **can be affected**.
