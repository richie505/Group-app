=== Eighth Schedule – Languages
# The 22 scheduled languages
- **Eighth Schedule = languages recognised by the Constitution** (Arts. **344(1) and 351**) – the "regional languages" / "official languages" schedule; **not** the Fourth, Sixth, Seventh or Ninth.
- **Number:** originally **14**, now **22** (not 18, 21 or 24).
- **The 22:** Assamese, Bengali, **Bodo**, **Dogri**, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, **Kashmiri**, **Konkani**, **Maithili**, Malayalam, **Manipuri**, Marathi, **Nepali**, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, **Santhali**, **Sindhi**, Tamil, Telugu, **Urdu**.
- **NOT in the Eighth Schedule:** **English**, **Rajasthani**, **Bhojpuri**, **Gondi**, Bhutia (Bhoti), Tulu, Chhattisgarhi, Gor Boli (Lambadi) – all asked as the odd one out.
- Hindi, Sanskrit, Urdu, Punjabi, Gujarati, Kashmiri were among the **original 14**.
# Languages added by amendment
| Amendment | Year | Languages added |
|---|---|---|
| **21st** | **1967** | **Sindhi** |
| **71st** | **1992** | **Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali** |
| **92nd** | **2003** | **Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali** |
- **71st ≠ Maithili:** Maithili came with the **92nd** (UPSC 2024: 71st = Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali only).
- **Konkani** entered in **1992** (not 2003). 96th Amendment (2011) renamed Oriya as **Odia** (no new language).
- In **1990** Sindhi was the only language added by amendment; since 1992 Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali also are.
# Official Language Commission (Art. 344)
- First **Official Language Commission** set up **7 June 1955** under Art. 344 – chairman **B.G. Kher**; report 1956, reviewed by a parliamentary committee under **G.B. Pant**; **Official Languages Act 1963**.
- Official language provisions = **Part XVII, Arts. 343–351**.
# Demands for inclusion (current linkage)
- No fixed criteria for inclusion (**Pahwa Committee 1996**, **Sitakant Mohapatra Committee 2003**); demands are raised in Parliament – e.g. **Chhattisgarhi** in the Rajya Sabha (CGPSC 2019, 2020) → the **8th Schedule**.
- **Jun 2026**: fresh demands to include **Bhojpuri** (Konkani, Maithili, Santali are already in).
- **Jul 2026**: Ministry of Tribal Affairs told Parliament of continuing demands to include **Gor Boli (Lambadi)**, the **Banjara** language of AP and Telangana (PIB).
- **Sep 2026**: Karnataka made **Tulu** an additional official language in Dakshina Kannada and Udupi – Tulu is **not** in the Eighth Schedule.
> Odd-one-out pattern: English (4 times), Rajasthani, Gondi, Bhojpuri, Bhutani – none is scheduled; Urdu, Sindhi, Nepali, Dogri, Kashmiri, Sanskrit are.

# Third Schedule – oaths
- Covers: Union and State **ministers**; candidates for and members of **Parliament** and **State legislatures**; Judges of the **Supreme Court** and **High Courts**; the **CAG**.
- "Bear true faith and allegiance … uphold the sovereignty and integrity of India … **perform the duties of my office without fear or favour** … **uphold the Constitution and the laws**" = Form IV – **Judges of the Supreme Court (incl. the Chief Justice of India) and the CAG**. The President's oath says "preserve, protect and defend"; an MP's "faithfully discharge the duty".
- **Outside the Third Schedule:** President – **Art. 60**; Vice-President – **Art. 69**; Governor – **Art. 159**.

=== Ninth Schedule – Land Reforms and Judicial Review
# Basics
- Added by the **First Amendment Act, 1951** – the **first Schedule added** – during the prime ministership of **Jawaharlal Nehru** (not the 8th, 9th or 42nd Amendment).
- Linked with **Article 31B**: Acts placed in it cannot be challenged for violating Fundamental Rights.
- Purpose: to protect **state land-reform and zamindari-abolition laws** from judicial scrutiny – so **land reform laws are in the 9th Schedule** (not the 7th, 8th or 10th).
- Acts: **13** originally → about **284** now (NextGen counts 282).
- **I.R. Coelho (2007):** laws added **after 24 April 1973** (the Kesavananda date) are **open to judicial review** on the basic-structure test.
# Tamil Nadu 69% reservation
- The **Tamil Nadu Reservation Act, 1993/94** (69% reservation for SCs, STs and OBCs – above the 50% Indra Sawhney limit) was placed in the **IX Schedule** by the **76th Amendment Act, 1994** to shield it from judicial review.

=== Tenth Schedule – Anti-defection
- **Anti-defection law = Tenth Schedule** (Arts. **102(2) and 191(2)**) – not the 2nd, 5th, 6th, 7th, 8th or 9th.
- Added by the **52nd Amendment Act, 1985** (Rajiv Gandhi government) – not the 42nd, 50th, 51st, 53rd, 85th or 95th.
- **91st Amendment (2003)** removed the **'split'** (one-third) exception; **merger** (two-thirds) still protected; the presiding officer decides disqualification (subject to judicial review – *Kihoto Hollohan*, 1992).

# Article 1 – Union of States
- India is a **"Union"** – not the result of an agreement among States; **States cannot secede**.
- "**An indestructible Union of destructible States**".
