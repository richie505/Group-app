## SUB 0 | Eighth Schedule – Languages
[0:0] # The 22 scheduled languages
[0:1] - **Eighth Schedule = languages recognised by the Constitution** (Arts. **344(1) and 351**) – the "regional languages" / "official languages" schedule; **not** the Fourth, Sixth, Seventh or Ninth.
[0:2] - **Number:** originally **14**, now **22** (not 18, 21 or 24).
[0:3] - **The 22:** Assamese, Bengali, **Bodo**, **Dogri**, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, **Kashmiri**, **Konkani**, **Maithili**, Malayalam, **Manipuri**, Marathi, **Nepali**, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, **Santhali**, **Sindhi**, Tamil, Telugu, **Urdu**.
[0:4] - **NOT in the Eighth Schedule:** **English**, **Rajasthani**, **Bhojpuri**, **Gondi**, Bhutia (Bhoti), Tulu, Chhattisgarhi, Gor Boli (Lambadi) – all asked as the odd one out.
[0:5] - Hindi, Sanskrit, Urdu, Punjabi, Gujarati, Kashmiri were among the **original 14**.
[0:6] # Languages added by amendment
[0:7] TABLE head: Amendment | Year | Languages added
  [0:7.0] 21st | 1967 | Sindhi
  [0:7.1] 71st | 1992 | Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali
  [0:7.2] 92nd | 2003 | Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali
[0:8] - **71st ≠ Maithili:** Maithili came with the **92nd** (UPSC 2024: 71st = Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali only).
[0:9] - **Konkani** entered in **1992** (not 2003). 96th Amendment (2011) renamed Oriya as **Odia** (no new language).
[0:10] - In **1990** Sindhi was the only language added by amendment; since 1992 Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali also are.
[0:11] # Official Language Commission (Art. 344)
[0:12] - First **Official Language Commission** set up **7 June 1955** under Art. 344 – chairman **B.G. Kher**; report 1956, reviewed by a parliamentary committee under **G.B. Pant**; **Official Languages Act 1963**.
[0:13] - Official language provisions = **Part XVII, Arts. 343–351**.
[0:14] # Demands for inclusion (current linkage)
[0:15] - No fixed criteria for inclusion (**Pahwa Committee 1996**, **Sitakant Mohapatra Committee 2003**); demands are raised in Parliament – e.g. **Chhattisgarhi** in the Rajya Sabha (CGPSC 2019, 2020) → the **8th Schedule**.
[0:16] - **Jun 2026**: fresh demands to include **Bhojpuri** (Konkani, Maithili, Santali are already in).
[0:17] - **Jul 2026**: Ministry of Tribal Affairs told Parliament of continuing demands to include **Gor Boli (Lambadi)**, the **Banjara** language of AP and Telangana (PIB).
[0:18] - **Sep 2026**: Karnataka made **Tulu** an additional official language in Dakshina Kannada and Udupi – Tulu is **not** in the Eighth Schedule.
[0:19] > Odd-one-out pattern: English (4 times), Rajasthani, Gondi, Bhojpuri, Bhutani – none is scheduled; Urdu, Sindhi, Nepali, Dogri, Kashmiri, Sanskrit are.

[1:8] # Third Schedule – oaths
[1:9] - Covers: Union and State **ministers**; candidates for and members of **Parliament** and **State legislatures**; Judges of the **Supreme Court** and **High Courts**; the **CAG**.
[1:10] - "Bear true faith and allegiance … uphold the sovereignty and integrity of India … **perform the duties of my office without fear or favour** … **uphold the Constitution and the laws**" = Form IV – **Judges of the Supreme Court (incl. the Chief Justice of India) and the CAG**. The President's oath says "preserve, protect and defend"; an MP's "faithfully discharge the duty".
[1:11] - **Outside the Third Schedule:** President – **Art. 60**; Vice-President – **Art. 69**; Governor – **Art. 159**.

## SUB 2 | Ninth Schedule – Land Reforms and Judicial Review
[2:0] # Basics
[2:1] - Added by the **First Amendment Act, 1951** – the **first Schedule added** – during the prime ministership of **Jawaharlal Nehru** (not the 8th, 9th or 42nd Amendment).
[2:2] - Linked with **Article 31B**: Acts placed in it cannot be challenged for violating Fundamental Rights.
[2:3] - Purpose: to protect **state land-reform and zamindari-abolition laws** from judicial scrutiny – so **land reform laws are in the 9th Schedule** (not the 7th, 8th or 10th).
[2:4] - Acts: **13** originally → about **284** now (NextGen counts 282).
[2:5] - **I.R. Coelho (2007):** laws added **after 24 April 1973** (the Kesavananda date) are **open to judicial review** on the basic-structure test.
[2:6] # Tamil Nadu 69% reservation
[2:7] - The **Tamil Nadu Reservation Act, 1993/94** (69% reservation for SCs, STs and OBCs – above the 50% Indra Sawhney limit) was placed in the **IX Schedule** by the **76th Amendment Act, 1994** to shield it from judicial review.
[2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.157, p.217, p.261; Rocket Polity Part 1 p.17; Indian_Polity Part 1 ch.5 p.90 (76th Amendment)

## SUB 4 | Tenth Schedule – Anti-defection
[4:0] - **Anti-defection law = Tenth Schedule** (Arts. **102(2) and 191(2)**) – not the 2nd, 5th, 6th, 7th, 8th or 9th.
[4:1] - Added by the **52nd Amendment Act, 1985** (Rajiv Gandhi government) – not the 42nd, 50th, 51st, 53rd, 85th or 95th.
[4:2] - **91st Amendment (2003)** removed the **'split'** (one-third) exception; **merger** (two-thirds) still protected; the presiding officer decides disqualification (subject to judicial review – *Kihoto Hollohan*, 1992).
[4:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.59, p.157; Rocket Polity Part 1 p.17
