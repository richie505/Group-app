[0:5] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [0:5.0] Date, place | 26-27 May 1913, Bapatla (Guntur district) – not Kakinada, Guntur, Nidadavolu, Madras, Gudivada or Tadepalligudem
  [0:5.1] President | B.N. Sarma (Bayya / Baiya Narasimheswara Sarma), member of the Madras Legislative Council – not Nyapati Subba Rao, Pattabhi or Konda Venkatappayya
  [0:5.2] Secretary | Konda Venkatappayya
  [0:5.3] Attendance | About 800 delegates, 2,000 visitors; delegates also from Hyderabad, Warangal, Nagpur
  [0:5.4] Province resolution | Moved by Vemavarapu Ramadasu Pantulu; opposed by Nyapati Subba Rao, M. Adinarayanaiah, Mocherla Ramachandra Rao → amended to "take it up at the next conference" (deferred); a committee under Pattabhi to collect opinion
  [0:5.5] University | A separate university for Andhras, with Telugu as medium, first demanded here
  [0:5.6] Other agenda | An English daily; lifting the ban on Telugus in the Army; fund for technical training abroad
[0:6] - **B.N. Sarma's caution:** the demand was premature and "might do **more harm than good**"; "**First we are Indians, then we are Andhras**" – so he presided but **opposed** a separate state (APPSC G2 2017).
[0:7] - **Result:** the awakening got a **respectable forum**; Krishna and Guntur delegates were disappointed; Rayalaseema, Ganjam and Visakhapatnam were lukewarm.
[0:8] - After Bapatla, the **Nellore** district meeting called the demand "**not yet ripe**".
[0:9] - **Aims of the Andhra movement** (propaganda phase 1913-20): Andhra province, Andhra University, development of Telugu, cultural revival – love of education, culture and high ideals, interest in sanitation, health and physical culture.

## SUB 3 | Causes, Press and Early Writings
[3:0] # Grievances in the Madras Presidency
[3:1] - Telugus about **40%** of the Presidency's people (58% of its area), lumped as "Madrasis"; dominant language **Tamil**; Tamils taunted them for lacking figures like Bhasyam Aiyengar.
[3:2] TABLE head: Post (c. 1911) | Telugu | Non-Telugu
  [3:2.0] High posts (64) | 8 | 56
  [3:2.1] Deputy Collectors | 21 | 39
  [3:2.2] District Munsifs | 30 | 93
  [3:2.3] District Judges | 0 | 19
[3:3] - **Main cause:** **unequal opportunities in education, employment and administration**; higher, technical and professional education out of reach (1915: 8 of 31 colleges, **no engineering, medical or law college**); Anglo-Indian officers knowing little Telugu.
[3:4] - Revenue grievance: surplus of the Andhra **deltas** spent on Tamil areas; early demands – province, university, **port at Visakhapatnam**.
[3:5] - **Keenest:** **Krishna and Guntur**; lukewarm: Rayalaseema, Nellore, Ganjam, Visakhapatnam.
[3:6] - **Precedent:** Bengal partition 1905, annulled 1911; **Bihar** separation announced at the **Delhi Durbar, 12 Dec 1911** → Bihar and Orissa province 1 April 1912 → provinces could be redrawn on linguistic lines.
[3:7] - **Tamil press:** opposed and **raised doubts about the Andhras' ability** to run a province; an Andhra Patrika writer replied that separation would **not harm national unity**.
