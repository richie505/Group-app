=== Space – ISRO Missions, Launches and Space Law
# PSLV-C62 Failure
- **12 January 2026**, 10:18 a.m., Sriharikota – **EOS-N1** mission by **NSIL**.
- **Third-stage anomaly** – **all 15 satellites lost** (7 Indian, 8 from France, Nepal, Brazil, UK).
- Payload included **DRDO's "Anvesh"** surveillance satellite (detects targets under foliage/bunkers) and **Dhruva Space** satellites.
- Came after **PSLV-C61/EOS-09 failed in May 2025** (also the 3rd stage) – **two PSLV failures in a row**; ISRO staff unions raised outsourcing under the 2023 Space Policy.

# World Space and Space Security
- **China's TPG-1000C** high-power **microwave weapon** – Northwest Institute of Nuclear Technology (team of Wang Gang); about **20 GW for about 1 minute**; can disrupt LEO satellites.
- **Space-debris impact prediction using seismometer networks** (sonic booms) – **Johns Hopkins University**; tested on Shenzhou-15 debris (2 April 2024) with 125 seismometers in California.
- **US Lunar Fission Surface Power Project** – small fission reactor on the Moon by the **early 2030s** for NASA's **Artemis Base Camp**; would be the **first permanent nuclear power source beyond Earth orbit**.
| Technology | Key point |
|---|---|
| Compact fission reactor | Shipping-container size; 10-100 kW; NASA KRUSTY demo up to 10 kW |
| Nuclear Thermal Propulsion (NTP) | Reactor heats propellant (liquid hydrogen); high thrust; US DRACO test by 2026 |
| Nuclear Electric Propulsion (NEP) | Reactor electricity powers ion/plasma thrusters; low continuous thrust |
| Space law | Key point |
| Outer Space Treaty 1967, Article IV | Bans nuclear weapons/WMDs in orbit and on celestial bodies – does not ban peaceful nuclear power |
| UN Principles on Nuclear Power Sources in Outer Space, 1992 | UNGA Resolution 47/68; non-binding; do not cover NTP/NEP propulsion |
| 1972 Liability Convention | Absolute liability for damage on Earth or to aircraft in flight; fault-based in outer space |
| COPUOS | UN Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space |
- **NPT Nuclear Weapon States**: **USA, Russia, UK, France, China** – India, Pakistan, Israel are not recognised. NSG membership does not mean automatic NPT membership.
- **RTG (Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generator):** turns the decay heat of plutonium-238 into electricity; gives a few hundred watts; used on deep-space probes.

=== Semiconductors, Processors and Supercomputing
# DHRUV64
- **India's first fully indigenous 1.0 GHz, 64-bit dual-core microprocessor**, by **C-DAC** under the **Microprocessor Development Programme (MDP)**.
- Architecture: open-source **RISC-V** (no licence costs); the **third chip fabricated under the Digital India RISC-V (DIR-V) Programme**.
- Uses: 5G infrastructure, automotive, consumer electronics, industrial automation, IoT. Next: **Dhanush and Dhanush+** (DHANUSH64, DHANUSH64+ SoC).
- India uses about **20% of the world's microprocessors** and has 20% of the world's chip design engineers.

=== Maritime Security, Ports and Ocean Technology
# Bureau of Port Security (BoPS)
- **Statutory body** under **Section 13 of the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025**; under the **Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways** (**not MHA**).
- **Modelled on the Bureau of Civil Aviation Security (BCAS)**.
- Mandate: enforce the **ISPS Code** (International Ship and Port Facility Security) at major and non-major ports; dedicated cybersecurity division.
- **CISF** – designated **Recognised Security Organisation**.
- Ports: **12 major** (Union List, about 53% cargo); 217 non-major (66 cargo ports); private Mundra, Sikka about 19%. Coastal shipping +118%; nine Indian ports in the World Bank Container Port Performance Index; coastline over 7,500 km.
- New laws 2025: **Indian Ports Act, 2025** (replaces 1908 Act), Merchant Shipping Act, 2025, Coastal Shipping Act, 2025; Maritime India Vision 2030.

=== Health – HIV, Vaccines, Neurotechnology and Biosecurity
# Lenacapavir (Yeztugo)
- **First twice-yearly injectable PrEP for HIV** – by **Gilead Sciences** (est. 1987, Foster City); brand **"Yeztugo"**.
- **US FDA approval 18 June 2025** – **world's first capsid-based HIV inhibitor**; trials: **100%** effective in PURPOSE 1 (zero infections in women) and **99.9%** in PURPOSE 2.
- **Not a vaccine**; only for **HIV-negative** persons; does **not replace ART**.
- **Zimbabwe rollout began 19 February 2026** at **Epworth** (~20 km south of Harare); 46,000 doses, 24 sites; funded by the US government and Global Fund; Zimbabwe among **9 countries** chosen by WHO for early access.
- **Zimbabwe PrEP order**: oral PrEP (**2016**), dapivirine vaginal ring (**2021**), injectable cabotegravir (**2024**), lenacapavir (**2026**).
- Science: resistant HIV replicates at only 20-30% of normal – the **capsid** is a high-constraint target (capsid shape paper 1999, Science). **AZT (zidovudine), 1987** – first HIV drug, targeted **reverse transcriptase**.
- Global: ~41 million living with HIV; ~27 million (67%) in Sub-Saharan Africa; 44 million+ deaths since the 1980s.
- **Tattooing** can transmit **Hepatitis B and HIV** – not Chikungunya.

# PERT and Other Genome Tools
- **PERT** = **Prime-Editing-mediated Readthrough of Premature Termination codons** – **edits a tRNA gene into a suppressor tRNA**; published in **Nature** by **Broad Institute, Harvard, University of Minnesota**.
- Targets **nonsense mutations** (premature stop codon, e.g. TAG) – about **25%** of disease-causing genetic changes.
- Human cells have **418 tRNA genes**; **4 promising** (leucine, arginine, tyrosine, serine); efficiency **60-80% vs HDR 10-20%**; mouse models – Batten, Tay-Sachs, Niemann-Pick C1.
- **AlphaGenome** – **Google DeepMind**; predicts DNA mutations and gene regulation.
- **DisoBind** – **NCBS (TIFR)** deep-learning tool for **intrinsically disordered proteins**; NCBS est. 1992, TIFR 1945.
- **PATH-GENI** – open-source software (Ministry of S&T) for **ligand unbinding pathways** and **drug residence time**.
- **Biomaterials**: **drop-in** (chemically identical to conventional, e.g. **bio-PET**), **drop-out** (chemically different, e.g. **PLA**), novel (self-healing, bioactive); India bioplastics market ~USD 500 million (2024).

=== Energy Materials – Batteries and Rare-earth Elements
# Sodium-ion vs Lithium-ion
| Feature | Lithium-ion | Sodium-ion |
|---|---|---|
| Current collector | Copper (anode side) | Aluminium on both electrodes |
| Anode / cathode | Anode = graphite; cathodes LiCoO2, LiMn2O4, LiFePO4 | Sodium from soda ash; layered oxides outperform polyanionic and Prussian blue analogues |
| Transport | Shipped at 30% charge or less | Stored and shipped at zero volts; not "Dangerous Goods" |
| Safety | – | Lower thermal-runaway risk |
- Li-ion price ~$1,100/kWh (early 2010s) -> ~$108/kWh (2025). **PLI for Advanced Chemistry Cells (2021)**: ~40 GWh allocated, only ~1 GWh commissioned.
- Sodium-ion global capacity ~70 GWh (2025) -> about **400 GWh by 2030**; expected to undercut Li-ion costs by 2035.
> Graphite is the anode of a Li-ion cell, not a cathode material.
# Rare-Earth Elements
- **17 elements = 15 lanthanides (lanthanum to lutetium) + scandium and yttrium** (Group 3).
- **Not geologically rare** (cerium is as abundant as copper); rarity is economic/technological (hard separation).
- **Lithium, cobalt, gallium, germanium are critical minerals, not REEs**.
- Magnetism and phosphor emission come from **localised 4f electrons**. Ores: **bastnäsite, monazite, ion-adsorption clays** (may contain thorium/uranium).
| Use | Elements |
|---|---|
| Permanent magnets (NdFeB) | Neodymium – EV motors, wind turbines |
| Phosphors (LEDs, flat-screen TVs, monitors) | Europium, terbium, yttrium – phosphorescent properties |
| Lasers, fibre optics | Neodymium, erbium |
| Refining catalysts | Lanthanum, cerium |
| Country (USGS) | Reserves (million tonnes REO) |
| China | 44 |
| Brazil | 21 |
| India | 6.9 |
- World reserves over 90 million tonnes. **China** – highest share in mining; about **91% of separation/refining**; 94% of sintered permanent magnets. REEs are widely used in AI, robotics, defence and space.
- **Japan** plans to extract REE-rich mud from 6 km underwater near **Minamitori Island** (early 2026).
