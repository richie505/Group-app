## SUB 0 | Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955
[0:0] - **Enacted 1955** as the **Untouchability (Offences) Act** (8 May 1955; **in force 1 June 1955**) – first law enforcing **Art. 17**; renamed **Protection of Civil Rights Act** by Act 106 of **1976** (w.e.f. 19 Nov 1976); **Rules 1977**.
[0:1] - **Extent – s.1(2): "the whole of India"** (J&K never excluded).
[0:2] - **Civil right** = any right accruing from the abolition of untouchability by Art. 17. **"Untouchability" is not defined** – neither in Art. 17 nor in the Act.
[0:3] - Punishes the **preaching and practice of untouchability**: religious and social disabilities.
[0:4] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [0:4.0] 3 | Religious disabilities – entry/worship in public places of worship (incl. a private temple allowed for public worship)
  [0:4.1] 4 | Social disabilities – shops, hotels, water sources, roads, jewellery and finery
  [0:4.2] 5 | Refusing admission to hospital, dispensary, educational institution, hostel
  [0:4.3] 6 | Refusing to sell goods or render services
  [0:4.4] 7 | Other offences – insulting an SC member (7(1)(d)); preaching or justifying untouchability
  [0:4.5] 7A | Compelling scavenging, removing carcasses, flaying – deemed untouchability
  [0:4.6] 10 | Abetment – a public servant wilfully neglecting investigation
  [0:4.7] 10A | Collective fine imposed by the State Government on inhabitants of an area
  [0:4.8] 12 | Court presumes the untouchability ground when the victim is a Scheduled Caste member (not ST)
  [0:4.9] 14 | Offences by companies – person in charge, and any director, manager, secretary or officer consenting
  [0:4.10] 15 | Cognizable; triable summarily (except where minimum term exceeds 3 months)
  [0:4.11] 15A | State duties – legal aid, special courts, committees, surveys of prone areas, annual report
  [0:4.12] 16A | No probation above 14 years
[0:5] - **First offence:** **1-6 months** and fine **₹100-500**. Offences are **non-compoundable** (the Act allows no compounding).
[0:6] - Not an offence when accused and victim belong to the **same social group** (no "ground of untouchability").
[0:7] - Convict is **disqualified** from elections to Parliament/State Legislatures (**s.8, RPA 1951** – for a fixed period, then may contest).
[0:8] - **Implementation:** States/UTs; nodal Centre – **Social Justice and Empowerment** with MHA. (NHRC is under the Protection of Human Rights Act 1993, not PCR.)
[0:9] - **Sukanya Shantha (2024):** Art. 17 bars caste-based **purity stigma**.
[0:10] > Trap: PCR **s.12** = presumption, **s.14** = companies, **s.10A** = collective fine; PCR in force **1955**, not 30 Jan 1990.

## SUB 1 | PoA Act 1989 – Enactment, Scope and Offences
[1:0] - **Act 33 of 1989**, assent **11 Sept 1989**; **in force 30 January 1990**; **23 sections**; Rules **1995**; amended **2015** (in force 26 Jan 2016) and **2018** (20 Aug 2018). J&K excluded until **2019**.
[1:1] - **Why:** the PCR Act 1955 and the IPC proved **inadequate** to check atrocities against SCs/STs.
[1:2] - **Long title (preamble):** prevent atrocities, set up **Special Courts**, and provide **relief and rehabilitation of victims**.
[1:3] - **Who:** offences by persons **"not being a member of a SC or ST"** – incl. government employees; not crimes among SCs/STs.
[1:4] TABLE head: Provision | Punishment
  [1:4.0] s.3(1) atrocities (e.g. dumping excreta, waste, carcasses in premises; begar; land grab; witchcraft accusation) | Not less than 6 months, up to 5 years, and fine
  [1:4.1] s.3(2)(i) false evidence to convict an SC/ST of a capital offence | Life imprisonment and fine (death if an innocent is executed)
  [1:4.2] s.3(2)(vii) offence by a public servant | Not less than 1 year
  [1:4.3] s.4 wilful neglect of duty by public servant | 6 months to 1 year
  [1:4.4] s.5 subsequent conviction | Not less than 1 year
[1:5] - **2015 additions to s.3(1):** tonsuring, **garlanding with footwear**, carcass disposal, manual scavenging, abuse by caste name, devadasi dedication, **witchcraft (zb)**, **social/economic boycott (zc)**, preventing entry to **educational institutions**, hospitals, places of worship.
[1:6] - **s.6:** applies IPC **s.34, Chapters III, IV (general exceptions – private defence), V, V-A, s.149, XXIII**. So the accused **can** plead private defence or that he belongs to the victim's caste.
[1:7] - **s.8 presumptions:** financial help → **abetment**; group offence from a land dispute → **common intention / common object**.
[1:8] > Trap: enacted **1989**, in force **1990**; PoA covers offences by **non-SC/ST** persons only.

## SUB 2 | PoA Act – Special Courts, Powers and Procedure
[2:0] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [2:0.0] 2(1)(bc) | Defines economic boycott (2015)
  [2:0.1] 7 | Special Court forfeits property of convict
  [2:0.2] 9 | State Government may confer police powers on its officers
  [2:0.3] 10 / 12 | Special Court may remove a likely offender from Scheduled/Tribal areas (up to 2 years); measurements and photographs
  [2:0.4] 14 | Special Court – a Court of Session specified by the State Government with concurrence of the HC Chief Justice, for each district, for speedy trial; Exclusive Special Courts (2015)
  [2:0.5] 14A | Appeal to High Court on facts and law within 90 days (incl. bail orders)
  [2:0.6] 15 | Special Public Prosecutors (7 years' practice)
  [2:0.7] 16 | State Government imposes and realises collective fine (applies PCR s.10A)
  [2:0.8] 17 | Preventive action; atrocity-prone areas
  [2:0.9] 18 | Anticipatory bail barred (CrPC 438, now BNSS 482)
  [2:0.10] 18A | No preliminary enquiry or approval before arrest (2018)
  [2:0.11] 19 | No probation
  [2:0.12] 21 | State Government's duties (legal aid, rehabilitation, committees)
  [2:0.13] 23 | Central Government makes rules (Rules 1995)
[2:1] - The Special Court **cannot impose a collective fine** and cannot authorise a non-police person for further investigation. No Central/Presidential approval is needed to set it up.
[2:2] - Arms licences of potential offenders in prone areas can be cancelled; SC/ST persons may get licences. **No power to dissolve local authorities.**
[2:3] > PCR **s.14** = companies; PoA **s.14** = Special Courts; PoA **s.16** = collective fine (State Govt).

## SUB 3 | PoA Rules 1995 – Investigation and Monitoring
[3:0] - **Rule 7(1):** investigation by a police officer **not below Deputy Superintendent of Police (DSP)** – not an Inspector.
[3:1] - **Rule 7(2):** investigation within **30 days** (original) → now investigation and **charge-sheet within 60 days** (2015 Act s.4(2)(e); Rules amended 14 Apr 2016).
[3:2] TABLE head: Rule | Body / item | Head
  [3:2.0] 16 | State Vigilance and Monitoring Committee | Chief Minister
  [3:2.1] 17 | District Vigilance and Monitoring Committee | Collector / District Magistrate
  [3:2.2] 18 | Material for Annual Report | State reports to Centre before 1 July
[3:3] - **Relief:** **₹85,000 to ₹8.25 lakh** (2016 Rules).
[3:4] - **National Helpline 14566** (toll-free, 24/7, DoSJE) for PoA and PCR grievances.
[3:5] - Centre's task force (July 2026): higher relief, counselling, State relief cells; FIRs sent to authorities within **24 hours**.

## SUB 4 | PoA Amendment Act 2015 (in force 26 January 2016)
[4:0] - Assent **31 Dec 2015** (Act 1 of 2016); **in force 26 January 2016**.
[4:1] - **Five broad areas** (Minister Thaawarchand Gehlot): offences of atrocities (Chapter II), institutional strengthening, **appeals (new s.14A)**, **rights of victims and witnesses (new Chapter IVA)**, preventive measures.
[4:2] - **New atrocities:** tonsuring, **garlanding with footwear**, carcass disposal, manual scavenging, devadasi dedication, **abusing by caste name**, witchcraft; **social and economic boycott** defined and made atrocities.
[4:3] - **Exclusive Special Courts + Exclusive Special PPs**; courts take **direct cognizance**; trial **within two months (60 days)** of charge-sheet.
[4:4] - Court presumes the accused knew the victim's caste if acquainted with the victim or family.
[4:5] ~~ Sources: web: taxheal.com Gehlot statement; vikaspedia (PRS summary); PoA Amendment Bill 2014 (prsindia.org); app notes 2:134 sub 4
## SUB 5 | Dilution and Restoration – the 2018 Episode
[5:0] TABLE head: Step | Fact
  [5:0.0] Subhash Kashinath Mahajan v State of Maharashtra (20 March 2018) | Seen as diluting the Act: preliminary enquiry by DSP, anticipatory bail if no prima facie case, prior approval before arrest of public servants
  [5:0.1] PoA Amendment Act 2018 (20 Aug 2018) | Inserted s.18A: no enquiry, no approval; bail bar restored
  [5:0.2] Review (Oct 2019) | SC recalled the Mahajan directions
  [5:0.3] Prithvi Raj Chauhan v UOI (Feb 2020) | Upheld the 2018 amendment; quashing only where no prima facie case
  [5:0.4] Kiran v Rajkumar Jivaraj Jain (2025) | Cancelled HC anticipatory bail; no "mini-trial" at bail stage
[5:1] > The 2018 insertion is **Section 18A**, not "Section 4A".
