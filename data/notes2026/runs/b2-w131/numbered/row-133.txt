## SUB 2 | Article 17, Untouchability and Its Removal
[2:0] - **Article 17:** "Untouchability" is **abolished**; its practice **in any form** forbidden; enforcing any disability from it is an **offence punishable in accordance with law**.
[2:1] - In the **Right to Equality** group; adopted with cries of "Mahatma Gandhi ki Jai"; **Ambedkar** ensured it became a punishable offence.
[2:2] - **Not defined** in the Constitution or any Act; **Mysore HC:** the practice as historically developed (social disabilities by birth in certain castes); excludes social boycott of a few individuals.
[2:3] - **Art. 35(a)(ii):** **only Parliament** prescribes punishment for Art. 17 offences.
[2:4] - **Horizontal:** binds **private persons** too (**PUDR v Union of India, 1982**).
[2:5] - Forms: temple-entry denial, separate wells, two-tumbler system, segregation, forced "polluting" work. **Seat reservation is protective, not untouchability.**
[2:6] - **Structural violence (Johan Galtung):** harm built into social structures.
[2:7] - **Removal:** by **laws, education and public awareness** – not by providing jobs (UPPCS 1992). State steps: fundamental rights, Art. 17, PCR 1955, PoA 1989 – **conversion is not a State step**.
[2:8] - **PoA Act 1989** gives effect to equality before law (14), non-discrimination (15) and Art. 17 – not freedom of religion.
[2:9] - **Sabarimala reference (Apr 2026):** Justice **B.V. Nagarathna** – Art. 17 cannot apply "for three days a month".
[2:10] - "Scheduled Castes": first used by the **Simon Commission**; GoI Act 1935; first list **1936**; first post-1950 list: **Constitution (SC) Order, 1950**.
[2:11] > Untouchability Act/PCR = **first law** enforcing Art. 17; Art. 23 = forced labour, Art. 18 = titles.
[2:12] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Part 1 p.140-141; NextGen Polity APPSC p.195-196; Indian_Polity Part 1 p.93; app notes 2:133 sub 1, 3
