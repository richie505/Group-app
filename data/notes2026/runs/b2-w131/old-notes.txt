## SUB 1 | 73rd/74th Amendments – Women in Local Bodies
[131:1:0] # Article 243D (panchayats) / 243T (municipalities)
[131:1:1] - **243D(2):** not less than **one-third of SC/ST seats** for SC/ST women (not 1/4, not 50%).
[131:1:2] - **243D(3):** not less than **one-third of total seats** in every Panchayat; rotated.
[131:1:3] - **243D(4):** not less than one-third of **Chairperson** offices (Pradhan, Pramukh, Adhyaksha).
[131:1:4] - **243D(5) sunset:** SC/ST reservation tied to **Art. 334** (to 2030); **women's reservation has no sunset**.
[131:1:5] - **243T** gives the same for municipalities; ST seats in municipalities = **243T(1)**. **BC reservation** is left to States (optional).
[131:1:6] - Tenure **5 years** from first meeting (**243E**); minimum age to contest **21** (243F, 243V). **73rd in force 24 April 1993**.
[131:1:7] # States at 50%
[131:1:8] - One-third is a **floor, not a ceiling**. **Bihar** first with **50% (2006)**; MP, Uttarakhand, HP followed.
[131:1:9] - **MoPR (April 2026): 21 States and 2 UTs** have 50% for women (about 20 when APPSC asked); **Haryana** moved to 50% in **2020**. Women are **49.75%** of 25 lakh+ elected panchayat representatives.
[131:1:10] - Earlier one-third-only States (APPSC flashcard): **UP, Haryana (till 2020), Goa, J&K**. **UP Panchayat Laws (Amendment) Act 1994:** **one-third** for women.
[131:1:11] - **Andhra Pradesh:** **50%** in panchayats and municipalities. AP 2026: 34% BC (rural) and 33.33% (urban) approved.
[131:1:12] - **Nagaland (2017):** violent protests against **33% reservation for women in urban local bodies** (claimed to violate Art. 371A).
[131:1:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity APPSC 24 p.5; web: static.pib.gov.in MoPR Highlights, National Panchayati Raj Day 24 Apr 2026; web search results (MoPR table: Haryana 2020); app notes 2:131 sub 1; general knowledge (Nagaland 2017), not checked
[131:1:14] - **Rotation:** reserved seats and chairpersons' offices rotate among constituencies (243D(3)-(4)); rotation of legislative women's seats after each delimitation is Art. 334A, not 243D.

## SUB 3 | 106th Amendment and the Women's Reservation Bill
[131:3:0] - **Constitution (106th Amendment) Act 2023 = Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam:** **one-third** of Lok Sabha, State Assembly and Delhi Assembly seats for women, incl. one-third of SC/ST seats; **no OBC sub-quota**; **not** Rajya Sabha or Councils.
[131:3:1] - Before it, Arts 330-334 had **no provision for women**, so a **constitutional amendment** was needed (party ticket quotas need none).
[131:3:2] TABLE head: Article | Content
  [131:3:2.0] 330A / 332A / 239AA(2)(ba) | Lok Sabha / State Assemblies / Delhi Assembly
  [131:3:2.1] 334A | Starts after the first Census after the Act + delimitation (2027 Census); 15 years, extendable; seats rotate after each delimitation
[131:3:3] - **Assent 28 September 2023**; brought into force **16 April 2026**; stated in force for **J&K and Puducherry** Assemblies from 17 April 2026 (app notes).
[131:3:4] # Bill history
[131:3:5] TABLE head: Year | Bill | Fate
  [131:3:5.0] 1996 | 81st Amendment Bill (Deve Gowda) | Lapsed
  [131:3:5.1] 2008 | 108th Bill (Rajya Sabha) | Referred to the Law and Justice Standing Committee (chair E.M. Sudarsana Natchiappan; report Dec 2009 signed by Jayanti Natarajan); passed RS 9 March 2010; lapsed 2014
  [131:3:5.2] 2023 | 128th Bill | Passed 21 Sept 2023 → 106th Amendment
  [131:3:5.3] 2026 | 131st Bill (Lok Sabha 550 → 850, delimitation on 2011 Census) | Defeated 17 April 2026: 298-230, short of 352 (Art. 368)
[131:3:6] - Women's groups demand removing the **Census-delimitation condition**.
[131:3:7] - **Arts 325-326** = equal franchise; **Arts 84/173** = women can contest.

[131:3:9] - **Piloted by:** the Ministry of **Law and Justice** (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 33% of Lok Sabha and Assembly seats).

## SUB 2 | Article 17, Untouchability and Its Removal
[133:2:0] - **Article 17:** "Untouchability" is **abolished**; its practice **in any form** forbidden; enforcing any disability from it is an **offence punishable in accordance with law**.
[133:2:1] - In the **Right to Equality** group; adopted with cries of "Mahatma Gandhi ki Jai"; **Ambedkar** ensured it became a punishable offence.
[133:2:2] - **Not defined** in the Constitution or any Act; **Mysore HC:** the practice as historically developed (social disabilities by birth in certain castes); excludes social boycott of a few individuals.
[133:2:3] - **Art. 35(a)(ii):** **only Parliament** prescribes punishment for Art. 17 offences.
[133:2:4] - **Horizontal:** binds **private persons** too (**PUDR v Union of India, 1982**).
[133:2:5] - Forms: temple-entry denial, separate wells, two-tumbler system, segregation, forced "polluting" work. **Seat reservation is protective, not untouchability.**
[133:2:6] - **Structural violence (Johan Galtung):** harm built into social structures.
[133:2:7] - **Removal:** by **laws, education and public awareness** – not by providing jobs (UPPCS 1992). State steps: fundamental rights, Art. 17, PCR 1955, PoA 1989 – **conversion is not a State step**.
[133:2:8] - **PoA Act 1989** gives effect to equality before law (14), non-discrimination (15) and Art. 17 – not freedom of religion.
[133:2:9] - **Sabarimala reference (Apr 2026):** Justice **B.V. Nagarathna** – Art. 17 cannot apply "for three days a month".
[133:2:10] - "Scheduled Castes": first used by the **Simon Commission**; GoI Act 1935; first list **1936**; first post-1950 list: **Constitution (SC) Order, 1950**.
[133:2:11] > Untouchability Act/PCR = **first law** enforcing Art. 17; Art. 23 = forced labour, Art. 18 = titles.
[133:2:12] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Part 1 p.140-141; NextGen Polity APPSC p.195-196; Indian_Polity Part 1 p.93; app notes 2:133 sub 1, 3

=== NOTES 2:134 | Sc/st (prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989 and Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955
## SUB 0 | Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955
[134:0:0] - **Enacted 1955** as the **Untouchability (Offences) Act** (8 May 1955; **in force 1 June 1955**) – first law enforcing **Art. 17**; renamed **Protection of Civil Rights Act** by Act 106 of **1976** (w.e.f. 19 Nov 1976); **Rules 1977**.
[134:0:1] - **Extent – s.1(2): "the whole of India"** (J&K never excluded).
[134:0:2] - **Civil right** = any right accruing from the abolition of untouchability by Art. 17. **"Untouchability" is not defined** – neither in Art. 17 nor in the Act.
[134:0:3] - Punishes the **preaching and practice of untouchability**: religious and social disabilities.
[134:0:4] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [134:0:4.0] 3 | Religious disabilities – entry/worship in public places of worship (incl. a private temple allowed for public worship)
  [134:0:4.1] 4 | Social disabilities – shops, hotels, water sources, roads, jewellery and finery
  [134:0:4.2] 5 | Refusing admission to hospital, dispensary, educational institution, hostel
  [134:0:4.3] 6 | Refusing to sell goods or render services
  [134:0:4.4] 7 | Other offences – insulting an SC member (7(1)(d)); preaching or justifying untouchability
  [134:0:4.5] 7A | Compelling scavenging, removing carcasses, flaying – deemed untouchability
  [134:0:4.6] 10 | Abetment – a public servant wilfully neglecting investigation
  [134:0:4.7] 10A | Collective fine imposed by the State Government on inhabitants of an area
  [134:0:4.8] 12 | Court presumes the untouchability ground when the victim is a Scheduled Caste member (not ST)
  [134:0:4.9] 14 | Offences by companies – person in charge, and any director, manager, secretary or officer consenting
  [134:0:4.10] 15 | Cognizable; triable summarily (except where minimum term exceeds 3 months)
  [134:0:4.11] 15A | State duties – legal aid, special courts, committees, surveys of prone areas, annual report
  [134:0:4.12] 16A | No probation above 14 years
[134:0:5] - **First offence:** **1-6 months** and fine **₹100-500**. Offences are **non-compoundable** (the Act allows no compounding).
[134:0:6] - Not an offence when accused and victim belong to the **same social group** (no "ground of untouchability").
[134:0:7] - Convict is **disqualified** from elections to Parliament/State Legislatures (**s.8, RPA 1951** – for a fixed period, then may contest).
[134:0:8] - **Implementation:** States/UTs; nodal Centre – **Social Justice and Empowerment** with MHA. (NHRC is under the Protection of Human Rights Act 1993, not PCR.)
[134:0:9] - **Sukanya Shantha (2024):** Art. 17 bars caste-based **purity stigma**.
[134:0:10] > Trap: PCR **s.12** = presumption, **s.14** = companies, **s.10A** = collective fine; PCR in force **1955**, not 30 Jan 1990.

## SUB 1 | PoA Act 1989 – Enactment, Scope and Offences
[134:1:0] - **Act 33 of 1989**, assent **11 Sept 1989**; **in force 30 January 1990**; **23 sections**; Rules **1995**; amended **2015** (in force 26 Jan 2016) and **2018** (20 Aug 2018). J&K excluded until **2019**.
[134:1:1] - **Why:** the PCR Act 1955 and the IPC proved **inadequate** to check atrocities against SCs/STs.
[134:1:2] - **Long title (preamble):** prevent atrocities, set up **Special Courts**, and provide **relief and rehabilitation of victims**.
[134:1:3] - **Who:** offences by persons **"not being a member of a SC or ST"** – incl. government employees; not crimes among SCs/STs.
[134:1:4] TABLE head: Provision | Punishment
  [134:1:4.0] s.3(1) atrocities (e.g. dumping excreta, waste, carcasses in premises; begar; land grab; witchcraft accusation) | Not less than 6 months, up to 5 years, and fine
  [134:1:4.1] s.3(2)(i) false evidence to convict an SC/ST of a capital offence | Life imprisonment and fine (death if an innocent is executed)
  [134:1:4.2] s.3(2)(vii) offence by a public servant | Not less than 1 year
  [134:1:4.3] s.4 wilful neglect of duty by public servant | 6 months to 1 year
  [134:1:4.4] s.5 subsequent conviction | Not less than 1 year
[134:1:5] - **2015 additions to s.3(1):** tonsuring, **garlanding with footwear**, carcass disposal, manual scavenging, abuse by caste name, devadasi dedication, **witchcraft (zb)**, **social/economic boycott (zc)**, preventing entry to **educational institutions**, hospitals, places of worship.
[134:1:6] - **s.6:** applies IPC **s.34, Chapters III, IV (general exceptions – private defence), V, V-A, s.149, XXIII**. So the accused **can** plead private defence or that he belongs to the victim's caste.
[134:1:7] - **s.8 presumptions:** financial help → **abetment**; group offence from a land dispute → **common intention / common object**.
