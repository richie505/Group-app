=== Stockholm 1972, UNEP and World Environment Day
# Stockholm Conference
- **UN Conference on the Human Environment (UNCHE)**, **Stockholm, Sweden, 5–16 June 1972** – the **first major world conference on the environment**.
- Outcomes: **Stockholm Declaration (26 principles)** + Action Plan; **UNEP** set up in **1972**; **5 June = World Environment Day** (first observed **1973**).
- **Indira Gandhi** was the **only visiting head of government**; she linked poverty and environment.
- **India's follow-up:** the **National Committee on Environmental Planning and Coordination (NCEPC)** was set up in **1972** (Feb 1972, under the Dept of Science and Technology); the Stockholm decisions were implemented through the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.
- **Sustainable development:** Stockholm first put environment and development together on the world agenda; the term was defined by the **Brundtland Report "Our Common Future" (1987)** and spread worldwide by **Rio 1992**.
# UNEP and UNEA
- **UNEP:** est. **1972** (Stockholm), HQ **Nairobi, Kenya**.
- **UN Environment Assembly (UNEA):** created at **Rio+20 (2012)**; meets **biennially in Nairobi**. **UNEA-4: 11–15 March 2019, Nairobi** – theme "Innovative solutions for environmental challenges and sustainable consumption and production"; India's resolutions on single-use plastics and sustainable nitrogen management adopted.
- **Global 500 awards** (protection of the environment) were launched in **1987 by UNEP**. **Champions of the Earth** (2005) = UNEP's highest honour.
- **International Nitrogen Initiative (INI):** set up in **2003** under **SCOPE and IGBP** (not UNEP); aim – **optimise nitrogen's beneficial role in sustainable food production** while minimising its harm.

=== Ramsar Convention and India's Wetlands
# Convention basics
- **Convention on Wetlands of International Importance** ("waterfowl convention"): adopted **2 February 1971** at **Ramsar, Iran** (hence **World Wetlands Day = 2 February**); in force **1975**; secretariat **Gland, Switzerland** (hosted by IUCN); not part of the UN system.
- Purpose: **conservation and wise use of wetlands** and their resources – the **only global environmental treaty dealing with a particular ecosystem**.
- **Three pillars:** **wise use** of all wetlands; designating **wetlands of international importance** (Ramsar List); **international cooperation** – never a ban on all human use.
- **Wise use** = conserve sites through an **ecosystem approach while allowing their sustainable use** (UPSC 2010) – not keeping them inaccessible or allowing only tourism.
- **Montreux Record** (1990): Ramsar sites whose ecological character is changing from human interference. Indian sites now: **Keoladeo NP + Loktak Lake**; **Chilika removed (2002)** after restoration.
- **Wetlands International** is a **global NGO** (a Ramsar International Organisation Partner), **not an intergovernmental body**; it works at ground level and uses field experience to advocate better policies (UPSC 2014 → "2 only").
- **World Wetlands Day 2017 theme:** **Wetlands for Disaster Risk Reduction**.
- **Ramsar Wetland Conservation Award 2018 (Wise Use):** **Fundación Global Nature, Spain** (presented at CoP13, Dubai).
- Ramsar **CoP15** (July 2025): India's resolution on **sustainable lifestyles for wise use of wetlands** adopted.
# India's Ramsar sites
- India a Party since **1982** (accession; first sites designated **1 October 1981**): **Chilika Lake** (Odisha) and **Keoladeo NP** (Rajasthan) – the first.
- **103 Ramsar sites** (as of 2026) after **Sukhna Wetland (Chandigarh)** and **Sauj Jheel (Mainpuri, UP)** were added in mid-October 2026 (Rocket CA: 15 Oct); India is **3rd in the world** after the UK and Mexico; **Tamil Nadu** has the most (20).
- **Udaipur and Indore** = India's first accredited **Wetland Cities** (January 2025).
- **Amrit Dharohar** (wetlands / Ramsar sites) and **MISHTI** (mangroves) – both launched on **World Environment Day, 5 June 2023**.

=== Cartagena and Nagoya Protocols
| Point | Cartagena Protocol | Nagoya Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | **Biosafety** – safe transfer, handling and use of **LMOs/GMOs** from modern biotechnology | **Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS)** of genetic resources |
| Adopted → in force | **29 Jan 2000 → 11 Sep 2003** | **2010 (COP10) → 2014** |
| Named after | **Cartagena, Colombia** | Nagoya, Japan |
| Parent | **CBD** | **CBD** |
| India | **MoEF(CC)** is the nodal ministry; **GEAC** approves GM organisms | Ratified **2012**; implemented through the **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** |
- **Both protocols are under the CBD** – not UNFCCC, Ramsar, CITES or the Paris Agreement.
# Cartagena
- **Advance Informed Agreement (AIA):** the importing country must consent **before the first transboundary movement** of an LMO meant for release; LMO shipments carry documents identifying the LMO.
- **Biosafety Clearing-House** = an **online information platform** on LMOs, **not a testing laboratory**.
- First COP/MOP-1: **February 2004, Kuala Lumpur** (not the Philippines). **India is a Party; the USA is not** a Party to the CBD or its Biosafety Protocol.
- **Nagoya–Kuala Lumpur Supplementary Protocol** on Liability and Redress (2010) – supplementary to **Cartagena**.
# Nagoya
- A **legally binding** supplementary agreement to the CBD – **not voluntary** (APPSC JL 2024 statement 2 false).
- **Utilisation of genetic resources** = **research and development on the genetic and/or biochemical composition** of genetic resources (so "biochemical R&D", not cloning humans, space research or fossil trade).
- Covers plant, animal and microbial genetic resources + associated **traditional knowledge**; **excludes human genetic material**.
- Pillars: **access** with **prior informed consent**, **benefit-sharing** on **Mutually Agreed Terms** (monetary/non-monetary), **compliance**.
- In India the **Biodiversity Management Committees** are key to Nagoya's objectives. India's **1st National Report**: **3,556 IRCCs**, over **60%** of the global total. **Red sanders** is India's single largest ABS earner.
> Cartagena = biosafety/LMO; Nagoya = ABS. Nagoya: adopted 2010, India ratified 2012, in force 2014.

=== CITES – Basics, Appendices and India
# Basics
- **Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora** = the **Washington Convention**: drafted after a **1963 IUCN** resolution, signed **3 March 1973, Washington D.C.** (hence **World Wildlife Day = 3 March**); in force **1 July 1975** (not 1985); members are called **Parties**.
- Aim: ensure that **international trade** in wild animals and plants **does not threaten their survival** – a watchdog on trade in endangered species (not conservation of migratory species = CMS, nor heritage = WHC).
- **Legally binding** on Parties, but it **does not take the place of national laws**. Secretariat **Geneva**, administered by **UNEP**.
- Coverage: about **40,900 species – ~6,610 animals and ~34,310 plants** (2026; older books: ~5,000 animals and ~29,000 plants).
- CoP meets **every 2-3 years**; species move between Appendices **only by a vote of the CoP**.
# Appendices
| Appendix | Listing criterion | Trade | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Threatened with extinction | **Commercial trade prohibited**; exceptional non-commercial trade with import + export permits | Tiger, rhino, clouded leopard, **Indian pangolin**, **Indian star tortoise** (CoP18, 2019) |
| II | **Not necessarily threatened**, but may become so without trade control | Allowed with **export permits** | Corals, most sharks and rays, **red sanders**, **rosewood (Dalbergia)**, striped hyena, **golden gecko** |
| III | Protected in one Party that asks others' help | Permits / certificates of origin | **Walrus (Canada)** |
- **Dalbergia** = the **rosewood** genus (legume family; D. latifolia, D. sissoo) – not cashew, coffee or tea.
# CITES CoPs
| CoP | Venue / year | Highlight |
|---|---|---|
| CoP3 | **New Delhi, 1981** | Only CITES CoP hosted by India |
| CoP19 | **Panama, 2022** | 54 sharks and rays to App II |
| CoP20 | **Samarkand, Uzbekistan, 2025** | **First CITES CoP in Central Asia**; 50 years of CITES; whale shark, oceanic whitetip and manta rays → **App I**; India defeated an EU proposal on **Guggul** (Commiphora wightii) |
- India's proposals at CoPs include rosewood, otters, tortoises, **Tokay gecko**, wedgefish.
# India and CITES
- India a Party since **1976**; implementing law **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** – the 2022 amendment added a CITES chapter and **Schedule IV for CITES-listed specimens**.
- **Wildlife Crime Control Bureau** (statutory, **2007**, under **MoEFCC**) helps Customs enforce CITES. **ZSI** hosts a CITES Cell. **CITES Trade Database** = **UNEP-WCMC**; **MIKE** monitors elephant poaching.
- **Red sanders** (Pterocarpus santalinus): endemic to **Seshachalam** / southern Eastern Ghats (AP); IUCN **Endangered**; CITES **App II**; India removed from the CITES **Review of Significant Trade** in **November 2023**.
> CITES 1973, in force 1975 (not 1963/1985); Washington = CITES, Bonn = CMS. CITES is binding – a statement "not legally binding" is the incorrect one.

=== Rio Earth Summit 1992 and Follow-ups
- **UN Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED)** = "**Earth Summit**", **Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, June 1992** – the **first international Earth Summit**.
- Outputs: **Agenda 21** (action plan for sustainable development), **Rio Declaration** (27 principles), **Forest Principles** (non-binding), and two binding treaties opened for signature – **UNFCCC** and **CBD**. **UNCCD** followed in **1994**.
- **UNFCCC** was drawn up at Rio 1992 (not Stockholm 1972, Johannesburg 2002 or Copenhagen 2009); in force **21 March 1994**.
- **UN Commission on Sustainable Development (CSD):** set up by the UNGA in **December 1992** to follow up Rio/Agenda 21 (replaced by the HLPF in 2013).
| Summit | Year / venue | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Rio+5 (Earth Summit +5)** | **1997**, UNGA special session, New York | Reviewed Agenda 21 |
| Rio+10 (WSSD) | **2002, Johannesburg** | Johannesburg Plan of Implementation |
| Rio+20 (UNCSD) | **2012**, Rio | **"The Future We Want"**; launched the SDG process; created **UNEA** |
# Global Environment Facility (GEF)
- Pilot GEF agreed **1990**, set up on the eve of Rio 1992; first partners **UNDP, UNEP, World Bank**.
- **Financial mechanism** for the **CBD, UNFCCC**, UNCCD, Stockholm (POPs) and Minamata conventions (UPSC 2014 → option a); it is not an OECD agency and does not itself do research.

=== CBD – Objectives, COPs, Aichi Targets and the Kunming-Montreal GBF
# Convention on Biological Diversity (1992)
- **Opened for signature at Rio 1992** (Earth Summit), not Stockholm 1972; text adopted **22 May 1992** (hence **International Day for Biological Diversity = 22 May**); **legally binding**; in force **29 December 1993**; secretariat **Montreal, Canada**; India ratified **1994**.
- **Three objectives:** **conservation** of biodiversity, **sustainable use** of its components, **fair and equitable sharing of benefits** from genetic resources (not compulsory technology transfer).
- One of the **three Rio Conventions** (UNFCCC, CBD, UNCCD); financial mechanism = **GEF**. In-situ = conservation in the natural habitat; **ex-situ = outside the natural habitat** (zoos, gene banks).
- India implements it through the **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** (amended **2023**; criticised for diluting ABS and the role of local communities): three tiers – **National Biodiversity Authority (Chennai)**, **State Biodiversity Boards**, **Biodiversity Management Committees** (prepare **People's Biodiversity Registers**; may **levy collection fees**).
- **AP State Biodiversity Board:** aims at **zero loss of biodiversity and ecosystem services by 2050**; portal **BIOMIS**.

# Aichi Biodiversity Targets
- **20 targets** under **5 strategic goals**, Strategic Plan **2011-2020**, adopted at **COP10, Nagoya (Aichi Prefecture), 2010** (not COP12 2014 as one NextGen line says). Deadline 2020; **none fully met** (Global Biodiversity Outlook 5).
# Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (Dec 2022)
- Adopted at **COP15, Montreal, December 2022** – not Kunming 2021, not COP16 Cali. **4 goals for 2050, 23 targets for 2030**.
| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Goal A | Halt human-induced extinction; cut extinction rate **tenfold** |
| Goal B / C / D | Sustainable use / **fair sharing of benefits from genetic resources** / finance and technology (close the US$700 bn gap) |
| Target 1 | Loss of high-biodiversity areas close to zero |
| Target 2 | **Restore 30%** of degraded land and water ecosystems |
| **Target 3 (30x30)** | **Conserve 30% of land and 30% of oceans by 2030** |
| Target 6 | Cut invasive alien species introduction by **at least 50%** |
| Target 7 | **Halve excess nutrients** and pesticide risk |
| Target 16 | **Halve food waste** |
| Target 18 | Cut harmful subsidies by **US$500 bn a year** |
| Target 19 | Mobilise **US$200 bn a year** by 2030 from all sources |
| Target 22 | Full participation and rights of **indigenous peoples and local communities** |
- **30x30 is a biodiversity (area-protection) target, NOT a renewable-energy target** (APPSC G1 2024). It is championed by the **High Ambition Coalition (HAC) for Nature and People**, co-chaired by **Costa Rica, France and the UK**; **India joined in Oct 2021 – the first BRICS country** to do so.
- Money: **GBF Fund under the GEF**; **Cali Fund** – large DSI users pay **1% of profits or 0.1% of revenue**. National reports under **Article 26**.
- **India's updated NBSAP 2024-2030:** **23 national targets**, released at **COP16 Cali**. India's **7th National Report** = first full review against the 23 KMGBF targets.
> US$200 bn = mobilise (T19); US$500 bn = subsidy cut (T18); US$700 bn = finance gap (Goal D). 30% conserve = T3; 30% restore = T2.
