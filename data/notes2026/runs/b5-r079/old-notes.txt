## SUB 1 | Stockholm Conference, 1972
[79:1:0] # Basics and Outcomes
[79:1:1] - Official name: **UN Conference on the Human Environment**, Stockholm, Sweden, **5–16 June 1972**.
[79:1:2] - Outcomes: **Stockholm Declaration (26 principles)** + Action Plan; **UNEP** set up in 1972 (HQ **Nairobi, Kenya**); **5 June** became **World Environment Day** (first observed **1973**).
[79:1:3] # India at Stockholm
[79:1:4] - PM **Indira Gandhi** attended – the **only visiting head of government**; she linked **poverty and environment**.
[79:1:5] - India: a "cautious participant" at Stockholm → leader by Paris 2015 and KMGBF 2022.
[79:1:6] - Stockholm decisions implemented in India through the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.
[79:1:7] - World Environment Day: **China hosted 2019**; WED 2022 theme **"Only One Earth"**.
## SUB 2 | Rio Earth Summit, 1992 and Follow-ups
[79:2:0] # Rio 1992
[79:2:1] - Official name: **UN Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED)**, "Earth Summit" – the **first international Earth Summit**.
[79:2:2] TABLE head: Rio output | Key point
  [79:2:2.0] Agenda 21 | Action plan for sustainable development at all levels
  [79:2:2.1] Rio Declaration | Principles on environment and development
  [79:2:2.2] Forest Principles | Non-binding statement on forests
  [79:2:2.3] UNFCCC and CBD | Opened for signature at Rio (CBD at Rio, not Stockholm)
  [79:2:2.4] UNCCD | Adopted 1994 as follow-up
[79:2:3] - **UNFCCC**: in force **21 March 1994**; aim – stabilise greenhouse-gas concentrations. UNFCCC is linked to UNCED.
[79:2:4] - **UNCCD**: desertification and land degradation; bottom-up, local-community approach; linked to **SDG 15** and Land Degradation Neutrality. **COP-17: Mongolia**, theme "Restoring Land. Restoring Hope"; 197 parties; land degradation affects up to 40% of land. India hosted **COP-14 (2019)**.
[79:2:5] # Global Environment Facility (GEF)
[79:2:6] - Pilot GEF agreed **1990**, set up on the eve of Rio 1992; first implementing partners **UNDP, UNEP, World Bank**.
[79:2:7] - Financial mechanism for **CBD, UNFCCC, UNCCD, Stockholm Convention, Minamata Convention**; grants for biodiversity, REDD+, climate change, land degradation, ozone layer.

## SUB 3 | Ramsar Convention, 1971
[79:3:0] # Convention Basics
[79:3:1] - **Convention on Wetlands**; signed **2 February 1971**, **Ramsar, Iran** (now World Wetlands Day); secretariat **Gland, Switzerland**; India a Party since **1982**.
[79:3:2] - **World Wetlands Day 2017 theme:** Wetlands for **Disaster Risk Reduction**.
[79:3:3] - Obligations ("three pillars"): designate **wetlands of international importance**; **wise use** of all wetlands; **international cooperation**.
[79:3:4] - **Wise use** = conserve through an ecosystem approach while allowing sustainable use.
[79:3:5] - **Montreux Record**: sites where changes in ecological character have occurred, are occurring or are likely, from **human interference** (technology, pollution). Indian sites: **Keoladeo NP + Loktak Lake**; **Chilika removed** after restoration.
[79:3:6] # India's Ramsar Sites
[79:3:7] - First Indian sites (**1 October 1981**): **Chilika Lake** and **Keoladeo NP**.
[79:3:8] - **16 October 2026**: tally **103** with **Sukhna Lake** (Chandigarh) and **Sauj Jheel** (Mainpuri, UP).
[79:3:9] - **Udaipur and Indore** = India's first accredited **Wetland Cities** (January 2025).
[79:3:10] - Ramsar **CoP-15** (30 Jul 2025): India's resolution on sustainable lifestyles for wise use of wetlands adopted. **Amrit Dharohar** strategy (Jun 2023).

## SUB 4 | Cites, 1973
[79:4:0] # Basics
[79:4:1] - Full form: **Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora**; other name **Washington Convention**.
[79:4:2] - Adopted **3 March 1973**, Washington D.C.; in force **1 July 1975**; secretariat **Geneva** (administered by **UNEP**); **184 Parties**.
[79:4:3] - Covers about **35,000 species**: bans commercial trade for ~3%, regulates ~97%.
[79:4:4] - **World Wildlife Day = 3 March** (CITES signing day; proclaimed by UNGA in 2013).
[79:4:5] - Legally **binding** on Parties but does not replace national laws.
[79:4:6] # Appendices
[79:4:7] TABLE head: Appendix | Listing criterion | Trade | Examples
  [79:4:7.0] I | Threatened with extinction | Commercial trade prohibited; only exceptional non-commercial (research) with import + export permits | Tiger, snow leopard, rhino, clouded leopard, Indian and Chinese pangolins
  [79:4:7.1] II | Not necessarily threatened, but could be without control | Allowed with export permit | Corals, most sharks and rays, red sanders
  [79:4:7.2] III | Protected in at least one Party that asks others' help | Permits / certificates of origin | Walrus (Canada)
[79:4:8] - Species move between Appendices **only by COP vote**.
[79:4:9] # CITES COPs
[79:4:10] - COP meets **every 2–3 years**.
[79:4:11] TABLE head: COP | Venue / year | Highlight
  [79:4:11.0] CoP3 | New Delhi, 1981 | Only CITES COP hosted by India
  [79:4:11.1] CoP18 | 2019 | Indian star tortoise uplisted to App I on India's proposal
  [79:4:11.2] CoP19 | Panama, 2022 | 54 shark and ray species to App II; rosewood, sea cucumbers
  [79:4:11.3] CoP20 | Samarkand, Uzbekistan, 2025 | First in Central Asia; 50 years of CITES; 77 species added; whale shark, oceanic whitetip, manta rays → App I
[79:4:12] - CoP20: India **defeated an EU proposal** on **Guggul** (Commiphora wightii).
[79:4:13] - India's proposals: rosewood, otters, tortoises, Tokay gecko, wedgefish.
## SUB 5 | Cites in India and Indian Species
[79:5:0] # Accession, Law and Bodies

[79:5:2] - **WPA Amendment 2022**: CITES chapter + new **Schedule IV** for CITES-listed specimens; Management Authority (permits) + Scientific Authority (advice).
[79:5:3] Schedule IV is for CITES-listed specimens, not invasive alien species (IAS are under a separate section).
[79:5:4] - **ZSI** hosts a CITES Cell.
[79:5:5] - **WCCB**: statutory body under MoEFCC, set up **2007** (WPA 2006 amendment); assists **Customs** in inspecting consignments under CITES and EXIM policy; keeps the wildlife-crime database.
[79:5:6] - **CITES Trade Database**: UNEP-WCMC. **MIKE**: monitors elephant poaching.

## SUB 7 | Convention on Biological Diversity (cbd)
[79:7:0] # Basics
[79:7:1] - Adopted (text **22 May 1992**), opened at **Rio 1992**; **legally binding**; in force **29 December 1993**; secretariat **Montreal, Canada**; India ratified **1994**.
[79:7:2] - Three objectives: **conservation**, **sustainable use**, **fair and equitable benefit-sharing** (not compulsory technology transfer).
[79:7:3] - Financial mechanism: **GEF**. In-situ = in natural habitat; ex-situ = outside it.

## SUB 8 | Cartagena and Nagoya Protocols
[79:8:0] TABLE head: Point | Cartagena Protocol | Nagoya Protocol
  [79:8:0.0] Subject | Biosafety – LMOs/GMOs from modern biotechnology | Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) of genetic resources
  [79:8:0.1] Adopted → in force | 29 Jan 2000 → 11 Sep 2003 | 2010 (COP10) → 2014
  [79:8:0.2] Named after | Cartagena, Colombia | Nagoya, Japan
  [79:8:0.3] Parties | 173 | 137
  [79:8:0.4] India | MoEF(&CC) nodal; GEAC approves GM | Ratified 2012; 3,556 certificates
[79:8:1] # Cartagena
[79:8:2] - **Advance informed agreement** procedure before first transboundary movement of LMOs.
[79:8:3] - **Biosafety Clearing-House**: online platform for LMO information – **not a testing lab**.
[79:8:4] - First COP/MOP-1: **February 2004, Kuala Lumpur**.
[79:8:5] - **Nagoya–Kuala Lumpur Supplementary Protocol** on Liability and Redress (2010) – supplementary to **Cartagena**, hence CBD.
[79:8:6] # Nagoya
[79:8:7] - **Legally binding supplementary agreement** to CBD (not voluntary).
[79:8:8] - "**Utilisation of genetic resources**" = **research and development** on genetic and/or **biochemical** composition.
[79:8:9] - Covers plant, animal, microbial genetic resources + traditional knowledge; **excludes human genetic material**.
[79:8:10] - Pillars: **prior informed consent**, **Mutually Agreed Terms**, compliance.
[79:8:11] - India's **1st National Report**: **3,556 IRCCs**, over **60%** of global total.
[79:8:12] India ratified Nagoya in 2012; 2014 is the entry-into-force year. Nagoya is part of **CBD** – not UNFCCC, Ramsar or Paris. Carbon trading belongs to **Kyoto**, not Montreal Protocol.
[79:8:13] - **Parent convention:** Both the **Cartagena Protocol** (biosafety, 2000) and the **Nagoya Protocol** (ABS, 2010) are protocols under the **Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)**.
