=== NOTES 5:106 | Environmental Hazards and Disaster Management – Dm Act 2005, Ndma and Ndrf, Sendai Framework, Disaster Types and Mitigation
## SUB 0 | Disaster Terms and the Dm Cycle
[106:0:0] # Key Terms
[106:0:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [106:0:1.0] Hazard | Potentially damaging event
  [106:0:1.1] Disaster | When a hazard meets vulnerability and causes serious harm
  [106:0:1.2] Vulnerability | How easily harmed – physical, social, economic, environmental
  [106:0:1.3] Risk | Probability of harm; Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability / Capacity
  [106:0:1.4] Resilience | Ability to resist, absorb and recover quickly
  [106:0:1.5] Capacity | Strengths and resources that reduce risk
[106:0:2] - Risk assessment dimensions: **hazard, vulnerability, exposure, capacity**.
[106:0:3] - **Disaster Crunch Model**: vulnerability comes from **socio-economic processes**.
[106:0:4] - **DRR works best when it addresses the underlying causes of vulnerability** (APPSC 2024).
[106:0:5] # Crisis / DM Cycle – Three Phases
[106:0:6] TABLE head: Phase | Focus | Example
  [106:0:6.0] Pre-crisis | Prevention, mitigation, preparedness | Embankments, mangroves, land-use zoning
  [106:0:6.1] During crisis | Response – rescue, relief | Search and rescue
  [106:0:6.2] Post-crisis | Recovery → rehabilitation → reconstruction | Rebuilding
[106:0:7] Embankments, mangroves and zoning = mitigation (pre-disaster), not response.
[106:0:8] # Vulnerable Groups
[106:0:9] TABLE head: Basis | Groups
  [106:0:9.0] Age | Children (esp. unaccompanied), elderly
  [106:0:9.1] Gender | Women – pregnant, lactating, single, widows; transgender persons
  [106:0:9.2] Disability | Persons with Disabilities (barriers in warning, evacuation, relief)
  [106:0:9.3] Socio-economic | Poor, SC/ST, informal workers, homeless
  [106:0:9.4] Location | Coastal areas, Seismic Zone V, flood plains, hills
## SUB 1 | Classification of Disasters
[106:1:0] # Broad Classes
[106:1:1] TABLE head: Class | Hazards
  [106:1:1.0] Geological / geophysical | Earthquake, tsunami, landslide, volcanic eruption
  [106:1:1.1] Hydrological | Flood, flash flood, avalanche
  [106:1:1.2] Meteorological | Cyclone, tornado, thunderstorm, hailstorm
  [106:1:1.3] Climatological | Drought, heat wave, cold wave, forest fire (NOT biological)
  [106:1:1.4] Biological | Epidemics, pandemics (COVID-19), pest attacks
  [106:1:1.5] Man-made | Industrial, chemical, nuclear, fire – human activity, negligence or technological failure (APPSC 2025)
[106:1:2] - **Fire from careless gas-cylinder handling = man-made** (APPSC 2025). A **nuclear explosion is not natural** (CBRN category).
[106:1:3] - Man-made subtypes: **technological** (industrial accidents, chemical leaks, dam failure), **transport**, **social or complex** (terrorism, riots, war).
[106:1:4] - **Emerging hazards**: **cyber attacks, space weather, AI failures**.
[106:1:5] - **Socio-natural hazards**: natural + human factors – floods worsened by deforestation; landslides from unscientific construction.
[106:1:6] - **Flooding causes**: melting snow/ice, lack of vegetation (more runoff), dam failures – a **biological disaster does not cause flooding**.
[106:1:7] - Nuclear disasters: reactor accidents; radiological: orphan sources, transport accidents, dirty bombs.
[106:1:8] # Notified Disasters
[106:1:9] - Eligible for **SDRF/NDRF** aid: **14 since August 2026** (earlier 12) – **heatwave and lightning** added on **16th Finance Commission** advice.
[106:1:10] - **No man-made disaster** is notified.
[106:1:11] - **Ministry of Agriculture** handles **drought, hailstorm, frost, pest attack**.
[106:1:12] **Biological disasters** (epidemics, pest attacks) are a **natural hazard class** – an APPSC answer key gave them as man-made.
[106:1:13] - **Notified disasters list (12, before Aug 2026):** cyclone, drought, earthquake, fire, flood, tsunami, hailstorm, landslide, avalanche, cloudburst, pest attack, and **frost and cold wave**; heatwave and lightning were added in Aug 2026 (14).

## SUB 3 | Dm Act 2005, Policy and Plans
[106:3:0] - **DM Act 2005**: enacted **23 December 2005**; **11 chapters, 79 sections**. Chapter II (Sec 3) = **NDMA**; Sec 42 = **NIDM**; Sec 44 = **NDRF**.
[106:3:1] - **National Policy on DM (NPDM)**: approved by Union Cabinet on **22 October 2009**.
[106:3:2] - DM Act: shift from **relief-oriented** action to **risk reduction**; gives **PRIs and ULBs** a legal role in local plans, relief and capacity building.
[106:3:3] - Act sets up **NDMA**, **NEC**, **SDMA**, **DDMA**, **NIDM**, **NDRF** (force) and funds; administered by **MHA** (**nodal ministry**).
[106:3:4] - **NDMA** notified **2006**; **NDRF** raised **2006** (Raising Day **19 January**) with **8 battalions**, now **16 battalions** (drawn from CAPFs).
[106:3:5] - **First National Disaster Management Plan: 2016**; revised **2019**. "Resilient India 2047": NDMA push for Ministry DM plans (Jun 2026).
[106:3:6] # Funds
[106:3:7] TABLE head: Fund | Use
  [106:3:7.0] SDRF | Primary State fund for immediate relief
  [106:3:7.1] NDRF (fund) | Severe calamities – response, relief, rehabilitation
  [106:3:7.2] NDMF / SDMF | Mitigation funds
[106:3:8] - **High Level Committee** (Union Home Minister): financial approvals from national funds.
[106:3:9] - **Finance Commissions:** **11th FC** recommended the national calamity reserve fund (**National Calamity Contingency Fund**); the **13th FC** led to NDRF/SDRF.
[106:3:10] - AP: SDRF/NDRF released Rs 479.01 crore in 2026-27 (Rs 277.63 crore in 2025-26).

## SUB 5 | Global Frameworks
[106:5:0] TABLE head: Framework | Year / period | Note
  [106:5:0.0] IDNDR (International Decade for Natural Disaster Reduction) | 1990–1999 | Proclaimed by UNGA Res. 44/236 (22 Dec 1989), began 1 Jan 1990; first concerted UN effort; prompted India's DM Cell
  [106:5:0.1] Yokohama Strategy | 1994 | 1st WCDRR; first global guidelines; blueprint from IDNDR
  [106:5:0.2] UNISDR | 1999 | Secretariat for ISDR
  [106:5:0.3] Hyogo Framework | 2005–2015 | 2nd WCDRR (Kobe); five priorities
  [106:5:0.4] Sendai Framework | 2015–2030 | Adopted 18 March 2015, 3rd WCDRR, Sendai
  [106:5:0.5] PM's 10-Point Agenda | 2016 | India's action plan aligned to Sendai (not a global blueprint)
  [106:5:0.6] CDRI | 2019 | India-led infrastructure-resilience coalition; secretariat New Delhi
[106:5:1] - **All three WCDRRs hosted by Japan**; UNISDR coordinated the 2nd and 3rd.
[106:5:2] - At Yokohama, UN member states and other states met at the **World Conference on Natural Disaster Reduction**.
[106:5:3] - **UNISDR → UNDRR: 1 May 2019**, **Geneva**; led by the SRSG for DRR; **custodian of Sendai** (implementation, monitoring, review); convenes the **Global Platform for DRR**.
[106:5:4] - **Post-2015 frameworks** shaping India's DRR: **Sendai, SDGs (2015–2030), Paris Agreement** – NOT Hyogo.
[106:5:5] - World Bank: India's disaster loss 1996–2000 ≈ **2.25% of GDP** a year.
[106:5:6] - **World Disasters Report 2010 (IFRC):** about **85%** of people affected by disasters in 2000-09 lived in **Asia-Pacific**.
[106:5:7] # Sendai – Four Priorities
[106:5:8] - 1. **Understanding disaster risk**
[106:5:9] - 2. **Strengthening disaster risk governance** (laws, institutions, policies, coordination)
[106:5:10] - 3. **Investing in DRR for resilience**
[106:5:11] - 4. **Enhancing preparedness** for effective response and **"Build Back Better"** (recovery used to reduce future risk)
[106:5:12] - Voluntary, **non-binding**; successor to Hyogo; States have the primary role; all-of-society, multi-hazard, people-centred.
[106:5:13] # Sendai – Seven Global Targets (by 2030)
[106:5:14] TABLE head: Target | Aim
  [106:5:14.0] A | Reduce disaster mortality
  [106:5:14.1] B | Reduce number of affected people
  [106:5:14.2] C | Reduce direct economic loss (vs GDP)
  [106:5:14.3] D | Reduce damage to critical infrastructure and disruption of basic services
  [106:5:14.4] E | Increase national and local DRR strategies – by 2020
  [106:5:14.5] F | Enhance international cooperation to developing countries
  [106:5:14.6] G | Access to multi-hazard early warning and risk information
[106:5:15] Sendai = 4 priorities, 7 targets; Hyogo = 5 priorities; Target E deadline = 2020.
## SUB 6 | Disaster Types – Criteria and Mitigation
[106:6:0] # Structural vs Non-structural
[106:6:1] TABLE head: Structural (engineering) | Non-structural (policy, awareness)
  [106:6:1.0] Embankments, dams, check dams | Building codes – IS 1893 (earthquake design), National Building Code
  [106:6:1.1] Cyclone shelters, sea walls | Flood Plain Zoning, land-use planning
  [106:6:1.2] Earthquake-resistant buildings, retrofitting | Insurance, awareness, early warning, training
[106:6:2] - Earthquake-resistant design: **door and window openings small and centrally located**.
[106:6:3] # Hazard → Mitigation
[106:6:4] TABLE head: Hazard | Criteria / mitigation
  [106:6:4.0] Cyclone, storm surge | IMD warning, shelters, evacuation, shelter belts, mangroves (Coringa, Krishna)
  [106:6:4.1] Earthquake | Seismic zoning (not for cyclones), safe construction
  [106:6:4.2] Tsunami | INCOI warnings; Tsunami Ready villages – 24 Odisha villages (UNESCO-IOC; target 100); especially hard to mitigate
  [106:6:4.3] Landslide | Hazard zonation mapping, early warning
  [106:6:4.4] Drought | Ministry of Agriculture
[106:6:5] - **UN-HABITAT** mitigation: planting **mangroves** (roots reduce tsunami impact and erosion); India's share of world mangrove cover ≈ **0.03**.
[106:6:6] - **ISRO**: **DMS programme** and **NDEM** (GIS database) support all hazards.
[106:6:7] - **Volcanoes**: fewest annual fatalities among natural hazards in the USA.
[106:6:8] # Heat Waves
[106:6:9] - Abnormally high temperatures above normal maximum; season **March–June** (sometimes July).
[106:6:10] - **IMD** forecasts (colour-coded alerts); **NDMA** guidelines – "Guidelines for Preparation of Action Plan – Prevention and Management of Heat Wave" (updated **2019**); NDRF responds.
[106:6:11] - **Heat Action Plans**: by States and cities with NDMA and IMD support. **Cool roofs**: reflective coatings or white paint.
[106:6:12] - Measures: **adjusted working hours = administrative**; **shade structures = structural**; **ORS = public-health/awareness**; cooling centres.
[106:6:13] # Cold Waves
[106:6:14] - Sharp fall in **minimum** temperature below normal in winter; season **November–March**, peak **December–February**.
[106:6:15] - **NDMA Guidelines on Cold Wave and Frost: 2021** (IMD forecasts; yellow, orange, red alerts) – preparedness, vulnerability mapping, awareness.
[106:6:16] - **Cold day**: maximum temperature departure **–4.5°C to –6.4°C**; **severe cold day**: below –6.4°C.
[106:6:17] - Impacts: hypothermia, frostbite; most vulnerable: homeless, elderly, children, outdoor workers. Measures: warm clothing, night shelters, community kitchens.
[106:6:18] - **Cyclone-prone coast (NDMA):** of India's **7,516 km** coastline, about **5,700 km** is prone to cyclones; **13 coastal States and UTs** (84 coastal districts) are affected by tropical cyclones.

## SUB 2 | Legal Basis of Epidemic Powers
[107:2:0] # Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897
[107:2:1] - Legal framework for biological disasters; used in **COVID-19**. States notify diseases (e.g. **20 states** made human **rabies** notifiable, Jan 2026).
[107:2:2] - Notifiable cases: **urgent within 24 hours**; routine within **3 days**.
[107:2:3] - **2020 amendment** (ordinance **April**, Act **September 2020**): violence against health workers **cognizable and non-bailable**; **3 months to 5 years** jail + **Rs 50,000 to Rs 2 lakh** fine; grievous hurt **6 months to 7 years**.
[107:2:4] - Criticism: **colonial**; **no definition of "dangerous epidemic disease"**; no rights safeguards.
