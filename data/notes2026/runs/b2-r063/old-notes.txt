=== NOTES 2:63 | Elections – Delimitation, Model Code of Conduct, Evm-vvpat, Nota, Election Petitions
## SUB 0 | Electoral Provisions in the Constitution
[63:0:0] # Part XV – Articles 324-329
[63:0:1] TABLE head: Article | Subject
  [63:0:1.0] 324 | Election Commission of India – superintendence, direction and control of elections; 324(2): CEC plus other Commissioners as the President decides
  [63:0:1.1] 325 | One general electoral roll per territorial constituency; no exclusion on religion, race, caste or sex
  [63:0:1.2] 326 | Adult suffrage for Lok Sabha and State Assemblies
  [63:0:1.3] 327 / 328 | Parliament / State Legislature may make election laws
  [63:0:1.4] 329 | Bar on courts: delimitation laws cannot be questioned; an election can be challenged only by an election petition
[63:0:2] - Local polls: **State Election Commissions** (Arts. **243K**, **243ZA**) run panchayat and municipal polls, not the ECI.
[63:0:3] - **Assembly elections** are conducted by the **ECI** (Art. 324), not the State Election Commission.
[63:0:4] - **Article 171** = composition of Legislative Councils (supervising elections is Article 324).
[63:0:5] - Qualifications/disqualifications: **Art. 84** and **102** (Parliament); **173** and **191** (State Legislatures).
[63:0:6] - **Simultaneous elections** may need amending Articles **83, 85, 172, 174 and 356**.
[63:0:7] - ECI constituted in **1950** – permanent, independent constitutional body; **IIIDEM** is its institute that trains election managers.
[63:0:8] - **Mohinder Singh Gill v CEC (1978)**: Art. 324 is a "**reservoir of power**" where Parliament has not legislated.
[63:0:9] - **State of Karnataka v Prathik Parasrampuria (2026)**: removing black money from elections is a core ECI duty.
[63:0:10] - **Articles for simultaneous polls:** **Art. 83** = duration of the Houses of Parliament; **Art. 85** = sessions and dissolution of Parliament; **Art. 172** = duration of State Legislatures; **Art. 174** = their sessions and dissolution; **Art. 356** = President's Rule.
[63:0:11] - **Qualification vs disqualification:** **Art. 84** = qualifications for membership of Parliament, **Art. 102** = disqualifications for Parliament; **Art. 173** and **Art. 191** = the same for State Legislatures.
## SUB 1 | Right to Vote and Electoral Systems
[63:1:0] # Universal Adult Franchise
[63:1:1] - **Article 326**: every citizen aged **18+** may vote unless disqualified for **non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime, corrupt or illegal practice**.
[63:1:2] - Voting age **21 → 18**: **61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988**.
[63:1:3] - **70th Amendment (1992)**: elected MLAs of **Delhi and Puducherry** added to the Presidential electoral college.
[63:1:4] - **Kuldip Nayar v Union of India (2006)**: right to vote and right to contest are **statutory rights**, not Fundamental Rights (RPA 1950 Sec. 19; RPA 1951).
[63:1:5] - Lok Sabha candidate: any citizen whose name is on the electoral roll of **a** constituency, at least **25 years** old.
[63:1:6] - **National Voters' Day: 25 January**.
[63:1:7] # Who is Elected How
[63:1:8] TABLE head: Office / House | Article | Electorate | Method
  [63:1:8.0] President | 54 | Elected MPs + elected MLAs (incl. Delhi, Puducherry) | Indirect; PR-STV; secret ballot
  [63:1:8.1] Vice-President | 66 | All MPs – elected and nominated | Indirect; PR-STV
  [63:1:8.2] Lok Sabha | 81 | Citizens directly | FPTP
  [63:1:8.3] Rajya Sabha | 80 | Elected MLAs | PR-STV
  [63:1:8.4] Legislative Assembly | 170 | Citizens directly | FPTP
  [63:1:8.5] Legislative Council | 171 | Indirect categories + Governor's nominees | PR-STV
  [63:1:8.6] Panchayats/Municipalities | – | Local voters | Minimum age 21
[63:1:9] - State Legislatures have **no role** in electing the Vice-President.
[63:1:10] # First-Past-the-Post vs Proportional Representation
[63:1:11] - **FPTP** = plurality: the candidate with the **most valid votes wins**; no 50% majority needed. Governments rest on a majority of **seats**, not votes.
[63:1:12] - Example: **Karnataka 1994** – Janata Dal won **116 seats with 36% votes**.
[63:1:13] - Reasons PR was rejected for Lok Sabha: difficulty for voters, fragmentation, **unstable governments**, expense, **no by-elections**, weaker voter-MP link.
[63:1:14] - "Guarantees direct election in every single-member constituency" is **NOT** such a reason.
[63:1:15] - PR is not needed where a two-party system has grown (e.g. UK); PR helps minorities in multi-party systems.
[63:1:16] Security deposit is forfeited when a candidate gets **one-sixth or less** of valid votes.
[63:1:17] - **Constituencies:** Lok Sabha and State Assembly members are elected directly from **single-member territorial constituencies** by first-past-the-post.

## SUB 4 | Delimitation
[63:4:0] # Meaning and Basis
[63:4:1] - **Delimitation** = fixing the **number of seats and boundaries** of Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies (including SC/ST reserved seats) on recent census figures, for equal representation.
[63:4:2] - Principle: "**one person, one vote, one value**" (APPSC-G1 2024); **Art. 81(2)** uniformity of representation.
[63:4:3] - Basis: **Art. 82** (Lok Sabha), **Art. 170** (Assemblies) – readjustment after each census.
[63:4:4] # Delimitation Commission
[63:4:5] - Constituted by the **Central Government** under an **Act of Parliament** (Delimitation Act); independent body.
[63:4:6] - Members (2002 Act): a retired/sitting **Supreme Court judge** (Chairperson), the **CEC** or an EC nominated by the CEC, and the **State Election Commissioner** of the State concerned – ex officio. **Associate members** have no vote.
[63:4:7] - Fixes SC/ST reserved seats. Orders have the **force of law**, **cannot be questioned in any court** (Art. 329) and **cannot be modified** by the Lok Sabha or Assemblies – two separate safeguards.
[63:4:8] TABLE head: Act | Commission | Chair
  [63:4:8.0] 1952 | 1952 | Justice N. Chandrasekhara Aiyar
  [63:4:8.1] 1962 | 1963 | Justice J.L. Kapur
  [63:4:8.2] 1972 | 1973 | Justice J.L. Kapur
  [63:4:8.3] 2002 | 2002 (12 July 2002) | Justice Kuldip Singh
  [63:4:8.4] J&K | – | Justice Ranjana Desai (orders 2022)
[63:4:9] - **Four** Commissions constituted (till December 2023); J.L. Kapur chaired two.
[63:4:10] # Freeze and Amendments
[63:4:11] TABLE head: Amendment | Effect
  [63:4:11.0] 42nd (1976) | Froze State seat shares on 1971 Census till 2000 (population control)
  [63:4:11.1] 84th (2001) | Freeze till the first census after 2026; boundaries tidied on 1991 Census
  [63:4:11.2] 87th (2003) | Boundary redrawing on the 2001 Census; total seats unchanged
[63:4:12] - **Lok Sabha: 543** elected seats; ceiling **550 (530 States + 20 UTs)**.
[63:4:13] "Maximum 552 including 2 Anglo-Indians" is outdated – Anglo-Indian seats ended in 2020.
[63:4:14] # Latest Developments (2026)
[63:4:15] - **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026**: Lok Sabha ceiling 550 → **850**, delimitation on 2011 Census; **defeated** in Lok Sabha (298 for, 230 against – short of special majority). Delimitation Bill 2026 withdrawn (April 2026).
[63:4:16] - Southern States fear losing seats to **UP, Bihar, Rajasthan** under population-based delimitation (April 2026).
[63:4:17] - **Tamil Nadu Assembly resolution (17 Aug 2026)**: permanent freeze at **543**, keep **2.2:1** Lok Sabha–Rajya Sabha ratio, women's quota from **2029** not tied to delimitation.
[63:4:18] - **EAC-PM (28 Aug 2026)**: split 170 oversized seats to reach **824**.
[63:4:19] # Andhra Pradesh Angle
[63:4:20] - **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014, Section 26**: Assembly seats AP **175 → 225**, Telangana **119 → 153**, but **subject to Article 170** – so the rise waits for delimitation after the **first census after 2026**. Supreme Court refused to order it (**July 2025**).
[63:4:21] - The AP Reorganisation Bill, 2014 made follow-up changes to **RPA 1950**.
[63:4:22] - **Nodal ministry:** delimitation work of the Union is handled by the **Ministry of Law and Justice** (Legislative Department), which notifies the Delimitation Commission.

## SUB 7 | Electoral Rolls and Election Machinery
[63:7:0] - Four **qualifying dates**: **1 January, 1 April, 1 July, 1 October** (Election Laws (Amendment) Act 2021) – not 1 December.
[63:7:1] - **Summary revision** = annual; **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** = rebuilds the whole roll. SC let the ECI's **Bihar SIR** proceed; it removed about **70 lakh** names, and turnout was the highest since Independence (CEC, 17 Aug 2026).
[63:7:2] - **Overseas elector** (Sec. **20A**, RPA 1950, 2010): NRI enrols at the **Indian passport** address.
[63:7:3] TABLE head: Officer | Role
  [63:7:3.0] Chief Electoral Officer | Assists ECI in each State/UT
  [63:7:3.1] District Election Officer | Usually the Collector; receives expense accounts
  [63:7:3.2] Returning Officer | Conducts poll in a constituency, declares result
  [63:7:3.3] ERO / BLO | Electoral rolls; BLOs carry standard photo ID cards (2026)
  [63:7:3.4] Observers (Sec. 20B) | General, police, expenditure – not judicial
[63:7:4] - During elections the ECI controls officers on poll duty – can **transfer** them or act against biased conduct.
[63:7:5] - **Polling measures 2026**: max **1,200 electors** per polling station; candidate booths just beyond **100 m**.

[63:8:0] # Legal Status and Timeline
[63:8:1] - **ECI guidelines** for parties, candidates and the ruling government – **not enacted by Parliament**, not statutory; authority: **Art. 324**.
[63:8:2] TABLE head: Year | Milestone

  [63:8:2.1] 1968 | Formalised by the ECI

[63:8:3] - In force from the **announcement of the election schedule** (**Harbans Singh Jalal v UoI, 1997, P&H HC**) until results.
[63:8:4] - Example: from **15 March 2026** for Assam, Kerala, TN, West Bengal, Puducherry; lifted **7 May 2026** except the Falta re-poll seat.
[63:8:5] - Covers speeches, polling booths, processions, manifestos, general conduct.
[63:8:6] # Part VII Curbs and Sanctions
[63:8:7] - Ministers may **not combine official visits with campaigning**; no official machinery/transport; **no new schemes or grants**.
[63:8:8] - Ordinary official visits without campaigning are **NOT** restricted.
[63:8:9] - Sanction: censure up to **suspension/withdrawal of recognition** under **Para 16A, Election Symbols Order, 1968**.
[63:8:10] - 2026: a petition alleged the PM's national broadcast during Assembly polls breached Part VII.
## SUB 9 | Evm-vvpat, Nota and Indelible Ink
[63:9:0] # EVM and VVPAT
[63:9:1] - EVM = vote-recording and counting device – **not an electoral system**; used under ECI control for Lok Sabha and Assembly polls.
[63:9:2] - **EVM first used: Parur (Kerala), 1982**; **VVPAT first: Noksen (Nagaland), 2013**.
[63:9:3] - **EVM rollout:** experimental in 16 Assembly seats (MP, Rajasthan, Delhi) in **1998**; first whole-State poll **Goa 1999**; ⚠ APPSC key (2025) gave the Goa statement as untrue.
[63:9:4] - **N. Chandrababu Naidu v UoI (8 April 2019)**: VVPAT slips tallied in **5 polling stations per Assembly segment** (up from one).
[63:9:5] - **ADR v ECI (26 April 2024)**: SC rejected **100% VVPAT** counting; second/third-placed candidates may seek checks of **5% of EVMs** per constituency by engineers, by written request within **7 days** of results, at their cost.
[63:9:6] - EVM totalisers: SC hearing (2 Sep 2026).
[63:9:7] # NOTA
[63:9:8] - **PUCL v Union of India, 27 September 2013** – NOTA button on all EVMs; secret negative vote linked to **Art. 19(1)(a)**.
[63:9:9] - First Lok Sabha use **2014** (1.1% of votes, over 6 million). Symbol (ballot with **black cross**) designed by **NID Ahmedabad**.
[63:9:10] - **Symbolic only**: even if NOTA gets most votes, the top candidate wins – **no fresh poll**.
[63:9:11] - Using NOTA = active voting, **not abstention**.
[63:9:12] - **Not** for Rajya Sabha/Legislative Councils (struck down by SC, **2018**).
[63:9:13] TABLE head: Basis | NOTA | Old Rule 49-O
  [63:9:13.0] How | Button on EVM | Voter refused to vote before the Presiding Officer; noted in Form 17-A
  [63:9:13.1] Effect | Symbolic | Broke secrecy; replaced
