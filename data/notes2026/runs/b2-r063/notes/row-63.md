=== Part XV and the Right to Vote – Articles 324-329, Adult Suffrage
# Part XV – Elections (Articles 324-329)
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| **324** | **Election Commission**: superintendence, direction and control of electoral rolls and of all elections to **Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President**; 324(2): CEC + other ECs as the **President** fixes |
| **325** | **One general electoral roll** for every territorial constituency; no one excluded on grounds only of **religion, race, caste or sex** |
| **326** | Elections to the **Lok Sabha and State Assemblies** on the basis of **adult suffrage** (universal franchise) |
| **327 / 328** | Parliament / State Legislature may make laws on elections |
| **329** | Bar on courts: delimitation laws cannot be questioned; an election can be challenged **only by an election petition** |
- Elections are in **Part XV** (not XIV, XVI or XVII). Correct pair: **ECI – Art. 324** (Supreme Court = 124, UPSC = 315, Attorney General = 76); Art. 280 = Finance Commission; **Art. 171** = Legislative Councils, not elections.
- ECI set up on **25 January 1950** – permanent constitutional body; its institute **IIIDEM** trains election managers. **Mohinder Singh Gill v CEC (1978)**: Art. 324 is a "**reservoir of power**" where Parliament has not legislated.
- **ECI** conducts Lok Sabha, Assembly, Rajya Sabha, Council, **President and Vice-President** polls and prepares **electoral rolls / registers voters**; **State Election Commissions** (Arts. **243K, 243ZA**) run panchayat and municipal polls.
- Qualifications/disqualifications: **Art. 84 / 102** (Parliament), **173 / 191** (State Legislatures). Simultaneous polls would need amending **Arts. 83** (duration of Houses), **85, 172, 174, 356**.
# Adult suffrage
- **Art. 326**: every citizen aged **18+** may vote unless disqualified by law for **non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime, corrupt or illegal practice** – universal franchise "subject to limitations by statute".
- Voting age **21 → 18**: **61st Amendment Act, 1988** (in force **28 March 1989**); 18-year-olds first voted in the **1989** Lok Sabha election (not 59th, 60th, 63rd, 69th or 73rd).
- Principles for Lok Sabha/Assembly elections (UPPSC 2011): adult suffrage, **one roll per territorial constituency**, no exclusion on religion, race, sex (Arts. 325-326); the UPPSC key also accepted "parties free to have their own norms" (All four).
- Democracy needs **political parties, an electorate, an independent judiciary and universal adult suffrage** (UPSC 1994: all four).
- Right to vote and to contest = **statutory rights** (**Kuldip Nayar v UoI, 2006**), not Fundamental Rights; with the right to petition they are **political rights** – the **right to life** (Art. 21) is a civil right.
- **Who cannot vote:** a person **confined in prison** (Sec. 62(5) RPA 1951); **preventive detenus** vote by **postal ballot**. **Overseas electors** (Sec. **20A** RPA 1950, 2010): Indian **citizen**, enrolled at the **passport address**, must vote **in person** in India.

=== Reservation of Seats – Articles 330-334 and Anglo-Indians
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **330** | Seats reserved for **SCs and STs in the Lok Sabha**, in proportion to their population |
| **331** | President nominated **2 Anglo-Indians** to the Lok Sabha |
| **332** | SC/ST seats in **State Assemblies** |
| **333** | **Governor** nominated **1 Anglo-Indian** to the Assembly |
| **334** | Time limit: originally **10 years (till 1960)**, extended by successive amendments (**79th, 1999** → 2010; **95th, 2009** → 2020); **104th (2019)** → SC/ST till **2030**, Anglo-Indian nomination **ended from 25 Jan 2020** |
| **334A** | Women one-third – **106th Amendment (2023)**, for 15 years; starts after the next census and delimitation |
- Anglo-Indian nomination **ceased in 2020** (not 2030, 2035, 2040); "Only A" (President 2, Governor 1) was correct before 2020.
- Reserved seats have a **common electorate** – all voters of the constituency vote; ST seats go where the ST share is highest, SC seats are spread and rotated.
- SC seats in a State follow its SC population share: **undivided AP (42 Lok Sabha seats) had 7 SC and 3 ST** after 2008 (6 SC before).
- **Madhya Pradesh** has the most ST-reserved Lok Sabha seats (**6**).
- **Municipalities (Art. 243T)**: not less than **one-third** of SC/ST seats reserved for SC/ST **women**.

=== Delimitation – Commission, Freeze and Amendments
# Meaning and basis
- **Delimitation** = fixing the **number and boundaries** of Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies, and the **SC/ST reserved seats**, on the latest **census** – "**one person, one vote, one value**".
- The body that decides the number and size of constituencies = **Delimitation Commission** (not the ECI, NITI Aayog or MHA); purpose = revising **constituency** boundaries, **not State boundaries** (Parliament, Art. 3).
- Basis: **Art. 82** (Lok Sabha), **Art. 170** (Assemblies) – readjustment after each census; seats go to States on **population, not area**.
# Delimitation Commission
- Set up by the **Central Government** under an **Act of Parliament**; can be set up for **select States/UTs** (2020: J&K, Assam, Manipur, Arunachal, Nagaland). Nodal ministry: **Ministry of Law and Justice** (Legislative Department).
- Members (2002 Act): a retired **Supreme Court judge** (chair), the **CEC** or an EC he nominates, and the **State Election Commissioner** – ex officio.
- Orders have the **force of law**: **cannot be questioned in any court** and **cannot be modified** when laid before the Lok Sabha/Assembly (UPSC 2012: both correct).
| Act | Commission | Chair |
|---|---|---|
| 1952 | **1952 (first)** | Justice **N. Chandrasekhara Aiyar** |
| 1962 | 1963 | Justice J.L. Kapur |
| 1972 | **1973** | Justice **J.L. Kapur** |
| 2002 | 2002 | Justice **Kuldip Singh** |
| J&K | 2020 (orders 2022) | Justice **Ranjana Desai** |
- **Four** Delimitation Commissions (1952, 1963, 1973, 2002) till December 2023 (UPSC 2024).
# Freeze
| Amendment | Effect |
|---|---|
| **42nd (1976)** | Froze State seat shares on the **1971 Census** till 2000 |
| **84th (2001)** | Freeze extended till the **first census after 2026**; lifted the freeze for redrawing boundaries (on 1991 Census) |
| **87th (2003)** | Boundaries redrawn on the **2001 Census**, total seats unchanged |
- Present constituencies (2001 Census) continue till the **first census after 2026**. Lok Sabha: **543** elected; ceiling **550 (530 States + 20 UTs)**.
- AP Reorganisation Act, 2014, **Sec. 26**: AP Assembly **175 → 225**, Telangana 119 → 153, subject to Art. 170 – waits for the **first census after 2026**. The **RPA 1950** deals with **allocation of seats and electoral rolls**.
- 2026: **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill** – Lok Sabha ceiling 550 → **850**; **defeated** in the Lok Sabha for want of a special majority. Southern States fear seats shifting to **UP, Bihar, Rajasthan**; Tamil Nadu Assembly resolution (Aug 2026) sought a permanent freeze at 543 and women's quota from 2029 not tied to delimitation.

=== Electoral Systems and Types of Elections
# Who is elected how
| Office / House | Electorate | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Lok Sabha / Legislative Assembly | Citizens directly (adult suffrage) | **First past the post**, single-member territorial constituencies |
| Rajya Sabha | **Elected MLAs**; seats allotted to States by **population** | **PR – single transferable vote** |
| President | Elected MPs + elected MLAs (incl. Delhi, Puducherry – **70th Amendment**) | PR-STV |
| Vice-President (Art. 66) | **All MPs – elected and nominated**; State Legislatures have no role | PR-STV |
| Legislative Council | MLAs, local bodies, graduates, teachers + Governor's nominees | PR-STV |
- India uses **direct election on adult suffrage** and **PR by single transferable vote**; the **list system** and **cumulative vote** are not used.
# First past the post vs proportional representation
- **FPTP** (plurality): the candidate with **more votes than any other** wins – no 50% needed. Vote-seat distortion, e.g. **Karnataka 1994**: Janata Dal 116 seats on 36% votes.
- **PR** gives seats in proportion to votes → **representation of minorities**; not needed where a **two-party system** has developed (UK). Rejected for the Lok Sabha: complex, fragmentation, **unstable governments**, no by-elections.
# Types of elections and special constituencies
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| General | All seats, at normal end of term |
| **Mid-term** | After the House is **dissolved before its term** ends |
| By-election | A single vacancy; within 6 months (Sec. 151A) – not if the remaining term is **under one year** |
- **Floating constituency**: no fixed geographical boundary – **Sangha (Sikkim)**: only registered **Buddhist monks** vote and contest (both statements correct).
- **Security deposit** is forfeited if a candidate does not get **more than one-sixth** of the valid votes (Sec. 158).

=== EVM-VVPAT, NOTA and Indelible Ink
# EVM and VVPAT
- EVM = electronic vote-recording and counting device (not an electoral system). **First used: Parur (Kerala), 1982**; older EVMs recorded up to **3,840** votes – current ECI EVMs up to **2,000**.
- **VVPAT first used: Noksen Assembly by-poll, Nagaland, 4 September 2013** (not LS 2014, Goa 2017).
- **N. Chandrababu Naidu v UoI (2019)**: VVPAT slips tallied in **5 polling stations per Assembly segment**. **ADR v ECI (2024)**: SC rejected **100% VVPAT** counting; 2nd/3rd-placed candidates may seek checks of 5% of EVMs.
# NOTA
- **PUCL v Union of India, 27 September 2013** – SC ordered a NOTA button on all EVMs (secret negative vote, **Art. 19(1)(a)**). NOTA introduced in **2013** – first used in the **Nov-Dec 2013 Assembly polls**; first Lok Sabha use **2014 = 16th Lok Sabha** (1.1% votes). The 12th-15th Lok Sabha polls had no NOTA, and it was not used in 2009.
- Symbol: ballot paper with a **black cross**, designed by **NID Ahmedabad**. NOTA votes are **not counted as valid votes** for result/deposit; even if NOTA tops, the leading candidate **wins** – no fresh poll; using NOTA is **voting, not abstention**.
- **Not** for **Rajya Sabha / Legislative Councils** (SC struck it down, **2018**) – only direct elections.
- Old **Rule 49-O**: voter refused to vote before the Presiding Officer, noted in **Form 17-A** – broke secrecy.

=== Model Code of Conduct, Polls and Voter Facilitation
# Model Code of Conduct
- **ECI guidelines** evolved by **consensus of parties** – **not enacted by Parliament**, not in the Constitution, IPC or RPA (voluntary compliance); authority: **Art. 324**.
- In force from the **announcement of the election schedule** (**Harbans Singh Jalal v UoI, 1997**) till results (A true, "enacted by Parliament" false).
| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1960 | First drafted and used in the **Kerala** Assembly election |
| 1968 | Formalised by the ECI |
| 1979 | **Part VII** ("party in power") added |
| 1991 | Strictly enforced by CEC **T.N. Seshan** |
- Part VII: ministers may **not combine official visits with campaigning**; ordinary official visits are allowed.
- On violation the ECI can **warn / censure** a candidate or party (it cannot disqualify, cancel results or fine); recognition can be suspended under **Para 16A, Election Symbols Order, 1968**.
# Exit polls and opinion polls
- **Exit poll** = **post-election survey** of voters just after leaving the booth, by private agencies; **Sec. 126A**: barred from the first phase till **half an hour after the last phase**. **Opinion poll** = pre-poll survey; **Sec. 126**: 48-hour silence. They are not the same.

| **Sahulat app** | **Jammu and Kashmir** (Rajouri, 2019) – **elderly voters** |

=== Candidates, RPA Acts, Machinery and Election Petitions
# Candidates and the RPA
- Lok Sabha nomination: **any citizen of India whose name is on the electoral roll of any constituency**, aged **25+** (residence in that constituency not needed).
- **Sec. 8 RPA 1951** (made by **Parliament**): conviction with **2+ years** → disqualified during the sentence + **6 years** after release (not permanently). A non-member minister must be **qualified for membership**, not merely a voter.
| Basis | RPA 1950 | RPA 1951 |
|---|---|---|
| Deals with | **Allocation of seats**, delimitation framework, voter qualifications, **electoral rolls** | Conduct of elections, disqualifications, **party registration (Sec. 29A)**, **corrupt practices**, election petitions |
| Linked Articles | 81, 82, 170, 171, 325, 326 | 84, 102, 173, 191, 324, 329 |
- **Sec. 14 / 15**: **President** (Lok Sabha) / **Governor** (Assembly) issue the formal notification on ECI's recommendation; the ECI notifies the schedule, scrutinises nominations, allots symbols. **Validity of an election** is settled only by the **courts**.
- **Sec. 77-78**: expense accounts to the **DEO** within **30 days**.
# Rolls, machinery and parties
- Rolls: four **qualifying dates** – 1 Jan, 1 Apr, 1 Jul, 1 Oct (not 1 December); **Special Intensive Revision** rebuilds the roll (**Bihar** SIR removed about 70 lakh names).
- Officers: **CEO** (State), **DEO** = usually the **District Collector**, **Returning Officer** conducts the poll and declares the result; observers (Sec. 20B) – general, police, **expenditure**. The ECI controls officers on poll duty.
- Parties: **registered** by the **ECI** under **Sec. 29A RPA 1951**; **recognised** by the ECI under the **Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968**. National party: State party in **4** States, or 6% votes in 4 States + 4 LS seats, or 2% LS seats from 3 States; State party: 3% of Assembly seats or **3** seats. **Six** national parties (2025): BJP, INC, BSP, CPI(M), AAP, NPP (not TMC). ECI delisted **808** registered unrecognised parties.
# Election petitions
| Section (RPA 1951) | Content |
|---|---|
| **80** | No election can be questioned except by an **election petition** (Art. 329(b)) |
| **80A** | Tried by the **High Court** (original jurisdiction); to try to decide in **6 months** |
| **81** | Filed within **45 days** of the result |
| **100** | **Grounds** for declaring an election void (disqualification, corrupt practice, improper rejection of nomination – not a small margin) |
- Appeal: **Supreme Court**. **President / Vice-President** election disputes: **only the Supreme Court** (Art. 71) – not Parliament or the ECI.
- Electoral offences (bribery, undue influence, personation): from **1 July 2024 BNS Chapter IX (Secs. 169-177)**.
