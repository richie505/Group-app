## SUB 0 | Energy Forms and Conversions – the Physics Behind a Hydro Plant
[0:0] # Hydro plant
[0:1] - Stored water (**potential**) → falling water (**kinetic**) → turbine (**mechanical**) → generator (**electrical**, by electromagnetic induction). Net: **potential → electrical** (APPSC 2023).
[0:2] - The **Mangal turbine** (water-lifting device of Mangal Singh) runs on the **kinetic energy of flowing water**.
[0:3] # Forms of energy
[0:4] - **Mechanical energy = kinetic + potential**; under a **conservative force** the **total mechanical energy** stays constant. SI unit of all energy = **joule** (watt = power).
[0:5] - **Kinetic energy** = ½mv² – depends on **mass and velocity**; a fast car has kinetic energy.
[0:6] - **Potential energy** = energy of position or shape: a **raised hammer**, a compressed spring or slinky.
[0:7] - **Chemical energy:** stored in the **bonds of atoms and molecules** (food, fuels, cells). **Radiant energy** = electromagnetic, **transverse** waves; **sound energy** = **longitudinal** (compression–rarefaction) waves.
[0:8] - **Stored forms:** chemical, nuclear, gravitational and elastic potential. **Electrical energy** is energy in transit, not a stored form (NDA 2016). Thermal energy is not a form of potential energy.
[0:9] - **Free fall:** loss of potential energy = gain of kinetic energy; after 5 m of a 20 m fall the body has lost **one-fourth of its potential energy**. A body rolling down a hill has **both** KE and PE; a rising swing loses KE and gains PE.
[0:10] - **Friction** turns **kinetic energy into heat**. **Lightning** carries huge **electrical energy**. **Fireworks:** chemical → heat, light, sound and kinetic energy (all).
[0:11] - **Photosynthesis:** light energy → **chemical energy**.
[0:12] TABLE head: Device | Conversion
  [0:12.0] Dynamo / generator | Mechanical → electrical (a converter of energy, not a source of charge)
  [0:12.1] Electric motor | Electrical → mechanical
  [0:12.2] Battery / dry cell | Chemical → electrical
  [0:12.3] Photoelectric / solar cell | Light → electrical
  [0:12.4] Wind turbine | Kinetic energy of wind (only kinetic) → mechanical → electrical
  [0:12.5] Diesel engine | Chemical (fuel) → heat → mechanical (not "light to electrical")
[0:13] - **Photoelectric effect** discovered by **Heinrich Hertz (1887)**; explained by Einstein.

## SUB 4 | Pumped Storage Hydropower
[4:0] - **Two reservoirs** at different heights: surplus/off-peak power **pumps water up**; at peak the water flows down through turbines – a "**giant water battery**".
[4:1] - Discussed as **long-duration energy storage** (UPSC 2024); the fastest-responding large-scale storage.
[4:2] - **No ISTS charges** for BESS and PSP **until 2028**; off-stream **closed-loop** PSPs need no CEA concurrence.
[4:3] - CEA plan to 2035-36: **174 GW** storage = **94 GW PSP + 80 GW BESS**.

## SUB 5 | Types of Hydro Plants, Small Hydro and Potential
[5:0] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [5:0.0] Run-of-the-river (diversion) | Little or no storage; natural flow
  [5:0.1] Storage (impoundment) | Dam stores water
  [5:0.2] Pumped storage | Surplus power pumps water up
[5:1] - **Small hydro ≤ 25 MW → MNRE**; large hydro > 25 MW → **Ministry of Power**. Up to 3 MW moved to MNRE in **1989**; raised to **25 MW** in **Nov 1999**. Large hydro declared **renewable** in **March 2019**.
[5:2] - **Small-hydro target: 5 GW** within the 175 GW renewable target for 2022 (100 solar, 60 wind, 10 bio, 5 small hydro); installed small hydro ~5.18 GW (2026).
[5:3] - Potential **over 145 GW**, about **36%** developed; **Arunachal Pradesh** largest. Small-hydro potential 21,133 MW at **7,133** sites.
[5:4] - India **3rd** in large dams (**6,628** specified dams); most in **Maharashtra**. East-flowing peninsular rivers suit **irrigation** more than power.

## SUB 6 | Large Dams – Trade-offs, Heights and Construction
[6:0] - **"Temples of modern India"** – **Jawaharlal Nehru**, of dams such as **Bhakra Nangal**.
[6:1] - Hydro: cheap and clean once built; weak in **drought and snowpack** variability.
[6:2] TABLE head: Trade-off | Detail
  [6:2.0] Displacement | Bhakra, Sardar Sarovar, Tehri
  [6:2.1] Seismicity | Himalayan risk
  [6:2.2] Sedimentation | Cuts reservoir capacity
  [6:2.3] Ecology | Blocks fish migration
[6:3] - Dam heights in India: **Tehri > Bhakra > Idukki > Koldam**.
[6:4] - World's highest dam in books: **Rogun (Tajikistan)**, about 335 m when complete (under construction); the tallest completed dam is Jinping-I, China (305 m).
[6:5] - **Earthen dams:** **rolled-fill** methods = **homogeneous, zoned and diaphragm** embankments; the **hydraulic-fill** dam is a separate method.
