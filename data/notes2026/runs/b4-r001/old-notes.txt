=== NOTES 4:1 | Earth – Shape, Size, Geoid; Latitudes and Longitudes, the Graticule
## SUB 0 | Shape of the Earth
[1:0:0] - **Geoid** = "earth-shaped"; Earth is an **oblate spheroid** (flat at poles, bulging at Equator).
[1:0:1] - First to say Earth is round: **Aristotle** ("On the Heavens").
[1:0:2] - Proofs: **circular shadow on the Moon** in a lunar eclipse; mast seen first; circumnavigation.
[1:0:3] - Weight: **minimum at Equator**, maximum at poles (centrifugal force maximum at Equator).
[1:0:4] - Surface point nearest the centre: **poles** (Arctic Ocean floor near North Pole).
[1:0:5] - At the Equator, stars rise **perpendicular** to the horizon.
## SUB 1 | Dimensions

  [1:1:0.0] Equatorial diameter | 12,756 km (~12,800)

  [1:1:0.3] Equatorial circumference | ~40,075 km
  [1:1:0.4] Water : land | 71 : 29 (about two-thirds water)
## SUB 2 | Graticule
[1:2:0] - Parallels at 1°: **179** (89 N + 89 S + Equator; poles are points). Meridians: **360**.
[1:2:1] - Latitudes run **east–west**; valid range 0–90° (longitude 0–180°).
[1:2:2] - **Great circles**: Equator (only great-circle parallel) and opposite meridian pairs (**180° + Prime Meridian**). **60°N is not** a great circle.
[1:2:3] - **0°, 0°** = **Gulf of Guinea**, Atlantic Ocean.
[1:2:4] - 1° latitude ≈ **111 km** everywhere; 1° longitude: 111 km at Equator → **55.5 km at 60°** → 0 at poles.
[1:2:5] - **Antipode**: (x°N, y°E) → (x°S, (180 − y)°W); **20°N 80°E → 20°S 100°W**.
[1:2:6] 179 parallels, not 181 (BPSC 2023).
[1:2:7] - **Meridians:** lines of longitude run north–south, from the North Pole to the South Pole.

=== NOTES 4:2 | Motions of the Earth – Rotation and Revolution; Effects (day and Night, Coriolis Force, Tides)
## SUB 0 | Rotation
[2:0:0] - Earth spins on its **axis** (line through both poles) **west to east** – counter-clockwise seen from the North Pole. Only **Venus and Uranus** spin the other way.
[2:0:1] - Axis tilted **23.5° (23°30')** from the ecliptic axis.
[2:0:2] - Angular speed **15° per hour** (1° in 4 minutes). Linear speed ~**1,670 km/h at the Equator**, **zero at the poles**.
[2:0:3] - Not felt: uniform motion; atmosphere moves along.
[2:0:4] # Sidereal vs Solar Day
[2:0:5] TABLE head: Day | Measured against | Length
  [2:0:5.0] Sidereal | A distant star | 23 h 56 min 4 s
  [2:0:5.1] Solar | The Sun | 24 h (~4 min longer, as Earth also moves in orbit)
[2:0:6] # Changes in Rotation
[2:0:7] - Ice melt moves mass towards the Equator, raising the moment of inertia: days longer by **~1.33 ms per century**.
[2:0:8] - Axis shift is mainly from mass redistribution (ice, groundwater loss) – **UPSC 2025**; solar flares only disturb the upper atmosphere.
## SUB 1 | Evidence of Rotation
[2:1:0] TABLE head: Person / proof | Fact
  [2:1:0.0] Aryabhata | First proposed rotation; explained the Sun's daily rise and set (Aryasiddhanta)
  [2:1:0.1] Foucault pendulum | Paris, 1851: plane of swing slowly turns
  [2:1:0.2] Other proofs | Coriolis, equatorial bulge, apparent sky motion
## SUB 2 | Day and Night
[2:2:0] - Caused by **rotation** – not revolution, not tilt alone.
[2:2:1] - **Circle of illumination** = terminator = "**grey line**" / twilight zone (**APPSC 2019**); passes through both poles **only at the equinoxes**.
[2:2:2] - Unequal day lengths: **tilt + revolution**.
[2:2:3] - **Equator**: ~12 h day all year; seasons marked by wet and dry periods. Variation grows with latitude; **poles: ~6 months day + 6 months night** (revolution with tilt).
[2:2:4] - **Midnight Sun**: beyond the Arctic and Antarctic Circles near the summer solstice; **Norway** = "Land of the Midnight Sun".
[2:2:5] # Twilight
[2:2:6] - **Shortest at Equator** (Sun rises and sets almost vertically); **longest at high latitudes** (oblique path; weeks near poles).
## SUB 3 | Coriolis and Centrifugal Effects
[2:3:0] - **Coriolis** = apparent deflection of moving air/water caused by **rotation** (APPSC-G1 2019; BPSC 2022).
[2:3:1] - **Ferrel's law**: deflection to the **right in NH**, **left in SH**.
[2:3:2] - Force = **2Ωv sin(latitude)**: **zero at Equator**, maximum at poles; grows with latitude and wind speed (UPSC 2024).
[2:3:3] - Cyclones rarely form within **5° of the Equator**; NH cyclones spin **anticlockwise**.
[2:3:4] - AP angle: **Cyclone Montha (2025)** was pushed north-northwest (steering winds, trade winds, Coriolis) to the **Kakinada** coast.
[2:3:5] - **Centrifugal force**: maximum at Equator, zero at poles → equatorial bulge, polar flattening (**oblate spheroid**); **weight least at Equator**.
## SUB 4 | Tides
[2:4:0] TABLE head: Tide | Position
  [2:4:0.0] Spring | Sun–Earth–Moon in line (new and full moon)
  [2:4:0.1] Neap | Sun and Moon at right angles (~7 days later)
[2:4:1] - Each twice a month. Two high, two low tides daily (~**12 h 25 min** apart).
[2:4:2] - High tide comes **~52 min later** each day (lunar day 24 h 52 min): 8:00 am → 8:52 am.
## SUB 5 | Revolution and Leap Year
[2:5:0] - Slightly **elliptical** orbit; period **365.25 days** (365 d 6 h 9 min 9.51 s); mean speed **29.78 km/s**.
[2:5:1] - Perihelion early January (**3 Jan in 2026**); aphelion early July (**6 July in 2026**). Earth–Sun distance is not constant.
[2:5:2] - **Sun's gravity** = centripetal force.
[2:5:3] - Gives the year, **seasons** (with tilt), solstices and equinoxes. Sun overhead at Equator on **21 March and 23 September**.
[2:5:4] - Extra quarter day → **leap year** of **366 days** with **29 February**; century year only if divisible by **400** (**2000 yes, 1900 no**).
[2:5:5] - Rotation gives **day and night, time zones, Coriolis**; revolution gives the year and seasons.
