=== NOTES 1:2 | Stone Age Cultures – Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic, Chalcolithic Sites of India
## SUB 0 | Stone Age Phases – Overview
[2:0:0] - Order: **Lower → Middle → Upper Palaeolithic → Mesolithic → Neolithic → Chalcolithic → Iron Age (Megalithic)**.
[2:0:1] - The **Indus civilisation** belongs to the **proto-historic** age.
## SUB 1 | Palaeolithic – Tool Techniques and Sites
[2:1:0] # Stage-wise Table
[2:1:1] TABLE head: Stage | Technique / tools | Sites
  [2:1:1.0] Lower | Acheulian = handaxe-cleaver; Soanian = chopper-chopping | Attirampakkam (TN), Hunsgi (Karnataka), Didwana (Rajasthan), Sohan valley (Pakistan Punjab)
  [2:1:1.1] Middle (c. 50,000–25,000 BCE) | Flake industry; prepared-core Levallois; scrapers, borers, points | Nevasa, Bhimbetka, Narmada, Godavari, Krishna valleys; Sembur (TN)
  [2:1:1.2] Upper (c. 40,000–10,000 BCE) | Blades, bone tools; first microliths | Kurnool caves, Jwalapuram, Patne, Baghor I and III
  [2:1:1.3] Mesolithic | Microlithic (end of the Ice Age) | Bagor, Adamgarh, Langhnaj
[2:1:2] Middle Palaeolithic = **flake-tool** stage, NOT the "microlithic stage". Microliths belong to the Upper Palaeolithic and Mesolithic.
[2:1:3] PYQ 2023: **Levallois** technique was used at **Sembur** (TN). No Neanderthal fossil has been found in India.
## SUB 2 | Mesolithic Sites and Microlith Centres
[2:2:0] TABLE head: Site | Region / feature
  [2:2:0.0] Bagor (Bhilwara) | Rajasthan; rock shelters, microliths, early domestication
  [2:2:0.1] Adamgarh (Narmadapuram) | MP; rock shelter, early domestication
  [2:2:0.2] Langhnaj | Gujarat
  [2:2:0.3] Sarai Nahar Rai, Mahadaha, Damdama | Ganga plain, UP (Pratapgarh)
  [2:2:0.4] Lekhahia, Baghai Khor | Rock shelters, Vindhyas (Mirzapur, UP)
  [2:2:0.5] Paisra | Bihar
  [2:2:0.6] Birbhanpur | West Bengal
  [2:2:0.7] Teri sites | Thoothukudi (TN) and Visakhapatnam coast
[2:2:1] - **Lekhahia**: Mesolithic **bone ornaments**; most human skeletons – **17** from rock shelter 1.
[2:2:2] - West to east: **Mahadaha → Lekhahia → Paisra → Birbhanpur**.
[2:2:3] - Taming of animals began in the **Mesolithic**; first proof at **Adamgarh** and **Bagor**.
## SUB 3 | Neolithic Sites
[2:3:0] TABLE head: Site | Region | Distinctive find
  [2:3:0.0] Mehrgarh | Baluchistan | Earliest farming (wheat, barley), mud-brick houses
  [2:3:0.1] Burzahom | Kashmir | Pit dwellings, dog buried with master
  [2:3:0.2] Gufkral | Kashmir | Pit dwellings
  [2:3:0.3] Chirand | Bihar | Bone and antler tools
  [2:3:0.4] Koldihwa | UP | Early rice; Neolithic–Chalcolithic–Iron sequence
  [2:3:0.5] Lehuradeva | UP | Early rice, c. 6500 BCE
  [2:3:0.6] Utnoor | Telangana | Ash mounds (cattle pens)
[2:3:1] - **Eastern Neolithic** (Birbhanpur, Chirand, Kuchai, Sankarjang): **pointed-butt celts**, **shouldered axes**; ties with **South-East Asia**.
[2:3:2] - Growing of cereals began in the **Neolithic**: Koldihwa (rice), Mehrgarh (wheat).
## SUB 4 | Neolithic–chalcolithic Sites of Andhra and South India
[2:4:0] - **Ash mounds** = burnt remains of seasonal camps of Neolithic herdsmen; at **Sanganakallu** (Ballari, Karnataka), **Piklihal** and **Utnur**.
[2:4:1] - **Tekkalakota** (Ballari): Neolithic hill-dwellers; excavated by **M.S. Nagaraja Rao** (1963–64).
[2:4:2] - **Palavoy** (Anantapur): four ash mounds and a Neolithic habitation; later phase used iron.
[2:4:3] - **Keesarapalli** (Krishna): Chalcolithic **copper tools**. **Patapadu** (Kurnool): Chalcolithic **painted pottery**.
## SUB 5 | Chalcolithic Cultures of India
[2:5:0] # Culture-wise Table
[2:5:1] TABLE head: Culture | Type site / sites | State | Date | Pottery / feature
  [2:5:1.0] Ahar-Banas | Ahar, Gilund, Balathal | Rajasthan (Mewar, Banas river) | c. 2800–1500 BCE | White-painted black-and-red ware; copper-using
  [2:5:1.1] Kayatha | Kayatha, Chambal valley | MP | 2450–1700 BCE | Chocolate-slipped ware; Early Chalcolithic
  [2:5:1.2] Malwa | Navdatoli (on the Narmada), Eran | MP | 1900–1400 BCE | Coarse Malwa ware
  [2:5:1.3] Jorwe | Inamgaon, Daimabad; also Savalda | Maharashtra | c. 1400–700 BCE | Painted red ware; village farming
[2:5:2] - Order of start: **Ahar-Banas → Kayatha → Malwa → Jorwe**.
[2:5:3] - **Ahar and Kayatha** lived alongside the **Harappans**; many other Chalcolithic cultures (Malwa, Jorwe) came later.
[2:5:4] - **Daimabad** (Pravara river, Maharashtra): fortified, copper objects, **bronze chariot**, buffalo; **southernmost Harappan site**.
[2:5:5] - **Savalda**: a Chalcolithic tradition of Maharashtra.
[2:5:6] **Navdatoli** is a Malwa-culture settlement in **Madhya Pradesh**, not a Gujarat port.
[2:5:7] First metal used by man = **Copper** (Chalcolithic).
## SUB 6 | Rock Art
[2:6:0] # Bhimbetka
[2:6:1] - Near **Bhopal** (**Raisen**, MP); discovered by **Dr. Vishnu S. Wakankar, 1957** (1957–58); **UNESCO site 2003**.
[2:6:2] - Rock shelter with the **most paintings** in India; continuous habitation from **Lower Palaeolithic** to medieval times.
[2:6:3] - Upper Palaeolithic art in about **300 caves** and shelters; Mesolithic paintings show **hunting** and people carrying baskets.
[2:6:4] - 12 May 2026: after contact with Chalcolithic farmers of **Malwa**, the style became **static**.
[2:6:5] - **Mandu fort** is NOT a World Heritage Site; Khajuraho, Bhimbetka, Sanchi are.
[2:6:6] # AP Rock Art
[2:6:7] - **Borra Caves** (Visakhapatnam), **Kethavaram** (Kurnool); most often painted animal = **deer** (not elephant).
[2:6:8] - **Petroglyph** = **carved**; a painting is applied on the surface; in India from the Upper Palaeolithic–Mesolithic.

## SUB 8 | Megaliths
[2:8:0] # Nature
[2:8:1] - **Graves with weapons, tools, pottery**, circled by **big stones**; burial sites away from homes; also **memorials**.
[2:8:2] - **Iron Age**, with **black-and-red ware**; c. **1500/1000 BCE – 300 CE**.
[2:8:3] - AP names: "**Rakshasa Gullu**", also Pandava Gullu, Kurambara Gullu, Morivara Gullu.
[2:8:4] - About **12 types** in AP, studied by **N. R. Banerji**; grave goods show ranks – **dolmens for elites, pits for commoners**.
[2:8:5] - Black-and-red ware at all Andhra megalithic sites **except Visakhapatnam**.
[2:8:6] # Types and AP Districts
[2:8:7] TABLE head: Type | Form | AP distribution
  [2:8:7.0] Cist | Stone-slab box | Common in AP; listed for Visakhapatnam, but "many cist burials in Visakhapatnam" is keyed incorrect (G2 2025)
  [2:8:7.1] Dolmen | Table-like chamber of large slabs | Krishna, Chittoor, East Godavari
  [2:8:7.2] Sarcophagus | Terracotta coffin, often with legs | Chittoor, Anantapur; one near Nallavaram; not Visakhapatnam
  [2:8:7.3] Menhir | Upright stone, 2–6 m | Krishna
  [2:8:7.4] Anthropomorphic | Human-shaped stone | Tirupati – India's largest collection
  [2:8:7.5] Others | Cairn circle, urn burial | –
[2:8:8] - AP megalithic sites: **Nagarjunakonda** (centre), Brahmagiri, Gudivada, **Jonnawada** (Nellore), Boodigapalli, Peddaveedu.
[2:8:9] - Kerala: rock-cut burial chamber at **Panayal** (Kasaragod).

=== NOTES 1:3 | Pre-historic Cultures of Andhra – Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic and Megalithic Sites
## SUB 0 | Stages in Andhra (oldest First)
[3:0:0] - 1. **Lower Palaeolithic** – handaxe, cleaver, chopper.
[3:0:1] - 2. **Middle Palaeolithic** – flakes, scrapers, borers, points.
[3:0:2] - 3. **Upper Palaeolithic** – blades, bone tools (Kurnool caves, Jwalapuram).
[3:0:3] - 4. **Mesolithic = Microlithic** – microliths.
[3:0:4] - 5. **Neolithic–Chalcolithic** – ash mounds; copper tools, painted pottery.
[3:0:5] - 6. **Megalithic = Iron Age** – black-and-red ware; grave goods.
[3:0:6] - Discoverer of AP prehistory: **Robert Bruce Foote**; he also found tools at **Pallavaram** near Chennai.
## SUB 1 | Tools by Stage and Raw Material
[3:1:0] TABLE head: Stage | Tools | Raw material
  [3:1:0.0] Lower Palaeolithic | Handaxe, cleaver, chopper | Quartzite (Kadapa–Kurnool); limestone (north of the Krishna)
  [3:1:0.1] Middle Palaeolithic | Flakes, scrapers, borers, points | Chert, jasper, chalcedony, quartz
  [3:1:0.2] Upper Palaeolithic | Blades, bone tools | Silica-rich stone, bone
  [3:1:0.3] Mesolithic | Microliths (arrowheads, blades, composite tools) | Chert, jasper; weapons of dolomite
  [3:1:0.4] Chalcolithic | Copper tools | Copper
[3:1:1] - Microliths were made by **flaking and chipping**.
[3:1:2] Middle Palaeolithic is the **flake** stage; microliths are Mesolithic. Do not call the Middle Palaeolithic the "small stone-tool stage".

=== NOTES 1:4 | Pre-harappan and Early Harappan Cultures – Mehrgarh, Amri, Kot Diji, Kalibangan-i
## SUB 0 | Early Farming Sites
[4:0:0] TABLE head: Site | Region | Note
  [4:0:0.0] Mehrgarh | Baluchistan, near the Bolan Pass | c. 7000 BCE; first farming, domestication, pottery; earliest settled life; remains from Stone Age to Harappan
  [4:0:0.1] Kili Gul Muhammad | Quetta valley | Early farming village
  [4:0:0.2] Rana Ghundai | Zhob-Loralai valley | Farming and pastoral village
  [4:0:0.3] Damb Sadaat | Quetta valley | Early Harappan-phase village
[4:0:1] - Neolithic Baluchistan and Indus plains: pastoralism and limited cultivation → permanent villages; crops **wheat, barley, dates, cotton**; main tamed animals **sheep and goats**.
## SUB 1 | Mehrgarh – Periods and Burials
[4:1:0] TABLE head: Period | Evidence
  [4:1:0.0] I (aceramic Neolithic, c. 7000 BCE) | Barley, wheat; sheep, goat, cattle; mud-brick houses
  [4:1:0.1] II | Pottery begins; cotton
  [4:1:0.2] III onward | Copper working, wheel-made painted pottery, beads
[4:1:1] - **Burials**: under or near houses, often **flexed**, with ornaments, tools, sometimes **goats**.
[4:1:2] Period I has NO pottery; pottery and cotton begin in Period II.
## SUB 2 | Early Harappan Cultures
[4:2:0] - Early Harappan phase: **3300–2600 BCE**; called the **Regionalisation Era** (not Integration Era); corresponds to the **Hakra phase**.
[4:2:1] TABLE head: Culture | Type site / area | Feature
  [4:2:1.0] Amri | Sindh | Transition pre- to Mature Harappan; rhinoceros remains; later Jhangar
  [4:2:1.1] Kot Diji | Sindh | Fortified, destroyed by fire; many stone implements
  [4:2:1.2] Sothi-Siswal | Rajasthan–Haryana | –
  [4:2:1.3] Hakra | Ghaggar-Hakra, Cholistan (not Gujarat) | –
  [4:2:1.4] Damb Sadaat | Quetta | –
[4:2:2] - Other sites: Padri, Kalibangan, Dholavira, Harappa, Balakot, Bhirrana, Gumla. **Banawali**: pre- and Mature; **Mitathal**: pre-, Mature and Late.
[4:2:3] - Pottery: regional **Amri, Kot Diji, Sothi-Siswal, Hakra** wares; Early Harappans used the **potter's wheel**; Mature pottery became uniform.
## SUB 3 | Kalibangan-i
[4:3:0] - **Earliest ploughed field**; **criss-cross furrows** = **mixed cropping**.
[4:3:1] - **Mud-brick fortification**; pottery in **six fabrics**.
[4:3:2] - Excavated **1961–69** by **B. B. Lal** and **B. K. Thapar**.
[4:3:3] Ploughed field = pre-Harappan level; fire altars = Mature phase.

[4:5:0] - Known in the Early phase: **copper, the wheel, the plough**; granaries and defence walls.

[4:5:2] - Unsolved: the **gap between Neolithic Mehrgarh and the urban phase**.

=== NOTES 1:5 | Indus Valley Civilization – Discovery, Chronology, Geographical Extent, Boundary Sites
## SUB 0 | Ivc – Basic Facts
[5:0:0] - **Largest Bronze Age civilisation by area** (larger than Egypt and Mesopotamia); India's **first urbanisation**.
[5:0:1] - Extent: about **13 lakh sq km (1.3 million sq km)**; population about **5 million**.
[5:0:2] - Most sites lie on the **Ghaggar–Hakra** system: about **250 sites**, of which **6 were cities**.
[5:0:3] - Main source of knowledge: **seals** (more than **3,000** found) and excavations; the **script is not yet deciphered**.
[5:0:4] - First **silver** in India: Harappan culture (Mohenjodaro, Harappa).
[5:0:5] - IVC rivers: **Sindhu, Chenab, Jhelum, Ravi, Sutlej** – **not the Ganga**; spread from the Jhelum to the **Hindon** (Alamgirpur).
