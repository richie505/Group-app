## SUB 1 | Discovery and Excavators
[1:0] - **1826**: **Charles Masson** described the Harappa ruins (1826-38). **1853, 1856**: **Alexander Cunningham** saw pottery, tools and a seal but missed their value.
[1:1] - **1857**: Harappan bricks used as ballast for the **Karachi–Lahore** railway. **1912**: **J. Fleet** published Harappan seals.
[1:2] - **Sir John Marshall** (DG, ASI 1902–28) directed the 1921-22 campaign; announced the civilisation in 1924; coined "Indus Valley Civilisation".
[1:3] - The two Indian discoverers: **Daya Ram Sahni** and **Rakhaldas Banerji**.

## SUB 2 | Basic Facts – Bronze Age, Area, Rivers, Non-Aryan Character
[2:0] - **Proto-historic** (script undeciphered), **Bronze Age**; India's **first urbanisation**.
[2:1] - Area about **1.3 million sq km** – larger than Egypt and Mesopotamia; population ~5 million; matured in **Sindh and Punjab** and spread **south and east**.
[2:2] - Highest concentration of sites on the **Ghaggar–Hakra** system (about 250 sites, 6 cities).
[2:3] - **Rivers:** Sindhu, **Chenab, Jhelum**, Ravi, Sutlej, Ghaggar – up to the **Hindon** (Alamgirpur); **not the Ganga**.
[2:4] - **Main sites:** Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Ropar, Kalibangan; roads, houses and drains were planned.
[2:5] - **Metals:** copper, bronze, gold, **silver** (first use of silver in India), lead, tin – but **no iron**.
[2:6] - **Non-Aryan:** Harappan culture was **urban**; the Rigvedic Aryans were **pastoral/rural**.
[2:7] TABLE head: Period | Economy
  [2:7.0] Indus Valley | Urban
  [2:7.1] Rigvedic | Pastoral
  [2:7.2] Later Vedic | Agrarian
[2:8] - **Not Harappan:** **Sohgaura** (Gorakhpur – Mauryan copper plate), **Pataliputra** (Magadha capital), Mehrgarh (Neolithic), Burzahom (Neolithic pit dwellings).
[2:9] - Assertion–reason: Mohenjodaro and Harappa are "dead" cities **and** were found by excavation – both true, but R does not explain A.

## SUB 4 | Largest Sites, UNESCO and Recent Developments
[4:0] - **Rakhigarhi** (Hisar, Haryana; ~550 ha) = **largest Harappan site in India**; **Dholavira** second; **Mohenjodaro** largest in Pakistan. Five largest: Harappa, Mohenjodaro, Ganweriwala, Rakhigarhi, Dholavira.
[4:1] - **Dholavira:** UNESCO WHS **2021** – India's **40th**, Gujarat's 4th, the **first IVC site in India**; absence of encroachment helped.
[4:2] - **Mohenjodaro:** UNESCO WHS since **1980**. **Rakhigarhi:** on the UNESCO **Tentative List** (Jun 2026).
[4:3] - **Union Budget 2020-21:** **five** iconic archaeological sites – **Rakhigarhi, Hastinapur, Shivsagar, Dholavira, Adichanallur**.
[4:4] - Jun 2026: Haryana protected **Tighrana and Mitathal** (Bhiwani) under the **Haryana Ancient and Historical Monuments Act, 1964**.
[4:5] - 23 Jun 2026: ASI transferred Rakhigarhi's **56 skeletons** to the **Anthropological Survey of India**; a 4,500-year-old skeleton gave the ancient DNA study.
