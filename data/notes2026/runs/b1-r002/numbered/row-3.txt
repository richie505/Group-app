## SUB 0 | Palaeolithic Andhra – Billa Surgam, Kurnool Caves and Sites
[0:0] # Billa Surgam and the Kurnool caves (most asked)
[0:1] - **Billa Surgam caves:** K. K. Kottala village, **Betamcherla** mandal, **Kurnool** (now Nandyal) district – **not Anantapuramu**; limestone caves; **Old Stone Age** site.
[0:2] - Explored by **Robert Bruce Foote** (1884) – the "Father of Indian prehistory", who also found the first Palaeolithic tool at **Pallavaram** (Chennai); **stone and rare bone tools** with Pleistocene animal fossils.
[0:3] - **Kurnool caves** and **Muchatla Chintamani Gavi**: the **only Indian sites with bone tools in an Upper Palaeolithic context**.
[0:4] - **Belum Caves** (Kolimigundla, Nandyal): **second-longest** cave system in India.
[0:5] - **Betamcherla** = famous for **prehistoric caves** (not temples or a dam).
[0:6] - Distractors in the G2 2017 question: **Keesarapalli** (Chalcolithic), **Utnur** (Neolithic ash mound), **Revarala** (megalithic menhir).
[0:7] # Sites by district
[0:8] TABLE head: District | Palaeolithic sites
  [0:8.0] Kurnool | Billa Surgam, Betamcherla, Veerapuram, Sangameswaram, Jwalapuram (Upper Palaeolithic)
  [0:8.1] Prakasam | Giddaluru, Dornala, Yerragondapalem (well-made quartzite blades), Nandikanuma
  [0:8.2] Guntur / Krishna valley | Nagarjunakonda, Karampudi, Chejarla
  [0:8.3] Chittoor | Renigunta (Upper Palaeolithic blade-and-burin industry), Gudimallam
  [0:8.4] Anantapur | Guntakal (bone implements), Lattavaram, Velpulamadugu
  [0:8.5] Nellore, Kadapa | Pennar valley (Foote: 28 quartzite tools), Sagileru
[0:9] - **Kurnool** is the Palaeolithic area of AP (Bagor = Rajasthan, Sarai Nahar Rai and Belan valley = UP).
[0:10] - **Nagarjunakonda:** Lower Palaeolithic tools in river gravels studied by K. V. Soundara Rajan, which record **alternating dry and wet climatic phases** (APPSC key: three cycles); also microliths and an Iron Age–Megalithic centre.
[0:11] - Mar 2026: Upper Palaeolithic **quartz tools** and **petroglyphs** at **Manchirevula** near Hyderabad.

## SUB 1 | Megalithic (Iron Age) Andhra – Rakshasa Gullu and Burial Types
[1:0] - Andhra megaliths = **Rakshasa Gullu** ("large stones"), of the **Iron Age**, with **black-and-red ware** (absent only at **Visakhapatnam**).
[1:1] - Local names: **Pandava Gullu** (Kurnool, Kadapa), **Kurambara Gullu** (Chittoor), **Morivara Gullu** (Anantapur).
[1:2] - About **12 types** in AP (studied by **N. R. Banerji**); grave goods: pottery, beads, **iron implements**, gold ornaments – not glass mirrors; **dolmens for elites, pits for commoners**.
[1:3] - Sites: **Nagarjunakonda** (main habitation centre), Brahmagiri, Gudivada, **Jonnawada** (Nellore), Boodigapalli, Naidupalli; a coffin with a male and a female body in one grave near **Yeleswaram**.
[1:4] - Jun 2026: **Mayiladumparai** (TN) AMS dating puts the South Indian Iron Age at c. **2100 BCE** (2172 BCE), earlier than the Gangetic Iron Age.
[1:5] - H. D. Sankalia: **rice** was first harvested and eaten in Andhra in this **Iron/Megalithic** age.
[1:6] TABLE head: Type | Form | AP area
  [1:6.0] Cist | Stone-slab box | NextGen: found everywhere except Visakhapatnam (the G2 key treated "many in Visakhapatnam" as correct)
  [1:6.1] Dolmen | Table-like chamber of slabs | Krishna, Guntur, East and West Godavari; also Chittoor
  [1:6.2] Sarcophagus | Terracotta coffin | Sankhavaram (Kurnool) – ram-shaped; Nagarjunakonda; NOT a Chittoor–Anantapuramu cluster
  [1:6.3] Menhir | Upright stone | Revarala (Krishna)
  [1:6.4] Anthropomorphic | Human-shaped stone | Tirupati – India's largest collection
[1:7] > G2 PYQ: the incorrect statement (official key) = "sarcophagus burials are more in Chittoor and Anantapuramu".
[1:8] - **Warangal** region: famous for **iron implements** and steel (wootz) – the iron tradition of the Telangana–Andhra region.

## SUB 2 | Mesolithic Andhra – Giddaluru and the Microlithic Centres
[2:0] - **Giddaluru (Prakasam)** = the **microlithic implement centre** of Andhra identified by archaeologists (Nellore, Kurnool, Guntur are distractors).
[2:1] - Microliths also at **Nagarjunakonda** and **Kondapur**; tool centres discovered by **Robert Bruce Foote**.
[2:2] - Mesolithic = **microlithic**; tools by **flaking and chipping**; Andhra microliths resemble those of **South India and the Caspian** region.
[2:3] - Microlithic implements of c. 5000 BCE at **Giddaluru and Khammam**.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP HISTORY Part 1 p.3-4
## SUB 3 | Neolithic–Chalcolithic Andhra – Patapadu, Keesarapalli, Utnur
[3:0] TABLE head: Site | Location | Feature
  [3:0.0] Patapadu | Kurnool | Chalcolithic – painted pottery (Copper Age evidence of Andhra)
  [3:0.1] Keesarapalli | Krishna | Chalcolithic – copper tools
  [3:0.2] Utnur | Mahbubnagar, Telangana | Neolithic ash mound ("mounds of burning soot")
  [3:0.3] Palavoy | Anantapur | Neolithic settlement, four ash mounds
  [3:0.4] Veerapuram | Kurnool, right bank of the Krishna | Neolithic–Chalcolithic to Early Historic sequence
  [3:0.5] Ramapuram | Kurnool | Neolithic–Chalcolithic
  [3:0.6] Sanganakallu, Tekkalakota | Ballari, Karnataka | Neolithic settlements – not Andhra
  [3:0.7] Brahmagiri | Chitradurga, Karnataka | Copper objects; megalithic graves
[3:1] - First Neolithic tool in South India: **Meadows Taylor, 1842**, at **Lingasugur** (Raichur) – not Jwalapuram.
[3:2] - Neolithic weapons of AP mostly of **dolomite**; sandstone axes near **Vaddamanu** (Amaravati).

## SUB 4 | Rock Art and Caves of Andhra
[4:0] - **Borra Caves** (Ananthagiri hills, Araku, **Visakhapatnam**): karstic limestone caves; discovered **1807** by William King (GSI); Mesolithic example.
[4:1] - **Ketavaram** (Kurnool): Mesolithic rock paintings; most painted animal in AP rock art = **deer**.
[4:2] - Neolithic paintings: **Chintagunta** (man with bow), **Velpulamadugu** (humped bulls facing each other), **Thenagala** (dog and deer in red) – Anantapur.
[4:3] - **Petroglyphs** are **carved**, not painted.
[4:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP HISTORY Part 1 p.4-5
