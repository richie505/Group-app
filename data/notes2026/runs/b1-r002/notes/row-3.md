=== Palaeolithic Andhra – Billa Surgam, Kurnool Caves and Sites
# Billa Surgam and the Kurnool caves (most asked)
- **Billa Surgam caves:** K. K. Kottala village, **Betamcherla** mandal, **Kurnool** (now Nandyal) district – **not Anantapuramu**; limestone caves; **Old Stone Age** site.
- Explored by **Robert Bruce Foote** (1884) – the "Father of Indian prehistory", who also found the first Palaeolithic tool at **Pallavaram** (Chennai); **stone and rare bone tools** with Pleistocene animal fossils.
- **Kurnool caves** and **Muchatla Chintamani Gavi**: the **only Indian sites with bone tools in an Upper Palaeolithic context**.
- **Belum Caves** (Kolimigundla, Nandyal): **second-longest** cave system in India.
- **Betamcherla** = famous for **prehistoric caves** (not temples or a dam).
- Distractors in the G2 2017 question: **Keesarapalli** (Chalcolithic), **Utnur** (Neolithic ash mound), **Revarala** (megalithic menhir).
# Sites by district
| District | Palaeolithic sites |
|---|---|
| Kurnool | Billa Surgam, Betamcherla, Veerapuram, Sangameswaram, Jwalapuram (Upper Palaeolithic) |
| Prakasam | **Giddaluru**, Dornala, **Yerragondapalem** (well-made quartzite blades), Nandikanuma |
| Guntur / Krishna valley | **Nagarjunakonda**, Karampudi, Chejarla |
| Chittoor | **Renigunta** (Upper Palaeolithic blade-and-burin industry), Gudimallam |
| Anantapur | Guntakal (bone implements), Lattavaram, Velpulamadugu |
| Nellore, Kadapa | Pennar valley (Foote: 28 quartzite tools), Sagileru |
- **Kurnool** is the Palaeolithic area of AP (Bagor = Rajasthan, Sarai Nahar Rai and Belan valley = UP).
- **Nagarjunakonda:** Lower Palaeolithic tools in river gravels studied by K. V. Soundara Rajan, which record **alternating dry and wet climatic phases** (APPSC key: three cycles); also microliths and an Iron Age–Megalithic centre.
- Mar 2026: Upper Palaeolithic **quartz tools** and **petroglyphs** at **Manchirevula** near Hyderabad.
# Tools by stage and raw material
| Stage | Tools | Raw material |
|---|---|---|
| Lower Palaeolithic | Handaxe, cleaver, chopper | **Quartzite** (Kadapa–Kurnool); limestone north of the Krishna |
| Middle Palaeolithic | Flakes, scrapers, borers, points | **Chert, jasper**, chalcedony |
| Upper Palaeolithic | Blades, burins, bone tools | Silica stone, bone |
| Mesolithic | Microliths | Chert, jasper; weapons of **dolomite** |

=== Megalithic (Iron Age) Andhra – Rakshasa Gullu and Burial Types
- Andhra megaliths = **Rakshasa Gullu** ("large stones"), of the **Iron Age**, with **black-and-red ware** (absent only at **Visakhapatnam**).
- Local names: **Pandava Gullu** (Kurnool, Kadapa), **Kurambara Gullu** (Chittoor), **Morivara Gullu** (Anantapur).
- About **12 types** in AP (studied by **N. R. Banerji**); grave goods: pottery, beads, **iron implements**, gold ornaments – not glass mirrors; **dolmens for elites, pits for commoners**.
- Sites: **Nagarjunakonda** (main habitation centre), Brahmagiri, Gudivada, **Jonnawada** (Nellore), Boodigapalli, Naidupalli; a coffin with a male and a female body in one grave near **Yeleswaram**.
- Jun 2026: **Mayiladumparai** (TN) AMS dating puts the South Indian Iron Age at c. **2100 BCE** (2172 BCE), earlier than the Gangetic Iron Age.
- H. D. Sankalia: **rice** was first harvested and eaten in Andhra in this **Iron/Megalithic** age.
| Type | Form | AP area |
|---|---|---|
| Cist | Stone-slab box | NextGen: found everywhere except Visakhapatnam (the G2 key treated "many in Visakhapatnam" as correct) |
| Dolmen | Table-like chamber of slabs | Krishna, Guntur, East and West Godavari; also Chittoor |
| Sarcophagus | Terracotta coffin | **Sankhavaram** (Kurnool) – ram-shaped; Nagarjunakonda; NOT a Chittoor–Anantapuramu cluster |
| Menhir | Upright stone | **Revarala** (Krishna) |
| Anthropomorphic | Human-shaped stone | **Tirupati** – India's largest collection |
> G2 PYQ: the incorrect statement (official key) = "sarcophagus burials are more in Chittoor and Anantapuramu".
- **Warangal** region: famous for **iron implements** and steel (wootz) – the iron tradition of the Telangana–Andhra region.

=== Mesolithic Andhra – Giddaluru and the Microlithic Centres
- **Giddaluru (Prakasam)** = the **microlithic implement centre** of Andhra identified by archaeologists (Nellore, Kurnool, Guntur are distractors).
- Microliths also at **Nagarjunakonda** and **Kondapur**; tool centres discovered by **Robert Bruce Foote**.
- Mesolithic = **microlithic**; tools by **flaking and chipping**; Andhra microliths resemble those of **South India and the Caspian** region.
- Microlithic implements of c. 5000 BCE at **Giddaluru and Khammam**.
~~ Sources: NextGen AP HISTORY Part 1 p.3-4

=== Neolithic–Chalcolithic Andhra – Patapadu, Keesarapalli, Utnur
| Site | Location | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Patapadu** | Kurnool | **Chalcolithic – painted pottery** (Copper Age evidence of Andhra) |
| **Keesarapalli** | Krishna | **Chalcolithic – copper tools** |
| Utnur | Mahbubnagar, Telangana | Neolithic ash mound ("mounds of burning soot") |
| Palavoy | Anantapur | Neolithic settlement, four ash mounds |
| Veerapuram | Kurnool, right bank of the Krishna | Neolithic–Chalcolithic to Early Historic sequence |
| Ramapuram | Kurnool | Neolithic–Chalcolithic |
| Sanganakallu, Tekkalakota | Ballari, Karnataka | Neolithic settlements – not Andhra |
| Brahmagiri | Chitradurga, Karnataka | Copper objects; megalithic graves |
- First Neolithic tool in South India: **Meadows Taylor, 1842**, at **Lingasugur** (Raichur) – not Jwalapuram.
- Neolithic weapons of AP mostly of **dolomite**; sandstone axes near **Vaddamanu** (Amaravati).

=== Rock Art and Caves of Andhra
- **Borra Caves** (Ananthagiri hills, Araku, **Visakhapatnam**): karstic limestone caves; discovered **1807** by William King (GSI); Mesolithic example.
- **Ketavaram** (Kurnool): Mesolithic rock paintings; most painted animal in AP rock art = **deer**.
- Neolithic paintings: **Chintagunta** (man with bow), **Velpulamadugu** (humped bulls facing each other), **Thenagala** (dog and deer in red) – Anantapur.
- **Petroglyphs** are **carved**, not painted.
