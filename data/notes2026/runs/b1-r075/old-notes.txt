=== NOTES 1:75 | Badami Chalukya Art – Aihole, Badami, Pattadakal (unesco); Origin of the Vesara Style
## SUB 0 | Three Phases of Chalukya Art
[75:0:0] - Order: **Badami** (rock-cut) → **Aihole** (experimental structural) → **Pattadakal** (mature) = birth of **Vesara**.
[75:0:1] - **Percy Brown**: Chalukyas first broke the monopoly of the **rock-cut tradition** in the Deccan.
[75:0:2] - Freestanding (mortar and stone) temples are said to begin in the **Gupta** period.
## SUB 1 | Temple Styles – Nagara, Dravida, Vesara
[75:1:0] TABLE head: Style | Region | Defining feature
  [75:1:0.0] Nagara | North India (north of the Vindhyas) | Curvilinear shikhara
  [75:1:0.1] Dravida | South India (Krishna–Kaveri) | Pyramidal vimana, gopurams
  [75:1:0.2] Vesara | Deccan (Vindhyas–Krishna) | Hybrid of Nagara + Dravida; Dravida plan with Nagara details; Chalukya style
[75:1:1] - Nagara, Dravida, Vesara = the **three main styles** of Indian temple architecture.
[75:1:2] - Kanchi = Pallavas; Tanjore = Cholas; Pattadakal = Chalukyas.
[75:1:3] - **Khajuraho**: all compartments connected internally and externally.
## SUB 2 | Badami Cave Temples
[75:2:0] - **Four** main sandstone caves around **Agastya lake** (6th–7th c.); earliest under **Mangalesha**.
[75:2:1] TABLE head: Cave | Deity
  [75:2:1.0] 1 | Shiva – 18-armed Nataraja
  [75:2:1.1] 2 | Vishnu (Varaha, Trivikrama)
  [75:2:1.2] 3 | Vishnu; largest; Mangalesha's inscription, 578 CE
  [75:2:1.3] 4 | Jain
## SUB 3 | Aihole
[75:3:0] - Epithet: "**Cradle of Indian temple architecture**" (**K.A. Nilakanta Sastri**) – basis: experimental, early structural phase.
[75:3:1] - **Lad Khan**: one of the **earliest** temples (RPSC 2018: early Chalukya temple); **earliest massive bracket capital**; rectangular, **flat slab roof**, stone grilles, pillared porch, **no shikhara**; Shiva; 12 pillars.
[75:3:2] - **Durga temple**: **apsidal (gajaprishtha)** plan + pillared gallery round the shrine.

[75:4:2] - Nagara shikhara pair: **Huchimalligudi (Aihole)** and **Dashavatara (Deogarh)**.
## SUB 5 | Pattadakal
[75:5:0] - **UNESCO World Heritage Site (1987)**; royal **coronation** site.
[75:5:1] - Mature phase: **Nagara and Dravida side by side**.
[75:5:2] - **Nandi mandapas**; friezes (**Ramayana, Mahabharata, Panchatantra**); polished pillars.
[75:5:3] - Patron queens: **Lokamahadevi** (Virupaksha, for Vikramaditya II's victory over the Pallavas) and **Trailokyamahadevi** (Mallikarjuna).
[75:5:4] - Line: Pallava **Kailasanatha** (Kanchi) → **Virupaksha** → Rashtrakuta **Kailasa** (Ellora).
## SUB 6 | Architects and Inscriptions

[75:7:1] - District: **Jogulamba Gadwal, Telangana** (earlier Mahabubnagar).
[75:7:2] - Alampur = **Dakshina Kashi**; **Jogulamba Shakti Peetha**; **western gateway of Srisailam**.

[75:7:4] - Oldest Saivite temple: **Parasurameswara, Gudimallam**; temple building in Andhra began under the **Ikshvakus**.
[75:7:5] - **Arasavalli** = Surya temple; Hampi much later.

=== NOTES 1:78 | Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi – Kubja Vishnuvardhana, Dynastic History, Patronage of Telugu (nannaya)
## SUB 0 | Origin and Identity of the Dynasty
[78:0:0] - Also called the **Chalukyas of Vengi**, Purva Chalukyas or Vengi Chalukyas; a **branch of the Badami Chalukyas** (Badami – Karnataka; Vengi – Andhra Pradesh).
[78:0:1] - **Founder: Kubja Vishnuvardhana**, **brother of Pulakeshin II** of Badami.
[78:0:2] - **Pulakeshin II** took the **Krishna-Godavari** region (Vengi) after defeating the remnants of the **Vishnukundins** (c. 616 CE).
[78:0:3] - Kubja was made **viceroy in 617 CE** and declared **independence in 624 CE**.
[78:0:4] - Rule: **624–1075 CE** (about 4.5 centuries); **1076–c.1210** = **Chalukya-Chola phase**. Some count the end as 1189 CE by including that phase.
[78:0:5] - Core territory: **Venginadu = Krishna-Godavari doab**; capitals Pithapuram, Pedavegi, Rajahmundry.
[78:0:6] - **Greatest king: Gunaga Vijayaditya III** (Tripura Martya Maheshwara); **last king: Vijayaditya VII** (1070–1075; died 1075 = end of the line).
[78:0:7] - An important power in Andhra–Deccan politics, with repeated wars and marriages with the **Cholas**; after **973 CE** the main rival was the **Kalyani Chalukyas** (Satyasraya, Someshvara I).
[78:0:8] - **Matwalin**: a traveller linked with an account of China–India relations during Chalukya rule.
[78:0:9] # Identity Markers

  [78:0:10.0] Royal emblem | Boar (Varaha)
  [78:0:10.1] Official language | Sanskrit – most of about 100 donation inscriptions
  [78:0:10.2] Matronymic | "Hariti-putra" (also used by Satavahanas, Ikshvakus, Kadambas) – read as a sign of southern origin
  [78:0:10.3] Seal sign manual | "Tribhuvanankusa" on copper-plate seals
  [78:0:10.4] Mother goddess | Hariti – the Chalukyas called themselves Haritiputras (APPSC G2 2019)
  [78:0:10.5] Main faith | Shaivism, tolerant of Jainism and Buddhism

## SUB 1 | Founder Kubja Vishnuvardhana
[78:1:0] - Title **Vishamasiddhi** ("conqueror of the forest" / difficult places).
[78:1:1] - **Other titles:** **Sarvasiddhi** and **Makaradhwaja** – also **Kubja Vishnuvardhana**.
[78:1:2] - **First capital: Pithapuram (Pishtapura)**; built the **Kunti Madhava temple, Pithapuram**.
[78:1:3] - **Kopparam copper plates (631 CE)** of Pulakeshin II call Kubja **"Yuvaraja"**; Kopparam (and **Marturu**) inscriptions record the conquest of the Krishna-Godavari region and call **Mangalagiri "Mangalapura"** (G2 2019 key).
[78:1:4] - **Chipurupalli (Cheepurupalli) plates**, Vizianagaram: grant of Kubja in his **18th regnal year** (632 CE), name him **Vishamasiddhi** and the scholar **Madhavasharma**.
