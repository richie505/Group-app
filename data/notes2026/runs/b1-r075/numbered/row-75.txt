## SUB 0 | Alampur Navabrahma Temples – the Andhra Angle of Badami Chalukya Art
[0:0] # Navabrahma temples – the most-asked fact of this row
[0:1] - **Where:** the **Navabrahma** temple complex is at **Alampur (Alampuram)** (G1 1983-84, 1987-88; G2 2008; APPSC PYQ ×3) – not Draksharama, Srisailam, Tirupati, Siddhavatam, Pushpagiri, Mahanandi, Badami or Aihole.
[0:2] - **Builders:** the **Badami Chalukyas** (7th–8th c.); Alampur is "the place in Andhra containing fine Badami Chalukyan temples" (G1 1983-84) – not Tadipatri, Ghantasala or Billasurgam.
[0:3] - **Number:** **nine** early Chalukya **Shiva** temples, on the **Tungabhadra** (near its confluence with the Krishna).
[0:4] - **Style:** **Nagara** – a northern **rekha-prasada (curvilinear) shikhara** (APPSC JA, NTR Health University) – not Vesara, Hoysala or "Ikshvaku".
[0:5]   - **⚠ Settled:** an older APPSC key gave "Vesara"; the Alampur group is the classic case of **northern (Nagara) towers built by a southern dynasty**.
[0:6] - **District:** **Jogulamba Gadwal, Telangana** (earlier Mahabubnagar).
[0:7]   - ⚠ Since **2014** Alampur is in **Telangana**, not Andhra Pradesh (the 1983-84 stem says "Andhra Pradesh" – undivided state).
[0:8] - **Other names:** **Dakshina Kashi**; seat of the **Jogulamba Shakti Peetha**; the **western gateway of Srisailam**.
[0:9] # Other Andhra temple facts linked here
[0:10] - **Eastern Chalukyas** carried the Chalukya (Vesara) idiom to **Bikkavolu** and **Draksharama**; NextGen: Vesara "belongs to the Chalukyas of Badami, introduced in Andhra by the Vengi Chalukyas".
[0:11] - **Oldest Saivite temple:** **Parasurameswara, Gudimallam**; temple building in Andhra began under the **Ikshvakus**.
[0:12] > Exam angle: "Where are the Navabrahma temples?" has been asked at least seven times – answer **Alampur**, built by the **Badami Chalukyas**, style **Nagara**.

## SUB 1 | Temple Styles – Nagara, Dravida and Vesara
[1:0] # The three styles
[1:1] TABLE head: Style | Region | Defining feature
  [1:1.0] Nagara | North India (north of the Vindhyas) | Curvilinear shikhara
  [1:1.1] Dravida | South India (Krishna–Kaveri) | Pyramidal vimana, gopurams
  [1:1.2] Vesara | Deccan (Vindhyas–Krishna) | Hybrid of Nagara + Dravida; Dravida plan with Nagara details; the Chalukya style
[1:2] - **Nagara, Dravida, Vesara = the three main styles of Indian temple architecture** (UPSC 1995, 2012) – not racial groups, linguistic divisions or musical gharanas.
[1:3] - **Vesara origin:** developed by the **Badami Chalukyas** (Aihole, Pattadakal); continued by the Rashtrakutas and later Chalukyas.
[1:4] - **Place → dynasty:** Badami/Vatapi = Chalukyas; Kanchi = Pallavas; Tanjore = Cholas.
[1:5] # Statement facts (State PSC)
[1:6] - **Freestanding (mortar and stone) temples** began in the **Gupta** period ✓.
[1:7] - **Lad Khan (Aihole)** is one of the **earliest** temples, of the Chalukyas of Badami ✓.
[1:8] - **Khajuraho:** all compartments of the temple are connected internally and externally ✓.
[1:9] - **✗ Kailasanatha, Kanchi is NOT the earliest** freestanding Dravida temple – the **Shore Temple** (same Rajasimha phase) is counted among the earliest; so the correct set is **(i), (ii), (iii)**.
[1:10] # Mixed style
[1:11] - **Teli ka Mandir, Gwalior:** its **pinnacle (barrel-vaulted roof) is Dravida-like**, while the ornament is Nagara – the "Dravida pinnacle" answer (State PSC); Bhitargaon, Kandariya Mahadeva and Osian are Nagara.

## SUB 2 | Badami Caves and the Phases of Chalukya Art
[2:0] # Three phases
[2:1] - **Order:** **Badami** (rock-cut caves) → **Aihole** (experimental structural) → **Pattadakal** (mature) = birth of **Vesara**.
[2:2] - **Rocket sheet:** "**Badami = rock-cut caves**; Aihole = experimental/early temple architecture; Pattadakal = synthesis of Nagara + Dravida".
[2:3] - **Percy Brown:** the Chalukyas first **broke the monopoly of the rock-cut tradition** in the Deccan (by building structural temples).
[2:4] # Badami cave temples
[2:5] - **Badami is famous for its rock-cut cave temples** (G1 1990-91) – the caves were built under the **Chalukyas** (SI Mains 2019), not Rashtrakutas, Pallavas or Cholas.
[2:6]   - **⚠ Settled:** the printed key "structural temples" is wrong for Badami – structural temples are the hallmark of **Aihole and Pattadakal**; Badami's fame rests on its four caves.
[2:7] - **Four** main sandstone caves around **Agastya lake** (6th c.); earliest work under **Mangalesha**.
[2:8] TABLE head: Cave | Deity / fact
  [2:8.0] 1 | Shiva – 18-armed Nataraja
  [2:8.1] 2 | Vishnu (Varaha, Trivikrama)
  [2:8.2] 3 | Vishnu; largest; Mangalesha's inscription, 578 CE
  [2:8.3] 4 | Jain
[2:9] - Badami also has the structural **Malegitti Shivalaya** (Dravida).

## SUB 3 | Aihole and Pattadakal (UNESCO)
[3:0] # Aihole
[3:1] - **"Cradle of Indian temple architecture"** (**K.A. Nilakanta Sastri**) – experimental, early structural phase.
[3:2] - **Lad Khan:** **earliest massive bracket capital**; rectangular, **flat slab roof**, stone grilles, **no shikhara**; Shiva.
[3:3] - **Durga temple:** **apsidal (gajaprishtha)** plan with a pillared gallery round the shrine.
[3:4] - **Meguti** (Jain): Dravida; bears the **Aihole inscription** composed by **Ravikirti** (poet and composer, not architect).
[3:5] TABLE head: Temple | Site | Style
  [3:5.0] Lad Khan | Aihole | No shikhara
  [3:5.1] Huchimalligudi | Aihole | Nagara
  [3:5.2] Ravana Phadi | Aihole | Rock-cut cave, no tower
  [3:5.3] Virupaksha, Mallikarjuna, Sangameshvara | Pattadakal | Dravida
  [3:5.4] Papanatha, Kashi Vishwanatha, Galaganatha | Pattadakal | Nagara
[3:6] - **Nagara shikhara pair:** **Huchimalligudi (Aihole)** and **Dashavatara (Deogarh)**.
[3:7] # Pattadakal
[3:8] - **UNESCO World Heritage Site (1987)**; royal **coronation** site; **Nagara and Dravida temples side by side**.
[3:9] - **Virupaksha:** built by queen **Lokamahadevi** for **Vikramaditya II's victory over the Pallavas**; modelled on the Pallava **Kailasanatha (Kanchi)**; Mallikarjuna by **Trailokyamahadevi**.
[3:10] - Friezes from the **Ramayana, Mahabharata and Panchatantra**; architects (sutradharis) **Gunda Anivaritachari** and **Sarvasiddhi Acharya** named in Virupaksha inscriptions.
[3:11] - **Line of influence:** Kailasanatha (Kanchi) → Virupaksha (Pattadakal) → Kailasa (Ellora).
[3:12] # UNESCO link
[3:13] - **UNESCO World Heritage City:** **Jaipur** (Walled City, **2019**) – after **Ahmedabad** (2017), India's first heritage city (APPSC FSO).
