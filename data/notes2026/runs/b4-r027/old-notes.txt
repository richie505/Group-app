=== NOTES 4:27 | Hydrological Disasters – Floods, Flash Floods, Cloudbursts, Droughts, Tsunamis, Storm Surges
## SUB 0 | Disasters – Meaning and Classification
[27:0:0] - **Disaster = Hazard × Vulnerability.**
[27:0:1] - Natural disasters cause most damage in **developing countries**; India is **not** disaster-free.
[27:0:2] # Classification
[27:0:3] TABLE head: Class | Examples
  [27:0:3.0] Hydrological / hydro-meteorological | Floods, flash floods, cloudbursts, storm surges
  [27:0:3.1] Climatological | Droughts, heatwaves, cold waves
  [27:0:3.2] Geological | Earthquakes, tsunamis, landslides (avalanches = **hydro-geological**)
  [27:0:3.3] Man-made (chemical/industrial, nuclear) | Bhopal gas tragedy, Chernobyl
[27:0:4] # Onset
[27:0:5] TABLE head: Rapid-onset | Slow-onset
  [27:0:5.0] Cyclone, flood, tsunami, flash flood, cloudburst | Drought – a "creeping emergency / creeping hazard"
[27:0:6] # Secondary Hazards
[27:0:7] TABLE head: Primary | Secondary hazard
  [27:0:7.0] Cloudburst | Flash flood
  [27:0:7.1] Flash flood | Landslide
  [27:0:7.2] River flood | Bank erosion
  [27:0:7.3] Flood | Water contamination, disease, crop loss
  [27:0:7.4] Coastal flood | Salinity intrusion
  [27:0:7.5] Dam failure | Downstream flash flooding
  [27:0:7.6] Urban flood | Infrastructure collapse
## SUB 1 | India's Vulnerability Profile
[27:1:0] TABLE head: Hazard | Exposure | Region
  [27:1:0.0] Earthquake | ~58.6% of landmass (moderate to very high) | Himalaya, Kutch
  [27:1:0.1] Flood | >40 million ha (~12%); Assam alone 31.05 lakh ha | Brahmaputra-Ganga plains
  [27:1:0.2] Drought | ~68% of cultivable area | Rajasthan, Deccan interior
  [27:1:0.3] Cyclone/tsunami | ~5,700 km of the ~7,500 km (old 7,516.6 km) coastline | East coast
  [27:1:0.4] Landslide | ~12.6% of land (~0.42 million sq km) | Himalaya, Western Ghats (not Thar)
[27:1:1] - **27 of 36** States/UTs are disaster-prone.
[27:1:2] - Cyclones affect **13 coastal States/UTs** and **84 coastal districts**.
[27:1:3] - **World Risk Index 2025**: India **2nd** most vulnerable, after the **Philippines**. (Global Climate Risk Index 2019: India 14th.)
[27:1:4] - India loses about **0.4% of GDP** a year to disasters (1990–2024), mainly floods and storms; China and Indonesia face higher seismic risk.
[27:1:5] - **2025**: ~331 days of climate impacts; **over 4,000 deaths**; heaviest in **Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand**.
[27:1:6] # Displacement Data (IDMC)
[27:1:7] - **IDMC** (Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre, **Geneva**) publishes the **Global Report on Internal Displacement (GRID)**.
[27:1:8] - IDMC (2020 to May 2021): India **seventh** worst climate-disaster-hit country.
[27:1:9] - **GRID 2025**: India had **5.4 million** disaster displacements in **2024** (highest in 12 years; floods about two-thirds); over **2015–2024** India recorded **32.3 million**, **3rd** after China and the Philippines.
## SUB 2 | Floods – Nature, Extent and Types
[27:2:0] # Nature and Extent
[27:2:1] - Overflow of water onto normally dry land when a channel cannot contain it; **rapid-onset**; the **most frequent natural disaster** in India; can be **mitigated through early warning**.
[27:2:2] - Of India's **329 mha**, more than **40 mha** is flood-prone (**Rashtriya Barh Ayog**); a recent estimate is about **49 mha**. About 10% of India is flood-affected.
[27:2:3] - **Most flood-prone state: Bihar**; worst-hit **Assam and Bihar**, then **UP and West Bengal**. Lower Assam is flood-prone (not drought-prone).
[27:2:4] - **Flood control is a State subject**; the Centre helps through **FMBAP**.
[27:2:5] # Types of Flood
[27:2:6] TABLE head: Type | Cause | Where
  [27:2:6.0] Riverine | Discharge exceeds channel capacity (monsoon) | Ganga, Brahmaputra, Kosi, Godavari; crop damage, bank erosion
  [27:2:6.1] Flash | Sudden intense rain, cloudbursts | Himalaya, steep catchments, cities
  [27:2:6.2] Urban | Rain + poor drainage, wetland encroachment | Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Delhi
  [27:2:6.3] Coastal | Storm surges, cyclones, high tides | East and west coasts; inundation, salinity
  [27:2:6.4] GLOF | Glacier retreat swells a proglacial lake, which bursts | Uttarakhand, Sikkim
  [27:2:6.5] Dam failure | Breach | Catastrophic downstream flooding
## SUB 3 | Floods – Causes, Management and Committees
[27:3:0] # Causes
[27:3:1] TABLE head: Cause | Effect
  [27:3:1.0] Heavy / prolonged rainfall | Discharge rises fast
  [27:3:1.1] Cloudburst | Very intense rain over a small area
  [27:3:1.2] Snowmelt | Sudden rise in river flow
  [27:3:1.3] Siltation (from soil erosion) | Reduces channel depth and capacity
  [27:3:1.4] Embankment failure | Water escapes the channel
  [27:3:1.5] Deforestation | Increases runoff
  [27:3:1.6] Floodplain encroachment | Raises human exposure
  [27:3:1.7] Poor drainage | Water accumulates
  [27:3:1.8] Dam failure | Sudden downstream discharge
[27:3:2] APPSC 2025: Flood causes = **heavy rainfall, poor drainage, deforestation, dam failure**.
[27:3:3] - **Dam failure** causes: weak foundation, design/material failure; extreme flood, landslides, earthquakes; **overtopping** (water spills over the crest); sedimentation reduces storage.
[27:3:4] - Prolonged rain can cause landslides that block rivers: **Pareechu river (Tibet)**, August 2004 landslide lake – flood threat on the **Sutlej** in Himachal Pradesh.
[27:3:5] # Measures
[27:3:6] TABLE head: Structural | Non-structural
  [27:3:6.0] Dams and reservoirs, channel and drainage improvement, diversion | Flood insurance, flood-plain zoning, flood-proofing, flood fighting
[27:3:7] - **Nature-based buffers**: wetlands store water (restoring them reduces urban floods); floodplains store water; **forests increase infiltration and interception**; mangroves buffer coasts; riparian plants hold banks.
[27:3:8] - **Odisha Flood Hazard Atlas**: built from satellite images of floods **2000–2018**.
[27:3:9] # Committees and Cooperation
[27:3:10] TABLE head: Body | Year | Role
  [27:3:10.0] Rashtriya Barh Ayog (National Flood Commission) | Set up 1976; report 1980 | Assessed ~40 mha flood-prone
  [27:3:10.1] Expert Committee for Scientific Assessment of Flood-Prone Areas | 2012 | Set up by Ministry of Water Resources
  [27:3:10.2] India-China Expert Level Mechanism on flood data | Apr 2025 | 16th meeting
## SUB 4 | Flash Floods, Cloudbursts and Glofs
[27:4:0] # Cloudburst
[27:4:1] - **IMD definition**: at least **100 mm (10 cm) in one hour** over about **20–30 sq km**.
[27:4:2] - Sudden, highly localised; mostly at **1,000–2,500 m**; causes **flash floods and landslides**; very hard to forecast (Doppler radar, nowcasting).
[27:4:3] TABLE head: Basis | Cloudburst | Ordinary heavy rain
  [27:4:3.0] Intensity | Extreme | Varying
  [27:4:3.1] Timing | Sudden | Can be prolonged
  [27:4:3.2] Area | Highly localised | Wider
[27:4:4] - **Flash floods**: very rapid onset, short warning time; main danger is debris; also landslides, erosion. **IMD** gives flash-flood guidance for India + **Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal**.
[27:4:5] - **HKH "monsoon paradox"**: uneven seasonal rain plus more short, intense cloudbursts.
[27:4:6] # Vulnerable Areas
[27:4:7] TABLE head: Region | Areas | Why
  [27:4:7.0] Himalaya | Uttarakhand, HP, J&K, Ladakh, Sikkim | Steep slopes, orographic effect
  [27:4:7.1] Western Ghats | Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra | Intense rain on the windward side
  [27:4:7.2] AP Eastern Ghats agency areas | Narrow valleys | Cloudburst flash floods
[27:4:8] # Events
[27:4:9] TABLE head: Event | Year | Note
  [27:4:9.0] Leh cloudburst | 2010 | Ladakh
  [27:4:9.1] Kedarnath | June 2013 | Cloudburst + GLOF; Chaurabari Glacier (north of the temple), lake Gandhi Sarovar
  [27:4:9.2] Chamoli glacier burst | 2021 | Uttarakhand
  [27:4:9.3] HP flash floods | 2023 | Unregulated slope construction
  [27:4:9.4] Mandi (HP) | 2025 | Nearly 20 times normal rain
  [27:4:9.5] Dharali-Harsil (Uttarkashi) | 5 Aug 2025 | Ice-patch collapse on the Srikanta glacier (ISRO); diverted the Bhagirathi
  [27:4:9.6] Nepal GLOF floods | 26 Aug 2026 | 26 GLOFs since the 1970s (UNDP)
[27:4:10] - Central Himalaya glacier area down **>20%** in three decades.
[27:4:11] - **Dharali-Harsil (5 Aug 2025):** a **flash flood** hit Dharali-Harsil in Uttarkashi after an ice patch collapsed on the Srikanta glacier.
## SUB 5 | Urban Flooding and Major Floods
[27:5:0] # Causes of Urban Flooding
[27:5:1] - Intense rain, cloudbursts, cyclone rain; **encroached drains and lakes**, **wetland destruction**, impervious surfaces, poor storm drains; high river discharge, **high tides**; unplanned growth, low ground, floodplain construction.
[27:5:2] # Major Floods
[27:5:3] TABLE head: Flood | Year | Cause
  [27:5:3.0] Mumbai | 26 July 2005 | 944 mm in 24 h; drainage failure
  [27:5:3.1] Bihar (Kosi) | 2008 | Embankment breach
  [27:5:3.2] Chennai | Dec 2015 | Extreme rain; wetland destruction
  [27:5:3.3] Kerala | Aug 2018 | Heavy rain; dam releases
[27:5:4] - **AP**: **Vijayawada – Budameru rivulet**; also Visakhapatnam, Guntur. **Krishna-Godavari deltas** flood worst when Srisailam and Nagarjuna Sagar release water with heavy Godavari inflows.
[27:5:5] - **AP City Flood Alert (July 2026)**: AI platform by **RTGS + APSDMA + IIT Gandhinagar**; 48-hour rain forecasts, 6-hour flash-flood warnings; satellite DEM + ~30 years of Budameru data; first in **Vijayawada**, later all ULBs.
[27:5:6] - AP: significant crop losses from floods (true); the claim that PMFBY farmer coverage in AP has been increasing is **false** (PYQ key).
## SUB 6 | Droughts
[27:6:0] # Nature and Types
[27:6:1] - Prolonged shortage of rain or moisture → water scarcity; **slow-onset**; hits agriculture, drinking water, industry.
[27:6:2] TABLE head: Type | Indicator | Time lag
  [27:6:2.0] Meteorological | Rain ≥ 25% below LPA | Immediate
  [27:6:2.1] Agricultural | Soil-moisture deficit → crop failure | Intermediate
  [27:6:2.2] Hydrological | Fall in reservoirs, rivers, aquifers | Weeks to months
  [27:6:2.3] Socio-economic | Demand exceeds supply; water/food shortage hits livelihoods and markets | Long-term
  [27:6:2.4] Ecological | Ecosystem productivity collapses | —
[27:6:3] - **IMD**: deficiency **26–50%** of LPA = **moderate**; **over 50%** = **severe** drought.
[27:6:4] # Triggers and Consequences
[27:6:5] - Triggers: delayed/erratic monsoon, monsoon failure and breaks, high heat and evaporation; **El Nino** (6 of 10 El Nino years depressed Indian rain). Normal SW-monsoon rain: **868.6 mm** (1971–2020).
[27:6:6] - Consequences: **groundwater depletion**, land degradation, **desertification**, **distress migration**, rural indebtedness, lower farm output, ecosystem damage.
[27:6:7] # Drought-prone Regions
[27:6:8] - **Western Rajasthan, Kutch-Saurashtra, Marathwada-Vidarbha, interior Karnataka, Rayalaseema (AP)**.
[27:6:9] - **Rayalaseema**: monsoon failure, crop loss, distress migration; **deep borewells** worsen groundwater loss.
## SUB 7 | Tsunamis
[27:7:0] # Nature and Causes
[27:7:1] - Japanese **"tsu" = harbour + "nami" = wave**; a natural disaster; **not a "tidal wave"** (tides come from Moon and Sun gravity).
[27:7:2] - Series of waves from **sudden vertical displacement of the ocean floor** – mainly **undersea earthquakes at subduction zones**; also submarine landslides, volcanic eruptions, explosions, asteroid/meteorite impacts.
[27:7:3] - Can occur at any time of day or night. Warning signs: **sudden receding of the sea**, rapid sea rise, quake shaking.
[27:7:4] # Behaviour
[27:7:5] TABLE head: Deep ocean | Near the coast
  [27:7:5.0] Very long wavelength; height < 1 m; speed ~700–800 km/h; barely noticed | Slows down; height rises sharply (shoaling); massive inundation
[27:7:6] - Impact is less over the ocean, more near the coast – **speed depends on water depth**.
[27:7:7] # 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami
[27:7:8] - **26 Dec 2004**; **Sumatra-Andaman earthquake, Mw ~9.1**; origin **Indian Ocean** (APPSC 2025); >12,000 deaths in India.
[27:7:9] - Hit **Tamil Nadu, AP, Puducherry, Kerala, A&N**; **worst-hit state Tamil Nadu**, worst district **Nagapattinam** (APPSC 2025).
[27:7:10] - **Mangroves** acted as a safety hedge – prop roots and pneumatophores resist uprooting.
[27:7:11] # Risk Zones
[27:7:12] TABLE head: Risk | States/UTs
  [27:7:12.0] Very high | Andaman & Nicobar (near the Sunda subduction zone)
  [27:7:12.1] High | Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, Odisha
  [27:7:12.2] Moderate | Kerala, Karnataka, Goa, Maharashtra, Gujarat
[27:7:13] - Sources: **Sunda-Andaman** subduction zone (east); **Makran zone** threatens the **west coast**.
[27:7:14] - **Tsunami warning system** (MoES/INCOIS) warns **25 Indian Ocean countries within 10 minutes**; **NIOT Nellore's OMNI buoys** feed it.
## SUB 8 | Storm Surges and Coastal Hazards
[27:8:0] - **Storm surge**: abnormal sea rise pushed by **cyclone winds + low pressure**; a high tide raises the baseline; **sea-level rise** worsens surge flooding and inundates low deltas; brings **salinity intrusion**.
[27:8:1] - **East coast** gets far more cyclones than the west. **AP** is the most cyclone-prone state; **coastal Odisha** is the most cyclone-prone area in one PYQ key (mangrove loss in the **Mahanadi delta**).
[27:8:2] - **Coastal defence**: **mangroves**, shelter belts, dune stabilisation, seawalls; mangroves reduce cyclone impact.
[27:8:3] # Coastline Remeasured (2023–24)
[27:8:4] - By the **National Hydrographic Office** with **Survey of India**; reported by MHA, **Dec 2024**: **11,098.81 km**.
[27:8:5] TABLE head: State | Coastline | Rank
  [27:8:5.0] Gujarat | 2,340.62 km | 1
  [27:8:5.1] Tamil Nadu | — | 2
  [27:8:5.2] Andhra Pradesh | 1,053.07 km | 3
[27:8:6] AP is now **3rd**, not 2nd; Tamil Nadu is second under the revised figures.
[27:8:7] - **AP angle**: coastal belt – cyclones and storm surges; deltas – floods; **Rayalaseema – drought**.
## SUB 9 | Landslides and Avalanches
[27:9:0] # Landslides
[27:9:1] - Mass movement of rock, debris or earth down a slope or riverbank under **gravity**; both **geological and hydro-meteorological**; mostly in **hilly terrain** (APPSC 2025); **heavy rainfall** is the main trigger in India.
[27:9:2] TABLE head: Region | Areas | Triggers
  [27:9:2.0] NW Himalaya | J&K, HP, Uttarakhand | Earthquakes, rain, road cutting
  [27:9:2.1] NE Himalaya | Sikkim, Arunachal, Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, Meghalaya | Heavy rain, jhum cultivation
  [27:9:2.2] Western Ghats, Konkan | Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Goa, Nilgiris | Monsoon rain, deforestation
  [27:9:2.3] Eastern Ghats | Araku region (AP) | Local heavy rain
[27:9:3] - Rising frequency: unregulated construction on unstable slopes, deforestation + road widening (**Char Dham**: 43 ha diverted, ~7,000 deodars approved for felling, 23 Jan 2026), cloudbursts, deglaciation. **Increased forestation is NOT a cause.**
[27:9:4] - Institutions: **GSI** – nodal agency; **National Landslide Forecasting Centre**; National Landslide Susceptibility Mapping. **NDMA Guidelines (landslides and snow avalanches) 2009**; **National Landslide Risk Management Strategy 2019**; **TAC** for Landslide Mitigation formed by the **Ministry of Mines**. Recent event: **Wayanad, Kerala, 2024**.
[27:9:5] - **Operation Phuktal (2015):** Army and NDMA used controlled blasting to drain a **landslide lake** on the **Phuktal river** (Zanskar, Kargil).
[27:9:6] # Avalanches
[27:9:7] - Rapid flow of snow, ice and debris down a mountain slope; in high Himalaya – **J&K (most affected)**, Ladakh, **HP (Lahaul-Spiti, Kinnaur, Rohtang, Chamba)**, Uttarakhand (Garhwal, Kumaon), Sikkim, Arunachal.
[27:9:8] - **Class:** avalanche = **hydro-geological** disaster (snow + steep slope).
[27:9:9] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [27:9:9.0] Slab | Large cohesive slab breaks off – most dangerous
  [27:9:9.1] Loose snow (sluff) | Small dry slide
  [27:9:9.2] Ice | Ice masses from glaciers
  [27:9:9.3] Rock/debris | Rock, soil and snow
[27:9:10] - Causes: heavy snowfall, weak snow layers, slopes of **30°–45°**, wind loading, quakes, rapid warming, rain on snow; human – skiing, mountaineering, explosives, road building.
[27:9:11] - **Nodal: Ministry of Defence**; **DGRE** (earlier **SASE**, DRDO) issues **daily winter bulletins**; **BRO** builds roads and protection structures.
[27:9:12] - Measures: structural – snow nets, bridges, retaining walls, galleries; non-structural – **hazard zonation**, restricted movement, training.
[27:9:13] - **Mana avalanche (28 Feb 2025):** hit a **BRO** labour camp near **Mana village**, about 4 km north of **Badrinath** (Chamoli, Uttarakhand); of **54** workers, **46** rescued, **8** died.
[27:9:14] - **NDMA avalanche guidelines:** cover both **structural** measures (snow nets, snow bridges, galleries) and **non-structural** measures (hazard zonation, early warning, training).
