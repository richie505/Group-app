## SUB 0 | Tsunamis – Meaning, Causes, Behaviour and the 2004 Tsunami
[0:0] # Meaning (asked 18 times)
[0:1] - **Tsunami** = Japanese word: **"tsu" = harbour + "nami" = wave** ("harbour wave") – **not Latin, Arabic or Hebrew** (UKPSC 2005; CDS 2017 "Latin word" = the wrong statement).
[0:2] - A **series of waves** of very long wavelength (over 500 km) and long period (10 minutes to 2 hours) – **not a single wave**; the **first wave is often not the largest**, later waves can be bigger.
[0:3] - A **natural disaster**; can strike at **any time of day or night**. **Not a "tidal wave"** – tides (gravity of Moon and Sun) play **no role** in generating it (CDS 2012).
[0:4] # Causes
[0:5] TABLE head: Cause | Note
  [0:5.0] Undersea earthquakes | Main / most common cause – sudden vertical displacement of the sea floor at subduction zones (shocks above about 7.5)
  [0:5.1] Submarine or coastal landslides | Displace water
  [0:5.2] Volcanic eruptions | E.g. Krakatoa 1883 (about 40 m wave), Anak Krakatau 2018
  [0:5.3] Explosions, meteorite (bolide) impacts | Rare
[0:6] - **Not caused by hurricanes / cyclones** (they raise **storm surges**) or deserts (CDS 2010, asked twice).
[0:7] - Most tsunamis occur in the **Pacific Ocean** (Ring of Fire – Alaska, Japan, Philippines, Indonesia).
[0:8] - **Coastal States/UTs** of India are vulnerable to **tsunamis** (not volcanic eruptions).
[0:9] # Behaviour
[0:10] TABLE head: Deep ocean | Near the coast
  [0:10.0] Very long wavelength; height under 1 m ("slight swell" about 30 cm); speed hundreds of km/h (about 700–850 km/h at 4,000–6,000 m depth) | Slows down (speed depends on depth); wavelength shortens, height rises sharply (shoaling) – 15 m or more; on coastal plains it moves at roughly 50 km/h
[0:11] - Impact is **less over the open ocean, more near the coast**; ships at sea hardly notice it.
[0:12] - **Warning sign: drawdown** – the sea **suddenly recedes** (sea level falls quickly) before the wave hits; also strong quake shaking.
[0:13] # 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami
[0:14] - **26 December 2004**; **Sumatra-Andaman earthquake, Mw about 9.1** (Indian plate under the Burma plate); speed about 800 km/h, falling to under 70 km/h near shore.
[0:15] - Hit **Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, Kerala, Andaman & Nicobar** – **not Gujarat**; **worst-hit state Tamil Nadu**, worst district **Nagapattinam**; India: over **12,000 deaths**; Indira Point submerged.
[0:16] - **Mangroves** acted as a safety hedge: their **extensive prop roots** (and pneumatophores) are **not uprooted by storms and tides** and absorb wave energy (UPSC 2011). Sand dunes and coastal cliffs also block waves; **open water bodies give no protection**.
[0:17] - **Fukushima (Japan), 11 March 2011**: Mw 9.0 Tohoku earthquake + tsunami → nuclear radiation.
[0:18] # Risk zones and warning
[0:19] TABLE head: Risk | States/UTs
  [0:19.0] Very high | Andaman & Nicobar (near the Sunda subduction zone)
  [0:19.1] High | Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, Odisha
  [0:19.2] Moderate | Kerala, Karnataka, Goa, Maharashtra, Gujarat
[0:20] - Sources for India: **Sunda-Andaman** subduction zone (east) and the **Makran** zone (west coast).
[0:21] - **Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC)** at **INCOIS, Hyderabad** (MoES): detects tsunamigenic quakes within **10 minutes** and can warn **25 Indian Ocean countries**; **NIOT (Nellore)** OMNI buoys feed it. International Tsunami Information Centre: **Honolulu**. World Tsunami Awareness Day: **5 November**.
[0:22] > Traps: Japanese (not Latin); tides play no role; hurricanes do not cause tsunamis; the wave slows near the shore; the first wave may not be the largest.

## SUB 1 | Floods – Extent, Causes and Management
[1:0] # Nature and extent (asked 17 times)
[1:1] - Flood = overflow of water onto normally dry land when rivers or other water bodies cannot contain it; **rapid-onset**; the **most frequent / most common natural disaster in India**; can be **mitigated through early warning**.
[1:2] - **Rashtriya Barh Ayog (National Flood Commission)**, set up **1976**, report **1980**: of India's **329 mha**, **more than 40 mha** (about **12%** of the area) is flood-prone; a recent estimate is about **49 mha**. About **10%** of India's area is actually flood-affected.
[1:3] - Worst-affected states: **Assam and Bihar**, then **Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal**; **most flood-prone state: Bihar** (UPPCS 2000). Tamil Nadu floods in Nov–Jan (retreating monsoon).
[1:4] - About **200 million** people are exposed to recurring floods every year (APPSC Group 1 2012 key).
[1:5] - **Flood control is a State subject**; the Centre helps through **FMBAP** (Flood Management and Border Areas Programme, 2021–26).
[1:6] # Causes
[1:7] TABLE head: Cause | Effect
  [1:7.0] Heavy / prolonged rainfall | Primary cause of riverine flooding in low-lying areas
  [1:7.1] Cloudburst | Very intense rain over a small area
  [1:7.2] Snowmelt | Sudden rise in river flow
  [1:7.3] Siltation (from soil erosion) | Reduces channel depth and capacity
  [1:7.4] Embankment breach | Water escapes the channel
  [1:7.5] Deforestation | Human factor: increases runoff
  [1:7.6] Floodplain encroachment | Raises exposure
  [1:7.7] Poor drainage | Water accumulates
  [1:7.8] Dam failure | Sudden downstream discharge; overtopping = water spills over the crest
[1:8] - Major causes in India = **heavy rainfall, poor drainage, deforestation and dam failure** (not high temperature, windstorms or ocean acidification).
[1:9] - A landslide can **block a river**: **Pareechu river (Tibet)**, August **2004** landslide lake – flood threat on the **Sutlej** in Himachal Pradesh.
[1:10] # Management
[1:11] TABLE head: Structural | Non-structural
  [1:11.0] Embankments, dams and reservoirs, channel and drainage improvement, diversion channels | Flood-plain zoning, flood insurance, flood-proofing, forecasting and warning, watershed management
[1:12] - **Afforestation** of flood-prone areas / catchments = **flood control (mitigation)** – forests **increase infiltration and interception**; wetlands and floodplains store water; mangroves buffer coasts.
[1:13] - **Watershed structures** (check dams, percolation tanks) mitigate both **drought and flood** – not tornadoes or fire.
[1:14] - **Forecasting**: **Central Water Commission (CWC)** (Jal Shakti) runs flood forecasting – using **GIS, radar** (Doppler) and **remote sensing / satellite** data (not the Richter or Pascal scale or MRI).
[1:15] - In flood response, high-resolution satellite imagery and GIS map the **inundated area**, from which the **number of people affected** and damage are estimated.
[1:16] - **Odisha Flood Hazard Atlas**: built from satellite images of historic flood inundation **2001–2018** (Rocket: 2000–2018).
[1:17] # Committees and programmes
[1:18] TABLE head: Body | Year | Role
  [1:18.0] National Flood Control Programme / National Flood Policy | 1954 | Launched after the 1954 floods
  [1:18.1] Rashtriya Barh Ayog | 1976 (report 1980) | Assessed about 40 mha flood-prone
  [1:18.2] Expert Committee for Scientific Assessment of Flood-Prone Areas | July 2012 | Set up by Ministry of Water Resources, under the CWC Chairman
  [1:18.3] NDMA Guidelines | 2008 (floods), 2010 (urban floods) | Policy

## SUB 2 | Disasters – Meaning, Classification and Onset
[2:0] # Meaning (asked about 16 times)
[2:1] - **Disaster = Hazard × Vulnerability** (with low capacity to cope); French "desastre" = "bad star".
[2:2] - Natural disasters cause most damage in **developing countries**; the **Bhopal gas tragedy** was **man-made**; **India is not disaster-free**; **mangroves reduce cyclone impact** → UPPCS 2018 (asked twice): **1, 2 and 4**.
[2:3] - Globally **drought (and famine)** are the **deadliest** weather/climate disasters, followed by **floods** (NCERT); **floods** cause the largest economic losses over the long run and affect the most people.
[2:4] # Classification
[2:5] TABLE head: Class | Examples
  [2:5.0] Geological | Earthquakes, tsunamis, landslides, volcanic eruptions, dam burst, mine fire
  [2:5.1] Hydrological / hydro-meteorological | Floods, flash floods, cloudbursts, storm surges, cyclones
  [2:5.2] Climatological | Droughts, heatwaves, cold waves
  [2:5.3] Biological | Epidemics, pest attacks, food poisoning
  [2:5.4] Accident-related (HPC 1999) | Urban/forest fires, road/rail/air accidents, building collapse, mine flooding
  [2:5.5] Man-made: chemical, industrial, nuclear | Bhopal gas tragedy, Chernobyl
[2:6] - **Drought is not geological** (it is climatological).
[2:7] - **Mine flooding** = **accident-related** disaster (not geological or water-and-climate).
[2:8] - **Avalanche** = classed as a **hydro-geological** disaster (snow + steep slope) in Indian keys; international (EM-DAT) lists it as hydrological.
[2:9] - **Socio-natural hazards** = natural + human factors: floods worsened by deforestation, **landslides due to unscientific construction**.
[2:10] - **Man-made hazard**: **fires** (not landslides, avalanches or droughts); **not man-made: mud slides** (natural) – deforestation and food poisoning come from human action.
[2:11] - **Chennai floods (2015)** = the most human-induced (wetland and lake encroachment); the Chennai tsunami, Anantapur low rainfall and Kedarnath flood had natural triggers.
[2:12] # Onset
[2:13] TABLE head: Rapid-onset | Slow-onset
  [2:13.0] Cyclone, flood, tsunami, earthquake, flash flood, cloudburst | Drought ("creeping emergency / creeping hazard"), pest infestation, environmental degradation
[2:14] ~~ Sources: Rocket Disaster Management p.40, 47-48; NCERT summary (OnlyIAS Indian Geography 05) p.15-17; PMF Internal Security & DM 02 p.42-43 (book p.116-117); UNDRR/ScienceDaily 2016 natural-disaster losses study (via web search); app notes 4:27 sub 0
## SUB 3 | Flash Floods, Cloudbursts and GLOFs
[3:0] # Flash floods (asked 10 times)
[3:1] - A flood that **occurs suddenly, unexpectedly and for a short duration** – from **local precipitation of high intensity** (intense rain, cloudbursts), or dam / glacial-lake / landslide-lake bursts; little warning time; main danger is **debris**; also landslides.
[3:2] - Heavy rain, urban settings or blocked drains are only possible causes – not the definition.
[3:3] - Vulnerable: **mountains, steep catchments and urban areas**. **IMD** gives flash-flood guidance for India + **Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal**.
[3:4] # Cloudburst
[3:5] - **Sudden, extremely heavy rainfall for a short period over a small area** (CDS 2012: copious rain lasting a few minutes) – **IMD: at least 100 mm (10 cm) in one hour over about 20–30 sq km**.
[3:6] - Localised, with **thunder and lightning**; **mostly in hilly areas** (1,000–2,500 m; orographic lifting); can give **250–300 mm in a couple of hours**; occurs **day or night** → CDS 2017: **I, II and III**.
[3:7] - Highly localised → very hard to forecast (Doppler radar, nowcasting). **HKH "monsoon paradox"**: erratic seasonal rain plus more short, intense cloudbursts.
[3:8] TABLE head: Region | Areas | Why
  [3:8.0] Himalaya | Uttarakhand, HP, J&K, Ladakh, Sikkim | Steep slopes, orographic effect
  [3:8.1] Western Ghats | Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra | Intense rain on the windward side
  [3:8.2] AP Eastern Ghats agency areas | Narrow valleys | Cloudburst flash floods
[3:9] # GLOFs and events
[3:10] - **GLOF**: glacier retreat enlarges a **proglacial (moraine-dammed) lake**, whose dam bursts. **NDMA GLOF Guidelines (October 2020)**: population risk highest in **Sikkim and Jammu & Kashmir**.
[3:11] TABLE head: Event | Year | Note
  [3:11.0] Leh cloudburst | 2010 | Ladakh
  [3:11.1] Kedarnath | June 2013 | Cloudburst + GLOF; Chorabari (Chaurabari) Glacier in Uttarakhand, north of the temple; lake Gandhi Sarovar
  [3:11.2] Operation Phuktal | 2015 | Army + NDMA blasted a landslide-debris lake on the Phuktal river (Zanskar, Kargil) to prevent flash floods
  [3:11.3] Chamoli glacier burst | 2021 | Uttarakhand
  [3:11.4] South Lhonak GLOF | Oct 2023 | Sikkim (Teesta III dam)
  [3:11.5] HP flash floods | 2023 | Unregulated slope construction
  [3:11.6] Mandi (HP) | 2025 | Nearly 20 times normal rain
  [3:11.7] Dharali-Harsil (Uttarkashi) | 5 Aug 2025 | Flash flood; ice-patch collapse on the Srikanta glacier (ISRO)
  [3:11.8] Nepal GLOF floods | 26 Aug 2026 | 26 GLOFs since the 1970s (UNDP)

## SUB 5 | Droughts
[5:0] # Nature and types (asked 7 times)
[5:1] - Prolonged deficiency of rainfall or moisture → water scarcity; **slow-onset**; in India drought is **coterminous with the monsoon** (its failure or delay) – **irregular or delayed monsoons** raise drought chances; >80% of rain falls in under 100 days of the SW monsoon.
[5:2] TABLE head: Type | Indicator | Time lag
  [5:2.0] Meteorological | Prolonged inadequate rainfall, maldistributed over time and space; rain ≥ 25% below LPA (degree of dryness + length of dry period) | Immediate
  [5:2.1] Agricultural | Soil-moisture deficit → crop failure | Intermediate
  [5:2.2] Hydrological | Fall in reservoirs, rivers, aquifers | Weeks to months
  [5:2.3] Socio-economic | Demand exceeds supply; water/food shortage hits livelihoods and markets | Long-term
  [5:2.4] Ecological | Ecosystem productivity collapses | —
[5:3] - **IMD**: deficiency **26–50%** of LPA = **moderate**; **over 50%** = **severe** drought (no separate "extreme" category).
[5:4] # Local names of drought (NCERT)
[5:5] TABLE head: Scarcity | Name
  [5:5.0] Food grains | Akal
  [5:5.1] Fodder | Trinkal
  [5:5.2] Water | Jalkal
  [5:5.3] All three | Trikal (most devastating)
[5:6] # Triggers, consequences, regions
[5:7] - Triggers: delayed/erratic monsoon, breaks, heat and evaporation; **El Nino**. Normal SW-monsoon rain **868.6 mm** (1971–2020).
[5:8] - Long-term consequences of recurrent drought: **groundwater depletion, land degradation, desertification, distress (rural) migration**, debt, lower farm output.
[5:9] - Drought is a **recurrent** hazard that can **regularly affect all regions** of India (APPSC key); 68% of cultivable area is vulnerable.
[5:10] - Regions: **Western Rajasthan, Kutch-Saurashtra, Marathwada-Vidarbha, interior Karnataka, Rayalaseema (AP)** – not Lower Assam. **Rayalaseema**: **deep borewells** worsen groundwater loss.
[5:11] - Management: NDMA Guidelines 2010; Manual for Drought Management 2016 (Agriculture); nodal ministry **Agriculture**.

## SUB 7 | Storm Surges and the Coastline
[7:0] - **Storm surge** = abnormal sea rise pushed by **cyclone winds and low pressure**; a **high tide** raises the baseline; **sea-level rise** worsens surge flooding (more, not less, exposure); heavy coastal rain causes drainage congestion; brings **salinity**.
[7:1] - **East coast** gets far more cyclones than the west (Bay of Bengal : Arabian Sea about 4 : 1).
[7:2] - Coastal defence: **mangroves, shelter belts, dune stabilisation, seawalls**.
[7:3] - **Coastline remeasured (2023–24)** by the **National Hydrographic Office with the Survey of India**, reported Dec 2024: **11,098.81 km**; **Gujarat** 1st (2,340.62 km), Tamil Nadu 2nd, **Andhra Pradesh 3rd (1,053.07 km)**.
[7:4] - **AP pattern**: coastal belt – cyclones and storm surges; deltas – floods; **Rayalaseema – drought**.

## SUB 8 | Related Hazards – Landslides, Avalanches, Nodal Ministries and Major Disasters
[8:0] # Landslides
[8:1] - **Mass movement of rocks, debris or earth down mountain slopes or riverbanks** under gravity; both **geological and hydro-meteorological**; **prolonged rainfall** is the main trigger. About **12.6%** of land landslide-prone.
[8:2] - Zones: Himalaya; **Western Ghats** (the other major zone); NE Himalaya (**jhum cultivation**); Eastern Ghats – **Araku region** (AP). Rising frequency: **unregulated construction on unstable slopes**, road widening.
[8:3] - **GSI** = nodal agency; **National Landslide Forecasting Centre**; **NDMA Guidelines 2009** (landslides and snow avalanches); **National Landslide Risk Management Strategy 2019**; nodal ministry **Mines**. **Wayanad (Kerala) 2024** = landslide.
[8:4] # Avalanches
[8:5] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [8:5.0] Slab | Large cohesive slab breaks off – most dangerous
  [8:5.1] Loose snow (sluff) | Small dry slide
  [8:5.2] Ice | Ice masses from glaciers
  [8:5.3] Rock/debris | Rock, soil and snow
[8:6] - **Rapid flow of snow, ice and debris down a mountain slope**; slopes **30°–45°**; human triggers: **skiing, mountaineering**, explosives. **J&K most affected**; **Lahaul-Spiti, Kinnaur – Himachal Pradesh**.
[8:7] - Nodal **Ministry of Defence**; **DGRE** (earlier **SASE**, DRDO) issues **daily winter bulletins**; **BRO** builds roads and protection structures. NDMA guidelines: **structural** (snow nets, bridges, galleries) **and non-structural** (**hazard zonation**) measures.
[8:8] - **Mana avalanche (28 Feb 2025)**: BRO camp near **Mana** village (Badrinath); 54 workers, 46 rescued, **8 died**.
