=== Part XIV-A – Articles 323-A and 323-B
# Constitutional place
- **Nature:** tribunals are **quasi-judicial** bodies – neither executive departments nor courts – set up to cut **pendency** in regular courts and bring **domain expertise**.
- **Not in the original Constitution:** the **42nd Amendment, 1976** inserted **Part XIV-A** ("Tribunals"), on the **Swaran Singh Committee**'s advice – only two Articles: **323-A** (administrative tribunals) and **323-B** (tribunals for other matters).
- **75th Amendment Act, 1993** (in force 1994) added clause **(h) rent and tenancy rights** to 323-B.
- Tribunal **chairpersons** generally get the status of a **High Court judge**.
| Part | Subject |
|---|---|
| VIII | **Union Territories** |
| IX-A | Municipalities (243-P – 243-ZG) |
| IX-B | Co-operative Societies (243-ZH – 243-ZT) |
| X | **Scheduled and Tribal Areas** |
| XI | **Union–State relations** |
| XIV | Services under the Union and the States (308–323) |
| **XIV-A** | **Tribunals (323-A, 323-B)** |
| XVII | **Official Language** |
- **Trap:** "Tribunals – Part XI" (or Part X / Part XIV) is **wrong** – tribunals are strictly **Part XIV-A**.
# Article 323-A vs Article 323-B
| Basis | Article 323-A | Article 323-B |
|---|---|---|
| Subjects | **Only public service matters** – recruitment and service conditions of Union, State, local-body, public-corporation employees | Taxation; foreign exchange, import-export; industrial and labour; **land reforms**; ceiling on urban property; **elections** to Parliament and State Legislatures; **foodstuffs**; rent and tenancy |
| Who can legislate | **Parliament only** (Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985) | **Parliament and State Legislatures** (within their competence) |
| Hierarchy | One tribunal for the Centre and one per State (or joint) – **no hierarchy** | A **hierarchy** of tribunals (original + appellate) may be created |
> Asked as: wrong Part–provision match (UPPCS 2023: "Tribunals – Part XI" is the mismatch); who legislates under 323-A; which Article lists elections/foodstuffs/land reforms (323-B).

=== Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT)
# Basis and setting up
- **Legal basis:** **Article 323-A** + **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985**; a **statutory**, quasi-judicial body set up by the **Central Government** – the **first tribunal under Art. 323-A**.
- **Date:** established with effect from **1 November 1985** (notification under the 1985 Act) – in **1985**, under PM **Rajiv Gandhi** (not Lal Bahadur Shastri – UPSC 2009).
- **APSLPRB SI 2016** printed only "2-10-1983/84/85/86" and keyed **2-10-1985**: the year is right but the date is not – CAT began on **1-11-1985**.
# Composition
| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| Members | Drawn from **both judicial and administrative** streams (not administrative only) |
| Sanctioned strength | **1 Chairman + 69 Members** (34 Judicial + 35 Administrative; Rajya Sabha answer 2023); Vice-Chairman post abolished in **2006** |
| Judicial members | Qualified to be High Court judges |
| Administrative members | **Additional Secretary** or above |
| Benches | **Principal Bench New Delhi + 18 other** regular benches (19 in all) |
| Appointment | Central Government, on a **search-cum-selection committee** headed by the CJI or an SC judge he nominates |
# Jurisdiction and procedure
- **Original jurisdiction** over recruitment and service matters of **All-India Services**, **Central civil services**, civil posts under the Centre and **civilian employees of defence**; not armed-forces members, Supreme Court staff, Parliament's secretarial staff or **private-sector** employees.
- **Not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908**; guided by **natural justice**; nominal fee (₹50); applicant may appear **in person** or through a lawyer.
- **Appeal:** originally only to the Supreme Court; since **L. Chandra Kumar (1997)** first to the **Division Bench of the High Court**, then the Supreme Court – not directly to the SC.
> Asked as: statements – statutory (yes), administrative members only (no), not bound by CPC (yes), covers AIS + Central services (yes), set up 1985 (yes) → "I, III, IV and V" (APPSC Group I 2023); date of formation.

=== National Green Tribunal (NGT)
- **NGT Act, 2010**; statutory body established **18 October 2010**; hears **environmental** disputes (forests, natural resources, compensation).
- Gives effect to the **right to a healthy environment** read into **Article 21**.
- Applies **sustainable development**, the **precautionary principle** and **polluter pays** (**Section 20**).
- **Not bound by the CPC, 1908**; guided by **natural justice**; can review its own decisions.
- **Benches:** Principal **New Delhi**; zonal **Bhopal** (Central), **Pune** (West), **Kolkata** (East), **Chennai** (South) – **no Jaipur** bench.
- **Appeal:** **directly to the Supreme Court within 90 days** (Section 22) – not to a High Court.
> Asked as: days for appeal to the SC (90 – MPPCS 2020); A-R on CPC and natural justice.

=== Judicial Review, Case Law, Merits and Criticism
| Case | Year | Held |
|---|---|---|
| **S.P. Sampath Kumar** | **1987** | Tribunals may **replace** HCs as an effective substitute (later overruled) |
| **L. Chandra Kumar** | **1997** | Judicial review under **Arts 226/227** (HCs) and **Art. 32** (SC) is **basic structure**; exclusion clauses **323-A(2)(d)** and **323-B(3)(d)** struck down; tribunals **supplement, not replace** HCs |
| **R. Gandhi** | **2010** | Members doing judicial work must be **independent of the executive** |
| **Rojer Mathew** | **2019** | First recommended a **National Tribunals Commission** |
| **Madras Bar Association** | 2021, **Nov 2025** | Struck down re-enacted provisions; restored the earlier framework |
- Judicial review cannot be taken away even by amendment under **Article 368** – amendments are tested on the **basic structure** doctrine.
- **Merits:** expertise, specialisation, speed, low cost; follow **natural justice**, not the full **CPC**.
- **Criticism:** run by the **same ministries** whose decisions they review; executive control over appointments (Finance Act 2017, 2021 Ordinance).

=== Tribunal Reforms – 2021 Act to the 2026 Act
# Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021
- **Abolished:** Film Certification Appellate Tribunal, **IPAB**, Airport Appellate Tribunal, Authority for Advance Rulings (Customs), Plant Varieties Protection Appellate Tribunal – functions to the **High Courts** (the NGT continues).
- **Tenure 4 years**; **minimum age 50**; Chairperson up to **70**, members up to **67**; search-cum-selection committee sent **two names** per vacancy.
# Madras Bar Association (Nov 2025)
- Struck down the **minimum age of 50**, the **4-year tenure** (made **5**), the **two-name** panel (now **one name per vacancy**) and equating members with civil servants; ordered a **National Tribunals Commission** within four months.
# Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026
- Moved by Law Minister **Arjun Ram Meghwal**; passed **Lok Sabha 10 Aug 2026** (voice vote) and **Rajya Sabha 11 Aug 2026**; **repeals the 2021 Act**; NTC to oversee 16 tribunals.
| NTC feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| HQ | New Delhi |
| Members | **5**: Chairperson (former SC judge or former **HC Chief Justice**), 2 judicial (consulting the CJI), 2 technical (25+ years) |
| Tenure | **5 years or age 70**, whichever earlier |
| Role | Selection, performance review, discipline |

=== State Administrative Tribunals – AP Angle
- **SATs** are set up by the **Central Government at the request of the State**; a **Joint Administrative Tribunal** may serve **two or more States**; SAT members are chosen by a committee chaired by the State HC Chief Justice.
- **32nd Amendment, 1973** – **Article 371D** gave constitutional authority for an **AP Administrative Tribunal (APAT)** for public-service grievances.
- Presidential order of **19 May 1975** under **Art. 371D(3)** constituted APAT; it worked from **6 July 1976**; reconstituted under the 1985 Act from **1 Nov 1989**.
- **2016:** Telangana removed from its jurisdiction; **abolished in 2020** (Centre's notification of **14 Jan 2020**) – its cases went to the **AP High Court**, not the CAT.
