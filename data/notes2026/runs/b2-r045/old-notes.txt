=== NOTES 2:45 | Tribunals – Articles 323A and 323B; Administrative and Specialised Tribunals; Tribunal Reforms
## SUB 0 | Tribunals – Nature and Constitutional Place
[45:0:0] - **Quasi-judicial** bodies: neither executive body nor court; set up to cut **pendency** and bring **domain expertise**.
[45:0:1] - Placed in **Part XIV-A** (Articles **323-A** and **323-B**), inserted by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**, on the **Swaran Singh Committee**'s advice.
[45:0:2] - **75th Amendment, 1994** added the **rent and tenancy** clause (h) to 323-B.
[45:0:3] - Chairpersons get the status of a **High Court judge**.
[45:0:4] TABLE head: Part | Subject
  [45:0:4.0] IX-A | Municipalities (243-P–243-ZG)
  [45:0:4.1] IX-B | Co-operative Societies (243-ZH–243-ZT)
  [45:0:4.2] X | Scheduled and Tribal Areas
  [45:0:4.3] XI | Union–State relations
  [45:0:4.4] XIV | Services (308–323)
  [45:0:4.5] XIV-A | Tribunals
[45:0:5] Tribunals are in Part XIV-A, not Part XI or Part X.
## SUB 1 | Article 323-A vs Article 323-B
[45:1:0] TABLE head: Basis | Article 323-A | Article 323-B
  [45:1:0.0] Subjects | Only public service matters: recruitment and service conditions of Union, State, local-authority and statutory-body employees (also PSUs) | Taxation, foreign exchange, industrial disputes and labour, land reforms, urban land ceiling, foodstuffs, elections to Parliament and State Legislatures, rent and tenancy
  [45:1:0.1] Who creates | Parliament only (Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985) | Parliament and State Legislatures (States within their competence)
  [45:1:0.2] Structure | One CAT + State tribunals; no hierarchy | Hierarchy allowed (original + appellate)
[45:1:1] 323-A | service matters | Parliament only
323-B | other subjects | Parliament + States
## SUB 2 | Administrative Adjudication – Merits and Criticism
[45:2:0] - **Merits:** expertise, specialisation, speed, low cost; follow **natural justice**, not the full **Code of Civil Procedure**.
[45:2:1] - **Criticism:** run by the **same ministries** whose decisions they review; executive control via the **Finance Act 2017** and the 2021 Ordinance.
[45:2:2] - **R. Gandhi (2010):** members performing judicial functions must be **independent of the executive**.
## SUB 3 | Central Administrative Tribunal (cat)
[45:3:0] # Basis and Setting Up
[45:3:1] - Legal basis: **Article 323A** + **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985**; statutory, quasi-judicial; set up by the **Central Government**.
[45:3:2] - Set up in **1985** under PM **Rajiv Gandhi** (not Lal Bahadur Shastri, UPSC 2009). **First tribunal under Art. 323A.**
[45:3:3] # Composition and Data
[45:3:4] TABLE head: Point | Fact
  [45:3:4.0] Members | From both judicial and administrative streams (APPSC-G1 2022)
  [45:3:4.1] Strength | 69 = 35 Judicial (incl. Chairperson) + 34 Administrative
  [45:3:4.2] Judicial members | Qualified as HC judges
  [45:3:4.3] Administrative members | Additional Secretary or above
  [45:3:4.4] Benches | Main bench New Delhi + 18 benches
  [45:3:4.5] Disposal rate | 92.9%
[45:3:5] # Jurisdiction and Procedure
[45:3:6] - **Original jurisdiction** over service matters of All-India Services, Central civil services, civil posts under the Centre, **civilian defence employees**, notified PSUs.
[45:3:7] - Not for **armed forces personnel** or **private-sector** employees.
[45:3:8] - Not bound by CPC; natural justice; **nominal fee**; parties may appear **in person**.
[45:3:9] - Appeal first to the **Division Bench of the High Court**, then the Supreme Court – not directly to SC.
[45:3:10] CAT covers civilian defence employees, not armed forces personnel.
## SUB 4 | State Administrative Tribunals – Ap Angle
[45:4:0] - SATs are set up by the **Centre at the request of the State**; **joint tribunals** possible for two or more States.
[45:4:1] - **32nd Amendment, 1973** – **Article 371D** gave constitutional authority for the **AP Administrative Tribunal** (public-service grievances).
[45:4:2] - Presidential order of **19 May 1975** under **Art. 371D(3)** constituted APAT; it worked from **6 July 1976**.
[45:4:3] - Reconstituted under the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 from **1 Nov 1989**.
[45:4:4] - **2016:** Telangana removed from its jurisdiction.
[45:4:5] - **Abolished in 2020** (Centre's notification of **14 Jan 2020**); its staff moved to the **AP High Court**, which now hears these service cases.

## SUB 6 | National Green Tribunal
[45:6:0] - **NGT Act, 2010**; statutory; established **18.10.2010**; hears **environmental disputes**.
[45:6:1] - Gives effect to the **right to a healthy environment** read into **Article 21**.
[45:6:2] - Applies **sustainable development**, **precautionary principle** and **polluter pays** (Section 20).
[45:6:3] - Guided by **natural justice**; **not bound by the CPC, 1908**.
[45:6:4] - Benches: Principal **New Delhi** + zonal **Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata, Chennai**.
[45:6:5] - Appeal **directly to the Supreme Court within 90 days**.

## SUB 8 | Tribunal Reforms
[45:8:0] # Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021
[45:8:1] - Abolished: **Film Certification Appellate Tribunal**, **IPAB**, **Airport Appellate Tribunal**, **Authority for Advance Rulings (Customs)**, **Plant Varieties Protection Appellate Tribunal**; functions to **High Courts**.
[45:8:2] - Tenure **4 years**; minimum age **50**; Chairperson up to **70**, members up to **67**.
[45:8:3] - Search-cum-Selection Committee sent **two names** per vacancy.
[45:8:4] # Madras Bar Association (Nov 2025)
[45:8:5] - Struck down **minimum age 50**, **4-year tenure** (made 5), **two names** (now **one name per vacancy**), equating members with civil servants; ordered an **NTC within four months**.
[45:8:6] # Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026
[45:8:7] - Moved by Law Minister **Arjun Ram Meghwal**; **Lok Sabha 10 Aug 2026** (voice vote), **Rajya Sabha 11 Aug 2026**; repeals the 2021 Act.
