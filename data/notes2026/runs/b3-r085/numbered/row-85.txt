[0:0] TABLE head: Particular | Narasimham-I | Narasimham-II

  [0:0.3] Focus | Financial-sector reform: lower SLR and CRR, phase out directed credit (priority sector redefined and cut to 10% of credit), interest-rate deregulation, prudential norms, competition, ARCs, end of dual control | Banking-sector reform: merge strong banks and DFIs, close weak banks, 3-tier structure, CRAR 10%, NPA management
[0:1] - **What N-I asked to cut:** **SLR** (38.5% → 25%), **CRR** (15% → 3-5%) and **priority-sector (directed) credit** (to 10%) – so "SLR, CRR and priority sector financing" (NextGen), not just "SLR and CRR". N-I did **not** set the 10% CRAR (that was N-II).
[0:2] - **Three tiers (N-II):** **2-3 international banks**, **8-10 national banks**, many **local banks** – the idea of **fewer and stronger banks** through mergers and closure of weak, unviable banks.
[0:3] - **Narrow banking:** weak banks to place their funds only in **risk-free assets** (G-secs) with matched maturities – a **Narasimham Committee** idea (Ramesh Singh credits CFS 1991; N-II 1998 recommended it for weak banks). Contrast **universal banking** (S.H. Khan, 1998).
[0:4] - "Which committee examined financial-sector reforms / banking structure reforms / economic (banking) reforms?" → **Narasimham** (not Abid Hussain = SSI, Chelliah = tax, Bhagwati).
[0:5] - Name of the 1991 committee: officially the **Committee on the Financial System**; it is popularly the **Narasimham Committee** (APPSC keys accept the official name).
[0:6] - N-I also recommended **ARCs** for NPAs and **SARFAESI**-type recovery powers; RRBs could stay separate or merge with sponsor banks.
[0:7] > Exam angle: N-I = Financial **System** (1991); N-II = **Banking Sector** Reforms (1998). Recommendation questions test SLR/CRR/PSL cuts (N-I) vs 3 tiers and 10% CRAR (N-II).

## SUB 2 | Financial Sector Reforms After 1991
[2:0] - Reforms covered **banking, capital, government debt and forex markets**: **CRR and SLR cuts** (SLR **38.5%** in 1991-92), **deregulation of interest rates**, **entry of private firms in banking and insurance**, prudential norms on NPAs, end of selective credit controls – "all of the above" in such MCQs.
[2:1] - **Prudential norms** (income recognition, asset classification, provisioning) and **CRAR introduced in 1992**.
[2:2] - **Private bank entry:** **HDFC, ICICI** (by 1995); **FDI up to 74%** in private banks (**2004**).
[2:3] - **RBI became the primary regulator**; **dual control** with the Finance Ministry's Banking Division ended.

## SUB 3 | Basel Accords and the BIS
[3:0] - Basel Accords are framed by the **Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS)** under the **Bank for International Settlements (BIS)**, **Basel**, Switzerland; BCBS set up end-**1974** after the failure of **Bankhaus Herstatt**.
[3:1] - **BIS (est. 1930)** = the **world's oldest international financial organisation**; the central banks' bank – **not related to the UN** (MIGA, IFC, ICSID are World Bank Group bodies in the UN system).
[3:2] TABLE head: Accord | Year | Core content
  [3:2.0] Basel I | 1988 | Credit risk only; five risk buckets (0, 10, 20, 50, 100%); minimum capital 8% of risk-weighted assets; India adopted 1999 (CRAR 9%)
  [3:2.1] Basel II | Proposed June 1999; framework June 2004 | International standards for measuring the adequacy of a bank's capital; three pillars: minimum capital requirements, supervisory review, market discipline
  [3:2.2] Basel III | 2010 (reply to the 2007-09 crisis); in India from 1 Jan 2013 | Improve banks' ability to deal with financial and economic stress, risk management, transparency; CET1, buffers, leverage ratio, LCR, NSFR
[3:3] - **Basel Convention** (**UNEP**, 1989, in force 1992) = **transboundary movement of hazardous wastes** – not bank capital; not civil aviation, cyber crime or doping.
[3:4] > Exam angle: Basel II/III "relates to" → capital adequacy / banking stress; BIS is the odd one out in "not related to the UN".
[3:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 08 p.6-7; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy p.52; app notes 3:85 sub 3
## SUB 4 | Capital Adequacy – CRAR, Tier Capital, RBI vs BCBS
[4:0] - **CRAR / CAR** = **capital ÷ risk-weighted assets**: the ratio banks must keep between their **risky assets and capital**; a standard measure of the **strength of banks' balance sheets** (ability to absorb losses). CAR and CRAR are the same ratio; "Capital-to-Risk-weighted Assets Ratio" is the precise name.
[4:1] - India introduced CRAR in **1992** under BIS standards; **9%** under Basel I (1999) – stricter than BCBS's 8%.
[4:2] TABLE head: Norm | BCBS minimum | RBI norm
  [4:2.0] CET1 | 4.5% | 5.5%
  [4:2.1] Tier 1 | 6% | 7% (not 8%)
  [4:2.2] Total CRAR | 8% | 9%
  [4:2.3] Capital conservation buffer (CCB) | 2.5% | 2.5%
  [4:2.4] Countercyclical buffer (CCyB) | 0-2.5% | Framework since 2015, not activated
  [4:2.5] Leverage ratio | 3% | 4% for D-SIBs, 3.5% for others
[4:3] # Capital terms
[4:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:4.0] Tier 1 | Core equity: paid-up capital, reserves, undistributed profits
  [4:4.1] Tier 2 | Mixed equity-debt, e.g. subordinated debt
  [4:4.2] CCyB | Extra capital in credit booms; main trigger = credit-to-GDP gap
  [4:4.3] Leverage ratio | Tier 1 capital ÷ total (non-risk-weighted) exposure
  [4:4.4] LCR ≥ 100% | High-quality liquid assets (HQLA) to survive a 30-day stress; Level 1 = cash, G-secs; Level 2 = highly rated corporate bonds
  [4:4.5] NSFR | Stable funding over a 1-year horizon
[4:5] - **Macroprudential tools:** **LTV caps** (loan limited to a share of collateral value), higher **risk weights** on unsecured consumer credit (Nov 2023). **Oct 2025:** RBI draft proposed **lower Basel III risk weights for MSMEs and real estate**.
[4:6] > Exam angle: "Minimum Tier 1 = 8%" is the wrong statement on Basel III in India (it is 7%).
[4:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 08 p.5-7; NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 07 p.9; app notes 3:85 sub 4

## SUB 6 | PSB Revival – Indradhanush, Recapitalisation, BBB/FSIB
[6:0] - **Indradhanush (Aug 2015):** **7-point plan (A-G)** – Appointments, Banks Board Bureau, Capitalisation, De-stressing, Empowerment, Framework of accountability, Governance; **₹70,000 crore** budget capital over 4 years.
[6:1] - **Recapitalisation (Oct 2017):** **₹2.11 lakh crore** – **₹1.35 lakh crore via recapitalisation bonds** (Govt gives bonds to PSBs, money returns as equity → no immediate cash outgo), ~₹58,000 crore raised by banks.
[6:2] - **4R:** Recognition, Resolution, Recapitalisation, Reforms.
[6:3] - **P.J. Nayak (2014, RBI):** weak PSB board governance; proposed a **Bank Investment Company** to hold the Govt stake, stake **below 50%**.
[6:4] - **Banks Board Bureau (BBB):** proposed by P.J. Nayak, part of Indradhanush; from **1 April 2016**; **selected chairmen / whole-time directors of PSBs** and boards of PSU insurers and FIs.
[6:5] - **Replaced (1 July 2022) by the Financial Services Institutions Bureau (FSIB)**, which now recommends PSB chairpersons and whole-time directors.

## SUB 7 | EASE Reforms and the EASE Index
[7:0] - **EASE = Enhanced Access and Service Excellence**: conceptualised 2015, launched **2017**; PSB Reforms Agenda from **Jan 2018**; for **public sector banks only** (not NBFCs).
[7:1] - Run by the **IBA's EASE Steering Committee** (not a board of heads of premier FIs); PSBs report quarterly against benchmarks.
[7:2] - **EASE Index**: **measures the performance of PSBs** on reforms (quarterly benchmarking of mid-size and large PSBs) – **not** MSME ease of doing business.
[7:3] - Themes: digital transformation, **customer service with digital enablement**, risk management, governance. **EASE 8.0 "EASE ₹ise" (FY2026):** Risk and Resilience, Innovation, Socio-economic impact, Excellence.
[7:4] > Exam angle: APPSC G1 statements – "governed by a board of FI heads" and "for PSBs and NBFCs" are false.

## SUB 9 | DICGC and the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act 2025
[9:0] - **DICGC**: **DICGC Act, 1961**; set up **1978** by merging the Deposit Insurance Corporation (1962) and the Credit Guarantee Corporation (1971); a **wholly owned RBI subsidiary**.
[9:1] - Cover **₹5 lakh per depositor per bank** (from 4 Feb 2020; raised from ₹1 lakh in **Budget 2020-21**, after the **PMC Bank** crisis).
[9:2] - Covers commercial banks (incl. **Indian branches of foreign banks**), RRBs, co-operative banks; excludes **government, inter-bank and NBFC** deposits. **Premium paid by the insured bank**.
[9:3] - **Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025** (in force **1 Aug 2025**): "substantial interest" ₹5 lakh → **₹2 crore**; co-operative bank directors' tenure 8 → **10 years**.
[9:4] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Part 5 p.120-121; app notes 3:85 sub 9
