## SUB 0 | Financial Sector Reforms After 1991
[85:0:0] # What the Reforms Covered
[85:0:1] - Covered banking, capital, government debt and forex markets.
[85:0:2] - **Interest-rate deregulation** (APPSC-GS 2023 key).
[85:0:3] - **CRR and SLR cuts**: SLR was **38.5%** in 1991-92.
[85:0:4] - **Prudential norms**: income recognition, asset classification, provisioning; **CRAR introduced in 1992**.
[85:0:5] - **Private bank entry**: **HDFC, ICICI** (by 1995); **FDI up to 74%** in private banks (**2004**).
[85:0:6] - **RBI became the primary regulator**; **dual control** with the Finance Ministry's Banking Division ended.
[85:0:7] # Reform → Year
[85:0:8] TABLE head: Year | Reform
  [85:0:8.0] 1992 | Prudential norms and CRAR; SEBI made statutory
  [85:0:8.1] 1993 | Market-determined exchange rate
  [85:0:8.2] 1995 | Banking Ombudsman
  [85:0:8.3] 1999 | FEMA replaced FERA; IRDA Act opened insurance
  [85:0:8.4] 2004 | FDI up to 74% in private banks
[85:0:9] # What Does NOT Belong
[85:0:10] - **FRBM Act, 2003**: a **fiscal** reform, not a financial-sector reform.
[85:0:11] - **Nationalisation of banks (1969, 1980)**: pre-reform.
[85:0:12] - **ICICI Bank** is **not a nationalised bank**.
[85:0:13] FRBM Act 2003 is the usual odd-one-out in "which is NOT a post-1991 financial sector reform".

## SUB 3 | Basel Accords
[85:3:0] # The Basel Committee and BIS
[85:3:1] - Basel Accords are framed by the **Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS)** under the **Bank for International Settlements (BIS)**, Basel, Switzerland.
[85:3:2] - BCBS set up end-**1974** after the failure of **Bankhaus Herstatt**.
[85:3:3] - **BIS est. 1930** → **world's oldest international financial organisation**; **not related to the UN** (MIGA, IFC, ICSID are World Bank Group).
[85:3:4] # Basel I, II, III
[85:3:5] TABLE head: Accord | Year | Core content
  [85:3:5.0] Basel I | 1988 | Credit risk only; five risk buckets (0, 10, 20, 50, 100%); minimum capital 8% of risk-weighted assets
  [85:3:5.1] Basel II | Proposed June 1999; framework June 2004 | World standards for measuring capital adequacy; three pillars: minimum capital requirements, supervisory review, market discipline
  [85:3:5.2] Basel III | 2010 (reply to 2007-09 crisis); in India from 1 Jan 2013 | Better capital (CET1), capital buffers, leverage ratio, LCR, NSFR, counterparty credit risk
[85:3:6] - Basel III helps banks **absorb stress** and improves **risk management** and disclosure.
[85:3:7] - **Leverage ratio** came only with **Basel III**.
[85:3:8] # Basel Accords vs Basel Convention
[85:3:9] - **Basel Convention** (**UNEP**, adopted 1989, in force 1992) = **transboundary movement of hazardous wastes**, not bank capital.
[85:3:10] Basel Accords | BCBS/BIS | Bank capital
Basel Convention | UNEP | Hazardous wastes
## SUB 4 | Capital Adequacy – Rbi vs Bcbs Norms
[85:4:0] # Minimums
[85:4:1] TABLE head: Norm | BCBS minimum | RBI norm
  [85:4:1.0] CET1 | 4.5% | 5.5%
  [85:4:1.1] Tier 1 | 6% | 7%
  [85:4:1.2] Total CRAR | 8% | 9%
  [85:4:1.3] Capital conservation buffer (CCB) | 2.5% | 2.5%
  [85:4:1.4] Countercyclical buffer (CCyB) | 0-2.5% | Framework since 2015, not activated (May 2026)
  [85:4:1.5] Leverage ratio | 3% | 4% for D-SIBs, 3.5% for others
[85:4:2] - India: CRAR introduced **1992** under BIS standards; set at **9%** under Basel I (1999). RBI's 9% is **stricter** than BCBS's 8%.
[85:4:3] - 4.5% is the BCBS **CET1** floor, not "Tier I"; Basel III minimum **Tier 1 in India is 7%, not 8%**.
[85:4:4] # Terms → Meaning
[85:4:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [85:4:5.0] CAR/CRAR | Capital ÷ risk-weighted assets; measures strength of banks' balance sheets
  [85:4:5.1] Tier 1 | Core equity: paid-up capital, reserves, undistributed (kept) profits
  [85:4:5.2] Tier 2 | Mixed equity-debt, e.g. subordinated debt
  [85:4:5.3] CCyB | Checks excess credit growth; RBI 2015; main trigger = credit-to-GDP gap
  [85:4:5.4] Leverage ratio | Tier 1 capital ÷ total (non-risk-weighted) exposure; caps lending against own capital (APPSC-GS 2019)
  [85:4:5.5] LCR ≥ 100% | Stock of HQLA to survive a 30-day stress
  [85:4:5.6] NSFR | Stable funding over a 1-year horizon
[85:4:6] - **HQLA**: Level 1 = cash, G-secs, central-bank reserves; Level 2 = highly rated corporate bonds.
[85:4:7] - **Macroprudential tools**: **LTV caps** (loan limited to a share of collateral value); **risk weights** raised on unsecured consumer credit (Nov 2023); liquidity ratios (LCR, NSFR).
[85:4:8] - **Oct 2025**: RBI draft proposed **lower Basel III risk weights for MSMEs and real estate**.
[85:4:9] LCR = 30 days | NSFR = 1 year | CCyB = credit boom | RBI CRAR 9% vs BCBS 8%

## SUB 6 | Psb Revival – Indradhanush, Recapitalisation, Bbb
[85:6:0] # Indradhanush (August 2015)
[85:6:1] - Capital infusion + governance reform for PSBs; **7-point plan (A-G)**: **Appointments, Banks Board Bureau, Capitalisation, De-stressing, Empowerment, Framework of accountability, Governance**.
[85:6:2] - **₹70,000 crore** budget capital over 4 years.
[85:6:3] # Recapitalisation (October 2017)
[85:6:4] - Package **₹2.11 lakh crore**: **₹1.35 lakh crore via recapitalisation bonds**; ~₹58,000 crore raised by banks.
[85:6:5] - **Recap bonds**: Govt gives bonds to PSBs, money returns as equity → **no fresh cash** spent.
[85:6:6] - **4R**: **Recognition, Resolution, Recapitalisation, Reforms**.
[85:6:7] # P.J. Nayak Committee and Banks Board Bureau
[85:6:8] - **P.J. Nayak (2014, RBI)**: weak PSB board governance; proposed a **Bank Investment Company** to hold Govt stake; stake **below 50%**; professional boards.
[85:6:9] - **Banks Board Bureau (BBB)**: proposed by P.J. Nayak, an Indradhanush idea; working from **1 April 2016**; autonomous advisory body.
[85:6:10] - Selects **PSB chairmen/whole-time directors** and boards of PSU insurers and FIs.
[85:6:11] - **Replaced in 2022** by the **Financial Services Institutions Bureau (FSIB)**.

## SUB 9 | Deposit Insurance and Banking Laws (amendment) Act 2025
[85:9:0] # DICGC
[85:9:1] - Set up **1978** by merging the Deposit Insurance Corporation (**1962**) and the Credit Guarantee Corporation; **DICGC Act, 1961**.
[85:9:2] - **Wholly owned RBI subsidiary**.
[85:9:3] - Cover **₹5 lakh per depositor per bank** (raised from ₹1 lakh in **Budget 2020-21**, after the **PMC Bank** crisis).
[85:9:4] - Covers all commercial banks (incl. **Indian branches of foreign banks**), RRBs, co-operative banks; excludes **government, inter-bank and NBFC** deposits.
[85:9:5] - **Premium paid by the insured bank**, not the depositor.
[85:9:6] # Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025
[85:9:7] - In force **1 Aug 2025**.

=== NOTES 3:86 | Non-performing Assets – Causes, Twin Balance Sheet Problem, Recognition and Provisioning
## SUB 0 | Npa Definition and Classification
[86:0:0] - **NPA**: loan or advance whose **interest or principal** stays overdue for **more than 90 days** (APPSC-G1 2022 key).
[86:0:1] - **Farm loans**: short-duration crops = overdue **2 crop seasons**; long-duration crops = **1 crop season**.
[86:0:2] TABLE head: Class | Criterion
  [86:0:2.0] Standard | Regular; no overdue beyond 90 days
  [86:0:2.1] SMA-0 / SMA-1 / SMA-2 | Overdue 1-30 / 31-60 / 61-90 days: early warning, still standard
  [86:0:2.2] Sub-standard | NPA for 12 months or less
  [86:0:2.3] Doubtful | NPA for more than 12 months
  [86:0:2.4] Loss | Loss identified by the bank, auditors or RBI inspection, but not wholly written off
[86:0:3] - Sequence: Standard → SMA-0/1/2 → NPA (sub-standard → doubtful → loss).
[86:0:4] SMA (up to 90 days) is still a **standard** asset, not an NPA.
## SUB 1 | Stress Indicators and Key Terms
[86:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [86:1:0.0] Gross NPA | All NPAs
  [86:1:0.1] Net NPA | Gross NPA − provisions
  [86:1:0.2] Stressed assets | NPAs + restructured loans + written-off assets
  [86:1:0.3] Interest coverage ratio (ICR) | EBIT ÷ interest due; below 1 = cannot cover interest; shows a borrower's present and emerging risk (UPSC 2020)
  [86:1:0.4] Evergreening | New loans to repay old ones → default stays hidden; ended by the AQR
## SUB 2 | Recognition and Provisioning
[86:2:0] - **IRACP** = **Income Recognition, Asset Classification, Provisioning**.
[86:2:1] - **Income recognition**: interest on an NPA is booked as income **only when actually received**.
[86:2:2] - **Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR)** = provisions held ÷ gross NPAs.
[86:2:3] # New Rules from 1 April 2027
[86:2:4] - **Borrower-level tagging**: one NPA loan → all that borrower's loans at the bank become NPAs; **upgrade only after all arrears** on every facility are paid; 90-day test stays; automated identification.
[86:2:5] - **ECL model**: RBI moves from incurred-loss to a forward-looking **3-stage Expected Credit Loss** model (EIR method).
[86:2:6] TABLE head: ECL stage | Provision
  [86:2:6.0] Stage 1 (low risk) | 12-month expected loss
  [86:2:6.1] Stage 2 (risk rises) | Lifetime loss
  [86:2:6.2] Stage 3 (over 90 days) | Full lifetime provision
## SUB 3 | Twin Balance Sheet Problem
[86:3:0] - Meaning: **over-leveraged corporates + capital-starved, NPA-laden banks** at the same time.
[86:3:1] - Named in **Economic Survey 2016-17** (CEA **Arvind Subramanian**).
[86:3:2] - Loan losses eat bank capital → banks stop new loans → firms cannot invest → **private investment falls**.
## SUB 4 | Causes of NPAs
[86:4:0] - **Aggressive lending (2010-15)** to **infrastructure, steel, power** without strict appraisal → stalled projects.
[86:4:1] - Global shocks: 2008 crisis + weak exports. **Evergreening** hid defaults.
[86:4:2] - Vicious cycle: lending + shocks → NPAs → provisioning → capital erosion → weak credit growth.
## SUB 5 | Asset Quality Review and Response
[86:5:0] - **AQR by RBI in 2015**: disclosed NPAs jumped, **peaking in 2018**; exposed the twin balance sheet problem.
[86:5:1] - **4R**: **Recognise, Resolve, Recapitalise, Reform**, with **IBC**, recapitalisation and **NARCL**.
