## SUB 1 | Deciduous, Thorn and Dry Evergreen Forests – Key Trees
[1:0] # Moist deciduous (100–200 cm)
[1:1] - Eastern slopes of the Western Ghats, **Bhabar–Terai/Siwalik strip**, Chota Nagpur, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, NE, A&N.
[1:2] - **Teak is the dominant species** (UPSC 2015); also **sal**, sandalwood, arjun, laurel, rosewood, bamboo, mahua. Teak and sal = products of the **tropical deciduous** forests.
[1:3] # Dry deciduous (70–100 cm)
[1:4] - Interior peninsula, central India, UP–Bihar plains; teak, **sandalwood (south)**, axlewood, red sanders, **tendu, palas, khair**, bamboo.
[1:5] - Most prone to **forest fires**; the dry deciduous forests of the **central Deccan** hold large protected habitats for the **tiger**.
[1:6] # Thorn forests (< 70 cm)
[1:7] - **Open stunted forests with long roots and sharp thorns** – Rajasthan, SW Punjab, W Haryana, Kutch–Saurashtra and the leeward Deccan, i.e. **Western Andhra Pradesh (Rayalaseema)** – not eastern Odisha, NE Tamil Nadu or the Terai.
[1:8] - Species: **acacia/babul**, ber, wild date, euphorbia, cacti, neem, **khejri**.
[1:9] # Tropical dry evergreen
[1:10] - **Coast of Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh** (NE-monsoon rain, ~100 cm); jamun, tamarind, neem.

## SUB 2 | Tropical Evergreen (Rain) Forests and Biodiversity
[2:0] - Tall trees (**> 45 m**, 45–60 m), **multi-layered continuous canopy**, little undergrowth; **lianas, epiphytes**, orchids, canes, bamboo; **leaf litter decomposes fastest** – soil surface almost bare (UPSC 2021).
[2:1] - **Maximum plant diversity and biomass**: "greatest variety of animal and plant species" = **tropical moist (rain) forests** – not temperate deciduous, taiga, savanna, grassland, tundra, mangrove or desert.
[2:2] - In India, maximum tree diversity in the wet evergreen/semi-evergreen forests of the **Western Ghats**, then the **North-East** (ISFR).
[2:3] - Near the **equator** (about 10°N–10°S): no dry season, so the trees do not shed leaves together.
[2:4] - **Where in India:** western slopes of the **Western Ghats** (Kerala, Karnataka), **Assam**, Meghalaya, Manipur, Tripura, Nagaland, **Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram**, **Andaman & Nicobar** – so "rain forest in India" = **Assam and Kerala**; **not Himachal Pradesh**, UP, MP, Chhattisgarh, Odisha.
[2:5] - **Species:** **rosewood, ebony**, mahogany, gurjan, ironwood, jamun, aini, champa, mesua, white cedar, canes; dominant species asked = **ebony** (not sandalwood, juniper, acacia).
[2:6] - **Cinchona** is an evergreen tropical (hill) plant – grown in WB (Darjeeling), Assam, Kerala/TN hills, **not Chhattisgarh**.
[2:7] - **Semi-evergreen:** 200–250 cm; buttressed trunks; laurel, rosewood, mesua, thorny bamboo.

[6:0] # Classification
[6:1] - **H.G. Champion (1935)**, refined with **S.K. Seth (1968)**: **5 major groups, 16 type groups** – Moist Tropical, Dry Tropical, Montane Sub-tropical, Montane Temperate, Alpine.
[6:2] TABLE head: Type | Rainfall | Leaf habit
  [6:2.0] Tropical wet evergreen (rain forest) | > 250 cm (NCERT: > 200 cm), temp > 22–25 °C | Evergreen – no leaf-shedding season
  [6:2.1] Tropical semi-evergreen | 200–250 cm | Mixed evergreen + deciduous
  [6:2.2] Tropical moist deciduous | 100–200 cm | Shed leaves in the dry season
  [6:2.3] Tropical dry deciduous | 70–100 cm | Leafless in the long dry season
  [6:2.4] Tropical thorn | < 70 cm (NCERT < 50 cm) | Open, stunted, xerophytic

[6:3] - **Deciduous** = sheds leaves before winter or in the dry spell (adaptation to moisture shortage); evergreen = no common leaf fall.

## SUB 7 | Forest Policy, Law, Silviculture, Social Forestry and Grasslands
[7:0] # National Forest Policy 1988
[7:1] - Principal aim: **ecological balance** / environmental stability; target **one-third (33%)** of India's area under forest/tree cover (**two-thirds in the hills** – not two-thirds of the country); social forestry, massive afforestation, people's movement incl. women, JFM.
[7:2] - **National Forestry Action Programme** – also aims at one-third under tree/forest cover.

[7:5] # Terms
[7:6] - **Silviculture** = the art and science of cultivating (raising and tending) forest crops.
[7:7] - **Coppice** = regrowth from stool shoots, short rotation; **high forest** = raised from seed, long rotation; **selection system** = trees removed singly or in groups, uneven-aged forest.
[7:8] - **Afforestation** = planting on land never forested; **reforestation** = re-establishing depleted forest.
[7:9] - **Social forestry** (NCERT) = management and protection of forests and afforestation on barren/public land for community benefit – urban, rural (agro/community) and farm forestry; community forestry = trees on public/community land.

## SUB 8 | Forestry Practice – Seeds, Logging, Timber and Forest Fires
[8:0] # Forest fires
[8:1] - **Types:** **ground** fire (burns humus/peat below the litter), **surface** fire (litter and undergrowth – grassland and scrub fires), **crown** fire (runs through the canopy; fastest-spreading and most dangerous in **coniferous** forests, whose resinous needles burn readily).
[8:2] - **Counter fire (back-firing)** = deliberately burning a strip of fuel ahead of the advancing fire, from a control line – the **traditional** method of fighting forest fires; fire lines, alert systems and mobile squads are aids.
[8:3] # Logging and timber
[8:4] - **Salvage logging** = removing trees killed or damaged by fire, wind, insects or disease to recover their timber value.
[8:5] - **Traditional logging tools:** axe, hand (cross-cut) saw, wedges, mauls – the **chainsaw** is a modern powered machine.
[8:6] - **Wood transport:** **rafting** = logs tied together and floated as one unit; **floating** = loose logs one by one; rope-ways and tramways run overland.
[8:7] - **Timber groups (macrostructure):** **hardwood** (broad-leaved angiosperms, e.g. teak, sal) and **softwood** (conifers, e.g. pine, deodar). **Wood anatomy** (cells, grain, density, pores) shows the best way to **process and use** a timber.
[8:8] - **Forest inventory** = systematic collection of forest data (area, stock, growth) for assessment and planning.
[8:9] # Seeds, planting and site
[8:10] - **Recalcitrant seed** (cannot be dried/stored) – **sal (Shorea robusta)**; **orthodox seed** (stores dry) – **babul (Vachellia nilotica)**; **vivipary** (germinates on the tree) – mangrove **Rhizophora mucronata**; **stump planting** – **teak (Tectona grandis)**.
[8:11] - **Timber line (tree line)** = upper altitude or high-latitude limit beyond which trees do not grow.
[8:12] - **Degraded, heavily eroded zone (NW India):** hardy dry species **Acacia modesta, Capparis aphylla (kair), Salvadora oleoides (pilu)** survive there.
