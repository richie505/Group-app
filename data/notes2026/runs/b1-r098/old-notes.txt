=== NOTES 1:98 | Reddi Kingdoms of Addanki, Kondavidu and Rajahmundry – Prolaya Vema Reddi to Kumaragiri Reddi
## SUB 0 | The Reddi Dynasty – Period and Features
[98:0:0] - Reddi kingdom: about **1325–1448 CE** – **Kondaveedu line c. 1325–1424**; **Rajamahendravaram line 1402–1448**.
[98:0:1] - Called the **"second golden age" of Telugu culture** and literature; peak of Telugu literature before Vijayanagara; kings were themselves authors.
[98:0:2] - Lineage: **Panta Vamsam** (also **Deseti kula**); emblem **Bull**; family deity **Mula Guramma**; royal/official language **Sanskrit**.
[98:0:3] - One of three **successor states after 1323** (fall of the Kakatiyas): **Reddis, Musunuris, Recherla Velamas**.
[98:0:4] - Prolaya Vema had served in **Prataparudra's** court and in the court of **Kapaya Nayaka** (Musunuri leader who freed Andhra from Delhi) – recorded in the Anitalli (Kaluvacheru) inscription.
## SUB 1 | Capitals and Forts
[98:1:0] # Capitals
[98:1:1] - Sequence: **Addanki → Kondaveedu (Kondavidu) → Rajamahendravaram (Rajahmundry)**.
[98:1:2] TABLE head: Capital / fort | Region | Feature
  [98:1:2.0] Addanki | Prakasam | First capital, Prolaya Vema
  [98:1:2.1] Kondavidu | Guntur (Guntur–Palnadu, near Chilakaluripet) | Replaced Addanki; later a battleground of Gajapatis and Vijayanagara
  [98:1:2.2] Rajahmundry | Godavari | Second seat, 1402–1448
  [98:1:2.3] Kondapalli | Krishna | Strategic hilltop fort; credited to Prolaya Vema; later held by the Gajapatis
[98:1:3] - **Who shifted Addanki → Kondavidu?** **Anavota (Anapota) Reddi**, son of Prolaya Vema – by the **Manyapuram (Manyamapura) inscription** (APPSC G2 2012 key); **not Anavema**.
[98:1:4] Kondavidu is in the Guntur region – NOT near Eluru (West Godavari) and NOT in East Godavari.
[98:1:5] # Forts
[98:1:6] - **Prolaya Vema Reddi built 74 forts**, including **Kondaveedu, Kondapalli and Dharanikota**.
[98:1:7] - **Kondapalli fort – architecture:** on a **granite hill**; main gate **Dargah Darwaza** (named after the tomb of **Gulab Shah**, killed in battle there); **Golconda Darwaza** at the other end, towards Jaggayyapeta; palace **Tanisha Mahal** between two hills. Came under the **Sultan of Golconda in 1531**.
## SUB 2 | Kondavidu Reddi Rulers – Order and Contribution
[98:2:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Period | Contribution
  [98:2:0.0] Prolaya Vema Reddi (founder) | 1324/1325–1353 | Expelled Tughluq forces from coastal Andhra; patron of Errana; steps to Patala Ganga (1346)
  [98:2:0.1] Anavota (Anapota) Reddi | 1353/54–1364 | Shifted capital to Kondavidu; abolished the gold tax; developed Motupalli; halted the Kalinga Gangas on the Krishna
  [98:2:0.2] Anavema Reddi (greatest) | 1364–1386 | Most charitable Reddi king; beat Kalinga Gajapatis; Veera Siromandapam, Srisailam
  [98:2:0.3] Kumaragiri Reddi | 1386–1402 | Vasantarajiyam; Vasantotsavam; ousted 1402
  [98:2:0.4] Pedakomati Vema Reddi | 1402–1420 | Sangeeta Chintamani; patron of Srinatha
  [98:2:0.5] Racha Vema Reddi (last) | 1420–1424 | Puriti Pannu; killed by his own people
[98:2:1] - **Order (APPSC 2025):** Prolaya Vema → **Anavota → Anavema → Kumaragiri → Pedakomati Vema** → Racha Vema.
[98:2:2] - **Kumaragiri succeeded Anavema.**
[98:2:3] # Prolaya Vema Reddi – Founder
[98:2:4] - **Founded the Reddy kingdom in 1324** (expelled Tughluq forces c. 1325); Panta clan.
[98:2:5] - Brother **Mallareddi (Mallaya Reddy)** took **Motupalli** by defeating Bahmani founder **Hasan Gangu**.
[98:2:6] - Land gifts to Brahmanas: title **Nissivu Bhudana Parasurama**.
[98:2:7] - Errana's **Harivamsam** dedicated to him.
[98:2:8] # Anavema Reddi – Conquests and Officials
[98:2:9] - With help of the **Padmanayakas** and **Vijayanagara**, defeated the **Kalinga Gajapatis**.
[98:2:10] - Took **Nidadavolu** (gave it to Choda Bhima), **Rajahmundry, Pithapuram, Simhachalam, Konaseema** – **not Kandukur**.
[98:2:11] - Minister **Mamidi Peddanamatya**; army chief **Chennamanayaka**.
[98:2:12] - Built **Anavemagiri mandapas at Simhachalam** and **Veera Siromandapam at Srisailam** (APPSC G2 2011).
[98:2:13] - No literary work is credited to Anavema.
[98:2:14] # Kumaragiri Reddi
[98:2:15] - Accession opposed by **Pedakomati Vema** → made him **feudatory of Chandolu and heir**.
[98:2:16] - Brother-in-law and commander **Kataya Vema** conquered the east up to **Cuttack**.
[98:2:17] - **Division:** made **Rajahmundry** a separate division under his son **Anavota**, then **Kataya Vema (1395)**.
[98:2:18] - **1402:** Pedakomati seized Kondavidu; Kumaragiri **fled to Rajahmundry** and died there in refuge.
[98:2:19] - Expert in **Bharata Natya**; court dancer **Lakumadevi**.
[98:2:20] # Pedakomati Vema and Racha Vema
[98:2:21] - **1404:** Pedakomati Vema regained territory from Vijayanagara.
[98:2:22] - **Racha Vema**, son of **Surambika**, cut the **Jaganobbaganda canals** to feed her tank; levied **Puriti Pannu** and was killed as a tyrant (**Kondaveeti Kaifiyat**).
## SUB 3 | Rajahmundry (rajamahendravaram) Reddis and the End of Reddi Rule
[98:3:0] # Rulers
[98:3:1] TABLE head: Ruler | Period | Contribution
  [98:3:1.0] Anavota Reddi (Kumaragiri's son) | to 1395 | First ruler of the division; died young
  [98:3:1.1] Kataya Vema Reddi | 1395–1414 | Independent after 1402; patron of poets; fought Pedakomati all his reign; killed at Kakaraparthi (1414) by Gajaraju Tippa, an ally of Pedakomati
  [98:3:1.2] Allada Reddi (regent) | 1414–c. 1420 | Ran the state for Veerabhadra Reddi
  [98:3:1.3] Veerabhadra Reddi | c. 1414–1448 | Nominal king; restored 1423 and 1429
  [98:3:1.4] Vema (Veda) Reddi | 1423–1448 | Veerabhadra's brother; prime minister and de facto ruler
[98:3:2] - Founders: **Pakanati Reddis** who moved east under Anavota and Anavema.
[98:3:3] - **Allada Reddi – opponents:** **Pedakomati Vema** (attacked Rajahmundry 1414; beat Kataya Vema and Deva Raya I's army at **Attili-Kakaraparthi**); **Annadeva** (took parts of **Vengi** with **Velama** support). Ally: **Vijayanagara** (Deva Raya I backed Kataya Vema). Helpers: sons Vema Reddi, Veerabhadra Reddi, Doddareddi.
[98:3:4] - Rivalry with Kondavidu ended with Kondavidu's fall (1424); Rajahmundry survived **24 more years** as a Vijayanagara feudatory.
[98:3:5] # Queen Anitalli
[98:3:6] - **Anitalli (Anithalli)**: daughter of **Kataya Vema**; wife of **Veerabhadra Reddi** (Allada's second son).
[98:3:7] - Issued the **Kaluvacheru inscription (1423)** after the recapture of Rajahmundry.
[98:3:8] # Gajapati–Vijayanagara Rivalry over Rajahmundry
[98:3:9] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [98:3:9.0] 1420 | Gajapati Ganga Ganapati took Rajahmundry
  [98:3:9.1] 1423 | Veerabhadra and Vema recovered it
  [98:3:9.2] 1429 | Kalinga king Veerabhanu Deva occupied it; Deva Raya II expelled him up to Simhachalam and restored the Reddis as feudatories
  [98:3:9.3] 1435 | Kapileswara Gajapati took Cuttack and pressed on the Reddis
  [98:3:9.4] 1444 | Draksharama inscription: Vijayanagara commander Mallappa Odayar came to help
  [98:3:9.5] 1446–48 | After Deva Raya II's death, Kapileswara Gajapati annexed Rajahmundry
[98:3:10] - Deva Raya II: title **Gajabetekara** ("hunter of elephants"), greatest of the Sangama dynasty; hired Muslim soldiers, especially archers (Ferishta).
[98:3:11] # End of Reddi Rule
[98:3:12] TABLE head: Line | Last ruler | Ended by
  [98:3:12.0] Kondavidu | Racha Vema Reddi | Vijayanagara (Deva Raya II), 1424
  [98:3:12.1] Rajahmundry | Veerabhadra Reddi and Vema Reddi | Kapileswara Gajapati of Orissa, 1448
  [98:3:12.2] Kandukur branch (Mallareddi's line) | – | Deva Raya I reduced them to Vijayanagara feudatories
## SUB 4 | Wars by Opponent

## SUB 5 | Administration and Taxes
[98:5:0] # Village Administration
[98:5:1] - **12 Ayagars** (village servants; Kakatiya pattern): **Karnam** – accountant; **Reddi / Peddakapu** – headman; **Talari** – watchman.
[98:5:2] - Paid in **Manyam** land or **Mera** (crop share); Karnam, Reddi and Talari held village judicial and police powers.
[98:5:3] - Nayakas held military-territorial charges.
[98:5:4] # Taxes
[98:5:5] TABLE head: Tax | Meaning / ruler
  [98:5:5.0] Puriti Pannu | Tax on childbirth – Racha Vema (APPSC G2 2019; PYQ 2018)
  [98:5:5.1] Kanika | Presents/gifts to king or officials
  [98:5:5.2] Pullari | Cattle-grazing tax
  [98:5:5.3] Illari | House tax
  [98:5:5.4] Ari | Land tax
  [98:5:5.5] Gold tax | Abolished by Anavota
[98:5:6] - Inherited Kakatiya taxes: Kanika, Pullari, Pannu, Siddhayam, Sangam, Darshanam, Niruvidi, Ardhayam.
[98:5:7] - Temple lands, Brahmin grants and Ayagar lands were **tax-exempt**.
[98:5:8] Puriti Pannu = Racha Vema (not Kumaragiri or Anavema). One key named Kataya Vema – Racha Vema is the standard answer.
## SUB 6 | Trade – Motupalli Port
[98:6:0] - **Motupalli** = chief Reddi port; captured by Mallaya Reddy; dock developed by **Anavota**.
[98:6:1] - **Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam**: issued by **Somaya**, minister of **Anavota**, to encourage trade.
[98:6:2] - **Motupalli epigraph**: fixes **tariff rates** on imports/exports. (Famine relief = Mahasthan inscription.)
[98:6:3] - **Haravilasam (Srinatha)**: information on **sea-borne trade**, imports and exports (APPSC 2019; PYQ 2018); dedicated to merchant **Avachi Tippayya Setti**.
[98:6:4] - **Komatis** ran inland and coastal trade.
[98:6:5] - Earlier route: Buddhist text **Vinayavatthu** mentions the **"Andhra Path"** (APPSC 2019).
[98:6:6] Assertion-Reason: Haravilasam gives sea-trade information (A) and is dedicated to Tippayya Setti (R) – both true, but R is NOT the explanation of A.
## SUB 7 | Society, Women and Festivals
[98:7:0] # Castes
[98:7:1] - Warrior-farmer backbone: **Kammas, Kapus, Reddis, Velamas**; traders: **Komatis**; Brahmana **agraharas** with tax-free grants.
[98:7:2] # Festivals and Dance
[98:7:3] - **Vasantotsavam** (spring festival) – most popular; celebrated grandly by **Kumaragiri**; funded by **Avachi Tippayya Setti** (recorded in Haravilasam).
[98:7:4] - Court dancer **Lakumadevi** (APPSC G2 2012) – Bharatanatyam, at Kumaragiri's court.
[98:7:5] - **C. Narayana Reddy** wrote **Karpura Vasanta Rayalu** on Kumaragiri.
[98:7:6] - **Jalakaranda** instrument – **Dharmasagara inscription** (PYQ G2 2019). **Ghatika Yantra** (water clock) – **Ghatikagara**.
[98:7:7] # Women and Tanks
[98:7:8] - **Surambika** (Suryambika), queen of **Pedakomati Vema**, dug the **Santana Sagaram** tank (APPSC GS 2018, G2 2019 keys; also Santana Payonidhi); some texts call it **Govardhana Samudram**.
[98:7:9] - **Devadasis**: temple women trained in music and dance; respected at Shiva temples incl. Pancharamas.
[98:7:10] Surambika's tank: if only one of Santana Sagaram or Govardhana Samudram is offered, that is the answer.
## SUB 8 | Religion and Temple Building
[98:8:0] - Kings: **Pashupata Shaivism**; people: **Veerashaivism** (popular religion); Bhairava and Mailara worship.
[98:8:1] - **Ghoderayas** spread Shaivism from **Tripurantakam**; Reddi kings were disciples of Ghoderayacharulu.
[98:8:2] - **Vaishnavism**: **Vadagalai** matha at **Ahobilam**; **Kumaragiri and Kataya Vema** = Vaishnava patrons; Reddis moved from Shaivism towards Vaishnavism.
[98:8:3] TABLE head: Work | Builder
  [98:8:3.0] Steps (Sopanamargas) to Patala Ganga, Srisailam (1346) and to Ahobilam | Prolaya Vema (not Pedakomati)
  [98:8:3.1] Pilgrim rest-houses at Ahobilam and Srisailam; walls of Ahobilam and Mahanandi | Prolaya Vema
  [98:8:3.2] Veera Siromandapam, Srisailam (for Siro-karthanam devotees) | Anavema
  [98:8:3.3] Anavemagiri mandapas, Simhachalam | Anavema
  [98:8:3.4] Amareswara temple, Amaravati restored | Vallaya Vemana, minister of Anavota
[98:8:4] - Pancharamas renovated: **Draksharama Bhimesvara**, **Samarlakota Kumararama**; Bikkavolu temples.

## SUB 10 | Court Poets
[98:10:0] # Errana (Errapragada)
[98:10:1] - Court poet of **Prolaya Vema** (PYQ G2 2011); dedicated **Harivamsam** to him.
[98:10:2] - **Last of the Kavitrayam**; completed the Telugu Mahabharata (**remaining Aranya Parva – Aranya Parva shesham**).
[98:10:3] - Titles **Prabandha Parameswara, Sambhudasa**; wrote **Nrusimha (Narasimha) Puranam** (PYQ G2 2017).
[98:10:4] # Srinatha (c. 1365–1441)
[98:10:5] - Title **Kavi Sarvabhauma**; **Vidyadhikari** (education officer) under **Pedakomati Vema** (PYQ 2002); composed his Sanskrit inscriptions.
[98:10:6] - Defeated **Gauda Dindima Bhatta** of **Deva Raya II's** court → **Kanakabhishekam** and **Ganda Penderam**.
[98:10:7] - After 1424 joined the Rajahmundry Reddis (**Veerabhadra Reddi**) with **Bendapudi Annamatya's** help; after 1448, poverty in Palnadu.
[98:10:8] - Works: **Haravilasam**, **Sringara Naishadham** (from Sriharsha's Naishadhiyacharita; to **Mamidi Singana**), **Kasikhandam**, **Bhimakhandam** (praises **Bhimeswara of Draksharamam**), **Palnati Veera Charitra** (life of Palnadu), Sivaratri Mahatyam, Maruttarat Charitra.
[98:10:9] - **Madanavilasabanam is NOT by Srinatha.**
[98:10:10] - Opened the **Kavya Yuga**; cited as evidence of **paper** use in the 15th century (G2 2012).

=== NOTES 1:99 | Musunuri Nayaks and the Velama (recherla) Chiefs of Rachakonda–devarakonda
## SUB 0 | Fall of Warangal and the Liberation Movement
[99:0:0] - **1323**: Warangal fell; **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** renamed it **Sultanpur (Sultanpurti)**; Andhra under **Malik Burhanuddin**, deputy **Malik Maqbul**.
[99:0:1] - Of Prataparudra's **75** generals only three survived: **Prolaya Nayaka, Recherla Singama, Prolaya Vema Reddi**.
[99:0:2] - **Vimochanodyamam** = **first organised resistance to Muslim rule** in Andhra; Andhra freed within about 2 years of Tughluq misrule (from 1326).
[99:0:3] - **Leader** of the confederacy: **Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka** (APPSC GS 2018; G2 2019).
[99:0:4] - Other chiefs (took part, did **not** lead): Prolaya Vema Reddi, Recherla Singama, **Koppula Prolaya Nayaka**, Kolani Rudra, Vengi Bhupala, Manchikonda Ganapati Nayaka, Kapaya Nayaka.
[99:0:5] - **Kaluvacheru grant (1423)**, issued by Reddi queen **Anitalli**, names **75 Nayakas** who backed Prolaya.
[99:0:6] - Successor states: **Musunuri (1325–1368), Recherla Velama (1325–1475), Reddi (1325–1448)**; alongside Vijayanagara (**1336**) and Bahmanis (**1347**).
## SUB 1 | Musunuri Nayaks
[99:1:0] # Prolaya Nayaka
[99:1:1] - Son of Potinayaka; **Kamma** clan (**Chaturthanvayas**).
[99:1:2] - Freed land between Krishna and Godavari; capital **Rekapalli** (on the Godavari, forest near **Bhadrachalam**) from **1325** (APPSC G2 2017).
[99:1:3] - **Vilasa copper-plate grant (1330)**: Tughluq atrocities, Prolaya's rise, agrarian relief, **agraharas restored** to Brahmins.
[99:1:4] - Childless → succeeded by cousin **Kapaya Nayaka**.
[99:1:5] # Kapaya Nayaka (1336–1368)
[99:1:6] - **1336**: with Hoysala **Veera Ballala III**, Prolaya Vema Reddi and Singama took **Orugallu** from Malik Maqbul; ruled from Warangal.
[99:1:7] - Singama cleared **Nalgonda–Mahabubnagar**; Prolaya Vema cleared **Kammanadu–Pakanadu**.
[99:1:8] - Nephew **Anapota Nayaka** = governor at **Vengi**.
[99:1:9] - **Polavaram inscription**: names 14 members of the lineage, only 3 ruled.
