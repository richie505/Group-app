=== Court Poets – Errana and Srinatha
# Errana (Errapragada)
- **Patron:** court poet of the founder **Prolaya Vema Reddi** (APPSC G2 2011, 2012; APPSC PYQ ×5) – not Anavota, Anavema, Allada Reddi or Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka.
- **Dedication:** his **Harivamsam** is dedicated to **Prolaya Vema**.
- **Kavitrayam:** **last of the Kavitrayam**; completed the Telugu Mahabharata (**remaining Aranya Parva – Aranya Parva shesham**).
- **Works:** **Nrisimha (Narasimha) Puranam** (APPSC G2 2017; PYQ ×2), Harivamsam, Ramayana.
- **Titles:** **Prabandha Parameswara**, **Sambhudasa**.
- **⚠ Trap:** the **Vidyadhikari** (education officer) post belonged to **Srinatha**, not Errana – a stem that calls Errana "Vidyadhikari" still points to his patron Prolaya Vema.
# Srinatha (c. 1365–1441)
- **Patron and office:** **Vidyadhikari** (education officer) under **Pedakomati Vema Reddi** (APPSC G1 2002; G2 2001, 2002; Welfare Officers 2017; PYQ ×2); composed his Sanskrit inscriptions.
- **Title:** **Kavi Sarvabhauma**; also Andhra Bhasha Naishadhabhava, Sarvavidya Parangata.
- **At Vijayanagara:** defeated **Gauda Dindima Bhatta**, court poet of **Deva Raya II** → **Kanakabhishekam**, **Ganda Penderam** and the Kavisarvabhauma honour.
- **Later life:** after 1424 joined the Rajahmundry court of **Veerabhadra Reddi**; after 1448, poverty in Palnadu.
| Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **Haravilasam** | Sea-borne trade; to **Avachi Tippayya Setti** |
| **Palnati Veera Charitra** | Life of the **Palnadu** people (PYQ) |
| **Sringara Naishadham** | From **Sriharsha's Naishadhiyacharita**; to **Mamidi (Madiki) Singana** |
| **Bhimakhandam** (Bhimeswara Puranam) | Praises **Bhimeswara of Draksharamam** |
| Kasikhandam | Religious; to Veerabhadra Reddi's court |
| Sivaratri Mahatyam, Maruttarat Charitra | Other works |
- **Kavya Yuga:** Srinatha is said to have opened the Kavya (prabandha) age; his writings are cited as evidence of **paper** use in the **15th century** (APPSC G2 2012).
- **⚠ Not his:** **Madanavilasabanam** (Naganatha, Anavota's court) is NOT by Srinatha.

=== Society, Festivals and Women
# Vasantotsavam and Dance
- **Vasantotsavam (spring festival):** the **most popular festival** of the Reddi period – colours and scents sprinkled (Holi-like) (APPSC G2 PYQ).
- **Popularised by:** **Kumaragiri Reddi**, who held it every year → title **Karpura Vasantarayalu** (Welfare Officers 2017 ×2); Anavema too was called Vasantaraya.
- **Funding:** merchant **Avachi Tippayya Setti**, made head of the **Sugandha Bhandagara** (scent store) for the festival (recorded in Haravilasam).
- **Court dancer:** **Lakumadevi** – **Bharatanatyam**, at **Kumaragiri's** court (APPSC G2 2012; PYQ ×3) – not Machaladevi, Chellava or Muddupalani.
- **Literature on him:** **C. Narayana Reddy** wrote **Karpura Vasanta Rayalu** on Kumaragiri.
# Women and Tanks
| Woman | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Surambika (Suryambika)** | Queen of **Pedakomati Vema**; dug the **Santana Sagaram (Santana Payonidhi)** tank at **Phirangipuram** (APPSC G2 2019; PYQ ×2) |
| **Lakumadevi** | Court dancer of **Kumaragiri Reddi** |
| **Anitalli** | Issued the **Kaluvacheru** inscription (1423) |
- **Tank inscription:** composed by **Srinatha**; her son **Racha Vema** cut the **Jaganobbaganda** canals to feed it.
- **⚠ Trap:** **Govardhana Samudram**, Anna Samudram and Vemana Samudram are NOT Surambika's tank – the answer is **Santana Sagaram**.
# Social Life
- **Castes:** warrior-farmer backbone **Kammas, Kapus, Reddis, Velamas**; traders **Komatis**; tax-free Brahmin **agraharas**.
- **Customs:** sati, devadasis, cockfights, ram fights, hero worship (Veerapuja); belief in **Parusavedi** (alchemy).
- **Devadasis:** trained in music, dance and ritual; respected performers at **Shiva temples** (incl. Pancharamas).
- **Time:** measured with clocks – the **Ghatika Yantra** (water clock), called **Ghatikagara** in the APPSC key (Telugu "gadiyaram" = clock).
- **Music:** instrument **Jalakaranda** – **Dharmasagara inscription**; desi dances Jakkini, Perani; foreign dance Parasika.

=== Rulers, Capitals and Chronology
# The Reddi Kingdom
- **Period:** about **1325–1448 CE** – **Kondavidu line c. 1325–1424**; **Rajahmundry line 1402–1448**.
- **Successor state:** one of the three states after **1323** (fall of the Kakatiyas) – **Reddis, Musunuris, Recherla Velamas**.
- **Called:** the **"second golden age" of Telugu** culture – kings were themselves authors.
- **Features:** **Panta** clan (Deseti kula); emblem **Bull**; family deity **Mula Guramma**; royal language **Sanskrit**.
# Kondavidu Rulers in Order
| Ruler | Period | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Prolaya Vema Reddi** (founder) | **1325–1353** | Capital **Addanki**; expelled Tughlaq forces; 74 forts; Errana |
| **Anavota (Anapota) Reddi** | 1353–1364 | **Shifted capital to Kondavidu**; developed Motupalli; abolished gold tax |
| **Anavema Reddi** (greatest) | 1364–1386 | Most charitable; beat Kalinga Gajapatis; Veera Siromandapam |
| **Kumaragiri Reddi** | 1386–1402 | Vasantarajiyam; Vasantotsavam; Lakumadevi |
| **Pedakomati Vema Reddi** | 1402–1420 | Sangeeta Chintamani; Srinatha |
| **Racha Vema Reddi** (last) | 1420–1424 | **Puriti Pannu**; killed by his people |
- **Order (APPSC G2 Mains 2025; Welfare Officers):** Prolaya Vema → **Anavota → Anavema → Kumaragiri → Pedakomati Vema** → Racha Vema.
- **Prolaya Vema's reign:** up to **1353** (Mani Kesavara inscription) – the "1325–1353" span (Inter Board 2012).
- **Kumaragiri succeeded Anavema**; Pedakomati occupied Kondavidu (1402) because Kumaragiri was childless.
# Capitals and Forts
- **Sequence:** **Addanki → Kondaveedu (Kondavidu) → Rajamahendravaram (Rajahmundry)**.
- **Who shifted Addanki → Kondavidu:** **Anavota (Anapota) Reddi**, son of Prolaya Vema – **Manyapuram inscription** (APPSC G2 2012; PYQ) – not Anavema, Kumaragiri or Pedakomati.
| Capital / fort | Region | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Addanki | Prakasam | First capital, Prolaya Vema |
| Kondavidu | **Guntur** (near Chilakaluripet) | Replaced Addanki; later fought over by Gajapatis and Vijayanagara |
| Rajahmundry | Godavari | Second seat, 1402–1448 |
| **Kondapalli** | **Krishna** | Hill fort credited to Prolaya Vema |
- **Forts:** Prolaya Vema built **74 forts**, including **Kondaveedu, Kondapalli and Dharanikota**.
- **Kondapalli fort:** main gate **Dargah Darwaza** (tomb of Gulab Shah); **Golconda Darwaza** at the other end; palace **Tanisha Mahal**.
- **⚠ Trap:** Kondavidu is in Guntur – NOT in East Godavari or near Eluru.
# Prolaya Vema Reddi – Founder
- **Career:** served **Prataparudra**, then in **Kapaya Nayaka's** court (Anitalli / Kaluvacheru inscription); founded the kingdom in **1324/1325** after expelling **Tughlaq** forces from coastal Andhra.
- **Brother Mallareddi:** took **Motupalli**, repulsing the Bahmani **Hasan Gangu** (1347).
# Anavema and Kumaragiri
- **Anavema:** with the **Padmanayakas and Vijayanagara** beat the **Kalinga Gajapatis**; took **Nidadavolu** (gave it to Choda Bhima), **Rajahmundry, Pithapuram, Simhachalam, Konaseema** – not Kandukur; minister **Mamidi Peddanamatya**, army chief **Chennamanayaka**; **most charitable** Reddi king.
- **Kumaragiri:** **Pedakomati Vema**, who opposed his accession, was made **feudatory of Chandolu** and heir; brother-in-law **Kataya Vema** conquered the east up to **Cuttack**.
- **Rajahmundry division:** first under his son **Anavota**, then **Kataya Vema (1395)**.
- **1402:** Pedakomati seized Kondavidu; Kumaragiri fled to Rajahmundry.
- **Pedakomati Vema:** recovered territory from **Vijayanagara (1404)**; died fighting the Velamas (1420).
- **Racha Vema:** last Kondavidu king; killed as a tyrant – **Kondaveeti Kaifiyat**.

=== Trade – Motupalli Port and Haravilasam
- **Haravilasam (Srinatha):** the Reddi-period work giving information on **sea-borne (foreign) trade**, imports and exports (APPSC G2 2011 ×2, 2012, 2019; G2 Mains; PYQ) – not Harivamsam, Kasikhandam or Bhimeswara Puranam.
- **Dedicated to:** merchant **Avachi Tippayya Setti**, famous for **overseas trade** (China, Sinhala, Sumatra, Malaya).
- **Assertion–Reason:** Haravilasam gives sea-trade information (A) and is dedicated to Tippayya Setti (R) – **both true, R not the explanation**.
- **Motupalli:** chief Reddi port; taken by **Mallaya Reddi**; dock developed by **Anavota**.
- **Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam:** issued by **Somaya**, minister of **Anavota**, to protect and encourage trade (bilingual Telugu–Tamil; lower duties).
- **Motupalli epigraph:** fixes **tariff rates** on port trade.
- **Imports:** Chinese goods, sandalwood, camphor, spices; elephants from Sri Lanka.
- **Traders:** **Komatis** ran inland and coastal trade; trade centres Penugonda, Kondaveedu, Rajahmundry, Draksharama.
- **Earlier route:** the Buddhist **Vinayavatthu** mentions the **"Andhra Path"**.

=== Religion and Temple Building
- **Popular religion:** **Veerashaivism** (APPSC G2 2011; PYQ); the kings patronised **Pashupata Shaivism** – the tempting wrong option.
- **Ghoderayas:** Shaiva gurus who spread Shaivism from **Tripurantakam**; the Reddi kings were their disciples.
- **Vaishnavism:** **Vadagalai** matha at **Ahobilam**; **Kumaragiri and Kataya Vema** were Vaishnava patrons.
- **Srisailam practices:** Bhrugupatham (leaping from the hill); **Champudu Gullu** at Tripurantakam.
| Work | Builder |
|---|---|
| **Veera Siromandapam, Srisailam** (for Siro-karthanam devotees) | **Anavema** (APPSC G2 2011; PYQ) |
| Anavemagiri mandapas, **Simhachalam** | **Anavema** |
| Steps (Sopanamargas) to **Patala Ganga, Srisailam** (1346) and to Ahobilam | **Prolaya Vema** (not Pedakomati) |
| Pilgrim rest-houses at Ahobilam and Srisailam; walls of Ahobilam and Mahanandi | **Prolaya Vema** |
| Amareswara temple, Amaravati restored | **Vallaya Vemana**, minister of Anavota |

=== Administration and Taxes
- **Puriti Pannu:** tax on **childbirth**, levied by **Racha Vema**, the last Kondavidu king, who was killed by his people for it (APPSC G2 2019; PYQ ×2) – not Kumaragiri, Kataya Vema or Anavota.
| Tax | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Puriti Pannu** | Childbirth – Racha Vema |
| **Kanika** | Presents / gifts to king or officials |
| **Pullari** | Cattle-grazing tax (Kakatiya usage) |
| **Illari** | House tax |
| **Ari** | Land tax |
| Meruka | Tax on wetlands |
| Gold tax | **Abolished by Anavota** |
- **Land revenue:** Bhumi Sistu – the main income; land measured with the Kesarpati gada.
- **Village:** **12 Ayagars** – **Karnam** (accountant), **Reddi / Peddakapu** (headman), **Talari** (watchman); paid in **Manyam** land or **Mera** (crop share).
- **Exempt:** temple lands, Brahmin grants and Ayagar lands.

=== Rajahmundry Reddis, Wars and the End of Reddi Rule
| Ruler | Period | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Anavota (Kumaragiri's son) | to 1395 | First ruler of the division |
| **Kataya Vema Reddi** | 1395–1414 | Fought Pedakomati; killed at **Kakaraparthi (1414)** by **Gajaraju Tippa** |
| **Allada Reddi** (regent) | 1414–c. 1420 | Ran the state for **Veerabhadra Reddi** |
| Veerabhadra Reddi | c. 1414–1448 | Nominal king; husband of **Anitalli** |
| **Vema (Veda) Reddi** | 1423–1448 | Prime minister and **de facto ruler** |
- **Anitalli:** daughter of **Kataya Vema**, wife of **Veerabhadra Reddi**; issued the **Kaluvacheru** inscription after the **recapture of Rajahmundry** (1423).
- **Allada Reddi's conflict:** **Deva Raya I** backed Kataya Vema against Kondavidu; **Annadeva** took parts of Vengi with **Velama** support.
# Wars by Opponent
| Opponent | Event |
|---|---|
| Velamas | **Battle of Bhimavaram** – Reddis expelled the Velamas from **Palnadu** (APPSC G2 2019 key) |
| Bahmanis | Mallareddi took Motupalli from Hasan Gangu; **Gajarao Tippa** beat Firoz Shah's army at **Kambhammetta (1396)** |
| Vijayanagara | Kataya Vema beat **Harihara II**; Sarpavaram treaty – Harihara's daughter **Vijayalakshmi** married Kataya's son |
| Gajapatis | Anavema beat the Kalinga Gajapatis; **Kapileswara** annexed Rajahmundry (1446–48) |
# Rajahmundry Between Gajapati and Vijayanagara
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1420** | Gajapati **Ganga Ganapati** took Rajahmundry |
| 1423 | Veerabhadra and Vema recovered it |
| 1429 | Veerabhanu Deva occupied it; **Deva Raya II** expelled him up to Simhachalam and restored the Reddis as feudatories |
| 1446–48 | **Kapileswara Gajapati** annexed Rajahmundry |
- **Deva Raya II:** title **Gajabetekara** ("hunter of elephants"); enlisted Muslim **archers** (Ferishta).
# End of Reddi Rule
| Line | Ended by |
|---|---|
| Kondavidu | **Vijayanagara (Deva Raya II), 1424** → Rajahmundry survived **24 more years** as a Vijayanagara feudatory |
| Rajahmundry | **Kapileswara Gajapati of Orissa, 1448** |
| Kandukur branch | **Deva Raya I** reduced them to feudatories |
