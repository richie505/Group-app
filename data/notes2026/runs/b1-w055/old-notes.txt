=== NOTES 1:55 | Vishnukundin Society, Religion, Administration and Coinage
## SUB 0 | Ghatikas – Education
[55:0:0] - **Ghatikas** = **educational institutions** – higher colleges of **Sanskrit and Vedic** learning (G2 1993; NTR Univ. JA) – not ports, tanks, dance centres, viharas or bathing ghats.
[55:0:1] - **First mention** in Andhra: **Indrabhattaraka Varma's** inscriptions (Ghatika-vapta-punyasanchaya); special Ghatikas at **Amaravati and Vengi**.
[55:0:2] - **Medium of instruction:** **Sanskrit** – also in the colleges attached to the large temples of South India (G1 PYQ).
[55:0:3] - **Pallava** educational institutions were also called **Ghatikas** (G2 2012) – e.g. the Kanchi ghatika; not Mathas, Vidyapithas or Gacchas.
[55:0:4] - **14 vidyas** = 4 Vedas + 6 Vedangas + Mimamsa + Nyaya + Dharmashastra + Puranas.
[55:0:5] - **Agraharas** to Vedic Brahmins were centres of learning.
[55:0:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Vishnukundis p.7, p.12, p.15; app notes 1:55 (sub 5)
## SUB 1 | Religion
[55:1:0] - **Vedic revival:** Madhava Varma II performed **11 Ashvamedhas, 16 Rajasuyas, 1 Purushamedha**.
[55:1:1] - **Family deity: Sriparvataswami** (Srisaila Mallikarjuna); kings styled "Sriparvataswami-padanudhyata"; titles Paramamaheswara, Parama Brahmanya.
[55:1:2] - **Ganapati** worship began: **Dantamukha Swami** installed at **Velpuru** in MV II's **33rd** year.

  [55:1:3.2] Village Irundhera to the same vihara | Vikramendra Varma II (Tummalagudem-2)
[55:1:4] - **Madhava Varma II** = "**destroyer of Buddhism**".
[55:1:5] - **Jainism declined** as **Kapalika Shaivism** rose; Jain shrines (e.g. Srisailam) converted; **Kumarilabhatta** attacked Jainism.

## SUB 0 | Undavalli and Mogalrajapuram – Which Dynasty
[56:0:0] - **Undavalli, Mogalrajapuram and Vijayawada (Indrakeeladri) cave temples** = **Vishnukundins** (G2 2019; G2 Mains 2025; Welfare Officers 2017 ×2; APPSC PYQ ×3) – not Ikshvakus, Salankayanas, Pallavas, Rashtrakutas, Cholas, Chalukyas, Kakatiyas or Reddi kings.
[56:0:1] - **Brahmanical (Hindu) caves** of Mogalrajapuram and Undavalli are assigned to the Vishnukundins; **Madhava Varma I** is credited with Undavalli, Bhairavakonda and Mogalrajapuram.
[56:0:2] - **Vishnukundins** = **first Hindu cave temples in the Telugu states**; **Guptas** = earliest Hindu cave temples in India (**Udayagiri**, Madhya Pradesh).

## SUB 1 | Undavalli Caves
[56:1:0] - **District:** **Guntur** (Endowment EO 2022) – not Krishna, NTR or Prakasam; the caves face **Vijayawada** across the Krishna (Boilers 2019: "near Vijayawada").
[56:1:1] - **Four storeys**, **three main caves**; carved **top-down** – upper levels finely finished, ground floor **crude and unfinished**.
[56:1:2] - Centrepiece: **monolithic reclining Vishnu** (**Ananta Padmanabha Swamy**), about 5 m; a Buddha in padmasana was later turned into Vishnu; **Purnakumbha** motif.
[56:1:3] - **Changing use:** **Jain abode → Buddhist monks → Hindu temple**.
[56:1:4] - **⚠ Trap:** on the **Krishna**, not the Godavari; "built by the Cholas as a Hindu temple, later Buddhist" is false.
[56:1:5] - **In news:** AP's two-day **Telugu Mahotsavam** at Undavalli Caves, **25–26 July 2026**.
[56:1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Vishnukundis p.15; app notes 1:56 (sub 1)
## SUB 2 | Bhairavakonda Caves
[56:2:0] - **Eight rock-cut caves**, all dedicated to **Shiva** (G2 2012) – not 4, 9 or 12; a centre of **Shaivism** (Kapalika sect).
[56:2:1] - **Location:** listed under **Nellore** district in NextGen/Rocket and the G2 2012 stem; on the ground in the **Nallamala hills** on the Nellore–Prakasam border.
[56:2:2] - **Attribution debate:** Madhava Varma I (Vishnukundin) vs **Pallava period (7th–8th c.)**; closely resembles the early Pallava caves of **Mahabalipuram**.
[56:2:3] - Pillars: **lion-based** with **kumbha (pot) capitals**.

=== NOTES 1:58 | Sangam Age – the Three Sangams, Sources; Tolkappiyam, Ettuthogai, Pattupattu, Pathinenkilkanakku
## SUB 0 | Twin Epics – Silappadikaram and Manimekalai
[58:0:0] # Silappadikaram – the first Tamil epic
[58:0:1] - **Author:** **Ilango Adigal** – a **Jain** ascetic, younger brother of Chera **Senguttuvan**, grandson (by tradition) of Chola **Karikala** (UPPSC 2002 ×2).
[58:0:2] - **Story:** merchant **Kovalan** of Puhar neglects his wife **Kannagi** for the dancer **Madhavi**; in Madurai he is **wrongly accused of stealing the queen's anklet** and executed by the **Pandya** king; Kannagi burns **Madurai** (APPSC FSO).
[58:0:3] - **Jaina elements** in the storyline (e.g. the Jain nun Kavunthi Adigal) (CDS 2016) – not a Rama story, Sri Lankan Buddhism or Shakti cult.
[58:0:4] - **River praised:** the **Kaveri (Cauvery)** (CDS 2017).
[58:0:5] # Manimekalai – the Buddhist sequel
[58:0:6] - **Author:** **Sittalai Sattanar (Sathanar)**, a Buddhist poet (CDS 2018).
[58:0:7] - **Story:** **Manimekalai**, daughter of Kovalan and the dancer Madhavi, becomes a **Buddhist nun** (IAS 2002).
[58:0:8] # The five Tamil epics (post-Sangam, c. 4th–6th c. CE)
[58:0:9] TABLE head: Epic | Author / faith
  [58:0:9.0] Silappadikaram | Ilango Adigal (Jain)
  [58:0:9.1] Manimekalai | Sittalai Sattanar (Buddhist)
  [58:0:9.2] Jivaka (Sivaga) Chintamani | Tiruttakkadevar (Jain ascetic); called Mana Nool (Book of Marriages)
  [58:0:9.3] Valayapathi, Kundalakesi | –
[58:0:10] - **Twin epics** of early Tamil literature = **Silappadikaram and Manimekalai** (UPPSC 2005); Tolkappiyam and Kural are not epics.
[58:0:11] > Exam angle: author, anklet story, Jaina elements, river Kaveri, dancing girl → nun.

## SUB 1 | Tolkappiyam and Tirukkural
[58:1:0] # Tolkappiyam
[58:1:1] - **Tolkappiyam** by **Tolkappiyar** = the **oldest extant Tamil grammar** – a work on **grammar and poetics** (UPPSC 1997, 2014; Jharkhand 2023); the only surviving work of the **Second Sangam**.
[58:1:2] - **Three parts:** **Ezhuttu, Col, Porul**; also gives the **tinai** scheme and the **four castes**.
[58:1:3] # Tirukkural
[58:1:4] - **Tirukkural (Kural)** by **Tiruvalluvar** – **Tamil**, **1,330 couplets** on philosophy and ethics in three books: **Aram** (virtue), **Porul** (wealth), **Inbam** (love) (MPPSC 1997).
[58:1:5] - **Other names:** **Laghuveda**, **Tamil Veda**, **Ulaga Podhumarai** (UPRO 2013 ×2) – not Kalingattuparani, Periyapuranam or Nandikkalambakam.
[58:1:6] - Part of the post-Sangam **Pathinenkilkanakku**.

  [58:1:7.0] Tirukkural | Philosophy
  [58:1:7.1] Tolkappiyam | Grammar
  [58:1:7.2] Silappadikaram | Love story
  [58:1:7.3] Manimekalai | Merchant story

## SUB 2 | Nature of Sangam Literature – Language, Themes, Anthologies
[58:2:0] # Language and content
[58:2:1] - **Language:** old **Tamil** (APPSC Gazetted 2010); the contemporary Satavahanas used **Prakrit**.
[58:2:2] - About **2,400 poems** by some 450 poets, about 30 of them **women** (Avvaiyar the most prolific).
[58:2:3] - **Varna was known** to Sangam poets, though society was not rigidly stratified; the poems are rich in **material culture** and **warrior ethic** (UPSC 2022 ×2).
[58:2:4] - **Silent on the trade routes** of ancient India – they describe ports and goods; the **Milindapanha** and **Jatakas** describe routes (State PSC).
[58:2:5] - **Mamulanar**, a Sangam poet, refers to the **Mauryan expedition** against a Tamil chieftain (IAS 2001).
[58:2:6] TABLE head: Type | Theme
  [58:2:6.0] Aham (interior) | Love, personal emotion, nature
  [58:2:6.1] Puram (exterior) | War, valour, royal deeds, ethics
[58:2:7] # The corpus
[58:2:8] TABLE head: Group | Count | Note
  [58:2:8.0] Ettuthogai | 8 anthologies | Purananuru, Akananuru, Padirruppattu (on the Chera kings)
  [58:2:8.1] Pattupattu | 10 idylls | Pattinappalai (Puhar and Karikala), Maduraikanchi, Mullaippattu
  [58:2:8.2] Pathinenkilkanakku | 18 minor works | Didactic, includes Tirukkural; post-Sangam (4th–6th c.)
[58:2:9] - **Melkanakku** = Ettuthogai + Pattupattu = the **18 major** works; both divided into **aham and puram**.
[58:2:10] - **A–R (UPSC 2000):** the aham and puram poems of the Padinenkilkanakku **continue** Sangam composition, and they are classed as **post-Sangam** works as against the Sangam works proper – both true, R explains A (PMF key; some keys give "R not the explanation").
[58:2:11] # Chronology
[58:2:12] - Accepted span **c. 300 BCE – 300 CE**; **Tamil-Brahmi** of the **3rd c. BCE** supports the early limit – not 1000–500 BCE.
[58:2:13] - Tirukkural, the epics and Pathinenkilkanakku are **post-Sangam**, not the earliest layer.

[58:3:0] # Assemblies of poets
[58:3:1] - **Sangam** = an **assembly of Tamil poets** ("Conference of Poets"), held under **Pandya** patronage – not the Buddhist Sangha or the Sangama dynasty (APPSC G1 2003).
[58:3:2] - The tradition comes from the preface of **Iraiyanar Agapporul**; the account is traditional, not historical.

  [58:3:3.0] First | Then Madurai (submerged) | Agastya | Nothing

  [58:3:3.2] Third | Madurai | Nakkirar | Ettuthogai, Pattupattu
[58:3:4] - **Third Sangam = Madurai** (UPPCS 2006) – not Arikamedu, Ernakulam or Tuticorin.

## SUB 0 | Indo-Roman Trade – Arikamedu, Muziris, Yavanapriya
[60:0:0] # Arikamedu – the east-coast Roman station
[60:0:1] - **Arikamedu** (near **Puducherry**) = **Poduke** of the Periplus; the **foremost Indo-Roman trading station of eastern India** (State PSC; APPSC ASO 2011; APPSC PYQ).
[60:0:2] - **Finds:** Roman **amphorae**, red-glazed **Arretine ware**, rouletted ware, beads and gems, an **intaglio of Augustus**, Tamil-Brahmi potsherds – proof of brisk India–Rome trade in the early Christian era (UPPCS 2003; UP Lower 2009).
[60:0:3] - **Excavated** by **Sir Mortimer Wheeler** in **1945**.
[60:0:4] - **Amphora** = a **tall double-handled jar** for **wine and oil**; found at Arikamedu, Kodumanal, Uraiyur, Alagankulam (UPPSC 2014) – not painted grey or black-and-red ware.
[60:0:5] # Muziris – the chief centre
[60:0:6] - **Muziris (Mushiri, Muchiri)**, the Chera pepper port, = the **chief centre of Indo-Roman trade** on the Indian side; Pliny's "first emporium of India" (APPSC G1). Alexandria was only the Roman-side entrepot.
[60:0:7] - Identified with **Pattanam** (**Ernakulam**, Kerala) by KCHR excavations (Jun 2026: still continuing).
[60:0:8] # Goods
[60:0:9] TABLE head: Exports | Imports
  [60:0:9.0] Pepper and spices, cotton and muslin, pearls, ivory, precious stones | Gold, horses, sweet wine, Roman pottery; Roman coins
[60:0:10] - **Yavanapriya** ("dear to the Yavanas") = **black pepper** (UPSC 1995 ×3) – not muslin, ivory or dancers.
[60:0:11] - **Sangam economy (statements):** pearls and precious stones were main exports; the Tamils traded with **Egypt and Arabia**; **decline of trade** helped the decay of the Tamil kingdoms; **Uraiyur**, not Madurai, was famous for **cotton**.
[60:0:12] # Evidence and peak
[60:0:13] TABLE head: Source | Evidence
  [60:0:13.0] Periplus of the Erythraean Sea | c. 60 CE, anonymous Greek merchant – chief account
  [60:0:13.1] Pliny the Elder | Rome lost 55 million sesterces a year to India
  [60:0:13.2] Coin hoards | Gold and silver of Augustus, Tiberius and Nero; hoards at Akkanpalle, Kondapur
[60:0:14] - **Peak:** **Satavahana–Sangam age** (1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE); trade grew after Rome took **Egypt (30 BCE)** and the monsoon route was used; it did **not** flourish after the Satavahanas (G2 2025).
[60:0:15] - **Indian embassies to Rome:** best known – the **Pandya ruler to Augustus**.
[60:0:16] - Tamil ports traded with **Berenike** and **Myos Hormos** on the **Egyptian Red Sea coast**.

## SUB 0 | Allahabad Prasasti – Harisena, the Pillar and Its Inscriptions
[61:0:0] # The prashasti
[61:0:1] - **Allahabad Pillar inscription (Prayaga Prashasti)** = the chief source for **Samudragupta**: his conquests and personality (UPSC 2006 ×2; UP Lower 2004; SSC 2019; APPSC Draughtsman).
[61:0:2] - **Composer:** **Harisena**, court poet, who was at once **Kumaramatya, Sandhivigrahika** (minister of peace and war) and **Mahadandanayaka** (SI Mains 2017) – not Ravikirti (Aihole, Pulakesin II).

[61:0:4] - Harisena of the prashasti is **not** the later Jain Harisena of the **Brihatkatha Kosha**.
[61:0:5] # The pillar

## SUB 3 | Chandragupta I and the Early Guptas
[61:3:0] # Origin and order
[61:3:1] - **Sri Gupta** = **founder of the dynasty** (SSC 2015); ruled a small area of **North Bengal and South Bihar**; succeeded by **Ghatotkacha**.
[61:3:2] - **Origin** debated: Vaishya, later treated as Kshatriya; a Brahmana origin argued from the **Dharana gotra** of Prabhavatigupta – the Junagadh inscription does not settle it.
[61:3:3] - **Early territory:** Magadha, **Prayag**, eastern UP (**Vayu Purana**).
[61:3:4] - **Order:** Sri Gupta → Ghatotkacha → Chandragupta I → Samudragupta → **Ramagupta** → Chandragupta II → Kumaragupta I → Skandagupta.
[61:3:5] # Chandragupta I (c. 319–335 CE)
[61:3:6] - **Real founder of the empire**; capital **Pataliputra**.
[61:3:7] - **Gupta era (Gupta Samvat):** **319–320 CE** = his accession (MPPCS 1991; SSC 2006; APPSC G1) – not Sri Gupta.
[61:3:8] - **Maharajadhiraja:** the first Gupta to take it (SSC 2018).
[61:3:9] - **Marriage:** **Kumaradevi**, a **Lichchhavi** princess – gave legitimacy and power.
[61:3:10] - **First Gupta coins:** gold **king-queen type** (Chandragupta I with Kumaradevi, legend "Lichchhavayah") (UPPCS 2010) – no coins of Sri Gupta or Ghatotkacha are known.
[61:3:11]   - **⚠ Settled:** one NextGen page says Chandragupta I "defeated Saka satraps" and "first minted silver coins" – wrong: both belong to **Chandragupta II** (PMF, NextGen p.4, p.7); Chandragupta I did **not** defeat the Sakas (APPSC G1 statement I false).
[61:3:12] # Gupta coins
[61:3:13] - Mostly **gold** (dinaras): show rulers, deities and rituals. **Samudragupta's types:** **lyrist (veena)**, **Ashvamedha** (to celebrate his conquests after the horse sacrifice), **archer**; the **king-queen** type is Chandragupta I's.
[61:3:14] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval India Part 3 p.55-56, p.60; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 3 p.2, p.4, p.7, p.17; app notes 1:61 (sub 1, 2, 3, 6)
[61:3:15] - **Gupta capitals:** **Pataliputra**, with Ayodhya, Prayaga and **Ujjayini** (Chandragupta II) also named – never Varanasi.

=== NOTES 1:62 | Chandragupta II Vikramaditya, Kumaragupta, Skandagupta; the Hunas and Gupta Decline
## SUB 0 | Chandragupta II Vikramaditya – Saka Conquest, Silver Coins, Titles and Fa-hien
[62:0:0] # Accession – the Ramagupta episode
[62:0:1] - **Killed his elder brother:** **Chandragupta II** took the throne after killing his elder brother **Ramagupta** and married his widow **Dhruvadevi (Dhruvaswamini)** – story in **Vishakhadatta's Devichandraguptam** (State PSC).
[62:0:2]   - Ramagupta, defeated by the Sakas, had agreed to surrender Dhruvadevi to the Saka king; Chandragupta II, in disguise, killed the Saka king, then Ramagupta.
[62:0:3] - **Order:** Samudragupta → (Ramagupta, brief and disputed) → Chandragupta II, c. **380–415 CE** (NextGen 380–413; PMF 380–412).
[62:0:4] # Saka conquest and Ujjain
[62:0:5] - **Saka-conqueror (Sakari):** Chandragupta II defeated **Rudrasimha III**, the last Western Kshatrapa (Saka) ruler, and annexed **western Malwa and Gujarat** with the western sea ports (UP UDA/LDA 2010).
[62:0:6] - **Second capital:** **Ujjain (Ujjayini)** (RPSC 2008) – Malwa and Saurashtra are regions, Mehrauli the site of his iron pillar.
[62:0:7] - **Evidence:** the **Udayagiri cave** inscription of his minister **Virasena**, and above all his **silver coins**.
[62:0:8] # Silver coins – the strongest evidence
[62:0:9] - **First Gupta to issue silver coins:** **Chandragupta II**, after the Saka conquest, on the model of the Western Kshatrapa silver drachms (UP UDA/LDA Mains 2010; MPPSC 2023). Earlier Guptas issued gold.
[62:0:10]   - ⚠ NextGen Indian History 03 p.2 says "Chandragupta I was the first Gupta king to mint silver coins" – an error: PMF, the app notes and numismatic sources credit Chandragupta II.
[62:0:11] - **UPPCS 2022 statements:** the silver coins are the **strongest evidence of the Saka victory**, and they weigh about **30–33 grains** like the Saka drachms → **both** statements correct.
[62:0:12] - **Coin name:** silver = **rupaka**; gold = **dinara**. Lion-slayer coins gave the title **Simhavikrama** (his father = tiger-slayer coins).
[62:0:13] # Titles and names
[62:0:14] TABLE head: Title / name | Note
  [62:0:14.0] Vikramaditya | Linked with the Saka victory (SSC 2010); also used by Skandagupta
  [62:0:14.1] Sakari | "Destroyer of the Sakas"
  [62:0:14.2] Simhavikrama | Lion-slayer coins
  [62:0:14.3] Devagupta, Devaraja, Devashri | Devagupta in Sanchi and Vakataka records; Devaraja at Sanchi (UPPCS Mains 2007)
[62:0:15] # Marriage alliances
[62:0:16] TABLE head: Alliance | Detail
  [62:0:16.0] Vakataka | Daughter Prabhavati Gupta married Rudrasena II; she ruled the Vakataka kingdom as regent for her sons (RAS 2023; JPSC 2023) – indirect Gupta control of the Deccan
  [62:0:16.1] Naga | Married the Naga princess Kuberanaga (Prabhavati's mother)
  [62:0:16.2] Dhruvadevi | Married after killing Ramagupta
[62:0:17] # Fa-hien and the court
[62:0:18] - **Fa-hien (Faxian):** came **c. 399–412 CE**, in the reign of **Chandragupta II** (UPSC 2025; State PSC) – not Samudragupta, Kumaragupta I or Skandagupta; he **did not name the king** – his interest was Buddhism. First of the three Chinese pilgrims (later **Xuan Zang**, **I-Qing**, 7th c.).
[62:0:19] - **Navaratnas** (tradition): **Kalidasa, Amarasimha, Varahamihira**, Dhanvantari, Vararuchi, Shanku, Ghatakarpara, Kshapanaka, Vetalabhatta – Fa-hien was **not** one of them.
[62:0:20] - **Not his minister:** **Harishena** was Samudragupta's court poet (Prayag prashasti), not Chandragupta II's minister.

## SUB 1 | The Hunas – Skandagupta, Toramana, Mihirakula and Yashodharman
[62:1:0] # Who the Hunas were
[62:1:1] - **Hunas = Hephthalites / White Huns** of Central Asia; strong in the **Oxus** valley, they entered India by taking **Gandhara** (mid-5th c.). **Toramana** was a **Huna** (IAS 1995) – not a Saka, Scythian or Yuezhi.
[62:1:2] # Waves of invasion
[62:1:3] TABLE head: Wave / leader | Gupta reign | Outcome
  [62:1:3.0] First attack | End of Kumaragupta I's reign | Checked by prince Skandagupta
  [62:1:3.1] Main invasion | Skandagupta (c. 455–467) | Hunas repulsed twice; title Vikramaditya
  [62:1:3.2] Toramana (second invasion) | Weak successors, late 5th c. | Conquered up to Eran; Eran boar inscription of his 1st regnal year
  [62:1:3.3] Mihirakula (Toramana's son, c. 515) | Narasimhagupta | Tyrant, persecuted Buddhists; defeated by Yashodharman
[62:1:4] - **Gupta ruler who defeated the Hunas:** **Skandagupta** (UPPSC Lower 2015; UPPCS Mains 2004; State PSC) – not Samudragupta, Chandragupta II, Ramagupta or Bhanugupta.
[62:1:5] - **Reign of the Huna invasion:** **Skandagupta** (UPPCS Mains 2006); the **Guptas** were the dynasty most troubled by the Hunas (UPPCS 1993).
[62:1:6] - **"First Huna invasion faced by" (APSC 2017):** the usual key is **Skandagupta** – PMF: Skandagupta "was the one who really faced the Hunas invasion"; NextGen adds that the first attack came when Kumaragupta I was very old and was repulsed by prince Skandagupta.
[62:1:7] # Records
[62:1:8] TABLE head: Inscription | Records
  [62:1:8.0] Bhitari pillar (Saidpur, Ghazipur, UP) | Skandagupta's victories over the Pushyamitras and Hunas (UPRO 2014) – not Allahabad, Mandsaur or Udayagiri
  [62:1:8.1] Junagadh (Girnar) | Skandagupta's governor Parnadatta repaired the Sudarshana lake
  [62:1:8.2] Eran boar (Varaha) | Huna rule over Eran in Toramana's first regnal year
  [62:1:8.3] Gwalior | Record of Mihirakula's rule, including Gwalior
  [62:1:8.4] Sondani / Mandsaur pillars | Yashodharman's victory over Mihirakula; Sondani composed by Vasula (MPPSC 2024)
[62:1:9] # Yashodharman of Mandasor
[62:1:10] - **Defeated Mihirakula:** **Yashodharman**, the **Aulikara** king of Malwa (Mandasor), in **c. 528 CE** (UPPSC 2016 ×2) – not Budhagupta, Shashanka or Prabhakaravardhana; **Narasimhagupta Baladitya** and the Maukharis also fought him.
[62:1:11] - **Victory pillars at Sondani:** c. **532 CE**; his rise **weakened** Gupta authority further.
[62:1:12] - **Skandagupta:** gold coins with **bow and arrow, Garuda standard and Lakshmi**; last great Gupta; governors became **hereditary feudatories**.
[62:1:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 03 p.5-6; PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.64, p.81-82; Rocket Indian History 01 (Bhitari → Skandagupta → Hunas); app notes 1:62 (sub 3, 4, 5)
[62:1:14] - **Skandagupta's wars:** the **Pushyamitras** and **Hunas** (Bhitari) and Vakataka pressure – **no Kalinga conquest** is recorded.

## SUB 3 | Gupta Decline and the Successor States
[62:3:0] # Causes of decline
[62:3:1] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [62:3:1.0] Huna invasions | Drained treasury and army; Hunas held Punjab, Rajasthan, Malwa by c. 485–500
  [62:3:1.1] Weak successors | After Skandagupta
  [62:3:1.2] Feudal set-up | Land grants and hereditary governors created feudatories who broke away (Yashodharman, Maitrakas)
  [62:3:1.3] Economic | Land grants cut revenue – no professional army; loss of western India cut trade income; silk weavers left Gujarat for Malwa
  [62:3:1.4] Buddhism of later Guptas | Listed as a cause in some books (pacifist policy)
[62:3:2] - **NOT a cause:** the **Arab invasion** (UKPCS 2012) – the Arabs came much later (Sindh 712 CE).
[62:3:3] - **Signs of collapse:** later gold coins had **less gold, more alloy**; coins slowly replaced by **payments in land**; no Gupta coins or inscriptions in **western Malwa and Saurashtra** after Skandagupta.
[62:3:4] # Successor states before Harsha
[62:3:5] TABLE head: Power | Seat | Exam fact
  [62:3:5.0] Later Guptas (Guptas of Magadha) | Magadha | Not the imperial line
  [62:3:5.1] Maukharis | Kannauj | Ishanavarman (Haraha), Ishvaravarman (Jaunpur), Sarvavarman
  [62:3:5.2] Maitrakas | Valabhi (Gujarat) | Rulers Dharapatta, Guhasena (APPSC 2023)
  [62:3:5.3] Pushyabhutis | Thanesar (Haryana), later Kannauj | Harsha's family
  [62:3:5.4] Aulikaras | Mandsaur | Yashodharman
[62:3:6] - **UPSC 2021:** between the Guptas and Harsha the north was held by the **Guptas of Magadha, Pushyabhutis of Thanesar, Maukharis of Kannauj and Maitrakas of Valabhi** (1, 3, 4, 6) – **not** the **Paramaras of Malwa** (9th–14th c.) or the **Yadavas of Devagiri** (12th–14th c.).
[62:3:7] # Later Gupta and Maukhari records (UPPCS 2022)
[62:3:8] TABLE head: Record | Ruler
  [62:3:8.0] Asirgarh copper seal (MP) | Maukhari Sarvavarman – so "Sarvavarman – Gaya copper plate" is the wrong pair
  [62:3:8.1] Jaunpur stone inscription | Ishvaravarman
  [62:3:8.2] Haraha stone inscription | Ishanavarman – also records his defeat of the Vishnukundin Indrabhattaraka Varma
  [62:3:8.3] Deobaranark inscription | Jivitagupta II (Later Gupta)
[62:3:9] - **Other neighbours:** **Vakatakas** (Vidarbha; Harishena patronised Ajanta), **Kadambas** of Banavasi (founded by **Mayurasharma**), **Vishnukundins** of Andhra (**Madhava Varma II**, 11 Ashvamedhas).
[62:3:10] - **Outcome:** the decline marked a transition from a single empire to **regional kingdoms and a feudal order** – not a return to Mauryan-style centralisation.
