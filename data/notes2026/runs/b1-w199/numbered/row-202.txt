## SUB 0 | Partition of Bengal, 1905 – Dates, Curzon, Motive, Annulment
[0:0] # Key facts
[0:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [0:1.0] Viceroy | Lord Curzon (1899-1905) – not Minto, Hardinge, Lytton or Napier
  [0:1.1] First proposal | December 1903 – Moderate protests 1903-05
  [0:1.2] Announced | 19 July 1905 (order issued; published 20 July)
  [0:1.3] Took effect | 16 October 1905 – not 15 October
  [0:1.4] New province | Eastern Bengal and Assam (Dacca, Chittagong, Rajshahi divisions + Assam), capital Dacca, 31 million
  [0:1.5] Rest of Bengal | West Bengal + Bihar + Orissa, 54 million – 18 million Bengalis, 36 million Biharis and Oriyas (Bengalis a minority)
  [0:1.6] Lt Governor of Bengal | Sir Andrew Fraser (1903-08), active in framing the partition
  [0:1.7] Annulled | 12 December 1911, Delhi Durbar of King George V – capital also shifted Calcutta → Delhi
  [0:1.8] Viceroy at annulment | Lord Hardinge II
[0:2] - **Stated motive:** **administrative convenience** – province of about 80 million "too big"; Curzon called it a readjustment of boundaries.
[0:3] - **Real motive:** **weaken Bengali nationalism** – "the nerve centre of Indian nationalism" (Curzon) – and **divide Hindus and Muslims** (Curzon at Dacca wooed the Muslims).
[0:4] - **H.H. Risley:** "**Bengal united is a power; Bengal divided will pull in several different ways.**"
[0:5] - **Surendranath Banerjea:** the announcement "**fell like a bombshell**"; he and K.K. Mitra **led the anti-partition agitation**.
[0:6] - **NDA 2013 statement:** Curzon **separated Dacca, Chittagong and Rajshahi divisions from Bengal and joined them to Assam** ✓; he did **not** succeed Canning, introduce the subsidiary alliance (Wellesley) or sympathise with the Congress.
[0:7] - **Impact:** the partition was the event with the **maximum impact on the freedom movement** of the options usually offered (Prince of Wales 1875, Delhi Durbar 1877, ICS 1861).
[0:8] - **Durbars:** 1877 (Lytton, Victoria Kaiser-i-Hind), **1903 (Curzon, Edward VII's coronation)**, **1911 (George V – the only one a sovereign attended; annulment)**. Japan's victory over Russia (1904-05) fuelled the anger.
[0:9] > Traps: annulment **1911** (not 1910, 1912 or 1947); it did not last until WWI, the CDM or 1947. "First partition of Bengal" = 1905 (the second = 1947).

## SUB 1 | Launch of the Swadeshi Movement – Cause, 7 August 1905, Boycott
[1:0] TABLE head: Date | Event
  [1:0.0] 13 July 1905 | K.K. (Krishna Kumar) Mitra first suggested boycott of British goods in his weekly Sanjibani
  [1:0.1] 19/20 July 1905 | Partition announced
  [1:0.2] 7 August 1905 | Calcutta Town Hall meeting – boycott resolution passed; formal proclamation of the Swadeshi Movement; boycott adopted as national policy
  [1:0.3] 16 October 1905 | Partition took effect – day of mourning, fasting, hartal, Ganga bath, Raksha Bandhan, Federation Hall foundation
[1:1] - **Immediate cause:** the **partition of Bengal by Curzon** – not Tilak's sentence, the Lajpat Rai–Ajit Singh deportation (1907) or the Chapekar sentence; not Curzon's universities or municipal measures.
[1:2] - **First use:** **Swadeshi and Boycott** were adopted as methods of struggle for the **first time** in the **anti-partition agitation** – not Home Rule, NCM or the Simon boycott.
[1:3] - **Movement born of the partition:** the **Swadeshi Movement** (1905-1911) – not NCM, CDM, Quit India or "Purna Swaraj".
[1:4] - **National Handloom Day = 7 August**, observed since **2015**, commemorates the **Swadeshi Movement** launched on 7 Aug 1905.
[1:5] - **Phases:** Moderates' petitions and meetings (1903-05: S.N. Banerjea, K.K. Mitra, Prithwishchandra Ray) → **Extremists take over from 1906** (passive resistance, Swaraj).
[1:6] - **Gandhi** had **no part** in it – he was in South Africa; Khilafat, Individual Satyagraha and Quit India were his movements.
[1:7] > "Launched / proclaimed" = **7 Aug 1905**; "took effect" = **16 Oct 1905**; "announced" = **19 July 1905** (BPSC key; many books say 20 July).
[1:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.55-57 (book p.447-449); NextGen Modern India L2 part 6 p.1-2; Spectrum (Ahir) Ch12 pdf p.294-295
## SUB 2 | Programme, Samitis, National Education and Swadeshi Enterprises
[2:0] # Programme
[2:1] - Programmes against the partition: **Boycott, Swadeshi, National education** (plus passive resistance) – **NOT Non-cooperation** (1920).
[2:2] - **Boycott:** foreign cloth, salt, sugar; bonfires of foreign cloth; picketing; priests refused foreign sugar, washermen foreign clothes – boycott was the **most successful** form.
[2:3] - **Samitis:** **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti** of **Barisal** – **Ashwini Kumar Dutt** (school teacher), who led the **Muslim peasants of Barisal** – not Liaquat Hussain, Pulin Das, Surya Sen or Barindra Ghosh.
[2:4] - **Mass mobilisation:** public meetings, processions, jatras, melas, folk songs, akharas and **lathi clubs**; Tilak's Ganapati and Shivaji festivals.
[2:5] - **Atmashakti** (self-reliance) – constructive work; the movement was **not "exclusively political"** – it reshaped art, literature, science and industry.
[2:6] - Swadeshi = India's **first mass movement**; Barisal Samiti also used **magic lanterns** and **arbitration courts** (old notes); Tilak started Ganapati (1893) and **Shivaji (1895)** festivals.
[2:7] - **Calcutta INC 1906 (Dadabhai Naoroji):** **Swaraj** declared the goal; resolutions on Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education.

[2:15] - **Participation:** students and **women** took an active part (first time); **some prominent Muslims** joined; the **Muslim peasantry and peasants in general stayed away** (Barisal an exception); it was **not limited to Bengal**.
[2:16] - **British journalist H.W. Nevinson** covered the Swadeshi Movement and Surat 1907 (The New Spirit in India).
[2:17] - **Sumit Sarkar** – author of *The Swadeshi Movement in Bengal 1903-1908*.

## SUB 3 | Swadeshi in Art, Literature and Song
[3:0] TABLE head: Field | Person | Work / fact
  [3:0.0] Song | Rabindranath Tagore | Amar Sonar Bangla (1905) – later national anthem of Bangladesh
  [3:0.1] Song | Bankim Chandra Chatterjee | Bande Mataram (Anandamath) – theme song / slogan of the Swadeshi agitation
  [3:0.2] Painting | Abanindranath Tagore | Bharat Mata (1905) – first painted image of Bharat Mata; Bengal School – revivalistic style, wash technique
  [3:0.3] Art society | Abanindranath and Gaganendranath Tagore | Indian Society of Oriental Art, Calcutta 1907; Nandalal Bose its first scholar
  [3:0.4] Book | Sakharam Ganesh Deuskar | Desher Katha (1904) – banned 1910
  [3:0.5] Other songs | Rajanikanta Sen, Dwijendralal Ray, Mukunda Das | Swadeshi songs
[3:1] - **Desher Katha (UPSC 2020):** warned against the colonial **"hypnotic conquest of the mind"** ✓; inspired **swadeshi street plays and folk songs** ✓; "desh" meant **India, not Bengal alone** ✗ → **1 and 2 only**.
[3:2] - **Tagore:** "**Gurudev**"; first Asian Nobel laureate (1913); preached **Atmashakti**; **critic of the Swadeshi Movement's economic side** (Ghare Baire) and pleaded for **East-West understanding**.
[3:3] - **Tagore's poetry, in order:** **Manasi (1890) → Sonar Tari (1894) → Gitanjali (1910) → Gitimalya (1914) → Balaka (1916)**.
[3:4] - **Vande Mataram** was the slogan of the **Swadeshi / anti-partition** movement – not 1857, NCM, Champaran, Rowlatt or Quit India.
[3:5] > Rabindranath did **not** found the Indian Society of Oriental Art; Nandalal Bose, Asit Haldar and Amrita Sher-Gil are distractors.
[3:6] - **Related art facts (old notes):** **Nandalal Bose** led the illumination of the original Constitution manuscript; **Raja Ravi Varma** set up a lithographic press at Bombay (1894).
