=== Dadabhai Naoroji – the Grand Old Man of India
# Life and firsts
- **Title:** **Grand Old Man of India**; Parsi educator, trader and India's **first economic thinker**.
- **Professor:** In **1854** the **first Indian to become a full professor** (mathematics and natural philosophy, **Elphinstone College**, Bombay); later Professor of **Gujarati at University College, London**.
- **Women's education:** Through the **Students' Literary and Scientific Society (1848)** he **pioneered women's education** in Bombay; its vernacular branch, the **Gyan (Dnyan) Prasarak Mandali**, spread knowledge among adults (UPSC 2009).
- **Diwan of Baroda:** 1874.

|---|---|---|

| **London Indian Society** | **1865** | With **W.C. Bonnerjee** |
| **East India Association** | **1866, London** | To put India's case before the British public; first lecture **2 May 1867** |

- **Quote:** Britain was "**bleeding India white**". The line "**Kings are made for the public, the public is not made for the King**" is credited to Naoroji in exam keys (UPPCS 2015).
# British Parliament

- In **1893** he moved a bill in the Commons for **simultaneous ICS examinations**; he served on the **Welby Commission (1895)**.

|---|---|

> Naoroji: first full Indian professor, first Indian MP (1892, Liberal), thrice INC president, Swaraj 1906, drain theory.

=== G.K. Gokhale and the Servants of India Society
# Gokhale (1866-1915)
- **Born** 1866 near **Ratnagiri**; graduate of Elphinstone College (**1884**, at 18); taught at **Fergusson College**, Poona.
- **Career (UPSC 1997 biography):** associate editor of **Sudharak** (Agarkar's paper), **Secretary of the Poona Sarvajanik Sabha**, Secretary of the Congress, witness before the **Welby Commission**, provincial and imperial legislator, **INC President at 39**.
- **Guru chain:** **M.G. Ranade → Gokhale → Gandhi**; Gandhi called Gokhale his **political guru** ("Gokhale – My Political Guru").
- **Deccan Sabha (1896):** founded by **Gokhale** under Ranade when Tilak's group captured the Sarvajanik Sabha.
- **President, Banaras Congress, 1905** (21st session) – condemned the **partition of Bengal** and Curzon's rule, backed **Swadeshi**.
- **Compared Curzon's rule to that of Aurangzeb.**
- **Refused a knighthood** (recommended by Hardinge) and **a seat in the Council of the Secretary of State for India** (UPSC 2008).
- Told Gandhi to spend his first year in India "**with his ears open but his mouth shut**"; Gandhi returned in **1915** on his advice.

=== Economic Critique of Colonialism – Drain of Wealth
# The critique
- **Greatest contribution of the Moderates** – "the most important contribution to the development of the national movement"; it **broke the myth of British benevolence**.
- **Drain theory:** **Dadabhai Naoroji** (*Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*) – he laid the **economic foundation of Indian nationalism**; the INC adopted the drain theory at **Calcutta, 1896**.
- **Channels of the drain:** **Home Charges**, **salaries and pensions** of British officials, interest on public debt, profits and dividends, unequal trade → India's wealth transferred to **Britain**.
# Home Charges (UPSC 2011)
- Spent in England for India: **India Office in London**, **salaries, pensions and furlough pay of British civil and military personnel**, interest on India's debt.
- **Wars outside India** were charged to Indian revenues but as military/"extraordinary" charges, **not Home Charges** → answer **I and II only**.

=== Moderate Phase – Leaders, Methods and Demands

# Methods
- **Constitutional agitation** within the law – **3 Ps: Prayers, Petitions, Protests**; resolutions, meetings, pamphlets.
- Faith in **British justice and fair play**; British rule seen as "**providential**".
- Two-pronged: build opinion in India and **persuade the British public**.
- **Distrusted mass action** – no non-cooperation, passive resistance or civil disobedience.
# Demands
| Area | Demands |
|---|---|
| Constitutional | **Larger councils with elected members and control over the budget**; self-government **within the Empire** |
| Administrative | **Indianisation of the services**; **simultaneous ICS exams** in India and England; higher ICS age limit; **separation of the judiciary from the executive** |
| Economic | **Lower land revenue**; **abolition of the salt tax**; **cut in military expenditure**; tariff protection for industry |
| Civil rights | Free speech, press, association; **repeal of the Arms Act**; protested **Tilak's arrest** and the **deportation of the Natu brothers** without trial |
- **NOT a Moderate demand:** **Purna Swaraj / complete independence** (NDA 2010).
# Limitations
- Annual session lasted **only about three days**; **no machinery between sessions**.
- **Narrow social base** – urban, educated; most leaders came from **urban areas**.
- **Partition of Bengal (1905)** exposed their "**naive faith**" in British justice.
- Social reform: Moderates backed the **Age of Consent Bill**; **Tilak opposed** it.
- Critics: Aurobindo – "begging institution"; Tilak – "Rights are not begged, they are claimed".

=== Achievements and the Official Attitude
# Achievements
- **Pan-Indian national identity**; **economic critique** of colonial rule; training in **democratic politics** and civil liberties – but **not complete independence**.
- Supported the **democratic and scientific culture of modern Europe** and **strongly criticised the economic side** of imperialism – both true (RPSC 2024).
# Indian Councils Act, 1892
- Passed under Viceroy **Lansdowne**.
- **More non-official members**, chosen on **recommendation** of municipal and district boards, universities, chambers of commerce – **indirect election** introduced.
- Members could **discuss the budget but not vote on or amend it**; could **ask questions**.
- INC found it **inadequate**: **no direct vote**, **official majority** kept.
# Welby Commission (1895)
- **Royal Commission on Indian Expenditure** (1895) – the commonly known name; **Naoroji** was its first Indian member, **Wedderburn** also sat on it and **Gokhale** gave evidence.
# What the Viceroys said
| Official | Remark |
|---|---|
| **Dufferin** (1887-88) | Congress represents only a "**microscopic minority**" of the people |
| **Curzon** (letter, 1900) | "The Congress is **tottering to its fall**, and one of my great ambitions while in India is to assist it to a **peaceful demise**" |
- **Curzon:** **youngest Viceroy** (1899, aged 39).
