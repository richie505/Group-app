=== Moderates vs Extremists – Leaders, Methods and Ideology
# Who were the Extremists
- **Extremists** = **Militant / Assertive Nationalists** (Neo-nationalists, **Garam Dal**); trend from the **1890s**, concrete by **1905**; dominated the movement **after 1906**.
- **Four pillars:** **Lal-Bal-Pal + Ghosh** – **Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Aurobindo Ghosh**; Tilak the most outstanding.
| Regional group | Leader |
|---|---|
| Maharashtra | **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** (with Vishnu Shastri Chiplunkar) |
| Bengal | **Bipin Chandra Pal**, **Aurobindo Ghosh** (also Raj Narain Bose, Ashwini Kumar Datta) |
| Punjab | **Lala Lajpat Rai** (with Ajit Singh) |
| Delhi (Swadeshi) | **Syed Haider Raza** |
- **Gujarat** was NOT a centre of Extremism (APPSC G1 2002 odd one out).
- **Moderates (never Extremists):** Dadabhai Naoroji, **Gokhale** (their leader), Pherozeshah Mehta, S.N. Banerjea, M.G. Ranade, G.V. Joshi, W.C. Bonnerjee, R.C. Dutt.
> Statement trap: "Tilak, Pal, Lajpat Rai and Aurobindo were Moderates" ✗.
# How they differed
| Basis | Moderates (1885-1905) | Extremists (1905 onwards) |
|---|---|---|
| Faith in British | Believed in British justice, "providential mission" | **No faith in British fairness or generosity**; rule exploitative |
| Goal | Reforms; self-government within the Empire, gradual | **Swaraj** – complete freedom, by own effort |
| Method | **Constitutional agitation**; the **3 Ps – prayer, petition, protest** | **Boycott, Swadeshi, national education, passive resistance**, mass agitation; **3 Ss – self-reliance, self-sacrifice, strong will** |
| Masses | Not ready for politics | Immense faith in the masses |
| Inspiration | Western liberal thought | Indian history, culture, **Hindu traditional symbols** |
| Social base | Zamindars, upper middle class | Educated middle and lower middle class |
- **Fundamental difference:** **belief in British fairness and the preferred mode of struggle** (not social base or princely states).
- **Extremist ideology (UPSC 1998):** **obtaining self-government by aggressive means** in place of petitions and constitutional ways.
- They called Moderate methods **"political mendicancy"**; **Tilak** attacked the "politics of **prayer, petition and protest**", and the prayer-and-petition policy ended under his lead.
- Remarks on the Congress: "**begging institute**" – credited to **B.C. Pal** in most keys (NextGen p.371 credits Aurobindo; Pal: Congress "playing with bubbles"); Tilak: "Rights are not begged, they are claimed".
- **Passive resistance** doctrine – **Aurobindo** (series in *Bande Mataram*, 1907); **Swadeshi** – Aurobindo the main Bengal leader.
- **Word Swaraj:** first used by **Dayanand Saraswati**; **Deuskar** brought it to Bengal (1902); as a political demand it is **Tilak's** ("Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it").
- **Atmashakti** = self-reliance (Swadeshi enterprises, national schools); **spiritual nationalism** saw the nation as a **mother goddess** (Bande Mataram, Bhavani Mandir).
- **Contribution:** turned nationalism into a **mass movement** and made **Swaraj** its goal.
- **Criticism:** Hindu revivalist symbols (Ganapati, Shivaji festivals, Bhavani) and **harping on the Hindu past** kept most **Muslims** away; politically radical but socially conservative.

=== Bal Gangadhar Tilak (1856-1920)
# Life and titles
- Born **23 July 1856, Ratnagiri**; with Agarkar and Chiplunkar – New English School (1880), **Deccan Education Society** (1884), **Fergusson College** (1885).
- Joined the Congress **1890**; **never INC President** (Lajpat Rai, Besant, Motilal, Naoroji, Gokhale, Bose, Kripalani, Sanjiva Reddy, M.A. Ansari all were).
| Title | Given by / when |
|---|---|
| **Lokmanya** ("accepted by the people as their leader") | by the people, after his **1897 sedition trial** (Rand murder by the Chapekar brothers – revolutionary phase) |
| **Father of Indian Unrest** | **Valentine Chirol**, *Indian Unrest* (1910); Tilak sued him for libel |
| Father of the Indian National Movement | popular title in quiz books |
- **Radical in politics, conservative in social matters** – opposed the **Age of Consent Bill (1891)**.
# Newspapers, festivals, books
- **Kesari** (**Marathi – vernacular**) and **Mahratta** (English), both **1881**.
- Made **Ganapati festival** a public, political festival (**1893**) and started **Shivaji festival** (**1895**).
- Slogan: "**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it**"; Kesari: Swarajya the trunk, Swadeshi and Boycott the branches.
- Books: **Gita Rahasya** (Marathi, written in **Mandalay jail**); **The Arctic Home in the Vedas** (1903 – Arctic as the **original home of the Aryans**); *The Orion* (1893).
- Quote (1918): "**If God were to tolerate untouchability, I would not recognise him as God at all.**"
# Trials and death
| Year | Sedition trial |
|---|---|
| 1897 | **18 months**; **Max Muller** pleaded for him ("my interest in Tilak is that of a Sanskrit scholar") |
| 1908 | Kesari articles on the Muzaffarpur bomb → **6 years in Mandalay (Burma)**; released **June 1914** |
| 1916 | Acquitted in the High Court |
- **Home Rule League:** **28 April 1916** (Belgaum; president **Joseph Baptista**).
- Died **1 August 1920** – the day the **Non-Cooperation Movement** was launched; bier lifted by **Gandhi, Shaukat Ali** and Saifuddin Kitchlew.
> Not Tilak's: **Satya Shodhak Samaj** (Jyotiba Phule, 1873); Bombay Chronicle (Pherozeshah Mehta).

=== Surat Split 1907 – Run-up, Causes and Aftermath
# Run-up
| Session | President | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Benaras 1905 | **Gokhale** | Mild resolution condemning partition; supported Swadeshi-Boycott in Bengal |
| Calcutta 1906 | **Dadabhai Naoroji** | Split averted; goal = "**swarajya or self-government**" like the UK colonies; **four resolutions – Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education** |
| Surat 1907 (26 Dec) | **Rash Behari Ghosh** (Moderates' choice) | **Split** |
- **Annulment of the partition** was **NOT** one of the four Calcutta resolutions (UPSC 2010).
# The split
- **Extremists (Garam Dal, led by Tilak):** session at **Nagpur**, **Tilak or Lajpat Rai** president, keep the four resolutions.
- **Moderates (Naram Dal – Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta):** Surat (host-province rule kept Tilak out), Rash Behari Ghosh president, drop the resolutions.
- **Main reason (UPSC 2016):** **Extremists' lack of faith in the Moderates' capacity to negotiate with the British**.
- Differences were over **strategy, objectives and mass participation** – all of these; the **Swadeshi Movement** produced the split.
- **First split of the INC = Surat, December 1907**; scuffle, session suspended; the Moderates pushed the Extremists out and kept the Congress machinery.
- **A-R (UPSC 1998):** the 1905 partition did **not** end the Moderates' role (A ✗); Surat separated the two wings (R ✓).
# Aftermath and repression
| Act | Year |
|---|---|
| Seditious Meetings Act | **1907** |
| Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act | **1908** |
| Criminal Law Amendment Act; Explosive Substances Act | **1908** |
| Indian Press Act | **1910** |
- Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh deported to Mandalay (May 1907); Tilak to Mandalay (1908); Ashwini Kumar Dutt, K.K. Mitra deported (1908); Aurobindo and Pal retired → Moderates dominated by 1910.
- **Andhra Tilak** = **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao**, jailed 1908.
- **Reunion: Lucknow, 1916.**
# Chronology
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1773 | Regulating Act |
| 1905 | Partition of Bengal; Swadeshi (7 Aug); 16 Oct mourning day |
| 1906 | Muslim League (Dacca); Calcutta session |
| 1907 | Surat Split |
| 1909 | Morley-Minto Reforms |
| 1911 | Partition annulled |
| 1916 | Home Rule Leagues (Tilak and Besant); Lucknow Pact |
| 1918 | Kheda Satyagraha |
- **Extremist phase events (APPSC G1):** only **Swadeshi** and **Surat Split**; Lucknow Pact and Home Rule came later.

=== Lala Lajpat Rai (1865-1928)
- **Punjab Kesari / Sher-e-Punjab** (Lion of Punjab); Arya Samaj leader; helped found the **DAV School, Lahore** (1886).
- Helped found **Punjab National Bank** (Lahore, **1894**).
- Political guru: **Mazzini**; wrote biographies of **Mazzini, Garibaldi, Shivaji, Shri Krishna**, Dayanand.
- Deported to **Mandalay** with **Ajit Singh** (May 1907); led protests against Punjab repression.
- **USA 1917-20:** **Indian Home Rule League of America** (1917); *Young India* (1916); later elected to the **Central Assembly**.
- **Only one of Lal-Bal-Pal to be INC President** – **Calcutta special session, 1920**.
- Works: **Unhappy India** (1928, reply to Katherine Mayo's *Mother India*), *The Story of My Deportation* (1908), *England's Debt to India*.
- Papers: Urdu daily **Bande Mataram** and English weekly **The People** (Lahore, **1920**); The Punjabee.
- First used the **safety-valve theory** (in *Young India*) to attack the Moderates – Congress was Dufferin's idea; "the Congress movement was **neither inspired by the people, nor devised or planned by them**".
- Called Congress sessions the "**Annual National Festival of Educated Indians**".
- Slogan "**Go back Simon**"; lathi-charged at **Lahore** (30 Oct 1928), died **17 November 1928** (Saunders shot by Bhagat Singh and Rajguru).

=== Aurobindo Ghosh and Bipin Chandra Pal
# Aurobindo Ghosh (15 Aug 1872 – 5 Dec 1950)
- **New Lamps for Old** (**1893-94**): articles in **Indu Prakash** (Bombay) – systematic critique of Moderate politics; Congress out of touch with the proletariat.
- Editor of **Bande Mataram** (1906) and **Karmayogin**; first principal of **Bengal National College** (1906); main Swadeshi leader in Bengal.
- **Alipore Bomb Case** (1908, Maniktala Garden raid): defended by **Chittaranjan Das**; acquitted 1909; retired to **Pondicherry** (1910) – **Sri Aurobindo Ashram**.
- Sayings: "**Political freedom is the life-breath of a nation**"; "The first principle of true teaching is that **nothing can be taught**" (*A System of National Education*).
- Works: **The Life Divine**, *Savitri*, *Essays on the Gita*, **The Foundations of Indian Culture**.
- Titles: **Prophet of Indian Nationalism**; chief ideologue of the Extremists.
# Bipin Chandra Pal (1858-1932)
- **Brahmo Samaj** member and social reformer; weekly **New India (1901)**; Bande Mataram (1906, Aurobindo editor); *The Soul of India*.
- "**Father of Revolutionary Thoughts**"; imprisoned 6 months (1907) for refusing to give evidence against Aurobindo – **not deported to Mandalay**; in England 1908-11.
- **April 1907: toured Andhra** – the Vande Mataram movement.
