## SUB 0 | Founding of the INC, 1885 – Who, When, Where
[199:0:0] # The core facts
[199:0:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [199:0:1.0] Year | 1885 (not 1883, 1886 or 1888)
  [199:0:1.1] Founder | A.O. Hume, a retired ICS officer (civil servant) – "Father/founder of the INC"
  [199:0:1.2] First President | W.C. (Womesh Chandra) Bonnerjee – not Hume, not S.N. Banerjea
  [199:0:1.3] General Secretary | A.O. Hume (1885-1906)
  [199:0:1.4] Dates | 28-31 December 1885
  [199:0:1.5] Venue | Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay
  [199:0:1.6] Delegates | 72 (only 2 Muslims); mainly lawyers, journalists and teachers
  [199:0:1.7] Viceroy | Lord Dufferin (1884-88) – not Ripon (left 1884), Lytton or Mayo
[199:0:2] - **Earlier name:** Hume's **Indian National Union** (1884) became the INC; the name **"Indian National Congress"** was suggested by **Dadabhai Naoroji** (not W.C. Bonnerjee).
[199:0:3] - **Prelude:** after the **Theosophical Convention at Madras (Dec 1884)** a private meeting of **17** people planned an all-India body.
[199:0:4] - **Venue shift:** planned for **Poona**, moved to Bombay at the last minute because of **cholera** in Poona (not plague).
[199:0:5] - **Absent:** **Surendranath Banerjea** and other Bengal leaders – busy with the **2nd Indian National Conference** at Calcutta (Dec 1885).
[199:0:6] - **Government officials** (e.g. some Indian officials) attended the first session as observers – NDA statement "some officials were present" ✓.
[199:0:7] - **Merger:** the **Indian National Conference** of the **Indian Association** (S.N. Banerjea, A.M. Bose; conferences 1883, 1885) **merged with the INC in 1886**.
[199:0:8] - **Dufferin's attitude:** Hume convinced him **not to obstruct** the Congress; he may have suggested an annual forum but did not found it; soon hostile – "**microscopic minority**" (1888), "factory of sedition".

## SUB 5 | A.O. Hume and the Safety-valve Debate
[199:5:0] # A.O. Hume
[199:5:1] - **Retired ICS officer** (retired **1882**); demoted in **1879** after a clash with **Lytton**; settled at **Shimla**.
[199:5:2] - Drew the idea of local self-government from **Ripon**; closer to the **Bombay-Poona** leaders than to Calcutta.
[199:5:3] - Founded the **Indian National Union**; **first General Secretary** of the INC.
[199:5:4] - Title: **"Father of Indian Ornithology"**.
[199:5:5] # Theories of the INC's origin
[199:5:6] TABLE head: Theory / view | Person
  [199:5:6.0] Safety-valve – first mentioned | William Wedderburn, biography of Hume (1913)
  [199:5:6.1] Safety-valve – used against the Moderates | Lala Lajpat Rai (*Young India*, 1916)
  [199:5:6.2] Conspiracy theory (Marxist) – to abort a popular uprising | R.P. Dutt (*India Today*)
  [199:5:6.3] "Colonial myth"; Hume a lightning conductor | Bipan Chandra (*India's Struggle for Independence*)
  [199:5:6.4] "No Indian could have started the Congress…" | G.K. Gokhale
[199:5:7] - **Basis claimed:** seven volumes of **secret reports** Hume read at **Simla in 1878**.
[199:5:8] - **Lightning conductor:** Indian leaders used Hume to shield the young body from official hostility.

## SUB 6 | Aims, Demands and Early Character
[199:6:0] - **Aims stated by W.C. Bonnerjee (1885):** (a) personal intimacy and **friendship among workers** in the national cause; (b) removing **race, creed and provincial prejudices** – **national unity**; (c) recording the opinions of the educated classes on **pressing (social) questions**; (d) laying down the **lines of political work** for the next 12 months.
[199:6:1] - **Exam answer:** the Congress kept **social issues off its platform** (Naoroji's rule at the 1886 session: only national questions) – so "to express views on social issues" is the option books treat as NOT an objective.
[199:6:2] - **First-session demands:** **simultaneous ICS examinations** in England and India; **elected** (not nominated) councils; **opposition to the annexation of Upper Burma**; **cut in military expenditure** – NOT complete provincial autonomy.
[199:6:3] - **Early demand:** **separation of the Executive and the Judiciary**.
[199:6:4] - **Character:** the **first all-India political platform**; urban, English-educated middle class – not a mass peasant body.
[199:6:5] ~~ Sources: oldspeeches.blogspot.com (Bonnerjee's 1885 address); NextGen Indian History APPSC 06 p.369-370; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.417-418; Rocket Indian History 02 #102 p.58

## SUB 0 | G.K. Gokhale and the Servants of India Society
[200:0:0] # Gokhale (1866-1915)
[200:0:1] - **Born** 1866 near **Ratnagiri**; graduate of Elphinstone College (**1884**, at 18); taught at **Fergusson College**, Poona.
[200:0:2] - **Career (UPSC 1997 biography):** associate editor of **Sudharak** (Agarkar's paper), **Secretary of the Poona Sarvajanik Sabha**, Secretary of the Congress, witness before the **Welby Commission**, provincial and imperial legislator, **INC President at 39**.
[200:0:3] - **Guru chain:** **M.G. Ranade → Gokhale → Gandhi**; Gandhi called Gokhale his **political guru** ("Gokhale – My Political Guru").
[200:0:4] - **Deccan Sabha (1896):** founded by **Gokhale** under Ranade when Tilak's group captured the Sarvajanik Sabha.
[200:0:5] - **President, Banaras Congress, 1905** (21st session) – condemned the **partition of Bengal** and Curzon's rule, backed **Swadeshi**.
[200:0:6] - **Compared Curzon's rule to that of Aurangzeb.**
[200:0:7] - **Refused a knighthood** (recommended by Hardinge) and **a seat in the Council of the Secretary of State for India** (UPSC 2008).
[200:0:8] - Told Gandhi to spend his first year in India "**with his ears open but his mouth shut**"; Gandhi returned in **1915** on his advice.

## SUB 1 | Dadabhai Naoroji – the Grand Old Man of India
[200:1:0] # Life and firsts
[200:1:1] - **Title:** **Grand Old Man of India**; Parsi educator, trader and India's **first economic thinker**.
[200:1:2] - **Professor:** In **1854** the **first Indian to become a full professor** (mathematics and natural philosophy, **Elphinstone College**, Bombay); later Professor of **Gujarati at University College, London**.
[200:1:3] - **Women's education:** Through the **Students' Literary and Scientific Society (1848)** he **pioneered women's education** in Bombay; its vernacular branch, the **Gyan (Dnyan) Prasarak Mandali**, spread knowledge among adults (UPSC 2009).

[202:0:0] TABLE head: Date | Event
  [202:0:0.0] 13 July 1905 | K.K. (Krishna Kumar) Mitra first suggested boycott of British goods in his weekly Sanjibani
  [202:0:0.1] 19/20 July 1905 | Partition announced

  [202:0:0.3] 16 October 1905 | Partition took effect – day of mourning, fasting, hartal, Ganga bath, Raksha Bandhan, Federation Hall foundation
[202:0:1] - **Immediate cause:** the **partition of Bengal by Curzon** – not Tilak's sentence, the Lajpat Rai–Ajit Singh deportation (1907) or the Chapekar sentence; not Curzon's universities or municipal measures.

[202:0:3] - **Movement born of the partition:** the **Swadeshi Movement** (1905-1911) – not NCM, CDM, Quit India or "Purna Swaraj".
[202:0:4] - **National Handloom Day = 7 August**, observed since **2015**, commemorates the **Swadeshi Movement** launched on 7 Aug 1905.
[202:0:5] - **Phases:** Moderates' petitions and meetings (1903-05: S.N. Banerjea, K.K. Mitra, Prithwishchandra Ray) → **Extremists take over from 1906** (passive resistance, Swaraj).
[202:0:6] - **Gandhi** had **no part** in it – he was in South Africa; Khilafat, Individual Satyagraha and Quit India were his movements.

## SUB 1 | Partition of Bengal, 1905 – Dates, Curzon, Motive, Annulment
[202:1:0] # Key facts
[202:1:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [202:1:1.0] Viceroy | Lord Curzon (1899-1905) – not Minto, Hardinge, Lytton or Napier
  [202:1:1.1] First proposal | December 1903 – Moderate protests 1903-05
  [202:1:1.2] Announced | 19 July 1905 (order issued; published 20 July)
  [202:1:1.3] Took effect | 16 October 1905 – not 15 October
  [202:1:1.4] New province | Eastern Bengal and Assam (Dacca, Chittagong, Rajshahi divisions + Assam), capital Dacca, 31 million
  [202:1:1.5] Rest of Bengal | West Bengal + Bihar + Orissa, 54 million – 18 million Bengalis, 36 million Biharis and Oriyas (Bengalis a minority)
  [202:1:1.6] Lt Governor of Bengal | Sir Andrew Fraser (1903-08), active in framing the partition
  [202:1:1.7] Annulled | 12 December 1911, Delhi Durbar of King George V – capital also shifted Calcutta → Delhi
  [202:1:1.8] Viceroy at annulment | Lord Hardinge II
[202:1:2] - **Stated motive:** **administrative convenience** – province of about 80 million "too big"; Curzon called it a readjustment of boundaries.
[202:1:3] - **Real motive:** **weaken Bengali nationalism** – "the nerve centre of Indian nationalism" (Curzon) – and **divide Hindus and Muslims** (Curzon at Dacca wooed the Muslims).
[202:1:4] - **H.H. Risley:** "**Bengal united is a power; Bengal divided will pull in several different ways.**"
[202:1:5] - **Surendranath Banerjea:** the announcement "**fell like a bombshell**"; he and K.K. Mitra **led the anti-partition agitation**.
[202:1:6] - **NDA 2013 statement:** Curzon **separated Dacca, Chittagong and Rajshahi divisions from Bengal and joined them to Assam** ✓; he did **not** succeed Canning, introduce the subsidiary alliance (Wellesley) or sympathise with the Congress.
[202:1:7] - **Impact:** the partition was the event with the **maximum impact on the freedom movement** of the options usually offered (Prince of Wales 1875, Delhi Durbar 1877, ICS 1861).
[202:1:8] - **Durbars:** 1877 (Lytton, Victoria Kaiser-i-Hind), **1903 (Curzon, Edward VII's coronation)**, **1911 (George V – the only one a sovereign attended; annulment)**. Japan's victory over Russia (1904-05) fuelled the anger.
[202:1:9] > Traps: annulment **1911** (not 1910, 1912 or 1947); it did not last until WWI, the CDM or 1947. "First partition of Bengal" = 1905 (the second = 1947).

[202:1:11] - **Bihar and Orissa** separated from Bengal – announced at the **Delhi Durbar (12 Dec 1911)** with the annulment, in effect from **1 April 1912**.

## SUB 3 | Programme, Samitis, National Education and Swadeshi Enterprises

## SUB 3 | Vandemataram Movement in Andhra – Name, Phase, Beginnings
[203:3:0] - **Name:** in Andhra the Swadeshi Movement was called the **Vandemataram Movement** (a **sequel to the partition of Bengal**); Andhra phase **1906-1911**, ended with the **1911 annulment**.
[203:3:1] - **Real political awakening** in Andhra began with it; **students** of Andhra took an active part in politics **from the Vandemataram movement onwards**.
[203:3:2] - **Madras, Sept 1905:** Madras Beach students' meeting under **G. Subramania Iyer** condemned the partition; Kela Sri Ramamurthy sang Vandemataram; Subramania Bharati sang Tamil songs.
[203:3:3] - **Madras, 1906:** an **Industrial Association** and a **Swadeshi League**; the League sent **Nyapati Subbarao and K. Venkataramana Rao** to tour Andhra and popularise Swadeshi – **before Pal's visit**.
[203:3:4] - **S.N. Banerjea's arrest (1906)** → protests at Rajahmundry, Kakinada, Vizianagaram, Gooty; slogan "**Vandemataram – Manade Rajyam**".
[203:3:5] - **Bala Bharati Samiti:** founded at **Rajahmundry, February 1907** by local youths (Chilukuri Veerabhadra Rao, Ganti Lakshmanna and others; members incl. T. Prakasam) – Andhra's response to the **partition of Bengal**; backed by Karanam Guneswara Rao.
[203:3:6] - **Legacy:** the movement roused interest in Andhra's language and history (Andhrula Charitramu, 1910) and **moulded the Andhra Movement** for a separate province.

## SUB 1 | Moderates vs Extremists – Leaders, Methods and Ideology
[204:1:0] # Who were the Extremists
[204:1:1] - **Extremists** = **Militant / Assertive Nationalists** (Neo-nationalists, **Garam Dal**); trend from the **1890s**, concrete by **1905**; dominated the movement **after 1906**.
[204:1:2] - **Four pillars:** **Lal-Bal-Pal + Ghosh** – **Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Aurobindo Ghosh**; Tilak the most outstanding.
[204:1:3] TABLE head: Regional group | Leader
  [204:1:3.0] Maharashtra | Bal Gangadhar Tilak (with Vishnu Shastri Chiplunkar)
  [204:1:3.1] Bengal | Bipin Chandra Pal, Aurobindo Ghosh (also Raj Narain Bose, Ashwini Kumar Datta)
  [204:1:3.2] Punjab | Lala Lajpat Rai (with Ajit Singh)
  [204:1:3.3] Delhi (Swadeshi) | Syed Haider Raza
[204:1:4] - **Gujarat** was NOT a centre of Extremism (APPSC G1 2002 odd one out).
[204:1:5] - **Moderates (never Extremists):** Dadabhai Naoroji, **Gokhale** (their leader), Pherozeshah Mehta, S.N. Banerjea, M.G. Ranade, G.V. Joshi, W.C. Bonnerjee, R.C. Dutt.
[204:1:6] > Statement trap: "Tilak, Pal, Lajpat Rai and Aurobindo were Moderates" ✗.
[204:1:7] # How they differed
[204:1:8] TABLE head: Basis | Moderates (1885-1905) | Extremists (1905 onwards)
  [204:1:8.0] Faith in British | Believed in British justice, "providential mission" | No faith in British fairness or generosity; rule exploitative
  [204:1:8.1] Goal | Reforms; self-government within the Empire, gradual | Swaraj – complete freedom, by own effort
  [204:1:8.2] Method | Constitutional agitation; the 3 Ps – prayer, petition, protest | Boycott, Swadeshi, national education, passive resistance, mass agitation; 3 Ss – self-reliance, self-sacrifice, strong will
  [204:1:8.3] Masses | Not ready for politics | Immense faith in the masses
  [204:1:8.4] Inspiration | Western liberal thought | Indian history, culture, Hindu traditional symbols
  [204:1:8.5] Social base | Zamindars, upper middle class | Educated middle and lower middle class
[204:1:9] - **Fundamental difference:** **belief in British fairness and the preferred mode of struggle** (not social base or princely states).
[204:1:10] - **Extremist ideology (UPSC 1998):** **obtaining self-government by aggressive means** in place of petitions and constitutional ways.
[204:1:11] - They called Moderate methods **"political mendicancy"**; **Tilak** attacked the "politics of **prayer, petition and protest**", and the prayer-and-petition policy ended under his lead.
[204:1:12] - Remarks on the Congress: "**begging institute**" – credited to **B.C. Pal** in most keys (NextGen p.371 credits Aurobindo; Pal: Congress "playing with bubbles"); Tilak: "Rights are not begged, they are claimed".
[204:1:13] - **Passive resistance** doctrine – **Aurobindo** (series in *Bande Mataram*, 1907); **Swadeshi** – Aurobindo the main Bengal leader.
[204:1:14] - **Word Swaraj:** first used by **Dayanand Saraswati**; **Deuskar** brought it to Bengal (1902); as a political demand it is **Tilak's** ("Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it").
[204:1:15] - **Atmashakti** = self-reliance (Swadeshi enterprises, national schools); **spiritual nationalism** saw the nation as a **mother goddess** (Bande Mataram, Bhavani Mandir).
[204:1:16] - **Contribution:** turned nationalism into a **mass movement** and made **Swaraj** its goal.
[204:1:17] - **Criticism:** Hindu revivalist symbols (Ganapati, Shivaji festivals, Bhavani) and **harping on the Hindu past** kept most **Muslims** away; politically radical but socially conservative.

## SUB 2 | Surat Split 1907 – Run-up, Causes and Aftermath
[204:2:0] # Run-up
[204:2:1] TABLE head: Session | President | What happened
  [204:2:1.0] Benaras 1905 | Gokhale | Mild resolution condemning partition; supported Swadeshi-Boycott in Bengal
  [204:2:1.1] Calcutta 1906 | Dadabhai Naoroji | Split averted; goal = "swarajya or self-government" like the UK colonies; four resolutions – Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education
  [204:2:1.2] Surat 1907 (26 Dec) | Rash Behari Ghosh (Moderates' choice) | Split
[204:2:2] - **Annulment of the partition** was **NOT** one of the four Calcutta resolutions (UPSC 2010).
[204:2:3] # The split
[204:2:4] - **Extremists (Garam Dal, led by Tilak):** session at **Nagpur**, **Tilak or Lajpat Rai** president, keep the four resolutions.
[204:2:5] - **Moderates (Naram Dal – Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta):** Surat (host-province rule kept Tilak out), Rash Behari Ghosh president, drop the resolutions.
[204:2:6] - **Main reason (UPSC 2016):** **Extremists' lack of faith in the Moderates' capacity to negotiate with the British**.
[204:2:7] - Differences were over **strategy, objectives and mass participation** – all of these; the **Swadeshi Movement** produced the split.
[204:2:8] - **First split of the INC = Surat, December 1907**; scuffle, session suspended; the Moderates pushed the Extremists out and kept the Congress machinery.
[204:2:9] - **A-R (UPSC 1998):** the 1905 partition did **not** end the Moderates' role (A ✗); Surat separated the two wings (R ✓).
[204:2:10] # Aftermath and repression

## SUB 4 | Aurobindo Ghosh and Bipin Chandra Pal
[204:4:0] # Aurobindo Ghosh (15 Aug 1872 – 5 Dec 1950)
[204:4:1] - **New Lamps for Old** (**1893-94**): articles in **Indu Prakash** (Bombay) – systematic critique of Moderate politics; Congress out of touch with the proletariat.
