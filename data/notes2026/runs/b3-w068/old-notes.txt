## SUB 0 | EPZs and Free Trade Zones – Kandla to Surat
[68:0:0] - **SEZ = Special Economic Zone** (not Special Export / Exemption / Environment Zone) – asked 3 times.
[68:0:1] - **Kandla** (Gulf of Kutch, Gujarat), **1965**: **India's and Asia's first Export Processing Zone**; also India's first Free Trade Zone; later Kandla SEZ.
[68:0:2] - **Surat**, **1997**: **first private-sector EPZ**; converted into an SEZ on **1 November 2000**.
[68:0:3] - **Free trade zone:** area where goods are landed, stored, processed and re-exported **without customs duty**; set up for **promotion of export industries** (not backward areas, SSI or IT).
[68:0:4] TABLE head: EPZ | Place
  [68:0:4.0] Kandla | Gujarat (1965)
  [68:0:4.1] SEEPZ | Santacruz, Mumbai
  [68:0:4.2] MEPZ | Chennai
  [68:0:4.3] Falta | West Bengal
  [68:0:4.4] Others | Noida, Cochin, Visakhapatnam
[68:0:5] - **Thiruvananthapuram had no FTZ** (Kerala's zone was at Cochin) – UPPSC 1999.
[68:0:6] - **"City without an SEZ" (UPPSC 2001 key): Kandla** – listed as an EPZ, not an SEZ, then; Chennai, Kochi, Surat had SEZs. From 2006 EPZs and EOUs could convert into SEZs.
[68:0:7] - **Area-based incentive:** in 2001 PM Vajpayee announced a **5-year excise duty holiday** for industries in **earthquake-ravaged Kutch** (UPSC 2002).

## SUB 2 | SEZ Act 2005 – Objectives and Features
[68:2:0] # Objectives
[68:2:1] - 1. Generation of **additional economic activity**.
[68:2:2] - 2. Promotion of **exports of goods and services**.
[68:2:3] - 3. Promotion of **investment from domestic and foreign sources**.
[68:2:4] - 4. Creation of **employment** opportunities.
[68:2:5] - 5. Development of **infrastructure** facilities.
[68:2:6] - So "all the above" (economic activity, employment, exports); infrastructure + foreign investment = **1 and 2 only** – "exports of **services only**" is NOT an objective (UPSC 2010); "**discouraging** foreign investment" is NOT an objective.
[68:2:7] # Features
[68:2:8] - A **duty-free enclave** for exports and FDI; developer: **Central Government, State Government or any person** (public, private, joint sector).
[68:2:9] - Purpose: manufacturing, **services** or Free Trade Warehousing Zone; proposal via **State Government → Board of Approval**; **single-window clearance**.
[68:2:10] - **100% FDI** under the automatic route for most manufacturing in SEZs.
[68:2:11] - Min. area (Rules 2006): multi-product **1,000 ha**; sector-specific reduced to **50 ha** (2019).
[68:2:12] - **Not true:** "SEZ is set up **primarily** to generate employment" – SEZs are **export-oriented** (APPSC G2 2017).
[68:2:13] - **A-R (Constable 2016 key):** SEZs attract foreign companies and give more local employment – both true, (R) taken as the explanation.
[68:2:14] - **s.18:** one IFSC per SEZ. **s.10AA:** **100%** exemption 5 years, 50% next 5, 50% of reinvested profit 5 more; holidays **phased out 2017-2020** (sunset clause).
[68:2:15] - **Japan** came forward to develop SEZs / Japanese industrial townships in India (with DMIC).

## SUB 3 | SEZ Performance and Criticism
[68:3:0] - Total investment in SEZs by **31 Dec 2011**: **₹2,49,630 crore** (Economic Survey 2011-12).
[68:3:1] - **Highest export growth over the previous year (2004-05 to 2007-08): 2007-08** (₹66,638 crore, about +92%).
[68:3:2] - FY 2024-25 SEZ exports **₹14.64 lakh crore**, about **21%** of India's exports; SEZs got **less than 3% of FDI**.
[68:3:3] TABLE head: State with most SEZs | Fact

  [68:3:3.1] Tamil Nadu | most operational SEZs now
  [68:3:3.2] Telangana | most approved SEZs now
[68:3:4] - **Nandigram** (Purba Medinipur, West Bengal), **2007**: **Salim Group** chemical-hub SEZ; violent protests against **land acquisition**.
[68:3:5] - Criticism: revenue loss, idle land, **WTO-inconsistent export subsidies**; employment gains not guaranteed.
[68:3:6] - **Baba Kalyani Committee (2018):** rename SEZs as **Employment and Economic Enclaves** (3Es).
[68:3:7] - **DESH Bill** (Development of Enterprise and Services Hubs, **Budget 2022-23**): removes positive NFE condition; still pending.
[68:3:8] - **BHAVYA – Bharat Audyogik Vikas Yojana (2026):** ₹33,660 crore; **100** plug-and-play parks (not 50).

## SUB 0 | Distribution Reform – UDAY, Electricity Act 2003 and Power Tariffs
[70:0:0] # UDAY (Ujwal DISCOM Assurance Yojana)
[70:0:1] - **Launched:** Cabinet approval **5 Nov 2015** (Ministry of Power) for the **financial and operational turnaround of DISCOMs** – not household electrification (Saubhagya) or LED bulbs (UJALA).
[70:0:2] - **Debt:** States take over **75% of DISCOM debt** (50% in 2015-16, 25% in 2016-17), cutting interest from 14-15% to 8-9% – the scheme 'to reduce interest payment in the electricity sector'.
[70:0:3] - **Four initiatives:** **improving operational efficiency; reduction of cost of power; reduction in interest cost; enforcing financial discipline** – regularising DISCOM employees was NOT one.
[70:0:4] - Targets: AT&C loss ~22% → **15%**, ACS-ARR gap to zero by 2018-19; optional for States.
[70:0:5] - Replaced **Indian Electricity Act 1910, Electricity (Supply) Act 1948, ERC Act 1998**.
[70:0:6] - **Section 63** = tariffs through **competitive bidding**.

[70:0:10] # Tariffs, consumption and policy
[70:0:11] - **Cross-subsidisation:** low domestic/farm tariffs are paid for by high industrial and commercial tariffs – why Indian household power is cheap and industrial power dear.
[70:0:12] - **Largest consumer of electricity:** **industry** (~40%), ahead of domestic and agriculture.
[70:0:13] - **Highest per capita consumption (larger States, exam answer):** **Punjab, Haryana, Gujarat**.

## SUB 1 | Globalisation – Meaning and Impact
[71:1:0] # Meaning
[71:1:1] - **Dismantling barriers** in international trade; interdependence through trade, technology, investment, people and information; **minimum possible restrictions** across borders.
[71:1:2] - **Includes**: lower import duties (incl. on capital goods), abolition of import licensing, free FDI flow, MNC entry.
[71:1:3] - **Excludes**: **disinvestment of public-sector equity** (privatisation), greater state control, reduced foreign trade.
[71:1:4] - **Free trade** = reduction or removal of trade barriers (tariffs, quotas).
[71:1:5] - Key element of India's market strategy since 1991 = **integration with the global economy**. A stable macroeconomy helps by withstanding shocks.
[71:1:6] TABLE head: Liberalisation | Globalisation
  [71:1:6.0] Removes domestic controls | Integrates with the world economy
  [71:1:6.1] De-licensing, deregulation, import liberalisation | Trade, foreign investment, MNC participation
  [71:1:6.2] Greater market orientation | Greater global integration; technology flows
[71:1:7] # Impact
[71:1:8] - Exports and imports both grew fast - but **imports grew faster than exports** (persistent trade deficit) (APPSC 2025).
[71:1:9] - India's world trade share 1.78% (1950) → 0.6% (1995); later rose.
[71:1:10] - **Largest FDI sources 1991-95: USA and Japan**. Export growth 1994-95: about **18%**.
[71:1:11] - **Services** (IT, outsourcing) benefited most; IT exports cushioned the CAD; 1,800+ GCCs earned $38.25 bn of services exports in July 2026.
[71:1:12] - Indian MNCs: Tata, Reliance, Infosys, Sun Pharma.
[71:1:13] - Net FDI: $44.0 bn (2020-21) → under $1 bn (2024-25) → $7.6 bn (2025-26); gross $94.6 bn.
[71:1:14] - **ISO 9001/9002** for many industries (BIS) and licensing liberalised on 24 July 1991 - both true, but R does not explain A.
[71:1:15] - **Major impact (APPSC 2010):** poverty fell but **economic disparities rose**.
[71:1:16] - **Trade/GDP:** merchandise trade 14.2% of GDP (1990-91) → ~43% (2011-12) – about **three times**.
[71:1:17] - **FII inflows began only after 1992** (FIIs allowed Sept 1992) – not in the 1980s.
[71:1:18] > A-R (UPSC 1996): lower import duty on capital goods is a liberalisation tool and helps firms upgrade technology – R explains A.

[71:5:0] # 1980s Groundwork (Indira and Rajiv Gandhi, 1984-89)
[71:5:1] - **Broad-banding of licences**; easier **capital-goods imports**; higher MRTP limit; **FERA relaxed**.
[71:5:2] - Growth broke the **Hindu rate**, touched **5.5%** - but it was **debt-fuelled**.
[71:5:3] - Some liberalisation began under **Rajiv Gandhi**, but the 1991 reforms were **far more comprehensive**.
[71:5:4] # Long-term Causes
[71:5:5] TABLE head: Cause | Effect / data
  [71:5:5.0] Fiscal profligacy | Gross fiscal deficit 5.4% (1981-82) → 8.0% (1989-90); average 6.3% (Sixth Plan) → 8.2% (Seventh)
  [71:5:5.1] Licence-Permit-Quota Raj | Heavy controls; MRTP Act 1970
  [71:5:5.2] PSU losses | Low returns on public investment
  [71:5:5.3] Weak exports | World export share 2.2% (1948) → 0.5% (1983)
  [71:5:5.4] Structural | CADs financed by foreign borrowing since the 1980s, incl. short-term ECBs
[71:5:6] - The **long-term** cause = constant **fiscal profligacy** and rising fiscal deficit; the others below were **immediate triggers**.

  [71:5:8.2] Capital flight | NRIs withdrew deposits; foreign banks stopped lending

## SUB 7 | Stabilisation vs Structural Adjustment
[71:7:0] TABLE head: Basis | Stabilisation | Structural adjustment
  [71:7:0.0] Term | Short-term | Long-term (medium/long-term efficiency and growth)

## SUB 8 | New Industrial Policy 1991, PSU Reform and National Renewal Fund
[71:8:0] # Industrial Policy (24 July 1991)
[71:8:1] - **Industrial licensing abolished** for all but a few industries (hazardous and strategic, e.g. hazardous chemicals, aerospace); now only **5-6** industries need a licence; reserved for public sector now only **atomic energy and railways** (+ specified minerals).
[71:8:2] - Delicensed firms: **pricing freedom**, no expansion curbs.
[71:8:3] - **Public-sector reserved industries cut from 17 (1956 policy) to 8**; later pruned further.
[71:8:4] - **MRTP**: **asset limits, pre-entry restrictions and merger/expansion controls abolished**. (SEBI's status was a separate reform.)
[71:8:5] - **FDI**: automatic approval up to **51%** in high-priority industries (now 100% in most sectors); NRIs/OCBs up to 100%; FIIs allowed into stock markets.
[71:8:6] - **BIFR**: set up to revive **sick industrial units**.
[71:8:7] # National Renewal Fund (February 1992)
[71:8:8] - A **safety net** for workers hit by industrial restructuring under the new policy.
[71:8:9] - Objectives: **retraining and redeployment** of employees affected by modernisation and restructuring; **compensation** of affected employees; counselling.
[71:8:10] - In practice mostly paid for **Voluntary Retirement Schemes (VRS)** in central PSUs; later limited to public sector units only.

## SUB 3 | Trade Terms and Trade Theory
[73:3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [73:3:0.0] Balance of trade | Exports minus imports of goods only (visible trade)
  [73:3:0.1] Intra-industry trade | A country imports and exports the same product (e.g. cars)
  [73:3:0.2] Inter-industry trade | Exchange of different products
  [73:3:0.3] Entrepot trade | Imported goods re-exported, with or without processing or packaging ("goods imported for export")
  [73:3:0.4] Free trade | Absence of tariffs and quantitative restrictions
  [73:3:0.5] Dumping | Exporting at a price below the home-market price or cost
  [73:3:0.6] Anti-dumping duty | Protects against goods exported below normal value
  [73:3:0.7] Countervailing duty | Levied on imported products to offset foreign subsidies; raises their domestic price
  [73:3:0.8] Tariff | Tax on imports
  [73:3:0.9] Import quota | Quantitative restriction on imports
  [73:3:0.10] Import licensing | Administrative restriction on imports
  [73:3:0.11] Rules of origin | Decide a product's national source and which goods get FTA concessions
  [73:3:0.12] SPS measures | Sanitary and phytosanitary rules (human, animal, plant health) – a non-tariff barrier
[73:3:1] - **India** used the **most anti-dumping measures in 1995-2005** (WTO data); in 2024 the USA had the most measures in force.
[73:3:2] - **Singer-Prebisch thesis:** primary-exporting LDCs suffer a **secular decline in their terms of trade** and so a **welfare loss**.
[73:3:3] - **Adam Smith = absolute advantage; David Ricardo = comparative advantage.** India's services surplus vs merchandise deficit = comparative advantage in services.
[73:3:4] - **Export promotion = outward-looking; import substitution = inward-looking.** **Direction** of trade = country/region-wise pattern; composition = items; volume = value.
[73:3:5] # Terms of trade and exchange rate
[73:3:6] TABLE head: Measure | Formula
  [73:3:6.0] Net (NTT) | Export price index / import price index × 100
  [73:3:6.1] Gross (GTT) | Import quantity / export quantity
  [73:3:6.2] Income (ITT) | NTT × export quantity index
[73:3:7] - **Devaluation/depreciation:** exports **cheaper abroad**, imports **dearer** → exports rise, imports fall.
[73:3:8] - **J-curve:** trade balance **first worsens**, then improves as volumes adjust. **Marshall-Lerner:** devaluation helps only if export + import demand elasticities **sum > 1**.
[73:3:9] - **1991 New Trade Policy:** QRs and import substitution → **price-based regime**.
[73:3:10] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Economy p.78, p.80; NextGen Indian Economy P1 - APPSC 02 p.36; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy (10th ed.) part 05 p.168; app notes 3:73 sub 0-2
[73:3:11] - **Trade** = exchange between buyers and sellers – it represents **both demand and supply**, not demand alone.
[73:3:12] - **Degree of openness** is measured by **openness to trade**, **openness to foreign capital** and extent of PSU dominance in commerce – the **level of online trade is not** a standard measure.
[73:3:13] - **Manmohan Singh** (Oxford thesis, published 1964 'India's Export Trends and Prospects for Self-Sustained Growth') argued early, against the import-substitution consensus, that **trade matters for development**.
