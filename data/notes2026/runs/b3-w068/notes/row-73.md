# Agricultural exports
- **1997-98 to 1999-2000: marine products** were the largest agricultural export by value; in recent years **cereals** (rice) lead.
- India became the **world's largest rice exporter**, displacing **Thailand** (asked for 2015; India has stayed No. 1).
- Agri exports **$51.1 bn (FY25)**, 11.7% of merchandise exports; India's share in world agri exports only 2.2%.

- India's share of world **clothing (apparel)** exports ≈ **4.1%** (WTO statistics, APPSC key).
- Gems & jewellery share of world exports ≈ **17.6%** (APPSC Group 2 2001 key, old data).
- Forex-earning opportunities: **tourism, medical care, garments, leather goods** – all four.
- Exports grew about **18%** in 1994-95 (dollar terms).

=== Composition of Imports
- **POL (petroleum, oil and lubricants) = largest import item** for most of the post-reform period – highest import bill and most forex spent (2024-25: **US$ 185.8 bn, 25.8%** of imports).
- Petroleum crude, **gold** and petroleum products = about **one-third of imports** (FY25); gold imports rose with prices.
- **Two largest import groups:** **petroleum products and capital goods** (capital goods 12.3% in 2024-25).
- **Edible oil** = main **agricultural** import (over 50% of agri imports); food & allied = 4.4% of imports.
- **Qatar** = biggest **LNG** supplier to India (15.3 bcm of 37.9 bcm in 2024).
- Most important item "in recent years" among chemicals, fertilisers, iron & steel: **petroleum (and edible oil)**.

=== Trade Terms and Trade Theory
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Balance of trade** | Exports minus imports of **goods** only (visible trade) |
| **Intra-industry trade** | A country **imports and exports the same product** (e.g. cars) |
| Inter-industry trade | Exchange of different products |
| **Entrepot trade** | **Imported goods re-exported**, with or without processing or packaging ("goods imported for export") |
| **Free trade** | **Absence of tariffs** and quantitative restrictions |
| **Dumping** | Exporting at a price below the home-market price or cost |
| **Anti-dumping duty** | Protects against goods exported below normal value |
| **Countervailing duty** | Levied on **imported** products to offset foreign subsidies; raises their domestic price |
| Tariff | Tax on imports |
| Import quota | Quantitative restriction on imports |
| Import licensing | Administrative restriction on imports |
| Rules of origin | Decide a product's national source and which goods get FTA concessions |
| SPS measures | Sanitary and phytosanitary rules (human, animal, plant health) – a **non-tariff barrier** |
- **India** used the **most anti-dumping measures in 1995-2005** (WTO data); in 2024 the USA had the most measures in force.
- **Singer-Prebisch thesis:** primary-exporting LDCs suffer a **secular decline in their terms of trade** and so a **welfare loss**.
- **Adam Smith = absolute advantage; David Ricardo = comparative advantage.** India's services surplus vs merchandise deficit = comparative advantage in services.
- **Export promotion = outward-looking; import substitution = inward-looking.** **Direction** of trade = country/region-wise pattern; composition = items; volume = value.
# Terms of trade and exchange rate
| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Net (NTT) | Export price index / import price index × 100 |
| Gross (GTT) | Import quantity / export quantity |
| Income (ITT) | NTT × export quantity index |
- **Devaluation/depreciation:** exports **cheaper abroad**, imports **dearer** → exports rise, imports fall.
- **J-curve:** trade balance **first worsens**, then improves as volumes adjust. **Marshall-Lerner:** devaluation helps only if export + import demand elasticities **sum > 1**.
- **1991 New Trade Policy:** QRs and import substitution → **price-based regime**.

=== Trade Deficit and Balance
- **Trade deficit = imports − exports** (negative balance of trade); India's story is "**oil and gold**".
- **Trade surplus only twice since independence: 1972-73 and 1976-77.**
- Deficit peaked in **2012-13 (US$ 195.66 bn)** and fell continuously to **2016-17 (US$ 112.44 bn)** as **both POL and non-POL deficits fell** (UPPSC 2017 A-R: both true, R explains A).
- **2020-21 vs 2019-20:** deficit **declined** ($157.5 bn → $102.2 bn) – imports fell more than exports (COVID).
- **UPSC 2020 (present):** merchandise exports < imports; **services exports > services imports**; India runs an overall trade/current account deficit → **1, 3 and 4** (imports of iron & steel, fertilisers, machinery did NOT fall).
- **Causes since 1991:** oil and gold imports, **South-East Asian crisis 1997-98**, competition from **China**, **non-tariff barriers** of partners, import liberalisation (capital goods, APIs, electronics). **NOT** "lacklustre services" – services were in surplus.
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Merchandise exports FY25 | **US$ 437.7 bn** |
| Merchandise imports FY25 | US$ 721.2 bn |
| Total exports / imports FY25 | $825.3 bn / $919.9 bn |
| Merchandise deficit FY25 | **$283.5 bn** (+17.6%); overall deficit $94.7 bn |
| Merchandise deficit FY26 (RBI BoP) | **$337.3 bn** |
- **Twin deficit hypothesis:** a rising **fiscal deficit** goes with a rising **trade deficit**.
- **Services surplus** (IT/ITeS) and **remittances** cushion the merchandise deficit.
