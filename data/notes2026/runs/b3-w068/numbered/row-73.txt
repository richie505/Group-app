## SUB 3 | Trade Terms and Trade Theory
[3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:0.0] Balance of trade | Exports minus imports of goods only (visible trade)
  [3:0.1] Intra-industry trade | A country imports and exports the same product (e.g. cars)
  [3:0.2] Inter-industry trade | Exchange of different products
  [3:0.3] Entrepot trade | Imported goods re-exported, with or without processing or packaging ("goods imported for export")
  [3:0.4] Free trade | Absence of tariffs and quantitative restrictions
  [3:0.5] Dumping | Exporting at a price below the home-market price or cost
  [3:0.6] Anti-dumping duty | Protects against goods exported below normal value
  [3:0.7] Countervailing duty | Levied on imported products to offset foreign subsidies; raises their domestic price
  [3:0.8] Tariff | Tax on imports
  [3:0.9] Import quota | Quantitative restriction on imports
  [3:0.10] Import licensing | Administrative restriction on imports
  [3:0.11] Rules of origin | Decide a product's national source and which goods get FTA concessions
  [3:0.12] SPS measures | Sanitary and phytosanitary rules (human, animal, plant health) – a non-tariff barrier
[3:1] - **India** used the **most anti-dumping measures in 1995-2005** (WTO data); in 2024 the USA had the most measures in force.
[3:2] - **Singer-Prebisch thesis:** primary-exporting LDCs suffer a **secular decline in their terms of trade** and so a **welfare loss**.
[3:3] - **Adam Smith = absolute advantage; David Ricardo = comparative advantage.** India's services surplus vs merchandise deficit = comparative advantage in services.
[3:4] - **Export promotion = outward-looking; import substitution = inward-looking.** **Direction** of trade = country/region-wise pattern; composition = items; volume = value.
[3:5] # Terms of trade and exchange rate
[3:6] TABLE head: Measure | Formula
  [3:6.0] Net (NTT) | Export price index / import price index × 100
  [3:6.1] Gross (GTT) | Import quantity / export quantity
  [3:6.2] Income (ITT) | NTT × export quantity index
[3:7] - **Devaluation/depreciation:** exports **cheaper abroad**, imports **dearer** → exports rise, imports fall.
[3:8] - **J-curve:** trade balance **first worsens**, then improves as volumes adjust. **Marshall-Lerner:** devaluation helps only if export + import demand elasticities **sum > 1**.
[3:9] - **1991 New Trade Policy:** QRs and import substitution → **price-based regime**.

## SUB 4 | Trade Deficit and Balance
[4:0] - **Trade deficit = imports − exports** (negative balance of trade); India's story is "**oil and gold**".
[4:1] - **Trade surplus only twice since independence: 1972-73 and 1976-77.**
[4:2] - Deficit peaked in **2012-13 (US$ 195.66 bn)** and fell continuously to **2016-17 (US$ 112.44 bn)** as **both POL and non-POL deficits fell** (UPPSC 2017 A-R: both true, R explains A).
[4:3] - **2020-21 vs 2019-20:** deficit **declined** ($157.5 bn → $102.2 bn) – imports fell more than exports (COVID).
[4:4] - **UPSC 2020 (present):** merchandise exports < imports; **services exports > services imports**; India runs an overall trade/current account deficit → **1, 3 and 4** (imports of iron & steel, fertilisers, machinery did NOT fall).
[4:5] - **Causes since 1991:** oil and gold imports, **South-East Asian crisis 1997-98**, competition from **China**, **non-tariff barriers** of partners, import liberalisation (capital goods, APIs, electronics). **NOT** "lacklustre services" – services were in surplus.
[4:6] TABLE head: Item | Value
  [4:6.0] Merchandise exports FY25 | US$ 437.7 bn
  [4:6.1] Merchandise imports FY25 | US$ 721.2 bn
  [4:6.2] Total exports / imports FY25 | $825.3 bn / $919.9 bn
  [4:6.3] Merchandise deficit FY25 | $283.5 bn (+17.6%); overall deficit $94.7 bn
  [4:6.4] Merchandise deficit FY26 (RBI BoP) | $337.3 bn
[4:7] - **Twin deficit hypothesis:** a rising **fiscal deficit** goes with a rising **trade deficit**.
[4:8] - **Services surplus** (IT/ITeS) and **remittances** cushion the merchandise deficit.
