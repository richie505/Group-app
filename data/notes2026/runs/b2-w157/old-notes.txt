=== NOTES 2:157 | Bretton Woods Institutions – Imf (quota, Sdr), World Bank Group; Adb, Aiib, Ndb
## SUB 0 | Bretton Woods Conference and the IMF
[157:0:0] # Bretton Woods (1944)
[157:0:1] - **United Nations Monetary and Financial Conference** = **Bretton Woods Conference**, **July 1944**, Mount Washington Hotel, **Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, USA** – **44 allied nations**, 730 delegates; plans of **Harry Dexter White** (US, adopted) and **John Maynard Keynes** (UK).
[157:0:2] - Created the "Bretton Woods twins": **IMF + IBRD (World Bank)**, both HQ **Washington, D.C.**; the planned **ITO** was never born → **GATT (1947)** filled the gap until the **WTO (1995)**.
[157:0:3] - Fixed exchange rates (dollar pegged to gold); **Smithsonian Agreement (1971)** widened the band to **2.25%**, lasted about **14 months**.
[157:0:4] TABLE head: Event | Date
  [157:0:4.0] IMF and IBRD founded (conceived) | July 1944
  [157:0:4.1] IMF Articles in force – formal existence (29 members) | 27 December 1945
  [157:0:4.2] IMF began operations | 1 March 1947
  [157:0:4.3] First IMF borrower | France, 8 May 1947
  [157:0:4.4] SDR created ("paper gold") | 1967-69 (first allocation 1970)
[157:0:5] - **India** = founding member of both the IMF and the IBRD.
[157:0:6] # IMF – functions and lending
[157:0:7] - **IMF** = **International Monetary Fund** – monetary stability; **short-term balance-of-payments (BoP)** support; technical assistance; **not** project/development finance (that is the World Bank's job).
[157:0:8] - Lends **only to member countries** (UPSC 2011, MPPSC 2017) – not to "any country", not only to developed ones, not to central banks as such.
[157:0:9] - Facilities: **Stand-By Arrangement**, **Extended Fund Facility**, **Rapid Financing Instrument (RFI)** and **Rapid Credit Facility (RCF)** (urgent BoP needs; RCF concessional for low-income members), PRGT. **Political-risk guarantees** = MIGA, not IMF.
[157:0:10] - **1991 BoP crisis**: India borrowed from the **IMF** (SDR 3.56 billion) – with the World Bank about **$7 bn** in all → New Economic Policy.
[157:0:11] # Quotas, votes, SDR
[157:0:12] - **Members: 191** – Liechtenstein the 191st (21 Oct 2024); **189** in January 2020 (Nauru 190th, 2021).
[157:0:13] - **Quotas** (in SDRs) decide financial contribution, **borrowing access** and **voting power**; do **not** fix exchange rates. Formula: GDP **50%**, openness **30%**, variability **15%**, reserves **5%**.
[157:0:14] - Major decisions need **85%** of votes → the **US** (~16.5%) alone holds a **veto**. **India 8th** by quota (~2.75%).
[157:0:15] - **SDR** basket of **five** currencies since **1 October 2016**: US dollar, euro, **Chinese renminbi (yuan)** (added 2016), yen, pound.
[157:0:16] - **IMFC** advises the Board of Governors; **Article IV consultations** = regular review of each member's economy.
[157:0:17] > Trap: 27 Dec 1945 = formal existence; 1 March 1947 = operations; 1944 = founding conference. HQ = Washington, not New York.

[157:0:19] - **India** is an **original (founding) member** of the IMF – a member from **27 December 1945**.
[157:0:20] - **PRGF** (Poverty Reduction and Growth Facility, 1999; now the PRGT) was set up by the **IMF**, not IDA; the **Singapore Regional Training Institute (STI)** is one of the **IMF's regional training centres**.
[157:0:21] - **IMFC** (International Monetary and Financial Committee): discusses concerns affecting the global economy and **advises the IMF** on the direction of its work; the **World Bank participates as an observer**.
## SUB 1 | World Bank Group
[157:1:0] - **IBRD** = the original "**World Bank**" (1944; began 1946); HQ **Washington, D.C.** (not New York, Manila, Geneva, Vienna or Brussels); gives **medium- and long-term** loans; **India a founding member**; first loan to India 1949 (Railways).
[157:1:1] TABLE head: Institution | Year | Function
  [157:1:1.0] IBRD | 1944 | Loans to middle-income governments for infrastructure, reconstruction and development
  [157:1:1.1] IFC | 1956 | Private-sector investment; conceived "Doing Business"
  [157:1:1.2] IDA | 1960 | "Soft window": interest-free credits, concessional (soft) loans and grants to the poorest; an affiliate of the World Bank, administered by the IBRD (same staff)
  [157:1:1.3] ICSID | 1966 (not 1976) | Settles investment disputes; India not a member
  [157:1:1.4] MIGA | 1988 | Political-risk insurance and guarantees (India joined 1994)
[157:1:2] - IBRD, IDA and IFC are UN specialised agencies; ICSID and MIGA are not.
[157:1:3] - **Match (UPSC 1999)**: WTO – multilateral trade negotiations; **IDA – soft loans**; **IMF – short-term BoP loans**; **IBRD – lending for reconstruction and development**.
[157:1:4] - **Aid India Consortium** (World Bank-chaired donor group, 1958) → renamed **India Development Forum**.
[157:1:5] - World Bank funds: **BioCarbon Fund Initiative for Sustainable Forest Landscapes (ISFL)** – managed by the **World Bank**; **Bangladesh Road Safety Project** – **$358 million** (June 2023; Gazipur-Elenga N4, Natore-Nawabganj N6 highways).
[157:1:6] ~~ Sources: PMF IAS International Relations p.168-169 (World Bank founded 1944, India founding member, five institutions, India not in ICSID, 1949 Railways loan); Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy, ch.16 p.191-192 (IBRD, IDA "soft window" 1960, IFC 1956, MIGA 1988, ICSID 1966); app notes 2:157 sub 1, 3; Aid India Consortium, ISFL, Bangladesh road safety loan: general knowledge, not checked
[157:1:7] - **IMF** and **IFAD** are also **UN specialised agencies** – so IBRD, IFC, IFAD and IMF are all UN agencies.
[157:1:8] - **IBRD Articles** came into force on **27 December 1945** (with the IMF's) – hence '1945' as the World Bank's establishment year in keys; it began operations in June 1946.

## SUB 3 | NDB, ADB and AIIB
[157:3:0] # New Development Bank (BRICS)
[157:3:1] - Idea: **4th BRICS Summit, New Delhi, 2012**. Agreement: **6th Summit, Fortaleza, Brazil, 15 July 2014** – the **Fortaleza Declaration** (BRICS, not ASEAN/OECD/WTO); operations 2015.
[157:3:2] - HQ **Shanghai, China**; regional offices Johannesburg, São Paulo, **GIFT City** (India), Moscow. First President **K.V. Kamath**.
[157:3:3] - Founders hold **equal votes; no veto**; open to all UN members. New members: Bangladesh, UAE (2021), **Egypt** (2023), Algeria (2025).
[157:3:4] - **Russia** = 20% shareholder; NDB froze Russia-linked work, March 2022. 16th BRICS Summit: **Kazan**, Oct 2024; **New Delhi Declaration** (18th, 2026): Global North (~15% of people) holds ~**60%** of IMF/WB votes.
[157:3:5] # Asian Development Bank
[157:3:6] - Est. **1966** at **UN-ESCAP's** initiative (not UNCTAD); HQ **Manila, Philippines**; **India a founding member** (31 founders); **69 members**; **Türkiye** newest (2025).
[157:3:7] - Largest shareholders **Japan and USA** (~15.6% each); **India 4th** (~6.3%); President traditionally **Japanese**.
[157:3:8] - Launched the **Nature Solutions Finance Hub for Asia and the Pacific** (2023).
[157:3:9] - India is a member of **ADB** and the **Colombo Plan**; **not** of **APEC** or the **OECD** (UPSC 2008).
[157:3:10] # AIIB
[157:3:11] - China-proposed; HQ **Beijing**; Articles signed **2015**; operations **January 2016**.
[157:3:12] - **Founding members** include **India, China and the UK**; the **USA** (and Japan) stayed out.
[157:3:13] - **111** approved members; **China largest** (~26.5% votes), **India second** (~7.6%).
[157:3:14] > Trap: Manila = ADB (Japan-led); Beijing = AIIB (China-led); Shanghai = NDB (BRICS, equal votes).
[157:3:15] ~~ Sources: PMF IAS International Relations p.113-114 (NDB: new members Bangladesh, UAE 2021, Egypt 2023, Algeria 2025; equal votes of founders; NDB vs AIIB, China 26.61%, India 2nd); Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy, ch.16 p.192-193 (ADB 1966, 31 founders, Manila) and p.197 (BRICS Bank, Fortaleza Declaration, one-nation one-vote); app notes 2:157 sub 6, 7, 8; NDB HQ Shanghai, Nature Solutions Finance Hub, AIIB founders: general knowledge, not checked

=== NOTES 2:158 | World Trade Organization – Structure, Agreements (AoA, Trips, Trims, Gats), Dispute Settlement, India's Issues
## SUB 0 | WTO Basics, Structure and Ministerial Conferences
[158:0:0] # Basics
[158:0:1] - Formed **1 January 1995** under the **Marrakesh Agreement**, after the **Uruguay Round (8th GATT round, 1986-94)**; **successor of GATT (1947)** – GATT survives as the WTO agreement on goods.
[158:0:2] - Deals with the **global rules of trade between nations**; helps producers, exporters and importers conduct business; frames rules on goods, **services (GATS)** and **intellectual property (TRIPS)** – it does not "distance itself" from IPR (CDS 2014).
[158:0:3] - HQ **Geneva** (not Montreal, Seattle, The Hague or Vienna); **one-country-one-vote**, consensus – gives developing countries bargaining room.
[158:0:4] - Members **166** (March 2026); latest **Comoros** and **Timor-Leste** (2024).
[158:0:5] - **Principles**: MFN and national treatment; **GATT Article XXIV** lets FTAs be MFN exceptions if they cover "substantially all trade"; Special and Differential Treatment (SDT).
[158:0:6] # Directors-General
[158:0:7] - **Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala** (Nigeria) – first woman and first African DG (March 2021); second term **1 Sept 2025 – 31 Aug 2029**.
[158:0:8] - **Pascal Lamy** (**France**, former EU Trade Commissioner) – DG **2005-2013**; then Roberto Azevêdo (2013-20). Arthur **Dunkel** was GATT DG (1980-93).
[158:0:9] # Structure
[158:0:10] TABLE head: Body | Role
  [158:0:10.0] Ministerial Conference | Highest body; meets at least once every two years
  [158:0:10.1] General Council | Runs the WTO between MCs; also sits as the DSB and TPRB
  [158:0:10.2] Dispute Settlement Body | Panels and Appellate Body
  [158:0:10.3] Trade Policy Review Body | Regular reviews of members' trade policies
  [158:0:10.4] Council for Trade in Goods | Oversees GATT; agriculture, TBT, SPS committees

## SUB 1 | NPT, CTBT and the Nuclear-weapon States
[161:1:0] # NPT
[161:1:1] - **Signed 1968**, **in force 1970**, **extended indefinitely in 1995**; Review Conferences every **5 years**.
[161:1:2] - Recognises **five Nuclear Weapon States** – those that **tested before 1 January 1967**: **USA, Russia, UK, France, China** (= UNSC P5).
[161:1:3] - Of China, France, India, Israel, Pakistan: **only China and France** are NPT-recognised NWS (UPSC 2015, asked twice).
[161:1:4] - **North Korea left the NPT in 2003**; it claimed its **first hydrogen-bomb test on 6 January 2016**.
[161:1:5] - India rejects the NPT as discriminatory – **"nuclear apartheid"**; wants **time-bound, universal, non-discriminatory disarmament** (Rajiv Gandhi Action Plan, 1988).
[161:1:6] # CTBT
[161:1:7] - Adopted **1996**; bans **all nuclear explosions**; **not in force** – needs ratification by all **44 Annex-2 States** (signing alone is not enough).
[161:1:8] - India has **not signed** (no time-bound disarmament commitment; subcritical tests still allowed).
[161:1:9] - **Iraq signed on 19 August 2008** and ratified on 26 September 2013 – so "Iraq and India have not signed" was already false in 2009.
[161:1:10] # India's nuclear doctrine
[161:1:11] - **No First Use**, **credible minimum deterrence**, massive retaliation; Pokhran-II **1998**.
