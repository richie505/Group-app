=== NOTES 5:64 | Renewable Energy Sources and Installed Capacities in India – Overall Targets
## SUB 0 | Classification of Energy Sources
[64:0:0] TABLE head: Basis | Types
  [64:0:0.0] Renewable | Solar, wind, hydro (incl. tidal/wave), geothermal, biomass
  [64:0:0.1] Non-renewable | Coal, oil, natural gas, nuclear fuel
  [64:0:0.2] Conventional | Coal, petroleum, gas, hydel, firewood, cattle-dung cake
  [64:0:0.3] Non-conventional | Solar, wind, geothermal – by definition renewable (UPPSC 2023)
[64:0:1] - **Fossil fuels** (coal, oil, gas): formed from organic material over millions of years; limited.
[64:0:2] - **Gobar gas** = mainly **methane**.
[64:0:3] - **Natural gas is NOT green energy**; sunlight, wind, algae-based sources are (APPSC 2019).
[64:0:4] - **Nuclear is NOT renewable** (APPSC 2024).
[64:0:5] Hydel is conventional yet renewable.
[64:0:6] # Not Derived from the Sun
[64:0:7] - Wind, hydro, biomass, fossil fuels = **stored solar energy**.
[64:0:8] - NOT from the Sun: **nuclear** (fission), **geothermal** (heat from Earth's formation and radioactive decay, stored in rocks and fluids), **tidal** (Moon).
[64:0:9] # Solar Energy Facts
[64:0:10] - Most important non-conventional source and **greatest potential** for India (APPSC G2 2017); largest storehouse of alternative energy.
[64:0:11] - No atmospheric pollution when turned into electricity; used most in organic form (**photosynthesis**).
[64:0:12] - **Solar (photovoltaic) cell**: light directly to electricity; most eco-friendly.

=== NOTES 5:65 | Solar Energy – Pv vs Thermal, National Solar Mission, Solar Parks, Rooftop Solar, Isa
## SUB 0 | Solar Technologies – Pv vs Thermal
[65:0:0] # Photovoltaic vs Solar Thermal
[65:0:1] TABLE head: Point | Photovoltaic (PV) | Solar thermal / CSP
  [65:0:1.0] Principle | Photovoltaic effect: light → electricity directly, using semiconductors (silicon) | Mirrors/collectors concentrate sunlight to heat a fluid → steam turbine (works on heat)
  [65:0:1.1] Output | Direct current (DC); inverter changes DC to AC | Heat for water, steam, power
  [65:0:1.2] Forms | Mono/polycrystalline silicon, thin-film | Flat-plate collectors, concentrators, power towers
  [65:0:1.3] Heat storage | No | Possible
[65:0:2] - **Photovoltaic cells = solar cells**; silicon is the most widely used material.
[65:0:3] - A **transformer** only changes voltage; it does not convert DC to AC.
[65:0:4] - **Artificial satellites** get electricity from **solar cells** (photovoltaic effect).
[65:0:5] - **Solar constant** ≈ **1.4 kW/m²** – solar energy per unit surface at one astronomical unit from the Sun.
[65:0:6] PV = light to electricity directly (DC); CSP = heat to steam turbine. Only CSP can store heat.
[65:0:7] # PV Cell Materials
[65:0:8] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [65:0:8.0] Crystalline silicon (mono, poly) | Dominant technology
  [65:0:8.1] Thin-film (CdTe, CIGS, a-Si) | Cheaper, bendable, less efficient
  [65:0:8.2] Perovskite | IIT Bombay (MNRE project): 26% cell (world's 2nd highest); 30.2% perovskite-silicon tandem
[65:0:9] # Applications and Efficiency
[65:0:10] - **Solar pumps** (PM-KUSUM B and C), solar water heating, floating solar, rooftop systems.
[65:0:11] TABLE head: Source | Capacity utilisation factor (CUF)
  [65:0:11.0] Solar | about 21%
  [65:0:11.1] Wind | 36–40%
  [65:0:11.2] Coal | about 85%

=== NOTES 5:66 | Wind Energy – Potential, Installed Capacity, Offshore Wind Policy, Hybrid Wind-solar Policy
## SUB 0 | How A Wind Turbine Makes Power
[66:0:0] - Energy conversion: wind's **kinetic energy** → **rotational (mechanical) energy** of blades/rotor → **electrical energy** in the generator.
[66:0:1] - Turbine power varies with the **cube of wind speed** (and with swept area).
[66:0:2] - **Betz limit**: at most **16/27 (about 59.3%)** of wind's kinetic energy can be captured.
[66:0:3] # HAWT vs VAWT
[66:0:4] TABLE head: Feature | HAWT | VAWT
  [66:0:4.0] Axis | Parallel to the wind (horizontal) | Vertical
  [66:0:4.1] Design | 3-blade; needs yaw control | Darrieus, Savonius
  [66:0:4.2] Wind direction | Must face the wind | Takes wind from any direction
  [66:0:4.3] Use | Standard utility turbine | Small sites; lower efficiency
[66:0:5] Darrieus and Savonius are VAWT designs, not HAWT.
[66:0:6] # Capacity Factor and Turbine Classes
[66:0:7] - **Capacity Utilisation Factor (CUF)** = actual output ÷ output at full rated power over the period.
[66:0:8] TABLE head: Source | CUF in India
  [66:0:8.0] Wind | about 36–40%
  [66:0:8.1] Solar | about 21%
  [66:0:8.2] Coal | about 85%
[66:0:9] - **Turbine classes (IEC I, II, III)**: set by site wind speed – Class I for high-wind, Class III for low-wind sites.
## SUB 1 | Onshore vs Offshore Wind
[66:1:0] TABLE head: Point | Onshore | Offshore
  [66:1:0.0] Location | On land | Sea / large lakes

  [66:1:0.3] Issues | Land conflict | Rusting, undersea cables, navigation, marine ecology
