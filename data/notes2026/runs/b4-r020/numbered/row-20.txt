## SUB 0 | Forests and Biomes of the World
[0:0] # Biome basics (asked 27 times)
[0:1] - **Biome:** a large naturally occurring community of flora and fauna occupying a **major habitat**, shaped mainly by climate (APPSC Panchayat Secretary).
[0:2] - **Anthromes** (anthropogenic biomes): units shaped by sustained human use – croplands, villages, cities.
[0:3] - Geomorphic factors of plant and animal distribution: **slope angle, slope aspect and relative relief** (CDS 2020).
[0:4] TABLE head: Forest | Climate | Vegetation | Distribution
  [0:4.0] Tropical rainforest (selva) | Near the Equator (about 10°N–10°S); hot and wet all year, rain over 200 cm | Broadleaf evergreen (leaves not shed together); mahogany, ebony; climbers (lianas) and epiphytes | Amazon (Brazil), Congo Basin, Indonesia; Ivory Coast (Gulf of Guinea)
  [0:4.1] Tropical deciduous (monsoon) | 100–200 cm seasonal rain | Shed leaves in the dry season; teak, sal | Most extensive in Asia (India, Myanmar, Thailand); also Africa, S America, N Australia
  [0:4.2] Temperate deciduous | Mid latitudes between the tropics and the polar regions; four seasons (APPSC G2 Mains 2025) | Broadleaf, shed in autumn; oak, beech, maple | E USA, W Europe, E Asia
  [0:4.3] Taiga (boreal, temperate coniferous) | Long severe winters, short summers | Needle-leaved evergreen softwoods: spruce, fir, pine, cedar; little undergrowth; growing period 50–100 days | Canada, Alaska, Scandinavia, Siberia (N hemisphere only)
  [0:4.4] Mediterranean | Dry summer, wet winter | Evergreen oaks, cork oak, olive, shrubs (sclerophyll) | Med basin, California, Central Chile, SW Australia, Cape
  [0:4.5] Mangrove (tidal) | Tropical and subtropical saline coasts | Salt-tolerant; pneumatophores let roots breathe in waterlogged mud | Sundarbans; stabilise the shoreline, bulwark against the sea (UPSC 1996 A-R: both true, R explains A)
[0:5] # Tropical rainforest – what is asked
[0:6] - **Maximum biodiversity** of all biomes (over half of known species) – UPPCS 2012, 2014.
[0:7] - Luxuriant growth = **hot, wet climate all year + intense sunlight**; the soils are **not fertile** (leached) and there are **no seasons** (NDA 2015).
[0:8] - **Leaching** is the main soil problem (UPPCS 1998); leaf litter **decomposes faster than in any other biome**, so the forest floor is often bare; plants reach the canopy by climbing (lianas) or as **epiphytes** (UPSC 2021).
[0:9] - **Amazon** = "**lungs of the planet**"; **Amazon fires, 2019** (Brazil; INPE recorded a sharp rise in fires). **Congo Basin** = 2nd-largest rainforest.
[0:10] - Tropical rainforest has the **highest share of the world's forest area** among the forest types (APSC 2017; FAO: tropical forests ≈ 45% of all forest).
[0:11] - Equatorial region = **megathermal** (hot all year) with **aerial streamlets** (climbers/epiphytes, water dripping from the canopy); it is **not sclerophyll** and not a region of high development.
[0:12] # Taiga and tundra
[0:13] - **Taiga = largest land biome**; it has a **very wide** (not narrow) annual temperature range – **APPSC G1** "incorrect statement"; moderate precipitation, snow in winter; acidic **podzol**; lowest productivity of forests; northernmost temperate biome (UPPCS 2016: taiga = **temperate region**).
[0:14] - **Tundra:** mosses and lichens, no trees, permafrost; it is wet because **low temperatures make evaporation slow** and permafrost blocks drainage. **Alpine tundra** lies above the tree line.
[0:15] - **Tree line:** the altitude/latitude beyond which trees cannot grow. Himalaya: **lower in the west** (about 3,300–3,600 m) than in the east (3,600–3,800 m), because the Eastern Himalaya is **nearer the Equator and the sea** (Bay of Bengal moisture) – UPSC 1995, UPPCS 2005.
[0:16] # Adaptations
[0:17] - Dry regions: smaller leaves **reduce transpiration** (UPSC 2011). Deciduous trees shed leaves in winter/dry season **to conserve water** (APPSC G1). Desert animals = **xerocoles** (fossorial = burrowing; arboreal = tree-dwelling).

  [0:19.2] Taiga | Moose, beavers, lynx, wolves

[2:2] # Fixed facts
[2:3] - **Savanna trees are kept in check** by **fire, grazing herbivores and seasonal (dry-season) rainfall** – UPSC 2021 key (2, 3 and 4); termites and soil are not the answer.
[2:4] - Dense forests lie mostly **near the Equator**. Rain throughout the year: **equatorial region**. Monsoon rains: **tropical regions**.
[2:5] - **Equatorial passage** (UPSC 2015): clear morning, sea breeze, heat builds, dark clouds, rain with thunder in the afternoon, soon over.
[2:6] - UPSC 2002: equatorial regions do **not** have four seasons; Mediterranean summers are **dry**; China type has rain all year; **tropical highlands show vertical zonation** – only 3 and 4 are correct.
[2:7] - UPSC 2024: low annual and daily range + rain all year + 50–250 cm = **Marine West Coast**.
[2:8] - **Western Europe**: rain in all months, lies in the **westerlies** – both correct (UPPCS key).
[2:9] - **Norway** (west-coast type): rain all year with the maximum in autumn–winter; China, Brazil, Nigeria get summer rain.
[2:10] - **Monsoon climate** outside Asia: **SE United States** (Gulf type, slightly monsoonal); Pacific coast of Colombia is equatorial, Central California is Mediterranean.
[2:11] - Climate chart with cold winters (3.9°C), hot summers (27.8°C) and summer-maximum rain all year = **China type** (UPSC 1998).
[2:12] - **Zaire → Netherlands**: equatorial → hot desert → Mediterranean → West European (1, 3, 2, 4).
[2:13] - **Asia** has all climatic zones (Equator to near the North Pole).
[2:14] - Of the Earth's land, **humid regions** cover the largest share; drylands (arid, semi-arid, dry sub-humid, hyper-arid) together ≈ 41%.
[2:15] - Equatorial climate suits plants more than people (constant heat and humidity).

## SUB 3 | Mediterranean Climate and Hot Deserts
[3:0] # Mediterranean (asked 17 times)
[3:1] - **Hot dry summers, mild wet winters** – **dry summer** is the most noticeable feature (CDS 2019); heavy rain in **winter**.
[3:2] - **Cause: seasonal shift of pressure and wind belts.** Summer: belts shift north, **subtropical high** and dry offshore (land) trade winds → drought. Winter (winter solstice, sun over the Tropic of Capricorn): belts shift **south**, **westerlies** and their depressions bring rain. (In the S hemisphere the shift is the reverse.)
[3:3] - UPPCS 2022 A-R (winter rain; summer under dry land winds): official key – **both true and R explains A**.
[3:4] - NOT Mediterranean features: **dry winter, no dry season, dry and very severe winter** (APPSC G1).
[3:5] - Vegetation: **evergreen oak**, cork oak, **olive** (most typical), chestnut, walnut; **teak is NOT** Mediterranean (it is monsoon forest).
[3:6] - Economy: "**orchard lands of the world**" – **citrus fruits**, grapes, wine; horticulture and market gardening (also in densely peopled temperate NW Europe, NE USA).
[3:7] TABLE head: Region | Towns
  [3:7.0] Mediterranean basin | Rome, Athens, Central Spain (Csa: dry hot summer)
  [3:7.1] California | Los Angeles, San Francisco
  [3:7.2] Central Chile | Santiago
  [3:7.3] SW Cape | Cape Town
  [3:7.4] SW and S Australia | Perth, Adelaide
[3:8] - NOT Mediterranean: **New York** (east coast), **Darwin / Northern Australia**, **Pampas** (humid temperate grassland).
[3:9] # Hot deserts
[3:10] - **Desert**: annual rainfall **below 25 cm**.
[3:11] - Hot deserts 15–30°, on **western margins**; most likely **near 23°** (Tropics): **subtropical high pressure + offshore trade winds + cold currents**. Sahara, Kalahari, Namib, Atacama, Arabian, Thar, Great Australian.
[3:12] - Temperate (cold) deserts: Patagonia, **Gobi**, Ladakh.

## SUB 4 | Elements and Controls of Climate
[4:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:0.0] Weather | State of the atmosphere at a particular place and time
  [4:0.1] Climate | Average weather over a long period; WMO normal = 30 years
  [4:0.2] Climograph | Monthly mean max/min temperature as a line + rainfall as bars (APSLPRB 2023)
  [4:0.3] Elements (measured) | Temperature, pressure, wind, humidity, cloudiness, precipitation, sunshine
  [4:0.4] Controls (factors) | Latitude (main factor), altitude, distance from the sea, pressure and wind belts, ocean currents, relief, land cover
[4:1] - **Distance from a river is NOT a control** of climate (APPSC Endowment, APPSC GS 2025). Cloudiness is an element, not a control.
[4:2] - **Latitude is the main factor** deciding climate: the climatic belts follow latitude.
[4:3] TABLE head: Control | How it works | Indian example
  [4:3.0] Latitude | Insolation falls towards the poles | Mainland 8°4′–37°6′ N; south of the Tropic of Cancer = tropical
  [4:3.1] Altitude | Normal lapse rate about 6.5°C per 1,000 m | Shimla cooler than Amritsar
  [4:3.2] Distance from sea | Coasts equable; interiors extreme (continental) | A&N Islands equable
  [4:3.3] Relief / mountain barriers | Windward wet, leeward rain-shadow | Himalayas block cold Central Asian air
  [4:3.4] Ocean currents | Cold currents make tropical west coasts cool and dry | –
  [4:3.5] Winds, pressure belts | Monsoons, westerlies, western disturbances | –
[4:4] - Tropic of Cancer crosses **8 states**: Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram.
[4:5] - India has a **tropical monsoon** climate; IMD SW monsoon normal (Jun–Sep, 1971–2020) = **868.6 mm**.

## SUB 6 | Koeppen and Thornthwaite Classification
[6:0] - **Wladimir Koeppen**: empirical scheme on **mean temperature and precipitation**, closely linked to **natural vegetation** – "vegetation is the true index of climate" (UPPCS 2018).
[6:1] - **Thornthwaite (1931, revised 1948)**: **precipitation effectiveness** and **thermal efficiency**.
[6:2] TABLE head: Letter | Group | Criterion
  [6:2.0] A | Tropical humid | Coldest month above 18°C
  [6:2.1] B | Dry | Evaporation > precipitation (BS steppe, BW desert)
  [6:2.2] C | Warm temperate | Coldest month between –3 and 18°C
  [6:2.3] D | Cold snow-forest | Coldest month below –3°C
  [6:2.4] E | Polar | Warmest month below 10°C (ET tundra, EF ice cap)
  [6:2.5] H | Highland | Controlled by altitude
[6:3] - Second letters: **f** = no dry season; **m** = monsoon; **w** = dry winter; **s** = dry summer. Third letter **a** = hot summer: **Cfa = humid subtropical** (Af tropical wet, Aw tropical wet and dry, ET tundra).
