=== NOTES 4:20 | Elements and Factors of Climate; World Climatic Classification (koeppen)
## SUB 0 | Weather, Climate and Elements
[20:0:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [20:0:0.0] Weather | State of the atmosphere at a particular place and time
  [20:0:0.1] Climate | Average weather over a long period; WMO normal = 30 years
  [20:0:0.2] Climograph | Monthly temperature as a line + rainfall as bars; used to read the climate type
  [20:0:0.3] Biome | Large natural community of flora and fauna over a major habitat, shaped by climate
  [20:0:0.4] Anthromes | Human-made biomes shaped by sustained human use: cropland, villages, cities
  [20:0:0.5] Elements (measured conditions) | Factors / controls
  [20:0:0.6] Temperature, temperature range | Latitude (main factor)
  [20:0:0.7] Atmospheric pressure | Altitude
  [20:0:0.8] Wind | Distance from the sea (continentality)
  [20:0:0.9] Humidity, cloudiness | Pressure and wind belts, jet streams
  [20:0:0.10] Precipitation, sunshine | Ocean currents, relief, vegetation / land cover
[20:0:1] **Cloudiness** is an element, not a control. **Distance from a river** is NOT a control of climate (**APPSC GS 2025**).
## SUB 1 | Controls of Climate – How Each Operates
[20:1:0] TABLE head: Control | How it works | Indian example
  [20:1:0.0] Latitude | Insolation falls towards the poles | Mainland 8°4′–37°6′ N; south of the Tropic of Cancer = tropical
  [20:1:0.1] Altitude | Normal lapse rate about 6.5°C per 1,000 m | Shimla (7,866 ft) cooler than Amritsar (756 ft), same latitude
  [20:1:0.2] Distance from sea | Coasts equable; interiors extreme (continental) | A&N Islands equable, humid; monsoon arrives first
  [20:1:0.3] Relief / mountain barriers | Windward wet, leeward rain-shadow | Himalayas keep north India 3–8°C warmer in winter; without them north India would tend to be a desert
  [20:1:0.4] Ocean currents | Cold currents cool and dry tropical west coasts; warm currents make coasts warmer, wetter | –
  [20:1:0.5] Winds, pressure belts | Monsoons, westerlies, western disturbances, cyclones | –
[20:1:1] - **Tropic of Cancer crosses 8 states**: Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram (Mahi river crossed twice).
[20:1:2] - India has a **tropical monsoon** climate though it does not lie wholly within the tropics. IMD SW monsoon normal (Jun–Sep, 1971–2020) = **868.6 mm**.
[20:1:3] - At the **winter solstice**, pressure belts shift **south**.
## SUB 2 | Koeppen and Thornthwaite Classification
[20:2:0] - **Wladimir Koeppen**: empirical scheme on **mean temperature and precipitation**, linked to **natural vegetation**.
[20:2:1] - **Thornthwaite (1931, revised 1948)**: **precipitation effectiveness** and **thermal efficiency** (potential evapotranspiration).
[20:2:2] TABLE head: Letter | Group | Criterion
  [20:2:2.0] A | Tropical humid | Coldest month above 18°C
  [20:2:2.1] B | Dry | Evaporation > precipitation (BS = steppe, BW = desert)
  [20:2:2.2] C | Warm temperate | Coldest month between –3 and 18°C
  [20:2:2.3] D | Cold snow-forest | Coldest month below –3°C
  [20:2:2.4] E | Polar | Warmest month below 10°C (ET tundra, EF ice cap)
  [20:2:2.5] H | Highland | Controlled by altitude
[20:2:3] - Second letters: **f** = no dry season; **m** = monsoon; **w** = dry winter; **s** = dry summer.

## SUB 4 | Mediterranean Climate and Hot Deserts
[20:4:0] - **Mediterranean**: hot dry summers (subtropical high, dry offshore winds), mild wet winters (**westerly depressions**). Cause: **seasonal shift of pressure and wind belts**. Economy: citrus, grapes, wine ("**orchard lands**"); olive most typical; also chestnuts, walnuts.
[20:4:1] TABLE head: Region | Towns
  [20:4:1.0] Mediterranean basin | Rome, Athens
  [20:4:1.1] California | Los Angeles, San Francisco
  [20:4:1.2] Central Chile | Santiago
  [20:4:1.3] SW Cape | Cape Town
  [20:4:1.4] SW and S Australia | Perth, Adelaide
[20:4:2] NOT Mediterranean: **Darwin**, **New York**, **Northern Australia**. NOT Mediterranean features: dry winter, no dry season, severe winter. **Teak** is not a Mediterranean tree.
[20:4:3] - **Hot deserts** (15–30°, western margins; deserts most likely near 23°): subtropical high + offshore trade winds + cold currents. Sahara, Kalahari, Namib, Atacama, Arabian, Thar, Great Australian.
[20:4:4] - **Temperate deserts**: Patagonia, Gobi, Ladakh.
## SUB 5 | Forests and Biomes
[20:5:0] TABLE head: Forest | Climate | Vegetation | Distribution
  [20:5:0.0] Tropical rainforest (selva) | Over 200 cm rain; hot, humid | Broadleaf evergreen, not shed together; climbers, epiphytes | Amazon, Congo, Indonesia; 10°N–10°S
  [20:5:0.1] Tropical deciduous (monsoon) | 100–200 cm seasonal rain | Shed leaves in dry season | India, Myanmar, Africa
  [20:5:0.2] Temperate deciduous | Distinct seasons; between tropics and polar (APPSC G2 2025) | Broadleaf, shed in autumn | E USA, W Europe, E Asia
  [20:5:0.3] Taiga (boreal) | Cold, long winters | Needle-leaved evergreen softwood, conical | Canada, Scandinavia, Siberia, Alaska
  [20:5:0.4] Mediterranean | Dry summer, wet winter | Evergreen shrubs, oaks, cork | Med basin, California, Chile, SW Australia, Cape
  [20:5:0.5] Mangrove (tidal) | Saline coasts | Salt-tolerant, pneumatophores (roots breathe in waterlogged mud) | Sundarbans; tropical/subtropical coasts; stabilise shoreline
[20:5:1] - **Rainforest**: highest biodiversity (over half of known species); "**lungs of the planet**" (Amazon); **leaching** is the main soil problem; soils nutrient-poor; litter decomposes fastest, ground often bare; cleared land loses fertility after **2–3 crops**. **Congo Basin** = 2nd-largest rainforest (Cameroon, CAR, DR Congo, Rep. Congo, Eq. Guinea, Gabon).
[20:5:2] - **Taiga**: **largest land biome**; **very wide temperature range** (not narrow; **APPSC G1 2023, 2024**); moderate precipitation; acidic **podzol**; lowest productivity of forests; spruce, fir, pine; northernmost temperate biome.
[20:5:3] - Deciduous trees shed leaves to cut transpiration.
[20:5:4] - **Tree line**: altitude/latitude beyond which trees cannot grow (**APPSC GS 2019**); alpine tundra above it. Lower in Western Himalaya (3,300–3,600 m) than Eastern (3,600–3,800 m).
