=== Forests and Biomes of the World
# Biome basics (asked 27 times)
- **Biome:** a large naturally occurring community of flora and fauna occupying a **major habitat**, shaped mainly by climate (APPSC Panchayat Secretary).
- **Anthromes** (anthropogenic biomes): units shaped by sustained human use – croplands, villages, cities.
- Geomorphic factors of plant and animal distribution: **slope angle, slope aspect and relative relief** (CDS 2020).
| Forest | Climate | Vegetation | Distribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tropical rainforest (selva)** | Near the Equator (about 10°N–10°S); hot and wet all year, rain over 200 cm | Broadleaf **evergreen** (leaves not shed together); mahogany, ebony; climbers (lianas) and **epiphytes** | **Amazon (Brazil)**, **Congo Basin**, Indonesia; **Ivory Coast** (Gulf of Guinea) |
| **Tropical deciduous (monsoon)** | 100–200 cm seasonal rain | Shed leaves in the **dry season**; teak, sal | Most extensive in **Asia** (India, Myanmar, Thailand); also Africa, S America, N Australia |
| **Temperate deciduous** | Mid latitudes **between the tropics and the polar regions**; four seasons (APPSC G2 Mains 2025) | Broadleaf, shed in autumn; oak, beech, maple | E USA, W Europe, E Asia |
| **Taiga (boreal, temperate coniferous)** | Long severe winters, short summers | Needle-leaved evergreen softwoods: **spruce, fir, pine, cedar**; little undergrowth; growing period 50–100 days | Canada, Alaska, Scandinavia, Siberia (N hemisphere only) |
| Mediterranean | Dry summer, wet winter | Evergreen oaks, **cork oak**, olive, shrubs (sclerophyll) | Med basin, California, Central Chile, SW Australia, Cape |
| **Mangrove (tidal)** | Tropical and subtropical saline coasts | Salt-tolerant; **pneumatophores** let roots breathe in waterlogged mud | Sundarbans; stabilise the shoreline, bulwark against the sea (UPSC 1996 A-R: both true, R explains A) |
# Tropical rainforest – what is asked
- **Maximum biodiversity** of all biomes (over half of known species) – UPPCS 2012, 2014.
- Luxuriant growth = **hot, wet climate all year + intense sunlight**; the soils are **not fertile** (leached) and there are **no seasons** (NDA 2015).
- **Leaching** is the main soil problem (UPPCS 1998); leaf litter **decomposes faster than in any other biome**, so the forest floor is often bare; plants reach the canopy by climbing (lianas) or as **epiphytes** (UPSC 2021).
- **Amazon** = "**lungs of the planet**"; **Amazon fires, 2019** (Brazil; INPE recorded a sharp rise in fires). **Congo Basin** = 2nd-largest rainforest.
- Tropical rainforest has the **highest share of the world's forest area** among the forest types (APSC 2017; FAO: tropical forests ≈ 45% of all forest).
- Equatorial region = **megathermal** (hot all year) with **aerial streamlets** (climbers/epiphytes, water dripping from the canopy); it is **not sclerophyll** and not a region of high development.
# Taiga and tundra
- **Taiga = largest land biome**; it has a **very wide** (not narrow) annual temperature range – **APPSC G1** "incorrect statement"; moderate precipitation, snow in winter; acidic **podzol**; lowest productivity of forests; northernmost temperate biome (UPPCS 2016: taiga = **temperate region**).
- **Tundra:** mosses and lichens, no trees, permafrost; it is wet because **low temperatures make evaporation slow** and permafrost blocks drainage. **Alpine tundra** lies above the tree line.
- **Tree line:** the altitude/latitude beyond which trees cannot grow. Himalaya: **lower in the west** (about 3,300–3,600 m) than in the east (3,600–3,800 m), because the Eastern Himalaya is **nearer the Equator and the sea** (Bay of Bengal moisture) – UPSC 1995, UPPCS 2005.
# Adaptations
- Dry regions: smaller leaves **reduce transpiration** (UPSC 2011). Deciduous trees shed leaves in winter/dry season **to conserve water** (APPSC G1). Desert animals = **xerocoles** (fossorial = burrowing; arboreal = tree-dwelling).

# Fixed facts
- **Savanna trees are kept in check** by **fire, grazing herbivores and seasonal (dry-season) rainfall** – UPSC 2021 key (2, 3 and 4); termites and soil are not the answer.
- Dense forests lie mostly **near the Equator**. Rain throughout the year: **equatorial region**. Monsoon rains: **tropical regions**.
- **Equatorial passage** (UPSC 2015): clear morning, sea breeze, heat builds, dark clouds, rain with thunder in the afternoon, soon over.
- UPSC 2002: equatorial regions do **not** have four seasons; Mediterranean summers are **dry**; China type has rain all year; **tropical highlands show vertical zonation** – only 3 and 4 are correct.
- UPSC 2024: low annual and daily range + rain all year + 50–250 cm = **Marine West Coast**.
- **Western Europe**: rain in all months, lies in the **westerlies** – both correct (UPPCS key).
- **Norway** (west-coast type): rain all year with the maximum in autumn–winter; China, Brazil, Nigeria get summer rain.
- **Monsoon climate** outside Asia: **SE United States** (Gulf type, slightly monsoonal); Pacific coast of Colombia is equatorial, Central California is Mediterranean.
- Climate chart with cold winters (3.9°C), hot summers (27.8°C) and summer-maximum rain all year = **China type** (UPSC 1998).
- **Zaire → Netherlands**: equatorial → hot desert → Mediterranean → West European (1, 3, 2, 4).
- **Asia** has all climatic zones (Equator to near the North Pole).
- Of the Earth's land, **humid regions** cover the largest share; drylands (arid, semi-arid, dry sub-humid, hyper-arid) together ≈ 41%.
- Equatorial climate suits plants more than people (constant heat and humidity).

=== Mediterranean Climate and Hot Deserts
# Mediterranean (asked 17 times)
- **Hot dry summers, mild wet winters** – **dry summer** is the most noticeable feature (CDS 2019); heavy rain in **winter**.
- **Cause: seasonal shift of pressure and wind belts.** Summer: belts shift north, **subtropical high** and dry offshore (land) trade winds → drought. Winter (winter solstice, sun over the Tropic of Capricorn): belts shift **south**, **westerlies** and their depressions bring rain. (In the S hemisphere the shift is the reverse.)
- UPPCS 2022 A-R (winter rain; summer under dry land winds): official key – **both true and R explains A**.
- NOT Mediterranean features: **dry winter, no dry season, dry and very severe winter** (APPSC G1).
- Vegetation: **evergreen oak**, cork oak, **olive** (most typical), chestnut, walnut; **teak is NOT** Mediterranean (it is monsoon forest).
- Economy: "**orchard lands of the world**" – **citrus fruits**, grapes, wine; horticulture and market gardening (also in densely peopled temperate NW Europe, NE USA).
| Region | Towns |
|---|---|
| Mediterranean basin | Rome, Athens, **Central Spain** (Csa: dry hot summer) |
| California | **Los Angeles**, San Francisco |
| Central Chile | Santiago |
| SW Cape | **Cape Town** |
| SW and S Australia | **Perth, Adelaide** |
- NOT Mediterranean: **New York** (east coast), **Darwin / Northern Australia**, **Pampas** (humid temperate grassland).
# Hot deserts
- **Desert**: annual rainfall **below 25 cm**.
- Hot deserts 15–30°, on **western margins**; most likely **near 23°** (Tropics): **subtropical high pressure + offshore trade winds + cold currents**. Sahara, Kalahari, Namib, Atacama, Arabian, Thar, Great Australian.
- Temperate (cold) deserts: Patagonia, **Gobi**, Ladakh.

=== Elements and Controls of Climate
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Weather | State of the atmosphere at a particular place and time |
| Climate | Average weather over a long period; WMO normal = **30 years** |
| **Climograph** | Monthly mean max/min temperature as a **line** + rainfall as **bars** (APSLPRB 2023) |
| Elements (measured) | Temperature, pressure, wind, humidity, **cloudiness**, precipitation, sunshine |
| Controls (factors) | **Latitude** (main factor), **altitude**, distance from the sea, pressure and wind belts, ocean currents, relief, land cover |
- **Distance from a river is NOT a control** of climate (APPSC Endowment, APPSC GS 2025). Cloudiness is an element, not a control.
- **Latitude is the main factor** deciding climate: the climatic belts follow latitude.
| Control | How it works | Indian example |
|---|---|---|
| Latitude | Insolation falls towards the poles | Mainland 8°4′–37°6′ N; south of the Tropic of Cancer = tropical |
| **Altitude** | Normal lapse rate about **6.5°C per 1,000 m** | Shimla cooler than Amritsar |
| Distance from sea | Coasts **equable**; interiors extreme (continental) | A&N Islands equable |
| Relief / mountain barriers | Windward wet, leeward rain-shadow | **Himalayas block cold Central Asian air** |
| Ocean currents | **Cold currents make tropical west coasts cool and dry** | – |
| Winds, pressure belts | Monsoons, westerlies, western disturbances | – |
- Tropic of Cancer crosses **8 states**: Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram.
- India has a **tropical monsoon** climate; IMD SW monsoon normal (Jun–Sep, 1971–2020) = **868.6 mm**.

=== Koeppen and Thornthwaite Classification
- **Wladimir Koeppen**: empirical scheme on **mean temperature and precipitation**, closely linked to **natural vegetation** – "vegetation is the true index of climate" (UPPCS 2018).
- **Thornthwaite (1931, revised 1948)**: **precipitation effectiveness** and **thermal efficiency**.
| Letter | Group | Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| A | Tropical humid | Coldest month above 18°C |
| B | Dry | **Evaporation > precipitation** (BS steppe, BW desert) |
| C | Warm temperate | Coldest month between –3 and 18°C |
| D | Cold snow-forest | Coldest month below –3°C |
| E | Polar | Warmest month below **10°C** (ET tundra, EF ice cap) |
| H | Highland | Controlled by altitude |
- Second letters: **f** = no dry season; **m** = monsoon; **w** = dry winter; **s** = dry summer. Third letter **a** = hot summer: **Cfa = humid subtropical** (Af tropical wet, Aw tropical wet and dry, ET tundra).
