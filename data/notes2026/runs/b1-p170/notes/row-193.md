=== Kuka (Namdhari) Movement – Punjab, Ram Singh, First Boycott
- **Region: Punjab** (not U.P., Malabar, Bihar, Bengal or Maharashtra); founded **1840** by **Bhagat Jawahar Mal (Sian Sahib)** in western Punjab (Spectrum) – he **started** the Kuka revolt.
- **Baba (Satguru) Ram Singh** – disciple of Balak Singh; **organised** it and founded the **Namdhari** sect (**1857**, **Bhaini, Ludhiana**) – not Guru Ram Das, Nanak or Gobind Singh, not Nankana Sahib.
- Practices: **white clothes**, no meat, alcohol or drugs, a **lathi**; against idol, tomb, tree and snake worship and **Brahmin-led rituals**; widow remarriage, intermarriage.
- **Boycott** of British **goods, English education, law courts and the postal system** → **Ram Singh = first to use non-cooperation and boycott (Swadeshi) as a political weapon** (APPSC 2025) – before Tilak, Pal or Gandhi.
- Suppression: **Malerkotla clash, 1872** – Kukas blown from cannons; Ram Singh **deported to Rangoon (Burma)**; Kuka movement under Viceroy Northbrook.
- Kuka and Wahabi both began as religious revolts and became anti-British political movements.
> Kuka = Punjab; begun by Jawahar Mal (1840); Ram Singh, Bhaini, boycott; Malerkotla 1872.

=== Singh Sabha, Nirankari and the Akali Movement
- **Singh Sabha:** **Amritsar, 1873**; more radical **Lahore Singh Sabha, 1879**; aims – modern Western education for Sikhs through **Khalsa schools**, **Gurmukhi/Punjabi** literature, remove un-Sikh practices, counter missionaries.
- **Khalsa College, Amritsar – 1892** (foundation stone) by Singh Sabha leaders – not 1894, 1896 or 1898.
- **Nirankari:** **Baba Dayal Das** (1855); God as **Nirankar** (formless); issued **hukamnamas**; own priests; **cooperated with the British** and became a separate group in Sikhism.
| Akali step | Year |
|---|---|
| **SGPC** formed; Akali (Gurdwara Reform) Movement begins | **1920** |
| **Nankana Sahib massacre** | **1921** |
| **Guru-ka-Bagh** morcha | **1922** |
| **Sikh Gurdwaras Act** – gurdwaras under the SGPC | 1922, amended **1925** |
- **Akali movement** = offshoot of the Singh Sabha; freed gurdwaras from corrupt **Udasi mahants**; leader **Kartar Singh Jhabbar**; later the **Akali Dal**. It is a **Sikh** movement – "Akali Movement – Tamilians" is the **wrong pair** (Rahnumai – Parsis, Aligarh – Muslims, Arya Samaj – Hindus are right).
> Singh Sabha 1873 Amritsar → Khalsa College 1892 → Akali / SGPC 1920 → Act 1925.

=== Parsi Reform – Rahnumai Mazdayasnan Sabha, 1851
- **Rahnumai Mazdayasnan Sabha** (Religious Reform Association), **Bombay, 1851** – to reform the **Parsis** (not Sikhs, Muslims or Anglo-Indians).
- Founders: **Naoroji Furdonji** (first president), **Dadabhai Naoroji**, **K.R. Cama**, **S.S. Bengalee**.
- Organ: **Rast Goftar** ("Truth-Teller"), edited by **Dadabhai Naoroji** – not Malabari.
- Work: restore Zoroastrian purity; **women's education**, removal of purdah, higher marriage age, legal status and uniform inheritance laws for Parsi women.
- **Behramji M. Malabari** – child-marriage campaign → **Age of Consent Act, 1891**; Seva Sadan (1908).
> Rahnumai = Parsis, Bombay 1851, Furdonji, Rast Goftar.

=== Other Hindu Bodies – Radhasoami, Dev Samaj, Dharma Sabha, Bharat Dharma Mahamandal
| Body | Founder | Place, year |
|---|---|---|
| **Radhasoami Satsang** | **Shiv Dayal Singh (Khatri) – "Soamiji Maharaj"**, also called Shiv Dayal Sahab | **Agra, 1861** (not Haridas Swami, Agnihotri, Shraddhanand) |
| **Dev Samaj** | **Shiv Narayan Agnihotri** (ex-Brahmo) | **Lahore, 1887**; text Deva Shastra |
| **Dharma Sabha** | **Radhakant Deb** | **Calcutta, 1830** – orthodox; **opposed the abolition of sati** |
| **Bharat Dharma Mahamandal** | Pandit Din Dayalu Sharma | Haridwar, 1887; combined 1902, HQ Varanasi – orthodox Hindus |
| **Sanatan Dharma Rakshini Sabha** | orthodox | **Calcutta** – the correctly matched pair (UPPCS 2022) |
- **Radhasoami:** one God, the **guru** and satsang; inner spiritual life.
- **Dev Samaj:** eternity of the soul, supremacy of the guru, good action; no bribes, alcohol or meat; against child marriage.
- **Bengali leader who opposed reform and supported orthodoxy = Radhakant Deb** (yet he favoured Western education, even for girls).
> Radhasoami = Agra 1861; Dev Samaj = Lahore 1887; Dharma Sabha = Calcutta 1830.
