=== Military Grievances – the Sepoys Start the Revolt
- The revolt **began as a mutiny of the sepoys** (not zamindars, peasants or plantation workers); **discontent of soldiers** was the first trigger; civilians joined later.
| Grievance | Detail |
|---|---|
| **General Service Enlistment Act 1856** (**Canning**) | New Bengal Army recruits to **serve overseas** – crossing the sea meant **loss of caste** |
| **Bhatta** | **Foreign service allowance** stopped for service in **Sindh and Punjab** |
| Promotion | An Indian could rise **no higher than Subedar** |
| Pay | Sepoy paid **much less** than his British counterpart of the same rank |
| Caste marks | Ban on caste and sectarian marks, beards, turbans |
| Post Office Act 1856 | Free postage of sepoys withdrawn |
| Troop ratio | About **2,32,000 sepoys : 45,000 Europeans** (about **5:1**); Indians about 86% of the army |
| **Awadh** | **"Nursery of the Bengal Army"**; the **largest number** of rebel sepoys came from **Oudh** (over 75,000) |
- Sepoys = **"peasants in uniform"** – they carried rural grievances into the army.
- European troops had been drawn off for the **Crimean and Persian wars**.
- **Not a cause:** the **inefficiency of the officers** (BPSC 2018).
- **Earlier mutinies:** Bengal 1764; **Vellore 1806** (new dress code, ban on caste marks and earrings); **Barrackpore Nov 1824** (**47th NI** refused to go to **Burma by sea**); 38th NI 1852; 1844 battalions over batta. The **Santhal revolt 1855-56** was a **civil**, not a sepoy, revolt.

=== Immediate Cause – Enfield Rifle and Greased Cartridges
- **Immediate cause:** the new **Enfield rifle**; its cartridge paper, reportedly greased with **cow and pig fat**, had to be **bitten off** – offended Hindus and Muslims alike → suspicion that the British were **interfering in religion** and converting them.
- Enfield **replaced the Brown Bess** musket; introduced **December 1856** (app notes; some accounts say late 1856 or Jan 1857); training at **Dum Dum, Ambala and Sialkot**.
- **Bone-dust rumour:** the Company's **flour (atta)** was said to be mixed with **bone dust**.
- **Chapatis** passed from village to village across **North India**; symbols of the revolt = **lotus and chapati**.
| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| 19th NI, **Berhampur** – refused cartridges | **Feb 1857** |
| **Mangal Pandey** (**34th BNI**), Barrackpore | **29 March 1857**; hanged **8 April 1857** |
| 85 sepoys of the **3rd Native Cavalry**, Meerut – 10 years' prison | 9 May 1857 |
| **Meerut rising** | **10 May 1857** |
| Rebels at Delhi proclaim **Bahadur Shah II** Shahenshah-e-Hindustan | 11-12 May 1857 |
- **First martyr:** **Mangal Pandey**.

=== Socio-religious Causes
| Measure | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Abolition of Sati | **1829** | **William Bentinck** (not Dalhousie) – resented by orthodox opinion |
| **Religious (Caste) Disabilities Act** / Lex Loci | **1850** | A **convert** to Christianity could **inherit ancestral property** (Spectrum misprints 1856) |
| **Hindu Widow Remarriage Act** | **1856** | Orthodoxy resented it |
- **Christian missionaries** preached under **police protection**; Western education, **railways and the telegraph** fed fears of mass conversion.
- After 1858 the Crown promised **religious neutrality**.

=== Economic, Political and Administrative Causes
| Class / cause | Grievance |
|---|---|
| Peasants | High, rigid land revenue → **debt** → land lost to **moneylenders and traders** |
| **Taluqdars of Awadh** | About **21,000** dispossessed (Summary Settlement 1856) |
| Estate owners | **Inam Commission (1852, Bombay)** – about 20,000 estates confiscated |
| Artisans | British machine goods **duty-free**, Indian goods heavily taxed; loss of princely patronage |
| Annexed courts | Officials, retainers and soldiers **thrown out of work** |
| Administration | **Corruption** in police and lower courts – courts favoured **the rich**; Indians **excluded from high posts** |
- **"Absentee sovereignty"** and **"drain of wealth"** – transfer of resources to Britain.
- Lapse (Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur), Awadh "misgovernance" (Wajid Ali Shah deposed), Mughal title.
