## SUB 0 | Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and the Aligarh Movement
[0:0] # Sir Syed (1817-1898)
[0:1] - In the Company's **judicial service** in 1857; **loyalist**; knighted 1888; **never connected with the Congress** – asked Muslims to keep away from it (Ferozshah Mehta, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Ghaffar Khan were all Congressmen).
[0:2] - Aim: modernise Muslims through **English / Western scientific education** and a **rational reading of the Quran**; promoted **Urdu**; opposed purdah and polygamy.
[0:3] - Early view: India a bride, Hindus and Muslims "**her two eyes**"; turned after the **Hindi-Urdu controversy (1867)**.

  [0:4.0] Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind | 1859 | Sir Syed, "Causes of the Indian Revolt"
  [0:4.1] Scientific Society | 1864, Ghazipur (PMF: 1863) → Aligarh | Sir Syed – translated Western science, history into Urdu; not Cornwallis or a company
  [0:4.2] MAO school (Madarsatul Uloom Musalmanan-i-Hind) | 1875 | Sir Syed Ahmad Khan – not Jinnah, Muhammad Ali or Shaukat Ali
  [0:4.3] Renamed Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College | 1877 | Modelled on Oxford and Cambridge – the centre of the Aligarh Movement
  [0:4.4] Muhammadan Educational Conference | 1886 | Sir Syed
  [0:4.5] United Indian Patriotic Association | 1888 | Sir Syed + Raja Shiva Prasad of Benaras – to counter the Congress
  [0:4.6] MAO Defence Association | 1893 | Sir Syed with Theodore Beck; loyalist, anti-Congress
  [0:4.7] Aligarh Muslim University | 1920 | MAO College raised to AMU
  [0:4.8] Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq | – | Sir Syed's journal

[0:5] - **Years-match (APPSC AEE):** Arya Samaj **1875**, Prarthana Samaj **1867**, MAO College **1877**, SNDP Yogam **1903** → A-1, B-4, C-2, D-3.
[0:6] # Aligarh Movement and MAO principals
[0:7] - **Initial objective:** promote **modern Western scientific education** among Indian Muslims (not a Muslim state, not traditional madrasa reform, not Hindu-Muslim unity against the British); social reform; built a **modern educated Muslim middle class**.
[0:8] - **Principals:** first **H.G.I. Siddons**; then **Theodore Beck (1883-99)** – encouraged Muslim separatism / communalism and **turned Sir Syed against the Congress**; then Theodore Morison.
[0:9] - **Pairs (IAS 1998):** Beck – MAO College ✔; Ilbert Bill – Ripon ✔; Pherozeshah Mehta – INC ✔; **Badruddin Tyabji – Muslim League ✘** (he was INC President 1887) → **1, 2 and 3**.
[0:10] - Later Aligarh leaders: Chiragh Ali, Altaf Husain Hali, Nazir Ahmad, Mohsin-ul-Mulk (not Mahmud-ul-Hasan – Deoband).
[0:11] - **Mohammedan Literary Society: Calcutta, 1863, Nawab Abdul Latif** – "Father of Muslim Renaissance in Bengal" (not Sir Syed, Ameer Ali, Salimullah); not Aligarh.
[0:12] > Aligarh = Sir Syed, MAO 1875/1877, Beck; Literary Society = Abdul Latif, Calcutta 1863.

## SUB 1 | Deoband School (Darul Uloom) and Nadwatul Ulama
[1:0] - **Darul Uloom, Deoband, 1866** (PMF: 1867), **Saharanpur district, United Provinces = Uttar Pradesh**, by **Muhammad Qasim Nanautavi and Rashid Ahmad Gangohi** – not 1885, 1888 or 1900.
[1:1] - **Aims:** propagate pure teachings of the **Quran and Hadis**, **keep alive jihad against foreign rulers**, **train religious leaders** – not Western education.
[1:2] - Politics: **welcomed the Congress**; **fatwa (1888)** against Sir Syed's United Patriotic Association and MAO Association; **Mahmud-ul-Hasan** gave it political content → **Jamiat-ul-Ulema** (1919).
[1:3] TABLE head: Feature | Aligarh | Deoband
  [1:3.0] Learning | Western, scientific | Quran and Hadis
  [1:3.1] British | Loyal | Anti-British
  [1:3.2] Congress | Opposed | Welcomed
[1:4] - **Nadwatul Ulama:** decided at **Kanpur** 1893, first session 1894; prime founder and first manager **Muhammad Ali Mungeri**; office moved to **Lucknow 1898**.
[1:5] - **Shibli Numani** – supporter of Deoband who wanted English and European science taught; "**founded the Nadwatal Ulama and Darul Uloom in Lucknow in 1894-96**" (Spectrum) → so "Dar-ul-Ulum was established by" (UPPCS 2014, options Shibli / Hussain Ahmad / Chakralavi / Ahmad Raza Khan) = **Shibli Numani**. (Deoband's own Darul Uloom = Nanautavi and Gangohi.)
[1:6] > Deoband 1866 (Nanautavi, Gangohi), UP; Nadwa Lucknow (Shibli, Mungeri).

## SUB 2 | Wahabi Movement – Barelvi, Patna, Anti-British; Hyderabad Angle
[2:0] - Inspired by **Abdul Wahab of Arabia** and **Shah Waliullah** (1702-63); popularised by Shah Abdul Aziz; Indian founder **Syed Ahmad Barelvi** (of Rae Bareli).
[2:1] - Aim: return to pure Islam; convert **Dar-ul-Harb into Dar-ul-Islam**; fought the **Sikhs** in Punjab, then after 1849 the **British** → character: **anti-British** (APPSC G2 2011, three versions) – not pro-British, anti-Muslim or merely pro-Muslim.
[2:2] - **Chief centre: Patna** (base at Sithana in the NW tribal belt) – not Lahore, Amritsar or Pune.
[2:3] - Main followers: **Muslim peasants and weavers / artisans**; trials at **Ambala (1864)** and **Patna (1865)**; crushed in the 1870s.
[2:4] - **Titu Mir** (Narkelberia, 1831), a disciple of Barelvi, led Muslim peasants of Bengal against zamindars and indigo planters.
[2:5] - **Hyderabad:** Wahabi conspiracy **1839** led by **Mubariz-ud-Daula**, the Nizam's brother, who resisted British influence in the Deccan; arrested 1839, died in prison.
[2:6] - **Nasaraiah** (Sayyid Muslim, d. 1825): popular among the **Madigas**; preached **one God**, opposed idol worship; **Vinukonda–Narasaraopet** (Guntur).
[2:7] - **Osmania University** (Hyderabad, 1918) taught through **Urdu**.
[2:8] > Wahabi = Barelvi, Patna, anti-British; Hyderabad 1839 = Mubariz-ud-Daula.

## SUB 3 | Faraizi, Taiyuni and Ahmadiyya Movements
[3:0] - **Faraizi (Fara'idi) Movement:** founded **1818** by **Haji Shariatullah** in **East Bengal (Faridpur)** – the Faraizis = **followers of Haji Shariatullah** (not Dadu Dayal, Arya Samaj or Muslim League); aim: enforce Islamic duties (*faraiz*), purge un-Islamic practices.
[3:1] - **Dudu (Dadu) Miyan**, his son, made it revolutionary from **1840** – peasants against **zamindars and indigo planters**; told followers not to pay rent; arrested 1847, died 1862 → **leader of the Farazi rebellion** (not Wazir Ali, Shamsher Gazi, Aga Muhammad Raza).
[3:2] - Founder vs leader trap: *founder* = Haji Shariatullah; *leader / rebellion* = Dudu Miyan.
[3:3] - **Taiyuni movement:** **Karamat Ali Jaunpuri** (Bengal) – opposed Faraizi radicalism; pairing it with Rashid Ahmad Gangohi (Deoband) is the **wrong pair**.
[3:4] - **Ahmadiyya:** **Mirza Ghulam Ahmad, Qadian (Gurdaspur, Punjab), 1889** (not 1866); claimed to be the Messiah; liberal, spread Western education, **opposed jihad**; rejected by orthodox Muslims.
[3:5] - Pairs (APPSC G2): Aligarh – Sir Syed ✔; Faraizi – Haji Shariatullah ✔; Ahmadiyya – Mirza Ghulam Ahmad ✔; Taiyuni – Gangohi ✘.
[3:6] > Faraizi = Haji Shariatullah 1818 (Dudu Miyan led); Ahmadiyya = Qadian 1889.
