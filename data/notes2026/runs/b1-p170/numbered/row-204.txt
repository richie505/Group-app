## SUB 0 | Moderates vs Extremists – Leaders, Methods and Ideology
[0:0] # Who were the Extremists
[0:1] - **Extremists** = **Militant / Assertive Nationalists** (Neo-nationalists, **Garam Dal**); trend from the **1890s**, concrete by **1905**; dominated the movement **after 1906**.
[0:2] - **Four pillars:** **Lal-Bal-Pal + Ghosh** – **Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Aurobindo Ghosh**; Tilak the most outstanding.
[0:3] TABLE head: Regional group | Leader
  [0:3.0] Maharashtra | Bal Gangadhar Tilak (with Vishnu Shastri Chiplunkar)
  [0:3.1] Bengal | Bipin Chandra Pal, Aurobindo Ghosh (also Raj Narain Bose, Ashwini Kumar Datta)
  [0:3.2] Punjab | Lala Lajpat Rai (with Ajit Singh)
  [0:3.3] Delhi (Swadeshi) | Syed Haider Raza
[0:4] - **Gujarat** was NOT a centre of Extremism (APPSC G1 2002 odd one out).
[0:5] - **Moderates (never Extremists):** Dadabhai Naoroji, **Gokhale** (their leader), Pherozeshah Mehta, S.N. Banerjea, M.G. Ranade, G.V. Joshi, W.C. Bonnerjee, R.C. Dutt.
[0:6] > Statement trap: "Tilak, Pal, Lajpat Rai and Aurobindo were Moderates" ✗.
[0:7] # How they differed
[0:8] TABLE head: Basis | Moderates (1885-1905) | Extremists (1905 onwards)
  [0:8.0] Faith in British | Believed in British justice, "providential mission" | No faith in British fairness or generosity; rule exploitative
  [0:8.1] Goal | Reforms; self-government within the Empire, gradual | Swaraj – complete freedom, by own effort
  [0:8.2] Method | Constitutional agitation; the 3 Ps – prayer, petition, protest | Boycott, Swadeshi, national education, passive resistance, mass agitation; 3 Ss – self-reliance, self-sacrifice, strong will
  [0:8.3] Masses | Not ready for politics | Immense faith in the masses
  [0:8.4] Inspiration | Western liberal thought | Indian history, culture, Hindu traditional symbols
  [0:8.5] Social base | Zamindars, upper middle class | Educated middle and lower middle class
[0:9] - **Fundamental difference:** **belief in British fairness and the preferred mode of struggle** (not social base or princely states).
[0:10] - **Extremist ideology (UPSC 1998):** **obtaining self-government by aggressive means** in place of petitions and constitutional ways.
[0:11] - They called Moderate methods **"political mendicancy"**; **Tilak** attacked the "politics of **prayer, petition and protest**", and the prayer-and-petition policy ended under his lead.
[0:12] - Remarks on the Congress: "**begging institute**" – credited to **B.C. Pal** in most keys (NextGen p.371 credits Aurobindo; Pal: Congress "playing with bubbles"); Tilak: "Rights are not begged, they are claimed".
[0:13] - **Passive resistance** doctrine – **Aurobindo** (series in *Bande Mataram*, 1907); **Swadeshi** – Aurobindo the main Bengal leader.
[0:14] - **Word Swaraj:** first used by **Dayanand Saraswati**; **Deuskar** brought it to Bengal (1902); as a political demand it is **Tilak's** ("Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it").
[0:15] - **Atmashakti** = self-reliance (Swadeshi enterprises, national schools); **spiritual nationalism** saw the nation as a **mother goddess** (Bande Mataram, Bhavani Mandir).
[0:16] - **Contribution:** turned nationalism into a **mass movement** and made **Swaraj** its goal.
[0:17] - **Criticism:** Hindu revivalist symbols (Ganapati, Shivaji festivals, Bhavani) and **harping on the Hindu past** kept most **Muslims** away; politically radical but socially conservative.

## SUB 1 | Bal Gangadhar Tilak (1856-1920)
[1:0] # Life and titles
[1:1] - Born **23 July 1856, Ratnagiri**; with Agarkar and Chiplunkar – New English School (1880), **Deccan Education Society** (1884), **Fergusson College** (1885).
[1:2] - Joined the Congress **1890**; **never INC President** (Lajpat Rai, Besant, Motilal, Naoroji, Gokhale, Bose, Kripalani, Sanjiva Reddy, M.A. Ansari all were).
[1:3] TABLE head: Title | Given by / when
  [1:3.0] Lokmanya ("accepted by the people as their leader") | by the people, after his 1897 sedition trial (Rand murder by the Chapekar brothers – revolutionary phase)
  [1:3.1] Father of Indian Unrest | Valentine Chirol, *Indian Unrest* (1910); Tilak sued him for libel
  [1:3.2] Father of the Indian National Movement | popular title in quiz books
[1:4] - **Radical in politics, conservative in social matters** – opposed the **Age of Consent Bill (1891)**.
[1:5] # Newspapers, festivals, books
[1:6] - **Kesari** (**Marathi – vernacular**) and **Mahratta** (English), both **1881**.
[1:7] - Made **Ganapati festival** a public, political festival (**1893**) and started **Shivaji festival** (**1895**).
[1:8] - Slogan: "**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it**"; Kesari: Swarajya the trunk, Swadeshi and Boycott the branches.
[1:9] - Books: **Gita Rahasya** (Marathi, written in **Mandalay jail**); **The Arctic Home in the Vedas** (1903 – Arctic as the **original home of the Aryans**); *The Orion* (1893).
[1:10] - Quote (1918): "**If God were to tolerate untouchability, I would not recognise him as God at all.**"
[1:11] # Trials and death
[1:12] TABLE head: Year | Sedition trial
  [1:12.0] 1897 | 18 months; Max Muller pleaded for him ("my interest in Tilak is that of a Sanskrit scholar")
  [1:12.1] 1908 | Kesari articles on the Muzaffarpur bomb → 6 years in Mandalay (Burma); released June 1914
  [1:12.2] 1916 | Acquitted in the High Court
[1:13] - **Home Rule League:** **28 April 1916** (Belgaum; president **Joseph Baptista**).
[1:14] - Died **1 August 1920** – the day the **Non-Cooperation Movement** was launched; bier lifted by **Gandhi, Shaukat Ali** and Saifuddin Kitchlew.
[1:15] > Not Tilak's: **Satya Shodhak Samaj** (Jyotiba Phule, 1873); Bombay Chronicle (Pherozeshah Mehta).
[1:16] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.774-777, p.804-805; PMF Part 4 p.450, p.463 (Rand); Spectrum Ch12 p.272, p.275; Rocket Indian History 02 #96 p.48, #119 p.85; NextGen APPSC 06 p.371-373; newworldencyclopedia.org (Lokamanya after the 1897 trial); en.wikipedia.org Bal Gangadhar Tilak (untouchability quote, Chirol)
## SUB 2 | Surat Split 1907 – Run-up, Causes and Aftermath
[2:0] # Run-up
[2:1] TABLE head: Session | President | What happened
  [2:1.0] Benaras 1905 | Gokhale | Mild resolution condemning partition; supported Swadeshi-Boycott in Bengal
  [2:1.1] Calcutta 1906 | Dadabhai Naoroji | Split averted; goal = "swarajya or self-government" like the UK colonies; four resolutions – Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education
  [2:1.2] Surat 1907 (26 Dec) | Rash Behari Ghosh (Moderates' choice) | Split
[2:2] - **Annulment of the partition** was **NOT** one of the four Calcutta resolutions (UPSC 2010).
[2:3] # The split
[2:4] - **Extremists (Garam Dal, led by Tilak):** session at **Nagpur**, **Tilak or Lajpat Rai** president, keep the four resolutions.
[2:5] - **Moderates (Naram Dal – Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta):** Surat (host-province rule kept Tilak out), Rash Behari Ghosh president, drop the resolutions.
[2:6] - **Main reason (UPSC 2016):** **Extremists' lack of faith in the Moderates' capacity to negotiate with the British**.
[2:7] - Differences were over **strategy, objectives and mass participation** – all of these; the **Swadeshi Movement** produced the split.
[2:8] - **First split of the INC = Surat, December 1907**; scuffle, session suspended; the Moderates pushed the Extremists out and kept the Congress machinery.
[2:9] - **A-R (UPSC 1998):** the 1905 partition did **not** end the Moderates' role (A ✗); Surat separated the two wings (R ✓).
[2:10] # Aftermath and repression
[2:11] TABLE head: Act | Year
  [2:11.0] Seditious Meetings Act | 1907
  [2:11.1] Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act | 1908
  [2:11.2] Criminal Law Amendment Act; Explosive Substances Act | 1908
  [2:11.3] Indian Press Act | 1910
[2:12] - Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh deported to Mandalay (May 1907); Tilak to Mandalay (1908); Ashwini Kumar Dutt, K.K. Mitra deported (1908); Aurobindo and Pal retired → Moderates dominated by 1910.
[2:13] - **Andhra Tilak** = **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao**, jailed 1908.
[2:14] - **Reunion: Lucknow, 1916.**
[2:15] # Chronology
[2:16] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [2:16.0] 1773 | Regulating Act
  [2:16.1] 1905 | Partition of Bengal; Swadeshi (7 Aug); 16 Oct mourning day
  [2:16.2] 1906 | Muslim League (Dacca); Calcutta session
  [2:16.3] 1907 | Surat Split
  [2:16.4] 1909 | Morley-Minto Reforms
  [2:16.5] 1911 | Partition annulled
  [2:16.6] 1916 | Home Rule Leagues (Tilak and Besant); Lucknow Pact
  [2:16.7] 1918 | Kheda Satyagraha
[2:17] - **Extremist phase events (APPSC G1):** only **Swadeshi** and **Surat Split**; Lucknow Pact and Home Rule came later.

## SUB 3 | Lala Lajpat Rai (1865-1928)
[3:0] - **Punjab Kesari / Sher-e-Punjab** (Lion of Punjab); Arya Samaj leader; helped found the **DAV School, Lahore** (1886).
[3:1] - Helped found **Punjab National Bank** (Lahore, **1894**).
[3:2] - Political guru: **Mazzini**; wrote biographies of **Mazzini, Garibaldi, Shivaji, Shri Krishna**, Dayanand.
[3:3] - Deported to **Mandalay** with **Ajit Singh** (May 1907); led protests against Punjab repression.
[3:4] - **USA 1917-20:** **Indian Home Rule League of America** (1917); *Young India* (1916); later elected to the **Central Assembly**.
[3:5] - **Only one of Lal-Bal-Pal to be INC President** – **Calcutta special session, 1920**.
[3:6] - Works: **Unhappy India** (1928, reply to Katherine Mayo's *Mother India*), *The Story of My Deportation* (1908), *England's Debt to India*.
[3:7] - Papers: Urdu daily **Bande Mataram** and English weekly **The People** (Lahore, **1920**); The Punjabee.
[3:8] - First used the **safety-valve theory** (in *Young India*) to attack the Moderates – Congress was Dufferin's idea; "the Congress movement was **neither inspired by the people, nor devised or planned by them**".
[3:9] - Called Congress sessions the "**Annual National Festival of Educated Indians**".
[3:10] - Slogan "**Go back Simon**"; lathi-charged at **Lahore** (30 Oct 1928), died **17 November 1928** (Saunders shot by Bhagat Singh and Rajguru).
[3:11] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.417-418, p.454, p.490-492; Rocket Indian History 02 #96 p.48, #119 p.84-85; PMF Part 5 p.777 (Young India, The People, Punjabee)
## SUB 4 | Aurobindo Ghosh and Bipin Chandra Pal
[4:0] # Aurobindo Ghosh (15 Aug 1872 – 5 Dec 1950)
[4:1] - **New Lamps for Old** (**1893-94**): articles in **Indu Prakash** (Bombay) – systematic critique of Moderate politics; Congress out of touch with the proletariat.
[4:2] - Editor of **Bande Mataram** (1906) and **Karmayogin**; first principal of **Bengal National College** (1906); main Swadeshi leader in Bengal.
[4:3] - **Alipore Bomb Case** (1908, Maniktala Garden raid): defended by **Chittaranjan Das**; acquitted 1909; retired to **Pondicherry** (1910) – **Sri Aurobindo Ashram**.
[4:4] - Sayings: "**Political freedom is the life-breath of a nation**"; "The first principle of true teaching is that **nothing can be taught**" (*A System of National Education*).
[4:5] - Works: **The Life Divine**, *Savitri*, *Essays on the Gita*, **The Foundations of Indian Culture**.
[4:6] - Titles: **Prophet of Indian Nationalism**; chief ideologue of the Extremists.
[4:7] # Bipin Chandra Pal (1858-1932)
[4:8] - **Brahmo Samaj** member and social reformer; weekly **New India (1901)**; Bande Mataram (1906, Aurobindo editor); *The Soul of India*.
[4:9] - "**Father of Revolutionary Thoughts**"; imprisoned 6 months (1907) for refusing to give evidence against Aurobindo – **not deported to Mandalay**; in England 1908-11.
[4:10] - **April 1907: toured Andhra** – the Vande Mataram movement.
[4:11] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.492-495; Spectrum Ch12 p.272, p.275; Rocket Indian History 02 #119 p.85; PMF Part 5 p.777 (New India, Bande Mataram)
