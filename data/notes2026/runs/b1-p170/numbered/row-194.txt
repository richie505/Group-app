## SUB 0 | Justice Party (South Indian Liberal Federation)
[0:0] # Background, aim and social base
[0:1] - **Why:** Brahmins were about **3%** of the Madras Presidency population but held **over 90%** of government jobs.
[0:2] - **Aim:** to further the interests of **non-Brahmins** (APPSC 2008, AP History PYQ ×2) – end Brahmin dominance in jobs, education, politics and religion; basically an **anti-Brahmin party** – "the first political party in India founded against Brahmins".
[0:3] - **Agenda of SILF:** social justice, a rightful place in services, political representation (information technology is an anachronism).
[0:4] - **Demand:** separate **communal representation** for non-Brahmins, on the lines given to Muslims by the Morley-Minto Reforms; opposed the Brahmin-dominated Congress.
[0:5] - **Social base:** non-Brahmin landed gentry, **intermediate landed castes** (Andhra: **Reddys, Kammas, Kapus**). Dalits built a parallel "Adi" movement.
[0:6] - **British view:** backed it to weaken **Home Rule**; used against Home Rule and Gandhi's movements; it **supported the British in the Second World War** (Congress, Forward Bloc, Yugantar did not).
[0:7] - **Andhra Mahasabha (Vijayawada, 1917)** sought reserved seats for non-Brahmins – **Ponaka Pattabhirama Reddy** and **Tripuraneni Ramaswamy Chowdary**.

## SUB 1 | Jyotiba Phule and the Satyashodhak Samaj
[1:0] # Jyotiba Phule (1827-1890)
[1:1] - Born **1827**, Satara; a gardener's son – **Mali community**, Maharashtra.
[1:2] - Title **"Mahatma"** given by **Vithalrao Krishnaji Vandekar (1888)**; called **"Father of Indian Social Revolution"**.
[1:3] - **Creed:** all men have the same features and intellect – **nobody is sacred by birth**; aimed at complete abolition of caste; against Sanskritic Hinduism.
[1:4] - **1848**: girls' school, **Pune**; taught by **Savitribai Phule** (Maharashtra) – **India's first woman teacher**; home for widows 1854; pioneer of widow remarriage → the Indian women's movement is credited largely to Phule's inspiration.
[1:5] - **Savitribai** headed the Samaj's women's section; worked for lower-caste girls.
[1:6] # Satyashodhak Samaj ("Society of Truth Seekers")
[1:7] - Founded **24 September 1873, Pune**, by **Jyotiba Phule** – its **first president**; an **anti-caste movement in Maharashtra** (UPSC 2016 ×3) – not a tribal, temple-entry or peasant movement.
[1:8] - Aim: **upliftment of the backward classes** – **Shudras and Ati-Shudras** against **Arya-Brahmin** exploitation (not for Arya-Brahmins); end of **priestly monopoly**; education of women and lower castes; social service.
[1:9] - Leaders from malis, telis, kunbis, saris, dhangars.
[1:10] - **Satyashodhak marriages**: **without priests**; allowed **inter-caste** marriage.
[1:11] - Symbol: **King Bali** (the oppressed) against the Brahmanical symbol of **Rama**.
[1:12] - **Ideas (CDS 2015 – all three true):** set up **1873** ✔; Brahmins = progeny of **'alien' Aryans** ✔; his 1880s-90s focus on the **Kunbi** peasantry privileged **Maratha identity** ✔.
[1:13] - **Din Bandhu** (Marathi weekly, **Pune, 1877**) – the Samaj's organ, started by **Krishnarao Bhalekar**, credited to **Phule's** movement; spread it to remote Maharashtra; later edited by **N.M. Lokhande**. Phule also ran Dinmitra.
[1:14] # Works of Phule
[1:15] TABLE head: Work | Note
  [1:15.0] Gulamgiri (1873) | "Slavery"; compared caste oppression with slavery
  [1:15.1] Shetkaryacha Asud | "The Cultivator's Whipcord"
  [1:15.2] Sarvajanik Satyadharma Pustak | published posthumously, 1891
  [1:15.3] Tritiya Ratna, Brahmananche Kasab | also Phule
[1:16] - Phule's symbol = **King Bali**; Justice Party symbol = **Scales**.
[1:17] - **Current:** the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment began a two-year nationwide celebration of Phule's **200th birth anniversary** on **11 April 2026**.

## SUB 3 | Adi-Andhra and Adi-Hindu Movements
[3:0] # Terms
[3:1] - **"Adi"** = original inhabitants; rejected "Panchama". **Adi-Andhra** = Dalits of the Andhra (Madras) region; **Adi-Hindu** = Dalits of Hyderabad State.
[3:2] - Early root: **Adi-Andhra Sabha (1890)**, first called the Pariya Mahasabha.
[3:3] # Madiri Bhagya Reddy Varma (born 1888, Hyderabad)
[3:4] - Original name **Madari Bhagaiah**; **"Father of the Dalit movement"** in Hyderabad/Andhra; used the terms **Adi-Andhra and Adi-Hindu** for Panchamas.
[3:5] - **Jagan Mitra Mandali (1906)** (not 1960) → **Manya Sangham (1911)** → **Adi-Hindu Social Service League**; journal **Adi Hindu**.
[3:6] - **Sahapankti bhojanams** (community dinners) and **Buddhism** (Buddha Jayanti from 1913) for Dalit consciousness; against child marriage, liquor, Devadasi system.
[3:7] - Presided over the **All India Adi Hindu Conference, Lucknow, 1930** – chose **Ambedkar** for the Round Table Conferences.

## SUB 5 | Periyar and the Self-Respect Movement
[5:0] - **E.V. Ramasamy Naicker = "Periyar"**; joined the **Congress in 1920**, took part in **Non-Cooperation** (cut 1000 coconut trees in the 1921 anti-liquor drive); President and secretary of the provincial Congress.
[5:1] - **1925**: left Congress over **communal representation** and caste segregation at the **Cheranmadevi Gurukulam**.
[5:2] - **Self-Respect Movement (1925)** – the populist phase of the non-Brahmin movement, founded by **Periyar** (UPSC 2025 – not Ambedkar, Jadhav or Javalkar): rationalism; against caste hierarchy and Brahmin priests; **self-respect marriages without priests** (from 1928); women's equality.
[5:3] - **Aims:** equality for backward classes, acting by reason, end of superior/inferior caste notions – a lack of belonging to the state was never an aim.
[5:4] - **Journals:** **Kudi Arasu** (Tamil weekly, May 1925 – the movement's organ), Viduthalai, Puratchi / Revolt.
[5:5] - **Caste movements (APPSC G1 2018):** Satyashodhak (Maharashtra) + Self-Respect (Tamil Nadu) ✔; Ghadar (Punjab) and Bijolia (Rajasthan) ✘.
[5:6] - In Andhra: inspired Bhagya Reddy Varma and Jala Rangaswamy to adopt the "Adi" identity and demand temple entry.
