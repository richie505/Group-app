## SUB 0 | P. Anandacharlu and Andhra at the Early Congress
[0:0] # P. Anandacharlu (Panapakkam Anandacharyulu, 1843-1907)
[0:1] - **First Telugu (Andhra) President of the INC** – **7th session, Nagpur, 1891** (not Kakinada, Meerut or Lucknow; not 1889, 1890 or 1892).
[0:2] - **Secretary of the Madras Mahajana Sabha (1884)**; attended the **first INC session (1885)**.
[0:3] - **⚠ Trap:** Pattabhi Sitaramayya, Konda Venkatappayya, Nyapathi Subbarao and Kaleswara Rao never were the first Telugu INC president.
[0:4] # Andhra at the first sessions
[0:5] - **Bombay 1885 (72 delegates):** Andhra delegates **P. Anandacharlu**, **P. Rangaiah Naidu**, **Nyapathi Subbarao (Rajahmundry)**, **Gutti Kesava Pillai (Gooty)**, **Venkata Subbarayudu (Machilipatnam)**.
[0:6] - **Demands of the first session:** **simultaneous ICS exams** in England and India; **elected legislative councils**; **cut in military expenditure**; **opposition to the annexation of Upper Burma**.
[0:7] - **Calcutta 1886:** presided by **Naoroji**; **21 Andhra members** out of 436.
[0:8] - Madras session **1914**: **256 Andhra delegates**.
[0:9] > NextGen's "Modern India Part 1" p.11 wrongly puts Anandacharlu at the 1886 session; the session list settles **Nagpur 1891**.
[0:10] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.856 (session list); NextGen Modern India L2 Part 6 p.1; NextGen Rocket Sheets AP History p.50; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.46-47

[1:2] # Krishna District Association (1892)
[1:3] - **Formed in 1892** – the **first district association in the Madras Presidency** (APPSC 2021), set up **on the lines suggested at the first INC session**, to fight British rule on social and public issues.
[1:4] - **Organiser: Ramaswamy Gupta** (not Gajula Lakshmi Narasu, Mutnuri Krishna Rao or Kokkonda Venkataratnam).
[1:5] - **First meeting (conference): Guntur, July 1892**; **second: Machilipatnam, 1893**.
[1:6] - **⚠ Key fixed:** one APPSC Group 2 (2012) key gives **Machilipatnam** for the first meeting – wrong; every NextGen source and Rocket give **Guntur**.
[1:7] - **1894:** Krishna ryots met at **Eluru** against higher water rates; the 1894 session was presided by **Nyapathi Subbarao**.
[1:8] - Its **1899 resolution** led to **Krishna Patrika (1902)**.

## SUB 2 | Early Telugu Press, 1835-1905
[2:0] TABLE head: Paper | Fact
  [2:0.0] Satyadoota (1835) | First Telugu journal – published by the Christian Association of Bellary
  [2:0.1] Andhra Prakasika (1885) | First political journal/weekly in Andhra; Telugu news weekly from Madras; editor A.C. (A.P.) Parthasarathi Naidu; supported the Congress
  [2:0.2] Krishna Patrika (2 Feb 1902) | Machilipatnam; started by Konda Venkatappayya with Dasu Narayana Rao; later edited by Mutnuri Krishna Rao
  [2:0.3] Vyjayanti; People's Magazine | Nyapathi Subbarao (monthly; editor)
  [2:0.4] Deshabhimani, Sasilekha, Rasikollasini, Gowtami, Vivekavardhani | Among about 20 Telugu journals by 1905
[2:1] - **Role:** journals **criticised administrative failures** and spread **nationalism and democracy**.
[2:2] > First Telugu journal = **Satyadoota**; first political journal = **Andhra Prakasika** (not Krishna Patrika, Deshabhimani or Golconda Patrika).

## SUB 3 | Andhra Moderate Leaders and Local Politics to 1905
[3:0] # Leaders
[3:1] TABLE head: Person | Role
  [3:1.0] Nyapathi Subbarao | Rajahmundry lawyer; titles Rajabahadur, Padyavinoda; founded Vyjayanti, the Godavari District Association and the Hindu Samaj, Rajahmundry; Congress General Secretary; later first president of the Andhra PCC and of the 2nd Andhra Conference (Vijayawada, 1914)
  [3:1.1] Mocherla Ramachandra Rao | "Gokhale of South India"
  [3:1.2] G. Subramania Iyer | "Father of South India" (not Telugu)
  [3:1.3] Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao | Later (1919, 7th Andhra Mahasabha, Guntur) gave a data-led critique of the drain from Andhra to Madras
  [3:1.4] Others | V.V. Jogaiah, Gade Raghava Rao, A.P. Patro, B.N. Sarma
[3:2] - Andhra Moderates **repeated the INC's economic critique** (drain of wealth) and its constitutional demands.
[3:3] - **After 1908**, with the Extremists crushed, **Konda Venkatappayya and B.N. Sarma** came to the fore.
[3:4] # Order of events
[3:5] - Madras Native Association (**1852**) → Madras Mahajana Sabha (**1884**) → first INC (**1885**) → Anandacharlu presides (**1891**) → Krishna District Association (**1892**) → Krishna Patrika (**1902**).
[3:6] # Local politics and 1905
[3:7] - **Guntur district** carved out of Krishna in **1904**; Konda Venkatappayya moved there.
[3:8] - **1905:** real political awakening came with **Vandemataram/Swadeshi**; Andhras opposed both the partition of Bengal and the **transfer of Ganjam** from Madras – opposing Ganjam's transfer is called the "**first victory**" of the Andhra movement.
[3:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Modern India L3 Part 1 p.73, 87; NextGen AP History APPSC Part 7 p.19; NextGen Rocket Sheets AP History p.50; old app notes (row 200-201) for titles and district years
