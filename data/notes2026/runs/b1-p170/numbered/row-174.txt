## SUB 0 | Charter Act 1813 – End of India Trade Monopoly, Education, Missionaries
[0:0] - **Trade:** ended the Company's **monopoly of Indian trade** (thrown open to all British merchants) **except tea, opium and the China trade** – a statement "abolished the monopoly **including its tea trade**" is false.
[0:1] - **Sovereignty:** asserted the **sovereignty of the British Crown** over the Company's Indian territories; charter renewed for **20 years**.
[0:2] - **Education:** at least **Rs 1 lakh a year** for the revival of literature and promotion of science among Indians – first State responsibility for education (the chief reason the Act is called important).
[0:3] - **Missionaries** allowed to come to India and preach (Wilberforce, Charles Grant).
[0:4] - Courts in India got more power over European British subjects; local governments could tax persons; separate commercial and territorial accounts.
[0:5] - **UPSC 2019:** (1) monopoly ended except tea and China ✓ (2) Crown sovereignty ✓ (3) "revenues now controlled by Parliament" ✗ → **1 and 2 only**.
[0:6] > Which Act ended the Company's India trade monopoly? **1813** (1833 ended the tea/China monopoly).

## SUB 1 | Charter Act 1833 – Governor-General of India and Centralisation
[1:0] - **GG of Bengal → Governor-General of India** (first: **Lord William Bentinck**) – the **final step towards centralisation**; GG-in-Council got exclusive law-making for all British India; laws called **Acts** (earlier Regulations).
[1:1] - **Trade:** ended the remaining monopoly in **tea and the China trade**; Company became a purely **administrative** body; Europeans free to settle.
[1:2] - **Law Member** added as **4th member**: **T.B. Macaulay** (a Briton) – **no Indian Law Member** was provided; **First Law Commission (1834)** under **Macaulay** to codify Indian laws (IPC). The Law Member post is the origin of the **Ministry of Law and Justice**.
[1:3] - **Section 87:** no Indian to be barred from office by religion, birth, descent or colour; slavery to be abolished (done 1843).
[1:4] - Territories held **"in trust for His Majesty"** (Laxmikanth); the 1853 Act repeated it without a term.
[1:5] - **Agra Presidency:** almost all of present UP separated from Bengal Presidency in **1834** → North-Western Provinces (1836).
[1:6] - **Macaulay** (Commons, 1833 Bill): "The Company is an **anomaly** but it is part of a system where everything is an anomaly".
[1:7] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Indian Polity 8th ed. Part 1 p.28; PMF Modern Indian History Part 2 p.106; PMF Modern Indian History Part 3 p.1; Spectrum Modern India p.537-538; app notes 1:174
## SUB 2 | Charter Act 1853 – Legislative Council and Open Competition
[2:0] - **Last** Charter Act; Company's rule renewed **with no fixed term** ("in trust" for the Crown till Parliament provides otherwise).
[2:1] - **First functioning Legislative Council:** legislative and executive functions separated; **6 new legislative members** (4 from **Madras, Bombay, Bengal, Agra** + **Chief Justice** + one Calcutta judge) → Central Legislative Council of 12, a **"mini-Parliament"**.
[2:2] - **Civil services:** Directors' patronage ended → **open competitive examination** (the Act of **1853**; not 1833, 1858 or 1882); **Macaulay Committee 1854**.
[2:3] - **Imperial Legislative Council** (under the Indian Councils Act) was British India's legislature from **1861** to 1947.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Indian Polity 8th ed. Part 1 p.28-29; PMF Modern Indian History Part 3 p.2; Spectrum Modern India p.538-539; app notes 1:174
## SUB 3 | Charter Act 1793, Centralisation Trend and Persons
[3:0] - **1793:** charter and trade monopoly renewed **20 years**; override power to all GGs and Governors; Board of Control paid from **Indian revenues**.
[3:1] TABLE head: Act | Step
  [3:1.0] Regulating Act 1773 | GG of Bengal; presidencies subordinated
  [3:1.1] Pitt's India Act 1784 | Board of Control
  [3:1.2] Charter Act 1833 | GG of India – peak of centralisation
  [3:1.3] Charter Act 1853 | Legislative Council with provincial members
  [3:1.4] Indian Councils Act 1861 | Legislative power restored to Bombay and Madras
[3:2] - **Persons:** Raja Ram Mohan Roy – first demand for Indians in services (1832); Fort William College (Wellesley, 1800) → Haileybury (1806); Macaulay's Minute (1835); Law Commission 1834-38.
[3:3] - **Chronology:** Plassey 1757 → Buxar 1764 → Diwani 1765 → 1773 → 1784 → 1813 → 1858.
