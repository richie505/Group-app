## SUB 0 | Prarthana Samaj (1867), Ranade and the Social Conference
[0:0] - **Founded:** **1867, Bombay**, by **Dr. Atmaram Pandurang**, helped by **Keshab Chandra Sen** (visits 1864, 1867); grew out of the **Paramahansa Sabha**.
[0:1] - **Leaders:** **M.G. Ranade** (joined 1870, **chief mentor** – gave it an all-India character), **R.G. Bhandarkar**, **N.G. Chandavarkar**.
[0:2] - **Beliefs:** **monotheism**, attached to the Maharashtra **bhakti** tradition; aimed to **reform Hindu society from within**; relied on education and **persuasion**, not confrontation; did not outright forbid image worship.
[0:3] - **Four-point agenda:** (i) disapproval of **caste**, (ii) **women's education**, (iii) **widow remarriage**, (iv) **raising the age of marriage** for both sexes – **abolition of untouchability is not in the list** (CDS 2012; ⚠ PMF adds a later Depressed Class Mission).
[0:4] - **Statements (Astt. Comm. 2019):** reform of Hindu society ✔, Maharashtra-based ✔, monotheism ✔; "it greatly influenced the Brahmo Samaj" ✘ – the Brahmo Samaj influenced it.
[0:5] - **In Andhra:** spread by **Veeresalingam** (Rajahmundry, 1878 – see Andhra reform row).
[0:6] # Ranade and the Indian National Social Conference
[0:7] - **Indian (National) Social Conference:** founded by **M.G. Ranade** and **Raghunath Rao**; first session **Madras, 1887**; met **at the same time and venue as the INC** – its "social reform cell", because Congress (Dadabhai, 1886) kept to political questions only (UPSC 2012).
[0:8] - **Ranade** relied on **legislation**; worked against child marriage and purdah (UPSC 1996 passage); also Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870); **not** the Theosophical Society.
[0:9] - **Widow Remarriage Association:** Ranade, usually dated **1861** (⚠ PMF: 1865).
[0:10] > Prarthana Samaj = Atmaram Pandurang 1867; chief mentor Ranade; agenda = caste, women's education, widow remarriage, marriage age.

## SUB 1 | Western India Reformers – Lokahitawadi, Jambhekar, Widow Remarriage, Deccan Education Society
[1:0] TABLE head: Person | Known for
  [1:0.0] Gopal Hari Deshmukh | 'Lokahitawadi' – pen name in the weekly Prabhakar (Shatapatre, 1848-50); "if religion does not sanction social reform, change religion"
  [1:0.1] Balshastri Jambhekar | Darpan (1832), first Marathi paper; attacked Brahminical orthodoxy and tried to reform popular Hinduism in Bombay; father of Marathi journalism
  [1:0.2] Vishnu Shastri Pandit | Vidhva Vivaha Uttejak Mandal (Society for the Encouragement of Widow Remarriage) – 1866; Indu Prakash
  [1:0.3] Karsondas Mulji | Satya Prakash (Gujarati, 1852) for widow remarriage
  [1:0.4] Gopal Ganesh Agarkar | co-founder of DES and Fergusson College; first editor of Kesari; Sudharak
[1:1] - **'Lokahitawadi' = Gopal Hari Deshmukh** – not Ranade, Gokhale, Chiplunkar, Vidyasagar or Lajpat Rai.
[1:2] - **Deccan Education Society (1884):** founded by **Tilak, Agarkar, Vishnushastri Chiplunkar**, Namjoshi, Apte; **Ranade was only a patron**; not founded by Ambedkar.
[1:3] > Lokahitawadi – Prabhakar; Jambhekar – Darpan; Karsondas – Satya Prakash; Vishnu Shastri – 1866 widow society.

## SUB 2 | Paramahansa Mandali (1849)
[2:0] - **Founded:** **1849, Maharashtra (Bombay)**, by **Dadoba Pandurang**, **Durgaram Mehtaji** (of the Manav Dharma Sabha, Surat) and others; Bipan Chandra dates religious reform in Bombay from **1840** by the Mandali.
[2:1] - **First socio-religious organisation in Maharashtra** – religious reform in Bombay began with it, not with the Prarthana Samaj (1867).
[2:2] - **A secret society**; believed in **one God**; aim: **against idolatry and the caste system** – members **ate food cooked by lower castes**; backed **widow remarriage and women's education**.
[2:3] - **Branches:** **Poona**, Satara; exposure in 1860 ended it; reorganised as the Prarthana Samaj (1867).
[2:4] > Paramahansa Mandali = secret, 1849, Dadoba Pandurang, anti-caste and anti-idolatry.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India (Ahir 2019) file p.244, p.246; PMF Modern History Part 3 p.176-177; old app notes row 189
## SUB 3 | Young Bengal Movement – Derozio
[3:0] - **Leader:** **Henry Louis Vivian Derozio** (1809-31), Anglo-Indian teacher at **Hindu College** (1826-31); inspired by the **French Revolution**; first nationalist poet of modern India.
[3:1] - **Derozians:** radical rationalism – question all authority; women's rights; demanded press freedom, trial by jury, Indians in higher services.
[3:2] - **Academic Association (1828):** debating forum of Derozio and his students.
[3:3] - **Dismissed** from Hindu College in **1831**; died of cholera the same year. Failed: radicalism bookish, no link with the masses.
[3:4] > Young Bengal = Derozio, Hindu College, Academic Association 1828, removed 1831.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India (Ahir 2019) file p.244-245; PMF Modern History Part 3 p.172-173; old app notes row 189
