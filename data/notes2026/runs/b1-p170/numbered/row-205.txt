## SUB 0 | Simla Deputation and Founding of the Muslim League, 1906
[0:0] # Simla Deputation (1 October 1906)
[0:1] - Led by **Aga Khan III**; met **Lord Minto** at **Simla (Shimla)**.
[0:2] - Demanded **separate electorates** for Muslims and representation **above their numbers** (weightage), citing their contribution "**to the defence of the Empire**"; also safeguards in government services.
[0:3] - **Jinnah**, then a Congressman (secretary to Naoroji at Calcutta, 1906), **opposed separate electorates** in 1906 – the Aga Khan called him the only well-known Muslim to do so.
[0:4] # Founding facts
[0:5] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [0:5.0] Date | 30 December 1906
  [0:5.1] Place | Dacca (Dhaka), at the session of the All-India Muhammadan Educational Conference
  [0:5.2] Initiative | Nawab Salimullah of Dacca, with Mohsin-ul-Mulk and Viqar-ul-Mulk (who chaired the Dacca meeting)
  [0:5.3] First president | Aga Khan III (president 1906/08-1913) – not Jinnah, who joined only in 1913
  [0:5.4] First annual session | Karachi, 1907 (constitution adopted); 2nd session Amritsar, 1908
  [0:5.5] Headquarters | Lucknow
  [0:5.6] London Muslim League | Syed Ameer Ali, 6 May 1908 – exam keys call it the League's "London branch" (PMF: strictly an independent body)
[0:6] # Character
[0:7] - **Loyalist, communal, conservative**: preached loyalty to the Empire, kept Muslim intelligentsia **away from the Congress**.
[0:8] - **Supported the partition of Bengal** and sought safeguards in government services – it did **not** oppose the partition (UPSC 2002 "not correct" statement).
[0:9] - **Other figures of the 2002 statement question:** **Hakim Ajmal Khan** – a leader of the militant nationalist **Ahrar movement** (with Mohammad Ali, Hasan Imam, Zafar Ali Khan, Mazharul Haq); **Syed Ahmad Khan** opposed the Congress (United Indian Patriotic Association, 1888); **Barkatullah and Obeidullah Sindhi** – Provisional Government of India, **Kabul (1915)**.
[0:10] - **W.W. Hunter** (*The Indian Musalmans*, 1871) – exam keys credit him with "The Muslims, if contented and satisfied, would become the greatest bulwark of British power in India".
[0:11] # Chronology and dates
[0:12] - **INC 1885 → Partition of Bengal 1905 → Swadeshi (7 Aug 1905) → Muslim League (Dec 1906) → Surat split 1907 → Morley-Minto 1909 → Lucknow Pact 1916**.
[0:13] TABLE head: Body | Founded
  [0:13.0] Brahmo Samaj | 1828
  [0:13.1] Indian National Congress | 1885
  [0:13.2] All India Muslim League | 1906
  [0:13.3] HSRA | 1928
[0:14] > Founded at **Dacca 1906**; first session **Karachi 1907**; first president **Aga Khan**; London League **Ameer Ali 1908**.

## SUB 1 | Indian Councils Act, 1909 – Morley-Minto Reforms
[1:0] # Names and background
[1:1] - **Names:** **Indian Councils Act, 1909** = **Morley-Minto (Minto-Morley) Reforms**; exam options also call it the "Government of India Act, 1909".
[1:2] - **Persons:** **John Morley** = Secretary of State for India (Liberal, from end-1905); **Lord Minto II** = Viceroy (1905-10), who succeeded **Curzon** in 1905.
[1:3] - **Aim:** "rally the Moderates" (carrot and stick) and win Muslim loyalty – the reforms were meant to **divide the nationalist ranks** and check Indian unity.
[1:4] # Provisions
[1:5] TABLE head: Area | Provision
  [1:5.0] Separate electorates | For the first time, separate electorates for Muslims – Muslim members elected only by Muslim voters = communal representation; "legalised communalism"
  [1:5.1] Weightage | Muslims got more seats than their population share, and a lower income qualification for voters
  [1:5.2] Elections | Elective principle recognised for non-official members – but indirect, multi-tier (local bodies → electoral college → provincial councils → central council)
  [1:5.3] Central Council | Additional members raised to 60 (from 16); official majority kept (69 members: 37 officials, 32 non-officials, 27 elected)
  [1:5.4] Provincial Councils | 30-50 additional members; non-official majority (but partly nominated)
  [1:5.5] Powers | Ask supplementary questions, move resolutions, vote on separate items of the budget – not on the budget as a whole; army spending non-votable
  [1:5.6] Executive Council | One Indian could be appointed: S.P. (Satyendra Prasanna) Sinha, Law Member, 1909 – first Indian on the Viceroy's Executive Council
[1:6] - **Before the Act (1907):** Morley put two Indians – K.G. Gupta and Syed Hussain Bilgrami – on the Secretary of State's India Council.
[1:7] - **Gokhale** went to England to meet Morley for self-government on colonial lines; Moderates co-operated with the reforms and lost popular support.
[1:8] # Verdict
[1:9] - **Most short-lived** of Britain's constitutional experiments in India (UPSC 1999) – replaced by the 1919 Act (in force 1921); **Ramsay MacDonald:** an incomplete deal between democracy and bureaucracy.
[1:10] - Morley: "if this chapter of reforms led ... to a parliamentary system in India, I would have nothing to do with it." The councils stayed **advisory**.
[1:11] > "Which Act introduced separate / communal electorates (communal representation)?" → **Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley-Minto)** – not 1892, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford) or 1935, which only extended it.
[1:12] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.308-310; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.66-70; Laxmikanth Part 1 p.31-32; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.52
## SUB 2 | Lucknow Session and the Lucknow Pact, 1916
[2:0] # The Lucknow Congress (December 1916)
[2:1] - **President:** **Ambika Charan Majumdar** (a Moderate). The League's Lucknow session was presided over by **Jinnah**; **both** bodies met at **Lucknow**.
[2:2] - **Reunion** of Moderates and Extremists – Tilak's group readmitted after **9 years** (Surat 1907). Chief architects of the reconciliation: **Annie Besant** and **Tilak**; Pherozeshah Mehta (died 1915) had blocked it.
[2:3] - First Congress session Gandhi attended after returning from South Africa; Champaran peasants' problems were put to him here.
[2:4] - **Tilak's slogan** "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it" – exam keys (UPPCS) place it at the **Lucknow session, 1916**; strictly it was the slogan of his Home Rule campaign of 1916 (league founded at Poona, April 1916).
[2:5] # The Pact
[2:6] - **Brokers:** **Tilak and Jinnah**; Besant also helped; Sarojini Naidu called Jinnah the "Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity".
[2:7] - **Main concession:** the **Congress accepted separate electorates for Muslims** – the **first time (1916)** the Congress did so; Muslims got a **fixed proportion of seats** (weightage).
[2:8] - **Joint demands:** self-government at an early date; expanded councils with **elected majorities**; **half** of the executive councils to be Indians; five-year council term; Secretary of State paid from the British treasury.
[2:9] - **Not in the Pact:** no merger (separate sessions continued), no power-sharing deal, no demand for complete independence.
[2:10] - **Unity period:** **1916-1922** (Lucknow Pact → Khilafat-Non-Cooperation) = high point of Congress-League unity.
[2:11] # Chronology
[2:12] - **Lucknow Pact (Dec 1916) → Jallianwala Bagh (13 Apr 1919) → Tilak's death (1 Aug 1920) → Swaraj Party (1 Jan 1923)**.
[2:13] > Lucknow 1916: Congress president **A.C. Majumdar**, League president **Jinnah**; Congress **accepted** separate electorates.
[2:14] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.332-334, 515; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.93-95; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.54; NextGen Modern India L2 Part 6 p.4
## SUB 3 | Separate Electorates – Divide and Rule, Father of the Communal Electorate
[3:0] - **Stated reason:** protecting the Muslim minority. **Real reason:** part of the British policy of **divide and rule** – to set Muslims against the Congress and maintain British supremacy.
[3:1] - **Effects:** checked India's unification, encouraged separatism, became a potent factor in the **growth of communalism** – the "seeds of discord" that ended in partition.
[3:2] - **Lord Minto** = "**Father of the Communal Electorate**" – used separate electorates to **turn Muslims against the Congress**. Exam key (BPSC 2018): **Rajendra Prasad** called **Lord Minto** the "**Father of Pakistan**" (attribution not traced in the sources).
[3:3] - **Extension:** 1909 Muslims → **1919** + Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans → **1935** + depressed classes, women, labour.
[3:4] > Separate electorates = Minto (1909); Congress accepted them = Lucknow (1916).
[3:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.69, 83; Laxmikanth Part 1 p.31-33; Spectrum p.310
## SUB 4 | Acts Compared – 1861 to 1935
[4:0] TABLE head: Act | Key feature
  [4:0.0] Indian Councils Act, 1861 | Indians associated with legislative councils (nominated)
  [4:0.1] Indian Councils Act, 1892 | Discussion of the annual financial statement (budget); questions; indirect (nominated-on-recommendation) members
  [4:0.2] Indian Councils Act, 1909 | Separate Muslim electorates (communal representation); elective principle; S.P. Sinha
  [4:0.3] GoI Act, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford) | Provincial dyarchy (reserved and transferred subjects); bicameralism and direct elections at the Centre; communal electorates extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans
  [4:0.4] GoI Act, 1935 | All-India Federation; provincial autonomy (dyarchy in provinces abolished); dyarchy at the Centre; separate electorates for depressed classes, women, labour
[4:1] - **Match (APPSC G2):** separate Muslim representation – 1909; dyarchy in provinces – 1919; federation – 1935; discussion of annual statement – 1892.
[4:2] - **1909 vs 1919:** 1909 = separate Muslim electorates; 1919 = dyarchy. No Act brought dyarchy "at the Centre as well as in the provinces".
[4:3] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Part 1 p.30-33; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.69
