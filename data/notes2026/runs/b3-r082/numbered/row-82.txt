## SUB 0 | CRR and SLR – Reserve Ratios
[0:0] # Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)
[0:1] - **CRR = Cash Reserve Ratio:** the share of a bank's **Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL)** that it must keep as **cash balances with the RBI** (CDS 2020; APPSC AEE 2008).
[0:2] - **"Required reserve ratio"** = the **certain percentage of deposits every bank must keep as reserves** = **CRR** (APPSC Degree Lecturers 2023).
[0:3] - CRR is **fixed by the RBI (the central bank)**, not the Finance Ministry or the market (APPSC G2 2011); governed by **Section 42 of the RBI Act, 1934**; **no floor or ceiling** since 2006.
[0:4] - Banks get **no interest** on CRR balances; CRR is in **cash only**.
[0:5] - **Raising CRR:** commercial banks have **less money to lend** (not the RBI or the Union Government) → **liquidity falls**, **credit creation falls** → helps control inflation (UPSC 2010 ×4; UPPSC 2004, 2010; CGPSC 2017).
[0:6]   - APPSC G1: a CRR hike causes **fall in money circulation** and **low demand for goods and services** (I and II); it does **NOT** raise purchasing power or the banks' lending capacity.
[0:7] - **Lowering CRR:** **credit creation increases** (APPSC G2 2017); banks get **higher leverage to liquidity** and the economy **may see increased investment** (NextGen: lower CRR "helps banks provide loans … for investment purposes" and "boosts the growth rate").
[0:8] - **Temporary I-CRR (Aug 2023):** **10%** on the increase in NDTL (19 May–28 Jul 2023), announced **10 Aug 2023**, effective fortnight from 12 Aug; **impounded about ₹1.1 lakh crore**; released in phases (9 Sep, 23 Sep, 7 Oct 2023) (APPSC G2 Mains 2025: both statements correct).
[0:9] - CRR applies to the **whole NDTL**; the I-CRR applied only to the **incremental deposit base** (not to "new borrowers") (APPSC G1 2024).
[0:10] # Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)
[0:11] - **SLR:** minimum share of NDTL a bank keeps **with itself** in liquid assets **before lending** – **cash, gold and unencumbered approved/Government securities (G-secs, T-bills, SDLs)**; **not priority-sector loans** (APPSC G1 2023: I, II and III).
[0:12] - **SLR = mechanism by which commercial banks provide credit to the Government** (they buy G-secs to meet it) (UPSC 2010 ×3).
[0:13] - Older wording "ratio between cash in hand and total assets" = **SLR** (UPSC 1998; UPPSC 2007) – strictly SLR is liquid assets to NDTL.
[0:14] - Governed by **Section 24, Banking Regulation Act, 1949**; range **0%–40% of NDTL**; the **25% floor** was removed by the **BR (Amendment) Act, 2007**; **MSS securities count** towards SLR.
[0:15] - **Shortfall penalty:** **bank rate + 3%**, then **bank rate + 5%** if it continues.
[0:16] - **SLR cut of 50 bps** → more lendable funds → **scheduled commercial banks may cut lending rates** (UPSC 2015 ×2) – not "GDP up drastically" or "liquidity down".
[0:17] - **Raise SLR to fight inflation; cut SLR for growth.**
[0:18] # Trend
[0:19] TABLE head: Date | SLR | CRR
  [0:19.0] 1991-92 | 38.5% | 15%
  [0:19.1] Since reforms | Both reduced (Narasimham Committee) | Both reduced
  [0:19.2] 5 Jan 2019 | 19.25% (Dec 2018 review: −25 bps every quarter) | 4%
  [0:19.3] 11 Apr 2020 | 18% | 3% (Mar 2020)
  [0:19.4] 2026 (now) | 18% | 3% (cut 4% → 3% in 2025, ~₹2.5 lakh crore released)
[0:20] # CRR vs SLR
[0:21] TABLE head: Basis | CRR | SLR
  [0:21.0] Kept | With RBI | By the bank itself
  [0:21.1] Form | Cash only | Cash, gold, approved securities
  [0:21.2] Return | No interest | Earns interest on G-secs
  [0:21.3] Law | Sec 42, RBI Act 1934 | Sec 24, BR Act 1949
[0:22] > Trap: SLR "19.25%" (APPSC G2, 2019 review) is now 18%; "required reserve ratio" = CRR, not SLR.

## SUB 1 | Policy Rates – Repo, Reverse Repo, Bank Rate, MSF and LAF
[1:0] # Definitions
[1:1] TABLE head: Rate | Who lends to whom | Key point
  [1:1.0] Repo | RBI lends to banks short term (overnight) against G-secs (repurchase agreement) | Principal policy rate; injects liquidity, regulates money supply
  [1:1.1] Reverse repo | Banks lend to / park funds with RBI | Absorbs surplus liquidity; always below repo
  [1:1.2] Bank Rate | RBI's official / discount rate for long-term loans; rediscounts bills of exchange | Penal rates linked to it; signal rate
  [1:1.3] MSF | Banks borrow overnight from RBI in emergency, dipping into SLR | Contains volatility in the overnight inter-bank rate; ceiling of corridor
  [1:1.4] SDF | Banks park surplus with RBI without collateral | Floor of corridor (since April 2022)
[1:2] - **Repo** = "Repurchase Option": bank sells G-secs to RBI and buys them back later; the difference is the interest – it is **not the cost of collateral** (CDS 2020) and has **nothing to do with the rupee's exchange value** (UPPSC 1998: "None of the above").
[1:3] - **Rate at which RBI lends short-term to banks / at which banks borrow from RBI = Repo rate** (UPPSC 2005, 2008, 2013; SI 2019; APPSC G2; AP recruitment); comes under **monetary policy**; used **to regulate liquidity** (APPSC G2 2017).
[1:4] - **Rate at which banks lend to / park funds with RBI = Reverse repo** (UPPSC 2008 ×2).
[1:5] - **Bank Rate** = rate at which **RBI discounts / rediscounts bills of exchange** (UPSC 1995; Degree Lecturers 2017); = **official rate of interest charged by the central bank** (State PSC).
[1:6] - **BPSC 2015 trap:** "Bank Rate = short-term" and "Repo = long-term" are **both wrong** (swapped): Bank Rate is long-term, repo short-term.
[1:7] - **LAF (June 2000):** the window through which banks **borrow via repos** and **lend via reverse repos** – "borrowing through repurchase agreements" = **LAF** (APPSC G2).
[1:8] - **MSF (May 2011):** began at repo + **100 bps**, up to **1% of NDTL**; now repo + **25 bps**, up to **2%** of NDTL; aligned with Bank Rate in **Feb 2012**; minimum ₹1 crore.
[1:9] - **MSF rate and NDTL** are terms of **banking operations** (UPSC 2014 ×2).
[1:10] - **LAF access:** scheduled commercial banks and **standalone Primary Dealers** (registered as NBFCs); other NBFCs (HFCs, MFIs, asset-finance cos) cannot use it.
[1:11] - **Repo − reverse repo gap:** **1.00 percentage point** in June 2010 – June 2011 (from Sep 2010); from **May 2011** only repo is fixed and reverse repo set **100 bps below** it.
[1:12] # The corridor
[1:13] TABLE head: Date | SDF (floor) | Repo | MSF = Bank Rate (ceiling) | Stance
  [1:13.0] 5 Aug 2026 | 5.00% | 5.25% (unanimous hold) | 5.50% | Neutral
  [1:13.1] 7 Oct 2026 | 5.25% | 5.50% (+25 bps, unanimous) | 5.75% | Calibrated tightening
[1:14] - Corridor width **50 bps**: SDF = repo − 25 bps; MSF = Bank Rate = repo + 25 bps. **Fixed-rate reverse repo 3.35%** (May 2026), used at RBI's discretion since SDF.
[1:15] - **SDF** (proposed 2015, enabled by **Budget 2018-19**, began **April 2022**): absorbs surplus liquidity **without collateral** – the only collateral-free absorption tool (APPSC G2).
[1:16] # Stances
[1:17] TABLE head: Stance | Meaning
  [1:17.0] Accommodative | Supports growth; cuts likely
  [1:17.1] Neutral | Either way, data-dependent
  [1:17.2] Calibrated tightening | No cuts; hikes measured, not at every meeting
  [1:17.3] Hawkish / Dovish | Worried about inflation, tightens / worried about growth, easing bias
[1:18] - Path: neutral → accommodative (Apr 2025) → **neutral (June 2025)**, kept through Aug 2026 → **calibrated tightening (Oct 2026)**.

## SUB 2 | OMO, Sterilisation and Liquidity Management
[2:0] # Open Market Operations
[2:1] - **OMO** = **purchase and sale of Government securities (and T-bills) by the RBI** (UPSC 2013; UPPSC 2010 ×2; NDA 2012) – "trading in securities", not forex auctions or gold.
[2:2] - OMO relates to **both sale and purchase** of G-secs (APPSC G2 2011); RBI deals **through banks, not directly with the public**.
[2:3] - **Sell G-secs → absorbs liquidity** (fights inflation); **buy G-secs → injects liquidity**, money supply up, **interest rates fall** (OPSC 2018: "to lower interest rates, purchase securities").
[2:4] - OMO is a **quantitative** tool and "**a part of credit policy**"; main tool for **durable** liquidity.
[2:5] - **Money supply rises** with **central-bank purchase of G-secs from the public** and **Government borrowing from the central bank** (UPSC 2012: I and III); depositing currency in banks only shifts money; sale of G-secs reduces it.
[2:6] # Sterilisation
[2:7] - **Sterilisation** = offsetting the rupee liquidity created by forex inflows (RBI buying dollars) by **selling G-secs through OMO / MSS** or VRRR – part of sterilisation = **conducting OMOs** (UPSC 2023 ×2).
[2:8] - **MSS (April 2004):** T-bills/dated securities issued to withdraw excess liquidity; proceeds in a **separate account the Government cannot spend**.
[2:9] # Other liquidity tools
[2:10] TABLE head: Tool | Feature
  [2:10.0] VRR / VRRR | Auction-based repo / reverse repo; VRRR absorbs for longer than overnight SDF
  [2:10.1] Fine-tuning operations | Short VRR/VRRR auctions to keep liquidity in line with the monetary policy stance – 49 in FY24 (APPSC G2 Mains 2025)
  [2:10.2] LTRO (2020) | 1–3 year funds at repo against G-secs
  [2:10.3] USD/INR buy-sell swap | Injects rupees without changing RBI's balance-sheet size ($5 bn, 3-year, Dec 2025)
[2:11] - **Surplus liquidity** pulls money-market rates **below repo** and speeds transmission; 4 Sep 2026: RBI absorbed **₹6.02 lakh crore** through two 3-day VRRRs.
[2:12] - **G-sec yields** are influenced by **US Fed actions, RBI actions and domestic inflation and short-term rates** – all three (UPSC 2021).
[2:13] > UPSC 2024 (official key: all three): NBFCs via Primary Dealers can use LAF; **FIIs/FPIs can hold G-secs**; **stock exchanges can run separate debt platforms**.
[2:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 06 p.10-11 (OMO, through banks, part of credit policy); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.41 (MSS April 2004); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 06 p.9 (LTRO); RBI Annual Report 2023-24 ch. ## SUB 3 | Quantitative vs Qualitative Instruments
[3:0] TABLE head: Basis | Quantitative (general) | Qualitative (selective)
  [3:0.0] Aim | Volume and cost of total credit / money supply | Direction of credit among borrowers and sectors
  [3:0.1] Other name | Traditional methods | Selective credit control
  [3:0.2] Tools | Bank rate, repo, reverse repo, OMO, CRR, SLR (variable reserve ratios) | Margin requirements, moral suasion, rationing of credit, consumer-credit regulation, direct action, differential interest rates, guidelines
[3:1] - **Not selective / not qualitative:** **variable (cash) reserve ratios** (UPSC 1995 ×2; NDA 2014) and **Bank Rate policy** (APPSC G1).
[3:2] - **Not quantitative:** **rationing of credit** (UPPSC 2017). **OMO** = quantitative (UPPSC 2017).
[3:3] - **Selective controls of RBI:** **margin requirements and moral suasion** – not CRR/SLR (CDS 2017).
[3:4] - **Margin** = value of security − loan; a **higher margin = smaller loan** against the same security.
[3:5] - **Moral suasion:** persuasion – closed-door meetings, appeals, warnings – not force.
[3:6] - **Not a credit-control method:** **devaluation of the rupee** (exchange-rate step).
[3:7] - **Components of monetary policy:** **Bank rate and OMO** – **public debt and public revenue are fiscal** (UPSC 2015; CDS 2017).
[3:8] - **Variable reserve ratios and OMO** are means of **monetary policy** (UPSC 1993).

  [3:9.0] Monetary policy | Bank rate
  [3:9.1] Trade policy | Imports and exports
  [3:9.2] Credit policy | Margin
  [3:9.3] Fiscal policy | Tax rate

## SUB 4 | Tool → Effect: Dear vs Cheap Money
[4:0] TABLE head: Tool | Control inflation (dear, tight, contractionary) | Boost growth (cheap, easy, expansionary)
  [4:0.0] CRR, SLR | Raise | Cut
  [4:0.1] Repo, reverse repo, Bank Rate, MSF | Raise | Cut
  [4:0.2] OMO | Sell G-secs | Buy G-secs
[4:1] - **'Dear money'** = **high rate of interest**, money hard to borrow (APPSC AEE 2011); cheap money = low rates.
[4:2] - **Inflation rising steadily** → central bank **increases the repo rate** (SSC 2020).
[4:3] - **Anti-inflation set:** **raise Bank Rate, CRR and SLR** – **buying** G-secs is expansionary (UPPSC RO 2016: 1, 2 and 3).
[4:4] - **Lowering Bank Rate** → **more liquidity** (UPSC 2011); it is **not a remedy for inflation** (APSLPRB 2012) – so CPI inflation **rises if Bank Rate is decreased** (RAS 2016 key).
[4:5] - **Increase in Bank Rate** = **tight (dear) money policy** (UPSC 2013).
[4:6] - **Expansionist policy would NOT increase the MSF rate**; it would cut SLR, Bank Rate and repo (UPSC 2020: only II).
[4:7] - **Interest-rate cut** → **higher investment expenditure** (UPSC 2014 ×2) – not lower consumption, more tax or more savings.
[4:8] - CRR/SLR cut → more lendable funds and a higher **money multiplier**; policy rates regulate the **capacity for credit creation**.
[4:9] - Monetary tools act on **demand**; supply-driven food inflation needs **buffer stocks, duty cuts, farm reform**.
[4:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 06 p.6-7 (dear vs cheap money, bank rate and inflation); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.26-27 (dear money policy); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.146 ('dear money' = higher interest rates); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 07 p.2 (instrument table); Rocket Indian Economy p.76
## SUB 5 | Objectives, Authority and Post-1991 Shifts
[5:0] - **Monetary policy** = macro policy on **money supply**, **framed by the RBI** (APPSC Welfare Organiser; UPPSC 2006) under the **RBI Act, 1934**; the **MPC** fixes the repo rate since 2016.
[5:1] - **Primary objective:** **price stability while keeping in mind growth** (MPPSC 2025; APPSC flashcard); also financial stability, exchange-rate stability.
[5:2] - **Not an objective:** **equitable distribution of income and assets** – a fiscal goal (UPPSC 2011).
[5:3] - **Fiscal vs monetary:** fiscal = Government, **taxes, spending, public debt**, direct but slow (legislative lags); monetary = RBI/MPC, indirect, quick (meets every two months). Fiscal deficit is fiscal policy.
[5:4] - **Post-1991 policy change:** **deregulation of interest rates** (AP recruitment).
[5:5] - **Post-1991 challenge:** **volatility in exchange rates** from open capital flows (not high reserves, low inflation or revenue surplus); ad hoc T-bills ended 1997 → **Ways and Means Advances**.
[5:6] TABLE head: Phase | Framework
  [5:6.0] 1985–1998 | Monetary targeting with feedback (M3; Chakravarty 1985)
  [5:6.1] 1998–2014/16 | Multiple indicator approach (LAF 2000, MSS 2004, MSF 2011) – dropped as opaque
  [5:6.2] 2016 onwards | Flexible inflation targeting (Urjit Patel Committee 2014)
[5:7] - Transmission obstacles: administered **small-savings rates** (Rakesh Mohan Committee 2004: align with G-sec yields), NPAs, informal credit outside RBI's reach, heavy Government borrowing (**fiscal dominance**).

## SUB 6 | Lending-rate Benchmarks and Transmission
[6:0] TABLE head: Fixed by RBI / MPC | Set by each bank | Market-determined
  [6:0.0] Repo, SDF, MSF, Bank Rate, reverse repo, CRR, SLR | PLR/BPLR (old), Base Rate, MCLR, spread over EBLR | Call money rate (WACR), TREPS, T-bill and G-sec yields
[6:1] - **RBI does NOT fix the PLR** (APPSC G2 2011; UPPSC 2008).
[6:2] TABLE head: Benchmark | From | Key point
  [6:2.0] PLR | 1994 | Rate for best borrowers, set by each bank
  [6:2.1] Base Rate | 1 July 2010 (replaced BPLR) | Minimum (floor) rate below which banks may not lend; average cost of funds
  [6:2.2] MCLR | 1 April 2016 | Marginal cost of funds + CRR/SLR cost + operating cost + tenor premium; internal benchmark
  [6:2.3] EBLR | 1 Oct 2019 | New floating personal/retail and MSME loans linked to repo, 3/6-month T-bill or FBIL rate; fastest
[6:3] - **Base rate of interest** = the **minimum rate for all loans** (few exceptions such as DRI and staff loans); the **actual lending rate = base rate + borrower-specific charges**.
[6:4] - **MCLR purposes (UPSC 2016):** **transparency** in banks' lending-rate method **and** credit at rates **fair to borrowers and banks** – both; also faster transmission.
[6:5] - **APPSC G1 2024 statements:** CRR hike **reduces** credit creation (I ✗); I-CRR targeted **incremental deposits**, not borrowers (II ✗); **tenor premium is an MCLR component** (III ✓); MSF is an **overnight** rate **equal to** Bank Rate, not long-term or lower (IV ✗) → **only III**.
[6:6] - Janak Raj Internal Study Group (2017) → external benchmark; draft RBI (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026 apply from 1 April 2027.
[6:7] - Transmission: repo up → money-market rates up → costlier credit → lower demand → inflation cools; channels: interest rate, credit, **exchange rate (weaker rupee helps exports)**, asset price, expectations.
[6:8] - Repo cut **125 bps** (Feb 2025–Feb 2026, 6.50% → 5.25%); fresh-loan WALR fell about **93 bps**; MCLR loans reset periodically, EBLR loans quickly.
