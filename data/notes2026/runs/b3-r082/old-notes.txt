=== NOTES 3:82 | Monetary Policy – Objectives, Instruments (crr, Slr, Repo, Reverse Repo, Msf, Omo, Laf), Transmission
## SUB 0 | Monetary Policy – Authority and Objectives
[82:0:0] - Formulated and implemented by the **RBI** under the **RBI Act, 1934** (APPSC GS 2023).
[82:0:1] - **Main objective: price stability**, keeping growth in mind; also financial stability, external balance, development goals.
[82:0:2] - The **MPC** sets the repo rate to hit the inflation target; liquidity is kept so money-market rates stay near repo.
[82:0:3] - Monetary policy instruments: **Repo, Reverse Repo, Bank Rate, CRR, SLR**. **Fiscal deficit** and **public revenue** belong to **fiscal policy**.
[82:0:4] Repurchase option (**Repo**) is used by RBI to regulate money supply and inflation.
## SUB 1 | Evolution of the Framework
[82:1:0] TABLE head: Phase | Framework | Landmarks
  [82:1:0.0] 1985–1998 | Monetary targeting with feedback (M3) | Chakravarty Committee 1985; Vaghul Committee 1987 (money market)
  [82:1:0.1] 1998–2016 | Multiple indicator approach (growth, inflation, exchange rate, credit) | LAF 2000; MSS 2004; Base Rate 2010; MSF 2011; MCLR 2016
  [82:1:0.2] 2016 onwards | Flexible inflation targeting | Urjit Patel Committee 2014; MPFA 2015; RBI Act amended 2016; SDF 2022
[82:1:1] - Key post-1991 change: **deregulation of interest rates** (APPSC GS 2023).
[82:1:2] - Multiple indicator approach dropped: **opaque**; markets could not predict RBI when indicators conflicted.
[82:1:3] - **"Seven Ages of India's Monetary Policy"**: **Shaktikanta Das**, **24 Jan 2020**, St. Stephen's College (APPSC GS 2024).
[82:1:4] - Repo − reverse repo gap was **1.00%** (June 2010–June 2011); from 2011-12 only repo is fixed, reverse repo set automatically.
## SUB 2 | Quantitative vs Qualitative Instruments
[82:2:0] TABLE head: Basis | Quantitative (general) | Qualitative (selective)
  [82:2:0.0] Aim | Volume and cost of total credit | Direction of credit
  [82:2:0.1] Other name | Traditional methods | Selective credit control
  [82:2:0.2] Tools | Bank rate, repo, reverse repo, OMO, CRR, SLR (variable reserve ratio) | Margin requirements, moral suasion, credit rationing, direct action, credit ceilings, minimum interest on advances for sensitive goods, consumer credit regulation
[82:2:1] - **Main instrument: policy repo rate** – the single operating rate under FIT and most-watched signal.
[82:2:2] - **OMOs**: main tool for **durable** liquidity.
[82:2:3] - **Margin** = value of security − loan; **higher margin = smaller loan** against the same security.
[82:2:4] **Variable reserve ratios** and **Bank Rate policy** are **quantitative**, not selective. Moral suasion, margin requirements, direct action = qualitative.
## SUB 3 | Policy Rates and the Laf Corridor
[82:3:0] # Instruments at a Glance
[82:3:1] TABLE head: Instrument | Meaning | Rate (Aug 2026)
  [82:3:1.0] Repo | RBI lends short-term funds to banks against G-secs; principal policy rate | 5.25% (MPC, 5 Aug 2026, unanimous, neutral)
  [82:3:1.1] SDF | Banks park surplus with RBI without collateral; floor | 5.00%
  [82:3:1.2] MSF | Overnight emergency borrowing at penal rate, dipping into SLR up to 2% of NDTL; ceiling, repo + 25 bps | 5.50%
  [82:3:1.3] Bank Rate | RBI's lending/discount rate; rediscounts bills of exchange or commercial paper; long-term; penal rates linked to it | 5.50%
  [82:3:1.4] Fixed reverse repo | RBI borrows from banks against G-secs; at RBI's discretion since SDF | 3.35% (May 2026)
  [82:3:1.5] CRR | Share of NDTL kept as cash with RBI; no interest | 3%
  [82:3:1.6] SLR | Share of NDTL kept by bank in cash, gold, approved G-secs | 18%
  [82:3:1.7] OMO | RBI buys/sells G-secs (through banks, not directly with the public) | –
[82:3:2] Corridor: **SDF 5.00% < Repo 5.25% < MSF = Bank Rate 5.50%**; width **50 bps**.
[82:3:3] - MSF began **May 2011** at repo + 100 bps, borrowing up to 1% of NDTL; now repo + 25 bps, up to 2%. MSF aligned to Bank Rate in **Feb 2012**.
[82:3:4] - **NBFC access to LAF**: only **Primary Dealers** (classed as NBFCs).
[82:3:5] # Comparisons
[82:3:6] TABLE head: Basis | Repo | Reverse repo | Bank Rate | MSF
  [82:3:6.0] Who borrows | Bank from RBI | RBI from bank | Bank from RBI | Bank from RBI
  [82:3:6.1] Use | Inject liquidity | Absorb surplus | Monetary policy signal | Marginal emergency need
  [82:3:6.2] Against | Eligible securities | G-secs | Bills of exchange/CP | SLR portfolio
  [82:3:6.3] Cost | Policy rate | Lower | = MSF | Higher than repo
[82:3:7] - **Reverse repo** = rate at which **banks lend to RBI**. **MSF rate** and **NDTL** are terms of banking operations.
[82:3:8] # Stance Terms
[82:3:9] TABLE head: Stance | Meaning
  [82:3:9.0] Accommodative | Supports growth; rate cuts likely
  [82:3:9.1] Neutral | Either way, data-dependent
  [82:3:9.2] Hawkish | Worried about inflation; tightens
  [82:3:9.3] Dovish | Worried about growth; easing bias
[82:3:10] - Path: neutral → accommodative (Apr 2025) → **neutral (June 2025)**, retained through Aug 2026.
[82:3:11] - **Calibrated tightening:** a stance in which rate cuts are off the table; rate hikes are measured and need not come at every meeting.
## SUB 4 | Crr vs Slr
[82:4:0] # Comparison
[82:4:1] TABLE head: Basis | CRR | SLR
  [82:4:1.0] Kept | With RBI | By the bank itself
  [82:4:1.1] Form | Cash only | Cash, gold, unencumbered G-secs, T-bills, SDLs, approved securities
  [82:4:1.2] Returns | No interest | Earns interest on G-secs
  [82:4:1.3] Law | Sec 42, RBI Act 1934 | Sec 24, BR Act 1949
  [82:4:1.4] Limits | No floor or ceiling (since 2006) | 0%–40% of NDTL
  [82:4:1.5] Now | 3% | 18% (Sep 2023)
[82:4:2] APPSC 2023: "**Required reserve ratio**" = **CRR**.
[82:4:3] # SLR Details
[82:4:4] - Minimum share of NDTL banks keep in liquid assets **before lending**; mechanism by which banks **provide credit to Government**.
[82:4:5] - **MSS securities count** towards SLR.
[82:4:6] - **25% floor removed** by **BR (Amendment) Act, 2007**.
[82:4:7] - Trend: **38.5% (1991-92)** → cut on **Narasimham Committee** advice → 19.25% (2019) → **18%**. Since reforms, both SLR and CRR reduced.
[82:4:8] - Shortfall penalty: **bank rate + 3%** → **bank rate + 5%** if it continues.
[82:4:9] - An SLR cut of 50 bps → banks may cut lending rates. **Lower CRR** → banks have higher leverage to liquidity.
## SUB 5 | Tool → Effect
[82:5:0] # Dear Money vs Cheap Money
[82:5:1] TABLE head: Tool | Control inflation (dear, contractionary) | Boost growth (cheap, expansionary)
  [82:5:1.0] CRR, SLR | Raise | Cut
  [82:5:1.1] Repo, reverse repo, Bank Rate | Raise | Cut
  [82:5:1.2] OMO | Sell G-secs | Buy G-secs
[82:5:2] - **Raising CRR**: less money in circulation + lower demand for goods and services (APPSC G1 2022: statements I and II); reduces liquidity; helps control inflation.
[82:5:3] Raising CRR does **NOT** raise purchasing power or lending capacity. **Purchase** of G-secs does **not** control inflation. **Reducing interest rates** is expansionary, not anti-inflation.
[82:5:4] - **Lower bank rate** → cheaper borrowing, more liquidity (expansionary); higher bank rate → tight.
[82:5:5] - CRR/SLR cut → more lendable funds, higher **money multiplier** (M3/M0 = **6.21**, 31 Dec 2025).
[82:5:6] - More money supply than real output growth → **inflation**.
[82:5:7] - **Expansionary policy** cannot stop the rupee's slide (it depreciates the rupee); rupee support = curb non-essential imports, promote exports, **Masala Bonds**, ease ECB conditions.
[82:5:8] - Policy rates directly regulate **capacity for credit creation**.
## SUB 6 | Liquidity Management and Sterilisation
[82:6:0] # Short-term Windows
[82:6:1] TABLE head: Tool | Feature
  [82:6:1.0] LAF | Repo + reverse repo/SDF; primary window for day-to-day liquidity
  [82:6:1.1] SDF | Proposed 2015; enabled by Budget 2018-19; began April 2022; absorbs without collateral (APPSC G2 2025)
  [82:6:1.2] VRR / VRRR | Auction-based; VRR injects, VRRR absorbs for longer than overnight SDF
  [82:6:1.3] Fine-tuning operations | Short VRR/VRRR auctions in line with stance; 49 in FY24 (APPSC G2 2025)
[82:6:2] # Durable Liquidity and Reserve Tools
[82:6:3] - **I-CRR**: temporary **10%**, announced **10 Aug 2023**; impounded about **₹1.1 lakh crore** (APPSC G2 2025: both statements correct).
[82:6:4] - **OMO** purchases: **₹2.39 lakh crore** (Apr–May 2025) + **₹1 lakh crore** (Dec 2025, to stabilise bond yields); more in March 2026.
[82:6:5] - **LTRO** (2020): 1–3 year funds at repo.
[82:6:6] - **USD/INR buy-sell swap** ($5 bn, 3-year, Dec 2025): injects rupees without changing RBI's balance-sheet size.
[82:6:7] # Sterilisation
[82:6:8] - Forex inflows swell rupee liquidity → RBI **sells G-secs (OMO/MSS)** or runs **VRRRs** to cancel the effect on money supply.
[82:6:9] - **MSS (April 2004)**: T-bills/MSBs issued; proceeds in a **separate account the Government cannot spend**.
[82:6:10] # Surplus vs Deficit Liquidity
[82:6:11] - **Surplus** liquidity speeds transmission and pulls rates below repo; **deficit** weakens it.
[82:6:12] - Dec 2025 tightness (currency leakage, tax outflows) → OMO + $5 bn swap.
[82:6:13] - By **3 Sep 2026**: surplus about **₹10.32 lakh crore** after FCNR(B) inflows; call rate below repo; on **4 Sep 2026** RBI absorbed **₹6.02 lakh crore** via two 3-day VRRRs.
## SUB 7 | Lending-rate Benchmarks
[82:7:0] # Who Sets Which Rate
[82:7:1] TABLE head: Fixed by RBI/MPC | Set by each bank | Market-determined
  [82:7:1.0] Repo, SDF, MSF, Bank Rate, reverse repo, CRR, SLR | PLR/BPLR (old), Base Rate, MCLR, spread over EBLR | Call money rate (WACR), TREPS, T-bill and G-sec yields
[82:7:2] - RBI does **not** fix the **PLR**.
[82:7:3] # Evolution
[82:7:4] TABLE head: Benchmark | From | Key point
  [82:7:4.0] PLR | 1994 | Rate for best borrowers, set by each bank
  [82:7:4.1] Base Rate | 1 July 2010 | Average cost of funds → slow transmission
  [82:7:4.2] MCLR | 1 April 2016 | Marginal cost of funds + CRR/SLR carry + operating cost + tenor premium; internal benchmark
  [82:7:4.3] EBLR | 1 Oct 2019 | New floating-rate personal/retail and MSME loans linked to repo, 3/6-month T-bill yield or FBIL rate; fastest
[82:7:5] - **MCLR purpose** (UPSC 2016, both correct): faster transmission.
[82:7:6] - **Janak Raj Internal Study Group (2017)**: MCLR transmission weak → external benchmark → EBLR mandate.
[82:7:7] - **Base Rate (2010, replaced BPLR):** minimum lending rate set by each bank; actual rate charged = **base rate + borrower-specific charges**.
[82:7:8] - **Draft RBI (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026**: from **1 April 2027**; banks, RRBs, co-ops, AIFIs, NBFCs/HFCs; floating personal and MSME loans stay on external benchmark; interest on daily reducing balance.
## SUB 8 | Transmission – Channels, Lags and Yields
[82:8:0] # Channels
[82:8:1] - 1. Repo up → 2. money-market rates up → 3. costlier credit → 4. lower consumption and investment → 5. lower aggregate demand → inflation cools.
[82:8:2] TABLE head: Channel | How a rate cut works
  [82:8:2.0] Interest rate | Cheaper loans raise consumption and investment
  [82:8:2.1] Credit | Banks lend more
  [82:8:2.2] Exchange rate | Weaker rupee helps exports
  [82:8:2.3] Asset price | Bond and equity prices rise (wealth effect)
  [82:8:2.4] Expectations | Credible target anchors expectations (does not remove all uncertainty)
[82:8:3] - Rate cut → **higher investment expenditure** (UPSC 2014), not higher savings or tax collection.
[82:8:4] # Lags
[82:8:5] - EBLR loans reprice quickly; **deposit rates and MCLR adjust slowly**. Outstanding-loan WALR **9.26%** (Sep 2025).
[82:8:6] - Repo cut **125 bps** (Feb 2025–Feb 2026, 6.50% → 5.25%); fresh-loan WALR fell about **93 bps** (Feb 2025–Mar 2026), about 80 bps from rate changes and 13 bps from loan mix.
[82:8:7] # G-sec Yields
[82:8:8] - Driven by **US Fed actions + RBI actions + domestic inflation and short-term rates** (all three, UPSC 2021).
[82:8:9] - Narrower spread of Indian over US yields → lower borrowing costs.

  [82:9:4.5] Structural rigidities | Banking rigidities (2026)
[82:9:5] - **Rakesh Mohan Committee (2004):** on administered interest rates and small savings; urged aligning small-savings rates with **market (G-sec) yields**.
