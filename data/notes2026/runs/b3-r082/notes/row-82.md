=== CRR and SLR – Reserve Ratios
# Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)
- **CRR = Cash Reserve Ratio:** the share of a bank's **Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL)** that it must keep as **cash balances with the RBI** (CDS 2020; APPSC AEE 2008).
- **"Required reserve ratio"** = the **certain percentage of deposits every bank must keep as reserves** = **CRR** (APPSC Degree Lecturers 2023).
- CRR is **fixed by the RBI (the central bank)**, not the Finance Ministry or the market (APPSC G2 2011); governed by **Section 42 of the RBI Act, 1934**; **no floor or ceiling** since 2006.
- Banks get **no interest** on CRR balances; CRR is in **cash only**.
- **Raising CRR:** commercial banks have **less money to lend** (not the RBI or the Union Government) → **liquidity falls**, **credit creation falls** → helps control inflation (UPSC 2010 ×4; UPPSC 2004, 2010; CGPSC 2017).
  - APPSC G1: a CRR hike causes **fall in money circulation** and **low demand for goods and services** (I and II); it does **NOT** raise purchasing power or the banks' lending capacity.
- **Lowering CRR:** **credit creation increases** (APPSC G2 2017); banks get **higher leverage to liquidity** and the economy **may see increased investment** (NextGen: lower CRR "helps banks provide loans … for investment purposes" and "boosts the growth rate").
- **Temporary I-CRR (Aug 2023):** **10%** on the increase in NDTL (19 May–28 Jul 2023), announced **10 Aug 2023**, effective fortnight from 12 Aug; **impounded about ₹1.1 lakh crore**; released in phases (9 Sep, 23 Sep, 7 Oct 2023) (APPSC G2 Mains 2025: both statements correct).
- CRR applies to the **whole NDTL**; the I-CRR applied only to the **incremental deposit base** (not to "new borrowers") (APPSC G1 2024).
# Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)
- **SLR:** minimum share of NDTL a bank keeps **with itself** in liquid assets **before lending** – **cash, gold and unencumbered approved/Government securities (G-secs, T-bills, SDLs)**; **not priority-sector loans** (APPSC G1 2023: I, II and III).
- **SLR = mechanism by which commercial banks provide credit to the Government** (they buy G-secs to meet it) (UPSC 2010 ×3).
- Older wording "ratio between cash in hand and total assets" = **SLR** (UPSC 1998; UPPSC 2007) – strictly SLR is liquid assets to NDTL.
- Governed by **Section 24, Banking Regulation Act, 1949**; range **0%–40% of NDTL**; the **25% floor** was removed by the **BR (Amendment) Act, 2007**; **MSS securities count** towards SLR.
- **Shortfall penalty:** **bank rate + 3%**, then **bank rate + 5%** if it continues.
- **SLR cut of 50 bps** → more lendable funds → **scheduled commercial banks may cut lending rates** (UPSC 2015 ×2) – not "GDP up drastically" or "liquidity down".
- **Raise SLR to fight inflation; cut SLR for growth.**

| Since reforms | **Both reduced** (Narasimham Committee) | **Both reduced** |

=== Policy Rates – Repo, Reverse Repo, Bank Rate, MSF and LAF
# Definitions
| Rate | Who lends to whom | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Repo** | **RBI lends to banks** short term (overnight) against G-secs (repurchase agreement) | Principal policy rate; **injects liquidity**, regulates money supply |
| **Reverse repo** | **Banks lend to / park funds with RBI** | **Absorbs** surplus liquidity; always below repo |
| **Bank Rate** | RBI's **official / discount rate** for **long-term** loans; **rediscounts bills of exchange** | Penal rates linked to it; signal rate |
| **MSF** | Banks borrow **overnight** from RBI in emergency, dipping into SLR | **Contains volatility in the overnight inter-bank rate**; ceiling of corridor |
| **SDF** | Banks park surplus with RBI **without collateral** | Floor of corridor (since April 2022) |
- **Repo** = "Repurchase Option": bank sells G-secs to RBI and buys them back later; the difference is the interest – it is **not the cost of collateral** (CDS 2020) and has **nothing to do with the rupee's exchange value** (UPPSC 1998: "None of the above").
- **Rate at which RBI lends short-term to banks / at which banks borrow from RBI = Repo rate** (UPPSC 2005, 2008, 2013; SI 2019; APPSC G2; AP recruitment); comes under **monetary policy**; used **to regulate liquidity** (APPSC G2 2017).
- **Rate at which banks lend to / park funds with RBI = Reverse repo** (UPPSC 2008 ×2).
- **Bank Rate** = rate at which **RBI discounts / rediscounts bills of exchange** (UPSC 1995; Degree Lecturers 2017); = **official rate of interest charged by the central bank** (State PSC).
- **BPSC 2015 trap:** "Bank Rate = short-term" and "Repo = long-term" are **both wrong** (swapped): Bank Rate is long-term, repo short-term.
- **LAF (June 2000):** the window through which banks **borrow via repos** and **lend via reverse repos** – "borrowing through repurchase agreements" = **LAF** (APPSC G2).
- **MSF (May 2011):** began at repo + **100 bps**, up to **1% of NDTL**; now repo + **25 bps**, up to **2%** of NDTL; aligned with Bank Rate in **Feb 2012**; minimum ₹1 crore.
- **MSF rate and NDTL** are terms of **banking operations** (UPSC 2014 ×2).
- **LAF access:** scheduled commercial banks and **standalone Primary Dealers** (registered as NBFCs); other NBFCs (HFCs, MFIs, asset-finance cos) cannot use it.
- **Repo − reverse repo gap:** **1.00 percentage point** in June 2010 – June 2011 (from Sep 2010); from **May 2011** only repo is fixed and reverse repo set **100 bps below** it.
# The corridor
| Date | SDF (floor) | Repo | MSF = Bank Rate (ceiling) | Stance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 Aug 2026 | 5.00% | **5.25%** (unanimous hold) | 5.50% | Neutral |
| **7 Oct 2026** | **5.25%** | **5.50%** (+25 bps, unanimous) | **5.75%** | **Calibrated tightening** |
- Corridor width **50 bps**: SDF = repo − 25 bps; MSF = Bank Rate = repo + 25 bps. **Fixed-rate reverse repo 3.35%** (May 2026), used at RBI's discretion since SDF.
- **SDF** (proposed 2015, enabled by **Budget 2018-19**, began **April 2022**): absorbs surplus liquidity **without collateral** – the only collateral-free absorption tool (APPSC G2).
# Stances
| Stance | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Accommodative | Supports growth; cuts likely |
| Neutral | Either way, data-dependent |
| **Calibrated tightening** | No cuts; hikes measured, not at every meeting |
| Hawkish / Dovish | Worried about inflation, tightens / worried about growth, easing bias |
- Path: neutral → accommodative (Apr 2025) → **neutral (June 2025)**, kept through Aug 2026 → **calibrated tightening (Oct 2026)**.

=== OMO, Sterilisation and Liquidity Management
# Open Market Operations
- **OMO** = **purchase and sale of Government securities (and T-bills) by the RBI** (UPSC 2013; UPPSC 2010 ×2; NDA 2012) – "trading in securities", not forex auctions or gold.
- OMO relates to **both sale and purchase** of G-secs (APPSC G2 2011); RBI deals **through banks, not directly with the public**.
- **Sell G-secs → absorbs liquidity** (fights inflation); **buy G-secs → injects liquidity**, money supply up, **interest rates fall** (OPSC 2018: "to lower interest rates, purchase securities").
- OMO is a **quantitative** tool and "**a part of credit policy**"; main tool for **durable** liquidity.
- **Money supply rises** with **central-bank purchase of G-secs from the public** and **Government borrowing from the central bank** (UPSC 2012: I and III); depositing currency in banks only shifts money; sale of G-secs reduces it.
# Sterilisation
- **Sterilisation** = offsetting the rupee liquidity created by forex inflows (RBI buying dollars) by **selling G-secs through OMO / MSS** or VRRR – part of sterilisation = **conducting OMOs** (UPSC 2023 ×2).
- **MSS (April 2004):** T-bills/dated securities issued to withdraw excess liquidity; proceeds in a **separate account the Government cannot spend**.
# Other liquidity tools
| Tool | Feature |
|---|---|
| VRR / VRRR | Auction-based repo / reverse repo; VRRR absorbs for longer than overnight SDF |
| **Fine-tuning operations** | Short VRR/VRRR auctions to keep liquidity **in line with the monetary policy stance** – **49 in FY24** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025) |
| LTRO (2020) | **1–3 year** funds at repo against G-secs |
| USD/INR buy-sell swap | Injects rupees without changing RBI's balance-sheet size ($5 bn, 3-year, Dec 2025) |
- **Surplus liquidity** pulls money-market rates **below repo** and speeds transmission; 4 Sep 2026: RBI absorbed **₹6.02 lakh crore** through two 3-day VRRRs.
- **G-sec yields** are influenced by **US Fed actions, RBI actions and domestic inflation and short-term rates** – all three (UPSC 2021).
> UPSC 2024 (official key: all three): NBFCs via Primary Dealers can use LAF; **FIIs/FPIs can hold G-secs**; **stock exchanges can run separate debt platforms**.

=== Quantitative vs Qualitative Instruments
| Basis | Quantitative (general) | Qualitative (selective) |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | **Volume and cost** of total credit / money supply | **Direction** of credit among borrowers and sectors |
| Other name | Traditional methods | **Selective credit control** |
| Tools | **Bank rate, repo, reverse repo, OMO, CRR, SLR (variable reserve ratios)** | **Margin requirements, moral suasion, rationing of credit, consumer-credit regulation, direct action, differential interest rates**, guidelines |
- **Not selective / not qualitative:** **variable (cash) reserve ratios** (UPSC 1995 ×2; NDA 2014) and **Bank Rate policy** (APPSC G1).
- **Not quantitative:** **rationing of credit** (UPPSC 2017). **OMO** = quantitative (UPPSC 2017).
- **Selective controls of RBI:** **margin requirements and moral suasion** – not CRR/SLR (CDS 2017).
- **Margin** = value of security − loan; a **higher margin = smaller loan** against the same security.
- **Moral suasion:** persuasion – closed-door meetings, appeals, warnings – not force.
- **Not a credit-control method:** **devaluation of the rupee** (exchange-rate step).
- **Components of monetary policy:** **Bank rate and OMO** – **public debt and public revenue are fiscal** (UPSC 2015; CDS 2017).
- **Variable reserve ratios and OMO** are means of **monetary policy** (UPSC 1993).
| Policy | Instrument (APPSC G2 Mains 2025 match) |
|---|---|
| Monetary policy | **Bank rate** |
| Trade policy | **Imports and exports** |
| Credit policy | **Margin** |
| Fiscal policy | **Tax rate** |

=== Tool → Effect: Dear vs Cheap Money
| Tool | Control inflation (dear, tight, contractionary) | Boost growth (cheap, easy, expansionary) |
|---|---|---|
| CRR, SLR | **Raise** | Cut |
| Repo, reverse repo, Bank Rate, MSF | **Raise** | Cut |
| OMO | **Sell** G-secs | **Buy** G-secs |
- **'Dear money'** = **high rate of interest**, money hard to borrow (APPSC AEE 2011); cheap money = low rates.
- **Inflation rising steadily** → central bank **increases the repo rate** (SSC 2020).
- **Anti-inflation set:** **raise Bank Rate, CRR and SLR** – **buying** G-secs is expansionary (UPPSC RO 2016: 1, 2 and 3).
- **Lowering Bank Rate** → **more liquidity** (UPSC 2011); it is **not a remedy for inflation** (APSLPRB 2012) – so CPI inflation **rises if Bank Rate is decreased** (RAS 2016 key).
- **Increase in Bank Rate** = **tight (dear) money policy** (UPSC 2013).
- **Expansionist policy would NOT increase the MSF rate**; it would cut SLR, Bank Rate and repo (UPSC 2020: only II).
- **Interest-rate cut** → **higher investment expenditure** (UPSC 2014 ×2) – not lower consumption, more tax or more savings.
- CRR/SLR cut → more lendable funds and a higher **money multiplier**; policy rates regulate the **capacity for credit creation**.
- Monetary tools act on **demand**; supply-driven food inflation needs **buffer stocks, duty cuts, farm reform**.

=== Objectives, Authority and Post-1991 Shifts
- **Monetary policy** = macro policy on **money supply**, **framed by the RBI** (APPSC Welfare Organiser; UPPSC 2006) under the **RBI Act, 1934**; the **MPC** fixes the repo rate since 2016.
- **Primary objective:** **price stability while keeping in mind growth** (MPPSC 2025; APPSC flashcard); also financial stability, exchange-rate stability.
- **Not an objective:** **equitable distribution of income and assets** – a fiscal goal (UPPSC 2011).
- **Fiscal vs monetary:** fiscal = Government, **taxes, spending, public debt**, direct but slow (legislative lags); monetary = RBI/MPC, indirect, quick (meets every two months). Fiscal deficit is fiscal policy.
- **Post-1991 policy change:** **deregulation of interest rates** (AP recruitment).
- **Post-1991 challenge:** **volatility in exchange rates** from open capital flows (not high reserves, low inflation or revenue surplus); ad hoc T-bills ended 1997 → **Ways and Means Advances**.
| Phase | Framework |
|---|---|
| 1985–1998 | **Monetary targeting with feedback** (M3; Chakravarty 1985) |
| 1998–2014/16 | **Multiple indicator approach** (LAF 2000, MSS 2004, MSF 2011) – dropped as **opaque** |
| 2016 onwards | **Flexible inflation targeting** (Urjit Patel Committee 2014) |
- Transmission obstacles: administered **small-savings rates** (Rakesh Mohan Committee 2004: align with G-sec yields), NPAs, informal credit outside RBI's reach, heavy Government borrowing (**fiscal dominance**).

=== Lending-rate Benchmarks and Transmission
| Fixed by RBI / MPC | Set by each bank | Market-determined |
|---|---|---|
| Repo, SDF, MSF, Bank Rate, reverse repo, **CRR, SLR** | **PLR**/BPLR (old), Base Rate, **MCLR**, spread over EBLR | Call money rate (WACR), TREPS, T-bill and G-sec yields |
- **RBI does NOT fix the PLR** (APPSC G2 2011; UPPSC 2008).
| Benchmark | From | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| PLR | 1994 | Rate for best borrowers, set by each bank |
| **Base Rate** | **1 July 2010** (replaced BPLR) | **Minimum (floor) rate** below which banks may not lend; average cost of funds |
| **MCLR** | **1 April 2016** | Marginal cost of funds + CRR/SLR cost + operating cost + **tenor premium**; internal benchmark |
| EBLR | 1 Oct 2019 | New floating personal/retail and MSME loans linked to repo, 3/6-month T-bill or FBIL rate; fastest |
- **Base rate of interest** = the **minimum rate for all loans** (few exceptions such as DRI and staff loans); the **actual lending rate = base rate + borrower-specific charges**.
- **MCLR purposes (UPSC 2016):** **transparency** in banks' lending-rate method **and** credit at rates **fair to borrowers and banks** – both; also faster transmission.
- **APPSC G1 2024 statements:** CRR hike **reduces** credit creation (I ✗); I-CRR targeted **incremental deposits**, not borrowers (II ✗); **tenor premium is an MCLR component** (III ✓); MSF is an **overnight** rate **equal to** Bank Rate, not long-term or lower (IV ✗) → **only III**.
- Janak Raj Internal Study Group (2017) → external benchmark; draft RBI (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026 apply from 1 April 2027.
- Transmission: repo up → money-market rates up → costlier credit → lower demand → inflation cools; channels: interest rate, credit, **exchange rate (weaker rupee helps exports)**, asset price, expectations.
- Repo cut **125 bps** (Feb 2025–Feb 2026, 6.50% → 5.25%); fresh-loan WALR fell about **93 bps**; MCLR loans reset periodically, EBLR loans quickly.
