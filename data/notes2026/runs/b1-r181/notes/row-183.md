- **First martyr:** **Mangal Pandey** (born 19 July 1827).
- **Commanding officer at Barrackpore:** **Lt Gen Sir John Bennet Hearsey**.
- After Meerut the sepoys moved **straight to Delhi** – the **march to Delhi's Red Fort** was the first event of the revolt proper.
- **Governor-General** during the revolt: **Lord Canning**.

|---|---|
| May 1857 | Ferozepur, Aligarh, Etawah, Bulandshahr, Nasirabad, Bareilly, Moradabad, Shahjahanpur |

=== Bahadur Shah Zafar – Symbolic Leader
- **Last Mughal emperor**; proclaimed **Shahenshah-e-Hindustan** (Emperor of India) by the rebels at Delhi.
- Unwilling at first, he **vacillated but agreed** to assume the title; wrote to rulers to form a confederacy.
- Proclamations stressed **Hindu-Muslim unity** and the return of the **Badshahi**.
- Pension: **Rs 1 lakh per month** (Rs 99,000 at Delhi + Rs 1,000 at Lucknow) – "less than one lakh" is wrong. An **emperor without an empire**; poets **Zauq and Ghalib** at his court; **Hasan Askari** his spiritual guide.
- **Fate:** captured after Delhi fell (Sep 1857), tried, **exiled to Rangoon (Yangon)** with Zeenat Mahal (Oct 1858); **died there in 1862** – died **in exile**.
- Hindu-Muslim unity of 1857 was later cited against the **two-nation theory**.

=== Affected, Unaffected and Loyal Areas
- **Main zone:** the **Doab, Awadh, Rohilkhand, Bihar, Bundelkhand and Central India** – **North and Central India**.
- **Largely unaffected:** **Bengal, Punjab, eastern, western and southern India**; **Chittor** unaffected; **Jaipur** not a centre (Ajmer, Neemuch, Auwa were).
- **Bihar:** Danapur, Patna, Arrah and Muzaffarpur were involved; **Munger** unaffected (BPSC 1995).
| Place | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Nasirabad** (May) and Neemuch (June 1857) | Sepoy risings in Rajputana |
| **Tonk and Bhopal** | Troops rebelled, rulers stayed loyal |
| **Kota** | Troops rose against their own ruler |
| Gwalior | **Scindia** loyal (most loyal dynasty); troops joined the Rani; he fled to **Agra** |
- **Did not join:** **Scindia of Gwalior, Nizam of Hyderabad** (Afzal-ud-Daula), Holkar, Mysore – the Nizam did **not** support the revolt.

=== Significance and Consequences
- First great, direct threat to British rule; **weaknesses**: lack of **coordination and central leadership**.
- **Government of India Act 1858**: power to the **Crown**; **Secretary of State for India** created; Governor-General given the title **Viceroy** (Canning, first Viceroy).
