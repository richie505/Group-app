=== NOTES 1:181 | Revolt of 1857 – Political Causes
## SUB 0 | Subsidiary Alliance (wellesley, 1798)
[181:0:0] - Ruler keeps a **British subsidiary force at his cost**, accepts a **Resident**, gives up **independent foreign policy** (no other Europeans in service).
[181:0:1] TABLE head: State | Year
  [181:0:1.0] Nizam of Hyderabad (FIRST) | Sep 1798, again 1800
  [181:0:1.1] Mysore / Tanjore | 1799
  [181:0:1.2] Awadh | Nov 1801
  [181:0:1.3] Peshwa | Dec 1802 (Treaty of Bassein)
  [181:0:1.4] Bhonsle of Berar / Scindia | Dec 1803 / Feb 1804
  [181:0:1.5] Holkar (last Maratha) | 1818
[181:0:2] - **AP angle:** 1800 – Nizam ceded **Rayalaseema** to pay for the force = **"Ceded Districts"**.
## SUB 1 | Doctrine of Lapse (dalhousie, 1848-56)
[181:1:0] - No **natural male heir** → state lapses; adopted son inherits **private property only**, not the throne.
[181:1:1] TABLE head: State | Year
  [181:1:1.0] Satara (first) | 1848
  [181:1:1.1] Sambalpur | 1849
  [181:1:1.2] Baghat | 1850
  [181:1:1.3] Jhansi (Damodar Rao not recognised) | 1854 (Raja died Nov 1853)
  [181:1:1.4] Nagpur (Raghuji III) | 1854
[181:1:2] - **Jaipur** was NOT annexed under lapse.
[181:1:3] - Withdrawn by **Queen Victoria's Proclamation**, read by **Canning** at the **Allahabad Durbar, 1 Nov 1858**; adoption accepted. Princes became **"breakwaters to the storm"** (Canning); after 1858 protected in return for **paramountcy**.
[181:1:4] - **Rani Chennamma** (Kittur) – British refused to recognise her adopted son.

  [182:0:0.4] Corruption | Lower courts, police; favoured the rich
[182:0:1] - **Annexations and jobs:** Annexation of princely states (such as Awadh) threw thousands of officials, retainers and soldiers of the princely courts out of work.

[185:4:3] - **Failed**, but broke the myth of British invincibility; seeds of later nationalism; heroes invoked by Bose and Bhagat Singh.
