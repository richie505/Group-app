## SUB 0 | Why the Revolt Failed
[0:0] - **Main cause:** lack of a **common strategy, coordination and central leadership/organisation**; no unified **programme or ideology**, no vision of the future.
[0:1] - **Military superiority** of the British – rebels had **swords, spears and old muskets**, the British the **Enfield rifle** and the **electric telegraph**; experienced generals; troops back from the **Crimean War**.
[0:2] - **Limited territorial and social base** – no **all-India character**; British power intact in the **East, West and South (Madras Presidency peaceful)**, from where troops came.
[0:3] - **Lack of support:** most **native kings supported the British**; big zamindars, moneylenders, merchants and the **educated middle class** stayed away; **Sikhs and "martial races"** did not join; traitors.
[0:4] - Weak leadership of the old **Bahadur Shah**; quarrels such as the **Begum of Awadh vs Maulvi Ahmadullah**.
[0:5] > All three – military superiority, no unified programme, lack of support from all sections – are correct (BPSC). A-R: the revolt failed; Bahadur Shah's leadership lacked wide support and the major rulers shied away – R explains A.
[0:6] - **Rajputana:** the British suppressed the rising there easily because the **local Rajput rulers did not support** the rebels.
