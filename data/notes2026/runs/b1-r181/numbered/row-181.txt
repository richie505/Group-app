## SUB 0 | Doctrine of Lapse – Dalhousie's Annexations (1848-56)
[0:0] - **Rule:** a dependent state whose ruler died **without a natural male heir** lapsed to the Company; an **adopted son** could inherit the ruler's **private property only**, not the throne.
[0:1] - **Lord Dalhousie** (1848-56) applied it with zeal, but he was **not its originator** (earlier cases existed). Announced/first applied **1848**.
[0:2] TABLE head: State | Year
  [0:2.0] Satara (FIRST) | 1848
  [0:2.1] Jaitpur (Bundelkhand) | 1849
  [0:2.2] Sambalpur (Orissa) | 1849
  [0:2.3] Baghat | 1850
  [0:2.4] Udaipur (Chhattisgarh) | 1852
  [0:2.5] Jhansi (adopted son Damodar Rao not recognised) | 1854
  [0:2.6] Nagpur (Bhonsle, Raghuji III) | 1854
[0:3] - **Chronology:** **Satara (1848) → Sambalpur (1849) → Jhansi (1854)**.
[0:4] - **Not annexed under lapse:** **Jaipur**, **Bharatpur**, "Karnataka" – and **Awadh** (misgovernment) and **Punjab** (conquest).
[0:5] - **Kittur (1824):** an earlier refusal to recognise an adopted son – **Rani Chennamma** fought the British.
[0:6] - **Withdrawn** by **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1 Nov 1858)**, read by **Canning** at the **Allahabad Durbar**; adoption accepted; princes guaranteed in return for **paramountcy**.
[0:7] - Canning called the loyal princes **"breakwaters to the storm"**.
[0:8] > A-R (CDS 2009): the revolt broke out soon after Dalhousie left (1856), and his **annexationist policy** caused great discontent – R explains A.

## SUB 1 | Annexation of Awadh (1856) – the "Misgovernment" Plea
[1:0] - **Awadh annexed Feb 1856** by Dalhousie on the ground of **"misgovernment" (maladministration)** – **not** lapse, war or subsidiary alliance.
[1:1] - Nawab **Wajid Ali Shah** was **dethroned and exiled to Calcutta**; the British accused him of **failing to control the rebellious chiefs and taluqdars** (after the 1801 Subsidiary Alliance he had no army of his own).
[1:2] - He was in fact widely loved; his couplet **"Dar-o-deewar pe hasrat se nazar karte hain, khush raho ahle-watan hum to safar karte hain"** was later sung by revolutionaries.
[1:3] - **Summary Revenue Settlement 1856** – made directly with **village proprietors**; **taluqdars** were **disarmed, forts destroyed**, estates confiscated (taluqdars' share of villages fell from 67% to 38%).
[1:4] - **Bengal Army** sepoys hurt – Awadh was their home; their **families bore the new revenue burden**.
[1:5] TABLE head: Annexation | Year | Ground
  [1:5.0] Satara | 1848 | Doctrine of Lapse
  [1:5.1] Punjab | 1849 | Conquest (Second Anglo-Sikh War; Duleep Singh)
  [1:5.2] Jhansi | 1854 | Doctrine of Lapse
  [1:5.3] Awadh | 1856 | Misgovernment
[1:6] > Pairs: Satara **1848** (not 1849), Punjab **1849** (not 1848), Jhansi **1854** (not 1855), Awadh **1856** – correct.
