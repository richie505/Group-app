## SUB 3 | Nature – Statement Judgements and Hindu-Muslim Unity
[3:0] - Began as a **military mutiny** → became a **popular rebellion**; a **traditional uprising** of classes against a common foreign enemy – **no idea of a nation-state**.
[3:1] - **Not carefully planned** by rajas, nawabs and taluqdars; **rumours and prophecies did play a role** (Plassey centenary, 23 June 1857); the British did **not** control it quickly or easily.
[3:2] - **Rebel proclamations repeatedly appealed to all sections irrespective of caste and creed** (Azamgarh Proclamation, 25 Aug 1857 – under "Muhammad and Mahavir"; urged allegiance to the **Badshahi**).
[3:3] - **Hindu-Muslim unity:** **Bahadur Shah II** accepted by both as **symbolic head**; cow slaughter banned; both well represented in leadership. British response: **divide and rule** (not handing power to the INC).
[3:4] - **Significance:** failed, but broke the myth of British invincibility; seeds of later nationalism. Same "mutiny or revolt?" debate on the **RIN revolt** (HMIS Talwar, **1946**).
[3:5] - Largely confined to **North India**; most of India neutral.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 3 (src/indian-history/pmf-modern-history-03.txt) p.139, p.152-154; Spectrum Modern India (src/indian-history/spectrum-modern-india.txt) p.211; app notes 1:185
