=== Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) – Appointment, Report and Democratic Decentralisation
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Appointed | **16 January 1957**, by the **Government of India** (Team for the Study of Community Projects and NES; NDC/Planning-side review) |
| Chairman | **Balwantrai Gopalji Mehta** |
| Reviewed | **Community Development Programme (1952)** and **National Extension Service (1953)** – not IRDP |
| Report | **24 November 1957** (so "constituted / reported" = **1957**) |
| Accepted | **National Development Council, January 1958** |
- **Remedy proposed:** the scheme of **"democratic decentralisation"**, which "ultimately came to be known as **Panchayati Raj**" – the committee **first recommended / used** the idea; Ashok Mehta, Rajagopalachari, Kripalani, Pannikar and Kunjru are distractors.
- **Balwant Rai Mehta = "Architect / Father of Panchayati Raj"**; the present pattern of panchayats "owes its parentage" to this committee; the **Panchayati Raj system was recommended by its 1957 report** – not by the GoI Act 1935, Cripps Mission or Independence Act.
- **Associated with:** **Panchayati Raj** (it was set up to review Community Development, but its report is identified with Panchayati Raj); also the basis of **decentralised planning** through PRIs (APPSC G1 2002) – not Mahalanobis or Gadgil.
- **Finding:** public participation is indispensable – Panchayati Raj needs the **full co-operation of the local public**; PR stands for **decentralisation of powers, participation of the people and community development** ("all of these"), i.e. **grass-root democracy**.
- **NDC (January 1958):** did **not insist on a single rigid pattern** – States free to **evolve their own patterns suited to local conditions**, basic principles identical.
- Other heads: **Jivraj Mehta, Shriman Narayan, Jagjivan Ram, B.R. Ambedkar, Krishna Iyer** – never chaired a PR committee.
> Most-asked: chairman, year (1957), CDP + NES reviewed, "democratic decentralisation", NDC flexibility.

=== Balwant Rai Mehta Model – Three Tiers and Detailed Recommendations
| Tier | Level | Election (BRM) | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gram Panchayat** | Village | **Direct** | Village body |
| **Panchayat Samiti** | **Block** | Indirect | **Executive body**; block (samithi) = **basic unit** of planning and development |
| **Zila Parishad** | District | Indirect | **Advisory, coordinating and supervisory** |
- **Three-tier** system – **first recommended** by BRM; tiers "**organically linked**" through **indirect elections**; samithis filled by **indirect election from the village panchayats** (not from graduates, co-operatives or sarpanches alone).
- **Panchayat Samiti** is an **administrative (executive) authority** at the **block level** – not advisory/consultative; it is the body for planning (APPSC Panchayat Secretary).
- **District Collector = chairman of the Zila Parishad**.
- **All planning and development activities** entrusted to these bodies; **genuine transfer of power and responsibility**; **adequate resources** to be transferred; further devolution in future.
- BRM did **NOT** recommend: a **two-tier** system, **direct election to the Zila Parishad**, the ZP as **executive**, a **village panchayat of indirectly elected members**, a samiti as **advisory**, or PRI power to levy **income tax / property tax** at all levels.
- **Report details (APPSC Social Welfare Mains):** each **municipality lying as an enclave in a samithi elects one member** to the samithi; a samithi **can be superseded in public interest**; the **village panchayat collects land revenue as an agency on payment of commission**.
- **Not a tier:** **Nyaya Panchayat**; **not a PRI:** a **Gram Cooperative Society** (Gram Sabha, Gram Panchayat, Nyaya Panchayat are PR bodies).
- Today the PR set-up (73rd Amendment) is also **three-tier – village, block (intermediate), district**; middle tier optional below 20 lakh population.
- Under the **73rd Amendment**, Panchayat Samiti members are **directly elected** (UP name: **Kshetra Panchayat**) – unlike BRM's indirect model.
> Code items: BRM = c and d (planning to PRIs; Collector ZP chairman); not a and b (two tiers; direct ZP elections).
~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.96; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 23 p.14; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 05 p.2; app notes 2:80

=== First States – Rajasthan (Nagaur) and Andhra Pradesh, 1959
| State | Date | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Rajasthan** | **2 October 1959** | **FIRST** – inaugurated by **Jawaharlal Nehru** at **Nagaur**; three-tier |
| **Andhra Pradesh** | **1 November 1959** | **Second** – Shadnagar; CM **N. Sanjiva Reddy** |
- "Which State first implemented PR in 1959?" – **Rajasthan**; AP followed in 1959 itself ("both in 1959"). Not Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Bihar, UP or Gujarat.
- AP books add that AP was the first to cover the **whole State at once**; Laxmikanth, NextGen and Rocket only say AP was **second**.
- **Three-tier** PR first adopted by **Rajasthan**; by mid-1960s most States had PRIs.
- **Name "Panchayati Raj":** most keys credit **M.K. Gandhi** (Gram Swaraj; UPRO 2017); George Mathew credits **Nehru** with coining it – Nehru inaugurated it, BRM is its architect.
- **No PRIs:** **Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram** (traditional/ADC bodies); Kerala, Assam, Tripura have PRIs.

=== Community Development Programme (1952) and National Extension Service (1953)
# Grow More Food Inquiry Committee (1952)
- Chairman: **V.T. Krishnamachari** (APPSC G2 2008) – not Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta or G.V.K. Rao; campaign (1943) found too narrow → **integrated village development** and a **nationwide extension organisation** → CDP and NES.
# CDP
- Launched **2 October 1952** (Gandhi Jayanti) by **Nehru**, **First Five Year Plan**; **55** pilot community projects; pioneered by **S.K. Dey** (later Union Minister of Community Development).
- **First major rural development programme / first effort at local governing bodies** in independent India; "mother of social development programmes"; launched **before** BRM (1957).
- **Aim:** **development of villages through public participation**; a step towards **Article 40**; raising the standard of living under **Article 47**.
- **Components:** agriculture, irrigation, communications, health, housing, education and **social education** (adult literacy, community centres) – social education was part of the **CDP**.
- **Statements (APPSC JA):** launched 1952 for comprehensive rural development ✔; focuses on infrastructure, education, health, agriculture ✔; "top-down, communities minimal" ✘ (its idea was people's participation).
- **Community development approach:** asset-based (build existing strengths), inclusive of diversity, aims at **sustainable self-sufficiency** and solving **community** issues – not individual prosperity in place of the community.
# NES and extension
- **National Extension Service (1953)** – described as the **first programme** of community development (spread the CDP pattern); NES followed CDP.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Block | Unit of planning and development (about 100 villages) |
| Block Development Officer | Heads the block team of extension officers |
| Village Level Worker (Gram Sevak) | Multipurpose extension worker for a group of villages |
- **Extension principle:** the extension worker translates laboratory findings so farm families adopt them **voluntarily** = **principle of applied science and democratic approach**.
- **Failure:** seen as **government-imposed**, inadequate people's participation → BRM review.
| Year | Programme |
|---|---|
| 1952 | CDP |
| 1953 | NES |
| 1975 | ICDS |
| 1977 | Food for Work |
| 2 Oct 1978 | National Adult Education Programme (15-35) |
| 1978-79; nationwide 2 Oct 1980 | IRDP |

=== Ashok Mehta Committee (1977-78)
- Appointed **December 1977** by the **Janata government** (PM **Morarji Desai**); chairman **Asoka (Ashok) Mehta**; report **August 1978**, **132 recommendations** – a committee on PRIs (not Abid Hussain, Khusro, Chakravarty).
- **Finding:** PRIs had declined, yet they had **generated a new leadership** committed to development.
- **Two-tier:** **Zila Parishad** (district, **executive**, district planning) + **Mandal Panchayat** (group of villages, **15,000-20,000** people) – the **Mandal Panchayat system** was its idea (not BRM, Vengal Rao, Narasimham); **executive powers to Mandal Panchayats**; the Mandal Panchayat to **assist Regional Rural Banks** with infrastructure.
- **Term 4 years**; **district = first point of decentralisation** below the State (not the village or block).
- **Official participation of political parties**; **periodic elections at all levels**; elections by the State CEO with the CEC; no supersession – elections **within 6 months**.
- **Compulsory taxation powers**: besides grants, panchayats must **mobilise their own resources** – no democratic body stays viable on external resources (1978).
- **Social audit** by a district agency and a legislators' committee; **Minister for PR** in the State cabinet; SC/ST reservation by population; separate **Nyaya Panchayats** under a qualified judge; **constitutional recognition**; **voluntary agencies** to mobilise support.
- **Fate:** Janata government fell → no central action; **Karnataka, West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh** acted on it ("second generation panchayats"; WB Act 1973, polls June 1978); Sixth Plan (1978-83) gave PRIs new life.
| Point | Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) | Ashok Mehta (1978) |
|---|---|---|
| Tiers | 3 | 2 |
| Unit | Block (Samiti executive) | District (ZP executive) |
| Elections | Direct at village, indirect above | Party participation at all levels |
| Collector | ZP chairman | Reduced role |
| Constitutional status | Not proposed | Recommended |
> Incorrect-statement items: "village as first point of decentralisation" and "three tiers" are NOT Ashok Mehta.

# Nyaya Panchayats
- Judicial panchayats for petty civil and criminal cases – rooted in the **ancient** village panchayat (not a modern formal court); e.g. UP Panchayat Raj Act 1947, Bihar's **Gram Kachahari**.
- The Constitution names no Nyaya Panchayat; books link them to **Article 39A** (equal justice, free legal aid).
- **Ashok Mehta:** separate from development panchayats, under a **qualified judge**; **Singhvi:** for **clusters of villages**.

=== Later Committees – Dantwala, Hanumantha Rao, G.V.K. Rao, Singhvi, Thungon, Gadgil
| Committee | Year | Key idea |
|---|---|---|
| Dantwala | 1978 | Block-level planning |
| Hanumantha Rao | 1984 | District planning; **Collector** as coordinator |
| **G.V.K. Rao** | 1985 (Planning Commission) | "Administrative Arrangements for Rural Development and Poverty Alleviation Programmes"; "**grass without roots**"; **ZP pivotal**; **district** the proper unit of planning; **District Development Commissioner = ZP CEO**; regular elections |
| **L.M. Singhvi** | 1986 (Rajiv Gandhi govt) | "**Revitalisation of PRIs for Democracy and Development**"; **constitutional recognition** in a new chapter; Gram Sabha = embodiment of direct democracy; Nyaya Panchayats for village clusters |
| P.K. Thungon | 1988 (Parliament consultative sub-committee) | Constitutional recognition; 3 tiers; 5-year term; list of subjects (→ Eleventh Schedule); SFC; **Collector as ZP CEO** |
| V.N. Gadgil | 1988 (Congress party) | Constitutional status; 3 tiers; **direct elections at all levels**; SFC, SEC; basis of the 64th Bill |

- **District = key unit** of administration and planning (G.V.K. Rao: "the district is the proper unit for planning and development").
- Collector's development role cut by BRM, First ARC, Ashok Mehta, G.V.K. Rao – **Hanumantha Rao the exception**.
- **Order:** BRM (1957) → Ashok Mehta (1977-78) → Dantwala (1978) → Hanumantha Rao (1984) → G.V.K. Rao (1985) → Singhvi (1986) → Thungon, Gadgil (1988).

- **L.M. Singhvi** also headed the **High Level Committee on the Indian Diaspora** (2000-01) that recommended **dual citizenship**.

=== Road to Constitutional Status (1989-1993)
| Step | Fact |
|---|---|
| 64th Amendment Bill | Rajiv Gandhi government, Lok Sabha, July 1989; passed LS August 1989; based on Singhvi, Thungon, Gadgil |
| Fate | Defeated in the **Rajya Sabha** (October 1989) – special majority not secured |
| 65th Amendment Bill | Nagarpalika (municipalities) bill; also defeated in the Rajya Sabha (1989) |
| National Front (V.P. Singh) bill | September 1990 – lapsed when the government fell / Lok Sabha dissolved |
| Narasimha Rao bills | Introduced September 1991 → Joint Parliamentary Committee → passed December 1992 |
| **73rd Amendment Act, 1992** | **Panchayati Raj constitutionalised**; in force **24 April 1993**; Part IX (Art. 243-243O); Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects) |
- Opposition objection: **Local Government is a State List** subject (entry 5) – the Bill would **centralise power**.
- Effect: PRIs made **justiciable**; constitutional obligation on States; **uniform** three-tier structure.
- Key Articles: **243B(2)** middle tier optional below **20 lakh**; **243D(3)** at least **one-third** seats for women; **243K** State Election Commission; regular remuneration to members is **not** a provision.
- **National Panchayati Raj Day: 24 April**.
