## SUB 0 | Lighting Technologies – LED, CFL, Fluorescent, Sodium, Filament
[0:0] TABLE head: Lamp | Features
  [0:0.0] LED | Highest efficacy, longest life, no mercury; semiconductor emitter (gallium arsenide family) – the most energy-saving lamp
  [0:0.1] CFL (Compact Fluorescent Lamp) | Mercury vapour + phosphor; ~75% less energy than bulbs; shorter life and less efficient than LED
  [0:0.2] Fluorescent tube | Low-pressure mercury vapour + argon (sometimes neon, krypton, xenon)
  [0:0.3] Incandescent / GLS filament | Cheapest for the same wattage; only ~10–15% of input radiated as light, rest heat; power factor = unity (purely resistive)
  [0:0.4] Sodium vapour | Yellow, monochromatic light that does not split in fog/water droplets → street lights
  [0:0.5] Mercury vapour | White light
[0:1] - **Why mercury in fluorescent lamps (UPSC 2010):** when switched on, **mercury vapour emits ultraviolet**; the **phosphor coating** converts UV to visible light (mercury itself does not convert UV).
[0:2] - **CFL vs LED (IAS 2011):** CFL uses mercury vapour + phosphor, LED a semiconductor ✓; CFL less efficient than LED ✓; "CFL lasts longer" ✗ → **I and III**.
[0:3] - **Vacuum filament lamps:** contain **no gas** (gas-filled lamps use argon/nitrogen) → "none of the above".
[0:4] - **A–R (APPSC 2021 ×2):** government urges replacing bright and CFL bulbs with **LEDs** because LEDs light with **great energy efficiency** → **A and R true, R explains A**.
[0:5] - **A–R (RAS 2013):** LED life longer than CFL ✓; LED more efficient than CFL ✓; books key "R explains A" (LEDs run cooler); strictly the long life comes from solid-state design (no filament or electrodes to wear).
[0:6] > Lighting is the heaviest PYQ block of this row (16 PYQs: UPSC 2010, IAS 2011, UPPCS 2000/2006/2007/2015, SSC 2017 ×2, RAS 2013, APPSC 2021, APPSC AEE flashcards).

## SUB 1 | Energy Conservation Act, BEE and Organisations
[1:0] # Energy Conservation Act, 2001
[1:1] - Set up the **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE)**; BEE operational from **1 March 2002**; statutory body under the **Ministry of Power**.
[1:2] - **Designated consumers:** mandatory **energy audits** and norms; **penalties** for non-compliance.
[1:3] - EE norms for buildings with connected load **>100 kW** or contract demand **>120 kVA**.
[1:4] # Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022
[1:5] - **Lok Sabha August 2022 → Rajya Sabha December 2022** – passed by Parliament in **December 2022** (APPSC EO 2023).
[1:6] TABLE head: Provision | Detail
  [1:6.0] Carbon Credit Trading Scheme | Legal basis for a national carbon market – Section 14AA
  [1:6.1] Non-fossil obligation | Designated consumers to use a minimum share of non-fossil energy
  [1:6.2] Building codes | Extended to large residential buildings (ECSBC)
  [1:6.3] Vehicles and vessels | Efficiency norms can be set
[1:7] # Organisations
[1:8] TABLE head: Body | Status / Role
  [1:8.0] BEE | Statutory (EC Act 2001), Ministry of Power; runs PAT, S&L, ECBC, SEEI, CAFE, NECA
  [1:8.1] EESL | JV of Ministry of Power PSUs; runs UJALA, SLNP, National Efficient Cooking Programme
  [1:8.2] PCRA | Petroleum Conservation Research Association (MoPNG); runs SAKSHAM (not BEE)
[1:9] - **NMEEE** = one of the **8 missions of NAPCC (2008)**. India imports ~**80%** of its oil → efficiency = energy security.

## SUB 2 | Standards and Labelling and Building Codes
[2:0] - **S&L programme:** launched **2006**; **BEE under Ministry of Power**; **1–5 star**; each extra star saves **~10–15%**.
[2:1] TABLE head: Mandatory | Voluntary
  [2:1.0] Refrigerators, ACs, ceiling fans, TVs, washing machines, geysers (storage water heaters), LED lamps, tubular fluorescent lamps, LPG stove, distribution transformers | Industrial motors, computers, microwave ovens
[2:2] - **UPSC 2016:** the star label is found on ceiling fans, electric geysers and tubular fluorescent lamps → **1, 2 and 3**.
[2:3] - **From 1 January 2026:** labels mandatory for fridges, TVs, LPG stoves; makers **penalised if actual use deviates** from the label.
[2:4] TABLE head: Code | Year | Covers

## SUB 5 | Everyday Conservation and PCRA
[5:0] - Efficiency **reduces the need for new energy sources** → sustainable development (APPSC FBO 2025).
[5:1] - Saving practices: **LED lights**, star-rated appliances, switching off idle loads, avoiding AC overuse; **bicycles save fuel** (and cut pollution, noise).
[5:2] - **Pressure cooker** saves about **20%** cooking gas for rice (PCRA tip).
[5:3] - **PCRA SAKSHAM** (Sanrakshan Kshamata Mahotsav): annual one-month **fuel conservation mega campaign**, launched **January 2020** edition (APPSC PL 2020); SAKSHAM 2026 at Chandigarh.

## SUB 7 | PAT and the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme
[7:0] - **PAT:** market mechanism under **NMEEE** – energy-reduction targets for **Designated Consumers**; over-achievers earn **ESCerts**, under-achievers buy them; traded on **power exchanges**.
[7:1] - **PAT Cycle I (2012-15): 478 units in 8 sectors** – thermal power, iron & steel, cement, aluminium, fertilisers, chlor-alkali, pulp & paper, textiles; **railways** added later.
[7:2] - After CCTS, PAT keeps 4 sectors: **thermal power, railways, DISCOMs, commercial buildings** (Jun 2026).
[7:3] TABLE head: Feature | PAT | CCTS
  [7:3.0] Certificate | ESCerts (energy intensity) | CCCs (emission intensity)
  [7:3.1] Start | 2012 | Notified 2023 – first mandatory carbon market
  [7:3.2] Coverage | 478 units, 8 sectors (Cycle I) | 9 heavy industries, 208 units
  [7:3.3] Administrator / Regulator | BEE | BEE / CERC
[7:4] - CCTS supports **45% cut in GDP emission intensity by 2030** (vs 2005); UK **CBAM** recognised CCTS (from **1 January 2027**).
