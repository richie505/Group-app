=== NOTES 3:142 | Expressways and Industrial Corridors Under the Act; Vizag-chennai Industrial Corridor
## SUB 0 | VCIC, ECEC and the Act's Commitment
[142:0:0] # What the Act says (Thirteenth Schedule)
[142:0:1] - **Item 5:** GoI shall, **within six months** of the appointed day, examine the feasibility of a **Vizag-Chennai Industrial Corridor** "along the lines of the **Delhi-Mumbai Industrial Corridor** (DMIC)" and take an expeditious decision (asked: Asst. Architectural Surveyor).
[142:0:2] - Distractors: Delhi-Kolkata, Delhi-Raipur, Mumbai-Kolkata corridors – none is in the Act.
[142:0:3] - Same Schedule: GoI to **"take steps"** for rapid **rail and road** connectivity from the new AP capital to **Hyderabad** and other Telangana cities – **no deadline**.
[142:0:4] - **AP-ICDA Act, 2017**: creates the AP Industrial Corridor Development Authority; industrial parks notified under **Section 3**.
[142:0:5] # VCIC at a glance
[142:0:6] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [142:0:6.0] Length | About 800 km, along NH-16 on the AP coast
  [142:0:6.1] Parent corridor | First phase of the East Coast Economic Corridor (ECEC) = India's first coastal economic corridor (planned 2,500 km, Kolkata to Tuticorin)
  [142:0:6.2] Districts | Traverses nine (old) districts of AP (asked: DAO Mains)
  [142:0:6.3] Purpose | Gateway to ASEAN and East Asian markets – Act East Policy, Make in India
  [142:0:6.4] Funding partner | ADB (not JICA); modelled on DMIC; port-led
[142:0:7] - **"VCIC is a key part of"** → ECEC + ASEAN link + nine AP districts = **"All of these"** (G2 Mains 2019).
[142:0:8] - AP is implementing **three** corridors: **VCIC, CBIC and HBIC** (Hyderabad-Bengaluru).
[142:0:9] - Key initiative of the residual State quoted as attracting about **$17 billion** and **20,000 jobs** = **VCIC** (JL 2020 key; figure not verified). ADB: corridor industrial output to rise fourfold to about **$64 billion a year**.
[142:0:10] # Nodes
[142:0:11] TABLE head: Stage | Nodes
  [142:0:11.0] ADB's four identified nodes (conceptual plan, 2016) | Visakhapatnam (anchor), Kakinada, Gannavaram-Kankipadu, Srikalahasti-Yerpedu (nine clusters in all)
  [142:0:11.1] Long-term phased nodes | Donakonda and Machilipatnam
  [142:0:11.2] Nodes now being developed (SES 2024-25) | Vizag, Chittoor (Srikalahasti-Yerpedu), Kopparthy – 33,000 acres, Rs 5,000 crore
[142:0:12] - **"How many potential nodes on VCIC?"** → **4** (Panchayat Secretary Mains).
[142:0:13] - Vizag node: **Bulk Drug Park 2,002 acres** + steel cluster (Phase-1 2,200 acres); **Rambilli + Nakkapalli** clusters (Visakhapatnam) ≈ **49%** of corridor output.
[142:0:14] - Chittoor node: Srikalahasti-Yerpedu start-up area **2,628 acres** (Rs 463 crore).
[142:0:15] - **Kopparthy** (YSR Kadapa): Phase-1 **2,596 acres**, trunk infra ~Rs 1,264 crore via **NICDIT** (not JICA); **CCEA approved 28 Aug 2024**.
[142:0:16] - **Orvakal** is an **HBIC** node, not VCIC.
[142:0:17] > Exam angle: "on the lines of DMIC", ECEC first phase, nine districts, four nodes; do not confuse ADB (VCIC) with JICA (CBIC).

## SUB 2 | CBIC, HBIC, DMIC and the National Programme
[142:2:0] # Corridors compared
[142:2:1] TABLE head: Corridor | Partner | AP node(s)
  [142:2:1.0] DMIC (Delhi-Mumbai) | Japan – MoU 2006; runs on the Western DFC | –
  [142:2:1.1] CBIC (Chennai-Bengaluru, ~560 km to Chitradurga) | Japan (JICA) | Krishnapatnam
  [142:2:1.2] HBIC (Hyderabad-Bengaluru) | NICDIT | Orvakal
  [142:2:1.3] VCIC | ADB | Vizag, Chittoor, Kopparthy
[142:2:2] - **Corridor developed with Japan** (State PSC): among Chennai-Vizag, Mumbai-Bengaluru, **Delhi-Mumbai**, Amritsar-Kolkata → **DMIC** (CBIC is also JICA-assisted but was not an option).
[142:2:3] # CBIC nodes
[142:2:4] - JICA's Comprehensive Integrated Master Plan for CBIC: master planning of **three** nodes – **Ponneri** (Tamil Nadu), **Tumakuru** (Karnataka), **Krishnapatnam** (AP) (G2 Prelims 2019 asked "how many"; printed key "five" is wrong).
[142:2:5] - **Kuppam is NOT a CBIC node** (Panchayat Secretary Mains) – it is only a town along the route.
[142:2:6] - **Krishnapatnam Industrial Area (KRIS City)**: **Tirupati district** (since 2022), CBIC; Krishnapatnam North Node **10,834 acres**, start-up area 2,500 acres, Rs 2,139 crore via **NICDIT**.
[142:2:7] - **Orvakal** (Kurnool, HBIC): **2,621 acres** Phase-1, Rs 1,781 crore; CCEA approved **28 Aug 2024**.
[142:2:8] # National bodies and the Budget
[142:2:9] TABLE head: Body | Fact
  [142:2:9.0] NICDC | Corporation, formerly DMICDC, Jan 2008; Commerce & Industry via DPIIT; runs NICDP and BHAVYA
  [142:2:9.1] NICDIT | Trust; funds trunk infra at Kopparthy, Krishnapatnam, Orvakal; Rs 3,000 crore BE 2026-27
  [142:2:9.2] NICDP | 11 industrial corridors; Aug 2024: 12 more node projects approved
  [142:2:9.3] BHAVYA | 2026-27 to 2031-32; Rs 33,660 crore; 100 parks; six in AP
[142:2:10] - **Union Budget 2024-25**: funds promised for **Kopparthi** (VCIC) and **Orvakal** (HBIC) nodes (G2 PYQ).
[142:2:11] > Exam angle: JICA = CBIC and DMIC; ADB = VCIC; CBIC nodes Ponneri-Tumakuru-Krishnapatnam; Kopparthi + Orvakal in Budget 2024-25.

## SUB 1 | Origin, Criteria and Benefits of SCS
[143:1:0] # Origin
[143:1:1] - SCS introduced in **1969**, usually credited to the **5th Finance Commission**; the **Gadgil Formula** (1968-69) approved by the **National Development Council (NDC)** gave SCS States a reserved first share of Normal Central Assistance – do not swap FC and NDC.
[143:1:2] - **Not in the Constitution** – an executive, **Planning Commission-era** classification; later **Gadgil-Mukherjee Formula (1991)**.
[143:1:3] - **First three:** **J&K, Assam, Nagaland**; **11** SCS States in all, **last = Uttarakhand**.
[143:1:4] # Criteria (NDC)
[143:1:5] - **Hilly and difficult terrain**; **low population density and/or sizeable tribal population**; **strategic location along international borders**; **economic and infrastructural backwardness**; **non-viable state finances**.
[143:1:6] - NOT criteria: high population density, advanced infrastructure, high per-capita income.
[143:1:7] - **Raghuram Rajan Committee (2013)**: proposed a **Multi-Dimensional Index (MDI)** of state backwardness (10 sub-indicators) to replace SCS-style allocation – States scoring ≥0.6 "least developed" (SI 2019).
[143:1:8] # Benefits
[143:1:9] TABLE head: Benefit | SCS State | General State
  [143:1:9.0] CSS funding | Automatic 90:10 | Normal
  [143:1:9.1] Central loans | Concessional interest | Normal
  [143:1:9.2] Industry | Tax breaks and incentives | None special
  [143:1:9.3] Unspent funds | Do not lapse – carried forward | Lapse
  [143:1:9.4] Consumer Welfare Fund seed money | 90:10 | 75:25
  [143:1:9.5] PMGSY habitations | 250+ | 500+ (plains)
  [143:1:9.6] GST registration threshold | Lower (Rs 10 lakh) | Rs 20 lakh
[143:1:10] - Rationale: a larger share of central funds to cover the gap from geographic disadvantage and backwardness.
[143:1:11] # SCS States
[143:1:12] TABLE head: Status | States
  [143:1:12.0] Granted (11) | Assam, Nagaland, J&K, Himachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Tripura, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Uttarakhand
  [143:1:12.1] Asked, never granted | Bihar, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh
  [143:1:12.2] Not SCS | Telangana
[143:1:13] - Centre's fear: SCS for AP would bring identical demands from **Bihar and Odisha**.
