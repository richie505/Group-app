[0:0] TABLE head: Plan | Period | Theme / objective
  [0:0.0] 9th | 1997–2002 | Growth with social justice and equality
  [0:0.1] 10th | 2002–07 | Faster growth with development
  [0:0.2] 11th | 1 Apr 2007 – 31 Mar 2012 | "Towards Faster and More Inclusive Growth" – the approach paper's theme; first Plan to make inclusive growth its core objective
  [0:0.3] 12th | 2012–17 | "Faster, Sustainable and More Inclusive Growth" – adds the sustainable focus; the last Five Year Plan
[0:1] - Questions that say "inclusive growth" alone → **11th Plan**; "sustainable growth" or the three-word theme → **12th Plan**. "Growth with social justice" is the 9th Plan, not the 11th.
[0:2] - Short forms used in options all mean the 12th Plan theme: "faster sustainable inclusive growth", "faster, sustainable and more inclusive growth"; a "theme = faster growth + more inclusive growth + sustainable growth" question → **all of these**.
[0:3] - **11th Plan inclusive-growth components** (NextGen): **agricultural growth; employment generation and poverty reduction; reduction of regional and other disparities; equitable growth** – plus 27 monitorable national targets (income and poverty, education, health, women and children, infrastructure, environment). **Strengthening the capital market** and "exclusive dependence on urban services" are **not** components.
[0:4] - **12th Plan aims:** cut poverty, improve regional equality, better living conditions of SC/ST/OBC/minorities; it **emphasised inclusive growth** (not corporate, competitive or cooperative growth).
[0:5] - The basic, constant goal of all Indian Plans: **growth** (with self-reliance, employment, equity) – not curbing private enterprise or communes.
[0:6] - **Plan era ended 31 March 2017** (Planning Commission replaced by **NITI Aayog**, 1 Jan 2015); a 2012 question "which Plan are we in?" = **12th Plan** – now there is no Five Year Plan.
[0:7] > Repeat angle: 20 PYQs (APPSC, UPSC 2010/2014, NDA 2016, UPPSC, UKPSC, RAS) ask only "which theme belongs to which Plan".

## SUB 1 | Inclusive Growth – Meaning, Requirements and Inclusive Governance
[1:0] - **Inclusive growth** = **broad-based, shared and pro-poor** growth: growth that advances **equitable opportunities** for all economic participants, with benefits reaching **every section** of society; it raises people's **participation** in the growth process.
[1:1] - It looks at both the **pace and the pattern** of growth (who benefits), plus gender and regional balance.
[1:2] - **Three pillars** of India's model: **Market Economics, Empowerment, Pragmatism** (APPSC-GS 2025) – not "Antyodaya, Empowerment, Poverty and Unemployment".
[1:3] TABLE head: Requirement | Role (Rocket #70)
  [1:3.0] Infrastructure development | Expands access and economic opportunities
  [1:3.1] Agricultural revival | Benefits the rural population
  [1:3.2] Education, vocational training | Improves capabilities – the answer for "underutilised and unskilled manpower"
  [1:3.3] Health, social services | Improves human capital
  [1:3.4] Employment, credit to farmers | Enables participation and productive activity
  [1:3.5] SHGs | Financial / economic participation (bottom-up)
  [1:3.6] MSMEs | Employment and entrepreneurship
  [1:3.7] Right to Education | Brings disadvantaged children into the mainstream
[1:4] - Inclusive growth "would necessitate" infrastructure, agricultural revival and social services → **all of the above**; SHGs, MSMEs and the RTE Act all further it → **1, 2 and 3** (UPSC 2011).
[1:5] - Inclusive growth is **NOT** expected to come from a **high national-income growth rate by itself**; it comes from rural development, agriculture and adequate farm credit. Focusing solely on urban areas, raising import tariffs or cutting social spending do not help.
[1:6] - Target segments: marginal farmers, landless labourers, SC/ST – **not** semi-urban persons alone (UPPSC 2016).
[1:7] - **Inclusive governance** (UPSC 2012): **District Planning Committees** in all districts, **more public-health spending**, **stronger Mid-day Meal** → **2, 3 and 4**; permitting NBFCs to do banking is **not** part of it.
[1:8] - **OECD (with the G20):** "Promoting Strong, Sustainable and Inclusive Growth" – the OECD's publication / theme for G20 policy coordination.
[1:9] - Instruments in numbers: **DAY-NRLM** 10.05 crore women in **90.09 lakh** SHGs; **MSMEs** 31.1% of GDP, 35.4% of manufacturing output, **48.58%** of exports; **VB-G RAM G** replaced MGNREGA, **100 → 125 days**; **Aspirational Districts** (Jan 2018, 49 KPIs, 5 themes), **Aspirational Blocks** (Jan 2023, 500 blocks).
[1:10] - **AP:** Navaratnalu (2019–24: Amma Vodi, YSR Rythu Bharosa) → **Super Six**: **Talliki Vandanam** ₹15,000 per school child; **Annadata Sukhibhava** ₹20,000 per farmer; **Deepam-2** 3 free LPG cylinders; **Stree Shakti** free bus travel for women (15 Aug 2025); AP Rural Inclusive Growth Project (five components).

## SUB 2 | Distributive Justice – Constitution and Socialism
[2:0] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [2:0.0] 38 | Social order with social, economic and political justice; 38(2) (44th Amendment, 1978): minimise inequalities in income, status, facilities and opportunities
  [2:0.1] 39(b) | Ownership and control of the material resources of the community distributed to subserve the common good
  [2:0.2] 39(c) | Operation of the economic system must not result in concentration of wealth and means of production to the common detriment
  [2:0.3] 31C | 25th Amendment, 1971: protects laws giving effect to 39(b) and 39(c)
[2:1] - **Socialism** aims at **distributive justice** – fair distribution of income, wealth and resources; both statements of 39(b) and 39(c) are **present** in the Constitution (DPSP, Part IV).
[2:2] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth, Indian Polity 8th ed. ch. Directive Principles (Art. 38, 39, 31C); app notes 3:15 sub 1
## SUB 3 | Approaches to Poverty and the Bhagwati–Sen Debate
[3:0] TABLE head: Approach | Illustration
  [3:0.0] Trickle-down | Part of the income of the asset-owning class filters down to the assetless; early Plans' growth-first strategy (criticised: widens inequality)
  [3:0.1] Direct attack | 5th Plan "Garibi Hatao"; wage-employment and livelihood missions
  [3:0.2] Welfare | Nordic states
  [3:0.3] Bottom-up | SHG movement
[3:1] - **Bhagwati–Sen debate** – growth first vs social infrastructure first:
[3:2] TABLE head:  | Jagdish Bhagwati (with Arvind Panagariya) | Amartya Sen (with Jean Drèze)
  [3:2.0] Priority | Economic growth first – rapid market-led growth creates jobs and revenue for welfare | Investment in social infrastructure first – health, education, nutrition
  [3:2.1] Book (2013) | *Why Growth Matters* | *An Uncertain Glory*
[3:3] - The debate (APPSC G1 2017) is **growth first vs social infrastructure first** – not industry vs agriculture, rural vs urban or private vs public investment.
[3:4] - Sen: backward countries should give top priority to **primary education and health** (APPSC-GS 2007).
[3:5] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy 10th ed. ch.2 (trickle-down); NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #36; app notes 3:15 sub 2-3
## SUB 4 | Measuring Inclusion – WEF Inclusive Development Index and Poverty Data
[4:0] - **WEF Inclusive Development Index (IDI)** – pillars: **growth and development; inclusion; intergenerational equity and sustainability**.
[4:1] TABLE head: Edition | Top (developing / emerging) | India
  [4:1.0] IDI 2017 (16 Jan 2017, Davos) | Lithuania (then Azerbaijan, Hungary) | 60th of 79 developing economies
  [4:1.1] IDI 2018 (Jan 2018) | Lithuania | 62nd of 74 emerging economies
[4:2] - Inclusion is measured by **HDI**, poverty rates, literacy, access to basic services and gender equality – not by GDP, industrial output or employment alone.
[4:3] - **World Bank:** extreme poverty ($3/day) **27.1% (2011-12) → 5.3% (2022-23)**; at $4.20 a day 57.7% → **23.9%**. World Inequality Database: income inequality **rising**.
