## SUB 1 | Human Poverty Index, Capability Poverty Measure and Income vs Human Poverty
[1:0] - UNDP's two categories of poverty: **income poverty** and **human poverty** – both.
[1:1] - **HPI** measures deprivation in **longevity, knowledge and a decent standard of living** (APPSC-GS 2018 key: it indicates the **standard of living**).
[1:2]   - HPI-1 (developing): survival to 40, adult illiteracy, no safe water, underweight children (**social services**).
[1:3]   - HPI-2 (developed) adds **income poverty** and **long-term unemployment** (social exclusion) → income, literacy, social-services and employment deprivations are **all** covered (CDS 2013).
[1:4] - **CPM (HDR 1996)** indicators: **children under 5 underweight** (child malnutrition), **births unattended** by trained personnel, **female illiteracy** – not male illiteracy, male labour-force participation or drinking water.
[1:5] ~~ Sources: UNDP HDR 1996 (CPM) and HDR 1997 / 2009 technical notes (HPI-1, HPI-2) (hdr.undp.org); app notes 3:17 sub 1-2
## SUB 2 | Gender Indices – GDI, GEM and GII
[2:0] TABLE head: Index | Dimensions / indicators | Reading
  [2:0.0] GDI | Female vs male health (life expectancy), knowledge (schooling), living standard (earned income) | Ratio female HDI / male HDI; 1 = parity, below 1 = women behind; India 0.874 (HDR 2025, Group 5)
  [2:0.1] GEM (1995) | Political participation (parliament seats), economic participation (managers, professionals), power over economic resources (earned income) | Empowerment – no health or education indicator
  [2:0.2] GII (2010) | Reproductive health (MMR, adolescent birth rate); empowerment (parliament seats, secondary education 25+); labour market (LFPR 15+) | 0 = equality, higher = more inequality
[2:1] - **GDI and GEM indicate inequalities in gender** (not income, expenditure or regions).
[2:2] - The GDI does **not** cover **gender violence**.
[2:3] - **India's GII:** **108th** (HDR 2023-24, for 2022; value 0.437) → **102nd**, 0.403 (HDR 2025, for 2023).
[2:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Exams 16 p.4, p.6-7 (GDI, GII); UNDP HDR 1995 (GEM); UNDP HDR 2023-24 and 2025 tables 4-5 (hdr.undp.org); app notes 3:17 sub 3
## SUB 3 | IHDI, PHDI and Beyond-HDI Statements
[3:0] - **IHDI** discounts each HDI dimension for inequality: **no inequality → IHDI = HDI**; otherwise IHDI < HDI. HDI = "potential", IHDI = "actual"; **HDI − IHDI = loss due to inequality** – that loss is **not** part of the HDI itself.
[3:1] - **PHDI** (2020) adjusts HDI for **CO2 emissions and material footprint**; it captures **intergenerational** inequality just as the IHDI captures **intragenerational** inequality.
[3:2] - **HDR 2021/22**: a **new uncertainty complex** – dangerous planetary change, sweeping societal transformations and **polarisation**.
[3:3] - A GDI **below 1** means women's HDI is **lower** than men's (not higher); same GNI with different outcomes shows the role of **policy**, not "growth over development".
[3:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Exams 16 p.2-3 (IHDI); UNDP HDR 2020 (PHDI), HDR 2021/22 overview (uncertainty complex) (hdr.undp.org); app notes 3:16 sub 6
## SUB 4 | MPI – Design and the Global MPI
[4:0] - **Global MPI** (2010): **OPHI (Oxford) + UNDP**, presented in HDR 2010; proposed by **Sabina Alkire and Maria Emma Santos**; method **Alkire-Foster**; uses household-survey micro-data; **not** a $1-a-day measure of extreme poverty.
[4:1] - **MPI = Headcount ratio (H) × Intensity (A)** – not a sum; MPI-poor = deprived in at least **one-third** of weighted indicators. Most suitable for the **intensity of poverty**.
[4:2] TABLE head: Dimension | Global MPI indicators (10) | Weight
  [4:2.0] Health | Nutrition, child mortality | 1/6 each
  [4:2.1] Education | Years of schooling, school attendance | 1/6 each
  [4:2.2] Standard of living | Cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets | 1/18 each
[4:3] - **India in the Global MPI** (UNDP-OPHI editions):
[4:4] TABLE head: Edition | India
  [4:4.0] HDR 2013 | 53.7% multidimensionally poor
  [4:4.1] Global MPI 2018 | 271 million out of poverty (2005-06 to 2015-16); Bihar poorest State
  [4:4.2] Global MPI 2022 (UNDP + OPHI) | MPI 0.069 (2019-21); headcount 16.4%; about 415 million (books: 414 million) out (2005-06 to 2019-21)
  [4:4.3] Global MPI 2023 (released 11 July 2023 by UNDP and OPHI) | Same India data; 415 million out in 15 years
  [4:4.4] Global MPI 2024 → 2025 | MPI 0.064 → 0.061

## SUB 5 | National MPI (NITI Aayog), States and Andhra Pradesh
[5:0] TABLE head: Feature | National MPI
  [5:0.0] By | NITI Aayog (with UNDP, OPHI); baseline Nov 2021; monitored under GIRG
  [5:0.1] Method | Alkire-Foster – same as the Global MPI
  [5:0.2] Indicators | 12 = 10 global + maternal health + bank account; standard of living has 7 (cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets, bank account) – years of schooling is an education indicator
[5:1] - **Progress Review 2023** (NFHS-4 → NFHS-5): headcount **24.85% → 14.96%**; MPI 0.117 → 0.066; intensity 47% → **44.39%**; about **13.5 crore** out; **rural fell faster** (32.59% → 19.28%) than urban (8.65% → 5.27%).

[5:3] - NITI Discussion Paper (Jan 2024): 29.17% (2013-14) → 11.28% (2022-23); 24.82 crore out; UP largest fall (5.94 crore).
[5:4] - **PM Garib Kalyan Package** (2020) included free foodgrain entitlements (PMGKAY).
[5:5] - **Andhra Pradesh:** State MPI report (2018, Planning Dept + OPHI): **41.6% (2005-06) → 21% (2016-17)**; NITI MPI 2023: AP **6.06%** (India 14.96%), MPI **0.053 (2015-16) → 0.025** (2019-21), intensity 41.12%, rural 7.71% / urban 2.20%; AP **9th**; lowest-poverty district **West Godavari 2.42%** (then Guntur 4.36%, Krishna 4.38%, Chittoor 5.66%).
