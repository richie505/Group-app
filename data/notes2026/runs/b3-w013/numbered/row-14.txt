## SUB 0 | Growth vs Development – Concepts and the New View
[0:0] TABLE head: Basis | Economic growth | Economic development
  [0:0.0] Meaning | Rise in real output / income (GDP, GNP, per capita income) | Growth plus better quality of life – health, education, nutrition, less poverty, unemployment, inequality
  [0:0.1] Nature | Quantitative; value-neutral (can be negative) | Qualitative + quantitative; normative
  [0:0.2] Scope | Narrow | Wider – includes growth
  [0:0.3] Relation | Necessary but not sufficient | The end; needs conscious public policy
[0:1] - Growth can be measured; development is a **policy intervention endeavour aimed at the economic and social well-being of people** – the definition asked by APPSC is **economic development** (not "sustainable growth").
[0:2] - Rise in absolute and per capita real GNP is **not** higher development if **poverty and unemployment increase** (UPSC 2018).
[0:3] - **Best / most appropriate measure of growth:** growth of **per capita real income** year to year – not national income at current prices (inflation, population), not GDP/NDP/NNP totals.
[0:4] - **Western (modernisation) model** (Rostow): key factor = **economic growth** (industrialisation, GDP).
[0:5] - **New view** (1970s): **Dudley Seers**, *The Meaning of Development* (**1969**) – development = falling **poverty, unemployment and inequality**; it does **not** stress the **simple growth rate**. Its key part: **eradication of poverty**.
[0:6] - **Trickle-down theory:** benefits of growth **percolate down** to the poor automatically (early Plans).
[0:7] - **UNDP HDR 1996:** five bad growths – **jobless, ruthless, voiceless, rootless, futureless**.
[0:8] - **Social development** = comprehensive change – economic, political, social and cultural.
[0:9] - **Negative economic development** (development going backward) is called **regression**; falling output itself is a **recession** / negative growth.
[0:10] - Economic welfare is linked to trickle-down benefits, sustainable development and reduced inequality – **not** to larger imports of consumption goods.
[0:11] - Development planning breaks obstacles to raise **income and employment**.
[0:12] - A-R asked: "development = improving living standards and welfare" (A) and "per capita income growth is not a sufficient measure" (R) – both true, **R explains A**.
[0:13] - **Other models:** **Gandhian** – decentralisation and village industry; **Directive Principles** – adequate livelihood and less inequality, not just higher national income; **capability approach** – broader than GDP.
[0:14] - **Indicators of development:** institutional change, inclusive growth, human resource development, rising capital formation and **structural transformation** (shift from agriculture to industry and services; best sign: rising share of **manufactured goods in exports**). A **larger GDP share from the primary sector** is NOT an indicator.
[0:15] > Kerala model: high human development at modest per capita income – a State with a lower income rank can do better in human development.

## SUB 2 | Determinants of Growth – Economic and Non-economic Factors
[2:0] TABLE head: Economic factors | Non-economic factors
  [2:0.0] Capital formation, savings, investment, natural resources, technical progress, foreign exchange / foreign trade, marketable agricultural surplus | Human resources, social organisation / social behaviour, institutions, political stability, corruption, rule of law
[2:1] - **Capital formation** = the factor that **necessarily** brings growth in a country (UPSC 2013); Planning Commission: the most important factor.
[2:2] - Capital formation depends on **savings** and effective **financial institutions**; **investment** is its essential factor – both statements true.
[2:3] - **Saving is one of the determinants** of growth – not the only or the major one.
[2:4] - "Not a major factor of growth": **technocrats and bureaucrats**; "not a non-economic factor": **market surplus in agriculture**.
[2:5] - Population and development affect **each other** (both directions true).
[2:6] - **Steel plough replacing a wooden one** = **capital-augmenting** technical progress (better tool, UPSC 2015).
[2:7] - **Resource curse / paradox of plenty**: resource-rich countries showing less growth, less democracy and less development.
[2:8] - Capital formation may fail to raise output when capital is idle or misused, complementary inputs (skills, infrastructure) are missing or demand is weak – **all of these**.
[2:9] - Workers moving out of agriculture raise productivity; **social overhead capital** and **human resource development** are part of development.
[2:10] - **Harrod-Domar**: saving (investment-income) ratio ÷ capital-output ratio = growth rate of **national income**; higher ICOR = lower growth.
[2:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.50 (capital formation in X); NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #68 p.98 (capital-output ratio); pmfias.com PYQ 2015 (steel plough); app notes 3:14 sub 7
## SUB 3 | Theories of Development and Books
[3:0] TABLE head: Theory / idea | Economist
  [3:0.0] Vicious circle of poverty – "a country is poor because it is poor" | Ragnar Nurkse – *Problems of Capital Formation in Underdeveloped Countries* (1953)
  [3:0.1] Balanced growth | Nurkse (with Rosenstein-Rodan)
  [3:0.2] Big push – large simultaneous investment in industry | Paul Rosenstein-Rodan (1943)
  [3:0.3] Unbalanced growth | A.O. Hirschman – *The Strategy of Economic Development* (1958)
  [3:0.4] Critical minimum effort | Harvey Leibenstein
  [3:0.5] Unlimited supplies of labour (dual economy) | W. Arthur Lewis (1954)
  [3:0.6] Stages of growth | W.W. Rostow
  [3:0.7] Cumulative causation | Gunnar Myrdal – *Economic Theory and Underdeveloped Regions* (1957)
  [3:0.8] Strategic Factors in Economic Development | Charles P. Kindleberger
  [3:0.9] Basic needs / welfare approach | ILO (1976) and Paul Streeten / Pigou
[3:1] - **Rostow's stages:** traditional society → pre-conditions → **take-off** → drive to maturity → high mass consumption.
[3:2] - **Criticisms of Rostow** – data contradict the take-off thesis, no explanation of growth after take-off, stages hard to test empirically: **all of the above**.
[3:3] - ***The Limits to Growth*** (1972, **Donella Meadows** and colleagues, Club of Rome): interactions of **population, resources and pollution** would limit growth (the geometric-ratio idea is Malthus).

## SUB 4 | Measures of Development – Per Capita Income, PQLI, HDI, GNH
[4:0] TABLE head: Measure | Developer / year | Components
  [4:0.0] Per capita income | Simon Kuznets, US, 1934 (Nobel 1971) | Income only – oldest; hides distribution
  [4:0.1] PQLI | Morris D. Morris, Overseas Development Council, mid-1970s (book 1979) | Literacy, infant mortality, life expectancy at age one; 0-100 – no income
  [4:0.2] HDI | UNDP, 1990; Mahbub ul Haq (with Sen, Inge Kaul) | Life expectancy at birth, education, GNI per capita (PPP); 0 to 1
  [4:0.3] MPI | OPHI + UNDP, 2010 | Health, education, living standards
  [4:0.4] BNI | Economic Survey | Water, sanitation, housing
  [4:0.5] GNH | Bhutan, 4th King Jigme Singye Wangchuck, 1970s | Alternative to GDP
  [4:0.6] MEW / NEW | Nordhaus-Tobin (1972) / Paul Samuelson | Welfare-adjusted income
[4:1] - **Order of introduction:** per capita income → **PQLI** → **HDI** (→ MPI).
[4:2] - "Most suitable estimate of physical quality of life": **infant mortality rate and literacy**.
[4:3] - Basic indices of quality of life in developing countries: **real income, health and educational attainment** (the HDI trio).
[4:4] - First State to publish a Human Development Report: **Madhya Pradesh**.
[4:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.38 (PQLI Morris 1979; HDI team Haq and Kaul); NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #52 p.77 (index table), #69 p.100; Ramesh Singh ch.2 p.2.4-2.6 (GNH Bhutan); app notes 3:14 sub 4
