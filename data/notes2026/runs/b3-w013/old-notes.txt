=== NOTES 3:14 | Economic Growth vs Economic Development; Measures of Development
## SUB 0 | Growth vs Development – Concepts and the New View
[14:0:0] TABLE head: Basis | Economic growth | Economic development
  [14:0:0.0] Meaning | Rise in real output / income (GDP, GNP, per capita income) | Growth plus better quality of life – health, education, nutrition, less poverty, unemployment, inequality
  [14:0:0.1] Nature | Quantitative; value-neutral (can be negative) | Qualitative + quantitative; normative
  [14:0:0.2] Scope | Narrow | Wider – includes growth
  [14:0:0.3] Relation | Necessary but not sufficient | The end; needs conscious public policy
[14:0:1] - Growth can be measured; development is a **policy intervention endeavour aimed at the economic and social well-being of people** – the definition asked by APPSC is **economic development** (not "sustainable growth").
[14:0:2] - Rise in absolute and per capita real GNP is **not** higher development if **poverty and unemployment increase** (UPSC 2018).
[14:0:3] - **Best / most appropriate measure of growth:** growth of **per capita real income** year to year – not national income at current prices (inflation, population), not GDP/NDP/NNP totals.
[14:0:4] - **Western (modernisation) model** (Rostow): key factor = **economic growth** (industrialisation, GDP).
[14:0:5] - **New view** (1970s): **Dudley Seers**, *The Meaning of Development* (**1969**) – development = falling **poverty, unemployment and inequality**; it does **not** stress the **simple growth rate**. Its key part: **eradication of poverty**.
[14:0:6] - **Trickle-down theory:** benefits of growth **percolate down** to the poor automatically (early Plans).
[14:0:7] - **UNDP HDR 1996:** five bad growths – **jobless, ruthless, voiceless, rootless, futureless**.
[14:0:8] - **Social development** = comprehensive change – economic, political, social and cultural.
[14:0:9] - **Negative economic development** (development going backward) is called **regression**; falling output itself is a **recession** / negative growth.
[14:0:10] - Economic welfare is linked to trickle-down benefits, sustainable development and reduced inequality – **not** to larger imports of consumption goods.
[14:0:11] - Development planning breaks obstacles to raise **income and employment**.
[14:0:12] - A-R asked: "development = improving living standards and welfare" (A) and "per capita income growth is not a sufficient measure" (R) – both true, **R explains A**.
[14:0:13] - **Other models:** **Gandhian** – decentralisation and village industry; **Directive Principles** – adequate livelihood and less inequality, not just higher national income; **capability approach** – broader than GDP.
[14:0:14] - **Indicators of development:** institutional change, inclusive growth, human resource development, rising capital formation and **structural transformation** (shift from agriculture to industry and services; best sign: rising share of **manufactured goods in exports**). A **larger GDP share from the primary sector** is NOT an indicator.
[14:0:15] > Kerala model: high human development at modest per capita income – a State with a lower income rank can do better in human development.
[14:0:16] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy 10th ed. ch.2 p.2.2-2.4 (growth, development, Seers 1969); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.37 (growth = quantitative rise); NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #69 p.100; Wikipedia "Economic development" (policy intervention definition); app notes 3:14 sub 0-1
[14:0:17] - A-R: development needs a **multi-dimensional approach** (true); the Government works on **both micro and macro** issues, not mainly micro ones → **A true, R false**.
[14:0:18] - **Development**, in short = **change towards betterment** (a better quality of life, not just more output).

## SUB 2 | Determinants of Growth – Economic and Non-economic Factors
[14:2:0] TABLE head: Economic factors | Non-economic factors
  [14:2:0.0] Capital formation, savings, investment, natural resources, technical progress, foreign exchange / foreign trade, marketable agricultural surplus | Human resources, social organisation / social behaviour, institutions, political stability, corruption, rule of law
[14:2:1] - **Capital formation** = the factor that **necessarily** brings growth in a country (UPSC 2013); Planning Commission: the most important factor.
[14:2:2] - Capital formation depends on **savings** and effective **financial institutions**; **investment** is its essential factor – both statements true.
[14:2:3] - **Saving is one of the determinants** of growth – not the only or the major one.
[14:2:4] - "Not a major factor of growth": **technocrats and bureaucrats**; "not a non-economic factor": **market surplus in agriculture**.
[14:2:5] - Population and development affect **each other** (both directions true).
[14:2:6] - **Steel plough replacing a wooden one** = **capital-augmenting** technical progress (better tool, UPSC 2015).
[14:2:7] - **Resource curse / paradox of plenty**: resource-rich countries showing less growth, less democracy and less development.
[14:2:8] - Capital formation may fail to raise output when capital is idle or misused, complementary inputs (skills, infrastructure) are missing or demand is weak – **all of these**.
[14:2:9] - Workers moving out of agriculture raise productivity; **social overhead capital** and **human resource development** are part of development.
[14:2:10] - **Harrod-Domar**: saving (investment-income) ratio ÷ capital-output ratio = growth rate of **national income**; higher ICOR = lower growth.

## SUB 4 | Theories of Development and Books
[14:4:0] TABLE head: Theory / idea | Economist
  [14:4:0.0] Vicious circle of poverty – "a country is poor because it is poor" | Ragnar Nurkse – *Problems of Capital Formation in Underdeveloped Countries* (1953)
  [14:4:0.1] Balanced growth | Nurkse (with Rosenstein-Rodan)
  [14:4:0.2] Big push – large simultaneous investment in industry | Paul Rosenstein-Rodan (1943)
  [14:4:0.3] Unbalanced growth | A.O. Hirschman – *The Strategy of Economic Development* (1958)
  [14:4:0.4] Critical minimum effort | Harvey Leibenstein

  [14:4:0.9] Basic needs / welfare approach | ILO (1976) and Paul Streeten / Pigou

## SUB 5 | Measures of Development – Per Capita Income, PQLI, HDI, GNH
[14:5:0] TABLE head: Measure | Developer / year | Components
  [14:5:0.0] Per capita income | Simon Kuznets, US, 1934 (Nobel 1971) | Income only – oldest; hides distribution
  [14:5:0.1] PQLI | Morris D. Morris, Overseas Development Council, mid-1970s (book 1979) | Literacy, infant mortality, life expectancy at age one; 0-100 – no income
  [14:5:0.2] HDI | UNDP, 1990; Mahbub ul Haq (with Sen, Inge Kaul) | Life expectancy at birth, education, GNI per capita (PPP); 0 to 1
  [14:5:0.3] MPI | OPHI + UNDP, 2010 | Health, education, living standards
  [14:5:0.4] BNI | Economic Survey | Water, sanitation, housing
  [14:5:0.5] GNH | Bhutan, 4th King Jigme Singye Wangchuck, 1970s | Alternative to GDP
  [14:5:0.6] MEW / NEW | Nordhaus-Tobin (1972) / Paul Samuelson | Welfare-adjusted income
[14:5:1] - **Order of introduction:** per capita income → **PQLI** → **HDI** (→ MPI).
[14:5:2] - "Most suitable estimate of physical quality of life": **infant mortality rate and literacy**.
[14:5:3] - Basic indices of quality of life in developing countries: **real income, health and educational attainment** (the HDI trio).
[14:5:4] - First State to publish a Human Development Report: **Madhya Pradesh**.
[14:5:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.38 (PQLI Morris 1979; HDI team Haq and Kaul); NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #52 p.77 (index table), #69 p.100; Ramesh Singh ch.2 p.2.4-2.6 (GNH Bhutan); app notes 3:14 sub 4

## SUB 1 | Inclusive Growth – Meaning, Requirements and Inclusive Governance
[15:1:0] - **Inclusive growth** = **broad-based, shared and pro-poor** growth: growth that advances **equitable opportunities** for all economic participants, with benefits reaching **every section** of society; it raises people's **participation** in the growth process.
[15:1:1] - It looks at both the **pace and the pattern** of growth (who benefits), plus gender and regional balance.
[15:1:2] - **Three pillars** of India's model: **Market Economics, Empowerment, Pragmatism** (APPSC-GS 2025) – not "Antyodaya, Empowerment, Poverty and Unemployment".
[15:1:3] TABLE head: Requirement | Role (Rocket #70)
  [15:1:3.0] Infrastructure development | Expands access and economic opportunities
  [15:1:3.1] Agricultural revival | Benefits the rural population
  [15:1:3.2] Education, vocational training | Improves capabilities – the answer for "underutilised and unskilled manpower"
  [15:1:3.3] Health, social services | Improves human capital
  [15:1:3.4] Employment, credit to farmers | Enables participation and productive activity
  [15:1:3.5] SHGs | Financial / economic participation (bottom-up)
  [15:1:3.6] MSMEs | Employment and entrepreneurship
  [15:1:3.7] Right to Education | Brings disadvantaged children into the mainstream
[15:1:4] - Inclusive growth "would necessitate" infrastructure, agricultural revival and social services → **all of the above**; SHGs, MSMEs and the RTE Act all further it → **1, 2 and 3** (UPSC 2011).
[15:1:5] - Inclusive growth is **NOT** expected to come from a **high national-income growth rate by itself**; it comes from rural development, agriculture and adequate farm credit. Focusing solely on urban areas, raising import tariffs or cutting social spending do not help.
[15:1:6] - Target segments: marginal farmers, landless labourers, SC/ST – **not** semi-urban persons alone (UPPSC 2016).
[15:1:7] - **Inclusive governance** (UPSC 2012): **District Planning Committees** in all districts, **more public-health spending**, **stronger Mid-day Meal** → **2, 3 and 4**; permitting NBFCs to do banking is **not** part of it.
[15:1:8] - **OECD (with the G20):** "Promoting Strong, Sustainable and Inclusive Growth" – the OECD's publication / theme for G20 policy coordination.
[15:1:9] - Instruments in numbers: **DAY-NRLM** 10.05 crore women in **90.09 lakh** SHGs; **MSMEs** 31.1% of GDP, 35.4% of manufacturing output, **48.58%** of exports; **VB-G RAM G** replaced MGNREGA, **100 → 125 days**; **Aspirational Districts** (Jan 2018, 49 KPIs, 5 themes), **Aspirational Blocks** (Jan 2023, 500 blocks).
[15:1:10] - **AP:** Navaratnalu (2019–24: Amma Vodi, YSR Rythu Bharosa) → **Super Six**: **Talliki Vandanam** ₹15,000 per school child; **Annadata Sukhibhava** ₹20,000 per farmer; **Deepam-2** 3 free LPG cylinders; **Stree Shakti** free bus travel for women (15 Aug 2025); AP Rural Inclusive Growth Project (five components).
[15:1:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.37 (broad-based, shared, pro-poor); NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #70 p.101 (requirements; inclusive governance); UPSC CSE 2011/2012 papers; app notes 3:15 sub 0, 5
[15:1:12] - **Inclusive governance** = governance and service delivery with **equal access to government facilities, participation and inclusion**, and public health, **planning and welfare services**.
[15:1:13] - **Regional disparities** stay high because of **investment concentrated in select locales**, **agro-climatically less conducive areas** and areas with **little agrarian transformation** → **1, 2 and 3**; political instability is not a cited cause.
[15:1:14] - Inclusion strategy focuses on **reducing poverty and inequality** and **diversifying livelihoods** (e.g. of tribal people) within the country – **not** on bringing poorer countries closer.
## SUB 2 | Distributive Justice – Constitution and Socialism
[15:2:0] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [15:2:0.0] 38 | Social order with social, economic and political justice; 38(2) (44th Amendment, 1978): minimise inequalities in income, status, facilities and opportunities
  [15:2:0.1] 39(b) | Ownership and control of the material resources of the community distributed to subserve the common good
  [15:2:0.2] 39(c) | Operation of the economic system must not result in concentration of wealth and means of production to the common detriment
  [15:2:0.3] 31C | 25th Amendment, 1971: protects laws giving effect to 39(b) and 39(c)
[15:2:1] - **Socialism** aims at **distributive justice** – fair distribution of income, wealth and resources; both statements of 39(b) and 39(c) are **present** in the Constitution (DPSP, Part IV).
[15:2:2] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth, Indian Polity 8th ed. ch. Directive Principles (Art. 38, 39, 31C); app notes 3:15 sub 1
[15:2:3] - **Functional distribution** = factor shares (wages, rent, interest, profit) – depends on **technology, the production function and the wage-rental (w/r) ratio**; **asset levels** decide the **personal (size) distribution**.
[15:2:4] - **Theories of justice:** **John Rawls** – principles for the **basic structure** of society (justice as fairness); **David Miller** – pluralist social justice (need, desert, equality) that applies to **both public and private spheres**.

=== NOTES 3:16 | Human Development Index – Components, Methodology, India's Rank and Trend
## SUB 0 | HDI Dimensions, Indicators and Methodology
[16:0:0] TABLE head: Dimension | Indicator | Min | Max
  [16:0:0.0] Long and healthy life | Life expectancy at birth | 20 | 85
  [16:0:0.1] Knowledge | Expected years of schooling | 0 | 18
  [16:0:0.2] Knowledge | Mean years of schooling (adults 25+) | 0 | 15
  [16:0:0.3] Decent standard of living | GNI per capita (PPP $), log | $100 | $75,000
[16:0:1] - HDI = composite of **life expectancy, educational attainment and per capita income**; base = **health, education, standard of living**. Before 2010 the income indicator was per capita **GDP**.
[16:0:2] - **NOT in the HDI:** **ownership of basic assets**, **access to banking**, **employment**, poverty, nutritional status, quality of life, environment, urbanisation, human security, gender, inequality (that is the **IHDI**).
[16:0:3] - Method: dimension index = (actual − min)/(max − min); education index = **arithmetic mean** of its two sub-indices; income uses the **logarithm** (diminishing returns); **HDI = geometric mean** of the three indices (**since 2010**), equal weights.
[16:0:4] - **PPP dollars** allow fair comparison across price levels.
[16:0:5] TABLE head: Category | HDI value
  [16:0:5.0] Very high | 0.800 and above
  [16:0:5.1] High | 0.700–0.799
  [16:0:5.2] Medium | 0.550–0.699
  [16:0:5.3] Low | below 0.550
[16:0:6] - India **0.685** (HDR 2025, for 2023) = **Medium**, just below 0.700.
[16:0:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Exams 15 p.6-9 (indicators, goalposts, groupings), 16 p.1 (geometric mean since 2010); NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #52 p.77; UNDP HDR 2025 Technical notes; app notes 3:16 sub 1-3
[16:0:8] - **HDI range: 0 to 1** – the upper limit is **1** (each dimension index also runs 0 to 1).
## SUB 1 | Human Development and the HDR – Origin, UNDP, Concept and Pillars
[16:1:0] - **UNDP** was the first to focus on the **human aspect of development**: first **Human Development Report (HDR), 1990**, which contained the first **HDI**; HDRs released most years since.
[16:1:1] - The HDR **compares countries on educational levels, health status and per capita income** ("Human Development Report" – not a population, education or quality report).
[16:1:2] - **Mahbub ul Haq** introduced the concept and **created the HDI (1990)**; **Amartya Sen** worked with him on the first HDRs (team incl. Inge Kaul, Paul Streeten, Frances Stewart). Sen = **capability approach**; **Martha Nussbaum** extended it with a list of central capabilities.
[16:1:3] - Human development = enlarging people's **choices, freedoms and opportunities**; people, not growth alone, are the criteria; income is a **means**, not the end.
[16:1:4] - **Four pillars of human development:** **Equity, Sustainability, Productivity, Empowerment** (CDS 2020) – not "inclusion" or "gender".
[16:1:5] TABLE head: Approach to human development | Idea
  [16:1:5.0] Income | Higher income = higher human development (oldest)
  [16:1:5.1] Welfare | People as passive beneficiaries of welfare spending
  [16:1:5.2] Minimum / basic needs (ILO) | Health, education, food, water, sanitation, housing – India's Minimum Needs Programme (5th Plan) = investment in human (resources) approach
  [16:1:5.3] Capabilities | Amartya Sen
[16:1:6] - **HDR 2016** ("Human Development for Everyone"): **universalism** is the key to human development.
[16:1:7] - UNDP identifies two categories of poverty: **income poverty** and **human poverty** – both.
[16:1:8] - **Same GNI, different HDI** shows the role of **national policy choices** (Malaysia richer than Chile, but Chile ~7 years more life expectancy and 3 more years of expected schooling).
[16:1:9] TABLE head: Basis | GDP / per capita income | Human development
  [16:1:9.0] Nature | Primarily economic measure | Broader, people-centred
  [16:1:9.1] Dimensions | One-dimensional | Multidimensional
[16:1:10] - **Countries covered:** HDR 2018 update **189**; HDR 2021/22 191; HDR 2023-24 and 2025 **193**.
[16:1:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Exams 15 p.1-6 (concept, Haq and Sen, four pillars, approaches, policy choices); NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #4 p.5, #53 p.79; UNDP HDR 2016 and 2018 Statistical Update (hdr.undp.org); app notes 3:16 sub 0

## SUB 0 | MPI – Design and the Global MPI
[17:0:0] - **Global MPI** (2010): **OPHI (Oxford) + UNDP**, presented in HDR 2010; proposed by **Sabina Alkire and Maria Emma Santos**; method **Alkire-Foster**; uses household-survey micro-data; **not** a $1-a-day measure of extreme poverty.
[17:0:1] - **MPI = Headcount ratio (H) × Intensity (A)** – not a sum; MPI-poor = deprived in at least **one-third** of weighted indicators. Most suitable for the **intensity of poverty**.

  [17:0:2.0] Health | Nutrition, child mortality | 1/6 each
  [17:0:2.1] Education | Years of schooling, school attendance | 1/6 each
  [17:0:2.2] Standard of living | Cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets | 1/18 each

## SUB 4 | Human Poverty Index, Capability Poverty Measure and Income vs Human Poverty
[17:4:0] - UNDP's two categories of poverty: **income poverty** and **human poverty** – both.
[17:4:1] - **HPI** measures deprivation in **longevity, knowledge and a decent standard of living** (APPSC-GS 2018 key: it indicates the **standard of living**).
[17:4:2]   - HPI-1 (developing): survival to 40, adult illiteracy, no safe water, underweight children (**social services**).
[17:4:3]   - HPI-2 (developed) adds **income poverty** and **long-term unemployment** (social exclusion) → income, literacy, social-services and employment deprivations are **all** covered (CDS 2013).
[17:4:4] - **CPM (HDR 1996)** indicators: **children under 5 underweight** (child malnutrition), **births unattended** by trained personnel, **female illiteracy** – not male illiteracy, male labour-force participation or drinking water.
[17:4:5] ~~ Sources: UNDP HDR 1996 (CPM) and HDR 1997 / 2009 technical notes (HPI-1, HPI-2) (hdr.undp.org); app notes 3:17 sub 1-2
## SUB 5 | IHDI, PHDI and Beyond-HDI Statements
[17:5:0] - **IHDI** discounts each HDI dimension for inequality: **no inequality → IHDI = HDI**; otherwise IHDI < HDI. HDI = "potential", IHDI = "actual"; **HDI − IHDI = loss due to inequality** – that loss is **not** part of the HDI itself.
[17:5:1] - **PHDI** (2020) adjusts HDI for **CO2 emissions and material footprint**; it captures **intergenerational** inequality just as the IHDI captures **intragenerational** inequality.
[17:5:2] - **HDR 2021/22**: a **new uncertainty complex** – dangerous planetary change, sweeping societal transformations and **polarisation**.
[17:5:3] - A GDI **below 1** means women's HDI is **lower** than men's (not higher); same GNI with different outcomes shows the role of **policy**, not "growth over development".
[17:5:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Exams 16 p.2-3 (IHDI); UNDP HDR 2020 (PHDI), HDR 2021/22 overview (uncertainty complex) (hdr.undp.org); app notes 3:16 sub 6

=== NOTES 3:19 | Environmental Degradation As A Development Challenge; Green Accounting and Green Gdp
## SUB 0 | Resource Depletion and Causes of Environmental Degradation
[19:0:0] - **Resource depletion** = use faster than regeneration; **overuse of natural resources → resource depletion** (not conservation or sustainability).
[19:0:1] - Best illustration of depletion with long-term impact: **overfishing in a coastal area** harming the whole marine ecosystem and livelihoods. A forest managed **sustainably for timber while keeping biodiversity** is NOT depletion.
[19:0:2] TABLE head: Resource | Cause of depletion
  [19:0:2.0] Groundwater | Deep bore tubewells for irrigation
  [19:0:2.1] Topsoil | Brick production
  [19:0:2.2] Biodiversity habitats | Expansion of agriculture
[19:0:3] - **Causes vs conservation practices:**
[19:0:4] TABLE head: Human-driven causes | NOT causes (conservation)
  [19:0:4.0] Deforestation (primary cause) | Sustainable agriculture
  [19:0:4.1] Chemical-intensive farming | Renewable energy production
  [19:0:4.2] Groundwater over-extraction, dams | Wildlife conservation
  [19:0:4.3] Road and urban expansion, industrial emissions, overfishing | –
[19:0:5] - **Deforestation** is driven by urbanisation, agricultural expansion and timber extraction, and **reduces** biodiversity – "more biodiversity" is false.
[19:0:6] - India's **two threats**: **poverty-induced degradation** + **pollution from affluence** and a rapidly growing industrial sector.
[19:0:7] - Green Revolution: HYV monoculture and chemical fertilisers cut soil fertility; **HYV irrigation needs** depleted the water table. **Urea ≈ 56%** of fertiliser use; low-carbon alternative **green urea** (green hydrogen + captured CO2).
[19:0:8] - India's low EPI score: **urban particulate pollution and coal-based power**.
[19:0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 03 p.53 (two threats); NCERT Indian Economic Development Class 11 ch.9; app notes 3:19 sub 1-2
[19:0:10] - **Industry** (factories, thermal power, mining) is the **largest contributor** to environmental pollution overall; transport is a major urban air polluter but smaller overall.
## SUB 1 | Functions of the Environment, Absorptive and Carrying Capacity
[19:1:0] TABLE head: Function | Meaning
  [19:1:0.0] Supplies resources (first function) | Both renewable (trees, fish – replaced naturally or inexhaustible) and non-renewable (fossil fuels – exhausted with use)
  [19:1:0.1] Assimilates waste | Absorbs and neutralises wastes
  [19:1:0.2] Sustains life | Genetic and biodiversity
  [19:1:0.3] Aesthetic services | Scenery and amenity
[19:1:1] - **Renewable resources** = those that **can be replaced naturally over time or are inexhaustible** – used without the resource becoming depleted.
[19:1:2] - **Absorptive capacity** = ability of the environment to absorb degradation = the **maximum amount of pollutants an ecosystem can process and neutralise without harmful effects**.
[19:1:3] - **Carrying capacity** (NCERT): the environment works without interruption as long as **resource extraction is not above the rate of regeneration** and **wastes are within the assimilating capacity** of the environment.
[19:1:4] - **Crisis**: when **extraction > regeneration** and waste > absorptive capacity, the environment **cannot sustain life** – "we are today at the threshold of environmental crisis".
[19:1:5] - **Recycling** is NOT a function of the environment; "supplies renewable resources only" is wrong.
[19:1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 03 p.52 (functions, renewable resources, absorptive capacity); NCERT Indian Economic Development Class 11 ch.9; app notes 3:19 sub 0
## SUB 2 | Green Economy, Green GDP and Green Accounting
[19:2:0] - **Green economy** (UNEP) = **low-carbon, resource-efficient and socially inclusive**; reduces environmental risks; focuses on **fostering green industries and creating green jobs**. **Green jobs** preserve or restore environmental quality.
[19:2:1] - **Green GDP** = conventional GDP with the **environmental consequences** of growth factored in = GDP **net of natural-resource depletion and degradation**. The term **Green GNP** emphasises **sustainable development** (not mere growth of GNP or per capita income).
[19:2:2] - **Uttarakhand** = first State to adopt green GDP, as **Gross Environment Product (GEP)**.
