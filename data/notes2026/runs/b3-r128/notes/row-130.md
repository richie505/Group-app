- **Budget 2017-18:** debt servicing (interest + repayments) ≈ **14.5%** of expenditure. **Budget 2023-24:** interest = **14% of revenue receipts** (pension 10%).
- Interest = **charged item (not voted)**; fixed, non-developmental; more than doubled since 2017-18. Sustainability signs: **debt-GSDP** and **interest/revenue receipts**; **golden rule** – borrow only for capital assets. AP runs a **primary deficit** → borrowing also funds **current spending**.
# Benchmarks and bifurcation
- **N.K. Singh Committee (2017):** general government debt **60%** = **Centre 40% + States 20%**; **FRBM (amended 2018)**: Central 40% by **31 March 2025**. **16th FC:** States' FD **3% of GSDP** (Union 3.5%); **Net Borrowing Ceiling** 3% of GSDP.
- Comparators (2023-24): **AP 34.6%**, Punjab 42.9%, West Bengal 38.3%, Rajasthan 37.9%; all States 29.2%.
- **APRA 2014 Section 54:** Public Debt and Public Account liabilities **split by population ratio**; loans re-lent for a project go to the State where it lies.
- **Centre (2016-17 RE):** outstanding liabilities about **46.7% of GDP** (APPSC key).
# Off-budget borrowings and guarantees
- Off-budget = loans by State PSUs/corporations **repaid from the State budget**, outside reported debt, used to **get around FRBM caps**; **₹1.28 lakh cr, 9.7% of GSDP (Mar 2023, CAG 2024)**; ₹1,40,470 cr (31 Mar 2024).
- **APSDC** escrowed **liquor revenue** to borrow; AP High Court questioned tax revenue going to APSDC instead of the **Consolidated Fund**. Centre adjusts them against **NBC** (e.g. Kerala's **KIIFB**); **16th FC**: bring them **on budget**; they **obscure true debt** and may hurt **sovereign ratings**.
- **Guarantees** = **contingent liabilities**, outside outstanding debt, due only **when invoked**: **₹1,77,328 cr (10% of GSDP)** in 2026-27; ₹1,54,797 cr (11%) in 2023-24; exposure ₹3.11 lakh cr (Dec 2025).
| Fund | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Consolidated Sinking Fund** | RBI-run; **amortise liabilities**; invested in GoI securities |
| **Guarantee Redemption Fund** | RBI-run; meet **invoked guarantees** |

=== Revenue Receipts and Own Tax Revenue
- **Revenue receipts** = income the government **need not pay back** = **own tax + own non-tax + share in central taxes + grants-in-aid from the Centre**. Corporation tax = tax revenue; interest, dividends, fees = non-tax; **market borrowings = capital receipt**; pensions = revenue expenditure. Centre's gross tax order (2009 question): customs > corporation tax > income tax > Union excise.

| Own non-tax | 5,417 | 7,432 | 10,576 | – |

| Grants-in-aid | 36,149 | 34,702 | 30,334 | 32,458 |

- **2018-19 revenue receipts: ₹1,57,599 cr** (not to be confused with ₹1,57,768 cr of 2022-23).
- **Grants-in-aid share:** **23.6%** of revenue receipts in **2016-17**; 15% in 2024-25 BE. CAG 2015-16: own tax ≈ 45%.
- **2024-25 RE:** ₹1,91,142 cr = own tax ₹94,967 + non-tax ₹7,018 + Centre ₹89,157. Own tax ~**half**, Centre **45–47%**, non-tax **~4%**; order: **Own Tax > Central Transfers > Own Non-Tax**. **2026-27:** own resources **59%** + central transfers **41%**.

=== Deficits and Expenditure Pattern
- **2014-15** (first post-bifurcation year): **revenue, fiscal and primary deficits all increased**. **2016-17 BE**: AP had **revenue, fiscal and primary deficits**. Revenue grew about **16%** in 2015-16 (APPSC key).
- 2023-24: RD ₹38,683 cr, FD ₹62,720 cr; 2024-25 RE: RD ₹48,311 cr, FD ₹73,362 cr.
- Revenue expenditure creates no asset (salaries, pensions, interest, **welfare/DBT**); capital expenditure creates assets or cuts liabilities. Welfare = revenue expenditure → widens the revenue deficit (ES 2025-26: conditional transfers or sunset clauses).
| Year | Revenue exp (₹ cr) | Capital outlay (₹ cr) | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014-15 | – | – | capex **8.95%** of total expenditure (APPSC key) |
| 2022-23 | 2,01,256 | 7,244 | ~3.4% |
| 2023-24 | 2,12,450 | 23,331 | ~9.9% |
| 2024-25 RE | 2,24,343 | 24,072 | ~9.7% |
| 2025-26 RE | 2,38,022 | 33,135 | ~11.9% |
| 2026-27 BE | 2,56,143 | 48,698 | ~15.7% |
- **2016-21:** AP capex **below 15%** of total expenditure; capex reflects the **commitment to infrastructure creation**. 2024-25 actual ₹16,141 cr vs BE ₹32,713 cr (~51% short). All States 2014-24: capex 13–20%. Capital outlay mainly **irrigation, roads, water supply, urban development**.

- **AP Guaranteed Pension System Act, 2023:** **50% of last basic pay**; **12th PRC** set up.

=== Own Non-tax Revenue and Minerals
- Non-tax = receipts not raised by taxing the public: **interest receipts, dividends and profits of PSUs, fees, fines, user charges, royalties** (GST is a tax, not non-tax). AP's 2023-24 BE non-tax: **₹15,400 cr**. **User charges** = a fiscal consolidation tool; State PSU profits/dividends are in "Others" (₹2,680 cr, 2024-25 RE).
| Head (₹ cr) | 2022-23 | 2023-24 | 2024-25 RE |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mines and Minerals** | 3,024 | 3,060 | 3,518 |
| Medical and Health | 177 | 211 | 313 |
| Interest receipts | 9 | 21 | 27 |
| Total non-tax | 5,417 | 7,432 | 7,018 |
- **Mines and Minerals = largest non-tax source every year** (2015-16, 2016-17 RE PYQs; **50% in 2024-25 RE**; 37% in 2025-26 RE, ahead of Medical and Health 34%); forests and interest receipts among the smallest.
- AP = "**Ratna Garbha**": barytes (**Mangampet, YSR Kadapa**), beach sands, limestone (Kadapa–Kurnool), dolomite, **mica (Nellore)**, **uranium**, iron ore, gold, diamonds, granite (**platinum not listed**). AP's share in India's mineral output ~**6%** (2016-17, APPSC key).
- Mining revenue **₹4,756 cr (2022-23)**. **Royalty** = **ad valorem** on **Average Sale Price** under the **MMDR Act, 1957** (⚠ APPSC key 2017 gave 1948). Ad valorem = % of value; specific = fixed per unit.
- **Minor minerals:** **sand** is a minor mineral; **States** frame the rules for granting minor-mineral concessions (MMDR s.15) and to **prevent illegal mining** (s.23C).
- **MADA v. SAIL (2024):** **royalty is not a tax**; States may tax mineral-bearing land; auction revenue goes to **States**. **DMF** = non-profit trust, **Section 9B** (MMDR Amendment 2015); **Section 9D** called unfair to mineral-rich States. MMDR amended 2023; India in **Minerals Security Partnership**; not rich in all **30 critical minerals**.
- **Free Sand Policy (8 July 2024):** buyers pay only operation cost, seigniorage and taxes.

=== Budget Basics, CFMS and Reform-linked Borrowing
| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| 112 | Annual Financial Statement of the **Union** |
| **202** | Annual Financial Statement of a **State** |
| 110 / 114 / 116 | Money Bill / Appropriation Bills / Vote on account |
| 246A / 269A / 279A | GST power / IGST / GST Council (three-fourths majority) |
| 270 / 275 / **280** | distribution of taxes / grants / **Finance Commission** |
| 293(3) | State needs Centre's consent to borrow while it owes the Centre |
- **101st Amendment, 2016**; GST from **1 July 2017**; GST compensation ended **June 2022**. GST introduction was first announced in the **Union Budget 2006-07**.
- Union model: **Gender Budget = Statement 13 (since 2005-06)**; **Child Budget = Statement 12 (since 2009-10)**.
- **Socio-Economic Survey**: tabled before the budget speech; **not a constitutional duty**; dropped in the 2026 Budget session.
- **CFMS (2018):** Comprehensive Financial Management System centralised treasury and payments online; criticised for **hurting civic bodies' autonomy**.
- **Reform-linked borrowing (2020-21):** four reforms – **One Nation One Ration Card, ease of doing business, urban local body reforms, power sector**; AP got ₹2,525 cr for ONORC (Sep 2020); **AP and MP first to complete three of four**; Feb 2021: ₹1,515 cr for **power-sector reform** (DBT of farm power, Srikakulam) – 2nd after MP.
