## SUB 0 | Joint Sitting of the Two Houses – Article 108
[0:0] # When it is held
[0:1] - **Article 108** = joint sitting; **Art. 109** = Money Bill procedure; **Art. 110** = definition of a Money Bill; **Art. 118(4)** = Speaker presides. "Art. 109 provides for the joint sitting" is **false**.
[0:2] - **Deadlock grounds** (after one House passes a Bill and sends it to the other): (1) the other House **rejects** it; (2) the Houses **finally disagree on amendments**; (3) more than **six months** pass without the other House passing it (periods of prorogation or adjournment for more than **4 consecutive days** are not counted).
[0:3] - "Both Houses agree to a joint sitting for speedy legislation" is **not** a ground – a joint sitting is only a deadlock-breaking device.
[0:4] - **Applies to:** **ordinary Bills** and **Financial Bills (I and II)**. **Not to:** **Money Bills** (the Lok Sabha has **overriding powers**, so no deadlock can arise) and **Constitution Amendment Bills** (each House must pass them separately by special majority).
[0:5] - A joint sitting is **not** used to elect the President or Vice-President.
[0:6] # Procedure
[0:7] TABLE head: Point | Fact
  [0:7.0] Who summons | President – he "notifies his intention" to summon the Houses (Art. 108(1)); not the Speaker, PM or Chairman
  [0:7.1] Who presides | Speaker of the Lok Sabha (Art. 118(4)) → Deputy Speaker → Deputy Chairman of RS → person chosen by members present
  [0:7.2] Chairman of RS (Vice-President) | Never presides – he is not a member of either House
  [0:7.3] Quorum | One-tenth of total members of both Houses
  [0:7.4] Majority | Simple majority of members of both Houses present and voting (Art. 108(4))
  [0:7.5] Rules followed | Rules of Procedure of the Lok Sabha
  [0:7.6] New amendments | Only those causing final disagreement or made necessary by delay
[0:8] - After the President notifies his intention, **neither House can proceed further** with the Bill.
[0:9] - If the President **does not summon** the joint sitting, the **Bill comes to an end** (APPSC G2).
[0:10] - Bill already **lapsed** on dissolution → no joint sitting. But if the **Lok Sabha is dissolved after** the President has notified the joint sitting, the Bill does not lapse and the **joint sitting is still held**.
[0:11] - The Lok Sabha, with greater numbers, normally wins at a joint sitting.
[0:12] # Joint sittings held – only three
[0:13] TABLE head: Year | Bill
  [0:13.0] 1961 (first) | Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1959/60
  [0:13.1] 1978 | Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1977
  [0:13.2] 2002 (last) | Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA)

## SUB 1 | Money Bill – Articles 109 and 110
[1:0] # Definition – Art. 110(1)
[1:1] - A Bill is a Money Bill if it contains **only** provisions on: (a) imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any **tax**; (b) regulation of **borrowing** or the **giving of any guarantee** by the Government of India; (c) **custody** of the Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund, payments into or withdrawals from them; (d) **appropriation** out of the CFI; (e) declaring expenditure **charged** on the CFI; (f) receipt, custody or issue of money of the CFI or Public Account, or the **audit of accounts of the Union or of a State**; (g) incidental matters.
[1:2] - **Not** Money Bills merely because they provide for (Art. 110(2)): **fines/penalties**, **fees for licences or services**, **taxes by local bodies** for local purposes.
[1:3] - **Speaker** decides finally whether a Bill is a Money Bill and **certifies** it (Art. 110(3)); the decision cannot be questioned in any court, in either House or by the President.
[1:4] # Procedure – Art. 109
[1:5] - Introduced **only in the Lok Sabha**, **only by a minister**, **only on the President's recommendation** (not the PM's). Never in the Rajya Sabha or a joint sitting.
[1:6] - **Rajya Sabha**: **cannot amend or reject** it; can only make **recommendations** and must return it within **14 days** (two weeks) – the maximum it can delay it.
[1:7] - **Lok Sabha may accept or reject** all or any of the RS recommendations – it is **not bound** to accept them, and no 14-day limit applies to the LS.
[1:8] - RS amends it substantially → the LS **may still proceed**, accepting or not accepting the recommendations. Not returned within 14 days → **deemed passed by both Houses** in the form passed by the LS, and sent to the President.
[1:9] - So **RS approval is not necessary** to pass a Money Bill; RS "has no power" over it beyond delay of 14 days.
[1:10] - **President**: may **give or withhold assent** but **cannot return** a Money Bill for reconsideration (normally assents, as it came on his recommendation).
[1:11] - **No joint sitting** for a Money Bill.
[1:12] - **Aadhaar Bill, 2016** was introduced and passed as a **Money Bill**; upheld in **K.S. Puttaswamy (2018)**; the Money Bill question went to a 7-judge bench in **Rojer Mathew (2019)**.
[1:13] - **Appropriation Bill** is a Money Bill (Art. 114).
[1:14] # Rajya Sabha in financial matters
[1:15] TABLE head: Rajya Sabha can | Rajya Sabha cannot
  [1:15.0] Discuss the Annual Financial Statement (budget) | Vote on the Demands for Grants (only LS votes, Art. 113)
  [1:15.1] Recommend changes to a Money Bill within 14 days | Amend or reject a Money Bill
  [1:15.2] Amend or reject Financial Bills I and II | Have a Money Bill or the Annual Financial Statement introduced first in it

## SUB 2 | Financial Bills and the President's Prior Recommendation
[2:0] # Three kinds of financial bills (Art. 117)
[2:1] TABLE head: Feature | Money Bill (Art. 110) | Financial Bill I (Art. 117(1)) | Financial Bill II (Art. 117(3))
  [2:1.0] Content | Only Art. 110 matters | Art. 110 matters + general legislation | Expenditure from CFI, no Art. 110 matter
  [2:1.1] Where introduced | LS only | LS only | Either House
  [2:1.2] President's recommendation | At introduction | At introduction | Only before consideration
  [2:1.3] Rajya Sabha | Recommends in 14 days | Can amend/reject | Can amend/reject
  [2:1.4] Joint sitting | No | Yes | Yes
  [2:1.5] President can return | No | Yes | Yes
[2:2] - **Every Money Bill is a Financial Bill, but not every Financial Bill is a Money Bill.**
[2:3] - **Article 117** = "special provisions as to Financial Bills"; Art. 117(1): no Bill **imposing a tax** can be introduced except on the **President's recommendation**, and not in the RS. An amendment **reducing or abolishing** a tax needs no recommendation.
[2:4] - A Bill that merely **involves expenditure** (no Art. 110 matter) can be **initiated in either House**.
[2:5] - **Finance Bill** = gives effect to the Budget's tax proposals, i.e. how the government collects revenue for the year; usually certified as a Money Bill – so exam keys treat "Finance Bill" as a Money Bill (14 days; President must sign, cannot return).
[2:6] # Bills needing the President's prior recommendation/sanction
[2:7] TABLE head: Bill | Article
  [2:7.0] Money Bill; Financial Bill I | Arts. 109, 117(1)
  [2:7.1] New State, alteration of areas, boundaries, names | Art. 3 (either House; simple majority; not an Art. 368 amendment)
  [2:7.2] Bill affecting taxation in which States are interested | Art. 274
  [2:7.3] State Bill restricting inter-State trade | Art. 304(b)
[2:8] - **Ordinary Bills** need **no** prior recommendation (except the listed cases); nor do Constitution Amendment Bills.
[2:9] - A Bill for a **new State** can be introduced in **either House** – "must be first introduced in the Rajya Sabha" is false.

## SUB 3 | Ordinary Bills – Kinds, Stages and the President's Assent
[3:0] # Public vs Private Bills
[3:1] - All legislative proposals come before Parliament as **Bills**; a Bill becomes an **Act** only after the **President's assent** (Art. 111).
[3:2] TABLE head: Public (Government) Bill | Private Member's Bill
  [3:2.0] Introduced by a minister (of either House) | By any MP who is not a minister (not necessarily nominated)
  [3:2.1] Reflects government policy | Does not reflect government policy
  [3:2.2] 7 days' notice | 1 month's notice
  [3:2.3] Drafted by the department with the Law Department | Drafted by the member
  [3:2.4] Rejection = loss of confidence in government | Rejection has no such implication
[3:3] - Both are governed by the **same general procedure** and pass through the **same stages**.
[3:4] - Only **14** Private Members' Bills have become law, the last in **1970** – none "recently for the first time".
[3:5] - **9 Dec 2022:** CPI(M) MP **Bikash Ranjan Bhattacharyya** introduced the private member's **National Judicial Commission Bill, 2022** in the Rajya Sabha.
[3:6] # Stages – three readings in each House
[3:7] TABLE head: Stage | What happens
  [3:7.0] First reading | Leave to introduce; Bill introduced and published in the Gazette
  [3:7.1] Second reading | General discussion, committee stage, clause-by-clause consideration with amendments
  [3:7.2] Third reading | Bill accepted or rejected as a whole – the formal vote/division
  [3:7.3] Second House | Same three readings; may pass, amend, reject, or keep pending (6 months → deadlock)
  [3:7.4] Assent | President: assent, withhold, or return once for reconsideration (suspensive veto)
[3:8] - An ordinary ("legislative") Bill can be introduced in **either House**.
[3:9] - **Defamation Bill, 1988**: passed by the Lok Sabha but **withdrawn** before the Rajya Sabha took it up, after press protests.
[3:10] # President's options on Bills (Art. 111)
[3:11] TABLE head: Bill | Assent | Withhold | Return
  [3:11.0] Ordinary / Financial I and II | Yes | Yes | Yes (once)
  [3:11.1] Money Bill | Yes | Yes | No
  [3:11.2] Constitution Amendment Bill | Must assent (24th Amendment, 1971) | No | No
[3:12] - President's powers also include summoning a joint sitting and **proroguing** the Houses and **dissolving the Lok Sabha** – he **cannot dissolve "Parliament"** (the Rajya Sabha is permanent).

## SUB 4 | Lapsing of Bills – Dissolution, Prorogation, Adjournment
[4:0] TABLE head: On dissolution of the Lok Sabha – lapses | Does not lapse
  [4:0.0] Bill pending in the LS (originating there or sent by the RS) | Bill pending in the RS, not passed by the LS
  [4:0.1] Bill passed by the LS, pending in the RS | Bill for which a joint sitting was notified before dissolution
  [4:0.2] – | Bill passed by both Houses, pending assent

[4:1] - **Prorogation** and **adjournment sine die** do **not** make Bills lapse – they remain alive and are taken up in the next session (only pending notices, motions, resolutions lapse on prorogation).
[4:2] - The **Rajya Sabha is never dissolved**.

## SUB 5 | Constitution Amendment Bills and Overriding a Court Ruling
[5:0] TABLE head: Feature | Ordinary Bill | Constitution Amendment Bill (Art. 368)
  [5:0.0] House | Either | Either
  [5:0.1] President's prior recommendation | Not needed | Not needed
  [5:0.2] Majority | Simple | Special – majority of total membership + 2/3 present and voting, each House separately
  [5:0.3] States | – | Ratification by half the States for federal provisions
  [5:0.4] Deadlock | Joint sitting | No joint sitting – Bill ends if one House rejects it
  [5:0.5] President | Assent / withhold / return | Must assent
[5:1] - Apart from the special majority, an amendment Bill goes through the **same stages as an ordinary Bill**.
[5:2] - When Government and Parliament dislike a Supreme Court decision they may **amend the Constitution or pass a law** that removes the basis of the judgment (both options) – not ignore it or change the judges.
[5:3] - **Latest:** Constitution (**129th Amendment**) Bill, 2024 (One Nation One Election, new **Art. 82A**) – with the JPC (as of 2026).
