=== NOTES 2:33 | Legislative Procedure – Ordinary, Money, Financial and Constitutional Amendment Bills; Joint Sitting
## SUB 0 | Bills – Basics
[33:0:0] - A **Bill** is proposed legislation; it becomes an **Act** only after the **President's assent** (**Article 111**).
[33:0:1] - **Article 79**: Parliament = **President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha**. The President is an integral part of Parliament but **not a member of either House**.
[33:0:2] - Ordinary Bill: in **either House**. Money Bill: **only in the Lok Sabha**. Creation of a new **All-India Service** starts with a **Rajya Sabha** resolution (Art. 312).
[33:0:3] # Articles at a Glance
[33:0:4] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [33:0:4.0] 107–108 | Ordinary Bills; 108 = Joint sitting
  [33:0:4.1] 109 | Special procedure for Money Bills
  [33:0:4.2] 110 | Definition of Money Bill
  [33:0:4.3] 111 | Assent to Bills
  [33:0:4.4] 112 | Annual Financial Statement (Budget)
  [33:0:4.5] 113 | Demands for Grants
  [33:0:4.6] 114 | Appropriation Bill
  [33:0:4.7] 116 | Vote on Account
  [33:0:4.8] 117 | Special provisions as to Financial Bills
  [33:0:4.9] 118(4) | Speaker presides over joint sitting
  [33:0:4.10] 119 | Regulation by law of financial business
  [33:0:4.11] 121 | Restriction on discussion in Parliament
  [33:0:4.12] 200 / 201 | Governor's / President's assent to State Bills
  [33:0:4.13] 368 | Constitutional amendment
## SUB 1 | Government Bill vs Private Member's Bill
[33:1:0] TABLE head: Feature | Government Bill | Private Member's Bill
  [33:1:0.0] Introduced by | A minister | Any MP who is not a minister (not necessarily nominated)
  [33:1:0.1] Notice | 7 days | 1 month
  [33:1:0.2] Drafted by | Concerned department with the Law Ministry | The member
  [33:1:0.3] Defeat | Signals loss of confidence | No bearing on the government
[33:1:1] - **Same procedure:** Government and Private Members' Bills pass through the **same readings and stages** in each House.
[33:1:2] - Lok Sabha: time is allotted by the **Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions**; it classifies them and examines private members' constitutional amendment bills before introduction.
[33:1:3] - Rajya Sabha has **no such committee**; its **Business Advisory Committee** allots time.
[33:1:4] Only **14** Private Members' Bills have become law; the last in **1970**.
## SUB 2 | Ordinary Bill – Stages and President's Assent
[33:2:0] # Three Readings in Each House
[33:2:1] TABLE head: Stage | What happens
  [33:2:1.0] First reading | Leave to introduce; introduction; title and objectives read; published in the Gazette
  [33:2:1.1] Second reading | General discussion → committee stage (select/joint committee) → consideration stage: clause-by-clause voting on amendments
  [33:2:1.2] Third reading | Bill accepted or rejected as a whole
[33:2:2] APPSC 2018: **General discussion** on a Bill takes place at the **Second Reading** – not the First or Third.
[33:2:3] # Second House
[33:2:4] - Same three stages. It may **pass, amend, reject** or **keep it pending for 6 months** → deadlock → **joint sitting**.
[33:2:5] - For an ordinary Bill the second House has **no time limit**; for a Money Bill the Rajya Sabha has **14 days**.
[33:2:6] # President's Options – Article 111
[33:2:7] TABLE head: Bill | Assent | Withhold | Return for reconsideration
  [33:2:7.0] Ordinary Bill | Yes | Yes | Yes (once)
  [33:2:7.1] Money Bill | Yes | Yes | No
  [33:2:7.2] Financial Bills I and II | Yes | Yes | Yes
  [33:2:7.3] Constitutional Amendment Bill | Must assent (24th CAA, 1971) | No | No
[33:2:8] - **Suspensive veto**: if a returned Bill is re-passed, with or without amendment, the President **must assent**.
[33:2:9] - **Pocket veto**: no time limit is fixed → possible. India has **no qualified veto**.
[33:2:10] - A Money Bill is normally assented to, because it is introduced on the President's recommendation.
## SUB 3 | Money Bill – Articles 109 and 110
[33:3:0] # Definition – Art. 110(1)
[33:3:1] A Bill is a Money Bill if it contains **only** these matters:
[33:3:2] - 1. Imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any **tax**.
[33:3:3] - 2. Regulation of **borrowing** by the Government of India.
[33:3:4] - 3. Custody of the **Consolidated Fund** or **Contingency Fund**, and money into or out of them.
[33:3:5] - 4. **Appropriation** of money out of the Consolidated Fund of India (CFI).
[33:3:6] - 5. Declaring any expenditure **charged** on the CFI.
[33:3:7] - 6. Receipt, custody or audit of money of the CFI or **Public Account**.
[33:3:8] - 7. Matters incidental to the above.
[33:3:9] # NOT Money Bills – Art. 110(2)
[33:3:10] - Bills only for **fines or penalties**.
[33:3:11] - **Fees for licences** or fees for services.
[33:3:12] - **Taxes by local bodies** for local purposes.
[33:3:13] # Procedure
[33:3:14] - Introduced **only in the Lok Sabha** (Art. 109(1)), **only by a minister**, **only on the President's recommendation** (APPSC 2019).
[33:3:15] - **Speaker certifies** it; the decision is **final** (Art. 110(3)); no need to consult anyone. The certificate is endorsed when sent to the Rajya Sabha (Art. 109) and to the President (Art. 111).
[33:3:16] - **Rajya Sabha**: only **recommends within 14 days**; **cannot amend or reject**. Lok Sabha is **not bound** to accept the recommendations (Art. 109(2)). Not returned in 14 days → **deemed passed** by both Houses.
[33:3:17] - **No joint sitting**; President **cannot return** it.
[33:3:18] - **Appropriation Bill** is a Money Bill: Rajya Sabha need not pass it; it is treated as passed if not returned in 14 days.
[33:3:19] - **AP angle:** the **AP Legislative Council** also only recommends on a Money Bill within **14 days**; it cannot reject or amend it finally. **Article 200**: a Governor **cannot return a Money Bill** for reconsideration.
[33:3:20] # Aadhaar Cases
[33:3:21] TABLE head: Case | Year | Holding
  [33:3:21.0] K.S. Puttaswamy | 2018 | Upheld the Aadhaar Act passed as a Money Bill
  [33:3:21.1] Rojer Mathew | 2019 | Money Bill question referred to a larger 7-judge bench
## SUB 4 | Money Bill vs Financial Bills vs Finance Bill
[33:4:0] # Article 117 – Three Categories of Financial Bills
[33:4:1] - **Money Bills** (Art. 110), **Financial Bill (I)** (Art. 117(1)), **Financial Bill (II)** (Art. 117(3)).
[33:4:2] TABLE head: Feature | Money Bill (Art. 110) | Financial Bill I (Art. 117(1)) | Financial Bill II (Art. 117(3))
  [33:4:2.0] Art. 110 matters | Only | Some, plus general matters | None – only spending from CFI
  [33:4:2.1] Speaker's certificate | Required | Not required | Not required
  [33:4:2.2] House of introduction | Lok Sabha only | Lok Sabha only | Either House
  [33:4:2.3] Who introduces | Minister only | Minister only | Minister or private member
  [33:4:2.4] President's recommendation | At introduction | At introduction | At consideration stage only
  [33:4:2.5] Rajya Sabha | Recommend only (14 days) | Amend or reject | Amend or reject
  [33:4:2.6] Joint sitting | Not allowed | Allowed | Allowed
  [33:4:2.7] President can return | No | Yes | Yes
[33:4:3] - In FB-I: an amendment **reducing or abolishing** a tax needs **no** recommendation; one **increasing** a tax needs it.
[33:4:4] - **Finance Bill**: gives effect to the **Budget's tax proposals**; usually certified as a Money Bill.
[33:4:5] - "No Bill imposing tax without the President's recommendation" – **Article 117**.
[33:4:6] Every Money Bill is a Financial Bill, but **not every Financial Bill is a Money Bill**.
[33:4:7] Not all financial bills start only in the Lok Sabha: **FB-II can start in either House**. A Bill only involving expenditure can start in either House.
## SUB 5 | Bills by House of Introduction and Prior Recommendation
[33:5:0] - **Cannot originate in the Rajya Sabha**: **Money Bills** and **Financial Bills (I)**.
[33:5:1] TABLE head: Bill | Article | President's prior recommendation
  [33:5:1.0] New States, alter boundaries/names | Art. 3 | Required (either House)
  [33:5:1.1] Money Bills and FB-I | Art. 117(1) / 110 | Required
  [33:5:1.2] FB-II / expenditure from CFI | Art. 117(3) | Before consideration
  [33:5:1.3] Taxation in which States are interested | Art. 274 | Required
[33:5:2] - State level: a Bill restricting **inter-state trade** needs the President's previous sanction before introduction in a State legislature.
## SUB 6 | Budget – Articles 112–117
[33:6:0] - **Annual Financial Statement** = **Article 112**; prepared by the **Budget Division** of the **Department of Economic Affairs**, Ministry of Finance.
[33:6:1] - **Rajya Sabha** can **discuss** the Annual Financial Statement but **cannot vote** on the **Demands for Grants** (Art. 113); voting is only in the Lok Sabha.
[33:6:2] - **Appropriation Bill** (Art. 114) – no withdrawal from CFI without it.
[33:6:3] - **Vote on Account** (Art. 116) – grant in advance for part of the year until the budget is passed.
[33:6:4] - **FIRST Union budget** of independent India: **26 November 1947**, by **R.K. Shanmukham Chetty**.
[33:6:5] # Cut Motions (only in Lok Sabha)
[33:6:6] TABLE head: Motion | Reduction | Purpose
  [33:6:6.0] Policy cut | Demand reduced to Re 1 | Disapproval of the policy
  [33:6:6.1] Economy cut | By a specified amount | Economy in spending
  [33:6:6.2] Token cut | By Rs 100 | A specific grievance
[33:6:7] - **Guillotine**: on the last day allotted, all remaining demands are put to vote **without discussion**.
## SUB 7 | Joint Sitting – Article 108
[33:7:0] # Grounds (deadlock after one House passes a Bill)
[33:7:1] - 1. The other House **rejects** it.
[33:7:2] - 2. Houses **finally disagree** on amendments.
[33:7:3] - 3. More than **six months** pass without it being passed (prorogation and adjournments of over **4 consecutive days** are excluded).
[33:7:4] # Procedure
[33:7:5] - **Summoned by the President**, who **"notifies his intention"** to summon it; then neither House proceeds further with the Bill.
[33:7:6] - If the President **does not summon** it → the **Bill comes to an end** (APPSC G2 2017).
[33:7:7] - **Presides**: Lok Sabha **Speaker** (Art. 118(4)) → Deputy Speaker → **Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman** → member chosen by those present.
[33:7:8] - **Quorum**: **one-tenth** of total membership; decided by **simple majority** of members of both Houses **present and voting** together.
[33:7:9] - **Excluded**: **Money Bills** and **Constitution Amendment Bills** (Art. 368). Applies to **ordinary bills**; **Financial Bills I and II** can go.
[33:7:10] The Vice-President / Rajya Sabha **Chairman does NOT preside** over a joint sitting.
[33:7:11] # Joint Sittings Held (only three)
[33:7:12] TABLE head: Year | Bill
  [33:7:12.0] 1961 | Dowry Prohibition Bill
  [33:7:12.1] 1978 | Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill
  [33:7:12.2] 2002 | Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA)
## SUB 8 | Constitutional Amendment Bill vs Ordinary Bill
[33:8:0] TABLE head: Feature | Ordinary Bill | Constitutional Amendment Bill (Art. 368)
  [33:8:0.0] House | Either House | Either House
  [33:8:0.1] Introduced by | Minister or private member | Minister or private member
  [33:8:0.2] President's prior recommendation | Not needed (except listed cases) | Not needed
  [33:8:0.3] Majority | Simple majority | Special majority – majority of total membership + two-thirds of members present and voting, in each House separately
  [33:8:0.4] State ratification | No | Half the States for federal provisions
  [33:8:0.5] Deadlock | Joint sitting possible | No joint sitting
  [33:8:0.6] President | Assent / withhold / return | Must give assent
[33:8:1] - **Latest:** **Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024** (One Nation One Election, new **Article 82A**) – still with the **JPC**, not passed; opposition argues the government lacks a two-thirds majority (Aug 2026).
## SUB 9 | Lapsing of Bills – Dissolution, Prorogation, Adjournment
[33:9:0] # On Dissolution of Lok Sabha
[33:9:1] TABLE head: Lapses | Does not lapse
  [33:9:1.0] Bill pending in Lok Sabha (originated there or sent by RS) | Bill pending in Rajya Sabha, not yet passed by LS
  [33:9:1.1] Bill passed by LS, pending in RS | Bill for which a joint sitting was notified before dissolution
  [33:9:1.2] Bill passed by RS, pending in LS | Bill passed by both Houses, pending assent
  [33:9:1.3] — | Bill returned by the President for reconsideration
[33:9:2] - **Rajya Sabha is never dissolved**.
[33:9:3] - **No joint sitting** can be summoned for a Bill that has **already lapsed**; a joint sitting **already summoned** is held as scheduled even after dissolution.
[33:9:4] APPSC G1 2024: "A Bill passed by LS but not taken up in RS does not lapse" = **FALSE**.
[33:9:5] # Effect of Other Events
[33:9:6] TABLE head: Event | Bills | Motions / notices
  [33:9:6.0] Adjournment / adjournment sine die | Do not lapse – taken up next session (APPSC 2025) | Continue
  [33:9:6.1] Prorogation | Do not lapse | Pending notices lapse, except notices for introducing Bills
  [33:9:6.2] Dissolution | As in the table above | All pending Lok Sabha business lapses
