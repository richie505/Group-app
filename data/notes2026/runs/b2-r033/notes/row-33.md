=== Joint Sitting of the Two Houses – Article 108
# When it is held
- **Article 108** = joint sitting; **Art. 109** = Money Bill procedure; **Art. 110** = definition of a Money Bill; **Art. 118(4)** = Speaker presides. "Art. 109 provides for the joint sitting" is **false**.
- **Deadlock grounds** (after one House passes a Bill and sends it to the other): (1) the other House **rejects** it; (2) the Houses **finally disagree on amendments**; (3) more than **six months** pass without the other House passing it (periods of prorogation or adjournment for more than **4 consecutive days** are not counted).
- "Both Houses agree to a joint sitting for speedy legislation" is **not** a ground – a joint sitting is only a deadlock-breaking device.
- **Applies to:** **ordinary Bills** and **Financial Bills (I and II)**. **Not to:** **Money Bills** (the Lok Sabha has **overriding powers**, so no deadlock can arise) and **Constitution Amendment Bills** (each House must pass them separately by special majority).
- A joint sitting is **not** used to elect the President or Vice-President.
# Procedure
| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| Who summons | **President** – he "**notifies his intention**" to summon the Houses (Art. 108(1)); not the Speaker, PM or Chairman |
| Who presides | **Speaker of the Lok Sabha** (Art. 118(4)) → Deputy Speaker → **Deputy Chairman of RS** → person chosen by members present |
| Chairman of RS (Vice-President) | **Never presides** – he is not a member of either House |
| Quorum | **One-tenth** of total members of both Houses |
| Majority | **Simple majority** of members of both Houses **present and voting** (Art. 108(4)) |
| Rules followed | Rules of Procedure of the **Lok Sabha** |
| New amendments | Only those causing final disagreement or made necessary by delay |
- After the President notifies his intention, **neither House can proceed further** with the Bill.
- If the President **does not summon** the joint sitting, the **Bill comes to an end** (APPSC G2).
- Bill already **lapsed** on dissolution → no joint sitting. But if the **Lok Sabha is dissolved after** the President has notified the joint sitting, the Bill does not lapse and the **joint sitting is still held**.
- The Lok Sabha, with greater numbers, normally wins at a joint sitting.

| **1961** (first) | **Dowry Prohibition Bill**, 1959/60 |
| **1978** | Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1977 |
| **2002** (last) | **Prevention of Terrorism Bill** (POTA) |

=== Money Bill – Articles 109 and 110
# Definition – Art. 110(1)
- A Bill is a Money Bill if it contains **only** provisions on: (a) imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any **tax**; (b) regulation of **borrowing** or the **giving of any guarantee** by the Government of India; (c) **custody** of the Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund, payments into or withdrawals from them; (d) **appropriation** out of the CFI; (e) declaring expenditure **charged** on the CFI; (f) receipt, custody or issue of money of the CFI or Public Account, or the **audit of accounts of the Union or of a State**; (g) incidental matters.
- **Not** Money Bills merely because they provide for (Art. 110(2)): **fines/penalties**, **fees for licences or services**, **taxes by local bodies** for local purposes.
- **Speaker** decides finally whether a Bill is a Money Bill and **certifies** it (Art. 110(3)); the decision cannot be questioned in any court, in either House or by the President.
# Procedure – Art. 109
- Introduced **only in the Lok Sabha**, **only by a minister**, **only on the President's recommendation** (not the PM's). Never in the Rajya Sabha or a joint sitting.
- **Rajya Sabha**: **cannot amend or reject** it; can only make **recommendations** and must return it within **14 days** (two weeks) – the maximum it can delay it.
- **Lok Sabha may accept or reject** all or any of the RS recommendations – it is **not bound** to accept them, and no 14-day limit applies to the LS.
- RS amends it substantially → the LS **may still proceed**, accepting or not accepting the recommendations. Not returned within 14 days → **deemed passed by both Houses** in the form passed by the LS, and sent to the President.
- So **RS approval is not necessary** to pass a Money Bill; RS "has no power" over it beyond delay of 14 days.
- **President**: may **give or withhold assent** but **cannot return** a Money Bill for reconsideration (normally assents, as it came on his recommendation).
- **No joint sitting** for a Money Bill.
- **Aadhaar Bill, 2016** was introduced and passed as a **Money Bill**; upheld in **K.S. Puttaswamy (2018)**; the Money Bill question went to a 7-judge bench in **Rojer Mathew (2019)**.
- **Appropriation Bill** is a Money Bill (Art. 114).
# Rajya Sabha in financial matters
| Rajya Sabha can | Rajya Sabha cannot |
|---|---|
| **Discuss** the Annual Financial Statement (budget) | **Vote** on the Demands for Grants (only LS votes, Art. 113) |
| Recommend changes to a Money Bill within 14 days | **Amend or reject** a Money Bill |
| Amend or reject Financial Bills I and II | Have a Money Bill or the **Annual Financial Statement** introduced first in it |

=== Financial Bills and the President's Prior Recommendation
# Three kinds of financial bills (Art. 117)
| Feature | Money Bill (Art. 110) | Financial Bill I (Art. 117(1)) | Financial Bill II (Art. 117(3)) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content | Only Art. 110 matters | Art. 110 matters + general legislation | Expenditure from CFI, no Art. 110 matter |
| Where introduced | LS only | LS only | **Either House** |
| President's recommendation | At introduction | At introduction | Only before **consideration** |
| Rajya Sabha | Recommends in 14 days | Can amend/reject | Can amend/reject |
| Joint sitting | No | Yes | Yes |
| President can return | No | Yes | Yes |
- **Every Money Bill is a Financial Bill, but not every Financial Bill is a Money Bill.**
- **Article 117** = "special provisions as to Financial Bills"; Art. 117(1): no Bill **imposing a tax** can be introduced except on the **President's recommendation**, and not in the RS. An amendment **reducing or abolishing** a tax needs no recommendation.
- A Bill that merely **involves expenditure** (no Art. 110 matter) can be **initiated in either House**.
- **Finance Bill** = gives effect to the Budget's tax proposals, i.e. how the government collects revenue for the year; usually certified as a Money Bill – so exam keys treat "Finance Bill" as a Money Bill (14 days; President must sign, cannot return).
# Bills needing the President's prior recommendation/sanction
| Bill | Article |
|---|---|
| Money Bill; Financial Bill I | Arts. 109, 117(1) |
| **New State, alteration of areas, boundaries, names** | **Art. 3** (either House; simple majority; not an Art. 368 amendment) |
| Bill **affecting taxation in which States are interested** | **Art. 274** |
| State Bill restricting inter-State trade | Art. 304(b) |
- **Ordinary Bills** need **no** prior recommendation (except the listed cases); nor do Constitution Amendment Bills.
- A Bill for a **new State** can be introduced in **either House** – "must be first introduced in the Rajya Sabha" is false.

=== Ordinary Bills – Kinds, Stages and the President's Assent
# Public vs Private Bills
- All legislative proposals come before Parliament as **Bills**; a Bill becomes an **Act** only after the **President's assent** (Art. 111).
| Public (Government) Bill | Private Member's Bill |
|---|---|
| Introduced by a **minister** (of **either House**) | By **any MP who is not a minister** (not necessarily nominated) |
| Reflects government policy | Does not reflect government policy |
| **7 days'** notice | **1 month's** notice |
| Drafted by the department with the **Law Department** | Drafted by the **member** |
| Rejection = loss of confidence in government | Rejection has no such implication |
- Both are governed by the **same general procedure** and pass through the **same stages**.
- Only **14** Private Members' Bills have become law, the last in **1970** – none "recently for the first time"; also: in the Lok Sabha the **Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions** (15 members, **Deputy Speaker** chairs) classifies such Bills and **allots time** (it also examines a private member's Constitution amendment Bill before introduction); the **Rajya Sabha has no such committee** – its **Business Advisory Committee** does this.
- **9 Dec 2022:** CPI(M) MP **Bikash Ranjan Bhattacharyya** introduced the private member's **National Judicial Commission Bill, 2022** in the Rajya Sabha.
# Stages – three readings in each House
| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| First reading | Leave to introduce; Bill introduced and **published in the Gazette** |
| **Second reading** | **General discussion**, committee stage, **clause-by-clause** consideration with amendments |
| **Third reading** | Bill accepted or rejected as a whole – the **formal vote/division** |
| Second House | Same three readings; may pass, amend, reject, or keep pending (6 months → deadlock) |
| Assent | President: assent, withhold, or **return once** for reconsideration (suspensive veto); no time limit is fixed for his decision → **pocket veto** possible (Zail Singh, Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986) |
- An ordinary ("legislative") Bill can be introduced in **either House**.
- **Defamation Bill, 1988**: passed by the Lok Sabha but **withdrawn** before the Rajya Sabha took it up, after press protests.
# President's options on Bills (Art. 111)
| Bill | Assent | Withhold | Return |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary / Financial I and II | Yes | Yes | Yes (once) |
| **Money Bill** | Yes | Yes | **No** |
| **Constitution Amendment Bill** | **Must assent** (24th Amendment, 1971) | No | No |
- President's powers also include summoning a joint sitting and **proroguing** the Houses and **dissolving the Lok Sabha** – he **cannot dissolve "Parliament"** (the Rajya Sabha is permanent).

=== Lapsing of Bills – Dissolution, Prorogation, Adjournment
| On dissolution of the Lok Sabha – lapses | Does not lapse |
|---|---|
| Bill **pending in the LS** (originating there or sent by the RS) | Bill **pending in the RS, not passed by the LS** |
| Bill **passed by the LS, pending in the RS** | Bill for which a **joint sitting was notified** before dissolution |
| – | Bill **passed by both Houses, pending assent** |
| – | Bill **returned by the President** for reconsideration (even if the LS is dissolved) |
- **Prorogation** and **adjournment sine die** do **not** make Bills lapse – they remain alive and are taken up in the next session (only pending notices, motions, resolutions lapse on prorogation).
- The **Rajya Sabha is never dissolved**.

=== Constitution Amendment Bills and Overriding a Court Ruling
| Feature | Ordinary Bill | Constitution Amendment Bill (Art. 368) |
|---|---|---|
| House | Either | Either |
| President's prior recommendation | Not needed | Not needed |
| Majority | Simple | Special – majority of total membership + 2/3 present and voting, **each House separately** |
| States | – | Ratification by **half the States** for federal provisions |
| Deadlock | Joint sitting | **No joint sitting** – Bill ends if one House rejects it |
| President | Assent / withhold / return | **Must assent** |
- Apart from the special majority, an amendment Bill goes through the **same stages as an ordinary Bill**.
- When Government and Parliament dislike a Supreme Court decision they may **amend the Constitution or pass a law** that removes the basis of the judgment (both options) – not ignore it or change the judges.
- **Latest:** Constitution (**129th Amendment**) Bill, 2024 (One Nation One Election, new **Art. 82A**) – with the JPC (as of 2026).
