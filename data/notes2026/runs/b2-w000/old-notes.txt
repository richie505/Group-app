=== NOTES 2:0 | Constitutional Evolution – Regulating Act 1773 to the Government of India Act 1935
## SUB 0 | Government of India Act, 1935 – Federation, Provincial Autonomy and the Constitution's Blueprint
[0:0:0] # Basis and size
[0:0:1] - **Based on:** the **Simon Commission** report, the **Round Table Conferences** (the third RTC) and the **White Paper (1933)**.
[0:0:2] - **Size:** **321 sections, 10 schedules** (14 parts) – the **longest Act** ever passed by the British Parliament up to then (APPSC JL 2001).
[0:0:3] - **Nehru's verdict:** a **"Charter of Slavery"** / "a machine with strong brakes but no engine" (UKPSC 2007).
[0:0:4] # Federation (never formed)
[0:0:5] - **All-India Federation** of British provinces + princely states – the first provision for a **federal polity** in India; the Act's own name for it = **"Federation of India"** (Part II, s.5).
[0:0:6] - **Never came into being:** joining was **optional** for the princes and the **rulers of princely states did not consent** to join (APPSC JL 2003).
[0:0:7] - **Real British intention** in bringing the princes in: to use them to **counter-balance the nationalist leaders** (UPSC 2002).
[0:0:8] - **Three lists:** **Federal (59 items), Provincial (54), Concurrent (36)** – first division of legislative powers between Centre and provinces; the basis of today's Centre–State scheme.
[0:0:9] - **Residuary powers → the Governor-General** (not the federal or provincial legislature, not the Governors) (UPSC 2018).
[0:0:10] - **Dyarchy at the Centre** proposed: **defence and foreign affairs** = reserved subjects under the **Governor-General** – **never implemented**.
[0:0:11] - **Bicameral Centre (proposed):** **Federal Assembly + Council of States**.
[0:0:12] # Provinces
[0:0:13] - **Provincial autonomy** = the **most important feature**; it **abolished provincial dyarchy** (of 1919) and gave responsible ministries from **April 1937**.
[0:0:14] - **Bicameral legislatures in 6 of 11 provinces:** **Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, United Provinces** – **not Punjab**.
[0:0:15] - **Governors kept** special powers: **veto**, ordinances and power to **legislate on their own** (Governor's Acts).
[0:0:16] - **Communal representation extended** (not abolished) to **depressed classes, women and labour**.
[0:0:17] - **Burma separated** from India; **Sindh** carved from **Bombay**, **Orissa** from **Bihar**.
[0:0:18] - **Franchise** extended to about **14%** of the population (about 3.5 crore voters); still **no adult franchise**.
[0:0:19] - **1937 elections:** Congress formed ministries in most provinces but **not in Punjab** (Unionist Party) or Bengal.
[0:0:20] - **⚠ Trap:** the 1935 Act did **NOT** bring dyarchy at **both** Centre and provinces – it **ended** provincial dyarchy and only **proposed** central dyarchy.
[0:0:21] # Institutions
[0:0:22] - **Federal Court** (Delhi) – inaugurated **1 October 1937**; first CJ **Sir Maurice Gwyer**; replaced by the Supreme Court in **1950**.
[0:0:23] - **RBI** set up on **1 April 1935** under the **RBI Act, 1934**; **Federal, Provincial and Joint PSCs** provided (no "All-India Services Commission").
[0:0:24] - **Council of India abolished**; the Secretary of State got a team of advisers instead.
[0:0:25] # Legacy in the Constitution
[0:0:26] - **Main source** of the Constitution: about **250 provisions / "nearly 75%"** drawn from it – federal scheme, office of Governor, judiciary, PSCs, emergency provisions, administrative details.
[0:0:27] - **Section 42 → Article 123** (President's ordinance power – a relic of the 1935 Act); **Section 63 → Article 175** (Governor's address).
[0:0:28] - **Centre–State relations** pattern traced to the **1935 Act** (not the US Constitution or the Nehru Report).
[0:0:29] > Statement angle (UPSC 2004): "abolition of diarchy in Governors' provinces" ✓; "Governors' power to veto and legislate" ✓; "abolition of communal representation" ✗ → **1 and 2 only**.
[0:0:30] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.97; Rocket Polity #1 p.2, #22 p.52-53; Indian_Polity Part 1 ch.1 (GoI Act 1935); app notes 2:0 sub 7
## SUB 1 | Charter Acts 1793–1853 – From Trading Company to Administrator
[0:1:0] TABLE head: Act | Key provisions
  [0:1:0.0] 1793 | Monopoly extended 20 years; override power extended to all later GGs and Presidency Governors; more control over Bombay and Madras; Board of Control salaries charged to Indian revenues; C-in-C not a council member
  [0:1:0.1] 1813 | Trade monopoly in India ended except tea and China trade; Christian missionaries allowed; Rs 1 lakh a year for education (first such provision); first asserted Crown sovereignty over Company territories
  [0:1:0.2] 1833 (Saint Helena Act) | GG of Bengal → Governor-General of India (William Bentinck – last GG of Bengal, first GG of India); Company's commercial functions ended – purely administrative; Law Member added (council to 4) – Macaulay; Law Commission under Macaulay; legislation centralised; Section 87: no Indian debarred by religion, birth, descent or colour; GG-in-Council to mitigate and abolish slavery
  [0:1:0.3] 1853 (last Charter Act) | Legislative and executive functions separated; 6 legislative members added – Indian (Central) Legislative Council, the first legislature / "mini-parliament"; open competition for civil services (Macaulay Committee 1854); territories held "in trust for Her Majesty", no fixed term
[0:1:1] # Charter Act, 1813
[0:1:2] - **Ended the trade monopoly** in India (MPPSC 2004; UKPSC 2016) – but kept **tea and China** trade.
[0:1:3] - **Christian missionary activity** permitted (Charles Grant, Wilberforce).
[0:1:4] - **English education chain:** **1813** grant of Rs 1 lakh → **General Committee of Public Instruction (1823)** → **Orientalist–Anglicist controversy** → Macaulay's Minute and **Bentinck's English Education Act (1835)** – all three led to English education (UPSC 2018).
[0:1:5] # Charter Act, 1833 – most asked
[0:1:6] - **Governor-General of Bengal designated Governor-General of India** – first GG of India **Bentinck** (1833) (asked 5 times: UPSC 2023, G2, SI 2012, UKPSC 2007).
[0:1:7] - **Final step towards centralisation**; GG-in-Council got **all law-making powers** for the whole of British India – often called the **beginning of constitution-making** under British rule.
[0:1:8] - **Law Member:** **Macaulay** (first) – the **Father of the Indian Penal Code** (Law Commission draft; IPC enacted 1860).
[0:1:9] - **Slavery:** the Act enjoined the government to take measures to **abolish slavery** and improve the condition of slaves (→ Slavery Act V of **1843**).
[0:1:10] - **⚠ Trap:** the 1833 Act did **NOT** provide that an **Indian** be the Law Member (UPSC 2003); first Law Member = Macaulay, not Impey.
[0:1:11] # Charter Act, 1853
[0:1:12] - **First official legislature:** the separate Legislative Council of 1853 (APPSC TWO 2012).
[0:1:13] - **Civil services:** appointments were by **patronage of the Directors till 1853**; the 1853 Act threw them open to **competition** (exam from **1855**).
[0:1:14] - **Order:** Regulating Act 1773 → Pitt's India Act 1784 → Charter Acts (1793 onwards) → Indian Councils Act 1861 (CDS 2000).
[0:1:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.88-91; Rocket Polity #1 p.2, #22 p.52, #23 p.55; Indian_Polity Part 1 ch.1 (Charter Act 1833); app notes 2:0 sub 2
[0:1:16] - **IPC dates:** drafted by Macaulay's First Law Commission (1837); **enacted 6 Oct 1860**; **came into force 1 Jan 1862**.
## SUB 2 | Government of India Act, 1919 – Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms
[0:2:0] # Basis
[0:2:1] - **Montagu's declaration, 20 August 1917:** "increasing association of Indians in every branch of administration" and gradual **responsible government**; **Montagu-Chelmsford Report 1918**.
[0:2:2] - **Edwin Montagu** = Secretary of State; **Lord Chelmsford** = Viceroy.
[0:2:3] # Provisions
[0:2:4] - **Dyarchy in the provinces** (Greek *di-arche*, double rule) – first time; provincial subjects split into **reserved** and **transferred** (MPPSC 2022; UPSC 2017).
[0:2:5] - **Reserved subjects:** administered by the **Governor** with his **Executive Council** (not answerable to the legislature).
[0:2:6] - **Transferred subjects:** Governor with **Indian ministers** responsible to the legislative council.
[0:2:7] - **Central and provincial subjects** separated – it **clearly defined the jurisdiction of the central and provincial governments** (UPSC 2015); **budgets separated** for the first time.
[0:2:8] - **Bicameral central legislature** first time: **Council of State** (upper, 60) + **Legislative Assembly** (lower, **145**: 104 elected + 41 nominated); **direct elections** on a limited franchise (property, tax, education) (asked 4 times).
[0:2:9] - Term **"responsible government"** first used; 3 of the 6 members of the Viceroy's Executive Council to be Indians.
[0:2:10] - **Communal representation extended** to **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans**.
[0:2:11] - **High Commissioner for India in London**; **Public Service Commission** provided – set up **1 October 1926** (Chairman + 4) (UPPSC 2008); **Public Accounts Committee 1921**.
[0:2:12] - **Civil services:** the ICS examination to be held **in India** too (from 1922); by **1941** Indians outnumbered Europeans in the ICS (UPRO 2021: both statements correct).
[0:2:13] - **Chamber of Princes**: **120** members, King George V's Proclamation **23 December 1919**; first Chancellor **Ganga Singh of Bikaner** (UPPSC 2023).
[0:2:14] - **Statutory commission after 10 years** → **Simon Commission** appointed **1927** (two years early), 7 British members under **Sir John Simon**; "Simon Go Back".
[0:2:15] - **Last election** of the Indian (central) legislature under this Act: **1945**.
[0:2:16] # Reserved vs Transferred
[0:2:17] TABLE head: Reserved (Governor + Executive Council) | Transferred (Governor + Indian Ministers)
  [0:2:17.0] Police, administration of justice, land revenue, law and order, irrigation, finance | Local self-government, education, public health, agriculture, public works
[0:2:18] > UPSC 2022: reserved = justice, land revenue, police → **1, 3 and 4**; local self-government was transferred.
[0:2:19] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.96; Rocket Polity #1 p.2, p.4-5, #22 p.52; Indian_Polity Part 1 ch.1 (GoI Act 1919); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Legislative_Assembly (145 members); app notes 2:0 sub 6
[0:2:20] - **First elections** to the reformed (Montford) councils: **November 1920** (boycotted by the Congress during Non-Cooperation); the new legislatures met in 1921.
## SUB 3 | Crown Rule – Government of India Act, 1858
[0:3:0] - **Rule transferred from the East India Company to the British Crown** – the main administrative consequence of the **Revolt of 1857**; British India under **direct Crown rule from 1858** (asked 8 times).
[0:3:1] - **"Act for the Good Government of India"**; Parliament passed it for a more responsible management of Indian affairs after the revolt exposed Company misrule – **not** because the Company was unwilling to rule (NDA 2006).
[0:3:2] - **Governor-General also styled Viceroy**: **Lord Canning** first; last Viceroy **Mountbatten**.
[0:3:3] - **Board of Control and Court of Directors abolished** – dual government ended (**⚠** "retained the Court of Directors" is the wrong statement).
[0:3:4] - **Secretary of State for India** created – a member of the British cabinet with a **15-member Council of India**; he **issued instructions to the Governor-General** and the Crown's powers were exercised through him.
[0:3:5] - **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1 Nov 1858)** – called the **"Magna Carta of Indian liberties"** (**⚠** not the Charter Act 1813).
[0:3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.92; Rocket Polity #1 p.2, #22 p.52; app notes 2:0 sub 3
## SUB 4 | Regulating Act 1773, Pitt's India Act 1784 and the Act of 1786
[0:4:0] # Background
[0:4:1] - **Act of 1767:** the Company to pay the British Government **£400,000 a year** (APPSC G2 2017).
[0:4:2] - **1772 crisis:** famine, falling revenue, **corruption and oppression by Company officials** (also in justice), loan of about £1 million sought → Parliament intervened; **Lord North** committee report.
[0:4:3] - **Before 1773:** the head of Fort William was the **Governor of Bengal** (1757–1772).
[0:4:4] # Regulating Act, 1773
[0:4:5] - **First** step by Parliament to **control** the Company – British Government's control increased; it **altered the Company's constitution at home** (Court of Directors, Court of Proprietors) and its structure in India.
[0:4:6] - **Governor of Bengal → Governor-General of Bengal**: **Warren Hastings** (first), with a **4-member Executive Council**.
[0:4:7] - **Bombay and Madras made subordinate** to Bengal (Calcutta).
[0:4:8] - **Supreme Court at Calcutta (Fort William)** – provided by the Act, set up by Charter in **1774**: **1 Chief Justice + 3 judges**; first CJ **Sir Elijah Impey** (asked 4 times).
[0:4:9] - **Banned private trade and bribes** by Company servants.
[0:4:10] - Directors to send letters on revenue, civil and military affairs to the British Government.
[0:4:11] - **⚠ Trap:** it did **NOT abolish the Court of Directors** – both it and the Board of Control went only in **1858** (APPSC 2019).
[0:4:12] - **Amending Act 1781 (Act of Settlement):** separated judicial and executive functions.
[0:4:13] # Pitt's India Act, 1784 and the Act of 1786
[0:4:14] - **Dual control / double government:** **Board of Control** (political, **6 members**) + **Court of Directors** (commercial).
[0:4:15] - Company territories called **"British possessions in India"**; GG's council cut to **3**.
[0:4:16] - **Act of 1786:** GG may **override his Council** and act as **Commander-in-Chief** – first used by **Lord Cornwallis**.
[0:4:17] > Match (UPSC 2002): 1813 – monopoly ended; Regulating Act – directors to submit correspondence; 1858 – power to the Crown; Pitt's Act – Board of Control → **A-2, B-4, C-3, D-1**.
[0:4:18] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.81-87; Rocket Polity #1 p.2, #22 p.52; Indian_Polity Part 1 ch.1 (Regulating Act); app notes 2:0 sub 0-1
## SUB 5 | Indian Councils Acts 1861 and 1892 – Ordinances, Portfolios, Indirect Election
[0:5:0] # Indian Councils Act, 1861
[0:5:1] - **Restored legislative powers of Bombay and Madras** (decentralisation); new councils in Bengal (1862), NWFP (1866), Punjab (1897).
[0:5:2] - **Beginning of representative institutions:** Canning nominated 3 Indians as non-officials – **Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao** (not Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, who criticised the absence of Indians as a lesson of 1857).
[0:5:3] - **Portfolio system** (begun by Canning, 1859) got statutory recognition – the Viceroy's authority over his council strengthened (UPSC 2002).
[0:5:4] - **Ordinance power** to the Viceroy in emergency – valid at most **six months** (UPPSC 2001).
[0:5:5] - **Indian High Courts Act 1861** → High Courts of **Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1862)**.
[0:5:6] # Indian Councils Act, 1892
[0:5:7] - **Indirect election** introduced (asked 4 times): nomination on the recommendation of **universities, district boards, municipalities, chambers of commerce**.
[0:5:8] - **Budget discussion** allowed (no vote); questions with **6 days' notice**, **no supplementary** questions.
[0:5:9] TABLE head: Feature | 1861 | 1892
  [0:5:9.0] Indians | Nominated non-officials | More non-officials
  [0:5:9.1] Election | None | Indirect begins
  [0:5:9.2] Budget | No discussion | Discussion, no vote
  [0:5:9.3] Other | Portfolio, ordinance | Right to ask questions
[0:5:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.93-94; Rocket Polity #22 p.52, #23 p.115; app notes 2:0 sub 4
## SUB 6 | Indian Councils Act, 1909 – Morley-Minto Reforms
[0:6:0] - **John Morley** = Secretary of State; **Lord Minto** = Viceroy – **"Father of Communal Electorate"**.
[0:6:1] - **Separate electorate for Muslims** = **communal representation** (asked 4 times: SI 2011; MPPSC 2017, 2019).
[0:6:2] - **Elected representation:** the Act that "provided for some elected representation" = **1909** (APPSC 2020); often called the **first attempt to introduce a representative and popular element** (SSC 2000).
[0:6:3] - **Indians associated with the executive councils** for the first time: **S.P. Sinha** = first Indian in the Viceroy's Executive Council, as **Law Member** – the first "closer association of Indians with the administration".
[0:6:4] - Central council: additional members raised to **60** (total **69**); **official majority** at the Centre, non-official majority in provinces; still **unicameral**.
[0:6:5] - **Supplementary questions**, resolutions and budget discussion allowed.
[0:6:6] - **⚠ Trap:** no dyarchy and no Chamber of Princes in 1909 (both 1919); indirect election was 1892, not 1909.
[0:6:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.95; Rocket Polity #1 p.2, #22 p.52; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Councils_Act_1909; app notes 2:0 sub 5
## SUB 7 | Allied Facts Asked with This Topic
[0:7:0] - **Local self-government:** **Lord Ripon** – Resolution of **1882**, the "Magna Carta of local self-government" (not Canning or Macaulay).
[0:7:1] - **Fundamental rights demand:** first in the **Constitution of India Bill, 1895**; the **Congress (Bombay special session, 1918)** demanded a **Declaration of Rights**; then Nehru Report 1928, Karachi 1931.
[0:7:2] - **Muslims and the Congress:** **Badruddin Tyabji** (Congress president 1887) appealed to Muslims to join the Congress; Sir Syed Ahmad Khan opposed it.
[0:7:3] - **Chronology:** INC formed **1885** → Simon Commission **1927** → Round Table Conferences **1930–32** → Quit India **1942**.

## SUB 2 | Composition and Method of Election
[1:2:0] TABLE head: Item | Figure
  [1:2:0.0] Total planned strength | 389
  [1:2:0.1] British India | 296 = 292 Governor's provinces + 4 Chief Commissioners' provinces (Delhi, Ajmer-Merwara, Coorg, British Baluchistan)
  [1:2:0.2] Princely States | 93
  [1:2:0.3] Ratio | 1 seat per 10 lakh (one million) population
  [1:2:0.4] After partition | 299 (provinces 229 + states 70) – members from Pakistan areas withdrew
  [1:2:0.5] 1946 result | Congress 208, Muslim League 73 (second largest), others/independents 15
  [1:2:0.6] Women members | 15
[1:2:1] - **Provincial seats** split among **Muslims, Sikhs and General** by population.
[1:2:2] - **Indirectly elected** by the members of the **Provincial Legislative Assemblies** (set up under the 1935 Act) – each community's MLAs voting by **proportional representation with single transferable vote** (the 292 provincial members).
[1:2:3] - **NOT** directly elected, **NOT** adult franchise (restricted franchise of the **1935 Act, Sixth Schedule** – tax, property, education), not nominated by the British, the GG, the Congress/League or Gandhi.
[1:2:4] - **Princely states' representatives nominated by the rulers**; so the body was **partly elected, partly nominated**.
[1:2:5] - **Minorities** (Christians, Anglo-Indians, Parsis…) were represented; it worked through many committees.
[1:2:6] - **Not members:** **Mahatma Gandhi** and **M.A. Jinnah** (all other leading personalities were in it).
[1:2:7] - **Ambedkar** – first elected from **Bengal** (eastern part, later East Pakistan); then from the **Bombay** seat vacated by **M.R. Jayakar** → he sat for **Bombay Presidency**.
[1:2:8] - **Provinces represented:** **Patel – Bombay**, **Rajendra Prasad – Bihar**, **H.C. Mookherjee – West Bengal**, **Durgabai Deshmukh – Madras**; Bihar also sent **A.N. Sinha**, **Jagjivan Ram**, Sachchidananda Sinha.
[1:2:9] - Princely states joined from the third session (**28 April 1947**: Baroda, Bikaner, Jaipur, Patiala, Rewa, Udaipur).

=== NOTES 2:2 | Sources of the Indian Constitution – Borrowed Features and Their Origin Countries
## SUB 0 | Ireland and South Africa – Directive Principles, President's Election, Rajya Sabha, Amendment
[2:0:0] # Ireland (Eire, Constitution of 1937)
[2:0:1] - **Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Arts. 36-51)** – borrowed from the **Irish Constitution** (Ireland itself took the idea from **Spain**) (asked 11 times: APPSC G2 2003, MPDO 1998, TWO 2012, SI 2019; UPPSC, MPPSC 2023).
[2:0:2] - **Method of election of the President** (indirect, proportional representation by single transferable vote) – Ireland.
[2:0:3] - **Nomination of members to the Rajya Sabha** (12 by the President) – Ireland.
[2:0:4] - **⚠ Trap:** DPSP is **not** from Weimar Germany, South Africa, Canada or Britain; Ambedkar called the DPSP a **"novel feature"** of the Constitution.
[2:0:5] # South Africa
[2:0:6] - **Procedure for amendment of the Constitution (Art. 368)** – South Africa (SSC 2016; APPSC FSO).
[2:0:7] - **Election of members of the Rajya Sabha** (by elected MLAs, proportional representation) – South Africa (APPSC 2019).
[2:0:8] - **⚠ Trap:** the amendment procedure is **not** from France, Ireland, Britain or the USA (APPSC Lecturers 2018: "Amendment – France" is the wrong statement).
[2:0:9] > RS pair: **election** of RS members = **South Africa**; **nomination** to the RS and the **President's election** = **Ireland**.
[2:0:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.154-155 (sources table); Rocket Polity #1 p.3; app notes 2:2 sub 5
## SUB 1 | USA – Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Preamble and the Judiciary
[2:1:0] TABLE head: Feature | Borrowed from
  [2:1:0.0] Fundamental Rights (Part III, Arts. 12-35) | USA (Bill of Rights)
  [2:1:0.1] Judicial review | USA
  [2:1:0.2] Independence of the judiciary | USA
  [2:1:0.3] Idea of the Preamble | USA
  [2:1:0.4] Post of Vice-President (ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha) | USA
  [2:1:0.5] Impeachment of the President | USA
  [2:1:0.6] Removal of Supreme Court and High Court judges | USA
  [2:1:0.7] Written Constitution | USA – the world's first written Constitution (1787), also the shortest (7 Articles)
[2:1:1] - **Fundamental Rights = USA** (asked 6 times: APPSC Degree Lecturers 2020; UPPSC, UPRO 2014, UDA 2010).
[2:1:2] - **Judicial review originated in the USA** – *Marbury v. Madison* (1803); the words "judicial review" appear in **neither** the US nor the Indian text; **Britain** (parliamentary sovereignty, no written constitution) has **no judicial review** of Acts of Parliament.
[2:1:3] - **Preamble idea = USA** (asked 5 times); only the **language** of the Preamble is from **Australia**; liberty, equality, fraternity = France.
[2:1:4] - Judicial review in India is narrower: Art. 21 uses **"procedure established by law"** (Japan), not the US **"due process of law"**.
[2:1:5] - **USA = presidential** form of government (India, Britain, Canada are parliamentary).
[2:1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.154-155, p.164; Rocket Polity #75 (Rule of Law, Part 2 p.85-86); app notes 2:2 sub 3
## SUB 2 | Britain – Parliamentary Features, Rule of Law, Single Citizenship
[2:2:0] TABLE head: Feature | Borrowed from
  [2:2:0.0] Parliamentary government, cabinet system | Britain
  [2:2:0.1] Single citizenship (USA has dual citizenship) | Britain
  [2:2:0.2] Bicameralism | Britain
  [2:2:0.3] Rule of Law, legislative (law-making) procedure | Britain
  [2:2:0.4] Office of Speaker and his role | Britain
  [2:2:0.5] Prerogative writs ("petitions") | Britain
  [2:2:0.6] Parliamentary privileges | Britain
  [2:2:0.7] Nominal head (President, like the Crown) | Britain
[2:2:1] - "Which feature is from Britain?" – **cabinet system**, **single citizenship**, **bicameralism**, **writs** (APPSC 2019, FRO; SSC 2019). **Independence of judiciary** is US, not British (APPSC Panchayat Secy 2017 → **1, 2, 3**).
[2:2:2] - **Britain has no single written constitution**, but the **concept of constitutional government** goes back to the **Magna Carta (1215)**; the first **written** constitution is the US one.
[2:2:3] - **Rule of Law** – A.V. **Dicey** (1885): supremacy of law, equality before law, and rights of the individual secured by courts; its main objective is to secure the **liberty of citizens** against arbitrary power. A **basic feature** (Kesavananda).
[2:2:4] - **Single citizenship** – one of the framers' crucial steps to **tackle regionalism** and build national unity.
[2:2:5] - **Contempt of court:** the power of courts to punish for contempt comes from **English common law** (British-era courts; now Arts. 129, 215).
[2:2:6] - **Integrated judiciary:** one hierarchy (SC → HCs → subordinate courts) enforces both Central and State laws, unlike the US dual courts; this **single system of courts was adopted from the GoI Act 1935** (Laxmikanth), so "borrowed from the British system" is the false reason; India's judicial system follows the British common-law pattern.

## SUB 4 | Government of India Act 1935 – the Largest Source, and the Length of the Constitution
[2:4:0] - **Single largest source:** federal scheme, **office of Governor**, **judiciary**, **Public Service Commissions**, **emergency provisions**, administrative details – "larger part" of the Constitution (APPSC JA 2012).
[2:4:1] - **Greatest / most profound influence** on the Constitution = **GoI Act 1935** (about 250 provisions), not the British Constitution (APPSC G1 2002; G1 PYQ – some answer keys gave "British Constitution").
[2:4:2] - **Federal system and distribution of powers** between Centre and States = the **1935 Act** scheme (UPSC 2012; APPSC G2).
[2:4:3] - **1935 lists:** Federal (**59**), Provincial (**54**), **Concurrent (36)** items (SI 2023) → today's **Union, State and Concurrent Lists**.
[2:4:4] - **Not** a 1935 constituent: a **written Constitution made by Indians** – accepted in principle only by the **August Offer, 1940** (UPPSC 2010).
[2:4:5] # Why the Constitution is the lengthiest
[2:4:6] - **Factors:** **vast geography and diversity**; **bulky 1935 Act**; a **single Constitution for Centre and States**; dominance of **legal luminaries**; detailed administrative and **social-economic** provisions; framers' wish to **safeguard the young democracy**.
[2:4:7] - **NOT a factor:** "adoption of the American model" (APPSC ATWO); **local bodies** were **not** in the original Constitution (Parts IX, IXA added in **1992**) (APPSC ASWO Mains).
[2:4:8] - **Original:** **395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules** → now about **470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules** (as of 2026).
[2:4:9] - **Criticisms:** **"bag of borrowings"** (sources), **"elephantine"** (length), **"lawyers' paradise"** (legal detail); reply – borrowed features were **adapted** to Indian conditions.
[2:4:10] - **Philosophical part** (FRs, DPSPs) from the USA and Ireland; **political part** (parliamentary system) from Britain; **structural part** from the 1935 Act.

=== NOTES 2:3 | Salient Features of the Indian Constitution
## SUB 0 | Democracy, Republic and Parliamentary Government
[3:0:0] # Democracy
[3:0:1] - **Word origin:** **Greek** – *demos* (people) + *kratos* (rule); not Latin, Roman or French (APPSC G2 Mains 2019; Town Planning).
[3:0:2] - **India = representative (indirect) democracy:** people elect representatives who govern (APPSC DL 2017; SSC CGL 2016).
[3:0:3] - **Direct democracy is NOT found in India** at the national level – no **referendum, initiative or recall** (APSLPRB 2019: "Direct democracy is found in India" = the incorrect statement).
[3:0:4] - **Tools of direct democracy:** **recall** = removing an elected representative before his term; **referendum** = people vote on a law/issue; **initiative** = people propose a law; **plebiscite** = people's verdict on a political question (APSLPRB Constable 2023: recall).
[3:0:5] - **Democracy needs people's participation:** without participation and co-operation democracy fails → **people should participate and co-operate with the government** (not compulsion, not a switch to the presidential system).
[3:0:6] - India is a **democratic country** and also has a **Constitution of its own** – both true, but having a constitution does not by itself make a country democratic (UPPSC 2017 A–R: both true, R not the explanation).
[3:0:7] - **Not authoritarian:** India has democratic government, rule of law and a government that follows the Constitution; **authoritarian government is NOT a feature** (UPPSC Mains 2011).
[3:0:8] # Republic
[3:0:9] - **Republic = no hereditary ruler; the head of State is elected** (directly or indirectly) for a fixed term (UPPSC 1997, 2017; Panchayat Secretary).
[3:0:10] - India's head of State, the **President, is elected indirectly** – the parliamentary system is **combined with republicanism** (a nominal elected head, real power with ministers) (RAS 2013 A–R: A true, R true and the correct explanation).
[3:0:11] - **"Republic" in the Preamble** – idea from the **French** Constitution (with liberty, equality, fraternity).
[3:0:12] - India is a **"Union of States"** (Art. 1), a **Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic with a parliamentary system**.
[3:0:13] - **Cardinal features of the Indian political system:** **democratic republic**, **parliamentary form of government**, **supreme power vests in the people**, and a **unified authority** (one Constitution, single citizenship, integrated judiciary) → **all four** (UPPSC 2009).
[3:0:14] # Parliamentary form of government
[3:0:15] - India chose the **British parliamentary system**, not the American **presidential** system → India is a **parliamentary democracy** (UPPSC 2005); **presidential government is NOT a feature** (UPPSC Mains 2015).
[3:0:16] - **Parliamentary system** = **co-operation and co-ordination** between legislature and executive; **presidential** = **separation of powers**.
[3:0:17] - **Features:** nominal (President) and real (Council of Ministers) executive; majority party rule; **collective responsibility of the Council of Ministers to the Lok Sabha** (Art. 75); ministers are **members of the legislature**; leadership of PM/CM; dissolution of the lower House. Adopted at both **Centre and States**.
[3:0:18] - **Why chosen:** already **familiar** (worked under the GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935); suits India's **vast size and diversity**; avoids **executive–legislature conflict** seen in the USA.
[3:0:19] - **Origins:** parliamentary form began in **Britain**; presidential form in the **USA**.
[3:0:20] # India vs the British model (UPSC 2021)
[3:0:21] TABLE head: Point | India | Britain
  [3:0:21.0] Parliament | Not sovereign – a creature of a written Constitution; its law-making power is limited (FRs, federalism, judicial review) | Sovereign – supreme in legislation
  [3:0:21.1] Validity of laws / amendments | Courts can strike them down; cases on interpreting the Constitution (incl. validity of an Amendment) go to a Constitution Bench of at least 5 judges (Art. 145(3)) | No judicial review of Acts of Parliament
  [3:0:21.2] Head of State | Elected (indirectly) – republic | Hereditary monarch
  [3:0:21.3] Citizenship | Single | Single
[3:0:22] > UPSC 2021: both statements correct → **Both 1 and 2** (UPSC official key C).

## SUB 1 | Written and Lengthiest Constitution – Parts, Schedules, Articles
[3:1:0] # Written and lengthiest
[3:1:1] - **A Constitution** = the document that lays down the **basic rules** for the people and the government (SSC 2018).
[3:1:2] - **Written constitution tradition began in the USA** – the **US Constitution (1787)** is the **first written** national constitution (and the shortest: 7 Articles); the **British** Constitution is **unwritten** (MPPSC 2015).
[3:1:3] - India's is the **lengthiest written constitution** of the world – longer than those of Canada, South Africa or Australia (APPSC HWO).
[3:1:4] - **Reasons for length:** vast size and **diversity**; influence of the bulky **GoI Act 1935**; **single Constitution for Centre and States**; dominance of **legal luminaries**; detailed **administrative provisions**; matters left to ordinary law elsewhere put in the Constitution.
[3:1:5] - **Original copies:** **two handwritten (calligraphed) originals – English and Hindi** – kept in helium/inert-gas cases in the **Parliament Library** (APPSC Inspector of Factories); English calligraphy by **Prem Behari Narain Raizada**, Hindi by **Vasant Krishan Vaidya**, decorated by Nandalal Bose's Santiniketan artists (members also signed a printed English version on 24 Jan 1950).

## SUB 2 | Constitutionalism and Supremacy of the Constitution
[3:2:0] # Constitutionalism
[3:2:1] - **Constitutionalism** = government whose powers are **limited** by a supreme Constitution; it **recognises the need to limit the powers of government** (APPSC G2 2011).
[3:2:2] - It is **not inherent in every constitution** – a country can have a constitution without constitutionalism (a dictator's constitution).
[3:2:3] - **Antithesis of constitutionalism = despotism** (absolute, unlimited rule); limited power and restraint of power are its features (APPSC G2 2011).
[3:2:4] - **Constitutional government** = one that places **effective restrictions on the authority of the State in the interest of individual liberty** – not restrictions on liberty in the interest of the State (UPSC 2014: **II only**).
[3:2:5] - **Limits in India:** Fundamental Rights (**Art. 13** – laws inconsistent with FRs are void), judicial review, federal division of powers, **basic structure** (**Kesavananda Bharati, 1973**).
[3:2:6] - Parliament's amending power is limited by the **basic structure doctrine**; basic-structure elements include **supremacy of the Constitution**, rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, limited amending power.
[3:2:7] # Supremacy of the Constitution
[3:2:8] - The **Constitution is supreme** in the Indian political system – not Parliament, not the Supreme Court, not religion (UPPSC Mains 2013).
[3:2:9] - **Governmental organs owe their origin to the Constitution** – the Union and States derive their powers from it (APPSC Asst. Social Welfare).
[3:2:10] - **Why the Constitution is above Parliament:** the **Constitution specifies how Parliament is formed and what its powers are** (not because it is older or its makers were more eminent) (APPSC HWO).
[3:2:11] # Judicial review
[3:2:12] - **Judicial review** = power of the SC/HCs to declare laws and executive orders **unconstitutional**; the phrase is **nowhere used in the Constitution**, but **Arts. 13, 32, 226** (and others) **explicitly confer** the power.
[3:2:13] - **Basic structure is NOT defined in the Constitution** – it was evolved by the Supreme Court (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973) (UPSC 2020, statement I wrong).
[3:2:14] - **Synthesis** of British **parliamentary sovereignty** and American **judicial supremacy**: courts can strike down laws; Parliament can amend most of the Constitution.
[3:2:15] - **Scope narrower than in the USA:** India has **"procedure established by law"** (Art. 21), the USA **"due process of law"**; courts test a law only **against the supreme written Constitution** (FR violation, lack of competence, repugnancy), not its wisdom → judicial review is **restricted because the Constitution is supreme**, not because the executive or legislature is supreme.
[3:2:16] > UPSC 2020: II (Constitution provides for judicial review) is correct on the text of Arts. 13, 32, 226 → **Only II**. (UPSC's official key said D, "Neither", on the view that the Constitution never uses the term "judicial review".)
[3:2:17] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams (full text) p.164 (synthesis), p.324-325 (judicial review, phrase not used, narrower than USA); Indian_Polity Part 1 ch.1 p.3-4 (constitutionalism, K.C. Wheare), ch.2; mrunal.org/2021/11/upsc-prelims-2020-answerkey-error-discrepancy.html (official key D); app notes 2:3 sub 4
[3:2:18] - **Nature:** the Constitution is the **fundamental (foundation) law** of the land – every other law derives its authority from it (not common, derived or divine law).
[3:2:19] - **What a constitution cannot do:** it sets basic rules, limits power, protects rights and expresses shared values – it **cannot ensure that the 'right' people come to power**.
## SUB 3 | Other Salient Features – Amendment, Emergency, Rights, Duties, Franchise
[3:3:0] # Blend of rigidity and flexibility
[3:3:1] TABLE head: Method | Examples
  [3:3:1.0] Simple majority of Parliament (outside Art. 368) | Admission of new States, change of State boundaries
  [3:3:1.1] Special majority of Parliament (Art. 368) | Most provisions (e.g. Fundamental Rights)
  [3:3:1.2] Special majority + ratification by half the State legislatures | Federal provisions
[3:3:2] - **Amending power: Article 368** (Part XX) (APPSC Social History 2019).
[3:3:3] - **First amendment: 1951** (1st Amendment Act – added the 9th Schedule).
[3:3:4] - Neither as rigid as the US nor as flexible as the British Constitution.
[3:3:5] # Rights, duties, franchise
[3:3:6] - **Fundamental Rights:** six categories (Part III); all suspendable in a National Emergency except **Arts. 20 and 21**; Right to Property moved out → now **Art. 300A**. Rights for individuals make it a **liberal** constitution (NDA 2010).
[3:3:7] - **DPSP** (Part IV) – Ambedkar's "novel feature".
[3:3:8] - **Fundamental Duties:** added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** on the **Swaran Singh Committee**'s recommendation – **10** duties; the **86th (2002)** added the 11th → **11** now (Part IVA, Art. 51A).
[3:3:9] - **Universal adult franchise** from the start (1950) at **21 years**; voting age **changed once – 21 → 18 by the 61st Amendment (1988, effective 1989)**; the same age for Lok Sabha and Assembly elections; no literacy or property test.
[3:3:10] - **Education:** moved from the State List to the **Concurrent List** by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**.
[3:3:11] # Emergency, bodies, tiers
[3:3:12] - **Three kinds of emergency:** **National (Art. 352)**, **State / President's Rule (Art. 356)**, **Financial (Art. 360)** – Part XVIII, Arts. 352-360 (APSLPRB SI 2017).
[3:3:13] - **Independent bodies:** ECI, CAG, UPSC, SPSCs.
[3:3:14] - **Integrated and independent judiciary:** one court system enforces **both Central and State laws**.
[3:3:15] - **Three-tier government:** **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992)** added the local tier.
[3:3:16] - **Kerala** was formed on **1 November 1956** (States Reorganisation Act, 1956) in place of the **Part B State of Travancore-Cochin** (APPSC MPDO 1998).
