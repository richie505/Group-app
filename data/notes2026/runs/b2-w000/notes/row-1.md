- **"First President" of the Assembly:** the first to preside was **Sachchidananda Sinha** (temporary); the elected (permanent) President was **Rajendra Prasad** – read which one the question wants.
- **Not the presiding officer:** **Nehru** (he moved the Objectives Resolution) and **Ambedkar** (he chaired only the Drafting Committee).
# Constitutional Adviser – B.N. Rau
- **Sir B.N. (Benegal Narsing) Rau** – **Constitutional / legal Adviser**; prepared the **first draft** in **October 1947**: **243 Articles, 13 Schedules**, placed before the Drafting Committee.
- Later a judge of the **International Court of Justice** (1952-53).
- **⚠ Not** Sachchidananda Sinha, K.M. Munshi, Alladi or M.C. Setalvad (Setalvad = first Attorney General).
# Drafting Committee
- Set up by a resolution of the Assembly on **29 August 1947**; **7 members**; sat **141 days**.
- **Chairman: Dr B.R. Ambedkar** – the **"architect" / "Father" of the Constitution**, "Modern Manu".
- Members: **N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar**, **Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar**, **K.M. Munshi**, **Mohammad Saadullah**, **B.L. Mitter** (replaced by **N. Madhava Rau**), **D.P. Khaitan** (died 1948; replaced by **T.T. Krishnamachari**).
- **NOT members:** **Nehru**, **Rajendra Prasad**, **J.B. Kripalani**, **Sardar Patel**, **Vijayalakshmi Pandit**, B.N. Rau (adviser, not member).
- **Preamble:** proposed before the Drafting Committee by **Jawaharlal Nehru** (it was based on his Objectives Resolution).
# Artwork of the original Constitution
- **Calligraphy (English):** **Prem Behari Narain Raizada** (flowing italic); Hindi copy: Vasant Krishan Vaidya.
- **Illustrations:** artists of **Shantiniketan** led by **Nandalal Bose**; **National Emblem** sketched by **Dinanath Bhargava** (under Bose); Preamble page illuminated by **Beohar Rammanohar Sinha**.

# Adoption vs commencement
- **Adopted, enacted and given to ourselves** by the **Constituent Assembly** on **26 November 1949** (not by the British Parliament, the GG or Parliament) – Preamble: "this **twenty-sixth day of November, 1949**".
- **In force on 26 Nov 1949:** **citizenship**, **elections**, provisional Parliament, temporary and transitional provisions (Arts. 5-9, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391-393) – **not** emergency provisions or appointment of judges.
- **Rest in force on 26 January 1950** – chosen because the Congress had observed **26 January 1930** as **Purna Swaraj (Independence) Day** (Lahore session resolution, **December 1929**, Nehru presiding) – ⚠ the resolution was not of "26 January 1929".
- **Anniversaries:** 26 Nov 2019 = **70th** anniversary of adoption; Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas) observed since **2015**.
> Statement angle (Raj PCS 2023): final session 24 Jan 1950 ✓ and Prasad declared elected President of India in it ✓ → **both correct**.

=== Composition and Method of Election
| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Total planned strength | **389** |
| British India | **296** = **292** Governor's provinces + **4** Chief Commissioners' provinces (Delhi, Ajmer-Merwara, Coorg, British Baluchistan) |
| Princely States | **93** |
| Ratio | **1 seat per 10 lakh** (one million) population |
| After partition | **299** (provinces 229 + states 70) – members from Pakistan areas withdrew |
| 1946 result | **Congress 208**, **Muslim League 73** (second largest), others/independents 15 |
| Women members | **15** |
- **Provincial seats** split among **Muslims, Sikhs and General** by population.
- **Indirectly elected** by the members of the **Provincial Legislative Assemblies** (set up under the 1935 Act) – each community's MLAs voting by **proportional representation with single transferable vote** (the 292 provincial members).
- **NOT** directly elected, **NOT** adult franchise (restricted franchise of the **1935 Act, Sixth Schedule** – tax, property, education), not nominated by the British, the GG, the Congress/League or Gandhi.
- **Princely states' representatives nominated by the rulers**; so the body was **partly elected, partly nominated**.
- **Minorities** (Christians, Anglo-Indians, Parsis…) were represented; it worked through many committees.
- **Not members:** **Mahatma Gandhi** and **M.A. Jinnah** (all other leading personalities were in it).
- **Ambedkar** – first elected from **Bengal** (eastern part, later East Pakistan); then from the **Bombay** seat vacated by **M.R. Jayakar** → he sat for **Bombay Presidency**.
- **Provinces represented:** **Patel – Bombay**, **Rajendra Prasad – Bihar**, **H.C. Mookherjee – West Bengal**, **Durgabai Deshmukh – Madras**; Bihar also sent **A.N. Sinha**, **Jagjivan Ram**, Sachchidananda Sinha.
- Princely states joined from the third session (**28 April 1947**: Baroda, Bikaner, Jaipur, Patiala, Rewa, Udaipur).

=== Demand for a Constituent Assembly and the Cabinet Mission
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 1895 | **Constitution of India Bill** – first explicit demand for fundamental rights |
| 1922 | Gandhi: India's destiny to be decided by Indians |
| 1931 | **Karachi Congress** – **Resolution on Fundamental Rights** and Economic Policy |
| **1934** | **M.N. Roy** first put forward the **idea**; **Swaraj Party** – first formal demand (**Ranchi**, May 1934); Congress adopted it (Patna, May 1934; CWC resolution June 1934) |
| 1938 | Nehru – CA elected on adult franchise |
| 1940 | **August Offer** – British accepted the principle |
| 1942 | **Cripps Mission** – proposal for an elected Assembly |
| **1946** | **Constituted under the Cabinet Mission Plan** (16 May 1946) |
- **Set up under the Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946** – **not** the 1935 Act, Cripps, Wavell Plan, Simon Commission or the Independence Act.
- **Cabinet Mission** (arrived **24 March 1946**): **Pethick-Lawrence** (Secretary of State for India), **Stafford Cripps** (Board of Trade), **A.V. Alexander** (Admiralty) – **NOT William Wood**.
- **Cripps proposals (1942):** Assembly **elected** by the lower houses of provincial legislatures by PR (princely states to **send** representatives); **any province not accepting** the new Constitution could **sign a separate agreement** with Britain.

=== National Symbols Adopted by the Assembly
- **National Flag** – design adopted **22 July 1947** (resolution moved by Nehru); the wheel = **Ashoka Chakra (Dharmachakra)**, **24 spokes**, the **wheel of law** – stands for **justice** (dharma).
- **Flag Code of India, 2002:** ratio of length to height **3:2**; standard sizes include 900×600 mm and 450×300 mm – **600×400 mm is not** a standard size.
- **Jana Gana Mana** (Tagore, originally **Bengali**) – adopted in its Hindi version as the **National Anthem** on **24 January 1950** (Prasad's statement, not a vote); full rendition **52 seconds**; first sung at the Calcutta Congress, 27 Dec 1911.
  - Its regions include **Sindh**, now in **Pakistan** (Utkala, Dravida, Maratha are in India).
  - **January 2017:** lakhs of devotees at **Khodaldham, Kagvad (near Rajkot)** set a **Guinness record** for **collective singing of the National Anthem**.
- **Vande Mataram** (Bankimchandra) – originally in **Sanskrit** (in Bengali script), **not Bengali**; declared **National Song** on 24 Jan 1950, **honoured equally**; first political singing 1896 Congress.
- **Art. 51A(a)** names the **Flag and Anthem**, not the Song; the **Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Act, 2026** (passed July 2026) gives the Song statutory protection like the Anthem; **Bijoe Emmanuel v State of Kerala (1986)** – standing respectfully without singing is no offence.
- **Satyameva Jayate** (Mundaka Upanishad) – national **motto**, adopted with the **State Emblem** on **26 January 1950**; inscribed below the emblem on currency notes and national documents.
- **National Calendar (Saka):** 1 Chaitra = **22 March** (21 March in a leap year).
- **National flower: Lotus** (*Nelumbo nucifera*).
> UPSC 2003 NOT-correct statement: "Vande Mataram composed originally in **Bengali**" ✗ (it was Sanskrit).
- **Language – Munshi-Ayyangar formula (14 Sep 1949):** **Hindi in Devanagari** as official language of the Union, **English continued for 15 years**, **14** other languages in the Eighth Schedule – **not** an immediate end of English.

=== Indian Independence Act, 1947 and the Transition
# Mountbatten Plan and the Act
- **3 June 1947 Plan (Mountbatten)** – partition, accepted by Congress and League – the **basis of the Indian Independence Act**.
- Bill introduced in the Commons **4 July 1947**; passed / **royal assent 18 July 1947**.
- **Radcliffe** chaired the **Punjab** and **Bengal** Boundary Commissions; award published **17 August 1947**.
# Provisions
- Ended **British sovereignty**; two independent **Dominions – India and Pakistan** – from **15 August 1947**.
- **Governor-General** (common or separate) to act on **Dominion ministers'** advice; British monarch nominal head; **Crown lost its veto**.
- **Constituent Assembly** = sovereign constitution-maker **and** Dominion legislature; the **Central Legislative Assembly ended**.
- **Paramountcy lapsed**: about **565** princely states theoretically free; expected to join India or Pakistan.
- **Instrument of Accession:** ceded **defence, external affairs, communications** (NOT finance); **Standstill Agreement** – Hyderabad, November 1947; **Kashmir** acceded **26 October 1947**; UN ceasefire 1 January 1949.
- Dominion status ended with the Republic, **26 Jan 1950**.
# Governors-General and after
- **Mountbatten**: last Viceroy and **first GG of independent India**; **C. Rajagopalachari**: last and only Indian GG (June 1948 – Jan 1950); he assented to the **Madras Estates (Abolition) Act** on 2 April 1949.
- **Nehru's "Tryst with Destiny"** ("At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom") – night of **14–15 August 1947**, in the Assembly.
- After 26 Jan 1950 the Assembly worked as the **Provisional Parliament** till **17 April 1952**; the **first Lok Sabha** was constituted on 17 April 1952 and its **first session began 13 May 1952**.
- Significance: sovereignty came with partition; Congress accepted dominion status for a quick, peaceful transfer.

=== Linguistic Provinces – Dar Commission and JVP Committee
- **Linguistic Provinces Commission** (**17 June 1948**): **S.K. Dar**, retired Allahabad HC judge.
- **Dar Commission** and **JVP Committee** (**J**awaharlal, **V**allabhbhai, **P**attabhi Sitaramayya) both **rejected linguistic states** for the time being; JVP left room for **Andhra**.
- **AP angle:** the Madras Assembly resolved (four months before independence) to divide the Presidency into **Andhra, Tamil, Karnataka, Kerala**; Nehru accepted the Andhra demand **27 Nov 1947**; Andhra dropped from the Draft of 21 Feb 1948; the demand led to **Andhra State, 1953**.
