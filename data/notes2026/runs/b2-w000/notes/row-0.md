=== Government of India Act, 1935 – Federation, Provincial Autonomy and the Constitution's Blueprint
# Basis and size
- **Based on:** the **Simon Commission** report, the **Round Table Conferences** (the third RTC) and the **White Paper (1933)**.
- **Size:** **321 sections, 10 schedules** (14 parts) – the **longest Act** ever passed by the British Parliament up to then (APPSC JL 2001).
- **Nehru's verdict:** a **"Charter of Slavery"** / "a machine with strong brakes but no engine" (UKPSC 2007).
# Federation (never formed)
- **All-India Federation** of British provinces + princely states – the first provision for a **federal polity** in India; the Act's own name for it = **"Federation of India"** (Part II, s.5).
- **Never came into being:** joining was **optional** for the princes and the **rulers of princely states did not consent** to join (APPSC JL 2003).
- **Real British intention** in bringing the princes in: to use them to **counter-balance the nationalist leaders** (UPSC 2002).
- **Three lists:** **Federal (59 items), Provincial (54), Concurrent (36)** – first division of legislative powers between Centre and provinces; the basis of today's Centre–State scheme.
- **Residuary powers → the Governor-General** (not the federal or provincial legislature, not the Governors) (UPSC 2018).
- **Dyarchy at the Centre** proposed: **defence and foreign affairs** = reserved subjects under the **Governor-General** – **never implemented**.
- **Bicameral Centre (proposed):** **Federal Assembly + Council of States**.
# Provinces
- **Provincial autonomy** = the **most important feature**; it **abolished provincial dyarchy** (of 1919) and gave responsible ministries from **April 1937**.
- **Bicameral legislatures in 6 of 11 provinces:** **Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, United Provinces** – **not Punjab**.
- **Governors kept** special powers: **veto**, ordinances and power to **legislate on their own** (Governor's Acts).
- **Communal representation extended** (not abolished) to **depressed classes, women and labour**.
- **Burma separated** from India; **Sindh** carved from **Bombay**, **Orissa** from **Bihar**.
- **Franchise** extended to about **14%** of the population (about 3.5 crore voters); still **no adult franchise**.
- **1937 elections:** Congress formed ministries in most provinces but **not in Punjab** (Unionist Party) or Bengal.
- **⚠ Trap:** the 1935 Act did **NOT** bring dyarchy at **both** Centre and provinces – it **ended** provincial dyarchy and only **proposed** central dyarchy.
# Institutions
- **Federal Court** (Delhi) – inaugurated **1 October 1937**; first CJ **Sir Maurice Gwyer**; replaced by the Supreme Court in **1950**.
- **RBI** set up on **1 April 1935** under the **RBI Act, 1934**; **Federal, Provincial and Joint PSCs** provided (no "All-India Services Commission").
- **Council of India abolished**; the Secretary of State got a team of advisers instead.
# Legacy in the Constitution
- **Main source** of the Constitution: about **250 provisions / "nearly 75%"** drawn from it – federal scheme, office of Governor, judiciary, PSCs, emergency provisions, administrative details.
- **Section 42 → Article 123** (President's ordinance power – a relic of the 1935 Act); **Section 63 → Article 175** (Governor's address).
- **Centre–State relations** pattern traced to the **1935 Act** (not the US Constitution or the Nehru Report).
> Statement angle (UPSC 2004): "abolition of diarchy in Governors' provinces" ✓; "Governors' power to veto and legislate" ✓; "abolition of communal representation" ✗ → **1 and 2 only**.

=== Charter Acts 1793–1853 – From Trading Company to Administrator
| Act | Key provisions |
|---|---|
| 1793 | Monopoly extended **20 years**; override power extended to all later GGs and Presidency Governors; more control over Bombay and Madras; Board of Control salaries charged to **Indian revenues**; C-in-C not a council member |
| 1813 | **Trade monopoly in India ended** except **tea and China trade**; **Christian missionaries allowed**; **Rs 1 lakh a year for education** (first such provision); first asserted **Crown sovereignty** over Company territories |
| 1833 (Saint Helena Act) | **GG of Bengal → Governor-General of India** (**William Bentinck** – last GG of Bengal, first GG of India); Company's **commercial functions ended** – purely administrative; **Law Member** added (council to 4) – **Macaulay**; Law Commission under Macaulay; legislation centralised; **Section 87**: no Indian debarred by religion, birth, descent or colour; GG-in-Council to **mitigate and abolish slavery** |
| 1853 (last Charter Act) | **Legislative and executive functions separated**; **6 legislative members** added – Indian (Central) Legislative Council, the **first legislature** / "mini-parliament"; **open competition** for civil services (Macaulay Committee 1854); territories held **"in trust for Her Majesty"**, no fixed term |
# Charter Act, 1813
- **Ended the trade monopoly** in India (MPPSC 2004; UKPSC 2016) – but kept **tea and China** trade.
- **Christian missionary activity** permitted (Charles Grant, Wilberforce).
- **English education chain:** **1813** grant of Rs 1 lakh → **General Committee of Public Instruction (1823)** → **Orientalist–Anglicist controversy** → Macaulay's Minute and **Bentinck's English Education Act (1835)** – all three led to English education (UPSC 2018).
# Charter Act, 1833 – most asked
- **Governor-General of Bengal designated Governor-General of India** – first GG of India **Bentinck** (1833) (asked 5 times: UPSC 2023, G2, SI 2012, UKPSC 2007).
- **Final step towards centralisation**; GG-in-Council got **all law-making powers** for the whole of British India – often called the **beginning of constitution-making** under British rule.
- **Law Member:** **Macaulay** (first) – the **Father of the Indian Penal Code** (Law Commission draft; IPC enacted 1860).
- **Slavery:** the Act enjoined the government to take measures to **abolish slavery** and improve the condition of slaves (→ Slavery Act V of **1843**).
- **⚠ Trap:** the 1833 Act did **NOT** provide that an **Indian** be the Law Member (UPSC 2003); first Law Member = Macaulay, not Impey.
# Charter Act, 1853
- **First official legislature:** the separate Legislative Council of 1853 (APPSC TWO 2012).
- **Civil services:** appointments were by **patronage of the Directors till 1853**; the 1853 Act threw them open to **competition** (exam from **1855**).
- **Order:** Regulating Act 1773 → Pitt's India Act 1784 → Charter Acts (1793 onwards) → Indian Councils Act 1861 (CDS 2000).

=== Government of India Act, 1919 – Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms
# Basis
- **Montagu's declaration, 20 August 1917:** "increasing association of Indians in every branch of administration" and gradual **responsible government**; **Montagu-Chelmsford Report 1918**.
- **Edwin Montagu** = Secretary of State; **Lord Chelmsford** = Viceroy.
# Provisions
- **Dyarchy in the provinces** (Greek *di-arche*, double rule) – first time; provincial subjects split into **reserved** and **transferred** (MPPSC 2022; UPSC 2017).
- **Reserved subjects:** administered by the **Governor** with his **Executive Council** (not answerable to the legislature).
- **Transferred subjects:** Governor with **Indian ministers** responsible to the legislative council.
- **Central and provincial subjects** separated – it **clearly defined the jurisdiction of the central and provincial governments** (UPSC 2015); **budgets separated** for the first time.
- **Bicameral central legislature** first time: **Council of State** (upper, 60) + **Legislative Assembly** (lower, **145**: 104 elected + 41 nominated); **direct elections** on a limited franchise (property, tax, education) (asked 4 times).
- Term **"responsible government"** first used; 3 of the 6 members of the Viceroy's Executive Council to be Indians.
- **Communal representation extended** to **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans**.
- **High Commissioner for India in London**; **Public Service Commission** provided – set up **1 October 1926** (Chairman + 4) (UPPSC 2008); **Public Accounts Committee 1921**.
- **Civil services:** the ICS examination to be held **in India** too (from 1922); by **1941** Indians outnumbered Europeans in the ICS (UPRO 2021: both statements correct).
- **Chamber of Princes**: **120** members, King George V's Proclamation **23 December 1919**; first Chancellor **Ganga Singh of Bikaner** (UPPSC 2023).
- **Statutory commission after 10 years** → **Simon Commission** appointed **1927** (two years early), 7 British members under **Sir John Simon**; "Simon Go Back".
- **Last election** of the Indian (central) legislature under this Act: **1945**.
# Reserved vs Transferred
| Reserved (Governor + Executive Council) | Transferred (Governor + Indian Ministers) |
|---|---|
| **Police**, **administration of justice**, **land revenue**, law and order, irrigation, finance | **Local self-government**, education, public health, agriculture, public works |
> UPSC 2022: reserved = justice, land revenue, police → **1, 3 and 4**; local self-government was transferred.

=== Crown Rule – Government of India Act, 1858
- **Rule transferred from the East India Company to the British Crown** – the main administrative consequence of the **Revolt of 1857**; British India under **direct Crown rule from 1858** (asked 8 times).
- **"Act for the Good Government of India"**; Parliament passed it for a more responsible management of Indian affairs after the revolt exposed Company misrule – **not** because the Company was unwilling to rule (NDA 2006).
- **Governor-General also styled Viceroy**: **Lord Canning** first; last Viceroy **Mountbatten**.
- **Board of Control and Court of Directors abolished** – dual government ended (**⚠** "retained the Court of Directors" is the wrong statement).
- **Secretary of State for India** created – a member of the British cabinet with a **15-member Council of India**; he **issued instructions to the Governor-General** and the Crown's powers were exercised through him.
- **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1 Nov 1858)** – called the **"Magna Carta of Indian liberties"** (**⚠** not the Charter Act 1813).

=== Indian Councils Acts 1861 and 1892 – Ordinances, Portfolios, Indirect Election
# Indian Councils Act, 1861
- **Restored legislative powers of Bombay and Madras** (decentralisation); new councils in Bengal (1862), NWFP (1866), Punjab (1897).
- **Beginning of representative institutions:** Canning nominated 3 Indians as non-officials – **Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao** (not Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, who criticised the absence of Indians as a lesson of 1857).
- **Portfolio system** (begun by Canning, 1859) got statutory recognition – the Viceroy's authority over his council strengthened (UPSC 2002).
- **Ordinance power** to the Viceroy in emergency – valid at most **six months** (UPPSC 2001).
- **Indian High Courts Act 1861** → High Courts of **Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1862)**.
# Indian Councils Act, 1892
- **Indirect election** introduced (asked 4 times): nomination on the recommendation of **universities, district boards, municipalities, chambers of commerce**.
- **Budget discussion** allowed (no vote); questions with **6 days' notice**, **no supplementary** questions.
| Feature | 1861 | 1892 |
|---|---|---|
| Indians | Nominated non-officials | More non-officials |
| Election | None | **Indirect** begins |
| Budget | No discussion | **Discussion**, no vote |
| Other | Portfolio, ordinance | Right to ask questions |

=== Indian Councils Act, 1909 – Morley-Minto Reforms
- **John Morley** = Secretary of State; **Lord Minto** = Viceroy – **"Father of Communal Electorate"**.
- **Separate electorate for Muslims** = **communal representation** (asked 4 times: SI 2011; MPPSC 2017, 2019).
- **Elected representation:** the Act that "provided for some elected representation" = **1909** (APPSC 2020); often called the **first attempt to introduce a representative and popular element** (SSC 2000).
- **Indians associated with the executive councils** for the first time: **S.P. Sinha** = first Indian in the Viceroy's Executive Council, as **Law Member** – the first "closer association of Indians with the administration".
- Central council: additional members raised to **60** (total **69**); **official majority** at the Centre, non-official majority in provinces; still **unicameral**.
- **Supplementary questions**, resolutions and budget discussion allowed.
- **⚠ Trap:** no dyarchy and no Chamber of Princes in 1909 (both 1919); indirect election was 1892, not 1909.

=== Regulating Act 1773, Pitt's India Act 1784 and the Act of 1786
# Background
- **Act of 1767:** the Company to pay the British Government **£400,000 a year** (APPSC G2 2017).
- **1772 crisis:** famine, falling revenue, **corruption and oppression by Company officials** (also in justice), loan of about £1 million sought → Parliament intervened; **Lord North** committee report.
- **Before 1773:** the head of Fort William was the **Governor of Bengal** (1757–1772).
# Regulating Act, 1773
- **First** step by Parliament to **control** the Company – British Government's control increased; it **altered the Company's constitution at home** (Court of Directors, Court of Proprietors) and its structure in India.
- **Governor of Bengal → Governor-General of Bengal**: **Warren Hastings** (first), with a **4-member Executive Council**.
- **Bombay and Madras made subordinate** to Bengal (Calcutta).
- **Supreme Court at Calcutta (Fort William)** – provided by the Act, set up by Charter in **1774**: **1 Chief Justice + 3 judges**; first CJ **Sir Elijah Impey** (asked 4 times).
- **Banned private trade and bribes** by Company servants.
- Directors to send letters on revenue, civil and military affairs to the British Government.
- **⚠ Trap:** it did **NOT abolish the Court of Directors** – both it and the Board of Control went only in **1858** (APPSC 2019).
- **Amending Act 1781 (Act of Settlement):** separated judicial and executive functions.
# Pitt's India Act, 1784 and the Act of 1786
- **Dual control / double government:** **Board of Control** (political, **6 members**) + **Court of Directors** (commercial).
- Company territories called **"British possessions in India"**; GG's council cut to **3**.
- **Act of 1786:** GG may **override his Council** and act as **Commander-in-Chief** – first used by **Lord Cornwallis**.
> Match (UPSC 2002): 1813 – monopoly ended; Regulating Act – directors to submit correspondence; 1858 – power to the Crown; Pitt's Act – Board of Control → **A-2, B-4, C-3, D-1**.

=== Allied Facts Asked with This Topic
- **Local self-government:** **Lord Ripon** – Resolution of **1882**, the "Magna Carta of local self-government" (not Canning or Macaulay).
- **Fundamental rights demand:** first in the **Constitution of India Bill, 1895**; the **Congress (Bombay special session, 1918)** demanded a **Declaration of Rights**; then Nehru Report 1928, Karachi 1931.
- **Muslims and the Congress:** **Badruddin Tyabji** (Congress president 1887) appealed to Muslims to join the Congress; Sir Syed Ahmad Khan opposed it.
- **Chronology:** INC formed **1885** → Simon Commission **1927** → Round Table Conferences **1930–32** → Quit India **1942**.
