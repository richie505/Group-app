=== NOTES 2:41 | State Legislature – Assembly, Legislative Council and Article 169
## SUB 0 | Legislative Assembly – Strength, Term, Qualifications, Quorum
[41:0:0] # Strength – Article 170
[41:0:1] - **Article 170:** each Assembly = **not more than 500, not less than 60** members, **directly elected** from territorial constituencies (not 40/400, 50/450 or 450 maximum; not 545, the Lok Sabha figure). "No minimum" is wrong.
[41:0:2] - **Exceptions to the 60 minimum:** **Sikkim (32, Art. 371F)**, **Goa (40)**, **Mizoram (40)**; Arunachal Pradesh's minimum is 30 (Art. 371H) but it has 60.

  [41:0:3.0] Uttar Pradesh (largest) | 403
  [41:0:3.1] Andhra Pradesh / Telangana | 175 / 119 (united AP: 301 in 1956, later 294)
  [41:0:3.2] Himachal Pradesh | 68 – larger than Arunachal, Manipur, Meghalaya (60 each)
  [41:0:3.3] Meghalaya, Nagaland, Tripura (also Arunachal, Manipur) | 60 each
  [41:0:3.4] Goa, Mizoram | 40 each
  [41:0:3.5] Sikkim – smallest State | 32 – incl. one Sangha seat reserved for Buddhist monasteries (a religion-based reservation)
  [41:0:3.6] Puducherry (UT) | 30 elected + up to 3 nominated
[41:0:4] - **Smallest Assembly:** most keys say **Puducherry (30 elected seats)**; by total strength (33 with nominated members) the smallest is **Sikkim (32)**.
[41:0:5] # Term – Article 172
[41:0:6] - **5 years** (not 2, 4 or 6); can be **dissolved earlier by the Governor** (on the CM's advice) – not by the Speaker, CM or Vice-President.
[41:0:7] - **National Emergency:** **Parliament** may extend it by law **one year at a time**, not beyond **six months** after the Emergency ends (not the President, Governor or State Legislature).
[41:0:8] # Qualifications – Article 173
[41:0:9] - **Article 173** (not 175, 177 or 178): citizen of India; at least **25 years** for the Assembly (30 for the Council); other qualifications prescribed by Parliament.
[41:0:10] - UPSC 2008: "not more than 450 members" ✗ (500); "not qualified if less than 25" ✓ → **B only**.
[41:0:11] # Quorum – Article 189(3)
[41:0:12] - **Ten members or one-tenth** of the total membership, **whichever is greater** (not 30, one-half or one-fourth).
[41:0:13] - **Reservation:** SC/ST seats in proportion to population (Art. 332); Anglo-Indian nomination ended (104th Amendment, 2020); one-third for women after delimitation (106th Amendment, 2023).

## SUB 1 | Legislative Procedure – Money Bills, Ordinary Bills and Deadlock
[41:1:0] # Money Bills – Articles 198, 199, 207
[41:1:1] - Introduced **only in the Legislative Assembly** (not in either House or the Council), only by a **Minister**, only on the **Governor's recommendation** (**Art. 207(1)**) – not Parliament, the President or a committee.
[41:1:2] - **Art. 199:** defines a Money Bill; the **Speaker certifies** it (final).
[41:1:3] - **Art. 198:** the Council must return it within **14 days** (not 7, 10, 21 or 30); it cannot reject or amend it; the Assembly may accept or reject its recommendations.
[41:1:4] # Ordinary Bills – Article 197
[41:1:5] - A Bill passed by the Assembly can be held by the Council **not more than three months** on first passage and **one month** on second – the Council can only **interpose delay** (max. 4 months); it cannot permanently block a Bill.
[41:1:6] - **Final authority = the Legislative Assembly**; the Governor has no "final say" on a deadlock.
[41:1:7] - **Demands for grants** are voted **only by the Assembly**; the Council only discusses the budget.
[41:1:8] - **No joint sitting** in a State Legislature (unlike Art. 108 for Parliament).
[41:1:9] - **Bill originating in the Council** and **rejected by the Assembly** → the Bill **ends (dies)**; no joint sitting, no reconsideration, no reference to the Governor.
[41:1:10] TABLE head: Point | Council's power
  [41:1:10.0] Money Bill | Delay 14 days
  [41:1:10.1] Ordinary Bill (Assembly's) | Delay 3 + 1 months; Assembly prevails
  [41:1:10.2] Ordinary Bill (Council's own), rejected by Assembly | Bill ends

[41:2:0] # Composition – Article 171
[41:2:1] TABLE head: Chosen by | Share

  [41:2:1.1] MLAs (from among non-members) | 1/3 – not 1/13
  [41:2:1.2] Graduates of 3 years' standing | 1/12
  [41:2:1.3] Teachers (secondary school and above) of 3 years' standing | 1/12

[41:2:2] - **Industrialists** are **not** a category. "1/13 elected by the Legislative Assembly" is the **wrong** statement (it is one-third).
[41:2:3] - Elections to the Council: **proportional representation by the single transferable vote**.
[41:2:4] - **Muthulakshmi Reddy** – India's first woman legislator (Madras Legislative Council).
[41:2:5] - **Article 171** = composition of the **Vidhan Parishad** (Art. 169 = creation / abolition; Art. 170 = Assembly).
[41:2:6] # Size
[41:2:7] - **Maximum one-third** of the Assembly's total membership (not one-half, two-thirds or one-fourth); **minimum 40** (not 44, 52 or 65). Parliament fixes the actual strength.
[41:2:8] - UPPSC 2023: "not more than one-third of the Assembly" ✓ and "at least forty members" ✓ → **both**.
[41:2:9] - UPSC 2015: the Council can be larger than half of the Assembly ✗; the Governor nominates the Chairman ✗ (the **Council elects** its Chairman, Art. 182) → **neither**.
[41:2:10] # Tenure
[41:2:11] - **Permanent body** – **cannot be dissolved, but can be abolished** (Art. 169); member's term **6 years**; **one-third retire every second year** – so its membership **changes every two years**.
[41:2:12] - The Assembly, Lok Sabha (House of the People) can be dissolved; the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) is permanent and cannot be abolished by Parliament under Art. 169.

## SUB 3 | Article 169 – Creation and Abolition of Legislative Councils
[41:3:0] - **Article 169** (not 167, 168, 170, 171, 368 or 162): **Parliament** may by law **abolish** a Council or **create** one.
[41:3:1] - **Step 1 – Assembly resolution by special majority:** a majority of the **total membership** and **two-thirds of members present and voting** (not a simple majority; not the Lok Sabha).
[41:3:2] - **Step 2 – Parliament** passes the law by a **simple majority**; it is **not deemed an amendment** under **Art. 368**.
[41:3:3] - The resolution is **recommendatory** – it **does not impose an obligation on the Union** (Parliament) to legislate. The **Governor has no role** (nor the Speaker).
[41:3:4] - Statements (NTR University): A – Art. 169 deals with creation/abolition ✓; B – resolution by **simple** majority ✗; C – Art. 171: not more than 1/3, not less than 40 ✓ → **A and C**.
[41:3:5] - **AP, January 2020:** the Assembly passed the resolution to abolish the Council under **Article 169(1)**; it was **withdrawn in November 2021**.
[41:3:6] - **AP Council history:** created 1958, **abolished 1985** (NTR), **revived 2007**; strength **50** under the AP Reorganisation Act 2014, **58** since the 2015 Amendment; Telangana **40**.
[41:3:7] > "Parliament ratifies the resolution passed by the Assembly with special majority" is the best of the loose options; Parliament itself needs only a simple majority.

## SUB 5 | Presiding Officers – Speaker and Chairman
[41:5:0] # Speaker – Articles 178-181, 189
[41:5:1] - **Officer of the Assembly** = the **Speaker** (and Deputy Speaker), elected by the Assembly from its members (**Art. 178**) – not the Governor, CM or a Minister.
[41:5:2] - **Resigns** by writing to the **Deputy Speaker** (**Art. 179**) – not the Governor, CM or President.
[41:5:3] - **Vacates office** if he **ceases to be a member**; is removed by a resolution of a majority of all the then members after **14 days' notice**.
[41:5:4] - **On dissolution** the Speaker **does not vacate** office; he continues until just before the **first meeting of the new Assembly**.
[41:5:5] - **Vote:** only a **casting vote in a tie** (**Art. 189**); during a resolution for his removal he **does not preside** but may speak and **vote in the first instance** (**Art. 181**).
[41:5:6] - Decides **Money Bill** certification (Art. 199(3)) and **Tenth Schedule** defection (subject to judicial review, Kihoto Hollohan 1992).
[41:5:7] - **K. Pratibha Bharati** – first woman Speaker of the AP Assembly (1999-2004).
[41:5:8] - **Immunity (Art. 194(2)):** no member is liable in any court for anything said or any vote given in the Legislature.
[41:5:9] # Chairman of the Council – Article 182
[41:5:10] - **Elected by the Council** from its members (not nominated by the Governor); resigns to the Deputy Chairman.
[41:5:11] > CDS 2006: continues after dissolution ✓, resigns to the Deputy Speaker ✓ → both. NDA 2012 / UPSC 2018: "vacates on dissolution" ✗.
[41:5:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.302-303; Laxmikanth Indian Polity 8th ed. ch.33 (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-02.txt p.36-39); app notes 2:41 (sub 8)
[41:5:13] - **Leader of the Opposition in an Assembly:** by convention / Speaker's direction the largest opposition party needs at least **one-tenth (10%)** of the House's seats (AP 2024: YSRCP's 11 of 175 fell short).

## SUB 7 | Disqualification of Legislators
[41:7:0] TABLE head: Ground | Decided by
  [41:7:0.0] Art. 191 – office of profit, unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, not a citizen, disqualified by a law of Parliament (RPA 1951) | Governor – his decision is final, but he acts on the binding opinion of the Election Commission (Art. 192)
  [41:7:0.1] Tenth Schedule – defection | Speaker / Chairman
[41:7:1] - So for matters **other than the Tenth Schedule** the final authority is the **Governor** – not the Speaker, the ECI, the State Election Commission, the CM or the High Court.
[41:7:2] - **Art. 190 – vacation of seats:** no one can be a member of **both Houses** (or of two States' legislatures); a resignation takes effect only on the **Speaker's / Chairman's acceptance** (190(3)(b)); **60 days' absence** without permission → seat **vacated** (190(4)) – vacation, not an Art. 191 disqualification.
[41:7:3] - Compare MPs: **Art. 103** – the **President** decides, on the **Election Commission's** binding opinion.
[41:7:4] - **Conviction:** an MLA sentenced to **2 years or more** is disqualified **immediately** (RPA s.8(3); Lily Thomas, 2013).

## SUB 1 | Jurisdiction – Original (Art. 131), Appellate (Arts. 132-136), Writ (Art. 32)
[43:1:0] # Jurisdictions at a glance
[43:1:1] TABLE head: Jurisdiction | Article | Key point
  [43:1:1.0] Original (federal) | 131 | Centre v. State(s), Centre + State(s) v. State(s), State v. State – exclusive: no other court
  [43:1:1.1] Writ | 32 | Fundamental Rights only; original but not exclusive (HCs also issue writs, Art. 226)
  [43:1:1.2] Appellate – constitutional | 132 (certificate under 134A) | Any case (civil, criminal or other) with a substantial question of constitutional interpretation
  [43:1:1.3] Appellate – civil | 133 | Substantial question of law of general importance, on HC certificate (₹20,000 limit removed by 30th Amendment, 1972)
  [43:1:1.4] Appellate – criminal | 134 | HC reversed acquittal and sentenced to death, or withdrew a case and sentenced to death – as of right; otherwise HC certificate
  [43:1:1.5] Special leave | 136 | Discretionary; any court or tribunal except military (136(2))
  [43:1:1.6] Review | 137 | Own judgments / orders
  [43:1:1.7] Advisory | 143 | President's references – opinion not binding
  [43:1:1.8] President / VP election disputes | 71 | Original, exclusive and final
[43:1:2] - The SC has **original, appellate and advisory** jurisdiction (plus writ, review and Court of Record powers) – "all the above" in the PYQs.
[43:1:3] # Original jurisdiction – Article 131
[43:1:4] - **SC as a federal court**: decides **Centre-State and inter-State** disputes that involve a **legal right** (question of law or fact); **exclusive** – no High Court shares it. It is the main forum for Centre-State **fiscal** disputes too.
[43:1:5] - First Art. 131 suit: **West Bengal v. Union (1961)** on the Coal Bearing Areas Act.
[43:1:6] - **Excluded from Art. 131**: disputes from **pre-Constitution treaties**, agreements, sanads (proviso – these can go to the President's reference under 143(2)); **inter-State river water disputes (Art. 262)**; matters referred to the **Finance Commission (Art. 280)**; adjustment of expenses and pensions (**Art. 290**); suits by **private persons** against the Union or a State; disputes between **private companies**; **Centre v. Union Territory**; **election disputes** of Parliament and State Legislatures (go to **High Courts**).
[43:1:7] > APPSC G1 2023 / UPSC 2012: NOT in Art. 131 = election disputes and Centre-UT disputes; included = Centre-State and State-State. APSLPRB 2019: water disputes and Finance Commission matters are outside it.
[43:1:8] # Appellate jurisdiction
[43:1:9] - **Highest court of appeal** (replaced the Privy Council); appeals in **constitutional, civil and criminal** cases from HCs.
[43:1:10] - **Art. 132 read with Art. 134A**: constitutional cases – HC certifies a substantial question of interpretation of the Constitution.
[43:1:11] - **Art. 134(1)**: appeal **as of right** when the HC has **on appeal reversed an acquittal and sentenced the accused to death**, or has withdrawn a case from a subordinate court and convicted and sentenced him to death; otherwise only on HC certificate. No appeal as of right if the HC **reverses a conviction**.
[43:1:12] - **Art. 134(2)**: **Parliament may by law enlarge** the criminal appellate jurisdiction – done in **1970** (reversal of acquittal with life or 10 years) – so "Parliament has no power" is **wrong**.
[43:1:13] - **Art. 136 – Special Leave to Appeal**: the SC **may in its discretion** grant special leave from **any judgment, decree, order** of **any court or tribunal** (not only HCs), final or interlocutory, in any matter; **not** against **armed forces** courts or tribunals. A plenary, residual power – used "sparingly"; it is **not** about judges' leave or vacations.
[43:1:14] # Writ jurisdiction – Article 32
[43:1:15] - SC is the **guarantor and defender of Fundamental Rights**; a citizen may go **directly** to it (Art. 32 is itself a Fundamental Right).
[43:1:16] - **Not exclusive**: HCs issue writs under **Art. 226** for FRs **and** for other legal rights – so the HC's writ power is **wider**; "SC has exclusive power to issue writs" is **false**.
[43:1:17] # Enlarging jurisdiction
[43:1:18] - **Art. 138(1)**: **Parliament** may by law confer further jurisdiction on the SC for matters in the **Union List**; Art. 138(2) – other matters by special agreement of Centre and State.
[43:1:19] - **Art. 139**: Parliament may confer writ power for other purposes; **Art. 139A** (44th Amendment, 1978): SC may **withdraw** cases from HCs or **transfer** cases from one HC to another – so the **Supreme Court** can transfer a case anywhere in India.
[43:1:20] - Parliament **can enlarge but not curtail** the SC's jurisdiction – UPSC 2005 "Parliament cannot enlarge it" is **false**.
[43:1:21] > UPSC 2005: both statements wrong – Parliament can enlarge jurisdiction (Art. 138), and HC administrative expenses are charged on the **Consolidated Fund of the State**, not of India.

## SUB 2 | Special Powers – Court of Record, Review, Arts. 141, 142, 144, 145; Guardian of the Constitution
[43:2:0] # Articles
[43:2:1] TABLE head: Article | Power
  [43:2:1.0] 129 | Court of Record – records of perpetual evidentiary value; power to punish contempt of itself (and, per 1991 ruling, of HCs and lower courts)
  [43:2:1.1] 137 | Review of its own judgments or orders – subject to Parliament's law and Art. 145 rules
  [43:2:1.2] 141 | Law declared by the SC binding on all courts within the territory of India
  [43:2:1.3] 142 | Decree or order for complete justice in any pending cause; enforceable throughout India
  [43:2:1.4] 144 | All authorities, civil and judicial, in India shall act in aid of the Supreme Court
  [43:2:1.5] 145 | Rules of practice and procedure – made with the approval of the President; 145(3): at least five judges for a substantial question of constitutional interpretation and for Art. 143 references
[43:2:2] - **Guardian / custodian of the Constitution** and **final interpreter** of it (judicial review); also guardian of **Fundamental Rights**, a **federal court** and the **highest court of appeal**. The Constitution's enforcement rests on the **Supreme Court** – not the President or Parliament.
[43:2:3] - **Not powers of the SC**: issuing **ordinances** (President, Art. 123), advising the President on **all** matters (only on references under Art. 143), striking down **any** law (only unconstitutional ones).
[43:2:4] - **Review (Art. 137)**: within **30 days**, before the same bench; grounds – new evidence, error apparent on the record, other sufficient reason. So the SC **can change its own judgment** – neither the President nor the Cabinet can review it.
[43:2:5] - **Curative petition**: **Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra (2002)**, after a review is dismissed; rests on **Arts. 129 and 142** (inherent power) – PYQ key **Art. 142**.
[43:2:6] - **Art. 142**: "prohibitions or limitations in **ordinary laws** cannot limit" it – the SC is **not constrained by ordinary laws** when doing complete justice. Uses: **ban on liquor shops within 500 m of highways (State of TN v. K. Balu, Dec 2016)**; deemed assent to 10 TN Bills (2025); Right to Safe Travel directives (27 May 2026).
[43:2:7] - **Rule-making (Art. 145)** needs the **President's approval** – "complete independence and autonomy to make rules" is **wrong**; it is the SC's own rule-making power, subject to that approval.
[43:2:8] - **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971** (H.N. Sanyal Committee) defines civil and criminal contempt; the Constitution does not.
[43:2:9] - **V.V. Giri** (1970) – only sitting President to appear **in person** before the SC in a petition challenging his own election (Art. 71).
[43:2:10] - **e-SCR** (2023): free digital database of the SC's reported judgments.
[43:2:11] > APPSC G1 2019: correct statement = "all civil and judicial authorities shall act in aid of the SC" (Art. 144). Resignation goes to the President, the SC can review its judgments, and rules need the President's approval.

## SUB 3 | Composition, Appointment, Qualifications, Tenure; Acting, Ad Hoc and Retired Judges
[43:3:0] # Appointment – Art. 124(2)
[43:3:1] - **Every judge is appointed by the President** by warrant under his hand and seal – the collegium only recommends.
[43:3:2] - HC judges too are appointed by the **President** by warrant (Art. 217) – **not by the Governor**; both SC and HC judges resign to the President.
[43:3:3] - **CJI**: President after consulting such SC and HC judges as he deems necessary; by convention (and Second Judges Case) the **senior-most judge**.
[43:3:4] - **Other judges**: consultation with the **CJI is obligatory**; the President **may** consult other SC and HC judges.
[43:3:5] - The President acts on the advice of the **Union Council of Ministers headed by the PM** (Art. 74) – the file goes Law Minister → PM → President (Memorandum of Procedure).
[43:3:6] - Convention of seniority broken: **A.N. Ray (1973)**, superseding Shelat, Hegde and Grover; **M.H. Beg (1977)**.
[43:3:7] # Qualifications – Art. 124(3)
[43:3:8] - **Citizen of India** and either **5 years as an HC judge**, or **10 years as an HC advocate**, or a **distinguished jurist** in the **President's** opinion (never used so far). **No minimum age**.
[43:3:9] # Oath, salary, tenure, resignation
[43:3:10] - **Oath** before the **President** or a person appointed by him (form in the **Third Schedule**).
[43:3:11] - **Salaries** determined by **Parliament by law** (Art. 125); not reduced after appointment except in a **Financial Emergency** (Art. 360).
[43:3:12] - Holds office till **65**; age question decided as Parliament provides (Art. 124(2A)). **No fixed tenure** in years.
[43:3:13] - **Resigns by writing addressed to the President** (Art. 124(2)(a)) – not the CJI, PM or Law Minister.

  [43:3:15.0] 126 | Acting CJI (office vacant, CJI absent or unable) | President appoints one of the other judges

  [43:3:15.2] 128 | Retired SC (or qualified HC) judge to sit and act | CJI with the President's previous consent and the person's consent

## SUB 5 | Advisory Jurisdiction – Article 143
[43:5:0] - **Art. 143(1)**: the **President** may refer a **question of law or fact** of **public importance** (arisen or likely) – the Court **may** give or **refuse** its opinion (e.g. Ram Janmabhoomi, 1993). Only the President can seek it – not the PM, a High Court, or the Court on its own; not limited to Fundamental Rights.
[43:5:1] - **Art. 143(2)**: disputes from **pre-Constitution treaties and agreements** (excluded from Art. 131) – the Court **must** give its opinion.
[43:5:2] - Opinion is **advisory, not binding on the President**; heard by at least **five judges** (Art. 145(3)).
[43:5:3] - Courts treat the law stated in a reference opinion as binding precedent (Art. 141) – the usual key "**not binding on the President, but binding on subordinate courts**".
[43:5:4] - **India v. USA**: advisory jurisdiction is the main difference – the **US Supreme Court has none**.
[43:5:5] - Also: India's review is under **'procedure established by law'** – narrower than the US **'due process of law'**; India blends **American judicial supremacy** with **British parliamentary sovereignty**.
