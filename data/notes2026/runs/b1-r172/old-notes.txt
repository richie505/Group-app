[172:5:1] - **Councils Act 1861:** **portfolio system** strengthened the Viceroy over his council; let the GG issue **Ordinances (max 6 months)**.

[172:5:4] - **Ilbert Bill:** showed racial discrimination – Indian judges could not try European British subjects on equal terms.
[172:5:5] Lytton owns three: **Royal Titles, VPA, Arms Act**; Ripon repealed the VPA.
## SUB 6 | Financial Decentralisation
[172:6:0] - **Mayo's Resolution, 14 Dec 1870** – start of financial decentralisation.
[172:6:1] - Before 1870 provinces sent all revenue to the Imperial Treasury at Calcutta and got grants for each item.
[172:6:2] - Provinces got **fixed annual grants** to run **police, jails, education, medical services and roads**, and could raise **local taxes** to balance their budgets.
[172:6:3] - **Later steps:** **Lytton (1877)** widened the provincial heads; **Ripon (1882)** divided revenue heads into **imperial, provincial and divided**.

=== NOTES 1:173 | Company's Constitutional Framework – Regulating Act 1773, Pitt's India Act 1784, Act of 1786
## SUB 0 | Background – Parliament Before 1773
[173:0:0] - 1. **1764:** Battle of **Buxar**.
[173:0:1] - 2. **1765:** **Shah Alam** granted the **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa** to the Company.
[173:0:2] - 3. **1767:** Parliament capped the Company's **dividend at 10 per cent** and made it pay **£400,000 a year** to the state for its territorial revenues.
[173:0:3] - 4. **1772:** Parliament appointed a **Select Committee (headed by Burgoyne)** and a **Secret Committee** to inquire into Company affairs.
[173:0:4] - 5. **1773:** their reports led to the **Regulating Act** – the **first Parliamentary control** of the Company.
## SUB 1 | Regulating Act, 1773
[173:1:0] - **First step** by the British Parliament to **control** the Company; **recognised its political and administrative functions**.
[173:1:1] - **Governor of Bengal → Governor-General of Fort William in Bengal**, with supervisory powers; **Bombay and Madras subordinated** to Bengal.
[173:1:2] - GG had a **council of four** (named in the Act: **Philip Francis, Clavering, Monson, Barwell**); decisions by majority; GG had only a **casting vote**.
[173:1:3] - **Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774):** **1 Chief Justice + 3 puisne judges**; **civil and criminal** jurisdiction – India's first Supreme Court (⚠ APPSC key (2008) gave 1776), at **Fort William**.
[173:1:4] - **Court of Directors** had to report on **revenue, civil and military** affairs to the British Government.
[173:1:5] - It **did not abolish the Court of Directors** (APPSC-GS 2019 "incorrect statement").
[173:1:6] # Firsts
[173:1:7] TABLE head: First | Person
  [173:1:7.0] First GG of Bengal under the Act | Warren Hastings
  [173:1:7.1] First Chief Justice, Supreme Court Calcutta | Sir Elijah Impey
[173:1:8] The 1773 Act did **not** abolish the Court of Directors and did **not** create the Ministry of Law and Justice.
## SUB 2 | Defects of 1773 → Remedies
[173:2:0] TABLE head: Defect | Remedy
  [173:2:0.0] GG had no veto; could be outvoted by his council (Hastings – Francis, Clavering, Monson opposed him) | Act of 1786 – override power
  [173:2:0.1] Supreme Court's jurisdiction over the Council and over Indians undefined → Council–Court clashes | 1781 Act of Settlement – official acts and revenue exempted
## SUB 3 | Amending Act, 1781 (act of Settlement)
[173:3:0] - **GG, Council and Company servants** exempted from the Supreme Court's jurisdiction for acts in their **official capacity**.
[173:3:1] - **Revenue** matters excluded from the Court's jurisdiction.
[173:3:2] - Court told to respect the **personal laws of Hindus and Muslims**.
## SUB 4 | Pitt's India Act, 1784
[173:4:0] - Created the **Board of Control** – up to **six commissioners**, including a **Secretary of State** and the **Chancellor of the Exchequer**.
[173:4:1] - **Commercial** affairs stayed with the **Court of Directors**; **political** affairs went to the **Board of Control** → **dual system ("Double Government")**.
[173:4:2] - Stricter supervision of the Company's rule in India.
[173:4:3] - The dual system lasted till **1858**, when the **Government of India Act, 1858** ended "Double Government".
[173:4:4] Court of Directors | Company | Commercial
Board of Control | Crown | Political
## SUB 5 | Act of 1786
[173:5:0] - **Why passed:** to meet **Lord Cornwallis's** condition for accepting office.
[173:5:1] - **Powers:** GG could **override his council** in special cases (right to reject the Council's decision) and also be **Commander-in-Chief**.
[173:5:2] - Fixed the 1773 defect under which **Hastings was repeatedly outvoted**.
[173:5:3] - **Charter Act of 1793** extended the overriding power to the **governors of the presidencies** (greater control over Bombay and Madras).
[173:5:4] 1781 = Act of Settlement (curbs the Supreme Court); 1786 = override power (Cornwallis).
## SUB 6 | Act → Body or Post Created – Comparison
[173:6:0] TABLE head: Act | Body / post / key provision
  [173:6:0.0] 1773 Regulating | GG of Bengal; Supreme Court, Calcutta
  [173:6:0.1] 1781 Amending (Act of Settlement) | Official acts and revenue exempted; personal laws respected
  [173:6:0.2] 1784 Pitt's India | Board of Control; dual system till 1858
  [173:6:0.3] 1786 | GG's overriding power + Commander-in-Chief
  [173:6:0.4] 1793 Charter | Override power to presidency governors
  [173:6:0.5] 1833 Charter | Law Member, first Law Commission – origin of the Ministry of Law and Justice
[173:6:1] - **Order:** Regulating Act 1773 → Amending Act 1781 → Pitt's India Act 1784 → Act of 1786.
[173:6:2] - **AP angle:** the **Madras Supreme Court (1801)** served British subjects in the Madras Presidency, including Andhra.
[173:6:3] - **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** is post-independence (earlier Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955; renamed 1976) – not a British-era landmark.

=== NOTES 1:174 | Charter Acts of 1793, 1813, 1833 and 1853 – Provisions and Significance
## SUB 0 | Charter Acts Compared
[174:0:0] TABLE head: Act | Trade | Key provisions
  [174:0:0.0] 1793 | Monopoly extended 20 years | Charter renewed 20 years; Board of Control members' salaries paid from Indian revenues
  [174:0:0.1] 1813 | Ended India trade monopoly; kept tea and China trade | Rs 1 lakh education grant; missionaries allowed; charter 20 years
  [174:0:0.2] 1833 | Ended tea/China monopoly; commercial functions ended | GG of India; Law Member; Section 87; laws called "Acts"
  [174:0:0.3] 1853 (last) | – | Separate legislature; open competitive exam; no fixed term
## SUB 1 | Trade Monopoly and Company Status
[174:1:0] TABLE head: Monopoly | Ended by
  [174:1:0.0] Trade with India (general) | Charter Act 1813
  [174:1:0.1] Tea trade and trade with China | Charter Act 1833
[174:1:1] - 1813 asserted the **sovereignty of the British Crown** over the Company's Indian possessions.
[174:1:2] - 1833: commercial rights ended; Company purely **administrative**; Europeans free to settle.
[174:1:3] - 1853: Company's authority renewed with no fixed term; territories held "**in trust for Her Majesty, her heirs and successors**" until Parliament provides otherwise.
[174:1:4] "1813 abolished the Company's trade monopoly" = not true; tea and China trade were kept.
## SUB 2 | Charter Act 1813 – Education and Missionaries
[174:2:0] - **Education grant: Rs 1,00,000 (1 lakh) a year** – first step towards State responsibility for education.
[174:2:1] - **Missionaries** allowed to enter and preach (Evangelical pressure: **William Wilberforce, Charles Grant**).
## SUB 3 | Charter Act 1833 – Peak of Centralisation
[174:3:0] - **GG of Bengal → GG of India**; first: **Lord William Bentinck** (also last GG of Bengal).
[174:3:1] - GG-in-Council got **exclusive legislative power** for all British India; Presidencies lost law-making; laws called **Acts** (not Regulations).
[174:3:2] - **Law Member** added as **4th member**: **T.B. Macaulay** (1834). The Act did not provide for an Indian Law Member.
[174:3:3] - **Section 87**: no Indian barred from office by **religion, place of birth, descent or colour**.
[174:3:4] - **Agra Presidency**: present UP separated from Bengal Presidency (1834) → renamed **North-Western Provinces (1836)**.
## SUB 4 | Charter Act 1853 – Separate Legislature and Open Competition
[174:4:0] - Legislative and executive functions separated; **6 new legislative members** → Central Legislative Council ("**mini-Parliament**"), **12 members** in all.
[174:4:1] - The 6: **4 appointed by provincial governments** (Madras, Bombay, Bengal, Agra) + **Chief Justice** + one Calcutta Supreme Court judge.
[174:4:2] - Directors' **patronage abolished** → open competitive exam (Directors appointed till 1853).
[174:4:3] - Board of Control salaries fixed by the Crown, paid from Company funds.
[174:4:4] - **Macaulay Committee (1854)**: competitive exams instead of nomination; preference for graduates.
## SUB 5 | Macaulay and Other Persons
[174:5:0] - **Macaulay**: "The Company is an **anomaly** but it is part of a system where everything is an anomaly" (Commons speech, 1833 Bill); headed **First Law Commission (1834–38)** → drafted **IPC**; **Minute on Education (1835)**.
[174:5:1] - **Raja Ram Mohan Roy**: first demanded Indians in services (1832).
[174:5:2] - **Fort William College** (1800, Wellesley) → **Haileybury** (1806).
## SUB 6 | Centralisation Trend 1773–1861
[174:6:0] TABLE head: Act | Step
  [174:6:0.0] Regulating Act 1773 | GG of Bengal; Bombay and Madras subordinated
  [174:6:0.1] Pitt's India Act 1784 | Board of Control
  [174:6:0.2] Charter Act 1833 | GG of India; exclusive legislative power – peak
  [174:6:0.3] Charter Act 1853 | Central Legislative Council with provincial members
  [174:6:0.4] Indian Councils Act 1861 | Restored power to Bombay, Madras (decentralisation)
[174:6:1] - Chronology: Plassey 1757 → Buxar 1764 → Diwani 1765 → 1773 → 1784 → 1813 → **GoI Act 1858** (Crown rule).
