## SUB 0 | Mayo, Lawrence and Dufferin – Assassination, Census, Shimla, Masterly Inactivity, INC
[0:0] # Lord Mayo (1869-72)
[0:1] - **Only Viceroy assassinated in office:** **Lord Mayo**, stabbed by the convict **Sher Ali Afridi** at Port Blair (**Andamans**) in **1872** while on tour – not Curzon, Minto, Ripon or Wellesley.
[0:2] - **First census:** **1872** under **Mayo** (non-synchronous, not all areas at once); from **1881 (Ripon)** the regular decennial, synchronous census.
[0:3]   - ⚠ NextGen Indian History p.3 prints "1871" – a slip; PMF and the Census give **1872**.
[0:4] - **Financial decentralisation:** started by **Mayo's Resolution of 1870** (not Ripon, Dalhousie or Metcalfe).
[0:5] - Also: **Rajkot College** (Kathiawar) and **Mayo College** (Ajmer) for princes; **Statistical Survey of India**; **Department of Agriculture and Commerce**; state railways; Indian Evidence Act 1872.
[0:6] # Sir John Lawrence (1864-69)
[0:7] - **Masterly Inactivity:** non-interference in Afghanistan – **John Lawrence** (not Bentinck, Canning or Mayo).
[0:8] - **Shimla** officially declared the **summer capital** of British India by **John Lawrence in 1864**.
[0:9] - Also: telegraph link with Europe (1865); High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1865); **Indian Forest Department**; Bhutan (Duar) War.
[0:10] # Lord Dufferin (1884-88)
[0:11] - **INC founded (Dec 1885)** under **Dufferin** – Ripon had left in 1884; Lytton, Lansdowne, Elgin II, Curzon are distractors.
[0:12] - In **1887** Dufferin mocked the Congress as representing only a **"microscopic minority"** of the people.
[0:13] - **Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885):** Upper Burma annexed (1 Jan 1886) = the **last major extension** of British Indian territory – Dufferin.
[0:14] # Canning, Elgin I, Northbrook
[0:15] - **Canning (1856-62):** last Company Governor-General and **first Viceroy** (1858); Revolt of 1857; withdrew the Doctrine of Lapse; universities at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1857); **Indian Councils Act 1861**, **portfolio system**; 3 Indians nominated (Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao).
[0:16] - **British PM during the Revolt of 1857:** **Palmerston** (1855-58).
[0:17] - **Elgin I (1862-63):** Wahabi movement. **Northbrook (1872-76):** abolished income tax; Kuka movement.
[0:18] - **Allahabad High Court** came in **1866** – after Canning's term.

## SUB 1 | Lytton and Ripon – VPA, Forward Policy, Ilbert Bill, Factory Act 1881
[1:0] # Lord Lytton (1876-80)
[1:1] TABLE head: Measure | Year
  [1:1.0] Royal Titles Act – Victoria "Kaiser-i-Hind" | 1876
  [1:1.1] Delhi Durbar in honour of Queen Victoria (held during famine) | 1 Jan 1877
  [1:1.2] Vernacular Press Act, Arms Act | 1878
  [1:1.3] Strachey Famine Commission | 1878
  [1:1.4] ICS maximum age cut 21 → 19; Statutory Civil Service | 1878-79
  [1:1.5] Second Afghan War; Treaty of Gandamak | 1878-80, 1879
[1:2] - **Forward / aggressive policy towards Afghanistan ("proud reserve", scientific frontier):** **Lytton** – a Disraeli nominee; not Minto, Dufferin, Elgin or Mayo.
[1:3] - **VPA (1878):** magistrates could demand a security bond from vernacular papers without prior government permission – passed by **Lytton**, **not Curzon**; **repealed by Ripon (1882)**.
[1:4] - Lytton is **not** linked with the **Ilbert Bill** (that is Ripon).
[1:5] # Lord Ripon (1880-84)
[1:6] - Sent by **Gladstone's** Liberal government; repealed the **VPA (1882)**; **Local Self-Government Resolution 1882** ("Father of Local Self-Government"); **Hunter Education Commission (1882)**; first synchronous census 1881; revenue heads split into imperial, provincial, divided (1882).
[1:7] - **Ilbert Bill (1883):** let Indian judges try Europeans → European agitation = **"White Mutiny"**; exposed racial discrimination in the courts.
[1:8] - **Malaviya (Congress 1909):** Ripon "the greatest and most beloved Viceroy whom India has known"; Ripon favoured Indians (not British interests) and undid Lytton's illiberal acts.
[1:9] # Factory Acts
[1:10] TABLE head: Act | Viceroy | Provisions
  [1:10.0] First Factory Act, 1881 | Ripon | Children below 7 barred; children 7-12 max 9 hours; four holidays a month; dangerous machinery fenced
  [1:10.1] Second Factory Act, 1891 | Lansdowne | Child age 9-14, 7 hours; women 11 hours with 1½-hour break; weekly holiday
[1:11] - The demand came from the **Lancashire** lobby; the **early nationalists (moderates) did NOT support** the Acts of 1881 and 1891 (feared loss of Indian industry's edge).
[1:12] # Chronology of the Acts
[1:13] - **Indian Civil Service Act 1861 → Royal Titles Act 1876 → VPA 1878 → First Factory Act 1881** → Ilbert Bill 1883 → Councils Act 1892.

## SUB 2 | Lord Curzon – Partition, Archaeology, Tibet, Hostility to Congress
[2:0] # Partition of Bengal and "divide and rule"
[2:1] - **Partition of Bengal:** decided by **Curzon**; announced 20 July 1905, in force **16 Oct 1905** – the classic example of **"Divide and Rule"** (Hindu–Muslim split).
[2:2]   - Minto II's separate electorates (1909) are the other "divide and rule" step – answer **Curzon** when the question is the strategy behind 1905.
[2:3] - **Annulled in 1911** (Hardinge II) after the Swadeshi / Vande Mataram agitation.
[2:4] # Archaeology
[2:5] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [2:5.0] 1784 | Asiatic Society (William Jones)
  [2:5.1] 1861 | Canning made Alexander Cunningham Archaeological Surveyor – ASI begun
  [2:5.2] 1871 | ASI a separate department
  [2:5.3] 1902 | Curzon revived it as a central Department of Archaeology; John Marshall Director-General
  [2:5.4] 1904 | Ancient Monuments Preservation Act – Curzon
[2:6] - **⚠ Disputed:** "Who set up the Department of Archaeology / ASI?" – exam keys (APPSC G1 2012, UPPSC) answer **Curzon**; strictly the ASI dates from **1861 under Canning**. Pick Curzon unless the question says 1861 / Cunningham.
[2:7] - **ASI today:** an **attached office of the Ministry of Culture**; motto "Pratnakirti Apavarini"; AMASR Act 1958 protects monuments over 100 years old.
[2:8] # Other Curzon measures
[2:9] - **Younghusband Mission to Tibet (1903-04)**; **Imperial Cadet Corps (1901)**; NWFP (1901); Police Commission (Frazer, 1902); Universities Commission (Raleigh, 1902) + **Indian Universities Act 1904**; Calcutta Corporation Act 1899; Dept of Commerce and Industry.
[2:10] - **On the Congress (1900):** "The Congress is **tottering to its fall**, and one of my great ambitions while in India is to assist it to a **peaceful demise**" – **Curzon** (not Dufferin, Minto or Hamilton).
[2:11] - Aloof, bureaucratic "efficiency" rule – credited in keys with the view that government should keep away from the people.
[2:12] - **Tenure 1899-1905:** the longest among Curzon, Dufferin, Hardinge, Mayo (overall longest Viceroy: **Linlithgow**, 1936-43).
[2:13] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.288, p.852; PMF Modern Indian History Part 5 p.186-187; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.4; Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.67; app notes 1:172
## SUB 3 | Hardinge II and 1911 – Durbar, Capital to Delhi, Bengal Reunited
[3:0] # The 12 December 1911 Durbar
[3:1] - **Delhi Durbar 1911:** coronation of **George V** and Queen Mary – the **only Durbar attended by the monarch** (1877 = Victoria / Lytton; 1903 = Edward VII / Curzon).
[3:2] - Announced: **Partition of Bengal annulled** (Bengal reunited **1911**); **capital moved from Calcutta to Delhi** (formal shift 1912; **New Delhi** by Lutyens and Baker, inaugurated **1931**); **Bihar and Orissa** a separate province (**1912**; Bihar alone from 1936).
[3:3] - The Viceroy who **proposed / effected** the transfer of capital = **Lord Hardinge II** (not Minto, Chelmsford, Reading, Curzon, Canning or Mayo).
[3:4] - Capital before Delhi = **Calcutta**.
[3:5] - **Gateway of India** (Bombay): marks George V's 1911 landing; **foundation stone 31 March 1913**; completed 1924.
[3:6] # Hardinge II (1910-16) – other facts
[3:7] - **Delhi Conspiracy / Hardinge Bomb Case:** bomb thrown at **Hardinge** on **23 Dec 1912** during his **state entry into Delhi** (Rash Behari Bose, Sachin Sanyal); he was wounded but did not change his attitude towards Indians.
[3:8] - Sympathetic to Indians in **South Africa**; **Gokhale's 1912** South Africa visit is linked with him; memoir **"My Indian Years"**; Hindu Mahasabha (1915).
[3:9] # Colonial architecture
[3:10] - Early 20th century **Indo-Saracenic / Indo-European hybrid** style (New Delhi, public buildings): by integrating Indian and European styles the British sought to show they were **legitimate rulers** of India – both statements true, II explains I.
[3:11] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.852; PMF Modern Indian History Part 5 p.187; PMF Modern Indian History Part 4 p.74; PMF Art & Culture Part 4 (Gateway of India); NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.5; app notes 1:172
