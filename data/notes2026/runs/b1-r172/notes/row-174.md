=== Charter Act 1813 – End of India Trade Monopoly, Education, Missionaries
- **Trade:** ended the Company's **monopoly of Indian trade** (thrown open to all British merchants) **except tea, opium and the China trade** – a statement "abolished the monopoly **including its tea trade**" is false.
- **Sovereignty:** asserted the **sovereignty of the British Crown** over the Company's Indian territories; charter renewed for **20 years**.
- **Education:** at least **Rs 1 lakh a year** for the revival of literature and promotion of science among Indians – first State responsibility for education (the chief reason the Act is called important).
- **Missionaries** allowed to come to India and preach (Wilberforce, Charles Grant).
- Courts in India got more power over European British subjects; local governments could tax persons; separate commercial and territorial accounts.
- **UPSC 2019:** (1) monopoly ended except tea and China ✓ (2) Crown sovereignty ✓ (3) "revenues now controlled by Parliament" ✗ → **1 and 2 only**.
> Which Act ended the Company's India trade monopoly? **1813** (1833 ended the tea/China monopoly).

=== Charter Act 1833 – Governor-General of India and Centralisation
- **GG of Bengal → Governor-General of India** (first: **Lord William Bentinck**) – the **final step towards centralisation**; GG-in-Council got exclusive law-making for all British India; laws called **Acts** (earlier Regulations).
- **Trade:** ended the remaining monopoly in **tea and the China trade**; Company became a purely **administrative** body; Europeans free to settle.
- **Law Member** added as **4th member**: **T.B. Macaulay** (a Briton) – **no Indian Law Member** was provided; **First Law Commission (1834)** under **Macaulay** to codify Indian laws (IPC).
- **Section 87:** no Indian to be barred from office by religion, birth, descent or colour; slavery to be abolished (done 1843).
- Territories held **"in trust for His Majesty"** (Laxmikanth); the 1853 Act repeated it without a term.
- **Agra Presidency:** almost all of present UP separated from Bengal Presidency in **1834** → North-Western Provinces (1836).
- **Macaulay** (Commons, 1833 Bill): "The Company is an **anomaly** but it is part of a system where everything is an anomaly".

=== Charter Act 1853 – Legislative Council and Open Competition
- **Last** Charter Act; Company's rule renewed **with no fixed term** ("in trust" for the Crown till Parliament provides otherwise).
- **First functioning Legislative Council:** legislative and executive functions separated; **6 new legislative members** (4 from **Madras, Bombay, Bengal, Agra** + **Chief Justice** + one Calcutta judge) → Central Legislative Council of 12, a **"mini-Parliament"**.
- **Civil services:** Directors' patronage ended → **open competitive examination** (the Act of **1853**; not 1833, 1858 or 1882); **Macaulay Committee 1854**.
- **Imperial Legislative Council** (under the Indian Councils Act) was British India's legislature from **1861** to 1947.

=== Charter Act 1793, Centralisation Trend and Persons
- **1793:** charter and trade monopoly renewed **20 years**; override power to all GGs and Governors; Board of Control paid from **Indian revenues**.
| Act | Step |
|---|---|
| Regulating Act 1773 | GG of Bengal; presidencies subordinated |
| Pitt's India Act 1784 | Board of Control |
| Charter Act 1833 | GG of India – peak of centralisation |
| Charter Act 1853 | Legislative Council with provincial members |
| Indian Councils Act 1861 | Legislative power restored to Bombay and Madras |
- **Persons:** Raja Ram Mohan Roy – first demand for Indians in services (1832); Fort William College (Wellesley, 1800) → Haileybury (1806); Macaulay's Minute (1835); Law Commission 1834-38.
- **Chronology:** Plassey 1757 → Buxar 1764 → Diwani 1765 → 1773 → 1784 → 1813 → 1858.
