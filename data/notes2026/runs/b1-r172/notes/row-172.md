=== Mayo, Lawrence and Dufferin – Assassination, Census, Shimla, Masterly Inactivity, INC
# Lord Mayo (1869-72)
- **Only Viceroy assassinated in office:** **Lord Mayo**, stabbed by the convict **Sher Ali Afridi** at Port Blair (**Andamans**) in **1872** while on tour – not Curzon, Minto, Ripon or Wellesley.
- **First census:** **1872** under **Mayo** (non-synchronous, not all areas at once); from **1881 (Ripon)** the regular decennial, synchronous census.
  - ⚠ NextGen Indian History p.3 prints "1871" – a slip; PMF and the Census give **1872**.
- **Financial decentralisation:** started by **Mayo's Resolution of 1870** (not Ripon, Dalhousie or Metcalfe).
- Also: **Rajkot College** (Kathiawar) and **Mayo College** (Ajmer) for princes; **Statistical Survey of India**; **Department of Agriculture and Commerce**; state railways; Indian Evidence Act 1872.
# Sir John Lawrence (1864-69)
- **Masterly Inactivity:** non-interference in Afghanistan – **John Lawrence** (not Bentinck, Canning or Mayo).
- **Shimla** officially declared the **summer capital** of British India by **John Lawrence in 1864**.
- Also: telegraph link with Europe (1865); High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1865); **Indian Forest Department**; Bhutan (Duar) War.
# Lord Dufferin (1884-88)
- **INC founded (Dec 1885)** under **Dufferin** – Ripon had left in 1884; Lytton, Lansdowne, Elgin II, Curzon are distractors.
- In **1887** Dufferin mocked the Congress as representing only a **"microscopic minority"** of the people.
- **Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885):** Upper Burma annexed (1 Jan 1886) = the **last major extension** of British Indian territory – Dufferin.
# Canning, Elgin I, Northbrook
- **Canning (1856-62):** last Company Governor-General and **first Viceroy** (1858); Revolt of 1857; withdrew the Doctrine of Lapse; universities at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1857); **Indian Councils Act 1861**, **portfolio system**; 3 Indians nominated (Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao).
- **British PM during the Revolt of 1857:** **Palmerston** (1855-58).
- **Elgin I (1862-63):** Wahabi movement. **Northbrook (1872-76):** abolished income tax; Kuka movement.
- **Allahabad High Court** came in **1866** – after Canning's term.

=== Lytton and Ripon – VPA, Forward Policy, Ilbert Bill, Factory Act 1881
# Lord Lytton (1876-80)
| Measure | Year |
|---|---|
| **Royal Titles Act** – Victoria "Kaiser-i-Hind" | 1876 |
| **Delhi Durbar** in honour of Queen Victoria (held during famine) | 1 Jan 1877 |
| **Vernacular Press Act**, **Arms Act** | 1878 |
| **Strachey Famine Commission** | 1878 |
| ICS maximum age cut **21 → 19**; Statutory Civil Service | 1878-79 |
| **Second Afghan War**; Treaty of Gandamak | 1878-80, 1879 |
- **Forward / aggressive policy towards Afghanistan ("proud reserve", scientific frontier):** **Lytton** – a Disraeli nominee; not Minto, Dufferin, Elgin or Mayo.
- **VPA (1878):** magistrates could demand a security bond from vernacular papers without prior government permission – passed by **Lytton**, **not Curzon**; **repealed by Ripon (1882)**.
- Lytton is **not** linked with the **Ilbert Bill** (that is Ripon).
# Lord Ripon (1880-84)
- Sent by **Gladstone's** Liberal government; repealed the **VPA (1882)**; **Local Self-Government Resolution 1882** ("Father of Local Self-Government"); **Hunter Education Commission (1882)**; first synchronous census 1881; revenue heads split into imperial, provincial, divided (1882).
- **Ilbert Bill (1883):** let Indian judges try Europeans → European agitation = **"White Mutiny"**; exposed racial discrimination in the courts.
- **Malaviya (Congress 1909):** Ripon "the greatest and most beloved Viceroy whom India has known"; Ripon favoured Indians (not British interests) and undid Lytton's illiberal acts.
# Factory Acts
| Act | Viceroy | Provisions |
|---|---|---|
| **First Factory Act, 1881** | **Ripon** | Children **below 7** barred; children 7-12 max **9 hours**; **four holidays a month**; dangerous machinery fenced |
| **Second Factory Act, 1891** | **Lansdowne** | Child age 9-14, 7 hours; women 11 hours with 1½-hour break; weekly holiday |
- The demand came from the **Lancashire** lobby; the **early nationalists (moderates) did NOT support** the Acts of 1881 and 1891 (feared loss of Indian industry's edge).
# Chronology of the Acts
- **Indian Civil Service Act 1861 → Royal Titles Act 1876 → VPA 1878 → First Factory Act 1881** → Ilbert Bill 1883 → Councils Act 1892.

=== Lord Curzon – Partition, Archaeology, Tibet, Hostility to Congress
# Partition of Bengal and "divide and rule"
- **Partition of Bengal:** decided by **Curzon**; announced 20 July 1905, in force **16 Oct 1905** – the classic example of **"Divide and Rule"** (Hindu–Muslim split).
  - Minto II's separate electorates (1909) are the other "divide and rule" step – answer **Curzon** when the question is the strategy behind 1905.
- **Annulled in 1911** (Hardinge II) after the Swadeshi / Vande Mataram agitation.
# Archaeology
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1784 | Asiatic Society (William Jones) |
| **1861** | **Canning** made **Alexander Cunningham** Archaeological Surveyor – ASI begun |
| 1871 | ASI a separate department |
| **1902** | **Curzon** revived it as a central **Department of Archaeology**; **John Marshall** Director-General |
| **1904** | **Ancient Monuments Preservation Act** – **Curzon** |
- **⚠ Disputed:** "Who set up the Department of Archaeology / ASI?" – exam keys (APPSC G1 2012, UPPSC) answer **Curzon**; strictly the ASI dates from **1861 under Canning**. Pick Curzon unless the question says 1861 / Cunningham.
- **ASI today:** an **attached office of the Ministry of Culture**; motto "Pratnakirti Apavarini"; AMASR Act 1958 protects monuments over 100 years old.
# Other Curzon measures
- **Younghusband Mission to Tibet (1903-04)**; **Imperial Cadet Corps (1901)**; NWFP (1901); Police Commission (Frazer, 1902); Universities Commission (Raleigh, 1902) + **Indian Universities Act 1904**; Calcutta Corporation Act 1899; Dept of Commerce and Industry.
- **On the Congress (1900):** "The Congress is **tottering to its fall**, and one of my great ambitions while in India is to assist it to a **peaceful demise**" – **Curzon** (not Dufferin, Minto or Hamilton).
- Aloof, bureaucratic "efficiency" rule – credited in keys with the view that government should keep away from the people.
- **Tenure 1899-1905:** the longest among Curzon, Dufferin, Hardinge, Mayo (overall longest Viceroy: **Linlithgow**, 1936-43).

=== Hardinge II and 1911 – Durbar, Capital to Delhi, Bengal Reunited
# The 12 December 1911 Durbar
- **Delhi Durbar 1911:** coronation of **George V** and Queen Mary – the **only Durbar attended by the monarch** (1877 = Victoria / Lytton; 1903 = Edward VII / Curzon).
- Announced: **Partition of Bengal annulled** (Bengal reunited **1911**); **capital moved from Calcutta to Delhi** (formal shift 1912; **New Delhi** by Lutyens and Baker, inaugurated **1931**); **Bihar and Orissa** a separate province (**1912**; Bihar alone from 1936).
- The Viceroy who **proposed / effected** the transfer of capital = **Lord Hardinge II** (not Minto, Chelmsford, Reading, Curzon, Canning or Mayo).
- Capital before Delhi = **Calcutta**.
- **Gateway of India** (Bombay): marks George V's 1911 landing; **foundation stone 31 March 1913**; completed 1924.
# Hardinge II (1910-16) – other facts
- **Delhi Conspiracy / Hardinge Bomb Case:** bomb thrown at **Hardinge** on **23 Dec 1912** during his **state entry into Delhi** (Rash Behari Bose, Sachin Sanyal); he was wounded but did not change his attitude towards Indians.
- Sympathetic to Indians in **South Africa**; **Gokhale's 1912** South Africa visit is linked with him; memoir **"My Indian Years"**; Hindu Mahasabha (1915).
# Colonial architecture
- Early 20th century **Indo-Saracenic / Indo-European hybrid** style (New Delhi, public buildings): by integrating Indian and European styles the British sought to show they were **legitimate rulers** of India – both statements true, II explains I.

=== Viceroys 1916–1947 – Rowlatt Act, Irwin, Order of Tenure
| Viceroy | Tenure | Key events |
|---|---|---|
| **Chelmsford** | 1916-21 | Home Rule; **Rowlatt Act 1919**; Jallianwala Bagh; GoI Act 1919 (**Dyarchy**); Sadler Commission |
| **Reading** | 1921-26 | Only **Jewish** Viceroy (Rufus Isaacs); Chauri Chaura; Swaraj Party; repealed Rowlatt Act (1922) |
| **Irwin** | 1926-31 | Simon Commission; Civil Disobedience (1930); Gandhi–Irwin Pact; **Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev hanged 23 Mar 1931** |
| **Willingdon** | 1931-36 | Communal Award (MacDonald, 1932); Poona Pact |
| **Linlithgow** | 1936-43 | WWII; **August Offer (1940)**; Cripps; Quit India |
| **Wavell** | 1943-47 | Wavell Plan / Simla 1945; Cabinet Mission 1946; Interim Govt (Asaf Ali – Railways); "The Viceroy's Journal" |
| **Mountbatten** | 1947-48 | Last Viceroy, first GG of free India; Independence Act 18 July 1947 |
- **Rowlatt Act** = **Chelmsford** (not Irwin, Reading, Wavell, Hardinge II or Minto II).
- **Match:** MacDonald – Communal Award; Linlithgow – August Offer; Dalhousie – Doctrine of Lapse; Chelmsford – Dyarchy.
- **Order:** Curzon (1899-1905) → **Hardinge II (1910-16)** → **Chelmsford (1916-21)** → Irwin (1926-31).
- **Full order:** Canning → Elgin I → Lawrence → Mayo → Northbrook → Lytton → Ripon → Dufferin → Lansdowne → Elgin II → Curzon → Minto II → Hardinge II → Chelmsford → Reading → Irwin → Willingdon → Linlithgow → Wavell → Mountbatten; C. Rajagopalachari = first and last Indian GG.
- **Other statement facts:** Haripura Congress (1938) = **Subhas Bose** (not C.R. Das); Swami Shraddhanand urged a no-tax protest against the Rowlatt Act.
