## SUB 0 | National Income – Meaning, NNP at Factor Cost and Formulas
[0:0] # Meaning
[0:1] - **National income (NI):** the **money value of all final goods and services** produced by the **normal residents** of a country during a year (UPSC 2013; G2 Prelims 2019) – only **final** goods, so no double counting.
[0:2]   - Distractors: "total value of goods and services produced by the nationals" (no "final"), "consumption + investment", "sum of personal incomes".
[0:3] - **Income view:** NI = **sum of factor incomes** (wages, rent, interest, profit) earned by normal residents (Police Constable 2019) – not government income, budget or PSU profits.
[0:4] - **Source:** generated only from **productive activity** (SSC 2011) – not "any money-making activity".
[0:5] - **Flow, not stock:** NI is measured over a period; **national wealth** is a stock at a point of time.
[0:6] - **Product = income:** the value of output (national product) is distributed as factor incomes, so national product and national income are **synonymous**; production by labour and capital **automatically generates income** (G1 Prelims 2024 A-R: both true, R explains A).
[0:7] # The standard measure
[0:8] - **National income = NNP at factor cost** (UPSC 1997, 2001; G2 Mains 2017; SSC 2012) – not GDP/GNP at market prices, not NDP.
[0:9] TABLE head: Route | Formula
  [0:9.0] From NNP at MP | NI = NNP(MP) − (indirect taxes − subsidies) (Chhattisgarh 2023)
  [0:9.1] From GNP at MP | NI = GNP(MP) − depreciation − indirect taxes + subsidies (UPSC 2001)
  [0:9.2] From NDP | NI = NDP(FC) + net factor income from abroad (AEE 2012)
  [0:9.3] National product at FC | = national product at MP − indirect taxes + subsidies (CDS 2012)
  [0:9.4] NNP at FC | = National Income (State PSC) – not "GNP at FC − net indirect taxes"
  [0:9.5] Worked example | GDP(MP) 110 + NFIA 10 = GNP 120; − dep 15 = NNP(MP) 105; − NIT 5 = NI 100
[0:10] > Most asked angle in this row: "National income = **NNP at factor cost**" (8 PYQs, UPSC/APPSC/SSC). Traps: GNP at MP, NDP at FC, "NNP at basic price".
[0:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.17, p.22-23 (NI = NNP at FC = NNP(MP) − indirect taxes + subsidy; flow not stock); NextGen Rocket Sheets Economy p.40 (NI = GNP(MP) − depreciation − indirect taxes + subsidies; NI = NNP at FC); Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 03 p.47; app notes 3:22 sub 2
## SUB 1 | Nominal vs Real Income; Measuring Growth
[1:0] # Nominal and real
[1:1] TABLE head: Basis | Nominal (current prices) | Real (constant prices)
  [1:1.0] Prices used | Current-year prices | Prices of a single base year
  [1:1.1] Includes price rise | Yes | No – price effect removed
  [1:1.2] Use | Size of the economy in rupees | Growth of output
[1:2] - **Real GDP** = value of total production at **prices of a single year** = GDP at **constant prices** (SSC 2006; APPSC G2 PYQ; G2 Mains flashcard).
[1:3] - **Real national income** = NI at **constant prices** (G2 2011); market value of national output in base-year prices = **GNP at constant prices** (AMVI).
[1:4] - **Real NI = nominal NI ÷ price index × 100**: base 1990-91, NI Rs 20,000 crore, index 250 → **Rs 8,000 crore** (G2 Mains 2019).
[1:5] - **Prices and money income both doubled** → real income **unchanged** (UPPCS 2004).
[1:6] - Per capita income grows **faster at current prices** than at constant prices because the constant-price series allows for the **rise in the price level** (UPSC 2000).
[1:7] - **India (UPSC 2015):** GDP at market prices in rupees has **risen steadily**; real GDP growth has **fluctuated** (e.g. contraction in 2020-21) → "Only II".
[1:8] - **Base year:** **2022-23** in the new series released **27 Feb 2026** (earlier 2011-12); it uses **double deflation** and Supply-Use Tables. GDP at current prices 2024-25 = **Rs 318.07 lakh crore** (new series).
[1:9] - Nominal growth exceeds real growth by roughly the **inflation rate**; 2025-26 estimates (old series): nominal **8.0%**, real **7.4%**.
[1:10] # Measuring economic growth
[1:11] - **Growth** = rise in **real** output: a growing country has **rising GNP at constant prices** (APRO 2008); GDP growth data is the usual yardstick (Polytechnic Lect. 2020).
[1:12] - **Best measure / sign of growth** = a **sustained increase in real per capita income** (per capita product) (UPPCS 2013; RAS 2013).
[1:13] - **Not** a growth measure: growth in **financial aid from the World Bank** (Jharkhand 2013); GDP, GNP and per capita GNP are.
[1:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Sheets Economy p.39 (real GVA: base year removes price changes); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.21 (nominal at current, real at constant prices); Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 03 p.46, p.48 (current vs constant prices); MoSPI press release 27 Feb 2026 (new series base 2022-23); app notes 3:22 sub 6, 7, 9
## SUB 2 | Personal, Disposable and Per Capita Income
[2:0] TABLE head: Measure | Derivation
  [2:0.0] Personal income (PI) | NI − undistributed corporate profits − social security contributions − corporate tax + government and business transfer payments + net interest paid by government
  [2:0.1] Personal disposable income (PDI) | PI − personal direct taxes − non-tax payments (fines, fees) (CDS 2019)
  [2:0.2] PI − PDI | = direct taxes (AMVI flashcard)
  [2:0.3] Per capita income (PCI) | National income ÷ population = NNP ÷ total population (SSC 2012; Endowment) – not GDP ÷ population
  [2:0.4] National savings | National disposable income − consumption
[2:1] - **PCI = average income** of a country (APPSC G2 PYQ); it shows the **standard of living** (UPPCS 2013) and **regional imbalances** (ACF).
[2:2] - **PCI falls while NI rises** when **population grows faster than NI** (G1 2023 A-R: both true, R explains A); PCI rises only when NI grows faster than population.
[2:3] - **Statements (Sachivalayam):** PCI is a better welfare measure than GNP ✓; "GNP is always higher than GDP" ✗ (India's NFIA is negative); disposable income is less than personal income ✓ → **I and III**.
[2:4] - **Chhattisgarh 2020:** NDP = GDP − depreciation (not +); PCI = NI (NNP) ÷ population (not NDP ÷ population); GDP, not NDP, is used to compare economies → **none true**.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.1-3 (PI, PDI = PI − personal tax − non-tax payments); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.17-18 (PCI = NI/population, average income); app notes 3:22 sub 6
## SUB 3 | GDP and GNP – Definitions and NFIA
[3:0] - **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** = value of **all final goods and services** produced **within the domestic territory** of a country during a year, whoever produces them (Endowment; Sachivalayam; AEE 2008).
[3:1]   - "Goods" alone or without "final" is incomplete; GDP = Q × P.
[3:2] - **GNP (Gross National Product)** = value of final goods and services produced by the **normal residents** and their property, **inside or outside** the country, in a year = **Gross National Income** (CDS 2014).
[3:3] TABLE head: Basis | GDP | GNP
  [3:3.0] Based on | Domestic territory (location) | Normal residents (nationality)
  [3:3.1] Indians earning abroad | Excluded | Included
  [3:3.2] Link | GDP = GNP − NFIA | GNP = GDP + NFIA
[3:4] - **Main difference** GDP vs GNP = **net factor income from abroad** (G2 PYQ) – not transfer payments or depreciation.
[3:5] - **NFIA** = factor income **received from** abroad − factor income **paid to** abroad; GNP = GDP + X − M (X = income of nationals abroad, M = income of foreigners at home).
[3:6] - **GDP = GNP is possible** when NFIA = 0 (e.g. a closed economy) (SSC 2009).
[3:7] - **India:** NFIA usually **negative** → GNP **below** GDP. An Indian resident's income earned in Saudi Arabia counts in India's **GNP only**.
[3:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.18-21 (GNP = GDP + X − M; closed economy GDP = GNP; GDP within geographical boundaries); NextGen Rocket Sheets Economy p.40 (GDP vs GNP table); app notes 3:22 sub 0, 1
## SUB 4 | Income Determination – Consumption, Saving, Investment, Multiplier
[4:0] TABLE head: Concept | Meaning
  [4:0.0] Consumption function | C = C̄ + cY – C = total consumption spending, C̄ = autonomous consumption, c = MPC (AP High Court)
  [4:0.1] MPC | ΔC/ΔY = slope of the consumption function (Degree Lect. 2017)
  [4:0.2] Saving | S = Y − C; MPS = ΔS/ΔY
  [4:0.3] MPC and MPS | MPC + MPS = 1 (JL/Polytechnic)
  [4:0.4] Autonomous spending | Independent of income
  [4:0.5] Induced spending / investment | Varies with the level of national income (G2 Mains 2025)
  [4:0.6] Aggregate demand | Total demand for final goods and services
  [4:0.7] Inventory | Stock of unsold goods
  [4:0.8] Multiplier (k) | k = 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/MPS – a low MPC gives a low multiplier (Asst. Comm. 2018); MPS 0.25 → k = 4
  [4:0.9] Accelerator | Induced investment ÷ change in income (J.M. Clark) – works on the change in income
  [4:0.10] Marginal efficiency of investment | Invest while MEI > interest rate: MEI 7% > 6% → profitable
[4:1] - **Match (JL):** C = C̄ + cY – consumption function; ΔC/ΔY – MPC; S = Y − C – savings; ΔS/ΔY – MPS → **A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4**.
[4:2] - **Mobilisation of domestic savings** depends on past behaviour, the **propensity to save** and **tax policy** (G2 2011).
[4:3] - Gross vs net investment: net = gross − depreciation; gross investment that only covers depreciation → net investment **zero**.
[4:4] - Realised investment = planned + unplanned (unintended change in inventories); equilibrium where planned saving = planned investment.
[4:5] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 04 p.77-79 (consumption function C = C̄ + cY, MPC, autonomous consumption); Ramesh Singh 10th ed. glossary (induced investment); app notes 3:22 sub 11
## SUB 5 | Gross to Net – Depreciation, NDP and NNP
[5:0] - **Depreciation** (consumption of fixed capital) = loss in value of **machinery** and other fixed capital through wear and tear (SSC CGL 2016) – deducted because output that only replaces worn-out capital is **not new output**.
[5:1] TABLE head: Aggregate | Formula
  [5:1.0] NNP | GNP − depreciation (SSC 2011, 2015)
  [5:1.1] GNP − NNP | = depreciation (G2 Gazetted 2006)
  [5:1.2] NNP at MP | GNP at MP − depreciation (RAS 2021)
  [5:1.3] NDP | GDP − depreciation (not plus)
  [5:1.4] NNP | = GDP + NFIA − depreciation (both forms correct – Uttarakhand 2021)
  [5:1.5] GNP | NNP + depreciation
[5:2] - **Incorrect combination (NDA 2015):** "NDP(FC) = GDP(MP) − depreciation" – moving to factor cost also needs removing net indirect taxes.
[5:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.21-22 (NNP = GNP − depreciation; why depreciation is deducted); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.5 (consumption of fixed capital); NextGen Rocket Sheets Economy p.40 (NNP = GNP − depreciation, GNP(MP) − depreciation = NNP(MP)); app notes 3:22 sub 2
## SUB 6 | PPP and India's World Rank
[6:0] - **PPP** exchange rates compare the **prices of the same basket** of goods and services in different countries (UPSC 2019 statement I ✓) – estimated under the **International Comparison Program (ICP)** of the UNSD; **World Bank** = global implementing agency; **NSO** = India's nodal agency.
[6:1] - India's PPP GDP is much larger than its market-rate GDP because **prices are lower in India**.
[6:2] TABLE head: Measure | India's rank
  [6:2.0] PPP GDP (World Bank ICP 2021; IMF 2017) | 3rd, after China and the USA – not 6th (UPSC 2019 statement II ✗)
  [6:2.1] Nominal GDP, 2025 | 4th (overtook Japan)
  [6:2.2] Nominal GDP, IMF WEO April 2026 | 6th (USD 4.15 trillion, behind Japan and the UK – weaker rupee and new base year)
[6:3] - **Older facts:** **Japan** was the third largest economy (PPP) when UPPCS 2007 asked; highest per capita income in **World Development Report 2000** = **Luxembourg** (USD 44,640); 2024 highest per capita GNI (PPP) = **Singapore**.
[6:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 15 p.11 (PPP rates vs market exchange rates); Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 03 p.45 (PPP); World Bank ICP 2021 results (icp.worldbank.org); IMF WEO April 2026 (reported by Indian Express, 21 Apr 2026); app notes 3:22 sub 9
## SUB 7 | Factor Cost vs Market Price – Indirect Taxes and Subsidies
[7:0] - **MP = FC + indirect taxes − subsidies**; **FC = MP − indirect taxes + subsidies** → **GDP at FC = GDP at MP − indirect taxes + subsidies** (G2 Mains flashcard).
[7:1] - **GDP at MP vs GDP at FC** differ by **indirect taxes and subsidies** (net indirect taxes) – **not direct taxes**, not depreciation (G2 PYQ; WBPSC 2020: "indirect taxes").
[7:2] - **Subsidy** = a **payment by the government to producers (the business sector)** that lowers the market price below cost (G2 2003; JL 2004) – not government purchase of goods and services (that is government expenditure).
[7:3] - Subsidies are **added** when going from MP to FC.
[7:4] TABLE head: Tax type | Meaning | Examples
  [7:4.0] Production taxes | Levied irrespective of output | Land revenue, stamp duty, property tax
  [7:4.1] Product taxes | Per unit of product | Excise, GST
[7:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.21 (GDP(FC) = GDP(MP) − indirect taxes + subsidies); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 03 p.9 ("Subsidy is a payment that a government makes to a producer"); Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 03 p.47-48 (factor cost, production vs product taxes); app notes 3:22 sub 3
## SUB 8 | Transfer Payments – Inclusions and Exclusions
[8:0] - **Transfer payments** = one-way payments with **no productive service in return** – **old age pensions**, unemployment allowance, scholarships, prizes (G2 2008) – **not included** in national income (AEE/AMVI; G2 Mains 2019).
[8:1]   - They are **added** when moving from NI to personal income.
[8:2] TABLE head: Included in NI | Excluded
  [8:2.0] Wages, rent, interest, profits, dividends | Transfer payments – pensions, scholarships, unemployment allowance
  [8:2.1] Value added at each stage | Intermediate goods (double counting)
  [8:2.2] Commission on resale (a productive service) | Second-hand sales; interest on public debt (a transfer)
[8:3] - **A-R (G2 PYQ):** "transfer earnings" are not included in NI because they are **not payments for factor services** → both true, R explains A (the question uses "transfer earnings" for transfer payments; in micro theory transfer earnings = the minimum payment to keep a factor in its present use).
[8:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.1 (transfer payments: pensions, scholarships, prizes); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.43 (transfer payments); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 02 p.25 (transfer earnings); app notes 3:22 sub 5
## SUB 9 | GVA and State Domestic Product
[9:0] - **GVA** = value added by producers and sectors – a **production-side** measure; **GDP = GVA at basic prices + taxes on products − subsidies on products**.
[9:1] TABLE head: Step | Result
  [9:1.0] GVA at factor cost + net production taxes | GVA at basic prices
  [9:1.1] GVA at basic prices + net product taxes | GVA at market prices = GDP
[9:2] - **GVA vs NVA:** GVA **includes** depreciation; **GVA − consumption of fixed capital = net value added**; at State level **GSDP − depreciation = NSDP** (G2 2011).
[9:3] - **GVA at basic prices** = headline sectoral measure since the 2011-12 series and still in the 2022-23 series; real GVA uses a base year to remove price changes.
[9:4] - **Q1 2026-27 (Sep 2026):** real GDP Rs 81.36 lakh crore vs real GVA Rs 73.82 lakh crore – the gap = **net product taxes**.
[9:5] - **State Domestic Product** = aggregate value of all goods and services produced **within the State's boundaries, counted without duplication**, during a year (AEE/AMVI – AP Socio-Economic Survey wording).
[9:6] - **Sectors for GSDP (old series):** **9 broad sectors** (G2 Mains 2017); **17 sectors and sub-sectors** in all (G2 2011).
[9:7] - **AP per capita income** is computed on **NSDP**: **Rs 2,68,653** vs India **Rs 2,00,162** (2024-25 FAE).
[9:8] - **Goa** = highest per capita income among States; **Maharashtra** = largest GSDP (Tamil Nadu 2nd, 2024-25).
[9:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Sheets Economy p.38-39 (GVA production-side; real/nominal GVA); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.5 (gross and net value added); AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.18-32 (SDP definition, per capita NSDP); app notes 3:22 sub 3, 4, 10
## SUB 10 | GDP Deflator and GNP Deflator
[10:0] - **GDP deflator = nominal GDP ÷ real GDP × 100** = ratio of GDP at **current prices to constant prices** (G2 Mains 2025) – not real ÷ nominal.
[10:1] - **GNP deflator** = nominal GNP ÷ real GNP, as a percentage (G2 PYQ).
[10:2] - **Most comprehensive measure of inflation:** covers **all goods and services in GDP**; WPI, CPI use fixed baskets.
[10:3] - A rising deflator → higher prices → **purchasing power of money falls**.
[10:4] - **Double deflation** (2022-23 series): output and input prices deflated separately for manufacturing and agriculture.
[10:5] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh 10th ed. ch.7 (GDP deflator, index p.13 of part 03); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.5-8 (inflation measures); app notes 3:22 sub 8
