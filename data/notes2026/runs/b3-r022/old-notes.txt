=== NOTES 3:22 | National Income Concepts – Gdp, Gnp, Ndp, Nnp, Factor Cost vs Market Price, Gva, Deflator
## SUB 0 | Gdp – Definition and Elements
[22:0:0] - **GDP** = market value of **all final goods and services** produced **within the geographical boundaries** of a country **during a year**, **whoever produces them** (GS 2025 key).
[22:0:1] - Three elements: **final** goods only (no intermediate goods), **domestic territory**, **time period** (one year).
[22:0:2] - **Gross** = before deducting depreciation; **Domestic** = within the country irrespective of nationality.
[22:0:3] - GDP = **Q x P**; counts production by both residents and non-residents inside the territory.
[22:0:4] - GDP is **place-based**; it does **NOT include NFIA**.
[22:0:5] - **National income** = money value of final goods and services produced (**no intermediate goods**).
[22:0:6] - **Income method**: GDP = domestic factor incomes − transfer payments.
[22:0:7] - **Hindu growth rate** is related to **GDP**.
## SUB 1 | Gnp and Gdp vs Gnp
[22:1:0] # GNP
[22:1:1] - **GNP (Gross National Product)** = total money value of final goods and services produced by the **normal residents** of a country during a period, **inside and outside** the country's boundaries.
[22:1:2] - GNP is **nationality/residence-based**.
[22:1:3] # Comparison
[22:1:4] TABLE head: Basis | GDP | GNP
  [22:1:4.0] Based on | Domestic territory | Normal residents / nationals
  [22:1:4.1] Measures | Production within the country | Production by residents within and outside the country
  [22:1:4.2] Nature | Location-based | Residence-based
  [22:1:4.3] Indians earning abroad | Excluded | Included
  [22:1:4.4] Link | GDP = GNP − NFIA | GNP = GDP + NFIA
[22:1:5] - **Main difference** between GDP and GNP = **net factor income from abroad (NFIA)**.
[22:1:6] - **NFIA** = factor income **received from abroad − factor income paid abroad**.
[22:1:7] - NFIA form: **GNP = GDP + X − M** (X = income of nationals abroad; M = income of foreigners in India).
[22:1:8] # When one exceeds the other
[22:1:9] TABLE head: Condition | Result
  [22:1:9.0] NFIA positive (residents earn more abroad than foreigners earn at home) | GNP > GDP
  [22:1:9.1] NFIA negative | GNP < GDP
  [22:1:9.2] NFIA zero | GNP = GDP
[22:1:10] - **India**: NFIA usually **negative** -> **GNP lower than GDP**.
[22:1:11] - An Indian resident's factor income earned in **Saudi Arabia** counts in India's **GNP only**, not GDP.
[22:1:12] GDP is NOT equal to GNP – GNP considers nationality (factor income from abroad).
## SUB 2 | Aggregates and Conversions
[22:2:0] # Formula table
[22:2:1] TABLE head: Aggregate | Formula
  [22:2:1.0] GNP | GDP + NFIA; also NNP + depreciation
  [22:2:1.1] NDP | GDP minus depreciation (not plus)
  [22:2:1.2] NNP | NDP + NFIA; also GNP − depreciation
  [22:2:1.3] NNP at MP | GNP at MP − depreciation
  [22:2:1.4] NNP (FC) | NNP (MP) − net indirect taxes
  [22:2:1.5] National Income | NNP at factor cost = NDP at FC + NFIA
  [22:2:1.6] National Income (long form) | GNP at MP − depreciation − indirect taxes + subsidies
  [22:2:1.7] National Income (from NNP) | NNP at MP − (indirect taxes − subsidies)
  [22:2:1.8] GDP at MP | GNP at MP − net income from abroad
  [22:2:1.9] Shortcut | NNP = GDP + NFIA − depreciation
[22:2:2] # Three bridges
[22:2:3] TABLE head: Bridge | Item | Rule
  [22:2:3.0] Domestic -> National | NFIA | GNP = GDP + NFIA
  [22:2:3.1] Gross -> Net | Depreciation | NDP = GDP − Dep; NNP = GNP − Dep
  [22:2:3.2] Market price -> Factor cost | Net indirect taxes | FC = MP − (indirect taxes − subsidies)
[22:2:4] - **Depreciation** (consumption of fixed capital) is removed because output that only replaces worn-out capital is **not new output**.
[22:2:5] # Path from GDP (MP) to National Income
[22:2:6] - 1. GDP (MP)
[22:2:7] - 2. + NFIA -> GNP (MP)
[22:2:8] - 3. − depreciation -> NNP (MP)
[22:2:9] - 4. − net indirect taxes -> **NNP (FC) = National Income**
[22:2:10] National Income = **NNP at factor cost** – the standard measure.
## SUB 3 | Factor Cost vs Market Price; Gva at Basic Prices
[22:3:0] # Factor cost vs market price
[22:3:1] - **MP = FC + indirect taxes − subsidies**; **FC = MP − indirect taxes + subsidies**.
[22:3:2] - GDP at MP and GDP at FC differ by **indirect taxes and subsidies – not direct taxes** (APPSC-G2 2025).
[22:3:3] Subsidies are **added back** when going from MP to FC; "− subsidies" in that step is the wrong sign.
[22:3:4] # Production taxes vs product taxes
[22:3:5] TABLE head: Type | Meaning | Examples
  [22:3:5.0] Production taxes | Levied irrespective of output | Land revenue, stamp duty, property tax
  [22:3:5.1] Product taxes | Levied per unit of product | Excise, sales tax/GST
[22:3:6] # Three price levels
[22:3:7] TABLE head: Step | Result
  [22:3:7.0] GVA at factor cost + net production taxes | GVA at basic prices
  [22:3:7.1] GVA at basic prices + net product taxes | GVA at market prices = GDP
[22:3:8] - **GDP = GVA + taxes on products − subsidies** (wider measure).
[22:3:9] - **GVA vs NVA**: GVA **includes** depreciation; NVA **excludes** it.
## SUB 4 | Gva – Meaning and Headline Measure
[22:4:0] - **GVA** = value added by producers and sectors – a **production-side** measure; GDP = broader measure after net taxes on products.
[22:4:1] - **Real GVA** at constant prices (base year **removes the effect of price changes**); **nominal GVA** at current prices.
[22:4:2] - **GVA at basic prices** = headline sectoral measure since the **2011-12 series**; still used in the **2022-23 series**; NSO headline growth is on GVA at basic prices.
[22:4:3] - **Sep 2026**: Q1 2026-27 real GDP **Rs 81.36 lakh crore** vs real GVA **Rs 73.82 lakh crore** (difference = net product taxes).
## SUB 5 | Inclusions and Exclusions
[22:5:0] TABLE head: Included | Excluded
  [22:5:0.0] Wages and salaries, rent, profits, dividends, interest on productive loans | Transfer payments – pensions, scholarships, unemployment allowance, prizes (no productive service in return)
  [22:5:0.1] Value added at each stage | Intermediate goods (double counting)
  [22:5:0.2] Commission on resale (payment for a productive service; NOT a transfer payment) | Second-hand sales (already counted when produced)
  [22:5:0.3]  | Interest on public debt, tax revenue (transfers, not factor payments)
  [22:5:0.4]  | Transfer earnings (not payments for factor services)
[22:5:1] - **Factor income** = wages, rent, interest, profit earned by the factors of production.
[22:5:2] - **National income at factor cost** = wages, rent, interest and profits of the country's **normal residents**.
[22:5:3] - Transfer payments are **added back** when moving from NI to personal income.
[22:5:4] APPSC-G2 2025 A-R: Transfer earnings are excluded – both A and R true, R explains A.
## SUB 6 | Personal, Disposable and Per Capita Income
[22:6:0] # Derivation steps
[22:6:1] TABLE head: Measure | Derivation
  [22:6:1.0] Personal Income | NI − undistributed corporate profits − social security contributions − corporate tax + government and business transfer payments + net interest paid by government
  [22:6:1.1] Personal Disposable Income | Personal income − personal direct taxes − non-tax payments (fines, fees) = what individuals can spend or save
  [22:6:1.2] National savings | National disposable income − consumption
  [22:6:1.3] Per Capita Income | National Income ÷ Population (APPSC 2025; GS 2025 key)
[22:6:2] - **Per capita income** = average income of a country; it rises **only when NI grows faster than population**.
[22:6:3] # Flow vs stock
[22:6:4] - **National income = flow** (over a period); **national wealth = stock** (point of time).
[22:6:5] # Growth measure
[22:6:6] - **Sign of economic growth** = sustained increase in **real per capita income**.
[22:6:7] - **Most appropriate measure** of economic growth = **per capita real income** (per capita product / per capita NNP).
[22:6:8] - Considered in growth: GDP, GNP, per capita GNP; **NOT considered: growth in financial aid from World Bank**.
## SUB 7 | Nominal vs Real Gdp
[22:7:0] TABLE head: Basis | Nominal GDP | Real GDP
  [22:7:0.0] Prices | Current-year prices | Constant / base-year prices
  [22:7:0.1] Includes price change | Yes | No
  [22:7:0.2] Better indicator of output growth | No | Yes
[22:7:1] - **Real GDP** = value of production at prices of a **single (base) year**.
[22:7:2] - **Base year now 2022-23** (earlier 2011-12).
[22:7:3] - Example: nominal GDP 1,000 -> 1,200 while price index 100 -> 120 -> **real GDP unchanged**.
[22:7:4] - **Prices and money income both doubled** -> **real income unchanged**.
[22:7:5] - Nominal growth exceeds real by roughly the **inflation rate**; 2025-26: nominal **8.0%** vs real **7.4%** (estimates, old series).
[22:7:6] - Per capita income grows faster at **current** prices than at **constant** prices, because constant prices allow for the rise in price level.
## SUB 8 | Gdp Deflator and Gnp Deflator
[22:8:0] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [22:8:0.0] GDP deflator | Nominal GDP / Real GDP x 100 = GDP at current prices / GDP at constant prices x 100 (APPSC-G2 2025)
  [22:8:0.1] GNP deflator | Ratio of nominal GNP to real GNP, as a percentage
  [22:8:0.2] Why most comprehensive | Covers all goods and services in GDP (consumption, investment, government, exports); WPI, CPI, CPI-IW use fixed baskets
  [22:8:0.3] Rising deflator | Higher prices -> purchasing power of money falls
[22:8:1] - GDP deflator = **most comprehensive measure of inflation** (G2 2025 key).
[22:8:2] - **Double deflation** (2022-23 series): deflates **output and input prices separately** for manufacturing and agriculture, replacing single deflation.
[22:8:3] - Sectoral deflators (2025-26): agriculture grew fastest -> services -> industry -> manufacturing; so manufacturing's nominal share fell while its real share stayed about **17-18%**.
## SUB 9 | New Gdp Series, Ppp and India's Rank
[22:9:0] # New series
[22:9:1] - **27 Feb 2026**: NSO released the new GDP series (**base 2022-23**) with **double deflation** and **Supply-Use Tables**.
[22:9:2] - GDP at current prices: **Rs 261.18 / 289.84 / 318.07 lakh crore** for 2022-23 / 2023-24 / 2024-25.
[22:9:3] # Purchasing Power Parity
[22:9:4] - **PPP** compares GDP by pricing a **common basket** at each country's own prices -> removes price-level differences.
[22:9:5] - Estimated under the **International Comparison Program (ICP)** of the **UNSD**; **World Bank** = global implementing agency; **NSO** = India's nodal agency.
[22:9:6] - India's **PPP GDP is much larger** than market-rate GDP because **prices are lower in India**.
[22:9:7] # India's rank
[22:9:8] TABLE head: Measure | India's rank
  [22:9:8.0] Nominal GDP, 2025 | 4th (overtook Japan; about USD 4.18 trillion)
  [22:9:8.1] Nominal GDP, IMF WEO April 2026 | 6th (USD 4.15 trillion)
  [22:9:8.2] PPP GDP | 3rd, after USA and China (ICP 2021)
[22:9:9] - Older facts: **Japan** was the third largest economy (UPPCS 2007); highest per capita income in World Development Report 2000 = **Luxembourg ($44,640)**; in 2024 **Singapore** had the highest per capita GNI (PPP).
## SUB 10 | State Domestic Product and Ap
[22:10:0] - **State Domestic Product** (APSES wording, asked word for word in APPSC-GS 2025) = aggregate economic value of all goods and services produced **within the State's boundaries**, **counted without duplication**, during a specified period, usually a year.
[22:10:1] - **AP per capita income** is computed on **NSDP**: **Rs 2,68,653** vs India **Rs 2,00,162** (2024-25 FAE).
[22:10:2] - Per capita GSDP = gross; per capita income = net -> compare gross with gross, net with net.
[22:10:3] - **Goa** = highest per capita income among Indian States; **Bihar** had the highest GSDP growth in 2010-11 among the States given.
[22:10:4] - **Largest State economy (2024-25):** **Maharashtra** has the largest GSDP among Indian States; **Tamil Nadu** is second.
## SUB 11 | Investment Concepts – Saving, Multiplier, Accelerator
[22:11:0] TABLE head: Concept | Meaning
  [22:11:0.0] Autonomous investment | Independent of income; driven by outside factors – new product, new technique, new market
  [22:11:0.1] Induced investment | Rises with income / demand for consumer goods
  [22:11:0.2] Gross vs net investment | Net = gross − depreciation; net addition to capital stock
  [22:11:0.3] Planned (ex-ante) vs unplanned | Unplanned = unintended change in inventories; realised = planned + unplanned
  [22:11:0.4] Saving-investment link | Equilibrium income where planned saving = planned investment
  [22:11:0.5] Multiplier (k) | k = 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/MPS
  [22:11:0.6] Accelerator | Induced investment ÷ change in income; linked to J.M. Clark
[22:11:1] - If planned saving > planned investment, unsold goods pile up, output and income fall until saving = investment.
[22:11:2] - Gross investment that only covers depreciation -> **net investment zero**.
[22:11:3] Multiplier works on **MPC/MPS**; accelerator works on the **change in income**, not its level.
