=== National Income – Meaning, NNP at Factor Cost and Formulas
# Meaning
- **National income (NI):** the **money value of all final goods and services** produced by the **normal residents** of a country during a year (UPSC 2013; G2 Prelims 2019) – only **final** goods, so no double counting.
  - Distractors: "total value of goods and services produced by the nationals" (no "final"), "consumption + investment", "sum of personal incomes".
- **Income view:** NI = **sum of factor incomes** (wages, rent, interest, profit) earned by normal residents (Police Constable 2019) – not government income, budget or PSU profits.
- **Source:** generated only from **productive activity** (SSC 2011) – not "any money-making activity".
- **Flow, not stock:** NI is measured over a period; **national wealth** is a stock at a point of time.
- **Product = income:** the value of output (national product) is distributed as factor incomes, so national product and national income are **synonymous**; production by labour and capital **automatically generates income** (G1 Prelims 2024 A-R: both true, R explains A).
# The standard measure
- **National income = NNP at factor cost** (UPSC 1997, 2001; G2 Mains 2017; SSC 2012) – not GDP/GNP at market prices, not NDP.
| Route | Formula |
|---|---|
| From NNP at MP | **NI = NNP(MP) − (indirect taxes − subsidies)** (Chhattisgarh 2023) |
| From GNP at MP | **NI = GNP(MP) − depreciation − indirect taxes + subsidies** (UPSC 2001) |
| From NDP | **NI = NDP(FC) + net factor income from abroad** (AEE 2012) |
| National product at FC | **= national product at MP − indirect taxes + subsidies** (CDS 2012) |
| NNP at FC | **= National Income** (State PSC) – not "GNP at FC − net indirect taxes" |
| Worked example | GDP(MP) 110 + NFIA 10 = GNP 120; − dep 15 = NNP(MP) 105; − NIT 5 = **NI 100** |
> Most asked angle in this row: "National income = **NNP at factor cost**" (8 PYQs, UPSC/APPSC/SSC). Traps: GNP at MP, NDP at FC, "NNP at basic price".
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.17, p.22-23 (NI = NNP at FC = NNP(MP) − indirect taxes + subsidy; flow not stock); NextGen Rocket Sheets Economy p.40 (NI = GNP(MP) − depreciation − indirect taxes + subsidies; NI = NNP at FC); Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 03 p.47; app notes 3:22 sub 2

=== Nominal vs Real Income; Measuring Growth
# Nominal and real
| Basis | Nominal (current prices) | Real (constant prices) |
|---|---|---|
| Prices used | Current-year prices | Prices of a **single base year** |
| Includes price rise | Yes | **No** – price effect removed |
| Use | Size of the economy in rupees | **Growth of output** |
- **Real GDP** = value of total production at **prices of a single year** = GDP at **constant prices** (SSC 2006; APPSC G2 PYQ; G2 Mains flashcard).
- **Real national income** = NI at **constant prices** (G2 2011); market value of national output in base-year prices = **GNP at constant prices** (AMVI).
- **Real NI = nominal NI ÷ price index × 100**: base 1990-91, NI Rs 20,000 crore, index 250 → **Rs 8,000 crore** (G2 Mains 2019).
- **Prices and money income both doubled** → real income **unchanged** (UPPCS 2004).
- Per capita income grows **faster at current prices** than at constant prices because the constant-price series allows for the **rise in the price level** (UPSC 2000).
- **India (UPSC 2015):** GDP at market prices in rupees has **risen steadily**; real GDP growth has **fluctuated** (e.g. contraction in 2020-21) → "Only II".
- **Base year:** **2022-23** in the new series released **27 Feb 2026** (earlier 2011-12); it uses **double deflation** and Supply-Use Tables. GDP at current prices 2024-25 = **Rs 318.07 lakh crore** (new series).
- Nominal growth exceeds real growth by roughly the **inflation rate**; 2025-26 estimates (old series): nominal **8.0%**, real **7.4%**.
# Measuring economic growth
- **Growth** = rise in **real** output: a growing country has **rising GNP at constant prices** (APRO 2008); GDP growth data is the usual yardstick (Polytechnic Lect. 2020).
- **Best measure / sign of growth** = a **sustained increase in real per capita income** (per capita product) (UPPCS 2013; RAS 2013).
- **Not** a growth measure: growth in **financial aid from the World Bank** (Jharkhand 2013); GDP, GNP and per capita GNP are.
~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Sheets Economy p.39 (real GVA: base year removes price changes); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.21 (nominal at current, real at constant prices); Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 03 p.30-33; MoSPI press release 27 Feb 2026 (new series base 2022-23); app notes 3:22 sub 6, 7, 9

=== Personal, Disposable and Per Capita Income
| Measure | Derivation |
|---|---|
| **Personal income (PI)** | NI − undistributed corporate profits − social security contributions − corporate tax + government and business **transfer payments** + net interest paid by government |
| **Personal disposable income (PDI)** | **PI − personal direct taxes − non-tax payments (fines, fees)** (CDS 2019) |
| PI − PDI | = **direct taxes** (AMVI flashcard) |
| **Per capita income (PCI)** | **National income ÷ population** = NNP ÷ total population (SSC 2012; Endowment) – not GDP ÷ population |
| National savings | National disposable income − consumption |
- **PCI = average income** of a country (APPSC G2 PYQ); it shows the **standard of living** (UPPCS 2013) and **regional imbalances** (ACF).
- **PCI falls while NI rises** when **population grows faster than NI** (G1 2023 A-R: both true, R explains A); PCI rises only when NI grows faster than population.
- **Statements (Sachivalayam):** PCI is a better welfare measure than GNP ✓; "GNP is always higher than GDP" ✗ (India's NFIA is negative); disposable income is less than personal income ✓ → **I and III**.
- **Chhattisgarh 2020:** NDP = GDP − depreciation (not +); PCI = NI (NNP) ÷ population (not NDP ÷ population); GDP, not NDP, is used to compare economies → **none true**.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.1-3 (PI, PDI = PI − personal tax − non-tax payments); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.17-18 (PCI = NI/population, average income); app notes 3:22 sub 6

=== GDP and GNP – Definitions and NFIA
- **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** = value of **all final goods and services** produced **within the domestic territory** of a country during a year, whoever produces them (Endowment; Sachivalayam; AEE 2008).
  - "Goods" alone or without "final" is incomplete; GDP = Q × P.
- **GNP (Gross National Product)** = value of final goods and services produced by the **normal residents** and their property, **inside or outside** the country, in a year = **Gross National Income** (CDS 2014).
| Basis | GDP | GNP |
|---|---|---|
| Based on | Domestic territory (location) | Normal residents (nationality) |
| Indians earning abroad | Excluded | Included |
| Link | **GDP = GNP − NFIA** | **GNP = GDP + NFIA** |
- **Main difference** GDP vs GNP = **net factor income from abroad** (G2 PYQ) – not transfer payments or depreciation.
- **NFIA** = factor income **received from** abroad − factor income **paid to** abroad; GNP = GDP + X − M (X = income of nationals abroad, M = income of foreigners at home).
- **GDP = GNP is possible** when NFIA = 0 (e.g. a closed economy) (SSC 2009).
- **India:** NFIA usually **negative** → GNP **below** GDP. An Indian resident's income earned in Saudi Arabia counts in India's **GNP only**.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.18-21 (GNP = GDP + X − M; closed economy GDP = GNP; GDP within geographical boundaries); NextGen Rocket Sheets Economy p.40 (GDP vs GNP table); app notes 3:22 sub 0, 1

=== Income Determination – Consumption, Saving, Investment, Multiplier
| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Consumption function** | **C = C̄ + cY** – C = **total consumption spending**, C̄ = autonomous consumption, c = MPC (AP High Court) |
| **MPC** | ΔC/ΔY = **slope of the consumption function** (Degree Lect. 2017) |
| Saving | **S = Y − C**; **MPS = ΔS/ΔY** |
| MPC and MPS | **MPC + MPS = 1** (JL/Polytechnic) |
| **Autonomous spending** | Independent of income |
| **Induced spending / investment** | Varies with the **level of national income** (G2 Mains 2025) |
| Aggregate demand | Total demand for final goods and services |
| Inventory | Stock of unsold goods |
| **Multiplier (k)** | **k = 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/MPS** – a **low MPC gives a low multiplier** (Asst. Comm. 2018); MPS 0.25 → k = 4 |
| Accelerator | Induced investment ÷ change in income (J.M. Clark) – works on the **change** in income |
| **Marginal efficiency of investment** | Invest while MEI > interest rate: MEI 7% > 6% → **profitable** |
- **Match (JL):** C = C̄ + cY – consumption function; ΔC/ΔY – MPC; S = Y − C – savings; ΔS/ΔY – MPS → **A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4**.
- **Mobilisation of domestic savings** depends on past behaviour, the **propensity to save** and **tax policy** (G2 2011).
- Gross vs net investment: net = gross − depreciation; gross investment that only covers depreciation → net investment **zero**.
- Realised investment = planned + unplanned (unintended change in inventories); equilibrium where planned saving = planned investment.
~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 04 (Macroeconomics ch.4 consumption function, MPC, MPS, multiplier); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 (propensity to consume); Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 03 (induced investment); app notes 3:22 sub 11

=== Gross to Net – Depreciation, NDP and NNP
- **Depreciation** (consumption of fixed capital) = loss in value of **machinery** and other fixed capital through wear and tear (SSC CGL 2016) – deducted because output that only replaces worn-out capital is **not new output**.
| Aggregate | Formula |
|---|---|
| **NNP** | **GNP − depreciation** (SSC 2011, 2015) |
| **GNP − NNP** | = **depreciation** (G2 Gazetted 2006) |
| **NNP at MP** | **GNP at MP − depreciation** (RAS 2021) |
| **NDP** | **GDP − depreciation** (not plus) |
| **NNP** | **= GDP + NFIA − depreciation** (both forms correct – Uttarakhand 2021) |
| GNP | NNP + depreciation |
- **Incorrect combination (NDA 2015):** "NDP(FC) = GDP(MP) − depreciation" – moving to factor cost also needs removing net indirect taxes.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.21-22 (NNP = GNP − depreciation; why depreciation is deducted); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.5 (consumption of fixed capital); NextGen Rocket Sheets Economy p.40 (NNP = GNP − depreciation, GNP(MP) − depreciation = NNP(MP)); app notes 3:22 sub 2

=== PPP and India's World Rank
- **PPP** exchange rates compare the **prices of the same basket** of goods and services in different countries (UPSC 2019 statement I ✓) – estimated under the **International Comparison Program (ICP)** of the UNSD; **World Bank** = global implementing agency; **NSO** = India's nodal agency.
- India's PPP GDP is much larger than its market-rate GDP because **prices are lower in India**.
| Measure | India's rank |
|---|---|
| **PPP GDP** (World Bank ICP 2021; IMF 2017) | **3rd**, after **China and the USA** – not 6th (UPSC 2019 statement II ✗) |
| Nominal GDP, 2025 | 4th (overtook Japan) |
| Nominal GDP, IMF WEO April 2026 | **6th** (USD 4.15 trillion, behind Japan and the UK – weaker rupee and new base year) |
- **Older facts:** **Japan** was the third largest economy (PPP) when UPPCS 2007 asked; highest per capita income in **World Development Report 2000** = **Luxembourg** (USD 44,640); 2024 highest per capita GNI (PPP) = **Singapore**.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 15 p.1 (PPP); Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 03 (PPP, ICP); World Bank ICP 2021 results (icp.worldbank.org); IMF WEO April 2026 (reported by Indian Express, 21 Apr 2026); app notes 3:22 sub 9

=== Factor Cost vs Market Price – Indirect Taxes and Subsidies
- **MP = FC + indirect taxes − subsidies**; **FC = MP − indirect taxes + subsidies** → **GDP at FC = GDP at MP − indirect taxes + subsidies** (G2 Mains flashcard).
- **GDP at MP vs GDP at FC** differ by **indirect taxes and subsidies** (net indirect taxes) – **not direct taxes**, not depreciation (G2 PYQ; WBPSC 2020: "indirect taxes").
- **Subsidy** = a **payment by the government to producers (the business sector)** that lowers the market price below cost (G2 2003; JL 2004) – not government purchase of goods and services (that is government expenditure).
- Subsidies are **added** when going from MP to FC.
| Tax type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Production taxes | Levied irrespective of output | Land revenue, **stamp duty**, property tax |
| Product taxes | Per unit of product | Excise, **GST** |
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 11 p.21 (GDP(FC) = GDP(MP) − indirect taxes + subsidies); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 03 p.9 ("Subsidy is a payment that a government makes to a producer"); Ramesh Singh 10th ed. part 03 p.47-48 (factor cost, production vs product taxes); app notes 3:22 sub 3

=== Transfer Payments – Inclusions and Exclusions
- **Transfer payments** = one-way payments with **no productive service in return** – **old age pensions**, unemployment allowance, scholarships, prizes (G2 2008) – **not included** in national income (AEE/AMVI; G2 Mains 2019).
  - They are **added** when moving from NI to personal income.
| Included in NI | Excluded |
|---|---|
| Wages, rent, interest, profits, dividends | Transfer payments – pensions, scholarships, unemployment allowance |
| Value added at each stage | Intermediate goods (double counting) |
| Commission on resale (a productive service) | Second-hand sales; interest on public debt (a transfer) |
- **A-R (G2 PYQ):** "transfer earnings" are not included in NI because they are **not payments for factor services** → both true, R explains A (the question uses "transfer earnings" for transfer payments; in micro theory transfer earnings = the minimum payment to keep a factor in its present use).
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.1 (transfer payments: pensions, scholarships, prizes); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.43 (transfer payments); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 02 p.25 (transfer earnings); app notes 3:22 sub 5

=== GVA and State Domestic Product
- **GVA** = value added by producers and sectors – a **production-side** measure; **GDP = GVA at basic prices + taxes on products − subsidies on products**.
| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| GVA at factor cost + net production taxes | **GVA at basic prices** |
| GVA at basic prices + net product taxes | GVA at market prices = **GDP** |
- **GVA vs NVA:** GVA **includes** depreciation; **GVA − consumption of fixed capital = net value added**; at State level **GSDP − depreciation = NSDP** (G2 2011).
- **GVA at basic prices** = headline sectoral measure since the 2011-12 series and still in the 2022-23 series; real GVA uses a base year to remove price changes.
- **Q1 2026-27 (Sep 2026):** real GDP Rs 81.36 lakh crore vs real GVA Rs 73.82 lakh crore – the gap = **net product taxes**.
- **State Domestic Product** = aggregate value of all goods and services produced **within the State's boundaries, counted without duplication**, during a year (AEE/AMVI – AP Socio-Economic Survey wording).
- **Sectors for GSDP (old series):** **9 broad sectors** (G2 Mains 2017); **17 sectors and sub-sectors** in all (G2 2011).
- **AP per capita income** is computed on **NSDP**: **Rs 2,68,653** vs India **Rs 2,00,162** (2024-25 FAE).
- **Goa** = highest per capita income among States; **Maharashtra** = largest GSDP (Tamil Nadu 2nd, 2024-25).
~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Sheets Economy p.38-39 (GVA production-side; real/nominal GVA); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.5 (gross and net value added); AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.18-32 (SDP definition, per capita NSDP); app notes 3:22 sub 3, 4, 10

=== GDP Deflator and GNP Deflator
- **GDP deflator = nominal GDP ÷ real GDP × 100** = ratio of GDP at **current prices to constant prices** (G2 Mains 2025) – not real ÷ nominal.
- **GNP deflator** = nominal GNP ÷ real GNP, as a percentage (G2 PYQ).
- **Most comprehensive measure of inflation:** covers **all goods and services in GDP**; WPI, CPI use fixed baskets.
- A rising deflator → higher prices → **purchasing power of money falls**.
- **Double deflation** (2022-23 series): output and input prices deflated separately for manufacturing and agriculture.
