## SUB 0 | Himalayan Drainage – Character and River Groups
[36:0:0] # Four Groups of Indian Rivers
[36:0:1] TABLE head: Group | Example
  [36:0:1.0] Himalayan | Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra
  [36:0:1.1] Deccan (Peninsular) | Godavari, Krishna, Mahanadi
  [36:0:1.2] Coastal | Short west-coast streams
  [36:0:1.3] Inland drainage | Western Rajasthan – Ghaggar, Luni, Rupen
[36:0:2] - **Inland drainage**: the river does not reach the sea; it ends in an inland lake or sand. River **Khari** is part of **indeterminate drainage**.
[36:0:3] # Character of Himalayan Rivers
[36:0:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [36:0:4.0] Antecedent | Rivers existed before the Himalayan uplift; kept their course by cutting deep gorges. Features: valley-in-valley, paired terraces, entrenched meanders
  [36:0:4.1] Perennial | Fed by rain + snowmelt
  [36:0:4.2] Plains behaviour | Gentle slope → meanders, shifting channels, ox-bow lakes, floodplains
[36:0:5] - Antecedent examples: **Indus** (gorge at Nanga Parbat), **Sutlej** (cuts across all Himalayan ranges; a **Trans-Himalayan** river), Brahmaputra (Dihang gorge).
[36:0:6] - In the APPSC key, the **Yamuna** was also taken as an antecedent river older than the Himalayas.
[36:0:7] APPSC A-R: "Himalayan rivers are perennial" (A) because "they are fed by rain and snowmelt" (R) – both true, R explains A.

## SUB 2 | Indus System
[36:2:0] # Course
[36:2:1] - Rises at **Singge Khabab** near Mansarovar; flows west between the **Ladakh and Zaskar** ranges.
[36:2:2] - **Leh** lies on its **right bank** (UT of Ladakh since **31 Oct 2019**); **Zanskar** joins at **Nimu**.
[36:2:3] - Turns south at **Nanga Parbat** through an antecedent gorge → **Arabian Sea near Karachi**; flows through north-western India and Pakistan.
[36:2:4] # Tributaries (west to east) and Ancient Names
[36:2:5] TABLE head: River | Greek | Vedic | Source / note
  [36:2:5.0] Jhelum | Hydaspes | Vitasta (Kashmiri: Vyeth) | Spring at Verinag, foot of Pir Panjal; Srinagar, Wular Lake
  [36:2:5.1] Chenab | Acesines | Asikni | Largest tributary; Chandra + Bhaga at Tandi (HP)
  [36:2:5.2] Ravi | Hydraotes | Parushni | Joins Chenab in Pakistan
  [36:2:5.3] Beas | Hyphasis | Vipasa | Beas Kund, Rohtang; joins Sutlej at Harike (Punjab)
  [36:2:5.4] Sutlej | Zaradros | Shutudri | Rakas Tal; receives Chenab
[36:2:6] - Chain: **Jhelum + Ravi → Chenab → Sutlej → Indus**. Only the **Sutlej** joins the Indus directly.
[36:2:7] - Tributaries of Sutlej: **Baspa, Spiti, Beas**. Ravi is a tributary of the Chenab, only a sub-tributary of the Sutlej.
[36:2:8] - **Spiti** rises at Kunzam La; joins Sutlej near **Khab** (Kinnaur). Ludhiana and Firozpur lie on the Sutlej's old bank.
[36:2:9] - **Kishanganga** = **Neelum** in Pakistan; main tributary of the **Jhelum** (not Sutlej).
[36:2:10] - **Chandra** rises near Chandra Tal, **Bhaga** near Bara Lacha La; both flow in **Lahaul** valley → Chandrabhaga.
[36:2:11] - Rivers of Himachal Pradesh: Beas, Chenab, Ravi, Sutlej, Yamuna. **Mahanadi** is not an Indus tributary.
[36:2:12] - Sutlej facts: antecedent, flows through **three countries**, rises on the Tibetan Plateau, important for irrigation.
[36:2:13] # Indus Waters Treaty, 1960
[36:2:14] TABLE head: Rivers | Allotted to
  [36:2:14.0] Eastern – Ravi, Beas, Sutlej | India
  [36:2:14.1] Western – Indus, Jhelum, Chenab | Mainly Pakistan
[36:2:15] - **Broker:** the Indus Waters Treaty (1960) was brokered by the **World Bank**.
[36:2:16] - **Tulbul** (navigation) project – Jhelum, mouth of Wular Lake; proposed **1984**, Pakistan protested **1987**.
[36:2:17] - **Salal** – 690 MW, **NHPC**, Chenab – India's first project in the region under the treaty (Jan 2026).
[36:2:18] - **Baglihar** – Chenab, **Ramban** district; run-of-the-river; Pakistan took it to the **World Bank**.
[36:2:19] - **Dulhasti** – 390 MW, Chenab, **Kishtwar** district.
[36:2:20] - **World's highest arch bridge** is on the Chenab; highest bridge in all categories is Duge Bridge, China.
[36:2:21] - **Order of the Punjab rivers (north to south / west to east):** Jhelum → Chenab → Ravi → Beas → Sutlej.
## SUB 3 | Ganga – Course
[36:3:0] # Headwaters and Course
[36:3:1] - 1. **Bhagirathi** – Gangotri glacier at **Gomukh**, **Uttarkashi** district; cuts through Central and Lesser Himalayas.
[36:3:2] - 2. **Alaknanda** – **Satopanth** glacier above Badrinath.
[36:3:3] - 3. Meet at **Devprayag** (Tehri Garhwal district; on Rishikesh–Badrinath route) → called **Ganga**.
[36:3:4] - 4. Plains at **Haridwar**; navigable up to **Prayagraj**, where the **Yamuna** joins.
[36:3:5] - 5. Flows past **Rajmahal hills**; at **Farakka** (WB): left arm **Bhagirathi-Hooghly**, right arm **Padma** into Bangladesh.
[36:3:6] - 6. Padma + Brahmaputra (Jamuna) + Meghna → **Bay of Bengal as the Meghna**.
[36:3:7] - **Uttarkashi** is on the **Bhagirathi**, not the Ganga; **Fatehpur, Bhagalpur, Kanpur** are on the Ganga.
[36:3:8] # Confluences (Panchprayag on the Alaknanda)

[36:4:12] - **Kaimur Range** – water divide between Yamuna and Son.
[36:4:13] - **Damodar** – **fault (rift) valley** drainage; "**Sorrow of Bengal**"; flows into the **Hugli**; main tributary **Barakar** (also Konar, Jamunia, Barki).
[36:4:14] - **Rajarappa** – confluence of Damodar and Bhera (Bhairavi); **Chinnamastika** temple, near Ramgarh.
[36:4:15] - Catchments: **Son 70,055 > Gandak 45,731 > Ramganga 30,811 > Mahananda 23,700** sq km.
[36:4:16] - **Bihar–Jharkhand MoU (2026)** on Son ended a 25-year dispute: of **7.75 MAF** under the **Bansagar Accord, 1973**, Bihar **5.75**, Jharkhand **2** MAF (1 Sep 2026).
