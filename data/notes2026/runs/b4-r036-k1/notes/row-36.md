=== Ganga Tributaries – Yamuna, Son, Damodar, Kosi and the Left Bank
# Left bank vs right bank
| Bank | Tributaries |
|---|---|
| Left (Himalayan) | **Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Mahananda** |
| Right (mostly Peninsular) | **Yamuna** (longest), **Son**, Tons, Punpun; **Damodar** joins the Hugli (right bank) |
- **Yamuna** = **westernmost and longest** tributary of the Ganga; **Son** = the largest right-bank tributary from the Peninsula.
- **Mahananda** = **last, easternmost** left-bank tributary; joins in **West Bengal**.
- **Son** is the main right-bank tributary that **flows northwards** (from Amarkantak) and **joins the Ganga directly** near **Maner (Patna)**; Gomti, Ghaghara, Kosi join from the **left**.
- **Chambal, Betwa, Ken, Sindh** join the **Yamuna**, not the Ganga; the Chambal therefore lies in the **Ganga basin** (not Narmada/Sabarmati/Godavari).
# Order of confluence below Prayagraj (west → east)
- **Gomti** (near Ghazipur, Kaithi) → **Ghaghara** (near **Chapra**, Revelganj, Saran) → **Son** (R, near Maner) → **Gandak** (Hajipur/**Sonpur**, opposite Patna) → **Kosi** (Kursela, Katihar, below Bhagalpur) → Mahananda.
> UPSC 2024 (asked twice): Himalayan rivers joining below Prayagraj, west → east = **Gomti – Ghaghara – Gandak – Kosi**.
# Left-bank (Himalayan) tributaries
| River | Facts |
|---|---|
| Ramganga | Rises in the **Dudhatoli** ranges (Pauri Garhwal, near Nainital); joins near **Kannauj**/Farrukhabad; **Kalagarh Dam**; a **Ganga** tributary, **not the Yamuna's** |
| Ghaghara | **Mapchachungo glacier** / Gurla Mandhata, south of Mansarovar (Tibet); **Karnali** in Nepal; receives the **Sharda** (called **Kali/Mahakali** in its hill course); joins at **Revelganj (Chapra)** |
| Gandak | **Narayani** / Gandaki in Nepal; **Sadanira** of the Shatapatha Brahmana; streams **Kali Gandak** and Trishuli; enters India at **Valmikinagar** (not Raxaul); joins at Hajipur/Sonpur |
| Kosi | **"Sorrow of Bihar"**; **maximum shifting of course**; seven headstreams – **Arun** most important (Sun Kosi, Tamur Kosi); **changed course in Aug 2008** (Kusaha breach) → Bihar flood calamity |
| Gomti | Rises at **Gomat Taal (Fulhar lake), Pilibhit** – the **only Ganga tributary rising in the plains**; Lucknow |
| Mahananda | Easternmost/last left-bank tributary |
- **Kosi and glaciers:** the Bihar Kosi has glacier-fed headwaters (NextGen); in the Uttarakhand (UKPSC) question the **Kosi of Kumaon** rises near **Kausani** (rain-fed), so "Kosi" is the river **without a glacier source** – Yamuna (Yamunotri), Alaknanda (Satopanth) and Mandakini (Chorabari) are glacier-fed.
- **Mahakali Treaty** – India–**Nepal**, **Feb 1996**; Mahakali = **Sharda**; Sharda and Tanakpur barrages, Pancheshwar project.
- **Gandak Project** – India–Nepal agreement **4 Dec 1959**; Bihar and UP.
- **Catchment (sq km):** **Son ~70,055** > Gandak ~45,700 > Ramganga ~30,800 > Mahananda ~23,700.
# Yamuna and its tributaries
- Rises at **Yamunotri glacier**, western slopes of **Bandarpunch (Banderpunch)**; joins the Ganga at **Prayagraj**.
| Bank | Tributaries of the Yamuna |
|---|---|
| Right (west → east) | **Chambal** (joins in Etawah) → **Sindh** → **Betwa** (joins at **Hamirpur**) → **Ken** |
| Left | Tons, **Hindon (Hindan)**, Hanuman Ganga, Sasur Khaderi |
- **Chambal:** rises at **Janapav hills near Mhow** (Dr Ambedkar Nagar), Malwa; **deep ravines** (badlands); tributaries **Banas** (left), **Kali Sindh, Parvati, Kshipra** (right) – the **Betwa does not join the Chambal**; Gandhi Sagar, Rana Pratap Sagar, Kota.
- **Betwa** rises in the Vindhyas (Bhopal plateau) and joins the **Yamuna** at Hamirpur.
- **Yamuna between Delhi and Agra** is almost a "dead river" for much of the year because of heavy abstraction and pollution – but the Yamuna itself is a **perennial** Himalayan river.
# Son, Damodar and the Chota Nagpur rivers
- **Son:** Amarkantak → north through Baghelkhand → Ganga near **Maner (Patna)**; tributaries Johilla, Banas, Gopad, **Rihand**, Kanhar, **North Koel**.
- **Kaimur range** = water divide between the **Yamuna (via the Tons) and the Son** (MPPSC).
- **North Koel:** rises on the **Ranchi plateau**, flows through the **north of Betla NP** (Palamu), joins the **Son** → part of the **Ganga basin**; **Mandal dam** on it. **South Koel and Sankh** go to the **Brahmani** (not Ganga).
- **Damodar:** Khamarpat hill/Tori, Chota Nagpur; flows in a **rift (fault) valley**; "**Sorrow of Bengal**" (before DVC); joins the **Hugli (Hooghly)** south of Kolkata; main tributary **Barakar** (also Konar, Jamunia, Barki).
- **Rajarappa** (Ramgarh, Jharkhand) – confluence of **Damodar and Bhera (Bhairavi)**; Chhinnamasta temple.
- **Bansagar Accord, 1973** on the Son: 7.75 MAF; the **2026 Bihar–Jharkhand MoU** gives **Bihar 5.75 MAF, Jharkhand 2 MAF** (as of 2026).

=== Indus System
# Course of the Indus
- Rises at **Singge Khabab** (Bokhar Chu glacier) on the **northern slopes of the Kailash range** near Mansarovar (5,182 m); in Tibet = **Singi Khamban** ("Lion's mouth").
- Flows west between the **Ladakh and Zaskar** ranges; **Leh** lies on its **right bank** (UT of Ladakh since 31 Oct 2019); **Zanskar** joins at **Nimu**.
- Takes a southerly course at **Nanga Parbat** through a deep **antecedent gorge** → Pakistan → **Arabian Sea near Karachi**; flows through **north-western India and Pakistan**.
- Length **2,880 km** (NCERT; NextGen 2,480 km), only **709 km** in India. Himalayan tributaries: **Shyok**, Gilgit, Zaskar, Shigar, Dras, Hunza.
- **Panjnad:** the five Punjab rivers join the Indus a little **above Mithankot**: Jhelum and Ravi → Chenab; Beas → Sutlej; Sutlej + Chenab = Panjnad.
> UPSC 2021 (asked twice): the river that "joins the Indus direct" = **Sutlej** (UPSC key, per PMF); strictly the Sutlej and Chenab merge as the Panjnad.
# The five Punjab rivers
| River | Greek / Vedic | Source and facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Jhelum** | Hydaspes / **Vitasta** | **Spring at Verinag**, foot of the Pir Panjal (NextGen: Sheshnag); flows through **Srinagar** and **Wular lake**; main tributary **Kishanganga** (= **Neelum** in Pakistan); Tulbul project |
| **Chenab** | Acesines / **Asikni** | **Chandra + Bhaga** (rise near Baralacha La, flow in **Lahaul**) meet at **Tandi** (HP); **largest tributary of the Indus**; Salal, Baglihar, Dul Hasti; Tawi is a Chenab tributary |
| **Ravi** | Hydraotes / **Parushni** | Kalu hills/Rohtang, HP; joins the **Chenab** in Pakistan; **Harappa** lies on the **Ravi**; Upper Bari Doab canal |
| **Beas** | **Hyphasis** / Vipasa | **Beas Kund near Rohtang**; joins the Sutlej at **Harike**; whole course in India; **Indus river dolphin** (Beas Conservation Reserve, Punjab – the only Indian population) |
| **Sutlej** | Zaradros / Shutudri | **Rakas Tal (Rakshastal)**, Kailash range, Tibet (Langchen Khambab); enters India at **Shipki La**; **antecedent, Trans-Himalayan – cuts across all the Himalayan ranges**; tributaries **Spiti** (joins at Khab), **Baspa, Beas**; Bhakra-Nangal |
- **North → south (also west → east):** **Jhelum → Chenab → Ravi → Beas → Sutlej**.
- **North → south, upper Indus streams:** **Shyok – Zaskar – Spiti – Sutlej** (UPSC 2006).
- **Ravi** is not a tributary of the Sutlej directly (it joins the Chenab); **Mahanadi** is a Peninsular river, not an Indus tributary.
- **Doabs:** **Bist (Doaba) = Beas–Sutlej**; Bari = Beas–Ravi; Rechna = Ravi–Chenab; Chaj = Chenab–Jhelum; Sind Sagar = Jhelum/Chenab–Indus. "Panjnad/Punjab" = land of five rivers.
- **Rivers of Himachal Pradesh:** **Beas, Chenab, Ravi, Sutlej and Yamuna**.
- **Sutlej identification (UPSC 2026):** antecedent, flows through **three countries** (China, India, Pakistan), rises on the Tibetan plateau, key for irrigation, forms **no distributaries** (it ends in the Panjnad).
# Indus Waters Treaty, 1960
| Rivers | Allotted to |
|---|---|
| **Eastern – Ravi, Beas, Sutlej** | India |
| Western – Indus, Jhelum, Chenab | Mainly Pakistan |
- Signed 19 Sep 1960 (Karachi), **brokered by the World Bank**. India has held the treaty **in abeyance since 23 Apr 2025** (after the Pahalgam attack; MEA, still in force as of Jul 2026).
- **Salal** (690 MW, NHPC, Chenab) – India's first project in the region under the treaty; **Baglihar** (Chenab, Ramban); **Dul Hasti** (390 MW, Chenab, Kishtwar).
- **Chenab rail bridge** (Reasi, J&K, 359 m above the river) = **world's highest railway arch bridge**; under construction for years, **opened 6 Jun 2025**.

=== Ganga – Headwaters, Panchprayag, Course and Delta
# Headwaters
- **Bhagirathi** – **Gangotri glacier at Gomukh**, Uttarkashi district (**Uttarkashi is on the Bhagirathi**).
- **Alaknanda** – **Satopanth glacier** above Badrinath = the **second glacial source** of the Ganga; **Badrinath** lies on the **Alaknanda**, **Kedarnath** on the **Mandakini**.
- The two meet at **Devprayag** (Tehri Garhwal); the river is called the **Ganga** from Devprayag.
# Panchprayag (on the Alaknanda, upstream → downstream)
| Prayag | River joining the Alaknanda |
|---|---|
| Vishnuprayag | **Dhauliganga** |
| Nandprayag | **Nandakini** |
| **Karnaprayag** | **Pindar** |
| **Rudraprayag** | **Mandakini** |
| **Devprayag** | **Bhagirathi** → Ganga begins |
- **Prayagraj (Triveni Sangam)** = **Ganga, Yamuna and the mythical Saraswati**.
- Kedarnath is **not** one of Adi Shankaracharya's four mathas (Jyotirmath, Sringeri, Dwarka, Puri), so a statement calling it his "biggest seat" is false.
# Course
- Rises in the western Himalayas of **Uttarakhand**, enters the plains at **Haridwar**; navigable up to **Prayagraj**; towns: **Kanpur (largest city on the Ganga)**, Prayagraj, Varanasi, **Patna**, Bhagalpur.
- In Bihar the Ganga touches **12 districts**; **Patna** district has its longest stretch (about 99 km).
- At **Farakka** (WB): left arm **Bhagirathi–Hooghly**; right arm **Padma** enters Bangladesh – in Bangladesh the **Ganga = Padma**, the **Brahmaputra = Jamuna**.
- Padma + Jamuna (at **Goalundo**) + Meghna → enters the **Bay of Bengal as the Meghna**; the delta ends in the **Sundarbans**.
- **Length:** **2,525 km** – the **longest river in India** (NextGen gives 2,465 km, 2,071 km in India); basin ≈ **26%** of India's area, about 40% of its population.
# Bengal delta
- **Moribund delta** (old, inactive, Nadia–Murshidabad), **mature delta**, and **active delta** (still growing, **North and South 24 Parganas**, Sundarbans) – "moribund" is a sub-division of the **Bengal delta**.
> Statement trap: "Burhi Dihing and Dhansiri are the Ganga's left-bank tributaries" and "it finally merges with the Padma" are NCERT lines about the **Brahmaputra**, so both are false for the Ganga.

=== Character of Himalayan Rivers – Antecedent, Perennial, Inland Drainage
# Four groups of Indian rivers
| Group | Example |
|---|---|
| Himalayan | Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra |
| Deccan (Peninsular) | Godavari, Krishna, Mahanadi – **seasonal**, not perennial |
| Coastal | Short west-coast streams |
| **Inland drainage** | Western Rajasthan – **Ghaggar**, **Luni** (into the Rann; the river of the "Great (Thar) Desert"), Rupen |
- **Inland drainage:** the river does not reach the sea – e.g. **Ghaggar** (not the Ghaghara, a Ganga tributary).
- **River Khari (Rajasthan):** the main **Khari** is a **Banas tributary** (Rajsamand → Deoli) → Banas → Chambal → Yamuna → Ganga → **Bay of Bengal**; other streams named Khari feed the Luni (Arabian Sea) and Sambhar lake (inland).
# Antecedent rivers
- **Definition:** a river that **existed before the Himalayas rose** and kept its course by **cutting deep gorges** as the land rose.
- Marks: **valley-in-valley**, **paired terraces**, **entrenched (ingrown) meanders**.
- Examples: **Indus** (gorge at Nanga Parbat – the river most cited for **gorges**), **Sutlej**, **Brahmaputra** (Dihang gorge) – the **Brahmaputra is "older than the Himalayas"**; the Yamuna, Kosi and Ganga are not the answer to that question.
- The **Ganga** is usually listed as an **antecedent** river in exam keys; it rises at Gangotri **within** the Himalayas, unlike the Indus, Sutlej and Brahmaputra which rise **beyond** them in Tibet (**Trans-Himalayan**).
# Perennial character and work in the plains
- **Perennial:** fed by **rain + snowmelt** (rainfall and melting ice); Himalayan rivers are **not dry in winter**.
- In the plains: gentle slope → **meanders, shifting channels, ox-bow lakes, floodplains**, braided channels and riverine islands.
- Himalayan rivers show **all three stages** of the erosion cycle (youth, maturity, old age); **Peninsular rivers have reached maturity**.
- The Northern Plain (not the "Western plain") is formed by the interplay of the **Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra**.
> A–R pattern: "Himalayan rivers are perennial" + "Kosi changes its course" → both true, R does **not** explain A; + "Himalayas get rain from the SW monsoon" → both true, R does not explain A (the snowmelt part is missing).

=== Brahmaputra System
# Names and course
| Region | Name |
|---|---|
| Tibet | **Tsangpo / Yarlung Zangbo** / Sanpu |
| Arunachal Pradesh | **Dihang / Siang** |
| Assam | **Brahmaputra** (after the **Dibang and Lohit** join near Sadiya) |
| Bangladesh | **Jamuna** (below Dhubri); joins the Padma at **Goalundo** |
- Rises at **Chemayungdung glacier**, east of Mansarovar (Kailash range); also traced to the Angsi glacier.
- Flows east, then makes a **U-turn at Namcha Barwa** and enters India through the deep **Dihang gorge** – "**born before the Himalayas**" (antecedent).
- Length **2,900 km**, only **885 km** in India; catchment **Tibet (China), Bhutan, India, Bangladesh**; navigable **1,280 km up to Dibrugarh**.
- **"Sorrow of Assam"** – floods; **Majuli** (upper Assam) = **largest riverine island** in the world.
- **China's dams:** **Zangmu** (construction from **Nov 2010**) on the Yarlung Zangbo, Tibet.
# Tributaries
| Right bank (north, Himalayan) | Left bank (south) |
|---|---|
| **Subansiri, Kameng (Jia Bharali), Manas**, Sankosh, **Teesta** | **Burhi Dihing, Dhansiri**, Dikhu, **Kopili** |
- Also: **Dibang, Lohit** (headstreams in Arunachal). **Lohit and Subansiri** flow through **Arunachal Pradesh**; the **Barak** does not.
- **Sankosh** – rises in northern Bhutan; forms the **Assam–West Bengal** boundary.
- **Teesta** – rises at **Tso Lhamo** lake, North Sikkim (**not** the Brahmaputra's source); largest tributary **Rangeet** (rises in Sikkim); joins the **Brahmaputra in Bangladesh**, not the Bay of Bengal.
# Barak (Meghna) system
- **Barak** rises in the **Manipur hills**; the **important river of Manipur and Mizoram**; headstream of the **Meghna**.
- Tributaries of the Barak: **Katakhal, Jiri, Chiri, Madhura (Modhura)**, Sonai, Dhaleswari.

=== Sources – the Mansarovar Group and Others
# Rising near Mansarovar (Tibet) vs elsewhere
| Rise in the Mansarovar–Kailash area (Tibet) | Rise elsewhere |
|---|---|
| **Indus** – Singge Khabab | **Ganga/Bhagirathi** – Gangotri (Gomukh), within India |
| **Sutlej** – **Rakas Tal (Rakshastal)** | Yamuna – Yamunotri |
| **Brahmaputra** – Chemayungdung | Chenab – Baralacha La |
| **Karnali (Ghaghara)** | Jhelum – Verinag |
| | **Beas** – Beas Kund, Rohtang |
| | Ravi – Kalu hills/Rohtang |
- Near Mansarovar lie the sources of **all three – Brahmaputra, Sutlej and Indus**; the **Brahmaputra and Indus** rise at almost the same point.
- **Rivers not rising in Indian territory:** **Brahmaputra**, **Sutlej** (only one of Beas/Chenab/Ravi/Sutlej), **Ghaghara**; Chambal, Jhelum, Ravi, Beas, Mahanadi, Ganga, Yamuna rise in India.
- The Sutlej rises within about 80 km of the Indus source and enters India at Shipki La.
