## SUB 0 | Ozone Layer – Location, Formation and UV Shield
[0:0] # Where the ozone layer lies (asked 17 times)
[0:1] - **Ozone (O₃) = three oxygen atoms** (APPSC BC Welfare). It lies mainly in the **stratosphere** – the standard answer to "ozone layer is located in / found mainly in / maximum concentration in / rich in ozone" (UPPCS ×8, CGPSC, SSC, CDS 2009, APPSC AEE, APPSC AMVI) – not the troposphere, mesosphere, ionosphere or exosphere.
[0:2] - **Lower stratosphere:** main layer **~15–30 km**, densest **~15–35 km** (NCERT/Rocket); exam heights "**15–20 km above the surface**" (State PSC) are the accepted option – never "below the surface", never 40–50 or 70–80 km.
[0:3] - About **90%** of all ozone is **stratospheric**, ~10% tropospheric.
[0:4] - Formation (**Chapman cycle**): **UV + O₂ → O + O; O + O₂ → O₃**; O₃ absorbs UV and splits again.
[0:5] - Column ozone is measured in **Dobson Units (DU)**; 1 DU = 0.01 mm of pure ozone at 0°C, 1 atm.
[0:6] # What it does (asked 13 times)
[0:7] - **Absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation** of the Sun and acts as **Earth's shield** (APPSC FBO, APPSC ASO; MPPSC 1994 ×2; UKPSC 2002, 2006; UPPCS 2006, 2013; CGPSC 2013) – it does **not** supply oxygen, control Earth's temperature, trap heat, reflect sunlight, cause rain or stop earthquakes/monsoon failure.
[0:8] - It does **not** absorb cosmic rays, infrared, visible light or microwaves – only **UV**.
[0:9] TABLE head: UV band | Wavelength | Ozone layer
  [0:9.0] UV-A | 315–400 nm | Mostly reaches the surface
  [0:9.1] UV-B | 280–315 nm | Mostly absorbed, part reaches the ground
  [0:9.2] UV-C | 100–280 nm (shortest, most harmful) | Completely absorbed (APPSC FSO 2025)
[0:10] - Excess UV → **skin (dermal) cancer** (UPPSC 2014), **cataract**, weaker immunity, lower crop yield → ozone is **essential for biotic life** because it screens high-energy radiation (UPPSC Mains 2010: A and R true, R explains A).
[0:11] - Stratosphere **warms with height** because ozone turns absorbed UV into heat.
[0:12] TABLE head: Ozone | Role
  [0:12.0] Stratospheric | Protective shield – good
  [0:12.1] Ground-level (tropospheric) | Secondary pollutant from NOx + VOCs in sunlight (photochemical smog); heatwaves raise it (north-west India, Indo-Gangetic Plain) – bad
[0:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Environment #27 p.47-49; NextGen Rocket General Geography #6 p.45; NextGen Indian Geography 03 p.4, 6 (pp.32, 34); app notes 4:14 sub 8
## SUB 1 | Ozone Depletion, the Ozone Hole and Treaties
[1:0] # Cause (asked 13 times)
[1:1] - Depletion happens in the **stratosphere** (APPSC AEE; APPSC Degree Lecturers).
[1:2] - **Main cause: halocarbons** – **CFCs** (most damaging), HCFCs, halons – that release **chlorine and bromine** (APPSC 2023 Lecturers ×2; APPSC 2025 Welfare Organiser and Sainik Welfare Officer: **CFCs**, not UV rays, heavy metals or reactive oxygen species).
[1:3] - **Carbon dioxide has no direct role** in ozone depletion (APPSC Sainik Welfare) – nor do CO, nitrogen, acetylene, ethylene, SO₂ or O₂.
[1:4] TABLE head: ODS | Use
  [1:4.0] CFCs | Refrigeration, ACs, aerosols, foam
  [1:4.1] HCFCs | CFC substitute – also deplete ozone
  [1:4.2] Halons | Fire extinguishers
  [1:4.3] Methyl bromide | Fumigant
  [1:4.4] Carbon tetrachloride, methyl chloroform | Solvents
[1:5] - CFCs, halons **and** carbon tetrachloride are all ozone-depleting (UPPSC 2012 → I, II and III).
[1:6] - **HFCs** spare ozone but have high global-warming potential; CFCs destroy ozone **and** are strong GHGs.
[1:7] # The Antarctic ozone hole (asked 8 times)
[1:8] - Maximum over **Antarctica** – the **poles**, not the equator or tropics (MPPSC 2008; CDS 2012); forms in the southern spring **Aug–Nov**.
[1:9] - Reason (UPSC 2011): **a prominent polar front (polar vortex) and polar stratospheric clouds (PSCs), plus the inflow of CFCs** – PSCs (12–22 km, nitric acid ice) turn inactive chlorine into reactive forms that destroy ozone when sunlight returns.
[1:10] - First reported in **1985** by a **British team** (British Antarctic Survey: **Farman, Gardiner, Shanklin**) (RAS 2012; UPPSC Mains 2013).
[1:11] - 2025 hole peaked at just over **21 million sq km** and closed early (record ~29 million sq km, 2006).
[1:12] TABLE head: Treaty | Year | Point
  [1:12.0] Vienna Convention | 1985 | Framework: research, monitoring
  [1:12.1] Montreal Protocol | 1987 | Legally binding; phases out ~100 ODS; universally ratified (first UN treaties ratified by all, with Vienna)
  [1:12.2] Kigali Amendment | 2016 (Kigali, Rwanda) | HFC phase-down 80–85%; in force 1 Jan 2019
[1:13] - **World Ozone Day: 16 September** (Montreal Protocol signing).
[1:14] - India: **first developing country** to phase out **HCFC-141b** in rigid foam; HCFCs cut **44%** vs 35% target by Jan 2020.
[1:15] - AP: **Mission SANA** (Red Balloon Aerospace) – India's first home-made **stratospheric super-pressure balloon**, launched from **Vijayawada** (May 2026).
[1:16] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Environment #27 p.48-49; PMF Environment 04 p.26-28 (PSCs, polar vortex); app notes 4:14 sub 9
## SUB 2 | Composition – Gases of the Atmosphere
[2:0] # Gases of dry air (asked 10 times)
[2:1] TABLE head: Gas | Share by volume | Type
  [2:1.0] Nitrogen | 78.08% (~78%) – most abundant | Permanent
  [2:1.1] Oxygen | 20.95% (~21%, rounded "20%" – APPSC G1 2012) | Permanent
  [2:1.2] Argon | 0.93% – third, inert | Permanent
  [2:1.3] Carbon dioxide | ~0.04% (0.036% in NCERT) | Variable
  [2:1.4] Water vapour | 0–4% | Variable
  [2:1.5] Neon, helium, krypton, xenon, hydrogen, ozone | Traces | —
[2:2] - **N₂ > O₂ > Ar > CO₂** (APPSC G1 2023). **Nitrogen + oxygen = 99%** – the air near the surface is "mainly nitrogen and oxygen" (RAS 1997).
[2:3] - **Permanent gases** keep a constant proportion: nitrogen, oxygen, argon (and the noble gases); **variable:** CO₂, water vapour, ozone → UPPCS Mains 2017 answer **nitrogen**.
[2:4] - **Chlorine** is **not** a normal constituent of air (APPSC G2 2008); neon, helium and CO₂ are.
[2:5] - **Xenon = "Stranger Gas"** (Greek *xenos*), found by **Ramsay and Travers, 1898** (UPSC 2008).
[2:6] - Weather balloons are filled with **helium** (light, inert) (RAS 1997).
[2:7] - **0.03% CO₂** = the optimum for natural balance (UPPCS 2017); today it is ~0.04% (**423.9 ppm in 2024**, WMO Greenhouse Gas Bulletin, Oct 2025).
[2:8] - Air is held to the Earth by **gravity** (SSC 2011).
[2:9] # Change with height and with time
[2:10] - **99% of the mass lies within 32 km.** Moving up, **CO₂ and water vapour vanish first (~90 km)**, then **oxygen (negligible by ~120 km)**, while nitrogen continues → order of disappearance **CO₂ → O₂ → N₂** (NDA 2009).
[2:11] - Light gases rise highest: the **exosphere** is mostly **hydrogen** (with helium) (NDA 2015).
[2:12] - **Early atmosphere:** **reducing** – methane, ammonia, hydrocarbons, little free oxygen; methane and hydrocarbons were oxidised and replaced by **carbon dioxide** (BPSC 1995); photosynthesis later made it oxygen-rich.
[2:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 03 p.3 (p.31); NextGen Rocket General Geography #6 p.42-43, 48; PMF Physical Geography 04 p.21 (oxygen negligible at 120 km, CO₂ to 90 km); app notes 4:14 sub 0
## SUB 3 | Layers – Basis, Sequence and Temperature Trend
[3:0] # Sequence (asked 10 times)
[3:1] - Layers are divided on the **vertical profile of air temperature** (APPSC G1) – not wind, humidity or pressure.
[3:2] TABLE head: Order | Layer | Height | Temperature | Identity
  [3:2.0] 1 | Troposphere – lowest (SSC CGL 2019; UKPSC 2016) | 0 to 8 km (poles) / 18 km (equator); avg 13 km | Falls | Weather
  [3:2.1] 2 | Stratosphere – second layer (APPSC AMVI) | to 50 km | Rises | Ozone
  [3:2.2] 3 | Mesosphere | 50–80 km | Falls to −100°C – coldest (Astt. Comm. 2018) | Meteors burn
  [3:2.3] 4 | Thermosphere (ionosphere 80–400/640 km) | from 80 km | Rises sharply | Ions, radio waves, aurora
  [3:2.4] 5 | Exosphere | outermost | Rarefied | Merges into space
[3:3] - Upward order **Troposphere → Stratosphere → Mesosphere → Thermosphere/Ionosphere** (UPSC 1998, CDS 2015, UPPCS 2005, UPPCS 2015).
[3:4] - The troposphere is the layer of **smallest height** (SSC 2011).
[3:5] - The **atmosphere** = the thin layer of gases around the Earth (APPSC Jr Asst); it has **no definite upper limit** and **thins gradually** until imperceptible (CDS 2015).
[3:6] - Temperature trend upward: **falls, rises, falls, rises**; reversal layers: **stratosphere and thermosphere**.
[3:7] TABLE head: Boundary | Height
  [3:7.0] Tropopause | 8–18 km
  [3:7.1] Stratopause | ~50 km
  [3:7.2] Mesopause | ~80 km
[3:8] # Matching (UPPCS Mains 2002)
[3:9] - Troposphere – weather; stratosphere – ozone; ionosphere – radio waves reflected to Earth → correct. **Mesosphere – aurorae is wrong** (aurora = ionosphere/thermosphere) → **1, 2 and 3**.
[3:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 03 p.6-7 (pp.34-35); NextGen Rocket General Geography #6 p.43, 47-49; app notes 4:14 sub 2-3
## SUB 4 | Troposphere and Tropopause
[4:0] # Troposphere (asked 12 times)
[4:1] - **Average height 13 km**; ~8 km at the poles, ~**18 km at the equator** (State PSC: "average height 13 km" is the true statement; it is not the top layer and its temperature falls with height).
[4:2] - **Thickest at the equator** because **high insolation and strong convection** carry heat to great heights → UPSC 2004 (A and R true, R explains A; "thickness of the atmosphere" in the stem means the troposphere) and UPSC 2024 (I and II correct, II explains I).
[4:3] - **Almost all weather** – clouds, rain, fog, hail, storms – happens here (RAS 1999; MPPSC 2023); the **cloud mass of a cyclone** rises only within the troposphere (APPSC AE 2017).
[4:4] - Contains nearly all **water vapour and dust**; most important for **biological activity**.
[4:5] - **Normal lapse rate ~6.5°C per km** (≈ **1°C per 165 m**) – temperature **decreases** with height (NDA 2018 option "6.4°C per km" = the approximate normal rate).
[4:6] - **Agra and Darjeeling** lie on about the same latitude; Darjeeling is much colder in January because temperature falls with **height** (UPPCS 2022: A and R true, R explains A).
[4:7] # Tropopause
[4:8] - **Transition zone between troposphere and stratosphere**, lying **below the stratosphere**; its height **varies with season** (higher in summer) and **latitude** (higher at the equator) → APPSC G1 2018: **i, ii and iii all correct**.
[4:9] - It is a **zone of mixing** (NDA 2012) – not formed by meteoric dust, not 5–10 km thick, and it is ~18 km (not 8 km) over the equator.
[4:10] - Temperature at the tropopause: about **−80°C over the equator**, **−45°C over the poles**.
[4:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 03 p.6 (p.34); NextGen Rocket General Geography #6 p.43-44; app notes 4:14 sub 4
## SUB 5 | Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Ionosphere and Exosphere
[5:0] # Phenomenon → layer (asked 9 times)
[5:1] TABLE head: Phenomenon | Layer
  [5:1.0] Jet aircraft fly in the lower stratosphere | No clouds or weather, dry, stable (JPSC 2010)
  [5:1.1] Meteors burn up | Mesosphere (APPSC G2) – not thermosphere/exosphere
  [5:1.2] Radio waves deflected / reflected; wireless communication; telecommunication | Ionosphere (UPSC 1996, UPPCS 2005, 2013, JPSC 2013)
  [5:1.3] Aurora Borealis (north) / Australis (south) | Ionosphere (the ionised part of the thermosphere) (UPPCS 2010)
  [5:1.4] Communication satellites (geostationary ~36,000 km) | Exosphere (UKPSC 2005)
[5:2] - Ionosphere: **80–400 km** (NextGen; 80–640 km in older books); **electrically charged ions** reflect radio waves back to Earth; expands by day, contracts at night.
[5:3] - Aurora: solar charged particles excite upper-air atoms near the magnetic poles.
[5:4] - **MST (Mesosphere–Stratosphere–Troposphere) radar** at the **National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL), Gadanki, near Tirupati** (APPSC Hostel Welfare Officer).

## SUB 6 | Carbon Dioxide, Water Vapour, Dust and the Greenhouse Property
[6:0] # CO₂ as a greenhouse gas (asked 5 times)
[6:1] - CO₂ is **transparent to incoming solar (short-wave) radiation** but **opaque to outgoing terrestrial (long-wave, infrared) radiation** – it **absorbs infrared** and re-radiates heat (UPSC 2012; CDS 2012; RAS 1992) – not UV, not all radiation, not water vapour.
[6:2] - **Greenhouse effect** = heating of the atmosphere by absorption of infrared by CO₂ and other gases; **CO₂** is the gas that contributes most (APPSC FBO) and whose rise enhances it (UPPSC 2015) – not CO, O₂, N₂ or argon.
[6:3] - Other GHGs: **methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour (most abundant GHG), CFCs**; not hydrogen, krypton, neon, helium or argon.
[6:4] # Water vapour and dust
[6:5] - **Water vapour:** up to **4%** in warm wet tropics, **under 1%** in deserts and polar air; decreases with height and from equator to poles; absorbs insolation and Earth's heat (**blanket**); decides air stability.
[6:6] - **Dust:** sea salt, fine soil, smoke-soot, ash, pollen, meteor dust; mostly in lower layers; **more in subtropical and temperate regions because of dry winds** than in equatorial and polar regions → UPSC 2025: **Statement I correct, Statement II ("less dry winds") wrong**.
[6:7] - Dust and salt act as **hygroscopic nuclei** for condensation → clouds.
[6:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 03 p.3-6 (pp.31-34); PMF Physical Geography 04 p.21-22; app notes 4:14 sub 0-1
