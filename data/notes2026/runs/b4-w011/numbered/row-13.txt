## SUB 0 | Karst Landforms – Groundwater Work
[0:0] - **Karst**: landscape made by **groundwater dissolving limestone/dolomite** (carbonation); named after the Karst region of **Slovenia**. Sinkholes form in **limestone topography**, not sandstone, polar or desert areas.
[0:1] TABLE head: Landform | Key feature
  [0:1.0] Lapiaz / karren | Grooved, ridged limestone surface
  [0:1.1] Sinkhole (doline) | Closed funnel depression – the commonest karst landform
  [0:1.2] Uvala | Joined (coalesced) dolines
  [0:1.3] Polje | Large enclosed karst depression
  [0:1.4] Swallow hole | Point where a surface stream disappears underground
  [0:1.5] Blind valley | Valley that ends abruptly where its stream sinks (steephead valley) – a karst feature
  [0:1.6] Stalactite | Calcium-carbonate deposit hanging from the cave roof
  [0:1.7] Stalagmite | Deposit rising from the cave floor
  [0:1.8] Pillar (column) | Stalactite and stalagmite joined
[0:2] - Rising size: **sinkhole → doline → uvala → polje**.
[0:3] - Stalactites, stalagmites and pillars are **depositional landforms of underground (ground) water** – water dissolves CaCO₃ and re-deposits it as it drips; not wind, glacier or river features.
[0:4] - Pot hole = river bed; blow hole = coast; playa = desert.
[0:5] - **Springs** are common in **well-jointed rocks**, **karst (limestone) regions** (resurgences / vauclusian springs) and **tilted strata** where permeable and impermeable beds alternate – **not** in arid areas (deep water table).

Leong ch.4 Springs (Part 5.1 p.1) and ch.8 Limestone landforms (Part 7.2-8.1); app notes 4:13 sub 6
## SUB 1 | Caves of Andhra Pradesh and India
[1:0] TABLE head: Cave | Place | Note
  [1:0.0] Belum | Nandyal district (earlier Kurnool) | Karst, black limestone, stalactites and stalagmites; 2nd-longest natural cave in India (~3.2 km); found by Robert Bruce Foote (1884); GSI Geo-Heritage Site (Jun 2025)
  [1:0.1] Borra | Ananthagiri hills, Araku (Alluri Sitharama Raju district) | Limestone karst; formed by the Gosthani river flowing through it; found 1807
  [1:0.2] Undavalli | Near Vijayawada (Guntur side of Krishna) | Rock-cut sandstone cave temple, Vishnukundins; reclining Vishnu – not karst
  [1:0.3] Bojjannakonda | Sankaram, Anakapalli | Buddhist rock-cut caves, stupas and monastic remains – not karst
  [1:0.4] Erravaram | Left bank of the Yeleru, near Rajahmundry | Early historic Buddhist site – not karst
  [1:0.5] Arwah | Near Cherrapunji (Sohra), Meghalaya | Limestone formations and fossils
  [1:0.6] Krem Liat Prah | Meghalaya | India's longest natural cave; world's longest = Mammoth Cave (USA)
[1:1] - **APPSC G1:** karst topography in AP = **Belum** (not Undavalli, Bojjannakonda, Erravaram).
[1:2] - **Billa Surgam**: Betamcherla, **Kurnool/Nandyal** – **not Anantapuramu**; Old Stone Age cave with animal remains. Kurnool caves and **Muchchatla Chintamanu Gavi** gave **Upper Palaeolithic bone tools** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025: only statement a is incorrect).
[1:3] - **Kethavaram** rock paintings (Kurnool): deer the most painted animal. **Gandikota** (YSR Kadapa): Penna gorge.

## SUB 2 | Coastal Landforms
[2:0] - **Main agent of coastal erosion: waves** (also tides, currents, tsunamis, humans). Processes: hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution (**saltation is wind**).
[2:1] TABLE head: Type | Landform | Formation
  [2:1.0] Erosional | Sea cliff, notch | Wave erosion; notch cut at the cliff base
  [2:1.1] Erosional | Wave-cut platform | Cliff retreat
  [2:1.2] Erosional | Sea cave → arch → stack → stump | Weak zones eroded; cave cuts a headland; arch collapses → stack
  [2:1.3] Depositional | Beach | Sand/shingle between tides
  [2:1.4] Depositional | Spit / hook | Ridge joined to land at one end (longshore drift); a hooked spit = hook
  [2:1.5] Depositional | Bar | Ridge across a bay; closes it into a lagoon
  [2:1.6] Depositional | Tombolo | Bar joining an island to the mainland
  [2:1.7] Depositional | Lagoon | Shallow water cut off by a bar (Chilika, Pulicat)
[2:2] - **NDA 2018:** stack is **not** depositional; notch, arch, cliff are erosional, hook is depositional.
[2:3] - Defences: soft = beach nourishment, dunes, **mangroves**; hard = seawalls, **groynes** (across longshore drift), breakwaters. Dams, harbours and sand mining cut sediment and worsen erosion.
[2:4] - East coast erodes more: soft low alluvium, no rocky cliffs. AP: severe at **Uppada (Kakinada)** and the Krishna coast (Jul 2026). Pulicat = saltwater; Kolleru = freshwater.
[2:5] TABLE head: Coast | Formed by | Example
  [2:5.0] Fiord | Drowned glacial valley | Norway
  [2:5.1] Ria | Drowned river valley; ranges at right angles to the coast | NW Spain
  [2:5.2] Dalmatian | Ranges parallel to the coast; long islands | Adriatic (Croatia)
[2:6] - **Emergent coast**: **east coast** (Coromandel) – broad plain, few natural ports. West: **Konkan** submerged by faulting; Kerala emergent.
[2:7] - Geo-heritage: **Erra Matti Dibbalu** (red dunes, Visakhapatnam) and **Tirumala Silathoranam** (quartzite arch) on the **UNESCO Tentative List (Sep 2025)**.
[2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket General Geography p.39 (coastal erosional and depositional landforms), p.41 (spit vs tombolo); NCERT Class 11 Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.7 (OnlyIAS NCERT Physical Geography 06 p.9-10); G.C. Leong ch.10 Coastal landforms; app notes 4:13 sub 8-9
## SUB 3 | Glacial Landforms
[3:0] TABLE head: Landform | Meaning
  [3:0.0] Cirque (corrie) | Bowl/armchair hollow at the valley head
  [3:0.1] Arête | Knife-edged ridge between two cirques
  [3:0.2] Horn | Sharp pyramidal peak (Matterhorn)
  [3:0.3] U-shaped valley | Glacier deepens and widens a river valley
  [3:0.4] Hanging valley | Tributary glacier valley left high above the deepened main valley; joins it by a waterfall – agent = glacier (Yosemite)
  [3:0.5] Fjord | Drowned glacial U-valley (Norway, Greenland, New Zealand)
  [3:0.6] Moraine / till / boulder clay | Unsorted glacial debris deposited by ice: lateral, terminal, medial, ground
  [3:0.7] Drumlin | Egg-shaped till hill; a field = "basket of eggs"
  [3:0.8] Esker | Winding ridge of stratified meltwater sediment from a sub-glacial tunnel
[3:1] - **APPSC G1 2017:** till, moraine and boulder clay = depositional landforms of **glacial activity**.
[3:2] - **Hanging glaciers** are stranded on steep slopes as the main glacier **thins**; collapse → ice-rock avalanches, GLOFs (Nepal, 1 Sep 2026, app CA). From 2025 **CWC** monitors **2,843** glacial lakes/water bodies (up from 902).

## SUB 4 | Arid (Aeolian) Landforms
[4:0] - Desert landforms are made **mainly by wind** (deflation, abrasion, deposition) with help from sheet floods. Wind transport: deflation, **saltation**, surface creep.
[4:1] TABLE head: Landform | Process | Key point
  [4:1.0] Barchan | Deposition | Crescent dune, horns downwind – e.g. Thar (Jaisalmer)
  [4:1.1] Seif | Deposition | Long narrow (longitudinal) dunes where sand is scarce
  [4:1.2] Sand dune | Deposition | Accumulation of wind-blown sand
  [4:1.3] Yardang | Erosion | Streamlined ridge parallel to the wind
  [4:1.4] Zeugen | Erosion | Table-like ridge, hard cap over soft rock
  [4:1.5] Mushroom rock | Erosion (abrasion) | Base abraded more than top
  [4:1.6] Ventifact | Erosion | Wind-faceted pebble
  [4:1.7] Deflation hollow | Erosion | Wind removal
  [4:1.8] Inselberg | Erosion (residual) | Isolated residual hill (Uluru)
  [4:1.9] Playa | Evaporation | Dry salt-lake bed
  [4:1.10] Loess | Deposition | Fine wind-blown silt (Loess Plateau, China)
  [4:1.11] Mesa / butte | Erosion | Flat-topped table hill / smaller remnant
[4:2] - **Barchans are depositional**, so they are "not an erosional landform of wind"; yardang, inselberg and ventifact are erosional.
[4:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket General Geography p.38 (desert landforms), p.41 (barchan vs seif); NCERT Class 11 Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.7 (OnlyIAS NCERT Physical Geography 06 p.11-12); G.C. Leong ch.7; app notes 4:13 sub 5
## SUB 5 | Landform → Agent (Overview)
[5:0] TABLE head: Agent | Landforms
  [5:0.0] Running water | Delta, V-valley, gorge, canyon, meander, alluvial fan, levee, peneplain
  [5:0.1] Glaciers | Cirque, arête, horn, U-valley, hanging valley, moraine, till, esker; fjord (with drowning)
  [5:0.2] Wind | Sand dunes, yardangs, mushroom rocks, loess
  [5:0.3] Groundwater (karst) | Sinkholes, doline, stalactites, stalagmites, blind valley
  [5:0.4] Waves and currents | Cliffs, sea caves, arches, stacks; bars and spits
  [5:0.5] Tectonic faulting | Rift valleys (Death Valley, Narmada), horsts, grabens
[5:1] - **APPSC G2 2024 match:** Delta – running water; Cirque – glaciers; Cliffs – waves and currents; Sand dunes – winds (a-III, b-IV, c-I, d-II).
[5:2] - Mountains: folding, faulting, volcanism, erosion; young fold mountains = **Alps, Andes, Rockies, Himalayas**; old = Appalachians, Urals, Pennines, Aravallis. Valleys: river, glacier, tectonic, karst.
