## SUB 0 | Heating of the Atmosphere – Radiation, Conduction, Convection, Advection
[0:0] # How the air is heated (asked 8 times)
[0:1] TABLE head: Process | Mechanism
  [0:1.0] Insolation | Direct heating by incoming short-wave solar radiation
  [0:1.1] Conduction | Heats the lower layers of air in contact with the warm ground
  [0:1.2] Convection | Vertical transfer – warm air rises, cool air sinks (troposphere)
  [0:1.3] Advection | Horizontal transfer by wind (e.g. the Loo)
  [0:1.4] Terrestrial radiation | Long-wave infra-red from the Earth = main heater of the air
[0:2] - APPSC Group-II 2024 match: **Advection – horizontal; Convection – vertical; Insolation – direct; Conduction – lower layers** (a-I, b-II, c-III, d-IV).
[0:3] - The atmosphere is **heated from below**: the troposphere is warmest near the ground because it is **heated by the Earth's surface** (SSC 2011) – not because it is closest to the Sun or makes its own heat. It is **not** mainly heated by incoming sunlight (UPSC 2024: that statement is wrong).
[0:4] - Sun's radiation = **short-wave**: mostly **visible light + short-wave (near) infra-red** (NDA 2019); the Earth re-radiates **long-wave infra-red**, not ultraviolet (Asst. Comm. 2009: both "receives at infra-red end" and "re-radiates as UV" are wrong → **neither**).
[0:5] - Long-wave terrestrial radiation is absorbed mainly by **carbon dioxide and water vapour** (concentrated in the lower atmosphere) – UPSC 2023 correct statement; infra-red is **not** part of the visible spectrum and is **not** two-thirds of insolation (about half of insolation is infra-red).
[0:6] - **Conduction, convection and radiation** are tied to long-wave (terrestrial) heat transfer; **scattering** is a short-wave process (IAS 2018 odd one out).
[0:7] - **Radiation needs no medium** (UPRO 2017); conduction and convection need matter.
[0:8] - Wind on a hot summer day = **convection current** of air (CDS 2011); trade winds too arise from **convection** (Hadley cell).
[0:9] ~~ Sources: NCERT Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.9 (via PMF Physical Geography 04 p.16, 29-31); NextGen Rocket General Geography #7 p.52; app notes 4:15 sub 0
## SUB 1 | Insolation – Meaning, Factors and Distribution
[1:0] # Meaning and factors (asked 7 times)
[1:1] - **Insolation = incoming solar radiation** (APPSC Group-1) – energy received by the Earth's surface as short waves.
[1:2] - Factors: **angle of the Sun's rays (set by latitude)**, **length of the day**, **transparency of the atmosphere** (clouds, dust, water vapour), Earth–Sun distance, land–sea distribution, slope and aspect.
[1:3] - Intensity depends mainly on **latitude** (NDA 2011) – not on **longitude** (APSLPRB SI 2023: longitude has no influence).
[1:4] - **Perihelion ~3 January, ~147 million km**; **aphelion ~4 July, ~152 million km** – distance matters little; tilt and angle matter more.
[1:5] # Distribution
[1:6] - Equator gets **direct** rays, poles **oblique** rays → insolation **decreases** from the Equator to the poles.
[1:7] - **Least seasonal variation: equatorial regions** (CDS 2013 – near-constant day length and high Sun); **greatest seasonal contrast: polar zones** (NDA 2013 – months of continuous day and night).
[1:8] - At the same latitude insolation is **higher over continents than oceans**; **maximum insolation and highest temperatures** over the **subtropical deserts** (clear skies, dry air), not the Equator.
[1:9] - Equator receives on the whole only about **2.5 times** (not 10 times) the annual insolation of the poles.
[1:10] - Heat **surplus in the tropics**, deficit at the poles; winds and ocean currents carry heat poleward.

## SUB 2 | Albedo, Heat Budget and Greenhouse Effect
[2:0] # Albedo (asked 7 times)
[2:1] - **Albedo = reflecting power** of a surface – the share of incoming radiation it reflects (UPPCS 2019).
[2:2] - Highest albedo: **fresh snow** (APPSC Group-I 2019; APPSC Group-1; UPSC 2010 ×2) – reflects **75–95%**; then sand > grass > forest > **water (lowest)**.
[2:3] TABLE head: Surface | Albedo (approx.)
  [2:3.0] Fresh snow | 75–95%
  [2:3.1] Dry sand / desert | 20–40%
  [2:3.2] Grass | 20–25%
  [2:3.3] Crop land (paddy) | 15–25%
  [2:3.4] Water (sun high) | below 10%
[2:4] - **Low, thick clouds reflect** sunlight (cooling); **high, thin clouds trap heat** (warming).
[2:5] - **Ice-albedo feedback**: melting ice exposes darker ocean that absorbs more heat; the **Arctic warms 3–4 times** the global average (Apr 2025).
[2:6] # Heat budget – 100 units
[2:7] TABLE head: Of 100 units of insolation | Units
  [2:7.0] Reflected to space (clouds 27, scattering 6, snow/ice 2) = planetary albedo | 35
  [2:7.1] Absorbed by the atmosphere | 14
  [2:7.2] Absorbed by the surface | 51
[2:8] - About **30–35%** of insolation goes back to space without heating the Earth = **Earth's albedo** (CDS 2013).
[2:9] - Surface gives back all 51: **17 directly to space**, 34 to the atmosphere; atmosphere radiates **48** (14 + 34) to space → 35 + 17 + 48 = 100, so the Earth neither warms nor cools.
[2:10] # Greenhouse effect
[2:11] - Atmosphere is **transparent to short waves**, **opaque to long waves**; greenhouse gases send part of the long-wave heat back.
[2:12] - Without the greenhouse effect / atmosphere: **about −18°C** instead of **+15°C** (UPPSC 2020); day–night extremes would **increase** (CDS 2010).
[2:13] TABLE head: Gas | Share of effect
  [2:13.0] CO₂ | ~60%
  [2:13.1] Methane ("marsh gas") | ~20%
  [2:13.2] CFCs | ~15%
  [2:13.3] N₂O | ~5%
[2:14] - **Water vapour** = most abundant GHG, acts as a feedback. **GWP (100 yr)**: CO₂ 1; methane **28–36**; N₂O **265–298**.
[2:15] - Global **land** temperature up **1.59°C** since pre-industrial (UNEP Emissions Gap Report 2025).
[2:16] ~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 04 p.32-35 (albedo, heat budget); NCERT Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.9; app notes 4:15 subs 2-4
## SUB 3 | Cloudy and Clear Nights; Range of Temperature
[3:0] # Cloudy vs clear nights (asked 5 times)
[3:1] - **Cloudy nights are warmer**: clouds **reflect / send back the heat given off by the Earth** (UPSC 2001) and **hinder terrestrial radiation** (NDA 2010) – a blanket.
[3:2] - **Clear nights are colder** because of free **(terrestrial) radiation** to space (State PSC; RRB 2016) – not conduction or condensation; clear skies give the greatest night cooling, dew and frost.
[3:3] # Range of temperature (asked 3 times)
[3:4] - **Interior / continental** = large daily and annual range; **coasts** = equable, small range.
[3:5] - **Highest daily range in India: desert areas of Rajasthan** (CGPSC 2011); highest winter diurnal range **NW India**.
[3:6] - **Chennai** (IAS 2002): annual mean **~26°C** and an annual range of only **~9°C** fit a tropical coastal station – Allahabad has a large range, Cherrapunji far more rain, Kolkata a cooler winter.
[3:7] - **Seasonal range maximum in high latitudes**; largest annual range in **continental interiors such as Siberia**; **minimum at the Equator**.
[3:8] - AP: **interior Rayalaseema** high daily range; coast (Visakhapatnam, Kakinada) small range – sea breezes.
[3:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket General Geography #7 p.52-53 (cloudy vs clear sky); PMF Physical Geography 04 p.37; app notes 4:15 sub 7
## SUB 4 | Distribution of Temperature – Isotherms, Lapse Rate, Inversion
[4:0] # Horizontal distribution (asked 4 times)
[4:1] - **Isotherms** join places of equal temperature (reduced to sea level); more regular in the **Southern Hemisphere** (mostly ocean).
[4:2] TABLE head: Month (NH) | Over land | Over oceans
  [4:2.0] January | Bend equatorward | Bend poleward (oceans warmer)
  [4:2.1] July | Bend poleward | Bend equatorward
[4:3] - UPSC 2025: in January isotherms bend equatorward over land and poleward over oceans **because air over the oceans is warmer than over land** → both statements true, II explains I.
[4:4] - **Highest temperatures** are recorded not at the Equator but around **20° N** (MPPCS 2010) – subtropical deserts.
[4:5] - **Thermal equator** (line of highest mean temperature) lies **north of the geographical equator** (CDS 2011) – more land in the NH; it moves with the Sun.
[4:6] - India: **January 18°C isotherm** divides India into tropical (south) and sub-tropical (north) (UPSC 1997); winter **20°C isotherm** runs from the Tapi mouth to the **Mahanadi delta**.
[4:7] - Controls: latitude, **altitude** (Himalayas cool), distance from sea, **ocean currents** (cold currents lower coastal temperature), relief, cloud cover.
[4:8] # Vertical distribution
[4:9] - **Normal lapse rate 6.5°C per km** – the ground heats air from below; higher air is thinner, with less water vapour and dust.
[4:10] # Temperature inversion
[4:11] - Temperature **rises with height** instead of falling. Favoured by **long winter nights, clear skies, calm dry air, snow cover** (not cloudy skies).
[4:12] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [4:12.0] Surface (radiation) | Night cooling of the ground
  [4:12.1] Valley (air drainage) | Cold air drains downslope → frost pockets on valley floor; orchards on upper slopes
  [4:12.2] Frontal | Warm air over a cold-air wedge along a front
  [4:12.3] Permanent | Stratosphere = permanent inversion layer
[4:13] - Effects: **fog, frost, smog**; pollutants trapped in the planetary boundary layer (north Indian winter fog).

## SUB 5 | Heat Waves and Temperature Extremes
[5:0] # IMD heat-wave criteria (asked 4 times)
[5:1] TABLE head: Region | Heat wave considered only when max reaches | Cold wave (min)
  [5:1.0] Plains | 40°C | 10°C or below
  [5:1.1] Coast | 37°C | —
  [5:1.2] Hills | 30°C (APPSC AMVI 2023 ×2) | 0°C or below
[5:2] - Departure from normal: heat wave **4.5–6.4°C**; **severe above 6.4°C**. Old IMD rule (for stations with normal max above 40°C): heat wave 4–5°C, **severe 6°C or more** – the APPSC ASO 2017 key.
[5:3] - Plains by actual maximum: **45°C = heat wave**, **47°C = severe**. Cold wave (plains, actual) 4°C or below; severe 2°C or below.
[5:4] - **NDMA** heat-wave guidelines first issued **2016** (revised 2017, 2019).
[5:5] # AP Heat Wave Action Plan
[5:6] - **AP Heat Wave Action Plan since 2016**; **APSDMA** district-wise alerts. Duties (APPSC ASO): **Rural Development – reschedule (MGNREGS) working hours; Animal Husbandry – posters and pamphlets; Labour – employers shift outdoor work or give shade and water; I&PR – wide circulation of alerts**.
[5:7] - Severe-heatwave districts (HAP 2026): Tirupati, Prakasam, SPSR Nellore, Markapuram, Guntur, Bapatla, **Palnadu**.
[5:8] - **2026 peak: 48.1°C at Piduguralla** (Palnadu), 21 May 2026.
