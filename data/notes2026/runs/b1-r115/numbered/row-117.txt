## SUB 0 | Jaunpur – the Sharqi Kingdom
[0:0] - **City:** founded by **Firoz Shah Tughlaq**, named after his cousin **Muhammad bin Tughlaq (Jauna Khan)**.
[0:1] - **Kingdom:** **Malik Sarwar** (Khwaja Jahan, title **Malik-us-Sharq**, "Lord of the East") made it independent in **1394**; **Jaunpur** was the Sharqi capital.
[0:2] - **Shiraz-i-Hind:** Jaunpur was called the "**Shiraz of India / of the East**" as a great **centre of learning**, at its height under **Ibrahim Shah Sharqi (1402–40)**.
[0:3] - **End:** last ruler **Hussain Shah Sharqi**; **Bahlul Lodi** conquered Jaunpur in **1479** – his main achievement.
[0:4] TABLE head: Monument | Builder
  [0:4.0] Atala Masjid (1408) | Begun under Firoz Tughlaq (1376), completed by Ibrahim Shah Sharqi – massive propylon
  [0:4.1] Jama Masjid, Jaunpur | Sharqi rulers
  [0:4.2] Lal Darwaza Mosque | Bibi Raje (Sharqi queen)
[0:5] - **Music:** the Sharqi court patronised **Khyal**; Sufi musician **Pir Bodhan** under **Husain Shah Sharqi** (not Ibrahim).

## SUB 1 | Bahmani Sultanate and the Vijayanagara Wars
[1:0] - **Founder:** **Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah** (**Hasan Gangu**, Zafar Khan) revolted against **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** – **1347**; the **first independent Muslim kingdom of the Deccan**.
[1:1] - **Capitals:** **Gulbarga** (renamed **Hasanabad**) → **Bidar**, shifted by **Ahmad Shah I (Wali, 1422–36)** after annexing Warangal (not Muhammad Shah I, not Firoz).
[1:2] - **Muhammad Shah I (1358–75):** built the **Gulbarga Jama Masjid** (compared to Cordoba); fought Bukka I.
[1:3] - **Firuz Shah (1397–1422):** scholar-king, observatory near Daulatabad, Hindus in administration; defeated Deva Raya I.
[1:4] - **Last Sultan:** **Kalimullah**; kingdom ended **1527**.
[1:5] - **Bone of contention:** the **Raichur Doab** between the **Krishna and Tungabhadra**; also the **Krishna-Godavari delta** and Marathwada.
[1:6] - **Gunpowder:** the **first recorded use** of firearms in India was by the **Bahmanis (Muhammad Shah I) against Vijayanagara**, c. **1366–68**; Babur only popularised field artillery (1526).
[1:7] - **Bidriware** (Bidar): blackened **zinc alloy inlaid with silver**; under the **Bahmanis**, not Vijayanagara.
[1:8] ~~ Sources: PMF IAS Ancient & Medieval India 2nd ed. p.506, p.515-516; NextGen AP History - qutbshahis p.1; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder_weapons_in_the_Bahmani_Sultanate; app notes 1:117 (sub 1)
## SUB 2 | Kashmir – Shah Mir Dynasty and Zain-ul-Abidin

  [2:0.2] Zain-ul-Abidin (1420–70) | Original name Shahi Khan (brother of Ali Shah); "Bud Shah" and "Akbar of Kashmir"

[2:4] - **Jama Masjid, Srinagar** (completed by him): **turrets**, **pagoda-like roofs (Buddhist)** and **Persian style** → **I, II and III**.

## SUB 3 | Malwa – Mandu
[3:0] - **Founder:** **Dilawar Khan Ghuri** made Malwa independent in **1401** (capital **Dhar**); **Hoshang Shah** moved it to **Mandu**.
[3:1] - **Mandu** lies in **Dhar district**, MP; "Group of Monuments at Mandu" includes the **Hindola Mahal** (T-shape, sloping walls, "swinging palace") and the **Jahaz Mahal** (between two tanks).
[3:2] TABLE head: Ruler | Work
  [3:2.0] Hoshang Shah | Capital to Mandu; his tomb = India's first marble tomb; began the Jami Masjid
  [3:2.1] Mahmud Khalji (1436–69) | Founded the Khalji line; completed the Jami Masjid, Mandu; Ashrafi Mahal
  [3:2.2] Ghiyasuddin Khalji (1469–1500) | Jahaz Mahal and Hindola Mahal (not Hoshang Shah)
  [3:2.3] Baz Bahadur | Last Sultan; romance with Rupmati
[3:3] - **⚠ MPPSC 2017 "Jantar Mahal":** misprint of **Jahaz Mahal**; with Ghiyasuddin not offered, key **Mahmud Shah Khalji** (his father, in whose reign it was begun) – never Alauddin Khalji of Delhi.

## SUB 6 | Mahmud Gawan
[6:0] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [6:0.0] Office | Persian (Afaqi) noble; wazir of Muhammad Shah III (1463–82); kingdom at its height
  [6:0.1] Reform | Divided the 4 tarafs into 8 provinces, each under a tarafdar; forts under central commanders
  [6:0.2] Revenue | Nobles' salaries fixed (cash or jagir); land measured and tax fixed; a khalisa tract in every province set apart for the Sultan (not the tarafdar)

  [6:0.4] Madrasa, Bidar | Three storeys, glazed tiles; Persian (Herat/Samarkand) model

  [6:0.6] End | Framed by rival nobles; executed 1481 → break-up

## SUB 7 | Bengal Sultanate
[7:0] - **Shamsuddin Ilyas Shah** unified Bengal in **1342** with **Pandua** as capital; **Sikandar Shah** built the **Adina Mosque (1373–75)**; **Jalaluddin Muhammad Shah** – **Eklakhi Tomb**.
[7:1] - **Hussain Shah (1493–1519)** and Nusrat Shah supported **Bengali Mahabharata** translations; **Chaitanya** lived in Hussain Shah's reign.
[7:2] - **Maladhar Basu**, author of *Sri Krishna Vijaya*, received the title **Gunaraj Khan** from the Bengal Sultan (Barbak Shah).
[7:3] - **Founding order:** **1342** Bengal – **1347** Bahmani – **1394** Sharqi – **1401** Malwa – **1407** Gujarat.
