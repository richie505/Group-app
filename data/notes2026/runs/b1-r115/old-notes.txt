=== NOTES 1:115 | Indo-islamic Architecture – Arcuate Style, Qutb Minar, Alai Darwaza, Tughlaqabad, Provincial Styles
## SUB 0 | Arcuate Style – Terms and Features
[115:0:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [115:0:0.0] Arcuate | Arch, dome and vault – brought by the Turks; true arch held by a keystone
  [115:0:0.1] Trabeate | Indian style: lintel, pillar, corbel – merged with arcuate
  [115:0:0.2] Surkhi | Brick dust in the lime mortar used for rubble masonry (ancient India used dry-stone masonry)
  [115:0:0.3] Mihrab / Minbar | Prayer niche / pulpit
  [115:0:0.4] Jali | Pierced stone screen
  [115:0:0.5] Arabesque | Floral and geometric vine patterns
[115:0:1] - Decoration moved away from **human and animal figures** to geometric patterns, arabesque and **Quranic calligraphy**.
[115:0:2] Arcuate = arch and dome (Turkish); trabeate = lintel and beam (Indian).
## SUB 1 | Architectural Firsts
[115:1:0] TABLE head: First | Building
  [115:1:0.0] First Indo-Islamic tomb | Sultan Ghari / Sultan Garhi (1231), built by Iltutmish for his son Nasiruddin Mahmud
  [115:1:0.1] First true arch | Tomb of Balban, Mehrauli
  [115:1:0.2] First true dome; first building fully on Islamic principles by Indian craftsmen | Alai Darwaza (1311)
  [115:1:0.3] Horse-shoe arch first introduced | Alai Darwaza (Alauddin Khalji)
  [115:1:0.4] First octagonal tomb | Khan-i-Jahan Tilangani's tomb (Firoz Tughlaq's reign)
  [115:1:0.5] Double domes on tombs (pioneers) | Lodis
  [115:1:0.6] First full Mughal double dome | Humayun's Tomb
  [115:1:0.7] Earliest Indo-Islamic building / first major Sultanate mosque | Quwwat-ul-Islam
[115:1:1] Sultan Garhi – Balban's tomb – Alai Darwaza = first tomb – first true arch – first true dome.
[115:1:2] # Other Oldest / Earliest
[115:1:3] - **Gudimallam** Shiva Linga – earliest Shiva Linga; **Lad Khan Temple, Aihole** – one of the earliest temples.
[115:1:4] - **Gupta period** – earliest freestanding temples (Kailasanatha, Kanchi is NOT the earliest).
[115:1:5] - **Lingaraja** – one of the oldest temples in Bhubaneswar.
[115:1:6] - **Sadak-e-Azam** of Sher Shah = **Grand Trunk Road**, one of Asia's oldest major roads.
## SUB 2 | Dynasty → Style Features
[115:2:0] TABLE head: Dynasty | Features | Example
  [115:2:0.0] Khalji | Horse-shoe arch with lotus-bud (spearhead) fringe; red sandstone, marble inlay | Alai Darwaza
  [115:2:0.1] Tughlaq | Sloping (battered) walls, pointed domes, false arches; plain, massive, fort-like (Mongol threat, poor finances – "crisis period"); Syrian/Byzantine influence | Tughlaqabad, Ghiyasuddin's tomb
  [115:2:0.2] Sayyid and Lodi | Octagonal tombs, double dome; Hindu-style gateways, plaster ornament, external drip-stones | Sikandar Lodi's tomb, Bara Gumbad, Shisha Gumbad (Lodi Garden)
  [115:2:0.3] Sharqi | Propylon screen, no minarets | Atala Masjid
[115:2:1] Lodis pioneered the double dome; Humayun's Tomb only brought it into Mughal architecture.

## SUB 4 | Qutb Minar and Qutb Complex
[115:4:0] # Storey → Builder
[115:4:1] TABLE head: Storey | Builder
  [115:4:1.0] 1st | Qutbuddin Aibak – began it, built only the first storey
  [115:4:1.1] 2nd–4th | Iltutmish – completed it
  [115:4:1.2] Top | Firoz Shah Tughlaq replaced the lightning-damaged top storey with two storeys (marble and sandstone) → five storeys
  [115:4:1.3] Repairs | Firoz Tughlaq; also Sikandar Lodi
[115:4:2] - Height about **72.5 m (238 ft)**; tapering; bands of Quranic calligraphy.
[115:4:3] - Named after Sufi saint **Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki** (commonly believed; debated).
[115:4:4] "Qutb Minar is 180 ft high" is FALSE – it is about 238 ft.
[115:4:5] # UNESCO Site
[115:4:6] - "**Qutb Minar and its Monuments, Delhi**" – World Heritage Site, **1993**.
[115:4:7] - Components: Qutb Minar, **Quwwat-ul-Islam**, **Alai Darwaza**, Alai Minar, **Iron Pillar**, **Iltutmish's tomb**, Alauddin's tomb and madrasa, **Imam Zamin's tomb**.
[115:4:8] **Tughlaqabad Fort is NOT** part of the Qutb complex WHS.
[115:4:9] - **Iron Pillar**: **Gupta** period; king "Chandra" = **Chandragupta II**; known for **rust resistance**; in the Quwwat-ul-Islam court.

## SUB 1 | Bahmani Sultanate
[117:1:0] - Founded **1347** by Hasan Gangu, who **revolted against Muhammad bin Tughlaq**; **first independent Muslim kingdom of the Deccan**.
[117:1:1] - First capital **Gulbarga** (named **Hasanabad**); four regions (tarafs): **Gulbarga, Daulatabad, Berar, Bidar**.
[117:1:2] - **Muhammad Shah I** built the **Gulbarga Jama Masjid** (fully covered courtyard).
[117:1:3] - **Firuz Shah (1397–1422)**: scholar-king; peak strength.
[117:1:4] - **Ahmad Shah I** (not Muhammad Shah I) shifted the capital **Gulbarga → Bidar**.
[117:1:5] - Last Sultan **Kalimullah**; kingdom ended **1527**.
[117:1:6] # Wars with Vijayanagara (AP Angle)
[117:1:7] - Main cause of conflict: **Raichur Doab** (between Krishna and Tungabhadra).
[117:1:8] - Other disputed zones: **Krishna-Godavari delta**, **Marathwada**.
[117:1:9] "Who shifted the capital to Bidar?" = **Ahmad Shah I**, not Muhammad Shah I.
## SUB 2 | Mahmud Gawan
[117:2:0] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [117:2:0.0] Origin / office | Persian (Afaqi); wazir (Prime Minister) of Muhammad Shah III; kingdom at its greatest extent
  [117:2:0.1] Administrative reform | Divided the 4 tarafs into 8 provinces; forts under central commanders – to curb provincial nobles
  [117:2:0.2] Military | Took Goa (1472); crushed Telangana revolts (from 1453) with Velama help (not Reddis)
  [117:2:0.3] Letters | *Riyaz-ul-Insha*
  [117:2:0.4] Madrasa at Bidar | Three storeys, glazed tiles, huge library; Persian (Herat/Samarkand) model
  [117:2:0.5] End | Framed by rival nobles; executed 1481 → break-up of the kingdom
[117:2:1] Madrasa model = Herat/Samarkand; the **Cordoba** comparison is for the **Gulbarga Jama Masjid**.

## SUB 4 | Kashmir (shah Mir Dynasty)
[117:4:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Fact
  [117:4:0.0] Sikandar (1389–1413) | "Butshikan" (idol-breaker); temples destroyed, jizya imposed, Brahmins driven out
  [117:4:0.1] Zain-ul-Abidin (1420–1470) | Original name Shahi Khan (brother of Ali Shah); "Bud Shah" (Great King) and "Akbar of Kashmir"; ruled ~50 years
[117:4:1] # Zain-ul-Abidin – Contributions
[117:4:2] - **First to abolish jizya** on Hindus; temples rebuilt; Brahmins called back; **banned cow killing**.
[117:4:3] - **Mahabharata** and Kalhana's **Rajatarangini** translated into Persian; **Jonaraja** continued the Rajatarangini.
[117:4:4] - **Zaina Kadal** bridge (Srinagar); **Zaina Lank** island in **Wular** lake.
[117:4:5] - Completed **Jama Masjid, Srinagar**: turrets, **pagoda-like roofs (Buddhist)**, Persian style – all three.
## SUB 5 | Jaunpur (sharqi)
[117:5:0] - City **founded by Firoz Shah Tughlaq**, named after **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (Jauna Khan).
[117:5:1] - Called **"Shiraz-i-Hind"** (Shiraz of India / of the East) – centre of learning; peak under **Ibrahim Shah (1402–40)**.
[117:5:2] - Last ruler **Hussain Shah**; fell to **Bahlul Lodi, 1479**. Jaunpur school dated 1394–1479.
[117:5:3] - Sharqi court supported **Khyal**; Sufi musician **Pir Bodhan** under **Husain Shah Sharqi** (not Ibrahim).
