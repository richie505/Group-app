=== Amir Khusrau – Life, Titles and Languages
| Angle | Fact |
|---|---|
| Name, birth | **Abul Hasan Yaminuddin Khusrau** (1253–1325); born at **Patiyali**, then **Etah** district (now **Kasganj**, UP) |
| Title | **Tuti-i-Hind** ("Parrot of India") – he called himself so; PMF: given by **Alauddin Khalji** |
| Court | Chiefly **Alauddin Khalji's** court poet; **poet laureate** with a state pension under **Ghiyasuddin Tughluq** |
| Sultans | Served about **seven** Sultans (Kaiqubad, Jalaluddin, Alauddin, Mubarak Shah, Ghiyasuddin …) |
| Guru | Disciple of Chishti saint **Nizamuddin Auliya** – a **Sufi**, NOT a Bhakti figure |
| Roles | **Poet, historian and musician** – "all the above" |
- **Languages:** savant of both **Persian and Hindavi (Hindi)**; **first to use Urdu** (emerging Hindavi/Rekhta) for poetry; called **Father of Khari Boli**; riddles and verses in Hindavi grew into Rekhta/Urdu.
- **Persian poetry:** founder of **Sabak-i-Hindi** (Indian style); **first Persian poet to depict the Indian environment**; said "Na Lafze Hindiwast az Farsi Kam".
- **Peasant quote:** "Every pearl in the royal crown is the crystallised drop of blood fallen from the tearful eyes of the poor peasant" – **Amir Khusrau**.

- **Fall of Warangal (1323):** described by **Isami, Barani and Ferishta**.
# Ziauddin Barani
- Born at **Baran (Bulandshahr)**, **not Golconda**; **nadim** (companion) of **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**; wrote under **Firoz**.
- **Jahandari** (kingship) follows the **Persian model** – awe and force; Sultans ruled by **zawabit** (state laws), so the state was not truly Islamic.
- High office only for the **high-born**.

=== Music – Sitar, Tabla, Qawwali
- **Sitar:** credited to **Amir Khusrau**; combines the Indian **veena** with the Iranian **tambura** – the classic example of **Hindu-Muslim harmony** in instruments.
- **Tabla:** also credited to **Amir Khusrau** (developed from the pakhawaj).
- **Origin:** sitar and sarod owe their origin to **Persian influence**; the **sarangi** is an **indigenous Indian** bowed instrument → answer to "NOT of Indo-Islamic origin" (⚠ MPPSC 2013 official key: shehnai).
- **Khusrau's forms:** **qawwali**, **qaul**, **tarana**, khayal; ragas **Aiman (Yaman)**, Zilaf, Sazgiri.
- **Chishti** khanqahs popularised **sama** and institutionalised **qawwali**.

| *Rag Vibodh* | **Somnath** |
| *Man Kautuhal* | **Raja Man Singh Tomar** of Gwalior |

=== Ibn Battuta and Other Travellers
- **Ibn Battuta:** **Moroccan**, born **Tangiers** (1304); reached Delhi **1333**; **Qazi of Delhi** under **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**; sent as **envoy to China** (1342); in India 1333–1347.
- ***Rihla*** (in **Arabic**) – the travelogue that throws light on **MBT's reign**; detailed account of the **postal system** (horse post **uluq**, foot post **dawa**), espionage, sati, slave markets.

=== Languages and Translations
- **Persian** (Arabicised, mixed with Turkish) = **official language** of the Sultanate; Arabic religious; **Hindavi → Urdu** grew in camps and bazaars; Deccan form = **Dakhni**.
- **Apabhramsa** = the late Prakrit dialects, **early forms of modern Indian languages**.

=== Nizamuddin Auliya and Sufi Literature
- **Nizamuddin Auliya** (Mehboob-i-Ilahi) lived through seven or more reigns but **never visited the court**.
- Refused **Alauddin Khalji**: "My house has **two doors** – if the Sultan enters by one, I will leave by the other." When **Jalaluddin** tried to meet him through Khusrau, he left for **Ajodhan**.
- ***Fawaid-ul-Fuad*** = his conversations (malfuzat), recorded by **Amir Hasan Sijzi**.
| Premakhyan / Sufi work | Author |
|---|---|
| ***Chandayan*** (Awadhi, 1379) | **Mulla Daud** |
| ***Mrigavati*** | **Qutban** |
| *Padmavat* | Malik Muhammad Jayasi (Urdu version by **Ghulam Ali**, court poet of **Tana Shah**) |
