=== Jaunpur – the Sharqi Kingdom
- **City:** founded by **Firoz Shah Tughlaq**, named after his cousin **Muhammad bin Tughlaq (Jauna Khan)**.
- **Kingdom:** **Malik Sarwar** (Khwaja Jahan, title **Malik-us-Sharq**, "Lord of the East") made it independent in **1394**; **Jaunpur** was the Sharqi capital.
- **Shiraz-i-Hind:** Jaunpur was called the "**Shiraz of India / of the East**" as a great **centre of learning**, at its height under **Ibrahim Shah Sharqi (1402–40)**.
- **End:** last ruler **Hussain Shah Sharqi**; **Bahlul Lodi** conquered Jaunpur in **1479** – his main achievement.
| Monument | Builder |
|---|---|
| **Atala Masjid** (1408) | Begun under Firoz Tughlaq (1376), completed by **Ibrahim Shah Sharqi** – massive **propylon** |
| Jama Masjid, Jaunpur | Sharqi rulers |
| **Lal Darwaza Mosque** | **Bibi Raje** (Sharqi queen) |
- **Music:** the Sharqi court patronised **Khyal**; Sufi musician **Pir Bodhan** under **Husain Shah Sharqi** (not Ibrahim).

=== Bahmani Sultanate and the Vijayanagara Wars
- **Founder:** **Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah** (**Hasan Gangu**, Zafar Khan) revolted against **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** – **1347**; the **first independent Muslim kingdom of the Deccan**.
- **Capitals:** **Gulbarga** (renamed **Hasanabad**) → **Bidar**, shifted by **Ahmad Shah I (Wali, 1422–36)** after annexing Warangal (not Muhammad Shah I, not Firoz).
- **Muhammad Shah I (1358–75):** built the **Gulbarga Jama Masjid** (compared to Cordoba); fought Bukka I.
- **Firuz Shah (1397–1422):** scholar-king, observatory near Daulatabad, Hindus in administration; defeated Deva Raya I.
- **Last Sultan:** **Kalimullah**; kingdom ended **1527**.
- **Bone of contention:** the **Raichur Doab** between the **Krishna and Tungabhadra**; also the **Krishna-Godavari delta** and Marathwada.
- **Gunpowder:** the **first recorded use** of firearms in India was by the **Bahmanis (Muhammad Shah I) against Vijayanagara**, c. **1366–68**; Babur only popularised field artillery (1526).
- **Bidriware** (Bidar): blackened **zinc alloy inlaid with silver**; under the **Bahmanis**, not Vijayanagara.

=== Kashmir – Shah Mir Dynasty and Zain-ul-Abidin
| Ruler | Fact |
|---|---|
| Shamsuddin Shah | Founded the Shah Mir line (1339) |
| **Sikandar** (1389–1413) | "**Butshikan**" (idol-breaker); jizya imposed |
| **Zain-ul-Abidin** (1420–70) | Original name **Shahi Khan** (brother of **Ali Shah**); "**Bud Shah**" and "**Akbar of Kashmir**" |
- **Tolerance:** **first to abolish jizya** (before Akbar), **banned cow slaughter**, rebuilt temples, called back the Brahmins.

=== Malwa – Mandu
- **Founder:** **Dilawar Khan Ghuri** made Malwa independent in **1401** (capital **Dhar**); **Hoshang Shah** moved it to **Mandu**.
- **Mandu** lies in **Dhar district**, MP; "Group of Monuments at Mandu" includes the **Hindola Mahal** (T-shape, sloping walls, "swinging palace") and the **Jahaz Mahal** (between two tanks).
| Ruler | Work |
|---|---|
| **Hoshang Shah** | Capital to Mandu; his tomb = **India's first marble tomb**; began the Jami Masjid |
| **Mahmud Khalji** (1436–69) | Founded the Khalji line; **completed the Jami Masjid**, Mandu; Ashrafi Mahal |
| **Ghiyasuddin Khalji** (1469–1500) | **Jahaz Mahal** and **Hindola Mahal** (not Hoshang Shah) |
| **Baz Bahadur** | Last Sultan; romance with **Rupmati** |
- **⚠ MPPSC 2017 "Jantar Mahal":** misprint of **Jahaz Mahal**; with Ghiyasuddin not offered, key **Mahmud Shah Khalji** (his father, in whose reign it was begun) – never Alauddin Khalji of Delhi.

| Ruler | Fact |

=== Mahmud Gawan
| Angle | Fact |
|---|---|
| Office | Persian (Afaqi) noble; **wazir of Muhammad Shah III** (1463–82); kingdom at its height |
| Reform | Divided the **4 tarafs into 8 provinces**, each under a tarafdar; forts under central commanders |
| Revenue | Nobles' salaries fixed (cash or jagir); land **measured** and tax fixed; a **khalisa** tract in every province set apart **for the Sultan** (not the tarafdar) |
| Military | Took **Goa (1472)**; crushed Telangana revolts with **Velama** help |
| Madrasa, Bidar | Three storeys, glazed tiles; **Persian (Herat/Samarkand)** model |
| Letters | ***Riyaz-ul-Insha*** |
| End | Framed by rival nobles; **executed 1481** → break-up |
~~ Sources: PMF IAS Ancient & Medieval India 2nd ed. p.516; app notes 1:117 (sub 2)

=== Bengal Sultanate
- **Shamsuddin Ilyas Shah** unified Bengal in **1342** with **Pandua** as capital; **Sikandar Shah** built the **Adina Mosque (1373–75)**; **Jalaluddin Muhammad Shah** – **Eklakhi Tomb**.
- **Hussain Shah (1493–1519)** and Nusrat Shah supported **Bengali Mahabharata** translations; **Chaitanya** lived in Hussain Shah's reign.
- **Maladhar Basu**, author of *Sri Krishna Vijaya*, received the title **Gunaraj Khan** from the Bengal Sultan (Barbak Shah).
- **Founding order:** **1342** Bengal – **1347** Bahmani – **1394** Sharqi – **1401** Malwa – **1407** Gujarat.
