=== The Five Dynasties – Order, Periods, Founders
- **Delhi Sultanate:** **1206-1526**; opened by **Second Tarain (1192)**, ended at **First Panipat (1526)**.
| Order | Dynasty | Period | Founder | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Slave / Mamluk** (Ilbari) | **1206-1290** | **Qutbuddin Aibak** | Turk |
| 2 | **Khalji** | **1290-1320** | **Jalaluddin Khalji** (crowned at **Kilokhari**) | **Turk** (Khalji tribe settled in Afghanistan) |
| 3 | **Tughlaq** | **1320-1414** | **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (Ghazi Malik)** | Turk (father a Turkish slave) |
| 4 | **Sayyid** | **1414-1451** | **Khizr Khan** (Timur's viceroy) | Claimed descent from the Prophet |
| 5 | **Lodi** | **1451-1526** | **Bahlol (Bahlul) Lodi** | **Afghan** – the only Afghan dynasty |
- **Order:** Slave → Khalji → Tughlaq → Sayyid → Lodi (codes: Slave, Khalji, Sayyid, Lodi = **4, 1, 3, 2**).
- **Longest dynasty:** **Tughlaq**, about **94 years**; **shortest:** **Khalji**, about **30 years** (Sayyids 37).
- **Longest reign:** **Bahlol Lodi**, **38 years (1451-89)** – not Firoz (1351-88), Alauddin (1296-1316), MBT (1325-51) or Balban (1266-87).
- **Ghori dynasty** never ruled from Delhi as a Delhi dynasty – Muhammad Ghori was only the overlord before 1206; Ghori's Indian conquests passed to **Aibak**.
- **Slave phases:** **Qutbi** (1206-11, Aibak), **First Ilbari** (1211-66, Iltutmish), **Second Ilbari** (1266-90, Balban).
- **Sayyids** = **Kulah-Daran** (wore a pointed cap, **kulah**).
- **Before the Sultanate:** Delhi first became a capital under the **Tomara Rajputs**; under the **Tomaras and Chauhans** Delhi became an **important commercial centre** (coins **dehliwal**). Khaljis – **market control**; Tughlaqs – Sultanate **extended to the far south**; Sayyids – founded by **Khizr Khan**.
- **Succession:** Slave: Aibak → Aram Shah → Iltutmish → Ruknuddin Firoz → Razia → Bahram Shah → Alauddin Masud → Nasiruddin Mahmud → Balban → Kaiqubad. Khalji: Jalaluddin → Alauddin → Shihabuddin (child set up by **Malik Kafur**) → Mubarak Shah. Sayyid: Khizr Khan → **Mubarak Shah** → Muhammad Shah → Alam Shah. Lodi: Bahlol → Sikandar → Ibrahim.
- **Two Nasiruddin Mahmuds:** one in the **Slave** line (before Balban), one the **last Tughlaq** (Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah, 1394-1412).
| First / last | Ruler |
|---|---|
| First Sultan | **Qutbuddin Aibak** (master **Muhammad Ghori**) |
| First woman ruler | **Razia** (daughter of Iltutmish) |
| First Afghan ruler | **Bahlol Lodi** |
| Last Slave ruler | **Kaiqubad**, killed 1290 |
| Last Khalji | **Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah**, killed by **Khusrau Khan** |
| Last Tughlaq | **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah** (1394-1412) |
| Last Sultan | **Ibrahim Lodi**, Panipat 1526 |
> Chronology and match questions: order of dynasties, their periods (1206-90, 1290-1320, 1320-1414, 1414-51, 1451-1526), longest/shortest.

=== Slave Dynasty – Aibak, Iltutmish, Razia, Balban
| Ruler | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Qutbuddin Aibak** (1206-10) | Turkish slave of **Muhammad Ghori**; got Ghori's Indian territories; capital **Lahore**; **Lakh Baksh**; built **Quwwat-ul-Islam** mosque and Arhai Din ka Jhonpra; **began Qutub Minar**; patron of **Hasan Nizami** and Fakhr-i-Mudabbir; died playing **chaugan** |
| **Iltutmish** (1211-36) | "**Slave of a slave**" (slave of Aibak, Ghori's slave), Aibak's son-in-law; **real founder** of the Sultanate; **first to shift the capital to Delhi**; **silver tanka, copper jital**; **completed Qutub Minar**; created the **Forty**; caliph's **investiture 1229** |
| **Razia** (1236-40) | Daughter of Iltutmish; first and only woman ruler; married **Malik Altunia** (governor of Bhatinda) |
| **Balban** (1266-87) | **Zil-i-Ilahi**; **blood and iron**; crushed **Tughril Khan** of Bengal (1279); broke the Forty; chain of forts on the Mongol frontier |
- **Aibak:** born in **Turkistan**; sold to the Qazi of Nishapur, then to Ghori; ruled largely as **Malik / Sipahsalar**; also called **Quran Khwan** (recited the Quran melodiously).
- **Iltutmish's rivals:** **Tajuddin Yalduz** (Ghazni; beaten at **Tarain 1215-16**), **Nasiruddin Qabacha** (Multan and Sindh, crushed **1228**), **Ali Mardan** (Lakhnauti) – **Malik Altunia was not one** (he was Razia's husband).
- **Chengiz (Genghis) Khan** reached the **Indus in 1221** under **Iltutmish**, chasing Khwarezm prince **Jalaluddin Mangbarni**; Iltutmish **refused him shelter**.
- **Qutub Minar:** begun by **Aibak** (basement/first storey), completed by **Iltutmish**; fourth storey damaged in **Firoz Shah's** time and repaired (fifth storey added); **Alauddin Khalji had no part**.
- **Alauddin Masud Shah** (1242-46): coins carried the name of **al-Musta'sim, the last Abbasid caliph of Baghdad**, for the first time.
- **Balban:** real name **Baha-ud-din**; title **Ulugh Khan** from **Nasiruddin Mahmud**, whom he succeeded; Balban's frontier policy was **adopted and completed by Alauddin**.
- Other battles: **Chandawar 1194** – Ghori beat **Jaichand of Kannauj**; Second Tarain 1192 – Ghori beat **Prithviraj Chauhan**.

=== Khaljis – Jalaluddin and Alauddin
- **Jalaluddin Khalji** (1290-96): took the throne at **70**; first Sultan **benevolent to Hindus**; murdered by nephew-son-in-law Alauddin at **Kara (1296)**.
- **Alauddin Khalji** (1296-1316): **market control** (price-fixed markets), **prohibited liquor**, harsh punishments; **largest standing army paid in cash** to guard the frontier against the Mongols; land revenue **one-half**; **dagh** and **huliya**.
- **Titles:** **Sikandar-i-Sani** (Alexander the Second) on his coins; also **Yamin-ul-Khilafat Nasir-i-Amir-ul-Mominin**.
- **New religion:** wanted to found one and conquer the world; dropped the idea on the advice of **Ala-ul-Mulk** (kotwal of Delhi) – the **ulema would oppose it** (Barani).
- **South:** **Malik Kafur** (his general) led campaigns against Devagiri, **Warangal**, Dwarasamudra and Madurai.
- Poet **Amir Khusrau** (Tuti-i-Hind) is chiefly associated with Alauddin's court.
- Built **Siri** and the **Alai Darwaza**.
> Alauddin is the commonest answer: market reforms, cash-paid army, Sikandar-i-Sani, half land revenue, ghari.

=== Tughlaqs – Ghiyasuddin, Muhammad bin Tughlaq, Firoz Shah
| Sultan | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Ghiyasuddin (Ghazi Malik)** (1320-25) | Founder; built **Tughlaqabad**; first canal-builder of the 14th century; improved the post |
| **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (1325-51) | Capital **Delhi → Daulatabad (Devagiri)** 1327; **token currency** (copper/brass, 1329-30); **Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi** (agriculture); built **Jahanpanah** (fourth city of Delhi); **completed the conquest of South India** (Sultanate at its largest); **famine relief** (taccavi loans) |
| **Firoz Shah** (1351-88) | **Maximum canals**; brought **two Ashokan pillars** (Topra, Meerut) to Delhi; **jizya on Brahmins**; **most slaves** (1,80,000; Diwan-i-Bandagan); **translation** of Sanskrit works into Persian; employment exchange; towns Hisar, Firozabad, Fatehabad, Jaunpur |
- **MBT** was the **first Sultan to join the public celebration of Holi**; consorted with jogis and Jain monk **Jinaprabha Suri**; visited the Ajmer shrine.
- **Badauni** on MBT's death (1351): "**the king was freed from his people and they from their king**".
- **Ibn Battuta** came in MBT's reign.
- MBT was succeeded by his **cousin Firoz**, not his uncle.

=== Timur, Sayyids and Lodis
- **Timur invaded in 1398** during the reign of **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah Tughlaq**, the last Tughlaq – **not MBT, Firoz or Bahlul Lodi**; Mahmud fled; Timur left **Khizr Khan** as governor of Multan.
- **Khizr Khan** took Delhi and founded the **Sayyid** dynasty (1414); Sayyids fought the Khokhars.
- **Bahlol Lodi** (1451-89): first Afghan Sultan; annexed **Jaunpur (Sharqi kingdom, 1476)**.
- **Sikandar Lodi** (1489-1517): founded **Agra (1504)**; pen name **Gulrukhi**; orthodox; Delhi Sultan when **Vasco da Gama reached Calicut (17 May 1498)**.
- **Ibrahim Lodi** (1517-26): beaten by **Rana Sanga at Khatoli (1517)**; killed at **Panipat 1526** by Babur – the **last Sultan**.
- **Rajon ki Baoli**, Mehrauli: stepwell built **1506** by Daulat Khan in **Sikandar Lodi's** reign.
- **1498 elsewhere:** Vijayanagara ruled by **Immadi Narasimha** (Saluva), not Deva Raya II (d. 1446).

=== Breakaway Kingdoms and the Andhra Angle
- **Bengal:** **Tughril Khan** revolted (1279) against Balban; later **Haji Iliyas** made Bengal independent; **Firoz failed** in two campaigns.
- **Vijayanagara (1336):** Harihara and Bukka, sent by MBT, returned to Hinduism under **Vidyaranya**; their settlement **Anegondi** grew into Vijayanagara. **Bahmani (1347)** – Deccan revolt.
- **Jaunpur (Sharqi):** founded by **Khwaja Jahan** under the last Tughlaq; annexed by Bahlol (1476).
- **AP angle:** Delhi invaded Andhra **five times (1303-1323)**; in **1323 Ulugh Khan** (future MBT) captured **Prataparudra II** and annexed the Kakatiya kingdom.
