=== Sharia Taxes and Cesses
| Tax | Nature | Payer |
|---|---|---|
| **Kharaj** | **Land tax**, usually 1/3 (up to 1/2) of produce | Non-Muslim cultivators |
| **Ushr** | **Land tax**, 1/10 (5-10%) of produce | Muslim landholders |
| **Jizya** | Poll / protection tax in place of military service | Non-Muslims |
| **Zakat** | Religious tax, about 2.5% of wealth | **Muslims only** |
| **Khams (Khums)** | **State's 1/5 share of war booty** (and treasure-troves) | Army booty |
- **Taxes on land produce:** kharaj, ushr (and other land levies) – **not khams**, which is war booty.
- Khams: **Alauddin and MBT took 4/5** of booty; **Firoz restored the sharia 1/5**.
- **Firoz Tughlaq** kept only the four sharia taxes – **kharaj, jizya, zakat, khams** – abolished about 24 cesses (incl. ghari, charai) and added **haq-i-sharb**.
| Cess | Nature | Sultan |
|---|---|---|
| **Ghari** | **House tax** (first levied) | **Alauddin** |
| **Charai** | **Grazing tax** on milch cattle | **Alauddin** |
| **Haq-i-sharb (sharb)** | **Irrigation tax**, 10% of yield, on the ulema's advice | **Firoz** |
- **Abwab** = **miscellaneous cesses** outside the sharia, such as house tax, grazing tax, irrigation tax.
- **Jizya** under **Aurangzeb** reimposed in 1679 (Akbar had abolished it).

=== Land Revenue Demand
- Peasants usually paid **one-third**; **Alauddin Khalji raised it to one-half** of the produce (kharaj 50%), measured the land and collected directly.
- **MBT** raised taxes in the **Doab** during a famine; neither Alauddin nor MBT took one-sixth – so "one-sixth" statements are **false** (CDS 2005).
- **Alauddin** held a religious discourse with the Jain **Acharya Mahasena** (Jain tradition) – CDS 2005 statement I true.
- **NDA 2002:** Alauddin **fixed grain prices**, **paid soldiers in cash** and **raised revenue to half** – all three correct.
- **Land categories:** **khalisa** (crown), **iqta** (assigned in place of salary), **inam** (religious grants).
- **MBT:** **taccavi (sondhar)** loans to extend cultivation; first Sultan to organise **famine relief** (loans, relief, Swargadwari camp).
- Alauddin's price-control markets show that **military needs shaped economic policy**.

=== Coins of the Sultanate
| Coin | Metal | Sultan |
|---|---|---|
| **Tanka** | **Silver**, 175 grains (about 11 g) | **Iltutmish** |
| **Jital** | **Copper** (48 jitals = 1 tanka) | **Iltutmish** |
| **Shashgani** | **Silver**, worth 6 jitals | Tughlaqs |
| Dinar | Gold, popular after the South Indian loot | Alauddin |
| **Token** | Copper / brass (1329-30), at par with silver; failed through **forgery** | **MBT** |
| Bahloli | Copper | Bahlol Lodi |
- **Iltutmish:** first Sultan to issue a **regular standard currency** and to make **Delhi the capital**; he also set up the **iqtadari** system.
- The **two basic Sultanate coins** = **silver tanka and copper jital**; **rupiya and dam** are Sher Shah's (Rupiya 178 grains, **173 pure silver**; 1 rupiya = **64 dams**).
- **Dehliwal** = pre-Sultanate coin of the Tomaras and Chauhans. **Ghori**'s gold coins bore **Lakshmi**.
- Sher Shah's revenue system was the model for **Todar Mal's bandobast**.

=== Agriculture and Irrigation
- **Araghatta** = **water-wheel for irrigation** – a large **wheel with earthen pots tied to the outer ends of its spokes** (ara = spokes, ghata = pot), **animal-driven**, lifting water from **open wells**; in the **Panchatantra** and **Rajatarangini**. Turks spread the **Persian wheel (saqiya)**.
- Crops of the 13th-14th c.: wheat, barley, gram, rice, sugarcane, cotton, oilseeds; **maize was not grown** (brought later by the **Portuguese**).
- **Canals:** **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** = first canal-builder; **Firoz Tughlaq** = **most canals** (longest Yamuna → **Hisar**).
- **MBT:** Diwan-i-Kohi and taccavi; **Firoz:** 1,200 orchards near Delhi.
- **AP angle:** Kakatiya **Maagani** = irrigated land, **Metta** = dry land; land tax **Ari**.

=== Towns, Crafts and Trade
- **Towns:** Lahore, Multan, Broach, Cambay grew; Siri (Alauddin), Tughlaqabad, Jahanpanah (MBT), Hisar, Firozabad, Jaunpur (Firoz), **Agra (Sikandar Lodi, 1504; later a Mughal imperial centre)**. Firoz took about **1,300 Sanskrit manuscripts** from the Jawalamukhi temple, **Nagarkot**.
- **Royal karkhanas:** Firoz ran about **36**, with thousands of slaves.
- **Crafts:** textiles, **leather, metal-work, carpet-weaving**; Turkish gains – **paper** (record-keeping), glass, **spinning wheel**, Persian wheel, arch and dome mortar.
- **Trade:** **Multanis and Khurasanis** – overseas trade and finance; **Gujaratis, Marwaris, Bohras** – inland trade; **Banjaras** carried grain on oxen.
- **Imports:** horses, textiles, glassware from **West Asia**; silk and porcelain from **China**. Ibn Battuta: Malabar **pepper**.
- **Amir Khusrau:** Warangal's wealth and **diamonds** drew the Delhi invasions.
- **Society in brief:** ulema kept the religious law; **khuts and muqaddams** collected peasants' revenue; caste continued, **sati and purdah** spread; slave markets (Ibn Battuta), Firoz's slave department.
