=== NOTES 1:110 | Delhi Sultanate – the Five Dynasties in Outline
## SUB 0 | The Five Dynasties
[110:0:0] - Delhi Sultanate: **1206-1526**; opened by **Second Tarain (1192)**, ended at **First Panipat (1526)**.
[110:0:1] - Five dynasties: **Turkish** (first four in origin, Sayyids claimed Prophet's descent) then **Afghan** (Lodi).
[110:0:2] TABLE head: Order | Dynasty | Period | Founder | Origin
  [110:0:2.0] 1 | Slave / Mamluk | 1206-1290 | Qutbuddin Aibak | Turk
  [110:0:2.1] 2 | Khalji | 1290-1320 | Jalaluddin Khalji (crowned at Kilokhari) | Turk (Khalji)
  [110:0:2.2] 3 | Tughlaq | 1320-1414 | Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (Ghazi Malik) | Turk (father Turkish slave, mother Jat)
  [110:0:2.3] 4 | Sayyid | 1414-1451 | Khizr Khan (Timur's viceroy) | Claimed descent from the Prophet
  [110:0:2.4] 5 | Lodi | 1451-1526 | Bahlol (Bahlul) Lodi | Afghan (first Afghan Sultan)
[110:0:3] - Slave phases: **Qutbi** (1206-11), **First Ilbari** under Iltutmish (1211-66), **Second Ilbari** under Balban (1266-90).
[110:0:4] - **Sayyids** were called **Kulah-Daran** (wore a pointed cap, **kulah**); fourth dynasty, between Tughlaqs and Lodis.
[110:0:5] - After 1526: Mughal rule by **Babur**; **Sur** dynasty = **Sher Shah Suri**.
[110:0:6] Order: Slave → Khalji → Tughlaq → Sayyid → Lodi. Questions jumble Sayyid and Khalji.
## SUB 1 | Longest and Shortest
[110:1:0] TABLE head: Item | Answer
  [110:1:0.0] Longest dynasty | Tughlaq, 94 years (1320-1414)
  [110:1:0.1] Shortest dynasty | Khalji, about 30 years (1290-1320)
  [110:1:0.2] Longest reign | Bahlol Lodi, 38 years (1451-89)
[110:1:1] - Longest-reign distractors: Firoz Tughlaq (1351-88), Alauddin (1296-1316), MBT (1325-51), Balban (1266-87).
## SUB 2 | Rulers – Reign and Contribution
[110:2:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Reign | Contribution
  [110:2:0.0] Qutbuddin Aibak | 1206-10 | Founded Slave dynasty; Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque; began Qutub Minar
  [110:2:0.1] Iltutmish | 1211-36 | Consolidated; tanka and jital; completed Qutub Minar
  [110:2:0.2] Razia | 1236-40 | First and only woman ruler
  [110:2:0.3] Balban | 1266-87 | Strong monarchy; Mongol frontier
  [110:2:0.4] Jalaluddin Khalji | 1290-96 | Khalji founder
  [110:2:0.5] Alauddin Khalji | 1296-1316 | Market control, military reforms, southern expeditions
  [110:2:0.6] Muhammad bin Tughlaq | 1325-51 | Token currency; capital to Daulatabad
  [110:2:0.7] Firoz Shah Tughlaq | 1351-88 | Canals, public works, Ashokan pillars
  [110:2:0.8] Khizr Khan | 1414-21 | Sayyid founder
  [110:2:0.9] Bahlol Lodi | 1451-89 | Lodi founder
  [110:2:0.10] Sikandar Lodi | 1489-1517 | Founded Agra
[110:2:1] # Key Facts by Dynasty
[110:2:2] - **Aibak**: born in **Turkistan**; capital **Lahore**; patron of **Hasan Nizami** and **Fakhr-i-Mudabbir**.
[110:2:3] - **Iltutmish**: "**slave of a slave**" (slave of Aibak, who was Ghori's slave); married Aibak's daughter; took Delhi after **Aram Shah's** death; **first Sultan to shift his capital to Delhi**.
[110:2:4] - **Qutub Minar**: begun by Aibak (basement only), completed by Iltutmish; **fourth floor damaged in Firoz Shah's time**; Alauddin Khalji had **no part**.
[110:2:5] - **Balban**: full name **Ghiyasuddin Balban**; ninth Mamluk Sultan; wazir and heir of **Nasiruddin Mahmud**.
[110:2:6] - **Alauddin**: raised a powerful army to guard the frontier; **Amir Khusrau** in his reign.
[110:2:7] - **MBT**: completed the **conquest of South India** (begun by Alauddin through **Malik Kafur**); **first to introduce token currency**; **Diwan-i-Kohi** (agriculture ministry); **Ibn Battuta** visited in his reign; **first Sultan to join the public celebration of Holi**.
[110:2:8] - **Badauni** (Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh) on MBT's death: "the king was **freed from his people** and they from their king".
[110:2:9] - **Firoz Tughlaq**: **maximum canals**; brought **Ashoka's Pillar** to Delhi; **Translation Department** (Sanskrit to Persian); **Employment Exchange** for the unemployed; planted **1,200 orchards** near Delhi and restored **30 gardens** of Alauddin.
[110:2:10] - **Sikandar Lodi**: founded **Agra (1504)** and made it the capital (not Delhi).

## SUB 6 | First and Last Rulers
[110:6:0] TABLE head: Item | Ruler

  [110:6:0.3] Last Slave ruler | Kaiqubad, killed 1290 (infant son Kayumars nominal last)

[110:6:1] - **Portuguese arrival**: **Vasco da Gama** at **Calicut, 17 May 1498** → Delhi Sultan **Sikandar Lodi**; Calicut under the **Zamorin**; Vijayanagara under **Immadi Narasimha**.
## SUB 7 | Battles and Rivals of Iltutmish
[110:7:0] TABLE head: Battle | Year | Result
  [110:7:0.0] Second Tarain | 1192 | Ghori beat Prithviraj; North India opened
  [110:7:0.1] Chandawar | 1194 | Ghori beat Jaichand of Kannauj
  [110:7:0.2] Tarain | 1215-16 | Iltutmish beat Tajuddin Yalduz of Ghazni
  [110:7:0.3] Khatoli | 1517 | Rana Sanga of Mewar badly defeated Ibrahim Lodi
[110:7:1] - Iltutmish's rivals: **Yalduz** (Ghazni), **Nasiruddin Qabacha** (Multan and Sindh, crushed **1228**), **Ali Mardan** (Lakhnauti). **Malik Altunia was not one**.
## SUB 8 | Mongols and Timur
[110:8:0] - **Chengiz (Genghis) Khan** reached the **Indus in 1221**, under **Iltutmish**, chasing Khwarezm prince **Jalaluddin Mangbarni**; Iltutmish **refused him shelter**.
[110:8:1] - **Balban**: held the north-west frontier with a **chain of forts** and a strong army; **Alauddin adopted and completed** this policy.
[110:8:2] - **Timur (1398)**: under **Nasiruddin Mahmud**, the last Tughlaq, **not MBT**; **Khizr Khan** = Timur's viceroy.
## SUB 9 | Breakaway Kingdoms
[110:9:0] TABLE head: Kingdom | How it broke away
  [110:9:0.0] Bengal | Tughril Khan revolted (1279) against Amin Khan's appointment; crushed by Balban. Haji Iliyas made Bengal independent; Firoz failed in two campaigns (second 1359-60)
  [110:9:0.1] Vijayanagara (1336) | MBT sent Harihara and Bukka against Deccan rebels; they returned to Hinduism under Vidyaranya, founded Anegondi, which grew into Vijayanagara
  [110:9:0.2] Bahmani (1347) | Revolt against Delhi in the Deccan
  [110:9:0.3] Jaunpur (Sharqi) | Founded by Khwaja Jahan under the last Tughlaq; annexed by Bahlol (1476)
[110:9:1] - **AP angle**: Delhi invaded Andhra **five times (1303-1323)**; in **1323 Ulugh Khan** (future MBT) captured **Prataparudra II**; Kakatiya kingdom annexed.
[110:9:2] - **Rajon ki Baoli**: four-tier stepwell in Mehrauli Archaeological Park, built by **Daulat Khan Lodi in 1506** (Sikandar Lodi's reign); restored by **ASI**, **World Monuments Fund India** and **TCS Foundation**.

=== NOTES 1:111 | Sultanate Central Administration – Sultan, Wazir, Diwans, Nobility, Theory of Kingship
## SUB 0 | The Sultan and the State
[111:0:0] - Sultans saw themselves as the **Caliph's representatives**; not every Sultan got investiture.
[111:0:1] - Sultan = **supreme commander** and **highest court of appeal**; supreme in military, legal and political matters.
[111:0:2] - **Succession: no fixed rule**; acceptance by **nobles and ulema**, or **military strength**.
[111:0:3] - **Naib**: ranked next to the Sultan; in practice controlled all departments.
## SUB 1 | Legal Basis and Nature of the State
[111:1:0] TABLE head: Element | Content
  [111:1:0.0] Sharia | Civil matters of Muslims
  [111:1:0.1] Zawabit | Royal decrees that supplemented the sharia
  [111:1:0.2] Criminal law | Followed the Sultan's rules
  [111:1:0.3] Hindus | Kept their own personal law
[111:1:1] - Government = mix of **Perso-Arabic, Turko-Mongol and Indian** elements (JPSC 2021).
[111:1:2] TABLE head: Perso-Arabic | Turko-Mongol
  [111:1:2.0] Title Sultan; Caliph's nominal authority; sijda, paibos, Nauroz | Army in decimal units (khan, malik, amir, sipahsalar); mounted archers
[111:1:3] - Essentially a **military, centralised** state.
## SUB 2 | Theory of Kingship
[111:2:0] TABLE head: Sultan | Theory / claim
  [111:2:0.0] Iltutmish | Legitimacy through caliphal investiture
  [111:2:0.1] Balban | Zil-i-Ilahi (Shadow of God) + Niyabat-i-Khudai (deputy of God); sijda, paibos, Nauroz; blood and iron; no low-born men in office
  [111:2:0.2] Alauddin | State interest above ulema; "kingship knows no kinship"; Sikandar-i-Sani (Alexander the Second)
  [111:2:0.3] MBT | Rationalist absolutism; appointed non-Turks and low-born men; coin legend "Al Sultan Zilli Allah"
[111:2:1] - **Balban**: **first** Muslim ruler with a theory like the **divine right of kings**; Sultan = God's representative on earth, ranked **next to the Paigambar**; term **Zillillah first used** by him.
[111:2:2] - Balban's real name **Baha-ud-din**; title **Ulugh Khan** from **Nasiruddin Mahmud**; he did **not** introduce the iqtadari system.
[111:2:3] Din-i-Ilahi = Akbar; Tuti-i-Hind = Amir Khusrau. Neither is a Sultanate kingship term.
## SUB 3 | Relations with the Caliphate
[111:3:0] TABLE head: Sultan | Relation
  [111:3:0.0] Iltutmish | Mansur / khillat of investiture from the Abbasid Caliph of Baghdad (1229); Sultanate part of Dar-ul-Islam; Caliph's name on coins
  [111:3:0.1] Balban | "Helper of a Caliph" in the Garhmukteshwar mosque inscription
  [111:3:0.2] Alauddin Khalji | Titles Yamin-ul-Khilafat Nasir-i-Amir-ul-Mominin on coins, without investiture (UPPSC 2023)
  [111:3:0.3] Mubarak Shah Khalji | Proclaimed himself Caliph; titles Al-Imam, Khilafatullah
  [111:3:0.4] MBT | Sought investiture from the Abbasid Caliph in Egypt
  [111:3:0.5] Firoz | Respected the Caliph
[111:3:1] - Coins: **Muhammad Ghori** = Goddess **Lakshmi** with the **Kalma**; **Mahmud of Ghazni** = silver coins in **Arabic and Devanagari Sanskrit**.

  [111:4:0.9] Diwan-i-Khairat | — | Charity | Firoz

## SUB 7 | Nobility Over Time
[111:7:0] # The Chalisa (Turkan-i-Chahalgani)
[111:7:1] - **Iltutmish** created **the Forty**: **Turkish slave-nobles**; Turkish monopoly of office.
[111:7:2] - They made and unmade Sultans after him, including over **Razia**.
[111:7:3] - **Balban**, himself one of the Forty, **destroyed** their power.
[111:7:4] - After Balban, nobles set aside his nominee **Kai Khusrau**, raised **Kaiqubad**; **Jalaluddin Khalji** took power in **1290**.
[111:7:5] # Later Dynasties
[111:7:6] TABLE head: Dynasty | Change
  [111:7:6.0] Khalji revolution (1290) | High office opened to non-Turks and Indian Muslims; Alauddin crushed Balbani and Jalali nobles, banned noble gatherings, spied on nobles
  [111:7:6.1] Tughlaq | MBT promoted foreigners and low-born; Firoz made iqtas hereditary
  [111:7:6.2] Lodi | Afghan tribal nobility; Sultan = first among equals
## SUB 8 | Iqta and Local Officials
[111:8:0] - **Iqta**: revenue assignment; the system was organised by **Iltutmish**; its holder (governor) = **muqti / wali**.
[111:8:1] - **Firoz Tughlaq** made iqtas **hereditary** (earlier they were not).
[111:8:2] TABLE head: Unit | Official | Role
  [111:8:2.0] Iqta (province) | Muqti / Wali | Governor
  [111:8:2.1] Shiq | Shiqdar | In charge of a shiq
  [111:8:2.2] Pargana | Amil | Revenue collection (UPSC 2019)
  [111:8:2.3] Village | Muqaddam / Chaudhari | Village headman; chief rural land-revenue official (not an accountant)
  [111:8:2.4] Village | Patwari | Village accountant
  [111:8:2.5] — | Khut | Hereditary rural landholder; Alauddin abolished the khut's cess
## SUB 9 | Military
[111:9:0] - **Balban** set up **Diwan-i-Arz** for a **large standing army** against the **Mongols**; head **Ariz-i-Mamalik** (recruitment, inspection, pay).
[111:9:1] - Army in **decimal units**; **horse-based warfare** with mounted archers.
[111:9:2] - **AP angle**: the Delhi model passed to the **Bahmanis** (Majlis-i-Malakat, Wakil-us-Saltanat, Amir-i-Jumla) and **Qutb Shahis** (Peshwa, **Mir Jumla = finance**, **Ain-ul-Mulk = army**, Dabir's Diwan-i-Insha). Sultanate diwans prefigured the Mughal **Diwan, Mir Bakshi, Sadr-us-Sudur**.

=== NOTES 1:112 | Sultanate Provincial and Military Administration – Iqta, Dagh and Huliya, Army
## SUB 0 | Iqta System
[112:0:0] - **Iqta** = land revenue of a territory assigned to an officer **instead of cash salary**; not ownership.
[112:0:1] - Origin: **Caliph's empire** (land to army officers for limited periods); a **Turkish, foreign** institution.
[112:0:2] - **First iqta in India**: **Hansi**, by **Muhammad Ghori** to **Qutbuddin Aibak**.
[112:0:3] - **Iltutmish** introduced it in the Sultanate as a **systematic institution** (UPPSC 2010); iqtas to Turkish officers.
[112:0:4] - Holder: **iqtadar / muqti** (**wali** for a province). Duties: keep **troops**, collect **revenue**, keep **law and order**, send **surplus (fawazil)** to the centre.
[112:0:5] - Iqta revenue did **not** go directly to the Sultan; the muqti collected it. Early Turkish administration was mainly **military** for this reason.
[112:0:6] - Iqtas were **transferable** until **Firoz** made them **hereditary**. Ancestor of the Mughal **jagir and mansab** system.
[112:0:7] "Iqta was an ancient indigenous institution" is FALSE (UPSC 2019).
## SUB 1 | Changes by Sultan and Army Pay
[112:1:0] TABLE head: Sultan | Change
  [112:1:0.0] Iltutmish | Organised the system; paid through iqtas
  [112:1:0.1] Balban | Tried to resume old soldiers' iqtas; khwajas (auditors) checked muqtis
  [112:1:0.2] Alauddin | First large standing army paid in cash; enlarged khalisa; took back Milq, Idrarat, Waqf lands
  [112:1:0.3] Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq | Inquiry into grants; unlawful grants seized; improved post (runners, horsemen)
  [112:1:0.4] Firoz | Iqtas hereditary; revived the jagir; hurt army efficiency
[112:1:1] Alauddin = cash pay; Firoz = hereditary iqtas.

## SUB 4 | Army, Dagh and Huliya
[112:4:0] - **Dagh** (branding of horses) and **huliya** (descriptive roll) = **Alauddin Khalji**; stopped **substandard horses** and **proxy soldiers**. **Sher Shah** revived dagh.
[112:4:1] - Ranks, lowest to highest: **sar-i-khail → sipah-salar → amir → malik → khan**; Khan commanded **10 maliks**.
[112:4:2] - Technology: **iron stirrup**, **mounted archers**, armour, stronger forts; horses imported from **West Asia**.
[112:4:3] "Balban introduced branding of horses" is FALSE (UPSC 2002).
[112:4:4] - **Unit sizes:** a **sar-i-khail** commanded **10 horsemen**; a sipah-salar commanded 10 sar-i-khails, and so on up to the khan (decimal system).
[112:4:5] - **War elephants:** keeping elephants was a **privilege of the Sultan** alone; a **separate department** trained and maintained them.

=== NOTES 1:113 | Sultanate Economy – Kharaj, Jizya, Zakat, Khams; Agriculture, Towns, Trade and Craft
## SUB 0 | Sharia Taxes
[113:0:0] TABLE head: Tax | Nature | Payer
  [113:0:0.0] Kharaj | Land tax, usually 1/3 (1/10 to 1/2) | Non-Muslim cultivators
  [113:0:0.1] Ushr | Land tax at 1/10 | Muslim landholders
  [113:0:0.2] Jizya | Poll tax; Firoz extended it to Brahmins | Non-Muslims
  [113:0:0.3] Zakat | Religious tax, about 2.5% of wealth | Muslims only
  [113:0:0.4] Khams | 1/5 of war booty and treasure-troves (APPSC 2012) | State's share
[113:0:1] - Khams: **Alauddin and MBT took 4/5**; **Firoz restored 1/5**.
[113:0:2] - **Firoz** ended illegal cesses; kept only **kharaj, jizya, zakat, khams**.
[113:0:3] - **Not sharia**: ghari, charai, haq-i-sharb (cesses), taccavi (loans), iqta/inam (assignments).
## SUB 1 | Land Revenue and Cesses
[113:1:0] - Peasants paid **1/3**; **Alauddin raised it to 1/2**.
[113:1:1] TABLE head: Cess | Nature | Sultan
  [113:1:1.0] Ghari | House tax (first charged) | Alauddin
  [113:1:1.1] Charai | Grazing tax on milch cattle | Alauddin
  [113:1:1.2] Haq-i-sharb | Irrigation tax, 10% of yield, on ulema's advice (first charged) | Firoz
[113:1:2] - Land categories: **khalisa** (crown land), **iqta** (assigned to officials in place of salary), **inam** (religious grants).
[113:1:3] - Alauddin began a **Public Distribution System**; price-control markets served **army needs**.
## SUB 2 | Agriculture and Irrigation
[113:2:0] - Crops: wheat, barley, gram, **rice**, sugarcane, cotton, oilseeds; Ibn Battuta saw **two harvests** (rabi, kharif); rice sown **three times** a year.
[113:2:1] - **Not grown**: **maize** (brought later by the **Portuguese**) and **tobacco** (UPPSC 2011).
[113:2:2] - **Araghatta** = water-wheel with **earthen pots** on its rim (Ar = spokes, Ghata = pot); **animal-driven**, lifts water from **open wells**; in **Panchatantra** and **Rajatarangini**. Turks spread the **Persian wheel (saqiya)**.
[113:2:3] - **First canal-builder** (14th c.): **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** (UPPSC 2017); **most canals**: **Firoz**, longest **Yamuna → Hisar**.
[113:2:4] - **MBT**: **Diwan-i-Kohi** and **taccavi (sondhar)** loans.
[113:2:5] - **AP angle**: Kakatiya **Maagani** (irrigated) vs **Metta** (rain-fed); land tax **Ari**; Bahmani kharaj 1/3.

=== NOTES 1:114 | Sultanate Society – Nobility, Ulema, Women, Slavery, Conversion and Syncretism
## SUB 0 | Social Groups
[114:0:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [114:0:0.0] Kulah-daran | Sayyids (pointed cap)
  [114:0:0.1] Dastar-bandan | Ulema (turban)
  [114:0:0.2] Ashraf | Muslims of foreign descent – elite (Turks, Persians, Afghans, Sayyids)
  [114:0:0.3] Ajlaf | Lower-status Muslims
  [114:0:0.4] Rais / Ranas | Local Hindu chiefs who paid tribute and collected taxes
[114:0:1] Kulah (cap) = Sayyids; Dastar (turban) = Ulema – do not swap.
## SUB 1 | Ulema, Qazis and Barani
[114:1:0] - **Sadr-us-Sudur**: religious grants + **Chief Qazi**.
[114:1:1] - **Qazi-ul-Quzat** = chief judge; qazis applied the Sharia as explained by the ulema.
[114:1:2] - **Barani**: Sultanate **not truly Islamic** because Sultans framed their own regulations (**zawabit**) beside sharia; also because most people did not follow Islam.
[114:1:3] - Barani's ideal of governance: ***Fatawa-i-Jahandari***; his history: ***Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi***.
## SUB 2 | Religious Policy of Sultans
[114:2:0] TABLE head: Sultan | Approach
  [114:2:0.0] Iltutmish | Tolerant; gave non-Muslims posts
  [114:2:0.1] Jalaluddin Khalji | First Sultan benevolent to Hindus
  [114:2:0.2] Alauddin Khalji | Kept ulema out; told Qazi Mughisuddin he ordered what was good for the state
  [114:2:0.3] Khusrau Shah (1320) | Hindu gods worshipped in the royal palace
  [114:2:0.4] Muhammad bin Tughlaq | Leaned on Sufis against ulema; visited Ajmer shrine
  [114:2:0.5] Firoz Tughlaq | Followed ulema; theocratic; respected Egyptian Caliph; extended jizya to Brahmins
  [114:2:0.6] Sikandar Lodi | Orthodox; destroyed Mathura temples, broke Jwalamukhi images at Nagarkot; imposed jizya
[114:2:1] - Temple destruction: also **political** (legitimacy) and economic (temple wealth).
## SUB 3 | Jizya
[114:3:0] - **Protection tax** on non-Muslims in place of military service.
[114:3:1] - Exempt: **women, children, old, disabled**, and at first Brahmins.
[114:3:2] - **Qutb Shahis of Golconda never imposed jizya**; Shia rulers who supported Hindu temples.
## SUB 4 | Position of Women
[114:4:0] - **Purdah** (seclusion, veiling) spread among upper-class women of north India from the 13th century.
[114:4:1] - **Sati** documented by **Ibn Battuta**.
[114:4:2] - **Jauhar** against Alauddin: **Ranthambhor (1301)**, **Chittor (1303)**.
[114:4:3] - **Razia Sultana (1236–40)**: only woman ruler of Delhi; opposed because she was a woman.
[114:4:4] Not Delhi rulers: **Lal Ded** (Kashmir saint), **Rudrama Devi** (Kakatiya), **Chand Bibi** (defended Ahmadnagar).
## SUB 5 | Slavery
[114:5:0] - Sources: **war captives**, vassal tribute, abduction; town slave markets.
[114:5:1] - **Alauddin's third market**: horses, slaves, cattle (besides grain market and cloth market **Sarai-i-Adl**).
[114:5:2] - **Firoz Tughlaq**: about **1,80,000** slaves; created **Diwan-i-Bandagan**.
[114:5:3] - **Aibak, Iltutmish, Balban** were slaves (bandagan) – hence **Slave/Mamluk dynasty**.
## SUB 6 | Conversion Theories
[114:6:0] TABLE head: Theory | Status
  [114:6:0.0] Sword (forced mass conversion) | Rejected as colonial trope; coercion isolated
  [114:6:0.1] Political patronage | Partial factor (office, tax relief)
  [114:6:0.2] Social liberation | Partial; caste-like ranks survived
  [114:6:0.3] Sufi influence + agrarian expansion | Major in Bengal, Punjab (pirs, dargahs)
[114:6:1] - **Meenakshipuram (Feb 1981)**: 558 Dalits embraced Islam – "social liberation" case.
## SUB 7 | Syncretism
[114:7:0] TABLE head: Person / text | Contribution
  [114:7:0.0] Al-Biruni | Compared Sufi divine love with the Gita (not Arthashastra); Sufi soul with Yoga Sutra
  [114:7:0.1] Amritakund | Hatha-yoga text into Arabic and Persian
  [114:7:0.2] Sheikh Nuruddin | Founded Kashmir's Rishi movement; vegetarian; served all castes
  [114:7:0.3] Chishtis (Muinuddin, Nizamuddin Auliya, Baba Farid) | Spoke in Hindavi; khanqahs, dargahs, Urs, qawwali
  [114:7:0.4] Amir Khusrau | Credited with sitar and qawwali
  [114:7:0.5] Malik Muhammad Jayasi | *Padmavat* – human love as divine love
  [114:7:0.6] *Dalail-i-Firozshahi* | Sanskrit texts into Persian under Firoz Tughlaq
[114:7:1] - **Bhakti** had native roots (**Alvars, Nayanars**); Islam strengthened its egalitarian, anti-ritual side.
[114:7:2] # AP and Deccan Angle
[114:7:3] - **Gesu Daraz (Bandanawaz)** moved to **Gulbarga** under Firoz Shah Bahmani; wrote ***Mirqat-ul-Ashyaq*** (Persian, early Dakhni).
