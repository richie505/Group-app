## SUB 0 | Sharia Taxes and Cesses
[0:0] TABLE head: Tax | Nature | Payer
  [0:0.0] Kharaj | Land tax, usually 1/3 (up to 1/2) of produce | Non-Muslim cultivators
  [0:0.1] Ushr | Land tax, 1/10 (5-10%) of produce | Muslim landholders
  [0:0.2] Jizya | Poll / protection tax in place of military service | Non-Muslims
  [0:0.3] Zakat | Religious tax, about 2.5% of wealth | Muslims only
  [0:0.4] Khams (Khums) | State's 1/5 share of war booty (and treasure-troves) | Army booty
[0:1] - **Taxes on land produce:** kharaj, ushr (and other land levies) – **not khams**, which is war booty.
[0:2] - Khams: **Alauddin and MBT took 4/5** of booty; **Firoz restored the sharia 1/5**.
[0:3] - **Firoz Tughlaq** kept only the four sharia taxes – **kharaj, jizya, zakat, khams** – abolished about 24 cesses (incl. ghari, charai) and added **haq-i-sharb**.
[0:4] TABLE head: Cess | Nature | Sultan
  [0:4.0] Ghari | House tax (first levied) | Alauddin
  [0:4.1] Charai | Grazing tax on milch cattle | Alauddin
  [0:4.2] Haq-i-sharb (sharb) | Irrigation tax, 10% of yield, on the ulema's advice | Firoz
[0:5] - **Abwab** = **miscellaneous cesses** outside the sharia, such as house tax, grazing tax, irrigation tax.
[0:6] - **Jizya** under **Aurangzeb** reimposed in 1679 (Akbar had abolished it).

## SUB 1 | Land Revenue Demand
[1:0] - Peasants usually paid **one-third**; **Alauddin Khalji raised it to one-half** of the produce (kharaj 50%), measured the land and collected directly.
[1:1] - **MBT** raised taxes in the **Doab** during a famine; neither Alauddin nor MBT took one-sixth – so "one-sixth" statements are **false** (CDS 2005).
[1:2] - **Alauddin** held a religious discourse with the Jain **Acharya Mahasena** (Jain tradition) – CDS 2005 statement I true.
[1:3] - **NDA 2002:** Alauddin **fixed grain prices**, **paid soldiers in cash** and **raised revenue to half** – all three correct.
[1:4] - **Land categories:** **khalisa** (crown), **iqta** (assigned in place of salary), **inam** (religious grants).
[1:5] - **MBT:** **taccavi (sondhar)** loans to extend cultivation; first Sultan to organise **famine relief** (loans, relief, Swargadwari camp).
[1:6] - Alauddin's price-control markets show that **military needs shaped economic policy**.
[1:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.59-60; Rocket Indian History 01 p.82 (Alauddin 50%); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.61, p.65; app notes 1:113 (sub 1, 2)
## SUB 2 | Coins of the Sultanate
[2:0] TABLE head: Coin | Metal | Sultan
  [2:0.0] Tanka | Silver, 175 grains (about 11 g) | Iltutmish
  [2:0.1] Jital | Copper (48 jitals = 1 tanka) | Iltutmish
  [2:0.2] Shashgani | Silver, worth 6 jitals | Tughlaqs
  [2:0.3] Dinar | Gold, popular after the South Indian loot | Alauddin
  [2:0.4] Token | Copper / brass (1329-30), at par with silver; failed through forgery | MBT
  [2:0.5] Bahloli | Copper | Bahlol Lodi
[2:1] - **Iltutmish:** first Sultan to issue a **regular standard currency** and to make **Delhi the capital**; he also set up the **iqtadari** system.
[2:2] - The **two basic Sultanate coins** = **silver tanka and copper jital**; **rupiya and dam** are Sher Shah's (Rupiya 178 grains, **173 pure silver**; 1 rupiya = **64 dams**).
[2:3] - **Dehliwal** = pre-Sultanate coin of the Tomaras and Chauhans. **Ghori**'s gold coins bore **Lakshmi**.
[2:4] - Sher Shah's revenue system was the model for **Todar Mal's bandobast**.

## SUB 3 | Agriculture and Irrigation
[3:0] - **Araghatta** = **water-wheel for irrigation** – a large **wheel with earthen pots tied to the outer ends of its spokes** (ara = spokes, ghata = pot), **animal-driven**, lifting water from **open wells**; in the **Panchatantra** and **Rajatarangini**. Turks spread the **Persian wheel (saqiya)**.
[3:1] - Crops of the 13th-14th c.: wheat, barley, gram, rice, sugarcane, cotton, oilseeds; **maize was not grown** (brought later by the **Portuguese**), nor **tobacco**.
[3:2] - **Canals:** **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** = first canal-builder; **Firoz Tughlaq** = **most canals** (longest Yamuna → **Hisar**).
[3:3] - **MBT:** Diwan-i-Kohi and taccavi; **Firoz:** 1,200 orchards near Delhi.
[3:4] - **AP angle:** Kakatiya **Maagani** = irrigated land, **Metta** = dry land; land tax **Ari**.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 (Araghatta table); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.72 (Persian wheel); app notes 1:113 (sub 2)
## SUB 4 | Towns, Crafts and Trade
[4:0] - **Towns:** Lahore, Multan, Broach, Cambay grew; Siri (Alauddin), Tughlaqabad, Jahanpanah (MBT), Hisar, Firozabad, Jaunpur (Firoz), **Agra (Sikandar Lodi, 1504; later a Mughal imperial centre)**. Firoz took about **1,300 Sanskrit manuscripts** from the Jawalamukhi temple, **Nagarkot**.
[4:1] - **Royal karkhanas:** Firoz ran about **36**, with thousands of slaves.
[4:2] - **Crafts:** textiles, **leather, metal-work, carpet-weaving**; Turkish gains – **paper** (record-keeping), glass, **spinning wheel**, Persian wheel, arch and dome mortar.
[4:3] - **Trade:** **Multanis and Khurasanis** – overseas trade and finance; **Gujaratis, Marwaris, Bohras** – inland trade; **Banjaras** carried grain on oxen.
[4:4] - **Imports:** horses, textiles, glassware from **West Asia**; silk and porcelain from **China**. Ibn Battuta: Malabar **pepper**.
[4:5] - **Amir Khusrau:** Warangal's wealth and **diamonds** drew the Delhi invasions.
[4:6] - **Society in brief:** ulema kept the religious law; **khuts and muqaddams** collected peasants' revenue; caste continued, **sati and purdah** spread; slave markets (Ibn Battuta), Firoz's slave department.
[4:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.71-72; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.58; app notes 1:113 (sub 4, 5, 6)
