=== NEW NOTES row 112
## SUB 0 | The Iqta System
[0:0] - **Iqta** = the revenue of a territory **assigned** to an officer **in lieu of salary**; not ownership of land. Holder = **iqtadar / muqti** (wali of a province).
[0:1] - **Origin:** an **Arabic / Caliphate** institution (land revenue to officers for limited periods; described in Nizam-ul-Mulk Tusi's **Siyasatnama**) – a **foreign Turkish import, not an ancient indigenous institution** (UPSC 2019).
[0:2] - **First iqta in India:** **Hansi**, granted by **Muhammad Ghori** to **Qutbuddin Aibak**.
[0:3] - **Iltutmish introduced** the iqta as a **systematic institution** of the Sultanate (iqtas to his Turkish officers).
[0:4] - **Muqti's duties:** collect revenue, **maintain troops** from it, keep law and order, pay his men, and send the **surplus (fawazil)** to the treasury. Revenue was **not deposited directly in the Sultan's treasury**.
[0:5] - **Fawazil** = the **excess amount** paid by the iqtadars to the exchequer after salary and troops.
[0:6] - **A-R (UPSC 1998):** early Turkish administration was **essentially military** **because** the country was parcelled out as **iqtas among military leaders** – both true, R explains A.
[0:7] TABLE head: Sultan | Change
  [0:7.0] Iltutmish | Organised the system
  [0:7.1] Balban | Tried to resume old soldiers' iqtas; khwajas (auditors) checked muqtis
  [0:7.2] Alauddin, MBT | Strict control: income assessed, accountants, cash pay; enlarged khalisa (took back milk, idrarat, waqf lands)
  [0:7.3] Firoz | Relaxed control; iqtas became hereditary; revived the jagir – army efficiency fell
[0:8] - Iqtas were **transferable** (muqtis rotated every 3-4 years) until Firoz; the iqta is the ancestor of the Mughal **jagir and mansab** system.

## SUB 1 | Army – Cash Pay, Dagh and Huliya, Ranks
[1:0] - **Alauddin Khalji:** **largest standing army**, recruited and **paid in cash** directly by the state – the **first Sultan to pay soldiers in cash**; raised it to protect the frontier against the **Mongols**.
[1:1] - **Dagh** (**branding of horses**) and **huliya / chehra** (**descriptive roll** of soldiers) = **Alauddin** – to stop substandard horses and proxy soldiers at the muster; **Sher Shah** revived dagh.
[1:2] - **Balban** set up the **Diwan-i-Arz** under the **Ariz-i-Mumalik**; the Sultan himself was commander-in-chief.
[1:3] - **Ranks (decimal):** **sar-i-khail** (10 horsemen) → sipah-salar → amir → malik → **khan** (highest; commanded 10 maliks).
[1:4] - **Turkish military gains:** **iron stirrup**, mounted archers, armour; horses imported from **West Asia**; war elephants were the **Sultan's monopoly**.
[1:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.57-58; NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.62 (cash pay, chehra and dagh); app notes 1:112 (sub 4)
## SUB 2 | Provincial and Local Units
[2:0] TABLE head: Unit | Officer
  [2:0.0] Iqta (province) | Muqti / wali / iqtadar
  [2:0.1] Shiq | Shiqdar
  [2:0.2] Pargana | Amil (revenue collection) and chaudhri
  [2:0.3] Village | Muqaddam / chaudhari (headman), khot; patwari (accountant)
[2:1] - **Amil** = in-charge of **revenue collection** (UPSC 2019, statement 1 true).
[2:2] - **Muqaddam or Chaudhari** = **village headman** (BPSC key); the **chaudhari** was the leading rural land-revenue official above the village (IAS 2004 key: "Chaudhary").
[2:3] - **Khuts, muqaddams, chaudharis** collected kharaj for the state; **Alauddin** ended their privileges and cesses.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.59-60, p.62 (BPSC); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.71; app notes 1:112 (sub 3)
## SUB 3 | Land Assignments, Post and Intelligence
[3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:0.0] Khalisa | Crown land; revenue for the court and household
  [3:0.1] Iqta | Revenue assignment to officials
  [3:0.2] Inam | Grants to religious men
  [3:0.3] Mahrousa | Protected town
  [3:0.4] Madad-i-Mash | Mughal revenue-free grant to saints, religious institutions, mashaikhs
  [3:0.5] Jagir / Amaram / Mokasa | Mughal / Vijayanagara / Marathas
[3:1] - Sultanate territory = **Khalisa and Iqta** lands. Maratha **chauth = one-fourth**, **sardeshmukhi = one-tenth**. Tanka = Iltutmish's **silver coin**.
[3:2] - **Post (Ibn Battuta):** **ulaq** = **horse post**; **dawa** = **foot post** (runners every third of a mile) – the **foot post, quicker than the horse post, was often used to bring the fruits of Khurasan** to India. **Dak chowki** = Sher Shah. **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** improved the post with runners and horsemen.
[3:3] - **Barids** (news-writers) and **munhiyans** (secret spies) sent Alauddin daily market reports.
[3:4] - **AP angle:** Kakatiya royal bodyguards = **Lenkas**.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: Ibn Battuta's Rihla in NCERT Themes in Indian History II, ch. 5; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.59-60; Rocket Indian History 01 p.82 (Maratha, Mughal terms); app notes 1:112 (sub 2, 5)
