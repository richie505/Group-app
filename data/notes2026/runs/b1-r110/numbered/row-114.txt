## SUB 0 | Barani, the Ulema and Social Groups
[0:0] - **Barani** refused to call the Sultanate truly Islamic because the **Sultan supplemented the sharia with his own state regulations (zawabit)** (UPSC 2002).
[0:1] - Barani's ideal of rule: ***Fatawa-i-Jahandari***; history: ***Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi***.
[0:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:2.0] Dastar-bandan | Ulema (turban-wearers)
  [0:2.1] Kulah-daran | Sayyids (pointed-cap wearers)
  [0:2.2] Ashraf | Muslims of foreign descent – Turks, Persians, Afghans
  [0:2.3] Ajlaf | Lower-status (Indian convert) Muslims
  [0:2.4] Rais / Ranas | Local Hindu chiefs who paid tribute and collected taxes
[0:3] - **Qazi-ul-Quzat** = chief judge; **Sadr-us-Sudur** handled religious grants and acted as Chief Qazi.
[0:4] - Muslim society: Turks at the top, then Persians and Afghans, Indian Muslims at the bottom; they rarely intermarried.

## SUB 1 | Religious Policy and Jizya
[1:0] TABLE head: Sultan | Approach
  [1:0.0] Jalaluddin Khalji | First Sultan benevolent to Hindus
  [1:0.1] Alauddin Khalji | Kept the ulema out of state affairs; told Qazi Mughisuddin he ordered what was good for the state
  [1:0.2] Nasiruddin Khusrau Shah (1320) | Hindu gods worshipped in the palace
  [1:0.3] MBT | Leaned on Sufis against the ulema; visited the Ajmer shrine; Holi
  [1:0.4] Firoz Tughlaq | Followed the ulema; theocratic; respected the Egyptian Caliph; first to levy jizya on Brahmins and made it a separate tax
  [1:0.5] Sikandar Lodi | Orthodox; destroyed Mathura temples, broke the Nagarkot images; jizya
[1:1] - **Jizya:** protection tax on non-Muslims (**zimmis**) **in place of military service**; women, children, old, disabled and at first **Brahmins exempt**.
[1:2] - **Qutb Shahis of Golconda** never imposed jizya.
[1:3] - Temple destruction often served **political** ends (royal legitimacy) as well as religious ones.
[1:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.60 (jizya); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.72 (jaziya on Brahmins); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 05 p.12; app notes 1:114 (sub 2, 3)
## SUB 2 | Women and Slavery
[2:0] - **Purdah** spread among upper-class women of north India from the **13th century**; **sati** recorded by **Ibn Battuta**; **jauhar** at **Ranthambhor (1301)** and **Chittor (1303)**.
[2:1] - **Razia Sultana (1236-40)**: only woman ruler of Delhi. **Lal Ded** = Kashmiri woman saint; Rudrama Devi (Kakatiya) and **Chand Bibi** (Ahmadnagar) never ruled Delhi.
[2:2] - **Slavery:** war captives and slave markets; Alauddin's third market dealt in **horses, slaves and cattle**.
[2:3] - **Firoz Tughlaq** had the **most slaves – about 1,80,000**; created the **Diwan-i-Bandagan**; slaves paid 10-100 tankas.
[2:4] - **Aibak, Iltutmish and Balban** had been slaves (bandagan) – hence "Slave" / "Mamluk" dynasty.
[2:5] - **AP angle:** in the Reddi period women who died as **sati** were deified and worshipped as **Perantallu** (Parantas, local deities); Vijayanagara slavery = **Benabaga**.
[2:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.72 (sati, purdah, slaves); NextGen AP History - APPSC 12 p.14 (Reddi society: Parantas); app notes 1:114 (sub 4, 5)
