## SUB 0 | Theory of Kingship and the Caliph
[0:0] TABLE head: Sultan | Theory / claim
  [0:0.0] Iltutmish | Legitimacy by caliphal investiture: mansur / khillat from the Abbasid Caliph of Baghdad (1229); Sultanate became part of Dar-ul-Islam; title Sultan-i-Azam
  [0:0.1] Balban | First Muslim ruler with a theory like the divine right of kings: Sultan = Niyabat-i-Khudai (deputy of God), Zil-i-Ilahi / Zillillah (Shadow of God), next to the Paigambar; sijda and paibos; blood and iron; no low-born men in office
  [0:0.2] Alauddin Khalji | State interest above the ulema; "kingship knows no kinship"; did not seek the Caliph's investiture – took Yamin-ul-Khilafat Nasir-i-Amir-ul-Mominin on his own
  [0:0.3] Mubarak Shah Khalji | Proclaimed himself Caliph (Al-Imam, Khilafatullah)
  [0:0.4] MBT | Rationalist absolutism; coin legend "Al Sultan Zilli Allah"; later sought investiture from the Abbasid Caliph in Egypt
  [0:0.5] Firoz | Respected the Egyptian Caliph
[0:1] - **Balban** called himself "**Helper of a Caliph**" in his inscription on the **Garhmukteshwar** mosque.
[0:2] - **Opposed / ignored the Khalifa's authority:** **Alauddin Khalji** (no investiture sought); Iltutmish and Balban acknowledged him.
[0:3] - **Balban** did **not** introduce the iqtadari system (Iltutmish did); he did propound Niyabat-i-Khudai, broke the Chahalgani and quelled Bengal.
[0:4] - **Din-i-Ilahi** = Akbar; **Tuti-i-Hind** = Amir Khusrau; **Kaisar-i-Hind** = British title – none is Balban's.
[0:5] - Coins: **Muhammad Ghori** – Goddess **Lakshmi** with the Kalma; **Mahmud of Ghazni** – silver coins in **Arabic and Devanagari Sanskrit**.
[0:6] - The Sultans saw themselves as **representatives (lieutenants) of the Caliph**, naming him in the khutba and on coins – a nominal link; the Caliph had only moral authority.

## SUB 2 | Nobility – the Forty and Key Nobles
[2:0] - **Iltutmish** created the **Turkan-i-Chahalgani** ("the Forty", Chalisa): Turkish slave-nobles; they made and unmade Sultans after him.
[2:1] - **Balban**, himself one of the Forty, **finally broke their power**.
[2:2] - After Balban the nobles set aside his nominee **Kai Khusrau** and raised **Kaiqubad**; **Jalaluddin Khalji** seized power in **1290** (Khalji revolution: high office opened to **non-Turks and Indian Muslims**).
[2:3] - **MBT** promoted foreigners and low-born men; **Lodis**: Afghan tribal nobility, Sultan = **first among equals**.

  [2:4.4] Khan-i-Jahan Maqbul | Firoz | Wazir; a Telugu Brahmin convert (from Warangal)

## SUB 3 | Nature of the State and Law
[3:0] - Government was a **composite**: **Perso-Arabic, Turko-Mongol and Indian** elements (JPSC 2021). Perso-Arabic: title Sultan, nominal Caliph, sijda, paibos, Nauroz. Turko-Mongol: army in **decimal units** with mounted archers.
[3:1] - **Zawabit** = **state laws** (royal regulations) that supplemented the sharia.
[3:2] - Civil law: **sharia** for Muslims (qazis); Hindus kept their personal laws (panchayats); **criminal law followed the Sultan's rules**.
[3:3] - **Succession:** no fixed rule (no primogeniture) – acceptance by **nobles and ulema**, or **military strength**; Iltutmish nominated his daughter.
[3:4] - Sultan = supreme military, legal and political authority; **commander-in-chief** and **final court of appeal**.
[3:5] - The state was essentially **military** and centralised at first.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.56, p.58 (judiciary); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.71; app notes 1:111 (sub 0, 1)
