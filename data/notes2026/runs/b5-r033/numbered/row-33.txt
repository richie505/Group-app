## SUB 0 | First Satellites and Early Experiments (1975-1988)
[0:0] # Aryabhata – India's first satellite
[0:1] - **First Indian satellite:** **Aryabhata**, launched **19 April 1975**; with it India **entered the space age** (asked 9 times: "first satellite", "1975", "19 April 1975", "first satellite experiment").
[0:2] - **Launch:** by the **Soviet Union** on a **Kosmos-3M** rocket from **Kapustin Yar** (USSR), not from Sriharikota; weight **360 kg**.
[0:3] - **Named after** the 5th-century astronomer Aryabhata; **conceived by Sarabhai**, launched after his death; satellite team led by **U.R. Rao**.
[0:4] # Early satellites and experiments
[0:5] TABLE head: Date | Satellite / experiment | Vehicle / site
  [0:5.0] 1 Aug 1975-76 | SITE (Satellite Instructional Television Experiment): first use of space technology for education; ~2,400 villages, ~5 million people | NASA's ATS-6 satellite
  [0:5.1] 19 Apr 1975 | Aryabhata: first Indian satellite, 360 kg | Soviet Kosmos-3M, Kapustin Yar
  [0:5.2] 1977-79 | STEP (Satellite Telecommunication Experiments Project) | Franco-German Symphonie satellite
  [0:5.3] 1979-81 | Bhaskara I (1979) and II: experimental earth observation | Soviet Intercosmos rocket, Baikonur
  [0:5.4] 18 Jul 1980 | Rohini (RS-1): first satellite on an Indian-made rocket; India the 6th nation to do so | SLV-3, Sriharikota (AP)
  [0:5.5] 19 Jun 1981 | APPLE (Ariane Passenger Payload Experiment): first experimental geostationary communication satellite | Ariane-1, Kourou, French Guiana
  [0:5.6] 1983 | INSAT-1B (INSAT system operational) | US Space Shuttle Challenger
  [0:5.7] 1988 | IRS-1A: first operational remote-sensing satellite | Soviet Vostok, Baikonur
[0:6] - **Chronology of programmes:** **SITE (1975) → STEP (1977) → APPLE (1981) → IGMDP (1983)**: code **D, C, A, B** (UPPCS 1998).
[0:7] - **Chronology of satellites:** **Aryabhata (1975) → Bhaskara I (1979) → Rohini (1980) → APPLE (1981)**.

## SUB 1 | Vikram Sarabhai and ISRO Chairmen
[1:0] # Vikram Sarabhai (1919-71)
[1:1] - **Father of the Indian Space Programme** (not Bhabha, Kalam, Dhawan or C.V. Raman); chaired **INCOSPAR** (1962); set up **TERLS**; **first ISRO Chairman** till his death in **1971**.
[1:2] - **Padma Bhushan 1966**; **Padma Vibhushan posthumously 1972**; succeeded **Homi Bhabha** at the Atomic Energy Commission (**1966**).
[1:3] - **COSPAR Vikram Sarabhai Medal** (since 1990): 2026 winner **Annapurni Subramaniam** – 4th Indian, first Indian woman.

## SUB 2 | Institutional Beginnings – INCOSPAR, ISRO, DoS, TERLS
[2:0] # INCOSPAR, ISRO, Department of Space
[2:1] TABLE head: Body | Year | Fact
  [2:1.0] INCOSPAR (Indian National Committee for Space Research) | 1962 | Under the Department of Atomic Energy; set up with Homi Bhabha, led by Vikram Sarabhai
  [2:1.1] ISRO | 15 Aug 1969 | Superseded INCOSPAR (not 1967, 1970 or 1974)
  [2:1.2] Department of Space + Space Commission | 1972 (1 June) | ISRO brought under DoS; DoS reports to the PM
[2:2] - INCOSPAR (1962) came first; ISRO (1969) replaced it; DoS and Space Commission came in 1972.
[2:3] # TERLS, Thumba
[2:4] - **Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station**, Thumba, Thiruvananthapuram (**Kerala**) – not UP, Bihar or Jharkhand; chosen because it lies close to the **magnetic equator** (equatorial electrojet studies); sounding rockets only, no satellites.
[2:5] - First sounding rocket: **Nike-Apache** (American-built), **21 Nov 1963**.
[2:6] - **RH-75**: first **Indian-made** sounding rocket, **20 Nov 1967**.
[2:7] - Satellite Telecommunication Earth Station, **Ahmedabad**: **1 Jan 1967**.
[2:8] > ⚠ Trap: INCOSPAR was under the **DAE**, not the Department of Space.
