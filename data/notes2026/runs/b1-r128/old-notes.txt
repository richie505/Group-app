=== NOTES 1:128 | Humayun and Sher Shah Suri – Sur Administration, Land Revenue, Sarais, Currency, Grand Trunk Road
## SUB 0 | Babur – the Background
[128:0:0] - Babur came from **Farghana** (Central Asia); Timurid on father's side, Chingizid on mother's side.
[128:0:1] - Invited by **Daulat Khan Lodi** (Governor of Punjab), **Alam Khan** (Ibrahim Lodi's uncle) and **Rana Sanga** (Mewar).
[128:0:2] - Memoir: **Tuzuk-i-Baburi (Baburnama)** in **Chaghatai Turkish**; **Persian** was the Mughal court language. It praises **Krishnadevaraya**.
[128:0:3] - After **Panipat I (1526)**, Babur took **Delhi and Agra** (APPSC 2023).
[128:0:4] - Panipat I = first large-scale use of **gunpowder artillery** in Indian warfare.
[128:0:5] # Babur's Tactics and Buildings
[128:0:6] TABLE head: Term / work | Meaning
  [128:0:6.0] Tulughma | Flanks wheel round to encircle the enemy – Uzbek origin
  [128:0:6.1] Rumi (Ottoman) method / araba | Carts linked into a barrier sheltering matchlockmen and artillery – Ottoman origin
  [128:0:6.2] Kabuli Bagh Mosque | Panipat, marks the 1526 victory
  [128:0:6.3] Ram Bagh | Agra, first Mughal garden
[128:0:7] - **Babur** is the Mughal emperor with **no mausoleum in India** – buried in **Kabul**.
## SUB 1 | Humayun – Accession and Problems
[128:1:0] - Accession: **29 December 1530**, three days after Babur's death.
[128:1:1] - Problems: rival brothers **Kamran, Askari, Hindal**; the **Afghans**; **Bahadur Shah** of Gujarat-Malwa.
[128:1:2] - **Rao Jaitsi of Bikaner** defeated Babur's son **Kamran Mirza** (1534), whom Humayun had sent against him (Rao-Jaitsi-Ro-Chhanda).
[128:1:3] # Humayun's Campaigns in Order
[128:1:4] TABLE head: Event | Year | Outcome
  [128:1:4.0] Kalinjar invaded | 1531 | Humayun's early campaign
  [128:1:4.1] Devara (Daurah / Dourah), on the Gomati | 1532 | Humayun beat Afghans; drove Mahmud Lodi from Jaunpur
  [128:1:4.2] Chunar Fort | 1532 | Four-month siege; Sher Khan accepted Humayun's supremacy
  [128:1:4.3] Raisina fort | 1535 | Taken
  [128:1:4.4] Gujarat | 1535 | Pressed by Humayun, Bahadur Shah let the Portuguese fortify Diu
  [128:1:4.5] Sher Khan's rise | 1538 | Won at Surajgarh; took Gaur
  [128:1:4.6] Chausa | 26 June 1539 | Sher Khan beat Humayun; took the title "Sher Shah"
  [128:1:4.7] Kannauj = Bilgram | 17 May 1540 | Sher Shah beat Humayun, took the throne; Humayun into exile
  [128:1:4.8] Sirhind (and Machhiwara) | 1555 | Humayun beat Sikandar Sur; retook Delhi and Agra (July 1555)
[128:1:5] Order: Devara 1532 → Chausa 1539 → Kannauj/Bilgram 1540 → Sirhind 1555.
[128:1:6] Kannauj and Bilgram are ONE battle (1540), a Sher Khan victory. "Battle of Samugarh" was **1658** (Aurangzeb vs Dara Shikoh), not a Humayun battle.
[128:1:7] # Exile, Return and Death
[128:1:8] - Married **Hamida Banu Begum** (Akbar's mother); **Akbar born at Amarkot (1542)**.
[128:1:9] - Went to **Persia**; the **Safavid Shah Tahmasp** helped him retake **Kandahar and Kabul**.
[128:1:10] - Returned **July 1555**, using quarrels among Sher Shah's successors.
[128:1:11] - Died **January 1556** – fell from the stairs of the **Sher Mandal** library, **Purana Qila (Dinpanah)**, Delhi.
[128:1:12] - **Akbar** was crowned at **Kalanaur** after Humayun's death.
[128:1:13] - **First ruler after the Delhi Sultanate to issue a gold coin – Humayun**; Sher Shah also issued one later.
## SUB 2 | Sher Shah – Rise, Titles, Conquests and Death
[128:2:0] # Rise
[128:2:1] - Born **Farid (Farid Khan)**, son of an Afghan jagirdar of **Sasaram**; of the **Sur** tribe.
[128:2:2] - Left home in **1494**; studied at **Jaunpur ("Siraj-i-Hind")**; managed his father's jagir from **1511**.
[128:2:3] - Got the title **"Sher Khan"** from **Bahar Khan** of Bihar for killing a **tiger**.
[128:2:4] - Served Babur; became deputy regent (de facto ruler) of **Bihar**.
[128:2:5] # Titles in Order
[128:2:6] TABLE head: Title | When
  [128:2:6.0] Sher Khan | From Bahar Khan (tiger)
  [128:2:6.1] Hazrat-e-Ala | After defeating Nusrat Shah of Bengal
  [128:2:6.2] Sher Shah (Sultan) | After Chausa, 26 June 1539; khutba read and coins struck in his name
[128:2:7] # Conquests and Rajput Resistance
[128:2:8] - **Raisen** fort taken from **Puran Mal**.
[128:2:9] - **Marwar**: **Maldeo Rathore** resisted; battle of **Samel (Sammel / Giri-Sumel), 1544**.
[128:2:10] - Maldeo's generals **Jaita and Kumpa** fought so bravely that Sher Shah said: "I would have lost the empire of Hindustan **for a handful of bajra (millet)**."
[128:2:11] # Death
[128:2:12] - Died **22 May 1545**, wounded by a **gunpowder explosion** at the siege of **Kalinjar** (a Rajput fort) – his last campaign.
[128:2:13] - Buried at **Sasaram**. Reign of only **five years** (1540-45).
[128:2:14] - His skills as a ruler are more striking than his battle wins.
[128:2:15] Sequence: Chunar submission 1532 → Chausa 1539 → Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque 1541 → death at Kalinjar 1545.
[128:2:16] "Sher Khan" came from Bahar Khan (tiger); "Sher Shah" was assumed after Chausa – not after Kannauj.
## SUB 3 | Sur Administration
[128:3:0] # Officers
[128:3:1] TABLE head: Level | Officer | Role
  [128:3:1.0] Sarkar | Chief Shiqdar (shiqdar-i-shiqdaran) | Law and order
  [128:3:1.1] Sarkar | Chief Munsif (munsif-i-munsifan) | Revenue / civil
  [128:3:1.2] Pargana | Shiqdar | Law and order
  [128:3:1.3] Pargana | Amin (munsif) | Assessment of revenue
  [128:3:1.4] Pargana | Fotedar | Treasurer
  [128:3:1.5] Pargana | Karkuns | Hindi and Persian writers
  [128:3:1.6] Village | Qanungo, patwari | Revenue / local records
[128:3:2] - Officers were **frequently transferred** so they did not build local interests.
[128:3:3] # Army
[128:3:4] - **Dagh** = branding of horses; **chehra / huliya** = descriptive roll of soldiers – both against fraud.
[128:3:5] - **Islam Shah** divided troops into units of **200, 250 and 500**.
[128:3:6] # Police, Justice and Dak
[128:3:7] - **Local headmen** had to trace crime; **censors of public morals**; efficient **spy network**.
[128:3:8] - Justice was uniform and harsh.
[128:3:9] Shiqdar = law and order; Munsif / Amin = revenue. Do not swap them.
## SUB 4 | Land Revenue System
[128:4:0] # Measurement and Assessment
[128:4:1] TABLE head: Point | Fact
  [128:4:1.0] Measurement | Uniform system of survey
  [128:4:1.1] Yard | Gaz-i-Sikandari (sikandari gaz); rope / jarib
  [128:4:1.2] Classes | Three – good, middling, bad (by produce)
  [128:4:1.3] State share | One-third of average produce
  [128:4:1.4] Payment | Cash or grain
  [128:4:1.5] Settlement | Direct state-peasant; illegal demands removed; tenant rights recognised
  [128:4:1.6] Legacy | Model for Todar Mal's Bandobast
[128:4:2] # Documents and Fees
[128:4:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [128:4:3.0] Patta | Title deed stating the state's share; given to the cultivator
  [128:4:3.1] Qabuliyat | Deed of acceptance signed by the peasant
  [128:4:3.2] Jaribana | Surveyor's fee
  [128:4:3.3] Muhasilana | Collector's fee
[128:4:4] - Patta and qabuliyat were introduced by **Sher Shah**.
[128:4:5] - Some call Sher Shah the first to introduce a ryotwari-type system.
[128:4:6] # Sher Shah vs Akbar
[128:4:7] TABLE head: Point | Sher Shah | Akbar – Todar Mal
  [128:4:7.0] Share | 1/3 | 1/3
  [128:4:7.1] Basis | Average produce | Ten-year averages
  [128:4:7.2] Land classes | Three | Four – polaj, parauti, chachar, banjar
[128:4:8] APPSC G1 2024: three categories – TRUE; uniform measurement – TRUE; "one-fourth" share – FALSE.
## SUB 5 | Currency, Roads and Sarais
[128:5:0] # Currency
[128:5:1] TABLE head: Coin | Metal | Note
  [128:5:1.0] Rupiya | Silver | About 178 grains; introduced 1540-45 (APPSC 2019); standard for centuries (APPSC G2 2017)
  [128:5:1.1] Dam | Copper | Retained by Akbar
  [128:5:1.2] Mohur | Gold | –
[128:5:2] - Minted pure gold and silver coins; the reform outlived the Sur dynasty.
[128:5:3] # Grand Trunk Road
[128:5:4] - **Sadak-e-Azam** (Grand Trunk Road) – **built / restored** by Sher Shah (APPSC 2018: he restored it).
[128:5:5] - Route: **Sonargaon (Bengal) to the Indus** (APPSC G1 2022: "Indus to Sonargaon"); also given as Sonargaon to Kabul / East Bengal to Peshawar.
[128:5:6] - **Other Sur roads:** Agra–Jodhpur–Chittor, Agra–**Burhanpur** and **Lahore–Multan**, with sarais at intervals.
[128:5:7] # Sarais
[128:5:8] - Built along roads; served as **dak-chowkis** (news and mail relay, with horses).
[128:5:9] - **Market towns** grew around them.
## SUB 6 | Sur Successors, Hemu and the End of Afghan Rule
[128:6:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Fact
  [128:6:0.0] Islam Shah (Jalal Khan) | Sher Shah's second son; ruled 1545-53; troop units of 200/250/500
  [128:6:0.1] Muhammad Adil Shah | Ruled the east; rival of Sikandar Sur
  [128:6:0.2] Sikandar Sur | Beaten by Humayun at Sirhind (1555)
  [128:6:0.3] Hemu (Hem Chandra Vikramaditya) | Adil Shah's Hindu army chief and Prime Minister; seized Agra and Delhi (1556)
[128:6:1] - **Panipat II – 5 November 1556**: Akbar (Bairam Khan) vs Hemu; Hemu struck in the **eye by an arrow** and captured; Afghan power ended.
[128:6:2] - **Surs = last Afghan dynasty** to rule Delhi; their revenue, coinage and roads set the Mughal pattern.
[128:6:3] Panipat II was fought against Hemu, not Sher Shah.

=== NOTES 1:130 | Akbar's Institutions – Mansabdari System, Todar Mal's Bandobast, Dahsala, Zabti and Other Revenue Systems
## SUB 0 | Road to the Dahsala
[130:0:0] - Mansabdari + zabti-dahsala = a **salaried, transferable nobility** + **predictable cash revenue**.
[130:0:1] Sher Shah's survey system → **Karori experiment (1574)** → karoris collect revenue and cultivation data → **Dahsala (1580)**, Todar Mal.
[130:0:2] # Karori Experiment – 1574
[130:0:3] - Core provinces split into **circles**, each to yield **one crore dams**, under officers called **karoris**.
[130:0:4] - Karoris collected revenue and reported cultivation data – groundwork for the dahsala.
[130:0:5] **1574 = Karori experiment; 1580 = Dahsala.**
## SUB 1 | Raja Todar Mal
[130:1:0] - Earlier served **Sher Shah**; built on his survey-based system.
[130:1:1] - **Diwan** (finance minister) of the empire; one of the **Navratnas**.
[130:1:2] - **Muzaffar Khan Turbati** was first given the proper status of Diwan by Akbar, with Todar Mal as assistant; Todar Mal and **Khwaja Shah Mansur** came later.
[130:1:3] TABLE head: Unit | Fact
  [130:1:3.0] Gaz-i-Ilahi | Unit of length, about 82 cm
  [130:1:3.1] Jarib | Bamboo pieces joined with iron rings – replaced hemp rope, which shrank or stretched
  [130:1:3.2] State share | One-third of average produce, paid in cash (dam); kind allowed in some areas
## SUB 2 | Revenue Systems
[130:2:0] TABLE head: System | Meaning | Region / year
  [130:2:0.0] Zabti (Bandobast) | Land measured; dasturs with cash rates per crop per bigha | Core North Indian provinces (Lahore to Allahabad, Malwa, Gujarat)
  [130:2:0.1] Dahsala (Ain-i-Dahsala) | Average produce and prices of the last ten years (1571-1580), turned into cash | 1580 (APPSC G2 2017)
  [130:2:0.2] Batai / Ghalla-bakhshi (Bantai) | Crop-sharing – actual produce divided between state and peasant | Thatta, Kabul, Kashmir
  [130:2:0.3] Nasaq | Rough assessment from past revenue records, no fresh measurement | Mainly Bengal
  [130:2:0.4] Kankut | Yield estimated on the standing crop | Where measuring was impractical
  [130:2:0.5] Plough assessment | By number of ploughs | Parts of the Deccan (J.N. Sarkar, History of Aurangzeb)
[130:2:1] - **"Bandobast system" = Dahsala** (JPSC 2013); also called "Zabti or Bandobast".
[130:2:2] - Zabti was introduced by **Akbar**.
[130:2:3] - Sher Shah's three systems: **Nasq / Muktai / Kankut**, **Nakadi or Jabti**, **Galla Bakshi or Bantai**.
[130:2:4] - **Iqta** = Sultanate revenue assignment (**Iltutmish**).
## SUB 3 | Land Classification

  [130:3:0.0] Polaj | Cultivated every year
  [130:3:0.1] Parauti | Fallow 1-2 years
  [130:3:0.2] Chachar | Fallow 3-4 years
  [130:3:0.3] Banjar | Fallow 5+ years

## SUB 4 | Revenue and Grant Terms
[130:4:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [130:4:0.0] Maal (mal) | Land revenue – the principal state income
  [130:4:0.1] Khalisa | Crown land; revenue straight to the imperial treasury; reserve for cash (naqd) salaries
  [130:4:0.2] Jagir | Right to collect revenue in lieu of salary – not ownership
  [130:4:0.3] Madad-i-maash | Revenue-free grant to scholars, holy men, the needy
  [130:4:0.4] Suyurghal | Another name for such charitable revenue-free grants
## SUB 5 | Mansabdari System
[130:5:0] - Introduced by **Akbar**; of **Central Asian / Mongol** military origin.
