## SUB 0 | Religious Policy – Ibadat Khana, Mahzar, Din-i-Ilahi, Sulh-i-Kul
[0:0] # Measures in order
[0:1] TABLE head: Year | Measure
  [0:1.0] 1563 | Pilgrimage tax abolished – by Akbar (not Bahlul Lodi, Sher Shah or Humayun)
  [0:1.1] 1564 | Jizya abolished
  [0:1.2] 1575 | Ibadat Khana (House of Worship) built at Fatehpur Sikri – first for Ulemas, Sayyids, Shaikhs
  [0:1.3] 1576 | Haj caravans from the state treasury under a Mir Haj – Akbar was the first Indian ruler to organise the Haj at state expense (UPPSC 1998)
  [0:1.4] 1578 | Ibadat Khana opened to all religions – Hindus, Jains, Christians, Zoroastrians
  [0:1.5] 1579 | Mahzar – an attested statement of leading Ulemas making Akbar supreme arbiter in religious disputes; issued by Akbar, drafted by Shaikh Mubarak
  [0:1.6] 1582 | Din-i-Ilahi (Tauhid-i-Ilahi) launched
[0:2] - **Chronology:** **jizya 1564 → Ibadat Khana 1575 → Mahzar 1579 → Din-i-Ilahi 1582 = 1, 2, 3, 4** (UP UDA 2006).
[0:3] - **Ibadat Khana = the hall where Akbar held discussions with scholars of various religions** (UPSC 2014) – **built by Akbar (1575)**; not a royal mosque, a private prayer chamber or a nobles' room.
[0:4] - Guiding principle **Sulh-i-Kul** = "**peace with all**" (universal harmony) – **promulgated by Akbar** (not Nizamuddin Auliya, Zainul Abidin or Nasiruddin Chirag); the result of **political generosity, religious tolerance and a liberal cultural attitude – all three** (UPPCS 1998).
[0:5] - Coins with **Rama and Sita** and the Devanagari legend **"Rama-Siya"** – **Akbar** (UPPCS 2011), not Bhoja, Siddharaja or Zain-ul-Abidin.
[0:6] - Akbar is called **"an enlightened despot"** (UP Mains 2009).
[0:7] - Akbar's reign is known for **annexation, provincial administration, judicial administration – NOT religious intolerance** (1, 2, 3); his popularity rested on **mansabdari, religious policy, land revenue and social reforms – all** (UPPSC 1990).
[0:8] - He did **not** forsake Islam – that is the factor NOT conducive to calling him a nationalist (RAS 1994); unity in law, administration and culture and his religious policy were.
[0:9] - **Pilgrim tax at Banaras and Allahabad:** a deputation of Banaras pandits led by **Kavindracharya** (Sarasvati) persuaded **Shah Jahan** to end it (UPPCS 2000).

[0:12] # Din-i-Ilahi (1582)
[0:13] - **Promoted / promulgated by Akbar** (MPPSC 1998; State PSC); **launched 1582** (CGPSC 2011).
[0:14] - Features: **one God**, greeting "Allah-hu-Akbar", reverence for **sun and fire**, avoiding meat; piety, prudence, kindness.
[0:15] - **Never forced** – a voluntary small circle of about **18**, not a mass religion; **Abul Fazl** its chief exponent; **Birbal the only Hindu**; it **ended with Akbar's death**.
[0:16] - **Why it failed (SSC 2009):** not patronised after Akbar, rejected by orthodox Muslims, never projected to the masses – **all of the above**.
[0:17] - Debate: an ideology based on **Sufi pantheism**, not a religion; **Mohsin Fani** (Jahangir's time) called it a religion in **Dabistan-i-Mazahib** – none of **Abul Fazl, Badauni, Nizamuddin** (UPPCS 2000: "none of these").
[0:18] - Orthodox critics: **Badauni** and **Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi** (Naqshbandi, "Mujaddid Alf-i-Sani").

  [1:0.5] Man Singh I | General (Kachhwaha of Amber) | Led the Mughals at Haldighati

[1:1] - **Ata Ali Khan** – the name of **Abul Faizi** (CGPSC 2014 key).
[1:2] - **Abul Fazl's death** was caused by **Prince Salim** (Jharkhand 2013); actual killer **Bir Singh Deo Bundela** (MPSC 2009) – not Hemu, Bairam Khan or Udai Singh.
[1:3] - **Abul Fazl** is the medieval writer who **refers to the discovery of America** ("alam-i-nau", the New World) (UPSC 1997 ×2) – not Jayasi, Amir Khusrau or Raskhan.
[1:4] - Abul Fazl **shaped, represented and articulated the ideas of Akbar's reign**; his qualities made Akbar choose him as **adviser and spokesperson** – statement II explains I (NDA 2019; NCERT Themes II).
[1:5] - **Sheikh Mubarak** – father of Abul Fazl and Faizi. Death order: **Sheikh Mubarak (1593) → Faizi (1595) → Abul Fazl (1602) → Prince Daniyal (1605)** = 2, 3, 1, 4 (UPPSC 2024).
[1:6] - **Persian Mahabharata (Razmnama)** – translated by Naqib Khan, Badauni and others **under the supervision of Faizi** (RAS 1999) – not Utbi, Naziri or Abul Fazl.
[1:7] - Akbar **learnt Hindu music from Lala Kalavant** (UPPSC 2016); Dhrupad under Akbar: Tansen and **Swami Haridas**; **Vilas Khan** – Jahangir's court.
[1:8] # Maktab Khana and translations

[1:10] - **Razmnama** = Persian **Mahabharata**; Atharvaveda, Rajatarangini and the Baburnama also translated; **Nizamuddin Panipati** – Persian **Yogavasistha**.
[1:11] - **Buland Darwaza** at Fatehpur Sikri commemorates the **Gujarat conquest (1572-73)**.

## SUB 2 | Accession, Bairam Khan's Regency and Conquests
[2:0] # Accession and regency
[2:1] - Born **15 October 1542** at **Amarkot**; enthroned **14 February 1556** at **Kalanaur (Gurdaspur, Punjab)** on news of Humayun's death (CGPSC 2015) – not Kabul, Lahore or Sirhind – by **Bairam Khan**.
[2:2] - **Age at coronation:** books and keys say **fourteen** ("only fourteen years old", NextGen); strictly **13 years 4 months** ("under 14", Rocket).
[2:3] - **Panipat II – 5 November 1556**: Hemu hit in the eye by an arrow; Afghan power ended.
[2:4] TABLE head: Phase | Years | Who dominated
  [2:4.0] Bairam Khan's regency | 1556-60 (JPSC 2010) | Bairam Khan – made Wakil/Wazir and given the title Khan-i-Khanan by Akbar at that time; dismissed 1560; killed 1561 on the way to Mecca
  [2:4.1] Petticoat government | 1560-64 | Maham Anaga and her son Adham Khan
[2:5] - **Adham Khan** was **personally killed by Akbar (1562)** for murdering the minister **Atagh (Atka) Khan** – not Bairam Khan, Baz Bahadur or Pir Muhammad.
[2:6] - Aurangzeb was crowned twice (21 July 1658 Delhi; 5 June 1659); his first coronation was NOT at Kalanaur.

## SUB 3 | Rajput Policy, Mewar and Haldighati
[3:0] - Twin policy of **conquest and conciliation**: surrender followed by restoration of **autonomy and honour**; high posts, marriages, toleration.
[3:1] - **First marriage alliance – 1562**, with the daughter of **Raja Bharmal (Bihari Mal) of Amber – the Kachhwahas** (State PSC; UPRO 2016; UPPCS 2004) – not Rathores, Sisodias, Chauhans or Bundelas; on Akbar's pilgrimage to **Ajmer**.
[3:2] - Top Rajput mansabdars: **Man Singh, Bhagwan Das**; also Raja Todar Mal and Raja Birbal.
[3:3] - **Contemporaries of Akbar:** Man Singh, Amar Singh, Udai Singh – **Jaswant Singh** of Marwar belongs to Shah Jahan–Aurangzeb (WBCS 2018).
[3:4] # Mewar – the holdout
[3:5] - **Mewar did not accept Akbar's supremacy willingly** (UP Lower 2004) – Amber, Marwar, Bikaner did.
[3:6] - **Haldighati – 18 June 1576** (UKPSC 2010; CGPSC 2016) – **Maharana Pratap** vs Akbar's army under **Man Singh of Amber**; aim: **to subdue Rana Pratap** (RAS 1992).
[3:7] - **Hakim Khan Sur** commanded Pratap's army (vanguard of Afghans) – not Amar Singh, Man Singh or Shakti Singh.
[3:8] - Rana Pratap employed **Baksariya Muslims** in his army (APPSC G1 2019 key).
[3:9] - Mewar submitted only under **Jahangir** – **Amar Singh**'s treaty, **1615**.
[3:10] - **Rao Chandrasena of Marwar** never surrendered (Bhadrajun 1571, Siwana; died 1581) – Rai Singh of Bikaner and Bharmal submitted; Amar Singh submitted to Jahangir (RAS 2012).
[3:11] - Pairs: Chausa = 1539 (not 1538), Kannauj = 1540 (not 1539), **Haldighati = 1576**, Khajwa = 1659 (not 1657).
[3:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History Part 4 p.26-27; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian History Part 1 p.73-74; app notes 1:129 (sub 2)
## SUB 4 | Social and Educational Reforms
[4:0] - **Sati** was prohibited / curbed under **Akbar** (WBCS 2018) – widows not to be burnt against their will.
[4:1] - **Monogamy order:** a man to marry **only one wife**, a second only if the first was **barren** – **Akbar** (UPPCS 2002).
[4:2] - **Marriage age fixed** (16 for boys, 14 for girls) and **consent of bride and groom** required – **both statements true** (UPPSC 2020).
[4:3] - Discouraged child marriage, dowries, marriages between close relatives; allowed widow remarriage.
[4:4] - **Educational reforms:** **Akbar** (MPPSC 2014) – curriculum of arithmetic, logic, geometry, astronomy, accountancy, agriculture, ethics, medicine, history.
[4:5] - **Memoirs:** Babur – Tuzuk-i-Baburi in **Turki**; Jahangir – Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri in **Persian**; Akbar wrote none (Abul Fazl's Akbarnama); Humayun was learned in Persian, Arabic and Turkish.
[4:6] - **Akbar was unlettered (illiterate)** but a great patron of learning (APPSC G2 2011) – Babur, Jahangir and Aurangzeb were literate.
[4:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History Part 4 p.12 (education), p.27 (social reforms); app notes 1:129 (sub 3, 8)
