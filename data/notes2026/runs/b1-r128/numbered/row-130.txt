## SUB 0 | Revenue Systems – Todar Mal's Bandobast, Dahsala, Zabti
[0:0] # Road to the Dahsala
[0:1] - Sher Shah's survey system → **Karori experiment (1574)** → **Dahsala (1580)** under **Raja Todar Mal**.
[0:2] - **Karori experiment (1574):** core provinces split into circles each yielding **one crore dams**, under officers called **karoris**, who collected revenue and cultivation data.
[0:3] - Mansabdari + zabti-dahsala = a **salaried, transferable nobility** + **predictable cash revenue**.
[0:4] # Raja Todar Mal
[0:5] - Earlier served **Sher Shah**; built on his survey-based system; **Diwan** (finance minister); one of the Navratnas.
[0:6] - **Todar Mal = land revenue reforms** (UP Mains 2012) – not law, literature or music.
[0:7] - **Muzaffar Khan Turbati** was the **first to be given the proper status of Diwan** by Akbar, with Todar Mal as assistant (UP Lower 2003) – not Asaf Khan, Munim Khan or Todar Mal; Todar Mal and **Khwaja Shah Mansur** came later.
[0:8] TABLE head: Unit | Fact
  [0:8.0] Gaz-i-Ilahi | Unit of length, about 82 cm
  [0:8.1] Jarib | Bamboo pieces joined with iron rings – replaced hemp rope
  [0:8.2] State share | One-third of average produce, paid in cash (dam); kind allowed in some areas
[0:9] # The systems
[0:10] TABLE head: System | Meaning | Region / year
  [0:10.0] Zabti | Land measured; dasturs = cash rates per crop per bigha | Introduced by Akbar; core North Indian provinces (Lahore to Allahabad, Malwa, Gujarat)
  [0:10.1] Dahsala (Ain-i-Dahsala) | Revenue on the average produce and prices of the last ten years (1571-80), converted into cash | 1580
  [0:10.2] Batai / Ghalla-bakhshi | Crop-sharing | Thatta, Kabul, Kashmir
  [0:10.3] Nasaq | Rough assessment from past revenue records | Mainly Bengal
  [0:10.4] Kankut | Yield estimated on the standing crop | Where measuring was impractical
  [0:10.5] Plough assessment | By number of ploughs | Parts of the Deccan under Akbar (UPPCS 2017; J.N. Sarkar)
[0:11] - **Dahsala = collection of revenue on the average of produce and prices of the last 10 years** (APPSC G2 2017) – NOT a fixed 10-year rate, a 10-yearly measurement, or a 10-year crop-sharing deal.
[0:12] - **"Bandobast system" = Dahsala** (Jharkhand 2013; JPSC 2013); also called Zabti-Bandobast.
[0:13] - **Zabti was introduced by Akbar** – not Ghiyasuddin Tughluq, Sikandar Lodi or Sher Shah.

## SUB 2 | Mansabdari System
[2:0] - **Introduced by Akbar** (APPSC Technical Education) – not Sher Shah, Jahangir or Aurangzeb; of **Central Asian / Mongol** military origin; basis of Mughal civil and military administration.
[2:1] - Mansabdars held **combined civil-military, non-hereditary** ranks, paid by **jagir** (or cash).
[2:2] - Ranks: **10 to 5,000** for nobles (later **7,000**); higher ranks only for **royal princes**.
[2:3] - **Zat** = personal rank and pay; **Sawar** = horsemen to maintain – dual ranks from **1595-96**; sawar equal to zat = **1st class**.
[2:4] - **Dagh** (horse branding) – **1573-74**.
[2:5] - **Horses:** for **every ten cavalrymen, twenty horses** – because horses had to be **rested on the march and replaced in war** (IAS 1999: A and R true, R explains A).
[2:6] - **Du-aspa sih-aspa** – troopers with two/three horses without raising zat – **Jahangir** (first for Mahabat Khan).
[2:7] - Land grants: **Iqta – Delhi Sultans; Jagir – Mughals; Amaram – Vijayanagara; Mokasa – Marathas** (UPSC 2000).
[2:8] - Mansabdar = civil-military, non-hereditary, paid by jagir; ICS = purely civil, fixed salary.
[2:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History Part 4 p.32-33; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian History Part 2 (src/indian-history/rocket-indian-history-02.txt) p.34; app notes 1:130 (sub 5), 1:129 (sub 7)
