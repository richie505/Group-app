## SUB 0 | Humayun – Battles, Chronology and Exile
[0:0] # Accession and problems
[0:1] - **Accession:** **29 December 1530**, three days after Babur's death; Babur's eldest son, well educated (Persian, Arabic, Turkish, astronomy); earlier governor of Badakhshan.
[0:2] - **Rival brothers:** **Kamran, Askari, Hindal** – NOT **Usman** (UP Lower 2008) and not Mirza Hakim (Akbar's half-brother). Kamran got the Indus region (Kabul–Punjab).
[0:3] - **Rao Jaitsi of Bikaner** defeated **Kamran Mirza** (1534), sent by Humayun – source **Rao-Jaitsi-Ro-Chhanda** (RAS 2024); Jaitsi did not lose Bikaner.
[0:4] - Other threats: the **Afghans** (Sher Khan, Mahmud Lodi) and **Bahadur Shah** of Gujarat–Malwa.
[0:5] # Humayun's campaigns in order
[0:6] TABLE head: Event | Year | Outcome
  [0:6.0] Kalinjar invaded | 1531 | Early campaign; siege lifted to face the Afghans
  [0:6.1] Devara / Dauhariya (Dourah), on the Gomati | 1532 | Humayun beat the Afghans; drove Mahmud Lodi from Jaunpur
  [0:6.2] Chunar Fort – first siege | 1532 | Four-month siege; Sher Khan accepted Humayun's supremacy (BPSC 2008) – not 1531 (Kalinjar)
  [0:6.3] Gujarat | 1535 | Pressed by Humayun, Bahadur Shah let the Portuguese fortify Diu
  [0:6.4] Bengal campaign | 1537-38 | Humayun = first Mughal emperor to campaign against Bengal (IAS 2003); Sher Khan had taken Gaur (1538)
  [0:6.5] Chausa | 26 June 1539 | Sher Khan beat Humayun; took the title "Sher Shah"; water-carrier Nizam saved Humayun
  [0:6.6] Kannauj = Bilgram | 17 May 1540 | Sher Shah beat Humayun, took the throne; Humayun into exile
  [0:6.7] Sirhind (and Machhiwara) | 1555 | Humayun beat Sikandar Sur; retook Delhi and Agra (July 1555)
[0:7] - **Order of Humayun's four battles:** **Devara (Dauhariya) → Chausa → Kannauj → Sirhind** (BPSC 1996; State PSC; UP RO 2017).
[0:8] - **Kannauj and Bilgram are ONE battle (1540)** – lost by Humayun; **Chausa** was Humayun vs Sher Khan, so "**Jahangir – Chausa**" is the wrong pair (APPSC G2); NDA 2007: Chausa = Humayun and Sher Khan.
[0:9] # Exile, return and death
[0:10] - Married **Hamida Banu Begum**; **Akbar born at Amarkot (1542)**; Akbar crowned at **Kalanaur** after Humayun's death.
[0:11] - Refuge in **Persia**: the Safavid **Shah Tahmasp** helped him retake **Kandahar and Kabul** (price: Kandahar, Shia leanings).
[0:12] - Returned **July 1555** – NOT 1566; he lost Kannauj (1540), not "Khanua", to Sher Khan – so in the Grama Sachivalayam statements "Khanua 1540" and "Delhi 1566" are **both wrong (none)**.
[0:13] - Died **January 1556** falling from the stairs of the **Sher Mandal** library, **Purana Qila (Dinpanah)**.
[0:14] - **First ruler after the Delhi Sultanate to issue a gold coin: Humayun** (Ghatna Chakra; UKPSC 2012 key gave Akbar); Sher Shah also struck gold.
[0:15] # Battles 1526–1707 – chronology questions

  [0:16.0] 1526 | Panipat I | Babur vs Ibrahim Lodi
  [0:16.1] 1527 | Khanwa | Babur vs Rana Sanga
  [0:16.2] 1528 | Chanderi | Babur vs Medini Rai
  [0:16.3] 1529 | Ghaghra | Babur vs Afghans (Mahmud Lodi) – Babur's last battle
  [0:16.4] 1532 | Devara / Dauhariya | Humayun vs Afghans
  [0:16.5] 1539 | Chausa | Sher Khan beat Humayun
  [0:16.6] 1540 | Kannauj / Bilgram | Sher Shah beat Humayun
  [0:16.7] 1555 | Sirhind | Humayun beat Sikandar Sur
  [0:16.8] 1556 | Panipat II | Akbar (Bairam Khan) vs Hemu

  [0:16.10] 1573 | Sarnal | Akbar vs Gujarat rebels (Mirzas)
  [0:16.11] 1576 | Haldighati | Man Singh (Akbar) vs Maharana Pratap

  [0:16.13] 1707 | Jajau | Bahadur Shah I vs Azam Shah
[0:17] - **Chanderi → Chausa → Kannauj → Talikota** = II, IV, I, III (UP RO 2016); **Khanwa → Ghaghra → Chausa → Samugarh** (CGPSC 2008); **Dauhariya → Chausa → Kannauj → Samugarh** (UP RO 2017); **Bilgram → Sarnal → Dharmat → Jajau** (UPPSC 2019).
[0:18] - **Samugarh (1658)** = Aurangzeb vs Dara Shikoh – not a Humayun battle. "Panipat II – Akbar and Sher Shah" is wrong (Hemu).
[0:19] - Rulers in order: **Raziyya (1236-40) → Alauddin Khalji (1296-1316) → Sher Shah (1540-45) → Akbar (1556-1605)**.
[0:20] - AP angle: **Sultan Quli Qutb Shah** of Golconda (**1518-1543**) was a contemporary of Babur, Humayun and Sher Shah.

## SUB 1 | Sher Shah – Rise, Titles, Rajput Wars and Death
[1:0] # Rise
[1:1] - Born **Farid (Farid Khan)**; father **Hasan Sur**, an Afghan **jagirdar of Sasaram** (Bihar); of the **Sur** tribe.
[1:2] - Left home (1494 / 1501) and **studied Arabic and Persian at Jaunpur** – the "**Siraj-i-Hind**" (RAS 2013) – not Sasaram, Patna or Lahore; one of the most learned medieval rulers.
[1:3] - Managed his father's jagir from **1511**; joined **Bahar Khan** of Bihar, who gave him the title **"Sher Khan"** for killing a **tiger**.
[1:4] - Served Babur; deputy regent and de facto ruler of Bihar; victory at **Surajgarh** (1534) was the turning point; took **Gaur** (1538) and **Rohtas**.
[1:5] - **Sher Khan's offer:** surrender Bihar and pay 10 lakh dinars a year if allowed to keep Bengal – Humayun refused (BPSC 2022).
[1:6] # Titles in order
[1:7] TABLE head: Title | When
  [1:7.0] Sher Khan | From Bahar Khan (tiger)
  [1:7.1] Hazrat-e-Ala | After defeating Nusrat Shah of Bengal
  [1:7.2] Sher Shah / Sultan | After Chausa, 26 June 1539; khutba read and coins struck in his name
[1:8] - **Hazrat-e-Ala first, Sultan afterwards = Sher Shah Suri** (not Bahlul/Sikandar Lodi or Islam Shah); his titles = **Hazrat-e-Ala and Sultan only** (BPSC 2000) – not Zill-e-Ilahi or Raiyat-e-Ala.
[1:9] # Conquests and Rajput resistance
[1:10] - **Raisen** taken from **Puran Mal**; Punjab, Malwa, Sind conquered.
[1:11] - **Marwar:** **Maldeo Rathore** resisted; battle of **Samel (Sammel / Giri-Sumel), 1544**.
[1:12] - Maldeo's generals **Jaita and Kumpa (Jayata–Kumpa) of Marwar** fought so bravely that Sher Shah said: "**I would have lost the empire of Hindustan for a handful of bajra (millet)**" (UPPSC 2007; UP Mains 2007; UPPCS 2022).
[1:13] # Death and verdict
[1:14] - **Last expedition: Kalinjar** (Kalanjar, a Rajput fort) – died **22 May 1545**, wounded by a **gunpowder explosion** (UP 1993; UP RO 2016).
[1:15] - Buried at **Sasaram**; reign only **five years (1540-45)**.
[1:16] - He was the **architect of a brilliant administrative system**, and **his qualities as a ruler were MORE remarkable than his battle victories** – so "not more remarkable" is false (UP RO 2021: only 1).
[1:17] - Sequence: Chunar submission 1532 → Chausa 1539 → Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque 1541 → death at Kalinjar 1545.
[1:18] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History Part 4 p.23-24; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian History Part 1 p.54 (Sher Khan title); app notes 1:128 (sub 2)
## SUB 2 | Currency, Grand Trunk Road and Sarais
[2:0] # Currency
[2:1] TABLE head: Coin | Metal | Note
  [2:1.0] Rupiya | Silver | 178 grains (173 pure silver); introduced 1540-45; standard for centuries (APPSC G2 2017)
  [2:1.1] Dam (daam) | Copper | 380 grains; kept by Akbar as the chief copper coin
  [2:1.2] Mohur | Gold | 166-169 grains
[2:2] - **Rupiya : Dam = 1 : 64** (UP UDA 2006).
[2:3] - **The rupiya / "rupee" was first issued by Sher Shah Suri** (CDS 2009; Jharkhand 2016; MPPSC 1991; APPSC G2 2017) – not Muhammad bin Tughlaq, Alauddin Khalji or Akbar.
[2:4] - Pure gold, silver and copper coins; mixed-metal coins abolished; name, title and mint in **Arabic and Devanagari**.
[2:5] - **Akbar, like Sher Shah, regulated the currency**, and the **dam** stayed Akbar's chief copper coin – both true, but the dam fact does not explain the regulation (UPPCS 2019).
[2:6] - ⚠ Silver coins (the **tanka**) were struck from **Iltutmish's** time; "introduced the coin of silver" in MPPSC 1991 means Sher Shah's **silver rupiya**.
[2:7] # Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-e-Azam)
[2:8] - **Sadak-e-Azam = Grand Trunk Road** – one of Asia's oldest major roads; **built / restored by Sher Shah Suri** (APPSC 2018; CGPSC 2018) – not Akbar, Jahangir, Islam Shah or Shah Jahan.
[2:9] - Route: **Sonargaon (East Bengal) to the Indus** – "**Indus to Sonargaon**" is the longest road (APPSC G1); also given as Sonargaon–Peshawar / Kabul; it **still exists**.
[2:10] - **Other Sur roads:** Agra–Jodhpur–Chittor, **Agra–Burhanpur**, **Lahore–Multan**.
[2:11] # Sarais
[2:12] - Sarais at intervals with shady trees; separate lodging for Hindus and Muslims.
[2:13] - Served as **dak-chowkis** (news and mail relay with horses); **market towns** (qasbas) grew around them.
[2:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History Part 4 p.24; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian History Part 1 p.100-101; app notes 1:128 (sub 5)

  [3:0.1] Purana Qila (Old Fort, Dinpanah) | Citadel begun by Humayun; present form by Sher Shah | Delhi

[3:1] - **Sher Shah's mausoleum / burial: Sasaram** (MPPSC 2016; UP Mains 2015; State PSC) – not Delhi, Kalinjar, Jaunpur, Patna, Maner, Sitamarhi or Pavapuri.
[3:2] - **Purana Qila in its present form = Sher Shah** (UP Mains 2016; UP Lower 2009 ×2) – not Firuz Tughlaq, Ibrahim Lodi, Babur, Akbar or Shah Jahan.
[3:3] - **Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque = Sher Shah** (IAS 1993 ×2; UP RO 2021) – Atala Masjid (Jaunpur) = Sharqis; **Bara Sona Masjid (Gaur)** = Bengal Sultans; **Quwwat-ul-Islam** = Qutbuddin Aibak.
[3:4] - **Built by Sher Shah: Sasaram Tomb + Qila-e-Kuhna Mosque (1, 2)** – the "Tomb of Sikandar" at Sikandra is Akbar's (completed by Jahangir, 1612); Delhi's **Jama Masjid** is Shah Jahan's (RAS 2024).
[3:5] - **Humayun-nama** – by **Gulbadan Begum**, Humayun's sister.

## SUB 4 | Sur Administration and Land Revenue
[4:0] # Administrative units and officers
[4:1] - Empire divided into **47 Sarkars**, each into **Parganas** (CDS 2014: statement I true); officers were appointed at every level – so "administered sarkar and pargana directly without officials" is false.
[4:2] TABLE head: Level | Officer | Role
  [4:2.0] Sarkar | Chief Shiqdar (shiqdar-i-shiqdaran) | Law and order
  [4:2.1] Sarkar | Chief Munsif (munsif-i-munsifan) | Revenue / civil matters
  [4:2.2] Pargana | Shiqdar | Law and order
  [4:2.3] Pargana | Amin (munsif) | Assessment of revenue
  [4:2.4] Pargana | Fotedar | Treasurer
  [4:2.5] Pargana | Karkuns | Hindi and Persian writers
  [4:2.6] Village | Qanungo, patwari, muqaddam | Revenue / local records
[4:3] - Officers were **frequently transferred** so they did not build local interests.
[4:4] - **Army:** regular army recruited directly; **Dagh** = branding of horses; **chehra / huliya** = descriptive roll of soldiers; Hindus in high army posts.
[4:5] - **Police and justice:** **local headmen** had to trace crime; censors of public morals; efficient **spy network**; uniform, harsh justice.
[4:6] - Sher Shah's reforms covered **revenue, administration, army and currency – all four** (UKPSC 2003).
[4:7] # Land revenue
[4:8] TABLE head: Point | Fact
  [4:8.0] Measurement | Uniform system of survey – Gaz-i-Sikandari (sikandari gaz), rope / jarib
  [4:8.1] Classes | Three – good, middling, bad – by produce
  [4:8.2] State share | One-third (1/3) of average produce
  [4:8.3] Payment | Cash or grain
  [4:8.4] Settlement | Direct state–peasant (ryotwari-type); illegal demands removed; tenant rights recognised
  [4:8.5] Legacy | Model for Todar Mal's Bandobast
[4:9] - **Rate of land tax: 1/3 of produce** (APPSC G1 2012) – not 1/4, 1/2 or 1/5.
[4:10] - **APPSC G1 PYQ:** three categories – TRUE; uniform measurement – TRUE; "1/4 collected" – FALSE → **I and II**. ⚠ NextGen writes "one fourth or one third"; the standard books (Satish Chandra) give **one-third**.
[4:11] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:11.0] Patta | Title deed stating the state's share; given to the cultivator
  [4:11.1] Qabuliyat | Deed of acceptance signed by the peasant
  [4:11.2] Jaribana | Surveyor's fee
  [4:11.3] Muhasilana | Collector's fee
[4:12] - **Patta and Qabuliyat were introduced by Sher Shah** (UP Mains 2009; WBCS 2008, 2020) – not Alauddin, Muhammad bin Tughluq, Akbar, Shivaji, Jahangir or Aurangzeb.
[4:13] - **Sher Shah vs Akbar:** both took **1/3**; Sher Shah 3 land classes, Akbar 4 (polaj, parauti, chachar, banjar) with ten-year averages.
[4:14] - **Mansabdari was introduced by Akbar**, not Sher Shah (APPSC Technical Education).
