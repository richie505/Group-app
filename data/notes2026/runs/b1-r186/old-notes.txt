## SUB 1 | Government of India Act, 1858
[186:1:0] # Core Change
[186:1:1] - Officially the **Act for the Good Government of India**; also called the **Act of Administrative Reforms in India**.
[186:1:2] - **Abolished Company rule**: government, territories and revenues of India moved from the Company to the **British Crown**; Company territories now governed by the Queen.
[186:1:3] - **1858 = watershed**: start of Crown rule (1858–1947).
[186:1:4] # Which Act Transferred Power? (option trap)

  [186:1:5.0] Pitt's India Act | 1784 | Left the Company in place (dual control)
  [186:1:5.1] Charter Act | 1833 | GG of India (Bentinck); Company purely administrative
  [186:1:5.2] Charter Act | 1853 | Company still in place
  [186:1:5.3] GoI Act | 1858 | Company rule ended; Crown rule via SoS and Viceroy
  [186:1:5.4] Indian Councils Act | 1861 | Came after the transfer
  [186:1:5.5] Royal Titles Act | 1876 | Victoria became Empress of India (proclaimed 1877)
  [186:1:5.6] GoI Act | 1935 | Abolished the Council of India
[186:1:6] Power to the Crown = **1858 Act** (APPSC 2018, 2019), not ICA 1861. GG of Bengal → GG of India = **Charter Act 1833** (APPSC G2 2024, 2025).
[186:1:7] # Offices Created in 1858
[186:1:8] TABLE head: Office | Role
  [186:1:8.0] Secretary of State for India | British cabinet minister and member of Parliament, responsible to Parliament; Crown used its powers through him; could issue orders to the Viceroy; first = Lord Stanley
  [186:1:8.1] Council of India | 15 members, in London; advised the SoS; abolished by the 1935 Act
  [186:1:8.2] Viceroy | GG got the additional title Viceroy and Governor-General (Crown's representative); first = Lord Canning
[186:1:9] - Same office-holder: **Viceroy** for the **princely states**, **Governor-General** for **British India**.
[186:1:10] - **Lord Canning (Charles John Canning)**: GG during the Revolt (1856–58), first Viceroy (1858); made **Allahabad** the emergency headquarters in 1857.
## SUB 2 | Queen's Proclamation, 1 November 1858
[186:2:0] # Basics
[186:2:1] - Read by **Lord Canning** at a **Durbar at Allahabad (Prayagraj)** on **1 November 1858**.
[186:2:2] - Addressed to "**the Princes, Chiefs and Peoples of India**".
[186:2:3] - Called the **"Magna Carta of the Indian people"** (promises on rulers, religion, public service, property, land).
[186:2:4] - **Objects (UPSC 2014):** disclaim annexation of Indian States + place administration under the Crown; **not** to regulate Company trade.
[186:2:5] # Promises – Kept vs Broken
[186:2:6] TABLE head: Promise | Detail | Fate
  [186:2:6.0] Princes | No further annexation; rights, dignity, honour respected; Company treaties honoured; adoption allowed | Kept
  [186:2:6.1] Religion | No interference in religion and social customs; no forced conversions | Kept
  [186:2:6.2] Public service | Appointments by merit and qualifications, without discrimination of race or creed | Broken – ICS stayed European-dominated
  [186:2:6.3] Laws and property | Ancient rights, usages, customs respected; property and succession protected; peasants' land rights while revenue was paid | Partly
[186:2:7] - SoS **Kimberley**: an adequate number of civil servants "**shall always be Europeans**".
[186:2:8] - **1858–1885 = "Era of Broken Promises"**.
[186:2:9] **Allahabad 1858** = Proclamation (Canning); **Delhi 1877** = Empress of India (Lytton).
## SUB 3 | Policy Towards Princes
[186:3:0] - Dalhousie's **annexation policy abandoned** – the promise actually kept.
[186:3:1] - **Lord Canning withdrew the Doctrine of Lapse**; **adoption** by childless rulers recognised (right restored by the 1858 announcement).
[186:3:2] - Princes kept their states subject to **British paramountcy** (Crown as ultimate sovereign); became **subordinate allies** and props of British rule, having stayed loyal in 1857.
[186:3:3] - Canning called loyal princes "**breakwaters to the storm**".
## SUB 4 | Army Reorganisation
[186:4:0] # Peel Commission (1859)
[186:4:1] - Before: three Company **presidency armies** – Bengal, Madras, Bombay.
[186:4:2] TABLE head: Measure | Detail
  [186:4:2.0] European : Indian ratio | Bengal 1:5 → 1:2; Madras and Bombay 2:5; European share raised
  [186:4:2.1] Artillery | Exclusively European; Indians barred from heavy weapons; key posts for Europeans
  [186:4:2.2] Regiments | "Divide and counterpoise" – regiments deliberately mixed by caste and region (Sikh, Gurkha, Rajput units) to prevent fraternisation
  [186:4:2.3] Recruitment | Shift to Gurkhas, Sikhs, Pathans ("martial races": Punjab, NWFP) who stayed loyal, rather than Awadh and Bihar
[186:4:3] # Eden Commission (1879)
[186:4:4] - Recommended abolishing the separate presidency armies → single **Indian Army** (1895) with **four commands** (not three).
[186:4:5] 1:2 = Bengal Army; 2:5 = Madras and Bombay; 4 = commands in 1895
## SUB 5 | Divide and Rule and Racial Attitudes

  [186:5:0.1] Landed class | Awadh zamindars and taluqdars restored to proprietary rights → conservative, loyalist class
  [186:5:0.2] Non-interference | Reforming zeal abandoned; state stepped back from missionary activity
  [186:5:0.3] Racial attitude | Racial arrogance grew; English-educated "Babus" viewed with suspicion
## SUB 6 | Indian Councils Act, 1861 – Lesson of 1857
[186:6:0] - Lesson of 1857: associate Indians with law-making.
[186:6:1] - Gave statutory recognition to Canning's **portfolio system** (introduced 1859): each member in charge of a department.
[186:6:2] - Viceroy could issue **ordinances** without the council, valid **six months**.
[186:6:3] - Additional legislative members: at least **half non-official**.
[186:6:4] - **1862**: Canning nominated the first **three Indians** – **Raja of Benares**, **Maharaja of Patiala**, **Sir Dinkar Rao**.
## SUB 7 | End of the Mughals and the Company
[186:7:0] - **Bahadur Shah II**: proclaimed **Shahenshah-e-Hindustan** by rebels; tried, exiled to **Rangoon**, died **1862** → Mughal rule formally ended. LAST Mughal emperor.
[186:7:1] - **East India Company**: chartered by **Elizabeth I**, **31 Dec 1600**; lost rule **1858**; dissolved **1874**.
[186:7:2] # AP Angle
[186:7:3] - Andhra came under direct Crown rule: **Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema** stayed in the **Madras Presidency**.
[186:7:4] - **Telangana** stayed under the **Nizam**, subject to British suzerainty (not run from London).
[186:7:5] - **Madras Native Association (1852)**, founded by **Gazula Lakshmi Narasu Chetty** = first to petition against non-implementation of the Allahabad Proclamation.

## SUB 0 | Overview – Resistance in Andhra Before and During 1857
[187:0:0] - South India's revolts were **local and scattered**; the **Nizam's loyalty** kept the Deccan closed to 1857.
[187:0:1] - It took the Company **50–60 years** to consolidate its hold on Andhra: **zamindars** on the coast + **palegars** in Rayalaseema.
[187:0:2] - The 1846 Uyyalawada revolt was the **last major palegar rising** in Rayalaseema, a decade before 1857.

## SUB 2 | Vizianagaram Rising and the Battle of Padmanabham, 1794
[187:2:0] # Background
[187:2:1] - **Chinna Vijayarama Raju** (ruled 1760–94) removed step-brother and Diwan **Sitarama Raju** for misrule (1784); Sitarama Raju bribed Council members and got his post back.
[187:2:2] - Company demanded **Rs 3 lakh** tribute + **disbanding of troops**; the Raja refused.
[187:2:3] - **2 Aug 1793**: British seized the **Vizianagaram fort**; estate split into **3 parts**; Raja ordered to live at **Machilipatnam** on a pension of **Rs 1,200**.
[187:2:4] # The Battle
[187:2:5] TABLE head: Point | Fact
  [187:2:5.0] Place | Padmanabham, between Bhimunipatnam and Vizianagaram
  [187:2:5.1] Date | 10 July 1794
  [187:2:5.2] Parties | East India Company vs Vizianagaram zamindar Pusapati Chinna Vijayarama Gajapati Raju II
  [187:2:5.3] British commander | Colonel Prendergast (also spelt Pendergast)
  [187:2:5.4] Cause | British revenue demands
  [187:2:5.5] Result | British victory; the Raja and about 1,000 Kshatriya warriors killed; compared to Culloden
[187:2:6] # Outcome
[187:2:7] - Vizianagaram reduced to an **ordinary zamindari**.
[187:2:8] - Minor son **Narayana Babu** (hid at **Makkuva**) restored on accepting the **Permanent Settlement (1802)** + **Rs 6 lakh peshkash**.
[187:2:9] Bobbili 1757 = Vizianagaram + French vs Bobbili; **Padmanabham 1794 = Company vs Vizianagaram** – it decided the defeat of Vizianagaram, not Bobbili.

## SUB 4 | Palegars / Poligars and Their Revolts
[187:4:0] # Who Were the Palegars?
[187:4:1] - **Poligars/Palegars** = **local military chiefs** and long-time **revenue farmers**; fortified chiefs with self-rule under the **Nizam's nominal suzerainty**.
[187:4:2] - Dozens in **Kurnool, Cuddapah, Bellary, Anantapur**.
[187:4:3] # Causes of Resistance in the Ceded Districts
[187:4:4] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [187:4:4.0] Cession of 1800 | Nizam ceded Cuddapah, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary under the Subsidiary Alliance (1798)
  [187:4:4.1] Revenue burden | Heavy revenue + peshkash
  [187:4:4.2] Estates | Estates taken / forfeited
  [187:4:4.3] Disarmament | Thomas Munro ordered palegars to disarm and pay peshkash
[187:4:5] - 1800–01: **About 80 palegars** resisted Munro.
[187:4:6] - **1801, Taranikallu (Adoni division)**: Patel **Mutakuri Goudappa** attacked the Sub-Collector's office → hanged by Sub-Collector **Dhakar** (not Munro).
[187:4:7] # Suppression
[187:4:8] - Officer: **Thomas Munro, Principal Collector** (PYQ 2011) + military officer **Campbell**.
[187:4:9] - Methods: proclamations to lay down arms → **18 months** of military action → **confiscation** of estates → small **pensions** for loyal heirs.
[187:4:10] - Settlement afterwards: **Ryotwari** in the Ceded Districts.
[187:4:11] Settlement in the Ceded Districts = **Ryotwari**, NOT Permanent Settlement.
[187:4:12] # Poligar Revolts in Tamil Nadu and Travancore
[187:4:13] - The Company's **1781** deal letting the **Nawab of Arcot** manage Tirunelveli and Carnatic revenue hit poligars' rights.

## SUB 9 | Limited Response, Repression and Consequences
[187:9:0] # Reasons for the Limited Response
[187:9:1] - **Nizam and Salar Jung loyal**; Madras Presidency quiet.
[187:9:2] - **Zamindars gained** from the Permanent Settlement.
[187:9:3] - People valued **Company stability** over the "earlier anarchy"; **loyalty meetings** at **Visakhapatnam, Rajahmundry, Vijayawada**.
[187:9:4] - Revolt lacked central leadership, a programme and a base in the south.
[187:9:5] APPSC G2 2025 A/R: A "Andhra was little affected for want of **local leaders**" = **TRUE**; R "Afzal-ud-Daula pro-British" = **TRUE** but **not the explanation** (coastal Andhra was under the Madras Presidency). Only sporadic risings: Turrebaz Khan, Subba Reddy, Dandasena, Kadiri attempt.
[187:9:6] # Who Did and Did Not Rebel
[187:9:7] - **Nizam of Hyderabad did NOT rebel** against the Company.
[187:9:8] - **Did rebel**: Raja of **Vizianagaram**, **Polygars** of Tamil Nadu, Diwan **Velu Thampi** of Travancore.
[187:9:9] # Repression
[187:9:10] - **Subba Reddy, Dandasena, Fida Ali hanged**; Sheikh Peer Sahib arrested; across India thousands executed after mock trials.
