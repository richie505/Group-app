  [1:1.4] — | Jaggayyapeta | Treasury looted

[1:2] - **Town in Rayalaseema where revolt was incited = Kadapa** (APPSC G2 2008; asked three times, one bank keyed Kadiri – wrong).
[1:3] - **Third-quarter-of-the-19th-century rebellion (1851-75) = Korukonda Subba Reddy's (1857-58)**; Rampa and Rampa-Rekapalli are 1879 (fourth quarter).
[1:4] - **All five places** – Parlakimidi (tribal), Visakhapatnam (placard + loyalty meeting), Kadapa (religious), Hyderabad (violent attack), Rajahmundry (loyalty meeting) – saw a reaction to 1857.
[1:5] # Why the response was limited
[1:6] - Apart from minor disturbances **Andhra stayed loyal**; people valued the Company's **stable government** over the "earlier anarchy"; the Hindu majority was indifferent.
[1:7] - **Nizam Afzal-ud-Daula** and Diwan **Salar Jung I** stayed **loyal** (true) – but coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema were in the **Madras Presidency**, not under the Nizam.
[1:8] - **Andhra did have local leaders** (Subba Reddy, Dandasena, Peer Saheb), so "Andhra was little affected **because it had no local leaders**" is **false** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025 A/R: **A false, R true**).
[1:9] - The revolt was concentrated in **North and Central India**; its impact on the South was small – true, but it does not explain Peer Saheb's jihad call (A/R: both true, R not the explanation).

## SUB 2 | Rampa Rebellion (1879), Rekapalle and the Agency Risings
[2:0] # Rampa 1879
[2:1] TABLE head: Point | Fact
  [2:1.0] Year | 1879 (March 1879 – 1880) – not 1851, 1854, 1866, 1874, 1876, 1878, 1892
  [2:1.1] Place | Rampa, Agency tract of the Godavari district, Madras Presidency
  [2:1.2] Causes | Oppressive mansabdar; toddy (chigur) tax and ban on domestic toddy tapping; limits on podu
  [2:1.3] Leaders | Chandrayya, Sambayya, Tamman Dora (Tomma Dora), Ambul Reddi
  [2:1.4] Tribes | Hill tribes, chiefly Koyas
  [2:1.5] Inquiry | Mr Sullivan, First Member of the Board of Revenue, appointed July 1879 to find the causes and suggest remedies – not Rutherford (Special Commissioner against Alluri, 1924)
  [2:1.6] End | Mansabdar deposed; leaders caught or killed by Aug 1879 – Feb 1880
[2:2] - **Podu** (shifting, slash-and-burn) cultivation was the tribal practice the forest/revenue rules restricted.
[2:3] - **Rekapalle revolt (1879)**: against higher taxes and **restrictions on podu** after Rekapalle was added to the Madras Presidency (**1874**); police stations attacked → people **rose against the English forest laws in 1879 in Rekapalli** (NextGen's PYQ list names **Chinturu**, in the same tract).

## SUB 3 | Hyderabad State in 1857 – Aurangabad and the Residency Attack
[3:0] # Order of events (APPSC G1 Prelims 2023)
[3:1] TABLE head: Order | Event
  [3:1.0] 1 | 1839 – Wahabi conspiracy of Mubariz-ud-Daula (Nizam's brother) → imprisoned in Golconda Fort
  [3:1.1] 2 | 16 May 1857 – Nizam Nasir-ud-Daula died; Afzal-ud-Daula succeeded, kept Salar Jung I
  [3:1.2] 3 | June 1857, Aurangabad – 1st Cavalry, Hyderabad Contingent suspected, disarmed and arrested (23 June: 90 sepoys); Dafedar Mir Fida Ali shot at Capt. Abbott → hanged; led with Jamedar Amir Khan – the first significant anti-British incident in Hyderabad State
  [3:1.3] 4 | 17 July 1857 – Turrebaz (Tura Baz) Khan, Rohilla jamedar, with Maulvi Allauddin, led ~5,000 against the British Residency to free Jamedar Chida Khan; repulsed by Resident Col. Davidson
  [3:1.4] 5 | 1858 – rebellion of Raja Venkatappa Nayaka of Shorapur
[3:2] - **Wahabi movement leader (Andhra/Hyderabad Muslims) = Mubariz-ud-Daula** – not Qasim Razvi (Razakars, 1940s) or Syed Ahmed Barelvi (all-India Wahabi founder).
[3:3] - **Aurangabad:** the cavalry refused to cross the **Nizam's frontiers** ("Nizam ke sarhad ke bahar nahi jayenge") rather than fight co-religionists – led by Amir Khan and Mir Fida Ali. **Allauddin was not** the Aurangabad leader, and the Residency attack came **in July, after** Aurangabad.
[3:4] - **Residency attack:** Rohillas held **two houses of moneylenders Abban Saheb and Jaigopal Das** facing the Residency and tore off the gate near **Putli Bowli**.
[3:5] - **Fates:** Turrebaz Khan – life transportation, escaped, **shot near Toopran**, body hung publicly; **Allauddin** – caught at Mangalapalli, died in the **Andamans (1884)**.
[3:6] - **Loyalists / suppressors:** **Nizam Afzal-ud-Daula** (Star of India), **Salar Jung I**, **Resident Davidson**; rewards – Raichur Doab and Dharashiv restored, debt (Rs 50 lakh) written off.

## SUB 4 | Palegars of Rayalaseema and Uyyalawada Narasimha Reddy
[4:0] - **Palegars (Poligars)** = local military chiefs and revenue farmers of the **Ceded Districts** (Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary – ceded by the Nizam in **1800** under the Subsidiary Alliance).
[4:1] - **About 80 palegars** resisted; Principal Collector **Thomas Munro** ordered them to disarm and pay **peshkash**; **18 months** of action; estates confiscated; Ryotwari followed.
[4:2] # Uyyalawada Narasimha Reddy (1846-47)
[4:3] - **The Palegar who fought British rule** = **Uyyalawada Narasimha Reddy** – not Bezawada Gopala Reddy (CM), Ravi Narayana Reddy (Communist) or Sardar Chinnappa Reddy.
[4:4] - **Palegar of Kurnool** district (Uyyalawada, **Koilakuntla** taluk; now Nandyal district) – born in **Kurnool district** (statement correct).
[4:5] - **1846:** attacked the **Koilakuntla treasury** (where he rebelled) and marched towards Cumbum; beaten at Mundlapadu by Capt. Nott; escaped to the Nizam's land; back six weeks later; captured; **hanged at Koilakuntla (22 Feb 1847)**.
[4:6] - Grievance: estate taken under Munro, family left a meagre pension.
[4:7] - Last major palegar rising of Rayalaseema; Orvakal airport (Kurnool) named after him.
[4:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen modern level 3 part 1 p.25; NextGen modern level 3 part 2 p.2; old app notes row 187
