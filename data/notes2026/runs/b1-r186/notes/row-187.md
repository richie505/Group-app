=== Battle of Bobbili (1757) and Battle of Padmanabham (1794)
# The two battles side by side
| Point | **Bobbili** | **Padmanabham (Padmanabha)** |
|---|---|---|
| Date | **24 January 1757** | **10 July 1794** |
| Parties | **Vizianagaram** (Gajapati **Vijayarama Raju**, Pusapati) + **French under Bussy** vs **Bobbili** | **English East India Company** (Col. **Prendergast/Pendergast**, sent by Madras Governor **John Andrews**) vs **Vizianagaram zamindar** (Chinna Vijayarama Raju II) |
| Bobbili side | Raja **Ranga Rao**; queen **Mallamma Devi** | — |
| Result | Bobbili fort fell; **Tandra Paparayudu** (Ranga Rao's brother-in-law) killed Vijayarama Raju in his camp – "Tiger of Bobbili" | Raja **defeated and killed**; it **decided the fate of Vijayarama Raju of Vizianagaram** – not of Bobbili, the Makkuva tribals or Ganjam zamindars |
| Context | Third Carnatic War; Bussy dominant in the Deccan (Treaty of Aurangabad 1753 gave the Circars to the French) | Company's revenue demands; arrears |
| Source | Ballad/play **Bobbili Yuddham** | — |
- **Help taken by Vijayarama Raju at Bobbili = the French (Bussy)** – not the British, the Nawab of the Carnatic or the Nizam. Capt. Camp, William Forde and "Captain Walisha" are wrong options.
- **Gajapati Vijayarama Raju belonged to Vizianagaram** – not Venkatagiri, Karvetinagar or Amaravati.
- Year traps: **Bobbili 1757** (not 1753/1755/1756/1758/1759); **Chandurthi 1758** (Forde defeated Conflans – Northern Circars to the English); **Padmanabham 1794** (not 1792/93/95/99/1803).
# Road to Padmanabham
- Vijayarama Raju (ruled 1760-94) and his half-brother/Diwan **Sitarama Raju** (removed 1784, bribed his way back).
- Company demanded arrears/tribute (**Rs 3 lakh**) and disbanding of troops; **1793**: estate seized, split into **three** parts; Raja pensioned (**Rs 1,200**) and told to live at **Machilipatnam**.
- He marched to **Padmanabham** (between **Bhimunipatnam and Vizianagaram**) instead → killed 10 July 1794.
- Son **Narayana Babu** (8 years; hid at **Makkuva** in the hills) restored on paying arrears; **Permanent Settlement 1802**, peshkash **Rs 6 lakh**.
- **Ranga Rao and Tandra Paparayudu** are remembered as **symbols of Telugu valour** (Bobbili).

=== Andhra in 1857 – Leaders, Places and the Limited Response
# Leader → place → outcome
| Leader | Place | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **Radhakrishna Dandasena (Dandu Sena)** | **Parlakimidi, Ganjam** (now Gajapati dt, Odisha) | **Savara** (Girijan) rising, Aug 1857; suppressed by Capt. Wilson; hanged |
| **Korukonda Subba Reddy** | **Errannagudem, Godavari Agency** | Rose "**in the spirit of Nana Saheb**" on rumours of Nana Saheb's advancing army; captured and executed (helped by Buttaigudem Munsab Shankar Swamy) |
| **Sheikh Peer Saheb** | **Kadapa (Cuddapah)** | Preached **jihad** in the mosque (**28 Aug 1857**), prayed for the Mughal Emperor's success; arrested |
| — | **Visakhapatnam** | Telugu placard at Muharram to raze the town and rename it Mohammad Patnam; also a loyalty meeting |
| — | **Jaggayyapeta** | Treasury looted |
| — | **Rajahmundry, Vijayawada, Visakhapatnam** | Public **loyalty meetings** for the British |
- **Town in Rayalaseema where revolt was incited = Kadapa** (APPSC G2 2008; asked three times, one bank keyed Kadiri – wrong).
- **Third-quarter-of-the-19th-century rebellion (1851-75) = Korukonda Subba Reddy's (1857-58)**; Rampa and Rampa-Rekapalli are 1879 (fourth quarter).
- **All five places** – Parlakimidi (tribal), Visakhapatnam (placard + loyalty meeting), Kadapa (religious), Hyderabad (violent attack), Rajahmundry (loyalty meeting) – saw a reaction to 1857.
# Why the response was limited
- Apart from minor disturbances **Andhra stayed loyal**; people valued the Company's **stable government** over the "earlier anarchy"; the Hindu majority was indifferent.
- **Nizam Afzal-ud-Daula** and Diwan **Salar Jung I** stayed **loyal** (true) – but coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema were in the **Madras Presidency**, not under the Nizam.
- **Andhra did have local leaders** (Subba Reddy, Dandasena, Peer Saheb), so "Andhra was little affected **because it had no local leaders**" is **false** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025 A/R: **A false, R true**).
- South India's risings were **local and scattered**; the **Nizam's loyalty** kept the Deccan out of the revolt.
- Subba Reddy was a village official of **Karaturu** (Godavari district); Dandasena's rising was put down by **Captain Wilson**; the **Faujdari Adalat** was a court (it tried Narasimha Reddy), not a centre of any movement.
| Madras Presidency under the Crown | Year |
|---|---|
| Queen's Proclamation | 1858 |
| Indian Penal Code | 1860 |
| Criminal Procedure Code | 1861 |
| **Madras High Court** replaced the Company courts | **1862** |
- The revolt was concentrated in **North and Central India**; its impact on the South was small – true, but it does not explain Peer Saheb's jihad call (A/R: both true, R not the explanation).

=== Rampa Rebellion (1879), Rekapalle and the Agency Risings
# Rampa 1879
| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| Year | **1879** (March 1879 – 1880) – not 1851, 1854, 1866, 1874, 1876, 1878, 1892 |
| Place | **Rampa, Agency tract of the Godavari district**, Madras Presidency |
| Causes | Oppressive **mansabdar**; **toddy (chigur) tax** and ban on domestic toddy tapping; limits on **podu** |
| Leaders | **Chandrayya**, Sambayya, **Tamman Dora (Tomma Dora)**, **Ambul Reddi** |
| Tribes | Hill tribes, chiefly **Koyas** |
| Inquiry | **Mr Sullivan, First Member of the Board of Revenue**, appointed **July 1879** to find the causes and suggest remedies – **not Rutherford** (Special Commissioner against Alluri, 1924) |
| End | Mansabdar deposed; leaders caught or killed by Aug 1879 – Feb 1880 |
- **Podu** (shifting, slash-and-burn) cultivation was the tribal practice the forest/revenue rules restricted.
- **Rekapalle revolt (1879)**: against higher taxes and **restrictions on podu** after Rekapalle was added to the Madras Presidency (**1874**); police stations attacked → people **rose against the English forest laws in 1879 in Rekapalli** (NextGen's PYQ list names **Chinturu**, in the same tract).

=== Hyderabad State in 1857 – Aurangabad and the Residency Attack
# Order of events (APPSC G1 Prelims 2023)
| Order | Event |
|---|---|
| 0 | Background: **1800** cession of Rayalaseema; **1788** Guntur taken from Nizam Ali for **Rs 7 lakh**; **1853 Berar treaty** (Berar, Raichur, Osmanabad/Dharashiv) opposed |
| 1 | **1839** – Wahabi conspiracy of **Mubariz-ud-Daula** (Nizam's brother) → **arrested**, imprisoned in **Golconda Fort**; the **Kurnool Nawabs** (of Golconda-noble descent) deposed the same year |
| 2 | **16 May 1857** – Nizam Nasir-ud-Daula died; **Afzal-ud-Daula** succeeded, kept **Salar Jung I** |
| 3 | **June 1857, Aurangabad** – **1st Cavalry, Hyderabad Contingent** suspected, **disarmed and arrested** (23 June: 90 sepoys); **Dafedar Mir Fida Ali** shot at **Capt. Abbott** → **hanged**; led with **Jamedar Amir Khan** – the **first significant anti-British incident in Hyderabad State** |
| 4 | **17 July 1857** – **Turrebaz (Tura Baz) Khan**, Rohilla jamedar, with **Maulvi Allauddin**, led ~**5,000** against the **British Residency** to free **Jamedar Chida Khan**; repulsed by Resident **Col. Davidson** |
| 5 | **1858** – rebellion of **Raja Venkatappa Nayaka of Shorapur** |
- **Wahabi movement leader (Andhra/Hyderabad Muslims) = Mubariz-ud-Daula** – not Qasim Razvi (Razakars, 1940s) or Syed Ahmed Barelvi (all-India Wahabi founder).
- **Aurangabad:** the cavalry refused to cross the **Nizam's frontiers** ("Nizam ke sarhad ke bahar nahi jayenge") rather than fight co-religionists – led by Amir Khan and Mir Fida Ali. **Allauddin was not** the Aurangabad leader, and the Residency attack came **in July, after** Aurangabad.
- **Residency attack:** Rohillas held **two houses of moneylenders Abban Saheb and Jaigopal Das** facing the Residency and tore off the gate near **Putli Bowli**.
- **Fates:** Turrebaz Khan – life transportation, escaped, **shot near Toopran**, body hung publicly; **Allauddin** – caught at Mangalapalli, died in the **Andamans (1884)**.
- **Loyalists / suppressors:** **Nizam Afzal-ud-Daula** (Star of India), **Salar Jung I**, **Resident Davidson**; rewards – Raichur Doab and Dharashiv restored, debt (Rs 50 lakh) written off.

=== Palegars of Rayalaseema and Uyyalawada Narasimha Reddy
- **Palegars (Poligars)** = local military chiefs and revenue farmers of the **Ceded Districts** (Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary – ceded by the Nizam in **1800** under the Subsidiary Alliance).
- **About 80 palegars** resisted; Principal Collector **Thomas Munro** ordered them to disarm and pay **peshkash**; **18 months** of action; estates confiscated; Ryotwari followed.
# Uyyalawada Narasimha Reddy (1846-47)
- **The Palegar who fought British rule** = **Uyyalawada Narasimha Reddy** – not Bezawada Gopala Reddy (CM), Ravi Narayana Reddy (Communist) or Sardar Chinnappa Reddy.
- **Palegar of Kurnool** district (Uyyalawada, **Koilakuntla** taluk; now Nandyal district) – born in **Kurnool district** (statement correct).
- **1846:** attacked the **Koilakuntla treasury** (where he rebelled) and marched towards Cumbum; beaten at Mundlapadu by Capt. Nott; escaped to the Nizam's land; back six weeks later; captured; **hanged at Koilakuntla (22 Feb 1847)**.
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Family | Son of Pedda Mallareddy, palegar of Uyyalawada; maternal grandfather **Nosam palegar Jayarama Reddy** |
| Grievance (immediate cause) | Estate taken under Munro; the brothers got a meagre pension of **Rs 35** |
| Support | About 5,000 – **farmers, Yanadis, tribal people**, people of the Uyyalawada and Nosam estates |
| 12 Jul 1846 | Looted Rudravaram and the **Koilakuntla treasury** |
| 24 Jul 1846 | Ambushed at **Mundlapadu** by **Captain Nott** |
| 7 Oct 1846 | Captured at the **Jagannatha Swamy temple, Pairamalla Konda** |
| Jan 1847 | Death sentence by the **Faujdari Adalat** |
| **22 Feb 1847** | Publicly hanged near Koilakuntla |
| Officers, record | Kadapa Collector **J.H. Cockrane** raised the alarm; main record = **the Kadapa Collector's reports** |
| Memory | Film **Sye Raa Narasimha Reddy** (2019); Kurnool (Orvakal) airport |
- **1846 was a single leader's revolt**, not a joint rising of 80 palegars (that was 1800-01).
- **1801, Taranikallu (Adoni):** Patel **Mutakuri Goudappa** attacked the Sub-Collector's office; hanged by Sub-Collector **Dhakar** (not Munro).
- Palegars were **fortified military chiefs with autonomous power** under the Nizam's nominal suzerainty.
- Last major palegar rising of Rayalaseema; Orvakal airport (Kurnool) named after him.

=== South India and Zamindari Resistance – Parlakimedi, Poligars, Velu Thampi
| Rising | Region | Fact |
|---|---|---|
