## SUB 0 | Electromagnetic Spectrum – Order, Uses and Wave Basics
[0:0] # Order of the spectrum
[0:1] TABLE head: Wave | Wavelength | Frequency | Main uses
  [0:1.0] Radio | > 1 m (longest) | < 3 × 10⁸ Hz (lowest) | AM/FM radio, TV, mobile, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, MRI; satellite links (radio family)
  [0:1.1] Microwave | 1 mm – 1 m | 300 MHz – 300 GHz | Satellite communication, radar, 5G, point-to-point telecom links, ovens
  [0:1.2] Infrared | 700 nm – 1 mm |  | TV/AC remotes, night vision, physiotherapy (muscle pain, paralysis), heaters
  [0:1.3] Visible | ~400–700 nm (violet shortest, red longest) |  | Vision, Li-Fi/VLC, optical fibre (some)
  [0:1.4] Ultraviolet | 10–400 nm |  | Sterilisation (UV lamps, semen straws, water), forgery detection on currency, vitamin D; sunscreens block UV
  [0:1.5] X-rays | 0.01–10 nm |  | Radiography, CT, baggage scans, cracks in metals
  [0:1.6] Gamma | < 0.01 nm (shortest) | highest | Radiotherapy, sterilising instruments
[0:2] - **Energy and frequency rise** Radio < Microwave < IR < Visible < UV < **X-ray** < **Gamma**; photon energy E = hν, so shorter wavelength = more energy (200–280 nm UV beats 280–400 nm; **blue** beats green, yellow, red).
[0:3] - **Decreasing frequency:** **Gamma > X-rays > UV > Radio**. Longest wavelength: **radio**; smallest among X-ray/visible/IR/microwave: **X-rays**; among radio/visible/IR/UV: **UV**.
[0:4] - Visible light range: **400–700 nm** (also given as **390–780 nm**; VLC uses 375–780 nm).
[0:5] - **Visible > X-ray in wavelength; X-ray photons > UV photons > visible photons in energy.**
[0:6] - **X-rays** are uncharged EM waves: **not deflected** by electric or magnetic fields; high penetration; wavelength far below visible.
[0:7] - **Red** is used in danger/traffic signals: **longest wavelength**, least scattered, seen from far.
[0:8] - **TV remote:** common remotes use **infrared** (some use radio). **Telecommunication** links: **microwaves**; **radar**: **microwaves**.
[0:9] # Wave basics and sound
[0:10] - **Wavelength** = distance between **two successive crests or troughs**.
[0:11] - EM waves are transverse and need no medium; **sound** is mechanical and **longitudinal** (radio, X-ray, gamma are EM).
[0:12] - **Supersonic waves are not electromagnetic.** **Sonic boom** = shock-wave bang from an object faster than sound (**supersonic aircraft**).
[0:13] - Sound travels **faster in steel (~5,000–6,000 m/s) than in air (~343 m/s)** – a listener at the rail hears a blow twice.
[0:14] > Exam angle: "which has the highest energy / longest wavelength / decreasing frequency" – about 25 PYQs (UPSC, UPPSC, RAS, NDA, CDS, SSC).

## SUB 1 | Optical Fibre and Total Internal Reflection
[1:0] # Total internal reflection (TIR)
[1:1] - Conditions: light goes from **denser → rarer** medium **and** angle of incidence **> critical angle**; light is reflected **with no loss of intensity**.
[1:2] - Refractive index: **Diamond (~2.42) > sapphire (~1.77) > dense flint glass > glass (~1.5) > fused quartz > water > air** – so TIR is possible **diamond → glass**, **diamond → air**, never air → water/glass.
[1:3] - Higher refractive index = **slower light** (among glass, water, air, vacuum: slowest in **glass**).
[1:4] TABLE head: Phenomenon / device | Principle
  [1:4.0] Optical fibre | TIR (successive TIRs let light follow a bent, zig-zag path)
  [1:4.1] Endoscope | Bundle of optical fibres, TIR
  [1:4.2] Brilliance of diamond | TIR (critical angle ≈ 24.4°)
  [1:4.3] Mirage (desert) | TIR in hot, rarer air near the ground
  [1:4.4] Glass beads on traffic signs | TIR (A and R both true, R explains)
  [1:4.5] Crack in glass seen obliquely | Looks silvery (TIR)
  [1:4.6] Rainbow | Dispersion + (total) internal reflection + refraction
  [1:4.7] Stick bent in water | Refraction (not scattering)
  [1:4.8] Sea looks blue | Reflection of sky + scattering of light by water
[1:5] # Optical fibre communication
[1:6] - Thin strand of **glass (silica)** or plastic; **core (higher n) + cladding (lower n)**; signal travels as **pulses of light (light waves)**.
[1:7] - **Fastest** medium vs copper, radio, satellite; main use **communication** (telecom, internet); very **low power**, **free from radio-frequency interference**.
[1:8] - In India **Sterlite (Sterlite Tech)** makes optical fibre (not Reliance). **Single-mode** = long distances; **multi-mode** = short runs.
[1:9] # Undersea cables and national fibre
[1:10] - Undersea cables ~**1.5 million km**, carry ~**99%** of international traffic.
[1:11] - **CANI** (Chennai–Andaman and Nicobar Islands), ~2,300 km, BSNL, inaugurated **10 Aug 2020**.
[1:12] - **NOFN** (approved 25 Oct 2011) → **BharatNet**: all **2,50,000 Gram Panchayats**, funded by **USOF** (now Digital Bharat Nidhi), **not NIIF**.
[1:13] - PM Gati Shakti NMP (2026): ~**13.5 lakh** route km OFC; **PM-WANI 3.15 lakh** hotspots.
[1:14] > Exam angle: "optical fibre works on…" = TIR – asked about 15 times.

## SUB 4 | Wireless Access – Li-Fi, Wi-Fi, WiMAX, Bluetooth
[4:0] TABLE head: Li-Fi (Light Fidelity) | Wi-Fi
  [4:0.0] Visible light from LEDs; term by Harald Haas (2011) | Radio waves (2.4 / 5 GHz)
  [4:0.1] Faster, more bandwidth, secure, no EM interference | Longer range
  [4:0.2] Does not pass through walls | Passes through walls
[4:1] - **VLC** (UPSC 2020): 375–780 nm, **faster than Bluetooth**, **no EM interference**, but **short-range** (not long-range) → **1, 3, 4**.
[4:2] - **WiMAX** = World Wide Interoperability for **Microwave Access** (IEEE 802.16), **communication technology**, mainly **2–3.5 GHz**.
[4:3] - **Bluetooth:** short-range **radio waves**, 2.4 GHz ISM band; secure **Personal Area Networks**; developed **1994** by **Jaap Haartsen**; wireless link between equipment.
[4:4] - **2.4 GHz ISM band** (licence-free): Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, cordless phones, microwave ovens – **not FM radio**.
[4:5] - **Wi-Fi 6 = 802.11ax; Wi-Fi 7 = 802.11be.**
[4:6] - **Short-range devices** (UPSC 2022): **CCTV, RFID and WLAN** – all three. **CCTV** = Closed-Circuit Television. **RFID**: radio tags (Kavach track tags). **FSO**: data by laser beams through the air.
[4:7] - **Project Loon** (Google/Alphabet): stratospheric helium **balloons** for internet = **wireless communication**; shut Jan 2021.
[4:8] - **Mumbai Central:** first station with Google free public Wi-Fi (**Jan 2016**). India **49th** in Network Readiness Index 2024 (from 60th).

## SUB 5 | Satellite Communication and Radio Propagation
[5:0] - Satellite communication works through **transponders** (receive uplink, amplify, retransmit downlink) and uses **microwaves** (radio family; SSC keys say "radio waves").
[5:1] TABLE head: Mode | Bands | Use
  [5:1.0] Ground wave | LF/MF | AM radio
  [5:1.1] Sky wave (ionosphere reflects) | HF 3–30 MHz | Shortwave radio – very distant stations
  [5:1.2] Space wave (line of sight) | VHF, UHF 300 MHz–3 GHz, microwaves | UHF = cellular phones (also TV, GPS); satellites
[5:2] - Dish TV fails in rain: raindrops **absorb** and **scatter** the energy; a **small antenna** cannot pick up the weakened signal.
[5:3] TABLE head:  | GEO | LEO
  [5:3.0] Altitude | ~35,786 km | Below ~2,000 km
  [5:3.1] Latency | Higher | Low
  [5:3.2] Examples | GSAT | Starlink, Eutelsat OneWeb (Bharti), Amazon Kuiper
[5:4] - **Starlink** (8,000+ satellites): **GMPCS** (Global Mobile Personal Communication by Satellite) licence **2025**; user terminals **made in India within 5 years**.
[5:5] - **Telecommunications Act 2023:** licensing → **authorisation**; terrestrial spectrum **auctioned**, satellite spectrum **assigned administratively**. ITU: de-orbit within **25 years**.
[5:6] - **SREESAT-1** (SNIST Hyderabad students, 2019): **balloon-based** environment monitor (temperature, pressure, humidity, UV/IR) – not a drone or Wi-Fi system.
