=== Speaker of the Lok Sabha – Election, Oath, Resignation, Removal (Articles 93-97)
- **Article 93**: the Lok Sabha **chooses (elects)** two of **its own members** as Speaker and Deputy Speaker – the Speaker is **not nominated or appointed by the President**, not elected by the public or by both Houses, but by **all members of the Lok Sabha**. Speaker's provisions: **Arts. 93-96**, 97 (salary), **100** (casting vote), **110** (Money Bill), **118** (joint sitting).
- Must already be a **member of the House** when elected – the "six months to become a member" grace belongs to **Ministers** (Art. 75(5)), not to the Speaker.
- Date of the Speaker's election is fixed by the **President** (Rule 7); the Deputy Speaker's by the **Speaker** (Rule 8).
- **No separate oath of office** – the Speaker (and Deputy Speaker) take only the **oath as an MP**; the President (Art. 60), VP (Art. 69), PM / ministers (Art. 75(4)) and judges take oaths of office.
- **Tenure**: not at the pleasure of the President – he holds office **so long as he is a member**; vacates on ceasing to be a member (**Art. 94(a)**).
- **Resignation (Art. 94(b))**: Speaker → **Deputy Speaker**; Deputy Speaker → **Speaker** (not the President, PM, CJI or RS Chairman). In a State: Speaker → **Deputy Speaker of the Assembly** (Art. 179).
- **Removal (Art. 94(c))** – of the Speaker **or the Deputy Speaker**: resolution of the **Lok Sabha** passed by a **majority of all the then members** (effective majority) after **at least 14 days' notice** – not two-thirds, not by the President, not by both Houses.
- **Dissolution**: the Speaker **does not vacate** office – continues **until immediately before the first meeting** of the new Lok Sabha (second proviso to Art. 94); the Deputy Speaker and members go at once. Same for a State Speaker (**Art. 179**); State Speaker and Deputy Speaker are provided by **Art. 178**.
- No constitutional duty to **resign from his party** on becoming Speaker (UPSC 2025: only I and III correct) – that is a British convention, not Indian law.
- **Article 96** – while a resolution for **his own removal** is under consideration:
| Situation | Presides | Speaks / takes part | Votes in first instance | Casting vote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal sitting | Yes | Yes | **No** | **Yes** (Art. 100(1)) |
| Own removal resolution | **No**, even if present | **Yes** | **Yes** | **No** |
- **Chairman (RS)** during his own removal resolution: cannot preside and **cannot vote** (not a member), but may speak.
- **Art. 97**: salaries of the Speaker and Deputy Speaker are **charged on the Consolidated Fund of India** (not voted).
- **14 days' notice** is needed to remove the **President, Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Deputy Chairman**; a **Governor** is removed by the President at will without notice.
- **Warrant of Precedence**: 1 President, 2 VP, 3 PM, **4 Governor within his State**, 5 former Presidents (5A Deputy PM), **6 CJI and Speaker of the Lok Sabha**, 7 Cabinet Ministers – so a Governor (in his State) ranks above the Speaker, and the Speaker ranks above all Cabinet Ministers except the PM.
- The Speaker is **not appointed by the President** – the CJI and the service chiefs are.
> Exam angle: "chosen/elected by the Lok Sabha", "resigns to the Deputy Speaker", "majority of all the then members + 14 days", "continues after dissolution", "no oath of office".

=== Powers of the Speaker – Money Bill, Casting Vote, Joint Sitting
| Power | Article / basis |
|---|---|
| Decides whether a Bill is a **Money Bill** – decision **final** | **Art. 110(3)** |
| Endorses his **certificate** on a Money Bill sent to the RS and to the President | **Art. 110(4)** |
| **Casting vote** only in a **tie** (no vote in the first instance) | **Art. 100(1)** |
| Presides over a **joint sitting** of both Houses (summoned by the **President**, Art. 108) | **Art. 118(4)** |
| Adjourns / suspends the House for want of **quorum (one-tenth)** | **Art. 100(3)** |
| Anti-defection decisions | Tenth Schedule – subject to judicial review (Kihoto Hollohan, 1992) |
| Recognises the **Leader of the Opposition** (party with one-tenth of the House); LoP statutory since the **1977 Act** | Speaker's direction |
| Decides **admissibility of questions**, motions and resolutions; adjourns the House **sine die** | Rules of Procedure |
- The Speaker's Money Bill decision cannot be questioned by the **President**, the **Rajya Sabha**, the **Finance Minister**, a JPC or the **Supreme Court**.
- **Casting vote**: only "in case of a tie when votes are equally divided" – not to save the Government, not for Constitutional Amendments or emergencies; the President, PM and CJI have no vote in the Lok Sabha. The **RS Chairman** likewise has a vote **only in a tie**.
- **Joint sitting**: presided by the **Speaker**; in his absence the **Deputy Speaker**; if he too is absent, the **Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**; then a person chosen by the members present. Never the RS Chairman / VP, the President, the PM or a minister.
- A **minister who resigns** may make a personal explanation in the House only with the **Speaker's permission** (consent), not the PM's or President's.
- Speaker is ex officio **President of the Indian Parliamentary Group** and chairman of the Conference of Presiding Officers; appoints chairmen of LS committees; chairs the Business Advisory, Rules and General Purposes Committees.
- Powers list (Raj PCS 2024): presides over joint sitting ✔, Money Bill certificate ✔, does not vote except in a tie ✔; "neither presides nor takes part during his own removal" ✘ (he may take part) → **(i), (ii), (iv)**.
- **Panel of Chairpersons**: nominated by the **Speaker** (up to **ten**); a member presides when **both** Speaker and Deputy Speaker are **absent** (not when the Speaker's office is vacant). If none is present, the House determines who presides (Art. 95(2)).
- **Indian Parliamentary Group (IPG)**: an **autonomous body** (1949); all MPs may be members, **former MPs** may become **associate members**; **Speaker** = ex officio President; **Deputy Speaker** and **Deputy Chairman** = ex officio Vice-Presidents; Secretary-General of the LS = its Secretary-General.
> Exam angle: "Whose decision is final on a Money Bill?" (asked ×9) → Speaker, Art. 110(3); "presides over joint sitting" (×12) → Speaker; casting vote Art. 100.

=== Deputy Speaker and Pro-Tem Speaker
- **Deputy Speaker**: elected by the LS from its members (Art. 93) on a date fixed by the **Speaker** (Rule 8), communicated to members by the **Secretary-General**.
- That the Deputy Speaker comes **from the Opposition** is only a **convention** – no constitutional or mandatory provision (UPSC 2017, 2022). Likewise no rule that the Speaker comes from the majority party. (The winner of an LS/Assembly election needs only a **plurality**, not 50% of votes.)
- **Art. 95**: performs the Speaker's duties when the office is **vacant** and presides when the Speaker is **absent**; when presiding he has the **same powers as the Speaker** and **no appeal lies to the Speaker** against his rulings – he is **not subordinate** to the Speaker.
- Out of the Chair he is an ordinary member – may debate, move motions, **vote in the first instance**. On being appointed to a committee he becomes its **chairman automatically**.
- Own removal resolution: cannot preside; may speak and vote in the first instance; no casting vote.
- UPSC 2022: date fixed by the Speaker ✔; Deputy Speaker has the Speaker's powers in the Chair, no appeal ✔; "mandatory opposition/ruling party" ✘; "moved by Speaker, seconded by PM" ✘ → **A and C only**.
- **Vacancy**: the LS Deputy Speaker's post has been **vacant since June 2019** – through the whole 17th and the 18th Lok Sabha (as of 2026); in the States, **Jharkhand**'s post was vacant for **21 years** (June 2026). Last LS Deputy Speaker: **M. Thambidurai** (2014-19).
- **Pro-Tem Speaker (Art. 95(1))**: **appointed and sworn in by the President**; usually **one of the senior-most members** of the newly elected House (list prepared by the Legislative section, sent to the **Minister of Parliamentary Affairs**, who seeks the President's approval).
  - Functions: **administers the oath** to new members and presides over the **election of the Speaker**; tenure ends when the regular Speaker is elected. Not elected by the House; not the outgoing Speaker, Deputy Speaker or LoP.
> Exam angle: "Deputy Speaker from the Opposition – Constitution?" → only convention; "function of the Pro-Tem Speaker" → oath to members.

=== Chairman and Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha
- The **Vice-President** is the **ex officio Chairman** of the Rajya Sabha (**Arts. 64, 89(1)**) – not the President, Speaker or Deputy Speaker.
- The Chairman is **not a member** of the Rajya Sabha – so the **Rajya Sabha** is the House presided over by a **non-member** (the Speaker of the LS / Vidhan Sabha and the Chairman of a Vidhan Parishad are members of their Houses).
- Vote: a **casting vote only in a tie** – not "like other members", not for Amendment Bills.
- Functions: presides and conducts business, protects members' privileges, is the House's spokesperson; he **cannot dissolve the Rajya Sabha** – it is a **permanent House**, never dissolved.
- Removal: only by **removal as Vice-President** (Art. 67(b)); during that resolution he cannot preside or vote but may speak.
- **Art. 91**: when the VP **acts as President** (or the Chairman's office is vacant), the **Deputy Chairman** performs the Chairman's duties; the VP then draws the **President's** salary. **Jan 2026 (Justice Varma case)**: SC upheld the Deputy Chairman's Art. 91 power to decide a judge-removal motion while the Chairman's office was vacant.
- **Deputy Chairman**: elected by the RS from its members (Art. 89(2)); resigns to the **Chairman**; removed by an RS effective majority after 14 days' notice (**Art. 90(c)**). **Panel of Vice-Chairpersons** nominated by the Chairman.

=== Sessions, Adjournment, Prorogation, Dissolution; President's Address
- **Art. 85(1)**: the **President summons** each House (separately or together) at such time and place as he thinks fit, but **six months shall not intervene** between the **last sitting of one session** and the **first sitting of the next** → Parliament must meet **at least twice a year**.
- **Three sessions** – **Budget** (Feb-May, **longest**), **Monsoon** (Jul-Sep), **Winter** (Nov-Dec, **shortest**) – a **convention**, not in the Constitution; no fixed number of sessions or minimum sitting days is laid down. States: **Art. 174** (same six-month rule); State Assemblies averaged only **24 sittings in 2025**.
- **Session**: first sitting → prorogation/dissolution. **Recess**: the period between **prorogation** and the reassembly in a new session.
- **Quorum**: **one-tenth** of the total members of the House, **including the presiding officer** – **Art. 100(3)**.
| Device | By whom | Ends | Effect on pending business |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adjournment** | Presiding officer | A **sitting**, for hours/days/weeks | Session continues |
| **Adjournment sine die** | Presiding officer (Speaker / Chairman) | A sitting **for an indefinite period** (no date fixed) | Bills continue |
| **Prorogation – Art. 85(2)(a)** | **President** on the **Council of Ministers'** advice (Governor in States) | The **session** | **Bills do not lapse**; notices lapse (except to introduce bills) |
| **Dissolution – Art. 85(2)(b)** | **President** – **Lok Sabha only** | The **life of the House** | Pending business lapses (subject to Art. 107); the RS is permanent |
- Sequence: adjournment **sine die** → President's notification of **prorogation** → recess → new session. After prorogation, **only the President** can summon the House.
- **Automatic dissolution**: the LS term is **five years from the date appointed for its first meeting** (Art. 83(2)); at its end it dissolves by efflux of time even without a formal order (UPSC 2004: a, b and c all correct).
- APPSC G1 2018: "adjournment sine die = terminating a sitting for an indefinite period" (key); prorogation ends the session; dissolution applies only to the LS; adjournment **never** ends a session.
- **Art. 86**: President may **address** either or both Houses, **require attendance** of members and send **messages** on pending Bills. **Art. 87**: **special address** at the start of the **first session after each general election** and the **first session of each year**.
- The address is **prepared by the Union Cabinet** (it states the Government's policy) and is debated on a **Motion of Thanks**; defeat of the Motion of Thanks = defeat of the **Government**, which must **resign**.
- **Gap between two sessions** – "shall not exceed **six months**" (flashcard).
> Exam angle: "minimum meetings a year" → twice; "how is six months counted" → last sitting to first sitting; prorogation by the President; recess.

=== Parliament Secretariat – Article 98
- **Art. 98(1)**: **each House** has a **separate secretarial staff**, but **posts common to both Houses** may be created.
- **Art. 98(2)**: **Parliament** may by law regulate recruitment and service conditions of the staff of either House.
- **Art. 98(3)**: until such a law, the **President**, **after consultation with the Speaker** (or the Chairman for the RS), makes the rules; individual appointment orders are issued by the Speaker / Secretary-General.
- **Independent of the Government (executive)**: the **Lok Sabha Secretariat** works under the **direct control and supervision of the Speaker** (the RS Secretariat under the Chairman) – not the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs, Home Ministry, PM or President. It is a permanent body, not a five-year one.
- Head: **Secretary-General** of each House – appointed by the **presiding officer**; rank of the **Cabinet Secretary**. The idea of a separate legislative secretariat was raised in **1926** by **Vithalbhai Patel**.
- **Presidential election returning officer**: the **Secretary-General of the Lok Sabha or of the Rajya Sabha, by rotation** (appointed by the ECI).
> Exam angle: "LS Secretariat under whom?" (×4) → Speaker; "separate secretariat but common posts" (Art. 98).
