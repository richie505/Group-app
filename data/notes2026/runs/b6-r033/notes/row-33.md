# Nine years of GST (1 July 2026)
- GST in force since **1 July 2017**; subsumed **17 taxes and 13 cesses**.
- Enabled by the **Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016**; **122nd** was only the **Bill** number (2014).
- Registered taxpayers: 66.5 lakh (2017) → **1.65 crore** (May 2026).
- Gross collections: ₹7.4 lakh crore (2017–18) → **₹22.27 lakh crore (2025–26)**.
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 2003 | **Kelkar Task Force on Indirect Taxes** recommended GST |
| 2006 | Union Budget proposed a national GST |
| 2014 | **122nd Constitutional Amendment Bill** introduced |
| 2016 | Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016 |
| 2017 | GST in force **1 July 2017** |
# Constitutional framework
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| 246A | Concurrent GST law-making power to Parliament and States; Parliament exclusive for inter-State supplies |
| **269A** | **GST on inter-State supplies** – levied by the Union, apportioned |
| **279A** | **GST Council**, constituted by the President; chaired by the **Union Finance Minister** (Nirmala Sitharaman) |
- GST is a **destination-based** consumption tax (not origin-based); implemented at the Centre by **CBIC** (Ministry of Finance).
- **Outside GST:** alcoholic liquor for human consumption; petroleum crude, petrol, HSD, **natural gas** and ATF (until the Council recommends); Basic Customs Duty, stamp duty, electricity duty, motor vehicle tax.
# Next-Generation GST reforms (56th meeting, effective September 2025)
- Slabs mainly **5% and 18%**; **40%** for luxury and sin goods.
- Registration threshold (goods) ₹20 lakh → **₹40 lakh**; composition limit ₹1.5 crore.
- GST on toys **12% → 5%**.
