=== NOTES 5:110 | Precision Agriculture, Drip Irrigation and Other Resource-efficient Technologies
## SUB 0 | Soilless and Protected Cultivation – Hydroponics, Aeroponics, Phytotron
[110:0:0] TABLE head: Technique | Meaning
  [110:0:0.0] Hydroponics | Growing plants without soil, roots in mineral nutrient solution (sand, gravel or water may support them); still needs water, light, nutrients; ~90% less water
  [110:0:0.1] Aeroponics | Roots suspended in air and misted – no soil medium
  [110:0:0.2] Aquaponics | Fish culture + hydroponics; fish waste feeds plants
  [110:0:0.3] Vertical farming | Stacked indoor layers, year-round output where land is scarce
  [110:0:0.4] Greenhouse / polyhouse | Controlled microclimate; protection from weather, pests and diseases
  [110:0:0.5] Phytotron | Enclosed research greenhouse to grow plants under controlled conditions
[110:0:1] - **Aeroponics limits disease transmission** (less plant-to-plant contact, oxygen-rich roots) – so "disease transmission is its major disadvantage" is **incorrect**; aeroponic plants have **high dry-weight biomass** and need less nutrient input than hydroponics.
[110:0:2] - "Hydrophonics" in old papers = hydroponics, i.e. growth **without soil**, not without water.
[110:0:3] > Exam angle: hydroponics definition (×5), aeroponics INCORRECT statement, phytotron.
[110:0:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment Part 7 p.28-30; Shankar Environment Part 20 (ocr) p.1 (aeroponics, hydroponics); app notes 5:110 sub 4
[110:0:5] - **eSoil** (Linköping University, Sweden, 2023): an electrically conductive growth substrate for hydroponics made of **cellulose** + the polymer PEDOT; stimulated barley seedlings grew ~50% more in 15 days.
## SUB 1 | Resource-conserving Technologies and Crop Practices
[110:1:0] TABLE head: Technology | Benefit
  [110:1:0.0] Zero tillage | New crop sown into previous residue; soil carbon sequestration, more earthworms, less runoff
  [110:1:0.1] Zero/reduced tillage + crop residue on field | Conserves water (UPSC 2017: 1 and 3); gypsum before irrigation does not
  [110:1:0.2] Contour bunding | Soil conservation on slopes
  [110:1:0.3] Relay cropping | Next crop sown before the standing crop is harvested
  [110:1:0.4] Laser land levelling | Uniform field; saves 20-25% irrigation water
  [110:1:0.5] SRI (alternate wetting and drying) | Less seed, water and methane
  [110:1:0.6] Direct-seeded rice | No nursery or transplanting; promoted with zero-till wheat (NICRA)
  [110:1:0.7] Sustainable Sugarcane Initiative | Single-bud chips / bud-chip settlings – very low seed cost, drip works well, more scope for intercropping; chemical fertilisers are still used (UPSC 2014: 1, 2 and 4)
[110:1:1] - **Carbon sequestration (UPSC 2012):** of contour bunding, relay cropping and zero tillage, **only zero tillage** (most keys).
[110:1:2] - **Sugarcane (UPSC 2020: 1 and 4):** **bud-chip settlings** raised in a nursery save seed material; **tissue-culture settlings** can be used. Single-budded setts germinate worse and survive worse than multi-bud setts in bad weather.
[110:1:3] - **Onion** is grown by **transplanting** nursery seedlings (maize, sorghum, soyabean are sown directly).
[110:1:4] - **Bio-drainage:** fast-transpiring trees such as **Eucalyptus** pump out excess water from waterlogged land.

## SUB 2 | Micro-irrigation and Fertigation
[110:2:0] - **Localised (micro) irrigation:** water piped under low pressure and given in small discharges at each plant – **drip, micro-sprinkler, bubbler**; **conserves water** and **reduces water consumption** (vs flood, canal or open-field watering).
[110:2:1] TABLE head: Method | Efficiency | How water is applied
  [110:2:1.0] Drip | ~90% (highest) | To the root zone through emitters
  [110:2:1.1] Sprinkler | 70-80% | Through nozzles like rain; loses more to wind and evaporation
  [110:2:1.2] Flood / furrow | 30-50% | Surface methods
[110:2:2] - **Drip irrigation** = the technology that reduces farming's environmental impact in **water-scarce regions** (APPSC G2); drip limit = **higher initial cost**; Evergreen Revolution ("more output at lower ecological cost") – **M.S. Swaminathan**.
[110:2:3] # Fertigation
[110:2:4] - **Fertigation = applying fertiliser through irrigation water** (through the drip line); not chemigation (pesticides) or "microgation".
[110:2:5] - Advantages (**UPSC 2020: I, III and IV**): **controls alkalinity** of irrigation water, **more nutrients available**, **less leaching**. Efficient application of **rock phosphate** is **not** possible (it is insoluble).
[110:2:6] > Exam angle: micro-irrigation conserves water (×2), fertigation definition and advantages.
[110:2:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy APPSC 20 p.11 (localised irrigation); PMF Environment Part 7 p.25-26 (fertigation in precision farming); Shankar Environment Part 2 (ocr) p.1 (UPSC 2020 fertigation PYQ); app notes 5:110 sub 0
## SUB 3 | Irrigation Schemes – PMKSY, Per Drop More Crop and APMIP
[110:3:0] - **PMKSY** (Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana): launched **1 July 2015 (2015-16)**; motto **"Har Khet Ko Pani"** + **"Per Drop More Crop"**; aims to **enhance physical access of water on farm**, **expand assured irrigation**, **reduce wastage and raise water-use efficiency**, and achieve **convergence of investment in irrigation at field level**; creates protected irrigation by **harnessing rainwater at micro level** (watershed component).
[110:3:1] TABLE head: Component | Fact
  [110:3:1.0] AIBP, Har Khet Ko Pani | Ministry of Jal Shakti (DoWR)
  [110:3:1.1] Per Drop More Crop (PDMC) | Agriculture Ministry; drip and sprinkler; under PMKSY 2015-16 to 2021-22, under RKVY from 2022-23
  [110:3:1.2] Watershed Development (WDC-PMKSY 2.0) | Department of Land Resources (Rural Development)
  [110:3:1.3] Micro Irrigation Fund | Rs 10,000 crore corpus with NABARD; operational 2019-20
[110:3:2] - PDMC: **102 lakh ha** under micro-irrigation (2015-16 to 2025-26); priority to rainfed, water-stressed and critical groundwater blocks; NMSA subsidy up to 55%, capped at 5 ha.
[110:3:3] - Gross irrigated area **41.7% (2001-02) → 55.8%** of GCA (2022-23); Soil Health Cards issued: **25.55 crore** (Nov 2025).
[110:3:4] # Andhra Pradesh Micro Irrigation Project (APMIP)
[110:3:5] - AP Micro Irrigation programme introduced **2003**; APMIP now implemented State-wide under **PMKSY**; **AP ranked 1st** in micro-irrigation (2024-25), led by **Ananthapuramu**.
[110:3:6] TABLE head: Farmer category (drip) | Subsidy

  [110:3:6.1] Other small and marginal | 90%
  [110:3:6.2] 5-10 acres | 70%
  [110:3:6.3] Above 10 acres | 50%
[110:3:7] - **7 Jan 2026**: automation-based micro-irrigation at **Governorpet, Vijayawada** – Minister **K. Atchannaidu**; APSES 2024-25 focus: precision farming, drones, IoT.

## SUB 4 | Precision Agriculture, Remote Sensing and Crop Monitoring
[110:4:0] - **Precision farming:** data-driven use of **GIS, GPS, remote sensing**, sensors and drones to apply water, fertiliser and pesticide where and when needed; tools include fertigation, greenhouse, mulching, micropropagation, high-density planting, drip.
[110:4:1] TABLE head: Tool | Use
  [110:4:1.0] Mahalanobis National Crop Forecast Centre (2012, Agriculture Ministry) | Institutionalises ISRO's crop forecasting (FASAL) and drought/disaster methodology
  [110:4:1.1] KISAN project (Oct 2015) | Space tech for crop-cutting experiments under PMFBY
  [110:4:1.2] GIS | Soil and Land Use Survey of India maps soils
  [110:4:1.3] Variable-rate technology | Inputs at varying rates within a field by sensor/GIS maps
  [110:4:1.4] GPS, drones, AI, sensors | Second Green Revolution / sustainable intensification
[110:4:2] - **Crop logging** = **plant (tissue) analysis** to assess the crop's nutrient requirement during growth (developed for sugarcane), not soil testing.
[110:4:3] > Exam angle: MNCFC (ISRO methodology), crop logging definition.

## SUB 5 | Drones, Digital Agriculture Mission, Solar Pumps and AI
[110:5:0] - **Kisan drones**: crop assessment, soil-health analysis, spraying; regulated by **DGCA** under the **Drone Rules, 2021**; spraying SOPs released by **Narendra Singh Tomar** (21 Dec 2021).
[110:5:1] - **Drone Shakti**: manufacturing and **Remote Pilot Certificate** training.
[110:5:2] - **Namo Drone Didi**: drones to **women SHGs**, target **15,000**, outlay **Rs 1,261 crore**, **80%** central assistance (max Rs 8 lakh).
[110:5:3] - **Drone Corporation of AP**: State-owned, **drone-as-a-service**; spraying in all 26 districts (Jan 2026).
[110:5:4] TABLE head: Digital Agriculture Mission | Fact
  [110:5:4.0] Approved | September 2024
  [110:5:4.1] Outlay | Rs 2,817 crore
  [110:5:4.2] DPI | AgriStack (Aadhaar-like, federated Centre-State), Krishi DSS (satellite, weather, soil, crop data), Soil Fertility Profile
[110:5:5] - **Digital crop survey**: plot-level, real-time crop acreage; AP **e-Crop (e-Panta)** through **Rythu Bharosa Kendras**; Karnataka **FRUITS** (2020).
[110:5:6] - **PM-KUSUM** (MNRE, 2019): A = solar plants on barren/fallow land; **B = standalone solar pumps**; **C = solarising grid-connected pumps**.
[110:5:7] - **Kisan e-Mitra**: AI chatbot for **PM-KISAN** queries.

=== NOTES 5:111 | Genetic Engineering – rDNA, Gm Crops, Gene Editing and Environmental Impact of Transgenics
## SUB 0 | rDNA Technology – Tools, Vectors and Steps
[111:0:0] # What genetic engineering does
[111:0:1] - **Genetic engineering / recombinant DNA (rDNA) technology:** cutting, joining and transferring genes between organisms; **rDNA = DNA formed by combining genetic material from different sources** – also called **chimaeric (chimeric) DNA**.
[111:0:2] - **Transgenic organism:** an organism whose DNA carries a **foreign gene from another species** (the "transgene"), inserted artificially for a **desired characteristic or product**.
[111:0:3] - **GMO:** any organism whose genome is altered by genetic engineering – genes moved **in ways that would not occur naturally**.
[111:0:4] - **Across species:** rDNA moves genes **plant to plant, animal to plant and microbe to higher organism** – all three possible (UPSC 2013).
[111:0:5] - **Mouse eight times its size** with a human growth-hormone gene = **genetic engineering** (not hybridisation, mutation breeding or hormonal feeding).
[111:0:6] - **Tried with success:** **disease resistance, growth promotion, animal cloning**; **human cloning is NOT** (banned, no fruitful result) → "1, 2 and 3".
[111:0:7] # Tools
[111:0:8] TABLE head: Tool | Role
  [111:0:8.0] Restriction endonuclease | "Molecular scissors" / chemical knives – cuts DNA at specific recognition (base) sequences, not at random
  [111:0:8.1] DNA ligase | "Molecular glue" – joins DNA fragments (gene + vector); in replication it joins Okazaki fragments
  [111:0:8.2] Plasmid | Small, extra-chromosomal, usually circular double-stranded DNA of bacteria; replicates independently; the commonest vector
  [111:0:8.3] pBR322 | Early, widely used E. coli cloning vector; p = plasmid, BR = Bolivar and Rodriguez
  [111:0:8.4] Origin of replication (ori) | The specific DNA sequence that initiates replication; alien DNA linked to it can multiply in the host
  [111:0:8.5] PCR | Amplifies (copies) a specific DNA segment in a test tube – no living cells needed (heat-stable Taq polymerase, 20-40 cycles)
  [111:0:8.6] RNAi | Gene silencing
[111:0:9] - **Two bacteria most used:** ***Escherichia coli*** (host for cloning) and ***Agrobacterium tumefaciens*** (plant vector).
[111:0:10] - ***Agrobacterium tumefaciens*:** soil bacterium that **causes crown gall disease** (dicots) by transferring **T-DNA** of its **Ti plasmid**; the disarmed Ti plasmid makes it "**Nature's genetic engineer**" → useful for agriculture, crown gall agent and gene-transfer tool = **all of the above**.
[111:0:11]   - Crown gall is NOT caused by *Bacillus thuringiensis* (insect pathogen) or *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (biocontrol).
[111:0:12] # Steps
[111:0:13] - **Isolate DNA → cut at specific sites (restriction enzyme) → join to vector with DNA ligase (rDNA) → insert into host (transformation) → select, multiply, express product.**
[111:0:14] - Recombinant plasmid enters a bacterium by **transformation**; bacteria multiply fast and act as factories.
[111:0:15] - **Gene cloning:** making many copies of a desired gene or DNA fragment; **genetic library** = a collection of cloned DNA of an organism.
[111:0:16] TABLE head: Field | Biotech use
  [111:0:16.0] Agriculture | Transgenic / GM crops
  [111:0:16.1] Medicine | Drugs, vaccines, diagnostics, gene therapy; human insulin from GE bacteria
  [111:0:16.2] Industry | Microbial production of useful products
  [111:0:16.3] Research | Biochips – DNA, RNA and protein chips for diagnostics and drug discovery
[111:0:17] > Exam angle: molecular scissors = restriction endonuclease (×3); plasmid; E. coli + Agrobacterium; crown gall; ligase joins Okazaki fragments; ori; PCR; chimaeric DNA; human cloning not achieved.
[111:0:18] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Biology p.61-62, p.67-69, p.71 (rDNA, restriction enzyme, ligase, plasmid, pBR322, PCR, GMO); Rocket Biology p.40 (Agrobacterium – crown gall); PMF S&T Part 2 p.86-91 (tools, steps, PCR, UPSC 2013); OnlyIAS NCERT S&T Part 12 p.14-18 (ori, ligase, Agrobacterium T-DNA); genome.gov PCR fact sheet (thermocycler, Taq polymerase – a machine reaction); app notes 5:111 sub 0-1

## SUB 1 | Bt Technology and Bt Cotton
[111:1:0] # Bt
[111:1:1] - **Bt = *Bacillus thuringiensis***: Gram-positive **soil bacterium** – not Bacillus subtilis, amyloliquefaciens or "thermophilus".
[111:1:2] - **Cry (delta-endo) toxins:** crystal **protoxin** activated in the larva's **alkaline gut** → pores in midgut → death; trait = **resistance to insects** (bollworms, borers), not to viruses, bacteria or fungi.
[111:1:3] - **Insect-resistant cotton = gene from a bacterium** (not virus, insect or plant); A: Bt cotton made by inserting the Bt gene, R: the gene is from a bacterium → **both true, R explains A**.
[111:1:4] - **Bt seed is associated with cotton.**

## SUB 6 | Environmental Impact of Transgenics and Terminator Seeds
[111:6:0] # Risks to environment
[111:6:1] - **Primary environmental concern = cross-pollination / gene flow:** transgenes move by **pollen** into non-GM crops and wild relatives → contamination of local varieties, **superweeds**, loss of endangered relatives (APPSC G2 Mains 2025) – not "reduced pesticide use" or "increased yield" (benefits).
[111:6:2] - Other risks: **build-up of insect resistance**, **harm to non-target organisms** (pollinators, soil microbes), effects on biodiversity, **genetic homogenisation** (uniform varieties over large areas).
[111:6:3] - Highest risk at a crop's **centre of diversity** – India for **brinjal**.
[111:6:4] - Bt insecticidal proteins were suspected to persist but **degrade in soil**.
[111:6:5] TABLE head: Biosafety risk | Case
  [111:6:5.0] Allergenicity | Soybean with a Brazil-nut methionine gene – never released
  [111:6:5.1] Antibiotic-resistance markers | Fear of spreading resistance to pathogens
  [111:6:5.2] Horizontal gene transfer | Genes moving between unrelated organisms
[111:6:6] # GURT – terminator technology
[111:6:7] - **GURT = Genetic Use Restriction Technology** (not transformation/prohibition/termination technology).
[111:6:8] TABLE head: Type | Mechanism
  [111:6:8.0] V-GURT ("terminator") | Seeds sterile in the next generation → farmers must buy seed every season
  [111:6:8.1] T-GURT ("traitor") | Seed stays fertile and transgenic; the trait is switched on/off by a chemical
[111:6:9] - So terminator technology promotes the sale of transgenic (fertile T-GURT) seed, GM plants and seed sterile in the next generation → "**all of the above**" (UPPSC 2004 key).
[111:6:10] - Backed by developed-country firms; seeks **improved seeds that cannot be used as seed for the next crop**.
[111:6:11] - India: the **PPV&FR Act, 2001** bars registration of GURT varieties; **National Gene Fund** for benefit sharing.
[111:6:12] > Exam angle: cross-pollination = primary concern; GURT full form; terminator = sterile seed (×2).

## SUB 7 | Gene Therapy and Genetic Medicine
[111:7:0] - **Gene therapy:** treating disease by correcting the gene defect – **inserting a normal gene to replace/compensate for a defective one** (also inactivating or editing genes); exam options "treating the diseased genes" and "replacement of defective genes" describe the same idea.
[111:7:1] - **Genetic medicines** (UPSC 2026): **correct or compensate for faulty genes**; carriers = **engineered viruses (viral vectors) and lipid nanoparticles**; they do **NOT alter the entire DNA sequence** → "1 and 2 only".
[111:7:2] - **Vectors:** adenovirus, retrovirus, lentivirus; liposomes, nanoparticles, electroporation.
[111:7:3] - **Somatic** gene therapy (only the patient) is accepted; **germline** (inheritable) is generally prohibited in humans.
[111:7:4] - CDSCO gene-therapy rules: **2023**; India's first indigenous **CRISPR-based gene therapy** – for **sickle-cell disease** (2026).
[111:7:5] - **Pharmacogenomics:** genetics and drug response; **personalised medicine** built on an individual's genome.
[111:7:6] - **Mitochondrial replacement therapy = "three-parent baby"**; concern = germline editing, designer babies.
[111:7:7] - **Synthetic biology:** insulin, artemisinin; **semaglutide grown in baker's yeast**.
[111:7:8] - **Recombinant vaccines:** Hepatitis B, HPV, Novavax.
[111:7:9] # Gene drives and GM animals
[111:7:10] - **Gene drive:** >90% of GM mosquito offspring inherit the trait (normal 50%); **suppression** (sterile females) vs **modification** (parasite cannot develop); concern = spreads through wild populations with **no simple recall**.
[111:7:11] - **AquAdvantage** = GM **salmon**; escapees may out-compete wild stock.

## SUB 0 | Graphene, Fullerene and Carbon Nanotubes
[113:0:0] # Carbon nanomaterials (all sp², all pure carbon)
[113:0:1] TABLE head: Material | Structure | Remember
  [113:0:1.0] Graphene | Single atom-thick layer of graphite; 2D hexagonal (honeycomb) lattice | Made of carbon only – NOT silicon; thinnest and strongest material (~200 times steel; stronger than steel, brass, German silver); excellent electrical and heat conductor; ~97% transparent; flexible
  [113:0:1.1] Fullerene C60 | Hollow cage ("buckyball", soccer ball): 12 pentagons + 20 hexagons, 60 C atoms | Carbon allotrope – NOT a fluorine compound (Teflon is the fluorine polymer); semiconductor; drug delivery, lubricants, catalysts
  [113:0:1.2] Carbon nanotubes (CNTs) | Rolled graphene cylinders (1D tubes) | Stronger than steel by weight, light; metallic or semiconducting; drug delivery, biosensors, batteries
[113:0:2] - **Graphene uses:** **conducting electrodes for touch screens, LCDs and OLEDs**, fast transistors, flexible circuits, sensors, batteries and supercapacitors.
[113:0:3] - **Discoverers:** graphene isolated **2004** by **Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov** (Manchester) – **Nobel Physics 2010**; fullerene C60 found **1985** by **Kroto (Britain), Curl and Smalley (USA)** – **Nobel Chemistry 1996** (no "Faimen" on the team); named after architect **R. Buckminster Fuller**; **CNTs** – **Sumio Iijima (1991)**.
[113:0:4] - **Pairs angle (UPPCS):** Dry ice – solid CO2; Teflon – fluorine polymer; Sevin – insecticide; Keratin – protein; Mustard gas – chemical weapon; **Fullerene – carbon allotrope** (the wrongly matched pair).

## SUB 1 | Diamond, Graphite and Other Forms of Carbon
[113:1:0] TABLE head: Allotrope | Bonding | Key property → use
  [113:1:0.0] Diamond | sp³; 3D tetrahedral network, each C bonded to four | Hardest natural substance (Mohs 10); least compressible; electrical insulator (no free electrons) but excellent heat conductor; transparent, high refractive index → cutting tools, jewellery
  [113:1:0.1] Graphite | sp² layers held by weak van der Waals forces | Soft, slippery, opaque, black; good electrical conductor → pencil "lead" (graphite + clay), dry lubricant for heavy machines, electrodes, reactor moderator
  [113:1:0.2] Amorphous carbon | Non-crystalline | Coal, charcoal, coke, lampblack; charcoal = the amorphous form among diamond/graphite/fullerene; activated charcoal – high surface area
[113:1:1] - **Diamond, graphite, graphene, fullerene, CNTs are all pure carbon** – so diamond is an allotrope of **carbon**; **methane (CH4) is a compound, NOT an allotrope**.
[113:1:2] - **Sand** (silica, SiO2) has **no carbon**; graphite, diamond and coal do.
[113:1:3] - **Lubricant:** **graphite** – not wood, steel, tungsten, bauxite, phosphorus, cuprite, haematite or cryolite.
[113:1:4] - **Hardest:** diamond (not glass, quartz, platinum). **Wurtzite boron nitride** is believed (by simulation) to be ~18% harder, but it is rare and untested – keys keep **diamond** as the hardest naturally occurring substance.
[113:1:5] - **Electrical conductor:** **graphite** (free electrons in layers) – diamond, peat and charcoal are poor conductors.

## SUB 2 | Nanoscale – Size, Units and Properties
[113:2:0] - **1 nm = 10^-9 m** (one billionth of a metre); **1 Å = 10^-10 m**, so **1 nm = 10 Å**.
[113:2:1] - **Nanoparticle size: 1-100 nm** (APPSC 2018, APPSC G2 2019, UKPSC 2012) – not 0.1-1 nm (atomic scale) or 100-1000 nm; a particle with at least one dimension **below 10^-7 m (100 nm)** is a **nanoparticle**.
[113:2:2] - **Scale:** human hair ~80,000-100,000 nm; red blood cell ~7,000 nm; virus ~50-100 nm; DNA width ~2 nm.
[113:2:3] - **Why different:** very high **surface-area-to-volume ratio** (more reactive, catalytic) + **quantum size effect** (discrete energy levels); also superparamagnetism, lower melting point, self-assembly.
[113:2:4] - **Most important property of nanomaterials (RAS 2013 key): friction** – the huge surface-to-volume ratio makes friction and wear dominant at the nanoscale.
[113:2:5] - **Device miniaturisation** = **nanotechnology**, the 21st-century technology (UPPCS 2004).
[113:2:6] - **Nanogold:** gold nanoparticles show **surface plasmon resonance** – look **red, purple (or blue)** instead of yellow, yet stay gold (biocompatible; used in conductive inks and lateral-flow test kits). APPSC key: "**blue gold with properties similar to gold**".
[113:2:7] # Quantum structures
[113:2:8] TABLE head: Structure | Nano-confined dimensions | Example
  [113:2:8.0] Quantum well | One dimension nano, two large (2D) | thin films
  [113:2:8.1] Quantum wire | Two dimensions nano (1D) | nanowires, nanotubes
  [113:2:8.2] Quantum dot | All three nano (0D) | semiconductor nanostructure, about 2-10 nm (TSLPRB: ~10 nm)
[113:2:9] - **Quantum dots:** size-tuned colour – **smaller dots → blue**, larger → red; QLED displays, bio-imaging, solar cells; **Nobel Chemistry 2023 – Bawendi, Brus, Ekimov**.
[113:2:10] - **Other nanomaterials:** **nanoporous materials – separation and catalysis**; nanofibres – filtration, smart clothing, scaffolds; nanocrystals – LEDs, imaging.

## SUB 3 | Piezoelectric Nanogenerators and Nano Devices
[113:3:0] - **Piezoelectric effect:** electric charge from **mechanical stress (pressure)** in crystals, ceramics, polymers; reversible (field → deformation).
[113:3:1] - **NCL Pune nanogenerator:** electricity when **pressure** is applied → **piezoelectric effect** (APPSC G1 2018, 2019) – NOT photoelectric, photovoltaic, thermoelectric, magnetoelectric or induction.
[113:3:2] - **Quartz crystal in watches** and **gas-stove (electronic) lighters** = **piezoelectric effect** (IAS 1993; APPSC).
[113:3:3] - **Light nanogenerator (contrast):** **IASST Guwahati** (DST) – graphene oxide + polyaniline device that harvests **light** energy to power wearables.
[113:3:4] - **Nanoear (2012, LMU Munich):** an **optically trapped gold nanoparticle** – detects sound down to **–60 dB** (not –120 dB; not silica or silver).
[113:3:5] - **Internet of Nano Things (IoNT):** network of nanodevices connected to the internet.
[113:3:6] - **Other devices:** gold-nanoparticle lateral-flow kits (malaria, dengue, typhoid in ~15 min); **graphene bendable wearable sensors (IISc + C-DAC)**.
