## SUB 0 | Carbon Sinks, Blue Carbon, REDD+ and Carbon Monitoring
[0:0] # Sinks
[0:1] - **Carbon sink:** a reservoir that accumulates and stores carbon. **Natural sinks: forests, soil, oceans** (chlor-alkali plants are NOT sinks). Forests act as sinks when they grow in density or area.
[0:2] - **Blue carbon:** carbon captured by **oceans and coastal ecosystems** – **mangroves, seagrass, salt marshes** (all three are blue-carbon ecosystems). Forest/soil carbon is not "blue"; fossil carbon is in petroleum and gas.
[0:3] - **Best sink per unit area = blue carbon ecosystems:** they store several times more carbon per area than tropical rainforests (seagrass ~3 times). Glaciers are not sinks.
[0:4] - **Tropical peatlands:** most carbon **per hectare** of any terrestrial ecosystem.
[0:5] - **Carbon fertilisation:** **increased plant growth** because more atmospheric CO2 raises photosynthesis (~**70%** of Earth's recent greening). Not warming, not ocean acidity.
[0:6] - **Bhutan** = carbon-negative country. Net zero = emissions balanced by removals.

[0:9] # IPCC carbon pools (5)
[0:10] - Above-ground biomass, below-ground biomass, dead wood, litter, soil organic carbon. **Atmospheric oxygen is NOT a pool.**
[0:11] # REDD+ and forest-carbon finance
[0:12] - **REDD+** = Reducing Emissions from **Deforestation and Forest Degradation** (+ conservation, sustainable management, enhancement of stocks).
[0:13] - **UN-REDD** (2008; **UNDP, FAO, UNEP**): proper design contributes to **biodiversity protection, forest resilience and poverty reduction – all three** (UPSC 2016).
[0:14] - **Forest Carbon Partnership Facility (World Bank):** global partnership of governments, businesses, civil society and indigenous peoples; **helps countries with REDD+** finance and technical aid. It does **not** fund universities/scientists for forestry research (UPSC 2015: I and III).
[0:15] - **Khasi Hills Community REDD+ Project** (East Khasi Hills, **Meghalaya**): **India's first community REDD+ project, Plan Vivo certified**; run by Khasi **himas**; ~27,000 ha (UPSC 2026).

## SUB 1 | Carbon Sequestration, CCS and Carbon Removal
[1:0] - **Carbon sequestration:** capturing and long-term storing of atmospheric CO2 – in plants (afforestation), soil, oceans or geological formations. **Afforestation = capturing and storing CO2.**
[1:1] - **CCS (Carbon Capture and Storage), "also known as carbon sequestration":** capture at a stationary point source (power plant, cement plant) → transport → storage (**3 steps**); store only. APPSC key for "technology that captures and stores CO2 from industrial processes" = **carbon sequestration**.
[1:2] - **CCUS:** capture, then **use or store**; supports coal gasification and the hydrogen economy.
[1:3] TABLE head: Method | Meaning
  [1:3.0] Geological storage | Abandoned/uneconomic coal seams, depleted oil and gas reservoirs, deep saline formations – all three (UPSC 2017)
  [1:3.1] Enhanced rock weathering | Finely ground basalt spread on farmland binds CO2 as carbonate
  [1:3.2] Ocean alkalinity enhancement | Adding lime/alkaline minerals to seawater
  [1:3.3] Mine storage | Industrial CO2 pumped into abandoned mines as carbonated water
  [1:3.4] Direct Air Capture (DAC) | CO2 straight from ambient air anywhere; a sequestration method; CO2 used in plastics, food/beverage processing; with hydrogen gives synthetic aviation fuel (UPSC 2025: all three)
  [1:3.5] BECCS | Bioenergy + CCS → net-negative emissions
[1:4] - UPSC 2023: basalt on farms, lime in oceans, CO2 into abandoned mines – **all three** are discussed for carbon capture and sequestration.
[1:5] - **Blue carbon (mangroves) is a sink, not geological storage.**
[1:6] - **NET Power** (La Porte, Texas): **Allam-Fetvedt cycle** – burns gas in oxygen with **supercritical CO2** as working fluid; claims power as cheap as a gas combined-cycle plant while **capturing nearly all CO2**.
[1:7] - **DST:** five **CCU testbeds** in the **cement** sector – a first-of-its-kind research cluster.

## SUB 2 | Carbon Credits, Markets and Carbon Taxes
[2:0] # Credits
[2:1] - **1 carbon credit = 1 tonne (1,000 kg) of CO2 / CO2e** avoided or removed – not of carbon.
[2:2] - **Kyoto Protocol (1997):** caps on GHG emissions; credit system ratified with Kyoto; credits go to countries/groups that cut emissions below quota; goal = limit emissions. **CDM** issues **CERs** (Certified Emission Reductions); JI issues ERUs.
[2:3] - **Prices set by supply and demand** on exchanges – **NOT fixed by UNEP** or any body (UPSC 2011 "not correct" option).
[2:4] - **ERPA (May 2011):** World Bank (for IBRD/BioCarbon) and **Himachal Pradesh** government – CERs from the Mid-Himalayan watershed (forestry) project.
[2:5] TABLE head: Voluntary standard | Run by
  [2:5.0] Verified Carbon Standard (VCS) | Verra
  [2:5.1] Gold Standard | Founded by WWF
[2:6] # India's compliance market
[2:7] - **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS):** notified **June 2023** under **Sec. 14AA, Energy Conservation Act 2001** (NOT EP Act); run by **BEE** (Ministry of Power); trades **Indian Carbon Credits**; India's **first mandatory compliance** market; 7 sectors, ~477 Mt CO2e.
[2:8] - **Oct 2025:** emission-intensity targets under CCTS for **aluminium, cement, chlor-alkali, pulp & paper** (282 entities). Carbon pricing covers **29%** of global GHGs.
[2:9] - **Net Zero Portal** (**MoEFCC**, 16 Sep 2026): voluntary registration/disclosure of net-zero commitments; launched with the NAPCC Dashboard.
[2:10] - **Budget 2026-27 carbon credit programme:** follows the DST roadmap – industrial, not farmer-only. India–Japan **Joint Crediting Mechanism** (Paris Art. 6.2).
[2:11] # Carbon taxes and border levies
[2:12] - **New Zealand:** first to **propose** a tax on agricultural (livestock) emissions ("burp tax", 2022, later scrapped) (APPSC G1); also credited (UPSC 2006) as first to propose a carbon tax for its people.
[2:13] - **Denmark:** first to **levy** a livestock carbon tax, **from 2030**.
[2:14] - **EU CBAM:** levy on imports to stop **carbon leakage**; transitional 2023, **definitive 1 Jan 2026**; covers iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen, electricity (not textiles). **Article 9**: carbon price already paid (e.g. CCTS) is deductible.
[2:15] - **UK CBAM:** from **1 Jan 2027**; Sep 2026 UK recognised CCTS as a qualifying carbon price.

## SUB 3 | Footprints and Measurement Tools
[3:0] TABLE head: Footprint | Measures
  [3:0.0] Carbon footprint | Total GHGs produced, directly and indirectly, to support human activities (t CO2/yr; individual, organisation, product)
  [3:0.1] Ecological footprint | Land and water needed to produce what we consume and absorb our waste; unit global hectare (gha)
  [3:0.2] Water footprint | Freshwater used – blue, green, grey water
  [3:0.3] Scope | Emissions
  [3:0.4] Scope 1 | Direct, from owned sources (e.g. diesel generator)
  [3:0.5] Scope 2 | Purchased electricity or heat
  [3:0.6] Scope 3 | Rest of value chain (manufacture, transport of inputs)
[3:1] - **Common Carbon Metric** (supported by **UNEP**): measures energy use and GHG emissions of **building operations** (UPSC 2021).
[3:2] - Carrying capacity = population an area can support; not a footprint.

## SUB 5 | Carbon Emissions – Emitters, Sources and Targets
[5:0] - **Biggest CO2 emitter: China** (~30-32%) > USA (~13%) > **India (3rd, ~8%)**; India has only ~3% of historical emissions (Global Carbon Budget 2025).
[5:1] - India per capita: about **2 tonnes** CO2 vs world ~4.7 t – **lowest in the G20**.
[5:2] - Sources: **fossil-fuel burning** – petrol and diesel in automobiles; **coal** in thermal power (CO2, NOx, SO2).
[5:3] TABLE head: India sector | Share of GHG emissions
  [5:3.0] Cement | ~6%
  [5:3.1] Aluminium | ~2.8%
  [5:3.2] Waste | 2.56% (2020, BUR-4)
[5:4] - **Coal thermal power – steps:** **supercritical technology** (higher efficiency, less coal/CO2 per unit); **Dec 2015 MoEFCC emission norms** (new limits for PM, **SO2, NOx, mercury**; water use). No freeze on coal capacity or gas conversion was ordered.
[5:5] - **Biomass co-firing:** 5% (FY 2024-25) → **7% (FY 2025-26)**, watched by the **SAMARTH** mission.
[5:6] - **NITI Aayog** decarbonisation roadmaps (cement, aluminium, MSMEs): round-the-clock RE, nuclear, CCUS.
[5:7] - **Carbon bombs:** fossil projects able to emit **over 1 Gt CO2** in their lifetime (LINGO list, 425 worldwide); India: **Gevra (Chhattisgarh)**, **Rajmahal (Jharkhand)** coal mines.
[5:8] - **2035 NDC:** **47%** emission-intensity cut vs 2005; **60%** non-fossil power capacity; net zero 2070.
