=== NOTES 4:43 | India – Soil Types, Characteristics and Distribution; Soil Erosion and Conservation
## SUB 0 | Soil Formation and Composition
[43:0:0] # Soil Formation Factors
[43:0:1] - Five factors acting together: **parent material (rock)**, **climate**, **relief (topography)**, **organisms (biological activity)** and **time**.
[43:0:2] - Parent rock passes on traits (e.g. limestone → calcium-rich soil); warm, moist climates form soil faster than cold or arid ones.
[43:0:3] - Two origins: **residual (in situ)** soils dominate **Peninsular India**; **drifted (ex situ)** soils dominate **North India**.
[43:0:4] # Soil Composition
[43:0:5] - Good soil by volume: **mineral 45%, organic matter 5%, water 25%, air 25%**.
[43:0:6] - **Soil organic carbon (SOC)**: binds particles into aggregates, raises infiltration, feeds microbes; **clay soils hold more SOC** than sandy soils; topsoil holds the most.
[43:0:7] - **Soil water:** **infiltration** = entry at surface; **percolation** = movement down through soil; **permeability** = ease of movement; **saturated flow** = all pores full.
[43:0:8] - India's average SOC reportedly fell from **1% to 0.3%** over 70 years.
[43:0:9] # Soil Profile (Horizons)
[43:0:10] TABLE head: Horizon | Meaning
  [43:0:10.0] O | Organic litter
  [43:0:10.1] A | Topsoil, humus-rich
  [43:0:10.2] E | Leached layer
  [43:0:10.3] B | Subsoil – minerals washed down from above accumulate
  [43:0:10.4] C | Weathered parent material
  [43:0:10.5] R | Bedrock
[43:0:11] - Order from surface: **O → A → B → C → R**.

## SUB 2 | Alluvial Terms and Karewa Soils
[43:2:0] # Khadar, Bhangar, Bhabar, Tarai
[43:2:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [43:2:1.0] Khadar | New alluvium; renewed by flood silt every year; more fertile; ideal for intensive agriculture
  [43:2:1.1] Bhangar | Old alluvium on higher ground beyond flood reach; kankar (calcareous concretions); old Kachhari clay; pale reddish brown
  [43:2:1.2] Bhabar | Coarse, pebbly piedmont belt (8–16 km) south of the Shiwalik; streams disappear
  [43:2:1.3] Tarai | Marshy lowland south of Bhabar; now largely reclaimed
[43:2:2] The claim that Khadar holds calcareous deposits is FALSE – kankar belongs to Bhangar.
[43:2:3] # Karewa Soils
[43:2:4] - **Lacustrine (old lake-bed) deposits** of gravel and mud; placed in the Tertiary rock system.
[43:2:5] - Plateaus above the Jhelum plains in the **Kashmir Valley** and Bhadarwah valley.
[43:2:6] - Famous for **saffron (Pampore)**, almonds and walnuts.

## SUB 4 | Black Soil and Peaty Soil – Special Angles
[43:4:0] - **Very deep black soils**: **north-western Deccan Plateau** (APPSC G2 2025).
[43:4:1] - **Large black cotton tracts:** **Maharashtra** (Deccan Trap), Gujarat, MP – not Assam; ⚠ APPSC key (2002) gave **Assam**.
[43:4:2] - **Peaty soils**: areas of **heavy rainfall, high humidity and good vegetation**; organic matter builds faster than it rots.
[43:4:3] "Semi-arid climate with salinity" describes **saline** soil, not peaty soil (G1 2024 distractor).

## SUB 6 | Soil Erosion
[43:6:0] # Types
[43:6:1] TABLE head: Type | Process | Example
  [43:6:1.0] Sheet | Thin uniform topsoil layer removed by overland flow; widespread, least noticed | Gentle cultivated slopes
  [43:6:1.1] Rill | Small channels cut by intense rain | Bare fields
  [43:6:1.2] Gully | Deep trenches; land unfit; final stage | Chambal ravines
  [43:6:1.3] Wind | Deflation, saltation, surface creep | Thar Desert
  [43:6:1.4] Mass (landslide) | Gravity on slopes | Himalayan monsoon landslides
  [43:6:1.5] Tunnel | Subsurface removal; sinkholes | Parts of Australia, Africa
[43:6:2] Increasing soil loss: splash → sheet → rill → gully. Water = most powerful erosion agent.
[43:6:3] - Wind erosion worst on **bare, dry, fine-textured** soil with strong, frequent winds.
[43:6:4] # Factors Controlling Erosion Rate
[43:6:5] - Rainfall **intensity** and duration, **slope**, **vegetation cover**/land use, soil texture.
[43:6:6] Rainfall **intensity** matters more than **total** rainfall.
[43:6:7] - Intense bursts exceed **infiltration capacity** → water runs off or evaporates instead of recharging; two 2026 studies (Nature, Nature Geoscience) link concentrated rain to drier soils.
[43:6:8] - **RUSLE** predicts long-term water erosion on slopes; ICAR made national **C- and P-factor maps**; a 2026 study pairs it with GIS for NE India's hills.
[43:6:9] - Steep slopes lose carbon-rich topsoil.
## SUB 7 | Soil Conservation
[43:7:0] TABLE head: Method | How it works
  [43:7:0.0] Contour ploughing / bunding | Along contour lines on hilly, sloping, rainfed land; slows runoff
  [43:7:0.1] Terracing | Steps on steep slopes
  [43:7:0.2] Strip cropping | Alternate erosion-resistant crops (grass) with row crops
  [43:7:0.3] Shelter belts | Tree rows cutting wind speed (arid W India)
  [43:7:0.4] Crop rotation | Different crops by season; raises fertility, cuts pests, prevents erosion (APPSC 2024)
  [43:7:0.5] Check dams | Trap sediment, stop gully growth
  [43:7:0.6] Conservation agriculture | FAO concept; minimum/zero tillage; retains crop residue as cover
  [43:7:0.7] Afforestation; banning shifting cultivation | Checks erosion
  [43:7:0.8] Straw checkerboards (China) | Stabilise desert dunes
  [43:7:0.9] Biochar | Raises water holding; cuts soil N2O by ~30–50%
[43:7:1] - Others: agroforestry, seed balls; **contour bunding, relay cropping, zero tillage** aid **carbon sequestration**.
[43:7:2] - **Soil conservation** = protecting soil from harm – controlling erosion **and** keeping fertility high (APPSC 2025).
[43:7:3] - **Seed modification does NOT** improve soil fertility; crop rotation, mixed and multiple cropping do.
[43:7:4] - Problem → remedy: deforestation → afforestation; soil erosion → **soil stabilisation**; habitat destruction → restoration; biodiversity loss → conservation.
## SUB 8 | Land Degradation and Problem Soils
[43:8:0] # Salinity, Alkalinity, Waterlogging
[43:8:1] - Caused by **unscientific irrigation and poor drainage**; salts hinder water and nutrient uptake.
[43:8:2] - Affected: **Rajasthan, Haryana, Punjab, UP, Bihar**; canal-irrigated **Punjab** after the Green Revolution. **Maximum alkali area: Uttar Pradesh**.
[43:8:3] - 50% of world's arable land could be affected by 2050 without action.
[43:8:4] TABLE head: Problem | Remedy
  [43:8:4.0] Alkaline (sodic)/saline | Gypsum + about one foot of water for leaching (long-term solution)
  [43:8:4.1] Acidic | Lime
  [43:8:4.2] Salinity (other) | Drainage, leaching, salt-tolerant crops
  [43:8:4.3] Waterlogging | Drains, land levelling
[43:8:5] Gypsum → alkaline soils; lime → acidic soils.
[43:8:6] # Causes → States
[43:8:7] TABLE head: Cause | States
  [43:8:7.0] Overgrazing | Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Maharashtra
  [43:8:7.1] Over-irrigation | Punjab, Haryana, western UP
  [43:8:7.2] Mining; deforestation | Jharkhand, Odisha, MP, Chhattisgarh (mining); hills (deforestation)
[43:8:8] # Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas
[43:8:9] - By **ISRO's Space Applications Centre (SAC), Ahmedabad**, June 2021: **97.85 mha (29.7%)** of India degraded in 2018-19 (28.76% in 2003-05).
[43:8:10] - Desertification rose in **28 of 31** states/UTs; water erosion is the largest driver.
[43:8:11] # Global Status
[43:8:12] - **FAO Status of the World's Soil Resources 2026** (75 countries): soil-biodiversity loss **4th of 7** threats (after erosion, SOC loss, nutrient mismanagement); sustainable practices worsening in **58%** of assessments, improving in ~10%.
