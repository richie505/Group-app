=== Soil Conservation – Methods and Practices
# Meaning
- **Soil conservation:** protecting soil from harm – **controlling erosion and keeping fertility high** (NCERT); it prevents **soil erosion**, not fertility or farming.
- **Best single method to prevent erosion:** **afforestation**; deforestation, overgrazing and heavy rain cause erosion.
# Methods
| Method | How it works / where |
|---|---|
| **Contour ploughing / contour bunding** | Ploughing and bunds along contour lines on **hilly, sloping, rainfed land** slow runoff – reduces erosion by water flow (not up-and-down ploughing) |
| **Terrace cultivation** | Steps on **steep slopes and mountain areas** – the best check on steep slopes; a conservation measure, **not** a cause of erosion |
| **Shelter belts** | **Rows of trees** that cut wind speed and protect soil (arid W India, coasts); CAZRI dune fixing in Rajasthan |
| **Check dams, gully plugging** | Trap sediment and stop gully growth |
| **Strip cropping** | Alternate erosion-resistant strips (grass) with row-crop strips |
| **Mulching** | Cover of residue/straw on the soil – **retains soil moisture**, reduces erosion |
| **Crop rotation** | Different crops in sequence on the same land – **increases soil fertility**, cuts pests, prevents degradation; an example of **sustainable agriculture** |
| Afforestation, regulated forestry, controlled grazing, cover cropping, mixed farming | NCERT remedial measures |
- **Government-promoted measures to prevent erosion:** **contour bunding and afforestation** (SI 2017).
- **Soil fertility is improved by** crop rotation, mixed cropping, multiple cropping, manuring, green manuring – **seed modification does not** improve fertility.
- **Contour bunding is NOT for** desert margins (wind), low flat flood plains or weedy scrublands – so "none of the above" (UPSC 2013).
# Conservation agriculture (FAO)
- Three principles: **minimum / zero tillage**, **permanent soil cover with crop residues**, **crop rotation / diversification** – so 2, 4 and 5 (UPSC 2018); "avoiding monoculture" and "avoiding plantation crops" are not its defined components.
- **Zero tillage:** new crop sown into the previous crop's residue without seed-bed preparation – raises soil organic matter, cuts runoff; of contour bunding, relay cropping and zero tillage, **only zero tillage** is counted as storing carbon in the soil (UPSC 2012).
- **Relay cropping:** next crop sown into the standing crop just before its harvest (e.g. paddy–lucerne).
- Other: agroforestry, biochar (raises water holding, cuts soil N2O), China's **straw checkerboards** (stabilise dunes).
- **Kurnool (Jul 2026):** Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing with **Jeevamrutham** to conserve soil moisture (target 92,000 acres).
# Schemes
- **Soil Health Card:** launched **19 Feb 2015** at **Suratgarh, Rajasthan**; slogan "**Swasth Dharaa, Khet Haraa**"; card **every two years**; **12 parameters** – N, P, K, S, Zn, Fe, Cu, Mn, B, pH, EC, organic carbon (micronutrients: **Zn, Fe, Cu, Mn, B**); **25.79 crore** cards by Feb 2026, most in **Uttar Pradesh**.
- Most Indian soils are deficient in **nitrogen, phosphorus and humus** – the reason for soil testing.
- **IWMP** (2009-10) merged in **2015-16** into the Watershed Development Component of **PMKSY**.
> Problem → remedy pairs: deforestation → afforestation; soil erosion → soil stabilisation; habitat destruction → restoration.

=== Alluvial Soil, Khadar–Bhangar and the Soil-Type Ranking
# Ranking by area
| Soil | Area | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Alluvial** | **15 lakh sq km, 45.6%** (NCERT: ~40%) | **Largest / most widespread** and most productive group |
| Black (regur) | 5.46–5.47 lakh sq km, 16.6% | Deccan Plateau |
| Red (and yellow) | 5.18 lakh sq km (NextGen); 3.5 lakh sq km, 10.6% (PMF) | Often called the "second largest" group – see the dispute below |
| Laterite | 2.48 lakh sq km | Ghats summits |
| Forest / mountain | 2.85 lakh sq km, 8.67% | Himalaya, Ghats |
| Arid / desert | 1.42 lakh sq km, ~4.3% | W Rajasthan |
- **Eight** major soil types (ICAR): alluvial, black, red and yellow, laterite, arid, saline and alkaline, peaty and marshy, forest and mountain.
- **Residual (in situ)** soils dominate **Peninsular India**; **drifted (ex situ)** soils dominate **North India**.
# Alluvial soil
- **Deposited by rivers** (transported/azonal); Indo-Gangetic–Brahmaputra plains, river valleys, **deltas** of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery, Narmada–Tapi valleys, coasts.
- **Sand content decreases from west to east**; becomes more loamy and clayey towards the Brahmaputra valley.
- NCERT: **rich in potash but poor in phosphorus**; poor in **nitrogen** and humus (PMF: potash, phosphate and lime adequate) – so alluvial soils are **deficient in nitrogen**.
- Most fertile / **most productive** soil; needs the **least chemical fertiliser**; crops rice, wheat, sugarcane, jute, cotton, oilseeds.
- **Very little alluvium:** **Madhya Pradesh** (Deccan Trap black soil) – not Bihar, Punjab or Tamil Nadu (deltas).
- **Depth:** average **1,000–2,000 m** in the plains, maximum **about 6,100 m** – exams answer "about 6,000 m".
- **Alluvial sand** has the **minimum water retention**; **clay** retains the most (tiny pores).
# Khadar, Bhangar, Bhabar, Tarai
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Khadar** | **New alluvium**, renewed by flood silt every year; lighter, sandy; **fertile, ideal for intensive agriculture** |
| **Bhangar** | **Old alluvium** on **higher ground** beyond flood reach; **kankar** (calcareous concretions); "old Kachhari clay"; pale reddish brown |
| **Bhabar** | Coarse, pebbly piedmont belt below the Shiwaliks; streams disappear |
| **Tarai** | Marshy lowland **south of Bhabar**; now largely reclaimed |
- Khadar and Bhangar are both **alluvial** soils – not regur, kankar or Bhabar.
- **⚠ Trap:** "Khadar contains kankar" is **false** – kankar belongs to Bhangar (NCERT notes kankar can occur in both, but the exam reason is false).
# Karewa soils
- **Lacustrine (old lake-bed) deposits** of glacial clay and moraines on plateaus above the Jhelum in the **Kashmir Himalayas** (and Bhadarwah).
- Famous for **saffron (zafran)** at Pampore, also almonds and walnuts.
> Dispute: by area in PMF and NextGen, black soil (5.46–5.47 lakh sq km) is larger than red soil, but most books and keys call red (and yellow) soil the second-largest group.

=== Black (Regur) Soil
# Formation and properties
- Formed by weathering of **Deccan Trap basalt** – **fissure volcanic rock** (lava); in Tamil Nadu from gneiss and schist. Also called **regur**, **black cotton soil**, tropical chernozem; Krebs called regur essentially a mature soil.
- **Clay above 62%**: **highest moisture retention** – **needs little irrigation**, suits dry farming.
- **Sticky when wet**, shrinks with **deep cracks** when dry – "**self-ploughing**" soil.
- Colour from **titaniferous magnetite / iron** and dark parent rock – **not from humus**.
- **Rich in** lime, iron, magnesia, alumina, potash (calcium, potassium, magnesium); **poor in nitrogen, phosphorus and humus** – so "rich in humus / nitrogen and organic matter" is a **false reason**.
- Neutral to alkaline; in USDA terms = **Vertisols** (= Grumusols; semi-arid; crack in summer; **not humus-rich**).
# Distribution
- **5.46 lakh sq km (16.6%)** – Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh (**Malwa Plateau**), western AP, north Karnataka, Telangana, Tamil Nadu.
- **Largest area / most widespread: Maharashtra** – not Assam, Bihar, Punjab, Tamil Nadu or Jharkhand. **Not** found in the Himalayan region.
- **Very deep black soils:** **north-western Deccan Plateau** and upper Godavari–Krishna valleys.
# Crops
- **Cotton** (most suitable soil), jowar, wheat, linseed, tobacco, chillies, oilseeds, sunflower.
| AP black-soil areas (Rocket) | Crops |
|---|---|
| Deep black – Adoni, Aluru; unclassified black – West Godavari, Krishna, Guntur, mid-Kurnool | Maize, bajra, tobacco, turmeric, chillies |
> Key note: the app's old notes record an APPSC Group-I 2002 key of **Assam** for the largest black-soil State; the verified answer is **Maharashtra**.

=== Laterite and Red Soils
# Laterite
- Latin **"later" = brick**; formed by **intense leaching** under **high temperature and heavy rainfall (> 200 cm)** with alternate wet and dry seasons; lime and silica leached, **iron and aluminium oxides** left.
- **Red** in colour (iron oxide); **acidic**, **low fertility** – **excess iron** makes it infertile; **poor in nitrogen, phosphate, calcium (lime)**, organic matter; NCERT: iron oxide and **potash** in excess (PMF: poor in potash).
- **Not** moisture-retentive with a large clay factor – it is porous and well drained (that describes black soil).
- **Building material:** cut into bricks that harden in air.
- Crops with manure: **tea, coffee, rubber, cashew, tapioca**, coconut, arecanut.
- **Where:** summits of Western and Eastern Ghats, **Malabar coast (Kerala)**, Vindhya–Satpura, Rajmahal hills, Malwa, south **Maharashtra**, Karnataka, Odisha, Assam, Meghalaya; **Gangani (West Medinipur, West Bengal)**; **not** Rajasthan, UP, HP or Punjab.
# Red (and yellow) soil
- From **ancient crystalline granite, gneiss and schist** under low rainfall; red colour from **ferric oxide** (wide diffusion of iron), **yellow when hydrated**.
- Porous, light, **low moisture retention**; poor in lime, humus, nitrogen, phosphates; fairly rich in potash.
- **Where:** Peninsular India – almost all of **Tamil Nadu**, **Andhra Pradesh**, Karnataka, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Chota Nagpur; Kanyakumari–Jhansi and Kutch–Rajmahal; surrounds the black soil on the south, east and north.
- Crops: ragi, groundnut, tobacco, millets, pulses.
# Andhra Pradesh
- Cultivated area: **red ~60%**, black ~25%, deltaic alluvium 5–6%, laterite ~1%; 22 soil categories; **six agro-climatic zones**.
- Red soils (Rayalaseema): red gram, green gram, horse gram, groundnut, castor; AP laterite is poor in nitrogen.

=== Soil Erosion – Types, Agents and Causes
# Types
| Type | Process | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Splash** | Raindrop impact breaks crumbs – first stage | Bare soil |
| **Sheet** | Thin uniform topsoil layer removed by overland flow; widespread, **least noticed** | Gentle slopes |
| **Rill** | **Small, shallow channels** on the soil surface | Bare fields |
| **Gully** | Deep trenches; **badlands and ravines**; final stage | **Chambal ravines** |
| Wind | Deflation, saltation, creep | Thar Desert |
| Mass (landslide) | Gravity on slopes | Himalayan monsoon slopes |
| Tunnel | Subsurface removal | Parts of Australia, Africa |
- **Increasing soil loss:** **splash → sheet → rill → gully** (II, IV, III, I).
- **Ravines:** Chambal–Yamuna basin on the **periphery of the Gangetic plain** (MP, Rajasthan, UP); also Tamil Nadu, West Bengal; India loses ~8,000 ha a year to gullies.
# Causes and rate
- **Deforestation** is the main human cause (removes root binding and humus); also overgrazing, shifting cultivation, faulty farming. **Terrace cultivation is a remedy**, and tropical climate is not counted a cause – "only deforestation" (UPSC 2014).
- **Deforestation generally decreases rainfall** (and increases erosion, drought, warming).
- Erosion rate depends on **all** of: rainfall intensity and duration, slope, vegetation cover, soil texture, land use.
- **Rainfall intensity matters more than total annual rainfall**; higher intensity → more runoff → **effective rainfall reduces**.
- Wind erosion worst on **bare, dry, fine-textured soil** with strong winds; **water is the most powerful agent**.
- Widely spaced row crops leave soil bare between rows – **sorghum** is the exam answer for fastest erosion (dense clover and wheat protect the soil).
- **RUSLE** model predicts long-term water erosion on slopes (ICAR C- and P-factor maps).
- Intense bursts exceed **infiltration capacity** → runoff, so soils can end up drier (2026 studies).

=== Problem Soils – Salinity, Alkalinity, Acidity and Degradation
# Saline and alkaline soils
- Local names **reh, kallar, usar**, thur, rakar, chopan; caused by **unscientific irrigation and poor drainage**; capillary rise leaves salts on top.
- **Salinisation** of irrigated land: salts left by evaporating water **make some soils impermeable** – it does not raise yields.
- **Largest saline area: Gujarat** (Rann of Kutch, Gulf of Khambhat); **maximum alkali area: Uttar Pradesh**.
- Affected: Rajasthan, Haryana, Punjab, UP, Bihar; Green Revolution canal areas of Punjab–Haryana.
# Amendments
| Problem | Remedy |
|---|---|
| **Alkaline / sodic** (and saline-alkali) | **Gypsum** (CaSO4·2H2O) – **replaces sodium with calcium**; long-term solution, with leaching water |
| Excess soluble salts | **Leaching** – removing salts by applying water |
| Alkalinity | **Acid-forming chemicals** (sulphur etc.) neutralise it |
| **Acidic** | **Lime** – **decreases soil acidity** |
| Poor fertility | **Green manuring** – boosts microbes and fertility |
# Degradation
- **Soil impoverishment** = soil becoming very deficient in plant nutrients.
- **Green Revolution reduced soil fertility**: chemical fertilisers without manure, over-irrigation, salinity.
| Cause | States |
|---|---|
| Overgrazing | Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Maharashtra |
| Over-irrigation | Punjab, Haryana, western UP |
| Mining | Jharkhand, Odisha, MP, Chhattisgarh |
- **Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas** (ISRO **SAC, Ahmedabad**, June 2021): **97.85 mha (29.7%)** degraded in 2018-19 (28.76% in 2003-05); rose in **28 of 31** States/UTs; **water erosion** the largest driver.
- **FAO Status of the World's Soil Resources 2026:** soil-biodiversity loss **4th of 7** threats (after erosion, SOC loss, nutrient mismanagement).

=== Other Soils, Rain-Forest Soils and the ICAR/USDA Orders
# Desert, peaty, forest soils
- **Arid / desert:** sandy, saline; poor in nitrogen and humus, **phosphate normal/high**; **calcium increases downward** (kankar) – **western Rajasthan soils are rich in calcium**.
- **Peaty / marshy:** areas of **heavy rainfall and high humidity with good vegetation**; organic matter 40–50%; black, heavy, highly acidic (NCERT: alkaline in places); Kottayam–Alappuzha, Sundarbans, coastal Odisha–TN, north Bihar, Almora. "Semi-arid with salinity" = saline soil.
- **Forest / mountain:** humus-rich in forests and lower valleys (NextGen, PMF), but **acidic with low humus** on snow-clad upper slopes (NCERT); poor in potash and phosphorus.
- **Podzol:** acidic leached soil of **coniferous (taiga)** forests (about 60°N).
# Tropical rain-forest soils
- **Poor in nutrients**: heat and moisture **decompose organic matter quickly** (true), but nutrients go straight into vegetation or are **leached** (UPSC 2023: I false, II true).
- Cleared rain forest **cannot** support intensive farming for years without fertilisers, though its **primary productivity is very high** (UPSC 2003: A false, R true).
# ICAR / USDA soil orders
| Order | Share of India |
|---|---|
| **Inceptisols** (largest) | 39.74% |
| Entisols | 28.08% |
| Alfisols | 13.55% |

| Ultisols | 2.51% |

- **Histosols** = organic (peat/bog) soils with very high organic matter; **Gelisols** frozen; **Spodosols** acidic leached (podzols); **Vertisols** cracking clays.

=== Soil Formation, Profile, Water and Nutrients
# Formation
- Five factors: **parent material, climate, relief, organisms, time** (soil colour is a property, not a factor); limestone → calcium-rich soil.
- **Tors:** rounded granite boulders left by weathering and erosion of granite – common in Andhra Pradesh (e.g. Rayalaseema).
# Profile
- **Soil profile** = the **vertical arrangement of horizons** down to the parent rock (a single layer is a horizon).
| Horizon | Meaning |
|---|---|
| O | Organic litter |
| A | Topsoil, humus-rich |
| E | Leached layer |
| B | Subsoil – minerals washed down accumulate |
| C | Weathered parent material |
| R | Bedrock |
- Order from the surface: **O → A → B → C → R**.
# Composition and water
- Good soil by volume: **mineral 45%, organic matter 5%, water 25%, air 25%**.
- **Soil organic carbon** binds particles into aggregates, raises infiltration, feeds microbes; India's average SOC reportedly fell from **1% to 0.3%** in 70 years.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Infiltration** | Entry of water at the surface |
| **Percolation** | Movement down through the soil column |
| **Permeability** | Ease of water movement |
| **Saturated flow** | All soil pores filled with water |
- **Clay** retains the most water because of its many **small** pores (not large ones).
# Nutrients
| Nutrient | Main source in soil |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen | Organic matter |
| Iron | Pyrites |
| Calcium | Gypsum |
| Boron | Tourmaline |
- **Zinc** is the micronutrient most widely deficient in Indian soils.
- Crops remove large amounts of **potassium** – among Ca, K, Zn and P the most depleted after harvest.
