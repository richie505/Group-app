## SUB 2 | Mahalanobis Model
[2:0] # Strategy and sectors
[2:1] - **Heavy sector strategy** / **unbalanced growth**: priority to **heavy capital goods industries** (steel, machinery) – most public-sector investment went there.
[2:2] - Originally conceived by **G.A. Feldman** (USSR, **1928**); Mahalanobis (**1953**) developed it independently – so the **Feldman-Mahalanobis** model, based on the **Russian (Soviet)** model.
[2:3] - **Four sectors:** (1) capital goods, (2) factory consumer goods, (3) household / small-scale consumer goods, (4) services.
[2:4] - **Stalin's USSR** began five-year plans in **1928** – **imperative** planning; India follows **indicative** planning.
[2:5] TABLE head: Included / emphasised | Excluded / neglected
  [2:5.0] Industrialisation through heavy industries | Foreign trade – assumed a closed economy
  [2:5.1] Infrastructure, basic and key industries | Agriculture (not the priority)
  [2:5.2] Dominant public sector (socialistic pattern, IPR 1956), self-reliance, import substitution | Exports / export promotion
  [2:5.3] Investment from 7% to 11% of national income | Village and cottage industries were not top priority (left consumer goods to them)
[2:6] # Criticisms
[2:7] - **Neglect of agriculture and small-scale industries**, **over-emphasis on heavy industry**, **neglect of social objectives** (employment, equity) – food and capital shortages, inflation in the late 1950s.
[2:8] - The emphasis on **growth** itself was the plan's aim, **not** the criticism (APPSC G2 Mains 2017).
[2:9] # Second Plan outcome
[2:10] - Growth: target **4.5%**, achieved **4.27%** (most books; NextGen prints 4.21%).
[2:11] - Steel plants **Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela**; **Chittaranjan** – locomotives; **Perambur** – coaches; Atomic Energy Commission.
[2:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.8-9; NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.46 (unbalanced growth); app notes 3:9 sub 3, 8
## SUB 3 | Harrod-Domar Model and Capital-Output Ratio
[3:0] - **g = s / k** (g = growth rate, s = **savings** rate, k = **capital-output ratio**): growth **rises with savings**, **falls with a higher COR** – so COR fixes growth **along with savings**.
[3:1] - **Capital-output ratio** = capital needed to raise output by **one unit** per annum.
[3:2] TABLE head: Basis | High COR | Low COR
  [3:2.0] Meaning | More capital per unit of output; investment gives less output | Less capital per unit of output
  [3:2.1] Capital productivity | Lower | Higher
  [3:2.2] Growth | Slower, even with high savings | Higher potential
[3:3] - ★ Low growth despite high savings and capital formation = **high capital-output ratio** (UPSC 1995, 2018).
[3:4] - ★ Slow per-capita income growth = **high COR + high population growth** (UPSC 1993: "1 and 2").
[3:5] # First Plan
[3:6] - Started **1 April 1951**; priority **agriculture**, irrigation and power; investment target 7% of national income.
[3:7] - Faced refugees, food shortage, inflation; succeeded mainly due to **good harvests in its last two years**.
[3:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.56 (high COR); NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy p.71 (COR); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.6-7; app notes 3:9 sub 2

## SUB 5 | Gandhian Economics and the Janata Strategy
[5:0] - Gandhian economy rests on **Trusteeship**: the wealthy act as trustees of wealth.
[5:1] - "**India's Economic Policy: The Gandhian Blue Print**" – **Charan Singh**; "**Gandhian Constitution for Free India**" – **Shriman Narayan Agarwal** (not M.N. Roy).
[5:2] - **Ram Rajya:** rule on moral authority and justice for the poorest; **not a Hindu state**.
[5:3] - **Janata alternative strategy (1977-80):** ended the Fifth Plan a year early (1978); **Rolling Plan 1978-80** (Gunnar **Myrdal's** concept); stressed **agriculture, rural development, employment, cottage and village industries, minimum needs** – **not** heavy industry, and **SEZs** (a 2000s idea) had no place in it.
[5:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.3; app notes 3:9 sub 6-7
## SUB 6 | Wage-goods Model
[6:0] - Authors: **C.N. Vakil and P.R. Brahmananda** – "**Planning for an Expanding Economy**" (**1956**).
[6:1] - Real constraint = supply of **wage goods** (food, essential consumer goods), **not capital goods**.
[6:2] - More wage goods → **disguised unemployed** put to productive work; stresses **employment**.
[6:3] - Priority: **agriculture and wage goods** vs Mahalanobis's heavy industry.
[6:4] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:9 sub 5
