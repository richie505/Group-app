## SUB 1 | Eco-Mark and AP Institutions
[1:0] - **Eco-Mark (1991):** Government of India (MoEF) scheme labelling **environment-friendly** consumer products that meet environmental criteria plus **BIS** quality standards; certified by the **BIS** (not CPCB); logo **earthen pot**.
[1:1]   - Not about purity, protein content or economic viability – the recurring distractors.
[1:2]   - Products: soaps, detergents, paper, plastics, cosmetics, aerosols, food items; **not drugs/antibiotics**.
[1:3] - **APPCB:** constituted **24 Jan 1976** under the Water Act; air role from **1981**; gives consents. **White** = practically non-polluting; **Green** = low pollution (Jul 2026).
[1:4] - **EPTRI** (Hyderabad): environment training and research.
[1:5] > Asked 4 times (UPSC 1998, CDS 2011, UPPCS 2022, IAS 1998): Eco-Mark = **environment friendly**.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 10th ed. part 21 p.11 (26.4 Eco Mark); NextGen Environment - APPSC 12 p.1 (Ecomark issued by BIS); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.55; app notes 3:21 sub 8
## SUB 2 | Environmental Principles
[2:0] TABLE head: Principle | Meaning
  [2:0.0] Precautionary | Take preventive action even without full scientific certainty of harm; burden of proof on the actor to show the activity is harmless (Rio Principle 15)
  [2:0.1] Polluter Pays | Polluter bears the cost of remediation + compensation for the damage
  [2:0.2] Public trust | The State holds natural resources in trust for the public
  [2:0.3] Absolute liability | Hazardous industries – liable with no exceptions
  [2:0.4] Sustainable development | Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations
[2:1] - **Environmental sustainability** aims to **conserve natural resources and protect global ecosystems** – not humans, wildlife or animals alone.
[2:2] - The NGT must apply sustainable development, the precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle in its orders.
[2:3] > APPSC 2024-25 (Endowment, others): precautionary = act even when not completely sure of the harm; trap = "only after full scientific proof".
[2:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 06 p.95 (NGT applies the three principles), PMF Environment 04 p.46 (polluter pays), p.51 (public trust); app notes 3:21 sub 0

## SUB 4 | Constitutional Provisions
[4:0] - **Article 48A** (DPSP) = duty of the **State**; **Article 51A(g)** (FD) = duty of the **citizen** (both added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976).
[4:1] - **Article 21** read to include a clean environment (Subhash Kumar, 1991).
[4:2] - **Article 253**: Parliament legislates to implement international agreements – basis of the Air Act and EP Act.
[4:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Sheets Environment p.10-11; app notes 3:21 sub 2
## SUB 5 | National Environment Policy 2006
[5:0] - **Year:** adopted in **2006** (approved 18 May 2006) – India's overarching environment policy.
[5:1] - Core idea: **conservation and livelihood security go together**.
[5:2] - Objectives: conserve critical resources; **inter-generational equity**; livelihood security for poor tribal communities; **integrate environment into development plans**; efficient resource use.
[5:3] - Principles: **precautionary + polluter pays + public trust**.
[5:4] - NOT an NEP objective: ending all development in ecologically sensitive areas.
[5:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Sheets Environment p.5 (NEP 2006); app notes 3:21 sub 4
