=== Lorenz Curve and Gini Coefficient
| Person | Measure | Year |
|---|---|---|
| **Max O. Lorenz** (American) | **Lorenz curve** – graphical distribution of income / wealth | **1905** ("Methods of measuring the concentration of wealth") |
| **Corrado Gini** (Italian statistician) | **Gini coefficient** ("Variability and Mutability") | **1912** |
| Simon Kuznets | Kuznets curve (inverted U) | 1955 |
- **Lorenz curve:** X-axis = cumulative % of population (poorest first); Y-axis = cumulative % of income; **45° line = perfect equality**; the farther the curve bows away, the **greater the inequality**. It is a graph, not a number – it **measures inequality of income / distribution of income**.
- **Gini coefficient:** **derived from the Lorenz curve** = area between the Lorenz curve and the equality line ÷ whole area under the equality line; measures **income inequality**.
- Range **0 (perfect equality) to 1 (one person has all income)**; Gini index = coefficient × 100 (0.40 → 40); **higher Gini = more inequality** (so "higher Gini, lower inequality" is wrong).
- **Relative poverty** within India is measured with the **Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient**; absolute poverty with the **poverty line**.
- India: consumption Gini (World Bank, Spring 2025) **28.8 (2011-12) → 25.5 (2022-23)**; income Gini (World Inequality Database) 52 (2004) → 62 (2023).
| Curve | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Lorenz** | Distribution of income (inequality) |
| Phillips | Inflation vs unemployment |
| Laffer | Tax rate vs tax revenue |
| Marshall (demand curve) | Price vs quantity demanded |
| Kuznets | Inequality vs per capita income |
> Most repeated in this row (UPSC CDS 2019, UPPSC 2018, CGPSC 2018, JPSC 2023, SSC 2005, APPSC): what the Lorenz curve / Gini measures, its year, and the statements on Gini.

=== Measuring Inequality in India – Consumption, Income and Wealth
- **NSSO/NSO indicator of household economic inequality = consumption** (**MPCE**, monthly per capita consumption expenditure) – not income, wealth or social status; poverty too is measured from household consumption expenditure.
| Inequality | Data source | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Consumption | NSS/HCES (MoSPI), every five years | Looks lower; may **understate top-end incomes** |
| Income | No official survey: PLFS wages, tax data, WID | Top 10% median about **13 times** bottom 10% (2023-24) |
| Wealth | **AIDIS** (All India Debt and Investment Survey) | Top 10% hold about **77%** |
- **Oxfam, "An economy for the 99%" (Davos, Jan 2017):** India's **richest 1% hold 58%** of total wealth; 57 billionaires = wealth of the bottom 70%; the top 10% share (about 77-80%) is the figure asked.
- **McKinsey Global Institute, "Poorer than their parents?" (2016):** **65-70% of households** in 25 advanced economies had flat or falling real incomes, 2005-2014.
- **Sen's poverty index** combines the headcount ratio, the income-gap ratio and the **Gini coefficient of income among the poor** – i.e. it brings in **income inequality**.
- **Inequality** (of income and assets) is the underlying cause of poverty within a society.
| Survey | Rural MPCE | Urban MPCE | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| NSS 2011-12 | ₹1,430 | ₹2,630 | about 84% |
| HCES 2022-23 | ₹3,773 | ₹6,459 | about 71% |
| HCES 2023-24 | ₹4,122 | ₹6,996 | about 70% |
- Inter-State: **Bihar** lowest per capita NSDP, **Goa** highest (2023-24). AP's district MPI gap (West Godavari 2.42% to Kurnool 12.84%) measures **intra-State** inequality; **Rayalaseema and North Coastal** lag.

=== Measures to Reduce Inequality
| Direct (redistribute assets) | Indirect (fiscal) |
|---|---|
| **Ceiling on land holdings**, land reforms, tenancy laws | Estate duty, wealth tax, capital gains tax, progressive income tax |
- **K.N. Raj Committee (1972)** on taxation of agricultural wealth and income recommended an **Agricultural Holdings Tax**.
- **Wealth tax** abolished from **1 April 2016**; **estate duty** abolished **1985**.
- **Art. 38(2)**: minimise inequalities in income; **Art. 39(c)**: prevent concentration of wealth.

=== Kuznets Curve and Environmental Kuznets Curve
| Curve | Plots | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Kuznets hypothesis** (Simon Kuznets, 1955) | **Inequality vs per capita income**: rises in early growth, then falls | **Inverted U** |
| **Environmental Kuznets Curve** | **Environmental degradation vs per capita income**: rises then falls after a threshold income | **Inverted U** |
- The curve that explains the relationship between **economic growth and environmental degradation** = **(Environmental) Kuznets curve** – not Lorenz, Phillips or Laffer.
- EKC message: "the solution to pollution is **economic growth**".
