=== Plan → Model It Was Based On
| Plan | Model / strategy |
|---|---|
| First (1951-56) | **Harrod-Domar** model (with modifications); priority **agriculture** |
| Second (1956-61) | **P.C. Mahalanobis** model (Nehru-Mahalanobis-**Feldman**; a modified **Soviet / Russian** model) |
| Third (1961-66), "Gadgil Yojana" | **Sandee-Chakravarty** model (NextGen); several books say "no specific model"; import substitution |
| Fourth (1969-74) | **Alan S. Manne – Ashok Rudra** (open) **consistency model**; Gadgil strategy (growth with stability) |
| Fifth (1974-79) | **Dhar** model |
| Eighth (1992-97) | John W. Miller model |
- **Order 1-2-3-4-5:** Harrod-Domar – Mahalanobis – Sandee-Chakravarty – Manne-Rudra – Dhar.
- **Mahalanobis model launched in 1956** (Second Plan) laid the foundation of India's industrial modernisation; Mahalanobis = **architect of the Second Plan**.
- Second Plan distractors: **wage-goods, Gadgil, CELP, Tendulkar, Kaldor, Joan Robinson, Solow** – the key is **Mahalanobis**. First Plan distractors: Lewis-Fei, Keynesian, Manne-Rudra, Gandhian, Sarvodaya – the key is **Harrod-Domar**.
- **Solow (Solow-Swan):** neoclassical model on a **Cobb-Douglas** production function; **no Indian plan was based on it.**
| Model | Bottleneck it assumed | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Harrod-Domar (Roy Harrod, Evsey Domar) | Limited **savings** | Raise savings and investment |
| Feldman-Mahalanobis | **Capital goods** | Heavy industry (steel, machinery) |
| Wage-goods (Vakil-Brahmananda, 1956) | **Food / wage goods** | Agriculture, employment |
> Match question (APPSC): Mahalanobis → Second; open consistency (Manne-Rudra) → Fourth; "no specific model" → Third; Harrod-Domar → First.

=== Mahalanobis Model
# Strategy and sectors
- **Heavy sector strategy** / **unbalanced growth**: priority to **heavy capital goods industries** (steel, machinery) – most public-sector investment went there.
- Originally conceived by **G.A. Feldman** (USSR, **1928**); Mahalanobis (**1953**) developed it independently – so the **Feldman-Mahalanobis** model, based on the **Russian (Soviet)** model.
- **Four sectors:** (1) capital goods, (2) factory consumer goods, (3) household / small-scale consumer goods, (4) services.
- **Stalin's USSR** began five-year plans in **1928** – **imperative** planning; India follows **indicative** planning.
| Included / emphasised | Excluded / neglected |
|---|---|
| **Industrialisation through heavy industries** | Foreign trade – assumed a **closed economy** |
| Infrastructure, basic and key industries | **Agriculture** (not the priority) |
| Dominant public sector (socialistic pattern, IPR 1956), self-reliance, import substitution | Exports / export promotion |
| Investment from **7% to 11%** of national income | Village and cottage industries were **not top priority** (left consumer goods to them) |
# Criticisms
- **Neglect of agriculture and small-scale industries**, **over-emphasis on heavy industry**, **neglect of social objectives** (employment, equity) – food and capital shortages, inflation in the late 1950s.
- The emphasis on **growth** itself was the plan's aim, **not** the criticism (APPSC G2 Mains 2017).
# Second Plan outcome
- Growth: target **4.5%**, achieved **4.27%** (most books; NextGen prints 4.21%).
- Steel plants **Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela**; **Chittaranjan** – locomotives; **Perambur** – coaches; Atomic Energy Commission.

=== Harrod-Domar Model and Capital-Output Ratio
- **g = s / k** (g = growth rate, s = **savings** rate, k = **capital-output ratio**): growth **rises with savings**, **falls with a higher COR** – so COR fixes growth **along with savings**.
- **Capital-output ratio** = capital needed to raise output by **one unit** per annum.
| Basis | High COR | Low COR |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | More capital per unit of output; investment gives less output | Less capital per unit of output |
| Capital productivity | Lower | Higher |
| Growth | Slower, even with high savings | Higher potential |
- ★ Low growth despite high savings and capital formation = **high capital-output ratio** (UPSC 1995, 2018).
- ★ Slow per-capita income growth = **high COR + high population growth** (UPSC 1993: "1 and 2").
# First Plan
- Started **1 April 1951**; priority **agriculture**, irrigation and power; investment target 7% of national income.
- Faced refugees, food shortage, inflation; succeeded mainly due to **good harvests in its last two years**.

=== Gandhian Economics and the Janata Strategy
- Gandhian economy rests on **Trusteeship**: the wealthy act as trustees of wealth.
- "**India's Economic Policy: The Gandhian Blue Print**" – **Charan Singh**; "**Gandhian Constitution for Free India**" – **Shriman Narayan Agarwal** (not M.N. Roy).
- **Ram Rajya:** rule on moral authority and justice for the poorest; **not a Hindu state**.
- **Janata alternative strategy (1977-80):** ended the Fifth Plan a year early (1978); **Rolling Plan 1978-80** (Gunnar **Myrdal's** concept); stressed **agriculture, rural development, employment, cottage and village industries, minimum needs** – **not** heavy industry, and **SEZs** (a 2000s idea) had no place in it.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 01 p.3; app notes 3:9 sub 6-7

=== Wage-goods Model
- Authors: **C.N. Vakil and P.R. Brahmananda** – "**Planning for an Expanding Economy**" (**1956**).
- Real constraint = supply of **wage goods** (food, essential consumer goods), **not capital goods**.
- More wage goods → **disguised unemployed** put to productive work; stresses **employment**.
- Priority: **agriculture and wage goods** vs Mahalanobis's heavy industry.
