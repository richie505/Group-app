=== Sex Ratio – National Trend, Child Sex Ratio and Causes
# Definition and Census 2011 figures
- **Sex ratio:** number of **females per 1,000 males**; "male : female" questions write it as **1000 : 943**.
- **Census 2011:** final **943** (provisional **940**) – rural **949**, urban **929**; 2001: **933** (1000 : 933).
- **Child sex ratio (0-6):** 2011 final **919** (provisional **914**); 2001: 927.
> 940 vs 943 and 914 vs 919 are the provisional vs final traps: a "provisional figures" stem wants 940 / 914; when only 940 is offered, key 940.
# Trend 1901-2011
| Census | Sex ratio | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 1901 | **972** | – |
| 1941 | 945 | −5 |
| 1951 | **946** | +1 |
| 1961 → 1971 | 941 → **930** | **−11 (sharpest decline)** |
| 1981 | 934 | +4 |
| 1991 | **927** (all-time low) | −7 |
| 2001 | 933 | +6 |
| 2011 | 943 | +10 |
- Long run **declining with ups and downs** (rises in 1951, 1981, 2001, 2011) – keys call the trend **"fluctuating"**; the 1991 figure itself was a fall (934 → 927).
- **Child sex ratio** has **fallen continuously** since 1961: **976 (1961), 964 (1971), 962 (1981), 945 (1991), 927 (2001), 919 (2011)**.
# Causes of the low sex ratio
- **Female foeticide / sex-determination tests** (the fear for the world sex ratio), **high maternal mortality**, **neglect and higher death rate of girl children**, and the natural **excess of male births** (about 105 boys per 100 girls) – all four are counted.
- **More boys than girls are born** each year in India (so "more female children are born" is false); boys have the higher biological (neonatal) death risk.
- **NFHS-5 (2019-21):** overall sex ratio **1,020** (NFHS-4: 991) – first time more women than men; **sex ratio at birth 929** (up from 919).
# International
- 2011 decreasing order: **Bangladesh > Pakistan > India > China**; most favourable among Sweden, Switzerland, Netherlands, Finland: **Finland**.

=== Literacy
# Definition and national figures
- **Literate (Census):** a person aged **7 years or above** who can **read and write with understanding** in any language.
- **2011 provisional:** **74.04%** (male **82.14%**, female **65.46%**); **final 72.98% (73.0%)** – male 80.9%, female **64.6%**; national gender gap **16 points**.
- **2001:** 64.84% final (65.38% P); **female 53.67% final (54.16% P)**. **1951:** 18.33% (male 27.2%, female 8.9%).

=== Population Growth – Phases, Decadal Record and the Great Divide
# Phases
| Phase | Feature |
|---|---|
| 1901-21 | **Stagnant** (stationary); population **fell in 1911-21** |
| 1921 | **"Year of the Great Divide" / "Demographic Divide"** |
| 1921-51 | **Steady** growth |
| 1951-81 | **Rapid growth / population explosion** (1961-71 the peak decade) |
| 1981-2011 | **High but declining** growth |
- **1921:** the **only Census with negative decadal growth (−0.31%)**; growth accelerated after it. **1951 is NOT the demographic divide** – but India's growth **since 1951 has been phenomenal** (36.1 crore → 102.9 crore by 2001).
# Decadal record
| Decade | Decadal growth | Annual exponential |
|---|---|---|
| 1951-61 | 21.64% | 1.96% |
| 1961-71 | **24.80% (highest)** | 2.20% |
| 1971-81 | 24.66% | **2.22% (highest)** |
| 1981-91 | 23.87% | 2.16% |
| 1991-2001 | **21.54%** | **1.97%** |
| 2001-11 | **17.64% P / 17.7% final** – lowest since 1921; sharpest fall since Independence | **1.64%** |
- **2001-11:** population rose by **18.1 crore** (181 million); India = **17.5%** of the world (not 18.5%) – "**one in six persons**" is Indian; with its high growth India was expected to overtake China (it did in 2023, UNFPA).
- **Urbanisation:** largest decadal urban growth **1971-81 (46.1%)**; UP's urban population also grew fastest in **1971-81 (60.9%)**.
- **UPPCS 2006:** "40% below the poverty line" was false (official estimate about 26-27%).
- World population by religion (descending): **Christians > Muslims > Hindus > Buddhists**.
- **Replacement level:** TFR **2.1**. **UNFPA SWOP 2025:** India **1,463.9 million**.

=== Census – History, Organisation and India at a Glance 2011
- **Census:** official enumeration of population, every **10 years**; **first attempted 1872** (non-synchronous, operations 1867-72; often loosely "1871-72"); **first complete / synchronous 1881**, regular since.
- **Census Organisation:** under the **Ministry of Home Affairs** – **Registrar General and Census Commissioner of India**, on a permanent footing since **1961**; Census Act 1948.
- **Census 2011:** **15th** national Census (7th after Independence); motto **"Our Census, Our Future"**; first with a **"No religion"** category.
