## SUB 0 | Article 19 – the Six Freedoms
[0:0] - **Article 19** guarantees **six freedoms** to citizens (not Arts. 14, 18, 20, 21 or 22) – **seven originally** (asked as "seven freedoms" in older papers); the 7th, property, was deleted.
[0:1] - **Right to Freedom** group = **Articles 19–22** (four Articles); Arts. 14–16 belong to the Equality group.
[0:2] TABLE head: Clause | Freedom | Restricted by
  [0:2.0] 19(1)(a) | Speech and expression | 19(2)
  [0:2.1] 19(1)(b) | Assemble peaceably and without arms | 19(3)
  [0:2.2] 19(1)(c) | Form associations or unions or co-operative societies (co-ops added by 97th, 2011) | 19(4)
  [0:2.3] 19(1)(d) | Move freely throughout the territory of India | 19(5)
  [0:2.4] 19(1)(e) | Reside and settle in any part of India | 19(5)
  [0:2.5] 19(1)(f) | Acquire, hold and dispose of property – deleted by the 44th Amendment, 1978 | —
  [0:2.6] 19(1)(g) | Practise any profession, or carry on any occupation, trade or business | 19(6)
[0:3] - **Match:** 19(1)(a) = speech and expression; (b) = assemble peaceably without arms; (c) = associations or unions; (d) = move freely throughout India.
[0:4] - **Not in Article 19(1):** **equal opportunity** (Art. 16), **property** (deleted 1978), **moving out of the country** (travel abroad = **Art. 21**, Maneka Gandhi 1978), **freedom of conscience** (Art. 25).
[0:5] - **Property** was removed as a Fundamental Right by the **44th Amendment Act, 1978** (now Art. 300A, a legal right).
[0:6] - **97th Amendment (2011)**: the new right to **form co-operative societies** in 19(1)(c); also **Article 43B** and **Part IXB**.
[0:7] - In force from **26 January 1950** – Art. 19 of the original Constitution provided freedom of expression.
[0:8] - Related Articles: **Art. 20** – protection in respect of conviction for offences; **Art. 21** – protection of life and personal liberty; **21A** (86th, 2002) – education; **Art. 22** – arrest and detention, preventive detention without an **Advisory Board**: max **three months**.
[0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.197, p.200-207; PMF Polity Notes p.62-63; Constitution (44th Amendment) Act, 1978
## SUB 1 | Freedom of Speech and Expression – Press and Implied Rights
[1:0] - Freedom of speech is **the most essential ingredient of democracy** among the freedoms.
[1:1] - **Freedom of the press is not named in the Constitution**; it is **implied in / secured under 19(1)(a)** (not specially provided, not from a law of Parliament, executive power, Art. 361A or the Rule of Law). Trade, residence and association **are** expressly listed.
[1:2] - Speech is protected **irrespective of the medium of communication** – press, TV, internet.
[1:3] - The **right to know, receive and impart information** is part of 19(1)(a); RTI itself is not named in the Constitution.
[1:4] TABLE head: Implied right | Case
  [1:4.0] Press and circulation | Romesh Thappar (1950); Sakal Papers (1961); Indian Express (1985)
  [1:4.1] Hoisting the National Flag (also on private buildings) – 19(1)(a) | Naveen Jindal (2004) – industrialist Naveen Jindal's PIL in the Delhi HC; the Flag Code is only executive instructions
  [1:4.2] Right to silence (not singing the National Anthem) | Bijoe Emmanuel v. Kerala (1986) – speech and conscience (Art. 25)
  [1:4.3] Right to information / to know | State of UP v. Raj Narain (1975); voters' right to know candidates
  [1:4.4] Commercial speech | Tata Press (1995)
  [1:4.5] Speech and trade over the internet | Anuradha Bhasin (2020) – 19(1)(a), 19(1)(g)
  [1:4.6] Telecast; no government monopoly on airwaves | Cricket Association of Bengal (1995)
  [1:4.7] Against phone tapping | PUCL (1997) – safeguards
[1:5] - **National Anthem:** compelling it violates speech/expression and conscience, and **no law obliges anyone to sing it** (the 1971 Act punishes only preventing its singing) → all three statements correct (UPSC 1996).
[1:6] - **Internet access** is linked to **Article 19** (SSC 2020 key; Anuradha Bhasin) – "Right to access internet: Art. 21" is the mismatched pair; privacy and shelter are Art. 21 rights.
[1:7] - **Films may be pre-censored**, unlike the press, because they **reach larger audiences**.
[1:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.197, p.200, p.206; PMF Polity Notes p.62-63; Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala (1986); Union of India v. Naveen Jindal (2004)
## SUB 2 | Reasonable Restrictions – 19(2) to 19(6)
[2:0] - In a democracy the right to freedom = **freedom with reasonable restrictions** – not unlimited, not as fixed by the Government or civil society.
[2:1] TABLE head: Clause | Freedom | Grounds
  [2:1.0] 19(2) | Speech | Eight: sovereignty and integrity, security of State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence
  [2:1.1] 19(3) | Assembly | Sovereignty and integrity; public order (not morality, not security of State)
  [2:1.2] 19(4) | Association | Sovereignty and integrity; public order; morality
  [2:1.3] 19(5) | Movement, residence | General public interest; protection of Scheduled Tribes
  [2:1.4] 19(6) | Profession, trade | General public interest; professional/technical qualifications; State trade or monopoly
[2:2] - **Not a 19(2) ground**: **sedition**, **views opposing the Government**, **unbecoming criticism**, "general public interest", economic grounds. The grounds are **exhaustive** (**Kaushal Kishor, 2023**).
[2:3] - **Defamation**: **slander = spoken** (oral), libel = written; civil (tort) and criminal liability.
[2:4] # Grounds added by amendments
[2:5] TABLE head: Amendment | Added
  [2:5.0] 1st (1951) | Public order, friendly relations, incitement; rewrote 19(6) – qualifications and State monopoly
  [2:5.1] 16th (1963) | Sovereignty and integrity of India in 19(2), (3), (4)
[2:6] # Test of reasonableness
[2:7] - **State of Madras v. V.G. Row (1952)**: nature of the right, purpose, extent and urgency of the evil, proportionality.
[2:8] - **Courts** decide reasonableness; a restriction must **not eclipse the right**; it must safeguard the **interest of the general public** and weigh **prevailing social values and needs**.
[2:9] - **Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar (1962)** – the landmark judgment on **sedition**: s.124A IPC valid only for incitement to violence or public disorder.
[2:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.198-200, p.201 (sedition not a ground); PMF Polity Notes p.63; Kedar Nath Singh (1962) in Indian Polity Part 1 (src/polity/indian-polity-part1.txt)
## SUB 3 | Availability – Citizens Only; Detenus; Golden Triangle
[3:0] - Art. 19 is for **citizens only** – not foreigners, not a company as a legal person (citizen shareholders may sue). Freedom of speech is guaranteed **only to citizens**; life and liberty, equality before law and freedom of religion extend to foreigners too.
[3:1] - A **British citizen** in India cannot claim **freedom of trade and profession** (19(1)(g)).
[3:2] - Available against **State action**; but **Kaushal Kishor (2023)**: Arts. 19 and 21 enforceable against **non-State actors** too.
[3:3] - A person whose movement is taken away by **imprisonment or detention** does **not** lose his **freedom of expression** – he may write and publish books (State of Maharashtra v. Prabhakar Pandurang Sanzgiri, 1966).
[3:4] - **Golden triangle**: Arts. **14, 19, 21** – **Maneka Gandhi (1978)**.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.200; PMF Polity Notes p.62; Indian Polity Part 1 (detenu rights)
## SUB 4 | Assembly, Association, Movement and Profession
[4:0] - Assembly must be **peaceful and unarmed**; excludes violent or **armed** gatherings. A **gherao** of officials is **not** part of 19(1)(b); 19(3) allows public-order limits.
[4:1] - Right to protest is read from 19(1)(a), (b) and 21. **22 May 2026**: SC – peaceful protest is everybody's right but **must not disturb other citizens** or occupy public space.
[4:2] TABLE head: Action | Status | Case
  [4:2.0] Demonstration | Protected; strike not | Kameshwar Prasad (1962)
  [4:2.1] Strike | Not an FR – only a legal right | All India Bank Employees' Assn (1962)
  [4:2.2] Strike by govt employees | No right | T.K. Rangarajan (2003)
  [4:2.3] Bandh | Illegal – curbs others' freedoms | CPI(M) v. Bharat Kumar
[4:3] - **FCRA**: Centre defends curbs under **19(4)** (25 Jun 2026); **INSAF (2020)** – only associations in **active politics** may be barred from foreign funds.
[4:4] - **19(5)**: entry and settlement of outsiders may be limited to protect **Scheduled Tribes** (Inner Line areas).
[4:5] - **19(6)**: State may prescribe **professional or technical qualifications** and run a trade **to the exclusion of citizens** (State monopoly) – inserted by the **1st Amendment**. **No right to a dangerous or immoral trade** (e.g. trafficking).
[4:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.199-200; PMF Polity Notes p.63
