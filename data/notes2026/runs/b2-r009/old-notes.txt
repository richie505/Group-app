=== NOTES 2:9 | Right to Equality – Articles 14-18; Reasonable Classification, Reservation Jurisprudence
## SUB 0 | Right to Equality – Articles 14 to 18
[9:0:0] - The Right to Equality has **five Articles, 14 to 18** (Article 13 is not among them).
[9:0:1] - Equality in India is **not absolute**: it allows **reasonable classification**.
[9:0:2] # Article-wise Content
[9:0:3] TABLE head: Article | Content | Available to
  [9:0:3.0] 14 | "The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India" | All persons (citizens and foreigners)
  [9:0:3.1] 15 | Prohibition of discrimination only on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth | Citizens only
  [9:0:3.2] 16 | Equality of opportunity in public employment | Citizens only
  [9:0:3.3] 17 | "Untouchability" abolished; its practice in any form forbidden; enforcing any disability from it is an offence punishable by law | All
  [9:0:3.4] 18 | Titles abolished, except military and academic distinctions | —
[9:0:4] - APPSC 2025: "Right to Equality" is guaranteed by **Article 14**. PYQ 2018: "Art. 15 is available only to citizens" is correct.
[9:0:5] - Right to equal protection of the law (Art. 14) is open to all persons; Arts. 15, 16, 19(1)(a) and cultural rights are not.
[9:0:6] # Clauses of Articles 15 and 16
[9:0:7] - **15(1)** grounds: religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth **only**; **descent is not among them** (it is in 16(2)).
[9:0:8] - **15(2)**: access to shops, restaurants, **hotels**, wells, tanks, bathing ghats – binds **private persons** as well as the State.
[9:0:9] - **16(1)** and **16(2)**: equal opportunity for all citizens in public employment; 16(2) bars religion, race, caste, sex, **descent**, place of birth, **residence**.
[9:0:10] - **16(3)**: **Parliament only** may prescribe a residence requirement for certain jobs.
[9:0:11] - **16(5)**: posts in a religious or denominational institution may be kept for persons of that religion/denomination.

[9:1:1] TABLE head: Clause | Beneficiaries / subject | Added by
  [9:1:1.0] 15(3) | Women and children | Original
  [9:1:1.1] 15(4) | SEBCs (OBCs), SCs, STs – special provisions | 1st Amendment, 1951 (after Champakam Dorairajan)
  [9:1:1.2] 15(5) | SEBC/SC/ST admission in educational institutions, including private aided or unaided; excludes Art. 30 minority institutions | 93rd Amendment, 2005
  [9:1:1.3] 15(6), 16(6) | EWS, up to 10% in education and public employment | 103rd Amendment, 2019 (passed as the 124th Amendment Bill in Lok Sabha)
  [9:1:1.4] 16(4) | Backward classes not adequately represented in services | Original
  [9:1:1.5] 16(4A) | SC/ST reservation in promotion; later with consequential seniority (nullified the "catch-up" rule) | 77th (1995); 85th (2001)
  [9:1:1.6] 16(4B) | Backlog (carried-forward) vacancies treated as a separate group, outside the 50% ceiling | 81st (2000)
[9:1:2] - **OBC reservation**: Articles **15(4) and 16(4)**. 15(4) and 16(4) cover SCs, STs or OBCs.
[9:1:3] - **105th Amendment (2021)** returned States' power to identify SEBCs (reversed the Maratha-case reading of the **102nd**).
[9:1:4] # Who is NOT covered
[9:1:5] - Article 15 special provisions cover women, children, SEBCs, SCs, STs, EWS [15(6)]; **aged persons are NOT covered** (APPSC GS 2023).
[9:1:6] - Art. 29: cultural and educational rights of any section; Art. 30: minority educational institutions.

## SUB 2 | Types of Equality
[9:2:0] TABLE head: Type | Articles
  [9:2:0.0] Social | 15, 17, 18 (PYQ 2017: Arts. 17-18 = social equality)
  [9:2:0.1] Economic | DPSP 39(b), 39(c), 39(d)
  [9:2:0.2] Political | 325, 326 – no exclusion from rolls, adult suffrage
[9:2:1] - **Economic equality is NOT part of the FR to equality.** Included: equality before law (14), social equality (15), equal opportunity (16).
[9:2:2] - Preamble promises "**equality of status and of opportunity**".
[9:2:3] - **Ambedkar**: liberty, equality, fraternity = an **inseparable trinity**.
## SUB 3 | Article 14 – Two Faces and Rule of Law
[9:3:0] # Equality before Law vs Equal Protection
[9:3:1] TABLE head: Equality before law | Equal protection of the laws
  [9:3:1.0] British origin (Dicey) | American origin (US 14th Amendment)
  [9:3:1.1] Negative: no special privilege; no one above law | Positive: like treated alike in equal circumstances
  [9:3:1.2] All equally subject to ordinary law and ordinary courts | Permits reasonable classification
[9:3:2] - State can treat unequals differently for a level playing field; among equals the law should be equal.
[9:3:3] # Rule of Law – Dicey
[9:3:4] - **A.V. Dicey** (British jurist), *Introduction to the Study of the Law of the Constitution* (**1885**); borrowed from Britain like Cabinet System, Parliamentary Government, Single Citizenship, Prerogative Writs.
[9:3:5] - **Three elements**: (1) **absence of arbitrary power** (limitation of powers) – regular law supreme, no punishment except for a distinct breach of law; (2) **equality before law**; (3) **primacy of individual rights** (liberty and civil rights) – rights from judicial decisions.
[9:3:6] - **Not an element**: "people's responsibility to the Government". "The State has inherent unlimited police powers" – false.
[9:3:7] - Dicey opposed the French **Droit Administratif**: officials are not subject only to special administrative tribunals.
[9:3:8] - **India accepts (i) and (ii) but reverses (iii)**: rights flow from the Constitution.
[9:3:9] - Rule of Law in India rests on **Article 14**; it is a **basic feature** – **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** and **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)**.
[9:3:10] - ★ Answer key: Indian Constitution is similar to **America** in the Rule of Law.
[9:3:11] - **Rule of Law Index**: released by the **World Justice Project** (measuring since 2008; eight areas).
[9:3:12] - Authoritarian government is not a feature of Indian polity.
[9:3:13] TABLE head: Rule of Law | Rule by Law
  [9:3:13.0] Government subordinate to law | Ruler above law; law as a weapon of state control
  [9:3:13.1] Laws just, fair, non-arbitrary | Governs citizens only
## SUB 4 | Reasonable Classification and Arbitrariness
[9:4:0] - Art. 14 forbids **class legislation**, not reasonable classification.
[9:4:1] - **Twin test**: **intelligible differentia** + **rational nexus** with the law's object.
[9:4:2] - **E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu (1974)**: "Equality and arbitrariness are **sworn enemies**" / equality is **antithetic to arbitrariness**.
[9:4:3] - **Maneka Gandhi (1978)**: any arbitrary State action breaks Art. 14; **unguided, uncontrolled discretionary power** may be struck down.
[9:4:4] - Article 14 puts a **limit on legislative power**.
[9:4:5] - 11 Apr 2026: SC held the State cannot treat **pensioners** differently from serving employees on **dearness allowance** without valid classification (Kerala/KSRTC).
[9:4:6] - Feb 2026: SC questioned the caste-discrimination clause of the **UGC equity regulations** (**Mritunjay Tiwari v. Union of India**).
## SUB 5 | Exceptions to Equality
[9:5:0] TABLE head: Provision | Immunity / special status
  [9:5:0.0] Art. 361 | President and Governors not answerable to courts; no criminal case during term; civil case only after two months' notice
  [9:5:0.1] Art. 361A (44th Amendment, 1978) | No liability for a true, non-malicious report of proceedings of Parliament/State legislatures
  [9:5:0.2] Arts. 105 / 194 | MPs / MLAs: immunity for anything said or vote given in the House
  [9:5:0.3] International law | Foreign sovereigns, ambassadors, diplomats and UN agencies immune from civil and criminal proceedings
## SUB 6 | Article 17 – Untouchability
[9:6:0] - Art. 17 secures **social equality**; "untouchability" is not defined in the Constitution.
[9:6:1] - **Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955** → renamed **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** by the 1976 amendment (effective **19 Nov 1976**).
[9:6:2] - **SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989** – further law against caste atrocities.
[9:6:3] The renamed Act keeps the year 1955: "Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955", not 1976.
[9:6:4] - **False evidence against SC/ST (Sec. 3(2)(i), SC/ST PoA Act, 1989):** a person not an SC/ST member who gives or fabricates false evidence so that an SC/ST member is convicted of a **capital offence** gets **imprisonment for life and fine**; if an innocent SC/ST member is **executed** as a result, the punishment is **death**.
## SUB 7 | Article 18 – Titles and Awards
[9:7:0] TABLE head: Clause | Provision
  [9:7:0.0] 18(1) | State confers no title except military or academic
  [9:7:0.1] 18(2) | A citizen shall not accept a foreign title at all – no consent route
  [9:7:0.2] 18(3) | A non-citizen holding an office of profit or trust needs the President's consent to accept a foreign title
  [9:7:0.3] 18(4) | No office-holder may accept a present, emolument or office from a foreign State without the President's consent (PYQ 2016)
[9:7:1] - Art. 18 helps **social equality** by ending artificial distinctions.
[9:7:2] - **Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India (Dec 1995)**: **Bharat Ratna and Padma awards are not titles**, but must **not be used as prefixes or suffixes**.
[9:7:3] - Highest civilian award: **Bharat Ratna**; started with **Padma Vibhushan in 1954**.
## SUB 8 | Reservation Jurisprudence
[9:8:0] TABLE head: Case | Year | Holding
  [9:8:0.0] Champakam Dorairajan | 1951 | Madras Communal G.O. struck down under Art. 29(2); DPSPs subordinate to FRs → 1st Amendment, Art. 15(4)
  [9:8:0.1] M.R. Balaji v. State of Mysore | 1962 | Caste not the sole test; reservation reasonable, generally below 50%
  [9:8:0.2] Janki Prasad Parimoo v. J&K | 1973 | Caste alone cannot be sole determinant of backwardness under 15(4)
  [9:8:0.3] Indra Sawhney (Mandal) | 16 Nov 1992 | 9 judges, 6:3; upheld 27% OBC; creamy layer excluded; 50% ceiling save extraordinary cases; no reservation in promotions; ordered a permanent body (NCBC)
  [9:8:0.4] Samatha v. State of AP | 1997 | No tribal land leases in Schedule V areas to non-tribals/private parties
  [9:8:0.5] M. Nagaraj | 2006 | 5 judges upheld 77th, 81st, 85th Amendments with conditions: quantifiable data on backwardness, inadequate representation, efficiency (Art. 335)
  [9:8:0.6] Ashoka Kumar Thakur | 2008 | Upheld 93rd Amendment; 27% OBC in central institutions; creamy layer excluded
  [9:8:0.7] Niyamgiri (Orissa Mining Corp.) | 2013 | Gram Sabha's role under Forest Rights Act, 2006; Dongria Kondh rights
  [9:8:0.8] Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta | 2018 | No proof of backwardness for SC/ST promotions; creamy layer extended to SC/ST promotions
  [9:8:0.9] Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil (Maratha) | 2021 | Maratha quota struck for crossing 50%; read 102nd as Central list only
  [9:8:0.10] Janhit Abhiyan | 2022 | Upheld 103rd Amendment, 10% EWS, 3:2; economic criteria alone valid
  [9:8:0.11] State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh | 1 Aug 2024 | 7-judge bench, 6:1; overruled E.V. Chinnaiah (2004); sub-classification of SCs/STs allowed on empirical data (Arts. 15(4), 16(4)); recommended creamy layer identification
[9:8:1] - Order: Champakam → Balaji → Indra Sawhney → Nagaraj.
[9:8:2] # Creamy Layer and the 50% Ceiling
[9:8:3] - Creamy layer and 50% cap **originate in Indra Sawhney (1992)**; OBC income limit **₹8 lakh a year (2017)**.
[9:8:4] - **AP angle (Apr 2026)**: Andhra Pradesh matched its OBC creamy layer with the central income limits.
[9:8:5] - Exceptions to 50%: **carried-forward vacancies (81st)**; **EWS 10%** (Janhit Abhiyan). No State legislature has free power to cross the ceiling.
[9:8:6] - Aug 2026: Centre opposes an income-based creamy layer for **SCs and STs**; calls Nagaraj's reference a "general observation".
[9:8:7] Nagaraj needed quantifiable data; Jarnail Singh removed the need to prove backwardness.

=== NOTES 2:10 | Right to Freedom – Article 19 (six Freedoms) and Reasonable Restrictions
## SUB 0 | Article 19 – the Six Freedoms
[10:0:0] - **Right to Freedom** group = **Articles 19-22** (four Articles); Art. 14 belongs to the Equality group.
[10:0:1] - Art. 20: protection in respect of conviction; Art. 21: life and personal liberty; **21A** (86th, 2002): education; Art. 22: arrest and detention – preventive detention without an **Advisory Board**: max **three months**.

  [10:0:3.0] 19(1)(a) | Speech and expression (right to information read in) | 19(2)

  [10:0:3.2] 19(1)(c) | Associations, unions, co-operative societies (co-ops added by 97th, 2011) | 19(4)
  [10:0:3.3] 19(1)(d) | Move freely throughout India | 19(5)

[10:0:6] - **97th Amendment (2011)**: constitutional status to co-operative societies; also **Article 43B** and **Part IXB**. 7th, 44th, 97th did not touch 19(6).
## SUB 1 | Availability
[10:1:0] - **Citizens only** – not foreigners, not a company as a legal person (citizen shareholders may sue).
[10:1:1] - A **British citizen** in India cannot claim **19(1)(g)**. Citizens only, not aliens: Art. 19 and minority rights (Art. 29).
[10:1:2] - Available against **State action**; private violation → ordinary remedy. But **Kaushal Kishor (2023)**: Arts. 19 and 21 enforceable against **non-State actors** too.
[10:1:3] - A **detained/imprisoned person keeps freedom of expression** (may write/publish books) though movement is lost (APPSC G1 2018).
[10:1:4] - In force from **26 Jan 1950** (APPSC 2018).
