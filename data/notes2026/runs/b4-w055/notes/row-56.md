=== Growth Terms – Natural Increase, RNI and Demographic Gap
# Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Natural increase** | **Births − deaths** (crude birth rate − crude death rate); migration and marriage are not part of it |
| **Demographic gap** | The difference **between the birth rate and the death rate** (widest in stage 2 of the transition) |
| Actual growth | Natural increase **+ net migration** (in-migrants − out-migrants) |
| Positive growth | Births exceed deaths, or permanent net in-migration |
| Negative growth | Births below deaths, or out-migration |
| Over-urbanisation | Urban growth faster than urban jobs and services (slums) |
- **CBR** = live births per 1,000 people a year; **CDR** = deaths per 1,000 a year.
# Rate of natural increase (RNI)
- **RNI (%) = (CBR − CDR) / 10** when both are per 1,000.
- **CBR 44, CDR 11:** 44 − 11 = 33 per 1,000 = **3.3%** a year (not 33% or 0.33%).
- CBR 30, CDR 10 → **2.0%**; CBR 25, CDR 7 → **1.8%**.

=== World Growth – Milestones, Explosion and Projections
- First billion took **over a million years**; **5 billion (1987) to 6 billion (1999) took only 12 years** – the late-20th-century **population explosion** (so the period is **1987–1999**).
- World population "exploded" after the **Industrial Revolution** (18th century) as death rates fell.
| Year (UN WPP 2022) | World population |
|---|---|
| 2025 | 8.2 billion |
| 2030 | **8.5 billion** |
| 2050 | **9.7 billion** |
| 2100 | **10.4 billion** |
- **Africa** is the fastest-growing continent (sub-Saharan Africa high growth); Europe and Northern America low to negative.
- More than half of the growth to 2050 will be in eight countries: DR Congo, Egypt, Ethiopia, India, Nigeria, Pakistan, Philippines, Tanzania.

=== Demographic Transition Model
| Stage | Birth / death rates | Growth | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 High stationary | Both high | Slow | Pre-industrial; India before 1921 |
| 2 **Early expanding** | High BR, falling DR | Rapid (explosion) | **Niger, sub-Saharan Africa; India 1951–81** |
| 3 **Late expanding** | Falling BR, low DR | Slowing | **Brazil; India now** |
| 4 **Low stationary** | Both low | Stable | **Andhra Pradesh** |
| 5 **Declining** | **BR below DR** | Falling | **Japan, Germany** |
- Order with development: **high birth–high death → high birth–low death → low birth–low death** (UPSC 2012: 2, 3, 1).
- AP's policy shift from population control to "**Pillale Sampada**" (children are wealth).

=== Low Fertility and Ageing Countries
# Total fertility rate
- **Replacement-level TFR = 2.1**; world average **2.2**.
| Country | TFR |
|---|---|
| **South Korea** | **0.72 (2023)**, world's lowest |
| Japan / Finland / USA | 1.3 / 1.4 / 1.6 |
| **Niger** | ~6.6, world's highest |
- **AP Draft Population Management Policy (5 March 2026):** raise TFR from **1.5 to 2.1**; Rs 30,000 for a third child, Rs 40,000 for a fourth.
# Ageing
- In the news for **low birth rates, ageing or declining population: Italy, Japan, South Korea** – so **1, 2 and 4** (UPSC 2024); Nigeria and South Africa are young, growing populations.
- **Asia-Pacific** ages fastest (65+ to 18.6% by 2050); world 65+ share 10% (2022) to 16% (2050).
- Oldest median age: **Monaco**; youngest: **Niger**. South Asia 65+: about **7.4%** (2025, World Bank).
- India (SRS 2024): elderly 9.7% – **Kerala** highest, **Assam** lowest; India 60+ to **325.3 million (20.5%) by 2051**, median age **40** by 2051.
# Dependency ratio
- **Dependency ratio** = population aged **0–14 and 65+** ÷ working-age population; a lower ratio means a potential **demographic dividend**.
- Economic Survey 2025-26: India's dependency ratio will **double**, making **geriatric care** a growing job sector.
- Highest working-age share (2021): **UAE 85.7%**, Qatar 85.6%. India's window: **2005-06 to 2055-56**, closing **after 2041**; dividend age group **15–64** (UN).

# Sex ratio across countries
- **Qatar** is the most male-skewed (WPP 2024), then the **UAE** – because of **male migrant labour**; Latvia has the most women per man.
# Urbanisation
| Pattern | Where |
|---|---|
| Most urbanised | **Northern America, Latin America, Europe** |
| Fastest urban growth | **Africa and Asia** |
| Largest cities (WUP 2025) | Jakarta, Dhaka, Tokyo, New Delhi |
- Urban share: **45%** of the world in **WUP 2025** (new degree-of-urbanisation method); the older UN series gave 55%.
- **Australia:** population is concentrated in the state-capital cities; **Tasmania**, small and least urbanised, has the highest **rural** share among NSW, Queensland and Tasmania (Perth is a city, not a state).
