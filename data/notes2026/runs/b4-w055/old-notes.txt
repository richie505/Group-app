## SUB 1 | Population Theories – Malthus, Ehrlich and Others
[55:1:0] # Malthusian theory
[55:1:1] - **Thomas Robert Malthus**, *An Essay on the Principle of Population* (**1798**): population grows in **geometric progression** (1, 2, 4, 8), food only in **arithmetic progression** (1, 2, 3, 4).
[55:1:2] - Unchecked growth outruns food and ends in **scarcity, famine, disease and war** – the classic **overpopulation** theory (APPSC: the scientist who said population is too large with severe consequences = **Malthus**, not Darwin, Ehrlich or Einstein).
[55:1:3] TABLE head: Check | Acts on | Examples
  [55:1:3.0] Preventive | Lowers births | Moral restraint, late marriage, celibacy
  [55:1:3.1] Positive | Raises deaths | Famine, disease, war
[55:1:4] - **Criticism:** food production and living standards rose faster than he predicted – **technology, innovation and sustainable resource management**; Marxists and liberals say poverty comes from unequal distribution, not numbers.
[55:1:5] # Neo-Malthusian works
[55:1:6] TABLE head: Book / report | Author | Year
  [55:1:6.0] An Essay on the Principle of Population | Thomas Malthus | 1798
  [55:1:6.1] The Population Bomb | Paul R. Ehrlich | 1968
  [55:1:6.2] The Limits to Growth (World3 computer model) | Donella Meadows and others, for the Club of Rome | 1972
  [55:1:6.3] The Population Explosion | Paul and Anne Ehrlich | 1990
[55:1:7] - **"The battle to feed all of humanity is over"** is the opening line of **The Population Bomb (1968)** – not Malthus's Essay or the 1990 sequel.
[55:1:8] - **"New Population Bomb"** = rapid **urban growth in the Third World**.
[55:1:9] # Other theories
[55:1:10] TABLE head: Theory | Thinker | Idea
  [55:1:10.0] Demographic transition | Warren Thompson (1929) first; Frank Notestein named and formalised it (1945, three stages) | Death rates fall first with development, birth rates later
  [55:1:10.1] Optimum population | Edwin Cannan | The size giving the highest output (income) per head for given resources and technology
  [55:1:10.2] Marxian (anti-Malthusian) | Karl Marx | Hunger and "surplus population" come from exploitation / capitalism, not nature

## SUB 2 | Distribution of World Population and Its Factors
[55:2:0] # Pattern
[55:2:1] - **90%** of the world's people live on about **10%** of its land; most live in the **Northern Hemisphere mid-latitudes and the tropics of Asia**.
[55:2:2] - Crowded regions: **East Asia, South and South-east Asia, Europe, north-eastern North America**; two great clusters = **East Asia** and **South Asia**; the **Ganges–Brahmaputra delta** is among the densest areas on Earth.
[55:2:3] - Sparse: high latitudes, tropical deserts, high mountains, equatorial forests.
[55:2:4] # Factors
[55:2:5] TABLE head: Factor | Effect | Example
  [55:2:5.0] Water | River valleys densest | Nile valley
  [55:2:5.1] Landform | Plains favoured, mountains sparse | Ganga plains
  [55:2:5.2] Climate | Moderate climates attract; hot and cold deserts repel | Sahara, polar lands
  [55:2:5.3] Soil | Fertile loamy alluvium supports dense farming | Java, eastern China
  [55:2:5.4] Minerals | Mining draws workers | Katanga–Zambia copper belt
  [55:2:5.5] Industry, urbanisation | Jobs concentrate people | Kobe–Osaka
  [55:2:5.6] Social-cultural | Religious centres attract; unrest repels | Varanasi, Vatican
[55:2:6] # The odd one out
[55:2:7] - **North-eastern USA** (New York, Philadelphia, Boston, Pittsburgh) is dense because of **industry and urbanisation** – it does **not depend directly on agriculture**, unlike the lower Nile valley, Java and the eastern plains of China (MPPSC 2021).

[55:2:9] - **China** has the world's **largest Buddhist population** (about half of all Buddhists); Thailand is second, then Myanmar and Japan.

=== NOTES 4:56 | World Population Growth, Demographic Transition and Regional Patterns
## SUB 0 | Growth Terms – Natural Increase, RNI and Demographic Gap
[56:0:0] # Terms
[56:0:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [56:0:1.0] Natural increase | Births − deaths (crude birth rate − crude death rate); migration and marriage are not part of it
  [56:0:1.1] Demographic gap | The difference between the birth rate and the death rate (widest in stage 2 of the transition)
  [56:0:1.2] Actual growth | Natural increase + net migration (in-migrants − out-migrants)
  [56:0:1.3] Positive growth | Births exceed deaths, or permanent net in-migration
  [56:0:1.4] Negative growth | Births below deaths, or out-migration
  [56:0:1.5] Over-urbanisation | Urban growth faster than urban jobs and services (slums)
[56:0:2] - **CBR** = live births per 1,000 people a year; **CDR** = deaths per 1,000 a year.
[56:0:3] # Rate of natural increase (RNI)
[56:0:4] - **RNI (%) = (CBR − CDR) / 10** when both are per 1,000.
[56:0:5] - **CBR 44, CDR 11:** 44 − 11 = 33 per 1,000 = **3.3%** a year (not 33% or 0.33%).
[56:0:6] - CBR 30, CDR 10 → **2.0%**; CBR 25, CDR 7 → **1.8%**.

=== NOTES 4:59 | Demographic Dividend, Ageing and Population Policy
## SUB 0 | Demographic Dividend – Meaning, Age Group and Window
[59:0:0] # Definition
[59:0:1] - **Demographic dividend** = the **rise in economic growth due to a rise in the share of working-age people** in the population – when the **working-age population outnumbers the non-working (dependent) population** (APPSC Group II 2017 key).
[59:0:2] - Not female work participation, not a rise in child population, not "skilled workers" alone.
[59:0:3] TABLE head: Source | Working-age group
  [59:0:3.0] UNFPA / UN / ILO | 15–64
  [59:0:3.1] Indian usage (Census, PMF, APPSC keys) | 15–59 – India's workforce age group
[59:0:4] - **Channels:** labour supply, savings and investment, lower dependency ratio, human capital – a rising share of **dependants hurts** it.
[59:0:5] # India's numbers
[59:0:6] - Over **62%** aged 15–59 and more than 54% below 25; about **65% below 35** (2011).
[59:0:7] - In **2020** the average Indian was to be only **29 years** old (China and USA 37, West Europe 45, Japan 48) – the youngest major population. Median age **28** (SRS 2024), **40 by 2051**.
[59:0:8] TABLE head: Estimate | Window / peak
  [59:0:8.0] UNFPA | Longest in the world: 2005-06 to 2055-56; opens at different times in different States
  [59:0:8.1] NITI Aayog | Dividend lasts to about 2055
  [59:0:8.2] ES 2018-19 | Peak about 2041 (59% aged 20–59)
  [59:0:8.3] ES 2025-26 | Peak about 2030
  [59:0:8.4] MoHFW (Mar 2026), 15–59 | Peak 1,009.0 mn in 2041; closes after 2041
[59:0:9] - **RBI:** **Kerala and Tamil Nadu** have crossed the demographic turning point; Bihar and UP have a wider window.
[59:0:10] # Terms
[59:0:11] - "**Demographic nightmare**" (ES 2018-19): the dividend lost without **health, education, skills and jobs**.
[59:0:12] - "**Demographic winter**": fertility below 2.1, inverted pyramid, persistent decline. "**Shrinconomics**": AP Health Minister, 1 Apr 2026.

## SUB 3 | Causes, Laws and Schemes
[60:3:0] # Causes
[60:3:1] - Accepted causes: **high maternal mortality, high female child mortality, female foeticide, more male births**. **Male** child mortality is naturally **higher** – so "lower male than female mortality" is not a cause.
[60:3:2] - Roots: **son preference, patriarchy**, neglect of girls' health, intra-household discrimination, male migration.
[60:3:3] - MMR: **254 (2004-06) → 88 (2021-23)**.
[60:3:4] - After curbs on prenatal sex selection, firstborn girls show **25% higher child mortality** than firstborn boys.
[60:3:5] - **Infant Mortality Rate:** deaths before the **1st birthday** per 1,000 live births; **child (under-5) mortality** = deaths before the **5th birthday**.
[60:3:6] - British India: literacy **below 16%**, **female** (not male) literacy about **7%**, IMR about **218**, life expectancy about **32 years** (older NCERT, the APPSC answer; the revised NCERT text in OnlyIAS gives **44 years**).
[60:3:7] - Census in British India: first (non-synchronous) Census **1872**; first synchronous Census **1881**.
[60:3:8] # Laws and schemes
[60:3:9] TABLE head: Measure | Year | Aim
  [60:3:9.0] Cradle Baby Scheme (Tamil Nadu) | 1992 | Against female infanticide
  [60:3:9.1] PCPNDT Act | 1994 | Bans sex selection; prenatal tests only for genetic/chromosomal abnormalities
  [60:3:9.2] Beti Bachao Beti Padhao | 22 Jan 2015, Panipat (Haryana) | Reverse falling CSR; multi-sectoral action in gender-critical districts
[60:3:10] - BBBP does **not** allow prenatal sex determination.
[60:3:11] - **Supreme Court (Jun 2026):** CSR shows signs of improvement but gender bias is **deep-seated** (son preference, patriarchy).
