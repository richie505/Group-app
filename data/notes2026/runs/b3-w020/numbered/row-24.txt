## SUB 1 | Who Compiles National Income – CSO to NSO (MoSPI)
[1:0] - National income was computed by the **Central Statistical Organisation (CSO)** – the key in every older PYQ; not the NSSO (surveys), ISI, NCAER, Indian Standards Institution, RBI, Finance Ministry or Planning Commission.
[1:1] - **2019**: CSO + NSSO merged into the **National Statistical Office (NSO)** under **MoSPI**, headed by the **Chief Statistician of India** (also Secretary, MoSPI) – NSO now releases GDP, GNI, NNI and per capita income (UP RO/ARO 2023).
[1:2] - **CSO** grew out of the Central Statistical Unit (1949) and was set up on **2 May 1951**; National Accounts Division = GDP, consumption, capital formation; **Economic Statistics Division (ESD) compiled the IIP**; Industrial Statistics Wing, Kolkata conducts the **ASI**.
[1:3] - **P.C. Mahalanobis** = **architect of independent India's statistical system** – first Statistical Adviser to the Cabinet (Jan 1949), founder of the **ISI (1931)** and the **NSS (1950)**; P.V. Sukhatme built agricultural statistics.

## SUB 10 | Statistics – Averages, Dispersion, Probability and Distributions
[10:0] # Central tendency
[10:1] - **Mean, median and mode** = **measures of central tendency** (averages).
[10:2] - **Mode** = most frequent value – **located by inspection** and easily understood by common people.
[10:3] - **Median** = the value that **divides an ordered series into two equal parts**.
[10:4] - **Empirical relation (moderately skewed data, Karl Pearson):** **Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean** ⇔ **Mean − Mode = 3 (Mean − Median)** ⇔ **Mode − Mean = 3 (Median − Mean)**.
[10:5] - For positive values (not all equal): **AM > GM > HM** (equal only when all values are equal).
[10:6] # Dispersion
[10:7] - **Standard deviation** = positive **square root of the variance**; for homogeneous data (all values equal) **SD = 0**.
[10:8] - **Histogram** = adjacent bars showing frequencies of values within **consecutive, fixed class intervals**.
[10:9] # Probability and distributions
[10:10] - **Independent events:** P(both miss) = (1 − 0.6) × (1 − 0.8) = **0.08**; an **impossible event** (six girls from a class with only five) has probability **zero**.
[10:11] - If the population is **normal**, the sampling distribution drawn from it is also **normal**.
[10:12] - **Chi-square distribution:** **asymmetric – positively (right) skewed**, nearly symmetric only for large degrees of freedom; used for **goodness-of-fit** tests (does a population follow a given theoretical distribution?).
[10:13] ~~ Sources: NCERT Class XI Statistics for Economics ch.4-6 (presentation, central tendency, dispersion); NCERT Class XII Mathematics ch.13 (probability); S.C. Gupta, Fundamentals of Statistics (empirical relation, chi-square)
## SUB 11 | Statistics – Sampling, Hypothesis Testing, Correlation and Regression
[11:0] # Sampling
[11:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [11:1.0] Population / universe | The whole group from which sample units are drawn
  [11:1.1] Simple random sampling | Every unit has an equal chance of selection; suits a homogeneous population
  [11:1.2] Stratified sampling | A heterogeneous population is divided into homogeneous strata (classes) and a sample is drawn from each
  [11:1.3] Sampling distribution | Distribution of a sample statistic over all possible samples
[11:2] # Hypothesis testing
[11:3] - **Hypothesis** = an assumption or proposition whose validity is to be tested.
[11:4] - **Type I error** = rejecting a true hypothesis; **Type II error** = **accepting a false hypothesis**.
[11:5] # Correlation and regression
[11:6] - **Correlation coefficient (Karl Pearson's r)** lies between **−1 and +1**.
[11:7] - **Multiple regression** has one intercept and **more than one partial regression coefficient**.
[11:8] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [11:8.0] Multicollinearity | Significant correlation between the independent variables
  [11:8.1] Autocorrelation | Correlation of errors over time – detected by the Durbin-Watson (DW) statistic
  [11:8.2] Adjusted R² | R² adjusted for degrees of freedom
  [11:8.3] ARIMA | Auto Regressive Integrated Moving Average – time-series forecasting model
