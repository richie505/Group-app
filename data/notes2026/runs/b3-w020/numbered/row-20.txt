## SUB 0 | Meaning and Goals of Sustainable Development
[0:0] - **Definition (Brundtland, 1987):** development that **meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs**.
[0:1] - It is a case of **inter-generational** sensibility in the use of **natural resources**; present use with **minimum degradation** of natural resources = sustainable development; "**saving energy and other resources for the future without sacrificing people's comfort in the present**"; **balancing the need to use resources and conserve them for the future**.
[0:2] - **Main goal:** **balanced growth of environment and economy** – not growth only or protection only. Base of sustainable development = **environmental approach**.
[0:3] - It matters for the **well-being of human society** because of the definition itself (A-R: both true, R explains A).
[0:4] - Intertwined with **carrying capacity** (UPSC 2010) – Earth's finite capacity.
[0:5] - **Three pillars / components:** **economic growth, social inclusion, environmental protection** – not "scientific association", political or military.
[0:6] TABLE head: Is an objective | NOT an objective
  [0:6.0] Meet present needs while keeping choices for the future | Prioritise individual wealth accumulation
  [0:6.1] Balance economy, society and environment | Profit maximisation, growth at any cost
  [0:6.2] Intergenerational equity | Unrestricted resource exploitation
[0:7] - **Green Agenda** = **concern for environment and for the future generations**.
[0:8] - Mid-1980s debate on the **negative aspects of development**: degradation of natural resources, pollution, **displacement and rehabilitation** – not "politics and development".
[0:9] - Major challenge to SDG 1 (no poverty): **economic inequality and unequal distribution of resources**.
[0:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 03 p.53 (definition); PMF Environment 06 p.6 (Brundtland definition); NCERT Indian Economic Development Class 11 ch.9; app notes 3:20 sub 0, 3
## SUB 1 | Milestones – Club of Rome to Rio+20
[1:0] TABLE head: Year | Milestone | Key fact
  [1:0.0] 1968 (Apr) | Club of Rome founded, Rome | First advocated resource conservation in a systematic way at the international level (NCERT)
  [1:0.1] 1972 | "The Limits to Growth" (Club of Rome) | A book on computer simulation of growth with finite resource supplies
  [1:0.2] 1972 | Stockholm – UN Conference on the Human Environment | Created UNEP
  [1:0.3] 1980 | World Conservation Strategy (IUCN with UNEP and WWF) | Term "sustainable development" first introduced
  [1:0.4] 1983 | WCED set up by UNGA | Chair Gro Harlem Brundtland (Prime Minister of Norway)
  [1:0.5] 1987 | "Our Common Future" – Brundtland Report | Defined and popularised sustainable development (first clear modern explanation)
  [1:0.6] 1992 | UNCED, Rio (Earth Summit) | Rio Declaration, Agenda 21 (signed 1992, not 1995), UNFCCC, CBD
  [1:0.7] 2002 | Johannesburg – World Summit on SD (Rio+10) | Johannesburg Declaration
  [1:0.8] 2012 | Rio+20 = UN Conference on Sustainable Development | "The Future We Want"; SDGs born
  [1:0.9] 2015 | Agenda 2030, 25 Sep 2015 | 17 SDGs
[1:1] - The Brundtland Report is **not** "The Limits to Growth"; the term was defined by the **Brundtland Commission**, not WHO, UNESCO, UNICEF or UNDP.
[1:2] - **2017** = UN International Year of **Sustainable Tourism for Development**.
[1:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 06 p.6-8 (Brundtland, Rio, Rio+20); Rocket Sheets Environment (Agenda 21, Rio+20); NCERT Contemporary India II ch.1 (Club of Rome 1968); IUCN World Conservation Strategy 1980; app notes 3:20 sub 1-2
## SUB 2 | MDGs, Agenda 2030 and the 17 SDGs
[2:0] - **MDGs:** **8** goals from the **Millennium Summit, September 2000**, New York; period 2000-2015.
[2:1] - **SDGs:** **17** goals, **169** targets, **231** unique indicators; adopted **25 Sep 2015** by the UN; 2016-2030.
[2:2] TABLE head: Goal | Theme
  [2:2.0] 1 | No poverty
  [2:2.1] 2 | Zero hunger
  [2:2.2] 4 | Quality education – free and compulsory education, school infrastructure and digital transformation
  [2:2.3] 5 | Gender equality
  [2:2.4] 6 | Clean water and sanitation – water availability and its sustainable management
  [2:2.5] 7 | Affordable and clean energy
  [2:2.6] 9 | Industry, innovation and infrastructure
  [2:2.7] 10 | Reduced inequalities
  [2:2.8] 11 | Sustainable cities and communities
  [2:2.9] 13 | Climate action – urgent action to combat climate change
  [2:2.10] 14 | Life below water
  [2:2.11] 15 | Life on land – terrestrial ecosystems
[2:3] - Agenda 2030 emphasises **ending all discrimination and violence against women and girls**; principles: universal, leave no one behind, integrated and indivisible.
[2:4] - **UN Secretary-General** presents an **annual SDG Progress Report** (global indicator framework); the **Global Sustainable Development Report** is produced **every four years** for the quadrennial review – not every quarter.
[2:5] - **National Indicator Framework** for SDGs is developed by **MoSPI**, not NITI Aayog; **Mission LiFE** (**Lifestyle for Environment**, 2021) aims to change individual demand, industry supply and **government and industrial policy** for sustainable consumption and production; SDG 4 "global citizenship" is education for global citizenship – not flexible citizenship rights.
[2:6] - **Roots of Agenda 2030:** the **MDGs** + the **Rio+20** outcome; grounded in the **UDHR**, human rights treaties, the Millennium Declaration and the 2005 World Summit Outcome; informed by the **Declaration on the Right to Development**. **Three** of the 8 MDGs were health goals (4, 5, 6) – merged into **SDG 3**.
[2:7] - **India's success is critical** for the global SDGs because India has nearly **one-sixth** of the world population (A-R: R explains A).
[2:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 06 p.8-9 (MDGs, SDGs); Rocket Sheets Environment (SDG numbers); UN Agenda 2030 (sdgs.un.org) and UN SDG Report / GSDR (un.org); MoSPI National Indicator Framework; PIB Mission LiFE; app notes 3:20 sub 6-7

## SUB 5 | Related Concepts and Rio Principles
[5:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [5:0.0] Carrying capacity | Maximum population an environment can support indefinitely without degradation
  [5:0.1] Earth Overshoot Day | Date when humanity's yearly demand exceeds what Earth regenerates; Global Footprint Network
  [5:0.2] Intergenerational equity | Fairness between present and future generations
[5:1] - **Rio 1992 endorsed:** **precautionary principle** (lack of full scientific certainty is no reason to postpone action), **polluter pays**, **CBDR** (developed countries carry more – historical emissions, greater capacity). **Not endorsed:** unrestricted resource exploitation.
[5:2] ~~ Sources: Rio Declaration 1992 (un.org); Global Footprint Network; app notes 3:20 sub 4-5
