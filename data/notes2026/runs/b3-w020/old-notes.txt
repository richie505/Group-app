=== NOTES 3:20 | Sustainable Development – Definition, Brundtland Report, Sdgs and India's Progress
## SUB 0 | Meaning and Goals of Sustainable Development
[20:0:0] - **Definition (Brundtland, 1987):** development that **meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs**.
[20:0:1] - It is a case of **inter-generational** sensibility in the use of **natural resources**; present use with **minimum degradation** of natural resources = sustainable development; "**saving energy and other resources for the future without sacrificing people's comfort in the present**"; **balancing the need to use resources and conserve them for the future**.
[20:0:2] - **Main goal:** **balanced growth of environment and economy** – not growth only or protection only. Base of sustainable development = **environmental approach**.
[20:0:3] - It matters for the **well-being of human society** because of the definition itself (A-R: both true, R explains A).
[20:0:4] - Intertwined with **carrying capacity** (UPSC 2010) – Earth's finite capacity.
[20:0:5] - **Three pillars / components:** **economic growth, social inclusion, environmental protection** – not "scientific association", political or military.
[20:0:6] TABLE head: Is an objective | NOT an objective
  [20:0:6.0] Meet present needs while keeping choices for the future | Prioritise individual wealth accumulation
  [20:0:6.1] Balance economy, society and environment | Profit maximisation, growth at any cost
  [20:0:6.2] Intergenerational equity | Unrestricted resource exploitation
[20:0:7] - **Green Agenda** = **concern for environment and for the future generations**.
[20:0:8] - Mid-1980s debate on the **negative aspects of development**: degradation of natural resources, pollution, **displacement and rehabilitation** – not "politics and development".
[20:0:9] - Major challenge to SDG 1 (no poverty): **economic inequality and unequal distribution of resources**.
[20:0:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 03 p.53 (definition); PMF Environment 06 p.6 (Brundtland definition); NCERT Indian Economic Development Class 11 ch.9; app notes 3:20 sub 0, 3
[20:0:11] - **Economic sustainability** = creating wealth that **improves living standards** over time without exhausting resources.
## SUB 1 | MDGs, Agenda 2030 and the 17 SDGs
[20:1:0] - **MDGs:** **8** goals from the **Millennium Summit, September 2000**, New York; period 2000-2015.
[20:1:1] - **SDGs:** **17** goals, **169** targets, **231** unique indicators; adopted **25 Sep 2015** by the UN; 2016-2030.
[20:1:2] TABLE head: Goal | Theme
  [20:1:2.0] 1 | No poverty
  [20:1:2.1] 2 | Zero hunger
  [20:1:2.2] 4 | Quality education – free and compulsory education, school infrastructure and digital transformation
  [20:1:2.3] 5 | Gender equality
  [20:1:2.4] 6 | Clean water and sanitation – water availability and its sustainable management
  [20:1:2.5] 7 | Affordable and clean energy
  [20:1:2.6] 9 | Industry, innovation and infrastructure
  [20:1:2.7] 10 | Reduced inequalities
  [20:1:2.8] 11 | Sustainable cities and communities
  [20:1:2.9] 13 | Climate action – urgent action to combat climate change
  [20:1:2.10] 14 | Life below water
  [20:1:2.11] 15 | Life on land – terrestrial ecosystems
[20:1:3] - Agenda 2030 emphasises **ending all discrimination and violence against women and girls**; principles: universal, leave no one behind, integrated and indivisible.
[20:1:4] - **UN Secretary-General** presents an **annual SDG Progress Report** (global indicator framework); the **Global Sustainable Development Report** is produced **every four years** for the quadrennial review – not every quarter.
[20:1:5] - **National Indicator Framework** for SDGs is developed by **MoSPI**, not NITI Aayog; **Mission LiFE** (**Lifestyle for Environment**, 2021) aims to change individual demand, industry supply and **government and industrial policy** for sustainable consumption and production; SDG 4 "global citizenship" is education for global citizenship – not flexible citizenship rights.
[20:1:6] - **Roots of Agenda 2030:** the **MDGs** + the **Rio+20** outcome; grounded in the **UDHR**, human rights treaties, the Millennium Declaration and the 2005 World Summit Outcome; informed by the **Declaration on the Right to Development**. **Three** of the 8 MDGs were health goals (4, 5, 6) – merged into **SDG 3**.
[20:1:7] - **India's success is critical** for the global SDGs because India has nearly **one-sixth** of the world population (A-R: R explains A).
[20:1:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 06 p.8-9 (MDGs, SDGs); Rocket Sheets Environment (SDG numbers); UN Agenda 2030 (sdgs.un.org) and UN SDG Report / GSDR (un.org); MoSPI National Indicator Framework; PIB Mission LiFE; app notes 3:20 sub 6-7
[20:1:9] - **SDG 3** = Good health and well-being – **ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages**.
## SUB 2 | Milestones – Club of Rome to Rio+20
[20:2:0] TABLE head: Year | Milestone | Key fact
  [20:2:0.0] 1968 (Apr) | Club of Rome founded, Rome | First advocated resource conservation in a systematic way at the international level (NCERT)
  [20:2:0.1] 1972 | "The Limits to Growth" (Club of Rome) | A book on computer simulation of growth with finite resource supplies
  [20:2:0.2] 1972 | Stockholm – UN Conference on the Human Environment | Created UNEP
  [20:2:0.3] 1980 | World Conservation Strategy (IUCN with UNEP and WWF) | Term "sustainable development" first introduced
  [20:2:0.4] 1983 | WCED set up by UNGA | Chair Gro Harlem Brundtland (Prime Minister of Norway)
  [20:2:0.5] 1987 | "Our Common Future" – Brundtland Report | Defined and popularised sustainable development (first clear modern explanation)
  [20:2:0.6] 1992 | UNCED, Rio (Earth Summit) | Rio Declaration, Agenda 21 (signed 1992, not 1995), UNFCCC, CBD
  [20:2:0.7] 2002 | Johannesburg – World Summit on SD (Rio+10) | Johannesburg Declaration
  [20:2:0.8] 2012 | Rio+20 = UN Conference on Sustainable Development | "The Future We Want"; SDGs born
  [20:2:0.9] 2015 | Agenda 2030, 25 Sep 2015 | 17 SDGs
[20:2:1] - The Brundtland Report is **not** "The Limits to Growth"; the term was defined by the **Brundtland Commission**, not WHO, UNESCO, UNICEF or UNDP.
[20:2:2] - **2017** = UN International Year of **Sustainable Tourism for Development**.

## SUB 5 | Related Concepts and Rio Principles
[20:5:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [20:5:0.0] Carrying capacity | Maximum population an environment can support indefinitely without degradation
  [20:5:0.1] Earth Overshoot Day | Date when humanity's yearly demand exceeds what Earth regenerates; Global Footprint Network
  [20:5:0.2] Intergenerational equity | Fairness between present and future generations
[20:5:1] - **Rio 1992 endorsed:** **precautionary principle** (lack of full scientific certainty is no reason to postpone action), **polluter pays**, **CBDR** (developed countries carry more – historical emissions, greater capacity). **Not endorsed:** unrestricted resource exploitation.
[20:5:2] ~~ Sources: Rio Declaration 1992 (un.org); Global Footprint Network; app notes 3:20 sub 4-5

## SUB 0 | National Income – Meaning, NNP at Factor Cost and Formulas
[22:0:0] # Meaning
[22:0:1] - **National income (NI):** the **money value of all final goods and services** produced by the **normal residents** of a country during a year (UPSC 2013; G2 Prelims 2019) – only **final** goods, so no double counting.
[22:0:2]   - Distractors: "total value of goods and services produced by the nationals" (no "final"), "consumption + investment", "sum of personal incomes".

## SUB 4 | GDP and GNP – Definitions and NFIA
[22:4:0] - **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** = value of **all final goods and services** produced **within the domestic territory** of a country during a year, whoever produces them (Endowment; Sachivalayam; AEE 2008).
[22:4:1]   - "Goods" alone or without "final" is incomplete; GDP = Q × P.
[22:4:2] - **GNP (Gross National Product)** = value of final goods and services produced by the **normal residents** and their property, **inside or outside** the country, in a year = **Gross National Income** (CDS 2014).
[22:4:3] TABLE head: Basis | GDP | GNP
  [22:4:3.0] Based on | Domestic territory (location) | Normal residents (nationality)
  [22:4:3.1] Indians earning abroad | Excluded | Included
  [22:4:3.2] Link | GDP = GNP − NFIA | GNP = GDP + NFIA
[22:4:4] - **Main difference** GDP vs GNP = **net factor income from abroad** (G2 PYQ) – not transfer payments or depreciation.
[22:4:5] - **NFIA** = factor income **received from** abroad − factor income **paid to** abroad; GNP = GDP + X − M (X = income of nationals abroad, M = income of foreigners at home).
[22:4:6] - **GDP = GNP is possible** when NFIA = 0 (e.g. a closed economy) (SSC 2009).
[22:4:7] - **India:** NFIA usually **negative** → GNP **below** GDP. An Indian resident's income earned in Saudi Arabia counts in India's **GNP only**.

## SUB 10 | GDP Deflator and GNP Deflator
[22:10:0] - **GDP deflator = nominal GDP ÷ real GDP × 100** = ratio of GDP at **current prices to constant prices** (G2 Mains 2025) – not real ÷ nominal.
[22:10:1] - **GNP deflator** = nominal GNP ÷ real GNP, as a percentage (G2 PYQ).
[22:10:2] - **Most comprehensive measure of inflation:** covers **all goods and services in GDP**; WPI, CPI use fixed baskets.
[22:10:3] - A rising deflator → higher prices → **purchasing power of money falls**.
[22:10:4] - **Double deflation** (2022-23 series): output and input prices deflated separately for manufacturing and agriculture.

## SUB 5 | Product (Value Added) Method and Double Counting
[23:5:0] - **Value added = value of output − intermediate consumption**; summing value added at every stage = value of the final product.
[23:5:1] - **Double counting:** adding sales at every stage (cotton farmer → weaver → tailor) counts the cotton again and again. Remedy: count **only final goods** or the **value added** at each stage; **intermediate goods are excluded**. Both routes give the same total.
[23:5:2] - **Gross value added − depreciation = net value added**.
[23:5:3] - **Sum of net value added at factor cost** of all sectors within the domestic territory = **Net Domestic Product at factor cost (domestic income)**; + NFIA = NNP at FC = national income.
[23:5:4] - **Change in inventories = production − sales**; treated as investment.
[23:5:5] - **Double deflation** (2022-23 series): **real GVA = real output − real intermediate consumption**, output and inputs deflated separately.
[23:5:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 12 p.4-5 (value added = production − intermediate goods; GVA − depreciation = NVA); OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 03 p.69 (value added, GVA/NVA, inventories); NIOS Economics 318 L25 (sum of NVA at FC = NDP at FC); app notes 3:23 sub 1

## SUB 1 | Statistics – Averages, Dispersion, Probability and Distributions
[24:1:0] # Central tendency
[24:1:1] - **Mean, median and mode** = **measures of central tendency** (averages).
[24:1:2] - **Mode** = most frequent value – **located by inspection** and easily understood by common people.
[24:1:3] - **Median** = the value that **divides an ordered series into two equal parts**.
[24:1:4] - **Empirical relation (moderately skewed data, Karl Pearson):** **Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean** ⇔ **Mean − Mode = 3 (Mean − Median)** ⇔ **Mode − Mean = 3 (Median − Mean)**.
[24:1:5] - For positive values (not all equal): **AM > GM > HM** (equal only when all values are equal).
[24:1:6] # Dispersion
[24:1:7] - **Standard deviation** = positive **square root of the variance**; for homogeneous data (all values equal) **SD = 0**.
[24:1:8] - **Histogram** = adjacent bars showing frequencies of values within **consecutive, fixed class intervals**.
[24:1:9] # Probability and distributions
[24:1:10] - **Independent events:** P(both miss) = (1 − 0.6) × (1 − 0.8) = **0.08**; an **impossible event** (six girls from a class with only five) has probability **zero**.
[24:1:11] - If the population is **normal**, the sampling distribution drawn from it is also **normal**.
[24:1:12] - **Chi-square distribution:** **asymmetric – positively (right) skewed**, nearly symmetric only for large degrees of freedom; used for **goodness-of-fit** tests (does a population follow a given theoretical distribution?).

## SUB 8 | Growth Concepts – Hindu Rate, Miracle Economies, Middle-income Trap
[25:8:0] - **Hindu rate of growth:** about **3.5%** a year in **1950-80**; term coined by **Raj Krishna**.
[25:8:1] - **Miracle economies:** GDP growth of **7%** for a **decade or more** (NextGen) – not 8, 8.5 or 7.5%.
[25:8:2] - **Middle-income trap:** countries that rose from low to middle income but stagnated there; causes = **poor infrastructure, weak institutions, unfavourable growth conditions** – **climate change is not** one of the listed causes.
[25:8:3] - **GDP does not measure income distribution**; dollar rankings move with exchange rates and base revisions.
[25:8:4] - **World Bank income groups:** low, lower-middle, upper-middle, high; WDR 1994 low income = per capita GNP **US $725 or less**.
