## SUB 0 | Classification of Resources and Conservation Milestones
[30:0:0] # Bases of Classification
[30:0:1] TABLE head: Basis | Types | Examples
  [30:0:1.0] Origin | Biotic (living) vs Abiotic (non-living) | Biotic: plants, animals, micro-organisms; Abiotic: land, water, minerals, rocks, metals
  [30:0:1.1] Exhaustibility | Renewable vs Non-renewable | Renewable: forests, fish, water, wildlife; Non-renewable: fossil fuels, minerals (exhausted with use)
  [30:0:1.2] Status of development | Potential; Developed; Stock; Reserve | Stock: hydrogen from water
[30:0:2] - **Stock** = resource exists but the **technology to use it is lacking** (e.g. hydrogen from water).
[30:0:3] - **Reserve** = the part (subset) of stock usable with **present technology**, kept for future use.
[30:0:4] Stock = technology lacking; Reserve = usable now and a part of stock. Do not swap them.
[30:0:5] # Resource Depletion
[30:0:6] - **Overuse** of natural resources leads to resource depletion (APPSC 2025).

[30:1:14] # Wasteland and Land Degradation
[30:1:15] - **Wasteland** = degraded (damaged) land that can be brought under vegetative cover with **reasonable effort**.
[30:1:16] - Mapped by the satellite-based **National Wastelands Monitoring**, using IRS remote-sensing data (IRS commissioned 1988).
[30:1:17] - Land degradation causes: **soil erosion, waterlogging, salinisation, nutrient depletion, falling water tables, soil pollution** – mostly human-induced.
[30:1:18] "Natural increase of nutrients" is NOT a cause of land degradation.
[30:1:19] # Watershed Management
[30:1:20] - **IWMP** (Integrated Watershed Management Programme): CSS from **2009 (2009-10)**, run by the **Department of Land Resources** (Ministry of Rural Development).
[30:1:21] - IWMP **merged DPAP, DDP and IWDP**; till **1 April 2008** the Ministry of Rural Development ran IWDP, DPAP and DDP.
[30:1:22] - IWMP objective: enhance **agricultural productivity and water conservation** on degraded and rainfed land.
[30:1:23] - Merged into **PMKSY in 2015** – now the **Watershed Development Component (WDC-PMKSY)**; WDC-PMKSY 2.0 for degraded and rain-fed areas.
[30:1:24] - Integrated watershed benefits: prevents **soil runoff**, rainwater harvesting, **groundwater recharge**, regeneration of vegetation – NOT linking perennial and seasonal rivers.
[30:1:25] - **Command Area Development and Water Management** is a PMKSY component, **not** part of IWMP.
[30:1:26] - **Neeranchal**: World Bank-assisted national watershed project.
[30:1:27] - **Watershed Development Fund**: set up by **NABARD**.
[30:1:28] DPAP, DDP, IWDP were implemented by the Department of Land Resources; pairing them with the Agriculture or Environment ministry is wrong.
## SUB 2 | Forest Resources
[30:2:0] # Terms
[30:2:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [30:2:1.0] Silviculture | Art and science of growing and tending forest crops (regeneration, thinning, rotation)
  [30:2:1.1] Agroforestry | Trees grown with crops or livestock on the same land
  [30:2:1.2] Shifting cultivation (jhum) | NE hill slopes (e.g. Arunachal); shorter fallow cycles degrade forests and raise soil erosion
[30:2:2] - India's first agroforestry policy: **National Agroforestry Policy 2014**.
[30:2:3] - **Largest forest area** among states: **Madhya Pradesh** (77,073 sq km, ISFR 2023).
[30:2:4] # National Forest Policy 1988
[30:2:5] - Target: **33%** of the country under forest; **two-thirds** in the **hills**.
[30:2:6] - Formally introduced **Joint Forest Management (JFM)**.
[30:2:7] APPSC 2024: "At least two-thirds of the country under forest" is NOT in NFP 1988 – two-thirds is only for hill areas.
[30:2:8] # Joint Forest Management and Forest Rights
[30:2:9] - JFM: Centre's (MoEF) guidelines **1990** – village committees share forest protection and usufruct.
[30:2:10] - Forerunner: **Arabari (West Bengal, 1972)**.
[30:2:11] - **Forest Rights Act 2006**: individual and community titles to STs and other traditional forest dwellers; **Gram Sabhas** manage, protect and regenerate community forest resources.

[30:2:14] # World Forest Types
[30:2:15] - **Amazon Basin**: largest continuous tropical rainforest; **Congo Basin** second.
[30:2:16] - Dense forests gather near the **equator**; rainforest soil after clearance is **nutrient-poor**, regeneration slow.
[30:2:17] - **Taiga** (boreal coniferous forest): **largest terrestrial biome** – Russia, Scandinavia, Canada; NOT in Amazonia.
[30:2:18] - **Bhutan**: Constitution keeps at least **60%** of area under forest (about **70%** now).
[30:2:19] - **Canada**: largest producer and exporter of **softwood** timber and wood pulp.

[30:3:9] - **Andhra Pradesh ranks first** in tank irrigation (about **28–29%** of India's tank-irrigated area); districts Nellore and Warangal.
[30:3:10] - **Tamil Nadu**: over **23%**; tanks are called **Eris**.
