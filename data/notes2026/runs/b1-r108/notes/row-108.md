=== Firoz Shah Tughlaq – Canals, Irrigation Tax, Towns and Books
- **Biggest network of canals among the Delhi Sultans:** **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** (UPSC 1998 ×2) – not Iltutmish, Ghiyasuddin or Sikandar Lodi.
- **Haqq-i-Sharb (irrigation tax):** first levied by **Firoz** (UPPSC 2010 ×2), at **one-tenth** of the produce on fields watered by royal canals.
- **State-promoted canal irrigation "initiated by":** books and keys give **Firoz Tughlaq** (APPSC G1); strictly **Ghiyasuddin** was the first Sultan to dig canals, Firoz built the most.
| Canal | Fact |
|---|---|
| Yamuna to Hisar | about **150 miles**, the longest in the old notes (PMF: Sutlej–Hansi, about 200 km) |
| Sutlej to Ghaggar | 96 miles |
| Sirmur hills to Hansi | irrigation canal |
| Ghaggar to Firozabad; Yamuna to Firozabad | irrigation |
| Rajabwah | restored under Akbar as the **Shihab (Shahab) Canal** |
- **150 wells** for irrigation and travellers.
# Towns
- **Firozabad** (Firoz Shah Kotla), **Fatehabad** (named after his son **Fateh Khan**; SSC 2019), **Hisar Firoza**, **Firozpur**, **Jaunpur**.
- **Jaunpur:** founded by Firoz **in memory of Muhammad bin Tughlaq (Jauna Khan)** (UPPSC 2004).
# Pillars and books
- **Ashokan pillars** brought to Delhi from **Topra (Ambala/Khizrabad)** and **Meerut**.
- Autobiography: **Futuhat-i-Firoz Shahi**.
- **Nagarkot (Jawalamukhi) campaign:** about **1,300 Sanskrit manuscripts** (some books: 300 volumes); translated into Persian by **Azizuddin Khalid Khani (Azizuddin Khan)** as **Dalail-i-Firoz Shahi** (UPPSC 2023) – not Talib Amuli, Abdul Baqi or Mirza Muhammad Ali.

=== Muhammad bin Tughlaq – Experiments and Coins
| Experiment | Year | Cause / aim | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Doab taxation | c. 1326 | More revenue (land tax raised to **one-half**) | Coincided with **famine**; **peasant revolts and flight** |
| Transfer of capital **Delhi → Devagiri (Daulatabad)** | **1327** | Central base to control the South | Hardship; court returned to Delhi |
| **Token currency** of copper/bronze (brass) | **1329-30** | **Shortage of silver**; Kublai Khan's paper money as model | **Mass forgery**; withdrawn, redeemed in silver/gold at heavy loss |
| Khurasan expedition | 1332 | Central Asian conquest | Army (3,70,000) **disbanded unpaid** |
| Qarachil (Kumaon hills) | 1333 | Secure the **hill frontier** | Army **destroyed** in the hills |
- **Capital shifted to Deogiri / Daulatabad:** **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (BC Welfare; UPPCS 2002) – not Ghiyasuddin, Firoz or Nasiruddin; Daulatabad is not a Firoz town.
- **First token currency in India:** **MBT** (UPPCS 2004) – not Akbar, Alauddin or Bahlul Lodi.
- **Failure of token currency:** the state had **no control over minting** – forgery everywhere (UPPCS 2006: A and R true, R explains A).
- **Dinar (A–R, UPSC 2006 ×2):** MBT issued a new **gold coin called Dinar** by Ibn Battuta ✓; the token coins were **copper/bronze, not gold**, and not for West Asian trade ✗ → **A true, R false**.
- **Coins (statements):** gold **Dinar** ✓ and silver **Adli** ✓ → **(b) and (c)**; **Adha and Bikh** (copper-silver alloy) were **Firoz's** small coins; the copper **Jital** goes back to **Iltutmish**.
- **MBT (Juna / Jauna Khan, Ulugh Khan):** became Sultan in **1325**, not 1300 (G1 2003 NOT-true); suspected of arranging his father's death (pavilion collapse); took **Orugallu (Warangal) 1323**, renamed **Sultanpur**.
- Called a "mixture of opposites" (Barani) and "a saint with the heart of a devil".
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.65-66; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.43-44; Rocket Indian History 01 (Delhi → Daulatabad → Delhi); app notes 1:108 (sub 2)

=== Muhammad bin Tughlaq – Agriculture Department and Agrarian Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi (Diwan-i-Kohi)** | MBT's **department of agriculture**: waste land under cultivation, better crops (**crop rotation**), direct aid; a trial block of 30 × 30 karohs; 70 lakh tankas spent, failed through corruption |
| **Taqavi (sondhar)** loans | State loans for **seeds and wells**; written off by Firoz |

- **It dealt with:** **agriculture** (UPPSC Mains 2017) – not army, revenue or entertainment.
- **End:** MBT died at **Thatta (1351)** chasing the rebel **Taghi** after a Gujarat revolt.

=== Ibn Battuta and the Historians on Muhammad bin Tughlaq
- **Nationality:** **Moroccan ("Moorish")**, born **Tangiers**, 1304-1377 (APPSC G1) – not Egyptian, Nigerian or Ethiopian.
- **Came under:** **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (CGPSC 2013; UPPSC 2011) – arrived **1333**, in India to 1342 (left Delhi for **China as envoy, 1342**).
- **Post:** MBT made him **Qazi (chief judge) of Delhi** (CDS 2005) – not Balban, Alauddin or Ghiyasuddin.
- **Rihla (Kitab-ul-Rihla), in Arabic, completed 1355:** CDS 2013 key = **"an account of the Delhi Sultans from Aibak to Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq"** (its India part retells Sultanate history); it is a travelogue – not a religious text or a Morocco trade account.
- Rihla also notes the **postal system** and that **sati needed the Sultan's permission**; Ibn Battuta also described **Harihara I's** Vijayanagara.
| Historian | Remark |
|---|---|
| **Badauni** | On MBT's death: "**the king was freed from his people and they from their king**" (UPSC 1999; MPPSC 1997) – not Balban, Alauddin or Firoz |
| **Barani** | MBT a "**mixture of opposites**"; wrote **Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi** and **Fatawa-i-Jahandari** |
~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.45-46; Rocket Indian History 01 (Rihla – Ibn Battuta); examrobot CDS 2013 key; app notes 1:108 (sub 4)

=== Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (1320-25)
- **Ghazi Malik:** son of a Turkish slave and a Jat woman; **Governor of Punjab** under Alauddin against the Mongols; killed **Khusrau Shah** and became Sultan in **1320**.
- **Revenue:** increase limited to **one-tenth (or one-eleventh)**, raised only gradually (UPPSC 2011) – not Balban, Alauddin or Firoz; remissions in drought; waste land farmed; **first Sultan to dig canals**.
- **Khalisa assessment:** dropped Alauddin's **measurement** for **sharing of the crop (batai / hukm-i-hasil)** (RPSC 2008; printed key "Kankut" ✗ – kankut is estimation).
- **Warangal:** Juna Khan's first attack (1321-22) failed; fell after a **five-month siege (1323)**, renamed **Sultanpur**; **Prataparudra II** died in the **Narmada** on the way to Delhi.
- Built **Tughlaqabad**; died **1325** when a **wooden pavilion collapsed at Afghanpur**; patron of **Amir Khusrau**.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.64-65; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.43; Moreland, Agrarian System of Moslem India (digi.econbiz.de); app notes 1:108 (sub 1)

=== Firoz Shah Tughlaq – Accession, Revenue, Jizya, Iqta
- **Accession:** son of Ghiyasuddin's brother **Rajab** (MBT's **cousin**, not uncle); placed on the throne at **Thatta (1351)**; ruled till **1388**.
- **Wazir Khan-i-Jahan Maqbul** (a Telugu Brahmin convert) paid **compensation** to MBT's victims.
- **Revenue:** wrote off taqavi loans; **fixed** land revenue; abolished about **24 taxes**; kept the **four Islamic taxes** – **kharaj, jizya, khams, zakat** (haq-i-sharb is extra, his irrigation cess).
- **Jizya:** made a **separate tax** and extended to **Brahmins** (ulema's advice).
- **Iqtas:** revived and made **hereditary**.
- **Campaigns:** two failed **Bengal** expeditions against **Haji Iliyas** (second 1359-60); **Jajnagar** and **Nagarkot** taken; **Jam Babaniya** of Sind agreed to pay tribute.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.66-68; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.47-48; app notes 1:108 (sub 6)

| Sultan | Distinctive measure |
|---|---|
| Iltutmish | Iqta, silver tanka, copper jital, Chahalgani |

- **IAS 2002 (correct statement):** "**Firuz Tughlaq set up a separate department of slaves**" ✓; Ariz department first = **Balban**, not Alauddin; horse branding = **Alauddin**, not Balban; MBT was succeeded by his **cousin**, not uncle.

=== Successors and the End of the Tughlaqs
- **Weak successors after 1388:** Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq II, **Abu Bakr**, Nasiruddin Muhammad, then rival Sultans **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah** (Delhi) and **Nusrat Shah** (Firozabad).
- **Last Tughlaq ruler:** **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah** (1394-1412) (IAS 2004) – not Firoz, Ghiyasuddin II or Nusrat Shah; in his reign **Timur** invaded (**1398**) and **Khwaja Jahan** founded **Jaunpur** (Sharqi).
- Tughlaq period **1320-1414** (longest Sultanate dynasty, about 94 years); **Khizr Khan** took Delhi **1414**.
