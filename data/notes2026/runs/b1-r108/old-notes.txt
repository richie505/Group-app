=== NOTES 1:108 | Tughlaq Dynasty – Ghiyasuddin, Muhammad Bin Tughlaq's Experiments, Firoz Shah's Reforms
## SUB 0 | Tughlaq Dynasty at A Glance

  [108:0:0.0] Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq | 1320-25
  [108:0:0.1] Muhammad bin Tughlaq (Juna Khan / Ulugh Khan) | 1325-51
  [108:0:0.2] Firoz Shah Tughlaq | 1351-88
  [108:0:0.3] Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah (last) | to 1412
[108:0:1] - Tughlaq period: **1320-1414** (Ghiyasuddin to **Khizr Khan** taking Delhi).
## SUB 1 | Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (1320-25)
[108:1:0] - Earlier name **Ghazi Malik**; son of a Turkish slave and a Jat woman; **Governor of Punjab** under Alauddin against the Mongols.
[108:1:1] - Killed **Khusrau Shah** and became Sultan in **1320**.
[108:1:2] - Revenue rise limited to about **one-tenth**, raised slowly; drought relief; waste land farmed; **first Sultan to dig canals**.
[108:1:3] - Sequence: Sultan (1320) → Juna Khan attacked Warangal (**1321-22**) → Warangal fell after a **five-month siege (1323)**, renamed **Sultanpur** → died at Afghanpur (1325).
[108:1:4] - **Prataparudra II** was taken towards Delhi and died in the **Narmada**.
[108:1:5] - Built **Tughlaqabad**. Died **1325** when a **wooden pavilion collapsed at Afghanpur**; Ibn Battuta suspected a plot by Juna Khan.
## SUB 2 | Muhammad Bin Tughlaq's Five Experiments
[108:2:0] TABLE head: Experiment | Cause / aim | Result
  [108:2:0.0] Transfer of capital Delhi → Devagiri (Daulatabad), 1327 | Central base to control the Deccan | Hardship; court returned to Delhi
  [108:2:0.1] Token currency of copper and brass (c. 1329-30) | Shortage of silver | Mass forgery; coins withdrawn and redeemed at heavy cost
  [108:2:0.2] Taxation in the Doab | More revenue | Coincided with famine; peasant revolts and flight
  [108:2:0.3] Khurasan expedition | Central Asian conquest | Army disbanded unpaid
  [108:2:0.4] Qarachil (Kumaon hills) | Secure the hill frontier | Army destroyed in the hills
[108:2:1] - MBT raised land revenue to **one-half** of produce; called an "unlucky idealist".
[108:2:2] - **First to introduce token currency** in India = MBT (Barani: lack of silver). It failed because the state had **no proper control over issue** of coins.
[108:2:3] - Token coins were copper/bronze, **not gold**, not meant for West Asian trade. New gold coin = **Dinar** (Ibn Battuta); silver coin = **Adli**.
[108:2:4] Daulatabad is MBT's renamed capital, not a Firoz town.
## SUB 3 | Mbt's Agrarian Measures and Terms
[108:3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [108:3:0.0] Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi (Diwan-i-Kohi) | MBT's agriculture department: waste land under cultivation, better crops, direct aid; trial land of 30 × 30 karohs
  [108:3:0.1] Taqavi (sondhar) | State loans for seeds and wells (MBT); written off by Firoz
  [108:3:0.2] Swarga-dwari | Famine-relief and cultivation camp on the Ganga near Kannauj during the Doab famine
  [108:3:0.3] Uluq / Dawa | Horse couriers every four miles / foot couriers, three stations to every mile
[108:3:1] - MBT died at **Thatta (1351)** on the way to Sindh, chasing the rebel **Taghi** after a Gujarat revolt.
## SUB 4 | Ibn Battuta and Historians on Mbt
[108:4:0] - **Moroccan ("Moorish")** traveller (**1304-1377**), born **Tangiers**.
[108:4:1] - In India **1333-1342** under MBT; made **Qazi of Delhi**; sent as **envoy to China (1342)**.
[108:4:2] - Work: **Rihla (Kitab-ul-Rihla)** in **Arabic**: Sultanate and Deccan; detailed account of the **postal system**; recorded that **sati** needed the Sultan's permission.
[108:4:3] - Called MBT eccentric, hot-tempered, generous. **First foreign traveller to Vijayanagara**, under **Harihara I**.
[108:4:4] TABLE head: Historian | Remark
  [108:4:4.0] Barani | MBT a "mixture of opposites"; wrote Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi and Fatawa-i-Jahandari
  [108:4:4.1] Badauni | On MBT's death: "the king was freed from his people and they from their king"
## SUB 5 | Revolts and New States in the South
[108:5:0] TABLE head: Revolt / state | Year | Leader
  [108:5:0.0] Coastal Andhra freed (Reddis) | c. 1325 | Prolaya Vema Reddi
  [108:5:0.1] Madurai Sultanate | 1335 | Jalaluddin Ahsan Shah
  [108:5:0.2] Warangal recaptured (Musunuri) | 1336 | Kapaya Nayaka (after Prolaya Nayaka)
  [108:5:0.3] Vijayanagara | 1336 | Harihara and Bukka (sent by MBT), guided by Vidyaranya
  [108:5:0.4] Bahmani kingdom | 1347 | Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah (Hasan Gangu)
[108:5:1] - **AP angle:** after 1323 Andhra was under **Malik Burhanuddin** of Devagiri, helped by **Malik Maqbul** at Warangal. Maqbul = captured Brahmin **Jagannatha Pandit**, later Firoz's wazir.
[108:5:2] - **Vilasa copper plate** (Prolaya Nayaka) and **Madhura Vijayam** (**Gangadevi**) describe Tughlaq rule.
[108:5:3] - Bahmani later split into **five Deccan sultanates**.
[108:5:4] 1336 = Vijayanagara founded AND Warangal recaptured by Kapaya Nayaka.
## SUB 6 | Firoz Shah Tughlaq – Revenue, Jizya, Iqta
[108:6:0] - Placed on the throne at **Thatta (1351)**; ruled till **1388**.
[108:6:1] - Wazir **Khan-i-Jahan Maqbul** compensated MBT's victims.
[108:6:2] - Wrote off taqavi loans; **fixed land revenue**; abolished about **24 taxes**; kept the **four Islamic taxes** (kharaj, jizya, khams, zakat).
[108:6:3] - Made **jizya a separate tax**, extended to **Brahmins for the first time** (ulema's advice).
[108:6:4] - Restored iqtas and made them **hereditary**.
[108:6:5] - Two failed **Bengal** expeditions against **Haji Iliyas** (second 1359-60); **Jam Babaniya** of Sind agreed to pay tribute.
## SUB 7 | Firoz Shah – Departments, Canals, Towns
[108:7:0] TABLE head: Department | Function
  [108:7:0.0] Diwan-i-Khairat | Charity: orphans, widows, marriages of poor Muslim girls
  [108:7:0.1] Diwan-i-Bandagan | Slaves (about 1,80,000)
  [108:7:0.2] Dar-ul-Shifa (Dar-ul-Shafa) | Free hospital
  [108:7:0.3] Diwan-i-Istihqaq | Pensions and grants
  [108:7:0.4] Public Works Department | Canals, towns, buildings
[108:7:1] # Canals
[108:7:2] - **Largest number of canals** among Delhi Sultans; began state-backed canal irrigation. Irrigation cess **haq-i-sharb = one-tenth** of produce.
[108:7:3] TABLE head: Canal | Fact
  [108:7:3.0] Yamuna to Hisar | 150 miles, the longest
  [108:7:3.1] Sutlej to Ghaggar | 96 miles
  [108:7:3.2] Sirmur hills to Hansi | Irrigation canal
  [108:7:3.3] Ghaggar to Firozabad; Yamuna to Firozabad | Irrigation
  [108:7:3.4] Rajabwah | Restored in Akbar's time as the Shihab (Shahab) Canal
[108:7:4] # Towns, Pillars, Books
[108:7:5] - Towns: **Firozabad** (Firoz Shah Kotla), **Fatehabad**, **Hisar Firoza**, **Firozpur**, **Jaunpur**; fruit gardens.
[108:7:6] - **Ashokan pillars** brought to Delhi from **Topra (Ambala)** and **Meerut**.
[108:7:7] - Autobiography: **Futuhat-i-Firoz Shahi**.
[108:7:8] - **Nagarkot** campaign: about **1,300 Sanskrit manuscripts** from the **Jawalamukhi** temple; some translated into Persian by **Azizuddin Khalid Khani** as **Dalail-i-Firoz Shahi**.

  [108:8:0.4] Firoz | Canals, public works, Diwan-i-Khairat, jizya on Brahmins
[108:8:1] - **Dahsala** = Todar Mal; **Akbarnama, Ain-i-Akbari** = Abul Fazl.
[108:8:2] - **Ain-ul-Mulk Multani** served Alauddin, MBT and Firoz (not Iltutmish); won **Malwa** for Alauddin (1305).

## SUB 9 | Successors and End

[108:9:1] - **Timur** invaded **1398** under **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah**, the last Tughlaq; **Khizr Khan** took Delhi **1414**.

[109:2:1] - Sultan on the throne: **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah Tughlaq**, the last Tughlaq (d. 1412).
[109:2:2] - Effects: Tughlaq state broke into regional kingdoms (**Jaunpur, Malwa, Gujarat**); Timur's nominee **Khizr Khan** later founded the Sayyid dynasty.
[109:2:3] The Mughal Empire was not an effect of Timur; it was founded by Babur in 1526.

[109:3:1] - Ruled as Timur's **deputy, without the title of Sultan**.

## SUB 4 | Lodi Dynasty (1451-1526)
[109:4:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Reign | Key points
  [109:4:0.0] Bahlol (Bahlul) Lodi | 1451-89 | Founder; Governor of Punjab; throne from Alam Shah; conquered Jaunpur; ruled longest

  [109:4:0.2] Ibrahim Lodi | 1517-26 | Arrogant to nobles; killed at Panipat
[109:4:1] - Order: Bahlol → Sikandar → Ibrahim (son follows father each time).
[109:4:2] - Lodi kingship: Sultan as "**first among equals**" among Afghan nobles.
[109:4:3] # Sikandar Lodi
[109:4:4] - **Nizam Khan**, third son of Bahlol; crowned **17 July 1489**.
[109:4:5] - Founded **Agra (1504)**; shifted the capital there from Delhi.
[109:4:6] - **Gaz-i-Sikandari** (Gaj-i-Sikandari): yard to **measure land**.
[109:4:7] - Tax remissions: abolished the **tax on grains**, several taxes and trade restrictions; used **audits and spies**; punished embezzlement by nobles.
[109:4:8] - Rule from **Punjab to Bihar**; took Jaunpur; defeated **Bengal** rulers and **Rajput** chiefs.
[109:4:9] - Wrote **Persian poetry** under the pen name **Gulrukhi**.
[109:4:10] - Destroyed **Jwalamukhi (Nagarkot)** images and **Mathura** temples, imposed **jizya**; yet had a **Sanskrit medical work translated into Persian**.
[109:4:11] - Built **Moth ki Masjid** and royal tombs of the **Lodi Gardens**.
[109:4:12] # Ibrahim Lodi
[109:4:13] - Nobles plotted to enthrone his brother **Jalal Khan**.
[109:4:14] - Seized **Gwalior** (which had sheltered Jalal Khan); **Vikramajit** of Gwalior made a vassal.
[109:4:15] - **Khatauli (1517)**: defeated by **Rana Sanga of Mewar**.
[109:4:16] - Contemporaries: **Krishnadevaraya**, **Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk** (Golconda, independent 1518), **Rana Sanga** (not Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq).

## SUB 6 | First Battle of Panipat, 1526
[109:6:0] - Date: **21 April 1526**; Babur (about **12,000**) beat Ibrahim (about **1,00,000**) by **skilled warfare** and **skilled commandants** despite a smaller army.

[109:6:6] - Babur captured **Delhi and Agra** and the Lodi treasure.
[109:6:7] - **End of the Delhi Sultanate**, start of the **Mughal Empire** (founded by Babur; consolidated by Akbar; Sher Shah = Sur dynasty).
