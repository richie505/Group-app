[0:1] - **Iron content:** **magnetite** highest (about 70–72%; "superior grade") > **haematite** (60–70%) > limonite (40–60%) > **siderite** (lowest). **Haematite** = most widely used and India's **largest reserve** type.
[0:2] - **Magnetite:** black ore, up to ~70% iron, magnetic – all three statements true.
[0:3] - **Not an ore:** **pig iron** (a smelted product); bauxite, malachite and zinc blende are ores.
[0:4] - **Native (free) state:** **gold** (best-known), silver, platinum, copper – a single type of atom = **native elements**. **Never free:** K, Na, Ca, Mg, **Al** (top of the reactivity series).
[0:5] - **Alloys:** **German silver** = copper + zinc + nickel (Cu 60, Ni 20, Zn 20) – **contains no silver**; **brass** = copper + zinc; bronze = copper + tin. Horn silver, ruby silver and lunar caustic all contain silver.
[0:6] - **Ferrous minerals** (contain iron): iron ore, manganese, chromite, nickel, cobalt (NCERT). **Non-ferrous:** copper, bauxite (aluminium), lead, zinc, tin (NCERT); precious: gold, silver, platinum.
[0:7] - **⚠ Tungsten:** exam keys treat it as **non-ferrous**, but NextGen lists it among ferrous (ferro-alloy) minerals with cobalt and nickel.
[0:8] - **Non-ferrous group:** **nickel, zinc, copper, aluminium** (not iron ore, pig iron or steel).
[0:9] - **Non-metallic:** **marble**, **gypsum**, mica, limestone, salt, potash, graphite (not haematite, bauxite, limonite, manganese, silver, lead).
[0:10] TABLE head: Deposit type | How formed | Minerals
  [0:10.0] Placer | Heavy minerals in alluvial / beach sands | Gold, tin, platinum
  [0:10.1] Veins / lodes | Cracks in igneous and metamorphic rocks | Tin, copper, zinc, lead
  [0:10.2] Sedimentary beds | Layers | Coal, gypsum, salt, potash
  [0:10.3] Residual weathering | Decomposed rock (laterite) | Bauxite
[0:11] - **Kimberlite** pipes (ultrabasic) = host rock of **diamond**.
[0:12] - **Talc:** softest mineral; a magnesium silicate (softness comes from its weakly bonded layers, not from being Mg silicate).
[0:13] - **Plaster of Paris** is made by heating **gypsum** (CaSO₄·½H₂O, written 2CaSO₄·H₂O); anhydrite = CaSO₄.

## SUB 3 | Non-metallic Minerals – Mica, Gypsum, Marble and Others
[3:0] # Mica
[3:1] - **Occurrence:** pegmatite veins in Archaean/Dharwar granite, gneiss, schist; varieties **muscovite** (white), **phlogopite** (amber/Mg) and biotite – **phlogopite and pegmatite → mica**.
[3:2] - **Use:** an **insulator** in electrical and electronic industries; India historically monopolised **sheet mica** (led world output when asked in 1992, 2001).
[3:3] - **Belts:** **Koderma–Gaya–Hazaribagh–Munger** ("Capital of Mica", ruby mica, Jharkhand–Bihar) – the largest belt; **Nellore** (AP); **Ajmer–Bhilwara** (Rajasthan).
[3:4] - **Reserves:** **AP 41%**, **Rajasthan 28%**, Odisha 17%, Maharashtra 13% (2015) – Rajasthan, AP, Bihar and **Jharkhand** are the classic reserve States.
[3:5] - **Producers:** AP long the **largest producer** (Nellore); IMYB 2020-21: **Rajasthan 17.7 thousand t, AP 16.7 thousand t**. Jharkhand, Rajasthan, AP produce mica; **Madhya Pradesh does not**.
