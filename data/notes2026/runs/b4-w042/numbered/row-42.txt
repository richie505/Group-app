## SUB 0 | Tiger Reserves – Project Tiger, NTCA and Reserve → State
[0:0] # Project Tiger and NTCA
[0:1] - **Project Tiger** launched **1 April 1973** with **9 reserves**; formally launched at **Corbett** (Uttarakhand) – **India's first tiger reserve**.
[0:2] - **NTCA** (National Tiger Conservation Authority): **statutory body** set up in **2006** under the amended **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972**; chaired by the Environment Minister.
[0:3] - **Count:** **58 tiger reserves** (as of Sep 2026; 47 a decade ago): 56th **Guru Ghasidas–Tamor Pingla** (Chhattisgarh, Nov 2024), 57th **Ratapani** (MP, Dec 2024), 58th **Madhav** (MP, Mar 2025). **Rajaji** (Uttarakhand) became the **48th** in 2015, then **Orang** (Assam) the 49th.
[0:4] - **Most tiger reserves:** **Madhya Pradesh (9)**. **Largest:** **Nagarjunasagar–Srisailam (NSTR, Andhra Pradesh)**, then Manas, then Guru Ghasidas–Tamor Pingla. NSTR is AP's only tiger reserve; its Telangana part is **Amrabad TR**.
[0:5] - **M-STrIPES**: app-based patrolling and monitoring tool used in tiger reserves.
[0:6] # Tiger numbers
[0:7] - **All India Tiger Estimation 2022:** average **3,682** (upper limit 3,925); **over 70% of the world's wild tigers**; 2006 baseline 1,411. A growing share live **outside** notified reserves → more human–tiger conflict.
[0:8] - **Most tigers by State:** **Madhya Pradesh 785** (2022) > Karnataka 563 > Uttarakhand 560 > Maharashtra 444. In the **2014** estimate **Karnataka (406)** led.

## SUB 2 | Ramsar Convention, Montreux Record and Wetland Programmes
[2:0] - **Ramsar Convention (Convention on Wetlands):** adopted **2 Feb 1971** at **Ramsar, Iran**; in force 1975; **India joined 1 Feb 1982**; Secretariat **Gland, Switzerland**. Aim: **conservation and wise use of wetlands** – not oceans, deserts, arid lands, hills or ozone.
[2:1] - **World Wetlands Day: 2 February**.
[2:2] - Other days (distractors): **22 March World Water Day**; **22 April Earth Day**; 5 June World Environment Day.
[2:3] - **Wetland definition:** marsh, fen, peatland or water; **natural or artificial**; fresh, brackish or salt; includes **marine water up to 6 m deep at low tide**.
[2:4] - **Montreux Record:** register of Ramsar sites where **changes in ecological character have occurred, are occurring or are likely to occur** because of technology, pollution or other human interference – not a WHS status, not a 5-km ban.
[2:5]   - India's entries: **Keoladeo** (1990) and **Loktak** (1993). **Chilika** was added in 1993 and **removed in 2002**.
[2:6] - **Supreme Court:** no mining within **10 km** of a Ramsar site without approval of **NBWL** and **MoEFCC**.
[2:7] - **National Wetland Conservation Programme (NWCP, 1985-86):** **115 wetlands** identified for urgent conservation; merged with NLCP into **NPCA** (2013).

## SUB 3 | Migratory Birds and Flyways
[3:0] TABLE head: Bird | Migration fact
  [3:0.0] Bar-headed goose | Breeds on high Central Asian lakes; crosses the Himalaya – world's highest-flying bird
  [3:0.1] Amur falcon | Longest raptor migration; SE Siberia/N China → stopover in Nagaland / NE India (Doyang, Pangti; Oct–Nov) → winters in southern Africa
  [3:0.2] Siberian crane | Winter visitor from Siberia; its favourite Indian wintering site was Keoladeo NP, Bharatpur (no longer comes)
  [3:0.3] Demoiselle crane | From Mongolia/Central Asia to Rajasthan (Khichan) and Gujarat
  [3:0.4] Arctic tern | Longest migration of any bird – Arctic to Antarctic every year
  [3:0.5] Black-necked crane (*Grus nigricollis*) | Breeds in Ladakh; Ladakh's State bird (2021)
[3:1] - Common raven = *Corvus corax*; golden eagle = *Aquila chrysaetos* (*Aquila clanga* is the greater spotted eagle).
[3:2] - **Chilika Lake** (Odisha) is the **largest wintering ground for migratory waterfowl** in the Indian subcontinent.
[3:3] - **Central Asian Flyway**: Arctic/Siberia → Indian Ocean; India hosted **CMS COP13** at **Gandhinagar (2020)**.
[3:4] - **National bird of India:** **Indian peafowl (peacock)**, *Pavo cristatus* (1963).

## SUB 6 | Elephant Reserves and Elephant Census
[6:0] - **Project Elephant:** launched **February 1992** (centrally sponsored); **33 elephant reserves in 14 States**; ERs **overlap** tiger reserves, sanctuaries and reserved forests.
[6:1] - **Most ERs: Assam and Tamil Nadu (5 each)**; Odisha 3 (Mayurbhanj – Similipal, Mahanadi, Sambalpur).
[6:2] - **Largest elephant population: Karnataka** – 6,000+ (2017) and **6,013** in the DNA-based **SAIEE 2021-25** (Oct 2025), then Assam 4,159, Tamil Nadu 3,136, Kerala 2,785. **Kerala is not first.**
[6:3] - India: **22,446** wild elephants (2025; 27,312 in 2017) – about 60% of the world's Asian elephants.
[6:4] - **Andhra Pradesh:** 1 ER (**Rayala**, incl. Koundinya WLS), 1 TR (NSTR), 1 BR (**Seshachalam**, 2010), Ramsar: Kolleru only.
