## SUB 0 | Tiger Reserves – Project Tiger, NTCA and Reserve → State
[42:0:0] # Project Tiger and NTCA
[42:0:1] - **Project Tiger** launched **1 April 1973** with **9 reserves**; formally launched at **Corbett** (Uttarakhand) – **India's first tiger reserve**.
[42:0:2] - **NTCA** (National Tiger Conservation Authority): **statutory body** set up in **2006** under the amended **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972**; chaired by the Environment Minister.
[42:0:3] - **Count:** **58 tiger reserves** (as of Sep 2026; 47 a decade ago): 56th **Guru Ghasidas–Tamor Pingla** (Chhattisgarh, Nov 2024), 57th **Ratapani** (MP, Dec 2024), 58th **Madhav** (MP, Mar 2025). **Rajaji** (Uttarakhand) became the **48th** in 2015, then **Orang** (Assam) the 49th.
[42:0:4] - **Most tiger reserves:** **Madhya Pradesh (9)**. **Largest:** **Nagarjunasagar–Srisailam (NSTR, Andhra Pradesh)**, then Manas, then Guru Ghasidas–Tamor Pingla. NSTR is AP's only tiger reserve; its Telangana part is **Amrabad TR**.
[42:0:5] - **M-STrIPES**: app-based patrolling and monitoring tool used in tiger reserves.
[42:0:6] # Tiger numbers
[42:0:7] - **All India Tiger Estimation 2022:** average **3,682** (upper limit 3,925); **over 70% of the world's wild tigers**; 2006 baseline 1,411. A growing share live **outside** notified reserves → more human–tiger conflict.
[42:0:8] - **Most tigers by State:** **Madhya Pradesh 785** (2022) > Karnataka 563 > Uttarakhand 560 > Maharashtra 444. In the **2014** estimate **Karnataka (406)** led.

## SUB 2 | Ramsar Convention, Montreux Record and Wetland Programmes
[42:2:0] - **Ramsar Convention (Convention on Wetlands):** adopted **2 Feb 1971** at **Ramsar, Iran**; in force 1975; **India joined 1 Feb 1982**; Secretariat **Gland, Switzerland**. Aim: **conservation and wise use of wetlands** – not oceans, deserts, arid lands, hills or ozone.
[42:2:1] - **World Wetlands Day: 2 February**.
[42:2:2] - Other days (distractors): **22 March World Water Day**; **22 April Earth Day**; 5 June World Environment Day.
[42:2:3] - **Wetland definition:** marsh, fen, peatland or water; **natural or artificial**; fresh, brackish or salt; includes **marine water up to 6 m deep at low tide**.
[42:2:4] - **Montreux Record:** register of Ramsar sites where **changes in ecological character have occurred, are occurring or are likely to occur** because of technology, pollution or other human interference – not a WHS status, not a 5-km ban.
[42:2:5]   - India's entries: **Keoladeo** (1990) and **Loktak** (1993). **Chilika** was added in 1993 and **removed in 2002**.
[42:2:6] - **Supreme Court:** no mining within **10 km** of a Ramsar site without approval of **NBWL** and **MoEFCC**.
[42:2:7] - **National Wetland Conservation Programme (NWCP, 1985-86):** **115 wetlands** identified for urgent conservation; merged with NLCP into **NPCA** (2013).

## SUB 6 | Elephant Reserves and Elephant Census
[42:6:0] - **Project Elephant:** launched **February 1992** (centrally sponsored); **33 elephant reserves in 14 States**; ERs **overlap** tiger reserves, sanctuaries and reserved forests.
[42:6:1] - **Most ERs: Assam and Tamil Nadu (5 each)**; Odisha 3 (Mayurbhanj – Similipal, Mahanadi, Sambalpur).
[42:6:2] - **Largest elephant population: Karnataka** – 6,000+ (2017) and **6,013** in the DNA-based **SAIEE 2021-25** (Oct 2025), then Assam 4,159, Tamil Nadu 3,136, Kerala 2,785. **Kerala is not first.**
[42:6:3] - India: **22,446** wild elephants (2025; 27,312 in 2017) – about 60% of the world's Asian elephants.
[42:6:4] - **Andhra Pradesh:** 1 ER (**Rayala**, incl. Koundinya WLS), 1 TR (NSTR), 1 BR (**Seshachalam**, 2010), Ramsar: Kolleru only.

=== NOTES 4:43 | India – Soil Types, Characteristics and Distribution; Soil Erosion and Conservation
## SUB 0 | Alluvial Soil, Khadar–Bhangar and the Soil-Type Ranking
[43:0:0] # Ranking by area
[43:0:1] TABLE head: Soil | Area | Note
  [43:0:1.0] Alluvial | 15 lakh sq km, 45.6% (NCERT: ~40%) | Largest / most widespread and most productive group
  [43:0:1.1] Black (regur) | 5.46–5.47 lakh sq km, 16.6% | Deccan Plateau
  [43:0:1.2] Red (and yellow) | 5.18 lakh sq km (NextGen); 3.5 lakh sq km, 10.6% (PMF) | Often called the "second largest" group – see the dispute below
  [43:0:1.3] Laterite | 2.48 lakh sq km | Ghats summits
  [43:0:1.4] Forest / mountain | 2.85 lakh sq km, 8.67% | Himalaya, Ghats
  [43:0:1.5] Arid / desert | 1.42 lakh sq km, ~4.3% | W Rajasthan
[43:0:2] - **Eight** major soil types (ICAR): alluvial, black, red and yellow, laterite, arid, saline and alkaline, peaty and marshy, forest and mountain.
[43:0:3] - **Residual (in situ)** soils dominate **Peninsular India**; **drifted (ex situ)** soils dominate **North India**.
[43:0:4] # Alluvial soil
[43:0:5] - **Deposited by rivers** (transported/azonal); Indo-Gangetic–Brahmaputra plains, river valleys, **deltas** of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery, Narmada–Tapi valleys, coasts.
[43:0:6] - **Sand content decreases from west to east**; becomes more loamy and clayey towards the Brahmaputra valley.
[43:0:7] - NCERT: **rich in potash but poor in phosphorus**; poor in **nitrogen** and humus (PMF: potash, phosphate and lime adequate) – so alluvial soils are **deficient in nitrogen**.
[43:0:8] - Most fertile / **most productive** soil; needs the **least chemical fertiliser**; crops rice, wheat, sugarcane, jute, cotton, oilseeds.
[43:0:9] - **Very little alluvium:** **Madhya Pradesh** (Deccan Trap black soil) – not Bihar, Punjab or Tamil Nadu (deltas).
[43:0:10] - **Depth:** average **1,000–2,000 m** in the plains, maximum **about 6,100 m** – exams answer "about 6,000 m".
[43:0:11] - **Alluvial sand** has the **minimum water retention**; **clay** retains the most (tiny pores).
[43:0:12] # Khadar, Bhangar, Bhabar, Tarai
[43:0:13] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [43:0:13.0] Khadar | New alluvium, renewed by flood silt every year; lighter, sandy; fertile, ideal for intensive agriculture
  [43:0:13.1] Bhangar | Old alluvium on higher ground beyond flood reach; kankar (calcareous concretions); "old Kachhari clay"; pale reddish brown
  [43:0:13.2] Bhabar | Coarse, pebbly piedmont belt below the Shiwaliks; streams disappear
  [43:0:13.3] Tarai | Marshy lowland south of Bhabar; now largely reclaimed
[43:0:14] - Khadar and Bhangar are both **alluvial** soils – not regur, kankar or Bhabar.
[43:0:15] - **⚠ Trap:** "Khadar contains kankar" is **false** – kankar belongs to Bhangar (NCERT notes kankar can occur in both, but the exam reason is false).
[43:0:16] # Karewa soils
[43:0:17] - **Lacustrine (old lake-bed) deposits** of glacial clay and moraines on plateaus above the Jhelum in the **Kashmir Himalayas** (and Bhadarwah).
[43:0:18] - Famous for **saffron (zafran)** at Pampore, also almonds and walnuts.
[43:0:19] > Dispute: by area in PMF and NextGen, black soil (5.46–5.47 lakh sq km) is larger than red soil, but most books and keys call red (and yellow) soil the second-largest group.
[43:0:20] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 08 p.3-4 (book p.101-102); OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Geography 06 p.3 (book p.68); PMF Indian Physical Geography 03 p.82-84, p.86; PMF Indian Physical Geography 01 p.73 (alluvium depth); Rocket Indian Geography p.56-58; app notes 4:43 (sub 1, 2)
[43:0:21] - **Sundarbans** (Ganga–Brahmaputra delta): saline **deltaic alluvial** soil (with peaty patches) – not red, laterite or black soil.
## SUB 1 | Soil Conservation – Methods and Practices
[43:1:0] # Meaning
[43:1:1] - **Soil conservation:** protecting soil from harm – **controlling erosion and keeping fertility high** (NCERT); it prevents **soil erosion**, not fertility or farming.
[43:1:2] - **Best single method to prevent erosion:** **afforestation**; deforestation, overgrazing and heavy rain cause erosion.
[43:1:3] # Methods
[43:1:4] TABLE head: Method | How it works / where
  [43:1:4.0] Contour ploughing / contour bunding | Ploughing and bunds along contour lines on hilly, sloping, rainfed land slow runoff – reduces erosion by water flow (not up-and-down ploughing)
  [43:1:4.1] Terrace cultivation | Steps on steep slopes and mountain areas – the best check on steep slopes; a conservation measure, not a cause of erosion
  [43:1:4.2] Shelter belts | Rows of trees that cut wind speed and protect soil (arid W India, coasts); CAZRI dune fixing in Rajasthan
  [43:1:4.3] Check dams, gully plugging | Trap sediment and stop gully growth
  [43:1:4.4] Strip cropping | Alternate erosion-resistant strips (grass) with row-crop strips
  [43:1:4.5] Mulching | Cover of residue/straw on the soil – retains soil moisture, reduces erosion
  [43:1:4.6] Crop rotation | Different crops in sequence on the same land – increases soil fertility, cuts pests, prevents degradation; an example of sustainable agriculture
  [43:1:4.7] Afforestation, regulated forestry, controlled grazing, cover cropping, mixed farming | NCERT remedial measures
[43:1:5] - **Government-promoted measures to prevent erosion:** **contour bunding and afforestation** (SI 2017).
[43:1:6] - **Soil fertility is improved by** crop rotation, mixed cropping, multiple cropping, manuring, green manuring – **seed modification does not** improve fertility.
[43:1:7] - **Contour bunding is NOT for** desert margins (wind), low flat flood plains or weedy scrublands – so "none of the above" (UPSC 2013).
[43:1:8] # Conservation agriculture (FAO)
[43:1:9] - Three principles: **minimum / zero tillage**, **permanent soil cover with crop residues**, **crop rotation / diversification** – so 2, 4 and 5 (UPSC 2018); "avoiding monoculture" and "avoiding plantation crops" are not its defined components.
[43:1:10] - **Zero tillage:** new crop sown into the previous crop's residue without seed-bed preparation – raises soil organic matter, cuts runoff; of contour bunding, relay cropping and zero tillage, **only zero tillage** is counted as storing carbon in the soil (UPSC 2012).
[43:1:11] - **Relay cropping:** next crop sown into the standing crop just before its harvest (e.g. paddy–lucerne).
[43:1:12] - Other: agroforestry, biochar (raises water holding, cuts soil N2O), China's **straw checkerboards** (stabilise dunes).
[43:1:13] - **Kurnool (Jul 2026):** Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing with **Jeevamrutham** to conserve soil moisture (target 92,000 acres).
[43:1:14] # Schemes
[43:1:15] - **Soil Health Card:** launched **19 Feb 2015** at **Suratgarh, Rajasthan**; slogan "**Swasth Dharaa, Khet Haraa**"; card **every two years**; **12 parameters** – N, P, K, S, Zn, Fe, Cu, Mn, B, pH, EC, organic carbon (micronutrients: **Zn, Fe, Cu, Mn, B**); **25.79 crore** cards by Feb 2026, most in **Uttar Pradesh**.
[43:1:16] - Most Indian soils are deficient in **nitrogen, phosphorus and humus** – the reason for soil testing.
[43:1:17] - **IWMP** (2009-10) merged in **2015-16** into the Watershed Development Component of **PMKSY**.
[43:1:18] > Problem → remedy pairs: deforestation → afforestation; soil erosion → soil stabilisation; habitat destruction → restoration.

## SUB 2 | Black (Regur) Soil
[43:2:0] # Formation and properties
[43:2:1] - Formed by weathering of **Deccan Trap basalt** – **fissure volcanic rock** (lava); in Tamil Nadu from gneiss and schist. Also called **regur**, **black cotton soil**, tropical chernozem; Krebs called regur essentially a mature soil.
[43:2:2] - **Clay above 62%**: **highest moisture retention** – **needs little irrigation**, suits dry farming.
[43:2:3] - **Sticky when wet**, shrinks with **deep cracks** when dry – "**self-ploughing**" soil.
[43:2:4] - Colour from **titaniferous magnetite / iron** and dark parent rock – **not from humus**.
[43:2:5] - **Rich in** lime, iron, magnesia, alumina, potash (calcium, potassium, magnesium); **poor in nitrogen, phosphorus and humus** – so "rich in humus / nitrogen and organic matter" is a **false reason**.
[43:2:6] - Neutral to alkaline; in USDA terms = **Vertisols** (= Grumusols; semi-arid; crack in summer; **not humus-rich**).
[43:2:7] # Distribution
[43:2:8] - **5.46 lakh sq km (16.6%)** – Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh (**Malwa Plateau**), western AP, north Karnataka, Telangana, Tamil Nadu.
[43:2:9] - **Largest area / most widespread: Maharashtra** – not Assam, Bihar, Punjab, Tamil Nadu or Jharkhand. **Not** found in the Himalayan region.
[43:2:10] - **Very deep black soils:** **north-western Deccan Plateau** and upper Godavari–Krishna valleys.
[43:2:11] # Crops
[43:2:12] - **Cotton** (most suitable soil), jowar, wheat, linseed, tobacco, chillies, oilseeds, sunflower.

## SUB 3 | Laterite and Red Soils
[43:3:0] # Laterite
[43:3:1] - Latin **"later" = brick**; formed by **intense leaching** under **high temperature and heavy rainfall (> 200 cm)** with alternate wet and dry seasons; lime and silica leached, **iron and aluminium oxides** left.
[43:3:2] - **Red** in colour (iron oxide); **acidic**, **low fertility** – **excess iron** makes it infertile; **poor in nitrogen, phosphate, calcium (lime)**, organic matter; NCERT: iron oxide and **potash** in excess (PMF: poor in potash).
[43:3:3] - **Not** moisture-retentive with a large clay factor – it is porous and well drained (that describes black soil).
[43:3:4] - **Building material:** cut into bricks that harden in air.
[43:3:5] - Crops with manure: **tea, coffee, rubber, cashew, tapioca**, coconut, arecanut.
[43:3:6] - **Where:** summits of Western and Eastern Ghats, **Malabar coast (Kerala)**, Vindhya–Satpura, Rajmahal hills, Malwa, south **Maharashtra**, Karnataka, Odisha, Assam, Meghalaya; **Gangani (West Medinipur, West Bengal)**; **not** Rajasthan, UP, HP or Punjab.
[43:3:7] # Red (and yellow) soil
[43:3:8] - From **ancient crystalline granite, gneiss and schist** under low rainfall; red colour from **ferric oxide** (wide diffusion of iron), **yellow when hydrated**.
[43:3:9] - Porous, light, **low moisture retention**; poor in lime, humus, nitrogen, phosphates; fairly rich in potash.
[43:3:10] - **Where:** Peninsular India – almost all of **Tamil Nadu**, **Andhra Pradesh**, Karnataka, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Chota Nagpur; Kanyakumari–Jhansi and Kutch–Rajmahal; surrounds the black soil on the south, east and north.
[43:3:11] - Crops: ragi, groundnut, tobacco, millets, pulses.
[43:3:12] # Andhra Pradesh
[43:3:13] - Cultivated area: **red ~60%**, black ~25%, deltaic alluvium 5–6%, laterite ~1%; 22 soil categories; **six agro-climatic zones**.
[43:3:14] - Red soils (Rayalaseema): red gram, green gram, horse gram, groundnut, castor; AP laterite is poor in nitrogen.
[43:3:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 08 p.7 (book p.105); OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Geography 06 p.4 (book p.69); PMF Indian Physical Geography 03 p.85-88 (UPSC 2010, 2013 PYQs); Rocket Indian Geography p.59-60; Rocket AP Geography p.43-44; app notes 4:43 (sub 1, 3, 9)
[43:3:16] - **Coffee** is grown on the red and laterite soils of the Western Ghats, not on alluvium.
[43:3:17] - **Tea** needs slightly acidic soil without calcium and cannot bear standing water – so it is grown on hill slopes.
## SUB 4 | Problem Soils – Salinity, Alkalinity, Acidity and Degradation
[43:4:0] # Saline and alkaline soils
[43:4:1] - Local names **reh, kallar, usar**, thur, rakar, chopan; caused by **unscientific irrigation and poor drainage**; capillary rise leaves salts on top.
[43:4:2] - **Salinisation** of irrigated land: salts left by evaporating water **make some soils impermeable** – it does not raise yields.
[43:4:3] - **Largest saline area: Gujarat** (Rann of Kutch, Gulf of Khambhat); **maximum alkali area: Uttar Pradesh**.
[43:4:4] - Affected: Rajasthan, Haryana, Punjab, UP, Bihar; Green Revolution canal areas of Punjab–Haryana.
[43:4:5] # Amendments
[43:4:6] TABLE head: Problem | Remedy
  [43:4:6.0] Alkaline / sodic (and saline-alkali) | Gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O) – replaces sodium with calcium; long-term solution, with leaching water
  [43:4:6.1] Excess soluble salts | Leaching – removing salts by applying water
  [43:4:6.2] Alkalinity | Acid-forming chemicals (sulphur etc.) neutralise it
  [43:4:6.3] Acidic | Lime – decreases soil acidity
  [43:4:6.4] Poor fertility | Green manuring – boosts microbes and fertility
[43:4:7] # Degradation
[43:4:8] - **Soil impoverishment** = soil becoming very deficient in plant nutrients.
[43:4:9] - **Green Revolution reduced soil fertility**: chemical fertilisers without manure, over-irrigation, salinity.
[43:4:10] TABLE head: Cause | States
  [43:4:10.0] Overgrazing | Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Maharashtra
  [43:4:10.1] Over-irrigation | Punjab, Haryana, western UP
  [43:4:10.2] Mining | Jharkhand, Odisha, MP, Chhattisgarh
[43:4:11] - **Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas** (ISRO **SAC, Ahmedabad**, June 2021): **97.85 mha (29.7%)** degraded in 2018-19 (28.76% in 2003-05); rose in **28 of 31** States/UTs; **water erosion** the largest driver.
[43:4:12] - **FAO Status of the World's Soil Resources 2026:** soil-biodiversity loss **4th of 7** threats (after erosion, SOC loss, nutrient mismanagement).

## SUB 5 | Soil Erosion – Types, Agents and Causes
[43:5:0] # Types
[43:5:1] TABLE head: Type | Process | Example
  [43:5:1.0] Splash | Raindrop impact breaks crumbs – first stage | Bare soil
  [43:5:1.1] Sheet | Thin uniform topsoil layer removed by overland flow; widespread, least noticed | Gentle slopes
  [43:5:1.2] Rill | Small, shallow channels on the soil surface | Bare fields
  [43:5:1.3] Gully | Deep trenches; badlands and ravines; final stage | Chambal ravines
  [43:5:1.4] Wind | Deflation, saltation, creep | Thar Desert
  [43:5:1.5] Mass (landslide) | Gravity on slopes | Himalayan monsoon slopes
  [43:5:1.6] Tunnel | Subsurface removal | Parts of Australia, Africa
[43:5:2] - **Increasing soil loss:** **splash → sheet → rill → gully** (II, IV, III, I).
[43:5:3] - **Ravines:** Chambal–Yamuna basin on the **periphery of the Gangetic plain** (MP, Rajasthan, UP); also Tamil Nadu, West Bengal; India loses ~8,000 ha a year to gullies.
[43:5:4] # Causes and rate
[43:5:5] - **Deforestation** is the main human cause (removes root binding and humus); also overgrazing, shifting cultivation, faulty farming. **Terrace cultivation is a remedy**, and tropical climate is not counted a cause – "only deforestation" (UPSC 2014).
[43:5:6] - **Deforestation generally decreases rainfall** (and increases erosion, drought, warming).
[43:5:7] - Erosion rate depends on **all** of: rainfall intensity and duration, slope, vegetation cover, soil texture, land use.
[43:5:8] - **Rainfall intensity matters more than total annual rainfall**; higher intensity → more runoff → **effective rainfall reduces**.
[43:5:9] - Wind erosion worst on **bare, dry, fine-textured soil** with strong winds; **water is the most powerful agent**.
[43:5:10] - Widely spaced row crops leave soil bare between rows – **sorghum** is the exam answer for fastest erosion (dense clover and wheat protect the soil).
[43:5:11] - **RUSLE** model predicts long-term water erosion on slopes (ICAR C- and P-factor maps).
[43:5:12] - Intense bursts exceed **infiltration capacity** → runoff, so soils can end up drier (2026 studies).

## SUB 6 | Other Soils, Rain-Forest Soils and the ICAR/USDA Orders
[43:6:0] # Desert, peaty, forest soils
[43:6:1] - **Arid / desert:** sandy, saline; poor in nitrogen and humus, **phosphate normal/high**; **calcium increases downward** (kankar) – **western Rajasthan soils are rich in calcium**.
[43:6:2] - **Peaty / marshy:** areas of **heavy rainfall and high humidity with good vegetation**; organic matter 40–50%; black, heavy, highly acidic (NCERT: alkaline in places); Kottayam–Alappuzha, Sundarbans, coastal Odisha–TN, north Bihar, Almora. "Semi-arid with salinity" = saline soil.
[43:6:3] - **Forest / mountain:** humus-rich in forests and lower valleys (NextGen, PMF), but **acidic with low humus** on snow-clad upper slopes (NCERT); poor in potash and phosphorus.
[43:6:4] - **Podzol:** acidic leached soil of **coniferous (taiga)** forests (about 60°N).
[43:6:5] # Tropical rain-forest soils
[43:6:6] - **Poor in nutrients**: heat and moisture **decompose organic matter quickly** (true), but nutrients go straight into vegetation or are **leached** (UPSC 2023: I false, II true).
[43:6:7] - Cleared rain forest **cannot** support intensive farming for years without fertilisers, though its **primary productivity is very high** (UPSC 2003: A false, R true).

## SUB 7 | Soil Science Terms – Pedology, Eluviation, pH, Water and Soil Biology
[43:7:0] # Branches and terms
[43:7:1] - **Pedology** = scientific study of soils in their natural setting (formation, classification, mapping); **edaphology** = soil in relation to plants; **soil geography** = soil formation, types, fertility status, distribution and use.
[43:7:2] - **Edaphic** = relating to the **soil** (its physical and chemical make-up) – as against climatic, biotic and topographic factors.
[43:7:3] - **Eluviation** = downward washing out (translocation) of clay, iron, humus from an upper horizon (the **E** layer); their deposit lower down = **illuviation** (B horizon).
[43:7:4] # Water, pH and salts
[43:7:5] - **Capillary rise** is strongest in **clayey** soil (finest pores) and weakest in sandy soil.
[43:7:6] - **Soil pH:** most common crops grow best near neutral, **pH 6–7**; pH 3–4 is strongly acidic, 9–10 strongly alkaline.
[43:7:7] - **Halophytes** (e.g. mangroves) grow in **saline** soils; cryophytes – cold soils; lithophytes – rock; xerophytes – deserts.
[43:7:8] # Soil biology
[43:7:9] - **Legumes** – **pea, black gram (urd)**, gram, moong – fix atmospheric nitrogen in root nodules (*Rhizobium*) and **improve soil fertility**; wheat, rice, sugarcane, sorghum deplete it.
[43:7:10] - **Smell of rain on parched soil (petrichor):** **geosmin** made by soil **Streptomyces** (actinomycetes).

## SUB 8 | Soil Formation, Profile, Water and Nutrients
[43:8:0] # Formation
[43:8:1] - Five factors: **parent material, climate, relief, organisms, time** (soil colour is a property, not a factor); limestone → calcium-rich soil.
[43:8:2] - **Tors:** rounded granite boulders left by weathering and erosion of granite – common in Andhra Pradesh (e.g. Rayalaseema).
[43:8:3] # Profile
[43:8:4] - **Soil profile** = the **vertical arrangement of horizons** down to the parent rock (a single layer is a horizon).
[43:8:5] TABLE head: Horizon | Meaning
  [43:8:5.0] O | Organic litter
  [43:8:5.1] A | Topsoil, humus-rich
  [43:8:5.2] E | Leached layer
  [43:8:5.3] B | Subsoil – minerals washed down accumulate
  [43:8:5.4] C | Weathered parent material
  [43:8:5.5] R | Bedrock
[43:8:6] - Order from the surface: **O → A → B → C → R**.
[43:8:7] # Composition and water
[43:8:8] - Good soil by volume: **mineral 45%, organic matter 5%, water 25%, air 25%**.
[43:8:9] - **Soil organic carbon** binds particles into aggregates, raises infiltration, feeds microbes; India's average SOC reportedly fell from **1% to 0.3%** in 70 years.
[43:8:10] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [43:8:10.0] Infiltration | Entry of water at the surface
  [43:8:10.1] Percolation | Movement down through the soil column
  [43:8:10.2] Permeability | Ease of water movement
  [43:8:10.3] Saturated flow | All soil pores filled with water
[43:8:11] - **Clay** retains the most water because of its many **small** pores (not large ones).
[43:8:12] # Nutrients
[43:8:13] TABLE head: Nutrient | Main source in soil
  [43:8:13.0] Nitrogen | Organic matter
  [43:8:13.1] Iron | Pyrites
  [43:8:13.2] Calcium | Gypsum
  [43:8:13.3] Boron | Tourmaline
[43:8:14] - **Zinc** is the micronutrient most widely deficient in Indian soils.
[43:8:15] - Crops remove large amounts of **potassium** – among Ca, K, Zn and P the most depleted after harvest.

## SUB 0 | Tsunami – Warning Centres, 2004 Tsunami and International Centres
[45:0:0] # Tsunami basics
[45:0:1] - **Tsunami:** large waves from a sudden movement of the **ocean floor** – mostly an **undersea earthquake** (also submarine landslides, volcanic eruptions); Japanese "harbour wave". A **natural** disaster, not man-made.
[45:0:2] - **26 December 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami** (Sumatra–Andaman earthquake): hit Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Puducherry, Kerala and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands; **Indira Point** (southern tip of Great Nicobar) went **under the sea**.
[45:0:3] - Most exposed: the **east coast** and the **Andaman & Nicobar Islands**.
[45:0:4] # Indian warning system
[45:0:5] TABLE head: Question | Answer
  [45:0:5.0] Tsunami Warning Centre of India / ITEWC | Hyderabad, at INCOIS
  [45:0:5.1] INCOIS | Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services, Hyderabad; autonomous body (1999) under the Ministry of Earth Sciences (ESSO unit)
  [45:0:5.2] ITEWC set up | After the 2004 tsunami; inaugurated 15 Oct 2007, working 24x7 since 2007
  [45:0:5.3] Detection | Tsunamigenic earthquakes detected within ~10 minutes; seismic network, buoys, tide gauges
  [45:0:5.4] Other INCOIS tools | Real-time coastal risk / wave-height / vulnerable-building forecast (first in the world), SAMUDRA app, storm-surge and high-wave warnings
[45:0:6] - **Not** IMD Delhi, **not** NIO Goa, **not** NIOT Chennai – the tsunami warning system is run by **INCOIS, Hyderabad**.
[45:0:7] - The **IOC (UNESCO)-coordinated Indian Ocean Tsunami Warning System** was set up after 2004; India (ITEWC) is one of its regional tsunami service providers (with Australia, Indonesia).
[45:0:8] - **Mock drills:** IOC-UNESCO's **IOWave 18** (Sept 2018, India + 23 Indian Ocean nations); later IOWave-23, IOWave-25.

## SUB 1 | Landslides and Avalanches – Causes, Prone Areas, Zonation, Mitigation
[45:1:0] # Where and why
[45:1:1] - **About 12.6% of India's area (~0.42 million sq km)** is landslide-prone (GSI 12.6–15%): **Himalayas, North-East, Western Ghats (incl. Nilgiris)**; parts of the Eastern Ghats.
[45:1:2] - Landslides occur **mostly in hilly terrain** on **steep slopes of rocky mountains** – not in mud valleys, seashores or plateaus.
[45:1:3] - **Natural causes:** heavy rain and cloudbursts, earthquakes, fragile young fold mountains, steep slopes.
[45:1:4] - **Man-made causes:** **deforestation**, **road building**, **shifting cultivation**, mining and quarrying, changes in **land-use pattern**, hill cutting. **Increased forestation is NOT a cause** – it is a remedy.
[45:1:5] - India's vulnerability = **deforestation + heavy rainfall in mountains** (India has little volcanic activity).
[45:1:6] - **Himalayas:** landslide frequency has **increased**, with large-scale **mining**, roads and dams (A and R both true, R explains A in the keys).
[45:1:7] - Western Ghats/Nilgiris: steep escarpments and orographic rain (not permafrost).
[45:1:8] # Major events
[45:1:9] TABLE head: Event | Type | Year
  [45:1:9.0] Malpa (Pithoragarh, Uttarakhand) | Landslide – Kailash-Mansarovar pilgrims killed | 18 Aug 1998
  [45:1:9.1] Wayanad (Kerala) | Landslides after very heavy rain | July 2024
  [45:1:9.2] Badrinath (Mana), Chamoli | Snow avalanche hit a BRO labour camp | 28 Feb 2025
  [45:1:9.3] Kedarnath | Cloudburst + flash flood | June 2013
  [45:1:9.4] Chamoli | Glacier (rock-ice) flood | 2021
[45:1:10] - **Avalanches** = rapid flow of snow and ice down steep (30°–45°) slopes – mainly in **high Himalayan hilly terrain**; nodal ministry: Defence (DGRE).
[45:1:11] - Hanging-glacier collapse in high seismic zones can trigger **GLOFs**.
[45:1:12] # Landslide hazard zonation
[45:1:13] - **Landslide (hazard) zoning:** division of hill areas into **homogeneous spatial units** by degree of **actual or potential** landslide susceptibility, hazard or risk; maps are **probabilistic** (likelihood, not exact time/place).
[45:1:14] - Preparing a full zonation map needs **huge data** on many variables over large slope areas, and **aerial photographs, satellite images and remote sensing** help collect it (both conclusions follow).
[45:1:15] TABLE head: Rock falls (number/annum/km of cliff or rock cut) | Frequency zone
  [45:1:15.0] > 10 | Very high
  [45:1:15.1] 1 to 10 | High
  [45:1:15.2] 0.1 to 1.0 | Moderate
  [45:1:15.3] 0.01 to 0.1 | Low
  [45:1:15.4] < 0.01 | Very low
[45:1:16] - NDMA's **National Landslide Risk Management Strategy (2019)**; GSI national susceptibility mapping; GSI **LANDSLIP** regional early warning system.
[45:1:17] # Mitigation
[45:1:18] - **Cheapest and most effective:** **increasing vegetative cover** (afforestation); walls and engineered works cost more.
[45:1:19] - **NCERT list:** **area-specific measures**, **limiting agriculture to valleys and moderate slopes**, **large-scale afforestation and bunds** to reduce water flow (all three).
[45:1:20] - **Structural:** retaining walls, rock bolting, wire mesh, drainage, slope grading. **Non-structural:** hazard zonation mapping, early warning, land-use regulation. **Regulatory:** restrict construction in high-risk zones.

## SUB 3 | Cyclone-prone Coasts
[45:3:0] - **Cyclone = low-pressure area**; Indian cyclones start from low-pressure areas in the **Bay of Bengal near the Andaman Sea / Andaman & Nicobar Islands** (8–14°N).
