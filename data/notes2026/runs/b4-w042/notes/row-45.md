=== Tsunami – Warning Centres, 2004 Tsunami and International Centres
# Tsunami basics
- **Tsunami:** large waves from a sudden movement of the **ocean floor** – mostly an **undersea earthquake** (also submarine landslides, volcanic eruptions); Japanese "harbour wave". A **natural** disaster, not man-made.
- **26 December 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami** (Sumatra–Andaman earthquake): hit Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Puducherry, Kerala and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands; **Indira Point** (southern tip of Great Nicobar) went **under the sea**.
- Most exposed: the **east coast** and the **Andaman & Nicobar Islands**.
# Indian warning system
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Tsunami Warning Centre of India / **ITEWC** | **Hyderabad**, at **INCOIS** |
| INCOIS | **Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services**, **Hyderabad**; autonomous body (1999) under the **Ministry of Earth Sciences** (ESSO unit) |
| ITEWC set up | After the 2004 tsunami; inaugurated **15 Oct 2007**, working 24x7 since **2007** |
| Detection | Tsunamigenic earthquakes detected within ~10 minutes; seismic network, buoys, tide gauges |
| Other INCOIS tools | Real-time coastal risk / wave-height / vulnerable-building forecast (first in the world), SAMUDRA app, storm-surge and high-wave warnings |
- **Not** IMD Delhi, **not** NIO Goa, **not** NIOT Chennai – the tsunami warning system is run by **INCOIS, Hyderabad**.
- The **IOC (UNESCO)-coordinated Indian Ocean Tsunami Warning System** was set up after 2004; India (ITEWC) is one of its regional tsunami service providers (with Australia, Indonesia).
- **Mock drills:** IOC-UNESCO's **IOWave 18** (Sept 2018, India + 23 Indian Ocean nations); later IOWave-23, IOWave-25.

=== Landslides and Avalanches – Causes, Prone Areas, Zonation, Mitigation
# Where and why
- **About 12.6% of India's area (~0.42 million sq km)** is landslide-prone (GSI 12.6–15%): **Himalayas, North-East, Western Ghats (incl. Nilgiris)**; parts of the Eastern Ghats.
- Landslides occur **mostly in hilly terrain** on **steep slopes of rocky mountains** – not in mud valleys, seashores or plateaus.
- **Natural causes:** heavy rain and cloudbursts, earthquakes, fragile young fold mountains, steep slopes.
- **Man-made causes:** **deforestation**, **road building**, **shifting cultivation**, mining and quarrying, changes in **land-use pattern**, hill cutting. **Increased forestation is NOT a cause** – it is a remedy.
- India's vulnerability = **deforestation + heavy rainfall in mountains** (India has little volcanic activity).
- **Himalayas:** landslide frequency has **increased**, with large-scale **mining**, roads and dams (A and R both true, R explains A in the keys).
- Western Ghats/Nilgiris: steep escarpments and orographic rain (not permafrost).
# Major events
| Event | Type | Year |
|---|---|---|
| **Malpa** (Pithoragarh, Uttarakhand) | Landslide – Kailash-Mansarovar pilgrims killed | **18 Aug 1998** |
| **Wayanad** (Kerala) | **Landslides** after very heavy rain | **July 2024** |
| **Badrinath (Mana)**, Chamoli | **Snow avalanche** hit a **BRO** labour camp | **28 Feb 2025** |
| Kedarnath | Cloudburst + flash flood | June 2013 |
| Chamoli | Glacier (rock-ice) flood | 2021 |
- **Avalanches** = rapid flow of snow and ice down steep (30°–45°) slopes – mainly in **high Himalayan hilly terrain**; nodal ministry: Defence (DGRE).
- Hanging-glacier collapse in high seismic zones can trigger **GLOFs**.
# Landslide hazard zonation
- **Landslide (hazard) zoning:** division of hill areas into **homogeneous spatial units** by degree of **actual or potential** landslide susceptibility, hazard or risk; maps are **probabilistic** (likelihood, not exact time/place).
- Preparing a full zonation map needs **huge data** on many variables over large slope areas, and **aerial photographs, satellite images and remote sensing** help collect it (both conclusions follow).
| Rock falls (number/annum/km of cliff or rock cut) | Frequency zone |
|---|---|
| > 10 | Very high |
| 1 to 10 | High |
| **0.1 to 1.0** | **Moderate** |
| 0.01 to 0.1 | Low |
| < 0.01 | Very low |
- NDMA's **National Landslide Risk Management Strategy (2019)**; GSI national susceptibility mapping; GSI **LANDSLIP** regional early warning system.
# Mitigation
- **Cheapest and most effective:** **increasing vegetative cover** (afforestation); walls and engineered works cost more.
- **NCERT list:** **area-specific measures**, **limiting agriculture to valleys and moderate slopes**, **large-scale afforestation and bunds** to reduce water flow (all three).
- **Structural:** retaining walls, rock bolting, wire mesh, drainage, slope grading. **Non-structural:** hazard zonation mapping, early warning, land-use regulation. **Regulatory:** restrict construction in high-risk zones.

=== India's Hazard Profile – the Number Game
| Hazard | Exposure (most-asked figure) |
|---|---|
| **Earthquake** | **~59% (58.6%)** of landmass prone to moderate-to-very-high intensity (MoES in Lok Sabha, July 2021 and Dec 2022) |
| **Flood** | **~40 million ha ≈ 12%** of land (Rashtriya Barh Ayog); Rocket also gives "about 10%" |
| **Cyclone and tsunami** | **~5,700 km of the 7,516 km** coastline (NDMA) |
| Drought | ~68% of cultivable area |
| Landslide | ~12.6% of area (0.42 million sq km) |
| **Share of world tropical cyclones** | **~10%** affect the Indian coasts (most books and keys); PMF: North Indian Ocean has 6–7% of global tropical cyclones |
- **Disaster risk = Hazard x Vulnerability.**
- Man-made disasters: war, hazardous-material exposure, transport accidents; **tsunami, cyclone, earthquake = natural**.
- **DM Act 2005** shifted focus from post-disaster **relief → prevention, mitigation, preparedness**.
- **APSDMA** is chaired by the **Chief Minister** (AP Disaster Management Rules, 2007). **NDMA–MoPR community DRR project:** Rs 507.37 crore, through **panchayats** in 81 districts of 20 States.

=== Seismic Zoning Map of India
- Prepared by **BIS** (**IS 1893**); **four zones – II, III, IV, V** (Zone V = very high damage risk, MSK IX+).
- **Killari (Latur) 1993** quake hit a "low-risk" area → **2002** revision merged **Zone I into Zone II**.
| Zone | Share of area | Places |
|---|---|---|
| **V** | ~11% | Entire **North-East (e.g. Meghalaya)**, J&K, Ladakh, HP, Uttarakhand, Kutch, North Bihar, **Andaman & Nicobar**; Guwahati, Aizawl |
| IV | ~18% | **Delhi**, parts of J&K/HP; Srinagar (IV–V) |
| III | ~30% | Indo-Gangetic plains, coasts, Kerala, Goa; **Amaravati** region |
| **II** | **~41% (largest single zone)** | Most of the stable peninsula – **Hyderabad** (least prone) |
- Karnataka, Maharashtra and Kerala are **not** Zone V. **Andhra Pradesh: Zones II and III only** (no Zone V).
- **Withdrawn revision:** a BIS probabilistic (PSHA) map adding a **Zone VI** was **rolled back in March 2026** – the four-zone map still applies.
- Codes: **IS 1893** = seismic design criteria; **IS 13920** = ductile detailing. GSI tracks crustal strain through **34 GNSS stations**.
| Earthquake | Type / year |
|---|---|
| **Koyna** (Maharashtra) | **Reservoir-induced**, 1967 |
| **Killari** (Latur) | **Intraplate**, 1993 |
| Bhuj (Kutch) | 26 Jan 2001 → DM Act 2005 |
- AP: reservoir-induced tremors not ruled out near **Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Polavaram**. **Liquefaction** hazard: Indo-Gangetic plain.
> "Majority of India's area" – official key Zone III; by the MoES shares Zone II (~41%) is the largest single zone.

=== Cyclone-prone Coasts
- **Cyclone = low-pressure area**; Indian cyclones start from low-pressure areas in the **Bay of Bengal near the Andaman Sea / Andaman & Nicobar Islands** (8–14°N).
- Bay of Bengal : Arabian Sea ≈ **4 : 1** → the **east coast** is hit far more (Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu).
- **Most cyclone-prone coast:** **Odisha (Orissa) coast** in most books (most landfalls over a century); **Andhra Pradesh** was hit most in 1995–2020 (NDMI tally) and is the APPSC GS 2025 key's "most cyclone-prone State".
- **West coast:** **Gujarat** is the most vulnerable (Kandla 1998, Tauktae 2021, Biparjoy 2023).
- **Mangroves** shield coasts; their clearing in the **Mahanadi delta** added to Odisha's cyclone risk.
- Exposure: **13 coastal States/UTs**, **84 coastal districts**. AP's most vulnerable stretch: **Ongole–Machilipatnam**; deadliest AP cyclone: **1977** cyclone.
- IMD cyclone warning centres (7): Chennai, Mumbai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Thiruvananthapuram, **Visakhapatnam**, Bhubaneswar – **not Hyderabad**. ISRO runs **NDEM**; GIS and remote sensing map coastal vulnerability.
