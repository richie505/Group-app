=== Ores, Alloys and Classification of Minerals
| Metal | Ores asked |
|---|---|
| Aluminium | **Bauxite** (Al₂O₃·xH₂O, chief ore), **cryolite** (Na₃AlF₆), **corundum** (anhydrous Al₂O₃), diaspore |
| Iron | **Haematite** Fe₂O₃, **magnetite** Fe₃O₄ (magnetic, "black ore"), **limonite** FeO(OH)·nH₂O (brown), **siderite** FeCO₃, pyrites FeS₂ (used for sulphur, not for iron) |
| Copper | Chalcopyrite CuFeS₂, chalcocite Cu₂S, **cuprite**, **malachite** Cu₂(CO₃)(OH)₂, azurite |
| Lead / Zinc | **Galena** PbS / **zinc blende** (sphalerite) ZnS, **calamine**, smithsonite ZnCO₃ |
| Mercury / Tin | **Cinnabar** HgS / **cassiterite** SnO₂ |
| Silver | Argentite Ag₂S, **horn silver** (AgCl), **ruby silver**; lunar caustic = AgNO₃ |
| Manganese | Pyrolusite MnO₂, psilomelane, manganite, hausmannite, braunite |
| Titanium | **Ilmenite** FeTiO₃, **rutile** TiO₂ (coastal beach sands) |
| Thorium / Uranium | **Monazite** (thorium + rare earths) / **pitchblende** (uranium, not copper) |
| Tungsten | **Wolframite** (China leads), scheelite |
| Na / K / Mg / Ca | Rock salt NaCl / sylvine, carnallite KCl·MgCl₂·6H₂O / magnesite MgCO₃, dolomite CaMg(CO₃)₂ / limestone CaCO₃, **gypsum CaSO₄·2H₂O** (calcium sulphate dihydrate), fluorspar CaF₂ |
- **Iron content:** **magnetite** highest (about 70–72%; "superior grade") > **haematite** (60–70%) > limonite (40–60%) > **siderite** (lowest). **Haematite** = most widely used and India's **largest reserve** type.
- **Magnetite:** black ore, up to ~70% iron, magnetic – all three statements true.
- **Not an ore:** **pig iron** (a smelted product); bauxite, malachite and zinc blende are ores.
- **Native (free) state:** **gold** (best-known), silver, platinum, copper – a single type of atom = **native elements**. **Never free:** K, Na, Ca, Mg, **Al** (top of the reactivity series).
- **Alloys:** **German silver** = copper + zinc + nickel (Cu 60, Ni 20, Zn 20) – **contains no silver**; **brass** = copper + zinc; bronze = copper + tin. Horn silver, ruby silver and lunar caustic all contain silver.
- **Ferrous minerals** (contain iron): iron ore, manganese, chromite, nickel, cobalt (NCERT). **Non-ferrous:** copper, bauxite (aluminium), lead, zinc, tin (NCERT); precious: gold, silver, platinum.
- **⚠ Tungsten:** exam keys treat it as **non-ferrous**, but NextGen lists it among ferrous (ferro-alloy) minerals with cobalt and nickel.
- **Non-ferrous group:** **nickel, zinc, copper, aluminium** (not iron ore, pig iron or steel).
- **Non-metallic:** **marble**, **gypsum**, mica, limestone, salt, potash, graphite (not haematite, bauxite, limonite, manganese, silver, lead).
| Deposit type | How formed | Minerals |
|---|---|---|
| Placer | Heavy minerals in alluvial / beach sands | **Gold, tin, platinum** |
| Veins / lodes | Cracks in igneous and metamorphic rocks | Tin, copper, zinc, lead |
| Sedimentary beds | Layers | **Coal, gypsum, salt, potash** |
| Residual weathering | Decomposed rock (laterite) | **Bauxite** |
- **Kimberlite** pipes (ultrabasic) = host rock of **diamond**.
- **Talc:** softest mineral; a magnesium silicate (softness comes from its weakly bonded layers, not from being Mg silicate).
- **Plaster of Paris** is made by heating **gypsum** (CaSO₄·½H₂O, written 2CaSO₄·H₂O); anhydrite = CaSO₄.

- **Copper ranks:** reserves – **Rajasthan ~52–54%** (largest), Jharkhand, MP; production – **MP first** (Malanjkhand). Jharkhand is not the 90% source.
- **Bauxite ranks:** reserves – **Odisha ~50%**, Chhattisgarh, **AP ~12–18%**, Jharkhand; production 2021-22 – **Odisha 73%**, **Gujarat 9% (2nd)**, Jharkhand 8%, Chhattisgarh 4%. Odisha + AP hold >90% of metallurgical-grade bauxite.
- **Rajasthan:** sole producer of **lead-zinc ores, wollastonite and selenite** – near monopoly in **zinc**; not the sole copper producer.
- **Tin resources:** Haryana ~64% and Chhattisgarh ~36% (IMYB 2024), but only Chhattisgarh produces.
- **Thorium:** from **monazite**; largest monazite concentration on the **Kerala** coast (Chavara; IREL Udyogamandal) – Kerala = leading thorium answer; monazite also on TN, AP and Odisha coasts. India is **self-sufficient in thorium**; uranium = main nuclear fuel.
- **Beach sands (seven heavy minerals):** ilmenite, rutile, zircon, sillimanite, garnet, monazite, leucoxene – Kerala (Neendakara–Kayamkulam), TN, Odisha, AP (Srikakulam, Visakhapatnam). Ilmenite and rutile = **titanium**.
- **Uranium oxide to metal:** U₃O₈ is ~84.8% uranium, so 1.22 lakh t U₃O₈ ≈ **1.04 lakh t** uranium.

=== Non-metallic Minerals – Mica, Gypsum, Marble and Others
# Mica
- **Occurrence:** pegmatite veins in Archaean/Dharwar granite, gneiss, schist; varieties **muscovite** (white), **phlogopite** (amber/Mg) and biotite – **phlogopite and pegmatite → mica**.
- **Use:** an **insulator** in electrical and electronic industries; India historically monopolised **sheet mica** (led world output when asked in 1992, 2001).
- **Belts:** **Koderma–Gaya–Hazaribagh–Munger** ("Capital of Mica", ruby mica, Jharkhand–Bihar) – the largest belt; **Nellore** (AP); **Ajmer–Bhilwara** (Rajasthan).
- **Reserves:** **AP 41%**, **Rajasthan 28%**, Odisha 17%, Maharashtra 13% (2015) – Rajasthan, AP, Bihar and **Jharkhand** are the classic reserve States.
- **Producers:** AP long the **largest producer** (Nellore); IMYB 2020-21: **Rajasthan 17.7 thousand t, AP 16.7 thousand t**. Jharkhand, Rajasthan, AP produce mica; **Madhya Pradesh does not**.
# Gypsum, marble and others
| Mineral | Use | Areas |
|---|---|---|
| Gypsum | Cement (setting regulator), fertiliser (soil fertility), Plaster of Paris | **Rajasthan ~99%** (Churu–Bikaner–Sri Ganganagar belt); also TN |
| Marble | Building | **Makrana** (Rajasthan) – finest; Taj Mahal, Victoria Memorial |
| Limestone | Cement, steel flux | MP, Rajasthan, AP (Kurnool), Gujarat, Chhattisgarh; Kaimur–Rohtas |
| Dolomite | Steel flux, refractories | Chhattisgarh, Odisha, AP |
| Asbestos | Fire-proofing | Resources **Rajasthan 59%**, Karnataka 36%; chrysotile – **AP (Kadapa)** ~96% |
| Barytes | Oil-well drilling mud | **Mangampet** (Kadapa, AP) – AP ~92% of resources |
| Kyanite / sillimanite | Refractories | **Lapsa Buru** (Jharkhand) / **Sona Pahar** (Meghalaya), Odisha |
| Salt | — | **Didwana** (Rajasthan), Gujarat |
- **Asbestos production:** only AP (Kadapa chrysotile) reported output until 2013-14; **no production in India since 2014-15**. Old keys name Rajasthan the largest producer.
- **Graphite:** Bellary area (Karnataka) in the UPSC 1998 match; graphite also at Rampa (AP Eastern Ghats).

=== Rock Systems and Mineral Belts
| Rock system | Where | Minerals |
|---|---|---|
| Archaean (>2,500 Ma; fossil-free, formed before life) | Peninsular shield | Metallic minerals; storehouse of mineral wealth |
| **Dharwar** (oldest schist belts; economically most important) | Karnataka, Aravallis, Singhbhum | **Iron ore, manganese, copper, lead, gold**, mica – ferrous and non-ferrous |
| Cuddapah (Purana; shale, slate, quartzite, limestone) | Kadapa basin, AP | **Barytes, asbestos**, copper, iron, lead, diamond, uranium – no coal |
| Vindhyan (Purana; Semri lower; Kaimur, Rewa, Bhander upper) | Central India | Limestone, sandstone, **Panna diamonds** |
| Dravidian (Palaeozoic) | Himalaya, Kashmir | Abundant marine fossils |
| **Gondwana** (Carboniferous–Jurassic) | Damodar, Son, Mahanadi, Godavari valleys | **Coal – about 98% (99.54% as on 1.4.2022)**; not natural gas |
| Deccan Trap (end-Cretaceous fissure basalt) | Maharashtra, Malwa | Black cotton (regur) soil |
| **Tertiary** (youngest) | Assam, Gujarat, Rajasthan, offshore | **Petroleum, natural gas**; Neyveli lignite |
- **Gondwana coal** is younger and lower in carbon than Carboniferous coal. A–R: Gondwana ~95%+ coal ✓ and Dharwar ferrous/non-ferrous ✓, but R does not explain A.
- **Madhya Pradesh geology:** part of the old **Gondwana land** (peninsular block) and holds Vindhyan, Gondwana and Deccan Trap rocks.
- **AP rocks:** Dharwar – Anantapur, Chittoor, Kadapa (iron ore, gold); Kurnool group – cement limestone; Eastern Ghats Mobile Belt (khondalite, charnockite) – bauxite, manganese, graphite.
| Belt | Area | Minerals |
|---|---|---|
| North-Eastern Plateau ("Golden Triangle") | Chota Nagpur, Odisha plateau, WB, parts of Chhattisgarh | Coal, iron ore, manganese, mica, bauxite, copper, uranium |
| Central | Chhattisgarh, MP, Maharashtra, Telangana | Iron ore (Bailadila), manganese (Balaghat), coal, limestone, bauxite |
| South-Western / Southern | Karnataka, Goa, TN uplands, Kerala, AP | High-grade iron ore, manganese, gold (Kolar), chromite, Neyveli lignite, Kerala monazite |
| North-Western | Aravalli (Rajasthan), Gujarat | **Non-ferrous copper**, lead-zinc, gypsum, salt, marble; Gujarat petroleum |
| North-Eastern | Assam, Meghalaya | Crude oil (Digboi), Meghalaya coal and limestone |
- **Chota Nagpur industrial region** grew on the discovery of **coal** in the Damodar valley; Jharia, Bokaro, Giridih, Raniganj, Karanpura = coalfields.
- **Statement keys:** natural gas – marine sedimentary basins, not Gondwana; mica – Koderma ✓; Dharwars – not petroleum; Cuddapah – barytes/asbestos, not "famous for diamonds".
- **Geological firsts:** GSI set up 1851; "Archaean" coined by J.D. Dana (1872); Indian rock grouping by T.H. Holland. Fossil parks: Siwalik Fossil Park, Saketi (HP); Marine Gondwana Fossil Park, Manendragarh (Chhattisgarh); Akal Wood Fossil Park (Jaisalmer); Lonar = meteorite crater lake.

=== Mining Law and Policy
- **MMDR Act 1957:** Ministry of Mines; excludes petroleum, gas, atomic minerals; major minerals – Centre; **minor minerals – States (Section 15)**.
| Amendment | Change |
|---|---|
| 2015 | Auction-only allotment; **DMF** and **NMET** created; PMKKKY uses DMF funds (yearly audit) |
| 2021 | NMET autonomous; captive mines may sell output |
| 2025 | New minerals added to leases (States approve in **30 days**); captive-sale limit removed |
| 2026 Bill (Aug) | Limits States' power to tax mineral rights and mineral-bearing land |
- **MADA v. SAIL (2024):** royalty is **not a tax**; States may tax mineral-bearing land. SC (Jul 2026) upheld royalty on an Average Sale Price including royalty, DMF and NMET.
- **Offshore:** **Art. 297** vests offshore minerals in the Union; OAMDR Amendment 2023 – auction-only leases. **National Mineral Policy 2019** replaced the 2008 policy.
