=== English Envoys at the Mughal Court – Hawkins, Roe, Terry
| Englishman | Arrival | Emperor | Fact asked |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ralph Fitch** | **1583** | Akbar | first English traveller to India |
| **Capt. William Hawkins** | Surat **1608** (ship *Hector*), Agra court **April 1609** | **Jahangir** | envoy of **James I**; spoke **Turki** without an interpreter; mansab of 400; title **"English Khan"**; married an Armenian (Mubarak Shah's daughter); left **Nov 1611**, no factory |
| Capt. Thomas Best | 1612 | Jahangir | beat the Portuguese off **Swally** (Surat) → Surat factory **1613** |
| **Nicholas Downton** | 1614 | – | another Surat sea fight with the Portuguese |
| **Sir Thomas Roe** | **1615** (court 1616-19) | Jahangir | ambassador of James I; no treaty, but factories at **Agra, Ahmedabad, Broach** |
| **Edward Terry** | 1616 | Jahangir | **Roe's chaplain**; his *Voyage to East-India* describes the emperor's drinking water brought from the **Ganga** |
- **Order of arrival:** **Fitch (1583) → Hawkins (1608) → Downton (1614) → Roe (1615)**.
- **"English Khan"** = **Hawkins** (not Roe, Terry, Albuquerque or Almeida).
- **⚠ Trap:** Hawkins came in **1608/09**, not "1611 as envoy" – 1611 is the year he left.

=== English Settlements – Surat, Masulipatnam, Madras, Bombay, Calcutta
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 31 Dec **1600** | Charter of **Elizabeth I**: exclusive trade for **15 years** (made indefinite 1609) |
| **1611** | trade at **Masulipatnam** (first in the south; factory 1616); HQ of the south till **1641** |
| **1613** | **first permanent English factory – Surat** (Jahangir's farman, Thomas Aldworth) |
| 1632 | **Golden Farman** of the **Sultan of Golconda** – free trade in Golconda ports for 500 pagodas a year |
| 1633 | first factory in the east – **Hariharpur / Balasore (Odisha)** |
| **1639** | **Francis Day** gets Madras from the **Raja of Chandragiri** (a Vijayanagara representative; Damarla brothers) → **Fort St. George**; Madras HQ from **1641** |
| **1661-62 / 1668** | **Bombay** – Portuguese dowry of **Catherine of Braganza** to Charles II; to the Company 1668 for £10 a year |
| 1690 / 1698 / 1700 | Job Charnock at Sutanuti; zamindari of **Sutanuti, Gobindapur, Kalikata**; **Fort William** |
| **1717** | **Farrukhsiyar's farman** ("Magna Carta" of the Company) – duty-free trade in Bengal |
| 1708 | rival companies merged: **United Company of Merchants** |
- **First European company to set up a factory at Surat = the English (1613)**; the Dutch came in 1616 and the French in 1668.
- **Bombay originally belonged to the Portuguese**, not the Dutch, Danes or French.

=== Entry of the European Powers – Statement Checks
- **Goa** was captured by **Albuquerque** in **1510** from the **Sultan of Bijapur** (with Vijayanagara help) – "Goa in 1499" is wrong.
- **English:** first factory in **South India at Masulipatnam**; first in **eastern India in Orissa (1633)** – both correct.
- **French (Dupleix's time) took Madras in 1746** (La Bourdonnais) – accepted as correct.
- **Dutch east-coast factories** stood on land granted by **Golconda** and the **Vijayanagara/Chandragiri** rulers (Pulicat, with Queen Eraivi's permission, 1609-10) – **not the Gajapatis**, who had ended in the 16th century.
- **Madras (1639)** was leased from a **representative of the Vijayanagara empire** (Damarla Venkatadri Nayaka under the Chandragiri Raja).
> UPSC 2022: "Dutch on Gajapati land" ✗, "Albuquerque took Goa from Bijapur" ✓, "Madras from a Vijayanagara representative" ✓ → B and C only.

=== European Companies – Charters and Joint-Stock Firsts
| Power | Company | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Portuguese | no company – **Estado da India** | state (Crown) enterprise |
| English | EIC, **1600** | private **joint-stock**, royal charter of **exclusive trading rights** |
| Dutch | **VOC, 1602** – States-General merged several companies | joint-stock; could **wage war, make treaties, take territory, build forts** |
| Danes | 1616 | – |
| French | **Compagnie des Indes Orientales, 1664** (Colbert, Louis XIV) | **state-controlled**; 50-year monopoly |
- **First joint-stock company to trade with India:** the English EIC (1600); the Dutch VOC (1602) is the answer when the English are not among the options (IAS 1994).
- **Why the English won:** a private joint-stock company with steady finance, naval strength, and use of Indian rulers' rivalries.
~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.67, p.69, p.74; PMF Modern History Part 2 p.1, p.4; old app notes row 151 (sub 0)
