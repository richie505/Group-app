=== NOTES 1:150 | Age of Discovery and the Sea Route to India – Vasco Da Gama; Motives of European Expansion
## SUB 0 | Why Europe Sought A Sea Route
[150:0:0] # The Old Route and Its Closure
[150:0:1] - Before the 15th century the most important route was **Europe → Constantinople → Central Asia → India**.
[150:0:2] - The trade was controlled by **Arabs**; **Venice** and **Genoa** distributed the goods in Europe.
[150:0:3] - **1453**: the Ottoman Sultan **Mehmed II** captured **Constantinople**, closing the main overland route to India.
[150:0:4] Fall of Constantinople = 1453, not 1452.
[150:0:5] # Motives – Factor and Effect
[150:0:6] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [150:0:6.0] Commodities | Spices (pepper, cinnamon, cardamom, ginger, cloves, nutmeg), silk; Indian textiles in huge demand
  [150:0:6.1] Fall of Constantinople (1453) | Overland route closed; need for a direct sea route
  [150:0:6.2] Religion | Spread of Christianity
  [150:0:6.3] Wealth and prestige | Profits of the spice trade
  [150:0:6.4] Rivalry | Spain, Portugal, Netherlands, England, France
  [150:0:6.5] Breaking Arab control | Direct access to Indian goods
[150:0:7] - Other motive: curiosity and exploration.
[150:0:8] "Roman imperial administration" is NOT a motive of European expansion.
[150:0:9] # Contemporary Remark
[150:0:10] - "Bear in mind that **the commerce of India is the commerce of the world**" – **Peter the Great** of Russia (APPSC Group 2, 2017). It refers to 18th-century India.
## SUB 1 | Navigational Advances
[150:1:0] - **Prince Henry "the Navigator"** (Portugal): set up **naval training centres**; first European to actively promote navigation.
[150:1:1] - **Caravel** (ship) and **astrolabe** (instrument) made long voyages safer.

## SUB 4 | Vasco Da Gama
[150:4:0] # First Voyage (1497-99)
[150:4:1] - Sponsor: King **Manuel I** of Portugal.
[150:4:2] - Left **Lisbon** on **8 July 1497** with **four ships** (São Gabriel, São Rafael, Berrio + supply ship).
[150:4:3] - Route: **Lisbon – Cape of Good Hope – East African coast – Calicut**.
[150:4:4] - Guided from **Malindi** to Calicut by the trader **Abdul Majid**; the pilot was a **Gujarati** (the old link with Arab navigator Ahmad ibn Majid is doubted).
[150:4:5] - Reached **Calicut (Kozhikode)**, western (Malabar) coast, on **20 May 1498**, landing at **Kappad** near Calicut.
[150:4:6] - Welcomed by the **Zamorin (Samuthiri)**, Hindu ruler of Calicut.
[150:4:7] - Arrival ships: **São Gabriel** (Vasco), **São Rafael** (Paulo da Gama), **Berrio** (Nicolau Coelho).
[150:4:8] - Back in Portugal 1499; spices sold at **60 times** the cost.
[150:4:9] APPSC-GS 2019 key: Vasco da Gama reached Calicut on 20 May 1498.
[150:4:10] # Three Voyages
[150:4:11] TABLE head: Voyage | Outcome
  [150:4:11.0] 1st (1497-99) | Calicut; Zamorin's welcome
  [150:4:11.1] 2nd (1502) | Zamorin refused trade; war; Calicut occupied; Portuguese settled at Kochi
  [150:4:11.2] 3rd (1524) | Came as Viceroy; died at Kochi the same year

## SUB 0 | European Companies – Formation, Charter, First Factory, Headquarters
[151:0:0] # Master Table

  [151:0:1.0] Portuguese | Arrival 1498 (no company) | State-controlled (Estado da India) | Calicut 1500 (Cabral) | Cochin, then Goa

[151:0:2] # Two Orders – Do Not Mix
[151:0:3] TABLE head: Order of | Sequence
  [151:0:3.0] Arrival | Portuguese → Dutch → English → Danes → French
  [151:0:3.1] Company formation | Portuguese (1498) → English 1600 → Dutch 1602 → Danes 1616 → French 1664
[151:0:4] English-before-Dutch is the order of FORMATION; Dutch-before-English is the order of ARRIVAL. The Dutch company is 1602, not 1592.
[151:0:5] # English Charter of 1600
[151:0:6] - Granted by **Elizabeth I** on **31 December 1600**.
[151:0:7] - **15-year monopoly** of East Indies trade between the **Cape of Good Hope** and the **Magellan Straits**; later made perpetual.
[151:0:8] - **1707**: rival English companies merged into the "**United Company of Merchants**".
[151:0:9] APPSC 2024 key: the 1600 Charter gave **exclusive trading rights** – not territorial or legislative powers. APPSC-G2 2025 asked the EIC year thrice: **1600**.
[151:0:10] First joint-stock company for India = English EIC (London, 1600); Dutch company 1602 – answer Dutch only when English is not an option.
[151:0:11] # Why the British Won
[151:0:12] - EIC was a **private joint-stock company** with steady finance; used rivalries among Indian rulers.
[151:0:13] - Portuguese naval monopoly was ended by the rise of the **Dutch and English**.
[151:0:14] - The Portuguese and French ventures were **state-controlled**; the English and Dutch were **merchant companies** with royal or state charters.
[151:0:15] # Company Profiles at a Glance
[151:0:16] - **Dutch (VOC)**: strongest in the spice islands; on the Coromandel coast held **Masulipatnam, Pulicat, Nagapatnam, Bheemunipatnam**; beaten by Travancore (1741) and by the English (1759).
[151:0:17] - **Danes**: small trading power; bases at **Tranquebar** (Tamil coast) and **Serampore** (Bengal); sold both to the British in 1845.
[151:0:18] - **French**: last to arrive; HQ **Pondicherry**; lost the struggle for power to the English at **Wandiwash (1760)**.
[151:0:19] - **English**: began at **Surat**, then built the three Presidency towns – **Madras (1639)**, **Bombay (1668)** and **Calcutta (1690-1700)**.

=== NOTES 1:152 | Portuguese in India – Almeida's Blue Water Policy, Albuquerque, Estado Da India, Cartaz System, Decline
## SUB 0 | The Portuguese in India – Overview
[152:0:0] - Portuguese = **first Europeans** to set up sea trade centres in India; **first to come, last to leave** (1961).
[152:0:1] - Their state in Asia was the **Estado da India**, with HQ first at **Cochin** and later **Goa**.
[152:0:2] - **Goa (1510)** = first European territory in India since **Alexander**.
[152:0:3] - New World crops brought by them reshaped Indian cuisine.
## SUB 1 | Viceroys and Governors – Policy and Key Acts
[152:1:0] # In Order
[152:1:1] TABLE head: Governor | Years | Title | Key act / policy
  [152:1:1.0] Francisco de Almeida | 1505-09 | First Viceroy | Blue Water Policy; won the Battle of Diu (1509)
  [152:1:1.1] Afonso de Albuquerque | 1509-15 | Real founder of Portuguese power | Conquest of Goa (1510) from Bijapur; marriage policy (mixed marriages); abolished Sati in his territories
  [152:1:1.2] Nino (Nuno) da Cunha | 1529-38 | Governor | HQ shift Cochin → Goa (1530); Bassein (1534) and Diu fort (1535) from Bahadur Shah
[152:1:2] # Blue Water Policy
[152:1:3] - Introduced by **Almeida**: naval mastery of the **Indian Ocean**, not inland territory.
[152:1:4] - Made the Portuguese the strongest naval power in the Indian Ocean after Diu (1509).
[152:1:5] Blue Water Policy = Almeida (APPSC-G2 2025, G2 Screening 2024). Albuquerque REVERSED it – he went for territorial conquest (Goa, Malacca, Hormuz).
[152:1:6] Almeida | First Viceroy | Blue Water Policy
Albuquerque | Real founder | Goa 1510, marriage policy
## SUB 2 | Cartaz System
[152:2:0] # How It Worked
[152:2:1] - 1. Every ship sailing the **Indian Ocean** had to buy a **Cartaz** (pass).
[152:2:2] - 2. Backed by **Caes** (convoy system) and **customs duties**.
[152:2:3] - 3. A ship without a pass was **confiscated or sunk**.
[152:2:4] - 4. Also used in the **Bay of Bengal**.
[152:2:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [152:2:5.0] Cartaz | Naval trade licence (pass) for Indian Ocean ships
  [152:2:5.1] Caes | Convoy system backing the Cartaz
  [152:2:5.2] Customs duties | Third tool of the maritime monopoly
[152:2:6] APPSC-GS 2018: Cartaz = naval trade licence (pass).
[152:2:7] - Portuguese **monopoly and heavy duties** caused constant conflict with the **Zamorin of Calicut**.
