## SUB 2 | Entry of the European Powers – Statement Checks
[2:0] - **Goa** was captured by **Albuquerque** in **1510** from the **Sultan of Bijapur** (with Vijayanagara help) – "Goa in 1499" is wrong.
[2:1] - **English:** first factory in **South India at Masulipatnam**; first in **eastern India in Orissa (1633)** – both correct.
[2:2] - **French (Dupleix's time) took Madras in 1746** (La Bourdonnais) – accepted as correct.
[2:3] - **Dutch east-coast factories** stood on land granted by **Golconda** and the **Vijayanagara/Chandragiri** rulers (Pulicat, with Queen Eraivi's permission, 1609-10) – **not the Gajapatis**, who had ended in the 16th century.
[2:4] - **Madras (1639)** was leased from a **representative of the Vijayanagara empire** (Damarla Venkatadri Nayaka under the Chandragiri Raja).

## SUB 3 | European Companies – Charters and Joint-Stock Firsts
[3:0] TABLE head: Power | Company | Nature
  [3:0.0] Portuguese | no company – Estado da India | state (Crown) enterprise
  [3:0.1] English | EIC, 1600 | private joint-stock, royal charter of exclusive trading rights
  [3:0.2] Dutch | VOC, 1602 – States-General merged several companies | joint-stock; could wage war, make treaties, take territory, build forts
  [3:0.3] Danes | 1616 | –
  [3:0.4] French | Compagnie des Indes Orientales, 1664 (Colbert, Louis XIV) | state-controlled; 50-year monopoly
[3:1] - **First joint-stock company to trade with India:** the English EIC (1600); the Dutch VOC (1602) is the answer when the English are not among the options (IAS 1994).
[3:2] - **Why the English won:** a private joint-stock company with steady finance, naval strength, and use of Indian rulers' rivalries.
[3:3] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.67, p.69, p.74; PMF Modern History Part 2 p.1, p.4; old app notes row 151 (sub 0)
