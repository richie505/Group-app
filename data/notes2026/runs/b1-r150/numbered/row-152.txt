## SUB 0 | Viceroys and Governors – Almeida's Blue Water Policy, Albuquerque, da Cunha
[0:0] TABLE head: Governor | Years | Title | Key acts
  [0:0.0] Francisco de Almeida | 1505-09 | first Governor and Viceroy (appointed by Manuel I) | Blue Water Policy; forts at Anjediva, Cannanore (Cochin); Battle of Diu 1509 – beat Gujarat + Egyptian Mamluks + Zamorin
  [0:0.1] Afonso de Albuquerque | 1509-15 | real founder of Portuguese power | took Goa (1510) from Bijapur; Malacca 1511, Hormuz 1515; mixed marriages; abolished sati; enforced the cartaz permit system
  [0:0.2] Nino (Nuno) da Cunha | 1529-38 | Governor | HQ Cochin → Goa (1530); Bassein 1534 and Diu from Bahadur Shah; settlers in Bengal (Hooghly)
[0:1] - **Blue Water Policy** = **Portuguese**, introduced by **Almeida**: mastery of the Indian Ocean ("As long as you may be powerful at sea you will hold India as yours"), trade not land – not Albuquerque, Cabral or da Cunha.
[0:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Albuquerque **reversed** it with territorial conquest; Spectrum calls the Blue Water Policy the "cartaze system", but the pass system is a separate tool (see Cartaz).
[0:3] - **Real founder** = **Albuquerque**, not Vasco da Gama, Dias, Almeida or Drake; **first Viceroy** = **Almeida**, not Albuquerque.
[0:4] - **Goa (1510)** = "the first bit of Indian territory under the Europeans since Alexander the Great".
[0:5] - **Pairs:** Blue Water Policy – Portuguese naval supremacy; Market Policy – **Alauddin Khalji**; Swadeshi – boycott; Home Rule – self-government.

## SUB 2 | Cartaz System – Naval Trade Licence
[2:0] - **Cartaz** = a **naval trade licence / pass** that Asian ships had to buy from the Portuguese to sail the Indian Ocean; begun by **Vasco da Gama (1502)**, systematised by **Albuquerque**.
[2:1] - A ship **without a cartaz was confiscated** and its crew punished; monopoly goods (pepper, horses, ginger, cinnamon …) could not be loaded.
[2:2] - Backed by the **cafila** (convoy of local ships under a Portuguese fleet; older app notes wrote "Caes") and **customs duties**.
[2:3] - Monopoly and duties caused constant war with the **Zamorin of Calicut**.
[2:4] > Distractors in Polytechnic 2020: "Marcent", "Shellback", "Pollywog" are not Portuguese systems.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 1 p.101; Spectrum Modern India p.58
## SUB 3 | Hooghly, Piracy and the Decline
[3:0] - **Hooghly** was the **Portuguese** base for **piracy** (with Arakanese pirates) and slave-raiding in the **Bay of Bengal**.
[3:1] - **1632:** **Shah Jahan** had Bengal governor **Qasim Khan** besiege and take Hooghly (June-Sept 1632).
[3:2] - **Shaista Khan** (Aurangzeb's Bengal governor) took **Sondip and Chittagong** and crushed the Arakanese pirates.
[3:3] - **Goa Inquisition** (from 1560): state machinery used for conversions and against religious dissent; Hindus too were persecuted.
[3:4] - **Causes of decline:** weak, corrupt successors after Albuquerque; **religious intolerance** and forced conversions (Goa Inquisition); piracy and dishonest trade; **Portugal united with Spain (1580)**; discovery of **Brazil**; Dutch and English competition.
[3:5] - **Losses:** Surat 1612 (English); **Hormuz 1622**; Hooghly 1632; **Nagapattinam 1658 and Cochin / Malabar forts 1663** (Dutch); Salsette-Bassein 1739 (Marathas).
[3:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.63-66; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.27; PMF Modern History Part 1 p.102, p.106
## SUB 4 | Contributions – Crops, Printing, Francis Xavier
[4:0] - **New World crops brought by the Portuguese:** **tobacco, potato, chilli, tomato, pineapple, papaya, guava, sapota, cashew, maize, groundnut**, lady's finger.
[4:1] - **Telugu soil:** the **tomato** (an American plant); not banana, apple or orange.
[4:2] - **Chilli** came with the Portuguese; opium, coffee and betel leaf did not.
[4:3] - **NOT Portuguese introductions:** **tea, coffee, rubber**; **wheat** is not a New World crop.
[4:4] - **Calico:** cotton cloth of **Calicut**, so called in Europe.
[4:5] - **Printing press:** Goa **1556**; Bible printed in Kannada and Malayalam.
[4:6] - **St. Francis Xavier:** a **co-founder of the Jesuit Order (1534)**; reached Goa 1542; **died on Shangchuan island, China (1552)** – not in Goa – but his body lies in the **Basilica of Bom Jesus, Goa**; feast **3 December** every year.
[4:7] > UPSC 2021: statements 1 and 3 ✓, "died in Goa" ✗. UPSC 2025: papaya, pineapple and guava – all three Portuguese introductions.

## SUB 5 | First to Come, Last to Leave – Goa 1961 and the Andhra Coast
[5:0] - **After 15 August 1947** Goa, Daman and Diu stayed **Portuguese**; the freedom struggle continued against **Portugal**.
[5:1] - **Operation Vijay, 19 December 1961** freed them; Goa statehood **30 May 1987**.
[5:2] - **Estado da India** = the Portuguese state in Asia (HQ Cochin, then Goa).
[5:3] - **Telugu name:** "**Budata Keechu**"; **Petapoli** = **Nizampatnam**; the **1669-70 Machilipatnam factory** allowed by **Abdullah Qutb Shah** was **French**.
[5:4] - **Andhra coast:** posts at Masulipatnam, Pulicat, San Thome; Pulicat lost to the Dutch (1610); influence faded by the mid-17th century.
