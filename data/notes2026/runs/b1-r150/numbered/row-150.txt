[0:5] - **Sea route via the Cape of Good Hope** = Vasco da Gama (not Columbus, Cabot or Amundsen).
[0:6] - **Contemporaries in 1498:** Delhi – **Sikandar Lodi**; Vijayanagara – **Immadi Narasimha Raya** (Saluva); Portugal – Manuel I.

## SUB 2 | Explorers and Motives of European Expansion
[2:0] # Why Europe sought a sea route
[2:1] - **1453:** Ottoman Sultan **Mehmed II** took **Constantinople**, closing the overland route; the Asian trade was controlled by **Arabs**, and **Venice and Genoa** distributed the goods in Europe.
[2:2] - **Motives:** spices (pepper, cinnamon, cardamom, ginger), silk and Indian textiles; profit; breaking the **Arab (Muslim) monopoly**; spreading **Christianity** (Prester John legend); rivalry of Portugal, Spain and later Holland, England, France. "Roman imperial administration" is NOT a motive.
[2:3] - **Aids:** **Prince Henry the Navigator** (naval school; papal bull of 1454); the **caravel** (ship) and **astrolabe**.
[2:4] - **"Bear in mind that the commerce of India is the commerce of the world"** – **Peter the Great** of Russia, about **18th-century** India (APPSC G2 2017); not da Gama, Clive or Dupleix.
[2:5] # Explorers
[2:6] TABLE head: Explorer | Sponsor | Year | Achievement
  [2:6.0] Bartholomew Dias | Portugal | 1487-88 | rounded the Cape of Good Hope ("Cape of Storms", renamed by King John II)
  [2:6.1] Christopher Columbus | Spain | 1492 | the Americas ("West Indies")
  [2:6.2] John Cabot | England | 1497 | North America
  [2:6.3] Vasco da Gama | Portugal | 1498 | sea route to India
  [2:6.4] Pedro Alvares Cabral | Portugal | 1500 | Brazil; factory at Calicut
  [2:6.5] Ferdinand Magellan | Spain | 1519-22 | first circumnavigation of the globe (completed by Elcano after Magellan's death)
[2:7] - **Dividing the world:** papal bull **Inter caetera (1493**, Alexander VI) and **Treaty of Tordesillas (1494)** – line 370 leagues west of Cape Verde; **east → Portugal, west → Spain**.
[2:8] - **New World civilisations, north → south:** **Aztec** (central Mexico) → **Maya** (Yucatan, Central America) → **Muisca** (Colombia) → **Inca** (Peru).

## SUB 3 | World History I – American and French Revolutions, Napoleon
[3:0] # American Revolution
[3:1] - **Cause:** colonists refused taxes voted by a Parliament where they had no seats – **"No taxation without representation"**; merchants **boycotted British goods**. It was colonists vs Britain, not settlers vs Native Americans.
[3:2] - **Stamp Act 1765** (repealed 1766) → **Stamp Act Congress, New York City, October 1765** (9 of the 13 colonies).
[3:3] - **Coercive (Intolerable) Acts 1774** made reconciliation virtually impossible; the earlier Stamp Act repeal does not explain that.
[3:4] - **First Continental Congress, Philadelphia, Sept 1774:** rejected **Galloway's plan** of union under British authority, drew up a **statement of grievances**, agreed the **Continental Association** (stop trade with England).
[3:5] # French Revolution
[3:6] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [3:6.0] June 1789 | Tennis Court Oath – Third Estate (National Assembly)
  [3:6.1] 1791 | Olympe de Gouges' Declaration of the Rights of Woman and the Female Citizen
  [3:6.2] 1792 | National Convention meets; monarchy abolished, republic
  [3:6.3] June 1793 – July 1794 | Jacobins (with the sans-culottes) purge the Girondins; Reign of Terror; ends with Robespierre's execution, July 1794
  [3:6.4] 1804 | Napoleon Emperor of the French (first emperor of France); Napoleonic Code
[3:7] - **Reign of Terror:** the **Law of Suspects (Sept 1793)** let anyone be arrested, not only the political class; the Jacobins were **anti-clerical** (de-Christianisation), not allies of the clergy.
[3:8] - **Olympe de Gouges:** politically active writer; "the nation is the **union of woman and man**"; not on the committee that drafted the Rights of Man (1789); opposed the Jacobins and was guillotined **1793**.
[3:9] # Napoleon
[3:10] - **Third Coalition (1805):** Britain, **Austria, Russia, Sweden** (Prussia joined 1806) against **France**.
[3:11] - **Austerlitz 1805** → Treaty of Pressburg; **Tilsit 1807** = treaties, not a battle; **Wagram 1809 → Treaty of Schönbrunn**.

## SUB 4 | World History II – Industrial, Russian and Chinese Revolutions; Ancient Firsts
[4:0] # Industrial Revolution (Britain first)
[4:1] - **Why Britain first:** **coal and iron**, **steam power**, regular **raw materials** (colonies), **navigable rivers/canals**, favourable social-political order, **capitalist** practice and scientific inventions; railways (1825-30) sustained rather than started it.
[4:2] - **Cotton inventions:** **Kay's flying shuttle 1733 → Hargreaves' spinning jenny 1764 → Arkwright's water frame 1769 → Crompton's mule 1779**.
[4:3] - **Factory system:** production under one roof in towns ruined the **guilds**; **merchant capitalists** gained control (not "production moved to the countryside").
[4:4] - **Impact:** cottage industry → **factory system**; **working-class movements**; overcrowded **towns** (not villages).
[4:5] # Russian Revolution, 1917
[4:6] - **Provisional Government (Feb/March 1917):** first headed by **Prince Lvov**, then by **Alexander Kerensky** (July-Oct 1917); overthrown in the **October (Bolshevik) Revolution**.
[4:7] - **Lenin** = father of the Revolution; Russia = first **Marxist** revolution.
[4:8] - **Bolsheviks** (Lenin): small, disciplined party; **Mensheviks**: party open to all, as in Germany.
[4:9] # Asia and other events
[4:10] - **Crimean War 1853-56:** **Russia vs Ottoman Turkey** (Britain, France, Sardinia on the Turkish side).
[4:11] - **Boxer Rebellion:** China, 1899-1901, peak **1900**.
[4:12] - **1911 Revolution (Xinhai):** ended the Qing; **Republic of China** (1912).
[4:13] # Ancient and Renaissance firsts
[4:14] - **First proper writing system:** **Sumerian** cuneiform (Mesopotamia), before Egyptian hieroglyphs; Indus script undeciphered.
[4:15] - **Mona Lisa** = **Leonardo da Vinci** (Italian Renaissance).
