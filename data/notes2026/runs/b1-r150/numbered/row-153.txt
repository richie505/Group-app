[1:1] - **Trading centres:** Nagapattinam, Chinsura, Machilipatnam, Surat, Broach, Agra, Cochin, Ahmedabad, Patna, Kasimbazar, Balasore – i.e. "all of the above".
[1:2] - **Bengal:** **Chinsurah** was Dutch; Bandel and Hooghly were Portuguese, **Serampore (Srirampur)** Danish.
[1:3] - **Kochi:** the Dutch took it from the Portuguese (**1663**), rebuilt the fort (**Fort Williams**, Fort Kochi); it passed to the **British in 1795** (1814 treaty).
[1:4] - **Mattancherry Palace** (Kochi) – Portuguese gift (c. 1555) to the Raja of Cochin, renovated by the Dutch → **"Dutch Palace"**.
[1:5] - **Dutch colony among Yanam, Mahe, Karaikal, Bheemili:** **Bheemili** – Yanam and Mahe were French, and Karaikal (Dutch 1645) became French in 1739.

## SUB 2 | VOC – Formation, Charter and Trade
[2:0] - **VOC (Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie):** **20 March 1602**, the **States-General** merged several companies; powers to **wage war, make treaties, acquire territory, build forts**. ("1592" in NextGen is a misprint.)
[2:1] - **Background:** **Philip II**'s ban on Lisbon; **Linschoten**'s *Itinerario*; **Cornelis de Houtman** reached **Sumatra and Bantam (1596)**.
[2:2] - **Asian HQ:** **Batavia (Jakarta)**; the spice islands came first, India was a cloth depot.
[2:3] - **Trade:** Coromandel textiles for spices; **indigo**, raw **silk**, cotton cloth, **saltpetre (Bihar)**, opium and rice; silk and porcelain imported.
[2:4] - **Andhra:** first Europeans in Andhra to **mint coins**; **Koringa** shipbuilding; Pulicat a hub of the **slave trade to Batavia**; network from **Machilipatnam to Bheemunipatnam** (Draksharama, Palakollu, Narsapur, Jagannathapuram).
[2:5] - **Industry (old app notes):** **Koringa** – shipbuilding; **Chittavalasa** – jute; Machilipatnam – handloom textiles; Pulicat did not make porcelain.
[2:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.67-68; PMF Modern History Part 2 p.1; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.29; NextGen modern India L2 Part 1 p.9; old app notes row 153 (sub 0, 4)
## SUB 3 | Battles and Decline – Colachel 1741, Bedara 1759
[3:0] TABLE head: Battle | Year | Sides | Result
  [3:0.0] Colachel | 1741 | Marthanda Varma (Travancore) vs Dutch (De Lannoy) | Dutch beaten – end of Dutch power in Kerala/Malabar
  [3:0.1] Bedara (Chinsurah / Hooghly) | Nov 1759 | English (Francis Forde, under Clive) vs Dutch | "a crushing blow to Dutch ambitions in India"
[3:1] - **Why the Dutch failed:** **English rivalry** (Amboyna 1623; **1667 compromise** – Dutch withdrew from India for Indonesia), the defeat at Bedara, and their priority on the **Indonesian spice trade**; by **1795** the English had expelled them – "the English forces made them leave India".
[3:2] - **Anglo-Dutch conferences of 1613 and 1615** failed to end the rivalry.
[3:3] - **Anglo-Dutch Treaty 1824:** remaining posts (Pulicat, Chinsura) to the British by **1825**; Dutch presence in India **1605-1825**.
