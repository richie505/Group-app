=== Stages, Configurations and Features – PSLV, GSLV, LVM3, SSLV
# Configurations
| Vehicle | Full form | Stages |
|---|---|---|
| SLV-3 | Satellite Launch Vehicle-3 | **4, all-solid**; 17 t, 22 m; 40 kg to LEO |
| ASLV | Augmented SLV | **5, all-solid**; 40 t, 24 m; 150 kg to LEO |
| PSLV | **Polar** Satellite Launch Vehicle | **4: solid–liquid–solid–liquid** (S139, Vikas, solid, twin-engine PS4); **no cryogenic** stage |
| GSLV Mk II | **Geosynchronous** SLV | **3: one solid + one earth-storable liquid (Vikas) + one cryogenic (CE-7.5)** + **4 liquid strap-ons** |
| LVM3 (ex-GSLV Mk III, "Bahubali") | Launch Vehicle Mark-3 | **3**: **2 S200 solid strap-ons** + **L110 liquid core** + **C25 cryogenic** (CE-20) |
| SSLV | **Small** SLV | **3 solid stages** + liquid **Velocity Trimming Module (VTM)** |
- **PSLV:** **"workhorse" of ISRO**; third-generation vehicle; **first Indian vehicle with liquid stages**; ~1,750 kg to SSO; built to launch **IRS (earth-resources)** satellites into sun-synchronous polar orbit.
- **PSLV variants:** CA (Core Alone) 0 strap-ons, DL 2, **QL 4**, XL 6 (extended).
- **GSLV:** fourth-generation vehicle; first flight GSLV-D1 with **GSAT-1** (18 Apr 2001); 2–2.5 t communication / navigation satellites to **GTO**; launched **INSAT-3D/3DR, NVS** satellites.
- **LVM3:** a **GSLV-class** vehicle (not built by DRDO); ~4 t to GTO, ~8 t to LEO; it launched **Chandrayaan-2 and -3**, **not** the Mars Orbiter Mission (PSLV).
- **LVM3 – heaviest Indian rocket:** ~**640 t** lift-off mass, 43 m; first orbital flight **GSLV Mk III-D1** (5 Jun 2017, GSAT-19), called the ISRO "**game changer**" and India's "heavy rocket" of 2017; it ended dependence on foreign launchers for **4-tonne** satellites.
- **LVM3 history:** **LVM3-X / CARE** suborbital test **18 Dec 2014**; operational since 2017.
- **Not operational now:** **SLV-3 and ASLV** (retired); PSLV, GSLV, LVM3, SSLV and sounding rockets are in use.
# SSLV
- **Design:** **500 kg to 500 km LEO** (10–500 kg class; ~300 kg to SSO); **three solid stages** + VTM, ~**120 t** lift-off, **34 m** – shorter than PSLV and GSLV; low cost, **launch-on-demand**, minimal infrastructure; can be assembled **vertically and horizontally**.
- **Flights:** **SSLV-D1** (Aug 2022) failed to place EOS-02 in orbit; **SSLV-D2 (10 Feb 2023)** – first successful SSLV launch; D3 (Aug 2024).
- **HAL** won the **Rs 511 crore** Transfer of Technology to build SSLV – first full ToT of an ISRO launch vehicle.
- **Second spaceport:** **Kulasekarapattinam** (Tamil Nadu) for SSLV, avoiding the dog-leg round Sri Lanka.
> ⚠ Trap: "SSLV has two solid stages" is WRONG – it has three (APPSC G1); "GSLV Mk III is four-staged S-L-S-L" is WRONG – S-L-S-L is PSLV (UPSC 2018).

=== Chronology and Early Vehicles – SLV-3 and ASLV
# Order of development
- **Order:** **SLV-3 → ASLV → PSLV → GSLV → LVM3 → SSLV** (first flights 1979, 1987, 1993, 2001, 2014, 2022).
| Vehicle | First flight | First (successful) payload |
|---|---|---|
| SLV-3 | 10 Aug 1979 (E1, partly successful) | **18 Jul 1980 (E2): Rohini RS-1** – India the 6th nation to orbit a satellite with its own rocket |
| ASLV | 24 Mar 1987 (D1, failed; D2 1988 failed) | **D3, 20 May 1992: SROSS-C**; D4 1994: SROSS-C2 |
| PSLV | 20 Sep 1993 (D1, failed) | 15 Oct 1994 (D2): **IRS-P2** |
| GSLV | 18 Apr 2001 (D1) | **GSAT-1** |
| LVM3 | 18 Dec 2014 (LVM3-X, suborbital) | CARE; 2017 D1: GSAT-19 |
| SSLV | Aug 2022 (D1, failed) | D2, Feb 2023 |
- **SLV-3:** project led by **A.P.J. Abdul Kalam**; Rohini launches 1980, 1981, 1983.
- **Match:** SLV-3 – **Rohini**; ASLV – **SROSS-C**; PSLV – **Chandrayaan-1**; GSLV – **EDUSAT** (GSLV-F01, 2004).
- **Mixed chronology:** Bangladesh formed **1971** → Pokhran-I **May 1974** → Sikkim the 22nd state **May 1975** → SLV-3 launch **1979/1980**.
- **ISRO chronology:** LVM3-X **Dec 2014** → ASTROSAT **Sep 2015** → IRNSS-1I **Apr 2018**.

=== Sounding Rockets and TERLS Thumba
- **TERLS** (Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station), near **Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala** – **India's first rocket launching station**; chosen because the **geomagnetic (magnetic) equator** passes through it.
- **First launch:** American **Nike-Apache**, **21 Nov 1963** – start of the Indian space programme; early imported rockets **M-100** (Russia) and **Centaure** (France).
- **Rohini Sounding Rockets (RSR):** ISRO's own series (programme consolidated 1975); used for **atmospheric research** and to qualify sub-systems of launch vehicles; sub-orbital, ~70–600 km.
| Rocket | Fact |
|---|---|
| **RH-200** | Two-stage, ~70–80 km, meteorology; **200th consecutive successful launch from Thumba, 23 Nov 2022** |
| RH-300 Mk-II | Single-stage, ~116–160 km |
| RH-560 Mk-III | Two-stage, ~470–550 km, ionosphere |
- **Number = diameter in mm**; SLV, ASLV and PSLV are orbital launch vehicles, not sounding rockets.
> Thumba = magnetic equator (sounding rockets); Sriharikota = east coast (orbital launches).

=== Orbits and Launch Physics
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **LEO** | **Low Earth Orbit** (up to ~2,000 km) |
| SSO | Sun-synchronous, near-polar; passes a place at the **same local solar time** – PSLV's speciality |
| **GTO** | **Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit**: elliptical, apogee near 36,000 km – GSLV, LVM3 |
| GEO | Geostationary orbit over the equator; satellite appears **fixed in the sky** (reached via GSLV, not PSLV's polar orbits) |
| Apogee / perigee | **Farthest / nearest** point of an orbit |
| Strap-on booster | Extra motors fixed to the core for lift-off thrust |
- **Eastward launch:** satellites are launched **eastward** to use the Earth's **west-to-east rotation** (a free speed boost); sites **nearer the equator** gain most. The boost does **not** give "escape velocity" – an orbiting satellite never reaches escape velocity.
- **Not always east:** polar / SSO launches go **southward** (from Sriharikota a **dog-leg around Sri Lanka**).
- **Sriharikota** (SDSC-SHAR, Andhra Pradesh) on the **east coast**: rockets fly over the sea; all Indian orbital launches, incl. private ones, go from here.

=== Cryogenic Stages of the Launch Vehicles
- **Early GSLVs** flew **Russian KVD-1** cryogenic stages; technology transfer was refused in the 1990s, so India built its own. **First indigenous cryogenic success: GSLV-D5 (Jan 2014)** – not GSLV-D1 (2001).
| Engine / type | Propellant | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| CE-7.5 (cryogenic) | LH2 + LOX | GSLV Mk II upper stage |
| **CE-20** (cryogenic) | LH2 + LOX | **C25 stage of LVM3** |
| SCE-200 / SE2000 (semi-cryogenic) | **Refined kerosene + LOX** | LVM3 upgrade, NGLV |
| Vikas (earth-storable) | UH25 + N2O4 | PSLV 2nd, GSLV 2nd stage |
| Solid | HTPB | GSLV 1st stage |
- **"CE-25 / C25" news (2017):** the **C25** cryogenic upper stage with the CE-20, India's first high-thrust indigenous cryogenic engine, was ground-tested for **GSLV Mk III**.
- **Engine testing:** **IPRC Mahendragiri (Tamil Nadu)**; LPSC Valiamala does design.
