=== Cryogenic Engines and Cryogenics
# What a cryogenic engine is
- **Cryogenics:** Greek **'Kryos'** (frost) + 'genic' (to produce); temperatures below about **-150°C**; liquid nitrogen, hydrogen and oxygen are the usual cryogenic liquids – the technology of obtaining/using liquid O2 or H2 at industrial level is **cryogenics**.
- **Propellants:** **liquid hydrogen (LH2, -253°C) as fuel** and **liquid oxygen (LOX, -183°C) as oxidiser** – not chlorine, fluorine, sulphur, helium or nitrogen; exhaust is only **water vapour**; specific impulse ~**450 s** (highest).
- **Use:** **rocket technology** – the upper stages of launch vehicles and the **space shuttle**; **not** submarine propulsion, frost-free refrigerators, Boeing aircraft or rail engines.
- **Why not missiles:** LH2 density only **0.071 g/ml** – huge tanks, cannot be stored fuelled for long.
- **Other cryogenic use:** **liquid nitrogen (-196°C)** stores **bull semen** for artificial insemination.
# India's cryogenic story
- **Glavkosmos deal (1991)** → US **MTCR** sanctions (**1992**) stopped technology transfer → Russia supplied **7 engines** (KVD-1).
- **CUSP** (Cryogenic Upper Stage Project) began **1994**; full stage test **2006**; **GSLV-D5** flew the indigenous **CE-7.5** on **5 Jan 2014**; INSAT-3DR (Sep 2016) went up on GSLV-F05 with the **indigenous cryogenic** stage.
- **India = 6th** country with cryogenic technology (after **US, Russia, France, Japan, China**) – not "the only one after USA, Russia and China".

=== Rocket Principle and Propellants
- **Principle:** a rocket works on **Newton's third law** (action–reaction; conservation of momentum) – it needs no air to push against.
- **Jet engine vs rocket:** a jet engine takes **oxygen from the surrounding air**, so it cannot work in space; a rocket carries its **own fuel and oxidiser** – as a **liquid (LOX) or solid oxidiser, not as gas**.
- **Propellant** = fuel + oxidiser; liquid hydrogen, liquid oxygen, hydrazine and kerosene (RP-1) are all used in rockets.
| Category | Example | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Solid | HTPB (S139, S200) | High initial thrust; cannot be throttled or stopped |
| Earth-storable liquid | UH25 + N2O4; hydrazine, MMH, UDMH | Throttleable, restartable |
| Hypergolic | Hydrazine family + N2O4 | Ignite on contact; spacecraft manoeuvring; highly toxic |
| Cryogenic | LH2 + LOX | Most efficient |
| Semi-cryogenic | Refined kerosene + LOX | Dense, cheaper, non-toxic |
| Hybrid | Solid fuel + liquid oxidiser | Can be stopped/restarted |
- **Ammonium perchlorate:** the **oxidiser** in solid rocket propellant, with **aluminium** powder (fuel) and HTPB binder.

=== Ramjet, Scramjet and Reusable Launch Vehicles
| Engine | Combustion airflow | Speed |
|---|---|---|
| Ramjet | **Subsonic** | Above Mach 2 |
| **Scramjet** (Supersonic Combustion Ramjet) | **Supersonic** | Mach 5–6+ |
- **Scramjet fuel:** ISRO's scramjet burns **hydrogen** with **oxygen from the atmosphere** – less oxidiser carried; needs a rocket booster to reach ignition speed.
- **ATV** (ISRO): scramjet flight test **Aug 2016**, Sriharikota; India **4th** (after US, Russia, China). **HSTDV** = **DRDO**; actively cooled combustor ran **20 minutes** on endothermic fuel (May 2026).
- **RLV-TD "Pushpak":** winged, reusable; HEX 2016, landing tests LEX 2023–24; aim **TSTO**; cost cut up to **80%**; needs a reusable **Thermal Protection System**.

=== Electric Propulsion and POEM
- **300 mN Stationary Plasma Thruster:** **Hall-type, xenon**; **1,000-hour** life test; for **communication satellites**, not boosters.
- **POEM** = **PSLV Orbital Experimental Module**: spent **PS4** stage as an orbital platform for start-up and academic payloads.
- **RUDRA 0.3 HPGP:** a **green monopropellant** thruster of **Bellatrix Aerospace** flew on **POEM-3** of **PSLV-C58** (1 Jan 2024).
