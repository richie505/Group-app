## SUB 0 | Sounding Rockets and Terls Thumba
[35:0:0] - **TERLS** (Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station), near **Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala**; work began 1962.
[35:0:1] - Chosen because the **geomagnetic (magnetic) equator** passes through it - suits sounding-rocket experiments.
[35:0:2] - First launch: American **Nike-Apache**, **21 Nov 1963** - start of the Indian space programme.
[35:0:3] - Early imported rockets: **M-100** (Russia), **Centaure** (France); indigenous rockets from **1965**.
[35:0:4] - **Rohini Sounding Rocket (RSR)** programme consolidated in **1975**.
[35:0:5] - RH rockets: atmospheric research + testing/qualifying subsystems for larger launch vehicles.
[35:0:6] - Number = **diameter in mm** (**RH-200, RH-300, RH-560**); three operational versions: payload **8-100 kg**, apogee **80-475 km**.
[35:0:7] - **RH-200**: **200th consecutive successful launch** from Thumba, **Nov 2022**.
[35:0:8] Thumba = magnetic equator (sounding rockets); Sriharikota = east coast (orbital launches).

## SUB 4 | Orbit Terms and Launch Physics
[35:4:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [35:4:0.0] LEO | Low Earth Orbit (up to ~2,000 km)
  [35:4:0.1] SSO | Sun-synchronous, near-polar; passes a place at the same local solar time (same imaging conditions) - PSLV's speciality
  [35:4:0.2] GTO | Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit: elliptical, apogee near 36,000 km - GSLV, LVM3
  [35:4:0.3] GEO | GSO over the equator; fixed in the sky
  [35:4:0.4] Apogee / perigee | Farthest / nearest point of an orbit
  [35:4:0.5] Strap-on booster | Extra motors fixed to the core for lift-off thrust
[35:4:1] - **Eastward** launches gain Earth's rotational speed and save fuel; sites **nearer the equator** gain most.
[35:4:2] - Sriharikota on the **east coast**: rockets fly over the sea, away from people.
[35:4:3] - Polar/SSO launches go **southward**; from Sriharikota a **dog-leg around Sri Lanka** is needed - hence **Kulasekarapattinam** (Tamil Nadu) for SSLV (foundation **27 Aug 2025**).
[35:4:4] - All orbital launches, incl. private **Agnibaan**, are from **Sriharikota** (AP); Chandrayaan-1 was launched from AP.

## SUB 7 | Records and Landmarks
[35:7:0] - **100th SHAR launch**: GSLV-F15/NVS-02, **29 Jan 2025** (17th GSLV flight); **105th** PSLV-C62; **107th** GSLV-F17 (19th GSLV flight).
[35:7:1] - **Heaviest payload from Indian soil**: BlueBird Block-2, 6,100 kg, LVM3-M6; also the largest commercial communication satellite in LEO (**223 m2** array). LVM3 has flown **6** missions.
[35:7:2] - Older record: **INSAT-4CR**, 2,130 kg, GSLV-F04, 2007.
[35:7:3] - PSLV setbacks: **PSLV-C61/EOS-09** (18 May 2025) and **PSLV-C62** (64th PSLV flight) both failed at the **third stage**; no ISRO launches in Q1 2026.
[35:7:4] - Heavy-lift "**game changer**": LVM3 ended dependence on foreign launchers for **4-tonne** satellites to GTO.
[35:7:5] - **49% automatic FDI** in launch vehicles and spaceports (Jul 2026). ISSAR 2025: 315 launches worldwide, 4,651 objects placed in orbit.

## SUB 1 | Cryogenic Technology
[36:1:0] - **Cryogenics**: Greek **'Kryos'** (frost); below about **-150°C**; uses liquid nitrogen, hydrogen, oxygen.
[36:1:1] - Fuel **LH2 (-253°C)**, oxidiser **LOX (-183°C)**; exhaust only **water vapour**; Isp ~**450 s** (highest).
[36:1:2] - LH2 density **0.071 g/ml**: huge tanks, cannot be stored long, so **unfit for military missiles**.
[36:1:3] - Used in **rocket upper stages** and the **space shuttle** (Discovery: LH2 + LOX tanks).
[36:1:4] - **Glavkosmos deal (1991)** → US **MTCR** sanctions (**1992**) stopped technology transfer → Russia supplied **7 engines** only.
[36:1:5] - **CUSP** (Cryogenic Upper Stage Project): **1994**, **Rs 300 crore**; full stage test **2006**; **GSLV-D5** flew the indigenous CE-7.5 on **5 Jan 2014**.
[36:1:6] - India = **6th** country (after US, Russia, France, Japan, China).
[36:1:7] India is NOT 4th in cryogenic technology - Europe and Japan came earlier.
