## SUB 0 | Ai – Meaning, Pioneers and Types
[16:0:0] - **AI**: machines copying thinking functions of human intelligence (see, understand, respond).
[16:0:1] # Pioneers
[16:0:2] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [16:0:2.0] Father of AI | John McCarthy – coined the term "artificial intelligence"
  [16:0:2.1] Event of the term | Dartmouth summer workshop (1956), proposed in 1955 by McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester and Shannon
  [16:0:2.2] Turing test | Alan Turing, paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (1950, journal *Mind*); original name "the imitation game"; opens with "Can machines think?"
[16:0:3] # Types of AI
[16:0:4] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [16:0:4.0] Narrow / Weak AI (ANI) | Made for one task (Siri, Alexa, Netflix recommendations); cannot move across fields
  [16:0:4.1] General / Strong AI (AGI) | Human-level reasoning across domains; not yet achieved
  [16:0:4.2] Artificial Superintelligence | Hypothetical; would beat humans in every way
## SUB 1 | Key Concepts and Hardware
[16:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [16:1:0.0] Machine learning | A branch (subset) of AI; learns patterns from data without explicit programming; improves with experience. Types: supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement
  [16:1:0.1] Deep learning | Subset of ML using multi-layered neural networks (self-driving cars, ChatGPT)
  [16:1:0.2] Random forest | Group of decision trees; reduces overfitting
  [16:1:0.3] NLP | Machines understand and generate human language (chatbots, translation)
  [16:1:0.4] Computer vision | Interprets images (facial recognition)
  [16:1:0.5] Generative AI | Creates new text, images, music
  [16:1:0.6] LLMs | General-purpose models trained on huge data; do many language tasks without task-specific retraining
  [16:1:0.7] Recommender systems | Suggest content from past behaviour (Netflix)
  [16:1:0.8] GPU | Massively parallel processing; workhorse of AI training
  [16:1:0.9] FPGA | Field Programmable Gate Array – IC re-settable after manufacturing; reconfigurable
  [16:1:0.10] TPU | Google's AI chip
[16:1:1] - **What AI can do well**: cut electricity use in industrial units, **disease diagnosis**, **text-to-speech** – not "meaningful short stories and songs" (keyed 1, 3 and 4).

## SUB 6 | Ethical Concerns and Challenges
[16:6:0] TABLE head: Concern | Point
  [16:6:0.0] Bias | Sources: data collection, algorithm design, human interpretation ("ability" is not a source); ML copies bias in training data
  [16:6:0.1] Health AI bias | Trained on urban/Western data, may miss India's social, genetic, demographic diversity (Aug 2026)
  [16:6:0.2] Deepfakes | Made with GANs, diffusion models, large neural networks
  [16:6:0.3] Privacy / facial recognition | Identification without consent, mass surveillance, predictive policing
  [16:6:0.4] Job displacement | Both manual and intellectual work
  [16:6:0.5] Explainability | Deep models are "black boxes" – decisions hard to justify
[16:6:1] - Challenges for India: **GERD ~0.64–0.70% of GDP**; GPU shortage and imported chips.
[16:6:2] - **Economic Survey 2025-26**: favour **small, application-specific models** and a state-built **"AI-OS"** modelled on **UPI and Aadhaar**.
[16:6:3] - **14 Aug 2026**: Supreme Court sought Centre's reply on lack of a legal framework for **high-risk AI in welfare delivery**.

## SUB 5 | Quantum Computing
[17:5:0] # Terms
[17:5:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [17:5:1.0] Qubit | Basic unit of quantum information; quantum version of the binary bit; two-state system (e.g. electron spin)
  [17:5:1.1] Superposition | Qubit can be 0, 1 or in between
  [17:5:1.2] Entanglement | Links qubits regardless of distance
  [17:5:1.3] Decoherence | Loss of quantum state through the environment = main barrier (9 Jul 2026)
  [17:5:1.4] Entanglement sudden death | Abrupt total loss of entanglement
  [17:5:1.5] Shor's algorithm | Can break public-key cryptography
  [17:5:1.6] Q-Day | Point when quantum computing (Shor's algorithm) can break public-key cryptography
[17:5:2] # Classical Bit vs Qubit
[17:5:3] TABLE head: Feature | Classical bit | Qubit
  [17:5:3.0] States | 0 or 1 | 0, 1 or both at once (superposition)
  [17:5:3.1] Linking | Independent bits | Entangled qubits share state at any distance
  [17:5:3.2] Weakness | Stable | Decoherence – environment destroys the state
  [17:5:3.3] Example | Transistor switch | Electron spin, photon (Jiuzhang), superconducting circuit (Sycamore)
[17:5:4] Qubit = quantum form of the classical bit; superposition + entanglement give quantum computers their power.
[17:5:5] # Applications
[17:5:6] - Quantum computing, **quantum communication**, **quantum sensing and metrology**, quantum materials and devices (the four thematic hubs of the National Quantum Mission).
[17:5:7] - Security: **post-quantum cryptography** migration – DST quantum-safe report (31 May 2026).
[17:5:8] - **Raman Research Institute** (16 Sep 2026): one well-timed local **"quantum flip"** can delay or stop entanglement sudden death.
[17:5:9] - **Microsoft Majorana 2**: raised qubit stability to **20–60 s** (2026).

=== NOTES 5:18 | Big Data, Cloud Computing, Edge Computing and Data Centres
## SUB 0 | Big Data
[18:0:0] - **Big data** = large volumes of **high-velocity, complex and variable** data (APPSC 2019 key).
[18:0:1] - Classic three Vs: **Volume, Velocity, Variety**; **Veracity** and **Value** added later.
[18:0:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [18:0:2.0] Data mining | Finding patterns in large datasets
  [18:0:2.1] Data warehouse | Cleaned, structured data for analysis
  [18:0:2.2] Data lake | Raw data in native format
  [18:0:2.3] Metadata | Data about data
## SUB 1 | Cloud Computing
[18:1:0] - Hosted services (storage, computing power) over the internet **on demand**.
[18:1:1] - Five features: **on-demand self-service, network access, metered use, elasticity** (resources grow/shrink with demand), **resource pooling** (shared resources).
[18:1:2] TABLE head: Model | User gets | Example
  [18:1:2.0] IaaS | Servers, storage, network | Amazon EC2
  [18:1:2.1] PaaS (Platform as a Service) | Platform to develop, deploy, run, manage apps | Google App Engine
  [18:1:2.2] SaaS | Ready software over the web; mobile access, customisable | Outlook, Gmail
[18:1:3] - Deployment: public, private, hybrid, community. Providers: AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud.
[18:1:4] TABLE head: Storage service | Company
  [18:1:4.0] SkyDrive (now OneDrive) | Microsoft
  [18:1:4.1] S3 | Amazon Web Services
  [18:1:4.2] Google Drive / iCloud / Dropbox | Google / Apple / Dropbox Inc.
[18:1:5] **"Hardware as a Service"** is not a separate layer (comes under IaaS); **spectrum allocation** is not a cloud service.
## SUB 2 | Edge, Fog, Serverless and Containers
[18:2:0] TABLE head: Model | Where | Feature
  [18:2:0.0] Cloud | Distant central data centres | Heavy storage, analytics
  [18:2:0.1] Fog | Between edge and cloud | Local aggregation
  [18:2:0.2] Edge | On/near device | Lowest latency; saves bandwidth; keeps private data local
[18:2:1] - **Virtualisation**: one server runs many VMs, **each with its own OS**.
[18:2:2] - **Containers** (Docker): lighter, **share the host OS kernel**.
[18:2:3] - **Serverless**: provider runs and scales servers, billing per use (AWS Lambda).

=== NOTES 5:19 | Blockchain, Distributed Ledger, Cryptocurrency and Cbdc (e-rupee)
## SUB 0 | Blockchain and Distributed Ledger – Terms
[19:0:0] - **Blockchain**: a **time-stamped** series of **immutable records** managed by a cluster of computers **not owned by any single entity** (APPSC 2018).
[19:0:1] - Records are **visible to stakeholders** without risk of alteration; database copies sit with the participants (UPSC 2020).
[19:0:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [19:0:2.0] Distributed ledger | Shared ledger copied across many nodes; one "single source of truth"; no central validator
  [19:0:2.1] Block | Transaction details + timestamp + hash of the previous block (not private keys)
  [19:0:2.2] Immutability | Data nearly impossible to change without consensus; no central authority changes it
  [19:0:2.3] Consensus | Rule by which nodes agree on valid transactions
[19:0:3] - **Hash and node:** a **hash** is a cryptographic fingerprint of a block (any change to the data changes it and reveals tampering); a **node** is a computer that holds a copy of the ledger.
## SUB 1 | Types and Consensus Mechanisms
[19:1:0] TABLE head: Public / permissionless | Private / permissioned
  [19:1:0.0] Anyone can join, read and validate (Bitcoin) | Only authorised entities join and validate (AP DigiVerify)
  [19:1:0.1] Proof of Work (PoW) | Proof of Stake (PoS)
  [19:1:0.2] Miners race to solve puzzles; far more energy | Validators picked by coins staked; lower energy
  [19:1:0.3] Bitcoin still uses PoW | Ethereum since The Merge (September 2022)
[19:1:1] Bitcoin = public + PoW; Ethereum = PoS since Sep 2022; AP DigiVerify = permissioned.
## SUB 2 | Cryptocurrency
[19:2:0] - **Cryptocurrency**: encrypted (cryptographic) **peer-to-peer** fund transfers without a central bank.
[19:2:1] - **Bitcoin** (**2009**): first cryptocurrency, by pseudonymous **Satoshi Nakamoto**; decentralised; **mining** validates transactions.
[19:2:2] - Bitcoins are **not tracked by central banks**; anyone with a Bitcoin address can send to and receive from any other address.
[19:2:3] - India: crypto is **not legal tender**; **VDA service providers** brought under **PMLA** in **March 2023**.
[19:2:4] - **VDA tax** (Finance Act 2022): **30%** on gains (Section 115BBH, from 1 Apr 2022); **1% TDS** on transfers (Section 194S, from 1 Jul 2022); losses cannot be set off.
## SUB 3 | Nfts, Smart Contracts, Defi – Web 3.0 Terms

  [19:3:0.0] Stablecoins | Pegged to fiat, commodities or crypto
  [19:3:0.1] NFT | Non-Fungible Token; unique, not exchangeable at equivalency; can represent physical assets
  [19:3:0.2] Smart contract | Self-executing code; cuts intermediaries
  [19:3:0.3] DeFi | Lending/trading via smart contracts without banks
