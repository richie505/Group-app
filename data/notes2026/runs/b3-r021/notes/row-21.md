=== Environmental Acts and Bodies – Years
| Act / body / plan | Year | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Wildlife (Protection) Act** | **1972** | Wildlife and habitats; regulates hunting; Schedules of species |
| **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act** | **1974** | **CPCB** set up under it (**1974**, statutory); SPCBs; later given Air Act functions too |
| Water Cess Act | 1977 | Cess on water use by industries |
| **Forest (Conservation) Act** | **1980** | Central approval to divert forest land |
| **Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act** | **1981** | Amended **1987** (stricter penalties, noise added) |
| **Environment (Protection) Act** | **1986** | Passed by Parliament in **May 1986** (in force 19 Nov 1986); **umbrella law**; enacted after the **Bhopal gas tragedy** (2-3 Dec 1984, methyl isocyanate, Union Carbide); under **Art. 253** to implement the **Stockholm Conference, 1972**; Sec. 3 powers; EIA, CRZ, ESZ, GEAC all rest on it |
| **Ganga Action Plan** | **1986** | Launched at Varanasi on **14 January 1986** (Central Ganga Authority set up 1985; some books give "first phase 1985") – **never 1982** |
| **Coastal Regulation Zone** notification | **1991** (revised 2011, 2019) | Under the EP Act; CRZ = land from the **High Tide Line up to 500 m** landward + the area between LTL and HTL |
| Environmental Protection Fund Rules | 2026 | Sec. 16 of the EP Act; pools fines collected under three Acts |
- **Abbreviation:** **EPA** = **Environmental Protection Agency** (the US federal body, 1970); in India "EPA" in notes usually means the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.
- **SPCB compensation powers:** **Sec. 33A Water Act** and **Sec. 31A Air Act** (Supreme Court, Aug 2025).
> Most repeated angle (8 PYQs): "the Environment (Protection) Act was enacted / passed in **1986**" and "Act passed after Bhopal = EP Act 1986". Traps: 1981 = Air Act, 1972 = Wildlife Act, 1974 = Water Act / CPCB. Incorrect pair (APPSC JL): **Ganga Action Plan 1982** – it is 1986.

=== Eco-Mark and AP Institutions
- **Eco-Mark (1991):** Government of India (MoEF) scheme labelling **environment-friendly** consumer products that meet environmental criteria plus **BIS** quality standards; certified by the **BIS** (not CPCB); logo **earthen pot**.
  - Not about purity, protein content or economic viability – the recurring distractors.
  - Products: soaps, detergents, paper, plastics, cosmetics, aerosols, food items; **not drugs/antibiotics**.
- **APPCB:** constituted **24 Jan 1976** under the Water Act; air role from **1981**; gives consents. **White** = practically non-polluting; **Green** = low pollution (Jul 2026).
- **EPTRI** (Hyderabad): environment training and research.
> Asked 4 times (UPSC 1998, CDS 2011, UPPCS 2022, IAS 1998): Eco-Mark = **environment friendly**.
~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 10th ed. part 21 p.11 (26.4 Eco Mark); NextGen Environment - APPSC 12 p.1 (Ecomark issued by BIS); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.55; app notes 3:21 sub 8

=== Environmental Principles
| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Precautionary** | Take **preventive action even without full scientific certainty** of harm; burden of proof on the **actor** to show the activity is harmless (Rio Principle 15) |
| **Polluter Pays** | Polluter bears the cost of **remediation + compensation** for the damage |
| **Public trust** | The **State holds natural resources in trust** for the public |
| **Absolute liability** | Hazardous industries – liable with **no exceptions** |
| **Sustainable development** | Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations |
- **Environmental sustainability** aims to **conserve natural resources and protect global ecosystems** – not humans, wildlife or animals alone.
- The NGT must apply sustainable development, the precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle in its orders.
> APPSC 2024-25 (Endowment, others): precautionary = act even when not completely sure of the harm; trap = "only after full scientific proof".
~~ Sources: PMF Environment 06 p.95 (NGT applies the three principles), PMF Environment 04 p.46 (polluter pays), p.51 (public trust); app notes 3:21 sub 0

=== Landmark Cases and Doctrines
| Case | Year | Doctrine / outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Ratlam Municipality v Vardhichand | 1980 | Municipal duty on public nuisance; health, sanitation |
| M.C. Mehta (Oleum gas, Delhi) | 1986 | **Absolute liability** |
| Subhash Kumar v Bihar | 1991 | Pollution-free water and air in Art. 21 |
| T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad | 1995 (petition; key order Dec 1996) | "Forest" regardless of ownership |
| **Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum** | 1996 | **Precautionary + Polluter Pays**; tanneries, **Palar** (TN) |
| Bichhri (Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action) | 1996 | Polluter Pays; Rajasthan chemical units |
| **M.C. Mehta v Kamal Nath** | **1997** (decided 13 Dec 1996, (1997) 1 SCC 388) | **Public trust doctrine** – Span Motels on the Beas, Himachal |
| Taj Trapezium (M.C. Mehta v UoI) | 1997 (order 30 Dec 1996) | Industries near the Taj to switch to natural gas (CNG) or relocate |
- Vellore = both principles; Bichhri = Polluter Pays only; Kamal Nath = public trust; Oleum gas = absolute liability.
- Gaurav Bansal v UoI (2015) = disaster-management compensation, not public trust.
> APPSC FRO screening: public trust upheld in **M.C. Mehta v Kamal Nath**.
~~ Sources: M.C. Mehta v Kamal Nath (1997) 1 SCC 388 (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._C._Mehta_v._Kamal_Nath, citing the judgment); PMF Environment 04 p.51 (public trust); app notes 3:21 sub 1

=== Constitutional Provisions
- **Article 48A** (DPSP) = duty of the **State**; **Article 51A(g)** (FD) = duty of the **citizen** (both added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976).
- **Article 21** read to include a clean environment (Subhash Kumar, 1991).
- **Article 253**: Parliament legislates to implement international agreements – basis of the Air Act and EP Act.
~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Sheets Environment p.10-11; app notes 3:21 sub 2

=== National Environment Policy 2006
- **Year:** adopted in **2006** (approved 18 May 2006) – India's overarching environment policy.
- Core idea: **conservation and livelihood security go together**.
- Objectives: conserve critical resources; **inter-generational equity**; livelihood security for poor tribal communities; **integrate environment into development plans**; efficient resource use.
- Principles: **precautionary + polluter pays + public trust**.
- NOT an NEP objective: ending all development in ecologically sensitive areas.
~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Sheets Environment p.5 (NEP 2006); app notes 3:21 sub 4

=== National Green Tribunal
- **NGT Act 2010**; in force **18 Oct 2010**; replaced the National Environment Appellate Authority.
- **Principal Bench: Delhi**; zonal: **Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata, Chennai**.
- Civil cases on substantial environmental questions under **7 Acts** in Schedule I.
~~ Sources: PMF Environment 06 p.95; NextGen Rocket Sheets Environment p.24; app notes 3:21 sub 6

=== NAPCC 2008 and Climate Programmes
- **NAPCC:** released **30 June 2008** with **eight** national missions: Solar; Enhanced Energy Efficiency; Sustainable Habitat; Water; Himalayan Ecosystem; **Green India**; **Sustainable Agriculture**; Strategic Knowledge for Climate Change. Climate change and human health is also listed (2026).
- **Coastal Security** is NOT a NAPCC mission.
- **Long-Term Ecological Observatories (LTEO):** announced by India at **COP-21 (Paris, 2015)** – eight observatories for **climate change** studies, under the Climate Change Action Programme.
