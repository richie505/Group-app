=== NOTES 2:72 | Attorney General and Advocate General; Solicitor General
## SUB 0 | Law Officers – an Overview
[72:0:0] - **Attorney General (AG)** = **highest / first law officer of India** and **chief legal adviser** to GoI (APPSC 2018, 2019).
[72:0:1] - Only the **AG** (Union) and **Advocate General** (State) are created by the Constitution; **SG and ASGs are statutory**.
[72:0:2] - The **Chief Justice of India** is head of the judiciary, **NOT** the government's first law officer; the **Law Minister** handles legal matters at government level.
[72:0:3] # AG vs SG vs Advocate General
[72:0:4] TABLE head: Basis | Attorney General | Solicitor General | Advocate General
  [72:0:4.0] Basis | Art. 76, Part V | Statutory (no Article) | Art. 165, Part VI
  [72:0:4.1] Serves | Union Government | Assists the AG | State Government
  [72:0:4.2] Appointed by | President | Appointments Committee of the Cabinet | Governor
  [72:0:4.3] Qualification | Of SC judge (Art. 124(3)) | Distinguished senior advocate | Of HC judge (Art. 217)
  [72:0:4.4] Tenure | Pleasure of President | Fixed statutory term (3 years) | Pleasure of Governor
  [72:0:4.5] Pay fixed by | President | – | Governor
  [72:0:4.6] Floor rights | Parliament, Art. 88 | None | State Legislature, Art. 177
  [72:0:4.7] Privileges | Art. 105(4) | – | Art. 194(4)
  [72:0:4.8] Precedence | 11 | 23 | 25 (with ASGs)
## SUB 1 | Attorney General – Article 76
[72:1:0] # Appointment and Qualification
[72:1:1] - **Art. 76(1)**: appointed by the **President** (on the government's advice); must be **qualified to be a Supreme Court judge**.
[72:1:2] - Qualifications (= SC judge): **citizen of India** and one of:
[72:1:3] - 1. **High Court judge** for at least **5 years**.
[72:1:4] - 2. **Advocate of a High Court** for at least **10 years**.
[72:1:5] - 3. **Distinguished jurist** in the opinion of the **President**.
[72:1:6] # Term and Removal
[72:1:7] - **No fixed tenure** in the Constitution; holds office **during the pleasure of the President** (Art. 76(4)) – NOT the Prime Minister.
[72:1:8] - **No grounds or procedure** for removal given; removable **at any time**.
[72:1:9] - **Resigns** to the President; by custom quits when the **Council of Ministers changes**.
[72:1:10] - **Remuneration** determined by the **President** – not by Parliament; **not charged** on the Consolidated Fund.
[72:1:11] - **Not a member of the Union Cabinet**; part of the **Union Executive**.
[72:1:12] APPSC-G2 2025: "AG has a fixed (five-year) term" = FALSE. "Qualified to be an SC judge" and "advises GoI" = correct.
## SUB 2 | Duties of the Attorney General
[72:2:0] - 1. **Advise GoI** on legal matters referred by the **President**.
[72:2:1] - 2. **Appear for GoI** in all **Supreme Court** cases concerning it.
[72:2:2] - 3. Represent GoI in **Art. 143** (Presidential reference) cases.
[72:2:3] - 4. Appear in **any High Court** when required.
[72:2:4] - 5. Other legal duties assigned by the President.
[72:2:5] - Matters go to the AG through the **Ministry of Law**; only legal matters of **real importance**.
[72:2:6] - The AG does **NOT** chair **joint sittings** of Parliament.
## SUB 3 | Rights and Limitations of the Ag
[72:3:0] # Rights
[72:3:1] - **Right of audience** in **all courts** in India.
[72:3:2] - **Art. 88**: right to **speak and take part** in proceedings of **either House**, joint sittings and **committees** of which he is named – **without being a member**.
[72:3:3] - **No right to vote**.
[72:3:4] - **Art. 105(4)**: all **privileges and immunities** of an MP.
[72:3:5] # Limitations
[72:3:6] - Should **not advise or hold a brief against** the Government of India.
[72:3:7] - Should **not defend an accused** in a criminal prosecution **without permission** of GoI.
[72:3:8] - **Not a full-time government servant**, so **not barred from private legal practice**.
## SUB 4 | Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General
[72:4:0] - **Not mentioned in the Constitution**; statutory, governed by the **Law Officers (Terms and Conditions) Rules, 1987**.
[72:4:1] - **SG = second law officer**; assists the AG; helped by several **ASGs**.
[72:4:2] - Appointed by the Centre through the **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet**; normal term **3 years**.
[72:4:3] - Appears in courts for the Union and also **tenders legal advice**.
[72:4:4] - SG has **no Art. 88** floor rights in Parliament.
[72:4:5] - **AG and SG** are **ex officio members** of the **Bar Council of India** (**Advocates Act, 1961**).
[72:4:6] - Convention: law officers resign when a new government comes in.
## SUB 5 | Advocate General – Article 165
[72:5:0] # Office and Appointment
[72:5:1] - **Highest law officer of the State** and official legal adviser to the State Government – the State counterpart of the AG (Art. 76).
[72:5:2] - Appointed by the **Governor** (Art. 165(1)) – not the President.
[72:5:3] - Qualification: qualified to be a **High Court judge** (Art. 217).
[72:5:4] - **No fixed tenure**; holds office **during the pleasure of the Governor** (Art. 165(3)); changes with the State government by convention.
[72:5:5] - **Remuneration fixed by the Governor**.
[72:5:6] - **Duties (Art. 165(2))**: advises the State Government; other legal duties assigned by the Governor.
[72:5:7] - Assisted by **Additional Advocates General**; order of precedence **No. 25**, below the ASG.
[72:5:8] "Holds office during the pleasure of the State Cabinet / Chief Minister" = FALSE; it is the Governor.
[72:5:9] # Rights
[72:5:10] - **Art. 177**: speak and take part in **either House** of the State Legislature and its committees – **no vote**.
[72:5:11] - **Right of audience** in any court **in the State** (AG: all courts in India).
[72:5:12] - **Art. 194(4)**: privileges of a member of the State Legislature.
[72:5:13] - HC judges are appointed by the **President**; the Advocate General by the **Governor**.

=== NOTES 2:73 | National Commission for SCs (338), STs (338A) and Backward Classes (338B); Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities (350B)
## SUB 0 | Article and Amendment – Evolution
[73:0:0] TABLE head: Body | Article | Created / changed by
  [73:0:0.0] Special Officer (Commissioner) for SCs and STs | 338 (original) | Constitution, 1950
  [73:0:0.1] Combined National Commission for SCs and STs | 338 | 65th Amendment, 1990
  [73:0:0.2] NCSC (separate) | 338 | 89th Amendment, 2003 (bifurcation); from 2004
  [73:0:0.3] NCST | 338A | 89th Amendment, 2003; set up 19 Feb 2004
  [73:0:0.4] NCBC | 338B | 102nd Amendment Act, 2018
  [73:0:0.5] Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities | 350B | 7th Amendment, 1956
[73:0:1] - 65th Amendment: multi-member constitutional commission replaced the Special Officer and the commission set up by the **1987 Resolution**.
[73:0:2] - **1999**: separate **Ministry of Tribal Affairs** → opened the way for a separate NCST.
[73:0:3] NCST = 89th Amendment, not the 65th. Combined commission = 65th.
## SUB 1 | Composition
[73:1:0] - NCSC / NCST / constitutional NCBC (each): **Chairperson + Vice-Chairperson + 3 members** (five).
[73:1:1] - Appointed by the **President** by **warrant under his hand and seal**; service conditions and tenure fixed by the President; **term 3 years** under the rules.
[73:1:2] Statutory NCBC (1993) = **5 members** headed by a **retired SC/HC judge**; constitutional NCBC (338B) = Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson + 3.
## SUB 2 | Functions
[73:2:0] - 1. **Investigate and monitor** constitutional safeguards.
[73:2:1] - 2. **Inquire into specific complaints** of deprivation of rights.
