=== NOTES 3:53 | Land Reforms – Abolition of Intermediaries, Tenancy Reform, Ceiling Laws, Consolidation; Outcomes
## SUB 0 | Components and Aims of Land Reforms
[53:0:0] TABLE head: Measure | Meaning / outcome
  [53:0:0.0] Abolition of intermediaries | removing zamindars, jagirdars, inamdars – the cultivator deals directly with the State
  [53:0:0.1] Tenancy reform | rent cap 1/4 to 1/6 of produce; security of tenure; ownership to tenants; failed through "voluntary surrenders" and evictions
  [53:0:0.2] Land ceiling | the legally stipulated maximum an individual/family may hold; surplus redistributed to the landless
  [53:0:0.3] Consolidation | pooling scattered plots
[53:0:1] - **Measures to make the agrarian structure conducive to growth:** abolition of intermediaries, tenancy reforms **and** ceiling on holdings – "all of the above". **Multiple cropping** is NOT a land reform.
[53:0:2] - **NextGen list:** abolition of intermediaries, tenancy reforms, consolidation, cooperative farming, ceiling, computerisation of land records; Raj Krishna – liberative, distributive, organisational, developmental.
[53:0:3] - **Primary aim:** **equitable distribution of land ownership** (land to the tiller); UPSC 2019 key – "the major aim was providing agricultural land to all the landless".
[53:0:4] - **UPSC 2019 traps:** ceilings were first on individual holdings (family unit only after 1972); exemptions were allowed (plantations, orchards, mechanised farms); land reforms did not make cash crops predominant.
[53:0:5] - **Failure of land reforms** and **limited use of new technology** are reasons for agricultural distress.
[53:0:6] - **Ceiling rounds:** 1950s laws took the **individual** as unit (fuelled **benami** transfers to relatives); **1972 national guidelines** – **family** unit; 10-18 acres two-crop irrigated, 27 acres single-crop, **54 acres dry**.
[53:0:7] - **Outcome:** ceilings redistributed **less than 2%** of cultivable area – because of exemptions for plantations, orchards and "mechanised" farms.
[53:0:8] - **AP land reforms:** ceiling and holding-size changes aimed at **productive utilisation of land** (APPSC G2 2011 key).
[53:0:9] > Definition and "NOT a part of land reform" stems are the most frequent (UPPSC, State PSC, UPSC 2019).

## SUB 1 | Land Distribution, Koneru Ranga Rao Committee and Leasing in AP
[53:1:0] - **Land distributed to the poor in AP** comes overwhelmingly from **government (assigned) land**, not ceiling surplus or forest land.
[53:1:1] - **Land distribution programme:** first inaugurated in AP in **2005**; second phase **21-23 August 2005** (APPSC G2 2011 key: **1,69,000 acres**).
[53:1:2] - **Indira Prabha (2004, Y.S. Rajasekhara Reddy):** development of **uncultivable barren assigned lands** allotted to landless poor.
[53:1:3] - **Koneru Ranga Rao Committee (AP Land Committee, 2006, YSR government):** to resolve **land-related problems** – **assigned lands**, confirmation of land rights, **tenancy regulation and land records**; **NOT corporate farming**.
[53:1:4] - **National Council for Land Reforms (2008):** chaired by the **Prime Minister**.
[53:1:5] - **Model Agricultural Land Leasing Act, 2016:** NITI Aayog (T. Haque committee). **First State to implement agricultural land leasing: Uttarakhand** (policy notified January 2020).
[53:1:6] - AP: CCRC Act 2019 (11-month cards); proposed new tenancy law with tenant entitlement cards.

## SUB 2 | Tenancy Reform, Consolidation, Bhoodan and Cooperative Farming
[53:2:0] - **Operation Barga** (**West Bengal**, Left Front, **1978**): recording names of sharecroppers (bargadars) through village panchayats; heritable rights and 75% of produce.
[53:2:1] - **Consolidation:** succeeded in **Punjab, Haryana, western UP** (compulsory in Punjab-Haryana); **failed to take off in Tamil Nadu** – no consolidation law in **Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Odisha and the Andhra area** (Rajya Sabha answer, 1972).
[53:2:2] - **Bhoodan:** **Acharya Vinoba Bhave**, **Pochampally** (Nalgonda, Telangana), **18 April 1951**; asked for 1/6th of land; "Bloodless Revolution"; led to Gramdan.
[53:2:3] - **Cooperative farming:** Kumarappa Committee (1949) → Nagpur Resolution (1959); **Charan Singh opposed** it (Nehru backed it).
[53:2:4] - **Joint cooperative farming:** land pooled **voluntarily**; owners keep their rights; management elected by members; produce shared by land and labour contributed – **compulsory membership is NOT a feature**.
[53:2:5] TABLE head: Amendment / case | Land-reform change
  [53:2:5.0] 1st (1951) | Arts. 31A, 31B, Ninth Schedule (after Kameshwar Singh v. Bihar)
  [53:2:5.1] 4th (1955) | widened 31A (zamindari abolition, planning, minerals)
  [53:2:5.2] 17th (1964) | land under personal cultivation within ceiling only at market value; widened "estate"; challenged in Golaknath
  [53:2:5.3] 25th (1971) | Art. 31C
  [53:2:5.4] 34th (1974) / 66th (1990) | more land laws (incl. AP Acts) in the Ninth Schedule
  [53:2:5.5] 75th (1993) | Art. 323B(h) – rent and tenancy tribunals
  [53:2:5.6] I.R. Coelho (2007) | Ninth Schedule laws added after 24 April 1973 open to basic-structure review

## SUB 3 | AP Land Laws – Abolition, Tenancy and Ceiling
[53:3:0] TABLE head: Law | Key provisions
  [53:3:0.0] Madras / AP (Andhra Area) Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act, 1948 (Act XXVI of 1948) | abolished zamindari and inamdari estates in the Andhra area; recommended by the Prakasam Committee (1938); NextGen dates its passage 1949 with effect from 1950
  [53:3:0.1] Hyderabad Jagir Abolition Regulation, 1949 | after Police Action; jagir and Sarf-e-khas lands became Diwani (ryotwari)
  [53:3:0.2] Hyderabad (Telangana Area) Tenancy Act, 1950 | 6 years' cultivation = protected tenant with heritable rights; ownership on paying 60% of market price in 20 instalments
  [53:3:0.3] AP (Andhra Area) Tenancy Act, 1956 | Subba Rao Committee; written leases; rent cap 30% wet / 25% dry; minimum 6-year lease
  [53:3:0.4] AP Ceiling Act, 1961 | individual unit; 27-324 acres; surplus under 15,000 acres
  [53:3:0.5] AP Land Reforms (Ceiling on Agricultural Holdings) Act, 1973 | P.V. Narasimha Rao; Ninth Schedule; in force 1 January 1975
  [53:3:0.6] AP Assigned Lands (Prohibition of Transfers) Act, 1977 | assigned land cannot be sold or transferred
  [53:3:0.7] AP Land Grabbing (Prohibition) Act, 1982 | punishes grabbers of government and weaker-section lands
[53:3:1] - **1973 Act unit:** a **family of five** (husband, wife, minor children); husband's and wife's land clubbed; each major son a separate unit; +1/5 ceiling per extra member.
[53:3:2] - **1973 Act ceilings (standard holding):** wet land irrigated for **two crops 10-18 acres**; **one crop 15-27 acres**; dry land **35-54 acres**.
[53:3:3] - Benami transfers after **30 Jan 1970** ignored; **Land Tribunal** headed by the **RDO**; exemptions – plantations, orchards, trusts, cooperatives, Bhoodan lands.
[53:3:4] - **Phase II:** pushed by Srikakulam Naxalite unrest and the 1966-69 droughts; 20 lakh acres surplus estimated, only about 5 lakh taken over.
[53:3:5] - **A-R (APPSC 2022):** "in force 1 Jan 1975" ✓ and "unit of not more than five members" ✓, but the unit does not explain the date → both true, R not the explanation.
[53:3:6] - **Feb 2026:** AP proposes to decriminalise two offences under the 1973 Act.

## SUB 1 | e-NAM – National Agriculture Market

[56:1:4] - **Direct payment** to the farmer's bank account; warehouse trading with **e-NWRs**.

## SUB 2 | APMC Acts, Model Acts and the Farm Laws 2020
[56:2:0] - Agricultural markets = **State List**; markets are regulated under **APMC Acts enacted by States** (UPSC 2015) – not the Essential Commodities Act or the Grading and Marking Act.
[56:2:1] - AP law: **AP (Agricultural Produce and Livestock) Markets Act, 1966**. Set-up order: **area notified** → Market Committee → market yard.
[56:2:2] - Regulated markets ensure **open auction, standard weights, recorded prices, licensed buyers**; ~**2,477** regulated markets; market fee **1%**.

[56:2:4] - Farmer's share in the consumer rupee (RBI, Oct 2024): tomato ~**33%**, pulses higher (gram ~**75%**).

=== NOTES 3:57 | Minimum Support Price – Mechanism, Cacp, Crops, Procurement, Criticism; Pm-aasha
## SUB 0 | MSP – Meaning, CACP and Who Fixes It
[57:0:0] - **Minimum Support Price (MSP):** a form of **market intervention** by the Government of India to insure farmers against a **sharp fall in farm prices** – a guarantee (floor) price announced **at the beginning of the sowing season**, before the crop reaches the market.
[57:0:1] - **Purpose:** save farmers from **distress sales** and **procure foodgrains for public distribution** (PDS buffer stocks → food security); it **ensures a minimum fixed price** and supports food security, but it is **not** a promise of "immense profit", and farmers sell to the government only when the **market price falls below MSP**.
[57:0:2] - **Mechanism:** if the market price falls below MSP (bumper crop, glut), **government agencies purchase the stock offered** by farmers at MSP.
[57:0:3] - **Who:** **CACP recommends → Government of India (Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs, CCEA) approves/fixes**. Exam answer for "MSP is determined/decided/recommended by": **CACP** – not ICAR, IARI, NABARD, Planning Commission, RBI, President, Finance Commission, State Governments or FCI.
[57:0:4] - **Legal status:** an **administrative decision**, not backed by a statute; not a legal right.
[57:0:5] - Government's tools to improve agricultural marketing: **PDS, MSP and buffer stocks** – all three ("more than one of the above").
[57:0:6] TABLE head: CACP fact | Detail
  [57:0:6.0] Origin | Agricultural Prices Commission (APC), January 1965 (same year as FCI)
  [57:0:6.1] Renamed | CACP in 1985
  [57:0:6.2] Status | Attached office of the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare; a decentralised agency, not statutory
  [57:0:6.3] Composition | Chairman + Member Secretary + 1 official member + 2 non-official members
  [57:0:6.4] Main objective | Recommend MSPs to incentivise farmers to adopt modern technology, raise productivity and output
  [57:0:6.5] Mandated objectives (UPSC 1995) | Stabilise agricultural prices; ensure meaningful real income to farmers; protect consumers via PDS – not "maximum price for the farmer"
[57:0:7] - **Ashok Gulati** (CACP Chairman 2011-14): long-term food security needs agricultural growth of **more than 4% a year**.
[57:0:8] > Exam angle: "Who determines MSP" – more than a dozen PYQs; the key is always CACP (strictly it recommends, CCEA decides).

## SUB 2 | PM-AASHA, PSF, MIS and Other Price Support
[57:2:0] - **PM-AASHA** = **Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay SanraksHan Abhiyan**, approved by the Union Cabinet **September 2018**, Ministry of Agriculture; beneficiaries: **farmers** – aims that MSP benefits reach farmers, including small and marginal ones.
[57:2:1] - **Outlay ₹35,000 crore** for the 15th Finance Commission cycle **up to 2025-26** (APPSC 2023).
[57:2:2] TABLE head: Component | Nature
  [57:2:2.0] PSS | Physical procurement of pulses, oilseeds, copra by NAFED, NCCF; cap 25%
  [57:2:2.1] PDPS | Pays MSP minus market price without buying; mainly oilseeds

## SUB 6 | CACP Factors, Cost Concepts and the MSP Formula
[57:6:0] - **Factors CACP considers:** **cost of production**, demand and supply, **price trends in domestic and international markets**, **inter-crop price parity**, terms of trade between agriculture and non-agriculture, a minimum 50% margin over cost, and the likely **effect on consumers / the economy**.
[57:6:1] - **Not a factor:** a **cost of living index** as such (CDS 2019) – CACP only weighs the *effect* of MSP on cost of living.
[57:6:2] TABLE head: Cost concept | Meaning
  [57:6:2.0] A2 | Actual paid-out costs (seed, fertiliser, hired labour, fuel, irrigation)
  [57:6:2.1] FL | Imputed value of family labour
  [57:6:2.2] A2+FL | Paid-out cost + family labour (leaves out rent on owned land and interest on fixed capital)
  [57:6:2.3] C2 | A2 + FL + land rent + interest on fixed capital – the fullest cost
[57:6:3] - **Present formula (since 2018-19):** MSP at least **1.5 × (A2+FL)** – a 50% margin over A2+FL, **not over C2**; in short, the support price is **"cost plus"** a margin (cost plus benefit).
[57:6:4] - **Swaminathan Commission (National Commission on Farmers, 2004-06):** MSP = **C2 + 50%**; farmer unions (SKM) say paddy at C2+50% should be ₹3,243/q for 2026-27 vs ₹2,441 fixed.
[57:6:5] - **Farm running (working) capital** = seed, fertiliser, irrigation water, hired labour; **land revenue** is a fixed charge, **not** running capital.
