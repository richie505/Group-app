## SUB 0 | Components and Aims of Land Reforms
[0:0] TABLE head: Measure | Meaning / outcome
  [0:0.0] Abolition of intermediaries | removing zamindars, jagirdars, inamdars – the cultivator deals directly with the State
  [0:0.1] Tenancy reform | rent cap 1/4 to 1/6 of produce; security of tenure; ownership to tenants; failed through "voluntary surrenders" and evictions
  [0:0.2] Land ceiling | the legally stipulated maximum an individual/family may hold; surplus redistributed to the landless
  [0:0.3] Consolidation | pooling scattered plots
[0:1] - **Measures to make the agrarian structure conducive to growth:** abolition of intermediaries, tenancy reforms **and** ceiling on holdings – "all of the above". **Multiple cropping** is NOT a land reform.
[0:2] - **NextGen list:** abolition of intermediaries, tenancy reforms, consolidation, cooperative farming, ceiling, computerisation of land records; Raj Krishna – liberative, distributive, organisational, developmental.
[0:3] - **Primary aim:** **equitable distribution of land ownership** (land to the tiller); UPSC 2019 key – "the major aim was providing agricultural land to all the landless".
[0:4] - **UPSC 2019 traps:** ceilings were first on individual holdings (family unit only after 1972); exemptions were allowed (plantations, orchards, mechanised farms); land reforms did not make cash crops predominant.
[0:5] - **Failure of land reforms** and **limited use of new technology** are reasons for agricultural distress.
[0:6] - **Ceiling rounds:** 1950s laws took the **individual** as unit (fuelled **benami** transfers to relatives); **1972 national guidelines** – **family** unit; 10-18 acres two-crop irrigated, 27 acres single-crop, **54 acres dry**.
[0:7] - **Outcome:** ceilings redistributed **less than 2%** of cultivable area – because of exemptions for plantations, orchards and "mechanised" farms.
[0:8] - **AP land reforms:** ceiling and holding-size changes aimed at **productive utilisation of land** (APPSC G2 2011 key).
[0:9] > Definition and "NOT a part of land reform" stems are the most frequent (UPPSC, State PSC, UPSC 2019).

## SUB 1 | AP Land Laws – Abolition, Tenancy and Ceiling
[1:0] TABLE head: Law | Key provisions
  [1:0.0] Madras / AP (Andhra Area) Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act, 1948 (Act XXVI of 1948) | abolished zamindari and inamdari estates in the Andhra area; recommended by the Prakasam Committee (1938); NextGen dates its passage 1949 with effect from 1950
  [1:0.1] Hyderabad Jagir Abolition Regulation, 1949 | after Police Action; jagir and Sarf-e-khas lands became Diwani (ryotwari)
  [1:0.2] Hyderabad (Telangana Area) Tenancy Act, 1950 | 6 years' cultivation = protected tenant with heritable rights; ownership on paying 60% of market price in 20 instalments
  [1:0.3] AP (Andhra Area) Tenancy Act, 1956 | Subba Rao Committee; written leases; rent cap 30% wet / 25% dry; minimum 6-year lease
  [1:0.4] AP Ceiling Act, 1961 | individual unit; 27-324 acres; surplus under 15,000 acres
  [1:0.5] AP Land Reforms (Ceiling on Agricultural Holdings) Act, 1973 | P.V. Narasimha Rao; Ninth Schedule; in force 1 January 1975
  [1:0.6] AP Assigned Lands (Prohibition of Transfers) Act, 1977 | assigned land cannot be sold or transferred
  [1:0.7] AP Land Grabbing (Prohibition) Act, 1982 | punishes grabbers of government and weaker-section lands
[1:1] - **1973 Act unit:** a **family of five** (husband, wife, minor children); husband's and wife's land clubbed; each major son a separate unit; +1/5 ceiling per extra member.
[1:2] - **1973 Act ceilings (standard holding):** wet land irrigated for **two crops 10-18 acres**; **one crop 15-27 acres**; dry land **35-54 acres**.
[1:3] - Benami transfers after **30 Jan 1970** ignored; **Land Tribunal** headed by the **RDO**; exemptions – plantations, orchards, trusts, cooperatives, Bhoodan lands.
[1:4] - **Phase II:** pushed by Srikakulam Naxalite unrest and the 1966-69 droughts; 20 lakh acres surplus estimated, only about 5 lakh taken over.
[1:5] - **A-R (APPSC 2022):** "in force 1 Jan 1975" ✓ and "unit of not more than five members" ✓, but the unit does not explain the date → both true, R not the explanation.
[1:6] - **Feb 2026:** AP proposes to decriminalise two offences under the 1973 Act.

## SUB 2 | Land Distribution, Koneru Ranga Rao Committee and Leasing in AP
[2:0] - **Land distributed to the poor in AP** comes overwhelmingly from **government (assigned) land**, not ceiling surplus or forest land.
[2:1] - **Land distribution programme:** first inaugurated in AP in **2005**; second phase **21-23 August 2005** (APPSC G2 2011 key: **1,69,000 acres**).
[2:2] - **Indira Prabha (2004, Y.S. Rajasekhara Reddy):** development of **uncultivable barren assigned lands** allotted to landless poor.
[2:3] - **Koneru Ranga Rao Committee (AP Land Committee, 2006, YSR government):** to resolve **land-related problems** – **assigned lands**, confirmation of land rights, **tenancy regulation and land records**; **NOT corporate farming**.
[2:4] - **National Council for Land Reforms (2008):** chaired by the **Prime Minister**.
[2:5] - **Model Agricultural Land Leasing Act, 2016:** NITI Aayog (T. Haque committee). **First State to implement agricultural land leasing: Uttarakhand** (policy notified January 2020).
[2:6] - AP: CCRC Act 2019 (11-month cards); proposed new tenancy law with tenant entitlement cards.

## SUB 3 | Tenancy Reform, Consolidation, Bhoodan and Cooperative Farming
[3:0] - **Operation Barga** (**West Bengal**, Left Front, **1978**): recording names of sharecroppers (bargadars) through village panchayats; heritable rights and 75% of produce.
[3:1] - **Consolidation:** succeeded in **Punjab, Haryana, western UP** (compulsory in Punjab-Haryana); **failed to take off in Tamil Nadu** – no consolidation law in **Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Odisha and the Andhra area** (Rajya Sabha answer, 1972).
[3:2] - **Bhoodan:** **Acharya Vinoba Bhave**, **Pochampally** (Nalgonda, Telangana), **18 April 1951**; asked for 1/6th of land; "Bloodless Revolution"; led to Gramdan.
[3:3] - **Cooperative farming:** Kumarappa Committee (1949) → Nagpur Resolution (1959); **Charan Singh opposed** it (Nehru backed it).
[3:4] - **Joint cooperative farming:** land pooled **voluntarily**; owners keep their rights; management elected by members; produce shared by land and labour contributed – **compulsory membership is NOT a feature**.
[3:5] TABLE head: Amendment / case | Land-reform change
  [3:5.0] 1st (1951) | Arts. 31A, 31B, Ninth Schedule (after Kameshwar Singh v. Bihar)
  [3:5.1] 4th (1955) | widened 31A (zamindari abolition, planning, minerals)
  [3:5.2] 17th (1964) | land under personal cultivation within ceiling only at market value; widened "estate"; challenged in Golaknath
  [3:5.3] 25th (1971) | Art. 31C
  [3:5.4] 34th (1974) / 66th (1990) | more land laws (incl. AP Acts) in the Ninth Schedule
  [3:5.5] 75th (1993) | Art. 323B(h) – rent and tenancy tribunals
  [3:5.6] I.R. Coelho (2007) | Ninth Schedule laws added after 24 April 1973 open to basic-structure review
