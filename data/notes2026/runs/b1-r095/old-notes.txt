## SUB 1 | Non-brahmanical Trends
[95:1:0] - Non-Brahmanisation of religion and politics **culminated under the Kakatiyas** (APPSC 2019).
[95:1:1] - Kakatiyas were **Sudras** (originally of **Pulinda** origin before adopting the varna order) → **Sudra kingship**.
[95:1:2] - Groups involved: reform sects (**Veerashaiva, Veeravaishnava**) that opposed Brahminical practice; officials from warrior and farmer groups (Nayankara).
[95:1:3] Question "in which dynasty did non-Brahmin trends culminate?" → Kakatiyas.
## SUB 2 | Religious Shift – Jain to Shaiva
[95:2:0] - 1. Early Kakatiyas were **Jains** (APPSC G2 2011).
[95:2:1] - 2. Turned **Shaiva** from **Prola II / Rudradeva**.
[95:2:2] - 3. Jainism declined through **Kalamukha and Veerashaiva hostility** (also persecution by some officials); the Kakatiyas are credited with its **final eradication** in the region; the **Lingayats** hurt Jainism badly.
[95:2:3] - Kakatiya age = the **"Shaiva era"**; dynastic deity **Svayambhu Shiva** (not Surya).
[95:2:4] - Yet Vaishnava shrines were endowed → **tolerance and syncretism**.
[95:2:5] # Jain Centres
[95:2:6] - **Padmakshi temple, Hanumakonda** – built by **Prola II**; **originally a Jain shrine**.
[95:2:7] - **Bodhan, Vemulawada, Patancheru, Kolanupaka, Potlacheruvu**.
[95:2:8] - **Kadalalaya Jain basadi, Anumakonda** – built by **Mailama**, wife of minister Bethanamatya.
## SUB 3 | Shaiva Sects – Pasupata, Kalamukha, Kapalika
[95:3:0] TABLE head: Sect | Founder / trait
  [95:3:0.0] Pasupata | Oldest Shaiva sect; founded by Lakulisa (Nakulisa), 1st–2nd c. AD; popular into the Reddi period; declined under Vijayanagara
  [95:3:0.1] Kalamukha | Kalasana (Kalanana), disciple of Lakulisa; temple managers; gave anna dana
  [95:3:0.2] Kapalika | Offshoot of Pasupata (⚠ APPSC key (2019) gave the Misra Saiva school); skull-bowl ascetics
  [95:3:0.3] Aradhya | Brahmin Shaivas under Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya; kept Vedic ritual and varna
[95:3:1] - **Aradhya acharyas:** 12 in four triads; **Siddhatraya** = **Revana, Marula, Ekorama**; others = Aradhya-, Acharya- and Panditatraya (Manchana, Sripati, Mallikarjuna).
[95:3:2] - Kakatiyas patronised the "extremist" **Pasupata and Kalamukha** sects.
[95:3:3] - Kalamukha / Shaiva centres: **Srisailam, Alampur, Vemulawada, Kaleshwaram, Tripurantakam, Mahanandi**.
[95:3:4] Kapalikas grew out of Pasupata (G2 2019 key; another key: Misra/Mixed Saivam), not Kalamukha. Popular religion in the Reddi period = Veerashaivism (G2 2011 key); the kings patronised Pasupata.
## SUB 4 | Veerashaivism
[95:4:0] - Founder **Basaveshwara**, minister of **Kalachuri Bijjala** (Karnataka); spread into Andhra in the Kakatiya period.
[95:4:1] - Followers = **Lingayats**; gurus = **Jangamas**; texts = **Agamas**.
[95:4:2] - **Rejected** Vedas, caste, untouchability, child marriage, animal sacrifice.
[95:4:3] - **Social effect:** spread in Andhra by **Palkuriki Somanatha**; drew **artisans and lower groups** → **upward social mobility**.
[95:4:4] - In Andhra the word "Veerashaiva" is absent from inscriptions; followers called **Veeravratulu / Asankhyakulu**.
[95:4:5] - Main Veerashaiva centre in Andhra: **Srisailam** (Kolanupaka = Jain centre).
[95:4:6] - **Panditaradhya Charitra**: Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya's debate with a Buddhist acharya.
[95:4:7] - Kakatiya stance: **neutral** – grants to orthodox Brahmin sects, no persecution of Lingayats.
## SUB 5 | Temples As Institutions
[95:5:0] - Temples were **landed corporations** with lands and organised staff; **Srisailam Mallikarjuna** held lands in many villages.
[95:5:1] - Staff: **Sthanapatis, Manulas, Sanus, Nibandhakaras**.
[95:5:2] - Attached **monasteries and feeding houses**.
[95:5:3] - **Temple women** and courtesans (**Kalavatis**) sustained **dance and music**; festivals were royal occasions.
[95:5:4] - **Malkapuram inscription (1261):** **Visvesvara Sambhu** (Golaki matha, **Pasupata** Saiva) set up a college, feeding house and **maternity home (prasuti sala)** at **Mandadam**.

## SUB 4 | Kakatiya Style – Techniques and Features
[96:4:0] TABLE head: Innovation | What it is
  [96:4:0.0] Stellate (star) plan | Raised star-shaped platform; more wall surface for sculpture, more light and air
  [96:4:0.1] Sandbox foundation | Deep pits filled with sand, stone chips and earth; shock absorber on black cotton soil; why Ramappa survived earthquakes – a cited heritage-engineering example
  [96:4:0.2] Floating bricks | Light porous bricks in Ramappa's vimana that float on water; reduce the dead weight of the tower
  [96:4:0.3] Ekasila | Monolithic carving – Nandi and pillars in black basalt/granite
  [96:4:0.4] Lathe-turned pillars | Polished granite pillars
  [96:4:0.5] Kirtimukha | "Face of glory" motif
  [96:4:0.6] Madanika brackets | Bracket figures projecting from outer walls in dance poses
[96:4:1] Floating bricks = lighter tower; sandbox foundation = earthquake cushion. Do not swap them.
