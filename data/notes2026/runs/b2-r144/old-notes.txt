=== NOTES 2:144 | Minority Rights – Articles 29 and 30, Ncm, Sachar Committee, Schemes
## SUB 0 | Meaning of Minority and Notified Communities
[144:0:0] # Meaning
[144:0:1] - **"Minority" is not defined** in the Constitution; the word is used in **Articles 29, 30, 350A and 350B**.
[144:0:2] - The Constitution recognises only **religious and linguistic minorities** (Articles 29 and 30 protect both).
[144:0:3] - **NCM Act, Section 2(c):** minority = any community **notified by the Central Government**; critics say it gives the Centre **"unbridled power"**, uneasy with T.M.A. Pai.
[144:0:4] - **Unit for status = the State**, not all India (**T.M.A. Pai, 2002**): status decided by population in the State concerned.
[144:0:5] - States can name their own minorities; **11 States** had declared Jains a minority before 2014.
[144:0:6] - **Minority rights are not absolute**: the State may regulate standards but not take away management.
[144:0:7] - Religious minorities → NCM and Ministry of Minority Affairs; linguistic minorities → identified State-wise, **Special Officer (Art. 350B)**; not notified under the NCM Act.

## SUB 1 | Constitutional Articles
[144:1:0] # Articles 29 and 30
[144:1:1] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [144:1:1.0] 29(1) | Any section of citizens with a distinct language, script or culture may conserve it
  [144:1:1.1] 29(2) | No denial of admission to State-maintained/aided institutions on grounds only of religion, race, caste or language
  [144:1:1.2] 30(1) | All minorities, religious or linguistic, may establish and administer educational institutions of their choice ("Charter of Education Rights"); madrasas run under it
  [144:1:1.3] 30(1A) | 44th Amendment: compensation protects minority institutions on compulsory acquisition of property
  [144:1:1.4] 30(2) | State shall not discriminate in aid on ground of minority management; may regulate standards without destroying minority character
[144:1:2] - Art. 29 is wider (all citizens, not confined to minorities); applies only to citizens. Art. 30 = **only minorities**.
[144:1:3] - Political promises to conserve a section's language are **not a "corrupt practice"** under the **RPA, 1951**.
[144:1:4] - Related: **Art. 16(2)** – no discrimination in public employment (religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth); **Art. 19** – freedom; **Art. 26** – manage religious affairs.
[144:1:5] 29(2) does not list **sex** or **place of birth**.
[144:1:6] # Linguistic Minority Articles
[144:1:7] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [144:1:7.0] 347 | President may recognise a language spoken by a substantial part of a State's population
  [144:1:7.1] 350 | Representation for grievances in any language used in the Union/State
  [144:1:7.2] 350A | Mother-tongue instruction at the primary stage
  [144:1:7.3] 350B | Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities, appointed by and reports to the President; reports laid before Parliament and sent to States
[144:1:8] - 350A and 350B added by **Section 21, 7th Amendment, 1956** (States reorganisation). There is no Special Officer for every State.
[144:1:9] - **Commissioner for Linguistic Minorities:** office set up **1957**; HQ **Prayagraj**; 10-point programme with States.

## SUB 8 | Waqf Administration
[144:8:0] - Order: **Waqf Act 1954 → Waqf Act 1995 → Amendment 2013 → Amendment 2025**.
[144:8:1] - **Central Waqf Council:** statutory, **1964** (Sec 8A of 1954 Act; now Sec 9 of 1995 Act); advises Centre on State Waqf Boards.
[144:8:2] - **2013 Amendment:** Council can direct State Boards; bars sale, gift, mortgage, exchange, transfer of waqf property.
[144:8:3] - **Waqf (Amendment) Bill 2024:** Lok Sabha **8 August 2024**; aim transparency, accountability, stopping misuse.
[144:8:4] - **2025 Act** (assent 5 April 2025; 1995 Act renamed **UMEED Act**): **waqf by user** only for properties registered before the law; **non-Muslim members** (at least two per Board); central portal within 6 months – **UMEED portal** (June 2025); **Limitation Act** applies; appeal to HC within 90 days.
[144:8:5] - **SC interim order (Sep 2025):** stayed the **5-years-practising-Islam** condition and Collector's power; capped non-Muslims at **3** (State Board) / **4** (Central Council); accepted the Limitation Act change.
[144:8:6] - **Waqf Act, 1995:** the main law for the administration of waqf properties, through State Waqf Boards, the Central Waqf Council and Waqf Tribunals.

## SUB 0 | The Four Labour Codes
[145:0:0] TABLE head: Code | Year
  [145:0:0.0] Code on Wages | 2019
  [145:0:0.1] Industrial Relations Code | 2020
  [145:0:0.2] Code on Social Security | 2020
  [145:0:0.3] Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code | 2020
[145:0:1] - Together they replaced **29** central labour laws (e.g. **Industrial Disputes Act 1947** into the IR Code).
[145:0:2] - All four came into force on **21 November 2025**.
[145:0:3] - Critique: **Indian Labour Conference** not held since 2015; digital exclusion.

  [145:1:0.4] Fixed-term workers | Same wages and benefits as permanent; gratuity after 1 year
## SUB 2 | Code on Social Security 2020 – Gig and Platform Workers
[145:2:0] # Key Terms
[145:2:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [145:2:1.0] Gig worker | Works outside a traditional employer-employee relationship
  [145:2:1.1] Platform worker | Works through online platforms
  [145:2:1.2] Aggregator | Digital intermediary; first recognised by the Code on Social Security 2020
[145:2:2] - The Code is the **first law** to recognise **aggregators, gig and platform workers**.
[145:2:3] - Gig workers **register from age 16**; **National and State Social Security Boards**.
[145:2:4] - Limits: registration gives **no employee status**, **no minimum wage** guarantee, no industrial-dispute remedies.
[145:2:5] - Gig workers: **NITI** estimate **77 lakh** (2020-21); Economic Survey **120 lakh**.

## SUB 3 | Unorganised Sector – 2008 Act and Nceus
[145:3:0] # Unorganised Workers' Social Security Act, 2008
[145:3:1] - Set up a **National Social Security Board**; welfare schemes in **Schedule I**.
[145:3:2] - **Schedule I schemes:** IGNOAPS, **National Family Benefit Scheme**, **Janani Suraksha Yojana**, Janashree Bima Yojana, **Aam Aadmi Bima Yojana**, **RSBY**, and schemes for weavers, artisans, fishermen.
[145:3:3] - **Atal Pension Yojana is NOT** in Schedule I.
[145:3:4] - Merged into the **Code on Social Security, 2020**; critics say limited impact.
[145:3:5] # NCEUS and Informal Workforce
[145:3:6] - **NCEUS** (National Commission for Enterprises in the Unorganised Sector): set up **2004**, chaired by **Arjun Sengupta**; found **77%** living on **under Rs 20 a day**.

[145:3:8] 77% = NCEUS (under Rs 20 a day); 77 lakh = NITI gig estimate.

[145:5:0] - Launched **26 August 2021** by the Ministry of Labour and Employment.
[145:5:1] - India's **first Aadhaar-seeded** **National Database of Unorganised Workers (NDUW)**.

[145:5:3] - **12-digit UAN** (Universal Account Number) → benefits **portable** anywhere in India.

[145:5:7] e-Shram age = 16-59; gig registration under the Code = from 16.

[145:6:2] - **One Nation One Ration Card**: migrants draw PDS ration anywhere in India.

=== NOTES 2:146 | Consumer Rights – Consumer Protection Act 2019, Ccpa, E-commerce Rules
## SUB 0 | Consumer Protection Act, 2019
[146:0:0] - **Replaced** the Consumer Protection Act, **1986**.
[146:0:1] - **Six rights:** safety, information, choice, to be heard, redressal, **consumer awareness** (no right to private ownership).
[146:0:2] - **Consumer** includes **online/electronic** purchases for personal use; buyer for **resale/business** is left out.
[146:0:3] - New: **CCPA**, **product liability**, e-commerce rules, penalties for adulterated goods, **mediation cells**, e-filing.
[146:0:4] - File where the complainant **lives or works**; complaint **deemed admitted** if not decided on within **21 days**.
[146:0:5] - **Filing fee:** **nil** up to **Rs 5 lakh**; Rs 200 to Rs 7,500 above that.
[146:0:6] - Consumers may get **food samples tested**; a **legal heir** of a deceased consumer may complain; complaint lies on any fault or failure.
## SUB 1 | Consumer Commissions
[146:1:0] TABLE head: Commission | Jurisdiction (2021 Rules) | Appeal
  [146:1:0.0] District | Up to Rs 50 lakh | To State in 45 days
  [146:1:0.1] State | Rs 50 lakh – Rs 2 crore | To National in 30 days
  [146:1:0.2] National (NCDRC, 1988) | Above Rs 2 crore | To Supreme Court in 30 days
[146:1:1] - Jurisdiction by **value of consideration paid**, not compensation claimed.
[146:1:2] - Called **Consumer Forums** under the 1986 Act; **quasi-judicial**, not civil courts.
[146:1:3] - **e-Daakhil** online filing now merged into **e-Jagriti**.
[146:1:4] Original 2019 slabs (Rs 1 crore / Rs 10 crore) were revised in 2021. A Rs 40 lakh complaint goes to the District Commission.
## SUB 2 | Central Consumer Protection Authority
[146:2:0] - Executive agency; in force from **24 July 2020**.
[146:2:1] - Core: **false or misleading advertisements**; inquire, order **recalls and refunds**, file **class-action** complaints, impose penalties.
[146:2:2] TABLE head: Section 21 | First | Repeat
  [146:2:2.0] Penalty | Up to Rs 10 lakh | Up to Rs 50 lakh
  [146:2:2.1] Endorser ban | Up to 1 year | Up to 3 years
[146:2:3] - Fined **Meta Rs 10 lakh** (walkie-talkie listings).
[146:2:4] - **Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954** bans objectionable drug ads.
## SUB 3 | E-commerce Rules
[146:3:0] - **E-Commerce Rules 2020**: **marketplace** entity = platform only; **inventory** entity = owns goods; grievance officer acknowledges in **48 hours**, redresses in **one month**.
[146:3:1] - **Dark Patterns Guidelines 2023**: **13** dark patterns (false urgency, basket sneaking).
[146:3:2] - **Amendment Rules 2026**: notified **9 Sept 2026**, effective **1 January 2027**; country of origin; prior price = lowest in **30 days**; sponsored listings disclosed; annual self-audit.
## SUB 4 | Helpline, Campaigns, Mediation
[146:4:0] - **National Consumer Helpline: 1915** (toll-free); e-commerce firms are partners; a helpline, not a court.
[146:4:1] - **Jago Grahak Jago**; mascot **"Jagriti"**.
[146:4:2] - **Mediation cells** attached to District, State and National Commissions (not High Courts).
