## SUB 0 | Kanishka's Court, the Fourth Buddhist Council and Mahayana
[0:0] # Fourth Buddhist Council
[0:1] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [0:1.0] Patron | Kanishka (UPRO 2013; IAS 2001) – not Ashoka, Harsha, Menander
  [0:1.1] Place | Kundalavana (Harwan), Kashmir
  [0:1.2] President | Vasumitra (UPSC 2001) – Parsva, Nagarjuna, Sudraka are distractors
  [0:1.3] Vice-president | Ashvaghosha
  [0:1.4] Language | Sanskrit – the first council in Sanskrit
  [0:1.5] Work | Mahavibhasha / Vibhasha Shastras (Vasumitra)
  [0:1.6] Outcome | Mahayana given its final shape; split into Mahayana and Hinayana
[0:2] - **Earlier councils:** 1st Rajagriha – **Ajatashatru**; 2nd Vaishali – **Kalasoka**; 3rd Pataliputra – **Ashoka**.
[0:3] # Court scholars
[0:4] TABLE head: Scholar | Role
  [0:4.0] Ashvaghosha | Court poet; Buddhacharita (WBCS 2008), Saundarananda, Sariputraprakarana
  [0:4.1] Vasumitra | President of the Council
  [0:4.2] Parshva | Urged Kanishka to hold the Council
  [0:4.3] Charaka | Court physician; Charaka Samhita (SSC 2016)
  [0:4.4] Nagarjuna | Madhyamika; Suhrllekha to a Satavahana king; "Einstein of India"
  [0:4.5] Mathara, Sushruta | Also in the traditional court list
[0:5] - **Contemporaries of Kanishka:** **Ashvaghosha and Vasumitra** (UPPCS 1994) – Kalidasa (Gupta) and Kamban (Chola) are not.
[0:6] - **Ashvaghosha's contemporary:** **Kanishka** (UKPCS 2010).
[0:7] - **NOT in Kanishka's court:** **Vishakhadatta** (Gupta-age dramatist) (UPPCS Mains 2005); **Patanjali** (Shunga age, Mahabhashya) (UP Lower 2008).
[0:8] - **⚠ Trap:** Buddhacharita = Ashvaghosha, not Nagasena, Nagarjuna or Vasumitra.
[0:9] - **Nagarjuna in Andhra:** Sriparvata and the Satavahana court; **I-tsing** saw students memorising his Suhrllekha.
[0:10] # Mahayana
[0:11] - **Kashmir link (2026):** stupas at **Zehanpora** (Baramulla) highlighted in 2026 show the valley's Kushana-Gandhara Buddhist heritage.
[0:12] - **Shift:** from Pali-Prakrit Hinayana to **Sanskrit Mahayana**, which worshipped the **Buddha as god through images**; spread to Central Asia, China, Korea, Japan.

## SUB 1 | Kushana Coinage
[1:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Coinage
  [1:0.0] Kujula Kadphises | Copper coins imitating Roman coins
  [1:0.1] Vima Kadphises | First Kushana gold – first gold coins on a large scale, for regular use; Shiva with trident and bull
  [1:0.2] Kanishka | Gold and copper; Buddha (BOΔΔO) on coins; Shiva as Oesho; Bactrian legends in Greek script
[1:1] - **First to issue gold coins on a large scale:** **Vima Kadphises** (State PSC; UPPCS Mains 2009; UPPCS 2015) – Menander's Indo-Greeks issued the first gold, but rarely; Kujula = copper.
[1:2] - **Most copper coins in north and north-west India:** the **Kushanas** (UPPCS 2005); Indo-Greeks, Sakas, Pratiharas are distractors.
[1:3] - **Buddha on coins:** **Kanishka** (UPPCS 2010); not Vima Kadphises, Nahapana or Budhagupta.
[1:4] - **Gold dinara:** Roman weight standard, about **8 g (120 grains)**; purer than Gupta gold.
[1:5] - **Portrait:** Kanishka standing in **peaked helmet, long coat and boots**, sacrificing at an **altar**; **30+ deities** (Greek, Iranian, Indian) on Kushana coins.
[1:6] - **Yaudheya** copper coins (Kushana age): six-headed **Kartikeya** with a peacock (UPPCS Mains 2008); not Vasudeva, Mitra or Indra.

## SUB 2 | Kushana Rulers, Empire and Kanishka
[2:0] # Origin
[2:1] - **Kushanas = Yuezhi (Yueh-chi)** of north-west China/Central Asia – one of five Yuezhi clans; Silk Road middlemen between **Han China, Rome and India**; trade fed a second wave of urbanisation (Mathura, Taxila, Ujjain, Shravasti).
[2:2] - **Empire beyond India:** the **Kushan** empire ran from the Oxus to the Ganga – Central Asia, Afghanistan, parts of Iran (UK UDA 2006).
[2:3] TABLE head: Ruler | Fact
  [2:3.0] Kujula Kadphises (Kadphises I) | Founder; united the five Yuezhi clans; took the Kabul valley – the "Kushan ruler" among the options (APPSC G2 2008; SSC 2016)
  [2:3.1] Vima Taktu | Between Kujula and Vima (Rabatak)
  [2:3.2] Vima Kadphises (Kadphises II) | Extended rule to Mathura and Varanasi; title Sarvalokeshvara ("lord of the whole world") and Maheshvara (RPSC 2024); father of Kanishka
  [2:3.3] Kanishka | Zenith; capitals Purushapura (Peshawar) and Mathura
  [2:3.4] Huvishka | Kanishka's successor
  [2:3.5] Vasudeva I | Last great Kushana; first with an Indian name
[2:4] - **Distractors:** Ugrasena, Vikramaditya, Dantidurga, Pushyamitra are not Kushanas; Sakari, Mahakshatrapa, Simha-chandra are not Vima's titles.
[2:5] - **Kanishka's titles:** Devaputra (China), Shaonano Shao (Persia), Kaisara (Rome), Maharaja (India).
[2:6] # Kanishka
[2:7] - **Coronation / era:** **78 AD**, starting the Saka era (UPPSC 1991) – modern estimates put his accession c. 127 CE (Falk).
[2:8] - **Rabatak inscription (1993, near Surkh Kotal, Afghanistan):** Bactrian language, Greek script; genealogy Kujula → Vima Taktu → Vima Kadphises → Kanishka; cities **Saketa, Kaushambi, Pataliputra, Sri Champa** – **Shravasti is NOT named** (UPRO 2014).
[2:9] - **Era of 78 CE:** called the **Saka era** because the **Western Kshatrapas** used it on their records and coins; also **Salivahana Saka** (a legend credits Gautamiputra Satakarni); it began **135 years after** the Vikrama era (57 BCE); India's national calendar since 1957.
[2:10] - **Trade evidence of the period:** the **Periplus of the Erythraean Sea** (text) and the **Arikamedu** excavation – not Inamgaon.
[2:11] - **Sarnath image:** Bodhisattva dedicated by Friar **Bala** in year 3 (81 AD).
[2:12] - **China:** refused a Han princess, the Kushanas marched against the Chinese general **Pan Chao (Ban Chao)** and were **defeated** (Jharkhand 2003); Ban Yong, Shi Huang Ti, He-Ti are distractors.

## SUB 3 | Central Asian Contributions – Dress, Cavalry, Surkhi and Art
[3:0] - **Dress (Shakas and Kushanas):** **turban, tunic, trousers and heavy long coat** (the sherwani descends from the coat); Central Asians also brought **caps, helmets and boots** → all five items, **I, II, III, IV and V** (APPSC G1 PYQ).
[3:1]   - **⚠ Settled:** the old app key excluded the turban (II, III, IV, V); PMF and R.S. Sharma list the turban first, so the key is all five.
[3:2] - **Cavalry:** better cavalry, wider use of **reins and saddles**, possibly toe-stirrups; not elephants.
[3:3] - **Surkhi** (brick-dust lime mortar): evidence first found in **Kushana** levels at Mathura – the key for "introduced by" (UPPCS Mains 2014); the Sultans used it widely later.
[3:4] - **Social status:** Manu called foreigners (Shakas, Kushanas) who did not follow priestly custom **"fallen Kshatriyas" (Vratya Kshatriyas)** (UPPSC 2014).
[3:5] - **Greatest development of the Kushan period:** **art** (SSC 2005) – Gandhara and Mathura schools under Kushana patronage.
[3:6] - **Gandhara art flourished under:** the **Kushans** (BPSC 1992); not Guptas, Akbar or Mauryas.
[3:7] - **Early Buddha images (1st c. CE):** Gandhara – standing Buddha in pleated robes; Mathura – seated Buddha with a halo; Amaravati – **white limestone** narrative panels (not red sandstone).
[3:8] - **First human statues worshipped in India:** those of the **Buddha** (RAS 2010) – Mahayana image worship.
