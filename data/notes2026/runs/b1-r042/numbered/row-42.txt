## SUB 0 | Milindapanho – Menander and Nagasena
[0:0] # The text
[0:1] - **Milindapanho (Milinda-Panha, "Questions of Milinda"):** a **Pali** Buddhist text – a dialogue on Buddhist doctrine between the Indo-Greek king **Milinda = Menander** and the Buddhist monk **Nagasena** (UPSC 1997 ×2; UPPCS 2023; UKPCS 2012).
[0:2] - **⚠ Trap:** the monk is **Nagasena**, not Nagarjuna (later Mahayana Madhyamika thinker), Nagabhatta (Pratihara king) or Kumarila Bhatta (Mimamsa philosopher); the text is in **Pali**, not Sanskrit.
[0:3] - **Which king it throws light on:** **Menander** – not Demetrius, Diodotus II or Strato I (UKPCS 2012).
[0:4] - **Agriculture:** the Milindapanho explains **eight phases of agricultural work** (UKPCS 2021); Avadanashataka, Aryamanjushrimulakalpa and Dipavamsa are distractors.
[0:5] # Menander (Milinda), c. 165-145 BCE
[0:6] - **Greatest Indo-Greek:** capital **Sakala (Sialkot)**, not Taxila; the only Indo-Greek king named in Indian sources; turned **Buddhist** under Nagasena.
[0:7] - **Coins:** early coins carry Greek deities; later ones Buddhist motifs – **elephant, bull, wheel of Dharma**.
[0:8] - **Bairat (Rajasthan):** a hoard of **16 coins of Menander** (RAS 2018); Nagari, Rairh and Nagar are distractors.
[0:9] - **Raids:** invaded the Ganga-Yamuna doab but could not hold it against the Shungas.
[0:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.146-147; NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.6; Rocket Indian History 01 p.40, p.55; app notes 1:42 (sub 2)
## SUB 1 | Indo-Greek Coins – Firsts, Deities and Metals
[1:0] # Firsts
[1:1] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [1:1.0] Coins definitely attributed to kings | First issued by the Indo-Greeks – first inscribed coins with names and portraits of kings
  [1:1.1] Gold coins | First issued in India by the Indo-Greeks (rare); increased under the Kushanas
  [1:1.2] Metals | Mostly silver, also copper; gold rare
  [1:1.3] Legends | Bilingual: Greek on the obverse, Prakrit in Kharoshthi on the reverse (rarely Brahmi)
  [1:1.4] Number of kings | More than 30 Indo-Greek kings are known mainly from their coins
[1:2] - **Kushana trap:** Kushana coins did NOT precede Indo-Greek portrait coins; Indo-Greeks = first gold, **Kushanas** = large-scale gold.
[1:3] # Indian deities on coins
[1:4] - **Sankarshana (Balarama) and Vasudeva (Krishna):** on the coins of **Agathocles** (c. 190-180 BCE) from **Ai-Khanoum** – the first known depiction of Krishna (UPPCS Mains 2017); not Huvishka, Kanishka or Samudragupta.
[1:5]   - Agathocles held the Paropamisadae and also issued **Greek + Brahmi** coins.
[1:6] - **Indian deities on Indo-Greek coins = Agathocles:** a question asking which Indo-Greek ruler "issued coins depicting Indian deities" is answered **Agathocles**; Menander's coins carry Buddhist **symbols** (wheel, elephant), not Indian deities (APPSC ASO; printed key Menander I).
[1:7] - **Balarama holds the plough** (hala) – hence **Halayudha**; Krishna, Kartikeya and Maitreya are not shown with it (UPPSC Mains 2007).
[1:8] # Other coin facts
[1:9] - **Lead coins:** **Strato II** (UPPSC 2014); Strato I, Demetrius and Menander are distractors.
[1:10] - **Largest ancient gold coin:** **Eucratides** (20 staters).
[1:11] - **Other early coinages:** Saka-Kshatrapas (Rudradaman) – **silver and copper**; **Chandragupta II** – silver **Rupaka** after defeating the Sakas; **Muhammad Ghori** – coins with **Lakshmi**, some with the Arabic **Kalma**; Kanishka – the Buddha; Mahmud of Ghazni – silver coins with Arabic and Sanskrit legends.
[1:12] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.147-148; Rocket Indian History 01 p.55; app notes 1:42 (sub 1, 3, 8); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agathocles_of_Bactria
## SUB 2 | Bactria, Rulers and the Order of Invaders
[2:0] - **Bactria (north Afghanistan):** the Indo-Greek kingdom set up there at the start of the 2nd century BC (UPSC 1999); Scythia = land of the Sakas, Gedrosia = **Balochistan**, Aria = Herat region.
[2:1] - **Independence:** **Diodotus** revolted against the **Seleucids** (mid-3rd c. BCE) and founded the Graeco-Bactrian kingdom.
[2:2] - **Why they came south:** Scythian (Saka) pressure behind them and the weak successors of Ashoka in India.
[2:3] TABLE head: Ruler | Exam fact
  [2:3.0] Demetrius | First Bactrian Greek to cross the Hindu Kush and invade India (c. 180 BCE); Greek defeat is credited to Pushyamitra Shunga, and Kharavela's Hathigumpha mentions a Yavana king retreating
  [2:3.1] Menander | Greatest; Sakala; Milindapanho
  [2:3.2] Antialcidas | Sent Heliodorus to Vidisha
  [2:3.3] Eucratides | 20-stater gold coin
  [2:3.4] Strato | Menander's son; Strato II issued lead coins
  [2:3.5] Hermaius | Last; Greek rule south of the Hindu Kush ended with him
[2:4] - **Raids:** Patanjali's **Mahabhashya** says the Yavanas reached **Saketa** and **Madhyamika**; the Yuga Purana takes them towards **Pataliputra**.
[2:5] - **Successors:** the **Sakas** replaced the Indo-Greeks first (Azes defeated the last Indo-Greek kings), then the Parthians, then the Kushanas.
[2:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.145-146; PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.5; app notes 1:42 (sub 0, 1)
## SUB 3 | Greek Impact – Military Governors, Heliodorus and Culture
[3:0] - **Military governorship:** first introduced in India by the **Greeks** (UPSC 2000) – governors called **strategos**; not the Shakas, Parthians or Mughals.
[3:1] - **Heliodorus:** a **Bhagavata** from **Takshashila (Taxila)** (UP UDA/LDA 2010), envoy of **Antialcidas** to the Shunga king **Bhagabhadra**; set up the **Besnagar (Vidisha) Garuda pillar** in honour of **Vasudeva**.
[3:2] - **Other envoys:** **Daimachos** – Antiochus I to **Bindusara**; **Megasthenes** – Seleucus I to Chandragupta Maurya.
[3:3] - **Why foreigners chose Buddhism:** Greeks, Kushanas and Shakas embraced Buddhism because it **provided easier access to Indian society** – no caste barrier to entry (UPSC 1998); "caste-ridden Hinduism did not attract them" is the tempting half-truth.
[3:4] - **Absorption:** the Smritis call the absorbed Greeks **Vratya Kshatriyas** (second-class Kshatriyas).
[3:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:5.0] Yavanika | Stage / theatre curtain
  [3:5.1] Hora | Horoscope
  [3:5.2] Romaka Siddhanta | Greek influence on Indian astronomy
[3:6] - **Art:** Hellenistic art fed the **Gandhara** school of the Kushana period.
[3:7] - **Philosophical exchange:** Milindapanho; religious interaction: Heliodorus; artistic synthesis: Gandhara.
[3:8] - **Match:** Milindapanho – Menander; **Taxila copper plate – Patika** (a Shaka); Junagadh – Rudradaman I; Gandhara art – Kushanas.
[3:9] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.5-6, p.20-21; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.148; app notes 1:42 (sub 2, 5)
