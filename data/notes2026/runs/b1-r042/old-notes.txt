=== NOTES 1:42 | Indo-greeks – Menander, Milindapanho and Indo-greek Coinage
## SUB 0 | Bactrian Greeks – Origin and Entry
[42:0:0] - After Alexander, Greeks settled in **Bactria** (**north Afghanistan**), early 2nd c. BC kingdom.
[42:0:1] - **Diodotus I** revolted against the **Seleucids** (3rd c. BCE) and founded free Bactria.
[42:0:2] - Pressed by the **Scythians (Sakas)** and helped by **Ashoka's weak successors**, they moved south into India.
[42:0:3] - Raids reached **Saketa, Madhyamika**, towards **Pataliputra** (account in Patanjali's **Mahabhashya**).
[42:0:4] - Invader order: **Greeks → Sakas → Parthians (Pahlavas) → Kushanas**.
[42:0:5] Scythia = land of the Scythians (Sakas); Gedrosia = today's Balochistan, not linked to Indo-Greeks.
## SUB 1 | Indo-greek Rulers
[42:1:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Contribution / coin feature
  [42:1:0.0] Demetrius | First to invade India (c. 180 BCE); defeated by Pushyamitra, and by Kharavela (Hathigumpha inscription)
  [42:1:0.1] Menander I (Milinda) | Greatest; 165-145 BCE; capital Sakala (Sialkot); coins with Indian symbols (Buddhist wheel)
  [42:1:0.2] Antialcidas | Sent Heliodorus to Vidisha
  [42:1:0.3] Agathocles | Coins with Sankarshana (Balarama) and Vasudeva (Krishna); ruled c. 190 BC; held Paropamisadae; first Greco-Bactrian ruler with bilingual coin legends
  [42:1:0.4] Eucratides | Largest ancient gold coin (20 staters)
  [42:1:0.5] Strato | Menander's son
  [42:1:0.6] Hermaius | Last; his defeat ended Greek rule south of the Hindu Kush
[42:1:1] First = Demetrius; greatest = Menander; last = Hermaius.
## SUB 2 | Menander and Milindapanho
[42:2:0] - **Milindapanho** ("Questions of Milinda"), in **Pali**: dialogue between King Milinda (Menander) and Buddhist monk **Nagasena**; Menander turned **Buddhist**.
[42:2:1] - It also explains **eight phases of agricultural work**.
[42:2:2] - **Bairat (Rajasthan)**: hoard of **16 coins of Menander**.
[42:2:3] - Greeks, Kushanas and Sakas took up **Buddhism**: it gave easier entry into Indian society (no rigid caste barriers).
[42:2:4] Match: Milindapanho | Menander
Taxila copper plate | Patika
Junagadh inscription | Rudradaman I
Gandhara art | Kushanas
## SUB 3 | Indo-greek Coinage and Its Significance
[42:3:0] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [42:3:0.0] Coins attributed to kings | First in India = Indo-Greeks
  [42:3:0.1] Gold coins | First issued in India by Indo-Greeks (2nd c. BC); but mostly silver and copper
  [42:3:0.2] Legends | Bilingual: Greek on front + Kharoshthi (Prakrit) on back; Agathocles also issued Greek + Brahmi coins
  [42:3:0.3] Portraits | Royal portraits
  [42:3:0.4] Lead | Strato II
  [42:3:0.5] Deities | Zeus, Athena, Heracles; Vasudeva, Sankarshana; Buddhist wheel
[42:3:1] - Coins are the main source for the list of **more than 30** Indo-Greek kings.
[42:3:2] Kushana coins did NOT come before Indo-Greek coins in portraits. Indo-Greeks = first gold; **Kushanas** (Vima Kadphises) = large-scale, regular gold.

## SUB 6 | Contributions – Coinage, Dress, Military
[43:6:0] - **Coinage**: many **silver** coins in western India (not only gold); **Kardamaka silver coins carry Saka-era dates**; Chashtana issued silver and copper (copper relatively rare).
[43:6:1] - **Gold-silver ratio 1:35**: 1 suvarna = 35 karshapanas (Nasik record).
[43:6:2] - **Dress**: Shakas and Kushanas introduced **trousers, long coats, helmets, boots**.
[43:6:3] - **Military**: Shakas, Parthians, Kushanas had **better cavalry** (not elephants).
[43:6:4] - **Epigraphy**: Sanskrit enters long royal records with Junagadh.
