=== Milindapanho – Menander and Nagasena
# The text
- **Milindapanho (Milinda-Panha, "Questions of Milinda"):** a **Pali** Buddhist text – a dialogue on Buddhist doctrine between the Indo-Greek king **Milinda = Menander** and the Buddhist monk **Nagasena** (UPSC 1997 ×2; UPPCS 2023; UKPCS 2012).
- **⚠ Trap:** the monk is **Nagasena**, not Nagarjuna (later Mahayana Madhyamika thinker), Nagabhatta (Pratihara king) or Kumarila Bhatta (Mimamsa philosopher); the text is in **Pali**, not Sanskrit.
- **Which king it throws light on:** **Menander** – not Demetrius, Diodotus II or Strato I (UKPCS 2012).
- **Agriculture:** the Milindapanho explains **eight phases of agricultural work** (UKPCS 2021); Avadanashataka, Aryamanjushrimulakalpa and Dipavamsa are distractors.
# Menander (Milinda), c. 165-145 BCE
- **Greatest Indo-Greek:** capital **Sakala (Sialkot)**, not Taxila; the only Indo-Greek king named in Indian sources; turned **Buddhist** under Nagasena.
- **Coins:** early coins carry Greek deities; later ones Buddhist motifs – **elephant, bull, wheel of Dharma**.
- **Bairat (Rajasthan):** a hoard of **16 coins of Menander** (RAS 2018); Nagari, Rairh and Nagar are distractors.
- **Raids:** invaded the Ganga-Yamuna doab but could not hold it against the Shungas.

=== Indo-Greek Coins – Firsts, Deities and Metals
# Firsts
| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Coins definitely attributed to kings | **First issued by the Indo-Greeks** – first inscribed coins with names and portraits of kings |
| Gold coins | **First issued in India by the Indo-Greeks** (rare); increased under the Kushanas |
| Metals | Mostly **silver**, also copper; gold rare |
| Legends | Bilingual: **Greek** on the obverse, **Prakrit in Kharoshthi** on the reverse (rarely Brahmi) |
| Number of kings | **More than 30** Indo-Greek kings are known mainly from their coins |
- **Kushana trap:** Kushana coins did NOT precede Indo-Greek portrait coins; Indo-Greeks = first gold, **Kushanas** = large-scale gold.
# Indian deities on coins
- **Sankarshana (Balarama) and Vasudeva (Krishna):** on the coins of **Agathocles** (c. 190-180 BCE) from **Ai-Khanoum** – the first known depiction of Krishna (UPPCS Mains 2017); not Huvishka, Kanishka or Samudragupta.
  - Agathocles held the Paropamisadae and also issued **Greek + Brahmi** coins.
- **Indian deities on Indo-Greek coins = Agathocles:** a question asking which Indo-Greek ruler "issued coins depicting Indian deities" is answered **Agathocles**; Menander's coins carry Buddhist **symbols** (wheel, elephant), not Indian deities (APPSC ASO; printed key Menander I).
- **Balarama holds the plough** (hala) – hence **Halayudha**; Krishna, Kartikeya and Maitreya are not shown with it (UPPSC Mains 2007).
# Other coin facts
- **Lead coins:** **Strato II** (UPPSC 2014); Strato I, Demetrius and Menander are distractors.
- **Largest ancient gold coin:** **Eucratides** (20 staters).

=== Bactria, Rulers and the Order of Invaders
- **Bactria (north Afghanistan):** the Indo-Greek kingdom set up there at the start of the 2nd century BC (UPSC 1999); Scythia = land of the Sakas, Gedrosia = **Balochistan**, Aria = Herat region.
- **Independence:** **Diodotus** revolted against the **Seleucids** (mid-3rd c. BCE) and founded the Graeco-Bactrian kingdom.
- **Why they came south:** Scythian (Saka) pressure behind them and the weak successors of Ashoka in India.
| Ruler | Exam fact |
|---|---|
| **Demetrius** | First Bactrian Greek to cross the Hindu Kush and invade India (c. 180 BCE); Greek defeat is credited to Pushyamitra Shunga, and Kharavela's Hathigumpha mentions a Yavana king retreating |
| **Menander** | Greatest; Sakala; Milindapanho |
| **Antialcidas** | Sent **Heliodorus** to Vidisha |
| **Eucratides** | 20-stater gold coin |
| **Strato** | Menander's son; Strato II issued lead coins |
| **Hermaius** | Last; Greek rule south of the Hindu Kush ended with him |
- **Raids:** Patanjali's **Mahabhashya** says the Yavanas reached **Saketa** and **Madhyamika**; the Yuga Purana takes them towards **Pataliputra**.
- **Successors:** the **Sakas** replaced the Indo-Greeks first (Azes defeated the last Indo-Greek kings), then the Parthians, then the Kushanas.

=== Greek Impact – Military Governors, Heliodorus and Culture
- **Military governorship:** first introduced in India by the **Greeks** (UPSC 2000) – governors called **strategos**; not the Shakas, Parthians or Mughals.
- **Heliodorus:** a **Bhagavata** from **Takshashila (Taxila)** (UP UDA/LDA 2010), envoy of **Antialcidas** to the Shunga king **Bhagabhadra**; set up the **Besnagar (Vidisha) Garuda pillar** in honour of **Vasudeva**.
- **Other envoys:** **Daimachos** – Antiochus I to **Bindusara**; **Megasthenes** – Seleucus I to Chandragupta Maurya.
- **Why foreigners chose Buddhism:** Greeks, Kushanas and Shakas embraced Buddhism because it **provided easier access to Indian society** – no caste barrier to entry (UPSC 1998); "caste-ridden Hinduism did not attract them" is the tempting half-truth.
- **Absorption:** the Smritis call the absorbed Greeks **Vratya Kshatriyas** (second-class Kshatriyas).
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Yavanika** | Stage / theatre curtain |
| **Hora** | Horoscope |
| **Romaka Siddhanta** | Greek influence on Indian **astronomy** |
- **Art:** Hellenistic art fed the **Gandhara** school of the Kushana period.
- **Philosophical exchange:** Milindapanho; religious interaction: Heliodorus; artistic synthesis: Gandhara.
- **Match:** Milindapanho – Menander; **Taxila copper plate – Patika** (a Shaka); Junagadh – Rudradaman I; Gandhara art – Kushanas.
