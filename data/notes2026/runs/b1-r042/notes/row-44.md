=== Kanishka's Court, the Fourth Buddhist Council and Mahayana
# Fourth Buddhist Council
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Patron | **Kanishka** (UPRO 2013; IAS 2001) – not Ashoka, Harsha, Menander |
| Place | **Kundalavana (Harwan), Kashmir** |
| President | **Vasumitra** (UPSC 2001) – Parsva, Nagarjuna, Sudraka are distractors |
| Vice-president | **Ashvaghosha** |
| Language | **Sanskrit** – the first council in Sanskrit |
| Work | **Mahavibhasha / Vibhasha Shastras** (Vasumitra) |
| Outcome | Mahayana given its final shape; split into Mahayana and Hinayana |
- **Earlier councils:** 1st Rajagriha – **Ajatashatru**; 2nd Vaishali – **Kalasoka**; 3rd Pataliputra – **Ashoka**.
# Court scholars
| Scholar | Role |
|---|---|
| **Ashvaghosha** | Court poet; **Buddhacharita** (WBCS 2008), Saundarananda, Sariputraprakarana |
| **Vasumitra** | President of the Council |
| **Parshva** | Urged Kanishka to hold the Council |
| **Charaka** | **Court physician**; Charaka Samhita (SSC 2016) |
| **Nagarjuna** | Madhyamika; Suhrllekha to a Satavahana king; "Einstein of India" |
| Mathara, Sushruta | Also in the traditional court list |
- **Contemporaries of Kanishka:** **Ashvaghosha and Vasumitra** (UPPCS 1994) – Kalidasa (Gupta) and Kamban (Chola) are not.
- **Ashvaghosha's contemporary:** **Kanishka** (UKPCS 2010).
- **NOT in Kanishka's court:** **Vishakhadatta** (Gupta-age dramatist) (UPPCS Mains 2005); **Patanjali** (Shunga age, Mahabhashya) (UP Lower 2008).
- **⚠ Trap:** Buddhacharita = Ashvaghosha, not Nagasena, Nagarjuna or Vasumitra.
- **Nagarjuna in Andhra:** Sriparvata and the Satavahana court; **I-tsing** saw students memorising his Suhrllekha.
# Mahayana
- **Shift:** from Pali-Prakrit Hinayana to **Sanskrit Mahayana**, which worshipped the **Buddha as god through images**; spread to Central Asia, China, Korea, Japan.

=== Kushana Coinage
| Ruler | Coinage |
|---|---|
| **Kujula Kadphises** | **Copper** coins imitating Roman coins |
| **Vima Kadphises** | **First Kushana gold** – first gold coins on a **large scale, for regular use**; Shiva with trident and bull |
| **Kanishka** | Gold and copper; **Buddha (BOΔΔO)** on coins; Shiva as Oesho; Bactrian legends in Greek script |
- **First to issue gold coins on a large scale:** **Vima Kadphises** (State PSC; UPPCS Mains 2009; UPPCS 2015) – Menander's Indo-Greeks issued the first gold, but rarely; Kujula = copper.
- **Most copper coins in north and north-west India:** the **Kushanas** (UPPCS 2005); Indo-Greeks, Sakas, Pratiharas are distractors.
- **Buddha on coins:** **Kanishka** (UPPCS 2010); not Vima Kadphises, Nahapana or Budhagupta.
- **Gold dinara:** Roman weight standard, about **8 g (120 grains)**; purer than Gupta gold.
- **Portrait:** Kanishka standing in **peaked helmet, long coat and boots**, sacrificing at an **altar**; **30+ deities** (Greek, Iranian, Indian) on Kushana coins.
- **Yaudheya** copper coins (Kushana age): six-headed **Kartikeya** with a peacock (UPPCS Mains 2008); not Vasudeva, Mitra or Indra.

=== Kushana Rulers, Empire and Kanishka
# Origin
- **Kushanas = Yuezhi (Yueh-chi)** of north-west China/Central Asia – one of five Yuezhi clans; Silk Road middlemen between **Han China, Rome and India**; trade fed a second wave of urbanisation (Mathura, Taxila, Ujjain, Shravasti).
- **Empire beyond India:** the **Kushan** empire ran from the Oxus to the Ganga – Central Asia, Afghanistan, parts of Iran (UK UDA 2006).
| Ruler | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Kujula Kadphises (Kadphises I)** | Founder; united the five Yuezhi clans; took the Kabul valley – the "Kushan ruler" among the options (APPSC G2 2008; SSC 2016) |
| Vima Taktu | Between Kujula and Vima (Rabatak) |
| **Vima Kadphises (Kadphises II)** | Extended rule to Mathura and Varanasi; title **Sarvalokeshvara** ("lord of the whole world") and Maheshvara (RPSC 2024); father of Kanishka |
| **Kanishka** | Zenith; capitals **Purushapura (Peshawar)** and Mathura |
| Huvishka | Kanishka's successor |
| **Vasudeva I** | Last great Kushana; first with an Indian name |
- **Distractors:** Ugrasena, Vikramaditya, Dantidurga, Pushyamitra are not Kushanas; Sakari, Mahakshatrapa, Simha-chandra are not Vima's titles.
- **Kanishka's titles:** Devaputra (China), Shaonano Shao (Persia), Kaisara (Rome), Maharaja (India).
# Kanishka
- **Coronation / era:** **78 AD**, starting the Saka era (UPPSC 1991) – modern estimates put his accession c. 127 CE (Falk).
- **Rabatak inscription (1993, near Surkh Kotal, Afghanistan):** Bactrian language, Greek script; genealogy Kujula → Vima Taktu → Vima Kadphises → Kanishka; cities **Saketa, Kaushambi, Pataliputra, Sri Champa** – **Shravasti is NOT named** (UPRO 2014).
- **Sarnath image:** Bodhisattva dedicated by Friar **Bala** in year 3 (81 AD).
- **China:** refused a Han princess, the Kushanas marched against the Chinese general **Pan Chao (Ban Chao)** and were **defeated** (Jharkhand 2003); Ban Yong, Shi Huang Ti, He-Ti are distractors.

=== Central Asian Contributions – Dress, Cavalry, Surkhi and Art
- **Dress (Shakas and Kushanas):** **turban, tunic, trousers and heavy long coat** (the sherwani descends from the coat); Central Asians also brought **caps, helmets and boots** → all five items, **I, II, III, IV and V** (APPSC G1 PYQ).
  - **⚠ Settled:** the old app key excluded the turban (II, III, IV, V); PMF and R.S. Sharma list the turban first, so the key is all five.
- **Cavalry:** better cavalry, wider use of **reins and saddles**, possibly toe-stirrups; not elephants.
- **Surkhi** (brick-dust lime mortar): evidence first found in **Kushana** levels at Mathura – the key for "introduced by" (UPPCS Mains 2014); the Sultans used it widely later.
- **Social status:** Manu called foreigners (Shakas, Kushanas) who did not follow priestly custom **"fallen Kshatriyas" (Vratya Kshatriyas)** (UPPSC 2014).
- **Greatest development of the Kushan period:** **art** (SSC 2005) – Gandhara and Mathura schools under Kushana patronage.
- **Gandhara art flourished under:** the **Kushans** (BPSC 1992); not Guptas, Akbar or Mauryas.
- **First human statues worshipped in India:** those of the **Buddha** (RAS 2010) – Mahayana image worship.
