=== NOTES 3:76 | Exchange Rate Systems and Rupee Movement; Currency Internationalisation
## SUB 0 | Exchange Rate and Forex Market Terms
[76:0:0] - **Exchange rate**: the price or value of one currency in terms of another.
[76:0:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [76:0:1.0] Direct quote | Units of domestic currency per one unit of foreign currency (e.g. ₹95 = $1)
  [76:0:1.1] Indirect quote | Units of foreign currency per one unit of domestic currency
  [76:0:1.2] Spot rate | Immediate delivery (settlement within two days)
  [76:0:1.3] Forward rate | Agreed now, delivered at a future date (importer's forward contract = hedging)
  [76:0:1.4] Arbitrage | Buy currency where cheaper and simultaneously sell where dearer
  [76:0:1.5] Hedging | Covering exchange-rate risk (forwards); speculation = open position on expected moves
  [76:0:1.6] Hot currency | Foreign currency that moves quickly, chasing higher short-term interest rates; hard = highest global faith, soft = easily available
  [76:0:1.7] Import cover | Months of imports payable from reserves
  [76:0:1.8] Tobin Tax | Tax on foreign exchange transactions; named after James Tobin
[76:0:2] PYQ "false" statement: the direct quote defined as foreign units per one domestic unit (definitions swapped).
[76:0:3] - Market participants: commercial banks, authorised dealers/brokers, monetary authorities. **Exchange banks (AD Category-I)** sanction forex for imports under FEMA.
[76:0:4] - Costliest of the common currencies: **Pound Sterling** (₹125.18 vs Euro ₹109.05, US$ ₹93.00, 17 April 2026).
## SUB 1 | Devaluation, Depreciation, Appreciation, Revaluation
[76:1:0] TABLE head: Term | System | Meaning | Done by
  [76:1:0.0] Devaluation | Fixed | Official reduction of value; Rs per $ raised | Government/monetary authority
  [76:1:0.1] Revaluation | Fixed | Official rise in value | Government/monetary authority
  [76:1:0.2] Appreciation | Flexible | Market rise; fewer rupees per $ | Demand and supply
  [76:1:0.3] Depreciation | Flexible | Market fall; more rupees per $ | Demand and supply
[76:1:1] # Reading a rate change
[76:1:2] - **$1 = ₹60 → ₹58**: rupee **appreciated**, dollar depreciated.
[76:1:3] - **$1 = ₹60 → ₹65** by market forces: rupee **depreciated**. ₹94 → ₹96 = depreciated; ₹96 → ₹94 = appreciated.
## SUB 2 | Effects of Devaluation / Depreciation
[76:2:0] - Makes **exports cheaper** abroad (lowers export prices in foreign currency) and **imports dearer** at home → **promotes exports**.
[76:2:1] - **Expenditure-switching**: spending switches to home-produced goods.
[76:2:2] - **Devaluation improves the balance of trade** (A true) - but the reason "it **raises** export prices" is **false** (APPSC G2 2025: A true, R false).
[76:2:3] - It does not **automatically** improve the trade balance - import costs rise too.
[76:2:4] - After **1991**, export growth rose due to devaluation (~18%).
[76:2:5] - **Limits**: **imported inflation**; inelastic oil demand → falling rupee widens the CAD; costlier oil and **ECB repayment**; a falling rupee makes buyers delay orders.
[76:2:6] - Net impact (ES 2025-26): a weaker currency helps the trade balance more than it hurts through capital outflows → net positive.
[76:2:7] - **South-East Asian devaluations (1997-98)** were unfavourable for Indian exports.
[76:2:8] - Recognised BoP correction methods: devaluation, exchange depreciation, exchange control, monetary/fiscal measures, import curbs.
## SUB 3 | Neer and Reer
[76:3:0] - **NEER**: weighted average of bilateral **nominal** rates against a **trade-weighted basket** of partner currencies; **base 2015-16 = 100**.
[76:3:1] - **REER** = NEER adjusted for relative **inflation** differentials (REER = NEER × domestic prices / foreign prices); CPI-based; the **better competitiveness measure**.
[76:3:2] - RBI indices: **40-currency** and **6-currency** trade-weighted (6-currency has a moving base).
[76:3:3] - **Rise in NEER/REER = rupee appreciation**: imports cheaper, exports less competitive. **High/rising REER = overvalued rupee**; low = undervalued.
[76:3:4] - Calendar 2025 (provisional): NEER **86.75**, REER **99.72**.
[76:3:5] A rise in REER does NOT mean better competitiveness - it signals an overvalued rupee.
## SUB 4 | Exchange Rate Regimes
[76:4:0] TABLE head: Regime | Meaning
  [76:4:0.0] Fixed / pegged | Fixed to a currency, basket or gold; government fixes the rate, RBI buys/sells to hold it
  [76:4:0.1] Floating / flexible | Set by demand and supply of foreign currency
  [76:4:0.2] Managed float | Market-set with central-bank intervention when necessary (direct forex deals or interest rates) - India's system
[76:4:1] - Post-Bretton Woods classes: **fixed** - currency unions, currency boards (Argentina 1991-Jan 2002), pegs; **intermediate** - bands, crawling pegs; **flexible** - managed and independent float.
[76:4:2] - India's current policy = **market-determined** exchange rate.
[76:4:3] The World Bank does NOT determine a currency's price.
## SUB 5 | Bretton Woods, Imf and India's Exchange Rate History
[76:5:0] # Bretton Woods and IMF
[76:5:1] - **Bretton Woods Conference** (UN Monetary and Financial Conference): **44 nations**, **1-22 July 1944**, New Hampshire; fixed rates based on the **dollar**.
[76:5:2] - Led to **IMF and IBRD** (Bretton Woods Twins, both HQ **Washington, D.C.**) - **not** the UN or WTO.
[76:5:3] - **IMF and World Bank formally set up 27 December 1945**; **India a founding member**.
[76:5:4] - IMF: monetary stability; lends for BoP problems, **only to members**. Members: **190** (PYQ key); **Liechtenstein 191st (Oct 2024)**.
[76:5:5] - India's IMF quota: **2.75%** of SDR quota; **2.63%** of votes (March 2026).
[76:5:6] # India's timeline

  [76:5:7.1] 1971 | Smithsonian Agreement (±2.5% band)
  [76:5:7.2] Sept 1975 | Rupee delinked from pound sterling; pegged to a basket of currencies
  [76:5:7.3] 1992-93 | LERMS (Union Budget 1992-93): dual rate - 40% official, 60% market; partial convertibility
  [76:5:7.4] 1 March 1993 | Rates unified, market-determined (managed float)
  [76:5:7.5] August 1994 | Article VIII - current account convertibility
  [76:5:7.6] 2010 | Rupee symbol ₹

## SUB 7 | Convertibility and Fera to Fema
[76:7:0] - **Convertibility**: free conversion of the rupee into other currencies and vice versa. **Capital account convertibility** = rupee exchangeable to trade financial assets.
[76:7:1] - India: **full current account**, only **partial capital account** convertibility.
[76:7:2] TABLE head: Committee | Facts
  [76:7:2.0] Tarapore I | Set up 28 Feb 1997; report May 1997; roadmap to capital account convertibility
  [76:7:2.1] Tarapore II (Fuller CAC) | Appointed 20 March 2006; report 31 July 2006; three phases over five years to 2010-11
[76:7:3] - **FERA 1973** (restrictive) → **FEMA 1999**: proposed in the **1997-98 Budget**; enacted 1999; **in force 1 June 2000** with a **two-year sunset clause**; finally implemented 2002.
[76:7:4] - Limit on forex for current-account heads lowered in 2000 (FEMA); FCA actually rose.
[76:7:5] FERA's replacement is FEMA, 1999. One APPSC key credited a "Foreign Exchange Control Act" - no such replacement Act exists.
## SUB 8 | Determinants and Impossible Trinity
[76:8:0] # Determinants
[76:8:1] - Demand and supply of forex; **inflation** (lowers the rupee's value), **fiscal deficit**, **CAD**, forex/import cover, short-term debt, interest rates, capital flows.
[76:8:2] - **Raghuram Rajan**: high inflation, high fiscal deficit, high **crude oil prices** weaken the rupee.
[76:8:3] - **PPP**: long-run rate linked to the inflation differential. Long-run anchors (ES 2025-26): PPP, **savings-investment balance**, current account (East Asian lesson: manufacturing exports).
[76:8:4] - Higher imports need not cause depreciation if backed by **net investment inflows**.
[76:8:5] - **Large capital inflows** → **appreciation** (linked with **Dutch disease**).
[76:8:6] - **Hawkish stance / repo hike** supports the rupee by attracting **carry-trade** capital.
[76:8:7] - **Deliberate undervaluation** (as alleged of China) = **currency manipulation**.
[76:8:8] # Impossible trinity (Mundell-Fleming trilemma)
[76:8:9] - A country cannot have at once: **fixed exchange rate + free capital flows + independent monetary policy**.
[76:8:10] - **Mundell-Fleming trilemma:** another name for the impossible trinity - a fixed exchange rate, free capital flows and an independent monetary policy cannot all be had together.
## SUB 9 | Rupee Movement and Rbi Tools
[76:9:0] # Rupee in 2025-26
[76:9:1] - **FY 2025-26 (to Jan 2026)**: rupee depreciated about **5.4%** (US tariffs, outflows); among the most depreciated, with the yen.
[76:9:2] - **18-19 May 2026**: crossed **₹96.2/$**, down 6.3% since 1 Jan 2026; **Brent near $110** + **FPI outflows over ₹2 lakh crore**.
[76:9:3] - Rupee at 95-96/$ through 2026 on the West Asia crude shock; two-month high **₹94.60** on 3 Sep 2026.
[76:9:4] # RBI tools
[76:9:5] - Aim: curb **excessive volatility**, not defend a fixed level.
[76:9:6] TABLE head: Tool | Example
  [76:9:6.0] Spot intervention | Selling dollars from reserves
  [76:9:6.1] Forex swaps | $5 bn, 3-year dollar-rupee buy/sell swap (Dec 2025; May 2026): banks sell dollars to RBI and buy back later at a premium; adds rupee liquidity
  [76:9:6.2] NDF curbs | Limit domestic banks' offshore NDF (Singapore/London) role; Net Open Position limits; Apr 2026 action on NDD contracts
  [76:9:6.3] FCNR(B) support | RBI bore full hedging cost on fresh FCNR(B) deposits till 30 Sep 2026; special measures mobilised $136.38 bn by 31 Aug 2026
  [76:9:6.4] Interest-rate signal | Repo 5.25%, unchanged for the 4th meeting (Aug 2026)
  [76:9:6.5] ECB easing | Capital-inflow step
[76:9:7] - **NOT RBI tools**: **fiscal policy adjustments** (APPSC 2025); **import curbs and export promotion** = Government structural measures.
[76:9:8] - Reserves fell **$23.6 bn in FY26** defending the rupee.

[76:10:4] # SDR
[76:10:5] - **SDR** = **artificial currency / international reserve asset** created by the IMF in **1969** (First Amendment, agreed 1967); "**paper gold**"; supplements members' official reserves; interest-bearing.
[76:10:6] - **Form: book-keeping entries** in IMF records (PYQ 2025); where options are only paper currency/gold/silver, the key chose paper currency.
[76:10:7] - First defined in gold (35 SDR = 1 ounce); redefined as a currency basket after Bretton Woods collapsed (1973).
[76:10:8] - Basket since **1 Oct 2016**: **US dollar, euro, renminbi, yen, pound sterling** - **no rupee**. Renminbi added (decided 2015). Weights from 1 Aug 2022: dollar 43.38%, euro 29.31%, renminbi 12.28%, yen 7.59%, pound 7.44%.
[76:10:9] - Largest general allocation: about **$650 bn (SDR 456 bn), August 2021**.
[76:10:10] - **Reserve (gold) tranche**: part of IMF quota paid in reserve assets (historically gold); **unconditional** drawing right.
## SUB 11 | Swaps and Rupee Internationalisation
[76:11:0] # Swap arrangements
[76:11:1] - **SAARC Currency Swap Framework**: RBI, **2012**; short-term BoP liquidity; **3-month** tenor, rollable twice. 2024-27: **₹250 bn INR window** + **$2 bn** USD-euro facility.
[76:11:2] - **Maldives** drew $400 mn (Oct 2024) and made the **first INR drawdown** (₹30 bn, **April 2026**).
[76:11:3] - **India-Japan BSA**: renewed from **28 Feb 2026**; two-way; local currencies against US dollars.
[76:11:4] # INR trade settlement
[76:11:5] - **RBI framework, July 2022**; built into **FTP 2023**: invoicing and settlement in rupees; cuts dollar dependence and exchange risk.
[76:11:6] - **Special Rupee Vostro Accounts**: foreign banks' rupee accounts in Indian banks (Russia, UAE, Tanzania); since 2025 surplus may go into **corporate bonds and CPs**.
[76:11:7] - **Vostro ("yours")** = foreign bank's account with a domestic bank; **Nostro ("ours")** = our account abroad.
[76:11:8] - **SWIFT:** **Belgium**-based cooperative **messaging** network (1973); sends payment instructions, does **not** move funds or impose sanctions
[76:11:9] - UAE: rupee-dirham settlement; rupee accepted at Dubai airports from July 2023.
[76:11:10] # Symbol and bonds
[76:11:11] - **Rupee symbol ₹**: adopted **15 July 2010**; designed by **D. Udaya Kumar**.
[76:11:12] - **Masala bond**: rupee-denominated, issued **abroad**; investor bears currency risk; **first by IFC, Nov 2014** (IFC named it); **Kerala first state (2019)**; first listed in **Luxembourg**.

[76:11:14] - **Resurgent India Bonds**: SBI, **5 August 1998**, five years; in US dollar, pound sterling, Deutsche Mark.
[76:11:15] - **Euro**: single currency of the European Monetary Union; used by **20 of 27** EU states. **Seychelles** currency = rupee; Bhutan ngultrum, Malaysia ringgit, Maldives rufiyaa.
