=== Civil Aviation – History, Bodies and Firsts
# Milestones
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1911** | First flight: airmail **Allahabad (Prayagraj) – Naini**, about 10 km, **18 Feb 1911** |
| **1953** | **Air Corporations Act** (assent 28 May 1953): air transport **nationalised** → **Indian Airlines** (domestic) + **Air India International** |
| 1995 | **Airports Authority of India (AAI)** formed; manages most Indian airports |
| 1999 | **Cochin (CIAL)** – first airport owned by a **public limited company** (PPP) |
| 2012 | **Kingfisher Airlines** stopped flying (licence suspended, Oct 2012) |
| 2016 | **UDAN** (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik) launched – works through **viability gap funding** |
| 2017 | India's first integrated **heliport**: **Rohini, Delhi** (Pawan Hans) |
| 24 Jun 2020 | **Kushinagar** (UP) declared an international airport |
- **1953 nationalisation:** **9 airline companies** in most keys – **8 domestic** carriers merged into Indian Airlines + Air-India International.
- **UDAN fare cap:** about half the seats on a ~1-hour (~500 km) flight capped at **Rs 2,500**.
- **Training:** **Civil Aviation Training Centre – Allahabad (Bamrauli)**; **Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Akademi – Fursatganj, Raebareli (UP)**.
- **Aakhiri Ahuti (Jan 2017):** **IndiGo** + Delhi Police fly **mortal remains** of needy **North-East** people free of cost.
- **Airlines:** **KLM** (Netherlands, 1919) = oldest airline still under its original name; **Lufthansa = Germany**.
- **Mayday** = international radio **distress call** (life-threatening emergency) at sea or in the air.
- **IC-814 hijack (Dec 1999):** Kathmandu → **Amritsar → Lahore → Dubai → Kandahar**.
> Exam angle: "1911 / Allahabad-Naini", "1953 / Air Corporations Act", "Cochin = public limited company" are the repeats.

=== Airports and Aviation Market
- **Operational airports:** **74 (2014) → 164 (2025)**; PMF: **149** operational in 2023 with **33 international** (older exam key: **16** international airports).
- India = **3rd largest domestic** aviation market.
- **Busiest airport:** **Delhi IGI** (~78.1 million passengers in 2025; 11th in world); Mumbai 2nd (~55.5 million).
- **Cochin (CIAL)** = world's **first fully solar-powered airport**.
- **Veer Savarkar International Airport = Port Blair** (Sri Vijaya Puram), Andaman and Nicobar.
- **Highest airports:** **Leh** = India's highest commercial airport; world's highest = **Daocheng Yading, China** (~4,411 m); **Lhasa Gonggar ~3,570 m** – the answer when Kathmandu, Tehran, Tashkent are the other options.

=== Major Ports – Types and Locations
| Port | State | Type / feature |
|---|---|---|

| **JNPA (Nhava Sheva)** | Maharashtra | Built to relieve Mumbai; **largest container port** |
| **Mormugao (Marmugao)** | **Goa** | Natural port at the **Zuari** estuary; major port 1963 |
| New Mangalore | Karnataka | Artificial **lagoon** port; Kudremukh iron ore |
| Kochi | Kerala | Natural harbour (Vembanad) |
| **VOC Tuticorin** | Tamil Nadu | Artificial deep-sea harbour |
| **Chennai** | Tamil Nadu | **Oldest (and largest) artificial** port |
| **Kamarajar (Ennore)** | Tamil Nadu | 12th major port; **first corporatised** port |
| **Visakhapatnam** | Andhra Pradesh | **Deepest land-locked and protected** port; "Brightest Jewel" |
| Paradip | Odisha | Deep-water port, Mahanadi delta; 8th major port (1966) |
| **Kolkata-Haldia** (Syama Prasad Mookerjee) | West Bengal | **Oldest major port; only riverine** port (Hooghly) |
- **Natural (sheltered) harbours on the west coast:** **Mumbai, Mormugao, Kochi**; east coast ports mostly **artificial**.
- **Three major ports each:** **Tamil Nadu** (Chennai, Kamarajar, VOC) – the CSD 2018 key; **Maharashtra** gets a third with **Vadhavan**.
- **13th major port: Vadhavan (Palghar, Maharashtra)**, approved 2020; older books call **Port Blair** the 13th (2010).
- **7** major ports on the west coast (incl. Vadhavan) vs **6** on the east coast. **Visakhapatnam = AP's only major port.**
- **West coast, south → north:** Thiruvananthapuram → Kochi → Kozhikode (Calicut) → Mangaluru.
- **Shortest sea route** among exam options: **Chennai – Port Blair** (~1,190 km).
> Match angle (SI 2016): Kandla – tidal, Chennai – artificial, Mumbai – biggest, Kolkata – riverine.

=== Major vs Non-major Ports; East and West Coasts
| Point | Major ports | Non-major ports |
|---|---|---|

| Law / run by | Major Port Authorities Act 2021; Union | **State Maritime Boards** |
- Shipping carries about **95%** of India's trade by **volume** and **68%** by **value** (2026).
- **East coast has fewer ports:** **shallow continental shelf** + **cyclones and storms** (APPSC 2023 key: statements 1 and 2).
- **Deep water close to shore** is found on the **west coast**, not the east; west = sheltered natural harbours, east = mostly artificial.

=== National Waterways and Inland Water Transport
- **Navigable inland waterways: about 14,500 km** (rivers, canals, backwaters, creeks) – about **1%** of transport.
- **IWAI:** IWAI Act **1985**, set up **1986**, HQ **Noida**. **CIWTC** (Central Inland Water Transport Corporation) HQ **Kolkata**.
- **National Waterways Act 2016** declared **111 NWs** (5 existing + 106 new); 25 viable, 13 operational. Before late 2008 only **3 NWs** existed (CDS 2008: statement "three NWs" was then true).
| NW | Route | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **NW-1** (1986) | Ganga-Bhagirathi-Hooghly, **Allahabad (Prayagraj) – Haldia** | **1,620 km** |
| NW-2 | Brahmaputra, **Sadiya-Dhubri** (Assam) | 891 km |
| NW-3 | West Coast Canal, Kollam-Kottapuram (Kerala) | 205 km |
| **NW-4** | Kakinada-Puducherry canals (incl. **Buckingham Canal**) + Godavari, Krishna (AP, TN) | **1,095 km** (old); **2,890 km** after 2016 |
| NW-5 | Talcher-Dhamra, Brahmani-Mahanadi delta + East Coast Canal (Odisha, WB) | 588 km |
- **Second longest NW (old figures): NW-4 / Buckingham Canal** after NW-1; NW-2 Sadiya-Dhubri is **not** the longest.
- NW-1 terminals include **Haldia, Kolkata, Varanasi, Prayagraj**; NW-2 terminals include **Dhubri, Jogighopa, Tezpur, Dibrugarh, Sadiya**.
- **Coastal shipping** grew **118%** in a decade.

| Scheme / law | Year | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Sagarmala | 2015, MoPSW | Port-led development; 800+ projects (~₹5.8 lakh crore) |
| Ministry renamed MoPSW | 2020 | Ports, Shipping and Waterways |
| Maritime India Vision 2030 | 2021 | 150+ initiatives; India among top 10 maritime nations |
| Sagarmala 2.0 | 2025 | Shipbuilding, ship repair, ship breaking and recycling |
| 2025 laws | 2025 | Merchant Shipping Act, Coastal Shipping Act, Indian Ports Act |

# Sagarmala – five pillars
- Port modernisation; port connectivity; port-led industrialisation; coastal community development; coastal shipping + inland water transport.
- **Nationalisation of private shipping companies** is NOT a Sagarmala pillar.
# Bodies and places
- **National Maritime Heritage Complex:** MoPSW, at **Lothal, Gujarat** – **Asia's largest maritime heritage museum**.
- **IMO** HQ: **London**; UN specialised agency; 176 members, 3 associate members.
- **Alang-Sosiya** (Bhavnagar, **Gujarat**, Gulf of Khambhat) = largest ship-breaking yard.
