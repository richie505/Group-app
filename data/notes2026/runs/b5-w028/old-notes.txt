=== NOTES 5:29 | Cyber Security Concerns – Cyber Crime Types, Malware, Ransomware, Phishing, Social Engineering
## SUB 0 | Malware – Virus, Worm, Trojan and Other Types
[29:0:0] # Basics
[29:0:1] - **Malware** (malicious software) = single word for viruses, worms, trojans etc.: software **designed to disrupt, damage or gain unauthorised access** to a computer (not merely buggy software).
[29:0:2] - **VIRUS** = "**Vital Information Resources Under Siege**"; a program that **replicates itself**, **attaches to files** and spreads from computer to computer – enters via **images, audio/video files, downloads, attachments**; infects storage such as a **pen drive**, not a display or keyboard.
[29:0:3] - **Bug** = a fault in a program that stops it working correctly (some GK keys also call a virus a "bug").
[29:0:4] - **Father of computer virology:** **John von Neumann** (1949 theory of self-reproducing automata).
[29:0:5] # Types → behaviour → example

=== NOTES 5:30 | Cyber Security Architecture – It Act 2000, Cert-in, Nciipc, Cyber Swachhta Kendra, I4C
## SUB 0 | IT Act 2000 – Purpose, Sections and Shreya Singhal
[30:0:0] # Purpose
[30:0:1] - **Information Technology Act, 2000** = India's **first cyber law**: **legal recognition** to transactions by **electronic data interchange** ("**electronic commerce**") and electronic records; framework against computer crimes.
[30:0:2] - Concepts (Sec. 2): **electronic record, digital signature, certifying authority** (all three).
[30:0:3] - **Special law**: may override the **IPC** where an offence is fully covered; **Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA)** licenses CAs, which issue Digital Signature Certificates.
[30:0:4] - **2008 amendment** (legal framework for cybercrime today): technology-neutral "**electronic signature**"; added sexually explicit material, **video voyeurism**, data leakage by intermediaries, e-commerce fraud; inserted 70A, 70B.
[30:0:5] # Sections
[30:0:6] TABLE head: Sec. | Subject
  [30:0:6.0] 4 / 5 | Recognition of e-records / e-signatures
  [30:0:6.1] 43 / 43A | Unauthorised access / compensation for failure to protect data
  [30:0:6.2] 66 | Hacking-type offences
  [30:0:6.3] 66A | Offensive messages – struck down (2015)
  [30:0:6.4] 66C / 66D | Identity theft / cheating by personation
  [30:0:6.5] 66E / 66F | Privacy / cyber terrorism (life)
  [30:0:6.6] 67 / 67A | Obscene / sexually explicit
  [30:0:6.7] 69 / 69A | Interception / blocking
  [30:0:6.8] 70 / 70A / 70B | Protected systems / NCIIPC / CERT-In
  [30:0:6.9] 79 / 79A | Safe harbour / Centre notifies Examiners of Electronic Evidence
  [30:0:6.10] 87 | Rule-making (IT Rules 2021)
[30:0:7] # Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (24 March 2015)
[30:0:8] - **66A struck down** as violating **Art. 19(1)(a)** (not saved by 19(2)); **69A upheld**; **79 read down** – "actual knowledge" = court order or government notice.
[30:0:9] - **IT Rules 2021**: no due diligence = loss of **safe harbour**; takedown within **three hours** (27 Mar 2026).

## SUB 1 | Digital Signature and PKI
[30:1:0] - **Digital signature** = an **electronic method of signing** an electronic document that **ensures the original content is unchanged**; gives **authentication, integrity, non-repudiation** – **not secrecy**; asymmetric crypto + hash; **private key signs**, public key verifies.
[30:1:1] - A record that **identifies the issuing Certifying Authority** = **digital certificate**, not the signature; proof of identity to access a server = certificate/login, not the signature.
[30:1:2] - **Public Key Infrastructure (PKI)** = **digital security infrastructure**: creates, manages and revokes certificates.
[30:1:3] > UPSC 2019 key: **only statement 3**; UPSC 2020: PKI = digital security infrastructure.

## SUB 0 | Cyber Security Basics, C-DAC Tools and Threats in News
[31:0:0] # Cyber security
[31:0:1] - **Cyber security:** protecting computer-based information from unauthorised access, transfer, modification or destruction; threats = hacking, malware, cybercrime, cyber terrorism, data theft.
[31:0:2] - **Features:** **compliance**, **defence against internal threats**, **threat prevention** – so "all of the above".
[31:0:3] - Legal base for cybercrime: **IT Act 2000**, amended **2008** (sexually explicit material, video voyeurism, data leakage by intermediaries, e-commerce fraud).
[31:0:4] # C-DAC security software
[31:0:5] - **NAYAN:** **C-DAC** software against **cyber attacks and data theft** (UPPCS key; AVARODH and TRIKAL are distractors).
[31:0:6] - **USB Pratirodh:** C-DAC tool that controls unauthorised **USB storage devices**; with **AppSamvid** (application whitelisting) released free via **Cyber Swachhta Kendra** (Feb 2017).
[31:0:7] - Exam key: **NAYAN, USB Pratirodh and Malware Resist** are all C-DAC tools (IAS key: I, II and III).
[31:0:8] # Threats in news
[31:0:9] - **StrandHogg** (Dec 2019): **Android** vulnerability flagged by MHA's cyber wing (I4C Threat Analytical Unit) – lets malicious apps pose as genuine apps and steal data; found by Promon (Norway). Trojan Horse, Morris Worm (1988), Timofonica (first phone virus, 2000) are older malware.
[31:0:10] # Forensic sciences
[31:0:11] - **Gujarat Forensic Sciences University (GFSU), Gandhinagar:** first university dedicated to **forensic and allied sciences**; set up by the **Gujarat Government** under a State Act (**Act 17 of 2008, published 30 Sep 2008**), **not PPP**; Director General **Dr J.M. Vyas**; working from **February 2009** (not July 2010). Upgraded to **National Forensic Sciences University** (INI, Oct 2020).

## SUB 1 | CERT-In, NCSP 2013 and the Nodal Agencies
[31:1:0] # CERT-In (Indian Computer Emergency Response Team)
[31:1:1] - **Status:** national nodal agency for **cyber security** and cyber incident response; operational since **January 2004**, under **MeitY**; statutory under **Sec. 70B, IT Act** (2008 amendment).
[31:1:2] - **Functions:** identify cyber incidents; **issue alerts and advisories on the latest cyber threats and countermeasures** (daily), vulnerability notes, guidelines and white papers; emergency measures; coordinate incident response.
[31:1:3] - **Not its job:** cyber treaties (MEA), private companies' security budgets, legal support in cybercrime cases (police / I4C). RAW, ISRO and NITI Aayog are not cyber-security agencies.
[31:1:4] - **CERT-In Directions 2022:** report specified incidents within **6 hours**; logs kept in India.
[31:1:5] # National Cyber Security Policy (NCSP) 2013
[31:1:6] - Released **2013** by DeitY, then under the Ministry of Communications and IT (now **MeitY**); India's first comprehensive cyber-security policy.
[31:1:7] - **Vision:** a **secure and resilient cyberspace** for citizens, businesses and government.
[31:1:8] - **Target:** **5 lakh** cyber security professionals in **5 years**.
[31:1:9] - **Aims:** create an **Information Security Assurance Framework**; protect critical information infrastructure; indigenous security technology, R&D and PPP; trained workforce.
[31:1:10] - Every ministry, department and organisation names a **Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)**.

=== NOTES 5:32 | Data Protection and Privacy - Puttaswamy, Dpdp Act, Data Localisation
## SUB 0 | Right to Privacy – Article 21 and Puttaswamy
[32:0:0] # Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd) v Union of India
[32:0:1] - **Decided 24 Aug 2017** by a **9-judge** bench, **unanimous**: right to privacy is **intrinsic to life and personal liberty under Article 21** and part of the freedoms of **Part III** – a **Fundamental Right**.
[32:0:2] - So **Article 21** secures **life, personal liberty and (read in) privacy**; not Art. 14, 15, 17, 19, 20, 22, 23, 24, 29, and not the DPSPs or any amendment.
[32:0:3] - Petition filed by **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd)**, a former Karnataka High Court judge, against Aadhaar.
[32:0:4] - Overruled **M.P. Sharma (1954)** and **Kharak Singh (1962)** insofar as they denied privacy.
[32:0:5] - **Three-fold test:** **legality** (a law is needed; a public aim alone is not enough), **legitimate aim**, **proportionality**.
[32:0:6] TABLE head: Case | Point
  [32:0:6.0] Gobind v State of MP (1975) | Privacy part of personal liberty
  [32:0:6.1] PUCL v UoI (1997) | Safeguards on telephone interception
  [32:0:6.2] Puttaswamy (2017) | Privacy = Fundamental Right (Art. 21 + Part III)
[32:0:7] > Asked 6 times (APPSC AEE 2023, Welfare Officers, Group-2; UPSC 2018 ×2, 2021): the answer is always **Article 21 / Part III**.
[32:0:8] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. Vol. 3 (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-03.txt) p.42; PMF Polity 01 p.64, p.74; app notes 5:32 sub 0
## SUB 1 | GDPR and the Right to Be Forgotten
[32:1:0] - **GDPR** (General Data Protection Regulation) – **European Union**: adopted **April 2016**, applied from **25 May 2018**; replaced the **1995** Data Protection Directive; explicit consent before processing.
[32:1:1] - GDPR: administrative fines, member states may add criminal penalties; DPDP Act: **financial penalties only**.
[32:1:2] - **Right to Be Forgotten:** began in **Google Spain v AEPD (2014)**; = **Right to Erasure, GDPR Art. 17** (DPDP: erasure Sec. 12). In India it limits **name-based searches** and **does not erase judicial records**.
[32:1:3] - **Data portability:** explicit GDPR right (Art. 20); no statutory right under the DPDP Act.
[32:1:4] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 6 p.46; app notes 5:32 sub 8
## SUB 2 | DPDP Rules 2025, Localisation and Surveillance
[32:2:0] - **DPDP Rules:** draft **3 Jan 2025**; final notified **14 Nov 2025** – Board provisions at once; **Consent Manager Nov 2026**; full compliance **May 2027** (18 months).
[32:2:1] - Rules require clear, specific, informed consent; plain-language notices; purpose limitation; data minimisation; retention controls.
[32:2:2] - **Localisation:** **RBI (April 2018)** – payment-system data stored **only in India**. DPDP cross-border transfer = **blacklist** (allowed unless the Centre restricts); GDPR = **whitelist / adequacy**.
[32:2:3] - First national standards for **cloud, data centres, ethical AI** focus on bias, data sovereignty, privacy.
[32:2:4] - **Pegasus:** SC (27 Oct 2021, CJI N.V. Ramana) set up a technical committee overseen by **Justice R.V. Raveendran** (retd SC judge); malware on **5 of 29** phones, not clearly Pegasus.
[32:2:5] - **AP:** AP Police **AI4AP** pilot (Chittoor, Guntur, Annamayya) raises DPDP issues.
[32:2:6] ~~ Sources: web: livelaw.in Pegasus judgment 27 Oct 2021; app notes 5:32 sub 7

## SUB 6 | Rights, Duties and Children
[32:6:0] - **Rights:** information/access, correction, **erasure (Sec. 12)**, grievance redress, withdrawal of consent, **nomination** (no right to impose criminal penalties).
[32:6:1] - **Duties:** no false information, impersonation or **frivolous complaints**.
[32:6:2] - **Children:** **verifiable parental consent below 18**; **no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted ads**.
