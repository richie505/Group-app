## SUB 0 | Cripps Mission, 1942 – Context and Proposals
[0:0] - **Arrived March 1942** (Delhi, 22-23 March); Cripps returned **mid-April 1942** after talks broke down.
[0:1] - Sent by British PM **Winston Churchill** (Conservative; PM 1940-45) – not MacDonald, Baldwin or Chamberlain.
[0:2] - **Sir Stafford Cripps** = left-wing **Labour** leader, Leader of the House of Commons, member of Churchill's **War Cabinet**; later also in the **Cabinet Mission (1946)**.
[0:3] - **Why sent:** Japanese advance (Pearl Harbor Dec 1941; **Rangoon fell March 1942**); pressure from the Allies (**Roosevelt, Chiang Kai-shek**); Congress offer to help if power was transferred.
[0:4] TABLE head: Proposal (Draft Declaration) | Detail
  [0:4.0] Status | Indian Union with Dominion status soon after the war, free to leave the Commonwealth – not complete independence, not a republic, not immediate partition
  [0:4.1] Constitution | Constitution-making body after the war, elected by provincial lower houses (proportional representation, electoral college 1/10th of their size) + nominees of the princes – the first concrete plan for an elected constituent assembly
  [0:4.2] Provincial option | Any province (or state) unwilling could stay outside and form a separate Union – "fatal flaw", Pakistan conceded in principle
  [0:4.3] Minorities | Treaty between the constitution-making body and Britain to protect minorities
  [0:4.4] Interim | Defence stays with Britain; Governor-General's powers intact; no equal-representation executive council (that was the Wavell Plan)
[0:5] - **Constitution-making body: yes; full independence: no** (UPSC 2009). All provinces and states were NOT bound to join.
[0:6] - Cripps on the urgency: **"Here is a revolution taking place and we should move quickly."**
[0:7] > Cripps = March 1942, Churchill, Dominion status after the war, elected constituent body, provincial opt-out, defence with Britain.

## SUB 1 | Rejection of Cripps – Negotiators, Remarks and the Road to Quit India
[1:0] - **Official Congress negotiators: Jawaharlal Nehru and Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**; Azad = **Congress President 1940-46**, negotiated with both **Cripps** and **Wavell**.
[1:1] - **Congress objected to:** Dominion status instead of independence; princely nominees; **provincial secession** (Balkanisation); **no immediate transfer of effective power**, no real share in defence – the main reason.
[1:2] - **Muslim League** rejected it: opposed a **single Indian Union**; Pakistan not explicitly conceded.
[1:3] - Cripps' **"take it or leave it"** attitude and Churchill/Linlithgow's obstruction added to the failure; the mission (Cripps and British staff) had **no Indian member**, but that was **not** why Congress rejected it.
[1:4] - **Gandhi** called the offer **"a post-dated cheque"**; "**on a crashing / failing / tottering bank**" was added in popular quotation (often credited to Nehru).
[1:5] - **Failure of Cripps → Quit India Movement (August 1942)**; Gandhi used "Quit India" in **Harijan** (April 1942). AP angle: rumours of Japanese bombing of **Vizag and Kakinada** during the mission.
[1:6] > Negotiators = Nehru + Azad; post-dated cheque = Gandhi; failure → Quit India.
[1:7] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.475-476 (book p.443-444); PMF Modern History Part 5 p.53; NextGen AP History Part 19 p.6; Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.102; old app notes row 240

## SUB 3 | Second World War and India – Background Facts
[3:0] - **WWII = 1939-1945** (Germany invaded Poland 1 Sept 1939; Britain declared war 3 Sept).
[3:1] - **Congress ministries resigned (Oct-Nov 1939)** because Viceroy **Linlithgow made India a party to the war without consulting Indians**.
[3:2] - **Muslim League** observed **22 December 1939** as the **"Day of Deliverance"** (Ambedkar joined in Bombay).
[3:3] - **Congress policy:** **cooperation if complete independence / a national government was assured** – not unconditional help, not neutrality. **Ramgarh session** (**53rd**, **March 1940**, Bihar – now Jharkhand; President **Azad**) – came **before** the August Offer, which answered the Congress offer of war help for a **provisional national government** at the Centre.
[3:4] - **Pakistan (Lahore) Resolution** of the Muslim League: **23 March 1940**.
[3:5] TABLE head: World-war fact | Answer
  [3:5.0] Humiliating treaty behind WWII | Treaty of Versailles (1919)
  [3:5.1] Axis powers | Germany, Italy, Japan – China fought with the Allies
  [3:5.2] Fascism / Nazism | Mussolini (Italy) / Hitler (Germany); socialism – Lenin; democracy – Woodrow Wilson
  [3:5.3] War-crimes trials | Nuremberg (1945-46)
  [3:5.4] First atom bomb | Hiroshima, 6 August 1945 (Nagasaki 9 August)
[3:6] - **British Prime Ministers** of the period:

  [3:7.1] Neville Chamberlain | Sept 1939 – May 1940
  [3:7.2] Winston Churchill | May 1940 – 1945: WWII PM, sent Cripps, PM during Quit India
  [3:7.3] Clement Attlee | 1945-51: Cabinet Mission, independence
  [3:7.4] Spencer Perceval | Only British PM assassinated – shot in the lobby of the House of Commons, 1812

## SUB 4 | Individual Satyagraha, 1940-41
[4:0] - Launched by **Gandhi on 17 October 1940** after the **August Offer was rejected**; started by **Vinoba Bhave** at **Paunar (Pavnar), Wardha**.
[4:1] - **Aim:** assert **freedom of speech against the war** (anti-war declaration; "wrong to help the British war effort with **men or money**"), show nationalist patience was not weakness – **not mass mobilisation**, so as not to embarrass Britain.
[4:2] - **First satyagrahi: Vinoba Bhave**; **second: Jawaharlal Nehru**; third: **Brahma Dutt** – not Rajaji, Patel, Sarojini Naidu or Bose. Also called **Individual Civil Disobedience**; "Delhi Chalo" march planned if not arrested.
[4:3] - **25,000** convicted by May 1941; suspended by the CWC in December 1941.
[4:4] - Government arrested **Vinoba, Nehru and Patel**; **Ongole Municipality** condemned the arrests (AP angle). Bihar: **Shyam Narayan Singh**.
[4:5] - APPSC AEE key: **only statement "Vinoba was the first" is correct** – the satyagraha's declared aim was free anti-war speech, not opposing the August Declaration.
[4:6] - **Mangal Bharat** – credited to **Vinoba Bhave** in the APPSC SI 2019 key (not verified).
[4:7] > 17 Oct 1940; Vinoba → Nehru → Brahma Dutt; free speech against the war.
[4:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.49-50; Spectrum Modern India p.472-473 (book p.440-441); NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.75; old app notes row 240
## SUB 5 | August Offer, 1940
[5:0] - Made by Viceroy **Lord Linlithgow** on **8 August 1940** (at Simla), in reply to Ramgarh / the Congress offer, when Britain was losing in Europe.
[5:1] TABLE head: Proposal | Detail
  [5:1.0] Goal | Dominion status
  [5:1.1] Constitution | Representative constitution-making body after the war – "mainly" Indians to frame it
  [5:1.2] Executive | Expanded Viceroy's Executive Council with more Indians; War Advisory Council
  [5:1.3] Minorities | No constitution without minorities' consent (League's veto)
  [5:1.4] Not included | Immediate transfer of defence / a national government
[5:2] - **Significance:** **first time** the British conceded that **framing the constitution was primarily the Indians' responsibility** (NDA 2020) – the Cripps plan (1942) made it "solely" Indian and gave the concrete elected body.
[5:3] - **Responses:** Congress **rejected** it – **Nehru: Dominion status "dead as a doornail"**; the League welcomed the veto but wanted partition.
[5:4] - Constituent Assembly demand first raised by **M.N. Roy (1934)**; Congress adopted it in 1935.
[5:5] > August Offer = Linlithgow, 8 Aug 1940, first acceptance of an Indian-framed constitution.
