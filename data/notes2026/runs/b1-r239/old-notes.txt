=== NOTES 1:239 | Gandhi-irwin Pact, Round Table Conferences, Communal Award, Poona Pact; Resumption and Withdrawal
## SUB 0 | Background – from Dandi to the First Rtc
[239:0:0] - **CDM** began with the **Dandi March (12 March – 6 April 1930)**; Viceroy **Lord Irwin**.
[239:0:1] - The **Simon Commission** (arrived 3 Feb 1928) and the Lahore **Purna Swaraj** (Dec 1929) set the stage; the British offered **Round Table Conferences** in **London** to frame a new constitution.
[239:0:2] - Lahore (1929) had already said there was **no benefit from the RTC**, so Congress stayed away from the first one.
[239:0:3] Viceroy sequence: Irwin (1926-31) – Pact and first phase; Willingdon (1931-36) – repression of the second phase.

## SUB 2 | Gandhi-irwin Pact (delhi Pact), 1931
[239:2:0] # Date and Mediators
[239:2:1] - Signed **5 March 1931** at Delhi (not March 1930); the CWC authorised Gandhi.
[239:2:2] - **Mediators: Tej Bahadur Sapru and M.R. Jayakar** (also V.S. Srinivasa Sastri).
[239:2:3] - Sarojini Naidu called Gandhi and Irwin the **"Two Mahatmas"**.
[239:2:4] # Terms
[239:2:5] TABLE head: Irwin agreed | Irwin refused | Gandhi / Congress agreed
  [239:2:5.0] Release of non-violent political prisoners | Public inquiry into police excesses | Suspend the CDM
  [239:2:5.1] Coastal villagers may make salt for own use | Commuting death sentences of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru | Attend the 2nd RTC
  [239:2:5.2] Withdraw ordinances; remit fines; return unsold confiscated lands | – | –
[239:2:6] # Criticism
[239:2:7] - **No commutation** for Bhagat Singh and comrades (executed **23 March 1931**).
[239:2:8] - **No police inquiry**; youth disillusioned.
[239:2:9] - Irwin's biographer **Alan Campbell Johnson** called Gandhi's gains **"Consolation Prizes"**.
[239:2:10] - Yet the Pact was **ratified at Karachi** and Congress was treated as an equal party for the first time.
[239:2:11] Gandhi-Irwin Pact = 1931. Dating it to March 1930 is WRONG.
## SUB 3 | Karachi Session, March 1931
[239:3:0] # Session
[239:3:1] - Dates: **29-31 March 1931**; **President: Vallabhbhai Patel**.
[239:3:2] - Held **days after the execution of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru** – Congress **admired their bravery but rejected political violence**.
[239:3:3] - **Endorsed** (did not oppose) the **Gandhi-Irwin (Delhi) Pact**; **reiterated Purna Swaraj** (= Complete Independence); chose Gandhi for the 2nd RTC.
[239:3:4] # Resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme
[239:3:5] - **Drafted by Jawaharlal Nehru** (with M.N. Roy's help); passed at the session Patel presided.
[239:3:6] TABLE head: Fundamental Rights (political) | Economic Programme
  [239:3:6.0] Civil liberties – free speech, press, assembly | Cuts in rent and revenue
  [239:3:6.1] Equality before law | Debt relief for peasants
  [239:3:6.2] Universal adult franchise | State control of key industries
  [239:3:6.3] Religious neutrality of the State | Labour welfare, worker protection
  [239:3:6.4] Free primary education | Economic justice as a duty of the State
[239:3:7] - It **foreshadowed Part III (Fundamental Rights) and Part IV (DPSP)** of the Constitution.
[239:3:8] Karachi 1931 | President | Vallabhbhai Patel
Karachi 1931 | Drafter | Jawaharlal Nehru

## SUB 6 | Communal Award and Poona Pact, 1932
[239:6:0] # Communal Award
[239:6:1] - Announced **16 August 1932** by British PM **Ramsay MacDonald**.
[239:6:2] - Gave **separate electorates** to Muslims, Sikhs, Christians and others, and also to the **depressed classes**.
[239:6:3] # Gandhi's Yerwada Fast
[239:6:4] - Gandhi, in **Yerwada Jail, Poona**, opposed separate electorates for depressed classes (it would split Hindu society).
[239:6:5] - **Fast unto death began 20 September 1932**; broken after the Pact (26 Sept 1932).
[239:6:6] # Poona Pact
[239:6:7] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [239:6:7.0] Date | 24 September 1932
  [239:6:7.1] Place | Yerwada Jail, Poona
  [239:6:7.2] Signed by | Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (depressed classes) and Madan Mohan Malaviya (caste Hindus); Gandhi himself did not sign
  [239:6:7.3] Main change | Separate electorates dropped → reserved seats in joint electorates
  [239:6:7.4] Seats for depressed classes | 148 in provincial legislatures (vs 71 under the Award)
  [239:6:7.5] Legacy | Root of reserved seats – Articles 330 and 332
[239:6:8] - **Harijan Sevak Sangh** (first named Anti-Untouchability League) founded **30 September 1932**; first president **G.D. Birla**.
[239:6:9] 71 seats under the Communal Award → 148 seats under the Poona Pact
[239:6:10] Poona Pact signatories = Ambedkar and Malaviya, not Gandhi and Ambedkar.

## SUB 8 | Withdrawal of Cdm and Council-entry Revival
[239:8:0] # Why the CDM Ended
[239:8:1] - 1. **RTC-2 deadlock**.
[239:8:2] - 2. Gandhi arrested; **ordinances and harsh repression** under Willingdon.
[239:8:3] - 3. **Mass movement suspended (14 July 1933)** – individual satyagraha.
[239:8:4] - 4. **Formally withdrawn (April 1934)** → the **Swarajist line revived**.
[239:8:5] # Council Entry Revived
[239:8:6] - In **1934** Congress returned to council entry and **contested the Central Assembly elections**.
[239:8:7] - Gandhi turned to constructive work, especially **"Service to Harijans"**, his top priority.
[239:8:8] CDM: suspended 14 July 1933 → withdrawn April 1934 → 1934 Central Assembly elections.
[239:8:9] # Two Phases of the CDM Compared
[239:8:10] TABLE head: Point | First phase (1930-31) | Second phase (1932-34)
  [239:8:10.0] Start | Dandi March, 12 March 1930 | January 1932, after RTC-2
  [239:8:10.1] Viceroy | Lord Irwin | Lord Willingdon
  [239:8:10.2] Government response | Arrests, but talks followed | Struck first: Congress banned, ordinances
  [239:8:10.3] End | Gandhi-Irwin Pact, 5 March 1931 | Suspended July 1933; withdrawn April 1934
  [239:8:10.4] Result | Congress invited to the 2nd RTC | Movement crushed; council entry revived
[239:8:11] # Significance
[239:8:12] - The Pact and the 2nd RTC showed the British dealing with Congress **as an equal party**.
[239:8:13] - The Karachi resolution gave the movement a **social and economic programme** for the first time.
[239:8:14] - The Poona Pact settled the depressed classes' representation through **joint electorates with reserved seats**, a system later carried into the Constitution.
[239:8:15] - The failure of the second phase pushed Congress back to **legislatures (1934)**.

=== NOTES 1:240 | Individual Satyagraha, August Offer, Cripps Mission
## SUB 0 | World War II and the Congress Ministries
[240:0:0] - **Trigger:** Viceroy **Lord Linlithgow** declared India a party to **World War II** (September 1939) **without consulting** Indians.
[240:0:1] - **Congress ministries resigned** in **October-November 1939** in protest.
[240:0:2] - **22 December 1939:** Muslim League observed the **"Day of Deliverance"**; **B.R. Ambedkar** joined it in **Bombay**.
[240:0:3] - **March 1940:** **Ramgarh session** (Bihar), the **53rd** Congress session - offered war help if a **provisional national government** was formed at the Centre.
[240:0:4] Ramgarh (March 1940) came BEFORE the August Offer (August 1940); the Offer was the reply to Ramgarh.
## SUB 1 | August Offer, 1940
[240:1:0] # Proposals
[240:1:1] - Made by Viceroy **Lord Linlithgow** on **8 August 1940**, when Britain was losing in Europe.
[240:1:2] TABLE head: Proposal | Detail
  [240:1:2.0] Constitution | A representative constitution-making body after the war
  [240:1:2.1] Executive Council | More Indians in the Viceroy's Executive Council
  [240:1:2.2] Advisory body | A War Advisory Council
  [240:1:2.3] Goal | Dominion Status
  [240:1:2.4] Minorities | Minority veto on any future constitution
[240:1:3] - **Not included:** immediate transfer of **defence**.
[240:1:4] August Offer = FIRST official acceptance that **Indians would frame their own constitution**.
[240:1:5] - The Constituent Assembly demand was first raised by **M.N. Roy (1934)**; adopted by Congress in **1935**.
[240:1:6] # Responses
[240:1:7] - **Congress rejected** it; **Nehru**: Dominion Status is **"dead as a doornail"**.
[240:1:8] - **Muslim League welcomed the minority veto** but said only **partition** would satisfy it.
## SUB 2 | Individual Satyagraha, 1940-41
[240:2:0] # Launch and Aim
[240:2:1] - Launched by **Gandhi** on **17 October 1940**, after the August Offer was rejected.
[240:2:2] - Started from **Paunar (Pavnar), Wardha, Maharashtra** (also spelt "Palner").
[240:2:3] - **Aim:** assert **freedom of speech** against the war; oppose being dragged into WWII - **not mass mobilisation** (hence "limited" / "individual").
[240:2:4] - Gandhi's line: "It is wrong to help the British war effort with **men or money**."
[240:2:5] - Chosen individuals made **anti-war speeches** and courted arrest one after another; also called **Individual Civil Disobedience**.
[240:2:6] - More than **25,000** satyagrahis were convicted by 1941.
[240:2:7] # First Three Satyagrahis
[240:2:8] TABLE head: Order | Satyagrahi
  [240:2:8.0] First | Acharya Vinoba Bhave
  [240:2:8.1] Second | Jawaharlal Nehru
  [240:2:8.2] Third | Brahma Dutt (chosen for moral conviction, not political prominence)
[240:2:9] - Government arrested **Vinoba, Nehru and Patel**.
[240:2:10] - **Bihar:** led by **Shyam Narayan Singh** (BPSC 2025).
[240:2:11] # Andhra Angle
[240:2:12] - **Ongole Municipality** passed a resolution **condemning the arrests** of Vinoba, Nehru and Patel.
## SUB 3 | Cripps Mission, 1942 - Context
[240:3:0] - Arrived **March 1942** (Delhi, **22-23 March**); Cripps returned in **mid-April** after talks broke down.
[240:3:1] - Headed by **Sir Stafford Cripps**, a **Labour** member of Churchill's **War Cabinet**.
[240:3:2] - Sent by British PM **Winston Churchill** (PM 1940-45, 1951-55).
[240:3:3] - Pressure from **USA (Franklin Roosevelt)**, **China (Chiang Kai-shek)** and **Australia** over Japan's rise.
[240:3:4] - **Japanese advance:** Pearl Harbor (1941); Malaya, Singapore overrun; **Rangoon fell March 1942**.
[240:3:5] - **Objective:** secure Indian cooperation in the war.
[240:3:6] - Cripps: "Here is a revolution taking place and we should move quickly"; he was also a member of the **Cabinet Mission (1946)**.
## SUB 4 | Cripps Proposals (draft Declaration)
[240:4:0] TABLE head: Proposal | Detail
  [240:4:0.0] Status | Indian Union with Dominion Status after the war, with the right to secede from the Commonwealth
  [240:4:0.1] Constitution | Indians to frame their own constitution; body elected by provincial lower houses as electoral college (1/10th of their size, proportional representation) + nominees of princely states
  [240:4:0.2] Provincial option | Any province/state could stay outside the Union and form separate unions - "the fatal flaw", Pakistan conceded in principle
  [240:4:0.3] Interim | Defence and army stay with the British; Governor-General's powers kept
[240:4:1] - Constitution-making body: **yes**; full independence: **no**.
[240:4:2] Cripps = FIRST to allow provincial opt-out - seed of Partition.
## SUB 5 | Rejection and Remarks
[240:5:0] # Congress
[240:5:1] - Negotiators: **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** (Azad was Congress President).
[240:5:2] - Objected to: Dominion Status only, **right to secede / provincial opt-out**, princely-state nominees, **no immediate transfer of power**, risk of **Balkanisation**.
[240:5:3] # Muslim League
[240:5:4] - **Rejected** it: opposed a **single Indian Union**; Pakistan was **not explicitly** granted.
[240:5:5] Congress | No immediate power | Feared Balkanisation
Muslim League | No explicit Pakistan | Opposed single Union
[240:5:6] # Why It Failed
[240:5:7] TABLE head: Cause | Effect
  [240:5:7.0] Cripps's "take it or leave it" attitude; Churchill's sabotage | Talks broke down
  [240:5:7.1] Too radical for Churchill, too conservative for Indians | Rejected by all sides
[240:5:8] - "Mission solely of whites" - true, but **not** the reason for rejection.
[240:5:9] # Gandhi's Remark
[240:5:10] - Gandhi called the offer a **"post-dated cheque"**; it is popularly quoted as "a post-dated cheque **on a crashing bank**".
