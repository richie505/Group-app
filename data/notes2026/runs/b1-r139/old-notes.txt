  [139:0:0.1] Jahandar Shah | 1712-1713 | Wazir Zulfiqar Khan; under mistress Lal Kunwar; called "Lampat" (the profligate) | Defeated and killed by nephew Farrukhsiyar with the Sayyids
  [139:0:0.2] Farrukhsiyar | 1713-1719 | Puppet of the Sayyid brothers; executed Banda Singh Bahadur (1716); 1717 farman – duty-free trade for the English in Bengal | Blinded, deposed and killed by the Sayyids, 1719
  [139:0:0.3] Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela' | 1719-1748 | Removed the Sayyids with Nizam-ul-Mulk's help; Nadir Shah 1739; Abdali's first raid 1748 | –
  [139:0:0.4] Ahmad Shah | 1748-1754 | Control to Udham Bai; Marathas took Malwa, Bundelkhand | Blinded and deposed by Imad-ul-Mulk (1754)
  [139:0:0.5] Alamgir II | 1754-1759 | Plassey 1757; repeated Abdali invasions; Punjab and Multan ceded to Abdali | Murdered by Imad-ul-Mulk
  [139:0:0.6] Shah Alam II (Ali Gauhar) | 1759-1806 | Third Panipat 1761, Buxar 1764; first Mughal pensioner of the Company; English captured Delhi in 1803 | Blinded by Ghulam Qadir Rohilla, 1788
  [139:0:0.7] Akbar II | 1806-1837 | Gave Ram Mohan Roy the title "Raja" | British pensioner
  [139:0:0.8] Bahadur Shah Zafar (II) | 1837-1857 | Last Mughal emperor; told his successors would lose the royal title and the Red Fort | Exiled to Rangoon after 1857; died 1862
[139:0:1] - ★ Order (APPSC G1 2024): **Bahadur Shah I → Jahandar Shah → Farrukhsiyar → Muhammad Shah → Shah Alam II**.
[139:0:2] - Order: Jahandar Shah → Muhammad Shah → Ahmad Shah → Alamgir II.
[139:0:3] # Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela'
[139:0:4] - Called "Rangeela" for wine, beauty and apathy to administration; watched animal fights; **eunuchs and ladies** controlled the government.
[139:0:5] - **Last Mughal emperor to sit on the Peacock Throne (Mayur Singhasan / Takht-i-Taus)**; emperor at the time of Nadir Shah's attack.
[139:0:6] # Shah Alam II's Exile
[139:0:7] - Not allowed to enter Delhi by Wazir **Ghaziuddin (Imad-ul-Mulk)** – he spent his first years away from the capital out of fear of his strong wazir and unstable politics, **not** fear of invasion from the north-west.
[139:0:8] - After Buxar he lived at **Allahabad** under Company protection; returned to Delhi in **1771-72** with Maratha help (**Mahadji Scindia**, in Madhav Rao I's time).
[139:0:9] Ahmad Shah ruled 1748-**1754**, not 1748-1757 (that overlaps Alamgir II). The Sayyids dominated **Farrukhsiyar**, not Jahandar Shah (whose wazir was Zulfiqar Khan).
[139:0:10] Exiled to Rangoon after 1857 = **Bahadur Shah II**, not Muhammad Shah.

## SUB 2 | Sayyid Brothers – King-makers
[139:2:0] - **Abdullah Khan** = **Wazir**; **Husain Ali Khan** = **Mir Bakhshi**.
[139:2:1] - King-makers **1713-1720**: made Farrukhsiyar emperor and later removed him; then puppet successors.
[139:2:2] - Eliminated under **Muhammad Shah** with **Nizam-ul-Mulk's** help; Husain Ali killed in 1720.
[139:2:3] - **Saadat Khan** joined the plot against the Sayyids, siding with Muhammad Shah.
[139:2:4] - The Sayyids' control bred corruption and mismanagement.
## SUB 3 | Invasions – Invader, Year and Consequence
[139:3:0] TABLE head: Invader | Year | Consequence
  [139:3:0.0] Nadir Shah (Persia) | 1739 | Defeated Muhammad Shah at Karnal; sacked Delhi; took the Koh-i-Noor and Shah Jahan's Peacock Throne
  [139:3:0.1] Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani) (Afghan) | Raids 1748-1767 | Punjab and Multan ceded; entered Delhi January 1757, looted Delhi, Mathura, Agra; Third Panipat, 14 January 1761
[139:3:1] - Nadir Shah's contemporary historian: **Anand Ram Mukhlis**. Plunderer who took the Koh-i-Noor = **Nadir Shah** (APPSC 2018).
[139:3:2] - **Abdali** had been **Nadir Shah's general**. In 1757 he made **Alamgir II** emperor, **Imad-ul-Mulk** Prime Minister, and **Najib Khan (Najib-ud-Daulah)**, the Rohilla chief he trusted, chief agent and Mir Bakhshi.
[139:3:3] # Third Battle of Panipat (1761)
[139:3:4] - Abdali vs the **Maratha Confederacy**.
[139:3:5] - **Immediate cause**: Marathas expelled Abdali's son/viceroy **Timur Shah** from **Lahore**.
[139:3:6] - **Result**: Maratha growth in the north stopped; the gap later helped British expansion.
[139:3:7] - Marathas = strongest native power after Mughal decline.
[139:3:8] - Impact of invasions: exposed Mughal military weakness; drained the treasury.

## SUB 6 | Asaf Jahi Hyderabad
[139:6:0] # Founding
[139:6:1] - Founder: **Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan Siddiqi**, the **first Nizam** (APPSC 2018, 2022).
[139:6:2] - Background: Hyderabad first envisaged by Deccan viceroy **Zulfiqar Khan**, governed through **Daud Khan** from 1708.
[139:6:3] - Farrukhsiyar made him Governor of the Deccan; he helped overthrow the Sayyids.
[139:6:4] - Defeated **Mubariz Khan** at **Shakar Kheda (1724)** → virtually independent; **1724 = founding year**.
[139:6:5] - Tolerant: **Puran Chand** as Diwan; Mughal-type jagirdari administration.
[139:6:6] - Kept the Marathas out in his lifetime; died **1748**, after which Hyderabad became vulnerable.

[139:6:9] Kilich Khan was not Mubariz Khan; the title Asaf Jah was not given by Zulfiqar Khan.

[139:6:13] - **Operation Polo (September 1948)** joined Hyderabad to the Indian Union; the Asaf Jahi dynasty was ruling then.
## SUB 7 | Causes of Decline
[139:7:0] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [139:7:0.0] Aurangzeb's policies | Empire at greatest extent: Kashmir to Jinji, Hindukush to Chittagong; intolerant policies + long Deccan wars emptied the treasury
  [139:7:0.1] Weak successors, noble factions | Nobles acted independently; rivalries broke central authority
  [139:7:0.2] Jagirdari and agrarian crises | Shrinking fiscal base → peasant and zamindar revolts
  [139:7:0.3] Others | Maratha rise, invasions of Nadir Shah and Abdali, European companies
[139:7:1] - Power gap filled by **regional states → Marathas → East India Company**.
## SUB 8 | Historiography of Decline
[139:8:0] TABLE head: Historian | Work | View
  [139:8:0.0] Jadunath Sarkar | History of Aurangzeb; Fall of the Mughal Empire | Aurangzeb's religious policy; weak successors
  [139:8:0.1] Irfan Habib | The Agrarian System of Mughal India (1963) | Agrarian crisis – over-exploitation of peasants → revolts
  [139:8:0.2] Satish Chandra | Parties and Politics at the Mughal Court, 1707-1740 | Jagirdari crisis – too few jagirs for too many nobles
  [139:8:0.3] M. Athar Ali | The Mughal Nobility under Aurangzeb | Enlarged nobility (Deccanis, Marathas) worsened jagir shortage
  [139:8:0.4] W.H. Moreland | India at the Death of Akbar; From Akbar to Aurangzeb | Economic exploitation and stagnation
  [139:8:0.5] François Bernier | Travels in the Mogul Empire | No private property in land → no incentive; predicted ruin
[139:8:1] - **Revisionist view**: the 18th century = **regional growth, not collapse**; successor states ran their own economy and law-and-order and controlled the peasant surplus.
[139:8:2] **Irfan Habib = agrarian crisis; Satish Chandra = jagirdari crisis.**

=== NOTES 1:141 | Peshwas and the Maratha Confederacy; Third Battle of Panipat (1761); Three Anglo-maratha Wars
## SUB 0 | Rise of the Peshwa As Virtual Ruler
[141:0:0] - **Balaji Vishwanath** made Peshwa by **Shahu, November 1713** – power began moving from the **Chhatrapati at Satara** to the **Peshwa at Poona** (Peshwas' seat, APPSC 2018).
[141:0:1] - Shahu owed his throne to Balaji Vishwanath; the office became **hereditary** (Balaji Baji Rao made Peshwa after Baji Rao I's death, 1740); all expansion was led by the Peshwas.
[141:0:2] - **Sangola agreement (1750)**, after Shahu's death (1749): Chhatrapati only nominal; Peshwa the real head. From **Balaji Baji Rao's** time the king became a nobody; by 1750 the kingdom was a **confederacy**.
[141:0:3] - **Raja Shahu**, grandson of Shivaji – first Maratha chief to offer **nazr** at the dargah of Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti, Ajmer.
## SUB 1 | Peshwas in Order

  [141:1:0.0] Balaji Vishwanath | 1713-20 | Restored order after Sambhaji; 1719 Mughal grant (swarajya + chauth, sardeshmukhi of the Deccan) via the Sayyids
  [141:1:0.1] Baji Rao I (his elder son) | 1720-40 | Malwa, Bundelkhand; raided Delhi (1737); Palkhed (1728); organised the confederacy
  [141:1:0.2] Balaji Baji Rao (Nana Saheb) | 1740-61 | Zenith (Lahore, Peshawar); took Gujarat (APPSC 2025); lost Third Panipat; died 1761
  [141:1:0.3] Madhav Rao I | 1761-72 | Revival: beat the Nizam, Haidar Ali of Mysore, Rohillas, Rajputs, Jats; died 1772

[141:1:3] - Most charismatic Maratha leader after Shivaji; ideology **Hindu Pad Padshahi**; "**strike the trunk, the branches will fall**" (strike the Mughal centre).
[141:1:4] - Formed the **confederacy** to keep factions together and satisfy older chiefs under **Senapati Dabhade**.
[141:1:5] **Nana Saheb = Balaji Baji Rao**, not Balaji Vishwanath. **Raghoba** was Madhav Rao I's **uncle**, not brother.

[141:3:0] - **Nana Phadnavis**: "**Maratha Machiavelli**"; led the twelve-member **Barabhai** council; regent for infant Madhav Rao II (1774-95); held the confederacy together in the First war.
