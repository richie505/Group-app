## SUB 0 | AP and SCS – the 2014 Assurance and the Controversy
[0:0] # The assurance
[0:1] - **20 Feb 2014**: PM **Manmohan Singh** in the **Rajya Sabha** (not Lok Sabha, not a joint sitting) announced SCS for AP for **five years** – "a solemn assurance".
[0:2] - **Not written into the AP Reorganisation Act, 2014** – so the Centre treated it as non-binding. Exam keys still answer "duration of special status as per the Act" = **five years** (it is really the PM's assurance).
[0:3] - After bifurcation, citing the **revenue deficit**, CM **Chandrababu Naidu**'s main demand to GoI = **grant Special Category Status** (JL 2020).
[0:4] # Timeline

## SUB 1 | Origin, Criteria and Benefits of SCS
[1:0] # Origin
[1:1] - SCS introduced in **1969**, usually credited to the **5th Finance Commission**; the **Gadgil Formula** (1968-69) approved by the **National Development Council (NDC)** gave SCS States a reserved first share of Normal Central Assistance – do not swap FC and NDC.
[1:2] - **Not in the Constitution** – an executive, **Planning Commission-era** classification; later **Gadgil-Mukherjee Formula (1991)**.
[1:3] - **First three:** **J&K, Assam, Nagaland**; **11** SCS States in all, **last = Uttarakhand**.
[1:4] # Criteria (NDC)
[1:5] - **Hilly and difficult terrain**; **low population density and/or sizeable tribal population**; **strategic location along international borders**; **economic and infrastructural backwardness**; **non-viable state finances**.
[1:6] - NOT criteria: high population density, advanced infrastructure, high per-capita income.
[1:7] - **Raghuram Rajan Committee (2013)**: proposed a **Multi-Dimensional Index (MDI)** of state backwardness (10 sub-indicators) to replace SCS-style allocation – States scoring ≥0.6 "least developed" (SI 2019).
[1:8] # Benefits
[1:9] TABLE head: Benefit | SCS State | General State
  [1:9.0] CSS funding | Automatic 90:10 | Normal
  [1:9.1] Central loans | Concessional interest | Normal
  [1:9.2] Industry | Tax breaks and incentives | None special
  [1:9.3] Unspent funds | Do not lapse – carried forward | Lapse
  [1:9.4] Consumer Welfare Fund seed money | 90:10 | 75:25
  [1:9.5] PMGSY habitations | 250+ | 500+ (plains)
  [1:9.6] GST registration threshold | Lower (Rs 10 lakh) | Rs 20 lakh
[1:10] - Rationale: a larger share of central funds to cover the gap from geographic disadvantage and backwardness.
[1:11] # SCS States
[1:12] TABLE head: Status | States
  [1:12.0] Granted (11) | Assam, Nagaland, J&K, Himachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Tripura, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Uttarakhand
  [1:12.1] Asked, never granted | Bihar, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh
  [1:12.2] Not SCS | Telangana
[1:13] - Centre's fear: SCS for AP would bring identical demands from **Bihar and Odisha**.

## SUB 2 | Finance Commissions and the 42% Argument
[2:0] TABLE head: FC | Chair | Period | States' share | AP's share
  [2:0.0] 13th | Vijay Kelkar | 2010-15 | 32% | –
  [2:0.1] 14th | Y.V. Reddy | 2015-20 | 42% | 4.305%
  [2:0.2] 15th | N.K. Singh | 2020-21; 2021-26 | 41% | 4.111% (2020-21), 4.047% (2021-26)
  [2:0.3] 16th | Arvind Panagariya | 1 Apr 2026 - 31 Mar 2031 | 41% | 4.217%
[2:1] - 14th FC accepted **24 Feb 2015**; the **32% → 42%** jump applied from **2015-16** (Polytechnic Lecturers 2020) – the largest increase ever.
[2:2] - 14th FC made **no distinction** between general and special category States; Centre: higher devolution **subsumed** SCS benefits.
[2:3] - AP: 42% went to all States alike; it ignored AP's **asymmetric loss of Hyderabad**.
[2:4] - 16th FC added a **"Contribution to GDP"** criterion (**10%** weight) and ended revenue deficit grants.
[2:5] > Exam angle: 32 → 42 → 41 → 41; 14th FC from 2015-16; Y.V. Reddy.
