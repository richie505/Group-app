=== NOTES 1:234 | Khilafat and Non-cooperation Movement – Programme, Spread, Chauri Chaura and Withdrawal
## SUB 0 | Khilafat Movement
[234:0:0] # Aim and Trigger
[234:0:1] - **Objective:** protect/restore the **Ottoman Sultan as Khalifa (Caliph)** and protect the **territorial integrity of the Ottoman Empire** after its defeat in World War I.
[234:0:2] - **Trigger:** **Treaty of Sevres (May 1920)** dismembered Turkey; the British PM had promised Turkey would not be humiliated.
[234:0:3] - **Organisation:** **Khilafat Committee (1919)**.
[234:0:4] - **End:** lost meaning when Turkey **abolished the Caliphate in 1924**.
[234:0:5] # Khilafat Leaders
[234:0:6] TABLE head: Leader | Fact
  [234:0:6.0] Ali brothers (Muhammad Ali, Shaukat Ali) | Toured India with Gandhi; at the Karachi Khilafat conference (July 1921) urged Muslims to quit the Army
  [234:0:6.1] Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, Dr. M.A. Ansari | Khilafat leaders
  [234:0:6.2] Hakim Ajmal Khan | Gave up the title Haziq-ul-Mulk
  [234:0:6.3] Hasrat Mohani | Proposed Swaraj = complete independence (Ahmedabad session, 1921); coined "Inquilab Zindabad"
[234:0:7] Tej Bahadur Sapru was NOT a Khilafat leader; he was a mediator of the Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931).
[234:0:8] # Gandhi's Support
[234:0:9] - Gandhi presided over the **All-India Khilafat Conference, Delhi, November 1919**: **non-violent non-cooperation first proposed** here.
[234:0:10] - He merged the Khilafat cause with **Swaraj**; called it a **"golden opportunity"** for Hindu-Muslim unity "such as would not arise in a hundred years".
[234:0:11] - Aitchison: "we could not play off the Mohammedans against the Hindus" – about the Khilafat-NCM phase (1919-22), the **high point of Hindu-Muslim unity**.
[234:0:12] - **M.A. Jinnah did NOT support** the movement.
## SUB 1 | Background and Launch of Ncm
[234:1:0] # Causes
[234:1:1] - **Khilafat issue**, **Rowlatt Act** and **Jallianwala Bagh (13 April 1919)**.
[234:1:2] - "Inadequate" **Montagu-Chelmsford reforms** (Amritsar session 1919, president **Motilal Nehru**, called them inadequate and disappointing).
[234:1:3] Simon Commission Report and the Salt Law were NOT causes of the NCM (NCM: 1920 – February 1922).

## SUB 8 | Chauri Chaura and Withdrawal
[234:8:0] # Chauri Chaura
[234:8:1] - Date: **4 February 1922**; **Gorakhpur district, Uttar Pradesh**.
[234:8:2] - Crowd protested the **arrest of their leader** while campaigning against liquor sale; police fired → crowd **burnt the police station**; **22 policemen killed**.
[234:8:3] - Gandhi was at **Bardoli** when it happened.
[234:8:4] - **Defence:** **Madan Mohan Malaviya** (with Krishna Kant); the Gorakhpur Sessions Judge sentenced **172 to death**; **19 finally hanged** (1923).
[234:8:5] - **Chauri Chaura Centenary** inaugurated by **PM Narendra Modi, 4 February 2021**.
[234:8:6] # Withdrawal and Reactions
[234:8:7] - Gandhi fasted and called off the movement; **Bardoli resolution, 11-12 Feb 1922** → constructive work.
[234:8:8] - Censure vote against Gandhi at the **AICC, Delhi, 24 Feb 1922** – **Dr. Moonje**.
[234:8:9] - **Subhas Bose:** "national calamity", "sprinkling cold water"; **M.N. Roy:** weak leadership; C.R. Das also criticised.
[234:8:10] - **Marxist view:** stopped to prevent a radical anti-landlord turn.
[234:8:11] - **Results:** Khilafat issue died; Hindu-Muslim unity broke; communal riots 1921-27; revolutionary activity rose again 1923-28.
[234:8:12] NCM was withdrawn after Chauri Chaura, NOT after Jallianwala Bagh.

## SUB 9 | Gandhi's Arrest and the Great Trial
[234:9:0] - **Arrest:** **10 March 1922** at **Sabarmati, Ahmedabad**.
[234:9:1] - **Charge:** **sedition, Section 124A IPC**, for three **Young India** articles (incl. "Tampering with Loyalty", "Shaking the Manes").
[234:9:2] - **Great Trial:** **Ahmedabad, 18 March 1922**, Judge **C.N. Broomfield**; Gandhi **pleaded guilty** and asked for the highest penalty.
[234:9:3] - **Sentence:** **6 years**; released **1924** on health grounds.
[234:9:4] - Earlier, **April 1919**: Gandhi detained at **Palwal** on the way to Punjab.
[234:9:5] Gandhi's papers: Young India, Navajivan, Indian Opinion, Harijan. The Bengalee was Surendranath Banerjea's.
