## SUB 0 | Khilafat Movement – Aims, Leaders, Gandhi's Role
[0:0] # Aim and trigger
[0:1] - **Aim:** keep the **Ottoman Sultan as Khalifa** with control of the Muslim holy places, and save the **territorial integrity of the Ottoman (Turkish) Empire** after World War I – not a separate Muslim state, not land-revenue relief.
[0:2] - **Trigger:** **Treaty of Sevres (May 1920)** dismembered Turkey; the Khalifa was removed from power.
[0:3] - **Khilafat Committee, 1919** (Bombay) – led by the **Ali brothers (Muhammad Ali, Shaukat Ali)** with **Maulana Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Hasrat Mohani**, Dr M.A. Ansari. Started by the Ali brothers.
[0:4] - **End:** Mustafa Kemal Pasha stripped the Sultan of power (**Nov 1922**), made Turkey a secular republic (1923) and **abolished the Caliphate in 1924** – the issue lost relevance (CDS 2011: both statements true, II explains I).
[0:5] # Leaders
[0:6] TABLE head: Leader | Fact
  [0:6.0] Ali brothers | Started/led the movement; at Karachi (July 1921) urged Muslims to quit the Army – arrested Sept 1921
  [0:6.1] Hakim Ajmal Khan | Gave up the title Haziq-ul-Mulk; acting president, Ahmedabad 1921
  [0:6.2] Hasrat Mohani | Coined "Inquilab Zindabad"; demanded complete independence (1921)
  [0:6.3] Tej Bahadur Sapru | NOT a Khilafat leader (Liberal)
  [0:6.4] Jinnah | Opposed it; warned Gandhi not to encourage the fanaticism of Muslim religious leaders; denounced Gandhi's participation
[0:7] # Gandhi's role
[0:8] - **All-India Khilafat Conference, Delhi, 23-24 Nov 1919** – **Gandhi elected president**; non-cooperation first proposed there. Gandhi was president of the All India Khilafat Committee.
[0:9] - Gandhi saw it as a **"golden opportunity" for Hindu-Muslim unity** "such as would not arise in a hundred years" – to win Muslim support against the British.
[0:10] - It arose after the **First** World War (not the Second).
[0:11] - **Did not support:** Jinnah, **Madan Mohan Malaviya**; supporters included Jawaharlal Nehru, Swami Shraddhanand.
[0:12] > Result: the Khilafat-NCM phase was the **high point of Hindu-Muslim unity** (Hindu-Muslim differences narrowed).

## SUB 1 | Background and Launch of the NCM, 1920
[1:0] # Causes
[1:1] - **Khilafat issue**, **Rowlatt Act**, **Jallianwala Bagh (13 April 1919)** and Punjab wrongs, Hunter Committee "eyewash", inadequate **Montagu-Chelmsford reforms**, post-war economic hardship.
[1:2] - **NOT causes:** the **Salt Law** (1930), Simon Commission (1927).
[1:3] # Road to launch
[1:4] TABLE head: Date | Event
  [1:4.0] Nov 1919 | Khilafat Conference, Delhi (Gandhi president)
  [1:4.1] Feb 1920 | Joint Hindu-Muslim deputation to the Viceroy failed
  [1:4.2] May 1920 | Treaty of Sevres
  [1:4.3] June 1920 | Allahabad conference of the Khilafat Committee / all parties – approved boycott, asked Gandhi to lead
  [1:4.4] 1 Aug 1920 | Gandhi launched the NCM – the day Tilak died; returned his Kaiser-i-Hind medal
  [1:4.5] 31 Aug 1920 | Khilafat Committee formally started non-cooperation
  [1:4.6] Sept 1920 | Calcutta special session (Lala Lajpat Rai) – resolution first adopted/passed (1886 to 884); C.R. Das opposed
  [1:4.7] Dec 1920 | Nagpur session (C. Vijayaraghavachariar) – endorsed/ratified; C.R. Das moved the resolution
[1:5] - Year of launch = **1920**; slogan **"Swaraj within a year"** belongs to the NCM.
[1:6] > "Passed" at Calcutta, "ratified" at Nagpur: most keys give **Calcutta** for "first adopted (special session)" and **Nagpur** for "adopted/passed by the Congress".
[1:7] ~~ Sources: Spectrum pdf p.360-365; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.141-143, p.150; NextGen AP History Part 18 p.27; old app notes (vote 1886-884, Kaiser-i-Hind)
## SUB 2 | Congress Sessions and the Nagpur Changes
[2:0] TABLE head: Session | President | Point
  [2:0.0] Amritsar, Dec 1919 | Motilal Nehru | Reforms "inadequate and disappointing"
  [2:0.1] Calcutta special, Sept 1920 | Lala Lajpat Rai | NCM approved
  [2:0.2] Nagpur, Dec 1920 | C. Vijayaraghavachariar | NCM endorsed; new creed
  [2:0.3] Ahmedabad, Dec 1921 | C.R. Das (in jail) – Hakim Ajmal Khan acting | Hasrat Mohani proposed Swaraj = complete independence free from all foreign control; Gandhi made sole authority
  [2:0.4] Gaya, Dec 1922 | C.R. Das | Council entry defeated
  [2:0.5] Belgaum, Dec 1924 | Gandhi | His only Congress presidency
[2:1] # Nagpur changes
[2:2] - Creed: **"attainment of Swaraj by all legitimate and peaceful means"** (not "constitutional means"); programme in **stages** – civil disobedience and **non-payment of taxes** only later, if Swaraj did not come within a year and the Government repressed (UPSC 2025: both statements correct).
[2:3] - **CWC of 15**, **AICC of 350**, **PCCs on linguistic lines**, membership fee **four annas** (not one rupee).
[2:4] - Congress first declared its **policy towards the Indian (princely) States** – asked the princes to grant full responsible government.
[2:5] - **Hasrat Mohani** was the first to demand complete independence (1921); Purna Swaraj resolution came only at **Lahore 1929**.
[2:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum pdf p.364-365, p.368, p.840, p.858 (sessions list); PMF Modern History Part 4 p.142-143, p.152; old app notes (princely States, Amritsar)
## SUB 3 | NCM Programme – Boycott and Constructive Items
[3:0] TABLE head: Boycott (non-constructive) | Constructive
  [3:0.0] Surrender of titles and honours | National schools and colleges
  [3:0.1] Government schools, colleges, law courts (→ panchayats for disputes) | Khadi, charkha, hand-spinning
  [3:0.2] Legislatures – Nov 1920 elections boycotted | Hindu-Muslim unity, removal of untouchability
  [3:0.3] Foreign cloth (bonfires; imports fell by half), durbars and official functions | Temperance – picketing liquor and toddy shops
  [3:0.4] Second stage: civil disobedience, non-payment of taxes | Tilak Swaraj Fund – Rs 1 crore, oversubscribed
[3:1] - **Strict non-violence** throughout; Congress volunteer corps.
[3:2] - **NOT in the programme:** **retention of titles**, **participation in elections**, complying with government orders.
[3:3] - **Tilak Swaraj Fund (1921)** was raised **to finance the NCM / freedom struggle**, not a memorial.
[3:4] - **Rabindranath Tagore** called the burning of foreign cloth an **"insensate (ruthless) waste"**.
[3:5] - In Bihar the government even circulated lists of prominent drinkers to win back excise revenue lost to the anti-liquor drive (BPSC 2019 printed key; not verified).
[3:6] - Gandhi's methods: **Satyagraha, Swadeshi, non-violence, Hindu-Muslim unity**.

[5:0] # Lawyers who gave up practice
[5:1] - **C.R. Das** ("**Deshabandhu**", lawyer from East Bengal, defended Aurobindo in the **Alipore Bomb Case**), **Motilal Nehru** (English paper **The Independent**, 1919), Jawaharlal Nehru, **Rajendra Prasad** (Bihar pleader), Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, **Saifuddin Kitchlew** (Punjab), Asaf Ali, **T. [5:3] - Jinnah, Annie Besant, B.C. Pal, G.S. Khaparde **left the Congress**; Jinnah did NOT support the NCM.

## SUB 6 | Chauri Chaura and the Withdrawal
[6:0] - **1 Feb 1922:** Gandhi's ultimatum – civil disobedience from **Bardoli** (Gujarat); Gandhi was at **Bardoli** when Chauri Chaura happened.
[6:1] - **Chauri Chaura, 4 Feb 1922** (Spectrum, PMF: 5 Feb), **Gorakhpur district, United Provinces (UP)** – police beat a volunteer leader campaigning against **liquor sale**, fired on the crowd → crowd **burnt the police station** → **22 policemen** killed.
[6:2] - Sequence: police firing → police station burnt → movement suspended (**Bardoli resolution, CWC, 11-12 Feb 1922**) → Gandhi arrested (**10 March 1922**).
[6:3] - **Reason for withdrawal: violence at Chauri Chaura** – not arrests, not British concessions, not Jallianwala Bagh (1919).
[6:4] - **Defence of the accused:** **Madan Mohan Malaviya with Krishna Kant**; 172 sentenced to death, **19 hanged**.
[6:5] - **AICC Delhi, 24 Feb 1922:** **Dr. B.S. Moonje** moved a censure vote against Gandhi.
[6:6] - Reactions: **Subhas Bose** – "national calamity"; **Jawaharlal Nehru** – "amazement and consternation"; **M.N. Roy** – weak leadership; Marxists – to stop a radical anti-landlord turn.
[6:7] - **Centenary:** PM Narendra Modi inaugurated the **Chauri Chaura centenary** on **4 Feb 2021**.
[6:8] TABLE head: Chronology | Date
  [6:8.0] Champaran | 1917
  [6:8.1] Amritsar (Jallianwala Bagh) | 1919
  [6:8.2] Moplah revolt | 1921
  [6:8.3] Chauri Chaura | Feb 1922
[6:9] ~~ Sources: Spectrum pdf p.368-370, p.842; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.147-148; NextGen AP History Part 18 p.28; NextGen Indian History Part 6 p.57; old app notes (Malaviya defence, Moonje, centenary)
## SUB 7 | Gandhi's Arrest, Outcome and Evaluation
[7:0] - Arrested **10 March 1922** (Sabarmati); **Great Trial, Ahmedabad, 18 March 1922**, Judge **C.N. Broomfield**; sedition **Sec. 124A IPC** for **Young India** articles ("Tampering with Loyalty"); **pleaded guilty**, asked for the highest penalty; **6 years**; released **1924** on health grounds.
[7:1] - Earlier, April 1919, Gandhi was detained at **Palwal**. Gandhi's papers: Young India, Navajivan, Harijan, Indian Opinion (The Bengalee = S.N. Banerjea).
[7:2] # Outcome
[7:3] - **Hindu-Muslim unity** at its peak; **Congress became a mass movement for the first time**; **fear of British might removed**; nationalism spread from the elite to the masses – but **no British concessions**, Swaraj not won in a year.
[7:4] - The **Gandhian era** of the freedom movement = **1920-1947**.
[7:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum pdf p.369-371; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.148-149; old app notes (trial details, Palwal)
