=== Swarajists (Pro-changers) and No-changers
- After the NCM was **suspended (Bardoli, Feb 1922)** and Gandhi arrested, the Congress split on **council entry**.
| Point | Swarajists (pro-changers) | No-changers |
|---|---|---|
| Leaders | **C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru**, Hakim Ajmal Khan, **Vithalbhai Patel**, S. Srinivasa Iyengar, N.C. Kelkar | **C. Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad, M.A. Ansari** |
| Strategy | **Enter councils to "end or mend" them**, obstruct from within | **Constructive work**, continue the **boycott of councils** |
- **Gaya session (Dec 1922, president C.R. Das):** Das's council-entry resolution **defeated** → **C.R. Das resigned the Congress presidency** and Motilal the secretaryship.
- **Congress-Khilafat Swaraj Party** announced **31 Dec 1922**, formed **1 Jan 1923** – **C.R. Das president, Motilal Nehru secretary** (not Jawaharlal, not Rajagopalachari, not Gokhale who died 1915).
- Reasons: **withdrawal of the NCM** and the aim to **enter the councils and wreck the 1919 Act from within**.
- **Independent Party (Dec 1922):** **Madan Mohan Malaviya + Motilal Nehru**.
- **Gandhi's attitude:** first opposed council entry, but trusted the Swarajists' **bona fides** and valued them as respected leaders; reconciled after release (Feb 1924).
- **C. Rajagopalachari** – No-changer leader, called the **"keeper of Gandhi's conscience"**.
- Regional: **Assam – Tarun Ram Phukan** (president); **Bihar Swaraj Dal – Shri Krishna Singh**; **Andhra branch – president Ayyadevara Kaleswara Rao, secretary Unnava Lakshminarayana, treasurer Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao**; Pithapuram Raja's People's Party backed them.

=== Swarajists in the Legislatures
- **Delhi special session, Sept 1923** (Maulana Azad) allowed the Swarajists to **contest the Nov 1923 elections**; they won **42 seats** in the Central Assembly and a majority in the **Central Provinces**; **V. Ramadas** won from Madras.
- **Nationalist Party (1924):** Swarajists + Jinnah's Independents + Malaviya's group (~70); outvoted the government, blocked the Finance Bill.
- **Muddiman Committee (1924)** – result of their demand to review the **1919 Act**.
- **Vithalbhai Patel** – **first elected Indian President (Speaker) of the Central Legislative Assembly, 1925**, by **58 to 56** over T. Rangachariar (Vallabhbhai Patel was a No-changer).
- **Public Safety Bill, 1928** – defeated/opposed by the Swarajists.
- **Responsivists** (Lajpat Rai, Malaviya, N.C. Kelkar) wanted **cooperation with the government**; **C.R. Das died June 1925**; walk-out from legislatures 1926 and finally **1930** after Lahore.

=== Constructive Programme of the 1920s
- No-changers' work: **khadi and spinning**, **national schools**, **Hindu-Muslim unity**, **removal of untouchability**, **prohibition / boycott of liquor**, **village panchayats**, ashrams among tribals and lower castes, flood relief – **not council entry**.
- Council work and constructive work were **complementary** and kept the movement alive in the **1922-28 lull**; constructive workers became the organisers of civil disobedience.
- Andhra: **Ponduru (Srikakulam) khadi**; women did **picketing and prohibition** work.
