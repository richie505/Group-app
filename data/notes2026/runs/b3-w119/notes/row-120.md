=== Land Use, Holdings and Crops
# Land Use
- Agriculture supports about **60% of population**; agriculture and allied = **33% of GVA** (2025-26).
- Geographical area **162.97 lakh ha**; net sown area **49.55 lakh ha = 30.40%** (2023-24, drought); **59.14 lakh ha = 36.29%** (2024-25).
- NSA fell from 67.26 lakh ha (2008-09) to 49.55 lakh ha (2023-24). Barren and uncultivable land 13.35 lakh ha (8.19%).
- **Older land-use keys:** least NSA **Vizianagaram**, largest barren land **YSR Kadapa** (2015-16).
- **NITI Aayog: cultivated area 10.1 million ha (2020-21) "= 37%" of geographical area** (keyed; 37% actually fits the net sown area).
- **Common Property Resources** (NSS 1998): about **9%** of AP's area (20.3 lakh ha) vs India's 15%.
- **Cropping intensity** (GCA / NSA): **1.25** (2016-17); **1.21** (2023-24).
# Operational Holdings
- Average holding **0.94 ha (2015-16) → 0.86 ha (2021-22)**; holdings **85.24 lakh → 96.90 lakh**; nationally **small and marginal (0-2 ha)** holdings dominate (**86.21%**).
- **Marginal holdings: about 49.84 lakh** (Agriculture Census 2010-11). Most holdings (2010-11, present AP): **Guntur** (keyed).
# Food Grains and Paddy
- **Paddy = largest area of any crop**: **20.97 lakh ha of 37.51 lakh ha** food-grain area (~56%, 2024-25); output **127.15 lakh t** (**kharif 78.79 > rabi 48.36**); yield ~6.1 t/ha.
- Food-grain area: **41.44 lakh ha (2021-22)** → 33.24 (2023-24) → 37.51 (2024-25); output 161.86 lakh t (2024-25).
- **Food-grain output 2018-19: 151 lakh t** (SES 2018-19; Ministry final figures lower).
- Area under food crops has been **growing more slowly / declining** relative to non-food crops (shift to commercial crops).
- Rain-fed yields are low: about **1 tonne/ha**.
- **Cotton ("white gold")**: commercial crop with the **largest area expansion**; **2nd crop in GVA after paddy**.
- Rice yield above national average: AP **3,846 kg/ha** vs India 2,844 (2022-23).
- Rice yield 2021-22: AP **3,392 kg/ha** vs India 2,798. Food-grain area **up 12.85%** in 2024-25 after an **11.7% rainfall surplus** (previous year 16.5% deficit).
- **Second food grain after paddy:** **jowar** in older keys; **maize** is now AP's second cereal. Largest food-crop area (undivided AP, key): **West Godavari**.
# Cropping Pattern Shifts
- Shift to **fruits, vegetables, flowers, spices** (profitability, exports); Godavari delta **paddy land converted to aquaculture**.
- Debate: paddy is a major **methane** source; experts want diversification with procurement support.
- Kharif 2026: only ~61% of normal area sown by 19 Aug (groundnut 34%, paddy 62%); El Nino action plan (Jul 2026): control room, RSK advisories, **one Mobile Rythu Bazaar per district** (no compulsory shift to sugarcane).

=== Natural Farming – ZBNF to APCNF
# Origin and Agency
- **ZBNF** developed **mid-1990s** by **Subhash Palekar** (Padma Shri); "zero budget" = **no purchased fertilisers or pesticides**.
- AP launched ZBNF in **2016**; renamed **APCNF = Andhra Pradesh Community-managed Natural Farming**. **2018** = AP's Year of Natural Farming.
- **RySS = Rythu Sadhikara Samstha**: **not-for-profit company** of the **AP Department of Agriculture**; incorporated **October 2014**; implements APCNF.
- **Target (2018)**: make AP **India's first 100% natural farming (ZBNF) State by 2024** (6 million farmers); now "100% natural farming" by 2047.
- **ZBNF won 3rd place** (top 10 of 120 entries) at the **Paris Peace Forum, Nov 2018**.
# Practices
| Practice | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Beejamrutham | Seed treatment |
| Jeevamrutham | Bio-fertiliser: cow dung, urine, jaggery, pulse flour, soil |
| Ghanajeevamrutham | Solid form of Jeevamrutham |
| Mulching (Acchadana) | Crop residue cover: saves moisture, checks weeds |
| PMDS | Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing on residual moisture |
| 365-day green cover | Soil covered all year by cover crops |
- Trap crops: marigold, castor. PMDS on 92,000 acres in Kurnool (Jul 2026).
# Coverage and Targets
- Now: **10.20 lakh farmers, 4.89 lakh ha**, 4,116 GPs. Targets: all GPs by 2026-27; 41 lakh farmers on 19 lakh ha by 2028-29.
- Unit = **cluster of about 5 Gram Panchayats/villages** (~2,000 farmers; 1,500-2,500 acres).
- **Pesticide use** falling: 2,650 MT (2014-15) → 1,990 MT (2023-24); **266.166 MT a.i. reduction in 2023-24** and 193.248 MT in Kharif 2024-25, credited to **awareness** by the Agriculture Department.
# Funding, Awards and Replication
- **KfW** (German bank) loan; **Azim Premji Foundation** grants.
| Award | Detail |
|---|---|
| M.S. Swaminathan Award for Environment Protection | To RySS (2022-23) |
| Gulbenkian Prize for Humanity (shared) | 2024; Lisbon, 11 July 2024 |
| **Food Planet Prize** | **Sweden** (Curt Bergfors Foundation); launched with US$1 million prizes; **2026 prize US$1.5 million**, Bastad, June 2026; APCNF first Indian finalist |
- APCNF = "**world's largest agro-ecological transition**"; copied in **Zambia and Sri Lanka**.
- **NMNF** = natural farming (no external inputs); **PKVY** = **organic**, clusters of **50 farmers / 50 acres**, PGS-India certified.
- NMNF action plans: **7.78 lakh ha, 19.75 lakh farmers**; PKVY: 200 tribal clusters in Visakhapatnam. RySS earlier ran the **Debt Redemption Scheme 2014**; ZBNF features: minimum tillage, livestock integration, crop diversity, no chemicals. Kenyan and Gujarat delegations visited Guntur (2026).
- **ISHA Foundation awards (28 July 2024)**: four APCNF paddy farmers from **Guntur, Krishna, West Godavari and Srikakulam** honoured at Sripuram (Vellore), Save Soil campaign.

=== Fisheries and Aquaculture
# Coastline, Output and Ranks
- **Coastline 1,053.07 km – 3rd in India** after Gujarat and Tamil Nadu (APSES 2025-26; PIB Dec 2025); older figure **974 km, "2nd longest"**.
- AP ≈ **30% of India's fish** and **31% of shrimp exports** (SES 2024-25); inland fisheries dominate (~82%).
- **Shrimp / cultured prawn**: AP **India's largest producer** (~**70%** of cultured prawns, Budget 2017-18).
- Aquaculture = core of AP's **Blue Economy**; 5 lakh+ families.
# Seafood Exports
| Share | Base |
|---|---|
| **45%** | India's seafood exports (AP Budget 2017-18) |
| **36%** | India's seafood export value, end of 2018-19 |
| 31% | India's shrimp exports (2024-25) |
| 75% | India's cultured-shrimp exports (2025-26) |
- **2019**: AP shrimp exports hit by a **fall in international shrimp prices**.
- **MPEDA**: under **Ministry of Commerce**; arms RGCA, NETFISH, NaCSA.
# Policy, Regulation and Infrastructure
- **AP Fisheries Policy 2015-20**: MI/Gram Panchayat tanks leased to fishermen cooperative societies for **3 years**.
- **APSADA Act, 2020**: licenses **freshwater** aquaculture (e-Matsyakar); farms **within 2 km of HTL** → **Coastal Aquaculture Authority (CAA Act, 2005)**.
- **India's first operational Mega Aqua Food Park**: **Godavari Mega Aqua Food Park, Tundurru (Bhimavaram), West Godavari**, commissioned Feb 2019.
- **10 fishing harbours** under **PMMSY** (not Sagarmala). Aqua-power tariff **Rs 1.50/unit** up to 10 acres in notified zones.
- Fishing harbours cost Rs 3,699.09 crore (CSS-PMMSY); aqua tariff **Rs 3.85** for bigger farms or outside zones (10-acre cap lifted Aug 2026).
- **PMMSY subsidy**: **40% general** (24% GoI + 16% GoAP), **60% women/SC/ST** (36% + 24%); PM-MKSSY covers institutional credit, aquaculture insurance, traceability (**no compulsory trawler replacement**).
- **China = 2nd-largest** seafood destination (2025-26, +20.1% volume); record exports Rs 73,890 crore; output 55.39 lakh t (2024-25), inland ~82% / marine ~18% (2017-18); exports depend on the US market.
- **PMMSY (2020)**: **Rs 20,050 crore**; **PM-MKSSY**: central sector sub-scheme, 2023-24 to 2026-27, **Rs 6,000 crore**; FIDF corpus Rs 7,522 crore.
# Fishermen Welfare
- **Marine fishing ban: 61 days, 15 April – 14 June**; relief **Rs 20,000 per family** (from 2024-25; Rs 10,000 from 2019-20, Rs 4,000 before).
- Diesel subsidy **Rs 9/litre**; ex-gratia **Rs 10 lakh**; **AP Fisheries University**: **Narsapur, West Godavari**.
- Ex-gratia 2024-25: Rs 315 lakh to 63 families; KCC to fishers since 2018-19. Fisheries University is **not** in East Godavari.

=== Horticulture and Oil Palm
# Size and Ranks
- **AP = largest fruit producer in India** (**1st**, 15.6% of national horticulture output); 18.23 lakh ha, 365.92 lakh MT (SES 2024-25). Budget 2017-18 placed AP **2nd** in fruits.
- **1st in productivity**: **oil palm, papaya, lime, cocoa, tomato, chillies**; **2nd**: **mango, sweet orange, turmeric** (SES 2024-25).
- **Rayalaseema** = horticulture hub: 45% of area, 52% of output, 71% of micro-irrigation area.
- Growth-engine crops: **11 crops in 24 clusters** (banana, mango, chilli, oil palm, cocoa, dragon fruit, onion, cashew, coconut, tomato, coffee).
- Horticulture GVA Rs 1,52,950 crore: AP **3rd** after UP and Maharashtra; target Rs 3 lakh crore by 2029-30.
# Oil Palm
- AP **1st in area, production, productivity** of oil palm: 2.27 lakh ha, 17.63 lakh MT FFB, 3.10 lakh MT CPO (2023-24); 2.49 lakh ha = 48% of India, FFB **22.81 lakh MT** (2025-26).
- **Palm oil production 17.81 lakh tonnes** (2017-18 agriculture statistics).
- **Palm oil = most consumed edible oil in India** (mostly imported).
- **NMEO-OP (2021)**: reach **10 lakh ha**; earlier Oilseeds Technology Mission (1986) → NMOOP (2014).
# MIDH, Micro-irrigation, Post-harvest
- **MIDH** (2014-15): NHM + HMNEH; **60:40** general, 90:10 NE/Himalayan. **NHM** from 2005-06.
- **APMIP** since **2003**: 37.90 lakh acres, 13.49 lakh farmers; targets **35% of GSA by 2030**, **50% by 2047**.
- Post-harvest target 2025-26: **1,030 units**. **Operation Greens (TOP)**: tomato, onion, potato.
- Operation Greens is modelled on **Operation Flood**; value-chain study crops: **mango, banana, tomato, pomegranate, sweet lime, papaya** (**not oil palm**).
- **NHM**: started **May 2005**; selected districts of **18 States and 5 UTs** (incl. UP); **NE and Himalayan States not under NHM**; objectives: high growth, post-harvest management, human resources (**financial assistance not an objective**). MIDH: **100% central for NHB, CDB, CIH**.
- APMIP: **75% drip**; target 15 lakh ha in five years. Oil palm: NMEO-OP adds **6.5 lakh ha**; CPO **11.20 lakh t by 2025-26**, **28 lakh t by 2029-30**; edible-oil import dependence **63.2% (2015-16) → 56.25% (2023-24)**. Coconut: AP 4th (1.06 lakh ha).
- Horticulture area **46.43 lakh acres**, output **406.38 lakh MT** (Survey 2025-26); 21% GVA growth (2024-25); **22 growth-engine crops**; Rayalaseema ~7.8 lakh ha; chillies 2nd (2025-26); 1st in productivity also coconut.
