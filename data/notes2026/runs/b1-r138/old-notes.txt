=== NOTES 1:138 | Mughal Painting, Music, Language and Literature; Persian-sanskrit Translation Movement
## SUB 0 | Mughal Painting – Emperor-wise Evolution
[138:0:0] - Evolution: **Persian miniature → realistic, colourful Indian style**.
[138:0:1] - **Humayun** laid the foundation: brought the Persian masters **Mir Sayyid Ali** (of Tabriz) and **Khwaja Abdus Samad** → founded the Mughal school (APPSC G2 2025, statement a).
[138:0:2] - **Jahangir's reign = golden period / zenith** of Mughal painting; **Aurangzeb withdrew patronage** and painting declined.
[138:0:3] # Emperor → Emphasis
[138:0:4] TABLE head: Emperor | Emphasis
  [138:0:4.0] Humayun | Foundation through the two Persian masters Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad
  [138:0:4.1] Akbar | Illustrated manuscripts; Tasvir Khana atelier (painting karkhana) of 100+ painters (Daswant, Basawan, Mukund); Hamzanama, Tutinama, Akbarnama, Razmnama; 3-D figures and realism
  [138:0:4.2] Jahangir | Zenith: portraits, albums, flora and fauna, naturalism and precision; European influence; introduced the halo (nimbus) behind the emperor's head
  [138:0:4.3] Shah Jahan | Formal court scenes, albums (muraqqas), floral borders, heavy use of gold
  [138:0:4.4] Aurangzeb | Withdrew patronage → painters moved to Rajasthan, Punjab Hills and Bihar, fuelling Rajput and Pahari schools
[138:0:5] - Akbar set up painting karkhanas at **Agra, Fatehpur Sikri and Lahore**.
[138:0:6] - **Jahangir** moved the focus from large illustrated manuscripts to **albums and individual portraits**: events of his life, birds, flowers, animals; he had his own studio and supported European and Persian art.
[138:0:7] # European Influence (NEW angle)
[138:0:8] - European pictures reached **Akbar's court through the Portuguese** → **portraiture** and the depiction of **smaller distant objects for spatial depth**.
[138:0:9] - Mughal style = muted, naturalistic colour, **perspective and shading** (European influence), cosmopolitan.
[138:0:10] APPSC G2 2025: Humayun brought the Persian masters + Jahangir's reign = golden age – both statements correct.
[138:0:11] - **Akbar's Goa mission (Jesuits):** at Akbar's request the **first Jesuit mission from Goa** reached his court in **1580**, led by Father **Rudolf Acquaviva** with Father **Antonio Monserrate** and Brother Francis Henriques. They brought a printed **Polyglot Bible**; Akbar had its Christian pictures shown to his painters.

## SUB 2 | Regional Painting Schools
[138:2:0] TABLE head: School | Region / features
  [138:2:0.0] Rajput | Rajasthan kingdoms; Hindu epics, love legends, daily life; bold, vivid colours; flat, 2-D; indigenous, decorative
  [138:2:0.1] Pahari | North-western Himalayan hill states: Himachal, Jammu and Kashmir, Tehri/Paudi Garhwal, Almora; 17th-19th c.
  [138:2:0.2] Kangra | Himalayan region; peaked under Raja Sansar Chand
  [138:2:0.3] Kishangarh | Rajasthan; patron Raja Sawant Singh (Nagari Das), throne 1748; Bani Thani; Radha-Krishna theme
  [138:2:0.4] Deccani | Began in the Bahmani court, continued in successor states; at Golconda founder Mir Hashim (later invited to the Mughal court); heavy gold
  [138:2:0.5] Kalighat | Native, later Bengal style – not shaped by Mughal painting
[138:2:1] - Mughal painting **influenced** the Pahari, Rajasthani and Kangra schools.
[138:2:2] - **Bani Thani**: by **Nihal Chand**; inspired by Sawant Singh's poem ***Bihari Jas Chandrika***. Other Kishangarh artists: Amirchand, Chhotu, Bhawanidas, Sitaram. Kishangarh rulers supported the **Nimbarka** sect; Sawant Singh wrote verses praising Krishna.
[138:2:3] - Deccan's first coloured miniatures: historian **Sherwani** – the **14 miniatures** (including the Battle of Rakshasi-Tangadi).
[138:2:4] - Qutb Shahi Deccani painting (Mir Hashim) = a school independent of the Mughal style.
[138:2:5] Which school was NOT affected by Mughal painting? **Kalighat**.

[138:3:7] - Non-Islamic (indigenous) vocal style: **Dhrupad** (⚠ APPSC key (2010) gave Taraka).
[138:3:8] - **Aurangzeb** ended court music, yet **played the veena**; many Persian works on classical music date from his reign.

## SUB 6 | Dara Shikoh and Hindi Poets
[138:6:0] # Dara Shikoh – Sufism-Vedanta Synthesis
[138:6:1] - **Shah Jahan's eldest son**; **Qadiri** Sufi, linked with **Miyan Mir** and **Muhibullah Shah**.
[138:6:2] - **Majma-ul-Bahrain** ("Mingling of the Two Oceans") – common ground of **Sufism and Vedanta**.
[138:6:3] - **Sirr-i-Akbar** ("The Great Secret", 1657) – Persian translation of about 52 Upanishads; its Latin version (**Anquetil-Duperron**) took the Upanishads to Europe.
[138:6:4] # Hindi Poets → Works and Patrons
[138:6:5] - **Akbar's reign = golden era of Hindi poetry** (his tolerance led to a surge).
[138:6:6] TABLE head: Poet | Works | Patron / period
  [138:6:6.0] Tulsidas | Ramcharitmanas (Awadhi devotional poem), Vinay Patrika | Contemporary of Akbar and Jahangir; not a court poet
  [138:6:6.1] Surdas | Sur Sagar | Akbar's age
  [138:6:6.2] Keshavdas | Ramchandrika, Kavipriya, Rasikpriya, Ratan Bavani, Chhandamala | Bundela court of Orchha
  [138:6:6.3] Kavindracharya Saraswati | *Kavindra-kalpalata* (Hindi) | Shah Jahan; got him to cancel the pilgrim tax at Banaras and Prayag
  [138:6:6.4] Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khana | Hindi dohas; Persian Baburnama | Akbar (Navaratna); a Muslim scholar with a big role in Hindi literature
  [138:6:6.5] Padmakar | Ram Rasayan, Ganga Lahari, Prabodh Pachasa | Later poet
[138:6:7] - **Faizi** = Persian poet with Sufi themes.
[138:6:8] MPPSC 2025: **Ram Rasayan is NOT by Keshavdas** – it is by Padmakar.
## SUB 7 | Languages – Persian, Urdu/rekhta and Deccani
[138:7:0] - **Persian = Mughal court language**; it had replaced Sanskrit and Arabic (from the Sultanate) as the language of administration, diplomacy and high culture.
[138:7:1] - **Urdu** (Hindavi → Rekhta, Lashkari, Urdu) grew out of **military camps and bazaars**; Perso-Arabic script; mix of Persian, Arabic, Turkic, local Prakrits and Braj. Later patronised by princely courts (**Delhi, Lucknow**).
[138:7:2] - **Deccani/Dakhni Urdu**: lingua franca of Deccan camps and bazaars; grew under the **Qutb Shahis**.
[138:7:3] TABLE head: Person / era | Contribution
  [138:7:3.0] Mulla Khiali | First Deccani poetry in Telangana
  [138:7:3.1] Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah | First Urdu poet with a diwan; built the Charminar
  [138:7:3.2] Abdullah Qutb Shah's era | Golden Age of Urdu
  [138:7:3.3] Ghavvasi | Tuti Nama (1639), from the Sanskrit Shuka Saptati
[138:7:4] # AP Angle – Qutb Shahi Culture
[138:7:5] - Qutb Shahi official language **Persian**, people's language **Telugu**; Deccani painting and Deccani Urdu flourished.
[138:7:6] - **Kuchipudi** grew under **Siddhendra Yogi**; **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** granted Kuchipudi village to its dancers.
[138:7:7] - **Aurangzeb annexed Golconda on 3 October 1687** as the 21st Suba; Nizam-ul-Mulk took part in the siege.
[138:7:8] - Translation + painting + Hindavi/Urdu = **composite Indo-Persian culture**.

[138:8:6] # Invasions of Nadir Shah and Ahmad Shah Abdali
[138:8:7] - **Nadir Shah** (Afsharid ruler of Iran) defeated Muhammad Shah at the **Battle of Karnal, 24 February 1739**; entered Delhi 20 March 1739 and sacked it; carried away the **Peacock Throne** (built by Shah Jahan) and the **Koh-i-Noor**.
[138:8:8] - **Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani)**, a commander of Nadir Shah, took the Afghan throne in 1747; invaded north India repeatedly **(1748-1767)**, plundering down to Delhi and Mathura; won the **Third Battle of Panipat (14 January 1761)** over the Marathas.
