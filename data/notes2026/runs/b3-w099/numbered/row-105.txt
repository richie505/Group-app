## SUB 0 | Deficit Definitions and Formulas
[0:0] TABLE head: Deficit | Formula | Shows
  [0:0.0] Revenue (RD) | Revenue expenditure − revenue receipts | Borrowing for day-to-day consumption (dissaving)
  [0:0.1] Effective RD (ERD) | RD − grants for creation of capital assets | True consumption deficit (C. Rangarajan concept)
  [0:0.2] Fiscal (FD / GFD) | Total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts, i.e. recovery of loans + disinvestment) | Total borrowing requirement
  [0:0.3] Primary (PD) | FD − interest payments | Current imbalance, excluding past debt
  [0:0.4] Budget deficit (old) | Total expenditure − total receipts (incl. borrowings) | Ad hoc T-bill era measure
  [0:0.5] Monetised | Part of FD financed by the RBI (high-powered money) | Inflationary
  [0:0.6] Net FD | GFD − net lending of the Centre | e.g. 1,200 − 400 = ₹800 crore
[0:1] - **FD** = total expenditure minus total receipts **excluding borrowings** = **budget deficit + borrowings and other liabilities** (market borrowings) (IAS 1994; APPSC Lecturers 2023; State PSC).
[0:2] - FD "closest to" the **interest-bearing borrowings during a year** (APPSC G2 2017) – a flow, not accumulated debt.
[0:3] - Loose exam wording: "excess of total expenditure over revenue receipts" = **fiscal deficit** (APPSC G2 2011) – strictly non-debt capital receipts are also deducted.
[0:4] - **GFD (financing side) = net borrowing at home + borrowing from RBI + borrowing from abroad** (RAS 2023). Net borrowing at home includes small savings and, indirectly, bank SLR holdings.
[0:5] - **FD = RD + capital expenditure − non-debt capital receipts**; RD is a part of FD.
[0:6] - **PD = FD − interest** (= **gross primary deficit** when taken from GFD) (APPSC G2 2017, UPPSC ×4); **PD + interest = FD** (UPPSC 2010). It is NOT revenue deficit minus interest.
[0:7] - **ERD** introduced in **Union Budget 2011-12**; added to FRBM by the **2012 amendment**; dropped as a target in 2018. ERD shows capital receipts used for consumption.
[0:8] - RD, FD, PD were adopted as official terms from **1997-98** (Ramesh Singh); budget deficit stopped being the headline measure after ad hoc T-bills ended (1997).
[0:9] - **Headline marker:** the **fiscal deficit** is the headline marker of fiscal health; RD and ERD show what the borrowing is used for.
[0:10] # Worked Examples
[0:11] - FD ₹1,50,000 crore − interest ₹1,20,000 crore = **PD ₹30,000 crore**.
[0:12] - Gross primary deficit: ₹50,000 − ₹1,500 = **₹48,500 crore** (non-debt capital receipts not deducted again).
[0:13] - FD ₹4 crore on GDP ₹400 crore = **1% of GDP** (NextGen).
[0:14] > Trap: "Primary deficit = revenue deficit − interest" is wrong; PD = **fiscal** deficit − interest.

## SUB 1 | Relationships Between Deficits
[1:0] - **FD > PD** always (interest is positive); **FD > RD** in practice (FD also covers borrowing for capital spending) (UPPSC 2008); **ERD ≤ RD**.
[1:1] - **PD is never higher than FD** – "PD always higher than FD" is false, "PD = FD − interest" is true.
[1:2] - **FD > budgetary deficit**: FD also counts borrowings (incl. from the RBI) and other liabilities (UPSC 1999 A-R: both true, R explains A).
[1:3] - Largest deficit in the GoI budget = **fiscal deficit** (UPPSC 2002 ×3). Descending order: **fiscal > revenue > primary** (2026-27 BE: **4.3 > 1.5 > 0.7**).
[1:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:4.0] Zero primary deficit | FD = interest; borrowing just equals interest payment – debt-trap signal
  [1:4.1] Debt trap | Borrowing to make interest payments
  [1:4.2] Zero RD | Balanced revenue budget
  [1:4.3] Primary surplus | Non-interest spending covered by non-debt receipts
[1:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.12 (PD, RD part of FD); Union Budget 2026-27 Budget at a Glance p.5 (FD 4.3, RD 1.5, PD 0.7); app notes 3:105 sub 5

## SUB 3 | Fiscal Policy – Meaning, Makers and Persons
[3:0] - **Fiscal policy** = government policy on **taxation, expenditure and public debt** (its income, spending and debt) to shape aggregate demand, allocation and distribution (MPPSC 1996; APPSC Dental 2019).
[3:1] - Word from Latin **fiscus** = basket / public purse.
[3:2] - Formulated by the **Ministry of Finance** – not the RBI (monetary policy), Finance Commission or Planning Commission (APSLPRB 2012; UPPSC 2012); Union Budget prepared by the Budget Division, **Department of Economic Affairs (DEA)**.
[3:3] - **Article 112** calls it the **"Annual Financial Statement"**; the word "Budget" is not in the Constitution. The **budget is an instrument of fiscal policy** (JPSC 2013).
[3:4] TABLE head: Person / body | Contribution
  [3:4.0] J.M. Keynes | *General Theory* (1936); fiscal policy as the tool against the Great Depression (UKPSC 2012)
  [3:4.1] V.P. Singh | Long-Term Fiscal Policy, laid in Parliament Dec 1985 (Budget year 1985-86) as Finance Minister
  [3:4.2] LTFP's key reform | MODVAT – introduced in the 1986-87 Budget (from 1 March 1986), on L.K. Jha Committee lines
  [3:4.3] Richard Musgrave | Three budget functions; differential tax incidence; popularised "fiscal federalism" (1959)
  [3:4.4] Montek Singh Ahluwalia | Chaired the RBI Advisory Group on Fiscal Transparency (report 2001)

## SUB 4 | Objectives and Instruments; Fiscal vs Monetary
[4:0] # Objectives (Musgrave functions)
[4:1] TABLE head: Function | Meaning
  [4:1.0] Allocation | Provide public goods (judiciary, lighthouse, fireworks display); education is a merit good, not a pure public good
  [4:1.1] Distribution | Reduce inequality: progressive taxation + progressive expenditure (UPSC 1996)
  [4:1.2] Stabilisation | Control inflation and unemployment – minimising the risk of inflation is a fiscal aim (APPSC G2 2011)
[4:2] - Objectives: **economic growth, distributive justice and price stability** (APPSC G2 2011), **full employment**, resource mobilisation, **balanced regional development**.
[4:3] - **Regulation of inter-state trade** is NOT a fiscal objective (UPPSC 2006); directing interest rates and managing money supply are monetary objectives.
[4:4] - **Economic growth, full employment, exchange stabilisation and price stability** = objectives of **monetary policy** (APPSC G2 2011) – exchange-rate stability is the monetary marker.
[4:5] - Progressive taxation evidence: direct taxes = **58.8%** of total taxes (FY25).
[4:6] - Components (taxation, expenditure, debt) and objectives (growth, employment, price stability, equity) are different things; the objectives do not explain the components.
[4:7] # Instruments
[4:8] - Fiscal tools: **taxation (tax policy)**, **public expenditure** (capex as **pump priming**), **public borrowing/debt**, the **budget**, subsidies/DBT, transfer payments, **deficit financing** (State PSC; RAS 2023).
[4:9] - **Interest rate, CRR, bank rate, credit ceiling** = monetary tools, NOT fiscal (APPSC FSO; RAS 2023).
[4:10] - Public borrowing: **internal** and **external**; external borrowing carries **currency risk**.
[4:11] - Public expenditure (Centre + States) ≈ **28% of GDP** (ES 2016-17); Union alone **13.6%** (BE 2026-27).
[4:12] TABLE head: Policy | Authority | Tools | Speed
  [4:12.0] Fiscal | Govt (MoF) | Taxation, expenditure, public debt, subsidies | Slow (budget cycle)
  [4:12.1] Monetary | RBI / MPC | Repo, CRR, SLR, OMOs, LAF | Fast (MPC bi-monthly)
[4:13] - Monetary policy is **complementary** to fiscal policy (UPPSC RO 2016).
[4:14] - **IS-LM classical range** (vertical LM): **fiscal policy is ineffective** (full crowding out) even if investment is interest-elastic; monetary policy is effective.

## SUB 5 | Financing the Fiscal Deficit
[5:0] - Sources: **market borrowings (G-Secs)**, T-bills, **small savings/NSSF**, **State Provident Funds**, external debt, **draw-down of cash**.
[5:1] - **Domestic borrowings** fill most of the FD (about **98-99%**) (UPPSC 2005); 2026-27 BE: net market borrowings **₹11.73 lakh crore** of ₹16.96 lakh crore, external debt only ₹15,385 crore.
[5:2] - **Ad hoc T-bills**: automatic monetisation from the mid-1950s; **1994** RBI-Government agreement; abolished **31 March 1997**; **WMA** from 1 April 1997.
[5:3] - From **2006-07** RBI barred from subscribing to **primary issues** of G-Secs, except under escape clause; WMA beyond the limit = **overdraft**.
[5:4] - **8th Plan** public outlay ₹4,34,100 crore – largest source **government borrowings (46.6%)**.
[5:5] - UPSC 2002 (all four true): **external liabilities in the Union Budget are shown at historical (book-value) exchange rates**; continued high borrowing kept **real interest rates high**; rising FD/GDP **crowds out private investment**; **interest payments = single largest item of non-plan revenue expenditure**.
[5:6] - Since the 2018 FRBM debt definition, the debt statement also values external debt at **current** exchange rates.
[5:7] - **PSU dividends** are **non-tax revenue**, NOT a source of financing the fiscal deficit.
[5:8] - Best way to finance a **current account deficit** = **FDI**; twin deficit = FD + **CAD** (dissaving cuts national savings).

## SUB 6 | Fiscal Stance, Recession and Crowding Out
[6:0] TABLE head: Stance | Action | Use / risk
  [6:0.0] Expansionary | More spending / lower taxes | Recession; inflation risk
  [6:0.1] Contractionary | Less spending / higher taxes | Overheating
  [6:0.2] Neutral | Balanced effect | Stable times
[6:1] - **Recession**: **increase expenditure on public projects** (UPSC 2021); **fiscal stimulus = cutting tax rates + more government spending** – abolishing subsidies is NOT (UPSC 2010 ×2).
[6:2] - **Counter-cyclical**: deficits in slumps (**Atma Nirbhar Bharat, 2020**), consolidation in booms.
[6:3] - **Double-dip recession:** recession → short-lived recovery → **another recession**; an ordinary recession = two consecutive quarters of falling GDP (APPSC G2 Mains 2017).
[6:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [6:4.0] Crowding out | Government borrowing raises interest rates, reducing private investment (UPSC 2026)
  [6:4.1] Crowding in | Public capex draws private investment
  [6:4.2] Fiscal multiplier | Change in GDP ÷ change in govt spending or taxes
  [6:4.3] Capex multiplier | 2.5-3 in India – largest for capital expenditure
[6:5] - **Quality of deficit**: high **RD/FD** ratio = borrowing funds consumption; 2026-27 RD 1.5 / FD 4.3 ≈ **one-third**.

## SUB 7 | Reducing the Deficit; Subsidies
[7:0] - Measures: **downsizing bureaucracy** (cuts spending) and **selling/offloading PSU shares** (disinvestment, raises receipts) – **not** FDI inflows or privatising higher education (UPSC 2010 ×3: 3 and 4 only).
[7:1] - **Reducing revenue expenditure** and **rationalising subsidies** cut the deficit; new welfare schemes add to it (UPSC 2015: I and III).
[7:2] - Spending side: **rationalising subsidies through DBT**, cutting unproductive spending; receipts side: widening tax base, disinvestment.
[7:3] - **Financial subsidy** is a big source of budget deficit (true); "subsidy is higher in Indian agriculture than in developed countries" – **false** (UPPSC 2002).
[7:4] - Major heads: **food, fertiliser, petroleum (LPG)**; total subsidies ≈ **8.5%** of Union expenditure (2026-27). **Urea** = largest fertiliser subsidy item.
[7:5] - **Fertiliser DBT (2018)**: paid to companies after retail sale on **PoS machines** with **Aadhaar** authentication.
[7:6] - **Hidden subsidies**: **tax concessions, cheap credit**, under-priced health and education. Forex reserves are not a subsidy head.
[7:7] - Q1 FY27 major subsidies up **37.4%** on crude prices.
[7:8] - Development needs a **multi-dimensional approach** (true); the Government focuses on both micro and macro issues (UPPSC 2015: A true, R false).
