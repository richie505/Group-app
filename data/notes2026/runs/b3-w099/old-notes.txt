## SUB 1 | Article 280 – Mandate, Composition and Nature
[99:1:0] - **Article 280:** the **President** constitutes a Finance Commission **every fifth year or earlier**; **Chairman + 4 members**; members are eligible for re-appointment.
[99:1:1] - **Qualifications:** fixed by **Parliament** – **Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951**: Chairman = experience in **public affairs**; members from: a High Court judge (or one qualified), finance and accounts of government, financial matters and administration, economics.
[99:1:2] TABLE head: Clause | Duty (recommendation to the President)
  [99:1:2.0] 280(3)(a) | Distribution of net proceeds of taxes between the Union and the States (vertical) and among the States (horizontal)
  [99:1:2.1] 280(3)(b) | Principles of grants-in-aid to States out of the Consolidated Fund of India (Art. 275)
  [99:1:2.2] 280(3)(bb), (c) | Measures to augment a State's Consolidated Fund for panchayats and municipalities, on State Finance Commission advice – added 1992 (73rd/74th Amendments)
  [99:1:2.3] 280(3)(d) | Any other matter referred by the President in the interests of sound finance
[99:1:3] - **Art. 281:** the President lays the report and an **explanatory memorandum** on action taken before **each House**.
[99:1:4] - **Nature:** **quasi-judicial**; recommendations **advisory**, not binding (Rajamannar: should not be turned down without compelling reasons); **"balancing wheel of fiscal federalism"**.
[99:1:5] - **Not permanent:** it is constituted afresh each time and its **tenure is fixed by the President's order** (it reports in about two years) – not a five-year or permanent body.
[99:1:6] - **Not its job:** preparing the Budget, assessing income tax, controlling expenditure, **Art. 282 discretionary grants**, GST sharing (GST Council), devolving State taxes such as trade/sales tax.
[99:1:7] > Exam angle: "primary function = distribute revenue between Centre and States"; "constituted by the President under Art. 280".
[99:1:8] - Also not an FC job: **maintaining the income-expenditure accounts** of the Union and the States.
[99:1:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity full p.430-432; Laxmikanth 8th ed. Part 02 p.163; NextGen Rocket Sheets Polity p.65-66; app notes 3:99 sub 0
## SUB 2 | Thirteenth and Fourteenth Finance Commissions
[99:2:0] TABLE head: Item | 13th FC (Kelkar, 2010-15) | 14th FC (Y.V. Reddy, 2015-20)
  [99:2:0.0] States' share | 32% (from 30.5%) | 42% (+10 points, largest-ever rise)
  [99:2:0.1] Highest-weight criterion | Fiscal capacity distance 47.5% | Income distance 50%
  [99:2:0.2] Other criteria | Population 1971 25%, area 10%, fiscal discipline 17.5% | Population 1971 17.5%, population 2011 10%, area 15%, forest cover 7.5% (first time)
[99:2:1] - **14th FC approach:** **tax devolution, not grants,** as the primary route ("compositional shift"); took a State's **entire revenue expenditure** – **ended the Plan/non-Plan distinction**; **no distinction** between general and **Special Category** States; **no sector-specific grants**; States to share more of CSS costs.
[99:2:2] - **14th FC fiscal roadmap:** Centre's fiscal deficit **3% of GDP** (2016-17 to 2019-20); States anchored at **3% of GSDP**; States **excluded from NSSF** loans from **1 April 2015**.
[99:2:3] - **14th FC local bodies:** **₹2,87,436 crore** (2015-20), of which gram panchayats **₹2,00,292 crore**; distributed **90% population (2011), 10% area**.
[99:2:4] - Government accepted the 14th FC report on **24 Feb 2015**.
[99:2:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.8-9; NextGen Polity full p.431-432; NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 03 p.1; app notes 3:99 sub 4
[99:2:6] - **Combined (Centre + States) debt target:** keyed **62% of GDP** for the 14th FC fiscal roadmap (13th FC: 68% by 2014-15) – figure not confirmed in the library.

[99:3:1] - **States' share 41%** (one point less than 42%, because **J&K became a Union Territory**).

## SUB 4 | Andhra Pradesh and the Finance Commissions
[99:4:0] - **Sec 46(1), AP Reorganisation Act, 2014:** the **13th FC** award to undivided AP is apportioned between AP and Telangana on **population ratio and other parameters**; the **14th FC** was asked to make **separate awards**.
[99:4:1] - AP relied on the **14th FC** revenue-deficit grant (about **₹22,113 crore**, 2015-20) and **15th FC** revenue-deficit grants and disaster funds; a special package was given **in lieu of Special Category Status**.
[99:4:2] - Revenue deficit 2014-15 to 2018-19: about **₹16,000 crore**; projected **₹48,000 crore** (2025-26).
[99:4:3] - **16th FC:** AP share **4.047% → 4.217%**; loss of up to ₹38,000 crore from dropped grants.

## SUB 5 | Sixteenth Finance Commission (2026-31)
[99:5:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [99:5:0.0] Constituted | 31 December 2023 (not 2024)
  [99:5:0.1] Chairman | Arvind Panagariya
  [99:5:0.2] Report submitted | 17 November 2025
  [99:5:0.3] Tabled in Parliament | 1 February 2026 (with Budget 2026-27, Art. 281)
  [99:5:0.4] In force | 1 April 2026; award 2026-27 to 2030-31
  [99:5:0.5] States' share | 41% retained (States' demand for 50% rejected)
[99:5:1] - **Horizontal formula:** income distance **42.5%**, population (2011) **17.5%**, demographic performance 10%, area **10%** (cut from 15%), forest 10%, **contribution to GDP 10% (new)**; **tax effort dropped**. It is the **first FC to use contribution to GDP**.
[99:5:2] - All **five southern States'** shares rose (AP **4.217%**).
[99:5:3] - **Grants:** total about **₹9.47 lakh crore**; **local bodies ₹7.91 lakh crore** (rural 4.35, urban 3.56); disaster corpus (SDRF + SDMF) **₹2,04,401 crore**; **no revenue-deficit, sector-specific or State-specific grants**.
[99:5:4] - **Disaster cost-share:** **90:10** for North-Eastern and Himalayan States, **75:25** for others; **heatwave and lightning** to be notified disasters.
[99:5:5] - **Fiscal path:** Centre's fiscal deficit **3.5% of GDP by 2030-31**; States **3% of GSDP**; combined debt projected to fall from 77.3% to 73.1% of GDP; off-budget borrowing to stop.
[99:5:6] - Budget 2026-27 gave States **₹1.4 lakh crore** as FC grants for 2026-27.
[99:5:7] - **16th FC terms of reference** (notified **31 Dec 2023**): the Art. 280(3) items – tax sharing, principles of grants-in-aid, augmenting States' funds for local bodies – plus clause (iv): it **may review the present arrangements on financing disaster management** (DM Act 2005 funds).

## SUB 0 | Vertical Devolution – States' Share
[100:0:0] - **Vertical devolution** = sharing of **net tax proceeds between the Union and the States**; **horizontal devolution** = allocation of the States' share **among the States**.

  [100:0:1.6] 16th | 41% retained
[100:0:2] - **80th Amendment (2000)**, effective **1 April 1996**: gave effect to the **10th FC** scheme; made **all central taxes shareable**.
[100:0:3] - **14th FC basis for the vertical share:** the spirit of the constitutional provisions, fiscal-space concerns of the Centre and States, and clarity on functional and expenditure responsibilities – **not** differing aspirations of State populations.

[100:3:0] - **Economic Survey 2016-17:** no **positive** relationship between **redistributive resource transfers** and per capita consumption, GSDP growth or governance; **suggestive negative** relationship, notably with **own tax effort** → link transfers to fiscal and governance effort.

## SUB 4 | Divisible Pool and Cesses
[100:4:0] - Pool = Art. 270 taxes (corporation tax, income tax, CGST, the Centre's IGST share) **minus cesses, surcharges and cost of collection**.
[100:4:1] - Pool as share of gross tax: **89.2% (FC-13) → 82.1% (FC-14) → 78.3% (FC-15)**; about **81%** in 2025-26 (89.1% in 2014-15, 74-80% in 2020-24).
[100:4:2] - **"41% illusion":** cesses and surcharges are outside the pool, so the stated share overstates the States' real share of gross tax.
[100:4:3] - **16th FC:** annual **CAG-certified** disclosure of net tax proceeds.

[101:1:0] - **Property (house) tax** = major own revenue of panchayats; they levy only **State-authorised** taxes: **local fairs**, house tax, fees, tolls.

=== NOTES 3:102 | Public Expenditure – Revenue and Capital, Developmental and Non-developmental, Trends; Subsidies
## SUB 0 | Largest Items and Trends of Union Expenditure
[102:0:0] - **Largest item of Union revenue expenditure = interest payments** – larger than defence, subsidies, pensions or grants to States; it was also the **largest non-plan item** (1991-92 onwards). BE 2026-27: **₹14,03,972 crore** = about **26% of total spending** and **40% of revenue receipts** (37% in 2013-14; 42% in 2020-21).
[102:0:1] - **Revenue expenditure** is the largest block of the Budget (bigger than plan, non-plan or capital expenditure): **88% of total (2014-15) → about 77%** (2026-27).
[102:0:2] - **Centre + States** spending ≈ **28% of GDP** (Economic Survey 2016-17).
[102:0:3] - Total Union spending BE 2026-27 **₹53,47,315 crore = 13.6% of GDP** (+7.7%); revenue expenditure ₹41.25 lakh crore (10.5% of GDP).
[102:0:4] - **Committed expenditure** (salaries, pensions, interest) = **65.3%** of revenue receipts.
[102:0:5] - **Why spending grows:** urbanisation, interest payments, subsidies and defence – all of them.
[102:0:6] TABLE head: Rupee goes to | 2023-24 BE | 2026-27 BE
  [102:0:6.0] States' share of taxes | 18% | 22%
  [102:0:6.1] Interest | 20% | 20%
  [102:0:6.2] Central Sector Schemes | 17% | 17%
  [102:0:6.3] Defence | 8% | 11%
  [102:0:6.4] Major subsidies | 7% (not 9%) | 6%
[102:0:7] - Then CSS 8%, FC transfers 7%, pensions 2% (2026-27).
[102:0:8] > Exam angle: "largest item of (revenue / non-plan / total) expenditure" = **interest payments** – asked by UPPCS, CGPSC and APPSC almost every year.
[102:0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.10, p.58; Union Budget 2026-27 at a Glance; app notes 3:102 sub 4
## SUB 1 | Revenue vs Capital Expenditure
[102:1:0] TABLE head: Revenue (no asset) | Capital (asset / liability change)
  [102:1:0.0] Salaries, pensions, interest | Roads, airports, irrigation, buildings
  [102:1:0.1] Subsidies (food, fertiliser) | Defence equipment (capital outlay)
  [102:1:0.2] Grants to States (even if assets are made) | Loans and advances to States, PSUs
  [102:1:0.3] Maintenance of existing assets | Repayment of loans
[102:1:1] - **Test:** changes the government's **assets or liabilities** = capital; the rest = revenue. Capital expenditure buys, creates, upgrades or **extends the life** of assets.
[102:1:2] - **Revenue deficit** = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts.
[102:1:3] - **Golden Rule:** borrow only for **asset creation** (capital expenditure).
[102:1:4] - Firms too: **acquiring new technology = capital expenditure**; debt vs equity is a way of **financing**, not a type of expenditure.
[102:1:5] - **Plan expenditure** had **both revenue and capital** parts.
[102:1:6] - **Budget terms:** the **per capita method** of estimation fixes a minimum amount per head; **budget control** keeps spending within the sanctioned budget and the laid-down rules.
[102:1:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.10; NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.8; app notes 3:102 sub 2
## SUB 2 | Plan vs Non-Plan Expenditure
[102:2:0] - Plan/non-plan split **ended with Budget 2017-18** (end of 12th Plan), announced in the Budget Speech 2016-17; replaced by **revenue vs capital**. **Andhra Pradesh** also dropped it from its **2017-18** Budget.
[102:2:1] - **C. Rangarajan High Level Expert Committee on Efficient Management of Public Expenditure (Planning Commission, 2011):** the split was **"dysfunctional"**, an obstacle to **outcome-based budgeting** → remove it.
[102:2:2] - **Non-plan items:** **interest payments (largest)**, defence, **subsidies** (food, fertiliser), salaries, pensions, loans to States/UTs/PSUs.
[102:2:3] - Plan = asset-creating, productive; non-plan = consumptive, committed. **Maintenance of completed Plan assets** was shifted to the **non-plan (non-developmental)** side.
[102:2:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.10, p.40; NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 02 p.11; app notes 3:102 sub 0
## SUB 3 | Subsidies
[102:3:0] - **2026-27:** **₹4,54,773 crore**; **food ₹2,27,629 crore (largest) + fertiliser ₹1,70,799 crore = 87%**.
[102:3:1] - Major subsidies (food, fertiliser, petroleum) **₹4.11 lakh crore = 1.0% of GDP**; Q1 2026-27 up 37.4% (crude, West Asia).
[102:3:2] - **2002-03:** Central subsidies about **1.6% of GDP** (never above 2% since 1991).
[102:3:3] TABLE head: Explicit | Hidden (implicit)
  [102:3:3.0] Budget line: food, fertiliser, LPG, export incentives, interest subvention | Under-pricing of public services: higher education in public universities, health, irrigation water
[102:3:4] - **Oil Pool Account** under the **Administered Pricing Mechanism** closed **1 April 2002** (FM **Yashwant Sinha**); under-recoveries later paid through **oil bonds**.
[102:3:5] - **NBS:** from **1 April 2010**; fixed subsidy per kg of **N, P, K, S**; **22 grades** of decontrolled P&K; **urea outside NBS**.
[102:3:6] - **16th FC:** well-targeted subsidies, **exclusion criteria, sunset clauses**, no off-budget financing.
[102:3:7] - **Rationalising subsidies** and cutting revenue expenditure reduce the deficit.
[102:3:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.10; RBI Annual Report (web search); app notes 3:102 sub 6
[102:3:9] - **2002-03:** total subsidies (Centre + States, explicit and implicit) ≈ **7% of GDP** (APPSC key), though Central budget subsidies alone were only ~1.6%.

## SUB 6 | State and Andhra Pradesh Expenditure
[102:6:0] - **RBI "State Finances: A Study of Budgets" (2018):** States' capital outlay was highest on **rural development**, then water supply and sanitation, urban development, education.

=== NOTES 3:103 | Public Debt - Internal and External, Debt-gdp Ratio, Sustainability, State Debt and Off-budget Borrowing
## SUB 0 | External Debt
[103:0:0] - **Size:** end-Dec 2025 **US$ 765.5 bn = 20.4% of GDP**. History: US$ 98.2 bn (Mar 2000) → 100.2 bn (Mar 2001, rise from **commercial borrowings and NRI deposits**); about **US$ 300 bn** around end-2010 (297.5 bn Dec 2010, 305.9 bn Mar 2011); ratio ~**21%** of GDP (Mar 2002), **23.7%** (Mar 2016).
[103:0:1] - **Who owes it:** mostly **non-sovereign** (companies, banks); government external debt ~2.6% of GDP.
[103:0:2] - **Largest component:** **commercial borrowings** (about 38% till 2017; still largest end-Dec 2025). **ADB** = largest multilateral lender (32.8%).
[103:0:3] - **Currency (Dec 2025):** **US dollar 54.8%**, rupee 30.1%, yen 6.3% – not all in dollars.
[103:0:4] - **ECBs** are regulated by the **RBI** (FEMA).
[103:0:5] - **World Bank:** India a **less indebted** country. **GDF 2010** most indebted: **Russia, China, Turkey, Brazil, India**; around 2000 **Brazil** was the top developing-country debtor; India ranked about **10th** of the top fifteen (GDF, ES 2001-02).
[103:0:6] - **Grants 2005-06:** the largest source was the **United Kingdom** (DFID).
[103:0:7] - "India's External Debt: A Status Report" – **DEA, Ministry of Finance**; RBI compiles quarterly data.
[103:0:8] > Exam angle: UPSC 2019 – "most debt is governmental" and "all in dollars" are both false.
[103:0:9] ~~ Sources: MoF external debt release 31 Mar 2011 (web search); app notes 3:103 sub 2
## SUB 1 | Components of Public Debt and Government Funds
[103:1:0] - **Art. 292:** the Union borrows on the security of the **Consolidated Fund of India**. Internal debt = **96.7%** of public debt; **G-Secs** over 90% of Central debt. Internal vs external is by **source** of borrowing.
[103:1:1] TABLE head: Head | Items
  [103:1:1.0] Internal – marketable | Market loans (dated G-Secs), Treasury Bills (91/182/364-day), Cash Management Bills
  [103:1:1.1] Internal – non-marketable | Special securities to NSSF and RBI, 14-day intermediate T-Bills, securities to international institutions
  [103:1:1.2] Other liabilities (Public Account) | Small savings (NSC, post-office deposits), provident funds, reserve funds
  [103:1:1.3] External | Loans from foreign governments and multilaterals
[103:1:2] - **Consolidated Fund (Art. 266)** = main account: **all revenues, loans raised and loan repayments**. **Public Account** = money held **in trust** (State Provident Fund credited here). **Contingency Fund** = urgent needs.
[103:1:3] - **Provident fund = contractual savings.** **Life insurance policies** are insurers' liabilities – **not** national (government) debt; NSCs and PF are.
[103:1:4] - **T-Bills** = short-term debt instruments; dated securities at market-related rates in auctions = the largest part of internal debt.
[103:1:5] - **Non-financial(-sector) debt** covers households (**housing loans, credit-card dues**), non-financial firms and government (**Treasury Bills**).
[103:1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.22; app notes 3:103 sub 0
## SUB 2 | Market Borrowings and Savings
[103:2:0] - **Market borrowings = largest source of capital receipts**; borrowings gave the maximum resources for plan expenditure.
[103:2:1] - 2026-27: gross **₹17.20 lakh crore**, net ₹11.73 lakh crore.
[103:2:2] - Household savings reach government via bank deposits (**SLR** holdings), small savings/NSSF, insurance and PF.
[103:2:3] - **Per capita (Union) debt:** ₹53,796 at end-March 2016 (₹49,270 a year earlier).
[103:2:4] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:103 sub 1
[103:2:5] - **Rakesh Mohan Committee** (administered interest rates and small savings, 2004): recommended **discontinuing KVP and NSC (VIII issue)** and market-linked small-savings rates.

[103:3:0] TABLE head: Measure | Value
  [103:3:0.0] Centre BE 2026-27 | 55.6% (peak about 61%, 2020-21)

  [103:3:0.2] FRBM (2018) / N.K. Singh Review Committee (2017) | General government 60% = Centre 40% + States 20% (Centre 40% by 2024-25)

## SUB 4 | State Debt
[103:4:0] - **Art. 293:** States borrow **only within India** on the security of their Consolidated Fund, via **SDLs**; a State **owing the Centre** needs its **consent** to borrow; the Centre may lend to States (Art. 293(2)). External loans to States are **routed through the Centre** since **2005-06**.
[103:4:1] - **Net borrowing ceiling:** **4% of GSDP in 2021-22** (+0.5% for power reforms); **16th FC: 3% of GSDP** (2026-31). Key ratios: debt-GSDP, interest/revenue receipts.

  [103:4:2.1] West Bengal | 38.3
  [103:4:2.2] Rajasthan | 37.9
  [103:4:2.3] Andhra Pradesh | 34.6
[103:4:3] - **AP** 2025-26 RE: **₹6,35,177 crore = 36.04% of GSDP**; **market borrowing** (₹4,83,093 crore) largest; crosses ₹7 lakh crore (BE 2026-27).
[103:4:4] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:103 sub 5
## SUB 5 | Off-budget Borrowing and Debt Management
[103:5:0] - Off-budget: PSU/SPV loans **serviced from the budget but kept outside the deficit** → hides true debt (makes it look lower), hurts ratings.
[103:5:1] - **FCI borrowing from NSSF** for food subsidy – ended in **Budget 2021-22**. FRBM: new Central guarantees ≤ **0.5% of GDP** a year.
[103:5:2] - **AP** off-budget **₹1.28 lakh crore** (9.7% of GSDP, March 2023). **16th FC:** stop it and bring existing debt **on-budget**.
[103:5:3] - **RBI** manages government debt (RBI Act 1934). **PDMC** (2016) is in the **DEA**, not the RBI. A statutory **PDMA** was proposed (2015-16) but **not created**.
[103:5:4] ~~ Sources: PMF LENS Feb 2026 Part 1 p.11; app notes 3:103 sub 6
## SUB 6 | Debt Sustainability and Debt Trap
[103:6:0] - **Debt trap:** a country has to **borrow to pay interest** on its outstanding loans.
[103:6:1] - **Zero primary deficit** = fresh borrowing only for interest payments.
[103:6:2] - **r − g:** if g > r the debt-GDP ratio falls; if **r > g a primary surplus** is needed.
[103:6:3] - Interest ≈ **40% of revenue receipts** → high interest **squeezes other spending**; a **rising debt-GDP ratio hampers** good fiscal administration.
[103:6:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.54; app notes 3:103 sub 3

=== NOTES 3:104 | Deficit Financing - Meaning, Methods and Consequences
## SUB 0 | Meaning and Purpose of Deficit Financing
[104:0:0] # Meaning
[104:0:1] TABLE head: Usage | Meaning
  [104:0:1.0] Indian | Budget gap met by borrowing from the RBI; RBI creates new money – the government "buys money" from the RBI
  [104:0:1.1] Western | Any financing of a budget deficit, mainly borrowing from the public/market
  [104:0:1.2] Effect | Increase in money supply; creates extra paper currency
[104:0:2] - **Deficit financing** = the act of financing a **deficit budget** – **spending in excess of revenue** (total expenditure > total receipts) and sustaining that gap (APPSC G1 1999).
[104:0:3] - In India, deficit financing = borrowing from the **RBI** (money creation), not from the public (APPSC AD Economics).
[104:0:4] - **Fiscal deficit** (Union Budget) = budgetary deficit + net increase in internal and external borrowings and other liabilities.
[104:0:5] - An economic unit that **spends more than it receives** in a period is a **deficit unit** (APPSC Degree Lecturers 2017).
[104:0:6] # Purpose in Indian Planning
[104:0:7] - Purpose: **raising resources for economic development** – not redemption of public debt, BoP adjustment or cutting foreign debt (UPSC 2013 ×2); a "pragmatic tool" to finance the **Five Year Plans**.
[104:0:8] - **Small doses** of deficit financing for productive investment need not be inflationary, and it is an **often-used tool** for financing budget deficits (CDS 2011: both true).
[104:0:9] - Needed after 1947 because of a **low tax base**, poor compliance, weak private banking and **low domestic savings** (not a high savings rate).
[104:0:10] - NextGen objectives: remedy for depression, raise aggregate demand, subsidies, anti-poverty programmes, PSU losses.
[104:0:11] - Independence to **late 1960s**: little use, to fund plans. **Second Plan**: about **₹1,100 crore** against ₹1,200 crore planned. **Third Plan**: reliance to be **strictly limited**.
[104:0:12] - **1970s onwards** (NextGen: "period of deficit financing 1970-1991"): heavy reliance (oil shocks, welfare spending) led to debt and inflation.
[104:0:13] > Exam angle: "In India deficit financing is used for" = **economic development**.
[104:0:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.1-2 (deficit financing, monetised deficit, period 1970-1991, objectives, effects); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.18.10 (definition, India from 1969/1970s); app notes 3:104 sub 0, 1
[104:0:15] - NextGen: **deficit financing** = the largest source of financing public-sector Plan outlay (Eighth Plan) – ahead of external borrowing.
## SUB 1 | Methods and Their Inflationary Effect
[104:1:0] # Methods (NextGen)
[104:1:1] - Running down **cash balances** with the RBI; **issue of new currency**; **borrowing from the RBI** (RBI prints notes); also internal and external borrowing, external aid and grants.
[104:1:2] - Ramesh Singh's order of preference: external aid/grants → external borrowing → internal borrowing → **printing currency (last resort, most inflationary)**.
[104:1:3] - **Monetised deficit**: borrowing from the RBI through fresh currency (**high-powered money**); the FRBM Act disallows it in normal times.
[104:1:4] # Inflationary Ranking
[104:1:5] TABLE head: Mode | Effect
  [104:1:5.0] Creation of new money | Most inflationary (UPSC 2013, 2021; State PSC)
  [104:1:5.1] Borrowing from banks | High
  [104:1:5.2] Borrowing from public | Lower – uses existing savings
  [104:1:5.3] Repayment of public debt | Not inflationary – withdraws money
[104:1:6] - Deficit financing is **inflationary in character** (APPSC G1); it **increases the money supply** (UPPSC 2016); if it fails to raise output it **generates inflation**, not deflation (UPPSC 1993).
[104:1:7] # Control and Phase-out
[104:1:8] TABLE head: Year | Step
  [104:1:8.0] Mid-1950s | Ad hoc Treasury Bills – automatic RBI financing of the government begins
  [104:1:8.1] Sep 1994 | RBI-Government agreement begins phasing out ad hoc T-bills (₹6,000 crore cap for 1994-95)
  [104:1:8.2] 31 Mar 1997 | Ad hoc T-bills abolished; Ways and Means Advances (WMA) from 1 April 1997
  [104:1:8.3] 2003 | FRBM Act
  [104:1:8.4] 1 Apr 2006 | RBI barred from subscribing to primary issues of G-Secs
  [104:1:8.5] 2018 | Monetised borrowing only on escape-clause grounds; deviation cap 0.5% of GDP a year
[104:1:9] - Market borrowing; liquidity is managed by **OMOs**: **sale** of G-Secs **drains** liquidity, **purchase adds** it.
[104:1:10] - **15 Sep 2026**: RBI announced **OMO sales of ₹1 lakh crore** with 91/182/364-day T-bill auctions.
[104:1:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.1-2 (methods, monetised deficit); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.18.11-18.12 (means of deficit financing, printing currency), p.18.18-18.20 (WMA, RBI primary bar 2006-07, 1994 agreement ₹6,000 crore); app notes 3:104 sub 3, 4
## SUB 2 | Consequences – Inflation, Debt Trap, Crowding Out
[104:2:0] TABLE head: Effect | Mechanism
  [104:2:0.0] Demand-pull inflation | Money supply and demand rise without more goods and services; hurts the poor most
  [104:2:0.1] Crowding out | Market borrowing raises interest rates, cuts private investment
  [104:2:0.2] Debt trap | Government borrows to make interest payments (APPSC G2); little room for health, education
  [104:2:0.3] Others (NextGen) | Adverse effect on savings and investment, rising inequality, BoP deficit, higher cost of production
  [104:2:0.4] Rating downgrades | Higher borrowing cost for government and firms
[104:2:1] # Public Debt Heads
[104:2:2] TABLE head: Head | Items
  [104:2:2.0] Internal debt | Market loans, Treasury Bills, special securities to NSSF
  [104:2:2.1] Other liabilities (Public Account) | Small savings, State Provident Funds, reserve funds and deposits
  [104:2:2.2] External debt | Loans from foreign sources
[104:2:3] # Current Data
[104:2:4] - Interest = **20 paise** of every rupee spent (Budget 2026-27); about **40% of revenue receipts**.
[104:2:5] - States borrowed **₹5.23 trillion** till Oct 2025 (**62%** of FY26 plan); RBI wants a shift from **subsidies to capital expenditure**.
[104:2:6] - **AP** fiscal deficit 2024-25: **5.05%** of GSDP vs 4% limit; **market loans ≈ 75% of AP's debt** – after bifurcation AP met its heavy deficit mainly through **market loans** (APPSC AD Economics).
[104:2:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.2 (effects); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.18.12 (printing currency, wage-price spiral); app notes 3:104 sub 2, 5 (2025-26 data)

## SUB 1 | Deficit Definitions and Formulas
[105:1:0] TABLE head: Deficit | Formula | Shows
  [105:1:0.0] Revenue (RD) | Revenue expenditure − revenue receipts | Borrowing for day-to-day consumption (dissaving)
  [105:1:0.1] Effective RD (ERD) | RD − grants for creation of capital assets | True consumption deficit (C. Rangarajan concept)
  [105:1:0.2] Fiscal (FD / GFD) | Total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts, i.e. recovery of loans + disinvestment) | Total borrowing requirement
  [105:1:0.3] Primary (PD) | FD − interest payments | Current imbalance, excluding past debt
  [105:1:0.4] Budget deficit (old) | Total expenditure − total receipts (incl. borrowings) | Ad hoc T-bill era measure
  [105:1:0.5] Monetised | Part of FD financed by the RBI (high-powered money) | Inflationary
  [105:1:0.6] Net FD | GFD − net lending of the Centre | e.g. 1,200 − 400 = ₹800 crore
[105:1:1] - **FD** = total expenditure minus total receipts **excluding borrowings** = **budget deficit + borrowings and other liabilities** (market borrowings) (IAS 1994; APPSC Lecturers 2023; State PSC).
[105:1:2] - FD "closest to" the **interest-bearing borrowings during a year** (APPSC G2 2017) – a flow, not accumulated debt.
[105:1:3] - Loose exam wording: "excess of total expenditure over revenue receipts" = **fiscal deficit** (APPSC G2 2011) – strictly non-debt capital receipts are also deducted.
[105:1:4] - **GFD (financing side) = net borrowing at home + borrowing from RBI + borrowing from abroad** (RAS 2023). Net borrowing at home includes small savings and, indirectly, bank SLR holdings.
[105:1:5] - **FD = RD + capital expenditure − non-debt capital receipts**; RD is a part of FD.
[105:1:6] - **PD = FD − interest** (= **gross primary deficit** when taken from GFD) (APPSC G2 2017, UPPSC ×4); **PD + interest = FD** (UPPSC 2010). It is NOT revenue deficit minus interest.
[105:1:7] - **ERD** introduced in **Union Budget 2011-12**; added to FRBM by the **2012 amendment**; dropped as a target in 2018. ERD shows capital receipts used for consumption.
[105:1:8] - RD, FD, PD were adopted as official terms from **1997-98** (Ramesh Singh); budget deficit stopped being the headline measure after ad hoc T-bills ended (1997).
[105:1:9] - **Headline marker:** the **fiscal deficit** is the headline marker of fiscal health; RD and ERD show what the borrowing is used for.
[105:1:10] # Worked Examples
[105:1:11] - FD ₹1,50,000 crore − interest ₹1,20,000 crore = **PD ₹30,000 crore**.
[105:1:12] - Gross primary deficit: ₹50,000 − ₹1,500 = **₹48,500 crore** (non-debt capital receipts not deducted again).
[105:1:13] - FD ₹4 crore on GDP ₹400 crore = **1% of GDP** (NextGen).
[105:1:14] > Trap: "Primary deficit = revenue deficit − interest" is wrong; PD = **fiscal** deficit − interest.
