=== Education Policy and School Schemes
# Policies
- **NPE 1986** launched **Operation Blackboard (1987)** – at least **two rooms and two teachers**, teaching aids, for primary schools; also stressed ST education. **Ramamurti Committee (1990)** reviewed NPE; NPE **revised 1992** (Programme of Action) gave more weight to tribal education.
- **NEP 2020:** replaced NPE 1986 after **34 years**; **5+3+3+4** – Foundational **3-8**, Preparatory 8-11, Middle 11-14, Secondary 14-18.
- NEP targets: **100% school GER by 2030**; **50% higher-education GER by 2035**; **100% youth and adult literacy by 2030**.
- **NIPUN Bharat:** foundational literacy by **Grade 3**; deadline moved to **2026-27**.
# Schemes
| Scheme | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Samagra Shiksha** (2018) | Subsumes **SSA, RMSA, Teacher Education** |
| **Unnat Bharat Abhiyan** | **Higher education** – institutions adopt villages |
| SEMIS | Secondary Education MIS – nodal agency **NUEPA, New Delhi** |
| Mana Badi Nadu-Nedu (AP) | 14 Nov 2019; **English labs** (not Operation Blackboard) |
# Learning and enrolment data
- **FLN Index** (Institute for Competitiveness for **EAC-PM**): 2nd edition (24 Feb 2023) – **Punjab** highest (64.19) among **small States**; ⚠ Trap: **Jharkhand 11th = lowest small State (45.28)** was the **1st edition (16 Dec 2021)**, not the 2nd. 2021 edition – **Kerala** top small State (67.95), **West Bengal** top large State (58.95), Bihar lowest large State (36.81).
- **AISHE 2023-24:** GER about **30**, female GER **31.2**, GPI **1.08**; female GER 28.5 → **30.2** (2021-23), above male; gender parity at all school levels.
- **UDISE+ 2024-25** dropout: preparatory **2.3%**, middle **3.5%**, secondary **8.2%** (from 3.7, 5.2, **10.9**).
- NFHS-5: women with **10+ years** of schooling **41%** (NFHS-4 35.7%).

=== Adult Literacy Programmes and Literacy Days
| Programme | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Farmers' Functional Literacy Programme | 1967-68 | Early adult education |
| NAEP | **2 Oct 1978** | Age **15-35** |
| **National Literacy Mission** | **5 May 1988** | **Total Literacy Campaign** = its main mass-campaign strategy |
| Total Literacy Campaign | 1989-90 | **Ernakulam** – first totally literate district (1990); **Kottayam** – first fully literate town (1989) |
| **Saakshar Bharat** | **8 Sep 2009** (International Literacy Day) | Adults 15+, focus on **women**; Centre : State **75 : 25** (NE 90 : 10); planned up to **March 2012**, extended to **31 March 2018**, then wound up |
| **ULLAS** (Nav Bharat Saaksharta Karyakram / New India Literacy Programme) | 2022 | FY **2022-23 to 2026-27**; Ministry of Education; non-literates **15+** |
- ULLAS = **Understanding of Lifelong Learning for All in Society**; five components – FLN, critical life skills, basic education, vocational skills, continuing education.
- Fully literate (ULLAS): **Ladakh** (first UT, 2024) → **Mizoram** (first State, 2025) → Goa, Tripura, Himachal Pradesh, Sikkim → **Uttarakhand 6th (Jul 2026)**.
- **Velland (Kerala)** – the exam spelling of **Vellanad** (Thiruvananthapuram; also the first grama panchayat in India with a full GIS) – is the exam answer for India's first fully **computer-literate panchayat**; Chamravattom (Malappuram) was declared the first 100% computer-literate village (2003).
- **International Literacy Day: 8 September** (UNESCO, declared 1966, first observed 1967). **2017 theme: "Literacy in a digital world"**; 2026: "Literacy for people, the planet and prosperity".
- **Information literacy:** the skill of defining, locating, accessing and using information.
> NAEP = 15-35; Saakshar Bharat and ULLAS = 15+.

=== Census 2011 – India, Gender Gap and States
# Definition
- **Literate** = aged **7 and above**, can **both read and write with understanding** in any language; reading only = **not literate**.
- Censuses 1951-1971 used age **5+**; **7+ from 1981**. PLFS uses the same 7+ definition but a **sample** – not directly comparable.
- Full literacy (ULLAS) = **95%+** among persons aged **15+**. **Census 2027** adds a **digital literacy** question.
# National figures
| Census | 1951 | 1961 | 1971 | 1981 | 1991 | 2001 | 2011 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Literacy % | **18.33** | 28.30 | 34.45 | 43.57 | 52.21 | 64.83 | **74.04** |
# Census 2011 – provisional vs final
| India 2011 | Total | Male | Female |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Provisional** | **74.04%** | 82.14% | 65.46% |
| Final | **73.0% (72.98%)** | 80.9% (80.88%) | **64.6% (64.63%)** |
- **Largest decadal gain: 1990s**. Males about 27% → 82% in 60 years; women from less than 1 in 10 (1951) to 2 in 3.
- **Gender gap 2011:** about **16-17 points** (82.14 − 65.46 = 16.68 provisional); widest in **Rajasthan (about 28 points)**; under **5 points** in **Meghalaya** (about 76 vs 73).
- Rural / urban (provisional): **68.9% / 85.0%**; largest deficit: **rural women**. Causes of the gap: distance and safety, early marriage, dowry, son preference, poverty.
- SC literacy **66.1%**, ST **59.0%**; low literacy is one of the **four PVTG criteria**.
