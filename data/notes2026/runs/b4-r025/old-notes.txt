=== NOTES 4:25 | Ocean-atmosphere Interaction – El Nino, La Nina, Enso, Iod, Walker Circulation
## SUB 0 | El Nino – Meaning and Mechanism
[25:0:0] - **El Nino** = abnormal warming of the **central and eastern equatorial Pacific**; the oceanic part of ENSO.
[25:0:1] - A warm current flows south along **Peru**, replacing the cold **Humboldt (Peruvian)** current; cold nutrient-rich **upwelling stops** → fewer phytoplankton, fish catch collapses.
[25:0:2] - Name: **"little boy / Christ child"** – appears around **Christmas**.
[25:0:3] - Recurs every **2–7 years**; **not every year**.
[25:0:4] - **2014:** El Nino conditions linked to India's monsoon **deficit** (about 12% below normal).
[25:0:5] - **Bjerknes feedback**: easterly trades weaken/reverse → warm western Pacific water shifts east → winds weaken further → air sinks over Indian Ocean (high pressure) → **late or weak Indian monsoon**.
[25:0:6] El Nino and La Nina are **climate patterns, not storms**.
## SUB 1 | El Nino vs La Nina
[25:1:0] TABLE head: Feature | El Nino | La Nina
  [25:1:0.0] Central/east Pacific SST | Warmer | Cooler (strong upwelling)
  [25:1:0.1] Trade winds | Weak / reversed | Stronger than usual
  [25:1:0.2] Indian monsoon | Weak, below normal (drought sign) | Normal to above normal
  [25:1:0.3] North India winter | — | Colder, frequent cold waves (e.g. January 2012)
  [25:1:0.4] Bay of Bengal | Fewer post-monsoon (Oct–Dec) cyclones (INCOIS) | More floods, severe cyclones
  [25:1:0.5] Australia/Indonesia | Drier | Heavy rain, floods
  [25:1:0.6] Walker circulation | Weakens, shifts east | Strengthens
[25:1:1] - **6 of 10** El Nino years brought depressed Indian rainfall; last global El Nino **2023-24**.
[25:1:2] - **2025** = warmest La Nina year on record.
## SUB 2 | El Nino Types
[25:2:0] TABLE head: Type | Maximum warming
  [25:2:0.0] Canonical (normal) | Eastern Pacific, near Peru
  [25:2:0.1] El Nino Modoki ("similar but different", Japanese) | Central Pacific; cooler on both sides (tripole)
## SUB 3 | Walker Circulation
[25:3:0] - Normal state: east-west (zonal) loop over the tropical Pacific – air **rises over warm western Pacific/Indonesia**, flows east aloft, **sinks over cool eastern Pacific**, surface **easterly trades** return west.
[25:3:1] - El Nino: loop weakens and shifts east → less convection over India.
## SUB 4 | Enso and Southern Oscillation
[25:4:0] - **Southern Oscillation**: atmospheric pressure **seesaw** between the Pacific and Indian Oceans – **Tahiti vs Port Darwin**; first described by **Gilbert Walker (1920s)**.
[25:4:1] - **SOI** = Tahiti minus Darwin; sustained **negative SOI = El Nino**, positive = La Nina.
[25:4:2] - ENSO is used for short-term climate prediction.
## SUB 5 | Enso Monitoring
[25:5:0] - **Nino 3.4 region**: **5°N–5°S, 120°W–170°W**, central equatorial Pacific.
[25:5:1] - **NOAA**: El Nino when the **Oceanic Nino Index** (3-month mean SST anomaly, Nino 3.4) is **≥ +0.5°C** for at least **5 consecutive overlapping seasons**.
[25:5:2] - **IMD April 2026**: monsoon forecast **92% of LPA (LPA 87 cm)**, below normal, due to a developing El Nino.
## SUB 6 | Indian Ocean Dipole (iod)
[25:6:0] TABLE head: IOD phase | West (Arabian Sea) | East (south of Indonesia) | India
  [25:6:0.0] Positive | Warmer | Cooler | More rain; can offset El Nino
  [25:6:0.1] Negative | Cooler | Warmer | Reduced rainfall
[25:6:1] - Discovered **1999**; measured by **Dipole Mode Index (DMI)**. Late May 2026 DMI −0.34°C = neutral.
[25:6:2] - **1997**: strong positive IOD saved India from drought despite a **Super El Nino**.
[25:6:3] - IOD runs **independently of ENSO**, though positive IOD events often co-occur with El Nino.
## SUB 7 | Madden-julian Oscillation (mjo)
[25:7:0] - Identified by **Madden and Julian, 1971-72**; eastward pulse of clouds and rain from a warm pool in the tropical Indian Ocean.
[25:7:1] - Recurs every **30–60 days**; convection up to **10 km**.
[25:7:2] - Shifts heavy rain east → **active and break spells** of the monsoon.
[25:7:3] - **AP angle**: cyclone swings and weak-monsoon years hit coastal AP and rain-fed **Rayalaseema** farming.

=== NOTES 4:26 | Biological Characteristics of Oceans – Marine Ecosystems, Coral Reefs, Bleaching, Upwelling

## SUB 4 | Coral Reefs – Nature and Conditions
[26:4:0] # Nature and Composition
[26:4:1] - Built from skeletons of tiny colonial **coral polyps**; skeleton of **calcium carbonate** (aragonite). (Oyster shells too are calcium carbonate.)
[26:4:2] - **Mutualism** with **zooxanthellae** (single-celled algae): algae photosynthesise and give **food and colour**; coral gives **shelter and nutrients**.
[26:4:3] # Growth Conditions
[26:4:4] TABLE head: Condition | Requirement
  [26:4:4.0] Latitude | Tropics, about 30°N–30°S (most within 25°N–25°S)
  [26:4:4.1] Temperature | Above ~20°C; optimum 23–25°C
  [26:4:4.2] Water | Clear, shallow (< ~50 m), sunlit, normal salinity
  [26:4:4.3] Avoid | Muddy river mouths – sediment and freshwater inhibit growth
[26:4:5] - **Biorock (mineral accretion)** technology restores damaged reefs.
[26:4:6] # Epithet and Roles
[26:4:7] - **"Rainforests of the Sea"** – cover **< 1%** of the ocean floor yet support **> 25%** of marine species; host far more animal phyla than rainforests.
[26:4:8] - Break **storm waves**, prevent coastal erosion; nurseries, fisheries, tourism.
[26:4:9] APPSC 2024 (G1 Prelims P2): "rainforests of the sea" and "break wave power" are **both true**, but the second does **not explain** the first (the epithet is due to species richness).

## SUB 7 | Coral Bleaching
[26:7:0] - **Meaning**: polyps **expel zooxanthellae** and lose pigments, turning white (APPSC G2 2017); stressed, **not necessarily dead**.
[26:7:1] - **Main cause**: **rising sea-surface temperature / marine heatwaves** (global warming).

## SUB 8 | Ocean Acidification and Algal Blooms
[26:8:0] # Ocean Acidification
[26:8:1] - **"Evil twin of global warming"** / "the other CO2 problem".
[26:8:2] - CO2 + water → **carbonic acid** → pH falls (seawater ~8.1, alkaline) → **carbonate ions fall** → corals, plankton, shellfish (**calcifiers**) struggle to build shells.
[26:8:3] - Oceans absorb about **30%** of human-made CO2.
[26:8:4] - Worst in **cold temperate seas** (higher CO2 solubility) and melting Arctic; it does not necessarily affect cloud formation.
[26:8:5] # Algal Blooms and Red Tides
