## SUB 0 | Coral Reef Places – World and India
[0:0] # Great Barrier Reef (asked 9 times)
[0:1] - **World's largest coral reef system** and largest structure built by living organisms: off **Queensland, north-eastern Australia**, in the **Coral Sea (Pacific Ocean)** – not New Zealand, Finland, Greenland or Ireland.
[0:2] - Length about **2,300 km** (PMF; G.C. Leong gives 1,200 miles) – so "more than 2,500 km" is **false** (UPRO/ARO 2023: only statement 1). Over **2,900 reefs**; dugong and green turtle habitat; **UNESCO World Heritage Site 1981**.
[0:3] - APPSC Group 1 2004: became the world's biggest protected marine sanctuary (July 2004). Next largest system: **Mesoamerican Barrier Reef**.
[0:4] # World distribution
[0:5] - **Most reefs lie in tropical waters** (between the **Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn**, mostly 25°N–25°S, up to 30°); **more than one-third** are in **Australia, Indonesia and the Philippines**; reefs host **far more animal phyla** than tropical rainforests – all three statements of UPSC 2018 (asked 3 times) are **correct**.
[0:6] - Few reefs on the **west coasts** of tropical continents (cold currents, upwelling).
[0:7] # India's reefs

## SUB 1 | Coral Reefs – Nature, Conditions and Types
[1:0] # Nature
[1:1] - Built from the **calcium carbonate** skeletons of tiny colonial **coral polyps** (phylum **Cnidaria**); the CaCO3 is **aragonite**. **Oyster shells** too are **calcium carbonate**.
[1:2] - **Mutualism with zooxanthellae** (single-celled dinoflagellate algae): the algae photosynthesise and give the coral **food and colour**; the coral gives **shelter and CO2/nutrients**.
[1:3] - **"Rainforests of the Sea"** – the marine counterpart of **tropical rain forests** – because of exceptional **species richness**: under **1%** of the ocean floor, over **25%** of marine species.
[1:4] - Reefs **break storm waves** and prevent coastal erosion; also nurseries, fisheries, tourism. APPSC Group 1: both statements true, but wave-breaking does **not explain** the epithet.
[1:5] - **Biorock (mineral accretion)**: a small electric current on a steel frame precipitates CaCO3 – used to **restore damaged reefs** (ZSI trial, Gulf of Kachchh) (UPSC 2022).
[1:6] # Growth conditions
[1:7] TABLE head: Condition | Requirement
  [1:7.0] Temperature | Not below about 20°C (20–21°C minimum); optimum 23–25°C; tropical seas
  [1:7.1] Light | Photic, shallow water – under about 50 m (Leong: not over 180 ft; ideal under 100 ft)
  [1:7.2] Water | Clean, sediment-free, normal sea salinity (about 3.5%, not 6%); freshwater and very saline water harm growth
  [1:7.3] Avoid | Muddy river mouths, cold currents
[1:8] - Astt. Comm. 2017: photic + clean water + 20–21°C = **I, II and IV**; "salinity of 6%" is wrong.
[1:9] # Reef types
[1:10] TABLE head: Type | Feature | Example
  [1:10.0] Fringing | Close to shore; shallow lagoon or none; commonest in India | Andamans, Gulf of Mannar
  [1:10.1] Barrier | Parallel to the coast, separated by a wide, deep lagoon | Great Barrier Reef
  [1:10.2] Atoll | Ring / horseshoe reef enclosing a lagoon, no central land | Lakshadweep

## SUB 2 | Marine Life, Ocean Zones and Productivity
[2:0] # Ocean zones
[2:1] TABLE head: Zone | Extent / feature
  [2:1.0] Littoral / intertidal | Between high- and low-tide marks
  [2:1.1] Neritic | From the high-water line over the continental shelf to about 200 m; richest fishing zone
  [2:1.2] Photic (euphotic) | Sunlit layer (to about 200 m); photosynthesis
  [2:1.3] Epipelagic (sunlit) | Highest share of marine habitats and life
  [2:1.4] Disphotic (twilight) | Above the aphotic zone; light too weak for photosynthesis
  [2:1.5] Aphotic | Dark; no photosynthesis
  [2:1.6] Pelagic / benthic | Open water column / sea floor
  [2:1.7] Abyssal, hadal | Deep floor and trenches
[2:2] - Marine ecosystems cover about **71%** of Earth's surface; **hydrothermal vents** support **chemosynthetic** life. Rivers and freshwater lakes are **freshwater**, not marine, ecosystems.
[2:3] # Organisms and oxygen
[2:4] TABLE head: Group | Feature
  [2:4.0] Plankton | Drift with water: phytoplankton (plants) + zooplankton (animals)
  [2:4.1] Nekton | Swim actively (fish, squid, marine mammals)
  [2:4.2] Benthos | Live on or in the bottom
[2:5] - **Phytoplankton** (diatoms, dinoflagellates, cyanobacteria) = **main primary producers** of the sea (not seaweeds or marine angiosperms).
[2:6] - Marine phytoplankton and photosynthetic bacteria produce **about 50%** of the world's oxygen – **more than rainforests**; air holds **far more oxygen** than even well-oxygenated water (about 25–35 times per litre) – UPSC 2025: **2 only**.
[2:7] - If phytoplankton were destroyed: the **carbon sink** and **food chains** would suffer; water density would not change (UPSC 2012: I and II).
[2:8] # Upwelling
[2:9] - Winds push surface water **offshore** → cold, **nutrient-rich** water rises → plankton boom → rich fisheries. Upwelling raises productivity by bringing **nutrients** to the surface – not decomposers or bottom organisms (UPSC 2011: only II).
[2:10] TABLE head: Upwelling zone | Current / fishery
  [2:10.0] Peru–Chile | Humboldt – anchoveta; suppressed by El Nino → fisheries fail
  [2:10.1] Namibia | Benguela
  [2:10.2] Somalia | Findlater jet, SW monsoon
  [2:10.3] Kerala / SW India | SW monsoon – oil sardine, mackerel
[2:11] # Algal blooms and red tides
[2:12] - **Red tide** = bloom of a **pigmented dinoflagellate** that discolours estuarine or coastal water (UPSC 1998).
[2:13] - **Harmful algal blooms in Indian seas:** **nutrient discharge from estuaries**, **monsoon land run-off** and **upwelling** (also mud banks) – all three (UPSC 2011). Excess N and P → eutrophication → **dead zones** (Gulf of Mexico – **Mississippi** run-off). **INCOIS** issues algal-bloom and coral-bleaching alerts.

## SUB 3 | Coral Bleaching and Ocean Acidification
[3:0] # Coral bleaching
[3:1] - **Meaning:** stressed polyps **expel (eject) their zooxanthellae** and lose pigment, turning **white**; a bleached coral is **stressed, not necessarily dead** (it can recover if conditions improve).
[3:2] - **Most significant cause:** **global warming** – rising sea-surface temperature, **marine heatwaves** (1°C above normal for weeks can trigger it); also UV, turbidity, pollution, salinity change, **extreme low tides**, **ocean acidification**, El Nino – **not** more carbonate, not mining or disease as the main cause.
[3:3] TABLE head: Global bleaching event | Period | Note
  [3:3.0] 1st | 1998 | Strong El Nino
  [3:3.1] 2nd | 2010 | –
  [3:3.2] 3rd | 2014–2017 | 68.2% of reef areas
  [3:3.3] 4th | 2023 – about mid-2025 | About 84% of reefs, 83 countries (NOAA–ICRI)
[3:4] - Every strong El Nino since 1998 came with a global bleaching event; recovery takes **more than 15 years**. GCRMN 2026: major disturbances now every **4–6 years**; **87%** of reefs faced heat stress in 2024. **Andaman** mass bleaching linked to about a **2°C** rise in Indian Ocean temperature.
[3:5] # Ocean acidification
[3:6] - CO2 + water → **carbonic acid** → bicarbonate + hydrogen (hydronium) ions → **pH falls** (sea water about **8.1**, alkaline) → **carbonate ions fall** → corals, calcareous plankton and shellfish **form shells less easily** (it does not strengthen reefs).
[3:7] - Oceans absorb about **30–40%** of human CO2; worst in **cold seas** (higher CO2 solubility). Nicknames: **"evil twin of global warming"**, "the other CO2 problem".
[3:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 05 p.93-96 (book p.441-445); NextGen Environment - APPSC Exams 14 p.3, 7-8; Rocket General Geography p.66; app notes 4:26 sub 7, 8
