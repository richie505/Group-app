## SUB 0 | Security Council
[0:0] - **15 members**: **5 permanent (P5) – China, France, Russia, UK, USA** + **10 non-permanent**.
[0:1] - Non-permanent members: elected by the **UNGA**, two-thirds secret ballot, for **2 years**; **no immediate re-election (Art. 23)**.
[0:2] - **Veto (Art. 27)**: only the P5; a P5 negative vote on a **substantive** matter blocks a decision; **procedural matters need 9 affirmative votes and no veto applies**. The Charter never uses the word "veto" (Art. 27(3) speaks of the "concurring votes of the permanent members").
[0:3] - **Germany, Japan, India, Canada are NOT** permanent members; India has been an elected (non-permanent) member, never a permanent one.
[0:4] - Only organ whose decisions **bind** all members (Ch. VII: Art. 41 sanctions, Art. 42 military action).
[0:5] - **P5 + 1** (Iran nuclear talks, JCPOA 2015): the **+1 = Germany**.
[0:6] - Resolutions: **2328 (Dec 2016)** – UN monitors for evacuations from **Aleppo** (Syria); **2428 (July 2018)** – arms embargo on **South Sudan**.
[0:7] - **April 2024**: full UN membership for **Palestine** – **vetoed by the USA**; UK and Switzerland abstained.
[0:8] TABLE head: Resolution | Year | Subject
  [0:8.0] 1267 | 1999 | Sanctions on Al-Qaida / ISIL; terrorist listings (Masood Azhar listed 2019)
  [0:8.1] 1373 | 2001 | Counter-Terrorism Committee (after 9/11)
  [0:8.2] 1540 | 2004 | Stopping WMD reaching non-state actors
[0:9] - AP angle: India's first **UNSCR 1540** capacity-building programme for Asia-Pacific – **NACIN, Palasamudram (AP)**, Feb 2025.

## SUB 1 | Charter, Founding, Languages and UN Days
[1:0] - **Atlantic Charter (1941)** → **Declaration by United Nations (1 Jan 1942)** – name "United Nations" devised by US President **Franklin D. Roosevelt**.
[1:1] - Charter signed by **50 nations** at the **San Francisco Conference**, **26 June 1945**; **Poland** signed later (ratified 16 Oct 1945) as the **51st original member**.
[1:2] - In force **24 October 1945** = **UN founded** = **UN Day**.
[1:3] - Members: **193**.
[1:4] - **Six official languages**: **Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish**. **Working languages** (Secretariat): **English and French**. **Hindi is NOT** an official UN language.
[1:5] - **UNGA resolution on multilingualism (June 2022, India co-sponsored)**: mentioned **Hindi** for the first time (also Bangla and Urdu).
[1:6] - **Amendment** (e.g. UNSC reform): **two-thirds** of members, incl. ratification by **all P5** (Art. 108).
[1:7] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [1:7.0] Art. 1 | Purposes – peace and security, friendly relations, cooperation, harmonising action
  [1:7.1] Art. 2 | Sovereign equality
  [1:7.2] Art. 18 | UNGA "important questions" = two-thirds majority
  [1:7.3] Art. 23 | No immediate re-election of a retiring non-permanent UNSC member
  [1:7.4] Art. 27 | Voting in the UNSC; P5 veto
[1:8] - **UN Decade of Sustainable Energy for All (2014–2024)**: declared by the **UN General Assembly**.

## SUB 2 | International Court of Justice
[2:0] - **Principal judicial organ**; seat **The Hague** (Netherlands) – the only principal organ outside New York.
[2:1] - **15 judges**, **9-year** terms (one-third elected every three years); elected by the **UNGA and UNSC voting separately** (absolute majority in both).
[2:2] - Indian judges: **B.N. Rau**, **Nagendra Singh** (ICJ President **1985-88**), **R.S. Pathak**, **Dalveer Bhandari**.
[2:3] - **Dalveer Bhandari**: elected **27 April 2012** (remaining term to Feb 2018); **re-elected** (second time) from 6 Feb 2018 for nine years.
[2:4] - **Kulbhushan Jadhav case (2019)**: ICJ **stayed his execution**; Pakistan breached the **Vienna Convention on Consular Relations**; review of conviction ordered.
[2:5] - **July 2025**: unanimous **advisory opinion on climate** obligations.
[2:6] > Exam angle: 15 = UNSC members and ICJ judges; 9 = ICJ term; 54 = ECOSOC.

## SUB 3 | Principal Organs, General Assembly and ECOSOC
[3:0] - **Six principal organs**: **General Assembly, Security Council, ECOSOC, Trusteeship Council, ICJ, Secretariat**. Amnesty International is an NGO, not a UN organ.
[3:1] TABLE head: Organ | Composition / mandate
  [3:1.0] General Assembly | All 193 members, one vote each – the "World Parliament"; meets in annual regular session
  [3:1.1] Security Council | 15 members; binding decisions
  [3:1.2] ECOSOC | 54 members, 3-year terms, elected by the UNGA (two-thirds majority, Art. 18)
  [3:1.3] Trusteeship Council | Inactive since 1994, after Palau became self-governing
  [3:1.4] ICJ | Judicial organ, The Hague
  [3:1.5] Secretariat | Headed by the Secretary-General
[3:2] - **UDHR (10 Dec 1948)** = a **UN General Assembly resolution (217 A)** – not a treaty.
[3:3] - UNGA: important questions need two-thirds (Art. 18); **Credentials Committee** – **9** members each session.
[3:4] - **Uniting for Peace (Res. 377), 1950**: lets the UNGA act when a veto blocks the UNSC; first used in the **1956 Suez Crisis** to send peacekeepers.
[3:5] - Emergency special sessions: the 10th – resolution ES-10/23 (10 May 2024) upgraded Palestine's rights as an observer State.
[3:6] - **Non-member observer States** (2): **Holy See** (since 1964) and **State of Palestine** (2012).
[3:7] - **Sweden** (Oct 2014) – first EU member state (while a member) to officially recognise the State of Palestine.
[3:8] - **Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit** – President of the 8th UNGA (1953), the first woman (and first Indian) to hold the post.
[3:9] - **81st UNGA President** (2026-27): **Khalilur Rahman** (Bangladesh).
[3:10] - 22 May 2026 climate resolution: India abstained (141-8-28).
[3:11] # ECOSOC functional commissions
[3:12] - **Commission on the Status of Women** (**1946**) – CSW-70 held 2026.
[3:13] - **Commission on Sustainable Development** – reviewed **Agenda 21** and the Rio Declaration; replaced by the **High-Level Political Forum** in **2013**.

## SUB 6 | UNSC Reform
[6:0] TABLE head: Group | Members / demand
  [6:0.0] G4 | Brazil, Germany, India, Japan – support each other's permanent seats, plus seats for Africa (China is not in it – already P5)
  [6:0.1] Uniting for Consensus ("Coffee Club") | Led by Italy; includes Pakistan; opposes new permanent seats
  [6:0.2] L.69 | Developing countries of Asia, Africa, Latin America, Pacific; India a member
  [6:0.3] Ezulwini Consensus (2005) | African Union: 2 permanent seats with veto + 5 non-permanent
[6:1] - G4 model: expand the UNSC from 15 to 25–26.
[6:2] - **IGN** (Inter-Governmental Negotiations): informal **UNGA** process, set up **2008**; annual "Elements Paper". India demands text-based negotiations (June 2026).
[6:3] - **China** opposes India's permanent seat.
