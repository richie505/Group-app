## SUB 0 | Cotton Textiles – Oldest Industry, Centres, Nicknames and Location
[0:0] # History and status
[0:1] - **Oldest / ancient industry of India:** **cotton textiles** (muslin, calico, chintz were famous worldwide) – older than jute, tea or sugar, which are modern mill industries (UPPSC 2004 twice).
[0:2] - **First cotton mill:** **1818, Fort Gloster near Kolkata (Calcutta)** – a commercial failure (UPUDA 2013; Jharkhand 2013).
[0:3] - **First successful mill:** **1854, Mumbai**, by Parsi entrepreneur **C.N. Davar** (local capital); growth surged after the American Civil War (1860s-70s).
[0:4] - **Only major industry in Indian hands at the end of the 19th century:** **cotton textiles** – jute, mining and tea were British-owned; Indian-owned steel began only with TISCO (1907).
[0:5] - **Largest organised modern industry of India:** cotton textiles; three tiers – handspun khadi, handloom/powerloom, and the capital-intensive mill sector (powerlooms now make most cloth).
[0:6] # Location and dispersal
[0:7] - Began in the **black-soil cotton belt of Maharashtra and Gujarat**; **Bombay–Ahmedabad** = the most prominent cotton textile region (APPSC G-I 2003).
[0:8] - **Dispersed** all over India because raw cotton is a **pure raw material that does not lose weight** in manufacture, so mills followed markets, ports, power and cheap labour (Kanpur, Kolkata, Coimbatore) (RAS 2013 A-R: both correct).
[0:9] - **Tamil Nadu – mill-made yarn:** hydel power, **rich pool of skilled/cheap labour**, local cotton; **black cotton soil is NOT predominant** in TN (UPSC 2010: only II).
[0:10] - **Largest number of mills:** **Tamil Nadu** (most produce yarn, Coimbatore has nearly half) – older books and keys say **Maharashtra** (Mumbai the largest centre).
[0:11] - **Not a cotton region:** **Dhanbad–Jamshedpur** = coal and steel; Mumbai–Pune, Madurai–Coimbatore, Indore–Ujjain are cotton regions (NDA 2017).
[0:12] - **Raw cotton exports:** India's largest buyers are **Bangladesh and China**, not South Korea.
[0:13] TABLE head: Nickname | City
  [0:13.0] Manchester of India | Ahmedabad (asked 3 times)
  [0:13.1] Cottonopolis (largest centre) | Mumbai
  [0:13.2] Manchester of South India | Coimbatore
  [0:13.3] Manchester of the North | Kanpur
  [0:13.4] Pittsburgh of India / Steel City | Jamshedpur
[0:14] # Allied centres
[0:15] - **Hosiery:** **Ludhiana** (Punjab's industrial capital; also Avon/Hero cycles). **Kota** = synthetic silk. Woollens: Kanpur (first woollen mill 1876), Dhariwal, Ludhiana, Jamnagar, Srinagar.
[0:16] - **Handloom and prints:** **Chanderi sarees – Madhya Pradesh**; **Nandana block print – Tarapur village (Neemuch), Madhya Pradesh**.
[0:17] > Statement angle (Sachivalayam 2019): "cotton textile = largest organised modern industry" ✓; first mill in New Delhi ✗; South Korea biggest importer ✗.

## SUB 1 | Sugar Industry – Producers, Belts, North–South Shift and Policy
[1:0] # World and India
[1:1] - **Largest producer:** **Brazil**; India **2nd** (~19%) and **largest consumer**; India overtook Brazil only in **2021-22** (first time in 16 years). Brazil is also the largest exporter.
[1:2] - **"Sugar Bowl of the world" = Cuba** (largest producer and exporter till the 1960s); **"Sugar Bowl of India" = Uttar Pradesh**.
[1:3] - **2nd largest agro-based industry** of India after cotton textiles; mills sit near cane because cane is **bulky, perishable and weight-losing** (sugar only ~10% of cane).
[1:4] # States
[1:5] TABLE head: State | Position
  [1:5.0] Maharashtra | Largest producer in 2015-16 and in the latest seasons (ISMA, Dec 2025)
  [1:5.1] Uttar Pradesh | Largest cane producer; largest long-run share (~35%) and most sugar factories among UP, TN, Bihar, Assam; now 2nd in sugar
  [1:5.2] Karnataka | 3rd
  [1:5.3] Tamil Nadu | 4th
[1:6] - **Top three:** **UP, Maharashtra, Karnataka** (order now Maharashtra > UP > Karnataka; 2015-16 descending: **Maharashtra, UP, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu**).
[1:7] - **Belts:** western UP (Meerut-Saharanpur-Muzaffarnagar), eastern UP (Gorakhpur-Deoria-Basti); Maharashtra cooperatives **Nasik, Pune, Satara, Sangli, Kolhapur, Sholapur**; AP: East and West Godavari, Visakhapatnam, Chittoor.
[1:8] - **Sakkar (Shakkar) Nagar:** near **Bodhan (Nizamabad)**, around the Nizam Sugar Factory – in undivided **Andhra Pradesh**, **now Telangana**.
[1:9] - **First sugar mill:** **1903, Pratappur (Deoria, UP)**.
[1:10] # North → South shift
[1:11] TABLE head: North (UP, Bihar) | South (Maharashtra, Karnataka, TN, AP)
  [1:11.0] Subtropical cane, lower sucrose and yield | Tropical cane, higher sucrose and higher yield per acre
  [1:11.1] Crushing season Nov–Feb (~4 months) | Longer crushing season (Oct to May-June)
  [1:11.2] More but smaller, older mills | Larger, modern, better-run cooperatives
[1:12] - **Reasons for the shift:** higher yield, higher sucrose, longer crushing season – **not** cheap labour, power or railways (OPSC 2018; UPPSC 2007).
[1:13] # Policy and by-products
[1:14] - **Rangarajan Committee (2012):** **decontrol** – end levy sugar and regulated release; **70:30** revenue sharing. Govt **partially decontrolled** sugar in **April 2013** (millers free to sell in the open market, no levy obligation to ration shops) (CDS 2013: both statements correct).
[1:15] - **By-products:** bagasse – boiler fuel and paper pulp; molasses – alcohol/ethanol; press mud – manure. **FRP** = Centre's cane price; **SAP** = State price.
[1:16] - About **3/4 of ethanol** now from grain (app notes, 2026).

## SUB 2 | Cement – Raw Materials, Firsts, Centres and Production
[2:0] - **Raw materials:** **limestone** (main), **clay** (silica, alumina) and **gypsum**; coal for fuel – so plants sit in limestone areas. **Mortar** = **sand + cement** (+ water) for brick walls.
[2:1] - **First attempt:** **1904, Madras (Chennai)**, sea shells as lime – failed; **first successful:** **Indian Cement Co., Porbandar**, set up **1912-13**, production **1914**. Exams answer "first cement factory = Chennai".
[2:2] - **World rank:** India **2nd** after China (also in 2010); per capita ~290 kg vs world ~540 kg.
[2:3] - **Largest state (2026):** Rajasthan, then AP (older books: AP first).

  [2:4.3] Satna, Katni | Madhya Pradesh
  [2:4.4] Kesoram Cements, Basantnagar | Karimnagar district of undivided AP (now Peddapalli, Telangana)
  [2:4.5] Nalgonda | Telangana

[2:5] - **Green cements:** PPC (fly ash) and PSC (steel slag).

## SUB 3 | Fertilisers, Petrochemicals and Refineries
[3:0] # Fertilisers
[3:1] - **First super-phosphate factory:** **Ranipet (TN), 1906**. **First large-scale fertiliser plant:** **FACT, Alwaye/Udyogamandal (Kerala)** – incorporated 1943, production **1947**. **Sindri (1951)** = first public-sector plant of independent India (many books call Sindri "the first").
[3:2] - **Raw materials:** naphtha, natural gas, coal, **rock phosphate**, **sulphur**, **gypsum** – **caustic soda is NOT** one. Urea = ammonia (Haber) + CO2; superphosphate = rock phosphate + sulphuric acid.
[3:3] - **Biggest cooperative plant:** **Phulpur (Prayagraj, UP)** – IFFCO. Other centres: Sri Ganganagar, **Haldia**, Kota, Nangal, Kakinada.
[3:4] - **Gujarat** = largest fertiliser capacity; UP = largest urea producer. Natural gas/sulphur: sulphur for phosphatic fertiliser mainly from **oil refineries**.
[3:5] TABLE head: Region | Centres
  [3:5.0] Gujarat–Maharashtra (largest) | Vadodara, Kalol, Kandla, Trombay
  [3:5.1] TN–Kerala | Neyveli, Tuticorin, Ennore, Alwaye, Kochi
  [3:5.2] North-West | Nangal, Bhatinda, Panipat
  [3:5.3] Chota Nagpur steel towns | Sindri, Jamshedpur, Rourkela, Durgapur, Bhilai
  [3:5.4] Coal-based FCI units | Talcher, Ramagundam, Korba
  [3:5.5] AP | Kakinada (Nagarjuna, Coromandel)
[3:6] # Petrochemicals and refineries
[3:7] - **Largest petrochemical centre:** **Jamnagar, Gujarat** (Reliance; world's largest refining complex); others Vadodara (Koyali), Hazira, Ankleshwar, Trombay.
[3:8] - **Bhopal** (MP) = pesticides (Union Carbide plant, 1984 gas tragedy).
[3:9] - **Most westward petrochemical place (CDS 2010):** key **Kota** (75.8°E); Nagpur 79°E, Barauni 86°E – Ujjain (75.78°E) is marginally west of Kota.
[3:10] - **Refineries in AP = 2:** **HPCL Visakhapatnam** and **ONGC mini refinery at Tatipaka (Konaseema)**.

## SUB 4 | Jute – Hooghly Belt, Retting and Andhra Mills
[4:0] - **First jute mill:** **Rishra, near Kolkata, 1855**; jute is India's 2nd textile industry after cotton.
[4:1] - **Location:** ~**90%** of capacity in a **100 km x 3 km belt along the Hooghly** – most mills in the **Hugli basin**, **West Bengal** (APPSC G-I 2024).
[4:2] - **Why Hooghly:** raw jute of the delta, **soft/standing water for retting**, NW-1 and **Kolkata port**, coal from Raniganj, cheap labour, capital.
[4:3] - **Retting:** soaking jute stems in water so microbes loosen the fibre – the process that separates fibre from the stem.
[4:4] - **Mills:** WB 71 of 97 (2026); **AP 2nd** – Chittivalasa, Nellimarla; **Bhajrang Jute Mill, Guntur** – shut down, a long labour/management dispute.
[4:5] - **Bhatpara** = jute centre (Titagarh = paper). **National Jute Policy 2005**; **JUTE-ICARE** = certified seed + microbial retting with **SONA**.
[4:6] - **Largest exporter:** Bangladesh. **Jute Packaging Materials Act 1987:** 100% foodgrains, 20% sugar in jute bags.

## SUB 5 | Paper and Newsprint
[5:0] - **Raw materials:** bamboo (60-70%), sabai grass, bagasse, **poplar**, eucalyptus, rags, waste paper.
[5:1] - **Newsprint:** **Nepanagar (Burhanpur, MP)** (asked 2 times).
[5:2] - **Most paper mills:** **Gujarat**, then **Uttar Pradesh** (UP = most among Karnataka, MP, UP, WB); Maharashtra = largest producer.
[5:3] - **Jagiroad (Assam)** = Hindustan Paper Corporation mill (shut 2017). Paper uses no rock phosphate.
[5:4] - **Mills:** Titagarh (WB), Sirpur-Kagaznagar (Telangana), Rajahmundry (AP), Ballarpur (Maharashtra).
[5:5] - **Asia Pulp & Paper (APP, Indonesia's Sinar Mas)** announced a pulp-and-paper venture in AP (Prakasam) after bifurcation.
