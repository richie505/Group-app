=== Cotton Textiles – Oldest Industry, Centres, Nicknames and Location
# History and status
- **Oldest / ancient industry of India:** **cotton textiles** (muslin, calico, chintz were famous worldwide) – older than jute, tea or sugar, which are modern mill industries (UPPSC 2004 twice).
- **First cotton mill:** **1818, Fort Gloster near Kolkata (Calcutta)** – a commercial failure (UPUDA 2013; Jharkhand 2013).
- **First successful mill:** **1854, Mumbai**, by Parsi entrepreneur **C.N. Davar** (local capital); growth surged after the American Civil War (1860s-70s).
- **Only major industry in Indian hands at the end of the 19th century:** **cotton textiles** – jute, mining and tea were British-owned; Indian-owned steel began only with TISCO (1907).
- **Largest organised modern industry of India:** cotton textiles; three tiers – handspun khadi, handloom/powerloom, and the capital-intensive mill sector (powerlooms now make most cloth).
# Location and dispersal
- Began in the **black-soil cotton belt of Maharashtra and Gujarat**; **Bombay–Ahmedabad** = the most prominent cotton textile region (APPSC G-I 2003).
- **Dispersed** all over India because raw cotton is a **pure raw material that does not lose weight** in manufacture, so mills followed markets, ports, power and cheap labour (Kanpur, Kolkata, Coimbatore) (RAS 2013 A-R: both correct).
- **Tamil Nadu – mill-made yarn:** hydel power, **rich pool of skilled/cheap labour**, local cotton; **black cotton soil is NOT predominant** in TN (UPSC 2010: only II).
- **Largest number of mills:** **Tamil Nadu** (most produce yarn, Coimbatore has nearly half) – older books and keys say **Maharashtra** (Mumbai the largest centre).
- **Not a cotton region:** **Dhanbad–Jamshedpur** = coal and steel; Mumbai–Pune, Madurai–Coimbatore, Indore–Ujjain are cotton regions (NDA 2017).
- **Raw cotton exports:** India's largest buyers are **Bangladesh and China**, not South Korea.
| Nickname | City |
|---|---|
| **Manchester of India** | **Ahmedabad** (asked 3 times) |
| Cottonopolis (largest centre) | Mumbai |
| Manchester of South India | Coimbatore |
| Manchester of the North | Kanpur |
| Pittsburgh of India / Steel City | Jamshedpur |
# Allied centres
- **Hosiery:** **Ludhiana** (Punjab's industrial capital; also Avon/Hero cycles). **Kota** = synthetic silk. Woollens: Kanpur (first woollen mill 1876), Dhariwal, Ludhiana, Jamnagar, Srinagar.
- **Handloom and prints:** **Chanderi sarees – Madhya Pradesh**; **Nandana block print – Tarapur village, Madhya Pradesh**.
> Statement angle (Sachivalayam 2019): "cotton textile = largest organised modern industry" ✓; first mill in New Delhi ✗; South Korea biggest importer ✗.

=== Sugar Industry – Producers, Belts, North–South Shift and Policy
# World and India
- **Largest producer:** **Brazil**; India **2nd** (~19%) and **largest consumer**; India overtook Brazil only in **2021-22** (first time in 16 years). Brazil is also the largest exporter.
- **"Sugar Bowl of the world" = Cuba** (largest producer and exporter till the 1960s); **"Sugar Bowl of India" = Uttar Pradesh**.
- **2nd largest agro-based industry** of India after cotton textiles; mills sit near cane because cane is **bulky, perishable and weight-losing** (sugar only ~10% of cane).
# States
| State | Position |
|---|---|
| **Maharashtra** | **Largest producer in 2015-16** and in the latest seasons (ISMA, Dec 2025) |
| **Uttar Pradesh** | Largest cane producer; largest long-run share (~35%) and **most sugar factories among UP, TN, Bihar, Assam**; now **2nd** in sugar |
| Karnataka | 3rd |
| Tamil Nadu | 4th |
- **Top three:** **UP, Maharashtra, Karnataka** (order now Maharashtra > UP > Karnataka; 2015-16 descending: **Maharashtra, UP, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu**).
- **Belts:** western UP (Meerut-Saharanpur-Muzaffarnagar), eastern UP (Gorakhpur-Deoria-Basti); Maharashtra cooperatives **Nasik, Pune, Satara, Sangli, Kolhapur, Sholapur**; AP: East and West Godavari, Visakhapatnam, Chittoor.
- **Sakkar (Shakkar) Nagar:** near **Bodhan (Nizamabad)**, around the Nizam Sugar Factory – in undivided **Andhra Pradesh**, **now Telangana**.
- **First sugar mill:** **1903, Pratappur (Deoria, UP)**.
# North → South shift
| North (UP, Bihar) | South (Maharashtra, Karnataka, TN, AP) |
|---|---|
| Subtropical cane, lower sucrose and yield | Tropical cane, **higher sucrose and higher yield per acre** |
| Crushing season Nov–Feb (~4 months) | **Longer crushing season** (Oct to May-June) |
| More but smaller, older mills | Larger, modern, better-run cooperatives |
- **Reasons for the shift:** higher yield, higher sucrose, longer crushing season – **not** cheap labour, power or railways (OPSC 2018; UPPSC 2007).
# Policy and by-products
- **Rangarajan Committee (2012):** **decontrol** – end levy sugar and regulated release; **70:30** revenue sharing. Govt **partially decontrolled** sugar in **April 2013** (millers free to sell in the open market, no levy obligation to ration shops) (CDS 2013: both statements correct).
- **By-products:** bagasse – boiler fuel and paper pulp; molasses – alcohol/ethanol; press mud – manure. **FRP** = Centre's cane price; **SAP** = State price.
- About **3/4 of ethanol** now from grain (app notes, 2026).

=== Cement – Raw Materials, Firsts, Centres and Production
- **Raw materials:** **limestone** (main), **clay** (silica, alumina) and **gypsum**; coal for fuel – so plants sit in limestone areas. **Mortar** = **sand + cement** (+ water) for brick walls.
- **First attempt:** **1904, Madras (Chennai)**, sea shells as lime – failed; **first successful:** **Indian Cement Co., Porbandar**, set up **1912-13**, production **1914**. Exams answer "first cement factory = Chennai".
- **World rank:** India **2nd** after China (also in 2010); per capita ~290 kg vs world ~540 kg.
- **Largest state (2026):** Rajasthan, then AP (older books: AP first).

=== Fertilisers, Petrochemicals and Refineries
# Fertilisers
- **First super-phosphate factory:** **Ranipet (TN), 1906**. **First large-scale fertiliser plant:** **FACT, Alwaye/Udyogamandal (Kerala)** – incorporated 1943, production **1947**. **Sindri (1951)** = first public-sector plant of independent India (many books call Sindri "the first").
- **Raw materials:** naphtha, natural gas, coal, **rock phosphate**, **sulphur**, **gypsum** – **caustic soda is NOT** one. Urea = ammonia (Haber) + CO2; superphosphate = rock phosphate + sulphuric acid.
- **Biggest cooperative plant:** **Phulpur (Prayagraj, UP)** – IFFCO. Other centres: Sri Ganganagar, **Haldia**, Kota, Nangal, Kakinada.
- **Gujarat** = largest fertiliser capacity; UP = largest urea producer. Natural gas/sulphur: sulphur for phosphatic fertiliser mainly from **oil refineries**.

| Gujarat–Maharashtra (largest) | Vadodara, Kalol, Kandla, Trombay |

| North-West | Nangal, Bhatinda, Panipat |
| Chota Nagpur steel towns | Sindri, Jamshedpur, Rourkela, Durgapur, Bhilai |
| Coal-based FCI units | Talcher, Ramagundam, Korba |
| AP | Kakinada (Nagarjuna, Coromandel) |
# Petrochemicals and refineries
- **Largest petrochemical centre:** **Jamnagar, Gujarat** (Reliance; world's largest refining complex); others Vadodara (Koyali), Hazira, Ankleshwar, Trombay.
- **Bhopal** (MP) = pesticides (Union Carbide plant, 1984 gas tragedy).
- **Most westward petrochemical place (CDS 2010):** key **Kota** (75.8°E); Nagpur 79°E, Barauni 86°E – Ujjain (75.78°E) is marginally west of Kota.
- **Refineries in AP = 2:** **HPCL Visakhapatnam** and **ONGC mini refinery at Tatipaka (Konaseema)**.

=== Jute – Hooghly Belt, Retting and Andhra Mills
- **First jute mill:** **Rishra, near Kolkata, 1855**; jute is India's 2nd textile industry after cotton.
- **Location:** ~**90%** of capacity in a **100 km x 3 km belt along the Hooghly** – most mills in the **Hugli basin**, **West Bengal** (APPSC G-I 2024).
- **Why Hooghly:** raw jute of the delta, **soft/standing water for retting**, NW-1 and **Kolkata port**, coal from Raniganj, cheap labour, capital.
- **Retting:** soaking jute stems in water so microbes loosen the fibre – the process that separates fibre from the stem.
- **Mills:** WB 71 of 97 (2026); **AP 2nd** – Chittivalasa, Nellimarla; **Bhajrang Jute Mill, Guntur** – shut down, a long labour/management dispute.
- **Bhatpara** = jute centre (Titagarh = paper). **National Jute Policy 2005**; **JUTE-ICARE** = certified seed + microbial retting with **SONA**.
- **Largest exporter:** Bangladesh. **Jute Packaging Materials Act 1987:** 100% foodgrains, 20% sugar in jute bags.

=== Paper and Newsprint
- **Raw materials:** bamboo (60-70%), sabai grass, bagasse, **poplar**, eucalyptus, rags, waste paper.
- **Newsprint:** **Nepanagar (Burhanpur, MP)** (asked 2 times).
- **Most paper mills:** **Gujarat**, then **Uttar Pradesh** (UP = most among Karnataka, MP, UP, WB); Maharashtra = largest producer.
- **Jagiroad (Assam)** = Hindustan Paper Corporation mill (shut 2017). Paper uses no rock phosphate.
- **Mills:** Titagarh (WB), Sirpur-Kagaznagar (Telangana), Rajahmundry (AP), Ballarpur (Maharashtra).
- **Asia Pulp & Paper (APP, Indonesia's Sinar Mas)** announced a pulp-and-paper venture in AP (Prakasam) after bifurcation.

=== First Modern Industrial Units – Chronology
- **First industry to develop in India:** the **cottage industry**.
| Unit | Place | Year |
|---|---|---|
| Paper mill | **Serampore** (WB) | 1812 |
| Cotton mill (failed) | Fort Gloster, near Kolkata | 1818 |
| Iron unit on modern lines | **Porto Novo** (TN) | 1830 |
| First successful cotton mill | Mumbai – C.N. Davar | 1854 |
| Jute mill | **Rishra**, near Kolkata | 1855 |
| Steel plant | **Bengal Iron Works Company** | 1870 |
| Woollen mill | **Kanpur** (army depot) | 1876 |
| Sugar mill | **Pratappur, Deoria** (UP) | 1903 |
| Cement (failed) | **Madras**, sea shells as lime | 1904 |
| Super-phosphate factory | **Ranipet** (TN) | 1906 |
| Integrated steel | TISCO, Jamshedpur | 1907 |
| First successful cement | **Indian Cement Co., Porbandar** | 1912-13 (production 1914) |

- **Sequence:** Serampore paper → Ranipet super-phosphate → TISCO → Bengaluru aircraft.
