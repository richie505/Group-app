## SUB 3 | Stupa, Chaitya and Vihara – Concept and Parts
[30:3:0] - **Stupa** = solid relic mound worshipped by circumambulation; the **concept is pre-Buddhist** (burial mounds; the word occurs in Vedic texts).
[30:3:1] - **Buddhist stupas** enshrine **relics**, are built as **objects of devotion** by monasteries and **commemorate events** of the Buddha's life – all four statements true (APPSC AEE flashcard). UPSC 2023: "Buddhist in origin" ✗, relic repository ✓, votive and commemorative ✓ → **only two**.
[30:3:2] - **Chaitya** (chaitya-shala / chaitya-griha) = **place of worship**, prayer hall with a stupa at the apsidal end – the Buddhist "temple" (**chaitya stupas**).
[30:3:3] - **Vihara** = **residence of monks**; **Arama** = complex of **stupa + vihara + chaitya + school**, e.g. Nandaluru.
[30:3:4] TABLE head: Part | Meaning
  [30:3:4.0] Anda | Hemispherical dome (the cosmos)
  [30:3:4.1] Medhi | Raised drum / terrace round the base of the dome, for circumambulation
  [30:3:4.2] Harmika | Square railing on top of the anda
  [30:3:4.3] Yashti | Central mast rising from the harmika
  [30:3:4.4] Chhatra / Chhatri | Umbrella(s) on the yashti
  [30:3:4.5] Vedika | Stone railing round the stupa
  [30:3:4.6] Torana | Ornamental gateways at the four directions
  [30:3:4.7] Pradakshinapatha | Path for clockwise circumambulation
  [30:3:4.8] Ayaka pillars / Ayakapata | Special feature of Andhra stupas
[30:3:5] - **Anda, harmika and chhatri** = parts of a **stupa** (APPSC G2 2024).
[30:3:6] TABLE head: Stupa type | Content
  [30:3:6.0] Dhatugarbha | Bodily relics (Amaravati, Bhattiprolu)
  [30:3:6.1] Paribhogika | Objects used by the Buddha
  [30:3:6.2] Uddeshika | Commemorative, no relics
  [30:3:6.3] Aniconic symbol | Event
  [30:3:6.4] Lotus and bull | Birth
  [30:3:6.5] Horse (Kanthaka) | Renunciation
  [30:3:6.6] Empty seat under the Bodhi tree | Enlightenment
  [30:3:6.7] Dharmachakra (wheel) | First sermon
  [30:3:6.8] Stupa / footprints | Parinirvana
[30:3:7] - **Hinayana** showed the Buddha by **symbols**; **Mahayana** adopted images; first human images – **1st c. CE, Gandhara and Mathura**.

## SUB 5 | Spread, Patrons and Decline of Buddhism
[30:5:0] - **Causes of spread:** simple doctrine, appeal to the lower orders, **missionary** zeal and the people's **language** (Pali/Prakrit) – I, II, III, IV (UPPSC 2009); not "soundness of the Vedic spirit".
[30:5:1] - **Mauryan followers:** **Ashoka** and **Dasharatha** (CGPSC 2013); Chandragupta became a Jain, Bindusara an Ajivika.
[30:5:2] - **Sites (IAS 1998):** **Lothal – dockyard**, **Sarnath – first sermon**, **Nalanda – learning** ✓; Rajgir – Lion Capital ✗ (the capital is from Sarnath).
[30:5:3] TABLE head: Ashoka's missionary | Region
  [30:5:3.0] Mahinda + Sanghamitta | Sri Lanka
  [30:5:3.1] Sona + Uttara | Suvarnabhumi
  [30:5:3.2] Majjhantika | Kashmir and Gandhara
  [30:5:3.3] Mahadeva | Mahishamandala (Mysore)
  [30:5:3.4] Rakkhita | Vanavasi
  [30:5:3.5] Maharakkhita | Yona country
[30:5:4] - Buddhism reached Central Asia and China by the Kushan-era **Silk Route**.
[30:5:5] - **Mahavamsa:** **Mahadeva Bhikshu** took Buddhism **from Andhra to Sri Lanka** (not the reverse).
[30:5:6] TABLE head: Andhra patron | Contribution
  [30:5:6.0] Satavahanas | Vedic kings but lavish donors; Sanchi toranas
  [30:5:6.1] Veerapurushadatta (Ikshvaku) | Nagarjunakonda Maha Chaitya; royal women donors
  [30:5:6.2] Upasika Bodhisri | Service in Ehuvala Chantamula's reign
  [30:5:6.3] Vishnukundins | Last dynasty to support Buddhism in Andhra; Indrapalanagara vihara
[30:5:7] - Greatest royal patronage in Andhra: **Ikshvakus** of Vijayapuri. **Lay patronage** – queens, merchants, guilds; 2,000 karshapanas with a **weavers'** guild for robes.
[30:5:8] - **Decline:** loss of royal patronage; **Brahmanical (Bhakti) revival**; Vajrayana ritualism; **Sanskrit replaced Pali**; hostile rulers **Pushyamitra Shunga, Shashanka, Mihirakula**; Turkish raids (Nalanda sacked by **Bakhtiyar Khalji**). **Hiuen Tsang** found Sriparvata **without patronage**.
[30:5:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.66; NextGen Part 2 p.43; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.123; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.68; old app notes 1:29-30

=== NOTES 1:31 | Other Heterodox Sects – Ajivikas (makkhali Gosala), Charvaka / Lokayata Materialism
## SUB 0 | Ajivikas – Makkhali Gosala and Niyati
[31:0:0] - **Founder (exam answer):** **Makkhali Gosala** (Gosala Maskariputra) – its most important leader and populariser (UPPCS 1996; UKPSC 2024; NDA 2015); earlier teachers like Nanda Vaccha are also named. Not Ananda, Upali, Vasubandhu or Dignaga.
[31:0:1] - **Niyati (fate):** "**Fate assigns everything, man is powerless**" = **Ajivikas** (UPPCS 2006; RAS 2013) – not Jains, Buddhists or Mimamsakas.
[31:0:2] - All thoughts and actions are **fixed before birth**; **no free will**; karma cannot change destiny; the **order of rebirth cannot be changed**.
[31:0:3] - **Practice:** belief in **determinism** and **severe austerities** (RAS 2024: 1 and 3); ascetics called **Sudra sannyasins**; **no caste discrimination**; held meetings in regular **sabhas** (State PSC: A, B and D).
[31:0:4] - **Atomic theory** of matter – influenced the later **Vaisheshika**.
[31:0:5] - Gosala was **Mahavira's companion for about six years**; Jain texts call him Mahavira's **disciple**; the **Bhagavati Sutra** records their quarrel; the **Majjhima Nikaya** records the Buddha's debates with Jains and Ajivikas.
[31:0:6] TABLE head: Ruler / record | Ajivika link
  [31:0:6.0] Bindusara | Popular under him; joined the sect
  [31:0:6.1] Ashoka, Dasharatha | Gifted the Barabar (and Nagarjuni) caves – among the earliest rock-cut caves in India
  [31:0:6.2] 7th Pillar Edict (Delhi-Topra) | Names Ajivikas with Brahmanas, the Sangha and Nirgranthas
[31:0:7] - Barabar caves were given by **Ashoka, not Chandragupta**; Ashoka himself was Buddhist.
[31:0:8] - **Decline:** survived about **2,000 years**, to the **14th c. CE** in **Karnataka and Tamil Nadu**; faded with the Puranic-Bhakti revival and loss of patronage.
[31:0:9] > Niyativada = Gosala (Ajivika); materialism = Charvaka.

## SUB 1 | Charvaka / Lokayata Materialism
[31:1:0] - **Other names:** **Lokayata** / **Lokayata Darshan** ("of the common people") (State PSC; CGPSC 2018); **Barhaspatya** after the legendary founder **Brihaspati**.
[31:1:1] - **Complete materialism**; highest value = **Kama** (pleasure) – not Dharma, Artha or Moksha (CGPSC 2017).
[31:1:2] - **Dehatmavada:** "**The conscious body is the soul**" (CGPSC 2024).
[31:1:3] - **Only pramana = perception** (pratyaksha) (CGPSC 2018); rejects inference, Vedic testimony, ritualism and the afterlife. Nyaya accepts **four** pramanas.
[31:1:4] - **Aphorism (IAS 1994):** "**Yavaj jivet sukham jivet, rinam kritva ghritam pibet**" – live well while you live, even on borrowing; once cremated there is no return → **Charvakas**.
[31:1:5] - Original **Brihaspati Sutra** is lost; Lokayata is known through **rivals' criticism**, e.g. **Madhava's Sarvadarshanasangraha**.
[31:1:6] TABLE head: School | Karma | Soul
  [31:1:6.0] Jainism | Removable by penance | Eternal jiva in all things
  [31:1:6.1] Buddhism | Intention-based | Anatta
  [31:1:6.2] Ajivika | Cannot alter destiny | Accepted
  [31:1:6.3] Charvaka | Does not decide rebirth | Conscious body
[31:1:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.50; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.123; Rocket Part 2 p.5; PMF Art & Culture Part 5 p.105; old app notes 1:31
## SUB 2 | Six Teachers and the Age of Religious Ferment
[31:2:0] - **6th century BCE:** an age of religious upheaval across the world, while the **Vedic religion had become complex** – both true (UKPSC 2012). Shramana movements arose amid **urbanisation and Magadhan patronage**.
[31:2:1] TABLE head: Teacher | Doctrine
  [31:2:1.0] Makkhali Gosala | Niyativada – fatalism (Ajivika)
  [31:2:1.1] Purana Kassapa | Akriyavada – actions bring neither merit nor demerit
  [31:2:1.2] Ajita Kesakambalin | Materialist – nothing survives death
  [31:2:1.3] Pakudha Kaccayana | Seven eternal, uncreated elements
  [31:2:1.4] Sanjaya Belatthiputta | Sceptic – "eel-wriggling"
  [31:2:1.5] Nigantha Nataputta | = Mahavira
[31:2:2] - Source: the **Samannaphala Sutta** of the **Digha Nikaya** – Ajatashatru reports the six teachers' views to the Buddha.
[31:2:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.86-87; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.50; old app notes 1:31
[31:2:4] - **Why the heterodox sects arose:** the Vedic religion in **Sanskrit** was beyond common people; costly **yajnas** and animal sacrifice consumed the cattle needed for iron-plough farming; **Kshatriyas** resented Brahmin dominance (Mahavira and the Buddha were Kshatriyas); newly rich **Vaishyas** sought higher status and backed the new sects, not Vedic sacrifice.

[35:0:0] # What Dhamma was
[35:0:1] - **A moral code and social ethic**, not a new religion – discipline, harmony among sects, welfare; Rajdharma on **longanimity, liberty and compassion** = **Ashoka** (UPPCS 1993) – not Akbar, Ranjit Singh or Shivaji.
[35:0:2] - **Central point:** **peace and non-violence** (RAS 2013) – RE I bans slaughter and sacrifice; toleration and respect for elders are parts, not the core.
[35:0:3] - **Source of the definition:** the Buddhist **Rahulovada Sutta** (spirit also in the Lakkhana Sutta and Dhammapada).
[35:0:4] - **Virtues:** kindness, **charity**, truthfulness, purity, respect for elders and teachers, regard for **Brahmanas and Shramanas**, courtesy to slaves and servants. **Vices:** violence, cruelty, anger, pride, jealousy. **Avoid:** wasteful festive gatherings (**samajas**).
[35:0:5] - **PE II** defines Dhamma as "**few faults, many good deeds**". It names no Four Noble Truths or Nibbana.

## SUB 1 | Kalinga War and the Change of Policy
[35:1:0] - **Date:** **c. 261 BCE**, **8th regnal year** (RE XIII). **Cause:** Kalinga had broken free in Bindusara's last years and controlled the **land and sea routes to the south**.
[35:1:1] - **UP Lower 2002 A–R:** A (Ashoka annexed Kalinga) ✓, R (Kalinga controlled land and sea routes to South India) ✓ and R explains A → **(a)**.
[35:1:2] - **Toll (RE XIII):** about **1,00,000 killed**, **1,50,000 deported/captive**, many more died later.
[35:1:3] - **Profound change in policy:** the **Kalinga War** (SSC 2009) – remorse led to **Dhammavijaya**; Bherighosha (war drum) → **Dhammaghosha**; no more wars of conquest. Buddhism, the Third Council and the Ceylon mission came later.
[35:1:4] - **Kalinga under the Mauryas:** province with capital **Tosali**; the Separate Kalinga Edicts at **Dhauli and Jaugada** omit RE XIII.
