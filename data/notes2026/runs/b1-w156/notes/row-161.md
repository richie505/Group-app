=== Battle of Wandiwash (1760) and Lally's Campaign
- **Third Carnatic War (1758-63)** = Indian theatre of the **Seven Years' War (1756-63)**; NextGen dates it 1757-63, some books 1756-63.
- **1758:** **Count de Lally** took **Fort St. David** (Cuddalore) and Vizianagaram; failed before **Madras** (no money, no naval support) and in a raid on **Thanjavur** for funds.
- Lally **recalled Bussy from Hyderabad** – a blunder that cost the French the Deccan; he quarrelled with his officers, did not understand Indian conditions and left troops unpaid.
- **Battle of Wandiwash (Vandavasi, Tamil Nadu), 22 January 1760:** **General Eyre Coote** (English) routed **Count de Lally** (French); **Bussy taken prisoner**.
- Result: **British defeated the French** – not the Portuguese, Dutch, Danes, Mysore or the Carnatic Nawab; French political power in India ended.
- Wandiwash **removed the last European rival** to British power (the Dutch had lost at **Bidara, 1759**); some historians call it the true turning point, though Plassey is usually called decisive.
- **Siege of Pondicherry:** Lally held it **eight months**, surrendered **16 January 1761**; **Jinji** and **Mahe** also fell; Lally was later executed in France (1766).
| Person | Side | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Eyre Coote** | English | Won Wandiwash (not Clive) |
| **Pocock** | English | Admiral; beat d'Aché |
| **Count de Lally** | French | Fort St. David 1758; lost Wandiwash; surrendered Pondicherry 1761 |
| **Bussy** | French | Taken prisoner at Wandiwash |
| **d'Aché** | French | Admiral; fleet beaten off Pondicherry, retired to Mauritius |
> Lally surrendered at **Pondicherry (1761)**; Bussy was captured at **Wandiwash (1760)**.

=== Why the English Won and the French Failed
| Factor | English | French |
|---|---|---|
| Company | **Private enterprise**; board of directors elected yearly; quick decisions | **State concern** – controlled by the French government (king held over 60% of shares; dividend guaranteed) |
| Navy | **Superior navy** cut the sea link to France | Fleet beaten; no sea support |
| Money | Commercial interests never neglected; **Bengal's revenue and men after Plassey (1757)** | Commerce subordinated to territorial ambition → short of funds |
| Bases | **Calcutta, Bombay, Madras** | Only **Pondicherry** |
| Leaders | **Eyre Coote, Stringer Lawrence, Clive** | Dupleix (recalled 1754), Lally |
- Strongest single answer to "why the French failed in the Deccan": **the English were the stronger military power** (navy, army, commanders) – not "Pondicherry was not strategic".
- **CDS 2011:** the French company was **heavily dependent on the French government**.
- The Company's wars were paid for from **money raised in India** – local taxes in its fortified towns and, after 1757-65, **Bengal's revenue** – not by the British government.
- **Plassey → Carnatic:** Clive's victory made Bengal's revenue available for the southern campaign; by 1758 Clive had also taken French **Chandernagore**.
> Private + navy + Bengal money + three presidencies = English; state-run + no navy + only Pondicherry = French.

=== Treaty of Paris (1763)
- Ended the **Seven Years' War** and the **Third Carnatic War** – not London, Madras or Mahe; Allahabad (1765) is the treaty after Buxar.
- French factories **restored only as trading posts**; **could not be fortified**; French lived under British protection.
- Enclaves kept (five): **Pondicherry, Karaikal, Mahe, Yanam, Chandernagore** – **Machilipatnam was not** (it had gone to the English through Salabat Jang's 1759 grant).
- After 1763 the English were the **supreme European power** in India.

=== Andhra Theatre – Tumnapalem, Chandurti and Machilipatnam
| Event | Date | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Tumnapalem | 1756 | Bussy aided by Vizianagaram ruler **Vijayaramaraju** |
| Bobbili | 24 Jan 1757 | Bussy and Vizianagaram destroyed Bobbili |
| **Chandurti (Condore)**, near Rajahmundry | **7 Dec 1758** | **Col. Forde** (sent by Clive from Bengal) with **Ananda Gajapati** beat the French under **Conflans** |
| Machilipatnam taken | April 1759 | Salabat Jang changed sides |
| Konduru | – | Ananda Gajapati beat **Jagapati Raju of Peddapuram** |
- Chandurti **drove the French out of the Northern Circars** – the English **first foothold** there; Malleson: "**a small battle with great results**".
- Chandurti is to British rule in **Andhra** what **Plassey** is to British rule in **India**.
- **1759:** Salabat Jang gave the English **Machilipatnam, Nizampatnam, Vakal Mannar** and part of **Kondapalli** = **first agreement between the British and Hyderabad**.
- Later milestones: **Treaty of Masulipatnam 1768**; **first Subsidiary Alliance with Hyderabad 1798**.
