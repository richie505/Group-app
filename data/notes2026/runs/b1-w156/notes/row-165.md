=== Dual Government in Bengal (1765-1772)
- **Introduced by Robert Clive** (second governorship 1765-67) after Buxar and the **First Treaty of Allahabad** (Diwani from **Shah Alam II**, tribute **Rs 26 lakh**).
- **Period: 1765-1772** – not 1764-72 or 1765-70.
| Diwani (Company) | Nizamat (Nawab) |
|---|---|
| Revenue collection and civil justice – real power, no responsibility | **Police, criminal justice**, law and order – responsibility without power or funds |
- Company got diwani from the emperor and controlled nizamat through its right to nominate the **deputy subahdar**; it collected revenue through **Indian deputies**:
| Naib Diwan | Region |
|---|---|
| **Mohammad Reza Khan** (Deputy Diwan of Murshidabad, appointed by Clive; also deputy nazim) | **Bengal** |
| **Raja Shitab Rai** | **Bihar** |
- **Why "dual":** hide the Company's imperial role; avoid British Parliament's scrutiny; other Europeans kept paying duties to the "Nawab" – **not** because the Company had enough British manpower (it lacked it).
- Criticised as "**power divorced from responsibility**"; conflict of the two sidelined the local nobility and bred resentment.
- **Abolished 1772 by Warren Hastings** (Court of Directors' decision) – he "tore the mask of Mughal sovereignty"; the Calcutta council acted as Diwan; **treasury Murshidabad → Calcutta**; Reza Khan and Shitab Rai removed.
- Hastings = **last Governor of Bengal (1772-73)** and **first Governor-General of Bengal (1773)**; **first Governor of Bengal = Clive**.
- Company debt → government loan (1772) → **Regulating Act, 1773**.
> Clive introduced (1765), Hastings abolished (1772).

=== Consequences and the Bengal Famine of 1770
- In under two decades **Bengal, the richest province**, was reduced to **acute poverty, famines and epidemics**.
- **Weavers** forced to sell to Company agents at low prices; Company servants misused **dastaks** for private trade (Mir Qasim had abolished internal duties for all in protest).
- **R.P. Dutt:** Bengal's surplus revenue drained in the **mercantilist phase, 1757-1813**.
- **Famine of 1770:** first major famine under Company rule; struck **Bengal and Bihar**; about **ten million** died – about **one-third (≈33%)** of Bengal's people.
- Causes: monsoon failure; **land revenue demanded in cash** kept rising, peasants sold grain; directly linked to the **Dual Government**.
- Company gave **little or no relief** and **kept collecting revenue**; Governor at the time: **John Cartier** (1769-72), not Hastings.
- Andhra's **Guntur (Dokkala) famine, 1832-33** is a separate event.

=== Sanyasi-Fakir Revolt and Bankim Chandra's Anandamath
- **Sanyasi (Fakir) Revolt, 1763-1800** (Bengal): causes – **famine of 1770**, Company insensitivity, **restrictions on visits to holy places**; sanyasis raided Company factories and treasuries; subdued by **Warren Hastings**.
- Leaders: **Majnum Shah**, Chirag Ali, Musa Shah, **Bhawani Pathak**, **Debi Chaudhurani** – not Shah Alam II.
- **Anandamath (1882)** by **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** (not Sarat Chandra) – based on the **Sanyasi revolt**, set in the 1770 famine; contains **Vande Mataram**.
| Work / fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Durgeshnandini (1865)** | First Bengali historical novel – Pathan–Mughal conflict in Akbar's reign |
| **Sitaram (1887)** | His last novel (Spectrum calls Rajasimha his finale – its enlarged edition was 1893) |
| First graduates of Calcutta University (1858) | **Bankim** and **Jodu Nath Bose** |
| Birthplace | **Naihati** (Kanthalpara) – Rail Coach Factory and Museum, Rail Budget 2012-13 |
