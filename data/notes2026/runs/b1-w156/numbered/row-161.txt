## SUB 0 | Battle of Wandiwash (1760) and Lally's Campaign
[0:0] - **Third Carnatic War (1758-63)** = Indian theatre of the **Seven Years' War (1756-63)**; NextGen dates it 1757-63, some books 1756-63.
[0:1] - **1758:** **Count de Lally** took **Fort St. David** (Cuddalore) and Vizianagaram; failed before **Madras** (no money, no naval support) and in a raid on **Thanjavur** for funds.
[0:2] - Lally **recalled Bussy from Hyderabad** – a blunder that cost the French the Deccan; he quarrelled with his officers, did not understand Indian conditions and left troops unpaid.
[0:3] - **Battle of Wandiwash (Vandavasi, Tamil Nadu), 22 January 1760:** **General Eyre Coote** (English) routed **Count de Lally** (French); **Bussy taken prisoner**.
[0:4] - Result: **British defeated the French** – not the Portuguese, Dutch, Danes, Mysore or the Carnatic Nawab; French political power in India ended.
[0:5] - Wandiwash **removed the last European rival** to British power (the Dutch had lost at **Bidara, 1759**); some historians call it the true turning point, though Plassey is usually called decisive.
[0:6] - **Siege of Pondicherry:** Lally held it **eight months**, surrendered **16 January 1761**; **Jinji** and **Mahe** also fell; Lally was later executed in France (1766).
[0:7] TABLE head: Person | Side | Role
  [0:7.0] Eyre Coote | English | Won Wandiwash (not Clive)
  [0:7.1] Pocock | English | Admiral; beat d'Aché
  [0:7.2] Count de Lally | French | Fort St. David 1758; lost Wandiwash; surrendered Pondicherry 1761
  [0:7.3] Bussy | French | Taken prisoner at Wandiwash
  [0:7.4] d'Aché | French | Admiral; fleet beaten off Pondicherry, retired to Mauritius
[0:8] > Lally surrendered at **Pondicherry (1761)**; Bussy was captured at **Wandiwash (1760)**.
[0:9] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.81-82; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.18; PMF Modern History Part 2 p.18-20; old app notes row 161
## SUB 1 | Why the English Won and the French Failed
[1:0] TABLE head: Factor | English | French
  [1:0.0] Company | Private enterprise; board of directors elected yearly; quick decisions | State concern – controlled by the French government (king held over 60% of shares; dividend guaranteed)
  [1:0.1] Navy | Superior navy cut the sea link to France | Fleet beaten; no sea support
  [1:0.2] Money | Commercial interests never neglected; Bengal's revenue and men after Plassey (1757) | Commerce subordinated to territorial ambition → short of funds
  [1:0.3] Bases | Calcutta, Bombay, Madras | Only Pondicherry
  [1:0.4] Leaders | Eyre Coote, Stringer Lawrence, Clive | Dupleix (recalled 1754), Lally
[1:1] - Strongest single answer to "why the French failed in the Deccan": **the English were the stronger military power** (navy, army, commanders) – not "Pondicherry was not strategic".
[1:2] - **CDS 2011:** the French company was **heavily dependent on the French government**.
[1:3] - The Company's wars were paid for from **money raised in India** – local taxes in its fortified towns and, after 1757-65, **Bengal's revenue** – not by the British government.
[1:4] - **Plassey → Carnatic:** Clive's victory made Bengal's revenue available for the southern campaign; by 1758 Clive had also taken French **Chandernagore**.
[1:5] > Private + navy + Bengal money + three presidencies = English; state-run + no navy + only Pondicherry = French.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.83, 85-86, 117-118; PMF Modern History Part 2 p.19-20; old app notes row 161
## SUB 2 | Treaty of Paris (1763)
[2:0] - Ended the **Seven Years' War** and the **Third Carnatic War** – not London, Madras or Mahe; Allahabad (1765) is the treaty after Buxar.
[2:1] - French factories **restored only as trading posts**; **could not be fortified**; French lived under British protection.
[2:2] - Enclaves kept (five): **Pondicherry, Karaikal, Mahe, Yanam, Chandernagore** – **Machilipatnam was not** (it had gone to the English through Salabat Jang's 1759 grant).
[2:3] - After 1763 the English were the **supreme European power** in India.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 2 p.19-20; Spectrum Modern India p.82, 90; old app notes row 161
## SUB 3 | Andhra Theatre – Tumnapalem, Chandurti and Machilipatnam
[3:0] TABLE head: Event | Date | Result
  [3:0.0] Tumnapalem | 1756 | Bussy aided by Vizianagaram ruler Vijayaramaraju
  [3:0.1] Bobbili | 24 Jan 1757 | Bussy and Vizianagaram destroyed Bobbili
  [3:0.2] Chandurti (Condore), near Rajahmundry | 7 Dec 1758 | Col. Forde (sent by Clive from Bengal) with Ananda Gajapati beat the French under Conflans
  [3:0.3] Machilipatnam taken | April 1759 | Salabat Jang changed sides
  [3:0.4] Konduru | – | Ananda Gajapati beat Jagapati Raju of Peddapuram
[3:1] - Chandurti **drove the French out of the Northern Circars** – the English **first foothold** there; Malleson: "**a small battle with great results**".
[3:2] - Chandurti is to British rule in **Andhra** what **Plassey** is to British rule in **India**.
[3:3] - **1759:** Salabat Jang gave the English **Machilipatnam, Nizampatnam, Vakal Mannar** and part of **Kondapalli** = **first agreement between the British and Hyderabad**.
[3:4] - Later milestones: **Treaty of Masulipatnam 1768**; **first Subsidiary Alliance with Hyderabad 1798**.
