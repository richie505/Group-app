## SUB 0 | Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757)
[0:0] - **Date/place:** **23 June 1757**, **Palashi (Plassey)** on the **Bhagirathi**, **Nadia district, West Bengal** (south of the Nawab's capital Murshidabad) – not Bihar or Orissa.
[0:1] - **Sides:** **Robert Clive** (Company, about 3,000) defeated **Siraj-ud-daula** (about 50,000); **Mir Jafar** was a conspirator on Clive's side, not the opponent.
[0:2] - **Conspirators:** **Mir Jafar** (Mir Bakshi, commander), **Rai Durlabh** (Diwan), **Jagat Seth** ("world banker"), **Omichund** (deceived with a decoy treaty), Yar Lutuf Khan.
[0:3] - **Loyal to Siraj:** **Mir Madan** and **Mohan Lal**.
[0:4] - Siraj was captured and murdered on the order of Mir Jafar's son **Miran**.
[0:5] - **Significance:** the **Company's first decisive military success**; regarded as the **foundation / starting point of British rule** in India; paved the way for **British mastery of Bengal**; Bengal's wealth began to flow to Britain (start of the drain).
[0:6] - Won by **treachery** – "a transaction", not a prolonged contest between equal armies; Buxar (1764) **confirmed** it.
[0:7] - **Gains:** Mir Jafar Nawab; **zamindari of the 24 Parganas**; duty-free trade; Bengal's money used to defeat the French.
[0:8] - **Siyar-ul-Mutakherin** (contemporary Persian history covering Plassey) – **Ghulam Husain** Khan Tabatabai.
[0:9] - **Robert Clive** = "**Founder of the British Empire in India**"; "**heaven-born general**" (**William Pitt the Elder**).

## SUB 1 | Siraj-ud-daula and the English – Calcutta, Black Hole, Alinagar
[1:0] TABLE head: Cause | Point
  [1:0.0] Misuse of dastaks | Company servants used the 1717 duty-free pass for private trade → loss of revenue to the Nawab
  [1:0.1] Fortification of Calcutta | Without permission; Siraj ordered both companies to stop – the French obeyed, the English did not
  [1:0.2] Asylum | The English sheltered the fugitive Krishna Das (son of Raj Ballabh) with his treasure
  [1:0.3] Court factions | English seen backing Ghaseti Begum and Shaukat Jang of Purnea
[1:1] - **Events of 1756-57 in order:**
[1:2] TABLE head: Date | Event
  [1:2.0] May 1756 | Kasimbazar factory seized – first act
  [1:2.1] June 1756 | Calcutta fell; Governor Drake fled to Fulta; Holwell surrendered
  [1:2.2] 20 June 1756 | Black Hole episode
  [1:2.3] 1756 | Siraj killed Shaukat Jang at Manihari
  [1:2.4] Jan 1757 | Clive and Admiral Watson (from Madras) retook Calcutta
  [1:2.5] 9 Feb 1757 | Treaty of Alinagar – signed by Siraj-ud-daula: restored the 1717 privileges and allowed fortification of Calcutta; Clive then took French Chandernagore
[1:3] - **Black Hole:** about **146** English prisoners in a cell 18 ft × 14 ft 10 in; **123** said to have died; the story rests mainly on **J.Z. Holwell**'s account; historians doubt the numbers (NextGen gives Holwell's 165-170 in, 16 out); Siraj was not personally responsible.
[1:4] - The Black Hole was in **1756**, not 1747.

## SUB 2 | Nawabs of Bengal – Murshid Quli Khan to Alivardi Khan
[2:0] TABLE head: Nawab | Period | Note
  [2:0.0] Murshid Quli Khan | Diwan 1700; Nawab 1717-27 | Founder of the autonomous Bengal Nawabi; moved capital Dacca → Murshidabad
  [2:0.1] Shuja-ud-din | 1727-39 | Son-in-law of Murshid Quli
  [2:0.2] Sarfaraz Khan | 1739-40 | Shuja-ud-din's son; killed by Alivardi at Giria (1740)
  [2:0.3] Alivardi Khan | 1740-56 | Earlier Deputy Governor of Bihar; banned English and French fortification
  [2:0.4] Siraj-ud-daula | 1756-57 | Grandson of Alivardi; ruled immediately after him
[2:1] - **Last Subahdar appointed by the Mughal emperor:** most books and keys – **Murshid Quli Khan** (later Nawabs still bought imperial sanction: Alivardi paid Muhammad Shah; Siraj got a farman).
[2:2] - **Revenue:** **ijaradari** (= **mal jasmani**) – land revenue collected through **contractors**; **jagir land turned into khalisa** = brought under direct state revenue administration.
[2:3] - **Jagat Seths** (world bankers) rose under these Nawabs; **Mir Jafar** was the puppet after Plassey, **not** the founder of the Nawabi.
