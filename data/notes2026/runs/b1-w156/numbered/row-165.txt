## SUB 0 | Dual Government in Bengal (1765-1772)
[0:0] - **Introduced by Robert Clive** (second governorship 1765-67) after Buxar and the **First Treaty of Allahabad** (Diwani from **Shah Alam II**, tribute **Rs 26 lakh**).
[0:1] - **Period: 1765-1772** – not 1764-72 or 1765-70.
[0:2] TABLE head: Diwani (Company) | Nizamat (Nawab)
  [0:2.0] Revenue collection and civil justice – real power, no responsibility | Police, criminal justice, law and order – responsibility without power or funds
[0:3] - Company got diwani from the emperor and controlled nizamat through its right to nominate the **deputy subahdar**; it collected revenue through **Indian deputies**:
[0:4] TABLE head: Naib Diwan | Region
  [0:4.0] Mohammad Reza Khan (Deputy Diwan of Murshidabad, appointed by Clive; also deputy nazim) | Bengal
  [0:4.1] Raja Shitab Rai | Bihar
[0:5] - **Why "dual":** hide the Company's imperial role; avoid British Parliament's scrutiny; other Europeans kept paying duties to the "Nawab" – **not** because the Company had enough British manpower (it lacked it).
[0:6] - Criticised as "**power divorced from responsibility**"; conflict of the two sidelined the local nobility and bred resentment.
[0:7] - **Abolished 1772 by Warren Hastings** (Court of Directors' decision) – he "tore the mask of Mughal sovereignty"; the Calcutta council acted as Diwan; **treasury Murshidabad → Calcutta**; Reza Khan and Shitab Rai removed.
[0:8] - Hastings = **last Governor of Bengal (1772-73)** and **first Governor-General of Bengal (1773)**; **first Governor of Bengal = Clive**.
[0:9] - Company debt → government loan (1772) → **Regulating Act, 1773**.
[0:10] > Clive introduced (1765), Hastings abolished (1772).
[0:11] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.125; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.16-17; old app notes row 165
## SUB 1 | Consequences and the Bengal Famine of 1770
[1:0] - In under two decades **Bengal, the richest province**, was reduced to **acute poverty, famines and epidemics**.
[1:1] - **Weavers** forced to sell to Company agents at low prices; Company servants misused **dastaks** for private trade (Mir Qasim had abolished internal duties for all in protest).
[1:2] - **R.P. Dutt:** Bengal's surplus revenue drained in the **mercantilist phase, 1757-1813**.
[1:3] - **Famine of 1770:** first major famine under Company rule; struck **Bengal and Bihar**; about **ten million** died – about **one-third (≈33%)** of Bengal's people.
[1:4] - Causes: monsoon failure; **land revenue demanded in cash** kept rising, peasants sold grain; directly linked to the **Dual Government**.
[1:5] - Company gave **little or no relief** and **kept collecting revenue**; Governor at the time: **John Cartier** (1769-72), not Hastings.
[1:6] - Andhra's **Guntur (Dokkala) famine, 1832-33** is a separate event.
[1:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 3 p.54, 86; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.17, 34; old app notes row 165
## SUB 2 | Sanyasi-Fakir Revolt and Bankim Chandra's Anandamath
[2:0] - **Sanyasi (Fakir) Revolt, 1763-1800** (Bengal): causes – **famine of 1770**, Company insensitivity, **restrictions on visits to holy places**; sanyasis raided Company factories and treasuries; subdued by **Warren Hastings**.
[2:1] - Leaders: **Majnum Shah**, Chirag Ali, Musa Shah, **Bhawani Pathak**, **Debi Chaudhurani** – not Shah Alam II.
[2:2] - **Anandamath (1882)** by **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** (not Sarat Chandra) – based on the **Sanyasi revolt**, set in the 1770 famine; contains **Vande Mataram**.
[2:3] TABLE head: Work / fact | Detail
  [2:3.0] Durgeshnandini (1865) | First Bengali historical novel – Pathan–Mughal conflict in Akbar's reign
  [2:3.1] Sitaram (1887) | His last novel (Spectrum calls Rajasimha his finale – its enlarged edition was 1893)
  [2:3.2] First graduates of Calcutta University (1858) | Bankim and Jodu Nath Bose
  [2:3.3] Birthplace | Naihati (Kanthalpara) – Rail Coach Factory and Museum, Rail Budget 2012-13
