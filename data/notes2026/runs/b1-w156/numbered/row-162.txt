## SUB 1 | First Carnatic War (1746-48)
[1:0] - Fought between the **British and the French** companies; an **extension of the Austrian War of Succession**.
[1:1] - **Immediate cause:** the English navy under **Barnett** **seized French ships**; France retaliated by seizing **Madras (Fort St. George), 1746** with **La Bourdonnais**'s fleet from Mauritius.
[1:2] - La Bourdonnais agreed to **ransom Madras** back to the English, but **Dupleix overruled him** and kept it.
[1:3] - **Battle of St. Thome (Adyar), 1746:** **Captain Paradis** with about **930** French beat about **10,000** men of **Nawab Anwaruddin** (under Mahfuz Khan); the Nawab had sided with the English.
[1:4] - Ended by **Aix-la-Chapelle (1748)**; did **not** give permanent British supremacy.
[1:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.76-77; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.18; old app notes row 162
## SUB 2 | Second Carnatic War (1749-54) – Dupleix, Ambur and Arcot
[2:0] TABLE head: Dispute | English candidate | French candidate
  [2:0.0] Hyderabad (Nizam-ul-Mulk died 1748) | Nasir Jang (son) | Muzaffar Jang (grandson)
  [2:0.1] Carnatic | Anwaruddin, then his son Muhammad Ali | Chanda Sahib
[2:1] - **Dupleix** – Governor of Pondicherry (from **1742**; Director-General 1741), first European to interfere in Indian succession politics; originator of the subsidiary-force idea (French army at Hyderabad).
[2:2] - **Ambur (near Vellore), 1749:** Muzaffar Jang, Chanda Sahib and the French killed **Anwaruddin**; Muzaffar Jang became Subahdar of the Deccan and **rewarded the French** with grants (Masulipatnam, lands near Pondicherry).
[2:3] - **Arcot, 1751:** **Clive** seized it with about **210** men and held it **53 days**; then won **Arni and Kaveripak** over **Raja Sahib and Chanda Sahib**.
[2:4] - **Trichinopoly:** **Stringer Lawrence** and Clive forced the French commander **Law** to surrender (1752); Chanda Sahib executed.
[2:5] - **Bussy** kept **Salabat Jang** in power at Hyderabad and won the **Northern Circars (1753)** for the French.
[2:6] - **Godeheu** replaced Dupleix and signed the **Treaty of Pondicherry (1754)**: no interference in princes' quarrels, each kept its possessions; the **Seven Years' War broke out before it was ratified** → Third war.
[2:7] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.77-81; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.18; old app notes row 162
