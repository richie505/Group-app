=== NOTES 1:118 | The Five Deccan Sultanates – Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmednagar, Bidar, Berar; Golconda and Andhra
## SUB 0 | The Five Deccan Sultanates
[118:0:0] - The **Bahmani** kingdom split into **five** successor states.
[118:0:1] TABLE head: Dynasty | Capital | Founder, year | End
  [118:0:1.0] Adil Shahi | Bijapur | Yusuf Adil Shah, 1489 (first to break away) | Aurangzeb, 1686
  [118:0:1.1] Nizam Shahi | Ahmednagar | Malik Ahmad (Ahmad Nizam Shah), 1490 – founded Ahmadnagar city | Shah Jahan, 1633
  [118:0:1.2] Imad Shahi | Berar (capital Ellichpur / Achalpur) | Fathullah Imad-ul-Mulk, 1490 | Annexed by Ahmadnagar, 1574
  [118:0:1.3] Barid Shahi | Bidar | Amir Ali Barid – "cunning fox of the Deccan" | Bijapur, 1619
  [118:0:1.4] Qutb Shahi | Golconda, later Hyderabad | Sultan Quli (Quli Qutb Shah), 1518 | Aurangzeb, 1687
[118:0:2] Bijapur = first to break away (1489); Golconda = last to break away (1518) and last to fall (1687).
[118:0:3] "Berar was founded by" = **Imad Shahis** (APPSC G2 2005).
## SUB 1 | Qutb Shahi Dynasty – Basics and Rulers
[118:1:0] - **1518–1687**, **7 rulers**, **Shia**; **Qara Qoyunlu (Black Sheep) Turkmen** from **Hamadan**; official language **Persian**.
[118:1:1] TABLE head: Ruler | Key contribution
  [118:1:1.0] Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk (1518–43) | Bahmani tarafdar of Telangana from 1496; title Qutb-ul-Mulk named the dynasty; independent 1518; ruled 31 years (counted from 1512); defeated by Krishnadevaraya at Goni (1517) / Battle of Golconda; strengthened Golconda fort, built Golconda Jama Masjid; killed by son Jamsheed
  [118:1:1.1] Jamsheed (1543–50) | Took throne by patricide; won Battle of Koulan over Bidar (1544); created cabinet – Wakil, Peshwa, Mir Jumla
  [118:1:1.2] Ibrahim Qutb Shah (1550–80) | 7 years exile at Vijayanagara; title Malkibharama (from subjects for tolerant rule); chief Telugu patron; led Talikota (1565) alliance; Hussain Sagar (1562); first Qutb Shahi coins; built high walls of Golconda; Golconda called "Second Egypt"
  [118:1:1.3] Muhammad Quli (1580–1612) | Founded Hyderabad (1591); Charminar, Dar-ul-Shifa (1595), Jama Masjid (1598); Dakhni poet "Maani" (*Kulliyat-e-Quli Qutb Shah*); let Dutch (1605) and English (1611) trade at Machilipatnam
  [118:1:1.4] Muhammad Qutb Shah (1612–26) | Nephew and son-in-law of Muhammad Quli; began Mecca Masjid (1614); pen name Zillullah
  [118:1:1.5] Abdullah Qutb Shah (1626–72) | Longest reign; with mother Hayat Bakshi Begum; Inqiyad Nama (1636) – accepted Mughal suzerainty; Bangaru Farmana (1632) to the English; Kshetrayya; Tavernier visited; Urdu peaked
  [118:1:1.6] Abul Hasan Tana Shah (1672–87) | Last ruler; relied on Madanna-Akkanna; treaty with Shivaji (1677); renamed Peshwa as Diwan
[118:1:2] Coinage does not explain the title Malkibharama – it came from the subjects for tolerant rule.
## SUB 2 | Andhra Under Golconda – Conquests and Society
[118:2:0] - **Sultan Quli** took **Rachakonda, Devarakonda, Warangal** (Shitab Khan), **Rajahmundry**, Eluru, Kovilakonda.
[118:2:1] - **Ibrahim** gained **Kondavidu** and Machilipatnam after 1565; annexed coastal Andhra (1570–78).
[118:2:2] - Qutb Shahis united the Telugu lands for the **third time** (after **Satavahanas** and **Kakatiyas**).
[118:2:3] - Hindus in high office: **Murari Rao, Jagadeva Rao, Madanna**.
[118:2:4] - Muharram celebrated in Andhra as **Peerla Panduga**.
[118:2:5] - **Bonalu** began at **Jagadamba Mahankali temple**, Golconda fort; roots in Kakatiya and Qutb Shahi periods.
## SUB 3 | Qutb Shahi Administration
[118:3:0] - Hierarchy: **Sultan → Taraf → Sarkar → Pargana → Village**.
[118:3:1] TABLE head: Official | Duty
  [118:3:1.0] Peshwa / Wakil | Prime minister; renamed Diwan under Abul Hasan (held by Madanna)
  [118:3:1.1] Mir Jumla (Jumlat-ul-Mulk) | Finance minister
  [118:3:1.2] Ain-ul-Mulk | Military department
  [118:3:1.3] Tarafdar | Governor of a Taraf: law and order, revenue, troops
  [118:3:1.4] Faujdar | Head of a Sarkar
  [118:3:1.5] Tahsildar / Deshmukh | Head of a Pargana
  [118:3:1.6] Mukaddam / Kulkarni | Village head / accountant
  [118:3:1.7] Kotwal | City police; also ran the mint
  [118:3:1.8] Mahasibs / Mustasibs | Public morals (like Mughal Muhtasib)
  [118:3:1.9] Shahbandar | Chief port officer, customs
  [118:3:1.10] Majumdar / Dabir | Auditor / secretary
  [118:3:1.11] Kayastha | Scribes (APPSC G2 2012)
[118:3:2] - Abdullah: **6 Tarafs**; Abul Hasan: **37 Sarkars**, **517 Parganas**.
[118:3:3] - Justice: Sultan → **Shariat Panch** (chief justice) → **Qazi** → **Pargana Majlis**.
[118:3:4] - **Sar Lashkar** = army chief (Akkanna).
[118:3:5] 6 Tarafs – 37 Sarkars – 517 Parganas = Tarafdar – Faujdar – Tahsildar
## SUB 4 | Revenue
[118:4:0] - **Main source: land revenue**, collected by **tax farming** – rights auctioned to the highest bidder.
[118:4:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [118:4:1.0] Mustajirs | Tax farmers (highest bidders, mostly Hindus); could be harsh
  [118:4:1.1] Haveli | Crown lands
  [118:4:1.2] Dasabandhu lands | Paid only one-tenth
[118:4:2] - Revenue farming example: **Machilipatnam** leased for **1,80,000 pagodas** a year.
[118:4:3] - Other sources: trade taxes, port customs, taxes on courtesans and professions (military service did not replace taxes).
[118:4:4] - **State monopolies: salt, betel leaf, tobacco**.

  [118:5:0.1] Titles | Surya Prakasa Rao, Alampana | –

[118:5:2] - Nephews: **Podili Linganna** – governor of the Karnatak; **Kancherla Gopanna (Bhakta Ramadasu)** – tahsildar of **Palvancha/Bhadrachalam**.

[118:5:4] - **Murdered 1686**, just before Golconda fell (1687).

## SUB 7 | Diamonds and Machilipatnam Trade
[118:7:0] TABLE head: Mine | Region
  [118:7:0.0] Kollur | On the Krishna (Guntur / Palnadu)
  [118:7:0.1] Paritala | Krishna
[118:7:1] - Diamonds traded at **Moti Darwaza**, polished at **Karwan**.
[118:7:2] - **Kohinoor**: from **Kollur** (APPSC G2 2011), found **1656**; given to **Shah Jahan** by **Mir Jumla** (minister of Abdullah); **Tavernier only recorded it**.
[118:7:3] - **Tavernier**: **French** jeweller; visited the mines under **Abdullah Qutb Shah** (APPSC G2 2019); wrote *Six Travels*.
[118:7:4] - **Duarte Barbosa** (Portuguese, early 16th c.) also described the region's **diamond trade**; Tavernier gave the fullest account of the mines.
[118:7:5] TABLE head: Company | Machilipatnam factory
  [118:7:5.0] Dutch | 1605 – first European factory in Andhra
  [118:7:5.1] English | 1611 – Captain Hippon, ship Globe; permission of Muhammad Quli
[118:7:6] - Machilipatnam famous for **muslin** and **Kalamkari**; also the outlet for Golconda **diamonds** (Kollur).
[118:7:7] - **Narsapuram** (Godavari delta): chief **shipbuilding** port; Machilipatnam was mainly a trade port.
[118:7:8] - Latest: Machilipatnam port works revive the old **Gilakaladindi** port (Golconda-era hub); **Golconda Blue** diamond (**23.24 ct**, fancy vivid blue, pear-shaped) in news **May 2025**.
[118:7:9] English arrival = **Muhammad Quli** (APPSC 2025); if he is not an option, choose Ibrahim (older G2 2008 key).

## SUB 9 | Bijapur and Ahmednagar
[118:9:0] # Ibrahim Adil Shah II (Bijapur, 1580–1627)
[118:9:1] - Called **"Jagadguru"** for his mixed-faith outlook; supported Hindu scholars.
[118:9:2] - ***Kitab-i-Nauras*** (Book of Nine Rasas) in **Dakhni** – songs to **Saraswati, Ganesha** and Muslim saints; **59 songs, 17 couplets**.
[118:9:3] - Founded **Nauraspur**; **Firishta** dedicated his work to him; married **Chand Sultana** (Muhammad Quli's sister).
[118:9:4] # Chand Bibi and Malik Ambar (Ahmednagar)
[118:9:5] - **Chand Bibi**: daughter of **Hussain Nizam Shah I**; married **Ali Adil Shah** → anti-Vijayanagara alliance; defended Ahmadnagar against Mughals (1595–96); died when it fell (**1600**).
[118:9:6] - **Malik Ambar**: **Abyssinian** regent; fought **Jahangir**; revenue on **Todar Mal's lines** – **survey, fixed assessment, no revenue farming**; founded **Khirki → Aurangabad**; son Fath Khan made peace.
[118:9:7] **Chand Sultana** (wife of Ibrahim Adil Shah II) is NOT **Chand Bibi** (wife of Ali Adil Shah).
[118:9:8] - **Ismail Adil Shah (Bijapur):** lost the **Battle of Raichur (1520)** to **Krishnadevaraya** of Vijayanagara, who then took the **Raichur fort**.
## SUB 10 | Mughal Annexation and Fall of Golconda
[118:10:0] - **Talikota (1565)**: four of the five – **all except Berar** – defeated Vijayanagara.
[118:10:1] TABLE head: Emperor | Action
  [118:10:1.0] Akbar | Ahmadnagar city (1600); Asirgarh/Khandesh (1601)
  [118:10:1.1] Shah Jahan | Ended Nizam Shahis (1633) – last ruler Husain Shah (Nizam Shah III) jailed at Gwalior; 1636 ahdnamas with Bijapur and Golconda; Mahawat Khan took Daulatabad (Fateh Khan bribed 10.5 lakh); Shahji Bhonsle then set up a puppet, Murtaza Nizam Shah III (1634), and held out till 1636, when the last Nizam Shahi lands were split between the Mughals and Bijapur
  [118:10:1.2] Aurangzeb | Attacked Golconda (1656) as subedar; annexed Bijapur (1686), Golconda (1687)
[118:10:2] - **21 September 1687**: traitor **Abdullah Khan Pani** opened the gates; **Abdur Razzaq Lari** honoured for the defence.
[118:10:3] - **Abul Hasan** imprisoned at Bidar, then **Daulatabad**; died **1699**; buried at **Khuldabad**.
[118:10:4] - Golconda became the **21st Mughal Suba**.
[118:10:5] 1600 Ahmadnagar city – 1633 Nizam Shahi – 1686 Bijapur – 1687 Golconda
