## SUB 0 | Kohinoor, Golconda Diamonds and the English at Machilipatnam
[0:0] # Kohinoor – the most-asked fact of this topic
[0:1] - **Mine:** the **Kohinoor** came from the **Kollur (Kolluru)** mine on the **Krishna** (Guntur / Palnadu) – APPSC G2 2011; AP History PYQ ×2. Not Paritala, Gollapalli, Malapalli, Panna or Hazaribagh.
[0:2] - **Kingdom:** Kollur lay in the **Golconda** kingdom, so "excavated from the mines of" = **Golconda** (SSC 2006) – not Bijapur, Odisha or Chota Nagpur.
[0:3] - **Found:** **1656** (NextGen); it is now in the British Crown Jewels.
[0:4] - **Presented to Shah Jahan by:** **Mir Jumla**, minister of **Abdullah Qutb Shah**, who went over to the Mughals (UPPSC 2015 ×2) – not Aurangzeb, Murad or Abul Hasan.
[0:5] - **⚠ Trap:** **Tavernier**, the **French** jeweller who visited the mines under **Abdullah Qutb Shah** and wrote *Six Travels*, only **recorded** the Kohinoor; he did not give it.
[0:6] - **Duarte Barbosa** (Portuguese, early 16th c.) also described the region's **diamond trade**; Tavernier gave the fullest account.
[0:7] # Golconda diamond mines and trade
[0:8] TABLE head: Mine | Region
  [0:8.0] Kollur | Krishna river, Guntur – the Kohinoor
  [0:8.1] Paritala | Krishna district
  [0:8.2] Ramalla Kota | Kurnool – 3 lakh pagodas a year
  [0:8.3] Vajra Karur | Anantapur
[0:9] - Diamonds were traded at **Moti Darwaza** (Golconda) and polished at **Karwan** (west of Charminar); mines were given on lease.
[0:10] - **Latest:** the **Golconda Blue** diamond (**23.24 ct**, fancy vivid blue, pear-shaped) was in the news in **May 2025**; Machilipatnam port works revive the old **Gilakaladindi** port.
[0:11] # Europeans at Machilipatnam
[0:12] TABLE head: Company | Machilipatnam factory
  [0:12.0] Dutch | 1605 – first European factory in Andhra, under Muhammad Quli
  [0:12.1] English | 1611 – Captain Hippon, ship Globe; permission of Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah
[0:13] - **Who allowed the English factory (1611):** **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** (1580–1612) – APPSC G2 2008 ×2; 2023 screening; AP History PYQ ×2. Not Ibrahim (died 1580), Abdullah (from 1626), Sultan Muhammad or Jamshid.
[0:14] - **Golden Farman (Bangaru Farmana):** **Abdullah Qutb Shah** gave the English trading rights (1632 in the old notes; NextGen dates it 1636).
[0:15] - Machilipatnam was famous for **muslin and Kalamkari** and was the outlet for Golconda diamonds; **Narsapuram** (Godavari delta) was the chief **shipbuilding** port.

## SUB 1 | Qutb Shahi Rulers – Founder to Tana Shah
[1:0] # Basics
[1:1] - **1518–1687**, **7 rulers**, **Shia**; **Qara Qoyunlu (Black Sheep) Turkmen** from **Hamadan**; official language **Persian**; capital **Golconda** (old name **Mankal**), from 1592 **Hyderabad**.
[1:2] - **"From where did the Qutb Shahis rule":** **Golconda** (G2-B 1991). **Golconda today** lies in **Hyderabad** (MPPSC 1990).
[1:3] # The seven rulers
[1:4] TABLE head: Ruler | Key facts
  [1:4.0] Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk (1518–43) | Founder (Bahmani Tarafdar of Golconda from 1496; title Qutb-ul-Mulk named the dynasty; independent 1518); 31 years counted from 1512; strengthened Golconda fort, built Golconda Jama Masjid; took Rachakonda, Devarakonda, Warangal (from Shitab Khan), Rajahmundry, Eluru, Kovilakonda; killed by son Jamsheed
  [1:4.1] Jamsheed (1543–50) | Patricide; won Battle of Koulan over Bidar (1544); created cabinet – Wakil, Peshwa, Mir Jumla; then minor son Subhan Quli
  [1:4.2] Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah (1550–80) | 7 years exile at Vijayanagara; title Malkibharama; chief Telugu patron; Talikota (1565) ally; Hussain Sagar (1562); first Qutb Shahi coins; high walls, 87 towers and 8 gates of Golconda; "Second Egypt"; gained Kondavidu after 1565
  [1:4.3] Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah (1580–1612) | Ascended 1580 at 14; founded Hyderabad (1591); Charminar, Dar-ul-Shifa (1595), Jama Masjid; Dakhni/Urdu poet "Maani" (*Kulliyat-e-Quli*); Dutch (1605) and English (1611) at Machilipatnam
  [1:4.4] Sultan Muhammad Qutb Shah (1612–26) | Nephew and son-in-law of Muhammad Quli; began Mecca Masjid (1614); pen name Zillullah
  [1:4.5] Abdullah Qutb Shah (1626–72) | Longest reign; with mother Hayat Bakshi Begum; Inqiyad Nama (1636) – accepted Mughal suzerainty; Bangaru Farmana to the English; Kshetrayya; Tavernier
  [1:4.6] Abul Hasan Tana Shah (1672–87) | Last ruler; Madanna–Akkanna; treaty with Shivaji (1677); Peshwa renamed Diwan
[1:5] - **Founder of the Golconda dynasty:** **(Sultan) Quli Qutb Shah** – not Jamshed, Subhan or Ibrahim.
[1:6] - **Malkibharama / Malikibharam:** **Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah** (G1 2002; G1 PYQ) – a title from his subjects for tolerant rule; coinage does not explain it.
[1:7] - **Last king:** **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (G1 PYQ) – title "Tanisha" from Sufi saint Shah Raju Qattal.
[1:8] - **How long Quli Qutb Shah ruled:** **31 years** (1512–1543; Deputy EO screening). ⚠ From formal independence (1518) it is only 25 years.
[1:9] - **Built by Muhammad Quli:** **Hyderabad city, Charminar, Jama Masjid** – **not Golconda** fort, which was a Kakatiya fort (Mankal) rebuilt by Sultan Quli and Ibrahim (G1 2003).
[1:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History - Qutb Shahis p.1-9; Wikipedia, Sultanate of Golconda (1512–1543); app notes 1:118 (sub 1, 2)
## SUB 2 | Ibrahim Adil Shah II of Bijapur – Jagadguru and Kitab-i-Nauras
[2:0] # Ibrahim Adil Shah II (1580–1627)
[2:1] - **"Jagadguru":** hailed so by his subjects for his **mixed-faith (secular) outlook** and support of Hindu scholars (UPSC 2000 ×3) – not Zain-ul-Abidin (Kashmir), Husain Shah (Bengal) or Mahmud II.
[2:2] - ***Kitab-i-Nauras*** (Book of Nine Rasas): written by **Ibrahim Adil Shah II** in **Dakhni** – songs to **Saraswati, Ganesha** and Muslim saints; **59 songs, 17 couplets** (UPPSC 2020).
[2:3] - **UPSC 2003 statements:** Kitab-i-Nauras by Ibrahim Adil Shah II ✓; **Amir Khusrau** originated the early **Qawwali** in India ✓ → **both correct** (asked ×3).
[2:4] - Founded **Nauraspur**; **Firishta** dedicated his history to him; a painter of the **Deccan Ragamala**; married **Chand Sultana** (Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah's sister).
[2:5] - **Bangab Nama** ("Book of the Pot Smoker"), a Dakhni poem praising bhang, was written by **Mahmud Bahri** of Bijapur (G1 PYQ) – not either Ibrahim Adil Shah or Namdev.

## SUB 4 | Ahmednagar – End of the Nizam Shahis, Chand Bibi and Malik Ambar
[4:0] # How the Nizam Shahis ended (UPSC 2004 ×3)
[4:1] - **Shah Jahan** annexed **Ahmadnagar** (**1633**) and the last ruler **Husain Shah (Nizam Shah III)** was **imprisoned for life at Gwalior**.
[4:2] - **Mahawat Khan** won **Daulatabad** by bribing **Fath Khan** (10.5 lakh); **Shahji Bhonsle** then set up a puppet, **Murtaza Nizam Shah III** (1634), and held out till **1636**, when the last lands were split between the Mughals and Bijapur.
[4:3] - **⚠ Wrong options:** "Malik Ambar defeated in 1631 and the royal family killed" (Malik Ambar died in **1626**); "Fateh Khan usurped the throne".
[4:4] # Chand Bibi and Malik Ambar
[4:5] - **Chand Bibi** is linked with **Ahmadnagar** (CG Patwari 2019): daughter of **Hussain Nizam Shah I**; married **Ali Adil Shah** of Bijapur (anti-Vijayanagara alliance); defended Ahmadnagar against the **Mughals** (1595–96); died when it fell (**1600**).
[4:6] - **⚠ Chand Sultana** (wife of Ibrahim Adil Shah II) is **not** Chand Bibi.
[4:7] - **Malik Ambar:** **Abyssinian** regent of the Nizam Shahis; fought **Jahangir**; revenue on **Todar Mal's lines** – survey, fixed assessment, **no revenue farming**; founded **Khirki → Aurangabad**.
[4:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 4 p.77; app notes 1:118 (sub 9, 10)

  [7:1.0] Adil Shahi | Bijapur | Yusuf Adil Shah, 1489/90 (first to break away) | Aurangzeb, 1686
  [7:1.1] Nizam Shahi | Ahmadnagar | Malik Ahmad (Ahmad Nizam Shah), 1490 – founded Ahmadnagar city | Shah Jahan, 1633
  [7:1.2] Imad Shahi | Berar (capital Ellichpur / Achalpur) | Fathullah Imad-ul-Mulk, 1490 | Annexed by Ahmadnagar, 1574

## SUB 8 | Mughal Annexation of the Deccan and the Fall of Golconda
[8:0] - **Talikota (1565):** four of the five – **all except Berar** – combined to defeat Vijayanagara.
[8:1] TABLE head: Emperor | Action
  [8:1.0] Akbar | Ahmadnagar city (1600); Asirgarh / Khandesh (1601)
  [8:1.1] Shah Jahan | Ended the Nizam Shahis (1633); 1636 treaties with Bijapur and Golconda (Inqiyad Nama)
  [8:1.2] Aurangzeb | Attacked Golconda (1656) as subedar; annexed Bijapur (1686) and Golconda (1687)
[8:2] - **Aurangzeb's two Deccan conquests:** **Bijapur and Golconda** (UPPSC 2004) – Ahmadnagar was Shah Jahan's, Bidar went to Bijapur.
[8:3] - **Golconda fell in 1687** (Medical Officer 2021; Dy. EO 2008): on **21 September 1687** the traitor **Abdullah Khan Pani** opened the **Fateh Darwaza**; **Abdur Razzaq Lari** fought bravely; Golconda became the **21st Mughal Suba**.
[8:4] - **Abul Hasan** was imprisoned at Bidar, then **Daulatabad**; died **1699** (NextGen: 1700); buried at **Khuldabad**.
[8:5] - **Akkanna and Madanna** were murdered in **1686**, just before the fall.
[8:6] - Order: 1600 Ahmadnagar city → 1633 Nizam Shahis → 1686 Bijapur → 1687 Golconda.

## SUB 9 | Qutb Shahi Administration and Revenue
[9:0] # Administration
[9:1] - Hierarchy: **Sultan → Taraf (Tarafdar) → Sarkar (Faujdar) → Pargana (Tahsildar) → Village**; Abdullah: **6 Tarafs**; Abul Hasan: **37 Sarkars, 517 Parganas**.
[9:2] TABLE head: Official | Duty
  [9:2.0] Peshwa / Wakil | Prime minister; renamed Diwan under Abul Hasan (held by Madanna)
  [9:2.1] Mir Jumla | Finance minister
  [9:2.2] Ain-ul-Mulk | Military department
  [9:2.3] Tarafdar | Governor of a Taraf: law and order, revenue, troops
  [9:2.4] Faujdar | Head of a Sarkar
  [9:2.5] Tahsildar / Deshmukh | Head of a Pargana
  [9:2.6] Mukaddam / Kulkarni | Village head / village accountant
  [9:2.7] Kotwal | City police; also ran the mint
  [9:2.8] Mahasibs / Mustasibs | In charge of public morals (like the Mughal Muhtasib)
  [9:2.9] Shahbandar | Chief port officer, customs
  [9:2.10] Majumdar / Dabir | Auditor / secretary
  [9:2.11] Kayastha | Scribes (record-keepers)
[9:3] - **Mahasibs / Mustasibs** (2023 screening) = **public morals** – not chief justice, Taraf head or civil cases.
[9:4] - **Kayastha** (AP History PYQ) = caste of **scribes** – not traders, physicians or professionals.
[9:5] - Justice: Sultan → **Shariat Panch** (chief justice) → **Qazi** → **Pargana Majlis**. **Sar Lashkar** = army chief (Akkanna).
[9:6] # Revenue
[9:7] - **Main source of income: land revenue** (2023 screening) – collected by **tax farming**, rights auctioned to the highest bidder; not tourism, customs or feudal levies.
[9:8] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [9:8.0] Mustajirs | Tax farmers (highest bidders, mostly Hindus)
  [9:8.1] Haveli | Crown lands
  [9:8.2] Dasabandhu lands | Paid only one-tenth
[9:9] - **Machilipatnam** was leased for **1,80,000 pagodas** a year.
[9:10] - Other sources: trade taxes, port customs, diamond-mine leases, taxes on courtesans and professions (military service did not replace taxes).
[9:11] - **State monopolies: salt, betel leaf, tobacco**.
[9:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History - Qutb Shahis p.10-13; NextGen AP History APPSC Part 7 (Kayastha); app notes 1:118 (sub 3, 4)
