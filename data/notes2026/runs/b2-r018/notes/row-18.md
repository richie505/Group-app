=== Article 368 – Where It Is, What It Says, Nature of the Procedure
- **Article 368**, the **only Article of Part XX**, gives Parliament the **power to amend the Constitution and the procedure** for it (not Arts. 14, 320, 330, 352, 356, 360, 366, 367, 369, 370 or 395).
- Wording: Parliament may, "in exercise of its **constituent power**, amend by way of **addition, variation or repeal** any provision of this Constitution in accordance with the procedure laid down" – subject to the **basic structure** (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973).
- **Art. 368 itself provides for two types** of amendment – **special majority**, and **special majority + ratification by half the States**. The **simple-majority** changes (Arts. 2-4, 169 etc.) lie **outside Art. 368**, so books also speak of "three ways" of amending the Constitution.
- **Art. 13(4)** (added by the **24th Amendment, 1971**): an amendment made under Art. 368 is **not "law"** under Art. 13, so **Art. 13(2) does not apply** to it (it still applies to laws of Parliament, ordinances and executive orders).
- Art. 368 has itself been amended by the **24th (1971)** and the **101st (2016**, GST Council Art. 279A added to the ratification list) Amendments – both statements true (MPPSC 2020).
- Borrowed from **South Africa** (with the election of Rajya Sabha members).
- Nature: **neither rigid nor flexible but a synthesis of both** – "partly rigid, partly flexible". Dual nature shown by: some provisions need a **special majority**, some only a **simple majority** like ordinary laws, and the **federal structure cannot be altered easily** (State ratification). FRs are **not** amendable "without judicial review".
- **K.C. Wheare**: the amending process strikes a **good balance between rigidity and flexibility** ("variety in the amending process is wise but rarely found"); he called the Constitution **quasi-federal**.
- **Granville Austin**: the process "appears **complicated**" but is "**merely diverse**" – one of the most ably conceived aspects of the Constitution.
- Latest enacted amendment (as of Nov 2026): the **106th (2023)**, women's reservation; the 129th and 130th are pending Bills and the 131st Bill was defeated (Apr 2026).
> Exam angle: "Which Article/Part deals with amendment?" is the single most repeated PYQ – Art. 368, Part XX.

=== Procedure under Article 368 – Step by Step
- 1. Initiated **only by a Bill in either House of Parliament** (Lok Sabha **or** Rajya Sabha) – **not** in a State Legislature and **not** by the President.
- 2. Moved by a **minister or a private member**; **no prior permission/recommendation of the President**.
- 3. Passed in **each House separately** by a **special majority** – a majority of the **total membership** and **two-thirds of members present and voting**.
- 4. **No joint sitting** (Art. 108 does not apply): if either House rejects it, the Bill **fails**.
- 5. If it touches a **federal provision**, it must also be **ratified by the legislatures of half the States** by a **simple majority** (not all States).
- 6. The **President must give assent** – he can neither withhold assent nor return the Bill; made obligatory by the **24th Amendment, 1971** (368(2) "**shall give his assent**").
- So "prior recommendation of the President" is **false**, "President must assent" is **true**, "special majority in both Houses, no joint sitting" is **true** (UPSC 2022). "Initiated in Lok Sabha only" and "ratified by all the States" are **false** (UPSC 2013).
| Basis | Ordinary Bill | Money Bill | Constitution Amendment Bill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Articles | 107, 108 | 109, 110 | **368** |
| Origin | Either House | Lok Sabha only | **Either House** |
| President's prior recommendation | Not required | Required | **Not required** |
| Majority | Simple | Simple | **Special, in each House separately** |
| Deadlock | **Joint sitting** (Art. 108) | No joint sitting (LS prevails) | **No joint sitting – Bill fails** |
| President | Assent, withhold or return once | Assent or withhold, never return | **Must assent** |
- **Financial Bill (I)**: starts only in Lok Sabha on the President's recommendation, but the Rajya Sabha **may reject or amend** it.

=== Three Kinds of Amendment and the Simple-Majority Provisions
| Route | Requirement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple majority** of Parliament (outside Art. 368) | Majority of members present and voting; such laws are **not deemed amendments** under Art. 368 | Arts. **2, 3, 4**; **169**; **citizenship**; **Fifth and Sixth Schedules**; quorum |
| **Special majority** of Parliament | Majority of total membership + **two-thirds present and voting**, each House | **Fundamental Rights (incl. Art. 32)**, **DPSPs**, all provisions not in the other two groups |
| **Special majority + ratification by half the States** | As above + simple majority in half the State legislatures | **Federal provisions** (next sub-section) |
- **New States** (admission/establishment, Art. 2), **formation of new States and alteration of areas, boundaries or names** (Art. 3) – by a **simple majority**, ordinary law; **Art. 4(2)**: such laws (and the consequent changes to the **First and Fourth Schedules**) are **not amendments for the purpose of Art. 368**. No State ratification is needed.
- **Art. 3 proviso**: the Bill needs the **President's recommendation** and is **referred to the State legislature** for its views – so "boundaries may be altered without intimation to the States" is **false**.
- **Legislative Council** (Art. 169): the **State Assembly passes a resolution by special majority** (majority of total membership + two-thirds present and voting), **followed by an Act of Parliament** by simple majority – not an Art. 368 amendment.
- Other simple-majority matters: **acquisition and termination of citizenship**; **Second Schedule** emoluments; **quorum**; MPs' salaries; rules of procedure; privileges; use of English in Parliament; **number of puisne judges of the SC** and more SC jurisdiction; **official language**; elections and **delimitation**; **Union Territories**; **Fifth Schedule** and **Sixth Schedule**.
- **Article 32** is amended by a **special majority ("two-thirds")**, **without** State ratification (APPSC G2 2017) – though the SC's power under it is part of the basic structure.
- Provisions that **cannot** be amended by simple majority and need **no** ratification → **special majority** (not ordinances, Cabinet decisions or Presidential orders).

=== Federal Provisions Needing Ratification by Half the States
- Special majority **plus** ratification by the legislatures of **not less than one-half of the States**, by **simple majority**:
| Provision | Articles / Schedule |
|---|---|
| **Election of the President and its manner** | 54, 55 |
| **Extent of the executive power** of the Union and the States | 73, 162 |
| **Supreme Court and High Courts** | Part V ch. IV, Part VI ch. V, 241 |
| **Distribution of legislative powers** between the Union and States | Part XI ch. I |
| **Any of the Lists in the Seventh Schedule** (e.g. the Union List) | Seventh Schedule |
| **Representation of States in Parliament** | **Fourth Schedule** |
| **GST Council** (added by the **101st Amendment, 2016**) | 279A |
| **Article 368 itself** | 368 |
- **Not** in the list: **qualifications, tenure, conditions of office of the President**; **conditions of the Governor's office**; Fundamental Rights; DPSPs; formation of new States; abolition of Legislative Councils; creation of UTs.
- So for **I. Union List, II. extent of a State's executive power, III. conditions of the Governor's office**, only **I and II** need ratification (UPSC 2025).
- **Not all States**: half the States are enough – it is **not necessary that all State legislatures act** on the Bill; **no time limit** for ratification.
- **First amendment ratified by the States: the 3rd Amendment (1954)** – re-enacted **Entry 33 of the Concurrent List** (trade and commerce in essential commodities).
- **Rajendra N. Shah (2021)**: Part IXB (97th, co-operatives) **struck down for State co-operatives** for **want of ratification**.

=== Majorities in Parliament
| Majority | Meaning | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | Majority of members **present and voting** | Ordinary, Money and Financial Bills; confidence motions; Arts. 2-4, 169 laws |
| **Absolute** | Majority of the **total membership** | Never alone – always with another majority |
| **Effective** | Majority of the House's **effective strength** (total minus vacancies) | Removal of the **Vice-President (RS)**, Deputy Chairman, **Speaker/Deputy Speaker** |
| **Special – Art. 249 type** | **Two-thirds of members present and voting**, **Rajya Sabha** only | Parliament legislating on a **State List** subject in the **national interest** (Art. 249); new **All-India Services** (Art. 312) |
| **Special – Art. 368 type** | Majority of **total membership + two-thirds present and voting**, each House | **Amendment**; removal of **SC/HC judges** (124, 217), CAG (148), CEC (324), SEC (243K); approval of **National Emergency** (352) |
| **Special – Art. 61 type** | **Two-thirds of the total membership** of the House | **Impeachment of the President** – the strictest |
- **High Court judges** (incl. the CJ) are removed by the **President** after an **address by Parliament** passed by a special majority (total membership + two-thirds present and voting) in each House – not by the Governor or the CJI.
- Worked example (2026): the 131st Amendment Bill got **298 for, 230 against**; two-thirds of **528 present and voting = 352**, so it **failed**.

=== Limits on the Amending Power – FRs and the Basic Structure
| Case / Act | Year | Held |
|---|---|---|
| **Shankari Prasad** | 1951 | Parliament **can amend FRs**; an amendment is **not "law"** under Art. 13(2); upheld the 1st Amendment |
| **Sajjan Singh** | 1965 | Reaffirmed Shankari Prasad |
| **Golaknath** | 1967 | Parliament **cannot** abridge FRs; an amendment **is** law – first to limit the power |
| **24th Amendment** | 1971 | Parliament can amend any part incl. FRs; Art. 13(4), 368(3); assent obligatory |
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | **24 April 1973** | **13 judges, 7:6**; FRs amendable but **not the basic structure**; upheld the 24th; overruled Golaknath |
| **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain** | 1975 | Doctrine first **applied** to strike down an amendment clause (39th) |
| **42nd Amendment** | 1976 | Added **368(4), (5)**: no limit on constituent power, no judicial review |
| **Minerva Mills** | 1980 | Struck down 368(4), (5); **limited amending power is itself a basic feature** |
| **Waman Rao** | 1981 | Doctrine applies to amendments after **24 April 1973** |
- So the power of Parliament to amend is **limited by the Constitution itself** (its basic structure); the "**unquestioned right of Parliament to amend any part**" is **not** a basic feature – FRs, independence of the judiciary and federalism are.
- **Who amends FRs?** **Parliament** (special majority) – not the Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha alone, and not the Supreme Court, which only reviews.
- Sequence: **Shankari Prasad → Golaknath → 24th Amendment → Kesavananda**.

=== Criticism of the Procedure and Amendment Bills in News
- **No special body** (convention, as in the USA) and **no referendum**; constituent power lies with **Parliament**.
- **States cannot initiate** an amendment (only a resolution for a Legislative Council under Art. 169); their role is **only to ratify** certain Bills; most of the Constitution is amended by Parliament alone.
- **No time limit** for ratification; silent on **withdrawal** of ratification; **no joint sitting**; provisions **too sketchy**.
