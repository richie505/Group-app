=== NOTES 2:18 | Amendment of the Constitution – Article 368, Majorities, Procedure, Limitations
## SUB 0 | Article 368 – Basics
[18:0:0] - **Part XX**, sole **Article 368** – Parliament's power and procedure to amend.
[18:0:1] - Wording: Parliament may, "in exercise of its **constituent power**, amend by way of **addition, variation or repeal** any provision" (within the basic-structure limit).
[18:0:2] - **Article 368(2)** mentions **two methods**; Art. 368 itself amended by the **24th (1971)** and **101st (2016)** Amendments.
[18:0:3] - Source: **South Africa** (also election of Rajya Sabha members) – not the very rigid USA.
[18:0:4] - Nature: **partly rigid, partly flexible** – a synthesis of both.
[18:0:5] - **Granville Austin**: process "appears complicated" but "is merely diverse"; one of the most ably conceived aspects.
[18:0:6] - **K.C. Wheare**: good balance between rigidity and flexibility; called the Constitution **quasi-federal**.
[18:0:7] - Latest enacted amendment (2026): **106th (2023)** – women's reservation.
## SUB 1 | Procedure – Step by Step
[18:1:0] - 1. Bill only in **either House of Parliament** – **not a State Legislature**, not the President.
[18:1:1] - 2. Moved by a **minister or private member**; **no prior permission of the President**.
[18:1:2] - 3. **Each House separately** by **special majority**; **no joint sitting** (Art. 108 does not apply) – rejection by either House means the Bill **fails**.
[18:1:3] - 4. Federal provisions: ratified by **half the States**.
[18:1:4] - 5. President **must assent** – cannot withhold or return; made obligatory by the **24th Amendment, 1971** ("shall give his assent").
[18:1:5] PYQ 2024: "prior permission", "joint sitting" and "President may return" are all FALSE for amendment Bills.

  [18:1:7.0] Articles | 107, 108 | 109, 110 | 368

[18:1:8] - **Financial Bill (I)**: starts only in Lok Sabha, but Rajya Sabha may reject or amend it.
## SUB 2 | Three Routes of Amendment
[18:2:0] TABLE head: Route | Requirement | Examples
  [18:2:0.0] Simple majority (outside 368) | Present and voting; not deemed amendments | Arts 2-4, 169, citizenship, official language, Fifth/Sixth Schedules, quorum
  [18:2:0.1] Special majority | Majority of total membership + two-thirds present and voting, each House | FRs (incl. Art. 32), DPSP, all other provisions
  [18:2:0.2] Special + State ratification | + one-half of States | Federal provisions (Section 5)
[18:2:1] - **Article 32**: special majority, **no ratification** (APPSC G2 2017) – but SC's power under it is **basic structure**.

[18:3:1] - **Only Parliament** can change a State's area, boundary or name (Art. 3); "a State may include a Union territory" is not a provision.
[18:3:2] - States can only pass a **resolution** for a Council (Art. 169).
[18:3:3] "Which is NOT amended by special majority?" – official language, citizenship, Fifth/Sixth Schedules.
## SUB 4 | Federal Provisions Needing State Ratification
[18:4:0] - **Election of the President** (Arts 54, 55) – not his **qualifications, conditions of office or tenure**.
[18:4:1] - **Extent of executive power** of Union and States (73, 162) – not conditions of the Governor's office.
[18:4:2] - **Supreme Court and High Courts** (incl. Art. 241).
[18:4:3] - **Seventh Schedule** – any List (e.g. Union List), distribution of legislative powers.
[18:4:4] - **Representation of States in Parliament** – **Fourth Schedule**.
[18:4:5] - **Article 368 itself**.
[18:4:6] - **GST Council (Art. 279A)** – added by the **101st Amendment, 2016**.
[18:4:7] - Ratified by **not less than half** the States (not all), by **simple majority** of each legislature; **no time limit**.
[18:4:8] - **First** ratified amendment: **3rd Amendment (1954)** – re-enacted **Entry 33, Concurrent List**.
[18:4:9] - **Rajendra N. Shah (2021)**: **Part IXB** (97th, co-operatives) struck down for State co-operatives for **want of ratification**.

## SUB 6 | Limitations – Can Parliament Amend FRs?

## SUB 8 | Criticism
[18:8:0] - **No special body** (convention) or **referendum**.
[18:8:1] - **No joint sitting** – no deadlock mechanism.
[18:8:2] - **No time limit** for ratification.
[18:8:3] - States **cannot initiate**; ratification only for a few provisions.
[18:8:4] - Simple-majority routes too easy; the 2026 vote shows special majority guards against slim majorities.
