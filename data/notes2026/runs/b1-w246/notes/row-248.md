=== Cabinet Mission, 1946
# Mission
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Sent by | PM **Clement Attlee** (Labour); decision 22 Jan, announced in the Commons **19 February 1946** |
| Members ("Ministerial Trio") | **Lord Pethick-Lawrence** (Secretary of State for India, **chairman / leader**), **Sir Stafford Cripps** (President of the Board of Trade), **A.V. Alexander** (First Lord of the Admiralty) |
| Not members | **John Simon** (Simon Commission), William Wood, **Wavell** (Viceroy – worked with it), Attlee, Linlithgow |
| Arrived | **24 March 1946** (Delhi) |
| Plan announced | **16 May 1946** ("May 16 Plan") |
| Congress President in the talks | **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** |
- Aim: machinery to frame the constitution + an **interim government**; the **last British attempt to keep India united** – its rejection = the last chance to avoid partition (UPSC 2002).
- Quotation angle: "It made its proposals in **May**. It still wanted a **united India** … a **Federal Union** of British provinces" = **Cabinet Mission** (UPSC 1999).
# Main points
- **Rejected a full Pakistan** – non-Muslims would be **38% in the NW** and **48% in the NE** zone.
- **Union of India** (British India + states) dealing only with **Defence, Foreign Affairs, Communications** – a loose **three-tier federation** ("Union of States"); **residuary powers with the provinces**.
- **Three tiers:** Union → **groups (sections)** → provinces.
| Section | Provinces |
|---|---|
| A | Madras, Bombay, UP, Bihar, CP, Orissa (Hindu-majority) |
| **B** | **Punjab, NWFP, Sindh** |
| **C** | **Bengal, Assam** |
- **Constituent Assembly:** **389** = **296 British India** (292 provinces + 4 Chief Commissioner's provinces) + **93 princely states**; provincial members elected by the provincial assemblies by **PR-STV**, community-wise (General, Muslim, Sikh); state members **nominated by the rulers** – the Constituent Assembly was **set up under the Cabinet Mission Plan** (not Cripps, Mountbatten or the Independence Act).
- **July 1946 elections:** **Congress 208, League 73, others 15**. First meeting **9 December 1946** (League absent).
- Demand for a CA: **M.N. Roy 1934** → **Congress 1935** → accepted in the August Offer 1940.
- **Interim cabinet of Indians** – yes; **constitution-framing right** – yes; **acceptance of Pakistan – NO** (UPSC 1996).
- Not in the plan: enlarging the courts' powers or more Indians in the ICS (UPSC 2015: only "federal government").
# Acceptance and withdrawal
- **Grouping:** Congress – **optional**; League – **compulsory** (stepping stone to Pakistan).
- League accepted **6 June**, Congress **24 June 1946**; **Nehru's statement of 10 July 1946** → League **withdrew its acceptance 29 July 1946** and called for "Direct Action" (16 August). The League later **joined the Interim Government (Oct 1946)** – true, but not the reason (A-R).
- **Who backed the plan fully:** keys give **Gandhi** (he called it the best document the British could produce); quiz sites give Patel; Azad claimed it as his own triumph.

=== Wavell Plan and Simla Conference, 1945
- **Lord Wavell** (Viceroy 1943-47); plan announced **14 June 1945**; **Simla (Shimla) Conference 25 June – 14 July 1945**.
- **Proposals:** Executive Council reconstituted – **all members Indians except the Viceroy and the Commander-in-Chief**; all portfolios **except defence (War)** to Indians; **caste Hindus and Muslims equal** (parity); an interim arrangement under the 1935 Act; constitution talks after the war.
- **UPSC 2008:** the body that suggested an all-Indian Executive Council = **Simla Conference (1945)** (strictly the War portfolio stayed with the C-in-C).
- **A-R (UPSC 2007):** parity of Hindu and Muslim members – **true**; "Wavell thought it would avoid partition" – **false** (aim: break the deadlock).
- **Failure:** **Jinnah insisted the League alone nominate all Muslim members**; Wavell **closed the conference on 14 July 1945** → League got a virtual veto. Congress led by **Azad**.
# Wavell's 'Breakdown Plan' (1946)
- Put to the Cabinet Mission in **May 1946** by **Viceroy Wavell**: phased British withdrawal to the Muslim provinces of the NW and NE, handing the rest to the Congress; superseded by the Cabinet Mission Plan.

=== Precursors – C.R. Formula and Desai-Liaquat Pact
- **C.R. (Rajaji) Formula, 1944** – by **C. Rajagopalachari** (not Nehru, C.R. Das, V.P. Menon or Wavell) to end the Congress-League deadlock; tacit acceptance of Pakistan; basis of the **Gandhi-Jinnah talks, Bombay, 9–27 Sept 1944**.
  - League to back independence and join a provisional government; after the war a **plebiscite of the whole population** of Muslim-majority NW and NE areas; if partition – joint agreements on defence, commerce, communications.
  - **Not in it:** Jinnah's demands – only Muslims to vote, no common centre (he rejected it).
- **"The Way Out"** – Rajaji's pamphlet after Quit India: **a solution for the constitutional deadlock** (UPSC 2010).
- **Desai-Liaquat Pact (Jan 1945):** **Bhulabhai Desai** + Liaquat Ali Khan – interim government with **equal Congress and League nominees + 20% for minorities**.
- League slogan **"Divide and Quit"** – **Karachi session, December 1943**.

=== Elections of 1945–46 and the Congress Leadership
- **Elections (winter 1945-46):** Congress swept general seats; **League won all 30 Muslim seats** in the Central Assembly; League ministries in **Bengal (H.S. Suhrawardy)** and Sindh; Congress in most provinces **including NWFP**; Punjab – **Khizr Hayat Khan** (Unionist-Congress-Akali); Madras – **Tanguturi Prakasam** premier (30 April 1946).
- Unrest: Red Fort INA trials (Nov 1945); **Rashid Ali Day, Calcutta, 11 Feb 1946**; RIN revolt 18 Feb 1946.
| Leader | Fact |
|---|---|

| **J.B. Kripalani** | Congress President from the **Meerut session (Nov 1946)** to 1947 |
