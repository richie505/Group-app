## SUB 1 | Hyderabad State – Nizam, Standstill Agreement, Razakars, UN Appeal
[247:1:0] # The state and its ruler
[247:1:1] - **Ruling dynasty in 1947-48:** **Asaf Jahi (Nizams)** – founded by **Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I (1724)**; not the Qutb Shahis (Golconda) or Adil Shahis.
[247:1:2] - **Last Nizam:** **Mir Osman Ali Khan, the 7th Nizam (Asaf Jah VII)**; Muslim ruler over a Hindu majority; sought **independence** (or ties with Pakistan), not accession to India.
[247:1:3] - **Largest and richest** princely state.
[247:1:4] # Standstill Agreement
[247:1:5] - Signed **29 November 1947** by the **Nizam** and **Lord Mountbatten** (Government of India) – **Hyderabad** was the party; existing arrangements kept for **one year** during talks.

## SUB 3 | Accession of the States – Legal Frame, Patel-Menon, Junagadh, Kashmir, Manipur
[247:3:0] # Legal frame
[247:3:1] - **Indian Independence Act 1947:** British paramountcy **lapsed 15 August 1947**; a state could **join India, join Pakistan or (in theory) stay independent**.
[247:3:2] - **Instrument of Accession (IoA):** ceded only **Defence, External Affairs, Communications**; **internal administration stayed with the rulers**.
[247:3:3] - **Hold-outs on 15 August 1947:** **Junagadh, Hyderabad, Kashmir** – ruler's religion differed from the majority's; **Bhopal** is the odd one out (it acceded).
[247:3:4] # Machinery
[247:3:5] - **States Department (July 1947):** **Sardar Patel** (head) with **V.P. Menon** (Secretary, chief negotiator); Patel's appeal of **5 July 1947**; bait: **privy purses**, rulers as Rajpramukhs.
[247:3:6] - **V.P. Menon** wrote **"The Story of the Integration of the Indian States"** (not B.N. Rau, Rajaji or Krishna Menon).
[247:3:7] - **Phases (Menon):** 1 **extinction** (**216** small states merged into provinces) → 2 **consolidation into unions** (Kathiawar → **Saurashtra**, Rajasthan, PEPSU, Madhya Bharat) → 3 **democratisation** → 4 privy purses ended (1971).
[247:3:8] - **1950 classes:** Part A = former Governor's provinces; **Part B = princely states/unions under a Rajpramukh**; Part C = Chief Commissioner's provinces; **Part D = Andaman and Nicobar**.
[247:3:9] # The hard cases
[247:3:10] TABLE head: State | Ruler / figure | Outcome
  [247:3:10.0] Junagadh | Nawab Mahabat Khanji III; Dewan Shah Nawaz Bhutto | Nawab announced accession to Pakistan (Sept 1947) though most people wanted India; blockade, Arzi Hukumat; Nawab fled; plebiscite 20 Feb 1948 – 91% for India (the princely state where a plebiscite was held)
  [247:3:10.1] Jammu and Kashmir | Maharaja Hari Singh | Tribal invasion from Pakistan → IoA 26 October 1947; army airlifted 27 October; India to the UN (31 Dec 1947); ceasefire 1 January 1949; Sheikh Abdullah PM (March 1948); Delhi Agreement 1952
  [247:3:10.2] Travancore | Dewan C.P. Ramaswami Iyer | Declared independence (monazite/thorium); his autocratic rule was opposed by the State Congress; joined 30 July 1947
  [247:3:10.3] Jodhpur | Maharaja Hanwant Singh | Tempted by Jinnah; joined India

## SUB 2 | Wavell Plan and Simla Conference, 1945
[248:2:0] - **Lord Wavell** (Viceroy 1943-47); plan announced **14 June 1945**; **Simla (Shimla) Conference 25 June – 14 July 1945**.
[248:2:1] - **Proposals:** Executive Council reconstituted – **all members Indians except the Viceroy and the Commander-in-Chief**; all portfolios **except defence (War)** to Indians; **caste Hindus and Muslims equal** (parity); an interim arrangement under the 1935 Act; constitution talks after the war.

=== NOTES 1:249 | Direct Action Day, Mountbatten Plan, Indian Independence Act 1947; Partition and Its Consequences
## SUB 0 | Mountbatten Plan (3 June Plan), 1947 and Its Acceptance
[249:0:0] - **Lord Mountbatten** came as Viceroy in **March 1947** (took charge **24 March 1947**); announced his plan on **3 June 1947** – the country "free but not united". It became the **basis of partition** and the platform/formula for the **transfer of power** (sovereignty) to Indian hands.
[249:0:1] - **Plan Balkan / "Dickie Bird Plan"** (April–May 1947) was the brain-child of **Mountbatten**: power to the separate provinces, which (with princely states) could join India, Pakistan or stay separate; dropped after **Nehru** reacted violently (Balkanisation).
[249:0:2] - **United Bengal plan** (1947) of **Sarat Chandra Bose and H.S. Suhrawardy** – a sovereign undivided Bengal; rejected by **both Congress and the League**.
[249:0:3] - **V.P. Menon's** innovation: immediate transfer on the basis of **dominion status**, without waiting for a new constitution.
[249:0:4] # Provisions
[249:0:5] - **Punjab and Bengal** Assemblies to meet in **two groups** (Muslim-majority / Hindu-majority districts) and vote; a simple majority of either group for partition = partition → **both voted to divide** (Bengal 20 June, Punjab 23 June).
[249:0:6] - **Sindh** to decide on its own (joined Pakistan); Baluchistan also went to Pakistan.
[249:0:7] - **Referendums only in NWFP and Sylhet** (Assam): NWFP chose Pakistan (**Khudai Khidmatgar**/Provincial Congress boycotted); Sylhet joined East Bengal.
[249:0:8] - **Boundary Commission** for Punjab and Bengal; **two dominions and two Constituent Assemblies** if partition.
[249:0:9] - **Princely states:** independence ruled out by the plan – join **India or Pakistan** (no third option); Hyderabad's accession to Pakistan ruled out.
[249:0:10] - Common Governor-General permitted; transfer of power on the basis of the **GoI Act 1935**; armed forces divided.
[249:0:11] - Date **advanced from June 1948 to 15 August 1947**.
[249:0:12] # Why it was accepted
[249:0:13] - **Congress:** to **avoid large-scale communal riots** and civil war, avoid Balkanisation, take power quickly – it **never accepted the Two-Nation Theory** (A true, R false). Kripalani (April 1947): "rather than have a battle, we shall let them have their Pakistan".
[249:0:14] - **Muslim League:** because it provided for the **creation of Pakistan**.
[249:0:15] - Patel: "We would not have had one Pakistan but several"; Pant: accepting 3 June was the alternative to "committing suicide".
[249:0:16] # AICC, New Delhi, 14–15 June 1947

  [249:0:17.0] Presided (Congress President) | Acharya J.B. Kripalani

[249:0:18] > 3 June 1947 = Mountbatten Plan; referendums only NWFP + Sylhet; Pant moved, Azad seconded, Kripalani presided.

## SUB 3 | Indian Independence Act, 1947
[249:3:0] TABLE head: Stage | Date
  [249:3:0.0] Bill introduced in the House of Commons (by Attlee) | 4 July 1947
  [249:3:0.1] Passed by Parliament | July 1947
  [249:3:0.2] Royal assent (Act enacted) | 18 July 1947
  [249:3:0.3] Dominions created (appointed day) | Pakistan 14 August, India 15 August 1947
[249:3:1] - Gave **legal force to the Mountbatten Plan**; created **two independent dominions**, India and Pakistan (the basis of **partition**), with the right to leave the Commonwealth.
[249:3:2] - **Ended:** office of **Viceroy**, **Secretary of State for India**, title **"Emperor of India"**; paramountcy and treaties with princely states lapsed.
[249:3:3] - **Princely states:** free to **join India or Pakistan or remain independent** (Laxmikanth); 562/565 states – not forced to merge, not abolished.
[249:3:4] - **Governor-General** for each dominion appointed by the **British King** on the dominion cabinet's advice – a constitutional head; Britain to have **no control** over the dominions (so British sovereignty did not continue, though the sovereign appointed the GG).
[249:3:5] - **Constituent Assembly** made **sovereign** – could frame any constitution and repeal any British law, even this Act; it also became the dominion legislature and assumed power at **midnight of 14/15 August 1947**.
[249:3:6] - Interim law: **GoI Act 1935, as adapted**, till new constitutions.
[249:3:7] - Most distinctive feature: an **"Act without a constitution"** – transferred power but left constitution-making to Indians.
[249:3:8] - NOT in the Act: Federal Railway Authority, extension of franchise (GoI Act 1935); declaring India a Republic (1950).
[249:3:9] > Introduced 4 July → royal assent 18 July → effective 15 August 1947.
[249:3:10] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Indian Polity Part 1 p.34-35, p.38, p.41; PMF Modern History Part 5 p.101-102; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.64-66; Spectrum Modern India p.527-528
[249:3:11] - **Common Governor-General:** the Act allowed the **same person as Governor-General of both dominions** if both agreed (Jinnah declined; Mountbatten stayed only for India).
## SUB 4 | Attlee's Statement and Mountbatten's Arrival
[249:4:0] - **20 February 1947:** British PM **Clement Attlee** (Labour) declared in the **House of Commons** that British rule would end by **30 June 1948**, even without agreement among Indian parties.
[249:4:1] - Power could go to some central government or to **provincial governments** (hint of Balkanisation); **Mountbatten to replace Wavell**.
[249:4:2] - Attlee quotes (exam keys): "**Imperialism is dead as dodo**"; "The British Government is **not responsible** for the partition of India".
[249:4:3] - Mountbatten's instruction: explore unity and division till October 1947 – **keep India united if possible**; he came in **March 1947**, not 1945.
[249:4:4] - Assertion-reason: Britain left in 1947 because it had become **weak after the Second World War**.
[249:4:5] > Attlee deadline = 30 June 1948; Mountbatten advanced it to 15 August 1947.
[249:4:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.523-525; PMF Modern History Part 5 p.96-97, p.99; Laxmikanth Indian Polity Part 1 p.34; old app notes row 249

## SUB 6 | Radcliffe Boundary Commissions
[249:6:0] - Two commissions – **Punjab** and **Bengal** – set up (30 June 1947) to **demarcate the India–Pakistan boundary**; each = **2 Congress + 2 League** nominees and a chairman.
[249:6:1] - **Sir Cyril Radcliffe**, a British lawyer with **no knowledge of Indian conditions**, chaired **both**; arrived 8 July, five weeks to decide.
[249:6:2] - The **Radcliffe Line** award was ready on **12 August** but published on **17 August 1947**, after independence, so Britain would escape the blame.
[249:6:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.100-101; Spectrum Modern India p.528; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.64
