=== NOTES 1:257 | Role of the Press and Newspapers in the Andhra Movement
## SUB 0 | First Telugu Papers – Satyadoota and Other Firsts
[257:0:0] # Satyadoota (Satya Duta) – the first Telugu journal
[257:0:1] - **Satyadoota:** the **first Telugu newspaper / journal / magazine**, started in **1835** at **Bellary** by the **Christian missionaries (Bellary Christian Society / Association)** to **propagate Christianity** – not Vruttanthini, Vartamana Tarangini, Desabhimani, Tattvabodhini or Andhra Patrika.
[257:0:2] - **Tattvabodhini (1864, Madras):** published by the **Madras Veda Samaj** for the propagation of Hinduism; supported widow remarriage.
[257:0:3] # Other "firsts" and "only"
[257:0:4] TABLE head: First / only | Paper
  [257:0:4.0] First Telugu newspaper printed in Andhra | Hitavadi (1841), Edmund Park
  [257:0:4.1] First Telugu paper started by a Muslim | Vartamana Tarangini (8 June 1842, Madras) – Syed Rahamatullah; passed to Puvvada Seshagiri Rao after 1843
  [257:0:4.2] First political weekly / journal in Telugu | Andhra Prakasika (1885) – A.P. Parthasarathi Naidu
  [257:0:4.3] Only Telugu daily of British Andhra | Desabhimani (Guntur)
  [257:0:4.4] First fully socialist Telugu journal | Navasakthi
  [257:0:4.5] First communist Telugu journal | Prajasakthi
  [257:0:4.6] First Telugu journal after independence | Jaya Bharat
  [257:0:4.7] Introduced the editorial column | Andhra Bhasa Sanjivani – Kokkonda Venkataratnam
  [257:0:4.8] First newspaper from Madras | Madras Courier (1785) – not The Hindu or The Mail
[257:0:5] - **Purushartha Pradayini (1872, Machilipatnam):** articles for and against widow remarriage.
[257:0:6] - **By 1905:** about **20** Telugu journals – Andhra Prakasika, Sasilekha, Krishna Patrika, Desabhimani, Rasikollasini.

## SUB 2 | Press and the Separate-Andhra Demand
[257:2:0] - **April 1911 – The Hindu:** a series of **six articles** by **Jonnavittula Gurunatham** (Guntur), "on the present position (condition) of the Telugu people", showed the under-representation of Telugus – not Krishna Patrika, Desabhimani or Indian Patriot.
[257:2:1]   - "Are Telugu people backward?" (15 Apr 1911): civil services **56 non-Andhras : 8 Andhras**; district judges **19 : 0**.
[257:2:2] - **Desabhimani (1911):** urged a separate Andhra political conference and Telugu papers writing on Andhra affairs; commented "**Unless there is a separate Government there is no protection for Telugu people's culture**".
[257:2:3] - **Krishna Patrika:** printed several articles on the need for a **separate Andhra province** (1912 debate with Desamata); 30 Nov 1912 – "strengthening the nation begins with the Andhra movement"; 19 Jul 1913 cartoon – Rayalaseema as an iceberg blocking the "Andhra Movement" ship.
[257:2:4] - **Janmabhumi (25 Aug 1920):** the Andhra movement strengthens national unity.
[257:2:5] - **Supporters of a separate Andhra:** Andhra Patrika, Krishna Patrika, Desabhimani, Desamata, Darbar; Andhra Patrika popularised the word "Andhra".
[257:2:6] - **Significance:** the press spread the national movement and built support for the separate-Andhra demand; newspapers also gave rise to reading rooms and libraries.
[257:2:7] > Exam angle: 1911 six articles = The Hindu; Desabhimani's "separate Government" remark.
[257:2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History Part 22 p.4-5; NextGen AP History Part 6 p.12; app notes 1:257 (sub 7, 8)
[257:2:9] - **The Indian Patriot** (Madras) published leading articles in favour of the Andhra movement and helped the Andhras' propaganda (printed key, not verified).

## SUB 4 | Press Against the Government – Grievances and Movements
[257:4:0] - **Lokaranjani (October 1875):** complained that the ryots were **better off under the East India Company** than under the Crown.
[257:4:1] - **Home Rule:** **Desamata (Desha Mata)** – Chilakamarti's Rajahmundry paper – strongly supported the **Home Rule movement** in Andhra (APPSC G2 Mains 2017) – not Andhra Mata; **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao** was the Andhra Home Rule leader (secretary).
[257:4:2] TABLE head: Movement | Paper
  [257:4:2.0] Vandemataram / Swadeshi | Krishna Patrika, Swarajya, Desamata, Andhra Kesari (Chilukuri Veerabhadra Rao), Navayuga
  [257:4:2.1] Home Rule | Desamata
  [257:4:2.2] Peasant / anti-zamindari | Zamin Ryot
  [257:4:2.3] Socialist / Communist | Navasakthi / Prajasakthi
  [257:4:2.4] Women's cause | Hindu Sundari (1902), Sri Savitri (1904)
[257:4:3] - **Vernacular Press Act, 1878** ("Gagging Act") – repealed by **Lord Ripon, 1882**.
[257:4:4] - **Swarajya sedition case:** Gadicherla (with Bodi Narayana Rao) convicted under **Section 124A IPC** for an article of 26 March 1908; **3 years** – the first Telugu editor in the Presidency imprisoned in the Vandemataram movement.
[257:4:5] - **"Veerabali":** NextGen says **Krowidi Lingaraju** was jailed for two years for the editorial "Veerabali" (1920s); Madduri Annapurnayya for the playlet "Chichula Pidugu".
[257:4:6] > Exam angle: Home Rule paper = Desamata; Lokaranjani 1875.

=== NOTES 1:258 | Journalists and Editors of the Andhra Movement – Mutnuri Krishna Rao, Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao
## SUB 0 | Krishna Patrika and Mutnuri Krishna Rao
[258:0:0] # Krishna Patrika (1902)
[258:0:1] - **Founded:** **February 1902** (1 Feb per NextGen; 2 Feb per Wikipedia), at **Machilipatnam (Bandar)**, as a **weekly** – in the **20th century**, not the 18th.
[258:0:2] - **Founders:** **Konda Venkatappayya** and **Dasu Narayana Rao** – not Veeresalingam, Duggirala, Kasinathuni or Mutnuri.
[258:0:3] - **First editor:** **Konda Venkatappayya** (edited till 1905; NextGen lists Dasu Narayana as first editor).
[258:0:4] - **Mutnuri Krishna Rao:** sub-editor from 1902, **editor from 1907 to his death in 1945** – the **second and most famous editor**, "**largely responsible for its success**" (FRO; APPSC GS 2024) – not Konda, Dasu Narayana Rao or Piratla Venkateswarlu.
[258:0:5]   - Made Krishna Patrika a **political weekly** and a **hub for freedom fighters**; called the **doyen of Telugu journalism**.
[258:0:6]   - Wrote scholarly editorials attacking British government spending; also ran the magazine **Andhra Bharati** for some time.
[258:0:7] - **Statements:** "Krishna Patrika was founded by Veeresalingam" – wrong; "edited by Mutnuri Krishna Rao" – right.
[258:0:8] - **Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya:** set up the **Andhra Vidya Peetha Goshti** (Chirala, 1920) – true in the statement questions.
[258:0:9] > Exam angle: founder Konda Venkatappayya (×7) vs editor Mutnuri (×7) – the core pair of this row.

## SUB 1 | Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao – Andhra Patrika and Bharati
[258:1:0] - **Life:** **1867–1947**; founded the **Amrutanjan** business and used the wealth for public work.
[258:1:1] - **Papers:** **Andhra Patrika** (9 Sept **1908**, Bombay; Telugu daily from 1914, Madras) – founder and first editor; **Bharati** (**1924**) – a **literary and cultural monthly** – not 1920-22.
[258:1:2] - **Publishing house:** **Andhra Granthamala**, named to popularise the word "Andhra".
[258:1:3] - **Library role:** any group of **ten youths** opening a village library got his paper, books and *Bharati* **free**; finished the **catalogue of printed Telugu books** begun by **Velidanda Hanumantha Rao**.
[258:1:4] - **Granthalayodyama Trimurthulu:** Kasinathuni + Ayyanki + Gadicherla.
[258:1:5] - **Sribagh:** his house in Madras; the **Sribagh Pact** was signed there on **16 November 1937**.

## SUB 0 | Sri Krishnadevaraya Andhra Bhasha Nilayam and the Hyderabad Libraries
[259:0:0] - **Sri Krishnadevaraya Andhra Bhasha Nilayam (SKDABN):** founded **1901** in **Hyderabad** by **Komarraju (Venkata) Lakshmana Rao** with associates, at the home of **Ravichettu Ranga Rao** – the **first Telugu library** of Hyderabad (Telangana) – not Machilipatnam, Vijayawada, Rajahmundry or **Kovvur 1916**; not Madapati, Adiraju or Burgula.

[259:0:2]   - His libraries: Hyderabad (**1901**), **Rajaraja Narendra Andhra Bhasha Nilayam, Hanamkonda (1904)**, Secunderabad (1905).
[259:0:3] - **Andhra Samvardhani Granthalayam:** **Secunderabad, 1905**.
[259:0:4] - **Andhra Saraswati Parishad:** Hyderabad, 1922.
[259:0:5] - Early Telugu libraries of Hyderabad State – SKDABN (1901), Andhra Samvardhani (1905), Vignana Chandrika Mandali (1906) – later the base for the **Andhra Jana Sangham** and the **Andhra Mahasabha** (first session **Jogipet**).

## SUB 4 | Andhra Desa Library Association, Conferences, Laws and Innovations
[259:4:0] # Organised movement
[259:4:1] - **By 1913:** about **125 libraries**; call for a conference by **Ayyanki** and **Suri Venkata Narasimha** through Andhra Patrika.

=== NOTES 1:260 | Library Movement Leaders – Ayyanki Venkataramanaiah, Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao
## SUB 0 | Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao – Rayalaseema, Swarajya and Jail
[260:0:0] # "Rayalaseema"
[260:0:1] - **Named the Ceded Districts (Dattamandalam) "Rayalaseema"** at the **1928 Nandyal Andhra Mahasabha** – after the Vijayanagara Rayas – not Sri Krishnadevaraya, Munro, Hyder Ali, Peddana or Bezawada Gopala Reddy.
[260:0:2] - **Sribagh Pact (1937):** signed it for Rayalaseema.
[260:0:3] # Journalist and prisoner
[260:0:4] - **Papers:** **Swarajya (1907, Vijayawada; earlier Sri Venkateswara Patrika)**, Navayugam, Andhra Patrika (editor 1916-18), Matru Seva, Panchayati Rajyam – **not Grandhalaya Sarvasvam** (Ayyanki's journal).
[260:0:5] - **Jailed for sedition in 1908** (Section 124A, 3 years) for Swarajya writings – the **first Telugu journalist / political prisoner jailed for sedition (treason) in South India** – not Kasinathuni, Challa Seshagiri Rao or Sivalenka Sambhu Prasad.
[260:0:6] - **Vandemataram:** the student expelled from the **Rajahmundry Teacher Training College (1907)** and blacklisted from government jobs.
[260:0:7] - **Home Rule:** started the Home Rule League branches in Andhra; its secretary; **linked the library movement to the Home Rule League**.
[260:0:8] # Library movement
[260:0:9] - **Granthalayodyama Trimurthulu:** Ayyanki (**built institutions**), **Gadicherla (mobilised people – the "radical mobiliser")**, Kasinathuni (**money and political backing**).
[260:0:10] - **Edward Corporation Library, Nandyal** (now Victoria Reading Room) – with **Kaderbad Narasinga Rao**.
[260:0:11] - **President** of the library association in **1946**; **Komma Sitaramayya's** gift bought a building → **Sarvottama Bhavan**, opened **1949** in his honour.
[260:0:12] > Exam angle: Rayalaseema name (×4); first Telugu journalist jailed for sedition (×2).

## SUB 1 | Ayyanki Venkata Ramanayya and Pathuri Nagabhushanam
[260:1:0] # Ayyanki (Iyyanki) Venkata Ramanayya
[260:1:1] - **Started (led) the library movement** – "**Father of the Library Movement**"; said "**Library Movement in Andhra is Swayambhu**"; slogan "Public libraries are temples of knowledge" – not Chilukuri Veerabhadra Rao or Veeresalingam.
[260:1:2] - **Secretary, Rama Mohana Library**, Vijayawada (1911); founder-secretary of the **ADLA (1914)**; edited ***Grandhalaya Sarvasvam*** (1915).
[260:1:3] - **Conceived the library pilgrimage** (village tours, 1934-48).
[260:1:4] - Organised the All India Public Library Conference (Madras, Nov 1919); General Secretary of the All India Public Library Association at its second conference (**Dec 1923**).
[260:1:5] - Associations he helped found, in order: ADLA (1914) → **All India Public Library Association (1919)** → **Bengal (1925)** → **Madras (1928)** → **Punjab (1929)**; the **Indian Library Association** was formed at Calcutta in **1933**.
[260:1:6] TABLE head: Title / award | Person
  [260:1:6.0] Granthalaya Pitamaha (from the Maharaja of Baroda); Saraswati Samrajya Pratishtapanacharya; Granthala Visaradha; first Indian with the Kaula Gold Medal; Padma Shri | Ayyanki
  [260:1:6.1] Andhra Desa Granthalayodyama Roopasilpi; "University of Humanity", "Walking Library" (from Karunasri – Jandhyala Papayya Sastri); Kala Prapoorna (Andhra University, 1975) | Pathuri Nagabhushanam
  [260:1:6.2] Library Gandhi | Velaga Venkatappayya – led tours in Bhimavaram, Tadepalligudem, Kovvur and Guntur taluks
[260:1:7] # Pathuri (Paturi) Nagabhushanam
[260:1:8] - **Pioneer of the boat library service** – on passenger boats on the **Bank Canal**, **1935-42** (seven years).
[260:1:9] - Started the **Bala Saraswati Bhandaramu** village library as a schoolboy; as Secretary of the **Guntur Library Association** set up libraries in about **160 villages**.
[260:1:10] - Member of the drafting committee of the **AP Public Libraries Act (1960)**.
[260:1:11] # Other helpers
[260:1:12] - **Madras Public Libraries Bill, 1938:** Vavilala Gopalakrishnayya – not Ayyanki.
[260:1:13] - **Vavilala Ramaswamy Sastrulu & Sons:** books to the Ramamohan Library and free copies to village libraries; Andhra Granthamala (Kasinathuni) also supplied books.
[260:1:14] > Exam angle: who started the library movement = Ayyanki; boat library = Pathuri.

## SUB 6 | Seventh Amendment 1956, Zonal Councils and Later Changes
[261:6:0] - **7th Constitutional Amendment (1956):** abolished **Part A/B/C/D** → **14 states and 6 UTs**; abolished the **Rajpramukh**; **extended the jurisdiction of High Courts to Union Territories** and allowed a common High Court for two or more states (not the 1st, 3rd or 5th Amendment); inserted **Art 350A** (mother-tongue instruction at primary stage) and **Art 350B** (Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities).
[261:6:1] # Zonal Councils (SR Act Part III, Sections 15–22)
[261:6:2] - **Five** statutory councils – Northern, Central, Eastern, Western, Southern; purpose **inter-state cooperation**.
[261:6:3] - **Chairman:** a Union Minister nominated by the President – in practice the **Union Home Minister**; Vice-Chairman: a member-state CM by annual rotation. Members: **CM + two ministers** of each state; up to two from each UT.
[261:6:4] - **APRA 2014** amended **Section 15** of the SR Act to read "Andhra Pradesh and Telangana".
[261:6:5] # Later changes
[261:6:6] - **1 May 1960:** bilingual Bombay split into **Gujarat** and **Maharashtra**. **Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966** (after the **Shah Commission**): **Haryana** from 1 Nov 1966; hill areas to **Himachal Pradesh**.
