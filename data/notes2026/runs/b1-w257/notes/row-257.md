=== First Telugu Papers – Satyadoota and Other Firsts
# Satyadoota (Satya Duta) – the first Telugu journal
- **Satyadoota:** the **first Telugu newspaper / journal / magazine**, started in **1835** at **Bellary** by the **Christian missionaries (Bellary Christian Society / Association)** to **propagate Christianity** – not Vruttanthini, Vartamana Tarangini, Desabhimani, Tattvabodhini or Andhra Patrika.
- **Tattvabodhini (1864, Madras):** published by the **Madras Veda Samaj** for the propagation of Hinduism; supported widow remarriage.
# Other "firsts" and "only"
| First / only | Paper |
|---|---|
| First Telugu newspaper printed in Andhra | **Hitavadi** (1841), Edmund Park |
| First Telugu paper started by a Muslim | **Vartamana Tarangini** (8 June 1842, Madras) – Syed Rahamatullah; passed to Puvvada Seshagiri Rao after 1843 |
| **First political weekly / journal in Telugu** | **Andhra Prakasika** (1885) – A.P. Parthasarathi Naidu |
| Only Telugu daily of British Andhra | **Desabhimani** (Guntur) |
| First fully socialist Telugu journal | Navasakthi |
| First communist Telugu journal | Prajasakthi |
| First Telugu journal after independence | Jaya Bharat |
| Introduced the editorial column | Andhra Bhasa Sanjivani – Kokkonda Venkataratnam |
| **First newspaper from Madras** | **Madras Courier** (1785) – not The Hindu or The Mail |
- **Purushartha Pradayini (1872, Machilipatnam):** articles for and against widow remarriage.
- **By 1905:** about **20** Telugu journals – Andhra Prakasika, Sasilekha, Krishna Patrika, Desabhimani, Rasikollasini.
> Exam angle: Satyadoota–Bellary–1835–missionaries (×9, the most repeated fact of the topic); Andhra Prakasika = first political weekly.

=== Press and the Separate-Andhra Demand
- **April 1911 – The Hindu:** a series of **six articles** by **Jonnavittula Gurunatham** (Guntur), "on the present position (condition) of the Telugu people", showed the under-representation of Telugus – not Krishna Patrika, Desabhimani or Indian Patriot.
  - "Are Telugu people backward?" (15 Apr 1911): civil services **56 non-Andhras : 8 Andhras**; district judges **19 : 0**.
- **Desabhimani (1911):** urged a separate Andhra political conference and Telugu papers writing on Andhra affairs; commented "**Unless there is a separate Government there is no protection for Telugu people's culture**".
- **Krishna Patrika:** printed several articles on the need for a **separate Andhra province** (1912 debate with Desamata); 30 Nov 1912 – "strengthening the nation begins with the Andhra movement"; 19 Jul 1913 cartoon – Rayalaseema as an iceberg blocking the "Andhra Movement" ship.
- **Janmabhumi (25 Aug 1920):** the Andhra movement strengthens national unity.
- **Supporters of a separate Andhra:** Andhra Patrika, Krishna Patrika, Desabhimani, Desamata, Darbar; Andhra Patrika popularised the word "Andhra".
- **Significance:** the press spread the national movement and built support for the separate-Andhra demand; newspapers also gave rise to reading rooms and libraries.
> Exam angle: 1911 six articles = The Hindu; Desabhimani's "separate Government" remark.

=== Press Against the Government – Grievances and Movements
- **Lokaranjani (October 1875):** complained that the ryots were **better off under the East India Company** than under the Crown.
- **Home Rule:** **Desamata (Desha Mata)** – Chilakamarti's Rajahmundry paper – strongly supported the **Home Rule movement** in Andhra (APPSC G2 Mains 2017) – not Andhra Mata; **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao** was the Andhra Home Rule leader (secretary).
| Movement | Paper |
|---|---|
| Vandemataram / Swadeshi | Krishna Patrika, Swarajya, Desamata, Andhra Kesari (Chilukuri Veerabhadra Rao), Navayuga |
| Home Rule | **Desamata** |
| Peasant / anti-zamindari | Zamin Ryot |
| Socialist / Communist | Navasakthi / **Prajasakthi** |
| Women's cause | Hindu Sundari (1902), Sri Savitri (1904) |
- **Vernacular Press Act, 1878** ("Gagging Act") – repealed by **Lord Ripon, 1882**.
- **Swarajya sedition case:** Gadicherla (with Bodi Narayana Rao) convicted under **Section 124A IPC** for an article of 26 March 1908; **3 years** – the first Telugu editor in the Presidency imprisoned in the Vandemataram movement.
- **"Veerabali":** NextGen says **Krowidi Lingaraju** was jailed for two years for the editorial "Veerabali" (1920s); Madduri Annapurnayya for the playlet "Chichula Pidugu".
> Exam angle: Home Rule paper = Desamata; Lokaranjani 1875.
