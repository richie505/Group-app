=== From Tribe to Territory – Kingship
- **Jana** (tribe) → **janapada / rashtra** (territory); the word **rashtra** first appears in the Later Vedic texts; tribal chiefdom → **hereditary territorial monarchy**, which prepared the way for the **Mahajanapadas** (6th century BCE).
- Kingship **hereditary**, usually to the **eldest son** (primogeniture); men became rajas by performing big sacrifices; traces of election survived.
- **Ekarat** (Atharvaveda, Aitareya Brahmana) = **sole / paramount ruler**.
| Region (Aitareya Brahmana) | Ruler's title |
|---|---|
| East | **Samrat** |
| South | **Bhoja** |
| West | **Svarat** |
| North | **Virat** |
- State forms in the Aitareya Brahmana: **Rajya, Bhojya, Swarajya, Vairajya, Samrajya** – **Ganatantra is NOT** one.
# Divine origin of kingship
| Text | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Aitareya Brahmana** | Earliest account: the gods, losing to the Asuras, chose a king to lead them; king = **protector of Brahmins** |
| Taittiriya Brahmana | Theory of the divine origin of kingship |
| **Shatapatha Brahmana** | Linked the king with **Prajapati**; Rajasuya and Vajapeya gave him divine qualities |
- Kingship was **not** traced to election by village assemblies.
| Early Vedic | Later Vedic |
|---|---|
| Tribe; cattle; elective chief | Territory; agriculture; hereditary king |
| Voluntary bali; strong assemblies | Compulsory taxes (bali, bhaga, shulka); assemblies decline; **Ratnins** |
> East = Samrat, North = Virat; rashtra first appears now.

=== Royal Sacrifices – Rajasuya, Ashvamedha, Vajapeya
| Sacrifice | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Rajasuya** | Royal **consecration**; conferred supreme power; includes the **Ratnahavimshi** (offerings at the Ratnins' houses) |
| **Ashvamedha** | **Horse sacrifice**; supremacy over **other kings** and control of the area the horse roamed |
| **Vajapeya** | "**Drink of strength**"; **chariot race** won by the king over his **kinsmen** |
| Punarabhisheka | Second anointing at the end of the Rajasuya |
- **Muvendavelan** is a title (Chola-period chief), **not** a royal sacrifice.
- Large **Shrauta** sacrifices needed many priests and heavy **dakshina** (up to 2,40,000 cows in a Rajasuya) → strengthened the **Brahmanas**.
- **AP angle:** Satavahanas, **Ikshvakus**, Vishnukundins performed these rites; Ikshvaku titles: **Vajapeya = Samrat/Virat; Ashvamedha = Swarat**; an **Ashvamedha altar at Nagarjunakonda**.
> Ashvamedha = over other kings; Vajapeya = over kinsmen; Rajasuya = consecration.

=== Ratnins and Officials
- **Ratnins ("jewels")** = the king's council of **12** royal functionaries: purohita, rajan, mahishi, senani, bhagadugha, sangrahitri, suta, kshata, govikartana, gramani, parivakti, akshavapa (some add palagala); honoured in the **Ratnahavimshi** rite of the Rajasuya.
| Official | Function |
|---|---|
| Purohita | Chief priest |
| **Senani** | Army chief |
| **Suta** | Charioteer and herald |
| **Bhagadugha** | **Revenue collector** (king's share) |
| **Sangrahitri** | **Treasurer** |
| **Akshavapa** | Accountant; superintendent of dice |
| **Palagala** | Messenger |
- More officers but no regular bureaucracy and **no standing army**.
> Bhagadugha collects; Sangrahitri stores.

=== Assemblies and Taxes
- **Vidatha disappeared**; **Sabha and Samiti continued** but declined, dominated by chiefs, nobles and Brahmanas; **women no longer sat** in the Sabha; village assemblies tried local cases.
| Tax | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Bali** | Tribute: voluntary in the Rigveda, now **regular and compulsory** – the king's tax on the people |
| **Bhaga** | King's share of produce (**one-sixth**; texts give 1/6 to 1/2) |
| **Shulka** | **Customs / tolls** |
- **Vaishyas** were the main (only) **tribute-payers**; Bhagadugha collected, Sangrahitri managed the revenue.
- Varman = armour, Vidatha = assembly – not taxes.
