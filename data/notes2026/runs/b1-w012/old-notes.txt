=== NOTES 1:12 | Ivc Religion – Pashupati Seal, Mother Goddess, Tree and Animal Worship, Fire Altars
## SUB 0 | Mother Goddess, Pashupati and Male Deities
[12:0:0] # Mother Goddess – the chief female deity
[12:0:1] - **Mother Goddess (Devi Mata):** terracotta female figurines, heavily jewelled, with fan-shaped head-dresses → **fertility cult**; probably the chief female deity (RAS 1993; SSC 2012: Devi Mata = **Indus Valley Civilisation**).
[12:0:2] - **Sites:** Harappa, Mohenjodaro, Kot Diji, Kalibangan, Banawali (with toy plough) and Gujarat sites; not a feature of Ropar (known for the dog burial).
[12:0:3] - **Continuity:** the Mother Goddess cult survives in later **Shakti cults**.
[12:0:4] - **⚠ Trap:** "Spirit and Brahma", "ritual" and "sacrificial system" are Vedic ideas – the Indus answer is **Mother Goddess**.
[12:0:5] # Pashupati seal – the chief male deity
[12:0:6] TABLE head: Feature | Detail
  [12:0:6.0] Site | Mohenjodaro (steatite seal)
  [12:0:6.1] Figure | Three-faced, horned head-dress, seated in a yogic posture
  [12:0:6.2] Animals around | Elephant, tiger, rhinoceros, buffalo; two deer below the seat – no lion
  [12:0:6.3] Identification | Proto-Shiva / Pashupati ("lord of animals") – John Marshall
  [12:0:6.4] Caution | Script undeciphered, so the reading is an inference from later Shiva worship
[12:0:7] - **Indus people worshipped Shiva** (proto-Shiva, UKPSC 2006) – Indra and Varuna are Rig Vedic gods; Brahma and Vishnu are later.
[12:0:8] - **Both male and female deities** were worshipped (Mother Goddess + Pashupati) – the only correct statement in UPSC 2013 (no palaces or temples, no horse-drawn chariots) → **"only 2"**.
[12:0:9] # Idol worship
[12:0:10] - **Idol worship began in the pre-Aryan (Harappan) period:** no temple has been found, yet idolatry was practised through figurines and seals (UPPCS 1992). The Rig Vedic Aryans had **no idols or temples**.
[12:0:11] > Exam angle: "people of the Indus Valley believed in …" → Mother Goddess; "beginning of idol worship" → pre-Aryan; "Shiva / Rudra" → Pashupati.
[12:0:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.7; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.99-101; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.108-109; app notes 1:12 (sub 0, 1)
## SUB 1 | Tree, Animal and Bird Worship; Fertility Symbols
[12:1:0] # Sacred trees, animals and birds
[12:1:1] - **Pipal:** the tree most often depicted on Indus seals (a deity standing among pipal branches) – MPPSC 2021; the pipal motif goes back to the **Early Harappan** phase and is still worshipped.
[12:1:2] - **Pigeon (dove):** listed by NextGen among sacred symbols – the bird "worshipped" by the Harappans (APPSC Group-II 2011); the peacock is only a pottery motif.
[12:1:3] - **Sacred animals:** the **unicorn** (mythical one-horned animal, the commonest seal motif), the **humped bull** (next in importance), elephant, tiger, buffalo, rhinoceros; cobras and horned deities also appear.
[12:1:4] - **Animals round Pashupati** were probably worshipped too.
[12:1:5] # Fertility and phallic symbols
[12:1:6] - **Linga and yoni:** conical stones (lingas) and **round pierced stones** (yoni) – fertility objects, possibly early Shaivite elements; found at Harappa.
[12:1:7] - **Swastika:** a seal motif, later an auspicious Hindu symbol – it has nothing to do with the Vedic yajna.
[12:1:8] - **Seals:** mostly of **steatite**, mostly **square** – some with a hole used as **amulets**.
[12:1:9] > Exam angle: "shape of which tree on Indus seals" → **pipal**; "bird worshipped" → **pigeon**; "mythical sacred animal" → **unicorn**.
[12:1:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.2, p.7; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.100; app notes 1:12 (sub 2)
## SUB 2 | Fire Altars, Great Bath and Ritual Practices
[12:2:0] # Fire altars
[12:2:1] TABLE head: Site | Evidence
  [12:2:1.0] Kalibangan (Rajasthan) | A row of seven fire altars on a mud-brick platform; triangular terracotta cake with a horned deity on one side and a human pulling a goat by a rope (animal sacrifice) on the other
  [12:2:1.1] Lothal (Gujarat) | Fire altar
  [12:2:1.2] Banawali, Rakhigarhi | Small pits with terracotta cakes regarded as fire altars
[12:2:2] - **Horned deity on a terracotta cake** → **Kalibangan** (State PSC) – not Banawali, Lothal or Surkotada.
[12:2:3] - **⚠ Caution:** Harappan fire altars are **not conclusive proof of the later Vedic yajna**.
[12:2:4] # Other ritual evidence
[12:2:5] - **Great Bath (Mohenjodaro):** probably for **ritual purification** (bathing).
[12:2:6] - **Amulets:** holed seals and amulets suggest belief in **ghosts and evil forces**; deities shown in **yogic poses** → practice of yoga and meditation.
[12:2:7] - **Burials with grave goods** point to a belief in life after death (details: burial row).
[12:2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.7, p.12-13; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.101; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.20-21; app notes 1:12 (sub 1, 3)
## SUB 3 | Nature of Harappan Religion and Continuities
[12:3:0] - **Character:** predominantly **secular** civilisation – religion present but not dominant; nature- and **fertility-oriented** worship.
[12:3:1] - **No temple** has been identified anywhere; worship was domestic or open-air (under trees).
[12:3:2] - **Undeciphered script:** every reading of Harappan religion (proto-Shiva, Mother Goddess) is inferred from later traditions.
[12:3:3] - **Continuities into Hinduism:** Shiva/Pashupati, Shakti (Mother Goddess), linga-yoni, pipal and animal veneration, yoga.
[12:3:4] - **Contrast with the Rig Veda:** Vedic religion = **male nature gods and open-air yajnas**, no idols; Harappan = Mother Goddess, idols, animal and tree worship.
[12:3:5] - **Significance:** the earliest material evidence of a Shiva-like deity, a Mother Goddess and fertility cults in India.
[12:3:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.100-101; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.7, p.28; app notes 1:12 (sub 0, 5)

=== NOTES 1:13 | Decline of the Ivc – Competing Theories; Late Harappan Cultures
## SUB 0 | Late Harappan Phase – Features and Sites
[13:0:0] # Features (c. 1900–1300 BCE, post-urban phase)
[13:0:1] - **Chalcolithic:** tools of **stone and copper**; **no metal objects requiring complicated casting**; people lived in **villages** (agriculture, stock-raising, hunting, fishing).
[13:0:2] - **Lost:** the extraordinary **uniformity** of the Mature phase (pottery, seals, bricks, weights), writing, long-distance trade, large public buildings and craft specialisation; pottery became **regionally varied**.
[13:0:3] - **Bhagwanpura (Kurukshetra, Haryana):** Late Harappan houses of **burnt brick** (so "no burnt bricks at Bhagwanpura" is **false**); Late Harappan pottery overlaps with **PGW**; a 13-roomed house (excavated by **J.P. Joshi**).
[13:0:4] - **APPSC Group-1 statements:** I chalcolithic ✓, II no complicated casting ✓, III villages ✓, IV no burnt bricks at Bhagwanpura ✗ → **I, II and III**.
[13:0:5] - **Astt. Comm. 2018:** uniformity of artefacts ✓ (Mature phase); "Late sites kept the same construction techniques" ✗ → **only I**.
[13:0:6] - **Iron** was absent in the Mature phase and came only later (PGW, Later Vedic).
[13:0:7] # Regional Late Harappan cultures
[13:0:8] TABLE head: Culture / site | Region
  [13:0:8.0] Cemetery H | Harappa region, Punjab (Pakistan)
  [13:0:8.1] Jhukar, Jhangar | Sindh
  [13:0:8.2] Rangpur | Kutch, Saurashtra and mainland Gujarat
  [13:0:8.3] Late Siswal, Bhagwanpura | Haryana
  [13:0:8.4] Bara, Dadheri | Punjab (India)
  [13:0:8.5] Alamgirpur | Uttar Pradesh (Meerut)
  [13:0:8.6] Daimabad | Maharashtra – southernmost Late Harappan site
  [13:0:8.7] Swat | Khyber Pakhtunkhwa
[13:0:9] - **Movement:** settlements shifted **eastward** to the Ganga–Yamuna Doab and **southward** to Gujarat and the Deccan.

## SUB 1 | OCP, Copper Hoards and Harappan–Vedic Continuity
[13:1:0] - **Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP):** the Ganga–Yamuna Doab Late Harappan phase; **Copper Hoards** often found with OCP in the **Doab**.
[13:1:1] - **Continuity view:** identification of the Ghaggar-Hakra with the Rigvedic **Sarasvati**; Bhagwanpura's **Late Harappan + PGW** overlap.
[13:1:2] - **Traits that continued:** crafts, pottery, beads, weights, agriculture – **not iron tools**, which are post-Harappan.
[13:1:3] - **Rakhigarhi:** the only Harappan site so far to yield **ancient DNA**.
[13:1:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.8; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.28; app notes 1:13 (sub 5)
[13:1:5] - **Did not continue:** the **Harappan script** disappeared with the cities; wheat and cotton cultivation and proto-Shiva (Pashupati) worship carried on.
## SUB 2 | Decline Theories – Proponents
[13:2:0] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent(s)
  [13:2:0.0] Aryan invasion | Ramaprasad Chanda (1926), elaborated by Mortimer Wheeler (1947) – Indra "Purandara", huddled skeletons
  [13:2:0.1] Floods | Marshall, Mackay, S.R. Rao; floods + tectonic uplift – M.R. Sahni
  [13:2:0.2] Shift of the Indus course | H.T. Lambrick – Mohenjodaro abandoned
  [13:2:0.3] Climate change / aridity | Aurel Stein, A.N. Ghosh
  [13:2:0.4] Drying of the Ghaggar-Hakra (Sarasvati) | D.P. Agrawal and Sood – Kalibangan, Banawali abandoned
  [13:2:0.5] Ecological imbalance | W.A. Fairservis
  [13:2:0.6] Decline of trade with Mesopotamia (c. 1900 BCE) | trade-decline school
[13:2:1] - **Other stresses:** **soil salinity**, deforestation, malaria at **Mohenjodaro**; **Chanhudaro** was flooded several times.
[13:2:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Aurel Stein = climate, **not** Aryan invasion.
[13:2:3] - **Consensus:** decline was **gradual and multi-causal** – urban collapse over about **200 years** (c. 1900–1700 BCE) and a long tail to c. 1300 BCE; climate change and the drying of the Sarasvati are the most widely accepted causes.
[13:2:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.105-106; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.13; app notes 1:13 (sub 0)
## SUB 3 | Climate and the 4.2 ka Event
[13:3:0] - **4.2 ka event:** sharp weakening of the monsoon about **4,200 years ago**; the same aridity is linked to the fall of **Akkad** and Old Kingdom Egypt.
[13:3:1] - **Deoria Tal (Uttarakhand):** pollen record – the **oak-to-pine ratio** shift shows the monsoon **weakened sharply**.
[13:3:2] - **IIT Kharagpur study:** continuous low rainfall for about **900 years** drove the decline and migration.
[13:3:3] - **Ghaggar-Hakra:** the densest cluster of Harappan sites; its drying ended cities like Kalibangan.
[13:3:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.105; app notes 1:13 (sub 2)
## SUB 4 | Invasion Theory and Its Rejection
[13:4:0] - **Wheeler's case:** the Rig Veda's fort-breaking Indra (Purandara) and the huddled skeletons of Mohenjodaro.
[13:4:1] - **Rejected because:** the Rig Veda postdates the decline; the skeletons belong to different levels (multiple burials, not a massacre); there is no evidence of large-scale conflict between Late Harappans and Indo-Aryans.
[13:4:2] - **Rakhigarhi DNA (female, c. 4,600 years ago):** no **steppe pastoralist** component → challenges invasion theories, supports **indigenous continuity**; migration (not invasion) is still argued from other DNA studies.
[13:4:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.105-107; app notes 1:13 (sub 1)

=== NOTES 1:14 | Aryan Question – Original Home Theories and Archaeological Correlates
## SUB 0 | Meaning of 'Arya' – A Speech Group, Not a Race
[14:0:0] - **'Aryan' = a speech (linguistic) group:** speakers of Indo-Aryan, a branch of the **Indo-European** family – not an ethnic group, nomadic people or "superior race" (UPSC 1999, asked 3 times).
[14:0:1] - **'Arya' in classical Sanskrit:** a **noble man**; originally associated with "**high birth**" (UP Lower Sub 1998).
[14:0:2] - **Max Muller:** popularised the term in modern scholarship and was the first European to speak of an "**Aryan race**" (SSC 2006), though he later said it meant only a language group.
[14:0:3] - **William Jones** (18th c.) found the Sanskrit–Latin–Greek kinship that led to the idea of the Indo-European family.
[14:0:4] - **Languages in India:** Indo-European (Aryan) about **73%** of speakers (NextGen); **Dardic** branch – Jammu and Kashmir.
[14:0:5] > Exam angle: "The term Aryan denotes" → **a speech group**.
[14:0:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.18; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.120-122; NextGen Indian Geography APPSC Part 15 p.18; app notes 1:14 (sub 0)
## SUB 1 | Avesta and Boghazkoi – Linguistic and Epigraphic Evidence
[14:1:0] - **Avesta:** the **oldest Iranian** (Zoroastrian) text, in **Avestan, an old Iranian language**; closely similar to the Rig Veda → relates to **Iran** (UP Lower Sub 2004; APPSC FBO).
[14:1:1] TABLE head: Boghazkoi inscription | Fact
  [14:1:1.0] Place | Asia Minor (Turkey) – Hittite capital
  [14:1:1.1] Date | c. 1380 BCE (14th century BCE)
  [14:1:1.2] Content | Treaty between a Hittite king and a Mitanni king
  [14:1:1.3] Gods named | Indra, Mitra, Varuna, Nasatyas – not Agni
  [14:1:1.4] Significance | Vedic-speaking people or gods in West Asia; taken as evidence of Aryan movement via Iran
[14:1:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Mansehra and Shahbazgarhi are Ashokan rock edicts; Junagarh is Rudradaman's – none names Vedic gods.
[14:1:3] - **Other early Indo-European records:** Kassite (Iraq, c. 1600 BCE), Mitanni (Syria, 14th c. BCE).
[14:1:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.19, p.22; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.121; app notes 1:14 (sub 1)

## SUB 4 | Migration Debate – Archaeology and Genetics
[14:4:0] - **Mainstream:** Indo-Aryans came from Central Asia in waves, the Rig Vedic wave c. **1500 BCE**; migration, not invasion (supporters: **Asko Parpola**, Romila Thapar, Upinder Singh – migration with interaction and assimilation).
[14:4:1] - **Route:** **Andronovo** (steppe, horses, chariots) → **BMAC** (Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, north Afghanistan – contact zone) → **Gandhara Grave culture** (Swat, horse remains, new burials): **3-1-2**.
[14:4:2] - **Indigenous / Out of India:** **S.R. Rao, B.B. Lal, Shrikant Talageri**; B.B. Lal linked **Painted Grey Ware** with the Vedic tradition (PGW–Later Vedic link still open).
[14:4:3] - **Genetics:** haplogroup **R1a**; 2021 *Science* paper – steppe migration 2000–1500 BCE.
[14:4:4] - **Rakhigarhi** (Hisar, Haryana; largest known Harappan site, on the seasonal **Drishadvati**): DNA of a woman of about 4,600 years ago with **no steppe ancestry**; **Mound 7** burial plot – **56** skeletons.
[14:4:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.106-107; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.22; app notes 1:14 (sub 3, 4)
## SUB 5 | Dasas, Dasyus and Panis
[14:5:0] - **Dasas:** a branch of the early Aryans (mentioned in Iranian literature); the Aryan chief was soft towards them.
[14:5:1] - **Dasyus:** the original inhabitants; possibly phallus-worshippers who kept no cattle for dairy; **Trasadasyu** = soft to Dasas, hostile to Dasyus.
[14:5:2] - **Divodasa** of the Bharata clan defeated **Sambara**.
[14:5:3] - **Panis:** non-Aryan cattle-holders and traders, condemned for greed.
[14:5:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.26; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.124; app notes 1:14 (sub 6)

## SUB 1 | Battle of Ten Kings and the Tribes
[15:1:0] # Dasarajna (Battle of Ten Kings)
[15:1:1] - **Where:** banks of the **Parushni (Ravi)** (UPPCS 2008) – not the Vitasta, Vipasa or Asikni.
[15:1:2] - **Victor:** **Sudas**, Bharata king of the **Tritsu** family, with the priest **Vasishtha**.
[15:1:3] - **Opponents:** a confederacy of ten tribes – five Aryan (**Puru, Yadu, Turvasa, Anu, Druhyu**) and five non-Aryan – whose priest was **Vishvamitra**; rivalry of Vasishtha and Vishvamitra led to the war.
[15:1:4] - **Source:** **Mandala VII** of the Rigveda.
[15:1:5] - **After:** the defeated **Purus** joined the Bharatas → **Kurus**; the Kurus and Panchalas ruled the upper Ganga basin.
[15:1:6] - **NDA 2012 "not true":** "Purus sided with the Bharatas" ✗ – they were in the confederacy. The Rig Veda does mention Dasarajna and Sudas; it has about 300 non-Indo-European words.
[15:1:7] # Tribes
[15:1:8] - **Panchajana:** **Anu, Druhyu, Yadu, Turvasa, Puru** – **Kikata** is not one (UPPCS 2009, twice); the Kikatas lived in **south Bihar (Magadha)**.
[15:1:9] - **Other tribes:** Bharatas, Tritsus, Krivis, Srinjayas; **Bharatavarsha** is named after the **Bharatas**.
[15:1:10] - **Divodasa** (Bharata) defeated **Sambara**.
[15:1:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.26-27; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.124-125; app notes 1:15 (sub 4, 5)
## SUB 2 | Sapta Sindhu, Chronology and Geography
[15:2:0] - **Period:** Early Vedic c. **1500–1000 BCE**; oldest core = **family books, Mandalas II–VII**.
[15:2:1] - **Sapta Sindhu:** land of seven rivers – Indus, its five Punjab tributaries and the Sarasvati; spread over **eastern Afghanistan, NWFP (Khyber Pakhtunkhwa), Punjab and Haryana**; the Later Vedic core shifted east to the Ganga–Yamuna Doab.
[15:2:2] - **Ganga is not one of the Sapta Sindhu**; Vipas is.
[15:2:3] - **Gandhara Grave culture (Swat):** horse remains – linked to the early Indo-Aryans.
[15:2:4] - **Terms:** **Himavant** = Himalayas; **Mujavant** = soma mountain in the **Hindu Kush**; **Dhanva** = desert land; **samudra** = a collection of water (the **Satapatha Brahmana** names the eastern and western seas).
[15:2:5] - **Later regions:** **Brahmarshi Desh** = Ganga–Yamuna Doab and adjoining area; **Madhya Desh** = between the Himalayas and the **Vindhyas**.
[15:2:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.18, p.20, p.22, p.29; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.123; app notes 1:15 (sub 0, 1, 3)

## SUB 1 | Nature of the Polity – Rajan and Tribal Chiefdom
[16:1:0] - **Tribal polity:** kinship-based **tribal chiefdom** (APPSC SQT 2018) – no formal bureaucracy, **no fixed territory**, no standing army.
[16:1:1] - **Hierarchy:** **Jana** (tribe; 275 mentions) → **Vis** (clan, under **Vispati**) → **Grama** (under **Gramani**) → **Kula** (family, under **Kulapa**); **janapada** is absent from the Rigveda.
[16:1:2] - **Rajan:** called **Gopati** (lord of cattle) / gopajanasya; protector of the tribe and cattle, **not ruler of a territory**; queen = **Mahisi**.
[16:1:3] - **Succession:** **monarchy**; the post tended to be **hereditary** (with traces of choice by the Samiti) → "**descended (hereditary) monarchy**" (RAS 1993).
[16:1:4] - **Divinity:** kingship not divine – only the Puru king **Trasadasyu** is called **ardhadeva** (demi-god).
[16:1:5] - **Later Vedic contrast:** territorial kingship, **Rajasuya**, **ratnins** and regular taxes.

=== NOTES 1:18 | Later Vedic Period – Eastward Expansion, Iron (krishna Ayas), Painted Grey Ware
## SUB 0 | Eastward Expansion – Kuru-Panchala, Videgha Madhava, Agastya
[18:0:0] # Period and spread
[18:0:1] - **Later Vedic (post-Rigvedic) period = 1000–600 BC (BCE).** Texts composed in the upper Gangetic basin.
[18:0:2] - **Course:** **Punjab → Ganga-Yamuna Doab** (Kurukshetra, Delhi, upper Doab) → **Kosala** (eastern UP), **Kashi** (Varanasi), **Videha** (north Bihar), reached by about 600 BCE.
[18:0:3] - **Kurus** = union of the **Bharatas and Purus**; first between the Sarasvati and Drishadvati, then Delhi and the upper Doab (Kurukshetra). **Panchalas** = middle Doab (Bareilly, Badaun, Farrukhabad).
[18:0:4] - **Kuru-Panchala** = **centre / axis of Aryan culture** in the post-Vedic period: the Vedic texts were compiled here (not Anga-Magadha, Kosala-Videha or Matsya-Shurasena).
[18:0:5] TABLE head: Place | Capital of
  [18:0:5.0] Hastinapur (Meerut district) | Kuru kingdom (Kauravas)
  [18:0:5.1] Indraprastha (Purana Qila, Delhi) | Kuru kingdom (Pandavas)
  [18:0:5.2] Ahichchhatra (Ramnagar, Bareilly) | Panchala
[18:0:6] # Videgha Madhava legend (Shatapatha Brahmana)
[18:0:7] - **Videgha Madhava** (Mathava), king of Videha, lived on the **Sarasvati**; with his priest **Gautama Rahugana** he followed **Agni Vaishvanara** (sacrificial fire) as it burnt the forests eastward.
[18:0:8] - They crossed the **Sadanira (Gandak)**; the land beyond was settled as **Videha**.
[18:0:9] - Read as the eastward movement and agricultural colonisation by **burning and clearing forests** (fire and iron axes).
[18:0:10] # Aryanisation of the South
[18:0:11] - **Agastya** = the sage said to have **Aryanised South India** (crossed the Vindhyas); not Vishvamitra, Vasishtha or Apastamba.
[18:0:12] > Axis of Aryan culture = Kuru-Panchala; Videgha Madhava's priest = Gautama Rahugana; river crossed = Sadanira (Gandak).

[18:0:14] - **Hastinapura** (Meerut district, UP) lies about 100 km north-east of **Delhi** – of Lucknow, Patna, Delhi, Chandigarh the nearest modern city is Delhi.
## SUB 1 | Pottery Sequence and Painted Grey Ware
[18:1:0] # Sequence of protohistoric pottery (Ganga-Yamuna Doab)
[18:1:1] TABLE head: Order | Pottery | Date | Phase
  [18:1:1.0] 1 | Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) | 2000–1500 BCE | Chalcolithic
  [18:1:1.1] 2 | Black and Red Ware (BRW) | 1450–1200 BCE | Chalcolithic; also Later Vedic; Megalithic south
  [18:1:1.2] 3 | Painted Grey Ware (PGW) | 1200–500 BCE | Early Iron Age / Later Vedic
  [18:1:1.3] 4 | Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) | 700–100 BCE (from c. 600 BCE in the Ganga valley) | Mahajanapadas, second urbanisation
[18:1:2] - **OCP** is an ill-fired red ware that leaves an **ochre colour on the fingers**; **christened (named) at Hastinapur by B.B. Lal (1950–52)**, not Ahichchhatra, Noh or Red Fort.
[18:1:3] - **Four wares known to the Later Vedic people:** **Red Ware** (most popular), **Black-slipped Ware**, **Black-and-Red Ware**, **Painted Grey Ware** (most distinctive) = I, II, III, IV. **NBPW is NOT** Later Vedic (post-Vedic, shiny black).
[18:1:4] # Painted Grey Ware culture
[18:1:5] - **Grey pottery with black linear / geometric painted designs**; mainly **bowls and dishes**; deluxe tableware of the upper strata; not polished black.
[18:1:6] - **Pottery of 1000–600 BC in the Gangetic plain = PGW.**
[18:1:7] - About **700 sites**, mainly in the **upper Ganga basin** and the Indo-Gangetic divide (Punjab, Haryana, western UP) – not the lower Indus basin.
[18:1:8] - First found at **Ahichchhatra** (excavations 1940–44); **Hastinapur** excavation (1950–52, **B.B. Lal**) established it as a culture.
[18:1:9] - Finds: **iron weapons**, copper objects (copper possibly from **Khetri**, Rajasthan), glass, bone, semi-precious beads (trade, barter; no coins).
[18:1:10] TABLE head: Site | Region
  [18:1:10.0] Hastinapur | Meerut, western UP (Kuru capital)
  [18:1:10.1] Atranjikhera | Etah, western UP (Doab)
  [18:1:10.2] Ahichchhatra | Bareilly, UP
  [18:1:10.3] Bhagwanpura | Haryana – 13-roomed mud house; pre-iron PGW with late Harappan pottery
  [18:1:10.4] Noh | Rajasthan (OCP–BRW–PGW sequence)
[18:1:11] - Early PGW (Ghaggar-Hakra, Bhagwanpura) has **no iron**; later Doab PGW is iron-using.
[18:1:12] > Later Vedic wares exclude NBPW; PGW = upper Ganga basin, 1000–600 BC; OCP named at Hastinapur.

[18:2:2] # Iron in North India
[18:2:3] - Iron technology about **1000–800 BCE**; common at most **PGW sites** of the Doab; iron weapons (arrowheads, spearheads) common in western UP from about 800 BCE.

## SUB 3 | Agriculture, Settlements and Crafts
[18:3:0] - **Agriculture** became the main livelihood; wooden ploughshare common; ploughs drawn by **6, 8, 12 and even 24 oxen**.
[18:3:1] TABLE head: Crop | Later Vedic term
  [18:3:1.0] Rice | Vrihi (ritual use; rice remains of 8th c. BCE at Hastinapur, Atranjikhera)
  [18:3:1.1] Wheat | Godhuma
  [18:3:1.2] Barley | Yava (continued)
  [18:3:1.3] Sugarcane | Ikshu

## SUB 4 | Ratnins and Officials
[19:4:0] - **Ratnins ("jewels")** = the king's council of **12** royal functionaries: purohita, rajan, mahishi, senani, bhagadugha, sangrahitri, suta, kshata, govikartana, gramani, parivakti, akshavapa (some add palagala); honoured in the **Ratnahavimshi** rite of the Rajasuya.
[19:4:1] TABLE head: Official | Function
  [19:4:1.0] Purohita | Chief priest
  [19:4:1.1] Senani | Army chief
  [19:4:1.2] Suta | Charioteer and herald
  [19:4:1.3] Bhagadugha | Revenue collector (king's share)
  [19:4:1.4] Sangrahitri | Treasurer
  [19:4:1.5] Akshavapa | Accountant; superintendent of dice
  [19:4:1.6] Palagala | Messenger
[19:4:2] - More officers but no regular bureaucracy and **no standing army**.
