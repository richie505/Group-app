## SUB 0 | Late Harappan Phase – Features and Sites
[0:0] # Features (c. 1900–1300 BCE, post-urban phase)
[0:1] - **Chalcolithic:** tools of **stone and copper**; **no metal objects requiring complicated casting**; people lived in **villages** (agriculture, stock-raising, hunting, fishing).
[0:2] - **Lost:** the extraordinary **uniformity** of the Mature phase (pottery, seals, bricks, weights), writing, long-distance trade, large public buildings and craft specialisation; pottery became **regionally varied**.
[0:3] - **Bhagwanpura (Kurukshetra, Haryana):** Late Harappan houses of **burnt brick** (so "no burnt bricks at Bhagwanpura" is **false**); Late Harappan pottery overlaps with **PGW**; a 13-roomed house (excavated by **J.P. Joshi**).
[0:4] - **APPSC Group-1 statements:** I chalcolithic ✓, II no complicated casting ✓, III villages ✓, IV no burnt bricks at Bhagwanpura ✗ → **I, II and III**.
[0:5] - **Astt. Comm. 2018:** uniformity of artefacts ✓ (Mature phase); "Late sites kept the same construction techniques" ✗ → **only I**.
[0:6] - **Iron** was absent in the Mature phase and came only later (PGW, Later Vedic).

## SUB 1 | Decline Theories – Proponents
[1:0] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent(s)
  [1:0.0] Aryan invasion | Ramaprasad Chanda (1926), elaborated by Mortimer Wheeler (1947) – Indra "Purandara", huddled skeletons
  [1:0.1] Floods | Marshall, Mackay, S.R. Rao; floods + tectonic uplift – M.R. Sahni
  [1:0.2] Shift of the Indus course | H.T. Lambrick – Mohenjodaro abandoned
  [1:0.3] Climate change / aridity | Aurel Stein, A.N. Ghosh
  [1:0.4] Drying of the Ghaggar-Hakra (Sarasvati) | D.P. Agrawal and Sood – Kalibangan, Banawali abandoned
  [1:0.5] Ecological imbalance | W.A. Fairservis
  [1:0.6] Decline of trade with Mesopotamia (c. 1900 BCE) | trade-decline school
[1:1] - **Other stresses:** **soil salinity**, deforestation, malaria at **Mohenjodaro**; **Chanhudaro** was flooded several times.
[1:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Aurel Stein = climate, **not** Aryan invasion.
[1:3] - **Consensus:** decline was **gradual and multi-causal** – urban collapse over about **200 years** (c. 1900–1700 BCE) and a long tail to c. 1300 BCE; climate change and the drying of the Sarasvati are the most widely accepted causes.

## SUB 2 | Climate and the 4.2 ka Event
[2:0] - **4.2 ka event:** sharp weakening of the monsoon about **4,200 years ago**; the same aridity is linked to the fall of **Akkad** and Old Kingdom Egypt.
[2:1] - **Deoria Tal (Uttarakhand):** pollen record – the **oak-to-pine ratio** shift shows the monsoon **weakened sharply**.
[2:2] - **IIT Kharagpur study:** continuous low rainfall for about **900 years** drove the decline and migration.
[2:3] - **Ghaggar-Hakra:** the densest cluster of Harappan sites; its drying ended cities like Kalibangan.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.105; app notes 1:13 (sub 2)
## SUB 3 | Invasion Theory and Its Rejection
[3:0] - **Wheeler's case:** the Rig Veda's fort-breaking Indra (Purandara) and the huddled skeletons of Mohenjodaro.
[3:1] - **Rejected because:** the Rig Veda postdates the decline; the skeletons belong to different levels (multiple burials, not a massacre); there is no evidence of large-scale conflict between Late Harappans and Indo-Aryans.
[3:2] - **Rakhigarhi DNA (female, c. 4,600 years ago):** no **steppe pastoralist** component → challenges invasion theories, supports **indigenous continuity**; migration (not invasion) is still argued from other DNA studies.
[3:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.105-107; app notes 1:13 (sub 1)
## SUB 4 | OCP, Copper Hoards and Harappan–Vedic Continuity
[4:0] - **Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP):** the Ganga–Yamuna Doab Late Harappan phase; **Copper Hoards** often found with OCP in the **Doab**.
[4:1] - **Continuity view:** identification of the Ghaggar-Hakra with the Rigvedic **Sarasvati**; Bhagwanpura's **Late Harappan + PGW** overlap.
[4:2] - **Traits that continued:** crafts, pottery, beads, weights, agriculture – **not iron tools**, which are post-Harappan.
[4:3] - **Rakhigarhi:** the only Harappan site so far to yield **ancient DNA**.
[4:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.8; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.28; app notes 1:13 (sub 5)
