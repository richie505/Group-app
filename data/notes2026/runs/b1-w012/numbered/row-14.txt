## SUB 0 | Meaning of 'Arya' – A Speech Group, Not a Race
[0:0] - **'Aryan' = a speech (linguistic) group:** speakers of Indo-Aryan, a branch of the **Indo-European** family – not an ethnic group, nomadic people or "superior race" (UPSC 1999, asked 3 times).
[0:1] - **'Arya' in classical Sanskrit:** a **noble man**; originally associated with "**high birth**" (UP Lower Sub 1998).
[0:2] - **Max Muller:** popularised the term in modern scholarship and was the first European to speak of an "**Aryan race**" (SSC 2006), though he later said it meant only a language group.
[0:3] - **William Jones** (18th c.) found the Sanskrit–Latin–Greek kinship that led to the idea of the Indo-European family.
[0:4] - **Languages in India:** Indo-European (Aryan) about **73%** of speakers (NextGen); **Dardic** branch – Jammu and Kashmir.
[0:5] > Exam angle: "The term Aryan denotes" → **a speech group**.
[0:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.18; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.120-122; NextGen Indian Geography APPSC Part 15 p.18; app notes 1:14 (sub 0)
## SUB 1 | Avesta and Boghazkoi – Linguistic and Epigraphic Evidence
[1:0] - **Avesta:** the **oldest Iranian** (Zoroastrian) text, in **Avestan, an old Iranian language**; closely similar to the Rig Veda → relates to **Iran** (UP Lower Sub 2004; APPSC FBO).
[1:1] TABLE head: Boghazkoi inscription | Fact
  [1:1.0] Place | Asia Minor (Turkey) – Hittite capital
  [1:1.1] Date | c. 1380 BCE (14th century BCE)
  [1:1.2] Content | Treaty between a Hittite king and a Mitanni king
  [1:1.3] Gods named | Indra, Mitra, Varuna, Nasatyas – not Agni
  [1:1.4] Significance | Vedic-speaking people or gods in West Asia; taken as evidence of Aryan movement via Iran
[1:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Mansehra and Shahbazgarhi are Ashokan rock edicts; Junagarh is Rudradaman's – none names Vedic gods.
[1:3] - **Other early Indo-European records:** Kassite (Iraq, c. 1600 BCE), Mitanni (Syria, 14th c. BCE).
[1:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.19, p.22; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.121; app notes 1:14 (sub 1)

## SUB 4 | Migration Debate – Archaeology and Genetics
[4:0] - **Mainstream:** Indo-Aryans came from Central Asia in waves, the Rig Vedic wave c. **1500 BCE**; migration, not invasion (supporters: **Asko Parpola**, Romila Thapar, Upinder Singh – migration with interaction and assimilation).
[4:1] - **Route:** **Andronovo** (steppe, horses, chariots) → **BMAC** (Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, north Afghanistan – contact zone) → **Gandhara Grave culture** (Swat, horse remains, new burials): **3-1-2**.
[4:2] - **Indigenous / Out of India:** **S.R. Rao, B.B. Lal, Shrikant Talageri**; B.B. Lal linked **Painted Grey Ware** with the Vedic tradition (PGW–Later Vedic link still open).
[4:3] - **Genetics:** haplogroup **R1a**; 2021 *Science* paper – steppe migration 2000–1500 BCE.
[4:4] - **Rakhigarhi** (Hisar, Haryana; largest known Harappan site, on the seasonal **Drishadvati**): DNA of a woman of about 4,600 years ago with **no steppe ancestry**; **Mound 7** burial plot – **56** skeletons.
[4:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.106-107; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.22; app notes 1:14 (sub 3, 4)
## SUB 5 | Dasas, Dasyus and Panis
[5:0] - **Dasas:** a branch of the early Aryans (mentioned in Iranian literature); the Aryan chief was soft towards them.
[5:1] - **Dasyus:** the original inhabitants; possibly phallus-worshippers who kept no cattle for dairy; **Trasadasyu** = soft to Dasas, hostile to Dasyus.
[5:2] - **Divodasa** of the Bharata clan defeated **Sambara**.
[5:3] - **Panis:** non-Aryan cattle-holders and traders, condemned for greed.
[5:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.26; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.124; app notes 1:14 (sub 6)
