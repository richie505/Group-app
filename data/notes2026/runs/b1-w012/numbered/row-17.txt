=== NEW NOTES row 17
## SUB 0 | Pastoral Economy – The Cow
[0:0] - **Economy:** **mainly pastoral**, agriculture secondary (mixed economy).
[0:1] - **Cow = most valuable property** (UPPCS 2015) and the **standard unit of value and exchange**; **Gau** named **176 times**.
[0:2] - **Aghanya** = "**not to be killed**" – the Rig Vedic term for the **cow** (UPPCS 2008; UDA 2010; UPPSC 2017).
[0:3] - **Cattle words:** **Gavishti** (war = search for cows), **Gomat** (rich man with many cattle), **Duhitri** (daughter = one who milks), **Godhuli** (dusk, when cattle return – time), **Gavyuti** (distance), **Goghna** (guest fed on cattle).
[0:4] - **Horse:** asva is mentioned 215 times – vital for war and movement.
[0:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.27; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.125; app notes 1:17 (sub 0)

  [1:0.0] Yava | Barley – the chief Rigvedic grain
  [1:0.1] Dhanya | Grain / cereals

  [1:0.3] Godhuma | Wheat (later texts)

  [1:0.5] Ikshu | Sugarcane
  [1:0.6] Sira / Langala | Plough drawn by bulls / wooden ploughshare
  [1:0.7] Sita | Furrow
  [1:0.8] Datra | Sickle
  [1:0.9] Urvara | Fertile land
  [1:0.10] Kshetra | Cultivated field
  [1:0.11] Kulya | Canal
  [1:0.12] Avata | Well
[1:1] - **Rice, wheat and cotton are not Rigvedic crops** – barley (yava) is the staple crop.
[1:2] - **RAS 2021 match:** Vrihi–rice, Mudga–mung, Yava–millet (only grain left in List-II), Ikshu–sugarcane → **A-ii, B-iii, C-iv, D-i**.
[1:3] - **Staple food:** **milk and its products** (ghee, curd), with barley (SSC 2008).
[1:4] - **Chief livelihood (UPPSC 1993):** cattle-rearing with farming; of the options, **agriculture**, which became the main livelihood in the Later Vedic age.
[1:5] - **Later Vedic contrast:** agrarian economy; **bhaga = 1/6** of produce; bali, bhaga and shulka collected by the **Bhagadugha**.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.27; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.126-127; app notes 1:17 (sub 1)

[2:0] TABLE head: Craftsman | Term
  [2:0.0] Carpenter (chariots, boats) | Takshan
  [2:0.1] Smith / metal worker | Karmara
  [2:0.2] Goldsmith | Hiranyakara
  [2:0.3] Potter | Kulala
  [2:0.4] Weaver | Vaya
  [2:0.5] Physician | Bhishak
  [2:0.6] Chariot-maker | Rathakara
[2:1] - **Ayas** = **copper or bronze**; iron = **krishna ayas** (Later Vedic) – so the **blacksmith** craft was not in use (SSC 2003).
[2:2] - **Nishka:** a **gold neck ornament** (nishka-griva) in the Rigveda; later a **gold coin** (UPSC 1993; UPPCS 2007).
[2:3] - **Exchange:** barter, **no coinage**; the cow was the unit of value.
[2:4] - **Panis:** the **traders**, rich in cattle, condemned for greed and for hiding cattle (MPPSC 2019).
[2:5] - **Stupa:** the word first occurs in the **Rigveda**, meaning a **tree stem** / raised heap (CGPSC 2015).
[2:6] - **Occupational mobility:** hymn of a poet whose father is a physician and mother a grinder of corn.
[2:7] - **Bali:** a voluntary offering by tribesmen to the rajan.
[2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.27; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.33, p.126; app notes 1:17 (sub 2, 3)
## SUB 3 | Family, Social Units and Varna Origins
[3:0] - **Family:** **patriarchal, patrilineal, patrilocal**, joint (three generations) under the kulapa; **monogamy** usual.
[3:1] - **Units (ascending):** **Kula – Grama – Vis – Jana**; **Vaishya** derives from **Vis**.
[3:2] - **Varna:** the word first occurs in the **Rigveda** (UPPSC 2012) – **arya varna vs dasa varna** = colour / cultural-linguistic distinction.
[3:3] - **Purusha Sukta (Rigveda X.90, 10th Mandala – a late addition):** first reference to the **four-fold varna** division (APPSC G1 2003, G1 PYQ) – Brahmana from the **mouth**, Kshatriya from the **arms**, Vaishya from the thighs, Shudra from the feet.
[3:4] - **Shudra:** first appears in the **10th Mandala**; not in the family books.
[3:5] - **Gotra:** in the Rigveda = cowpen; as lineage first in the **Atharvaveda**; gotra exogamy not yet established.
[3:6] - **Slavery:** slave women given as gifts to **priests**.
[3:7] - **Change:** the shift from cattle-tribe to agrarian economy explains later territorial kingship and varna hierarchy.
[3:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.22, p.28-29; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.127, p.129; app notes 1:17 (sub 4, 5)
## SUB 4 | Position of Women
[4:0] - **Status:** relatively good (better than later) within a patriarchal society – "**somewhat superior**" (APPSC Sociology 2002); it **declined** in the Later Vedic period.
[4:1] - **Rights:** education, **choice of husband**, attendance at **assemblies**, sacrifices **with the husband**; no child marriage; **monogamy**; **niyoga** (childless widow with the husband's brother) and widow remarriage; could own cattle.
[4:2] - **Not:** holding the **highest positions of authority** (CDS 2014 – the incorrect statement); no women priests.
[4:3] - **Women seers (brahmavadinis):** **Lopamudra** (wife of **Agastya**, who Aryanised **South India**), **Ghosha**, **Apala**, **Vishvavara** – Rig Vedic (UPSC 1995).
[4:4] - **⚠ Trap:** **Gargi** is Later Vedic – her dialogue with Yajnavalkya is in the **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad** (not Rigvedic).
[4:5] - **Brahmavadini:** a woman who studied and expounded Vedic knowledge.
[4:6] - **Sons preferred**, but daughters were educated.
[4:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.129-130; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.28, p.31; app notes 1:17 (sub 6)
## SUB 5 | Dress, Drinks and Pastimes
[5:0] - **Garments:** **Nivi** (undergarment), **Paridhana** (garment), **Adhivasa** (outer wrap) (CDS 2005).
[5:1] - **Drinks:** **soma** (ritual drink) and **sura** (fermented, intoxicating drink).
[5:2] - **Pastimes:** chariot racing, dicing – the **gambler's hymn (X.34)**, music and dance.
[5:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 1 p.20; app notes 1:17 (sub 7)
