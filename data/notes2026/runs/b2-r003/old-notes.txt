## SUB 1 | Lengthiest Written Constitution
[3:1:0] # Written, Single Document
[3:1:1] - **Written** like the US Constitution; the British Constitution is **unwritten**.
[3:1:2] - Contained in a **single document**.
[3:1:3] # Number Game – Original vs Now
[3:1:4] TABLE head: Item | Original (26 Nov 1949) | Now (2026)
  [3:1:4.0] Articles | 395 (with Preamble) | about 470
  [3:1:4.1] Parts | 22 | 25
  [3:1:4.2] Schedules | 8 | 12
[3:1:5] - New provisions are added as **lettered Articles** (e.g. **21A, 51A**); numbering still ends at **Article 395**.
[3:1:6] Old notes say 9 original Schedules and 397 Articles – wrong. Original = 8 Schedules, 395 Articles, 22 Parts.
[3:1:7] # Reasons for Length
[3:1:8] - India's **size, diversity and complexity**.
[3:1:9] - Heavy influence of the **Government of India Act, 1935**.
[3:1:10] - **One Constitution for both Centre and States**.
[3:1:11] - Dominance of **legal luminaries** (lawyers) in the Assembly.
[3:1:12] - Detailed **administrative provisions**.

## SUB 3 | Parliamentary Form of Government
[3:3:0] # Features and Reasons
[3:3:1] - Follows the **British model**, not the American presidential one.
[3:3:2] - **Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (Art. 74(1), Art. 75).
[3:3:3] - Ministers are **members of the legislature**.
[3:3:4] - Chosen because: already **familiar under the GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935**; suits India's **size and diversity**; avoids executive–legislature conflict.
[3:3:5] - India is described as **a Union of States with a parliamentary system**.
[3:3:6] - Cardinal features: **democratic republic**, **parliamentary form**, **supreme power vests in the People**; power not in a single authority (separation of powers).
[3:3:7] # India vs USA vs Britain
[3:3:8] TABLE head: Point | India | Other
  [3:3:8.0] Executive | Parliamentary (British model) | USA: presidential, separation of powers
  [3:3:8.1] Legislature–executive | Cooperation and coordination | USA: separation
  [3:3:8.2] Parliament | Not sovereign – a creature of the Constitution | Britain: parliamentary sovereignty
  [3:3:8.3] Head of State | Elected (indirectly) | Britain: hereditary
  [3:3:8.4] Citizenship | Single | USA: dual; states have own constitutions
  [3:3:8.5] Judicial review | Narrower (Art. 21: "procedure established by law") | USA: judicial supremacy
[3:3:9] APPSC GS 2019: "Britain's head of State is elected" = WRONG. India's head is elected; Indian Parliament is not sovereign.
[3:3:10] - **Origins:** the **presidential** form of government began in the **USA**; the **parliamentary** form began in **Britain**.
## SUB 4 | Democracy, Republic and Constitutionalism
[3:4:0] # Terms and Meanings
[3:4:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:4:1.0] Representative (indirect) democracy | People elect representatives; no referendum, initiative or recall nationally
  [3:4:1.1] Republic | No hereditary ruler; head of State elected
  [3:4:1.2] Sovereign democratic republic | Elected head; ultimate authority with the people
  [3:4:1.3] Parliamentary Democracy | What India is
  [3:4:1.4] Ultimate source of authority | "We, the People of India" (Preamble)
  [3:4:1.5] Supreme authority | The Constitution, not Parliament
[3:4:2] - IYB 2026 description: "a **union of states**, a **Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic** with a parliamentary system of government".
[3:4:3] - President elected **indirectly**; parliamentary system combined with **republicanism**.
[3:4:4] - **Democracy:** from **Greek** demos (people) + kratos (rule); earliest direct democracy **Athens** – not Latin.
[3:4:5] # Constitutionalism
[3:4:6] - **Constitutionalism**: government power **limited by a written, supreme Constitution**; power used by rules, not the ruler's wish (**absolute rule**).
[3:4:7] - **Constitutional government**: government limited by the terms of the Constitution.
[3:4:8] - Limits in India: **Fundamental Rights (Art. 13** – inconsistent laws void), **judicial review**, federal division of powers, **basic structure** (**Kesavananda Bharati, 1973**).
[3:4:9] - Parliament's power to amend is limited by the **basic structure doctrine**.
[3:4:10] - Basic-structure elements: **supremacy of the Constitution**, rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, limited amending power.
[3:4:11] # Supremacy of the Constitution
[3:4:12] - Constitution is the **supreme law**; **Union and States derive their powers from it**.
## SUB 5 | Federal System with Unitary Bias
[3:5:0] TABLE head: Federal features | Unitary features
  [3:5:0.0] Two levels of government (later three) | Strong Centre (Canada model)
  [3:5:0.1] Division of powers (three Lists) | Single citizenship
  [3:5:0.2] Written, supreme Constitution | Integrated judiciary
  [3:5:0.3] Independent judiciary | Emergency provisions
  [3:5:0.4] Bicameral Parliament | States have no separate constitutions
[3:5:1] - **Quasi-federal**: federal in form, unitary in spirit (Wheare).
[3:5:2] - In an emergency the **federal structure turns unitary without formal amendment**.
[3:5:3] - Indian States have **no separate constitutions and no dual citizenship** (the USA has both).
## SUB 6 | Blend of Rigidity and Flexibility
[3:6:0] TABLE head: Method | Examples
  [3:6:0.0] Simple majority of Parliament (outside Art. 368) | Admission of new States, change of State boundaries
  [3:6:0.1] Special majority of Parliament (Art. 368) | Most provisions (e.g. Fundamental Rights)
  [3:6:0.2] Special majority + ratification by half the State legislatures | Federal provisions
[3:6:1] - Neither as rigid as the US nor as flexible as the British Constitution.
## SUB 7 | Secular State
[3:7:0] - India is a **secular state**: **no state religion**.
[3:7:1] - **Indian model (positive)**: equal respect to all religions; the State may legislate on religious matters for social reform.
[3:7:2] - **Non-believers:** secularism protects freedom of religion and of **non-religion**; atheists are equally covered.
[3:7:3] - **Western model (negative)**: strict separation of State and religion.
[3:7:4] - Freedom of religion: **Articles 25–28**.
[3:7:5] A theocratic / religious state is NOT a feature (APPSC GS 2018).
## SUB 8 | Other Salient Features
[3:8:0] # Judiciary
[3:8:1] - **Synthesis of parliamentary sovereignty (Britain) and judicial supremacy (USA)**: courts can strike down laws.
[3:8:2] - **Integrated and independent judiciary**: a single court system enforces both **Central and State laws**.
[3:8:3] # Rights, Duties and Franchise
[3:8:4] TABLE head: Feature | Provision
  [3:8:4.0] Fundamental Rights | Six categories; all suspendable in National Emergency except Arts. 20 and 21
  [3:8:4.1] Right to Property | Moved out of FRs; now Article 300A
  [3:8:4.2] Fundamental Duties | 11 (42nd Amendment + one by the 86th)
  [3:8:4.3] Universal adult franchise | From the start; voting age 21 → 18 by 61st Amendment, 1988
[3:8:5] # Bodies, Emergency and Tiers
[3:8:6] - **Independent bodies**: ECI, CAG, UPSC, SPSCs.
[3:8:7] - **Emergency provisions**: **Part XVIII, Arts. 352–360** – National, State (President's Rule), Financial.
[3:8:8] - **Three-tier government**: **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992)** added the local tier to the original two-level polity.
[3:8:9] - Features form the base of the basic structure list: supremacy, federalism, secularism, parliamentary system, judicial review, independence of judiciary.
