=== Democracy, Republic and Parliamentary Government
# Democracy
- **Word origin:** **Greek** – *demos* (people) + *kratos* (rule); not Latin, Roman or French (APPSC G2 Mains 2019; Town Planning).
- **India = representative (indirect) democracy:** people elect representatives who govern (APPSC DL 2017; SSC CGL 2016).
- **Direct democracy is NOT found in India** at the national level – no **referendum, initiative or recall** (APSLPRB 2019: "Direct democracy is found in India" = the incorrect statement).
- **Tools of direct democracy:** **recall** = removing an elected representative before his term; **referendum** = people vote on a law/issue; **initiative** = people propose a law; **plebiscite** = people's verdict on a political question (APSLPRB Constable 2023: recall).
- **Democracy needs people's participation:** without participation and co-operation democracy fails → **people should participate and co-operate with the government** (not compulsion, not a switch to the presidential system).
- India is a **democratic country** and also has a **Constitution of its own** – both true, but having a constitution does not by itself make a country democratic (UPPSC 2017 A–R: both true, R not the explanation).
- **Not authoritarian:** India has democratic government, rule of law and a government that follows the Constitution; **authoritarian government is NOT a feature** (UPPSC Mains 2011).
# Republic
- **Republic = no hereditary ruler; the head of State is elected** (directly or indirectly) for a fixed term (UPPSC 1997, 2017; Panchayat Secretary).
- India's head of State, the **President, is elected indirectly** – the parliamentary system is **combined with republicanism** (a nominal elected head, real power with ministers) (RAS 2013 A–R: A true, R true and the correct explanation).
- **"Republic" in the Preamble** – idea from the **French** Constitution (with liberty, equality, fraternity).
- India is a **"Union of States"** (Art. 1), a **Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic with a parliamentary system**.
- **Cardinal features of the Indian political system:** **democratic republic**, **parliamentary form of government**, **supreme power vests in the people**, and a **unified authority** (one Constitution, single citizenship, integrated judiciary) → **all four** (UPPSC 2009).
# Parliamentary form of government
- India chose the **British parliamentary system**, not the American **presidential** system → India is a **parliamentary democracy** (UPPSC 2005); **presidential government is NOT a feature** (UPPSC Mains 2015).
- **Parliamentary system** = **co-operation and co-ordination** between legislature and executive; **presidential** = **separation of powers**.
- **Features:** nominal (President) and real (Council of Ministers) executive; majority party rule; **collective responsibility of the Council of Ministers to the Lok Sabha** (Art. 75); ministers are **members of the legislature**; leadership of PM/CM; dissolution of the lower House. Adopted at both **Centre and States**.
- **Why chosen:** already **familiar** (worked under the GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935); suits India's **vast size and diversity**; avoids **executive–legislature conflict** seen in the USA.
- **Origins:** parliamentary form began in **Britain**; presidential form in the **USA**.
# India vs the British model (UPSC 2021)
| Point | India | Britain |
|---|---|---|
| Parliament | **Not sovereign** – a creature of a written Constitution; its law-making power is **limited** (FRs, federalism, judicial review) | **Sovereign** – supreme in legislation |
| Validity of laws / amendments | Courts can strike them down; cases on interpreting the Constitution (incl. validity of an Amendment) go to a **Constitution Bench** of at least **5 judges** (**Art. 145(3)**) | No judicial review of Acts of Parliament |
| Head of State | **Elected** (indirectly) – republic | **Hereditary** monarch |
| Citizenship | Single | Single |

=== Written and Lengthiest Constitution – Parts, Schedules, Articles
# Written and lengthiest
- **A Constitution** = the document that lays down the **basic rules** for the people and the government (SSC 2018).
- **Written constitution tradition began in the USA** – the **US Constitution (1787)** is the **first written** national constitution (and the shortest: 7 Articles); the **British** Constitution is **unwritten** (MPPSC 2015).
- India's is the **lengthiest written constitution** of the world – longer than those of Canada, South Africa or Australia (APPSC HWO).
- **Reasons for length:** vast size and **diversity**; influence of the bulky **GoI Act 1935**; **single Constitution for Centre and States**; dominance of **legal luminaries**; detailed **administrative provisions**; matters left to ordinary law elsewhere put in the Constitution.
- **Original copies:** **two handwritten (calligraphed) originals – English and Hindi** – kept in helium/inert-gas cases in the **Parliament Library** (APPSC Inspector of Factories); English calligraphy by **Prem Behari Narain Raizada**, Hindi by **Vasant Krishan Vaidya**, decorated by Nandalal Bose's Santiniketan artists (members also signed a printed English version on 24 Jan 1950).
# Number game
| Item | Original (26 Nov 1949) | Now (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Articles | **395** (with Preamble) | about **470** (numbering still ends at Art. 395) |
| Parts | **22** | **25** |
| Schedules | **8** | **12** |
- **Schedules now = 12** (the 12th added by the 74th Amendment, 1992) – also 12 in 2003 (APPSC G2 2003; Junior Steno).
- **Parts now = 25:** 22 + 4 added (**IVA, IXA, IXB, XIVA**) − 1 repealed (**VII**) (SSC 2017). Older books say 24 Parts – that count is from before Part IXB (97th Amendment, 2011).
- New provisions are added as **lettered Articles** (e.g. **21A, 51A**); numbering still ends at **Article 395**.
- **⚠ Trap:** some books print "9 original Schedules / 397 Articles" – wrong. Original = **8 Schedules, 395 Articles, 22 Parts**.

=== Constitutionalism and Supremacy of the Constitution
# Constitutionalism
- **Constitutionalism** = government whose powers are **limited** by a supreme Constitution; it **recognises the need to limit the powers of government** (APPSC G2 2011).
- It is **not inherent in every constitution** – a country can have a constitution without constitutionalism (a dictator's constitution).
- **Antithesis of constitutionalism = despotism** (absolute, unlimited rule); limited power and restraint of power are its features (APPSC G2 2011).
- **Constitutional government** = one that places **effective restrictions on the authority of the State in the interest of individual liberty** – not restrictions on liberty in the interest of the State (UPSC 2014: **II only**).
- **Limits in India:** Fundamental Rights (**Art. 13** – laws inconsistent with FRs are void), judicial review, federal division of powers, **basic structure** (**Kesavananda Bharati, 1973**).
- Parliament's amending power is limited by the **basic structure doctrine**; basic-structure elements include **supremacy of the Constitution**, rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, limited amending power.
# Supremacy of the Constitution
- The **Constitution is supreme** in the Indian political system – not Parliament, not the Supreme Court, not religion (UPPSC Mains 2013).
- **Governmental organs owe their origin to the Constitution** – the Union and States derive their powers from it (APPSC Asst. Social Welfare).
- **Why the Constitution is above Parliament:** the **Constitution specifies how Parliament is formed and what its powers are** (not because it is older or its makers were more eminent) (APPSC HWO).
# Judicial review
- **Judicial review** = power of the SC/HCs to declare laws and executive orders **unconstitutional**; the phrase is **nowhere used in the Constitution**, but **Arts. 13, 32, 226** (and others) **explicitly confer** the power.
- **Basic structure is NOT defined in the Constitution** – it was evolved by the Supreme Court (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973) (UPSC 2020, statement I wrong).
- **Synthesis** of British **parliamentary sovereignty** and American **judicial supremacy**: courts can strike down laws; Parliament can amend most of the Constitution.
- **Scope narrower than in the USA:** India has **"procedure established by law"** (Art. 21), the USA **"due process of law"**; courts test a law only **against the supreme written Constitution** (FR violation, lack of competence, repugnancy), not its wisdom → judicial review is **restricted because the Constitution is supreme**, not because the executive or legislature is supreme.
> UPSC 2020: II (Constitution provides for judicial review) is correct on the text of Arts. 13, 32, 226 → **Only II**. (UPSC's official key said D, "Neither", on the view that the Constitution never uses the term "judicial review".)

=== Other Salient Features – Amendment, Emergency, Rights, Duties, Franchise
# Blend of rigidity and flexibility
| Method | Examples |
|---|---|
| Simple majority of Parliament (outside Art. 368) | Admission of new States, change of State boundaries |
| Special majority of Parliament (Art. 368) | Most provisions (e.g. Fundamental Rights) |
| Special majority + ratification by half the State legislatures | Federal provisions |
- **Amending power: Article 368** (Part XX) (APPSC Social History 2019).
- **First amendment: 1951** (1st Amendment Act – added the 9th Schedule).
- Neither as rigid as the US nor as flexible as the British Constitution.
# Rights, duties, franchise
- **Fundamental Rights:** six categories (Part III); all suspendable in a National Emergency except **Arts. 20 and 21**; Right to Property moved out → now **Art. 300A**.
- **DPSP** (Part IV) – Ambedkar's "novel feature".
- **Fundamental Duties:** added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** on the **Swaran Singh Committee**'s recommendation – **10** duties; the **86th (2002)** added the 11th → **11** now (Part IVA, Art. 51A).
- **Universal adult franchise** from the start (1950) at **21 years**; voting age **changed once – 21 → 18 by the 61st Amendment (1988, effective 1989)**; the same age for Lok Sabha and Assembly elections; no literacy or property test.
- **Education:** moved from the State List to the **Concurrent List** by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**.
# Emergency, bodies, tiers
- **Three kinds of emergency:** **National (Art. 352)**, **State / President's Rule (Art. 356)**, **Financial (Art. 360)** – Part XVIII, Arts. 352-360 (APSLPRB SI 2017).
- **Independent bodies:** ECI, CAG, UPSC, SPSCs.
- **Integrated and independent judiciary:** one court system enforces **both Central and State laws**.
- **Three-tier government:** **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992)** added the local tier.
- **Kerala** was formed on **1 November 1956** (States Reorganisation Act, 1956) in place of the **Part B State of Travancore-Cochin** (APPSC MPDO 1998).

=== Federal System with Unitary Bias
| Federal features | Unitary features |
|---|---|
| Two levels of government (later three) | Strong Centre (Canada model) |
| **Division of powers** (three Lists, 7th Schedule) | **Single citizenship** |
| Written, **supreme** Constitution | Integrated judiciary |
| Rigid constitution | Emergency provisions |
| **Independent judiciary** | States have no separate constitutions |
| Bicameral Parliament | Governor appointed by the Centre; all-India services |
- The word **"federation" is not used** in the Constitution; India is a "Union of States".
- **Prominent federal features** asked: **division of powers**, **independent judiciary**, **supremacy of the Constitution** – "**allocation of financial resources**" is **not** listed as one (APPSC G2 Prelims 2017); the Finance Commission, integrated judiciary and emergency powers are not "the" federal feature (Sachivalayam: **division of powers**).
- **Single citizenship**, not dual: **dual citizenship is NOT a feature** (APPSC Draughtsman); Part II deals with citizenship.
- **Quasi-federal**: federal in form, unitary in spirit (Wheare); in an emergency the structure turns **unitary without a formal amendment**.
- Indian States have **no separate constitutions and no dual citizenship** (the USA has both).

=== Secular State
- India is a **secular state**: it **does not uphold any religion as the official religion** – the Indian State has **no religious basis** (APPSC Asst. Director).
- **"Secular" was added to the Preamble** by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** → the **Preamble** describes India as a secular State (Deputy Information Engineers).
- **Indian model (positive):** equal respect to / equal protection of all religions; **Western model (negative):** complete separation of State and religion.
- **Secular provisions:** Preamble; Arts. 14, 15, 16, **25-28** (freedom of religion), 29, 30, 44.
- **Religious (theocratic) state is NOT a feature** of the Constitution (APPSC DL 2020).
