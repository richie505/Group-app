=== NOTES 1:98 | Reddi Kingdoms of Addanki, Kondavidu and Rajahmundry – Prolaya Vema Reddi to Kumaragiri Reddi
## SUB 0 | Court Poets – Errana and Srinatha
[98:0:0] # Errana (Errapragada)
[98:0:1] - **Patron:** court poet of the founder **Prolaya Vema Reddi** (APPSC G2 2011, 2012; APPSC PYQ ×5) – not Anavota, Anavema, Allada Reddi or Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka.
[98:0:2] - **Dedication:** his **Harivamsam** is dedicated to **Prolaya Vema**.
[98:0:3] - **Kavitrayam:** **last of the Kavitrayam**; completed the Telugu Mahabharata (**remaining Aranya Parva – Aranya Parva shesham**).
[98:0:4] - **Works:** **Nrisimha (Narasimha) Puranam** (APPSC G2 2017; PYQ ×2), Harivamsam, Ramayana.
[98:0:5] - **Titles:** **Prabandha Parameswara**, **Sambhudasa**.
[98:0:6] - **⚠ Trap:** the **Vidyadhikari** (education officer) post belonged to **Srinatha**, not Errana – a stem that calls Errana "Vidyadhikari" still points to his patron Prolaya Vema.
[98:0:7] # Srinatha (c. 1365–1441)
[98:0:8] - **Patron and office:** **Vidyadhikari** (education officer) under **Pedakomati Vema Reddi** (APPSC G1 2002; G2 2001, 2002; Welfare Officers 2017; PYQ ×2); composed his Sanskrit inscriptions.
[98:0:9] - **Title:** **Kavi Sarvabhauma**; also Andhra Bhasha Naishadhabhava, Sarvavidya Parangata.
[98:0:10] - **At Vijayanagara:** defeated **Gauda Dindima Bhatta**, court poet of **Deva Raya II** → **Kanakabhishekam**, **Ganda Penderam** and the Kavisarvabhauma honour.
[98:0:11] - **Later life:** after 1424 joined the Rajahmundry court of **Veerabhadra Reddi**; after 1448, poverty in Palnadu.

## SUB 1 | Society, Festivals and Women
[98:1:0] # Vasantotsavam and Dance
[98:1:1] - **Vasantotsavam (spring festival):** the **most popular festival** of the Reddi period – colours and scents sprinkled (Holi-like) (APPSC G2 PYQ).
[98:1:2] - **Popularised by:** **Kumaragiri Reddi**, who held it every year → title **Karpura Vasantarayalu** (Welfare Officers 2017 ×2); Anavema too was called Vasantaraya.
[98:1:3] - **Funding:** merchant **Avachi Tippayya Setti**, made head of the **Sugandha Bhandagara** (scent store) for the festival (recorded in Haravilasam).
[98:1:4] - **Court dancer:** **Lakumadevi** – **Bharatanatyam**, at **Kumaragiri's** court (APPSC G2 2012; PYQ ×3) – not Machaladevi, Chellava or Muddupalani.
[98:1:5] - **Literature on him:** **C. Narayana Reddy** wrote **Karpura Vasanta Rayalu** on Kumaragiri.
[98:1:6] # Women and Tanks
[98:1:7] TABLE head: Woman | Fact
  [98:1:7.0] Surambika (Suryambika) | Queen of Pedakomati Vema; dug the Santana Sagaram (Santana Payonidhi) tank at Phirangipuram (APPSC G2 2019; PYQ ×2)
  [98:1:7.1] Lakumadevi | Court dancer of Kumaragiri Reddi
  [98:1:7.2] Anitalli | Issued the Kaluvacheru inscription (1423)
[98:1:8] - **Tank inscription:** composed by **Srinatha**; her son **Racha Vema** cut the **Jaganobbaganda** canals to feed it.
[98:1:9] - **⚠ Trap:** **Govardhana Samudram**, Anna Samudram and Vemana Samudram are NOT Surambika's tank – the answer is **Santana Sagaram**.
[98:1:10] # Social Life
[98:1:11] - **Castes:** warrior-farmer backbone **Kammas, Kapus, Reddis, Velamas**; traders **Komatis**; tax-free Brahmin **agraharas**.
[98:1:12] - **Customs:** sati, devadasis, cockfights, ram fights, hero worship (Veerapuja); belief in **Parusavedi** (alchemy).
[98:1:13] - **Devadasis:** trained in music, dance and ritual; respected performers at **Shiva temples** (incl. Pancharamas).
[98:1:14] - **Time:** measured with clocks – the **Ghatika Yantra** (water clock), called **Ghatikagara** in the APPSC key (Telugu "gadiyaram" = clock).
[98:1:15] - **Music:** instrument **Jalakaranda** – **Dharmasagara inscription**; desi dances Jakkini, Perani; foreign dance Parasika.
[98:1:16] ~~ Sources: NextGen post kakatiyas p.6, p.9, p.12-14; NextGen AP History - APPSC Exams 08 p.24-25; Rocket Sheets p.7; app notes 1:98 (sub 7)
[98:1:17] - **Perantallu:** women who committed **sati** were deified as demi-goddesses and worshipped as **Perantallu** in Reddi-period Andhra.

## SUB 3 | Rulers, Capitals and Chronology
[98:3:0] # The Reddi Kingdom
[98:3:1] - **Period:** about **1325–1448 CE** – **Kondavidu line c. 1325–1424**; **Rajahmundry line 1402–1448**.
[98:3:2] - **Successor state:** one of the three states after **1323** (fall of the Kakatiyas) – **Reddis, Musunuris, Recherla Velamas**.
[98:3:3] - **Called:** the **"second golden age" of Telugu** culture – kings were themselves authors.
[98:3:4] - **Features:** **Panta** clan (Deseti kula); emblem **Bull**; family deity **Mula Guramma**; royal language **Sanskrit**.
[98:3:5] # Kondavidu Rulers in Order
[98:3:6] TABLE head: Ruler | Period | Key fact
  [98:3:6.0] Prolaya Vema Reddi (founder) | 1325–1353 | Capital Addanki; expelled Tughlaq forces; 74 forts; Errana
  [98:3:6.1] Anavota (Anapota) Reddi | 1353–1364 | Shifted capital to Kondavidu; developed Motupalli; abolished gold tax
  [98:3:6.2] Anavema Reddi (greatest) | 1364–1386 | Most charitable; beat Kalinga Gajapatis; Veera Siromandapam
  [98:3:6.3] Kumaragiri Reddi | 1386–1402 | Vasantarajiyam; Vasantotsavam; Lakumadevi
  [98:3:6.4] Pedakomati Vema Reddi | 1402–1420 | Sangeeta Chintamani; Srinatha
  [98:3:6.5] Racha Vema Reddi (last) | 1420–1424 | Puriti Pannu; killed by his people
[98:3:7] - **Order (APPSC G2 Mains 2025; Welfare Officers):** Prolaya Vema → **Anavota → Anavema → Kumaragiri → Pedakomati Vema** → Racha Vema.
[98:3:8] - **Prolaya Vema's reign:** up to **1353** (Mani Kesavara inscription) – the "1325–1353" span (Inter Board 2012).
[98:3:9] - **Kumaragiri succeeded Anavema**; Pedakomati occupied Kondavidu (1402) because Kumaragiri was childless.
[98:3:10] # Capitals and Forts
[98:3:11] - **Sequence:** **Addanki → Kondaveedu (Kondavidu) → Rajamahendravaram (Rajahmundry)**.
[98:3:12] - **Who shifted Addanki → Kondavidu:** **Anavota (Anapota) Reddi**, son of Prolaya Vema – **Manyapuram inscription** (APPSC G2 2012; PYQ) – not Anavema, Kumaragiri or Pedakomati.
[98:3:13] TABLE head: Capital / fort | Region | Feature
  [98:3:13.0] Addanki | Prakasam | First capital, Prolaya Vema
  [98:3:13.1] Kondavidu | Guntur (near Chilakaluripet) | Replaced Addanki; later fought over by Gajapatis and Vijayanagara
  [98:3:13.2] Rajahmundry | Godavari | Second seat, 1402–1448
  [98:3:13.3] Kondapalli | Krishna | Hill fort credited to Prolaya Vema
[98:3:14] - **Forts:** Prolaya Vema built **74 forts**, including **Kondaveedu, Kondapalli and Dharanikota**.
[98:3:15] - **Kondapalli fort:** main gate **Dargah Darwaza** (tomb of Gulab Shah); **Golconda Darwaza** at the other end; palace **Tanisha Mahal**.
[98:3:16] - **⚠ Trap:** Kondavidu is in Guntur – NOT in East Godavari or near Eluru.
[98:3:17] # Prolaya Vema Reddi – Founder
[98:3:18] - **Career:** served **Prataparudra**, then in **Kapaya Nayaka's** court (Anitalli / Kaluvacheru inscription); founded the kingdom in **1324/1325** after expelling **Tughlaq** forces from coastal Andhra.
[98:3:19] - **Brother Mallareddi:** took **Motupalli**, repulsing the Bahmani **Hasan Gangu** (1347).
[98:3:20] # Anavema and Kumaragiri
[98:3:21] - **Anavema:** with the **Padmanayakas and Vijayanagara** beat the **Kalinga Gajapatis**; took **Nidadavolu** (gave it to Choda Bhima), **Rajahmundry, Pithapuram, Simhachalam, Konaseema** – not Kandukur; minister **Mamidi Peddanamatya**, army chief **Chennamanayaka**; **most charitable** Reddi king.
[98:3:22] - **Kumaragiri:** **Pedakomati Vema**, who opposed his accession, was made **feudatory of Chandolu** and heir; brother-in-law **Kataya Vema** conquered the east up to **Cuttack**.
[98:3:23] - **Rajahmundry division:** first under his son **Anavota**, then **Kataya Vema (1395)**.
[98:3:24] - **1402:** Pedakomati seized Kondavidu; Kumaragiri fled to Rajahmundry.
[98:3:25] - **Pedakomati Vema:** recovered territory from **Vijayanagara (1404)**; died fighting the Velamas (1420).
[98:3:26] - **Racha Vema:** last Kondavidu king; killed as a tyrant – **Kondaveeti Kaifiyat**.

## SUB 4 | Trade – Motupalli Port and Haravilasam
[98:4:0] - **Haravilasam (Srinatha):** the Reddi-period work giving information on **sea-borne (foreign) trade**, imports and exports (APPSC G2 2011 ×2, 2012, 2019; G2 Mains; PYQ) – not Harivamsam, Kasikhandam or Bhimeswara Puranam.
[98:4:1] - **Dedicated to:** merchant **Avachi Tippayya Setti**, famous for **overseas trade** (China, Sinhala, Sumatra, Malaya).
[98:4:2] - **Assertion–Reason:** Haravilasam gives sea-trade information (A) and is dedicated to Tippayya Setti (R) – **both true, R not the explanation**.
[98:4:3] - **Motupalli:** chief Reddi port; taken by **Mallaya Reddi**; dock developed by **Anavota**.
[98:4:4] - **Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam:** issued by **Somaya**, minister of **Anavota**, to protect and encourage trade (bilingual Telugu–Tamil; lower duties).
[98:4:5] - **Motupalli epigraph:** fixes **tariff rates** on port trade.
[98:4:6] - **Imports:** Chinese goods, sandalwood, camphor, spices; elephants from Sri Lanka.
[98:4:7] - **Traders:** **Komatis** ran inland and coastal trade; trade centres Penugonda, Kondaveedu, Rajahmundry, Draksharama.
[98:4:8] - **Earlier route:** the Buddhist **Vinayavatthu** mentions the **"Andhra Path"**.
[98:4:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen post kakatiyas p.10, p.13; NextGen AP History - APPSC Exams 08 p.23-26; Rocket Sheets p.9; app notes 1:98 (sub 6)
## SUB 5 | Religion and Temple Building
[98:5:0] - **Popular religion:** **Veerashaivism** (APPSC G2 2011; PYQ); the kings patronised **Pashupata Shaivism** – the tempting wrong option.
[98:5:1] - **Ghoderayas:** Shaiva gurus who spread Shaivism from **Tripurantakam**; the Reddi kings were their disciples.
[98:5:2] - **Vaishnavism:** **Vadagalai** matha at **Ahobilam**; **Kumaragiri and Kataya Vema** were Vaishnava patrons.
[98:5:3] - **Srisailam practices:** Bhrugupatham (leaping from the hill); **Champudu Gullu** at Tripurantakam.

## SUB 7 | Administration and Taxes
[98:7:0] - **Puriti Pannu:** tax on **childbirth**, levied by **Racha Vema**, the last Kondavidu king, who was killed by his people for it (APPSC G2 2019; PYQ ×2) – not Kumaragiri, Kataya Vema or Anavota.
[98:7:1] TABLE head: Tax | Meaning
  [98:7:1.0] Puriti Pannu | Childbirth – Racha Vema
  [98:7:1.1] Kanika | Presents / gifts to king or officials
  [98:7:1.2] Pullari | Cattle-grazing tax (Kakatiya usage)
  [98:7:1.3] Illari | House tax
  [98:7:1.4] Ari | Land tax
  [98:7:1.5] Meruka | Tax on wetlands
  [98:7:1.6] Gold tax | Abolished by Anavota
[98:7:2] - **Land revenue:** Bhumi Sistu – the main income; land measured with the Kesarpati gada.
[98:7:3] - **Village:** **12 Ayagars** – **Karnam** (accountant), **Reddi / Peddakapu** (headman), **Talari** (watchman); paid in **Manyam** land or **Mera** (crop share).
[98:7:4] - **Exempt:** temple lands, Brahmin grants and Ayagar lands.

## SUB 0 | Liberation Movement and Prolaya Nayaka
[99:0:0] # Fall of Warangal (1323)
[99:0:1] - **1323:** Warangal fell; **Prataparudra** died (jumped into the Narmada); **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** renamed it **Sultanpur (Sultanpurti)**; Andhra under **Malik Burhanuddin**, deputy **Malik Maqbul**.
[99:0:2] - Of Prataparudra's **75** generals only **three** survived: **Prolaya Nayaka, Recherla Singama, Prolaya Vema Reddi**.
[99:0:3] # The Liberation Movement (Vimochanodyamam)
[99:0:4] - **What:** the **first organised resistance to Muslim rule** in Andhra – the "freedom struggle of the Andhras"; Andhra freed within about two years.
[99:0:5] - **Leader:** **Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka** (APPSC PYQ ×2; G2) – not Prolaya Vema Reddi, Singama, Kapaya, Kolani Rudra or Annayadeva.
[99:0:6] - **Headquarters:** **Rekapalli** (near Bhadrachalam).
[99:0:7] - **Participants (did not lead):** Prolaya Vema Reddi, Recherla Singama, **Koppula Prolaya Nayaka**, Kolani Rudra, Vengi Bhupala, Manchikonda Ganapati Nayaka, Kapaya Nayaka.
[99:0:8] - **Kaluvacheru grant (1423):** issued by the Reddi queen **Anitalli**; names the **75 Nayakas** who backed Prolaya.
[99:0:9] - **Successor states:** **Musunuri (1325–1368), Recherla Velama (1325–1475), Reddi (1325–1448)**.
[99:0:10] # Prolaya Nayaka – Founder of the Musunuri Kingdom
[99:0:11] - **Family:** son of **Potinayaka**; **Kamma** community (**Chaturthanvayas**); surname from the village **Musunur**.
[99:0:12] - **Capital:** **Rekapalli** on the **Godavari** near Bhadrachalam (APPSC G2 2017; PYQ ×2) – not Rachakonda (Recherla) or Sangama.
[99:0:13] - **Vilasa copper-plate grant (1330):** issued by **Prolaya Nayaka**; describes **conditions after the fall of the Kakatiyas** – Tughlaq atrocities, his rise, agrarian relief, **agraharas restored** (village Vilasa to the Brahmin Vennama) (PYQ ×2).
[99:0:14] - **Successor:** childless → his cousin **Kapaya Nayaka**.
[99:0:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen post kakatiyas p.1-4; Rocket Sheets p.6, p.42; app notes 1:99 (sub 0, 1)

## SUB 2 | Kapaya Nayaka – Titles, Warangal and the Bahmanis
[99:2:0] - **1336:** with Hoysala **Veera Ballala III**, **Prolaya Vema Reddi** and **Singama**, took **Orugallu (Warangal)** from **Malik Maqbul**; ruled from Warangal (1336–1368).
[99:2:1] - **Nephew Anapota Nayaka** = governor of **Vengi**; **Polavaram inscription** names 14 members of the lineage, only 3 ruled.
[99:2:2] # Titles

  [99:2:3.2] Katakachurakara | Kapaya

[99:2:5] # Kapaya and the Bahmanis
[99:2:6] TABLE head: Year | Event

  [99:2:6.1] 1350 | Lost Kaulas
  [99:2:6.2] 1356 | Lost Bhuvanagiri
  [99:2:6.3] 1361 | Son Vinayaka Deva burnt alive by Muhammad Shah I
  [99:2:6.4] 1364–65 | Truce: Golconda ceded + 33 lakhs, turquoise throne, elephants, horses; Golconda became the boundary
  [99:2:6.5] 1367–68 | Battle of Bhimavaram: Anapota Nayaka (Recherla) killed Kapaya – Musunuri rule ends; Telangana to the Velamas
[99:2:7] - **Allies sought:** **Bukka I** of Vijayanagara; **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** of Delhi.
[99:2:8] - **After Kapaya:** the **Reddis took coastal Andhra**, the **Velamas absorbed Musunuri Telangana**.

## SUB 5 | Recherla Velamas – Rulers, Capitals and Forts
[99:5:0] - **Also called:** **Padmanayakas**; began as Kakatiya feudatories; family history **Velugoti Varivamsavali**; lineage founder **Betala Nayudu**; dated **c. 1325–1475**.
[99:5:1] TABLE head: Ruler | Fact
  [99:5:1.0] Singama Nayaka I | Founder of the independent kingdom; freed Nalgonda–Mahabubnagar; expanded to the Krishna–Tungabhadra doab; killed by the Jallipalli chiefs (not at Bhimavaram)
  [99:5:1.1] Anapota Nayaka | Singama's son; capital Amanagallu → Rachakonda; won Bhimavaram (1367–68)
  [99:5:1.2] Sarvagna Singa Bhupala (II) | Sanskrit scholar-king of Rachakonda
[99:5:2] - **Devarakonda:** second branch capital; **Mada Nayaka**, younger brother of **Anapota Nayaka**, made its ruler; succeeded by **Pedda Vedagiri**.
[99:5:3] - **End:** **Sultan Quli Qutb Shah** of Golconda annexed **Rachakonda and Devarakonda**.
[99:5:4] - **Forts:** **Rachakonda** – **cyclopean masonry without mortar**; **Devarakonda** – **14th-century hilltop fort**.
[99:5:5] - **⚠ Trap:** Anapota **Nayaka** (Velama) killed Kapaya; Anapota **Reddi** moved the Reddi capital to Kondaveedu.

## SUB 0 | Bahmani Kingdom – Founder and Capitals
[100:0:0] - **Founded:** **1347** (14th century) by **Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah** – **Hasan Gangu / Zafar Khan** (UPPSC 1995, 2005; UKPSC 2002; State PSC) – not Firuz Shah, Mahmud Gawan, Malik Ambar or Mujahid Shah.
[100:0:1] - **First capital:** **Gulbarga**, renamed **Hasanabad** (CGPSC 2014) – not Bidar or Daulatabad.
[100:0:2] - **Four regions:** **Gulbarga, Daulatabad, Berar, Bidar** (Golconda was not one).
[100:0:3] - **Andhra link:** **Kapaya Nayaka** helped Hasan Gangu found it.
[100:0:4] - **Bidar:** **Ahmad Shah I** shifted the capital from Gulbarga to Bidar (APPSC G2 2003).
[100:0:5] - **Mahmud Gawan:** minister of **Muhammad III**; kingdom largest under him; **executed in 1481** – the kingdom then broke into five sultanates.
[100:0:6] - **⚠ Dates:** **1336** = Vijayanagara; **1347** = Bahmani.
[100:0:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen qutabshahis p.1; NextGen post kakatiyas p.4; app notes 1:100 (sub 0)
## SUB 1 | Krishnadevaraya and Prataparudra Gajapati
[100:1:0] - **Defeated Gajapati:** **Prataparudra Gajapati** (APPSC PYQ) – Kalinga campaign **1513–1519**.
[100:1:1] - **Order of campaign:** 1. **Udayagiri** (Nellore) – fell after an **18-month siege**; 2. Kandukuru, Vinukonda, Addanki, **Kondaveedu**; 3. **Kondapalli**, Bejawada; 4. march to **Cuttack**.
[100:1:2] - **Victory pillars:** at **Potnuru (Potnur)** and **Simhachalam** (APPSC G2-B 1991) – not Penugonda, Lepakshi or Hampi.
[100:1:3] - **Peace (c. 1518–19):** Prataparudra's daughter **Annapurna Devi** (Bhadradevi / Tukkadevi) married Krishnadevaraya; **lands north of the Krishna returned** to the Gajapati.
[100:1:4] - **⚠ Not a term:** **Cuttack and the Gajapati heartland were NOT annexed**.
[100:1:5] - **Earlier:** **Saluva Narasimha** drove the Gajapatis out of south-eastern Andhra by **c. 1470** and occupied land up to the Godavari.

## SUB 3 | Telugu Language, Earliest Inscriptions and Court Languages
[101:3:0] # The language
[101:3:1] - **Family:** **Dravidian** (South-Central branch, with **Gondi and Kui**) – not Aryan, Sanskrit or Pali families (Dy. EO 2008).
[101:3:2] - **Oldest** spoken and literary language of South India = **Tamil** (Polytechnic Lecturers 2012); Tamil got classical status first (2004).
[101:3:3] - **"Italian of the East":** **Telugu** – coined by the Venetian **Niccolo de' Conti** (15th c.) because its words **end in vowels**.
[101:3:4] - **"Desa bhashalandu Telugu lessa":** **Krishnadevaraya**, in Amuktamalyada.
[101:3:5] - **Official languages of AP:** **Telugu** (since 1966) and **Urdu** as second official language (**all districts** since the 2022 amendment).
[101:3:6] - **Classical language:** Telugu and **Kannada**, **31 October 2008**; total **11** classical languages after **3 October 2024**.
[101:3:7] - **Telugu Language Day:** **29 August**, birth anniversary of **Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy** (not Gurajada).
[101:3:8] - **Trilinga** theory: Srisailam, Kaleshwaram, Draksharama.
[101:3:9] # Before written Telugu literature
[101:3:10] - **Satavahana royal language = Prakrit**; **Hala's Gathasaptasati** is in **Prakrit** (Maharashtri), with Telugu words like atta, potta, pilla, pandi.
[101:3:11] - **First Telugu word:** **Nagabu** (Amaravati; found by **Veturi Prabhakara Sastri**); **Vepuru** – Myakadoni; **-ambul** – Chikkulla plates (Vishnukundins).

[101:3:20] - **Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi:** asked "official language" → **Telugu** (G2 2008; NextGen previous papers; Rocket) – Telugu entered royal records and literature under them; ⚠ the prasasti (praise) of their grants stayed in **Sanskrit**, grant details in **Telugu**.
[101:3:21] - **Kakatiyas:** inscriptions **mostly in Telugu** (APPSC PYQ key) – Telugu became the administrative language; ⚠ NextGen calls Sanskrit their royal language; Telugu grammar, poetics and desi metres grew in this age.

## SUB 4 | Kreedabhiramam and Kakatiya Court Works
[101:4:0] # Kreedabhiramam
[101:4:1] - **Author:** **Vinukonda Vallabharaya (Vallabhamatya)**, early 15th c.; a Telugu rendering of **Ravipati Tripurantakudu's** Sanskrit **Premabhiramam**.
