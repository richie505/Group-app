=== NEW NOTES row 99
## SUB 0 | Liberation Movement and Prolaya Nayaka
[0:0] # Fall of Warangal (1323)
[0:1] - **1323:** Warangal fell; **Prataparudra** died (jumped into the Narmada); **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** renamed it **Sultanpur (Sultanpurti)**; Andhra under **Malik Burhanuddin**, deputy **Malik Maqbul**.
[0:2] - Of Prataparudra's **75** generals only **three** survived: **Prolaya Nayaka, Recherla Singama, Prolaya Vema Reddi**.
[0:3] # The Liberation Movement (Vimochanodyamam)
[0:4] - **What:** the **first organised resistance to Muslim rule** in Andhra – the "freedom struggle of the Andhras"; Andhra freed within about two years.
[0:5] - **Leader:** **Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka** (APPSC PYQ ×2; G2) – not Prolaya Vema Reddi, Singama, Kapaya, Kolani Rudra or Annayadeva.
[0:6] - **Headquarters:** **Rekapalli** (near Bhadrachalam).
[0:7] - **Participants (did not lead):** Prolaya Vema Reddi, Recherla Singama, **Koppula Prolaya Nayaka**, Kolani Rudra, Vengi Bhupala, Manchikonda Ganapati Nayaka, Kapaya Nayaka.
[0:8] - **Kaluvacheru grant (1423):** issued by the Reddi queen **Anitalli**; names the **75 Nayakas** who backed Prolaya.
[0:9] - **Successor states:** **Musunuri (1325–1368), Recherla Velama (1325–1475), Reddi (1325–1448)**.
[0:10] # Prolaya Nayaka – Founder of the Musunuri Kingdom
[0:11] - **Family:** son of **Potinayaka**; **Kamma** community (**Chaturthanvayas**); surname from the village **Musunur**.
[0:12] - **Capital:** **Rekapalli** on the **Godavari** near Bhadrachalam (APPSC G2 2017; PYQ ×2) – not Rachakonda (Recherla) or Sangama.
[0:13] - **Vilasa copper-plate grant (1330):** issued by **Prolaya Nayaka**; describes **conditions after the fall of the Kakatiyas** – Tughlaq atrocities, his rise, agrarian relief, **agraharas restored** (village Vilasa to the Brahmin Vennama) (PYQ ×2).
[0:14] - **Successor:** childless → his cousin **Kapaya Nayaka**.
[0:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen post kakatiyas p.1-4; Rocket Sheets p.6, p.42; app notes 1:99 (sub 0, 1)
## SUB 1 | Kapaya Nayaka – Titles, Warangal and the Bahmanis
[1:0] - **1336:** with Hoysala **Veera Ballala III**, **Prolaya Vema Reddi** and **Singama**, took **Orugallu (Warangal)** from **Malik Maqbul**; ruled from Warangal (1336–1368).
[1:1] - **Nephew Anapota Nayaka** = governor of **Vengi**; **Polavaram inscription** names 14 members of the lineage, only 3 ruled.
[1:2] # Titles
[1:3] TABLE head: Title | Holder
  [1:3.0] Andhra Suratrana | Kapaya Nayaka – first holder (APPSC PYQ; G2 2011 ×2; Welfare Officers)
  [1:3.1] Andhradesadhisvara | Kapaya (earlier Kakatiya Ganapatideva)
  [1:3.2] Katakachurakara | Kapaya
[1:4] - **⚠ Andhra Suratrana:** NextGen and Rocket give it **only to Kapaya**; one G2 2011 key gave **Anavema Reddi**, who is said to have used it later. **Kavirajamarga** is NOT Kapaya's title.
[1:5] # Kapaya and the Bahmanis
[1:6] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [1:6.0] 1347 | Kapaya helped Hasan Gangu found the Bahmani kingdom at Gulbarga
  [1:6.1] 1350 | Lost Kaulas
  [1:6.2] 1356 | Lost Bhuvanagiri
  [1:6.3] 1361 | Son Vinayaka Deva burnt alive by Muhammad Shah I
  [1:6.4] 1364–65 | Truce: Golconda ceded + 33 lakhs, turquoise throne, elephants, horses; Golconda became the boundary
  [1:6.5] 1367–68 | Battle of Bhimavaram: Anapota Nayaka (Recherla) killed Kapaya – Musunuri rule ends; Telangana to the Velamas
[1:7] - **Allies sought:** **Bukka I** of Vijayanagara; **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** of Delhi.
[1:8] - **After Kapaya:** the **Reddis took coastal Andhra**, the **Velamas absorbed Musunuri Telangana**.
[1:9] - **Ferishta's Tarikh-i-Ferishta** calls Telangana **"Tilang"**.
[1:10] - **Order:** Fall of Warangal (1323) → Vilasa grant (1330) → Orugallu recovered (1336) → Bhimavaram (1367–68). Vijayanagara (1336) arose **before** the Bahmanis (1347).
[1:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen post kakatiyas p.4-5; Rocket Sheets p.47; app notes 1:99 (sub 1, 2)

## SUB 3 | Recherla Velamas – Rulers, Capitals and Forts
[3:0] - **Also called:** **Padmanayakas**; began as Kakatiya feudatories; family history **Velugoti Varivamsavali**; lineage founder **Betala Nayudu**; dated **c. 1325–1475**.
[3:1] TABLE head: Ruler | Fact
  [3:1.0] Singama Nayaka I | Founder of the independent kingdom; freed Nalgonda–Mahabubnagar; expanded to the Krishna–Tungabhadra doab; killed by the Jallipalli chiefs (not at Bhimavaram)
  [3:1.1] Anapota Nayaka | Singama's son; capital Amanagallu → Rachakonda; won Bhimavaram (1367–68)
  [3:1.2] Sarvagna Singa Bhupala (II) | Sanskrit scholar-king of Rachakonda
[3:2] - **Devarakonda:** second branch capital; **Mada Nayaka**, younger brother of **Anapota Nayaka**, made its ruler; succeeded by **Pedda Vedagiri**.
[3:3] - **End:** **Sultan Quli Qutb Shah** of Golconda annexed **Rachakonda and Devarakonda**.
[3:4] - **Forts:** **Rachakonda** – **cyclopean masonry without mortar**; **Devarakonda** – **14th-century hilltop fort**.
[3:5] - **⚠ Trap:** Anapota **Nayaka** (Velama) killed Kapaya; Anapota **Reddi** moved the Reddi capital to Kondaveedu.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen post kakatiyas p.5; app notes 1:99 (sub 3)
## SUB 4 | Velama Battles and Foreign Relations
[4:0] TABLE head: Battle | Opponents | Outcome

  [4:2.4] Gajapatis | Lingamanidu of Devarakonda sought help from Gajapati prince Hamvira
