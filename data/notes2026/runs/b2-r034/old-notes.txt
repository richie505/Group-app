=== NOTES 2:34 | Budget in Parliament – Stages, Cut Motions, Appropriation and Finance Bills, Funds
## SUB 0 | Annual Financial Statement – Article 112
[34:0:0] - The **Budget** = **Annual Financial Statement (AFS)**, **Article 112** (APPSC 2018, 2023).
[34:0:1] - The word **"Budget" is not used in the Constitution**. ("bougette" = French for little bag.)
[34:0:2] - **President** causes the AFS to be **laid before both Houses** every year; presented in the **Lok Sabha** by the **Finance Minister** (Union Budget 2023-24: **Nirmala Sitharaman**, APPSC 2023).
[34:0:3] - Financial year: **1 April – 31 March**.
[34:0:4] - Prepared by the **Budget Division, Department of Economic Affairs (DEA)**, **Ministry of Finance**; process starts with the **Budget Circular** (usually **September**).
[34:0:5] - **Halwa ceremony** (North Block) marks the start of printing of Budget documents.
[34:0:6] - **Economic Survey** is presented **before** the Budget each year.
[34:0:7] - Covers all **three funds**; presented as **Revenue Budget + Capital Budget**.
[34:0:8] FIRST Union Budget of independent India: **26 November 1947**, **R.K. Shanmukham Chetty** (APPSC 2018).
[34:0:9] # Budget Articles in Order
[34:0:10] TABLE head: Article | Subject
  [34:0:10.0] 112 | Annual Financial Statement; 112(3) charged items
  [34:0:10.1] 113 | Procedure for estimates – Demands for Grants
  [34:0:10.2] 114 | Appropriation Bill
  [34:0:10.3] 115 | Supplementary, additional, excess grants
  [34:0:10.4] 116 | Vote on account, vote of credit, exceptional grants
  [34:0:10.5] 117 | Financial Bills
  [34:0:10.6] 266 / 267 | Consolidated Fund and Public Account / Contingency Fund
## SUB 1 | Stages of the Budget
[34:1:0] - 1. **Presentation** of the AFS.
[34:1:1] - 2. **General discussion** – both Houses; no vote.
[34:1:2] - 3. **Scrutiny by Departmentally Related Standing Committees**.
[34:1:3] - 4. **Voting on Demands for Grants** (Art. 113) – **Lok Sabha only**; it may **pass, reject or reduce** (cut) a demand. Estimates are sent as Demands for Grants (Art. 113(2)).
[34:1:4] - 5. **Guillotine** – on the last allotted day, all remaining demands are put to vote **without discussion**.
[34:1:5] - 6. **Passing of the Appropriation Bill** (Art. 114).
[34:1:6] - 7. **Passing of the Finance Bill** – gives effect to the tax proposals.
[34:1:7] Vote on account, vote of credit and exceptional grants are **NOT stages** of normal financial law-making.
[34:1:8] - Rajya Sabha can **discuss** the AFS but **cannot vote** on Demands for Grants.
## SUB 2 | Cut Motions
[34:2:0] TABLE head: Cut motion | Reduces the demand | Purpose
  [34:2:0.0] Policy cut | To Re 1 | Complete disapproval of the policy behind the demand
  [34:2:0.1] Economy cut | By a specified amount | Economy in expenditure
  [34:2:0.2] Token cut | By Rs 100 | Air a specific grievance within government responsibility
[34:2:1] - A cut motion = proposal to **reduce** a demand for grant; aims to draw the House's attention to the matter named.
[34:2:2] - Moved **only in the Lok Sabha**, during **voting on Demands for Grants**.
[34:2:3] - If **passed** → **loss of confidence** → the Government must **resign**.
## SUB 3 | Appropriation Bill and Finance Bill – Article 114
[34:3:0] - Authorises **withdrawal from the CFI** for **voted grants + charged expenditure**, not exceeding the AFS amount.
[34:3:1] - **Art. 114(3)**: no money withdrawn from the CFI **except under appropriation made by law** – even charged expenditure needs an Appropriation Act.
[34:3:2] - **No amendment** may vary the amount or alter the destination of a voted grant, or vary charged expenditure.
[34:3:3] - Carries an **automatic repeal clause** (APPSC G1 2024).
[34:3:4] - Appropriation Bill is a **Money Bill**; **Finance Bill** puts the Budget's **tax proposals** into effect.
[34:3:5] - **Finance Bill amendments:** may **reduce or abolish** a tax but **not increase** it.
[34:3:6] The **President, Prime Minister, Finance Minister and CAG** cannot withdraw from the CFI on their own authority. Right to issue money from the CFI belongs to none of them – only Parliament by law.
[34:3:7] - **Parliament's financial control**: placing the AFS; withdrawal only after the Appropriation Act; rules on supplementary grants.
## SUB 4 | Charged vs Voted Expenditure
[34:4:0] - **Charged** = **non-votable**; not put to vote but **can be discussed** in both Houses. Ensures **independence** of constitutional offices.
[34:4:1] - Three separate questions: who **fixes** the salary (law), which **fund pays**, and whether **charged or voted**.
[34:4:2] # Charged on the Consolidated Fund of India – Art. 112(3) (full list)
[34:4:3] - 1. **President** – emoluments, allowances, office expenses.
[34:4:4] - 2. **Chairman and Deputy Chairman** of RS; **Speaker and Deputy Speaker** of LS – salaries and allowances.
[34:4:5] - 3. **Supreme Court judges** – salaries, allowances, pensions.
[34:4:6] - 4. **High Court judges** – **only pensions** (Art. 112(3)(d)(iii)).
[34:4:7] - 5. **CAG** – salary, allowances, pension, administrative expenses.
[34:4:8] - 6. **Debt charges** – interest, sinking fund charges.
[34:4:9] - 7. Sums to satisfy a **judgment, decree or arbitral award**.

[34:4:14] - **Second Schedule** – emoluments, allowances, privileges; **Third Schedule** – forms of oaths.
## SUB 5 | Special Grants – Articles 115 and 116
[34:5:0] TABLE head: Grant | Article | Meaning
  [34:5:0.0] Supplementary | 115 | Sanctioned amount insufficient
  [34:5:0.1] Additional | 115 | New service not in the Budget
  [34:5:0.2] Excess | 115 | Spent more than granted; needs PAC approval first
  [34:5:0.3] Vote on account | 116 | Advance for part of the year (about two months, one-sixth); 3-5 months in election years
  [34:5:0.4] Vote of credit | 116 | Blank cheque for an emergency; details cannot be stated
  [34:5:0.5] Exceptional | 116 | Not part of the current service of any year
  [34:5:0.6] Token grant | — | For re-appropriation; must be voted by Lok Sabha
[34:5:1] - **Vote on account** is needed when the regular Budget will not pass in time (e.g. elections); allows withdrawal from CFI before the Budget (Art. 116). Since the **1 February Budget (2017)**, usually needed only in **interim-Budget** years.
[34:5:2] - **Latest:** Lok Sabha approved the **second batch of Supplementary Demands for Grants 2025-26** (Art. 115).
## SUB 6 | Consolidated Fund, Public Account, Contingency Fund
[34:6:0] TABLE head: Basis | Consolidated Fund of India | Public Account of India | Contingency Fund of India
  [34:6:0.0] Article | 266(1) | 266(2) | 267(1)
  [34:6:0.1] States | 266(1) | 266(2) | 267(2)
  [34:6:0.2] Receipts | All revenues, loans (Treasury Bills, ways and means advances, market and foreign loans), loan repayments | Money in trust: provident funds, small savings, judicial deposits, savings bank deposits, remittances | Imprest for unforeseen expenses
  [34:6:0.3] Controlled by | Parliament | Executive | President; held by Finance Secretary, DEA (not Expenditure)
  [34:6:0.4] Withdrawal | Only by Appropriation Act | No vote needed | Advance; approval later
  [34:6:0.5] Nature | Main treasury account | Government acts as banker/trustee | Fixed corpus
[34:6:1] - Contingency Fund of India: set up by **Parliament by law** – **Contingency Fund of India Act, 1950**; corpus **Rs 30,000 crore** from **FY 2021-22** (earlier Rs 500 crore).
[34:6:2] - **State Contingency Fund**: set up by the **State Legislature** by law (Art. 267(2)); at the **Governor's** disposal.
[34:6:3] - **CAG audits** all three funds of both Union and States.
[34:6:4] Public Account is **266(2)**, not 266(1).

=== NOTES 2:35 | Parliamentary Committees – Pac, Estimates, Public Undertakings, DRSCs, Ad Hoc
## SUB 0 | Role and Types of Committees
[35:0:0] - Committees are a primary tool of **parliamentary control over administration**: they do the **detailed scrutiny** the whole House has no time for.
[35:0:1] # Two Types
[35:0:2] TABLE head: Type | Nature | Examples
  [35:0:2.0] Standing | Permanent; reconstituted annually or periodically | Financial committees, DRSCs, Petitions, Privileges
  [35:0:2.1] Ad hoc | Temporary; for a specific task; end after the report | Inquiry committees; Select / Joint Committees on Bills; JPCs
[35:0:3] # Full Classification
[35:0:4] TABLE head: Group | Committees
  [35:0:4.0] Standing – financial | Estimates, PAC, COPU
  [35:0:4.1] Standing – departmental | 24 DRSCs
  [35:0:4.2] Standing – other | Petitions, Privileges, Subordinate Legislation, Government Assurances, Ethics, Business Advisory, Welfare of SCs and STs, Papers Laid on the Table, Rules
  [35:0:4.3] Lok Sabha-only | Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions; Committee on Absence of Members
  [35:0:4.4] Ad hoc | Inquiry committees (e.g. MPLADS, Security in Parliament Complex, Railway Convention); Select / Joint Committees on Bills
[35:0:5] **Inquiry committees** are **ad hoc**, not standing committees.
## SUB 1 | Financial Committees
[35:1:0] TABLE head: Committee | Strength | Rajya Sabha members | Chairman
  [35:1:0.0] Estimates | 30 (largest) | None – all Lok Sabha | Appointed by Speaker; invariably ruling party
  [35:1:0.1] PAC | 22 (15 LS + 7 RS) | Yes (associate) | Appointed by Speaker; Opposition member since 1967 (convention)
  [35:1:0.2] COPU | 22 (15 LS + 7 RS) | Yes | From LS members, appointed by Speaker
[35:1:1] - Members **elected yearly** by **proportional representation – single transferable vote**; term **1 year**; **Ministers ineligible**.
[35:1:2] - Role: keep "an **unremitting vigil**" over spending and performance.
[35:1:3] - **Budget** = control **before** expenditure; financial committees = control **after** expenditure – they support, not replace, the Budget.
[35:1:4] Estimates = 30, no RS member, ruling-party chair; PAC = 22, Opposition chair.
## SUB 2 | Public Accounts Committee (pac)
[35:2:0] - Examines **Appropriation Accounts**, **Finance Accounts** and **CAG audit reports**.
[35:2:1] - Checks that money was spent "**within the scope of the demand**" approved by Parliament; looks at waste and nugatory expenditure.
[35:2:2] - Tests **efficiency, economy and propriety**, not only legality; may take up matters on its own.
[35:2:3] - **Reports to the Lok Sabha** (Speaker).
[35:2:4] - **Limits:** recommendations **not binding**; examines only **execution of policy, not policy** itself – a **post-mortem** check.
[35:2:5] - **Chairman 2026-27: K.C. Venugopal** (Opposition, by convention).
[35:2:6] - **Murli Manohar Joshi** (BJP, then Opposition) chaired the LS PAC **2010–14** – Opposition-chair **convention**.
[35:2:7] The Opposition chair since **1967** is a **convention**, not a constitutional or statutory rule (APPSC G2 2017).
## SUB 3 | Committee Nicknames and Roles

  [35:3:0.0] Petitions | "Ombudsman" of Parliament (APPSC 2025)

  [35:3:0.2] PAC | "Watchdog" on departmental expenditure and irregularities (APPSC G2 2017)
[35:3:1] - **Watchdog group** (full list): Subordinate Legislation, Government Assurances, Estimates, PAC, COPU, DRSCs – **not** the Business Advisory Committee.
[35:3:2] Ombudsman | Petitions | Public grievances
Watchdog | PAC | Expenditure
[35:3:3] The **Estimates Committee**, not the PAC, suggests economies and alternative policies.

## SUB 5 | Departmentally Related Standing Committees
[35:5:0] - **24 DRSCs** = **16 under Lok Sabha + 8 under Rajya Sabha** (about 2:1).
[35:5:1] - Each: **31 members = 21 LS + 10 RS**; **quorum 11** (not 15); **Ministers ineligible**.
[35:5:2] - Functions: examine **Demands for Grants**, **Bills referred**, **annual reports** and **long-term policy** of their ministries.
[35:5:3] - 24 DRSCs and ad hoc committees may review **independent regulators** (telecom, insurance, electricity) in their departments.
[35:5:4] 22 is the strength of the **PAC**, not the number of DRSCs (24).
