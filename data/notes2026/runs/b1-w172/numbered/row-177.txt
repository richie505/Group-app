## SUB 2 | Famine Commissions, Famine Code and Famine Policy
[2:0] TABLE head: Commission | Year | Viceroy | Note
  [2:0.0] Campbell (first) | 1866 | John Lawrence | Orissa famine
  [2:0.1] Richard Strachey | appointed 1878, report 1880 | Lytton | after the Great Famine 1876-78 → Famine Code 1883
  [2:0.2] Lyall | 1898 | Elgin II | after the 1896-97 famine – causes, measures
  [2:0.3] MacDonnell | 1901 | Curzon | early relief, more irrigation
  [2:0.4] Woodhead (last) | 1944 | Wavell | Bengal Famine 1943
[2:1] - **Hunter Commission (1882)** = education, not famine; **Hartog** (1929) = education; Indigo Commission (1860) = indigo.
[2:2] - **Indian Famine Code, 1883:** prepared from the **Strachey Commission**; **relief works for the able-bodied**, **gratuitous relief for the old and infirm**.
[2:3] - **Temple wage:** Sir Richard Temple, 1877 Madras famine – about **1 lb of grain a day** on relief works ("starvation wage").
[2:4] - Public works as relief go back to **Kautilya**; the **Mauryan state** took interest in **irrigation** and in **laying and repairing roads**.
[2:5] - **Nationalist critique:** famines were not due to **monsoon failure alone** but to high, rigid land revenue, food exports and the drain – **Dadabhai Naoroji and R.C. Dutt**; R.C. Dutt, ***Open Letters to Lord Curzon* (1900)**.

## SUB 3 | Arthur Cotton and the Anicuts
[3:0] - **Sir Arthur Cotton** (born 15 May 1803): the **Guntur famine (1833)** convinced the Company that irrigation was needed.
[3:1] - **Godavari anicut at Dowleswaram:** report **1844** → sanctioned **1846** → begun **1847** → completed **April 1852**; cost about **Rs 1,50,000**; designed and built by **Cotton**.
[3:2] - **Veenam Veeranna** – the Andhra official who helped Cotton build the Godavari anicut; managed thousands of labourers; made **Rao Bahadur** and assistant engineer.
[3:3] - The old anicut was replaced by the **Sir Arthur Cotton Barrage**.
