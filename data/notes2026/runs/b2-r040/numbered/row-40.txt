## SUB 0 | Appointment of the CM – Article 164 and the Six-Month Rule
[0:0] # Who appoints – Article 164(1)
[0:1] - **Appointed by:** the **Governor** under **Article 164(1)** (not Art. 163, 165 or 166; not the President, a High Court judge or the Attorney General). Other Ministers are appointed by the Governor **on the CM's advice**.
[0:2]   - Exception: in **Delhi (Art. 239AA)** and **Puducherry (Art. 239A / GUT Act 1963)** the **President** appoints the CM.
[0:3] - **Constitutional (nominal) executive head** of the State = the **Governor**; the **real executive** = the CM and the Council of Ministers (not the Chief Secretary or a Deputy CM).
[0:4] - **Hung Assembly:** the Constitution lays down **no criteria**; the Governor uses discretion. The principal consideration = the person **most likely to command a stable majority** (not simply the largest party), followed by an early **floor test**.
[0:5]   - **Sarkaria (1988) / Punchhi (2010) order:** 1. pre-poll alliance → 2. single largest party with support of others → 3. post-poll coalition (all partners join) → 4. post-poll alliance with some outside support.
[0:6] # Article 164 – clause by clause
[0:7] TABLE head: Clause | Provision
  [0:7.0] 164(1) | Governor appoints the CM; other Ministers on the CM's advice; Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the Governor; Tribal Welfare Minister proviso
  [0:7.1] 164(1A) | Size: maximum 15% of the Assembly, minimum 12 (91st Amendment, 2003)
  [0:7.2] 164(2) | Council collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly (not the Council or the Governor)
  [0:7.3] 164(3) | Oath of office and secrecy administered by the Governor
  [0:7.4] 164(4) | A non-member may be a Minister for six consecutive months; then he ceases to be a Minister
  [0:7.5] 164(5) | Salaries and allowances = as the State Legislature by law determines (until then, Second Schedule) – not the CM, Governor or Chief Secretary
[0:8] - **Six months, not 3 months, 1 year or 3 years:** a Minister (incl. the CM) can continue without being elected to the State Legislature for **six months** (Art. 164(4)).
[0:9] - **Case – P. Shanmugam (Puducherry):** a non-member CM, resigned in **October 2001** after failing to get elected within six months; N. Rangasamy succeeded him (27 Oct 2001). Jayalalithaa (Sept 2001) went after the Supreme Court annulled her appointment – a different ground.
[0:10] - **Term:** **not fixed** by the Constitution; the CM stays while he has the **Assembly's confidence** (on losing it – resign or be dismissed). Resignation is addressed to the **Governor**.

## SUB 3 | Size of the Council of Ministers and the Tribal Welfare Minister
[3:0] # 91st Amendment Act, 2003 – Article 164(1A)
[3:1] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [3:1.0] Subject of the 91st Amendment | Limiting the size of the Council of Ministers (and anti-defection changes) – not RTI, education or corruption
  [3:1.1] Maximum | 15% of the total strength of the Assembly, including the CM
  [3:1.2] Minimum | 12 Ministers including the CM (not 7, 10, 13, 14 or 15)
  [3:1.3] Andhra Pradesh (175 seats) | at most 26 Ministers
[3:2] - Date trap: the **91st Amendment is of 2003** (not 2001); the 90th, 92nd and 93rd are other subjects.
[3:3] # Tribal Welfare Minister – proviso to Article 164(1)
[3:4] - **Compulsory in:** **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha** – of Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh and MP only **Madhya Pradesh** qualifies. He may also look after SC and backward class welfare.
[3:5] - **Bihar** was dropped by the **94th Amendment, 2006**.

## SUB 4 | Position and Powers of the CM; Duties under Article 167
[4:0] # Position
[4:1] - CM = **real executive head** and head of the Government; State counterpart of the **PM (Arts. 74-75)**; the State has no counterpart of the Vice-President.
[4:2] - **Art. 163(1):** Council of Ministers headed by the CM aids and advises the Governor, **except in his discretionary functions**.
[4:3] - Council of Ministers: Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers; collectively answerable to the Assembly.
[4:4] # Powers
[4:5] - Recommends names of Ministers, **allocates portfolios**, can ask a Minister to resign.
[4:6] - Advises the Governor on **summoning and prorogation**; can recommend **dissolution** of the Assembly.
[4:7] # Article 167 – duties of the CM
[4:8] - **Article 167** defines the CM's duties towards the Governor (not Arts. 162, 164 or 165):
[4:9]   - 1. **Communicates** all Council decisions on administration and **proposals for legislation**.
[4:10]   - 2. **Furnishes information** the Governor calls for (a constitutional discretion of the Governor).
[4:11]   - 3. If the Governor requires, **submits for the Council's consideration** a matter decided by a single Minister.
[4:12] - CM = the **chief link** between the Governor and the Council of Ministers.

## SUB 5 | Deputy Chief Minister
[5:0] - **Not mentioned in the Constitution** – not in Art. 164, 74 or 52; a **political arrangement**; the Deputy CM is sworn in as a Minister ("None of the above" is the answer to "which Article").
