=== Legislative Assembly – Strength, Term, Qualifications, Quorum
# Strength – Article 170
- **Article 170:** each Assembly = **not more than 500, not less than 60** members, **directly elected** from territorial constituencies (not 40/400, 50/450 or 450 maximum; not 545, the Lok Sabha figure). "No minimum" is wrong.
- **Exceptions to the 60 minimum:** **Sikkim (32, Art. 371F)**, **Goa (40)**, **Mizoram (40)**; Arunachal Pradesh's minimum is 30 (Art. 371H) but it has 60.
| State / UT | Seats |
|---|---|
| Uttar Pradesh (largest) | **403** |
| Andhra Pradesh / Telangana | 175 / 119 |
| **Himachal Pradesh** | **68** – larger than Arunachal, Manipur, Meghalaya (60 each) |
| **Meghalaya, Nagaland, Tripura** (also Arunachal, Manipur) | **60** each |
| **Goa**, Mizoram | **40** each |
| **Sikkim** – smallest State | **32** – incl. one **Sangha** seat reserved for Buddhist monasteries (a **religion-based** reservation) |
| Puducherry (UT) | **30 elected** + up to 3 nominated |
- **Smallest Assembly:** most keys say **Puducherry (30 elected seats)**; by total strength (33 with nominated members) the smallest is **Sikkim (32)**.
# Term – Article 172
- **5 years** (not 2, 4 or 6); can be **dissolved earlier by the Governor** (on the CM's advice) – not by the Speaker, CM or Vice-President.
- **National Emergency:** **Parliament** may extend it by law **one year at a time**, not beyond **six months** after the Emergency ends (not the President, Governor or State Legislature).
# Qualifications – Article 173
- **Article 173** (not 175, 177 or 178): citizen of India; at least **25 years** for the Assembly (30 for the Council); other qualifications prescribed by Parliament.
- UPSC 2008: "not more than 450 members" ✗ (500); "not qualified if less than 25" ✓ → **B only**.
# Quorum – Article 189(3)
- **Ten members or one-tenth** of the total membership, **whichever is greater** (not 30, one-half or one-fourth).
- **Reservation:** SC/ST seats in proportion to population (Art. 332); Anglo-Indian nomination ended (104th Amendment, 2020); one-third for women after delimitation (106th Amendment, 2023).

=== Legislative Procedure – Money Bills, Ordinary Bills and Deadlock
# Money Bills – Articles 198, 199, 207
- Introduced **only in the Legislative Assembly** (not in either House or the Council), only by a **Minister**, only on the **Governor's recommendation** (**Art. 207(1)**) – not Parliament, the President or a committee.
- **Art. 199:** defines a Money Bill; the **Speaker certifies** it (final).
- **Art. 198:** the Council must return it within **14 days** (not 7, 10, 21 or 30); it cannot reject or amend it; the Assembly may accept or reject its recommendations.
# Ordinary Bills – Article 197
- A Bill passed by the Assembly can be held by the Council **not more than three months** on first passage and **one month** on second – the Council can only **interpose delay** (max. 4 months); it cannot permanently block a Bill.
- **Final authority = the Legislative Assembly**; the Governor has no "final say" on a deadlock.
- **No joint sitting** in a State Legislature (unlike Art. 108 for Parliament).
- **Bill originating in the Council** and **rejected by the Assembly** → the Bill **ends (dies)**; no joint sitting, no reconsideration, no reference to the Governor.
| Point | Council's power |
|---|---|
| Money Bill | Delay **14 days** |
| Ordinary Bill (Assembly's) | Delay **3 + 1 months**; Assembly prevails |
| Ordinary Bill (Council's own), rejected by Assembly | Bill **ends** |

=== Legislative Council – Composition, Size and Tenure
# Composition – Article 171
| Chosen by | Share |
|---|---|
| **Local bodies** (municipalities, district boards etc.) | **1/3** |
| **MLAs** (from among non-members) | **1/3** – not 1/13 |
| **Graduates** of 3 years' standing | **1/12** |
| **Teachers** (secondary school and above) of 3 years' standing | **1/12** |
| **Nominated by the Governor** (literature, science, art, co-operative movement, social service) | **1/6** |
- **Industrialists** are **not** a category. "1/13 elected by the Legislative Assembly" is the **wrong** statement (it is one-third).
- **Article 171** = composition of the **Vidhan Parishad** (Art. 169 = creation / abolition; Art. 170 = Assembly).
# Size
- **Maximum one-third** of the Assembly's total membership (not one-half, two-thirds or one-fourth); **minimum 40** (not 44, 52 or 65). Parliament fixes the actual strength.
- UPPSC 2023: "not more than one-third of the Assembly" ✓ and "at least forty members" ✓ → **both**.
- UPSC 2015: the Council can be larger than half of the Assembly ✗; the Governor nominates the Chairman ✗ (the **Council elects** its Chairman, Art. 182) → **neither**.
# Tenure
- **Permanent body** – **cannot be dissolved, but can be abolished** (Art. 169); member's term **6 years**; **one-third retire every second year** – so its membership **changes every two years**.
- The Assembly, Lok Sabha (House of the People) can be dissolved; the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) is permanent and cannot be abolished by Parliament under Art. 169.

=== Article 169 – Creation and Abolition of Legislative Councils
- **Article 169** (not 167, 168, 170, 171, 368 or 162): **Parliament** may by law **abolish** a Council or **create** one.
- **Step 1 – Assembly resolution by special majority:** a majority of the **total membership** and **two-thirds of members present and voting** (not a simple majority; not the Lok Sabha).
- **Step 2 – Parliament** passes the law by a **simple majority**; it is **not deemed an amendment** under **Art. 368**.
- The resolution is **recommendatory** – it **does not impose an obligation on the Union** (Parliament) to legislate. The **Governor has no role**.
- Statements (NTR University): A – Art. 169 deals with creation/abolition ✓; B – resolution by **simple** majority ✗; C – Art. 171: not more than 1/3, not less than 40 ✓ → **A and C**.
- **AP, January 2020:** the Assembly passed the resolution to abolish the Council under **Article 169(1)**; it was **withdrawn in November 2021**.
> "Parliament ratifies the resolution passed by the Assembly with special majority" is the best of the loose options; Parliament itself needs only a simple majority.

=== Presiding Officers – Speaker and Chairman
# Speaker – Articles 178-181, 189
- **Officer of the Assembly** = the **Speaker** (and Deputy Speaker), elected by the Assembly from its members (**Art. 178**) – not the Governor, CM or a Minister.
- **Resigns** by writing to the **Deputy Speaker** (**Art. 179**) – not the Governor, CM or President.
- **Vacates office** if he **ceases to be a member**; is removed by a resolution of a majority of all the then members after **14 days' notice**.
- **On dissolution** the Speaker **does not vacate** office; he continues until just before the **first meeting of the new Assembly**.
- **Vote:** only a **casting vote in a tie** (**Art. 189**); during a resolution for his removal he **does not preside** but may speak and **vote in the first instance** (**Art. 181**).
- Decides **Money Bill** certification (Art. 199(3)) and **Tenth Schedule** defection (subject to judicial review, Kihoto Hollohan 1992).
- **Immunity (Art. 194(2)):** no member is liable in any court for anything said or any vote given in the Legislature.
# Chairman of the Council – Article 182
- **Elected by the Council** from its members (not nominated by the Governor); resigns to the Deputy Chairman.
> CDS 2006: continues after dissolution ✓, resigns to the Deputy Speaker ✓ → both. NDA 2012 / UPSC 2018: "vacates on dissolution" ✗.

=== Disqualification of Legislators
| Ground | Decided by |
|---|---|
| **Art. 191** – office of profit, unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, not a citizen, disqualified by a law of Parliament (RPA 1951) | **Governor** – his decision is **final**, but he acts on the **binding opinion of the Election Commission** (**Art. 192**) |
| **Tenth Schedule** – defection | **Speaker / Chairman** |
- So for matters **other than the Tenth Schedule** the final authority is the **Governor** – not the Speaker, the ECI, the State Election Commission, the CM or the High Court.
- **Conviction:** an MLA sentenced to **2 years or more** is disqualified **immediately** (RPA s.8(3); Lily Thomas, 2013).

=== MLAs and MLCs in Indirect Elections
| Election | Elected MLAs vote? | MLCs vote? |
|---|---|---|
| **President** (Art. 54) | **Yes** | **No** |
| **Vice-President** | **No** – only MPs | No |
| **Rajya Sabha** members | **Yes** (PR-STV) | No |
| **Legislative Council** | **Yes** – elect one-third of MLCs | – |
- The Assembly takes part in electing the **President, Rajya Sabha members and MLCs** (I, III, IV) – not the Vice-President.
- A CM who is a **member of the Legislative Council** is **not eligible to vote** in the Presidential election (being an acting CM or yet to prove majority does not matter; an MLA, Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha elected member can vote).
