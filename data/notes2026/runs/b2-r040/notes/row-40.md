=== Appointment of the CM – Article 164 and the Six-Month Rule
# Who appoints – Article 164(1)
- **Appointed by:** the **Governor** under **Article 164(1)** (not Art. 163, 165 or 166; not the President, a High Court judge or the Attorney General). Other Ministers are appointed by the Governor **on the CM's advice**.
  - Exception: in **Delhi (Art. 239AA)** and **Puducherry (Art. 239A / GUT Act 1963)** the **President** appoints the CM.
- **Constitutional (nominal) executive head** of the State = the **Governor**; the **real executive** = the CM and the Council of Ministers (not the Chief Secretary or a Deputy CM).
- **Hung Assembly:** the Constitution lays down **no criteria**; the Governor uses discretion. The principal consideration = the person **most likely to command a stable majority** (not simply the largest party), followed by an early **floor test**.
  - **Sarkaria (1988) / Punchhi (2010) order:** 1. pre-poll alliance → 2. single largest party with support of others → 3. post-poll coalition (all partners join) → 4. post-poll alliance with some outside support.
# Article 164 – clause by clause
| Clause | Provision |
|---|---|
| **164(1)** | Governor appoints the CM; other Ministers on the CM's advice; Ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the Governor**; Tribal Welfare Minister proviso |
| **164(1A)** | Size: maximum **15%** of the Assembly, minimum **12** (91st Amendment, 2003) |
| **164(2)** | Council **collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly** (not the Council or the Governor) |
| **164(3)** | Oath of office and secrecy administered by the **Governor** |
| **164(4)** | A non-member may be a Minister for **six consecutive months**; then he **ceases** to be a Minister |
| **164(5)** | Salaries and allowances = as the **State Legislature by law** determines (until then, Second Schedule) – not the CM, Governor or Chief Secretary |
- **Six months, not 3 months, 1 year or 3 years:** a Minister (incl. the CM) can continue without being elected to the State Legislature for **six months** (Art. 164(4)).
- **Case – P. Shanmugam (Puducherry):** a non-member CM, resigned in **October 2001** after failing to get elected within six months; N. Rangasamy succeeded him (27 Oct 2001). Jayalalithaa (Sept 2001) went after the Supreme Court annulled her appointment – a different ground.
- **Term:** **not fixed** by the Constitution; the CM stays while he has the **Assembly's confidence** (on losing it – resign or be dismissed). Resignation is addressed to the **Governor**.

=== Size of the Council of Ministers and the Tribal Welfare Minister
# 91st Amendment Act, 2003 – Article 164(1A)
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Subject of the **91st Amendment** | **Limiting the size of the Council of Ministers** (and anti-defection changes) – not RTI, education or corruption |
| Maximum | **15%** of the total strength of the Assembly, **including the CM** |
| Minimum | **12** Ministers including the CM (not 7, 10, 13, 14 or 15) |
| Andhra Pradesh (175 seats) | at most **26** Ministers |
- Date trap: the **91st Amendment is of 2003** (not 2001); the 90th, 92nd and 93rd are other subjects.
# Tribal Welfare Minister – proviso to Article 164(1)
- **Compulsory in:** **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha** – of Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh and MP only **Madhya Pradesh** qualifies. He may also look after SC and backward class welfare.
- **Bihar** was dropped by the **94th Amendment, 2006**.

=== Position and Powers of the CM; Duties under Article 167
# Position
- CM = **real executive head** and head of the Government; State counterpart of the **PM (Arts. 74-75)**; the State has no counterpart of the Vice-President.
- **Art. 163(1):** Council of Ministers headed by the CM aids and advises the Governor, **except in his discretionary functions**.
- Council of Ministers: Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers; collectively answerable to the Assembly.
# Powers
- Recommends names of Ministers, **allocates portfolios**, can ask a Minister to resign.
- Advises the Governor on **summoning and prorogation**; can recommend **dissolution** of the Assembly.
# Article 167 – duties of the CM
- **Article 167** defines the CM's duties towards the Governor (not Arts. 162, 164 or 165):
  - 1. **Communicates** all Council decisions on administration and **proposals for legislation**.
  - 2. **Furnishes information** the Governor calls for (a constitutional discretion of the Governor).
  - 3. If the Governor requires, **submits for the Council's consideration** a matter decided by a single Minister.
- CM = the **chief link** between the Governor and the Council of Ministers.

=== Deputy Chief Minister
- **Not mentioned in the Constitution** – not in Art. 164, 74 or 52; a **political arrangement**; the Deputy CM is sworn in as a Minister ("None of the above" is the answer to "which Article").
- **First Deputy CM of Andhra Pradesh:** **K.V. Ranga Reddy** (1956, Sanjiva Reddy ministry) – not J.V. Narsing Rao, B.V. Subba Reddy or C. Jagannadha Rao (later Deputy CMs).
- AP had **five** Deputy CMs in 2019-24; **K. Pawan Kalyan** (Jana Sena) Deputy CM since June 2024.

=== Advocate General (Article 165) and the 130th Amendment Bill
| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Article | **165** – State counterpart of the Attorney General (Art. 76) |
| Appointed by | **Governor**; holds office **during his pleasure**; no fixed term |
| Qualification | Qualified to be a **High Court judge** |
| Remuneration | Determined by the **Governor** |
| In the Legislature | **Art. 177** – may speak and take part, **no vote** |
- **130th Constitution Amendment Bill, 2025:** amends **Arts. 75, 164 and 239AA** – a PM, CM or Minister in custody for **30 consecutive days** for an offence punishable with **5+ years** is removed on the **31st day**.
