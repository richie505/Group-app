=== NOTES 1:167 | The Nizam of Hyderabad, Subsidiary Alliance, and the Cession of the Northern Circars
## SUB 0 | Asaf Jahi Dynasty – Founder and Nizams
[167:0:0] # Founder
[167:0:1] - **Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan**, title **Nizam-ul-Mulk, Asaf Jah I**; independent rule from **1724**, after defeating the Mughal governor **Mubariz Khan** at **Shakar Kheda (11 Oct 1724)**; kept nominal allegiance to the Mughals.
[167:0:2] # Nizams and their reigns
[167:0:3] TABLE head: Nizam | Reign | Key point
  [167:0:3.0] Nizam Ali Khan (Asaf Jah II) | 1762–1803 | Capital Aurangabad → Hyderabad (1763); Circars leased; first Subsidiary Alliance (1798)
  [167:0:3.1] Sikandar Jah (Asaf Jah III) | 1803–29 | Secunderabad cantonment named after him
  [167:0:3.2] Nasir-ud-Daula (Asaf Jah IV) | 1829–57 | Wahabi conspiracy (1839) – Mubariz-ud-Daula arrested; Berar Treaty (1853) under Dalhousie gave Berar, Raichur, Osmanabad (Dharashiv); Salar Jung family rose; died 16 May 1857
  [167:0:3.3] Afzal-ud-Daula (Asaf Jah V) | 1857–69 | Nizam during 1857; title "Star of India" for loyalty
  [167:0:3.4] Mir Mahbub Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VI) | 1869–1911 | Youngest Asaf Jahi ruler – Nizam as an infant (APPSC 2022); Salar Jung I regent
[167:0:4] Afzal-ud-Daula = 1857 + "Star of India"; **Mahbub Ali Khan = youngest**.
[167:0:5] # Nizam Ali Khan – treaties with the British

  [167:0:6.1] 1768 | Treaty of Masulipatnam – confirmed the Circars

  [167:0:6.5] 12 Oct 1800 | Treaty ceding the Ceded Districts
## SUB 1 | Salar Jung Family and 1857 at Hyderabad
[167:1:0] - **Mir Turab Ali Khan** made Diwan on **31 May 1853** after the death of Siraj-ul-Mulk → **Salar Jung I**; the family rose under **Nasir-ud-Daula**.
[167:1:1] - Served **three Nizams** (Nasir-ud-Daula, Afzal-ud-Daula, Mahbub Ali Khan); died **1883**; **abolished revenue farming**.
[167:1:2] - **Zillabandi (1865):** Salar Jung I divided the state into **districts** under **talukdars** for revenue and administration.
[167:1:3] - **Hyderabad (Secunderabad)–Wadi railway:** built with the Nizam's money, opened **1874**, joining the Bombay–Madras line; ⚠ APPSC key (2012) gave 1872.
[167:1:4] - **Chanda Railway Scheme (1883):** protests against its handling = **first public political awakening** in Hyderabad State.
[167:1:5] - **17 July 1857:** **Turrebaz Khan** attacked the Residency to free **Chida Khan**; repulsed by **Davidson**.
## SUB 2 | Northern Circars and Guntur Circar
[167:2:0] # Grant and lease
[167:2:1] TABLE head: Year | Step
  [167:2:1.0] 1759 | Salabat Jang gave the British Machilipatnam, Nizampatnam, Vakal Mannar and part of Kondapalli
  [167:2:1.1] 1765 | Mughal firman of Shah Alam II, received by Clive, granted the Circars; Nizam Ali refused it at first
  [167:2:1.2] 1766 | Treaty of Hyderabad (12 Nov 1766): Circars leased for military aid or ₹9 lakh a year; signed by envoy Calliaud
  [167:2:1.3] 1768 | Treaty of Masulipatnam: Nizam acknowledged Shah Alam's grant; Company kept the Circars for ₹9 lakh + military help
  [167:2:1.4] 18 Sep 1788 | Guntur handed over for ₹7 lakh, after Basalat Jung's death (1782) and 6 years of talks
[167:2:2] - **Circars handed over (1766):** **Mustafanagar (Kondapalli), Eluru, Rajamahendravaram, Srikakulam** – **not Kurnool** (Ceded Districts, 1800).
[167:2:3] - Modern coastal districts: Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Visakhapatnam, East and West Godavari, Krishna.
[167:2:4] # The Andhra negotiator
[167:2:5] - **Kandregula Jogipantulu** – go-between sent by the British to Nizam Ali after the 1765 grant; used the **Gollapudi pay mutiny** of the Nizam's army to propose a lease at **₹15 lakh**; the 1766 treaty fixed **₹9 lakh** (APPSC G2 2019).
[167:2:6] - Nizam Ali made him **Majumdar of Rajamahendravaram**; British gave him **"Raja Bahadur"**.
[167:2:7] - **Col. Forde** – took **Machilipatnam** from the French (**1759**); **not** the 1766 negotiator, who was **Kandregula Jogipantulu**. ⚠ APPSC key (2019) gave Col. Forde.
[167:2:8] # Guntur Circar – the distinction
[167:2:9] - **Guntur = the fifth Circar** (**Kondaveedu / Murtazanagar**); held by Nizam Ali's brother **Basalat Jung**.
[167:2:10] - **Main area of conflict between Nizam Ali and the British** = Guntur Circar, not the Circars as a whole.
[167:2:11] ₹15 lakh = proposed lease; ₹9 lakh = 1766/1768 figure; ₹7 lakh = Guntur (1788)
## SUB 3 | Road to 1798 and the Hyderabad Residency
[167:3:0] - Nizam beaten by the Marathas at **Kharda (1795)** → blamed **Sir John Shore's neutrality** → dismissed British battalions, relied on **French troops under Raymond** → **Wellesley** forced the alliance.
[167:3:1] - **Resident James Kirkpatrick** reported the French battalions; built the Residency, the "Koti".
[167:3:2] - **Resident's role:** controlled the Nizam's **foreign relations** and supervised the **subsidiary force**.
[167:3:3] - **Russell Brigade:** reformed Hyderabad force raised by **Resident Henry Russell** (c. 1813); later part of the **Hyderabad Contingent**.
## SUB 4 | Subsidiary Alliance – Aims, Terms, Effects
[167:4:0] # Author and aims
[167:4:1] - **Lord Wellesley (1798–1805)**, who came in 1798 amid the struggle with France (fear of **Napoleon**); he did **not invent** the method but gave it its **final shape**.
[167:4:2] - **Aims:** counter **French influence**; **British paramountcy without direct administration**; large army at the states' cost – military and political dominance, **not a fixed income**.
[167:4:3] # Terms
[167:4:4] - 1. Accept a **British Resident** at the court.
[167:4:5] - 2. **Surrender foreign affairs**; no dealings with other states without Company consent.
[167:4:6] - 3. Maintain a **permanent subsidiary force**, paid in cash or by **ceding territory**.
[167:4:7] - 4. **Employ no other Europeans** without Company consent.
[167:4:8] - 5. In return the Company **protects the borders**.
[167:4:9] # Effects
[167:4:10] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [167:4:10.0] Sovereignty | Lost; foreign policy gone
  [167:4:10.1] Finances | Drained → territorial cessions (Nizam's Ceded Districts)
  [167:4:10.2] Government | Rulers dependent, neglected government → pretext for annexation

[167:5:4] First under **Wellesley's system** = Hyderabad, not Awadh.
## SUB 6 | Ceded Districts and Andhra Consequences
[167:6:0] - By the treaty of **12 October 1800** the Nizam ceded lands gained from Mysore (1792, 1799): **Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary**, in lieu of the subsidy = **Dattamandalalu (Ceded Districts)**.
[167:6:1] - Name **Rayalaseema** proposed by **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao (1928)**; approved at the **Andhra Mahasabha, Nandyal**, presided by **Dr S. Radhakrishnan**.
[167:6:2] - **10 July 1794 – Padmanabham:** Company defeated **Chinna Vijayaramaraju** of Vizianagaram; Chandurti (1758) – Conflans beaten.
[167:6:3] - **1801:** **Nellore and Chittoor** from the **Carnatic Nawab** → whole Andhra under the Company.
[167:6:4] Northern Circars + Ceded Districts ≈ **Coastal Andhra + Rayalaseema**; **Telangana stayed under the Nizam**.
## SUB 7 | Policies and Their Authors
[167:7:0] TABLE head: Policy | Author | Aim
  [167:7:0.0] Ring-fence | Warren Hastings | Buffer states to protect Company frontiers
  [167:7:0.1] Subsidiary Alliance | Wellesley (1798) | Political and military control of states
  [167:7:0.2] Subordinate isolation | Lord Hastings (1813–23) | Each state bound separately to the Company
  [167:7:0.3] Doctrine of Lapse | Dalhousie (from 1848) | Annex states without a natural male heir; adopted heirs barred
  [167:7:0.4] Subordinate union | Crown, after 1858 | Princes = "breakwaters to the storm"
  [167:7:0.5] Dhamma | Ashoka | Moral and social harmony
  [167:7:0.6] Blood and Iron | Balban | Strong monarchy
  [167:7:0.7] Market Control | Alauddin Khalji | Large army at lower cost
  [167:7:0.8] Blue Water | Francisco de Almeida | Portuguese naval supremacy
  [167:7:0.9] Non-Alignment | Nehru | Independent foreign policy
[167:7:1] - **Annexation policy = Doctrine of Lapse** (APPSC 2018).
[167:7:2] - **Awadh = classic ring-fence buffer** after Buxar (1764); Hastings sold **Kora and Allahabad** to its Nawab; it shielded Bengal from the **Marathas and Afghans**.
[167:7:3] - Chronology: Permanent Settlement (1793) → Subsidiary Alliance → Doctrine of Lapse → **Partition of Bengal (last)**.
[167:7:4] **Warren Hastings = ring-fence**; **Lord Hastings = subordinate isolation**.
## SUB 8 | Paramountcy and the Rajput Alliances
[167:8:0] - **Rajput treaties (1817–18)** – Jodhpur, Jaipur, Bundi – under **Lord Hastings**; negotiator **Charles Metcalfe**.
[167:8:1] - **Aims:** protection from **Marathas and Pindaris**; accept British paramountcy / sovereignty; isolate them from the Maratha powers.
[167:8:2] - **Grounds for intervention:** states had no external life; British gave effect to their **international commitments** (APPSC G2 2017); also **succession disputes** and **misgovernment**.
[167:8:3] - **After 1858:** annexation stopped; **right of adoption** recognised.

[167:9:2] - Titles and pensions of the rulers of **Tanjore and the Carnatic** abolished.
[167:9:3] - **Not lapse:** Karnataka (Carnatic), **Jaipur** (Jaitpur was); **Gwalior** never annexed.
[167:9:4] - Lapse and Awadh angered rulers, taluqdars and sepoys → **Revolt of 1857**.

## SUB 2 | Ceded Districts and Rayalaseema
[168:2:0] # Region and background
[168:2:1] - **Ceded Districts (1800):** **Kadapa (Cuddapah), Kurnool, Anantapur** (now AP) + **Bellary** (now Karnataka) = today's **Rayalaseema** (APPSC GS 2024).
[168:2:2] - **Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan** had taken these lands from Hyderabad.
[168:2:3] - **Nizam Ali** regained them as a British ally in the **Mysore wars of 1790–92 and 1799**.
[168:2:4] - Under the **Subsidiary Alliance (1798)** he could not pay in cash → ceded them in **1800**.
[168:2:5] # Headquarters
[168:2:6] - **1800–1807:** a single collectorate under **Munro**, with headquarters at **Anantapur**.
[168:2:7] - **1808:** split into two collectorates – **Bellary** and **Cuddapah**.
[168:2:8] - APPSC 2011 key: headquarters = **Anantapur** (the single collectorate till 1808); **Bellary** and **Cuddapah** became seats only after the 1808 split.
[168:2:9] # Names
[168:2:10] TABLE head: Name | Fact
  [168:2:10.0] Dattamandalam / Dattamandalalu | Old name, "ceded lands"; used until 1928
  [168:2:10.1] Rayalaseema | Suggested by Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao (APPSC G2 2012 key); adopted at the Andhra Mahasabha, Nandyal, November 1928, presided over by S. Radhakrishnan; the resolution was moved by Chilukuri Narayana Rao
[168:2:11] Rayalaseema name year = **1928**, not 1927; place = **Nandyal**.
## SUB 3 | Carnatic Districts, 1801
[168:3:0] - Covers **Nellore**, the **Prakasam** area and part of **Chittoor**.
[168:3:1] - Earlier under **Aravidu Sriranga Raya III**, then part of the **Carnatic**.
[168:3:2] - After the **Second Carnatic War** the British made **Muhammad Ali** Nawab of the Carnatic (secure only after **1763**).
[168:3:3] - After Muhammad Ali's death the Company annexed these districts in **1801** by the **Carnatic Treaty** with Nawab **Azim-ud-Daula**.

## SUB 5 | Company Officials and the Poligars
[168:5:0] # Sir Thomas Munro (1761–1827)
[168:5:1] - **Read and Munro** – **Baramahal** experiment, **1792** (now Salem/Krishnagiri, Tamil Nadu).
[168:5:2] - **First Principal Collector of the Ceded Districts (1800)**; sub-collectors at **Harpanahalli, Kadapa, Adoni, Kurnool**.
[168:5:3] - **Suppressed the palegars (poligars)** of Rayalaseema and abolished revenue **auctions** there.

[168:5:5] - Telugu-speaking Collector who needed no interpreters; remembered locally.
[168:5:6] - **Governor of Madras 1820–27**; died at **Pattikonda (Kurnool)** on **6 July 1827**.

## SUB 6 | Early Administration – Councils, Committee, Board, Collector
[168:6:0] # Factories and the Chiefs and Councils
[168:6:1] - Before acquisition: Company factories at **Machilipatnam** and **Visakhapatnam**.
[168:6:2] - **Provincial Chiefs and Councils** at these stations governed the Circars; the **Chief-in-Council at Masulipatnam** was the main one.
[168:6:3] - Abolished in **1794** → replaced by district **Collectors**.
[168:6:4] # Committee of Circuit (Circuit Committee)
[168:6:5] - Ordered by the Court of Directors in **1775**, drawn from the **Madras Council**.
[168:6:6] - Purpose: examine the **revenue** of the Northern Circars and whether zamindars' payments were fair.
[168:6:7] - Findings: unfavourable to the zamindars; treated **Vizianagaram** as too powerful; recommended **against the auction (highest-bidder) system** as it hurt both government and ryots.
[168:6:8] # Board of Revenue
[168:6:9] - **Madras Board of Revenue set up in 1786** (order of **20 June 1786**); state-level apex of the revenue hierarchy.
[168:6:10] - Andhra **Board of Revenue (1953)** formed after Andhra State was created.

  [168:6:11.1] District | Collector | Zilla Court (District Judge)
  [168:6:11.2] Division | Sub-Collector / Revenue Officer | —
  [168:6:11.3] Taluk | Tahsildar | Munsiff Court
  [168:6:11.4] Firka | Deputy Tahsildar / Revenue Inspector | —
  [168:6:11.5] Village | Karanam, Munsab (hereditary) | Panchayat court
[168:6:12] # The Collector
[168:6:13] - **1794:** District Collectors replaced the Chief-and-Council system in the Circars.
[168:6:14] - **1802:** office of Collector introduced in the North Sarkar region under the new settlement.
[168:6:15] - Duties: **land revenue** (main), law and order, **magistrate**.
[168:6:16] - **1816:** Collectors lost judicial powers; later some magisterial powers returned under ryotwari; civil justice stayed with the District Judge.
[168:6:17] Collectors replaced the Chiefs and Councils in **1794**; for "Collector's administration in place of the Revenue Board" the APPSC G2 2012 answer is 1800 (Munro's Ceded Districts collectorate), not **1786** (the year the Board of Revenue was set up).
[168:6:18] The 1794 Collector change was **not** caused by the 1802 Permanent Settlement – it came earlier.

## SUB 8 | Revenue Systems and Taxes
[168:8:0] # Zamindari vs Ryotwari
[168:8:1] - **Before 1802:** zamindaris given by **auction**.
[168:8:2] - **Permanent Settlement:** **Cornwallis, 1793**; fixed demand replaced auctions; applied to Andhra in **1802**.
[168:8:3] - British land-tax management in Andhra had **three methods**; the Zamindari system was imposed in the Circars.
[168:8:4] TABLE head: Feature | Zamindari (Permanent Settlement) | Ryotwari | Mahalwari
  [168:8:4.0] Where | Vizianagaram, Bobbili, Pithapuram (Northern Circars) | Rayalaseema first (1802); later most of Andhra | Nellore
  [168:8:4.1] Settled with | Zamindar | Ryot directly | Village headmen
  [168:8:4.2] Effect | Absentee landlordism, poor tenants | Over-assessment (often 50%+), moneylender debt, land alienation | —
[168:8:5] - **First region of Ryotwari:** **Baramahal (1792)**; in Andhra, the **Ceded Districts (1802)**.
[168:8:6] - **1820:** Ryotwari extended across Madras under Governor Munro.
[168:8:7] - **Zamindari abolition:** **Madras Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act, 1948** (Madras Act XXVI of 1948); came into force **19 April 1949**; the first estates (e.g. **Venkatagiri**) were taken over from **7 Sep 1949**.
[168:8:8] - **Zamindari lands:** 9/10 of the estimated revenue to government, 1/10 to the zamindar; agreements for 10 years.
[168:8:9] # Revenue terms
[168:8:10] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [168:8:10.0] Moturpha | Tax on looms – a trade/profession tax on weavers, shops, artisans (APPSC G2 2011); not a salt tax; opposed by the Madras Native Association (1861 petition); abolished in 1861
  [168:8:10.1] Peshkash / Peshcush | Fixed tribute/revenue share paid by zamindars and other intermediaries to the British (PYQ 2025: true)
  [168:8:10.2] Korunu | Revenue zamindars collected from farmers
  [168:8:10.3] Haveli lands | Lands under direct British control in coastal Andhra
  [168:8:10.4] Sayer | Inland transit and customs duties
  [168:8:10.5] Abkari | Excise on liquor and toddy

Korunu | farmer | to zamindar
## SUB 9 | Judiciary and Police
[168:9:0] # Court hierarchy
[168:9:1] TABLE head: Level | Court
  [168:9:1.0] Taluk | Munsiff court
  [168:9:1.1] District | Zilla court under a District Judge (post created by the Cornwallis Code, 1793)
  [168:9:1.2] Appeal | Provincial Courts of Appeal / Circuit courts (powers expanded 1820)
  [168:9:1.3] Apex (natives) | Sadar Adalat (civil) and Sadar Faujdari Adalat (criminal), Madras
  [168:9:1.4] British subjects, Madras town | Madras Supreme Court (1801)
[168:9:2] # Munro's judicial reforms
[168:9:3] - **Munro = President of the Judicial Commission (1814)**; report **April 1816**, mostly accepted.
[168:9:4] - **Madras Regulations of 1816:** **Munsiff courts** and **Panchayat courts** (in every large village and district) as courts of first instance – Indian officials at the base.
[168:9:5] - Criticism: slow, costly British procedure displaced village panchayats → more lawsuits and debt.
[168:9:6] # Police
[168:9:7] - **Cornwallis:** **thanas under darogas** (Indian officers with constables), blamed for abuse of power.
[168:9:8] - Madras: village watchmen supplemented by central police under a District Superintendent.
[168:9:9] - **Munro (1816):** police supervision moved **from judges to Collectors**.
[168:9:10] - **Codes:** CPC **1859**, IPC **1860**, CrPC **1861**; **Madras District Police Act 1859**.
[168:9:11] # Latest developments
[168:9:12] - **20 Feb 2026:** sericulture in Rayalaseema traced to British promotion in the "Ceded Districts"; silk hubs today **Dharmavaram** and **Madanapalle**.
[168:9:13] - Fertile, educated Circars vs dry, neglected Ceded Districts → later the **Sri Bagh Pact** debate.
[168:9:14] - **Revenue judicial service plea (1 May 2026):** the Supreme Court issued notice on a plea for a separate "revenue judicial service" cadre; the plea, under **Article 14**, argued that revenue officers without legal training should not decide land-title disputes.

## SUB 1 | Collector and Governance
[169:1:0] - The **District Collector** held revenue, magisterial and police powers – the pivot of British rule.
[169:1:1] - In the Agency tracts the **Ganjam Collector** acted as **Agent to the Governor** (from 1819).
[169:1:2] - **Munro** – Ryotwari: settlement made directly with the ryot.
[169:1:3] - Most Collectors did not know Telugu and leaned on **dubashis** (interpreters) and native subordinates, a source of corruption.
[169:1:4] - Revenue was collected **in cash**; the Collector's first duty was to secure it.
[169:1:5] - The district hierarchy ran **Board of Revenue → Collector → Tahsildar → village officers (Karanam, Munsab)**.
[169:1:6] Collector = revenue + magistrate; Agent to the Governor = special charge of hill (Agency) areas.
