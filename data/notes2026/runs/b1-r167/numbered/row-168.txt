## SUB 0 | Revenue Systems and Taxes – Moturpha, Peshkash, Zamindari and Ryotwari
[0:0] # Revenue terms
[0:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:1.0] Moturpha | tax on looms – a tax on trades and occupations (weavers, shops, artisans); not a salt tax; attacked in the Madras Native Association's 1861 petition; abolished 1861
  [0:1.1] Peshkash / Peshcush | the share of revenue (land tax / tribute) paid by zamindars to the British
  [0:1.2] Korunu | revenue the zamindars collected from the farmers
  [0:1.3] Haveli lands | lands under direct British (government) control
  [0:1.4] Sunkams / Sayer | land customs, inland transit duties
  [0:1.5] Abkari | excise on liquor and toddy
[0:2] - **Moturpha (asked ×7):** "tax on looms" (APPSC G2 2011 ×2, AP History PYQ, Asst Social Welfare, BC Welfare); **G2 Mains 2025 statements:** Moturpha a salt tax – **false**; Peshcush = revenue from the zamindars – **true** → "(A) false, (B) true".
[0:3] # Three methods of land revenue (both statements true)
[0:4] TABLE head: System | Where | Settled with
  [0:4.0] Zamindari (Permanent Settlement) | coastal Northern Circars (Uttara Sarkarlu) – Vizianagaram, Bobbili, Pithapuram | zamindar pays peshkash
  [0:4.1] Ryotwari | Ceded Districts / Rayalaseema (Datta mandalalu) first (from 1802), later most of Madras (1820, Munro) | the ryot directly
  [0:4.2] Mahalwari | Nellore | village headmen
[0:5] - **Permanent Settlement:** Cornwallis **1793** ended the auctions and fixed the demand; applied to Andhra in **1802** (Vizianagaram peshcush fixed at ₹6 lakh); zamindars kept 1/10, government 9/10.
[0:6] - **Effects:** zamindari → absentee landlords, rack-rented tenants; ryotwari → over-assessment, cash payment, moneylender debt, land alienation.
[0:7] - **Zamindari abolished:** Madras Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act **1948**, in force **19 April 1949**.

## SUB 1 | Ceded Districts – Headquarters and Rayalaseema
[1:0] - **Ceded Districts (1800)** = **Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur** (now AP) + **Bellary** (now Karnataka) = present-day **Rayalaseema**; old name **Dattamandalam / Dattamandalalu**.
[1:1] - **Headquarters (asked ×3, G2 2011):** one collectorate till **1808** with its **headquarters at Anantapur**; Munro Principal Collector (Nov 1800) with four sub-collectors; divisions Cuddapah, Cumbum, Adoni, Harpanahalli. **Bellary** and **Cuddapah** became district seats only after the **1808** split.
[1:2] - **Rayalaseema districts:** Kurnool, Kadapa, Anantapur, Chittoor (and their 2022 offshoots) – **Guntur is coastal**, not Rayalaseema.
[1:3] - **Name:** **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao**, Andhra Mahasabha at **Nandyal, Nov 1928**, presided by **S. Radhakrishnan**; resolution moved by Chilukuri Narayana Rao (old app notes).
[1:4] > Trap: "Dattamandalam headquarters" = Anantapur (not Bellary); Bellary is now in Karnataka.

## SUB 2 | How Andhra Came Under the Company and Its Early Administration
[2:0] TABLE head: Region | Year | How
  [2:0.0] Machilipatnam and parganas | 1759 | grant of Salabat Jung
  [2:0.1] Northern Circars (Godavari etc.) | 1765-66 | Shah Alam II's farman, lease from Nizam Ali
  [2:0.2] Guntur Circar | 1788 (18 Sep) | Nizam Ali, ₹7 lakh, after Basalat Jang's death
  [2:0.3] Ceded Districts | 1800 | Subsidiary Alliance
  [2:0.4] Nellore, Chittoor (Carnatic) | 1801 | Carnatic Treaty with Nawab Azim-ud-Daula (NextGen: 1802); Andhra merged into the Madras Presidency
[2:1] - **Guntur:** came under the Company in **1788** and became a Madras district (G2 JA); "finally took possession" = **1788** (not 1768/1775/1782/1789).
[2:2] - **Godavari district** came under the Company in **1766**.
[2:3] - **Before 1794:** factories and warehouses at **Machilipatnam and Visakhapatnam**; **Chiefs and Councils** (provincial revenue boards) there ran the Circars; "Sarkars came in 1799" is false (1766).
[2:4] - **Committee of Circuit (Circuit Committee), 1775:** sent by the Madras Council to inquire into the zamindars' (samsthanams') revenue and abuses; recommended **against the auction system**. ("Union of Circuits" in a G2-A 1984 translation = this committee.)
[2:5] - **Madras Board of Revenue:** set up **1786**.
[2:6] - **Collector system:** **1794** – district **Collectors replaced the Chiefs and Councils** in the coastal districts (G2-A 1989, G2 2012); **1800** – Munro Collector of the Ceded Districts. 1786 is the Board of Revenue, not the Collectors.
[2:7] - **Carnatic districts:** Muhammad Ali installed as Nawab after the Second Carnatic War; annexed in 1801 after his death.
[2:8] - Hierarchy: Board of Revenue → Collector → Tahsildar (taluk) → Karanam, Munsab (village); Andhra's own Board of Revenue 1953. Collectors leaned on **dubashis**.

## SUB 5 | Thomas Munro and the Company's Officials
[5:0] - **Three officials revered in Andhra:** **Sir Thomas Munro, Sir Arthur Cotton and Charles Phillip Brown**.
[5:1] - **Munro (1761-1827):** with **Read** in the **Baramahal (1792)** – first Ryotwari; **first Principal Collector of the Ceded Districts (1800-07)**; **suppressed the palegars** (80 resisted, 18 months; with Gen. Campbell) and ended auctions; survey **1802-07**, Ryotwari (1802); spoke Telugu, needed no dubashis; President, **Judicial Commission 1814** (report April 1816); **Governor of Madras 1820-27**; died of cholera at **Pattikonda** (Kurnool), **6 July 1827**; "Mandava Rishi".
[5:2] - Others: **C.P. Brown** – Kadapa Collector, Vemana, Telugu-English dictionary; **Cotton** – Dowleswaram; **Charles Orr** – Krishna anicut; **Veenam Veeranna** – Cotton's assistant; **G.N. Taylor** – Anglo-vernacular schools.

## SUB 6 | Judiciary, Police and Later Links
[6:0] TABLE head: Level | Court
  [6:0.0] Taluk | Munsiff court
  [6:0.1] District | Zilla court under a District Judge (Cornwallis Code, 1793)
  [6:0.2] Apex (natives) | Sadar Adalat; Sadar Faujdari Adalat (criminal), Madras
  [6:0.3] British subjects | Madras Supreme Court (1801); Madras High Court 1862
[6:1] - **Munro's Judicial Commission (1814; report April 1816):** Madras Regulations of 1816 – **Munsiff and Panchayat courts** as courts of first instance; **police supervision from judges to Collectors**; Collectors lost **judicial** powers in 1816.
[6:2] - **Police:** Cornwallis's **thanas under darogas**; Madras District Police Act **1859**; CPC **1859**, IPC **1860**, CrPC **1861**.
[6:3] - Criticism: slow, costly British procedure → more litigation and debt; judges drawn from the revenue service.
[6:4] - **Links (2026):** Rayalaseema sericulture traced to British promotion in the Ceded Districts – silk hubs **Dharmavaram, Madanapalle** (20 Feb 2026); Supreme Court notice (1 May 2026) on a plea under **Article 14** for a separate revenue judicial service; the Circars-Ceded gap later shaped the **Sri Bagh Pact**.
