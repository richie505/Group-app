## SUB 0 | Anicuts and Irrigation – Cotton, Veeranna, Orr and Ramapada Sagar
[0:0] # Dowleswaram (Godavari) anicut
[0:1] - 1. **Guntur famine (1832-33)** – the trigger (about 40% of Guntur's people died).
[0:2] - 2. **1844:** Cotton's report to the Board of Revenue for an anicut near Rajahmundry.
[0:3] - 3. Sanctioned late **1846**; begun **1847**; **completed April 1852** at about **Rs 1.5 lakh** (NextGen L3 p.49 says 1849 – the standard year is **1852**).
[0:4] - **Veenam Veeranna** – the **Andhra (Indian) official** who managed the labour; made **Rao Bahadur** and Assistant Engineer; name on the anicut wall.
[0:5] TABLE head: Anicut | River | Engineer | Years
  [0:5.0] Dowleswaram | Godavari | Sir Arthur Cotton, assisted by Veenam Veeranna | 1847-1852
  [0:5.1] Krishna anicut, Bezwada (now Prakasam Barrage) | Krishna | proposed by Col. Henry Atlock (Lake); executed by Captain (Charles) Orr on Cotton's plan | 1850-1855
  [0:5.2] Sangam anicut | Penna (Nellore) | – | 1882-86
[0:6] - **Captain Orr** = the **English officer** who assisted Cotton (Krishna anicut); Captain Nolt defeated Narasimha Reddy – a distractor.
[0:7] - **Cotton (1803-1899):** "**pioneer of irrigation in South India**"; knighted **1861**; "Apara Bhagiratha", "Cotton Dora"; on his suggestion Rajahmundry → **Godavari** and Machilipatnam → **Krishna** districts (1859); today's **Sir Arthur Cotton Barrage** replaced the old anicut.
[0:8] - **Ramapada Sagar(am) at Polavaram:** the "dream-child" of **S.V. (Sonti Venkata) Ramamurthi, ICS**, who named it after the backwaters reaching Lord Rama's feet at Bhadrachalam (1941-42; first survey by **L. Venkatakrishna Iyer**) = forerunner of the **Polavaram** project (APPSC G2 Prelims 2019). Not Cotton or K.L. Rao.
[0:9] - **Godavari and Krishna deltas** = the "**Rice Bowl of Andhra**". **Feb 2026:** crest-gate telemetry and SCADA installed at the **Prakasam Barrage**.

## SUB 2 | Railways and Communications
[2:0] - **First railway line in Andhra:** **Puttur–Renigunta**, opened **15 September 1862** (Madras Railway) – asked ×4; later Gooty–Renigunta.
[2:1] - Railways linked the interior with the **Madras port**; built mainly for military and commercial needs.
[2:2] - **Buckingham Canal:** salt-water canal through **Pulicat Lake**; extended as famine relief in **1876-78**; now part of **NW-4**.
[2:3] - **Fort St. George** was built at **Madrasapatnam** (1639-40).

## SUB 4 | Economic Effects and Famines
[4:0] - Cash revenue demand → **commercial crops**: **tobacco in Guntur**, indigo, cotton (Ganjam 1865-66 famine followed the cotton boom of the American Civil War).
[4:1] - **Deindustrialisation:** duty-free British cloth ruined weavers – Visakhapatnam exports ₹7 lakh → ₹1 lakh by the 1830s; Machilipatnam dyed cloth, Eluru carpets, Kurnool blankets declined.
[4:2] TABLE head: Famine | Year | Note
  [4:2.0] Doji Bara (Skull) | 1791-92 | Madras region
  [4:2.1] Guntur (Nandana / Pedda Karuvu) | 1832-33 | ~1.5 lakh deaths; led to Dowleswaram
  [4:2.2] Ganjam (Orissa) | 1865-66 | after the cotton boom
  [4:2.3] Great Famine | 1876-78 | worst in the Ceded Districts
