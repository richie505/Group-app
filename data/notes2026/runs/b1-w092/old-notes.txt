=== NOTES 1:92 | Kakatiyas – Origins, Beta I, Rudradeva, Ganapatideva; Kakatiya–yadava–hoysala Relations
## SUB 0 | Origins, Overlords and First Mentions
[92:0:0] # Overlords
[92:0:1] - **First overlords:** the **Rashtrakutas** (APPSC G2 2005 ×2; G2 Mains 2017 ×2) – the Magallu record calls Gundaya and Eriya "Rashtrakuta", and the early emblem was **Garuda**.
[92:0:2] - **Then:** feudatories of the **Kalyani (Western) Chalukyas** – emblem **Varaha** (changed by **Prola I**); title **Mahamandaleswara** (APPSC G2 2001), borne by Beta II.
[92:0:3] - **⚠ Trap:** the **Eastern Chalukyas** only issued the Magallu grant; they were never Kakatiya overlords.
[92:0:4] - **Phases:** samanta (feudatory) 956–1162 → **sovereign 1163–1323** – the Kakatiyas ruled Andhra in the **12th–13th centuries** (APPSC Social & Cultural History).
[92:0:5] - **UPSC 2023:** none of the Hoysalas (10th–11th c.), Gahadavalas (11th c.), Kakatiyas or Yadavas (12th c.) was founded in the early 8th century → **None**.
[92:0:6] # First mentions and progenitor
[92:0:7] TABLE head: Record | Fact
  [92:0:7.0] Magallu grant (c. 950–956) of Eastern Chalukya Danarnava | First mention of the Kakatiyas – Kakatiya Gundyana (Kakara Gundana) (G2 Mains 2017; APPSC PYQ)
  [92:0:7.1] Bayyaram tank inscription of Mailamba (Ganapati's sister) | Best genealogy; line begins with Venna (Venna Bhupati) (Group-II)
  [92:0:7.2] Kazipet (Dargah) inscription | Names Beta I as the first Kakatiya king (APPSC G1 2002; APPSC PYQ ×2); records the accession of Durga Nripathi
[92:0:8] - **Early base:** **Hanamkonda (Anumakonda) Vishaya**, received by Beta I through his aunt **Viriyala Kamasani**.
[92:0:9] # Religion and identity
[92:0:10] - **Jains first:** the Kakatiyas were initially **Jains** and later **Shaivas** – the clan whose first rulers were Jains (2002 paper).
[92:0:11] - **Garuda emblem** from Jain Tirthankara **Shantinatha**; clan deity **Kakatamma (Kakati Devi)** = the Jain **Kushmandini**; **"Kakati" = gourd**.
[92:0:12] - **Saiva gurus:** **Rameswara Pandita** (Mallikarjuna matha, **Srisailam**) → **Beta II**; **Dhruveshwara Pandita** → **Mahadeva**; **Visvesvara Sivacharya** (Golaki Matha) → **Ganapatideva** (raja guru, APPSC PYQ ×3).
[92:0:13] - **Unifiers of Andhra:** the Kakatiyas were the **second dynasty after the Satavahanas** to unite all Telugu lands; they "gave a comprehensive form to Andhraism" – the dynasty that gave the **ultimate design to Andhra culture** (DAO 2011).
[92:0:14] - **Loose PYQ:** "one of the major dynasties that ruled Andhra" – key **Satavahanas**, the first major Andhra dynasty (all four options ruled Andhra).
[92:0:15] - **Eastern Deccan:** the **Kakatiyas** ruled the eastern Deccan in the 12th–14th c.; the Seunas held the west.
[92:0:16] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History - Kakatiyas p.35-37, p.41; PMF Ancient & Medieval India p.410; app notes 1:92 (sub 0, 9)
[92:0:17] - **Historian's verdict:** **P.V. Parabrahma Sastry** pointed out that **Kannada (Chalukya) rule in Andhra ended** with the establishment of the Kakatiya kingdom.
## SUB 1 | Capitals – Anumakonda to Orugallu (Warangal)
[92:1:0] # Who shifted the capital?
[92:1:1] TABLE head: Step | Ruler | Capital
  [92:1:1.0] 1 | Prola I | Made Anumakonda (Hanumakonda) the capital
  [92:1:1.1] 2 | Prola II | Developed Hanamkonda; Swayambhu temple in Orugallu
  [92:1:1.2] 3 | Rudradeva | Founded Orugallu and began the Warangal fort
  [92:1:1.3] 4 | Ganapatideva | Completed the fort and transferred the capital from Hanumakonda to Orugallu (Rocket: 1254); called it Andhranagari
  [92:1:1.4] 5 | After 1323 | Renamed Sultanpur
[92:1:2] - **Answer to "who shifted the capital?":** **Ganapatideva** (APPSC G2 2012; Architecture 2011; APPSC PYQ; G1 2024 match B-I).
[92:1:3] - **⚠ Disputed:** the Bekkallu inscription (1195) already calls Orugallu Rudradeva's capital, so some keys (G2 2019, Asst. Welfare Officers) gave **Rudradeva**; NextGen, Rocket and the G1 2024 official match credit **Ganapatideva**, who completed the move.
[92:1:4] - **Orugallu fort:** construction **begun by Rudradeva** (APSLPRB 2023), completed by Ganapatideva, strengthened by Rudramadevi.
[92:1:5] - **Kakatiya capital:** **Orugallu / Warangal** (APPSC 2019 ×2; Polytechnic Lecturers 2020) – not Vijayanagara, Amaravati, Vijayawada, Guntur or Visakhapatnam.
[92:1:6] - **Orugallu** = "single rock", the ancient name of Warangal; **Warangal** is the place that best reflects the Kakatiya contribution to culture (fort, Thousand Pillar temple, Ramappa nearby) (AEE 2008).

## SUB 3 | Ganapatideva (1199–1262)
[92:3:0] - **Reign:** **1199–1262** (about **63 years**, the longest; APPSC AE 2011); first inscription **Manthena, 26 Dec 1199**; title **Andhra Deshadhiswara**.
[92:3:1] - **Accession:** held captive by Yadava **Jaitugi**; **Recherla Rudra** crushed revolts and installed him.
[92:3:2] - **Unifier:** brought **the whole Telugu area under one rule** after the Satavahanas (1199 PYQ ×2), from Ganjam to Kanchi – the Kakatiya who ruled all Andhradesa as one sovereign (APPSC G1 2003).
[92:3:3] - **Kanchi:** the most powerful Kakatiya, who **extended the empire up to Kanchi** (Group-I 1990-91; G1 1994) – not Beta I, Prola I or Prola II.
[92:3:4] - **Gurus:** raja guru **Visvesvara Sivacharya** – not Vidyanatha, Narahari Tirtha, Rameswara Pandita or Dhruveshwara Pandita.
[92:3:5] # Campaigns
[92:3:6] - **Velanadu:** 1201 coastal campaign – general **Mutyalachaundaraya** took **Divi**; married **Narama and Perama**, daughters of **Pinnachodi**; made their brother **Jayapa** **Gajasahani**.
[92:3:7] - **1210:** with **Choda Tikka** of Nellore killed **Prithviswara** → Velanati kingdom ended (title **Prithviswara Shirakhandika Kreeda Vinoda**); **1257:** with Akkana and Bayyana killed **Mummadi Rajendra Chola** – final end of the Velanati Chodas.
[92:3:8] - **Pithapuram inscription** of Velanati **Prithvisvara** names **Kolleru Lake** (G2 2017).
[92:3:9] - **Nellore:** restored **Manumasiddhi II** after the poet **Tikkana** came as envoy; Tikkana dedicated **Nirvachanottara Ramayana** to **Manumasiddhi**, not Ganapati.
[92:3:10] - **Kalinga:** fought the **Eastern Gangas** for southern Kalinga and Vengi.
[92:3:11] - **Defeat, 1263:** **Jatavarman Sundara Pandya** beat the Kakatiyas at **Muttukur** (Nellore); **Nellore fell to the Pandyas** (G2 Mains 2017 ×2) – not the Velanati Chodas, Yadavas or Hoysalas.
[92:3:12] # Works and succession
[92:3:13] - **Ramappa temple** (Recherla Rudra, 1213), **Lakkavaram** and **Ghanapur** tanks, **Ganapavaram** villages, **Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam** (1244).
[92:3:14] - **Telugu replaced Sanskrit** in inscriptions from his reign.
[92:3:15] - **Family:** no son → daughter **Rudramadevi succeeded him** (APPSC PYQ), raised as a son (putrika), trained by her mother **Kupama Devi**; married **Veerabhadra** of the **Nidadavolu Eastern Chalukyas**; daughter **Ganapamba** married the **Kota** chief; sisters **Mailamba** and **Kundamamba** married the sons of **Natavadi Buddharaja**.
[92:3:16] - Rudrama defeated the Pandyas, Eastern Gangas and Seunas – **not the Western Chalukyas**; Marco Polo visited Motupalli ("Mutfili") in her reign.
[92:3:17] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History - Kakatiyas p.11-15, p.47-51; app notes 1:92 (sub 4, 5)
[92:3:18] - **Greatest Kakatiya:** **Ganapatideva** largely restored the **political unity of the Telugu country**; he had **two daughters**, **Rudramadevi** and **Ganapamba (Ganapamadevi)**.

## SUB 0 | Prataparudra Yasobhushanam and Kakatiya Court Literature
[93:0:0] - **Prataparudra Yasobhushanam:** Sanskrit work on **alankara (poetics)** praising **Prataparudra II**, by his court poet **Vidyanatha** (APPSC G2 2000, 2011; Police Constable 2016; GS common paper; APPSC PYQ ×5) – not Prataparudra I or II themselves, Jayapa, Ambadeva, Sarangadhara, Rudradeva, Tikkana, Ketana, Baddena or Palkuriki Somanatha.
[93:0:1] - **Vidyanatha = Agastya** (G2 2012): historians identify the two; Agastya also wrote **Nalakirtikaumudi**, **Balabharata** and **Krishnacharita**.
[93:0:2] - **"What Bana was to Harsha, Vidyanatha was to Prataparudradeva."**
[93:0:3] - **Gangadevi**, author of **Madhura Vijayam** (Vijayanagara), hailed **Agastya** as her guru; Madhura Vijayam also describes the hardship after the conquest.

## SUB 0 | Motupalli Port and the Abhaya Sasanam
[94:0:0] - **Chief port:** **Motupalli** – the main Kakatiya seaport for external trade (APPSC G2 2000, 2011, 2012) – not Warangal (inland capital), Krishnapatnam, Pulicat, Nellore, Machilipatnam, Ghantasala, Dharanikota or Korangi.
[94:0:1] - **Other name:** **Desiyakonda (Deseeyakkonda / Desyakkonda) Pattanam** (APPSC G2 2019 ×2; G2 2012; APPSC PYQ) – not Nidadavolu, Vadarevu, Motukur, Mudigonda, Mukalapura or Malakapuram.
[94:0:2] # Abhaya Sasanam (Motupalli Charter)
[94:0:3] - **Issuer:** **Ganapatideva**, **1244** CE, a pillar inscription at Motupalli (APPSC G2 2019; Group-II-A 1984; Welfare Officers 2017 ×2; APPSC PYQ ×3) – not Rudramba, Ambadeva or Prataparudra.
[94:0:4] - **Content:** **protection and shelter to foreign (sea) merchants**; unfair taxes cancelled, only fixed duties; **compensation for shipwreck or theft**; no harassment by officials.
[94:0:5] - **Official posted:** **Siddayya Devudu**.
[94:0:6] - **Most important commercial document** of the period.
[94:0:7] - **Goods at Motupalli:** **sandal, camphor, rose-water, ivory**, pearls, corals, copper, silk, pepper.
[94:0:8] - **Marco Polo** visited Motupalli in **Rudramadevi's** reign.

[94:1:1] - **Ari (Aari):** the Kakatiya **land tax / land revenue** (Juvenile Welfare 2011; APPSC PYQ) – generally **one-sixth** of the produce; taxpayers = **Arigapulu**; collectors = **Kolakandru**.
[94:1:2] - **Land classes:** **Magani** = irrigated wet land (food crops); **Metta** = rain-fed dry land.
[94:1:3] - **Tax-free:** temple lands, Brahmin grants, Ayagar lands, **Dashabandhamu** (land for tank upkeep).

## SUB 4 | Nayankara System and Administration
[94:4:0] # Nayankara system
[94:4:1] - **Introduced by:** **Prataparudra II** (Asst. Welfare Officers PYQ; APPSC 2025), who organised the kingdom into **77 Nayankaras** – ⚠ one book key and a NextGen Level-II line say Rudrama.
[94:4:2] - **How:** military commanders (**Nayakas**) were made administrators of **Sthalas / Mandalams** and given charge of **forts** – the Kakatiya empire was ruled through vassals called **Nayakas** (2002 paper).
[94:4:3] - **Land instead of salary:** villages called **Nayakasthalam**; Nayankaras held about **one-fourth** of the land; no monthly cash salary.
[94:4:4] - **Duties:** collect revenue → keep a fixed army for the king → pay a fixed annual sum.
[94:4:5] - **Sources:** **Ekamranatha's Pratapa Charitra**; Baddena's Niti Sastra Muktavali.
[94:4:6] - **Successor systems:** Vijayanagara **Amaranayaka** (land = **Amaram**) → **Poligars**.
[94:4:7] # Administration
[94:4:8] - **Nature of the state:** most scholars – **decentralised monarchy**; **Romila Thapar** – "metropolitan state"; **Burton Stein** – "segmentary state"; **Cynthia Talbot** – "Golden Age of the Shudras".
[94:4:9] - **Prime ministers:** **Malyala Hemadri Reddi** (Ganapatideva); **Muppidi Nayudu** (Prataparudra II).
[94:4:10] - **Units:** **Rashtra / Mandala** (largest) → Nadu → Sthala → Grama.
[94:4:11] - **72 Niyogas** (departments) under the **Bahattara Niyogadhipati**.
[94:4:12] - **Tirthulu / Ashtadasa Tirthas:** the **18 high officers (ministers) of state** (Sakala Neeti Sammatamu of **Madiki Singana**) – ministers, not water sources, temples or spies (APPSC PYQ).
[94:4:13] TABLE head: Officer | Role
  [94:4:13.0] Pradvivaka | Judicial adviser
  [94:4:13.1] Kayasthas | Scribes
  [94:4:13.2] Sunkadhikari | Collector of tolls and customs
[94:4:14] # Army
[94:4:15] - **Mula Balam** = king's own standing army; **Gaja Sahini** (elephants) = **Jayapa Senani** under Ganapatideva; **Ashwa Sahini** (cavalry) = **Gangaya Sahini**.
[94:4:16] - **Giridurga** = hill fort (also Jala, Vana, Sthala durgas); **Padmanayakas** = Recherla Velama chiefs, key commanders.
[94:4:17] - Warangal fort: inner stone wall with **45 bastions**.

=== NOTES 1:95 | Kakatiya Society and Religion – Veerashaivism, Jainism, Temple Institutions
## SUB 0 | Shaiva Sects, Veerashaivism and the Aradhyas
[95:0:0] # Shaiva sects
[95:0:1] TABLE head: Sect | Founder / trait
  [95:0:1.0] Pasupata | Oldest Shaiva sect; founded by Lakulisa (Nakulisa), 1st–2nd c. AD; popular into the Reddi period, declined under Vijayanagara
  [95:0:1.1] Kalamukha | Founded by Kalasana (Kalanana), disciple of Lakulisa; extremist; temple managers, gave anna dana; Simha Parishads
  [95:0:1.2] Kapalika | Offshoot of Pasupata (the "Vamacharyas" of the Kalamukhas); worship Bhairava; skull-bowl, Panchamudras
  [95:0:1.3] Aradhya | Brahmin Shaivas led by Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya; kept Vedic ritual and varna
[95:0:2] - **Pasupata founder:** **Lakulisa** (APPSC PYQ) – not Basava (Veerashaivism), Bijjala (Kalachuri king) or Kallata.
[95:0:3] - **Kapalikas emerged from:** **Pasupata Shaivism** (APPSC G2 2019; APPSC PYQ).
[95:0:4]   - ⚠ Sources differ: NextGen's PYQ list says "the Kapalikas emerged from the school of **Misra Saivam**" – the app keys Pasupata (most books) and shows the dispute.
[95:0:5] - **Kakatiya patronage:** early kings (Beta, Durga) were disciples of **Kalamukha** acharyas of the Srisailam Silamatha; Kalamukha centres – **Srisailam, Alampur, Vemulawada, Kaleshwaram**, Tripurantakam.
[95:0:6] # Aradhya acharyas – Siddhatraya
[95:0:7] - **12 Aradhya acharyas** in **four triads**; **Siddhatraya** = **Revana, Marula, Ekorama** (G2 2012; APPSC PYQ).
[95:0:8] - **Not a Siddhacharya:** **Udbhata** – a Sanskrit poetician (alankarika).
[95:0:9] # Veerashaivism
[95:0:10] - **Founder:** **Basaveshwara** (Basava), minister of **Kalachuri Bijjala** (Karnataka); spread into Andhra in the Kakatiya period through **Palkuriki Somanatha** (Basavapuranam, Panditaradhya Charitra).
[95:0:11] - **Followers:** **Lingayats**; gurus **Jangamas**; in Andhra inscriptions called **Veeravratulu**; main Andhra centre **Srisailam**.
[95:0:12] - **Teaching:** **rejected** the Vedas, caste, child marriage and sacrifice → drew artisans and lower groups (social mobility).
[95:0:13] - **Reddi period:** the **popular religion was Veerashaivism** (G2 2011), alongside Pasupata patronised by the kings.
[95:0:14] > Kakatiya age = the **"Shaiva era"**; dynastic deity **Svayambhu Shiva**.
[95:0:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History - APPSC 04 p.28 (sects); NextGen Kakatiyas p.31; NextGen Post-Kakatiyas (Reddi religion); NextGen Previous Papers p.47; app notes 1:95 (sub 3, 4)
[95:0:16] - **Standard text:** **(Veera Saiva) Siddhanta Shikhamani** of **Shivayogi Shivacharya** is regarded as the complete and standard scripture of Veerashaivism; Basava Puranam is a biography of Basava.

[95:1:6] - **Why:** the Kakatiyas were **Sudras** (of **Pulinda/Boya** origin) who rose through military service; officials came from warrior and peasant groups (Nayankara); **Brahmins lost political clout**.

[95:2:0] - **Early Kakatiyas:** **Jains** (G2 2011) → **Shaiva** from **Prola II / Rudradeva**.
[95:2:1] - **Decline of Jainism:** **Kalamukha and Veerashaiva hostility**; Jain–Saiva conflicts from Rudradeva's reign.
