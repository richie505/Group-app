## SUB 0 | Poverty Line in India – Basis and Calorie Norms
[0:0] - **Poverty line:** the minimum income / consumption level needed for basic needs; a person is poor if income or consumption falls **below that minimum** (APPSC: statements A and B correct).
[0:1] - It **varies with time and place** (basic needs and prices differ) and is **not** estimated by the Census every ten years (UPPCS 2025: A and R true, R explains A).
[0:2] - **Basis:** **household consumption expenditure** – **monthly per capita consumption expenditure (MPCE)** – not income: incomes of the self-employed and daily-wage workers are hard to judge and change with seasons. MCQs offering only "income method" among invented options key **income method**.
[0:3] - **Calorie norm:** **2,400 kcal rural / 2,100 kcal urban** per person per day (ICMR; **Alagh 1979**, Lakdawala 1993); Dandekar-Rath (1971): 2,250 for both.
[0:4] - Poverty in India is "defined in terms of" **calorie intake**; BPL = whether a person can obtain a **minimum prescribed food basket**.
[0:5] - **Poverty Line Basket (PLB):** socially accepted minimum basket of basic needs bought in the market.
[0:6] - Methods used: **Head Count Ratio, calorie intake, household consumption expenditure** (not per capita income); **literacy is not used** to fix the line.
[0:7] - **Survey:** **NSSO** household consumption expenditure survey (now **HCES**, MoSPI) about every 5 years; estimates by the Planning Commission / **NITI Aayog**; **BPL census** by the Ministry of Rural Development.
[0:8] - Official poverty 1999-2000 (MRP): about **26% (26.1)**.

## SUB 1 | Types and Concepts of Poverty
[1:0] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [1:0.0] Absolute | Below a fixed minimum subsistence / calorie norm; India's concern; UN definition adopted at Copenhagen (World Summit for Social Development), 1995
  [1:0.1] Relative | Income well below the median of the society – comparison of income groups (top vs bottom fractiles), not a fixed calorie norm; persists in USA, UK; measured by Lorenz curve / Gini
  [1:0.2] Subjective | A person's own perception of his position (perception is subjective)
  [1:0.3] Chronic | Always poor + usually poor – long periods or across generations
  [1:0.4] Transient | Churning poor (move in and out of poverty – small farmers, seasonal workers) + occasionally poor
  [1:0.5] Culture of poverty | Oscar Lewis, American anthropologist – poverty passed on as a way of life
[1:1] - **Cyclic / churning poor:** those who **keep moving between poor and non-poor**.
[1:2] - Types accepted: **absolute, relative, subjective** – "functional poverty" is not one.
[1:3] - **Capability approach:** **Amartya Sen**. **Engel's Law:** as income rises, the **share** spent on food **falls**.
[1:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 16 p.8-11 (absolute, relative, chronic and transient poor charts, Engel); app notes 3:29 sub 0
## SUB 2 | Causes and Social Indicators of Poverty
[2:0] - Causes: **slow growth, rapid population growth, unemployment** (unemployment raises the number below the line); a **rising rate of investment reduces** poverty.
[2:1] - Economic insecurity comes from **poverty, unemployment and falling real wages** – all three.
[2:2] - Roots of **urban poverty lie in rural areas**: low rural education → migrants get poor urban jobs (A and R true, R explains A).
[2:3] - **Social indicators:** illiteracy, lack of **safe drinking water** and sanitation, lack of health care, lack of **job opportunities**, malnutrition; **size of the house** and **number of means of transport** are **not** counted. **MPCE** (the poverty line itself) is an economic, not a social, indicator.
[2:4] - Solving poverty needs **a complex mix of methods**; economic and social issues are both relevant.
[2:5] - **MPI** (UNDP-OPHI, Alkire-Santos 2010): **health, education, living standards**.
[2:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 16 p.4-5 (MPI indicators); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 17 p.2-3 (Alkire-Santos); app notes 3:29 sub 3; PYQ explanations UPPCS 2013 / UP RO 2013 / UPPCS 2025
## SUB 3 | Poverty Measures – HCR, Poverty Gap, FGT
[3:0] TABLE head: Measure | Captures
  [3:0.0] Head Count Ratio | Incidence – proportion of population below the poverty line
  [3:0.1] Poverty Gap Index | Depth / intensity – average shortfall from the line
  [3:0.2] Squared Poverty Gap | Severity – more weight to the poorest
[3:1] - All three = **Foster-Greer-Thorbecke (FGT)** family; the **head count** method estimates **poverty**.
[3:2] - If the poor become poorer but their number is unchanged → **HCR unchanged**, PGI rises.
[3:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 16 p.11-12; NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 17 p.1
