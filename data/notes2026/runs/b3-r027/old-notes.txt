=== NOTES 3:27 | Demographic Transition Theory and India's Position; Demographic Dividend and Its Window
## SUB 0 | Population Theories → Propounder
[27:0:0] TABLE head: Theory | Propounder | Core idea
  [27:0:0.0] Principle of Population (1798) | T.R. Malthus | Population grows geometrically, food arithmetically; positive checks raise deaths, preventive checks (late marriage, restraint) lower births
  [27:0:0.1] Demographic transition | Warren Thompson (1929, early form); Frank Notestein (1945, standard form) | Birth and death rates fall as societies develop
  [27:0:0.2] Optimum population | Edwin Cannan | Population giving the highest income per head
  [27:0:0.3] The Population Bomb (1968) | Paul Ehrlich | Neo-Malthusian warning
[27:0:1] - **Demographic transition** = slow change in the pattern of population growth over a long period.
## SUB 1 | Demographic Transition Model
[27:1:0] TABLE head: Stage | Birth rate | Death rate | Result
  [27:1:0.0] I | High | High | Low growth
  [27:1:0.1] II | High | Falling | Population explosion
  [27:1:0.2] III | Falling | Low | Growth slows
  [27:1:0.3] IV | Low | Low | Stable, low growth
[27:1:1] - Order with development: **high birth & high death → high birth & low death → low birth & low death**.
[27:1:2] - **India: late Stage 3, moving into Stage 4**; **Andhra Pradesh: Stage 4** (low fertility).
[27:1:3] - **Stable population**: birth rate = death rate and no migration.
[27:1:4] - **Urbanisation reduced both birth and death rates** (education, work, medical care).
[27:1:5] - Low birth rates / ageing / declining population in news: **Italy, Japan, South Korea**.
## SUB 2 | Key Terms
[27:2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning / base
  [27:2:0.0] CBR / CDR | Births / deaths per 1,000 mid-year population
  [27:2:0.1] IMR | Infant deaths (before age 1) per 1,000 live births
  [27:2:0.2] MMR (maternal mortality ratio) | Maternal deaths per 1,00,000 live births
  [27:2:0.3] Maternal mortality rate | Per women aged 15-49
  [27:2:0.4] TFR | Total live births an imagined woman would have through the reproductive ages at current age-specific fertility rates
  [27:2:0.5] Dependency ratio | (0-14 + 65+) ÷ (15-64)
  [27:2:0.6] Working age | 15-64; young dependents below 15; old 65+
  [27:2:0.7] Youth (NYP 2014) | 15-29 years; approved 9 Jan 2014, replaced NYP 2003
[27:2:1] MMR is per 1,00,000 live births, not per 1,000.
[27:2:2] - **Rate of natural increase:** CBR minus CDR (per 1,000 population). Example: CBR 30 - CDR 10 = 20 per 1,000 = 2.0% a year.

## SUB 5 | Demographic Dividend and Its Window
[27:5:0] # Meaning
[27:5:1] - **Demographic dividend**: growth potential from a high share of the **15-64** working-age group.
[27:5:2] - **Demographic bonus** (around 2016): jump in the productive-age population.
[27:5:3] - Working age **15-64 = 62.9%**; aged 20+ (Census 2011) **59.29%**; under 14 about **29% (28.67%)**; above 65 **5-6%** (4.8% in Census 2011).
[27:5:4] # Window
[27:5:5] - India's window: **2005-06 to 2055-56**.
[27:5:6] - Peak: around **2041**, when the 20-59 share reaches about **59%** (Economic Survey 2018-19).
[27:5:7] - 0-19 share: **41% (2011) → 25% (2041)**; 60+ share **8.6% → 16%**; **20.5% (325.3 mn) by 2051**.
## SUB 6 | Dependency Ratio
[27:6:0] - Lower ratio = fewer dependants per worker → **potential dividend** (UPSC).
[27:6:1] - Falling mainly due to **fall in fertility**; the working-age (15-59) group is relatively high.
[27:6:2] - Fell **79% (1975) → 58% (2008) → about 48% (2021)**.
[27:6:3] - **Old-age dependency ratio (2011): 14.2%** (60+ relative to 15-59).
[27:6:4] - Dependent population in the pyramid: **0-14** (and 65+).
## SUB 7 | Conditions to Harness the Dividend
[27:7:0] - Need **health, education, skills and adequate jobs**; otherwise a **"demographic nightmare"** (Economic Survey 2018-19).
[27:7:1] - Full benefit needs **skill development** (closing the skill demand-supply gap).
[27:7:2] - **Female LFPR**: 23.3% (2017-18) → **41.7% (2023-24)**; cutting it would weaken the dividend.
[27:7:3] - Highest economically active women share of working-age women (1996, options): **China**.

[27:8:4] # Determinants of Birth Rate
[27:8:5] - **Female education and higher age at marriage**; **child survival** (lower IMR); **urbanisation** and cost of children; **contraception** access.
[27:8:6] - High literacy linked to low birth rate; urbanisation lowers birth rate (both correct).
[27:8:7] - Rising **Couple Protection Rate** cuts birth rate most when couples are **younger**.
[27:8:8] - More male children born each year; infant mortality among boys is higher than girls.
[27:8:9] - Low sex ratio causes: high maternal deaths, female child deaths, female foeticide, more male births (about **105 boys per 100 girls**).
[27:8:10] # Andhra Pradesh

[27:8:12] - **Median age 32.5** (India 28); by 2036 under-14 share 25% → 15%; 60+ **10% → 19%**.
[27:8:13] - AP is not in the wide-window phase like Bihar and UP.

=== NOTES 3:29 | Poverty – Concepts (absolute, Relative, Chronic), Poverty Line Debates
## SUB 0 | Types of Poverty
[29:0:0] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [29:0:0.0] Absolute | Lack of minimum resources for survival; set by a fixed consumption / calorie norm (India's focus); defined by the UN at the World Summit for Social Development, Copenhagen, 1995
  [29:0:0.1] Relative | Income well below the median income of society; compares fractile groups; used in developed economies (persists in USA, UK) – NOT a fixed calorie norm
  [29:0:0.2] Subjective | A person's own perception of their position
  [29:0:0.3] Chronic | Lasts over long periods or across generations
  [29:0:0.4] Transient / cyclical | Temporary – shocks, seasons, cycles
  [29:0:0.5] Culture of poverty | Oscar Lewis (American anthropologist): poverty handed down as a way of life
[29:0:1] - Types asked: **absolute, relative, subjective** – NOT "functional".
[29:0:2] - Grouping: by **base information** (objective / subjective) and by **threshold scale** (absolute / relative).
[29:0:3] - **Amartya Sen**: **capability approach**.
[29:0:4] - Absolute poverty is measured by the **World Bank line** and calorie norm (APSES 2024-25).
[29:0:5] - **Unemployment leads to poverty**: lack of income raises the number below the line.
[29:0:6] Fixed calorie norm = absolute poverty, not relative.
## SUB 1 | Poverty Measures – Fgt Family
[29:1:0] TABLE head: Measure | Captures | Formula
  [29:1:0.0] Head Count Ratio | Incidence – share below the line | HC = Nq / N
  [29:1:0.1] Poverty Gap Index | Depth – average shortfall | (1/n) Σ (Z − yi)/Z
  [29:1:0.2] Squared Poverty Gap | Severity – more weight to the poorest | (1/n) Σ [(Z − yi)/Z]²
[29:1:1] - All three = **Foster-Greer-Thorbecke (FGT)** family.
[29:1:2] - If the poor become poorer but their number is unchanged → **HCR unchanged; PGI changes**.
[29:1:3] - **Engel's Law**: as income rises, the **share** spent on food **falls** (absolute amount may rise).
[29:1:4] Incidence – depth – severity = HCR – PGI – squared poverty gap.
## SUB 2 | Poverty Line in India – Basis
[29:2:0] - **Survey**: **NSSO** household consumption expenditure survey (now **HCES**, MoSPI).
[29:2:1] - Basis = **consumption expenditure (MPCE), not income** – incomes of self-employed and daily-wage workers are hard to judge and change with seasons.
[29:2:2] - Poor = consumption below the line (APPSC-GS 2024: statements A and B correct).
[29:2:3] - Poverty in India defined in terms of **calorie intake**: **2,400 kcal rural / 2,100 kcal urban** per capita per day (ICMR, Alagh).
[29:2:4] - **Poverty Line Basket (PLB)**: socially accepted minimum basket of basic needs bought in the market.
[29:2:5] - BPL = whether a person can obtain a **minimum prescribed food basket**.
[29:2:6] - **Literacy is NOT used** to decide the poverty line.
[29:2:7] - Lines differ across time and countries because **basic needs vary by region and over time** (UPPSC 2025: A and R true, R explains A).
[29:2:8] - Roots of **urban poverty lie in rural areas**: low rural education → migrants get poor jobs.
[29:2:9] - Official poverty 1999-2000 (Mixed Recall Period): about **26% (26.1)**.
## SUB 3 | Social Indicators of Poverty
[29:3:0] - Poverty shows in **illiteracy**, lack of **safe drinking water**, poor **housing**, and lack of **jobs**; these are social indicators used to judge deprivation.
[29:3:1] - The poverty line itself uses **consumption**, not literacy or other social indicators.
[29:3:2] - Shift to **multidimensional measures (MPI)** covers health, education and living standards.
