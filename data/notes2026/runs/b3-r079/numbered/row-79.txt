[1:3]   - **Now 5 RBI-regulated AIFIs:** **EXIM Bank, NABARD, NHB, SIDBI + NaBFID** (added 2022); EXIM = term-lending institution; NABARD, NHB, SIDBI = **refinance institutions** (APPSC AEE: **NHB**, not IFCI, TFCI or EXIM).
[1:4] - **"All-India development finance institution"** (G2 2017): **SIDBI** – an all-India development bank; EXIM Bank is a specialised FI, UTI an investment institution, ECGC an "other" institution.
[1:5] - **Under the control of the RBI:** **NABARD** – RBI held **72.5%** of NABARD until it sold 71.5% in **October 2010** (residual sold 26 Feb 2019); **NHB** was **100% RBI-owned until 19 March 2019**. IDBI was an RBI subsidiary only from 1964 to **1976**.
[1:6] - **Set up with RBI help:** IDBI, SIDBI, NABARD, NHB, EXIM Bank (**not SEBI**); all now Government-owned.
[1:7] - **Quasi-regulators:** NABARD (RRBs, cooperative banks), SIDBI (SFCs), NHB (HFCs – supervision).
[1:8] - **Decline of DFIs:** with the capital market and banks entering term lending, "the role of DFIs as an exclusive source of development finance has **diminished**"; ICICI (2002) and IDBI (2004) became banks → the institution whose importance has **fallen most = development banks** (UPPCS 2010).
[1:9] - **Public sector dominance (IAS 1993):** commercial banking (nationalised banks) – the keyed answer; but in 1993 the organised term-lending FIs (IFCI, IDBI, SIDBI, IIBI) were also wholly public sector, ICICI excepted.

## SUB 3 | Microfinance – Grameen Model, AP Crisis and Malegam Committee
[3:0] # Origin
[3:1] - **Microfinance pioneer:** **Prof. Muhammad Yunus** (Bangladesh), founder of the **Grameen Bank** (Nobel Peace Prize 2006) – the Grameen model was developed in **Bangladesh**.
[3:2] # AP crisis to regulation
[3:3] - 1. **AP crisis 2010:** multiple lending, over-indebtedness, coercive recovery, borrower suicides; the AP MFI loan book then **collapsed** (did not expand).
[3:4] - 2. **AP MFI Ordinance, October 2010** (Act 2011): MFIs to register with **district authorities**; doorstep recovery curbed, interest capped.
[3:5] - 3. **Malegam Committee** (RBI sub-committee, **October 2010**, Y.H. Malegam) – set up **after the AP crisis**; created the **NBFC-MFI** category (framework **2 December 2011**); margin and interest caps; no more than **2 MFIs per borrower**. (Y.H. Malegam also chaired SEBI's 1995 committee on primary-market disclosure – hence "Malegam = capital market reforms" against Kelkar (tax), Naresh Chandra (corporate governance), Swaminathan.)
[3:6] - 4. **MFIN** (**2009**, association of NBFC-MFIs) and **Sa-Dhan**: RBI-recognised **SROs (2014)**.
[3:7] # Framework and issues
[3:8] - **NBFC-MFI:** non-deposit-taking; at least **75%** of assets in microfinance loans; minimum NOF **₹10 crore** (new registrations from 1 Oct 2022; earlier ₹5 crore, ₹2 crore in the North East).
[3:9] - **Microfinance loan (RBI, March 2022):** **collateral-free**; household annual income up to **₹3 lakh**; repayments ≤ **50%** of income.
[3:10] - **Funding:** MFIs borrow mainly from **banks and FIs** (they cannot take deposits) – **not government subsidies**.
[3:11] - **Impact:** unregulated lending can cause **over-indebtedness**; **technology** cuts costs; regulation (lending caps, margin and interest ceilings) **affects profitability**.
[3:12] - **Borrowers:** **95% women**, 80% rural; portfolio share (Mar 2025): **NBFC-MFIs 39%**, banks 32%, SFBs 16%, NBFCs 12%; banks' MFI book fell 40% (Q3 FY26); NCGTC guarantee scheme to June 2026.
[3:13] > Statements (APPSC ASO): over-indebtedness ✓, government subsidies the main source ✗, technology cuts costs ✓ → **1 and 3**.

## SUB 5 | NBFCs – Definition, Regulators, Categories and Scale-Based Regulation
[5:0] # What an NBFC is
[5:1] - A company registered under the **Companies Act** engaged in **loans and advances, acquisition of shares, stocks, bonds, debentures and government securities**, leasing, hire-purchase – banking services **without a banking licence**.
[5:2] - **Principal business (50-50) test:** financial assets > 50% of total assets **and** financial income > 50% of gross income.
[5:3] - **100% FDI** through the automatic route; **FDI is prohibited in Nidhi companies** (and chit funds).
[5:4] - **Liquid debt mutual funds** = main source of short-term funds for NBFCs.
[5:5] # NBFC vs bank
[5:6] TABLE head: Basis | Bank | NBFC
  [5:6.0] Demand deposits | Yes | No (deposit-taking NBFCs take term deposits only)
  [5:6.1] Cheques drawn on itself | Yes | Cannot issue
  [5:6.2] Payment and settlement system | Part of it | Not part
  [5:6.3] Deposit insurance (DICGC) | Covered | Not covered
  [5:6.4] CRR / SLR | Both | No CRR; deposit-taking NBFCs keep liquid assets
  [5:6.5] RBI LAF (repo) access | Yes | No
[5:7] - **Not all NBFCs register with the RBI:** venture capital, merchant banking, stock broking → **SEBI**; insurance → **IRDAI**; HFCs → NHB (supervision; regulation now RBI); **Nidhi** → **Ministry of Corporate Affairs**; **chit funds → State Governments**.
[5:8] - **UPSC 2010:** "cannot acquire government securities" ✗; "cannot accept demand deposits" ✓ → **B only**. **UPSC 2026:** no demand deposits ✓, all must register with RBI ✗, part of payment system / issue cheques ✗, no DICGC cover ✓ → **1 and 4**.
[5:9] - **Payments banks cannot lend**; 11 Small Finance Banks (differentiated banks, Nachiket Mor Committee 2013).
[5:10] # Categories and capital
[5:11] - Categories: **AFC, Loan Company, Investment Company, IFC, NBFC-MFI, CIC, HFC, IDF, NBFC-P2P** – "Regional Rural Banking Company" is NOT one.
[5:12] - **NBFC-P2P (2017):** RBI-regulated online platforms for **peer-to-peer lending** – **individuals lend to individuals through online services**.
[5:13] TABLE head: Category | Minimum Net Owned Fund
  [5:13.0] NBFC-MFI | ₹10 crore (new from 1 Oct 2022; existing ₹7 crore by March 2025, ₹10 crore by March 2027)
  [5:13.1] NBFC-ICC | ₹10 crore (raised from ₹2 crore; glide path to March 2027)
  [5:13.2] HFC | ₹20 crore
[5:14] # Scale-Based Regulation (Oct 2021)
[5:15] TABLE head: Layer | Who / what applies
  [5:15.0] Base | Smaller non-deposit NBFCs
  [5:15.1] Middle | All deposit-taking NBFCs; HFCs, IFCs, CICs

## SUB 7 | Organised vs Unorganised Financial System
[7:0] TABLE head: Organised (RBI-regulated) | Unorganised
  [7:0.0] Commercial banks, cooperative banks, foreign banks, DFHI, provident funds, STCI, mutual funds | Indigenous bankers, moneylenders, unregulated finance/loan companies, chit funds, nidhis
  [7:0.1] Instruments: call money, Treasury bills, commercial bills, certificates of deposit, CPs, repo, MMMFs | Serves farmers, artisans, small traders
[7:1] - **Finance companies are NOT part of the organised money market** (APPSC G2 2017); commercial banks, DFHI and cooperative banks are inside it.
[7:2] - **Unregulated non-bank intermediaries:** indigenous bankers, moneylenders, loan companies, chit funds – **commercial and trade bills** are organised money-market **instruments**, not intermediaries.
[7:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [7:3.0] Indigenous banker | Accepts deposits and lends, combining banking with trade or commission business; deals in hundis (Shroffs, Seths, Mahajans, Multanis, Chettiars)
  [7:3.1] Moneylenders | Professional or agricultural; covered by state money-lending laws
  [7:3.2] Chit funds | Regulated by the State Government (Chit Funds Act 1982)
  [7:3.3] Shadow banking | Bank-like credit intermediation outside full bank regulation; term coined by Paul McCulley, 2007
  [7:3.4] Regulatory arbitrage | Using gaps in regulatory treatment to work outside the stricter banking framework
[7:4] - One who "**lends only in kind**" is **NOT** an indigenous banker.
