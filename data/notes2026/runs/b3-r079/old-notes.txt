=== NOTES 3:79 | Structure of the Indian Financial System – Banks, Nbfcs, Dfis, Cooperatives
## SUB 0 | Organised vs Unorganised Financial System
[79:0:0] # Components of Each
[79:0:1] TABLE head: Organised (RBI-regulated) | Unorganised
  [79:0:1.0] Commercial banks, cooperative banks, foreign banks, DFHI, provident funds, STCI, mutual funds | Indigenous bankers, moneylenders, unregulated finance/loan companies, chit funds, nidhis
  [79:0:1.1] Instruments: call money, Treasury bills, commercial bills, certificates of deposit, CPs, repo, MMMFs | Serves farmers, artisans, small traders
[79:0:2] APPSC Group 2 (2017) key: **finance companies are NOT part of the organised money market**; commercial banks, DFHI and cooperative banks are inside it.
[79:0:3] # Unregulated Non-bank Intermediaries – Terms
[79:0:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [79:0:4.0] Indigenous banker | Accepts deposits and lends, combining banking with trade or commission business; deals in hundis (Shroffs, Seths, Mahajans, Multanis, Chettiars)
  [79:0:4.1] Moneylenders | Professional or agricultural; covered by state money-lending laws
  [79:0:4.2] Chit funds | Regulated by the State Government (Chit Funds Act, 1982)
  [79:0:4.3] Shadow banking | Bank-like credit intermediation outside full bank regulation (NBFCs, money-market/liquid funds); seen as a cause of the global financial crisis; term coined by Paul McCulley (US economist), 2007
  [79:0:4.4] Regulatory arbitrage | Using gaps in regulatory treatment to work outside the stricter banking framework
[79:0:5] One who "**lends only in kind**" is NOT an indigenous banker.
[79:0:6] - **Liquid debt mutual funds** = main short-term funding source for NBFCs.
[79:0:7] - 2010 PYQ: the institution whose importance has **fallen** most = **pension funds** (superannuation funds; retirement income).
## SUB 1 | Development Finance Institutions
[79:1:0] # DFIs – Year and Sector
[79:1:1] TABLE head: Institution | Established | Sector / role
  [79:1:1.0] IFCI | 1 July 1948 (special Act) | First DFI, first all-India term-lending institution; long-term industrial finance; now a public sector NBFC-ND-SI managing PLI projects
  [79:1:1.1] ICICI | January 1955 | Industrial credit; World Bank initiative; private sector; later universal bank; first moving ATM, Mumbai, 12 Dec 2002
  [79:1:1.2] IDBI | July 1964 (under RBI; autonomous 1976) | Apex industrial finance (medium and large industries); now IDBI Bank, private after LIC took majority control (2019)
  [79:1:1.3] EXIM Bank | January 1982 | Export-import finance; Lines of Credit abroad; HQ Mumbai
  [79:1:1.4] NABARD | 12 July 1982 (Act 1981) | Apex agricultural and rural credit; on B. Sivaraman Committee (CRAFICARD) advice
  [79:1:1.5] NHB | 9 July 1988 (NHB Act 1987) | Apex housing finance; C. Rangarajan group
  [79:1:1.6] SIDBI | 2 April 1990 (Act 1989) | MSME finance; began as IDBI subsidiary; regulates State Financial Corporations
  [79:1:1.7] NaBFID | 2021 (NaBFID Act 2021) | Infrastructure; capital ₹20,000 crore + ₹5,000 crore grant; HQ Mumbai
[79:1:2] - IFCI's setting up began the **era of development financing** (APPSC 2018).
[79:1:3] - IFCI, ICICI and IDBI all gave **medium and long-term industrial finance**.
[79:1:4] - **MUDRA Bank**: development and refinance for loans to micro units.
[79:1:5] - IDBI Bank: Government + LIC selling a combined **60.72%** stake to a private buyer.
[79:1:6] 1948 IFCI → 1955 ICICI → 1964 IDBI and UTI → 1982 EXIM, NABARD → 1988 NHB → 1990 SIDBI → 2021 NaBFID
[79:1:7] NHB **Act 1987**, NHB **set up 1988**. 1964 = **both** IDBI and UTI.
[79:1:8] - **Why DFIs are needed:** banks take mostly short-term deposits, so lending long-term causes an asset-liability mismatch; DFIs fill this long-term finance gap.

## SUB 5 | Nbfcs – Categories and Regulation
[79:5:0] # Registration and Categories
[79:5:1] - Registered under the **Companies Act**; regulated by RBI.
[79:5:2] - **Principal business (50-50) test**: financial assets > 50% of total assets **and** financial income > 50% of gross income.
[79:5:3] - Categories: **AFC, Loan Company, Investment Company, IFC, NBFC-MFI, CIC, HFC, IDF**. "Regional Rural Banking Company" is NOT one.
[79:5:4] - **NBFC-P2P (2017):** RBI-regulated online platforms for **peer-to-peer lending**, where individuals lend directly to borrowers.
[79:5:5] TABLE head: Category | Minimum Net Owned Fund
  [79:5:5.0] NBFC-MFI | ₹10 crore (APPSC 2023 key, March 2022)
  [79:5:5.1] NBFC-ICC | ₹10 crore (raised from ₹2 crore; glide path to March 2027)
  [79:5:5.2] HFC | ₹20 crore
[79:5:6] - **NBFC-MFI**: non-deposit-taking; at least **75%** of assets in microfinance loans.
[79:5:7] - Microfinance loan (RBI, March 2022): **collateral-free**; household annual income up to **₹3 lakh**; repayment ≤50% of income.
[79:5:8] # Scale-Based Regulation (SBR)
[79:5:9] TABLE head: Layer | Who / what applies
  [79:5:9.0] Base | Smaller non-deposit NBFCs
  [79:5:9.1] Middle | All deposit-taking NBFCs; HFCs, IFCs, CICs
  [79:5:9.2] Upper | Identified by RBI; bank-like norms for at least 5 years; listing within 3 years
  [79:5:9.3] Top | Kept empty; only for a sharp rise in systemic risk
[79:5:10] - NBFC-UL list 2026-27: **17** companies incl. **Tata Sons** (15 in April 2026); RBI rejected Tata Sons' bid to surrender its licence and asked it to list (13 Sep 2026).
[79:5:11] - Proposed: single **₹1 lakh crore** asset test for UL in place of parametric scoring.
[79:5:12] # NBFC Ombudsman
[79:5:13] - **NBFC Ombudsman Scheme 2018** → merged into **RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2021** (now also covers state and central cooperative banks).

## SUB 9 | Microfinance and Stress Episodes
[79:9:0] # AP Crisis to Regulation
[79:9:1] - 1. **AP crisis 2010**: multiple lending, over-indebtedness, coercive recovery, borrower suicides.
[79:9:2] - 2. **AP MFI Ordinance, Oct 2010** (Act 2011): MFIs register with **district authorities**; limited doorstep recovery; capped interest; AP MFI loan book collapsed.
[79:9:3] - 3. **Malegam Committee** (RBI sub-committee, **15 October 2010**, Y.H. Malegam): created **NBFC-MFI** category (2011); margin and interest caps; no more than **2 MFIs per borrower**.
[79:9:4] - 4. **MFIN** (2009, association of NBFC-MFIs) and **Sa-Dhan**: RBI-recognised **SROs (2014)**.
[79:9:5] - Borrowers: **95% women**, 80% rural; 627 lakh (FY25). Share (Mar 2025): **NBFC-MFIs 39%**, banks 32%, SFBs 16%, NBFCs 12%.
[79:9:6] - Banks' MFI portfolio fell 40% to ₹65,687 crore (Q3 FY26); NCGTC guarantee scheme for MFIs to June 2026.
