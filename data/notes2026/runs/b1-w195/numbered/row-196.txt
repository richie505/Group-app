## SUB 0 | C.P. Brown and the Colonial Scholars of Telugu
[0:0] # Charles Philip Brown (1798-1884)
[0:1] - **Post:** British revenue officer; **Collector of Cuddapah (Kadapa)**; learnt Telugu from **Kodandarama Pantulu**.
[0:2] - **Vemana:** **C.P. Brown collected the poems of Vemana** (about 2,000) and published **Verses of Vemana (1829)**; named Vemana's metre **Ataveladi**.
[0:3] - **Dictionaries:** **C.P. Brown** compiled the **first Telugu-English and English-Telugu dictionaries (1852-53)** – not Munro, Campbell or Cotton; his Telugu Grammar came in **1840**.
[0:4] - **Classics:** edited **Nannaya, Tikkana and Pothana**; preserved Mackenzie's **Kaifiyats** in the Madras Library.
[0:5] - **"Italian of the East":** **C.P. Brown called Telugu the "Italian of the East"** (exam answer); the phrase is first traced to the 15th-century traveller **Nicolo de Conti**.
[0:6] - **C.P. Brown Memorial Trust Library = Kadapa (Cuddapah)**, built on the site of Brown's old bungalow ("Brown's College") – not Rajahmundry, Kakinada or Anantapur (APPSC G2 2001, 2005 and AP History ×2).
[0:7] # Other scholars
[0:8] TABLE head: Scholar | Contribution
  [0:8.0] Benjamin Schultze | German Lutheran, Danish-Halle Mission; came 1719; began the first Telugu Bible translation (1727); Telugu Bible and books printed at Halle, Germany (1746-47)
  [0:8.1] Colin Mackenzie (1754-1821) | Reached the Amaravati Stupa in 1797; first Surveyor General (1815); collected the Kaifiyats with the Kavali brothers (Borraiah, Venkataramaiah, Lakshmaiah)
  [0:8.2] William Carey | Serampore Mission; first printed Telugu Bible (NT 1812, full 1818-19); Telugu typefaces
  [0:8.3] A.D. Campbell | First systematic Telugu grammar (1816)
  [0:8.4] Thomas Munro | Kept Telugu as an administrative language
[0:9] # Gentoo
[0:10] - **Gentoo:** European name for the **Telugu** people and language (Madras Presidency), most likely from Portuguese **gentio** ("gentile, heathen") – so "a term of the Portuguese" (APPSC G2 2019 key). William Brown's **Grammar of the Gentoo Language (1817)** used the same word for Telugu.
[0:11] > Recall angle: library → Kadapa; Vemana and the dictionary → C.P. Brown; first systematic grammar → Campbell; Kaifiyats → Mackenzie.

  [1:0.5] Musalamma Maranamu (printed 1900) | Kattamanchi Ramalinga (C.R.) Reddy | A woman gives her life to save the village from a reservoir breach
  [1:0.6] Kavitva Vicharamu (1914) | C.R. Reddy | Start of modern Telugu literary criticism
  [1:0.7] Kanyasulkam | Gurajada Apparao | Bride-price; first staged 1893
  [1:0.8] Gabbilam (1941) | Gurram Jashuva | A bat carries a Dalit's prayer to God

[1:2] - **Veeresalingam** first rendered Kalidasa's **Abhijnana Shakuntalam** and **Malavikagnimitram** into Telugu.

## SUB 3 | Literary Associations and Cultural Bodies
[3:0] - **Andhra Sahitya Parishat:** founded **1911 at Madras**, later at **Kakinada**; sessions at **Rajahmundry** (1912, 1913) presided over by **Kokkonda Venkataratnam**; since **1973** the **Andhra Sahitya Parishat Government Museum and Research Institute, Kakinada**.
[3:1] - **Sri Krishnadevaraya Andhra Bhasha Nilayam** (Hyderabad, **1901**): Komarraju Lakshmana Rao, Ravichettu Ranga Rao, Nayani Venkata Ranga Rao.
[3:2] - **Vijnana Chandrika Mandali:** publishing body; first editor **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao**, later Komarraju Lakshmana Rao. **Komarraju Lakshmana Rao** also published the Telugu encyclopaedia **Andhra Vijnana Sarvasvam**.
[3:3] - **Young Men's Association, Rajahmundry:** active in **Bipin Chandra Pal's Rajahmundry visit** (1907). **Young Men's Literary Association** (Guntur): **Jonnavittula Gurunatham**.
[3:4] - **Rajahmundry** – Veeresalingam's base, a cultural capital since **Eastern Chalukya** times.
[3:5] - **Kokkonda Venkataratnam** – orthodox scholar, "Andhra Bhasha Johnson"; **Veeresalingam** – Gadya Tikkana, father of the Telugu renaissance.
[3:6] - **Telugu Language Day – 29 August**, birth anniversary of **Gidugu Ramamurthy**; Telugu a **classical language** since **2008**.
[3:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Modern India L2 Part 3 p.9-12; app notes 1:196 (sub 5, 6, 7)
