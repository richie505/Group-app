## SUB 0 | Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar and Widow Remarriage
[0:0] # Vidyasagar (1820-1891)
[0:1] - **Widow remarriage:** the reformer who worked successfully to **legalise widow remarriage**; he **cited the ancient (Vedic) texts** (Parashara) to show widows could remarry → **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (Act XV of 1856)**, drafted under **Dalhousie**, **passed under Lord Canning** (July 1856).
[0:2] - **First lawful Hindu widow remarriage:** **Calcutta, 7 December 1856**, under his supervision.
[0:3] - **Women's education:** in 1850 Bethune made him **Secretary of the Hindu Female School**, later the **Bethune School** (founded **1849** by **J.E.D. Bethune**, with Dakshinaranjan Mukherjee); he ran about **35 girls' schools**.
[0:4] - **Principal of the Sanskrit College, Calcutta** (1851) – opened it to non-Brahmins; title "**Vidyasagar**" (Ocean of Learning) for his Sanskrit learning.
[0:5] - **Books:** **Bahubivah (Bahu Bibaho)** against polygamy; Bidhobabivah (widow remarriage); **Barnaparichay** (Bengali primer); his prose shaped modern **Bengali**.
[0:6] - **Somprakash (Som / Shome Prakash), 1858:** Bengali weekly **started on Vidyasagar's plan**, edited by Dwarkanath Vidyabhushan – keys answer **Vidyasagar**.
[0:7] - **Quote:** "I have no time to think about God; a lot of work has to be done on this earth" – **Vidyasagar**.
[0:8] - **Not his:** **Sambad Kaumudi** (1821) and **Mirat-ul-Akhbar** (1822) were **Rammohan Roy**'s; the 1829 sati ban was Roy's campaign under **Bentinck** (not Keshab Chandra Sen).
[0:9] - Andhra link: he sent support for **Veeresalingam's** third widow remarriage.

## SUB 1 | Social-Reform Acts – Year, Governor-General, Provision
[1:0] TABLE head: Year | Act | Governor-General | Provision
  [1:0.0] 1795, 1804 | Bengal Regulations XXI and III | – | Infanticide declared murder
  [1:0.1] 1802 | Anti-infanticide regulation | Wellesley | Child sacrifice at Sagar Island banned
  [1:0.2] 1829 | Sati Regulation (XVII) | William Bentinck | Sati illegal; abetment = culpable homicide
  [1:0.3] 1843 | Act V of 1843 | Ellenborough | Slavery illegal (Charter Act 1833 had directed abolition; IPC 1860 made slave trade an offence)
  [1:0.4] 1850 | Caste Disabilities Removal Act (Lex Loci, Act XXI) | Dalhousie | A convert to Christianity could still inherit ancestral property
  [1:0.5] 1856 | Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (Act XV) | Drafted Dalhousie, passed Canning | Widow remarriage legal; children legitimate
  [1:0.6] 1870 | Female Infanticide Prevention Act | Mayo | Compulsory registration of births; watch on female infants
  [1:0.7] 1872 | Native (Civil / Brahmo) Marriage Act – Act III of 1872 | – | A social-reform Act: girls 14, boys 18; inter-caste allowed; polygamy barred; urged by Keshab Chandra Sen
  [1:0.8] 1891 | Age of Consent Act | Lansdowne | Consent (consummation) for girls raised from 10 to 12
  [1:0.9] 1929 | Child Marriage Restraint Act (Sarda Act) | Irwin | Prevention of child marriage: girls 14, boys 18; moved by Har Bilas Sarda; in force 1 April 1930
[1:1] - **Order:** Caste Disabilities (1850) → Widows' Remarriage (1856) → Age of Consent (1891) → Child Marriage Restraint (1929).
[1:2] - **Ages ladder:** consent 10 (IPC 1860) → **12 (1891)** → 14 (1925) → 16 (1949) → 18 (POCSO 2012 / BNS); marriage 14/18 (1872, 1929) → **18/21** (1978 amendment; **Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006**). The 2021 proposal to raise women's age to 21 is not law (as of 2026).
[1:3] - **Thuggee:** suppressed by **Colonel William Sleeman** (1830s).
[1:4] - Keshab's 1878 marriage of his minor daughter to the **Cooch Behar** prince broke the 1872 Act and split the Brahmo Samaj.
[1:5] > Trap: Female Infanticide Act = **1870 (Mayo)**, not 1856; 1872 and 1929 = **14/18**, 1891 = **12**.

## SUB 2 | Age of Consent Act 1891 – Malabari, Tilak, Rakhmabai
[2:0] - **Behramji M. Malabari** (1853-1912): **Parsi** from Western India (born Baroda), "**greatest Parsi social reformer of the 19th century**"; editor of **Indian Spectator** and **Voice of India**; **Notes on Infant Marriage and Enforced Widowhood (1884)**; **chief crusader for the Age of Consent Act**.
[2:1] - **Tilak opposed** the Bill (Kesari) as foreign interference in Hindu custom – so "Tilak supported the Bill" is the wrong statement; the Moderates (Ranade, Telang, Bhandarkar) supported it.
[2:2] - **Rakhmabai case (1884-88):** married as a child, she refused to live with husband Dadaji; he sued for **restitution of conjugal rights** – the case raised **age of consent** and conjugal rights, **not women's education**; she became one of India's first women doctors.
[2:3] - **Phulmoni Dasi** (1889-90): a child bride's death that pushed the 1891 Act.
[2:4] - The 1891 Act made intercourse with a girl **below 12** an offence, married or not.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 3 p.185, p.196, p.198; Spectrum p.229, p.255; app notes 1:197 (sub 3)

## SUB 5 | Andhra – Veeresalingam and Women's Reform
[5:0] - **Kandukuri Veeresalingam** performed the **first widow remarriage in Andhra – 11 December 1881**, Rajahmundry (bride **Sitamma/Gauramma**, groom **Gogulapati Sriramulu**; aid from Pyda Ramakrishnaiah); second on 15 Dec 1881.
[5:1] - **Girls' schools:** **Dowlaiswaram, 1874** (first in Andhra); **Innispeta, Rajahmundry, 1881**; co-education.
[5:2] - **Widow Remarriage Association, Rajahmundry (1880)**; Madras widow home (**1897**); Hitakarini Samajam (**1905**).
[5:3] - Journal for women **Satihita Bodhini (1883)**.
[5:4] - Title **"Vidyasagar of South India"** from **M.G. Ranade**.
[5:5] TABLE head: Person | Contribution
  [5:5.0] Kandukuri Rajyalakshmamma | Veeresalingam's wife; hosted widow and inter-caste marriages; sheltered widows

  [5:5.2] Unnava Lakshmibayamma | Widow asylum, Guntur (1902) → Sarada Niketan; monthly Sri Sarada Niketanam
  [5:5.3] Kotikalapudi Seethamma | Ahalya Bai; Sadhu Rakshana Shatakam
