## SUB 1 | Widow Remarriage, Girls' Schools and Reform Bodies
[195:1:0] # Milestones
[195:1:1] TABLE head: Year | Step
  [195:1:1.0] 1874 | first girls' school in Andhra – Dowlaiswaram (Dhavaleswaram), near Rajahmundry; first principal Malladi Achchanna Sastri
  [195:1:1.1] 1878 | Rajahmundry Social Reform Association – first organised reform body in Andhra; for widow remarriage, against nautch girls
  [195:1:1.2] 3 Aug 1879 | lecture at Rajahmundry: widow remarriage valid by Hindu scripture
  [195:1:1.3] 1880 | Widow Remarriage Association (Stree Punarvivaha Sangham), with Challapalli Bapayya and Basavaraju Gavarraju
  [195:1:1.4] 11 Dec 1881 | first widow remarriage in Andhra, Rajahmundry
  [195:1:1.5] 15 Dec 1881 | second remarriage – Ratnamma and Racharla Ramachandraiah
  [195:1:1.6] 1881 | second girls' school, Innispeta, Rajahmundry
  [195:1:1.7] 1897 / 16 Jan 1905 | widow homes at Madras / Rajahmundry (Vitantu Saranalayam)
  [195:1:1.8] 1906 | Hitakarini Samajam, Rajahmundry – he gave it all his property; Veeresalingam its founder president
[195:1:2] - **First widow remarriage:** **11 December 1881**, at the **Ananda Udyana Mandapam** behind his house; groom **Gogulapati Sriramulu**, bride **Seethamma (Gauramma)**; money from **Paidah (Pyda) Ramakrishnayya**, Kakinada.
[195:1:3] - By 1892 he had held about **20** widow remarriages.
[195:1:4] - Bengal comparison: **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act 1856** (Act XV, **Lord Canning**); first Bengal remarriage **7 Dec 1856** (Vidyasagar).
[195:1:5] - **Hitakarini date:** **1906**, registered **2 May 1908** (Hans India); ⚠ NextGen gives 1905.
[195:1:6] - **Rajahmundry Social Reform Association:** founded **1878** (RAS 2021 stem says 1871 – still Veeresalingam); main aim = **widow remarriages**.
[195:1:7] - **Kakinada:** Paidah Ramakrishnayya, as municipal president, funded remarriages and built houses for the grooms.
[195:1:8] - **Opponents:** **Kokkonda Venkataratnam** (Andhra Bhasha Sanjeevini); **Vedam Venkataraya Sastri** – **Stree Punarvivaha Durvada Nirvaranam (1883)**, first major anti-remarriage book; **Kasibhatta Brahmayya Sastri** – **Aryananda Brundavani Sabha**, Kakinada; A.P. Parthasarathi Naidu also opposed him – **Uma Ranganayakulu** was his ally (general knowledge, not checked for Parthasarathi Naidu).
[195:1:9] - **Mrs Manning** (UK) willed **£50** to his widow home.
[195:1:10] - Also first in Andhra: a **night school**, a **Theosophical High School**, **co-education**.
[195:1:11] - **Evils fought:** ban on widow remarriage, child marriage, **dowry, bride-price**, caste, superstition, **nautch/Bhogam**, **official corruption**; scientific education was an aim, not an evil.
[195:1:12] - **Methods:** journals and satire, lectures, schools, remarriages at his own cost, widow homes, prayer without idols – the **press, not direct politics**.
[195:1:13] > 1874 girls' school (Dowlaiswaram) → 1878 Social Reform Association → 1880 Widow Remarriage Association → 11 Dec 1881 first remarriage → 1906 Hitakarini.
[195:1:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History Part 21 p.26-27; NextGen Modern India L2 Part 3 p.6-7; Rocket AP History p.44; thehansindia.com (Hitakarini Samajam, 1906, regd 2 May 1908); old app notes row 195
[195:1:15] - **Social laws:** Sati Regulation 1829 (Bentinck); Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act 1856 (Canning); Native Marriage Act 1872; Age of Consent Act 1891 (Lansdowne).
## SUB 2 | Kandukuri Veeresalingam – Life and Titles
[195:2:0] # Titles
[195:2:1] TABLE head: Title | Note
  [195:2:1.0] Father of the Andhra (Telugu) Renaissance; Father of the Social Reform Movement in Andhra | most asked
  [195:2:1.1] Vidyasagar of South India (Iswarachandra Vidyasagar of the South) | given by M.G. Ranade, 1898, when Veeresalingam presided over the Indian Social Reform Congress, Madras
  [195:2:1.2] Rao Bahadur | British government, 1893
  [195:2:1.3] Gadya Tikkana ('Tikkana of prose'), Gadya Vajmaya Brahma, Yuga Karta, Andhra Vaitalikudu | –
  [195:2:1.4] Raja Rammohan Roy of Andhra (Andhra Rammohan Roy) | –
[195:2:2] - **Ranade**, not Tilak, Naoroji, Tagore, Keshab, Debendranath, D.K. Karve, N.G. Chandavarkar, Vedam Venkataraya Sastri or Mrs Manning, gave the Vidyasagar title.
[195:2:3] - **Not his titles:** Andhra Ratna (Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya), Andhra Kesari (Prakasam), Andhra Bhishma (Nyapathi Subba Rao).
[195:2:4] # Life
[195:2:5] - **Born 16 April 1848, Rajahmundry** (Subbarayudu and Purnamma); father died when he was four; first guru Atmuri Lakshmi Narasimham.
[195:2:6] - Wife **Rajyalakshmamma (Bapamma)**, married 1860.
[195:2:7] - Matriculation **1869** → first teacher's job at **Korangi** (headmaster, 1872) → Telugu pandit, Rajahmundry → **Telugu Pandit, Presidency College, Madras (1899-1904)**.
[195:2:8] - **Died 27 May 1919, Madras** (Vedanivas).
[195:2:9] - **Thought (NDA 2017):** believed in **universal education** (girls' schools, night school, co-education), science and morality, cultural nationalism.
[195:2:10] - Fought superstition – joined Korangi school on a New Moon day; gave up the sacred thread (1906); held an inter-caste marriage.
[195:2:11] > Born 1848, died 27 May 1919 Madras; Rao Bahadur 1893; Ranade's "Vidyasagar of South India" 1898.

## SUB 3 | Veeresalingam's Literary Works – the First Telugu Novel
[195:3:0] TABLE head: Work | Fact
  [195:3:0.0] Rajasekhara Charitra (1878) | first Telugu novel; based on Goldsmith's The Vicar of Wakefield
  [195:3:0.1] Satyaraju Purvadesayatralu | based on Swift's Gulliver's Travels
  [195:3:0.2] Brahma Vivaham | satire on the opponents of reform
  [195:3:0.3] Satyavati Charitra, Victoria Maharani Charitra, Esuchristu Charitra | prose works

## SUB 4 | Brahmo and Prarthana Samaj in Andhra
[195:4:0] - **First Prarthana (Brahmo) Samaj in Andhra:** **Rajahmundry, 1878**, by **Kandukuri Veeresalingam** – not Sivanatha Sastri, Venkataratnam Naidu or Mokkapati Subbarayudu.
[195:4:1] - Founded **under the influence of Mannava Butchaiah Pantulu** – the **first Andhra Brahmo**, editor of the **Hindu Reformer**.
[195:4:2] - **Founder of the Brahmo Samaj in (united) Andhra = Veeresalingam**; **R. Venkataratnam Naidu spread it**.
[195:4:3] - **Brahmo Trinity (Brahmatraya):** **Veeresalingam, Raghupathi Venkataratnam Naidu, Desiraju Peda Bapayya** – not Chilakamarthi or Mannava.
[195:4:4] - **Desiraju Peda Bapayya (Bapatla):** first practising (**Anushthana**) Brahmo; helped Veeresalingam become an Anushthana Brahmo (giving up the sacred thread); paper **Voice of Truth**; **Young Men's Prayer Union**, **Temperance and Social Purity Association**, Rajahmundry.
[195:4:5] - **Chembeti Sridharalu Naidu** (Madras): renamed the Veda Samaj the **Dakshina Bharata Brahmo Samaj**; **translated Brahmo books into Tamil and Telugu**.
[195:4:6] - **Centres:** Rajahmundry (Veeresalingam), **Kakinada** (Venkataratnam's Brahma Mandir), **Pithapuram** (Raja Surya Rao, patron) – "all are correct".
[195:4:7] TABLE head: Year | Samaj | Founder
  [195:4:7.0] 1878 | Prarthana Samaj, Rajahmundry | Veeresalingam
  [195:4:7.1] 1882 | Brahmo Samaj, Machilipatnam | R. Venkataratnam Naidu
  [195:4:7.2] 1887 | Brahmo Samaj (Brahmo Mandir), Rajahmundry | –
  [195:4:7.3] 1888 | Kakinada Samaj | Tayi Surya Prakasarao Naidu
  [195:4:7.4] 1898 | Guntur Samaj | Pillarisetty Sitaramayya
[195:4:8] - **K. Seethamma** organised its women's wing.
[195:4:9] - Prarthana Samaj did **not** reject idol worship or caste outright; the Brahmo Samaj did.
[195:4:10] - **Pithapuram Raja** gave **Rs 1 lakh** to the Brahma Dharma Prachara Nidhi – to train preachers and translate Bengali Brahmo books.
[195:4:11] - Decline: splits after Venkataratnam Naidu joined the **Justice Party**.
[195:4:12] > 1878 Rajahmundry Prarthana Samaj = Veeresalingam (influence: Mannava Butchaiah); Trinity = Veeresalingam, Venkataratnam Naidu, Peda Bapayya.
[195:4:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Modern India L2 Part 3 p.2, p.6, p.8; NextGen AP History Part 6 p.8; Rocket AP History p.22, p.33; PMF Modern History Part 3 p.188; old app notes row 195
[195:4:14] - **Effect of the Brahmo movement in Andhra:** spread **monotheistic ideas** and was the **first to start constructive work for the depressed classes**.
## SUB 5 | Raghupathi Venkataratnam Naidu – Social Purity and Anti-Nautch
[195:5:0] - **Titles:** **Brahmarshi** (1932), Kulapati, Abhinava Socrates, **Knighthood (1924)** – first non-official educator knighted; the Telugu "Uttama Purusha".
[195:5:1] - **Papers:** **Brahma Prakasika**, Fellow Worker, People's Friend.
[195:5:2] - **Social Purity Association (Samajika Shuddhi Samajam):** **1891**, Bandar (Machilipatnam) – against **Bhogam melas** (nautch).
[195:5:3] - **Bhogam (devadasi) caste** disappeared from Andhra society mainly through **his** efforts – not Veeresalingam, Gurajada or Unnava.
[195:5:4] - **Career:** Noble College lecturer; principal, **Mahbub College, Secunderabad** (Hyderabad) and **Pithapuram Raja's College, Kakinada (1905-18)**; **Vice-Chancellor, Madras University (1919-22)** – first Telugu VC.
[195:5:5] - **Brahmo work:** inspired by **Pandit Sivanath Sastri**; Brahmo Samaj Machilipatnam (1882); **Brahma Mandir at Kakinada**; condemned idol worship.
[195:5:6] - Kept away from the **national movement**; did not join the Theosophical (Divyajnana) Society.
[195:5:7] > Venkataratnam Naidu = Brahmarshi; Social Purity Association 1891; anti-Bhogam; VC Madras.

## SUB 6 | Gurajada, Gidugu, Tripuraneni and the Language Movement
[195:6:0] # Gurajada Appa Rao (1861-1915)
[195:6:1] - **Kanyasulkam** – social play on **bride-price** and child marriage, in spoken (vyavaharika) Telugu, **Srikakulam dialect**; written 1890, **first staged 13 Aug 1892** (Jagannatha Vilasa Nataka Sangham, Vizianagaram; NextGen: 1893), **published 1897** (Vavilla), 2nd edition 1909 dedicated to **Ananda Gajapati**.
[195:6:2] - Characters: **Madhuravani** – reformed courtesan; **Girisham** – educated fraud; Ramappa Pantulu – hypocrite; Buchamma – widow; **Soujanya Rao** – reformer.
[195:6:3] - Kanyasulkam is NOT by Kommaraju Lakshmana Rao, Veeresalingam or Panuganti.
[195:6:4] - Other works: **Mutyala Saralu** (1910, new metre), **Diddubatu** (first modern Telugu short story), **Purnamma** (girl married off for money), Kanyaka; Saranga Dhara (1883) – English poem with brother Shyamala Rao.
[195:6:5] - Song **Desamunu Preminchumanna** – "Desamante matti kadoyi, desamante manushuloyi" (a country is its people, not its soil).
[195:6:6] - Patron court: **Vizianagaram** (Ananda Gajapati); titles Navayuga Vaitalikudu.
[195:6:7] # Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy (1863-1940)
[195:6:8] - Born **29 Aug 1863, Ponduru (Srikakulam)**; Telugu teacher at **Parlakimidi**; **Telugu Language Day = 29 August** (his birthday).
[195:6:9] - **Father of the Vyavaharika (spoken Telugu) movement**, against the Sanskritised **granthika** style; title Vyavaharika Bhashodyama Pitamaha; **Kaisar-e-Hind (1934)**.
[195:6:10] - **Savara:** script, Savara-Telugu dictionaries, **Savara grammar/manual in English (1931)**, Savara-English dictionary (1936).
[195:6:11] - Works: **A Memorandum on Modern Telugu**, Bala Kavi Saranyam; monthly **Telugu (1919)**.
[195:6:12] - **Vartamana Vyavaharikandhra Bhasha Parivartaka Samajam:** **founded by Veeresalingam** (president), **Gidugu secretary** – not founded by Gidugu.
[195:6:13] - Grammars: Andhra Bhashanusasanam – Malladi Suryanarayana Sastri; Vyavaharika Bhasha Vyakaranam – Vadlamudi Gopalakrishnayya; Aadhunikapramanikandhra Vyakaranam – Vajjala Venkateswarlu.
[195:6:14] - Vyavaharika Bhashodyama Samstha (1910, after a Visakhapatnam meeting); inspired by **J.A. Yates**.
[195:6:15] TABLE head: Side | Leaders
  [195:6:15.0] Granthika (classical) | Kokkonda Venkataratnam, Jayanti Ramayya Pantulu (Andhra Sahitya Parishad; A Defence of Literary Telugu, 1913), Vedam Venkataraya Sastri

[195:6:20] - Song **"Veeragandhamu techinaramu"** – Civil Disobedience.

## SUB 7 | Background – Centres, Women's Bodies and Missions
[195:7:0] - **Rajahmundry** = Andhra's first renaissance centre and centre of reform, prose, journalism and the novel; peak **1870-1935**; led by the **English-educated middle class**; other centres Kakinada, Machilipatnam, Bapatla (Peda Bapayya), Guntur; **Kavi Club, Kumudavalli (1897)**.
[195:7:1] - Cradle of the language movement: **North Coastal Andhra** (Vizianagaram, Srikakulam, Parlakimidi).
[195:7:2] - **Atmuri Lakshmi Narasimham** – first guru of the early reform movement.
[195:7:3] - **Ram Mohan Roy School (1909)** – Chilakamarti Lakshmi Narasimham.

## SUB 1 | C.P. Brown and the Colonial Scholars of Telugu
[196:1:0] # Charles Philip Brown (1798-1884)
[196:1:1] - **Post:** British revenue officer; **Collector of Cuddapah (Kadapa)**; learnt Telugu from **Kodandarama Pantulu**.
[196:1:2] - **Vemana:** **C.P. Brown collected the poems of Vemana** (about 2,000) and published **Verses of Vemana (1829)**; named Vemana's metre **Ataveladi**.
[196:1:3] - **Dictionaries:** **C.P. Brown** compiled the **first Telugu-English and English-Telugu dictionaries (1852-53)** – not Munro, Campbell or Cotton; his Telugu Grammar came in **1840**.
[196:1:4] - **Classics:** edited **Nannaya, Tikkana and Pothana**; preserved Mackenzie's **Kaifiyats** in the Madras Library.
[196:1:5] - **"Italian of the East":** **C.P. Brown called Telugu the "Italian of the East"** (exam answer); the phrase is first traced to the 15th-century traveller **Nicolo de Conti**.
[196:1:6] - **C.P. Brown Memorial Trust Library = Kadapa (Cuddapah)**, built on the site of Brown's old bungalow ("Brown's College") – not Rajahmundry, Kakinada or Anantapur (APPSC G2 2001, 2005 and AP History ×2).
[196:1:7] # Other scholars
[196:1:8] TABLE head: Scholar | Contribution
  [196:1:8.0] Benjamin Schultze | German Lutheran, Danish-Halle Mission; came 1719; began the first Telugu Bible translation (1727); Telugu Bible and books printed at Halle, Germany (1746-47)
  [196:1:8.1] Colin Mackenzie (1754-1821) | Reached the Amaravati Stupa in 1797; first Surveyor General (1815); collected the Kaifiyats with the Kavali brothers (Borraiah, Venkataramaiah, Lakshmaiah)
  [196:1:8.2] William Carey | Serampore Mission; first printed Telugu Bible (NT 1812, full 1818-19); Telugu typefaces
  [196:1:8.3] A.D. Campbell | First systematic Telugu grammar (1816)
  [196:1:8.4] Thomas Munro | Kept Telugu as an administrative language
[196:1:9] # Gentoo
[196:1:10] - **Gentoo:** European name for the **Telugu** people and language (Madras Presidency), most likely from Portuguese **gentio** ("gentile, heathen") – so "a term of the Portuguese" (APPSC G2 2019 key). William Brown's **Grammar of the Gentoo Language (1817)** used the same word for Telugu.

## SUB 0 | Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar and Widow Remarriage
[197:0:0] # Vidyasagar (1820-1891)
[197:0:1] - **Widow remarriage:** the reformer who worked successfully to **legalise widow remarriage**; he **cited the ancient (Vedic) texts** (Parashara) to show widows could remarry → **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (Act XV of 1856)**, drafted under **Dalhousie**, **passed under Lord Canning** (July 1856).
[197:0:2] - **First lawful Hindu widow remarriage:** **Calcutta, 7 December 1856**, under his supervision.
[197:0:3] - **Women's education:** in 1850 Bethune made him **Secretary of the Hindu Female School**, later the **Bethune School** (founded **1849** by **J.E.D. Bethune**, with Dakshinaranjan Mukherjee); he ran about **35 girls' schools**.
[197:0:4] - **Principal of the Sanskrit College, Calcutta** (1851) – opened it to non-Brahmins; title "**Vidyasagar**" (Ocean of Learning) for his Sanskrit learning.
[197:0:5] - **Books:** **Bahubivah (Bahu Bibaho)** against polygamy; Bidhobabivah (widow remarriage); **Barnaparichay** (Bengali primer); his prose shaped modern **Bengali**.
[197:0:6] - **Somprakash (Som / Shome Prakash), 1858:** Bengali weekly **started on Vidyasagar's plan**, edited by Dwarkanath Vidyabhushan – keys answer **Vidyasagar**.
[197:0:7] - **Quote:** "I have no time to think about God; a lot of work has to be done on this earth" – **Vidyasagar**.
[197:0:8] - **Not his:** **Sambad Kaumudi** (1821) and **Mirat-ul-Akhbar** (1822) were **Rammohan Roy**'s; the 1829 sati ban was Roy's campaign under **Bentinck** (not Keshab Chandra Sen).
[197:0:9] - Andhra link: he sent support for **Veeresalingam's** third widow remarriage.

## SUB 3 | Age of Consent Act 1891 – Malabari, Tilak, Rakhmabai
[197:3:0] - **Behramji M. [197:3:1] - **Tilak opposed** the Bill (Kesari) as foreign interference in Hindu custom – so "Tilak supported the Bill" is the wrong statement; the Moderates (Ranade, Telang, Bhandarkar) supported it.

[197:3:3] - **Phulmoni Dasi** (1889-90): a child bride's death that pushed the 1891 Act.
[197:3:4] - The 1891 Act made intercourse with a girl **below 12** an offence, married or not.

## SUB 3 | Factors of Nationalism; Lytton and the Ilbert Bill
[198:3:0] # Factors
[198:3:1] - **Political and administrative unification** – railways, telegraph, post, uniform laws; **K.M. Panikkar**: "the most notable achievement of British rule was the **unification of India**".
[198:3:2] - **Western education** – liberal ideas; **English as a link language**; rise of the **educated middle class**.
[198:3:3] - **Press and literature**; **rediscovery of India's past**; socio-religious reform.
[198:3:4] - **Contemporary world movements** – South American, Greek, **Italian** and Irish struggles; **German unification** – **Bismarck**'s "**blood and iron**" speech (1862).
[198:3:5] - **Economic critique** – drain of wealth, ruin of artisans.
[198:3:6] - Early associations trained the educated class in **petitions, memorials and all-India coordination** → INC 1885.
[198:3:7] # Lytton's resented measures (1876-80)
[198:3:8] TABLE head: Measure | Year | Why resented
  [198:3:8.0] ICS maximum age cut | 1876 | 21 → 19 (Lytton's period; ordered by Secretary of State Salisbury)
  [198:3:8.1] Delhi Durbar | 1877 | Proclaimed Queen Victoria Empress of India during famine
  [198:3:8.2] Vernacular Press Act | 1878 | Gagged the Indian-language press; Amrita Bazar Patrika (1868) turned English overnight in 1878
  [198:3:8.3] Arms Act | 1878 | Disarmed Indians but spared Europeans
  [198:3:8.4] Second Afghan War | 1878-80 | Burden on Indian revenues
[198:3:9] # Ilbert Bill (1883)
[198:3:10] - **Ripon**'s government, drafted by **Sir Courtenay Ilbert (Law Member)**: Indian magistrates and judges could **try European British subjects**; European agitation forced a compromise – a European accused could claim a jury of **at least half Europeans**; passed **25 January 1884**. **Not a measure of Lytton.**

[198:3:12] - **Cambridge School** (Anil Seal, John Gallagher): saw nationalism as elite competition for British favours and **denied colonialism as an exploitative economic, political, social and cultural structure**.
