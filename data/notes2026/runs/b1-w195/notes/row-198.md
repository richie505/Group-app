=== Pre-Congress Political Associations – Bengal, Bombay and London
| Association | Year | Founder / leader | Place |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bangabhasha Prakasika Sabha** | **1836** | Associates of **Rammohan Roy** | Calcutta (NextGen: the first organised political association) |
| **Landholders' Society (Zamindari Association)** | **1838** (PMF 1837) | Dwarkanath Tagore, Prasanna Kumar Tagore, **Radhakant Deb** | Calcutta – the **first political organisation** in the keys; landlords' interests |
| British India Society | 1839 | William Adam | **London** |
| **Bengal British India Society** | **1843** | George Thompson, Dwarkanath Tagore | Calcutta |
| **British Indian Association** | **1851** | Merger of the two above; **first president Radhakant Deb**, **secretary Debendranath Tagore** | Calcutta – the **major landlord body of 1851** |
| **Bombay Association** | **1852** | Jagannath Shankar Sheth | Bombay |
| Madras Native Association | 1852 | Gazula Lakshminarasu Chetty | Madras |
| **East India Association** | **1866** | **Dadabhai Naoroji** | **London** |
| **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** | **1870** (began 1867) | **M.G. Ranade, G.V. Joshi** ("Sarvajanik Kaka"), **S.H. Chiplunkar** | Poona – **not V.B. Phadke** |
| **Indian League** | **1875** | **Sisir Kumar Ghosh** | Calcutta |
| **Indian Association** (Indian National Association) | **26 July 1876** | **Surendranath Banerjee** and **Ananda Mohan Bose** | Calcutta – the most important pre-Congress body |
| **Madras Mahajana Sabha** | **1884** | M. Viraraghavachariar, G. Subramania Iyer, P. Anandacharlu | Madras |
| Bombay Presidency Association | 1885 | Pherozeshah Mehta, K.T. Telang, Badruddin Tyabji | Bombay |
- **All the above were pre-Congress bodies** (INC 1885) – the Indian Association (1876) included.
- **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** sent the **1875 petition to the House of Commons** demanding India's **direct representation in the British Parliament**.
- **Debendranath Tagore** was a founder (secretary) of the **British Indian Association**; he reorganised, but did not found, the Brahmo Samaj (Rammohan Roy, 1828).
- **G.V. Joshi at the Delhi Durbar (1877):** asked the Queen to give Indians the **same political and social status as British subjects**.
- **Indian Association:** fought the ICS age cut (21 → 19), the Arms and Vernacular Press Acts; held the all-India conference of **Dec 1883**.
- **Chronology codes:** Bangabhasha 1836 → Landholders 1838 → BBIS 1843 → Indian League 1875; Bombay Assn 1852 → Indian League 1875 → Indian Assn 1876 → Madras Mahajana Sabha 1884; East India Assn 1866 → PSS 1870 → MMS 1884 → Bombay Presidency Assn 1885.
- **Nature:** early bodies were **regional**, led by **landlords or the urban elite**, worked by **petitions and memorials**; after 1858 the **educated middle class** led them, and they grew into all-India coordination → **INC 1885**.

=== Nationalist Literature – Anandamath, Vande Mataram, Nil Darpan
| Work | Author | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Nil Darpan** (Neeldarpan) | **Dinabandhu Mitra** | Written 1858-59, published **1860**; cruelty of **indigo planters** – earliest of Neeldarpan / Satyendranath Tagore's ICS (1863-64) / Arya Samaj (1875) / Anandamath (1882) |
| **Anandamath** | **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee** | **1882**; set in the **Sannyasi Rebellion** (1763-1800); contains **Vande Mataram** |
| Durgeshnandini | Bankim Chandra Chatterjee | His **first novel** (1865) |
| Rajasimha, Rajani, Indira | Bankim Chandra Chatterjee | Other novels |
- **Anandamath = "Bible of Bengali Patriotism"** – the book associated with the rise of the national movement (not Gitanjali, Gora, Satyarth Prakash, Gita Rahasya).
- **Bankim made the Sannyasi Rebellion famous** – not the Bhil, Rangpur or Kuka revolts.
- **Vande Mataram:** composed by **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee** – India's **National Song** (adopted 24 Jan 1950); **first sung at the INC by Rabindranath Tagore at the Calcutta session, 1896** (Tagore sang it, he did not compose it).
- **Telugu translation** of Vande Mataram: **Y. Doraiswamayya and M. Ramarao**; "**The Cry of Mother India**" (Krishna Patrika, **15 Dec 1905**) – first nationalist song of Andhra.
- **Nationalist poets of Bengal:** **Rangalal Banerjee, Hem Chandra Banerjee, Nabin Chandra Sen** – **Shiv Kumar Batalvi** is a post-independence Punjabi poet, not one of them.

=== Madras and Andhra Associations
# Madras Native Association (26 February 1852)
- **Founder and first president:** **Gazula (Gajula) Lakshminarasu Chetty** – first person from Andhra in the national movement; also ran **The Crescent** (1844); secretary **Somasundaram Chettiyar**.
- **First political body of South India**; began as a branch of the British Indian Association, then independent.
- **Petitions:** 1852 (before the **Charter renewal of 1853**), **1859** and **1861** – against ryotwari over-assessment, torture by revenue men, the salt monopoly and **moturpha**, a **tax on trades and occupations** (looms).
- Its agitation helped bring the **Torture Commission (1854)**.
# Madras Mahajana Sabha (16 May 1884)
- **President: P. Rangaiah Naidu**; **secretary: P. Anandacharlu** (NextGen); founders with M. Viraraghavachariar and G. Subramania Iyer.
- Demanded larger councils and more Indian representation.
# District associations
- **Krishna District Association (1892):** organised by **Ramaswamy Gupta**; **first district association in the Madras Presidency**; conferences at **Guntur (July 1892)** and Machilipatnam (1893).
- **Inspiration:** the **formation of the INC (1885)** led to the district associations.
- **Angalur conference (1900)** of the Association demanded **agricultural banks**; its 1899 resolution led to **Krishna Patrika (1902)**.
- **Kakinada (Coconada) Literary Association:** memorandums (1884-85) for a **higher ICS age limit** and **simultaneous examinations**.

=== Factors of Nationalism; Lytton and the Ilbert Bill
# Factors
- **Political and administrative unification** – railways, telegraph, post, uniform laws; **K.M. Panikkar**: "the most notable achievement of British rule was the **unification of India**".
- **Western education** – liberal ideas; **English as a link language**; rise of the **educated middle class**.
- **Press and literature**; **rediscovery of India's past**; socio-religious reform.
- **Contemporary world movements** – South American, Greek, **Italian** and Irish struggles; **German unification** – **Bismarck**'s "**blood and iron**" speech (1862).
- **Economic critique** – drain of wealth, ruin of artisans.
- Early associations trained the educated class in **petitions, memorials and all-India coordination** → INC 1885.
# Lytton's resented measures (1876-80)
| Measure | Year | Why resented |
|---|---|---|
| ICS maximum age cut | 1876 | **21 → 19** (Lytton's period; ordered by Secretary of State **Salisbury**) |
| **Delhi Durbar** | **1877** | Proclaimed **Queen Victoria Empress of India** during famine |
| Vernacular Press Act | 1878 | Gagged the Indian-language press; **Amrita Bazar Patrika (1868)** turned English overnight in **1878** |
| Arms Act | 1878 | **Disarmed Indians but spared Europeans** |
| Second Afghan War | 1878-80 | Burden on Indian revenues |
# Ilbert Bill (1883)
- **Ripon**'s government, drafted by **Sir Courtenay Ilbert (Law Member)**: Indian magistrates and judges could **try European British subjects**; European agitation forced a compromise – a European accused could claim a jury of **at least half Europeans**; passed **25 January 1884**. **Not a measure of Lytton.**

=== Surendranath Banerjee
- **ICS:** passed **1869**, **dismissed in 1874** on a minor irregularity – the leader the British dismissed from the ICS.
- Founded the **Indian Association (1876)** with A.M. Bose; toured India against the ICS age cut (**21 → 19**).
- Convened the **Indian National Conference (1883, 1885)**, which **merged with the INC in 1886**.
- Title **"Rashtraguru"**; paper **The Bengalee** (from 1879); autobiography **A Nation in Making**.
- INC president at **Poona 1895** and **Ahmedabad 1902** – not Surat 1907.
