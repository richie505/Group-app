=== NOTES 5:85 | Biodiversity Hotspots in India, Endemism and Biological Diversity Act 2002
## SUB 0 | Biodiversity Hotspot – Concept and Criteria
[85:0:0] - **Definition:** a biogeographic region with a large reservoir of biodiversity that is **threatened with destruction**.
[85:0:1] - Basis: **richness, endemism and threat**. **Habitat destruction** is the main cause of biodiversity loss.
[85:0:2] - Proposed by **Norman Myers, 1988**; adopted by **Conservation International (CI), 1989**.
[85:0:3] # Both Criteria Needed
[85:0:4] TABLE head: Criterion | Threshold
  [85:0:4.0] Endemism | At least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species (over 0.5% of world total)
  [85:0:4.1] Threat | Lost at least 70% of original habitat
[85:0:5] APPSC 2017: Hotspots are marked by **endemic flowering plants and threat perception**.
[85:0:6] # Timeline and Count
[85:0:7] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [85:0:7.0] 1988 | Myers proposes the concept
  [85:0:7.1] 1989 | CI adopts it
  [85:0:7.2] 1999 | Numerical thresholds (published Myers et al., 2000); 25 hotspots
  [85:0:7.3] 2016 | 36th: North American Coastal Plain
[85:0:8] - Count grew 25 → 34 → 35 → **36** (present).
[85:0:9] - Once **15.7%** of Earth's land; about **86%** of that habitat lost; now only about **2.5%** of land.
[85:0:10] - Hold about **50% of vascular plants** (1,50,000 species) and **42% of vertebrates** as endemics.
[85:0:11] # Distribution and Types
[85:0:12] - Most lie in the **tropics**; South-East Asian islands among the richest; but hotspots are **not only tropical**.
[85:0:13] - **Temperate hotspots:** Mediterranean Basin, California Floristic Province, North American Coastal Plain.
[85:0:14] - **Hottest hotspots:** **8** with highest endemism under greatest threat; include **Western Ghats-Sri Lanka**.
[85:0:15] - **Coldspots:** relatively low diversity but losing habitat fast; still matter for rare species, ecosystem functions and genetic diversity.
## SUB 1 | Hotspots vs KBAs vs Global 200
[85:1:0] TABLE head: Concept | Body | Basis
  [85:1:0.0] Biodiversity hotspot | Conservation International (Myers) | 1,500+ endemic plants and 70%+ habitat lost; large regions
  [85:1:0.1] Key Biodiversity Area | IUCN-led partnership (global standard, 2016) | Single sites important for global persistence of biodiversity
  [85:1:0.2] Global 200 | WWF | Priority ecoregions (terrestrial, freshwater, marine) chosen for representativeness, not only threat

## SUB 3 | Western Ghats
[85:3:0] - Also **Sahyadri**; about **1,600 km** through **6 States**: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu.
[85:3:1] - "**Water tower of peninsular India**": feeds Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery. One of the **8 hottest hotspots**.
[85:3:2] - **UNESCO World Heritage Site 2012**: serial site of **39 component areas in 7 clusters**. IUCN 2025 Outlook: "**significant concern**".
[85:3:3] - Western Ghats alone: about **5,000** vascular plants, **1,700 (34%)** endemic, **58 endemic genera**.
[85:3:4] # Gadgil vs Kasturirangan
[85:3:5] TABLE head: Panel | Year | Recommendation
  [85:3:5.0] WGEEP – Madhav Gadgil (set up by MoEF 2010, report 2011) | 2010 | Whole Ghats as ESA: 142 talukas in 44 districts, three ESZs
  [85:3:5.1] HLWG – K. Kasturirangan | 2012 | Set up after State opposition; ESA limited to about 37% (~60,000 sq km)
[85:3:6] - MoEF&CC issued **draft** ESA notifications across six States; legal force on notification under the **EP Act, 1986**.
[85:3:7] - Supreme Court ordered eviction of encroachers in **Agasthyamalai Biosphere Reserve** (Kerala-TN; UNESCO MAB 2016).

## SUB 5 | Biological Diversity Act 2002
[85:5:0] - Gives effect to **CBD (1992)**; Presidential assent **5 February 2003**.
[85:5:1] TABLE head: Tier | Body | Role
  [85:5:1.0] National | NBA, Chennai (2003) | Foreign/commercial access and IPR; advises Centre; civil court powers
  [85:5:1.1] State | SBBs; UT Biodiversity Councils | Commercial use by Indian entities
  [85:5:1.2] Local | BMCs | Document biodiversity, prepare PBRs, levy collection fees
[85:5:2] - **Section 37:** Biodiversity Heritage Sites. **Section 38:** threatened species (1,500+ plants declared by States).
[85:5:3] # Amendment Act 2023 (in force 1 April 2024)
[85:5:4] - Offences **decriminalised** (replaced by penalties).
[85:5:5] - **AYUSH** practitioners and codified traditional knowledge exempt from prior intimation to SBBs; cultivated medicinal plants exempt.
[85:5:6] - **Biological Diversity Rules, 2024** with statutory National Biodiversity Authority Fund.
[85:5:7] - **Amendment in force:** the Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023 came into force on **1 April 2024**.
## SUB 6 | BMCs, PBRs and Heritage Sites
[85:6:0] - **PBRs are prepared by BMCs** (APPSC 2019). About **2.62 lakh BMCs** (2024); ~**2.72 lakh PBRs** (Jun 2026).
[85:6:1] - BMCs are key to **Nagoya Protocol** objectives and can levy collection fees.
[85:6:2] - **BHS:** notified by the **State Government** with local bodies on SBB advice; no change to ownership or livelihood rights; traditional uses continue; may protect crop wild relatives.
[85:6:3] - Count: **54** (NBA, March 2026); 45 (Feb 2024) → 48 (Apr 2025).

## SUB 7 | Access and Benefit Sharing
[85:7:0] - Commercial benefits from bioresources shared with providing communities; covers plant, animal, microbial resources and traditional knowledge; **excludes human genetic material**. CBD 1992 → **Nagoya Protocol 2010**.
[85:7:1] - **Kani model:** Kani tribe's knowledge of **Arogyapacha** (Trichopus zeylanicus) → **TBGRI** drug **Jeevani** (anti-fatigue).
[85:7:2] - ABS raised over **₹266 crore** since 2008: **Red Sanders 45%** (₹120 crore), seed sector 32.3%.
[85:7:3] - Repository rule: **25-40%** to repository, **60-75%** to local communities via SBBs/UTBCs.
[85:7:4] - **AP:** APSBB vision of **zero biodiversity loss by 2050**; Red Sanders makes AP vulnerable to biopiracy and the largest ABS earner.
