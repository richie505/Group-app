## SUB 2 | Hotspot Concept, Criteria and Count
[2:0] - **Definition:** a biogeographic region with a significant reservoir of biodiversity that is **threatened with destruction** – i.e. **exceptional (endemic) plant diversity + serious habitat loss**, not just "maximum biodiversity".
[2:1] - Concept introduced by **Norman Myers (British ecologist), 1988**; adopted by **Conservation International (CI) in 1989**, which **reassessed** it from 1996. **Not** BirdLife International, IUCN or WWF.
[2:2] - Basis: **richness, endemism and threat**. **Habitat destruction** is the main cause of biodiversity loss.
[2:3] TABLE head: Criterion (both needed – "two strict criteria") | Threshold
  [2:3.0] Endemism | At least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species (over 0.5% of world total)
  [2:3.1] Threat | Lost at least 70% of original habitat (30% or less natural vegetation left) – not 90%
[2:4] - **UPSC 2011:** Western Ghats–Sri Lanka and Indo-Burma recognised for **species richness, endemism, threat perception** (1, 3 and 5) – not vegetation density, ethno-botany or adaptation.
[2:5] - **APPSC Group 2:** hotspots are characterised by **endemic flowering plants and threat perception**.
[2:6] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [2:6.0] 1988 | Myers proposes the concept
  [2:6.1] 1989 | CI adopts it
  [2:6.2] 1999 | CI identifies 25 hotspots (numerical thresholds; Myers et al., 2000)
  [2:6.3] 2011 | 35th: Forests of East Australia
  [2:6.4] 2016 | 36th: North American Coastal Plain
[2:7] - Count: 25 → 34 → 35 → **36** (present; "as of 2023" also 36).
[2:8] - Once **15.7%** of Earth's land; about **86%** of that habitat lost; now only about **2.3–2.5%** of land.
[2:9] - Hold about **50% of vascular plants** (1,50,000 species) and **42% of terrestrial vertebrates** as endemics.
[2:10] - Most lie in the **tropics**, but hotspots are **not only tropical**: **temperate** ones include the **Mediterranean Basin** (incl. the **Eastern Mediterranean**), California Floristic Province, North American Coastal Plain. **North-western Australia is not** a hotspot (the Australian ones are SW Australia and East Australian forests) → UPSC 2009: **Eastern Himalayas and Eastern Mediterranean only**.
[2:11] - **Hottest hotspots:** **8** with highest endemism under greatest threat; include **Western Ghats–Sri Lanka**, Indo-Burma, Sundaland, Madagascar, Philippines.
[2:12] - **Coldspots:** relatively low diversity but losing habitat fast; still matter for rare species, ecosystem functions and genetic diversity.
[2:13] TABLE head: Concept | Body | Basis
  [2:13.0] Biodiversity hotspot | Conservation International (Myers) | 1,500+ endemic plants and 70%+ habitat lost; large regions
  [2:13.1] Key Biodiversity Area | IUCN-led partnership (global standard, 2016) | Single sites important for global persistence of biodiversity
  [2:13.2] Global 200 | WWF | Priority ecoregions (terrestrial, freshwater, marine) chosen for representativeness
  [2:13.3] Important Bird and Biodiversity Areas (IBAs) | BirdLife International (a global partnership of conservation NGOs) | Bird sites – BirdLife did not originate the hotspot concept (UPSC 2015)
[2:14] - **India's share (TSPSC 2023):** **2.4%** of world land, about **7–8%** of recorded species; main threat = habitat loss and fragmentation (urbanisation, infrastructure, deforestation, pollution, climate change).

## SUB 3 | Western Ghats – India's Richest Biodiversity Region
[3:0] - Also **Sahyadri**; about **1,600 km** through **6 States**: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu (not AP).
[3:1] - "**Water tower of peninsular India**": feeds Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery. One of the **8 hottest hotspots**; the **most biodiversity-rich / "biodiversity-saturated" area of India** in PYQs (UPPSC 2015, 2019).
[3:2] - **Silent Valley NP** (Nilgiri BR, Kerala) – the "richest biodiversity" answer among valleys (CGPSC 2011), against Kashmir valley, Valley of Flowers, Surma valley.
[3:3] - About **5,000** vascular plants, **1,700 (34%)** endemic, **58 endemic genera**; ~433 plant species shared only with Sri Lanka.
[3:4] - **UNESCO World Heritage Site 2012**: serial site of **39 component areas in 7 clusters**. IUCN 2025 World Heritage Outlook: "**significant concern**".
[3:5] - **New species:** **Feb 2017** – Delhi University (S.D. Biju) described **7 new night frogs (Nyctibatrachus)**, **4 of them miniature** (12–15 mm).
[3:6] TABLE head: Panel | Year | Recommendation
  [3:6.0] WGEEP – Madhav Gadgil (set up by MoEF 2010, report 2011) | 2010 | Whole Ghats as ESA, graded ESZ I–III; 142 talukas in 44 districts
  [3:6.1] HLWG – K. Kasturirangan | 2012 | Set up after State opposition; ESA limited to about 37% (~60,000 sq km)
[3:7] - MoEF&CC issued **draft** ESA notifications across six States (2018 onwards, still not final); legal force on notification under the **EP Act, 1986**.
[3:8] - Supreme Court ordered eviction of encroachers in **Agasthyamalai Biosphere Reserve** (Kerala–TN; UNESCO MAB 2016).
[3:9] > Gadgil and Kasturirangan reports = **protection of the Western Ghats** (UPSC 2016; APPSC 2019).

## SUB 4 | Biological Diversity Act 2002 and 2023 Amendment
[4:0] - Gives effect to **CBD (1992)**; passed by Parliament in **December 2002** (Lok Sabha 2 Dec, Rajya Sabha 11 Dec); Presidential assent **5 February 2003** (Act 18 of 2003).
[4:1] TABLE head: Tier | Body | Role
  [4:1.0] National | NBA, Chennai (2003) | Foreign/commercial access and IPR; advises Centre; civil court powers
  [4:1.1] State | SBBs; UT Biodiversity Councils | Commercial use by Indian entities
  [4:1.2] Local | BMCs | Document biodiversity, prepare PBRs, levy collection fees
[4:2] - **Section 37:** Biodiversity Heritage Sites. **Section 38:** threatened species (1,500+ plants declared by States).
[4:3] - **Amendment Act 2023** (in force **1 April 2024**): offences **decriminalised** (penalties only); **AYUSH** practitioners and codified traditional knowledge exempt from prior intimation; cultivated medicinal plants exempt.
[4:4] - **Biological Diversity Rules, 2024** replace the 2004 Rules, with a statutory National Biodiversity Authority Fund.

## SUB 6 | Access and Benefit Sharing
[6:0] - Commercial benefits from bioresources shared with providing communities; covers plant, animal, microbial resources and traditional knowledge; **excludes human genetic material**. CBD 1992 → **Nagoya Protocol 2010**.
[6:1] - **Kani model:** Kani tribe's knowledge of **Arogyapacha** (Trichopus zeylanicus) → **TBGRI** drug **Jeevani** (anti-fatigue).
[6:2] - ABS raised over **₹266 crore** since 2008: **Red Sanders 45%** (₹120 crore), seed sector 32.3%.
[6:3] - Repository rule: **25–40%** to repository, **60–75%** to local communities via SBBs/UTBCs.
[6:4] - **AP:** APSBB vision of **zero biodiversity loss by 2050**; Red Sanders makes AP vulnerable to biopiracy and the largest ABS earner.
