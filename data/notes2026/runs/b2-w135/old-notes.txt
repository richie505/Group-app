## SUB 2 | Constitutional Basis – Articles 15, 16, 335, 338, 340
[135:2:0] TABLE head: Article | Content | Added by
  [135:2:0.0] 15(4) | Special provisions for SEBCs, SCs, STs | 1st CAA, 1951
  [135:2:0.1] 15(5) | Reservation in admission incl. private aided/unaided; minority institutions excluded | 93rd CAA, 2005
  [135:2:0.2] 15(6) / 16(6) | EWS in education / posts | 103rd CAA, 2019
  [135:2:0.3] 16(4) | Reservation of posts for backward classes | Original Constitution
  [135:2:0.4] 16(4A) | Reservation in promotion for SCs/STs | 77th CAA, 1995
  [135:2:0.5] 16(4B) | Backlog vacancies as a separate class | 81st CAA, 2000
  [135:2:0.6] 335 proviso | Relaxed marks for SCs/STs in promotion | 82nd CAA, 2000
  [135:2:0.7] 338B | National Commission for Backward Classes | 102nd CAA, 2018
  [135:2:0.8] 342A | SEBC lists | 102nd CAA, 2018
  [135:2:0.9] 366(26C) | Defines SEBCs by reference to Art. 342A | 102nd CAA, 2018
  [135:2:0.10] 340 | President appoints a commission to investigate the conditions of backward classes and recommend remedies | Original
  [135:2:0.11] 341 / 342 | SC / ST lists | Original
[135:2:1] - **"Backward Classes" is NOT defined** in the Constitution (APPSC G2: the "defined" statement is the wrong one).
[135:2:2] - Correct term: **Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBC)** – not "Socially and Economically".
[135:2:3] - **Art. 16(4)** backward classes are **mainly social**; they need not be both socially and educationally backward (Indra Sawhney) – identified mainly by **caste and social backwardness**.
[135:2:4] - **Art. 338(10)** (before 2018): references to SCs included such backward classes as the **President** specified – BC complaints went to the SC Commission.
[135:2:5] - For the CEI Act, OBCs are the classes "so determined by the **Central Government**".
[135:2:6] - **Protective discrimination** = e.g. **reservation of posts for STs**; SHG aid or minority scholarships are welfare schemes; one-region-only jobs would be plain discrimination.
[135:2:7] - **First reservation in India:** **Kolhapur State, 1902**, under **Shahu Maharaj** (for non-Brahmins and backward classes).
[135:2:8] > Number game: Art. 340 = commission (Kalelkar, Mandal, Rohini all under it); 338B = NCBC; 342A = SEBC list; NHRC is statutory, not constitutional.
[135:2:9] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt) p.139-140; Rocket Indian Society p.3, p.5-6 (Kolhapur 1902); app notes 2:135 sub 0, 1
[135:2:10] - **Purpose of SC reservation:** to **remove historical disadvantages** (untouchability, exclusion) and ensure **equality of opportunity** (Arts. 15(4), 16(4), 46, 335).
[135:2:11] - **Domicile (residence) reservation** is **not impermissible** under **Article 15** – Art. 15 bars discrimination on **place of birth**, not residence (D.P. Joshi v. Madhya Bharat, 1955).
## SUB 3 | Landmark Cases – Indra Sawhney to Janhit Abhiyan
[135:3:0] # Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992, "Mandal case")
[135:3:1] - **Caste** can be the main criterion of backwardness (overruled Balaji on this); **upheld 27%** OBC quota.
[135:3:2] - Total reservation **not above 50%** except in **extraordinary cases**; applied **year by year**; **carry-forward** allowed within 50%.
[135:3:3] - **Creamy layer** excluded from OBCs (the concept originates here).
[135:3:4] - Reservation only at **initial appointment, not promotion** → answered by the **77th** Amendment.
[135:3:5] - Directed a **permanent statutory body** for OBC lists → **NCBC** (1993).
[135:3:6] TABLE head: Case | Year | Issue decided
  [135:3:6.0] State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan | 1951 | Caste-based quota struck down → 1st Amendment, Art. 15(4)
  [135:3:6.1] M.R. Balaji v. State of Mysore | 1962 | Reservation generally within 50%; caste not the sole test
  [135:3:6.2] P.A. Inamdar | 2005 | No quota in private unaided institutions → undone by 93rd
  [135:3:6.3] M. Nagaraj | 2006 | Upheld 77th/81st/82nd/85th; promotion quota needs data on backwardness, inadequacy and efficiency
  [135:3:6.4] Ashoka Kumar Thakur v. UoI | 2008 | Upheld the 93rd Amendment and 27% OBC quota in central educational institutions (CEI Act); creamy layer applies to OBCs
  [135:3:6.5] Ram Singh v. UoI | 2015 | Jats struck out of the Central OBC list
  [135:3:6.6] Jarnail Singh | 2018 | Creamy layer can apply to SC/ST promotions
  [135:3:6.7] Jaishri Patil (Maratha case) | 5 May 2021 | Maratha SEBC quota struck down (breach of 50%); only Centre identifies SEBCs → reversed by 105th
  [135:3:6.8] Janhit Abhiyan | 7 Nov 2022 | EWS upheld
  [135:3:6.9] Union of India v. Rohith Nathan | 11 March 2026 | Parental salary alone cannot place a candidate in the creamy layer
[135:3:7] - **OBC quota timeline:** 27% central jobs from **1993**; **CEI (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006** – 27% OBC seats, in force **2007**.
[135:3:8] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt) p.139-140; Rocket Indian Society p.94; app notes 2:135 sub 4, 7
[135:3:9] - **Bihar (Nov 2023):** Assembly unanimously raised SC/ST/EBC/OBC quotas in jobs and education to **65%** (75% with EWS) after its caste survey; **struck down by the Patna High Court (June 2024)** for breaching the 50% ceiling.

[135:4:2] - **Muslim quota:** AP was the State that **proposed/ordered Muslim reservation**: **5%** (2004, 2005) struck down by the AP HC; **2007 Act – 4%** struck down by the AP HC in 2010, but the **Supreme Court's interim order** lets it continue pending a Constitution Bench.

## SUB 5 | Creamy Layer and EWS Reservation
[135:5:0] # Creamy layer
[135:5:1] - **Status-based exclusions**: children of constitutional post-holders, **Class-I officers**, parents **both Class-II** officers, senior armed-forces officers, large landowners, high earners – **not all OBC children** are excluded.
[135:5:2] TABLE head: Year | Income limit (per annum)
  [135:5:2.0] 1993 | ₹1 lakh
  [135:5:2.1] 2004 | ₹2.5 lakh
  [135:5:2.2] 2008 | ₹4.5 lakh
  [135:5:2.3] 2013 | ₹6 lakh
  [135:5:2.4] 2017 | ₹8 lakh (still current)
[135:5:3] - **SC/ST creamy layer debate:** the Union Government opposed it (August 2026) – SC/ST status flows from **Arts. 341 and 342**.
[135:5:4] # EWS – 103rd Amendment (2019)
[135:5:5] - **Up to 10%** for economically weaker sections **not covered** by SC/ST/OBC quotas; **over and above** existing reservation (outside the 50% cap).
[135:5:6] - In jobs and education, including **private** institutions; **enabling, not mandatory** for States.
[135:5:7] - Criteria: family income **below ₹8 lakh**; agricultural land below **5 acres**; flat below **1,000 sq ft**.
[135:5:8] - **First State to implement** the 10% EWS quota: **Gujarat**, from **14 January 2019**.
[135:5:9] - **Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022):** upheld **3:2** by a **five-judge** bench; dissent: **CJI U.U. Lalit** and **Justice S. Ravindra Bhat**.

## SUB 0 | Forest Rights Act, 2006
[136:0:0] # The Act
[136:0:1] - Full name: **Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006** – aims at **redressal of the historical injustice** done to forest-dwelling STs and other traditional forest dwellers.
[136:0:2] - **Parliament passed it in December 2006**: Lok Sabha **15 Dec**, Rajya Sabha **18 Dec 2006** (passage complete); President's assent **29 Dec 2006**; in force **31 Dec 2007**; Rules **1 Jan 2008**.
[136:0:3] - **Nodal ministry: Ministry of Tribal Affairs** (not MoEFCC) (UPSC 2021). The Act **overrides** inconsistent laws.
[136:0:4] # Who decides
[136:0:5] - **Sec. 6(1): the Gram Sabha** is the authority to **initiate** the process of determining the nature and extent of individual or community forest rights (UPSC 2013) – not the Forest Department, Collector or Tahsildar.
[136:0:6] - Claims go up to the six-member **Sub-Divisional** and **District Level Committees**; the **District Level Committee's decision on the record of forest rights is final** (Sec. 6(6)).
[136:0:7] TABLE head: Category | Eligibility (cut-off 13 Dec 2005)
  [136:0:7.0] Forest-dwelling STs | Occupying forest land before the cut-off
  [136:0:7.1] Other Traditional Forest Dwellers | 3 generations (75 years) of residence
[136:0:8] - Ceiling **4 ha** per family; rights **heritable but not alienable**.
[136:0:9] TABLE head: Right | Type
  [136:0:9.0] Habitation, self-cultivation | Individual
  [136:0:9.1] Nistar, grazing, fishing | Community
  [136:0:9.2] Ownership of minor forest produce | Community
  [136:0:9.3] CFR management, PVTG habitat | Community
[136:0:10] # Bamboo, habitat, CWH
[136:0:11] - **Bamboo is a minor forest produce** under the FRA, and the FRA gives **ownership of MFP** to forest dwellers. The **Indian Forest (Amendment) Act, 2017** only removed bamboo grown **outside forests** from "tree" – it gave **no right to fell bamboo in forests** (UPSC 2019: 2 and 3 only). **No ownership of minerals.**
[136:0:12] - **Critical Wildlife Habitat** is defined **only in the FRA**: areas of national parks and sanctuaries to be **kept inviolate**, notified after an **open process of consultation**.
[136:0:13] - **Habitat rights** for PVTGs are decided under the FRA by the State machinery with the Ministry of **Tribal Affairs** guidelines – **not by MoEFCC**. **First habitat rights in India: the Baiga** (Baigachak, Dindori, Madhya Pradesh, 2015-16); later the **Bharia** (Patalkot, MP, 2022) and Kamar and Baiga (Chhattisgarh). UPSC 2018 key: statements 1 and 2.
[136:0:14] - **Protecting reserve forests:** allowing **limited rights** (e.g. grazing) to locals works better than keeping them out or giving unlimited rights or privatising.
[136:0:15] - **Niyamgiri (2013):** Dongria Kondh Gram Sabhas rejected bauxite mining under the FRA.
[136:0:16] - **Implementation gaps:** focus on individual titles over **CFR**, Gram Sabhas bypassed, MFP transit permits, **weak MoTA capacity**. **MoEF circular (3 Aug 2009):** no diversion until FRA rights are settled and Gram Sabha consent obtained (no public-importance exception).

[137:0:0] # National Policy on Education
[137:0:1] - First NPE: **1968** (Kothari Commission); second NPE: **1986** (revised 1992).

## SUB 1 | RTE Act, 2009 – Provisions
[137:1:0] - Full name: **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009**; gives effect to **Art. 21A**; free and compulsory **elementary** education for **6–14** years (Classes 1–8); in force **1 April 2010** – the Art. 51A(k) duty became a statutory right then.

[138:1:4] - A **delinquent** = a **minor** (below 18) who commits an offence – a "juvenile in conflict with law".
[138:1:5] - **Heinous offence** = minimum punishment **7 years or more**. A child of **16–18** accused of a heinous offence: **JJB preliminary assessment** (not a finding of guilt) → may transfer to the **Children's Court** (a **Sessions Court**) to be tried as an adult.
[138:1:6] - **CARA (Central Adoption Resource Authority)** given **statutory** status – the **nodal body for adoption**, under the **Ministry of Women and Child Development**; regulates **in-country and inter-country adoption**.
[138:1:7] - **Compulsory registration** of all **Child Care Institutions**; **working children** = children in need of care and protection.

  [138:1:8.0] Juvenile Justice Board | Children in conflict with law; Principal Magistrate + 2 social workers, at least one a woman
  [138:1:8.1] Child Welfare Committee | Children in need of care and protection; Chairperson + 4 members
  [138:1:8.2] Children's Court | Transferred 16–18 heinous cases; a Sessions Court
[138:1:9] - Every district has a JJB and a CWC. Juvenile delinquents are dealt with by **juvenile courts / Juvenile Justice Boards**, which put the **well-being and best interests of the child** first – child-friendly, rehabilitation-oriented justice (not punishment, humiliation or precedent).
