## SUB 0 | Child Labour – Article 24 and the 1986 Act as Amended in 2016
[0:0] # Constitution and age limits
[0:1] TABLE head: Provision | Content
  [0:1.0] Article 24 | No child below 14 years to be employed in any factory, mine or other hazardous employment (Right against Exploitation) – the Article on child labour (not Arts. 23, 26 or 29)
  [0:1.1] Article 21A | Free and compulsory education, 6–14 years (RTE Act, 2009)
  [0:1.2] Article 39(e) | Tender age of children not abused
  [0:1.3] Article 39(f) | Children protected against exploitation and against moral and material abandonment
  [0:1.4] Factories Act, 1948 | No child below 14 in a factory
  [0:1.5] Mines Act, 1952 | No person below 18 in a mine (not 16)
[0:2] - **National Policy for Children, 2013**: every person **below 18** is a child; best interests first. The **Draft National Policy on Child Protection** asks for a **zero-tolerance code of conduct** in all organisations.
[0:3] # Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986 and the 2016 Amendment
[0:4] - Enacted **1986** (in force **23 December 1986**) – the first comprehensive child labour law; child = a person who has not completed **14 years**. Followed the **Gurupadaswamy Committee (1979)**, Ministry of Labour: poverty the root cause; **dual approach** – ban hazardous work, regulate the rest → 1986 Act and **National Policy on Child Labour, 1987**.
[0:5] - **2016 Amendment** (in force **1 September 2016**) renamed it the **Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act**.
[0:6] TABLE head: Feature | 1986 Act | 2016 Amendment
  [0:6.0] Below 14 | Banned in 18 occupations and 65 processes; rest regulated | Banned in all occupations and processes (linked to RTE age)
  [0:6.1] 14–18 | — | New "adolescent" – banned in hazardous occupations only (mines, inflammable substances/explosives, Factories Act hazardous processes; earlier list had 83 entries)
  [0:6.2] Exceptions | — | Child helping the family enterprise after school hours; child artist in audio-visual entertainment (not circus)
  [0:6.3] Penalty | 3 months–1 year and/or fine ₹10,000–20,000 | Cognizable; 6 months–2 years and/or fine ₹20,000–50,000
[0:7] - **Child and Adolescent Labour Rehabilitation Fund** set up; enforcement through the **District Magistrate**.
[0:8] - **Penalty PYQ (APPSC):** the key is "**2 years imprisonment and fine up to ₹20,000**"; strictly the 2016 law gives 6 months–2 years and/or ₹20,000–50,000.
[0:9] - **Bonded labour** = work without freedom to leave, against a debt – e.g. a **child in a brick kiln not allowed to leave** (not unpaid help in a family shop or occasional work). **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976**; debt bondage can run across generations.
[0:10] > Exam angle: Art. 24 = below 14; Act year = 1986; "adolescent" exists only since 2016.

## SUB 1 | Child Labour – Magnitude, Enforcement, ILO Conventions and Cases
[1:0] # Magnitude
[1:1] - **Census 2011 (5–14 years)**: about **10.1 million** child workers (incl. marginal; **4.35 million main workers**) – about **5.6 million boys and 4.5 million girls (≈55:45)**; most in rural areas, rising in urban areas.
[1:2] - **Uttar Pradesh** had the largest number (about **21.8 lakh**, **one-fifth**), followed by Bihar, Rajasthan, MP, Maharashtra. India has the **largest number** of child labourers in the world.
[1:3] - Causes: **poverty**, demand for cheap labour, family breakdown, illiteracy, debt bondage, migration, weak enforcement.
[1:4] # Enforcement
[1:5] TABLE head: Mechanism | Fact
  [1:5.0] PENCIL (2017) | Platform for Effective Enforcement for No Child Labour; Ministry of Labour and Employment; for enforcing the amended Child and Adolescent Labour Act; inter-agency tracking of child labour cases; components: Child Tracking System, Complaint Corner, State Government, NCLP, Convergence (not a National Adoption Registry). Wrong options: SPARC, SRIJAN, YUKTI
  [1:5.1] NCLP (1988) | National Child Labour Project, Ministry of Labour and Employment (not Social Justice); withdraws children from labour; Special Training Centres for 9–14: bridge education, vocational training, mid-day meal, stipend
  [1:5.2] Operation Smile / Operation Muskaan | MHA campaign-mode drives to trace, rescue and rehabilitate missing children (2015)
  [1:5.3] CHILDLINE 1098 | Integrated with ERSS-112
[1:6] - Trafficking: widened by the **Criminal Law Amendment Act, 2013** (sexual exploitation, slavery, servitude, forced labour, organ removal) – now **Section 143, BNS 2023**.
[1:7] # ILO conventions and cases
[1:8] TABLE head: ILO Convention | Subject | India
  [1:8.0] 138 (1973) | Minimum age for admission to employment | Ratified 13 June 2017
  [1:8.1] 182 (1999) | Worst forms of child labour | Ratified 13 June 2017
[1:9] - Both relate to **child labour** (UPSC 2018) and are among the **eight fundamental** ILO conventions. **World Day Against Child Labour: 12 June**.
[1:10] TABLE head: Case | Ruling

  [1:10.1] M.C. Mehta v. State of TN (1996) | Sivakasi; ban in hazardous industries; employer deposits ₹20,000 per child in a Child Labour Rehabilitation-cum-Welfare Fund
  [1:10.2] Bachpan Bachao Andolan (2011) | Trafficking, child begging, circus children; oversight of child care institutions
  [1:10.3] Independent Thought (2017) | Marital-rape exception (Sec. 375 IPC) struck down for wives below 18

## SUB 2 | Juvenile Justice Act 2015 and Earlier Juvenile Laws
[2:0] # Earlier laws
[2:1] TABLE head: Law | Note
  [2:1.0] Children Act 1960 | For Union Territories
  [2:1.1] Juvenile Justice Act 1986 | First uniform juvenile law; juvenile = boys 16, girls 18
  [2:1.2] JJ (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2000 | Uniform age 18; homes: Observation Homes, Special Homes, Children's Homes, Shelter Homes – a "Protective Home" is not one of them (that is under the Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act)
  [2:1.3] JJ Act 2015 | In force 15 January 2016; response to the Delhi gang rape case (2012)
[2:2] # Key features of the 2015 Act
[2:3] - Replaced "**juvenile**" with "**child**" / "child in conflict with law"; two categories – children **in conflict with law** and children **in need of care and protection**.
[2:4] - A **delinquent** = a **minor** (below 18) who commits an offence – a "juvenile in conflict with law".
[2:5] - **Heinous offence** = minimum punishment **7 years or more**. A child of **16–18** accused of a heinous offence: **JJB preliminary assessment** (not a finding of guilt) → may transfer to the **Children's Court** (a **Sessions Court**) to be tried as an adult.
[2:6] - **CARA (Central Adoption Resource Authority)** given **statutory** status – the **nodal body for adoption**, under the **Ministry of Women and Child Development**; regulates **in-country and inter-country adoption**.
[2:7] - **Compulsory registration** of all **Child Care Institutions**; **working children** = children in need of care and protection.
[2:8] TABLE head: Body | Deals with / composition
  [2:8.0] Juvenile Justice Board | Children in conflict with law; Principal Magistrate + 2 social workers, at least one a woman
  [2:8.1] Child Welfare Committee | Children in need of care and protection; Chairperson + 4 members
  [2:8.2] Children's Court | Transferred 16–18 heinous cases; a Sessions Court
[2:9] - Every district has a JJB and a CWC. Juvenile delinquents are dealt with by **juvenile courts / Juvenile Justice Boards**, which put the **well-being and best interests of the child** first – child-friendly, rehabilitation-oriented justice (not punishment, humiliation or precedent).
[2:10] - **2021 Amendment** (in force **1 September 2022**): **District Magistrate** (and ADM) = focal point; DMs issue **adoption orders** instead of courts.
[2:11] - **Private Member's Bill (Dec 2025)**: lower the heinous-offence age threshold from **16 to 14**.
[2:12] - **AP**: follows the JJ Act 2015 + **Probation of Offenders Act, 1958**.

## SUB 3 | POCSO Act 2012
[3:0] # Basics
[3:1] - **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012** (not "social offences") – addresses **child sexual abuse**; drafted by **MWCD**.
[3:2] - In force **14 November 2012** (Children's Day) – the year to remember is **2012**.
[3:3] - Child = **below 18**; gender-neutral; consent below 18 **legally irrelevant**. After the **BNS** (from **1 July 2024**) POCSO continues as a **separate special law**.
[3:4] # Offences and procedure
[3:5] - Offences: penetrative and non-penetrative sexual assault, their **aggravated** forms (by a person in **trust or authority**), sexual harassment, child pornography. **Child marriage is not** a POCSO offence.
[3:6] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [3:6.0] 19 | Mandatory reporting; failure – up to 6 months and/or fine
  [3:6.1] 22 | False complaints punishable (except good faith)
  [3:6.2] 29–30 | Presumption – burden of proof on the accused
[3:7] - Child-friendly procedure: statement at the child's residence by a **woman officer**, not in uniform; **no child kept at the police station at night**; **Special Courts**, **in-camera** trial; investigation in **2 months**, trial in **1 year**. Monitored by NCPCR/SCPCRs; **POCSO e-Box (2016, NCPCR)** – confidential online reporting.
[3:8] # Amendments
[3:9] TABLE head: Year | Change
  [3:9.0] Ordinance, 21 April 2018 | Criminal Law (Amendment) Ordinance – harsher punishment (up to death) for rape of girls below 12; the POCSO amendment cleared by the Union Cabinet in 2018–19
  [3:9.1] 2019 Amendment (16 Aug 2019) | Penetrative assault min 10 years; aggravated penetrative assault min 20 years, up to life or death; child pornography defined; punished giving chemicals to hasten sexual maturity
[3:10] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Society p.88, p.101; NextGen Society - Group 2 Prelims Part 1 p.72

## SUB 5 | Child Abuse – Forms and Data
[5:0] TABLE head: Form | Examples
  [5:0.0] Physical | Commission (beating, burning) or omission (medical/nutritional neglect)
  [5:0.1] Emotional | Verbal battering, educational neglect, encouraging delinquency
  [5:0.2] Social | Infanticide, child marriage, child prostitution, beggary
  [5:0.3] Online (CSAM) | 4.5 lakh+ reports (2023); IT Act 2000; IT Rules 2021 – large platforms detect CSAM automatically
[5:1] - **Causes** (e.g. poverty, family breakdown, **parental alcoholism**) vs consequences (trauma, delinquency, dropout).
[5:2] - **Study on Child Abuse: India 2007 (MWCD)**: 13 States; **two out of three** children faced physical abuse; **53.22%** reported sexual abuse; **over 50%** of abusers known to the child or in trust.
[5:3] - **NCRB 2021**: **97.1%** of child sexual-abuse offenders known to the child. **NCRB 2023**: **Madhya Pradesh** highest number of crimes against children; **Assam** highest rate.
[5:4] - Women are **more likely to be sexually abused at home** (by known persons) than outside; **dress is not a cause** of sexual abuse.
