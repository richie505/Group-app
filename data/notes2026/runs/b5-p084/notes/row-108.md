=== Genetics and DNA – Basics and Discoverers
# DNA, genes and heredity (asked ~20 times)
- **Genetics** = study of genes, **heredity and variation** (not only Mendel's laws or DNA structure).
- **Gene** = the **unit of heredity** (a DNA segment coding for a trait); **DNA** controls hereditary characters; **chromosome** and nucleus only carry it.
- **Nucleic acids (DNA, RNA)**: **storage and expression of genetic information** – not oxygen transport, energy or body temperature.
- **DNA bases: adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine**; **RNA has uracil in place of thymine** (A and G occur in both).
- **RNA** is usually **single-stranded** (a "single helix"); DNA is a **double helix**.
- DNA occurs in the **nucleus** and also in **mitochondria and chloroplasts** (they have their own DNA).
- **Histones** = basic proteins in the **nucleus**; DNA wraps around them (nucleosomes, chromatin).
- DNA can store and pass on information because its two strands are **complementary** (A–T, G–C): each strand is a template (UPSC 2001).
- **Mutation** = change in the **base sequence** of a gene (transversion is only one kind of substitution).
- **Phenotype** = observable traits; **genotype** = genetic make-up.
- **Genetic code**: triplet codons for **20 amino acids**; non-overlapping, non-ambiguous, commaless; has **start and stop codons**; "universal" – nearly so (a few mitochondrial exceptions).
- **Gene mapping** (position of genes on a chromosome) helps trace **livestock pedigree** and breed **disease-resistant animals**; it cannot explain "all human diseases" (UPSC 2011 → I and III).

=== Microbes in Industry – Fermentation, Vinegar and Curd
# Microbe → product (asked ~20 times)
| Microbe | Product |
|---|---|
| **Lactobacillus** (bacterium) | **Curd** – turns lactose into **lactic acid** (sour milk, curd, sour taste); souring/spoiling of milk |
| **Yeast – Saccharomyces cerevisiae** (fungus) | **Ethanol** – wine, beer (malt sugar → alcohol by **fermentation**), bread, **toddy** |
| **Acetobacter** | **Vinegar** = dilute aqueous **acetic acid** (CH3COOH, ethanoic acid) |
| Aspergillus niger | Citric acid |
| Methanogens | Biogas (methane) |
| Diatoms | Diatomaceous earth |
- **Anaerobic respiration of yeast** gives **alcohol** (ethanol) + CO2 – not water, methane or acetic acid.
- **Milk → curd** is done by **bacteria** (Lactobacillus), not a vitamin or an enzyme alone; sourness = **lactic acid** (not acetic, citric or tartaric).
- **India's national microbe (2012): Lactobacillus** (*L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*), chosen by children on the Science Express.
- **Vinegar:** made by Acetobacter from fermented sugarcane juice/molasses/ethanol; acid = **acetic acid** – **not benzoic**, tartaric, lactic, citric or formic; used as a **preservative** (RAS 2016 → A, B, D). "Sour taste of vinegar" with no acetic option → **None of these**.
- Other food acids (contrast): tartaric – tamarind, grapes; citric – lemon, orange; oxalic – tomato, spinach; malic – apple; **formic (methanoic) – nettle and ant sting**; "acetic acid – potato" is a wrong pair.
- Industry uses **bacteria, microalgae and fungi** – all three (IAS 1998). **Yeast and mushrooms = fungi** (no chlorophyll).
- **Mushrooms:** some are **medicinal**, some **psychoactive** (psilocybin), some **insecticidal**, some **bioluminescent** – **all four** true (UPSC 2023).
- **Diatoms** in lungs/bone marrow help diagnose **death by drowning** (forensic).
- Microbes clean **oil spills**: **Pseudomonas putida** – A.M. Chakrabarty's genetically engineered oil-eating "superbug" (Diamond v. Chakrabarty, 1980: first patent on a living organism).
- First eukaryote genome fully sequenced: **yeast S. cerevisiae, 1996** (IAS 1997).
# Enzymes and products
- **Protease** is made commercially from **both plants and microbes** – **papain** (papaya), bromelain (pineapple), *Bacillus* proteases in detergents; trypsin is animal (pancreas).
- Recombinant enzymes: **chymosin – cheese**; amylase – bread; **pectinase – fruit juice**.
- **Xylitol** (sugar substitute for diabetics) from **sugarcane bagasse** by ultrasound-assisted fermentation – **IIT Guwahati** (announced Aug 2022).
- **2G ethanol** from crop residue: **IOCL Panipat**; E20 blending. **Bioplastics** = PLA, PHA. **India's first biodiesel plant: Kakinada, AP**.
- **Gobar-Dhan** biogas; municipal waste → CNG in Hyderabad, Pune. **DBT biofuel targets**: fuel from agri residue and municipal waste, algal fuels, SAF, integrated biorefineries.
- First microbe-made semiconductor diode (cadmium sulphide) – yeast **Schizosaccharomyces pombe**.
- **DRDO** herbal products: **Lukoskin** (with DIBER) – leucoderma; **Eczit** – eczema; **Amtooth** – toothache.
> Trap: curd = bacterium (Lactobacillus); bread, beer, wine, toddy = yeast (Saccharomyces); vinegar = Acetobacter.

=== Medical Biotechnology – Stem Cells, Antibodies, Gene Therapy
# Antibodies and hybridoma (asked 5 times)
- **Hybridoma technique: Georges Köhler and César Milstein, 1975** – fusion of an antibody-making **B cell with a myeloma cell**; used for commercial production of **monoclonal antibodies** (not interferon or antibiotics).
- **Monoclonal antibodies**: **man-made (lab) proteins** that bind one specific antigen and so modulate immune function; used against viral infections such as **Nipah** (m102.4) – UPSC 2025: **I, II and III**.
- **Plasma cells** (from B cells) **form antibodies**.
# Stem cells and transplants
- **Stem cells** divide, self-renew and can become **any/many cell types**; focus of research on serious diseases (endodermal, ectodermal, muscle cells are already specialised).
- **Bone-marrow stem cells** = the bioethically non-controversial alternative to embryonic stem cells. **Mesenchymal stem cells** were first found in **bone marrow** (a place, not a person).
- National Guidelines for Stem Cell Research: **2017**.
- **First human heart transplant: Christian Barnard, South Africa, 1967** (William Harvey = blood circulation).
- **Test-tube baby (IVF):** ovum **fertilised in a test tube**, embryo **develops in the uterus**.
# Genetic disease and gene therapy
- **Haemophilia**: **hereditary**, **X-linked (sex-linked) recessive** clotting defect – **carried by women, appears in men** ("royal haemophilia", Queen Victoria); not caused by bacteria, virus or pollutants. Tay-Sachs and cystic fibrosis are autosomal.

=== DNA Fingerprinting
# Basis and uses (asked 15 times)
- **Developed by Alec Jeffreys (1984)**; in India pioneered by **Lalji Singh** (CCMB) – father of Indian DNA fingerprinting.
- **Basis: DNA polymorphism** – highly variable repetitive regions (**VNTRs**); most DNA is the same in all people. Not the double helix, replication or "errors in base sequence".
- Tissues of the same person show the **same pattern**; polymorphisms are **inherited** (child gets half from each parent) → **paternity**.
- **Technique: Southern blotting** of DNA fragments (ELISA, RIA = antigen–antibody tests; Northern blot = RNA).
- **Uses:** identity, **paternity** (not protein analysis, chromosome counting, gene therapy, cloning or rDNA), **forensic** cases, **rape and assault** (trace hair, saliva, dried semen are enough – A and R true, R explains A), **conservation** – "all of the above"; WWF-India's anti-poaching tool for **rhinos** (RhODIS – rhino DNA index).
- **Samples:** blood, bone, hair **with root**, saliva, skin, semen – all usable; **burnt bones** lose usable DNA. **Biochips** = a technique, not a sample.
- **First crime solved:** **England** – the murders of **1983** and 1986 (solved 1987–88).
- **CDFD** (Centre for DNA Fingerprinting and Diagnostics): **Hyderabad**, under DBT.

=== Tools, Omics and Identification Techniques
| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| **PCR** | Amplifies DNA; key enzyme **DNA polymerase**; denaturation → annealing → extension |
| Gel electrophoresis | Separates DNA by **size** |
| Restriction enzymes | Cut DNA ("molecular scissors") – EcoRI, BamHI, HindIII |
| **Ligase** | Only **joins** DNA |
| Plasmids | Vectors (pBR322) |
| Bioreactors | Large-scale culture |
| **CRISPR**–Cas9 | **Genome editing** |
- **Genome**: complete genetic information; zygote genome = DNA of **two gametes**; **human genome ≈ 3 billion bases**, over 99% shared.
- **DNA sequencing** finds the base order (A, G, C, T); genetic information depends on the order of bases (A and R true, R explains A).
- **Transcriptome**: the **full range of mRNA** molecules expressed by an organism; **varies with conditions**, unlike the genome (UPSC 2016).
- **Microsatellite DNA** (short tandem repeats): **evolutionary relationships** among species, genetic diversity (UPSC 2023).
- **DNA barcoding**: **species identification** from a short standard gene region (animals: mitochondrial **COI**) – distinguishes **look-alike species** and detects **undesirable material in processed food**; it **cannot tell age** (UPSC 2022 → B and C).
- **Serological tests** (antigen–antibody reactions): **identification** – blood groups, pathogens and serotypes (**Widal, ELISA**); also used in serotaxonomy.

=== Nature and Scope of Biotechnology
- **Biotechnology** = **industrial use of living organisms**, substances from them or their processes (e.g. yeast for liquor) (IAS 1993); CBD (1992): use of **biological systems** to **make or modify products**.
- Traditional biotech: **curd**, bread, wine, vinegar; modern: rDNA, PCR, cloning, CRISPR. **Earliest biotechnologists: farmers**.
- Applications: agriculture (GM crops), medicine (drugs, vaccines, diagnostics, gene therapy), industry (microbial products).
| Colour | Field |
|---|---|
| **Red** | Medical |
| Green | Agricultural |
| **White** | Industrial – enzymes, biofuels, bioplastics (NOT marine) |
| **Blue** | **Marine** |
- **Enzymes** = biological catalysts of reactions in living systems (not bacteria or DNA).
- **Artificial enzymes (synzymes)** are made by **genetic/protein engineering** and by **synthesis of host molecules such as crown ethers** and cyclodextrins; **denaturing** proteins destroys enzyme activity (AEE 2019 → a and b).
- **Bionics** = using properties of biological systems in the mechanical world; **bionomics** = organism and environment; **bionomy** = laws of life; **biometry** = statistics of biology.

=== Bioremediation – Meaning and Methods
- **Bioremediation**: removal or degradation of pollutants by **organisms or their enzymes**; covers soil, water, air – oil spills, heavy metals, pesticides, solvents; engineered microbes may be used. **Slower but cheaper** than conventional (thermal, chemical) clean-up, no secondary pollution; results vary with pollutant and site.
- Not bioremediation: burning/incineration (thermal), **biomagnification** (DDT, mercury rising up food chains), **eutrophication** (nutrient-driven algal blooms), biofortification.
| Basis | In-situ | Ex-situ |
|---|---|---|
| Site | At the site, **no excavation**; passive, cheapest, slow | Excavated, treated elsewhere |
| Techniques | Bioventing, biosparging, bioaugmentation | **Landfarming, composting, biopiles, bioreactors** (bioreactors fastest) |
# Techniques
| Technique | How / target |
|---|---|
| **Bioaugmentation** | Adds chosen **degrading microbes** – oil spills |
| **Biostimulation** | Adds N, P, oxygen to native microbes – polluted lakes |
| **Bioventing** | Air/nutrients via wells into **soil** |
| **Biosparging** | Air injected **below the water table** – groundwater |
| Mycoremediation | **Fungi** – organic pollutants |
| Rhizofiltration | Roots absorb/precipitate contaminants from **water** |
| Rhizoremediation | Roots stimulate rhizosphere microbes |
