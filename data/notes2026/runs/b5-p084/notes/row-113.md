=== Graphene, Fullerene and Carbon Nanotubes
# Carbon nanomaterials (all sp², all pure carbon)
| Material | Structure | Remember |
|---|---|---|
| **Graphene** | **Single atom-thick layer** of graphite; **2D** hexagonal (honeycomb) lattice | **Made of carbon only – NOT silicon**; thinnest and **strongest material** (~200 times steel; stronger than steel, brass, German silver); excellent electrical and heat conductor; ~97% transparent; flexible |
| **Fullerene C60** | Hollow **cage** ("buckyball", soccer ball): **12 pentagons + 20 hexagons**, 60 C atoms | **Carbon allotrope – NOT a fluorine compound** (Teflon is the fluorine polymer); semiconductor; drug delivery, lubricants, catalysts |
| **Carbon nanotubes (CNTs)** | **Rolled graphene** cylinders (**1D** tubes) | Stronger than steel by weight, light; metallic or semiconducting; drug delivery, biosensors, batteries |
- **Graphene uses:** **conducting electrodes for touch screens, LCDs and OLEDs**, fast transistors, flexible circuits, sensors, batteries and supercapacitors.
- **Discoverers:** graphene isolated **2004** by **Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov** (Manchester) – **Nobel Physics 2010**; fullerene C60 found **1985** by **Kroto (Britain), Curl and Smalley (USA)** – **Nobel Chemistry 1996** (no "Faimen" on the team); named after architect **R. Buckminster Fuller**; **CNTs** – **Sumio Iijima (1991)**.
- **Pairs angle (UPPCS):** Dry ice – solid CO2; Teflon – fluorine polymer; Sevin – insecticide; Keratin – protein; Mustard gas – chemical weapon; **Fullerene – carbon allotrope** (the wrongly matched pair).
# UPSC 2012 – graphene statements
- 1 (2D, good conductor) ✔, 2 (thinnest but strongest) ✔, 3 (**entirely silicon**) ✘, 4 (conducting electrodes for touch screens, LCDs, OLEDs) ✔ → **1, 2 and 4**.
# UPSC 2020 – carbon nanotubes statements
- 1 drug and antigen carriers ✔; 3 biochemical sensors ✔ (CNT biosensors); 2 artificial blood capillaries and 4 biodegradable are the contested ones.
- **UPSC official key: (c) 1, 3 and 4** – CNTs can be degraded by some bacteria, fungi and enzymes; capillaries rejected (CNTs trigger platelet activation and clotting).
- ⚠ Books differ: PMF answers (a) 1 and 2 (CNTs "not biodegradable"); many coaching keys gave (d) all four. Learn the official (c).
> Exam angle: graphene = carbon, not silicon; fullerene = carbon, not fluorine; Iijima = nanotubes, Geim-Novoselov = graphene.

=== Diamond, Graphite and Other Forms of Carbon
| Allotrope | Bonding | Key property → use |
|---|---|---|
| **Diamond** | sp³; 3D tetrahedral network, **each C bonded to four** | **Hardest natural substance** (Mohs 10); least compressible; **electrical insulator (no free electrons)** but excellent **heat** conductor; transparent, high refractive index → cutting tools, jewellery |
| **Graphite** | sp² layers held by **weak van der Waals forces** | Soft, slippery, opaque, black; **good electrical conductor** → **pencil "lead"** (graphite + clay), **dry lubricant for heavy machines**, electrodes, reactor moderator |
| **Amorphous carbon** | Non-crystalline | **Coal, charcoal, coke, lampblack**; **charcoal** = the amorphous form among diamond/graphite/fullerene; activated charcoal – high surface area |
- **Diamond, graphite, graphene, fullerene, CNTs are all pure carbon** – so diamond is an allotrope of **carbon**; **methane (CH4) is a compound, NOT an allotrope**.
- **Sand** (silica, SiO2) has **no carbon**; graphite, diamond and coal do.
- **Lubricant:** **graphite** – not wood, steel, tungsten, bauxite, phosphorus, cuprite, haematite or cryolite.
- **Hardest:** diamond (not glass, quartz, platinum). **Wurtzite boron nitride** is believed (by simulation) to be ~18% harder, but it is rare and untested – keys keep **diamond** as the hardest naturally occurring substance.
- **Electrical conductor:** **graphite** (free electrons in layers) – diamond, peat and charcoal are poor conductors.

=== Nanoscale – Size, Units and Properties
- **1 nm = 10^-9 m** (one billionth of a metre); **1 Å = 10^-10 m**, so **1 nm = 10 Å**.
- **Nanoparticle size: 1-100 nm** (APPSC 2018, APPSC G2 2019, UKPSC 2012) – not 0.1-1 nm (atomic scale) or 100-1000 nm; a particle with at least one dimension **below 10^-7 m (100 nm)** is a **nanoparticle**.
- **Scale:** human hair ~80,000-100,000 nm; red blood cell ~7,000 nm; virus ~50-100 nm; DNA width ~2 nm.
- **Why different:** very high **surface-area-to-volume ratio** (more reactive, catalytic) + **quantum size effect** (discrete energy levels); also superparamagnetism, lower melting point, self-assembly.
- **Most important property of nanomaterials (RAS 2013 key): friction** – the huge surface-to-volume ratio makes friction and wear dominant at the nanoscale.
- **Device miniaturisation** = **nanotechnology**, the 21st-century technology (UPPCS 2004).
- **Nanogold:** gold nanoparticles show **surface plasmon resonance** – look **red, purple (or blue)** instead of yellow, yet stay gold (biocompatible; used in conductive inks and lateral-flow test kits). APPSC key: "**blue gold with properties similar to gold**".
# Quantum structures
| Structure | Nano-confined dimensions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Quantum well** | **One** dimension nano, two large (2D) | thin films |
| **Quantum wire** | Two dimensions nano (1D) | nanowires, nanotubes |
| **Quantum dot** | All three nano (0D) | **semiconductor nanostructure**, about **2-10 nm** (TSLPRB: ~10 nm) |
- **Quantum dots:** size-tuned colour – **smaller dots → blue**, larger → red; QLED displays, bio-imaging, solar cells; **Nobel Chemistry 2023 – Bawendi, Brus, Ekimov**.
- **Other nanomaterials:** **nanoporous materials – separation and catalysis**; nanofibres – filtration, smart clothing, scaffolds; nanocrystals – LEDs, imaging.

=== Piezoelectric Nanogenerators and Nano Devices
- **Piezoelectric effect:** electric charge from **mechanical stress (pressure)** in crystals, ceramics, polymers; reversible (field → deformation).
- **NCL Pune nanogenerator:** electricity when **pressure** is applied → **piezoelectric effect** (APPSC G1 2018, 2019) – NOT photoelectric, photovoltaic, thermoelectric, magnetoelectric or induction.
- **Quartz crystal in watches** and **gas-stove (electronic) lighters** = **piezoelectric effect** (IAS 1993; APPSC).
- **Light nanogenerator (contrast):** **IASST Guwahati** (DST) – graphene oxide + polyaniline device that harvests **light** energy to power wearables.
- **Nanoear (2012, LMU Munich):** an **optically trapped gold nanoparticle** – detects sound down to **–60 dB** (not –120 dB; not silica or silver).
- **Internet of Nano Things (IoNT):** network of nanodevices connected to the internet.
- **Other devices:** gold-nanoparticle lateral-flow kits (malaria, dengue, typhoid in ~15 min); **graphene bendable wearable sensors (IISc + C-DAC)**.

=== Generations of Nanoproducts and Synthesis Tools
| Generation (M.C. Roco) | Nature | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| I | **Passive** nanostructures | **Aerosols**, colloids, polymers, ceramics, coatings |
| II | **Active** nanostructures | **Targeted drugs**, sensors, 3-D transistors, amplifiers |
| III | **Systems of nanosystems** | **Robotics**, **3-D networking**, nanoelectronics |
| IV | **Molecular nanosystems** | **Molecular manufacturing** (molecular devices) |
- **Order (RAS 2016):** Aerosol → Targeted drugs → 3D networking → Molecular manufacturing (**A, D, B, C**). Third generation example = **robotics** (APPSC G2 2011).
| Tool / route | Fact |
|---|---|
| **STM** (1981) | Images atoms on **conducting** surfaces only |
| **AFM** (1986) | Works on **non-conductors** too |
| **Top-down** | Cut bulk down – **lithography, milling, etching** |
| **Bottom-up** | Build atom by atom – **self-assembly**, CVD, sol-gel |
~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 7 p.695 (top-down, bottom-up); app notes 5:113 sub 3-4

=== Nanoparticles in Nature, Health and Environmental Concerns
| Origin | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Natural** | **Volcanic ash**, sea spray, clay, natural colloids (proteins, viruses) |
| Incidental | Soot, diesel exhaust, welding fumes |
| **Engineered** | Quantum dots, CNTs, **nano-silver**, TiO2 |
- **Cosmetics:** **TiO2 and ZnO** nanoparticles in **sunscreens** (UV protection).
- **UPSC 2022:** "nanoparticles do not exist in nature" ✘; metallic-oxide nanoparticles in cosmetics ✔; nanoparticles from commercial products entering the environment unsafe ✔ → **B and C**.
- **Nanotoxicity:** enter by inhalation, ingestion, skin; cross the blood-brain barrier; **free radicals (ROS) → oxidative stress, inflammation**; persistence, **bioaccumulation**, food-chain entry (UPSC 2014: all three concerns correct).
- **Regulation:** **no dedicated nano law**; DGFASLI (occupational), **FSSAI 2022**, **SCOMET** list (dual-use); **Guidelines for Evaluation of Nano-based Agri-input and Food Products (2020)** – **DBT, Agriculture Ministry, FSSAI**. Also cost and the "**nano-divide**".
~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 7 p.694-695 (origin, UPSC 2022), p.704-707 (TiO2, ZnO, issues, UPSC 2014); app notes 5:113 sub 10
