=== Global Frameworks – Yokohama, Hyogo, Sendai, UNDRR and DRR Days
# From IDNDR to Sendai
| Framework / event | Year / period | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **IDNDR** (International Decade for Natural Disaster Reduction) | **1990–1999** | Proclaimed by the UNGA in **1989**; first concerted UN effort; prompted India's DM Cell |
| **Rio Earth Summit** | 1992 | Earlier than all the DRR frameworks |
| **Yokohama Strategy** (1st WCDRR – World Conference on Natural Disaster Reduction) | **23–27 May 1994**, Yokohama | First global guidelines on prevention, preparedness, mitigation |
| **UNISDR** | 1999 | Secretariat for the International Strategy for Disaster Reduction |
| **Hyogo Framework for Action** (2nd WCDRR, Kobe) | **2005–2015** | **5 priorities**; aim: reduce disaster losses in lives and social, economic, environmental assets |
| **Sendai Framework** (3rd WCDRR, **Sendai**, Japan) | **2015–2030** | Adopted **18 March 2015**; **4 priorities, 7 targets**; voluntary, non-binding |
| **Global Platform for DRR** | May 2017 | Held at **Cancun, Mexico** |
| **UNISDR → UNDRR** | **1 May 2019** | **Geneva**; custodian of Sendai; convenes the Global Platform |
- **Chronology:** Rio (1992) → Yokohama (1994) → Hyogo (2005) → Sendai (2015). **All three WCDRRs were hosted by Japan.**
- **UNISDR** = **United Nations International Strategy for Disaster Reduction**; **DRR** = **Disaster Risk Reduction**.
- Statement trap (APPSC FRO 2022): states met at Yokohama in May 1994 – true; "the decade 1994–2004 was declared IDNDR at Yokohama" – **false** (IDNDR = 1990s, proclaimed 1989).
- **Post-2015 frameworks** with a bearing on India's DRR: **Sendai, SDGs (2015–2030), Paris Agreement** – all three; **Hyogo is NOT** one of them.
# Sendai – four priorities and seven targets
- Priorities: **1** Understanding disaster risk; **2** Strengthening disaster risk governance; **3** Investing in DRR for resilience; **4** Enhancing preparedness for effective response and **"Build Back Better"**.
| Target | Aim (by 2030 unless stated) |
|---|---|
| A | Reduce global disaster mortality |
| B | Reduce number of affected people |
| C | Reduce direct economic loss in relation to global GDP |
| D | Reduce damage to critical infrastructure and disruption of basic services |
| **E** | Increase countries with **national and local DRR strategies – by 2020** |
| F | Enhance international cooperation to developing countries |
| G | Access to multi-hazard early warning systems and risk information |
- Principles: States have the primary role; all-of-society; multi-hazard (natural + man-made); people-centred.

=== DM Act 2005, NDMA, NDRF and the National Set-up
# The Act and the plans
- **DM Act 2005**: Presidential assent **23 December 2005**; Act No. 53; **11 chapters, 79 sections**; triggered by the **2004 tsunami** (and Bhuj 2001). Sec 3 NDMA, Sec 8 NEC, Sec 14 SDMA, Sec 25 DDMA, **Sec 42 NIDM**, **Sec 44 NDRF**, Sec 46 NDRF (fund), Sec 47 NDMF, Sec 48 SDRF.
- **NDMA**: first set up by executive order in **2005**; formally **notified 27 September 2006** under the Act – most keys answer "**2005**" for "when was NDMA formed/constituted".
- **NDMA functions (Sec 6)**: lays down **policies**, **approves the National Plan**, **approves plans of Union Ministries/Departments**, lays down guidelines for **State Authorities** (so "does not interfere with State Plans" is wrong).
- **National Plan**: prepared by the **NEC**, **approved by NDMA**. **First NDMP 2016** – document dated **May 2016**, **released by the PM on 1 June 2016**; revised 2019. Its response part lists **18** broad activities.
- **National Policy on DM (NPDM)**: approved **22 October 2009**; approach **holistic, pro-active**, multi-disaster, technology-driven – **from relief-centric to prevention, mitigation, preparedness**.
- DM Act gives **PRIs and ULBs** a legal role; the **2025 amendment** adds Urban DM Authorities and statutory status to NCMC and HLC.
# Who chairs what
| Body | Chair |
|---|---|
| **NDMA** | **Prime Minister** (ex-officio); up to 9 members |
| NEC | Union Home Secretary |
| **NCMC** (National Crisis Management Committee) | **Cabinet Secretary** |
| NPDRR; High Level Committee (fund approvals) | Union Home Minister |
| CCS | PM – nuclear, biological, chemical emergencies |
| SDMA / State Crisis Management Committee | Chief Minister / Chief Secretary |
| DDMA | District Collector / DM |
- **Primary responsibility for disaster management lies with the State Government**; the Centre (MHA) supports with resources, NDRF and funds.
- **Nodal ministry: Ministry of Home Affairs** (shifted from Agriculture in **2002**, after Bhuj); MHA's DG Fire Services, Civil Defence & Home Guards gives **technical advice on fire protection, prevention and legislation** (fire = State subject).

# NDRF and other institutions
- **NDRF**: raised **2006** (Sec 44) with **8 battalions**; **12 by 2017**; **16 now** (as of 2026), drawn from CAPFs; ~1,149 per battalion; motto "Aapada Seva Sadaiv Sarvatra"; Academy **Nagpur**; regional HQ for AP–Telangana–Karnataka **Vijayawada**.
- **National Civil Defence College**: founded **29 April 1957** at **Nagpur** (as Central Emergency Relief Training Institute); National Fire Service College also at Nagpur.
- **Mock exercises** are conducted regularly by **NDMA** (with NIDM, NDRF and States). **Aapda Mitra** trains community volunteers as first responders.
- **First Indian Disaster Management Congress**: **29 November 2006**. **SACHET**: NDMA alert system on the Common Alerting Protocol (C-DOT).
- **Funds:** SDRF = primary State fund (Centre 75:25 general, 90:10 special category); NDRF fund for severe calamities; NDMF/SDMF for mitigation (after 15th FC). 11th FC → National Calamity Contingency Fund; 13th FC → NDRF/SDRF.
- **Notified disasters** (SDRF/NDRF aid): **12** – cyclone, drought, earthquake, fire, flood, tsunami, hailstorm, landslide, avalanche, cloudburst, pest attack, frost & cold wave – **14 since Aug 2026** with **heatwave and lightning** (Finance Commission advice, MHA notification); no man-made disaster is notified.
> NDRF battalions is a moving number: 8 (2006) → 12 (2017) → 16 (now). NCMC = Cabinet Secretary, not Home Minister.

=== Concepts – Hazard, Vulnerability, Risk and Classification
# Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Hazard** | A circumstance/event that **poses potential harm or danger** |
| **Disaster** | When a hazard meets vulnerability and causes serious harm beyond coping capacity |
| **Vulnerability** | Susceptibility to harm – physical, social, economic, **environmental** |
| **Risk** | **Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability / Capacity** – infinite capacity → **zero risk** |
| Vulnerability profile | **Assessment of the susceptibility** of a community or system to hazards |
| Hazard mapping | **Mapping areas at risk** of various hazards |
- "**All disasters are hazards but all hazards are not disasters**" – taught as **true** (a hazard becomes a disaster only when it strikes a vulnerable community).
- Disaster = hazard + **exposure** + **vulnerability** (+ location); **social status** alone is not a factor forming a disaster (APPSC key). **Gales** = a **hazard characteristic** (APPSC key).
- **Disaster Crunch (Pressure-and-Release) Model**: vulnerability is **rooted in socio-economic processes**.
- **DRR works best when it addresses the underlying causes of vulnerability**; objective of DM = **reduce loss of life and property**.
- **Environmental vulnerability** = natural-resource depletion and degradation; **environmental degradation** magnifies drought and can trigger natural disasters (it is not itself a natural geological disaster).
- Rising vulnerability comes from population growth, climate change, pollution – **not "stagnation in S&T"**. Development and disaster deaths are **inversely** related.
- Most vulnerable: people in an **old city with narrow roads**; women and children.
- **GIS**: manages large spatial data for **vulnerability and hazard assessment**.
# Classification
| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Geophysical / geological** | Earthquake, **tsunami**, **landslide**, volcano, avalanche |
| Hydrological | Flood, flash flood |
| Meteorological | Cyclone, tornado, thunderstorm, **hailstorm** |
| Climatological | Drought, heat wave, cold wave |
| **Biological** | Epidemics, **pest attack**, **food poisoning** (not hailstorm) |
| Man-made | Industrial, chemical, **nuclear explosion**, fire, transport, war |
- **Natural** disasters arise from environmental/geological phenomena; **man-made** from technological failure or human activity. **Urban flooding** = **mostly man-made**.
- Floods: melting snow, lack of vegetation, broken dams – a **biological disaster does not cause flooding**.
- Tsunami causes: undersea earthquake, underwater landslide, underwater volcano (and big explosions) – **underwater mining explosions** are the "not a cause" option.
- Socio-natural hazards: floods worsened by deforestation; emerging hazards: cyber attacks, space weather.
> Definition and "NOT a" items dominate; the classification table answers tsunami/landslide (geophysical) and hailstorm (not biological).

=== Mitigation, Safety and First Aid
# Mitigation by hazard
| Hazard | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Earthquake** | **Earthquake-resistant construction**; **reframing building codes and enforcing them** (IS 1893, NBC) |
| **Cyclone** | **Planting trees (shelter belts, mangroves) along the coast**; cyclone shelters; IMD warnings |
| **Flood** | **Desilting/dredging**, afforestation, check dams, diversion levees, decongesting channels, depopulating flood plains – **not "deindustrialisation"**; **basements** worsen flood damage |
| Landslide | Regulate settlements; retaining walls; hazard zonation |
| Drought | Water conservation, check dams |
- **Strict enforcement of building codes** = a mitigation strategy; **tsunamis** are especially hard to mitigate.
- **CWC flood bulletins**: **Orange** = severe – above danger level but **below HFL** (incl. within 0.5 m of it); **Red** = extreme – HFL reached or crossed.
# Earthquake-safe buildings
- Plan: **simple rectangular** (symmetric) – not star, oval or circular; RCC with **small windows and openings away from pillars**; door/window openings **small and centrally located**.
- Roofs: a **pyramidal (hipped)** roof reduces wind force; heavy roofs are bad.
- **Quake Safe guidelines** (APPSC key): buildings with **fire-fighting equipment in working order**.
- After a quake use a **battery torch** (gas leaks – no candles or flames); outdoors stay **in an open field away from buildings**, trees, bridges.
- **Seismic Vulnerability Assessment of Building Types in India** (NDMA, 2013): building-type catalogue and vulnerability functions – coordinated by **IIT Bombay**.
- **CFLOWS-Chennai** (Coastal Flood Warning System app, 2019): developed by **IIT Bombay** with NCCR (MoES).
- **Occupational hazard identification and risk analysis** standards: **Bureau of Indian Standards** (IS 15656).
- **Stampede-free fair**: proper crowd control with police at various places.
# First aid
- Action plan **DRABC** = Danger, Response, **Airway**, Breathing, Circulation; **ABC** of life support: **A = Airway**.
- Golden rule: **Be calm – don't panic**.
> Mitigation PYQs are matching "hazard → measure"; earthquake-design items come from APPSC AEE/technical papers.

=== Man-made Disasters – Bhopal, Chernobyl and Industrial Accidents
| Event | Year | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhopal gas tragedy** | **2–3 December 1984** | **Methyl isocyanate (MIC)** from **Union Carbide** pesticide plant – a **chemical** (man-made) disaster |
| Great Smog of London | 1952 | Air pollution |
| Seveso (Italy) | 1976 | Dioxin release |
| Three Mile Island | 1979 | USA, partial meltdown |
| **Chernobyl** | **26 April 1986** | **Nuclear reactor accident**, **Ukraine** (then USSR); INES level 7 |
| Kumbakonam school fire | 16 July 2004 | Tamil Nadu – 94 children died |
| LG Polymers, Visakhapatnam | May 2020 | Styrene vapour |
- MIC was used at Bhopal to make **pesticides** (carbaryl/Sevin); "NIC, OIC, CFC, chlorine, ammonia, phosgene" are the distractors.
- Chemical accidents: MSIHC Rules 1989 + CAEPPR Rules 1996; nodal ministry **MoEFCC**.
- **Rail-accident prevention**: replace over-aged tracks and bridges, fire sensing in coaches, road over/under bridges – **watershed structures are not** a rail measure.
- **Nuclear explosion is not a natural disaster** (CBRN; CCS handles NBC emergencies).
> Bhopal = MIC, 1984 – asked about 20 times in many forms.

=== Heat Waves, Cold Waves and Community DRR
- **Heat wave**: March–June; IMD forecasts; NDMA heat-wave action-plan guidelines (updated 2019); Heat Action Plans by States/cities; cool roofs; adjusted working hours = administrative measure.
- **Cold wave**: November–March; NDMA Guidelines on Cold Wave and Frost **2021**; cold day = max departure −4.5°C to −6.4°C.
- **Community DRR**: community = first responder; NDMA Disability-inclusive DRR guidelines **2019**; School Safety; gender mainstreaming (10-Point Agenda); 20 Model GPs in PRIs; social media complements, not replaces, official systems.
- 2004 tsunami: up to **four times as many women as men** died in some areas.
- Tsunami: Tsunami Ready villages – 24 in Odisha (UNESCO-IOC); ITEWC at INCOIS Hyderabad. ISRO's DMS programme and NDEM support all hazards.
