=== Meaning, Definition and Levels of Biodiversity
- Term **"biodiversity"** coined by **Walter G. Rosen (1985)**; **popularised by E.O. Wilson** – not Tansley (coined "ecosystem"), Darwin or Barrow.
- **Biodiversity** = the range/variety of **different species** in an environment; includes **genetic diversity** within populations as well as the diversity of **species and ecosystems** (not atmosphere, lithosphere or "environment").
- **CBD (1992) definition:** variability among living organisms from **all sources** (terrestrial, marine, other aquatic) and the **ecological complexes** of which they are part – **within species, between species and of ecosystems**.
| Level | Meaning / example |
|---|---|
| **Genetic** | Variation **within a species** – the basic source; all rice varieties are *Oryza sativa* but differ in size, aroma, nutrients; lets a population **adapt and respond to natural selection** |
| **Species** | Variety of species in an area – **richness + abundance** |
| **Ecosystem (ecological)** | Variety of habitats/ecosystems in a region; one ecosystem **cannot replace** another |
- **Narrow genetic base:** the cultivated **banana** (seedless, vegetatively propagated Cavendish clones) may be wiped out by disease – a classic case for genetic diversity.
- **Crop diversity in space and time** = **polyculture** (mixed/inter-cropping and rotation), not monoculture.
- Chinese, Americans, Indians and Black Africans = **one species, *Homo sapiens*** (tigers–lions–cats or pigeons–doves–partridges are different species).
- **Biomes:** no two biomes are exactly alike because **climate and geographic location** give unique combinations of plants, animals and soils (A and R true, R explains A).

=== Measuring Biodiversity – Alpha, Beta, Gamma
| Measure | Scale |
|---|---|
| **Alpha (α)** | **Within one habitat/area** at a local scale – mean species diversity / **species richness** there |
| **Beta (β)** | **Comparison between ecosystems** – change (turnover) in species composition along a gradient |
| **Gamma (γ)** | **Overall diversity across the ecosystems of a whole region** (total geographical area) |
| **Endemism** | Species **restricted to a specific region** |
| Species richness / evenness | **Number of species** / their **relative abundance** (low evenness = few species dominate) |
- Terms alpha, beta, gamma by **R.H. Whittaker**; indices: **Shannon-Wiener** and **Simpson**.
- Forest A (2 tigers, 6 deer, 7 rabbits) vs B (1, 6, 8): **same richness, A more even**.
| Measure | Key characteristic (match, NTRUHS) |
|---|---|
| Phylogenetic diversity | **Evolutionary relationships** between species |
| Ecological diversity | Diversity of **habitats/ecosystems** in a region |
| Genetic diversity | Variety of **genetic traits within species** populations |
| Species diversity | Species **richness and evenness** in a community |
- **Functional diversity:** range of **ecological roles** performed by organisms.
- Field sampling: **mist nets** capture **birds and bats**; dipnets, creel surveys and seines are aquatic methods.
> Alpha index asked twice (RAS 2018); two match questions (NTRUHS Mains).

=== Richness Patterns – Latitude, Altitude, Himalaya
- **Latitudinal gradient:** biodiversity **increases towards the equator** and falls towards the poles – greatest in **lower latitudes** and, along mountains, in **lower altitudes** (UPSC 2011: both true).
- Ants: ~**10 species near the North Pole vs ~2,000 at the equator**.
- Richest: **tropical rainforests** (land), **coral reefs** (sea).
- Tropics richest: **long undisturbed evolutionary time (no glaciation)**, stable climate, more solar energy → productivity.
- **Butterflies:** maximum species in **tropical countries**; books and keys give "butterflies cannot tolerate low temperatures" as the reason (A and R true, R explains A).
- **Himalaya** rich because it is a **confluence of different biogeographical zones** (Palearctic + Indo-Malayan) – not rainfall, absence of invasives or low human interference.
- Estimates: about **1.5 million** living species described (+300,000 fossil); **Wilson (1992) 10–50 million**; **Robert May ~7 million**. **Over 70%** of recorded species are animals (**insects** richest); plants ~**22%**.
- **Species-area relationship** (**Humboldt**, log S = log C + Z log A).

=== Values of Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services
| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Consumptive | Used locally, not sold: fuelwood, fodder, wild food |
| Productive | Sold commercially: timber, **fibre**, medicines, genetic material |
| **Ecosystem services** | **Soil formation, prevention of soil erosion, recycling of waste, pollination**, clean air and water, climate |
| Social / cultural | Sacred groves, indigenous knowledge |
| Aesthetic | Beauty, **ecotourism** |
| Option | Unknown future uses – new drugs, crop genes |
- **Most significant aspect:** **maintenance of the ecosystem**; role in sustainable development: **supports ecosystem services such as food, water and air quality**.
- **Butterfly decline (UPSC 2017):** pollination of some plants affected ✓; fall in populations of **wasps, spiders and birds** that eat them ✓; fungal infections ✗ → **I and III**.
- **Himalayan nettle** (*Girardinia diversifolia*): sustainable source of **textile fibre** (UPSC 2019).
- **Rivet popper hypothesis** (**Paul Ehrlich**): losing a key rivet (**keystone species**) may collapse the ecosystem.
- **TEEB:** **G8+5** initiative, **2007**; hosted by **UNEP** (not IMF/WEF); led by **Pavan Sukhdev**; draws attention to the **economic benefits of biodiversity** and helps decision-makers **recognise, demonstrate and capture** its value → **2 and 3 only**.
- **Dasgupta Review (2021):** **Partha Dasgupta** for the **UK Treasury**; nature as an asset.
- **International Day for Biological Diversity: 22 May** (22 March = World Water Day). CBD in force **29 Dec 1993**.
- **National Biodiversity Congress, Thiruvananthapuram (Feb 2017):** focal theme **"Mainstreaming Biodiversity for Sustainable Development"**.

| Zone | Note |
|---|---|

| **Deccan Peninsula** | **Largest zone** (~42%); **5 provinces**; **Andhra Pradesh** lies here |

- **N.I. Vavilov:** **8 centres** of origin; **Hindustan (Indo-Burma):** rice, sugarcane, mango, pigeon pea, brinjal, cucumber, black pepper.
- **Nokrek** (Garo Hills, Meghalaya) = **Citrus Gene Sanctuary**; **Borjuli** (Assam, BHS) = *Oryza rufipogon*, wild ancestor of **rice**.

|---|---|

- **Eastern Ghats:** **Mahanadi to the Nilgiris**; highest peak **Arma Konda (~1,680 m, ASR district)**; **Mahendra Girulu** in **Srikakulam** (not Anantapur); **not a global hotspot**.

- **Grey slender loris:** **dry Eastern Ghats scrub**.
- **APSBB:** ~**13,000 BMCs**; **5% of Red Sanders profits** to farmers.
- **Mangrove cover (ISFR):** Andhra Pradesh **fourth** among States/UTs (after West Bengal, Gujarat, A&N).
