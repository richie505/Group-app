=== India's Four Hotspots – Which Region Is, Which Is Not
| Hotspot | Indian part | Shared with |
|---|---|---|
| **Himalaya** | Entire Indian Himalaya – **Eastern Himalaya** (Sikkim, Arunachal, N. West Bengal) and Western Himalaya | Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, Tibet, Myanmar |
| **Indo-Burma** | **North-East** (south of the Brahmaputra) + **Andaman Islands** | Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam, S. China (Yunnan) |
| **Western Ghats and Sri Lanka** | Entire **Western Ghats** (Sahyadri) | Sri Lanka (one single hotspot) |
| **Sundaland** | **Only the Nicobar Islands** | Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Brunei |
- **Andamans = Indo-Burma; Nicobars = Sundaland** – "Andaman group is part of Sundaland" is the INCORRECT statement (APPSC NTRUHS). Indo-Burma does **not** cover western India.
- The Eastern Himalaya was first counted in Indo-Burma; the **2004 reappraisal** split it into Indo-Burma and the newly defined **Himalaya** hotspot.
- **Old framing (25 hotspots, 1999–2000):** India had **two** – the **North-East / Eastern Himalaya** and the **Western Ghats**. So "two hotspots in India" = **Eastern Himalayas and Western Ghats** (APPSC G1, RAS 2008, UPPCS 2012, RAS 2013); now **four**.
| Region | Global hotspot? |
|---|---|
| Western Ghats, Eastern Himalaya, North-East, Andaman (Indo-Burma), Nicobar (Sundaland) | **Yes** |
| **Eastern Ghats** | **No** (rich in endemics, not a hotspot) |
| Aravallis, **Vindhyas**, Satpuras, Shivaliks | No |
| **Thar Desert**, Deccan Plateau, Central India | No |
| **Sundarbans**, Manas, Cherrapunji, **Gangetic Plains**, Gujarat | No |
| **Naro Hills** (MP) | No – not a globally accepted hotspot (MPPSC 2023) |
- **"India has four hotspots i.e. Eastern Himalayas, Western Himalayas, Western Ghats and Andaman & Nicobar"** (IAS 2010) is **wrong**: the four are Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats–Sri Lanka, Sundaland. With "hotspots only in tropics" also false → **neither** (NextGen, PMF).
- Threats by hotspot: **Western Ghats** – monocultures, invasive species, tourism and infrastructure, hydropower, fires; **Himalaya** – young fragile geology, **landslides**, infrastructure; **Indo-Burma** – **jhum** (shifting cultivation), logging, hunting; **Sundaland/Nicobar** – invasives, the **2004 tsunami**.
> Most-asked angle (≈17 PYQs): pick the hotspot – almost always **Western Ghats** or **Eastern Himalaya**; the odd one out is a desert, the **Eastern Ghats**, the Vindhyas or a plain.

=== Hotspot Concept, Criteria and Count
- **Definition:** a biogeographic region with a significant reservoir of biodiversity that is **threatened with destruction** – i.e. **exceptional (endemic) plant diversity + serious habitat loss**, not just "maximum biodiversity".
- Concept introduced by **Norman Myers (British ecologist), 1988**; adopted by **Conservation International (CI) in 1989**, which **reassessed** it from 1996. **Not** BirdLife International, IUCN or WWF.
- Basis: **richness, endemism and threat**. **Habitat destruction** is the main cause of biodiversity loss.
| Criterion (both needed – "two strict criteria") | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Endemism** | At least **1,500 endemic vascular plant species** (over **0.5%** of world total) |
| **Threat** | Lost at least **70%** of original habitat (30% or less natural vegetation left) – **not 90%** |
- **UPSC 2011:** Western Ghats–Sri Lanka and Indo-Burma recognised for **species richness, endemism, threat perception** (1, 3 and 5) – not vegetation density, ethno-botany or adaptation.
- **APPSC Group 2:** hotspots are characterised by **endemic flowering plants and threat perception**.
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1988 | Myers proposes the concept |
| 1989 | CI adopts it |
| 1999 | CI identifies **25** hotspots (numerical thresholds; Myers et al., 2000) |
| 2011 | 35th: Forests of East Australia |
| 2016 | **36th: North American Coastal Plain** |
- Count: 25 → 34 → 35 → **36** (present; "as of 2023" also 36).
- Once **15.7%** of Earth's land; about **86%** of that habitat lost; now only about **2.3–2.5%** of land.
- Hold about **50% of vascular plants** (1,50,000 species) and **42% of terrestrial vertebrates** as endemics.
- Most lie in the **tropics**, but hotspots are **not only tropical**: **temperate** ones include the **Mediterranean Basin** (incl. the **Eastern Mediterranean**), California Floristic Province, North American Coastal Plain. **North-western Australia is not** a hotspot (the Australian ones are SW Australia and East Australian forests) → UPSC 2009: **Eastern Himalayas and Eastern Mediterranean only**.
- **Hottest hotspots:** **8** with highest endemism under greatest threat; include **Western Ghats–Sri Lanka**, Indo-Burma, Sundaland, Madagascar, Philippines.
- **Coldspots:** relatively low diversity but losing habitat fast; still matter for rare species, ecosystem functions and genetic diversity.
| Concept | Body | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Biodiversity hotspot | Conservation International (Myers) | 1,500+ endemic plants and 70%+ habitat lost; large regions |
| Key Biodiversity Area | IUCN-led partnership (global standard, 2016) | Single sites important for global persistence of biodiversity |
| Global 200 | WWF | Priority ecoregions (terrestrial, freshwater, marine) chosen for representativeness |
| Important Bird and Biodiversity Areas (IBAs) | **BirdLife International** (a global partnership of conservation NGOs) | Bird sites – BirdLife did **not** originate the hotspot concept (UPSC 2015) |
- **India's share (TSPSC 2023):** **2.4%** of world land, about **7–8%** of recorded species; main threat = habitat loss and fragmentation (urbanisation, infrastructure, deforestation, pollution, climate change).

=== Western Ghats – India's Richest Biodiversity Region
- Also **Sahyadri**; about **1,600 km** through **6 States**: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu (not AP).
- "**Water tower of peninsular India**": feeds Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery. One of the **8 hottest hotspots**; the **most biodiversity-rich / "biodiversity-saturated" area of India** in PYQs (UPPSC 2015, 2019).
- **Silent Valley NP** (Nilgiri BR, Kerala) – the "richest biodiversity" answer among valleys (CGPSC 2011), against Kashmir valley, Valley of Flowers, Surma valley.
- About **5,000** vascular plants, **1,700 (34%)** endemic, **58 endemic genera**; ~433 plant species shared only with Sri Lanka.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site 2012**: serial site of **39 component areas in 7 clusters**. IUCN 2025 World Heritage Outlook: "**significant concern**".
- **New species:** **Feb 2017** – Delhi University (S.D. Biju) described **7 new night frogs (Nyctibatrachus)**, **4 of them miniature** (12–15 mm).
| Panel | Year | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| WGEEP – **Madhav Gadgil** (set up by MoEF 2010, report 2011) | 2010 | **Whole Ghats as ESA**, graded ESZ I–III; 142 talukas in 44 districts |
| HLWG – **K. Kasturirangan** | 2012 | Set up after State opposition; ESA limited to about **37%** (~60,000 sq km) |
- MoEF&CC issued **draft** ESA notifications across six States (2018 onwards, still not final); legal force on notification under the **EP Act, 1986**.
- Supreme Court ordered eviction of encroachers in **Agasthyamalai Biosphere Reserve** (Kerala–TN; UNESCO MAB 2016).
> Gadgil and Kasturirangan reports = **protection of the Western Ghats** (UPSC 2016; APPSC 2019).

=== Biological Diversity Act 2002 and 2023 Amendment
- Gives effect to **CBD (1992)**; passed by Parliament in **December 2002** (Lok Sabha 2 Dec, Rajya Sabha 11 Dec); Presidential assent **5 February 2003** (Act 18 of 2003).
| Tier | Body | Role |
|---|---|---|
| National | **NBA, Chennai** (2003) | Foreign/commercial access and IPR; advises Centre; civil court powers |
| State | SBBs; UT Biodiversity Councils | Commercial use by Indian entities |
| Local | BMCs | Document biodiversity, prepare PBRs, levy collection fees |
- **Section 37:** Biodiversity Heritage Sites. **Section 38:** threatened species (1,500+ plants declared by States).
- **Amendment Act 2023** (in force **1 April 2024**): offences **decriminalised** (penalties only); **AYUSH** practitioners and codified traditional knowledge exempt from prior intimation; cultivated medicinal plants exempt.
- **Biological Diversity Rules, 2024** replace the 2004 Rules, with a statutory National Biodiversity Authority Fund.

=== BMCs, PBRs and Biodiversity Heritage Sites
- **PBRs are prepared by BMCs** (APPSC 2019). About **2.62 lakh BMCs** (2024); ~**2.72 lakh PBRs** (Jun 2026).
- BMCs are key to **Nagoya Protocol** objectives and can levy collection fees (UPSC 2023).
- **BHS:** notified by the **State Government** in consultation with local bodies (Section 37(1)); no change to ownership or livelihood rights; traditional uses continue.
- Count: **54** (NBA, March 2026); 45 (Feb 2024) → 48 (Apr 2025).

=== Access and Benefit Sharing
- Commercial benefits from bioresources shared with providing communities; covers plant, animal, microbial resources and traditional knowledge; **excludes human genetic material**. CBD 1992 → **Nagoya Protocol 2010**.
- **Kani model:** Kani tribe's knowledge of **Arogyapacha** (Trichopus zeylanicus) → **TBGRI** drug **Jeevani** (anti-fatigue).
- ABS raised over **₹266 crore** since 2008: **Red Sanders 45%** (₹120 crore), seed sector 32.3%.
- Repository rule: **25–40%** to repository, **60–75%** to local communities via SBBs/UTBCs.
- **AP:** APSBB vision of **zero biodiversity loss by 2050**; Red Sanders makes AP vulnerable to biopiracy and the largest ABS earner.
