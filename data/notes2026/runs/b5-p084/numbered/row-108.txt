## SUB 0 | Genetics and DNA – Basics and Discoverers
[0:0] # DNA, genes and heredity (asked ~20 times)
[0:1] - **Genetics** = study of genes, **heredity and variation** (not only Mendel's laws or DNA structure).
[0:2] - **Gene** = the **unit of heredity** (a DNA segment coding for a trait); **DNA** controls hereditary characters; **chromosome** and nucleus only carry it.
[0:3] - **Nucleic acids (DNA, RNA)**: **storage and expression of genetic information** – not oxygen transport, energy or body temperature.
[0:4] - **DNA bases: adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine**; **RNA has uracil in place of thymine** (A and G occur in both).
[0:5] - **RNA** is usually **single-stranded** (a "single helix"); DNA is a **double helix**.
[0:6] - DNA occurs in the **nucleus** and also in **mitochondria and chloroplasts** (they have their own DNA).
[0:7] - **Histones** = basic proteins in the **nucleus**; DNA wraps around them (nucleosomes, chromatin).
[0:8] - DNA can store and pass on information because its two strands are **complementary** (A–T, G–C): each strand is a template (UPSC 2001).
[0:9] - **Mutation** = change in the **base sequence** of a gene (transversion is only one kind of substitution).
[0:10] - **Phenotype** = observable traits; **genotype** = genetic make-up.
[0:11] - **Genetic code**: triplet codons for **20 amino acids**; non-overlapping, non-ambiguous, commaless; has **start and stop codons**; "universal" – nearly so (a few mitochondrial exceptions).
[0:12] - **Gene mapping** (position of genes on a chromosome) helps trace **livestock pedigree** and breed **disease-resistant animals**; it cannot explain "all human diseases" (UPSC 2011 → I and III).
[0:13] # Discoverers

  [0:14.1] Friedrich Miescher | First isolated DNA ("nuclein"), 1869

## SUB 2 | Medical Biotechnology – Stem Cells, Antibodies, Gene Therapy
[2:0] # Antibodies and hybridoma (asked 5 times)
[2:1] - **Hybridoma technique: Georges Köhler and César Milstein, 1975** – fusion of an antibody-making **B cell with a myeloma cell**; used for commercial production of **monoclonal antibodies** (not interferon or antibiotics).
[2:2] - **Monoclonal antibodies**: **man-made (lab) proteins** that bind one specific antigen and so modulate immune function; used against viral infections such as **Nipah** (m102.4) – UPSC 2025: **I, II and III**.
[2:3] - **Plasma cells** (from B cells) **form antibodies**.
[2:4] # Stem cells and transplants
[2:5] - **Stem cells** divide, self-renew and can become **any/many cell types**; focus of research on serious diseases (endodermal, ectodermal, muscle cells are already specialised).
[2:6] - **Bone-marrow stem cells** = the bioethically non-controversial alternative to embryonic stem cells. **Mesenchymal stem cells** were first found in **bone marrow** (a place, not a person).
[2:7] - National Guidelines for Stem Cell Research: **2017**.
[2:8] - **First human heart transplant: Christian Barnard, South Africa, 1967** (William Harvey = blood circulation).
[2:9] - **Test-tube baby (IVF):** ovum **fertilised in a test tube**, embryo **develops in the uterus**.
[2:10] # Genetic disease and gene therapy
[2:11] - **Haemophilia**: **hereditary**, **X-linked (sex-linked) recessive** clotting defect – **carried by women, appears in men** ("royal haemophilia", Queen Victoria); not caused by bacteria, virus or pollutants. Tay-Sachs and cystic fibrosis are autosomal.

## SUB 3 | DNA Fingerprinting
[3:0] # Basis and uses (asked 15 times)
[3:1] - **Developed by Alec Jeffreys (1984)**; in India pioneered by **Lalji Singh** (CCMB) – father of Indian DNA fingerprinting.
[3:2] - **Basis: DNA polymorphism** – highly variable repetitive regions (**VNTRs**); most DNA is the same in all people. Not the double helix, replication or "errors in base sequence".
[3:3] - Tissues of the same person show the **same pattern**; polymorphisms are **inherited** (child gets half from each parent) → **paternity**.
[3:4] - **Technique: Southern blotting** of DNA fragments (ELISA, RIA = antigen–antibody tests; Northern blot = RNA).
[3:5] - **Uses:** identity, **paternity** (not protein analysis, chromosome counting, gene therapy, cloning or rDNA), **forensic** cases, **rape and assault** (trace hair, saliva, dried semen are enough – A and R true, R explains A), **conservation** – "all of the above"; WWF-India's anti-poaching tool for **rhinos** (RhODIS – rhino DNA index).
[3:6] - **Samples:** blood, bone, hair **with root**, saliva, skin, semen – all usable; **burnt bones** lose usable DNA. **Biochips** = a technique, not a sample.
[3:7] - **First crime solved:** **England** – the murders of **1983** and 1986 (solved 1987–88).
[3:8] - **CDFD** (Centre for DNA Fingerprinting and Diagnostics): **Hyderabad**, under DBT.

## SUB 5 | Tools, Omics and Identification Techniques
[5:0] TABLE head: Tool | Use
  [5:0.0] PCR | Amplifies DNA; key enzyme DNA polymerase; denaturation → annealing → extension
  [5:0.1] Gel electrophoresis | Separates DNA by size
  [5:0.2] Restriction enzymes | Cut DNA ("molecular scissors") – EcoRI, BamHI, HindIII
  [5:0.3] Ligase | Only joins DNA
  [5:0.4] Plasmids | Vectors (pBR322)
  [5:0.5] Bioreactors | Large-scale culture
  [5:0.6] CRISPR–Cas9 | Genome editing
[5:1] - **Genome**: complete genetic information; zygote genome = DNA of **two gametes**; **human genome ≈ 3 billion bases**, over 99% shared.
[5:2] - **DNA sequencing** finds the base order (A, G, C, T); genetic information depends on the order of bases (A and R true, R explains A).
[5:3] - **Transcriptome**: the **full range of mRNA** molecules expressed by an organism; **varies with conditions**, unlike the genome (UPSC 2016).
[5:4] - **Microsatellite DNA** (short tandem repeats): **evolutionary relationships** among species, genetic diversity (UPSC 2023).
[5:5] - **DNA barcoding**: **species identification** from a short standard gene region (animals: mitochondrial **COI**) – distinguishes **look-alike species** and detects **undesirable material in processed food**; it **cannot tell age** (UPSC 2022 → B and C).
[5:6] - **Serological tests** (antigen–antibody reactions): **identification** – blood groups, pathogens and serotypes (**Widal, ELISA**); also used in serotaxonomy.

## SUB 6 | Nature and Scope of Biotechnology
[6:0] - **Biotechnology** = **industrial use of living organisms**, substances from them or their processes (e.g. yeast for liquor) (IAS 1993); CBD (1992): use of **biological systems** to **make or modify products**.
[6:1] - Traditional biotech: **curd**, bread, wine, vinegar; modern: rDNA, PCR, cloning, CRISPR. **Earliest biotechnologists: farmers**.
[6:2] - Applications: agriculture (GM crops), medicine (drugs, vaccines, diagnostics, gene therapy), industry (microbial products).
[6:3] TABLE head: Colour | Field
  [6:3.0] Red | Medical
  [6:3.1] Green | Agricultural
  [6:3.2] White | Industrial – enzymes, biofuels, bioplastics (NOT marine)
  [6:3.3] Blue | Marine
[6:4] - **Enzymes** = biological catalysts of reactions in living systems (not bacteria or DNA).
[6:5] - **Artificial enzymes (synzymes)** are made by **genetic/protein engineering** and by **synthesis of host molecules such as crown ethers** and cyclodextrins; **denaturing** proteins destroys enzyme activity (AEE 2019 → a and b).
[6:6] - **Bionics** = using properties of biological systems in the mechanical world; **bionomics** = organism and environment; **bionomy** = laws of life; **biometry** = statistics of biology.

## SUB 9 | Bioremediation – Meaning and Methods
[9:0] - **Bioremediation**: removal or degradation of pollutants by **organisms or their enzymes**; covers soil, water, air – oil spills, heavy metals, pesticides, solvents; engineered microbes may be used. **Slower but cheaper** than conventional (thermal, chemical) clean-up, no secondary pollution; results vary with pollutant and site.
[9:1] - Not bioremediation: burning/incineration (thermal), **biomagnification** (DDT, mercury rising up food chains), **eutrophication** (nutrient-driven algal blooms), biofortification.
[9:2] TABLE head: Basis | In-situ | Ex-situ
  [9:2.0] Site | At the site, no excavation; passive, cheapest, slow | Excavated, treated elsewhere
  [9:2.1] Techniques | Bioventing, biosparging, bioaugmentation | Landfarming, composting, biopiles, bioreactors (bioreactors fastest)
[9:3] # Techniques
[9:4] TABLE head: Technique | How / target
  [9:4.0] Bioaugmentation | Adds chosen degrading microbes – oil spills
  [9:4.1] Biostimulation | Adds N, P, oxygen to native microbes – polluted lakes
  [9:4.2] Bioventing | Air/nutrients via wells into soil
  [9:4.3] Biosparging | Air injected below the water table – groundwater
  [9:4.4] Mycoremediation | Fungi – organic pollutants
  [9:4.5] Rhizofiltration | Roots absorb/precipitate contaminants from water
