## SUB 0 | Fertilisers, Plant Nutrients, Manures and Soil Health
[0:0] # Plant nutrients
[0:1] TABLE head: Group | Elements
  [0:1.0] Macronutrients (9) | C, H, O, N, P, K (primary), Ca, Mg, S (secondary)
  [0:1.1] Micronutrients (8) | Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo, Cl, Ni – trace amounts (< 1% of dry weight)
[0:2] - **Magnesium is a macronutrient** (secondary), not a micronutrient; **manganese and zinc are micronutrients** supplied by the soil.
[0:3] - Inorganic fertilisers supply the macronutrients **nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium (NPK)**; desirable soil NPK use ratio in India **4:2:1**.
[0:4] - **Zinc deficiency in maize = "white bud" / white tip of young leaves**; **Khaira** in rice is also zinc deficiency.
[0:5] - **Excess nitrogen delays maturity** (lush vegetative growth, lodging); phosphorus **hastens** maturity.
[0:6] # Fertiliser facts
[0:7] TABLE head: Fertiliser | Fact
  [0:7.0] Urea CO(NH2)2 | ~46% N – highest of common solid N fertilisers; nitrogen in amide form; 100/46 ≈ 2.2 kg urea gives 1 kg N; most popular for foliar spray (high solubility)
  [0:7.1] Ammonium nitrate / CAN / ammonium sulphate | 34% / 26% / 20.6% N
  [0:7.2] DAP (NH4)2HPO4 | 18% N, 46% P2O5 – balanced N + P supply
  [0:7.3] Superphosphate | Calcium dihydrogen phosphate (+ gypsum); acidic, so continued use lowers soil pH (< 7)
  [0:7.4] Potassic (MOP, SOP) | Disease and drought resistance, quality
  [0:7.5] Phosphatic | Root development, flowering, fruiting
  [0:7.6] Neem-coated urea | Slows dissolution of urea and nitrification → slow N release, higher N-use efficiency
[0:8] - Urea → ammoniacal (NH4+) → nitrate in soil; **ammoniacal N is preferred for paddy and potato** (held by soil, less lost in flooded fields).
[0:9] - Nitrogen is the main fertiliser for **wheat** (vegetative growth).
[0:10] - **Nitrogen-use efficiency** is raised by **split application, nitrification inhibitors and slow-release N fertilisers** (all of these).
[0:11] - **Balanced fertilisation** raises production, improves fertiliser-use efficiency and maintains soil productivity (all of these); legumes need little N, so their NPK is P- and K-heavy (exam key **1:2:2**).
[0:12] - Excess N: **soil acidification, nitrate leaching, eutrophication**; continuous chemical use: **lower soil organic matter**, fewer microbes, lower nutrient density in food.
[0:13] - Broadcasting granular fertiliser loses **50-70%** of the nitrogen; **PM-PRANAM**: States keep **50%** of the subsidy saved, as grants.

[0:16] # Organic manures and amendments
[0:17] TABLE head: Input | Meaning
  [0:17.0] Animal manure (FYM) | Dung, urine and litter of farm animals
  [0:17.1] Compost | Decomposed plant and animal waste – raises organic matter and microbial activity
  [0:17.2] Crop residue | Remains of harvested crops worked back into the soil
  [0:17.3] Green manure | Fresh crops (mostly legumes) ploughed in green – sunhemp (most suitable), dhaincha, sesbania, cowpea

[0:18] - **Biochar (UPSC 2020 – all three):** used in the **growing medium of vertical farming**, **promotes nitrogen-fixing and mycorrhizal microbes**, and **retains water** longer; also cuts leaching and acidity and stores carbon for millennia.
[0:19] - **Earthworms** improve soil fertility – **"farmers' friends"**; an earthworm has **more than two pairs** of "hearts" (aortic arches – 4 pairs in Pheretima).
[0:20] - **Humus** = dark **organic colloid** from decayed plant and animal matter.
[0:21] - AP plans **1,500 MT/day** of manure from **107 waste-processing centres** (Feb 2026); Kharif 2026 green-manure subsidy Rs 62.20 crore, **sunn hemp 50,311 quintals**.

## SUB 1 | Biofertilisers
[1:0] - **Biofertilisers** = living microbial inoculants (bacteria, algae, fungi) that **fix nitrogen, solubilise phosphorus** or decompose organic matter; they are **applied to seed/soil** (not only leaves), **do not alter soil chemistry** like chemicals, and **can be used with organic manures**.
[1:1] TABLE head: Biofertiliser | Type | Crop / role
  [1:1.0] Rhizobium | Symbiotic bacterium | Legumes (pea, pulses) – root nodules fix N2
  [1:1.1] Azotobacter | Free-living aerobic bacterium | Cereals – non-symbiotic N fixation
  [1:1.2] Azospirillum | Associative bacterium | Cereal roots (rice rhizosphere), no nodules
  [1:1.3] Blue-green algae (Anabaena, Nostoc) | Cyanobacteria | Paddy – fix atmospheric N into a form crops absorb (heterocysts)
  [1:1.4] Azolla | Aquatic fern with the BGA Anabaena | Water-logged rice fields; 2-3% N; often used with BGA
  [1:1.5] PSB | Bacteria | Solubilise phosphate
  [1:1.6] VAM / Mycorrhiza | Fungus–root association | Phosphatic biofertiliser (P uptake)
[1:2] - **Mycorrhiza (UPSC 2013 – all three):** increases **absorptive area / drought resistance**, helps plants **tolerate extreme pH**, and **resists disease** – hence used to rehabilitate degraded sites.
[1:3] - **Legumes** (pea) enrich soil with nitrogen; wheat, coffee, rubber, potato, sorghum do not.
[1:4] - Rhizobium, BGA and Azolla are **all** biofertilisers; **alfalfa** is a legume fodder, not a biofertiliser.
[1:5] - Not biofertilisers: Salvinia, Marsilea, Pteridium (ferns), Wolffia, Trapa, Lemna (other aquatic plants).
[1:6] > Exam angle: BGA → paddy (×5), Azolla = fern + Anabaena (×4), VAM = phosphatic, mycorrhiza statements, biofertiliser statements (CDS 2010: I and IV).

## SUB 2 | Sustainable Agriculture and Environmental Impact of Modern Farming
[2:0] - **Sustainable agriculture:** produce food while **protecting the environment and conserving resources for future generations**; maintains soil fertility; may use synthetic inputs **judiciously** (unlike organic farming).
[2:1] TABLE head: Sustainable (exam answers) | Not sustainable
  [2:1.0] Crop rotation, organic farming | Excessive chemical fertilisers, overuse of pesticides
  [2:1.1] Multi-, inter-, mixed and contour cropping, green manure | Intense mono-cropping / monoculture
  [2:1.2] IPM, drip irrigation, reduced tillage | Over-irrigation
[2:2] - **Eco-friendly technology in agriculture = organic farming** (NextGen PYQ); GM crops, pesticide spraying and chemical fertilisers are not.
[2:3] - **Environmental impact (APPSC 2022, 2023, G2 Mains 2025 – I and II):** synthetic fertilisers/pesticides **pollute soil and water**; sustainable farming **prioritises soil health and biodiversity**. Modern agriculture **does** deplete freshwater, and monoculture **harms** ecosystem stability.
[2:4] - Most conspicuous effects of modern agriculture: **soil degradation, pollution and ecological imbalance – all of these**; indiscriminate agro-chemicals cause **secondary pest outbreaks, pesticide resistance** and ecosystem contamination.
[2:5] - **Rachel Carson, *Silent Spring* (1962):** pesticides (DDT) harming wildlife and the environment → a founding text of **environmental protection**.
[2:6] - **GACSA** (Global Alliance for Climate-Smart Agriculture): launched at the UN Climate Summit, New York, **2014**; facilitated by **FAO**; voluntary – **membership creates no binding obligations** (UPSC 2018: 2 only).
[2:7] - Agricultural science covers crop and soil cultivation and animal products; **rainwater harvesting** is a water-conservation technique (APPSC ACF key: NOT part of agricultural science technology).
[2:8] - **Carbosulfan** – Centre proposed a ban (29 Aug 2026) under the **Insecticides Act, 1968**: breaks down to highly toxic **carbofuran**, harms birds and aquatic life.
[2:9] - **Evergreen revolution** (M.S. Swaminathan): ZBNF, organic farming, water conservation, IPM.

## SUB 4 | Organic Farming – Sikkim, Schemes and Certification
[4:0] - **Organic farming** avoids synthetic fertilisers, pesticides, growth regulators and feed additives; relies on crop rotation, residues, manures, legumes, green manure, biofertilisers and biological pest control.
[4:1] - Main advantage: **reduced chemical input and improved soil health** – not guaranteed higher yields (yield dip of 2-3 years in transition).
[4:2] # Sikkim
[4:3] - **Sikkim = India's first fully organic State (January 2016)** and the **world's first 100% organic State**; **FAO Future Policy (Gold) Award 2018**; Lakshadweep = first organic UT.
[4:4] - NE States pursuing organic status: **Sikkim, Meghalaya, Nagaland**; **Assam is not one of them**.
[4:5] # Schemes and certification

  [4:6.0] PKVY (Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana) | 2015; organic farming by cluster approach (50 farmers / 50 acres) + PGS certification; AP's organic initiative among PMFBY/NFSM/RKVY options; portal jaivikkheti.in

## SUB 5 | Natural Farming – ZBNF, APCNF and NMNF
[5:0] # Zero Budget Natural Farming
[5:1] - **ZBNF** was developed by **Subhash Palekar** (Maharashtra; Padma Shri 2016) in the mid-1990s as an alternative to Green Revolution inputs; spread first through **Karnataka** (farmers' movement; general knowledge); in Union Budget 2019-20.
[5:2] - **Chemical fertilisers are not used** – on-farm **cow dung, urine, jaggery** preparations replace them; Palekar **opposes vermicompost**.
[5:3] TABLE head: Input / practice | Meaning
  [5:3.0] Jeevamrutha | Fermented microbial culture from cow dung and urine
  [5:3.1] Beejamrutha | Seed treatment
  [5:3.2] Achhadana | Mulching
  [5:3.3] Whapasa | Soil aeration and moisture
  [5:3.4] Agniastra, Brahmastra, Neemastra | Pest-management preparations
[5:4] - ZBNF promoted through **farmer collectives / organic clusters** (village clusters, SHGs).
[5:5] - **Natural vs organic:** natural farming uses on-farm inputs, avoids ploughing and bulk manures, is near zero-cost and uncertified; organic farming uses approved (often bought) inputs and is certified (PGS/NPOP).
[5:6] - **Permaculture:** Bill Mollison and David Holmgren; **Fukuoka**: *One-Straw Revolution* (1975).
[5:7] # Andhra Pradesh – APCNF
[5:8] - **APCNF = Andhra Pradesh Community-managed Natural Farming** (formerly ZBNF); launched **2016** by **Rythu Sadhikara Samstha (RySS)** under the AP Agriculture Department.
[5:9] - **2018 declared AP's Year of Natural Farming.**
[5:10] - Reach (Dec 2024): **9.53 lakh farmers on 3.58 lakh ha**; 14% of farmers, 28% of villages; target **41 lakh farmers, 19 lakh ha by 2028-29**; all gram panchayats by 2026-27; technical support to 12 States.
[5:11] - Awards: **Gulbenkian Prize for Humanity 2024** (11 July 2024, Lisbon); **Food Planet Prize 2026** (USD 1.5 million, Curt Bergfors Foundation, Sweden).
[5:12] - Kurnool study: APCNF farmers about **35% less likely to use pesticides**; Kenyan delegation praised the Guntur model (July 2026); **NTR district** QR-based traceability.
[5:13] # National Mission on Natural Farming (NMNF)

## SUB 6 | Integrated Pest Management, Pests and Plant Diseases
[6:0] - **IPM (FAO):** use **all suitable techniques** to keep pest populations **below the economic injury level**; aim is to **optimise, not maximise**, control.
[6:1] - IPM = **combination of biological, cultural and mechanical methods**, chemicals only when needed (last resort); key element: **cultural control**.
[6:2] TABLE head: Component | Examples
  [6:2.0] Cultural | Crop rotation, resistant varieties, changed irrigation, trap crops
  [6:2.1] Mechanical / physical | Traps, hand collection, mulches
  [6:2.2] Biological | Predators, parasites, pathogens (green lacewing → aphids, ladybugs), NPV, pheromone traps
  [6:2.3] Chemical | Selective pesticides only when needed
[6:3] - **ICAR (2017): pests waste about 30-35% of India's annual crop yield.**
[6:4] # Plant diseases and vectors
[6:5] - Plant pathogens are spread by **birds, dust, rain and wind** (all four).
[6:6] TABLE head: Crop | Disease

  [6:6.1] Pea | Powdery mildew
  [6:6.2] Gram | Wilt
  [6:6.3] Okra | Yellow vein mosaic – virus spread by whitefly
