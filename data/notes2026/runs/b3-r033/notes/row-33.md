=== Disguised Unemployment – Meaning, Test and Where Found
- **Meaning:** **more persons are employed on a job than are actually needed** ("a job which a few can accomplish"); the extra workers appear employed but add nothing to output.
- **Test:** the **marginal productivity of labour is zero** (or negative) – **withdrawing some workers leaves total output unchanged**, so a shift of people out of agriculture **need not reduce production**.
- **Where:** mainly in **agriculture / the primary sector**, on family farms in **rural areas** (also the unorganised sector) – not in industry, factories, services, trade, banking or urban areas.
- **NCERT example:** five family members work a two-hectare plot that needs fewer hands; NCERT calls it hidden **underemployment** – people appear to be working but are not fully employed.
- **Hidden, not open:** the persons appear employed, so it is **not captured in PLFS headline unemployment rates**.
- **Nurkse:** disguised unemployment is a **potential source of saving** – surplus farm labour can be withdrawn for capital formation (roads, irrigation, railways, housing) in under-developed countries.
- Distractors: "persons with no jobs" (open), "unemployment of women / of people above 60", "fall in productivity", "change in technology", "data concealment".
> Most repeated item of the whole topic (about 25 PYQs, APPSC 1998-2025, UPSC 2013, UPPSC, SSC): meaning = more workers than needed; MPL = zero; found in agriculture (I and II = agriculture + rural). When disguised is not offered, the agriculture answer is **seasonal**.

=== Types of Unemployment – Cause and Where Predominant
| Type | Cause / feature | Example | Where predominant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seasonal** | Work only at certain times of the year | Farm labour idle in off-season months | **Agriculture**, tourism, agro-industries |
| **Disguised** | Surplus labour, **MPL = 0** | Five family members on a plot needing two | Agriculture, family farms, rural |
| **Structural** | Demand for labour short of supply; skill / technology mismatch; long-run | Labour-intensive economy turning capital-intensive | **India's basic problem** |
| **Frictional** (search) | Time between jobs; **workers moving from one job to another** | Graduate seeking a first job | Normal; **exists even at full employment** |
| **Cyclical** (Keynesian, demand-deficient) | **Fall in aggregate demand – recession / depression** | Lay-offs in a recession | Developed / capitalist economies |
| **Technological** | Change in technology, automation; a **subtype of structural** | Robots replace car workers | Manufacturing and routine services |
| **Educated** | Qualified persons cannot find matching jobs | – | **Urban** |
| Institutional | Labour laws, high minimum wages, union rules | Hiring halted due to rigid layoff rules | – |
| Vulnerable | No formal contract or social security | Construction labour, street vending | Informal sector |
| Casual / Classical | Day-to-day contract ends / wages too high (real-wage unemployment) | – | – |
- **Rural = seasonal + disguised; urban = educated** (NCERT rule). Under PLFS, open unemployment is higher in **urban** areas (AP 2023-24: rural 3.4%, urban 5.9%).
- India has **all** types – structural, seasonal, disguised and underemployment ("All the above").
- **At full employment** frictional (and structural) unemployment still exist; "functional" in an old APPSC paper is a misprint for **frictional**.
- **Capital-intensive industries cause unemployment**; a labour-intensive industry is one that **employs more hands**.
- Automation and AI reduce demand mainly for **low-skilled, routine jobs**.
- **Structural unemployment** in a developing economy arises from **inadequate productive capacity** (too little capital to employ the growing labour force), not deflation.
> Depression → cyclical (APPSC 2019); job-to-job → frictional (SSC); agriculture → disguised, or seasonal when disguised is absent (APPSC 2025).

=== Nature and Causes of Unemployment in India
- **India's problem = chronic unemployment and under-employment** of a **structural** kind (demand for labour falls short of supply), with seasonal and disguised forms; **cyclical** unemployment is negligible in India and typical of developed capitalist economies.
- **Cause in a developing country:** **lack of complementary factors of production – deficiency of capital**; the rate of capital formation limits jobs for a fast-growing population. Keynes's "lack of effective demand" explains developed economies.
- India is a **labour-surplus economy**; Indian agriculture is **land-scarce and labour-surplus**; projected skilled-labour surplus about **245.3 million by 2030**.
- **Causes:** rapid **population growth**, **labour immobility**, shift to **capital-intensive** production, **stagnation of the industrial sector**, neglected secondary sector and **MSMEs**, stringent labour laws, **delay in official paperwork**, **slow infrastructure**, import-oriented economy, **jobless growth** led by IT and finance.
- About **90%** of the workforce is informal.
- Odd ones out: "high growth of labour-intensive manufacturing" and "government aid to the unemployed" do NOT cause unemployment; disguised unemployment, poverty and low productivity hold villages back – the **Panchayat system** does not.
- **Latest:** monthly PLFS (CWS) rate **5.5% in May 2026** (5.2% April); **30-35 million** seasonal labourers migrate every year (7 Sep 2026).
- **Debate:** a falling rate may reflect **distress entry** into low-productivity work, not real job creation.
> APPSC asks the "best description" (chronic unemployment and under-employment) and the cause (deficiency of capital / complementary factors); Keynes's effective demand is the developed-country answer.

=== Theories – Keynes, Classical, Phillips, Friedman, Okun
| Economist / school | Idea |
|---|---|
| **J.M. Keynes** | Unemployment in developed economies = **deficiency of effective demand**; macro equilibrium is an **under-employment, short-run** equilibrium |
| **Classical** | Assumes **full employment**; **wage cuts reduce unemployment**; full employment is consistent with seasonal and frictional unemployment |
| **A.W. Phillips (1958)** | **Inverse relation between inflation and unemployment** (Phillips curve; Economica 1958, UK data 1861-1957) |
| **Milton Friedman** | **Natural rate of unemployment** hypothesis |
| **Friedman-Phelps / monetarists** | **Long-run Phillips curve is vertical** at the natural rate (NAIRU) |
| **Arthur Okun** | 1 point of unemployment above the natural rate ≈ **2% fall in output** below potential GDP |
- **Natural rate = frictional + structural** unemployment, not cyclical.
- **Stagflation** (high inflation + high unemployment, 1970s USA) broke the Phillips trade-off.
> Phillips = inflation; Okun = output; Friedman = natural rate; Keynes = effective demand.

=== Educated Unemployment and Youth
- **Urban** phenomenon; cause: **curriculum mismatch** with industry needs.
- **Educated unemployment can rise with high economic growth** when professional / skilled education is lacking (A and R both true, R explains A).
- **Kerala** has the highest percentage of educated unemployed (**K.P. Kannan**); Kerala also topped the HDI (IHDR 2011) and had the highest unemployment rate (Census 2011), but the jobless rate does not explain the HDI.
| PLFS 2025 group | Rate |
|---|---|
| Youth 18-29 | 14.8% |
| Tertiary-educated youth 18-29 | **29.4%** |
| Secondary schooling and above | 6.5% |
| Gen Z (15-26) | **11.9%** |
- **40.1%** of tertiary-educated youth are **NEET** (25 Aug 2026).
- **AP (PLFS 2023-24, AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25):** youth (15-29) unemployment **17.5%**; urban female youth **29.1%**.

=== Underemployment and Other Classifications
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Able and willing to work, no job at all |
| **Voluntary** | Unwilling to work at the prevailing wage (generous benefits, high marginal tax, waiting for a better job) |
| **Involuntary** | Willing at the going wage but no job – the **labour force counts only involuntary unemployment** |
| **Visible underemployment** | **Fewer than normal hours** of work |
| **Invisible underemployment** | Full-time but very low income or below ability (**MBA working as a driver**) |
| **Long-term** | Unemployed for more than **12 months** |
| **Unemployment trap** | Benefits discourage taking up work |
| Chronic | Long-term unemployment persisting in the economy |
- **Disguised ≠ underemployment** in strict use: underemployment = not using full skills or hours; disguised = surplus labour with zero marginal output (NCERT uses "underemployment" loosely for both).
