## SUB 0 | Part XIV-A – Articles 323-A and 323-B
[0:0] # Constitutional place
[0:1] - **Nature:** tribunals are **quasi-judicial** bodies – neither executive departments nor courts – set up to cut **pendency** in regular courts and bring **domain expertise**.
[0:2] - **Not in the original Constitution:** the **42nd Amendment, 1976** inserted **Part XIV-A** ("Tribunals"), on the **Swaran Singh Committee**'s advice – only two Articles: **323-A** (administrative tribunals) and **323-B** (tribunals for other matters).
[0:3] - **75th Amendment Act, 1993** (in force 1994) added clause **(h) rent and tenancy rights** to 323-B.
[0:4] - Tribunal **chairpersons** generally get the status of a **High Court judge**.
[0:5] TABLE head: Part | Subject
  [0:5.0] VIII | Union Territories
  [0:5.1] IX-A | Municipalities (243-P – 243-ZG)
  [0:5.2] IX-B | Co-operative Societies (243-ZH – 243-ZT)
  [0:5.3] X | Scheduled and Tribal Areas
  [0:5.4] XI | Union–State relations
  [0:5.5] XIV | Services under the Union and the States (308–323)
  [0:5.6] XIV-A | Tribunals (323-A, 323-B)
  [0:5.7] XVII | Official Language
[0:6] - **Trap:** "Tribunals – Part XI" (or Part X / Part XIV) is **wrong** – tribunals are strictly **Part XIV-A**.
[0:7] # Article 323-A vs Article 323-B
[0:8] TABLE head: Basis | Article 323-A | Article 323-B
  [0:8.0] Subjects | Only public service matters – recruitment and service conditions of Union, State, local-body, public-corporation employees | Taxation; foreign exchange, import-export; industrial and labour; land reforms; ceiling on urban property; elections to Parliament and State Legislatures; foodstuffs; rent and tenancy
  [0:8.1] Who can legislate | Parliament only (Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985) | Parliament and State Legislatures (within their competence)
  [0:8.2] Hierarchy | One tribunal for the Centre and one per State (or joint) – no hierarchy | A hierarchy of tribunals (original + appellate) may be created
[0:9] > Asked as: wrong Part–provision match (UPPCS 2023: "Tribunals – Part XI" is the mismatch); who legislates under 323-A; which Article lists elections/foodstuffs/land reforms (323-B).

## SUB 1 | Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT)
[1:0] # Basis and setting up
[1:1] - **Legal basis:** **Article 323-A** + **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985**; a **statutory**, quasi-judicial body set up by the **Central Government** – the **first tribunal under Art. 323-A**.
[1:2] - **Date:** established with effect from **1 November 1985** (notification under the 1985 Act) – in **1985**, under PM **Rajiv Gandhi** (not Lal Bahadur Shastri – UPSC 2009).
[1:3] - **APSLPRB SI 2016** printed only "2-10-1983/84/85/86" and keyed **2-10-1985**: the year is right but the date is not – CAT began on **1-11-1985**.
[1:4] # Composition
[1:5] TABLE head: Point | Fact
  [1:5.0] Members | Drawn from both judicial and administrative streams (not administrative only)
  [1:5.1] Sanctioned strength | 1 Chairman + 69 Members (34 Judicial + 35 Administrative; Rajya Sabha answer 2023); Vice-Chairman post abolished in 2006
  [1:5.2] Judicial members | Qualified to be High Court judges
  [1:5.3] Administrative members | Additional Secretary or above
  [1:5.4] Benches | Principal Bench New Delhi + 18 other regular benches (19 in all)
  [1:5.5] Appointment | Central Government, on a search-cum-selection committee headed by the CJI or an SC judge he nominates
[1:6] # Jurisdiction and procedure
[1:7] - **Original jurisdiction** over recruitment and service matters of **All-India Services**, **Central civil services**, civil posts under the Centre and **civilian employees of defence**; not armed-forces members, Supreme Court staff, Parliament's secretarial staff or **private-sector** employees.
[1:8] - **Not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908**; guided by **natural justice**; nominal fee (₹50); applicant may appear **in person** or through a lawyer.
[1:9] - **Appeal:** originally only to the Supreme Court; since **L. Chandra Kumar (1997)** first to the **Division Bench of the High Court**, then the Supreme Court – not directly to the SC.
[1:10] > Asked as: statements – statutory (yes), administrative members only (no), not bound by CPC (yes), covers AIS + Central services (yes), set up 1985 (yes) → "I, III, IV and V" (APPSC Group I 2023); date of formation.

## SUB 3 | National Green Tribunal (NGT)
[3:0] - **NGT Act, 2010**; statutory body established **18 October 2010**; hears **environmental** disputes (forests, natural resources, compensation).
[3:1] - Gives effect to the **right to a healthy environment** read into **Article 21**.
[3:2] - Applies **sustainable development**, the **precautionary principle** and **polluter pays** (**Section 20**).
[3:3] - **Not bound by the CPC, 1908**; guided by **natural justice**; can review its own decisions.
[3:4] - **Benches:** Principal **New Delhi**; zonal **Bhopal** (Central), **Pune** (West), **Kolkata** (East), **Chennai** (South) – **no Jaipur** bench.
[3:5] - **Appeal:** **directly to the Supreme Court within 90 days** (Section 22) – not to a High Court.
[3:6] > Asked as: days for appeal to the SC (90 – MPPCS 2020); A-R on CPC and natural justice.

## SUB 4 | Judicial Review, Case Law, Merits and Criticism
[4:0] TABLE head: Case | Year | Held
  [4:0.0] S.P. Sampath Kumar | 1987 | Tribunals may replace HCs as an effective substitute (later overruled)
  [4:0.1] L. Chandra Kumar | 1997 | Judicial review under Arts 226/227 (HCs) and Art. 32 (SC) is basic structure; exclusion clauses 323-A(2)(d) and 323-B(3)(d) struck down; tribunals supplement, not replace HCs
  [4:0.2] R. Gandhi | 2010 | Members doing judicial work must be independent of the executive
  [4:0.3] Rojer Mathew | 2019 | First recommended a National Tribunals Commission
  [4:0.4] Madras Bar Association | 2021, Nov 2025 | Struck down re-enacted provisions; restored the earlier framework
[4:1] - Judicial review cannot be taken away even by amendment under **Article 368** – amendments are tested on the **basic structure** doctrine.
[4:2] - **Merits:** expertise, specialisation, speed, low cost; follow **natural justice**, not the full **CPC**.
[4:3] - **Criticism:** run by the **same ministries** whose decisions they review; executive control over appointments (Finance Act 2017, 2021 Ordinance).

## SUB 5 | Tribunal Reforms – 2021 Act to the 2026 Act
[5:0] # Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021
[5:1] - **Abolished:** Film Certification Appellate Tribunal, **IPAB**, Airport Appellate Tribunal, Authority for Advance Rulings (Customs), Plant Varieties Protection Appellate Tribunal – functions to the **High Courts** (the NGT continues).
[5:2] - **Tenure 4 years**; **minimum age 50**; Chairperson up to **70**, members up to **67**; search-cum-selection committee sent **two names** per vacancy.
[5:3] # Madras Bar Association (Nov 2025)
[5:4] - Struck down the **minimum age of 50**, the **4-year tenure** (made **5**), the **two-name** panel (now **one name per vacancy**) and equating members with civil servants; ordered a **National Tribunals Commission** within four months.
[5:5] # Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026
[5:6] - Moved by Law Minister **Arjun Ram Meghwal**; passed **Lok Sabha 10 Aug 2026** (voice vote) and **Rajya Sabha 11 Aug 2026**; **repeals the 2021 Act**; NTC to oversee 16 tribunals.
[5:7] TABLE head: NTC feature | Detail
  [5:7.0] HQ | New Delhi
  [5:7.1] Members | 5: Chairperson (former SC judge or former HC Chief Justice), 2 judicial (consulting the CJI), 2 technical (25+ years)
  [5:7.2] Tenure | 5 years or age 70, whichever earlier
  [5:7.3] Role | Selection, performance review, discipline

## SUB 6 | State Administrative Tribunals – AP Angle
[6:0] - **SATs** are set up by the **Central Government at the request of the State**; a **Joint Administrative Tribunal** may serve **two or more States**; SAT members are chosen by a committee chaired by the State HC Chief Justice.
[6:1] - **32nd Amendment, 1973** – **Article 371D** gave constitutional authority for an **AP Administrative Tribunal (APAT)** for public-service grievances.
[6:2] - Presidential order of **19 May 1975** under **Art. 371D(3)** constituted APAT; it worked from **6 July 1976**; reconstituted under the 1985 Act from **1 Nov 1989**.
[6:3] - **2016:** Telangana removed from its jurisdiction; **abolished in 2020** (Centre's notification of **14 Jan 2020**) – its cases went to the **AP High Court**, not the CAT.
