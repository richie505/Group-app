=== Indigo Revolt, Nil Darpan, Pabna and the Deccan Riots
# Indigo Revolt (1859-60), Bengal
- European planters forced ryots to grow **indigo instead of paying crops like rice**, through **advances and fraudulent contracts**, at prices that did not pay; intimidation, flogging, seizure of cattle.
- Leaders **Digambar and Bishnu Biswas** of **Nadia**; ryots refused to sow, went on rent strike and used the courts.
- **Supported by the Bengali intelligentsia** – press (**Harish Chandra Mukherjee's *Hindoo Patriot***), meetings, legal aid; missionaries too.
- **Indigo Commission (March 1860):** found planters coercive; ryots must **fulfil existing contracts** but **could refuse future ones**; Nov 1860 notification – no compulsion. It did **not** find indigo profitable to ryots.
- Came **immediately after 1857** (Sanyasi 1763-1800 and Santhal 1855-56 earlier; Pabna 1873-76 later).

# Pabna and Deccan
- **Pabna (1873-76):** agrarian league of **Ishan Chandra Roy and Shambhu Pal** against **rent hikes** by zamindars; legal, peaceful → **Bengal Tenancy Act 1885**.
- **Deccan Riots (1875):** **Poona and Ahmednagar**; ryotwari peasants vs **Marwari and Gujarati moneylenders**; debt bonds burnt; cause incl. the **Limitation Law of 1859** → **Deccan Agriculturists' Relief Act 1879** (protection from **moneylenders**).

- **After 1857:** Indigo, Deccan riots, Birsa Munda – **not** Santhal (UPPCS 2017).
- **Match (UPSC 1997):** Moplah – Kerala, Pabna – Bengal, Eka – Awadh, Birsa – Bihar.
- **Phases:** sporadic revolts (pre-1917) → **Gandhian satyagraha** (Champaran, Kheda, Bardoli) → organised **Kisan Sabhas** and Left-led struggles.
- **Permanent Settlement (1793)** made zamindars **proprietors** and cultivators tenants – the root of anti-zamindari struggles.

| Body | Fact |
|---|---|

- Sahajanand = **peasant (Kisan Sabha) movement in Bihar** in the 1930s – not tribal, labour or caste.

| Body | Year | Leaders | Issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UP Kisan Sabha** | **Feb 1918** | **Gauri Shankar Mishra, Indra Narayan Dwivedi**; backed by **M.M. Malaviya** | Peasant grievances – **not** Nehru |
| **Awadh (Oudh) Kisan Sabha** | **Oct 1920** | **Jawaharlal Nehru, Baba Ramchandra** | Rent hikes, bedakhali, **begar** (unpaid forced labour) |

- Eka began in **Hardoi, Bahraich, Sitapur** (also Barabanki) – keyed aim: **conversion of produce rent into cash**.
- **Eka vs Bardoli (UPSC 2026):** Bardoli fought a **30% hike in land revenue**; Eka fought **high rents** of taluqdars and turned violent, outside Congress control.

=== Telangana Armed Struggle (1946-51)
- Against the **Nizam's feudal order** – deshmukhs, jagirdars, **vetti** (forced labour); led by the **CPI** through the **Andhra Mahasabha** – the biggest peasant guerrilla war (3,000 villages).
- Land redistributed; **village soviets** set up; **called off October 1951**.
- **Doddi Komaraiah:** **first martyr** – shot at **Kadavendi** (Jangaon taluka, Nalgonda) on **4 July 1946** by the **Visnur deshmukh's** men while **defending a poor washerwoman's land** (**Chakali Ailamma**).
- Leaders: **P. Sundarayya, Chandra Rajeswara Rao**; woman fighter **Mallu Swarajyam**; Andhra Mahasabha president 1941-46 **M. Suryanarayana**; **Ravi Narayana Reddy** presided Chilkur (1941); 11th session **Bhongir (1944)** communist-dominated.

=== Moplah Revolt (1921) and Bardoli (1928)
- **Moplah (Mappila):** **Muslim tenants of Malabar** against **Hindu landlords (jenmis)** and the British – no security of tenure, high rents, renewal fees; merged with the **Khilafat** agitation; arrest of **Ali Musliyar** (Aug 1921); leader **Variyankunnath Kunjahammad Haji**.
- **Wagon Tragedy, November 1921:** Moplah prisoners suffocated in a closed railway wagon.
- **Bardoli (Surat, Gujarat):** against a **30% land-revenue hike**; **Vallabhbhai Patel** – women gave him the title **"Sardar"**; inquiry cut the hike to 6.03%.
~~ Sources: Spectrum p.611-612; app notes 1:216 (sub 4)

=== Tebhaga Movement (1946-47)
- Call of the **Bengal Provincial Kisan Sabha (Sept 1946)** to implement the **Floud Commission's** tebhaga: **two-thirds of the crop to the bargadar** instead of one-half – the landlord's share cut **from one-half to one-third**.
- **Bargadars / adhiars** (sharecroppers) against **jotedars**; slogan "take the paddy to our own threshing floor".
- Outcome: **Bargadari Bill / Act (1950)**.
~~ Sources: Spectrum p.615; app notes 1:216 (sub 5)
