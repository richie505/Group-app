=== NOTES 1:213 | Nataka Samasthalu – Theatre Companies and Their Nationalist Role
## SUB 0 | Origins of Telugu Drama Companies
[213:0:0] - 1. **Marathi Darwada Nataka Samajam** toured and set up a tent at **Rajamahendravaram (1880-81)**.
[213:0:1] - 2. This inspired **Guntur's Hindu Nataka Samajam** and **Nellore's Lakshmi Vilasa Nataka Sabha**.
[213:0:2] - 3. **Surabhi**: a family of **Tholu Bommalata** (leather-puppet) performers replaced puppets with live actors.
[213:0:3] - 4. Surabhi is still a **family theatre**, about 140 years on.
[213:0:4] - Drama companies were the **main mass entertainment** of the time → carriers of reform and nationalist messages.

## SUB 2 | Bellary and Rayalaseema Theatre
[213:2:0] - **Bellary** (then in the Madras Presidency) was an early base of Telugu theatre.
[213:2:1] - **Dharmavaram Ramakrishnamacharyulu** – Sarasa Vinodini Sabha, Bellary (also listed as "Vinodini Sabha – Bellary").
[213:2:2] - **Kolachalam Srinivasa Rao** – Bellary playwright known for **historical plays**.
[213:2:3] - **Bellary Raghava** – celebrated stage actor, relative of Dharmavaram; a Bellary theatre is named after him.
[213:2:4] - Rayalaseema companies: **Surabhi (Kadapa)**, **Rama Vilasa Sabha (Chittoor)**.

## SUB 4 | Plays Banned by the British
[213:4:0] - **Viplava Parivartanamu**: published and **banned during the Non-Cooperation Movement**.
[213:4:1] - Author: **Kodali Anjaneyulu** (APPSC 2018 and G2 Mains 2019 keys); not **Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri**.
[213:4:2] - Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri supported the **NCM boycott of elections (1920)**.
[213:4:3] - **Swadeshi and NCM years**: troupes such as the **Jatiya Nataka Rangam** promoted swadeshi goods and national education.
[213:4:4] - Why watched: a drama company could put a political idea before **thousands of illiterate people in one evening**, so colonial officials watched troupes closely.
[213:4:5] For Viplava Parivartanamu the keyed author is Kodali Anjaneyulu (2018, G2 2019); Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri is the trap.

=== NOTES 1:216 | Peasant Movements – Indigo, Deccan Riots, Moplah, Bardoli, Tebhaga, Telangana Armed Struggle
## SUB 0 | Peasant Movements – Overview
[216:0:0] # Three Phases
[216:0:1] - Phase 1 (pre-1917): **sporadic revolts** against zamindars, planters and moneylenders.
[216:0:2] - Phase 2: **Gandhian satyagraha** (Champaran 1917, Kheda 1918, Bardoli 1928).
[216:0:3] - Phase 3 (1930s-40s): **organised Kisan Sabhas** and Left-led struggles (Tebhaga, Telangana).
[216:0:4] - Root cause in Andhra: the **Permanent Settlement (1793, Cornwallis)** made zamindars **proprietors** and cultivators **tenants**.

## SUB 1 | Indigo Revolt (1859-60) and Pabna
[216:1:0] # Indigo Revolt
[216:1:1] - European planters forced peasants to grow indigo under the **Tinkathia system** – indigo on **3/20 of the land** at poor prices.
[216:1:2] - Leaders: **Digambar Biswas and Bishnu Biswas**; region **Nadia (Bengal)**.
[216:1:3] - Outcome: **Indigo Commission (1860)** declared Tinkathia illegal.
[216:1:4] # Nil Darpan – Who Did What
[216:1:5] TABLE head: Role | Person
  [216:1:5.0] Written by | Dinabandhu Mitra
  [216:1:5.1] English translation | Michael Madhusudan Dutt
  [216:1:5.2] Published by | Rev. James Long
[216:1:6] Nil Darpan = written by Mitra, translated by Dutt, published by Long. Do not swap the three roles.
[216:1:7] # Pabna (1873-76)
[216:1:8] - **Pabna Agrarian League** – **Ishan Chandra Roy and Shambhu Pal**.
[216:1:9] - Against **rent hikes by zamindars**; legal and peaceful; led to the **Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885**.
[216:1:10] Pabna was against rent hikes, NOT against indigo, and has no link with Dinabandhu Mitra.
## SUB 2 | Deccan Riots (1875)
[216:2:0] - Region: **Poona and Ahmednagar** (Maharashtra), under ryotwari.
[216:2:1] - Cause: the **Limitation Law of 1859** let moneylenders (outsiders from **Gujarat and Marwar**) seize peasants' land.
[216:2:2] - Target: **moneylenders** – peasants attacked their houses and **burned debt bonds**.
[216:2:3] - Outcome: **Deccan Agriculturists' Relief Act, 1879**.
[216:2:4] 1859 = Limitation Law (cause) | 1875 = Riots | 1879 = Relief Act

## SUB 4 | Moplah Revolt (1921) and Bardoli (1928)
[216:4:0] # Moplah (Malabar)
[216:4:1] - **Muslim tenants (Moplahs)** against **Hindu landlords (jenmis)** and the British.
[216:4:2] - Grievances: **no security of tenure**, renewal fees, high rent.
[216:4:3] - Leaders: **Variyankunnathu Kunjahammad Haji** and **Ali Musliyar**.
[216:4:4] - **Wagon Tragedy:** **November 1921** – Moplah prisoners suffocated in a closed railway wagon; the revolt grew out of the **Khilafat** agitation
[216:4:5] # Bardoli Satyagraha
[216:4:6] - Gujarat, **1928**; against a **30% hike in land revenue**.
[216:4:7] - Led by **Patel**, who got the title **"Sardar"** here.
## SUB 5 | All India Kisan Sabha and Tebhaga

[216:5:3] # Tebhaga (1946-47, Bengal)
[216:5:4] - Led by the **Bangiya Pradeshik Kisan Sabha** (AIKS/CPI).
[216:5:5] - **Bargadars / adhiars** (sharecroppers) against **jotedars**.
[216:5:6] - Demand: crop share raised from **one-half to two-thirds** – that is, the landlord's share cut from **one-half to one-third**, as the **Floud Commission** had recommended; crop to be stored in the bargadars' own godowns.
[216:5:7] - Outcome: **Bargadari Act (1950)**; short-run failure from limited spread and a Hindu-Muslim rift.
[216:5:8] Tebhaga = "three shares": two-thirds to the bargadar, one-third to the landlord (UPSC 2013).
## SUB 6 | Telangana Armed Struggle (1946-1951)
[216:6:0] # Cause and Course
[216:6:1] - Against the **Nizam's feudal order** – **jagirdars, deshmukhs** and **Vetti** (forced labour); peasants and tribals joined.
[216:6:2] - Led by the **CPI** through the **Andhra Mahasabha**.
[216:6:3] - Land seized and redistributed; **village soviets** set up.
[216:6:4] - Called off in **1951** after the Indian Army integrated Hyderabad (1948) and land reform was promised.

=== NOTES 1:217 | Anti-zamindari Movements in Andhra – Causes, Leaders and Outcomes
## SUB 0 | Causes of the Anti-zamindari Movement
[217:0:0] - About **40%** of Andhra's land was still under zamindars at **Independence**.
[217:0:1] - In Madras Presidency, **70%** of zamindari land lay in Andhra districts (Vizag, Nellore, the two Godavaris, Krishna, Chittoor).
[217:0:2] - **Pattabhi** first called for an anti-zamindari struggle in **1920**.
[217:0:3] - **Inam ryots** fought for abolition of inam estates under the **Madras Estates Land Act, 1908**.
[217:0:4] # Grievances and Exactions – Terms

  [217:0:5.0] Nazarana | Annual renewal fee for tenancy
  [217:0:5.1] Vetti | Forced free labour

  [217:0:5.3] Bedalli | Eviction for non-payment
