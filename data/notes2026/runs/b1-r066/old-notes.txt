=== NOTES 1:66 | Gupta Art, Literature, Science and Technology – Kalidasa, Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Ajanta, Mehrauli Pillar
## SUB 0 | The "classical Age" Label
[66:0:0] # Why it is called the Classical / Golden Age
[66:0:1] - Gupta rule is called the **Golden Age** of ancient India for art and culture.
[66:0:2] - Peak of **Sanskrit literature**, **mathematics and astronomy** (zero, decimal place value), **Ajanta painting**, **Sarnath sculpture** and the **first structural temples**.
[66:0:3] - First images of Hindu gods (Vishnu, Shiva) became common; shift from **rock-cut to free-standing structural temples**.
[66:0:4] - **Nalanda** drew students from China, Tibet, Korea and South-East Asia; **Balaputradeva of Srivijaya** funded a monastery there.
[66:0:5] # Criticism of the label
[66:0:6] - Spread of **vishti** (forced labour).
[66:0:7] - **Land grants and feudatories** weakened central power.
[66:0:8] - **Decline of trade and towns**; rigid caste; **falling status of women**.
[66:0:9] - Varahamihira's Romaka and Paulisa Siddhantas show Greek input, so Indian science was not isolated.

## SUB 4 | Scientists and Their Texts
[66:4:0] # Scientist – field – work – contribution
[66:4:1] TABLE head: Scientist | Field | Works | Contribution
  [66:4:1.0] Aryabhata | Maths, astronomy | Aryabhatiya (499 CE) | First to treat maths as a separate subject (Ganitapada); Earth spherical and rotates on its axis (day and night); scientific cause of eclipses; pi = 3.1416; used zero and place value
  [66:4:1.1] Varahamihira (505–587 CE, Avanti) | Astronomy, astrology, geography, botany, architecture | Panchasiddhantika, Brihat Samhita, Brihat Jataka | Improved sine tables; shifting of equinoxes; earthquake theories; termites as sign of underground water
  [66:4:1.2] Brahmagupta (c. 598–668 CE) | Maths, astronomy | Brahmasphutasiddhanta, Khandakhadyaka | Rules of arithmetic with zero and negative numbers; before Newton: all things gravitate to the earth; first to study cyclic quadrilaterals
  [66:4:1.3] Bhaskara I | Astronomy | Commentary on Aryabhatiya | Wrote decimal numbers with a circle for zero (629 CE)
  [66:4:1.4] Bhaskara II (12th c.) | Maths (Algebra) | Siddhanta Shiromani | Earth's circumference; Lilavati has ideas of calculus
  [66:4:1.5] Mahavira (9th c., Karnataka) | Maths | Ganitasarasangraha | –
[66:4:2] # Varahamihira's texts
[66:4:3] - **Panchasiddhantika**: summary of **5 siddhantas** – Surya, **Romaka**, **Paulisa**, Vasishtha, Paitamaha; based on Greek (also Roman, Egyptian, Indian) astronomy.
[66:4:4] - **Romaka Siddhanta**: deals with **astronomy**; Roman-Greek influence, like Paulisa.
[66:4:5] - **Brihat Samhita**: encyclopaedia (astronomy, astrology, geography, botany, architecture).
[66:4:6] - **Brihat Jataka**: standard work on **astrology**.
[66:4:7] - Siddhanta Shiromani has four parts: **Lilavati**, **Bijaganita** (Algebra), Ganitadhyaya, Goladhyaya.
[66:4:8] Lilavati = Bhaskara II (12th c.), not Gupta. Amarakosha is by Amarasimha, not Varahamihira.
## SUB 5 | Zero and the Place-value System
[66:5:0] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [66:5:0.0] Earliest epigraphic place-value use | Mankani (Sankheda) plates, Gujarat, AD 595–596 (Kalachuri year 346)
  [66:5:0.1] Bhaskara I | Circle for zero in decimal numbers, 629 CE
  [66:5:0.2] Earliest zero written as a circle (inscription) | Gwalior inscription, 876 CE
  [66:5:0.3] Decimal place value credited to | Aryabhata
  [66:5:0.4] Zero arithmetic set by | Brahmagupta
[66:5:1] - Zero was discovered by an anonymous Indian (2nd c. BC); **Arabs** learned it from India (Arab use from 873 AD) and spread it to the **Islamic world and Europe**. Zero did not come from Europe.
[66:5:2] Decimal system and zero = the lasting gift of the Gupta age to world mathematics.

[66:8:1] - Gupta stone sculpture grew out of the **Mathura school**.
[66:8:2] - **Sarnath school**: polished **Chunar (buff) sandstone**, little decoration, **Dharmachakra mudra**; model for Buddhist art across Asia.
[66:8:3] TABLE head: Image | Place | Feature
  [66:8:3.0] Sultanganj Buddha | Bhagalpur, Bihar | Bronze, over 2 m – finest Gupta metal image
  [66:8:3.1] Eran Varaha | Eran (MP) | Vaishnava presence
[66:8:4] - Apr 2025: the PM gifted a **Sarnath Buddha in Dhyan mudra** to Thailand's King.
[66:8:5] # Mehrauli iron pillar
[66:8:6] - Inscription names the king **"Chandra"** = **Chandragupta II**.
[66:8:7] - Originally at **Udayagiri near Vidisha (MP)**; now at **Mehrauli, Delhi**.
[66:8:8] - **Not rusted for over fifteen centuries** – **high phosphorus** iron forms a protective layer.

## SUB 9 | Ajanta, Bagh and Udayagiri Caves
[66:9:0] # Ajanta basics
[66:9:1] - **Buddhist**; **Aurangabad district, Maharashtra**; horseshoe gorge of the **Waghora river**, Sahyadri ranges.
[66:9:2] - **30 caves** (29 numbered, plus 15A); 5 chaityas (9, 10, 19, 26, 29), rest viharas; 25 viharas in older counts.
[66:9:3] - Found **1819** by British officer **John Smith** while hunting tigers; **UNESCO 1983** (with Ellora).
[66:9:4] - Patrons: **Satavahanas** (early, 2nd–1st c. BCE) → mainly the **Vakatakas** (King **Harishena**, 5th c.).
[66:9:5] # Cave and panel
[66:9:6] TABLE head: Cave | Panel / fact
  [66:9:6.0] 9, 10 | Earliest paintings in India – Satavahana period, Amaravati style
  [66:9:6.1] 10 | Chhaddanta (Shwetagaja) Jataka – first "completely evolved painting"
  [66:9:6.2] 1 | Bodhisattva Padmapani ("one who holds the lotus"), Vajrapani
  [66:9:6.3] 16 | Excavated by minister Varahadeva (of Harishena); Dying Princess
  [66:9:6.4] 17 | Mother and Child, Bhava-chakra (wheel of life), Brahmi inscription
  [66:9:6.5] 19 | Chaitya; standing Buddha with two Yakshas
  [66:9:6.6] 26 | Mahaparinirvana – a sculpture, not a painting
[66:9:7] - Themes: **Jataka tales**, secular life, Bodhi-tree worship, group dances.
[66:9:8] - Technique: **tempera on dry lime plaster (fresco secco)**, not true fresco; outlines in **red** first; lapis-lazuli blue is used.
[66:9:9] - Known for mural paintings: **Ajanta and Lepakshi** (not Sanchi Stupa). Ajanta and Mahabalipuram share only **rock-cut** form.
[66:9:10] # Other caves
[66:9:11] TABLE head: Site | Place | Feature
  [66:9:11.0] Bagh | Dhar district, MP, Vindhya slopes, Baghani river | 9 Buddhist viharas, late 4th–6th c.; Hallisaka painting = joyous folk dance; Cave 4 = Rang Mahal
  [66:9:11.1] Udayagiri | Near Vidisha (MP) | 20 caves (19 Hindu, 1 Jain) under Chandragupta II; colossal Varaha panel; Virasena's inscription on his conquests
  [66:9:11.2] Ellora | Aurangabad | Buddhist Caves 1–12; Rashtrakuta cave temples
  [66:9:11.3] Mandapeshwar | Mumbai | Buddhist, later Christian
[66:9:12] - AP angle: **Mogalrajapuram** – 5 caves, Nataraja; **Bhairavakonda** – 8 Kapalika caves; Vishnukundinas adapted the Gupta rock-cut style (4th–5th c.).
[66:9:13] Gupta Udayagiri is in **MP**, not Kharavela's Jain Udayagiri in Odisha.
