## SUB 0 | Ajanta Caves – Phases, Patrons and Paintings
[0:0] # Basics
[0:1] - **Ajanta** = **Buddhist** rock-cut caves in **Aurangabad (Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar) district, Maharashtra**, cut in a horseshoe-shaped gorge of the **Waghora river** (UPSC 2021) in the Sahyadri ranges – not Sanchi (Betwa side), Pandu-lena (Nashik) or Amaravati (Krishna).
[0:2] - **30 caves** (29 numbered + 15A): **5 chaityas** (9, 10, 19, 26, 29), the rest viharas.
[0:3] - Rediscovered in **1819** by British officer **John Smith** on a tiger hunt; **UNESCO World Heritage Site 1983** (with Ellora).
[0:4] - Ajanta is famous for **Buddhist cave temples** (Uttarakhand 2006) – Elephanta is Shaiva, Khajuraho Hindu-Jain, Nalanda a monastery-university.
[0:5] # Two phases
[0:6] TABLE head: Phase | Patron | Caves | Note
  [0:6.0] Early (2nd–1st c. BCE) | Satavahanas | 9, 10 (also 8, 12, 13, 15A) | Earliest paintings of historical India – Amaravati style
  [0:6.1] Later (5th c. CE) | Vakatakas – King Harishena, minister Varahadeva | 1, 2, 16, 17, 19, 26 … | Most paintings and sculpture
[0:7] - Ajanta's art was **mainly patronised by the Vakatakas** (Jharkhand 2013) – not Chalukyas, Pallavas or Gangas; the Vakataka phase is contemporary with the **Gupta** age, so "Ajanta built in the Gupta period" (SSC 2014) is the accepted answer.
[0:8] - **Earliest paintings** (APPSC AP History PYQ) = **Satavahana** period, Caves 9 and 10 (prehistoric rock art such as Bhimbetka aside).
[0:9] # Caves and panels
[0:10] TABLE head: Cave | Panel / fact
  [0:10.0] 10 | Shwetagaja (Chhaddanta) Jataka – first "completely evolved" painting (APPSC G2 2012)
  [0:10.1] 1 | Bodhisattva Padmapani ("lotus-holder"), Vajrapani, Black Princess (Krishna Yuvarani)
  [0:10.2] 16 | Dying Princess; excavated by Varahadeva
  [0:10.3] 17 | Mother and Child, Bhava-chakra (wheel of life)
  [0:10.4] 19 | Chaitya; standing Buddha
  [0:10.5] 26 | Mahaparinirvana – a sculpture, not a painting
[0:11] - **Black Princess = Cave 1 = Vakataka** phase (late 5th c.) – **not** Satavahana; she is neither the earliest nor the "first" painting (APPSC Welfare 2017 keys gave Satavahana).
[0:12] - **Padmapani** = **Avalokiteshvara** (UPSC 1997); his painting is the most famous at **Ajanta** (UPSC 2017) – not Bagh, Badami or Ellora.
[0:13] # Themes and technique
[0:14] - Themes: **Jataka tales** (Buddha's previous births) and the **life of Gautama Buddha** (SSC 2019; APSC 2017; MPPSC 2012) – not the epics, Panchatantra, Vikramaditya or Mahavira; also court and secular life.
[0:15] - Technique: **tempera on dry lime plaster (fresco secco)**, not true fresco; red outlines first.
[0:16] - Mural paintings: **Ajanta and Lepakshi** – not **Sanchi** (UPSC 2013).
[0:17] - Ajanta vs **Mahabalipuram**: only the **rock-cut** form is common – different periods and religions (UPSC 2016).
[0:18] - UPSC 2014 pairs: **Mahaparinirvana – Ajanta** ✓; **Arjuna's Penance – Mamallapuram** ✓; **Varaha rescuing Earth – Udayagiri** (not Mount Abu) ✗.
[0:19] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 3 p.13, p.19 (Ajanta paintings, Jataka themes, Dying Princess, Mother and Child); PMF Art & Culture Part 1 (Ajanta, Vakataka phase); https://www.understandupsc.com/?p=8506 (Black Princess Cave 1); app notes 1:66 (sub 9)
## SUB 1 | Bagh, Udayagiri and Other Cave Sites
[1:0] TABLE head: Site | Place | Feature
  [1:0.0] Bagh | Dhar district, MP, Vindhya slopes, Baghani river | 9 Buddhist viharas, late 4th–6th c.; Hallisaka painting = a joyous folk dance (UPSC 2026); Cave 4 = Rang Mahal
  [1:0.1] Udayagiri | Near Vidisha, MP | 20 caves (19 Hindu, 1 Jain) under Chandragupta II; colossal Varaha panel; Virasena's inscription on his conquests
  [1:0.2] Ellora | Aurangabad | Buddhist, Hindu and Jain caves (Rashtrakuta Kailasa)
  [1:0.3] Mandapeshwar | Mumbai | Buddhist, later Christian
[1:1] - The only two surviving examples of **Gupta-age cave painting** = **Ajanta and Bagh** (UPSC 2010); Bagh paintings follow the Ajanta school with folk themes.
[1:2] - **Buddhist caves:** Ajanta, Bagh; **Sanchi** is a Buddhist stupa site; **Bulsar** (Valsad, Gujarat) has **no** Buddhist caves (MPPSC 2022 key).
[1:3] - Gupta **Udayagiri (MP)** ≠ Kharavela's Jain Udayagiri (Odisha). Udayagiri is **Hindu** – Ajanta, Bagh and Amaravati are Buddhist.
[1:4] - AP angle: **Mogalrajapuram** (Vijayawada) – 5 caves with a **Nataraja**; **Bhairavakonda** – 8 Kapalika caves (Vishnukundin-era rock-cut style).
[1:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 3 p.19 (Bagh epitomises the Ajanta school); PMF Art & Culture Part 1; app notes 1:66 (sub 9)

## SUB 4 | Scientists – Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara
[4:0] TABLE head: Scientist | Works | Known for
  [4:0.0] Aryabhata | Aryabhatiya (499 CE) | First to treat mathematics as a distinct subject (Ganitapada) (APPSC 2010); Earth rotates on its axis → day and night (State PSC); cause of eclipses; pi = 3.1416; decimal place value (UPPSC 2014)
  [4:0.1] Varahamihira (6th c., Avanti) | Panchasiddhantika, Brihat Samhita, Brihat Jataka | Astronomy, astrology; termites as a sign of underground water
  [4:0.2] Brahmagupta (598–668) | Brahmasphutasiddhanta, Khandakhadyaka | Rules for zero and negative numbers; anticipated Newton – all things gravitate to the earth (UPSC 1995); cyclic quadrilaterals
  [4:0.3] Bhaskara I | Commentary on Aryabhatiya | Circle for zero, 629 CE
  [4:0.4] Bhaskara II (12th c.) | Siddhanta Shiromani | Algebra (Bijaganita) (UPPCS 2002); Lilavati = mathematics (APPSC G2 2000; State PSC)
  [4:0.5] Mahavira (9th c.) | Ganitasarasangraha | Mathematics
[4:1] # Varahamihira's texts
[4:2] - **Panchasiddhantika** = summary of **5 siddhantas** – Surya, **Romaka**, **Paulisa**, Vasishtha, Paitamaha – based mainly on **Greek** astronomy (State PSC).
[4:3] - **Romaka Siddhanta** deals with **astronomy** (APPSC G2), with Roman-Greek influence.
[4:4] - **Brihat Samhita** = an **encyclopaedic** work (IAS 1993) – astronomy, astrology, geography, botany, architecture; Amarakosha is a lexicon, Siddhantasiromani mathematics, Ashtangahridaya medicine.
[4:5] - **Brihat Jataka** = astrology. **Amarakosha** is by **Amarasimha**, not Varahamihira (CGPSC 2022).
[4:6] - **Siddhanta Shiromani** has four parts: **Lilavati**, **Bijaganita**, Ganitadhyaya, Goladhyaya.
[4:7] # Zero and place value
[4:8] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [4:8.0] Decimal place value credited to | Aryabhata
  [4:8.1] Zero arithmetic | Brahmagupta
  [4:8.2] Earliest epigraphic place value | Mankani (Sankheda) plates, Gujarat, AD 595–596
  [4:8.3] Bhaskara I's circle for zero | 629 CE
  [4:8.4] Gwalior inscription – zero as a circle | 876 CE
[4:9] - Zero and decimals reached the **Islamic world and Europe through the Arabs** – zero did not come to India from Europe.
[4:10] - Romaka and Paulisa Siddhantas show **Greek** input in Gupta science.
[4:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 3 p.10 (Brahmagupta gravitation, Aryabhata, Varahamihira and Greek astronomy), p.26 (Siddhanta Shiromani, Panchasiddhantika); app notes 1:66 (sub 4, 5)

## SUB 7 | Sculpture and the Mehrauli Iron Pillar
[7:0] # Sculpture
[7:1] - Gupta stone sculpture grew out of the **Mathura school**; Gupta art showed Vishnu, Shiva and other Hindu gods.
[7:2] - **Sarnath school**: polished **Chunar buff sandstone**, little decoration; typical mudra = **Dharmachakra**; the Sarnath **Bhumisparsha mudra** Buddha also belongs to the **Gupta period** (UP UDA/LDA 2010) – not Kushana, Vardhana or Rajput.
[7:3] TABLE head: Image | Place | Feature
  [7:3.0] Sultanganj Buddha | Bhagalpur, Bihar | Bronze, over 2 m
  [7:3.1] Eran Varaha | Eran, MP | Vaishnava presence
[7:4] - Apr 2025: the PM gifted a Sarnath-style Buddha in **Dhyan mudra** to the King of Thailand.
[7:5] # Mehrauli iron pillar
[7:6] - Inscription names the king **"Chandra"** = **Chandragupta II** → a **Gupta** work (SSC 2012; UPPCS 2002) – not Ashoka, Harsha or Anangapal (who later moved it to Delhi).
[7:7] - Stands in the courtyard of the **Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque**, Qutb complex; originally at **Udayagiri near Vidisha** (Vishnupadagiri).
[7:8] - Studied for its **rustlessness** (CDS 2011) – **high-phosphorus** iron forms a protective film.
[7:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 3 p.13 (Mehrauli pillar not rusted; Sultanganj; Mathura school; Sarnath Dharmachakra); app notes 1:66 (sub 8)
## SUB 8 | Gupta Coins
[8:0] - **Gold = Dinara** (UPPCS 1992); **silver = Rupaka** (UPSC 1997; IAS 1996) – not karshapana, nishka or pana.
[8:1] - **Chandragupta I** = first Gupta coin issuer (king-queen type with Kumaradevi).
[8:2] - Silver began under **Chandragupta II** after the conquest of the **Western Kshatrapas**, modelled on **Saka** silver issues.
[8:3] - Largest hoard: **Bayana (Bharatpur, Rajasthan)**.
[8:4] TABLE head: Coin type | Ruler

  [8:4.2] Archer, lion-slayer, silver | Chandragupta II
  [8:4.3] King feeding a peacock (Kartikeya) | Kumaragupta I

[8:5] - The **veena-player** coins reveal the **Guptas'** love of music (SSC 2011).

## SUB 9 | The Golden Age Label
[9:0] - **Gupta** rule is called the **Golden (Classical) Age** of ancient India (UPPCS 2004) – famed for **art and architecture** (SSC 2001), not imperialism or land reform.
[9:1] - Peak of **Sanskrit literature**, **mathematics and astronomy**, **Ajanta painting**, **Sarnath sculpture**; shift from **rock-cut to free-standing structural temples**.
[9:2] - **Nalanda** drew foreign students; **Balaputradeva of Srivijaya** endowed a monastery there.
[9:3] - **Criticism:** spread of **vishti** (forced labour), land grants and feudatories, decline of trade and towns, rigid caste, falling status of women.
[9:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 3 p.6-8, p.13; app notes 1:66 (sub 0)
