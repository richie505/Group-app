## SUB 1 | Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang) – Journey, Si-Yu-Ki and Observations
[1:0] # Profile
[1:1] - Chinese Buddhist monk, the "**Prince of Pilgrims**"; visited India in **Harsha's** reign (SSC 2019; UPPCS 2012; State PSC) – **Fa-Hien** came under **Chandragupta II** (so "Fa-Hien under Harsha, Hiuen Tsang under Chandragupta II" are both wrong).
[1:2] - Left **Changan** (Tang capital) in **629**; emperor **Taizong** had banned foreign travel, so he went **on his own** by the Silk Route – **no one sent him as an emissary** (UPPCS 2007); back in China in **645**, about **16 years** later.
[1:3] - At Harsha's court **631**; studied at **Nalanda** under **Silabhadra** (Yogashastra) and taught there.
[1:4] - His record = **Si-Yu-Ki** ("Records of the Western World") (UPPCS 2016) – the main reason he is remembered (RAS 1992). Not Fa-Hien, Al-Biruni or Megasthenes.
[1:5] - Praised **Harsha**, **Pulakesin II** and Pallava **Narasimhavarman I**.

## SUB 2 | Harsha – Dynasty, Family, Rivals and Capital
[2:0] # Pushyabhuti (Vardhana) dynasty
[2:1] - Founded at **Thanesar (Sthanvishvara)** by **Pushyabhuti** per Bana's Harshacharita (State PSC); NextGen names **Naravardhana** as the first historical king.
[2:2] - **Prabhakaravardhana** (title Maharajadhiraja), Harsha's father; mother **Yashomati** became sati in 605.
[2:3] - Order: **Adityavardhana → Prabhakaravardhana → Rajyavardhana → Harshavardhana**.
[2:4] - **Rajyavardhana**, the **elder** brother, was killed by **Shashanka**; Harsha then took the throne (SSC 2019).
[2:5] # Family and rivals
[2:6] TABLE head: Person | Role
  [2:6.0] Grahavarman (Maukhari) | Harsha's brother-in-law, husband of Rajyashri
  [2:6.1] Devagupta | Ruler of Malwa who killed Grahavarman and held Rajyashri at Kannauj
  [2:6.2] Shashanka (Sasanka) | Ruler of Gauda (Bengal), capital Karnasuvarna; killed Rajyavardhana; cut the Bodhi tree at Gaya; Harsha's Bengal contemporary (RRB 2005)
  [2:6.3] Bhaskaravarman | Ruler of Kamarupa, Harsha's friend and ally
  [2:6.4] Dhruvasena II | Valabhi (Maitraka); defeated, made vassal, married Harsha's daughter
  [2:6.5] Pulakesin II | Badami Chalukya; defeated Harsha on the Narmada, took the title Paramesvara
[2:7] - SI 2023 match: **Devagupta – Malwa (killed the brother-in-law)**, **Sasanka – Gauda (killed the brother)**, **Bhaskaravarman – Kamarupa friend**, **Grahavarman – brother-in-law**.
[2:8] # Capital, titles, dates
[2:9] - Capital moved from **Thanesar to Kannauj** (UPPCS 1992; AP Sachivalayam) – Kannauj became the capital of his empire (UPPCS 1993), not Prayaga, Pataliputra or Delhi.
[2:10] - Titles: **Siladitya** (APPSC G1), **Sakalottarapathanatha** (lord of all North India).
