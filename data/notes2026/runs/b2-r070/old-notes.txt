=== NOTES 2:70 | Finance Commission – Composition, Terms of Reference, Functions; 15TH and 16TH Finance Commission
## SUB 0 | Nature and Constitutional Basis
[70:0:0] - Finance Commission (FC) = **constitutional body** under **Article 280**, **Part XII**.
[70:0:1] - **Quasi-judicial** body; the anchor of **fiscal federalism**.
[70:0:2] - **Not permanent**: constituted by the **President** within two years of the start of the Constitution, then **every fifth year or earlier** when necessary (Art. 280(1)).
[70:0:3] - Independent; recommendations **advisory, not binding**.
[70:0:4] - **P.V. Rajamannar** (4th FC) view: recommendations should not be rejected without very compelling reasons.
[70:0:5] # Articles – Number Game
[70:0:6] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [70:0:6.0] 280 | Constitution, composition and functions of the FC
  [70:0:6.1] 280(2) | Parliament fixes qualifications by law
  [70:0:6.2] 281 | President lays recommendations + explanatory memorandum on action taken before each House of Parliament
  [70:0:6.3] 290 | Pensions – NOT the FC
[70:0:7] APPSC-G2 2025: "Article 290 provides for the Finance Commission" is INCORRECT – it is Article 280.
## SUB 1 | Composition and Qualifications
[70:1:0] - **Chairman + four other members** (5 in all), appointed by the **President** (Art. 280(1)).
[70:1:1] - Term: as the **President specifies**; members are **eligible for reappointment**; no fixed term in the Constitution.
[70:1:2] - Qualifications fixed by **Parliament** in the **Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951** – NOT by the President or the Union Cabinet.
[70:1:3] - Has **powers of a civil court**: summoning persons, production of documents.
[70:1:4] # Qualifications – FC (Misc. Provisions) Act, 1951
[70:1:5] TABLE head: Post | Qualification
  [70:1:5.0] Chairman | Experience in public affairs
  [70:1:5.1] Member | High Court judge or qualified to be one
  [70:1:5.2] Member | Special knowledge of government finance and accounts
  [70:1:5.3] Member | Wide experience in financial matters and administration
  [70:1:5.4] Member | Special knowledge of economics
[70:1:6] - **No** member post for an expert in **monetary policy operations**.
[70:1:7] NOT "a Chairman and six members"; the FC does NOT report to NITI Aayog – it reports to the President.
## SUB 2 | Functions – Article 280(3)
[70:2:0] - 1. **Distribution of net proceeds of taxes** between Centre and States (**vertical**) and among States (**horizontal**).
[70:2:1] - 2. **Principles of grants-in-aid** to States from the **Consolidated Fund of India** (Art. 275).
[70:2:2] - 3. **Measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State** to supplement resources of **Panchayats and Municipalities** (on the basis of State Finance Commission recommendations).
[70:2:3] - 4. **Any other matter** referred by the President in the interest of sound finance.
[70:2:4] - Main function: decide the **share of States in central taxes**.
[70:2:5] - **Divisible pool** = gross tax revenue − **cesses, surcharges** and cost of collection; net amount certified by the **CAG**.
[70:2:6] - Rising **cesses and surcharges** shrink the divisible pool.
[70:2:7] The FC does not fix GST rates and does not itself release funds; it only recommends.

## SUB 4 | 14TH Finance Commission (2015–20)
[70:4:0] - Constituted **2 January 2013**; recommendations for **1 April 2015 – 31 March 2020**; GoI accepted the report on **24 February 2015**.
[70:4:1] - Share raised **32% → 42%** = **largest increase ever**.
[70:4:2] - Made **tax devolution** the main route (not grants); ignored the **Plan/non-Plan** distinction.
[70:4:3] - Made **no distinction** between **special and general category** States.
[70:4:4] - Population base: **1971 and 2011**.
[70:4:5] - Criteria: income distance **50%**, population 1971 **17.5%**, population 2011 **10%**, area **15%**, forest cover **7.5%**.
[70:4:6] - Local-body grants **₹2,87,436 crore** (panchayats ₹2,00,292 crore), as basic and performance grants.
[70:4:7] - After 42%, the Centre increased **non-shareable cesses**.
## SUB 5 | 15TH Finance Commission (2020–26)
[70:5:0] - Formed **27 November 2017**; Chairman **N.K. Singh**.
[70:5:1] - **First FC to cut** the vertical share: **42% → 41%** due to the change in **J&K's status**.
[70:5:2] - Used **2011 population** only (not 1971).
[70:5:3] - **Added** two new criteria: **demographic performance** and **tax effort**.
[70:5:4] - Local-body grants **₹4.36 lakh crore**; urban local bodies share **36%**.
## SUB 6 | Vertical vs Horizontal Devolution
[70:6:0] # Meaning
[70:6:1] Vertical devolution | Centre ↔ States | share of divisible pool (41%)
Horizontal devolution | among States | criteria and weights
[70:6:2] # Horizontal Criteria → Weight (%)
[70:6:3] TABLE head: Criterion | 15th FC | 16th FC
  [70:6:3.0] Income distance | 45 | 42.5
  [70:6:3.1] Population (2011) | 15 | 17.5
  [70:6:3.2] Area | 15 | 10
  [70:6:3.3] Forest and ecology | 10 | 10
  [70:6:3.4] Demographic performance | 12.5 | 10
  [70:6:3.5] Tax and fiscal effort | 2.5 | dropped
  [70:6:3.6] Contribution to GDP | – | 10 (new)
[70:6:4] - 16th FC: highest weight = **income distance (42.5%)**.
[70:6:5] - 16th FC is the **first** to use a **Contribution to GDP** criterion.
[70:6:6] - 16th FC forest criterion now includes **open forests** too.
[70:6:7] NUMBER GAME: 32 → 42 → 41 → 41 (13th → 14th → 15th → 16th)
## SUB 7 | 16TH Finance Commission (2026–31)
[70:7:0] # Constitution and Terms of Reference
[70:7:1] - Constituted **31 December 2023**; Chairman **Arvind Panagariya**; award **2026-27 to 2030-31**.
[70:7:2] - Members: **T. Rabi Sankar, Annie George Mathew, Manoj Panda, Soumya Kanti Ghosh**.
[70:7:3] - Terms of reference came straight from the **Constitution**, without detailed Central directives.
[70:7:4] - Population base 2011 (demographic performance measured against 1971).
[70:7:5] # Report and Recommendations
[70:7:6] - Report to the President **November 2025**; tabled in Parliament **1 February 2026** (Budget day).
[70:7:7] - Devolution accepted by the Centre in the **Budget 2026-27** speech; States got the full 41%; total transfers **₹25.44 lakh crore** for 2026-27.
[70:7:8] - 41% applies only to the **divisible pool** (excludes cesses, surcharges, cost of collection).
[70:7:9] - Total grants **₹9.47 lakh crore**; **revenue-deficit grants discontinued**.
[70:7:10] - **Local-body grants ₹7.91 lakh crore**: rural ~**₹4.35 lakh crore**, urban ~**₹3.56 lakh crore** (= **45%**); urbanisation premium **₹10,000 crore**.
[70:7:11] - **Disaster corpus ₹2.04 lakh crore**; sharing **90:10** NE/Himalayan States, **75:25** others.
[70:7:12] - **Heatwaves and lightning** proposed as new notified disasters.
[70:7:13] - **State deficits capped at 3% of GSDP**.
[70:7:14] - All five **southern States** gained: Karnataka **4.131%** (15th: 3.64%; 14th: 4.71%), Tamil Nadu **4.097%**.
[70:7:15] - **Kerala**: fiscal stress – neither revenue deficit grants nor State-specific grants.
[70:7:16] # Criticism and Debates
[70:7:17] - Rising **cesses and surcharges** are kept out of the divisible pool, so the real share of States in gross tax revenue is lower than 41%.
[70:7:18] - Southern States call the use of population and income distance **unfair** to better-performing States, even though all five gained under the 16th FC.
[70:7:19] - Ending **revenue-deficit grants** hurts States such as **Andhra Pradesh** and **Kerala**, which must now cut their revenue deficits on their own.
[70:7:20] - Local-body grants need **duly constituted** elected bodies, which pushes States to hold timely ULB and panchayat elections.
[70:7:21] Devolution is "tax sharing" (formula-based, untied); grants are "transfers for a purpose" – the 14th FC shifted the weight from grants to tax sharing.

## SUB 9 | Special Category Status
[70:9:0] - Introduced in **1969** on the recommendation of the **5th Finance Commission** (Mahavir Tyagi); funds allotted by the **Gadgil formula** (NDC).
[70:9:1] - First States: **Assam, Jammu & Kashmir, Nagaland**.
[70:9:2] - Criteria: **hilly and difficult terrain**, low population density or large **tribal** share, **strategic border** location, economic and infrastructure **backwardness**.

=== NOTES 2:71 | Comptroller and Auditor General – Appointment, Duties, Types of Audit, Audit Reports and Pac Linkage
## SUB 0 | Status and Name
[71:0:0] - Official name: **Comptroller and Auditor-General of India** (not "Controller").
[71:0:1] - **Part V, Chapter V**, Articles **148–151** (not Part XII); an **independent constitutional authority** created by Art. 148 – NOT a statutory body under the Finance Ministry.
[71:0:2] - Head of the **Indian Audit and Accounts Department**; "**Guardian of the Public Purse**".
[71:0:3] - **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**: the **most important officer** under the Constitution.
[71:0:4] - Four bulwarks of democracy: **CAG, Supreme Court, UPSC, Election Commission**.
[71:0:5] # Articles 148–151
[71:0:6] TABLE head: Article | Content
  [71:0:6.0] 148 | Appointment, oath, term, removal; 148(4) post-retirement bar; 148(6) expenses charged
  [71:0:6.1] 149 | Duties and powers
  [71:0:6.2] 150 | Form of accounts – prescribed by the President on CAG's advice
  [71:0:6.3] 151 | Audit reports: Union → President (151(1)); State → Governor (151(2))
## SUB 1 | Appointment, Term and Removal
[71:1:0] - Appointed by the **President** by **warrant under his hand and seal** (Art. 148(1)) – not PM, FM or PAC.
[71:1:1] - **Oath** before the President; form in the **Third Schedule**.
[71:1:2] - **Term: 6 years or age 65**, whichever earlier (Art. 148(3) + CAG Act, 1971) (APPSC 2022).
[71:1:3] - **Removal** like a **Supreme Court judge** (Art. 148(1) with Art. 124(4)): address by both Houses, **special majority**, proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
[71:1:4] - Does **not** hold office at the President's pleasure; resigns to the President.
[71:1:5] - **Constitution sets no qualifications** for the CAG.
[71:1:6] - Other appointments by the President: Attorney General (Art. 76), Governor (Art. 155).
[71:1:7] # Term Compared
[71:1:8] TABLE head: Office | Term / retirement
  [71:1:8.0] CAG | 6 years or 65
  [71:1:8.1] CEC / ECs | 6 years or 65 (Election Commission Act 1991, now 2023)
  [71:1:8.2] UPSC members | 6 years or 65
  [71:1:8.3] SPSC members | 6 years or 62
  [71:1:8.4] SC judge | retires at 65; no term in years
  [71:1:8.5] HC judge | 62 (15th Amendment, 1963: 60 → 62)
  [71:1:8.6] President / VP / Lok Sabha member | 5 years
[71:1:9] APPSC-G2 2017: Longest term among President, VP, MP and CAG = **CAG**.
## SUB 2 | Independence Safeguards
[71:2:0] - Salary = **SC judge**; salary and service conditions fixed by Parliament – **Second Schedule (Part E)**; cannot be varied to his disadvantage.
[71:2:1] - **Leave** granted or refused by the **President**.
[71:2:2] - Expenses (salaries, allowances, pensions of CAG and staff, office costs) **charged on the CFI** – **non-votable** (Art. 148(6)).
[71:2:3] - **Complete bar** on further office under GoI or any State (Art. 148(4)); UPSC Chairman also barred, but retired judges often head commissions.
[71:2:4] - **No Minister** represents the CAG in Parliament.
[71:2:5] - Order of Precedence: CAG comes first among the usual options.
## SUB 3 | Duties – Article 149 and the 1971 Act
[71:3:0] - **CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971**.
[71:3:1] - **1976 amendment** (in force **1 March 1976**): **accounting separated from audit**; Union accounts now by the **CGA** (Ministry of Finance); CAG still keeps most **State accounts**.
[71:3:2] # What the CAG Audits
[71:3:3] - Receipts and expenditure of the **Consolidated Funds** of India, States and UTs with legislature.
[71:3:4] - **Contingency Funds** and **Public Accounts**; trading, manufacturing, profit-and-loss accounts; stores and stocks.
[71:3:5] - **Government companies** and bodies **substantially financed** by government.
[71:3:6] - **Local bodies** only on request of President/Governor; Sixth Schedule councils covered.
[71:3:7] - Audit is **post-expenditure** (ex-post facto).
[71:3:8] - Upholds the Constitution and laws of Parliament in money matters; checks **legality and propriety**.
## SUB 4 | Types of Audit
[71:4:0] TABLE head: Type | Nature | Tests
  [71:4:0.0] Legal and regulatory (compliance) | Obligatory | Money legally available, spent as rules require
  [71:4:0.1] Financial attest | Obligatory | Accounts true
  [71:4:0.2] Propriety | Discretionary | Wisdom, faithfulness, economy; flags extravagance
  [71:4:0.3] Performance | Discretionary | 3 Es: economy, efficiency, effectiveness
[71:4:1] - **Social audits** feed CAG performance audits; MGNREGA social audit standards **2016**.

## SUB 6 | Audit Reports – Article 151
[71:6:0] # Report to Action
[71:6:1] - 1. CAG report → 2. **President / Governor** → 3. Laid before **Parliament / Legislature** → 4. **PAC** examines (PSUs: **Committee on Public Undertakings**) → 5. **Action Taken Notes** by the executive.
[71:6:2] - Three Union reports: **Appropriation Accounts, Finance Accounts, Public Undertakings** (not Estimates).
[71:6:3] - **Final action** on reports rests with **Parliament** / State Legislature.
[71:6:4] Reports go first to the President/Governor, **not directly** to the PAC.
[71:6:5] # Public Accounts Committee
[71:6:6] - Set up **1921** (GoI Act, 1919); **22 members (15 LS + 7 RS)**, elected yearly by **STV**; **no Minister**.
[71:6:7] - Chair appointed by the Speaker, from the **Opposition since 1967**; 2026-27 chair **K.C. Venugopal** (earlier: Murali Manohar Joshi); reports to the **Speaker**.
[71:6:8] - CAG = PAC's "**friend, philosopher and guide**" (attends meetings; not its chairman).
[71:6:9] - Recommendations **not binding**; backlog of Action Taken Notes (23 May 2026).
