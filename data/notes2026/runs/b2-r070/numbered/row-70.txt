## SUB 0 | Finance Commission – Article 280, Composition and Qualifications
[0:0] # Constitutional basis
[0:1] - **Article 280** (Part **XII**) provides for the Finance Commission (FC), a **quasi-judicial constitutional body**; Articles **280 and 281** deal with it (Art. **290** = pensions, not the FC; Art. 148 = CAG).
[0:2] - Constituted by the **President** within two years of the Constitution's start and then **every fifth year or earlier** as he considers necessary – a **temporary** body, not permanent.
[0:3] - The Constitution envisages the FC as the **balancing wheel of fiscal federalism** (Laxmikanth); "Federal Finance Commission" = finances **between the Centre and the States**.
[0:4] - **Art. 281:** the **President** lays every recommendation, with an **explanatory memorandum** on the action taken, before each House of Parliament.
[0:5] - Recommendations are **advisory, not binding**; **P.V. Rajamannar** (4th FC): they should not be turned down without **very compelling reasons**.
[0:6] # Composition and qualifications
[0:7] - **Chairman + four other members** (5 in all), appointed by the **President** (Art. 280(1)) – not a Chairman and six members.
[0:8] - They hold office for **such period as the President specifies**; members are **eligible for reappointment**; no term fixed in the Constitution.
[0:9] - **Parliament** fixes qualifications and manner of selection (Art. 280(2)) – the **Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951** (not the President, the Union Cabinet or the Finance Minister).
[0:10] TABLE head: Post | Qualification (1951 Act)
  [0:10.0] Chairman | Experience in public affairs
  [0:10.1] Member | High Court judge or qualified to be one (one of the possible fields – not compulsory)
  [0:10.2] Member | Special knowledge of government finance and accounts
  [0:10.3] Member | Wide experience in financial matters and administration
  [0:10.4] Member | Special knowledge of economics
[0:11] - There is no member post for a monetary policy expert or a Parliament member.
[0:12] - The FC has the **powers of a civil court** (summoning persons, production of documents).
[0:13] - It reports to the **President**, not to NITI Aayog or Parliament directly.
[0:14] - The **101st Amendment (2016)** introduced **GST** – it did not implement any FC report.
[0:15] > Statement items test: Art. 280 vs 290, "every fifth year", Chairman + 4, Parliament (not President) fixes qualifications, report → President → Art. 281.
[0:16] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 20 p.6-7, 26 p.10-12; Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.46 p.614-616
## SUB 1 | Functions – Article 280(3) and the Divisible Pool
[1:0] # Functions (Art. 280(3)) – recommendations to the President
[1:1] - 1. **Distribution of the net proceeds of taxes** between the Centre and the States (**vertical**) and **allocation among the States** (**horizontal**).
[1:2] - 2. **Principles governing grants-in-aid** to the States out of the **Consolidated Fund of India** (Art. **275**).
[1:3] - 3. **Measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State** to supplement the resources of **Panchayats and Municipalities**, on the basis of the **State Finance Commission**'s recommendations (added by the **73rd/74th Amendments, 1992**).
[1:4] - 4. **Any other matter** referred by the President **in the interests of sound finance**.
[1:5] - Till 1960 it also suggested grants to Assam, Bihar, Odisha and West Bengal in lieu of jute export duty (Art. 273).
[1:6] - **Main function:** deciding the **share of States in Central taxes** – the body that recommends the principles of sharing revenues (not the Inter-State Council, NDC, NITI Aayog, Planning Commission or Finance Ministry).
[1:7] - **Not FC functions:** allocation of funds to constitutional bodies, maintaining income-expenditure details of governments, financial control over the Centre or States, fixing **GST rates**, releasing funds.
[1:8] - **Agricultural income tax** is a **State** tax (State List entry 46) – the Constitution assigns it; UPSC 1995's key names the FC as the body linked to sharing taxes with States.
[1:9] # Divisible pool
[1:10] - **Divisible pool** = gross tax revenue − **cesses, surcharges** and **cost of collection**; the **CAG certifies** net proceeds (Art. 279).
[1:11] - Rising **cesses and surcharges** shrink the divisible pool, so the States' real share of gross tax revenue is below the headline %.
[1:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 20 p.7, 26 p.10, 12; Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.46 p.614-615

## SUB 3 | 14th Finance Commission (2015–20)
[3:0] - Constituted **2 January 2013** (Y.V. Reddy); award **1 April 2015 – 31 March 2020**; GoI accepted the report on **24 February 2015**.
[3:1] - Share in the divisible pool raised **32% → 42%** – the **largest increase ever**.
[3:2] - **Tax devolution = the primary route** of transfer (a "compositional shift" from grants to devolution); **sector-specific grants dropped**; ignored the **Plan/non-Plan** distinction.
[3:3] - Made **no distinction** between **special and general category** States – a formula-based mechanism with no room for political bargaining (the Constitution has no provision for Special Category Status).
[3:4] - **Base year** for its projections: **2014-15** (reassessed base year).
[3:5] - Population base: **1971 and 2011**; **fiscal discipline** dropped as a criterion; **forest cover** added.
[3:6] TABLE head: Criterion (14th FC) | Weight
  [3:6.0] Income distance | 50% (highest)
  [3:6.1] Population 1971 | 17.5%
  [3:6.2] Population 2011 (demographic change) | 10%
  [3:6.3] Area | 15%
  [3:6.4] Forest cover | 7.5%
[3:7] - **Andhra Pradesh's share: 4.305%** (residuary AP, first award after bifurcation).
[3:8] - Local-body grants **₹2,87,436 crore** (panchayats ₹2,00,292 crore) as **basic and performance** grants to duly constituted bodies (2011 population 90%, area 10%).
[3:9] - After the 42% award the Centre raised **non-shareable cesses**.

## SUB 5 | 15th and 16th Finance Commissions – Devolution Formula
[5:0] # 15th Finance Commission (2020–26)
[5:1] - Formed **27 November 2017**; Chairman **N.K. Singh**; **first FC to cut** the vertical share: **42% → 41%**, because of the change in **J&K's status** (2019).
[5:2] - Used **2011 population** only; **added demographic performance and tax effort**.
[5:3] - Local-body grants **₹4.36 lakh crore** (urban share 36%).
[5:4] # 16th Finance Commission (2026–31)
[5:5] - Constituted **31 December 2023**; Chairman **Arvind Panagariya**; members **T. Rabi Sankar, Annie George Mathew, Manoj Panda, Soumya Kanti Ghosh**; award **2026-27 to 2030-31**.
[5:6] - Terms of reference taken straight from the **Constitution**; population base 2011 (demographic performance measured against 1971).
[5:7] - Report to the President **17 November 2025**; tabled in Parliament **1 February 2026** (Budget day); devolution accepted in the **Budget 2026-27**; **41%** of the divisible pool retained; total transfers **₹25.44 lakh crore** for 2026-27.
[5:8] - Total grants **₹9.47 lakh crore**; **revenue-deficit grants discontinued**; **Kerala** got neither revenue-deficit nor State-specific grants.
[5:9] - **Local-body grants ₹7.91 lakh crore**: rural ~**₹4.35 lakh crore**, urban ~**₹3.56 lakh crore** (= **45%**); urbanisation premium ₹10,000 crore.
[5:10] - **Disaster corpus ₹2.04 lakh crore**; sharing **90:10** NE/Himalayan States, **75:25** others; **heatwaves and lightning** proposed as new notified disasters.
[5:11] - **State deficits capped at 3% of GSDP**.
[5:12] - All five **southern States** gained: Karnataka **4.131%** (15th 3.64%), Tamil Nadu **4.097%**; southern States still call population and income distance criteria unfair.

  [5:13.2] Area | 15 | 10

[5:15] - Number game: 32 → 42 → 41 → 41 (13th → 14th → 15th → 16th).
