## SUB 0 | Air Masses – Nature, Source Regions and Classification
[0:0] # Air mass (asked 2 times)
[0:1] - **Air mass** = a large body of air with relatively **uniform temperature and humidity**, formed over a uniform **source region** (land **or** ocean); it controls **temperature, humidity, stability and precipitation**, and a new air mass changes the weather.
[0:2] - Ideal source regions are **high-pressure areas with gentle, divergent (anticyclonic) circulation** – the subtropical highs and the polar highs (Siberian, Canadian). Air masses do **not** develop in **cyclonic** conditions, and there are no major source regions in the mid-latitudes (NDA 2019).
[0:3] - **Bergeron** code: **first letter = moisture** (c = continental, dry; m = maritime, moist); **second letter = thermal source region** (T tropical, P polar, A arctic, E equatorial).
[0:4] TABLE head: Code | Source region | Properties
  [0:4.0] mE | Warm equatorial oceans | Hot, very humid
  [0:4.1] mT | Tropical oceans (Gulf of Mexico, Pacific, Atlantic) | Warm, moist, unstable
  [0:4.2] cT | Hot deserts – Sahara, Arabia, Australia | Hot, dry, stable
  [0:4.3] mP | Cold high-latitude (polar) oceans, 40°–60° | Cool, moist
  [0:4.4] cP | Siberia, northern Canada (cold land) | Cold, dry, stable
  [0:4.5] cA | Arctic basin, Greenland ice | Very cold, very dry
[0:5] - NDA 2015 correct pair: **Maritime Equatorial – warm oceans in the equatorial zone**; mP is not from warm tropical oceans, cT is a desert (not ocean) air mass, cP forms over land.
[0:6] - **Polar anticyclones** (Siberian, Canadian, Antarctic Highs) are built by cold, dense **cP/cA** air: **cold-core, strongest near the surface**; clear skies, fog, frost.
[0:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 06 p.34-37; NextGen Rocket General Geography #8 p.55; app notes 4:18 sub 0
## SUB 1 | Air-mass Modification
[1:0] # Modification
[1:1] - Leaving its source, an air mass **exchanges heat and moisture** with the surfaces it crosses and loses its original character.
[1:2] TABLE head: Situation | Result | Weather
  [1:2.0] Cold air over a warmer surface | Heated from below, unstable | Convection, cumulus, showers
  [1:2.1] Warm air over a colder surface | Cooled from below, stable | Fog, stratus, drizzle
  [1:2.2] Over ocean / land | Gains / loses moisture | –
[1:3] - A new air mass can cause a **sudden rise or fall in temperature**; cold waves, heat waves, blizzards follow air-mass shifts.
[1:4] - **Hygroscopic nuclei**: dust and salt particles on which vapour condenses.
[1:5] - **Atmospheric pressure** = force exerted by the weight of air; **wind** blows from **high to low pressure**.

## SUB 2 | Fronts and the Polar Front Theory
[2:0] # Fronts
[2:1] - **Front** = sloping boundary zone between two contrasting air masses; lighter **warm air rises over denser cold air** (never the reverse) → adiabatic cooling, cloud, rain. **Mid-latitude (temperate) cyclones form along fronts**.
[2:2] TABLE head: Front | Formation | Slope | Weather
  [2:2.0] Warm | Warm air rides over cold | Gentle, gradual uplift | Prolonged light–moderate rain
  [2:2.1] Cold | Cold air undercuts warm, lifts it sharply | Steep | Cumulonimbus, heavy short showers, squalls, sudden temperature fall
  [2:2.2] Occluded | Cold front overtakes warm front | – | Mixed; last stage of a temperate cyclone
  [2:2.3] Stationary | Neither air mass advances | – | Cloud and rain for days
[2:3] - **Frontogenesis** = forming/sharpening of a front; **frontolysis** = its weakening and dissolution.
[2:4] - Warm front approach: **cirrus → cirrostratus → altostratus → nimbostratus**; after a cold front passes: **cold, clear air**. **Arcus (shelf/roll) clouds** form at the leading edge of thunderstorm outflows or cold fronts.
[2:5] # Polar front theory
[2:6] - By **Vilhelm and Jacob Bjerknes**, **Bergen School (Norway)**: wave on the polar front → warm and cold fronts → **occlusion** → dissipation.
[2:7] - **Western disturbances** reach NW India at the **occluded** stage.
[2:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 06 p.37-38; NextGen Rocket General Geography #8 p.56; app notes 4:18 sub 2-3
## SUB 3 | Air Masses Affecting India and in News
[3:0] # India and news
[3:1] - Winter: the **Himalayas block cold, dry Central Asian air** → north India about **3–8°C** warmer than places on the same latitude; the anticyclonic sinking air gives **clear skies, light northerly winds, low humidity, large daily range**.
[3:2] - SW monsoon = **moist maritime Indian Ocean air** fed by the low-level **Somali Jet**; AP also gets the **NE monsoon** when winter air returns over the Bay of Bengal.
[3:3] - Windward slope = orographic rain; **leeward slope = rain shadow**.
[3:4] - **Tornado Alley** (US Great Plains): **polar cold air meets warm, humid tropical (Gulf) air**.
[3:5] - **Strong polar vortex** keeps cold air confined to the north; **Arctic amplification**: a meandering jet carries warm air into the Arctic.
[3:6] - Jul 2025: an Antarctic high reaching far north brought snow to Chile's **Atacama** Desert, the first in over a decade.
