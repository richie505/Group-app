## SUB 0 | Clouds – Families, Types and Thunderclouds
[0:0] # Cloud basics (asked 16 times)
[0:1] - **Cloud** = mass of minute water droplets or ice crystals formed by **condensation** of water vapour in free air around **hygroscopic nuclei**, mainly by **adiabatic cooling** of rising air below its dew point – clouds are the result of **condensation** (UKPSC 2016), not evaporation.
[0:2] - Clouds **float** because of their **low density** (UPSC 1995): the droplets are tiny, fall extremely slowly and are held up by gentle updrafts.
[0:3] - Four basic forms: **cirrus, cumulus, stratus, nimbus** ("nimbus" = rain-bearing; black or dark grey, dense, opaque).
[0:4] TABLE head: Family (height) | Cloud | Appearance | Weather
  [0:4.0] High (8,000–12,000 m; ice crystals) | Cirrus | Thin, detached, feathery, silky, always white | Fair; no rain
  [0:4.1] High | Cirrostratus | Thin milky sheet; produces the halo | Warm front coming
  [0:4.2] High | Cirrocumulus | Small globular masses, ripples ("mackerel sky") | Fair
  [0:4.3] Middle (2–6 km) | Altostratus / altocumulus | Grey-blue sheet; Sun as through ground glass | Light continuous rain
  [0:4.4] Low (below 2 km) | Stratus, stratocumulus, nimbostratus | Layered; nimbostratus dark, thick, shapeless | Stratus – drizzle; nimbostratus – steady, continuous rain
  [0:4.5] Vertical development | Cumulus | Heaped cauliflower, flat base, cotton wool (4,000–7,000 m) | Fair; convective; grows into Cb
  [0:4.6] Vertical development (to ~15 km) | Cumulonimbus | Towering, anvil top | Thunderstorm, heavy short rain, hail, cloudburst
[0:5] - APPSC Group-I 2024 match: **Cirrus – silky; Cirrostratus – milky; Cirrocumulus – small globular masses; Cumulus – cauliflower** (A-II, B-I, C-IV, D-III).
[0:6] - **Cirrus clouds are composed of ice crystals** (UPSC 2004 correct statement); rising domes with flat base = cumulus, and cumulus is **not** a high cloud.
[0:7] - **Low cloud set: stratus, nimbus (nimbostratus), cumulus** (IAS 2004); **nimbostratus is a low cloud** (NDA 2007), cirro- = high, alto- = middle.
[0:8] - **Continuous precipitation = nimbostratus** (NDA 2018); **highly intense rain = cumulonimbus** (JPSC 2003).
[0:9] - Highest of a given set: **cirrostratus** is the only high cloud among altocumulus, altostratus, cumulus, cirrostratus (UP Lower 2009).
[0:10] - **Greatest vertical extent**, convectional rain with lightning and thunder = **cumulonimbus** (IAS 2003); rain-bearing clouds = **nimbostratus and cumulonimbus**.
[0:11] - **Tornadoes form from cumulonimbus** (supercell) clouds (APPSC FRO 2020); the funnel cloud and wall cloud are parts hanging from the Cb, not the parent cloud.
[0:12] - Rain clouds look **dark/black** because they are thick and dense with droplets, so very little sunlight gets through to the base (CDS 2012 – book key: the droplets **absorb** the sunlight; strictly the light is mostly scattered/reflected away).
[0:13] # Thunderclouds and thunder
[0:14] - Thundercloud (Cb) development needs **condensation, high temperature and humidity, orography and vertical winds – all four** (UPSC 2002; orographic thunderstorms are common).
[0:15] - **Thunder** is the sound of air **suddenly heated and expanded by lightning** (shock wave) – not clouds meeting, lightning "separating" clouds or upward air movement → **none of these** (IAS 2013).
[0:16] - **Waterspout**: funnel extending from a cumulus/cumulonimbus cloud **over water**; one recorded over the Brahmaputra in Barpeta, Assam (26 Aug 2026).

## SUB 1 | Evaporation, Latent Heat, Boiling Point and Adiabatic Change
[1:0] # Evaporation (asked 15 times)
[1:1] - **Evaporation / vaporisation** = change from liquid to gas (SSC 2019); it is a **physical change** (UPPSC 1992) and goes on at all temperatures from the surface.
[1:2] - Rate **rises with larger surface area** (CDS 2019), higher temperature, **lower humidity**, **stronger wind** and lower pressure; falls with salinity.
[1:3] - Oceans give **84%** of annual evaporation, continents 16%.
[1:4] # Latent heat
[1:5] - **Evaporation absorbs** latent heat; **condensation releases** it → powers **thunderstorms and cyclones**. Freezing (snow forming in clouds) also releases heat = **exothermic**; melting ice absorbs heat = **endothermic**.
[1:6] TABLE head: Change | Latent heat | Note
  [1:6.0] Fusion of ice | 80 cal/g (3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg) | Melts at 0°C; temperature constant
  [1:6.1] Vaporisation of water | 540 cal/g (2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg) | Boils at 100°C; temperature constant
[1:7] - Heat taken to turn liquid into gas **without change in temperature = latent heat of vaporisation** (NDA 2017); at the boiling point the **temperature remains constant** (SSC 2016).
[1:8] - Latent heat of vaporisation is **much greater** than that of fusion; **steam at 100°C holds more heat** than water at 100°C → **steam burns are worse** (SSC 2015) – it releases latent heat on condensing.
[1:9] - UPSC 2003: only "an air-conditioner extracts heat at the **evaporator** coils and rejects it at the **condenser** coils" is correct. **Refrigerator**: refrigerant absorbs heat on evaporation, releases it on condensation.
[1:10] # Pressure and boiling point
[1:11] - **Lower pressure lowers the boiling point** (Asst. Comm. 2018): at high altitude water boils below 100°C because **air pressure is less** (NDA 2017) → a **pressure cooker** raises pressure and boiling point (CDS 2010).
[1:12] # Adiabatic change and the uplift chain
[1:13] - **Adiabatic** temperature change = by **expansion (cooling) and compression (warming)** of air, without heat being added or removed (CDS 2018); compressing gas in a bicycle pump = **adiabatic heating**.
[1:14] - Air **descending** a mountain is compressed → **adiabatic warming** (AE 2017), as in the leeward rain shadow.
[1:15] - Uplift chain: warm air rises → **adiabatic cooling to the dew point** → **condensation** around nuclei → clouds → droplets grow and fall → precipitation. Rising air does **not** become warmer.
[1:16] ~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 05 p.22-24 (evaporation, condensation, adiabatic change); NextGen Rocket General Geography #7 p.52; NCERT Science (latent heat, boiling point); app notes 4:17 sub 1

## SUB 3 | Precipitation – Forms and Types of Rainfall
[3:0] # Forms (asked 10 times)
[3:1] TABLE head: Form | Feature
  [3:1.0] Rain | Drops over 0.5 mm
  [3:1.1] Drizzle | Fine drops under 0.5 mm (APPSC FSO flashcard), mostly from stratus
  [3:1.2] Snow | Ice crystals formed below 0°C
  [3:1.3] Sleet | Rain frozen into pellets falling through cold air
  [3:1.4] Hail | Layered ice balls (5–50 mm) in cumulonimbus with strong updrafts
[3:2] - Raindrops grow by two processes: **collision–coalescence** in **warm clouds that do not extend beyond the freezing level** (CDS 2018) and the **ice-crystal (Bergeron)** process in clouds rising above it.
[3:3] - **Langmuir precipitation** = Langmuir's **chain reaction** of drop collision, break-up and coalescence in warm convective clouds, giving heavy showers – APPSC FSO key: **cloud burst**.
[3:4] - **Cloudburst**: about **100 mm/hour** or more over **20–30 sq km** from cumulonimbus; the stored water falls when **updrafts weaken**; Dharali, **Uttarkashi** (Aug 2025).
[3:5] - **Blood (coloured) rain**: dust or spores mixed with raindrops – Kerala's red rain (**2001**). 16th Finance Commission recommended adding **heatwaves and lightning** to notified disasters (Apr 2026).
[3:6] # Types of rainfall
[3:7] TABLE head: Type | Mechanism | Where
  [3:7.0] Convectional | Strong heating → air rises, cools → cumulonimbus; afternoon, short, heavy | Equatorial belt (Amazon, Congo, SE Asian islands); mango showers
  [3:7.1] Cyclonic / frontal | Warm air lifted over cold air along fronts – cyclonic activity (SSC 2011) | Temperate regions; western disturbances (10–15 cm winter rain, NW India)
  [3:7.2] Orographic (relief) | Moist wind forced up a mountain barrier | Western Ghats (Agumbe), Mawsynram; leeward = rain shadow
[3:8] - **Amazon basin** has convectional rainfall (IAS 2007); convectional rain occurs **daily in the afternoon** near the equator, is **short** and comes from **cumulonimbus** – all three true (NDA 2008).
[3:9] - Equatorial areas get rain **all year** because high temperature and humidity cause **afternoon convectional rain** – A and R true, R explains A (IAS 2003; UPSC 1996).
[3:10] - **Rain-shadow effect** and **inversion of rainfall** (rain increases with height only up to a level, then decreases) are features of **orographic rainfall** (NDA 2011; NDA 2019).
[3:11] - Rain-shadow area = dry **leeward** side; descending air warms and dries (Pune ~70 cm vs Mahabaleshwar 600 cm). **Eastern side of the Western Ghats** is leeward → peninsular rainfall **falls west to east**; **Aravallis** give little rain to Rajasthan as winds blow **parallel** to them.
[3:12] - AP: average rainfall ~**966 mm**, about **two-thirds from the SW monsoon**; **Rayalaseema** in rain shadow, **Hagari valley lowest**, **Sileru–Sabari valleys highest**; Sri Sathya Sai district 57% deficit (Aug 2026).
[3:13] ~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 05 p.31-35; NextGen Rocket General Geography #7 p.52; Langmuir (1948) chain-reaction theory (atmos.albany.edu precipitation processes notes); app notes 4:17 sub 5-7
## SUB 4 | Cloud Seeding and Acid Rain
[4:0] # Cloud seeding (asked 10 times)
[4:1] - Artificial rain uses **silver iodide (AgI)** – its crystal shape mimics ice (UPPCS 2008, 2013, Spl. 2008; APPSC flashcard); also **potassium iodide**, **sodium chloride**, **dry ice** (solid CO₂), liquid propane; not calcium carbonate.
[4:2] - Artificial rain to reduce air pollution: **silver iodide and potassium iodide** (UPSC 2025).
[4:3] - **AP cloud seeding (July 2004)**: **silver iodide–sodium chloride**.
[4:4] - Works **only when suitable clouds already exist**. Kazakhstan began seeding with UAE help (Aug 2026).
[4:5] # Acid rain
[4:6] - **Acid rain**: **pH below 5.6**, mainly from **SO₂ and NOx**; term coined by **Robert Angus Smith (1872)** in Manchester.
[4:7] - Coal thermal stations cause acid rain, but through **SO₂ and NOx**, not oxides of carbon → A and R true, R not the explanation (UPSC 2003).
[4:8] - UPPCS 2024: CO₂, SO₂ and NO₂ dissolve in rain to give acid rain, and air is polluted by these gases → **both true, R explains A**.
[4:9] - **Rain cleans a polluted environment** – it washes dust and gases out of the air (UP UDA/LDA 2013).
[4:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment (acid rain); UPSC 2025 key; app notes 4:17 sub 9
## SUB 5 | Hydrological Cycle and Transpiration
[5:0] # Water cycle (asked 9 times)
[5:1] - Continuous exchange of water by **evaporation, transpiration, condensation and precipitation**; correct sequence **evaporation → condensation → precipitation → infiltration** (NDA 2010).
[5:2] TABLE head: In the cycle | NOT in the cycle
  [5:2.0] Evaporation, transpiration (= evapotranspiration) | Photosynthesis
  [5:2.1] Sublimation, condensation, precipitation | Combustion
  [5:2.2] Infiltration/percolation, runoff, groundwater flow | Respiration, hydration
[5:3] - **Hydration** = water absorbed into minerals (a weathering process) – **not** part of the hydrological cycle (APPSC Group-1).
[5:4] - **Underground water may be connected to surface water** (CDS 2015); transpiration does contribute to clouds and rainfall recharges groundwater.
[5:5] - **Forests** control the hydrological cycle (UPRO 2023) – "water engineers": interception, transpiration, deep roots, groundwater recharge.
[5:6] # Transpiration
[5:7] - Plants lose water by **transpiration** (APPSC Group-1), mainly through **stomata**; it pulls water up, **cools the plant** = "**nature's air conditioner**", adds moisture to air and enhances rainfall – it does **not decrease humidity**.
[5:8] - Rate is **reduced by decreased light intensity** (stomata close), high humidity, low temperature and still air.
[5:9] - **Hydrometeorology** = transfer of water and energy between land surface and lower atmosphere. India: precipitation ~**4,000 BCM**/year; utilisable **1,139 BCM** (**690 surface + 449 groundwater**).
[5:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 05 p.19; NCERT Science Class 10 (transpiration); app notes 4:17 sub 2
## SUB 6 | Humidity and Dew Point
[6:0] # Measures of humidity (asked 7 times)
[6:1] - **Humidity** = water vapour present in the air – the result of the **presence of moisture in the air** (JPSC 2016); humid summer heat = **muggy** weather (UPUDA 2002).
[6:2] TABLE head: Measure | Meaning | Unit
  [6:2.0] Absolute humidity | Mass of water vapour per unit volume of air | g/m³
  [6:2.1] Specific humidity | Vapour per unit weight of air; unchanged by volume/temperature change | g/kg
  [6:2.2] Relative humidity (RH) | Actual vapour ÷ capacity at that temperature × 100 | %
  [6:2.3] Dew point | Temperature at which air becomes saturated; condensation begins below it | °C
[6:3] - When air temperature **equals the dew point, RH = 100%** (APPSC FSO flashcard) = saturation.
[6:4] - **Saturation deficit** = capacity minus actual vapour; **dew point** = temperature to which air must be cooled to saturate → both correct (NDA 2009).
[6:5] - Warmer air can hold **more** vapour – capacity **increases** with temperature (JPSC 2003); same vapour + warming → **RH falls**.
[6:6] - Water vapour: 0–4% of the air (up to **4%** in humid tropics), **decreases with height and latitude**; RH higher over oceans than continents.
[6:7] - AP: coastal Andhra RH **70–90%**; Rayalaseema below **30%** in peak summer.
[6:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 05 p.19-22; NextGen Rocket General Geography #7 p.52; app notes 4:17 sub 0
## SUB 7 | Optical Phenomena – Rainbow and Halo
[7:0] # Rainbow and halo (asked 7 times)
[7:1] TABLE head: Phenomenon | Cause
  [7:1.0] Rainbow | Refraction, dispersion and (total) internal reflection of sunlight in raindrops; Sun behind the observer
  [7:1.1] Halo | Refraction through hexagonal ice crystals of thin high cirrus/cirrostratus; ring of about 22°
  [7:1.2] Corona | Diffraction by small water droplets of thin clouds
[7:2] - Rainbow is formed by **reflection, refraction and dispersion** (APPSC ASO 2017; APPSC Group-1); if only one is offered, **dispersion** (APPSC ABCWO; NDA 2017).
[7:3] - **Primary rainbow**: one internal reflection, **red outside, violet inside** → terminal bands **violet and red** (UP Lower 2015). Secondary: two reflections, colours reversed.
[7:4] - APPSC Group-I 2024: Sun halo = refraction in hexagonal ice crystals (I) and a **22° ring** (II) correct; III wrong – the halo comes from thin **cirrostratus**, not cumulonimbus.
[7:5] - Sun's halo = refraction in **ice crystals in cirrus clouds** (IAS 2002).

## SUB 8 | Forms of Condensation – Dew, Frost, Fog and Mist
[8:0] # Dew, frost, fog, mist (asked 5 times)
[8:1] TABLE head: Form | Condition
  [8:1.0] Dew | Droplets on cool surfaces; dew point above 0°C
  [8:1.1] Frost | Dew point at or below 0°C; ice crystals (Lambasingi, AP)
  [8:1.2] Fog | Cloud at ground level; visibility under 1 km
  [8:1.3] Mist | More moisture per nucleus; visibility 1–2 km; a result of condensation (CDS 2008)
  [8:1.4] Smog | Fog + smoke; London smog sulphurous, Los Angeles photochemical (PAN)
[8:2] - Outside of a glass of cold water gets wet by **condensation of atmospheric water vapour** (APPSC ASWO screening).
[8:3] - Dew/frost need **clear sky, calm air, high RH, long cold nights**; no dew on windy nights.
[8:4] - **No dew on a cloudy night: clouds reflect back the Earth's radiation**, keeping the surface warm (UPSC 2019 ×3).
