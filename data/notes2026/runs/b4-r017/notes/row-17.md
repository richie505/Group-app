=== Clouds – Families, Types and Thunderclouds
# Cloud basics (asked 16 times)
- **Cloud** = mass of minute water droplets or ice crystals formed by **condensation** of water vapour in free air around **hygroscopic nuclei**, mainly by **adiabatic cooling** of rising air below its dew point – clouds are the result of **condensation** (UKPSC 2016), not evaporation.
- Clouds **float** because of their **low density** (UPSC 1995): the droplets are tiny, fall extremely slowly and are held up by gentle updrafts.
- Four basic forms: **cirrus, cumulus, stratus, nimbus** ("nimbus" = rain-bearing; black or dark grey, dense, opaque).
| Family (height) | Cloud | Appearance | Weather |
|---|---|---|---|
| High (8,000–12,000 m; **ice crystals**) | **Cirrus** | Thin, detached, **feathery, silky**, always white | Fair; no rain |
| High | **Cirrostratus** | Thin **milky** sheet; produces the **halo** | Warm front coming |
| High | **Cirrocumulus** | **Small globular masses**, ripples ("mackerel sky") | Fair |
| Middle (2–6 km) | **Altostratus / altocumulus** | Grey-blue sheet; Sun as through **ground glass** | Light continuous rain |
| Low (below 2 km) | **Stratus, stratocumulus, nimbostratus** | Layered; nimbostratus dark, thick, shapeless | Stratus – **drizzle**; nimbostratus – **steady, continuous rain** |
| Vertical development | **Cumulus** | Heaped **cauliflower**, **flat base**, cotton wool (4,000–7,000 m) | Fair; convective; grows into Cb |
| Vertical development (to ~15 km) | **Cumulonimbus** | Towering, **anvil** top | Thunderstorm, heavy short rain, hail, cloudburst |
- APPSC Group-I 2024 match: **Cirrus – silky; Cirrostratus – milky; Cirrocumulus – small globular masses; Cumulus – cauliflower** (A-II, B-I, C-IV, D-III).
- **Cirrus clouds are composed of ice crystals** (UPSC 2004 correct statement); rising domes with flat base = cumulus, and cumulus is **not** a high cloud.
- **Low cloud set: stratus, nimbus (nimbostratus), cumulus** (IAS 2004); **nimbostratus is a low cloud** (NDA 2007), cirro- = high, alto- = middle.
- **Continuous precipitation = nimbostratus** (NDA 2018); **highly intense rain = cumulonimbus** (JPSC 2003).
- Highest of a given set: **cirrostratus** is the only high cloud among altocumulus, altostratus, cumulus, cirrostratus (UP Lower 2009).
- **Greatest vertical extent**, convectional rain with lightning and thunder = **cumulonimbus** (IAS 2003); rain-bearing clouds = **nimbostratus and cumulonimbus**.
- **Tornadoes form from cumulonimbus** (supercell) clouds (APPSC FRO 2020); the funnel cloud and wall cloud are parts hanging from the Cb, not the parent cloud.
- Rain clouds look **dark/black** because they are thick and dense with droplets, so very little sunlight gets through to the base (CDS 2012 – book key: the droplets **absorb** the sunlight; strictly the light is mostly scattered/reflected away).
# Thunderclouds and thunder
- Thundercloud (Cb) development needs **condensation, high temperature and humidity, orography and vertical winds – all four** (UPSC 2002; orographic thunderstorms are common).
- **Thunder** is the sound of air **suddenly heated and expanded by lightning** (shock wave) – not clouds meeting, lightning "separating" clouds or upward air movement → **none of these** (IAS 2013).
- **Waterspout**: funnel extending from a cumulus/cumulonimbus cloud **over water**; one recorded over the Brahmaputra in Barpeta, Assam (26 Aug 2026).

=== Evaporation, Latent Heat, Boiling Point and Adiabatic Change
# Evaporation (asked 15 times)
- **Evaporation / vaporisation** = change from liquid to gas (SSC 2019); it is a **physical change** (UPPSC 1992) and goes on at all temperatures from the surface.
- Rate **rises with larger surface area** (CDS 2019), higher temperature, **lower humidity**, **stronger wind** and lower pressure; falls with salinity.
- Oceans give **84%** of annual evaporation, continents 16%.
# Latent heat
- **Evaporation absorbs** latent heat; **condensation releases** it → powers **thunderstorms and cyclones**. Freezing (snow forming in clouds) also releases heat = **exothermic**; melting ice absorbs heat = **endothermic**.
| Change | Latent heat | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Fusion of ice | **80 cal/g** (3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg) | Melts at 0°C; temperature constant |
| Vaporisation of water | **540 cal/g** (2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg) | Boils at 100°C; temperature constant |
- Heat taken to turn liquid into gas **without change in temperature = latent heat of vaporisation** (NDA 2017); at the boiling point the **temperature remains constant** (SSC 2016).
- Latent heat of vaporisation is **much greater** than that of fusion; **steam at 100°C holds more heat** than water at 100°C → **steam burns are worse** (SSC 2015) – it releases latent heat on condensing.
- UPSC 2003: only "an air-conditioner extracts heat at the **evaporator** coils and rejects it at the **condenser** coils" is correct. **Refrigerator**: refrigerant absorbs heat on evaporation, releases it on condensation.
# Pressure and boiling point
- **Lower pressure lowers the boiling point** (Asst. Comm. 2018): at high altitude water boils below 100°C because **air pressure is less** (NDA 2017) → a **pressure cooker** raises pressure and boiling point (CDS 2010).
# Adiabatic change and the uplift chain
- **Adiabatic** temperature change = by **expansion (cooling) and compression (warming)** of air, without heat being added or removed (CDS 2018); compressing gas in a bicycle pump = **adiabatic heating**.
- Air **descending** a mountain is compressed → **adiabatic warming** (AE 2017), as in the leeward rain shadow.
- Uplift chain: warm air rises → **adiabatic cooling to the dew point** → **condensation** around nuclei → clouds → droplets grow and fall → precipitation. Rising air does **not** become warmer.

=== Precipitation – Forms and Types of Rainfall
# Forms (asked 10 times)
| Form | Feature |
|---|---|
| Rain | Drops **over 0.5 mm** |
| **Drizzle** | Fine drops **under 0.5 mm** (APPSC FSO flashcard), mostly from stratus |
| Snow | Ice crystals formed below 0°C |
| Sleet | Rain frozen into pellets falling through cold air |
| Hail | Layered ice balls (5–50 mm) in cumulonimbus with strong updrafts |
- Raindrops grow by two processes: **collision–coalescence** in **warm clouds that do not extend beyond the freezing level** (CDS 2018) and the **ice-crystal (Bergeron)** process in clouds rising above it.
- **Langmuir precipitation** = Langmuir's **chain reaction** of drop collision, break-up and coalescence in warm convective clouds, giving heavy showers – APPSC FSO key: **cloud burst**.
- **Cloudburst**: about **100 mm/hour** or more over **20–30 sq km** from cumulonimbus; the stored water falls when **updrafts weaken**; Dharali, **Uttarkashi** (Aug 2025).
- **Blood (coloured) rain**: dust or spores mixed with raindrops – Kerala's red rain (**2001**). 16th Finance Commission recommended adding **heatwaves and lightning** to notified disasters (Apr 2026).
# Types of rainfall
| Type | Mechanism | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Convectional** | Strong heating → air rises, cools → cumulonimbus; **afternoon**, short, heavy | **Equatorial belt** (Amazon, Congo, SE Asian islands); mango showers |
| **Cyclonic / frontal** | Warm air lifted over cold air along fronts – **cyclonic activity** (SSC 2011) | Temperate regions; western disturbances (10–15 cm winter rain, NW India) |
| **Orographic (relief)** | Moist wind forced up a mountain barrier | Western Ghats (Agumbe), Mawsynram; leeward = **rain shadow** |
- **Amazon basin** has convectional rainfall (IAS 2007); convectional rain occurs **daily in the afternoon** near the equator, is **short** and comes from **cumulonimbus** – all three true (NDA 2008).
- Equatorial areas get rain **all year** because high temperature and humidity cause **afternoon convectional rain** – A and R true, R explains A (IAS 2003; UPSC 1996).
- **Rain-shadow effect** and **inversion of rainfall** (rain increases with height only up to a level, then decreases) are features of **orographic rainfall** (NDA 2011; NDA 2019).
- Rain-shadow area = dry **leeward** side; descending air warms and dries (Pune ~70 cm vs Mahabaleshwar 600 cm). **Eastern side of the Western Ghats** is leeward → peninsular rainfall **falls west to east**; **Aravallis** give little rain to Rajasthan as winds blow **parallel** to them.
- AP: average rainfall ~**966 mm**, about **two-thirds from the SW monsoon**; **Rayalaseema** in rain shadow, **Hagari valley lowest**, **Sileru–Sabari valleys highest**; Sri Sathya Sai district 57% deficit (Aug 2026).

=== Cloud Seeding and Acid Rain
# Cloud seeding (asked 10 times)
- Artificial rain uses **silver iodide (AgI)** – its crystal shape mimics ice (UPPCS 2008, 2013, Spl. 2008; APPSC flashcard); also **potassium iodide**, **sodium chloride**, **dry ice** (solid CO₂), liquid propane; not calcium carbonate.
- Artificial rain to reduce air pollution: **silver iodide and potassium iodide** (UPSC 2025).
- **AP cloud seeding (July 2004)**: **silver iodide–sodium chloride**.
- Works **only when suitable clouds already exist**. Kazakhstan began seeding with UAE help (Aug 2026).
# Acid rain
- **Acid rain**: **pH below 5.6**, mainly from **SO₂ and NOx**; term coined by **Robert Angus Smith (1872)** in Manchester.
- Coal thermal stations cause acid rain, but through **SO₂ and NOx**, not oxides of carbon → A and R true, R not the explanation (UPSC 2003).
- UPPCS 2024: CO₂, SO₂ and NO₂ dissolve in rain to give acid rain, and air is polluted by these gases → **both true, R explains A**.
- **Rain cleans a polluted environment** – it washes dust and gases out of the air (UP UDA/LDA 2013).

=== Hydrological Cycle and Transpiration
# Water cycle (asked 9 times)
- Continuous exchange of water by **evaporation, transpiration, condensation and precipitation**; correct sequence **evaporation → condensation → precipitation → infiltration** (NDA 2010).
| In the cycle | NOT in the cycle |
|---|---|
| Evaporation, transpiration (= evapotranspiration) | Photosynthesis |
| Sublimation, condensation, precipitation | Combustion |
| Infiltration/percolation, runoff, groundwater flow | Respiration, **hydration** |
- **Hydration** = water absorbed into minerals (a weathering process) – **not** part of the hydrological cycle (APPSC Group-1).
- **Underground water may be connected to surface water** (CDS 2015); transpiration does contribute to clouds and rainfall recharges groundwater.
- **Forests** control the hydrological cycle (UPRO 2023) – "water engineers": interception, transpiration, deep roots, groundwater recharge.
# Transpiration
- Plants lose water by **transpiration** (APPSC Group-1), mainly through **stomata**; it pulls water up, **cools the plant** = "**nature's air conditioner**", adds moisture to air and enhances rainfall – it does **not decrease humidity**.
- Rate is **reduced by decreased light intensity** (stomata close), high humidity, low temperature and still air.
- **Hydrometeorology** = transfer of water and energy between land surface and lower atmosphere. India: precipitation ~**4,000 BCM**/year; utilisable **1,139 BCM** (**690 surface + 449 groundwater**).

=== Humidity and Dew Point
# Measures of humidity (asked 7 times)
- **Humidity** = water vapour present in the air – the result of the **presence of moisture in the air** (JPSC 2016); humid summer heat = **muggy** weather (UPUDA 2002).
| Measure | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute humidity** | **Mass of water vapour per unit volume** of air | g/m³ |
| Specific humidity | Vapour per unit **weight** of air; unchanged by volume/temperature change | g/kg |
| Relative humidity (RH) | Actual vapour ÷ capacity at that temperature × 100 | % |
| **Dew point** | Temperature at which air becomes **saturated**; condensation begins below it | °C |
- When air temperature **equals the dew point, RH = 100%** (APPSC FSO flashcard) = saturation.
- **Saturation deficit** = capacity minus actual vapour; **dew point** = temperature to which air must be cooled to saturate → both correct (NDA 2009).
- Warmer air can hold **more** vapour – capacity **increases** with temperature (JPSC 2003); same vapour + warming → **RH falls**.
- Water vapour: 0–4% of the air (up to **4%** in humid tropics), **decreases with height and latitude**; RH higher over oceans than continents.
- AP: coastal Andhra RH **70–90%**; Rayalaseema below **30%** in peak summer.

=== Optical Phenomena – Rainbow and Halo
# Rainbow and halo (asked 7 times)
| Phenomenon | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Rainbow** | **Refraction, dispersion and (total) internal reflection** of sunlight in raindrops; Sun behind the observer |
| **Halo** | **Refraction** through hexagonal **ice crystals** of thin high **cirrus/cirrostratus**; ring of about **22°** |
| Corona | Diffraction by small water droplets of thin clouds |
- Rainbow is formed by **reflection, refraction and dispersion** (APPSC ASO 2017; APPSC Group-1); if only one is offered, **dispersion** (APPSC ABCWO; NDA 2017).
- **Primary rainbow**: one internal reflection, **red outside, violet inside** → terminal bands **violet and red** (UP Lower 2015). Secondary: two reflections, colours reversed.
- APPSC Group-I 2024: Sun halo = refraction in hexagonal ice crystals (I) and a **22° ring** (II) correct; III wrong – the halo comes from thin **cirrostratus**, not cumulonimbus.
- Sun's halo = refraction in **ice crystals in cirrus clouds** (IAS 2002).

=== Forms of Condensation – Dew, Frost, Fog and Mist
# Dew, frost, fog, mist (asked 5 times)
| Form | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Dew** | Droplets on cool surfaces; dew point **above 0°C** |
| Frost | Dew point at or below 0°C; ice crystals (Lambasingi, AP) |
| Fog | Cloud at ground level; visibility **under 1 km** |
| **Mist** | More moisture per nucleus; visibility 1–2 km; a result of **condensation** (CDS 2008) |
| Smog | Fog + smoke; London smog sulphurous, Los Angeles photochemical (PAN) |
- Outside of a glass of cold water gets wet by **condensation of atmospheric water vapour** (APPSC ASWO screening).
- Dew/frost need **clear sky, calm air, high RH, long cold nights**; no dew on windy nights.
- **No dew on a cloudy night: clouds reflect back the Earth's radiation**, keeping the surface warm (UPSC 2019 ×3).
