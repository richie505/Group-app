=== NOTES 4:17 | Humidity, Evaporation, Condensation, Clouds and Forms of Precipitation
## SUB 0 | Humidity – Measures and Distribution
[17:0:0] - **Humidity** = presence of water vapour in the air. Humid summer heat = **muggy weather**.
[17:0:1] TABLE head: Measure | Meaning | Unit
  [17:0:1.0] Absolute humidity | Weight of water vapour per unit volume of air | g/m³ (grains per cubic foot)
  [17:0:1.1] Specific humidity | Water vapour per unit weight of air; not changed when volume changes | g/kg
  [17:0:1.2] Relative humidity (RH) | Actual vapour ÷ capacity × 100 | %
  [17:0:1.3] Dew point | Temperature at which air becomes saturated; condensation starts | °C
  [17:0:1.4] Saturation | RH = 100% | —
[17:0:2] - Warm air holds more vapour. Same vapour + **temperature rises → RH falls**; cooling → RH rises.
[17:0:3] - RH is higher over oceans, lower over continents.
[17:0:4] - Water vapour: up to **4%** in humid tropics; **under 1%** in deserts and polar areas; **falls with height and latitude**.
[17:0:5] - **AP**: Coastal Andhra RH **70–90%** (high heat index); **Rayalaseema below 30%** in peak summer.
## SUB 1 | Evaporation, Latent Heat and the Uplift Chain
[17:1:0] # Latent Heat
[17:1:1] - **Evaporation takes** latent heat; **condensation releases** it → drives **thunderstorms and cyclones**.
[17:1:2] TABLE head: Change | Latent heat | Note
  [17:1:2.0] Fusion of ice | 80 cal/g (3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg) | Melts at 0°C; temperature constant
  [17:1:2.1] Vaporisation of water | 540 cal/g (2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg) | Boils at 100°C; temperature constant
[17:1:3] - Latent heat of vaporisation is **much greater** than that of fusion.
[17:1:4] - **Steam at 100°C burns worse** than boiling water – it releases latent heat on condensing.
[17:1:5] - Ice cools because its latent heat of fusion absorbs much heat.
[17:1:6] - **Refrigerator**: refrigerant absorbs heat on evaporation (inside), releases it on condensation (at the back). An **air-conditioner takes heat out** of room air.
[17:1:7] - **Higher pressure raises boiling point** (pressure cooker); lower pressure at altitude lowers it.
[17:1:8] # Uplift Chain
[17:1:9] - 1. Warm air uplifted.
[17:1:10] - 2. **Adiabatic cooling** to the dew point.
[17:1:11] - 3. **Condensation** (vapour → liquid or ice) around nuclei → **clouds** (tiny droplets or ice crystals).
[17:1:12] - 4. Droplets grow and fall → **precipitation** (rain, snow, sleet).
[17:1:13] - Clouds **float** because of low density – tiny droplets fall very slowly, held up by gentle updrafts.
## SUB 2 | Hydrological Cycle
[17:2:0] TABLE head: In the cycle | NOT in the cycle
  [17:2:0.0] Evaporation, transpiration (= evapotranspiration) | Photosynthesis
  [17:2:0.1] Sublimation, condensation, precipitation | Combustion
  [17:2:0.2] Infiltration/percolation, runoff, groundwater flow | Respiration
[17:2:1] - **Hydration:** water absorbed into minerals (a weathering process); **not** a stage of the hydrological cycle.
[17:2:2] - **Transpiration**: plants lose vapour mainly through **stomata**; moves water up, controls temperature.
[17:2:3] - **Forests = "water engineers"**: interception, transpiration, deep roots pump soil water, groundwater recharge.
[17:2:4] - **Hydrometeorology**: study of transfer of **water and energy between land surface and lower atmosphere** (rainfall, evaporation, floods, droughts).
[17:2:5] - **India water budget (2026)**: precipitation **~4,000 BCM**/year; utilisable **1,139 BCM** (**690 surface + 449 groundwater**).
[17:2:6] - Heavy bursts exceed soil infiltration → water pools and evaporates → recharge falls (Jun 2026).
## SUB 3 | Forms of Condensation
[17:3:0] TABLE head: Form | Condition
  [17:3:0.0] Dew | Moisture on cool surfaces when they fall below dew point; dew point above 0°C
  [17:3:0.1] Frost | Dew point at or below 0°C; ice crystals (Lambasingi, AP, ~2°C)
  [17:3:0.2] Fog | Cloud at ground level; often under inversion; visibility under 1 km
  [17:3:0.3] Mist | Thinner, wetter; visibility over 1 km
  [17:3:0.4] Smog | Fog + smoke/pollutants
[17:3:1] - Dew/frost best with **clear sky, calm air, high RH, long cold nights**.
[17:3:2] - Prevented by **cloudy or windy** nights and low humidity. **No dew on a cloudy night** – clouds reflect back Earth's radiation.
[17:3:3] London smog | sulphurous | cool, humid
Los Angeles smog | photochemical | sunlight, PAN, ground-level ozone
## SUB 4 | Clouds
[17:4:0] # Cloud Families
[17:4:1] TABLE head: Family | Cloud | Appearance | Weather
  [17:4:1.0] High (8,000–12,000 m; ice crystals) | Cirrus | Thin, feathery, silky, wispy | Fair; no rain
  [17:4:1.1] High | Cirrostratus | Thin milky sheet; causes halo | Warm front coming
  [17:4:1.2] High | Cirrocumulus | Small globular masses, ripples ("mackerel sky") | Fair
  [17:4:1.3] Middle (2–6 km) | Altostratus / altocumulus | Grey-blue sheet; sun as through ground glass | Light continuous rain
  [17:4:1.4] Low (below 2 km) | Nimbostratus | Dark, thick, shapeless | Steady, prolonged rain
  [17:4:1.5] Vertical (4,000–7,000 m) | Cumulus | Heaped cauliflower, flat base | Fair; convective; grows into Cb
  [17:4:1.6] Vertical (to ~15 km) | Cumulonimbus | Towering anvil top | Thunderstorms, rainstorms, hail, cloudbursts
[17:4:2] - Cirrus: ice crystals, above 20,000 ft. Cirrostratus: **highest-altitude cloud** (over 18,000 ft).
[17:4:3] - **Rain-bearing**: nimbostratus and cumulonimbus; "**nimbus**" = rain-bearing.
[17:4:4] - **Drizzle** comes from **stratus**.
[17:4:5] APPSC-G1 2024 match: cirrus – silky; cirrostratus – milky; cirrocumulus – globular; cumulus – cauliflower.
[17:4:6] APPSC 2019: **Cumulonimbus** produces rainstorms.
[17:4:7] # Thundercloud Growth and Waterspouts
[17:4:8] - Cumulonimbus grows from **condensation, high temperature and humidity, orography, vertical wind** (all four) – strong updrafts in hot, humid tropics.
[17:4:9] - **Waterspout**: funnel from cumulus/cumulonimbus over water; recorded over the **Brahmaputra in Barpeta, Assam** (26 Aug 2026).
## SUB 5 | Forms of Precipitation
[17:5:0] TABLE head: Form | Feature
  [17:5:0.0] Rain | Drops over 0.5 mm
  [17:5:0.1] Drizzle | Fine drops under 0.5 mm
  [17:5:0.2] Snow | Ice crystals formed below 0°C
  [17:5:0.3] Sleet | Rain frozen into pellets falling through cold air
  [17:5:0.4] Hail | Layered ice balls in cumulonimbus with strong updrafts
[17:5:1] - **Coloured (blood) rain**: dust or spores in rain – **Sirocco dust over Italy**; **Kerala's red rain (2001)**.
[17:5:2] - **Cloudburst**: about **100 mm/hour** or more over **20–30 sq km**; from cumulonimbus; orographic lifting in the Himalaya; usually June–August at 1,000–2,500 m; stored water falls when updrafts weaken. **Dharali, Uttarkashi** (Aug 2025).
[17:5:3] - **16th Finance Commission** recommended adding **heatwaves and lightning** to nationally notified disasters (Apr 2026).
## SUB 6 | Types of Precipitation
[17:6:0] TABLE head: Type | Mechanism | Place
  [17:6:0.0] Convectional | Strong heating → air rises, cools → cumulonimbus; afternoon showers | Equatorial belt (rain almost daily, all year); mango showers
  [17:6:0.1] Cyclonic / frontal | Warm air lifted over cold along fronts; cyclone lows | Temperate regions; Bay of Bengal cyclones on east coast; western disturbances (10–15 cm, NW India, rabi)
  [17:6:0.2] Orographic (relief) | Moist wind forced up a barrier; windward wet, leeward dry | Western Ghats (Agumbe), Mawsynram (windward)
[17:6:1] Mango showers = convectional; Mawsynram = orographic.
## SUB 7 | Rain Shadow and Relief
[17:7:0] - **Rain-shadow area**: dry **leeward** side; descending air **warms and dries**.
[17:7:1] - **Eastern side of Western Ghats** is leeward – interior Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, AP, Tamil Nadu get scanty rain; **peninsular rainfall falls west → east**.
[17:7:2] - **Himalayas**: orographic barrier, confine the monsoon.
[17:7:3] - **Aravallis**: little rain in Rajasthan – winds run **parallel** to them (no effective barrier).
[17:7:4] TABLE head: AP place | Rainfall
  [17:7:4.0] Rayalaseema | In rain shadow
  [17:7:4.1] Hagari valley | Lowest (below 51 cm)
  [17:7:4.2] Sileru, Sabari valleys (north coastal AP) | Highest (140–152 cm) – SW monsoon + Eastern Ghats
[17:7:5] - AP average rainfall **~966 mm**: about **two-thirds SW monsoon**, one-third NE monsoon.
[17:7:6] - 2026 El Niño year: **Sri Sathya Sai** district 57% rain deficit (Aug 2026).
## SUB 8 | Optical Phenomena
[17:8:0] TABLE head: Phenomenon | Cause
  [17:8:0.0] Halo (sun/moon) | Refraction through hexagonal ice crystals in thin high cirrostratus/cirrus; ring of about 22°
  [17:8:0.1] Rainbow | Refraction, dispersion and total internal reflection in raindrops; sun behind observer, drops in front
  [17:8:0.2] Corona | Diffraction by small water droplets of thin clouds; coloured rings close to sun/moon
[17:8:1] - **Primary rainbow**: one TIR; **red outside, violet inside**.
[17:8:2] - **Secondary rainbow**: **two** TIRs; **colours reversed**; fainter.
[17:8:3] APPSC-G1 2024 key: halo statements I and II correct.
## SUB 9 | Cloud Seeding and Acid Rain
[17:9:0] - **Cloud seeding**: **silver iodide (AgI)** – crystal shape mimics ice; others: potassium iodide, **sodium chloride**, **dry ice**, liquid propane.
[17:9:1] - Works **only when suitable clouds already exist**.
[17:9:2] - **AP cloud seeding (July 2004)**: silver iodide–sodium chloride.
[17:9:3] - Kazakhstan began seeding with **UAE** help for drought-hit Turkestan (Aug 2026).
[17:9:4] - **Acid rain**: **pH below 5.6**, from **SO₂ and NOx**; term coined by **Robert Angus Smith (1872)**.

=== NOTES 4:18 | Air Masses and Fronts – Classification and Associated Weather
## SUB 0 | Air Mass – Nature and Classification
[18:0:0] - **Air mass**: a large body of air with relatively **uniform temperature and humidity**; controls temperature, humidity, stability, precipitation.
[18:0:1] - Forms over a uniform **source region** (land or ocean); boundary between two air masses = **front**.
[18:0:2] - **Bergeron** code: **first letter = moisture** (c = continental, dry; m = maritime, moist); second letter = thermal source (T, P, A, E).
[18:0:3] TABLE head: Code | Source region | Properties
  [18:0:3.0] mT | Tropical oceans | Warm, moist
  [18:0:3.1] cT | Sahara, Arabia (hot deserts) | Hot, dry
  [18:0:3.2] mP | High-latitude (polar) oceans | Cold, moist
  [18:0:3.3] cP | Siberia, Canada | Cold, dry
  [18:0:3.4] cA | Arctic basin, Greenland ice | Very cold, very dry
  [18:0:3.5] mE | Equatorial oceans | Hot, very humid
[18:0:4] - **Polar anticyclones** (Siberian, Canadian, Antarctic Highs): cold, dense cP/cA air; cold-core, strongest near the surface; clear skies, fog, frost.
## SUB 1 | Air Mass Modification
[18:1:0] - Leaving its source, it exchanges heat and moisture with the surface and loses its nature.
[18:1:1] TABLE head: Situation | Result | Weather
  [18:1:1.0] Cold air over warmer surface | Heated from below, unstable | Convection, cumulus, showers
  [18:1:1.1] Warm air over colder surface | Cooled from below, stable | Fog, stratus, drizzle
  [18:1:1.2] Over ocean / land | Gains / loses moisture | –
[18:1:2] - **Hygroscopic nuclei**: dust and salt particles for condensation.
[18:1:3] - A new air mass can cause a sudden rise or fall in temperature.
[18:1:4] - **Atmospheric pressure** = force exerted by air; **wind** blows from high to low pressure, stronger with a steeper gradient.
## SUB 2 | Fronts – Types and Weather
[18:2:0] - Lighter warm air rises over denser cold air. Mid-latitude cyclones form along fronts.
[18:2:1] TABLE head: Front | Formation | Slope | Weather
  [18:2:1.0] Warm | Warm air rides over cold | Gentle, gradual uplift | Prolonged light-moderate rain
  [18:2:1.1] Cold | Cold air undercuts warm, lifts it sharply | Steep | Cumulonimbus, heavy short showers, squalls, sudden temperature fall
  [18:2:1.2] Occluded | Cold front overtakes warm front; warm air lifted off ground | – | Mixed; last stage of temperate cyclone
  [18:2:1.3] Stationary | Neither air mass advances | – | Cloud and rain for days
[18:2:2] - **Frontogenesis** = forming or sharpening of a front; **frontolysis** = break-up of a front.
[18:2:3] - **Arcus (shelf/roll) clouds** form at the leading edge of thunderstorm outflows or cold fronts.
[18:2:4] # Passage of a Front
[18:2:5] - 1. Warm front approaches: **cirrus → cirrostratus → altostratus → nimbostratus**; pressure falls, steady rain.
[18:2:6] - 2. Warm sector: warm, humid air.
[18:2:7] - 3. Cold front: cumulonimbus, heavy showers, wind shift; then cold, clear air.
## SUB 3 | Polar Front Theory
[18:3:0] - **Vilhelm and Jacob Bjerknes**, **Bergen School (Norway)**.
[18:3:1] - Wave cyclones form on the polar front.
[18:3:2] - Life cycle: wave → warm and cold fronts → occlusion → dissipation.
[18:3:3] - **Western disturbances** reach NW India at the occluded stage.
## SUB 4 | Air Masses Affecting India and in News
[18:4:0] TABLE head: Season | Air | Effect
  [18:4:0.0] Winter | Cold continental Central Asian air | Himalayas block it: north India about 3–8°C warmer than same latitude

[18:4:2] - Windward slope = orographic rain (Western Ghats); leeward = rain shadow.
[18:4:3] - **Tornado Alley** (US Great Plains/Mississippi): polar cold air meets warm humid tropical air.
[18:4:4] - **Strong polar vortex** keeps cold air north; **Arctic amplification**: meandering jet carries warm air into the Arctic.
