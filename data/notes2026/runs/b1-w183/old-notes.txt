## SUB 5 | Bahadur Shah Zafar – Symbolic Leader
[183:5:0] - **Last Mughal emperor**; proclaimed **Shahenshah-e-Hindustan** (Emperor of India) by the rebels at Delhi.
[183:5:1] - Unwilling at first, he **vacillated but agreed** to assume the title; wrote to rulers to form a confederacy.
[183:5:2] - Proclamations stressed **Hindu-Muslim unity** and the return of the **Badshahi**.
[183:5:3] - Pension: **Rs 1 lakh per month** (Rs 99,000 at Delhi + Rs 1,000 at Lucknow) – "less than one lakh" is wrong. An **emperor without an empire**; poets **Zauq and Ghalib** at his court; **Hasan Askari** his spiritual guide.
[183:5:4] - **Fate:** captured after Delhi fell (Sep 1857), tried, **exiled to Rangoon (Yangon)** with Zeenat Mahal (Oct 1858); **died there in 1862** – died **in exile**.
[183:5:5] - Hindu-Muslim unity of 1857 was later cited against the **two-nation theory**.

[183:7:0] - First great, direct threat to British rule; **weaknesses**: lack of **coordination and central leadership**.

=== NOTES 1:184 | Suppression of the Revolt; Why It Failed; Loyal Areas
## SUB 0 | Why the Revolt Failed
[184:0:0] - **Main cause:** lack of a **common strategy, coordination and central leadership/organisation**; no unified **programme or ideology**, no vision of the future.
[184:0:1] - **Military superiority** of the British – rebels had **swords, spears and old muskets**, the British the **Enfield rifle** and the **electric telegraph**; experienced generals; troops back from the **Crimean War**.
[184:0:2] - **Limited territorial and social base** – no **all-India character**; British power intact in the **East, West and South (Madras Presidency peaceful)**, from where troops came.
[184:0:3] - **Lack of support:** most **native kings supported the British**; big zamindars, moneylenders, merchants and the **educated middle class** stayed away; **Sikhs and "martial races"** did not join; traitors.
[184:0:4] - Weak leadership of the old **Bahadur Shah**; quarrels such as the **Begum of Awadh vs Maulvi Ahmadullah**.
[184:0:5] > All three – military superiority, no unified programme, lack of support from all sections – are correct (BPSC). A-R: the revolt failed; Bahadur Shah's leadership lacked wide support and the major rulers shied away – R explains A.
[184:0:6] - **Rajputana:** the British suppressed the rising there easily because the **local Rajput rulers did not support** the rebels.

## SUB 4 | Nature – Statement Judgements and Hindu-Muslim Unity
[185:4:0] - Began as a **military mutiny** → became a **popular rebellion**; a **traditional uprising** of classes against a common foreign enemy – **no idea of a nation-state**.
[185:4:1] - **Not carefully planned** by rajas, nawabs and taluqdars; **rumours and prophecies did play a role** (Plassey centenary, 23 June 1857); the British did **not** control it quickly or easily.
[185:4:2] - **Rebel proclamations repeatedly appealed to all sections irrespective of caste and creed** (Azamgarh Proclamation, 25 Aug 1857 – under "Muhammad and Mahavir"; urged allegiance to the **Badshahi**).
[185:4:3] - **Hindu-Muslim unity:** **Bahadur Shah II** accepted by both as **symbolic head**; cow slaughter banned; both well represented in leadership. British response: **divide and rule** (not handing power to the INC).
[185:4:4] - **Significance:** failed, but broke the myth of British invincibility; seeds of later nationalism. Same "mutiny or revolt?" debate on the **RIN revolt** (HMIS Talwar, **1946**).
[185:4:5] - Largely confined to **North India**; most of India neutral.

## SUB 1 | Lord Canning – Governor-General in 1857 and First Viceroy
[186:1:0] # Canning (1856-62)
[186:1:1] - **Governor-General during the Revolt of 1857 = Lord Canning** (asked 8 times: UPSC 2006, IAS 2006, UPRO 2013, UKPSC 2016, WBCS 2017, State PSCs) – not Dalhousie (left 1856), Hardinge, Lytton, Minto, Bentinck, Hastings, Amherst, Auckland.
[186:1:2] - **First Viceroy of India = Canning** (1858) – the GoI Act 1858 gave the Governor-General the added title "Viceroy"; Clive, Cornwallis and Ripon are wrong options. Canning was the last GG of the Company and the first Viceroy of the Crown.
[186:1:3] - Made **Allahabad** the **emergency headquarters** of the government in 1857-58 and read the Proclamation there.
[186:1:4] - **White Mutiny (1859-60) under Canning:** the Company's **European troops** resented transfer to the Crown without a new bounty; their **batta** was stopped; Canning's legalistic reading of the transfer angered them; most took a free discharge and passage home.
[186:1:5] - Other Canning measures: **portfolio system (1859)**, Indian Councils Act 1861, Indian High Courts Act 1861 (High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras), Hindu Widow Remarriage Act 1856, withdrawal of the Doctrine of Lapse; called loyal princes "**breakwaters to the storm**".

## SUB 2 | Government of India Act, 1858 – Transfer to the Crown
[186:2:0] # Core change
[186:2:1] - Passed **2 August 1858** – "**Act for the Better Government of India**" (also called Act for the Good Government of India); introduced by Lord Stanley.
[186:2:2] - **Transferred the government of India from the East India Company to the British Crown** (UPPCS 2007) – not the Charter Acts of 1833/1853 or the Indian Councils Act 1861; Queen Victoria declared sovereign of British India. **Year of transfer = 1858**, not 1857, 1859 or 1947.
[186:2:3] - The Revolt "**gave a death blow to the system of East India Company rule**" (UPPCS Mains 2010) – the true statement; British writers called it a "mutiny", not Indian historians.
[186:2:4] TABLE head: Aftermath | Date
  [186:2:4.0] British recaptured Delhi | 20-21 Sept 1857
  [186:2:4.1] Rani Lakshmibai died | 18 June 1858
  [186:2:4.2] GoI Act passed | 2 Aug 1858
  [186:2:4.3] Queen's Proclamation | 1 Nov 1858
  [186:2:4.4] Peel Commission | 1859
  [186:2:4.5] East India Company dissolved | 1874
[186:2:5] - Board of Control and Court of Directors abolished → **Secretary of State for India** (cabinet minister, responsible to Parliament) with **supreme control over the Government of India** (CDS 2008) + **Council of India of 15 members** (London; abolished by the 1935 Act).
[186:2:6] - After 1870 (Suez Canal 1869, Red Sea cable 1870) the SoS became the **de facto ruler**; the Viceroy was responsible to him.
[186:2:7] TABLE head: Act | Year | What it did
  [186:2:7.0] Pitt's India Act | 1784 | Dual control – Company stayed
  [186:2:7.1] Charter Act | 1833 | GG of India (Bentinck); Company purely administrative
  [186:2:7.2] Charter Act | 1853 | Company stayed, no fixed term
  [186:2:7.3] GoI Act | 1858 | Company rule ended; Crown rule via SoS and Viceroy
  [186:2:7.4] Indian Councils Act | 1861 | After the transfer
[186:2:8] # British India and the princely states
[186:2:9] - After 1858 India had **two kinds of territory: British India** (provinces, ruled directly through governors and the Viceroy) and **Princely States** (ruled by Indian princes under **British paramountcy**). The reverse statement ("provinces under local kings, states under British rule") is **incorrect** (AP FBO 2019).
[186:2:10] - Same office-holder: **Viceroy** for the princely states, **Governor-General** for British India.
[186:2:11] # Ends and Andhra angle
[186:2:12] - **Bahadur Shah II** – proclaimed **Shahenshah-e-Hindustan** by rebels; exiled to **Rangoon**, died **1862**. East India Company (charter **31 Dec 1600**) lost rule 1858, **dissolved 1874**.
[186:2:13] - **Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema** stayed in the **Madras Presidency** under the Crown; **Telangana** stayed with the **Nizam** under British suzerainty.

## SUB 3 | Empress of India (1876-77) and the Delhi Durbars
[186:3:0] - **Royal Titles Act, 1876** (Disraeli; Viceroy **Lytton**): Queen Victoria **assumed the title "Empress of India" (Kaiser-i-Hind) in 1876**.
[186:3:1] - It was **proclaimed in India at the Delhi Durbar (Imperial Assemblage), 1 January 1877** – so exam keys give **1877** for "appointed/proclaimed Empress", **1876** for "under the Royal Titles Act".
[186:3:2] - **Delhi Durbars** = coronation ceremonies held **to mark the succession of an Emperor/Empress** of India – **1877, 1903, 1911** (not to listen to petitions or declare laws).
[186:3:3] > 1876 Act / title → 1 Jan 1877 Delhi proclamation (Lytton) → Durbars 1903 (Edward VII, Curzon), 1911 (George V, Hardinge).

## SUB 4 | Army Reorganisation and Divide and Rule
[186:4:0] # Peel Commission (1859)
[186:4:1] TABLE head: Measure | Detail
  [186:4:1.0] European : Indian ratio | 1:2 in the Bengal Army; 2:5 in the Madras and Bombay armies
  [186:4:1.1] Artillery | Exclusively European; key posts and officers European
  [186:4:1.2] "Division and counterpoise" | Regiments mixed by caste, community and region to balance each other
  [186:4:1.3] Recruitment | From the "martial races" – Gurkhas, Sikhs, Punjabis, Pathans (Punjab, Nepal, NWFP) who had stayed loyal – not from Awadh, Bihar, Central India, Bengal or the South (BPSC 2008)
[186:4:2] - **Army Amalgamation Scheme 1861** moved the Company's European troops to the Crown. **Eden Commission (1879)** → single **Indian Army (1895)** with **four commands**.

## SUB 5 | Indian Councils Act, 1861 – Portfolio System
[186:5:0] - **Portfolio system:** introduced by **Canning in 1859**; the **Indian Councils Act, 1861** gave it statutory recognition – it **strengthened the Viceroy's authority over his executive council by substituting the portfolio (departmental) system for corporate functioning** (IAS 2002) – not the Acts of 1858, 1892 or 1909.
[186:5:1] - Each of the five members was placed in charge of a department (home, revenue, military, finance, law).
[186:5:2] - Lesson of 1857: associate Indians with law-making – at least **half the additional members non-official**; Viceroy's **ordinances valid six months**.
[186:5:3] - **1862:** Canning nominated the first **three Indians** – **Raja of Benares, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao**.

## SUB 2 | Brahmo Samaj, 1828 – Founding and Beliefs
[188:2:0] - **Founded:** **Brahmo Sabha**, **20 August 1828**, Calcutta, by **Raja Rammohan Roy**; later called the **Brahmo Samaj**. First secretary Tarachand Chakravarti.
[188:2:1] - **Year traps:** 1829 = sati ban; **1843** = Debendranath joined; correct founding year = **1828** (option added where missing).
[188:2:2] - **Monotheism:** worship of **one formless, eternal God**; denounced **polytheism and idol worship**; no images in Samaj halls; no faith in **avatars**.
[188:2:3] - **Reason + Upanishads:** rested on reason and the Vedas/Upanishads but **rejected the infallibility of the Vedas** – no scripture above reason and conscience.
[188:2:4] - **No priestly class** needed to interpret texts; criticised **caste**; took in the best of Western/other religious thought.
[188:2:5] - **Correct statements:** opposed idolatry ✔; denied need for priests ✔; "Vedas infallible" ✘ (IAS 2012); "accepted need of priestly class" ✘ (SI 2023: a, b, c).
[188:2:6] - **Nature:** a **monotheistic reformist** movement within Hinduism (not polytheistic, not political); **reformist**, while the **Arya Samaj** is **revivalist**.
[188:2:7] - **Significance:** condemned sati, purdah, child marriage, polygamy; backed widow remarriage and women's education; influence hardly went beyond **Bengal**.
[188:2:8] - **Opponent:** **Radhakant Deb's Dharma Sabha (1830)** – orthodox, against the sati ban.
[188:2:9] - **Brahmo Dharma Gitanjali:** collection of devotional songs sung in Samaj prayers.
[188:2:10] > Brahmo = one God + reason; no idols, no avatars, no priests, no infallible scripture.

[188:2:12] - **Renamed:** the Brahmo Sabha became the **Brahmo Samaj** under **Debendranath Tagore**.

## SUB 1 | Prarthana Samaj (1867), Ranade and the Social Conference
[189:1:0] - **Founded:** **1867, Bombay**, by **Dr. Atmaram Pandurang**, helped by **Keshab Chandra Sen** (visits 1864, 1867); grew out of the **Paramahansa Sabha**.
[189:1:1] - **Leaders:** **M.G. Ranade** (joined 1870, **chief mentor** – gave it an all-India character), **R.G. Bhandarkar**, **N.G. Chandavarkar**.
[189:1:2] - **Beliefs:** **monotheism**, attached to the Maharashtra **bhakti** tradition; aimed to **reform Hindu society from within**; relied on education and **persuasion**, not confrontation; did not outright forbid image worship.
[189:1:3] - **Four-point agenda:** (i) disapproval of **caste**, (ii) **women's education**, (iii) **widow remarriage**, (iv) **raising the age of marriage** for both sexes – **abolition of untouchability is not in the list** (CDS 2012; ⚠ PMF adds a later Depressed Class Mission).
[189:1:4] - **Statements (Astt. Comm. 2019):** reform of Hindu society ✔, Maharashtra-based ✔, monotheism ✔; "it greatly influenced the Brahmo Samaj" ✘ – the Brahmo Samaj influenced it.

[189:1:7] - **Indian (National) Social Conference:** founded by **M.G. Ranade** and **Raghunath Rao**; first session **Madras, 1887**; met **at the same time and venue as the INC** – its "social reform cell", because Congress (Dadabhai, 1886) kept to political questions only (UPSC 2012).
