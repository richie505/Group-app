=== NOTES 5:73 | Environment, Ecology and Ecosystem – Components, Food Chains, Energy Flow and Ecological Pyramids
## SUB 0 | Environment, Ecology and Levels of Organisation
[73:0:0] # Environment – Meaning and Categories
[73:0:1] - **Environment**: the surroundings and conditions in which organisms live; biotic and abiotic factors interact; it is never static.
[73:0:2] - **Composite nature**: physical (nutrients, water, air) + biological (organisms) + chemical interactions (**carbon and nitrogen cycles**).
[73:0:3] TABLE head: Category | Includes
  [73:0:3.0] Physical | Lithosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere
  [73:0:3.1] Biotic | Plants, animals, microbes (microbes are biotic, not physical)
  [73:0:3.2] Cultural | Man-made – social, economic, political institutions
[73:0:4] - **Primary functions**: provide resources, **assimilate wastes**, habitat for biodiversity.
[73:0:5] APPSC 2024: "Generating synthetic chemicals" is NOT a function of the environment.
[73:0:6] - A **habitat always has life**; an environment may exist without life.
[73:0:7] # Ecology and Levels of Organisation
[73:0:8] - **Ecology**: study of relationships between organisms and their environment; term coined by **Ernst Haeckel (1866)**.
[73:0:9] - Ecological order: **organism → population → community → ecosystem → biome → biosphere**.
[73:0:10] TABLE head: Level | Meaning
  [73:0:10.0] Population | Individuals of one species in an area
  [73:0:10.1] Community | All populations of different species in an area (organisms interacting with one another)
  [73:0:10.2] Ecosystem | Community + its non-living environment; basic functional unit of ecology
  [73:0:10.3] Biome | Large region defined by climate + dominant vegetation (rainforest, tundra, desert)
  [73:0:10.4] Biosphere | All ecosystems together; Earth inhabited by living organisms; an open system
[73:0:11] - **Biodiversity**: flora and fauna of an area; measured as **alpha, beta, gamma**; "biological diversity" coined by **Dasmann**.
[73:0:12] - **Biodiversity hotspot** (Myers; Conservation International): at least **1,500 endemic vascular plants** and lost at least **70%** of original habitat.
[73:0:13] - **Sere** = entire sequence of communities in succession; **habitat fragmentation** = continuous habitat broken into isolated patches; **anthrome** (anthropogenic biome) = human-altered terrestrial biosphere, e.g. cropland, crop field, park, aquarium, garden.

## SUB 1 | Habitat, Niche and Population
[73:1:0] # Habitat vs Niche
[73:1:1] TABLE head: Habitat | Niche
  [73:1:1.0] Physical place where an organism lives – its "address" | Functional role of a species – its "profession" (food, space, time, conditions used)
  [73:1:1.1] One habitat holds many species | Unique to each species – no two species share one niche
[73:1:2] - Niche is not only physical space but also the functional role in the community; types: habitat niche (where it lives), food niche (what it eats).
[73:1:3] - **Fundamental niche**: full range of biotic and abiotic conditions and resources a species could use **free from other species** (APPSC 2021). **Realised niche**: actual range used under competition.
[73:1:4] - **Niche breadth**: specialists (**koala** – only eucalyptus) vs generalists.
[73:1:5] - **Resource partitioning**: competing species coexist by using different parts of a shared resource (e.g. pests feeding on different parts of one plant).
[73:1:6] APPSC 2022: "niche is unique to a species" and "many species share a habitat" – both correct.
[73:1:7] # Population Attributes
[73:1:8] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [73:1:8.0] Natality | Births; rate per unit population per unit time (PYQ 2025)
  [73:1:8.1] Mortality | Deaths per unit time
  [73:1:8.2] Density | Individuals per unit area
  [73:1:8.3] Others | Age structure, sex ratio (NOT dominant vegetation)
  [73:1:8.4] J-curve | Exponential growth, unlimited resources (dN/dt = rN)
  [73:1:8.5] S-curve | Logistic growth levelling at carrying capacity K (dN/dt = rN(K−N)/K)
[73:1:9] - Age pyramids: **triangular = expanding; bell = stable; urn = declining**.
[73:1:10] - **Carrying capacity**: largest population an environment can support for ever; resource extraction not above regeneration rate, wastes within assimilating capacity.
[73:1:11] TABLE head: r-selected | K-selected
  [73:1:11.0] Many small offspring, little care, short life, unstable habitats – insects, weeds | Few large offspring, much care, long life, near K – elephants, humans
## SUB 2 | Ecosystem – Concept, Structure and Components
[73:2:0] # Concept
[73:2:1] - **Ecosystem**: functional unit of nature – biotic community + its non-living surroundings, linked by **energy flow and nutrient cycling**; term by **Tansley, 1935**.
[73:2:2] - It is an **open system**, has its own productivity and **no fixed universal boundary**.
[73:2:3] - Ecosystems are open for energy and matter; **the Earth is open for energy but nearly closed for matter**.
[73:2:4] TABLE head: Structure (what is there) | Function (what it does)
  [73:2:4.0] Species composition, trophic levels, abiotic materials | Energy flow (one-way), nutrient cycling, primary and secondary production, self-regulation
[73:2:5] - **Food chain** = the **dynamic (functional)** part of an ecosystem; producers, consumers and decomposers form its structure.
[73:2:6] - **Biogeochemical (nutritional) cycle**: movement of chemical elements (C, N, P) through biotic and abiotic components; **energy does not cycle**.
[73:2:7] - Carbon cycle: **photosynthesis removes CO₂**; respiration, decay, fossil-fuel burning and **volcanic action** add it. Water cycle is driven by solar energy; phosphorus cycle begins with **rock weathering**.
[73:2:8] # Abiotic Factors
[73:2:9] - **Abiotic factor**: any non-living component affecting organisms (APPSC 2018); can be limiting or enabling.
[73:2:10] TABLE head: Type | Factors
  [73:2:10.0] Climatic | Light, temperature, rainfall, humidity, wind
  [73:2:10.1] Edaphic | Soil – texture, pH, minerals, drainage
  [73:2:10.2] Topographic | Altitude, slope, aspect
[73:2:11] - **Liebig's Law of the Minimum**: growth limited by the nutrient in shortest supply. **Shelford's Law of Tolerance**: both too little and too much limit.
[73:2:12] - **Xeric condition**: low humidity, scarce water (arid, extremely cold habitats, salt marshes).
[73:2:13] # Biotic Components
[73:2:14] - Biotic list: green plants, non-green plants, decomposers, **parasites, symbionts**, animals, man.
[73:2:15] TABLE head: Component | Examples
  [73:2:15.0] Producers (autotrophs) | Green plants; ocean: diatoms, cyanobacteria; vents: chemosynthetic bacteria
  [73:2:15.1] Primary consumers | Cow, rabbit, zooplankton, copepod
  [73:2:15.2] Secondary consumers | Wolves
  [73:2:15.3] Tertiary consumers | Lion (when eating wolves – not primary)
  [73:2:15.4] Omnivores | Humans
  [73:2:15.5] Decomposers (saprotrophs) | Bacteria and fungi
  [73:2:15.6] Detritivores | Earthworms, millipedes, woodlice, nematodes
[73:2:16] - **Producers** are intermediaries between biotic and abiotic components; **decomposers link** biotic and abiotic parts.
[73:2:17] - Macroconsumers = phagotrophs; microconsumers = saprotrophs.
[73:2:18] - Unusual herbivores: **green turtle, parrot fish, dugong**.
[73:2:19] - Cold-blooded: frogs, snakes, lizards, fish, crocodile. APPSC 2023 match: humans = omnivores; saprotrophs = dead matter; reptiles = cold-blooded; camel = desert-adapted.
[73:2:20] - **Stenothermal**: tolerate only a **narrow** temperature range (reptiles); **eurythermal**: wide range; likewise **stenohaline/euryhaline** for salinity.
[73:2:21] # Producers and Autotroph Types
[73:2:22] TABLE head: Type | Energy source – examples
  [73:2:22.0] Photoautotrophs | Sunlight – green plants, algae, cyanobacteria
  [73:2:22.1] Chemoautotrophs | Chemical oxidation – bacteria in deep ocean trenches oxidising hydrogen sulphide; nitrifying bacteria; many archaea (methanogens)
[73:2:23] - Aquatic producers: **phytoplankton, algae, submerged and floating plants**; hydrothermal vent chains rest on chemosynthetic bacteria, not sunlight.
[73:2:24] - Some bacteria (green sulphur bacteria) photosynthesise with invisible (infrared) light; marine algae can use moonlight.
[73:2:25] - Most of Earth's oxygen comes from algae: about **90%** of photosynthesis by aquatic plants and algae (**85% in sea**); marine phytoplankton and photosynthetic bacteria give about **50% of world's oxygen**.
[73:2:26] - **Chlorella** – highest efficiency in turning solar to chemical energy.
[73:2:27] # Decomposition
[73:2:28] - Breakdown of organic matter by bacteria and fungi into simple inorganic nutrients reused by producers.
[73:2:29] - Steps: **fragmentation → leaching → catabolism → humification → mineralisation**.
[73:2:30] TABLE head: Factor | Rate
  [73:2:30.0] Detritus rich in nitrogen and sugars; warm, moist | Fast
  [73:2:30.1] Lignin, chitin, low temperature, anaerobic | Slow
[73:2:31] - Useful microbes: Lactobacillus – curd; Rhizobium, Anabaena, Azotobacter (free-living) – nitrogen fixation.
[73:2:32] - **Protists:** unicellular **eukaryotes** of water and moist places, some parasitic – not prokaryotes. Cyanobacteria are photosynthetic **prokaryotes** (many fix nitrogen); methanogens (archaea) live in anaerobic wetlands and the gut of ruminants.

[73:3:0] # Grazing vs Detritus Chain

  [73:3:1.0] Grazing | Living green plants | Grass → goat → human; grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle

[73:3:3] # Trophic Levels
[73:3:4] TABLE head: Level | Organisms
  [73:3:4.0] I | Producers – green plants, phytoplankton, seaweeds, chemosynthetic bacteria (vents, active volcanoes)
  [73:3:4.1] II | Herbivores, zooplankton
  [73:3:4.2] III | Carnivores
  [73:3:4.3] IV | Top carnivores
  [73:3:4.4] None single | Detritivores and decomposers recycle all levels

[73:3:6] Missing-level question: "tiger, grass, snake, frog" lacks the **herbivore (second level)**.
[73:3:7] # Food Web and Stability
[73:3:8] - **Food web**: network of interlinked food chains; **more complex → more stable** (alternative pathways). Fewer trophic levels do not increase biodiversity.
[73:3:9] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [73:3:9.0] Resistance | Withstand a disturbance
  [73:3:9.1] Resilience | Recover after disturbance (APPSC 2024)
  [73:3:9.2] Homeostasis | Self-regulation up to a limit through feedback
[73:3:10] - Diverse systems (rainforest, coral reef) more stable than tundra, desert, monoculture; **marine ecosystem** – most stable overall (unchanged for long periods).
[73:3:11] - Warming and ocean acidification weaken calcifying plankton and corals, disrupting marine food webs.
[73:3:12] # Bioaccumulation vs Biomagnification
[73:3:13] TABLE head: Bioaccumulation | Biomagnification
  [73:3:13.0] Build-up inside one organism over its life | Concentration rises at each higher trophic level
[73:3:14] - Only persistent, fat-soluble, non-biodegradable pollutants (DDT, mercury, PCBs) biomagnify.
[73:3:15] - DDT (chlorinated hydrocarbon, organic halogen, harms nervous system): food crop → rat → snake → **hawk** – highest in hawk; causes **eggshell thinning**, seabird crashes.
[73:3:16] - **Methylmercury** – Minamata disease (Japan); peaks in dolphins, orcas.
[73:3:17] - Pollutants: biodegradable = sewage; non-biodegradable = DDT, plastic, asbestos; primary = CO, SO₂, NOx; secondary = O₃, **PAN**, smog.
[73:3:18] - **Trophic level:** the functional position (feeding step) an organism occupies in a food chain – producer, herbivore, carnivore or top carnivore.
## SUB 4 | Energy Flow and Productivity
[73:4:0] # Energy Flow
[73:4:1] - **Unidirectional**: sun → producers → herbivores → carnivores; not cycled. Primary source and driving force = **Sun**.
[73:4:2] - Obeys thermodynamics: first law – energy transformed, not destroyed; second law – each transfer loses heat.
[73:4:3] - Under 50% of sunlight is **PAR**; plants fix about **2-10% of PAR**; green plants capture about 1% of sunlight falling on leaves.
[73:4:4] - **Total primary production efficiency** = share of incoming solar energy used in photosynthesis – about **1-2%** (APPSC 2024).
[73:4:5] # Ten Percent Law
[73:4:6] - **Raymond Lindeman (1942)**: about **10%** passes to the next level; about **90%** lost (respiration, excretion, locomotion, heat); hence 4-5 levels.
[73:4:7] # Productivity
[73:4:8] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [73:4:8.0] GPP | Total rate of solar energy fixed by producers
  [73:4:8.1] NPP | GPP − producer respiration; energy for consumers
  [73:4:8.2] Primary productivity | Rate solar energy is stored in green plants as food
  [73:4:8.3] Secondary productivity | Rate consumers form new organic matter
[73:4:9] - Highest NPP per unit area: **tropical rainforest** (APPSC 2025 key); estuaries, coral reefs, swamps, mangroves also high. Lowest: **deserts and open ocean** (ocean total still large by area).
[73:4:10] - Decreasing order: **mangroves > grasslands > lakes > oceans**; ecotones (mangroves, estuaries) more productive than surroundings.
[73:4:11] 1-2% = solar energy captured; 10% = passed between levels.
## SUB 5 | Ecological Pyramids
[73:5:0] - Show trophic levels; base = producers, apex = top consumers; **three types**.
[73:5:1] TABLE head: Pyramid | Upright | Inverted
  [73:5:1.0] Numbers | Grassland, pond | Tree ecosystem; parasitic chain
  [73:5:1.1] Biomass | Forest, grassland | Pond, marine (small fast-turning phytoplankton crop)
  [73:5:1.2] Energy | Always | Never
[73:5:2] - **Biomass** = total weight of organisms per unit area or volume.
[73:5:3] - **Standing crop:** living biomass present in a trophic level per unit area **at a given time** – a quantity, not a **rate** like productivity.
[73:5:4] - Limitations: assume simple chain, ignore food webs; a species cannot be placed at two levels; **saprophytes and decomposers get no place**; size ignored.
[73:5:5] G2 Mains 2025: energy pyramid can never be inverted.
