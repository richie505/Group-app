=== NOTES 1:268 | Development of Telugu Literature After 1956 – Digambara, Viplava, Dalit, Feminist and Muslim Minority Streams
## SUB 0 | The Streams in Order
[268:0:0] - Post-1956 streams moved Telugu literature from **classical/romantic themes** to **caste, class, gender and minority identity**.
[268:0:1] - Base of feminist and Dalit fiction: **Gidugu–Gurajada's spoken-Telugu revolution**.
[268:0:2] TABLE head: Stream / body | Year | Key fact
  [268:0:2.0] Digambara kavulu | 1965 | "Naked poets"; three volumes of Digambara Kavitvam (1965–68)
  [268:0:2.1] Virasam (Viplava Rachayitala Sangham) | 4 July 1970 | First president Sri Sri
  [268:0:2.2] Dalit literature | Roots in Gurram Jashuva; surge after Karamchedu (1985) | Dalit Mahasabhas formed
  [268:0:2.3] Feminist poetry | Neeli Meghalu (1993) | Edited by Volga and others
  [268:0:2.4] Muslim minority poetry | 1990s | Anthology Jaljala
[268:0:3] 1965 = Digambaras; 1969 = Srikakulam struggle; 1970 = Virasam.
## SUB 1 | Digambara Kavulu
[268:1:0] - Six **anti-establishment** "naked poets" of the mid-1960s.
[268:1:1] TABLE head: Pen name | Real name
  [268:1:1.0] Cherabandaraju | Baddam Bhaskara Reddy
  [268:1:1.1] Mahaswapna | Kammisetti Venkateswara Rao
[268:1:2] - **Cherabandaraju, Jwalamukhi, Nikhileswar** moved to **Virasam in 1970**.
## SUB 2 | Viplava Sahityam – Virasam
[268:2:0] - Context: **Srikakulam peasant struggle (1969)** under **Vempatapu Satyam** spread Naxalism in Andhra.
[268:2:1] - Trigger: Visakhapatnam students' pamphlet **"Writers, where do you stand?"** (1970).
[268:2:2] - Founded **4 July 1970**; first president **Sri Sri** (not Gaddar).
[268:2:3] - **Sri Sri**: **Maha Prasthanam** broke Telugu **prosody**; a call to the oppressed. Also the **first National Award for a Telugu lyricist** ("Teluguveera Levara", film Alluri Seetharamaraju).
## SUB 3 | Dalit Literature
[268:3:0] # Gurram Jashuva
[268:3:1] - **First modern Telugu Dalit poet**; works: **Gabbilam (1941), Firdausi, Kandiseekudu (The Refugee), Kreesthu Charitra**.
