## SUB 1 | Nature of the Indian Party System
[1:0] - **Party systems (Laxmikanth):** **one-party** (USSR; today China, **Cuba, North Korea, Eritrea** – not **Chile**, a multi-party democracy); **two-party** (USA, Britain); **multi-party** (France, Switzerland, Italy, **India**).
[1:1] - **India is multi-party** – continental size, diverse society (region, language, caste, religion, class), universal adult franchise; India has the **largest number of parties** in the world.
[1:2] - The **party system is a part (sub-system) of the political system**.
[1:3] - **Features:** one-dominant party (Congress) till 1967; lack of clear ideology (except BJP, CPI, CPM); **personality cult**; parties on traditional factors; rise of **regional parties**; factions and defections (after 1967); lack of effective opposition.
[1:4] - The **original Constitution had no provision on parties**; they entered via the **Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985)**.
[1:5] - **ECI classes:** national, State, **registered-unrecognised** parties (RUPPs – over 2,800, only ~750 contested in 2024).
[1:6] - **Ruling-party misuse:** government servants, vehicles and discretionary grants may be misused to woo voters – "political parties" are not such a government resource.

## SUB 3 | First Non-Congress and Coalition Governments
[3:0] - **Centre, 1977:** the **Janata Party** (merger of Congress (O), Jana Sangh, BLD, Socialists; PM **Morarji Desai**, 1977-79) – the **first non-Congress government** and, in most books, the **first coalition government** at the Centre (Laxmikanth Table 85.1 lists it first).
[3:1] - Strictly, Janata was one merged party with its own majority (295); the first formal multi-party coalition was the **National Front (1989)**.
[3:2] - **States:** the usual answer for the first non-Congress government is the **CPI in Kerala (1957, E.M.S. Namboodiripad)** – the first elected communist government; strictly, **PEPSU's United Front** ministry (**Gian Singh Rarewala, April 1952**) came first.
[3:3] - **Coalition government** = an **arrangement of convenience** among parties (Latin *coalitio*, "to grow together") – **not mentioned in the Constitution**, not at the will of the President or Speaker.
[3:4] > Asked as: year of the first non-Congress / coalition government (1977 – four PYQs); Janata came to power (1977); first non-Congress State government (CPI, Kerala).

[5:0] - **After 1967:** almost every opposition-ruled State had a coalition – exception **Tamil Nadu (DMK alone)**; **Bihar** saw the **most coalition governments (nine) in 1967-71**.
