## SUB 1 | Telugu Desam Party – Founding, Firsts and National Role
[1:0] # Founding and first victory
[1:1] - Founded by **NTR** on **29 March 1982** on **Telugu self-respect (Telugu atma gauravam)**; ideology: none specific beyond safeguarding the **political, economic, social and cultural foundations of Telugu-speaking people**.
[1:2] - Won power within **nine months** – **January 1983** Assembly election – ending the **Congress's unbroken rule since 1956**.
[1:3] - NTR was CM three times (1983-84, 1984-89, 1994-95) – **about seven years** in all.
[1:4] - **Pragathi Patham:** TDP's **15-point programme** (NTR era) – welfare thrust: drinking water, SC/ST/BC programmes, farm inputs.
[1:5] # National role
[1:6] - **8th Lok Sabha (1984):** TDP with **30 seats** = **main opposition party** – the **first regional party** to reach that position.
[1:7] - **1988:** NTR = **chairman of the National Front**, which formed the **V.P. Singh** government in **1989**.
[1:8] - **Gujarat censure motion (debated 30 April, voted 1 May 2002):** the **TDP** (backing the NDA from outside) **walked out before the vote** – it did not take part; the **AIADMK** and National Conference abstained, the **BSP voted against**; the motion was defeated (276-182).
[1:9] - **2018:** TDP **left the NDA** over **Special Category Status** for residuary AP; **2024:** back with the NDA – TDP leads AP's ruling coalition with **BJP** (and Jana Sena) support.
[1:10] - AP politics: **Congress vs TDP** (1983-2014); **TDP vs YSRCP** since 2014.

## SUB 4 | Rise, Role and Impact of Regional Parties
[4:0] # Features and classification (Laxmikanth)
[4:1] - Operates within one State/region; articulates a cultural, linguistic, religious or ethnic identity; seeks power at State level and **greater State autonomy**.
[4:2] TABLE head: Type | Examples
  [4:2.0] Regional culture / ethnicity | Akali Dal, National Conference, DMK, Telugu Desam, Shiv Sena, AGP
  [4:2.1] All-India outlook, no national base | Samajwadi Party, NCP
  [4:2.2] Split from national parties | BJD, RJD, Trinamool, YSR Congress
  [4:2.3] Personalised (charismatic leader) | short-lived
[4:3] # Causes of rise
[4:4] - Cultural/ethnic pluralism; **economic disparities and regional imbalance (neglect)**; linguistic reorganisation of States; **failure of national parties** to meet regional aspirations; charismatic leaders; Congress centralisation; caste and religion.
[4:5] - Roots of regionalism: **historical, geographical, linguistic, religious, ethnic**. In Andhra, the **Justice Party** era awakened non-Brahmin agrarian castes – **Reddys, Kammas, Kapus, Velamas**.
[4:6] - After **1989** coalition arithmetic raised their weight.
[4:7] # Role and dysfunctions
[4:8] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [4:8.0] Coalitions | Kingmakers – supplied Speakers, Union Ministers, survival votes; PM became "first among equals"
  [4:8.1] Federalism | Challenged one-party dominance; made Centre-State bargaining real
  [4:8.2] Merits | Representation of backward regions, balanced development, wider voter choice
  [4:8.3] Demerits | Parochialism, instability, populism, inter-State disputes
[4:9] - **Special Category Status** for residuary AP – TDP left the NDA over it (**2018**).
[4:10] - BJP defeated regional parties: **BJD (Odisha, 2024)**, **AAP (Delhi, 2025)**, **Trinamool (West Bengal, 2026)**.
