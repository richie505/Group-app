=== Nature of the Indian Party System
- **Party systems (Laxmikanth):** **one-party** (USSR; today China, **Cuba, North Korea, Eritrea** – not **Chile**, a multi-party democracy); **two-party** (USA, Britain); **multi-party** (France, Switzerland, Italy, **India**).
- **India is multi-party** – continental size, diverse society (region, language, caste, religion, class), universal adult franchise; India has the **largest number of parties** in the world.
- The **party system is a part (sub-system) of the political system**.
- **Features:** one-dominant party (Congress) till 1967; lack of clear ideology (except BJP, CPI, CPM); **personality cult**; parties on traditional factors; rise of **regional parties**; factions and defections (after 1967); lack of effective opposition.
- The **original Constitution had no provision on parties**; they entered via the **Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985)**.
- **ECI classes:** national, State, **registered-unrecognised** parties (RUPPs – over 2,800, only ~750 contested in 2024).
- **Ruling-party misuse:** government servants, vehicles and discretionary grants may be misused to woo voters – "political parties" are not such a government resource.

=== First Non-Congress and Coalition Governments
- **Centre, 1977:** the **Janata Party** (merger of Congress (O), Jana Sangh, BLD, Socialists; PM **Morarji Desai**, 1977-79) – the **first non-Congress government** and, in most books, the **first coalition government** at the Centre (Laxmikanth Table 85.1 lists it first).
- Strictly, Janata was one merged party with its own majority (295); the first formal multi-party coalition was the **National Front (1989)**.
- **States:** the usual answer for the first non-Congress government is the **CPI in Kerala (1957, E.M.S. Namboodiripad)** – the first elected communist government; strictly, **PEPSU's United Front** ministry (**Gian Singh Rarewala, April 1952**) came first.
- **Coalition government** = an **arrangement of convenience** among parties (Latin *coalitio*, "to grow together") – **not mentioned in the Constitution**, not at the will of the President or Speaker.
> Asked as: year of the first non-Congress / coalition government (1977 – four PYQs); Janata came to power (1977); first non-Congress State government (CPI, Kerala).

| Government | PM(s) | Period | Support |
|---|---|---|---|

| BJP | A.B. Vajpayee | May 1996 | **13 days** |
| **United Front** | **H.D. Deve Gowda**, **I.K. Gujral** | 1996-98 | Congress outside – **withdrew** from Deve Gowda (1997) |
| BJP-led / **NDA** | A.B. Vajpayee | 1998-99, 1999-2004 | "**National Agenda for Governance**" |
| **UPA** | Manmohan Singh | 2004-14 | "**National Common Minimum Programme**"; CPI and CPM outside (2004-08) |
- **Governments formed in 1990-99: seven** – Chandra Shekhar (1990), Rao (1991), Vajpayee (1996), Deve Gowda (1996), Gujral (1997), Vajpayee (1998), Vajpayee (1999); with V.P. Singh's (Dec 1989) eight; **six** different PMs.
- **UPA-I partners (2004):** Congress 145, **RJD 24 (second largest)**, **DMK 16**, NCP 9, PMK, LJP, JMM.
- **Coordination:** CMP + **coordination committee** (critics: a "super-cabinet"). The NDA's National Agenda gave **policy guidance, promises and a few guiding principles** without detailing everything.
- **Why the coalition era:** growth of **regional parties**; inability of national parties to meet **regional aspirations**; coalitions **reflect popular opinion** more broadly – **not** greater stability (they are prone to instability).
- **Consequences:** increased role of **regional and small parties**; consensus but slow decisions; no single party has absolute power; PM becomes "**primus inter pares**" (first among equals) who must consult partners; defections → **Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985)**; India did **not** become a two-party system.
- Deve Gowda – 12th PM (counting Nanda); **Narendra Modi – 15th** PM.

=== Party Systems in the States (incl. Andhra Pradesh)
- **After 1967:** almost every opposition-ruled State had a coalition – exception **Tamil Nadu (DMK alone)**; **Bihar** saw the **most coalition governments (nine) in 1967-71**.
| State | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **Kerala** | Bipolar: **UDF (Congress-led)** vs **LDF (CPI(M)-led)**; **A.K. Antony** resigned as CM of the **UDF** government on **29 Aug 2004** after the Lok Sabha rout |
| **Andhra Pradesh** | **Congress dominance until 1983**; then Congress vs **TDP**; since 2014 TDP vs YSRCP; now a coalition led by a dominant regional party with the **BJP as a supporting partner** |
- **Congress's lowest AP Assembly tallies (undivided AP):** 1983 – 60, 1985 – 50, **1994 – 26 (lowest)**, 1999 – 91 – against **NTR's TDP**.
- **AP roots:** Justice Party-era rise of **non-Brahmin agrarian castes** → base for regional parties.
- **Aug 2026:** BJP rules alone in **11** States, Congress 3, regional parties alone 4, coalitions 10.
> Asked as: Bihar 1967-71 record; Congress's lowest AP tally (1994); Antony's UDF government (2004).
