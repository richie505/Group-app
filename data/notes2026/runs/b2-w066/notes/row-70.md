=== Finance Commission – Article 280, Composition and Qualifications
# Constitutional basis
- **Article 280** (Part **XII**) provides for the Finance Commission (FC), a **quasi-judicial constitutional body**; Articles **280 and 281** deal with it (Art. **290** = pensions, not the FC; Art. 148 = CAG).
- Constituted by the **President** within two years of the Constitution's start and then **every fifth year or earlier** as he considers necessary – a **temporary** body, not permanent.
- The Constitution envisages the FC as the **balancing wheel of fiscal federalism** (Laxmikanth); "Federal Finance Commission" = finances **between the Centre and the States**.
- **Art. 281:** the **President** lays every recommendation, with an **explanatory memorandum** on the action taken, before each House of Parliament.
- Recommendations are **advisory, not binding**; **P.V. Rajamannar** (4th FC): they should not be turned down without **very compelling reasons**.
# Composition and qualifications
- **Chairman + four other members** (5 in all), appointed by the **President** (Art. 280(1)) – not a Chairman and six members.
- They hold office for **such period as the President specifies**; members are **eligible for reappointment**; no term fixed in the Constitution.
- **Parliament** fixes qualifications and manner of selection (Art. 280(2)) – the **Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951** (not the President, the Union Cabinet or the Finance Minister).
| Post | Qualification (1951 Act) |
|---|---|
| Chairman | Experience in **public affairs** |
| Member | **High Court judge** or qualified to be one (one of the possible fields – not compulsory) |
| Member | Special knowledge of **government finance and accounts** |
| Member | Wide experience in **financial matters and administration** |
| Member | Special knowledge of **economics** |
- There is no member post for a monetary policy expert or a Parliament member.
- The FC has the **powers of a civil court** (summoning persons, production of documents).
- It reports to the **President**, not to NITI Aayog or Parliament directly.
- The **101st Amendment (2016)** introduced **GST** – it did not implement any FC report.
> Statement items test: Art. 280 vs 290, "every fifth year", Chairman + 4, Parliament (not President) fixes qualifications, report → President → Art. 281.

=== Functions – Article 280(3) and the Divisible Pool
# Functions (Art. 280(3)) – recommendations to the President
- 1. **Distribution of the net proceeds of taxes** between the Centre and the States (**vertical**) and **allocation among the States** (**horizontal**).
- 2. **Principles governing grants-in-aid** to the States out of the **Consolidated Fund of India** (Art. **275**).
- 3. **Measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State** to supplement the resources of **Panchayats and Municipalities**, on the basis of the **State Finance Commission**'s recommendations (added by the **73rd/74th Amendments, 1992**).
- 4. **Any other matter** referred by the President **in the interests of sound finance**.
- Till 1960 it also suggested grants to Assam, Bihar, Odisha and West Bengal in lieu of jute export duty (Art. 273).
- **Main function:** deciding the **share of States in Central taxes** – the body that recommends the principles of sharing revenues (not the Inter-State Council, NDC, NITI Aayog, Planning Commission or Finance Ministry).
- **Not FC functions:** allocation of funds to constitutional bodies, maintaining income-expenditure details of governments, financial control over the Centre or States, fixing **GST rates**, releasing funds.
- **Agricultural income tax** is a **State** tax (State List entry 46) – the Constitution assigns it; UPSC 1995's key names the FC as the body linked to sharing taxes with States.
# Divisible pool
- **Divisible pool** = gross tax revenue − **cesses, surcharges** and **cost of collection**; the **CAG certifies** net proceeds (Art. 279).
- Rising **cesses and surcharges** shrink the divisible pool, so the States' real share of gross tax revenue is below the headline %.

=== 14th Finance Commission (2015–20)
- Constituted **2 January 2013** (Y.V. Reddy); award **1 April 2015 – 31 March 2020**; GoI accepted the report on **24 February 2015**.
- Share in the divisible pool raised **32% → 42%** – the **largest increase ever**.
- **Tax devolution = the primary route** of transfer (a "compositional shift" from grants to devolution); **sector-specific grants dropped**; ignored the **Plan/non-Plan** distinction.
- Made **no distinction** between **special and general category** States – a formula-based mechanism with no room for political bargaining (the Constitution has no provision for Special Category Status).
- **Base year** for its projections: **2014-15** (reassessed base year).
- Population base: **1971 and 2011**; **fiscal discipline** dropped as a criterion; **forest cover** added.
| Criterion (14th FC) | Weight |
|---|---|
| **Income distance** | **50%** (highest) |
| Population 1971 | 17.5% |
| Population 2011 (demographic change) | 10% |
| Area | 15% |
| Forest cover | 7.5% |
- **Andhra Pradesh's share: 4.305%** (residuary AP, first award after bifurcation).
- Local-body grants **₹2,87,436 crore** (panchayats ₹2,00,292 crore) as **basic and performance** grants to duly constituted bodies (2011 population 90%, area 10%).
- After the 42% award the Centre raised **non-shareable cesses**.

=== Grants, Disaster Funds, Special Category Status and Andhra Pradesh
# Grants and disaster funds
| FC | Notable recommendation |
|---|---|
| 5th (1969) | **Special Category Status** introduced (Mahavir Tyagi); funds by the **Gadgil formula** (NDC) |
| **9th** | **Calamity Relief Fund** (from 1990) |
| **10th** | Local bodies: **₹100 per capita of rural population (1971 Census)** for Panchayats |
| **13th** | **National Disaster Response Reserve (NDRR)**, ₹250 crore; NCCF merged into the NDRF |
| 14th | Tax devolution over grants; 42% |
| 16th | Revenue-deficit grants ended; 3% GSDP deficit cap; GDP contribution criterion |
- Grants under **Art. 275** are for needy States; the FC does not decide **Plan** transfers.
# Special Category Status
- First States: **Assam, Jammu & Kashmir, Nagaland**; criteria: **hilly terrain**, low density or large **tribal** share, **strategic border**, economic and infrastructure **backwardness** (not high income).
# Andhra Pradesh
| FC | AP's share of the States' pool |
|---|---|
| 13th | for undivided AP – apportioned between AP and Telangana on population ratio (AP Reorganisation Act, 2014) |
| **14th (2015–20)** | **4.305%** |
| 15th (2021–26) | 4.047% (≈4.05%) |
| 16th (2026–31) | 4.217% (≈4.22%) |
- AP's share **fell** from the 14th to the 15th FC and **rose** under the 16th (still below the 14th).
- **SCS for AP**: **promised in Parliament in 2014**, **not** in the AP Reorganisation Act, 2014; the Centre gave a **special assistance** measure instead.
- 16th FC grants to AP: rural local bodies **₹16,627 crore**, urban **₹12,158 crore**, disaster **₹6,125 crore**; **Vijayawada and Visakhapatnam** eligible for up to **₹5,000 crore each** for **wastewater** systems.
- AP memorandum (Feb 2026): higher vertical devolution + criteria for **capital-city and infrastructure** costs from post-bifurcation imbalances.
- AP Finance Minister: AP lost up to **₹38,000 crore** when revenue-deficit grants were dropped; AP Budget 2026-27 revenue deficit **1.1%**.
- Aug 2026: ULB elections for **100 of 123** ULBs – only duly constituted ULBs can draw 16th FC grants.

=== 15th and 16th Finance Commissions – Devolution Formula
# 15th Finance Commission (2020–26)
- Formed **27 November 2017**; Chairman **N.K. Singh**; **first FC to cut** the vertical share: **42% → 41%**, because of the change in **J&K's status** (2019).
- Used **2011 population** only; **added demographic performance and tax effort**.
- Local-body grants **₹4.36 lakh crore** (urban share 36%).
# 16th Finance Commission (2026–31)
- Constituted **31 December 2023**; Chairman **Arvind Panagariya**; members **T. Rabi Sankar, Annie George Mathew, Manoj Panda, Soumya Kanti Ghosh**; award **2026-27 to 2030-31**.
- Terms of reference taken straight from the **Constitution**; population base 2011 (demographic performance measured against 1971).
- Report to the President **17 November 2025**; tabled in Parliament **1 February 2026** (Budget day); devolution accepted in the **Budget 2026-27**; **41%** of the divisible pool retained; total transfers **₹25.44 lakh crore** for 2026-27.
- Total grants **₹9.47 lakh crore**; **revenue-deficit grants discontinued**; **Kerala** got neither revenue-deficit nor State-specific grants.
- **Local-body grants ₹7.91 lakh crore**: rural ~**₹4.35 lakh crore**, urban ~**₹3.56 lakh crore** (= **45%**); urbanisation premium ₹10,000 crore.
- **Disaster corpus ₹2.04 lakh crore**; sharing **90:10** NE/Himalayan States, **75:25** others; **heatwaves and lightning** proposed as new notified disasters.
- **State deficits capped at 3% of GSDP**.
- All five **southern States** gained: Karnataka **4.131%** (15th 3.64%), Tamil Nadu **4.097%**; southern States still call population and income distance criteria unfair.
| Criterion (horizontal) | 15th FC | 16th FC |
|---|---|---|
| **Income distance** | **45** | **42.5** (highest) |
| Population (2011) | 15 | 17.5 |
| Area | 15 | 10 |
| Forest and ecology | 10 | 10 (incl. open forests) |
| Demographic performance | 12.5 | 10 |
| Tax and fiscal effort | 2.5 | **dropped** |
| **Contribution to GDP** | – | **10 (new, first time)** |
- **Vertical devolution** = Centre ↔ States (share of the pool); **horizontal** = among States (criteria and weights).
- Number game: 32 → 42 → 41 → 41 (13th → 14th → 15th → 16th).
