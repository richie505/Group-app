## SUB 1 | Nature of the Indian Party System
[66:1:0] - **Party systems (Laxmikanth):** **one-party** (USSR; today China, **Cuba, North Korea, Eritrea** – not **Chile**, a multi-party democracy); **two-party** (USA, Britain); **multi-party** (France, Switzerland, Italy, **India**).
[66:1:1] - **India is multi-party** – continental size, diverse society (region, language, caste, religion, class), universal adult franchise; India has the **largest number of parties** in the world.
[66:1:2] - The **party system is a part (sub-system) of the political system**.
[66:1:3] - **Features:** one-dominant party (Congress) till 1967; lack of clear ideology (except BJP, CPI, CPM); **personality cult**; parties on traditional factors; rise of **regional parties**; factions and defections (after 1967); lack of effective opposition.
[66:1:4] - The **original Constitution had no provision on parties**; they entered via the **Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985)**.
[66:1:5] - **ECI classes:** national, State, **registered-unrecognised** parties (RUPPs – over 2,800, only ~750 contested in 2024).
[66:1:6] - **Ruling-party misuse:** government servants, vehicles and discretionary grants may be misused to woo voters – "political parties" are not such a government resource.

## SUB 2 | First Non-Congress and Coalition Governments
[66:2:0] - **Centre, 1977:** the **Janata Party** (merger of Congress (O), Jana Sangh, BLD, Socialists; PM **Morarji Desai**, 1977-79) – the **first non-Congress government** and, in most books, the **first coalition government** at the Centre (Laxmikanth Table 85.1 lists it first).
[66:2:1] - Strictly, Janata was one merged party with its own majority (295); the first formal multi-party coalition was the **National Front (1989)**.
[66:2:2] - **States:** the usual answer for the first non-Congress government is the **CPI in Kerala (1957, E.M.S. Namboodiripad)** – the first elected communist government; strictly, **PEPSU's United Front** ministry (**Gian Singh Rarewala, April 1952**) came first.
[66:2:3] - **Coalition government** = an **arrangement of convenience** among parties (Latin *coalitio*, "to grow together") – **not mentioned in the Constitution**, not at the will of the President or Speaker.
[66:2:4] > Asked as: year of the first non-Congress / coalition government (1977 – four PYQs); Janata came to power (1977); first non-Congress State government (CPI, Kerala).

## SUB 5 | Party Systems in the States (incl. Andhra Pradesh)
[66:5:0] - **After 1967:** almost every opposition-ruled State had a coalition – exception **Tamil Nadu (DMK alone)**; **Bihar** saw the **most coalition governments (nine) in 1967-71**.
[66:5:1] TABLE head: State | Pattern
  [66:5:1.0] Kerala | Bipolar: UDF (Congress-led) vs LDF (CPI(M)-led); A.K. Antony resigned as CM of the UDF government on 29 Aug 2004 after the Lok Sabha rout
  [66:5:1.1] Andhra Pradesh | Congress dominance until 1983; then Congress vs TDP; since 2014 TDP vs YSRCP; now a coalition led by a dominant regional party with the BJP as a supporting partner
[66:5:2] - **Congress's lowest AP Assembly tallies (undivided AP):** 1983 – 60, 1985 – 50, **1994 – 26 (lowest)**, 1999 – 91 – against **NTR's TDP**.
[66:5:3] - **AP roots:** Justice Party-era rise of **non-Brahmin agrarian castes** → base for regional parties.
[66:5:4] - **Aug 2026:** BJP rules alone in **11** States, Congress 3, regional parties alone 4, coalitions 10.

[67:4:0] # Features and classification (Laxmikanth)
[67:4:1] - Operates within one State/region; articulates a cultural, linguistic, religious or ethnic identity; seeks power at State level and **greater State autonomy**.

  [67:4:2.3] Personalised (charismatic leader) | short-lived
[67:4:3] # Causes of rise
[67:4:4] - Cultural/ethnic pluralism; **economic disparities and regional imbalance (neglect)**; linguistic reorganisation of States; **failure of national parties** to meet regional aspirations; charismatic leaders; Congress centralisation; caste and religion.

## SUB 2 | Registration and Recognition – Role of the ECI
[68:2:0] TABLE head: Step | Law | Authority
  [68:2:0.0] Registration | Sec. 29A, RPA 1951 | ECI
  [68:2:0.1] Recognition (national/State) and symbols | Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968 | ECI
  [68:2:0.2] Split disputes | Para 15; Sadiq Ali (1971) majority test | ECI
  [68:2:0.3] Suspend/withdraw recognition | Para 16A – defying the Model Code of Conduct | ECI
[68:2:1] - Three statuses: **unregistered**; **registered but unrecognised (RUPP)**; **recognised** (national or State).
[68:2:2] - Apply for registration within **30 days** of formation; the party constitution must pledge allegiance to the **Constitution, socialism, secularism, democracy** and the unity and integrity of India.
[68:2:3] - The ECI **recognises** parties (not the President, Law Ministry or Speaker) on the basis of **poll performance**, and acts as a **court** in recognition and symbol disputes – it **does** settle disputes between splinter groups of recognised parties.
[68:2:4] - NCERT: the ECI requires parties to hold **organisational elections** and file income-tax returns.
[68:2:5] - **Nature of the 1968 Order:** a **statutory order of the ECI** under the RPA 1951 and **Art. 324** – not an Act of Parliament.
[68:2:6] - **Deregistration:** only for **fraud** in obtaining registration, ceasing allegiance to the Constitution, or being declared unlawful (**INC v. Institute of Social Welfare, 2002**); Law Commission **255th** Report seeks a limited power.
[68:2:7] - RUPPs: over **2,800**; about **750** contested in 2024.

## SUB 4 | State Party Criteria
[68:4:0] - A party is a **State party** in a State if it fulfils **any one** of five conditions:
[68:4:1] TABLE head: Route | Condition
  [68:4:1.0] 1 | 6% valid votes in the Assembly election + 2 Assembly seats
  [68:4:1.1] 2 | 6% valid votes in the Lok Sabha election from the State + 1 Lok Sabha seat
  [68:4:1.2] 3 | 3% of Assembly seats or 3 seats, whichever is more
  [68:4:1.3] 4 | 1 Lok Sabha seat for every 25 allotted to the State
  [68:4:1.4] 5 | 8% of valid votes (Lok Sabha or Assembly) – no seat needed
[68:4:2] - The **8%** vote-only route is the one candidates forget (not 10%).

=== NOTES 2:70 | Finance Commission – Composition, Terms of Reference, Functions; 15TH and 16TH Finance Commission
## SUB 0 | Finance Commission – Article 280, Composition and Qualifications
[70:0:0] # Constitutional basis
[70:0:1] - **Article 280** (Part **XII**) provides for the Finance Commission (FC), a **quasi-judicial constitutional body**; Articles **280 and 281** deal with it (Art. **290** = pensions, not the FC; Art. 148 = CAG).
[70:0:2] - Constituted by the **President** within two years of the Constitution's start and then **every fifth year or earlier** as he considers necessary – a **temporary** body, not permanent.
[70:0:3] - The Constitution envisages the FC as the **balancing wheel of fiscal federalism** (Laxmikanth); "Federal Finance Commission" = finances **between the Centre and the States**.
[70:0:4] - **Art. 281:** the **President** lays every recommendation, with an **explanatory memorandum** on the action taken, before each House of Parliament.
[70:0:5] - Recommendations are **advisory, not binding**; **P.V. Rajamannar** (4th FC): they should not be turned down without **very compelling reasons**.
[70:0:6] # Composition and qualifications
[70:0:7] - **Chairman + four other members** (5 in all), appointed by the **President** (Art. 280(1)) – not a Chairman and six members.
[70:0:8] - They hold office for **such period as the President specifies**; members are **eligible for reappointment**; no term fixed in the Constitution.
[70:0:9] - **Parliament** fixes qualifications and manner of selection (Art. 280(2)) – the **Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951** (not the President, the Union Cabinet or the Finance Minister).
[70:0:10] TABLE head: Post | Qualification (1951 Act)
  [70:0:10.0] Chairman | Experience in public affairs
  [70:0:10.1] Member | High Court judge or qualified to be one (one of the possible fields – not compulsory)
  [70:0:10.2] Member | Special knowledge of government finance and accounts
  [70:0:10.3] Member | Wide experience in financial matters and administration
  [70:0:10.4] Member | Special knowledge of economics
[70:0:11] - There is no member post for a monetary policy expert or a Parliament member.
[70:0:12] - The FC has the **powers of a civil court** (summoning persons, production of documents).
[70:0:13] - It reports to the **President**, not to NITI Aayog or Parliament directly.
[70:0:14] - The **101st Amendment (2016)** introduced **GST** – it did not implement any FC report.
[70:0:15] > Statement items test: Art. 280 vs 290, "every fifth year", Chairman + 4, Parliament (not President) fixes qualifications, report → President → Art. 281.

[70:0:17] - **First FC Chairman: K.C. Neogy** (not K. Santhanam, who chaired the 2nd).

## SUB 3 | Functions – Article 280(3) and the Divisible Pool
[70:3:0] # Functions (Art. 280(3)) – recommendations to the President
[70:3:1] - 1. **Distribution of the net proceeds of taxes** between the Centre and the States (**vertical**) and **allocation among the States** (**horizontal**).
[70:3:2] - 2. **Principles governing grants-in-aid** to the States out of the **Consolidated Fund of India** (Art. **275**).
[70:3:3] - 3. **Measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State** to supplement the resources of **Panchayats and Municipalities**, on the basis of the **State Finance Commission**'s recommendations (added by the **73rd/74th Amendments, 1992**).
[70:3:4] - 4. **Any other matter** referred by the President **in the interests of sound finance**.
[70:3:5] - Till 1960 it also suggested grants to Assam, Bihar, Odisha and West Bengal in lieu of jute export duty (Art. 273).
[70:3:6] - **Main function:** deciding the **share of States in Central taxes** – the body that recommends the principles of sharing revenues (not the Inter-State Council, NDC, NITI Aayog, Planning Commission or Finance Ministry).
[70:3:7] - **Not FC functions:** allocation of funds to constitutional bodies, maintaining income-expenditure details of governments, financial control over the Centre or States, fixing **GST rates**, releasing funds.
[70:3:8] - **Agricultural income tax** is a **State** tax (State List entry 46) – the Constitution assigns it; UPSC 1995's key names the FC as the body linked to sharing taxes with States.
[70:3:9] # Divisible pool
[70:3:10] - **Divisible pool** = gross tax revenue − **cesses, surcharges** and **cost of collection**; the **CAG certifies** net proceeds (Art. 279).
[70:3:11] - Rising **cesses and surcharges** shrink the divisible pool, so the States' real share of gross tax revenue is below the headline %.
[70:3:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 20 p.7, 26 p.10, 12; Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.46 p.614-615

## SUB 5 | 15th and 16th Finance Commissions – Devolution Formula
[70:5:0] # 15th Finance Commission (2020–26)
[70:5:1] - Formed **27 November 2017**; Chairman **N.K. Singh**; **first FC to cut** the vertical share: **42% → 41%**, because of the change in **J&K's status** (2019).

[70:5:3] - Local-body grants **₹4.36 lakh crore** (urban share 36%).
[70:5:4] # 16th Finance Commission (2026–31)
[70:5:5] - Constituted **31 December 2023**; Chairman **Arvind Panagariya**; members **T. Rabi Sankar, Annie George Mathew, Manoj Panda, Soumya Kanti Ghosh**; award **2026-27 to 2030-31**.
[70:5:6] - Terms of reference taken straight from the **Constitution**; population base 2011 (demographic performance measured against 1971).
[70:5:7] - Report to the President **17 November 2025**; tabled in Parliament **1 February 2026** (Budget day); devolution accepted in the **Budget 2026-27**; **41%** of the divisible pool retained; total transfers **₹25.44 lakh crore** for 2026-27.
[70:5:8] - Total grants **₹9.47 lakh crore**; **revenue-deficit grants discontinued**; **Kerala** got neither revenue-deficit nor State-specific grants.
[70:5:9] - **Local-body grants ₹7.91 lakh crore**: rural ~**₹4.35 lakh crore**, urban ~**₹3.56 lakh crore** (= **45%**); urbanisation premium ₹10,000 crore.

=== NOTES 2:71 | Comptroller and Auditor General – Appointment, Duties, Types of Audit, Audit Reports and Pac Linkage
## SUB 0 | CAG – Status, Appointment, Term and Removal
[71:0:0] # Status
[71:0:1] - Official name: **Comptroller and Auditor-General of India**; **Part V, Chapter V**, Articles **148–151** – an **independent constitutional authority created by the Constitution** (Art. 148), not by an Act, a Cabinet resolution or under the Finance Ministry.
[71:0:2] TABLE head: Article | Content
  [71:0:2.0] 148 | Appointment, oath, term, removal; 148(4) post-retirement bar; 148(6) expenses charged
  [71:0:2.1] 149 | Duties and powers (Parliament prescribes them – CAG Act, 1971)
  [71:0:2.2] 150 | Form of accounts – prescribed by the President on the CAG's advice
  [71:0:2.3] 151 | Audit reports: Union → President (151(1)); State → Governor (151(2))
[71:0:3] - Head of the **Indian Audit and Accounts Department**; "**Guardian of the Public Purse**" / of public finance.
[71:0:4] - **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**: the CAG is "the **most important officer** under the Constitution"; one of the bulwarks of democracy with the **Supreme Court, Election Commission and UPSC**; auditing both Union and State accounts is a **unitary** feature.
[71:0:5] - The CAG is **not part of the Union Executive** (President, VP, PM, Council of Ministers, Attorney General), nor is the **Speaker** (part of Parliament).
[71:0:6] - For the President's election, **CAG** is an **office of profit**; the posts of VP, Governor and Minister are not (Art. 58 explanation).
[71:0:7] - **Table of Precedence:** CAG is at **9A** with the **Chairperson UPSC** and the **Chief Election Commissioner**; Chief Justices of High Courts are at 14.
[71:0:8] # Appointment, term and removal
[71:0:9] - Appointed by the **President** by **warrant under his hand and seal** (Art. 148(1)) – not the PM, FM, UPSC, Parliament or a PM–LoP committee; Attorney General (Art. 76) and Governor (Art. 155) are also appointed by the President.
[71:0:10] - **Oath** before the **President** (or a person he appoints); form in the **Third Schedule**.
[71:0:11] - **Term: 6 years or age 65, whichever is earlier** (CAG Act, 1971) – the longest among President, VP and Lok Sabha member (5 years). Same as **CEC/ECs and UPSC members**; SPSC members 6 years or **62**; **SC judge retires at 65**, **HC judge at 62** (15th Amendment, 1963: 60 → 62).
[71:0:12] - **Removal** in like manner and on like grounds as a **Supreme Court judge** (Art. 148(1) with Art. 124(4)): by the President on an **address by both Houses** with **special majority** for **proved misbehaviour or incapacity** – he does **not** hold office at the President's pleasure; resigns to the President.
[71:0:13] - **Constitution prescribes no qualifications** for the CAG; it is a **single-member** office.
[71:0:14] # Persons

  [71:0:15.0] V. Narahari Rao | First CAG of independent India (from 15 August 1948)

## SUB 1 | Audit Reports, PAC and Parliamentary Control
[71:1:0] # Report to action
[71:1:1] - **CAG report → President (Union) / Governor (State)** under **Art. 151** → laid before **Parliament / State Legislature** → examined by the **Public Accounts Committee** (PSUs: **Committee on Public Undertakings**) → **Action Taken Notes**.
[71:1:2] - Reports go first to the **President**, not to the PAC, the Speaker, the PM, the CJI or the Vice-President; State reports to the **Governor** (Art. **151(2)**).
[71:1:3] - Three Union reports: **Appropriation Accounts, Finance Accounts, Public Undertakings** (not Estimates).
[71:1:4] - **Ultimate action** on CAG reports rests with **Parliament** (not the President, SC or NDC).
[71:1:5] - **Art. 281/151/338:** the President must lay before Parliament the **FC recommendations**, the **CAG report** and the **NCSC report** – the **PAC report** is not laid by him (it goes to the Speaker).
[71:1:6] # Public Accounts Committee
[71:1:7] - Set up **1921** (GoI Act, 1919); **22 members (15 LS + 7 RS)**, elected yearly by **single transferable vote**; **no Minister**.
[71:1:8] - Chairman appointed by the **Speaker**, from the **Opposition since 1967**; 2026-27 chair **K.C. Venugopal**; PAC reports to the **Speaker** / Parliament.
[71:1:9] - Main function: **examine the CAG's audit reports** (Appropriation and Finance Accounts) – not day-to-day interference or orders.
[71:1:10] - CAG = PAC's "**friend, philosopher and guide**" – he **attends its meetings** (not the AG or Solicitor General); not of the Estimates Committee or COPU.
[71:1:11] - PAC recommendations are **not binding**; backlog of Action Taken Notes (May 2026).
[71:1:12] # CAG and Parliament
[71:1:13] - **No Minister represents the CAG** in Parliament (not the Finance Minister).
[71:1:14] - The CAG has **no right to speak, take part or vote** in either House or a joint sitting – these Art. 88 rights (speak and take part, **no vote**) belong to **Ministers and the Attorney General**.
[71:1:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 20 p.12, 27 p.5; Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.52 p.634; app's current notes (row 71)
## SUB 2 | Duties and Powers – Article 149, 1971 Act, Types of Audit
[71:2:0] # Duties
[71:2:1] - **CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971**; **1976 amendment** (in force **1 March 1976**): **accounts separated from audit** (departmentalisation) – Union accounts now compiled by the **Controller General of Accounts** (Finance Ministry), who gives the FM a **monthly** analytical review; the CAG still keeps most **State accounts**.
[71:2:2] - Audits: expenditure from the **Consolidated Funds** of India, States and UTs with legislature; **Contingency Funds** and **Public Accounts**; trading, manufacturing, profit-and-loss accounts; **receipts** payable into the CFI; **stores and stock**.
[71:2:3] - Also **government companies** and bodies **substantially financed** by government; **local bodies** only on request of the President/Governor.
[71:2:4] - **Certifies net proceeds** of any tax or duty (incl. income tax, Art. 279) – his certificate is final; advises the President on the **form of accounts** (Art. 150).
[71:2:5] - Duty: **uphold the Constitution and laws of Parliament** in financial administration – checks not only **legality** but also **propriety** of expenditure.
[71:2:6] # Types of audit
[71:2:7] TABLE head: Type | Nature | Tests
  [71:2:7.0] Legal and regulatory (compliance) | Obligatory | Money legally available, spent as rules require
  [71:2:7.1] Financial attest | Obligatory | Accounts true
  [71:2:7.2] Propriety | Discretionary | Wisdom, faithfulness, economy; flags extravagance
  [71:2:7.3] Performance | Discretionary (2006 OM) | 3 Es: economy, efficiency, effectiveness
[71:2:8] - **Social audit**: CAG laid down the **Auditing Standards for Social Audit** of **MGNREGA** (2016).
[71:2:9] # Limits
[71:2:10] - Only an **Auditor General**, not a Comptroller: **no control over the issue of money** from the Consolidated Fund or the receipt/lodging of revenue (Britain's C&AG has it); audit is **ex-post**.
[71:2:11] - He does **not control the entire financial system of the Union only** – he audits both **Centre and States**; cannot disallow expenditure, enforce recovery, prosecute or **suo motu investigate corruption** – he only reports.
[71:2:12] - **Secret service expenditure**: accepts a **certificate** of the competent authority.
[71:2:13] TABLE head: Category | Example
  [71:2:13.0] Audited directly by CAG | DVC, ONGC
  [71:2:13.1] Private auditors on CAG's advice + CAG supplementary audit | Central Warehousing Corp., IFCI
  [71:2:13.2] Wholly private audit (no CAG role) | LIC, RBI
[71:2:14] - **Paul Appleby** criticised the CAG's role and suggested relieving it of audit.
[71:2:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 20 p.11-12, 27 p.4-5; Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.52 p.633-636
## SUB 3 | Independence Safeguards
[71:3:0] - **Security of tenure** – removal only by the constitutional procedure.
[71:3:1] - **Salary = Supreme Court judge**, determined by Parliament – **Second Schedule (Part E)**; salary, leave, pension and retirement age **cannot be varied to his disadvantage**.
[71:3:2] - **Leave** granted or refused by the **President**.
[71:3:3] - His **salary** (Art. 112(3)) and the office's expenses (salaries, allowances, pensions of staff) are **charged on the Consolidated Fund of India** – non-votable (Art. 148(6)); the Attorney General's and Service Chiefs' salaries are not charged.
[71:3:4] - **Complete bar** on further office under GoI or any State after retirement (Art. 148(4)) – the UPSC Chairman is also barred; retired judges often head commissions.
[71:3:5] - Service conditions of IA&AD staff and his administrative powers are prescribed by the President **after consulting the CAG**.
[71:3:6] - **Not** a safeguard: holding office during the President's pleasure.

=== NOTES 2:72 | Attorney General and Advocate General; Solicitor General
## SUB 0 | Attorney General – Highest Law Officer, Appointment and Qualification (Art. 76)
[72:0:0] # The office
[72:0:1] - **Attorney General (AG) of India** = the **highest law officer** of the country and the **first law officer / chief legal adviser** of the **Government of India** (Art. 76).
[72:0:2]   - Distractors: the **Chief Justice of India** heads the judiciary, the **Law Minister** looks after legal matters in the Cabinet, the **Solicitor General** is only the **second** law officer, the CAG is the auditor.
[72:0:3] - **Part V** (The Union, Chapter I – The Executive); the AG is part of the **Union Executive** but **not a member of the Union Cabinet**.
[72:0:4] - **Only the AG** (Union) and the **Advocate General** (State) are constitutional law officers; the **Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General are not mentioned in Art. 76**.
[72:0:5] # Appointment and qualification – Art. 76(1)
[72:0:6] - Appointed by the **President** (on the advice of the Union Cabinet) – **not** by the PM, the CJI or "after consulting the Vice-President".
[72:0:7] - Must be **qualified to be appointed a judge of the Supreme Court** – not merely a High Court judge, "any advocate" or "a retired SC judge".
[72:0:8] TABLE head: SC-judge qualification (Art. 124(3)) = AG qualification
  [72:0:8.0] Citizen of India, and
  [72:0:8.1] (a) a High Court judge for 5 years, or
  [72:0:8.2] (b) an advocate of a High Court for 10 years, or
  [72:0:8.3] (c) a distinguished jurist in the opinion of the President
[72:0:9] - **Advocate General** (State) needs only the **High Court-judge** qualification (Art. 165(1)).

## SUB 1 | Attorney General – Rights in Parliament and in the Courts
[72:1:0] # Parliament – Art. 88
[72:1:1] - The AG has the right to **speak and otherwise take part** in the proceedings of **both Houses**, their **joint sitting** and **any parliamentary committee of which he is named a member** – **without being an MP**.
[72:1:2] - He **cannot vote** (UPSC 2013: "take part, be a committee member, speak" correct; "vote" wrong).
[72:1:3] - **Art. 105(4)**: he enjoys the **privileges and immunities of an MP**.
[72:1:4] - **Only the AG** among the listed officers has this right: **not** the Solicitor General, CJI, CEC, CAG, Finance Commission chairman, NHRC chairman or National Security Adviser.
[72:1:5]   - Ministers also speak in both Houses (Art. 88), so "AG and SG are the only officers allowed to participate in Parliament" is **wrong** on both counts (UPSC 2022).
[72:1:6] # Courts – Art. 76(3)
[72:1:7] - **Right of audience in all courts in the territory of India** – not only the Supreme Court, not only High Courts or Sessions courts.
[72:1:8] - The President has assigned him to **appear for the Government of India in all Supreme Court cases** in which it is concerned, to represent it in **Art. 143** references, and to appear in any High Court when required.

## SUB 2 | Advocate General of the State – Article 165
[72:2:0] # Office and appointment
[72:2:1] - **Highest (first) law officer of the State** and its **official legal adviser** – the State counterpart of the AG (Art. 76). Not the Government Pleader, Standing Counsel or Amicus Curiae.
[72:2:2] - **Part VI** (The States). Appointed by the **Governor** (Art. 165(1)) – **not the President** "on the Governor's recommendation", not the CM or Law Minister (UPSC 2009: statement false).
[72:2:3] - Qualification: **qualified to be a High Court judge** – citizen + **10 years in a judicial office** or **10 years as a High Court advocate** (no "jurist" route) – not Vidhan Sabha / Lok Sabha membership, not a magistrate.
[72:2:4] - **Pleasure of the Governor** (Art. 165(3)) – not the CM or State Cabinet; no fixed term; by convention resigns with the State government.
[72:2:5] - **Remuneration determined by the Governor** – not equal to an HC judge, Chief Secretary or State minister (APPSC Group-II Prelims 2017).
[72:2:6] # Duties and rights
[72:2:7] - **Art. 165(2)**: advise the **State Government** on legal matters referred by the Governor and perform other legal duties assigned.
[72:2:8] - **Art. 177**: may speak and take part in **either House of the State Legislature** and its committees – **no vote**; **Art. 194(4)**: privileges of a member of the State Legislature.
[72:2:9] - Right of audience: in courts **in the State** (the AG: all courts in India).
[72:2:10] - **Not appointed by the Governor:** **High Court judges** (the **President** appoints them, Art. 217); the Governor does appoint the CM, the Advocate General and the SPSC chairman.
[72:2:11] - **AP:** the Advocate General of AP is appointed by the **Governor of AP**; **Dammalapati Srinivas** was reappointed on **19 June 2024**. The Advocate General may use the **multi-colour AP State emblem** (G.O. of 14 Nov 2018).

## SUB 3 | Attorney General – Term, Remuneration, Duties and Limitations
[72:3:0] # Term and removal – Art. 76(4)
[72:3:1] - **No fixed term** in the Constitution; **no grounds or procedure for removal**; holds office **during the pleasure of the President** – not the PM, not Parliament. So "the President cannot remove the AG" is **wrong**.
[72:3:2] - **Resigns** to the **President**; **by convention** (not by the Constitution) he resigns when the Council of Ministers resigns or is replaced (UPSC 2022: "the Constitution requires him to resign with the government" = wrong).
[72:3:3] - **Remuneration**: "such remuneration as the **President** may determine" – **not Parliament**, not fixed by the Constitution, not charged on the Consolidated Fund.
[72:3:4] # Duties – Art. 76(2)
[72:3:5] - 1. **Advise the Government of India** on legal matters **referred by the President**.
[72:3:6] - 2. Perform other duties of a legal character **assigned by the President**.
[72:3:7] - 3. Discharge functions conferred by the Constitution or any law.
[72:3:8] - References reach him through the **Ministry of Law and Justice (Department of Legal Affairs)** – only legal matters of real importance.
[72:3:9] - He does **not** preside over joint sittings of Parliament.
[72:3:10] # Limitations
[72:3:11] - Should **not advise or hold a brief against the Government of India**, nor in cases where he must advise or appear for it.
[72:3:12] - Should **not defend accused persons** in criminal prosecutions, or become a company director, **without the permission of the Government of India**.
[72:3:13] - **Not a full-time counsel** and **not a government servant**, so **not debarred from private practice**.

## SUB 4 | Solicitor General and the Office-holders
[72:4:0] # Solicitor General (SG) and ASGs
[72:4:1] - **SG = second law officer** of India; **assists the AG**; is himself assisted by **Additional Solicitors General**.
[72:4:2] - He is a **legal adviser** (tenders advice and appears for the Union) – not an administrative officer, not the President's personal law officer.

[72:4:5] - **AG and SG** are **ex officio members of the Bar Council of India** (**Advocates Act, 1961**).
