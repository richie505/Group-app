=== NOTES 4:37 | Peninsular Drainage – East-flowing and West-flowing Rivers, River Basins, Watershed
## SUB 0 | Drainage Divisions – Himalayan vs Peninsular
[37:0:0] # Comparison
[37:0:1] TABLE head: Feature | Himalayan rivers | Peninsular rivers
  [37:0:1.0] Regime | Perennial (rain + snowmelt) | Seasonal, rain-fed (monsoon)
  [37:0:1.1] Valley | Deep antecedent gorges | Broad, shallow, graded valleys; older
  [37:0:1.2] Course | Meandering, shifting | Fixed, straight (hard rock)
  [37:0:1.3] Mouth | Large deltas | Deltas (east) / estuaries (west)
  [37:0:1.4] Use | Irrigation, navigation | Hydropower; delta irrigation (east)
[37:0:2] - **Antecedent rivers**: rise in the trans-Himalaya and kept their course by cutting gorges as ranges rose – **Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra**.
[37:0:3] - **Chambal and Son** – **superimposed** rivers. **Damodar** – east-flowing in a **rift valley** (down-warping), Chota Nagpur, **Gondwana trough**.
[37:0:4] - Deccan Plateau is higher in the west and slopes **east** – so most peninsular rivers flow east.
[37:0:5] # Drainage Patterns
[37:0:6] TABLE head: Pattern | Form | Example
  [37:0:6.0] Dendritic | Tree-like branching on uniform rock/alluvium | Ganga plains; Godavari, Krishna
  [37:0:6.1] Trellis | Main streams in parallel valleys of folded hard and soft rocks; tributaries at right angles | Folded ranges
  [37:0:6.2] Rectangular | Right-angle bends along joints and faults | Vindhyan region
  [37:0:6.3] Radial | Streams flow outward from a central high point | Amarkantak – Narmada, Son, Mahanadi
  [37:0:6.4] Centripetal | Streams converge into a central low/lake | Loktak (Manipur)
## SUB 1 | East-flowing vs West-flowing Rivers
[37:1:0] # Comparison
[37:1:1] TABLE head: Feature | East-flowing (Bay of Bengal) | West-flowing (Arabian Sea)
  [37:1:1.0] Rivers | Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi, Pennar, Palar, Vamsadhara, Nagavali, Subarnarekha, Brahmani | Narmada, Tapi, Sabarmati, Mahi, Periyar, Mandovi, Sharavati, Kali, Vaitarna
  [37:1:1.1] Mouth | Large deltas | Estuaries → natural harbours
  [37:1:1.2] Use | Irrigation | Hydropower
[37:1:2] - East-flowing, north→south: **Subarnarekha, Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Pennar, Cauvery, Vaigai**.
[37:1:3] - **Periyar** – west-flowing, via **Vembanad** to the Arabian Sea. **Krishna** does NOT reach the Arabian Sea (rises ~64 km from it, flows ~1,400 km to the Bay of Bengal).
[37:1:4] - About **600** short, swift rivers drain the west coast.
[37:1:5] - A-R: West-flowing rivers form **no deltas** because they do not carry alluvial sediments – both true, R explains A.
[37:1:6] - **Tapi** – one of only **three** peninsular rivers running east to west; makes **no delta**.
[37:1:7] - **Betwa** is not west-flowing; **Rapti** is a Ghaghara tributary flowing NW→SE.

[37:1:12] - **Sabarmati** – Aravallis (Rajasthan) → Gujarat; Ahmedabad.
[37:1:13] - **Sahibi** – north-flowing river from the Aravallis.
[37:1:14] - Ghaggar water is NOT used in the Indira Gandhi Canal.

[37:6:5] - **Kali (Kalinadi)** – rises near **Diggi**, Uttara Kannada; 184 km; Karwar, Kaiga.
[37:6:6] - **Periyar** – Kerala; **Mullaperiyar** dam (Periyar lake is man-made); **Cheruthoni Dam** on a Periyar tributary, Idukki.
[37:6:7] - **Mithi** – rises at **Vihar Lake**, Mumbai; Powai Lake water joins after 2 km; 18 km to the sea near **Mahim Bay**.
