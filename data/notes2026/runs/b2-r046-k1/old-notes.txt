=== NOTES 2:46 | Federal Structure of India – Federalism, Unitary Bias, Seventh Schedule and Residuary Powers
## SUB 0 | Federalism – Meaning and Types
[46:0:0] # Definitions
[46:0:1] - **Federalism:** Union and States **share power**, each **supreme in its own sphere**, under a **written Constitution**, with an **independent umpire** (judiciary).
[46:0:2] - **Unitary government:** one supreme central government; local units are its **creations** with delegated power; **no constitutional division of powers**.
[46:0:3] - **Confederation:** loose league of **sovereign States** that keep the **right to secede**.
[46:0:4] - Federation = constitutional division of powers.
[46:0:5] # Unitary Government – Merits and Demerits
[46:0:6] TABLE head: Merits | Demerits
  [46:0:6.0] Uniform laws and policy | Over-centralisation
  [46:0:6.1] Quick decisions | Local needs neglected
  [46:0:6.2] Strong crisis response | Poorly suited to large, diverse countries
[46:0:7] - Regional diversity through autonomous governments is a **federal** trait, not a unitary one.
[46:0:8] "Ideal for large countries" is NOT a characteristic of unitary government.
[46:0:9] # Holding-together vs Coming-together
[46:0:10] TABLE head: Type | Meaning | Examples
  [46:0:10.0] Coming-together | Independent States pool sovereignty (integration) | USA, Switzerland, Australia
  [46:0:10.1] Holding-together | Large country divides power to hold diversity; stronger Centre (disintegration) | India, Spain, Belgium
[46:0:11] - India moved from a **unitary colonial system** to a federal one, so it has a **centripetal bias**.
[46:0:12] - Centralising design because of **Partition** and the joining of **14 provinces** and **500+ princely States**.
[46:0:13] USA | integration | coming-together
India | disintegration | holding-together
[46:0:14] # Federal and Unitary Countries
[46:0:15] TABLE head: Federal | Unitary
  [46:0:15.0] USA (by agreement of States), Canada, Australia, Argentina | UK (Parliament holds all law-making power)
  [46:0:15.1] Switzerland, Germany, Russia, Brazil | France, Italy, Japan, China, Sri Lanka
[46:0:16] Sri Lanka is unitary, not federal.
## SUB 1 | India – A Union of States
[46:1:0] # Article 1
[46:1:1] - **Art. 1(1):** "**India, that is Bharat**, shall be a **Union of States**." Names used: **Bharat and India**.
[46:1:2] - The word "**Federation**" is **nowhere** used in the Constitution.
[46:1:3] - "A Union of States" is derived from the **British North America Act, 1867** (Canada).
[46:1:4] - Territory of India = **States + UTs + acquired territories**; **Union of India** = States only. Both listed in the **First Schedule**.
[46:1:5] # Ambedkar's Reasons for "Union"
[46:1:6] - 1. The federation is **not the result of an agreement** among States (unlike USA).
[46:1:7] - 2. **No State has the right to secede**; the Union is **indestructible**.
[46:1:8] - States are divisions only for **administrative convenience**; the Union of India is an **indissoluble union** of States.
[46:1:9] # Indestructible Union of Destructible States – Articles 2-4
[46:1:10] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [46:1:10.0] 2 | Admission or establishment of new States not part of India (e.g. Sikkim)
  [46:1:10.1] 3 | Formation of new States; alter area, boundary, name – by simple majority
  [46:1:10.2] 4 | Laws under Arts 2-3 amend First and Fourth Schedules; not Art. 368 amendments
[46:1:11] - Art. 3 Bill needs the **President's prior recommendation** and reference to the State legislature, whose views are **not binding**; **no consent** needed.
[46:1:12] - Power to make a new State lies with **Parliament**.
[46:1:13] - No territorial guarantee, so India = "**indestructible Union of destructible States**"; USA = "indestructible Union of **indestructible** States".
[46:1:14] Article 3: Parliament can alter State boundaries without the State's consent.
## SUB 2 | Federal vs Unitary Features
[46:2:0] # Classification Table
[46:2:1] TABLE head: Federal features | Unitary (non-federal) features
  [46:2:1.0] Dual polity (two governments) | Strong Centre (Canadian model); Union prevails in Concurrent List
  [46:2:1.1] Written Constitution; supremacy of the Constitution | Residuary powers with Parliament
  [46:2:1.2] Division of powers (Seventh Schedule, three Lists) | Single Constitution (States cannot frame their own)
  [46:2:1.3] Rigid Constitution (federal parts need State ratification) | Flexibility: most provisions amended by Parliament alone
  [46:2:1.4] Independent judiciary (umpire in Centre-State disputes) | Single citizenship
  [46:2:1.5] Bicameralism (Rajya Sabha for States) | Integrated judiciary; Centre appoints HC judges
  [46:2:1.6] Unequal Rajya Sabha seats listed by some as federal | Governor appointed by Centre (Arts 155-156)
  [46:2:1.7]  | All India Services (Art. 312) – recruited by UPSC, central control
  [46:2:1.8]  | Emergency provisions (Arts 352-360)
  [46:2:1.9]  | Parliament can alter State boundaries (Art. 3)
  [46:2:1.10]  | Integrated audit (CAG) and elections (EC)
  [46:2:1.11]  | "Union of States", no right to secede; unequal Rajya Sabha representation
  [46:2:1.12]  | President's approval on State Bills; Parliament's authority over State List
[46:2:2] - **Independence of the judiciary** = the **essential** feature showing India is federal (UPSC 2021); **judicial review** strengthens the federal principle – not a unitary element.
[46:2:3] - Judicial review includes examining **executive orders**; it does not question the **policy merits** of laws.
[46:2:4] - Federal features in another key: written Constitution, distribution of powers, supremacy, independent judiciary, unequal representation in Rajya Sabha, dual governments.
[46:2:5] - India is federal **because of the distribution of powers** between Union and States.
[46:2:6] - The Constitution is **neither wholly rigid nor wholly flexible**.
[46:2:7] "Not a federal feature" answers: Office of the Governor (APPSC-G1 2018), CAG (APPSC-G2 2017), "Allocation of financial resources" (APPSC-G2 2017), Single Citizenship.
[46:2:8] # Centrally Controlled Institutions – Violating Federal Principles
[46:2:9] - APPSC 2025 key: **Office of the Governor**, **CAG**, **Emergency powers** (esp. **Art. 356**) violate federal principles; **Central Services** treated as cooperation, not a violation.
[46:2:10] - **All India Services** (Art. 312): recruited and controlled by the Centre but serving the States – a centrally controlled institution.
[46:2:11] Do not leave out **Central Services** – left out of that four-option key, yet All India Services are still said to limit State autonomy (central service rules, dismissal only by the Centre).
[46:2:12] - Critics call the Governor the **Centre's agent**.
[46:2:13] - CAG audits **both Union and State** accounts; EC runs elections to Parliament and State legislatures.
[46:2:14] # Form of Government
[46:2:15] - **Parliamentary form** (British model): nominal head = **President**; real executive = **Council of Ministers**, collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha** (Arts **74(1), 75**).
[46:2:16] - India has an **integrated judicial and administrative** framework.

## SUB 5 | Residuary Powers and Borrowed Features

[46:5:1] - **Article 248** (Part **XI**, Chapter I) + **Entry 97, List I**: Parliament has **exclusive** power over matters in **no list**.
[46:5:2] - Includes **residuary taxes** (gift tax, wealth tax, expenditure tax).
[46:5:3] - Art. 248 made "**subject to Article 246A**" by the **101st Amendment, 2016** (GST).

## SUB 6 | 42ND Amendment Transfers (1976)
[46:6:0] - **Five** subjects moved State → Concurrent: **education, forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals and birds, administration of justice**.
[46:6:1] - Administration of justice = courts **other than** the SC and HCs. Higher education moved too.
[46:6:2] - Inserted **Entry 20A**: **population control and family planning**.
[46:6:3] Public order was NOT transferred; it stays in the State List.
[46:6:4] - Debate: Centre uses Concurrent entries for sweeping laws – **farm laws** (Entry 33), **NEP** and **NMC Act** (education).
## SUB 7 | Switching to Unitary Mode – Emergency and Overriding Powers
[46:7:0] # Overriding Powers
[46:7:1] TABLE head: Article | Power
  [46:7:1.0] 249 | Rajya Sabha resolution (2/3 present and voting) in national interest → Parliament legislates on State List; valid 1 year, renewable; Lok Sabha takes no part
  [46:7:1.1] 250 | During National Emergency, Parliament legislates on State List; laws lapse 6 months after revocation
  [46:7:1.2] 252 | Two or more States request Parliament to legislate
  [46:7:1.3] 253 | Laws to implement international agreements
  [46:7:1.4] 356 | President's Rule – Parliament/President exercise State powers
[46:7:2] - Emergency turns the system unitary **without a formal amendment** – a unique feature.
[46:7:3] # Emergencies Compared (Part XVIII, Arts 352-360)
[46:7:4] TABLE head: Basis | 352 National | 356 President's Rule | 360 Financial
  [46:7:4.0] Ground | War, external aggression, armed rebellion | Failure of constitutional machinery (also Art. 365) | Threat to financial stability or credit
  [46:7:4.1] Approval | 1 month, special majority | 2 months, simple | 2 months, simple
  [46:7:4.2] Duration | 6 months at a time, no maximum | 6 months at a time, max 3 years | Until revoked
  [46:7:4.3] Used | 1962, 1971, 1975 | Over 100 times | Never
[46:7:5] - Beyond **1 year**, President's Rule needs a **National Emergency** in operation + **EC certification** (Art. 356(5)).
[46:7:6] - If the Lok Sabha is dissolved after the Rajya Sabha approves, the proclamation survives **30 days** from the new Lok Sabha's first sitting (all three).
[46:7:7] # Effects of National Emergency on States
[46:7:8] - Union can **direct any State** on its executive power.
[46:7:9] - Parliament may extend the **Lok Sabha and Assembly terms** by **1 year at a time**, at most **6 months** after it ends.
[46:7:10] - **Art. 354:** President may **modify revenue distribution** (Arts 268-281) – **reduce or cancel** transfers; lasts till the end of the **financial year** in which the proclamation ceases.
[46:7:11] - **Art. 19** suspended automatically only for **war or external aggression** (Art. 358); others by order (Art. 359) except **Arts 20-21**.
[46:7:12] - Only National Emergency affects Fundamental Rights; Arts 356 and 360 do not.
[46:7:13] "Increase the transfer" to States is a WRONG statement (APPSC-G2 2017).
[46:7:14] # Financial Emergency (Art. 360)
[46:7:15] - Directions on **financial propriety**; State **Money Bills** reserved for the President; **salary cuts** incl. **SC and HC judges**.
[46:7:16] - No automatic takeover of State legislative powers. "Art. 311 – Financial Emergency" is a mismatch.
[46:7:17] # 44th Amendment, 1978 – Safeguards
[46:7:18] - "Internal disturbance" → "**armed rebellion**" (APPSC-GS 2018); approval **2 months → 1 month**; **Arts 20-21** non-suspendable; Cabinet's **written advice**.
[46:7:19] - **1/10th** of Lok Sabha members' notice → sitting within **14 days** to disapprove.
[46:7:20] - 38th Amendment barred judicial review of Emergency; **Minerva Mills** reopened it.
[46:7:21] - **1975 Emergency:** 25 June 1975 (internal disturbance); withdrawn **21 March 1977**. **Samvidhan Hatya Diwas: 25 June**.
[46:7:22] - **AP** under President's Rule in **1973** (Jai Andhra agitation).
[46:7:23] - **Emergency Part:** the Emergency provisions are in **Part XVIII** (Articles 352-360) of the Constitution.
