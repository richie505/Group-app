## SUB 0 | The Two Home Rule Leagues (1916)
[0:0] # Why and when
[0:1] - **Home Rule Movement** = the popular movement of India **during the First World War** (not Swadeshi, Separatist or Swaraj Party); on the lines of the **Irish Home Rule Leagues**; aim **self-government within the British Empire**.
[0:2] - Causes: need for popular pressure, disillusion with the **Morley-Minto reforms**, wartime miseries; **Tilak released from Mandalay (June 1914)** after six years.
[0:3] - **Two leagues, 1916**, led by **Tilak and Annie Besant** (not Aurobindo, Lajpat Rai or B.C. Pal); they demarcated areas and cooperated.
[0:4] # Comparison
[0:5] TABLE head: Feature | Tilak's Indian Home Rule League | Besant's All India Home Rule League
  [0:5.0] Founded | April 1916 (28 April) – the first league | September 1916
  [0:5.1] Place | Launched at the Bombay Provincial Conference, Belgaum; headquarters Poona | Madras (HQ Adyar)
  [0:5.2] Area | Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces, Berar | Rest of India, including Bombay city
  [0:5.3] Branches | 6 | about 200
  [0:5.4] Office-bearers | President Joseph Baptista; Secretary N.C. Kelkar | Organising secretary George Arundale; C.P. Ramaswamy Aiyar, B.P. Wadia
[0:6] - **Associated leaders:** **S. Subramania Iyer** (co-leader with Besant), G.S. Khaparde, Joseph Baptista, **Jinnah** (Bombay; also popularised it in UP), Motilal and Jawaharlal Nehru, Madan Mohan Malaviya, Tej Bahadur Sapru, Lajpat Rai.
[0:7] - **H.S. Olcott** – co-founder of the **Theosophical Society** (died 1907), **not** a Home Rule leader.
[0:8] - **C.R. Das** – the usual answer for "not associated" (he only joined the agitation later; Spectrum p.329 lists him among those who joined).
[0:9] - **1915 Bombay Congress:** Besant **failed** to get the Congress to approve her Home Rule League scheme – no Home Rule resolution.
[0:10] # Fate
[0:11] - Tilak's League later merged with Besant's; **1920:** Gandhi became President of the **All India Home Rule League** and renamed it **Swarajya Sabha**; it soon merged into the Congress.
[0:12] - **Year of merger of the two leagues = 1920** (not 1916, 1918 or 1923).
[0:13] > Statement trap (APPSC FRO): "Tilak founded the Indian Home Rule League in **1914** and was its **president**" ✗ (April 1916; president Joseph Baptista); "Tilak played a key role in the **Lucknow Pact (1916)** with Jinnah" ✓.

## SUB 1 | Annie Besant – Theosophist, Papers and Congress President
[1:0] # Life and institutions
[1:1] - Irish theosophist; came to India in **1893**; President of the **Theosophical Society** from **1907** (HQ Adyar); called the "**Iron Lady of Ireland**".
[1:2] - **Central Hindu College, Varanasi (1898)** – became the core of **Banaras Hindu University**, whose founder is **Madan Mohan Malaviya** (1916) – not Besant or Asutosh Mukherjee.
[1:3] - **National College, Madanapalle (1916)** – first principal **J.H. Cousins**; protégé **Jiddu Krishnamurti** (*At the Feet of the Master*).
[1:4] # Her papers
[1:5] TABLE head: Paper | Fact
  [1:5.0] The Commonweal | English weekly, Madras, January 1914 – editor Annie Besant
  [1:5.1] New India | She bought the *Madras Standard* (July 1914) and renamed it New India (a daily)
[1:6] - **Both papers = Besant** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025); "New Hindu" and "The Aryans" are distractors; New India ≠ Gandhi.
[1:7] # Congress President and later work
[1:8] TABLE head: President | Session | Fact
  [1:8.0] Annie Besant | Calcutta, 1917 | First woman President of the INC
  [1:8.1] Sarojini Naidu | Kanpur, 1925 | First Indian woman President
  [1:8.2] Nellie Sengupta | Calcutta, 1933 | After president-elect Malaviya was arrested
[1:9] - Sucheta Kripalani, Aruna Asaf Ali, Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit, Kasturba – never INC presidents.
[1:10] - **1918:** Besant visited **Bihar (Patna)** to strengthen the Home Rule League.
[1:11] - Quote: "**I am an Indian Tom-Tom**, whose work is waking up all the sleepers…" – **Annie Besant**.
[1:12] - **Chief architect** of the **Moderate–Extremist reunion** at Lucknow (1916), with Tilak.
[1:13] # Commonwealth of India Bill (1925)
[1:14] - Drafted by the **National Convention** (Sapru); amended by an All Parties Conference sub-committee chaired by **Besant**; introduced in the House of Commons by **George Lansbury** (Labour), **December 1925**; defeated.
[1:15] - Proposed **dominion status** ("equal footing with the self-governing dominions") and a **parliamentary system**; its Article 7 listed **seven fundamental rights**.
[1:16] - Called the "Dominion of India Bill" in the APPSC key – not the Nehru Report (1928) or the Sapru Report.

## SUB 2 | Tilak, Lucknow 1916 and the Lucknow Pact
[2:0] # Tilak
[2:1] - **"Father of Indian Unrest"** – **Valentine Chirol** (*Indian Unrest*); Tilak went to England (Sept 1918) to fight a libel case against him.
[2:2] - **"Lokmanya"** ("beloved / respected by the people") – exam keys tie it to the **Home Rule Movement**; Spectrum p.593 says he got it after his **1897 sedition trial**.
[2:3] - "**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it**" – Tilak; papers **Kesari (Marathi)** and **Mahratta (English)**.
[2:4] # Lucknow session, December 1916
[2:5] - President **A.C. Majumdar**; the **Surat split (1907)** healed – Extremists readmitted; helped by Tilak's and Besant's efforts and the deaths of Gokhale and Pherozeshah Mehta (1915).
[2:6] - The session where the **Home Rule supporters demonstrated their political power** (the Home Rule Leagues held a joint meeting of 1,000+ delegates).
[2:7] - **Lucknow Pact (Congress–League):** negotiated by **Tilak and Jinnah**; Congress accepted **separate electorates**; "**Better two united fists…**" – Tilak.
[2:8] - Tilak proposed at Lucknow a small **Working Committee** to turn the Congress into a body able to lead a sustained movement (created only in 1920).

## SUB 3 | Impact, Decline and the August Declaration
[3:0] # Repression and the British answer
[3:1] - **June 1917:** Besant, **B.P. Wadia** and **George Arundale** interned (Ootacamund); S. Subramania Aiyar renounced his knighthood; released **September 1917**.
[3:2] - Montagu: "Shiva … cut his wife into fifty-two pieces …" – on the folly of interning Besant.
[3:3] - **Montagu (August) Declaration, 20 August 1917:** by **Edwin Montagu**, Secretary of State (not Morley, Minto or Chelmsford) – "**progressive realisation of responsible government**" through self-governing institutions.
[3:4] # Why it faded (1918-19)
[3:5] - Moderates pacified by the reform promise; Besant's vacillation; the **Montford reforms (July 1918)** split the ranks; Tilak away in England; Gandhi's rise.
[3:6] # Why it was a new phase
[3:7] - It put the demand for **self-government (Home Rule)** before the nation as a clear programme; shifted the emphasis from the elite to the **masses**; linked town and country; created a generation of nationalists; prepared the ground for Gandhian politics.
[3:8] - Not because Gandhi led it or because of Hindu-Muslim struggle (that is the Lucknow Pact / Khilafat).

[3:11] # World-war context
[3:12] - **First World War (1914-18):** the **Treaty of Versailles (1919)** humiliated **Germany**.
[3:13] - **Second World War (1939-45):** the **deadliest conflict** in history by total casualties.
