[207:1:0] # Basic facts

  [207:1:1.0] Founded | 21 April 1913, USA, by Indians living in the US and Canada; first meeting in Oregon
  [207:1:1.1] Grew out of | Pacific Coast Hindustan Association, San Francisco
  [207:1:1.2] Headquarters | Yugantar Ashram, San Francisco
  [207:1:1.3] First president (chairman) | Sohan Singh Bhakna (called Sarabha his "General")
  [207:1:1.4] Moving spirit / secretary | Lala Har Dayal
  [207:1:1.5] Other founders | Kartar Singh Sarabha, Bhagwan Singh, Baba Harnam Singh (Tundilat), Pt. Kanshi Ram

  [207:1:1.7] Meaning | "Ghadar" = mutiny / rebellion

[207:1:4] - **NOT associated:** **Khudiram Bose**, **Barindra Kumar Ghosh**, **Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee** (UPSC 2022 trap).
[207:1:5] # The wartime uprising (1915)
[207:1:6] - Cause of the Ghadar revolution: outbreak of the **First World War (1914)**.
[207:1:7] - Pan-India revolt in the British Indian Army planned for **21 February 1915** in **Punjab**; **betrayed by informers** and crushed.
[207:1:8] - **45 leaders hanged** under the **Defence of India Act**.
[207:1:9] - **First Lahore Conspiracy Case:** **Kartar Singh Sarabha** executed **16 November 1915**, aged **19**, with **Vishnu Ganesh Pingle**; Sarabha was born **24 May 1896**, Sarabha (Ludhiana).

[208:8:8] # Importance

[208:8:10] - Shifted the movement from elite to masses; linked town and country → prepared ground for Gandhi.
[208:8:11] - **Moderates-Extremists unity:** the Surat rift closed at Lucknow (1916), and Besant's internment (1917) pushed even Moderates into the agitation.
[208:8:12] - **Congress-League unity:** 1916 was the high point; Congress accepted at Lucknow the separate electorates it had opposed under the **1909 Act**.
[208:8:13] - **Spread:** Tilak's League covered Maharashtra, Karnataka, Central Provinces and Berar; Besant's about 200 branches covered the rest of India; Jinnah carried it to UP; in Andhra it used Telugu pamphlets and libraries as lecture venues.
[208:8:14] - **Limit:** the August Declaration blunted and effectively ended the agitation; the Mont-Ford proposals split the Congress at the Bombay special session (Aug 1918).
