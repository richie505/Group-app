=== The Two Home Rule Leagues (1916)
# Why and when
- **Home Rule Movement** = the popular movement of India **during the First World War** (not Swadeshi, Separatist or Swaraj Party); on the lines of the **Irish Home Rule Leagues**; aim **self-government within the British Empire**.
- Causes: need for popular pressure, disillusion with the **Morley-Minto reforms**, wartime miseries; **Tilak released from Mandalay (June 1914)** after six years.
- **Two leagues, 1916**, led by **Tilak and Annie Besant** (not Aurobindo, Lajpat Rai or B.C. Pal); they demarcated areas and cooperated.
# Comparison
| Feature | Tilak's **Indian Home Rule League** | Besant's **All India Home Rule League** |
|---|---|---|
| Founded | **April 1916** (28 April) – the **first** league | **September 1916** |
| Place | Launched at the Bombay Provincial Conference, **Belgaum**; headquarters **Poona** | **Madras** (HQ **Adyar**) |
| Area | Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Karnataka, **Central Provinces**, Berar | Rest of India, including **Bombay city** |
| Branches | 6 | about **200** |
| Office-bearers | President **Joseph Baptista**; Secretary **N.C. Kelkar** | Organising secretary **George Arundale**; C.P. Ramaswamy Aiyar, B.P. Wadia |
- **Associated leaders:** **S. Subramania Iyer** (co-leader with Besant), G.S. Khaparde, Joseph Baptista, **Jinnah** (Bombay; also popularised it in UP), Motilal and Jawaharlal Nehru, Madan Mohan Malaviya, Tej Bahadur Sapru, Lajpat Rai.
- **H.S. Olcott** – co-founder of the **Theosophical Society** (died 1907), **not** a Home Rule leader.
- **C.R. Das** – the usual answer for "not associated" (he only joined the agitation later; Spectrum p.329 lists him among those who joined).
- **1915 Bombay Congress:** Besant **failed** to get the Congress to approve her Home Rule League scheme – no Home Rule resolution.
# Fate
- Tilak's League later merged with Besant's; **1920:** Gandhi became President of the **All India Home Rule League** and renamed it **Swarajya Sabha**; it soon merged into the Congress.
- **Year of merger of the two leagues = 1920** (not 1916, 1918 or 1923).
> Statement trap (APPSC FRO): "Tilak founded the Indian Home Rule League in **1914** and was its **president**" ✗ (April 1916; president Joseph Baptista); "Tilak played a key role in the **Lucknow Pact (1916)** with Jinnah" ✓.

=== Annie Besant – Theosophist, Papers and Congress President
# Life and institutions
- Irish theosophist; came to India in **1893**; President of the **Theosophical Society** from **1907** (HQ Adyar); called the "**Iron Lady of Ireland**".
- **Central Hindu College, Varanasi (1898)** – became the core of **Banaras Hindu University**, whose founder is **Madan Mohan Malaviya** (1916) – not Besant or Asutosh Mukherjee.
- **National College, Madanapalle (1916)** – first principal **J.H. Cousins**; protégé **Jiddu Krishnamurti** (*At the Feet of the Master*).
# Her papers
| Paper | Fact |
|---|---|
| **The Commonweal** | **English weekly**, **Madras**, **January 1914** – editor **Annie Besant** |
| **New India** | She bought the *Madras Standard* (**July 1914**) and renamed it **New India** (a daily) |
- **Both papers = Besant** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025); "New Hindu" and "The Aryans" are distractors; New India ≠ Gandhi.
# Congress President and later work
| President | Session | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Annie Besant** | **Calcutta, 1917** | **First woman President of the INC** |
| **Sarojini Naidu** | **Kanpur, 1925** | **First Indian woman President** |
| Nellie Sengupta | Calcutta, 1933 | After president-elect Malaviya was arrested |
- Sucheta Kripalani, Aruna Asaf Ali, Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit, Kasturba – never INC presidents.
- **1918:** Besant visited **Bihar (Patna)** to strengthen the Home Rule League.
- Quote: "**I am an Indian Tom-Tom**, whose work is waking up all the sleepers…" – **Annie Besant**.
- **Chief architect** of the **Moderate–Extremist reunion** at Lucknow (1916), with Tilak.
# Commonwealth of India Bill (1925)
- Drafted by the **National Convention** (Sapru); amended by an All Parties Conference sub-committee chaired by **Besant**; introduced in the House of Commons by **George Lansbury** (Labour), **December 1925**; defeated.
- Proposed **dominion status** ("equal footing with the self-governing dominions") and a **parliamentary system**; its Article 7 listed **seven fundamental rights**.
- Called the "Dominion of India Bill" in the APPSC key – not the Nehru Report (1928) or the Sapru Report.

=== Tilak, Lucknow 1916 and the Lucknow Pact
# Tilak
- **"Father of Indian Unrest"** – **Valentine Chirol** (*Indian Unrest*); Tilak went to England (Sept 1918) to fight a libel case against him.
- **"Lokmanya"** ("beloved / respected by the people") – exam keys tie it to the **Home Rule Movement**; Spectrum p.593 says he got it after his **1897 sedition trial**.
- "**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it**" – Tilak; papers **Kesari (Marathi)** and **Mahratta (English)**.
# Lucknow session, December 1916
- President **A.C. Majumdar**; the **Surat split (1907)** healed – Extremists readmitted; helped by Tilak's and Besant's efforts and the deaths of Gokhale and Pherozeshah Mehta (1915).
- The session where the **Home Rule supporters demonstrated their political power** (the Home Rule Leagues held a joint meeting of 1,000+ delegates).
- **Lucknow Pact (Congress–League):** negotiated by **Tilak and Jinnah**; Congress accepted **separate electorates**; "**Better two united fists…**" – Tilak.
- Tilak proposed at Lucknow a small **Working Committee** to turn the Congress into a body able to lead a sustained movement (created only in 1920).

=== Impact, Decline and the August Declaration
# Repression and the British answer
- **June 1917:** Besant, **B.P. Wadia** and **George Arundale** interned (Ootacamund); S. Subramania Aiyar renounced his knighthood; released **September 1917**.
- Montagu: "Shiva … cut his wife into fifty-two pieces …" – on the folly of interning Besant.
- **Montagu (August) Declaration, 20 August 1917:** by **Edwin Montagu**, Secretary of State (not Morley, Minto or Chelmsford) – "**progressive realisation of responsible government**" through self-governing institutions.
# Why it faded (1918-19)
- Moderates pacified by the reform promise; Besant's vacillation; the **Montford reforms (July 1918)** split the ranks; Tilak away in England; Gandhi's rise.
# Why it was a new phase
- It put the demand for **self-government (Home Rule)** before the nation as a clear programme; shifted the emphasis from the elite to the **masses**; linked town and country; created a generation of nationalists; prepared the ground for Gandhian politics.
- Not because Gandhi led it or because of Hindu-Muslim struggle (that is the Lucknow Pact / Khilafat).

- **First World War (1914-18):** the **Treaty of Versailles (1919)** humiliated **Germany**.
- **Second World War (1939-45):** the **deadliest conflict** in history by total casualties.
