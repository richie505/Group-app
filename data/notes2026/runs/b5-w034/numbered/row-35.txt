## SUB 1 | Stages, Configurations and Features – PSLV, GSLV, LVM3, SSLV
[1:0] # Configurations
[1:1] TABLE head: Vehicle | Full form | Stages
  [1:1.0] SLV-3 | Satellite Launch Vehicle-3 | 4, all-solid; 17 t, 22 m; 40 kg to LEO
  [1:1.1] ASLV | Augmented SLV | 5, all-solid; 40 t, 24 m; 150 kg to LEO
  [1:1.2] PSLV | Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle | 4: solid–liquid–solid–liquid (S139, Vikas, solid, twin-engine PS4); no cryogenic stage
  [1:1.3] GSLV Mk II | Geosynchronous SLV | 3: one solid + one earth-storable liquid (Vikas) + one cryogenic (CE-7.5) + 4 liquid strap-ons
  [1:1.4] LVM3 (ex-GSLV Mk III, "Bahubali") | Launch Vehicle Mark-3 | 3: 2 S200 solid strap-ons + L110 liquid core + C25 cryogenic (CE-20)
  [1:1.5] SSLV | Small SLV | 3 solid stages + liquid Velocity Trimming Module (VTM)
[1:2] - **PSLV:** **"workhorse" of ISRO**; third-generation vehicle; **first Indian vehicle with liquid stages**; ~1,750 kg to SSO; built to launch **IRS (earth-resources)** satellites into sun-synchronous polar orbit.
[1:3] - **PSLV variants:** CA (Core Alone) 0 strap-ons, DL 2, **QL 4**, XL 6 (extended).
[1:4] - **GSLV:** fourth-generation vehicle; first flight GSLV-D1 with **GSAT-1** (18 Apr 2001); 2–2.5 t communication / navigation satellites to **GTO**; launched **INSAT-3D/3DR, NVS** satellites.
[1:5] - **LVM3:** a **GSLV-class** vehicle (not built by DRDO); ~4 t to GTO, ~8 t to LEO; it launched **Chandrayaan-2 and -3**, **not** the Mars Orbiter Mission (PSLV).
[1:6] - **LVM3 – heaviest Indian rocket:** ~**640 t** lift-off mass, 43 m; first orbital flight **GSLV Mk III-D1** (5 Jun 2017, GSAT-19), called the ISRO "**game changer**" and India's "heavy rocket" of 2017; it ended dependence on foreign launchers for **4-tonne** satellites.
[1:7] - **LVM3 history:** **LVM3-X / CARE** suborbital test **18 Dec 2014**; operational since 2017.
[1:8] - **Not operational now:** **SLV-3 and ASLV** (retired); PSLV, GSLV, LVM3, SSLV and sounding rockets are in use.
[1:9] # SSLV
[1:10] - **Design:** **500 kg to 500 km LEO** (10–500 kg class; ~300 kg to SSO); **three solid stages** + VTM, ~**120 t** lift-off, **34 m** – shorter than PSLV and GSLV; low cost, **launch-on-demand**, minimal infrastructure; can be assembled **vertically and horizontally**.
[1:11] - **Flights:** **SSLV-D1** (Aug 2022) failed to place EOS-02 in orbit; **SSLV-D2 (10 Feb 2023)** – first successful SSLV launch; D3 (Aug 2024).
[1:12] - **HAL** won the **Rs 511 crore** Transfer of Technology to build SSLV – first full ToT of an ISRO launch vehicle.
[1:13] - **Second spaceport:** **Kulasekarapattinam** (Tamil Nadu) for SSLV, avoiding the dog-leg round Sri Lanka.
[1:14] > ⚠ Trap: "SSLV has two solid stages" is WRONG – it has three (APPSC G1); "GSLV Mk III is four-staged S-L-S-L" is WRONG – S-L-S-L is PSLV (UPSC 2018).

## SUB 3 | Sounding Rockets and TERLS Thumba
[3:0] - **TERLS** (Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station), near **Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala** – **India's first rocket launching station**; chosen because the **geomagnetic (magnetic) equator** passes through it.
[3:1] - **First launch:** American **Nike-Apache**, **21 Nov 1963** – start of the Indian space programme; early imported rockets **M-100** (Russia) and **Centaure** (France).
[3:2] - **Rohini Sounding Rockets (RSR):** ISRO's own series (programme consolidated 1975); used for **atmospheric research** and to qualify sub-systems of launch vehicles; sub-orbital, ~70–600 km.
[3:3] TABLE head: Rocket | Fact
  [3:3.0] RH-200 | Two-stage, ~70–80 km, meteorology; 200th consecutive successful launch from Thumba, 23 Nov 2022
  [3:3.1] RH-300 Mk-II | Single-stage, ~116–160 km
  [3:3.2] RH-560 Mk-III | Two-stage, ~470–550 km, ionosphere
[3:4] - **Number = diameter in mm**; SLV, ASLV and PSLV are orbital launch vehicles, not sounding rockets.
[3:5] > Thumba = magnetic equator (sounding rockets); Sriharikota = east coast (orbital launches).
[3:6] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 1 p.98; PMF S&T Part 2 p.9-10; NextGen S&T G2 Part 2 p.6; app notes 5:35 sub 0
## SUB 4 | Orbits and Launch Physics
[4:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:0.0] LEO | Low Earth Orbit (up to ~2,000 km)
  [4:0.1] SSO | Sun-synchronous, near-polar; passes a place at the same local solar time – PSLV's speciality
  [4:0.2] GTO | Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit: elliptical, apogee near 36,000 km – GSLV, LVM3
  [4:0.3] GEO | Geostationary orbit over the equator; satellite appears fixed in the sky (reached via GSLV, not PSLV's polar orbits)
  [4:0.4] Apogee / perigee | Farthest / nearest point of an orbit
  [4:0.5] Strap-on booster | Extra motors fixed to the core for lift-off thrust
[4:1] - **Eastward launch:** satellites are launched **eastward** to use the Earth's **west-to-east rotation** (a free speed boost); sites **nearer the equator** gain most. The boost does **not** give "escape velocity" – an orbiting satellite never reaches escape velocity.
[4:2] - **Not always east:** polar / SSO launches go **southward** (from Sriharikota a **dog-leg around Sri Lanka**).
[4:3] - **Sriharikota** (SDSC-SHAR, Andhra Pradesh) on the **east coast**: rockets fly over the sea; all Indian orbital launches, incl. private ones, go from here.
[4:4] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 2 p.3; PMF S&T Part 1 p.64-66; app notes 5:35 sub 4
