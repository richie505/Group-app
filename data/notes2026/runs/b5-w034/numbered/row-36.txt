## SUB 0 | Cryogenic Engines and Cryogenics
[0:0] # What a cryogenic engine is
[0:1] - **Cryogenics:** Greek **'Kryos'** (frost) + 'genic' (to produce); temperatures below about **-150°C**; liquid nitrogen, hydrogen and oxygen are the usual cryogenic liquids – the technology of obtaining/using liquid O2 or H2 at industrial level is **cryogenics**.
[0:2] - **Propellants:** **liquid hydrogen (LH2, -253°C) as fuel** and **liquid oxygen (LOX, -183°C) as oxidiser** – not chlorine, fluorine, sulphur, helium or nitrogen; exhaust is only **water vapour**; specific impulse ~**450 s** (highest).
[0:3] - **Use:** **rocket technology** – the upper stages of launch vehicles and the **space shuttle**; **not** submarine propulsion, frost-free refrigerators, Boeing aircraft or rail engines.
[0:4] - **Why not missiles:** LH2 density only **0.071 g/ml** – huge tanks, cannot be stored fuelled for long.
[0:5] - **Other cryogenic use:** **liquid nitrogen (-196°C)** stores **bull semen** for artificial insemination.
[0:6] # India's cryogenic story
[0:7] - **Glavkosmos deal (1991)** → US **MTCR** sanctions (**1992**) stopped technology transfer → Russia supplied **7 engines** (KVD-1).
[0:8] - **CUSP** (Cryogenic Upper Stage Project) began **1994**; full stage test **2006**; **GSLV-D5** flew the indigenous **CE-7.5** on **5 Jan 2014**; INSAT-3DR (Sep 2016) went up on GSLV-F05 with the **indigenous cryogenic** stage.
[0:9] - **India = 6th** country with cryogenic technology (after **US, Russia, France, Japan, China**) – not "the only one after USA, Russia and China".

## SUB 1 | Rocket Principle and Propellants
[1:0] - **Principle:** a rocket works on **Newton's third law** (action–reaction; conservation of momentum) – it needs no air to push against.
[1:1] - **Jet engine vs rocket:** a jet engine takes **oxygen from the surrounding air**, so it cannot work in space; a rocket carries its **own fuel and oxidiser** – as a **liquid (LOX) or solid oxidiser, not as gas**.
[1:2] - **Propellant** = fuel + oxidiser; liquid hydrogen, liquid oxygen, hydrazine and kerosene (RP-1) are all used in rockets.
[1:3] TABLE head: Category | Example | Feature
  [1:3.0] Solid | HTPB (S139, S200) | High initial thrust; cannot be throttled or stopped
  [1:3.1] Earth-storable liquid | UH25 + N2O4; hydrazine, MMH, UDMH | Throttleable, restartable
  [1:3.2] Hypergolic | Hydrazine family + N2O4 | Ignite on contact; spacecraft manoeuvring; highly toxic
  [1:3.3] Cryogenic | LH2 + LOX | Most efficient
  [1:3.4] Semi-cryogenic | Refined kerosene + LOX | Dense, cheaper, non-toxic
  [1:3.5] Hybrid | Solid fuel + liquid oxidiser | Can be stopped/restarted
[1:4] - **Ammonium perchlorate:** the **oxidiser** in solid rocket propellant, with **aluminium** powder (fuel) and HTPB binder.

## SUB 2 | Ramjet, Scramjet and Reusable Launch Vehicles
[2:0] TABLE head: Engine | Combustion airflow | Speed
  [2:0.0] Ramjet | Subsonic | Above Mach 2
  [2:0.1] Scramjet (Supersonic Combustion Ramjet) | Supersonic | Mach 5–6+
[2:1] - **Scramjet fuel:** ISRO's scramjet burns **hydrogen** with **oxygen from the atmosphere** – less oxidiser carried; needs a rocket booster to reach ignition speed.
[2:2] - **ATV** (ISRO): scramjet flight test **Aug 2016**, Sriharikota; India **4th** (after US, Russia, China). **HSTDV** = **DRDO**; actively cooled combustor ran **20 minutes** on endothermic fuel (May 2026).
[2:3] - **RLV-TD "Pushpak":** winged, reusable; HEX 2016, landing tests LEX 2023–24; aim **TSTO**; cost cut up to **80%**; needs a reusable **Thermal Protection System**.
