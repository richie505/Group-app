## SUB 1 | Sriharikota (SDSC-SHAR)
[34:1:0] # Location and history
[34:1:1] - **District:** **Nellore** when the APPSC 2019 papers were set; **Tirupati** district now (April 2022 reorganisation).
[34:1:2] - Barrier island between **Pulicat Lake (west)** and the **Bay of Bengal (east)**; it divides the lake from the sea (not Chilika, Mahanadi or Godavari mouth).
[34:1:3] - Set up **1971** as **Sriharikota High Altitude Range (SHAR)**; renamed **2002** after **Satish Dhawan**.
[34:1:4] - Why chosen: **east coast** (eastward launch gains from Earth's rotation), **near the equator**, **sea** for spent stages, **thinly populated**.
[34:1:5] # Facilities and launches
[34:1:6] TABLE head: Pad | Vehicles
  [34:1:6.0] First Launch Pad | PSLV, SSLV
  [34:1:6.1] Second Launch Pad | GSLV, LVM3; standby for PSLV
  [34:1:6.2] Third Launch Pad | For NGLV; approved 16 Jan 2025, Rs 3,984.86 crore, 48 months
[34:1:7] - **SPROB** (Solid Propellant Space Booster Plant): casts **S200** boosters of LVM3.
[34:1:8] - **Launch View Gallery:** opened **31 March 2019** (PSLV-C45); first phase seats **5,000** visitors.
[34:1:9] TABLE head: Launch | Mission | Date
  [34:1:9.0] ISRO's 100th space mission | PSLV-C21, from Sriharikota | Sept 2012
  [34:1:9.1] 100th launch from SHAR | GSLV-F15/NVS-02 | 29 Jan 2025
[34:1:10] - **February 2004:** blast at the solid propellant plant at SHAR, **Sriharikota**.
[34:1:11] # Second spaceport – Kulasekarapattinam
[34:1:12] - **SSLV Launch Complex**, **Thoothukudi** district, **Tamil Nadu** (approved 2023); **direct southward launch** to polar/sun-synchronous orbit, without the fuel-costly dog-leg around **Sri Lanka**.

[34:2:0] - **IIRS** (Indian Institute of Remote Sensing), **Dehradun** (not New Delhi, Lucknow, Nagpur); constituent unit of **ISRO, Department of Space**.

## SUB 1 | Stages, Configurations and Features – PSLV, GSLV, LVM3, SSLV
[35:1:0] # Configurations
[35:1:1] TABLE head: Vehicle | Full form | Stages
  [35:1:1.0] SLV-3 | Satellite Launch Vehicle-3 | 4, all-solid; 17 t, 22 m; 40 kg to LEO
  [35:1:1.1] ASLV | Augmented SLV | 5, all-solid; 40 t, 24 m; 150 kg to LEO
  [35:1:1.2] PSLV | Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle | 4: solid–liquid–solid–liquid (S139, Vikas, solid, twin-engine PS4); no cryogenic stage
  [35:1:1.3] GSLV Mk II | Geosynchronous SLV | 3: one solid + one earth-storable liquid (Vikas) + one cryogenic (CE-7.5) + 4 liquid strap-ons
  [35:1:1.4] LVM3 (ex-GSLV Mk III, "Bahubali") | Launch Vehicle Mark-3 | 3: 2 S200 solid strap-ons + L110 liquid core + C25 cryogenic (CE-20)
  [35:1:1.5] SSLV | Small SLV | 3 solid stages + liquid Velocity Trimming Module (VTM)
[35:1:2] - **PSLV:** **"workhorse" of ISRO**; third-generation vehicle; **first Indian vehicle with liquid stages**; ~1,750 kg to SSO; built to launch **IRS (earth-resources)** satellites into sun-synchronous polar orbit.
[35:1:3] - **PSLV variants:** CA (Core Alone) 0 strap-ons, DL 2, **QL 4**, XL 6 (extended).
[35:1:4] - **GSLV:** fourth-generation vehicle; first flight GSLV-D1 with **GSAT-1** (18 Apr 2001); 2–2.5 t communication / navigation satellites to **GTO**; launched **INSAT-3D/3DR, NVS** satellites.
[35:1:5] - **LVM3:** a **GSLV-class** vehicle (not built by DRDO); ~4 t to GTO, ~8 t to LEO; it launched **Chandrayaan-2 and -3**, **not** the Mars Orbiter Mission (PSLV).
[35:1:6] - **LVM3 – heaviest Indian rocket:** ~**640 t** lift-off mass, 43 m; first orbital flight **GSLV Mk III-D1** (5 Jun 2017, GSAT-19), called the ISRO "**game changer**" and India's "heavy rocket" of 2017; it ended dependence on foreign launchers for **4-tonne** satellites.
[35:1:7] - **LVM3 history:** **LVM3-X / CARE** suborbital test **18 Dec 2014**; operational since 2017.
[35:1:8] - **Not operational now:** **SLV-3 and ASLV** (retired); PSLV, GSLV, LVM3, SSLV and sounding rockets are in use.
[35:1:9] # SSLV
[35:1:10] - **Design:** **500 kg to 500 km LEO** (10–500 kg class; ~300 kg to SSO); **three solid stages** + VTM, ~**120 t** lift-off, **34 m** – shorter than PSLV and GSLV; low cost, **launch-on-demand**, minimal infrastructure; can be assembled **vertically and horizontally**.
[35:1:11] - **Flights:** **SSLV-D1** (Aug 2022) failed to place EOS-02 in orbit; **SSLV-D2 (10 Feb 2023)** – first successful SSLV launch; D3 (Aug 2024).
[35:1:12] - **HAL** won the **Rs 511 crore** Transfer of Technology to build SSLV – first full ToT of an ISRO launch vehicle.
[35:1:13] - **Second spaceport:** **Kulasekarapattinam** (Tamil Nadu) for SSLV, avoiding the dog-leg round Sri Lanka.
[35:1:14] > ⚠ Trap: "SSLV has two solid stages" is WRONG – it has three (APPSC G1); "GSLV Mk III is four-staged S-L-S-L" is WRONG – S-L-S-L is PSLV (UPSC 2018).

## SUB 5 | Orbits and Launch Physics
[35:5:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [35:5:0.0] LEO | Low Earth Orbit (up to ~2,000 km)
  [35:5:0.1] SSO | Sun-synchronous, near-polar; passes a place at the same local solar time – PSLV's speciality
  [35:5:0.2] GTO | Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit: elliptical, apogee near 36,000 km – GSLV, LVM3
  [35:5:0.3] GEO | Geostationary orbit over the equator; satellite appears fixed in the sky (reached via GSLV, not PSLV's polar orbits)
  [35:5:0.4] Apogee / perigee | Farthest / nearest point of an orbit
  [35:5:0.5] Strap-on booster | Extra motors fixed to the core for lift-off thrust
[35:5:1] - **Eastward launch:** satellites are launched **eastward** to use the Earth's **west-to-east rotation** (a free speed boost); sites **nearer the equator** gain most. The boost does **not** give "escape velocity" – an orbiting satellite never reaches escape velocity.
[35:5:2] - **Not always east:** polar / SSO launches go **southward** (from Sriharikota a **dog-leg around Sri Lanka**).
[35:5:3] - **Sriharikota** (SDSC-SHAR, Andhra Pradesh) on the **east coast**: rockets fly over the sea; all Indian orbital launches, incl. private ones, go from here.
[35:5:4] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 2 p.3; PMF S&T Part 1 p.64-66; app notes 5:35 sub 4

## SUB 0 | Cryogenic Engines and Cryogenics
[36:0:0] # What a cryogenic engine is
[36:0:1] - **Cryogenics:** Greek **'Kryos'** (frost) + 'genic' (to produce); temperatures below about **-150°C**; liquid nitrogen, hydrogen and oxygen are the usual cryogenic liquids – the technology of obtaining/using liquid O2 or H2 at industrial level is **cryogenics**.
[36:0:2] - **Propellants:** **liquid hydrogen (LH2, -253°C) as fuel** and **liquid oxygen (LOX, -183°C) as oxidiser** – not chlorine, fluorine, sulphur, helium or nitrogen; exhaust is only **water vapour**; specific impulse ~**450 s** (highest).
[36:0:3] - **Use:** **rocket technology** – the upper stages of launch vehicles and the **space shuttle**; **not** submarine propulsion, frost-free refrigerators, Boeing aircraft or rail engines.
[36:0:4] - **Why not missiles:** LH2 density only **0.071 g/ml** – huge tanks, cannot be stored fuelled for long.
[36:0:5] - **Other cryogenic use:** **liquid nitrogen (-196°C)** stores **bull semen** for artificial insemination.
[36:0:6] # India's cryogenic story
[36:0:7] - **Glavkosmos deal (1991)** → US **MTCR** sanctions (**1992**) stopped technology transfer → Russia supplied **7 engines** (KVD-1).
[36:0:8] - **CUSP** (Cryogenic Upper Stage Project) began **1994**; full stage test **2006**; **GSLV-D5** flew the indigenous **CE-7.5** on **5 Jan 2014**; INSAT-3DR (Sep 2016) went up on GSLV-F05 with the **indigenous cryogenic** stage.
[36:0:9] - **India = 6th** country with cryogenic technology (after **US, Russia, France, Japan, China**) – not "the only one after USA, Russia and China".

## SUB 1 | Rocket Principle and Propellants
[36:1:0] - **Principle:** a rocket works on **Newton's third law** (action–reaction; conservation of momentum) – it needs no air to push against.
[36:1:1] - **Jet engine vs rocket:** a jet engine takes **oxygen from the surrounding air**, so it cannot work in space; a rocket carries its **own fuel and oxidiser** – as a **liquid (LOX) or solid oxidiser, not as gas**.
[36:1:2] - **Propellant** = fuel + oxidiser; liquid hydrogen, liquid oxygen, hydrazine and kerosene (RP-1) are all used in rockets.
[36:1:3] TABLE head: Category | Example | Feature
  [36:1:3.0] Solid | HTPB (S139, S200) | High initial thrust; cannot be throttled or stopped
  [36:1:3.1] Earth-storable liquid | UH25 + N2O4; hydrazine, MMH, UDMH | Throttleable, restartable
  [36:1:3.2] Hypergolic | Hydrazine family + N2O4 | Ignite on contact; spacecraft manoeuvring; highly toxic
  [36:1:3.3] Cryogenic | LH2 + LOX | Most efficient
  [36:1:3.4] Semi-cryogenic | Refined kerosene + LOX | Dense, cheaper, non-toxic
  [36:1:3.5] Hybrid | Solid fuel + liquid oxidiser | Can be stopped/restarted
[36:1:4] - **Ammonium perchlorate:** the **oxidiser** in solid rocket propellant, with **aluminium** powder (fuel) and HTPB binder.

## SUB 2 | Geostationary Orbit – Physics of a Communication Satellite
[37:2:0] TABLE head: Orbit | Fact
  [37:2:0.0] Low Earth | 160–2,000 km; ISS ~400 km
  [37:2:0.1] Geostationary (GEO) | About 36,000 km (35,786 km) above the equator = orbit radius ~42,000 km (42,164 km from Earth's centre); period 24 hours (1 day)
  [37:2:0.2] Polar | Passes over the poles
  [37:2:0.3] Elliptical | Apogee and perigee differ
  [37:2:0.4] Circular | Centripetal force equals gravitational force
[37:2:1] - **Geostationary:** revolves **in the same direction as the Earth rotates** (west to east) with the same period, so it appears fixed over one point; it stays at a **fixed height** that does **not depend on its mass**.
[37:2:2] - **What keeps it in orbit:** the **centripetal force**, supplied by the **Earth's gravitational pull on the satellite** – not a rocket engine, not the Sun.
[37:2:3] - **No energy needed** to keep orbiting: in near-vacuum there is no drag; solar panels only power the instruments.
[37:2:4] - **Global TV:** **geostationary satellites** relay TV across the world (three cover nearly the whole globe); ground and space waves are short-range.
[37:2:5] - **Uplink / downlink:** sending data **up to the satellite / network** = **uplink**; receiving from it = downlink.
[37:2:6] - Launcher → elliptical **GTO** → satellite's own motor raises it to GEO.

=== NOTES 5:38 | Navigation Satellites – Irnss/navic and Gagan
## SUB 0 | NavIC (IRNSS) – Constellation, Coverage and Services
[38:0:0] # Names and genesis
[38:0:1] - **IRNSS = Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System**; operational name **NavIC = Navigation with Indian Constellation**, given by PM **Narendra Modi** (2016).
[38:0:2] - Genesis: the US denied GPS data to India in the **1999 Kargil War** (**Operation Vijay**).
[38:0:3] - NavIC is **independent** of GPS: it keeps its own **Indian time** (IRNSS Network Time, from on-board atomic clocks), not GPS time.
[38:0:4] - Providing PNT since **2018**; India is the **only developing country** with its own independent regional system.
[38:0:5] # Constellation and coverage
[38:0:6] - **7 satellites: 3 GEO + 4 inclined GSO** (at **29°**), about 36,000 km up – not 4 GEO + 3 GSO, not 12.
[38:0:7] - Covers India and **about 1,500 km** beyond its borders (not "5,500 sq km"); **regional, not global** (UPSC 2018: only statement 1 correct).
[38:0:8] - Signals in **L5 and S bands**; the new **NVS** satellites add a civilian **L1** signal.
[38:0:9] TABLE head: Service | Users | Accuracy
  [38:0:9.0] Standard Positioning Service (SPS) | All civilians (open) | Better than 20 m (about 5 m in practice)
  [38:0:9.1] Restricted Service (RS) | Military / authorised | Encrypted
[38:0:10] - Nodal body for NavIC and GAGAN: **Satellite Navigation Programme Office**, ISRO.
[38:0:11] > Exam angle: IRNSS ≠ GAGAN – a statement "IRNSS is also known as GAGAN" is false.
[38:0:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 2 p.6; PMF S&T Part 1 p.113-118; testbook TSPSC HWO 2018 key (Indian clock)
## SUB 1 | Satellite Navigation Systems of the World – Owners and Full Forms
[38:1:0] # Four global, two regional systems
[38:1:1] TABLE head: System | Owner | Scope
  [38:1:1.0] GPS – Global Positioning System | USA (Dept of Defense) | Global
  [38:1:1.1] GLONASS | Russia (not the UK) | Global
  [38:1:1.2] Galileo | European Union (not the USA) – live 2016, under civilian control, works with GPS and GLONASS | Global
  [38:1:1.3] BeiDou | China | Global
  [38:1:1.4] NavIC (formerly IRNSS) | India | Regional
  [38:1:1.5] QZSS (Michibiki) | Japan | Regional
[38:1:2] - **Which country has its own system?** Among Australia, Canada, Israel, Japan only **Japan** (QZSS) does (UPSC 2023).
[38:1:3] - **Galileo Project** (UPSC 2007): Europe's **multi-satellite navigation** project, not a missile shield.
[38:1:4] # GPS basics
[38:1:5] - **GPS** programme begun **1973**, first satellite 1978, fully operational 1995.
[38:1:6] - Original design: **24 satellites in 6 orbits** at about **20,200 km** (MEO); each circles Earth **twice a day**; about 30 in use (2026).
[38:1:7] - Signals in **L1** and **L2** bands; a **1 microsecond** clock error ≈ **300 m** position error.
[38:1:8] - A receiver needs **4 satellites**: 3 for position, 1 to correct its own clock error (trilateration).
[38:1:9] > Exam angle: "wrong pair" items swap owners – GLONASS–UK and Galileo–USA are the traps (APPSC 2018, 2019).

## SUB 2 | Uses of GPS and NavIC; Space Weather
[38:2:0] # Applications
[38:2:1] - **GPS / satellite timing** is used in **mobile phone networks, banking and power grids** – all three (UPSC 2018).
[38:2:2] - **Tectonic plate movement** is monitored with **GPS** (with VLBI and SLR) – not GIS or weather radar.
[38:2:3] - NavIC: terrestrial, aerial, marine navigation; **disaster management**; vehicle tracking and **fleet management**; precise timing (IST, power, telecom, banking); mapping and geodesy; **precision farming**; hikers and drivers.
[38:2:4] - **MoRTH AIS-140:** NavIC/GPS trackers and panic buttons compulsory in **public transport and commercial vehicles** (not all private cars).
[38:2:5] - **Qualcomm Snapdragon** chipsets support NavIC; reconfigured **IRNSS-1A** and GEMINI devices send cyclone, tsunami and high-wave alerts to **fishermen**.
[38:2:6] - **Elena Geo Systems:** India's **first NavIC-based device**, for precise directions on infrastructure projects (APPSC 2023).
[38:2:7] - **Himachal Pradesh** made a **GPS device mandatory for trekkers** (June 2019).
[38:2:8] - **APSDMA** uses **Satellite Based Mobile Data Voice (SBMDV)** terminals for real-time satellite communication (IRNSS, NavIC, SBAS are navigation, not communication).
[38:2:9] - Strategic autonomy: forces need not depend on foreign systems in a conflict.
[38:2:10] # Solar storms
[38:2:11] - A major **solar storm** can make **GPS fail**, damage **power grids**, cause **auroras** far from the poles, disturb **satellite orbits** and cut **shortwave radio** on polar flights – not tsunamis or forest fires (UPSC 2022).

## SUB 3 | GAGAN and Other Satellite-Based Augmentation Systems
[38:3:0] - **GAGAN = GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation**: India's **SBAS**, built jointly by **ISRO + Airports Authority of India** (not DRDO); operational since **2015** (DGCA-certified).
[38:3:1] - Not an independent system: ground reference stations compute live corrections for orbit, clock and **ionospheric delay**, sent via **GEO** satellites → more **accuracy and integrity**.
[38:3:2] - Payloads on **GSAT-8, GSAT-10, GSAT-15**; covers the **Indian Flight Information Region**; precision-like approach (APV-I) without ILS.
[38:3:3] - Benefits go **beyond aviation** – maritime (GEMINI), railways, roads, survey, disaster response – so "only aviation" is false (UPSC 2025: I and II only).
[38:3:4] - GAGAN is one of the systems ISRO uses for **satellite navigation services** (INSAT, IRS, AstroSat are not).
[38:3:5] TABLE head: SBAS | Region
  [38:3:5.0] WAAS | USA
  [38:3:5.1] EGNOS | Europe
  [38:3:5.2] MSAS | Japan
  [38:3:5.3] GAGAN | India
[38:3:6] > Exam angle: GAGAN augments GPS for aviation; NavIC is an independent regional system.

## SUB 4 | IRNSS and NVS Satellites – Launches and Atomic Clocks
[38:4:0] # Launches
[38:4:1] - **IRNSS-1A**, India's first dedicated navigation satellite: **PSLV-C22**, **1 July 2013**, **Sriharikota**; the whole IRNSS-1 series flew on **PSLV**.
[38:4:2] - **11** navigation satellites launched since 2013, all from Sriharikota.
[38:4:3] TABLE head: Satellite | Vehicle / date | Note
  [38:4:3.0] NVS-01 (2nd generation) | GSLV-F12, May 2023 | First indigenous rubidium clock
  [38:4:3.1] NVS-02 | GSLV-F15, 29 Jan 2025 | 100th Sriharikota launch; valve (pyro connector) glitch → wrong orbit
[38:4:4] # Atomic clocks
[38:4:5] - **Caesium** clock is the world's **standard of time** (the SI second); NavIC satellites carry **rubidium** clocks (three each), smaller but slightly less accurate.
[38:4:6] - First-generation rubidium clocks from Swiss firm **SpectraTime** failed → indigenous clocks by **SAC, Ahmedabad**.
[38:4:7] - **13 Mar 2026:** last clock on **IRNSS-1F** stopped → only **3** PNT satellites (1B, 1I, NVS-01), below the **4** needed for a fix.
[38:4:8] # Limitations
[38:4:9] - **Regional** coverage only; thin **receiver** ecosystem (L5/S rarely in phone chips; L1 on NVS helps); clock failures + NVS-02 failure → NavIC could not fully replace GPS (Mar 2026).
[38:4:10] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 1 p.114; Uttarakhand PCS 2012 explanation (PSLV-C22); app notes 5:38 sub 2, 5
[38:4:11] - **IRNSS-1H** (PSLV-C39, 31 Aug 2017) **failed** because the **heat shield (payload fairing) did not separate** – the satellite stayed trapped inside.

## SUB 0 | Electromagnetic Spectrum, Sensors and Bands
[39:0:0] # Spectrum order
[39:0:1] - Wavelength falls: **radio (incl. microwave) → infrared → visible (red … violet) → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays**.
[39:0:2] - **Colour** of light is set by its **wavelength**; in visible light **red** is longest and **violet** shortest.
[39:0:3] - **Infrared** is longer than red; gamma rays shortest of all.
[39:0:4] - **X-rays** strongly darken (expose) **photographic plates and film** – the basis of radiography.
[39:0:5] # Passive vs active sensors
[39:0:6] TABLE head: Type | Energy | Examples
  [39:0:6.0] Passive | Reflected sunlight or emitted heat | Cameras (visible, infrared, multispectral), radiometers, MODIS
  [39:0:6.1] Active | Sends its own pulse, reads the echo | Radar/SAR (microwave), laser / LiDAR, altimeters
[39:0:7] - **Laser** is the active sensor in a list of cameras (APPSC 2023).
[39:0:8] - **LiDAR = Light Detection And Ranging**: pulsed laser measures distances → elevation, 3D terrain, canopy height. **Airborne Laser Terrain Mapping (ALTM)** is airborne LiDAR: a fast, accurate, cost-effective 3D picture of the ground, used mainly for mapping and survey.
[39:0:9] - Laser uses: LASIK, LiDAR, holography – **MRI is not** a laser use (it uses magnets and radio waves).
[39:0:10] # Which band for what
[39:0:11] TABLE head: Band | Use
  [39:0:11.0] Optical (visible/NIR) | Reflected light; needs daylight and clear sky
  [39:0:11.1] Thermal infrared | Emitted heat → volcanic eruptions, forest fires, urban heat
  [39:0:11.2] Microwave (radio region) – SAR | Penetrates clouds and darkness → all-weather imaging of cloud-covered disaster areas
  [39:0:11.3] Hyperspectral | Hundreds of narrow bands → materials and chemicals
[39:0:12] > ⚠ Cloud-covered disaster area: the answer is the **radio/microwave** band; visible light cannot pass through cloud (the APPSC 2021 key "Visible" is a factual error).
[39:0:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 2 p.5; PMF S&T Part 1 p.80-81, 94 (RISAT all-weather), p.69 (active sensors)

## SUB 2 | Remote Sensing – Definition, Components and Platforms
[39:2:0] - **Remote sensing:** gathering information about an object **without physical contact**, by measuring **reflected and emitted electromagnetic radiation** from a distance (satellite, aircraft, drone); it uses **electromagnetic waves**, not sonar or electric waves.
[39:2:1] # Components (stages)
[39:2:2] - 1. Energy source / illumination → 2. Radiation through the atmosphere → 3. **Interaction with the target** → 4. Recording by the sensor → 5. **Transmission, reception and processing** → 6. Interpretation and analysis → 7. Application.
[39:2:3] - "Interaction of Energy with **Minimisation**" is **not** a component (APPSC 2022).
[39:2:4] # Disaster uses and platforms
[39:2:5] - Disaster applications: **glacial lakes**, **wildfires**, **wind/cyclone tracking**, floods, landslides – "hills management" is not one.
[39:2:6] - **UAV (drone)** is best for **detailed, localised** damage assessment; satellites for wide areas.
[39:2:7] - **Infrared (thermal) cameras** detect people trapped under **rubble** after an earthquake by their body heat.
[39:2:8] - **GEOSS = Global Earth Observation System of Systems** (built by the Group on Earth Observations, GEO).
[39:2:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen S&T Group 2 Part 2 p.5; PMF S&T Part 1 p.80-81; app notes 5:39 sub 0
[39:2:10] - **JACIE (Joint Agency Commercial Imagery Evaluation):** collaboration of five US federal agencies (NASA, NOAA, NGA, USGS, USDA) that are major users and producers of satellite land remote-sensing data.
