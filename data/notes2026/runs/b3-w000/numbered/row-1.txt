## SUB 0 | Sectoral Shares of GDP – Trend and Ranking
[0:0] # Order and trend
[0:1] - **Decreasing order of contribution to GDP now: services > industry > agriculture** (UPSC 2007, NDA 2014, UPPCS 2017) – the **tertiary (service) sector is the largest source of national income** in the 21st century.
[0:2] - **At Independence agriculture** was the largest contributor (about half of GDP); over the plan period the composition **shifted in favour of the service sector**.
[0:3] - **As the economy develops the tertiary share rises**; since 1980 it has shown an **increasing trend**; agriculture's contribution is **decreasing**.

[0:5] - **Agriculture around 2008-13: about 15%** of GDP (BPSC 2008, UPRO 2013 key) – now about 18% after the revisions.
[0:6] # New GDP series (base 2022-23, Feb 2026)
[0:7] - **2024-25, current prices: primary 21.4%, secondary 25.8%, tertiary 52.9%** – figures to quote now; the new series shows higher agriculture and industry shares, lower services.
[0:8] - **The 21.4% primary share includes mining**, so it is not comparable with the old agriculture-forestry-fishing share.
[0:9] - **Manufacturing 14.7%** in the new series (not 14.3%); built from **ASI** (factories) + **ASUSE** (unincorporated units).
[0:10] # Sub-sectors
[0:11] - **2000-01 (constant prices) order:** agriculture-forestry-fishing > trade-hotels-transport-communication > manufacturing > finance-insurance-real estate-business services (UPSC 2002: **1, 3, 2, 4**).
[0:12] - **Highest services share in 2006:** trade, hotels, transport and communication; **fastest-growing service 2006-2010: communication**.
[0:13] - **Mining and quarrying ≈ 3% of GVA** in 2016-17 at 2011-12 prices (about 2.4% at current prices) – small output share, key raw-material supplier.
[0:14] - **1999-2008 (1999-2000 prices):** within the primary sector the share of **forestry** declined steeply (APPSC G2 2011 key).
[0:15] - **Fishing and aquaculture** (old series): **0.8% (2011-12) → 1.3% (2022-23)**; manufacturing 17.4% → 14.3%.
[0:16] - **Agriculture's importance** shows in its contribution to **national income and employment, industrial raw materials, trade and foodgrains – all**; its large contribution to GNP is in terms of **value of output**, not exports or revenue.
[0:17] - **Highest growth in industrial production** (APPSC G1 2003): **manufacturing**.

## SUB 2 | Output vs Employment – the Mismatch
[2:0] - **The maximum number of people work in the primary sector** (agriculture) – the **largest employer**, though it gives the least output; the tertiary sector gives the most output (APPSC JL 2004, AD 2017).
[2:1] - Agriculture employs **46.1%** of workers (PLFS 2023-24); earlier **more than 50%** of the working population depended on the primary sector (WBCS 2017).
[2:2] TABLE head: Sector (2021-22) | Share of GDP | Share of workforce
  [2:2.0] Primary | 21.82% | 43.96%
  [2:2.1] Tertiary | 53.89% | 33.80%
[2:3] - Services were **22.82%** of employment in 1993-94 (agriculture 64.75%).
[2:4] - **Manufacturing** employs only **11.4%** of workers (2023-24); **MSMEs** are the second-largest employer after agriculture.
[2:5] - **Organised-sector employment growth declined** in recent years mainly because the **manufacturing share of GDP did not increase** (APPSC AD 2017).
[2:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 22 p.12; PLFS Annual Report 2023-24 (MoSPI); app notes 3:1 sub 5
## SUB 3 | Structural Change and Services-led Growth
[3:0] - **Structural change** = changing shares of the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors in national income; it occurred **because the process of economic growth was initiated** (APPSC G1: A and R true, **R explains A**).
[3:1] - **Shifting of workers from agriculture to other sectors** = an indication of **economic growth** (APPSC G2 2011), not backwardness.
[3:2] TABLE head: Model | Author | Prediction
  [3:2.0] Three-sector | Colin Clark and Allan Fisher | As income rises, labour and output shift primary → secondary → tertiary
  [3:2.1] Dual-sector | W. Arthur Lewis (1954) | Surplus farm labour moves to modern industry until it runs out – the Lewis turning point
[3:3] - **Post-1991 growth was service-led** – not import-, export- or agriculture-led (APPSC 2018); in **1992-2001 services** grew fastest and contributed most to GDP.
[3:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:4.0] Missing middle | Agriculture → services without a manufacturing-led stage
  [3:4.1] Premature deindustrialisation | Services generally require high skills and may not absorb semi-skilled farm workers → jobless growth
  [3:4.2] Premature leapfrogging | Services absorb value while manufacturing fails to absorb surplus farm labour
  [3:4.3] Nominal vs real share | Fall in manufacturing's nominal share = relative-price effect (Economic Survey 2025-26)
  [3:4.4] Share logic | A sector growing slower than GDP loses share even if its output rises
[3:5] - **EAC-PM Chairman** (Nov 2025): services-led growth cannot be sustained without a manufacturing base.
[3:6] - India: **2.9%** of global manufacturing value added; **1.8%** of world merchandise exports; **44th** on the Economic Complexity Index.
[3:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 22 p.12; Economic Survey 2025-26 (economic-survey-2025-26-full); app notes 3:1 subs 2, 6
