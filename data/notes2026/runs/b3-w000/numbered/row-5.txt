## SUB 2 | Informal and Unorganised Employment
[2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [2:0.0] Unorganised sector (general) | Small and scattered units largely outside the control of the government; low pay, no job security (APPSC GS, SSC 2017)
  [2:0.1] Unorganised sector (NCEUS) | Unincorporated private enterprises of individuals/households, fewer than 10 workers
  [2:0.2] Organised manufacturing | Registered factories – 10+ workers with power / 20+ without
  [2:0.3] Informal worker (NCEUS) | Unorganised-sector workers + organised-sector workers without social security
  [2:0.4] Informalisation of formal enterprises | Contract and outsourced workers inside formal firms
  [2:0.5] "Formal informality" | New EPFO 'formal' jobs that are short-term, low-wage or contractual
[2:1] - **NCEUS**: chaired by **Dr. Arjun Sengupta**; formed **20.09.2004**, term ended 30.04.2009; found **77%** of Indians lived on less than **Rs 20 a day**.
[2:2] - Informal employment: **over 80%** of the workforce (Mar 2026); non-farm informal share about **78%** (PLFS 2022-23).
[2:3] - **NSS 73rd round (2015-16)**: unincorporated non-agricultural enterprises employed about **11 crore**.
[2:4] - **e-Shram**: **31 crore+** registered; **four Labour Codes** merged **29** laws (Code on Wages **2019**; others 2020).
[2:5] - Social-protection coverage: 19% (2015) → **64.3%** (2025) – India 2nd after China.
[2:6] - Direct factory employment 61% → **47%** (2011-2023); contract workers = 42%; small and marginal farmers ~**86%**.
[2:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 17 p.7 (Arjun Sengupta, Rs 20 a day); NCEUS Report 2007; NCERT Class 10 Economics ch.2; app notes 3:5 sub 6
## SUB 3 | Meaning and Theory of Occupational Structure
[3:0] - **Occupational structure** = **distribution of the working population among different occupations** – primary, secondary and tertiary.
[3:1] - **Clark-Fisher thesis**: as income rises, labour shifts primary → secondary → tertiary; India moved from agriculture **directly to services** with no manufacturing-led stage.
[3:2] - **Structural retardation**: the occupational structure changed far more slowly than output – because **investment was directed towards capital-intensive industries** (UPSC 1995).
[3:3] - **Premature deindustrialisation**: manufacturing fails to absorb enough farm labour.
[3:4] - An **increase in the working population** is **not** an adverse effect of population growth (smaller holdings, unemployment, less food per head are).
[3:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 03 p.21 (occupational structure); app notes 3:5 sub 0

## SUB 5 | Rural Workers and Women Workers
[5:0] - Census 2011: **72.4%** of workers in **rural** areas, against **68.8%** of population.
[5:1] - **Feminisation of agriculture:** female workers **55%** agricultural labourers, 24% cultivators; women hold only **12.8%** of operational holdings; driven by **subsistence farming, unpaid family work, distress employment** (not formal manufacturing jobs).
[5:2] - Rural women self-employed: 60% (2011-12) → **73%** (2023-24); UN: **2026 = International Year of the Woman Farmer**.
[5:3] - Female LFPR (15+): 23.3% (2017-18) → **30%** (2019-20) → **40%** (2025); rural 33.0% → **45.9%**; women = **32.5%** of employment (2025).
[5:4] - Labour participation of women about **32%** (APPSC AEE 2012 key, ILO estimate); Census 2011 female WPR 25.5%.
[5:5] ~~ Sources: Census of India 2011; PLFS Annual Reports 2017-18 to 2023-24; app notes 3:5 sub 4

## SUB 7 | Jobless Growth and the Slow Shift Out of Agriculture
[7:0] - **Jobless growth**: output grows without a matching rise in quality employment – **late 1990s**.
[7:1] - **Employment elasticity:** **negative in agriculture**; **construction** and services most employment-intensive (RBI study, 2014).
[7:2] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [7:2.0] History | Colonial deindustrialisation pushed artisans onto land
  [7:2.1] Capital intensity | Growth failed to absorb surplus farm labour
  [7:2.2] Skills | Only 4.2% had formal vocational training
  [7:2.3] Job deficit | ~20 million entrants a year vs ~2 million new jobs
[7:3] ~~ Sources: RBI study on employment elasticity (2014); app notes 3:5 sub 8
## SUB 8 | Gig and Platform Work
[8:0] - **Gig worker**: earns outside the traditional employer-employee relationship; defined **for the first time** in the **Code on Social Security, 2020**.
[8:1] - **Platform work**: uses an **online platform** to reach others or provide services; gig workers = about **2%** of the 61.6 crore workforce; **not yet a separate PLFS category**.
[8:2] ~~ Sources: Code on Social Security 2020 s.2(35), 2(61); app notes 3:5 sub 7
