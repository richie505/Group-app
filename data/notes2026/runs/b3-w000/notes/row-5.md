=== Census – History and Worker Data
# History
- First Census of the modern (non-synchronous) system: **1872** under Lord **Mayo** – the Viceroy who arranged the first Census; first **regular**, synchronous and scientific Census: **1881** under Lord **Ripon**.
- **Census 2011** = **15th** since 1872, **7th** after Independence; covered **6,40,867 villages**. **Census 2027** = first **app-based digital** Census.
# Worker categories
| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Main worker | Worked **6 months or more** in the reference year |
| Marginal worker | Worked **less than 6 months** |
| Cultivator | Works on own/leased land |
| Agricultural labourer | Works on **another person's land for wages** |
| Household industry | Production in the household, by family members |
| Other workers | All other work – services, trade, factories |
# Census 2011 numbers
- Total workers **48.19 crore** (481.9 million, not 382 million); **main workers 36.26 crore**; male workers about **332 million**.
- **Work participation rate 39.8%** (39.79%) – not 39.1, 42.3 or 43.6.
- Census 2001: AP's female WPR **35.1%**; rural female WPR in AP about **43.3%** (APPSC G2 2011 key); mining and quarrying employed about **0.6%** of workers.
~~ Sources: Census of India 2011, Primary Census Abstract – Data Highlights (censusindia.gov.in); Census 2001 Final Population Totals; app notes 3:5 sub 2

=== Workforce Share by Sector Over Time
| Year / survey | Agriculture share of workers |
|---|---|
| 1951 | **72%** |
| Census 2001 | 58.2% (labour force in agriculture 56.7%) |
| NSS 2011-12 | **48.9%** |
| Census 2011 | **54.6%** |
| PLFS 2023-24 | **46.1%** |
| PLFS Q2 FY26 | **42.4%** |
- **Agriculture** = always the **highest employment share** (falling); the main livelihood **on the eve of Independence**. Direct employment in agriculture: about **55%** of the labour force (Census 2011), so 20, 30, 40 and 65% are all off (UPPCS Mains 2012).
- Now: agriculture about **18% of GVA** but **42.4%** of the workforce.
- 2021-22 workforce: primary 43.96%, secondary 22.24%, tertiary **33.80%**; urban employment: services **62.0%** (Q2 FY26).
- PLFS Jan-Mar 2026: rural agricultural employment 58.5% → 55.8%; workforce **61.6 crore** (PLFS 2025).
- **Agricultural households = 57.8%** of rural households (NSSO 70th round, 2013).
- **NSS 2011-12: self-employed** = largest category of workers (about 52%), then casual labour, then regular salaried.

=== Informal and Unorganised Employment
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unorganised sector (general) | **Small and scattered units largely outside the control of the government**; low pay, no job security (APPSC GS, SSC 2017) |
| Unorganised sector (NCEUS) | Unincorporated private enterprises of individuals/households, fewer than **10** workers |
| Organised manufacturing | Registered factories – **10+** workers with power / 20+ without |
| Informal worker (NCEUS) | Unorganised-sector workers + organised-sector workers **without social security** |
| Informalisation of formal enterprises | Contract and outsourced workers inside formal firms |
| "Formal informality" | New EPFO 'formal' jobs that are short-term, low-wage or contractual |
- **NCEUS**: chaired by **Dr. Arjun Sengupta**; formed **20.09.2004**, term ended 30.04.2009; found **77%** of Indians lived on less than **Rs 20 a day**.
- Informal employment: **over 80%** of the workforce (Mar 2026); non-farm informal share about **78%** (PLFS 2022-23).
- **NSS 73rd round (2015-16)**: unincorporated non-agricultural enterprises employed about **11 crore**.
- **e-Shram**: **31 crore+** registered; **four Labour Codes** merged **29** laws (Code on Wages **2019**; others 2020).
- Social-protection coverage: 19% (2015) → **64.3%** (2025) – India 2nd after China.
- Direct factory employment 61% → **47%** (2011-2023); contract workers = 42%; small and marginal farmers ~**86%**.
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 17 p.7 (Arjun Sengupta, Rs 20 a day); NCEUS Report 2007; NCERT Class 10 Economics ch.2; app notes 3:5 sub 6

=== Meaning and Theory of Occupational Structure
- **Occupational structure** = **distribution of the working population among different occupations** – primary, secondary and tertiary.
- **Clark-Fisher thesis**: as income rises, labour shifts primary → secondary → tertiary; India moved from agriculture **directly to services** with no manufacturing-led stage.
- **Structural retardation**: the occupational structure changed far more slowly than output – because **investment was directed towards capital-intensive industries** (UPSC 1995).
- **Premature deindustrialisation**: manufacturing fails to absorb enough farm labour.
- An **increase in the working population** is **not** an adverse effect of population growth (smaller holdings, unemployment, less food per head are).
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 03 p.21 (occupational structure); app notes 3:5 sub 0

=== Cultivators vs Agricultural Labourers
| Category | Census 2001 | Census 2011 | Share 2011 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cultivators | 127.3 million | **118.8 million** (11.87 crore) ↓ | **24.7%** |
| Agricultural labourers | 106.8 million | **144.3 million** (14.43 crore) ↑ | **30.0%** |
- **2011** = first Census in which **labourers outnumbered cultivators**.
- Cause – **fragmentation**: average holding **2.28 ha (1971) → 1.08 ha (2015-16)**.
~~ Sources: Census of India 2011, Primary Census Abstract; Agriculture Census 2015-16; app notes 3:5 sub 3

=== Rural Workers and Women Workers
- Census 2011: **72.4%** of workers in **rural** areas, against **68.8%** of population.
- **Feminisation of agriculture:** female workers **55%** agricultural labourers, 24% cultivators; women hold only **12.8%** of operational holdings; driven by **subsistence farming, unpaid family work, distress employment** (not formal manufacturing jobs).
- Rural women self-employed: 60% (2011-12) → **73%** (2023-24); UN: **2026 = International Year of the Woman Farmer**.
- Female LFPR (15+): 23.3% (2017-18) → **30%** (2019-20) → **40%** (2025); rural 33.0% → **45.9%**; women = **32.5%** of employment (2025).
- Labour participation of women about **32%** (APPSC AEE 2012 key, ILO estimate); Census 2011 female WPR 25.5%.
~~ Sources: Census of India 2011; PLFS Annual Reports 2017-18 to 2023-24; app notes 3:5 sub 4

=== Jobless Growth and the Slow Shift Out of Agriculture
- **Jobless growth**: output grows without a matching rise in quality employment – **late 1990s**.
- **Employment elasticity:** **negative in agriculture**; **construction** and services most employment-intensive (RBI study, 2014).
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| History | **Colonial deindustrialisation** pushed artisans onto land |
| Capital intensity | Growth failed to absorb surplus farm labour |
| Skills | Only **4.2%** had formal vocational training |
| Job deficit | ~20 million entrants a year vs ~2 million new jobs |
~~ Sources: RBI study on employment elasticity (2014); app notes 3:5 sub 8

=== Gig and Platform Work
- **Gig worker**: earns outside the traditional employer-employee relationship; defined **for the first time** in the **Code on Social Security, 2020**.
- **Platform work**: uses an **online platform** to reach others or provide services; gig workers = about **2%** of the 61.6 crore workforce; **not yet a separate PLFS category**.
~~ Sources: Code on Social Security 2020 s.2(35), 2(61); app notes 3:5 sub 7

=== Types of Unemployment
| Type | Feature | Linked with |
|---|---|---|
| Disguised | More people than needed; **marginal productivity near zero** | Agriculture |
| Seasonal | Work only in **sowing/harvest** | Agriculture |
| Structural | **Skill-job mismatch** (new technology) | Industry, educated |
| Frictional | **Short gap between jobs** | Urban/organised |
| Cyclical | **Demand falls in a business-cycle slump** | Industry |
~~ Sources: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development ch.7; app notes 3:5 sub 9

| Census 2011 | Andhra Pradesh (13 districts) | India |
|---|---|---|
| Cultivators | **14.5%** | 24.7% |
