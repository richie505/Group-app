## SUB 1 | Article 17 and Untouchability
[133:1:0] # Provision
[133:1:1] - "Untouchability" is **abolished**; its practice **in any form** is forbidden; enforcing any disability from it is an **offence**.
[133:1:2] - In the **Right to Equality** group; a "statement of principle" and basis of SC/ST rights.
[133:1:3] - **Art. 35(a)(ii):** **only Parliament** prescribes punishment for Art. 17 offences.
[133:1:4] - **Horizontal:** binds **private persons** too (**PUDR v Union of India, 1982**).
[133:1:5] - **Ambedkar** ensured untouchability became a **punishable offence**.
[133:1:6] - **Sabarimala reference (Apr 2026):** Justice **B.V. Nagarathna** – Art. 17 cannot apply "for three days a month".
[133:1:7] # Definition and Forms
[133:1:8] - **Not defined** in the Constitution or any Act. **Mysore HC:** the practice as historically developed; excludes social boycott of a few individuals.
[133:1:9] - Forms: **temple-entry denial**, separate **wells**, **two-tumbler system**, residential segregation, forced "polluting" work. Seat reservation is protective, **not** untouchability.
[133:1:10] - **Structural violence (Johan Galtung):** harm built into social structures. UPPSC 2019 A-R: untouchability is the worst structural violence (A), practised in the name of religious sanction (R) – both true, R explains A.
[133:1:11] - **Manual scavenging:** "the **worst surviving symbol** of untouchability".
[133:1:12] - "Scheduled Castes": first used by the **Simon Commission**; given shape in the **GoI Act 1935**; first list **1936**; first post-1950 list: **Constitution (SC) Order, 1950**.

## SUB 3 | Measures and Manual Scavenging
[133:3:0] # Measures
[133:3:1] - Untouchability is abolished through **laws, education and public awareness** – not by providing jobs (UPPSC 1992).
[133:3:2] - Economic: **NSFDC** (BPL SCs), **NSKFDC** (sanitation workers), **PM-DAKSH** (skills), **VCF-SC**.
[133:3:3] # Manual Scavenging
[133:3:4] - **1993 Act:** banned employment of manual scavengers and dry latrines.
[133:3:5] - **Rashtriya Garima Abhiyan (2001):** National Campaign for Dignity – end manual scavenging.
[133:3:6] - **2013 Act:** bans hazardous cleaning of sewers/septic tanks by **anyone, incl. private contractors**; manual cleaning of insanitary latrines; mandates **mechanisation** and rehabilitation; **does not legalise dry latrines**.
[133:3:7] - **Bandicoot:** manhole-cleaning **robot** by Kerala start-up **Genrobotics**, replacing manual scavenging (not Vyommitra).
[133:3:8] - **SRMS (2007**, revised 2013, 2021) **subsumed in NAMASTE** (MoSJE + MoHUA; NSKFDC implements; goal **zero sanitation deaths**).
[133:3:9] - Status (Aug 2026): **90,915** sewer/septic workers + 1.3 lakh waste pickers profiled; **58,000** manual scavengers identified, only 2,652 projects approved.
## SUB 4 | Sc/st Lists – Articles 341 and 342
[133:4:0] - **Art. 341:** **President** specifies SCs for a State/UT **after consulting the Governor** by public notification; later changes **only by Parliament** by law.
[133:4:1] - **States have no power:** SC (July 2024) quashed **Bihar's 2015** notification moving **Tanti-Tantwa** from EBC to SC.
[133:4:2] - **Art. 342:** ST lists; **ST status is religion-neutral**.
[133:4:3] - **Chinthada Anand v State of AP (2026):** a **Christian convert** cannot claim SC status; reconversion needs proof of original SC status, genuine reconversion and community acceptance.
[133:4:4] - Count: **1,241** caste groups notified as SCs (Census 2011).
[133:4:5] Adding castes is **Art. 341**, not 344 (344 = Official Language Commission).

=== NOTES 2:134 | Sc/st (prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989 and Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955
## SUB 0 | Pcr Act 1955 vs PoA Act 1989
[134:0:0] TABLE head: Item | PCR Act | PoA Act
  [134:0:0.0] Origin | Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955; renamed 1976 | Enacted 1989 (assent 11 Sept 1989)
  [134:0:0.1] In force | 1955; Rules 1977 | 30 January 1990; Rules 1995
  [134:0:0.2] Extent / scope | Whole of India (s.1(2); APPSC 2019) | Offences by non-SC/ST persons against SCs/STs
  [134:0:0.3] Amended | 1976 | 2015, 2018 (20 Aug 2018)
[134:0:1] - Both rest on **Article 17**; PoA rests on **protective discrimination (Art. 15(4))**. PoA has **23 sections**.
[134:0:2] - **"Untouchability"** is defined neither in the Constitution nor the Act; **Mysore HC**: the historically developed caste practice.
[134:0:3] - Since **1 July 2024**, IPC/CrPC references read as **BNS/BNSS**. PoA applies to **J&K since 2019**.
[134:0:4] PCR **1955** (renamed 1976, Rules 1977) vs PoA **1989** (in force 30 Jan 1990, Rules 1995).
[134:0:5] - **Why the PoA Act:** the PCR Act proved inadequate to check atrocities against SCs/STs, so Parliament enacted the PoA Act in 1989.
## SUB 1 | Pcr Act – Sections and Punishment
[134:1:0] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [134:1:0.0] 3 | Religious disabilities – entry/worship in public places of worship
  [134:1:0.1] 4 | Social disabilities – shops, hotels, water sources, roads
  [134:1:0.2] 5 | Refusing admission to hospital, dispensary, educational institution
  [134:1:0.3] 6 | Refusing to sell goods or render services
  [134:1:0.4] 7 | Insulting an SC member (7(1)(d)); preaching or justifying untouchability
  [134:1:0.5] 10 | Abetment – public servant wilfully neglecting investigation
  [134:1:0.6] 10A | Collective fine on inhabitants of an area
  [134:1:0.7] 12 | Court presumes the untouchability ground when the victim is a Scheduled Caste member
  [134:1:0.8] 14 | Offences by companies – Director, Manager or Secretary held guilty
  [134:1:0.9] 15 | Cognizable, triable summarily, non-compoundable; no prior sanction
  [134:1:0.10] 15A | State duties – legal aid, special courts, surveys of prone areas, vigilance officers, annual reports
[134:1:1] - **First offence:** **1-6 months** and fine **₹100-500**.
[134:1:2] - Convict is **disqualified** from elections to Parliament and State Legislatures.
[134:1:3] - Not an offence when accused and victim belong to the **same social group**.
[134:1:4] - **Implementation:** States/UTs; nodal Centre – **Social Justice and Empowerment** with **MHA** (not Minority Affairs).
[134:1:5] - **Sukanya Shantha (2024):** Art. 17 bars caste-based **purity stigma**.
## SUB 2 | PoA Act – Key Sections
[134:2:0] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [134:2:0.0] 2(1)(bc) | Defines economic boycott (2015)
  [134:2:0.1] 3 | List of atrocities; public servant committing one – minimum 1 year
  [134:2:0.2] 4 | Wilful neglect of duty by public servants
  [134:2:0.3] 6 | Applies IPC s.34, Chapters 3, 4, 5, 5-A, s.149, Chapter 23
  [134:2:0.4] 8 | Court may presume abetment, common intention, common object
  [134:2:0.5] 14 | Special Courts; Exclusive Special Courts (2015)
  [134:2:0.6] 15 | Special Public Prosecutors
  [134:2:0.7] 14A | Appeal to High Court within 90 days (incl. bail orders), on facts and law
  [134:2:0.8] 16 | State Government imposes and collects collective fine
  [134:2:0.9] 18 | Anticipatory bail barred (CrPC 438, now BNSS 482)
  [134:2:0.10] 18A | No preliminary enquiry or approval before arrest (2018)

  [134:2:0.12] 23 | Central Government rule-making (Rules 1995)
[134:2:1] - Special Court: a **Court of Session** specified by the **State Government** with concurrence of the **HC Chief Justice**, in each district. It **cannot impose a collective fine** and cannot authorise a non-police person for further investigation.
[134:2:2] - Accused **cannot claim private defence**. Offences are cognizable.
[134:2:3] - Arms licences of potential offenders in **atrocity-prone areas** can be cancelled; SC/ST persons may get licences. No power to dissolve local authorities.
[134:2:4] PCR **s.12** = presumption, **s.14** = companies; PoA **s.14** = Special Courts, **s.18** = no anticipatory bail.
