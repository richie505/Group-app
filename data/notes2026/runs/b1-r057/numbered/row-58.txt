## SUB 0 | Twin Epics – Silappadikaram and Manimekalai
[0:0] # Silappadikaram – the first Tamil epic
[0:1] - **Author:** **Ilango Adigal** – a **Jain** ascetic, younger brother of Chera **Senguttuvan**, grandson (by tradition) of Chola **Karikala** (UPPSC 2002 ×2).
[0:2] - **Story:** merchant **Kovalan** of Puhar neglects his wife **Kannagi** for the dancer **Madhavi**; in Madurai he is **wrongly accused of stealing the queen's anklet** and executed by the **Pandya** king; Kannagi burns **Madurai** (APPSC FSO).
[0:3] - **Jaina elements** in the storyline (e.g. the Jain nun Kavunthi Adigal) (CDS 2016) – not a Rama story, Sri Lankan Buddhism or Shakti cult.
[0:4] - **River praised:** the **Kaveri (Cauvery)** (CDS 2017).
[0:5] # Manimekalai – the Buddhist sequel
[0:6] - **Author:** **Sittalai Sattanar (Sathanar)**, a Buddhist poet (CDS 2018).
[0:7] - **Story:** **Manimekalai**, daughter of Kovalan and the dancer Madhavi, becomes a **Buddhist nun** (IAS 2002).
[0:8] # The five Tamil epics (post-Sangam, c. 4th–6th c. CE)
[0:9] TABLE head: Epic | Author / faith
  [0:9.0] Silappadikaram | Ilango Adigal (Jain)
  [0:9.1] Manimekalai | Sittalai Sattanar (Buddhist)
  [0:9.2] Jivaka (Sivaga) Chintamani | Tiruttakkadevar (Jain ascetic); called Mana Nool (Book of Marriages)
  [0:9.3] Valayapathi, Kundalakesi | –
[0:10] - **Twin epics** of early Tamil literature = **Silappadikaram and Manimekalai** (UPPSC 2005); Tolkappiyam and Kural are not epics.
[0:11] > Exam angle: author, anklet story, Jaina elements, river Kaveri, dancing girl → nun.

## SUB 1 | Tolkappiyam and Tirukkural
[1:0] # Tolkappiyam
[1:1] - **Tolkappiyam** by **Tolkappiyar** = the **oldest extant Tamil grammar** – a work on **grammar and poetics** (UPPSC 1997, 2014; Jharkhand 2023); the only surviving work of the **Second Sangam**.
[1:2] - **Three parts:** **Ezhuttu, Col, Porul**; also gives the **tinai** scheme and the **four castes**.
[1:3] # Tirukkural
[1:4] - **Tirukkural (Kural)** by **Tiruvalluvar** – **Tamil**, **1,330 couplets** on philosophy and ethics in three books: **Aram** (virtue), **Porul** (wealth), **Inbam** (love) (MPPSC 1997).
[1:5] - **Other names:** **Laghuveda**, **Tamil Veda**, **Ulaga Podhumarai** (UPRO 2013 ×2) – not Kalingattuparani, Periyapuranam or Nandikkalambakam.
[1:6] - Part of the post-Sangam **Pathinenkilkanakku**.
[1:7] TABLE head: Work | Subject (UPPSC 2014 match)
  [1:7.0] Tirukkural | Philosophy
  [1:7.1] Tolkappiyam | Grammar
  [1:7.2] Silappadikaram | Love story
  [1:7.3] Manimekalai | Merchant story

## SUB 2 | Nature of Sangam Literature – Language, Themes, Anthologies
[2:0] # Language and content
[2:1] - **Language:** old **Tamil** (APPSC Gazetted 2010); the contemporary Satavahanas used **Prakrit**.
[2:2] - About **2,400 poems** by some 450 poets, about 30 of them **women** (Avvaiyar the most prolific).
[2:3] - **Varna was known** to Sangam poets, though society was not rigidly stratified; the poems are rich in **material culture** and **warrior ethic** (UPSC 2022 ×2).
[2:4] - **Silent on the trade routes** of ancient India – they describe ports and goods; the **Milindapanha** and **Jatakas** describe routes (State PSC).
[2:5] - **Mamulanar**, a Sangam poet, refers to the **Mauryan expedition** against a Tamil chieftain (IAS 2001).
[2:6] TABLE head: Type | Theme
  [2:6.0] Aham (interior) | Love, personal emotion, nature
  [2:6.1] Puram (exterior) | War, valour, royal deeds, ethics
[2:7] # The corpus
[2:8] TABLE head: Group | Count | Note
  [2:8.0] Ettuthogai | 8 anthologies | Purananuru, Akananuru, Padirruppattu (on the Chera kings)
  [2:8.1] Pattupattu | 10 idylls | Pattinappalai (Puhar and Karikala), Maduraikanchi, Mullaippattu
  [2:8.2] Pathinenkilkanakku | 18 minor works | Didactic, includes Tirukkural; post-Sangam (4th–6th c.)
[2:9] - **Melkanakku** = Ettuthogai + Pattupattu = the **18 major** works; both divided into **aham and puram**.
[2:10] - **A–R (UPSC 2000):** the aham and puram poems of the Padinenkilkanakku **continue** Sangam composition, and they are classed as **post-Sangam** works as against the Sangam works proper – both true, R explains A (PMF key; some keys give "R not the explanation").
[2:11] # Chronology
[2:12] - Accepted span **c. 300 BCE – 300 CE**; **Tamil-Brahmi** of the **3rd c. BCE** supports the early limit – not 1000–500 BCE.
[2:13] - Tirukkural, the epics and Pathinenkilkanakku are **post-Sangam**, not the earliest layer.

## SUB 3 | The Three Sangams
[3:0] # Assemblies of poets
[3:1] - **Sangam** = an **assembly of Tamil poets** ("Conference of Poets"), held under **Pandya** patronage – not the Buddhist Sangha or the Sangama dynasty (APPSC G1 2003).
[3:2] - The tradition comes from the preface of **Iraiyanar Agapporul**; the account is traditional, not historical.
[3:3] TABLE head: Sangam | Place | Presided by | Survives
  [3:3.0] First | Then Madurai (submerged) | Agastya | Nothing
  [3:3.1] Second | Kapatapuram (submerged) | Tolkappiyar | Only Tolkappiyam
  [3:3.2] Third | Madurai | Nakkirar | Ettuthogai, Pattupattu
[3:4] - **Third Sangam = Madurai** (UPPCS 2006) – not Arikamedu, Ernakulam or Tuticorin.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval India Part 3 p.39; app notes 1:58 (sub 0)
