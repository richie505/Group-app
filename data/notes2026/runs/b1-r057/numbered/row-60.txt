## SUB 0 | Indo-Roman Trade – Arikamedu, Muziris, Yavanapriya
[0:0] # Arikamedu – the east-coast Roman station
[0:1] - **Arikamedu** (near **Puducherry**) = **Poduke** of the Periplus; the **foremost Indo-Roman trading station of eastern India** (State PSC; APPSC ASO 2011; APPSC PYQ).
[0:2] - **Finds:** Roman **amphorae**, red-glazed **Arretine ware**, rouletted ware, beads and gems, an **intaglio of Augustus**, Tamil-Brahmi potsherds – proof of brisk India–Rome trade in the early Christian era (UPPCS 2003; UP Lower 2009).
[0:3] - **Excavated** by **Sir Mortimer Wheeler** in **1945**.
[0:4] - **Amphora** = a **tall double-handled jar** for **wine and oil**; found at Arikamedu, Kodumanal, Uraiyur, Alagankulam (UPPSC 2014) – not painted grey or black-and-red ware.
[0:5] # Muziris – the chief centre
[0:6] - **Muziris (Mushiri, Muchiri)**, the Chera pepper port, = the **chief centre of Indo-Roman trade** on the Indian side; Pliny's "first emporium of India" (APPSC G1). Alexandria was only the Roman-side entrepot.
[0:7] - Identified with **Pattanam** (**Ernakulam**, Kerala) by KCHR excavations (Jun 2026: still continuing).
[0:8] # Goods
[0:9] TABLE head: Exports | Imports
  [0:9.0] Pepper and spices, cotton and muslin, pearls, ivory, precious stones | Gold, horses, sweet wine, Roman pottery; Roman coins
[0:10] - **Yavanapriya** ("dear to the Yavanas") = **black pepper** (UPSC 1995 ×3) – not muslin, ivory or dancers.
[0:11] - **Sangam economy (statements):** pearls and precious stones were main exports; the Tamils traded with **Egypt and Arabia**; **decline of trade** helped the decay of the Tamil kingdoms; **Uraiyur**, not Madurai, was famous for **cotton**.
[0:12] # Evidence and peak
[0:13] TABLE head: Source | Evidence
  [0:13.0] Periplus of the Erythraean Sea | c. 60 CE, anonymous Greek merchant – chief account
  [0:13.1] Pliny the Elder | Rome lost 55 million sesterces a year to India
  [0:13.2] Coin hoards | Gold and silver of Augustus, Tiberius and Nero; hoards at Akkanpalle, Kondapur
[0:14] - **Peak:** **Satavahana–Sangam age** (1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE); trade grew after Rome took **Egypt (30 BCE)** and the monsoon route was used; it did **not** flourish after the Satavahanas (G2 2025).
[0:15] - **Indian embassies to Rome:** best known – the **Pandya ruler to Augustus**.
[0:16] - Tamil ports traded with **Berenike** and **Myos Hormos** on the **Egyptian Red Sea coast**.

## SUB 1 | Social Groups and Customs – Vellalar, Vattakirutal
[1:0] # Tolkappiyam's four castes
[1:1] TABLE head: Order | Caste | Role
  [1:1.0] 1 | Arasar | Rulers
  [1:1.1] 2 | Anthanar | Priests (Brahmins)
  [1:1.2] 3 | Vanigar | Traders
  [1:1.3] 4 | Vellalar | Rich peasants / landowners – the fourth caste (APPSC G1)
[1:2] - Other groups: **Uzhavar** = ordinary farmers (ploughmen); **Kadaisiyar, Adimai** = landless labourers and slaves; **Panar** = wandering bards; **Parayar** = drummers who worked with skins; **Viraliyar** = women dancers at courts.
[1:3] # Society of the Sangam age (IAS 1998)
[1:4] - **No complex caste system** – a rigid varna system had not developed; kings married into Vellala families.
[1:5] - **Gradual Aryanisation** – Brahmins gained influence; Pandya kings performed **Vedic sacrifices**. Only this statement fits.

## SUB 3 | Religion, Women and Festivals
[3:0] # Gods
[3:1] - Chief deity: **Seyon / Murugan**; his six abodes = **Arupadai Veedu**.
[3:2] - **Mayon** = Vishnu; **Vendan** = Indra; **Varunan**; **Korravai** = goddess of **war and victory**.
[3:3] - **Hero stones** and megalithic burials were practised.
[3:4] - **Onam:** Kerala harvest festival of the return of king **Mahabali**, already mentioned in the Sangam idyll **Maduraikanchi** (UPPSC 2011) – not Rama's victory or Durga's killing of Mahishasura.
[3:5] # Women
[3:6] - Women poets: **Avvaiyar**, **Nachchellaiyar, Kakkaipadiniyar**.
[3:7] - **Sati** was prevalent mainly in the **higher strata**; widows' life was hard.
[3:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval India Part 3 p.37, p.40; https://www.britannica.com/topic/Onam; app notes 1:60 (sub 3, 4)
