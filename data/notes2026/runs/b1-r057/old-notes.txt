=== NOTES 1:57 | Andhra Society and Culture in the Early Period – Varna, Women, Education and Festivals
## SUB 0 | Varna and Caste
[57:0:0] - Early Andhra society was shaped by the **Vedic varna system**; the **Satavahanas** played a crucial role (APPSC GS 2024). The fourfold system existed and the Satavahanas were **Brahmins**; kings upheld **varna-dharma**.
[57:0:1] - **Nanaghat inscription:** Brahmins trained in Vedic learning and ritual; Brahmins got **tax-free villages**.
[57:0:2] - **Kshatriya claims by assimilation:** **Greeks, Sakas, Pahlavas, Kushanas** (also Kardhamakas, Western Kshatrapas) took Indian names and faiths – e.g. Nahapana's daughter **Dakshamitra**, son-in-law **Rishabhadatta**, Rudradaman.
[57:0:3] - **Vishnukundins** followed varnasramadharma; **farmers = "the backbone"**, got land grants; Vaisyas traded; Sudras (carpenters, sculptors, weavers) had a recognised place.
[57:0:4] # Occupational Sub-castes
[57:0:5] TABLE head: Term | Occupation
  [57:0:5.0] Halikas | Farmers (emerging agricultural group)
  [57:0:5.1] Sethis | Merchants
  [57:0:5.2] Selavaddikas | Stone sculptors / masons
  [57:0:5.3] Swarnakaras | Goldsmiths
[57:0:6] - Later terms: **Samayamu** = caste associations (**Kakatiya** period); **Kayasthas** = record-keepers.
## SUB 1 | Marriage Customs
[57:1:0] - Marriage = **sacred**; **polygamy** among upper classes; widows had to observe **celibacy**.
[57:1:1] TABLE head: Type | Union | Children
  [57:1:1.0] Anuloma | Higher-caste man + lower-caste woman | Ugra
  [57:1:1.1] Viloma (Pratiloma) | Lower-caste man + higher-caste woman | Chandala
[57:1:2] - **Kathasaritsagara** mentions inter-caste marriages – caste not fully rigid.

  [57:1:3.2] Vasishthiputra Satakarni | Daughter of Saka Rudradaman
## SUB 2 | Status of Women
[57:2:0] - **Patriarchal joint family**, yet women had **considerable respect and freedom** – joined men in festivals, assemblies, public life.
[57:2:1] - **Matronymics:** Gautamiputra Satakarni, Vasishthiputra Pulumavi. Titles from husbands: **Mahasenapatni, Mahabhojaki, Maharathi**.
[57:2:2] - Female poets in the Gathasaptasati: **Madhavi, Revati, Anupamalabdha, Anulakshmi**.
[57:2:3] - **Sati:** a **Nagarjunakonda sculpture** and Greek writer **Strabo**.
[57:2:4] TABLE head: Woman | Dynasty | Patronage
  [57:2:4.0] Gautami Balasri | Satavahana | Cave-vihara at Nasik (not a Gathasaptasati poet)
  [57:2:4.1] Bodhisiri | Ikshvaku | Greatest woman patron of Buddhism; shrines, viharas at Nagarjunakonda
  [57:2:4.2] Chantisiri | Ikshvaku | Sister of Vasishthiputra Chantamula; built the Mahachaitya, Nagarjunakonda
[57:2:5] Bodhisiri and Chantisiri are Ikshvaku, NOT Satavahana.
## SUB 3 | Education
[57:3:0] TABLE head: Centre | Nature
  [57:3:0.0] Viharas | Buddhist monastic learning (Nagarjunakonda a leading centre)
  [57:3:0.1] Ghatikasthanas | Vedic colleges (Vishnukundins)
  [57:3:0.2] Agraharas | Land grants to Vedic Brahmins (Agnisarma, Easwarasarma)
[57:3:1] - **Indrabhattaraka Varma** – "**Ghatika Vapta punyasancharya**"; credited with founding ghatikas.
## SUB 4 | Religion and Folk Cults
[57:4:0] - **Amaravati reliefs:** worship of **nagas, yakshas and sacred trees** beside Buddhist symbols.
[57:4:1] - **Ikshvaku kings** – Vedic sacrifices (Chantamula: Asvamedha, Vajapeya); **queens** – Buddhism; Brahmanical shrines such as **Pushpabhadraswami** at Nagarjunakonda.
[57:4:2] - Vishnukundins: family deity **Sriparvataswamy**; titles Paramamaheswara, Parama Brahmanya.
## SUB 5 | Festivals, Amusements, Dress
[57:5:0] - **Gathasaptasati (Gaha Sattasai)** by **Hala**: ~**700 verses**, **Maharashtri Prakrit**; love and country verses; begins with a prayer to **Shiva (Pasupati)**; **main literary source** of Satavahana society (APPSC 2019).
[57:5:1] - "**Maiputa**" used by dancers = **Kusuma Lepanam** (APPSC 2019).
[57:5:2] - **Amphitheatre at Nagarjunakonda (Vijayapuri)** – for performances and games, not Vedic sacrifices.
[57:5:3] - Reliefs: **men** – lower cloth and **turban**; **women** – heavy **anklets, bangles, girdles, necklaces**.

=== NOTES 1:58 | Sangam Age – the Three Sangams, Sources; Tolkappiyam, Ettuthogai, Pattupattu, Pathinenkilkanakku
## SUB 0 | The Three Sangams
[58:0:0] - **Sangam** = "Conference of Poets" / "Forum of Scholars"; all three under **Pandya** patronage (not Chola).
[58:0:1] - Tradition comes from the preface of **Iraiyanar Agapporul**.
[58:0:2] TABLE head: Sangam | Place | Presiding scholar | Survives
  [58:0:2.0] First | Then Madurai (submerged) | Agastya | Nothing
  [58:0:2.1] Second | Kapatapuram (submerged) | Tolkappiyar (also linked to Agastya) | Only Tolkappiyam
  [58:0:2.2] Third | Madurai | Nakkirar | Most of the extant corpus
[58:0:3] First and Third Sangams were both at Madurai; Second at Kapatapuram.
## SUB 1 | Chronology
[58:1:0] - Accepted span: **c. 300 BCE – 300 CE**; **Tamil-Brahmi** dated to the 3rd c. BCE supports the early limit.
[58:1:1] - The three-Sangam account (two submerged cities) is **traditional, not historical**.
[58:1:2] - Tirukkural, twin epics and Pathinenkilkanakku are **post-Sangam**.
## SUB 2 | Literature – Language and Themes
[58:2:0] - Language: **old Tamil**; about **2,400 poems** by several hundred poets, including **women**.
[58:2:1] TABLE head: Type | Theme
  [58:2:1.0] Aham (interior) | Love, personal emotion, nature
  [58:2:1.1] Puram (exterior) | War, valour, royal deeds, ethics
[58:2:2] - Rich in **material culture** and **warrior ethic**; poets knew the **varna** classification, but society was not rigidly stratified (UPSC 2022). **Vellalars** = rich peasants, landowners, the fourth caste.
[58:2:3] - Sangam literature is **silent on the trade routes** of ancient India.
[58:2:4] "Sangam literature is devoid of material culture" is FALSE.
## SUB 3 | Anthologies
[58:3:0] TABLE head: Group | Count | Note
  [58:3:0.0] Ettuthogai | 8 anthologies | Short poems; Padirruppattu (Chera kings), Purananuru, Akananuru
  [58:3:0.1] Pattupattu | 10 idylls | Long poems; Pattinappalai (Puhar, Karikala), Maduraikkanji, Mullaippattu, Tirumurugarruppadai, Nedunalvadai, Kurinjippattu
  [58:3:0.2] Pathinenkilkanakku | 18 minor | Ethics; includes Tirukkural; kept Aham/Puram themes but post-Sangam
[58:3:1] - **Melkanakku** = Ettuthogai + Pattupattu = **18 major** works; both divided into aham and puram.
## SUB 4 | Tamil Works – Authors
[58:4:0] TABLE head: Work | Author | Subject
  [58:4:0.0] Tolkappiyam | Tolkappiyar (disciple of Agastya) | Oldest extant Tamil grammar; formula style; 3 parts Ezhuttu, Col, Porul
  [58:4:0.1] Tirukkural | Tiruvalluvar | 1,330 couplets; Aram, Porul, Inbam
  [58:4:0.2] Silappadikaram | Ilango Adigal | First Tamil epic; Kannagi–Kovalan (anklet)
  [58:4:0.3] Manimekalai | Sittalai Sattanar | Buddhist epic, sequel
  [58:4:0.4] Sivaga Sindamani | Tiruttakkadevar (Jain ascetic) | Jain epic of prince Jivaka; called Mana Nool (Book of Marriages)
[58:4:1] - Kural is also called **Laghuveda** (UPPSC 2013), **Tamil Veda**, **Ulaga Podhumarai**.
[58:4:2] - UPPSC 2014 match: Kural = philosophy; Tolkappiyam = grammar; Silappadikaram = love story; Manimekalai = merchant story.
## SUB 5 | Twin Epics
[58:5:0] - **Ilango Adigal** = younger brother of Chera **Senguttuvan**; grandson of Chola **Karikala**.
[58:5:1] - Silappadikaram: merchant **Kovalan**, wife **Kannagi**, dancer **Madhavi**; Kovalan executed by the Pandya king → Kannagi burns **Madurai**.
[58:5:2] - Manimekalai: daughter of Kovalan and Madhavi becomes a **Buddhist nun**. Both epics are **post-Sangam**.

=== NOTES 1:60 | Sangam Society and Economy – Tinai, Indo-roman Trade, Ports, Religion; the Kalabhra Interregnum
## SUB 0 | Tinai – the Five-fold Division of Land
[60:0:0] - The five-fold division of land (**tinai**) is given in the **Tolkappiyam**.
[60:0:1] # Five Tinais – Landscape, Occupation, Deity
[60:0:2] TABLE head: Tinai | Landscape | Main occupation | Deity
  [60:0:2.0] Kurinji | Hilly | Hunting, honey gathering | Murugan (Seyon)
  [60:0:2.1] Mullai | Pastoral / forest | Cattle rearing | Mayon (Vishnu)
  [60:0:2.2] Marutam | Agricultural (riverine plains) | Agriculture | Vendan (Indra)
  [60:0:2.3] Neidal | Coastal | Fishing, salt making | Varunan
  [60:0:2.4] Palai | Desert / dry land | Robbery | Korravai
[60:0:3] Marutam = agriculture and Vendan (Indra); Neidal = coast and Varunan. Do not swap them.
## SUB 1 | Social Groups – Occupation and Status
[60:1:0] # Tolkappiyam's Four Castes (in order)
[60:1:1] - 1. **Arasar** – ruling class; governance, law and order.
[60:1:2] - 2. **Anthanar** – Brahmins, priests; political and religious influence.
[60:1:3] - 3. **Vanigar** – traders / merchants.
[60:1:4] - 4. **Vellalar** – rich peasants / agricultural landowners; the **fourth** caste.
[60:1:5] # Other Groups
[60:1:6] TABLE head: Group | Occupation / status
  [60:1:6.0] Uzhavar | Ordinary farmers (tillers)
  [60:1:6.1] Kadaisiyar, Adimai | Landless labourers and slaves (lowest rung)
  [60:1:6.2] Panar | Wandering bards / minstrels who sang for chiefs
  [60:1:6.3] Parayar | Drummers; worked with animal skins
  [60:1:6.4] Viraliyar | Dancers who performed at courts; patronised by kings and nobles
[60:1:7] - A rigid **varna** system had not fully developed in early Sangam society, so social mobility was possible.
[60:1:8] Vellalar = landowners (fourth caste); Uzhavar = tillers; Kadaisiyar = labourers.
[60:1:9] APPSC G1 2024: "Vellalars were **rich peasants** who formed the **fourth caste**" (distractors: poets, labourers, forest tribes).
## SUB 2 | Sangam Customs and Terms
[60:2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [60:2:0.0] Vattakirutal | A king defeated in battle commits ritual suicide by starving himself to death
  [60:2:0.1] Kalavu | Pre-marital (secret) love
  [60:2:0.2] Karpu | Married life / wedded chastity
  [60:2:0.3] Pukal | Glory – the hero's goal in puram poems
  [60:2:0.4] Nadukal | Hero stone for a warrior who fell in battle
[60:2:1] UPSC 2023: Vattakirutal = ritual suicide of a defeated king by starvation.
## SUB 3 | Religion
[60:3:0] - Chief deity: **Seyon / Murugan**; his six abodes = **Arupadai Veedu**.
[60:3:1] - Other deities: **Mayon** (Vishnu), **Vendan** (Indra), **Varunan**, **Korravai** (goddess of war/victory).
[60:3:2] - **Hero stones (Nadukal):** raised for warriors who fell in battle; carved with their deeds and inscriptions; found across Tamil Nadu.
[60:3:3] - **Megalithic burials** were also practised.
## SUB 4 | Women
[60:4:0] - Women poets: **Avvaiyar, Nachchellaiyar, Kakkaipadiniyar** – contributed to Sangam poetry.
[60:4:1] - **Viraliyar** – women dancers at courts.
[60:4:2] - **Sati** was prevalent mainly in the higher strata of society; the life of widows was hard.

## SUB 1 | Origin of the Guptas
[61:1:0] - Origin is **uncertain and debated**; they rose from modest beginnings.
[61:1:1] - **Caste:** originally **Vaishyas**, later treated by brahmanas as **Kshatriyas**; a **Brahmana** origin is argued from the **Dharana gotra** of Prabhavatigupta (Junagadh does not settle it).
[61:1:2] - **Original home:** Sri Gupta ruled a small part of **North Bengal and South Bihar**; early territory = **Magadha, Prayag and eastern UP**.

## SUB 3 | Chandragupta I (c. 319–335 Ce)
[61:3:0] - First important king; capital **Pataliputra**; ruled South Bihar, eastern UP (**Saketa, Prayaga**), Bengal.
[61:3:1] - **Marriage:** **Kumaradevi**, a **Lichchhavi** princess (Nepal) – gave legitimacy, prestige and power.
[61:3:2] - Started the **Gupta Era (Gupta Samvat)** in **319–320 CE** = date of his accession; ⚠ APPSC key (2010) gave Sri Gupta.
[61:3:3] - **First Gupta ruler to issue coins** – gold dinaras of the **king-queen type** (Chandragupta I + Kumaradevi), in the joint names of king, queen and Lichchhavis. No coins of Sri Gupta or Ghatotkacha are known.
[61:3:4] - **First** to take the title **Maharajadhiraja**.
[61:3:5] Kumaradevi was the queen of **Chandragupta I**, not Ghatotkacha. Chandragupta I did **not** defeat the Sakas or issue silver coins – that was Chandragupta II (G1 2024 statement I false).
