  [0:0.3] Mantri | Waqia-navis (Waknis) | Intelligence, king's diary of daily doings and court happenings, household
  [0:0.4] Senapati | Sar-i-Naubat (Sarnobat) | Military commander
  [0:0.5] Sumant | Dabir | Foreign affairs; master of ceremonies, received envoys
  [0:0.6] Nyayadhish | – | Chief justice
  [0:0.7] Panditrao | Danadhyaksha | Religious affairs and charitable grants
[0:1] - **Shivaji's council of eight (by 1674)** – Maratha, not Gupta, Chola, Vijayanagara, Harsha or Mughal (UPSC 1995, IAS 1998, BPSC 2019 …).
[0:2] - **Advisory only**: each minister was **directly responsible to the Chhatrapati**; Shivaji could accept or reject advice; not a cabinet – so "each held **independent** charge" is false (UPPCS 1997: A true, R false). Ashtapradhan advisory vs the hierarchical Mughal wazir system.
[0:3] - Posts **not hereditary**; ministers transferable. All except **Panditrao and Nyayadhish** led campaigns. The Peshwa became hereditary and supreme only later (1720 onwards).
[0:4] - **All four Persian titles – Mazumdar, Dabir, Waqia-navis, Surnis – were Ashtapradhan officers** (CGPSC 2017).
[0:5] - **Chitnis** = secretary who **assisted the king with correspondence** (CDS 2002).
[0:6] - **Sarnobat** also = military officer of a fort; **Deshmukh** (pargana) collected **land revenue** for the state (APPSC G1 2019).

## SUB 1 | Shivaji's Career – Birth, Guru, Forts, Bijapur and the Mughals
[1:0] - **Born at Shivneri fort** near Pune, **19 February 1630** (Maharashtra government date; PMF); Jadunath Sarkar's **1627** gives the key "**1627, 1674**" (UPPCS 2015). Parents **Shahaji Bhonsle and Jijabai**; **Dadaji Kondadev** = keeper of the **Pune jagir** and tutor.
[1:1] - **Pune jagir:** first granted to grandfather Maloji by the **Nizam Shahis** (1595); **Shahaji held it as a mansabdar of Bijapur (Adil Shahi)** after 1636 – the key "Adil Shahi" (MPPSC 2010).
[1:2] - **Guru:** **Samarth Ramdas** (Dasbodh, Shivastuti); saint contemporary **Tukaram**. Ramdas, not Eknath (died 1599), is the APPSC G1 2012 answer.
[1:3] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [1:3.0] 1646 | Torna – first fort, from Bijapur, at age 16
  [1:3.1] 1656 | Raigad (and Javli) from the Mores of Javli – later capital
  [1:3.2] 1659 | Afzal Khan (Bijapur) killed with a wagh nakh at the foot of Pratapgad
  [1:3.3] 1660 | Siddi Jauhar besieges Panhala; escape to Vishalgad
  [1:3.4] 1663 | Night raid on Shaista Khan (Aurangzeb's maternal uncle, sent 1660) at Pune (Lal Mahal)
  [1:3.5] 1664, 1670 | Surat sacked twice
  [1:3.6] 1665 | Treaty of Purandar with Raja Jai Singh of Amber (with Diler Khan): 23 of 35 forts ceded; Sambhaji to Mughal service
  [1:3.7] 1666 | Agra – house arrest at Jaipur Bhawan; escaped with Sambhaji in fruit/sweet baskets
  [1:3.8] 1672 | Salher – Mughals beaten in open battle
  [1:3.9] 1674 | Coronation at Raigad (6 June)
  [1:3.10] 1677 | Hyderabad visit; Karnataka campaign – Gingee, Vellore (from Venkoji/Ekoji), Ballari
  [1:3.11] 1680 | Died (3 April)
[1:4] - After Agra he **recaptured** most Purandar forts (Purandar, Lohagarh, Mahuli …) – but **Shivneri**, held by the Mughals, was **never retaken** (UPPSC 2009).
[1:5] - **Coronation:** priest **Gaga Bhatt** (Vishweshwar) of Banaras; titles "**Shri Raja Shiva Chhatrapati**" and **Haindava Dharmoddharak** (not "Hindavi Sultan"); era **Rajyabhisheka Shaka**.
[1:6] - **Golconda alliance (1677, AP angle):** with **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** via **Madanna (Mir Jumla)** and **Akkanna (Sar Lashkar)** – Golconda to fund the southern conquests and share the spoils (Shivaji did not share).
[1:7] - **Causes of the Maratha rise:** Sahyadri geography (**geographical security**), **Bhakti saints** (religious consciousness), service in **Bijapur and Ahmadnagar** (political awakening) – all of the above (UPPSC 1992); Aurangzeb's 27-year Deccan war **drained** Mughal finances.
[1:8] - **Big Deshmukhs opposed Shivaji** – they wanted to stay feudal lords of Bijapur (UPPCS 2024: A and R true, R explains A).

## SUB 2 | Revenue System – Chauth, Sardeshmukhi and Land Terms
[2:0] TABLE head: Levy | Rate | Nature
  [2:0.0] Chauth | 25% (one-fourth) of land revenue | Protection money levied on neighbouring territories outside direct Maratha rule (Mughalai) to be spared Maratha raids
  [2:0.1] Sardeshmukhi | Additional 10% | Claimed by the king's hereditary right as Sardeshmukh
[2:1] - The pair **Chauth and Sardeshmukhi** = Maratha taxation (not iqta–jagir, zat–sawar, polaj–parauti) (SSC CGL 2019).
[2:2] - Funded expansion and political hegemony **without the cost of direct governance**. Recognised by the **Mughals (1719)** and the **Nizam at Palkhed (1728)**; the 1795 chauth burden pushed the Nizam into the Subsidiary Alliance (1798).
[2:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [2:3.0] Ryotwari-type settlement | State dealt directly with the cultivator; demand 30%, later 40%
  [2:3.1] Kathi | Measuring rod – Annaji Datto's survey
  [2:3.2] Jirayat / Bagayat | Dry land / irrigated land
  [2:3.3] Saranjam | Service land grants – abolished by Shivaji, restored under Shahu and the Peshwas
  [2:3.4] Upari | Tenancy tenure of tenants-at-will (no hereditary rights); Thalkari / Mirasdar held hereditary rights
[2:4] - **Jagirs abolished** – officials paid in **cash**; hereditary rights of **deshmukhs and deshpandes** (watandars) curbed.
[2:5] TABLE head: Unit | Official
  [2:5.0] Prant (province) | Subedar / Sarsubedar
  [2:5.1] Pargana, Tarf | Deshmukh, Deshpande
  [2:5.2] Mauza (village) | Patil (head), Kulkarni (accountant)

## SUB 3 | Military, Navy, Coins and Records
[3:0] - **Guerrilla warfare (Ganimi Kava)** with swift cavalry in the Sahyadri; chain of **hill forts on the table mountains** of the Western Deccan – both statements true (CDS 2012).
[3:1] - **Over 300 forts**; each under three officers of equal rank – **Havaldar** (garrison commander), **Sabnis** (records), **Sarnaubat** (military).
[3:2] TABLE head: Fort type | Examples
  [3:2.0] Giri Durg (hill) | Raigad, Pratapgad, Torna, Rajgad, Sinhagad, Panhala
  [3:2.1] Bhu Durg (land) | Vishalgad
  [3:2.2] Jal Durg (sea) | Sindhudurg, Vijaydurg, Khanderi, Suvarnadurg, Kolaba
[3:3] - Cavalry: **Bargirs** – horses and arms from the **state**; **Shiledars** – **own horses**. Infantry: **Mavalis**.
[3:4] - **Navy:** sea forts guarded the **Konkan**; Shivaji = "**Father of the Indian Navy**".
[3:5] - **Cannons and ammunition** were obtained from the **Portuguese** (treaty supplies from Goa) (SSC 2011).
[3:6] - **UNESCO 2025:** "**Maratha Military Landscapes of India**" (12 forts) inscribed as World Heritage.
[3:7] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:7.0] Hon / Shivrai | Gold hon; copper Shivrai
  [3:7.1] Rajvyavahar Kosh | Replaced Persian official terms with Sanskrit
  [3:7.2] Modi script | Cursive script of Maratha (Marathi) documents – not Wodeyar, Zamorin or Hoysala

## SUB 4 | Successors of Shivaji – Sambhaji, Rajaram, Tarabai, Shahu
[4:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Period | Key steps
  [4:0.0] Sambhaji | 1680-89 | Won the succession fight with Rajaram (backed by Shivaji's widow Soyarabai); sheltered Prince Akbar; captured at Sangameshwar and executed by Aurangzeb (1689)
  [4:0.1] Rajaram | 1689-1700 | Resisted from Gingee (Jinji); created the post of Pratinidhi
  [4:0.2] Tarabai (regent for Shivaji II) | 1700-07 | Rajaram's widow; led the struggle against the Mughals from 1700; led the Marathas when Aurangzeb died (1707)
  [4:0.3] Shahu | 1707-49 | Sambhaji's son; released by Bahadur Shah I (1707); beat Tarabai at Khed (1707); ruled from Satara; made Balaji Vishwanath Peshwa (1713)
[4:1] - **Order (UPPSC 2005):** **Sambhaji → Rajaram → Shivaji II (Tarabai) → Shahu**.
[4:2] - **Treaty of Warna (1731):** between **Sambhaji II of Kolhapur and Shahu of Satara** – Tarabai's line settled at **Kolhapur**.
[4:3] - **Current linkage:** **Aurangabad was renamed Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar** (state decision 2022, notified 2023) after Sambhaji; Osmanabad became Dharashiv.
[4:4] - Peak of Maratha power: **Attock to Cuttack**; major northern expansion under **Baji Rao I**.
