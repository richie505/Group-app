## SUB 0 | Court Poets – Errana and Srinatha
[0:0] # Errana (Errapragada)
[0:1] - **Patron:** court poet of the founder **Prolaya Vema Reddi** (APPSC G2 2011, 2012; APPSC PYQ ×5) – not Anavota, Anavema, Allada Reddi or Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka.
[0:2] - **Dedication:** his **Harivamsam** is dedicated to **Prolaya Vema**.
[0:3] - **Kavitrayam:** **last of the Kavitrayam**; completed the Telugu Mahabharata (**remaining Aranya Parva – Aranya Parva shesham**).
[0:4] - **Works:** **Nrisimha (Narasimha) Puranam** (APPSC G2 2017; PYQ ×2), Harivamsam, Ramayana.
[0:5] - **Titles:** **Prabandha Parameswara**, **Sambhudasa**.
[0:6] - **⚠ Trap:** the **Vidyadhikari** (education officer) post belonged to **Srinatha**, not Errana – a stem that calls Errana "Vidyadhikari" still points to his patron Prolaya Vema.
[0:7] # Srinatha (c. 1365–1441)
[0:8] - **Patron and office:** **Vidyadhikari** (education officer) under **Pedakomati Vema Reddi** (APPSC G1 2002; G2 2001, 2002; Welfare Officers 2017; PYQ ×2); composed his Sanskrit inscriptions.
[0:9] - **Title:** **Kavi Sarvabhauma**; also Andhra Bhasha Naishadhabhava, Sarvavidya Parangata.
[0:10] - **At Vijayanagara:** defeated **Gauda Dindima Bhatta**, court poet of **Deva Raya II** → **Kanakabhishekam**, **Ganda Penderam** and the Kavisarvabhauma honour.
[0:11] - **Later life:** after 1424 joined the Rajahmundry court of **Veerabhadra Reddi**; after 1448, poverty in Palnadu.
[0:12] TABLE head: Work | Note
  [0:12.0] Haravilasam | Sea-borne trade; to Avachi Tippayya Setti
  [0:12.1] Palnati Veera Charitra | Life of the Palnadu people (PYQ)
  [0:12.2] Sringara Naishadham | From Sriharsha's Naishadhiyacharita; to Mamidi (Madiki) Singana
  [0:12.3] Bhimakhandam (Bhimeswara Puranam) | Praises Bhimeswara of Draksharamam
  [0:12.4] Kasikhandam | Religious; to Veerabhadra Reddi's court
  [0:12.5] Sivaratri Mahatyam, Maruttarat Charitra | Other works
[0:13] - **Kavya Yuga:** Srinatha is said to have opened the Kavya (prabandha) age; his writings are cited as evidence of **paper** use in the **15th century** (APPSC G2 2012).
[0:14] - **⚠ Not his:** **Madanavilasabanam** (Naganatha, Anavota's court) is NOT by Srinatha.

## SUB 2 | Society, Festivals and Women
[2:0] # Vasantotsavam and Dance
[2:1] - **Vasantotsavam (spring festival):** the **most popular festival** of the Reddi period – colours and scents sprinkled (Holi-like) (APPSC G2 PYQ).
[2:2] - **Popularised by:** **Kumaragiri Reddi**, who held it every year → title **Karpura Vasantarayalu** (Welfare Officers 2017 ×2); Anavema too was called Vasantaraya.
[2:3] - **Funding:** merchant **Avachi Tippayya Setti**, made head of the **Sugandha Bhandagara** (scent store) for the festival (recorded in Haravilasam).
[2:4] - **Court dancer:** **Lakumadevi** – **Bharatanatyam**, at **Kumaragiri's** court (APPSC G2 2012; PYQ ×3) – not Machaladevi, Chellava or Muddupalani.
[2:5] - **Literature on him:** **C. Narayana Reddy** wrote **Karpura Vasanta Rayalu** on Kumaragiri.
[2:6] # Women and Tanks
[2:7] TABLE head: Woman | Fact
  [2:7.0] Surambika (Suryambika) | Queen of Pedakomati Vema; dug the Santana Sagaram (Santana Payonidhi) tank at Phirangipuram (APPSC G2 2019; PYQ ×2)
  [2:7.1] Lakumadevi | Court dancer of Kumaragiri Reddi
  [2:7.2] Anitalli | Issued the Kaluvacheru inscription (1423)
[2:8] - **Tank inscription:** composed by **Srinatha**; her son **Racha Vema** cut the **Jaganobbaganda** canals to feed it.
[2:9] - **⚠ Trap:** **Govardhana Samudram**, Anna Samudram and Vemana Samudram are NOT Surambika's tank – the answer is **Santana Sagaram**.
[2:10] # Social Life
[2:11] - **Castes:** warrior-farmer backbone **Kammas, Kapus, Reddis, Velamas**; traders **Komatis**; tax-free Brahmin **agraharas**.
[2:12] - **Customs:** sati, devadasis, cockfights, ram fights, hero worship (Veerapuja); belief in **Parusavedi** (alchemy).
[2:13] - **Devadasis:** trained in music, dance and ritual; respected performers at **Shiva temples** (incl. Pancharamas).
[2:14] - **Time:** measured with clocks – the **Ghatika Yantra** (water clock), called **Ghatikagara** in the APPSC key (Telugu "gadiyaram" = clock).
[2:15] - **Music:** instrument **Jalakaranda** – **Dharmasagara inscription**; desi dances Jakkini, Perani; foreign dance Parasika.

## SUB 3 | Rulers, Capitals and Chronology
[3:0] # The Reddi Kingdom
[3:1] - **Period:** about **1325–1448 CE** – **Kondavidu line c. 1325–1424**; **Rajahmundry line 1402–1448**.
[3:2] - **Successor state:** one of the three states after **1323** (fall of the Kakatiyas) – **Reddis, Musunuris, Recherla Velamas**.
[3:3] - **Called:** the **"second golden age" of Telugu** culture – kings were themselves authors.
[3:4] - **Features:** **Panta** clan (Deseti kula); emblem **Bull**; family deity **Mula Guramma**; royal language **Sanskrit**.
[3:5] # Kondavidu Rulers in Order
[3:6] TABLE head: Ruler | Period | Key fact
  [3:6.0] Prolaya Vema Reddi (founder) | 1325–1353 | Capital Addanki; expelled Tughlaq forces; 74 forts; Errana
  [3:6.1] Anavota (Anapota) Reddi | 1353–1364 | Shifted capital to Kondavidu; developed Motupalli; abolished gold tax
  [3:6.2] Anavema Reddi (greatest) | 1364–1386 | Most charitable; beat Kalinga Gajapatis; Veera Siromandapam
  [3:6.3] Kumaragiri Reddi | 1386–1402 | Vasantarajiyam; Vasantotsavam; Lakumadevi
  [3:6.4] Pedakomati Vema Reddi | 1402–1420 | Sangeeta Chintamani; Srinatha
  [3:6.5] Racha Vema Reddi (last) | 1420–1424 | Puriti Pannu; killed by his people
[3:7] - **Order (APPSC G2 Mains 2025; Welfare Officers):** Prolaya Vema → **Anavota → Anavema → Kumaragiri → Pedakomati Vema** → Racha Vema.
[3:8] - **Prolaya Vema's reign:** up to **1353** (Mani Kesavara inscription) – the "1325–1353" span (Inter Board 2012).
[3:9] - **Kumaragiri succeeded Anavema**; Pedakomati occupied Kondavidu (1402) because Kumaragiri was childless.
[3:10] # Capitals and Forts
[3:11] - **Sequence:** **Addanki → Kondaveedu (Kondavidu) → Rajamahendravaram (Rajahmundry)**.
[3:12] - **Who shifted Addanki → Kondavidu:** **Anavota (Anapota) Reddi**, son of Prolaya Vema – **Manyapuram inscription** (APPSC G2 2012; PYQ) – not Anavema, Kumaragiri or Pedakomati.
[3:13] TABLE head: Capital / fort | Region | Feature
  [3:13.0] Addanki | Prakasam | First capital, Prolaya Vema
  [3:13.1] Kondavidu | Guntur (near Chilakaluripet) | Replaced Addanki; later fought over by Gajapatis and Vijayanagara
  [3:13.2] Rajahmundry | Godavari | Second seat, 1402–1448
  [3:13.3] Kondapalli | Krishna | Hill fort credited to Prolaya Vema
[3:14] - **Forts:** Prolaya Vema built **74 forts**, including **Kondaveedu, Kondapalli and Dharanikota**.
[3:15] - **Kondapalli fort:** main gate **Dargah Darwaza** (tomb of Gulab Shah); **Golconda Darwaza** at the other end; palace **Tanisha Mahal**.
[3:16] - **⚠ Trap:** Kondavidu is in Guntur – NOT in East Godavari or near Eluru.
[3:17] # Prolaya Vema Reddi – Founder
[3:18] - **Career:** served **Prataparudra**, then in **Kapaya Nayaka's** court (Anitalli / Kaluvacheru inscription); founded the kingdom in **1324/1325** after expelling **Tughlaq** forces from coastal Andhra.
[3:19] - **Brother Mallareddi:** took **Motupalli**, repulsing the Bahmani **Hasan Gangu** (1347).
[3:20] # Anavema and Kumaragiri
[3:21] - **Anavema:** with the **Padmanayakas and Vijayanagara** beat the **Kalinga Gajapatis**; took **Nidadavolu** (gave it to Choda Bhima), **Rajahmundry, Pithapuram, Simhachalam, Konaseema** – not Kandukur; minister **Mamidi Peddanamatya**, army chief **Chennamanayaka**; **most charitable** Reddi king.
[3:22] - **Kumaragiri:** **Pedakomati Vema**, who opposed his accession, was made **feudatory of Chandolu** and heir; brother-in-law **Kataya Vema** conquered the east up to **Cuttack**.
[3:23] - **Rajahmundry division:** first under his son **Anavota**, then **Kataya Vema (1395)**.
[3:24] - **1402:** Pedakomati seized Kondavidu; Kumaragiri fled to Rajahmundry.
[3:25] - **Pedakomati Vema:** recovered territory from **Vijayanagara (1404)**; died fighting the Velamas (1420).
[3:26] - **Racha Vema:** last Kondavidu king; killed as a tyrant – **Kondaveeti Kaifiyat**.

## SUB 4 | Trade – Motupalli Port and Haravilasam
[4:0] - **Haravilasam (Srinatha):** the Reddi-period work giving information on **sea-borne (foreign) trade**, imports and exports (APPSC G2 2011 ×2, 2012, 2019; G2 Mains; PYQ) – not Harivamsam, Kasikhandam or Bhimeswara Puranam.
[4:1] - **Dedicated to:** merchant **Avachi Tippayya Setti**, famous for **overseas trade** (China, Sinhala, Sumatra, Malaya).
[4:2] - **Assertion–Reason:** Haravilasam gives sea-trade information (A) and is dedicated to Tippayya Setti (R) – **both true, R not the explanation**.
[4:3] - **Motupalli:** chief Reddi port; taken by **Mallaya Reddi**; dock developed by **Anavota**.
[4:4] - **Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam:** issued by **Somaya**, minister of **Anavota**, to protect and encourage trade (bilingual Telugu–Tamil; lower duties).
[4:5] - **Motupalli epigraph:** fixes **tariff rates** on port trade.
[4:6] - **Imports:** Chinese goods, sandalwood, camphor, spices; elephants from Sri Lanka.
[4:7] - **Traders:** **Komatis** ran inland and coastal trade; trade centres Penugonda, Kondaveedu, Rajahmundry, Draksharama.
[4:8] - **Earlier route:** the Buddhist **Vinayavatthu** mentions the **"Andhra Path"**.
[4:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen post kakatiyas p.10, p.13; NextGen AP History - APPSC Exams 08 p.23-26; Rocket Sheets p.9; app notes 1:98 (sub 6)
## SUB 5 | Religion and Temple Building
[5:0] - **Popular religion:** **Veerashaivism** (APPSC G2 2011; PYQ); the kings patronised **Pashupata Shaivism** – the tempting wrong option.
[5:1] - **Ghoderayas:** Shaiva gurus who spread Shaivism from **Tripurantakam**; the Reddi kings were their disciples.
[5:2] - **Vaishnavism:** **Vadagalai** matha at **Ahobilam**; **Kumaragiri and Kataya Vema** were Vaishnava patrons.
[5:3] - **Srisailam practices:** Bhrugupatham (leaping from the hill); **Champudu Gullu** at Tripurantakam.

## SUB 7 | Administration and Taxes
[7:0] - **Puriti Pannu:** tax on **childbirth**, levied by **Racha Vema**, the last Kondavidu king, who was killed by his people for it (APPSC G2 2019; PYQ ×2) – not Kumaragiri, Kataya Vema or Anavota.
[7:1] TABLE head: Tax | Meaning
  [7:1.0] Puriti Pannu | Childbirth – Racha Vema
  [7:1.1] Kanika | Presents / gifts to king or officials
  [7:1.2] Pullari | Cattle-grazing tax (Kakatiya usage)
  [7:1.3] Illari | House tax
  [7:1.4] Ari | Land tax
  [7:1.5] Meruka | Tax on wetlands
  [7:1.6] Gold tax | Abolished by Anavota
[7:2] - **Land revenue:** Bhumi Sistu – the main income; land measured with the Kesarpati gada.
[7:3] - **Village:** **12 Ayagars** – **Karnam** (accountant), **Reddi / Peddakapu** (headman), **Talari** (watchman); paid in **Manyam** land or **Mera** (crop share).
[7:4] - **Exempt:** temple lands, Brahmin grants and Ayagar lands.
