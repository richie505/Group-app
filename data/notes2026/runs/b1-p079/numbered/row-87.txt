## SUB 0 | Army and Navy
[0:0] - **Army:** **elephants, cavalry, infantry** (the three limbs) **and a navy** – **chariots were not part of it** (Police Constable 2016).
[0:1] - **Naval power:** the **Cholas** were the South Indian state famous for naval power (UPPSC 2004 ×2); **Rajaraja I** built the navy.
[0:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:2.0] Kaikkolar | Royal infantry regiments

[0:3] - Rajaraja I's navy destroyed the Chera fleet at **Trivandrum (Kandalur)**; Rajendra I's 1025 fleet took **Kadaram = Kedah**; the Bay of Bengal became a **"Chola Lake"**.

## SUB 1 | Kingship and Chola Records
[1:0] - **Successor named in the king's lifetime:** the **Cholas** – the yuvaraja was made **co-regent** (UPPSC 2001); Rajendra I co-regent **1012**, king 1014.
[1:1] - **⚠ Trap:** "the successor was named only after the king's death" is false.
[1:2] - **King's image:** titles *ko*, *perumal*, *rajarajadhiraja*; praised as warrior, patron of arts and protector of **varnashrama dharma**; Rajaraja I = **Ulakalanda Perumal** – measured the earth like **Vishnu's three strides**.
[1:3] - **Royal orders:** a **Sanskrit preamble** (genealogy, conquests) + a **Tamil operative part**.
[1:4] - **Temple-wall records:** Rajaraja I and Rajendra I had their victories inscribed on **temple walls** – the main reason we know far more about the Cholas than their predecessors.

## SUB 2 | Land Classes, Taniyur and Revenue
[2:0] # Land classes
[2:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [2:1.0] Eripatti | Land whose revenue maintained the village tank
  [2:1.1] Brahmadeya | Rent-free land gifted to Brahmins (sabha villages)
  [2:1.2] Vellanvagai | Land of non-Brahmin peasant proprietors (ur villages)
  [2:1.3] Devadana / Tirunamattukkani | Land to temples
  [2:1.4] Shalabhoga | Land for a school
  [2:1.5] Pallichchandam | Land to Jaina institutions
  [2:1.6] Ghatikas | Colleges attached to temples
[2:2] - **Taniyur:** a **very large village** (an important brahmadeya or nagaram) **administered as a single, separate unit**, outside the nadu (APPSC G1) – not kurram, kottam or nadu.
[2:3] - **UPSC 2016 pairs:** Eripatti ✓, Ghatikas ✓; "Taniyurs = villages donated to a single Brahmin or group of Brahmins" ✗ → **A and C (1 and 3)**.
[2:4] - **Land rights (kani):** **karanmai** = right to cultivate; **mitachi** = superior possessory right; **kaniyudaiyar** = land-owning farmers; **ulukudi** = tenants.
[2:5] # Revenue
[2:6] - **Rate:** land revenue normally **1/6 of the produce** (like the Pallavas and Kakatiyas); collected by village assemblies in cash, kind or both; **Rajaraja I's land survey**.
[2:7] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [2:7.0] Kadamai | Land revenue in kind
  [2:7.1] Antarayam | Rural tax in cash
  [2:7.2] Eccoru | Duty to feed visiting officials
  [2:7.3] Nettal | Compulsory labour
  [2:7.4] Vetti, Muttaiyal, Kudimai | Forced labour services
  [2:7.5] Irai / Ayam | Land tax / royal dues
[2:8] - **Remission:** exemptions granted through the **ur**; **Kulottunga I = Sungam Tavirtta Chola** (abolished tolls).
[2:9] - **Modern parallel:** **DILRMP** first phase ran **2008–2016**.

## SUB 3 | Officials and Provinces
[3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:0.0] Perundaram / Sirudaram | Highest / lower officials
  [3:0.1] Tirumandira-olai | Wrote the king's oral orders on palm leaf
  [3:0.2] Olai-nayagam | Checked and countersigned orders
  [3:0.3] Adhikari | Royal officer supervising local bodies
  [3:0.4] Nattar | Members of the nadu assembly
[3:1] - **Order (small to large):** **Ur → Nadu (kurram) → Kottam / Valanadu → Mandalam** – a kottam is above the nadu, not below it.
[3:2] - **Eight mandalams**, each under a viceroy, often a **prince**; officials paid in **revenue-bearing land**.
[3:3] - **Samanta:** a defeated king restored after accepting overlordship, tribute and military duty; tribute-paying lands = **samanta (vassal) territories**.

## SUB 4 | Irrigation and the Temple
[4:0] - **Eri variyam:** managed village tanks and water distribution; repairs paid from **eripatti** land.
[4:1] - **Kallanai (Grand Anicut)** on the Kaveri = **Karikala** (Sangam); **tank irrigation** was a major feature of the Chola economy (also Vijayanagara, Kakatiyas).
[4:2] - **Araghatta:** a water wheel with earthen pots tied to its rim, used for irrigation.
[4:3] - **Temple:** biggest landholder, employer and lender, and the **meeting place of the village assembly**; Rajaraja I made the Cholamandalam sabhas serve the **Brihadeeswara, Thanjavur**.
[4:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval India p.414; Rocket Indian History #89; app notes 1:87 (sub 6)
