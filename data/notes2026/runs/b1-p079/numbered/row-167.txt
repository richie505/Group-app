## SUB 0 | Subsidiary Alliance – Wellesley, Terms, Aims and the Order of States
[0:0] # Author and aims
[0:1] - **Lord Wellesley (Governor-General 1798-1805)** used it to build the empire; he did **not invent** it – Dupleix first hired out troops, Awadh's **1765** treaty "anticipated" it – but made **cession of territory for the subsidy** the general rule (its final shape).
[0:2] - **Aims:** keep the **French** out (fear of **Napoleon**); bring the states into dependence = **British paramountcy** without direct rule; a large army kept **at the states' expense**. A **fixed income** for the Company was **not** an aim (UPSC 2018 ×2: "does not apply" = fixed income).
[0:3] - **Rajput states (1818):** the Company's aim = to **establish British sovereignty / paramountcy** (RAS 1992); the Rajputs themselves sought protection from the **Marathas and Pindaris**; treaties negotiated by **Charles Metcalfe** under Lord Hastings.
[0:4] # Terms
[0:5] - 1. Permanent **subsidiary force** in the state, paid by a subsidy – or, if unpaid, by **ceding territory**.
[0:6] - 2. A **British Resident** at the court.
[0:7] - 3. **No other Europeans** in service without the Company's consent.
[0:8] - 4. **No war or negotiation** with other states without the Governor-General = **foreign affairs surrendered**.
[0:9] - 5. In return the Company **defends the ruler** and promises **non-interference** in internal affairs.
[0:10] - **Effects:** rulers lost independence and revenue, became weak and irresponsible, subjects exploited; unpaid subsidies → cessions (the **Nizam's Ceded Districts**).
[0:11] # Order of acceptance
[0:12] TABLE head: State | Year
  [0:12.0] Hyderabad (Nizam Ali Khan) | 1 Sep 1798 (and Oct 1800) – the first
  [0:12.1] Mysore (restored Wodeyars) | 1799
  [0:12.2] Tanjore | Oct 1799
  [0:12.3] Awadh | Nov 1801
  [0:12.4] Peshwa Baji Rao II (Treaty of Bassein) | 31 Dec 1802 – first Maratha
  [0:12.5] Bhonsle (Berar/Nagpur) | Dec 1803
  [0:12.6] Sindhia | Feb 1804
  [0:12.7] Jodhpur, Jaipur, Bundi, Bharatpur, Holkar | 1818 – Holkar the last Maratha
[0:13] - **First state (asked ×9):** **Hyderabad, 1798** – APPSC G2 2002, 2008 ×2, JL 2011, IAS 1994, AP History PYQ; **⚠ APPSC Inspector of Factories key gave Awadh** – Awadh's 1765 defence treaty only anticipated the system and Awadh joined Wellesley's system in **1801**.
[0:14] - **Year with the Nizam = 1798** (G2-A 1991); first Maratha sardar = **Peshwa Baji Rao II** (1802).
[0:15] - **Not accepted under Wellesley:** **Holkar** of Indore resisted (signed only in 1818 after defeat); **Tipu Sultan** refused → Fourth Mysore War (1799).
[0:16] - **Rayalaseema** came to the British **through the Subsidiary Alliance** – ceded by the Nizam on **12 Oct 1800** in lieu of the subsidy.
[0:17] # Policies towards the states
[0:18] TABLE head: Policy | Author
  [0:18.0] Ring-fence – defend neighbours' frontiers (Awadh as buffer shielding Bengal from Marathas and Afghans) | Warren Hastings
  [0:18.1] Subsidiary Alliance | Wellesley (1798)
  [0:18.2] Subordinate isolation | Lord Hastings (1813-23)
  [0:18.3] Doctrine of Lapse – annex states without a natural male heir | Dalhousie
  [0:18.4] Subordinate union – princes as "breakwaters to the storm"; right of adoption recognised | Crown, after 1858
[0:19] - Other "policy" pairs used as distractors: Dhamma – Ashoka; Blood and Iron – Balban; Market control – Alauddin Khalji; Blue Water – Almeida.
[0:20] - **Grounds for intervention** in states: succession disputes, misgovernment, and the need to fulfil their **international commitments**.

## SUB 1 | Asaf Jahi Nizams – Reigns and Firsts
[1:0] TABLE head: Nizam | Reign | Key point
  [1:0.0] Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan, Nizam-ul-Mulk, Asaf Jah I | from 1724 | independent after defeating Mubariz Khan at Shakar Kheda; founder (Chin Qilich Khan)
  [1:0.1] Nizam Ali Khan (Asaf Jah II) | 1762-1803 | capital Aurangabad → Hyderabad, 1763; Circars leased (1766); Guntur given (1788); first Subsidiary Alliance (1798); Ceded Districts (1800)
  [1:0.2] Sikandar Jah (Asaf Jah III) | 1803-29 | Secunderabad cantonment (near Hussain Sagar) named after him
  [1:0.3] Nasir-ud-Daula (Asaf Jah IV) | 1829-57 | Wahabi conspiracy 1839 (Mubariz-ud-Daula arrested); Berar Treaty 1853 (Berar, Raichur, Dharashiv/Osmanabad); Salar Jung I made Diwan (1853); died 16 May 1857
  [1:0.4] Afzal-ud-Daula (Asaf Jah V) | 1857-69 | Nizam during the 1857 revolt in Hyderabad; loyal → title "Star of India"
  [1:0.5] Mir Mahbub Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VI) | 1869-1911 | youngest Asaf Jahi ruler – Nizam as an infant (turned 18 only in 1884); Salar Jung I regent
  [1:0.6] Mir Osman Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VII) | 1911-48 | last Nizam; Razakar struggle for an independent Hyderabad → Police Action (Sept 1948)

[1:3] - **Residency attack:** **Turrebaz (Tura Baz) Khan**, a Rohilla jamedar, with ~5,000 men, to free **Chida Khan**; repulsed by **Col. Davidson**; the Nizam and Salar Jung stayed loyal.
[1:4] > Trap: Sikandar Jah = Secunderabad; Mahbub Ali Khan = youngest; Afzal-ud-Daula = 1857 + Star of India; Osman Ali Khan = Razakars.

## SUB 2 | Salar Jung I and the Hyderabad Reforms (1853-1883)
[2:0] - **Mir Turab Ali Khan = Salar Jung I**, made **Diwan on 31 May 1853** (after Siraj-ul-Mulk) by **Nasir-ud-Daula** → the **Salar Jung family rose under Nasir-ud-Daula**; served **three Nizams** (Nasir-ud-Daula, Afzal-ud-Daula, Mahbub Ali Khan) till his death in **1883**.
[2:1] - **Revenue:** curbed the daftardars; **paid talukdars** replaced the **auctioning of revenue to contractors** = he **abolished revenue farming**; **Zillabandi (1865)** – districts under talukdars; Halisikka rupee, central mint at Hyderabad.
[2:2] - **Post-1857 offices (ATWO A-R):** more work in the Minister's **Dar-ul-Insha** → a **Munshi Khana** for correspondence with the **taluqdars**, beside the offices for the restored districts (**Azla-i-Mustarida**).
[2:3] # Judiciary
[2:4] TABLE head: Court | Role
  [2:4.0] Mahkama-i-Sadar | High Court of Hyderabad (controlled munsiffs, mir adils)
  [2:4.1] Majlis-i-Murafa | Supreme Court / Court of Appeal above it
  [2:4.2] Buzurg and Khurd Diwani Adalat | civil courts (senior, junior)
  [2:4.3] Faujdari Adalat | criminal court, chief judge titled Nazim
  [2:4.4] Darul Kazi / Mahkama-i-Sadarat | Muhammadan law / religious endowments
[2:5] - **Police (1865 on):** Mahkama-i-Kotwali; force = Nizamat; SP = Muhtamim, inspector = Amin, station = chauki.
[2:6] - **Education:** Darul-ulum (1855), City High School (1870), Chaderghat (1872), Madrasa-e-Aliya (1873); **Nizam College 1887**.
[2:7] - **Railways:** Hyderabad-Wadi line completed **1878** (NextGen; old app notes said 1874).
[2:8] - **Chanda Railway Scheme (1883):** the elite under **Dr Aghornath Chattopadhyaya** demanded its details; he was deported on **20 May 1883** → the **beginning of public political awakening** in Hyderabad.

## SUB 3 | Northern Circars Lease, Kandregula Jogipantulu and Guntur
[3:0] TABLE head: Year | Step
  [3:0.0] 1759 | Salabat Jang gave the British Machilipatnam, Nizampatnam, Vakal Mannar, part of Kondapalli
  [3:0.1] 1765 | Mughal Shah Alam II's farman, received by Clive, granted the five Circars – Nizam Ali refused it at first
  [3:0.2] 12 Nov 1766 | Treaty of Hyderabad (envoy Calliaud): Circars leased – ₹9 lakh a year or military aid
  [3:0.3] 1768 | Treaty of Masulipatnam: Nizam accepted Shah Alam's grant and resigned the Circars
  [3:0.4] 18 Sep 1788 | Guntur handed over by Nizam Ali Khan for ₹7 lakh, after Basalat Jang's death (1782)
[3:1] - **Negotiator (asked ×2):** **Kandregula Jogipantulu** – mediator between Nizam Ali and the Company's men at **Machilipatnam**; used the **Gollapudi pay mutiny** to propose a lease at **₹15 lakh** (1766 treaty fixed ₹9 lakh); Nizam made him **Majumdar of Rajamahendravaram**, the British "**Raja Bahadur**". **Col. Forde** took Machilipatnam from the French (1759) – not the negotiator.
[3:2] - **Circars of 1766:** Chicacole, Rajahmundry, Ellore, **Mustafanagar (Kondapalli)** – **not Kurnool** (Ceded District, 1800); **Guntur (Murtazanagar / Kondaveedu) = the fifth Circar**, excepted as Basalat Jang's estate.
[3:3] - **"Main point of difference between Nizam Ali and the English" (G2 2011 ×2):** **the Northern Sarkars** – the 1765 grant over his head, the 1766-68 treaties (NextGen previous papers). Guntur was the later, narrower dispute (1779 lease from Basalat Jang, handover 1788).

## SUB 4 | Road to 1798 – Kharda, Raymond and the Hyderabad Residency
[4:0] - Nizam beaten by the Marathas at **Kharda (1795)** → blamed **Sir John Shore's neutrality** → relied on **French battalions under Raymond** → **Wellesley** forced the alliance (1 Sep 1798).
[4:1] - **Resident James Kirkpatrick** reported the French battalions; built the Residency ("**Koti**"). The Resident controlled the Nizam's **foreign relations** and the subsidiary force.
[4:2] - **Russell Brigade** – reformed Hyderabad force raised by **Resident Henry Russell** (c. 1813).

## SUB 5 | Ceded Districts and Andhra Consequences
[5:0] - **12 Oct 1800:** the Nizam ceded the lands he had gained from Mysore – **Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary** – in lieu of the subsidy = **Dattamandalalu (Ceded Districts)**.
[5:1] - Name **Rayalaseema** (1928) proposed by **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao** at the **Nandyal Andhra Mahasabha**, presided by **Dr S. Radhakrishnan**.
[5:2] - **1801:** Nellore and Chittoor from the Carnatic Nawab → the whole of Andhra under the Company; **Telangana stayed under the Nizam**.
[5:3] - **Padmanabham (10 July 1794):** the Company defeated and killed **Chinna Vijayaramaraju** of Vizianagaram.
