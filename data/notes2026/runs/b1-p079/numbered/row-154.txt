## SUB 0 | Surat – the First Permanent Factory and the Mughal Court
[0:0] - **Surat (1613)** = the English Company's **first permanent factory in India** – on a **farman of Jahangir** (not Akbar, Shah Jahan or Aurangzeb); Pune, Goa, Pondicherry, Bangalore were never its first factory.
[0:1] - **Masulipatnam (1611)** came earlier but only as a temporary trading post (permanent factory 1616) – so "first factory in India" = **Surat**, "first in South India / Andhra" = **Machilipatnam**.
[0:2] - **Captain William Hawkins** – third voyage, landed at **Surat 1608**, at Jahangir's court (Agra) **1609**; first envoy of **James I**; permission withdrawn under Portuguese pressure.
[0:3] - **Battle of Swally (1612)**: **Captain Thomas Best** defeated the **Portuguese** off Surat → Jahangir's farman of **1613** (factory under Thomas Aldworth).
[0:4] - **Sir Thomas Roe (1615-19)** = ambassador of **King James I** to **Jahangir**; no commercial treaty, but privileges to set up factories at **Agra, Ahmedabad and Broach**; his chaplain was **Edward Terry**.
[0:5] - **Ralph Fitch (1583-86)** = first Englishman to visit **Akbar's** court (Agra, Fatehpur Sikri) – before Hawkins and Roe.
[0:6] - **Western Presidency**: seat first at **Surat**, shifted to **Bombay in 1687**.
[0:7] - **Factories of the 1st quarter of the 17th century**: **Broach** (yes, Roe's privileges); **Chicacole** and **Trichinopoly** – no (UPSC 2021: 1 only).
[0:8] TABLE head: Order | Person | Year | Emperor
  [0:8.0] 1 | Ralph Fitch | 1583-86 | Akbar
  [0:8.1] 2 | William Hawkins | 1608-11 | Jahangir
  [0:8.2] 3 | Thomas Best (Swally) | 1612 | Jahangir
  [0:8.3] 4 | Sir Thomas Roe | 1615-19 | Jahangir
[0:9] > Company formed under **Akbar** (1600), first factory under **Jahangir** (1613, Surat); Roe was sent by **James I**.

## SUB 1 | The Charter of 1600 and the Company's Formation
[1:0] - **31 December 1600**: **Queen Elizabeth I** granted the charter → the Company was founded in **1600** (not 1601, 1604 or 1607).
[1:1] - Name: "**The Governor and Company of Merchants of London Trading into the East Indies**"; promoted by the "**Merchant Adventurers**" (1599).
[1:2] - Charter gave only **exclusive trading rights** – a **15-year monopoly** between the **Cape of Good Hope** and the **Straits of Magellan** (made indefinite in 1609); **no territorial governance or legislative power**.
[1:3] - **Emperor of India in 1600 = Akbar** (1556-1605), contemporary of **Elizabeth I** (1558-1603).
[1:4] - Management: a **Governor + 24-member committee** (later Court of Directors); shareholders = General Court (later Court of Proprietors).
[1:5] - **First voyage 1602** under **James Lancaster** (flagship **Red Dragon**, capital £68,000) → factory at **Bantam** (Java), not India.
[1:6] - **Levant Company (1592)** traded with the Ottoman lands; the **East India Company** was the first chartered to trade with India; "The English Company trading to the East Indies" was the rival of **1698**.
[1:7] TABLE head: Year | Change
  [1:7.0] 1657 | Cromwell's charter – permanent joint stock
  [1:7.1] Charles II, James II | Coin money, jurisdiction, war and peace with non-Christians
  [1:7.2] 1708 | Rival companies merged – United Company of Merchants of England Trading to the East Indies
  [1:7.3] 1858 | Company rule ended – Government of India Act, 1858
[1:8] - Early objective: **eastern trade** (spices, textiles), paid for with **imported bullion**; secret of later success (UPPCS 1994): absence of nationalism, western-trained army – "all the above".

## SUB 2 | Madras – Francis Day and Fort St. George
[2:0] - **1639**: **Francis Day** (with **Andrew Cogan**), a member of the Masulipatnam council, got **Madraspatnam** on lease from the **Chandragiri** ruler **Venkatapati Raya III**, through the **Damerla brothers (Venkatadri, Ayyappa)** – not John Child, Roe or Oxenden.
[2:1] - **Fort St. George** at Madraspatnam (near **Mylapore**/San Thome): built from 1 March 1640, completed **23 April 1644** (St George's Day) = **first fort built by the English in India** and the **first fortified English settlement** (not Fort William, Mahim or Aguada).
[2:2] - **Chennapatnam** named after **Damerla Chennappa**; Madras = chief of east-coast factories (1641), **presidency 1684**, **first municipality in India (1687-88)**.
[2:3] - Main business of the early English settlements in Andhra and Fort St. George = **trade in locally woven piece-goods** (not conquest, spices or agriculture).
[2:4] - **Mid-18th century populations** of the fortified English settlements: **Madras about 3 lakh**, Calcutta 2 lakh, Bombay 70,000 → **Madras largest**.
[2:5] TABLE head: Presidency | Seat
  [2:5.0] Madras | Fort St. George
  [2:5.1] Bombay | Bombay (replaced Surat, 1687)
  [2:5.2] Bengal | Fort William, Calcutta

## SUB 3 | Bengal and Calcutta – Hugli, Job Charnock, Fort William
[3:0] - **1651**: **Shah Shuja** (subahdar) let the English trade in Bengal for Rs 3,000 a year → **first English factory in Bengal = Hugli (1651)**, then **Qasim Bazar**, Patna, Rajmahal (earlier Hariharpur and Balasore, 1633, are in **Odisha**).
[3:1] - **Job Charnock**, chief of the **Qasim Bazar** factory, returned to **Sutanuti** in **August 1690** → **founder of Calcutta** (not Charles Eyre, Aungier, Norris or John Surman).
[3:2] - **1698**: zamindari of **Sutanuti, Gobindapur and Kalikata** bought; **1700**: fortified settlement named **Fort William** – Sir **Charles Eyre** first president.
[3:3] - Fort built by the British at Calcutta = **Fort William** (Fort St. David is at Cuddalore; Fort St. George at Madras).
[3:4] - Europeans got the best **saltpetre (shora) and opium from Bihar**.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.68, 71-72 (book p.36, 39-40); old app notes row 154
## SUB 4 | Bombay – Dowry, Lease and Gerald Aungier
[4:0] - **1661**: Bombay came to **Charles II** as dowry of the **Portuguese** princess **Catherine of Braganza** (married 1662) – taken from the **Portuguese**, not Dutch, French or Danes.
[4:1] - **1668**: Charles II gave it to the Company for **£10 a year** (some books 1674/1688 – the lease was 1668).
[4:2] - **Gerald Aungier** (President of Surat and Governor of Bombay, 1669-77) made Bombay a major commercial hub – "founding father" of Bombay.
[4:3] - Bombay **replaced Surat** as the chief English settlement on the west coast (1687).
[4:4] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.70; NextGen Modern India L3 Part 1 p.17; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Aungier; old app notes row 154
## SUB 5 | Chronology, Child's War and the Farman of 1717
[5:0] - Order of English centres: **Surat 1613 → Madras 1639 → Bombay 1668 → Calcutta 1690**.
[5:1] - **Child's War (1686-90)**: under **Josiah Child** (London) and **Sir John Child** (in India) the Company fought **Aurangzeb**, lost, was pardoned on paying a fine – Aurangzeb demanded Sir John Child's expulsion.
[5:2] - **Shivaji** sacked Surat twice (1664, 1670) and threatened the English factory.
[5:3] - **Farman of 1717** (mission of **John Surman**, 1715) by **Farrukhsiyar** = "**Magna Carta**" of the Company.
[5:4] TABLE head: Region | Privilege (1717)
  [5:4.0] Bengal | Duty-free trade for Rs 3,000 a year; dastaks
  [5:4.1] Surat | All duties dropped for Rs 10,000 a year
  [5:4.2] Bombay | Company coins current in the Mughal empire
[5:5] - Misuse of dastaks for private trade drained the Nawab's revenue → Plassey (1757), Buxar (1764) → **Bengal** = first major territorial base.
[5:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.72-73 (book p.40-41); NextGen Modern India L3 Part 1 p.17; old app notes row 154
