## SUB 0 | Ashtadiggajas, Bhuvana Vijayam and the Golden Age of Telugu
[0:0] # The Ashtadiggajas
[0:1] - **Who they were:** **eight Telugu poets** ("eight elephants" holding up the eight directions) – not ministers, warriors or advisers (UP UDA 2001).
[0:2] - **Patron:** **Krishnadevaraya** (APPSC G2 2019; AP History PYQ; RAS 2010) – not Rama Raya, Achyutaraya, Tirumala Raya, Devaraya II, Shivaji or Rajendra I.
[0:3] - **Golden age:** his reign (1509–29) = **golden age of Telugu literature**, the **Prabandha** era (UP UDA 2006) – not Konkani, Malayalam or Tamil.
[0:4] - **Why we remember him:** for his **literary patronage** (AP History PYQ) – the Ashtadiggajas in the **Bhuvana Vijayam**.
[0:5] # Poet → work → title
[0:6] TABLE head: Poet | Main work | Title / note
  [0:6.0] Allasani Peddana | *Manucharitramu* (Swarochisha Manu Sambhavam) | Andhra Kavita Pitamaha; greatest of the eight; poet of Telugu and Sanskrit
  [0:6.1] Nandi Timmana | *Parijatapaharanamu* | Mukku Timmana; the king's favourite
  [0:6.2] Dhurjati | *Sri Kalahasteeswara Mahatyam* and Satakam | Shaiva poet of Kalahasti
  [0:6.3] Madayyagari Mallana | *Rajasekhara Charitramu* | Early social narrative
  [0:6.4] Ayyalaraju Ramabhadrudu | *Ramabhyudayamu*, Sakalakathasara Sangrahamu | Ramayana in Telugu
  [0:6.5] Pingali Surana | *Raghava Pandaviyamu*, *Kalapurnodayamu*, Prabhavati Pradyumnamu | Dvyarthi kavya (double meaning)
  [0:6.6] Ramarajabhushana (Bhattumurti) | *Vasucharitramu*, Narasabhupaliyamu | Vasucharitra dedicated to Tirumala Raya
  [0:6.7] Tenali Ramakrishna | *Panduranga Mahatmyamu* (also Ghatikachala Mahatmyam, Udbhataradhya Charitra) | Court wit
[0:7] - **Manucharitramu:** by **Allasani Peddana** (APPSC G2 2019; IAS 2007) – not Kalapurnodayam or Raghava Pandaviyam (Pingali Surana), or Haravilasam (Srinatha).
[0:8] - **Andhra Kavita Pitamaha:** **Allasani Peddana** (Raj. PCS 2023; WBCS 2007) – not Bhattumurti, Tenali Ramakrishna, Pingali Surana, Srinatha, Sayana or Madhava. **Andhra Bhoja** is the king's own title.
[0:9] - **Panduranga Mahatyam:** **Tenali Ramakrishna** (APPSC Jr Asst) – not Peddana, Dhurjati or the king.
[0:10] - **RAS 2021 statements:** Krishnadevaraya wrote Amuktamalyada ✓; he is called Andhra Bhoja ✓; Peddana "well versed in **Sanskrit and Tamil**" ✗ (he was a **Telugu and Sanskrit** poet) → **1 and 2 only**.
[0:11] - **Gandapenderam:** the king himself fixed this anklet on **Peddana's** foot.
[0:12] - **"Prabandha Parameshwara"** is **Errana's** title, not Nandi Timmana's.
[0:13] # Bhuvana Vijayam and the Tippalur grant
[0:14] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:14.0] Bhuvana Vijayam | "Conquest of the world"; Krishnadevaraya's court hall of poets; House of Victory
  [0:14.1] Ashtadiggajas | Eight elephants of the eight directions → eight pillar-poets
  [0:14.2] Kavula Samstha | Institution of poets – the Tippalur inscription's name for them
[0:15] - **Tippalur inscription:** gift of **Tippalur agrahara** (Kadapa) to the poets by **Krishnadevaraya**, not Achyutaraya.
[0:16] # Languages patronised
[0:17] - Vijayanagara patronised **Telugu, Kannada, Sanskrit and Tamil** – all four (Grama Sachivalayam) – so "**Tamil, Telugu and Sanskrit**" is the SSC 2007 answer (Hindi, Marathi, Malayalam, Urdu are wrong).
[0:18] - **Kannada:** **Mallanarya** of Krishnadevaraya's court wrote *Bhava Chinta Ratna* and *Veerasaivamrita* in **Kannada** (Raj. PCS 2024).
[0:19] - **Correct combination (Grama Sachivalayam):** **Krishnadevaraya – Amuktamalyada – Sahiti Samarangana Sarvabhauma** ✓. Wrong: Hala's title is not Devanampriya (Ashoka's); Peddana wrote **Manucharitra**, not Vasucharitra; Srinatha's title is **Kavisarvabhauma**.

## SUB 1 | Amuktamalyada and Krishnadevaraya's Own Works
[1:0] # Amuktamalyada
[1:1] - **Author:** **Krishnadevaraya** himself (APPSC AEE; Tribal Welfare Officer; AP History PYQ) – not Peddana, Tenali Ramakrishna, Nannaya, Nanne Choda, Palkuriki Somanatha, Gona Budda Reddy, Gangamba or Praudha Devaraya.
[1:2] - **Language:** **Telugu** – his only Telugu work; a work on **statecraft** (rajaniti) as well as devotion. NDA 2017 "NOT correct": "**wrote Amuktamalyada in Sanskrit**".
[1:3] - **Other name:** **Vishnuchittiyam**; meaning "**one who offers the garland she has worn**".
[1:4] - **Legend:** **Andhra Mahavishnu of Srikakulam** (Krishna district) told the king in a **dream** to write it, while he was **resting at Srikakulam** during the **Kalinga campaign**; dedicated to **Lord Venkateswara**.
[1:5] - **Central theme:** the **wedding of Goda Devi (Andal) with Sri Ranganatha** (Endowment 2022) – not his victories, the Ashtadiggajas or his literary achievements; sub-stories **Yamunacharya Charitra** and **Maladasari Katha**.
[1:6] - **Political maxims:** kingship, administration and statecraft; social, religious and economic life.
[1:7] - Preface: "**Desa Bhashalandu Telugu Lessa**" (Telugu is the best of the regional languages).
[1:8] - One of the **Telugu Pancha Kavyas**; the **first Telugu work named after its heroine**.
[1:9] # What Amuktamalyada does NOT contain (G2 2003; AP History PYQ)
[1:10] - Its preface **describes the king's Kalinga campaign** (the march to Vijayawada and the halt at Srikakulam), and the work **discusses political matters**.
[1:11] - It does **not** reveal a **genealogy from Bukkaraya to Krishnadevaraya** – the Tuluvas were not descendants of Bukka → this is the answer.
[1:12] - ⚠ Some keys of the G2 2003 paper print "describes the invasions"; that is wrong, since the preface narrates the Kalinga campaign.
[1:13] # Works → language
[1:14] TABLE head: Work | Language
  [1:14.0] Amuktamalyada | Telugu (his only Telugu work)
  [1:14.1] Jambavati Kalyanam / Parinayam (drama; Krishna weds Jambavati; mentions Virupaksha, Vasantotsavams) | Sanskrit (APPSC G2 2019; G2 Mains; AP History PYQ)
  [1:14.2] Usha Parinayam | Sanskrit
  [1:14.3] Madalasa Charita | Sanskrit
  [1:14.4] Rasamanjari | Sanskrit
  [1:14.5] Jnana Chintamani | Sanskrit
  [1:14.6] Satyavadhu Parinayam | Sanskrit
[1:15] - **Court musician:** **Lakshminatha** wrote *Sangita Suryodaya*.

## SUB 2 | The Saluva Dynasty and the Two Usurpations
[2:0] # Decline of the Sangamas
[2:1] - **Nuniz** called **Virupaksha II** a drunkard and **Praudha Raya** worthless; Gajapati and Bahmani invasions ate into the kingdom.
[2:2] # The two usurpations
[2:3] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [2:3.0] 1485 | First usurpation: Saluva Narasimha of Chandragiri expels Praudha Raya; ends the Sangama dynasty, begins the Saluva dynasty
  [2:3.1] 1505 | Second usurpation: Vira Narasimha (son of Tuluva Narasa Nayaka) kills Immadi Narasimha, the last Saluva, at Penugonda; begins the Tuluva dynasty
  [2:3.2] 1509 | Krishnadevaraya crowned on 8 August 1509
  [2:3.3] 1529 | Achyutaraya crowned at Tirupati
  [2:3.4] 1543 | Sadasiva made a figurehead under Aliya Rama Raya
  [2:3.5] Jan 1565 | Talikota (Rakshasi-Tangadi / Bannihatti): Rama Raya beheaded; flight to Penukonda
[2:4] - **Dynasty after the first usurpation:** **Saluva** (AP History PYQ). **Came to power:** **1485** (G2 2012).
[2:5] - **Founder of the Saluva dynasty:** **Saluva Narasimha (Narasimharaya)** (SSC 2005; AP History PYQ) – not Immadi Narasimha (last Saluva), Vira Narasimha (Tuluva founder), Praudha Devaraya or Rama Raya.
[2:6] - **UPSC 2003 A–R:** A "Saluva Narasimha ended the old dynasty and took the royal title" ✓; R "to save the kingdom from further degeneration and disintegration" ✓ and **explains A**.
[2:7] - **IAS 2004 (×3): all four correct** – Saluva Narasimha ended the Sangamas; Vira Narasimha deposed the last Saluva; Vira Narasimha → younger brother **Krishnadevaraya**; Krishnadevaraya → half-brother **Achyuta Raya**.
[2:8] - **Chronology (APSLPRB 2023):** Deva Raya II (Sangama) → Saluva Narasimha → Achyuta Deva Raya (Tuluva) → Venkatapati II (Aravidu) = **ii, iv, i, iii**.
[2:9] # Saluva Narasimha (1485–91)
[2:10] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [2:10.0] Origin | Telugu clan from Kalyana; governor of Chandragiri
  [2:10.1] Capital | Kalyanapuram → Chandragiri
  [2:10.2] Title | Raya Maharasu; his general Tuluva Narasa Nayaka took the throne for him
  [2:10.3] Army | Arab horses from Ormuz, Arab cavalry; introduced Samugaris (gunmen)
  [2:10.4] Lost | Udayagiri to Gajapati Purushottama
  [2:10.5] Court poet | Rajanatha Dindima – *Saluvabhyudayam*
  [2:10.6] Contemporary | Annamacharya (Tallapaka, 1408–1503): 32,000 kirtanas on Venkateswara; wife Timmakka wrote Subhadra Kalyanam
[2:11] - **Annamayya was a contemporary of:** **Saluva Narasimha Raya** (APPSC G2 2011; AP History PYQ) – not Harihara, Devaraya I, II or III.
[2:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History – Vijayanagara p.3, p.13-15; NextGen previous papers (src/ap-history/nextgen-previous-papers.txt); Rocket AP History; app notes 1:120 (sub 0, 1)

## SUB 4 | Tuluva Succession, Court and Contemporaries
[4:0] # Tuluva origin
[4:1] - From **Tuluvanadu** near Mysore; progenitor **Timmaraju**; mother tongue **Kannada**, but **Telugu** favoured at court.
[4:2] - **Tuluva Narasa Nayaka:** Saluva general and real ruler under Immadi Narasimha; died 1503. **Founder of the Tuluva dynasty = Vira Narasimha** (1505), not Narasa Nayaka.
[4:3] - **Krishnadevaraya belonged to the Tuluva dynasty** (AMVI 2023) – not Pandya, Kakatiya or Qutb Shahi.
[4:4] # Succession
[4:5] TABLE head: Ruler | Immediate successor
  [4:5.0] Praudha Deva Raya | Saluva Narasimha
  [4:5.1] Immadi Narasimha | Vira Narasimha
  [4:5.2] Vira Narasimha (1505–09) | Krishnadevaraya (his younger half-brother)
  [4:5.3] Krishnadevaraya (1509–29) | Achyuta Deva Raya (his half-brother)
[4:6] - **King before Krishnadevaraya:** **Vira Narasimha** (G2-A 1989). **After him:** **Achyuta Devaraya** (Asst. Archaeologist 2011) – not Rama Raya or Sadasiva.
[4:7] - **Aravidu rule began with:** **Tirumala Deva Raya** (Penukonda).
[4:8] - **Death of Krishnadevaraya:** **1529** (NextGen; reign often written 1509–1529/30).
[4:9] # Court
[4:10] - **Prime Minister:** **Saluva Timmarusu (Appaji)** – minister of **Krishnadevaraya** (AP History PYQ), not Saluva Narasimha, Rajaraja Narendra or Kyatya Varma; later blinded on suspicion.
[4:11] - **Rajaguru:** **Vyasatirtha (Vyasaraya)**, Madhva saint (AP History PYQ) – not Vidyaranya (Sangama founders' guru). His disciple **Purandaradasa** (father of Carnatic music) was a contemporary.
[4:12] # Contemporaries
[4:13] - **Babur** (1526–30): Krishnadevaraya was his contemporary (FRO 2009; SI 2011); Babur praised him in the **Tuzuk-i-Baburi** as the most powerful ruler of India. Not Akbar, Aurangzeb, Firoz Tughlaq or Alauddin Khalji.
[4:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History – Vijayanagara p.3-5, p.17-20; app notes 1:120 (sub 2)
## SUB 5 | Krishnadevaraya's Campaigns – Raichur, Golconda and Kalinga
[5:0] # Campaigns
[5:1] TABLE head: Campaign | Year | Result
  [5:1.0] Diwani / Kovilkonda | 1509–10 | Bahmani "jihad" crushed; Yusuf Adil Khan killed
  [5:1.1] Raichur Doab and Bidar | 1510–12 | Raichur taken; freed Sultan Mahmud Shah from Barid → title Yavanarajya Sthapanacharya
  [5:1.2] Ummattur / Sivasamudram | 1512–13 | Gangaraja defeated; new province at Srirangapatna
  [5:1.3] Kalinga | 1513–1519 | Udayagiri, Kondaveedu, Kondapalli, Simhachalam, Cuttack (1518)
  [5:1.4] Battle of Raichur | 1520 | Ismail Adil Shah of Bijapur routed with Portuguese help; Gulbarga taken 1523
[5:2] - **Bijapur Sultan at Raichur (1520):** **Ismail Adil Khan / Shah** (G2 2000; AP History PYQ) – not Ahmed Barid, Muhammad Shah I or Mujahid Shah.
[5:3] - **"Great battle of 1520":** the battle of Raichur, which Telugu texts name **Gobburu** (the field near Raichur) – G2-A 1989; **Mudgal** was Devaraya I vs Firoz Shah Bahmani.
[5:4] - **Battle of Golconda:** fought against **Quli Qutb Shah** of Golconda (BPSC 1999), who had taken forts while the king fought the Gajapatis – not Qutbuddin Aibak, Ismail Adil Shah or the Gajapati.
[5:5] - **Yusuf Adil Khan** (Kovilkonda, 1509–10) ≠ **Ismail Adil Shah** (Raichur, 1520).
[5:6] # The Kalinga campaign against Prataparudra Gajapati
[5:7] TABLE head: Fort / place | What happened
  [5:7.0] Udayagiri (1513–14) | Fell after an 18-month siege; Balakrishna idol taken to Hampi (Krishna temple built for it)
  [5:7.1] Kondaveedu | Taken with Saluva Timmarusu's help (split Veerabhadra Gajapati's army); king performed Talapurusha Danam at Amaravati
  [5:7.2] Kondapalli and Bejawada | Gajapati forces defeated
  [5:7.3] Vinukonda, Addanki, Kandukuru | Also taken
  [5:7.4] Cuttack | Reached in 1518; Gajapati sued for peace
[5:8] - **Idol taken from Udayagiri:** **Balakrishna** (G2-A 1989) – not Ramachandra, Ugranarasimha or Virupaksha.
[5:9] - **Victory pillar (Jayastambha) for the conquest of coastal Andhra:** **Potnuru** (Padmanabham mandal, near Visakhapatnam; Bezawada inscription 1516: "Simhadri-Potnuru") – AP History PYQ; G2 PYQ. Not Kondaveedu, Kondapalli, Udayagiri, Rajahmundry or Nagarjunakonda. Lists of his pillars give **Potnuru and Simhachalam** (the inscription's "Simhadri-Potnuru").
[5:10] - ⚠ The old note "APPSC key (2025) gave Kondaveedu" is unverified; Kondaveedu was a fort taken, not the pillar site.
[5:11] - **Temple grants:** **Simhachalam** – diamond necklace to Varaha Narasimha; **Mangalagiri** – land to **Lakshmi Narasimha**.
[5:12] - **Peace terms:** Prataparudra's daughter (**Tukka / Bhadradevi**) married Krishnadevaraya; lands **north of the Krishna** went back to the Gajapati.
[5:13] - **Salt:** **Pedaganjam and Chinaganjam** were famous for salt; the salt pans at **Pedaganjam** were taxed.

## SUB 8 | Achyutaraya, Sadasiva and Aliya Rama Raya
[8:0] - **Achyutaraya** (1529–42): named heir by Krishnadevaraya; crowned at **Tirupati** and **Srikalahasti** (the only king crowned at Tirumala-Tirupati).
[8:1] - **Assembly hall:** Achyutaraya's sabha = **Venkatavilasa Mantapam** (AP History PYQ); Krishnadevaraya's = **Bhuvana Vijayam**. (NextGen also has Venkatapati II's literary meets at "Venkatapati Vilasam".)
[8:2] - **Aliya Rama Raya:** Krishnadevaraya's **son-in-law**, of the **Aravidu** family; killed **Salakam Tirumala** (1543); made **Sadasiva** a figurehead.
[8:3] - **Talikota** (January **1565**), also **Rakshasi-Tangadi** / Bannihatti: Rama Raya beheaded; Sadasiva and Tirumala fled to **Penukonda**. **Last Tuluva ruler = Sadasiva**.
[8:4] - **Latest – Sadasivakona inscriptions** (ASI, **June 2026**): three **trilingual** (Telugu, Tamil, Kannada) inscriptions of **1554 CE** in the **Seshachalam forest, Tirupati district**; record Sadasiva Raya's holy bath and transfer of **Kaanika** taxes from Gudimallam temple lands.
[8:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History – Vijayanagara p.5, p.20-22; app notes 1:120 (sub 11)
