## SUB 0 | Mughal Painting – Emperor-wise Evolution, Zenith and European Influence
[0:0] # Emperor → emphasis
[0:1] TABLE head: Emperor | Emphasis
  [0:1.0] Babur, Humayun | Humayun laid the foundation: brought the Persian masters Mir Sayyid Ali (of Tabriz) and Khwaja Abdus Samad from Persia (met at Tabriz/Kabul) – they formed the nucleus of the Mughal school, fusing Persian (Islamic) and Indian (non-Islamic) elements
  [0:1.1] Akbar | Separate painting department / atelier Tasvir Khana of 100+ painters under Abdus Samad; painting karkhanas at Agra, Fatehpur Sikri, Lahore; frescoes at Fatehpur Sikri; mostly illustrated manuscripts (Hamzanama, Tutinama, Akbarnama, Razmnama) and portraits; 3-D figures, foreshortening; Persian style became Indianised
  [0:1.2] Jahangir | Zenith / golden age of Mughal painting; shift from illustrated manuscripts to albums and individual portraits; events of his own life, birds, flowers, animals (naturalism); his own private atelier, a keen judge who could name the artist of a painting; decorated margins; halo (nimbus) behind the emperor's head; patronised European and Persian art
  [0:1.3] Shah Jahan | More interested in architecture; formal court scenes, albums (muraqqas), floral borders, more gold and silver, artificial stillness; painters Mir Hashim, Anup, Chitra
  [0:1.4] Aurangzeb | Opposed pictorial art, withdrew patronage → painters moved to provincial courts (Awadh, Hyderabad, Mysore, Bengal), Rajasthan, the Punjab Hills and Bihar → fed the Rajput and Pahari schools
[0:2] - **Zenith under Jahangir** (UPSC 1996; State PSC; UKPSC Lower 2010) – not Akbar (built the atelier) or Shah Jahan (architecture). **Jahangir mainly patronised painting** (UPPCS 2016).
[0:3] - **Shift from illustrated manuscripts to albums and individual portraits = Jahangir** (UPSC 2019).
[0:4] - Subjects of Mughal painting: **battle scenes, animals-birds-nature, court scenes – all of the above** (UPPSC 1991).
[0:5] - NDA 2012: **Akbar's paintings were mostly book illustrations and portraits** (I) and **Mir Sayyid Ali + Abdus Samad formed the nucleus of the school fusing non-Islamic and Islamic elements** (II) – **both correct**.
[0:6] - **Foundation laid by Humayun** (UPPSC 2012), not Akbar.
[0:7] # European influence
[0:8] - European pictures reached **Akbar's court through the Portuguese (Jesuit priests)** → **portraiture** and **smaller distant objects for spatial depth**; Mughal style = muted naturalistic colour, perspective and shading. So **European painting was introduced in Akbar's court** (BPSC 2019) – not Jahangir's, and **not by Danish merchants**.
[0:9] - **First Jesuit mission from Goa** reached Akbar in **1580**: Father **Rudolf Acquaviva** with Father **Antonio Monserrate** and Brother Francis Henriques; they brought the **Polyglot Bible**, whose pictures Akbar showed to his painters.
[0:10] - **APPSC G2 Prelims 2024:** a (Humayun brought Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad) and b (Jahangir = golden period) are **correct**; c (European painting brought by **Danish merchants** under Jahangir) is **incorrect** → **c only**.
[0:11] > Exam angle: "zenith / golden age" and "albums and portraits" = Jahangir; "foundation" = Humayun; "European painting arrived" = Akbar via the Portuguese.

## SUB 3 | Music at the Mughal Court
[3:0] # Musicians
[3:1] TABLE head: Musician | Facts
  [3:1.0] Tansen | Original name Ramtanu Pande (Tanna Mishra); trained at Man Singh Tomar's Gwalior school; disciple of Swami Haridas; first at the court of Raja Ramchandra of Rewa (Bandhavgarh) – Akbar sent a firman for him; one of Akbar's Navaratnas; composed dhrupads on Hindu gods and on his patrons; invented ragas (Mian ki Todi, Mian ki Malhar, Darbari Kanada); tomb at Gwalior
  [3:1.1] Tansen's titles | 'Tansen' from Raja Vikramjit of Gwalior – NOT from Akbar; 'Mian' from Akbar
  [3:1.2] Baiju Bawra | Dhrupad singer at the court of Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior, later of Bahadur Shah of Gujarat
  [3:1.3] Swami Haridas | Dhrupad singer of Akbar's period (Vrindavan), Tansen's guru
  [3:1.4] Vilas Khan | Tansen's son; main singer under Jahangir
  [3:1.5] Baz Bahadur | Last Sultan of Malwa (1555-62), musician-poet; Rupmati; later served Akbar
[3:2] - **Dhrupad singers of Akbar's reign: Tansen and Haridas** (1 and 2); Vilas Khan belongs to Jahangir.
[3:3] - Abul Fazl lists **36 singers** at Akbar's court. **Aurangzeb** ended court music yet **played the veena**; many Persian works on music date from his reign.
[3:4] # Form → origin / patron
[3:5] TABLE head: Form | Origin / patron
  [3:5.0] Dhrupad | Oldest Hindustani vocal form; roots in Vedic chanting; devotional/spiritual; alap uses Sanskrit syllables from mantras; developed by Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior (1486-1516) – before the Mughals; non-Islamic (indigenous) in character
  [3:5.1] Khayal | Persian-influenced; Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela' – composers Sadarang and Adarang
  [3:5.2] Tappa | Folk songs of camel riders of the north-west; made a court form under Muhammad Shah; refined by Shori Miyan (Lucknow, late 18th c.)
  [3:5.3] Qawwali | Sufi (Islamic) devotional form
[3:6] - UPSC 2012 Dhrupad: 1 (originated in Rajput kingdoms during the Mughal period) is **wrong**; 2 and 3 are right → **2 and 3**.
[3:7] - **Mal Gurjari** = a **raga** of Man Singh Tomar, not a person; greats = Man Singh Tomar, Tansen, Sadarang-Adarang.
[3:8] - **Sitar** = Persian long-necked lute (**setar**) + Indian **veena**.
[3:9] # Hindustani music – instruments and ragas
[3:10] - **Ustad Amjad Ali Khan = sarod**; Ravi Shankar = sitar; Zakir Hussain = tabla.
[3:11] - **Todi** = early-morning raga; Bhimpalasi = afternoon; Bhopali = evening; Darbari Kanada = late night.

[4:4] # Dara Shikoh
[4:5] - **Shah Jahan's eldest son**; **Qadiri** Sufi, disciple of **Miyan Mir** of Lahore (and Mulla Shah Badakhshi).
[4:6] - Translated the **Upanishads (Sirr-i-Akbar)**, the **Bhagavad Gita** and the Yoga Vasistha into Persian – the answer to "**first Muslim to study Hindu scriptures**" in keys.
[4:7] - Original work **Majma-ul-Bahrain** ("Mingling of the Two Oceans") – common ground of **Sufism and Vedanta**.
[4:8] - Sirr-i-Akbar's Latin version by **Anquetil-Duperron** took the Upanishads to Europe.

[5:3] # Languages
[5:4] - **Persian = court language** of the Mughals (state language till **1835**) – not Turkish, Arabic or Urdu.
[5:5] - **Urdu** (Zaban-i-Hindavi → Rekhta) grew in **military camps and bazaars**; first encouraged by the Deccan sultans; **Muhammad Shah** was the first Mughal to encourage Urdu (invited the Deccani poet Wali); later courts of **Delhi and Lucknow**.
[5:6] - **Mirza Ghalib**: born at **Agra** (1797), settled in Delhi.
[5:7] - **Deccani (Dakhni) Urdu** under the Qutb Shahis: **Mulla Khiali** – first Deccani poetry in Telangana; **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** – first Urdu poet with a diwan, built the Charminar; **Abdullah Qutb Shah's era = Golden Age of Urdu**; **Ghavvasi** – Tuti Nama (1639) from the Sanskrit Shuka Saptati.
[5:8] - Qutb Shahi official language **Persian**, people's language **Telugu**; translation + painting + Hindavi/Urdu = **composite Indo-Persian culture**.

## SUB 6 | Regional and Later Painting Schools
[6:0] TABLE head: School | Region / features
  [6:0.0] Rajput | Rajasthan; Hindu epics, love legends; bold colours, flat; Jaipur and Nathdwara centres
  [6:0.1] Pahari | North-western Himalayan hill states (Himachal, J&K, Tehri/Paudi Garhwal); 17th-19th c.
  [6:0.2] Kangra | Pahari sub-school; peak under Raja Sansar Chand
  [6:0.3] Kishangarh | Rajasthan; patron Raja Sawant Singh (pen-name Nagari Das, throne 1748, Krishna verses, Nimbarka sect); Bani Thani by Nihal Chand, inspired by Sawant Singh's poem Bihari Jas Chandrika; Radha-Krishna theme
  [6:0.4] Deccani | Began at the Bahmani court; at Golconda Mir Hashim
  [6:0.5] Kalighat | Bengal, 19th c.; indigenous bazaar style (Kali, Sita-Rama, Radha-Krishna) – not affected by Mughal painting
  [6:0.6] Company paintings | 18th-19th c. hybrid of Indian and European styles for the East India Company
[6:1] - **Mughal painting influenced the Pahari, Rajasthani and Kangra schools; Kalighat was not affected** (UPSC 1995).
[6:2] - Pahari, Rajput, Mughal and Kangra **schools = styles of painting** (UPPCS 1994).
[6:3] - **CDS 2015 (18th-early 19th c. visual culture):** Patna and Murshidabad painters moved to Calcutta and made **English-style watercolours** (I); **landscape and portraiture** became important (II); **Zayn-al-Din, Bhawani Das and Ram Das** made albums of Indian birds and animals for the English (Impey) (III) – all true; IV is false, **European artists did visit regional courts** (Tilly Kettle, Zoffany at Lucknow) → **I, II and III**.
