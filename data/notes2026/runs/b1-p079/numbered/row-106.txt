## SUB 1 | Malik Kafur and the Deccan Campaigns
[1:0] - **Origin:** a eunuch slave seized at **Cambay** by **Nusrat Khan** in the **Gujarat campaign (1299)** and presented to Alauddin; called **Hazar Dinari** because his first master had **bought him for 1,000 dinars**; rose to **Malik Naib**.
[1:1] - **Commander** of all of Alauddin's **Deccan campaigns** (WBCS 2020).
[1:2] TABLE head: Year | Kingdom | Ruler | Result
  [1:2.0] 1307-08 | Devagiri (Yadava) | Ramachandra | Taken to Delhi; vassal, tribute
  [1:2.1] 1309-10 | Warangal (Kakatiya) | Prataparudra II | Treaty and annual tribute; loot on 1,000 camels
  [1:2.2] 1310-11 | Dwarasamudra (Hoysala) | Vira Ballala III | Submitted
  [1:2.3] 1311 | Madurai (Pandya) | Vira Pandya / Sundara Pandya | Sacked; Kakatiya troops aided Kafur
[1:3] - **Order:** **Devagiri → Warangal → Dwarasamudra → Madurai**.
[1:4] - **Pairs (UPPSC 2018):** Devagiri – Ramachandra; Hoysala – Vira Ballala; Madurai – Vira Pandya; **Warangal – Prataparudra II, not Shankar Dev** (Shankaradeva was Ramachandra's Yadava son).
[1:5] - **Aim:** **wealth and tribute, not annexation** – "financial ventures" (K.S. Lal); so in "A: financial ventures, R: wanted to annex" → **A true, R false** (UPPSC 1997). Annexation came only under **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**.
[1:6] - **Significance (CDS 2013):** boldness and spirit of adventure, **untold wealth** brought to Delhi, **fresh geographical knowledge** of the south, and Kafur's elevation as **Malik Naib**.
[1:7] - **Koh-i-Noor:** a later popular tradition only; no contemporary record.
[1:8] # AP Angle: Kakatiya–Delhi Relations
[1:9] - **1303:** first attack on Warangal by **Malik Fakhruddin Juna** (with Malik Chhajju) **failed at Upparapalli** – the **Kakatiya army defeated Alauddin's army** (UPPSC 2017); victors **Potuganti Maili** and **Recherla Venna**.
[1:10] - **1309-10:** **Kafur** with **Khwaja Haji** besieged Warangal; **Amir Khusrau** went along – Khazain-ul-Futuh: **double-walled fort** with a **moat**.
[1:11] - Tribute **stopped after 1316**; **third invasion (1318)** by **Khusrau Khan** under Mubarak Shah. Telugu sources count **8** invasions.

[2:0] TABLE head: Year | Kingdom | Ruler | Note
  [2:0.0] 1299 | Gujarat (Vaghela) | Rai Karan (Karnadeva II) | Ulugh Khan and Nusrat Khan; Somnath plundered; queen Kamala Devi taken; Kafur acquired
  [2:0.1] c. 1299 | Jaisalmer | Bhatti Rajputs | Raided on the way to Gujarat (PMF)
  [2:0.2] 1301 | Ranthambore | Hammir Deva Chauhan (sheltered New Mussalmans) | First siege 1299 failed, Nusrat Khan killed; Alauddin took it in person
  [2:0.3] 1303 | Chittor (Mewar) | Rana Ratan Singh | Renamed Khizrabad
  [2:0.4] 1305 | Malwa | Mahlak Deva | Ain-ul-Mulk Multani; Ujjain, Mandu, Dhar, Chanderi
  [2:0.5] c. 1308-11 | Siwana, Jalore | Shital Deva (Siwana) | Rajputana completed
  [2:0.6] 1309-10 | Warangal | Prataparudra II | Malik Kafur
[2:1] - **Chronology asked:** **Gujarat → Ranthambore → Chittor → Warangal** (UPPSC 2022); **Jaisalmer → Ranthambore → Chittor → Warangal** (UPPSC 2025).
[2:2] - **Statements (AP paper):** conquests **began with Gujarat (1299)**; **Ulugh Khan and Nusrat Khan** sacked **Somnath**; **Ranthambore** was the first **main** target in Rajasthan.
[2:3] - **Events in order:** **Gujarat (1299) → Mongols under Ali Beg and Tartaq (1305) → Kafur sacks the Pandyas (1311) → Yadava kingdom annexed (1313 by Kafur; finally 1318)**.
[2:4] - **Policy:** **north annexed**; **south** – kings kept thrones and **paid tribute**.
[2:5] - **First Turkish invasion of the south:** Alauddin's **Devagiri** raid (**1296**) as governor of Kara.

## SUB 3 | Alauddin – Rise, Titles, Nobles and Kingship
[3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:0.0] Ali Gurshasp | Earlier name
  [3:0.1] Amir-i-Tuzuk | Master of ceremonies (post under Jalaluddin)
  [3:0.2] Ariz-i-Mumalik | Army minister (post under Jalaluddin)
  [3:0.3] Sikandar-i-Sani | "Second Alexander", on his coins
[3:1] - **Nobles:** **Ulugh Khan, Nusrat Khan, Zafar Khan, Ain-ul-Mulk Multani, Malik Kafur, Ghazi Malik**; court poet **Amir Khusrau**. **Munim Khan** was **Akbar's** noble, not Alauddin's (CDS 2007).
[3:2] - **Ain-ul-Mulk Multani** served **Alauddin** (Malwa 1305) and later Sultans; he could **not** have served **Iltutmish** (d. 1236) (MPPSC 2017).
[3:3] - **Ambitions:** world conquest and a **new religion** – dropped on the advice of the **Kotwal Ala-ul-Mulk** (Barani's uncle).
[3:4] - **Kingship:** told **Qazi Mughisuddin of Bayana** he acted in the **interest of the state**, not by the ulema; revived **Balban's** harsh centralised rule without religious sanction.
[3:5] - **Barani** separated **Shariat** from **Zawabit** (state laws made by the ruler).

## SUB 5 | Jalaluddin Khalji (1290-1296)
[5:0] - Khalji of **Turkestan** stock; crowned himself at **Kilokhari (1290)** at about **70**.
[5:1] - **First Sultan with a benevolent attitude to Hindus**; lenient to rebels such as **Malik Chhajju**.
[5:2] - Married his daughter to **Ulugh Khan**, a descendant of **Chengiz Khan**; settled Mongol converts as **New Mussalmans**.
[5:3] - **Murdered at Kara (1296)** by his nephew and son-in-law **Alauddin**.
[5:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 03 p.58; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.35-36
