## SUB 1 | Tipu Sultan – Capital, Embassies, Jacobin Club
[1:0] - **Capital: Srirangapatna (Seringapatam)**; born 1750; **"Tiger of Mysore"**; also **Padshah** (1787) and **"Citizen Tipu"** – "Lion of Mysore" is not his title.
[1:1] - **Embassies on modern lines:** **France** (three ambassadors in Paris, 1788), **Ottoman Empire**, Oman, Persia, **Afghanistan (Zaman Shah)**, Arabia, Mauritius – not Haidar, Mir Qasim or Shah Alam II.
[1:2] - **Navy:** Board of Admiralty (1796), dockyards at Mangalore, Wajedabad, Molidabad.
[1:3] - **Jacobin Club** at Seringapatam (**1797**); he became **Citizen Tipu** and **planted the Tree of Liberty**.
[1:4] - **Reforms:** new calendar, coinage, weights and measures; sericulture; **war rockets** (pioneer – he did **not** discontinue them).
[1:5] TABLE head: Monument (Srirangapatna) | Feature
  [1:5.0] Gumbaz | Mausoleum of Haidar Ali and Tipu
  [1:5.1] Daria Daulat Bagh | Summer palace with war murals
[1:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.130-131; PMF Modern History Part 1 p.79; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.17; old app notes row 166

## SUB 3 | Haidar Ali (1761-82)
[3:0] - Rose in the **Wodeyar** army under ministers **Nanjaraj** (sarvadhikari) and **Devaraj** (dalwai), who had made **Chikka Krishnaraja Wodeyar II** a puppet.
[3:1] - **Faujdar of Dindigul (1755)** under **Nanjaraja**; set up a **modern arsenal at Dindigul with French help**; trained troops on Western lines; de facto ruler **1761**.
[3:2] - **First war diplomacy:** paid the **Marathas** to stay neutral and **won over the Nizam**; appeared before Madras → humiliating **Treaty of Madras (1769)** – the **first South Indian ruler to defeat British armies**.
[3:3] - Defeated by Peshwa **Madhav Rao** (1764, 1766, 1771); the English did not help him in 1771 → Second war.
[3:4] - **Velu Nachiyar** of Sivaganga recovered her kingdom with Haidar's help – the first queen to fight the British.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.126-128; PMF Modern History Part 1 p.78; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.17, 36; old app notes row 166
## SUB 4 | Third War and the Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)
[4:0] - Cause: Tipu attacked **Travancore** (1789/90), a Company ally; Tipu beat **General Meadows (1790)**; **Cornwallis** took **Bangalore (March 1791)** via Ambur and Vellore.
[4:1] - **Both the Nizam and the Marathas** joined the British (the grand alliance) – only in the **Third** war.
[4:2] - **Treaty of Seringapatam** – with **Tipu Sultan** (not Haidar): about **half his territory** ceded, indemnity **Rs 3 crore** (330 lakh), **two sons as hostages**.
[4:3] TABLE head: Gainer | Share
  [4:3.0] British | Malabar, Coorg (protectorate), Dindigul, Bara Mahal – Ryotwari began here (Read and Munro)
  [4:3.1] Nizam | Krishna to beyond the Pennar – Bellary, Gurramkonda, Gandikota, Kambham, Kadapa
  [4:3.2] Marathas | Around the Tungabhadra
[4:4] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.129-130; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.19, Part 6 p.18; PMF Modern History Part 2 p.39; old app notes row 166
## SUB 5 | Fourth War (1799) and the Settlement of Mysore
[5:0] - Tipu refused the **Subsidiary Alliance**; **Wellesley** declared war (17 April – 4 May 1799); led by **General George Harris**, General Stuart and **Arthur Wellesley**.
[5:1] - **Nizam** fought as a subsidiary ally (since 1798); the **Marathas** are listed as allies by NextGen, PMF and Spectrum, but in fact gave **no active help** and refused their share.
[5:2] - Wodeyars restored: boy ruler **Krishnaraja Wodeyar III** under a subsidiary alliance; Nizam got **Gooty and Gurramkonda**.
[5:3] - The Nizam's Mysore lands were handed to the Company in **1800** = **Ceded Districts** (**Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary**).
[5:4] - Removed the French-backed threat; brought Mysore and Rayalaseema under the Company; Bentinck took over Mysore 1831, Ripon restored it 1881.
[5:5] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.130-132; PMF Modern History Part 2 p.40-41; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 5 p.18; old app notes row 166
