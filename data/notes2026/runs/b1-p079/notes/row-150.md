=== Vasco da Gama and the Sea Route, 1498
# The voyage
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Sponsor | King **Manuel I** of Portugal |
| Left Lisbon | **8 July 1497** (four ships incl. a store ship; **São Gabriel** carried Vasco himself) |
| Route | Lisbon → **Cape of Good Hope** → East African coast (**Malindi**) → Calicut |
| Guide | the **Gujarati** pilot **Abdul Majid**, taken on at Malindi |
| Landfall | **Kappad**, near **Calicut (Kozhikode)**, Malabar (western) coast |
| Arrival | **May 1498** – land sighted ~17-18 May, anchored **20 May 1498** |
| Received by | the **Zamorin (Samuthiri)**, Hindu ruler of Calicut |
| Stay / return | about three months; back in Portugal **1499** with a cargo sold at a huge profit |
- **Year asked (×10):** Vasco da Gama reached Calicut / found the sea route in **1498** (the voyage = **1497-98**); 1492 = Columbus, 1453 = fall of Constantinople, 1488 = Dias.
- **"May 17, 1498"** (APPSC G2-B 1989 option) is the only 1498 option – accept it; the usual date is 20 May.
- **First landing port:** **Calicut** (not Cochin, Quilon or Surat); he went to the court of the **Zamorin of Calicut**, not Travancore, Poona or Cambay.
- **Sea route via the Cape of Good Hope** = Vasco da Gama (not Columbus, Cabot or Amundsen).
- **Contemporaries in 1498:** Delhi – **Sikandar Lodi**; Vijayanagara – **Immadi Narasimha Raya** (Saluva); Portugal – Manuel I.
# Later voyages
| Voyage | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 2nd, **1502** (Spectrum: 1501) | Zamorin refused to expel Arab traders; war; factory at **Cannanore**; Portuguese settle at **Kochi** |
| 3rd, **1524** | came as **Viceroy**; died at **Kochi** the same year |
- **Cabral (1500):** first Portuguese factory at **Calicut**, then trading centres at **Cannanore and Cochin**.
- **Profit:** the first cargo sold at about **60 times** its cost (old app notes); buying pepper through Muslim middlemen cost ten times more (Spectrum).
> Contemporaries angle: Krishnadeva Raya (1509-29) came after da Gama; Sikandar, not Ibrahim, Lodi ruled Delhi in 1498.

=== Order of European Arrival – First and Last
| Power | Arrival / company | First factory | Headquarters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Portuguese** | **1498** (no company; state enterprise) | Calicut **1500** (Cabral) | Cochin → **Goa (1530)** |
| **Dutch** | VOC **1602** | **Masulipatnam 1605** | Pulicat → **Nagapattinam (1690)** |
| **English** | EIC **31 Dec 1600** | Surat **1613** (permanent) | Surat → Bombay / Madras / Calcutta |
| **Danes** | **1616** | Tranquebar 1620 | Tranquebar; Serampore (1755) |
| **French** | **1664** (Colbert) | Surat **1668** | **Pondicherry** |
- **Arrival order:** **Portuguese → Dutch → English → Danes → French** (code "4, 2, 1, 3" for English-Dutch-French-Portuguese).
- **Formation order:** English 1600 → Dutch 1602 → Danes 1616 → French 1664.
- **First Europeans** to come for trade / to land / to set up sea-trade centres = **Portuguese**; **last** to come as traders = **French** ("the last Europeans to come to India with the purpose of trade", Spectrum).
- **First to come and last to leave** = Portuguese (Goa, Daman, Diu freed **1961**).
- **Chronology (IAS 2000):** Qutub Minar (begun by Aibak, finished by Iltutmish) → death of **Firuz Tughlaq (1388)** → arrival of the **Portuguese (1498)** → reign of **Krishnadeva Raya (1509-29)** = **2, 4, 3, 1**.
- **VOC (1602)** is widely called the **first joint-stock company to sell shares to the public**.

=== Explorers and Motives of European Expansion
# Why Europe sought a sea route
- **1453:** Ottoman Sultan **Mehmed II** took **Constantinople**, closing the overland route; the Asian trade was controlled by **Arabs**, and **Venice and Genoa** distributed the goods in Europe.
- **Motives:** spices (pepper, cinnamon, cardamom, ginger), silk and Indian textiles; profit; breaking the **Arab (Muslim) monopoly**; spreading **Christianity** (Prester John legend); rivalry of Portugal, Spain and later Holland, England, France. "Roman imperial administration" is NOT a motive.
- **Aids:** **Prince Henry the Navigator** (naval school; papal bull of 1454); the **caravel** (ship) and **astrolabe**.
- **"Bear in mind that the commerce of India is the commerce of the world"** – **Peter the Great** of Russia, about **18th-century** India (APPSC G2 2017); not da Gama, Clive or Dupleix.
# Explorers
| Explorer | Sponsor | Year | Achievement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bartholomew Dias** | Portugal | **1487-88** | rounded the **Cape of Good Hope** ("Cape of Storms", renamed by King John II) |
| Christopher Columbus | Spain | 1492 | the Americas ("West Indies") |
| John Cabot | England | 1497 | North America |
| Vasco da Gama | Portugal | 1498 | sea route to India |
| Pedro Alvares Cabral | Portugal | 1500 | Brazil; factory at Calicut |
| **Ferdinand Magellan** | Spain | 1519-22 | **first circumnavigation** of the globe (completed by Elcano after Magellan's death) |
- **Dividing the world:** papal bull **Inter caetera (1493**, Alexander VI) and **Treaty of Tordesillas (1494)** – line 370 leagues west of Cape Verde; **east → Portugal, west → Spain**.
- **New World civilisations, north → south:** **Aztec** (central Mexico) → **Maya** (Yucatan, Central America) → **Muisca** (Colombia) → **Inca** (Peru).
- **AP and Brazil:** Brazil's **Nelore** cattle descend from **Nellore** (AP) cattle; in **July 2025** the PM's visit for the **17th BRICS Summit** (Rio de Janeiro) recalled ties going back to Cabral's landing (1500).

=== World History I – American and French Revolutions, Napoleon
# American Revolution
- **Cause:** colonists refused taxes voted by a Parliament where they had no seats – **"No taxation without representation"**; merchants **boycotted British goods**. It was colonists vs Britain, not settlers vs Native Americans.
- **Stamp Act 1765** (repealed 1766) → **Stamp Act Congress, New York City, October 1765** (9 of the 13 colonies).
- **Coercive (Intolerable) Acts 1774** made reconciliation virtually impossible; the earlier Stamp Act repeal does not explain that.
- **First Continental Congress, Philadelphia, Sept 1774:** rejected **Galloway's plan** of union under British authority, drew up a **statement of grievances**, agreed the **Continental Association** (stop trade with England).
# French Revolution
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **June 1789** | **Tennis Court Oath** – Third Estate (National Assembly) |
| 1791 | Olympe de Gouges' **Declaration of the Rights of Woman and the Female Citizen** |
| **1792** | **National Convention** meets; monarchy abolished, republic |
| June 1793 – July 1794 | Jacobins (with the **sans-culottes**) purge the Girondins; **Reign of Terror**; ends with **Robespierre's execution, July 1794** |
| 1804 | Napoleon **Emperor of the French** (first emperor of France); **Napoleonic Code** |
- **Reign of Terror:** the **Law of Suspects (Sept 1793)** let anyone be arrested, not only the political class; the Jacobins were **anti-clerical** (de-Christianisation), not allies of the clergy.
- **Olympe de Gouges:** politically active writer; "the nation is the **union of woman and man**"; not on the committee that drafted the Rights of Man (1789); opposed the Jacobins and was guillotined **1793**.
# Napoleon
- **Third Coalition (1805):** Britain, **Austria, Russia, Sweden** (Prussia joined 1806) against **France**.
- **Austerlitz 1805** → Treaty of Pressburg; **Tilsit 1807** = treaties, not a battle; **Wagram 1809 → Treaty of Schönbrunn**.

=== World History II – Industrial, Russian and Chinese Revolutions; Ancient Firsts
# Industrial Revolution (Britain first)
- **Why Britain first:** **coal and iron**, **steam power**, regular **raw materials** (colonies), **navigable rivers/canals**, favourable social-political order, **capitalist** practice and scientific inventions; railways (1825-30) sustained rather than started it.
- **Cotton inventions:** **Kay's flying shuttle 1733 → Hargreaves' spinning jenny 1764 → Arkwright's water frame 1769 → Crompton's mule 1779**.
- **Factory system:** production under one roof in towns ruined the **guilds**; **merchant capitalists** gained control (not "production moved to the countryside").
- **Impact:** cottage industry → **factory system**; **working-class movements**; overcrowded **towns** (not villages).
# Russian Revolution, 1917
- **Provisional Government (Feb/March 1917):** first headed by **Prince Lvov**, then by **Alexander Kerensky** (July-Oct 1917); overthrown in the **October (Bolshevik) Revolution**.
- **Lenin** = father of the Revolution; Russia = first **Marxist** revolution.
- **Bolsheviks** (Lenin): small, disciplined party; **Mensheviks**: party open to all, as in Germany.
# Asia and other events
- **Crimean War 1853-56:** **Russia vs Ottoman Turkey** (Britain, France, Sardinia on the Turkish side).
- **Boxer Rebellion:** China, 1899-1901, peak **1900**.
- **1911 Revolution (Xinhai):** ended the Qing; **Republic of China** (1912).
# Ancient and Renaissance firsts
- **First proper writing system:** **Sumerian** cuneiform (Mesopotamia), before Egyptian hieroglyphs; Indus script undeciphered.
- **Mona Lisa** = **Leonardo da Vinci** (Italian Renaissance).
