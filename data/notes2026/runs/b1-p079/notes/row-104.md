=== Persian Chronicles and Their Authors
- **Tawarikh** (singular **tarikh**): histories in **Persian**, the administrative language of the Sultans, written by secretaries, administrators, poets and courtiers.
| Work | Author | Subject / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Taj-ul-Maasir** | **Hasan Nizami** (UPPSC 1997) | First official chronicle of the Sultanate; patron **Qutbuddin Aibak** |
| **Tabaqat-i-Nasiri** | **Minhaj-us-Siraj** (State PSC) | Ghurids and early Sultans up to 1260; **earliest account of Bakhtiyar Khalji's Bihar invasion** (BPSC 2018); dedicated to **Nasiruddin Mahmud** |
| **Khazain-ul-Futuh** | **Amir Khusrau** | Alauddin's campaigns (Warangal 1310) |
| **Futuh-us-Salatin** | **Abdul Malik Isami** | Ghaznavids to Muhammad bin Tughlaq; written at the **Bahmani** court c. 1350 |
| **Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi** | **Ziauddin Barani**; another by **Shams-i-Siraj Afif** | Balban to early Firuz; main account of Alauddin's market rules |
| **Fatawa-i-Jahandari** | **Ziauddin Barani** (UKPSC 2024) | Political theory of kingship – advice to Muslim rulers |
| **Futuhat-i-Firuz Shahi** | **Firuz Shah Tughlaq** | The Sultan's own account and ordinances |
| **Tarikh-i-Mubarak Shahi** | **Yahya bin Ahmad Sarhindi** | Sayyid Sultan Mubarak Shah |
| **Jawami-ul-Hikayat**, Lubab-ul-Albab | **Sadiduddin Muhammad Aufi** | **Earliest reference to the magnetic compass in Indian seas** (JPSC 2016) |
- **Matches asked:** Futuh-us-Salatin – **Isami**; Futuhat-i-Firuz Shahi – **Firuz Shah**; Tarikh-i-Firuz Shahi – **Barani**; Khazain-ul-Futuh – **Khusrau** (UP RO/ARO 2023). Barani – Tarikh-i-Firuzshahi; Hasan Nizami – Taj-ul-Maasir; Minhaj – Tabaqat-i-Nasiri; Yahya – Tarikh-i-Mubarakshahi (UPPSC 1997).
- **Periods covered, in order:** Taj-ul-Maasir → Tabaqat-i-Nasiri → Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi → Tabaqat-i-Akbari.
- **Tabaqat-i-Nasiri:** **23 volumes** (tabaqat); English translation by **H.G. Raverty**.
| Later work | Author / note |
|---|---|
| Tabaqat-i-Akbari | **Nizamuddin Ahmad** (not Badauni) |
| Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh | **Badauni** |
| Fawaid-ul-Fawad | **Amir Hasan Sijzi** – conversations of Nizamuddin Auliya |
| Tarikh-i-Firishta (Gulshan-i-Ibrahimi) | **Firishta**; dedicated to **Ibrahim Adil Shah of Bijapur**; calls Telangana "**Tilang**" |
| Muntakhab-ul-Lubab | **Khafi Khan** – Aurangzeb's conquest of Golconda |
| Burhan-i-Maasir | Deccan history; Sadashivaraya's income 12 crore hons |
- **⚠ Not:** Kitab-ul-Hind ≠ Firdausi (Firdausi = Shahnama); Futuh-us-Salatin ≠ Khusrau.

=== Al-Biruni and Kitab-ul-Hind
- **Came to India with Mahmud of Ghazni** (UKPSC 2010) – 11th century; native of **Khwarazm (Khiva)**, present **Uzbekistan**.
- **Kitab-ul-Hind = Tahqiq-i-Hind** (Tahqiq ma li'l-Hind) – written in **Arabic**, c. **1030** (APPSC G2 2011).
- **Learned Sanskrit**; studied Indian religion, philosophy, astronomy, caste – **first Muslim scholar to study the Puranas** (UP Lower 2002).
- **Diwali** recorded on **Kartika Amavasya**.
- Mahmud also patronised **Firdausi** (Shahnama) and **Utbi** (Kitab-ul-Yamini).
~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.29; app notes 1:104 (sub 2); Britannica "al-Biruni"

=== Using the Sources – Bias and Cross-checking
- **Foreign accounts supplement** Indian texts and must be **cross-verified** with inscriptions and archaeology.
- **Court bias:** chroniclers wrote for rewards and glorified the patron; **Amir Khusrau** – court poet of Alauddin, went with **Malik Kafur's 1310** Warangal campaign.
- **Barani:** orthodox **Sunni** view; theorised kingship.
- Firishta's story of **Deva Raya kidnapping Nehal** – **rejected** by modern historians.
