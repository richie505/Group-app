=== Revenue Systems – Todar Mal's Bandobast, Dahsala, Zabti
# Road to the Dahsala
- Sher Shah's survey system → **Karori experiment (1574)** → **Dahsala (1580)** under **Raja Todar Mal**.
- **Karori experiment (1574):** core provinces split into circles each yielding **one crore dams**, under officers called **karoris**, who collected revenue and cultivation data.
- Mansabdari + zabti-dahsala = a **salaried, transferable nobility** + **predictable cash revenue**.
# Raja Todar Mal
- Earlier served **Sher Shah**; built on his survey-based system; **Diwan** (finance minister); one of the Navratnas.
- **Todar Mal = land revenue reforms** (UP Mains 2012) – not law, literature or music.
- **Muzaffar Khan Turbati** was the **first to be given the proper status of Diwan** by Akbar, with Todar Mal as assistant (UP Lower 2003) – not Asaf Khan, Munim Khan or Todar Mal; Todar Mal and **Khwaja Shah Mansur** came later.
| Unit | Fact |
|---|---|
| Gaz-i-Ilahi | Unit of length, about **82 cm** |
| Jarib | **Bamboo pieces joined with iron rings** – replaced hemp rope |
| State share | **One-third** of average produce, paid in cash (**dam**); kind allowed in some areas |
# The systems
| System | Meaning | Region / year |
|---|---|---|
| **Zabti** | Land measured; **dasturs** = cash rates per crop per bigha | **Introduced by Akbar**; core North Indian provinces (Lahore to Allahabad, Malwa, Gujarat) |
| **Dahsala (Ain-i-Dahsala)** | Revenue on the **average produce and prices of the last ten years (1571-80)**, converted into cash | **1580** |
| Batai / **Ghalla-bakhshi** | Crop-sharing | Thatta, Kabul, Kashmir |
| **Nasaq** | Rough assessment from past revenue records | Mainly Bengal |
| **Kankut** | Yield estimated on the standing crop | Where measuring was impractical |
| **Plough assessment** | By **number of ploughs** | **Parts of the Deccan** under Akbar (UPPCS 2017; J.N. Sarkar) |
- **Dahsala = collection of revenue on the average of produce and prices of the last 10 years** (APPSC G2 2017) – NOT a fixed 10-year rate, a 10-yearly measurement, or a 10-year crop-sharing deal.
- **"Bandobast system" = Dahsala** (Jharkhand 2013; JPSC 2013); also called Zabti-Bandobast.
- **Zabti was introduced by Akbar** – not Ghiyasuddin Tughluq, Sikandar Lodi or Sher Shah.

=== Revenue Officials and Revenue Terms
# Revenue officials
| Official | Duty |
|---|---|
| **Amalguzar** (amil) | **Sarkar (district) revenue collector** – assessment and collection of land revenue; extend cultivation |
| **Qanungo** | Kept the **records of revenue payable by villages** / pargana land records |
| **Karkun** | Prepared the **seasonal crop statistics** / recorded cultivation |
| **Bitikchi** | **Accountant** – records on land and produce |
| Karori | Circle yielding one crore dams |
| Potdar | Treasurer |
| Patwari | Village accountant |
- **Amalguzar = assessment and collection of land revenue** (NDA 2002) – law and order was the faujdar's, the household the Mir Saman's, the treasury the potdar's / khazanadar's.
- **APPSC G1 match:** Amalguzar–district revenue collector (II), Karkuns–crop statistics (IV), Qanungo–village revenue records (I), Bitikchi–accountant (III) = **a-II, b-IV, c-I, d-III**.
- **Deshmukh** (medieval Deccan): **collected land revenue** from the peasants and deposited it in the state treasury (APPSC G1) – a hereditary watandar over a pargana.

=== Mansabdari System
- **Introduced by Akbar** (APPSC Technical Education) – not Sher Shah, Jahangir or Aurangzeb; of **Central Asian / Mongol** military origin; basis of Mughal civil and military administration.
- Mansabdars held **combined civil-military, non-hereditary** ranks, paid by **jagir** (or cash).
- Ranks: **10 to 5,000** for nobles (later **7,000**); higher ranks only for **royal princes**.
- **Zat** = personal rank and pay; **Sawar** = horsemen to maintain – dual ranks from **1595-96**; sawar equal to zat = **1st class**.
- **Dagh** (horse branding) – **1573-74**.
- **Horses:** for **every ten cavalrymen, twenty horses** – because horses had to be **rested on the march and replaced in war** (IAS 1999: A and R true, R explains A).
- **Du-aspa sih-aspa** – troopers with two/three horses without raising zat – **Jahangir** (first for Mahabat Khan).
- Land grants: **Iqta – Delhi Sultans; Jagir – Mughals; Amaram – Vijayanagara; Mokasa – Marathas** (UPSC 2000).
- Mansabdar = civil-military, non-hereditary, paid by jagir; ICS = purely civil, fixed salary.

=== Jagirs, Central Ministers and Provincial Units
# Jagirs
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| Tankha | In lieu of salary; transferable every **3-4 years** |
| **Watan** | Zamindar's homeland; **hereditary, not transferable** |
| Mashrut | Conditional, tied to specified duties |
| In'am | Reward; no service obligation |
- **Jagirdari crisis** (Aurangzeb): too many mansabdars for too few jagirs; pressure on peasants.
# Central ministers
| Office | Function |
|---|---|
| Wakil | Highest in rank; lost financial powers |
| Wazir / Diwan-i-kul | Finance – revenue, treasury, accounts |
| **Mir Bakhshi** | Military – dagh, chehra, promotions |
| **Sadr-us-sudur** | Religious affairs, charitable grants |
| **Mir Saman** | Royal household and karkhanas |
# Provincial and local units
| Unit | Officials |
|---|---|
| Suba (**12 in 1580**) | **Subedar** (head), diwan, bakhshi, sadr, qazi |
| Sarkar | **Faujdar** (law and order), **amalguzar** (revenue) |
| Pargana | Shiqdar, amil, qanungo |
| Town | **Kotwal** – law and order in the city |
| Village | Muqaddam (headman), patwari, chowkidar |
- The provincial **Diwan** ran revenue and was an independent check on the subedar.
