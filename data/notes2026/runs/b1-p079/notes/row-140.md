=== Ashtapradhan and Shivaji's Officials
| Minister | Persian / other title | Portfolio |
|---|---|---|
| **Peshwa** | Mukhya Pradhan | **Prime Minister**, general administration |
| **Amatya** | **Majumdar (Mazumdar)** | **Finance** – treasury, accounts, revenue |
| **Sachiv** | **Shurunavis (Surnis)** | **Royal correspondence** and records (PMF: land revenue) |
| **Mantri** | **Waqia-navis (Waknis)** | Intelligence, **king's diary** of daily doings and court happenings, household |
| **Senapati** | **Sar-i-Naubat (Sarnobat)** | **Military commander** |
| **Sumant** | **Dabir** | **Foreign affairs**; **master of ceremonies**, received envoys |
| **Nyayadhish** | – | **Chief justice** |
| **Panditrao** | Danadhyaksha | **Religious affairs** and charitable grants |
- **Shivaji's council of eight (by 1674)** – Maratha, not Gupta, Chola, Vijayanagara, Harsha or Mughal (UPSC 1995, IAS 1998, BPSC 2019 …).
- **Advisory only**: each minister was **directly responsible to the Chhatrapati**; Shivaji could accept or reject advice; not a cabinet – so "each held **independent** charge" is false (UPPCS 1997: A true, R false). Ashtapradhan advisory vs the hierarchical Mughal wazir system.
- Posts **not hereditary**; ministers transferable. All except **Panditrao and Nyayadhish** led campaigns. The Peshwa became hereditary and supreme only later (1720 onwards).
- **All four Persian titles – Mazumdar, Dabir, Waqia-navis, Surnis – were Ashtapradhan officers** (CGPSC 2017).
- **Chitnis** = secretary who **assisted the king with correspondence** (CDS 2002).
- **Sarnobat** also = military officer of a fort; **Deshmukh** (pargana) collected **land revenue** for the state (APPSC G1 2019).

=== Shivaji's Career – Birth, Guru, Forts, Bijapur and the Mughals
- **Born at Shivneri fort** near Pune, **19 February 1630** (Maharashtra government date; PMF); Jadunath Sarkar's **1627** gives the key "**1627, 1674**" (UPPCS 2015). Parents **Shahaji Bhonsle and Jijabai**; **Dadaji Kondadev** = keeper of the **Pune jagir** and tutor.
- **Pune jagir:** first granted to grandfather Maloji by the **Nizam Shahis** (1595); **Shahaji held it as a mansabdar of Bijapur (Adil Shahi)** after 1636 – the key "Adil Shahi" (MPPSC 2010).
- **Guru:** **Samarth Ramdas** (Dasbodh, Shivastuti); saint contemporary **Tukaram**. Ramdas, not Eknath (died 1599), is the APPSC G1 2012 answer.
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1646** | **Torna** – first fort, from Bijapur, at age 16 |
| 1656 | **Raigad** (and Javli) from the **Mores of Javli** – later capital |
| **1659** | **Afzal Khan (Bijapur)** killed with a wagh nakh at the foot of **Pratapgad** |
| 1660 | Siddi Jauhar besieges **Panhala**; escape to Vishalgad |
| **1663** | **Night raid on Shaista Khan** (Aurangzeb's maternal uncle, sent 1660) at Pune (Lal Mahal) |
| 1664, 1670 | **Surat sacked** twice |
| **1665** | **Treaty of Purandar** with **Raja Jai Singh** of Amber (with Diler Khan): **23 of 35 forts** ceded; **Sambhaji** to Mughal service |
| **1666** | **Agra** – house arrest at Jaipur Bhawan; escaped with Sambhaji in fruit/sweet baskets |
| **1672** | **Salher** – Mughals beaten in open battle |
| **1674** | **Coronation at Raigad** (6 June) |
| 1677 | Hyderabad visit; Karnataka campaign – **Gingee, Vellore** (from Venkoji/Ekoji), Ballari |
| **1680** | Died (3 April) |
- After Agra he **recaptured** most Purandar forts (Purandar, Lohagarh, Mahuli …) – but **Shivneri**, held by the Mughals, was **never retaken** (UPPSC 2009).
- **Coronation:** priest **Gaga Bhatt** (Vishweshwar) of Banaras; titles "**Shri Raja Shiva Chhatrapati**" and **Haindava Dharmoddharak** (not "Hindavi Sultan"); era **Rajyabhisheka Shaka**.
- **Golconda alliance (1677, AP angle):** with **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** via **Madanna (Mir Jumla)** and **Akkanna (Sar Lashkar)** – Golconda to fund the southern conquests and share the spoils (Shivaji did not share).
- **Causes of the Maratha rise:** Sahyadri geography (**geographical security**), **Bhakti saints** (religious consciousness), service in **Bijapur and Ahmadnagar** (political awakening) – all of the above (UPPSC 1992); Aurangzeb's 27-year Deccan war **drained** Mughal finances.
- **Big Deshmukhs opposed Shivaji** – they wanted to stay feudal lords of Bijapur (UPPCS 2024: A and R true, R explains A).

=== Revenue System – Chauth, Sardeshmukhi and Land Terms
| Levy | Rate | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **Chauth** | **25% (one-fourth)** of land revenue | **Protection money** levied on neighbouring territories outside direct Maratha rule (**Mughalai**) to be spared Maratha raids |
| **Sardeshmukhi** | **Additional 10%** | Claimed by the king's **hereditary right as Sardeshmukh** |
- The pair **Chauth and Sardeshmukhi** = Maratha taxation (not iqta–jagir, zat–sawar, polaj–parauti) (SSC CGL 2019).
- Funded expansion and political hegemony **without the cost of direct governance**. Recognised by the **Mughals (1719)** and the **Nizam at Palkhed (1728)**; the 1795 chauth burden pushed the Nizam into the Subsidiary Alliance (1798).
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ryotwari-type settlement | State dealt directly with the cultivator; demand 30%, later 40% |
| Kathi | Measuring rod – **Annaji Datto's** survey |
| Jirayat / Bagayat | Dry land / **irrigated** land |
| **Saranjam** | **Service land grants** – abolished by Shivaji, **restored under Shahu and the Peshwas** |
| **Upari** | **Tenancy tenure** of tenants-at-will (no hereditary rights); Thalkari / Mirasdar held hereditary rights |
- **Jagirs abolished** – officials paid in **cash**; hereditary rights of **deshmukhs and deshpandes** (watandars) curbed.
| Unit | Official |
|---|---|
| Prant (province) | Subedar / Sarsubedar |
| Pargana, Tarf | **Deshmukh**, Deshpande |
| Mauza (village) | **Patil** (head), **Kulkarni** (accountant) |
~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.130 (chauth 25%, sardeshmukhi 10%, revenue 30-40%); NextGen Indian History APPSC 05 p.13, p.16 (Palkhed; watandar); old app notes 1:140 sub 5-6

=== Military, Navy, Coins and Records
- **Guerrilla warfare (Ganimi Kava)** with swift cavalry in the Sahyadri; chain of **hill forts on the table mountains** of the Western Deccan – both statements true (CDS 2012).
- **Over 300 forts**; each under three officers of equal rank – **Havaldar** (garrison commander), **Sabnis** (records), **Sarnaubat** (military).
| Fort type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Giri Durg (hill) | Raigad, Pratapgad, Torna, Rajgad, Sinhagad, Panhala |
| Bhu Durg (land) | Vishalgad |
| Jal Durg (sea) | Sindhudurg, Vijaydurg, **Khanderi**, Suvarnadurg, Kolaba |
- Cavalry: **Bargirs** – horses and arms from the **state**; **Shiledars** – **own horses**. Infantry: **Mavalis**.
- **Navy:** sea forts guarded the **Konkan**; Shivaji = "**Father of the Indian Navy**".
- **Cannons and ammunition** were obtained from the **Portuguese** (treaty supplies from Goa) (SSC 2011).
- **UNESCO 2025:** "**Maratha Military Landscapes of India**" (12 forts) inscribed as World Heritage.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hon / Shivrai | **Gold hon**; **copper Shivrai** |
| Rajvyavahar Kosh | Replaced **Persian official terms with Sanskrit** |
| **Modi script** | Cursive script of **Maratha** (Marathi) documents – not Wodeyar, Zamorin or Hoysala |

=== Successors of Shivaji – Sambhaji, Rajaram, Tarabai, Shahu
| Ruler | Period | Key steps |
|---|---|---|
| **Sambhaji** | 1680-89 | Won the succession fight with **Rajaram** (backed by Shivaji's widow Soyarabai); sheltered Prince Akbar; captured at Sangameshwar and **executed by Aurangzeb (1689)** |
| **Rajaram** | 1689-1700 | Resisted from **Gingee (Jinji)**; created the post of **Pratinidhi** |
| **Tarabai** (regent for Shivaji II) | 1700-07 | Rajaram's widow; **led the struggle against the Mughals from 1700**; **led the Marathas when Aurangzeb died (1707)** |
| **Shahu** | 1707-49 | Sambhaji's son; **released by Bahadur Shah I (1707)**; beat Tarabai at **Khed (1707)**; ruled from **Satara**; made **Balaji Vishwanath Peshwa (1713)** |
- **Order (UPPSC 2005):** **Sambhaji → Rajaram → Shivaji II (Tarabai) → Shahu**.
- **Treaty of Warna (1731):** between **Sambhaji II of Kolhapur and Shahu of Satara** – Tarabai's line settled at **Kolhapur**.
- **Current linkage:** **Aurangabad was renamed Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar** (state decision 2022, notified 2023) after Sambhaji; Osmanabad became Dharashiv.
- Peak of Maratha power: **Attock to Cuttack**; major northern expansion under **Baji Rao I**.
