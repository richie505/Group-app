=== Guru Gobind Singh and the Khalsa
- **Guru Gobind Singh** (1666-1708) = **tenth and last human Guru** of the Sikhs – not Tegh Bahadur, Har Krishan or Banda Bahadur.
- **Born 1666 at Patna** (Bihar) – Takht Sri Patna Sahib marks the birthplace.
- **Founded the Khalsa** ("army of the pure") on **Baisakhi, 13 April 1699**, at **Anandpur** (Takht Sri **Keshgarh Sahib**, Rupnagar) – not Amritsar; the **Panj Pyare** (five volunteers) were baptised first with sacred nectar.
- **His greatness:** he **turned the Sikhs into a military order** (Khalsa; surnames **Singh / Kaur**) – he did **not** found the Sikh state (that was Banda Bahadur, 1710) and did not defeat Aurangzeb.
- **Five Ks:** **Kesh** (hair), **Kangha** (comb), **Kara** (steel bangle), **Kachera** (undergarment), **Kirpan** (sword) – **Kirtan is not** one of them.
- Wrote the **Zafarnama** (Persian letter to Aurangzeb accusing the Mughals of betrayal); Dasam Granth at **Paonta Sahib**; the **Chali Mukte** (40 liberated ones) died defending him at **Muktsar** (Maghi).
- Fought Aurangzeb from 1699; made peace with **Bahadur Shah**; killed at **Nanded** (1708); declared the **Guru Granth Sahib the eternal Guru** – personal Guruship ended.
- **"How many years ago" (MPPSC 2000):** 1699 → about **300 years**.
- **350th birth anniversary (Prakash Parv):** PM attended the celebrations at **Patna** (Gandhi Maidan) on **5 January 2017** and released a stamp – not Nanded, Amritsar or Anandpur.
> Gobind Singh: Patna 1666 → Khalsa, Anandpur, Baisakhi 1699 → Nanded 1708; 10th and last Guru.

=== Maharaja Ranjit Singh
- Born **1780** (13 Nov; Spectrum gives 2 Nov) at **Gujranwala**; son of **Maha (Mahan) Singh**, chief of the **Sukerchakia misl** – not Bhangi, Ahluwalia, Ramgarhia, Singhpuria or Sandhawalia.
- Father died when he was a boy (12 per Spectrum; PMF gives 1790, aged 10); called **"Sher-e-Punjab" (Lion of Punjab)**.
- **Zaman Shah** of Afghanistan invaded Punjab (1798); Ranjit Singh returned his cannons lost in the Jhelum, and in **1799 Zaman Shah made him governor of Lahore with the title "Raja"**.
- **1799: captured Lahore** → **political capital Lahore**, **religious capital Amritsar** (Amritsar taken 1802/1805) – not Patiala, Kapurthala or Multan.
- **1801:** proclaimed **Maharaja**; army on European lines (**Allard**, Ventura; **Fauj-i-Khas**); commander **Hari Singh Nalwa**.
- **Treaty of Amritsar, 25 April 1809** (Lord **Minto**; negotiated by **Charles Metcalfe**): **Sutlej** = boundary; Ranjit Singh gave up the cis-Sutlej states and turned west.
- **Conquests:** **Multan 1818, Kashmir (Srinagar) 1819**, Peshawar 1834 – his empire included **Srinagar**, not Delhi, Kabul or Makrana (Rajasthan).
- **1813:** got the **Kohinoor** from **Shah Shuja** (Nadir Shah had taken it from Delhi in 1739); **Tripartite Treaty 1838** (British, Shah Shuja, Ranjit Singh).
- **Secular ruler:** Hindu and Muslim ministers; blind in one eye – "**God intended me to look upon all religions with one eye, that is why He took away the light from the other**" = **Ranjit Singh**.
- High court at Lahore = **Adalat-i-Ala**; covered the Harmandir Sahib in gold → "Golden Temple".
> Sukerchakia → Lahore 1799 (Zaman Shah's "Raja") → Amritsar treaty 1809 (Sutlej) → Kohinoor 1813 → Kashmir 1819 → died 1839.

=== Gurus and the Mughal Emperors – Martyrdoms
| Guru | Emperor | Relation |
|---|---|---|
| **Ram Das** (4th) | **Akbar** | Friendly – grant of about **500 bighas** of land where Ramdaspur (Amritsar) grew |
| **Arjan Dev** (5th) | **Jahangir** | **Executed 1606** for helping rebel Prince **Khusrau** – **first martyr Guru** |
| **Hargobind** (6th) | **Jahangir** | Held in **Gwalior Fort**, freed 1619 (with 52 prisoners – Bandi Chhor Divas) – not executed |
| Har Rai, Har Krishan | Aurangzeb | Conflict, summoned / detained |
| **Tegh Bahadur** (9th) | **Aurangzeb** | **Publicly executed at Delhi (Chandni Chowk), 1675**, for refusing Islam – "**Hind-di-Chadar**" |
| **Gobind Singh** (10th) | Aurangzeb, then **Bahadur Shah** | Wars from 1699; Zafarnama; later peace with Bahadur Shah |
- **Sikh Guru executed by Aurangzeb = Tegh Bahadur** (not Arjan Dev, Gobind Singh, Ram Das or Angad).
- **Gurus put to death by the rulers = Arjan Dev (1606) and Tegh Bahadur (1675)** – Angad was not, and Hargobind was only imprisoned.
- **Sis Ganj** = place of Tegh Bahadur's execution; **Rakab Ganj** = his cremation; 350th martyrdom year observed in 2025-26.
> Akbar–Ram Das (land); Jahangir–Arjan (1606) and Hargobind (Gwalior); Aurangzeb–Tegh Bahadur (1675).

=== After Ranjit Singh – Kharak Singh to Duleep Singh
- Ranjit Singh died **June 1839** → disputed successions: **Kharak (Khadag) Singh (1839, immediate successor)** → Nau Nihal Singh → Sher Singh → **Duleep (Dalip) Singh (1843)** – **Hari Singh Nalwa** was a general, not a successor.
- **Duleep Singh** = youngest son, crowned at five (1843) with mother **Rani Jindan (Jind Kaur)** as regent = **last king of the Sikh empire (1843-1849)**.
- First Anglo-Sikh War 1845-46 (Treaty of **Lahore**, 9 March 1846; Treaty of **Bhairowal**, Dec 1846); Second 1848-49 → **Dalhousie annexed Punjab (1849)**.
- Raised in England, **converted to Christianity (1853)**, **returned to Sikhism (1886)**; **went to Russia** (1887) seeking Tsar Alexander III's help.
- **Died in Paris, October 1893** (22 Oct in most references; some books and the UPPCS key give 23 Oct); **buried with Christian rites at Elveden, England** – not cremated at Nasik.
> Kharak Singh first, Duleep Singh last; Duleep: Christian 1853 → Sikh 1886 → Russia 1887 → died Paris 1893, buried Elveden.

=== Guru Nanak, the Word "Sikh" and the Ten Gurus
- **"Sikh"** = **disciple** (from Sanskrit **shishya**, learner) – not soldier, monk or devotee; **Khalsa** = "pure".
- **Guru Nanak** (**1469-1539**) = **founder of Sikhism**; born at **Talwandi = Nankana (Sahib)** near the Ravi (now Pakistan) – not Amritsar, Nabha, Nanded, Gurdaspur or Lahore.
- Teachings: one formless God (**Ik Onkar**), **Naam Simran**, **Sangat**, **Langar**, honest householder life; against idol worship and rituals of both faiths.
- **Successor = Bhai Lehna → Guru Angad** (appointed by Nanak before his death, 1539) – not Amar Das or Ram Das.
| No. | Guru | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nanak Dev** | Founder; Ik Onkar; appointed Angad |
| 2 | **Angad Dev** | Started / standardised **Gurmukhi** script |
| 3 | **Amar Das** | **22 Manjis**; strengthened langar |
| 4 | **Ram Das** | Founded **Ramdaspur (Amritsar)**; land from Akbar; holy tank |
| 5 | **Arjan Dev** (1581-1606) | Compiled **Adi Granth (1604)**; built **Harmandir Sahib**; first martyr |
| 6 | **Hargobind** | Militarised the Panth; **Miri-Piri**; **Akal Takht** (1609); Gwalior Fort; battle of Kartarpur |
| 7 | **Har Rai** | Herbal medicine, service |
| 8 | **Har Krishan** | Child (youngest) Guru |
| 9 | **Tegh Bahadur** | Born **Tyag Mal**; founded **Anandpur Sahib (Chak Nanki)**; 116 hymns; executed 1675 |
| 10 | **Gobind Singh** | Born Patna 1666; **Khalsa 1699**; Five Ks; Guruship to the Granth |
> Sikh = disciple; Nanak (Talwandi 1469) → Angad (Gurmukhi) → Amar Das (Manjis) → Ram Das (Amritsar) → Arjan (Adi Granth).

=== Amritsar, the Golden Temple and Sacred Places
| Place | Link |
|---|---|
| **Amritsar** (first **Ramdaspur**) | City and tank founded by **Guru Ram Das** on land granted by Akbar |
| **Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple)** | Built / completed by **Guru Arjan Dev** (1604), who installed the Adi Granth; gold covering by Ranjit Singh |
| **Akal Takht**, Amritsar | Guru Hargobind, 1609 – temporal seat |
| **Anandpur Sahib** (Rupnagar) | Founded by Tegh Bahadur; Khalsa born here 1699 (Keshgarh Sahib); **Hola Mohalla** |
| **Patna Sahib** | Birthplace of Gobind Singh |
| **Damdama Sahib** (Talwandi Sabo, Bathinda) | Final version of the Guru Granth Sahib (1705); Baisakhi fair |
| **Hazur Sahib, Nanded** (Godavari, Maharashtra) | **Death / cremation place of Guru Gobind Singh (1708)** – Takht Sachkhand Sri Hazur Abchalnagar Sahib |
| Sri Muktsar Sahib | **Maghi Mela** (Chali Mukte) |
- **Amritsar was established by Guru Ram Das**; **Golden Temple completed by Guru Arjan Dev**.
- **Nanded is sacred because of Guru Gobind Singh** (stabbed by an Afghan, died there 1708) – not Amar Das, Angad or Arjan.
> Ram Das = city; Arjan = temple; Hargobind = Akal Takht; Nanded = Gobind Singh's samadhi.

=== Guru Granth Sahib
- **Adi Granth compiled by Guru Arjan (5th) in 1604**; **final version by Guru Gobind Singh at Damdama Sahib (1705)**; declared the **eternal Guru** (1708).
- Script **Gurmukhi**; language "Sant Bhasha" (mix of Punjabi, Hindi and dialects).
- Contains hymns of six Gurus (incl. **116 hymns of Tegh Bahadur**) and of non-Sikh saints: **Kabir**, Sufi **Sheikh (Baba) Farid**, **Namdev**, **Ravidas** (**41 shabdas**), Jaidev.
- Sikh marriage **Anand Karaj** = four circumambulations of the Granth while **Lavan** hymns are sung.
> Arjan 1604 (Adi Granth) → Gobind Singh 1705 (final) → eternal Guru 1708.

=== Banda Bahadur and the Sikh Misls
- **Original name Lachhman Dev**; Guru Gobind Singh named him **Banda** and sent him north from **Nanded (1708)**.
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1709 | Seized **Samana** |
| 1710 | Defeated **Wazir Khan** at **Chappar Chiri**; conquered **Sirhind** → **first sovereign Sikh state**, capital **Lohgarh** (Sirhind Fateh Diwas, 12-14 May) |
| 1715 | Eight-month siege of **Gurdas Nangal**; captured by **Farrukhsiyar's** forces |
| 1716 | **Executed at Delhi** by **Farrukhsiyar** for refusing to convert |
- Abolished zamindari, gave farmers ownership of land; **coins in the names of Guru Nanak and Guru Gobind Singh**.
- After him: Bandai and Tat Khalsa split (healed 1721, Bhai Mani Singh); **Dal Khalsa** (Kapur Singh) = combined force; Khalsa rule between the **Sutlej and Jamuna**.
- **12 misls** (misl = "equal"; democratic military brotherhoods): **Bhangi, Ahluwalia, Ramgarhia, Kanhaiya, Sukerchakia, Phulkian** … – Kanphata were Nath yogis, not a misl.
> Banda: Lachhman Dev → Sirhind 1710 (Lohgarh) → executed 1716 by Farrukhsiyar; 12 misls → Ranjit Singh (Sukerchakia).
