=== Kohinoor, Golconda Diamonds and the English at Machilipatnam
# Kohinoor – the most-asked fact of this topic
- **Mine:** the **Kohinoor** came from the **Kollur (Kolluru)** mine on the **Krishna** (Guntur / Palnadu) – APPSC G2 2011; AP History PYQ ×2. Not Paritala, Gollapalli, Malapalli, Panna or Hazaribagh.
- **Kingdom:** Kollur lay in the **Golconda** kingdom, so "excavated from the mines of" = **Golconda** (SSC 2006) – not Bijapur, Odisha or Chota Nagpur.
- **Found:** **1656** (NextGen); it is now in the British Crown Jewels.
- **Presented to Shah Jahan by:** **Mir Jumla**, minister of **Abdullah Qutb Shah**, who went over to the Mughals (UPPSC 2015 ×2) – not Aurangzeb, Murad or Abul Hasan.
- **⚠ Trap:** **Tavernier**, the **French** jeweller who visited the mines under **Abdullah Qutb Shah** and wrote *Six Travels*, only **recorded** the Kohinoor; he did not give it.
- **Duarte Barbosa** (Portuguese, early 16th c.) also described the region's **diamond trade**; Tavernier gave the fullest account.
# Golconda diamond mines and trade
| Mine | Region |
|---|---|
| **Kollur** | Krishna river, Guntur – the Kohinoor |
| **Paritala** | Krishna district |
| Ramalla Kota | Kurnool – 3 lakh pagodas a year |
| Vajra Karur | Anantapur |
- Diamonds were traded at **Moti Darwaza** (Golconda) and polished at **Karwan** (west of Charminar); mines were given on lease.
- **Latest:** the **Golconda Blue** diamond (**23.24 ct**, fancy vivid blue, pear-shaped) was in the news in **May 2025**; Machilipatnam port works revive the old **Gilakaladindi** port.
# Europeans at Machilipatnam
| Company | Machilipatnam factory |
|---|---|
| **Dutch** | **1605** – first European factory in Andhra, under **Muhammad Quli** |
| **English** | **1611** – **Captain Hippon**, ship **Globe**; permission of **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** |
- **Who allowed the English factory (1611):** **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** (1580–1612) – APPSC G2 2008 ×2; 2023 screening; AP History PYQ ×2. Not Ibrahim (died 1580), Abdullah (from 1626), Sultan Muhammad or Jamshid.
- **Golden Farman (Bangaru Farmana):** **Abdullah Qutb Shah** gave the English trading rights (1632 in the old notes; NextGen dates it 1636).
- Machilipatnam was famous for **muslin and Kalamkari** and was the outlet for Golconda diamonds; **Narsapuram** (Godavari delta) was the chief **shipbuilding** port.

=== Qutb Shahi Rulers – Founder to Tana Shah
# Basics
- **1518–1687**, **7 rulers**, **Shia**; **Qara Qoyunlu (Black Sheep) Turkmen** from **Hamadan**; official language **Persian**; capital **Golconda** (old name **Mankal**), from 1592 **Hyderabad**.
- **"From where did the Qutb Shahis rule":** **Golconda** (G2-B 1991). **Golconda today** lies in **Hyderabad** (MPPSC 1990).
# The seven rulers
| Ruler | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk** (1518–43) | **Founder** (Bahmani Tarafdar of Golconda from 1496; title **Qutb-ul-Mulk** named the dynasty; independent **1518**); **31 years** counted from **1512**; strengthened Golconda fort, built **Golconda Jama Masjid**; took Rachakonda, Devarakonda, Warangal (from Shitab Khan), Rajahmundry, Eluru, Kovilakonda; killed by son **Jamsheed** |
| **Jamsheed** (1543–50) | Patricide; won Battle of **Koulan** over Bidar (1544); created cabinet – **Wakil, Peshwa, Mir Jumla**; then minor son **Subhan Quli** |
| **Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah** (1550–80) | **7 years exile at Vijayanagara**; title **Malkibharama**; chief Telugu patron; Talikota (1565) ally; **Hussain Sagar (1562)**; **first Qutb Shahi coins**; high walls, 87 towers and 8 gates of Golconda; "Second Egypt"; gained **Kondavidu** after 1565 |
| **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** (1580–1612) | **Ascended 1580 at 14**; founded **Hyderabad (1591)**; Charminar, Dar-ul-Shifa (1595), Jama Masjid; Dakhni/Urdu poet **"Maani"** (*Kulliyat-e-Quli*); Dutch (1605) and English (1611) at Machilipatnam |
| **Sultan Muhammad Qutb Shah** (1612–26) | Nephew and son-in-law of Muhammad Quli; began **Mecca Masjid (1614)**; pen name **Zillullah** |
| **Abdullah Qutb Shah** (1626–72) | **Longest reign**; with mother **Hayat Bakshi Begum**; **Inqiyad Nama (1636)** – accepted Mughal suzerainty; **Bangaru Farmana** to the English; Kshetrayya; Tavernier |
| **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (1672–87) | **Last ruler**; Madanna–Akkanna; treaty with **Shivaji (1677)**; Peshwa renamed **Diwan** |
- **Founder of the Golconda dynasty:** **(Sultan) Quli Qutb Shah** – not Jamshed, Subhan or Ibrahim.
- **Malkibharama / Malikibharam:** **Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah** (G1 2002; G1 PYQ) – a title from his subjects for tolerant rule; coinage does not explain it.
- **Last king:** **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (G1 PYQ) – title "Tanisha" from Sufi saint Shah Raju Qattal.
- **How long Quli Qutb Shah ruled:** **31 years** (1512–1543; Deputy EO screening). ⚠ From formal independence (1518) it is only 25 years.
- **Built by Muhammad Quli:** **Hyderabad city, Charminar, Jama Masjid** – **not Golconda** fort, which was a Kakatiya fort (Mankal) rebuilt by Sultan Quli and Ibrahim (G1 2003).

=== Ibrahim Adil Shah II of Bijapur – Jagadguru and Kitab-i-Nauras
# Ibrahim Adil Shah II (1580–1627)
- **"Jagadguru":** hailed so by his subjects for his **mixed-faith (secular) outlook** and support of Hindu scholars (UPSC 2000 ×3) – not Zain-ul-Abidin (Kashmir), Husain Shah (Bengal) or Mahmud II.
- ***Kitab-i-Nauras*** (Book of Nine Rasas): written by **Ibrahim Adil Shah II** in **Dakhni** – songs to **Saraswati, Ganesha** and Muslim saints; **59 songs, 17 couplets** (UPPSC 2020).
- **UPSC 2003 statements:** Kitab-i-Nauras by Ibrahim Adil Shah II ✓; **Amir Khusrau** originated the early **Qawwali** in India ✓ → **both correct** (asked ×3).
- Founded **Nauraspur**; **Firishta** dedicated his history to him; a painter of the **Deccan Ragamala**; married **Chand Sultana** (Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah's sister).
- **Bangab Nama** ("Book of the Pot Smoker"), a Dakhni poem praising bhang, was written by **Mahmud Bahri** of Bijapur (G1 PYQ) – not either Ibrahim Adil Shah or Namdev.

=== Bijapur – Gol Gumbaz, Goa and the Adil Shahis
# Gol Gumbaz
- **Tomb of Muhammad Adil Shah** (17th c., 1656), who built it for himself (SSC 2008; UPPSC 2010) – not Yusuf or Ismail Adil Shah or Mahmud Gawan.
- At **Bijapur** (Vijayapura, Karnataka) – SSC CGL 2019.
- Its dome is **one of the largest in the world** (IAS 1995) – not Sher Shah's tomb, Jama Masjid or Ghiyasuddin Tughluq's tomb; famous **whispering gallery**.
- **⚠ NOT true:** "there is **no mosque and no arches** near the tomb" – the complex has a **mosque** and arches (G2 2003; AP History PYQ).

=== Ahmednagar – End of the Nizam Shahis, Chand Bibi and Malik Ambar
# How the Nizam Shahis ended (UPSC 2004 ×3)
- **Shah Jahan** annexed **Ahmadnagar** (**1633**) and the last ruler **Husain Shah (Nizam Shah III)** was **imprisoned for life at Gwalior**.
- **Mahawat Khan** won **Daulatabad** by bribing **Fath Khan** (10.5 lakh); **Shahji Bhonsle** then set up a puppet, **Murtaza Nizam Shah III** (1634), and held out till **1636**, when the last lands were split between the Mughals and Bijapur.
- **⚠ Wrong options:** "Malik Ambar defeated in 1631 and the royal family killed" (Malik Ambar died in **1626**); "Fateh Khan usurped the throne".
# Chand Bibi and Malik Ambar
- **Chand Bibi** is linked with **Ahmadnagar** (CG Patwari 2019): daughter of **Hussain Nizam Shah I**; married **Ali Adil Shah** of Bijapur (anti-Vijayanagara alliance); defended Ahmadnagar against the **Mughals** (1595–96); died when it fell (**1600**).
- **⚠ Chand Sultana** (wife of Ibrahim Adil Shah II) is **not** Chand Bibi.
- **Malik Ambar:** **Abyssinian** regent of the Nizam Shahis; fought **Jahangir**; revenue on **Todar Mal's lines** – survey, fixed assessment, **no revenue farming**; founded **Khirki → Aurangabad**.

|---|---|---|

- Muharram was celebrated in Andhra as **Peerla Panduga**.
- **Bonalu** began at the **Jagadamba Mahankali temple**, Golconda fort; roots in the **Kakatiya and Qutb Shahi** periods.

=== The Five Deccan Sultanates – Origin and Capitals
- The **Bahmani** kingdom (Hasan Gangu, 1347, Gulbarga) split into **five** successor states – so Adil Shahi, Nizam Shahi and Qutb Shahi all came out of it (**all of the above**).
| Dynasty | Capital | Founder, year | End |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adil Shahi** | **Bijapur** | **Yusuf Adil Shah**, 1489/90 (first to break away) | Aurangzeb, 1686 |
| **Nizam Shahi** | **Ahmadnagar** | **Malik Ahmad** (Ahmad Nizam Shah), 1490 – founded Ahmadnagar city | Shah Jahan, 1633 |
| **Imad Shahi** | **Berar** (capital **Ellichpur / Achalpur**) | Fathullah **Imad-ul-Mulk**, 1490 | Annexed by Ahmadnagar, 1574 |
| **Barid Shahi** | **Bidar** | **Amir Ali Barid** – "cunning fox of the Deccan" | Bijapur, 1619 |
| **Qutb Shahi** | **Golconda**, later Hyderabad | **Sultan Quli (Quli Qutb Shah)**, 1518 | Aurangzeb, 1687 |
- **IAS 2008 match:** Adil Shahi–Bijapur, Barid Shahi–Bidar, Imad Shahi–Berar, Nizam Shahi–Ahmadnagar, Qutb Shahi–Golconda → **4 3 2 1 5**.
- **Wrongly matched (UPPSC 2004):** **Yusuf Adil Shah – Ahmadnagar** (he founded Bijapur). Baz Bahadur–Malwa, Qutb Shah–Golconda, Muzaffar Shah–Gujarat are correct.
- **Bijapur** = first to break away (1489); **Golconda** = last to break away (1518) and last to fall (1687). "Berar was founded by" = **Imad Shahis** (APPSC G2 2005).

=== Mughal Annexation of the Deccan and the Fall of Golconda
- **Talikota (1565):** four of the five – **all except Berar** – combined to defeat Vijayanagara.
| Emperor | Action |
|---|---|
| **Akbar** | Ahmadnagar city (**1600**); Asirgarh / Khandesh (1601) |
| **Shah Jahan** | Ended the Nizam Shahis (**1633**); 1636 treaties with Bijapur and Golconda (Inqiyad Nama) |
| **Aurangzeb** | Attacked Golconda (1656) as subedar; annexed **Bijapur (1686)** and **Golconda (1687)** |
- **Aurangzeb's two Deccan conquests:** **Bijapur and Golconda** (UPPSC 2004) – Ahmadnagar was Shah Jahan's, Bidar went to Bijapur.
- **Golconda fell in 1687** (Medical Officer 2021; Dy. EO 2008): on **21 September 1687** the traitor **Abdullah Khan Pani** opened the **Fateh Darwaza**; **Abdur Razzaq Lari** fought bravely; Golconda became the **21st Mughal Suba**.
- **Abul Hasan** was imprisoned at Bidar, then **Daulatabad**; died **1699** (NextGen: 1700); buried at **Khuldabad**.
- **Akkanna and Madanna** were murdered in **1686**, just before the fall.
- Order: 1600 Ahmadnagar city → 1633 Nizam Shahis → 1686 Bijapur → 1687 Golconda.

=== Qutb Shahi Administration and Revenue
# Administration
- Hierarchy: **Sultan → Taraf (Tarafdar) → Sarkar (Faujdar) → Pargana (Tahsildar) → Village**; Abdullah: **6 Tarafs**; Abul Hasan: **37 Sarkars, 517 Parganas**.
| Official | Duty |
|---|---|
| **Peshwa / Wakil** | Prime minister; renamed **Diwan** under Abul Hasan (held by Madanna) |
| **Mir Jumla** | **Finance** minister |
| **Ain-ul-Mulk** | Military department |
| **Tarafdar** | Governor of a Taraf: law and order, revenue, troops |
| **Faujdar** | Head of a Sarkar |
| **Tahsildar / Deshmukh** | Head of a Pargana |
| **Mukaddam / Kulkarni** | Village head / village accountant |
| **Kotwal** | City police; also ran the **mint** |
| **Mahasibs / Mustasibs** | In charge of **public morals** (like the Mughal Muhtasib) |
| **Shahbandar** | Chief **port** officer, customs |
| **Majumdar / Dabir** | Auditor / secretary |
| **Kayastha** | **Scribes** (record-keepers) |
- **Mahasibs / Mustasibs** (2023 screening) = **public morals** – not chief justice, Taraf head or civil cases.
- **Kayastha** (AP History PYQ) = caste of **scribes** – not traders, physicians or professionals.
- Justice: Sultan → **Shariat Panch** (chief justice) → **Qazi** → **Pargana Majlis**. **Sar Lashkar** = army chief (Akkanna).
# Revenue
- **Main source of income: land revenue** (2023 screening) – collected by **tax farming**, rights auctioned to the highest bidder; not tourism, customs or feudal levies.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mustajirs** | Tax farmers (highest bidders, mostly Hindus) |
| **Haveli** | Crown lands |
| **Dasabandhu** lands | Paid only **one-tenth** |
- **Machilipatnam** was leased for **1,80,000 pagodas** a year.
- Other sources: trade taxes, port customs, diamond-mine leases, taxes on courtesans and professions (military service did not replace taxes).
- **State monopolies: salt, betel leaf, tobacco**.
