## SUB 2 | Official Language – Articles 343-351 and the Commission
[2:0] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [2:0.0] 343 | Union language: Hindi in Devanagari, international numerals; English for 15 years (1950-65)
  [2:0.1] 344 | President's Official Language Commission (after 5 and 10 years) and Parliamentary Committee
  [2:0.2] 345 | State Legislature adopts the State's official language(s)
  [2:0.3] 346 | Language between States / State and Union (two States may agree on Hindi)
  [2:0.4] 348 | English in SC, HCs and Acts
  [2:0.5] 350 / 350A | Representations in any language / mother-tongue instruction at the primary stage
  [2:0.6] 350B | Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities (1957, Allahabad)
  [2:0.7] 351 | Union's duty to develop Hindi
[2:1] - **No national language:** the Constitution gives national-language status to **none**; Hindi is the **official** language of the Union, not a "national language".
[2:2] - **Official Language Commission:** appointed **1955**, chairman **B.G. Kher**; report **1956**; examined by a Parliamentary Committee (1957, **G.B. Pant**); the second commission due in 1960 was **never appointed**.
[2:3] - **Official Languages Act, 1963**: English continues with Hindi after 1965, indefinitely; amended 1967. Under its Section 4 the **Committee of Parliament on Official Language** was set up in **1976**; the **Official Language Rules** also date from **1976**.
[2:4] - **Munshi-Ayyangar formula, 14 Sep 1949** (Hindi Diwas): Hindi in Devanagari after a **15-year** transition.
[2:5] - **Vishwa (World) Hindi Diwas: 10 January** (first World Hindi Conference, Nagpur, 10 Jan 1975); **Hindi Diwas: 14 September**.
[2:6] > Exam angle: Art. 343 does not call Hindi a "national language" and says nothing on English within States or Hindi between States.
[2:7] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Indian Polity 8th ed. ch.74 (polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-02 p.318-320, book p.770-772); app notes 4:68 (sub 6); 1976 Committee and Rules, Vishwa Hindi Diwas: general knowledge, not checked

## SUB 4 | Andhra Pradesh – Telugu and Urdu
[4:0] - **AP Official Language Act, 1966**: **Telugu** official; **2022 Amendment** made **Urdu second official language statewide** (reaffirmed 17 Mar 2026). AP official = **Telugu and Urdu** (2019 PYQ).
[4:1] - Telugu: Dravidian, scheduled, **classical since 2008**; called the "**Italian of the East**" (vowel-ending sound; phrase credited to the 15th-century traveller Niccolo de' Conti).
[4:2] - **CESCT** (CIIL wing) moved to **Nellore**.
[4:3] - **Telugu Language Day – 29 August**, birth anniversary of **Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy** (Vyavaharika Telugu movement; script and dictionaries for Savara).
[4:4] - Earliest Telugu word "**Nagabu**" (Amaravati); first full Telugu inscription **Kalamalla (575 CE, Renati Cholas, Kadapa)**.
[4:5] - Dialects: **Coastal (Krishna-Godavari)** = base of standard Telugu; **Kalinga/Uttarandhra** = Srikakulam-Vizianagaram-Visakhapatnam.
[4:6] - Agency languages: **Savara, Koya, Kui, Gadaba**; Urdu – **Deccani**, from Qutb Shahis (Persian official, Telugu spoken).
[4:7] - **Telugu newspapers:** Eenadu, Sakshi, Andhra Jyothy, Vaartha; **Telangana Today** is an **English** daily (not Telugu).
