=== Types of Migration and Theories
- **Migration:** change of residence from one place to another; **Census** records it by **place of birth** (lifetime migrant) or **place of last residence**. **PLFS Usual Place of Residence**: **6 months**+.
| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| Boundary | **Internal** (between villages, towns, districts, States) / **international** (legal, illegal immigrants, refugees) |
| Time | Temporary or **seasonal** / permanent; return |
| Distance / Number | Short or long / individual or **mass** |
| Cause | Economic, social, political, religious |
| Will | **Voluntary** (weighing push and pull) / **forced** (persecution, development-induced displacement, exploitation) |
- **Refugee**: fears persecution (race, religion, nationality, social/political group); cannot return.
- **Step (stepwise) migration:** village → small town → larger town → **city**, in stages (Ravenstein's "migration by stages").
- **Seasonal / circular migration** is the **dominant trend of migrant labour** in India – back-and-forth with the farm season.
| Theory | Proponent |
|---|---|
| **Push-pull, "A Theory of Migration" (1966)** | **Everett S. Lee** – migration as a permanent or semi-permanent change of residence |
| Laws of migration (1885) | E.G. Ravenstein |
| Mobility transition | Wilbur Zelinsky |
| Migration zone | Griffith Taylor |
- Demographic transition = **Notestein, Thompson**; diet theory = **Doubleday** – not Lee.

=== Streams, Causes and Census Data
| Stream | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Rural → rural** | **Largest** (2011); **female, marriage**; short-distance, intra-state |
| Rural → urban | **Males, employment**, inter-state; drives urbanisation |
| Urban → urban | Developed countries |
| Urban → rural | Smallest; return; counter-urbanisation |
- **Women migrate mainly for marriage**: PLFS 2020-21 female rate **47.9%**, **86.8%** for marriage; male rate **10.7%**, mainly for **employment** (22.8% in search of work; about half employment-related).
- PLFS 2020-21 overall migration rate **28.9%** (rural 26.5%, urban 34.9%).
| Push (origin) | Pull (destination) |
|---|---|
| Low farm income, monsoon failure, drought, rural unemployment | Jobs, higher wages |
| Poverty, poor rural infrastructure | Education, health care |
| Conflict, political instability, disasters | Security, amenities |
- Adults most mobile; information networks (**Keralites, Sikhs**). Rural-urban: no rural jobs, urban labour demand, unbalanced development.
- Migration is the main cause of the rural-urban growth gap (natural increase differs little).
- **Census 2011:** **455.8 million** migrants (last residence) = **37.6%** of the population (nearest option "35%"); 141.2 million moved in 2001-11; **4.1 crore+** inter-state.
- **Source States:** **UP, Bihar** lead. **Destination:** **Delhi, Maharashtra** lead (also TN, Gujarat, AP, Kerala); **Delhi's largest inflow is from Uttar Pradesh**.
- **MoSPI/NSO Migration Survey (Jul 2026-Jun 2027)**: first since **2007-08**; short-term = **15 days-6 months** (earlier 1-6 months).
> Exam angle: largest stream = rural-rural (not rural-urban); women = marriage; men = employment.

|---|---|

=== International Migration, Diaspora and the GCM
- **GCM** (Global Compact for Safe, Orderly and Regular Migration): adopted at **Marrakech, Dec 2018**; endorsed by the **UNGA 19 Dec 2018**; **non-binding**; **international migration only** (not internal displacement); **not adopted by all** UN members (US and others stayed out) – so all three UPSC 2023 statements are wrong.
- **World Migration Report 2026** – **IOM** (1951); **biennial since 2000**; 13th edition.
- Top share: **UAE**; most migrants: **Europe**; top percentage: **Oceania**.
- India: **largest diaspora (35 million+)**; ~3.5 million in UAE.
- **Top remittance recipient: USD 137 billion (2024)** – only one above USD 100 billion; USD 135 billion FY 2024-25, more from skilled professionals.
~~ Sources: app notes 4:69 (sub 6); PMF Human & Economic Geography 01 p.68-69

=== Laws and Schemes for Migrants
- **Inter-State Migrant Workmen (RECS) Act, 1979**: establishments with **5+** migrants; **displacement allowance 50%** of monthly wages + journey allowance; merged into **OSH Code 2020**.
- **OSH Code**: self-migrating workers, annual travel allowance, PDS and social security portability; four Labour Codes (29 laws), **Nov 2025**.
- **e-Shram (2021)**: One-Stop-Solution, 15 schemes, **22 languages (BHASHINI)**.
- **ONORC**: ration portability, all **36** States/UTs.
- **PDOT (Jan 2018)**: MEA, ECR emigrants. **GKRA (2020)**: returnees.
~~ Sources: app notes 4:69 (sub 7)

=== Migration from Andhra Pradesh
- Gulf source: drought-prone **Rayalaseema – Anantapur, Kadapa, Chittoor** (domestic servants, unskilled labour).
- **Konaseema**: ~15,000 to GCC (Dec 2025); **70%+ women**, housemaids.
- Seasonal labour: **Rayalaseema**, north-coastal AP; **Srikakulam fishermen → Gujarat coast**.
- Telugu diaspora: **Gulf + USA**. **APNRT** (AP Non-Resident Telugu Society and **APNRT Trust**): nodal agency for non-resident Telugus – Gulf help desk, and pooling NRT contributions for schools, hospitals and village infrastructure.
- CM: **Bhogapuram airport** will end Uttarandhra migration.
