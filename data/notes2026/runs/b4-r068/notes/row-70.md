=== Trends, Components and Causes of Urbanisation
| Census | Urban % | Towns |
|---|---|---|
| 1901 | 10.84 | 1,827 |
| 1931 | 11.99 | 2,049 |
| 1961 | 17.97 | 2,330 |
| 1971 / 1981 / 1991 | 19.91 / 23.34 / 25.72 | – |
| 2001 | **27.81** (28.6 crore) | 5,161 |
| **2011** | **31.16** (377.1 million) | **7,935** |
| Phase | Years | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Slow | 1901-31 | ~0.35% a year; 33.46 million in 2,049 towns (1931) |
| Medium | 1931-61 | Towns 2,049 → 2,330; 78.94 million urban (1961) |
| Rapid | 1961 onwards | 78.94 → 377.11 million (+262%) |
- **2001-11:** urban **+31.8%** decadal growth; rural/urban **68.84% / 31.16%**; absolute increase urban **91.0 m > rural 90.4 m** – first time since Independence.
- **Census towns 1,362 → 3,894**; statutory towns 3,799 → 4,041.
- **Main reason for the higher urban increase 2001-11: reclassification** of erstwhile rural areas as urban (new census towns), not migration (**APPSC G1 2017**).
- **Three components**: **natural increase**, **net rural-urban migration**, **reclassification / new towns**.
- **Causes (APPSC 2008 – "all of the above")**: growth of **industry and trade** in towns; **population pressure on rural land**; **low farm productivity**; services and transport.
- Hidden urbanisation: World Bank (2010) **55.3%** "urban-like"; EAC-PM ~**63%** (2015, **satellite data**); UN "Degree of Urbanisation" would count peri-urban areas.

=== Census Definitions and Class-size of Towns
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Statutory town | Any place with a municipality, corporation, cantonment board or notified town area committee |
| Census town | Place without urban status meeting the Census criteria (set in **1981**): 5,000+ people, 75% of male workers non-farm, 400+ persons per sq km |
| Urban agglomeration (UA) | Continuous urban spread = town + outgrowths, or two or more contiguous towns |
| Million-plus / metropolitan city | 10 lakh+; Art. 243P metropolitan area = 10 lakh or more |
| Mega city | 10 million+ |
| Rural | Any area that is not urban |
| Class | Population |
|---|---|
| I | 1,00,000 and above |
| II | 50,000-99,999 |
| III | 20,000-49,999 |
| IV | 10,000-19,999 |
| V | 5,000-9,999 |
| VI | below 5,000 |
- **"Top-heavy"**: Class I cities hold **~70%** of urban population (Economic Survey 2025-26; **60.45%** in 2011).
- 1981 criteria criticised for **male bias**, uniform thresholds; retained for **Census 2027**; the definition makes urbanisation look **slower**.
- Statistics term: each group into which raw data are condensed = a **class interval** (not class size or class mark).

=== 74th Amendment, Urban Schemes and Urban Problems
- **74th Amendment, 1992**: in force **1 June 1993**; **Part IXA** (Art. 243P-243ZG) + **Twelfth Schedule** (18 functions). **Art. 243ZE**: Metropolitan Planning Committee; two-thirds elected by municipal members and panchayat chairpersons.
- ULBs: Nagar Panchayat – area in transition; Municipal Council – smaller urban area; Municipal Corporation – larger urban area.
- **16th Finance Commission (2026-31)**: **₹3.6 lakh crore** for ULBs; urban share **36% → 45%**. ES 2025-26: cities "**administered, not governed**" (too many agencies, low own revenue).
| Scheme | Year | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| Smart Cities Mission | 2015 | **100** cities, **4** rounds; includes Visakhapatnam |
| AMRUT | **25 Jun 2015** | 500 cities, now **485**; AMRUT 2.0 (2021) ₹2,99,000 crore |
| HRIDAY | 2015 | **12** heritage cities, incl. **Amaravati** |
| PMAY-U 2.0 | 2024 | **1 crore** families, ₹2.30 lakh crore, to 2028-29 |
| SPM Rurban Mission | 2016 | **Rural** clusters – **Ministry of Rural Development** (not urban rejuvenation) |
| SBM-U 2.0 | **1 Oct 2021** | "Garbage Free" cities |
| Urban Challenge Fund | Budget 2025-26 | ₹1 lakh crore, competition-based |
| City Economic Regions | Budget 2026-27 | ₹5,000 crore per CER |
- **Slums: 6.54 crore** (2011); cities **~3% of land, ~60% of GDP** – ES 2025-26: "**critical economic infrastructure**"; urban population **~60 crore by 2031**.
- **Peri-urban areas**: **recharge zones** for cities. **Right to the City** – **Henri Lefebvre** (1968).
- **UN-Habitat** (1978, **Nairobi**): **World Cities Report 2026** at WUF **Baku** – 3.4 billion face housing shortage, **1.1 billion** in slums; MoU for a Centre of Excellence for Urban Affairs, **Amaravati** (Jul 2026).
- **APMCHUD**: set up **2006**, first meeting **New Delhi**, secretariat hosted by **India**.
- **World Urbanization Prospects 2025 (UN DESA)**: **33 megacities** in 2025, 37 by 2050.
