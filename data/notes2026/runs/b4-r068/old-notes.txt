## SUB 8 | Languages of Andhra Pradesh
[68:8:0] - **AP Official Language Act, 1966**: **Telugu** official; **2022 Amendment** made **Urdu second official language statewide** (reaffirmed 17 Mar 2026). AP official = **Telugu and Urdu** (2019 PYQ).
[68:8:1] - Telugu: Dravidian, scheduled, **classical since 2008**; "**Italian of the East**".
[68:8:2] - **CESCT** (CIIL wing) moved to **Nellore**.
[68:8:3] - **Telugu Language Day – 29 August**, birth anniversary of **Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy** (Vyavaharika Telugu movement; script and dictionaries for Savara).
[68:8:4] - Earliest Telugu word "**Nagabu**" (Amaravati); first full Telugu inscription **Kalamalla (575 CE, Renati Cholas, Kadapa)**.
[68:8:5] - Dialects: **Coastal (Krishna-Godavari)** = base of standard Telugu; **Kalinga/Uttarandhra** = Srikakulam-Vizianagaram-Visakhapatnam.
[68:8:6] - Agency languages: **Savara, Koya, Kui, Gadaba**; Urdu – **Deccani**, from Qutb Shahis (Persian official, Telugu spoken).
## SUB 9 | Bilingualism and Endangered Languages
[68:9:0] - Census 2011: **~26% bilingual, ~7% trilingual**.
[68:9:1] - **UNESCO Atlas: ~197** endangered Indian languages – most of any country.
[68:9:2] - Threats: globalisation, English, migration, nuclear families, no mother-tongue schooling, **lack of scripts** (having a script is not a threat).
[68:9:3] TABLE head: Scheme | Year | Feature
  [68:9:3.0] SPPEL | 2013 | Education Ministry; CIIL Mysuru; languages under 10,000 speakers
  [68:9:3.1] Bhasha Sangam | 2018 | Ek Bharat Shreshtha Bharat; sentences in all 22
  [68:9:3.2] Adi Vaani | 2026 | First AI translation platform for tribal languages
[68:9:4] - **Odisha**: bilingual tribal dictionary series (APPSC 2019); 21 tribal languages in 1,483 schools.
[68:9:5] - Centre's **Devanagari** push for script-less NE tribal languages opposed by Tripura's CPI(M).
[68:9:6] - **Three-language formula:** recommended by the **Kothari Commission (1964-66)** and adopted in the **National Policy on Education, 1968**. **NEP 2020** keeps it with flexibility: at least **two of the three languages native to India**; no language is imposed on any State.
