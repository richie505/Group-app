=== NOTES 3:9 | Plan Models – Harrod-domar, Mahalanobis, Wage-goods (vakil-brahmananda), Gandhian and Bombay Plans
## SUB 0 | Models Compared
[9:0:0] TABLE head: Model | Bottleneck | Priority | Plan
  [9:0:0.0] Harrod-Domar (Roy Harrod, Evsey Domar) | Limited savings | Raise savings and investment | First (1951-56)
  [9:0:0.1] Feldman-Mahalanobis | Capital goods | Heavy industry (steel, machinery) | Second (1956-61)
  [9:0:0.2] Wage-goods (Vakil-Brahmananda, 1956) | Food / wage goods | Agriculture, employment | Alternative to Mahalanobis
[9:0:1] Bottleneck: Harrod-Domar = savings; Mahalanobis = capital goods; wage-goods = food.
## SUB 1 | Plan → Model It Was Based on
[9:1:0] TABLE head: Plan | Model / strategy
  [9:1:0.0] First (1951-56) | Harrod-Domar (with modifications)
  [9:1:0.1] Second (1956-61) | P.C. Mahalanobis (Nehru-Mahalanobis-Feldman; modified Soviet model)
  [9:1:0.2] Third (1961-66), "Gadgil Yojana" | Sandy-Chakravarty model; import substitution
  [9:1:0.3] Fourth (1969-74) | Allen Manne – Ashok Rudra model; Gadgil strategy (growth with stability)
  [9:1:0.4] Fifth (1974-79) | Dhar model
[9:1:1] - **Solow (Solow-Swan):** neoclassical model on a **Cobb-Douglas** production function; growth from technology + capital. **No Indian plan was based on it.**
[9:1:2] - Second Plan distractors: **wage-goods, Gadgil, CELP, Tendulkar** – the key is **Mahalanobis**.
[9:1:3] Order 1-2-3-4-5 = Harrod-Domar – Mahalanobis – Sandy-Chakravarty – Manne-Rudra – Dhar.
## SUB 2 | Harrod-domar Model
[9:2:0] # Equation
[9:2:1] - **g = s / k** (g = growth rate, s = savings rate, k = **capital-output ratio**).
[9:2:2] - Growth **rises with savings**, **falls with a higher COR**.
[9:2:3] - Chain: savings ↑ → investment ↑ (multiplier raises output and employment) → k sets output per unit of capital → growth.
[9:2:4] # Capital-Output Ratio (COR)
[9:2:5] TABLE head: Basis | High COR | Low COR
  [9:2:5.0] Meaning | More capital per unit of output; investment gives less output | Given capital gives more output
  [9:2:5.1] Capital productivity | Lower | Higher
  [9:2:5.2] Growth | Slower, even with high savings | Higher potential
[9:2:6] - ★ Low growth despite high savings and capital formation = **high capital-output ratio**.
[9:2:7] - ★ Slow per-capita income growth in India = **high COR + high population growth**.
[9:2:8] # First Plan
[9:2:9] - Started **1 April 1951**; priority **agriculture** (primary sector).
[9:2:10] - Faced refugee influx, food shortage, inflation; succeeded mainly due to **good harvests in its last two years**.
## SUB 3 | Mahalanobis Model
[9:3:0] # Strategy and Sectors
[9:3:1] - **Unbalanced growth**; priority to the **capital goods sector** ("heavy sector strategy").
[9:3:2] - Idea: build machines at home → later self-sufficiency in consumer goods.
[9:3:3] - **Four sectors:** (1) capital goods, (2) factory consumer goods, (3) household / small-scale consumer goods, (4) services.
[9:3:4] - Feldman (USSR, **1928**) and Mahalanobis (**1953**) developed it independently.
[9:3:5] # Included vs Not Included
[9:3:6] TABLE head: Included / emphasised | Excluded / neglected
  [9:3:6.0] Import substitution, self-reliance | Foreign trade – assumed a closed economy
  [9:3:6.1] Dominant public sector (socialistic pattern, IPR 1956) | Consumer goods left to small/cottage sector
  [9:3:6.2] Investment from 7% to 11% of national income | Exports / export promotion
  [9:3:6.3] Heavy industry | Agriculture
[9:3:7] - **Criticisms:** shortages of food and capital; severe **food shortages** and **high inflation** by the late 1950s.
[9:3:8] # Outcome – Second Plan
[9:3:9] - Growth: target **4.5%**, achieved **4.27%**.
[9:3:10] - Steel plants: **Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela**; **Chittaranjan** – locomotives; **Perambur** – coaches; Atomic Energy Commission.
[9:3:11] Mahalanobis neglected **agriculture, consumer goods and exports** – these are "NOT included" answers.
## SUB 4 | P.c. Mahalanobis
[9:4:0] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [9:4:0.0] Role | Architect of the Second Plan
  [9:4:0.1] Indian Statistical Institute | Founded 17 Dec 1931, Kolkata; Institution of National Importance by the ISI Act, 1959
  [9:4:0.2] National Income Committee (1949) | He chaired it → recommended the National Sample Survey; first NSS round 1950-51
  [9:4:0.3] Honours | Padma Vibhushan (1968), FRS (1945), Weldon Medal (1944)
  [9:4:0.4] National Statistics Day | 29 June (his birth anniversary), since 2007

[9:4:2] - Statistics Day 2026 theme: "**Unlocking the Potential of Administrative Data**" (30 Jun 2026).
## SUB 5 | Wage-goods Model
[9:5:0] - Authors: **C.N. Vakil and P.R. Brahmananda** – book "**Planning for an Expanding Economy**" (**1956**).
[9:5:1] - Real constraint = supply of **wage goods** (food, essential consumer goods), **not capital goods**.
[9:5:2] - More wage goods → **disguised unemployed** put to productive work; stresses **employment**.
[9:5:3] - Priority: **agriculture and wage goods** vs Mahalanobis's heavy industry.
## SUB 6 | Non-official Plans → Author and Year
[9:6:0] # Full List
[9:6:1] TABLE head: Plan | Year | Author(s) | Core proposals
  [9:6:1.0] Visvesvaraya Plan ("Planned Economy for India") | 1934 | M. Visvesvaraya ("Father of Indian Planning") | First blueprint; double national income in a decade via industrialisation
  [9:6:1.1] National Planning Committee (Congress) | 1938 | Set up under Subhas Chandra Bose; chaired by Nehru | Precursor
  [9:6:1.2] Bombay Plan ("A Plan of Economic Development for India") | 1944 | 8 Bombay industrialists incl. J.R.D. Tata, G.D. Birla, Kasturbhai Lalbhai | Big State role, public-sector dominance, licensing, protection of infant industry; treble national income in 15 years
  [9:6:1.3] Gandhian Plan | 1944 | Shriman Narayan Agarwal | Agriculture, cottage and village industries; self-contained villages (⚠ APPSC key (2002) marked Gandhian Plan – Shriman Narayan as the mismatched pair); Swadeshi, Sarvodaya, Trusteeship
  [9:6:1.4] People's Plan | 1945 | M.N. Roy | Marxist socialism; basic necessities; equal weight to agriculture and industry
  [9:6:1.5] Sarvodaya Plan | 1950 | Jayaprakash Narayan | Gandhian constructive work and Vinoba Bhave's Sarvodaya
[9:6:2] - **Chronology:** NPC (1938) → Bombay and Gandhian Plans (1944) → People's Plan (1945) → First Plan (1951).
[9:6:3] - Precursor: **Karachi Resolution (1931)**; **Dalal's Planning and Development Department (1944)**; **Advisory Planning Board (1946)** headed by **K.C. Neogi**.
[9:6:4] - People's Plan echoed in the 1990s slogan "**reforms with a human face**".
[9:6:5] - JP wrote the Sarvodaya Plan on Gandhian ideals; from **1954** committed to the Sarvodaya Movement.
[9:6:6] Bombay Plan was **not** by Rajaji and **Dhirubhai Ambani** was not an architect; it wanted a **big State role**, not a non-interventionist State.
[9:6:7] # Gandhian Ideals – Books and Terms
[9:6:8] - "**Gandhian Constitution for Free India**" – **Shriman Narayan Agarwal** (not M.N. Roy).
[9:6:9] - "**India's Economic Policy: The Gandhian Blue Print**" – **Charan Singh**.
[9:6:10] - **Trusteeship:** the wealthy act as trustees of wealth.
[9:6:11] - **Ram Rajya:** rule on moral authority and justice for the poorest; **not a Hindu state**.
## SUB 7 | Janata Government Alternative Strategy
[9:7:0] - Janata Party came to power in **1977**, during the Fifth Plan; **ended the Fifth Plan a year early (1978)**.
[9:7:1] - Adopted the **Rolling Plan (1978-80)** – **Gunnar Myrdal's** concept.
[9:7:2] - **Emphasis:** **agriculture and rural development, employment, small and cottage industries, minimum needs** – not heavy industry.
## SUB 8 | Imperative vs Indicative Context
[9:8:0] - **Stalin's USSR** began five-year plans in **1928** – **imperative** planning.
[9:8:1] - India follows **indicative** planning.
