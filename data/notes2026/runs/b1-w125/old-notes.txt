## SUB 3 | Classical Dances – Kuchipudi and the Eight Forms
[125:3:0] - **Kuchipudi** = classical dance of **Andhra Pradesh** (MPPCS 1995; UKPCS 2002) – named after **Kuchipudi (Kuchelapuram) village, Krishna district**; shaped by **Siddhendra Yogi**; **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (last Qutb Shahi) gifted the village to the performers; popular shows Bhama Kalapam, Usha Parinayam.
[125:3:1] - **Kuchipudi vs Bharatanatyam (IAS 2012):** dancers **speak dialogue** in Kuchipudi ✓; **Tarangam** – dancing on the **rim of a brass plate** – is a **Kuchipudi** feature, not Bharatanatyam ✗ → **only 1**.
[125:3:2] - Kuchipudi = **dance-drama** tradition (Bhagavata Mela, male Brahmin troupes); Bharatanatyam = solo temple-dance tradition; **Lakumadevi**, court dancer of **Kumaragiri Reddy**, was famed for Bharatanatyam.
[125:3:3] - **Solo dance (IAS 1997):** **Mohiniyattam** (Kerala; women) – Kuchipudi, Bharatanatyam and Odissi are also staged by groups.

  [125:3:4.3] Mohiniyattam | Kerala
  [125:3:4.4] Sattriya | Assam – introduced in the 15th century by the Vaishnava saint Sankaradeva (not Bihu, Jhumar or Bagurumba)
  [125:3:4.5] Odissi | Odisha

[125:3:6] - **Sonal Mansingh** – **Odissi** (also Bharatanatyam); **Padma Bhushan 1992**, Padma Vibhushan 2003 – not Birju Maharaj (Kathak), Mallika Sarabhai or Yamini Krishnamurthy.
[125:3:7] - **Uday Shankar** – blended Indian classical dance with **Western ballet / European theatre** – pioneer of modern Indian dance.
[125:3:8] - **Kuravanji** – a **group dance-drama by women** on a girl's love for her beloved (Tamil; Telugu **Koravanji** = earliest type of Yakshaganam); **Rukmini Devi Arundale** staged Kuravanji (Kutrala Kuravanji) at **Kalakshetra**. APPSC asked "a famous Kuravanji performer" twice: one key **Rukmini Arundale**, the G2 2019 key **Jakka Radhika** (no source names her).

[125:3:10] - **Pandit Birju Maharaj** (Lucknow gharana): widely called the face / father of modern **Kathak**.
[125:3:11] - **Abhinaya Darpanam** (Sanskrit treatise on gesture in dance): by **Nandikeshvara** – Natyashastra is Bharata's, Nritta Ratnavali Jayapa's.

## SUB 6 | Festivals of Andhra Pradesh
[125:6:0] - **Ugadi** = **Telugu New Year** (Chaitra), in AP, Karnataka and Telangana (APPSC JL) – not Dasara, Sankranti or "Panduga".
[125:6:1] - **Sankranti** (January harvest): Bhogi → Sankranti → **Kanuma** (day of **thanksgiving to cattle and birds**, APPSC Endowment 2022); Gangireddulaata, **Kolla Pandalu** (cockfights), Pottella Pandalu (ram fights).
[125:6:2] - **Vinayaka Chavithi** – Vinayaka is worshipped with **21 kinds of leaves** (Ekavimsati Patra Puja) (APPSC Endowment 2022).
[125:6:3] - **Vasantotsavam** (spring festival) – **most popular festival under the Reddi kings** (APPSC G2 2019; AP History PYQ) – not Sankranti, Dussehra or Deepavali; **Kumaragiri Reddy** styled himself **Karpura Vasantarayalu**; merchant **Avachi Tippayashetti** headed the **Sugandhabhandagara** (perfume store) that supplied it.
[125:6:4] - **Atla Taddi** – women's festival of **Ashwayuja** (not Chaitra).
[125:6:5] - **Bathukamma, Bonalu** = Telangana identity festivals.
[125:6:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History - APPSC 12 p.10-14 (Vasantotsavam, Karpura Vasanta Rayalu); NextGen AP History - APPSC 08 p.24; PMF Art & Culture 06 p.10, p.21 (Ugadi); app notes 1:125 (sub 4)
[125:6:7] - **Chandanotsavam, Simhachalam:** the sandal paste of Sri Varaha Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy is removed for **Nijaroopa darshan** and fresh sandalwood paste applied on **Akshaya Tritiya**.

[125:7:1] - **Folk songs:** **Jola patalu** = lullabies; **Kolatam kirtanas** (for the stick dance) – **Annamacharya**; Sankranti songs and Gangireddulaata – January harvest.

=== NOTES 1:126 | Battle of Talikota (1565), the Aravidu Dynasty and the Legacy of Vijayanagara
## SUB 0 | Battle of Talikota (1565) – Rakshasa Tangadi
[126:0:0] - **Date:** **23 January 1565** (some books 26 January) – year **1565** (APPSC FRO 2022, 2023; JL 2004; UPPCS 1993; MPPCS 1997) – not 1526 (Panipat I), 1545, 1555, 1560, 1564 or 1576.
[126:0:1] - **Names:** **Talikota / Tallikota** (Sewell, *A Forgotten Empire*); **Rakshasa-Tangadi / Rakshasi-Tangadi** (NextGen: Nilakanta Sastri; app notes: Fr. Heras); **Bannihatti** (H.K. Sherwani); fought near Tallikota between the **Krishna** and its tributary.
[126:0:2] - **King:** **Sadasiva Raya** (nominal, last Tuluva) – real power with regent **Aliya Rama Raya**, **son-in-law of Krishnadevaraya** (APPSC JL 2006) – not Achyuta Raya, Tirumala Raya or Venkata.
[126:0:3] - **Rama Raya's policy:** the "adventurous ruler" who **played one Sultan against another** until they united (APPSC 2019 Social & Cultural History) – not Krishnadevaraya, Harihara I/II.
[126:0:4] TABLE head: Sultanate | Ruler at Talikota
  [126:0:4.0] Bijapur (Adil Shahi) | Ali Adil Shah
  [126:0:4.1] Ahmadnagar (Nizam Shahi) | Hussain Nizam Shah – beheaded Rama Raya
  [126:0:4.2] Golconda (Qutb Shahi) | Ibrahim Qutb Shah (key organiser)
  [126:0:4.3] Bidar (Barid Shahi) | Ali Barid Shah
[126:0:5] - **Berar (Imad Shahi)** stayed out → enemy = **the Deccan Sultanates / combined forces of Bijapur, Ahmadnagar and Golconda** (APPSC G1; UPPSC 2014) – **not the Bahmani kingdom** (already broken up), the Mughals or the Marathas.
[126:0:6] - **"Who fought Rakshasi-Tangadi?" (APPSC G1):** **the Adil Shahis of Bijapur and Ramaraya** (Rocket Sheet wording) – Berar's Imad Shahis stayed out; Veeranarasimha is a Tuluva of 1505; "Ramaraya and Nizam Shah" names only the sultan who killed him.
[126:0:7] - **Causes:** 1546-47 Rama Raya backed Ahmadnagar against Bijapur and Bidar and took the **Raichur doab**; 1557-63 backed Bijapur and razed Ahmadnagar; sheltered **Jagadeva Rao**, a rebel of Golconda; marriage links – **Chand Bibi–Ali Adil Shah**, Bibi Jamal–Ibrahim Qutb Shah.
[126:0:8] - **Results:** Hampi **looted for about six months**; **Sewell** called it a "graveyard" (possibly exaggerated); Sadasiva and **Tirumala** fled to **Penukonda**; the empire's centre moved to the **Telugu country**; Nayakas grew autonomous.

## SUB 1 | Aravidu Dynasty (1570-1646/80) – Penukonda, Chandragiri, Vellore
[126:1:0] - **Fourth** Vijayanagara dynasty; named after **Araviti (Aravidu)** village, **Kurnool** district.

[126:1:2] - **Capital shifts:** Hampi → **Penukonda** (1570, Tirumala) → **Chandragiri** (Venkatapati II) → **Vellore** (c. 1600).

  [126:1:3.0] Tirumala Raya (1570-72) | Founder, Rama Raya's brother; removed Sadasiva; crowned at Penukonda

## SUB 1 | Babur – Origin, Name and Titles
[127:1:0] - **Name:** **Zahir-ud-din Muhammad** (APPSC G1); "**Babur**" = **tiger** – he took the name Babur (CGPSC 2003); not Abdul Raheem, Umar Sheikh (his father) or Daulat Khan.
[127:1:1] - **Born 14 Feb 1483** at **Farghana (Ferghana)**, Central Asia (CDS 2001) – not Khiva, Khorasan or Seistan; ruler of Farghana at 12 (1494).
[127:1:2] - **Descent:** **Timur** (father's side) and **Chingiz Khan** (mother's side).
[127:1:3] - **Mughals were Turks** – **Chagatai Turks**, named after **Chagatai, second son of Chingiz Khan** (APPSC G1 2002; UDA 2010) – not Mongols, Afghans, Persians or Arabs; they called themselves **Timurids**.
[127:1:4] - **Title Padshah:** first adopted at **Kabul** (taken **1504**; title in 1507), dropping the ancestral "Mirza" (UPPCS 2015) – not Ferghana, Delhi or Samarkand (held only 8 months, 1501).
[127:1:5] - **Founder of Mughal rule in India:** **Babur** (1526) (SSC 2017).

## SUB 1 | Currency, Grand Trunk Road and Sarais
[128:1:0] # Currency
[128:1:1] TABLE head: Coin | Metal | Note
  [128:1:1.0] Rupiya | Silver | 178 grains (173 pure silver); introduced 1540-45; standard for centuries (APPSC G2 2017)
  [128:1:1.1] Dam (daam) | Copper | 380 grains; kept by Akbar as the chief copper coin
  [128:1:1.2] Mohur | Gold | 166-169 grains
[128:1:2] - **Rupiya : Dam = 1 : 64** (UP UDA 2006).
[128:1:3] - **The rupiya / "rupee" was first issued by Sher Shah Suri** (CDS 2009; Jharkhand 2016; MPPSC 1991; APPSC G2 2017) – not Muhammad bin Tughlaq, Alauddin Khalji or Akbar.
[128:1:4] - Pure gold, silver and copper coins; mixed-metal coins abolished; name, title and mint in **Arabic and Devanagari**.
[128:1:5] - **Akbar, like Sher Shah, regulated the currency**, and the **dam** stayed Akbar's chief copper coin – both true, but the dam fact does not explain the regulation (UPPCS 2019).
[128:1:6] - ⚠ Silver coins (the **tanka**) were struck from **Iltutmish's** time; "introduced the coin of silver" in MPPSC 1991 means Sher Shah's **silver rupiya**.
[128:1:7] # Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-e-Azam)
[128:1:8] - **Sadak-e-Azam = Grand Trunk Road** – one of Asia's oldest major roads; **built / restored by Sher Shah Suri** (APPSC 2018; CGPSC 2018) – not Akbar, Jahangir, Islam Shah or Shah Jahan.
[128:1:9] - Route: **Sonargaon (East Bengal) to the Indus** – "**Indus to Sonargaon**" is the longest road (APPSC G1); also given as Sonargaon–Peshawar / Kabul; it **still exists**.
[128:1:10] - **Other Sur roads:** Agra–Jodhpur–Chittor, **Agra–Burhanpur**, **Lahore–Multan**.
[128:1:11] # Sarais
[128:1:12] - Sarais at intervals with shady trees; separate lodging for Hindus and Muslims.
[128:1:13] - Served as **dak-chowkis** (news and mail relay with horses); **market towns** (qasbas) grew around them.

## SUB 3 | Sher Shah – Rise, Titles, Rajput Wars and Death
[128:3:0] # Rise
[128:3:1] - Born **Farid (Farid Khan)**; father **Hasan Sur**, an Afghan **jagirdar of Sasaram** (Bihar); of the **Sur** tribe.
[128:3:2] - Left home (1494 / 1501) and **studied Arabic and Persian at Jaunpur** – the "**Siraj-i-Hind**" (RAS 2013) – not Sasaram, Patna or Lahore; one of the most learned medieval rulers.
[128:3:3] - Managed his father's jagir from **1511**; joined **Bahar Khan** of Bihar, who gave him the title **"Sher Khan"** for killing a **tiger**.
[128:3:4] - Served Babur; deputy regent and de facto ruler of Bihar; victory at **Surajgarh** (1534) was the turning point; took **Gaur** (1538) and **Rohtas**.
[128:3:5] - **Sher Khan's offer:** surrender Bihar and pay 10 lakh dinars a year if allowed to keep Bengal – Humayun refused (BPSC 2022).
[128:3:6] # Titles in order
[128:3:7] TABLE head: Title | When
  [128:3:7.0] Sher Khan | From Bahar Khan (tiger)
  [128:3:7.1] Hazrat-e-Ala | After defeating Nusrat Shah of Bengal
  [128:3:7.2] Sher Shah / Sultan | After Chausa, 26 June 1539; khutba read and coins struck in his name
[128:3:8] - **Hazrat-e-Ala first, Sultan afterwards = Sher Shah Suri** (not Bahlul/Sikandar Lodi or Islam Shah); his titles = **Hazrat-e-Ala and Sultan only** (BPSC 2000) – not Zill-e-Ilahi or Raiyat-e-Ala.
[128:3:9] # Conquests and Rajput resistance
[128:3:10] - **Raisen** taken from **Puran Mal**; Punjab, Malwa, Sind conquered.
[128:3:11] - **Marwar:** **Maldeo Rathore** resisted; battle of **Samel (Sammel / Giri-Sumel), 1544**.
[128:3:12] - Maldeo's generals **Jaita and Kumpa (Jayata–Kumpa) of Marwar** fought so bravely that Sher Shah said: "**I would have lost the empire of Hindustan for a handful of bajra (millet)**" (UPPSC 2007; UP Mains 2007; UPPCS 2022).
[128:3:13] # Death and verdict
[128:3:14] - **Last expedition: Kalinjar** (Kalanjar, a Rajput fort) – died **22 May 1545**, wounded by a **gunpowder explosion** (UP 1993; UP RO 2016).
[128:3:15] - Buried at **Sasaram**; reign only **five years (1540-45)**.
[128:3:16] - He was the **architect of a brilliant administrative system**, and **his qualities as a ruler were MORE remarkable than his battle victories** – so "not more remarkable" is false (UP RO 2021: only 1).
[128:3:17] - Sequence: Chunar submission 1532 → Chausa 1539 → Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque 1541 → death at Kalinjar 1545.

## SUB 4 | Sur Administration and Land Revenue
[128:4:0] # Administrative units and officers
[128:4:1] - Empire divided into **47 Sarkars**, each into **Parganas** (CDS 2014: statement I true); officers were appointed at every level – so "administered sarkar and pargana directly without officials" is false.

  [128:4:2.0] Sarkar | Chief Shiqdar (shiqdar-i-shiqdaran) | Law and order
  [128:4:2.1] Sarkar | Chief Munsif (munsif-i-munsifan) | Revenue / civil matters
  [128:4:2.2] Pargana | Shiqdar | Law and order
  [128:4:2.3] Pargana | Amin (munsif) | Assessment of revenue

  [128:4:2.6] Village | Qanungo, patwari, muqaddam | Revenue / local records

[128:4:4] - **Army:** regular army recruited directly; **Dagh** = branding of horses; **chehra / huliya** = descriptive roll of soldiers; Hindus in high army posts.
[128:4:5] - **Police and justice:** **local headmen** had to trace crime; censors of public morals; efficient **spy network**; uniform, harsh justice.
[128:4:6] - Sher Shah's reforms covered **revenue, administration, army and currency – all four** (UKPSC 2003).

  [128:4:8.3] Payment | Cash or grain
  [128:4:8.4] Settlement | Direct state–peasant (ryotwari-type); illegal demands removed; tenant rights recognised
  [128:4:8.5] Legacy | Model for Todar Mal's Bandobast
[128:4:9] - **Rate of land tax: 1/3 of produce** (APPSC G1 2012) – not 1/4, 1/2 or 1/5.

## SUB 4 | Social and Educational Reforms
[129:4:0] - **Sati** was prohibited / curbed under **Akbar** (WBCS 2018) – widows not to be burnt against their will.

=== NOTES 1:130 | Akbar's Institutions – Mansabdari System, Todar Mal's Bandobast, Dahsala, Zabti and Other Revenue Systems
## SUB 0 | Revenue Systems – Todar Mal's Bandobast, Dahsala, Zabti
[130:0:0] # Road to the Dahsala
[130:0:1] - Sher Shah's survey system → **Karori experiment (1574)** → **Dahsala (1580)** under **Raja Todar Mal**.
[130:0:2] - **Karori experiment (1574):** core provinces split into circles each yielding **one crore dams**, under officers called **karoris**, who collected revenue and cultivation data.
[130:0:3] - Mansabdari + zabti-dahsala = a **salaried, transferable nobility** + **predictable cash revenue**.
[130:0:4] # Raja Todar Mal
[130:0:5] - Earlier served **Sher Shah**; built on his survey-based system; **Diwan** (finance minister); one of the Navratnas.
[130:0:6] - **Todar Mal = land revenue reforms** (UP Mains 2012) – not law, literature or music.
[130:0:7] - **Muzaffar Khan Turbati** was the **first to be given the proper status of Diwan** by Akbar, with Todar Mal as assistant (UP Lower 2003) – not Asaf Khan, Munim Khan or Todar Mal; Todar Mal and **Khwaja Shah Mansur** came later.

## SUB 2 | Mansabdari System
[130:2:0] - **Introduced by Akbar** (APPSC Technical Education) – not Sher Shah, Jahangir or Aurangzeb; of **Central Asian / Mongol** military origin; basis of Mughal civil and military administration.
[130:2:1] - Mansabdars held **combined civil-military, non-hereditary** ranks, paid by **jagir** (or cash).
[130:2:2] - Ranks: **10 to 5,000** for nobles (later **7,000**); higher ranks only for **royal princes**.
[130:2:3] - **Zat** = personal rank and pay; **Sawar** = horsemen to maintain – dual ranks from **1595-96**; sawar equal to zat = **1st class**.
[130:2:4] - **Dagh** (horse branding) – **1573-74**.
[130:2:5] - **Horses:** for **every ten cavalrymen, twenty horses** – because horses had to be **rested on the march and replaced in war** (IAS 1999: A and R true, R explains A).
[130:2:6] - **Du-aspa sih-aspa** – troopers with two/three horses without raising zat – **Jahangir** (first for Mahabat Khan).
[130:2:7] - Land grants: **Iqta – Delhi Sultans; Jagir – Mughals; Amaram – Vijayanagara; Mokasa – Marathas** (UPSC 2000).
[130:2:8] - Mansabdar = civil-military, non-hereditary, paid by jagir; ICS = purely civil, fixed salary.
