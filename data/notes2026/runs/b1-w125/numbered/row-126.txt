## SUB 0 | Battle of Talikota (1565) – Rakshasa Tangadi
[0:0] - **Date:** **23 January 1565** (some books 26 January) – year **1565** (APPSC FRO 2022, 2023; JL 2004; UPPCS 1993; MPPCS 1997) – not 1526 (Panipat I), 1545, 1555, 1560, 1564 or 1576.
[0:1] - **Names:** **Talikota / Tallikota** (Sewell, *A Forgotten Empire*); **Rakshasa-Tangadi / Rakshasi-Tangadi** (NextGen: Nilakanta Sastri; app notes: Fr. Heras); **Bannihatti** (H.K. Sherwani); fought near Tallikota between the **Krishna** and its tributary.
[0:2] - **King:** **Sadasiva Raya** (nominal, last Tuluva) – real power with regent **Aliya Rama Raya**, **son-in-law of Krishnadevaraya** (APPSC JL 2006) – not Achyuta Raya, Tirumala Raya or Venkata.
[0:3] - **Rama Raya's policy:** the "adventurous ruler" who **played one Sultan against another** until they united (APPSC 2019 Social & Cultural History) – not Krishnadevaraya, Harihara I/II.
[0:4] TABLE head: Sultanate | Ruler at Talikota
  [0:4.0] Bijapur (Adil Shahi) | Ali Adil Shah
  [0:4.1] Ahmadnagar (Nizam Shahi) | Hussain Nizam Shah – beheaded Rama Raya
  [0:4.2] Golconda (Qutb Shahi) | Ibrahim Qutb Shah (key organiser)
  [0:4.3] Bidar (Barid Shahi) | Ali Barid Shah
[0:5] - **Berar (Imad Shahi)** stayed out → enemy = **the Deccan Sultanates / combined forces of Bijapur, Ahmadnagar and Golconda** (APPSC G1; UPPSC 2014) – **not the Bahmani kingdom** (already broken up), the Mughals or the Marathas.
[0:6] - **"Who fought Rakshasi-Tangadi?" (APPSC G1):** **the Adil Shahis of Bijapur and Ramaraya** (Rocket Sheet wording) – Berar's Imad Shahis stayed out; Veeranarasimha is a Tuluva of 1505; "Ramaraya and Nizam Shah" names only the sultan who killed him.
[0:7] - **Causes:** 1546-47 Rama Raya backed Ahmadnagar against Bijapur and Bidar and took the **Raichur doab**; 1557-63 backed Bijapur and razed Ahmadnagar; sheltered **Jagadeva Rao**, a rebel of Golconda; marriage links – **Chand Bibi–Ali Adil Shah**, Bibi Jamal–Ibrahim Qutb Shah.
[0:8] - **Results:** Hampi **looted for about six months**; **Sewell** called it a "graveyard" (possibly exaggerated); Sadasiva and **Tirumala** fled to **Penukonda**; the empire's centre moved to the **Telugu country**; Nayakas grew autonomous.
[0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Vijayanagara Kingdom p.5 (Tallikota/Rakshasatangadi/Bannihatti; Hussain Nizam Shah; Penukonda); NextGen AP Rocket Sheet #12 p.17 (Tallikota – between Adil Shahis of Bijapur and Ramaraya); PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.71 (combined forces of Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golkonda and Bidar); app notes 1:126 (sub 0)
## SUB 1 | Aravidu Dynasty (1570-1646/80) – Penukonda, Chandragiri, Vellore
[1:0] - **Fourth** Vijayanagara dynasty; named after **Araviti (Aravidu)** village, **Kurnool** district.
[1:1] - **Capital after 1565 / of the Aravidus:** **Penukonda (Penugonda)** (APPSC G1 1991; G2 2000; AP History PYQ ×3) – not Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Kampili, Kondavidu, Addanki, Vizianagaram, Anantapur or Chandragiri.
[1:2] - **Capital shifts:** Hampi → **Penukonda** (1570, Tirumala) → **Chandragiri** (Venkatapati II) → **Vellore** (c. 1600).
[1:3] TABLE head: Ruler | Key fact
  [1:3.0] Tirumala Raya (1570-72) | Founder, Rama Raya's brother; removed Sadasiva; crowned at Penukonda
  [1:3.1] Sriranga I (1572-85) | Lost Kondaveedu to Ibrahim Qutb Shah
  [1:3.2] Venkatapati Raya II (1585-1614) | Greatest and longest; "Second Krishnadevaraya", "Second Andhra Bhoja"; moved to Chandragiri; literary contests at Venkatapati Vilasam; Vilapakam (1601) and Siddhavatam (1605) inscriptions; Dutch at Pulicat; court scholar Appayya Dikshita
  [1:3.3] Sriranga II (1614) | Killed by Jagga Raya
  [1:3.4] Ramadeva (1616-30) | Battle of Toppur (1616) – "second Talikota", won by Velugoti Yachama Nayaka and Raghunatha of Tanjore
  [1:3.5] Venkatapati III (1630-43) | Madras granted to Francis Day (1639) by Damarla Venkatadri Nayaka (Srikalahasti)
  [1:3.6] Sriranga III (1643-) | Last; defeated by Bijapur and Golconda (Vandavasi; Mir Jumla)
[1:4] - **Jesuits:** **Venkatapati Raya II** allowed Jesuit churches at **Nellore and Chandragiri** (APPSC G1 2023; G1 PYQ) – not Krishnadevaraya, Achyuta Raya, Sadasiva, Devaraya II or Tirumala.
[1:5] - **Mysore:** **Raja Wodeyar** took **Srirangapatna (1610)** and founded the Mysore kingdom under **Venkata II** (IAS 2006 ×2) – not Sadasiva, Tirumala or Ranga II.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Vijayanagara Kingdom p.5-6 (Tirumalaraya at Penukonda; Venkatapatiraya II – Chandragiri; Venkatapatiraya III – Madras); NextGen previous papers p.40 (capital of Vijayanagara after the Tallikota battle – Penugonda); NextGen AP Rocket Sheets (src/ap-history/rocket-ap-history.txt); app notes 1:126 (sub 1, 2, 3)
## SUB 2 | Nayaka Kingdoms, Kshetrayya and Court Literature
[2:0] TABLE head: Kingdom | Founder | Fact
  [2:0.0] Madurai | Vishwanatha Nayaka (after capturing his rebel father Nagama) | Krishnappa made it independent c. 1565; Tirumala Nayaka (1623-59) built the Tirumala Rayala Mahal
  [2:0.1] Tanjore | Sevappa Nayaka (under Achyutaraya) | Raghunatha; last king Vijayaraghava Nayaka
  [2:0.2] Gingee | Krishnappa Nayaka | –
  [2:0.3] Ikkeri (Keladi) | Chowdappa Nayaka | –
  [2:0.4] Mysore | Raja Wodeyar (1610) | under Venkata II
[2:1] - **Kshetrayya** (original name Varadayya; "Pada Kavita Pitamaha"): born at **Movva (Muvva), Krishna district**; padams with the mudra **Muvva Gopala**; sang for **Tirumala Nayaka of Madurai**, **Vijayaraghava Nayaka of Tanjore** and **Abdullah Qutb Shah of Golconda** (1500 padams dedicated to him) → **"All the above"** (APPSC G1 2003).
[2:2] - **Govinda Dikshita** – minister of the **Tanjore Nayaks** (Achyutappa, Raghunatha); wrote ***Sahitya Sudha*** – not Ayyappa Dikshita, Vedanta Desika or Vyasaraya.
[2:3] - **Appayya Dikshita** – *Shaivarka Mani Dipika*; **Ramaraja Bhushanudu** – *Vasucharitra*.
[2:4] - **Amara-nayakas** → hereditary **Palegars** → forerunners of zamindars (Kalahasti, Chittoor); **Mir Jumla** melted temple idols into cannon at **Gandikota (1657)**; Raya gopurams and kalyana mandapas spread through the Nayaka courts.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Qutb Shahis p.8 (Kshetrayya – Mowva; 1500 poems to Abdullah Qutub Shah); NextGen AP History - APPSC 15 p.16 (same); app notes 1:126 (sub 4, 5)
## SUB 3 | Rediscovery of Hampi
[3:0] - **Colin Mackenzie** – surveyed and mapped the ruins of **Hampi in 1800**; later the **first Surveyor General of India** (NDA 2019; APPSC 2019) – not James Prinsep (Brahmi), James Rennell, Charles Metcalfe, Charles Harvey or Lord Hastings.
[3:1] - **Caesar Frederick** (Venetian) saw the ruined city in **1567**; **Robert Sewell** – *A Forgotten Empire* (1900).
[3:2] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.66 (Colin Mackenzie); NextGen AP History - APPSC 02 p.10 (Mackenzie, first Surveyor General); app notes 1:126 (sub 5)
