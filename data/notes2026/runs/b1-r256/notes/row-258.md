=== Krishna Patrika and Mutnuri Krishna Rao
# Krishna Patrika (1902)
- **Founded:** **February 1902** (1 Feb per NextGen; 2 Feb per Wikipedia), at **Machilipatnam (Bandar)**, as a **weekly** – in the **20th century**, not the 18th.
- **Founders:** **Konda Venkatappayya** and **Dasu Narayana Rao** – not Veeresalingam, Duggirala, Kasinathuni or Mutnuri.
- **First editor:** **Konda Venkatappayya** (edited till 1905; NextGen lists Dasu Narayana as first editor).
- **Mutnuri Krishna Rao:** sub-editor from 1902, **editor from 1907 to his death in 1945** – the **second and most famous editor**, "**largely responsible for its success**" (FRO; APPSC GS 2024) – not Konda, Dasu Narayana Rao or Piratla Venkateswarlu.
  - Made Krishna Patrika a **political weekly** and a **hub for freedom fighters**; called the **doyen of Telugu journalism**.
  - Wrote scholarly editorials attacking British government spending; also ran the magazine **Andhra Bharati** for some time.
- **Statements:** "Krishna Patrika was founded by Veeresalingam" – wrong; "edited by Mutnuri Krishna Rao" – right.
- **Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya:** set up the **Andhra Vidya Peetha Goshti** (Chirala, 1920) – true in the statement questions.
> Exam angle: founder Konda Venkatappayya (×7) vs editor Mutnuri (×7) – the core pair of this row.

=== Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao – Andhra Patrika and Bharati
- **Life:** **1867–1947**; founded the **Amrutanjan** business and used the wealth for public work.
- **Papers:** **Andhra Patrika** (9 Sept **1908**, Bombay; Telugu daily from 1914, Madras) – founder and first editor; **Bharati** (**1924**) – a **literary and cultural monthly** – not 1920-22.
- **Publishing house:** **Andhra Granthamala**, named to popularise the word "Andhra".
- **Library role:** any group of **ten youths** opening a village library got his paper, books and *Bharati* **free**; finished the **catalogue of printed Telugu books** begun by **Velidanda Hanumantha Rao**.
- **Granthalayodyama Trimurthulu:** Kasinathuni + Ayyanki + Gadicherla.
- **Sribagh:** his house in Madras; the **Sribagh Pact** was signed there on **16 November 1937**.

- **Pairs (APPSC):** Krishna Patrika–Konda Venkatappayya, Andhra Patrika–Kasinathuni, Swarajya–Gadicherla – all three correct.
- **The Crescent:** English weekly of **Gazulu Lakshminarasu Chetty** – not Mutnuri.
- **Kotamraju Punnaiah:** Telugu journalist, editor of **The Sindh Observer, Karachi** from 1920 (about 25 years), hailed as "architect of modern Sindh"; a **Karachi street** bears his name – not C.Y. Chintamani or M. Chalapathi Rau.
- **Konda Venkatappayya ("Desabhakta"):** booklet **Andhrodyamamu** (1912) on the neglect of the Telugu areas; also Srusti Vicharam, an autobiography.
- **Andhra Jateeya Kalasala (Andhra National College), Machilipatnam:** founded **20 Feb 1910** by **Kopalle Hanumantha Rao** (founder-principal), inaugurated by Konda Venkatappayya (president); later run with **Mutnuri Krishna Rao** and Pattabhi Sitaramayya – not Mutnuri, Gadicherla or Madapati as founder.
- **Editors as institution-builders:** Kasinathuni – Amrutanjan, Andhra Granthamala; Gadicherla – Edward Corporation Library, Nandyal; Chilukuri Veerabhadra Rao – Bala Bharati Samithi; Khasa Subba Rao – Swatantra, Swarajya (English).

=== Mutnuri and Bipin Chandra Pal's Tour (1907)
- **Organiser:** **Mutnuri Krishna Rao** organised and invited Bipin Chandra Pal's tour of Andhra (**April 1907**) – not T. Prakasam, Konda Venkatappayya or Gadicherla.
- **Lectures:** Kakinada (17 Apr 1907); **five lectures at Rajahmundry** (19-24 Apr 1907); Machilipatnam.
- **Bala Bharati Samithi** (Feb 1907, Rajahmundry; Chilukuri Veerabhadra Rao, Gante Laxmanna, Unguturi Sriramulu) took part in organising the Rajahmundry leg.
- **Rajahmundry College incident (1907):** Gadicherla expelled / blacklisted from government jobs.
> Exam angle: Pal's tour = Mutnuri (×3).
