## SUB 1 | Semiconductors and Indigenous Chips
[1:0] # Basics
[1:1] - **Silicon** = the **semiconductor** used for computer (IC) chips (exam key for "newest substance in chips"; strictly **gallium arsenide** is the newer compound semiconductor).
[1:2] - **Not a semiconductor:** **Krypton** (noble gas); silicon, germanium, selenium are.
[1:3] - **p-type:** trivalent doping → **holes** = majority carriers; n-type → electrons.
[1:4] - **Diode:** current one way only → **AC to DC** (rectification); a **semiconductor diode is non-ohmic** (copper coil, heater coil, rheostat are ohmic).
[1:5] - **Silicon carbide** (carborundum): very hard → abrasive for **cutting very hard substances**; also a power semiconductor.
[1:6] - **Piezoelectric MEMS** (May 2023): **IIT Madras + DRDO** (NPOL) – PZT thin-film **underwater** acoustic sensor for Navy SONAR.
[1:7] # Indigenous processors → developer

## SUB 2 | Extended Reality, Metaverse and Haptics
[2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [2:0.0] Virtual Reality (VR) | Fully computer-generated, immersive world that shuts out the real one; the user is still a real person
  [2:0.1] Augmented Reality (AR) | Digital content overlaid on the real world via a phone/PC camera (Pokemon Go); does not block the physical world
  [2:0.2] Mixed Reality (MR) | Real and virtual objects interact in real time
  [2:0.3] Extended Reality (XR) | Umbrella for VR + AR + MR
  [2:0.4] Metaverse | Interoperable network of 3D virtual worlds, accessed by millions at once, with property rights over virtual items (UPSC 2024)
  [2:0.5] Haptic technology | Tactile feedback – recreates touch by forces, vibrations or motions
[2:1] - **UPSC 2019:** "AR shuts out the physical world" ✗ (that is VR); "VR projects images onto real objects" ✗ (that is AR) → **3 and 4 only**.
[2:2] - Thomas Caudell (Boeing) coined "augmented reality" (1990); **VARCoE** at IIT Bhubaneswar; **IMAGE CoE** at Hyderabad.

## SUB 3 | 3D Printing
[3:0] - **Additive manufacturing:** objects built **layer by layer** from a **CAD** model; opposite of subtractive machining; **4D printing** adds materials that **change shape over time**.
[3:1] - **Applications (UPSC 2018 key: all five):** **confectionery**, **bionic ears**, **automotive industry**, **reconstructive surgery**, data-processing technologies; also aerospace, construction, implants.
[3:2] - **Surgery:** 3D printing gives the surgeon a **physical model of the patient's anatomy** (surgical planning).
[3:3] - **India's first 3D-printed post office:** **Bengaluru** (Cambridge Layout, near Ulsoor), **2023**; 1,021 sq ft, built in **43 days**.
[3:4] > ⚠ Disputed: PMF IAS (book p.772) prints (a) 1, 3 and 4 only for the 2018 question; the UPSC key and most books give (d) all five.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: PMF S&T Part 7 book p.770-772; app notes 5:15 sub 8
## SUB 4 | Internet of Things, RFID and Wearables
[4:0] # IoT
[4:1] - **IoT:** sensor-equipped devices sharing data over a network **without human intervention**; term by **Kevin Ashton** (1999).
[4:2] - **Agriculture (APPSC FRO 2022 A-R):** traditional farming is labour-intensive (A true); IoT sensors offer an alternative (R true) – **R does not explain A**.
[4:3] TABLE head: Layer | Job
  [4:3.0] Perception | Senses (sensors, RFID)
  [4:3.1] Network | Transmits
  [4:3.2] Application | Smart services
[4:4] - Four-stage version adds a **transport** layer.
[4:5] TABLE head: Variant | Concerns
  [4:5.0] IIoT | Industrial automation
  [4:5.1] IoNT | Nano-devices
  [4:5.2] Internet of Mobile Things | Mobile objects (not assembly lines)
  [4:5.3] Mission-critical IoT | Military applications
[4:6] - **IoT vs AI:** smart thermostat, fitness wearable = **IoT**; chatbot, image recognition = **AI** (analyses and decides).
[4:7] - Uses: **smart farming, homes, grids**, cities, healthcare; **NDCP 2018** covers 5G, IoT, M2M; IoT CoEs (MeitY-NASSCOM): Bengaluru, Gurugram, Gandhinagar, Visakhapatnam.
[4:8] # RFID
[4:9] TABLE head: Tag | Power | Reply
  [4:9.0] Passive | No battery – powered by the reader's field | Backscatters the reader's signal
  [4:9.1] Semi-passive | Battery runs the chip only | Uses the reader's radio waves to reply
  [4:9.2] Active | Own battery and transmitter | Broadcasts itself, long range
[4:10] - RFID needs **no line of sight** within range → APPSC G1: **II and IV only** correct.
[4:11] # Wearables
[4:12] - Smartwatches, fitness bands: **location**, **sleep monitoring**, heart rate; **hearing aids** for the hearing impaired → UPSC 2019: **I, II and III**.
