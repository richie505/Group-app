## SUB 1 | Mobile Telephony – Firsts, Standards and Terms
[1:0] # Firsts
[1:1] - **First handheld mobile call:** **Martin Cooper** of **Motorola**, **3 April 1973** (to Joel Engel of Bell Labs); so Cooper is credited with inventing the **mobile phone**.
[1:2] - **First mobile service in India:** exam key **Hutchison** (Max Touch, Mumbai); India's **first mobile call** was on **Modi Telstra**, Kolkata, **31 July 1995** (Jyoti Basu to Sukh Ram).
[1:3] - **Motorola** (March 2003) showed a phone displaying the user's location; **Tata Teleservices PTT** (push-to-talk, May 2004) – then only in USA, Japan, Malaysia (**not Australia**).
[1:4] - **QR code:** **Quick Response**, read quickly by a phone camera; designed **1994** (not 1996) by **Denso Wave**, Japan, for the **car industry**.
[1:5] - **Mobile Number Portability:** pilot **Haryana**, 25 Nov 2010; pan-India **20 Jan 2011**; full nationwide porting 3 Jul 2015.
[1:6] - India = world's **second-largest** telecom market (May 2025).
[1:7] # Standards and full forms
[1:8] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:8.0] GSM | Global System for Mobile Communications – 2G digital standard (India 1995)
  [1:8.1] CDMA | Code Division Multiple Access
  [1:8.2] GPRS | General Packet Radio Service – 2G packet data: Internet on the phone without modem or computer ('GRPS' in the 2003 paper = GPRS)
  [1:8.3] LTE | Long-Term Evolution – raises capacity and speed; 4G (uses OFDM and MIMO); WiMAX lost out
  [1:8.4] SIM | Subscriber Identity Module
  [1:8.5] OTG | On-The-Go (USB OTG: phone acts as USB host)
[1:9] - **LTE and IMT-Advanced:** original LTE did **not** meet the ITU **IMT-Advanced** 4G requirements; only **LTE-Advanced** did (UPSC 2010: only statement I correct).
[1:10] - Generations: 1G analog → 2G GSM/CDMA → 3G WCDMA → 4G LTE → 5G NR; **6G expected early 2030s**.
[1:11] - **Android 16** released for Pixel phones in June 2025; **Pixel Buds** (2017): Google Assistant with real-time translation of **40 languages**.
[1:12] # NFC (Near Field Communication)
[1:13] - **Contactless** communication using **electromagnetic radio fields** (**not light waves**); range **a few centimetres** (~4 cm, not a metre); can **encrypt**; built into **smartphones**.
[1:14] - Speed **106–424 kbps**; easier pairing and **lower power than Bluetooth**; RBI allows **offline UPI Lite** payments over NFC; RuPay tap-and-pay.
[1:15] > UPSC 2015: "a metre apart" ✗ → answer **a and c only**; APPSC: "uses light waves" = the untrue statement.

## SUB 2 | E-mail and Video Conferencing
[2:0] # E-mail
[2:1] - **E-mail** = **Electronic Mail**; address = **user name @ domain name** – in mark.sttol@ITdesk.info the **domain is ITdesk.info**.
[2:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [2:2.0] SMTP | Sends mail
  [2:2.1] POP3 / IMAP | Receive mail
  [2:2.2] CC | Sends copies (all see each other)
  [2:2.3] BCC | Blind carbon copy – hides recipients' addresses from one another
  [2:2.4] Video mail | Pre-recorded video by e-mail – can carry graphics, video clips, video messages (all)
  [2:2.5] Finger | Protocol/command to find a user's e-mail address and login details (Archie = FTP file search)
  [2:2.6] Listserv | Mailing list linking people with similar interests worldwide
  [2:2.7] Instant messaging | Real-time messages between two or more people
[2:3] - **Free e-mail providers:** **Hotmail, Rediffmail, Yahoo** (and Gmail) – all free.
[2:4] - **Rajasthan:** first state to give residents a **free e-mail address in Hindi** (2016).
[2:5] # Video conferencing
[2:6] - **Live two-way video and audio** between two or more places using telecom technology (with data).
[2:7] - **Essentials:** **video camera, display, microphone**, speaker, broadband, codec software; a **telephone is not essential** (nor an RFID reader).
[2:8] > Match (FSO): e-mail = instant delivery with attachments; listserv = similar interests; video conferencing = video + audio + data; instant messaging = real-time exchange.
