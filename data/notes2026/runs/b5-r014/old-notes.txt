## SUB 3 | E-mail and Video Conferencing
[14:3:0] # E-mail
[14:3:1] - **E-mail** = **Electronic Mail**: digital messages over a computer network.
[14:3:2] - Address parts: **username @ domain name** (e.g. name@gov.in).
[14:3:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [14:3:3.0] SMTP | Sends mail
  [14:3:3.1] POP3 / IMAP | Receive mail
  [14:3:3.2] Attachments | Files sent with a mail
  [14:3:3.3] CC | Sends copies
  [14:3:3.4] BCC (blind carbon copy) | Hides recipients' addresses from one another
  [14:3:3.5] Video mail | Sends a pre-recorded video by e-mail; can carry graphics and video clips
[14:3:4] # Video Conferencing
[14:3:5] - Live **two-way video and audio** between places using telecom/internet technology.
[14:3:6] - **Essentials**: **video camera, display, microphone**; also speaker, **broadband** link and conferencing **software (codec)**.
[14:3:7] A **telephone** is **not** essential for video conferencing; an **RFID reader** is not a component.
## SUB 4 | Digital Media and Multimedia

[14:4:1] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [14:4:1.0] Multimedia components | Text, images/graphics, audio, video, animation

  [14:4:1.2] Video editing software | Adobe Premiere Pro; FCP (Final Cut Pro) – Apple's professional non-linear video editing software

[14:4:4] - **'Knowledgeable sources':** not official, but with **access to official information**
[14:4:5] - **BARC** (Broadcast Audience Research Council, **2015**): measures **TV ratings**, replacing **TAM**
[14:4:6] - **The Wall Street Journal:** business daily from **New York, USA** (Dow Jones, News Corp)
[14:4:7] - **Heliodisplay:** **mid-air** display projecting images onto a thin layer of **mist/fog**; no physical screen.

=== NOTES 5:15 | Ict and Industry – Industry 4.0, Internet of Things, Robotics and Automation
## SUB 0 | Industrial Revolutions 1.0 to 5.0
[15:0:0] # Stages and Defining Technology
[15:0:1] TABLE head: Stage | Defining technology
  [15:0:1.0] Industry 1.0 | Mechanisation, steam power (steam-powered weaving looms)
  [15:0:1.1] Industry 2.0 | Electricity, mass production, assembly line
  [15:0:1.2] Industry 3.0 | Automation, computers and electronics
  [15:0:1.3] Industry 4.0 | Cyber-physical systems, IoT, networks, AI, big data; smart factories
  [15:0:1.4] Industry 5.0 | Human-machine collaboration; puts people at the centre of industrial innovation
[15:0:2] - The term **'Industry 4.0'** was coined in **Germany** (Hannover Fair, **2011**).
[15:0:3] - **'Fourth Industrial Revolution'** was coined/popularised by **Klaus Schwab**, founder of the **WEF**, in **2016**: merging of **digital, physical and biological** technologies.
[15:0:4] # Indian Initiatives for Industry 4.0
[15:0:5] - **SAMARTH Udyog Bharat 4.0** (**Smart Advanced Manufacturing and Rapid Transformation Hub**) – **Ministry of Heavy Industries**; spreads Industry 4.0 among manufacturers through **demonstration centres**.
[15:0:6] - **NM-ICPS** (National Mission on Interdisciplinary Cyber-Physical Systems): **robotics** is one vertical of its **25 Technology Innovation Hubs**.
[15:0:7] - **AP angle**: **WEF Fourth Industrial Revolution Centre** in AP would be **India's third**, after **Mumbai and Telangana**; AP has an **Industry Policy 4.0** (24 Apr 2026).
[15:0:8] - **Criticism**: automation and AI replace **intellectual as well as manual work** → job displacement.

[15:2:0] # Meaning

[15:2:2] - **Kevin Ashton** coined **"Internet of Things"** (**1999**).
[15:2:3] - Examples: an alarm switching on the geyser; a smart mirror showing weather; a **smart thermostat** (IoT, not AI).
