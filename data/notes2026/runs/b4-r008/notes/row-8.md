=== Types of Mountains – Fold, Block, Volcanic, Residual, Dome
# Five types (asked about 16 times)
| Type | How formed | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Young fold** | Tertiary (Alpine) compression of geosyncline sediments | Very high, rugged, sharp conical peaks; **world's highest peaks** | **Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies**, Atlas |
| **Old fold** | Caledonian/Hercynian or Precambrian folding + long denudation | Low, rounded, highly eroded | **Aravallis** (Precambrian), **Appalachians**, **Urals**, **Pennines** |
| **Block** | **Faulting** – uplifted block (**horst**) beside a sunken block (graben) | Steep fault scarps; linked with rift valleys | **Sierra Nevada**, **Vosges** (France), **Black Forest** (Germany), Salt Range |
| **Volcanic** | Accumulation of lava and ash around a vent | Conical, with a crater | **Kilimanjaro**, **Fujiyama**, **Etna** (Sicily), Mount St. Helens, Cotopaxi |
| **Residual** | Erosion leaves the resistant core of an old range | Remnants of older surfaces | **Aravallis**, Nilgiris, Vindhyas, Appalachians, Pennines |
| **Dome** | Magma pushes the crust up without erupting | Dome-shaped uplift | **Black Hills**, Bighorns (USA) |
- **Mountain:** most geologists take a landform rising at least **300 m (1,000 ft)** above its surroundings as a mountain (APPSC FSO).
- **World's highest peaks** (Everest) lie in **young fold** mountains; Everest and Mont Blanc are fold, **not volcanic**.
- **Young vs old:** **Alps** is the only young fold range among Appalachians, Aravallis, Alps, Urals (APPSC G2); the **Andes** are young, not old (UKPSC 2016); the **Appalachians** are not Tertiary (UPPSC 2014 – Kunlun, Alps, Andes are). Pennines, Appalachians, Aravallis = **old mountains** (State PSC).
- **Block:** **Sierra Nevada** is a block mountain, not young fold (MPPSC 2021); **Black Forest = Germany** (Feldberg 1,493 m), Vosges = France, across the Rhine graben.
- **Residual mountain of India = Aravallis** (UPPSC 2005; APPSC Asst Director). Match (UP UDA 2013): **Alps – folded, Vosges – block, Vindhya – residual, Fujiyama – volcanic**.
- **Volcanic mountain:** **Mount Etna** (APPSC G1); Kilimanjaro, Fuji.
- **Highest of Cotopaxi, Kilimanjaro, Pinatubo, Taal:** **Cotopaxi** (~5,897 m, Ecuador) just above Kilimanjaro (5,895 m) (UPPSC 2015). Highest volcano overall: Ojos del Salado (6,893 m).
- **Andes** (~7,000 km) = longest range; Himalayas = highest.
- **"Island of precipitation":** **dome** mountains like the Black Hills rise abruptly from dry plains and get about twice the plains' rainfall (APPSC G2 2011, printed as "Down mountains").
> Trap: Aravallis are both old fold and residual; when "old mountains" and "fold mountains" are both offered, the books' key is "old mountains".

=== Faults, Block Mountains and Rift Valleys
# Faults
| Fault | Force | Movement |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | Tension | Hanging wall down |
| Reverse | Compression | Hanging wall up |
| Thrust | Compression | Low-angle reverse |
| Strike-slip | Shear | Horizontal |
- **Horst** = uplifted fault block → **block mountain** (APPSC AEE 2023); **graben** = down-faulted block → **rift valley**.
- **Rift valley:** tension → faulting → central block subsides → elongated depression. Examples: **Rhine** (between Vosges and Black Forest), **Great Rift Valley** (East Africa), **Death Valley** (California – UPPSC 2012), **Narmada, Tapi**. Sierra Nevada = horst.

# Rift valleys of India (asked 6 times)
- **Narmada** flows **west** through a **rift valley (fault trough)** between the **Vindhyas (north)** and **Satpuras (south)** → valley of **structural** origin (CDS 2010); forms an **estuary, no delta** (A false, R true – UPPSC 2005).
- Why the Narmada flows west: **only because it occupies a rift valley** (UPSC 2015: "Only I"); lying between Vindhya and Satpura is just its location.
- **Damodar** = east-flowing river in a **rift valley due to down-warping** (IAS 1998). Tapi also flows in a rift.

=== The Aravalli – India's Oldest Fold (Residual) Range
- **Oldest mountain range of India** (asked 6 times: vs Himalaya, Shiwalik, Vindhya, Satpura, Nilgiri, Western Ghats); Precambrian, from Dharwar sediments; age about **570 million years** (IAS 2001).
- **Old fold mountain** (APPSC FSO flashcard: "fold mountain") and a **residual** mountain – "continuously denuded, typical residual hills" 400–600 m.
- Runs ~800 km from **Delhi** through **Haryana** and **Rajasthan** to northern **Gujarat** – mainly **Rajasthan** (UPRO 2014).
| Peak | Height | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Guru Shikhar** | **1,722 m** | Highest; Mount Abu, **Sirohi district, Rajasthan** (UPSC 2007, asked twice) |
| **Kumbhalgarh** | ~1,224 m | Highest when Guru Shikhar is not offered (RAS 2012) |
| Sajjangarh, Lilagarh, Taragarh | ~938, 874, 873 m | Lower |
- Guru Shikhar is also the **highest peak of the Central Highlands** of the Peninsular Plateau (APPSC Degree Lecturers 2020); Parasnath and Dalma are Chotanagpur, Kamet is Himalaya.
- **Supreme Court (Nov 2025):** an Aravalli **hill** = landform rising at least **100 m** above local relief (not 500 m); an Aravalli **range** = hills lying within **500 m** of each other; new mining leases paused. MoEF&CC banned new leases on **24 Dec 2025**; the SC later stayed its own directions (Dec 2025).

=== Young Fold Mountains – Evidence from the Himalaya
- **Youngest/most recent range:** the **Himalayas** (CGPSC 2018); within them the **Shiwaliks** are the youngest chain (UPPSC 2010).
- **Evidence of youth** (UPSC 2012, asked twice → **all four**): **deep gorges**, **U-turn river courses**, **parallel mountain ranges**, **steep gradients causing landslides**.
- Formed by the **northward movement of the Indian Plate** squeezing the **Tethys** sediments; main uplift Eocene–Pliocene.
- **Tertiary (Alpine) orogeny:** Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies, Atlas, Kunlun. **Caledonian:** Appalachians (old).

=== Diastrophism – Endogenic and Exogenic Forces
- **Endogenic** (internal heat → mantle dynamics → plate movement): **diastrophism and volcanism**; **earthquakes are endogenic** (APPSC Inspector of Factories). **Exogenic:** weathering, erosion, deposition.
- **Diastrophism** = all processes that move, raise or build up the crust: orogeny, epeirogeny, **earthquakes**, **plate tectonics**; **"heterogenic" is NOT** one (MPPSC 2023).
| Orogenic | Epeirogenic |
|---|---|
| **Mountain building**; severe folding in narrow belts | **Continent building**; uplift or warping of large areas |
| **Horizontal** (tangential) forces → **folding** (State PSC) | **Vertical** (radial) forces (UKPSC 2024) |
- **APPSC G1 – incorrect statement:** "Orogeny is the continental building process" – orogeny is **mountain** building; epeirogeny is continental building.
~~ Sources: NCERT Class 11 Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.5; NextGen Rocket General Geography p.31; app notes 4:8 sub 0

=== Folds
- **Anticline** = **upfold** (arch; oldest strata in the core) (NDA 2015); **syncline** = downfold (trough).
- Rising pressure: symmetrical → asymmetrical → overturned → **recumbent** (axial plane horizontal; **both limbs horizontal and parallel** – NDA 2019) → **nappe** (recumbent fold broken and thrust far forward).
- **Isoclinal** fold = limbs parallel to each other (and to the axial plane).
- Shiwaliks: simple anticline–syncline folds (Jura type). Cuddapah sediments lie in **synclinal troughs**.
~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 02 p.47-49 (pp.134-136); app notes 4:8 sub 3

=== Isostasy and Geosynclines
- **Isostasy:** crustal blocks float in balance on denser material below.
| Airy | Pratt |
|---|---|
| Uniform density, varying thickness | Varying density, same depth of compensation |
| Mountains have deep roots | Higher blocks are lighter |
- **Kober's geosynclinal theory:** long shallow sediment-filled troughs (geosynclines) between rigid forelands (**kratogens**) are squeezed into mountains; Tethys → Himalaya. Stages: **lithogenesis** (sedimentation) → **orogenesis** → **gliptogenesis**.
