=== NOTES 4:8 | Folding, Faulting, Mountain Building; Isostasy
## SUB 0 | Diastrophism
[8:0:0] - Endogenic processes → **mantle dynamics** → plate movement → folding, faulting, earthquakes (A-R: both true, R not the explanation).
[8:0:1] - **Diastrophism** = all processes that move, raise or build up the crust.
[8:0:2] TABLE head: Orogenic | Epeirogenic
  [8:0:2.0] Mountain building, severe folding in narrow belts | Continent building: uplift or warping of large areas
  [8:0:2.1] Horizontal (tangential) forces | Vertical (radial) forces (UKPSC 2024)
  [8:0:2.2] Folding comes from orogenic force | e.g. Peninsular Block (transgression, regression)
[8:0:3] - Includes orogeny, epeirogeny, earthquakes, plate movements; **"heterogenic" is NOT** included (MPPSC 2023; that key also put plate tectonics outside it). Endogenic forces: thermal asymmetry inside Earth.
[8:0:4] Orogeny = mountain, not continent, building. Weathering, erosion = exogenic.
## SUB 1 | Mountain Types
[8:1:0] TABLE head: Type | Formed by | Examples
  [8:1:0.0] Young fold | Tertiary/Alpine compression; rugged | Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies, Atlas
  [8:1:0.1] Old fold | Long denudation; low, rounded | Aravallis (Precambrian), Appalachians (Caledonian), Urals, Pennines
  [8:1:0.2] Block | Faulting (horst) | Sierra Nevada, Vosges (France), Black Forest (Germany; Feldberg 1,493 m), Salt Range
  [8:1:0.3] Volcanic | Lava accumulation; cone with crater | Kilimanjaro, Fujiyama, Etna (Sicily), Mount St. Helens (Washington, active), Cotopaxi
  [8:1:0.4] Dome | Magma bulges crust, no eruption | Black Hills, Bighorns
  [8:1:0.5] Residual | Erosion leaves resistant rock | Aravalli, Eastern Ghats (AP), Nilgiris
[8:1:1] - **World's highest peaks (Everest)** lie in young fold mountains. Everest and Mont Blanc are fold, not volcanic.
[8:1:2] - Active: Cotopaxi (Ecuador), Etna, Fujiyama; **Aconcagua not active**. Highest of Cotopaxi, Kilimanjaro, Pinatubo, Taal: **Cotopaxi**.
[8:1:3] - **Andes** (~7,000 km) = longest range (young fold, not old); Himalayas = highest.
[8:1:4] - Himalayan youth: deep gorges, U-turn rivers, parallel ranges, landslide slopes; uplift began ~70 million years ago (Eocene–Pliocene). **Shiwaliks** = youngest chain.
[8:1:5] APPSC G2 2024: of Appalachians, Aravallis, Alps, Urals – only the **Alps** are young fold. Sierra Nevada = block (MPPSC 2021).
## SUB 2 | The Aravalli
[8:2:0] - Rajasthan, Haryana, Delhi; India's **oldest** range, ~570 million years; residual.
[8:2:1] - Highest: **Guru Shikhar, 1,722 m**, Mount Abu, **Sirohi**; without it, Kumbhalgarh (1,224 m).
[8:2:2] - **SC (Nov 2025)**: hill = **100 m** above local relief (not 500 m); range = hills within 500 m; new mining leases paused. MoEF&CC **ban on new leases, 24 Dec 2025**. SC stayed its directions (Dec 2025).
## SUB 3 | Folds
[8:3:0] - **Anticline** = up-arch; **syncline** = down-trough. Rising pressure: symmetrical → asymmetrical → overturned (overfold) → **recumbent** → **nappe** (broken, thrust far forward); isoclinal = parallel limbs.
[8:3:1] - Siwaliks: Jura-type anticline–syncline folds, not block. Cuddapah sediments lie in **synclinal troughs**.
## SUB 4 | Faults, Horsts and Rift Valleys
[8:4:0] TABLE head: Fault | Force | Movement
  [8:4:0.0] Normal | Tension | Hanging wall down
  [8:4:0.1] Reverse | Compression | Hanging wall up
  [8:4:0.2] Thrust | Compression | Low-angle reverse
  [8:4:0.3] Strike-slip | Shear | Horizontal
[8:4:1] - **Horst** = uplifted block (block mountain, APPSC 2022); **graben** = down-faulted block (rift valley).
[8:4:2] - Grabens: **Rhine** (between Vosges and Black Forest), **Great Rift Valley** (Lake Malawi), **Death Valley** (California). Sierra Nevada = horst.
[8:4:3] - **Narmada** flows west in a rift between Vindhyas (north) and Satpuras (south); forms **no delta** (A false, R true). Tapi also rift; **Damodar** = east-flowing rift river from down-warping.
[8:4:4] - UPSC 2024: right pairs only Appalachians–fold, Andes–fold; Vosges not fold, Alps not block.
## SUB 5 | Isostasy and Geosynclines
[8:5:0] - **Isostasy**: crustal blocks float in balance on denser material.
[8:5:1] TABLE head: Airy | Pratt
  [8:5:1.0] Uniform density, varying thickness | Varying density, same compensation depth
  [8:5:1.1] Mountains have deep roots | Higher blocks are lighter
[8:5:2] - **Kober**: geosynclinal theory – long shallow troughs between rigid **kratogens** squeezed into mountains; Tethys → Himalaya.
[8:5:3] - Stages: **lithogenesis** → **orogenesis** → **gliptogenesis**.

=== NOTES 4:9 | Volcanoes – Types, Materials, Landforms, Global Distribution
## SUB 0 | Basic Terms
[9:0:0] - **Magma**: molten and semi-molten rock **below** the surface (about **600°C–1200°C**), held in a **magma chamber**; magma causes volcanism.
[9:0:1] - **Lava**: magma once it **reaches the surface** through the **vent**; dominated by silica.
[9:0:2] - **Volcano**: outlet that gives **lava, ash and gases**.
[9:0:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [9:0:3.0] Crater | Funnel-shaped mouth at the top of a cone
  [9:0:3.1] Caldera | Pot (cauldron)-shaped hollow: magma chamber empties after a big eruption → summit collapses
[9:0:4] Below surface = magma; at surface = lava.

  [9:0:6.0] Tiltmeter | Ground tilt over faults and volcanoes

[9:1:1] - Commonest gas: **water vapour**, then **CO₂**, then **SO₂**.
[9:1:2] - SO₂ → aerosols scatter sunlight, **cool Earth for years**; ash falls back within weeks.

  [9:1:4.0] Silica-rich, viscous | Silica-poor, fluid, flows far
