=== NOTES 3:90 | Primary and Secondary Markets – Stock Exchanges, Indices, Ipo Process, Derivatives
## SUB 0 | Primary vs Secondary Market
[90:0:0] TABLE head: Basis | Primary (new issue) market | Secondary market
  [90:0:0.0] Deals in | New securities | Existing securities
  [90:0:0.1] Transactions | Company → investors | Between investors
  [90:0:0.2] Examples | IPOs, FPOs, rights issues, private placement | Stock-exchange trading; gilt-edged trading of existing securities
  [90:0:0.3] Money goes to | The issuer | The selling investor
[90:0:1] - **Offer for sale (OFS)**: proceeds go to **selling shareholders**; only a **fresh issue** brings money to the firm.
[90:0:2] - A **stock exchange** is an organised market for securities, part of the capital market.
## SUB 1 | Ipo Process
[90:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [90:1:0.0] Book building | Bids within a price band (floor to cap); final price set from bids
  [90:1:0.1] Anchor investors | QIBs bidding before the issue opens, with lock-in
  [90:1:0.2] SME IPOs | Separate platforms: BSE SME, NSE Emerge
  [90:1:0.3] Listing | Within T+3 days of issue closing
  [90:1:0.4] Large-company float | Very large firms may offer only 2.75% (earlier 5%) (SEBI, Sep 2025)
[90:1:1] - **Stag**: buys IPO shares to sell soon after listing.
[90:1:2] - **ASBA = Application Supported by Blocked Amount**: introduced by **SEBI in 2008**; the IPO application money stays **blocked in the investor's bank account** and is debited only on allotment.
[90:1:3] - **Greenshoe option:** an over-allotment option that lets the issuer sell **extra shares** (usually up to 15% of the issue) when demand is high.
## SUB 2 | Stock Exchanges
[90:2:0] TABLE head: Exchange | Facts
  [90:2:0.0] BSE | Brokers moved to Dalal Street 1874; set up 1875; Asia's oldest; first recognised under SCRA on 31 Aug 1957; promoted by the Native Share and Stock Brokers' Association; promoted CDSL
  [90:2:0.1] NSE | Set up November 1992, Mumbai, on the Manohar J. Pherwani Committee; recognised April 1993; trading from 1994; first segment Wholesale Debt Market, then Cash Market; first fully automated electronic exchange; promoters IDBI, SBI, IFCI, LIC, GIC, IDFC
  [90:2:0.2] MSE | Mumbai; began 2008 as MCX-SX, renamed 2015
  [90:2:0.3] India INX (BSE) | GIFT City IFSC, 30 Dec 2016 – India's first international exchange
  [90:2:0.4] NSE IX | GIFT City; NSEIX Global Access lets Indians buy US stocks
[90:2:1] - **NSE** had the **highest turnover in 2000-01** (₹8.39 lakh crore) and is still the largest by volume.
[90:2:2] - **IFSC exchanges** (India INX, NSE IX) are regulated by **IFSCA, not SEBI**.
[90:2:3] BSE = Asia's oldest (1875); NSE = first fully automated (1992); India INX = first international exchange (2016).

## SUB 4 | Market Participants and Terms
[90:4:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [90:4:0.0] Bull | Expects prices to rise; bull market = rising
  [90:4:0.1] Bear | Expects prices to fall; sells to buy back lower
  [90:4:0.2] Stag | Buys IPO shares to sell on listing
  [90:4:0.3] Lame Duck | A share-market term (with Bull, Bear, Stag)
  [90:4:0.4] Blue chip | Huge, well-known firms with steady earnings and dividends
  [90:4:0.5] Short selling | Sell borrowed shares, buy back lower
  [90:4:0.6] Beta | Systematic risk of a security vs the market (beta 1 = moves with market)
  [90:4:0.7] Circuit breaker | Trading halt when an index or stock moves past limits
  [90:4:0.8] Sweat equity | Shares to employees/directors for know-how or services, often at a discount
  [90:4:0.9] STT | Securities Transaction Tax on domestic exchange trades
[90:4:1] - A **broker is not a speculator**; speculators include Bulls and Bears.
[90:4:2] - **Depositories**: **NSDL** (**first**, Mumbai, 6 Nov 1996) and **CDSL** (promoted by BSE) hold shares in **demat** form.
[90:4:3] - **Settlement**: **T+1** (one trading day after the trade), optional **T+0**.
## SUB 5 | Investor Claims and Instruments
[90:5:0] TABLE head: Holder / instrument | Claim
  [90:5:0.0] Shareholders | Owners; dividends; residual claim
  [90:5:0.1] Debenture/bondholders | Creditors (borrowed capital, fixed interest); paid before shareholders
  [90:5:0.2] Mutual funds | Pooled money priced at NAV; arbitrage funds keep at least 65% in equity
[90:5:1] - **Equity crowdfunding** is not permitted: SEBI has called online platforms offering it unauthorised (2016).
## SUB 6 | Derivatives
[90:6:0] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [90:6:0.0] Futures | Obligation to buy/sell at a fixed future price
  [90:6:0.1] Options | Right, not obligation: call = right to buy, put = right to sell
  [90:6:0.2] Swaps | Exchange of cash flows (fixed vs floating interest)
  [90:6:0.3] Commodity derivatives | FMC merged into SEBI (2015); NCDEX RAINMUMBAI = India's first weather derivative; NSE electricity futures
[90:6:1] - F&O market designed by the **L.C. Gupta Committee (1998)** and **J.R. Varma Committee (2002)**.
[90:6:2] - India's options market is **among the world's largest by contracts**: Indian exchanges handled about **84% of equity options** traded worldwide (Q1 2024).
[90:6:3] - SEBI: **9 in 10 option traders lost money**.
[90:6:4] - **Budget 2026-27 STT**: futures **0.02% → 0.05%**; options **0.10% → 0.15%**; aim: **curb speculation** (Nifty fell 1.96%).
[90:6:5] - **Commodity Transaction Tax (CTT)**: Budget **2013-14**; **0.01%**.
## SUB 7 | Sebi – Functions
[90:7:0] - Regulates **stock exchanges**, brokers, depositories (CDSL), **mutual funds**, **credit rating agencies**; regulates primary and secondary markets; prevents capital-market scams.
[90:7:1] - **Government securities** are under the **RBI**, not SEBI.

## SUB 1 | Sub-prime Crisis Mechanics
[92:1:0] - 1. Loans to **weak borrowers** → **securitised** into mortgage-backed securities sold worldwide.
[92:1:1] - 2. **Rates rise** → **home-loan defaults**.
[92:1:2] - 3. **Failure of US banks** → crash in the Indian stock market.
[92:1:3] - **Teaser loans**: low fixed rate first, higher later → a form of sub-prime lending; **future default risk** for banks (UPSC 2011).
[92:1:4] - **Credit Default Swap**: insurance-like derivative on bonds; seller pays on default; **counterparty risk**.
[92:1:5] Sub-prime crisis was caused by a **real estate recession**, **not oil prices** (UPPSC 2010).
## SUB 2 | Shadow Banking and Nbfcs
[92:2:0] - **Shadow banking** = financial activity of **non-bank entities** (e.g. NBFCs) **outside regular bank regulation** (UPPSC 2014).
[92:2:1] - **Asset-liability mismatch**: **borrow short** (CPs, mutual fund money), **lend long** (infrastructure, housing) – exposed by the **IL&FS** default.
[92:2:2] - NBFCs: registered under the **Companies Act**; **cannot take demand deposits, issue cheques or join the payment system**; deposits **not DICGC-covered**.
[92:2:3] - **Scale-Based Regulation (Oct 2022)**: **Base, Middle, Upper, Top** layers.
