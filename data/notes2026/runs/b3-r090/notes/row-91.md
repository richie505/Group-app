=== SEBI – Establishment, Statutory Status and Board
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Full form | **Securities and Exchange Board of India** |
| Set up (non-statutory) | **12 April 1988**, by a government resolution |
| Statutory powers | **30 January 1992** (ordinance) under the **SEBI Act, 1992** (Act passed **April 1992**); SEBI dates its statutory establishment to **12 April 1992** |
| Recommended by | **Narasimham Committee (1991)** – statutory status for SEBI, abolition of the Controller of Capital Issues |
| Took over from | **Controller of Capital Issues** |
| Headquarters | **Mumbai** (regional offices New Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Ahmedabad) |
| Ministry | **Ministry of Finance** |
| Chairperson | **Tuhin Kanta Pandey** (from 1 March 2025; in office 2026); earlier U.K. Sinha (2011-17) → **Ajay Tyagi (1 Mar 2017 – 28 Feb 2022)** → Madhabi Puri Buch (Mar 2022 – Feb 2025, first woman) |
| Role | **Main regulator of the capital and securities (stock) market** |
- **"SEBI was established in …"**: most books say **1988** (non-statutory); the statutory body, as SEBI itself puts it, was established in **1992** – read the options and the exam key (APPRB 2011 key: 1992).
- **Parent law:** the **SEBI Act, 1992**, not the SCRA, 1956.
- **SEBI Act Preamble:** **investor protection**, **development** and **regulation** of the securities market; bank lending rates are the RBI's job.
- **Why statutory powers:** to **prevent (recurrence of) scams** in the capital market (Harshad Mehta, 1992) – the Government gave regulatory powers to **SEBI**, not RBI, SBI or ICICI.
- **Board (SEBI Act, Sec. 4):** **Chairman** + **2** members from the **Finance and Law** ministries + **1** from the **RBI** + **5** other members, of whom **at least 3 are whole-time**.
- **Functions:** registering intermediaries, levying fees, **investor education**, inspection and audit of exchanges and intermediaries.
| Chronology | Year |
|---|---|
| SEBI non-statutory | 1988 |
| SEBI statutory (SEBI Act) | 1992 |
| NSE set up / recognised | Nov 1992 / April 1993 |
| SAT set up | 1995 |
| FMC merged into SEBI | 2015 |
- ⚠ The Chakravarty Committee (monetary system), Chelliah Committee (tax reform) and Tendulkar Committee (poverty) had nothing to do with SEBI.

=== Commodity Regulation – Forward Markets Commission
- **Forward Markets Commission (FMC):** statutory regulator of **commodity futures** trading, set up **1953** under the **Forward Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1952**; HQ Mumbai; moved from the Consumer Affairs Ministry to the **Ministry of Finance (2014)**.
- In 2010 (UPSC) the FMC regulated **commodities futures trading** – not currency, equity or financial futures (those were SEBI's).
- **Merged with SEBI on 28 September 2015**; SEBI now regulates commodity derivatives (now the answer to "who regulates commodity futures" = **SEBI**).
- After the merger: brokers, NSE/BSE and FPIs allowed into commodity derivatives; **Category III AIFs** allowed.

=== SEBI Powers, SAT and Investor Protection
| Power | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Quasi-legislative | Framing regulations |
| Quasi-judicial | Orders, penalties, **disgorgement** |
| Investigative | Inspection, search and seizure |
- **Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT):** set up **1995** under the SEBI Act (**Section 15K**); headed by a **Presiding Officer**; hears appeals against **SEBI and stock exchanges**; further appeal **SAT → Supreme Court**.
- **NSE co-location and dark-fibre cases:** the Supreme Court disposed of SEBI's appeals after a **₹1,492 crore** settlement.
| Tool | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Insider trading** (PIT Regulations) | Trading on **non-public, material** (price-sensitive) information; 2025 amendment widened **UPSI** |
| PFUTP Regulations | Ban **fraudulent and unfair trade practices** |
| **SCORES** | SEBI's **online investor complaints redress** system |
| IEPF Authority | Under the **Companies Act 2013, Ministry of Corporate Affairs** – not SEBI |
| Mission Jagrook | Launched on SEBI's **38th Foundation Day** |

=== Recent SEBI Reforms – T+0, BRSR, Finfluencers, Securities Markets Code
- **T+0 (same-day) settlement:** **optional**, alongside **T+1**. Goals (APPSC G2 2025): **enhance liquidity, reduce settlement risk, minimise counterparty risk, market efficiency, less impact of volatility** – **not** higher operational costs → **I, II and III**.
- **BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report):** prescribed by **SEBI (not RBI)** for the **top 1,000 listed companies** (not all); disclosures are **largely non-financial** (ESG); **replaced the BRR**; **BRSR Core** needs **reasonable assurance**.
- **Finfluencers:** stock tips or return claims need **SEBI registration**; verified "tick mark" for broker apps with **Google**.
- **SME IPOs** surged **87%** (FY23-25); **AP:** AP MSME Development Corporation + NSE SME IPO awareness programme in **Vijayawada**.
- **Securities Markets Code Bill, 2025** (Lok Sabha, Dec 2025): merges the **SEBI Act 1992, SCRA 1956, Depositories Act 1996**; SEBI board up to **15**; **MIIs** get statutory status.

=== Debt Market and Infrastructure Financing
- **REIT and InvIT Regulations** (SEBI, **2014**); **municipal bonds** – SEBI frames the issue and listing regulations.
- **Large corporate framework (2018):** large listed companies must raise **25%** of fresh borrowing through **debt securities** (bonds).
- SEBI **lowered** (did not raise) the **bond ticket (face value) size** to draw retail investors.
- **RBI Retail Direct Scheme:** retail investors buy **G-Secs** directly from the **RBI** – an RBI scheme, not a SEBI-RBI one.
> APPSC G1 2024: only the 25%-debt framework statement is valid → **Only II** (statement I's "exempted" is wrong – SEBI eased the InvIT/municipal bond rules; III and IV false).
