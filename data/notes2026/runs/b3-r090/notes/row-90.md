=== Stock Exchanges – BSE, NSE, India INX and Depositories
| Exchange | Facts |
|---|---|
| **BSE** (Bombay Stock Exchange) | Brokers moved to **Dalal Street, Mumbai in 1874**; set up **1875** as the Native Share and Stock Brokers' Association; **Asia's oldest** exchange; first exchange recognised under the SCRA, on **31 Aug 1957**; index **SENSEX**; promoted the depository **CDSL** |
| **NSE** (National Stock Exchange) | Set up **November 1992**, **Mumbai**, on the **Manohar J. Pherwani Committee**; recognised by SEBI **April 1993**; trading from **1994**; first segment **Wholesale Debt Market**, then Cash Market; India's **first fully automated electronic** exchange; **largest by trading volume** |
| **India INX** | Set up by **BSE** at **GIFT City IFSC, Gandhinagar** on **30 Dec 2016** – **India's first international exchange** |
| NSE IX | Also GIFT City; NSE IX Global Access lets Indians buy US stocks |
| MSE (Metropolitan Stock Exchange) | Mumbai; began **2008 as MCX-SX**, renamed 2015 |
- **NSE promoters:** leading financial institutions at the Government's behest – **IDBI, SBI, IFCI, LIC, GIC, IDFC** etc. → "SBI, LIC and GIC, IDBI – **all of the above**".
- **Bulk of the business:** the **NSE** handles the bulk of Indian stock-market trading (Ramesh Singh: about 75%); it had the **highest turnover in 2000-01** (₹8.39 lakh crore) and is still the largest by volume. The RBI is not an exchange.
- **BSE statements** "oldest exchange of India", "Dalal Street", "index = SENSEX" are **all correct**; "BSE was set up in 1992 by financial institutions" is false (that is the NSE).
- **Regulator:** stock exchanges (and both primary and secondary markets) are regulated by **SEBI**; exchanges in the **IFSC (India INX, NSE IX)** are regulated by **IFSCA**, not SEBI.
- **Depositories** (Depositories Act, 1996) hold shares in **electronic (demat)** form: **NSDL** (National Securities Depository Ltd, **first**, Mumbai, 1996) and **CDSL** (promoted by **BSE**, not NSE).
- **NSE chair:** **Girish Chandra Chaturvedi** became chairman in **Dec 2019** (term ended July 2024); **now Srinivas Injeti**, chairperson of the NSE Governing Board from **9 Sep 2025** (as of 2026).
> Most-asked: NSE/BSE location = **Mumbai** (6 PYQs); first international exchange = **Gandhinagar (GIFT City)**; BSE year = **1875**.

=== Stock Indices – SENSEX, NIFTY, GREENEX and World Indices
| Index | Exchange | Stocks | Method | Launch / base |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SENSEX** | BSE | **30** blue chips | **Free-float market capitalisation** | Published **1 Jan 1986** (India's first equity index); base **1978-79 = 100** |
| **NIFTY 50** | NSE | **50** | Free-float market capitalisation | Launched 22 April **1996**; base **3 Nov 1995 = 1000** |
| DOLLEX-30 | BSE | 30 | Dollar-linked Sensex | 25 July 2001 |
| **GREENEX** | BSE | **25** (20 at launch) | Top green companies by greenhouse-gas emissions (carbon efficiency) | 22 Feb **2012** |
| **Nifty REITs & InvITs** | NSE Indices Ltd | Listed REITs and InvITs | India's first such index | Launched **11 April 2023**; base 1 Jul 2019 = 1000 |
- **SENSEX** = the free-float market-cap-weighted index of the **top 30** well-established, financially sound (blue-chip) companies on the BSE; each stock's weight is **proportional to its free-float market value**.
- **A rise in SENSEX** = an **overall rise in prices of the group of 30 companies**, **not all BSE shares** and not NSE shares.
- **UPSC 2005 statements:** Sensex on 50 stocks ✗ (30; 50 = Nifty); stocks given proportional (market-cap) weightage ✓; NYSE the oldest exchange ✗ (Amsterdam, 1602, is the oldest) → **B only**.
- **NIFTY** is managed by **NSE Indices Ltd** (formerly IISL), an NSE subsidiary.
- **Not share-price indices:** **Moody's** (credit-rating agency), CRISIL (rating agency), **SAPs** (Structural Adjustment Programmes – IMF/World Bank policies).
| World index | Market |
|---|---|
| **Dow Jones Industrial Average** | **New York** |
| S&P 500 | 500 large US companies (market-cap weighted) |
| NASDAQ | US technology-heavy exchange |
| **NIKKEI 225** | **Tokyo** Stock Exchange |
| **Hang Seng** | **Hong Kong** (not Seoul) |
| **FTSE 100** | **London** |

- **First Indian company at ₹10 lakh crore market value:** **Reliance Industries** (28 Nov 2019).

=== Market Players and Terms – Bull, Bear, Stag, Blue Chip, Beta
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bull** (Tejadia) | Expects prices to **rise**; bull market = rising |
| **Bear** (Mandadia) | Expects prices of a security to **fall**; sells now to buy back lower |
| **Stag** | Buys **IPO** shares to sell soon after listing |
| Lame duck | A share-market term (with Bull, Bear, Stag) |
| **Blue chip** | Shares of large, well-established, financially sound companies giving **consistent high returns** |
| **Beta** | Measures a stock's **fluctuations (systematic risk) relative to the overall market** (beta 1 = moves with the market) |
| Short selling | Selling borrowed shares to buy back lower |
| Circuit breaker | Trading halt when an index or stock moves past limits |
| **Sweat equity** | Shares issued to **employees or directors** for know-how or services, often at a discount |
| **Insider trading** | Trading on non-public, material information – a **share-market** offence |
- **Bull and Bear** are **share-market** terms, not foreign trade, banking or manufacturing.
- **Speculators:** Bull, Bear, Stag. A **broker** is a registered member who buys and sells on clients' behalf for a commission – **not a speculator** (nor a "bookie").
- **Settlement:** **T+1** (one trading day after the trade); optional **T+0**.
> NOT-angle: "not a speculator" / "not a bookie" → **Broker** (UP RO/ARO 2017, twice).

=== Primary vs Secondary Market and the IPO Process
| Basis | Primary (new issue) market | Secondary market |
|---|---|---|
| Deals in | **New** securities (issued for the first time) | **Existing** securities |
| Transactions | Issuing company → investors | Investor ↔ investor |
| Examples | IPOs, FPOs, rights issues, private placement | **Stock-exchange** trading; trading in the **gilt-edged** (G-Sec) market |
| Price | Fixed by the firm | Demand and supply |
| Money goes to | The issuer | The selling investor |
- Transactions in the **stock exchange and the gilt-edged market** = **secondary market**; SEBI regulates both markets.
- **Gilt-edged market** = market for government and semi-government securities ("of the best quality", no default risk), backed by the RBI.
- **Offer for sale (OFS):** proceeds go to **selling shareholders**; only a **fresh issue** brings money to the company.
| IPO term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Book building | Bids within a **price band**; final price set from the bids |
| Anchor investors | **QIBs** bidding **before** the issue opens, with a lock-in |
| SME IPOs | Separate platforms **BSE SME** and **NSE Emerge** (2012) |
| Listing | Within **T+3** days of issue closing |
| Large-company float | Very large firms may offer only **2.75%** (earlier 5%) (SEBI, Sep 2025) |
| **ASBA** | **Application Supported by Blocked Amount** (SEBI, **2008**): money stays blocked in the investor's account until allotment |
| Greenshoe option | Over-allotment of usually up to 15% extra shares |
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Exams 10 p.23, 11 p.2 (primary, secondary, gilt-edged); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.32; NextGen Rocket Sheets Indian Economy #10 p.13; Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy 05 p.132 (SME platforms), p.137, p.139 (greenshoe); app notes 3:90 sub 0, 1

=== Instruments – Shares, Debentures, Mutual Funds and ETFs
| Holder / instrument | Claim |
|---|---|
| **Shareholders** | **Owners**; dividends; residual claim on earnings and assets |
| **Debenture holders** | **Creditors** – debentures are long-term **debt** at a fixed rate of interest; paid before shareholders in liquidation |
| Mutual funds | Pooled money priced at **NAV** (computed at the end of each trading session); arbitrage funds keep at least 65% in equity |
| **ETF** | Fund **traded on exchanges like a share**, price changes **through the day**; NAV still computed daily; **high liquidity and low costs** |
- Debenture holders are **not** shareholders, debtors or directors of the company.
- **ETF NOT-statement (CDS 2018):** "ETFs have **lower** liquidity and **higher** fees than mutual funds" is **wrong** – ETFs are known for low costs.
- **Equity crowdfunding** is not permitted: SEBI called online platforms offering it unauthorised (2016).
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Exams 10 p.23, 11 p.1 (shares, debentures, mutual funds, NAV); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 01 p.25 (ETF); Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy 05 p.65 (ETFs), p.140 (debenture holders' primacy); app notes 3:90 sub 5

=== Derivatives and Transaction Taxes (STT, CTT)
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Futures** | **Obligation** to buy/sell at a fixed future price |
| **Options** | **Right, not obligation**: **call** = right to buy, **put** = right to sell |
| **Swaps** | Exchange of cash flows (fixed vs floating interest) |
| Commodity derivatives | Regulated by **SEBI** since the **FMC merger (2015)**; **NCDEX RAINMUMBAI** = India's first weather derivative |
- Derivatives derive their value from an **underlying** (equity, index, interest rate, commodity); they are securities under the **SCRA, 1956**.
- F&O market designed by the **L.C. Gupta (1998)** and **J.R. Varma (2002)** Committees.
- Indian exchanges handled about **84%** of equity options traded worldwide (Q1 2024); SEBI: **9 in 10** option traders lost money.
- **STT** (Securities Transaction Tax, 2004) on exchange trades; **Budget 2026-27**: futures **0.02% → 0.05%**, options **0.10% → 0.15%** – aim: **curb speculation**.
- **Commodity Transaction Tax (CTT):** introduced in the **Union Budget 2013-14**, **0.01%** on non-agricultural commodity futures.
