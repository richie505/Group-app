## SUB 0 | Article 21A – Right to Education, 86th Amendment and RTE Act
[0:0] # Article 21A
[0:1] - **Text:** the State shall provide **free and compulsory education** to all children of the age of **6 to 14 years** "in such manner as the State may, **by law**, determine". Only **elementary** education is an FR – not higher or professional education.
[0:2] - **Inserted by:** the **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002** (introduced as the **93rd Amendment Bill**, 2001) – not the 42nd, 44th, 74th, 91st or the RTE Act itself (an Act cannot add an Article). The **93rd Amendment Act, 2005** is a different law (OBC reservation in educational institutions, Art. 15(5)).
[0:3] - **In force:** Art. 21A and the **RTE Act** both came into force on **1 April 2010** – so the year the right to education "came into force as an FR" is **2010**, though the amendment is of **2002**.
[0:4] - **Nature:** a **Fundamental Right** (not statutory, natural or a DPSP right); an **extension of the right to life (Art. 21)**; essential to the right to development as a human right. Aim: **"Education for All"**.
[0:5] - **Earlier position:** free and compulsory education was a **Part IV (DPSP)** provision – the original **Art. 45** asked the State to provide it for all children **until they complete 14 years** (within ten years).
[0:6] TABLE head: 86th Amendment changed | New content
  [0:6.0] Art. 21A (FR) | Free, compulsory education 6–14 years
  [0:6.1] Art. 45 (DPSP) | Early childhood care and education for children below 6
  [0:6.2] Art. 51A(k) (Fundamental Duty) | Parent/guardian to provide opportunities for education to child 6–14 – the 11th duty
[0:7] - **Not** a change of the 86th Amendment: deleting the **Right to Property** – that was the **44th (1978)**.
[0:8] # Cases before 21A
[0:9] TABLE head: Case | Year | Ruling
  [0:9.0] Mohini Jain v. State of Karnataka | 1992 | Right to education flows from Art. 21
  [0:9.1] Unnikrishnan J.P. v. State of Andhra Pradesh | 1993 | Read education into Art. 21: free education up to 14 years is a right; beyond that it depends on the State's economic capacity
[0:10] - **Sequence:** Mohini Jain (1992) → Unnikrishnan (1993) → 86th Amendment (2002) → RTE Act (2009) → in force (2010).
[0:11] # RTE Act, 2009 (Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act)
[0:12] - **Passed by Parliament:** Rajya Sabha 20 July 2009, Lok Sabha **4 August 2009** (assent 26 Aug 2009); **in force 1 April 2010**.
[0:13] - **Sec. 3:** every child of **6–14** has a right to free and compulsory education in a **neighbourhood school** till elementary education is complete.
[0:14] - **25%** of entry-level seats (class I or pre-school) in **unaided private schools** for children of weaker and disadvantaged groups of the neighbourhood; schools reimbursed; **not minority schools**.
[0:15] - **Duties:** appropriate Government and local authority to establish schools in the neighbourhood within **3 years**; **every parent/guardian** to admit the child; **funding is a concurrent responsibility of Centre and States** – the Centre does **not** bear sole responsibility.
[0:16] - Amendments: 2012, 2017, **2019** (no-detention policy relaxed – regular exams in classes 5 and 8), 2022.

## SUB 1 | Article 20 – Protection in Respect of Conviction for Offences
[1:0] - **Available to:** every person – **citizens, foreigners and legal persons** (companies). It cannot be suspended in an emergency (see last sub-section).
[1:1] TABLE head: Clause | Protection | Scope / NOT covered
  [1:1.0] 20(1) | No ex post facto criminal law – no conviction except for violating a "law in force" when the act was done, and no penalty greater than that law allowed | Only criminal laws – civil or tax laws can be retrospective; bars conviction/heavier penalty, not the trial; not applicable to preventive detention
  [1:1.1] 20(2) | No double jeopardy – no person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once | Only before a court or judicial tribunal; not departmental or administrative proceedings
  [1:1.2] 20(3) | No self-incrimination – no person accused of an offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself | Covers testimonial compulsion (oral and documentary); not physical evidence – fingerprints, specimen signatures, blood, DNA (Kathi Kalu Oghad, 1961); voice sample for investigation is allowed (Ritesh Sinha, 2019)
[1:2] - **Ex post facto law** = a **retrospective** law (not prospective, not a Bill). Hence **Parliament cannot** make a **criminal law with retrospective effect** that convicts or enhances punishment.
[1:3] - **Double jeopardy:** maxim *nemo debet bis vexari*; Art. 20(2) rests on **autrefois convict** (previously convicted) – both prosecution **and** punishment are needed, so *autrefois acquit* is not covered. CrPC Sec. 300 → **Sec. 337 BNSS**.
[1:4] - **Nature:** 20(1) is **substantive** law of criminal liability and penalty; 20(2) and 20(3) are **procedural**. "Art. 20 does not protect against testimonial compulsion" is **false**.
[1:5] - **Selvi v. State of Karnataka (2010):** involuntary **narco-analysis, polygraph and brain-mapping** violate **Art. 20(3)** and **Art. 21** (personal liberty, privacy).
[1:6] - **Not in Art. 20:** **prohibition against torture** (that is **Art. 21**); preventive detention safeguards (Art. 22).
[1:7] > Asked as: "which Article – double jeopardy / self-incrimination" (about 10 PYQs), the clause (20(2), 20(3)), maxims (autrefois convict, ex post facto = retrospective), "NOT part of Art. 20" (torture), voice sample, Selvi.

## SUB 2 | Rights Read into Article 21 and the Right to Privacy
[2:0] # Article 21 at its widest
[2:1] - **Right to life** and **personal liberty** ("freedom"); available to **citizens and non-citizens**. Many unenumerated rights are read in by the Supreme Court – e.g. **food**, health, **shelter**, **livelihood**, dignity; **no right to permanent housing** is enumerated anywhere.

[2:3] - **Also under Art. 21:** rights of **transgender persons** (NALSA, 2014), of **craniopagus (conjoined) twins**, of a mentally retarded woman to bear a child (Suchita Srivastava, 2009) – all three (CDS 2017); protection against **torture** and **solitary confinement**.
[2:4] - **Marriage:** Art. 21 protects the choice of partner (**UPSC 2019: Art. 21**), but **Supriyo Chakraborty (2023)**: there is **no unqualified fundamental right to marry** as such. **Travel abroad** is an FR (Maneka). So APPSC G2 Mains 2025 key: **both statements right**.
[2:5] # Right to Privacy
[2:6] - **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India, 24 Aug 2017:** a **nine-judge** bench, unanimous (**9:0**) – privacy is a Fundamental Right, an intrinsic part of **Article 21 and the freedoms of Part III** (UPSC 2018, 2024: **Art. 21**).
[2:7] - **Overruled** M.P. Sharma (1954) and Kharak Singh (1962) to the extent they denied privacy as an FR.
[2:8] - Test for limiting privacy: **legality** (a law), **legitimate aim**, **proportionality**.

## SUB 3 | Article 21 – Procedure Established by Law, Due Process, Natural Justice
[3:0] - **Text:** "No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to **procedure established by law**" – phrase from the **Japanese** Constitution; "**due process of law**" is American. Art. 21 does not use the words "due process".
[3:1] TABLE head: A.K. Gopalan (1950) | Maneka Gandhi (1978)
  [3:1.0] Any procedure enacted by the legislature suffices | Procedure must be just, fair and reasonable
  [3:1.1] Arts. 19, 21, 22 separate | Arts. 14, 19, 21 linked – the golden triangle
  [3:1.2] Protects only against the executive | Also against arbitrary legislation – in effect American due process read into Art. 21
[3:2] - **Due process of law**, in essence = the **principle of natural justice** (UPSC 2023); the doctrine is now read into **Article 21**.
[3:3] - **Natural justice:** **audi alteram partem** = "hear the other side" – the **right to be heard**; *nemo judex in causa sua* = no one a judge in his own cause.
[3:4] - **Protection is against** the State's **executive and legislative** action (Laxmikanth). *(Since **Kaushal Kishor v. State of UP, 2023**, Arts. 19 and 21 can also be enforced against private persons.)*
[3:5] - **Unguided, uncontrolled executive discretion** breaks **Article 14** (arbitrary).
[3:6] - **Capital punishment** does not violate Art. 21 – **Bachan Singh (1980)**, "rarest of rare"; so it is "not within the purview" of Art. 21 rights.

## SUB 4 | Article 22 – Arrest and Preventive Detention
[4:0] # Punitive arrest – 22(1), 22(2)
[4:1] - Arrested person must be **told the grounds** of arrest; may **consult and be defended by a lawyer of choice**; must be **produced before the nearest magistrate within 24 hours** (**Art. 22(2)**, excluding journey time) – not 12, 48 hours or one week.
[4:2] - **22(3) exclusions:** **enemy aliens** and persons held under a **preventive detention** law – not ordinary foreigners or OCIs. So detention beyond 24 hours without a magistrate is allowed only in **preventive detention**.
[4:3] - **Also outside:** arrest on a **court order** (person in jail under judicial order), **civil arrest**, deportation of an alien.
[4:4] - **Not guaranteed:** **bail as of right**, trial by jury. CrPC Secs. 50, 57 → BNSS Secs. 47, 58. **Vihaan Kumar v. State of Haryana (2025):** grounds must be meaningfully communicated to every arrestee.
[4:5] # Preventive detention – 22(4) to 22(7)
[4:6] - **Preventive** = detention **without trial**, on suspicion of future harmful acts; **punitive** = after trial and conviction. Justified for **public order**, not routine **law and order**.
[4:7] - **22(4):** no detention beyond **3 months** unless an **Advisory Board** (**High Court judges** or persons qualified to be) reports sufficient cause. **44th Amendment (1978)** cut it to **2 months**, but that clause was **never notified** – 3 months still applies.
[4:8] - **22(5):** grounds communicated + **earliest opportunity of representation**. **22(7):** **Parliament** fixes the maximum period and Advisory Board procedure.
[4:9] TABLE head: Who legislates | Subjects
  [4:9.0] Parliament alone | Defence, foreign affairs, security of India
  [4:9.1] Parliament and States | Public order, security of a State, essential supplies

## SUB 6 | Articles 20 and 21 During an Emergency
[6:0] - **44th Amendment (1978):** the enforcement of **Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended** under Art. 359 even during a National Emergency – the only such FRs (Arts. 14, 19, 22 can be).
[6:1] - So "right to life and personal liberty" can never be suspended; freedom of movement, residence, profession (Art. 19) can.
