=== NOTES 2:11 | Articles 20, 21, 21A and 22 – Protection in Conviction, Life and Liberty, Education, Preventive Detention
## SUB 0 | The Four Articles at A Glance
[11:0:0] TABLE head: Article | Subject | Available to
  [11:0:0.0] 20 | Protection in respect of conviction for offences | Citizens, foreigners and legal persons (companies)
  [11:0:0.1] 21 | Protection of life and personal liberty | Citizens and non-citizens
  [11:0:0.2] 21A | Right to education (6-14 years) | Children
  [11:0:0.3] 22 | Protection against arrest and detention | Not enemy aliens, not preventive detainees (for 22(1)-(2))
## SUB 1 | Article 20 – Three Protections
[11:1:0] TABLE head: Clause | Protection | Scope / NOT covered
  [11:1:0.0] 20(1) | No ex post facto criminal law – no conviction except for a law in force at the time; no heavier penalty | Only criminal laws; not retrospective civil or tax laws; bars conviction/greater penalty, not the trial
  [11:1:0.1] 20(2) | No double jeopardy – not prosecuted and punished twice for the same offence | Only prosecution and punishment by a court/judicial tribunal; not departmental proceedings
  [11:1:0.2] 20(3) | No self-incrimination – accused not compelled to be a witness against himself | Criminal cases; oral and documentary testimony; not physical evidence like fingerprints/specimens (Kathi Kalu Oghad, 1961)
[11:1:1] - **Selvi v. State of Karnataka (2010)**: **narco-analysis, polygraph, brain-mapping without consent** violate 20(3).
[11:1:2] - Art. 20 does **not** cover **preventive detention**. **Prohibition against torture** is not in Art. 20; protection from torture is under **Article 21**.
[11:1:3] - Double jeopardy: CrPC Sec. 300 → **Sec. 337 BNSS**.
## SUB 2 | Article 21 – Procedure Established by Law vs Due Process
[11:2:0] TABLE head: A.K. Gopalan (1950) | Maneka Gandhi (1978)
  [11:2:0.0] "Procedure established by law" = any procedure enacted by legislature (from Japan) | Procedure must be just, fair and reasonable
  [11:2:0.1] Arts. 19, 21, 22 separate | Arts. 14, 19, 21 linked – the golden triangle
  [11:2:0.2] Protects only against the executive | Also against arbitrary legislation – in effect American due process
[11:2:1] - Article 21's text uses "procedure established by law"; there is no due-process clause, and Gopalan said courts could not test reasonableness.
[11:2:2] - **Due process of law** in essence = **natural justice**. "Procedure established by law" (Japan) is more definite than "due process" (USA).
[11:2:3] - **Unguided, uncontrolled executive discretion** breaks **Article 14** (arbitrary, weakens the Rule of Law).
[11:2:4] - ★ **Capital punishment** is not barred by Article 21.
[11:2:5] - **Audi alteram partem:** means 'hear the other side' – the right to be heard, a core rule of natural justice.

## SUB 4 | Right to Privacy
[11:4:0] - **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India**, **24 Aug 2017**: **nine-judge** bench, unanimous (**9:0**) – privacy is a Fundamental Right, mainly under **Article 21** (with Part III).
[11:4:1] - **Overruled**: **M.P. Sharma** and **Kharak Singh** (to the extent they denied privacy as a Fundamental Right).
[11:4:2] - Test for limiting privacy: **legality** (a law), **legitimate aim**, **proportionality**.
## SUB 5 | Right to Die and Euthanasia
[11:5:0] TABLE head: Case | Year | Ruling
  [11:5:0.0] Maruti Shripati Dubal (Bombay HC) | 1987 | Right to die part of Art. 21; Sec. 309 IPC unconstitutional
  [11:5:0.1] P. Rathinam | 1994 | Sec. 309 IPC (attempted suicide) unconstitutional
  [11:5:0.2] Gian Kaur v. Punjab | 1996 | Overruled Rathinam: no right to die, but right to die with dignity; Sec. 309 valid
  [11:5:0.3] Aruna Shanbaug | 2011 | Plea rejected; passive euthanasia allowed under High Court supervision
  [11:5:0.4] Common Cause | 2018 | Dying with dignity (passive euthanasia) an FR under Art. 21; living will / advance directive upheld
[11:5:1] - **Chenna Jagadeeswar v. State of AP (1988):** **AP High Court** upheld Sec. 309 IPC – Art. 21 gives **no right to die**.
[11:5:2] - **Mental Healthcare Act, 2017**: attempted suicide effectively not a crime.
[11:5:3] - **BNS, 2023** (from **1 July 2024**): **no general offence of attempted suicide**; **Sec. 226 BNS** punishes only an attempt to compel or restrain a public servant.
[11:5:4] - 16 Mar 2026: Harish Rana (PVS for 13 years) – parents sought withdrawal of life support in the SC.
## SUB 6 | Article 21A and the 86TH Amendment
[11:6:0] - **Art. 21A**: State shall provide **free and compulsory education** to all children of **6 to 14 years** "in such manner as the State may determine". Only **elementary** education is an FR.
[11:6:1] - Inserted by the **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002** (passed as the **93rd Amendment Bill**); in force with the RTE Act on **1 April 2010**.
[11:6:2] - The **RTE Act, 2009 only gives effect** to Art. 21A – an Act cannot add an Article. Added by the **86th, not 91st**; **property** was removed by the **44th (1978)**, not the 86th.
[11:6:3] - Nature: an **FR** (PYQ 2018), an **extension of the right to life** (PYQ 2019); education essential to the right to development.
[11:6:4] TABLE head: 86th Amendment changed | New content
  [11:6:4.0] Art. 21A (FR) | Free, compulsory education 6-14 years
  [11:6:4.1] Art. 45 (DPSP) | Early childhood care and education below 6
  [11:6:4.2] Art. 51A(k) (FD) | Parent/guardian to provide education to child 6-14 – the 11th duty
[11:6:5] - Free and compulsory education was earlier a **Part IV** (DPSP) provision.
[11:6:6] # RTE Act 2009 and Earlier Cases
[11:6:7] - **Sec. 3**: every child 6-14 has a right to free, compulsory education in a **neighbourhood school** till elementary education is complete.
[11:6:8] - **25%** entry-level seats in **unaided private schools** for weaker groups; **not minority schools**; schools reimbursed.
[11:6:9] - Amendments: 2012, 2017, 2019 (assessment and detention allowed at certain stages), 2022. Aim: **Education for All**.
[11:6:10] TABLE head: Case | Year | Ruling
  [11:6:10.0] Mohini Jain v. Karnataka | 1992 | Right to education flows from Art. 21
  [11:6:10.1] Unnikrishnan v. State of AP | 1993 | AP angle: free education up to 14 years; beyond that subject to State's economic capacity
[11:6:11] - Sequence: Mohini Jain (1992) → Unnikrishnan (1993) → 86th Amendment (2002) → RTE Act (2009).
## SUB 7 | Article 22 – Rights of Arrested Persons
[11:7:0] - **22(1)-(2)**: be **told the grounds** of arrest; **consult and be defended by a lawyer of choice**; produced before the **nearest magistrate within 24 hours** (excluding journey time).
[11:7:1] - **22(3) exclusions**: **enemy aliens** and persons held under **preventive detention** law.
[11:7:2] - Also outside: arrest under court orders, civil arrest, deportation of aliens.
[11:7:3] - **Not guaranteed**: **bail as of right**, **trial by jury**.
[11:7:4] - CrPC Secs. 50, 57 → **BNSS Secs. 47 and 58**.
[11:7:5] - **Vihaan Kumar v. State of Haryana (2025)**: grounds of arrest must be meaningfully communicated to every arrestee.
## SUB 8 | Preventive Detention
[11:8:0] - **Preventive** = without trial, on suspicion of future acts; **punitive** = after conviction. Justified for **public order**, not routine **law and order** (SC, Jun 2026).
[11:8:1] - **22(4)**: no detention beyond **3 months** unless an **Advisory Board** (HC judges or persons qualified to be) reports sufficient cause.
[11:8:2] - **22(5)**: grounds communicated + **earliest opportunity of representation**. **22(7)**: **Parliament** fixes the maximum period.
[11:8:3] - **44th Amendment (1978)** cut the period to **2 months**, but the clause was **never notified** → **3 months** still applies (16 Jun 2026: demands to notify it).
[11:8:4] TABLE head: Who legislates | Subjects
  [11:8:4.0] Parliament alone | Defence, foreign affairs, security of India
  [11:8:4.1] Parliament and States | Public order, security of a State, essential supplies

  [11:8:6.1] UAPA | 1967 | Anti-terror law; SC (6 Jan 2026): delay alone not a "trump card" for bail
  [11:8:6.2] MISA | 1971 | Used in the Emergency; repealed 1978
  [11:8:6.3] COFEPOSA | 1974 | Smugglers and foreign-exchange manipulators
  [11:8:6.4] NSA | 1980 | Detention up to 12 months without charges
  [11:8:6.5] J&K PSA, Kerala KAPA | — | State detention laws
[11:8:7] - Roots: **Regulation III of 1818** and the **Rowlatt Act**. NCRB: over **1.2 lakh** detentions a year, about **half quashed** by HCs (Jun 2026).
## SUB 9 | Articles 20 and 21 During Emergency

[11:9:1] - Puttaswamy (2017) reaffirmed 20 and 21 as non-derogable.

## SUB 1 | Articles 23-24 – Right Against Exploitation
[12:1:0] # Article 23
[12:1:1] - **23(1)**: prohibits **traffic in human beings, begar** and other **forced labour**.
[12:1:2] - **Begar** = forced unpaid labour exacted by officials/zamindars.
[12:1:3] - **23(2)**: State may impose **compulsory service for public purposes** (military/social service); payment not required in every case, but **no discrimination on religion, race, caste or class**.
[12:1:4] # Article 24
[12:1:5] - No child **below 14** in any factory, mine or other hazardous employment – an **absolute prohibition**; **harmless/innocent work** is not barred.
[12:1:6] - Related DPSPs: **39(e), 39(f)** – tender age of children not abused. UN Charter: childhood deserves special assistance.
[12:1:7] - **Arts. 21A and 24** together (education 6-14 + no hazardous work) make the right against exploitation meaningful for children (**APPSC G1 2022**).

## SUB 2 | Article 25 – Text, Powers, Limits
[12:2:0] # Text and Terms
[12:2:1] - **25(1)**: "all persons are equally entitled to **freedom of conscience** and the right freely to **profess, practise and propagate** religion" – subject to **public order, morality and health** and to the **other provisions of Part III**. Quoted text → identify Article (asked **2018, 2023**).
[12:2:2] - Limits: **public order, morality, health**; **humanism is not** a ground.
[12:2:3] - **Prisoners:** a person does **not lose freedom of religion** merely by lawful detention; FRs survive custody (**Sunil Batra**).
[12:2:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [12:2:4.0] Conscience | Inner freedom to shape one's relation with God; includes freedom to follow no religion

  [12:2:4.2] Propagate | Spreading one's beliefs – does NOT include a right to convert another (it would impinge on the other's freedom of conscience)
[12:2:5] # State Powers – 25(2)
[12:2:6] - **25(2)(a)**: regulate **economic, financial, political or other secular activity** associated with religion – not spiritual belief.
[12:2:7] - **25(2)(b)**: social welfare and reform; **throw open Hindu religious institutions** of a public character to all classes of Hindus. State may step in to remove a social evil; no forcible conversion.
[12:2:8] # Explanations
[12:2:9] - **Explanation I**: wearing and carrying **kirpans** = part of the profession of the **Sikh** religion.
[12:2:10] - **Explanation II**: "**Hindus**" includes **Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists** – **not Parsis**.
