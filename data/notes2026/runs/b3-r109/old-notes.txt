=== NOTES 3:109 | Andhra Pradesh Economy After Bifurcation – Gsdp, Growth Rate, Sectoral Shares
## SUB 0 | Bifurcation and Its Impact on the Economy
[109:0:0] # Bifurcation facts
[109:0:1] - AP bifurcated on **2 June 2014** under the **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014**.
[109:0:2] - Residual AP initially had **13 districts** (specified in the Act).
[109:0:3] - **Revenue divisions (2014):** about **50** in residual AP's 13 districts; numbers changed with the **2022** district reorganisation.
[109:0:4] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [109:0:4.0] Revenue loss | Hyderabad alone ≈ 48% of undivided AP's total revenue of ₹70,548 crore (2012-13)
  [109:0:4.1] AP got | Bulk of the agrarian population, irrigation-project liabilities, power-sector deficits
  [109:0:4.2] Telangana kept | IT, commercial and industrial assets and the capital
  [109:0:4.3] Result | AP became a chronic revenue-deficit State
  [109:0:4.4] Capital city | New capital Amaravati; major institutions built from scratch
[109:0:5] - Hyderabad-centred services and industry went to Telangana, so **agriculture's share in AP rose and industry's share fell**.
[109:0:6] - IT base lost; new IT parks at **Visakhapatnam, Tirupati, Vijayawada**.
[109:0:7] # The "missing middle"
[109:0:8] - Manufacturing only about **11% of GSDP**: AP is moving from agriculture to services **without a manufacturing-led stage**.
[109:0:9] - **Industrial Development Policy 4.0 (2024-29)** puts manufacturing at the centre to tackle the missing middle.

## SUB 2 | Gsdp – Concepts, Compilation and Estimate Cycle
[109:2:0] # Terms
[109:2:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [109:2:1.0] SDP | Value of all goods and services produced within the State in a year, counted once (no duplication)
  [109:2:1.1] GSDP | GVA + product taxes – product subsidies
  [109:2:1.2] Per capita income | Computed on NSDP
[109:2:2] # Compilation
[109:2:3] - Compiled by the **Directorate of Economics & Statistics (DES), AP** – **not NSO** – at current prices and constant **2011-12** prices.
[109:2:4] - **3 broad sectors, 17 sub-sectors**:
[109:2:5] TABLE head: Sector | Sub-sectors
  [109:2:5.0] Agriculture & allied (5) | Agriculture (crops), horticulture, livestock, forestry & logging, fishing & aquaculture
  [109:2:5.1] Industry (4) | Mining & quarrying, manufacturing, electricity-gas-water supply, construction
  [109:2:5.2] Services (8) | Trade, hotels & restaurants; railways; transport by other means & storage; communications; banking & insurance; real estate & ownership of dwellings; public administration; other services
[109:2:6] - Base year: AP Survey tables still use **2011-12** (2019-20 is wrong). **India's new GDP series** (MoSPI, released **27 Feb 2026**) has base **2022-23**; it revised India's 2023-24 growth to 7.2% and 2024-25 to 7.1%.
[109:2:7] # Estimate cycle
[109:2:8] - Advance estimates (AE/FAE) → First Revised (FRE) → Second Revised (SRE) → Third Revised (TRE).
[109:2:9] - Example: 2021-22 "**record**" growth of **11.43%** (constant) was later revised to **7.14%** (TRE); current-price 17.36% (SRE).

  [110:0:1.0] Per capita income (PCI) | NSDP ÷ population – a net measure (after depreciation)
  [110:0:1.1] Per capita GSDP | Gross measure; current or constant prices
  [110:0:1.2] Current prices | Include inflation
  [110:0:1.3] Constant (2011-12) prices | Show real growth
  [110:0:1.4] District Domestic Product (DDP) | District GDP, compiled by the State Directorate of Economics and Statistics (DES) – not NSO; DES is also the nodal agency for GSDP
[110:0:2] - **Standard of living** is shown by **per capita income**.
[110:0:3] - PCI = income ÷ population, so it **rises only when income grows faster than population**.
[110:0:4] - PCI **can fall in a developing country even as national income rises** – when population growth exceeds income growth.
[110:0:5] - Causes of slow PCI growth: population growth, low productivity, low-income/low-saving "vicious circle".
[110:0:6] # Limits
[110:0:7] - PCI is an **average**: it **hides inequality** and ignores **cost-of-living** differences; high PCI can coexist with poverty.
[110:0:8] - The **P4 scheme** targets the gap between high average PCI and lasting poverty and inequality.
[110:0:9] Per capita GSDP (gross) and PCI (net, NSDP) are not interchangeable – never compare AP's per capita GSDP with India's per capita income.
