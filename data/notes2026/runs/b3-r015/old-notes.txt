=== NOTES 3:15 | Distributive Justice and Inclusive Growth
## SUB 0 | Concept and Three Pillars
[15:0:0] - **Inclusive growth** = **broad-based, shared, pro-poor** growth that raises people's participation; benefits shared by all sections (comprehensive, shared and pro-poor: **all of these**).
[15:0:1] - Covers both **pace and pattern** of growth (who benefits), plus gender and regional balance.
[15:0:2] - **Three pillars**: **Market Economics, Empowerment, Pragmatism** (APPSC-GS 2025).
[15:0:3] -
[15:0:4] - OECD/G20 theme: Promoting Strong (Sustainable) and Inclusive Growth.
[15:0:5] Distractors: "Antyodaya, Empowerment, Poverty and Unemployment".

=== NOTES 3:16 | Human Development Index – Components, Methodology, India's Rank and Trend
## SUB 0 | Human Development – Concept and Origin
[16:0:0] # The Idea
[16:0:1] - **People and their capabilities**, not growth alone, are the ultimate criteria of development.
[16:0:2] - Human development approach: focuses on **people, opportunities and choices**; broader than an economic measure.
[16:0:3] - **UNDP** was the first to focus on the human aspect of development – **first HDR, 1990**, which had an HDI.
[16:0:4] - HDI developed by a team led by **Mahbub ul Haq** (with **Amartya Sen**; team incl. **Inge Kaul**).
[16:0:5] - HDR is published by **UNDP**, released most years since 1990; it compares countries on **education, health and per-capita income** (APPSC-GS 2025 key: "Human Development Report").
[16:0:6] - UNDP's two categories of poverty: **income poverty** and **human poverty** (both).
[16:0:7] - **Minimum Needs Programme** = same as investment in the human approach.
[16:0:8] # Persons → Contribution
[16:0:9] TABLE head: Person | Contribution
  [16:0:9.0] Amartya Sen | Capability approach (*Development as Freedom*): development = expanding real freedoms; co-conceived HDI
  [16:0:9.1] Martha Nussbaum | Extended Sen's approach with a list of central human capabilities (*Women and Human Development*)
  [16:0:9.2] Mahbub ul Haq | Conceived the HDI; led the first HDR team
[16:0:10] # GDP vs Human Development
[16:0:11] TABLE head: Basis | GDP / per-capita income | Human development
  [16:0:11.0] Nature | Economic measure | Broader, people-centred
  [16:0:11.1] Focus | Income and output | Capabilities and well-being
  [16:0:11.2] Dimensions | One-dimensional | Multidimensional
[16:0:12] - Income (GNI) is a **means**, not the end; **higher GNI per capita does not guarantee a higher HDI**.
[16:0:13] - **Same GNI, different HDI** shows the role of **national policy choices** (APPSC-G1 2024).
[16:0:14] - Example: **Malaysia** has higher GNI per capita than **Chile**, but Chile has ~**7 years** more life expectancy and **3** more years of expected schooling → much higher HDI.
## SUB 1 | Dimensions, Indicators and Goalposts
[16:1:0] TABLE head: Dimension | Indicator | Minimum | Maximum
  [16:1:0.0] Long and healthy life | Life expectancy at birth | 20 | 85
  [16:1:0.1] Knowledge (education) | Expected years of schooling | 0 | 18
  [16:1:0.2] Knowledge (education) | Mean years of schooling | 0 | 15
  [16:1:0.3] Decent standard of living | GNI per capita (PPP $), log | $100 | $75,000
[16:1:1] - HDI = composite of **life expectancy, educational attainment, per capita GNI**; max value **1**, min **0**.
[16:1:2] - **Before 2010**, per capita **GDP** was used; GNI and the two schooling indicators came with the **2010 revision**.
[16:1:3] # What HDI Leaves Out (odd-one-out)
[16:1:4] - NOT in HDI: **condition of environment**, **urbanisation**, **health and nutrition** (as a separate item), human security, ownership of basic assets, inequality, poverty, gender gaps, empowerment, participation, MPI.
## SUB 2 | Methodology
[16:2:0] - 1. **Dimension index** = (actual − minimum) / (maximum − minimum). Example: actual 50, min 20, max 80 → (50−20)/(80−20) = **0.50**.
[16:2:1] - 2. **Education index** = **arithmetic mean** of expected-years and mean-years sub-indices.
[16:2:2] - 3. **Income**: uses the **logarithm** of GNI per capita → **diminishing returns** to income.
[16:2:3] - 4. **HDI** = **geometric mean** of the three dimension indices (**since 2010**) – a strong dimension cannot fully offset a weak one.
[16:2:4] - **PPP dollars** allow fair cross-country comparison across price levels (based on ICP surveys; goalposts first used 2011 PPP).

=== NOTES 3:17 | Related Undp Indices – Ihdi, Gii, Gdi, Mpi
## SUB 0 | Year Introduced – Firsts
[17:0:0] TABLE head: Index | Introduced | Note
  [17:0:0.0] GDI (Gender-related Development Index) | HDR 1995 | Gender inequalities in HDI achievements
  [17:0:0.1] GEM (Gender Empowerment Measure) | HDR 1995 | Gender inequalities in empowerment
  [17:0:0.2] Capability Poverty Measure | HDR 1996 | Three indicators
  [17:0:0.3] Human Poverty Index (HPI) | HDR 1997 | "Human poverty"; complemented HDI
  [17:0:0.4] MPI, GII, IHDI | HDR 2010 | MPI replaced HPI; GII fixed GDI/GEM weaknesses
  [17:0:0.5] Gender Social Norms Index | 5 March 2020 (first) | Social beliefs blocking gender equality (76 countries)
[17:0:1] 1995 – 1996 – 1997 – 2010 = GDI/GEM – CPM – HPI – MPI/GII/IHDI
## SUB 1 | Human Poverty Index
[17:1:0] - Deprivation in **longevity, knowledge, decent standard of living**; indicates **standard of living** (APPSC-GS 2018 key).
[17:1:1] - **HPI-1** for developing, **HPI-2** for developed countries; replaced by **MPI in HDR 2010**.
## SUB 2 | Capability Poverty Measure (hdr 1996)
[17:2:0] - Lack of three basic capabilities (well-fed and healthy, healthy reproduction, educated). Indicators:
[17:2:1] - 1. **Children under 5 underweight** (child malnutrition)
[17:2:2] - 2. **Births unattended** by trained health personnel
[17:2:3] - 3. **Female illiteracy**
[17:2:4] Male labour-force participation is NOT a CPM indicator.
## SUB 3 | Gender Inequality Index and Gdi
[17:3:0] # GII (HDR 2010)
[17:3:1] TABLE head: Dimension | Indicators
  [17:3:1.0] Reproductive health | Maternal mortality ratio, adolescent birth rate
  [17:3:1.1] Empowerment | Women's share of parliamentary seats; adults 25+ with some secondary education
  [17:3:1.2] Labour market | Female and male labour force participation (15+)
[17:3:2] - **Higher GII = greater inequality**. India (HDR 2025): **102nd of 193**, value **0.403** (up from 108).
[17:3:3] # GDI and IHDI
[17:3:4] - **GDI** = ratio of female HDI to male HDI (health, education, earned income); **GDI = 1 = parity**; countries in **5 groups** by distance from parity. India (HDR 2025): **0.874**, **Group 5**.
[17:3:5] - **IHDI** = HDI discounted for inequality in each dimension; **HDI − IHDI = loss** (in %). IHDI = HDI with no inequality, otherwise always lower.
[17:3:6] "0 = equal, 1 = worst" describes the GII, not the GDI.
## SUB 4 | Mpi Design
[17:4:0] - Proposed (**2010**) by **Sabina Alkire and Maria Emma Santos**; developed by **OPHI with UNDP**, presented in HDR 2010; method **Alkire-Foster**.
[17:4:1] - **MPI = Headcount ratio (H) × Intensity (A)** – hence small values (India 0.066); not a sum, not a geometric mean (that is HDI), not a $1-a-day measure.
[17:4:2] - **MPI-poor** = deprived in at least **one-third** of weighted indicators. Uses household-survey micro-data.
[17:4:3] - Most suitable index for the **intensity of poverty** in India.
[17:4:4] # Global MPI – 3 Dimensions, 10 Indicators
[17:4:5] TABLE head: Dimension | Indicators | Weight
  [17:4:5.0] Health | Nutrition, child mortality | 1/6 each
  [17:4:5.1] Education | Years of schooling, school attendance | 1/6 each
  [17:4:5.2] Standard of living | Electricity, drinking water, sanitation, housing, cooking fuel, assets | 1/18 each
[17:4:6] # National MPI – 3 Dimensions, 12 Indicators
[17:4:7] TABLE head: Dimension | Indicators
  [17:4:7.0] Health (3) | Nutrition, child and adolescent mortality, maternal health
  [17:4:7.1] Education (2) | Years of schooling, school attendance
  [17:4:7.2] Standard of living (7) | Cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets, bank account
[17:4:8] - Years of schooling is NOT a standard-of-living indicator.
[17:4:9] - Social indicators of poverty: illiteracy, lack of healthcare, jobs, malnutrition, safe drinking water, sanitation – **not** size of house or fewer means of transport.
[17:4:10] Global 10 vs National 12. Saying the National MPI does not use Alkire-Foster is wrong. The 2010 MPI does NOT measure only extreme poverty below $1 a day (UPPSC 2025).
