=== Timeline of the UNDP Indices
| Index | Introduced | Note |
|---|---|---|
| HDI | HDR 1990 | Health, education, income |
| **GDI** (Gender-related Development Index) and **GEM** (Gender Empowerment Measure) | **HDR 1995** | Gender inequalities in HDI achievements / in empowerment |
| Capability Poverty Measure (CPM) | HDR 1996 | Lack of three basic capabilities |
| **Human Poverty Index (HPI)** | **HDR 1997** | Deprivation; HPI-1 developing, HPI-2 developed countries |
| **MPI, GII, IHDI** | **HDR 2010** | **MPI replaced HPI**; GII replaced GDI/GEM as the gender-inequality measure |
| PHDI | HDR 2020 | HDI adjusted for planetary pressures |
| **Gender Social Norms Index** | **5 March 2020** (first) | **UNDP**; social beliefs blocking gender equality |
- Order: 1995 GDI/GEM → 1996 CPM → 1997 HPI → 2010 MPI/GII/IHDI → 2020 PHDI and GSNI.

=== Human Poverty Index, Capability Poverty Measure and Income vs Human Poverty
- UNDP's two categories of poverty: **income poverty** and **human poverty** – both.
- **HPI** measures deprivation in **longevity, knowledge and a decent standard of living** (APPSC-GS 2018 key: it indicates the **standard of living**).
  - HPI-1 (developing): survival to 40, adult illiteracy, no safe water, underweight children (**social services**).
  - HPI-2 (developed) adds **income poverty** and **long-term unemployment** (social exclusion) → income, literacy, social-services and employment deprivations are **all** covered (CDS 2013).
- **CPM (HDR 1996)** indicators: **children under 5 underweight** (child malnutrition), **births unattended** by trained personnel, **female illiteracy** – not male illiteracy, male labour-force participation or drinking water.
~~ Sources: UNDP HDR 1996 (CPM) and HDR 1997 / 2009 technical notes (HPI-1, HPI-2) (hdr.undp.org); app notes 3:17 sub 1-2

=== Gender Indices – GDI, GEM and GII
| Index | Dimensions / indicators | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **GDI** | Female vs male **health** (life expectancy), **knowledge** (schooling), **living standard** (earned income) | Ratio female HDI / male HDI; **1 = parity**, below 1 = women behind; India 0.874 (HDR 2025, Group 5) |
| **GEM** (1995) | **Political participation** (parliament seats), **economic participation** (managers, professionals), **power over economic resources** (earned income) | Empowerment – **no health or education** indicator |
| **GII** (2010) | **Reproductive health** (MMR, adolescent birth rate); **empowerment** (parliament seats, secondary education 25+); **labour market** (LFPR 15+) | **0 = equality**, higher = more inequality |
- **GDI and GEM indicate inequalities in gender** (not income, expenditure or regions).
- The GDI does **not** cover **gender violence**.
- **India's GII:** **108th** (HDR 2023-24, for 2022; value 0.437) → **102nd**, 0.403 (HDR 2025, for 2023).

=== IHDI, PHDI and Beyond-HDI Statements
- **IHDI** discounts each HDI dimension for inequality: **no inequality → IHDI = HDI**; otherwise IHDI < HDI. HDI = "potential", IHDI = "actual"; **HDI − IHDI = loss due to inequality** – that loss is **not** part of the HDI itself.
- **PHDI** (2020) adjusts HDI for **CO2 emissions and material footprint**; it captures **intergenerational** inequality just as the IHDI captures **intragenerational** inequality.
- **HDR 2021/22**: a **new uncertainty complex** – dangerous planetary change, sweeping societal transformations and **polarisation**.
- A GDI **below 1** means women's HDI is **lower** than men's (not higher); same GNI with different outcomes shows the role of **policy**, not "growth over development".
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Exams 16 p.2-3 (IHDI); UNDP HDR 2020 (PHDI), HDR 2021/22 overview (uncertainty complex) (hdr.undp.org); app notes 3:16 sub 6

=== MPI – Design and the Global MPI
- **Global MPI** (2010): **OPHI (Oxford) + UNDP**, presented in HDR 2010; proposed by **Sabina Alkire and Maria Emma Santos**; method **Alkire-Foster**; uses household-survey micro-data; **not** a $1-a-day measure of extreme poverty.
- **MPI = Headcount ratio (H) × Intensity (A)** – not a sum; MPI-poor = deprived in at least **one-third** of weighted indicators. Most suitable for the **intensity of poverty**.
| Dimension | Global MPI indicators (10) | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Health** | Nutrition, child mortality | 1/6 each |
| **Education** | Years of schooling, school attendance | 1/6 each |
| **Standard of living** | Cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets | 1/18 each |
- **India in the Global MPI** (UNDP-OPHI editions):

| Edition | India |
|---|---|
| HDR 2013 | **53.7%** multidimensionally poor |
| Global MPI 2018 | **271 million** out of poverty (2005-06 to 2015-16); **Bihar** poorest State |
| Global MPI 2022 (UNDP + OPHI) | MPI **0.069** (2019-21); headcount 16.4%; about **415 million** (books: 414 million) out (2005-06 to 2019-21) |
| **Global MPI 2023** (released **11 July 2023** by **UNDP and OPHI**) | Same India data; 415 million out in 15 years |
| Global MPI 2024 → 2025 | MPI 0.064 → 0.061 |

=== National MPI (NITI Aayog), States and Andhra Pradesh
| Feature | National MPI |
|---|---|
| By | **NITI Aayog** (with UNDP, OPHI); baseline Nov 2021; monitored under GIRG |
| Method | **Alkire-Foster** – same as the Global MPI |
| Indicators | **12** = 10 global + **maternal health** + **bank account**; standard of living has 7 (cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets, bank account) – **years of schooling is an education indicator** |
- **Progress Review 2023** (NFHS-4 → NFHS-5): headcount **24.85% → 14.96%**; MPI 0.117 → 0.066; about **13.5 crore** out; **rural fell faster** (32.59% → 19.28%) than urban (8.65% → 5.27%).
- NFHS-5: **J&K 4.80%** and **Himachal 4.93%** both under 6%; UP 22.93%. Lowest MPI Kerala; highest Bihar.
- NITI Discussion Paper (Jan 2024): 29.17% (2013-14) → 11.28% (2022-23); 24.82 crore out; UP largest fall (5.94 crore).
- **PM Garib Kalyan Package** (2020) included free foodgrain entitlements (PMGKAY).
- **Andhra Pradesh:** State MPI report (2018, Planning Dept + OPHI): **41.6% (2005-06) → 21% (2016-17)**; NITI MPI 2023: AP **6.06%** (India 14.96%), MPI 0.025, intensity 41.12%, rural 7.71% / urban 2.20%; AP 9th.
