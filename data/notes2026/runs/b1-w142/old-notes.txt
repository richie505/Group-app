## SUB 0 | Alvars and Nayanars – the Tamil Beginning
[142:0:0] TABLE head: Feature | Alvars | Nayanars
  [142:0:0.0] Devotees of | Vishnu (Vaishnava) | Shiva (Shaiva)
  [142:0:0.1] Number | 12 | 63 (3 women)
  [142:0:0.2] Hymns / canon | Nalayira Divya Prabandham (4,000 hymns, "Tamil Veda"; called pasurams), compiled by Nathamuni (10th c.) | Tirumurai (12 books, "Fifth Veda"), compiled by Nambi Andar Nambi; Tevaram = hymns of Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar
  [142:0:0.3] Women | Andal – only woman Alvar | Karaikkal Ammaiyar
  [142:0:0.4] Notable | Poigai, Bhoothath, Pey = first three Alvars; Nammalvar = foremost (Tiruvaymoli); Periyalvar | Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar, Manikkavachakar = the Nalvar / Samayacharyas; Tiruvasagam = Manikkavachakar
[142:0:1] - **Bhakti movement was started by the Alvar (and Nayanar) saints** of the Tamil South, **6th-9th c.** – not by the Sufis, Surdas or Tulsidas.
[142:0:2] - **First Vaishnava devotees of South India = Alvars** (APPSC G2 2000, AP History PYQ; HPSC 2012) – not Nayanars, Bhagavatas or Virashaivas.
[142:0:3] - **Pasurams (pasuras)** = Tamil hymns of the Alvars **in praise of Vishnu** (AP Police 2016); Nayanar hymns on Shiva are the Tevaram.
[142:0:4] - **Composers of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham = the Alvars** (CDS 2018); Appar and Sambandar were Nayanars.
[142:0:5] - **Nayanars = Shaivites** (UP UDA 2006, UPRO 2014).
[142:0:6] - Both came from **all castes**, even "untouchables" (Panars, Pulaiyars), and were critical of Buddhism and Jainism.
[142:0:7] - **Periya Puranam** (lives of the 63 Nayanars) = **Sekkizhar** under **Kulottunga II** (12th Tirumurai).
[142:0:8] > Alvars = Vishnu, 12, Divya Prabandham (pasurams); Nayanars = Shiva, 63, Tevaram/Tirumurai.
[142:0:9] ~~ Sources: PMF Art & Culture Part 5 p.116, p.120-121; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 4 p.36; old app notes row 142
[142:0:10] - **Tevaram** (hymns of Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar) = the **earliest and most sacred** part (books 1–7) of the Tirumurai.
[142:0:11] - **Chola patronage of the Nayanars:** **Rajaraja I** consecrated bronzes of all **63 Nayanmars at Chidambaram**; Parantaka I had installed statues of Appar, Sambandar and Sundarar.

## SUB 2 | Origins, Roots and Common Features
[142:2:0] - **Bhakti** from the root **"bhaj"** – to share / participate; devotion to a personal God.
[142:2:1] - **Roots:** **Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, Bhagavata Purana** – not the Arthashastra.
[142:2:2] - **Bhagavad Gita gives equal importance to Jnana, Karma and Bhakti** as means of liberation (UPPSC 2005); **Chapter 12 = Bhakti Yoga**. Advaita privileges jnana.
[142:2:3] - **Bhagavata Purana** (Tamil land, 9th-10th c.) put bhakti at the centre; **Navadha (ninefold, 9) bhakti** – shravana, kirtana, smarana, padasevana, archana, vandana, dasya, sakhya, atmanivedana (UP UDA 2010).
[142:2:4] - **Spread:** Tamil South 6th-9th c. → north from the 12th-13th c.; **reborn / zenith in the 15th-16th c.** with Kabir, Nanak, Sur, Tulsi, Mira (UPPCS 1993).
[142:2:5] - **Why it arose:** reaction against ritual, priestly dominance and caste; **jnana and karma margas too hard** for ordinary people.
[142:2:6] - **Common feature of all Bhakti saints:** they **composed in the language of the people** (vernacular) (State PSC) – not all rejected priests or idols (Saguna saints kept idols).
[142:2:7] - **Ramanuja's movement in Andhra** (Vishnu devotion + social equality) = the **Bhakti movement** (APPSC 2022) – not Arya Samaj or Lingayatism.
[142:2:8] - Core beliefs: **monotheism**, **guru** essential, **prapatti** (self-surrender); devotion above birth and status.
[142:2:9] - Modern scholars trace its roots to the **Alvars and Nayanars**, not to Islam; Islam shaped later egalitarian ideas.
[142:2:10] > Gita = jnana + karma + bhakti; Bhagavata = 9 forms; vernacular = the common feature.
[142:2:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 4 p.34-38; PMF Art & Culture Part 5 p.115, p.118; old app notes row 142

## SUB 4 | Nirguna and Saguna Streams
[142:4:0] TABLE head: Stream | Idea of God | Saints
  [142:4:0.0] Nirguna | Formless, without attributes; rejects idols | Kabir, Guru Nanak, Dadu Dayal, Ravidas
  [142:4:0.1] Saguna – Ramamargi | Rama | Tulsidas
  [142:4:0.2] Saguna – Krishnamargi | Krishna | Surdas, Mirabai, Chaitanya, Vallabha
[142:4:1] - Both streams drew on Upanishadic **Advaita**; both monotheistic.
[142:4:2] - **Dadu Panth** – founded by **Dadu Dayal** in Rajasthan, in Kabir's tradition.
[142:4:3] - **Raskhan** was a devotee of **Krishna**, not Rama.
[142:4:4] > Nirguna = Kabir, Nanak, Dadu, Ravidas; Saguna = Tulsi (Rama), Sur, Mira, Chaitanya (Krishna).

## SUB 5 | Virashaiva Movement and the Vachanas
[142:5:0] - **Basavanna (Basaveshwara)** (12th c., born **Bagewadi**) led the **Virashaiva / Lingayat** movement in **Karnataka**; treasurer at **Kalyana** under Kalachuri **Bijjala**.
[142:5:1] - **Anubhava Mantapa** – assembly of sharanas, presided by **Allama Prabhu**; **Akkamahadevi** a leading woman sharana.
[142:5:2] - **Vachanas** – short **Kannada** sayings, signed **Kudalasangamadeva**.
[142:5:3] - Rejected caste, temple ritual and idol worship.
[142:5:4] - **Andhra:** Veerashaivism came through **Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya** and **Palkuriki Somanatha** (**Basava Puranam**, Telugu).
[142:5:5] > Basavanna – Kalyana – Bijjala – Kannada vachanas – Kudalasangama.
[142:5:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Art & Culture Part 5 p.125; old app notes row 142
## SUB 6 | Nathpanthis, Siddhas and Yogis
[142:6:0] - Popularised devotional religion in **Northern India**; led by **Matsyendranath** and **Gorakhnath**.
[142:6:1] - Practised **hatha yoga**, meditation and **renunciation**; rejected ritual and caste; open to "low" castes.
[142:6:2] - **Charyapada** – songs of the Buddhist **Siddhacharyas** (earliest Bangla poems); manuscript found by **Haraprasad Shastri** in the Nepal royal library (1907).

## SUB 2 | Kabir
[144:2:0] - Born (tradition) to a **Brahmin widow**, raised by Muslim weavers **Niru and Nima**; disciple of **Ramananda**; **Nirguna Bhakti** (APPSC FRO).
[144:2:1] - One God of many names – Ram, Rahim, Allah, Hari; **condemned pilgrimage, idol worship** and ritual of Hindus and Muslims; stood for Hindu-Muslim unity, universal love, and **householder life** → "he was not against idol worship" is the **wrong** statement (CDS 2011).
[144:2:2] - **Bijak** = his dohas and sakhis, holy book of the **Kabirpanthis** (MPPSC 2000, 2002); also **Sakhi, Sabad, Ramaini**.
[144:2:3] - **Amarmul (Anurag Sagar)** = dialogues of **Kabir and Dharamdas** (UPPCS 2003).
[144:2:4] - "**Pothi padh padh jag mua … dhai akhar prem ka, padhe so pandit hoy**" = **Kabir** (MPPSC 2022).
[144:2:5] - Language **Sadhukkadi** (panchmel khichri); hymns in the Adi Granth.
[144:2:6] > Kabir: Ramananda's disciple, Nirguna, Bijak, Amarmul (with Dharamdas).
[144:2:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 5 p.2; PMF Art & Culture Part 5 p.122; old app notes row 144
[144:2:8] - **Kabir's verses survive in three traditions:** the **Bijak** (Kabirpanth, Varanasi), the **Kabir Granthavali** (Dadupanth, Rajasthan) and the **Adi Granth Sahib**.

[144:3:0] - **Chaitanya** (1486, **Nabadwip**, Nadia; childhood name **Nimai**) – **Vaishnava**, Krishna devotee; founded **Gaudiya Vaishnavism**; **Sankirtan**; lived at **Puri** under Prataparudra Gajapati.

[144:3:3] - **Mirabai** – Rathore princess of Merta (born Kudki); married **Bhojraj**, son of **Rana Sanga** of Mewar (RAS 1997); widowed young; Krishna devotee.

## SUB 0 | Telugu Bhakti Composers – Bhakta Ramadasu, Tyagaraja, Kshetrayya and Others
[145:0:0] # Bhakta Ramadasu (Kancherla Gopanna)
[145:0:1] - **Kancherla Gopanna = Bhakta Ramadasu**: Golconda revenue official (tahsildar of **Bhadrachalam**); used state revenue to rebuild the **Bhadrachalam Rama temple**.
[145:0:2] - Jailed **12 years in Golconda fort** by **Abul Hasan Tana Shah**; in prison he composed the **Dasarathi Satakam** (104 poems to Rama) and the Ramadasu kirtanas.
[145:0:3] - **Dasarathi Satakam = Gopanna** (APPSC G1 2002, asked three times) – NOT **Sumati Satakam** (Baddena), Bhaskara Satakam (Maravi Venkayya) or Vemana Satakam.
[145:0:4] - English translation of Dasarathi Satakam credited (APPSC G2 2001 key) to **Dr. Bhanumathi Ramakrishna**.

## SUB 1 | Tallapaka Annamacharya and the Tallapaka Family
[145:1:0] # Annamacharya (1408–1503)
[145:1:1] - Born at **Tallapaka** village (Rajampet mandal) – historically **Cuddapah (Kadapa) district** (APPSC 2012 key). The area went to the new **Annamayya district** (named after him) in April 2022 and, with the **Rajampet division, back to YSR Kadapa district** from 1 Jan 2026 (as of 2026).
[145:1:2] - Contemporary of **Saluva Narasimha Raya** (APPSC G2 2011) – not Harihara, Deva Raya I or III; also a contemporary of **Purandara Dasa**.
[145:1:3] - About **32,000 sankirtanas** to **Lord Venkateswara of Tirumala**; about **12,000** survive on **copper plates** found at Tirumala (1922); recovered and edited by **Veturi Prabhakara Sastry**. Compositions are Adhyatma (spiritual) and Sringara.
[145:1:4] - Titles: **Pada Kavita Pitamaha**, Sankirtanacharya; earliest composer of sankirtanas → **pioneer of the kriti form**; the kirtana structure shaped later Carnatic music. Opposed untouchability (**Brahmam Okkate**).
[145:1:5] - Other works: **Sri Venkateswara Satakam** (first Telugu Shringara Satakam), Sringara Manjari, Sankirtana Lakshanam (Sanskrit).
[145:1:6] - **Arudra** called him "Incarnated Telugu".

## SUB 3 | Virashaivism and Saiva Bhakti in Andhra
[145:3:0] # Basava and the Virashaivas (Lingayats)
[145:3:1] - Founder: **Basavanna (Basaveshwara)**, 12th c., a **Brahmana** and **minister of the Kalachuri ruler Bijjala II** at **Kalyana**; Anubhava Mantapa ("religious parliament"); **Akka Mahadevi** and Allama Prabhu were Vachana saints (12th-c. Karnataka).
[145:3:2] - Lingayats worship **Shiva** in the form of the **Ishtalinga** worn on the body; theology **Shakti Vishishtadvaita**; **Kayaka** = honest labour, **Dasoha** = sharing.
[145:3:3] - Social message: rejected caste, **untouchability** and Brahmanical ritual, **condemned child marriage**, **questioned the theory of rebirth**, and encouraged practices the **Dharmashastras disapproved** – **post-puberty marriage and widow remarriage**.
[145:3:4] - **NDA 2019:** I (Basavanna, a Brahmana, minister of a Kalachuri ruler) ✔; II says the practices were "approved by the Dharmashastras" ✘ → **only I**.
[145:3:5] - **UPSC 2016:** Siddhas (Sittars) of Tamil Nadu were **monotheistic and condemned idolatry** ✔; Lingayats **questioned rebirth and rejected caste hierarchy** ✔ → **both**.

[146:1:10] - **10 maqamat (stages):** **Tauba** (repentance), Wara, Zuhd, **Faqr** (poverty), **Sabr** (patience), Shukr, Khauf, Raja, Tawakkul, **Riza** (last). **Namaz – daily prayer is not one of the 10 stages** → the incorrect pair.
[146:1:11] - Ba-shara (within Sharia: Chishti, Suhrawardi, Qadiri, Naqshbandi, Firdausi) vs **Be-shara** (Qalandars, Madaris).
[146:1:12] # Practices and yoga
[146:1:13] - **UPSC 2012:** Sufis practised **meditation and breath control**, **severe ascetic exercises in a lonely place**, and **holy songs (sama) to arouse ecstasy** → **1, 2 and 3**.

[146:1:16] - **Devotional music as a way to God = Muinuddin Chishti** (UPSC 1996 ×2) – Chishtis made sama central.
