## SUB 0 | Alvars and Nayanars – the Tamil Beginning
[0:0] TABLE head: Feature | Alvars | Nayanars
  [0:0.0] Devotees of | Vishnu (Vaishnava) | Shiva (Shaiva)
  [0:0.1] Number | 12 | 63 (3 women)
  [0:0.2] Hymns / canon | Nalayira Divya Prabandham (4,000 hymns, "Tamil Veda"; called pasurams), compiled by Nathamuni (10th c.) | Tirumurai (12 books, "Fifth Veda"), compiled by Nambi Andar Nambi; Tevaram = hymns of Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar
  [0:0.3] Women | Andal – only woman Alvar | Karaikkal Ammaiyar
  [0:0.4] Notable | Poigai, Bhoothath, Pey = first three Alvars; Nammalvar = foremost (Tiruvaymoli); Periyalvar | Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar, Manikkavachakar = the Nalvar / Samayacharyas; Tiruvasagam = Manikkavachakar
[0:1] - **Bhakti movement was started by the Alvar (and Nayanar) saints** of the Tamil South, **6th-9th c.** – not by the Sufis, Surdas or Tulsidas.
[0:2] - **First Vaishnava devotees of South India = Alvars** (APPSC G2 2000, AP History PYQ; HPSC 2012) – not Nayanars, Bhagavatas or Virashaivas.
[0:3] - **Pasurams (pasuras)** = Tamil hymns of the Alvars **in praise of Vishnu** (AP Police 2016); Nayanar hymns on Shiva are the Tevaram.
[0:4] - **Composers of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham = the Alvars** (CDS 2018); Appar and Sambandar were Nayanars.
[0:5] - **Nayanars = Shaivites** (UP UDA 2006, UPRO 2014).
[0:6] - Both came from **all castes**, even "untouchables" (Panars, Pulaiyars), and were critical of Buddhism and Jainism.
[0:7] - **Periya Puranam** (lives of the 63 Nayanars) = **Sekkizhar** under **Kulottunga II** (12th Tirumurai).
[0:8] > Alvars = Vishnu, 12, Divya Prabandham (pasurams); Nayanars = Shiva, 63, Tevaram/Tirumurai.

## SUB 2 | Origins, Roots and Common Features
[2:0] - **Bhakti** from the root **"bhaj"** – to share / participate; devotion to a personal God.
[2:1] - **Roots:** **Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, Bhagavata Purana** – not the Arthashastra.
[2:2] - **Bhagavad Gita gives equal importance to Jnana, Karma and Bhakti** as means of liberation (UPPSC 2005); **Chapter 12 = Bhakti Yoga**. Advaita privileges jnana.
[2:3] - **Bhagavata Purana** (Tamil land, 9th-10th c.) put bhakti at the centre; **Navadha (ninefold, 9) bhakti** – shravana, kirtana, smarana, padasevana, archana, vandana, dasya, sakhya, atmanivedana (UP UDA 2010).
[2:4] - **Spread:** Tamil South 6th-9th c. → north from the 12th-13th c.; **reborn / zenith in the 15th-16th c.** with Kabir, Nanak, Sur, Tulsi, Mira (UPPCS 1993).
[2:5] - **Why it arose:** reaction against ritual, priestly dominance and caste; **jnana and karma margas too hard** for ordinary people.
[2:6] - **Common feature of all Bhakti saints:** they **composed in the language of the people** (vernacular) (State PSC) – not all rejected priests or idols (Saguna saints kept idols).
[2:7] - **Ramanuja's movement in Andhra** (Vishnu devotion + social equality) = the **Bhakti movement** (APPSC 2022) – not Arya Samaj or Lingayatism.
[2:8] - Core beliefs: **monotheism**, **guru** essential, **prapatti** (self-surrender); devotion above birth and status.
[2:9] - Modern scholars trace its roots to the **Alvars and Nayanars**, not to Islam; Islam shaped later egalitarian ideas.
[2:10] > Gita = jnana + karma + bhakti; Bhagavata = 9 forms; vernacular = the common feature.
[2:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 4 p.34-38; PMF Art & Culture Part 5 p.115, p.118; old app notes row 142

  [3:0.1] Karaikkal Ammaiyar | Tamil | Nayanar

  [3:0.3] Mirabai | Rajasthan | Krishna; guru Ravidas (tradition)
  [3:0.4] Daya Bai | Rajasthan / Delhi | Disciple of Saint Charandas (Charandasi Sampradaya); Dayabodh, Vinay Malika
  [3:0.5] Sahajobai | Rajasthan / Delhi | Disciple of Charandas; Sahaj Prakash

[3:1] - **Daya Bai = disciple of Saint Charandas** (RAS 2018) – not Nimbarka, Raidas or Ramcharan.
[3:2] - **Buddha and Mirabai – common view:** both held that **the world is full of sorrow** and turned away from worldly life (RAS 1992 key).
[3:3] TABLE head: Guru | Disciple(s)
  [3:3.0] Ramananda | Kabir (weaver), Raidas (cobbler), Dhanna (Jat), Sena (barber), Pipa (Rajput), Padmavati
  [3:3.1] Ravidas | Mirabai
  [3:3.2] Vallabhacharya | Surdas
  [3:3.3] Charandas | Daya Bai, Sahajobai
[3:4] - **Ramananda** – **first medieval reformer to denounce caste outright**; the movement weakened caste and brought **Hindus and Muslims** closer, but did not end caste.
[3:5] - **Palnati Brahmanayudu** (Andhra) – **Chapakudu**, common dining for all castes.
[3:6] > Charandas → Daya Bai; Ravidas → Mirabai; Vallabha → Surdas; Ramananda → Kabir.

## SUB 5 | Virashaiva Movement and the Vachanas
[5:0] - **Basavanna (Basaveshwara)** (12th c., born **Bagewadi**) led the **Virashaiva / Lingayat** movement in **Karnataka**; treasurer at **Kalyana** under Kalachuri **Bijjala**.
[5:1] - **Anubhava Mantapa** – assembly of sharanas, presided by **Allama Prabhu**; **Akkamahadevi** a leading woman sharana.
[5:2] - **Vachanas** – short **Kannada** sayings, signed **Kudalasangamadeva**.
[5:3] - Rejected caste, temple ritual and idol worship.
[5:4] - **Andhra:** Veerashaivism came through **Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya** and **Palkuriki Somanatha** (**Basava Puranam**, Telugu).
[5:5] > Basavanna – Kalyana – Bijjala – Kannada vachanas – Kudalasangama.
[5:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Art & Culture Part 5 p.125; old app notes row 142
## SUB 6 | Nathpanthis, Siddhas and Yogis
[6:0] - Popularised devotional religion in **Northern India**; led by **Matsyendranath** and **Gorakhnath**.
[6:1] - Practised **hatha yoga**, meditation and **renunciation**; rejected ritual and caste; open to "low" castes.
[6:2] - **Charyapada** – songs of the Buddhist **Siddhacharyas** (earliest Bangla poems); manuscript found by **Haraprasad Shastri** in the Nepal royal library (1907).
