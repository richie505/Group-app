## SUB 1 | Receipts, Expenditure and Deficits – Classification
[1:0] # Receipts
[1:1] TABLE head: Basis | Revenue receipts | Capital receipts
  [1:1.0] Liability | Create no liability | Create a liability
  [1:1.1] Assets | Do not reduce assets | Or reduce assets
  [1:1.2] Examples | Tax: income, corporation tax, GST, customs, excise; non-tax: interest receipts, dividends and profits, fees, fines, grants | Borrowings (market loans, T-Bills, World Bank loans), small savings/NSC, recovery of loans, disinvestment
[1:2] - **Order of classification:** **Revenue receipts → Tax revenue → Non-tax revenue → Capital receipts** (APPSC: A → C → D → B; the question was later deleted).
[1:3] - **UPSC 2025:** capital receipts create a liability or reduce assets (I ✓); borrowings and disinvestment are capital receipts (II ✓); **interest received** is **non-tax revenue**, it creates no liability (III ✗) → **I and II only**.
[1:4] - **Disinvestment** = capital receipt (reduces assets), **not** a revenue receipt (CDS 2017).
[1:5] - **Small savings** – not in the revenue account; debt receipts in the capital account (RAS 2018).
[1:6] - **Current (revenue) account income:** **corporation tax, profits of public enterprises, excise duties**; NSC sales and World Bank loans are capital (UPPCS 1998).
[1:7] - **Non-tax revenue of the Centre:** mainly interest, dividends and profits, fees; among mines, forests, opium and roads, the biggest head is **Roads and Bridges** (national-highway fees: ₹3,681 crore in 2012-13 BE, against forestry ₹23 crore, non-ferrous mining ₹27.5 crore); mines and forests yield mainly to the **States**.
[1:8] - **Paper taxes:** wealth tax, gift tax, estate duty – never large, now abolished.
[1:9] # Expenditure
[1:10] TABLE head: Pair | Distinction
  [1:10.0] Revenue vs capital | Revenue creates no asset (salaries, pensions, interest, subsidies, grants); capital creates assets or cuts liabilities (capital works, loans given, debt repayment)
  [1:10.1] Plan vs non-plan (to 2016-17) | Non-plan = interest, salaries, pensions, defence, subsidies, public administration (WBCS 2018)
  [1:10.2] Developmental vs non-developmental | Development = economic and social services (railways, power, S&T, education, health, grants to States); defence = non-development
[1:11] - **Interest payments** = **largest non-plan item (2015-16)** and the **most important revenue-account expenditure** (UPPCS 2008, 2015); still the largest single item of spending.
[1:12] - **Plan/non-plan ended 2017-18** (revenue/capital only), not 2010-11.
[1:13] - **Capital receipts < revenue receipts** – in 2010-11 and now (2026-27 BE: revenue ₹35.33 lakh crore, capital ₹18.14 lakh crore).
[1:14] # Deficits
[1:15] - **Budget (budgetary) deficit** = **total expenditure − total receipts** (all receipts, revenue + capital); discontinued from **1996-97**.
[1:16] - **Fiscal deficit** = total expenditure − receipts excluding borrowings; **primary deficit** = FD − interest; **revenue deficit** = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts.
[1:17] - Worked: FD ₹80,000 crore − interest ₹25,000 crore = **PD ₹55,000 crore**.
[1:18] - **Golden rule:** borrow only to invest, not to finance revenue spending.
[1:19] > Trap: "Excess of all expenditure over revenue receipts" is not the budget deficit – the budget deficit nets **all** receipts.

## SUB 2 | Budget Basics – Constitution, Ministry of Finance and Funds
[2:0] - **Budget** comes from the old **French "bougette"** = little bag; the Constitution does **not** use the word "budget".
[2:1] - **Article 112: Annual Financial Statement** – laid before **both Houses** by the President; the main instrument of **fiscal policy** (allocation, redistribution, stabilisation); primary purpose = **planning the allocation of resources**.
[2:2] - **Prepared and presented by** the **Budget Division, Department of Economic Affairs (DEA), Ministry of Finance** (UPSC 2010; APPSC 2018; APSLPRB 2023), with NITI Aayog and spending ministries; DEA also prepares budgets of States under President's Rule and UTs.
[2:3] - **Revenue estimates:** the **Ministry of Finance** (Department of Revenue with **CBDT and CBIC**) (Jharkhand PCS 2013).
[2:4] - **Departments of the Ministry of Finance:** Economic Affairs, Expenditure, Revenue, Financial Services, DIPAM, Public Enterprises. **Not departments:** **"Banking Division"**, **"Department of Banking Supervision"** (an RBI department).
[2:5] - **Financial year:** 1 April – 31 March; **Madhya Pradesh** decided in 2017 to move to **January–December**.
[2:6] # Articles and funds
[2:7] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [2:7.0] 110(1)(a) | Finance Bill (tax provisions) as Money Bill
  [2:7.1] 112 / 112(3) | Annual Financial Statement / list of charged expenditure
  [2:7.2] 113 | Demands for Grants – only Lok Sabha votes
  [2:7.3] 114 / 114(3) | Appropriation Bill / no withdrawal from CFI without an Appropriation Act
  [2:7.4] 115 | Supplementary, additional or excess grants
  [2:7.5] 116 | Vote on account, vote of credit, exceptional grants
  [2:7.6] 266(1) / 266(2) | Consolidated Fund of India / Public Account
  [2:7.7] 267 | Contingency Fund – corpus ₹30,000 crore from FY 2021-22
[2:8] - **Consolidated Fund of India:** **all revenues**, loans raised and loan repayments received are credited to it; no withdrawal without a parliamentary law (UPSC 2011).
[2:9] - **Public Account:** provident funds, small savings, deposits – government acts as **banker**; withdrawals need **no appropriation**.

## SUB 4 | Types of Budgeting – Zero-based, Performance, Gender
[4:0] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [4:0.0] Zero-based (ZBB) | Every item justified afresh from zero each period; fresh evaluation of every item of expenditure; looks to future needs, not past spending
  [4:0.1] Performance | Links spending to activities and work done; from the USA – first Hoover Commission (1949); India late 1960s (ARC)
  [4:0.2] Outcome | Links outlays to outputs and outcomes; India 2005-06
  [4:0.3] Gender | Gender-sensitive allocation and assessment; not a separate budget for women
  [4:0.4] Balanced / surplus / deficit | Receipts = / > / < expenditure
[4:1] - **ZBB:** proposed by **Peter Pyhrr**; first adopted in the **USA**; first used in government by **Jimmy Carter** (Georgia, 1973 budget; US budget 1979).
[4:2] - **ZBB in India:** first in the **Department of Science and Technology (1983)**; adopted by the Government of India for all ministries from **1986-87** (the usual exam answer).
[4:3] - **Gender budgeting:** started with **Union Budget 2005-06** (Gender Budget Statement; Ramesh Singh dates it to 2006-07, ₹28,737 crore and gender-budget cells in 32 ministries). Components: aligning gender-equality policy with macro policy, gender assessment of outcomes, better targeting – a **separate budget for women is not** one.
[4:4] - **Gender Budget Statement = Statement 13**; 2026-27: **₹5.01 lakh crore (9.37%)** – highest ever. Part A 100% for women, Part B 30-99%, Part C below 30%.
[4:5] - **Child budget:** **Madhya Pradesh** presented one for the first time with its 2022-23 budget.
[4:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.5-6 (ZBB Phyrr, Carter; gender, outcome budget 2005); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.26 (gender budgeting 2005-06, not a separate budget for women); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.24-26 (ZBB, gender budgeting 2006-07); Rocket Indian Economy p.5 (ZBB first adopted USA); app notes 3:107 sub 2
## SUB 5 | Budget in Parliament – Stages, Motions and Grants
[5:0] TABLE head: Stage | Key facts
  [5:0.0] 1. Presentation | FM in Lok Sabha, on 1 February; President's recommendation; no discussion that day
  [5:0.1] 2. General discussion | Both Houses, 3-4 days; no cut motion, no vote
  [5:0.2] 3. Committee scrutiny | Departmental standing committees
  [5:0.3] 4. Voting on demands | Only Lok Sabha; cut motions here; guillotine on the last day
  [5:0.4] 5. Appropriation Bill | Art. 114
  [5:0.5] 6. Finance Bill | Money Bill; enacted within 75 days (Provisional Collection of Taxes Act, 1931)
[5:1] # Motions and special grants
[5:2] TABLE head: Motion / term | Meaning
  [5:2.0] Policy cut | Demand reduced to ₹1 – disapproval of policy
  [5:2.1] Economy cut | Reduced by a specified amount
  [5:2.2] Token cut | Reduced by ₹100 – a specific grievance
  [5:2.3] Guillotine | Speaker puts all outstanding demands to vote without further discussion
  [5:2.4] Vote on account (Art. 116) | Grant in advance for part of the year pending the budget; normally two months (one-sixth); 3-5 months in election years
  [5:2.5] Interim budget | Full statement of receipts and expenditure by the government in an election year
[5:3] - **Cut motion** is the motion linked with the Budget (not censure, call-attention or adjournment motions).
[5:4] - **Vote on account vs interim budget (UPSC 2011):** vote on account deals **only with expenditure**; interim budget has **both receipts and expenditure** (II ✓); both are used by a regular government in an election year, not a "caretaker" device (I ✗).
[5:5] - **Charged expenditure (Art. 112(3)):** discussable, not voted – President's emoluments, presiding officers' salaries, **SC judges' salaries and pensions**, **HC judges' pensions only**, CAG, UPSC, **debt charges**, court decrees.
[5:6] - **Public Accounts Committee** = Parliament's control over the **audit** of the Budget (examines CAG reports); approves excess grants first.
[5:7] - **Budget documents:** Budget Speech, AFS, Demands for Grants, Finance Bill + Memorandum, FRBM statements (MEFS, FPSS, MTFPS), Expenditure Budget, Receipt Budget, Expenditure Profile, **Outcome Budget**, Budget at a Glance.
[5:8] - **Process:** Budget circular in **September**; **Halwa ceremony** marks printing at North Block.
