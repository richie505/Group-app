## SUB 1 | CHANDRAYAAN-1 (2008)
[41:1:0] - **Orbiter + Moon Impact Probe (MIP)**; **1,380 kg**; **11 instruments** from India, USA, UK, Germany, Sweden, Bulgaria.
[41:1:1] - NASA's **M3 (Moon Mineralogy Mapper)** → water/hydroxyl; **confirmed water molecules** on the surface.
[41:1:2] - Other payloads: TMC, HySI, LLRI, HEX, **CIXS**, SIR-2, SARA, **Mini-SAR**, RADOM. (**Lyman Alpha Photometer** was a Mangalyaan payload.)
[41:1:3] - **MIP** hit the south polar region on **14 Nov 2008** → **Jawahar Point**.
[41:1:4] - India **4th** to place its flag on the Moon via impact probe, after **USSR, USA, Japan** (**Japan** landed an impact probe before India).
[41:1:5] - 3,400+ orbits; contact lost **29 Aug 2009** after 312 days.

  [41:1:7.2] LCROSS | NASA, 2009 | Hit a polar crater to find water
  [41:1:7.3] SOFIA | NASA, Oct 2020 | Water on the Moon's sunlit surface
## SUB 2 | CHANDRAYAAN-2 (2019)
[41:2:0] - **Orbiter + lander Vikram + rover Pragyan**; aimed at the **south polar region** (surface, minerals, water ice) – **not the North Pole**.
[41:2:1] - Approved **2008** (Union Cabinet, PM Manmohan Singh).
[41:2:2] - **Originally with Russia:** Roscosmos was to supply the lander but withdrew after 2013, so ISRO built **Vikram** itself.
[41:2:3] - **Vikram crash-landed (Sep 2019)** → **Tiranga Point**; the **orbiter still works**, giving the highest-resolution lunar images.
## SUB 3 | CHANDRAYAAN-3 (2023)
[41:3:0] # Mission
[41:3:1] - **Propulsion Module + lander Vikram + rover Pragyan** (names kept from Ch-2; APPSC 2022).
[41:3:2] - Follow-on to Ch-2: shows end-to-end **safe landing and roving**.
[41:3:3] - Lunar orbit **5 Aug 2023**; Propulsion Module took the lander to **100 km** orbit.
[41:3:4] - Mass **3,900 kg** (lander 1,752 kg incl. **26 kg** rover); life **one lunar day (~14 Earth days)**.
[41:3:5] - Soft-landing limits: vertical ≤ 2 m/s, horizontal ≤ 0.5 m/s, slope ≤ 12°.
[41:3:6] # Payloads → function
[41:3:7] TABLE head: Payload | On | Function
  [41:3:7.0] ChaSTE | Lander | Surface thermal profile
  [41:3:7.1] ILSA | Lander | Moonquakes
  [41:3:7.2] LRA (NASA) | Lander | Passive laser retroreflector
  [41:3:7.3] APXS (Alpha Particle X-ray Spectrometer) | Rover | Elements (Mg, Al, Si, K, Ca, Ti, Fe)
  [41:3:7.4] LIBS | Rover | Elements
  [41:3:7.5] SHAPE | Propulsion Module | Studies Earth from lunar orbit
[41:3:8] # Landing site, rank, findings
[41:3:9] - **23 Aug 2023**, **Shiv Shakti Point** (~600 km from the South Pole, between craters Manzinus C and Simpelius N) → **National Space Day, 23 August**.
[41:3:10] - India **4th** to soft-land (after **US, Russia, China**) and **1st near the south pole**; Japan's **SLIM** later made Japan 5th.
[41:3:11] - Pragyan **confirmed sulphur** (Aug 2023; APPSC 2023); LIBS also found **Al, Ca, Fe, Cr, Ti, Mn, Si, O**.
[41:3:12] - APXS: soil rich in **iron, magnesium**, poor in aluminium; like meteorite ALHA 81005.
## SUB 4 | Mangalyaan – Mars Orbiter Mission
[41:4:0] - India's **first interplanetary mission**; PSLV-C25, 5 Nov 2013; used a **Hohmann transfer**.
[41:4:1] - Mars orbit **24 Sep 2014** (highly elliptical).
[41:4:2] - **First country to reach Mars orbit on its maiden attempt**; **first Asian nation**; ISRO **4th agency** after **NASA, Roscosmos, ESA** (not CNSA); lowest-cost Mars mission.
[41:4:3] - **5 payloads**: **Methane Sensor for Mars**, **Lyman Alpha Photometer**, **MENCA**, **Mars Colour Camera**, **TIS**.
[41:4:4] - Designed for **6 months** → worked about **8 years**; contact lost **2022**.
[41:4:5] - Methane in Mars' air was first measured by **ESA's Mars Express**; also by NASA's **Curiosity**.
[41:4:6] - Mars: planet that may suit life. **Spirit (MER-A)**: first US rover on Mars, 4 Jan 2004 (twin Opportunity).
## SUB 5 | ADITYA-L1 (2023)
[41:5:0] - India's **first space-based solar observatory / first solar mission**; launched **2 Sep 2023**, PSLV-C57.
[41:5:1] - **Halo orbit** around **Sun-Earth L1**, ~**1.5 million km**; inserted **6 Jan 2024**; continuous view, no eclipse.
[41:5:2] APPSC 2022: "launched 25 January 2024" is WRONG.
[41:5:3] # Seven payloads (4 remote sensing + 3 in-situ)
[41:5:4] TABLE head: Type | Payload | Studies
  [41:5:4.0] Remote | VELC (Visible Emission Line Coronagraph) | Corona; by IIA
  [41:5:4.1] Remote | SUIT (Solar Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope) | Photosphere, chromosphere (near-UV); by IUCAA
  [41:5:4.2] Remote | SoLEXS | Soft X-ray
  [41:5:4.3] Remote | HEL1OS | Hard X-ray
  [41:5:4.4] In-situ | ASPEX | Solar-wind protons, ions
  [41:5:4.5] In-situ | PAPA | Solar-wind electrons, ions
  [41:5:4.6] In-situ | Magnetometer | Magnetic field
[41:5:5] - 2025 solar maximum: VELC made the first visible-light spectroscopic observations of **CMEs**; **27 TB** data released.
[41:5:6] - **Photosphere** = the Sun's bright "surface" emitting most visible light.
[41:5:7] - Earth's **magnetic field** turns charged particles to the poles → **aurora**.
## SUB 6 | Astrosat and Xposat
[41:6:0] TABLE head: Feature | AstroSat (2015) | XPoSat (2024)
  [41:6:0.0] FIRST | India's first multi-wavelength space observatory | India's first polarimetry mission; world's second after NASA's IXPE
  [41:6:0.1] Bands / target | Visible, UV, X-ray | X-ray polarisation of black holes, neutron stars
  [41:6:0.2] Payloads | UVIT, LAXPC, SXT, CZTI, SSM | POLIX (RRI) and XSPECT
  [41:6:0.3] Partner | TIFR, IUCAA, IIA, RRI | Raman Research Institute, Bengaluru
[41:6:1] A PYQ dated XPoSat May 2023; it flew on 1 Jan 2024.
[41:6:2] - **Pulsars** = rotating **neutron stars** (highly dense stars) sending regular pulses; strong magnetic fields.
[41:6:3] - **Cepheids** = stars that brighten and dim periodically (cosmic yardsticks); **nebulae** = dust and gas clouds.

[41:9:1] - **1980 SLV-3/Rohini**: India the **6th** nation to launch a satellite indigenously.
[41:9:2] - Space Shuttle first flight: **Columbia, Apr 1981 (STS-1)**.
[41:9:3] - **First satellite repaired in orbit**: NASA's **Solar Maximum Mission**, by **Challenger** crew, **Apr 1984 (STS-41C)**.
