=== Major Ports of India – State, Coast and Features
# The Major Ports (west coast north to south, then east coast south to north)
| Port | State | Coast | Remember |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deendayal (Kandla)** | **Gujarat** | West | **Tidal** port at the head of the **Gulf of Kutch** (Gandhidham); built in the **1950s** to replace **Karachi** lost at Partition; free trade zone / Asia's first EPZ (1965); handles crude, petroleum, edible oil, foodgrains, salt |
| **Mumbai** | Maharashtra | West | **Largest natural harbour**, on **Salsette Island** (Thane Creek); "**biggest port**" of the books; principal gateway since Suez (1869); largest oil terminal |
| **Jawaharlal Nehru Port (JNPA, Nhava Sheva)** | Maharashtra | West | Navi Mumbai, across Elephanta; commissioned **1989** as a **satellite port to decongest Mumbai**; **largest container port**; first 100% landlord major port |
| **Mormugao** | **Goa** | West | Natural harbour at the entrance of the **Zuari** estuary; premier **iron-ore** exporter |
| **New Mangalore** | **Karnataka** | West | Panambur; exports **Kudremukh iron ore**; only major port of Karnataka |
| **Kochi (Cochin)** | **Kerala** | West | **Natural** harbour at the head of **Vembanad Lake** (Willingdon Island); "**Queen of the Arabian Sea**"; close to the Suez–Colombo route |
| **V.O. Chidambaranar (Tuticorin)** | Tamil Nadu | **East** | **Artificial** deep-sea, all-weather harbour on the Gulf of Mannar, **north of Adam's Bridge**; southernmost major port |
| **Chennai** | Tamil Nadu | East | **Oldest artificial port** (1881); second-largest by traffic; not the deepest |
| **Kamarajar (Ennore)** | Tamil Nadu | East | **12th major port**, 24 km north of Chennai; built to **relieve Chennai** and handle **coal**; **artificial**; **first major port registered as a company** (Companies Act 1956) – the only corporatised major port, not run by a Port Trust Board |
| **Visakhapatnam** | **Andhra Pradesh** | East | **Only major port of AP**; **deepest landlocked and protected** natural harbour; "**Brightest Jewel**"; outer harbour for iron-ore exports |
| **Paradip** | **Odisha** | East | Mouth of the **Mahanadi** (delta); declared major port **18 Apr 1966** – first east-coast major port after Independence; built to **decongest Kolkata and Visakhapatnam**; iron ore to **Japan**; **artificial lagoon-type harbour** with two breakwaters; deep-water |
| **Kolkata–Haldia** | **West Bengal** | East | Kolkata = oldest major port and **only riverine port** (Hooghly, needs constant dredging); **Haldia** = its **out-port / decongestion port** at the Haldi–Hooghly confluence |
- **13th major port:** **Vadhavan** (Palghar, Maharashtra), SPV **JNPA 74% : Maharashtra Maritime Board 26%**; old books named **Port Blair** (2010) as the 13th.
- **Decongestion pairs:** **JNPT → Mumbai**; **Ennore → Chennai**; **Haldia → Kolkata**; **Paradip → Kolkata and Visakhapatnam**; Kandla → Mumbai (and lost Karachi).
- **Not on the east coast:** **Kandla**, Nhava Sheva, Mormugao, New Mangalore, Kochi. **West coast:** Nhava Sheva (not Visakhapatnam, Chennai, Tuticorin).
- **North → south across both coasts:** **Kandla (23°N) – Haldia (22°N) – Paradip (20°N) – Kochi (10°N)**.
- **Wrong state traps:** Haldia is in West Bengal (not Odisha), Paradip in Odisha (not WB/AP), Marmugao in Goa (not Karnataka), **Mundra in Gujarat** (not AP), Kakinada in AP (non-major).
- **Western coast towns:** **Janjira** (Raigad, Maharashtra), **Udupi** (Karnataka); Auroville (Puducherry) and Tuticorin are on the east.
> Most-asked: state of a port (Paradip, Haldia, Kandla, Marmugao ×8), decongestion ports (JNPT ×3, Paradip, Haldia out-port), Ennore company/12th port (×3), UPSC 2023 pairs (×2), Queen of the Arabian Sea (×2).

| Type / record | Ports | Trap |

- **Kolkata** handled iron ore, leather, textiles; **Visakhapatnam** – manganese ore, iron ore, coking coal.

=== Coastline, Major and Non-major Ports, LNG Terminals, Sagarmala
- **Coastline (official, revised):** **11,098.81 km** incl. islands (PIB 4 Dec 2025; Ports Ministry circular 29 Apr 2025) – old figure **7,516.6 / 7,517 km**. **Gujarat 2,340.62 km (longest)**, Tamil Nadu 1,068.69 km (2nd), **Andhra Pradesh 1,053.07 km (3rd)**.
- **Major ports** – Union List, **Major Port Authorities Act 2021** (earlier Major Port Trusts Act 1963); **12 operational + Vadhavan = 13**. **~200 non-major ports** – Concurrent List, **State Maritime Boards**.
- **Non-major examples:** **Dahej** (Gulf of Khambhat, Bharuch), **Mundra** (largest private port, Gujarat), **Quilon (Kollam)**, **Kakinada**, Krishnapatnam, Gangavaram, Dhamra, **Sikka** (liquid bulk leader); **Vizhinjam** (Kerala) – first deep-water transshipment port.
- **Ports carry ~95% of trade by volume, ~68% by value**; coastal shipping up 118% in a decade.
- **Gujarat port towns:** Okha, **Porbandar**, **Veraval**, Bedi, Bhavnagar, Dahej, Jafarabad, Mandvi, Navlakhi, Alang, Surat; **Jamnagar** is treated as a refinery city, not a port town (its ports are Bedi and Sikka).
| LNG terminal | State |
|---|---|
| **Dahej**, **Hazira**, Mundra | Gujarat |
| **Kochi** | Kerala |
| Dabhol | Maharashtra |
| Ennore | Tamil Nadu |
| **Dhamra** | **Odisha** |
- **No LNG terminal:** **Kandla** (UPPCS 2005).
- **West coast** – narrow, submerged and indented, deep water near the shore, estuaries → natural harbours. **East coast** – broad deltaic plain, **shallow shelf**, cyclones, **more sediment** → fewer ports.
- **Sagarmala (2015, MoPSW):** **port-led development**; pillars include port modernisation, connectivity, **port-led industrialisation**, coastal community development.
> Asked: coastline (DAO), non-major Dahej (UPSC 2009), Quilon not a major port (APPSC G2), no LNG terminal (Kandla), Jamnagar not a port town.

=== Sea Routes, Canals and Chokepoints
| Route / waterway | Connects | Fact asked |
|---|---|---|
| **North Atlantic ("Big Trunk")** | NE USA – NW Europe | **Busiest sea route** (one-fourth of world trade) |
| Mediterranean–Indian Ocean (Suez) | Europe – Asia | Shortest Europe–Asia route; serves most countries |
| Cape of Good Hope | Europe – Asia round southern Africa | Longer alternative when Suez is blocked |
| **Suez Canal** (Egypt, 1869) | **Mediterranean – Red Sea** (Port Said – Suez) | **193.30 km**, sea-level, **no locks**; cut India–Europe distance by **~7,000 km**; **greatest economic and strategic significance**; largest navigation canal of the books |
| **Panama Canal** (1914) | Atlantic – Pacific | **Lock system** (differs from Suez); shortens New York–San Francisco by 13,000 km |
| **SUMED pipeline** | **Ain Sukhna (Gulf of Suez, Red Sea) – Sidi Kerir (Mediterranean)** | Strategic route for Gulf oil and gas to Europe (both statements true, II explains I – UPSC 2024) |
| **Malacca Strait** | Andaman Sea – South China Sea; **Malay Peninsula – Sumatra** | **Singapore** at its southern end; busiest strait; ~1/3 of trade |
| **Hormuz** | **Persian Gulf – Gulf of Oman** | Most critical oil chokepoint (~20% of oil); closed early March 2026 |
| Bab-el-Mandeb | Red Sea – Gulf of Aden | Houthis took **Perim Island** (Sept 2026) |
| **Lombok Strait** | **Bali – Lombok**; Java Sea – Indian Ocean | Alternative to Malacca (with Sunda) |
| **Bering Strait** | **Arctic – Pacific** | Not Torres, Dover or Malacca |
| Turkish Straits | Black Sea – Mediterranean | **Bosphorus + Dardanelles** |
- **Air route Delhi → Tokyo:** **Bangkok – Hanoi – Hong Kong – Taipei** (west to east).
- **World Maritime Day 2017 theme:** "**Connecting Ships, Ports and People**".
> Most-asked: Suez (×8 – seas joined, 7,000 km, significance, A-R), SUMED (UPSC 2024 ×2), Malacca/Lombok/Hormuz/Bering (×4), Panama locks, busiest route.

- **Shanghai** – world's busiest container port (Shanghai, Singapore, Ningbo in 2024); in 2004 it was the **second biggest** after Singapore.
- **Singapore** – global transshipment / entrepot hub; **Rotterdam** – Europe's largest port (Rhine mouth, Netherlands).

| **Montevideo** | Uruguay |
| **Jakarta** | Indonesia |
| Ningbo | China |

- **London** – largest metal trading centre (London Metal Exchange).
- **Brent crude** – benchmark classification of crude, sourced from the **North Sea**; light and low-sulphur but **not sulphur-free**.

=== India's Trade – Composition, Commodities and Partners
| Exports | Imports |
|---|---|
| **Petroleum products** top (14.48%, 2024-25); engineering goods; electronics **3rd** in FY25 (>$40 bn) | **Crude petroleum** – largest import |
| Pharma ($31.11 bn), gems and jewellery, rice ($11.53 bn FY26) | **Gold, silver** (drove FY26 rise), coal, fertilisers |
| Manufactured goods, **handicrafts, textiles, gems** | **Vegetable (edible) oils** – largest **farm** import (11.6 mt 2013-14 → 16.5 mt 2022-23) |
- **Not an export:** **milk and milk products**; **iron ore** is exported, not imported.
- **Import dependence:** crude **88-89%**, gas **47-51%**, **coal ~26%**. **Bulk imports:** petroleum, cereals, pulses, **edible oils**, fertiliser; non-bulk: capital goods, plastics, chemicals.
- **Gold:** **Switzerland** = largest exporter of gold to India (35.69% of gold imports 2022-23) and a leading world gold exporter by value (refining hub) – but it does **not** hold the second-largest gold reserves (USA, then Germany).
- **Crude suppliers:** Saudi Arabia earlier → **Iraq** top in **2017-18** (and 2022-23) → **Russia** top in 2025. West Asia supplies ~**90% of LPG**.
- **Top foreign-exchange earner (UPPCS 2003):** **iron and steel** among tea, jute, iron and steel, sugar.
- **Wheat import 1998:** agreement with **Australia** for 15 lakh tonnes.
- **Vegan export (Sept 2022):** first plant-based meat consignment from **Nadiad (Kheda), Gujarat** to California (APEDA).
- **Destinations 2024-25:** **USA 19.77%**, UAE 8.37%, UK, China; UAE share fell 8.5 → 6.5%. **Import sources:** **China, UAE, Russia, USA, Iraq**. Post-1947 shift: UK/W. Europe → **USA** → Asia. China-border trade (**Lipulekh, Shipki La, Nathu La**) suspended since 2020.

|---|---|

| Sugar | **Brazil** largest exporter; India largest producer in 2021-22 |
| Spices | India top producer, consumer and exporter |
| Jute | India largest producer (Bangladesh leads raw-jute exports) |
| Palm oil | **Indonesia**, then Malaysia |
