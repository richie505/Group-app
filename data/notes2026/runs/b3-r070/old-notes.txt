## SUB 3 | Biofuels

[70:3:1] - Ethanol feedstocks: sugarcane juice, sugar beet, sweet sorghum, **damaged food grains**, rotten potatoes, maize, cassava.

## SUB 5 | Coal Sector
[70:5:0] - **Nationalisation**: coking coal mines 1971-72; non-coking under **Coal Mines (Nationalisation) Act, 1973**.
[70:5:1] - **Coal Mines (Special Provisions) Act 2015**: after the Supreme Court cancelled coal block allocations; allocation by **auction**, run by the **Nominated Authority**.
[70:5:2] - **Coal Controller's Organisation** (Ministry of Coal; HQ **Kolkata**): checks **coal quality and grading**, settles quality complaints, collects coal statistics.
[70:5:3] - **Energy Statistics India** - published by **NSO, MoSPI** (2026 edition, 30 March 2026); coal supplies about 79% of domestic energy.
[70:5:4] - **Washed coal:** cuts ash and raises plant efficiency, but little used – washing cost raises variable cost and hurts **merit-order** dispatch ranking.
[70:5:5] - **Why India imports coal:** domestic supply of **high-quality, low-ash coking coal** (for steel) is limited; power plants **designed on imported coal** and high-grade coal for **blending** cannot use domestic coal. Coal imports are under **Open General Licence (OGL)** (PIB, Dec 2021).

## SUB 8 | Energy Security
[70:8:0] - Imports: about **85% of petroleum** (over 80%), about **50% of gas**.
[70:8:1] - **Strategic Petroleum Reserves**: **Visakhapatnam, Mangaluru, Padur** - about **9.5 days** cover vs IEA norm **90 days**.
[70:8:2] - Price decontrol: **petrol 26 June 2010**; **diesel 19 October 2014**.
[70:8:3] - **Power imports:** India imports hydropower from **Bhutan** (Chukha, Tala, Kurichhu, Mangdechhu); also trades power with Nepal and Bangladesh.
[70:8:4] - **HELP (2016):** replaced NELP; **revenue-sharing** contracts (not production sharing); a **single licence** for all hydrocarbons.

[71:0:2] - Growth broke the **Hindu rate**, touched **5.5%** - but it was **debt-fuelled**.

## SUB 6 | New Industrial Policy 1991 and National Renewal Fund
[71:6:0] # Industrial Policy (24 July 1991)
[71:6:1] - **Industrial licensing abolished** for all but a few industries (hazardous and strategic, e.g. hazardous chemicals, aerospace); now only **5-6** industries need a licence.
[71:6:2] - Delicensed firms: **pricing freedom**, no expansion curbs.
[71:6:3] - **Public-sector reserved industries cut from 17 (1956 policy) to 8**; later pruned further.
[71:6:4] - **MRTP**: **asset limits, pre-entry restrictions and merger/expansion controls abolished**. (SEBI's status was a separate reform.)
[71:6:5] - **FDI**: automatic approval up to **51%** in high-priority industries (now 100% in most sectors); NRIs/OCBs up to 100%; FIIs allowed into stock markets.
[71:6:6] - **BIFR**: set up to revive **sick industrial units**.
[71:6:7] # National Renewal Fund (February 1992)
[71:6:8] - A **safety net** for workers hit by industrial restructuring under the new policy.
[71:6:9] - Objectives: **retraining and redeployment** of employees affected by modernisation and restructuring; **compensation** of affected employees; counselling.
[71:6:10] - In practice mostly paid for **Voluntary Retirement Schemes (VRS)** in central PSUs; later limited to public sector units only.
