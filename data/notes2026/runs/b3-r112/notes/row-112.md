=== Resource Gap and Deficits of the Bifurcation Year (2014-15)
# Dates
- **Appointed day = 2 June 2014** (both AP and Telangana came into being); the Act got presidential **assent on 1 March 2014** – not "2 March" or "1 April".
# Resource gap
- **Resource gap** = **revenue deficit** (revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts) for the **first ten months** (2 June 2014 – 31 March 2015).
- Method difference: the **Centre** used standardised accounting and **excluded new State schemes** (e.g. farm-loan waiver); **AP** counted its **actual expenditure**.
| Estimate by | Resource gap 2014-15 |
|---|---|
| Union Finance Ministry | ₹4,118 crore |
| **CAG** (2 Jun 2014 – 31 Mar 2015) | **₹13,776 crore** |
| State (AP) | ₹16,078 crore |
- **Central assistance to bridge the 2014-15 gap**: **₹3,979.50 crore**, released in instalments from 2 June 2014 up to **2016-17**.
- Bifurcation-year gap: **more than ₹15,000 crore** on the State's count.
# Fiscal deficit 2014-15
- **CAG**: fiscal deficit **₹31,717 crore = 6.10% of GSDP** in 2014-15 – **slightly more than double the FRBM ceiling of 3%**.
- Before 2014-15 the undivided State ran **revenue surpluses for about eight years** (2006-07 to 2013-14 – printed APPSC key; not checked in the library); bifurcation turned it into a large revenue deficit.
> Exam angle: CAG figures – RD **₹13,776 cr**, FD **6.10%** (≈ double the 3% ceiling); gap grant **₹3,979.50 cr**; appointed day **2 June 2014**.

=== Loss of Hyderabad – Revenue Base and Challenges
- **Hyderabad region (with Rangareddy and Medak)** gave **nearly 50%** of undivided AP's **tax and non-tax revenue**.
- **Srikrishna Committee (2010)**: **Hyderabad and Rangareddy's share of the State's GDP from modern services** rose from **18% (1999-2000) to about 25% (2005-06)**.
- Revenue lost: **corporate taxes, stamp duties, commercial taxes** (not agricultural land revenue).
- **APRA 2014 gives AP no share of Hyderabad's tax revenue** – taxes accrue to the State where the activity is (Telangana); only the 13th FC award was split by population (Sec. 46).
- The bifurcation's **adverse effect on revenue fell on Andhra Pradesh State** (residual AP), not on Telangana.
| Asset lost | Effect |
|---|---|
| HITEC City, Financial District | Loss of IT/ITeS exports |
| Genome Valley pharma cluster; corporate and bank head offices; commercial real estate; MSME supply chains | Narrower tax base (GST, stamp duty, motor-vehicle tax) |
| Loss of Hyderabad as a whole | Structural revenue deficit |
- Residual AP kept its **agricultural base** – that was not lost.
- Division followed the **population ratio 58.32 : 41.68** (AP : Telangana).
- **Schedule IX and X institutions:** Schedule IX = government companies and corporations, divided as recommended by the expert committee headed by **Sheela Bhide**; **Schedule X = 142 institutions** (most in Telangana); AP wants assets shared in the population ratio, Telangana by location.
- **Biggest challenges after 2014 (all of them)**: building **infrastructure for a new capital**, moving the **administration from Hyderabad to Amaravati**, and a **severe financial crisis with scarcity of human resources**.
> Exam angle: Hyderabad ≈ **50%** of revenue; Srikrishna: Hyderabad + Rangareddy ≈ **25%** of modern-services GDP; **no share** of Hyderabad's taxes for AP.

=== Power Sector – Reforms, Discoms and Dues
- **Department of Expenditure** borrowing window (Budget 2021-22): extra borrowing of **0.5% of GSDP** linked to **power-sector reforms**.
- **4 February 2021**: AP became the **second State** to complete the stipulated reforms – **DBT of electricity subsidy to farmers** (Srikakulam pilot from Sep 2020) – and could borrow an extra **₹1,515 crore**.
- **UDAY**: AP signed on **24 June 2016** – the **13th State**; State took over about **₹11,000 crore** of DISCOM debt.
- CAG on power subsidy: spending at **121% (2023)** and **120% (2024)** of budget estimates.
- **Power dues**: under the power-sharing arrangement **Telangana has to pay Andhra Pradesh** for power supplied; on 29 Aug 2022 the Union Power Ministry (Sec. 92, APRA) ordered Telangana to pay about **₹6,756 crore** to APGENCO; the **Telangana High Court quashed** the order (Oct 2023).
> Exam angle: AP = **second** State (not first) in DoE power reforms; dues flow **Telangana → AP**.

=== Demands to the Centre – Special Category Status
- **N. Chandrababu Naidu** demanded **Special Category Status (SCS)** citing post-bifurcation revenue loss.
- SCS for **5 years** was promised on the floor of the **Rajya Sabha (2014)**.
- Under SCS the **Centre bears 90%** of CSS costs (ordinary States 60%) plus tax breaks.
- Centre's refusal: the **14th FC** ended the general/special category distinction and raised devolution **32% → 42%**.
- Chronology: **APRA (2014) → 14th FC award (2015) → TDP leaves NDA over SCS (2018)**.
| Provision | Demand / grant |
|---|---|
| Sec. 94(1) | Tax incentives |
| Sec. 46 | Backward-area packages (Rayalaseema, North Coastal) |
| Sec. 94(3) | Special support for capital buildings (₹2,500 crore released) |
- Other demands: revenue deficit grants; full Polavaram funding at revised cost; capital-city grants.
~~ Sources: AP Reorganisation Act 2014 (apra.txt s.46, 94); old app notes 3:112 sub 2

=== FRBM Breaches, Debt and CAG Remarks
- **Accounts at a Glance 2024-25** (Principal AG; tabled 6 Mar 2026): **RD 3.75% vs FRBM target 2.70%**; **FD 5.05% vs 4% ceiling**; borrowings ₹81,071 crore.
- **Debt (2024-25 RE): ₹5,64,488 crore = 35.15% of GSDP**; 2025-26: ₹6,35,177 crore (36.04%); market loans 75% of debt.
- **Off-budget borrowings** (CAG): ₹1,18,394 crore (Mar 2022) → **₹1.28 lakh crore = 9.7% of GSDP (Mar 2023)**.
- **Guarantees** (2023-24): ₹1,54,797 crore (11% of GSDP).
- 2024-25: 3.75% / 5.05% (Accounts at a Glance) vs 3.79% / 5.11% (Finance Accounts).
