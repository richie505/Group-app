=== NOTES 3:112 | Bifurcation and Ap's State Revenue – Revenue Deficit, Loss of Hyderabad, Resource Gap
## SUB 0 | Loss of Hyderabad Revenues
[112:0:0] - **Hyderabad region (with Rangareddy and Medak)** gave **nearly 50%** of undivided AP's **tax and non-tax revenue**.
[112:0:1] - Revenue lost: **corporate taxes, stamp duties, commercial taxes** (not agricultural land revenue).
[112:0:2] TABLE head: Asset lost | Effect
  [112:0:2.0] HITEC City, Financial District | Loss of IT/ITeS exports
  [112:0:2.1] Genome Valley pharma cluster; corporate and bank head offices; commercial real estate; MSME supply chains | Narrower tax base (GST, stamp duty, motor-vehicle tax)
  [112:0:2.2] Loss of Hyderabad as a whole | Structural revenue deficit
[112:0:3] - Residual AP kept its **agricultural base** – that was not lost.
[112:0:4] - Division followed the **population ratio 58.32 : 41.68** (AP : Telangana).
[112:0:5] - **Schedule IX and X institutions:** Schedule IX = government companies and corporations, divided as recommended by the expert committee headed by **Sheela Bhide**; **Schedule X = 142 institutions** (most in Telangana); AP wants assets shared in the population ratio, Telangana by location.
## SUB 1 | Resource Gap of the First Year
[112:1:0] # Meaning
[112:1:1] - **Appointed day = 2 June 2014**; the Act got presidential **assent on 1 March 2014**.
[112:1:2] - **Resource gap** = **revenue deficit** (revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts) for the **first ten months** (2 June 2014 – 31 March 2015).
[112:1:3] - Method difference: the **Centre** used standardised accounting and **excluded new State schemes** (e.g. farm-loan waiver); **AP** counted its **actual expenditure**.
[112:1:4] The State was divided on 2 June 2014, not "2 March 2014".
[112:1:5] # Competing estimates for 2014-15
[112:1:6] TABLE head: Estimate by | Resource gap 2014-15
  [112:1:6.0] Union Finance Ministry | ₹4,118 crore
  [112:1:6.1] CAG (2 Jun 2014 – 31 Mar 2015) | ₹13,776 crore (APPSC 2016)
  [112:1:6.2] State (AP) | ₹16,078 crore
[112:1:7] - Bifurcation-year gap: **more than ₹15,000 crore** (APPSC 2022 key).
## SUB 2 | Demands to the Centre
[112:2:0] # Special Category Status
[112:2:1] - **N. Chandrababu Naidu** demanded **Special Category Status (SCS)** citing post-bifurcation revenue loss (APPSC 2018).
[112:2:2] - SCS for **5 years** was promised on the floor of the **Rajya Sabha (2014)**.
[112:2:3] - Under SCS the **Centre bears 90%** of CSS costs (ordinary States 60%) plus tax breaks.
[112:2:4] - Centre's refusal: the **14th FC** ended the general/special category distinction and raised devolution **32% → 42%**.
[112:2:5] - Chronology: **APRA (2014) → 14th FC award (2015) → TDP leaves NDA over SCS (2018)**.
[112:2:6] # APRA sections behind the demands
[112:2:7] TABLE head: Provision | Demand / grant
  [112:2:7.0] Sec. 94(1) | Tax holidays
  [112:2:7.1] Sec. 46 | Backward-area packages
  [112:2:7.2] Sec. 94(3) | ₹2,500 crore for capital buildings
[112:2:8] - Other demands: revenue deficit grants; full Polavaram funding at revised cost; capital-city grants.

## SUB 0 | Constitutional and Legal Base
[113:0:0] - **Article 262**: Parliament may make law for adjudicating **inter-State river water disputes**.
[113:0:1] - **Article 262(2)**: Parliament may provide that **neither the Supreme Court nor any other court** shall exercise jurisdiction over such disputes.
[113:0:2] - **Inter-State River Water Disputes (ISRWD) Act, 1956** – enacted under Article 262.
[113:0:3]   - **Section 11**: bars the Supreme Court and other courts from disputes that may be referred to a tribunal.
[113:0:4]   - The Supreme Court held (Cauvery case, 1991) that this bar does **not** cover its appeal jurisdiction under **Article 136**.
[113:0:5]   - **Section 5(1)**: used in **October 2023** to give **KWDT-II further terms of reference**.
[113:0:6] - The **Apex Council** refers disputes not covered by KWDT to a tribunal under the ISRWD Act, 1956.
