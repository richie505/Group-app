## SUB 1 | Simla Deputation, 1906
[205:1:0] - Date: **1 October 1906**; organised and led by **Aga Khan III**; met **Lord Minto** at Simla (Shimla).
[205:1:1] - Demanded **separate electorates** and **weightage** for Muslims, citing their "contribution to the **defence of the Empire**".
[205:1:2] - Also wanted special safeguards for Muslims in **government services**.

## SUB 3 | Indian Councils Act, 1909 (morley-minto Reforms)
[205:3:0] # Name and aim
[205:3:1] - Popular name: **Morley-Minto (Minto-Morley) Reforms**; also called the Government of India Act 1909.
[205:3:2] - Introduced by **John Morley** (Secretary of State) and **Lord Minto** (Viceroy).
[205:3:3] - Aim: **rally (win) the Moderates** and **reward Muslim loyalty** through limited concessions.
[205:3:4] # Provisions
[205:3:5] TABLE head: Area | Provision
  [205:3:5.0] Councils | Central Legislative Council enlarged: additional members up to 60; provincial councils enlarged
  [205:3:5.1] Majority | Official majority kept at the Centre; non-official majority in provinces (but Governor's veto made them powerless)
  [205:3:5.2] Electorates | Separate electorates for Muslims with weightage: Muslim members elected only by Muslim voters
  [205:3:5.3] Election | Indirect, multi-tier elections (not direct universal elections); elected non-official members for the first time
  [205:3:5.4] Budget | Could discuss the budget, ask supplementary questions, move resolutions; could not vote on the budget or question military affairs
  [205:3:5.5] Executive Council | Indian member: S.P. Sinha (Law Member)
[205:3:6] - **1909** = legal beginning of separate (communal) electorates.
[205:3:7] - Critique: the **most short-lived** constitutional experiment; **Ramsay MacDonald**: an incomplete deal between democracy and bureaucracy.
[205:3:8] "Which Act introduced the communal electorate?" → **Indian Councils Act, 1909**.

## SUB 5 | Lucknow Session and Lucknow Pact, 1916
[205:5:0] # Why Lucknow (December 1916) is historic
[205:5:1] - **Reunion of Moderates and Extremists**: Extremists re-entered Congress **after 9 years** (since Surat 1907); Tilak's return.
[205:5:2] - **Lucknow Pact**: Congress–League alliance; League led by its "**Young Party**" (Jinnah, Ali Brothers).
[205:5:3] - Tilak repeated "Swaraj is my birthright, I shall have it" at the Lucknow session.
[205:5:4] - **1916** = high point of Congress-League unity; **1916–1922** = period of agreement.
[205:5:5] # Terms of the Pact
[205:5:6] TABLE head: Clause | Detail
  [205:5:6.0] Goal | Self-government accepted by both
  [205:5:6.1] Separate electorates | Congress accepted Muslim separate electorates (major concession) – the first time (1916)
  [205:5:6.2] Weightage | Muslims in minority provinces got more seats than their population share
  [205:5:6.3] Communal veto | No bill on a community passed if three-fourths of that community's members opposed it
  [205:5:6.4] Others | Separation of executive and judiciary; elected majorities in provincial legislatures
[205:5:7] - Limit: Congress and League still held separate annual sessions.
[205:5:8] - **AP:** **Besant toured Andhra** (Kakinada, Rajahmundry, Eluru) after the Lucknow session.
## SUB 6 | Critique – Father of the Communal Electorate
[205:6:0] - **Lord Minto** = **Father of the Communal Electorate**.
[205:6:1] - Separate electorates divided the electorate on religious lines and fed later separatism.
[205:6:2] - **AP angle (repression under Minto-Morley):** **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao** and **Bodi Narayana Rao** arrested **18 July 1908** for a **Swarajya** article = **first Telugu political prisoners**.
## SUB 7 | Muslim League – Later Sessions and Leadership Shift
[205:7:0] # Sessions
[205:7:1] TABLE head: Session | Place / fact
  [205:7:1.0] First annual session, 1907 | Karachi (not its founding)
  [205:7:1.1] Second session, 1908 | Amritsar
  [205:7:1.2] 1916 | Lucknow, presided by Jinnah
  [205:7:1.3] 1930 | Allahabad: Iqbal proposed a Muslim state in the North-West
  [205:7:1.4] 1940 | Lahore, 22–24 March; presided by Jinnah
[205:7:2] # Leadership shift
[205:7:3] - **1912–24:** League dominated by younger Muslim nationalists; their nationalism took a communal view of politics.
[205:7:4] - **Jinnah** joined in **1913**; later led the movement for **Pakistan**; criticised Gandhi's support of the **Khilafat Movement** and warned him not to encourage fanaticism of Muslim religious leaders.
[205:7:5] - **1937–39:** League alleged "**Hindu Raj**" under Congress ministries.
[205:7:6] # Two-nation milestones
[205:7:7] TABLE head: Year | Milestone
  [205:7:7.0] 1930 | Iqbal – first idea of a separate Muslim nation; Hindus and Muslims two nations
  [205:7:7.1] 1933 | Chaudhry Rahmat Ali coined "PAKISTAN" (pamphlet *Now or Never*)
  [205:7:7.2] 23 March 1940 | Lahore (Pakistan) Resolution – separate independent states for Muslims; put Jinnah's Two Nation Theory into effect
  [205:7:7.3] 23 March 1943 | Pakistan Day celebrated
  [205:7:7.4] 16 August 1946 | Direct Action Day; large riots in Calcutta
[205:7:8] - Lahore Resolution: drafted by **Sikandar Hayat Khan**; presented (moved) by **A.K. Fazlul Haq** (Premier of Bengal); approved by **Khaliquzzaman**; Jinnah presided.
[205:7:9] - **Liaquat Ali Khan** – Pakistan's **first Prime Minister**.
[205:7:10] - Quote: "Nehru is a patriot, while Jinnah is a politician" – attributed to Iqbal (Nehru, *Discovery of India*).
[205:7:11] Muslim state **idea** = **Iqbal (1930)**; the **name** "Pakistan" = **Rahmat Ali (1933)**.

[206:0:0] - Rose after the **Surat Split (1907)** and the failure of mass Swadeshi: individual heroic action (assassinations, "**swadeshi dacoities**") kept radical nationalism alive.
