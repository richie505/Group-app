=== Indian Councils Act, 1909 – Morley-Minto Reforms
# Names and background
- **Names:** **Indian Councils Act, 1909** = **Morley-Minto (Minto-Morley) Reforms**; exam options also call it the "Government of India Act, 1909".
- **Persons:** **John Morley** = Secretary of State for India (Liberal, from end-1905); **Lord Minto II** = Viceroy (1905-10), who succeeded **Curzon** in 1905.
- **Aim:** "rally the Moderates" (carrot and stick) and win Muslim loyalty – the reforms were meant to **divide the nationalist ranks** and check Indian unity.
# Provisions
| Area | Provision |
|---|---|
| Separate electorates | **For the first time**, **separate electorates for Muslims** – Muslim members elected only by Muslim voters = **communal representation**; "legalised communalism" |
| Weightage | Muslims got **more seats than their population share**, and a lower income qualification for voters |
| Elections | **Elective principle** recognised for non-official members – but **indirect, multi-tier** (local bodies → electoral college → provincial councils → central council) |
| Central Council | Additional members raised to **60** (from 16); **official majority kept** (69 members: 37 officials, 32 non-officials, 27 elected) |
| Provincial Councils | 30-50 additional members; **non-official majority** (but partly nominated) |
| Powers | Ask **supplementary questions**, move **resolutions**, vote on separate items of the budget – **not on the budget as a whole**; army spending non-votable |
| Executive Council | One Indian could be appointed: **S.P. (Satyendra Prasanna) Sinha**, **Law Member, 1909** – first Indian on the Viceroy's Executive Council |
- **Before the Act (1907):** Morley put two Indians – K.G. Gupta and Syed Hussain Bilgrami – on the Secretary of State's India Council.
- **Gokhale** went to England to meet Morley for self-government on colonial lines; Moderates co-operated with the reforms and lost popular support.
# Verdict
- **Most short-lived** of Britain's constitutional experiments in India (UPSC 1999) – replaced by the 1919 Act (in force 1921); **Ramsay MacDonald:** an incomplete deal between democracy and bureaucracy.
- Morley: "if this chapter of reforms led ... to a parliamentary system in India, I would have nothing to do with it." The councils stayed **advisory**.
> "Which Act introduced separate / communal electorates (communal representation)?" → **Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley-Minto)** – not 1892, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford) or 1935, which only extended it.

=== Separate Electorates – Divide and Rule, Father of the Communal Electorate
- **Stated reason:** protecting the Muslim minority. **Real reason:** part of the British policy of **divide and rule** – to set Muslims against the Congress and maintain British supremacy.
- **Effects:** checked India's unification, encouraged separatism, became a potent factor in the **growth of communalism** – the "seeds of discord" that ended in partition.
- **Lord Minto** = "**Father of the Communal Electorate**" – used separate electorates to **turn Muslims against the Congress**. Exam key (BPSC 2018): **Rajendra Prasad** called **Lord Minto** the "**Father of Pakistan**" (attribution not traced in the sources).
- **Extension:** 1909 Muslims → **1919** + Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans → **1935** + depressed classes, women, labour.
> Separate electorates = Minto (1909); Congress accepted them = Lucknow (1916).

=== Simla Deputation and Founding of the Muslim League, 1906
# Simla Deputation (1 October 1906)
- Led by **Aga Khan III**; met **Lord Minto** at **Simla (Shimla)**.
- Demanded **separate electorates** for Muslims and representation **above their numbers** (weightage), citing their contribution "**to the defence of the Empire**"; also safeguards in government services.
- **Jinnah**, then a Congressman (secretary to Naoroji at Calcutta, 1906), **opposed separate electorates** in 1906 – the Aga Khan called him the only well-known Muslim to do so.
# Founding facts
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Date | **30 December 1906** |
| Place | **Dacca (Dhaka)**, at the session of the **All-India Muhammadan Educational Conference** |
| Initiative | **Nawab Salimullah of Dacca**, with Mohsin-ul-Mulk and Viqar-ul-Mulk (who chaired the Dacca meeting) |
| First president | **Aga Khan III** (president 1906/08-1913) – not Jinnah, who joined only in **1913** |
| First annual session | **Karachi, 1907** (constitution adopted); 2nd session **Amritsar, 1908** |
| Headquarters | Lucknow |
| London Muslim League | **Syed Ameer Ali**, **6 May 1908** – exam keys call it the League's "London branch" (PMF: strictly an independent body) |
# Character
- **Loyalist, communal, conservative**: preached loyalty to the Empire, kept Muslim intelligentsia **away from the Congress**.
- **Supported the partition of Bengal** and sought safeguards in government services – it did **not** oppose the partition (UPSC 2002 "not correct" statement).
- **Other figures of the 2002 statement question:** **Hakim Ajmal Khan** – a leader of the militant nationalist **Ahrar movement** (with Mohammad Ali, Hasan Imam, Zafar Ali Khan, Mazharul Haq); **Syed Ahmad Khan** opposed the Congress (United Indian Patriotic Association, 1888); **Barkatullah and Obeidullah Sindhi** – Provisional Government of India, **Kabul (1915)**.
- **W.W. Hunter** (*The Indian Musalmans*, 1871) – exam keys credit him with "The Muslims, if contented and satisfied, would become the greatest bulwark of British power in India".
# Chronology and dates
- **INC 1885 → Partition of Bengal 1905 → Swadeshi (7 Aug 1905) → Muslim League (Dec 1906) → Surat split 1907 → Morley-Minto 1909 → Lucknow Pact 1916**.
| Body | Founded |
|---|---|
| Brahmo Samaj | 1828 |
| Indian National Congress | 1885 |
| All India Muslim League | 1906 |
| HSRA | 1928 |
> Founded at **Dacca 1906**; first session **Karachi 1907**; first president **Aga Khan**; London League **Ameer Ali 1908**.

=== Lucknow Session and the Lucknow Pact, 1916
# The Lucknow Congress (December 1916)
- **President:** **Ambika Charan Majumdar** (a Moderate). The League's Lucknow session was presided over by **Jinnah**; **both** bodies met at **Lucknow**.
- **Reunion** of Moderates and Extremists – Tilak's group readmitted after **9 years** (Surat 1907). Chief architects of the reconciliation: **Annie Besant** and **Tilak**; Pherozeshah Mehta (died 1915) had blocked it.
- First Congress session Gandhi attended after returning from South Africa; Champaran peasants' problems were put to him here.
- **Tilak's slogan** "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it" – exam keys (UPPCS) place it at the **Lucknow session, 1916**; strictly it was the slogan of his Home Rule campaign of 1916 (league founded at Poona, April 1916).
# The Pact
- **Brokers:** **Tilak and Jinnah**; Besant also helped; Sarojini Naidu called Jinnah the "Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity".
- **Main concession:** the **Congress accepted separate electorates for Muslims** – the **first time (1916)** the Congress did so; Muslims got a **fixed proportion of seats** (weightage).
- **Joint demands:** self-government at an early date; expanded councils with **elected majorities**; **half** of the executive councils to be Indians; five-year council term; Secretary of State paid from the British treasury.
- **Not in the Pact:** no merger (separate sessions continued), no power-sharing deal, no demand for complete independence.
- **Unity period:** **1916-1922** (Lucknow Pact → Khilafat-Non-Cooperation) = high point of Congress-League unity.
# Chronology
- **Lucknow Pact (Dec 1916) → Jallianwala Bagh (13 Apr 1919) → Tilak's death (1 Aug 1920) → Swaraj Party (1 Jan 1923)**.
> Lucknow 1916: Congress president **A.C. Majumdar**, League president **Jinnah**; Congress **accepted** separate electorates.

=== Acts Compared – 1861 to 1935
| Act | Key feature |
|---|---|
| Indian Councils Act, **1861** | Indians associated with legislative councils (nominated) |
| Indian Councils Act, **1892** | **Discussion of the annual financial statement (budget)**; questions; indirect (nominated-on-recommendation) members |
| Indian Councils Act, **1909** | **Separate Muslim electorates** (communal representation); elective principle; S.P. Sinha |
| GoI Act, **1919** (Montagu-Chelmsford) | **Provincial dyarchy** (reserved and transferred subjects); **bicameralism and direct elections** at the Centre; communal electorates extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans |
| GoI Act, **1935** | **All-India Federation**; **provincial autonomy** (dyarchy in provinces abolished); dyarchy at the Centre; separate electorates for depressed classes, women, labour |
- **Match (APPSC G2):** separate Muslim representation – 1909; dyarchy in provinces – 1919; federation – 1935; discussion of annual statement – 1892.
- **1909 vs 1919:** 1909 = separate Muslim electorates; 1919 = dyarchy. No Act brought dyarchy "at the Centre as well as in the provinces".
