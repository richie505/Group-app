## SUB 0 | Anushilan Samiti and Yugantar – Bengal
[0:0] # Anushilan Samiti
[0:1] - **Founded 1902, Calcutta**, by **Pramathanath (P.) Mitra** with **Satish Chandra Basu**; named after Bankim's *Anushilan Tattva*; associated with Aurobindo, Barindra Ghosh, Bipin Pal.
[0:2] - A **secret revolutionary society** – trained youth for armed struggle; dacoities, bombs, assassinations = **militant nationalism through revolutionary acts** (not social reform, women or labour welfare).
[0:3] - **Barindra Kumar Ghosh** (Aurobindo's younger brother) – his activities built its revolutionary core; head of the secret society in the Alipore case.
[0:4] - **Dhaka Anushilan (1906):** **Pulin Behari Das** – outgrew the Calcutta parent; organised the **Barrah dacoity (1908)** in Dhaka district = the **first major venture of the revolutionaries in East Bengal**, to raise funds.
[0:5] - **Sachindranath Sanyal:** joined Anushilan (1907); exam keys: set up its **Patna branch (1913)**; with Rash Behari Bose in the Hardinge bomb (1912).
[0:6] - **Tripura** was drawn into the movement early in the 20th century because **Bengal revolutionaries took shelter there**.
[0:7] # Yugantar (Jugantar)
[0:8] - **Weekly started April 1906** by an Anushilan inner circle – **Barindra Kumar Ghosh, Bhupendranath Dutta** (and Abinash Bhattacharya).
[0:9] - **Bhupendranath Datta** (Vivekananda's brother) stood before the British as **Yugantar's editor** and was jailed for sedition (1907).
[0:10] - The western Anushilan under **Jatindranath Mukherjee (Bagha Jatin)** emerged as the **Yugantar party**; German Plot (1915); Jatin died at **Balasore, September 1915**.
[0:11] > Anushilan = **P. Mitra 1902**; Dhaka branch = **Pulin Das**; Yugantar paper = **Barindra + Bhupendranath**; Yugantar party = **Bagha Jatin**.
[0:12] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.316-318; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.73-74, 169; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.72, 77; Wikipedia "Bhupendranath Datta"

## SUB 2 | Early Actions – Phadke, Chapekars, Muzaffarpur
[2:0] # Maharashtra
[2:1] - **Vasudev Balwant Phadke (1879):** organised **Ramosi peasants** into a force (the "**Ramosi Krishak Jatha**") – an **armed uprising in the 1870s**; the first revolutionary activity in Maharashtra; crushed.
[2:2] - **Chapekar brothers** (Damodar and Balkrishna, Tilak's disciples) shot **Plague Commissioner W.C. Rand** and Lt. Ayerst, **Poona, 1897**.
[2:3] # Muzaffarpur bomb (April 1908)
[2:4] - **Khudiram Bose and Prafulla Chaki** bombed a carriage at **Muzaffarpur (Bihar)** meant for Judge **Kingsford**; two British ladies died; **Chaki shot himself**, **Khudiram was hanged**.
[2:5] > Phadke = **Ramosis, 1879**; Rand = **Chapekars, 1897**; Kingsford = **Muzaffarpur, 1908**.
[2:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.316, 318; PMF Modern History Part 4 p.71-73
## SUB 3 | Alipore and Delhi Conspiracy Cases
[3:0] # Alipore (Maniktala) conspiracy case, 1908
[3:1] - After Muzaffarpur the whole Anushilan group was arrested – **Aurobindo and Barindra Ghosh**; called the Manicktolla or Muraripukur conspiracy.
[3:2] - **Chittaranjan Das defended Aurobindo** → acquitted; Barindra and Ullaskar Dutt – death, commuted to life.
[3:3] # Delhi conspiracy case – Hardinge bomb, 1912
[3:4] - **23 December 1912:** bomb at **Viceroy Hardinge** in **Chandni Chowk** during his state entry into Delhi – injured, not killed.
[3:5] - **Mastermind:** **Rash Behari Bose** (with **Sachin Sanyal**); bomb thrown by **Basanta Kumar Biswas**; organisation credited: **Anushilan Samiti** (UPPCS 2023) – not Yugantar.
[3:6] - Executed: **Master Amir Chand**, Avadh Behari, Bal Mukund, Basanta Biswas; Rash Behari escaped to **Japan**.

## SUB 4 | Revolutionary Press and Organisations
[4:0] - **Sandhya** and **Yugantar** (Bengal) and **Kal** (Maharashtra) advocated revolutionary action – **all three**.
[4:1] - **Bandi Jivan** ("A Life of Captivity", 1922) – **Sachindranath Sanyal**, written in the Cellular Jail.
[4:2] - **Revolutionary bodies:** **Abhinav Bharat** and **Anushilan Samiti**. Not revolutionary: the **(United) Indian Patriotic Association (1888)** – Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and Raja Shiv Prasad, to oppose the Congress; "New Nationalist Party".
[4:3] TABLE head: Paper | Founder / editor
  [4:3.0] Yugantar (1906) | Barindra Kumar Ghosh, Bhupendranath Dutta
  [4:3.1] Sandhya (1906) | Brahmabandhab Upadhyay
  [4:3.2] Bande Mataram (Calcutta) | Bipin Chandra Pal; edited by Aurobindo
  [4:3.3] Mukti Kon Pathe (1906) | Barindra Kumar Ghosh (manual)
[4:4] - **Bharat Mata Society** = **Ajit Singh, Punjab** – not Bengal.
[4:5] - **Background:** first phase rose after the Surat split and Swadeshi repression; weakness – never a **mass movement**; Aurobindo's pamphlet **Bhawani Mandir (1905)**.
