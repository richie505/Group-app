=== Part III at a Glance – Six Groups, Number, Location
# Where and how many
- **Location:** **Part III, Articles 12–35** of the Constitution; called the **"Magna Carta of India"** (not Part I, II or IV; not Arts. 14–32 only).
- **Number:** originally **seven**, now **six** Fundamental Rights (groups) – the **Right to Property (Art. 31)** was deleted by the **44th Amendment, 1978** (Janata Party government) and is now a **legal/constitutional right under Art. 300A (Part XII)**.
- **Borrowed from:** the **Bill of Rights of the USA**; the **Motilal Nehru Report (1928)** had demanded a bill of rights.
- **Why "fundamental":** they are **guaranteed and specifically protected by the Constitution**, the fundamental law of the land – not rights granted by any ordinary law, nor merely natural-law rights.
# The six groups
| Group | Articles | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Right to Equality | **14–18** | 14 equality before law; **15 no discrimination (religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth)**; 16 public employment; **17 untouchability abolished**; 18 titles abolished |
| Right to Freedom | **19–22** | 19 six freedoms (speech, assembly, **association**, movement, residence, profession); 20 conviction; **21 life and personal liberty**; 21A education; 22 arrest and detention |
| Right against Exploitation | **23–24** | 23 traffic in human beings, forced labour; 24 child labour in factories |
| Right to Freedom of Religion | **25–28** | conscience, religious affairs, taxes, religious instruction |
| Cultural and Educational Rights | **29–30** | minorities' culture; minorities' institutions |
| Right to Constitutional Remedies | **32** alone | writs; Art. 226 (High Courts) is outside Part III |
- **Classification traps:** **Art. 15** (prohibition of discrimination) and **Art. 17** (untouchability) fall under the **Right to Equality** – not Freedom of Religion, Exploitation or Cultural rights (UPSC 1995, 2020; SSC 2018).
- **Wrong pair:** "Right to protection of life – **Art. 20**" is wrong; life and liberty is **Art. 21** (Art. 20 = protection in respect of conviction). Art. 15 discrimination, Art. 19 association and Art. 32 remedies are correct pairs.
- **Description:** "a pledge to our people and a pact with the civilised world" – **S. Radhakrishnan**.
> Asked as: "How many FRs?" (six – APPSC Drug Inspectors, Polytechnic Lecturers; SSC CGL 2017), "Which Part?" (III – APPSC G2 2008; SSC 2008, 2017), "Which Articles?" (12–35; Equality 14–18; Freedom 19–22), CDS 2003 "originally seven, now six – both correct".

=== FR or Not? – Look-alike Rights
# Not Fundamental Rights
| Right | Where it really is |
|---|---|
| **Right to work / employment** | DPSP **Art. 41** (not an FR) |
| **Equal pay for equal work** | DPSP **Art. 39(d)** |
| **Right to property** | **Art. 300A**, legal right since the **44th Amendment (1978)** |
| **Right to vote and to be elected** | **Constitutional right (Art. 326)** – UPSC 2017 official key; given effect by statute (RPA 1950 s.19, **RPA 1951 s.62**); NextGen calls it a statutory right |
| **Right to information** | Not listed in Part III; the Supreme Court reads it into **Art. 19(1)(a)** (RTI Act 2005 is a statute) |
| Right to protest, right to govern, right to citizenship, right of equal shelter | Not FRs (shelter is read into Art. 21 by courts, but no "right of equal shelter" is granted) |
# Look-alikes that ARE Fundamental Rights
- **Education (6–14 years):** **Art. 21A**, inserted by the **86th Amendment, 2002**; the **RTE Act** was enacted in **2009**; both came into force on **1 April 2010**.
- **Right to settle in any part of India** – Art. 19(1)(e); **gender equality** – Arts. 14, 15; **form associations**, **practise any profession** – Art. 19.
- **Right to Equality, Freedom, against Exploitation, Freedom of Religion** – all FR groups (the "odd one out" is always the DPSP or statutory right).
> NDA 2015: of education, work, associations and profession, only the **right to work** is not an FR. Voting age was lowered to **18** by the **61st Amendment (1988)**.

=== Citizens Only vs All Persons; Aliens
# Who gets which right
| Citizens only | All persons (citizens + foreigners) |
|---|---|
| **Art. 15** – no discrimination | **Art. 14** – equality before law, equal protection of laws |
| **Art. 16** – public employment | **Art. 20** – protection in respect of conviction |
| **Art. 19** – six freedoms (speech, movement, **trade and profession**…) | **Art. 21 / 21A** – life and liberty, education |
| **Art. 29** – minorities' language and culture | **Art. 22** – arrest safeguards (**not enemy aliens**) |
| **Art. 30** – minorities' institutions | **Arts. 23–24** – exploitation; **Arts. 25–28** – religion; **Art. 32** – remedies |
- **Five citizens-only Articles: 15, 16, 19, 29, 30** (Raj. PCS 2024; APPSC G1 2023: denied to aliens = Arts. **29–30, 19, 16** – **not 23–24**).
- A **British citizen** (any foreigner) in India cannot claim **freedom of trade and profession** or **freedom of speech and expression**, free movement or residence (all Art. 19) – but gets equality before law, life and liberty, religious freedom (UPSC 1999).
- **Non-citizens** get the **right to life and liberty** and **constitutional remedies** – not freedom of expression or of association (Art. 19).
- **Aliens** are not given the citizens-only FRs – they are the category to whom FRs are "not given" (bankrupt or sick persons and political sufferers keep all their FRs).
- Also citizens only: **contesting elections** (not an FR at all).
- **Aliens:** the Constitution **does distinguish friendly and enemy aliens** – **enemy aliens** lose even the arrest-and-detention safeguards of **Art. 22(1)-(2)**, by **Art. 22(3)**.
- **Companies** (legal persons) can claim the all-persons rights, but **not Art. 19**.

=== Features and Nature of Fundamental Rights
# Features
- **Justiciable:** a person can move the **Supreme Court directly (Art. 32)** and **High Courts (Art. 226)**; FRs are **above ordinary laws** (Art. 13).
- **Not absolute but qualified:** the State can impose **reasonable restrictions** by law; the **courts judge reasonableness**; **Art. 19** lists **11 grounds** of restriction in all.
- **Claimed against the State:** most FRs are available against **arbitrary State action** and **limit the power of the State – even Parliament**; some (15(2), 17, 23, 24) also bind private persons.
- **Not sacrosanct:** Parliament can curtail them **only by a constitutional amendment** (not an ordinary law), without destroying the **basic structure**; they can be **suspended only as the Constitution prescribes** (Arts. 358-359).
- **Negative and positive:** many are **negatively worded** (14, 15(1), 16(2), 18(1), 20, 22(1), 28(1)); **21A, 29, 30** confer privileges; **15, 17, 18, 20, 24** are absolute limits on legislative power.
- **Both natural and legal**; they protect **individuals and minority groups** from arbitrary State action (both statements correct).
- **Britain** has **no written FRs**; it relies on conventions and the rule of law.
# Statement traps
- **Wrong:** "FRs are **unamendable**", "**absolute**", "partly justiciable and absolute", "not limited by the Constitution", "all FRs are suspended in an emergency".
- **Right:** "not absolute", "some only for citizens", "drawn from the American Constitution", "justiciable", "six in number", "granted by the Constitution".
- **CDS 2004:** FRs are fundamental because they are protected and enforced by the Constitution, can be suspended only as it prescribes, and are enforceable against the State (I, II, III) – **not** because they cannot be amended.
- **A–R (UPPSC 2015):** the Constitution is the longest (A true) and the FR chapter follows the American model (R true), but R does not explain A.
- **Right to die:** **Gian Kaur (1996)** – no right to die (suicide) under Art. 21; **Common Cause (2018)** – right to **die with dignity** (passive euthanasia, living will) is part of Art. 21.
- **India is not a fully federal constitution** – it is federal with a unitary bias ("quasi-federal").
- **Golden Triangle:** **Arts. 14, 19 and 21** (Maneka Gandhi 1978; Minerva Mills 1980) – not 14-20-21, 14-15-16, 19-20-21 or 14-21-32.

=== Limits on FRs – Restrictions, Armed Forces (Arts. 33-35), Emergency (358-359)
# Who can restrict
- **Reasonable restrictions** are imposed by **law** made by **Parliament** (and State legislatures within their field) – not by the President, Prime Minister or Supreme Court; the courts only **judge** their reasonableness.
# Articles 33–35
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **33** | **Parliament alone** may restrict or abrogate FRs of the **armed forces**, **forces charged with maintenance of public order** (police), **intelligence/counter-intelligence** staff, and persons in **telecommunication systems set up for those forces** – including non-combatants (barbers, cooks, carpenters). Examples: **Army Act 1950, Air Force Act 1950**, Navy Act 1957 |
| **34** | Restrictions while **martial law** is in force; Parliament may pass an **indemnity Act**; "martial law" is **not defined** in the Constitution |
| **35** | **Only Parliament** (not States) legislates on 16(3), 32(3), 33, 34 and punishments for Part III offences – for uniformity |
- **CDS 2015 trap:** staff of **communication systems set up in the country in general** are **not** covered by Art. 33 – they enjoy FRs at par with other citizens; the armed forces, public-order forces and **their** communication staff are the restricted categories.
# Emergency
- **Art. 358:** when a National Emergency is declared on **war or external aggression**, **Art. 19 (Right to Freedom) is suspended automatically** (not on armed rebellion).
- **Art. 359:** the **President** may by order suspend the right to move courts for **specified FRs** – **never Arts. 20 and 21** (**44th Amendment, 1978**).

=== Article 13 – Laws Void, Meaning of "Law", Amendments
# Clauses
| Clause | Provision |
|---|---|
| **13(1)** | **Pre-constitution laws** inconsistent with FRs are **void to the extent of inconsistency** |
| **13(2)** | The State **shall not make any law that takes away or abridges FRs**; such post-constitution laws are void to that extent |
| **13(3)(a)** | **"Law" includes** ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, **custom or usage** having the force of law |
| **13(4)** | Art. 13 does **not** apply to **Art. 368 amendments** – added by the **24th Amendment, 1971** |
- **Basis of judicial review** (with Arts. 32 and 226); it gives **constitutional provisions (FRs) precedence over laws** of Parliament and State legislatures.
- 13(1) refers to laws in force before the **commencement of the Constitution** (26 Jan 1950), not "before Independence".
- **UPSC 2026:** "law" in Art. 13 includes **custom or usage** – so the view that custom is included is correct and the view excluding it is wrong (only Y correct).
# Are amendments "law"?
| Case / Amendment | Year | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **Shankari Prasad** | 1951 | Amendment is **not "law"** under Art. 13; Parliament can amend FRs (first such case) |
| **Golaknath** | **1967** | FRs have a transcendental position – **Parliament cannot abridge FRs**; prospective overruling |
| **24th Amendment** | 1971 | Added **Art. 13(4)** in reply |
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | 1973 | FRs amendable, but the **basic structure** cannot be destroyed |
- **Ninth Schedule:** added by the **1st Amendment (1951)** with **Arts. 31A and 31B**; **I.R. Coelho (2007)** – laws placed in it after 24 Apr 1973 are open to basic-structure review.

=== Doctrines under Article 13
| Doctrine | Meaning | Case |
|---|---|---|
| **Severability** | Law is void **only to the extent of inconsistency**; the consistent, severable part **remains valid** | A.K. Gopalan (1950); R.M.D.C. (1957); **Motor General Traders v. State of AP (1984)** |
| **Eclipse** | A **pre-constitution law** inconsistent with an FR is **not wiped out** – it is dormant (overshadowed) and **revives when the conflict is removed** | **Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v. State of MP (1955)**; **State of Gujarat v. Ambica Mills (1974)** – extended to post-constitution laws for non-citizens |
| **Waiver** | A person **cannot waive** an FR | **Basheshar Nath v. CIT (1959)** |
| **Prospective overruling** | A ruling operates only for the future | **Golaknath (1967)** |
| **Progressive interpretation** | Constitution as a living document | **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** widened Art. 21; **SaveLIFE Foundation v. UoI (2026)** – right to **trauma care** under Art. 21 |
| **Absolute liability** | Hazardous industry liable without exceptions | **M.C. Mehta (1987)** – Oleum gas leak |
| **Territorial nexus** | State law can operate outside the State if there is sufficient connection | **State of Bombay v. R.M.D.C. (1957)** |
| **Pith and substance** | Encroachment on another List – look at the true nature of the law | Federal doctrine (with aspect, colourable legislation, incidental and ancillary powers, repugnancy) |
- **Eclipse vs severability:** eclipse = law **sleeps and revives**; severability = **bad part cut off, rest lives**. Eminent domain and pleasure are unrelated doctrines.

=== Article 12 – Meaning of "the State"
# Definition
- **Government and Parliament of India**; **Government and Legislature of each State**; **all local authorities** (municipalities, panchayats, district boards); **other authorities** within India or under the control of the Government of India (LIC, ONGC, electricity boards).
- **Ambiguity – courts:** whether the **judiciary** is "other authority" is the grey area; in its judicial function it is **not** "State" (**Naresh Mirajkar v. State of Maharashtra, 1967**).
- **Private bodies:** a writ does not ordinarily lie merely because a private body performs a public activity, unless the State is involved.
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal** | 1967 | **Statutory bodies** are "other authorities" |
| **R.D. Shetty v. International Airport Authority** | 1979 | Laid down tests for an **instrumentality or agency** of the State |
| **Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib** | 1981 | Society running a Regional Engineering College = State; test is **creation and control, not legal form** |
| **Pradeep Kumar Biswas** | 2002 | State if **financially, functionally and administratively dominated** by government |

=== Violation Scenarios
| Scenario | Article violated |
|---|---|
| **Not paying minimum wages** | **Art. 23** – forced labour (**PUDR / Asiad workers, 1982**) |
| Child below 14 in a factory or mine | Art. 24 |
| Admission denied in a State-aided institution on religion, race, caste, language | Art. 29(2) |
| Dalits kept from a public well | Art. 15(2) |
| Dalits kept from a public shop | Art. 17 |
| Banning an association | Art. 19(4) – valid only on permitted grounds |
| Arts. 19/21 violated by a private individual | Ordinary legal remedy, not a writ |
- **Not violations (reasonable restrictions):** **banning secessionist activities** (sovereignty and integrity, 16th Amendment 1963), **no loudspeakers in a religious procession** (public order, health – Church of God, 2000); a **book ban** is valid if it falls within Art. 19(2) grounds.
