## SUB 0 | Jahangir's Reign (1605-1627)
[131:0:0] - Prince **Salim**, Akbar's eldest son (named after **Sheikh Salim Chishti** of Sikri); crowned **1605** as **Nur-ud-din Muhammad Jahangir**.
[131:0:1] TABLE head: Event | Fact
  [131:0:1.0] Khusrau's revolt | Eldest son (mother Man Bai) 1606; blinded
  [131:0:1.1] Guru Arjan Dev (5th Guru) | Executed 1606 for backing Khusrau – political, not general persecution
  [131:0:1.2] Mewar | Rana Amar Singh submitted 1615 (Treaty of Chittor); Mewar and Chittorgarh returned; son Karan Singh made mansabdar
  [131:0:1.3] Kandahar | Lost to Persia (Shah Abbas I) in 1622
  [131:0:1.4] Khurram's revolt | 1622-26; Nur Jahan backed Shahryar (her son-in-law) as heir

[131:0:2] APPSC G1 2022: Arjan Dev's execution, Mewar's submission and the English arrival = Jahangir. **Annexation of Kabul and Kandahar** = NOT Jahangir.
## SUB 1 | Nur Jahan and the Junta
[131:1:0] - **Mehrunnisa**, born **1577** at Kandahar; daughter of **Mirza Ghiyas Beg**; widow of **Sher Afghan**; childhood name per APPSC key **Bahu Begam**.
[131:1:1] - Married Jahangir **1611**; titles **Nur Jahan** ("Light of the World"), later **Padshah Begum**; poet, pen name **"Makhfi"**.
[131:1:2] - **Name on coins and farmans** – a privilege no other Mughal consort had.
[131:1:3] - Built the **Tomb of Itimad-ud-Daulah, Agra** (marble inlay; precursor of the Taj).

## SUB 4 | Shah Jahan (1628-1658)
[131:4:0] - Crowned at **Agra, 1628**. Early revolts: Bundela **Jujhar Singh**; **Khan Jahan Lodi**.
[131:4:1] - **Hughli (1632)**: Portuguese defeated over trading abuses.
[131:4:2] - Capital moved from Agra to **Shahjahanabad (Delhi)**; built **Lal Qila**, Jama Masjid, **Taj Mahal**.
[131:4:3] - **Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus)** in the Diwan-i-Khas; carried away by **Nadir Shah, 1739**.
[131:4:4] - Ended **Sijda** (begun by Balban). Slightly more orthodox; kept many Hindu nobles.
[131:4:5] - Poet laureate: **Kaleem (Abu Jalih)**.
[131:4:6] TABLE head: Work | Author
  [131:4:6.0] Padshahnama | Abdul Hamid Lahori (official historian)
  [131:4:6.1] Shahjahannama | Inayat Khan (English: A.R. Fuller)
  [131:4:6.2] Masir-i-Alamgiri | Saqi Mustaid Khan (Aurangzeb)
  [131:4:6.3] Adab-i-Alamgiri | Aurangzeb's letters on the father-son money dispute
  [131:4:6.4] Fatawa-i-Alamgiri | Compiled under Aurangzeb, not Shah Jahan
[131:4:7] - **"Golden age"** of Mughal architecture.
[131:4:8] - **Captivity**: Agra Fort, nursed by Jahanara, till death in **1666** (about **8 years**).

## SUB 6 | Deccan Policy
[131:6:0] - **1636**: **Ahmadnagar** (Nizam Shahi) annexed – Malik Ambar's son **Fath Khan** submitted.
[131:6:1] - **1636 ahdnamas** with **Bijapur and Golconda**: Mughal suzerainty, tribute, Mughal arbitration.
[131:6:2] - Golconda's **Inqiyad Nama** (Abdullah Qutb Shah): Shah Jahan's name in **khutba and coins**.
[131:6:3] - Four Deccan subas: **Khandesh, Berar, Telangana, Daulatabad**.
[131:6:4] - **Aurangzeb** as Deccan viceroy: **1636-44** and **1652-58**; attacked Golconda in **1656**, urged by **Mir Jumla**.
[131:6:5] TABLE head: Kingdom | Annexed | Last ruler
  [131:6:5.0] Bijapur | 1686 | Sikandar Adil Shah
  [131:6:5.1] Golconda | 1687 (21st suba) | Abul Hasan Tana Shah – exiled to Daulatabad, died 1699
[131:6:6] - AP angle: Jahangir's envoy to Golconda **Mir Makki**; Tana Shah employed **Madanna and Akkanna**, helped Shivaji.
[131:6:7] - Marathas: **Sambhaji** executed 1689; **Rajaram** from **Jinji**, then **Tarabai**; **Shahu** released by **Bahadur Shah I**; Aurangzeb died in the Deccan, 1707.

=== NOTES 1:132 | Aurangzeb – Religious Policy, Deccan Policy and Resistance
## SUB 0 | Aurangzeb – Titles and Coronations
[132:0:0] - Regnal title: **Alamgir** ("World Conqueror"). Popular epithet: **Zinda Pir** ("Living Saint"), for his simple, pious life.
[132:0:1] - **Double coronation**: first, hurried – **1658** (after **Samugarh**); second, grand – **1659** (after **Deorai**).
[132:0:2] - Reign: 1658–1707. The Mughal empire reached its **greatest extent** yet was overstretched.
[132:0:3] # Titles of Royal Women
[132:0:4] TABLE head: Title | Holder | Note
  [132:0:4.0] Padshah Begum | Roshanara | Backed Aurangzeb in the succession war
  [132:0:4.1] Sahibat-uz-Zamani ("Mistress of the Age") | Jahanara | Given to her later
[132:0:5] Roshanara = Padshah Begum; Jahanara = Sahibat-uz-Zamani. Do not swap them.
[132:0:6] - FIRST: first Mughal emperor to fight the English – **Child's War (1686–90)**.
## SUB 1 | Religious Policy – Taxes and Measures
[132:1:0] TABLE head: Measure | Detail
  [132:1:0.0] Jizya | Reimposed in 1679 (Akbar had abolished it); suspended in the Deccan in 1704
  [132:1:0.1] Temples | Newly built temples ordered destroyed; 1669 order – Kashi Vishwanath and Keshava Rai (Mathura) demolished
  [132:1:0.2] Muhtasibs | Appointed to enforce the Sharia (public morals)
  [132:1:0.3] Court customs | Jharokha darshan and tuladan stopped; Navroz banned
  [132:1:0.4] Court music | Banned at court, yet he played the veena; most Persian works on Indian classical music were written in his reign
  [132:1:0.5] Zawabit | State-made secular regulations; he stressed the supremacy of Sharia over them
[132:1:1] - **Hindu nobles' share rose** to about **31.6% (1679–1707)**, many of them Marathas (Athar Ali) – so "anti-Hindu" is not the whole story.
[132:1:2] Jizya reimposed 1679 – the same year the Rajput war began.
## SUB 2 | Rajput Resistance – Marwar and Mewar
[132:2:0] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [132:2:0.0] Trigger | Maharaja Jaswant Singh of Marwar died 1678; Aurangzeb took Marwar under direct control
  [132:2:0.1] Heir | Posthumous son Ajit Singh, championed by Durgadas Rathore
  [132:2:0.2] Mewar war (1679–81) | Rana Raj Singh joined Marwar
  [132:2:0.3] Rebel prince | Prince Akbar (his son) joined the Rajputs (1681) → fled to Sambhaji → finally Persia
[132:2:1] - Result: the **Rajput alliance** (pillar of Akbar's empire) was broken.
## SUB 3 | Jat and Satnami Revolts
[132:3:0] TABLE head: Revolt | Leader | Region / fact
  [132:3:0.0] Jat (1669) | Gokula | Mathura (Tilpat); peasant revolt against heavy revenue
  [132:3:0.1] Jat (1680s) | Rajaram | Plundered Akbar's tomb at Sikandra
  [132:3:0.2] Jat (after 1700) | Churaman | Built the power that became Bharatpur state
  [132:3:0.3] Satnami (1672) | Satnami sect | Narnaul (Haryana); peasants and artisans; crushed
[132:3:1] Satnami revolt of 1672 has no link with **Guru Ghasidas** (19th-century Satnampanth, Chhattisgarh).
[132:3:2] Jat Rajaram (Sikandra) ≠ Maratha Rajaram (Shivaji's son).
[132:3:3] - **Surajmal (Bharatpur):** Jat ruler of Bharatpur (ruled 1755-1763). A contemporary historian called him "the Plato of the Jat tribe" for his political wisdom.
## SUB 4 | Sikh Resistance
[132:4:0] - **Guru Tegh Bahadur** (9th Guru) – arrested 1675 and **executed at Delhi in 1675** after refusing to accept Islam.
[132:4:1] - **Guru Gobind Singh** (10th Guru) – fought the Mughals; after **Chamkaur (1704)** wrote the **Zafarnama**, a Persian letter to Aurangzeb accusing him of breaking oaths sworn on the Quran.
## SUB 5 | Deccan Policy – Bijapur and Golconda
[132:5:0] # Bijapur (1686)
[132:5:1] - Long siege (1685–86); Aurangzeb took personal command; fell **September 1686**.
[132:5:2] - Last ruler **Sikandar Adil Shah** – presented in silver chains; sent to **Daulatabad**; Adil Shahi dynasty ended.
[132:5:3] # Fall of Golconda (1687) – Sequence
[132:5:4] - 1. Ministers **Madanna–Akkanna** murdered (**1686**).
[132:5:5] - 2. Siege **1687**; **Abdur Razzaq Lari** defended (later honoured as **Mustafa Khan**).
[132:5:6] - 3. Traitor **Abdullah Pani** opened the gates – fort opened with "**keys of gold**" (as Akbar opened Asirgarh).
[132:5:7] - 4. Fort taken **1687** → **21st Mughal suba**.
[132:5:8] - 5. Last ruler **Abul Hasan Qutb Shah (Tana Shah)** – imprisoned at Bidar, then **Daulatabad**; died **1699**; buried at **Khuldabad**.
[132:5:9] Tana Shah was exiled to **Daulatabad**, not Gwalior (Gwalior = Murad Bakhsh).
[132:5:10] - **AP angle**: fall of Golconda (1687) ended **Qutb Shahi rule over Andhra**; **Bhakta Ramadasu** was the nephew of Akkanna–Madanna.

=== NOTES 1:133 | Mughal Administration – Mansabdari, Jagirdari and the Jagirdari Crisis
## SUB 0 | Mansabdari System
[133:0:0] - Introduced by **Akbar**; "Mansab" = **Arabic** for **rank/position**. Model borrowed from **Central Asia / Mongolia** (Genghis Khan's decimal army grading).
[133:0:1] - One grading for **army and civil service**; aim – clean administration; run by the **Mir Bakhshi**.
[133:0:2] - Ranks **10 to 10,000**; Abul Fazl: **66 grades** in theory, **33** in practice; highest reserved for princes.
[133:0:3] - **Not hereditary** – rank and jagir ended with the holder. **Not all were military** – high civil officials were also mansabdars.
[133:0:4] - Paid in cash (**naqd**) or by a revenue assignment (**jagir**). Mughal state = basically a **military state**.
## SUB 1 | Zat, Sawar and Changes
[133:1:0] TABLE head: Term / class | Meaning
  [133:1:0.0] Zat | Personal rank – status and salary
  [133:1:0.1] Sawar | Cavalrymen to be maintained
  [133:1:0.2] First / Second / Third class | Sawar = zat / half or more of zat / less than half
[133:1:1] - **Horse rule**: every **10 sawars → 20 horses**.
[133:1:2] Sawar could NOT exceed zat under the system.
[133:1:3] TABLE head: Change | Emperor | Effect
  [133:1:3.0] Du-aspa sih-aspa | Jahangir | Part of sawar counted double; zat not raised
  [133:1:3.1] Month-scale | Shah Jahan | Shashmaha = half-yield; sihmaha = quarter-yield
  [133:1:3.2] Reduced obligation | Shah Jahan | Keep 1/5 to 1/3 of sawar
  [133:1:3.3] Mashrut | Aurangzeb | Temporary, conditional rise in sawar for a task
## SUB 2 | Army and Muster Terms
[133:2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [133:2:0.0] Ahadis | Gentlemen troopers recruited and paid by the emperor; served singly, emperor as their colonel
  [133:2:0.1] Dakhili | State-paid troops placed under mansabdars
  [133:2:0.2] Tabinan | Mansabdars' own contingents
  [133:2:0.3] Walashahi | Emperor's bodyguard
  [133:2:0.4] Dagh / Chehra / Tashiha | Horse branding / descriptive roll / muster check
[133:2:1] - Akbar **revived** Dagh and Chehra; army mostly mansabdars' cavalry.
## SUB 3 | Central Officials
[133:3:0] TABLE head: Official | Function
  [133:3:0.0] Wakil | Chief adviser/regent; made symbolic by Akbar
  [133:3:0.1] Wazir / Diwan | Revenue and finance
  [133:3:0.2] Mir Bakhshi | Military, mansabdars, intelligence
  [133:3:0.3] Mir Saman | Royal household, karkhanas
  [133:3:0.4] Sadr-us-Sudur | Religion, charity, madad-i-maash
  [133:3:0.5] Qazi-ul-Quzat | Chief justice (sharia)
[133:3:1] - Espionage: **waqia-navis** (news-writers), **khufia-navis** (secret), **harkaras** – under the Mir Bakhshi.
## SUB 4 | Provincial Set-up and Revenue Officials
[133:4:0] - Ascending: **Village → Pargana → (Chakla) → Sarkar → Subah**. **Chakla** = group of parganas (Shah Jahan).
[133:4:1] - Subahs: **12 (1580) → 15** under Akbar; **21** under Aurangzeb.
[133:4:2] TABLE head: Level | Officials
  [133:4:2.0] Subah | Subahdar (law and order); Diwan (revenue, check on Subahdar)
  [133:4:2.1] Sarkar | Faujdar (military, law and order); Amalguzar (revenue); Bitikchi (land records, revenue papers); Khazanadar (treasurer)
  [133:4:2.2] Pargana | Shiqdar (executive, police); Amil (revenue); Qanungo (head of patwaris, records); Karkun (clerk)
  [133:4:2.3] Town / Village | Kotwal (town police, markets); Patwari (village accountant)
## SUB 5 | Jagirs – Types; Jagirdar vs Zamindar
[133:5:0] TABLE head: Type | Nature
  [133:5:0.0] Tankhwah | In lieu of salary; transferable
  [133:5:0.1] Watan | Hereditary home of zamindar/Rajput chief; not transferred
  [133:5:0.2] Mashrut | Conditional
  [133:5:0.3] In'am | Free of service
  [133:5:0.4] Altamgha | Perpetual
  [133:5:0.5] Khalisa | Crown land – NOT a jagir
  [133:5:0.6] Jagirdar | Zamindar
  [133:5:0.7] Revenue right in lieu of salary; no land ownership, no judicial/police duty | Hereditary local rights
  [133:5:0.8] Often transferred | State–peasant intermediary
[133:5:1] - **Madad-i-maash:** revenue-free land grants to scholars and religious men; managed by the Sadr (Sadr-us-Sudur).
## SUB 6 | Jagir Transfers and Jagirdari Crisis
[133:6:0] - Transfers every **3–4 years** – to stop local roots and feudal power.
[133:6:1] - **Bejagiri** – mansabdars waiting without jagirs → discontent, faction.
[133:6:2] - Under **Aurangzeb**: more mansabdars, less good land (**paibaqi**) → peasants squeezed → main reason for **Mughal decline**.

  [133:6:3.2] Athar Ali | Influx of Deccani and Maratha nobles
[133:6:4] - AP angle: **Asaf Jahi Hyderabad** kept Mughal-style jagirdari.
