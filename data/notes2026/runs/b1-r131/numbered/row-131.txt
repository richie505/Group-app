## SUB 0 | Jahangir (1605-27) – Events of the Reign and Nur Jahan's Junta
[0:0] # Jahangir – key events
[0:1] - **Accession:** Prince **Salim** (named after **Sheikh Salim Chishti** of Sikri; mother Jodha Bai/Harkha Bai), revolted against Akbar c. 1599-1604; crowned **1605** as **Nur-ud-din Muhammad Jahangir**.
[0:2] TABLE head: Event | Fact
  [0:2.0] Khusrau's revolt | Eldest son (mother Man Bai = Shah Begum) rebelled 1606; defeated near Lahore, imprisoned, blinded
  [0:2.1] Guru Arjan Dev (5th Guru) | Executed 1606 for backing Khusrau
  [0:2.2] Mewar – Treaty of Chittor | 1615: Rana Amar Singh submitted; Mewar and Chittor returned; son Karan Singh made a mansabdar
  [0:2.3] Deccan | 1616: Khan-i-Khanan beat Ahmadnagar, Bijapur, Golconda with Maratha sardars; Malik Ambar rose
  [0:2.4] Kangra | Annexed 1620
  [0:2.5] Kandahar | Lost to Persia (Shah Abbas I) in 1622 – NOT annexed by Jahangir
  [0:2.6] Khurram's revolt | 1622-26; ordered to Kandahar, refused; Nur Jahan backed Shahryar
  [0:2.7] Mahabat Khan's coup | 1626: seized Jahangir on the Jhelum
  [0:2.8] English | Hawkins 1608-09, factory at Surat 1613, Roe 1615-19
[0:3] - **Rebels against Jahangir:** **Khusrau, Khurram, Mahabat Khan** – NOT **Asaf Khan** (UPPSC 2013).
[0:4] - **Events of Jahangir's reign (APPSC G1):** Arjan Dev's execution, Mewar's submission, the English arrival – **"annexation of Kabul and Kandahar" is the trap**.
[0:5] - **Shah Begum (Man Bai, Manbhawati Bai)** – Jahangir's Rajput wife, mother of Khusrau – **committed suicide (opium), 1605** over the father-son quarrel (UPPSC 2008).
[0:6] - **Orchha:** Bundela **Bir Singh Deo** of Orchha was Salim's ally (killed Abul Fazl, 1602, for him); the **Jahangir Mahal** at Orchha was built for him – the printed key for "fort where Jahangir took shelter".
[0:7] - Chain of justice; **Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri**; lived mostly at Lahore; painting at its zenith; pietra dura began.
[0:8] # Kandahar not recaptured under Jahangir (CDS 2000)
[0:9] - Khurram **refused to go**, an expedition was **hard to organise**, the **cold** of Afghanistan – (printed key II, III, IV).
[0:10] # Nur Jahan and the junta
[0:11] - **Mehr-un-Nisa** (Mehrunnisa), born **1577** at Kandahar; daughter of **Mirza Ghiyas Beg**; widow of **Sher Afghan**; married Jahangir **1611**; titles **Nur Mahal → Nur Jahan** ("Light of the World"), **Padshah Begum**; pen name **"Makhfi"**.
[0:12] - **Childhood name = Mehr-un-Nisa** ("Maharul Nisa" in APPSC's option) – the APPSC key's **"Bahu Begam" is wrong**.
[0:13] - **Name on coins and farmans** – the only Mughal consort with this privilege; built the **Tomb of Itimad-ud-Daulah, Agra** for her father.

  [0:14.2] Ghiyas Beg – Itimad-ud-Daulah | Father; Jahangir's diwan
  [0:14.3] Asaf Khan | Brother; Shah Jahan's father-in-law
  [0:14.4] Prince Khurram | Married her niece Arjumand Banu Begum (1612)
[0:15] - **NOT junta members: Jahangir himself and Mahabat Khan.**
[0:16] TABLE head: Emperor | Diwan

[1:2] # Shah Jahan (1628-58)
[1:3] - Crowned at **Agra, 1628**; early revolts: Bundela **Jujhar Singh**, **Khan Jahan Lodi**; Portuguese beaten at **Hughli (1632)** for trading abuses.
[1:4] - **Capital shifted from Agra to Shahjahanabad (Delhi), 1638-48**; Red Fort, Jama Masjid, **Taj Mahal** (for Mumtaz – not Jodha Bai or Nur Jahan); **Peacock Throne** (taken by **Nadir Shah, 1739**); reign = "golden age" of architecture.
[1:5] - **Poet laureate: Abu Talib Kalim (Kaleem)**; official historian **Abdul Hamid Lahori – Padshahnama**; **Inayat Khan – Shahjahannama**.
[1:6] - **Captivity:** deposed 1658 and kept in **Agra Fort** (nursed by Jahanara) till death in **1666** – Aurangzeb imprisoned his father for life.

## SUB 4 | Central Asian Policy – Kandahar and Balkh
[4:0] TABLE head: Campaign | Commander | Result
  [4:0.0] Kandahar recovered, 1638 | Persian governor Ali Mardan Khan surrendered it | Mughal gain (lost 1622 → regained 1638)
  [4:0.1] Balkh-Badakhshan, 1646-47 | Murad Bakhsh, then Aurangzeb (not Dara) | Failed, heavy losses
  [4:0.2] Kandahar lost, 1649 | – | To Persia (Shah Abbas II)
  [4:0.3] Sieges 1649, 1652 | Aurangzeb | Failed
  [4:0.4] Siege 1653 | Dara Shikoh | Failed – Kandahar lost for good
[4:1] - **Aim of the Balkh campaign:** to set up a **friendly ruler in Balkh and Badakhshan, which bordered Kabul** – not to conquer Samarqand/Farghana or fix the Amu Darya "scientific line" (UPSC 2002).
[4:2] - **Kandahar:** frontier fortress on the routes India–Persia–Central Asia – its loss was a blow as a **strategic stronghold** (UPSC 1998).
[4:3] - **UPSC 1998 A-R:** Dara led only the **Qandahar** siege (1653); Balkh-Badakhshan were Murad and Aurangzeb; the expeditions **failed** – so R is false.
[4:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 04 p.29; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.110; Satish Chandra, Medieval India II (Balkh aim); old app notes 1:131 (sub 5)
## SUB 5 | Deccan Policy of Shah Jahan
[5:0] - **1633:** **Daulatabad** fell to Mahabat Khan when **Fath Khan** (son of **Malik Ambar**) surrendered it – Malik Ambar had **died in 1626**; **1636:** Nizam Shahi **Ahmadnagar** extinguished.
[5:1] - **1636 ahdnamas** with **Bijapur and Golconda**: Mughal suzerainty, tribute, Mughal arbitration; Golconda's **Inqiyad Nama** (Abdullah Qutb Shah) put Shah Jahan's name in **khutba and coins**.
[5:2] - Four Deccan subas: **Khandesh, Berar, Telangana, Daulatabad**.
[5:3] - **Aurangzeb – viceroy of the Deccan** for most of the reign: **1636-44** and **1652/53-58**; attacked Golconda in **1656**, urged by **Mir Jumla**.

  [5:4.1] Golconda | 1687 | Abul Hasan Tana Shah – exiled to Daulatabad
[5:5] - AP angle: Jahangir's envoy to Golconda **Mir Makki**; Tana Shah employed **Madanna and Akkanna** and helped Shivaji.
