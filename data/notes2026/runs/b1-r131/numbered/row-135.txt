## SUB 1 | Trade, Ports, Merchants and Credit
[1:0] TABLE head: Group | Role / region
  [1:0.0] Banjaras | Traders carrying bulk goods (grain, salt) over long distances in bullock caravans (tanda)
  [1:0.1] Seths, Bohras | Wholesale, long-distance trade
  [1:0.2] Baniks / Beoparis | Local retail traders
  [1:0.3] Mahajans | Moneylenders-cum-merchants
  [1:0.4] Sarrafs (shroffs) | Money-changers and bankers; issued hundis
  [1:0.5] Nagarseth | Head of a merchant community
  [1:0.6] Marwaris | Oswals, Maheshwaris, Agarwals of Rajasthan
  [1:0.7] Chettis / Muslim merchants | Coromandel coast / Malabar
[1:1] - **Hundi** – **letter of credit** issued by shroffs against deposited cash; encashed later or at another place – not Farman (royal order), Parcha, Rokad (cash). **Bima** – insurance of goods in transit.
[1:2] - Overseas trade under the Sultanate: **Multanis and Khurasanis**; merchant strikes at **Ahmedabad**.
[1:3] TABLE head: Port | Epithet / trade
  [1:3.0] Surat | Chief west-coast port; Bab-ul-Makka (Gate of Mecca) – Haj port; "Meccai dwar"
  [1:3.1] Cambay (Khambhat) | Old Gujarat port; declined with silting
  [1:3.2] Lahari (Lahori) Bandar | Port of Thatta in the Indus delta – Thatta itself was the inland town, not the port
  [1:3.3] Masulipatnam | Qutb Shahi port; Kalamkari (chintz), saltpetre; Dutch 1605 → English 1611 → French 1669
  [1:3.4] Hugli | Portuguese base in Bengal; expelled by Shah Jahan, 1632
[1:4] - AP angle: **Machilipatnam** = first European centre in Andhra (Dutch, 1605); saltpetre at Machilipatnam, Narsapur, Pulicat; copper at **Motupalli**; **Nirmal** swords; **Warangal** carpets; **Tobacco Board at Guntur**.

## SUB 3 | Agriculture – Crops, Canals and the Marketed Share
[3:0] - **Marketed share:** about **15%** of rural agricultural produce (APPSC G2 2017, printed key in both PYQ copies) – the old app notes gave **25%**; disputed, see the PYQ note. Cash revenue pushed peasants to sell.
[3:1] TABLE head: Crop group | Crops
  [3:1.0] Jins-i-kamil ("perfect crops") | Commercial crops – cotton, sugarcane, indigo, oilseeds, opium
  [3:1.1] New World crops (via the Portuguese) | Tobacco, maize, chillies
  [3:1.2] Regional | Gujarat – indigo, food grains; Bengal – sugar, rice
[3:2] - **Shahab (Shihab) Nahr** – restored **Firuz Shah's Rajabwah**; repaired under Akbar by Delhi governor **Shihabuddin Khan**.
[3:3] - Contrast: **Vijayanagara** agriculture through ponds, tanks, dams and wells.
[3:4] - Zabt revenue in cash – state share **one-third** of the average produce of the past ten years; measured with the **Ilahi Gaz**; **patta** = title deed, **qabuliyat** = deed of agreement; Khwaja Shah Mansoor and Muzaffar Khan Turbati helped Todar Mal.
[3:5] - Land classes: polaj (every year), parauti (fallow 1-2 years), chachar, banjar.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 04 p.31; old app notes 1:135 (sub 0-1)
## SUB 4 | Exports, Imports, Silver Influx and Indian Ocean Commerce
[4:0] TABLE head: Exports | Imports
  [4:0.0] Textiles (calico, muslin, chintz), saltpetre, indigo, sugar, opium, spices | War horses, silver, ivory, porcelain, wine, glass, watches, tin, copper
[4:1] - **EIC staples from Bengal (mid-18th century): cotton, silk, saltpetre and opium** – not copper, gold or tea (UPSC 2018).
[4:2] - India = "**sink of precious metals**": Europeans paid in **bullion**; silver from **Spanish America (New World)** → more rupee coinage, **rise in prices**.
[4:3] TABLE head: Firman | Year | Provision
  [4:3.0] Golden Firman – Abdullah Qutb Shah | 1632 | Free trade for the English at Golconda ports
  [4:3.1] Farrukhsiyar's firman | 1716/1717 | Cut English duties
[4:4] - Merchant groups: Gujaratis, Bohras, Chettis, Malabar Muslims, **Armenians**; Europeans – Portuguese, Dutch, English EIC (1600), French; balance of trade favoured India till the mid-18th century.
[4:5] - British period: Indian exports heavily taxed in Britain, British goods favoured in India.

## SUB 5 | Karkhanas and Rural Society
[5:0] - **Karkhanas** – royal workshops run by the **Mir Saman (Khan-i-Saman)**; a Sultanate tradition; made court luxuries – gold/silver articles, fine textiles, carpets, arms, jewellery (not bulk grain).
[5:1] TABLE head: Class | Features

  [5:1.2] Khud-kasht | Peasant cultivating land in his own village
  [5:1.3] Pahi-kasht | Peasant cultivating land in another village
  [5:1.4] Artisans | Worked in karkhanas or private enterprise
