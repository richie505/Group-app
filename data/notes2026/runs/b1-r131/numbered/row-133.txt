## SUB 0 | Mansabdari System – Origin, Purpose and Nature
[0:0] - Introduced by **Akbar** (1570s); "Mansab" = **Arabic** for **rank/position**; model **borrowed from Mongolia/Central Asia** (Genghis Khan's decimal army grading) – not Persia, Turkey or Afghanistan.
[0:1] - **One grading for army and civil service** – the Mughal **military system** was based on it; **not all mansabdars were military officers** – high civil officials were mansabdars too, graded alike.
[0:2] - **Purpose (UPSC 1996 key):** "**effecting clean administration**" – a single graded service (Ghatna Chakra/key); army recruitment is the tempting option.
[0:3] - Run by the **Mir Bakhshi** – responsible for its smooth functioning.
[0:4] - Ranks **10 to 10,000**; Abul Fazl: **66 grades** in theory, **33** in practice; highest ranks for princes.
[0:5] - **Not hereditary** – rank and jagir ended with the holder; appointment, promotion and dismissal at the emperor's will (on merit/ability).
[0:6] - Paid in cash (**naqd**) or by a revenue assignment (**jagir**). Mughal state = basically a military state.
[0:7] - **UPPSC 2010 statements:** zat (and sawar) ranks granted ✓; **three classes** ✓; hereditary ✗ – printed key "only 1 & 3 true".
[0:8] - **Statements (APPSC ASO):** Babur founded the empire at **Panipat 1526**; Akbar's mansabdari; decline after **1707** – but the **Taj Mahal was Shah Jahan's**, not Aurangzeb's.
[0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 04 p.32; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.106; old app notes 1:133 (sub 0)
## SUB 1 | Zat, Sawar, Classes and Later Changes
[1:0] TABLE head: Term / class | Meaning
  [1:0.0] Zat | Personal rank – status in the hierarchy and salary
  [1:0.1] Sawar | Number of cavalrymen (horsemen) to be maintained
  [1:0.2] First class | Sawar = zat
  [1:0.3] Second class | Sawar half or more of zat
  [1:0.4] Third class | Sawar less than half of zat
[1:1] - **Three classes** of mansabdars; **sawar could NOT exceed zat**.
[1:2] - **Chehra** is the descriptive roll, **not a rank** – ranks were only zat and sawar (CDS 2009).
[1:3] - Nobles (**umara**): mansabdars of **1,000 zat or above** (NCERT) – a "1,000 sawar" statement is wrong.
[1:4] - **Horse rule**: every **10 sawars → 20 horses**.
[1:5] TABLE head: Change | Emperor | Effect
  [1:5.0] Du-aspa sih-aspa | Jahangir | Part of sawar counted double; zat not raised
  [1:5.1] Month-scale | Shah Jahan | Shashmaha = half-yield; sihmaha = quarter-yield
  [1:5.2] Reduced obligation | Shah Jahan | Keep 1/5 to 1/3 of sawar
  [1:5.3] Mashrut | Aurangzeb | Temporary, conditional rise in sawar for a task
[1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 04 p.32-33; NCERT Class 7 Our Pasts II ch.4; old app notes 1:133 (sub 1)
## SUB 2 | Army Terms and Central Officials
[2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [2:0.0] Ahadis | Gentlemen troopers recruited singly, attached to no chief, the emperor as their colonel
  [2:0.1] Dakhili | Troops raised and paid by the state, placed under mansabdars
  [2:0.2] Tabinan | Mansabdars' own contingents
  [2:0.3] Walashahi | Emperor's bodyguard
  [2:0.4] Dagh / Chehra / Tashiha | Horse branding / descriptive roll / muster check
[2:1] - Akbar **revived** Dagh and Chehra (1573-74); the army was mostly mansabdars' cavalry.
[2:2] TABLE head: Official | Function
  [2:2.0] Wakil | Chief adviser/regent; made symbolic by Akbar
  [2:2.1] Wazir / Diwan | Revenue and finance
  [2:2.2] Mir Bakhshi | Head of the military department, mansabdars, intelligence
  [2:2.3] Mir Saman | Royal household, karkhanas
  [2:2.4] Sadr-us-Sudur | Religion, charity, madad-i-maash
  [2:2.5] Qazi-ul-Quzat | Chief justice (sharia)
  [2:2.6] Muhtasib | Saw that people lived by the shariat (public morals) – stressed by Aurangzeb
[2:3] - Espionage: **waqia-navis** (news-writers), **khufia-navis** (secret), **harkaras** – under the Mir Bakhshi.

## SUB 3 | Jagirs and the Jagirdari Crisis
[3:0] TABLE head: Type | Nature
  [3:0.0] Tankhwah | In lieu of salary; transferable
  [3:0.1] Watan | Hereditary home of a zamindar/Rajput chief; not transferred
  [3:0.2] Mashrut | Conditional
  [3:0.3] In'am | Free of service; land to learned and religious men
  [3:0.4] Altamgha | Perpetual
  [3:0.5] Khalisa | Crown land – income straight to the treasury; NOT a jagir
[3:1] - **Jagir** = territorial assignment to mansabdars **in lieu of salary**; **Mauza = village** (rebellious tract = mawas) – Astt. Comm. 2017 wrong pair.
[3:2] - **Madad-i-maash:** revenue-free grants to scholars and religious men, under the Sadr.
[3:3] - Transfers every **3-4 years** – to stop local roots; **bejagiri** – mansabdars waiting without jagirs.
[3:4] - Under **Aurangzeb**: more mansabdars, too little good land (**paibaqi**) → peasants squeezed → core of **Mughal decline**.
[3:5] TABLE head: Historian | Explanation
  [3:5.0] Satish Chandra | Shortage of jagirs
  [3:5.1] Irfan Habib | Agrarian crisis
  [3:5.2] Athar Ali | Influx of Deccani and Maratha nobles
[3:6] - AP angle: **Asaf Jahi Hyderabad** kept Mughal-style jagirdari.
