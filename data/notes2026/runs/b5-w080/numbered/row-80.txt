## SUB 0 | Meaning and the Brundtland Definition
[0:0] # Brundtland Commission (1987)
[0:1] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [0:1.0] Formal name | World Commission on Environment and Development (WCED) – UN-mandated, set up in the early 1980s
  [0:1.1] Chair | Gro Harlem Brundtland, then Prime Minister of Norway ("Mother of Sustainable Development")
  [0:1.2] Report | "Our Common Future" = the Brundtland Report
  [0:1.3] Year | 1987
  [0:1.4] Contribution | First widely accepted (official) definition; popularised the term and linked economic development with environmental stability
[0:2] - **Definition:** development that "**meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs**" – given by the **WCED (1987)**, not UNEP, the World Bank, the IMF, UNCED 1992 or the Tbilisi Declaration (1977).
[0:3] - **Our Common Future on poverty:** "**Poverty is a major cause and effect of global environmental problems**" – so environment cannot be tackled without tackling poverty and inequality.
[0:4] - **Concepts in one line:** sustainable development = the definition above; Our Common Future = the 1987 report; **intergenerational equity** = fair sharing of resources between present and future generations.
[0:5] - Exam shorthand: "**improving the conditions of the present generation without affecting the needs of future generations**" = sustainable development exactly.
[0:6] > Most-asked (×20): who defined it / which report / which year → **Brundtland, Our Common Future, 1987**. Rio 1992 and Stockholm 1972 are the tempting wrong options.
[0:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment Part 6 p.6 (21.4.1 Brundtland Commission); NextGen Society Group 2 Part 2 p.10-11; NextGen Rocket Environment p.71; PMF Human & Economic Geography Part 2 p.45; en.wikisource.org Brundtland Report ch.1 (poverty sentence)
## SUB 1 | Rio Earth Summit 1992 and Agenda 21
[1:0] # UNCED – the first Earth Summit
[1:1] - **UNCED = United Nations Conference on Environment and Development** ("Earth Summit"), **Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, 3-14 June 1992**; 20th anniversary of Stockholm 1972; about **178-179 countries**.
[1:2] - **Outcomes:** **Agenda 21**, **Rio Declaration** (**27 principles**), **Forest Principles** (non-binding); legally binding conventions opened for signature: **UNFCCC** and **CBD** (UNCCD followed in 1994).
[1:3] - **UNFCCC** was negotiated in New York and **drawn/opened for signature at UNCED, Rio 1992** – not at Stockholm 1972, WSSD 2002 or Copenhagen 2009.
[1:4] # Agenda 21
[1:5] TABLE head: Point | Fact
  [1:5.0] What | Comprehensive (non-binding) global action plan for sustainable development – globally, nationally, locally, wherever humans affect the environment
  [1:5.1] Origin | UNCED, Rio 1992 – NOT the Johannesburg WSSD 2002 (UPSC 2016: statement 1 only)
  [1:5.2] "21" | 21st century
  [1:5.3] Key objective | Every local government should draw its own Local Agenda 21 (LA21)
  [1:5.4] Sample programme area | Ch.2: "Promoting sustainable development through trade"; Ch.4 is *changing* (not maintaining) consumption patterns
  [1:5.5] Aim | Combat environmental damage, poverty and disease through global co-operation
[1:6] - Agenda 21 is not a law that each country must enact, and not a "20th-century only" target.
[1:7] > Rio 1992 is the "first Earth Summit" (not Washington, Geneva or Buenos Aires); MPPSC 1992 printed "UN Conference on Sustainable Development" for Rio – formally the 1992 meeting was UNCED.

## SUB 2 | Follow-up Summits – Rio+5, WSSD, Rio+20, 2030 Agenda
[2:0] TABLE head: Year | Formal name (popular name) | Venue | Outcome
  [2:0.0] 1972 | UN Conference on the Human Environment (UNCHE) | Stockholm | First world environment conference; UNEP (HQ Nairobi)
  [2:0.1] 1992 | UNCED – Earth Summit | Rio de Janeiro | Agenda 21, Rio Declaration, UNFCCC, CBD
  [2:0.2] 1997 | Earth Summit +5 (Rio+5), UNGA special session | New York | Reviewed Agenda 21
  [2:0.3] 1997 | Kyoto COP3 | Kyoto | Kyoto Protocol (climate)
  [2:0.4] 2000 | Millennium Summit | New York | MDGs (2000-2015)
  [2:0.5] 2002 | World Summit on Sustainable Development (WSSD) – Rio+10 | Johannesburg | Johannesburg Declaration; Johannesburg Plan of Implementation (JPOI)
  [2:0.6] June 2012 | UN Conference on Sustainable Development (UNCSD) – Rio+20 | Rio de Janeiro | "The Future We Want"; green economy; SDGs born here; PAGE
  [2:0.7] 25 Sept 2015 | UN Sustainable Development Summit (UNGA 70) | New York | "Transforming our World: the 2030 Agenda" – 17 SDGs
[2:1] - **JPOI:** states to formulate **national strategies for sustainable development** and **begin their implementation by 2005**.
[2:2] - **Rio+20:** 20-year follow-up to 1992, 10-year follow-up to 2002; third SD conference, hosted by Brazil. The SDGs were **born at Rio+20, 2012** (not 2010).
[2:3] - **PAGE (Partnership for Action on Green Economy):** **emerged at Rio+20 (2012)** as a response to The Future We Want (launched 2013); agencies **UNEP, UNDP, ILO, UNIDO, UNITAR**.
[2:4] - **2015** also saw the **COP21 Paris Agreement** – the SDGs coincided with it.
[2:5] # UN Commission on Sustainable Development (CSD)
[2:6] - **Established by the UN General Assembly in December 1992** (Res. 47/191) to **review implementation of Agenda 21 and the Rio Declaration**.
[2:7] - **Replaced in 2013** by the **High-level Political Forum on Sustainable Development (HLPF)** (under ECOSOC/UNGA), as decided at Rio+20.

## SUB 3 | 2030 Agenda and SDGs – Basics
[3:0] - **17 SDGs, 169 targets, 232 indicators**; adopted by **all 193 UN member states** in **September 2015**; in force **1 Jan 2016**; target year **2030**.
[3:1] - **Not legally binding** and not a single development model; they **replaced the MDGs (2000-2015)**.
[3:2] - **Five Ps:** **People, Planet, Prosperity, Peace, Partnership** (not Patriotism).
[3:3] - **Dimensions:** **economic, social, environmental**.
[3:4] - **Goal 1:** "**End poverty in all its forms everywhere**" – no 50%/75% target.
[3:5] - Aim: get rid of poverty, protect the environment and ensure peace and prosperity for all; scope covers goals like life below water, decent work and cultural diversity (target 4.7) – **not political alliances**.
[3:6] - Biggest hurdle for poverty eradication: **economic inequality and unequal distribution of resources**.
[3:7] - **UNDP** is the UN development agency that leads SDG support work in countries; in India **NITI Aayog** coordinates.
[3:8] - **Andhra Pradesh** adopted **all 17 SDGs** with **212 indicators** to monitor progress to 2030; SDG targets are built into **Swarna Andhra @2047**.
[3:9] # Sustainable Development Report (UN SDSN)
[3:10] - **SDR 2025:** India **99th of 167** – first time in the top 100.
[3:11] - **SDR 2026:** India **94th of 169** (best ever); **Finland** first, Sweden and Denmark next.
[3:12] > Detail on each goal and the SDG India Index: see row 81.

## SUB 4 | Three Pillars and Types of Sustainability
[4:0] TABLE head: Pillar | Aim
  [4:0.0] Economic | Growth that lasts – NOT profit maximisation that ignores social or environmental impact
  [4:0.1] Social | Social equity / equality, inclusion, community development
  [4:0.2] Environmental | Reduce waste, conserve natural resources, protect ecosystems
[4:1] - UN wording: **economic growth, environmental protection, social equity** – interlinked; SD's key goal is to ensure all three together.
[4:2] - **Triple bottom line:** **People, Planet, Profit**.
[4:3] - **NOT a pillar:** political, military, cultural, technological, legal or financial.
[4:4] - "Social sustainability focuses exclusively on growth and jobs" ✗; "economic sustainability = maximising profits" ✗ (A false, R true).
[4:5] - **Sustainability** broadly = programmes and actions to preserve a particular resource; SD is achieved by social and economic improvement that protects the environment and supports equality (A/R: both true, R not the explanation).
[4:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society Group 2 Part 2 p.10 (parameters of SD); PMF Environment Part 6 p.6; app notes 5:80 sub 4
## SUB 5 | Principles, Nature and Scope
[5:0] TABLE head: Principle | Meaning
  [5:0.0] Intergenerational equity | Present must not deplete (natural) resources at the cost of future generations – "inter-generational sensibility" in the use of natural resources
  [5:0.1] Carrying capacity | Human use within what ecosystems can support – the Brundtland idea is intertwined with carrying capacity (UPSC 2010)
  [5:0.2] Precautionary principle | Lack of full scientific certainty is no reason to postpone measures against harm
  [5:0.3] Polluter pays | The polluter (person or company) bears the cost of preventing or remedying the damage – not the government or public
  [5:0.4] CBDR | Shared duty; developed nations carry more
  [5:0.5] Integration | Environment and economy are integrated, not opposites
[5:1] - **NOT a principle:** over-exploitation of resources, growth at the cost of environment, more pollution for industry.
[5:2] - **NCERT principles:** respect all life, improve quality of life, **conserve the earth's vitality and diversity**, **minimise depletion of natural resources**, change attitudes, let communities care for their own environment.
[5:3] - **Measures:** renewables, recycling, lower and more efficient energy use; all pollution minimised; consumption must **not exceed** the rate of regeneration.
[5:4] - **Challenges:** **over-consumption**; **increased use of fossil fuels** is a negative impact of technology.
[5:5] - **Education** helps by **promoting environmental awareness**; communication for SD aims at **transformative learning**.
[5:6] - **Environmental sustainability needs participatory, open and democratic decision-making**; **environmental protection** maintains (and restores) a healthy natural environment and **is not a luxury** at today's level of development.
[5:7] - SD includes a **constant or rising stock of capital assets**; natural **climatic change over time** is not part of the concept.
[5:8] - **Scope includes** meeting present needs, **equity within and across generations**, inclusion ("leave no one behind"); **excludes** growth that runs down natural capital and treating environment and development as opposites.
[5:9] - **Poverty alleviation** is printed as "not related to environmental protection" (UP 2013 key).
[5:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment Part 6 p.13-14 (carrying capacity); PMF Environment Part 4 p.48 (polluter pays); NCERT Human Geography (OnlyIAS) Part 10 p.25; NextGen Society Group 2 Part 2 p.10; app notes 5:80 sub 5-6

[6:0] TABLE head: Year | Milestone | Point
  [6:0.0] 1962 | Silent Spring – Rachel Carson | Set off the modern environmental movement

[6:3] - The Club of Rome did NOT propose the SDGs (UPSC 2016).

## SUB 7 | Measuring Sustainability – Natural Capital and Footprints
[7:0] TABLE head: Measure | Meaning
  [7:0.0] Natural capital | Resources created by nature – air, water, land, minerals, forests; roads are NOT (man-made)
  [7:0.1] Ecological footprint | Biologically productive land and sea area needed to supply a person's/population's consumption and absorb its wastes; unit global hectare
  [7:0.2] Earth Overshoot Day | Date when humanity's yearly demand exceeds what Earth can regenerate – a sign of environmental degradation; calculated by Global Footprint Network; conceived by Andrew Simms (New Economics Foundation), first campaign 2006
  [7:0.3] Green GDP | GDP – net natural capital consumption (depletion + degradation)
  [7:0.4] Genuine Progress Indicator | Adds unpaid work; subtracts pollution, crime, resource loss
[7:1] - Humanity's footprint ≈ **1.5 Earths**; India ≈ 0.9 (sustainable lifestyle).
[7:2] - Biota, biome and niche are not area measures.
[7:3] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 3 (OCR) p.16-17; Shankar Environment 19 (OCR) p.20; NextGen Rocket Environment p.43; PMF Environment Part 2 p.47-48; app notes 5:80 sub 7
