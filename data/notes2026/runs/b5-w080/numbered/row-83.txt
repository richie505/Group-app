## SUB 0 | Ozone Depletion – CFCs, Chlorine and Bromine
[0:0] # Cause
[0:1] - **CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons)** are the **main cause** of ozone depletion and of the "ozone hole" – not CO2, CO, methane, N2 or SO2.
[0:2] - **Full form:** CFC = **Chlorofluorocarbon** – carbon + chlorine + fluorine; entirely **man-made**, non-toxic, non-flammable.
[0:3] - **Mechanism:** under UV in the stratosphere CFCs release **chlorine**; **chlorine and bromine atoms destroy ozone catalytically** (one Cl atom breaks thousands of O3 molecules before it is locked up again) → more UV reaches the Earth.
[0:4] - **Bromine** comes from **halons** (fire extinguishers) – about **100 times more potent** than CFCs; **carbon tetrachloride** and **methyl chloroform** also release chlorine.
[0:5] - Depletion happens in the **stratosphere** (not in the lower atmosphere where humans live, not in the mesosphere).
[0:6] - **CFCs are also greenhouse gases** – they cause both warming and ozone loss (A-R: both true, but "CFCs are used in fridges, ACs, aerosols" is **not the explanation** of that).
[0:7] - **A–R (APPSC EO):** "ozone depletion = reduction of stratospheric ozone" ✓; "caused by high chlorine and bromine from CFCs and halons" ✓ → **R explains A**.
[0:8] - **Wrong statements trap (TSPSC JL):** ozone is in the **stratosphere, not mesosphere**; it is the **chlorine**, not the carbon, of CFCs that splits O3.
[0:9] # Sources of CFCs and related gases
[0:10] TABLE head: Gas | Main source
  [0:10.0] CFCs | Freon refrigerant, ACs, aerosol propellants, plastic foams, cleaning electronic parts
  [0:10.1] Halons | Fire extinguishers
  [0:10.2] Nitrogen oxides | Fertiliser plants, vehicles, combustion
  [0:10.3] Methane | Bacterial decomposition (wetlands, paddy, livestock)
[0:11] - **CFC uses (UPSC 2012):** **plastic foams, cleaning electronic components, aerosol pressurising agents** – **not tubeless tyres** → **1, 3 and 4**.
[0:12] > Recall angle (asked ~20 times: UPSC, UPPCS, UKPSC, MPPSC, APPSC G2, Forest, TSPSC): gas most responsible for ozone depletion = **CFC**.

## SUB 1 | Ozone Layer, Dobson Unit and the Ozone Hole
[1:0] # Ozone layer
[1:1] - **Ozone = O3** (three oxygen atoms); formed when **UV radiation splits O2** (UV + O2 → O3).
[1:2] - About **90%** of ozone is in the **stratosphere** (**10–50 km**; main layer ~15–30 km) – **protective**, absorbs harmful **UV (UV-B)**; ~10% is in the troposphere, where it is a **pollutant**.
[1:3] - Ozone protects the biosphere from **ultraviolet rays** – not infra-red, X-rays or gamma rays.
[1:4] - **Natural regulation:** stratospheric ozone is naturally regulated by **nitrogen oxides – "nitrogen dioxide"** in books and keys (the NO/NO2 cycle; N2O from soils is the source gas).
[1:5] # Dobson Unit
[1:6] - **Dobson Unit (DU)** measures the **thickness/concentration of ozone in a column of air from the ground to the top of the atmosphere** – not decibel (sound), angstrom (length), knot, poise or Maxwell.
[1:7] - **1 DU = 0.01 mm** of pure ozone at **0°C and 1 atm**; an **"ozone hole"** = total column ozone **below 220 DU**.
[1:8] # Ozone hole
[1:9] - Most affected region: **Antarctica**; seasonal thinning in the **Southern Hemisphere spring (Aug–Nov)**.
[1:10] - **Polar stratospheric clouds** in extreme cold turn stable chlorine compounds into **active chlorine**, which destroys ozone when sunlight returns.
[1:11] - Ozone began to **drop sharply in the 1980s**; the Antarctic hole was **reported in 1985 by the British Antarctic Survey** (NextGen: first noticed 1983).
[1:12] - **Tibetan ozone low ("halo") 2005:** discovered by **G.W. Kent Moore**, University of Toronto.
[1:13] - **Recovery:** NASA/WMO report healing of the Antarctic hole, mainly due to the **ban on CFCs** under Montreal.
[1:14] TABLE head: Region | Back to 1980 levels
  [1:14.0] Most of the world | 2040
  [1:14.1] Arctic | 2045
  [1:14.2] Antarctic | 2066

## SUB 2 | Vienna Convention and Montreal Protocol
[2:0] - **Vienna Convention (1985):** framework treaty for **protection of the ozone layer** – research, monitoring, reporting.
[2:1] - **Montreal Protocol:** the action treaty under Vienna to **phase out production and consumption of ozone-depleting substances (ODS)** – not GHGs/global warming, acid rain, smog, DDT, petrol or marine life.
[2:2] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [2:2.0] Signed | 16 September 1987, Montreal, Canada
  [2:2.1] In force | 1 January 1989 ("the 1989 treaty to protect the ozone layer")
  [2:2.2] Nature | Legally binding; adjustments/amendments: London, Copenhagen, Montreal, Beijing, Quito Adjustment, Kigali (2016)
  [2:2.3] Coverage | ~100 chemicals: CFCs, halons, CTC, methyl chloroform, HCFCs, methyl bromide
  [2:2.4] Parties | 198 – first and only UN treaties universally ratified (Vienna + Montreal)
  [2:2.5] Secretariat | Ozone Secretariat, Nairobi (UNEP)
[2:3] - Montreal is **not part of the Kyoto Protocol** (Kyoto belongs to UNFCCC) → statements "signed 1987", "phases out ODS", "universally ratified" are correct.
[2:4] - **UNEP's role:** **monitors and implements** international agreements like the Montreal Protocol (Ozone Secretariat).
[2:5] - **Results:** **~99% of ODS phased out** (2024); avoids **0.5–1°C** warming by 2050; widely called the most **successful** environmental treaty.
[2:6] - **Multilateral Fund (1991):** technical and financial aid to developing countries. **MOP37** focused on implementing **Kigali**.
[2:7] TABLE head: Treaty | Concern
  [2:7.0] Montreal Protocol | Ozone / ODS
  [2:7.1] Kyoto Protocol (1997, UNFCCC) | Binding GHG cuts by developed countries
  [2:7.2] Basel (1989) | Hazardous waste
  [2:7.3] Cartagena (2000, CBD) | Biosafety
  [2:7.4] Nagoya (2010, CBD) | Access and benefit sharing
  [2:7.5] Bretton Woods (1944) | IMF/World Bank – economic, not ozone
[2:8] > Montreal "related to / focussed on" asked 15 times; signing year **1987** (JL 2020 ×2).

## SUB 3 | ODS vs HFCs – Refrigerants, Foams and Fire Extinguishers
[3:0] TABLE head: Substance | ODS? | Main uses
  [3:0.0] CFCs (CFC-12 in refrigerators) | Yes – main culprit | Refrigerators, ACs, aerosols, foams, solvents
  [3:0.1] Halons (Halon-1211, 1301) | Yes – ~100× CFC | Portable fire extinguishers
  [3:0.2] HCFCs (HCFC-141b) | Yes, weaker – interim substitute | Foam blowing (HCFC-141b), ACs
  [3:0.3] CTC, methyl chloroform | Yes | Solvents, feedstock
  [3:0.4] Methyl bromide | Yes | Agricultural fumigation and quarantine
  [3:0.5] Nitrous oxide (N2O) | Not controlled under Montreal (books: "not responsible") | Fertilisers, laughing gas; a GHG
  [3:0.6] HFCs | No (non-ODS) – but very high GWP | ODS substitutes in ACs, refrigeration → Kigali
[3:1] - **HFCs** are entirely **man-made**; used as **aerosol propellants, foam-blowing agents and fire retardants/suppressants (clean agents)** – **not lubricants** → UPSC 2023: **only three**.
[3:2] # Refrigerants and extinguishers
[3:3] TABLE head: Item | Use
  [3:3.0] Freon (CFC; DuPont trade name, stable halocarbon) | Domestic refrigerators
  [3:3.1] Mafron (halogenated hydrocarbon, trade name) | Refrigerator gas (UKPSC 2010 key)
  [3:3.2] Liquid ammonia | Large cold-storage plants
  [3:3.3] Carbon dioxide | Best extinguisher for electrical fires (non-conducting)
  [3:3.4] Soda-acid extinguisher | NaHCO3 + dilute H2SO4 → CO2
[3:4] - **Ammonia** liquefies at ambient temperature only at **high pressure** (not low) → A true, R false (IAS 2000).
[3:5] - Radon, sodium, fluorine, neon, oxygen are not refrigerants.

## SUB 5 | Kigali Amendment and India's Ozone Action
[5:0] # Kigali Amendment (2016)
[5:1] - Adopted **2016, Kigali, Rwanda** (197 nations); **amends the Montreal Protocol** (not Kyoto, Bali or Paris); legally binding; in force **1 January 2019**.
[5:2] - Phases down **HFCs** – not ODS but potent GHGs (ACs, refrigeration); expected to avoid up to **0.5°C** warming by 2100; Parties cut HFCs **80–85%** from baselines by the late 2040s.
[5:3] TABLE head: Group | Baseline | Freeze | Final cut
  [5:3.0] Developed | 2011–13 | Reductions from 2019 | 85% by 2036
  [5:3.1] Group 1 (incl. China) | 2020–22 | 2024 | 80% by 2045
  [5:3.2] India (Group 2, with Gulf states, Iran, Iraq, Pakistan) | 2024–26 | 2028 | 85% by 2047
[5:4] - India **ratified Kigali in 2021**; MoEFCC: **no environmental clearance** for new/additional HFC production **after 31 December 2027**.
[5:5] # India's ozone action
[5:6] - **Ozone Cell (MoEFCC)** = India's **National Ozone Unit**; **ODS (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000** under the **Environment (Protection) Act**.
[5:7] TABLE head: Substance | Phased out in India by
  [5:7.0] Halons (except essential use) | 1 Jan 2001
  [5:7.1] CFCs in manufacturing (except MDIs) | 1 Jan 2003
  [5:7.2] CTC, methyl chloroform, CFCs in MDIs | 1 Jan 2010
  [5:7.3] Methyl bromide | 1 Jan 2015
  [5:7.4] HCFCs | Allowed up to 1 Jan 2040
[5:8] - **HPMP Stage-II:** India **first developing country** to fully phase out **HCFC-141b**, used by **foam manufacturing enterprises** (rigid foam) – the most potent ODS after CFCs.
[5:9] - **India Cooling Action Plan (MoEFCC, March 2019):** 20-year horizon to **2037–38**; cut **cooling demand 20–25%** and **refrigerant demand 25–30%**; six themes – space cooling in buildings, cold chain, servicing, domestic production, R&D, transport – **not coal-based power**.

## SUB 7 | Chemicals and Waste Conventions – Basel, Rotterdam, Stockholm, Minamata
[7:0] TABLE head: Convention | Year (in force) | Focus
  [7:0.0] Basel | 1989 (1992) | Transboundary movement of hazardous wastes and their disposal
  [7:0.1] Rotterdam | 1998 (2004) | Prior Informed Consent (PIC) for hazardous chemicals and pesticides in trade
  [7:0.2] Stockholm | 2001 (2004) | Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)
  [7:0.3] Minamata | 2013 (2017) | Mercury
[7:1] # Basel
[7:2] - India ratified **1992**; Basel's PIC operates in India under the **Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016**.
[7:3] - **Ban Amendment:** bans hazardous-waste exports from **OECD/EU + Liechtenstein** to other countries; in force **5 December 2019**. Wrong options: carbon emissions, wetlands, deforestation, biodiversity.
[7:4] # Rotterdam and Stockholm
[7:5] - **PIC:** the **importing country must agree** before a listed chemical is exported; Annex III lists **pesticides and industrial chemicals banned or severely restricted**. India **acceded 2005**; DNAs: **Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilisers + Ministry of Agriculture**.
[7:6] - **BRS:** Basel, Rotterdam, Stockholm share **one joint secretariat** under UNEP; India – MoEFCC **Hazardous Substances Management Division**.
[7:7] - **POPs:** **toxic, persistent, bioaccumulate, travel long distances**; **POPs Review Committee** assesses candidates. India ratified Stockholm **2006**; **7 more POPs ratified 2020**.
[7:8] - Initial **12 "dirty dozen"** (DDT, PCBs, dioxins, furans, aldrin…); now **37** listed.
[7:9] TABLE head: Annex | Meaning | Example
  [7:9.0] A | Elimination | Aldrin
  [7:9.1] B | Restriction | DDT (malaria control)
  [7:9.2] C | Unintentional by-products | Dioxins, furans
[7:10] - **2025 Annex A additions:** **chlorpyrifos** (neurotoxic insecticide – not in the dirty dozen), **long-chain PFCAs** ("**forever chemicals**"), medium-chain chlorinated paraffins.
[7:11] # Minamata (mercury)
[7:12] - Protects from **man-made emissions of mercury**; named after **Minamata, Japan** (factory wastewater mercury poisoned fish-eaters, 1950s).
[7:13] - **Mercury pollution (UPSC 2023):** **gold mining** ✓, **coal-based thermal power plants** ✓, **no known safe level of exposure** (WHO) ✓ → **all three**. Solar PV panels in normal operation are not a source.
[7:14] - **Dental amalgam** phase-out by **2034**. "Mercury – Rotterdam" is a wrong pair.
