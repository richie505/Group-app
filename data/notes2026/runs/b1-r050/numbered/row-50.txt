## SUB 0 | Capital Vijayapuri and the Dynasty at a Glance
[0:0] # Capital
[0:1] - **Capital:** **Vijayapuri** = modern **Nagarjunakonda**, in the Nagarjuna Sagar valley, now **Palnadu** district (asked 7 times: G2 2008 ×3, G2 2011 ×3, G2 2012).
[0:2] - **⚠ Trap:** not **Amaravati** (Dhanyakataka, a Satavahana centre), not Vengi (Salankayanas), not Vemulawada, Srikakulam or Pithunda.
[0:3] - **⚠ Option clash (G2 2012):** "Vijayapuri" and "Nagarjunakonda" both appear – the exam key is **Vijayapuri**, the ancient name of the capital.
[0:4] - **Name:** Vijayapuri is thought to be named after Satavahana **Vijaya Satakarni**; the hill was **Sriparvata**.
[0:5] - **Excavated** at Nagarjunakonda by the Andhra archaeologist **R. Subrahmanyam** (1954–60 rescue dig).
[0:6] - **Sites and ports:** **Ghantasala (Kantakasala)** and **Motupalli** = ports of the Ikshvaku period; Phanigiri (Suryapet, Telangana) – a pot of **3,730 lead coins** of the Ikshvaku period found in **2024**.
[0:7] # The dynasty in one look
[0:8] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [0:8.0] Successors of | Satavahanas in the Krishna valley (3rd c. CE); first their feudatories (Mahatalavaras)
  [0:8.1] Period | About 75 years, c. AD 225–300 (3rd century CE)
  [0:8.2] Kings | Puranas: 7; inscriptions confirm 4
  [0:8.3] Symbol / court language | Lion / Prakrit
  [0:8.4] Official religion | Vedic-Brahmanical (Shaivism); Buddhism patronised by the queens
[0:9] - **Immediate successors of the Satavahanas** in the Krishna valley = **Ikshvakus**, not the Pallavas or Vishnukundins.
[0:10] - **Bridge dynasty:** carried the **Amaravati school to its peak**, built the **first temples** and moved Andhra towards an **agrarian, Puranic order**; they did **not** replace Buddhism with Jainism.
[0:11] - **Deccan dynasty that promoted Buddhism** (G2 2000 backlog): the **Ikshvakus** – their period is the golden age of Buddhism in Andhra.

## SUB 1 | Rulers – Founder to Last King
[1:0] TABLE head: No. | King | Exam fact
  [1:0.0] 1 | Vasishthiputra Sri Santamula (Chamtamula, Shantamula I) | Founder; overthrew the last Satavahana Pulomavi III; Vedic sacrifices
  [1:0.1] 2 | Mathariputra Virapurushadatta | Greatest king; golden age of Buddhism; Sri Parvatadhipati, "Ashoka of the South"
  [1:0.2] 3 | Ehuvala Santamula (Shantamula II) | Son of Virapurushadatta and Bhatti Mahadevi; ruled about 24 years; first Sanskrit inscriptions
  [1:0.3] 4 | Rudrapurushadatta | Last king; Gurajala (Gurazala) inscription
[1:1] - **Founder:** **Vasishthiputra Santamula** (ASO 2011) – not Vishnuvardhana, Satakarni or Ganapatideva.
[1:2] - **Lineage:** **Veera Purushadatta** belongs to the **Ikshvakus** (G1 1995) – not the Rashtrakutas, Vemulawada Chalukyas or Salankayanas.
[1:3] - **Last king:** **Rudrapurushadatta** (G2 2019 ×2); Sri Chantamula, Virapurushadatta, Ehuvala are distractors.
[1:4] - **Gurajala (Gurazala) inscription:** issued by **Rudrapurushadatta** (APPSC PYQ ×2) – Jaggayyapeta belongs to Virapurushadatta.
[1:5] # Vasishthiputra Santamula – Vedic king and agrarian patron
[1:6] - **Sacrifices:** **Ashvamedha, Vajapeya, Agnishtoma, Agnihotra, Rajasuya**; evidence = the **Ashvamedha platform** found at **Nagarjunakonda**.
[1:7] - **Intelligentsia in farming:** **Sri Chantamula** tried to make the **intelligentsia partners in agricultural development** (G2 2012 ×2) – gifts of land, ploughs, bullocks and cows to Brahmins.
[1:8] - **Titles:** **Gosatasahasra**, **Halasatasahasra** (Satasahasrahalaka), **Mahadanapati** – giver of lakhs of cows and ploughs; crores of gold coins.
[1:9] - **Inscriptions:** Rentala, Kesanapalli, Dechepalli.
[1:10] # Virapurushadatta
[1:11] - **Sri Parvatadhipati** (lord of Sriparvata) = **Virapurushadatta** (APPSC PYQ ×2) – not Shantamula I or II or Rudrapurushadatta.
[1:12] - Only Ikshvaku king to turn to **Buddhism**; his queens and aunts were great Buddhist donors.
[1:13] # Ehuvala Santamula and Rudrapurushadatta
[1:14] - **Ehuvala** was named after his **grandfather** (Santamula) – an Ikshvaku naming custom; under him **Sanskrit** entered the records and Brahmanical temples rose beside Buddhist ones.
[1:15] - **Gummadidurru** inscription = Ehuvala Santamula.
[1:16] - **Rudrapurushadatta:** Gurajala record (land grant to **Halampuraswami**, i.e. Nagulapuram, Palnadu); defeated by Pallava **Simhavarman** (Manchikallu).
[1:17] ~~ Sources: NextGen Ikshvakus p.1, p.4, p.10-14; Rocket AP History sheet "Inscriptions" (Gurazala), #16 (founders); NextGen Previous Papers p.8, p.13, p.28; app notes 1:50 (sub 4)
## SUB 2 | Puranic Names, Origin Theories and the Ayodhya Claim
[2:0] # Puranic names
[2:1] - **Sriparvatiyas (Sri Parvatiya Andhras):** the **Puranas** call the Ikshvakus "Sriparvatiyas" (UPPCS 2020) – not the Vakatakas, Sakas or Kharavela.
[2:2] - They are also called **Andhrabhrityas** – a Puranic name of the dynasty, not a title of Ehuvala Santamula.
[2:3] # Origin theories
[2:4] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent
  [2:4.0] Andhra / Telugu people | Bishop Caldwell (G2 2011 ×2) – the accepted view
  [2:4.1] Tamil | K. Gopalachari
  [2:4.2] Kannada | Vogel
  [2:4.3] North Indian | Rapson, Buhler
  [2:4.4] Name from ikshu (sugarcane, their tribal symbol) | B.S.L. Hanumantha Rao
[2:5] - **Proof of Andhra origin:** southern names (Bapi, Adavi, Chelike) + the southern custom of marrying the **maternal uncle's daughter** (cross-cousin marriage).
[2:6] - **⚠ Trap:** V.A. Smith and Oldenberg are distractors; Gopalachari = Tamil, not Telugu.
[2:7] # The Ayodhya claim (G2 Mains 2025)
[2:8] - **Incorrect statement (official final key):** "In their inscriptions, the Ikshvakus of Vijayapuri described themselves as descendants of the **Ikshvakus of Ayodhya**."
[2:9] - **True statements in the same question:** Puranas call them **Sri Parvathiyas**; matrimonial ties with the **Sakas / Western Satraps of Ujjain**; **Virapurushadatta's** reign = golden age of Buddhism in Andhra.
[2:10] - The Ayodhya link comes from later texts (Ramayana, Puranas, the Kannada Jain *Dharmamrita* of Nyayasena), not from a clear claim in the Vijayapuri records.
[2:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Ikshvakus p.1, p.8, p.10; APPSC G2 Mains 2025 Paper 1 final key (Q3, Series A, https://bloomappsc.com/documents/G2_MAINS_2025_P1_SeriesA_FINAL_KEY.pdf); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andhra_Ikshvaku
## SUB 3 | Marriage Alliances
[3:0] - **Princess of Ujjain:** **Virapurushadatta** married **Rudradhara Bhattarika**, daughter of the Saka (Western Kshatrapa) ruler of **Ujjain** (Rudrasena II) → tie with the **Sakas / Western Kshatrapas** (APPSC PYQ).
[3:1] - **⚠ Trap:** she came from Ujjain (Sakas), not from the Pallavas; not the wife of Santamula or Ehuvala.
[3:2] - **Santamula's sister Santisri** married **Skandasri of the Pugiya** family; his daughter **Adavi Santisri** married **Skanda Visakha of the Dhanika** family.
[3:3] - **Virapurushadatta's daughter Kodabalisri** married **Vishnurudra Sivalananda Satakarni of the Chutus**.
[3:4] - **Cross-cousin marriage** (maternal uncle's daughter) was practised – Virapurushadatta married his aunt's daughters **Bapisri** and **Shastisri**.

## SUB 4 | Inscriptions and the End of the Dynasty
[4:0] TABLE head: Inscription | Issuer | What it shows
  [4:0.0] Alluru | Virapurushadatta (NextGen; Rocket names Ehuvala) | Gifts to many sections and institutions; Ikshvakus as Satavahana vassals
  [4:0.1] Nagarjunakonda | Virapurushadatta and royal women | Founding of the kingdom at Vijayapuri by Santamula; Buddhist gifts (ayaka pillars)
  [4:0.2] Jaggayyapeta | Virapurushadatta | Ikshvaku rule in the lower Krishna
  [4:0.3] Gummadidurru | Ehuvala Santamula | Later Ikshvaku rule
  [4:0.4] Gurajala | Rudrapurushadatta | Last Ikshvaku king
  [4:0.5] Manchikallu | Pallava Simhavarman | Found near Vijayapuri; Rudrapurushadatta defeated → Pallava rule
  [4:0.6] Mayidavolu plates | Sivaskandavarman (crown prince, from Kanchi) | Grant concerning Dhanyakataka → Pallava hold on the Krishna–Guntur region
[4:1] - **Who ended the Ikshvakus:** **D.C. Sircar** – Simhavarman helped bring them down (end of the 3rd c. CE); **T.V. Mahalingam** – Simhavarman bore no royal title, **Sivaskandavarman I** completed the takeover.
[4:2] - An **Abhira** inscription at the capital is read by some as an Abhira occupation; by the mid-4th century the **Pallavas** held the region south of the Krishna, the **Brihatphalayanas** the north.
[4:3] - **Firsts:** first dated inscriptions (year, month, day) and first sculptors' names; first structural Hindu temples (Pushpabhadraswami, Ashtabhuja Narayana at Nagarjunakonda).
[4:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Ikshvakus p.8-9, p.14; Rocket AP History sheet "Inscriptions" (Manchikallu, Alluru); Powerplay #1 Part 8; app notes 1:50 (sub 7, 8)
