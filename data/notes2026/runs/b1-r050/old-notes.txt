=== NOTES 1:50 | Ikshvakus – Political History, Vasishthiputra Chamtamula and Virapurushadatta
## SUB 0 | The Ikshvakus – an Overview
[50:0:0] - **Ikshvakus of Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda)** = **successors of the Satavahanas** in the **3rd century CE**.
[50:0:1] - They were first **feudatories (lords) under the Satavahanas** and became free after the Satavahana decline.
[50:0:2] - Duration: about **75 years** (c. **AD 225–300**).
[50:0:3] - Number of kings: **Puranas list 7**; **inscriptions confirm 4**.
[50:0:4] - Symbol: **Lion**; royal language: **Prakrit**.
[50:0:5] - A **bridge dynasty**: took the **Amaravati school to its peak**, built the **first temples**, and moved Andhra towards a **farming (agrarian), Puranic order**.
[50:0:6] - They did **not** replace Buddhism with Jainism.
## SUB 1 | Position in the Sequence of Andhra Dynasties
[50:1:0] TABLE head: Order | Dynasty | Note
  [50:1:0.0] 1 | Satavahanas | Ikshvakus were their feudatories
  [50:1:0.1] 2 | Ikshvakus | Vijayapuri, 3rd c. CE
  [50:1:0.2] 3 | Brihatpalayanas | Capital Koduru (Kuduru) near Machilipatnam; Kondamudi plates of Jayavarma (near Tenali)
  [50:1:0.3] 4 | Shalankayanas | Vengi
  [50:1:0.4] 5 | Vishnukundins | –
  [50:1:0.5] 6 | Pallavas | Took over the Krishna–Guntur region
  [50:1:0.6] 7 | Eastern Chalukyas | Vengi
[50:1:1] Immediate successors of the Satavahanas in the Krishna valley = Ikshvakus, not the Pallavas or Vishnukundins.
## SUB 2 | Origin Debate
[50:2:0] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent
  [50:2:0.0] Telugu/Andhra people | Caldwell (G2 2011 key); Gopalachari suggested Tamil links
  [50:2:0.1] North Indian | Rapson, Buhler
  [50:2:0.2] Kannada | Vogel
  [50:2:0.3] Kshatriya (from Kshatriya yajnas) or mixed Kshatriya-Brahmin | D.R. Bhandarkar
  [50:2:0.4] Name from ikshu (sugarcane) | B.S.L. Hanumantha Rao
[50:2:1] - **Andhra origin proof**: southern names + the southern custom of marrying the **maternal uncle's daughter** (cross-cousin marriage).
[50:2:2] - Tradition links the name to the **Solar/Rama (Ayodhya)** line; they **claimed descent from the Ikshvakus of Ayodhya** (a true statement in G2 2025).
[50:2:3] G2 2025 key: "Puranas call them Sri Parvathiyas" = **incorrect** statement (key: Andhra Bhrityas); Ayodhya descent, Saka marriage ties and Virapurushadatta's Buddhism were the true statements.
## SUB 3 | Capital, Sites and Ports
[50:3:0] TABLE head: Place | Feature
  [50:3:0.0] Vijayapuri | Capital = modern Nagarjunakonda, in the Nagarjuna Sagar valley, now Palnadu district; not at Amaravati
  [50:3:0.1] Nagarjunakonda | Excavated by R. Subrahmanyam; amphitheatre (possibly built with Roman help), Ashvamedha platform, many Roman coins
  [50:3:0.2] Ghantasala (Kantakasala) | Ports
[50:3:1] - Nagarjunakonda (Palnadu) is the main **Ikshvaku heritage site** of AP.
[50:3:2] - **Name:** Vijayapuri is thought to be named after Satavahana **Vijaya Satakarni**; the hill was **Sriparvata**.
[50:3:3] - **Phanigiri** (Suryapet, Telangana): pot of **3,730 lead coins** of the Ikshvaku period found in **2024**.
## SUB 4 | Rulers – Period and Contribution
[50:4:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Contribution
  [50:4:0.0] Vasishthiputra (Sri) Chamtamula / Santamula / Shrisantmula | Founder at Vijayapuri; first a Satavahana feudatory; Vedic yajnas; made the intelligentsia (scholars) partners in agricultural development (G2 2012) by gifts of land, ploughs, bullocks and cows to Brahmins
  [50:4:0.1] Mathariputra Virapurushadatta | Golden age of Buddhism; queens great donors; Alluru and Nagarjunakonda inscriptions; title Sri Parvatadhipati
  [50:4:0.2] Ehuvala Chamtamula | Son of Virapurushadatta and Bhatti Mahadevi; ruled about 24 years; first Sanskrit inscriptions in the Telugu lands; Gummadidurru inscription
  [50:4:0.3] Rudrapurushadatta | Last ruler; issued the Gurajala (Gurazala) inscription
[50:4:1] # Vasishthiputra Chamtamula – Vedic Sacrifices
[50:4:2] - **Ashvamedha, Vajapeya, Agnishtoma, Agnihotra** and **Rajasuya**.
[50:4:3] - Evidence: the **Ashvamedha platform** found at **Nagarjunakonda**.
[50:4:4] - Titles **Gosatasahasra** and **Halasatasahasra** = giver of lakhs of cows and ploughs.
[50:4:5] # Virapurushadatta – Buddhism
[50:4:6] - The Ikshvaku period = **golden age of Buddhism in Andhra**; Nagarjunakonda (Vijayapuri) a great Buddhist centre.
[50:4:7] - The king was Vedic, but **his queens** were important Buddhist donors.
[50:4:8] # Ehuvala Chamtamula and Rudrapurushadatta
[50:4:9] - Ehuvala was named after his **grandfather** (Chamtamula), a common Ikshvaku naming custom.
[50:4:10] - Under him **Sanskrit** began to replace Prakrit in records, and Brahmanical temples rose beside Buddhist monuments.
[50:4:11] - **Rudrapurushadatta**, the last king, is known from the **Gurajala** record; after him the Krishna valley passed to new powers.
## SUB 5 | Titles and Puranic Names
[50:5:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [50:5:0.0] Sri Parvatiyas (Sri Parvatiya Andhras) | Puranic name of the dynasty
  [50:5:0.1] Andhrabhrityas | Puranic name of the dynasty in general – not a title of Ehuvala Chamtamula
  [50:5:0.2] Gosatasahasra, Halasatasahasra | Titles of Chamtamula
  [50:5:0.3] Sri Parvatadhipati | Title of Virapurushadatta
[50:5:1] An APPSC Group-2 key accepted "Puranas call them **Andhra Bhrityas**" and marked the "Sri Parvathiyas" statement incorrect; the UPPCS 2020 key accepted "**Shriparvatiya** in the Puranas". Follow the exam's key.
## SUB 6 | Matrimonial Alliances
[50:6:0] - **Virapurushadatta** married **Rudrabhattarika (Rudradhara Bhattarika)**, princess of **Ujjain**, daughter of the **Saka** ruler → tie with the **Sakas / Western Kshatrapas** (G2 2025).
[50:6:1] - **Cross-cousin marriage** (maternal uncle's daughter) was practised.
## SUB 7 | Inscriptions on Succession and Replacement
[50:7:0] TABLE head: Inscription | Issuer | What it shows
  [50:7:0.0] Alluru | Virapurushadatta | Donations to many communities and institutions
  [50:7:0.1] Nagarjunakonda | Virapurushadatta and queens | Buddhist gifts; ayaka-pillar records
  [50:7:0.2] Jaggayyapeta | Virapurushadatta | Ikshvaku rule in the lower Krishna
  [50:7:0.3] Gummadidurru | Ehuvala Chamtamula | Later Ikshvaku rule
  [50:7:0.4] Gurajala | Rudrapurushadatta | Last Ikshvaku king
  [50:7:0.5] Manchikallu | Pallava Simhavarman | Earliest Pallava record; found near Vijayapuri → Pallava conquest that ended the Ikshvakus (end of 3rd c. CE)
  [50:7:0.6] Mayidavolu plates | Sivaskandavarman (as crown prince, from Kanchi) | Grant concerning Dhanyakataka → Pallava control of the Krishna–Guntur region
[50:7:1] # Who Ended the Ikshvakus?
[50:7:2] - **D.C. Sircar**: the Manchikallu findspot near Vijayapuri shows **Simhavarman** helped bring down the Ikshvakus around the end of the 3rd or start of the 4th century CE.
[50:7:3] - **T.V. Mahalingam**: Simhavarman had no royal title in that record; **Sivaskandavarman I** completed the takeover.
[50:7:4] - An **Abhira** inscription at the Ikshvaku capital is read by some as an Abhira occupation of the kingdom.
[50:7:5] - By the **mid-4th century** the **Pallavas** held the former Ikshvaku territory.
[50:7:6] - Ikshvaku rule ended after clashes with the **Abhiras, Traikutakas and Brihatpalayanas**, with the **Pallavas** finally taking the region.
## SUB 8 | Ikshvaku Firsts and Lasts
[50:8:0] - The Ikshvakus stand between the **Satavahana trade economy** and the **early medieval temple-and-agrahara order** – this is why examiners call them a **bridge dynasty**.
[50:8:1] - **Earliest Hindu temples** in Andhra: **Pushpabhadraswami**, **Ashtabhuja Narayana** at Nagarjunakonda.
[50:8:2] - **First dated inscriptions naming sculptors**.
[50:8:3] - **First Sanskrit inscription** in the Telugu lands: **Ehuvala Chamtamula**.
[50:8:4] - Last ruler: **Rudrapurushadatta**.

[54:7:0] - **Indrabhattaraka Varma** was defeated by Maukhari **Ishanavarma**, whose son married his daughter (**Jaunpur inscription**).

[54:7:2] - **End:** **Pulakeshin II** (Badami Chalukya) defeated **Manchana Bhattaraka** at **Kunala (Kolleru)**, **624 CE**.
