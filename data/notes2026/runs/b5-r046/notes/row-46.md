=== Space Situational Awareness – NETRA, IS4OM and Digantara
# Space Situational Awareness (SSA)
- **SSA:** the capability to **detect, track, monitor and predict** the movement of objects in space (satellites, debris); used for collision avoidance and space security.
| System | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Project NETRA** (Network for Space Object Tracking and Analysis) | **ISRO**'s indigenous SSA system; aim = **safeguard Indian space assets** from debris and collisions; 24–72 hours' warning |
| **SSA Control Centre** | foundation stone laid by **ISRO** in **August 2019** at **Bengaluru** (Peenya, ISTRAC campus); the NETRA control centre |
| **IS4OM** | **ISRO System for Safe and Sustainable Space Operations Management**; dedicated to the nation in **2022**; compiles the annual **ISSAR** |
| **Digantara Research and Technologies** | private SSA start-up based in **Bengaluru**; authorised by **IN-SPACe** on **24 June 2022** to launch its payloads; space-surveillance satellites, debris tracking |
- **ISSAR 2025 (8 Apr 2026):** **18 collision avoidance manoeuvres** (CAMs) by Indian satellites in 2025; **RISAT-2B** avoided a defunct US satellite in 2019.
> NETRA is asked twice (APPSC JL 2020, DAO 2020): agency **ISRO** (not RBI, ICSSR, ICHR) and aim **safeguard Indian space assets** (not a space station, Gaganyaan or the Vikram lander search).

=== Space Debris and Re-entry
# Space debris
- **Space debris:** all **non-functional man-made objects**, including fragments, in orbit or re-entering (defunct satellites, spent rocket stages, collision and explosion fragments, paint flakes).
- **Main source = fragmentation** from collisions and in-orbit explosions; **ASAT tests** add debris (China 2007; **Mission Shakti**, DRDO, 27 Mar 2019, Microsat-R at ~300 km).
- Over **1.2 million objects > 1 cm** (ESA 2025); objects move at ~7–8 km/s in LEO.
- **Kessler Syndrome** (Donald Kessler, NASA, **1978**): debris density → collisions → more fragments → a self-sustaining cascade that can make orbits unusable.
- **MMOD** (micrometeoroids and orbital debris) is the top risk in **LEO**, not GEO.
# Controlled re-entry
- **POEM-4** (PSLV Orbital Experimental Module of **PSLV-C60 / SpaDeX**): re-entered and impacted the **Indian Ocean on 4 April 2025** (ISRO: controlled re-entry).
- **Mir** (Russian station, ~140 t): de-orbited in a controlled re-entry over the Pacific on **23 March 2001** after ~15 years, having made **86,331 orbits**.

=== Mitigation and Debris-free Missions
- **Debris Free Space Missions (DFSM) by 2030** – India's pledge at the **2024 IADC** meeting.
- **25-year rule:** LEO satellites should re-enter within 25 years of mission end (NASA, then IADC and UN guidelines); GEO satellites go to a **graveyard orbit**.
- **Active Debris Removal:** a servicer with a **robotic arm or net** to capture a defunct Indian rocket body (tested at URSC).
- **PSLV-C62** (Jan 2026) planned a PS4 de-boost; **2026 re-entry rules:** warn aircraft and ships **at least 45 days** before re-entry.
- **Gaps:** IADC/UN guidelines are non-binding; no international **duty-of-care** standard; lax licensing regimes attract operators.

=== Space Law – Treaties and the Space Activities Bill
| Instrument | Core rule | India |
|---|---|---|
| **Outer Space Treaty 1967** | **No national sovereignty**; **Art. VI** national responsibility incl. private actors; **Art. VII** liability; Art. IX due regard | Party (ratified **1982**) |
| **Rescue Agreement 1968** | Return of astronauts | Party |
| **Liability Convention 1972** | **Absolute** liability on Earth; **fault** liability in space | Party |
| **Registration Convention 1975** | Register space objects with the UN | Party |
| **Moon Agreement 1979** | Common heritage | **Signed, not ratified** |
- The 1960s–70s treaties do not cover cumulative debris or mega-constellations.
# Space Activities Bill (draft, 2017)
- Aims to **facilitate the overall growth of space activities** in India; a **regulatory body to license** space activities (non-governmental too); protection of **IPR** created through space activity; liability and penalties.
- It does **not** create a **military wing of the armed forces in outer space** (the incorrect statement, Sachivalayam PYQ).
- India still has **no Space Activities Act** on liability.
