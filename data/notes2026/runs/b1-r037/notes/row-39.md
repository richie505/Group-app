=== Kharavela of Kalinga – the Hathigumpha Inscription
- **Hathigumpha inscription** (2nd–1st c. BCE): the year-by-year record of **Kharavela** of Kalinga (UPPSC 2018) – not Ashoka, Harsha or Kanishka; it is not the Girnar, Junagadh or Sarnath inscription.
- **Place:** **Udayagiri** hill near Bhubaneswar, Odisha – **not Khandagiri**.
- **Udayagiri and Khandagiri:** both natural and artificial caves; **Udayagiri 18, Khandagiri 15** caves (CGPSC 2021: statements i and ii correct).
- **Dynasty:** **Mahameghavahana** family of the **Chedi** line (UPPCS 2018 keys Mahameghavahana; Chedi names the same line).
- **Religion:** a **Jaina** – the king who "heavily leaned towards Jainism" (not Dasharatha, Brihadratha or Huvishka); gave caves to Jain monks; brought back from Magadha the **Jina image** taken by the Nandas.
- **Campaigns:** sent an army west **ignoring Satakarni**; raided Magadha; southern victories; canal work begun by a Nanda king.
- **Krishna river = "Kannabenna"** (Kanhabemna) in the Hathigumpha inscription (APPSC AP History).
- **Other pairs:** Nasik – Gautami Balashri; **Nanaghat – Naganika**; Junagadh – Rudradaman.

=== End of the Dynasty and the Successors
- **Brihadratha**, the last Maurya and **7th king after Ashoka**, was killed by his **senani Pushyamitra Sunga** at an **army review** – a military coup.
- **Immediate successors of the Mauryas in Magadha: the Sungas** (APPSC G2 2003, 2008) – not Kushanas, Pandyas or Satavahanas; Sunga dynasty founded **187 BC** (NextGen; PMF: 185 BCE).
- **In the Deccan the Mauryas were succeeded by the Satavahanas** (APPSC ASO) – earlier Mauryan feudatories; founder **Simuka (Srimukha)**; the Puranas call them **Andhra-bhrityas**.
- **Pushyamitra:** Brahmana of the **Bharadvaja gotra**, **Senapati**; two Ashvamedhas (**Ayodhya inscription of Dhanadeva**); **Malavikagnimitram** – grandson **Vasumitra** beat a Yavana army on the Sindhu.
- **Kanvas:** **Vasudeva Kanva** killed the last Sunga **Devabhuti**; **four** kings (c. 73–28 BC); the last, **Susharman**, fell to the **Satavahanas**, not Menander.
- **Persecution debate:** the Divyavadana calls Pushyamitra a persecutor, yet **Sanchi and Bharhut** grew under the Sungas.

=== Theories of Decline → Historian
| Theory | Historian | Core argument |
|---|---|---|
| Brahmanical reaction | **H.P. Shastri** | Pro-Buddhist policy and ban on sacrifices angered Brahmins; Sungas and Kanvas were Brahmins |
| Ashoka's pacifism | **H.C. Raychaudhuri** | Non-violence after Kalinga dulled the army |
| Financial crisis | **D.D. Kosambi** | Army, bureaucracy and gifts to the Sangha emptied the treasury; heavy taxes even on actors and prostitutes |
| Over-centralisation, no national consciousness | **Romila Thapar** | Rule depended on the king; weak successors |
| Oppressive provincial rule | **Divyavadana** evidence | Revolts at Taxila against wicked officials |
- **Financial-crisis evidence:** spending on **Dhamma missions** and the **Sangha**; **debasement of later punch-marked silver coins**; gold images melted.
- **Other factors:** spread of iron and material culture to outlying areas; neglect of the **north-west frontier** while China built the Great Wall (c. 220 BC).

=== Later Mauryas, Division and Foreign Threats
- **Later Mauryas:** Kunala, Dasharatha, **Samprati** (Jain patron), Shalishuka, Devavarman, Shatadhanvan, **Brihadratha** – the Vishnu Purana lists seven successors.
| Part / threat | Ruler / power |
|---|---|
| Western part | **Kunala, later Samprati**; Indo-Greeks supplanted it by **180 BC** |
| Eastern part | Pataliputra – down to Brihadratha |
| North-west | Bactrian Greeks **Demetrius** and **Menander** |
| Hindu Kush, c. 206 BC | **Antiochus III** took tribute from **Subhagasena** |
| Great Wall of China (c. 220 BC) | Diverted Scythian tribes westward and southward towards India |
| Andhra / Deccan | **Satavahanas**, former feudatories |
| Vidarbha | **Yajnasena** (Malavikagnimitram) |
- **Menander = Milinda**; capital **Sagala (Sialkot)**; dialogue with **Nagasena** in the Milindapanha.
- **Not a cause:** a permanent alliance with the Indo-Greeks.
