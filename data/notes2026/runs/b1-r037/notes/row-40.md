|---|---|

| Andhapatha (also cited) | Vinayavatthu, in some lists |

- **First inscriptional "Andhrapatha":** Mayidavolu copper plates of **Sivaskandavarma** (c. 300 CE).

=== Andhras in Vedic, Epic and Greek Sources
| Source | Reference |
|---|---|
| **Aitareya Brahmana** | **First mention of the word "Andhra"** – as a race, in the **Sunahshepa** story: **Vishwamitra** cursed his **50** sons to become Andhras, Pundras, Pulindas (APPSC SI 2011) |
| **Rigveda** | **No mention** of the Andhras – the text that does not mention the Telugu people (APPSC G2 2002); the Arthashastra and Rajatarangini do |
| **Mahabharata** | Andhras fought on the **Kaurava** side (APPSC 2011; Tribal Welfare 2012) |
| Vayu Purana | Places the Andhra people in the Deccan |
| **Megasthenes' Indica** | The **Greek ambassador** who mentions the Andhras – first foreign source; **30 walled towns** (first to mention Andhra forts), **1,00,000 infantry, 2,000 cavalry, 1,000 elephants** (report preserved by **Pliny**) |
| **Ashoka's 13th Rock Edict** | Andhras among peoples within the empire following his Dhamma |
- **Megasthenes** was the envoy of **Seleucus** – not Pliny, Ptolemy or Marco Polo, who wrote later.
~~ Sources: NextGen AP History 01 p.6-8; app notes 1:40 (sub 0, 1, 6)

=== Early Buddhist Centres and Excavated Sites
| Site | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Bhattiprolu** (Guntur/Bapatla) | **Oldest stupa of Andhra**; very large bricks; dome **132 ft** across on a base of 148 ft (the figure APPSC asked as its "height"); relic caskets name **Raja Kubhiraka (Kuberaka)**, ruler of Bhattiprolu in the 3rd c. BCE |
| **Dhanyakataka** (Amaravati–Dharanikota) | On the Krishna in **Andhra**; Buddhist centre of the **Mahasanghikas**; capital of the **later Satavahanas**; Buddhism here predates the Mauryas (UPSC 2023) |
| **Gottiprolu** (Nellore region) | **ASI dig 2019**: early-historic **trade centre** in a massive brick wall (2nd–1st c. BCE), near an ancient port; imitation amphorae; declared a **Monument of National Importance** (July 2026) |
| Gudiwada Dibba (Krishna) | Early historic mound; occupation from Mauryan to Satavahana times; Siddharthaka chaitya identified with **Gudivada** |
| Adurru (East Godavari) | Mahasanghika Buddhist centre |
| Vaddamanu (near Amaravati) | **Jain** stupa, excavated 1981–85 |
| Sriparvata / Vijayapuri | Nagarjunakonda |
- **Guntupalli** = Rajagirika centre; **P.C. Ray** identified the river at Amaravati-Dharanikota as the Krishna.

=== Assaka, Mulaka and the Bavari Legend
| Janapada | Capital | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Assaka (Asmaka)** | **Bodhan (Potali / Podana)** (APPSC G2 2012) – not Paithan, Kotilingala or Dhanyakataka | The only Mahajanapada in the Deccan, on the Godavari |
| Mulaka | **Pratishthana** | Neighbour of Assaka |
- **Bavari:** a Brahmin teacher from **Kosala** who settled on the **Godavari** in Assaka territory; sent **16 disciples** to the Buddha (Suttanipata) – Buddhism reached the Godavari region **before the Mauryan expansion**.
- **Trade:** Andhra lay on the **Dakshinapatha**; merchant guilds used Kalingapatnam and later Masulipatnam.
~~ Sources: NextGen AP History 01 p.6; app notes 1:40 (sub 4, 5)

=== Ashokan Edicts and Mauryan Contact
- **Before the Satavahanas** Andhra was already a distinct people and region; by the 3rd c. BCE it was ruled by **Mauryan feudatories**, and the Mauryan phase made Buddhism **institutional** here.
| Site | District | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Erragudi** | Kurnool (now Nandyal) | Major and minor rock edicts; teachers must inculcate **Dhamma** in pupils |
| **Rajula Mandagiri** | Kurnool | **Minor** rock edict (not a pillar edict) |
| **Amaravati** | Guntur | Fragment of an Ashokan **pillar** edict |
- **Mahadeva Bhikshu:** by Andhra tradition sent by Ashoka to **Amaravati** – first Buddhist teacher in Andhra; enshrined the Buddha's bone relics.
- **End of Mauryan rule:** Andhra rulers declared independence; the **Satavahanas** rose.
~~ Sources: NextGen AP History 01 p.6; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.141; app notes 1:40 (sub 7)
