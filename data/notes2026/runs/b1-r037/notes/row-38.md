=== Stupas – Sanchi, Bharhut and the Stupa Cult
- **Stupa cult:** the idea predates Buddhism (Vedic burial mounds); **Ashoka popularised it** – by tradition he redistributed the relics of the **8** original stupas into **84,000**. Stupas held relics of the Buddha and monks and stood in towns **near trade routes**, funded by merchants and guilds – **not in rural areas** (CDS 2018).
- **Sanchi Stupa was built by Ashoka** (brick core) (MPPSC 1995; UPPCS 1991) – not Chandragupta, Kautilya, Bimbisara, Harsha or Pushyamitra.
| Stupa | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Sanchi** (ancient **Kakanaya / Kaknam**) | Raisen district, MP, near Vidisha; Ashoka's brick core → **Sunga** stone casing, railings and toranas → **Satavahana** carving of gateways → Gupta shrines; considered the **best** stupa (UPPCS 2008); **oldest stone structure in India** among Ajanta, Konark, Brihadeeswara |
| Sanchi No. 2 / No. 3 | Relics of 10 monks (incl. Dhamma Mahamatras) / **Sariputta and Mahamoggallana** |
| **Bharhut** | Satna, MP; **Sunga**, c. 150 BCE; found by Cunningham |
| Dharmarajika | Sarnath and Taxila |
| **Dhamek** | Sarnath; present form **Gupta** (c. 500 CE) |
| Kanakamuni | Niglihawa (Nepal); enlarged in Ashoka's 14th year |
| Ranabhar | Kushinagar; built by the Mallas |
| Kesariya | East Champaran; largest (about 104 ft) |
| Amaravati | Right bank of the Krishna, AP; traced by Colin Mackenzie, 1797 |
- **Chronological order (UPPCS Mains 2014):** **Bharhut → Sanchi → Amaravati → Dhamek** (Bharhut and Sanchi stone work 2nd–1st c. BCE, Amaravati's peak 1st–2nd c. CE, Dhamek Gupta). The old key "Dhamek, Bharhut, Amaravati, Sanchi" puts the Gupta stupa first and is wrong.
- **Sanchi gateway:** Ashoka's visit to the **Bodhi tree with his two queens**; south gateway – Ashoka at Ramagrama.

=== Pillars, Capitals and Court Art
- **Ashokan pillars:** **monolithic**, free-standing, **highly polished** ("Mauryan polish"), round **tapering** shafts of **Chunar sandstone**, **no base** – independent monuments, **not parts of architectural structures** (UPSC 1997 – the incorrect statement).
- **Capitals:** bull, lion and elephant – **not only lions** (AP Sachivalayam: "A and B" correct, "only lion capital" wrong).
- **Parts:** shaft → bell-shaped **inverted lotus** → abacus → animal(s).
| Capital | Site |
|---|---|
| Four lions + Dharmachakra | **Sarnath** – the **National Emblem** (26 Jan 1950) |
| Bull | **Rampurva** (now in Rashtrapati Bhavan) |
| Single lion | **Lauriya Nandangarh** |
| Elephant | **Sankisa** |
| Persian (Achaemenid) pillars | Built in pieces, **with base**, **fluted** shaft – Mauryan pillars are monolithic, baseless, smooth |
- **Persian influence:** polish, animal capitals, "lipi/dipi" – but the Mauryan pillar is an **Indian adaptation**.
- **Two streams:** **court art** (pillars, capitals, palaces) and **popular/folk art** (yakshas, terracotta); stone became the main medium for the first time.

=== Popular Art – Yakshas, Dhauli Elephant, Didarganj Yakshi
| Figure | Findspot |
|---|---|
| **Chauri-bearer Yakshi** (polished) | **Didarganj** (Patna) – Mauryan |
| **Manibhadra Yaksha** (inscribed) | **Parkham** near Mathura (RAS 2021) – not Noh, Besnagar or Jhing-ka-Nagra |
| Yakshi | Besnagar (Vidisha) |
| Yaksha | Noh (Bharatpur) |
| **Rock-cut elephant** | **Dhauli** on the Daya river near Bhubaneswar, with Ashoka's edicts |

- **Yakshas:** earliest large human sculptures, revered in **Jainism, Buddhism and Hinduism**; folk (popular) art.

- **All four Mauryan (APPSC G1):** Dhauli elephant, Didarganj Yakshi, Great Stupa at Sanchi (Ashokan core), 80-pillared hall at Kumrahar.
- **Kanaganahalli** Ashoka slab ("Ranyo Ashoka") is **Satavahana-era**, not Mauryan.

=== Barabar and Nagarjuni Caves
- **Barabar caves** (Jehanabad, Bihar, near Gaya): cut in **granite** with mirror polish; given by **Ashoka** (and Nagarjuni caves by his grandson **Dasaratha**) to the **Ajivikas** (State PSC) – not Jains, Tharus or Tantrikas.
- **Oldest surviving rock-cut caves** (3rd c. BCE) – so **not Badami**, and made by **Ashoka**, not Chandragupta (UPSC 2013: only "Ellora has caves of different faiths" is correct).
| Hill | Caves |
|---|---|
| Barabar (4) | **Lomas Rishi**, **Sudama**, Viswamitra, Karna Chopar |
| Nagarjuni (3) | Vahiyaka, **Gopika** (largest), Vadathika |
- **4 + 3 = Satghar.** Three Barabar caves carry Ashoka's Brahmi inscriptions ("**King Piyadasi**").
- **Lomas Rishi:** earliest stone **ogee (chaitya) arch**, copying wooden/thatched architecture.
- **Nagarjuni caves are in Bihar**, with 3rd-c. BCE (not 6th-c. CE) inscriptions – not Nagarjunakonda in AP.

=== Society – Megasthenes' Seven Classes, Slavery and Women
- **Megasthenes** (envoy of **Seleucus**, author of **Indica**) divided society into **seven classes** (State PSC): philosophers, farmers, herdsmen/hunters, artisans and traders, soldiers, overseers/spies, councillors and assessors.
- **Two types of philosophers: Brahmins and Shramanas** – stated by **Megasthenes** (UKPCS 2024).
- **Slavery:** Megasthenes found **no slaves**, but the **Arthashastra and Buddhist texts mention slaves** – Kautilya lists **nine** kinds of dasas.
  - Sources: birth, sale, war capture, **judicial punishment**; **no Arya** could be made a slave.
  - A **female slave who bore her master a son became legally free**, and the **son got the legal status of the master's son** (UPSC 2022: all three statements correct).
- **Women:** **armed women bodyguards** of the king; **divorce** (moksha) and **widow remarriage** allowed – so "Megasthenes' seven castes, women bodyguards, widow remarriage/divorce" are correct and "texts denied slavery" is wrong.
- **Divorce for a deserted wife:** allowed by the **Arthashastra** (UPSC 1996) – not the Kamasutra, Manusmriti or Sukranitisara.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Karmakara | Wage labour |
| Vishti | Unpaid forced labour (→ Telugu Vetti) |
| Moksha | Divorce |
| Stridhana | Woman's own property (jewellery, maintenance) |
| Antyavasayins | Five mixed castes outside Aryan society |
| Sishtas | Learned men |
- **Caste:** Shudras could own land; **Chandalas and Shvapakas** grew in number. Learning: **Taxila** (most famous), Ujjain, Varanasi. The Arthashastra is compared to Machiavelli's **The Prince**.

=== Economy – Coins, Trade and Ports
| Coin | Metal |
|---|---|
| **Pana** (karshapana) | **Silver** – the Mauryan silver coin (APPSC G2 2011), not Niska, Kakini or Shatamana |
| Mashaka | Copper |
| Kakani | Copper, 1/4 mashaka |
| Suvarna | Gold (not in common use) |
- **Coinage metals: silver and copper** (CDS 2012); punch-marked silver coins with symbols (peacock, hill, crescent), **no portraits**, going back to the Mahajanapada age.
- **Tamralipti** on the east coast was the **main port of the Ganga basin** (APPSC GS 2010); Bharukachchha (Broach) and Sopara on the west coast; Berenice (Bernis) on the Red Sea for Egypt trade.
| Route | Fact |
|---|---|
| Uttarapatha | Taxila → Pataliputra → Tamralipti |
| Dakshinapatha | Great southern route; Krishna–Godavari delta a node |
- **Land grants:** the Mauryan kings did **not** give land grants on religious grounds, and there was no peasant revolt against land grants (UPPSC Mains 2004: A true, R false).
- **State control:** crown land (**sita**) worked by **slaves and hired labour**; state monopoly of mining, forests, salt, liquor, arms, metallurgy; interest **15%** a year; rainfall measured in **drona**; **NBPW** pottery; caravans led by a **Sarthavaha**; guilds (**shrenis**) and merchants funded Buddhist centres, including in Andhra; good roads and state incentives spread crafts; exports included diamonds, pearls and fine textiles.

=== Pataliputra – Palace and City
- **Chandragupta's palace at Pataliputra was mainly of wood** (State PSC); the city had a **wooden wall** with towers and gates; polished stone pillars only in key halls.
| Site | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Kumrahar** | **80-pillared hall** (10 rows of 8), polished pillars, wooden floor and roof |
| **Bulandibagh** | Wooden palisade |
- Excavated by **D.B. Spooner**; Megasthenes compared the palace to **Susa and Ecbatana**; **Fa-Hien** (5th c. CE) admired it.

=== Religious Conditions under the Mauryas
| Faith | Patron / evidence |
|---|---|
| Jainism | **Chandragupta** followed **Bhadrabahu** to Shravanabelagola |
| Ajivikas | Bindusara; Ashoka and Dasaratha gave the Barabar/Nagarjuni caves; **Pillar Edict VII** names Ajivikas and Nirgranthas separately |
| Buddhism | Ashoka – stupas, Sangha |
| Brahmanism | Continued; Kautilya called **dvijarshabh** (superior Brahmana) |
- **Policy:** liberal and tolerant, while protecting varna and ashrama.
- **News (2026):** **Balirajgarh** (Madhubani, Bihar) ASI dig – Mauryan to Pala layers, punch-marked coins; **Vaishya Tekri** (Ujjain) – possible 3rd-c. BCE stupa larger than Sanchi, with punch-marked and Avanti coins.
