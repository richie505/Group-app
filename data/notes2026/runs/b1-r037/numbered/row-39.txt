## SUB 0 | Kharavela of Kalinga – the Hathigumpha Inscription
[0:0] - **Hathigumpha inscription** (2nd–1st c. BCE): the year-by-year record of **Kharavela** of Kalinga (UPPSC 2018) – not Ashoka, Harsha or Kanishka; it is not the Girnar, Junagadh or Sarnath inscription.
[0:1] - **Place:** **Udayagiri** hill near Bhubaneswar, Odisha – **not Khandagiri**.
[0:2] - **Udayagiri and Khandagiri:** both natural and artificial caves; **Udayagiri 18, Khandagiri 15** caves (CGPSC 2021: statements i and ii correct).
[0:3] - **Dynasty:** **Mahameghavahana** family of the **Chedi** line (UPPCS 2018 keys Mahameghavahana; Chedi names the same line).
[0:4] - **Religion:** a **Jaina** – the king who "heavily leaned towards Jainism" (not Dasharatha, Brihadratha or Huvishka); gave caves to Jain monks; brought back from Magadha the **Jina image** taken by the Nandas.
[0:5] - **Campaigns:** sent an army west **ignoring Satakarni**; raided Magadha; southern victories; canal work begun by a Nanda king.
[0:6] - **Krishna river = "Kannabenna"** (Kanhabemna) in the Hathigumpha inscription (APPSC AP History).
[0:7] - **Other pairs:** Nasik – Gautami Balashri; **Nanaghat – Naganika**; Junagadh – Rudradaman.
[0:8] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian History 01 p.7; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.144; app notes 1:39 (sub 5)
## SUB 1 | End of the Dynasty and the Successors
[1:0] - **Brihadratha**, the last Maurya and **7th king after Ashoka**, was killed by his **senani Pushyamitra Sunga** at an **army review** – a military coup.
[1:1] - **Immediate successors of the Mauryas in Magadha: the Sungas** (APPSC G2 2003, 2008) – not Kushanas, Pandyas or Satavahanas; Sunga dynasty founded **187 BC** (NextGen; PMF: 185 BCE).
[1:2] - **In the Deccan the Mauryas were succeeded by the Satavahanas** (APPSC ASO) – earlier Mauryan feudatories; founder **Simuka (Srimukha)**; the Puranas call them **Andhra-bhrityas**.
[1:3] - **Pushyamitra:** Brahmana of the **Bharadvaja gotra**, **Senapati**; two Ashvamedhas (**Ayodhya inscription of Dhanadeva**); **Malavikagnimitram** – grandson **Vasumitra** beat a Yavana army on the Sindhu.
[1:4] - **Kanvas:** **Vasudeva Kanva** killed the last Sunga **Devabhuti**; **four** kings (c. 73–28 BC); the last, **Susharman**, fell to the **Satavahanas**, not Menander.
[1:5] - **Persecution debate:** the Divyavadana calls Pushyamitra a persecutor, yet **Sanchi and Bharhut** grew under the Sungas.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.45; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.139-140; app notes 1:39 (sub 3, 4)
## SUB 2 | Theories of Decline → Historian
[2:0] TABLE head: Theory | Historian | Core argument
  [2:0.0] Brahmanical reaction | H.P. Shastri | Pro-Buddhist policy and ban on sacrifices angered Brahmins; Sungas and Kanvas were Brahmins
  [2:0.1] Ashoka's pacifism | H.C. Raychaudhuri | Non-violence after Kalinga dulled the army
  [2:0.2] Financial crisis | D.D. Kosambi | Army, bureaucracy and gifts to the Sangha emptied the treasury; heavy taxes even on actors and prostitutes
  [2:0.3] Over-centralisation, no national consciousness | Romila Thapar | Rule depended on the king; weak successors
  [2:0.4] Oppressive provincial rule | Divyavadana evidence | Revolts at Taxila against wicked officials
[2:1] - **Financial-crisis evidence:** spending on **Dhamma missions** and the **Sangha**; **debasement of later punch-marked silver coins**; gold images melted.
[2:2] - **Other factors:** spread of iron and material culture to outlying areas; neglect of the **north-west frontier** while China built the Great Wall (c. 220 BC).
[2:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.140-141; app notes 1:39 (sub 0)
## SUB 3 | Later Mauryas, Division and Foreign Threats
[3:0] - **Later Mauryas:** Kunala, Dasharatha, **Samprati** (Jain patron), Shalishuka, Devavarman, Shatadhanvan, **Brihadratha** – the Vishnu Purana lists seven successors.
[3:1] TABLE head: Part / threat | Ruler / power
  [3:1.0] Western part | Kunala, later Samprati; Indo-Greeks supplanted it by 180 BC
  [3:1.1] Eastern part | Pataliputra – down to Brihadratha
  [3:1.2] North-west | Bactrian Greeks Demetrius and Menander
  [3:1.3] Hindu Kush, c. 206 BC | Antiochus III took tribute from Subhagasena
  [3:1.4] Great Wall of China (c. 220 BC) | Diverted Scythian tribes westward and southward towards India
  [3:1.5] Andhra / Deccan | Satavahanas, former feudatories
  [3:1.6] Vidarbha | Yajnasena (Malavikagnimitram)
[3:2] - **Menander = Milinda**; capital **Sagala (Sialkot)**; dialogue with **Nagasena** in the Milindapanha.
[3:3] - **Not a cause:** a permanent alliance with the Indo-Greeks.
[3:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.45; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.141, p.146; app notes 1:39 (sub 1, 2)
