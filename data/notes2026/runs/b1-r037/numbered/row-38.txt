## SUB 0 | Stupas – Sanchi, Bharhut and the Stupa Cult
[0:0] - **Stupa cult:** the idea predates Buddhism (Vedic burial mounds); **Ashoka popularised it** – by tradition he redistributed the relics of the **8** original stupas into **84,000**. Stupas held relics of the Buddha and monks and stood in towns **near trade routes**, funded by merchants and guilds – **not in rural areas** (CDS 2018).
[0:1] - **Sanchi Stupa was built by Ashoka** (brick core) (MPPSC 1995; UPPCS 1991) – not Chandragupta, Kautilya, Bimbisara, Harsha or Pushyamitra.
[0:2] TABLE head: Stupa | Fact
  [0:2.0] Sanchi (ancient Kakanaya / Kaknam) | Raisen district, MP, near Vidisha; Ashoka's brick core → Sunga stone casing, railings and toranas → Satavahana carving of gateways → Gupta shrines; considered the best stupa (UPPCS 2008); oldest stone structure in India among Ajanta, Konark, Brihadeeswara
  [0:2.1] Sanchi No. 2 / No. 3 | Relics of 10 monks (incl. Dhamma Mahamatras) / Sariputta and Mahamoggallana
  [0:2.2] Bharhut | Satna, MP; Sunga, c. 150 BCE; found by Cunningham
  [0:2.3] Dharmarajika | Sarnath and Taxila
  [0:2.4] Dhamek | Sarnath; present form Gupta (c. 500 CE)
  [0:2.5] Kanakamuni | Niglihawa (Nepal); enlarged in Ashoka's 14th year
  [0:2.6] Ranabhar | Kushinagar; built by the Mallas
  [0:2.7] Kesariya | East Champaran; largest (about 104 ft)
  [0:2.8] Amaravati | Right bank of the Krishna, AP; traced by Colin Mackenzie, 1797
[0:3] - **Chronological order (UPPCS Mains 2014):** **Bharhut → Sanchi → Amaravati → Dhamek** (Bharhut and Sanchi stone work 2nd–1st c. BCE, Amaravati's peak 1st–2nd c. CE, Dhamek Gupta). The old key "Dhamek, Bharhut, Amaravati, Sanchi" puts the Gupta stupa first and is wrong.
[0:4] - **Sanchi gateway:** Ashoka's visit to the **Bodhi tree with his two queens**; south gateway – Ashoka at Ramagrama.

## SUB 1 | Pillars, Capitals and Court Art
[1:0] - **Ashokan pillars:** **monolithic**, free-standing, **highly polished** ("Mauryan polish"), round **tapering** shafts of **Chunar sandstone**, **no base** – independent monuments, **not parts of architectural structures** (UPSC 1997 – the incorrect statement).
[1:1] - **Capitals:** bull, lion and elephant – **not only lions** (AP Sachivalayam: "A and B" correct, "only lion capital" wrong).
[1:2] - **Parts:** shaft → bell-shaped **inverted lotus** → abacus → animal(s).
[1:3] TABLE head: Capital | Site
  [1:3.0] Four lions + Dharmachakra | Sarnath – the National Emblem (26 Jan 1950)
  [1:3.1] Bull | Rampurva (now in Rashtrapati Bhavan)
  [1:3.2] Single lion | Lauriya Nandangarh
  [1:3.3] Elephant | Sankisa
  [1:3.4] Persian (Achaemenid) pillars | Built in pieces, with base, fluted shaft – Mauryan pillars are monolithic, baseless, smooth
[1:4] - **Persian influence:** polish, animal capitals, "lipi/dipi" – but the Mauryan pillar is an **Indian adaptation**.
[1:5] - **Two streams:** **court art** (pillars, capitals, palaces) and **popular/folk art** (yakshas, terracotta); stone became the main medium for the first time.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.131-133; NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.52-54; app notes 1:38 (sub 6)

## SUB 3 | Barabar and Nagarjuni Caves
[3:0] - **Barabar caves** (Jehanabad, Bihar, near Gaya): cut in **granite** with mirror polish; given by **Ashoka** (and Nagarjuni caves by his grandson **Dasaratha**) to the **Ajivikas** (State PSC) – not Jains, Tharus or Tantrikas.
[3:1] - **Oldest surviving rock-cut caves** (3rd c. BCE) – so **not Badami**, and made by **Ashoka**, not Chandragupta (UPSC 2013: only "Ellora has caves of different faiths" is correct).
[3:2] TABLE head: Hill | Caves
  [3:2.0] Barabar (4) | Lomas Rishi, Sudama, Viswamitra, Karna Chopar
  [3:2.1] Nagarjuni (3) | Vahiyaka, Gopika (largest), Vadathika
[3:3] - **4 + 3 = Satghar.** Three Barabar caves carry Ashoka's Brahmi inscriptions ("**King Piyadasi**").
[3:4] - **Lomas Rishi:** earliest stone **ogee (chaitya) arch**, copying wooden/thatched architecture.
[3:5] - **Nagarjuni caves are in Bihar**, with 3rd-c. BCE (not 6th-c. CE) inscriptions – not Nagarjunakonda in AP.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.133; NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.52; app notes 1:38 (sub 9)
## SUB 4 | Society – Megasthenes' Seven Classes, Slavery and Women
[4:0] - **Megasthenes** (envoy of **Seleucus**, author of **Indica**) divided society into **seven classes** (State PSC): philosophers, farmers, herdsmen/hunters, artisans and traders, soldiers, overseers/spies, councillors and assessors.
[4:1] - **Two types of philosophers: Brahmins and Shramanas** – stated by **Megasthenes** (UKPCS 2024).
[4:2] - **Slavery:** Megasthenes found **no slaves**, but the **Arthashastra and Buddhist texts mention slaves** – Kautilya lists **nine** kinds of dasas.
[4:3]   - Sources: birth, sale, war capture, **judicial punishment**; **no Arya** could be made a slave.
[4:4]   - A **female slave who bore her master a son became legally free**, and the **son got the legal status of the master's son** (UPSC 2022: all three statements correct).
[4:5] - **Women:** **armed women bodyguards** of the king; **divorce** (moksha) and **widow remarriage** allowed – so "Megasthenes' seven castes, women bodyguards, widow remarriage/divorce" are correct and "texts denied slavery" is wrong.
[4:6] - **Divorce for a deserted wife:** allowed by the **Arthashastra** (UPSC 1996) – not the Kamasutra, Manusmriti or Sukranitisara.
[4:7] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:7.0] Karmakara | Wage labour
  [4:7.1] Vishti | Unpaid forced labour (→ Telugu Vetti)
  [4:7.2] Moksha | Divorce
  [4:7.3] Stridhana | Woman's own property (jewellery, maintenance)
  [4:7.4] Antyavasayins | Five mixed castes outside Aryan society
  [4:7.5] Sishtas | Learned men
[4:8] - **Caste:** Shudras could own land; **Chandalas and Shvapakas** grew in number. Learning: **Taxila** (most famous), Ujjain, Varanasi. The Arthashastra is compared to Machiavelli's **The Prince**.
[4:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.46-47, p.51-52; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.109-110; app notes 1:38 (sub 3)
## SUB 5 | Economy – Coins, Trade and Ports
[5:0] TABLE head: Coin | Metal
  [5:0.0] Pana (karshapana) | Silver – the Mauryan silver coin (APPSC G2 2011), not Niska, Kakini or Shatamana
  [5:0.1] Mashaka | Copper
  [5:0.2] Kakani | Copper, 1/4 mashaka
  [5:0.3] Suvarna | Gold (not in common use)
[5:1] - **Coinage metals: silver and copper** (CDS 2012); punch-marked silver coins with symbols (peacock, hill, crescent), **no portraits**, going back to the Mahajanapada age.
[5:2] - **Tamralipti** on the east coast was the **main port of the Ganga basin** (APPSC GS 2010); Bharukachchha (Broach) and Sopara on the west coast; Berenice (Bernis) on the Red Sea for Egypt trade.
[5:3] TABLE head: Route | Fact
  [5:3.0] Uttarapatha | Taxila → Pataliputra → Tamralipti
  [5:3.1] Dakshinapatha | Great southern route; Krishna–Godavari delta a node
[5:4] - **Land grants:** the Mauryan kings did **not** give land grants on religious grounds, and there was no peasant revolt against land grants (UPPSC Mains 2004: A true, R false).
[5:5] - **State control:** crown land (**sita**) worked by **slaves and hired labour**; state monopoly of mining, forests, salt, liquor, arms, metallurgy; interest **15%** a year; rainfall measured in **drona**; **NBPW** pottery; caravans led by a **Sarthavaha**; guilds (**shrenis**) and merchants funded Buddhist centres, including in Andhra; good roads and state incentives spread crafts; exports included diamonds, pearls and fine textiles.
[5:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.46-47, p.53; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.115; app notes 1:38 (sub 0, 1, 2)
## SUB 6 | Pataliputra – Palace and City
[6:0] - **Chandragupta's palace at Pataliputra was mainly of wood** (State PSC); the city had a **wooden wall** with towers and gates; polished stone pillars only in key halls.
[6:1] TABLE head: Site | Finding
  [6:1.0] Kumrahar | 80-pillared hall (10 rows of 8), polished pillars, wooden floor and roof
  [6:1.1] Bulandibagh | Wooden palisade
[6:2] - Excavated by **D.B. Spooner**; Megasthenes compared the palace to **Susa and Ecbatana**; **Fa-Hien** (5th c. CE) admired it.
