## SUB 1 | Revenue System – Taxes and Penalties
[1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:0.0] Sita | Revenue from crown land (UPPCS 2013) – land farmed for the state under the Sitadhyaksha
  [1:0.1] Bhaga | King's share of produce – normally 1/6 (PMF); NextGen and Megasthenes give 1/4 (range 1/4 to 1/6)
  [1:0.2] Bali | Additional levy / tribute
  [1:0.3] Pindakara | Tax assessed on groups of villages, paid by the village as a unit – the "pin tax" (IAS 2009)
  [1:0.4] Pranaya | Emergency tax ("gift of affection") (UKPCS 2021)
  [1:0.5] Kara | Tax on fruit and flower gardens
  [1:0.6] Hiranya | Tax paid only in cash
  [1:0.7] Vivita | Pasture-land dues
  [1:0.8] Setu / Vana | Gardens, orchards, root crops / forests
[1:1] - **Land revenue** was the main income; the land belonged to the king; an **irrigation tax** was levied on those who used state irrigation.
[1:2] - **Tax evasion → death:** Megasthenes says evasion of the sales tax was punished with **death** (JKPSC 2013).
[1:3] - **Pankodakasannirodhe:** the Arthashastra fine for **slushing the road** with dirt and water (RAS 2013) – not for filthy water, garbage or temples.
[1:4] - **Other income:** tolls, ferries, mines, pastures, licences, fines; state monopoly of mining, liquor, salt and arms. Lumbini: freed of bali, bhaga cut to **1/8**.
[1:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.46, p.50; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.115; Rocket Indian History 01 p.4; app notes 1:37 (sub 8, 9)
## SUB 2 | District and Local Officials – Pradeshika, Rajuka, Yukta
[2:0] - **Ashokan district officers:** **Pradeshika, Rajuka and Yukta** were officers at the **district level** (UPSC 2025) – survey and assessment of land, tours, revenue, law and order; they toured every five years (Rock Edict III).
[2:1] TABLE head: Officer | Level / role
  [2:1.0] Pradeshika | Chief district administrator, like a modern collector – revenue, police
  [2:1.1] Rajuka | District – land survey and assessment, with judicial powers (Pillar Edict IV); the Mauryan officer for the head of an ahara (CDS 2009: not Krori, Faujdar or Chirastadar, which are medieval)
  [2:1.2] Yukta | Subordinate revenue/office officer assisting the Rajukas
  [2:1.3] Sthanika | Tax collector over a larger division
  [2:1.4] Gopa | Accountant over 5–10 (PMF: 10–15) villages
  [2:1.5] Gramika / Gramani | Village head, with elders
  [2:1.6] Anta-mahamattas | Frontier forts
  [2:1.7] Rakshinas | City police
[2:2] - **Rajjukas** were therefore **officers in the Mauryan administration** (UPPCS 1996) – not Chola traders, Gupta feudal lords or Saka soldiers.
[2:3] - **Not Mauryan:** **Agraharika** (RAS 2013); **Mahabaladhikrita, Mahadandanayaka, Mahasandhivigrahika** are **Gupta** titles; Bhagadugha, Sangrahitri are Vedic.
[2:4] - **City:** municipality under a **Nagaraka / Purmukhya**.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.114-115, p.124; NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.45-46, p.49; app notes 1:37 (sub 5)
## SUB 3 | Megasthenes – City Committees and Army Boards
[3:0] - **Pataliputra:** a municipal board of **30 members in 6 committees of 5** (Megasthenes).
[3:1] TABLE head: Committee | Function
  [3:1.0] 1 | Industrial arts and crafts
  [3:1.1] 2 | Foreigners
  [3:1.2] 3 | Registration of births and deaths
  [3:1.3] 4 | Trade and commerce, prices, weights and measures
  [3:1.4] 5 | Supervision of manufactured goods
  [3:1.5] 6 | Tax on sales (one-tenth, excise and customs) – NOT land revenue
[3:2] - **Continuity:** **registration of births and deaths** is the municipal function that has continued from Mauryan times to today (RAS 1992; State PSC).
[3:3] - **Agronomoi** = district officers in charge of **roads**, irrigation and land measurement (UPPSC 2020: "construction of roads"); **Astynomoi** = city officers.
[3:4] - **Army:** also **6 boards of 5** – infantry, cavalry, chariots, elephants, navy, transport/commissariat. Chandragupta's army: **6,00,000 foot, 30,000 cavalry, 9,000 elephants**.
[3:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.114-115; NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.46, p.49-50; app notes 1:37 (sub 6)
## SUB 4 | Saptanga Theory and the Nature of the State
[4:0] TABLE head: No. | Element | Meaning
  [4:0.0] 1 | Swami | King (comes first)
  [4:0.1] 2 | Amatya | Ministers and officials
  [4:0.2] 3 | Janapada | Territory and people
  [4:0.3] 4 | Durga | Fort
  [4:0.4] 5 | Kosha | Treasury
  [4:0.5] 6 | Danda / Bala | Army, force
  [4:0.6] 7 | Mitra | Ally – "the ear of the state"; the seventh limb (UPPCS 2015)
[4:1] - **Order varies by book** (PMF: Swami, Amatya, Durga, Danda, Janapada, Kosha, Mitra) but **Swami is first and Mitra last** in every list.
[4:2] - **Saptanga includes king, territory, administration (ministers) and treasury** (NDA 2010) – not ragas, monks or traders.
[4:3] - **Shatru (enemy)** is NOT one of the standard seven; NextGen adds it only as an eighth. Allies help in peace and war; natural allies are better than artificial ones.
[4:4] - **Nature of the state:** **highly centralised and bureaucratic** (AP High Court 2026) – "**paternal despotism**", the king heading executive, legislature and judiciary.
[4:5] - **Checks:** the **Mantriparishad**; Kautilya – "**a chariot cannot move on one wheel**" (the king needs ministers).
[4:6] - **Mauryan achievements** (UPPSC 1990): culture, art and literature; **provincial division**; kingship up to the **Hindukush** – but **not gold coins** (the Mauryan currency was punch-marked silver).
[4:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.107-108, p.112; NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.42, p.46, p.48; app notes 1:37 (sub 0, 1)

## SUB 6 | Judiciary and Espionage
[6:0] TABLE head: Court | Cases | Presided by
  [6:0.0] Dharmasthiya | Civil – marriage, contract, inheritance | 3 dharmasthas
  [6:0.1] Kantakashodhana ("removal of thorns") | Criminal | 3 pradeshtris
[6:1] - **Pradvivaka:** chief judge / legal adviser to the king (judicial system).
[6:2] - **Ladder:** village court → **Sangrahana** → Dronamukha → Janapada → **king** (supreme judge).
[6:3] - **Punishments:** fines, maiming, death; severity varied by **varna**. Megasthenes: **no written code**.
[6:4] - **Spies:** **Gudhapurushas** watched officials and provinces; **Samstha** (stationary – kapatika, grihapatika, vaidehaka, tapasa) and **Sanchara** (wandering – satri, tikshna, rasada, **parivrajika**); Prativedakas were special reporters.
[6:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.46, p.50-51; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.116; app notes 1:37 (sub 7, 8)
