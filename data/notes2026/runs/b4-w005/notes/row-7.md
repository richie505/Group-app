=== Continental Drift – Pangaea and Gondwanaland
# Wegener's theory (asked 11 times)
- **Continental drift:** **Alfred Wegener** (German), **1912** – not Wilson, Du Toit or Hess.
- Drift forces: **pole-fleeing force** (Earth's rotation) and **tidal force** (Sun and Moon) – **too weak**, so the theory was rejected.
- Original single landmass: **Pangaea** (UPPCS 2016), surrounded by the mega-ocean **Panthalassa**.
- Pangaea split into **Laurasia** (north) and **Gondwanaland** (south), separated by the **Tethys Sea**; the split began **about 200 million years ago** (NCERT; PMF); older books round Pangaea's break-up to **~225 million years** – the figure the app's MCQs use for Gondwanaland's break-up.
| Name | Present landmasses |
|---|---|
| Laurasia | North America, Greenland, Europe, Asia (minus India) |
| **Gondwanaland** | **South America, Africa, India, Australia, Antarctica, Madagascar, Arabia** |
- **India (Peninsular)** was part of **Gondwanaland** (IAS 1995, asked twice; APPSC) – not Laurasia; "all of the above" for South America, Africa, Australia (MPPSC 2008). **North America was not** part of Gondwanaland (UPPCS 2016).
- APPSC G2 statements "Pangaea split 150 million years ago" / "Gondwanaland broke up 65 million years ago": NCERT puts the split at ~200 million years; dates differ between textbooks.
# Evidence of drift
| Evidence | Example |
|---|---|
| Jig-saw fit | South America and Africa; **Bullard's 1964** computer fit |
| Rock belts | ~**2,000-million-year** belt of the Brazil coast matches **West Africa** |
| Placer deposits | **Ghana coast gold** has no local source; gold-bearing veins in **Brazil** – the continents lay side by side (A and R both true, R explains A) |
| Tillite | **Gondwana** glacial sediments of India have counterparts in **six** southern landmasses |
| Fossils | **Mesosaurus** (South Africa and Brazil only); **Glossopteris**; lemurs |
- **UPSC 2025:** rock belts, Ghana gold from the Brazil plateau and the Gondwana counterparts – **all three** are evidence (NCERT wording).

=== Plate Tectonics and the Plates
# The theory (asked 6 times)
- **Plate tectonics:** pieces of the Earth's crust (lithospheric plates) are in **constant slow motion driven by movement in the mantle** – rigid lithosphere on the semi-fluid **asthenosphere**.
- Put together in **1967 by McKenzie and Parker**, and independently by **Morgan**; it is **based on sea-floor spreading**. It was **not given by H.H. Hess in 1967** (Hess gave sea-floor spreading, 1960–62) → CGPSC 2021: Statement I false, II true.
- Order: 1912 Wegener → 1930s **Arthur Holmes** (convection currents; heat from **radioactive decay**) → 1960–62 **Hess** (sea-floor spreading) → 1963 Vine–Matthews → 1967 plate tectonics.
# Plates
- **Seven major plates** (APPSC 2020): **Pacific**, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian, Antarctic.
| Minor plate | Location |
|---|---|
| **Cocos** | Between **Central America and the Pacific plate** |
| Nazca | Between South America and the Pacific plate |
| Caroline | Between the Philippine and Indian plates |
| Philippine | Between the Asiatic and Pacific plates |
| Arabian | Mostly the Saudi Arabian landmass |
- Cocos, Arabian, Philippine and **Nazca** are **minor** plates (NDA 2019).
- Plate movement is monitored with **GPS** data.

=== The Indian Plate and the Himalayas
- **Palaeomagnetic** data show the Indian landmass has moved **northward** (UPPCS 1998) – from south of the equator; the Peninsula was joined to Africa until the **Cretaceous**.
- **Deccan Traps** erupted as India crossed near the equator over the **Réunion hotspot**.
- Collision with **Eurasia** began **~40–50 million years ago** (4–5 crore); the Tethys closed and its sediments were folded into the Himalayas.
- India still moves north **~5 cm/year** → the **Himalayas are still rising** (~5 mm/year): A and R both true, R explains A.
- Earthquakes from the **Himalayas to the Arakan Yoma** and in the **north-west** of the subcontinent: **collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates** – plate tectonic activity, not volcanoes, corals or dams.
- The collision explains the Himalayas and the **Indo-Gangetic foreland basin**; **delamination** of the plate's lower layer at ~100 km under southern Tibet.

=== Sea-floor Spreading and Palaeomagnetism
- **Sea-floor spreading** (Harry Hess, 1960–62): magma rises at mid-ocean ridges → **new crust is continually added**; old crust is destroyed by **subduction** – the two roughly balance.
- Away from the ridge, the **age, density and thickness** of oceanic crust and its sediments **increase** (APPSC).
- Ocean-floor rocks are young; the oldest are ~**200 million years**.
- Rocks record the field of their time (**palaeomagnetism**); **Vine and Matthews (1963)**: normal and reversed bands lie **symmetrically** on either side of the ridge.
- **UPSC 2018:** the Earth's magnetic field **has reversed** every few hundred thousand years (A true); early atmosphere had **no 54% oxygen** (B false); living organisms **modified** the early atmosphere (C true) → **A and C only**.
- Spreading comes in **bursts**: one observed episode – seafloor sank 4 m, spread over 1 m, 160 million m³ of lava in 16 days.
~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 02 p.13-16; NCERT Class 11 Fundamentals ch.2 (evolution of the atmosphere); app notes 4:7 sub 3

=== Plate Boundaries and Earth Movements
| Boundary | Motion | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Divergent (constructive) | Apart; **new crust** | Mid-Atlantic Ridge; Red Sea, East African Rift (Nubian, Somalian, Arabian plates near the **Afar** triple junction) |
| Convergent (destructive) | Collision / subduction; mountains, trenches | Himalayas (continent–continent); **Andes** (Nazca under South American); **Mariana Trench** (ocean–ocean) |
| Transform (conservative) | Slide past | **San Andreas** (not divergent); **Sagaing Fault** (Myanmar, Indian–Eurasian) |
- No new crust at convergent boundaries; earthquakes there come from subduction friction (Ring of Fire).
- **Owen Fracture Zone** separates the Arabian and Indian plates. **Ladakh Magmatic Arc:** northward subduction of the **Neo-Tethys** plate.
- **Sudden movements** inside the Earth cause earthquakes and **volcanoes** (APPSC AEE); slow (diastrophic) movements build continents and mountains.
