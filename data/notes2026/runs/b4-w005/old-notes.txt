=== NOTES 4:5 | Interior of the Earth – Crust, Mantle, Core; Discontinuities and Seismic Evidence
## SUB 0 | Layers of the Earth and Their Composition
[5:0:0] # Crust, mantle, core (asked 11 times)
[5:0:1] TABLE head: Layer | Depth | State | Density (g/cm³) | Made of
  [5:0:1.0] Crust – outermost layer | Oceanic ~5 km, continental ~30 km (~70 km under mountains) | Solid | 2.8–3.0 | Continental: granite (Si, Al); oceanic: basalt (Fe, Mg)
  [5:0:1.1] Mantle – largest layer | Moho → 2,900 km | Solid, flows plastically (convection) | ~4.6 | Silicate minerals of iron and magnesium (upper mantle mostly silicates)
  [5:0:1.2] Outer core | 2,900–5,150 km (~2,260 km thick) | Liquid iron–nickel | ~10–12 | Fe + Ni
  [5:0:1.3] Inner core | 5,150–6,371 km (radius ~1,220 km) | Solid – immense pressure; P-waves at 11.23 km/s | 13.3–13.6 | Mostly iron
[5:0:2] - **Mantle = 83% of volume, 68% of mass** → the **largest** layer; **core = 16% of volume, 32% of mass**.
[5:0:3] - **Core is mainly iron** (with nickel) – not aluminium, chromium or silicon (UPSC 2009, asked twice). Its **lighter elements: silicon, sulphur, oxygen** – the APPSC G2 answer is **silicon**; not aluminium, magnesium, calcium or cobalt.
[5:0:4] - **Mantle = "mixed metals and silicates"** (iron–magnesium silicates) (SSC 2013); Sial is only silica + aluminium, Nife only metal.
[5:0:5] - **Lithosphere = crust + uppermost solid mantle** (~100 km) (NDA 2019).
[5:0:6] - Continental crust: thicker, older, granitic, lighter; oceanic crust: thinner, younger, **basaltic**, denser.
[5:0:7] - **Compositional layering:** crust, mantle, core. **Mechanical:** lithosphere, asthenosphere, mesosphere, outer core, inner core.
[5:0:8] # Suess's chemical layers
[5:0:9] TABLE head: Suess term | Elements | Feature
  [5:0:9.0] SIAL | Silica + Aluminium | Granite; forms the continents; density ~2.9
  [5:0:9.1] SIMA | Silica + Magnesium | Basalt; source of magma and lava
  [5:0:9.2] NIFE | Nickel + Ferrum (iron) | Core; very dense (~11); explains Earth's magnetism (SSC 2018; State PSC "Nickel and ferrum")
[5:0:10] - **Eduard Suess** coined SIAL, SIMA and NIFE.
[5:0:11] - **Whole Earth by mass:** **iron 32.1%** > oxygen 30.1% > **silicon 15.1%** > magnesium 13.9% > sulphur 2.9% > nickel 1.8% (RRB 2005: after iron and oxygen comes **silicon**).
[5:0:12] > Trap: crust question → oxygen/aluminium; whole-Earth question → iron.

## SUB 1 | Seismic Waves, Shadow Zone and the Seismograph
[5:1:0] # Seismic waves (asked 7 times)
[5:1:1] TABLE head: Basis | P (primary) | S (secondary) | Surface (L)
  [5:1:1.0] Type | Longitudinal, push–pull, compressional; vibration parallel | Transverse, shear; vibration perpendicular | Along the surface (Love, Rayleigh)
  [5:1:1.1] Speed | Fastest, arrive first | Slower | Slowest
  [5:1:1.2] Medium | Solid, liquid, gas | Solids only | –
  [5:1:1.3] Damage | Lower | – | Highest
[5:1:2] - An earthquake generates **P, S and L (surface) waves** – "P.S.L" (CGPSC 2013). P and S are **body waves**.
[5:1:3] - **P-waves are sound (acoustic) waves:** compressional like sound, and they become audible sound when they reach the air – so of P, S and L, **P-waves** are the sound waves.
[5:1:4] - Wave speed changes with density → **reflection and refraction** at boundaries reveal the layers. The **S-wave** behaviour (vanishing in the outer core) gave the key evidence of a **liquid outer core** – the CDS 2012 answer "secondary waves".
[5:1:5] - **Shadow zone:** P-waves strongly refracted at the mantle–core boundary → no direct P **~105°–145°** (Rocket: 103°–142°); **S-waves absent everywhere beyond ~105°**. The belt **105°–145°** gets **neither P nor S** (APSLPRB 2023).
[5:1:6] - Each earthquake has its **own** shadow zone, set by its epicentre (CGPSC 2020).
[5:1:7] # Recording
[5:1:8] - **Seismograph** records seismic waves (FBO). NCERT Class 8: the seismograph is a **vibrating rod** or pendulum that starts vibrating with the tremors.
[5:1:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket General Geography p.29-30; NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.1 (p.15); PMF Physical Geography 01 (P-waves "take the form of sound waves" in air); NCERT Class 8 Science ch.12 (seismograph); app notes 4:5 sub 1
## SUB 2 | Earth's Magnetism, Heat and Pressure
[5:2:0] # Magnetic field (asked 4 times)
[5:2:1] - **Cause:** electric **currents in the liquid outer core** – convection of molten iron = **geodynamo** (UPPCS 2011) – not a giant magnet at the centre. It shields life from **solar wind and cosmic radiation**; flow changes are linked to **reversals**.
[5:2:2] - **Magnetic axis ~11.5°** to the geographic axis (not 23½°).
[5:2:3] - **Magnetic equator:** field lines **horizontal**, dip = 0 (NDA 2017); it passes near **Thumba** (Kerala) – hence TERLS. At the magnetic poles the field is vertical.
[5:2:4] - **North magnetic pole:** first located (1831) on the **Boothia Peninsula, northern Canada** – UPSC 2005 key; it has since drifted into the Arctic Ocean towards Siberia (~86°N as of 2025, World Magnetic Model 2025).
[5:2:5] - **Direction of the field** at a place = **declination** (angle from true north) **and inclination/dip** (angle from horizontal) (CDS 2018).
[5:2:6] - ESA **Swarm** mission: outer-core flow under the Pacific switched from westward to eastward.
[5:2:7] # Heat, pressure, density
[5:2:8] - Temperature, pressure and density **all rise with depth**; ~**1°C per 32 m** in the upper crust. Earth's mean density **5.5 g/cm³** (densest planet).
[5:2:9] - Geothermal sites: **Puga Valley (Ladakh)**, Manikaran (Himachal), Tattapani (Chhattisgarh), Cambay (Gujarat), **Bakreshwar (West Bengal)**.
[5:2:10] - Diamonds rise from the mantle via **kimberlite pipes**; **Madhya Pradesh** ~90% of India's reserves.
[5:2:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket General Geography p.31; NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.4 (p.18); NOAA/BGS World Magnetic Model 2025 (north magnetic pole position); app notes 4:5 sub 6
[5:2:12] - **Isogonic lines** join points of **equal magnetic declination** (agonic line = zero declination); isoclinic lines join points of equal dip.
## SUB 3 | Discontinuities, Lithosphere and Asthenosphere
[5:3:0] TABLE head: Discontinuity | Separates | Note
  [5:3:0.0] Conrad | Upper and lower crust (SIAL–SIMA) | 1st from the surface
  [5:3:0.1] Mohorovicic (Moho) | Crust and mantle (APPSC G1) | P 6.9 → 8.1 km/s; 1909, Andrija Mohorovicic (Croatian)
  [5:3:0.2] Repetti | Upper and lower mantle | ~700–1,000 km
  [5:3:0.3] Gutenberg (Weichert–Gutenberg) | Mantle and outer core | ~2,900 km; S-waves vanish
  [5:3:0.4] Lehmann | Outer and inner core | ~5,150 km
[5:3:1] - 410 km and 660 km: mantle mineral **phase changes**. IUGG mantle zones: Moho–200, 200–700, 700–2,900 km.
[5:3:2] TABLE head: Basis | Lithosphere | Asthenosphere
  [5:3:2.0] Nature | Rigid; crust + uppermost mantle (~100 km) | Weak, plastic, partly molten (~100–200 km), in the upper mantle
  [5:3:2.1] Role | Broken into plates | Plates glide over it; main source of magma
[5:3:3] - Sequence: lithosphere → asthenosphere → mesosphere → **barysphere** (core).
[5:3:4] - Indian plate under Tibet: dense lower layer **delaminating** into the mantle at ~**100 km** (94 seismic stations).
[5:3:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.2-3 (pp.16-17); NextGen Rocket General Geography p.29-30; app notes 4:5 sub 3-4

=== NOTES 4:6 | Rocks and Minerals – Igneous, Sedimentary, Metamorphic; the Rock Cycle
## SUB 0 | Igneous Rocks
[6:0:0] - **Formed by cooling and solidification of magma or lava** (State PSC); called **primary rocks**; **no layers, no fossils**; both **crystalline and non-crystalline** (State PSC: the true statement).
[6:0:1] TABLE head: Type | Formation | Examples
  [6:0:1.0] Intrusive (plutonic) | Cools slowly at depth → large grains / crystals | Granite (APPSC G2 2024), gabbro, diorite, pegmatite
  [6:0:1.1] Extrusive (volcanic) | Lava cools fast at the surface → fine grains | Basalt (APPSC FBO), rhyolite, andesite
[6:0:2] - **By silica:** rhyolite = felsic extrusive; granite = felsic intrusive; **diorite = intermediate intrusive**; basalt = mafic extrusive.
[6:0:3] - **Felsic:** silica-rich, light-coloured. **Mafic (basic):** rich in Fe, Mg, Ca; dark, dense.
[6:0:4] - **Not igneous:** **quartzite** (metamorphic, APSLPRB 2023), **dolomite** (sedimentary carbonate, NDA 2017). Granite and basalt = igneous (NDA 2007).
[6:0:5] - **Granite has no fossils** – formed from magma (JPSC 2013).
[6:0:6] # Deccan Trap basalt
[6:0:7] - The **Deccan Plateau / Deccan Traps** are made mainly of **basalt** – igneous lava flows (asked 4 times by APPSC).
[6:0:8] - Fissure eruptions at the end of the Cretaceous, **~65–66 million years ago** (APPSC Tech. Asst: nearest 65), as India passed over the Réunion hotspot.
[6:0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.6 (p.20); NextGen Rocket General Geography p.32; NextGen AP Geography 02 p.4 (Deccan Trap: Cretaceous fissure lava, basalt); app notes 4:6 sub 2, 9
[6:0:10] - **Olivine and pyroxene** are mafic minerals and **dark** in colour; felsic rocks are rich in quartz and feldspar, poor in Fe, Mg, Ca.

## SUB 2 | Sedimentary Rocks, Fossils and Geological Time
[6:2:0] - Formed at the Earth's surface by the **hydrological system**, from **weathering of pre-existing rocks**; sediments are transported, deposited, compacted and **lithified** in **layers** (strata) → "stratified rocks"; they **contain fossils** – all four UPSC 2001 statements are correct.
[6:2:1] - Cover about **75% of the Earth's surface**; hold most fossils and **coal, petroleum, natural gas**.
[6:2:2] TABLE head: Mode | Examples
  [6:2:2.0] Mechanically (clastic) | Sandstone, conglomerate, shale, loess
  [6:2:2.1] Chemically | Rock salt, gypsum, halite, chert, limestone
  [6:2:2.2] Organically | Coal (Uttarakhand 2006), chalk, coral limestone
[6:2:3] - **Sandstone** forms **under water** (MPPSC 1993); the **Red Fort and Agra Fort** are built of red **sandstone** – sedimentary.
[6:2:4] - **Himalayan rocks** are mainly **sedimentary** (folded Tethys sediments) (AP surveyor 2017; Sachivalayam 2020).
[6:2:5] - **Limestone** occurs white as well as black; igneous rocks have no layers; fossils are **not** found in igneous rocks.

[6:2:7] TABLE head: Term | Rocks
  [6:2:7.0] Porous and permeable | Chalk (very permeable, aquifers), sandstone
  [6:2:7.1] Porous but impermeable | Clay – very fine pores, poorly connected
  [6:2:7.2] Neither | Granite

[6:2:9] # Fossils and time
[6:2:10] - **Fossils** = preserved remains, impressions or traces of ancient organisms (SSC 2018).
[6:2:11] - **Dinosaurs: Mesozoic era** (Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous); died out ~66 million years ago.
[6:2:12] - **Age of the Earth:** **uranium (radiometric) dating**; carbon dating only for young organic remains.
[6:2:13] - Giovanni Arduino (1760) made the first geological time scale.

## SUB 3 | Metamorphic Rocks
[6:3:0] - Igneous **or** sedimentary rocks recrystallise under **heat and pressure** ("change of form") – they come from **both** (JPSC 2011).
[6:3:1] TABLE head: Parent rock | Metamorphic rock
  [6:3:1.0] Limestone (and chalk, dolomite) | Marble (UPPCS 1991; APPSC Panchayat Secretary)
  [6:3:1.1] Shale / clay | Slate (then schist)
  [6:3:1.2] Sandstone | Quartzite (UPSC 2001, asked twice) – not limestone, obsidian or shale
  [6:3:1.3] Granite | Gneiss
  [6:3:1.4] Basalt | Amphibolite, schist
  [6:3:1.5] Coal | Graphite (via anthracite)
[6:3:2] - Typical metamorphic minerals: **garnet** (UP 2010), kyanite, sillimanite, staurolite; **halite is not**.
[6:3:3] - Odd one out: **gneiss** (metamorphic) among sandstone, limestone, shale (sedimentary); **granite** (igneous) among marble, quartzite, slate (metamorphic); limestone among marble, slate, quartzite.
[6:3:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.6 (p.20); app notes 4:6 sub 5
[6:3:5] - Match key "**coal → diamond**": both are carbon (diamond forms from carbon under extreme pressure); the true metamorphic series is coal → anthracite → **graphite**.
## SUB 4 | Petrology and the Rock Cycle
[6:4:0] - **Petrology** = science (study) of rocks, a branch of geology (APPSC G2 2019). Rocks: igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic.
[6:4:1] - **Rock cycle:** magma → (cooling) igneous → (weathering, erosion, deposition, **lithification**) sedimentary → (heat, pressure) metamorphic → (melting) magma; **any rock type can become another**; exposed metamorphic rock weathers into sediment.
[6:4:2] - Rainwater with dissolved **CO₂** forms **carbonic acid**, which weathers rocks.
[6:4:3] - **Enhanced rock weathering:** crushed **basalt** spread on farmland locks CO₂ as bicarbonate.
[6:4:4] - **Lithology** = physical character of a rock unit – texture, grain size, composition.
[6:4:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 02 p.1 (p.15); app notes 4:6 sub 1, 7-8

## SUB 0 | Continental Drift – Pangaea and Gondwanaland
[7:0:0] # Wegener's theory (asked 11 times)
[7:0:1] - **Continental drift:** **Alfred Wegener** (German), **1912** – not Wilson, Du Toit or Hess.

## SUB 1 | Plate Tectonics and the Plates
[7:1:0] # The theory (asked 6 times)
[7:1:1] - **Plate tectonics:** pieces of the Earth's crust (lithospheric plates) are in **constant slow motion driven by movement in the mantle** – rigid lithosphere on the semi-fluid **asthenosphere**.
[7:1:2] - Put together in **1967 by McKenzie and Parker**, and independently by **Morgan**; it is **based on sea-floor spreading**. It was **not given by H.H. Hess in 1967** (Hess gave sea-floor spreading, 1960–62) → CGPSC 2021: Statement I false, II true.
[7:1:3] - Order: 1912 Wegener → 1930s **Arthur Holmes** (convection currents; heat from **radioactive decay**) → 1960–62 **Hess** (sea-floor spreading) → 1963 Vine–Matthews → 1967 plate tectonics.
[7:1:4] # Plates
[7:1:5] - **Seven major plates** (APPSC 2020): **Pacific**, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian, Antarctic.

  [7:1:6.4] Arabian | Mostly the Saudi Arabian landmass

[7:1:8] - Plate movement is monitored with **GPS** data.

## SUB 2 | The Indian Plate and the Himalayas
[7:2:0] - **Palaeomagnetic** data show the Indian landmass has moved **northward** (UPPCS 1998) – from south of the equator; the Peninsula was joined to Africa until the **Cretaceous**.
[7:2:1] - **Deccan Traps** erupted as India crossed near the equator over the **Réunion hotspot**.
[7:2:2] - Collision with **Eurasia** began **~40–50 million years ago** (4–5 crore); the Tethys closed and its sediments were folded into the Himalayas.
[7:2:3] - India still moves north **~5 cm/year** → the **Himalayas are still rising** (~5 mm/year): A and R both true, R explains A.
[7:2:4] - Earthquakes from the **Himalayas to the Arakan Yoma** and in the **north-west** of the subcontinent: **collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates** – plate tectonic activity, not volcanoes, corals or dams.
[7:2:5] - The collision explains the Himalayas and the **Indo-Gangetic foreland basin**; **delamination** of the plate's lower layer at ~100 km under southern Tibet.

## SUB 3 | Sea-floor Spreading and Palaeomagnetism
[7:3:0] - **Sea-floor spreading** (Harry Hess, 1960–62): magma rises at mid-ocean ridges → **new crust is continually added**; old crust is destroyed by **subduction** – the two roughly balance.
[7:3:1] - Away from the ridge, the **age, density and thickness** of oceanic crust and its sediments **increase** (APPSC).
[7:3:2] - Ocean-floor rocks are young; the oldest are ~**200 million years**.
[7:3:3] - Rocks record the field of their time (**palaeomagnetism**); **Vine and Matthews (1963)**: normal and reversed bands lie **symmetrically** on either side of the ridge.
[7:3:4] - **UPSC 2018:** the Earth's magnetic field **has reversed** every few hundred thousand years (A true); early atmosphere had **no 54% oxygen** (B false); living organisms **modified** the early atmosphere (C true) → **A and C only**.
[7:3:5] - Spreading comes in **bursts**: one observed episode – seafloor sank 4 m, spread over 1 m, 160 million m³ of lava in 16 days.

  [7:4:0.1] Convergent (destructive) | Collision / subduction; mountains, trenches | Himalayas (continent–continent); Andes (Nazca under South American); Mariana Trench (ocean–ocean)

## SUB 2 | Faults, Block Mountains and Rift Valleys
[8:2:0] # Faults
[8:2:1] TABLE head: Fault | Force | Movement
  [8:2:1.0] Normal | Tension | Hanging wall down
  [8:2:1.1] Reverse | Compression | Hanging wall up
  [8:2:1.2] Thrust | Compression | Low-angle reverse
  [8:2:1.3] Strike-slip | Shear | Horizontal

## SUB 3 | Diastrophism – Endogenic and Exogenic Forces
[8:3:0] - **Endogenic** (internal heat → mantle dynamics → plate movement): **diastrophism and volcanism**; **earthquakes are endogenic** (APPSC Inspector of Factories). **Exogenic:** weathering, erosion, deposition.
[8:3:1] - **Diastrophism** = all processes that move, raise or build up the crust: orogeny, epeirogeny, **earthquakes**, **plate tectonics**; **"heterogenic" is NOT** one (MPPSC 2023).
[8:3:2] TABLE head: Orogenic | Epeirogenic
  [8:3:2.0] Mountain building; severe folding in narrow belts | Continent building; uplift or warping of large areas
  [8:3:2.1] Horizontal (tangential) forces → folding (State PSC) | Vertical (radial) forces (UKPSC 2024)
[8:3:3] - **APPSC G1 – incorrect statement:** "Orogeny is the continental building process" – orogeny is **mountain** building; epeirogeny is continental building.
[8:3:4] ~~ Sources: NCERT Class 11 Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.5; NextGen Rocket General Geography p.31; app notes 4:8 sub 0
[8:3:5] - **Slow v. sudden:** orogeny and epeirogeny are the slow **diastrophic** movements; the **sudden** endogenic movements – **earthquakes and volcanism** – are treated separately (Savindra Singh). Uplift or warping of whole continents is **epeirogeny**, not orogeny.
[8:3:6] - **Epeirogenic example:** vertical movements of the **Peninsular Block** (marine transgression and regression).
## SUB 4 | Young Fold Mountains – Evidence from the Himalaya
[8:4:0] - **Youngest/most recent range:** the **Himalayas** (CGPSC 2018); within them the **Shiwaliks** are the youngest chain (UPPSC 2010).
[8:4:1] - **Evidence of youth** (UPSC 2012, asked twice → **all four**): **deep gorges**, **U-turn river courses**, **parallel mountain ranges**, **steep gradients causing landslides**.
[8:4:2] - Formed by the **northward movement of the Indian Plate** squeezing the **Tethys** sediments; main uplift Eocene–Pliocene.
[8:4:3] - **Tertiary (Alpine) orogeny:** Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies, Atlas, Kunlun. **Caledonian:** Appalachians (old).
[8:4:4] ~~ Sources: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment ch.2; PMF Physical Geography 02 p.48 (p.135); NextGen Rocket General Geography p.33; app notes 4:8 sub 1
## SUB 5 | Folds
[8:5:0] - **Anticline** = **upfold** (arch; oldest strata in the core) (NDA 2015); **syncline** = downfold (trough).
[8:5:1] - Rising pressure: symmetrical → asymmetrical → overturned → **recumbent** (axial plane horizontal; **both limbs horizontal and parallel** – NDA 2019) → **nappe** (recumbent fold broken and thrust far forward).
[8:5:2] - **Isoclinal** fold = limbs parallel to each other (and to the axial plane).
[8:5:3] - Shiwaliks: simple anticline–syncline folds (Jura type). Cuddapah sediments lie in **synclinal troughs**.
[8:5:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 02 p.47-49 (pp.134-136); app notes 4:8 sub 3
## SUB 6 | Isostasy and Geosynclines
[8:6:0] - **Isostasy:** crustal blocks float in balance on denser material below.
[8:6:1] TABLE head: Airy | Pratt
  [8:6:1.0] Uniform density, varying thickness | Varying density, same depth of compensation
  [8:6:1.1] Mountains have deep roots | Higher blocks are lighter
[8:6:2] - **Kober's geosynclinal theory:** long shallow sediment-filled troughs (geosynclines) between rigid forelands (**kratogens**) are squeezed into mountains; Tethys → Himalaya. Stages: **lithogenesis** (sedimentation) → **orogenesis** → **gliptogenesis**.

## SUB 2 | Magma, Lava and Volcanic Materials
[9:2:0] - **Magma** = molten rock **below** the surface (~600–1,200°C, in a magma chamber); **lava** = magma at the surface. "Molten material inside the Earth" = magma → "None of the above" when only lava, basalt, obsidian are offered (UKPSC 2006).
[9:2:1] - Solidified magma/lava → **igneous rocks**.
[9:2:2] TABLE head: Acidic (felsic) lava | Basic (mafic) lava
  [9:2:2.0] Silica-rich, viscous; solidifies faster, does not flow far | Silica-poor, fluid, flows far
  [9:2:2.1] Explosive eruptions; steep cones, stratovolcanoes | Quiet outpourings; shield volcanoes, lava plateaus (basalt)
[9:2:3] - **More silica → faster solidification** (APPSC Dental Asst Surgeons).
[9:2:4] - **Products of eruptions (UPSC 2024 → all four):** pyroclastic debris, ash and dust, **nitrogen** and **sulphur** compounds; gases = **water vapour (most abundant)** > CO₂ > SO₂. Pyroclasts: ash, lapilli, bombs; pyroclastic flows and **lahars** (mudflows) come from stratovolcanoes.
[9:2:5] - SO₂ aerosols scatter sunlight and **cool the Earth for years**; ash falls back within weeks.
[9:2:6] - NDA 2016: eruptions are **accompanied by earthquakes** and **emit water vapour and dust** – both true, but II does **not explain** I.

  [9:2:8.2] Saffir–Simpson scale | Hurricane intensity
  [9:2:8.3] Anemometer | Wind speed (wind vane gives direction) – the wrong pair

## SUB 4 | Types of Volcanoes – Activity and Form

  [9:4:0.0] Active | Erupts fairly frequently | Barren Island, Stromboli, Etna, Mauna Loa

[9:4:1] # By form
[9:4:2] TABLE head: Form | Shape | Examples

  [9:4:2.2] Cinder cone | Small steep cone of ash, cinders, scoria around one vent | Cerro Negro (Nicaragua)

## SUB 0 | Measurement – Magnitude, Intensity, Scales and Instruments
[10:0:0] TABLE head: Feature | Magnitude | Intensity
  [10:0:0.0] Measures | Energy released at the focus (from the amplitude of seismic waves) | Degree of shaking and damage observed at a place
  [10:0:0.1] Nature | Quantitative; one value per earthquake | Qualitative; varies from place to place, falls with distance from the epicentre
  [10:0:0.2] Scale | Richter (Charles F. Richter, 1935); now Moment Magnitude (Mw) | Modified Mercalli (MMI), MSK-64, old Rossi-Forel – all I to XII
[10:0:1] - **Richter scale:** logarithmic, based on the **amplitude of seismic waves**; each whole number = **10× amplitude** and about **31–32× energy** (two steps ≈ 1,000×) – **not** a hundredfold rise in energy (UPSC 2001).
[10:0:2] - **Moment magnitude (Mw):** based on the **rupture area of the fault** and the size of the waves; preferred over Richter for large quakes (Richter works best for regional quakes up to ~M5).
[10:0:3] - **"Richter measures intensity"** is strictly wrong (Richter = magnitude, Mercalli = intensity), but when the options offer only Richter as a seismic scale, the keyed answer is Richter (APPSC 2017, UKPSC 2002, WBCS 2020). When both are offered, intensity = **Mercalli** (APPSC 2018).
[10:0:4] - **Mercalli scale** → measures **earthquakes** (intensity, from observed effects) – not cyclones, floods or tornadoes. **Mercator** is a map projection, not a seismic scale.
[10:0:5] - **MSK-64** (Medvedev–Sponheuer–Karnik) – the intensity scale recommended in **1964** at the UNESCO intergovernmental meeting; used in India (BIS zones are defined in MSK).
[10:0:6] TABLE head: Magnitude | Effect (PMF / USGS)
  [10:0:6.0] below 3.0 | Micro/minor – recorded, rarely felt, no damage
  [10:0:6.1] 3.0–3.9 | Often felt, very rarely damage
  [10:0:6.2] 4.0–4.9 | Light – felt by most, none to minimal damage
  [10:0:6.3] 5.0–5.9 | Moderate – damages poorly built houses
  [10:0:6.4] 6.0–6.9 | Strong (Latur 1993 6.2–6.4)
  [10:0:6.5] 7.0–7.9 | Major (Bhuj 2001 Mw 7.7)
  [10:0:6.6] 8.0 + | Great (Assam–Tibet 1950 8.6; Chile 1960 9.5)

[10:0:8] TABLE head: Instrument | Use
  [10:0:8.0] Seismograph | Detects and records seismic waves; the readings give magnitude (and, in the books, "the device for intensity")
  [10:0:8.1] Seismometer | The sensor (pendulum / spring mass) inside a seismograph
  [10:0:8.2] Seismogram | The recording itself
  [10:0:8.3] Seismoscope | Only shows that a tremor occurred and its time

## SUB 2 | Focus, Epicentre and Basic Terms
[10:2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [10:2:0.0] Focus / hypocentre | Point inside the Earth where the earthquake originates – energy is released here
  [10:2:0.1] Epicentre | Point on the surface directly above the focus; first surface point to receive the waves; intensity and damage are greatest here
  [10:2:0.2] Isoseismal line | Joins places of equal intensity
  [10:2:0.3] Homoseismal (coseismal) line | Joins places where the shock arrives at the same time (CDS 2014)
  [10:2:0.4] Seismology | Branch of geology that studies earthquakes; the scientist = seismologist
[10:2:1] - **John Milne** = **Father of Modern Seismology** (horizontal seismograph).
[10:2:2] - Wrong statement traps: "epicentre is directly **below** the focus"; "the point of origin is the epicentre".
[10:2:3] - **Depth of focus:** shallow **0–70 km**, intermediate 70–300 km, deep 300–700 km. **Shallow-focus** quakes are the **most damaging** (energy hits a small area); deep quakes spread energy over a wide area.
[10:2:4] - Foreshock (before), aftershocks (after), swarm (many small quakes); **seismic doublet** = two independent quakes of similar magnitude close in time and place.
[10:2:5] - Rare continental-mantle quakes start 80+ km below the Moho; **Stanford** made the first global map of them. **26 Jun 2026**: USGS-described doublet at **Caracas, Venezuela** (M 7.1 and 7.5) (app CA).

## SUB 3 | Earthquake as a Disaster – Classification and Prediction
[10:3:0] - An earthquake is a **natural**, **geological** disaster – **not** an atmospheric one (blizzards, lightning, hailstorms are atmospheric). War, industrial and train accidents are man-made.
[10:3:1] - **High Powered Committee (J.C. Pant, 1999)** – "Geologically related disasters": **landslides and mudflows, earthquakes, dam failures/dam bursts, mine fires**. Cattle epidemics = biological. **Sea surge / storm surge** = hydro-meteorological, **not** geological.
[10:3:2] - **Prediction:** earthquakes **cannot yet be predicted accurately** (time, place, magnitude); science only gives **probabilities** of future events from data. Thunderstorms, floods, hailstorms can be forecast to an extent.
[10:3:3] - Possible precursors studied: foreshocks, changes in well-water and **oil-well flow** (pore pressure rising as tectonic stress builds – NDA 2013), radon emission, animal behaviour.
[10:3:4] - **Tremors** are not caused only by earthquakes – a **meteor impact**, volcanic eruption, explosion or collapse can also shake the ground.
[10:3:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Disaster Management p.25, 40, 47-48 (geological disaster, hazard classes); PMF Internal Security & DM 02 p.44 (natural vs anthropogenic); HPC Report 2001 (PreventionWeb); NCERT Class 11 Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.5 (prediction); app notes 4:10 sub 0

## SUB 5 | Effects – Tsunami, Landslides, Liquefaction
[10:5:0] - **Tsunami** (Japanese word) = large waves from sudden **displacement of the sea floor**; most commonly caused by **undersea (sea) earthquakes**; also submarine landslides, volcanic eruptions, explosions, meteor impacts – **not** heavy rain, wind storms or deserts.
[10:5:1] - Tsunamigenic conditions: magnitude above ~6.5, **shallow focus** (< ~70 km), **vertical** sea-floor movement; over 80% from megathrust subduction quakes. For a tsunami to hit the **Indian coast** the quake must be **above M 7**. **Not all** major earthquakes are tsunamigenic; slow-rupture ("tsunami earthquakes") make **larger** tsunamis than their magnitude suggests.
[10:5:2] - India's tsunami warning: **ITEWC at INCOIS, Hyderabad** (set up after the **2004** tsunami).
[10:5:3] TABLE head: Effect | Where / why
  [10:5:3.0] Ground shaking and rupture | Collapse of buildings, dams, bridges; ~95% of quake deaths in poorly built 1–3 storey houses
  [10:5:3.1] Landslides | Fragile Himalaya and North-East
  [10:5:3.2] Liquefaction | Water-saturated soft soils – Indo-Gangetic Plain
  [10:5:3.3] Fires | Broken gas and power lines (San Francisco 1906, Tokyo 1923)
  [10:5:3.4] Floods, seiches | Dam damage; standing waves in lakes and reservoirs
[10:5:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Disaster Management p.10-12 (tsunami, ITEWC); PMF Physical Geography 02 p.102 (book p.189, effects); INCOIS / NDMA tsunami FAQ (M > 7 for the Indian coast); app notes 4:10 sub 6
[10:5:5] - **"Earthquakes don't kill people, buildings do":** most deaths come from the collapse of **badly designed, non-engineered buildings** – so a badly designed large building kills more people than the shaking itself.
[10:5:6] - **Site effect:** ground shaking is **amplified on soft soil** (loose alluvium, filled land) and is least on hard bedrock.
## SUB 6 | Causes and Types of Earthquakes
[10:6:0] TABLE head: Type | Cause | Example
  [10:6:0.0] Tectonic | Sudden release of stress along a fault at plate boundaries – the main cause (tectonism) | Himalaya; San Andreas (transform)
  [10:6:0.1] Volcanic | Magma movement, eruptions; usually milder | Ring of Fire volcanoes
  [10:6:0.2] Reservoir-induced | Water load and seepage reactivate an old fault | Koyna 1967 (M 6.3), Maharashtra
  [10:6:0.3] Intraplate | Hidden old faults in the stable shield | Latur (Killari) 1993, Jabalpur 1997, Bhuj (Kutch)
  [10:6:0.4] Human-induced (other) | Mining, fracking, nuclear tests; low magnitude | –
[10:6:1] - Earthquakes are most likely **near faults**. UPSC 2004: **all four** – development of a fault, movement along a fault, volcanic eruption, folding of rocks – can cause earthquakes.
[10:6:2] - **Koyna**: dam on an **old fault plane**, activated by changes in reservoir water level → region likely to become more quake-prone (R explains A).
[10:6:3] - **Latur 1993**: far from any plate boundary → quakes can occur in **stable areas along hidden faults**.
[10:6:4] - Denudation, rotation and revolution do **not** cause earthquakes. San Andreas movement is tectonic, not induced.
[10:6:5] - **AP**: reservoir-triggered tremors possible near **Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Polavaram** and old Eastern Ghats faults.
[10:6:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Physical Geography 02 p.91-92 (book pp.178-179, fault zones, reservoir-induced Koyna); NCERT Class 11 Fundamentals of Physical Geography ch.3; NextGen Rocket Disaster Management p.26; app notes 4:10 sub 1
[10:6:7] - **Plate settings:** transform boundary – San Andreas; divergent – Mid-Atlantic Ridge; convergent – Himalaya; the Ring of Fire is a convergent (subduction) belt, not a transform one.
