## SUB 0 | Part II – Articles 5 to 11
[0:0] # Constitutional framework
[0:1] - Citizenship = **Part II, Articles 5–11** (Part I = Arts. 1–4 Union and territory; Part III = Arts. 12–35 Fundamental Rights).
[0:2] - Part II only **identifies who became citizens at the commencement (26 January 1950)**; acquisition and loss afterwards are left to **Parliament (Art. 11)** → **Citizenship Act, 1955**.
[0:3] - **Arts. 5–8** are the Articles that define the categories of citizens at commencement (APPSC G2 Mains 2017 key "Articles 5 to 8"); Arts. 9–11 are general provisions.
[0:4] TABLE head: Article | Deals with
  [0:4.0] 5 | Citizenship at the commencement – domiciled in India + (a) born in India, or (b) either parent born in India, or (c) ordinarily resident 5 years before 1950
  [0:4.1] 6 | Migrants from Pakistan to India – cut-off 19 July 1948 (after it, registration after 6 months' residence)
  [0:4.2] 7 | Migrants to Pakistan after 1 March 1947 who returned under a resettlement permit
  [0:4.3] 8 | Persons of Indian origin residing outside India – register at an Indian diplomatic or consular office
  [0:4.4] 9 | Voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship → not a citizen
  [0:4.5] 10 | Continuance of the rights of citizenship, subject to Parliament's law
  [0:4.6] 11 | Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by law
[0:5] - **Wrong statement trap:** "Articles **4 to 10** deal with migrants to Pakistan and persons of Indian origin abroad" – false (that is **Arts. 7 and 8**; Part II is 5–11).
[0:6] - Art. 6 = migrants **FROM** Pakistan; Art. 7 = migrants **TO** Pakistan.
[0:7] # What citizenship gives
[0:8] - Citizen-only rights: **Arts. 15, 16, 19, 29, 30**; the **right to vote** for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies; the **right to become a member** of Parliament and State legislatures – "citizenship means **full civil and political rights**" (CDS 2014: all three).
[0:9] - Citizen-only offices: **President, Vice-President, SC/HC judges, Governor, Attorney General, Advocate General**.
[0:10] - **President:** in India both a citizen **by birth and a naturalised** citizen are eligible; in the **USA only a natural-born** citizen.

## SUB 1 | Acquisition – Five Modes
[1:0] # Modes and Sections of the Citizenship Act, 1955
[1:1] TABLE head: Mode | Section | Key condition
  [1:1.0] Birth | 3 | jus soli (modified) – see periods below
  [1:1.1] Descent | 4 | Born outside India, either parent an Indian citizen at birth; birth registered at an Indian consulate – jus sanguinis
  [1:1.2] Registration | 5 | PIO ordinarily resident 7 years; spouses of citizens; minor children of citizens
  [1:1.3] Naturalisation | 6 | 12 years (11 of the preceding 14 + the last 12 months); oath of allegiance; waivable for distinguished service to science, art, literature, world peace
  [1:1.4] Incorporation of territory | 7 | Centre notifies who become citizens when a territory joins – Goa, Pondicherry, Sikkim
[1:2] - **Five modes:** **Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, Incorporation of territory** (Act passed **1955**).
[1:3] - **Not modes:** **property or land ownership**, **depositing money in Indian banks**, **ethnic identity**; **marriage** is not a separate mode (only a registration category). **Domicile** was a condition only under Art. 5.
[1:4] # Citizenship by birth – three periods
[1:5] TABLE head: Born in India | Rule
  [1:5.0] 26 Jan 1950 – 30 Jun 1987 | Citizen regardless of parents' nationality (1986 Amendment)
  [1:5.1] 1 Jul 1987 – 2 Dec 2004 | At least one parent an Indian citizen
  [1:5.2] From 3 Dec 2004 | Both parents citizens, or one Indian and the other not an illegal migrant
[1:6] - So today a person born in India is a citizen **only if at least one parent is Indian**, subject to other conditions (APPSC ASWO key).
[1:7] - **Excluded** from birth: children of **foreign diplomats** and **enemy aliens**.
[1:8] # Residence periods – then and now
[1:9] - Before the 2003 Act (in force 3 Dec 2004) naturalisation needed **1 year + 4 of the preceding 7 years = 5 years** (APPSC G1 2003 key: 5 years); **now 12 years**; registration of PIO now **7 years**; CAA communities **5 years**.

## SUB 2 | Single Citizenship – No Dual Citizenship
[2:0] - India: **single citizenship** and a **single domicile** for the Union and the States – allegiance only to the Union; **no separate State citizenship** (UPSC 2021: "one citizenship and one domicile" is the only correct statement).
[2:1] - **Source:** **Britain**; **Canada** also has single citizenship.
[2:2] - **USA** and **Switzerland**: **dual** – national + State/cantonal (UPSC 2005: "India and USA both have single citizenship" is false).
[2:3] - India does **not allow dual citizenship** with another country (Art. 9, Sec. 9): a citizen who **acquires another country's citizenship loses Indian citizenship** – APPSC A–R: both true, R explains A (key).
[2:4] TABLE head: Country | Citizenship
  [2:4.0] India, Britain, Canada | Single
  [2:4.1] USA, Switzerland | Dual (national + state)
[2:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams (full text) p.243 (single citizenship, USA, Switzerland, Canada); Indian_Polity Part 1 (src/polity/indian-polity-part1.txt) ch. Citizenship; app notes 2:7 sub 2
## SUB 3 | Citizenship (Amendment) Acts and the CAA, 2019
[3:0] # Amendments to the 1955 Act
[3:1] TABLE head: Year | Change
  [3:1.0] 1986 | Birth from 1 July 1987 needs one parent an Indian citizen
  [3:1.1] 2003 | Birth rule from 3 Dec 2004 (other parent not an illegal migrant); Overseas Citizenship for PIOs of 16 specified countries (Sec. 7A); Sec. 14A (National Register)
  [3:1.2] 2005 | OCI widened to PIOs of all countries except Pakistan and Bangladesh
  [3:1.3] 2015 | PIO card merged with OCI → "OCI Cardholder"
  [3:1.4] 2019 (CAA) | Route for 6 communities from 3 countries; naturalisation residence cut to 5 years
[3:2] - **No amendment in 2008** (APPSC key); the 1986 Act took effect on 1 July 1987 – there is no "1987 Act".
[3:3] - Basis moved from **jus soli to jus sanguinis**.
[3:4] # CAA, 2019
[3:5] TABLE head: Stage | Date
  [3:5.0] Lok Sabha | 9 December 2019
  [3:5.1] Rajya Sabha (Parliament passed) | 11 December 2019
  [3:5.2] President's assent | 12 December 2019
  [3:5.3] Rules effective | 11 March 2024
[3:6] - **Purpose:** citizenship for **persecuted minorities** – **Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi, Christian** (**six**; Muslims not covered) from **Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Pakistan** (not Sri Lanka) – A–R: both true, R explains A.
[3:7] - Cut-off: entered India **on or before 31 December 2014**; citizenship counts **from the date of entry**.
[3:8] - **Exempt:** **Sixth Schedule** tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Tripura and **Inner Line Permit** areas.
[3:9] - **2026:** Citizenship (Amendment) Rules, 2026 – affidavit on any Pakistani, Afghan or Bangladeshi passport, **surrender within 15 days** of approval; **19 Aug 2026 MHA order** – applications in 8 States/UTs processed by **District Collectors**; challenge under **Art. 14** pending before a Bench under **CJI Surya Kant**.

## SUB 4 | Who Regulates Citizenship – Parliament (Art. 11)
[4:0] TABLE head: Authority | Role
  [4:0.0] Parliament (Art. 11) | Alone legislates on and prescribes conditions for acquisition and termination → Citizenship Act, 1955
  [4:0.1] Central Government (Home Ministry) | Administers the Act; determines questions of acquisition of foreign citizenship (Sec. 9(2)); frames Citizenship Rules (2009, amended 2026) under Sec. 18; deprives under Sec. 10
  [4:0.2] Election Commission | Checks citizenship only for electoral rolls (Art. 324, Art. 326 adult suffrage)
[4:1] - **No power** to make citizenship law: **State Legislatures**, the **President**, the **Election Commission**, the Union Cabinet, the Supreme Court, the Law Commission.
[4:2] - "**Exclusive power of determining the issue of citizenship**" (CGPSC 2013) → **Central Government** (Sec. 9(2)); "**competent to prescribe conditions**" → **Parliament**.

## SUB 5 | NRI, PIO and OCI
[5:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [5:0.0] NRI | Indian citizen ordinarily residing outside India; holds an Indian passport
  [5:0.1] PIO | Foreign citizen who, or any of whose ancestors, was an Indian national; card merged into OCI (2015)
  [5:0.2] OCI | Person registered as an OCI Cardholder under Sec. 7A of the Citizenship Act, 1955
  [5:0.3] Commonwealth citizen | Citizen of a member country of the Commonwealth of Nations
  [5:0.4] Single citizenship | One citizenship for all of India
[5:1] - **OCI eligible:** foreign citizens of Indian origin, their **children/minor children of Indian citizens**, **foreign-origin spouses** of Indian citizens or OCIs.
[5:2] - **OCI barred:** if the person, a **parent or grandparent** was a citizen of **Pakistan or Bangladesh** – so an Indian who migrated to Pakistan after Partition is not eligible.
[5:3] - **OCI gets:** **lifelong (multiple-entry) visa**; OCI card alone enough to enter India (since 2016). **Not:** voting, contesting elections, constitutional offices; OCIs are **not citizens**.
[5:4] - **2015 Amendment** did **not** give NRIs any new voting right; **2026 Rules:** e-OCI cards.
[5:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams (full text) p.243 (OCI and PIO merger); Indian_Polity Part 1 (src/polity/indian-polity-part1.txt) ch. Citizenship (OCI rights); app notes 2:7 sub 7
## SUB 6 | Loss of Citizenship – Three Modes
[6:0] TABLE head: Mode | Section | How
  [6:0.0] Renunciation | 8 | Voluntary, by declaration; minor children lose it too but may resume within 1 year of majority
  [6:0.1] Termination | 9 | Automatic on voluntarily acquiring foreign citizenship
  [6:0.2] Deprivation | 10 | Compulsory, by the Central Government; only citizens by naturalisation, registration or Art. 5(c)
[6:1] - **Deprivation grounds:** fraud; **disloyalty** to the Constitution; helping an enemy in war; **2 years' imprisonment within 5 years** of naturalisation/registration; **7 years' continuous residence abroad**.
[6:2] - **A naturalised citizen can be deprived** (Sec. 10) – "never deprived" is false.
[6:3] - **No effect** on citizenship: **marrying a foreigner**, **criticising the government**, bank deposits, property → loss only by **renouncing** or **acquiring foreign citizenship** (UPPSC: **1 and 2 only**); all three modes → "All of the above" (CGPSC 2013).
[6:4] TABLE head: Section | Subject
  [6:4.0] 7A | Registration of OCI cardholders
  [6:4.1] 14A | National Register of Indian Citizens and national identity card
  [6:4.2] 18 | Power to make Rules

## SUB 7 | NRC, NPR and Proof of Citizenship
[7:0] - **NRC (Assam):** identifies **genuine citizens in Assam**; eligibility = names in the **NRC of 1951** or electoral rolls up to **24 March 1971** (cut-off of the **Assam Accord, 1985**); **draft NRC published for Assam** (2018). The **2019 CAA was not an NRC amendment** – the Accord was given effect by **Sec. 6A** (1985).
[7:1] - **NRC** (citizens only) is a **subset of the NPR** (all usual residents); Sec. **14A** of the Citizenship Act.
[7:2] - **Not proof of citizenship:** **Aadhaar** (identity only, **Sec. 9, Aadhaar Act**), voter ID, ration card, passport.
