=== NOTES 2:6 | Union and Its Territory – Articles 1-4, New States and Cession
## SUB 0 | Articles 1–4 – the Union and Its Territory
[6:0:0] # What Each Article Says
[6:0:1] TABLE head: Article | Subject
  [6:0:1.0] 1 | "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States"; States and their territories listed in the First Schedule
  [6:0:1.1] 2 | Admission or establishment of new States (from outside India)
  [6:0:1.2] 3 | Formation of new States; alteration of areas, boundaries or names of existing States
  [6:0:1.3] 4 | Laws under Arts. 2 and 3 amend the First and Fourth Schedules; not deemed an amendment under Article 368
[6:0:2] - India is a **"Union"**: not the result of an agreement among States; **no State has a right to secede**.
[6:0:3] - "**An indestructible Union of destructible States**".
[6:0:4] - A **new State** created → the **First Schedule must be amended** (it lists States and UTs).
[6:0:5] # Union of India vs Territory of India
[6:0:6] TABLE head: Union of India | Territory of India (Art. 1(3))
  [6:0:6.0] States only – the members of the federal system | States + Union Territories + territories that may be acquired
  [6:0:6.1] Narrower concept | Wider concept
[6:0:7] - As a sovereign State, India can **acquire foreign territory** by **cession, treaty or conquest** – e.g. **Puducherry, Goa** (acquired territories).
[6:0:8] - Acquired territory becomes part of the Territory of India; its people become citizens by **incorporation of territory**.
[6:0:9] 'Union of India' does not include UTs; 'Territory of India' does.
[6:0:10] Part I (Arts. 1–4) = Union and its Territory; First Schedule = names and territories of States and UTs.
## SUB 1 | Article 3 Procedure and Article 4
[6:1:0] # Steps Under Article 3
[6:1:1] - 1. Bill introduced in Parliament only on the **recommendation of the President**.
[6:1:2] - 2. President **refers the Bill to the State legislature concerned** to express its views within a specified period.
[6:1:3] - 3. Parliament is **not bound** by those views.
[6:1:4] - 4. Passed by a **simple majority** in the ordinary legislative process.
[6:1:5] # Article 4
[6:1:6] - A law under Article 2 or 3 is **not an amendment for the purposes of Article 368** – hence simple majority.
[6:1:7] - **Renaming Kerala → 'Keralam'**: Union Cabinet approved; **Article 3 procedure** started.
[6:1:8] # What Parliament Can Do Under Article 3
[6:1:9] - Form a new State by **separating territory** from a State, **uniting** two or more States or parts, or uniting territory with a part of a State.
[6:1:10] - **Increase or diminish** the area of a State; alter its **boundaries** or **name**.
[6:1:11] - **Cannot** cede Indian territory to a foreign country (Berubari, 1960) – that needs an **Art. 368** amendment.
[6:1:12] Article 3 laws need only a simple majority – not the special majority of Article 368.

## SUB 3 | Linguistic Reorganisation – Andhra to 1956
[6:3:0] # Andhra State Act, 1953
[6:3:1] - **Telugu districts** separated from **Madras State**.
[6:3:2] - **Andhra State (1953)** = **first linguistic State**, formed on **1 October 1953** after **Potti Sriramulu's** death (**15 December 1952**).
[6:3:3] # States Reorganisation Act, 1956
[6:3:4] - Came into force **1 November 1956**; created **14 States + 6 UTs**.
[6:3:5] - **Andhra State + Telangana region of Hyderabad = Andhra Pradesh**.
[6:3:6] - **Kerala** formed; **Zonal Councils** set up.
[6:3:7] - UTs replaced the **Part C and Part D States** – **7th Amendment, 1956**.
[6:3:8] - **Capital of Andhra State (1953):** **Kurnool**; Hyderabad became the capital when Andhra Pradesh was formed in 1956.

## SUB 6 | Union Territories – Number and Administration
[6:6:0] # Current Count (2026)
[6:6:1] - **28 States and 8 UTs** (APPSC 2021 key).
[6:6:2] - 8 UTs: **Andaman and Nicobar, Chandigarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, J&K, NCT of Delhi, Ladakh, Lakshadweep, Puducherry**.
[6:6:3] - **DNH + Daman and Diu** merged, effective **26 Jan 2020**.
[6:6:4] - **Dadra and Nagar Haveli** was under **Portuguese** rule (not French) until **1954**.
[6:6:5] # Administration
[6:6:6] - **Art. 239**: President administers each UT through an **administrator**.
[6:6:7] - **Art. 239(2)**: Governor of an **adjoining State** may be administrator – e.g. **Punjab Governor for Chandigarh**.
[6:6:8] - **Governor as UT administrator:** acts **independently of his State Council of Ministers**, as the President's administrator.
[6:6:9] - **Delhi**: a UT (First Schedule); **National Capital Region**; special status by the **69th Amendment, 1991**.

=== NOTES 2:7 | Citizenship – Articles 5-11, Citizenship Act 1955, Oci and Caa 2019
## SUB 0 | Part II – Articles 5 to 11
[7:0:0] # Constitutional Framework
[7:0:1] - Citizenship = **Part II, Articles 5–11** (⚠ APPSC key (2017) gave Articles 5 to 8); it names only who were citizens **at commencement (26 January 1950)**; the rest is left to Parliament (**Art. 11**).
[7:0:2] - Neighbouring Parts: **Part I (Arts. 1–4)** Union and territory; **Part III (Arts. 12–35)** Fundamental Rights.
[7:0:3] # Article-wise
[7:0:4] TABLE head: Article | Deals with
  [7:0:4.0] 5 | At commencement – domiciled in India + (a) born in India, or (b) either parent born in India, or (c) ordinarily resident 5 years before 1950
  [7:0:4.1] 6 | Migrants from Pakistan to India – permit cut-off 19 July 1948
  [7:0:4.2] 7 | Migrants to Pakistan after 1 March 1947 (returnees under resettlement permit)
  [7:0:4.3] 8 | Persons of Indian Origin abroad – register at an Indian diplomatic or consular office
  [7:0:4.4] 9 | Voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship → automatic loss
  [7:0:4.5] 10 | Continuance of rights of citizenship, subject to Parliament's law
  [7:0:4.6] 11 | Parliament regulates acquisition, termination and all related matters by law
[7:0:5] Art. 6 = migrants FROM Pakistan; Art. 7 = migrants TO Pakistan.
## SUB 1 | Competent Authority to Regulate Citizenship
[7:1:0] TABLE head: Authority | Role
  [7:1:0.0] Parliament (Art. 11) | Alone legislates on citizenship → Citizenship Act, 1955
  [7:1:0.1] Union Home Ministry | Frames Citizenship Rules (2009, amended 2026) under Section 18
  [7:1:0.2] Election Commission | Checks citizenship only for electoral rolls (Art. 324); doubtful cases go to the Centre
[7:1:1] - **No power** to make citizenship laws: **State Legislatures**, the **President**, the **Election Commission**.
[7:1:2] One exam key gave "the President" for "exclusive power of determining citizenship"; constitutionally, rules for getting citizenship are set by **Parliament (Art. 11)**.
## SUB 2 | Single vs Dual Citizenship
[7:2:0] - India: **single citizenship** and a **single domicile** for Union and States – a **unitary feature**; allegiance only to the Union; **no separate State citizenship**.
[7:2:1] - **Source**: **Britain**; **Canada** also has single citizenship.
[7:2:2] - **USA** and **Switzerland**: **dual** – national + State/cantonal.
[7:2:3] - India does **not allow dual citizenship** (Art. 9) – APPSC 2023: A and R both true, R explains A.
[7:2:4] TABLE head: Country | Citizenship
  [7:2:4.0] India, Britain, Canada | Single
  [7:2:4.1] USA, Switzerland | Dual (national + state)
[7:2:5] # Citizen-only Rights and Offices
[7:2:6] - Rights: **Arts. 15, 16, 19, 29, 30**; right to **vote and contest**.
[7:2:7] - Offices: **President, Vice-President, judges, Governor, Attorney General**.
[7:2:8] - **President (Art. 58)**: in India both a citizen **by birth and a naturalised** citizen are eligible; in the **USA only a natural-born** citizen.
## SUB 3 | Acquisition – Five Modes (b-d-r-n-i)
[7:3:0] # Modes and Sections
[7:3:1] TABLE head: Mode | Section | Key condition
  [7:3:1.0] Birth | 3 | jus soli (modified) – see 4.2
  [7:3:1.1] Descent | 4 | Born outside India; either parent an Indian citizen at birth; birth registered at an Indian consulate – jus sanguinis
  [7:3:1.2] Registration | 5 | PIO ordinarily resident 7 years; spouses and minor children of citizens
  [7:3:1.3] Naturalisation | 6 | 12 years (11 of preceding 14 + last 12 months); oath of allegiance; waivable for distinguished service to science, art, literature, world peace
  [7:3:1.4] Incorporation of territory | 7 | Centre notifies citizens when a territory joins – Goa, Pondicherry, Sikkim
[7:3:2] # Citizenship by Birth – Three Periods
[7:3:3] TABLE head: Born in India | Rule
  [7:3:3.0] 26 Jan 1950 – 30 Jun 1987 | Citizen regardless of parents' nationality
  [7:3:3.1] 1 Jul 1987 – 2 Dec 2004 | At least one parent an Indian citizen
  [7:3:3.2] From 3 Dec 2004 | Both parents citizens, or one Indian and the other not an illegal migrant
[7:3:4] - **Excluded** from birth: children of **foreign diplomats** and **enemy aliens**.
[7:3:5] # What is NOT a Mode
[7:3:6] - **Marriage** – not a separate mode (a registration category).
[7:3:7] - **Depositing money in Indian banks**.
[7:3:8] - **Ownership of land or other property** in India.
[7:3:9] Five modes (APPSC 2023): Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, Incorporation of territory.
## SUB 4 | Loss – Three Modes (r-t-d)
[7:4:0] TABLE head: Mode | Section | How
  [7:4:0.0] Renunciation | 8 | Voluntary, by declaration; minor children lose it too but may resume within 1 year of majority
  [7:4:0.1] Termination | 9 | Automatic on voluntarily acquiring foreign citizenship (linked to Art. 9)
  [7:4:0.2] Deprivation | 10 | Compulsory, by the Centre; only citizens by naturalisation, registration or Art. 5(c)
[7:4:1] - Deprivation grounds: **fraud**, **disloyalty**, **unlawful trade with / helping an enemy** during war, **2 years' jail within 5 years** of naturalisation/registration.
[7:4:2] - **Neither give nor take** citizenship: bank deposits, property, **marrying a foreigner**, **criticising the government**.
[7:4:3] "A naturalised citizen can never be deprived of citizenship" – FALSE (Section 10).
[7:4:4] # Other Key Sections
[7:4:5] TABLE head: Section | Subject
  [7:4:5.0] 7A | Registration of OCI cardholders
  [7:4:5.1] 14A | National Register of Indian Citizens and national identity card
  [7:4:5.2] 18 | Power to make Rules

## SUB 7 | Diaspora Terms and Oci

[7:7:2] # OCI Cardholder

[7:7:7] - **Rules 2026**: **e-OCI cards**, **central OCI database**, online application and renunciation; a **minor cannot hold another passport** with an Indian one.
