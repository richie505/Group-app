=== Union Territories – Count, Administration and Legislatures
# How many States and UTs
- **Now (2026): 28 States and 8 Union Territories** (APPSC 2021 key; Indian_Polity Part 1 p.48).
- **29 States + 7 UTs** was true only from 2014 (Telangana) to **5 August 2019**; **28 + 7** was the count from 1987 to 2014 (APPSC Draftsman 2012 key) and again from 31 Oct 2019 to 25 Jan 2020; **28 + 8** from **26 January 2020** (merger of DNH and Daman & Diu).
- **8 UTs:** **Andaman and Nicobar, Chandigarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Delhi (NCT), Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Lakshadweep, Puducherry**.
- **Goa is a State** (since 1987), not a UT; **Delhi is a UT** (First Schedule) with special status as the **National Capital Territory** by the **69th Amendment, 1991**.
- **Smallest UT** by area: **Lakshadweep** (about 32 sq km); largest: **Ladakh**.
- UTs replaced the **Part C and Part D States** under the **7th Amendment, 1956**.
# Puducherry – spread over three States
- **Puducherry** UT = **Puducherry and Karaikal (in Tamil Nadu)**, **Mahe (in Kerala)** and **Yanam (in Andhra Pradesh)** → spread over **3 States**; **Yanam** is the part of a UT located in AP.
- **Coastal UTs (other than island UTs):** **Puducherry** and **Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu** → **two** (Lakshadweep and A&N are islands).
- **Dadra and Nagar Haveli** was under **Portuguese** rule (not French) until **1954**; **Diu** is an island in the **Gulf of Khambhat**; **Daman and Diu** were separated from Goa in 1987 (Goa statehood, 56th Amendment).
# Administration
- **Art. 239:** the **President administers every UT through an Administrator** (LG / Chief Commissioner / Administrator).
- **Art. 239(2):** the **Governor of an adjoining State** may be the administrator – the **Governor of Punjab is the Administrator of Chandigarh**; he then acts **independently of his State Council of Ministers**.
- **Lakshadweep** has its own **Administrator**, not the Governor of Kerala (UPSC 2009: only the Chandigarh statement is correct).
- **Art. 240:** the **President makes regulations** for **A&N Islands, Lakshadweep, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Ladakh**, and for **Puducherry** only while its legislature is suspended or dissolved (the old "Goa, Daman and Diu" entry was omitted in 1987).
# Legislatures and High Courts
| UT | Legislature | High Court |
|---|---|---|
| Delhi | **Art. 239AA** (69th, 1991) | **Own High Court** (the only UT with one of its own) |
| Puducherry | **Art. 239A** (14th, 1962) | Madras HC |
| Jammu and Kashmir | J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019 (Art. 239A applied) | Common HC of J&K and Ladakh |
| Ladakh | **None** | Common HC of J&K and Ladakh |
| Chandigarh | None | Punjab and Haryana HC |
| Lakshadweep | None | **Kerala HC** |
| A&N Islands | None | Calcutta HC |
| DNH and Daman & Diu | None | **Bombay HC** (also covers Goa) |
- **"State" in Art. 54 (election of the President)** includes **NCT of Delhi and UT of Puducherry** (Explanation added by the **70th Amendment, 1992**); the 2019 Act applied Art. 239A to J&K but did not amend Art. 54.
> Bombay HC covers Maharashtra, Goa, DNH and Daman & Diu – **Lakshadweep** is the odd one out (APSLPRB SI 2019).

# UT entries by amendment
- **Goa, Daman and Diu** made a UT → **12th Amendment, 1962**; **Pondicherry** → **14th Amendment, 1962**.
# Sikkim
- **35th Amendment (1974):** Sikkim an **"associate State"** – the **first and only** associate State (inserted **Art. 2A** and the **Tenth Schedule**); came into force 1 March 1975.
- **36th Amendment (1975):** after a **referendum** and **abolition of the monarchy**, Sikkim became a **full State – the 22nd** (omitted Art. 2A and the Tenth Schedule); Assembly of **not less than 32 members**. Today Sikkim is simply **part of the Indian territory** (a State in the First Schedule).
- **Government of Sikkim Act, 1974** set up its first elected government.
# North-East facts
- **Arunachal Pradesh:** capital **Itanagar**, named after **Ita Fort** ('fort of bricks'); two National Parks – **Namdapha, Mouling**.
- **Tripura:** **Part C State** (1950) → **UT** (1956) → **full State** (1972).
- **Nagaland:** created by the **State of Nagaland Act, 1962**, with the **13th Amendment** inserting **Art. 371A** – UPSC 2025 accepted "came into existence on the basis of a Constitutional Amendment Act" (all three pairs correct).

=== Articles 1–4 – Union, Territory and the First Schedule
# What each Article says
| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| 1 | "**India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States**"; States and territories in the **First Schedule**; Art. 1(3) territory of India |
| 2 | **Admission or establishment of new States** (not part of the Union of India) |
| 3 | **Formation of new States; alteration of areas, boundaries or names** of existing States |
| 4 | Laws under Arts. 2 and 3 amend the **First and Fourth Schedules**; **Art. 4(2): not deemed an amendment under Art. 368** |
- Part I = **Articles 1–4**; the Constitution calls the Centre the **"Union"** (Union Government, Union List), not "Centre".
- **"Union" not "Federation" (Ambedkar):** the Indian federation is **not the result of an agreement among States**, and **no State has the right to secede** – "an **indestructible Union of destructible States**".
# Union of India vs Territory of India
| Union of India | Territory of India (Art. 1(3)) |
|---|---|
| **States only** – members of the federal system sharing powers with the Union | **States + Union Territories + territories that may be acquired** |
- So **UTs are covered by "Territory of India" and not by "Union of India"** (APPSC G1: **Only IV**); both expressions are in the Constitution, and the **First Schedule** lists **States and UTs**.
- Grama Sachivalayam 2020: the **Union of India** includes **States only (A only)**.
- **Acquiring territory:** India as a sovereign State acquires foreign territory **"as understood in public international law"** – cession, conquest, occupation (**N. Masthan Sahib v. Chief Commissioner, Pondicherry**); e.g. **Pondicherry, Goa, Daman and Diu, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Sikkim**. Such territory need not join an existing State (it may be a UT).
# First Schedule
- **First Schedule = names of the States and UTs and their territorial extent** → it **must be amended to create a new State** (UPSC 2001; APPSC G2 Mains 2019; APPSC HO 2021). Fourth Schedule = Rajya Sabha seats (also amended).
# Article 3 – power and procedure
- **Power vests in Parliament** (not the President, the Council of Ministers or an SRC): form a new State by **separating** territory, **uniting** States or parts, uniting territory to a part of a State; **increase or diminish** area; alter **boundaries or name**.
- **Admission of new States is NOT under Art. 3** – it is **Art. 2**; creating a new State out of existing States is **Art. 3** (CGPSC 2014).
- **Procedure:** Bill only on the **President's recommendation**; President **refers it to the State legislature concerned** for views within a time limit; **Parliament is not bound** by the views; **no fresh reference** needed for each amendment to the Bill; for a **UT, no reference** at all.
- **Consent of the State is not required** to alter its boundaries – Parliament acts "without their consent".
- Passed by a **simple majority** in the ordinary legislative process (**Art. 4(2)**).
- **Renaming Kerala → 'Keralam'**: Union Cabinet approval began the **Article 3** procedure.
- Parliament **cannot cede** territory under Art. 3 – cession needs an **Art. 368** amendment (see Cession).

=== Reorganisation Commissions, Andhra State and the SRA 1956
# Commissions
| Body | Year | Members | Recommended |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dhar Commission** | June 1948 | **S.K. Dhar** (chairman), Panna Lall, Jagat Narain Lal | **Administrative convenience, not language** |
| **JVP Committee** (Congress) | December 1948 | **J**awaharlal Nehru, **V**allabhbhai Patel, **P**attabhi Sitaramayya | **Rejected language** as the basis |
| **Fazl Ali Commission (SRC)** | **22 December 1953** | **Fazl Ali** (chairman), **K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru** | Broadly **accepted language**, rejected "one language–one State"; reported **30 Sept 1955** |
- **Dhar Commission** was appointed by **Rajendra Prasad** (President of the Constituent Assembly); it held linguistic States a threat to national unity.
- **Pattabhi Sitaramayya** was in the **JVP Committee**, **not in the SRC**.
- **SRC appointment:** announced **22 December 1953** (MHA resolution dated 29 Dec 1953); suggested **Telangana** stay separate till about **1961**.
- **SRC's four factors:** unity and security of India; linguistic and cultural homogeneity; financial, economic and administrative considerations; welfare of the people.
- **SRC recommended:** abolish the **Part A/B/C** classification; abolish the **Rajpramukh** institution and special agreements with princely States; abolish the Centre's general control under **Art. 371** (a question that prints "Article 171" makes that item false → "Only C" is not a recommendation).
# Andhra State, 1953
- **Potti Sriramulu** died (**15 December 1952**) after a **56-day fast** → **Andhra State** formed **1 October 1953** from the **Telugu-speaking districts of Madras State** – the **first linguistic State**; capital **Kurnool**.
- Exams also ask "Andhra Pradesh created as a language-based State in **1953**" – they mean Andhra State.
# States Reorganisation Act, 1956
- In force **1 November 1956**: **14 States + 6 UTs**; **Andhra State + Telangana region of Hyderabad = Andhra Pradesh** (capital **Hyderabad**); Kerala formed.
- Large-scale reorganisation **on a linguistic basis = 1956**; the related amendment = **7th Amendment, 1956** (Part A/B/C/D classification ended).
- Set up **five Zonal Councils** (the Indian territory divided into **five zones**).

=== Cession of Territory
| Instance | Year | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Berubari reference** | 1960 | Cession of Berubari (West Bengal) to Pakistan (Nehru–Noon 1958): Art. 3 does not cover cession → **Art. 368 amendment** → **9th Amendment, 1960** |
| SC position | **1969** | **Boundary settlement ≠ cession** – done by **executive action**, no amendment |
| India–Bangladesh LBA | 1974 (2011 protocol) | **111 Indian enclaves to Bangladesh, 51 Bangladeshi enclaves to India**; **100th Amendment, 2015**; amended First Schedule entries of Assam, West Bengal, Meghalaya, Tripura |
| Katchatheevu | **1974–76** | To Sri Lanka by agreements, **no constitutional amendment** |
- The Constitution lets Parliament **change names and admit territories** by ordinary law; **ceding** territory needs a constitutional amendment.
