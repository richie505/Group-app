=== Part II – Articles 5 to 11
# Constitutional framework
- Citizenship = **Part II, Articles 5–11** (Part I = Arts. 1–4 Union and territory; Part III = Arts. 12–35 Fundamental Rights).
- Part II only **identifies who became citizens at the commencement (26 January 1950)**; acquisition and loss afterwards are left to **Parliament (Art. 11)** → **Citizenship Act, 1955**.
- **Arts. 5–8** are the Articles that define the categories of citizens at commencement (APPSC G2 Mains 2017 key "Articles 5 to 8"); Arts. 9–11 are general provisions.
| Article | Deals with |
|---|---|
| 5 | **Citizenship at the commencement** – domiciled in India + (a) born in India, or (b) either parent born in India, or (c) **ordinarily resident 5 years** before 1950 |
| 6 | Migrants **from Pakistan to India** – cut-off **19 July 1948** (after it, registration after 6 months' residence) |
| 7 | Migrants **to Pakistan** after 1 March 1947 who returned under a resettlement permit |
| 8 | **Persons of Indian origin residing outside India** – register at an Indian diplomatic or consular office |
| 9 | **Voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship** → not a citizen |
| 10 | **Continuance of the rights of citizenship**, subject to Parliament's law |
| 11 | **Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by law** |
- **Wrong statement trap:** "Articles **4 to 10** deal with migrants to Pakistan and persons of Indian origin abroad" – false (that is **Arts. 7 and 8**; Part II is 5–11).
- Art. 6 = migrants **FROM** Pakistan; Art. 7 = migrants **TO** Pakistan.
# What citizenship gives
- Citizen-only rights: **Arts. 15, 16, 19, 29, 30**; the **right to vote** for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies; the **right to become a member** of Parliament and State legislatures – "citizenship means **full civil and political rights**" (CDS 2014: all three).
- Citizen-only offices: **President, Vice-President, SC/HC judges, Governor, Attorney General, Advocate General**.
- **President:** in India both a citizen **by birth and a naturalised** citizen are eligible; in the **USA only a natural-born** citizen.

=== Acquisition – Five Modes
# Modes and Sections of the Citizenship Act, 1955
| Mode | Section | Key condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Birth** | **3** | jus soli (modified) – see periods below |
| **Descent** | **4** | Born **outside India**, either parent an Indian citizen at birth; birth registered at an Indian consulate – jus sanguinis |
| **Registration** | **5** | PIO **ordinarily resident 7 years**; spouses of citizens; minor children of citizens |
| **Naturalisation** | **6** | **12 years** (11 of the preceding 14 + the last 12 months); oath of allegiance; waivable for distinguished service to science, art, literature, world peace |
| **Incorporation of territory** | **7** | Centre notifies who become citizens when a territory joins – **Goa, Pondicherry, Sikkim** |
- **Five modes:** **Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, Incorporation of territory** (Act passed **1955**).
- **Not modes:** **property or land ownership**, **depositing money in Indian banks**, **ethnic identity**; **marriage** is not a separate mode (only a registration category). **Domicile** was a condition only under Art. 5.
# Citizenship by birth – three periods
| Born in India | Rule |
|---|---|
| **26 Jan 1950 – 30 Jun 1987** | Citizen **regardless of parents' nationality** (1986 Amendment) |
| 1 Jul 1987 – 2 Dec 2004 | **At least one parent** an Indian citizen |
| From 3 Dec 2004 | **Both parents citizens**, or one Indian and the other **not an illegal migrant** |
- So today a person born in India is a citizen **only if at least one parent is Indian**, subject to other conditions (APPSC ASWO key).
- **Excluded** from birth: children of **foreign diplomats** and **enemy aliens**.
# Residence periods – then and now
- Before the 2003 Act (in force 3 Dec 2004) naturalisation needed **1 year + 4 of the preceding 7 years = 5 years** (APPSC G1 2003 key: 5 years); **now 12 years**; registration of PIO now **7 years**; CAA communities **5 years**.

=== Who Regulates Citizenship – Parliament (Art. 11)
| Authority | Role |
|---|---|
| **Parliament (Art. 11)** | **Alone** legislates on and **prescribes conditions** for acquisition and termination → **Citizenship Act, 1955** |
| **Central Government** (Home Ministry) | Administers the Act; **determines** questions of acquisition of foreign citizenship (**Sec. 9(2)**); frames Citizenship Rules (2009, amended 2026) under **Sec. 18**; deprives under Sec. 10 |
| Election Commission | Checks citizenship only for electoral rolls (Art. 324, Art. 326 adult suffrage) |
- **No power** to make citizenship law: **State Legislatures**, the **President**, the **Election Commission**, the Union Cabinet, the Supreme Court, the Law Commission.
- "**Exclusive power of determining the issue of citizenship**" (CGPSC 2013) → **Central Government** (Sec. 9(2)); "**competent to prescribe conditions**" → **Parliament**.

=== Single Citizenship – No Dual Citizenship
- India: **single citizenship** and a **single domicile** for the Union and the States – allegiance only to the Union; **no separate State citizenship** (UPSC 2021: "one citizenship and one domicile" is the only correct statement).
- **Source:** **Britain**; **Canada** also has single citizenship.
- **USA** and **Switzerland**: **dual** – national + State/cantonal (UPSC 2005: "India and USA both have single citizenship" is false).
- India does **not allow dual citizenship** with another country (Art. 9, Sec. 9): a citizen who **acquires another country's citizenship loses Indian citizenship** – APPSC A–R: both true, R explains A (key).
| Country | Citizenship |
|---|---|
| India, Britain, Canada | Single |
| USA, Switzerland | Dual (national + state) |

=== Citizenship (Amendment) Acts and the CAA, 2019
# Amendments to the 1955 Act
| Year | Change |
|---|---|
| 1986 | Birth from 1 July 1987 needs one parent an Indian citizen |
| 2003 | Birth rule from 3 Dec 2004 (other parent not an illegal migrant); **Overseas Citizenship for PIOs of 16 specified countries** (Sec. 7A); **Sec. 14A** (National Register) |
| 2005 | OCI widened to PIOs of **all countries except Pakistan and Bangladesh** |
| 2015 | **PIO card merged with OCI** → "OCI Cardholder" |
| 2019 (CAA) | Route for **6 communities from 3 countries**; naturalisation residence cut to **5 years** |
- **No amendment in 2008** (APPSC key); the 1986 Act took effect on 1 July 1987 – there is no "1987 Act".
- Basis moved from **jus soli to jus sanguinis**.
# CAA, 2019
| Stage | Date |
|---|---|
| Lok Sabha | 9 December 2019 |
| **Rajya Sabha (Parliament passed)** | **11 December 2019** |
| President's assent | 12 December 2019 |
| Rules effective | 11 March 2024 |
- **Purpose:** citizenship for **persecuted minorities** – **Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi, Christian** (**six**; Muslims not covered) from **Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Pakistan** (not Sri Lanka) – A–R: both true, R explains A.
- Cut-off: entered India **on or before 31 December 2014**; citizenship counts **from the date of entry**.
- **Exempt:** **Sixth Schedule** tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Tripura and **Inner Line Permit** areas.
- **2026:** Citizenship (Amendment) Rules, 2026 – affidavit on any Pakistani, Afghan or Bangladeshi passport, **surrender within 15 days** of approval; **19 Aug 2026 MHA order** – applications in 8 States/UTs processed by **District Collectors**; challenge under **Art. 14** pending before a Bench under **CJI Surya Kant**.

=== Loss of Citizenship – Three Modes
| Mode | Section | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Renunciation** | 8 | **Voluntary**, by declaration; minor children lose it too but may resume within **1 year** of majority |
| **Termination** | 9 | **Automatic** on **voluntarily acquiring foreign citizenship** |
| **Deprivation** | 10 | **Compulsory, by the Central Government**; only citizens by **naturalisation, registration or Art. 5(c)** |
- **Deprivation grounds:** fraud; **disloyalty** to the Constitution; helping an enemy in war; **2 years' imprisonment within 5 years** of naturalisation/registration; **7 years' continuous residence abroad**.
- **A naturalised citizen can be deprived** (Sec. 10) – "never deprived" is false.
- **No effect** on citizenship: **marrying a foreigner**, **criticising the government**, bank deposits, property → loss only by **renouncing** or **acquiring foreign citizenship** (UPPSC: **1 and 2 only**); all three modes → "All of the above" (CGPSC 2013).
| Section | Subject |
|---|---|
| 7A | Registration of OCI cardholders |
| 14A | National Register of Indian Citizens and national identity card |
| 18 | Power to make Rules |

=== NRI, PIO and OCI
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **NRI** | **Indian citizen** ordinarily residing outside India; holds an Indian passport |
| **PIO** | Foreign citizen who, or any of whose ancestors, was an Indian national; card merged into OCI (2015) |
| **OCI** | Person **registered as an OCI Cardholder** under **Sec. 7A** of the Citizenship Act, 1955 |
| Commonwealth citizen | Citizen of a member country of the Commonwealth of Nations |
| Single citizenship | One citizenship for all of India |
- **OCI eligible:** foreign citizens of Indian origin, their **children/minor children of Indian citizens**, **foreign-origin spouses** of Indian citizens or OCIs.
- **OCI barred:** if the person, a **parent or grandparent** was a citizen of **Pakistan or Bangladesh** – so an Indian who migrated to Pakistan after Partition is not eligible.
- **OCI gets:** **lifelong (multiple-entry) visa**; OCI card alone enough to enter India (since 2016). **Not:** voting, contesting elections, constitutional offices; OCIs are **not citizens**.
- **2015 Amendment** did **not** give NRIs any new voting right; **2026 Rules:** e-OCI cards.

=== NRC, NPR and Proof of Citizenship
- **NRC (Assam):** identifies **genuine citizens in Assam**; eligibility = names in the **NRC of 1951** or electoral rolls up to **24 March 1971** (cut-off of the **Assam Accord, 1985**); **draft NRC published for Assam** (2018). The **2019 CAA was not an NRC amendment** – the Accord was given effect by **Sec. 6A** (1985).
- **NRC** (citizens only) is a **subset of the NPR** (all usual residents); Sec. **14A** of the Citizenship Act.
- **Not proof of citizenship:** **Aadhaar** (identity only, **Sec. 9, Aadhaar Act**), voter ID, ration card, passport.
