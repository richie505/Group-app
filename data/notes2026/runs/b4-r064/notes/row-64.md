# Marriage and family
- **Polyandry** (one wife, several husbands): **Toda** (classic), **Khasa**, **Jaunsari** (Jaunsar-Bawar), Kota – "Jaunsaris and Todas" = **III and IV** (UPPSC 2009).
- **Polygamy** = plural marriage, of two kinds: **polyandry** (Toda, Khasa) and **polygyny** – one husband, several wives (Bhil, Gond, Naga, Muria). Exam keys for "which tribe practises polygamy" answer **Toda**; strictly the polygynous tribes are polygamous too.
- **Dudh-lautawa ('Duvautana')** – cross-cousin marriage (to the mother's brother's child, like Telugu *menarikam*): **Gonds of MP**.
- **Ho:** **'Andi'** = marriage by negotiation, **'Oportipi'** = marriage by capture.
- **Santhal:** commonest form = **Bapla** (Kirin bahu bapla, arranged); Itut (forced sindur), Nir-bolok (intrusion), Sanga (widow remarriage) are minor forms.
- **Khasi, Garo, Jaintia:** matrilineal – the youngest daughter (Khasi) inherits.
- **Oraon:** youth dormitory **Ghumkaria**, village chief Mahato; Gond dormitory **ghotul**.
# Economy and crafts
- **Vidyarthi and Rai:** hunters **Birhor, Chenchu**; agriculturists Santhal, Munda; pastoralists Toda; artisans Irula.
- **Hunting / food-gathering tribes:** Chenchu (AP), Kamar (MP/Chhattisgarh), Irula (TN, Kerala), Birhor – the **Meena** of Rajasthan are settled agriculturists (**not a hunting tribe**).
- **Transhumance** = **seasonal movement of people with their herds** between lowland and highland pastures: Bhotiya, Bakarwal (J&K), Gaddi; Maasai = Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda.
- **Kamar** – livelihood on **bamboo** (baskets); **Khairwar** – make **catechu** (khair); **Agaria** – iron smelting; **Bhunjia** – sacred kitchen **'Lal Bangla'** no outsider may enter.
- Longpi pottery – **Tangkhul Naga**; last head-hunters – **Konyak Naga**.
- Sahariya ('Sehara' = wilderness) – starvation deaths in news.
- **Living root bridges** – **Meghalaya** (Khasi and Jaintia hills).
- **Traditional dresses:** Bokhu – Sikkim; Mekhla – Assam; Pheran – Kashmir; **Mundu – Kerala** (not Chhattisgarh).

=== ST Profile – Numbers, Largest Tribes, State Extremes
# National profile (Census 2011)
- STs **10.43 crore = 8.6%** of India (the **5–10%** band); **89.97% rural**; sex ratio **990**.
- **705** notified STs in **30** States/UTs (older: 698).
- **North-East holds only about 12%** of India's STs (≈1.24 crore) – "NE accounts for a little over half" is **not correct** (UPSC 2005).
| Measure | State / UT |
|---|---|
| Largest ST population | Madhya Pradesh (153.17 lakh) |
| Highest share | Lakshadweep 94.8%; Mizoram 94.4% (highest State) > Nagaland 86.5 > Meghalaya 86.1 > Arunachal 68.8 |
| Lowest share | State Goa; UT Andaman & Nicobar |
| Most notified tribes | Odisha (62) |
| Lowest NE share | Tripura ~31.8% (APPSC 2019) |
| No notified STs | Punjab, Haryana, Chandigarh, Delhi, Puducherry |
- NE ascending: **Tripura < Meghalaya < Nagaland < Mizoram**.
# Largest tribes
- Order: **Bhil (1,70,71,049) > Gond (1,32,56,928) > Santhal > Mina** (then Naikda, Oraon). Bhil ≈ 1.7 crore = about **16%** of STs (the "38%" in some books is the Bhil share of **MP's** STs, 37.7%).
- **Census 2001 too: Bhil 1.27 crore > Gond 1.09 crore > Santhal 58 lakh > Mina 38 lakh** – so Bhil is the answer for any year; answer **Gond only when Bhil is not an option** (MPPSC 2013).
- **Largest tribe of Uttarakhand: Tharu** (91 thousand) > Jaunsari > Buksa > Bhotia.
- Minor tribes: Jarawa, Onge, Andamanese, Arandan.
# Who is a tribe
- **Basis of determination:** **cultural specification and distinct habitation** – Lokur criteria: primitive traits, distinctive culture, geographical isolation, shyness of contact, backwardness.
- **No definition in the Constitution:** Art. 366(25) defines STs only by reference to **Art. 342** notification.

=== Andaman & Nicobar Tribes and PVTGs
# Island tribes
| Tribe | Place | Race |
|---|---|---|
| **Jarawa** | South and Middle **Andaman** | Negrito; often called the "most primitive"; tourist-road ban |
| **Onge** | **Little Andaman** (Dugong Creek, South Bay) | Negrito |
| **Sentinelese** | **North Sentinel** Island | Negrito; no contact; tribal reserve since 1956 |
| Great Andamanese | Strait Island | Negrito |
| **Shompen** | **Great Nicobar** | Mongoloid; 229 (2011) |
| Nicobarese | Nicobar Islands | Mongoloid |
- Six native peoples of A&N: Great Andamanese, Jarawa, Onge, Sentinelese, Shompen, Nicobarese – **Rengma** (Naga, Nagaland) is not one of them.
- **"Jarawa – Nicobar Island" (CGPSC 2016):** the Jarawa live in the **Andaman** group of the A&N Islands; the option "Nicobar Island" stands for the UT.
- **Shompen and elections:** reports in **2014** said Shompens voted in the 16th Lok Sabha poll (UPPCS 2014 key); the Election Commission marked **19 April 2024** as their **first vote** in a general election (7 of 98 registered voters, booth "Shompen Hut").
# PVTGs
- Criteria (4): **pre-agricultural technology, low literacy, economic backwardness, declining/stagnant population** (not Sixth Schedule residence).
- Timeline: Dhebar → **52 PTGs (1975)** → +23 (1993) = **75** → renamed **PVTGs 2006** → Feb 2024: 7 variants added (Odisha 4; AP 3: Bondo Porja, Khond Porja, Konda Savara).
- Count: **Odisha 13 > AP + Telangana 12** > Bihar incl. Jharkhand 9.
- Largest PVTG **Sahariya**; smallest **Sentinelese, Great Andamanese**. Baiga – MP; Chenchu – AP; Toda, Kota, Irula – TN.
- **Jharkhand PVTGs:** Asur, Birhor, Birjia, Hill Kharia, **Korwa**, Mal Paharia, Parhaiya, Sauria Paharia, Savar – Kawar, Kora, Karmali are not PVTGs.
- **Chhattisgarh PVTGs:** Abujhmaria, **Baiga (largest)**, Birhor, Pahadi Korwa, Kamar (+ Bharia).
- **Habitat rights (FRA Sec 3(1)(e))**: **Baiga** first in India (Dindori, MP, 2015-16), then Bharia and Kamar; Kamar first in Chhattisgarh (2023 Baiga second there). "Critical Wildlife Habitat" is defined in **FRA 2006**; habitat rights are recognised under FRA by the gram sabha and district committees, with the **Ministry of Tribal Affairs** as nodal ministry – not declared by MoEFCC (UPSC 2018 → "1 and 2 only").
