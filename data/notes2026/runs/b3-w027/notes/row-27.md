=== Vital Rates – Definitions and Key Terms
| Term | Meaning / base |
|---|---|
| **Fertility** | Ability of women to give **live birth** (actual reproductive performance) |
| **CBR / CDR** | Births / deaths per **1,000** mid-year population |
| **IMR** | Deaths of infants **before age 1** per 1,000 live births |
| **Neonatal mortality** | Death **within the first four weeks (28 days)** of life |
| **MMR (maternal mortality ratio)** | Maternal deaths per **1,00,000** live births |
| **TFR** | Average number of live births a woman would have over her reproductive span at current age-specific fertility rates |
| **Replacement-level TFR** | **2.1** children per woman (zero growth in the long run) |
| **Growth rate (natural increase)** | **Birth rate − death rate** in a given period (CBR 30 − CDR 10 = 20 per 1,000 = 2.0%) |
| **Dependency ratio** | (0-14 + 65+) ÷ (15-64) |
| **Youth (National Youth Policy 2014)** | **15-29 years** (approved 9 Jan 2014, replaced NYP 2003) |
- Population change = **births, deaths, emigration and immigration** – all four.
- **Zero population growth:** births (+ immigrants) = deaths (+ emigrants), so the population does not change.
- Birth-rate data arranged **year by year** = **chronological** classification.
- **SRS TFR:** 2016 – **2.3** (rural 2.5, urban 1.8); 2018 – 2.2; **NFHS-4 (2015-16) 2.2**; **NFHS-5 (2019-21) 2.0**; NFHS-5 highest Bihar 3.0; AP 1.7.
- SRS 2024: TFR below replacement for the **fifth year running**.
> IMR is per 1,000 live births; MMR per 1,00,000 – the classic base trap.

=== Demographic Transition and Population Theories
# The model
| Stage | Birth rate | Death rate | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | High | High | Slow growth (pre-industrial) |
| II | High (rises or stays high) | **Falling** | **Population explosion** |
| III | **Falling significantly** | Low | Growth slows |
| IV | Low | Low | Stable or slow growth |
- Order with development: **high birth & high death → high birth & low death → low birth & low death** (UPSC 2012: 2, 3, 1).
- Third stage = **significant fall in the birth rate** (death rate never becomes zero).
- Theory: early form **Warren Thompson (1929)**; formal theory **Frank Notestein** (1940s-50s).
- **India:** late Stage 3, moving into Stage 4; Stage-4 States (2011, low fertility): **Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh**.
- **Urbanisation reduced both birth and death rates** (education, work, medical care).
- Low birth rates / ageing / declining population in news: **Italy, Japan, South Korea** (not Nigeria, South Africa).
# Population theories
| Theory | Propounder |
|---|---|
| Principle of Population (1798): population grows geometrically, food arithmetically; positive and **preventive checks** | **T.R. Malthus** – a theory of **population** |
| Optimum population | **Edwin Cannan** |
| The Population Bomb (1968) | **Paul Ehrlich** |
- Population growth: **larger working population is not an adverse effect**; smaller holdings, unemployment and less food per head are.

=== Demographic Dividend and Its Window
- **Demographic dividend / bonus / window:** growth potential from a rising share of the **working-age (15-64)** population – all three names are used (APPSC G2 2019: "All of these").
- India is called a dividend country because of its **high population aged 15-64** (UPSC 2011); the "demographic bonus" around 2016 = **surge in the productive age group**.
- Working age about **62.9%** (PMF: over 62% aged 15-59; 54% below 25).
- **Census 2011 age shares:** aged 20+ **59.29%**; under 14 about **29% (28.67%)**; 65+ about **5% (4.8%)** – the "5-6%" option.
- **Window:** **2005-06 to 2055-56** (NITI Aayog: until 2055); peak around **2041**, when 20-59 is about **59%** (Economic Survey 2018-19).
- 0-19 share **41% (2011) → 25% (2041)**; 60+ **8.6% → 16% (2041)** and **20.5% (2051)**.
- To obtain full benefits: **promote skill development** (UPSC 2013); without health, education, skills and jobs it becomes a **"demographic nightmare"**.
- Broad-based age pyramid (underdeveloped country): **many young people and few old people**.

=== Dependency Ratio and Harnessing the Dividend
- **Dependent population** in the pyramid: **0-14** (and 65+); working age 15-64.
- India's dependency ratio is falling because the **15-59 (working-age) share is relatively high**, itself due to the **fall in fertility**: **79% (1975) → 58% (2008) → about 48% (2021)**.
- **Old-age dependency ratio (2011): 14.2%** (60+ ÷ 15-59; 10.9% in 1961).
- Lower dependency ratio → **potential demographic dividend**.
- **Female LFPR (PLFS):** 23.3% (2017-18) → **41.7% (2023-24)**; cutting it would weaken the dividend.
- RBI typology: **ageing – Kerala, Tamil Nadu**; wide window – Bihar, UP; narrowing – Telangana, Uttarakhand; Kerala 60+ **22.8% by 2036**.

=== Fertility Determinants, Kerala and Andhra Pradesh
- Birth rate falls with **female education and later marriage**, **child survival**, **urbanisation** and **contraception**; **urbanisation lowers the birth rate and high literacy goes with a low birth rate** (UPPCS 2021: both).
- **Kerala's** lowest population growth: heavy **investment in promoting literacy**, especially female literacy (94% in 2011).
- **Couple Protection Rate (CPR):** share of eligible couples protected by an approved contraceptive method; a rising CPR cuts the birth rate only when the protected couples are **young** (older couples have finished child-bearing).
- More boys than girls are born (about **105 : 100**).
- **Andhra Pradesh:** Stage 4; decadal growth peak **21.13% (1981-91) → 9.21% (2001-11)**; median age **32.5** (India 28); by 2036 under-14 share **25% → 15%**, 60+ **10% → 19%**; NFHS-5 TFR **1.7**.
- AP: Gram Sabhas (May 2026) for feedback on incentives to reverse falling fertility.
