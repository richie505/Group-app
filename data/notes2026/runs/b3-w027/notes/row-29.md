=== Poverty Line in India – Basis and Calorie Norms
- **Poverty line:** the minimum income / consumption level needed for basic needs; a person is poor if income or consumption falls **below that minimum** (APPSC: statements A and B correct).
- It **varies with time and place** (basic needs and prices differ) and is **not** estimated by the Census every ten years (UPPCS 2025: A and R true, R explains A).
- **Basis:** **household consumption expenditure** – **monthly per capita consumption expenditure (MPCE)** – not income: incomes of the self-employed and daily-wage workers are hard to judge and change with seasons. MCQs offering only "income method" among invented options key **income method**.
- **Calorie norm:** **2,400 kcal rural / 2,100 kcal urban** per person per day (ICMR; **Alagh 1979**, Lakdawala 1993); Dandekar-Rath (1971): 2,250 for both.
- Poverty in India is "defined in terms of" **calorie intake**; BPL = whether a person can obtain a **minimum prescribed food basket**.
- **Poverty Line Basket (PLB):** socially accepted minimum basket of basic needs bought in the market.
- Methods used: **Head Count Ratio, calorie intake, household consumption expenditure** (not per capita income); **literacy is not used** to fix the line.
- **Survey:** **NSSO** household consumption expenditure survey (now **HCES**, MoSPI) about every 5 years; estimates by the Planning Commission / **NITI Aayog**; **BPL census** by the Ministry of Rural Development.
- Official poverty 1999-2000 (MRP): about **26% (26.1)**.

=== Types and Concepts of Poverty
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Absolute** | Below a fixed **minimum subsistence / calorie norm**; India's concern; UN definition adopted at **Copenhagen (World Summit for Social Development), 1995** |
| **Relative** | Income **well below the median** of the society – comparison of income groups (top vs bottom fractiles), **not** a fixed calorie norm; persists in **USA, UK**; measured by Lorenz curve / Gini |
| **Subjective** | A person's own perception of his position (perception is **subjective**) |
| **Chronic** | **Always poor + usually poor** – long periods or across generations |
| **Transient** | **Churning poor** (move in and out of poverty – small farmers, seasonal workers) + **occasionally poor** |
| **Culture of poverty** | **Oscar Lewis**, American anthropologist – poverty passed on as a way of life |
- **Cyclic / churning poor:** those who **keep moving between poor and non-poor**.
- Types accepted: **absolute, relative, subjective** – "functional poverty" is not one.
- **Capability approach:** **Amartya Sen**. **Engel's Law:** as income rises, the **share** spent on food **falls**.

=== Causes and Social Indicators of Poverty
- Causes: **slow growth, rapid population growth, unemployment** (unemployment raises the number below the line); a **rising rate of investment reduces** poverty.
- Economic insecurity comes from **poverty, unemployment and falling real wages** – all three.
- Roots of **urban poverty lie in rural areas**: low rural education → migrants get poor urban jobs (A and R true, R explains A).
- **Social indicators:** illiteracy, lack of **safe drinking water** and sanitation, lack of health care, lack of **job opportunities**, malnutrition; **size of the house** and **number of means of transport** are **not** counted. **MPCE** (the poverty line itself) is an economic, not a social, indicator.
- Solving poverty needs **a complex mix of methods**; economic and social issues are both relevant.
- **MPI** (UNDP-OPHI, Alkire-Santos 2010): **health, education, living standards**.

=== Poverty Measures – HCR, Poverty Gap, FGT
| Measure | Captures |
|---|---|
| **Head Count Ratio** | **Incidence** – proportion of population **below the poverty line** |
| **Poverty Gap Index** | **Depth / intensity** – average shortfall from the line |
| **Squared Poverty Gap** | **Severity** – more weight to the poorest |
- All three = **Foster-Greer-Thorbecke (FGT)** family; the **head count** method estimates **poverty**.
- If the poor become poorer but their number is unchanged → **HCR unchanged**, PGI rises.

=== International Poverty Lines and the Global Poor
| Line (per person per day) | Year set | PPP base |
|---|---|---|
| $1 ("dollar a day") | 1990 | 1985 |
| $1.25 | 2008 | 2005 |
| **$1.90** | Oct 2015 | 2011 |
| $2.15 | 2022 | 2017 |
| **$3.00** (latest) | June 2025 | 2021 |
- **ADB Asian Poverty Line (2014): $1.51 a day** (2005 PPP) – the "$1.50" option.
- **World Development Report 2005:** about **80%** of Indians below **US$ 2 a day**.
- Largest number of poor under the World Bank line: **India** (World Bank, 2016); World Data Lab (2018): **Nigeria** may have overtaken India.
- India under $1.25: **421 mn (1981) → 456 mn (2005)**; India below $4.20: **57.7% → 23.9%** (World Bank, Fall 2025); global extreme poor ($2.15) about **9.2%**.
- **World Poverty Clock** (NGO **World Data Lab**): real-time tool to monitor progress against poverty globally.

=== Tendulkar vs Rangarajan and Latest Estimates
| Line (2011-12) | Rural | Urban | Poverty |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tendulkar** (NSS **68th round**, Planning Commission) | **₹816/month (₹27.20/day)** | **₹1,000/month (₹33.33/day)** | **21.9%** (rural **25.7%**, urban 13.7%) |
| Rangarajan | ₹972 (₹32/day) | **₹1,407** (₹47/day) | **29.5%** |
- "33.35% below the line" is **not** a 68th-round figure.
- Tendulkar (Expert Group 2005, report Dec 2009): moved away from calorie anchoring to **MRP** consumption; criticised as **too low**. **MMRP** (7-day recall for some food items) raises measured consumption 10-12% – **first used by Rangarajan**.
- **AP (Tendulkar, 2009-10):** rural line **₹693.80**, urban ₹926.40 per capita per month; AP HCR **9.20%** (2011-12) vs India 21.92%.
- **HCES 2022-23 / 2023-24:** first full MPCE update in over a decade; Tendulkar-line poverty **4.7% → 2.3%**.
