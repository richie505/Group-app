=== Family Planning Programme and National Population Policy 2000
# Milestones
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1952** | **National Family Planning Programme** (First Plan) – **India the first country in the world** to launch one |
| 1976 (16 April) | **First National Population Policy**; marriage age raised to **21 (men) / 18 (women)**; 42nd Amendment put population control in the **Concurrent List** |
| 1977 | Renamed **Family Welfare Programme** after Emergency sterilisations |
| 1996 | **Target-free approach** (not 1986) |
| 1997 | **Reproductive and Child Health (RCH)** |
| **2000** | **NPP 2000** declared **15 February 2000** |
- India is also the **7th largest country by area** – with the 1952 programme, both statements true.
# NPP 2000
- **Target-free, voluntary, informed choice**; two-child norm; delivery through **government, industry and NGOs**.
| Objective | Target |
|---|---|
| Immediate | Meet the **unmet need for contraception**, health infrastructure and personnel, integrated RCH services |
| Medium-term | **TFR 2.1 by 2010** |
| Long-term | **Stable population by 2045** – later **revised to 2070** |
- "Original NPP target" = **2045**; "current / revised target" = **2070**. TFR 2.1 was reached only around **NFHS-5 (2019-21)**.
- **10-year action plan** included **SHG involvement**, **free compulsory education (6-14)**, **compulsory registration of marriages and pregnancies**; it did **not** include a special reward for **men** adopting terminal methods.
- **National Commission on Population (2000)**: chaired by the **Prime Minister**; members include Chief Ministers and Union ministers.
- **Need** = the gap between **what is and what ought to be** (the starting point of planning).
- Poverty eradication methods: **population control, higher savings and productive investment** – all.

=== Maternal and Child Health Schemes – JSY, JSSK, PMMVY, NRHM
| Scheme | Fact |
|---|---|
| **NRHM** | Launched **2005** (12 April) |
| **Janani Suraksha Yojana** (April 2005, under NRHM) | Safe-motherhood intervention; **100% centrally sponsored**; cash incentive to **promote institutional delivery** and **reduce maternal and neonatal mortality** among poor pregnant women; **replaced the National Maternity Benefit Scheme** |
| **Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram** (1 June 2011) | **Free, cashless** delivery (incl. caesarean) and care of sick newborns in public facilities |
| **PMMVY** (earlier Indira Gandhi Matritva Sahyog Yojana) | Conditional cash (DBT) to pregnant and lactating women for the **first live birth**, given in instalments **before and after delivery**; compensates **wage loss**; promotes appropriate practices and care during pregnancy, safe delivery and lactation |
| **Mission Parivar Vikas** | TFR 2.1 by **2025** in **7 high-fertility States** (MP, UP, Bihar, Assam, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan – **not Odisha**); **146 districts** |
- JSY statements: **reduce maternal/neonatal mortality** and **promote institutional delivery** are correct; "State Health Department intervention" and "care for sick infants up to one year" are wrong (UPSC 2023: only two).
- JSY gives **monetary assistance for delivery**, not **wage-loss** compensation (IAS 2012: I and II).
- **ASHA** under JSY: identify and register pregnant women, arrange ANC and escort to hospital – she does **not herself give immunisation**.
- **NUHM:** **Mahila Arogya Samitis** promote community participation in urban health.
- ASHA birth-spacing: 2-year gap after marriage, 3-year spacing after the first child.

=== Migration
- Census records migration by **place of birth** and **place of last residence**; **141.2 million** moved during 2001-11.
- **Largest stream: rural → rural**, dominated by **women** moving for **marriage**; men dominate the rural → urban / inter-State stream for work. So "rural → urban is the largest" is **false**.
- PLFS 2020-21: female migration rate **47.9%** (86.8% for marriage); male 10.7% (49.6% for employment).
- Inter-State female migration by last residence (2011): **Urban-Urban > Rural-Urban > Rural-Rural > Urban-Rural**.
| Theory | Proponent |
|---|---|
| Laws of Migration (1885) | **E.G. Ravenstein** |
| **Push-pull with intervening obstacles (1966)** | **Everett Lee** |
| Expected-income rural-urban model | Michael Todaro |
- Notestein and Thompson = demographic transition, not migration.

=== Ageing and Senior Citizens
- Elderly 60+: **9.7%** (SRS 2024); about **100 mn (2011) → 230 mn (2036)**.
- **NITI Aayog** – problems of senior citizens: **shortage of trained care personnel, lack of income security, nuclear families with parents living apart, medical care concentrated in urban areas** (all four).
- Nearly **70% of elderly women** lack a formal pension vs about **40%** of elderly men; ageing could cut GDP growth by about 70 basis points a year (IMF).
| Policy / scheme | Fact |
|---|---|
| National Policy on Older Persons (1999, Ministry of Social Justice) | **14** areas of intervention |
| Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007 | Maintenance a **legal duty** of children and relatives |
| NPHCE | **2010-11** |
| National Council for Senior Citizens | Highest advisory body |
| **Varishtha Pension Bima Yojana** (Budget 2003-04) | **Senior citizens** |
| **Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana** (assistive devices for BPL senior citizens) | First camp / national launch at **Nellore (AP), 1 April 2017** |

=== Seat Freeze, Population Incentives and AP Population Management Policy 2026
| Amendment / Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **42nd (1976)** | Froze Lok Sabha and Assembly seats on 1971 Census **until 2000** |
| **84th (2001)** | Freeze extended until the **first Census after 2026** |
| **87th (2003)** | Boundaries readjusted on **2001 Census**, seat numbers unchanged |
| **Art. 82, 170** | Seats allotted on population |
- State two-child norms: **Assam** Small Family Norms – no direct government jobs for parents of more than two children from **1 Jan 2021**; **UP** draft Population Bill **July 2021**.
- Barriers to family planning: **social orthodoxy and psychological factors** (low rural impact), son preference, early marriage, low female literacy; **CPR** = eligible couples effectively protected by an approved method; female sterilisation **37.9%** (NFHS-5).
# AP draft Population Management Policy (March 2026)
- Shift from **population control → population management**; **₹25,000** for a second or third child; **removes the two-child norm for local body elections**; AP TFR about **1.5**.
- Pillars: **Matrutva** (maternal health), **Shakti** (women's work), **Kshema** (elderly care), **Naipunyam** (skills), **Sanjeevani** (digital health).
