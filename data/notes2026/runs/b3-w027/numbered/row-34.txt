## SUB 0 | Status Approaches – UPS, UPSS, CWS, CDS
[0:0] TABLE head: Approach | Reference period / unit | Counted employed if | Captures
  [0:0.0] UPS (Usual Principal Status) | 365 days | Worked the major time (183 days or more) | Chronic (long-term) unemployment
  [0:0.1] UPSS (Principal + Subsidiary) | 365 days | Also subsidiary work for at least 30 days | Broadest worker count
  [0:0.2] CWS (Current Weekly Status) | 7 days | Worked at least 1 hour on any day | Chronic + seasonal
  [0:0.3] CDS (Current Daily Status) | Each day of the reference week; unit = half day | 4 hours or more = full day; 1-4 hours = half day | Daily / intermittent; highest rate
[0:1] - Persons **unemployed for a major part of the year** = **usual status** unemployment; chronic unemployment is measured with **usual status** data, and the weekly status also captures it (so "both US and CWS").
[0:2] - **CDS gives the highest rate** – it also counts the days of unemployment of persons recorded as employed under CWS.
[0:3] - NSS uses **three reference periods** (year, week, day); **"Usual / Current Monthly Status" is NOT an NSSO approach**.
[0:4] - **Census:** **main worker = worked 183 days or more** in the year; marginal worker = less than 183 days. The 183-day line is not an NSS approach.
[0:5] > UPPSC 2005, 2016, 2019 and APPSC Group-I: chronic = usual status; "monthly" approach is the odd one out.
[0:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 18 p.12; NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 19 p.1, p.11; Rocket Sheets Indian Economy p.49; app notes 3:34 sub 0
## SUB 1 | Labour Force Indicators – LFPR, WPR, UR
[1:0] TABLE head: Indicator | Definition
  [1:0.0] Labour force | Employed + unemployed (seeking or available for work); workforce = employed only
  [1:0.1] LFPR | Labour force (employed + those willing and available) ÷ working-age population (per 100 / 1000)
  [1:0.2] WPR (Worker Population Ratio) | Employed persons as a percentage of the total population
  [1:0.3] UR (Unemployment Rate) | Unemployed ÷ labour force × 100
  [1:0.4] PU (Proportion Unemployed) | Unemployed ÷ population
  [1:0.5] Identity | WPR = LFPR × (1 − UR)
[1:1] - **Students, homemakers** and others not seeking work are **outside** the labour force.
[1:2] - Casual worker = non-regular employment; fixed-term worker = employed for a predetermined period.
[1:3] - NCERT: about **35 of every 100** persons are workers (Census WPR); PLFS 2022-23 WPR (15+, usual status) **56.0%**.
[1:4] > "Percentage of employed persons in the population" = **WPR** (APPSC 2023 Degree Lecturers, RAS 2024).
[1:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 19 p.10-11; Rocket Sheets Indian Economy p.72; AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.287-289; OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 04 p.84; app notes 3:34 sub 1

## SUB 3 | Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)
[3:0] - Conducted by the **National Statistical Office (NSO), MoSPI** – NSO's **standard method** for measuring unemployment since 2017.
[3:1] - Designed by the **Standing Committee on Labour Force Statistics (SCLFS)**, set up on the advice of the **National Statistical Commission**.
[3:2] - **Launched April 2017**; **first annual report (2017-18) released May 2019**; replaced the quinquennial NSSO rounds (last = **68th, 2011-12**).
[3:3] TABLE head: Feature | Original | From January 2025
  [3:3.0] Monthly | – | Monthly all-India CWS estimates (LFPR, WPR, UR)
  [3:3.1] Quarterly | Urban only (CWS) | Extended to rural
  [3:3.2] Annual | Rural + urban, UPSS and CWS | Calendar year (Jan-Dec)
  [3:3.3] Sample | – | Raised (not reduced) to about 2,72,304 households
[3:4] - **Criticism:** CWS (1 hour a week = employed) **masks underemployment** and misses **circular migration**; CMIE and PLFS diverge (monthly, larger sample, different method).
[3:5] ~~ Sources: AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.287; OnlyIAS NCERT Economy 05 p.5; app notes 3:34 sub 2, 7
