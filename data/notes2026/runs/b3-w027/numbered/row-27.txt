## SUB 1 | Vital Rates – Definitions and Key Terms
[1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning / base
  [1:0.0] Fertility | Ability of women to give live birth (actual reproductive performance)
  [1:0.1] CBR / CDR | Births / deaths per 1,000 mid-year population
  [1:0.2] IMR | Deaths of infants before age 1 per 1,000 live births
  [1:0.3] Neonatal mortality | Death within the first four weeks (28 days) of life
  [1:0.4] MMR (maternal mortality ratio) | Maternal deaths per 1,00,000 live births
  [1:0.5] TFR | Average number of live births a woman would have over her reproductive span at current age-specific fertility rates
  [1:0.6] Replacement-level TFR | 2.1 children per woman (zero growth in the long run)
  [1:0.7] Growth rate (natural increase) | Birth rate − death rate in a given period (CBR 30 − CDR 10 = 20 per 1,000 = 2.0%)
  [1:0.8] Dependency ratio | (0-14 + 65+) ÷ (15-64)
  [1:0.9] Youth (National Youth Policy 2014) | 15-29 years (approved 9 Jan 2014, replaced NYP 2003)
[1:1] - Population change = **births, deaths, emigration and immigration** – all four.
[1:2] - **Zero population growth:** births (+ immigrants) = deaths (+ emigrants), so the population does not change.
[1:3] - Birth-rate data arranged **year by year** = **chronological** classification.
[1:4] - **SRS TFR:** 2016 – **2.3** (rural 2.5, urban 1.8); 2018 – 2.2; **NFHS-4 (2015-16) 2.2**; **NFHS-5 (2019-21) 2.0**; NFHS-5 highest Bihar 3.0; AP 1.7.
[1:5] - SRS 2024: TFR below replacement for the **fifth year running**.
[1:6] > IMR is per 1,000 live births; MMR per 1,00,000 – the classic base trap.

## SUB 2 | Demographic Transition and Population Theories
[2:0] # The model
[2:1] TABLE head: Stage | Birth rate | Death rate | Result
  [2:1.0] I | High | High | Slow growth (pre-industrial)
  [2:1.1] II | High (rises or stays high) | Falling | Population explosion
  [2:1.2] III | Falling significantly | Low | Growth slows
  [2:1.3] IV | Low | Low | Stable or slow growth
[2:2] - Order with development: **high birth & high death → high birth & low death → low birth & low death** (UPSC 2012: 2, 3, 1).
[2:3] - Third stage = **significant fall in the birth rate** (death rate never becomes zero).
[2:4] - Theory: early form **Warren Thompson (1929)**; formal theory **Frank Notestein** (1940s-50s).
[2:5] - **India:** late Stage 3, moving into Stage 4; Stage-4 States (2011, low fertility): **Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh**.
[2:6] - **Urbanisation reduced both birth and death rates** (education, work, medical care).
[2:7] - Low birth rates / ageing / declining population in news: **Italy, Japan, South Korea** (not Nigeria, South Africa).
[2:8] # Population theories
[2:9] TABLE head: Theory | Propounder
  [2:9.0] Principle of Population (1798): population grows geometrically, food arithmetically; positive and preventive checks | T.R. Malthus – a theory of population
  [2:9.1] Optimum population | Edwin Cannan
  [2:9.2] The Population Bomb (1968) | Paul Ehrlich
[2:10] - Population growth: **larger working population is not an adverse effect**; smaller holdings, unemployment and less food per head are.

## SUB 3 | Demographic Dividend and Its Window
[3:0] - **Demographic dividend / bonus / window:** growth potential from a rising share of the **working-age (15-64)** population – all three names are used (APPSC G2 2019: "All of these").
[3:1] - India is called a dividend country because of its **high population aged 15-64** (UPSC 2011); the "demographic bonus" around 2016 = **surge in the productive age group**.
[3:2] - Working age about **62.9%** (PMF: over 62% aged 15-59; 54% below 25).
[3:3] - **Census 2011 age shares:** aged 20+ **59.29%**; under 14 about **29% (28.67%)**; 65+ about **5% (4.8%)** – the "5-6%" option.
[3:4] - **Window:** **2005-06 to 2055-56** (NITI Aayog: until 2055); peak around **2041**, when 20-59 is about **59%** (Economic Survey 2018-19).
[3:5] - 0-19 share **41% (2011) → 25% (2041)**; 60+ **8.6% → 16% (2041)** and **20.5% (2051)**.
[3:6] - To obtain full benefits: **promote skill development** (UPSC 2013); without health, education, skills and jobs it becomes a **"demographic nightmare"**.
[3:7] - Broad-based age pyramid (underdeveloped country): **many young people and few old people**.
[3:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Human & Economic Geography 01 p.43-44 (definition, until 2055, Economic Survey 2018-19, nightmare); NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.49 (skill development); NextGen Society Group 2 Prelims 02 p.14-15 (2041 peak, shares); app notes 3:27 sub 5
## SUB 4 | Dependency Ratio and Harnessing the Dividend
[4:0] - **Dependent population** in the pyramid: **0-14** (and 65+); working age 15-64.
[4:1] - India's dependency ratio is falling because the **15-59 (working-age) share is relatively high**, itself due to the **fall in fertility**: **79% (1975) → 58% (2008) → about 48% (2021)**.
[4:2] - **Old-age dependency ratio (2011): 14.2%** (60+ ÷ 15-59; 10.9% in 1961).
[4:3] - Lower dependency ratio → **potential demographic dividend**.
[4:4] - **Female LFPR (PLFS):** 23.3% (2017-18) → **41.7% (2023-24)**; cutting it would weaken the dividend.
[4:5] - RBI typology: **ageing – Kerala, Tamil Nadu**; wide window – Bihar, UP; narrowing – Telangana, Uttarakhand; Kerala 60+ **22.8% by 2036**.
[4:6] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:27 sub 6-8; PMF Human & Economic Geography 01 p.43 (working age); PYQ explanation UPPCS 2016 (old-age dependency 14.2, MoSPI)
## SUB 5 | Fertility Determinants, Kerala and Andhra Pradesh
[5:0] - Birth rate falls with **female education and later marriage**, **child survival**, **urbanisation** and **contraception**; **urbanisation lowers the birth rate and high literacy goes with a low birth rate** (UPPCS 2021: both).
[5:1] - **Kerala's** lowest population growth: heavy **investment in promoting literacy**, especially female literacy (94% in 2011).
[5:2] - **Couple Protection Rate (CPR):** share of eligible couples protected by an approved contraceptive method; a rising CPR cuts the birth rate only when the protected couples are **young** (older couples have finished child-bearing).
[5:3] - More boys than girls are born (about **105 : 100**).
[5:4] - **Andhra Pradesh:** Stage 4; decadal growth peak **21.13% (1981-91) → 9.21% (2001-11)**; median age **32.5** (India 28); by 2036 under-14 share **25% → 15%**, 60+ **10% → 19%**; NFHS-5 TFR **1.7**.
[5:5] - AP: Gram Sabhas (May 2026) for feedback on incentives to reverse falling fertility.
[5:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.57 (CPR one-liner); app notes 3:27 sub 8; PYQ explanations IAS 1993 / 1999
