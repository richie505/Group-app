## SUB 0 | Family Planning Programme and National Population Policy 2000
[0:0] # Milestones
[0:1] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [0:1.0] 1952 | National Family Planning Programme (First Plan) – India the first country in the world to launch one
  [0:1.1] 1976 (16 April) | First National Population Policy; marriage age raised to 21 (men) / 18 (women); 42nd Amendment put population control in the Concurrent List
  [0:1.2] 1977 | Renamed Family Welfare Programme after Emergency sterilisations
  [0:1.3] 1996 | Target-free approach (not 1986)
  [0:1.4] 1997 | Reproductive and Child Health (RCH)
  [0:1.5] 2000 | NPP 2000 declared 15 February 2000
[0:2] - India is also the **7th largest country by area** – with the 1952 programme, both statements true.
[0:3] # NPP 2000
[0:4] - **Target-free, voluntary, informed choice**; two-child norm; delivery through **government, industry and NGOs**.
[0:5] TABLE head: Objective | Target
  [0:5.0] Immediate | Meet the unmet need for contraception, health infrastructure and personnel, integrated RCH services
  [0:5.1] Medium-term | TFR 2.1 by 2010
  [0:5.2] Long-term | Stable population by 2045 – later revised to 2070
[0:6] - "Original NPP target" = **2045**; "current / revised target" = **2070**. TFR 2.1 was reached only around **NFHS-5 (2019-21)**.
[0:7] - **10-year action plan** included **SHG involvement**, **free compulsory education (6-14)**, **compulsory registration of marriages and pregnancies**; it did **not** include a special reward for **men** adopting terminal methods.
[0:8] - **National Commission on Population (2000)**: chaired by the **Prime Minister**; members include Chief Ministers and Union ministers.
[0:9] - **Need** = the gap between **what is and what ought to be** (the starting point of planning).
[0:10] - Poverty eradication methods: **population control, higher savings and productive investment** – all.

## SUB 1 | Maternal and Child Health Schemes – JSY, JSSK, PMMVY, NRHM
[1:0] TABLE head: Scheme | Fact
  [1:0.0] NRHM | Launched 2005 (12 April)
  [1:0.1] Janani Suraksha Yojana (April 2005, under NRHM) | Safe-motherhood intervention; 100% centrally sponsored; cash incentive to promote institutional delivery and reduce maternal and neonatal mortality among poor pregnant women; replaced the National Maternity Benefit Scheme
  [1:0.2] Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (1 June 2011) | Free, cashless delivery (incl. caesarean) and care of sick newborns in public facilities
  [1:0.3] PMMVY (earlier Indira Gandhi Matritva Sahyog Yojana) | Conditional cash (DBT) to pregnant and lactating women for the first live birth, given in instalments before and after delivery; compensates wage loss; promotes appropriate practices and care during pregnancy, safe delivery and lactation
  [1:0.4] Mission Parivar Vikas | TFR 2.1 by 2025 in 7 high-fertility States (MP, UP, Bihar, Assam, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan – not Odisha); 146 districts
[1:1] - JSY statements: **reduce maternal/neonatal mortality** and **promote institutional delivery** are correct; "State Health Department intervention" and "care for sick infants up to one year" are wrong (UPSC 2023: only two).
[1:2] - JSY gives **monetary assistance for delivery**, not **wage-loss** compensation (IAS 2012: I and II).
[1:3] - **ASHA** under JSY: identify and register pregnant women, arrange ANC and escort to hospital – she does **not herself give immunisation**.
[1:4] - **NUHM:** **Mahila Arogya Samitis** promote community participation in urban health.
[1:5] - ASHA birth-spacing: 2-year gap after marriage, 3-year spacing after the first child.

## SUB 3 | Migration
[3:0] - Census records migration by **place of birth** and **place of last residence**; **141.2 million** moved during 2001-11.
[3:1] - **Largest stream: rural → rural**, dominated by **women** moving for **marriage**; men dominate the rural → urban / inter-State stream for work. So "rural → urban is the largest" is **false**.
[3:2] - PLFS 2020-21: female migration rate **47.9%** (86.8% for marriage); male 10.7% (49.6% for employment).
[3:3] - Inter-State female migration by last residence (2011): **Urban-Urban > Rural-Urban > Rural-Rural > Urban-Rural**.
[3:4] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent
  [3:4.0] Laws of Migration (1885) | E.G. Ravenstein
  [3:4.1] Push-pull with intervening obstacles (1966) | Everett Lee
  [3:4.2] Expected-income rural-urban model | Michael Todaro
[3:5] - Notestein and Thompson = demographic transition, not migration.

## SUB 4 | Ageing and Senior Citizens
[4:0] - Elderly 60+: **9.7%** (SRS 2024); about **100 mn (2011) → 230 mn (2036)**.
[4:1] - **NITI Aayog** – problems of senior citizens: **shortage of trained care personnel, lack of income security, nuclear families with parents living apart, medical care concentrated in urban areas** (all four).
[4:2] - Nearly **70% of elderly women** lack a formal pension vs about **40%** of elderly men; ageing could cut GDP growth by about 70 basis points a year (IMF).
[4:3] TABLE head: Policy / scheme | Fact

  [4:3.3] National Council for Senior Citizens | Highest advisory body
  [4:3.4] Varishtha Pension Bima Yojana (Budget 2003-04) | Senior citizens
  [4:3.5] Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana (assistive devices for BPL senior citizens) | First camp / national launch at Nellore (AP), 1 April 2017

## SUB 5 | Seat Freeze, Population Incentives and AP Population Management Policy 2026
[5:0] TABLE head: Amendment / Article | Provision
  [5:0.0] 42nd (1976) | Froze Lok Sabha and Assembly seats on 1971 Census until 2000
  [5:0.1] 84th (2001) | Freeze extended until the first Census after 2026
  [5:0.2] 87th (2003) | Boundaries readjusted on 2001 Census, seat numbers unchanged
  [5:0.3] Art. 82, 170 | Seats allotted on population
[5:1] - State two-child norms: **Assam** Small Family Norms – no direct government jobs for parents of more than two children from **1 Jan 2021**; **UP** draft Population Bill **July 2021**.
[5:2] - Barriers to family planning: **social orthodoxy and psychological factors** (low rural impact), son preference, early marriage, low female literacy; **CPR** = eligible couples effectively protected by an approved method; female sterilisation **37.9%** (NFHS-5).
[5:3] # AP draft Population Management Policy (March 2026)
[5:4] - Shift from **population control → population management**; **₹25,000** for a second or third child; **removes the two-child norm for local body elections**; AP TFR about **1.5**.
[5:5] - Pillars: **Matrutva** (maternal health), **Shakti** (women's work), **Kshema** (elderly care), **Naipunyam** (skills), **Sanjeevani** (digital health).
