## SUB 0 | The 1991 Crisis and New Economic Policy
[93:0:0] - Trigger: **NRI deposit flight**; forex reserves fell to **$975 million (June 1991)** = about **two weeks of imports**.
[93:0:1] - **67 tonnes of gold** pledged with **Bank of England and UBS**.
[93:0:2] - **IMF** approached for about **$7 billion** with **structural-adjustment conditions**.
[93:0:3] - NEP (LPG) announced on **24 July 1991**: PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**, FM **Dr. Manmohan Singh**.

=== NOTES 2:94 | Impact of Lpg on the Role of the State – Provider to Regulator and Enabler
## SUB 0 | The 1991 Shift
[94:0:0] - Trigger: 1991 crisis + LPG (PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**, FM **Dr. Manmohan Singh**): **state-controlled → market-oriented** economy.
[94:0:1] - **Government to Governance**: State became **facilitator, regulator and catalyst**; it did **not fully withdraw**.
[94:0:2] # Models of the State
[94:0:3] TABLE head: Model | Role | Indian marker
  [94:0:3.0] Welfare / provider | Owns and runs production; "commanding heights" | IPR 1956
  [94:0:3.1] Regulatory | Sets rules; independent regulators watch markets | SEBI, TRAI, CERC, IRDA
  [94:0:3.2] Facilitator / enabler | Delivery through private or civil-society actors | PPPs, DBT, GeM, UMANG
[94:0:4] - **RBI** after 1991: regulator → **facilitator**; freer bank decisions, entry of private and foreign banks; still regulates.
[94:0:5] - Housing: State moved from provider → enabler of private real estate (22 Apr 2026).
[94:0:6] - **AP – Deepam-2**: 3 free LPG cylinders a year to BPL families via **DBT** = provider → enabler (Jan 2026).
## SUB 1 | Rolling Back the State
[94:1:0] - Licensing abolished except alcohol, cigarettes, hazardous chemicals, industrial explosives, electronic aerospace and defence (drugs since delicensed).
[94:1:1] - Public-sector reserved list: **17 (Schedule A, 1956) → 8 (1991) → 3 → now 2**: **atomic energy and railway operations**.
[94:1:2] - **Defence production opened to private sector in 2001**; last SSI items de-reserved **2015**.
[94:1:3] - PSE autonomy: **MoUs** and **Navratna / Maharatna** status.
[94:1:4] - Disinvestment began **1991-92** (target **Rs 2,500 crore**): first to bridge the fiscal deficit, later for efficiency.
[94:1:5] - **LIC**: SEBI deadline **16 May 2027** for 10% minimum public shareholding (4 Aug 2026).
[94:1:6] - Ending **MRTP asset limits** removed curbs on concentration, entry, expansion, mergers and acquisitions.
## SUB 2 | Independent Regulators
[94:2:0] TABLE head: Regulator | Year / Act | Sector
  [94:2:0.0] SEBI | 1992, SEBI Act; replaced Controller of Capital Issues | Securities
  [94:2:0.1] TRAI | 1997 | Telecom
  [94:2:0.2] CERC | 1998; now Electricity Act 2003 | Power tariffs, inter-state transmission; carbon trading
  [94:2:0.3] IRDA | 1999 | Insurance
  [94:2:0.4] PNGRB | 2006 | Petroleum, gas pipelines
[94:2:1] - **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme**: run by **BEE**; trading regulated by **CERC** (not SEBI).
[94:2:2] - **Economic Survey 2025-26**, Ch. 3 "**Refining the Regulatory Touch**" (31 Jan 2026): dedicated regulatory careers for **younger professionals**, not retired bureaucrats; RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA moving reactive → **anticipatory**.
## SUB 3 | Minimum Government, Maximum Governance
[94:3:0] - Slogan from **2014**: a leaner State that governs better through **rules, technology, accountability**.
[94:3:1] - E-governance cuts discretion and middlemen: **Aadhaar, DBT, GeM**, UMANG (not Navratna status); RTI Act 2005.
## SUB 4 | Market Failure vs Government Failure
[94:4:0] TABLE head: Case | Basis
  [94:4:0.0] Market failure → planning-era State | Public goods, externalities, monopoly, information asymmetry
  [94:4:0.1] Government failure → 1991 | PSU low efficiency, losses, political interference, no autonomy, labour problems, project delays
[94:4:1] - **Public choice theory (James Buchanan)** = basis of the government-failure case.
[94:4:2] - Critique: PSU assets **undervalued**; proceeds plugged revenue gaps; agri public investment fell; social spending curbed; overlap (TRAI vs MIB on TV ratings).
## SUB 5 | Washington Consensus
[94:5:0] - **John Williamson, 1989**; IMF + World Bank + US Treasury (**not WTO**).
[94:5:1] - Prescriptions include fiscal discipline, reordering public spending, tax reform, **market-determined interest rates**, competitive exchange rate, trade and FDI liberalisation, privatisation, deregulation, property rights.
[94:5:2] - India 1991: interest deregulation (**Narasimham**), tax cuts (**Chelliah**), market-determined exchange rate (**1993**).
[94:5:3] - **Financial liberalisation:** in the Washington Consensus, **market-determined interest rates** are the financial-liberalisation prescription; a **competitive exchange rate** is a separate exchange-rate prescription.

=== NOTES 2:95 | Impact of Lpg on Administration – Downsizing, Contractualisation, Npm
## SUB 0 | Downsizing the State
[95:0:0] - **Downsizing, disinvestment, privatisation** shrink the State; **FDI promotion** is a growth/external-sector measure, **not** a cut in State size.
[95:0:1] - Keyed set: downsizing, disinvestment, privatising higher education (UPSC 2010).
[95:0:2] - **Disinvestment**: sale of part of PSU equity to the public; need not transfer ownership or management. **Privatisation**: withdrawal or outright sale.
[95:0:3] - **Economic Survey 2025-26**: redefine "Government Company" so the State may hold **less than 51%**.
[95:0:4] # Pay Commissions and Staff Strength
[95:0:5] TABLE head: Commission | Chair / year | Key change
  [95:0:5.0] Fifth | Justice S. Ratnavel Pandian, 1997 | Staff cut about 30%; freeze on new posts
  [95:0:5.1] Sixth | Justice B.N. Srikrishna, 2008 | Group D merged into Group C; running pay bands; PRIS
  [95:0:5.2] Seventh | Justice A.K. Mathur, 2015 (effective 2016) | Pay matrix replaced pay bands; performance-linked pay
## SUB 1 | New Public Management
[95:1:0] - Private-sector techniques in government (Phase 4 of Indian public administration after 1991); term popularised by **Christopher Hood (1991)**.
[95:1:1] - **Hood's doctrines**: hands-on professional management, explicit standards and performance measures, **output controls**, competition.
[95:1:2] - Features: performance auditing, **contracting out non-core services**, citizen as customer; it does **not** reject performance measures.
[95:1:3] - Pioneers: **UK (Thatcher), New Zealand, Australia, USA**.
## SUB 2 | Reinventing Government (osborne and Gaebler, 1992)
[95:2:0] - "**Steering, not rowing**." Ten principles:
[95:2:1] TABLE head: Principle | Meaning
  [95:2:1.0] Catalytic | Steer rather than row
  [95:2:1.1] Community-owned | Empower rather than serve
  [95:2:1.2] Competitive | Inject competition
  [95:2:1.3] Mission-driven | Rules → mission
  [95:2:1.4] Results-oriented | Fund outcomes
  [95:2:1.5] Customer-driven | Meet customer needs
  [95:2:1.6] Enterprising | Earn rather than spend
  [95:2:1.7] Anticipatory | Prevent rather than cure
  [95:2:1.8] Decentralised | Hierarchy → participation and teamwork
  [95:2:1.9] Market-oriented | Change through the market
## SUB 3 | Critiques of Npm
[95:3:0] TABLE head: Thinker | Work | Idea
  [95:3:0.0] Denhardt & Denhardt | New Public Service (2000) | "Serve, not steer"; citizenship, public interest
  [95:3:0.1] Mark Moore | Creating Public Value (1995) | Value includes legitimacy and trust, not only efficiency
[95:3:1] - Both reject citizen → customer and State → service seller.
## SUB 4 | Contractualisation and Outsourcing
[95:4:0] - **Outsourcing**: hiring outside sources (legal, IT, advertising, security) for work once done in-house.
[95:4:1] - Contract labour: **20% (1999) → 40.7%** of manufacturing workforce (2022-23, ASI); such firms **31% lower productivity** (Aug 2025).
[95:4:2] - Weaker direct employer relation → weaker unions; **Industrial Relations Code 2020**: 10% or 100 workers to form a union.
[95:4:3] # AP Angle
[95:4:4] - **APCOS** (2020): recruits outsourced staff and **pays directly**, cutting contractor middlemen.
[95:4:5] - APSRTC: about 8,000 outsourced staff paid via contractors; wage, PF, ESI irregularities (28 Jul 2026).
[95:4:6] - **Civil Services Board**: curbs political interference in postings.
[95:4:7] - Upper age limit for non-uniform direct recruitment raised **42 → 44** till 30 Sep 2027 (G.O. Ms. No. 187).
## SUB 5 | Lateral Entry
[95:5:0] - Normal route: JS/Director posts by tenure deputation (**Central Staffing Scheme, DoPT**).
[95:5:1] - **Second ARC**: lateral entry at **Joint Secretary** level; performance-based appraisal and promotion; performance incentives.
[95:5:2] - **NITI Aayog Action Agenda**: wider entry at middle/senior levels; longer, stable tenures.
[95:5:3] - First lateral JS joined **2019**; **Aug 2024**: UPSC told to cancel advertisement for **45** posts.
[95:5:4] - Debate: domain expertise vs no reservation in single posts, conflict of interest, demotivated career officers.
## SUB 6 | Performance Management Tools
[95:6:0] - **RFD (2009)**, PMES, Cabinet Secretariat: objectives, success indicators, targets = **Key Result Areas**.
[95:6:1] - **Referral Board** reviews adverse ACR remarks; **360-degree** multi-source feedback.
[95:6:2] - **Good Governance Index**: uniform framework for States/UTs. **NeSDA** (2019; 2025 portal June 2026). **GRAI**: **11 indicators, 4 dimensions** on CPGRAMS.
[95:6:3] - India **97th** in UN EGDI 2024.
## SUB 7 | Citizen As Customer
[95:7:0] - Instruments: Citizens' Charters, service standards, grievance redressal.
[95:7:1] - Criticism: citizen has **rights, not just purchasing power**; equity and accountability suffer.
