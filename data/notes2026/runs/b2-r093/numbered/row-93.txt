[0:3] # Capital account convertibility
[0:4] - **Meaning:** freedom to convert rupees into any major currency (and back) for **trading financial assets** – investment, loans, portfolio flows (UPSC 1998). Conversion for trade in goods and services = **current account** convertibility.
[0:5] - **Current account:** fully convertible since **August 1994** (IMF Article VIII obligation).
[0:6] - **Capital account:** never fully convertible; India allows **partial** CAC with limits (e.g. individuals up to **$2,50,000 a year** under LRS).
[0:7] - **Tarapore I (1997) preconditions:** fiscal consolidation – Centre's gross fiscal deficit cut from **4.5% to 3.5% of GDP** over three years (by 1999-2000); a mandated inflation target; a strong financial system; a phased move over three years from 1997-98.
[0:8] - **Capital account convertibility was not part of the 1991 LPG package.**
[0:9] > Recall angle: "Tarapore" → capital account convertibility (asked as "framework for full CAC", "fuller CAC" and "3.5% fiscal deficit precondition").

## SUB 1 | The 1991 Crisis and the New Economic Policy
[1:0] - **Trigger:** **flight of NRI deposits**; forex reserves fell to about **$975 million (June 1991)** = about **two weeks of imports**.
[1:1] - **Gold:** **67 tonnes** pledged with the **Bank of England and UBS**.
[1:2] - **IMF:** approached for about **$7 billion**, with **structural-adjustment conditions**.
[1:3] - **Other 1991 numbers:** fiscal deficit about **9.4%** of GDP, inflation about **12%**, GDP growth only about **1.3%**.
[1:4] - **NEP (LPG) announced:** the New Industrial Policy on **24 July 1991** – so economic reforms were formally introduced in **July 1991**.
[1:5] - **Leaders:** PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**, FM **Dr. Manmohan Singh** (UPPSC 2013); the earlier Janata (1977), Indira (1980) and Rajiv (1985) governments did not launch it.
[1:6] - **Components:** **Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation**, with **disinvestment** as the main form of privatisation; **nationalisation** is the opposite of the NEP (APPSC 2018).
[1:7] # Stabilisation vs structural adjustment
[1:8] TABLE head: Aspect | Stabilisation | Structural adjustment
  [1:8.0] Time frame | Short-term, demand side | Medium/long-term, supply side
  [1:8.1] Aim | BoP, inflation, fiscal deficit | Efficiency, competitiveness
  [1:8.2] Measures | Devaluation, deficit cuts | Delicensing, trade, financial, tax, PSU reforms
[1:9] > Trap: devaluation = stabilisation; delicensing = structural adjustment.

## SUB 2 | 1991 Measures – Delicensing, De-reservation, FDI and the Rupee
[2:0] TABLE head: Measure | Part of 1991 LPG?
  [2:0.0] Industrial de-licensing | Yes – licensing first kept for 18 industries, now only 5 (alcohol, cigarettes, hazardous chemicals, industrial explosives, electronic aerospace and defence; drugs since delicensed)
  [2:0.1] Public-sector reservation | Cut 17 → 8 → 3 (atomic energy, railways, specified minerals); now only atomic energy and railway operations
  [2:0.2] FDI automatic route | Yes – up to 51% in 34 priority industries; later up to 100% in hotels, infrastructure, software
  [2:0.3] PSU autonomy | MoUs; Navratnas (1997)
  [2:0.4] Current account convertibility | Yes, 1994
  [2:0.5] Capital account convertibility | No; never fully adopted (Tarapore)
  [2:0.6] FERA → FEMA | 1999, not 1991
  [2:0.7] Foreign Investment Promotion Board | Set up to promote FDI
[2:1] - **Defence opened to the private sector:** **May 2001** (Press Note 4 of 2001, 21 May 2001) – Indian private firms allowed to make **arms and ammunition** (100% Indian private, FDI up to 26%), under licence (APPSC G2 Mains).
[2:2] - **Rupee:** devalued about **18-20% on 1 and 3 July 1991**; LERMS dual rate 1992, unified **market-determined** rate from **March 1993** – today the rupee's rate is **market determined** (managed float), not pegged to the dollar, the euro or a basket.
[2:3] - **Financial sector:** RBI's role moved from **regulator to facilitator**; private Indian and foreign banks allowed.

## SUB 3 | LPG Terms
[3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:0.0] Liberalisation | Removing controls: licences, quotas, entry curbs; freedom to fix prices, expand, import
  [3:0.1] Privatisation | Government leaves ownership/management – withdrawal or outright sale of PSUs; narrow sense = private ownership in a PSU, broad sense = bigger role for the private sector
  [3:0.2] Disinvestment | Sale of part of PSU equity to the public; above 51% private stake transfers control
  [3:0.3] Globalisation | Joining the world economy; the outcome (not precondition) of L and P
[3:1] - **Navratnas:** nine PSUs given greater autonomy in **1997** (NextGen NCERT summary says 1996 – the scheme was launched July 1997); **Miniratnas: 97**; Maharatna from 2010.
[3:2] - **New PSE Policy for Atmanirbhar Bharat (2021):** favours **strategic disinvestment**.
[3:3] - **Private sector** = engine of growth after 1991 (before: size of the public sector measured "socialisation").
[3:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC Part 3 p.41 (privatisation, disinvestment, globalisation as outcome, Navratna, 97 Miniratnas); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC Part 8.2 p.10; Rocket Indian Economy p.94 (Navratna 1997, Maharatna 2010)

## SUB 5 | Trade Reforms, Sequencing and the Washington Consensus
[5:0] - Import licensing abolished except hazardous/environmentally sensitive items; peak customs duty cut from about **130% to 30%**.
[5:1] TABLE head: Generation | Content
  [5:1.0] First (1991-2000) | Trade and industrial delicensing; tariffs ~125% → ~30%; FDI; financial reform
  [5:1.1] Second (2000s) | Factor markets (labour, land, power), fiscal rules, disinvestment, governance
[5:2] - **Washington Consensus:** **John Williamson, 1989**; 10 prescriptions (fiscal discipline, tax reform, trade and FDI liberalisation, privatisation, deregulation, property rights).
[5:3] - **Foreign Trade Policy 2023:** announced 31 March 2023, in force from **1 April 2023**; unlike the earlier five-year EXIM/trade policies it has **no end date** and is updated when needed; target **$2 trillion** exports by 2030.
[5:4] > Trap: factor-market reforms are **second-generation**; globalisation is the outcome, not the first step.
