## SUB 0 | Evolution of Panchayati Raj in Ap

  [84:0:1.0] Basis | Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957); accepted by NDC January 1958

  [84:0:4.0] AP Panchayat Samithis and Zilla Parishads Act (first AP law) | 1959
  [84:0:4.1] AP Gram Panchayats Act (village level) | 1964
  [84:0:4.2] Mandal system (NTR Government): Panchayat Samithis → Mandal Praja Parishads; ZPs → Zilla Praja Parishads | 1986
  [84:0:4.3] AP Panchayat Raj Act (Act 13 of 1994) – gives effect to the 73rd Amendment | 21 April 1994
  [84:0:4.4] AP PESA amending Act | 1998
[84:0:5] - 1994 Act: **278 sections, 8 Parts** – II Gram Panchayat; III Mandal Parishads; IV Zilla Parishads; V elections; VI Finance Commission.
[84:0:6] - **Kasu Brahmananda Reddy** – Minister for Local Bodies in N. Sanjiva Reddy's cabinet. First polls triggered village and caste clashes.
[84:0:7] - The 1986 Mandal system drew on **Ashok Mehta**'s "Mandal Panchayat" of 15,000–20,000 people.
[84:0:8] # Committees
[84:0:9] TABLE head: Committee | Key recommendation
  [84:0:9.0] Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) | 3 tiers, indirect election; Panchayat Samiti executive; ZP advisory with Collector as Chairman
  [84:0:9.1] Ashok Mehta (1977-78) | 2 tiers (ZP + Mandal Panchayat); party participation; compulsory taxation; 132 recommendations
  [84:0:9.2] G.V.K. Rao (1985) | PRIs "grass without roots"
  [84:0:9.3] L.M. Singhvi (1986) | Constitutional recognition; Gram Sabha as direct democracy
  [84:0:9.4] Thungon (1988) | ZP as pivot; supersession max six months (not one year); SFC in each State
[84:0:10] - **Thungon Committee (1988):** also recommended **constitutional recognition** of Panchayati Raj bodies.
## SUB 1 | Three Tiers Under the Ap Panchayat Raj Act, 1994
[84:1:0] TABLE head: Tier | Members | Head and mode | Term / age
  [84:1:0.0] Gram Panchayat | Ward members (Panch), directly elected | Sarpanch, directly elected | 5 years; 21
  [84:1:0.1] Mandal Parishad | MPTC members, directly elected | MPP President, indirect from MPTCs | 5 years; 21
  [84:1:0.2] Zilla Parishad | ZPTC members, directly elected | ZP Chairperson, indirect from ZPTCs | 5 years; 21
[84:1:1] - AP's intermediate tier = **Mandal Parishad**, not Panchayat Samiti. (Elsewhere, e.g. Jharkhand PR Act 2001 s. 32, Panchayat Samiti members are directly elected.)
[84:1:2] - **Article 243C:** village chairperson's mode left to State; intermediate and district chairpersons indirect. Other qualifications as for State Legislature.
[84:1:3] - Village(s) split into **wards**, each directly electing a Panch.
[84:1:4] - **13 Zilla Praja Parishads → 28** (not 26) after district reorganisation.

## SUB 3 | Ap State Election Commission
[84:3:0] - **Art. 243K** (panchayats) + **243ZA** (municipalities); superintendence of electoral rolls and all local polls – **not** State legislature or **co-operative** polls (APPSC 2025).
[84:3:1] - **Commissioner:** appointed by the **Governor** for **five years**; **status of a High Court Judge**; removed only like a **HC judge**. Both statements correct (APPSC).
[84:3:2] - Separate constitutional SEC ended States' power to postpone local polls indefinitely.
[84:3:3] - Original AP SEC formed **17 June 1994**; after bifurcation (2 June 2014) the Governor constituted the SEC for residuary AP on **30 January 2016** (APPSC-GS 2022 key).
[84:3:4] - **Anil Chandra Punetha** – present SEC (appointed Apr 2026). **8 Sep 2026:** photo rolls for MPTC/ZPTC polls; ward rolls for **41 ULBs** (12 corporations + 29) for 22 Sep 2026.
[84:3:5] # Reservation and polls
[84:3:6] - SC/ST by population, **by rotation**; women **at least one-third**.
[84:3:7] - **AP Cabinet, 18 Aug 2026:** **34% BC** in rural bodies; Mandal Parishad Presidents stay indirect.
[84:3:8] - MPP terms end **23 Sep 2026**, ZPP **24 Sep 2026**; second ordinary MPTC/ZPTC polls under preparation.
[84:3:9] - **Unanimous-election incentives (Jan 2021):** ₹5 lakh (population < 2,000), ₹10 lakh (2,001–5,000), ₹15 lakh (5,001–10,000), ₹20 lakh (> 10,000). 2021 panchayat polls held in four phases, Jan–Feb 2021.
## SUB 4 | Finances and Devolution
[84:4:0] - **SFC** (243I/243Y) – formed by the **Governor** (not SEC) every five years; Part VI of the 1994 Act.
[84:4:1] - **AP SFCs:** 4th SFC report Oct 2019; **5th SFC** constituted 20 March 2023, report for 2025–30.
[84:4:2] - **14th and 15th FC** grants favoured **drinking water and sanitation**.
[84:4:3] - **16th FC (2026-31):** AP rural local bodies **₹16,627 crore** (AP Budget 2026-27); only **duly constituted** bodies get grants – ₹12,150 crore at risk if polls slip past October 2026.
[84:4:4] - **Main own revenue of a GP – property (house) tax** (FC grants, GST share are transfers; stamp duty is a State tax).
[84:4:5] - **Own tax domain:** State Acts give taxes (property, profession tax) mainly to **Gram Panchayats**; intermediate and district tiers rely on **grants**, tolls and fees.
[84:4:6] - **Atmanirbhar Panchayat Program** (MoPR, RGSA): GP own revenue **≥ ₹50 lakh**, Block **≥ ₹1 crore**; **3 years** of term left; **written Gram Sabha consent**.
[84:4:7] - **3Fs** – Funds, Functions, Functionaries – not fully devolved; PRIs remain implementers (May 2026).
## SUB 5 | Urban Local Bodies in Ap
[84:5:0] # Numbers
[84:5:1] - **AP SES 2025-26:** **123 ULBs = 17 Municipal Corporations + 76 Municipalities + 30 Nagar Panchayats**.
[84:5:2] - Polls due in 100 of 123 ULBs; merger of 23 municipalities deferred (Aug 2026).
[84:5:3] # Municipal laws
[84:5:4] TABLE head: Law | Key fact
  [84:5:4.0] AP Municipalities Act, 1965 | In force 2 April 1965 (APPSC-GS 2021); Municipalities and Nagar Panchayats
  [84:5:4.1] AP Municipal Corporations Act, 1994 | Corporations; applies the Hyderabad Municipal Corporations Act, 1955 as parent law
[84:5:5] - Municipal grades (by annual income): **Special, Selection, Grade I, II, III**.
[84:5:6] - **Nagar Panchayat** – transitional area (243Q); classification by population, density, revenue, % non-farm employment.
[84:5:7] - **33.33% BC quota** in ULBs (G.O. Rt. 1065, 19 Aug 2026) – under the **1994 and 1965 Acts** (not the PR Act).
[84:5:8] # Amendment Bills
[84:5:9] - **AP Municipal Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2024** – Minister **P. Narayana** (MA&UD); passed **without debate**; **removed the two-child norm** for municipal polls, reversing a 1990s restriction (APPSC-GS 2024: both statements correct). Companion **AP Panchayat Raj (Amendment) Bill** did the same (Nov 2024).
[84:5:10] - **AP Municipal Laws (Third Amendment) Bill 2026** (17 Aug 2026) – **Uniform Mutation Fee** on title transfers.
[84:5:11] # Mayor election and corporations
[84:5:12] - Mayor so far **indirect**; **AP Cabinet (18 Aug 2026)** approved **direct election** of Mayors and Municipal/Nagar Panchayat Chairpersons.
[84:5:13] - Corporation = Council (Mayor) + **Standing Committees** + Commissioner; **GVMC** has zonal commissioners under the Municipal Commissioner. Major: **Greater Visakhapatnam, Vijayawada, Guntur**, Tirupati, Kurnool.
[84:5:14] - First Municipal Corporation – **Madras (1688)**; Bombay and Calcutta 1726.
[84:5:15] - 74th Amendment – Part IX-A, 243P–243ZG, **18** Twelfth Schedule subjects; 1 June 1993.
[84:5:16] # Urban development authorities
[84:5:17] - **APCRDA** (Amaravati capital region, **APCRDA Act 2014**); **VMRDA** (Visakhapatnam, 2018).
[84:5:18] - UDAs set up by State Act or resolution; **not under ULBs** – fragment urban governance.
[84:5:19] - Three-level uniform building checks and Occupancy Certificates across NPs, municipalities, corporations, UDAs (Aug 2026).
[84:5:20] - **AIIB**-aided AP Urban Water Supply Project – ₹300 crore, 3.3 million people, 50 ULBs.

=== NOTES 2:85 | Public Policy – Meaning, Types, Policy Cycle (formulation, Implementation, Evaluation), Policy Instruments
## SUB 0 | Meaning of Public Policy
[85:0:0] - **Public policy** – planned state action to tackle public issues, reach development goals and regulate behaviour; the **bridge between political intent and administrative action**.
[85:0:1] - Indian bases: Constitution, laws, **judicial interpretation** (e.g. **Right to Food** read into **Article 21**), NITI Aayog agendas, Budget, Economic Survey.
[85:0:2] # Definitions → thinker
[85:0:3] TABLE head: Thinker | Definition / idea
  [85:0:3.0] Thomas Dye | "Whatever governments choose to do or not to do" – inaction is policy too
  [85:0:3.1] David Easton | "Authoritative allocation of values for the whole society"; systems model
  [85:0:3.2] Harold Lasswell | "Projected programme of goals, values and practices"; founder of policy sciences
  [85:0:3.3] James Anderson | "Purposive course of action" by actors dealing with a problem
[85:0:4] Easton gives both a definition and the systems model; Lasswell = policy sciences; Dye = "do or not to do".
## SUB 1 | Types of Policy – Lowi Classification
[85:1:0] TABLE head: Lowi type | Does | Indian example
  [85:1:0.0] Distributive | Benefits to specific groups; costs borne by all | PM-KISAN, farm input subsidies
  [85:1:0.1] Redistributive | Transfers from one group to another | Land reforms, progressive income tax
  [85:1:0.2] Regulatory | Limits individual/firm behaviour | Licensing, food-safety standards
  [85:1:0.3] Constitutive | Creates or reorganises institutions | NITI Aayog, delimitation
## SUB 2 | Policy Cycle
[85:2:0] TABLE head: Stage | What happens
  [85:2:0.0] 1. Agenda setting | Problem identified; media, advocacy, experts push it
  [85:2:0.1] 2. Formulation | Research, stakeholder consultation, cost-benefit analysis, impact assessment
  [85:2:0.2] 3. Adoption | Option formally chosen
  [85:2:0.3] 4. Implementation | Planning, resources, coordination
  [85:2:0.4] 5. Monitoring and evaluation | Data test whether goals are met; feedback
  [85:2:0.5] 6. Termination / revision | Failing policy ended or changed; policy learning goes back to agenda setting
[85:2:1] - **Communication and transparency** run through **every stage** (not only evaluation).
[85:2:2] - Example: **Draft National Policy for Women, 2016** – **Pam Rajput Committee**.
[85:2:3] - AP example: GoM under Excise Minister **Kollu Ravindra** framing a new retail excise policy; 2024-26 policy expires **30 Sep 2026**.
