## SUB 0 | Democracy, Republic and Parliamentary Government
[0:0] # Democracy
[0:1] - **Word origin:** **Greek** – *demos* (people) + *kratos* (rule); not Latin, Roman or French (APPSC G2 Mains 2019; Town Planning).
[0:2] - **India = representative (indirect) democracy:** people elect representatives who govern (APPSC DL 2017; SSC CGL 2016).
[0:3] - **Direct democracy is NOT found in India** at the national level – no **referendum, initiative or recall** (APSLPRB 2019: "Direct democracy is found in India" = the incorrect statement).
[0:4] - **Tools of direct democracy:** **recall** = removing an elected representative before his term; **referendum** = people vote on a law/issue; **initiative** = people propose a law; **plebiscite** = people's verdict on a political question (APSLPRB Constable 2023: recall).
[0:5] - **Democracy needs people's participation:** without participation and co-operation democracy fails → **people should participate and co-operate with the government** (not compulsion, not a switch to the presidential system).
[0:6] - India is a **democratic country** and also has a **Constitution of its own** – both true, but having a constitution does not by itself make a country democratic (UPPSC 2017 A–R: both true, R not the explanation).
[0:7] - **Not authoritarian:** India has democratic government, rule of law and a government that follows the Constitution; **authoritarian government is NOT a feature** (UPPSC Mains 2011).
[0:8] # Republic
[0:9] - **Republic = no hereditary ruler; the head of State is elected** (directly or indirectly) for a fixed term (UPPSC 1997, 2017; Panchayat Secretary).
[0:10] - India's head of State, the **President, is elected indirectly** – the parliamentary system is **combined with republicanism** (a nominal elected head, real power with ministers) (RAS 2013 A–R: A true, R true and the correct explanation).
[0:11] - **"Republic" in the Preamble** – idea from the **French** Constitution (with liberty, equality, fraternity).
[0:12] - India is a **"Union of States"** (Art. 1), a **Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic with a parliamentary system**.
[0:13] - **Cardinal features of the Indian political system:** **democratic republic**, **parliamentary form of government**, **supreme power vests in the people**, and a **unified authority** (one Constitution, single citizenship, integrated judiciary) → **all four** (UPPSC 2009).
[0:14] # Parliamentary form of government
[0:15] - India chose the **British parliamentary system**, not the American **presidential** system → India is a **parliamentary democracy** (UPPSC 2005); **presidential government is NOT a feature** (UPPSC Mains 2015).
[0:16] - **Parliamentary system** = **co-operation and co-ordination** between legislature and executive; **presidential** = **separation of powers**.
[0:17] - **Features:** nominal (President) and real (Council of Ministers) executive; majority party rule; **collective responsibility of the Council of Ministers to the Lok Sabha** (Art. 75); ministers are **members of the legislature**; leadership of PM/CM; dissolution of the lower House. Adopted at both **Centre and States**.
[0:18] - **Why chosen:** already **familiar** (worked under the GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935); suits India's **vast size and diversity**; avoids **executive–legislature conflict** seen in the USA.
[0:19] - **Origins:** parliamentary form began in **Britain**; presidential form in the **USA**.
[0:20] # India vs the British model (UPSC 2021)
[0:21] TABLE head: Point | India | Britain
  [0:21.0] Parliament | Not sovereign – a creature of a written Constitution; its law-making power is limited (FRs, federalism, judicial review) | Sovereign – supreme in legislation
  [0:21.1] Validity of laws / amendments | Courts can strike them down; cases on interpreting the Constitution (incl. validity of an Amendment) go to a Constitution Bench of at least 5 judges (Art. 145(3)) | No judicial review of Acts of Parliament
  [0:21.2] Head of State | Elected (indirectly) – republic | Hereditary monarch
  [0:21.3] Citizenship | Single | Single
[0:22] > UPSC 2021: both statements correct → **Both 1 and 2** (UPSC official key C).

## SUB 1 | Written and Lengthiest Constitution – Parts, Schedules, Articles
[1:0] # Written and lengthiest
[1:1] - **A Constitution** = the document that lays down the **basic rules** for the people and the government (SSC 2018).
[1:2] - **Written constitution tradition began in the USA** – the **US Constitution (1787)** is the **first written** national constitution (and the shortest: 7 Articles); the **British** Constitution is **unwritten** (MPPSC 2015).
[1:3] - India's is the **lengthiest written constitution** of the world – longer than those of Canada, South Africa or Australia (APPSC HWO).
[1:4] - **Reasons for length:** vast size and **diversity**; influence of the bulky **GoI Act 1935**; **single Constitution for Centre and States**; dominance of **legal luminaries**; detailed **administrative provisions**; matters left to ordinary law elsewhere put in the Constitution.
[1:5] - **Original copies:** **two handwritten (calligraphed) originals – English and Hindi** – kept in helium/inert-gas cases in the **Parliament Library** (APPSC Inspector of Factories); English calligraphy by **Prem Behari Narain Raizada**, Hindi by **Vasant Krishan Vaidya**, decorated by Nandalal Bose's Santiniketan artists (members also signed a printed English version on 24 Jan 1950).
[1:6] # Number game
[1:7] TABLE head: Item | Original (26 Nov 1949) | Now (2026)
  [1:7.0] Articles | 395 (with Preamble) | about 470 (numbering still ends at Art. 395)
  [1:7.1] Parts | 22 | 25
  [1:7.2] Schedules | 8 | 12
[1:8] - **Schedules now = 12** (the 12th added by the 74th Amendment, 1992) – also 12 in 2003 (APPSC G2 2003; Junior Steno).
[1:9] - **Parts now = 25:** 22 + 4 added (**IVA, IXA, IXB, XIVA**) − 1 repealed (**VII**) (SSC 2017). Older books say 24 Parts – that count is from before Part IXB (97th Amendment, 2011).
[1:10] - New provisions are added as **lettered Articles** (e.g. **21A, 51A**); numbering still ends at **Article 395**.
[1:11] - **⚠ Trap:** some books print "9 original Schedules / 397 Articles" – wrong. Original = **8 Schedules, 395 Articles, 22 Parts**.

## SUB 2 | Other Salient Features – Amendment, Emergency, Rights, Duties, Franchise
[2:0] # Blend of rigidity and flexibility
[2:1] TABLE head: Method | Examples
  [2:1.0] Simple majority of Parliament (outside Art. 368) | Admission of new States, change of State boundaries
  [2:1.1] Special majority of Parliament (Art. 368) | Most provisions (e.g. Fundamental Rights)
  [2:1.2] Special majority + ratification by half the State legislatures | Federal provisions
[2:2] - **Amending power: Article 368** (Part XX) (APPSC Social History 2019).
[2:3] - **First amendment: 1951** (1st Amendment Act – added the 9th Schedule).
[2:4] - Neither as rigid as the US nor as flexible as the British Constitution.
[2:5] # Rights, duties, franchise
[2:6] - **Fundamental Rights:** six categories (Part III); all suspendable in a National Emergency except **Arts. 20 and 21**; Right to Property moved out → now **Art. 300A**. Rights for individuals make it a **liberal** constitution (NDA 2010).
[2:7] - **DPSP** (Part IV) – Ambedkar's "novel feature".
[2:8] - **Fundamental Duties:** added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** on the **Swaran Singh Committee**'s recommendation – **10** duties; the **86th (2002)** added the 11th → **11** now (Part IVA, Art. 51A).
[2:9] - **Universal adult franchise** from the start (1950) at **21 years**; voting age **changed once – 21 → 18 by the 61st Amendment (1988, effective 1989)**; the same age for Lok Sabha and Assembly elections; no literacy or property test.
[2:10] - **Education:** moved from the State List to the **Concurrent List** by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**.
[2:11] # Emergency, bodies, tiers
[2:12] - **Three kinds of emergency:** **National (Art. 352)**, **State / President's Rule (Art. 356)**, **Financial (Art. 360)** – Part XVIII, Arts. 352-360 (APSLPRB SI 2017).
[2:13] - **Independent bodies:** ECI, CAG, UPSC, SPSCs.
[2:14] - **Integrated and independent judiciary:** one court system enforces **both Central and State laws**.
[2:15] - **Three-tier government:** **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992)** added the local tier.
[2:16] - **Kerala** was formed on **1 November 1956** (States Reorganisation Act, 1956) in place of the **Part B State of Travancore-Cochin** (APPSC MPDO 1998).

## SUB 3 | Constitutionalism and Supremacy of the Constitution
[3:0] # Constitutionalism
[3:1] - **Constitutionalism** = government whose powers are **limited** by a supreme Constitution; it **recognises the need to limit the powers of government** (APPSC G2 2011).
[3:2] - It is **not inherent in every constitution** – a country can have a constitution without constitutionalism (a dictator's constitution).
[3:3] - **Antithesis of constitutionalism = despotism** (absolute, unlimited rule); limited power and restraint of power are its features (APPSC G2 2011).
[3:4] - **Constitutional government** = one that places **effective restrictions on the authority of the State in the interest of individual liberty** – not restrictions on liberty in the interest of the State (UPSC 2014: **II only**).
[3:5] - **Limits in India:** Fundamental Rights (**Art. 13** – laws inconsistent with FRs are void), judicial review, federal division of powers, **basic structure** (**Kesavananda Bharati, 1973**).
[3:6] - Parliament's amending power is limited by the **basic structure doctrine**; basic-structure elements include **supremacy of the Constitution**, rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, limited amending power.
[3:7] # Supremacy of the Constitution
[3:8] - The **Constitution is supreme** in the Indian political system – not Parliament, not the Supreme Court, not religion (UPPSC Mains 2013).
[3:9] - **Governmental organs owe their origin to the Constitution** – the Union and States derive their powers from it (APPSC Asst. Social Welfare).
[3:10] - **Why the Constitution is above Parliament:** the **Constitution specifies how Parliament is formed and what its powers are** (not because it is older or its makers were more eminent) (APPSC HWO).
[3:11] # Judicial review
[3:12] - **Judicial review** = power of the SC/HCs to declare laws and executive orders **unconstitutional**; the phrase is **nowhere used in the Constitution**, but **Arts. 13, 32, 226** (and others) **explicitly confer** the power.
[3:13] - **Basic structure is NOT defined in the Constitution** – it was evolved by the Supreme Court (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973) (UPSC 2020, statement I wrong).
[3:14] - **Synthesis** of British **parliamentary sovereignty** and American **judicial supremacy**: courts can strike down laws; Parliament can amend most of the Constitution.
[3:15] - **Scope narrower than in the USA:** India has **"procedure established by law"** (Art. 21), the USA **"due process of law"**; courts test a law only **against the supreme written Constitution** (FR violation, lack of competence, repugnancy), not its wisdom → judicial review is **restricted because the Constitution is supreme**, not because the executive or legislature is supreme.
[3:16] > UPSC 2020: II (Constitution provides for judicial review) is correct on the text of Arts. 13, 32, 226 → **Only II**. (UPSC's official key said D, "Neither", on the view that the Constitution never uses the term "judicial review".)

## SUB 5 | Federal System with Unitary Bias
[5:0] TABLE head: Federal features | Unitary features
  [5:0.0] Two levels of government (later three) | Strong Centre (Canada model)
  [5:0.1] Division of powers (three Lists, 7th Schedule) | Single citizenship

  [5:0.3] Rigid constitution | Emergency provisions
  [5:0.4] Independent judiciary | States have no separate constitutions
  [5:0.5] Bicameral Parliament | Governor appointed by the Centre; all-India services
[5:1] - The word **"federation" is not used** in the Constitution; India is a "Union of States".
[5:2] - **Prominent federal features** asked: **division of powers**, **independent judiciary**, **supremacy of the Constitution** – "**allocation of financial resources**" is **not** listed as one (APPSC G2 Prelims 2017); the Finance Commission, integrated judiciary and emergency powers are not "the" federal feature (Sachivalayam: **division of powers**).
[5:3] - **Single citizenship**, not dual: **dual citizenship is NOT a feature** (APPSC Draughtsman); Part II deals with citizenship.
[5:4] - **Quasi-federal**: federal in form, unitary in spirit (Wheare); in an emergency the structure turns **unitary without a formal amendment**.
[5:5] - Indian States have **no separate constitutions and no dual citizenship** (the USA has both).

## SUB 6 | Secular State
[6:0] - India is a **secular state**: it **does not uphold any religion as the official religion** – the Indian State has **no religious basis** (APPSC Asst. Director).
[6:1] - **"Secular" was added to the Preamble** by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** → the **Preamble** describes India as a secular State (Deputy Information Engineers).
[6:2] - **Indian model (positive):** equal respect to / equal protection of all religions; **Western model (negative):** complete separation of State and religion.
[6:3] - **Secular provisions:** Preamble; Arts. 14, 15, 16, **25-28** (freedom of religion), 29, 30, 44.
[6:4] - **Religious (theocratic) state is NOT a feature** of the Constitution (APPSC DL 2020).
