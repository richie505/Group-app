## SUB 0 | Article 25 – Freedom of Conscience and Religion
[0:0] # Text and limits
[0:1] - **25(1):** "all persons are equally entitled to **freedom of conscience** and the right freely to **profess, practise and propagate** religion" – subject to **public order, morality and health** and to the **other provisions of Part III**. Quoted text → **Art. 25(1)**.
[0:2] - **Grounds of restriction:** public order, morality, health – **humanism is not** a ground. So the right to religion is **not without limitations**.
[0:3] - Right to religion is a **Fundamental Right** (Arts. 25–28), available to **all persons**, citizens and foreigners. Freedom of conscience = **Art. 25**; "freedom to practise any religion" = **Art. 25**.
[0:4] - **Prisoners** do not lose FRs merely by lawful detention (**Sunil Batra**).
[0:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:5.0] Conscience | Inner freedom to mould one's relation with God; includes freedom to follow no religion
  [0:5.1] Profess | Declare one's beliefs openly
  [0:5.2] Practise | Worship, rituals, ceremonies
  [0:5.3] Propagate | Spread one's beliefs – not a right to convert another (it would impinge on the other's freedom of conscience)
[0:6] # State powers – 25(2)
[0:7] - **25(2)(a):** regulate **economic, financial, political or other secular activity** associated with religion – not spiritual belief.
[0:8] - **25(2)(b):** **social welfare and reform**, and **throw open Hindu religious institutions of a public character to all classes and sections of Hindus** (temple entry). The State may step in to root out a social evil.
[0:9] # Explanations
[0:10] - **Explanation I:** wearing and carrying **kirpans** is part of the profession of the **Sikh** religion – a **Right to Freedom of Religion** (Art. 25).
[0:11] - **Explanation II:** "**Hindus**" includes **Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists** – **not Parsis**.
[0:12] > Asked as: which Article (conscience, kirpan, temple entry 25(2)(b)), quoted text 25(1), "not a ground" (humanism), what freedom of religion is subject to (I, II, III – not defamation, an Art. 19 ground), "without limitations" is wrong.

## SUB 1 | Articles 23-24 – Right Against Exploitation
[1:0] # Article 23
[1:1] - **23(1):** prohibits **traffic in human beings, begar** and other **forced labour**; available to **citizens and non-citizens**, and protects against the **State and private persons**.
[1:2] - **Begar** = involuntary forced labour **without payment**, exacted by officials/zamindars.
[1:3] - **23(2):** State may impose **compulsory service for public purposes** (military/social service); it need not pay in every case, but may **not discriminate on religion, race, caste or class**.
[1:4] # Article 24
[1:5] - **No child below 14** shall be employed in any **factory, mine or other hazardous employment** – an **absolute prohibition**; harmless or innocent work is not barred.
[1:6] - Child labour in hazardous work is prohibited by the **Constitution (Art. 24)** and by the **Supreme Court (M.C. Mehta v. State of Tamil Nadu, 10 Dec 1996)**; the international instrument is the **UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989, Art. 32)** – not the UN Charter.
[1:7] - Art. 24 is the FR **directly related to the exploitation of children**. Related DPSPs: **39(e), 39(f)**.
[1:8] - **Arts. 21A and 24** together (education + no hazardous work) make the right meaningful for children (**APPSC G1 2023**).

## SUB 2 | Secularism – Meaning and the Indian Model
[2:0] - **Secularism** = delinking of the State from religion: **no official (state) religion**, **equal status/respect for all religions**, no religious discrimination, freedom of religion – India is secular for **all** these reasons. It is **not** anti-God, anti-religion or atheist.
[2:1] - **Applies to all**, including **irreligious persons** – "not applicable to irreligious persons" is NOT a component.
[2:2] - **Modern Indian concept:** **principled distance** – the State **intervenes when a religious practice violates fundamental rights** (reform, Art. 25(2)); not complete distance, not protection of minorities only.
[2:3] - **Articles 25–28** emphasise the secular character of Part III; also Arts. 14–16, 29–30, **325** (no exclusion from rolls on religion).
[2:4] - 'Secular' added to the Preamble by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**; the Constituent Assembly **rejected 'God'** in the Preamble (1949). Roots: **Karachi Resolution (1931)**, Nehru Report (1928).
[2:5] - **Sarva Dharma Sambhava** = state neutrality + **positive intervention for reform**.
[2:6] TABLE head: Indian (positive) | Western (negative)
  [2:6.0] Principled distance, equal respect | Strict separation
  [2:6.1] State may reform, temple entry | Religion purely private
  [2:6.2] Aid allowed (Haj, minority institutions) | No state aid
[2:7] - **Theocratic state**: an official religion guides law and policy – **not** India.
[2:8] TABLE head: Case | Holding
  [2:8.0] Kesavananda Bharati (1973) | Basic structure doctrine
  [2:8.1] S.R. Bommai (1994) | Secularism a basic feature; anti-secular State govts can be dismissed under Art. 356
  [2:8.2] Abhiram Singh (2017) | Widened Sec. 123(3) RPA – appeals to religion, caste etc. = corrupt practice
  [2:8.3] Shakti Vahini (2018) | Khap interference, honour killings illegal
  [2:8.4] Amit Sahni (2020) (Shaheen Bagh) | Peaceful protest, but public ways cannot be occupied indefinitely
[2:9] - Indian pluralism also protects language: **Art. 343** – **Hindi** (Devanagari) is the **official language of the Union**; India has **no national language**.

## SUB 7 | AP Endowments Act, 1987 – Authorities, Temple Property and the Tribunal
[7:0] # The Act and its officers
[7:1] - **Act:** **AP Charitable and Hindu Religious Institutions and Endowments Act, 1987 (Act 30 of 1987)** – regulates Hindu temples, mutts and charitable endowments in AP (State power under Art. 25(2)(a): secular activities of religion).
[7:2] - **Hierarchy:** Commissioner → Regional Joint Commissioner (RJC) → Deputy Commissioner (DC) → Assistant Commissioner (AC) → **Executive Officers**; EOs outside the RJC, DC and AC cadres are in **three grades** (Grade I, II, III).
[7:3] - **Executive Officer:** manages the institution and **represents the temple in legal proceedings** before courts.
[7:4] - **Commissioner:** may **group institutions** and appoint a **single Executive Officer** for each group.
[7:5] - **Budgets:** the **Assistant Commissioner** sanctions budgets of temples published under **Sec. 6(c)** (the lowest-income category).
[7:6] # Temple property
[7:7] - **Purchase** of immovable property for a temple: only with the **prior sanction of the Commissioner** (Sec. 79).
[7:8] - **Sale** of temple land by **public auction:** needs the **Commissioner's** permission; objections are first called and disposed of and permission obtained **before** the auction – an E.O. **cannot auction first and seek approval later**.
[7:9] - **Sec. 81 – unauthorised sale void:** a sale of endowment immovable property made **without the permission of the Government / Commissioner**, whether **before or after** the commencement of Act 30/1987, is **void**.
[7:10] - **Sec. 82 – tenants:** a **small or marginal farmer** in possession of temple land at the commencement of the Act who cannot buy it may **continue as tenant** on paying at least **two-thirds of the market rent** (upheld by the SC in *State of AP v. Nallamilli Rami Reddi*, 2001).
[7:11] # Encroachment and eviction
[7:12] - **Group encroachment** on endowment lands: action initiated by the **Deputy Commissioner**.
[7:13] - **Eviction petition** against an encroacher before the **Endowments Tribunal**: filed by the **Assistant Commissioner**, who also **implements the Tribunal's order**.
[7:14] # Income of temples
[7:15] - Sources: **hundi collections, devotees' offerings and kanukas, rents and income from the temple's own lands and properties** – **government grants are not** a regular source (Art. 27 bars taxes spent to promote a religion).
