## SUB 0 | Election – Electoral College, Value of Votes, Disputes (Arts. 54, 55, 71)
[0:0] TABLE head: Votes in the Presidential election | Does NOT vote
  [0:0.0] Elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha) | Nominated members of Parliament and of State Assemblies
  [0:0.1] Elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies | All members of State Legislative Councils (elected or nominated) – a CM who sits in the Council also cannot vote
  [0:0.2] Elected members of the Assemblies of NCT Delhi and Puducherry (added by the 70th Amendment, 1992) | Governors, Lt. Governors; UTs without Assemblies (e.g. Chandigarh, Daman and Diu)
[0:1] - **Art. 54** = the electoral college; **Art. 55** = manner of election; Arts. 54 and 55 together = **election of the President** (not Arts. 49, 56, 62, 74, 78, 103).
[0:2] - **Keyword – "elected" members**: "all the members of the two Houses" is **wrong** (nominated MPs are out); the correct code is **elected MPs + elected MLAs** (States, Delhi, Puducherry).
[0:3] - **Indirect election**: the President is chosen by the elected representatives, not directly by the people, and not by Parliament or the Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha alone.
[0:4] - **Art. 55(3)**: **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote**, by **secret ballot** – method borrowed from **Ireland**.
[0:5] - PR-STV is also used for the **Vice-President**, **Rajya Sabha** members and **Legislative Council** members; **Lok Sabha** and State Assembly members are elected directly (first-past-the-post).
[0:6] TABLE head: Elector | Value of vote | Effect
  [0:6.0] MLA | Population of the State ÷ elected MLAs × 1/1000 | Varies from State to State (uniformity among States, Art. 55(1))
  [0:6.1] MP | Total value of all MLAs' votes ÷ total elected MPs | Same for every MP, Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha (parity between the Union and the States)
[0:7] - **Population base: 1971 Census** – frozen by the 42nd Amendment (till 2000) and then by the **84th Amendment (2001)** till the first Census after **2026**.
[0:8] - **Vote value depends on the population–seats ratio, not on the number of seats**: Puducherry MLA **16** > Arunachal Pradesh MLA **8**; Kerala MLA **152** > Madhya Pradesh MLA **131** (2022 values). So "more Assembly seats → higher MLA vote value" is **wrong**.
[0:9] - **Nomination**: at least **50 electors as proposers and 50 as seconders** (since 1997; **10 + 10** from 1974 to 1997); deposit ₹15,000. Vice-President: **20 + 20**.
[0:10] - **Quota**: more than half of valid vote value; lowest candidate eliminated and votes transferred by preference (V.V. Giri, 1969, won on **second-preference** votes).
[0:11] - **Art. 71**: all doubts and disputes about the election are decided **only by the Supreme Court**, whose decision is **final** (not the Election Commission or the RS Chairman).
[0:12] - **Acts done before the election is declared void remain valid** ("not invalidated").
[0:13] - **Art. 71(4)**: the election **cannot be challenged or postponed** because of **vacancies in the electoral college** – members of a **dissolved Assembly** simply do not vote.
[0:14] - **Election vs impeachment**: **elected MLAs vote in the election but take no part in impeachment** (Parliament alone impeaches); **nominated MPs** do the reverse. **Legislative Councils** do neither.
[0:15] > UPSC 1996 (twice) and State PSCs: "part of the electoral college but not of impeachment" = **State Legislative Assemblies**.
[0:16] - **Electoral colleges compared**: President = elected MPs + elected MLAs; Vice-President = all MPs; Rajya Sabha = elected MLAs; Lok Sabha = the people – so a **Lok Sabha member** shares no electoral college with the others.

## SUB 1 | Qualifications, Term, Re-election, Oath, Resignation, Emoluments (Arts. 56–60)
[1:0] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [1:0.0] 56 | Term: 5 years from the date he enters upon his office; resignation in writing to the Vice-President; continues until the successor enters office
  [1:0.1] 57 | Eligibility for re-election – any number of times, no upper limit
  [1:0.2] 58 | Qualifications: citizen of India; 35 years completed; qualified for election to the Lok Sabha; no office of profit
  [1:0.3] 59 | Conditions of office: not a member of any legislature; no office of profit; rent-free residence; emoluments cannot be diminished
  [1:0.4] 60 | Oath before the CJI (in his absence the senior-most SC judge)
[1:1] - **Art. 58 – what is NOT required**: **education**, citizenship **by birth** (any citizen), a **maximum age** (there is none). Qualified for the **Lok Sabha** (House of the People), not the Rajya Sabha or a State legislature.
[1:2] - **Office of profit**: a person holding an office of profit under the Union, a State or a local authority is **not eligible**; the offices of President, VP, Governor and Union/State Minister are not offices of profit.
[1:3] - **Art. 59**: an MP or MLA may contest, but his **seat is deemed vacated on the date he enters upon his office** as President (no prior resignation needed).
[1:4] - **Resignation**: addressed to the **Vice-President**, who **communicates it to the Speaker of the Lok Sabha** (not to the CJI, PM, RS Chairman or the electoral college).
[1:5] - **Oath (Art. 60)**: to **faithfully execute the office**, **preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law**, and devote himself to the **service and well-being of the people of India** – administered by the **CJI**, not the Vice-President.
[1:6] - **Emoluments**: fixed by **Parliament by law**, originally listed in the **Second Schedule**; charged on the Consolidated Fund; **cannot be diminished during the term**. Salary **₹5 lakh per month** (Finance Act, 2018, w.e.f. 1 January 2016).
[1:7] - **Term continues**: if the successor has not entered office when the 5 years end, the **outgoing President continues** – the VP or the CJI does not step in.
[1:8] - No term limit in India (USA: two terms, 22nd Amendment); only **Rajendra Prasad** has been elected twice (1952, 1957).

## SUB 2 | Powers of the President
[2:0] - **Art. 53**: the **executive power of the Union is vested in the President**, exercised directly or through subordinate officers **in accordance with the Constitution** – so his power is **not unlimited** (Art. 74: aid and advice).
[2:1] - **Art. 53(2)**: **Supreme Command of the Defence Forces** is vested in the **President** (regulated by law) – not the Defence Minister, Army Chief or a Field Marshal.
[2:2] - **Union executive**: **President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers and Attorney General** (Part V, Chapter I) – not the Speaker or the RS Deputy Chairman.
[2:3] TABLE head: Power | Examples
  [2:3.0] Executive | Appoints the PM and on his advice other Ministers; Governors (Art. 155), CAG (Art. 148), AG (Art. 76), Election Commissioners (Art. 324), UPSC, Finance Commission; Ambassadors and High Commissioners; administers UTs; Art. 240 regulations for A&N, Lakshadweep, DNH and Daman & Diu, and Puducherry when its Assembly is suspended or dissolved
  [2:3.1] Legislative | Integral part of Parliament (Art. 79: President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha); summons and prorogues the Houses, dissolves the Lok Sabha (Art. 85); summons a joint sitting (Art. 108 – presided over by the Speaker); addresses and sends messages (Art. 86); special address (Art. 87); nominates 12 to the Rajya Sabha; assent to Bills; ordinances
  [2:3.2] Financial | Budget laid in his name; Money Bills and demands for grants only on his recommendation; advances from the Contingency Fund; constitutes the Finance Commission
  [2:3.3] Judicial | Appoints SC/HC judges (Third Judges Case 1998: CJI + four senior-most judges collegium); Art. 143 advisory opinion – not binding; Art. 72 pardon
  [2:3.4] Diplomatic and military | Treaties in his name; sends and receives envoys; declares war and concludes peace on the Council's advice
  [2:3.5] Emergency | Art. 352 National, Art. 356 President's Rule, Art. 360 Financial
[2:4] - **Prior recommendation of the President** is needed for: **Money Bills (Art. 117)**, **Art. 3 Bills** (new States, boundaries), Bills involving expenditure from the Consolidated Fund, State Bills restricting trade (Art. 304) – not for Constitution Amendment Bills or every private member's Bill.
[2:5] - **Reports laid before Parliament by the President**: **Finance Commission** recommendations (Art. 281), **CAG** reports (Art. 151), **National Commission for SCs** (Art. 338), UPSC, Linguistic Minorities – but **not the PAC** report (a committee of Parliament itself).
[2:6] - **Art. 78 (PM's duties)**: communicate **all decisions of the Council of Ministers** on administration and **proposals for legislation**; furnish information the President calls for; place before the Council a matter **decided by a single Minister** if the President so requires. With **Art. 86** (address and messages) – all four CDS 2013 statements are correct.
[2:7] - **Address to Parliament (Art. 87)**: at the **first session after each general election** and the **first session of each year**; the speech is **prepared by the Union Cabinet** (Council of Ministers).
[2:8] - **When the Lok Sabha is dissolved** the Council of Ministers continues (Art. 75 needs a Council at all times), and the President still **acts on its advice**.
[2:9] - **First citizen** of India; "**represents the nation but does not lead the nation**" (the PM leads).

## SUB 3 | Vacancy and Acting President (Arts. 62, 65)
[3:0] - **Causes of vacancy**: expiry of term, **death**, **resignation**, **removal by impeachment**, disqualification, election declared void.
[3:1] - **Art. 62(1) – expiry**: the election must be **completed before the term expires**; if delayed, the **outgoing President continues** (no vacancy, so the VP does **not** act).
[3:2] - **Art. 62(2) – death, resignation, removal or otherwise**: election **within six months** of the vacancy (the office can stay vacant at most 6 months); the new President gets a **full five-year term**, not the remainder.
[3:3] - **Art. 65(1)**: in a vacancy by **death, resignation or removal**, the **Vice-President acts as President** until the new President enters office; **Art. 65(2)**: the VP also **discharges the functions** when the President is absent, ill or otherwise unable.
[3:4] - While acting, the VP has all the powers, immunities and emoluments of the President and does **not** act as RS Chairman – the **Deputy Chairman presides** over the Rajya Sabha.
[3:5] - **Both offices vacant**: the **Chief Justice of India**, and in his absence the **senior-most SC judge**, discharges the President's functions – **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969** – not the PM, Speaker, AG or CEC.
[3:6] TABLE head: Acting President | When | Why
  [3:6.0] V.V. Giri (VP) | 3 May – 20 July 1969 | Zakir Husain died in office
  [3:6.1] M. Hidayatullah (CJI) | 20 July – 24 August 1969 | Giri resigned as VP to contest; both offices vacant
  [3:6.2] B.D. Jatti (VP) | February – July 1977 | Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed died in office
  [3:6.3] M. Hidayatullah (VP) | 6–31 October 1982 | Discharged the functions while Zail Singh was abroad for surgery
[3:7] - **M. Hidayatullah** – the **only CJI to act as President** (first and only, 1969); the only person who has been **CJI, acting President and Vice-President (1979–84)**; he performed the President's functions **twice** (1969 as CJI, 1982 as VP).
[3:8] - **Not CJIs who acted**: P.N. Bhagwati, P.B. Gajendragadkar.

## SUB 4 | Impeachment (Art. 61)
[4:0] - **Art. 61** = procedure for **impeachment** (removal) of the President (not Arts. 53, 60, 63, 71, 72, 74, 78); Art. 56(1)(b) refers to it.
[4:1] - **Only ground: "violation of the Constitution"** – not corruption, misbehaviour, criminal offence, moral degradation, unsoundness of mind or professional misconduct (the Constitution does not define the term).
[4:2] - **Who removes him**: **Parliament** (the two Houses) – not the PM, the CJI, the party in power, a court, or the Lok Sabha alone.
[4:3] - **Initiated in either House of Parliament** (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha) – not only the Lok Sabha and not a joint sitting.
[4:4] TABLE head: Step | Requirement
  [4:4.0] Notice | 14 days' written notice, resolution signed by one-fourth of the total members of the initiating House
  [4:4.1] Initiating House | Passes the charge by two-thirds of its total membership
  [4:4.2] Other House | Investigates the charge (President may appear or be represented) and sustains it by two-thirds of its total membership → removed from that date
[4:5] - **Quasi-judicial procedure**; borrowed from the **USA**. **No President has ever been impeached.**
[4:6] - **Nominated MPs take part**; **elected MLAs (States, Delhi, Puducherry) do not** – the President is removed by the national Parliament alone, keeping removal free from narrow regional considerations.
[4:7] - "Impeachment" is used **only for the President**; judges and the Vice-President are "removed". **VP removal**: Rajya Sabha resolution by a majority of all its then members, agreed to by the Lok Sabha; **no ground** is specified.
[4:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.375-376; PMF Polity Notes Part 1 p.138 and Part 2 p.1, p.8-10 (file pages); Rocket Polity Part 1 p.22-23

## SUB 6 | Constitutional Position, Article 74 and Discretion
[6:0] - **Nominal / de jure** executive and **constitutional head of the State**; the **PM and Council of Ministers** are the **real / de facto** executive. A-R: "the Constitution vests executive power in the President" (A) because "he is the constitutional Head of the State" (R) – both true, R explains A.
[6:1] - **Art. 74(1)**: a Council of Ministers headed by the PM to aid and advise the President. **42nd Amendment (1976)**: advice made **binding** ("shall act"). **44th Amendment (1978)**: he may **return the advice once for reconsideration**, then must act on it.
[6:2] - So the President's influence on ministers = he **may seek reconsideration once** before putting his seal on a matter.
[6:3] - **Art. 74(2)**: whether and what advice was tendered **cannot be inquired into by any court**.
[6:4] - **Art. 361**: President/Governor not answerable to any court for official acts; no criminal proceedings during the term; **civil proceedings only after two months' written notice**; no court can compel a Governor to perform a duty.
[6:5] - **Situational discretion** (no constitutional discretion): appointing a PM when no party has a clear majority; dismissing a Ministry that has lost confidence; dissolving the Lok Sabha if the Ministry lost its majority; day-to-day decisions under a **caretaker government**; **suspensive veto** (returning a Bill), **pocket veto** (keeping a Bill pending); asking the Council to **reconsider** advice.
[6:6] - **Not discretionary**: **summoning and proroguing Parliament**, appointing Ministers, appointing the majority leader as PM (a convention), assent to Bills.
[6:7] - **PM** = "keystone of the Cabinet arch" – the President is only the formal head.

## SUB 8 | Veto, Ordinance and Pardon – Summary (details in row 26)
[8:0] TABLE head: Veto | Meaning | Indian President?
  [8:0.0] Absolute | Withholds assent – private members' Bills; Government Bills after the Cabinet resigns | Yes
  [8:0.1] Suspensive | Returns once; must assent if re-passed – not for Money Bills | Yes
  [8:0.2] Pocket | Keeps the Bill pending – the Constitution fixes no time limit | Yes
  [8:0.3] Qualified | Overridable by a higher majority (USA) | No
[8:1] - **Constitution Amendment Bill**: must give assent (**24th Amendment, 1971**). The **2025 Presidential Reference** (opinion of 20 November 2025) held courts cannot fix timelines or create "deemed assent".
[8:2] - **Ordinance (Art. 123)**: only when Parliament (either House) is not in session; must be laid and **lapses six weeks after reassembly**; may be **withdrawn at any time**; subject to **judicial review**. **D.C. Wadhwa (1987)**: re-promulgation in Bihar a fraud on the Constitution; **Krishna Kumar Singh (2017)**: **seven judges**, laying is mandatory.
[8:3] - **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026** (President Droupadi Murmu): SC judges **33 → 37, excluding the CJI** (38 with the CJI).
[8:4] TABLE head: Pardon term | Meaning
  [8:4.0] Pardon | Removes both sentence and conviction
  [8:4.1] Commutation | Lighter form (death → rigorous life imprisonment)
  [8:4.2] Remission | Shorter period, same character
  [8:4.3] Respite | Lesser sentence for special reasons (pregnancy, disability)
  [8:4.4] Reprieve | Temporary stay of execution
[8:5] - **Art. 72** (President): **court-martial**, offences against **Union law**, **all death sentences**; **Art. 161** (Governor): State laws; the Governor **cannot pardon a death sentence or a court-martial** sentence (he may suspend, remit or commute a death sentence).
[8:6] - **Cases**: **Maru Ram (1980)** – exercised on the Council's advice; **Kehar Singh (1989)** – may examine evidence afresh, **no right of oral hearing**; **Epuru Sudhakar (2006)** – **limited judicial review** on mala fide, arbitrary or irrelevant grounds.
