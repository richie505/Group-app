## SUB 0 | Constitutional Government = Limited Government
[0:0] - **Constitutional government** by definition = **limited government** – a government **limited by the terms of the Constitution** (UPSC 2020, 2021 ×2) – not "government by the legislature", "popular", "multi-party", "federal structure" or a head with nominal/real powers.
[0:1] - "**Persons may change but rules should not change**" = principle of **constitutional government** (rule of law, not of persons).
[0:2] - In a democracy the powers of the government are limited by the **Constitution**.
[0:3] - **Constitutionalism**: limits + rule of law + checks and balances + judicial review + FRs; a **legislative majority cannot override constitutional limitations**.
[0:4] - Not an indication of true democracy: "**rule by majority only**" – free and fair elections, respect for the individual and equality before law are.
[0:5] - **PIL** is **not indispensable** for the rule of law, which existed before PIL through **Arts. 14, 32, 226**.

## SUB 1 | Separation of Powers – Montesquieu and the Indian Degree
[1:0] - **Montesquieu**: **division of powers** best protects liberty; "**The Spirit of Laws**" (**1748**); drew on the **British** constitution.
[1:1] - **Best safeguard of liberty in a liberal democracy = separation of powers** (UPSC 2021) – not a committed judiciary, centralisation or an elected government.
[1:2] - **Presidential** government operates on **separation of powers**; **parliamentary** = **fusion** (ministers drawn from and responsible to the legislature). Division of powers between Centre and States = federalism.
[1:3] - The organ made up of the **people's elected representatives** = the **legislature** (judges are appointed; the media is not an organ of government).
[1:4] - India: **Nominal head** President; **real executive** Council of Ministers. **Ambedkar** preferred **responsibility over stability**; reasons: familiarity (1919/1935 Acts), diversity, avoiding American-style deadlock.
[1:5] - India: **no rigid separation** – implicit, not absolute, **functional**; overlaps → checks and balances (**Ram Jawaya Kapur, 1955**). Separation of powers = basic feature (**Kesavananda, 1973**).
[1:6] - **Art. 50 (DPSP)**: separate the judiciary from the executive **in the public services of the State**. Judiciary insulated: **Arts. 50, 121, 211, 235**.
[1:7] - **Munro's Madras Regulations (1816)** reversed the Cornwallis separation – the **Collector** again got **magisterial and police powers**.

## SUB 4 | Judicial Checks and Overriding a Judgment
[4:0] - **Judicial review** of laws and executive action: **Arts. 13, 32, 226**; an ultra vires law is **void**.
[4:1] - **Art. 121**: no discussion in Parliament on an SC/HC judge's conduct except on a removal motion; **Art. 211** for States. **Arts. 122, 212**: courts cannot question legislative proceedings for irregular procedure.
[4:2] - **Art. 142** complete justice – fills gaps (Vishaka before the POSH Act, 2013).
[4:3] TABLE head: Case | Holding
  [4:3.0] Ram Jawaya Kapur (1955) | No rigid separation
  [4:3.1] Kesavananda (1973) | Separation of powers = basic feature
  [4:3.2] Raj Narain (1975) | Legislature cannot decide an election dispute; 39th Amendment validation of PM's election struck
  [4:3.3] Minerva Mills (1980) | 42nd Amendment bar on review struck
  [4:3.4] I.R. Coelho (2007) | Post-24 April 1973 Ninth Schedule entries reviewable
[4:4] - Ways the legislature can respond to a judgment, and their limits:
[4:5] TABLE head: Option to override a judgment | Limit
  [4:5.0] Constitutional amendment | Basic structure
  [4:5.1] Validating law | Must remove the basis; cannot simply declare the judgment void
  [4:5.2] Ninth Schedule (76th, TN 69%) | Coelho review
  [4:5.3] Review Art. 137; reference Art. 143 | Art. 143 advice not binding
  [4:5.4] 1st / 24th / 26th / 77th Amendment | Overrode Champakam / Golaknath / Madhav Rao (privy purses) / Indra Sawhney
[4:6] - Parliament overrides a judgment by **changing the law or constitutional basis** – not by a simple resolution.
[4:7] - **One Nation One Election Bill** (129th Amendment Bill, 2024): amends **Arts. 83, 172, 327** (and adds 82A).
