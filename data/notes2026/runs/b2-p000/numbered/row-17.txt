## SUB 0 | Basics – Part IVA, Article 51A, 42nd and 86th Amendments
[0:0] - **Fundamental Duties = Part IVA**, which has **only one Article – Article 51A** (not Part IV, VI-A, III; not Arts. 49, 50, 51, 52). Opening words: "It shall be the duty of **every citizen of India**".
[0:1] - The **original Constitution had no Fundamental Duties** (not in the text completed in Nov 1949 or on 26 Jan 1950); they are a **separate Part**, **not part of the Fundamental Rights**.
[0:2] - Added by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** (Indira Gandhi govt; "Mini Constitution"), on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**; in force **3 January 1977**. Not the 24th, 31st, 38th, 41st, 44th or 46th.
[0:3] - **Number**: **10** duties in 1976 (clauses a–j – the "**10 commandments**") → **11 at present**; the **11th duty, 51A(k)**, added by the **86th Amendment Act, 2002** (not 87th, 96th or "2001").
[0:4] TABLE head: Particular | 42nd CAA, 1976 | 86th CAA, 2002
  [0:4.0] Duties | Inserted Part IVA, Art. 51A, clauses (a)–(j) | Added 51A(k) – parent/guardian to give education to child 6–14 years
  [0:4.1] Same Act also | Added DPSP Art. 48A; moved forests and protection of wild animals and birds to the Concurrent List (Entries 17A, 17B) | Inserted Art. 21A (Right to Education as an FR); substituted Art. 45
[0:5] - **51A(g)** (environment) came in 1976, after the **1972 Stockholm Conference**.
[0:6] - Part IVA is one of **four Parts added after 1950**: **IVA, IXA, IXB, XIVA**. Other Parts: **Part VI = The States**; **Part IXA = The Municipalities** (Part XI is Centre–State relations).
[0:7] - Number game: **8** suggested (Swaran Singh) – **10** added (1976) – **11** now (2002).

## SUB 1 | The Eleven Duties – Clause-wise
[1:0] TABLE head: Clause | Fundamental Duty | Nature
  [1:0.0] 51A(a) | Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and National Anthem | Civic
  [1:0.1] 51A(b) | Cherish and follow the noble ideals of the national struggle for freedom | Moral
  [1:0.2] 51A(c) | Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India | Civic
  [1:0.3] 51A(d) | Defend the country and render national service when called upon | Civic
  [1:0.4] 51A(e) | Promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood transcending religious, linguistic, regional or sectional diversities; renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women | Civic/Moral
  [1:0.5] 51A(f) | Value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture | Moral
  [1:0.6] 51A(g) | Protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife; compassion for living creatures | Civic
  [1:0.7] 51A(h) | Develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform | Moral
  [1:0.8] 51A(i) | Safeguard public property and abjure violence | Civic
  [1:0.9] 51A(j) | Strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity | Moral
  [1:0.10] 51A(k) | Parent/guardian to provide opportunities for education to child or ward aged 6–14 | Civic
[1:1] - **Moral duties: (b), (f), (h), (j)**; civic duties: (a), (c), (d), (g), (i).
[1:2] - The **first duty**: respect the ideals of the **Constitution, National Flag and National Anthem** (all three) – so respecting the National Flag is a **fundamental duty** (not a right, DPSP or "ordinary/statutory" duty).
[1:3] - Weaker sections / STs: the duty closest to social justice is **51A(e) – fraternity and common brotherhood**; protecting cultural diversity = **51A(f) – composite culture**.
[1:4] - **Protection** of **wildlife** is a duty (51A(g)); the Flag is to be **respected**, not "protected".
[1:5] - **Fraternity**: 51A(e) speaks of harmony and common brotherhood; the Preamble speaks of fraternity.

  [2:0.0] To vote in public/general elections | Nowhere (a statutory right)
  [2:0.1] To pay taxes | Swaran Singh proposal not accepted
  [2:0.2] Family planning / population control | Nowhere
  [2:0.3] Compulsory military service | 51A(d) says only "when called upon"
  [2:0.4] Protect weaker sections from social injustice / their educational and economic interests | DPSP Art. 46
  [2:0.5] Protect monuments and places of national importance | DPSP Art. 49
  [2:0.6] Organise village panchayats | DPSP Art. 40
  [2:0.7] Abolition of untouchability | FR Art. 17
  [2:0.8] Abolition of titles | FR Art. 18
  [2:0.9] Separation of judiciary; free and compulsory education (as a State duty) | DPSP Arts. 50, 45
[2:1] - Also **not** duties: "to protect the **elderly, differently abled and children**" (51A(k) only covers a parent's duty to educate), "to **respect parents and teachers**".
[2:2] - **48A vs 51A(g)**: environment as a **DPSP** (State) = **Art. 48A**; as a **citizen's duty** = **Art. 51A(g)**. Public property by citizens = 51A(i), not 48A.
[2:3] - Scientific temper (51A(h)) and public property (51A(i)) are **only duties**, with no DPSP look-alike.

## SUB 4 | Nature, Enforceability, Source and Criticism
[4:0] - **Non-justiciable** (like DPSPs): **cannot be enforced by writs**; the Constitution gives **no legal sanction** for violation – **only FRs are justiciable** (enforceable in courts; not DPSPs, the Preamble or Duties).
[4:1] - **Parliament may enforce them by legislation**; courts use them to **interpret ambiguous laws**, test **constitutionality** and judge the **reasonableness** of a law restricting an FR.
[4:2] - **UPSC 2017**: "a legislative process has been provided to enforce them" and "they are correlative to legal duties" – **both false**.
[4:3] - **Citizens only** – not foreigners (no exception, not even 51A(i)); FRs such as Arts. 14, 20, 21, 25 extend to foreigners too. **51A(k)** is the only duty addressed to **parents/guardians**.
[4:4] - **Source: Constitution of the former USSR** (Russian); among major democracies only **Japan** has a comparable list. India was **not** the first country to adopt duties.
[4:5] # Justice J.S. Verma Committee (1999) – laws already giving effect to duties
[4:6] TABLE head: Law | Duty
  [4:6.0] Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971 – insult to Flag/Constitution: imprisonment up to 3 years, or fine, or both (Sec. 2) | 51A(a) – Constitution, Flag, Anthem
  [4:6.1] Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 | 51A(e) – harmony
  [4:6.2] Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967 | 51A(c) – sovereignty and integrity
  [4:6.3] Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972; Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980 | 51A(g) – environment
  [4:6.4] Representation of the People Act, 1951 | Corrupt practices on religion / enmity
[4:7] - The Verma Committee was set up to **operationalise** duties and **identified existing legal provisions** – it did not ask for implementation "irrespective of" them.
[4:8] - Imputations prejudicial to national integration (51A(c)): now **Sec. 197, BNS 2023** (earlier IPC 153B). **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** rests on **Art. 253**.
[4:9] # Criticism and significance
[4:10] - Criticism: **not exhaustive** (omits voting, taxes, family planning); **vague** ("noble ideals", "scientific temper"); a "**code of moral precepts**" (non-justiciable); **superfluous**; an **appendage to Part IV** instead of following Part III.
[4:11] - Significance: reminder of obligations; warning against anti-national acts; **help courts on reasonableness**; promote discipline and participation.

## SUB 5 | Rights and Duties – Correlation and Cases
[5:0] - **Rights are correlative with duties** (UPSC 2017) – every right implies a duty; **Holland**: others' forbearance from interfering with a right is called a duty. "**Rights without duties are like men without shadows**."
[5:1] - Example: **Art. 19(1)(a)** (free speech) goes with **51A(c)** (sovereignty, unity, integrity).
[5:2] TABLE head: Case | Year | Held
  [5:2.0] AIIMS Students' Union v. AIIMS | 2001 (reported 2002) | Duties are as important as Fundamental Rights; rights and duties correlative
  [5:2.1] Mohan Kumar Singhania v. Union of India | 1991 | Art. 51A may be used to interpret laws and test their validity
  [5:2.2] Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala | 1986 | Duties cannot override FRs; respect for the Anthem needs no compelled singing
  [5:2.3] M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India | – | CSR includes environmental protection, linked to 51A(g)
  [5:2.4] SC on corporations | Mar 2026 | Corporations, as legal persons, also carry the 51A(g) duty (CSR = at least 2% of average net profit, Sec. 135, Companies Act 2013)
  [5:2.5] SC on Pollution Control Boards | Aug 2025 | Linked PCB enforcement to 51A(g)
[5:3] - **Ramlila Maidan case (2012)**: Parts III, IV and IVA are to be read together. **Subhash Kumar (1991)**: clean environment is part of **Art. 21**.
[5:4] - **Vande Mataram** recitation debate (Aug 2026): duties under 51A **cannot curtail Arts. 25 and 26** (freedom of religion).
