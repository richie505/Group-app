=== Ireland and South Africa – Directive Principles, President's Election, Rajya Sabha, Amendment
# Ireland (Eire, Constitution of 1937)
- **Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Arts. 36-51)** – borrowed from the **Irish Constitution** (Ireland itself took the idea from **Spain**) (asked 11 times: APPSC G2 2003, MPDO 1998, TWO 2012, SI 2019; UPPSC, MPPSC 2023).
- **Method of election of the President** (indirect, proportional representation by single transferable vote) – Ireland.
- **Nomination of members to the Rajya Sabha** (12 by the President) – Ireland.
- **⚠ Trap:** DPSP is **not** from Weimar Germany, South Africa, Canada or Britain; Ambedkar called the DPSP a **"novel feature"** of the Constitution.
# South Africa
- **Procedure for amendment of the Constitution (Art. 368)** – South Africa (SSC 2016; APPSC FSO).
- **Election of members of the Rajya Sabha** (by elected MLAs, proportional representation) – South Africa (APPSC 2019).
- **⚠ Trap:** the amendment procedure is **not** from France, Ireland, Britain or the USA (APPSC Lecturers 2018: "Amendment – France" is the wrong statement).
> RS pair: **election** of RS members = **South Africa**; **nomination** to the RS and the **President's election** = **Ireland**.

=== USA – Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Preamble and the Judiciary
| Feature | Borrowed from |
|---|---|
| **Fundamental Rights** (Part III, **Arts. 12-35**) | USA (Bill of Rights) |
| **Judicial review** | USA |
| **Independence of the judiciary** | USA |
| **Idea of the Preamble** | USA |
| **Post of Vice-President** (ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha) | USA |
| **Impeachment of the President** | USA |
| **Removal of Supreme Court and High Court judges** | USA |
| **Written Constitution** | USA – the world's **first written Constitution** (1787), also the **shortest** (7 Articles) |
- **Fundamental Rights = USA** (asked 6 times: APPSC Degree Lecturers 2020; UPPSC, UPRO 2014, UDA 2010).
- **Judicial review originated in the USA** – *Marbury v. Madison* (1803); the words "judicial review" appear in **neither** the US nor the Indian text; **Britain** (parliamentary sovereignty, no written constitution) has **no judicial review** of Acts of Parliament.
- **Preamble idea = USA** (asked 5 times); only the **language** of the Preamble is from **Australia**; liberty, equality, fraternity = France.
- Judicial review in India is narrower: Art. 21 uses **"procedure established by law"** (Japan), not the US **"due process of law"**.
- **USA = presidential** form of government (India, Britain, Canada are parliamentary).

=== Britain – Parliamentary Features, Rule of Law, Single Citizenship
| Feature | Borrowed from |
|---|---|
| **Parliamentary government**, **cabinet system** | Britain |
| **Single citizenship** (USA has dual citizenship) | Britain |
| **Bicameralism** | Britain |
| **Rule of Law**, **legislative (law-making) procedure** | Britain |
| **Office of Speaker** and his role | Britain |
| **Prerogative writs** ("petitions") | Britain |
| **Parliamentary privileges** | Britain |
| **Nominal head** (President, like the Crown) | Britain |
- "Which feature is from Britain?" – **cabinet system**, **single citizenship**, **bicameralism**, **writs** (APPSC 2019, FRO; SSC 2019). **Independence of judiciary** is US, not British (APPSC Panchayat Secy 2017 → **1, 2, 3**).
- **Britain has no single written constitution**, but the **concept of constitutional government** goes back to the **Magna Carta (1215)**; the first **written** constitution is the US one.
- **Rule of Law** – A.V. **Dicey** (1885): supremacy of law, equality before law, and rights of the individual secured by courts; its main objective is to secure the **liberty of citizens** against arbitrary power. A **basic feature** (Kesavananda).
- **Single citizenship** – one of the framers' crucial steps to **tackle regionalism** and build national unity.
- **Contempt of court:** the power of courts to punish for contempt comes from **English common law** (British-era courts; now Arts. 129, 215).
- **Integrated judiciary:** one hierarchy (SC → HCs → subordinate courts) enforces both Central and State laws, unlike the US dual courts; this **single system of courts was adopted from the GoI Act 1935** (Laxmikanth), so "borrowed from the British system" is the false reason; India's judicial system follows the British common-law pattern.

=== Federal Features – Canada and Australia
# Canada
- **Federation with a strong Centre** – Indian federalism follows the **Canadian** pattern (SSC 2008; UPPSC 2017; APPSC DAO 2011).
- **"Union of States" (Art. 1):** **not the result of an agreement** among States and **no right to secede**; the Republic of India rests on the **declared will of the people** ("We, the people of India").
- **Residuary powers with the Centre** – **Art. 248** (Parliament) – Canada (SSC CGL 2019; APPSC HWO); in the **USA and Australia** residuary powers lie with the **States**.
- **Appointment of State Governors by the Centre** – Canada.
- **Advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court (Art. 143)** – Canada.
# Australia
- **Concurrent List** – Australia (asked 7 times: APPSC 2017, Social History 2019, G2; UPPSC 2015, 2016).
- **Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse** – Australia (not Canada).
- **Joint sitting** of the two Houses (**Art. 108**, presided over by the **Lok Sabha Speaker**) – Australia.
- **Language of the Preamble** – Australia.
- Makers followed the Canadian scheme of division but **added a third list** (Concurrent); in a clash, the **Union law prevails**.
- **Seventh Schedule today:** Union **98**, State **59**, Concurrent **52** (originally **97, 66, 47**).
> Residuary powers = **Canada**, not USA; Concurrent List = **Australia**, not Canada. With no 1935 option, "scheme of federation" = **Canada**.

=== Government of India Act 1935 – the Largest Source, and the Length of the Constitution
- **Single largest source:** federal scheme, **office of Governor**, **judiciary**, **Public Service Commissions**, **emergency provisions**, administrative details – "larger part" of the Constitution (APPSC JA 2012).
- **Greatest / most profound influence** on the Constitution = **GoI Act 1935** (about 250 provisions), not the British Constitution (APPSC G1 2002; G1 PYQ – some answer keys gave "British Constitution").
- **Federal system and distribution of powers** between Centre and States = the **1935 Act** scheme (UPSC 2012; APPSC G2).
- **1935 lists:** Federal (**59**), Provincial (**54**), **Concurrent (36)** items (SI 2023) → today's **Union, State and Concurrent Lists**.
- **Not** a 1935 constituent: a **written Constitution made by Indians** – accepted in principle only by the **August Offer, 1940** (UPPSC 2010).
# Why the Constitution is the lengthiest
- **Factors:** **vast geography and diversity**; **bulky 1935 Act**; a **single Constitution for Centre and States**; dominance of **legal luminaries**; detailed administrative and **social-economic** provisions; framers' wish to **safeguard the young democracy**.
- **NOT a factor:** "adoption of the American model" (APPSC ATWO); **local bodies** were **not** in the original Constitution (Parts IX, IXA added in **1992**) (APPSC ASWO Mains).
- **Original:** **395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules** → now about **470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules** (as of 2026).
- **Criticisms:** **"bag of borrowings"** (sources), **"elephantine"** (length), **"lawyers' paradise"** (legal detail); reply – borrowed features were **adapted** to Indian conditions.
- **Philosophical part** (FRs, DPSPs) from the USA and Ireland; **political part** (parliamentary system) from Britain; **structural part** from the 1935 Act.

=== Emergency, USSR, France, Japan
| Feature | Borrowed from |
|---|---|
| **Emergency provisions** (general scheme) | **GoI Act 1935**; with no 1935 option → **Germany (Weimar)** |
| **Suspension of Fundamental Rights during Emergency** | **Weimar Constitution of Germany** |
| **Fundamental Duties (Art. 51A)** | **USSR** (added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**; 11th duty by the **86th**, 2002) |
| **Ideals of justice – social, economic, political** (Preamble) | **USSR** |
| **Five-Year Plans** / Planning Commission idea | **USSR** |
| **Republic**; **liberty, equality, fraternity** (Preamble) | **France** |
| **Procedure established by law** (Art. 21) | **Japan** |
- **Emergency:** "on the lines of" Germany (APPSC 2017; NDA 2009 – Weimar); **not** from South Africa, the USA or the UK.
- In a National Emergency FRs can be suspended **except Arts. 20 and 21**; **Art. 19** is the most easily suspended.
- **⚠ Trap:** "Ideals of **Justice**" is the one Preamble ideal **not** from France – it is from the USSR (APPSC DAO 2020); Fundamental Duties = USSR, not France.
- **Election Commission (Art. 324)** – an Indian provision, not inspired by any foreign constitution (JPSC 2023).
