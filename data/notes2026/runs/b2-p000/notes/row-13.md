=== Right to Property Deleted – the 44th Amendment
# What was deleted, by whom, when
- **Amendment:** the **44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978** deleted the right to property from the list of Fundamental Rights – not the 42nd (1976), 43rd, 24th, 25th or 62nd.
- **Articles omitted:** **Art. 19(1)(f)** (acquire, hold and dispose of property) and **Art. 31** (compulsory acquisition) were taken out of **Part III**; a new **Art. 300A** was inserted in **Part XII (Chapter IV)**.
- **Government:** passed under the **Janata Party government of Morarji Desai** (1977-79) – not Indira Gandhi, Narasimha Rao or Vajpayee; nor a "Congress Government".
- **Date:** the Act is "of 1978" (exam answer for "which year": **1978**); it got assent on **30 April 1979**, and the omission of Art. 31 (s.6) took effect from **20 June 1979** – so property **ceased to be an FR from 1979**.
- **Why:** property rights were the most litigated FR and kept blocking **land reform** and acquisition for public use.
- **Result:** FRs were **originally seven, now six**; property is the **only FR ever deleted** (not speech, religion, equality or constitutional remedies).
> Asked as: "Which amendment deleted property?" (44th – APPSC G2 2008, MPDO 1998; SSC 2008, 2017; MPPSC 2020; UPPSC; CGPSC), "The right to ___ has ceased to be an FR" (property – G2 2011), "Which is not an FR?" (property), A-R "300A because 44th removed it from Part III" (A and R true, R explains A – APPSC G2, NDA 2000).

=== Right to Property Today – Article 300A, a Legal Right
# Article 300A
- **Text:** "**No person shall be deprived of his property save by authority of law**" – the Article that bars the State from taking property except by law (**300A**, not Art. 300 = suits by/against Government, Art. 299 = contracts, Art. 301 = freedom of trade; there is no 299A).
- **Status:** a **legal right** (also called a **constitutional right**, since it is in the Constitution) – **not a Fundamental Right**, not a DPSP, not a natural right.
- **Who:** **any person** – citizens, non-citizens and **companies** (UPSC 2021: "legal right available to any person").
- **Enforcement:** through **Art. 226** or an ordinary suit – **not Art. 32**; it protects against **executive** action without law, not against legislation; the State is not constitutionally bound to pay compensation.
- **Human right:** the Supreme Court has repeatedly held that the right to property is **also a human right** (forcible dispossession without due process = human-rights violation); UDHR 1948 Art. 17 recognises it.
- **Not basic structure:** in **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** the Court held the right to property is **not part of the basic structure** – so "right to property is part of the basic structure" is the **wrong** statement.
- **AP angle:** **GGR Housing v. State** (AP High Court, May 2026) – strict statutory due process in land acquisition under Art. 300A.
> Asked as: "At present, the right to property is a…" (legal right – APPSC AEE 2012, AMVI; UPPCS 1992, 1996; UKPSC 2021; MPPSC 2025), "Which Article?" (300A – APPSC G1, AD Economics), UPSC 2005 statements (only "legal right, not FR" correct: Art. 301 is trade; 300A came from the Janata government).

=== Cultural and Educational Rights – Articles 29 and 30
# Article 29 – protection of interests of minorities
| Clause | Provision |
|---|---|
| 29(1) | **Any section of citizens** with a distinct **language, script or culture** has the right to **conserve** it (not limited to minorities) |
| 29(2) | No citizen denied admission to a State-maintained or State-aided institution on grounds only of **religion, race, caste, language** (sex and place of birth are not in the list) |
- **Marginal heading:** Art. 29 = "**Protection of interests of minorities**" – the Article that "protects the interests of minorities" (not 30, 28 or 32).
- **Objective of the group:** to help **minorities conserve their culture** – not to evolve a single culture or eradicate illiteracy.
# Article 30 – minority educational institutions
| Clause | Provision |
|---|---|
| 30(1) | **All minorities, religious or linguistic**, may **establish and administer educational institutions of their choice** – the "**Charter of Education Rights**"; madrasas run under it |
| 30(1A) (44th Amendment, 1978) | On compulsory acquisition of a minority institution's property, the amount fixed must not restrict or abrogate the Art. 30 right |
| 30(2) | No discrimination in grant of aid because an institution is under minority management |
- Art. 29 applies to **citizens only**; Art. 30's text is not confined to citizens.
- Art. 30 is **not absolute**: the State may regulate **standards**, but may **not take away the minority character** ("right to administer is not a right to maladminister").
- **Spreading religion** (propagation) is **Art. 25** – a right of all persons, minorities included.
# Article 350A – mother tongue
- **Art. 350A:** every State and local authority to provide **instruction in the mother tongue at the primary stage** to children of **linguistic minority** groups (not 300A, 351A, 301A); **Art. 350B** = Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities.
> Statement trap (APPSC ATWO): "only religious minorities may set up institutions" ✗ (linguistic too); "religious minorities are defined in Art. 350A" ✗; "religious minorities may establish and administer educational institutions" ✓; "minorities may spread their religion" ✓ → **3 and 4 only**.

=== Legal Right or Fundamental Right? – Vote and Information
# The distinction
- **Every FR is a constitutional right, but not every constitutional right is an FR**; a **legal right** comes from ordinary law and can be changed by it; every FR is a human right, but not the reverse.
| Right | Status | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Property | Legal (constitutional) right | Art. 300A |
| Vote and to be elected | **Constitutional right – UPSC 2017 official key**; **statutory/legal right – Kuldip Nayar (2009)**, UPPCS keys and NextGen | Art. 326 (adult suffrage, 18+); **Sec. 62 RPA 1951**; registration Sec. 19 RPA 1950 |
| Information | **Legal right** under the **RTI Act, 2005**; the Supreme Court also reads it into **Art. 19(1)(a)** (so "both fundamental and legal" is also accepted – NDA 2010) | RTI Act 2005; Art. 19(1)(a) |
| Work | DPSP | Art. 41 |
| Education (6-14) | Fundamental Right | Art. 21A |
- **Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India (2009)**: voting is only a **statutory/legal** right.
- **Voting paradox**: voting is statutory, yet the **right to know candidate details, NOTA and ballot secrecy** are recognised under **Art. 19(1)(a)**.
> Read the options: if "constitutional right" and "legal right" both appear for the vote, UPSC (2017) keyed constitutional; UPPCS (2010, 2015) keyed legal. For RTI with no "both" option, the answer is **legal right**.

=== Who Is a Minority?
- **Not defined:** "minority" is used in **Arts. 29, 30, 350A, 350B** but **not defined** in the Constitution (Art. 30 does not define it); no 10% or 10,000 population test exists.
- **Recognised kinds:** the Constitution recognises **religious and linguistic** minorities only – not ethnic minorities (UPSC 1999).
- **NCM Act, 1992:** "minority" = a community **notified by the Central Government**; the **NCM** is a **statutory** body (quasi-judicial, civil-court powers), not constitutional.
- **Unit = the State:** religious and linguistic minorities are determined **State-wise** (**T.M.A. Pai, 2002**); tests: numerical inferiority, non-dominance, distinct identity.
| Basis | Determined by |
|---|---|
| Religious minority | NCM, Ministry of Minority Affairs – national notification |
| Linguistic minority | State-wise |
- **Six notified minorities:** **Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Parsis, Jains** (Jains added 2014); linguistic minorities are not among these religious categories.
- National notification brings a group within NCM and central schemes (scholarships, **PMJVK**, **PM VIKAS**).
- **Mar 2026:** Kerala's **Malayalam Bhasha Bill** contested as harming the **Kannada** linguistic minority in **Kasaragod**.
> A-R (UPPCS 2019): "Art. 30 does not define minorities" ✓; "the Constitution recognises only linguistic and religious minorities" ✓ – but R does not explain A.

=== Land Acquisition Laws and Enemy Property
# Land Acquisition Act, 1894 → LARR Act, 2013
- **1894 Act:** made by the **British**; in force **1 March 1894**; purpose was **not land reform** but acquisition for public purposes, institutions and companies; became disputed and was **replaced** by the **LARR Act, 2013**.
- **LARR Act, 2013:** **Social Impact Assessment compulsory**; compensation up to **4x** market value (**rural**), **2x** (**urban**); **unused land returned after 5 years**.
# Enemy Property (Amendment and Validation) Act, 2017 (Bill of 2016)
- Enemy property stays **vested in the Custodian**; **the law of succession/inheritance does not apply** to it, and no legal heir can claim it – it is **not returned to heirs**.

=== Before 1978 – Saving Clauses and Ninth Schedule
# The journey of Article 31
| Stage | Position |
|---|---|
| 1950 | FR under **Art. 19(1)(f)** + **Art. 31** – the most disputed FR |
| 1st Amendment, 1951 | **Arts. 31A, 31B** and the **Ninth Schedule** to shield land-reform laws |
| 4th (1955), 17th (1964), 25th (1971) | Narrowed compensation; **17th**: land under personal cultivation acquired only on paying **market value** |
| 44th Amendment, 1978 | Deleted 19(1)(f) and 31 → Art. 300A |
# Saving clauses – 31A, 31B, 31C
| Article | Inserted by | Saves |
|---|---|---|
| **31A** | 1st, 1951 | Laws on **acquisition of estates** etc. |
| **31B** | 1st, 1951 | Acts in the **Ninth Schedule** – immune from FR challenge |
| **31C** | **25th, 1971** | Laws giving effect to **Arts. 39(b), 39(c)**; the 42nd's extension to all DPSPs struck down in **Minerva Mills (1980)** |
- **I.R. Coelho (2007)**, 9 judges: Ninth Schedule laws inserted **after 24 April 1973** are open to **basic structure** review.
