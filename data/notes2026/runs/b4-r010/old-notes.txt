=== NOTES 4:10 | Earthquakes – Causes, Seismic Waves, Measurement, Distribution, Effects
## SUB 0 | Earthquake Basics and Terms
[10:0:0] - An earthquake is a **geological** disaster, not an atmospheric one; a natural event **not yet predictable accurately** (time, place, magnitude) (APPSC 2022).
[10:0:1] - **Seismology** = branch of geology that studies earthquakes. **John Milne** = **Father of Modern Seismology** (early horizontal seismograph).
[10:0:2] # Focus vs Epicentre
[10:0:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [10:0:3.0] Focus (hypocentre) | Point of origin inside Earth where rupture starts and energy is released; shallow or deep
  [10:0:3.1] Epicentre | Surface point directly above the focus; first to record waves; intensity and damage worst here
[10:0:4] - Typical crustal quakes: **10–29 km** deep. Rare **continental mantle** quakes start **80+ km below the Moho**; **Stanford** made the **first global map** of them.
[10:0:5] # Seismic Doublet
[10:0:6] - **Seismic doublet**: two **independent** quakes of **similar magnitude**, close in time and place (unlike aftershocks, which taper off).
[10:0:7] - **26 Jun 2026**: USGS-described doublet hit **Caracas, Venezuela** (M 7.1 and M 7.5) on the Caribbean–South American **strike-slip** boundary; 589 dead.
[10:0:8] Energy is released at the FOCUS; waves are first recorded at the EPICENTRE.
## SUB 1 | Causes and Types of Earthquakes
[10:1:0] TABLE head: Type | Cause | Examples
  [10:1:0.0] Tectonic | Fault stress exceeds rock strength → rupture; at divergent, convergent, transform boundaries | Himalayas; San Andreas (transform)
  [10:1:0.1] Volcanic | Rising magma fractures rock; volcanic structures collapse | Ring of Fire volcanoes
  [10:1:0.2] Reservoir-induced | Weight of water when large reservoirs fill | Koyna 1967 (Maharashtra) – India's classic case
  [10:1:0.3] Intraplate | Old hidden faults in stable shields | Latur (Killari) 1993, Koyna, Jabalpur, Bhuj (Kutch)
  [10:1:0.4] Induced (other) | Mining, hydraulic fracturing (fracking); low magnitude | –
[10:1:1] - **Latur 1993**: far from any plate boundary → quakes strike stable areas along hidden faults (APPSC 2025).
[10:1:2] - **Koyna** more earthquake-prone: dam on an **old fault plane**, triggered by water-level changes (R explains A).
[10:1:3] - All four are causes: fault development, movement along a fault, volcanic eruption, folding of rocks.
[10:1:4] - San Andreas movement is **tectonic**, not induced. Ring of Fire = volcanic and convergent-margin activity (not transform).
[10:1:5] - **AP**: reservoir-triggered tremors possible near **Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Polavaram** and old Eastern Ghats faults.
## SUB 2 | Seismic Waves
[10:2:0] TABLE head: Wave | Arrival | Particle motion | Medium
  [10:2:0.0] P (primary) | 1st (fastest) | Longitudinal, push-pull, along direction of travel | Solid, liquid, gas
  [10:2:0.1] S (secondary) | 2nd | Transverse, perpendicular to travel | Solids only
  [10:2:0.2] Surface (L) | Last (slowest) | Love: side to side; Rayleigh: rolling like ocean waves | Along surface; most damage
[10:2:1] - **Shadow zone**: no S-waves beyond about **105°** from the epicentre → **outer core is liquid**.
[10:2:2] - Love and Rayleigh waves are both **surface waves**.
## SUB 3 | Measurement – Magnitude vs Intensity
[10:3:0] TABLE head: Feature | Magnitude | Intensity
  [10:3:0.0] Measures | Energy released at the focus | Observed effects on people, buildings, ground at a place
  [10:3:0.1] Nature | Quantitative; one value per quake (APPSC 2025) | Qualitative; falls with distance from epicentre
  [10:3:0.2] Scale | Richter (Charles F. Richter, 1935); now Moment Magnitude (Mw) | Modified Mercalli (MMI) and MSK, both I–XII
[10:3:1] - **Richter**: logarithmic, based on **wave amplitude**; each +1 = **10× amplitude**, about **31× energy**.
[10:3:2] - **Mw**: based on **rupture area** of the fault and size of seismic waves; has largely replaced Richter for strong quakes.
[10:3:3] - MMI: I (not felt) to XII (total destruction).
[10:3:4] "Richter measures intensity" is strictly wrong – it measures magnitude; intensity = Mercalli. But one APPSC key accepted Richter for "intensity" as the closest option.
[10:3:5] # Instruments
[10:3:6] TABLE head: Instrument | Use
  [10:3:6.0] Seismograph | Records seismic waves (APPSC 2025)
  [10:3:6.1] Seismometer | The sensor (pendulum/spring) inside a seismograph; broadband ones in India's network
  [10:3:6.2] Seismogram | The recording itself
  [10:3:6.3] Seismoscope | Only shows that a tremor occurred and its time
  [10:3:6.4] Tiltmeter | Ground tilt at faults and volcanoes
  [10:3:6.5] Creepmeter | Slow slip (creep) along a fault
## SUB 4 | World Distribution
[10:4:0] TABLE head: Belt | Plate setting | Examples
  [10:4:0.0] Circum-Pacific (Ring of Fire) | Subduction; most earthquakes (~90%) | Japan, Philippines, Indonesia, Chile, Andes, Alaska, California
  [10:4:0.1] Mid-Continental (Alpine-Himalayan / Mediterranean-Himalayan) | Continental collision; second | Italy, Türkiye, Himalayas, India
  [10:4:0.2] Mid-oceanic ridges (Mid-Atlantic) | Divergent; shallow, milder quakes | Iceland
  [10:4:0.3] Hotspots | Mantle plumes under moving plate | Hawaii, Galapagos, Reunion
[10:4:1] - Ring of Fire = earthquakes, volcanoes, Pacific Ocean (**not forest fires**); holds about **75% of active volcanoes** → most eruptions.
[10:4:2] - **Japan** = most earthquake-prone nation. **World's largest earthquake: M 9.5, Chile, 22 May 1960**.

## SUB 6 | Effects of Earthquakes
[10:6:0] TABLE head: Effect | Condition / region
  [10:6:0.0] Tsunami | Needs M > 6.5, focus shallower than ~70 km, vertical uplift of sea floor; over 80% from megathrust subduction quakes (Aug 2025)
  [10:6:0.1] Landslides | Fragile Himalaya and North-East
  [10:6:0.2] Liquefaction | Soft alluvial soils of the Indo-Gangetic Plain (Bihar, UP, WB)
  [10:6:0.3] Seismic seiches | Standing waves in rivers, lakes, reservoirs, ponds; reported in the Myanmar quake
  [10:6:0.4] Fires | Broken gas and power lines
[10:6:1] - About **95%** of quake deaths occur in **poorly built 1–3 storey houses**.
[10:6:2] - Tsunami Early Warning System built after the **2004 Sumatra** earthquake.
## SUB 7 | India's Tectonic Setting and Seismic Zones
[10:7:0] - India lies in the **Alpine-Himalayan belt**, which continues east through the North-East (Arakan Yoma) into **Myanmar**; **Sagaing Fault** = Indian–Eurasian boundary.
[10:7:1] - **Himalayan region** has the highest concentration of high zones (mostly **IV and V**) due to many **longitudinal thrust zones** (R explains A).
[10:7:2] - Peninsula (Latur, Koyna, Jabalpur): severe intraplate quakes possible. **Karnataka Plateau** is NOT a high-intensity zone.
[10:7:3] # Seismic Zones (IS 1893: 2016, in force)
[10:7:4] TABLE head: Zone | Risk | MSK intensity | Zone factor | Areas
  [10:7:4.0] V | Very high | IX+ | 0.36 | Entire North-East, J&K, Ladakh, Himachal, Uttarakhand, parts of north Bihar, Kutch, A&N
  [10:7:4.1] IV | High | VIII | 0.24 | New Delhi, rest of Bihar, parts of UP, Punjab, Himalayan foothills, Indo-Gangetic belt
  [10:7:4.2] III | Moderate | VII | 0.16 | Kerala, Goa, coastal areas, Vijayawada, Chennai, Amaravati region, much of coastal AP
  [10:7:4.3] II | Low (least active) | VI or less | 0.10 | Stable Peninsula: Visakhapatnam, Hyderabad
[10:7:5] - **56%** of India's area is in Zones III–V. **Delhi = Zone IV** (Earthquake Disaster Risk Index Report, 2019). **Bhuj is not in Zone IV** (it is V).
[10:7:6] - After **Latur 1993**, **Zone I merged into Zone II** → four zones (IS 1893: 2002).
[10:7:7] # Andhra Pradesh
[10:7:8] - AP lies in **Zones II and III**; **no area in Zone V** (Assam, Bihar, J&K have) (APPSC 2024).
[10:7:9] - **Visakhapatnam = Zone II** (BIS; May 2026); **Vijayawada = Zone III**.
[10:7:10] Visakhapatnam is Zone II, not V; New Delhi is Zone IV.

## SUB 9 | Volcanoes – Products and Types
[10:9:0] - Products: **pyroclastic debris**, ash, volcanic bombs, lava. Gases: mostly **water vapour**, then **CO₂, SO₂, H₂S**.
[10:9:1] TABLE head: Type | Features | Example
  [10:9:1.0] Shield | Fluid basalt, gentle slopes | Mauna Loa
  [10:9:1.1] Composite / strato | Viscous lava, steep, explosive; ~60% of Earth's volcanoes | Etna, Mayon, Fuego
  [10:9:1.2] Caldera | Collapse depression after explosive eruption | Mount Aso (Japan) – among world's largest; Taal
  [10:9:1.3] Complex | Several vents, overlapping cones, domes | Taal (Philippines), Dukono (Indonesia)
  [10:9:1.4] Strombolian | Frequent, mild bursts | Fuego (every 15–20 min)
  [10:9:1.5] Vulcanian | More explosive | –
[10:9:2] - Activity: **active**; **dormant** (quiet long, may erupt); **extinct** (no eruption expected).
## SUB 10 | Volcanic Landforms
[10:10:0] TABLE head: Intrusive form | Shape / position
  [10:10:0.0] Batholith | Huge, often dome-shaped granite mass at depth; mountain cores; exposed by denudation
  [10:10:0.1] Laccolith | Dome-shaped, flat base, fed by a pipe (Karnataka granite domes)
  [10:10:0.2] Lopolith | Saucer-shaped (concave upward)
  [10:10:0.3] Phacolith | Wavy mass at base of a syncline / top of an anticline

  [10:10:0.5] Dyke | Vertical wall cutting across beds; fed the Deccan Traps (western Maharashtra)
[10:10:1] - **Extrusive**: lava plateaus (Deccan), cinder and composite cones, craters, calderas, lava flows.
[10:10:2] - **Ladakh Magmatic Arc**: Trans-Himalaya, between **Indus and Shyok** suture zones.
[10:10:3] APPSC G1 2024: "phacoliths are large domed mounds" is WRONG – domes are laccoliths.
