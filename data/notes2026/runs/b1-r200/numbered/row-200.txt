## SUB 0 | Dadabhai Naoroji – the Grand Old Man of India
[0:0] # Life and firsts
[0:1] - **Title:** **Grand Old Man of India**; Parsi educator, trader and India's **first economic thinker**.
[0:2] - **Professor:** In **1854** the **first Indian to become a full professor** (mathematics and natural philosophy, **Elphinstone College**, Bombay); later Professor of **Gujarati at University College, London**.
[0:3] - **Women's education:** Through the **Students' Literary and Scientific Society (1848)** he **pioneered women's education** in Bombay; its vernacular branch, the **Gyan (Dnyan) Prasarak Mandali**, spread knowledge among adults (UPSC 2009).
[0:4] - **Diwan of Baroda:** 1874.

## SUB 1 | G.K. Gokhale and the Servants of India Society
[1:0] # Gokhale (1866-1915)
[1:1] - **Born** 1866 near **Ratnagiri**; graduate of Elphinstone College (**1884**, at 18); taught at **Fergusson College**, Poona.
[1:2] - **Career (UPSC 1997 biography):** associate editor of **Sudharak** (Agarkar's paper), **Secretary of the Poona Sarvajanik Sabha**, Secretary of the Congress, witness before the **Welby Commission**, provincial and imperial legislator, **INC President at 39**.
[1:3] - **Guru chain:** **M.G. Ranade → Gokhale → Gandhi**; Gandhi called Gokhale his **political guru** ("Gokhale – My Political Guru").
[1:4] - **Deccan Sabha (1896):** founded by **Gokhale** under Ranade when Tilak's group captured the Sarvajanik Sabha.
[1:5] - **President, Banaras Congress, 1905** (21st session) – condemned the **partition of Bengal** and Curzon's rule, backed **Swadeshi**.
[1:6] - **Compared Curzon's rule to that of Aurangzeb.**
[1:7] - **Refused a knighthood** (recommended by Hardinge) and **a seat in the Council of the Secretary of State for India** (UPSC 2008).
[1:8] - Told Gandhi to spend his first year in India "**with his ears open but his mouth shut**"; Gandhi returned in **1915** on his advice.

## SUB 2 | Economic Critique of Colonialism – Drain of Wealth
[2:0] # The critique
[2:1] - **Greatest contribution of the Moderates** – "the most important contribution to the development of the national movement"; it **broke the myth of British benevolence**.
[2:2] - **Drain theory:** **Dadabhai Naoroji** (*Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*) – he laid the **economic foundation of Indian nationalism**; the INC adopted the drain theory at **Calcutta, 1896**.
[2:3] - **Channels of the drain:** **Home Charges**, **salaries and pensions** of British officials, interest on public debt, profits and dividends, unequal trade → India's wealth transferred to **Britain**.
[2:4] # Home Charges (UPSC 2011)
[2:5] - Spent in England for India: **India Office in London**, **salaries, pensions and furlough pay of British civil and military personnel**, interest on India's debt.
[2:6] - **Wars outside India** were charged to Indian revenues but as military/"extraordinary" charges, **not Home Charges** → answer **I and II only**.
[2:7] # Economic critics and their books
[2:8] TABLE head: Book | Author
  [2:8.0] Poverty and Un-British Rule in India (1901) | Dadabhai Naoroji
  [2:8.1] The Economic History of India | R.C. Dutt (not R.P. Dutt)
  [2:8.2] Some Economic Aspects of British Rule in India | G. Subramania Iyer
  [2:8.3] Essays on Indian Economics | M.G. Ranade
  [2:8.4] Prosperous British India | William Digby
  [2:8.5] The Rise and Growth of Economic Nationalism in India (1966) | Bipan Chandra (historian)
[2:9] - **Economic critics of colonialism:** Naoroji, **G. Subramania Iyer**, **R.C. Dutt** – **all three** (UPSC 2015); also Ranade, Wacha, G.V. Joshi, Gokhale.
[2:10] - **R.C. Dutt:** colonial policies caused India's economic exploitation; INC president **Lucknow 1899**.
[2:11] - **Sir Syed Ahmed Khan did not accept** the drain theory – he saw British rule as modernising (UPSC 1996).

## SUB 3 | Moderate Phase – Leaders, Methods and Demands
[3:0] # Phase and leaders
[3:1] - **Moderate (liberal) phase = 1885-1905**; leaders were staunch believers in **liberalism**; base **urban**, English-educated.
[3:2] TABLE head: Leader | Known as / for
  [3:2.0] W.C. Bonnerjee | First INC President (Bombay 1885); London Indian Society with Naoroji (1865)
  [3:2.1] Surendranath Banerjee | Rashtraguru; Indian Association; edited The Bengalee; wrote *A Nation in Making*; presided 1895 and 1902
  [3:2.2] Pherozeshah Mehta | Lion of Bombay; Bombay Presidency Association (1885); INC president 1890
  [3:2.3] M.G. Ranade | Judge, economist, reformer; Poona Sarvajanik Sabha, Prarthana Samaj; *Rise of the Maratha Power*; Gokhale's guru
  [3:2.4] G. Subramania Iyer | Founded The Hindu and Swadesamitran; Madras Mahajana Sabha
  [3:2.5] R.C. Dutt | Economic historian; INC president Lucknow 1899
  [3:2.6] Dinshaw Wacha | INC president Calcutta 1901
  [3:2.7] Madan Mohan Malaviya | "No taxation without representation"
[3:3] - **Bombay Trimurti:** **Pherozeshah Mehta, K.T. Telang, Badruddin Tyabji** – founders of the Bombay Presidency Association (1885).
[3:4] - **Founders of the INC:** A.O. Hume is regarded as the founder; **Naoroji and Dinshaw Wacha** are counted among its founders too.
[3:5] # Newspapers and editors (match)
[3:6] TABLE head: Newspaper | Editor

  [3:6.1] The Bengalee | Surendranath Banerjee

  [3:6.3] Free Hindustan | Taraknath Das
[3:7] # Methods
[3:8] - **Constitutional agitation** within the law – **3 Ps: Prayers, Petitions, Protests**; resolutions, meetings, pamphlets.
[3:9] - Faith in **British justice and fair play**; British rule seen as "**providential**".
[3:10] - Two-pronged: build opinion in India and **persuade the British public**.
[3:11] - **Distrusted mass action** – no non-cooperation, passive resistance or civil disobedience.
[3:12] # Demands
[3:13] TABLE head: Area | Demands
  [3:13.0] Constitutional | Larger councils with elected members and control over the budget; self-government within the Empire
  [3:13.1] Administrative | Indianisation of the services; simultaneous ICS exams in India and England; higher ICS age limit; separation of the judiciary from the executive
  [3:13.2] Economic | Lower land revenue; abolition of the salt tax; cut in military expenditure; tariff protection for industry
  [3:13.3] Civil rights | Free speech, press, association; repeal of the Arms Act; protested Tilak's arrest and the deportation of the Natu brothers without trial
[3:14] - **NOT a Moderate demand:** **Purna Swaraj / complete independence** (NDA 2010).
[3:15] # Limitations
[3:16] - Annual session lasted **only about three days**; **no machinery between sessions**.
[3:17] - **Narrow social base** – urban, educated; most leaders came from **urban areas**.
[3:18] - **Partition of Bengal (1905)** exposed their "**naive faith**" in British justice.
[3:19] - Social reform: Moderates backed the **Age of Consent Bill**; **Tilak opposed** it.
[3:20] - Critics: Aurobindo – "begging institution"; Tilak – "Rights are not begged, they are claimed".

## SUB 4 | Achievements and the Official Attitude
[4:0] # Achievements
[4:1] - **Pan-Indian national identity**; **economic critique** of colonial rule; training in **democratic politics** and civil liberties – but **not complete independence**.
[4:2] - Supported the **democratic and scientific culture of modern Europe** and **strongly criticised the economic side** of imperialism – both true (RPSC 2024).
[4:3] # Indian Councils Act, 1892
[4:4] - Passed under Viceroy **Lansdowne**.
[4:5] - **More non-official members**, chosen on **recommendation** of municipal and district boards, universities, chambers of commerce – **indirect election** introduced.
[4:6] - Members could **discuss the budget but not vote on or amend it**; could **ask questions**.
[4:7] - INC found it **inadequate**: **no direct vote**, **official majority** kept.
[4:8] # Welby Commission (1895)
[4:9] - **Royal Commission on Indian Expenditure** (1895) – the commonly known name; **Naoroji** was its first Indian member, **Wedderburn** also sat on it and **Gokhale** gave evidence.
[4:10] # What the Viceroys said

  [4:11.0] Dufferin (1887-88) | Congress represents only a "microscopic minority" of the people
  [4:11.1] Curzon (letter, 1900) | "The Congress is tottering to its fall, and one of my great ambitions while in India is to assist it to a peaceful demise"
[4:12] - **Curzon:** **youngest Viceroy** (1899, aged 39).
