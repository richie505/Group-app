## SUB 0 | Mahaviharas – Founder and Location
[0:0] # The five great monasteries
[0:1] TABLE head: Mahavihara | Founder | Present location
  [0:1.0] Nalanda | Kumaragupta I (Gupta, 5th c. AD) | Nalanda, Bihar (ancient Magadha); Pala patronage, revived by Dharmapala
  [0:1.1] Odantapuri (Uddandapura, Udantpur) | Gopala | Bihar Sharif, Bihar; model for Tibetan monasteries
  [0:1.2] Vikramashila | Dharmapala | Antichak, Bhagalpur district, Bihar, on the Ganga
  [0:1.3] Somapura | Dharmapala | Paharpur, Naogaon, Bangladesh
  [0:1.4] Jagaddala | Ramapala | Varendra, Naogaon, Bangladesh
[0:2] - **Vikramashila = Dharmapala:** asked 10 times (UPPSC 2002, 2008, 2016; UPRO 2017, 2018; Uttarakhand 2024; APPSC G1) – not Gopala, Devapala, Mahipala, Ramapala, Kumaragupta I, Harsha or Lakshmanasena.
[0:3] - **Odantapuri = Gopala** (APPSC) – in **Bihar** (State PSC).
[0:4] - **Somapura = Dharmapala** (UPPSC 1999).
[0:5] - **Nalanda = Kumaragupta I, Gupta dynasty** (APPSC G1; State PSC) – not a Pala foundation; the **oldest** of these (RAS 1992).
[0:6]   - **⚠ Disputed:** "most ancient monastery" keys give **Nalanda**; strictly the Bhaja rock-cut vihara (2nd c. BCE) is older.
[0:7] - Together = **Panchamahavihara** (Tibetan sources).
[0:8] - **Vikramashila:** centre of **Tantric / Vajrayana** Buddhism and logic; ~3,000 scholars, six colleges, *dvarapandita* gatekeepers; trained monks for Tibet. **Jagaddala** translated books into **Tibetan**.
[0:9] - **Destruction:** by **Bakhtiyar Khilji**, c. **1193–1200** (1194 for Nalanda in one tradition); Jagaddala sacked in the early **13th** c.
[0:10] - **Nava Nalanda Mahavihara:** a **Pali research centre** founded by **Bhikshu Jagadish Kashyap**; foundation stone **20 November 1951** (State PSC).
[0:11] - **⚠ Trap:** Vikramashila is in **Bhagalpur** (not Banka).

## SUB 2 | Gopala and the Pala Rulers
[2:0] # Gopala – the founder
[2:1] - **Founder of the Pala dynasty:** **Gopala** (UPPSC 2015) – not Dharmapala, Devapala or Ramapala.
[2:2] - **Elected** by the chiefs (c. 750) to end **matsyanyaya** (anarchy, law of the jungle); born near **Pundravardhana** (per Taranatha).
[2:3] - **Source:** the **Khalimpur inscription of Dharmapala** records his election.
[2:4] - Replaced the later Guptas of Magadha and the **Khadgas** of **eastern Bengal**; built **Odantapuri**.
[2:5] - **Kings chosen by the people:** **Gopala** and **Nandivarman II Pallavamalla**.
[2:6] # Later Palas
[2:7] TABLE head: Ruler | Achievement
  [2:7.0] Dharmapala (770–810) | Founded Vikramashila and Somapura; installed Chakrayudha at Kannauj; defeated by Dhruva and by Nagabhata II near Monghyr
  [2:7.1] Devapala (810–850) | Granted five villages for Balaputradeva's Nalanda monastery (Nalanda copper plate); beat Amoghavarsha and Mihira Bhoja
  [2:7.2] Mahipala I | Revived Pala power; defeated by Rajendra Chola (c. 1023)
  [2:7.3] Ramapala (c. 1072–1126) | Last strong ruler; Kaivarta revolt; founded Jagaddala
[2:8] - Palas = **last great royal patrons of Buddhism** (Mahayana, Vajrayana) in India; took it to Tibet and SE Asia.
[2:9] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.141-144; app notes 1:69 (sub 0, 1)
## SUB 3 | Acharyas, Tibet and Southeast Asia
[3:0] # Acharyas
[3:1] TABLE head: Acharya | Institution | Note
  [3:1.0] Shilabhadra | Nalanda | Head in Harsha's time
  [3:1.1] Shantarakshita | Nalanda | Founded Samye monastery in Tibet
  [3:1.2] Atisha Dipankara Srijnana | Vikramashila | Went to Tibet in the 11th c., revived Buddhism (APPSC G1)
  [3:1.3] Sakya Sri Bhadra | Jagaddala | Took Tantric Buddhism to Tibet
[3:2] - **Kautilya** was NOT a mahavihara acharya; Nagarjuna, Vasubandhu are earlier; Pujyapada was a Jain grammarian.
[3:3] - **AP angle:** Andhra's **Dharmakirti** taught at Nalanda; studied under **Dharmapala of Kanchi**, head of Nalanda (not the Pala king).
[3:4] # Southeast Asia
[3:5] - **Balaputradeva:** **Shailendra** ruler of **Suvarnadvipa / Suvarnabhumi** (Sumatra–Java); built a monastery at Nalanda; his envoy obtained **five villages** from **Devapala** (BPSC 2018; State PSC) – not Dharmapala.
