[69:0:2] - **Vikramashila = Dharmapala:** asked 10 times (UPPSC 2002, 2008, 2016; UPRO 2017, 2018; Uttarakhand 2024; APPSC G1) – not Gopala, Devapala, Mahipala, Ramapala, Kumaragupta I, Harsha or Lakshmanasena.

[69:0:4] - **Somapura = Dharmapala** (UPPSC 1999).
[69:0:5] - **Nalanda = Kumaragupta I, Gupta dynasty** (APPSC G1; State PSC) – not a Pala foundation; the **oldest** of these (RAS 1992).
[69:0:6]   - **⚠ Disputed:** "most ancient monastery" keys give **Nalanda**; strictly the Bhaja rock-cut vihara (2nd c. BCE) is older.
[69:0:7] - Together = **Panchamahavihara** (Tibetan sources).
[69:0:8] - **Vikramashila:** centre of **Tantric / Vajrayana** Buddhism and logic; ~3,000 scholars, six colleges, *dvarapandita* gatekeepers; trained monks for Tibet. **Jagaddala** translated books into **Tibetan**.
[69:0:9] - **Destruction:** by **Bakhtiyar Khilji**, c. **1193–1200** (1194 for Nalanda in one tradition); Jagaddala sacked in the early **13th** c.

[69:0:11] - **⚠ Trap:** Vikramashila is in **Bhagalpur** (not Banka).

## SUB 4 | Gopala and the Pala Rulers
[69:4:0] # Gopala – the founder
[69:4:1] - **Founder of the Pala dynasty:** **Gopala** (UPPSC 2015) – not Dharmapala, Devapala or Ramapala.
[69:4:2] - **Elected** by the chiefs (c. 750) to end **matsyanyaya** (anarchy, law of the jungle); born near **Pundravardhana** (per Taranatha).
[69:4:3] - **Source:** the **Khalimpur inscription of Dharmapala** records his election.
[69:4:4] - Replaced the later Guptas of Magadha and the **Khadgas** of **eastern Bengal**; built **Odantapuri**.
[69:4:5] - **Kings chosen by the people:** **Gopala** and **Nandivarman II Pallavamalla**.
[69:4:6] # Later Palas
[69:4:7] TABLE head: Ruler | Achievement
  [69:4:7.0] Dharmapala (770–810) | Founded Vikramashila and Somapura; installed Chakrayudha at Kannauj; defeated by Dhruva and by Nagabhata II near Monghyr
  [69:4:7.1] Devapala (810–850) | Granted five villages for Balaputradeva's Nalanda monastery (Nalanda copper plate); beat Amoghavarsha and Mihira Bhoja
  [69:4:7.2] Mahipala I | Revived Pala power; defeated by Rajendra Chola (c. 1023)
  [69:4:7.3] Ramapala (c. 1072–1126) | Last strong ruler; Kaivarta revolt; founded Jagaddala
[69:4:8] - Palas = **last great royal patrons of Buddhism** (Mahayana, Vajrayana) in India; took it to Tibet and SE Asia.

=== NOTES 1:74 | Chalukya-harsha and Chalukya-pallava Conflicts; Chalukya Administration
## SUB 0 | Chalukya–Harsha Conflict – Battle of the Narmada
[74:0:0] - **Victor:** **Pulakesin II** (Badami Chalukya) stopped **Harsha's** southward march on the **Narmada** (UPSC 2003 ×2; UPPSC 2016) – not Pulakesin I, Vikramaditya I/II or Kirtivarman II.
[74:0:1] - **Title Paramesvara** after defeating Harsha = **Pulakesin II** (APPSC G1) – not Pulakesin I or the Pallava Mahendravarmans.
[74:0:2] - **Date:** **618 CE** (some books 630–634); before the **Aihole inscription** (634) recorded it.
[74:0:3] - **Sources:** **Aihole inscription + Hiuen Tsang** (NOT the Kailasanatha inscription).
[74:0:4] - **Titles:** Paramesvara and **Dakshinapatheshwara** ("lord of the south") vs Harsha's **Uttarapatheshwara**; Harsha accepted the Narmada as his southern border.
[74:0:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 3 p.21; NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.48, p.119, p.121; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.107; app notes 1:74 (sub 0)
## SUB 1 | Chalukya–Pallava Rivalry
[74:1:0] # Four rounds
[74:1:1] TABLE head: Round | Chalukya | Pallava | Result
  [74:1:1.0] 1 | Pulakesin II | Mahendravarman I | Advanced to Kanchi; won the Krishna–Godavari land (Maruturu, Kopparam)
  [74:1:1.1] 2 | Pulakesin II | Narasimhavarman I | Pallava victory; Vatapi taken (642), Pulakesin II killed
  [74:1:1.2] 3 | Vikramaditya I | Narasimhavarman I / Paramesvaravarman I | Vatapi recovered (c. 655)
  [74:1:1.3] 4 | Vikramaditya II | Nandivarman II | Kanchi taken three times, spared
[74:1:2] - Pallava occupation of Vatapi: **642 to c. 655** (about **13 years**).
[74:1:3] - **End:** Badami Chalukyas replaced by the **Rashtrakutas (Dantidurga)**, NOT the Cholas; Nandivarman II did not overthrow the Rashtrakutas.

  [74:1:5.2] Vatapi (642) | Narasimhavarman I; sacked by general Paranjoti

## SUB 2 | Women in Chalukya Administration
[74:2:0] - **Dynasty that often gave women high administrative posts = Chalukyas** (UPPSC 2007) – not Cholas, Palas or Senas.
[74:2:1] TABLE head: Woman | Role
  [74:2:1.0] Vijayabhattarika (Vijaya-mahadevi) | Queen of Chandraditya (son of Pulakesin II); two copper plates / land grants in her own name; poetess; administered the kingdom after his death
  [74:2:1.1] Kumkumdevi | Sister of Vikramaditya I; village granted to a Jain monastery
  [74:2:1.2] Lokamahadevi, Trailokyamahadevi | Queens of Vikramaditya II; Virupaksha and Mallikarjuna temples, Pattadakal

## SUB 3 | Chalukya Occupation of Kanchi
[74:3:0] - **Vikramaditya II** entered Kanchi **c. 740 CE** (**Nandivarman II's** reign); **no plunder**.
[74:3:1] - Gave gold to the **Rajasimheshvara (Kailasanatha)** temple; left a **Kannada inscription** on a pillar.
[74:3:2] - **Lokamahadevi's Virupaksha temple, Pattadakal** copies the **Kailasanatha** of Kanchi.
[74:3:3] - **Kanchi** – Pallava capital, temple city and seat of learning, the "**Kashi (Benares) of the South**".
[74:3:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.108, p.135; app notes 1:74 (sub 4)
## SUB 4 | Chalukya Administration and the Andhra Angle
[74:4:0] - Units: **rashtra** (province) → **vishaya** (district, under the **vishayapati**) → **grama** (village, under the gramakuta).
[74:4:1] - **Vatapi and Aihole** = urban and trade centres.
[74:4:2] - **Agraharas:** Brahmin land grants run by **mahajanas**.
[74:4:3] - Feudatories: **Rashtrakutas**, Alupas, Gangas, Sendrakas; **Renati Chodas** (Rayalaseema) – feudatories of the early Pallavas, then the **Badami Chalukyas**.
[74:4:4] - **AP angle:** the wars led to the rise in Rayalaseema of the **Renati Chodas, Banas, Vaidumbas**; the **Pitikayagulla** inscription (8th c., early Telugu script) shows **post-Badami Chalukya influence**.
[74:4:5] ~~ Sources: app notes 1:74 (sub 5)

[75:0:0] # Navabrahma temples – the most-asked fact of this row
[75:0:1] - **Where:** the **Navabrahma** temple complex is at **Alampur (Alampuram)** (G1 1983-84, 1987-88; G2 2008; APPSC PYQ ×3) – not Draksharama, Srisailam, Tirupati, Siddhavatam, Pushpagiri, Mahanandi, Badami or Aihole.

[75:0:5]   - **⚠ Settled:** an older APPSC key gave "Vesara"; the Alampur group is the classic case of **northern (Nagara) towers built by a southern dynasty**.
[75:0:6] - **District:** **Jogulamba Gadwal, Telangana** (earlier Mahabubnagar).
[75:0:7]   - ⚠ Since **2014** Alampur is in **Telangana**, not Andhra Pradesh (the 1983-84 stem says "Andhra Pradesh" – undivided state).
[75:0:8] - **Other names:** **Dakshina Kashi**; seat of the **Jogulamba Shakti Peetha**; the **western gateway of Srisailam**.

## SUB 1 | Temple Styles – Nagara, Dravida and Vesara
[75:1:0] # The three styles
[75:1:1] TABLE head: Style | Region | Defining feature
  [75:1:1.0] Nagara | North India (north of the Vindhyas) | Curvilinear shikhara
  [75:1:1.1] Dravida | South India (Krishna–Kaveri) | Pyramidal vimana, gopurams
  [75:1:1.2] Vesara | Deccan (Vindhyas–Krishna) | Hybrid of Nagara + Dravida; Dravida plan with Nagara details; the Chalukya style
[75:1:2] - **Nagara, Dravida, Vesara = the three main styles of Indian temple architecture** (UPSC 1995, 2012) – not racial groups, linguistic divisions or musical gharanas.
[75:1:3] - **Vesara origin:** developed by the **Badami Chalukyas** (Aihole, Pattadakal); continued by the Rashtrakutas and later Chalukyas.
[75:1:4] - **Place → dynasty:** Badami/Vatapi = Chalukyas; Kanchi = Pallavas; Tanjore = Cholas.
[75:1:5] # Statement facts (State PSC)
[75:1:6] - **Freestanding (mortar and stone) temples** began in the **Gupta** period ✓.
[75:1:7] - **Lad Khan (Aihole)** is one of the **earliest** temples, of the Chalukyas of Badami ✓.
[75:1:8] - **Khajuraho:** all compartments of the temple are connected internally and externally ✓.
[75:1:9] - **✗ Kailasanatha, Kanchi is NOT the earliest** freestanding Dravida temple – the **Shore Temple** (same Rajasimha phase) is counted among the earliest; so the correct set is **(i), (ii), (iii)**.
[75:1:10] # Mixed style
[75:1:11] - **Teli ka Mandir, Gwalior:** its **pinnacle (barrel-vaulted roof) is Dravida-like**, while the ornament is Nagara – the "Dravida pinnacle" answer (State PSC); Bhitargaon, Kandariya Mahadeva and Osian are Nagara.

## SUB 2 | Badami Caves and the Phases of Chalukya Art
[75:2:0] # Three phases
[75:2:1] - **Order:** **Badami** (rock-cut caves) → **Aihole** (experimental structural) → **Pattadakal** (mature) = birth of **Vesara**.
[75:2:2] - **Rocket sheet:** "**Badami = rock-cut caves**; Aihole = experimental/early temple architecture; Pattadakal = synthesis of Nagara + Dravida".
[75:2:3] - **Percy Brown:** the Chalukyas first **broke the monopoly of the rock-cut tradition** in the Deccan (by building structural temples).
[75:2:4] # Badami cave temples
[75:2:5] - **Badami is famous for its rock-cut cave temples** (G1 1990-91) – the caves were built under the **Chalukyas** (SI Mains 2019), not Rashtrakutas, Pallavas or Cholas.
[75:2:6]   - **⚠ Settled:** the printed key "structural temples" is wrong for Badami – structural temples are the hallmark of **Aihole and Pattadakal**; Badami's fame rests on its four caves.
[75:2:7] - **Four** main sandstone caves around **Agastya lake** (6th c.); earliest work under **Mangalesha**.

  [75:2:8.1] 2 | Vishnu (Varaha, Trivikrama)

  [75:2:8.3] 4 | Jain

## SUB 3 | Aihole and Pattadakal (UNESCO)
[75:3:0] # Aihole
[75:3:1] - **"Cradle of Indian temple architecture"** (**K.A. Nilakanta Sastri**) – experimental, early structural phase.
[75:3:2] - **Lad Khan:** **earliest massive bracket capital**; rectangular, **flat slab roof**, stone grilles, **no shikhara**; Shiva.
[75:3:3] - **Durga temple:** **apsidal (gajaprishtha)** plan with a pillared gallery round the shrine.
[75:3:4] - **Meguti** (Jain): Dravida; bears the **Aihole inscription** composed by **Ravikirti** (poet and composer, not architect).
[75:3:5] TABLE head: Temple | Site | Style
  [75:3:5.0] Lad Khan | Aihole | No shikhara
  [75:3:5.1] Huchimalligudi | Aihole | Nagara
  [75:3:5.2] Ravana Phadi | Aihole | Rock-cut cave, no tower
  [75:3:5.3] Virupaksha, Mallikarjuna, Sangameshvara | Pattadakal | Dravida
  [75:3:5.4] Papanatha, Kashi Vishwanatha, Galaganatha | Pattadakal | Nagara
[75:3:6] - **Nagara shikhara pair:** **Huchimalligudi (Aihole)** and **Dashavatara (Deogarh)**.
[75:3:7] # Pattadakal
[75:3:8] - **UNESCO World Heritage Site (1987)**; royal **coronation** site; **Nagara and Dravida temples side by side**.
[75:3:9] - **Virupaksha:** built by queen **Lokamahadevi** for **Vikramaditya II's victory over the Pallavas**; modelled on the Pallava **Kailasanatha (Kanchi)**; Mallikarjuna by **Trailokyamahadevi**.
[75:3:10] - Friezes from the **Ramayana, Mahabharata and Panchatantra**; architects (sutradharis) **Gunda Anivaritachari** and **Sarvasiddhi Acharya** named in Virupaksha inscriptions.
[75:3:11] - **Line of influence:** Kailasanatha (Kanchi) → Virupaksha (Pattadakal) → Kailasa (Ellora).
[75:3:12] # UNESCO link
[75:3:13] - **UNESCO World Heritage City:** **Jaipur** (Walled City, **2019**) – after **Ahmedabad** (2017), India's first heritage city (APPSC FSO).

## SUB 0 | Mahendravarman I (600–630) – Titles and the Mattavilasa Prahasana
[76:0:0] # Titles
[76:0:1] - **Mahendravarman I**, son of **Simhavishnu**: titles **Mattavilasa, Vichitrachitta, Gunabhara, Chitrakarapuli, Lalitankura** (UPSC 2025 ×2; APPSC G1 PYQ "Vichitrachitta") – not Simhavishnu, Narasimhavarman I or Simhavarman.
[76:0:2]   - **⚠ Trap:** **Vichitrachitta** is the father's title; **Narasimhavarman I** is **Mamalla / Vatapikonda**.
[76:0:3] - **Not his title:** **Vatapikonda** (Narasimhavarman I).
[76:0:4] # Mattavilasa Prahasana
[76:0:5] - **Author: Mahendravarman I** (G1 Prelims 2012; UP 2010) – not Ramanujacharya, Narasimhavarman, Dandin, Gautamiputra Satakarni, Rudradaman or Pulakesin II.
[76:0:6] - A **Sanskrit** one-act **farce (prahasana)** mocking **Kapalikas and Buddhist monks**; he also wrote **Bhagavadajjuka**.
[76:0:7] # Faith and building
[76:0:8] - Began as a **Jain** → turned **Shaiva** under **Appar (Tirunavukkarasar)**, not Sambandar.
[76:0:9] - **Rock-cut temples of South India begin with him** – **Mandagapattu** (no brick, timber, metal or mortar); **Kudumiamalai** music inscription credited to his patronage.
[76:0:10] - Wars with **Pulakesin II** began under him; he lost the Krishna-Godavari lands (Maruturu, Kopparam records).

## SUB 1 | Origin, Territory, Seal and the Andhra Links
[76:1:0] # Territory and emblem
[76:1:1] - **Tondaimandalam** = Pallava territory (APPSC 2023), capital **Kanchipuram** (between the Pennar and Vellar).
[76:1:2] - **Royal seal / crest: the bull (Nandi)** (G1 1983-84) – tiger = Chola, boar = Chalukya, fish = Pandya.
[76:1:3] - **Origin theories:** Parthian (Pahlava), **Andhra** (early Prakrit charters from the Krishna-Guntur region; replaced the Ikshvakus) and local Tamil – **not Scythian**; originally **feudatories of the Satavahanas**.

## SUB 3 | Kanchi as a Centre of Learning – Ghatikas, Dandin, Bharavi
[76:3:0] # Ghatika
[76:3:1] - **Ghatika = a Brahminical (Vedic) college / settlement of Brahmin scholars** (G1 2012; G1 PYQ), i.e. **Hindu colleges** (G1 Prelims 2008) – not merchants, Jain monks, artisans or Buddhist centres; usually **attached to temples**; **Sanskrit** medium.
[76:3:2] - Andhra link: **Vishnukundins** and **Eastern Chalukya Jayasimha I** (Asanapura ghatika) also set up ghatikas.
[76:3:3] - **Mayurasharman**, a Brahmin student at the **Kanchi Ghatika**, was insulted by a **Pallava horseman** → founded the **Kadamba** kingdom (**Talagunda inscription**).

[76:3:7] - **Hiuen Tsang** visited Kanchi **c. 640 CE**, under Narasimhavarman I.
[76:3:8] - **Grantha** script of the Pallavas = parent of Southeast Asian scripts; the Pallavas pioneered **Dravida** temple art.

## SUB 4 | Narasimhavarman I Mamalla (630–668)
[76:4:0] - **Titles:** **Mamalla / Mahamalla** ("great wrestler") and **Vatapikonda**; **founded the port city of Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram)** (Sachivalayam) – not Simhavishnu, Narasimhavarman II or Paramesvaravarman I.
[76:4:1] - **Wars:** killed **Pulakesin II** at **Manimangala (642)**; general **Paranjoti** sacked **Vatapi**.
[76:4:2] - **Navy:** **two naval expeditions to Ceylon** (c. 642 onwards) to restore his ally **Manavarma** (UP RO 2016) – not Rajaraja, Kirtivarman I or Jayasimha I.
[76:4:3] - **Art:** the **monolithic rathas** at Mahabalipuram; a firm Shaiva; Hiuen Tsang visited Kanchi in his reign.
