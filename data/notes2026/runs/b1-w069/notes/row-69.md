=== Mahaviharas – Founder and Location
# The five great monasteries
| Mahavihara | Founder | Present location |
|---|---|---|
| **Nalanda** | **Kumaragupta I** (Gupta, **5th c. AD**) | Nalanda, **Bihar** (ancient Magadha); Pala patronage, revived by Dharmapala |
| **Odantapuri** (Uddandapura, Udantpur) | **Gopala** | **Bihar Sharif**, Bihar; model for Tibetan monasteries |
| **Vikramashila** | **Dharmapala** | Antichak, **Bhagalpur** district, Bihar, on the Ganga |
| **Somapura** | **Dharmapala** | **Paharpur**, Naogaon, Bangladesh |
| **Jagaddala** | **Ramapala** | **Varendra**, Naogaon, Bangladesh |
- **Vikramashila = Dharmapala:** asked 10 times (UPPSC 2002, 2008, 2016; UPRO 2017, 2018; Uttarakhand 2024; APPSC G1) – not Gopala, Devapala, Mahipala, Ramapala, Kumaragupta I, Harsha or Lakshmanasena.
- **Odantapuri = Gopala** (APPSC) – in **Bihar** (State PSC).
- **Somapura = Dharmapala** (UPPSC 1999).
- **Nalanda = Kumaragupta I, Gupta dynasty** (APPSC G1; State PSC) – not a Pala foundation; the **oldest** of these (RAS 1992).
  - **⚠ Disputed:** "most ancient monastery" keys give **Nalanda**; strictly the Bhaja rock-cut vihara (2nd c. BCE) is older.
- Together = **Panchamahavihara** (Tibetan sources).
- **Vikramashila:** centre of **Tantric / Vajrayana** Buddhism and logic; ~3,000 scholars, six colleges, *dvarapandita* gatekeepers; trained monks for Tibet. **Jagaddala** translated books into **Tibetan**.
- **Destruction:** by **Bakhtiyar Khilji**, c. **1193–1200** (1194 for Nalanda in one tradition); Jagaddala sacked in the early **13th** c.
- **Nava Nalanda Mahavihara:** a **Pali research centre** founded by **Bhikshu Jagadish Kashyap**; foundation stone **20 November 1951** (State PSC).
- **⚠ Trap:** Vikramashila is in **Bhagalpur** (not Banka).
~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.60; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.141-144; app notes 1:69 (sub 2)

=== Odd One Out – Centres of Learning
- **Early medieval (Pala) centres:** Nalanda, Vikramashila, **Odantapuri (Uddandapur)**, Somapura.
- **Taxila = odd one out** (Uttarakhand 2016): early Buddhist centre, capital of **Gandhara** (Rawalpindi district, Pakistan); students – Chanakya, Chandragupta Maurya, **Charaka**, Jivaka.
- **Valabhi:** **Hinayana** centre of Harsha's time (Maitrakas, Gujarat) – not a Pala mahavihara.

=== Gopala and the Pala Rulers
# Gopala – the founder
- **Founder of the Pala dynasty:** **Gopala** (UPPSC 2015) – not Dharmapala, Devapala or Ramapala.
- **Elected** by the chiefs (c. 750) to end **matsyanyaya** (anarchy, law of the jungle); born near **Pundravardhana** (per Taranatha).
- **Source:** the **Khalimpur inscription of Dharmapala** records his election.
- Replaced the later Guptas of Magadha and the **Khadgas** of **eastern Bengal**; built **Odantapuri**.
- **Kings chosen by the people:** **Gopala** and **Nandivarman II Pallavamalla**.
# Later Palas
| Ruler | Achievement |
|---|---|
| **Dharmapala** (770–810) | Founded Vikramashila and Somapura; installed **Chakrayudha** at Kannauj; defeated by **Dhruva** and by **Nagabhata II** near **Monghyr** |
| **Devapala** (810–850) | Granted **five villages** for Balaputradeva's Nalanda monastery (**Nalanda copper plate**); beat Amoghavarsha and Mihira Bhoja |
| **Mahipala I** | Revived Pala power; defeated by **Rajendra Chola** (c. 1023) |
| **Ramapala** (c. 1072–1126) | Last strong ruler; **Kaivarta revolt**; founded Jagaddala |
- Palas = **last great royal patrons of Buddhism** (Mahayana, Vajrayana) in India; took it to Tibet and SE Asia.
~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.141-144; app notes 1:69 (sub 0, 1)

=== Acharyas, Tibet and Southeast Asia
# Acharyas
| Acharya | Institution | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Shilabhadra** | Nalanda | Head in Harsha's time |
| **Shantarakshita** | Nalanda | Founded **Samye** monastery in Tibet |
| **Atisha Dipankara Srijnana** | **Vikramashila** | Went to **Tibet in the 11th c.**, revived Buddhism (APPSC G1) |
| **Sakya Sri Bhadra** | **Jagaddala** | Took Tantric Buddhism to Tibet |
- **Kautilya** was NOT a mahavihara acharya; Nagarjuna, Vasubandhu are earlier; Pujyapada was a Jain grammarian.
- **AP angle:** Andhra's **Dharmakirti** taught at Nalanda; studied under **Dharmapala of Kanchi**, head of Nalanda (not the Pala king).
# Southeast Asia
- **Balaputradeva:** **Shailendra** ruler of **Suvarnadvipa / Suvarnabhumi** (Sumatra–Java); built a monastery at Nalanda; his envoy obtained **five villages** from **Devapala** (BPSC 2018; State PSC) – not Dharmapala.
~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.143-145; app notes 1:69 (sub 4, 5)

=== Sena Successors and the Lakshmana Era
| Ruler | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Samantasena** | Founder; from Karnataka; Pala feudatory in Rarh |
| **Vijayasena** | Founded independent Sena rule; expelled the last Pala Madanapala |
| **Ballalasena** | Scholar king (**Danasagara, Adbhutasagara**) |
| **Lakshmanasena** | Last great Sena; court poet **Jayadeva**; ousted by **Bakhtiyar Khilji** (Nadia) |
- **New Samvat:** **Lakshmana Samvat** (Lakshmana era, from c. **1119** AD) = **Lakshmanasena**, **Sena** dynasty (UPPSC 1999, 2005) – not Dharmapala, Devapala or the Palas.
~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 3 p.144; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmana_Sena; app notes 1:69 (sub 7)
