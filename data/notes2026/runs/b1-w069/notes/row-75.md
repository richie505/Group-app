=== Alampur Navabrahma Temples – the Andhra Angle of Badami Chalukya Art
# Navabrahma temples – the most-asked fact of this row
- **Where:** the **Navabrahma** temple complex is at **Alampur (Alampuram)** (G1 1983-84, 1987-88; G2 2008; APPSC PYQ ×3) – not Draksharama, Srisailam, Tirupati, Siddhavatam, Pushpagiri, Mahanandi, Badami or Aihole.
- **Builders:** the **Badami Chalukyas** (7th–8th c.); Alampur is "the place in Andhra containing fine Badami Chalukyan temples" (G1 1983-84) – not Tadipatri, Ghantasala or Billasurgam.
- **Number:** **nine** early Chalukya **Shiva** temples, on the **Tungabhadra** (near its confluence with the Krishna).
- **Style:** **Nagara** – a northern **rekha-prasada (curvilinear) shikhara** (APPSC JA, NTR Health University) – not Vesara, Hoysala or "Ikshvaku".
  - **⚠ Settled:** an older APPSC key gave "Vesara"; the Alampur group is the classic case of **northern (Nagara) towers built by a southern dynasty**.
- **District:** **Jogulamba Gadwal, Telangana** (earlier Mahabubnagar).
  - ⚠ Since **2014** Alampur is in **Telangana**, not Andhra Pradesh (the 1983-84 stem says "Andhra Pradesh" – undivided state).
- **Other names:** **Dakshina Kashi**; seat of the **Jogulamba Shakti Peetha**; the **western gateway of Srisailam**.
# Other Andhra temple facts linked here
- **Eastern Chalukyas** carried the Chalukya (Vesara) idiom to **Bikkavolu** and **Draksharama**; NextGen: Vesara "belongs to the Chalukyas of Badami, introduced in Andhra by the Vengi Chalukyas".
- **Oldest Saivite temple:** **Parasurameswara, Gudimallam**; temple building in Andhra began under the **Ikshvakus**.
> Exam angle: "Where are the Navabrahma temples?" has been asked at least seven times – answer **Alampur**, built by the **Badami Chalukyas**, style **Nagara**.

=== Temple Styles – Nagara, Dravida and Vesara
# The three styles
| Style | Region | Defining feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Nagara** | North India (north of the Vindhyas) | **Curvilinear shikhara** |
| **Dravida** | South India (Krishna–Kaveri) | **Pyramidal vimana**, gopurams |
| **Vesara** | **Deccan** (Vindhyas–Krishna) | **Hybrid of Nagara + Dravida**; Dravida plan with Nagara details; the Chalukya style |
- **Nagara, Dravida, Vesara = the three main styles of Indian temple architecture** (UPSC 1995, 2012) – not racial groups, linguistic divisions or musical gharanas.
- **Vesara origin:** developed by the **Badami Chalukyas** (Aihole, Pattadakal); continued by the Rashtrakutas and later Chalukyas.
- **Place → dynasty:** Badami/Vatapi = Chalukyas; Kanchi = Pallavas; Tanjore = Cholas.
# Statement facts (State PSC)
- **Freestanding (mortar and stone) temples** began in the **Gupta** period ✓.
- **Lad Khan (Aihole)** is one of the **earliest** temples, of the Chalukyas of Badami ✓.
- **Khajuraho:** all compartments of the temple are connected internally and externally ✓.
- **✗ Kailasanatha, Kanchi is NOT the earliest** freestanding Dravida temple – the **Shore Temple** (same Rajasimha phase) is counted among the earliest; so the correct set is **(i), (ii), (iii)**.
# Mixed style
- **Teli ka Mandir, Gwalior:** its **pinnacle (barrel-vaulted roof) is Dravida-like**, while the ornament is Nagara – the "Dravida pinnacle" answer (State PSC); Bhitargaon, Kandariya Mahadeva and Osian are Nagara.

=== Badami Caves and the Phases of Chalukya Art
# Three phases
- **Order:** **Badami** (rock-cut caves) → **Aihole** (experimental structural) → **Pattadakal** (mature) = birth of **Vesara**.
- **Rocket sheet:** "**Badami = rock-cut caves**; Aihole = experimental/early temple architecture; Pattadakal = synthesis of Nagara + Dravida".
- **Percy Brown:** the Chalukyas first **broke the monopoly of the rock-cut tradition** in the Deccan (by building structural temples).
# Badami cave temples
- **Badami is famous for its rock-cut cave temples** (G1 1990-91) – the caves were built under the **Chalukyas** (SI Mains 2019), not Rashtrakutas, Pallavas or Cholas.
  - **⚠ Settled:** the printed key "structural temples" is wrong for Badami – structural temples are the hallmark of **Aihole and Pattadakal**; Badami's fame rests on its four caves.
- **Four** main sandstone caves around **Agastya lake** (6th c.); earliest work under **Mangalesha**.
| Cave | Deity / fact |
|---|---|
| 1 | Shiva – **18-armed Nataraja** |
| 2 | Vishnu (Varaha, Trivikrama) |
| 3 | Vishnu; **largest**; **Mangalesha's inscription, 578 CE** |
| 4 | **Jain** |
- Badami also has the structural **Malegitti Shivalaya** (Dravida).

=== Aihole and Pattadakal (UNESCO)
# Aihole
- **"Cradle of Indian temple architecture"** (**K.A. Nilakanta Sastri**) – experimental, early structural phase.
- **Lad Khan:** **earliest massive bracket capital**; rectangular, **flat slab roof**, stone grilles, **no shikhara**; Shiva.
- **Durga temple:** **apsidal (gajaprishtha)** plan with a pillared gallery round the shrine.
- **Meguti** (Jain): Dravida; bears the **Aihole inscription** composed by **Ravikirti** (poet and composer, not architect).
| Temple | Site | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Lad Khan | Aihole | No shikhara |
| Huchimalligudi | Aihole | Nagara |
| Ravana Phadi | Aihole | Rock-cut cave, no tower |
| Virupaksha, Mallikarjuna, Sangameshvara | Pattadakal | **Dravida** |
| **Papanatha**, **Kashi Vishwanatha**, Galaganatha | Pattadakal | **Nagara** |
- **Nagara shikhara pair:** **Huchimalligudi (Aihole)** and **Dashavatara (Deogarh)**.
# Pattadakal
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site (1987)**; royal **coronation** site; **Nagara and Dravida temples side by side**.
- **Virupaksha:** built by queen **Lokamahadevi** for **Vikramaditya II's victory over the Pallavas**; modelled on the Pallava **Kailasanatha (Kanchi)**; Mallikarjuna by **Trailokyamahadevi**.
- Friezes from the **Ramayana, Mahabharata and Panchatantra**; architects (sutradharis) **Gunda Anivaritachari** and **Sarvasiddhi Acharya** named in Virupaksha inscriptions.
- **Line of influence:** Kailasanatha (Kanchi) → Virupaksha (Pattadakal) → Kailasa (Ellora).
# UNESCO link
- **UNESCO World Heritage City:** **Jaipur** (Walled City, **2019**) – after **Ahmedabad** (2017), India's first heritage city (APPSC FSO).
