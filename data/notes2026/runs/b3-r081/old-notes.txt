=== NOTES 3:81 | Money Supply – M0 to M4, High-powered Money, Money Multiplier, Velocity
## SUB 0 | Functions and Kinds of Money
[81:0:0] # Functions of Money
[81:0:1] TABLE head: Class | Functions
  [81:0:1.0] Primary | Medium of exchange; measure of value (unit of account)
  [81:0:1.1] Secondary | Store of value; standard of deferred payments; transfer of value
  [81:0:1.2] Contingent | Basis of credit; distribution of national income; giving liquidity
[81:0:2] APPSC 2025: **Production of goods is NOT a function of money**.
[81:0:3] # Kinds of Money
[81:0:4] TABLE head: Pair | Difference
  [81:0:4.0] Hard vs soft money | Hard = backed by gold/credible assets; soft = paper backed by government bonds
  [81:0:4.1] Bank money vs cash | Bank money = secondary money; cash = high-powered money
  [81:0:4.2] Legal tender vs near money | Legal tender must be accepted; near money is liquid but cannot directly pay for goods
[81:0:5] - **RBI notes = unlimited legal tender**; **coins = limited legal tender**.
[81:0:6] - **Digital Rupee** = legal tender under the RBI Act (amended by **Finance Act 2022**).
[81:0:7] - **Plastic money** (credit, debit, prepaid cash, smart and store cards; mainly issued by banks) = used in place of bank notes, but **NOT legal tender**.
[81:0:8] - Not every highly liquid asset is legal tender.
## SUB 1 | Money Terms
[81:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [81:1:0.0] Seigniorage | Face value minus cost of production of currency = issuer's profit; a ₹500 note costs about ₹4; a source of RBI surplus
  [81:1:0.1] Legal tender | Money a creditor is legally bound to accept in settlement of claims (UPSC 2018)
  [81:1:0.2] Fiat money | Notes and coins with no intrinsic value; money by government order
  [81:1:0.3] Near money | Time deposits, T-bills, bills of exchange, bonds near redemption, savings certificates
  [81:1:0.4] Token coin | Face value higher than metal value (rupee coin)
  [81:1:0.5] Velocity | Average number of times a unit of currency changes hands in a period; ₹10 note used twice = ₹20 of transactions
  [81:1:0.6] Required Reserve Ratio | Fixed percentage of deposits every bank must keep as reserves
[81:1:1] - **Currency expansion** → value of money falls → higher prices.
[81:1:2] - A rise in general prices can come from more money supply, lower output and higher effective demand (all three).
[81:1:3] - **Money supply is NOT included in a country's wealth**.

## SUB 4 | High-powered Money (M0)
[81:4:0] - Names: **reserve money, monetary base, base money, central bank money**, high-powered money = **M0** (⚠ APPSC key (2010) gave M2).
[81:4:1] - **Basis of credit creation**; most important money indicator for RBI.
[81:4:2] APPSC Group 2, 2025: **H = currency held by the public + cash reserves of commercial banks (with themselves and with RBI) + other deposits with RBI**. H does **not** include time deposits.
[81:4:3] TABLE head: Side | Components
  [81:4:3.0] Uses side | Currency with public + banks' cash in hand + banks' deposits with RBI + other deposits with RBI
  [81:4:3.1] Sources side | Net RBI credit to Government + RBI credit to banks and commercial sector + net forex assets of RBI + Government's currency liabilities − net non-monetary liabilities of RBI
[81:4:4] - **Currency in circulation** = notes + rupee coins + small coins (held by public + banks' cash in hand).
[81:4:5] - **"Other" deposits with RBI**: quasi-government and financial institutions (incl. primary dealers), foreign central banks and governments, international agencies such as the **IMF**.
## SUB 5 | Monetary Aggregates M1 to M4
[81:5:0] # Second Working Group on Money Supply (1977)
[81:5:1] TABLE head: Aggregate | Components | Type
  [81:5:1.0] M0 | Currency in circulation + bankers' deposits with RBI + other deposits with RBI | Reserve money
  [81:5:1.1] M1 | Currency with public + demand deposits + other deposits with RBI | Narrow, most liquid
  [81:5:1.2] M2 | M1 + post office savings bank deposits | Narrow
  [81:5:1.3] M3 | M1 + time deposits with banks | Broad money ("aggregate monetary resources")
  [81:5:1.4] M4 | M3 + all post office deposits (excluding NSCs) | Broad, least liquid
[81:5:2] - **M3** = most commonly used broad money; = currency with public + demand deposits + time deposits + other deposits with RBI (all four).
[81:5:3] - **M3 − M1 = time deposits** with banks. Time deposits are **not in M1**.
[81:5:4] - **Liquidity order**: **M1 > M2 > M3 > M4**.
[81:5:5] - Assets by liquidity: **currency > demand deposits > savings deposits > time deposits**; demand deposits can be withdrawn instantly, time deposits are locked.
[81:5:6] - **Time deposits** = most important component of commercial banks' liabilities; then savings and demand deposits.
[81:5:7] - Sources of broad money: net bank credit to Government + bank credit to commercial sector + net forex assets of banking sector + Government's currency liabilities − net non-monetary liabilities.
[81:5:8] **M2 = M1 + PO savings**; **M3 = M1 (not M2) + time deposits**.
[81:5:9] # New Aggregates (1998)
[81:5:10] - **Third Working Group** (1998), chaired by **Y. V. Reddy** (Money Supply: Analytics and Methodology of Compilation).
[81:5:11] - **NM0–NM3**: residency-based; separate short-term from long-term deposits.
[81:5:12] - **L1, L2, L3** = **liquidity aggregates**: add post-office deposits, term money and CDs of FIs, NBFC public deposits (**not RBI gold reserves**).
[81:5:13] - **M1–M4 framework (1977):** RBI introduced the money supply measures M1, M2, M3 and M4 on the recommendation of the **Second Working Group on Money Supply** (1977).
## SUB 6 | Money Multiplier and Credit Creation
[81:6:0] # Formula and Process
[81:6:1] - 1. RBI creates reserve money (issues notes).
[81:6:2] - 2. Banks keep CRR (with RBI) and SLR (with themselves).
[81:6:3] - 3. They lend the rest; each loan creates a new deposit.
[81:6:4] - **m = (1 + cdr) / (cdr + rdr)**; cdr = currency-deposit ratio, rdr = reserve-deposit ratio.
[81:6:5] - **Money multiplier = M3 ÷ M0** (total money supply / base money).
[81:6:6] - 10% reserves, no leakages: m = 1/0.10 = **10**; ₹100 supports about **₹1,000** of deposits.
[81:6:7] TABLE head: Money creator | Creates
  [81:6:7.0] RBI | Reserve money (notes)
  [81:6:7.1] Government | Coins and ₹1 note
  [81:6:7.2] Commercial banks | Deposit money by lending beyond CRR/SLR
[81:6:8] # What Moves Money Supply
[81:6:9] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [81:6:9.0] Higher CRR, SLR or cdr | Lower multiplier; less credit creation
  [81:6:9.1] Stronger banking habit | Higher multiplier (UPSC 2019)
  [81:6:9.2] RBI buys G-secs (OMO purchase); Government borrows from RBI; deficit financing | Money supply rises (injects reserve money)
  [81:6:9.3] RBI sells G-secs (UPSC 2012) | Money supply falls
  [81:6:9.4] Public deposits currency in banks | Does not raise money supply
  [81:6:9.5] Cash withdrawal ₹1 lakh from demand deposit | Aggregate money supply unchanged (demand deposits −₹1 lakh, currency with public +₹1 lakh)
[81:6:10] - **Raising SLR** = contractionary tool used in inflation; locks bank funds, reduces loan funds. An SLR cut frees funds.
[81:6:11] - **CRR increase** → banks have less money to lend (more parked with RBI).
[81:6:12] - High cash preference (high cdr) limits India's credit multiplier.
