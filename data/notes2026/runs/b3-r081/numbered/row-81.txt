## SUB 0 | Money – Functions, Kinds and Key Terms
[0:0] TABLE head: Class | Functions of money
  [0:0.0] Primary | Medium of exchange; measure of value (unit of account)
  [0:0.1] Secondary | Store of value; standard of deferred payments; transfer of value
  [0:0.2] Contingent | Basis of credit; distribution of national income; giving liquidity
[0:1] - **Not a function:** **production of goods** (APPSC).
[0:2] # Kinds of money
[0:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:3.0] Fiat money | Notes and coins with no intrinsic value; money by government order; also legal tender
  [0:3.1] Legal tender | Money a creditor is under compulsion to accept in settlement of claims (UPSC 2018); RBI notes unlimited, coins limited legal tender
  [0:3.2] Commodity money | Money with intrinsic value (gold, silver); a paper note is not commodity money
  [0:3.3] Near money | Liquid assets that cannot directly pay: bills of exchange, time deposits, T-bills, bonds near redemption, savings certificates
  [0:3.4] Plastic money | Credit cards (and debit/prepaid cards), mainly issued by banks; used in place of cash but not legal tender
  [0:3.5] Cheque | Bank (credit) money – an alternative to cash; can be refused, so not legal tender
  [0:3.6] Hard / soft money | Hard = backed by gold or credible assets; soft = paper backed by government bonds
  [0:3.7] Bank money / cash | Bank money = secondary money; cash = high-powered money
  [0:3.8] Token coin | Face value higher than metal value (rupee coin)
[0:4] - **Digital Rupee** = legal tender under the RBI Act, amended by the **Finance Act 2022**; not every highly liquid asset is legal tender.
[0:5] # Key terms
[0:6] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:6.0] Seigniorage | Face value minus cost of producing currency = issuer's (central bank's) profit from issuing notes; a ₹500 note costs about ₹4 – not a tax
  [0:6.1] Gresham's Law | Bad money drives good money out: good (full-value) coins are hoarded, melted and exported; applies to metallic coins
  [0:6.2] Velocity | Average number of times a unit of money changes hands in a period; ₹10 used twice = ₹20 of transactions
  [0:6.3] Required Reserve Ratio | Fixed percentage of deposits every bank must keep as reserves
[0:7] - **Money supply** = **currency + deposit money** (deposits of the public with banks).
[0:8] - **Small coins** are phased out when their **production cost exceeds face value**.
[0:9] - **Currency expansion** → value of money falls → **higher prices**.
[0:10] - **Money supply is NOT part of a country's wealth** (mines, dams, livestock are).
[0:11] > Traps: plastic money = credit card, not a coated note; seigniorage = profit, not a tax; Gresham = good money hoarded, melted AND exported.

## SUB 1 | Demonetisation and Currency Issue
[1:0] TABLE head: Year | Notes withdrawn | How
  [1:0.0] January 1946 | ₹1,000 and ₹10,000 | By ordinance
  [1:0.1] 16 January 1978 | ₹1,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000 | High Denomination Bank Notes (Demonetisation) Act, 1978 – PM Morarji Desai
  [1:0.2] 8 November 2016 | ₹500 and ₹1,000 (Mahatma Gandhi series) | Announced by the PM on the night of 8 Nov, effective midnight; new ₹500 and ₹2,000 issued
[1:1] - **Legal basis:** **Section 26(2), RBI Act 1934** (withdrawal of legal tender), on the recommendation of RBI's **Central Board** – not PMLA or the Banking Regulation Act.
[1:2] - **Aims (Economic Survey 2016-17):** curb **corruption**, **counterfeiting**, use of high-denomination notes for **terrorist activities**, and accumulation of **black money**. Digital payments was a later, added aim.
[1:3] - **Size:** SBNs were nearly **86%** of the value of notes in circulation on 8 Nov 2016 (RBI Annual Report 2016-17).
[1:4] - **Returned:** RBI AR 2016-17 – ₹15.28 of ₹15.44 lakh crore = **98.96%** (to 30 June 2017); final, RBI AR 2017-18 – ₹15.31 of ₹15.42 lakh crore = **99.3%**.
[1:5] - **₹2,000 notes:** **50.2%** of the value of banknotes in circulation at end-March 2017 (RBI AR 2016-17); withdrawn **May 2023**, still legal tender.
[1:6] - **Specified Bank Notes (Cessation of Liabilities) Act, 2017:** after the grace period a person may hold **not more than 10 notes** (any denomination), or 25 for study/research/numismatics; fine up to ₹10,000 or five times the face value.
[1:7] - **Paradox noticed:** the cash crunch cut spending – the **paradox of thrift** (APPSC G2 Mains 2019).
[1:8] - SC upheld the 2016 move **4:1** (2023). **FICN**: NCRB Crime in India 2024 → **₹54.61 crore** fake currency seized.
[1:9] - **Abroad – Venezuela (Dec 2016):** Maduro withdrew the **100-bolivar** note, then **backtracked** after protests and cash shortages.

  [1:11.1] Other notes | RBI (Sec 22, RBI Act)

## SUB 2 | Monetary Aggregates M1 to M4 and Liquidity Aggregates
[2:0] TABLE head: Aggregate | Components | Type
  [2:0.0] M1 | Currency with public + demand deposits with banks + other deposits with RBI (NCERT: CU + DD) | Narrow money, most liquid
  [2:0.1] M2 | M1 + post office savings bank deposits | Narrow
  [2:0.2] M3 | M1 + time deposits with banks | Broad money ("aggregate monetary resources")
  [2:0.3] M4 | M3 + all post office deposits (excluding NSCs) | Broad, least liquid
[2:1] - **Second Working Group on Money Supply (1977)** → RBI publishes M1–M4 besides Reserve Money.
[2:2] - **M3** = most commonly used broad money = **currency with public + demand deposits + time deposits + other deposits with RBI** (all four).
[2:3] - **M3 − M1 = time deposits**; time deposits are **not in M1**; M3 = M1 (not M2) + time deposits.
[2:4] - **Liquidity order:** M1 > M2 > M3 > M4; assets: **currency > demand deposits > savings deposits > time deposits** (4-1-3-2).
[2:5] - **Highly liquid asset** = converted into cash in a very short time with little loss of value.
[2:6] - **Banks' liabilities:** deposits are the main liability; **time deposits** = the largest component (over 85% of aggregate deposits).
[2:7] # New aggregates (1998)
[2:8] - **Third Working Group** (1998), chaired by **Y. V. Reddy**: residency-based **NM0–NM3** (separate short-term and long-term deposits).
[2:9] - **L1, L2, L3** = **liquidity aggregates**: add post-office deposits, term money and CDs of FIs, NBFC public deposits.
[2:10] > APPSC repeats: L1-L3 = liquidity aggregates; M1 = narrow money; M3 components; liquidity order.

## SUB 3 | Money Multiplier and Credit Creation
[3:0] - **Money multiplier = money supply ÷ reserve money (M3 ÷ M0)**; m = (1 + cdr) / (cdr + rdr).
[3:1] - 10% reserves, no leakages: m = 1/0.10 = **10**; ₹100 supports about **₹1,000** of deposits.
[3:2] - **Credit creation:** banks lend beyond their cash; **loans exceeding deposits = credit creation**; capacity depends on **cash reserves** (strictly, a bank lends out its **excess reserves**).
[3:3] - **Interbank transfer** to a bank brings it **reserves**, raising its lending potential.
[3:4] TABLE head: Money creator | Creates
  [3:4.0] RBI | Reserve money (notes)
  [3:4.1] Government | Coins and ₹1 note
  [3:4.2] Commercial banks | Deposit money by lending beyond CRR/SLR
[3:5] # What moves the multiplier and money supply
[3:6] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [3:6.0] Higher CRR, SLR or cash preference (cdr) | Lower multiplier, less credit creation
  [3:6.1] Stronger banking habit | Higher multiplier (UPSC 2019, 2021)
  [3:6.2] RBI buys G-secs from the public; Government borrows from RBI | Money supply rises (UPSC 2012: 1 and 3)
  [3:6.3] RBI sells G-secs; public deposits currency in banks | Falls / no rise
  [3:6.4] Cash withdrawal ₹1 lakh from a demand deposit | Unchanged (DD −₹1 lakh, currency +₹1 lakh) (UPSC 2020)
[3:7] - **Raising SLR** locks more funds in liquid assets (kept by the bank itself; CRR is kept with RBI) → fewer loanable funds; an SLR cut frees funds.
[3:8] - **CRR increase** → banks have less to lend.

## SUB 6 | High-powered Money (Reserve Money, M0)
[6:0] - Names: **reserve money, monetary base, base money, central bank money** = **M0** – the "most important indicator"; **basis of credit creation**.
[6:1] - **H = currency held by the public + cash reserves of banks (with themselves and with RBI) + other deposits with RBI**; no time deposits, no post-office deposits, no forex held by persons.
[6:2] TABLE head: Side | Components
  [6:2.0] Uses side | Currency with public + banks' cash in hand + bankers' deposits with RBI (legal reserves) + other deposits with RBI
  [6:2.1] Sources side | Net RBI credit to Government + RBI credit to banks and commercial sector + net forex assets of RBI + Government's currency liabilities − net non-monetary liabilities of RBI
[6:3] - **Currency in circulation** = notes + rupee coins + small coins (held by public + banks' cash in hand).
[6:4] - **"Other" deposits with RBI**: quasi-government and financial institutions, foreign central banks and governments, international agencies such as the **IMF**.
[6:5] > High-powered money = M0, not M2 (an old APPSC key gave M2).
