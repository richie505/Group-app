=== Jnanpith Award
# 60th Jnanpith (2025) – R. Vairamuthu
- **R. Vairamuthu** – Tamil poet, lyricist and writer; announced **March 2026**; **3rd Tamil** winner; first honoured mainly for **poetry**; not posthumous.
- Other honours: **National Film Award for Best Lyricist seven times**; **Padma Shri 2003**; **Padma Bhushan 2014**.
# About the award
- Established **1961**; first given **1965** to **G. Sankara Kurup (Malayalam)**.
- Given by **Bharatiya Jnanpith**, for Indian citizens writing in **Eighth Schedule languages and English**.
- Award: **₹11 lakh**, citation and **bronze statue of Vagdevi (Saraswati)**.
- **Prof. Amartya Sen** is **not** a Jnanpith recipient (the award is for Indian literature).

=== Padma Awards – Rules, Order and Past Lists
- Instituted **1954**; announced on **Republic Day** (eve, 25 January) through a **Ministry of Home Affairs notification**.
- Conferred by the **President** at **Rashtrapati Bhavan** (around March–April).
- Annual limit **120** (excluding **posthumous** and **foreigners**).
- **Posthumous** award normally not given – allowed only if **death within one year**.
- **No cash prize**.
| Award | Service recognised | Rank among civilian awards |
|---|---|---|
| Padma Vibhushan | Exceptional and distinguished service | 2nd (after Bharat Ratna) |
| Padma Bhushan | Distinguished service of high order | 3rd |
| Padma Shri | Distinguished service in any field | 4th |
- **Fields:** Arts, Social Work, Public Affairs, Science and Engineering, Trade and Industry, Medicine, Literature and Education, Civil Services, Sports, Others.
- Earlier Padma facts: **2004** Padma awards – **Narlikar** (science), **Amrita Pritam** (literature), **Venkatachalaiah** (law/judiciary); odd one out **M.V. Kamath**. **Chandra Kochhar** (CEO & MD, **ICICI Bank**) – **Padma Bhushan 2011**, trade and industry.
- **Padma Shri 2024 (Arts):** **Dasari Kondappa** (Telangana, **Burra Veena**), **Drona Bhuyan**, **Ghulam Nabi Dar**.
- **Padma 2017:** **Princess Maha Chakri Sirindhorn** (Thailand) – **Padma Bhushan**; **Ganjam Venkatasubbiah** – Padma Shri, **Kannada** lexicographer (not Telugu); **seven** Padma Shri in sports.
- **Padma 2011:** **Montek Singh Ahluwalia** (Planning Commission Deputy Chairman) – **Padma Vibhushan**.
- **Eligibility:** open to **all persons** without distinction of race, occupation, position or sex; **government servants** (incl. PSU staff) are **not eligible**, **except doctors and scientists**; **NRIs, OCIs and foreigners are eligible** (foreigners counted outside the 120 limit; 6 in 2026).
- **Padma 2023:** **106** awards (6 PV, 9 PB, 91 PS); **S.R. Srinivasa Varadhan** – **Padma Vibhushan 2023** (Science and Engineering); **8 Padma Shri** in **Science and Engineering**.
- **Padma 2017:** **Cho Ramaswamy** – **Padma Bhushan (posthumous)**; **Girish Bharadwaj** ('Setu Bandhu') – Padma Shri for **low-cost steel bridges** in rural areas.
- **Sports Padma:** **Devendra Jhajharia** – Padma Shri 2012, **Padma Bhushan 2022**; **Dr Satyapal Singh** (para-athletics coach) – **Padma Shri 2025**; **P.R. Sreejesh** (hockey) – **Padma Bhushan 2025**.
- **Padma Vibhushan 2025:** **Dr D. Nageshwar Reddy** (gastroenterologist, AIG Hospitals) – field **Medicine**.

# Netaji – background
- Born **23 January 1897**; died **18 August 1945**, air crash near **Taihoku (Taiwan)**.
- **125th birth anniversary** (23 January 2022) – statue near **India Gate** (sculptor **Arun Yogiraj**).
- **Forward Bloc** founded **3 May 1939**; **Indian National Army** (1943–45).
- **Azad Hind Government:** **21 October 1943, Singapore** – recognised by 9 countries; Bose was PM, Foreign and Defence Minister.
- **30 December 1943:** Tricolour hoisted at **Port Blair**.
- **6 July 1944:** first to call Gandhi "**Father of the Nation**" (radio, Singapore).
- **2018:** Ross Island renamed **Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose Island**.
- Azad Hind Fauj originally formed **29 October 1915** by Raja Mahendra Pratap Singh, Rash Behari Bose, Niranjan Singh Gill; INA handed to Bose by **Rash Behari Bose** on **4 July 1943**.
- **Bharat Ratna to Netaji:** announced posthumously in **1992**, but withdrawn in **1997** after a Supreme Court case – the only time a Bharat Ratna announcement was cancelled.
