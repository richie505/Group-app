=== NOTES 1:109 | Timur's Invasion; Sayyid and Lodi Dynasties; First Battle of Panipat, 1526
## SUB 0 | First Battle of Panipat, 21 April 1526
[109:0:0] - **Babur defeated (and killed) Ibrahim Lodi** at Panipat (MPPSC 2005) – not Rana Sanga, Sikandar Lodi or Sher Shah; year **1526** (UPPSC 2016; SSC 2017 "Babur attacked India") – 1527 is Khanwa.
[109:0:1] - **Reason for victory (UPPSC 1990):** **artillery** – field guns and muskets under **Ustad Ali Quli** and **Mustafa**, which Ibrahim lacked; one printed key gives "skilled commandant".
[109:0:2] - **"Main reason" (State PSC):** **his skilled warfare** – about **12,000** men beat about **1,00,000**; tulughma and artillery are parts of it.
[109:0:3] - **First use of artillery in an Indian battle:** **First Panipat** (UPPSC 1996) – Babur first mentions guns at **Bajaur (1519)**.
[109:0:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [109:0:4.0] Tulughma | Flanking (side) attack
  [109:0:4.1] Araba | Carts chained together as a defence line ("Rumi"/Ottoman device)
  [109:0:4.2] Field artillery and muskets | Decisive; gunners Ustad Ali Quli and Mustafa
[109:0:5] # Consequences
[109:0:6] - Babur captured **Delhi and Agra** and the Lodi treasure (APPSC 2023) – not Kanpur or Allahabad.
[109:0:7] - **End of the Delhi Sultanate**; the **Mughal Empire was founded by Babur** (APPSC G1) – Akbar consolidated it.
[109:0:8] - Ibrahim = **only Delhi Sultan to die on the battlefield**.
[109:0:9] - **21 April 2026** = **500th anniversary** of the battle.
[109:0:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.70; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.50-51; Wikipedia "First Battle of Panipat"; app notes 1:109 (sub 6)
## SUB 1 | Sikandar Lodi (1489-1517) – Agra, Gulrukhi, Taxes
[109:1:0] - **Founder of Agra (1504), made it the capital:** **Sikandar Lodi** (G2 2011; CGPSC 2018; UPPSC 1993, 2011 ×2) – not Firoz, MBT, Alauddin, Bahlul, Balban, Iltutmish, Akbar or Shah Jahan.
[109:1:1] - **Pen name Gulrukhi:** **Sikandar Lodi**, who wrote **Persian poetry** (UPPSC 2015) – not Firoz, Bahlul or Ibrahim.
[109:1:2] - **Abolished the tax on grains:** **Sikandar Lodi** (UDA/LDA 2010), with several taxes and trade restrictions – not Alauddin, Ghiyasuddin or Firoz.
[109:1:3] - **Delhi ruler when the Portuguese came (Vasco da Gama, 1498):** **Sikandar Lodi** (APPSC Endowment) – not Ibrahim or Bahlul.
[109:1:4] - **Nizam Khan**, third son of Bahlol; crowned **17 July 1489**; **greatest Lodi**; rule from **Punjab to Bihar**, took **Jaunpur**, beat **Bengal** rulers and **Rajput** chiefs.
[109:1:5] - **Gaz-i-Sikandari:** yard to **measure land** (used later by Sher Shah and Akbar); audits and **spies**; punished embezzlement.
[109:1:6] - **Religion:** broke images at **Jwalamukhi (Nagarkot)**, destroyed **Mathura** temples, imposed **jizya** – yet had a **Sanskrit medical work translated into Persian**.
[109:1:7] - Built **Moth ki Masjid** (by his minister) and royal tombs of the **Lodi Gardens** (double dome).
[109:1:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.70; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.50-51; app notes 1:109 (sub 4)
## SUB 2 | Timur's Invasion, 1398
[109:2:0] - **Timur (Taimur / Tamerlane)**, the 14th-century Turkish conqueror (APPSC G1 2004) – not Chengiz Khan (Temujin) or Daulat Khan Lodi; born 1336 at Kesh; claimed descent from Chengiz.
[109:2:1] - **Year:** **1398** (MPPSC 2005); crossed the **Indus** (30 Sept 1398) → **Multan, Bhatnair** → sacked Delhi for three days (**December 1398**).
[109:2:2] - **Sultan at the time:** **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah Tughlaq** (printed "Nasiruddin Mohammad Tughlaq"), the last Tughlaq – not Daulat Khan Lodi, Abu Bakr or Tughlaq Shah.
[109:2:3] - **Dynasty after Timur:** the **Sayyids** (Khizr Khan, 1414) (State PSC) – the Tughlaqs lingered only to 1412-14.
[109:2:4] - **Effects:** Tughlaq state broke into regional kingdoms (**Jaunpur, Malwa, Gujarat**); the Mughal Empire was **not** an effect (Babur, 1526).
[109:2:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.49; Rocket Indian History 01 (Timur – Delhi 1398 – Nasiruddin Mahmud); app notes 1:109 (sub 2)
## SUB 3 | Lodi Dynasty – Bahlol and Ibrahim; Order
[109:3:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Reign | Key points
  [109:3:0.0] Bahlol (Bahlul) Lodi | 1451-89 | Founder; Governor of Punjab (Sirhind, Lahore); throne from Alam Shah; conquered Jaunpur; longest reign; Bahlul coin
  [109:3:0.1] Sikandar Lodi | 1489-1517 | Son of Bahlol; Agra, Gaz-i-Sikandari, Gulrukhi
  [109:3:0.2] Ibrahim Lodi | 1517-26 | Son of Sikandar; arrogant to nobles; killed at Panipat
[109:3:1] - **Order of the Afghan rulers:** **Bahlol → Sikandar → Ibrahim** (UPSC 2006 ×2).
[109:3:2] - **Last Lodi ruler and last Delhi Sultan:** **Ibrahim Lodi** (SSC 2001).
[109:3:3] - **First Lodi Sultan = Bahlol**, so "Ibrahim was the first Lodi Sultan" ✗ (Sachivalayam 2019).
[109:3:4] - Lodis = **first Afghan** dynasty; Sultan = "**first among equals**" among Afghan nobles.
[109:3:5] # Ibrahim Lodi
[109:3:6] - Nobles plotted to enthrone his brother **Jalal Khan**; Ibrahim seized **Gwalior** (which sheltered Jalal) and made **Vikramajit** a vassal.
[109:3:7] - **Khatoli (Khatauli), 1517-18:** **Rana Sanga** defeated Ibrahim (RAS 2012) – not Sarangpur, Siwana or Khanua.
[109:3:8] - Contemporaries: **Krishnadevaraya**, **Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk** (Golconda), **Rana Sanga** (not Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq).
[109:3:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.70; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.50-51; app notes 1:109 (sub 4)

## SUB 5 | Sayyid Dynasty (1414-51)
[109:5:0] TABLE head: Order | Ruler | Reign
  [109:5:0.0] 1 | Khizr Khan | 1414-21
  [109:5:0.1] 2 | Mubarak Shah | 1421-34
  [109:5:0.2] 3 | Muhammad Shah | 1434-45
  [109:5:0.3] 4 | Alauddin Alam Shah | 1445-51
[109:5:1] - **Order:** Khizr Khan → Mubarak Shah → Muhammad Shah → Alam Shah = **1, 3, 2, 4** (UP RO/ARO 2023).
[109:5:2] - **Khizr Khan:** Timur's governor of Multan / **viceroy**; arrested **Daulat Khan Lodi**, took Delhi **1414**; ruled as a deputy **without the title of Sultan** and **accepted the suzerainty of Timur's son Shah Rukh** (CDS 2004) – not Firoz, MBT or Sikandar Lodi.
[109:5:3] - **Mubarak Shah:** failed against the **Khokhars**; **Tarikh-i-Mubarak Shahi** by **Yahiya bin Ahmad Sarhindi**; his octagonal tomb has **blue enamelled tiles** and **guldasta** motifs.
[109:5:4] - Sayyids claimed descent from the **Prophet**; called **Kulah-Daran** (cap-wearers).
[109:5:5] - **End:** **Alam Shah voluntarily** gave the throne to **Bahlol Lodi** (1451) – the only Sultan to abdicate.
[109:5:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.69; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.49-50; app notes 1:109 (sub 3)

## SUB 0 | Slave Dynasty – Aibak, Iltutmish, Razia, Balban
[110:0:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Key facts
  [110:0:0.0] Qutbuddin Aibak (1206-10) | Turkish slave of Muhammad Ghori; got Ghori's Indian territories; capital Lahore; Lakh Baksh; built Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque and Arhai Din ka Jhonpra; began Qutub Minar; patron of Hasan Nizami and Fakhr-i-Mudabbir; died playing chaugan
  [110:0:0.1] Iltutmish (1211-36) | "Slave of a slave" (slave of Aibak, Ghori's slave), Aibak's son-in-law; real founder of the Sultanate; first to shift the capital to Delhi; silver tanka, copper jital; completed Qutub Minar; created the Forty; caliph's investiture 1229
  [110:0:0.2] Razia (1236-40) | Daughter of Iltutmish; first and only woman ruler; married Malik Altunia (governor of Bhatinda)
  [110:0:0.3] Balban (1266-87) | Zil-i-Ilahi; blood and iron; crushed Tughril Khan of Bengal (1279); broke the Forty; chain of forts on the Mongol frontier
[110:0:1] - **Aibak:** born in **Turkistan**; sold to the Qazi of Nishapur, then to Ghori; ruled largely as **Malik / Sipahsalar**; also called **Quran Khwan** (recited the Quran melodiously).
[110:0:2] - **Iltutmish's rivals:** **Tajuddin Yalduz** (Ghazni; beaten at **Tarain 1215-16**), **Nasiruddin Qabacha** (Multan and Sindh, crushed **1228**), **Ali Mardan** (Lakhnauti) – **Malik Altunia was not one** (he was Razia's husband).
[110:0:3] - **Chengiz (Genghis) Khan** reached the **Indus in 1221** under **Iltutmish**, chasing Khwarezm prince **Jalaluddin Mangbarni**; Iltutmish **refused him shelter**.
[110:0:4] - **Qutub Minar:** begun by **Aibak** (basement/first storey), completed by **Iltutmish**; fourth storey damaged in **Firoz Shah's** time and repaired (fifth storey added); **Alauddin Khalji had no part**.
[110:0:5] - **Alauddin Masud Shah** (1242-46): coins carried the name of **al-Musta'sim, the last Abbasid caliph of Baghdad**, for the first time.
[110:0:6] - **Balban:** real name **Baha-ud-din**; title **Ulugh Khan** from **Nasiruddin Mahmud**, whom he succeeded; Balban's frontier policy was **adopted and completed by Alauddin**.
[110:0:7] - Other battles: **Chandawar 1194** – Ghori beat **Jaichand of Kannauj**; Second Tarain 1192 – Ghori beat **Prithviraj Chauhan**.

## SUB 1 | The Five Dynasties – Order, Periods, Founders
[110:1:0] - **Delhi Sultanate:** **1206-1526**; opened by **Second Tarain (1192)**, ended at **First Panipat (1526)**.
[110:1:1] TABLE head: Order | Dynasty | Period | Founder | Origin
  [110:1:1.0] 1 | Slave / Mamluk (Ilbari) | 1206-1290 | Qutbuddin Aibak | Turk
  [110:1:1.1] 2 | Khalji | 1290-1320 | Jalaluddin Khalji (crowned at Kilokhari) | Turk (Khalji tribe settled in Afghanistan)
  [110:1:1.2] 3 | Tughlaq | 1320-1414 | Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (Ghazi Malik) | Turk (father a Turkish slave)
  [110:1:1.3] 4 | Sayyid | 1414-1451 | Khizr Khan (Timur's viceroy) | Claimed descent from the Prophet
  [110:1:1.4] 5 | Lodi | 1451-1526 | Bahlol (Bahlul) Lodi | Afghan – the only Afghan dynasty
[110:1:2] - **Order:** Slave → Khalji → Tughlaq → Sayyid → Lodi (codes: Slave, Khalji, Sayyid, Lodi = **4, 1, 3, 2**).
[110:1:3] - **Longest dynasty:** **Tughlaq**, about **94 years**; **shortest:** **Khalji**, about **30 years** (Sayyids 37).
[110:1:4] - **Longest reign:** **Bahlol Lodi**, **38 years (1451-89)** – not Firoz (1351-88), Alauddin (1296-1316), MBT (1325-51) or Balban (1266-87).
[110:1:5] - **Ghori dynasty** never ruled from Delhi as a Delhi dynasty – Muhammad Ghori was only the overlord before 1206; Ghori's Indian conquests passed to **Aibak**.
[110:1:6] - **Slave phases:** **Qutbi** (1206-11, Aibak), **First Ilbari** (1211-66, Iltutmish), **Second Ilbari** (1266-90, Balban).
[110:1:7] - **Sayyids** = **Kulah-Daran** (wore a pointed cap, **kulah**).
[110:1:8] - **Before the Sultanate:** Delhi first became a capital under the **Tomara Rajputs**; under the **Tomaras and Chauhans** Delhi became an **important commercial centre** (coins **dehliwal**). Khaljis – **market control**; Tughlaqs – Sultanate **extended to the far south**; Sayyids – founded by **Khizr Khan**.
[110:1:9] - **Succession:** Slave: Aibak → Aram Shah → Iltutmish → Ruknuddin Firoz → Razia → Bahram Shah → Alauddin Masud → Nasiruddin Mahmud → Balban → Kaiqubad. Khalji: Jalaluddin → Alauddin → Shihabuddin (child set up by **Malik Kafur**) → Mubarak Shah. Sayyid: Khizr Khan → **Mubarak Shah** → Muhammad Shah → Alam Shah. Lodi: Bahlol → Sikandar → Ibrahim.
[110:1:10] - **Two Nasiruddin Mahmuds:** one in the **Slave** line (before Balban), one the **last Tughlaq** (Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah, 1394-1412).
[110:1:11] TABLE head: First / last | Ruler
  [110:1:11.0] First Sultan | Qutbuddin Aibak (master Muhammad Ghori)
  [110:1:11.1] First woman ruler | Razia (daughter of Iltutmish)
  [110:1:11.2] First Afghan ruler | Bahlol Lodi
  [110:1:11.3] Last Slave ruler | Kaiqubad, killed 1290
  [110:1:11.4] Last Khalji | Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah, killed by Khusrau Khan
  [110:1:11.5] Last Tughlaq | Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah (1394-1412)
  [110:1:11.6] Last Sultan | Ibrahim Lodi, Panipat 1526

## SUB 3 | Khaljis – Jalaluddin and Alauddin
[110:3:0] - **Jalaluddin Khalji** (1290-96): took the throne at **70**; first Sultan **benevolent to Hindus**; murdered by nephew-son-in-law Alauddin at **Kara (1296)**.

[110:3:6] - Built **Siri** and the **Alai Darwaza**.

## SUB 0 | Theory of Kingship and the Caliph
[111:0:0] TABLE head: Sultan | Theory / claim
  [111:0:0.0] Iltutmish | Legitimacy by caliphal investiture: mansur / khillat from the Abbasid Caliph of Baghdad (1229); Sultanate became part of Dar-ul-Islam; title Sultan-i-Azam
  [111:0:0.1] Balban | First Muslim ruler with a theory like the divine right of kings: Sultan = Niyabat-i-Khudai (deputy of God), Zil-i-Ilahi / Zillillah (Shadow of God), next to the Paigambar; sijda and paibos; blood and iron; no low-born men in office
  [111:0:0.2] Alauddin Khalji | State interest above the ulema; "kingship knows no kinship"; did not seek the Caliph's investiture – took Yamin-ul-Khilafat Nasir-i-Amir-ul-Mominin on his own
  [111:0:0.3] Mubarak Shah Khalji | Proclaimed himself Caliph (Al-Imam, Khilafatullah)
  [111:0:0.4] MBT | Rationalist absolutism; coin legend "Al Sultan Zilli Allah"; later sought investiture from the Abbasid Caliph in Egypt
  [111:0:0.5] Firoz | Respected the Egyptian Caliph
[111:0:1] - **Balban** called himself "**Helper of a Caliph**" in his inscription on the **Garhmukteshwar** mosque.
[111:0:2] - **Opposed / ignored the Khalifa's authority:** **Alauddin Khalji** (no investiture sought); Iltutmish and Balban acknowledged him.
[111:0:3] - **Balban** did **not** introduce the iqtadari system (Iltutmish did); he did propound Niyabat-i-Khudai, broke the Chahalgani and quelled Bengal.
[111:0:4] - **Din-i-Ilahi** = Akbar; **Tuti-i-Hind** = Amir Khusrau; **Kaisar-i-Hind** = British title – none is Balban's.
[111:0:5] - Coins: **Muhammad Ghori** – Goddess **Lakshmi** with the Kalma; **Mahmud of Ghazni** – silver coins in **Arabic and Devanagari Sanskrit**.
[111:0:6] - The Sultans saw themselves as **representatives (lieutenants) of the Caliph**, naming him in the khutba and on coins – a nominal link; the Caliph had only moral authority.

## SUB 2 | Nobility – the Forty and Key Nobles
[111:2:0] - **Iltutmish** created the **Turkan-i-Chahalgani** ("the Forty", Chalisa): Turkish slave-nobles; they made and unmade Sultans after him.
[111:2:1] - **Balban**, himself one of the Forty, **finally broke their power**.
[111:2:2] - After Balban the nobles set aside his nominee **Kai Khusrau** and raised **Kaiqubad**; **Jalaluddin Khalji** seized power in **1290** (Khalji revolution: high office opened to **non-Turks and Indian Muslims**).
[111:2:3] - **MBT** promoted foreigners and low-born men; **Lodis**: Afghan tribal nobility, Sultan = **first among equals**.

## SUB 3 | Nature of the State and Law
[111:3:0] - Government was a **composite**: **Perso-Arabic, Turko-Mongol and Indian** elements (JPSC 2021). Perso-Arabic: title Sultan, nominal Caliph, sijda, paibos, Nauroz. Turko-Mongol: army in **decimal units** with mounted archers.
[111:3:1] - **Zawabit** = **state laws** (royal regulations) that supplemented the sharia.
[111:3:2] - Civil law: **sharia** for Muslims (qazis); Hindus kept their personal laws (panchayats); **criminal law followed the Sultan's rules**.
[111:3:3] - **Succession:** no fixed rule (no primogeniture) – acceptance by **nobles and ulema**, or **military strength**; Iltutmish nominated his daughter.
[111:3:4] - Sultan = supreme military, legal and political authority; **commander-in-chief** and **final court of appeal**.
[111:3:5] - The state was essentially **military** and centralised at first.
[111:3:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.56, p.58 (judiciary); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.71; app notes 1:111 (sub 0, 1)

=== NOTES 1:112 | Sultanate Provincial and Military Administration – Iqta, Dagh and Huliya, Army
## SUB 0 | The Iqta System
[112:0:0] - **Iqta** = the revenue of a territory **assigned** to an officer **in lieu of salary**; not ownership of land. Holder = **iqtadar / muqti** (wali of a province).
[112:0:1] - **Origin:** an **Arabic / Caliphate** institution (land revenue to officers for limited periods; described in Nizam-ul-Mulk Tusi's **Siyasatnama**) – a **foreign Turkish import, not an ancient indigenous institution** (UPSC 2019).
[112:0:2] - **First iqta in India:** **Hansi**, granted by **Muhammad Ghori** to **Qutbuddin Aibak**.
[112:0:3] - **Iltutmish introduced** the iqta as a **systematic institution** of the Sultanate (iqtas to his Turkish officers).
[112:0:4] - **Muqti's duties:** collect revenue, **maintain troops** from it, keep law and order, pay his men, and send the **surplus (fawazil)** to the treasury. Revenue was **not deposited directly in the Sultan's treasury**.
[112:0:5] - **Fawazil** = the **excess amount** paid by the iqtadars to the exchequer after salary and troops.
[112:0:6] - **A-R (UPSC 1998):** early Turkish administration was **essentially military** **because** the country was parcelled out as **iqtas among military leaders** – both true, R explains A.
[112:0:7] TABLE head: Sultan | Change
  [112:0:7.0] Iltutmish | Organised the system
  [112:0:7.1] Balban | Tried to resume old soldiers' iqtas; khwajas (auditors) checked muqtis
  [112:0:7.2] Alauddin, MBT | Strict control: income assessed, accountants, cash pay; enlarged khalisa (took back milk, idrarat, waqf lands)
  [112:0:7.3] Firoz | Relaxed control; iqtas became hereditary; revived the jagir – army efficiency fell
[112:0:8] - Iqtas were **transferable** (muqtis rotated every 3-4 years) until Firoz; the iqta is the ancestor of the Mughal **jagir and mansab** system.
[112:0:9] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.58-59 (iqta), p.61 (UPSC 2019); Rocket Indian History 01 p.97 (iqta – Iltutmish); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 05 p.12; app notes 1:112 (sub 0, 1)
## SUB 1 | Army – Cash Pay, Dagh and Huliya, Ranks
[112:1:0] - **Alauddin Khalji:** **largest standing army**, recruited and **paid in cash** directly by the state – the **first Sultan to pay soldiers in cash**; raised it to protect the frontier against the **Mongols**.
[112:1:1] - **Dagh** (**branding of horses**) and **huliya / chehra** (**descriptive roll** of soldiers) = **Alauddin** – to stop substandard horses and proxy soldiers at the muster; **Sher Shah** revived dagh.
[112:1:2] - **Balban** set up the **Diwan-i-Arz** under the **Ariz-i-Mumalik** to keep a large standing army against the **Mongols**; the Sultan himself was commander-in-chief.
[112:1:3] - **Ranks (decimal):** **sar-i-khail** (10 horsemen) → sipah-salar → amir → malik → **khan** (highest; commanded 10 maliks).
[112:1:4] - **Turkish military gains:** **iron stirrup**, mounted archers, armour; horses imported from **West Asia**; war elephants were the **Sultan's monopoly**.
[112:1:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.57-58; NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.62 (cash pay, chehra and dagh); app notes 1:112 (sub 4)
## SUB 2 | Provincial and Local Units

  [112:2:0.0] Iqta (province) | Muqti / wali / iqtadar
  [112:2:0.1] Shiq | Shiqdar
  [112:2:0.2] Pargana | Amil (revenue collection) and chaudhri
  [112:2:0.3] Village | Muqaddam / chaudhari (headman), khot; patwari (accountant)
[112:2:1] - **Amil** = in-charge of **revenue collection** (UPSC 2019, statement 1 true).
[112:2:2] - **Muqaddam or Chaudhari** = **village headman** (BPSC key); the **chaudhari** was the leading rural land-revenue official above the village (IAS 2004 key: "Chaudhary").
[112:2:3] - **Khuts, muqaddams, chaudharis** collected kharaj for the state; **Alauddin** ended their privileges and cesses.

## SUB 3 | Land Assignments, Post and Intelligence
[112:3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [112:3:0.0] Khalisa | Crown land; revenue for the court and household
  [112:3:0.1] Iqta | Revenue assignment to officials
  [112:3:0.2] Inam | Grants to religious men
  [112:3:0.3] Mahrousa | Protected town
  [112:3:0.4] Madad-i-Mash | Mughal revenue-free grant to saints, religious institutions, mashaikhs
  [112:3:0.5] Jagir / Amaram / Mokasa | Mughal / Vijayanagara / Marathas
[112:3:1] - Sultanate territory = **Khalisa and Iqta** lands. Maratha **chauth = one-fourth**, **sardeshmukhi = one-tenth**. Tanka = Iltutmish's **silver coin**.
[112:3:2] - **Post (Ibn Battuta):** **ulaq** = **horse post**; **dawa** = **foot post** (runners every third of a mile) – the **foot post, quicker than the horse post, was often used to bring the fruits of Khurasan** to India. **Dak chowki** = Sher Shah. **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** improved the post with runners and horsemen.
[112:3:3] - **Barids** (news-writers) and **munhiyans** (secret spies) sent Alauddin daily market reports.

=== NOTES 1:113 | Sultanate Economy – Kharaj, Jizya, Zakat, Khams; Agriculture, Towns, Trade and Craft
## SUB 0 | Sharia Taxes and Cesses
[113:0:0] TABLE head: Tax | Nature | Payer
  [113:0:0.0] Kharaj | Land tax, usually 1/3 (up to 1/2) of produce | Non-Muslim cultivators
  [113:0:0.1] Ushr | Land tax, 1/10 (5-10%) of produce | Muslim landholders
  [113:0:0.2] Jizya | Poll / protection tax in place of military service | Non-Muslims
  [113:0:0.3] Zakat | Religious tax, about 2.5% of wealth | Muslims only
  [113:0:0.4] Khams (Khums) | State's 1/5 share of war booty (and treasure-troves) | Army booty
[113:0:1] - **Taxes on land produce:** kharaj, ushr (and other land levies) – **not khams**, which is war booty.
[113:0:2] - Khams: **Alauddin and MBT took 4/5** of booty; **Firoz restored the sharia 1/5**.
[113:0:3] - **Firoz Tughlaq** kept only the four sharia taxes – **kharaj, jizya, zakat, khams** – abolished about 24 cesses (incl. ghari, charai) and added **haq-i-sharb**.
[113:0:4] TABLE head: Cess | Nature | Sultan
  [113:0:4.0] Ghari | House tax (first levied) | Alauddin
  [113:0:4.1] Charai | Grazing tax on milch cattle | Alauddin
  [113:0:4.2] Haq-i-sharb (sharb) | Irrigation tax, 10% of yield, on the ulema's advice | Firoz
[113:0:5] - **Abwab** = **miscellaneous cesses** outside the sharia, such as house tax, grazing tax, irrigation tax.
[113:0:6] - **Jizya** under **Aurangzeb** reimposed in 1679 (Akbar had abolished it).

## SUB 1 | Coins of the Sultanate
[113:1:0] TABLE head: Coin | Metal | Sultan
  [113:1:0.0] Tanka | Silver, 175 grains (about 11 g) | Iltutmish
  [113:1:0.1] Jital | Copper (48 jitals = 1 tanka) | Iltutmish
  [113:1:0.2] Shashgani | Silver, worth 6 jitals | Tughlaqs
  [113:1:0.3] Dinar | Gold, popular after the South Indian loot | Alauddin
  [113:1:0.4] Token | Copper / brass (1329-30), at par with silver; failed through forgery | MBT
  [113:1:0.5] Bahloli | Copper | Bahlol Lodi
[113:1:1] - **Iltutmish:** first Sultan to issue a **regular standard currency** and to make **Delhi the capital**; he also set up the **iqtadari** system.
[113:1:2] - The **two basic Sultanate coins** = **silver tanka and copper jital**; **rupiya and dam** are Sher Shah's (Rupiya 178 grains, **173 pure silver**; 1 rupiya = **64 dams**).
[113:1:3] - **Dehliwal** = pre-Sultanate coin of the Tomaras and Chauhans. **Ghori**'s gold coins bore **Lakshmi**.
[113:1:4] - Sher Shah's revenue system was the model for **Todar Mal's bandobast**.
[113:1:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.62 (Iltutmish coins); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.56 (tanka); app notes 1:113 (sub 3)
## SUB 2 | Agriculture and Irrigation
[113:2:0] - **Araghatta** = **water-wheel for irrigation** – a large **wheel with earthen pots tied to the outer ends of its spokes** (ara = spokes, ghata = pot), **animal-driven**, lifting water from **open wells**; in the **Panchatantra** and **Rajatarangini**. Turks spread the **Persian wheel (saqiya)**.
[113:2:1] - Crops of the 13th-14th c.: wheat, barley, gram, rice, sugarcane, cotton, oilseeds; **maize was not grown** (brought later by the **Portuguese**), nor **tobacco**.
[113:2:2] - **Canals:** **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** = first canal-builder; **Firoz Tughlaq** = **most canals** (longest Yamuna → **Hisar**).
[113:2:3] - **MBT:** Diwan-i-Kohi and taccavi; **Firoz:** 1,200 orchards near Delhi.
[113:2:4] - **AP angle:** Kakatiya **Maagani** = irrigated land, **Metta** = dry land; land tax **Ari**.
[113:2:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 (Araghatta table); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.72 (Persian wheel); app notes 1:113 (sub 2)
## SUB 3 | Land Revenue Demand
[113:3:0] - Peasants usually paid **one-third**; **Alauddin Khalji raised it to one-half** of the produce (kharaj 50%), measured the land and collected directly.
[113:3:1] - **MBT** raised taxes in the **Doab** during a famine; neither Alauddin nor MBT took one-sixth – so "one-sixth" statements are **false** (CDS 2005).
[113:3:2] - **Alauddin** held a religious discourse with the Jain **Acharya Mahasena** (Jain tradition) – CDS 2005 statement I true.
[113:3:3] - **NDA 2002:** Alauddin **fixed grain prices**, **paid soldiers in cash** and **raised revenue to half** – all three correct.
[113:3:4] - **Land categories:** **khalisa** (crown), **iqta** (assigned in place of salary), **inam** (religious grants).
[113:3:5] - **MBT:** **taccavi (sondhar)** loans to extend cultivation; first Sultan to organise **famine relief** (loans, relief, Swargadwari camp).
[113:3:6] - Alauddin's price-control markets show that **military needs shaped economic policy**.
[113:3:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.59-60; Rocket Indian History 01 p.82 (Alauddin 50%); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.61, p.65; app notes 1:113 (sub 1, 2)
## SUB 4 | Towns, Crafts and Trade
[113:4:0] - **Towns:** Lahore, Multan, Broach, Cambay grew; Siri (Alauddin), Tughlaqabad, Jahanpanah (MBT), Hisar, Firozabad, Jaunpur (Firoz), **Agra (Sikandar Lodi, 1504; later a Mughal imperial centre)**. Firoz took about **1,300 Sanskrit manuscripts** from the Jawalamukhi temple, **Nagarkot**.
[113:4:1] - **Royal karkhanas:** Firoz ran about **36**, with thousands of slaves.
[113:4:2] - **Crafts:** textiles, **leather, metal-work, carpet-weaving**; Turkish gains – **paper** (record-keeping), glass, **spinning wheel**, Persian wheel, arch and dome mortar.
[113:4:3] - **Trade:** **Multanis and Khurasanis** – overseas trade and finance; **Gujaratis, Marwaris, Bohras** – inland trade; **Banjaras** carried grain on oxen.
[113:4:4] - **Imports:** horses, textiles, glassware from **West Asia**; silk and porcelain from **China**. Ibn Battuta: Malabar **pepper**.
[113:4:5] - **Amir Khusrau:** Warangal's wealth and **diamonds** drew the Delhi invasions.
[113:4:6] - **Society in brief:** ulema kept the religious law; **khuts and muqaddams** collected peasants' revenue; caste continued, **sati and purdah** spread; slave markets (Ibn Battuta), Firoz's slave department.
[113:4:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.71-72; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.58; app notes 1:113 (sub 4, 5, 6)

=== NOTES 1:114 | Sultanate Society – Nobility, Ulema, Women, Slavery, Conversion and Syncretism
## SUB 0 | Barani, the Ulema and Social Groups
[114:0:0] - **Barani** refused to call the Sultanate truly Islamic because the **Sultan supplemented the sharia with his own state regulations (zawabit)** (UPSC 2002).
[114:0:1] - Barani's ideal of rule: ***Fatawa-i-Jahandari***; history: ***Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi***.
[114:0:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [114:0:2.0] Dastar-bandan | Ulema (turban-wearers)
  [114:0:2.1] Kulah-daran | Sayyids (pointed-cap wearers)
  [114:0:2.2] Ashraf | Muslims of foreign descent – Turks, Persians, Afghans
  [114:0:2.3] Ajlaf | Lower-status (Indian convert) Muslims
  [114:0:2.4] Rais / Ranas | Local Hindu chiefs who paid tribute and collected taxes
[114:0:3] - **Qazi-ul-Quzat** = chief judge; **Sadr-us-Sudur** handled religious grants and acted as Chief Qazi.
[114:0:4] - Muslim society: Turks at the top, then Persians and Afghans, Indian Muslims at the bottom; they rarely intermarried.
[114:0:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.61 (social hierarchy); PMF Ancient & Medieval part4 (Kulah-Daran, Dastar-Bandan); app notes 1:114 (sub 0, 1)
## SUB 1 | Religious Policy and Jizya
[114:1:0] TABLE head: Sultan | Approach
  [114:1:0.0] Jalaluddin Khalji | First Sultan benevolent to Hindus
  [114:1:0.1] Alauddin Khalji | Kept the ulema out of state affairs; told Qazi Mughisuddin he ordered what was good for the state
  [114:1:0.2] Nasiruddin Khusrau Shah (1320) | Hindu gods worshipped in the palace
  [114:1:0.3] MBT | Leaned on Sufis against the ulema; visited the Ajmer shrine; Holi
  [114:1:0.4] Firoz Tughlaq | Followed the ulema; theocratic; respected the Egyptian Caliph; first to levy jizya on Brahmins and made it a separate tax
  [114:1:0.5] Sikandar Lodi | Orthodox; destroyed Mathura temples, broke the Nagarkot images; jizya
[114:1:1] - **Jizya:** protection tax on non-Muslims (**zimmis**) **in place of military service**; women, children, old, disabled and at first **Brahmins exempt**.
[114:1:2] - **Qutb Shahis of Golconda** never imposed jizya.
[114:1:3] - Temple destruction often served **political** ends (royal legitimacy) as well as religious ones.
[114:1:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.60 (jizya); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.72 (jaziya on Brahmins); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 05 p.12; app notes 1:114 (sub 2, 3)
## SUB 2 | Women and Slavery
[114:2:0] - **Purdah** spread among upper-class women of north India from the **13th century**; **sati** recorded by **Ibn Battuta**; **jauhar** at **Ranthambhor (1301)** and **Chittor (1303)**.
[114:2:1] - **Razia Sultana (1236-40)**: only woman ruler of Delhi. **Lal Ded** = Kashmiri woman saint; Rudrama Devi (Kakatiya) and **Chand Bibi** (Ahmadnagar) never ruled Delhi.
[114:2:2] - **Slavery:** war captives and slave markets; Alauddin's third market dealt in **horses, slaves and cattle**.
[114:2:3] - **Firoz Tughlaq** had the **most slaves – about 1,80,000**; created the **Diwan-i-Bandagan**; slaves paid 10-100 tankas.
[114:2:4] - **Aibak, Iltutmish and Balban** had been slaves (bandagan) – hence "Slave" / "Mamluk" dynasty.
[114:2:5] - **AP angle:** in the Reddi period women who died as **sati** were deified and worshipped as **Perantallu** (Parantas, local deities); Vijayanagara slavery = **Benabaga**.
[114:2:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.72 (sati, purdah, slaves); NextGen AP History - APPSC 12 p.14 (Reddi society: Parantas); app notes 1:114 (sub 4, 5)

=== NOTES 1:115 | Indo-islamic Architecture – Arcuate Style, Qutb Minar, Alai Darwaza, Tughlaqabad, Provincial Styles
## SUB 0 | Arcuate Style – Features and Architectural Firsts
[115:0:0] - **Arcuate style:** **arch, dome and vault** brought by the Turks; the **true arch** is held by a **keystone**. Indian **trabeate** style = **lintel, pillar and corbel**; the two merged.
[115:0:1] - **Materials:** rubble masonry in **surkhi** (brick dust in lime mortar), chunam plaster, red sandstone and marble; decoration moved away from **human and animal figures** to **arabesque**, geometry and **Quranic calligraphy**.
[115:0:2] - **Terms:** **mihrab** = prayer niche; minbar = pulpit; **jali** = pierced stone screen.
[115:0:3] TABLE head: First | Building
  [115:0:3.0] Earliest Indo-Islamic building; first Sultanate mosque | Quwwat-ul-Islam, Delhi (Aibak, completed 1198)
  [115:0:3.1] First Islamic tomb (strict) | Sultan Ghari (1231), built by Iltutmish for his son Nasiruddin Mahmud
  [115:0:3.2] First tomb in a purely Indo-Islamic style; first true arch | Balban's tomb, Mehrauli
  [115:0:3.3] First true dome; first building fully on Islamic principles; horse-shoe arch introduced | Alai Darwaza (1311, Alauddin Khalji)
  [115:0:3.4] First octagonal tomb | Khan-i-Jahan Tilangani's tomb (Firoz Tughlaq's reign)
  [115:0:3.5] First double dome | Sikandar Lodi's tomb (Lodis pioneered it; Humayun's Tomb brought it to the Mughals)
[115:0:4] - **⚠ Disputed (UPPSC 2004 "first Indo-Islamic tomb"):** most keys say **Balban's tomb** (true arch); strictly **Sultan Ghari** is older.
[115:0:5] # Dynasty → style
[115:0:6] TABLE head: Dynasty | Features | Example
  [115:0:6.0] Khalji | Horse-shoe arch with lotus-bud fringe; true dome; red sandstone with marble (Seljuq influence) | Alai Darwaza
  [115:0:6.1] Tughlaq | Battered (sloping) walls, arch-beam combination, plain fort-like mass, octagonal plan emerges, encaustic tiles; Syrian/Byzantine-type false arches | Tughlaqabad; Ghiyasuddin's tomb (sloping walls, marble dome)
  [115:0:6.2] Sayyid and Lodi | Octagonal tombs with verandah, double dome, Hindu-style gateways, plaster ornament, drip-stones | Sikandar Lodi's tomb (octagonal), Bara Gumbad
  [115:0:6.3] Sharqi (Jaunpur) | Massive propylon gateway with inclined walls, no minarets | Atala Masjid, Lal Darwaza Mosque
  [115:0:6.4] Vijayanagara | Bodegai (bracket capital) on pillars | Hampi
[115:0:7] - **RAS 2013 match:** lotus-bud fringe – **Khaljis**; octagonal tomb – **Tughluqs**; bodegai – **Vijayanagara**; massive inclined-wall gate – **Sharqis** → **3 4 2 1**.
