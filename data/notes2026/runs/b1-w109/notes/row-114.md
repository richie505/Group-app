=== Barani, the Ulema and Social Groups
- **Barani** refused to call the Sultanate truly Islamic because the **Sultan supplemented the sharia with his own state regulations (zawabit)** (UPSC 2002).
- Barani's ideal of rule: ***Fatawa-i-Jahandari***; history: ***Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi***.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Dastar-bandan** | **Ulema** (turban-wearers) |
| **Kulah-daran** | **Sayyids** (pointed-cap wearers) |
| Ashraf | Muslims of foreign descent – Turks, Persians, Afghans |
| Ajlaf | Lower-status (Indian convert) Muslims |
| Rais / Ranas | Local Hindu chiefs who paid tribute and collected taxes |
- **Qazi-ul-Quzat** = chief judge; **Sadr-us-Sudur** handled religious grants and acted as Chief Qazi.
- Muslim society: Turks at the top, then Persians and Afghans, Indian Muslims at the bottom; they rarely intermarried.

=== Religious Policy and Jizya
| Sultan | Approach |
|---|---|
| Jalaluddin Khalji | First Sultan benevolent to Hindus |
| Alauddin Khalji | Kept the ulema out of state affairs; told **Qazi Mughisuddin** he ordered what was good for the state |
| Nasiruddin Khusrau Shah (1320) | Hindu gods worshipped in the palace |
| MBT | Leaned on Sufis against the ulema; visited the Ajmer shrine; Holi |
| **Firoz Tughlaq** | Followed the ulema; theocratic; respected the Egyptian Caliph; **first to levy jizya on Brahmins** and made it a separate tax |
| Sikandar Lodi | Orthodox; destroyed Mathura temples, broke the Nagarkot images; jizya |
- **Jizya:** protection tax on non-Muslims (**zimmis**) **in place of military service**; women, children, old, disabled and at first **Brahmins exempt**.
- **Qutb Shahis of Golconda** never imposed jizya.
- Temple destruction often served **political** ends (royal legitimacy) as well as religious ones.

=== Women and Slavery
- **Purdah** spread among upper-class women of north India from the **13th century**; **sati** recorded by **Ibn Battuta**; **jauhar** at **Ranthambhor (1301)** and **Chittor (1303)**.
- **Razia Sultana (1236-40)**: only woman ruler of Delhi. **Lal Ded** = Kashmiri woman saint; Rudrama Devi (Kakatiya) and **Chand Bibi** (Ahmadnagar) never ruled Delhi.
- **Slavery:** war captives and slave markets; Alauddin's third market dealt in **horses, slaves and cattle**.
- **Firoz Tughlaq** had the **most slaves – about 1,80,000**; created the **Diwan-i-Bandagan**; slaves paid 10-100 tankas.
- **Aibak, Iltutmish and Balban** had been slaves (bandagan) – hence "Slave" / "Mamluk" dynasty.
- **AP angle:** in the Reddi period women who died as **sati** were deified and worshipped as **Perantallu** (Parantas, local deities); Vijayanagara slavery = **Benabaga**.

=== Conversion and Syncretism
| Theory of conversion | Status |
|---|---|
| Sword (forced mass conversion) | Rejected as a colonial trope |
| Political patronage | Partial factor |
| Social liberation | Partial (Meenakshipuram, Feb 1981, 558 Dalits) |
| Sufi influence + agrarian expansion | Major factor in **Bengal and Punjab** |
- **Syncretism – persons and texts:**
| Person / text | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Al-Biruni | Compared Sufi divine love with the **Bhagavad Gita** |
| Amritakund | **Hatha-yoga** text put into Arabic and Persian |
| Sheikh Nuruddin | Founded Kashmir's **Rishi** movement |
| **Chishtis** | Spoke in **Hindavi** |
| Amir Khusrau | Credited with the **sitar** and qawwali |
| Malik Muhammad Jayasi | ***Padmavat*** |
| *Dalail-i-Firozshahi* | Sanskrit texts in Persian under **Firoz Tughlaq** |
- **Bhakti** had native roots in the **Alvars and Nayanars**.
- **Deccan:** **Gesu Daraz** moved to Gulbarga under Firoz Shah Bahmani (*Mirqat-ul-Ashyaq*); Golconda: foreign Muslims = **Afaqis**, Brahmins held **Mustajir** (tax-farming) rights; **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** granted villages to **Bhadrachalam**; **Akkanna and Madanna** of Golconda = "Andhra Sultans".
