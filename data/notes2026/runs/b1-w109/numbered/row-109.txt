## SUB 0 | Sikandar Lodi (1489-1517) – Agra, Gulrukhi, Taxes
[0:0] - **Founder of Agra (1504), made it the capital:** **Sikandar Lodi** (G2 2011; CGPSC 2018; UPPSC 1993, 2011 ×2) – not Firoz, MBT, Alauddin, Bahlul, Balban, Iltutmish, Akbar or Shah Jahan.
[0:1] - **Pen name Gulrukhi:** **Sikandar Lodi**, who wrote **Persian poetry** (UPPSC 2015) – not Firoz, Bahlul or Ibrahim.
[0:2] - **Abolished the tax on grains:** **Sikandar Lodi** (UDA/LDA 2010), with several taxes and trade restrictions – not Alauddin, Ghiyasuddin or Firoz.
[0:3] - **Delhi ruler when the Portuguese came (Vasco da Gama, 1498):** **Sikandar Lodi** (APPSC Endowment) – not Ibrahim or Bahlul.
[0:4] - **Nizam Khan**, third son of Bahlol; crowned **17 July 1489**; **greatest Lodi**; rule from **Punjab to Bihar**, took **Jaunpur**, beat **Bengal** rulers and **Rajput** chiefs.
[0:5] - **Gaz-i-Sikandari:** yard to **measure land** (used later by Sher Shah and Akbar); audits and **spies**; punished embezzlement.
[0:6] - **Religion:** broke images at **Jwalamukhi (Nagarkot)**, destroyed **Mathura** temples, imposed **jizya** – yet had a **Sanskrit medical work translated into Persian**.
[0:7] - Built **Moth ki Masjid** (by his minister) and royal tombs of the **Lodi Gardens** (double dome).
[0:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.70; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.50-51; app notes 1:109 (sub 4)
## SUB 1 | First Battle of Panipat, 21 April 1526
[1:0] - **Babur defeated (and killed) Ibrahim Lodi** at Panipat (MPPSC 2005) – not Rana Sanga, Sikandar Lodi or Sher Shah; year **1526** (UPPSC 2016; SSC 2017 "Babur attacked India") – 1527 is Khanwa.
[1:1] - **Reason for victory (UPPSC 1990):** **artillery** – field guns and muskets under **Ustad Ali Quli** and **Mustafa**, which Ibrahim lacked; one printed key gives "skilled commandant".
[1:2] - **"Main reason" (State PSC):** **his skilled warfare** – about **12,000** men beat about **1,00,000**; tulughma and artillery are parts of it.
[1:3] - **First use of artillery in an Indian battle:** **First Panipat** (UPPSC 1996) – Babur first mentions guns at **Bajaur (1519)**.
[1:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:4.0] Tulughma | Flanking (side) attack
  [1:4.1] Araba | Carts chained together as a defence line ("Rumi"/Ottoman device)
  [1:4.2] Field artillery and muskets | Decisive; gunners Ustad Ali Quli and Mustafa
[1:5] # Consequences
[1:6] - Babur captured **Delhi and Agra** and the Lodi treasure (APPSC 2023) – not Kanpur or Allahabad.
[1:7] - **End of the Delhi Sultanate**; the **Mughal Empire was founded by Babur** (APPSC G1) – Akbar consolidated it.
[1:8] - Ibrahim = **only Delhi Sultan to die on the battlefield**.
[1:9] - **21 April 2026** = **500th anniversary** of the battle.
[1:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.70; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.50-51; Wikipedia "First Battle of Panipat"; app notes 1:109 (sub 6)
## SUB 2 | Timur's Invasion, 1398
[2:0] - **Timur (Taimur / Tamerlane)**, the 14th-century Turkish conqueror (APPSC G1 2004) – not Chengiz Khan (Temujin) or Daulat Khan Lodi; born 1336 at Kesh; claimed descent from Chengiz.
[2:1] - **Year:** **1398** (MPPSC 2005); crossed the **Indus** (30 Sept 1398) → **Multan, Bhatnair** → sacked Delhi for three days (**December 1398**).
[2:2] - **Sultan at the time:** **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah Tughlaq** (printed "Nasiruddin Mohammad Tughlaq"), the last Tughlaq – not Daulat Khan Lodi, Abu Bakr or Tughlaq Shah.
[2:3] - **Dynasty after Timur:** the **Sayyids** (Khizr Khan, 1414) (State PSC) – the Tughlaqs lingered only to 1412-14.
[2:4] - **Effects:** Tughlaq state broke into regional kingdoms (**Jaunpur, Malwa, Gujarat**); the Mughal Empire was **not** an effect (Babur, 1526).
[2:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.49; Rocket Indian History 01 (Timur – Delhi 1398 – Nasiruddin Mahmud); app notes 1:109 (sub 2)
## SUB 3 | Sayyid Dynasty (1414-51)
[3:0] TABLE head: Order | Ruler | Reign
  [3:0.0] 1 | Khizr Khan | 1414-21
  [3:0.1] 2 | Mubarak Shah | 1421-34
  [3:0.2] 3 | Muhammad Shah | 1434-45
  [3:0.3] 4 | Alauddin Alam Shah | 1445-51
[3:1] - **Order:** Khizr Khan → Mubarak Shah → Muhammad Shah → Alam Shah = **1, 3, 2, 4** (UP RO/ARO 2023).
[3:2] - **Khizr Khan:** Timur's governor of Multan / **viceroy**; arrested **Daulat Khan Lodi**, took Delhi **1414**; ruled as a deputy **without the title of Sultan** and **accepted the suzerainty of Timur's son Shah Rukh** (CDS 2004) – not Firoz, MBT or Sikandar Lodi.
[3:3] - **Mubarak Shah:** failed against the **Khokhars**; **Tarikh-i-Mubarak Shahi** by **Yahiya bin Ahmad Sarhindi**; his octagonal tomb has **blue enamelled tiles** and **guldasta** motifs.
[3:4] - Sayyids claimed descent from the **Prophet**; called **Kulah-Daran** (cap-wearers).
[3:5] - **End:** **Alam Shah voluntarily** gave the throne to **Bahlol Lodi** (1451) – the only Sultan to abdicate.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.69; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.49-50; app notes 1:109 (sub 3)
## SUB 4 | Lodi Dynasty – Bahlol and Ibrahim; Order
[4:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Reign | Key points
  [4:0.0] Bahlol (Bahlul) Lodi | 1451-89 | Founder; Governor of Punjab (Sirhind, Lahore); throne from Alam Shah; conquered Jaunpur; longest reign; Bahlul coin
  [4:0.1] Sikandar Lodi | 1489-1517 | Son of Bahlol; Agra, Gaz-i-Sikandari, Gulrukhi
  [4:0.2] Ibrahim Lodi | 1517-26 | Son of Sikandar; arrogant to nobles; killed at Panipat
[4:1] - **Order of the Afghan rulers:** **Bahlol → Sikandar → Ibrahim** (UPSC 2006 ×2).
[4:2] - **Last Lodi ruler and last Delhi Sultan:** **Ibrahim Lodi** (SSC 2001).
[4:3] - **First Lodi Sultan = Bahlol**, so "Ibrahim was the first Lodi Sultan" ✗ (Sachivalayam 2019).
[4:4] - Lodis = **first Afghan** dynasty; Sultan = "**first among equals**" among Afghan nobles.
[4:5] # Ibrahim Lodi
[4:6] - Nobles plotted to enthrone his brother **Jalal Khan**; Ibrahim seized **Gwalior** (which sheltered Jalal) and made **Vikramajit** a vassal.
[4:7] - **Khatoli (Khatauli), 1517-18:** **Rana Sanga** defeated Ibrahim (RAS 2012) – not Sarangpur, Siwana or Khanua.
[4:8] - Contemporaries: **Krishnadevaraya**, **Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk** (Golconda), **Rana Sanga** (not Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq).
[4:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.70; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.50-51; app notes 1:109 (sub 4)
