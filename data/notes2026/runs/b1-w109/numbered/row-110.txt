## SUB 0 | The Five Dynasties – Order, Periods, Founders
[0:0] - **Delhi Sultanate:** **1206-1526**; opened by **Second Tarain (1192)**, ended at **First Panipat (1526)**.
[0:1] TABLE head: Order | Dynasty | Period | Founder | Origin
  [0:1.0] 1 | Slave / Mamluk (Ilbari) | 1206-1290 | Qutbuddin Aibak | Turk
  [0:1.1] 2 | Khalji | 1290-1320 | Jalaluddin Khalji (crowned at Kilokhari) | Turk (Khalji tribe settled in Afghanistan)
  [0:1.2] 3 | Tughlaq | 1320-1414 | Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (Ghazi Malik) | Turk (father a Turkish slave)
  [0:1.3] 4 | Sayyid | 1414-1451 | Khizr Khan (Timur's viceroy) | Claimed descent from the Prophet
  [0:1.4] 5 | Lodi | 1451-1526 | Bahlol (Bahlul) Lodi | Afghan – the only Afghan dynasty
[0:2] - **Order:** Slave → Khalji → Tughlaq → Sayyid → Lodi (codes: Slave, Khalji, Sayyid, Lodi = **4, 1, 3, 2**).
[0:3] - **Longest dynasty:** **Tughlaq**, about **94 years**; **shortest:** **Khalji**, about **30 years** (Sayyids 37).
[0:4] - **Longest reign:** **Bahlol Lodi**, **38 years (1451-89)** – not Firoz (1351-88), Alauddin (1296-1316), MBT (1325-51) or Balban (1266-87).
[0:5] - **Ghori dynasty** never ruled from Delhi as a Delhi dynasty – Muhammad Ghori was only the overlord before 1206; Ghori's Indian conquests passed to **Aibak**.
[0:6] - **Slave phases:** **Qutbi** (1206-11, Aibak), **First Ilbari** (1211-66, Iltutmish), **Second Ilbari** (1266-90, Balban).
[0:7] - **Sayyids** = **Kulah-Daran** (wore a pointed cap, **kulah**).
[0:8] - **Before the Sultanate:** Delhi first became a capital under the **Tomara Rajputs**; under the **Tomaras and Chauhans** Delhi became an **important commercial centre** (coins **dehliwal**). Khaljis – **market control**; Tughlaqs – Sultanate **extended to the far south**; Sayyids – founded by **Khizr Khan**.
[0:9] - **Succession:** Slave: Aibak → Aram Shah → Iltutmish → Ruknuddin Firoz → Razia → Bahram Shah → Alauddin Masud → Nasiruddin Mahmud → Balban → Kaiqubad. Khalji: Jalaluddin → Alauddin → Shihabuddin (child set up by **Malik Kafur**) → Mubarak Shah. Sayyid: Khizr Khan → **Mubarak Shah** → Muhammad Shah → Alam Shah. Lodi: Bahlol → Sikandar → Ibrahim.
[0:10] - **Two Nasiruddin Mahmuds:** one in the **Slave** line (before Balban), one the **last Tughlaq** (Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah, 1394-1412).
[0:11] TABLE head: First / last | Ruler
  [0:11.0] First Sultan | Qutbuddin Aibak (master Muhammad Ghori)
  [0:11.1] First woman ruler | Razia (daughter of Iltutmish)
  [0:11.2] First Afghan ruler | Bahlol Lodi
  [0:11.3] Last Slave ruler | Kaiqubad, killed 1290
  [0:11.4] Last Khalji | Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah, killed by Khusrau Khan
  [0:11.5] Last Tughlaq | Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah (1394-1412)
  [0:11.6] Last Sultan | Ibrahim Lodi, Panipat 1526

## SUB 1 | Slave Dynasty – Aibak, Iltutmish, Razia, Balban
[1:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Key facts
  [1:0.0] Qutbuddin Aibak (1206-10) | Turkish slave of Muhammad Ghori; got Ghori's Indian territories; capital Lahore; Lakh Baksh; built Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque and Arhai Din ka Jhonpra; began Qutub Minar; patron of Hasan Nizami and Fakhr-i-Mudabbir; died playing chaugan
  [1:0.1] Iltutmish (1211-36) | "Slave of a slave" (slave of Aibak, Ghori's slave), Aibak's son-in-law; real founder of the Sultanate; first to shift the capital to Delhi; silver tanka, copper jital; completed Qutub Minar; created the Forty; caliph's investiture 1229
  [1:0.2] Razia (1236-40) | Daughter of Iltutmish; first and only woman ruler; married Malik Altunia (governor of Bhatinda)
  [1:0.3] Balban (1266-87) | Zil-i-Ilahi; blood and iron; crushed Tughril Khan of Bengal (1279); broke the Forty; chain of forts on the Mongol frontier
[1:1] - **Aibak:** born in **Turkistan**; sold to the Qazi of Nishapur, then to Ghori; ruled largely as **Malik / Sipahsalar**; also called **Quran Khwan** (recited the Quran melodiously).
[1:2] - **Iltutmish's rivals:** **Tajuddin Yalduz** (Ghazni; beaten at **Tarain 1215-16**), **Nasiruddin Qabacha** (Multan and Sindh, crushed **1228**), **Ali Mardan** (Lakhnauti) – **Malik Altunia was not one** (he was Razia's husband).
[1:3] - **Chengiz (Genghis) Khan** reached the **Indus in 1221** under **Iltutmish**, chasing Khwarezm prince **Jalaluddin Mangbarni**; Iltutmish **refused him shelter**.
[1:4] - **Qutub Minar:** begun by **Aibak** (basement/first storey), completed by **Iltutmish**; fourth storey damaged in **Firoz Shah's** time and repaired (fifth storey added); **Alauddin Khalji had no part**.
[1:5] - **Alauddin Masud Shah** (1242-46): coins carried the name of **al-Musta'sim, the last Abbasid caliph of Baghdad**, for the first time.
[1:6] - **Balban:** real name **Baha-ud-din**; title **Ulugh Khan** from **Nasiruddin Mahmud**, whom he succeeded; Balban's frontier policy was **adopted and completed by Alauddin**.
[1:7] - Other battles: **Chandawar 1194** – Ghori beat **Jaichand of Kannauj**; Second Tarain 1192 – Ghori beat **Prithviraj Chauhan**.
[1:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.53-58; NextGen Indian History - APPSC 05 p.12; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.62; app notes 1:110 (sub 2, 7, 8)
## SUB 2 | Khaljis – Jalaluddin and Alauddin
[2:0] - **Jalaluddin Khalji** (1290-96): took the throne at **70**; first Sultan **benevolent to Hindus**; murdered by nephew-son-in-law Alauddin at **Kara (1296)**.
[2:1] - **Alauddin Khalji** (1296-1316): **market control** (price-fixed markets), **prohibited liquor**, harsh punishments; **largest standing army paid in cash** to guard the frontier against the Mongols; land revenue **one-half**; **dagh** and **huliya**.
[2:2] - **Titles:** **Sikandar-i-Sani** (Alexander the Second) on his coins; also **Yamin-ul-Khilafat Nasir-i-Amir-ul-Mominin**.
[2:3] - **New religion:** wanted to found one and conquer the world; dropped the idea on the advice of **Ala-ul-Mulk** (kotwal of Delhi) – the **ulema would oppose it** (Barani).
[2:4] - **South:** **Malik Kafur** (his general) led campaigns against Devagiri, **Warangal**, Dwarasamudra and Madurai.
[2:5] - Poet **Amir Khusrau** (Tuti-i-Hind) is chiefly associated with Alauddin's court.
[2:6] - Built **Siri** and the **Alai Darwaza**.
[2:7] > Alauddin is the commonest answer: market reforms, cash-paid army, Sikandar-i-Sani, half land revenue, ghari.
[2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 03 p.58-63; NextGen Indian History - APPSC 05 p.12; PMF Ancient & Medieval 04 p.62; app notes 1:110 (sub 2)
## SUB 3 | Tughlaqs – Ghiyasuddin, Muhammad bin Tughlaq, Firoz Shah
[3:0] TABLE head: Sultan | Key facts
  [3:0.0] Ghiyasuddin (Ghazi Malik) (1320-25) | Founder; built Tughlaqabad; first canal-builder of the 14th century; improved the post
  [3:0.1] Muhammad bin Tughlaq (1325-51) | Capital Delhi → Daulatabad (Devagiri) 1327; token currency (copper/brass, 1329-30); Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi (agriculture); built Jahanpanah (fourth city of Delhi); completed the conquest of South India (Sultanate at its largest); famine relief (taccavi loans)
  [3:0.2] Firoz Shah (1351-88) | Maximum canals; brought two Ashokan pillars (Topra, Meerut) to Delhi; jizya on Brahmins; most slaves (1,80,000; Diwan-i-Bandagan); translation of Sanskrit works into Persian; employment exchange; towns Hisar, Firozabad, Fatehabad, Jaunpur
[3:1] - **MBT** was the **first Sultan to join the public celebration of Holi**; consorted with jogis and Jain monk **Jinaprabha Suri**; visited the Ajmer shrine.
[3:2] - **Badauni** on MBT's death (1351): "**the king was freed from his people and they from their king**".
[3:3] - **Ibn Battuta** came in MBT's reign.
[3:4] - MBT was succeeded by his **cousin Firoz**, not his uncle.

## SUB 4 | Timur, Sayyids and Lodis
[4:0] - **Timur invaded in 1398** during the reign of **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah Tughlaq**, the last Tughlaq – **not MBT, Firoz or Bahlul Lodi**; Mahmud fled; Timur left **Khizr Khan** as governor of Multan.
[4:1] - **Khizr Khan** took Delhi and founded the **Sayyid** dynasty (1414); Sayyids fought the Khokhars.
[4:2] - **Bahlol Lodi** (1451-89): first Afghan Sultan; annexed **Jaunpur (Sharqi kingdom, 1476)**.
[4:3] - **Sikandar Lodi** (1489-1517): founded **Agra (1504)**; pen name **Gulrukhi**; orthodox; Delhi Sultan when **Vasco da Gama reached Calicut (17 May 1498)**.
[4:4] - **Ibrahim Lodi** (1517-26): beaten by **Rana Sanga at Khatoli (1517)**; killed at **Panipat 1526** by Babur – the **last Sultan**.
[4:5] - **Rajon ki Baoli**, Mehrauli: stepwell built **1506** by Daulat Khan in **Sikandar Lodi's** reign.
[4:6] - **1498 elsewhere:** Vijayanagara ruled by **Immadi Narasimha** (Saluva), not Deva Raya II (d. 1446).
[4:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 05 p.13 (Timur, Sayyids, Lodis); PMF Ancient & Medieval part4 (Timur, Khizr Khan); app notes 1:110 (sub 6, 8, 9)

## SUB 6 | Breakaway Kingdoms and the Andhra Angle
[6:0] - **Bengal:** **Tughril Khan** revolted (1279) against Balban; later **Haji Iliyas** made Bengal independent; **Firoz failed** in two campaigns.
[6:1] - **Vijayanagara (1336):** Harihara and Bukka, sent by MBT, returned to Hinduism under **Vidyaranya**; their settlement **Anegondi** grew into Vijayanagara. **Bahmani (1347)** – Deccan revolt.
[6:2] - **Jaunpur (Sharqi):** founded by **Khwaja Jahan** under the last Tughlaq; annexed by Bahlol (1476).
[6:3] - **AP angle:** Delhi invaded Andhra **five times (1303-1323)**; in **1323 Ulugh Khan** (future MBT) captured **Prataparudra II** and annexed the Kakatiya kingdom.
[6:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC 05 p.13; app notes 1:110 (sub 9)
