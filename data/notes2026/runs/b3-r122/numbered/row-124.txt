## SUB 0 | Rythu Bazars
[0:0] - **Concept:** **direct marketing between farmers and consumers**, no middlemen → **better price to farmers and fair price to consumers**.
[0:1] - **Started 1999**; first Rythu Bazars **functioning from January 1999**.
[0:2] - **Ryot Bazar idea first in Punjab (Apni Mandi)**; **AP was the second** State.
[0:3] - Run day-to-day by an **Estate Officer**; district coordinating officer = **Joint Collector** (not the District Collector); outside the purview of Agricultural Market Committees.
[0:4] - **Pricing:** about **25% above wholesale** and **25% below local retail** prices.
[0:5] - **Number:** 124 functioning, 28 under construction (2024-25); 129 (2025-26).
[0:6] - **Price intervention:** the State's quickest channel – government procures **perishables** through them when prices swing.

  [0:7.1] Managed by | Agricultural Market Committee | Estate Officer

## SUB 1 | Market Yards, Market Committees and the 1966 Act
[1:0] - Farmers selling alone face **bargaining imbalance**: forced sales, false weights, unrecorded prices, middlemen margins.
[1:1] TABLE head: Act / rule | Year | Provision
  [1:1.0] AP (Agricultural Produce and Livestock) Markets Act | 1966 | Act No. 16 of 1966; crops, livestock and products
  [1:1.1] AP (AP&L) Markets Rules | 1969 | Under Section 33 of the Act; licensing
  [1:1.2] Three central farm laws | 2020 | Repealed Nov 2021 → 1966 framework remains operative
  [1:1.3] Model APMC Act | 2003 | Single market fee, registration in place of licensing
[1:2] - **1966 Act mechanisms:** open auction, standard weights, recorded prices, **licensed buyers**.
[1:3] - **Setting up:** notified area → Market Committee → notified market (yard). District supervision: **DATMO**.
[1:4] - **Market fee: 1%**; Rs 433.39 crore (2024-25 to Dec 2024). AMCs pay **25%** of income (10% before **Oct 2015**) into the **Central Market Fund**.
[1:5] - **Adoni** = second largest market yard; **Guntur Mirchi Yard** = Asia's largest chilli market; **Agri Export Zone for red chillies** in Guntur.
[1:6] - **Pulpwood trading (Feb 2016):** **Subabul, Eucalyptus and Casuarina** sold to paper mills through market committees – **not bamboo**.

## SUB 2 | Cooperative Marketing – MARKFED and Price Stabilisation
[2:0] - **AP MARKFED** (AP State Cooperative Marketing Federation Ltd) = **apex organisation of the Cooperative Marketing Societies**; supplies inputs, cattle feed, fertilisers; works through RSKs and PACS.
[2:1] - **13 DCMSs** procure produce at minimum administered prices, also on behalf of MARKFED.
[2:2] - **Price Stabilisation Fund:** **Rs 3,000 crore** (2019-20); **MARKFED** played the key role; protects MSP for **turmeric, minor millets, sweet orange, banana, onion**; 2019 – groundnut farmers of 7 districts compensated.

## SUB 3 | Pledge Loans, Storage and Cold Storage
[3:0] - **Rythu Bandhu Pathakam** (Raitu Bandhu): **pledge loan** scheme – farmer stores produce in **market-committee godowns** and borrows against it (hypothecation); avoids distress sale.
[3:1] - **Capital Intensive Subsidy Scheme for cold storages:** introduced **July 1999**.
[3:2] - **APSWC:** set up **August 1958**; share capital **50:50 GoAP and CWC**.

## SUB 4 | e-Marketing – e-NAM and eFarmarket
[4:0] - **e-NAM:** 1,656 mandis in 23 States and 4 UTs; **AP 33 markets**; each AP mandi got free software + **Rs 30 lakh**; 39-52 FPOs registered.
[4:1] - **Mostly intra-mandi:** most e-NAM trades are within one mandi; AP: only **11 mandis** in inter-mandi trade (Rs 13.98 crore, Dec 2024). Helped **price discovery**, not yet payments or inter-mandi trade.
[4:2] - **Model APLM Act 2017:** makes the **whole State a single market**, private markets, e-trading, unified licence – i.e. pushes **inter-mandi and inter-State** trade, not intra-mandi.
[4:3] - **eFarmarket:** AP = **first State** with an e-marketing platform linking its farmers to traders across India (APFEVCL SPV, 2021). **CM APP** (Continuous Monitoring of Agricultural Prices and Procurement): farm-gate prices collected **daily from every RBK** – AP the only State.
