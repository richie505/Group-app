- **Peshwa's end:** Lord Hastings **removed Baji Rao II**, annexed his lands and sent him as a pensioner to **Bithur near Kanpur**; **Pratap Singh** given the small state of **Satara**. Baji Rao II died **1851**; adopted son **Nana Sahib** was refused the pension.
- **Marathas = the power that opposed the British most vigorously;** the British took India from the **Marathas, not the Mughals**; after **1818** British paramountcy was unchallenged.
- **Why the Marathas lost:** inept leaders (Baji Rao II, Daulatrao Sindhia, Jaswantrao Holkar), loose confederacy, inferior artillery and military system, superior English diplomacy and spies.
- **Chronology (asked):** Wandiwash **1760** → Nand Kumar hanged **1775** → Salbai **1782** → Tipu's death **1799**.
- **Treaties in order:** Salbai **1782** → Seringapatam **1792** → Bassein **1802** → Amritsar **1809** (and 1846) → Sugauli 1815-16 → Yandabo 1826.
- **Years match:** 1775 First Anglo-Maratha; **1780** Second Anglo-Mysore; **1824** First Anglo-Burmese; **1838** First Anglo-Afghan.
> **Arthur Wellesley** (Assaye; later Duke of Wellington) is not **Lord (Richard) Wellesley**, the Governor-General.

=== Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley, 1798-1805)
- **Introduced as a system by Lord Wellesley** (Governor-General **1798-1805**); not invented by him – **Dupleix** first hired out troops; **Awadh (1765)** was the first state in the protection trap; Cornwallis (Carnatic, 1787) first barred foreign relations.
- **Terms:** British force permanently stationed in the state, paid for by a **subsidy or ceded territory**; a **British Resident** at the court; no Europeans in service without consent; no war or negotiation without the Governor-General; in return protection and (nominal) non-interference.
- **Order of acceptance:** **Hyderabad (Sept 1798, 1800)** → **Mysore (1799)** → Tanjore (1799) → **Awadh (Nov 1801)** → **Peshwa (Bassein, Dec 1802)** → Bhonsle (Dec 1803) → **Sindhia (Feb 1804)** → Rajput states, Holkar (1818). Hyderabad was the **first**.
- **Wellesley against Napoleon:** while Napoleon broke the empires of Europe, Wellesley's forward policy (Mysore 1799, Marathas 1803-05, Tanjore, Surat, Carnatic) **kept the British flag flying high** – "converted the British Empire in India into the British Empire of India".
- **Effects:** states lost independence and revenue; rulers became irresponsible; the Company spread its troops at strategic points and kept the French out.

=== Anglo-Nepal War and Treaty of Sugauli; Burmese and Afghan Wars
- **Anglo-Nepal War (1814-16):** under **Lord Hastings** (Governor-General 1813-23); Gorkhas had taken **Butwal and Sheoraj**; **Ochterlony** forced terms after Almora (1815).
- **Treaty of Sugauli (Sagauli):** signed **2 Dec 1815**, ratified **March 1816** – with **Nepal**. Nepal accepted a **British Resident** (at Kathmandu), ceded **Garhwal and Kumaon**, gave up claims to the **Terai**, withdrew from **Sikkim**.
- **Gains:** empire reached the Himalayas; trade with Central Asia; **sites for hill stations – Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital** (Spectrum); **Ranikhet and Nainital** are in Kumaon; **Kathmandu stayed Nepal's capital**; Gorkhas joined the army.
| War | Years | Governor-General | Treaty |
|---|---|---|---|
| First Anglo-Burmese | **1824-26** | **Amherst** | **Yandabo (1826)** – Assam, Manipur, Arakan, Tenasserim |
| First Anglo-Afghan | **1838/39-42** | **Auckland** (Tripartite Treaty 1838) | British disaster; ended under Ellenborough |
> Sugauli 1815 (signed) / 1816 (ratified) – exams use both years; Yandabo is **1826**, not 1824 (the year the war began).

=== Conquest of Sindh (1843)
- **Governor-General: Lord Ellenborough (1842-44)**; general **Sir Charles Napier** defeated the **Talpur Amirs** at **Miani** and **Dabo** (1843); **Sindh annexed 1843**, Napier its first governor.
- **Steps:** Treaty of "eternal friendship" (1809, Minto) → **Pottinger's treaty (1832, Bentinck)** – Indus trade → **protectorate (1838, Auckland)** → **subsidiary alliance (Feb 1839)** – force at Shikarpur and Bukkar → **Outram**'s new treaty → war and annexation.
- **Motives:** recover prestige after the **First Afghan War**, counter **Russia**, control the **Indus** – not the Doctrine of Lapse. Called the **"Tail of the Afghan Storm"**.
- **Napier:** "We have no right to seize Sindh, yet we shall do so ... a very advantageous, useful, humane piece of rascality".

=== Ranjit Singh and the Anglo-Sikh Wars
- **Treaty of Amritsar (25 April 1809)** – Ranjit Singh and the Company (**Minto I**, envoy **Charles Metcalfe**): **Sutlej** the boundary. **Tripartite Treaty (June 1838)** with Auckland and Shah Shuja.
- **Bentinck** met Ranjit Singh at **Ropar (1831)** – treaty of "perpetual friendship".
- Army trained by **Ventura and Allard**; model brigade **Fauj-i-Khas**. Ranjit Singh died **June 1839** → Kharak Singh, Nau Nihal Singh, Sher Singh, then **Duleep Singh** (regent **Rani Jindan**) – the **last Sikh ruler**.
| | First War (1845-46) | Second War (1848-49) |
|---|---|---|
| Governor-General | **Lord Hardinge I** | **Lord Dalhousie** |
| Cause | Sikh army **crossed the Sutlej (11 Dec 1845)** | **Mulraj**'s revolt at **Multan** (murder of Vans Agnew and Anderson) |
| Battles | Mudki, Ferozeshah, Aliwal, **Sobraon (10 Feb 1846)** – lost by the treachery of **Lal Singh and Teja Singh** | Ramnagar, **Chillianwala (13 Jan 1849, Gough vs Sher Singh – indecisive)**, **Gujarat (21 Feb 1849)** – decisive |
| Result | **Treaty of Lahore (March 1846)**: Jalandhar Doab, indemnity, Resident **Henry Lawrence**; **Kashmir sold to Gulab Singh for Rs 75 lakh (Treaty of Amritsar, 16 March 1846)**; **Bhairowal (Dec 1846)** – Resident the virtual ruler | **Punjab annexed (29 March 1849)**; Duleep Singh pensioned and sent to England; **Kohinoor** to Queen Victoria |
- **Order asked:** First Anglo-Sikh War (1845-46) → Santhal revolt (1855-56) → Lytton's Delhi Durbar (1877) → Ilbert Bill (1883).

=== Paramountcy – Lord Hastings and the Rajput States
- **Lord Hastings (1813-23)** turned subsidiary alliances into **paramountcy**: ended non-intervention, crushed the **Pindaris** (Amir Khan, Karim Khan, Chitu) and the Marathas, imposed humiliating treaties on the Peshwa and Sindhia.
- **Rajputana (1817-18):** Udaipur, Jaipur, Jodhpur, Bundi and others accepted **subordinate alliances**, negotiated by **Charles Metcalfe** – British protection in return for giving up **external relations**.
- States kept **internal administration** but had **no international life**; the British intervened **to give effect to their international commitments** (and on **misgovernance** – Mysore 1831, Coorg 1834, Awadh 1856).

=== Dalhousie (1848-56) – Doctrine of Lapse and Annexations
- **Doctrine of Lapse:** an **adopted son** could inherit the ruler's private property but **not the state**; a dependent state without a natural heir **lapsed** to the Company. Applied by **Lord Dalhousie** (not its originator – Kittur 1824 is an earlier case).
| State | Year |
|---|---|
| **Satara** | **1848** (first) |
| Jaitpur, Sambalpur | 1849 |
| Baghat | 1850 |
| Udaipur (Chhattisgarh, not Mewar) | 1852 |
| **Jhansi** | **1853** |
| **Nagpur** | **1854** |
# Four grounds of annexation
| Ground | Cases |
|---|---|
| Conquest | Sindh 1843 (Ellenborough), **Punjab 1849**, Lower Burma / Pegu 1852 |
| Lapse | Satara to Nagpur; Jhansi |
| Misgovernance | Mysore (1831), Coorg + Central Cachar (1834) – Bentinck; **Awadh (1856)** – Wajid Ali Shah exiled |
| Abolition of titles/pensions | **Carnatic and Tanjore (1855)**; Nana Sahib's pension |
- **Sikkim (1850):** Dalhousie annexed parts of Sikkim (Terai, Darjeeling area) on the excuse that its king detained **two English doctors**.
- **Awadh (1856)** came before the Indigo Revolt (1859-60), the Second Afghan War (1878-80) and the Ilbert Bill (1883).
- By 1856 British India ran from the **Hindukush to Burma**; lapse, Awadh and lost titles fed the **Revolt of 1857**; Canning **withdrew the Doctrine** after 1857.
