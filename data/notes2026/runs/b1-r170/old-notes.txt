  [170:0:0.0] Carnatic Wars (Wandiwash 1760 in the Third) | 1746–63 | 3

  [170:0:0.4] Anglo-Sikh | 1845–49 | 2
[170:0:1] - Mysore wars began **first (1767)**; the Maratha wars overlapped them.
[170:0:2] - The British effectively took India **from the Marathas, not the Mughals**; after **1818** British paramountcy was unchallenged.
[170:0:3] - **Order (BPSC 2025):** Wandiwash (1760) → Nand Kumar hanged (1775) → Salbai (1782) → Tipu's death (1799).
[170:0:4] - First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46) came before the Santhal revolt (1855–56), Lytton's Delhi Durbar (1877) and the Ilbert Bill.
## SUB 1 | Anglo-mysore Wars
[170:1:0] # Causes
[170:1:1] - British interest in **Malabar pepper and cardamom**; Mysore's threat to **Madras**; Mysore's **French** ties.
[170:1:2] - Capital: **Srirangapatnam (Seringapatam)**.
[170:1:3] # Wars → treaty and Governor-General
[170:1:4] TABLE head: War | Years | GG / commander | Treaty / result

  [170:1:4.1] Second | 1780–84 | Warren Hastings (APPSC-GS 2025) | Porto Novo (1 July 1781) – Eyre Coote beat Haidar; Treaty of Mangalore (1784) – status quo
  [170:1:4.2] Third | 1790–92 | Cornwallis (led the army himself) | Trigger: Tipu attacked Travancore (1789) – British ally, Company's only pepper supplier; Treaty of Seringapatam (1792) – Tipu gave up half his territory, paid Rs 3 crore, sent two sons as hostages
  [170:1:4.3] Fourth | 1799 | Wellesley | Feared a Tipu–Napoleon alliance; Tipu killed; Mysore partitioned, no treaty (APPSC-G1 2004)

## SUB 3 | Subsidiary Alliance and the Nizam
[170:3:0] - **Wellesley (1798–1805)** – introduced the **Subsidiary Alliance**; forward policy; kept the British flag flying while Europe fell to Napoleon.
[170:3:1] - **Order of acceptance:** **Hyderabad (1798)** → Mysore → Oudh → Scindia; **Hyderabad = first princely state**.
[170:3:2] - **First Mysore War:** Nizam + Marathas + British against Haidar.
[170:3:3] - **Third War:** Nizam Ali joined the British; got back half the Rayalaseema lands (Bellary, Gurramkonda, Gandikota, Kambham, Kadapa) under Seringapatam (1792).
[170:3:4] - **Kharda (1795):** Marathas defeated the Nizam; **Sir John Shore** kept neutral; Resident **Kirkpatrick** reported → Nizam accepted the alliance (1798).
[170:3:5] - **1800:** Nizam ceded **Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary** = **Ceded Districts (Dattamandalalu)** to pay for the force.
[170:3:6] - **1801:** Carnatic Nawab's **Nellore and Chittoor** annexed on a charge of **collusion with Tipu**; Wellesley merged Andhra into the Madras Presidency.
## SUB 4 | Anglo-nepal War and Other Wars
[170:4:0] - **Anglo-Nepal War (1814–16)** – **Lord Hastings**; first campaign (1814) without result; **1815** Almora (Kumaon) won, **Ochterlony** forced terms.
[170:4:1] - **Treaty of Sugauli** – signed **2 Dec 1815**, ratified **4 Mar 1816**; Nepal ceded **Kumaon, eastern Garhwal, parts of the Terai**; Company got **Ranikhet and Nainital** (not Kathmandu, not Shimla) (APPSC-GS 2018).
[170:4:2] - **First Anglo-Burmese (1824–26)** – **Amherst**; **Yandabo (1826)** – Assam, Manipur, Arakan, Tenasserim.
[170:4:3] - **First Anglo-Afghan (1838/39–42)** – **Auckland** (Tripartite Treaty, 1838).
[170:4:4] "1813–23" is **Lord Hastings' tenure**, not the Nepal war (1814–16).
## SUB 5 | Ranjit Singh and the Sikh State
[170:5:0] - **Treaty of Amritsar (1809)** – Ranjit Singh + British (**Minto I**); **Sutlej** fixed as the boundary.
[170:5:1] - **Khalsa army** modernised with European officers – **Ventura and Allard** (joined 1822) trained infantry and cavalry; **Fauj-i-Khas** model brigade.
[170:5:2] - **Ranjit Singh died 1839** (not 1836) → chaos.
[170:5:3] - **Successors:** **Kharak Singh** (1839–40) → **Nau Nihal Singh** (died 1840) → regent **Chand Kaur** → **Sher Singh** (1841–43) → **Duleep Singh** (1843; mother **Jind Kaur** regent) – **last Sikh ruler**.

## SUB 7 | Conquest of Sindh (1843)
[170:7:0] - **GG:** **Lord Ellenborough (1842–44)**; general **Sir Charles Napier** vs the **Amirs of Sindh**.
[170:7:1] - **Battles:** **Miani** and **Dabo**; Napier followed a **"bullying policy"**.
[170:7:2] - **Aims:** recover prestige after the **First Afghan War**, counter **Russia**, secure **Indus trade** – **not** the Doctrine of Lapse.
[170:7:3] - Called the **"Tail of the Afghan Storm"**.
## SUB 8 | Paramountcy
[170:8:0] - **Lord Hastings** turned subsidiary alliances into **paramountcy**; imposed humiliating treaties on the Peshwa and Scindia; ended non-intervention.
[170:8:1] - **Rajputana (1818):** Udaipur, Jaipur, Jodhpur and others accepted **subordinate alliances** – British protection in exchange for giving up **external relations**.
[170:8:2] - States kept internal administration but had **no international life**.
[170:8:3] - **Grounds for intervention:** to give effect to their **international commitments** (APPSC-G2 2017 key); **misgovernance** (Coorg, Awadh).
## SUB 9 | Dalhousie – Doctrine of Lapse and Annexations
[170:9:0] # Doctrine of Lapse
[170:9:1] - **Lord Dalhousie (1848–56):** a dependent state whose ruler died **without a natural heir** lapsed to the Company; an **adopted son** could not inherit the state.
[170:9:2] TABLE head: State | Year
  [170:9:2.0] Satara | 1848 (first)
  [170:9:2.1] Jaitpur, Sambalpur | 1849
  [170:9:2.2] Baghat | 1850
  [170:9:2.3] Udaipur (Chhattisgarh, not Mewar) | 1852
  [170:9:2.4] Jhansi | 1853
  [170:9:2.5] Nagpur | 1854 (Raghuji III died without heir)
[170:9:3] # Four grounds of annexation
[170:9:4] TABLE head: Ground | Cases
  [170:9:4.0] Conquest | Sindh (1843), Punjab (1849), Lower Burma/Pegu (1852)
  [170:9:4.1] Lapse | Satara to Nagpur; Jhansi
  [170:9:4.2] Misgovernance | Coorg + Central Cachar (1834, Bentinck); Awadh (1856)
  [170:9:4.3] Abolition of titles/pensions | Carnatic and Tanjore (1855); Nana Sahib's pension (after 1851)
[170:9:5] - **Sikkim (parts, 1850, e.g. Darjeeling):** Dalhousie, on the excuse that the king detained **two English doctors**.
[170:9:6] - **Awadh (1856):** wanted for its **cotton market** and agrarian wealth.
[170:9:7] # Dalhousie's imperialism → 1857
[170:9:8] - 1. Lapse annexations + **Awadh (1856)**.
[170:9:9] - 2. Loss of titles and pensions (Nana Sahib, the Mughal house).
[170:9:10] - 3. Princes, **taluqdars** and **Awadh sepoys** alienated.
[170:9:11] - 4. **Revolt of 1857**.
[170:9:12] - By **1856** British India stretched from the **Hindukush to Burma**.
[170:9:13] - Dalhousie also: **railways, telegraph, Public Works Department (1854)**.
[170:9:14] - **Order:** Awadh (1856) → Indigo Revolt (1859–60) → Second Afghan War (1878–80) → Ilbert Bill (1883).
