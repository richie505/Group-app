=== Parliamentary System – Meaning and Why India Is Parliamentary
- **Names:** **cabinet government**, **responsible government**, Westminster model – the executive stays in power only while it has a **majority (confidence) in the popular House**.
- **Defining test:** India is parliamentary because the **Council of Ministers is responsible to the Lok Sabha** (Art. 75(3); States Art. 164(2)) – not because the Lok Sabha is directly elected, Parliament can amend the Constitution or the Rajya Sabha cannot be dissolved (UPSC 2015).
- **Parliamentary executive** = an executive **dependent on the support of a majority in Parliament** – not "elected by Parliament", not "Parliament acting as executive".
- **Main principle:** **responsibility of the executive to the legislature**; the legislature controls the executive. "Legislature and Executive are independent" is the **presidential** feature.
- **Removal:** the government is **responsible to Parliament and can be removed by it** (no-confidence) – not "all parties represented", not "elected by the people", not "fixed term" (UPSC 2020).
- **Basis:** **fusion of powers** – co-operation of legislature and executive; presidential = separation of powers.
- **Source of authority:** the Constitution sets up the parliamentary system at the **Centre and in the States**; both governments draw authority from the **Constitution**.
- India = **secular, federal, parliamentary** – "presidential system" ✗; "government by experts" ✗ (ministers are amateurs). India is parliamentary, cabinet and responsible government.
- **Statements:** "President **represents the nation but does not rule** it" ✓; "executive independent of and not accountable to the legislature" ✗.
> Exam angle: "There is parliamentary government in India because…" = **CoM responsible to the Lok Sabha** (UPSC 2015 ×2); A-R UPPSC 2025: both true, R explains A.

=== Features – Nominal Executive, Collective Responsibility, Double Membership
| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Nominal and real executive | **President** = de jure head (Head of State), like the **British Monarch**; **PM + Council of Ministers** = real (de facto) executive |
| Aid and advice | **Art. 74(1)**: advice **binding** on the President |
| Majority party rule | President appoints (**Art. 75(1)**) the **leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha**, or of the **alliance** with a majority there, as PM – a convention |
| **Collective responsibility** | **Art. 75(3)**: to the **Lok Sabha only** (not both Houses, not the President, not the ruling party); ministers hold office together while they command a majority – "swim and sink together" |
| Individual responsibility | **Art. 75(2)**: ministers hold office during the **pleasure of the President** – individually responsible to the **President** (dismissed on the PM's advice) |
| Double membership | Ministers are MPs of **either House**; a **non-member** must enter Parliament within **six months** (Art. 75(5)), else ceases to be a minister |
| Leadership of the PM | Heads the Council of Ministers; **Leader of the Lok Sabha** (if a member) and chief spokesperson |
| Dissolution | Lok Sabha can be dissolved early on the PM's advice |
- **Not in the Constitution:** "PM is leader of the majority party" (convention); the **ranks** of ministers (Cabinet, State, Deputy).
- **⚠ Traps:** collective responsibility to "Parliament" is accepted only when the Lok Sabha is not offered; "Council of Ministers responsible to both Houses" ✗ (UPSC 2007 A false, R true – members of both Houses can be ministers); "Cabinet headed by the Head of State" ✗ (UPSC 2013: 1 and 2 only); "President is the real head of the executive" ✗ (APPSC G2).
- **Cabinet membership** is **not restricted** to Lok Sabha members.
> Exam angle: "collectively responsible to" (×6) = **Lok Sabha**, **Art. 75(3)**; "individually responsible to" = **President**; non-member minister = **six months**.

=== Presidential System – Features and Contrast
- President = **Head of State and Head of Government** – **single executive**; all executive power vested in him.
- **Elected** for a **fixed term** (US: electoral college, 4 years); removable only by **impeachment**; **not accountable** to the legislature; **cannot dissolve** the legislature.
- **Appoints his own Council of Ministers** (secretaries), who are not members of the legislature and are responsible only to him.
- **Principle:** **separation of powers** with **checks and balances** – President and legislature check each other (veto, Senate confirmation, impeachment). "Fusion of powers" = parliamentary; "division of powers" = federalism.
- **Origin:** first formed in the **USA** (oldest and purest form); USA has a presidential form – India, Canada and Britain are parliamentary.
- APPSC G2 key: head of State ✓, head of Government ✓, directly elected ✓, "**accountable to the legislature**" ✗ → "i, ii and iii".
| Parliamentary | Presidential |
|---|---|
| Nominal head + real head (PM) – dual executive | One person is both heads – single executive |
| Collective executive (cabinet); ministers are legislators | Secretaries outside the legislature |
| Fusion of powers | Separation of powers |
| Removable by no-confidence | Fixed tenure; only impeachment; **fixed term** |
| Lower House can be dissolved early | No dissolution |
- **Semi-presidential:** directly elected President with real powers + **PM responsible to the legislature** – **France (Fifth Republic, 1958), Russia, Sri Lanka (1978 Constitution)**; **not India** (President indirectly elected, nominal).
- **Parliamentary examples:** UK, Japan, Canada, India.

=== India Compared with Britain and the USA
| Feature | India | Britain |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Westminster model, from **Britain** – the British Parliament is the "**Mother of Parliaments**" | Parliamentary form first introduced here |
| Head of State | Indirectly elected President, 5 years | Hereditary monarch |
| Supremacy | **Constitutional supremacy** (written Constitution) – **not sovereignty of Parliament** | **Sovereignty of Parliament** |
| **Judicial review** | Courts can strike down laws; Parliament subject to the **basic structure** | Courts cannot question Acts of Parliament |
| PM from | **Either House** | **House of Commons** |
| Ministers | Non-member allowed for six months | Must be MPs |
| Shadow cabinet; legal responsibility of ministers | No | Yes |
- **Key difference (UPSC 1998):** India has **judicial review**; a real and nominal executive, collective responsibility and bicameralism exist in both.
- UK examples: ruling party can replace the PM (e.g. **Boris Johnson**, UK PM 2019-22, resigned as Conservative leader July 2022); in India removal needs loss of **Lok Sabha confidence**.
| Feature | India | USA |
|---|---|---|
| Executive | Parliamentary | Presidential |
| **Residuary powers** | **Union** (Parliament) | **States** |
| Pocket veto | President has it | President has it – **common** |
| Upper house | Nominated members (Rajya Sabha) | No nominated members in the Senate |
| Judicial review | Narrower – "**procedure established by law**" | Wider – "**due process of law**"; SC called a "**third chamber**" |
| Term limit | None for the PM | **22nd Amendment (1951)** |
- **US facts:** Constitution in force **1789** (not 1810); **revenue bills originate in the House of Representatives**; **John Quincy Adams** and **George W. Bush** were both sons of Presidents.
- **Common** to India and USA: written Constitution, federal structure, independent judiciary with judicial review, Fundamental Rights / Bill of Rights, pocket veto.
- **Records (as of 2026):** longest-serving Chief Minister = **Pawan Kumar Chamling** (Sikkim, 1994-2019); on **22 March 2026** **Narendra Modi** (8,931 days as head of an elected government, CM + PM) passed Chamling's **8,930** (app notes).

=== Merits, Demerits and the Constituent Assembly's Choice
| System | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|
| Parliamentary | **Responsible government** – executive continuously accountable; harmony of legislature and executive; prevents despotism; ready alternative government | Unstable government; no continuity of policy; **cabinet dictatorship** with a large majority; "government by amateurs"; against separation of powers |
| Presidential | **Stable, fixed-term executive**; continuity of policy; experts as secretaries | **Executive-legislature deadlock**; personality cult; irresponsible executive |
- **Main advantage** of the parliamentary form: the **executive remains responsible to the legislature** (UPSC 2017) – "continuity of policy" is the presidential merit.
- **Why the Constituent Assembly chose it:** **familiarity** from the **1919 and 1935 Acts**; vast size and diversity; avoid US-style deadlock.
- **Dr B.R. Ambedkar:** a democratic executive must satisfy **stability and responsibility**, which cannot both be maximised; the Draft preferred **more responsibility** to more stability. The parliamentary system gives a "**daily as well as a periodic assessment**" of the Government (CAD, 4 Nov 1948).
| Person / body | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Vasant Sathe** | Proposed a presidential form, 1970s-80s |
| **NCRWC** (Venkatachaliah, 2000-02) | Advised **keeping the parliamentary system** |
- **One Nation, One Election:** amendments to Arts **83, 172, 327**; critics say fixed tenures graft presidential mechanics onto the system.
- **Shamsher Singh (1974):** a Governor publicly criticising Cabinet policy runs "**counter to the parliamentary system**".
