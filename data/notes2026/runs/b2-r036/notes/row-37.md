=== Leader of the Opposition
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| First recognised | **Ram Subhag Singh** (Congress-O), **1969** – the first official LoP in the Lok Sabha |
| First under the statute | **Y.B. Chavan** (1977) – the answer most keys give for "first LoP" when Ram Subhag Singh is not offered |
| Statute | **Salary and Allowances of Leaders of Opposition in Parliament Act, 1977** – the office is **statutory** (not in the Constitution) |
| Status | Salary, allowances and facilities **equal to a Cabinet Minister** |
| Threshold | Largest opposition party with at least **one-tenth (10%)** of the House's total strength – about **55** Lok Sabha seats (Speaker's direction) |
- **⚠ Traps:** "**Morarji Desai** (1969-71) was the first LoP" ✗; **A.K. Gopalan** led the largest opposition group in the **first Lok Sabha** (CPI, 16 seats) but was **never recognised**; the largest opposition party in the first Lok Sabha was the **CPI**, not the Swatantra Party (founded 1959); "**75 members**" ✗ (it is one-tenth); 12%, 15%, 20%, 1/5, 1/15 ✗.
- **Roles:** constructive criticism and an **alternative government** (Jennings: "alternative Prime Minister"); in the USA the same functionary is the "minority leader".
- **Tools:** Question Hour, motions, **PAC chaired by an Opposition member since 1967**.
- **Monsoon 2026 (app notes):** united opposition pushed the **FCRA (Amendment) Bill, 2026** to a JPC.
> Exam angle: "first LoP" (×4) – key **Y.B. Chavan** among the usual options, ⚠ strictly Ram Subhag Singh 1969; "threshold" (×3) = **1/10**; UPSC 2018 = "B only" (1969).

=== Sitting Days and Productivity
- **Art. 85(1):** **not more than six months** may pass between the last sitting of one session and the first of the next; **no minimum** number of sitting days. States: **Art. 174**.
- Average sittings: **~135 days/year** (**1st Lok Sabha**) → **~55** (**17th**, 2019-24); the Lok Sabha now sits **60-70 days** a year vs **150+** in the **UK House of Commons**.
- **17th Lok Sabha:** Question Hour ran only about **60%** of its scheduled time.
| Monsoon Session 2026 (PRS, app notes) | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
|---|---|---|
| Productivity | **19%** (lowest in 22 years) | **39%** |
| Question Hour run | **1%** | 12% |
| Oral answers | 2 | 16 |
- **Joint sittings** held only **three** times (1961, 1978, 2002).

=== Disruptions and Suspension Rules
| House | Rule | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Lok Sabha | **373** | Speaker orders a member to **withdraw for the day** |
| Lok Sabha | **374** | Suspension by **House motion**, up to the rest of the session |
| Lok Sabha | **374A** | **Automatic** suspension for grave disorder – **5 sittings** or the rest of the session |
| Rajya Sabha | **255** | Chairman directs withdrawal **for the day** |
| Rajya Sabha | **256** | Suspension by **House motion**, not beyond the session |
- **Cost of disruption:** in **2012** the Parliamentary Affairs Minister (**Pawan Kumar Bansal**) put the cost of running Parliament at about **Rs 2.5 lakh per minute**.

=== Weak Scrutiny and Bypass Routes
| Lok Sabha | Bills referred to committees |
|---|---|
| 14th | 60% |
| **15th** | **71% (highest)** |
| 16th | 25% |
| **17th** | **16%** |
- 17th LS: **58%** of Bills passed within two weeks of introduction; **35%** debated for under an hour.
- India: **less than one-fifth** of Bills go to committees; **UK**: every Bill examined by committees. Regular **DRSC** system only from **1993**.
- **Delegated legislation:** the legislature delegates **rule-making power to the executive** under a parent Act; its growth (with **ordinances, Art. 123**) is a cause of Parliament's decline.
- **Ordinances:** proposals to define "immediate necessity", let courts test urgency per **Krishna Kumar Singh (2017)**, publish reasons.
- **Money Bill route (Art. 110):** can **bypass the Rajya Sabha** for laws not strictly within Art. 110 – e.g. the **Aadhaar Act, 2016**.
