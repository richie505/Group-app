=== NOTES 2:36 | Parliamentary Privileges; Anti-defection Law (tenth Schedule) and the Speaker
## SUB 0 | Parliamentary Privileges
[36:0:0] TABLE head: Article | Subject
  [36:0:0.0] 105 / 194 | Privileges, freedom of speech, immunity for anything said or voted – Parliament / State Legislatures
  [36:0:0.1] 122 / 212 | Courts cannot question proceedings for procedural irregularity
[36:0:1] - **Not codified** in one law; rest on **conventions** (House of Commons), rules, statutes, court rulings.
[36:0:2] - **Notice**: LS **Rule 222** / RS **Rule 187**, usually after **Question Hour**.
[36:0:3] - Procedure: Presiding Officer consents → House or **Privileges Committee** recommends → **House decides**. RS committee: **10** members, usually chaired by the **Deputy Chairman**; LS: **15** nominated by Speaker.
[36:0:4] - **Contempt** is wider (any act obstructing the House or members); **breach** = breaking a specific privilege.
[36:0:5] - Punishments: **admonition, reprimand, imprisonment, suspension/expulsion**.
[36:0:6] - **Freedom from arrest:** only in **civil** cases, not criminal or preventive detention; during session and **40 days** before and after; outsiders too can be punished for breach.
[36:0:7] - **Collective privileges** (of the House): publish its debates and stop others publishing them; **exclude strangers** and hold secret sittings; receive **immediate information of a member's arrest**, detention or release; make its own rules; punish members and outsiders for breach or contempt.
[36:0:8] - **Individual privileges** (of members): freedom of speech; freedom from arrest in **civil cases** (40 days before and after a session); **exemption from jury service**; may decline to give evidence in a court while the House is in session.
[36:0:9] TABLE head: Case | Holding
  [36:0:9.0] Raja Ram Pal (2007) | Privilege decisions reviewable only for gross illegality or unconstitutionality
  [36:0:9.1] Sita Soren (2024) | 7 judges: no immunity for bribery to speak or vote; overruled P.V. Narasimha Rao (1998)
[36:0:10] Rule 222 = LS; Rule 187 = RS.
## SUB 1 | Making of the Anti-defection Law
[36:1:0] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [36:1:0.0] 1967 | "Aaya Ram Gaya Ram" (Haryana); Y.B. Chavan Committee (1967-69)
  [36:1:0.1] 1973, 1978 | Anti-defection Bills lapsed
  [36:1:0.2] 1979 | Jammu and Kashmir – first State with its own law
  [36:1:0.3] 1984 | AP: Nadendla Bhaskara Rao toppled NTR; NTR restored after a month
  [36:1:0.4] 1985 | 52nd Amendment: Tenth Schedule; amended Arts 101, 102, 190, 191; assent 15 Feb 1985, in force 1 March 1985
  [36:1:0.5] 2003 | 91st Amendment tightened it
[36:1:1] - Aim: curb defections "induced by the **lure of office**", ensure party discipline and stable governments.
[36:1:2] 52nd Amendment (1985) = **first constitutional recognition of political parties** (APPSC 2019).
[36:1:3] Old Sikkim "Tenth Schedule" = **35th Amendment (1974)**, omitted by the **36th (1975)**.

## SUB 3 | Coverage and Grounds
[36:3:0] - Covers **both Houses of Parliament** and **State Legislatures**, incl. **MLCs**; party, **independent** and **nominated** members.
[36:3:1] TABLE head: Member | Disqualified for | Exception
  [36:3:1.0] Party member | Voluntarily giving up membership (from conduct); voting/abstaining against whip (Para 2(1)(b)) | Prior permission, or party condones within 15 days
  [36:3:1.1] Independent | Joining any party after election | May become a Minister without joining a party
  [36:3:1.2] Nominated | Joining a party after 6 months | Within 6 months allowed
  [36:3:1.3] Presiding officer | Joining another party | May leave party on election, rejoin after office (Para 5)
[36:3:2] - Giving up membership is possible **without resigning** – e.g. campaigning for a rival party.
## SUB 4 | Merger vs Split
[36:4:0] TABLE head: Exception | Rule | Status
  [36:4:0.0] Merger (Para 4) | Original party merges + two-thirds of legislature party agree | In force
  [36:4:0.1] Split (Para 3) | One-third break away | Deleted, 91st (2003)
[36:4:1] - Criticism: the two-thirds rule can defend **mass defection**.
[36:4:2] Merger needs **two-thirds**, not one-third.
[36:4:3] - **Main criticism of the law:** it stifles intra-party dissent and members' free expression, yet it has not stopped mass defections (the two-thirds merger route).
## SUB 5 | Speaker As Deciding Authority
[36:5:0] TABLE head: Basis | Defection | General disqualification
  [36:5:0.0] Provisions | Tenth Sch.; Arts 102(2), 191(2) | Arts 102(1), 103 / 191(1), 192
  [36:5:0.1] Decided by | Speaker / Chairman as a tribunal (Para 6) | President / Governor
  [36:5:0.2] ECI | Not consulted | Opinion binding
[36:5:1] - **Para 7** barred courts – struck down (Kihoto).
[36:5:2] - **Judicial review only after** the decision: mala fides, perversity, natural justice, constitutional mandate.
[36:5:3] - **No time limit** in the Schedule; Speakers stay party members.
[36:5:4] President/Governor have no role in defection cases; ECI gives no opinion (UPSC 2025).

=== NOTES 2:38 | Parliamentary Form of Government – Features, Merits, Demerits; Comparison with Presidential System
## SUB 0 | Meaning of Parliamentary Government
[38:0:0] - Also called **cabinet** or **responsible government**: stays in power while it has a **majority in the popular House**.
[38:0:1] - Based on **cooperation and co-ordination** of legislature and executive (**fusion of powers**); presidential = **separation of powers**.
[38:0:2] - India adopted the **British** model (influenced by the UK); parliamentary system at the **Centre and in the States**; both get power from the Constitution.
[38:0:3] Defining test: India is parliamentary because the **executive is responsible to the legislature** (Council of Ministers headed by PM, to the Lok Sabha) – A and R both true, R explains A (UPPSC 2025).
## SUB 1 | Features of Parliamentary Government (india)
[38:1:0] - 1. **Nominal and real executive**: **President** = de jure head (Head of State); **PM + Council of Ministers** = real executive. Executive powers of the Union are vested in the President.
[38:1:1] - 2. **Art. 74(1)**: President acts on **aid and advice**; advice **binding**.
[38:1:2] - 3. **Majority party rule**: leader of the majority in the Lok Sabha becomes PM.
[38:1:3] - 4. **Collective responsibility** to the Lok Sabha (**Art. 75**); ministers individually responsible to the **President**.
[38:1:4] - 5. **Dual membership**: ministers must be MPs; a **non-member** must enter Parliament within **six months**, else stops being minister.
[38:1:5] - 6. **Leadership of the PM**: heads Council of Ministers; **Leader of the Lok Sabha**; chief spokesperson.
[38:1:6] - 7. **Dissolution** of the lower House possible.
[38:1:7] - 8. Government can be **removed by Parliament** (no-confidence); ministers stay while they keep its confidence.
[38:1:8] The President is **not** the real head of the executive (APPSC G2 2017). India is **not** a presidential system.
[38:1:9] India is democratic and has a written constitution, but the written constitution does **not** explain democracy (UK is democratic without one).
## SUB 2 | Presidential System – Features
[38:2:0] - President = **Head of State and Head of Government**; **single executive**; power centralised.
[38:2:1] - **Directly elected**; **fixed term**; removable only by **impeachment**.
[38:2:2] - **Not accountable** to the legislature; **cannot dissolve** the legislature.
[38:2:3] - **Appoints his own Council of Ministers** (secretaries); US secretaries do not sit in the legislature.
[38:2:4] APPSC G2 2025 key: head of State, head of Government, directly elected = correct; "**accountable to the legislature**" = **wrong**.
[38:2:5] # Parliamentary vs Presidential
[38:2:6] TABLE head: Parliamentary | Presidential
  [38:2:6.0] Nominal head + real head (PM) | One person is both heads
  [38:2:6.1] Collective executive (cabinet) | Single executive

  [38:2:6.3] Removable by no-confidence | Fixed tenure; only impeachment
