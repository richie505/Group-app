## SUB 0 | Meaning and Definitions
[0:0] # Indian Meaning
[0:1] - **NextGen:** communalism is the belief that because a group follows one religion it also has **common social, political and economic interests**.
[0:2] - In India, communalism is most often seen as **religious difference** between groups that leads to tension and rioting.
[0:3] - A group promotes its own interests **at the expense of others**.
[0:4] # Definition → Scholar
[0:5] TABLE head: Scholar | Definition / view
  [0:5.0] Robert Melson and Howard Wolpe | "The political assertiveness of a community to maintain its identity in a plural society undergoing modernisation" (asked in APPSC G2 2025)
  [0:5.1] Bipan Chandra | The belief that because a group follows one religion, it also has common social, political and economic interests; gave the three stages
  [0:5.2] Ram Ahuja | An ideology that society is divided into religious communities whose interests differ and often oppose each other; "communal persons" do politics through religion
  [0:5.3] T.K. Oommen | Six dimensions of communalism (assimilationist to secessionist)
  [0:5.4] W.C. Smith (*Modern Islam in India*) | Ideology that treats each religious group as the basic social, political and economic unit and stresses antagonism between such groups
[0:6] - **⚠ Trap:** The name is **T.K. Oommen**, not "T.R. Oommen". Oommen = six dimensions; Bipan Chandra = three stages.
[0:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.86; app notes 2:117 sub 0
## SUB 1 | Bipan Chandra's Three Stages
[1:0] TABLE head: Stage | Features
  [1:0.0] 1. Communal nationalism | Followers of one religion share common secular (political, economic) interests; still nationalist in outlook
  [1:0.1] 2. Liberal communalism | Interests of one community seen as different but reconcilable within one nation
  [1:0.2] 3. Extreme communalism | Interests seen as mutually antagonistic; politics of fear, hatred and violence
[1:1] - After the **1937 elections**, the Muslim League turned to extreme communalism "based on fear, hatred and violence"; echoed by the **Hindu Mahasabha and RSS**.
[1:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Order: communal nationalism → liberal communalism → extreme communalism.
[1:3] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:117 sub 1
## SUB 2 | Causes of Communalism
[2:0] TABLE head: Type | Causes
  [2:0.0] Historical / colonial | British divide and rule; separate electorates; caste and religion recorded in the first census (1871-72); 19th-century revivalist movements
  [2:0.1] Political | Vote-bank and identity politics; religion-based parties
  [2:0.2] Economic | Poverty, unemployment, lopsided development, relative deprivation
  [2:0.3] Legal / administrative | Delayed justice, lenient punishment, police inaction, biased intelligence
  [2:0.4] Social / media | Fake news and hate messages on social media; media amplifying communal narratives
  [2:0.5] Psychological / international | Prejudices, rumours; foreign funding
[2:1] - **1920s:** Arya Samaj **Shuddhi and Sangathan** vs Muslim **Tabligh and Tanzim**.

## SUB 3 | Growth in Colonial India (1887-1940)
[3:0] # Lord Minto and the 1909 Act
[3:1] - **Oct 1906 – Simla Deputation** led by the **Aga Khan** to **Lord Minto** demanded separate electorates.
[3:2] - The **1909 Act** gave the **first legal separate electorate** for Muslims.
[3:3] - Lord Minto is called the "**Father of the Communal Electorate**".
[3:4] # Timeline

  [3:5.1] Oct 1906 | Simla Deputation to Lord Minto

  [3:5.3] 1909 | First legal separate electorate

## SUB 4 | Communal Award and Two-nation Theory
[4:0] - **Syed Ahmad Khan** = ideological father of the **two-nation theory** (Hindi-Urdu controversy; advised Muslims to stay out of Congress).
[4:1] - **Communal Award (16 Aug 1932):** announced by British PM **Ramsay MacDonald** (not Churchill, Attlee or Pitt); separate electorates for Muslims, Sikhs, Christians and **Depressed Classes** (for **20 years**); **71 seats** for depressed classes.
[4:2] - **Poona Pact:** replaced Dalit separate electorates with reserved seats in **joint electorates**; **71 → 148** reserved seats in provincial legislatures.
[4:3] - **⚠ Trap:** 71 = Communal Award seats; 148 = Poona Pact reserved seats; 20 years = Award's separate electorates
[4:4] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India l.16269 (Ramsay MacDonald); app notes 2:117 sub 4
## SUB 5 | Forms – Oommen's Six Dimensions
[5:0] TABLE head: Dimension | Meaning | Example
  [5:0.0] Assimilationist | Small religious groups absorbed into a larger one | Claiming STs, Jains, Sikhs, Buddhists are Hindus
  [5:0.1] Welfarist | Works for one community's welfare only | Christian or Parsi associations
  [5:0.2] Retreatist | Small community keeps away from politics | Bahai community
  [5:0.3] Retaliatory | Tries to harm members of other communities | Riots, revenge cycles
  [5:0.4] Separatist | Separate territorial state within India | Gorkhaland, Bodoland
  [5:0.5] Secessionist | Independent state outside India | Khalistan, "Azad Kashmir"
[5:1] - Other forms: **Political** = uses religion for votes; **Economic** = grows from competition for jobs and resources.
[5:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Separatist = within India; Secessionist = outside India.
[5:3] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:117 sub 5
## SUB 6 | Religious Fundamentalism vs Communalism
[6:0] - **Fundamentalism:** strict adherence to religious beliefs; often rejects modern interpretations.
[6:1] - Features: **literal reading of scripture**, conservatism, resistance to change, **group exclusiveness**.
[6:2] - Causes: **identity crisis**, globalisation, **social insecurity**, political mobilisation, economic inequality.
[6:3] TABLE head: Religious fundamentalism | Communalism
  [6:3.0] Return to "fundamentals"; mainly doctrinal | Political mobilisation of religious identity
  [6:3.1] Reforms one's own community (often anti-modern) | Directed against other communities; seeks power and resources
  [6:3.2] Can exist without inter-group conflict | Always "us vs them"
[6:4] - **⚠ Trap:** Group exclusiveness is a **feature** of fundamentalism, not a cause.
[6:5] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:117 sub 6
