## SUB 0 | Caste as Stratification – Features, Caste vs Class
[0:0] # Features of caste
[0:1] - Caste membership and status are fixed by **birth** (ascribed); based on the **Chaturvarna** (four-varna) scheme; **hierarchy** is the essence of caste (Dumont).
[0:2] - **Essential features:** hierarchy, **endogamy** ("closed marriage", "intermarriage" in Telugu-English papers = marriage within the caste), restrictions on feeding and social intercourse, hereditary occupation, **caste panchayat**, purity-pollution. **Belief in reincarnation** and "social dynamics" are **not** essential features.
[0:3] - **Closed system:** a person can **neither rise nor descend** in the hierarchy (A.W. Green: ideally no mobility up or down); caste is a **closed group** that no outsider can join.
[0:4] - **Varna** = four-fold, theoretical; **Jati** = real, local birth groups; ranking by **ritual purity and pollution**.
[0:5] - **Vertical** classification = ranking by birth; **horizontal** = division into separate caste groups.
[0:6] - Caste status is linked to **occupation**; hereditary occupations (potter, **blacksmith**, weaver) weakened by education, **industrialisation**, urbanisation, occupational mobility.
[0:7] - **Jajmani system:** barber, washerman etc. are **service castes** working for patron (jajman) families; weakened today.
[0:8] - **S.C. Dube:** ritual purity has two stages – **purification procedures** and **initiation rites**.
[0:9] - Harappa: citadel vs lower town, **no rigid caste**; varna hardened in the **Later Vedic** period.
[0:10] - Today inter-caste conflicts are about **power**, not ritual; Muslims: **Ashraf, Ajlaf, Arzal**.
[0:11] # Caste vs class
[0:12] TABLE head: System | Feature
  [0:12.0] Caste | Ascribed at birth; endogamy, inherited occupation; closed; cumulative inequality
  [0:12.1] Class | Achieved – education, wealth, occupation; open, mobility possible
  [0:12.2] Estate | Medieval Europe – clergy, nobility, commoners
  [0:12.3] Slavery | Person as property – Greece, Rome
[0:13] - Both caste and class are forms of stratification and give the individual a status; castes are **innumerable** (only the varnas are four).
[0:14] - **Social classes** = people of similar income, occupation, education.
[0:15] - **Andre Beteille:** *Caste, Class and Power* (**1965**, **Sripuram, Thanjavur**) – used **Weber's** three dimensions; caste, class and power **diverge**; also *Studies in Agrarian Social Structure*; argued **varna is a hierarchy** term and **race a classification** term.
[0:16] - **Louis Dumont**, *Homo Hierarchicus*: purity-pollution; the **Brahman is at the top**, the **king second** (status encompasses power).
[0:17] - **Ghurye**, *Caste and Race in India* (1932): hierarchy, endogamy, restrictions.

## SUB 1 | Stratification – Meaning, Bases, Theories
[1:0] - **Stratification:** ranking of groups by unequal **wealth, prestige, power**.
[1:1] - **Bases:** caste, class, **gender (sex stratification = between genders)**, **age**, race, ethnicity; traditional – caste, varna, tribe, religion; modern – education, occupation, income, **political power**, urbanisation. **Language is NOT** a basis.
[1:2] - **Differentiation vs stratification:** **sex** (biological) is differentiation; class, caste, gender are stratification.
[1:3] - **Tumin:** stratification is social, **ancient, universal**, diverse, **consequential** (decides life chances).
[1:4] TABLE head: Approach | Thinker | Idea
  [1:4.0] Functionalist | Davis and Moore (1945) | Unequal rewards pull scarce talent into key posts
  [1:4.1] Critique | Melvin Tumin | Wastes talent; passes inequality on
  [1:4.2] Conflict | Marx | Ownership of means of production; class struggle; false consciousness hides the real status of the oppressed
  [1:4.3] Multi-dimensional | Weber | Class, status, party (power/authority); market situation decides life chances
  [1:4.4] Ideological | Dumont | Purity-pollution
[1:5] # Terms
[1:6] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:6.0] Status inconsistency (Lenski) | Different ranks on different dimensions
  [1:6.1] Status group (Weber; "floor group" in translation) | Shared lifestyle; caste = a closed status group
  [1:6.2] Life chances (Weber) | Access to necessities, opportunities

## SUB 2 | Social Mobility – Types and Examples
[2:0] - **Social mobility** = **movement of people between social strata/positions**.
[2:1] TABLE head: Type | Meaning / example
  [2:1.0] Vertical (upward/downward) | Farmer's son → IAS officer
  [2:1.1] Horizontal | Change without status change: clerk to an equal job; change of religion (an untouchable converting to Christianity – Sorokin)
  [2:1.2] Inter-generational | Parents → children: labourer's/farmer's son → engineer
  [2:1.3] Intra-generational | Within one lifetime
  [2:1.4] Structural | Change in the occupational structure itself
[2:2] - **Sorokin**, *Social Mobility* (**1927**): first set out vertical and horizontal mobility.
[2:3] - **Bogardus:** social mobility as **change on the same status level** (APPSC Hostel Welfare key).
[2:4] # Status and role
[2:5] - **Ascribed status:** given at birth – **sex, age, caste**, race, kinship; **achieved**: IAS officer, doctor.
[2:6] - **Status set:** the many statuses one person holds; **role** = the behaviour pattern of the rights and duties of a status ("status is occupied, roles are played").
[2:7] - Paired statuses such as **husband-wife, guru-disciple** – reciprocal statuses (APPSC key: "processing status").
[2:8] - **Dominant caste** (Srinivas): **numerical strength**, sizeable **arable land (economic power)**, **political power**, fairly **high ritual rank**; later western education and administrative jobs.
[2:9] - **Kshatriyaisation (Hermann Kulke):** Marathas, Jats, Yadavs claim Kshatriya status – mobility that **reproduces the hierarchy**.
[2:10] - **Nomadic society:** marked by moving from place to place (migration).

## SUB 3 | Agrarian Classes and Middle Class
[3:0] - **Daniel Thorner:** by land rights and income – **Maliks** (landlords on rent), **Kisans** (own land, family labour), **Mazdoors** (tenants, sharecroppers, landless).
[3:1] - Dominant caste is a concept for **stratification in rural India**.
[3:2] - **Macaulay's Minute, 1835** → English-educated middle class (**A.R. Desai**, *Social Background of Indian Nationalism*).
[3:3] - Middle class: World Bank **$10-50**/day (PPP); NCAER **₹2-10 lakh**; PRICE **₹5-30 lakh**; **43.2 crore (31%) in 2021 → 61% by 2047**; OECD: world's largest by **2035**.

## SUB 4 | Mobility Routes, OBCs and Andhra
[4:0] - **ES 2025-26:** drivers – **health, education, technology, institutions** (not land inheritance); low mobility **entrenches historical inequality**; poverty **21.9% (2011-12) → 2.3% (2023-24)**.
[4:1] - **Mandal Commission:** OBCs **over 52%**; **11 indicators = 4 social + 4 economic + 3 educational** (social, economic, educational categories).
[4:2] - **Telangana caste survey:** **242 castes**; **135** above average backwardness.
[4:3] - **Marathas, Gujjars, Patels** seek OBC status mainly for **greater life chances**.
[4:4] - Reservation demand: **Periyar's Self-Respect Movement**; **Ezhavas** (Kerala): toddy tapping → tailoring (occupational mobility); city anonymity weakens caste.
[4:5] - **Dalits:** state policy, reform movements, Sanskritisation and Westernisation.
[4:6] - **Medieval Andhra:** **Kakatiya** society relatively open (**Velamas, Kammas, Reddys** rose); **Saivism (Virashaivism)** a "**lubricant**" of upward mobility.
