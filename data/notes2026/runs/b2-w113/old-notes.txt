=== NOTES 2:113 | Processes of Social Change – Sanskritisation, Westernisation, Modernisation, Secularisation
## SUB 0 | Social Change – Meaning, Sources and Theories
[113:0:0] # Meaning and sources
[113:0:1] - **Social change** = **alteration in the social order** (structure and relationships) of a society.
[113:0:2] TABLE head: Factor | Example
  [113:0:2.0] Technology | Industrial Revolution (Watt, Hargreaves; term popularised in English by Arnold Toynbee, 1760-1820); internet – 820 million+ users (Dec 2025)
  [113:0:2.1] Education | Spread of modern education
  [113:0:2.2] Law | Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955; widow remarriage and sati laws
  [113:0:2.3] Media | Spreads awareness, forms opinion; cultural diffusion; behaviour-change communication (ES 2025-26)
  [113:0:2.4] Migration / urbanisation | Industrialisation and urbanisation
  [113:0:2.5] Planning | Green Revolution; inclusive growth (11th Plan)
[113:0:3] - **Seasonal migration of nomads** is a routine cycle – **NOT** a source of social change.
[113:0:4] - New development model for developing societies stresses **non-material indicators** (quality of life, equity, **wellbeing**).
[113:0:5] - **ILO (2025):** India's social protection coverage **64.3%** (19% in 2015).
[113:0:6] - Dalit change came through state policy, reform movements, Sanskritisation and Westernisation.
[113:0:7] # Theories
[113:0:8] TABLE head: Theory | Proponents
  [113:0:8.0] Evolutionary (linear) | Comte (positivism, three stages, 1830s-40s), Herbert Spencer (simple → complex; coined "survival of the fittest", 1864), L.H. Morgan, Durkheim (mechanical → organic solidarity, *Division of Labour*, 1893)
  [113:0:8.1] Cyclical | Spengler (*Decline of the West*), Toynbee (challenge and response), Sorokin (sensate/ideational), Pareto (circulation of elites)
  [113:0:8.2] Diffusion | Elliot Smith, Perry, Boas
  [113:0:8.3] Technological | Veblen (*Theory of the Leisure Class*), Ogburn (adaptive culture, cultural lag)
  [113:0:8.4] Biological / evolutionary struggle | Darwin – survival of the fittest ("worthy living")
[113:0:9] - **Chronology:** Comte's positivism → Spencer → Durkheim's division of labour.
[113:0:10] - **W.G. Sumner** (*Folkways*, 1906): Social Darwinist; in-group/out-group antagonism and struggle – usually grouped with the early **conflict** school.
[113:0:11] TABLE head: Diffusionist school | Scholars | Idea
  [113:0:11.0] British | Elliot Smith, Perry | Egypt as the source
  [113:0:11.1] German-Austrian | Graebner, Schmidt | Culture circles
  [113:0:11.2] American | Boas, Wissler | Culture areas
[113:0:12] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 1 p.6-9; app notes 2:113 sub 0-1; general knowledge (Toynbee, Sumner), not checked
[113:0:13] - **Millenarian movement:** a **radical cultural (religious) movement** expecting a sudden, total transformation of society (a coming golden age) – arises in times of extreme cultural change; e.g. Birsa Munda's movement.
[113:0:14] - **Unintended (latent) result:** a literacy drive that also raises health awareness shows an **unanticipated / latent consequence** of planned change (Merton; 'Anabhivyata prakaram' in the translated APPSC paper).
[113:0:15] - **L.H. Morgan** (*Ancient Society*, 1877): three stages of socio-cultural evolution – **savagery (wild), barbarism, civilisation**.
[113:0:16] - **Change-promoting vs change-resisting movements:** reform, Dalit and women's movements promote change; an **anti-reservation movement** resists it (reactionary).
## SUB 1 | Culture Contact – Acculturation, Assimilation, Pluralism, Ethnocentrism
[113:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [113:1:0.0] Acculturation | Change through sustained contact: a group is influenced by another (dominant) culture and its way of life changes, but it stays distinct
  [113:1:0.1] Assimilation ("merger") | A group (e.g. immigrants) loses its own cultural identity and is absorbed into the host/dominant culture
  [113:1:0.2] Integration | Taking part in the wider society while retaining identity
  [113:1:0.3] Amalgamation | Biological/cultural fusion of groups into a new one ("melting pot")
  [113:1:0.4] Cultural pluralism / multiculturalism | Peaceful coexistence of distinct groups and cultures in one society
  [113:1:0.5] Cultural diffusion | Spread of culture (e.g. through media)
  [113:1:0.6] Cultural relativism | Understanding a culture on its own terms
  [113:1:0.7] Ethnocentrism | Own group seen as the centre of everything and superior; others rated by it (Sumner)
  [113:1:0.8] Xenocentrism / Xenophobia | Preferring other cultures / fear of foreigners
  [113:1:0.9] Ethnic group | Group sharing a common culture
  [113:1:0.10] Regionalism / Communalism / Casteism | Loyalty to a region / religious group / caste
[113:1:1] - **⚠ Trap:** pluralism = coexistence; amalgamation = fusion; assimilation = identity lost; acculturation = influenced but distinct.
[113:1:2] - **Ghurye**, *The Aborigines So-Called and Their Future*: argued for **tribal assimilation** (tribes as "backward Hindus").
[113:1:3] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Society p.19-20; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 2 p.36-37; app notes 2:113 sub 2
## SUB 2 | Sanskritisation and Dominant Caste (M.N. Srinivas)
[113:2:0] - **Coined by M.N. Srinivas** in *Religion and Society among the Coorgs of South India* (**1952**); developed in *Social Change in Modern India*; first called **"Brahminisation"**.
[113:2:1] - **Meaning:** a **low caste, tribe or group** adopts the customs, rituals and way of life of a higher, **twice-born** caste (**imitates the upper-caste lifestyle**, observes its rites) to claim a higher rank.
[113:2:2] - **Reference groups:** Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas or the **local dominant caste**.
[113:2:3] - A claim to higher status succeeds only when the caste wins **acceptance by others** (group recognition), usually after economic or political gains.
[113:2:4] - Brings **positional, not structural** change – **upward mobility** for a caste, hierarchy intact; **downward mobility** is no part of it.
[113:2:5] - **De-sanskritisation:** rejecting Sanskritic norms and asserting own identity (non-Brahmin / Dravidian movements).
[113:2:6] - **Dominant caste** (Srinivas): concept also his; used to study **stratification in rural India**.
[113:2:7] - Srinivas's set: **Sanskritisation + Westernisation + Secularisation + Dominant Caste**.
[113:2:8] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Society p.5, p.13, p.18; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 3 p.13-14; app notes 2:113 sub 3
## SUB 3 | Westernisation, Modernisation and Secularisation
[113:3:0] # Westernisation (Srinivas)
[113:3:1] - Changes from **150+ years of British rule** in **technology, institutions, ideology, values**; primary, secondary, tertiary Westernisation.
[113:3:2] - Source-specific (West/British) and **ethically neutral**; modernisation is **universal** and value-loaded ("progress").
[113:3:3] - The **English-educated Hindu middle class** was the first to be Westernised.
[113:3:4] # Modernisation
[113:3:5] - **Daniel Lerner**, *The Passing of Traditional Society* (**1958**): **urbanisation → literacy → media exposure → participation**; **"empathy"** = **psychic mobility**.
[113:3:6] - **Yogendra Singh**, *Modernization of Indian Tradition* (**1973**): universalistic, evolutionary cultural response; macro and micro levels.
[113:3:7] - **Milton Singer**, *When a Great Tradition Modernizes*: tradition and modernity **coexist** (Madras temple culture).
[113:3:8] - **Indicators:** rationality, science, industrialisation, urbanisation, literacy, mass media, democratic participation – **not hereditary occupation**.
[113:3:9] - **Zygmunt Bauman – "liquid modernity":** constant **mobility and change in relationships, identities and global economics** in contemporary society.
[113:3:10] # Secularisation
[113:3:11] - Decline of religion's hold on social life; differentiation and **rationalisation**; **Max Weber: "disenchantment of the world"**; Bryan Wilson – decline of organised religious participation; in India, **secularisation of caste** (caste as political pressure group).
[113:3:12] TABLE head: Sociological secularisation | Constitutional secularism
  [113:3:12.0] Change in society | Duty of the state
  [113:3:12.1] Beliefs and practices change | State neutrality and equal respect (Arts. 25-28)
[113:3:13] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 3 p.14-16; Rocket Indian Society p.14; app notes 2:113 sub 4-5, 7; general knowledge (Bauman), not checked
[113:3:14] - **How modernisation changes traditions (APPSC key B, C, D):** by **enforcing legal rules**, by **raising incomes** and by **strengthening the power of the government** – not by 'banning tribalism'.
## SUB 4 | Cultural Lag, Great and Little Traditions, Books
[113:4:0] - **W.F. Ogburn**, *Social Change* (**1922**): **material culture** (technology) changes faster; **non-material culture** trails = **delay in adjustment** = **cultural lag**; "**material culture change lies behind social change**".
[113:4:1] - Lagging parts: **religion, law, tradition** – **not politics**.
[113:4:2] - Example: fast digital spread vs slow rules on children's online safety – **DPDP Act, 2023** (parental consent **under 18**).
[113:4:3] - **Culture** = the sum of human life-ways, behaviour, beliefs, feelings and thought.
[113:4:4] TABLE head: Concept | Originator
  [113:4:4.0] Great Tradition and Little Tradition | Robert Redfield; applied to India by Singer and Marriott
  [113:4:4.1] Universalisation (little → great) and Parochialisation (great → little) | McKim Marriott
  [113:4:4.2] Sanskritisation, Westernisation, Dominant Caste | M.N. Srinivas
[113:4:5] # Books
[113:4:6] TABLE head: Book | Author
  [113:4:6.0] *The Remembered Village*; *Village, Caste, Gender and Method* | M.N. Srinivas
  [113:4:6.1] *Caste and Race in India* | G.S. Ghurye
  [113:4:6.2] *Kinship Organization in India*; *Indian Kinship System* | Irawati Karve
  [113:4:6.3] *Castes in India* (1916); *Annihilation of Caste* (1936); *Who Were the Shudras?* (1946); *The Untouchables* (1948 – origin of untouchability) | B.R. Ambedkar

=== NOTES 2:114 | Social Stratification and Mobility in India
## SUB 0 | Caste as Stratification – Features, Caste vs Class
[114:0:0] # Features of caste
[114:0:1] - Caste membership and status are fixed by **birth** (ascribed); based on the **Chaturvarna** (four-varna) scheme; **hierarchy** is the essence of caste (Dumont).
[114:0:2] - **Essential features:** hierarchy, **endogamy** ("closed marriage", "intermarriage" in Telugu-English papers = marriage within the caste), restrictions on feeding and social intercourse, hereditary occupation, **caste panchayat**, purity-pollution. **Belief in reincarnation** and "social dynamics" are **not** essential features.
[114:0:3] - **Closed system:** a person can **neither rise nor descend** in the hierarchy (A.W. Green: ideally no mobility up or down); caste is a **closed group** that no outsider can join.
[114:0:4] - **Varna** = four-fold, theoretical; **Jati** = real, local birth groups; ranking by **ritual purity and pollution**.
[114:0:5] - **Vertical** classification = ranking by birth; **horizontal** = division into separate caste groups.
[114:0:6] - Caste status is linked to **occupation**; hereditary occupations (potter, **blacksmith**, weaver) weakened by education, **industrialisation**, urbanisation, occupational mobility.
[114:0:7] - **Jajmani system:** barber, washerman etc. are **service castes** working for patron (jajman) families; weakened today.
[114:0:8] - **S.C. Dube:** ritual purity has two stages – **purification procedures** and **initiation rites**.
[114:0:9] - Harappa: citadel vs lower town, **no rigid caste**; varna hardened in the **Later Vedic** period.
[114:0:10] - Today inter-caste conflicts are about **power**, not ritual; Muslims: **Ashraf, Ajlaf, Arzal**.
[114:0:11] # Caste vs class
[114:0:12] TABLE head: System | Feature
  [114:0:12.0] Caste | Ascribed at birth; endogamy, inherited occupation; closed; cumulative inequality
  [114:0:12.1] Class | Achieved – education, wealth, occupation; open, mobility possible
  [114:0:12.2] Estate | Medieval Europe – clergy, nobility, commoners
  [114:0:12.3] Slavery | Person as property – Greece, Rome
[114:0:13] - Both caste and class are forms of stratification and give the individual a status; castes are **innumerable** (only the varnas are four).
[114:0:14] - **Social classes** = people of similar income, occupation, education.
[114:0:15] - **Andre Beteille:** *Caste, Class and Power* (**1965**, **Sripuram, Thanjavur**) – used **Weber's** three dimensions; caste, class and power **diverge**; also *Studies in Agrarian Social Structure*; argued **varna is a hierarchy** term and **race a classification** term.
[114:0:16] - **Louis Dumont**, *Homo Hierarchicus*: purity-pollution; the **Brahman is at the top**, the **king second** (status encompasses power).
[114:0:17] - **Ghurye**, *Caste and Race in India* (1932): hierarchy, endogamy, restrictions.
[114:0:18] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.2, p.32-35; Rocket Indian Society p.15, p.21-22; app notes 2:114 sub 2; general knowledge (Dube, Beteille on race, Dumont), not checked
[114:0:19] - **We-feeling** is strongest in **primary groups**, above all the **family** (Cooley); class is a loose, impersonal stratum.
## SUB 1 | Social Mobility – Types and Examples
[114:1:0] - **Social mobility** = **movement of people between social strata/positions**.
[114:1:1] TABLE head: Type | Meaning / example
  [114:1:1.0] Vertical (upward/downward) | Farmer's son → IAS officer
  [114:1:1.1] Horizontal | Change without status change: clerk to an equal job; change of religion (an untouchable converting to Christianity – Sorokin)
  [114:1:1.2] Inter-generational | Parents → children: labourer's/farmer's son → engineer
  [114:1:1.3] Intra-generational | Within one lifetime
  [114:1:1.4] Structural | Change in the occupational structure itself
[114:1:2] - **Sorokin**, *Social Mobility* (**1927**): first set out vertical and horizontal mobility.
[114:1:3] - **Bogardus:** social mobility as **change on the same status level** (APPSC Hostel Welfare key).
[114:1:4] # Status and role
[114:1:5] - **Ascribed status:** given at birth – **sex, age, caste**, race, kinship; **achieved**: IAS officer, doctor.
[114:1:6] - **Status set:** the many statuses one person holds; **role** = the behaviour pattern of the rights and duties of a status ("status is occupied, roles are played").
[114:1:7] - Paired statuses such as **husband-wife, guru-disciple** – reciprocal statuses (APPSC key: "processing status").
[114:1:8] - **Dominant caste** (Srinivas): **numerical strength**, sizeable **arable land (economic power)**, **political power**, fairly **high ritual rank**; later western education and administrative jobs.
[114:1:9] - **Kshatriyaisation (Hermann Kulke):** Marathas, Jats, Yadavs claim Kshatriya status – mobility that **reproduces the hierarchy**.
[114:1:10] - **Nomadic society:** marked by moving from place to place (migration).
[114:1:11] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Society p.18, p.23; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 1 p.14-15; app notes 2:114 sub 3; general knowledge (Sorokin on religion, Bogardus), not checked
[114:1:12] - **'Status'** is a **relational term** – a position defined in relation to other positions ('term of relations' in the translated key), not a moral, personal or ideal term.
## SUB 2 | Stratification – Meaning, Bases, Theories
[114:2:0] - **Stratification:** ranking of groups by unequal **wealth, prestige, power**.
[114:2:1] - **Bases:** caste, class, **gender (sex stratification = between genders)**, **age**, race, ethnicity; traditional – caste, varna, tribe, religion; modern – education, occupation, income, **political power**, urbanisation. **Language is NOT** a basis.
[114:2:2] - **Differentiation vs stratification:** **sex** (biological) is differentiation; class, caste, gender are stratification.
[114:2:3] - **Tumin:** stratification is social, **ancient, universal**, diverse, **consequential** (decides life chances).
[114:2:4] TABLE head: Approach | Thinker | Idea
  [114:2:4.0] Functionalist | Davis and Moore (1945) | Unequal rewards pull scarce talent into key posts
  [114:2:4.1] Critique | Melvin Tumin | Wastes talent; passes inequality on
  [114:2:4.2] Conflict | Marx | Ownership of means of production; class struggle; false consciousness hides the real status of the oppressed
  [114:2:4.3] Multi-dimensional | Weber | Class, status, party (power/authority); market situation decides life chances
  [114:2:4.4] Ideological | Dumont | Purity-pollution
[114:2:5] # Terms
[114:2:6] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [114:2:6.0] Status inconsistency (Lenski) | Different ranks on different dimensions
  [114:2:6.1] Status group (Weber; "floor group" in translation) | Shared lifestyle; caste = a closed status group
  [114:2:6.2] Life chances (Weber) | Access to necessities, opportunities
[114:2:7] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Society p.21-22; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 1 p.13-14; app notes 2:114 sub 0-1, 7
[114:2:8] - **Social stratification** is about **hierarchy and difference** – groups ranked one above another, not mere diversity.
## SUB 3 | Mobility Routes, OBCs and Andhra
[114:3:0] - **ES 2025-26:** drivers – **health, education, technology, institutions** (not land inheritance); low mobility **entrenches historical inequality**; poverty **21.9% (2011-12) → 2.3% (2023-24)**.
[114:3:1] - **Mandal Commission:** OBCs **over 52%**; **11 indicators = 4 social + 4 economic + 3 educational** (social, economic, educational categories).
[114:3:2] - **Telangana caste survey:** **242 castes**; **135** above average backwardness.
[114:3:3] - **Marathas, Gujjars, Patels** seek OBC status mainly for **greater life chances**.
[114:3:4] - Reservation demand: **Periyar's Self-Respect Movement**; **Ezhavas** (Kerala): toddy tapping → tailoring (occupational mobility); city anonymity weakens caste.
[114:3:5] - **Dalits:** state policy, reform movements, Sanskritisation and Westernisation.
[114:3:6] - **Medieval Andhra:** **Kakatiya** society relatively open (**Velamas, Kammas, Reddys** rose); **Saivism (Virashaivism)** a "**lubricant**" of upward mobility.
[114:3:7] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:114 sub 6; NextGen Society G2 Part 2 p.35
[114:3:8] - **Most influential factor in Dalit transformation (studies):** **collective action** – movements and political mobilisation.
## SUB 4 | Agrarian Classes and Middle Class
[114:4:0] - **Daniel Thorner:** by land rights and income – **Maliks** (landlords on rent), **Kisans** (own land, family labour), **Mazdoors** (tenants, sharecroppers, landless).
[114:4:1] - Dominant caste is a concept for **stratification in rural India**.
[114:4:2] - **Macaulay's Minute, 1835** → English-educated middle class (**A.R. Desai**, *Social Background of Indian Nationalism*).
[114:4:3] - Middle class: World Bank **$10-50**/day (PPP); NCAER **₹2-10 lakh**; PRICE **₹5-30 lakh**; **43.2 crore (31%) in 2021 → 61% by 2047**; OECD: world's largest by **2035**.

=== NOTES 2:115 | Rural, Urban and Tribal Society in India – Features and Interlinkages
## SUB 0 | Village Economy – Jajmani, Peasants and Agrarian Society
[115:0:0] # Jajmani system
[115:0:1] - **Jajmani:** a socio-economic and ritual arrangement in which one caste secures the service of another; each works for a family with whom it is **hereditarily linked** – between landholder patron (**jajman**) and service castes (**kamin**).
[115:0:2] - **Exchange by tradition:** goods and services pass by **custom and tradition**, not law or market value of goods.
[115:0:3] - **Features:** **functional reciprocity** between castes within the **village social structure**; the **Mahalwari system** is a British land-revenue settlement, not a jajmani feature (CSP 1998).
[115:0:4] - **First scientific study:** **William Wiser**, *The Hindu Jajmani System* (1936), a UP village.
[115:0:5] # Peasants and agrarian society
[115:0:6] - **Peasant (rythu, kisan):** **owns/holds land and cultivates it with the labour of family members** (Thorner's kisan); one who cultivates only through hired labour is a landlord-farmer.
[115:0:7] - **Agricultural vs industrial society:** differ in **premises, method of production and social dynamics** – all of these.
[115:0:8] - Forms of exchange (Polanyi): **reciprocity, redistribution, market exchange**; "antagonism" is not a form of exchange.
[115:0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society Group 2 Part 3 p.35; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 4 p.5; general knowledge (Wiser 1936, Thorner, Polanyi)
[115:0:10] - **Jajmani payment** is **in kind** (grain shares at harvest, customary gifts) – **money is not** a characteristic.
## SUB 1 | Rural Community and Village Studies
[115:1:0] # The village as a unit
[115:1:1] - **Rural sociology:** the study of rural society – its **social structure, institutions, change and conflict** in villages.
[115:1:2] - **Elements of a rural community:** **territory, we-feeling, relative self-sufficiency, small size, homogeneity**.
[115:1:3]   - **⚠ Trap:** **cultural diversity** is NOT a rural element; diversity and heterogeneity are urban (UPPSC 2021).
[115:1:4] - **Charles Metcalfe:** villages as self-reliant **"little republics"**.
[115:1:5] - **Robert Redfield**, *The Little Community* (1955): the village as a **"little community"** with four traits – **distinctiveness, smallness, homogeneity, self-sufficiency** (TSPSC 2023; CSP 2002). Tonnies = Gemeinschaft-Gesellschaft; Maine = "village communities".
[115:1:6] - **Myth of self-sufficiency:** **M.N. Srinivas and A.M. Shah** ("The Myth of the Self-Sufficiency of the Indian Village", 1960) – villages depended on outside markets; Srinivas debunked villages as unchanging, self-sufficient "little republics".
[115:1:7] - **Andre Beteille** (1980): "the village was not merely a place where people lived; it had a **design** in which were reflected the **basic values of Indian civilisation**".
[115:1:8] - **S.C. Dube:** argued for **systematic study of village communities for better planning**; his Shamirpet study (Osmania University project) was a laboratory for rural development programmes.

## SUB 2 | Tribal Society
[115:2:0] - Features: common **name, dialect**, territory, ancestor, **animism/totemism**, clans, **youth dormitories**, simple economy.

## SUB 3 | Urban Society and Census Data
[115:3:0] - Urban life: **competition, impersonal relations, anonymity, weak control** – **not informal ties** (JPSC 2021).
[115:3:1] - **Louis Wirth**, *Urbanism as a Way of Life* (**1938**): **size, density, heterogeneity**.
[115:3:2] - **Urbanism** = social-cultural side; **urbanisation** = population shift.
[115:3:3] - **Urban area** = statutory towns (Municipality, Corporation, Cantonment Board, Notified Town Area Committee) + census towns.
[115:3:4] - **Census town:** **5,000** people; **75%** (not 60%) of male main workers non-farm; **400/sq km**; unchanged since **1981**; kept for **Census 2027** (17 Sep 2026), though MoHUA called it outdated.
[115:3:5] - Urban share **11% (1901) → 27.81% (2001) → 31.16% (2011)**; centres 1,827 → 7,935.
[115:3:6] - **2001-11 urban jump:** mainly **reclassification** of villages as census towns (about **2,500** added); urban share 31.1%.
[115:3:7] - **Largest UAs (2001):** **Greater Mumbai > Kolkata > Delhi**; by 2011 Delhi passed Kolkata.
[115:3:8] - 2011: **68.8% rural**, **6,40,867 villages**.
[115:3:9] - **Hidden urbanisation** (DEGURBA): ~**63%** urban (2015).
[115:3:10] - **16th FC (2026-31):** ULB grant share **36% → 45%**; 41% urban by 2031.
[115:3:11] > Census criteria angle: "60% of male workers non-agricultural" is the wrong criterion – it is 75%.

## SUB 4 | Urban Problems and Migration
[115:4:0] - **Industrialisation** drives urbanisation; **density** raises **crime**; maladjustment, rich-poor segregation, alienation.
[115:4:1] - **ES 2025-26:** informality (slums, informal labour) is **structural** – integrate, not eradicate; cities lack **fiscal autonomy**; **35%** rural households lack healthcare within 5 km.
[115:4:2] - Caste persists through slums and job networks.
[115:4:3] - **Jockin (Joachim) Arputham:** Magsaysay awardee (2000); President of the **National Slum Dwellers' Federation**; accompanied **Prince Charles** in **Dharavi** (Mumbai), among Asia's largest slums (5 Nov 2003).
[115:4:4] # Migration
[115:4:5] - **Jan Breman**, *Footloose Labour* (**1996**): circular migrants in seasonal, casual, informal jobs.
[115:4:6] - Sources: **UP, Bihar**; push: monsoon failure, unemployment; urban wages **1.7-2x** rural.
[115:4:7] - **SPMRM (2016):** "rural soul, urban amenities"; **PMEGP** (KVIC).

=== NOTES 2:117 | Communalism – Meaning, Causes, Forms and Growth in India
## SUB 0 | Communal Award and Two-nation Theory
[117:0:0] - **Syed Ahmad Khan** = ideological father of the **two-nation theory** (Hindi-Urdu controversy; advised Muslims to stay out of Congress).
[117:0:1] - **Communal Award (16 Aug 1932):** announced by British PM **Ramsay MacDonald** (not Churchill, Attlee or Pitt); separate electorates for Muslims, Sikhs, Christians and **Depressed Classes** (for **20 years**); **71 seats** for depressed classes.
[117:0:2] - **Poona Pact:** replaced Dalit separate electorates with reserved seats in **joint electorates**; **71 → 148** reserved seats in provincial legislatures.
[117:0:3] - **⚠ Trap:** 71 = Communal Award seats; 148 = Poona Pact reserved seats; 20 years = Award's separate electorates
[117:0:4] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India l.16269 (Ramsay MacDonald); app notes 2:117 sub 4
## SUB 1 | Secularism and Safeguards
[117:1:0] TABLE head: Provision | What it does
  [117:1:0.0] Preamble | "Secular" added by 42nd Amendment (1976)
  [117:1:0.1] Art. 14-16 | Equality
  [117:1:0.2] Art. 25-28 | Freedom of religion
  [117:1:0.3] Art. 29-30 | Minority rights
  [117:1:0.4] Art. 51A(e) | Promote harmony and common brotherhood
  [117:1:0.5] Sec. 123(3), RP Act 1951 | Appeal on ground of religion = corrupt practice
  [117:1:0.6] Religious Institutions (Prevention of Misuse) Act, 1988 | Bars religious premises for political activity, hiding criminals or arms
  [117:1:0.7] Places of Worship (Special Provisions) Act, 1991 | In force 11 July 1991; freezes religious character as on 15 August 1947 (with statutory exceptions)
[117:1:1] - **Match (ATWO PYQ):** Secular State – **Preamble & Arts. 25-28**; minority rights – **Arts. 29-30**; SC/ST seats in legislatures – **Arts. 330, 332**; universal adult franchise – **Art. 326**.

## SUB 2 | Meaning and Definitions
[117:2:0] # Indian Meaning
[117:2:1] - **NextGen:** communalism is the belief that because a group follows one religion it also has **common social, political and economic interests**.
[117:2:2] - In India, communalism is most often seen as **religious difference** between groups that leads to tension and rioting.
[117:2:3] - A group promotes its own interests **at the expense of others**.
[117:2:4] # Definition → Scholar
[117:2:5] TABLE head: Scholar | Definition / view
  [117:2:5.0] Robert Melson and Howard Wolpe | "The political assertiveness of a community to maintain its identity in a plural society undergoing modernisation" (asked in APPSC G2 2025)
  [117:2:5.1] Bipan Chandra | The belief that because a group follows one religion, it also has common social, political and economic interests; gave the three stages
  [117:2:5.2] Ram Ahuja | An ideology that society is divided into religious communities whose interests differ and often oppose each other; "communal persons" do politics through religion
  [117:2:5.3] T.K. Oommen | Six dimensions of communalism (assimilationist to secessionist)
  [117:2:5.4] W.C. Smith (*Modern Islam in India*) | Ideology that treats each religious group as the basic social, political and economic unit and stresses antagonism between such groups
[117:2:6] - **⚠ Trap:** The name is **T.K. Oommen**, not "T.R. Oommen". Oommen = six dimensions; Bipan Chandra = three stages.
[117:2:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.86; app notes 2:117 sub 0
## SUB 3 | Bipan Chandra's Three Stages
[117:3:0] TABLE head: Stage | Features
  [117:3:0.0] 1. Communal nationalism | Followers of one religion share common secular (political, economic) interests; still nationalist in outlook
  [117:3:0.1] 2. Liberal communalism | Interests of one community seen as different but reconcilable within one nation
  [117:3:0.2] 3. Extreme communalism | Interests seen as mutually antagonistic; politics of fear, hatred and violence
[117:3:1] - After the **1937 elections**, the Muslim League turned to extreme communalism "based on fear, hatred and violence"; echoed by the **Hindu Mahasabha and RSS**.
[117:3:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Order: communal nationalism → liberal communalism → extreme communalism.
[117:3:3] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:117 sub 1
## SUB 4 | Causes of Communalism
[117:4:0] TABLE head: Type | Causes
  [117:4:0.0] Historical / colonial | British divide and rule; separate electorates; caste and religion recorded in the first census (1871-72); 19th-century revivalist movements
  [117:4:0.1] Political | Vote-bank and identity politics; religion-based parties
  [117:4:0.2] Economic | Poverty, unemployment, lopsided development, relative deprivation
  [117:4:0.3] Legal / administrative | Delayed justice, lenient punishment, police inaction, biased intelligence
  [117:4:0.4] Social / media | Fake news and hate messages on social media; media amplifying communal narratives
  [117:4:0.5] Psychological / international | Prejudices, rumours; foreign funding
[117:4:1] - **1920s:** Arya Samaj **Shuddhi and Sangathan** vs Muslim **Tabligh and Tanzim**.

## SUB 5 | Growth in Colonial India (1887-1940)
[117:5:0] # Lord Minto and the 1909 Act
[117:5:1] - **Oct 1906 – Simla Deputation** led by the **Aga Khan** to **Lord Minto** demanded separate electorates.
[117:5:2] - The **1909 Act** gave the **first legal separate electorate** for Muslims.
[117:5:3] - Lord Minto is called the "**Father of the Communal Electorate**".
[117:5:4] # Timeline
[117:5:5] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [117:5:5.0] 1887 | Lord Dufferin used Syed Ahmad Khan to keep Muslims away from Congress
  [117:5:5.1] Oct 1906 | Simla Deputation to Lord Minto
  [117:5:5.2] 1906 | Muslim League founded at Dacca under the Aga Khan and Nawab Salimullah; first communal party
  [117:5:5.3] 1909 | First legal separate electorate
  [117:5:5.4] 1915 | All India Hindu Mahasabha founded under the aegis of the Maharaja of Kasim Bazar; associated with Madan Mohan Malaviya
  [117:5:5.5] 1928 | Nehru Report
  [117:5:5.6] 1929 | Jinnah's Fourteen Points (reply to Nehru Report)

## SUB 6 | Forms – Oommen's Six Dimensions
[117:6:0] TABLE head: Dimension | Meaning | Example
  [117:6:0.0] Assimilationist | Small religious groups absorbed into a larger one | Claiming STs, Jains, Sikhs, Buddhists are Hindus
  [117:6:0.1] Welfarist | Works for one community's welfare only | Christian or Parsi associations
  [117:6:0.2] Retreatist | Small community keeps away from politics | Bahai community
  [117:6:0.3] Retaliatory | Tries to harm members of other communities | Riots, revenge cycles
  [117:6:0.4] Separatist | Separate territorial state within India | Gorkhaland, Bodoland
  [117:6:0.5] Secessionist | Independent state outside India | Khalistan, "Azad Kashmir"
[117:6:1] - Other forms: **Political** = uses religion for votes; **Economic** = grows from competition for jobs and resources.
[117:6:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Separatist = within India; Secessionist = outside India.
[117:6:3] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:117 sub 5
## SUB 7 | Religious Fundamentalism vs Communalism
[117:7:0] - **Fundamentalism:** strict adherence to religious beliefs; often rejects modern interpretations.
[117:7:1] - Features: **literal reading of scripture**, conservatism, resistance to change, **group exclusiveness**.
[117:7:2] - Causes: **identity crisis**, globalisation, **social insecurity**, political mobilisation, economic inequality.
[117:7:3] TABLE head: Religious fundamentalism | Communalism
  [117:7:3.0] Return to "fundamentals"; mainly doctrinal | Political mobilisation of religious identity
  [117:7:3.1] Reforms one's own community (often anti-modern) | Directed against other communities; seeks power and resources
  [117:7:3.2] Can exist without inter-group conflict | Always "us vs them"
[117:7:4] - **⚠ Trap:** Group exclusiveness is a **feature** of fundamentalism, not a cause.
[117:7:5] ~~ Sources: app notes 2:117 sub 6

=== NOTES 2:119 | Regionalism – Meaning, Causes, Forms and Sons-of-the-soil Movements
## SUB 0 | Meaning of Regionalism
[119:0:0] - **NextGen definition:** regionalism = a feeling of **loyalty to a particular part of a country** and a wish for it to be **more politically independent**; a culmination of socio-economic and political factors, not just a territorial unit.
[119:0:1] - **Best represents regionalism:** regional demands for autonomy or, at the extreme, **secession by certain States**; preference for one's religion = **communalism**, caste loyalty = **casteism**, favouring one's language = **linguism**.
[119:0:2] # Terms
[119:0:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [119:0:3.0] Region | Area shaped by history, with its own language, culture, identity and political implications
  [119:0:3.1] Regionalism | Emotional attachment to one area; may resist a dominant culture or challenge the nation-state
  [119:0:3.2] Supra-state | Several States sharing interests (e.g., the North-East)
  [119:0:3.3] Inter-state | Conflict between States
  [119:0:3.4] Intra-state | A region within a State seeking recognition
[119:0:4] # Positive vs Negative Regionalism
[119:0:5] TABLE head: Positive | Negative
  [119:0:5.0] Regional parties give backward regions a voice | Parochialism; hostility to migrants (Shiv Sena, MNS)
  [119:0:5.1] Cultural pride, balanced development, local innovation | Law-and-order breakdowns; openings for extremists
  [119:0:5.2] Solidarity (Tripura Tribal Autonomous District Council) | Inter-state disputes; new linguistic minorities
[119:0:6] - Regional feeling turns **negative** when exploited for votes by regional parties.
[119:0:7] # Regionalism and tribal welfare (ATWO PYQs)
[119:0:8] - Regionalism **empowers local governance** for better tribal welfare and **strengthens State-centric tribal-welfare policies** (not centralisation, not 'no effect').
[119:0:9] - **Sub-regionalism** **enhances the focus on local tribal needs within smaller regions** (agency tracts, autonomous councils).
[119:0:10] - Intra-state regionalism is a **positive** form – self-dependence and development of the region (Saurashtra, East UP, Vidarbha).
[119:0:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.80, p.82-83; app notes 2:119 sub 0
