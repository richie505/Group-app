=== NOTES 4:61 | India - Literacy: Levels, Gender and Regional Gaps, Trends
## SUB 0 | Education Policy and School Schemes
[61:0:0] # Policies
[61:0:1] - **NPE 1986** launched **Operation Blackboard (1987)** – at least **two rooms and two teachers**, teaching aids, for primary schools; also stressed ST education. **Ramamurti Committee (1990)** reviewed NPE; NPE **revised 1992** (Programme of Action) gave more weight to tribal education.
[61:0:2] - **NEP 2020:** replaced NPE 1986 after **34 years**; **5+3+3+4** – Foundational **3-8**, Preparatory 8-11, Middle 11-14, Secondary 14-18.
[61:0:3] - NEP targets: **100% school GER by 2030**; **50% higher-education GER by 2035**; **100% youth and adult literacy by 2030**.
[61:0:4] - **NIPUN Bharat:** foundational literacy by **Grade 3**; deadline moved to **2026-27**.
[61:0:5] # Schemes
[61:0:6] TABLE head: Scheme | Fact
  [61:0:6.0] Samagra Shiksha (2018) | Subsumes SSA, RMSA, Teacher Education
  [61:0:6.1] Unnat Bharat Abhiyan | Higher education – institutions adopt villages
  [61:0:6.2] SEMIS | Secondary Education MIS – nodal agency NUEPA, New Delhi
  [61:0:6.3] Mana Badi Nadu-Nedu (AP) | 14 Nov 2019; English labs (not Operation Blackboard)
[61:0:7] # Learning and enrolment data
[61:0:8] - **FLN Index** (Institute for Competitiveness for **EAC-PM**): 2nd edition – **Punjab** highest (64.19) among **small States**; Jharkhand was **not** the lowest small State in the web summaries. 2021 edition – West Bengal top among large States.
[61:0:9] - **AISHE 2023-24:** GER about **30**, female GER **31.2**, GPI **1.08**; female GER 28.5 → **30.2** (2021-23), above male; gender parity at all school levels.
[61:0:10] - **UDISE+ 2024-25** dropout: preparatory **2.3%**, middle **3.5%**, secondary **8.2%** (from 3.7, 5.2, **10.9**).
[61:0:11] - NFHS-5: women with **10+ years** of schooling **41%** (NFHS-4 35.7%).

[61:0:13] - **NEP drafting committee** (draft 2019, adopted 2020): chaired by **Dr K. Kasturirangan** (former ISRO chief).

## SUB 2 | Adult Literacy Programmes and Literacy Days
[61:2:0] TABLE head: Programme | Year | Note
  [61:2:0.0] Farmers' Functional Literacy Programme | 1967-68 | Early adult education
  [61:2:0.1] NAEP | 2 Oct 1978 | Age 15-35
  [61:2:0.2] National Literacy Mission | 5 May 1988 | Total Literacy Campaign = its main mass-campaign strategy
  [61:2:0.3] Total Literacy Campaign | 1989-90 | Ernakulam – first totally literate district (1990); Kottayam – first fully literate town (1989)
  [61:2:0.4] Saakshar Bharat | 8 Sep 2009 (International Literacy Day) | Adults 15+, focus on women; Centre : State 75 : 25 (NE 90 : 10); planned up to March 2012, extended to 31 March 2018, then wound up
  [61:2:0.5] ULLAS (Nav Bharat Saaksharta Karyakram / New India Literacy Programme) | 2022 | FY 2022-23 to 2026-27; Ministry of Education; non-literates 15+
[61:2:1] - ULLAS = **Understanding of Lifelong Learning for All in Society**; five components – FLN, critical life skills, basic education, vocational skills, continuing education.
[61:2:2] - Fully literate (ULLAS): **Ladakh** (first UT, 2024) → **Mizoram** (first State, 2025) → Goa, Tripura, Himachal Pradesh, Sikkim → **Uttarakhand 6th (Jul 2026)**.
[61:2:3] - **Velland (Kerala)** is the exam answer for India's first fully **computer-literate panchayat**; Chamravattom (Malappuram) was declared the first 100% computer-literate village (2003).
[61:2:4] - **International Literacy Day: 8 September** (UNESCO, declared 1966, first observed 1967). **2017 theme: "Literacy in a digital world"**; 2026: "Literacy for people, the planet and prosperity".
[61:2:5] - **Information literacy:** the skill of defining, locating, accessing and using information.
[61:2:6] > NAEP = 15-35; Saakshar Bharat and ULLAS = 15+.

## SUB 3 | Census 2011 – India, Gender Gap and States
[61:3:0] # Definition
[61:3:1] - **Literate** = aged **7 and above**, can **both read and write with understanding** in any language; reading only = **not literate**.
[61:3:2] - Censuses 1951-1971 used age **5+**; **7+ from 1981**. PLFS uses the same 7+ definition but a **sample** – not directly comparable.
[61:3:3] - Full literacy (ULLAS) = **95%+** among persons aged **15+**. **Census 2027** adds a **digital literacy** question.
[61:3:4] # National figures
[61:3:5] TABLE head: Census | 1951 | 1961 | 1971 | 1981 | 1991 | 2001 | 2011
  [61:3:5.0] Literacy % | 18.33 | 28.30 | 34.45 | 43.57 | 52.21 | 64.83 | 74.04
[61:3:6] # Census 2011 – provisional vs final
[61:3:7] TABLE head: India 2011 | Total | Male | Female
  [61:3:7.0] Provisional | 74.04% | 82.14% | 65.46%
  [61:3:7.1] Final | 73.0% (72.98%) | 80.9% (80.88%) | 64.6% (64.63%)
[61:3:8] - **Largest decadal gain: 1990s**. Males about 27% → 82% in 60 years; women from less than 1 in 10 (1951) to 2 in 3.
[61:3:9] - **Gender gap 2011:** about **16-17 points** (82.14 − 65.46 = 16.68 provisional); widest in **Rajasthan (about 28 points)**; under **5 points** in **Meghalaya** (about 76 vs 73).
[61:3:10] - Rural / urban (provisional): **68.9% / 85.0%**; largest deficit: **rural women**. Causes of the gap: distance and safety, early marriage, dowry, son preference, poverty.
[61:3:11] - SC literacy **66.1%**, ST **59.0%**; low literacy is one of the **four PVTG criteria**.
[61:3:12] # States

  [61:3:13.0] Total | Kerala 94.0% > Lakshadweep 91.8% > Mizoram 91.3% | Bihar 61.8%
  [61:3:13.1] Female | Kerala 92.1% | Bihar 51.5% (final); Rajasthan 52.7% (provisional)
  [61:3:13.2] AP / MP / Uttarakhand / UP (final) | Uttarakhand 78.8% is highest of the four | AP 67.02%, UP 67.7%, MP 69.3%
[61:3:14] > Lowest female literacy: Bihar in the final data, Rajasthan in the provisional data – keys use both.

## SUB 1 | Female Work Participation and Gender
[62:1:0] - Female LFPR (15+): **23.3% (2017-18)** → 30% (2019-20) → **37% (2022-23)** → about **40% (2025)**.
[62:1:1] - **Claudia Goldin U-curve:** India on the falling half till **2018-19**.
[62:1:2] - Rise: subsistence farming, unpaid family work, hardship, return migration, **wider survey coverage**.
[62:1:3] - Low levels: **mechanisation**, more schooling of girls, no flexible hours, distance, safety, family duties.
[62:1:4] - Women (2011): **55%** of agricultural labourers, 24% of cultivators, only **12.8%** of operational holdings; men's **out-migration** → **feminisation of farm work**.
[62:1:5] # Gender and work (sociology)
[62:1:6] - All societies divide household tasks by gender; for women **production and reproduction** are interlinked; much of women's productive work is **unpaid**; men have played a minor role in domestic work (all four statements correct – TSPSC 2023).
[62:1:7] - **Engels / Marxist view:** women form part of the **reserve army of labour** – drawn into work in booms and pushed out in slumps (UPSC 2000).

## SUB 1 | Rural-Urban Continuum – PURA and Rurban Mission
[65:1:0] - **PURA** (Providing Urban Amenities to Rural Areas): concept of **A.P.J. Abdul Kalam**; announced by PM Vajpayee, **2003**; pilot in 7 clusters (2004-05); restructured 2012.
[65:1:1] - **Four connectivities of PURA:** **physical** (roads), **electronic** (telecom, internet), **knowledge** (education, training) → leading to **economic** connectivity – **cultural connectivity is not one of them**.
[65:1:2] - **Shyama Prasad Mukherji Rurban Mission**: successor to PURA; launched **21 Feb 2016 at Kurubhat, Rajnandgaon (Chhattisgarh)**; **300 Rurban clusters**.
[65:1:3] ~~ Sources: app notes 4:65 sub 6; general knowledge (PURA connectivities, not checked)
## SUB 2 | Census Definition of Urban and Components of Growth
[65:2:0] TABLE head: Type | Criteria
  [65:2:0.0] Statutory town | Any place with a municipality, corporation, cantonment board or notified town area committee, declared by State law
  [65:2:0.1] Census town | Population 5,000+; 75%+ of male main workers non-agricultural; density 400+ per sq km
[65:2:1] - 2011 towns **7,935** = **4,041 statutory** + **3,894 census towns**; urban population 37.7 crore (7.9 crore in 1961).
[65:2:2] - Census towns **1,362 (2001) → 3,894 (2011)** – shows reclassification as a major component.
[65:2:3] # Components of urban growth
[65:2:4] - 1. **Natural increase** – similar in rural and urban areas.
[65:2:5] - 2. **Rural-to-urban migration** – explains most of the rural-urban gap.
[65:2:6] - 3. **Reclassification** – villages turn into towns / new towns form.
[65:2:7] - 4. **Expansion of town boundaries** over villages.
[65:2:8] - "Decline" or contraction is not a component; migration, not natural increase, explains the gap.
[65:2:9] # Drivers
[65:2:10] TABLE head: Factor | Examples
  [65:2:10.0] Pull | Industrialisation and jobs, trade and commerce, better health and education, transport links
  [65:2:10.1] Push | Failed monsoon → rural unemployment → migration; poverty, landlessness
[65:2:11] - **Rakesh Mohan**: three processes – **metropolitanisation, commercialisation, rural (subsistence) urbanisation**.
