=== NOTES 5:90 | Forest and Wildlife Legal Framework – Ifa 1927, Fca 1980, Wpa 1972, Bd Act 2002
## SUB 0 | Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 – Year, Scope and Key Sections
[90:0:0] # Year and scope
[90:0:1] - **Enacted by Parliament in 1972** (assent 9 Sept 1972) – not 1962, 1974, 1982, 1986 or 1990; first comprehensive wildlife law.
[90:0:2] - **Protects wild animals, birds and plants** and manages their habitats – **not animals only**; it is the Act that **regulates National Parks and Wildlife Sanctuaries** (not EPA 1986, FCA 1980 or BD Act 2002).
[90:0:3] - **Poaching** = illegal hunting of wild animals – barred by the Act.
[90:0:4] - **66 sections, 7 chapters**; amended 1982, 1986, 1991, 1993, 2002, 2006, 2013, **2022**.
[90:0:5] # Key sections
[90:0:6] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [90:0:6.0] 2(1) | "Animal" = amphibians, birds, mammals, reptiles, their young, eggs of birds and reptiles
  [90:0:6.1] 2(37) | "Wildlife"
  [90:0:6.2] 9 | Hunting of scheduled animals prohibited, subject to exceptions
  [90:0:6.3] 11 | Animal dangerous to human life (or crops), diseased or disabled beyond recovery may be captured/killed with the Chief Wildlife Warden's permission
  [90:0:6.4] 12 | Permits for education, research, scientific management, translocation
  [90:0:6.5] 38J | Teasing, molesting, injuring or feeding animals in a zoo prohibited
  [90:0:6.6] 39(1) | Wild animals = State Government property; in a Centre-declared sanctuary/NP = Central Government
  [90:0:6.7] 38V(3) | Tiger Conservation Plan for every TR (2006)
  [90:0:6.8] 62 | Centre may notify a non-Schedule I animal as vermin
[90:0:7] - **Gharial, Indian wild ass, wild buffalo** cannot be hunted by anyone except as the law permits (UPSC 2017: all three).
[90:0:8] - **UPSC 2022 (official key "1 and 2"):** wild animals are government property; a protected animal gets equal protection inside or outside PAs; mere *apprehension* of danger is not enough to kill it.
[90:0:9] - **Protected animal may be killed** when it endangers human life **and** when it is diseased – both true, but disease does not explain the danger clause.
[90:0:10] - **Mining:** barred inside NPs and sanctuaries; SC bars mining within **1 km** of every NP/sanctuary unless a larger ESZ is notified.
[90:0:11] ~~ Sources: Rocket Environment p.25; NextGen Environment APPSC 06 p.14; PMF Environment 06 p.71-76; app notes 5:90 sub 4
## SUB 1 | WPA Schedules and the 2022 Amendment
[90:1:0] TABLE head: Before 2022 (6 schedules) | After 2022 (4 schedules)
  [90:1:0.0] I and II: absolute protection (tiger, rhino) | I: highest protection
  [90:1:0.1] III and IV: lesser (game species) | II: lower protection
  [90:1:0.2] V: vermin (common crow, fruit bats, rats, mice) | III: protected plants
  [90:1:0.3] VI: specified plants – cultivation only with a licence | IV: CITES-listed specimens
[90:1:1] - **Number of schedules:** **6** in the 1972 Act (the usual key); **4** since the 2022 amendment (in force 1 April 2023).
[90:1:2] - **Schedule I effect:** a tortoise in Schedule I gets **the same protection as the tiger** (not "extinct in the wild" or "endemic").
[90:1:3] - **Schedule VI (old):** a **licence is required to cultivate** the plant; a specified plant cannot be picked, uprooted or traded without permission.
[90:1:4] - **Flying fox** = a **fruit bat** (Pteropus), not a kite, stork or vulture; it does **not** feed on blood. It was **vermin (Schedule V)**; since 2022 it is in **Schedule II**.
[90:1:5] - **Schedule I examples:** Asiatic lion, tiger, elephant, snow leopard, rhino, Gangetic dolphin, dugong, GIB, **blackbuck**; mugger (Kurnool, Sep 2026) and rock eagle-owl (Dharmavaram, Aug 2026) in news.
[90:1:6] # 2022 amendment
[90:1:7] - **Schedules 6 → 4**; vermin schedule deleted (Sec 62 power stays with the **Central Government**); **CITES** provisions (Management and Scientific Authorities); Centre regulates **invasive alien species** (African catfish, Lantana).
[90:1:8] - Voluntary **surrender of captive animals without compensation**; Chief Wildlife Warden in every State; Centre may notify conservation reserves.
[90:1:9] - Fines: specially protected animals – minimum **₹25,000** (from ₹10,000); general violations – maximum **₹1 lakh** (from ₹25,000).
[90:1:10] # Earlier amendments
[90:1:11] - **2002:** **NBWL** replaced the Indian Board for Wildlife; **Conservation Reserves** (government land, e.g. **Tsarap Chu**) and **Community Reserves** (private/community land).
[90:1:12] - **2006:** **NTCA and WCCB**.
[90:1:13] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 06 p.76-78; Shankar Environment Part 14 p.12; app notes 5:90 sub 5
## SUB 2 | Indian Forest Act 1927 and Colonial Forest Laws
[90:2:0] TABLE head: Year | Law | Point
  [90:2:0.0] 1865 | First Indian Forest Act | Drafted 1865
  [90:2:0.1] 1878 | Indian Forest Act | Created reserved, protected, village forests
  [90:2:0.2] 1927 | Indian Forest Act | Consolidating law, still in force
[90:2:1] - **Degree of protection:** **Reserved > Protected > Village** forest; a **village forest** is a **reserved forest** assigned to a village community.
[90:2:2] - **Reserved forest (Ch II):** everything banned unless permitted; **Sec 26** = acts prohibited in a reserved forest, incl. punishable **trespass**.
[90:2:3] - **Protected forest:** activities allowed unless prohibited – people could take **head-loads of wood**, collect produce for their own use and **graze cattle** (entry not barred).
[90:2:4] - **Unclassed forest:** outside the reserved and protected categories. **Sec 35:** State may regulate non-government forests.
[90:2:5] - **2017 amendment** (ordinance 2017, Act 2018): **bamboo grown outside forest areas** removed from the definition of "tree" (**Sec 2(7)**) – may be felled and moved without permit. Bamboo **inside** forests is not freed.
[90:2:6] - **Colonial plantations:** British policy encouraged **monocropping through plantations** (tea, coffee, teak), a cause of environmental degradation.
[90:2:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 06 p.80, p.84; NextGen Environment APPSC 05 p.11; Rocket Environment p.18; app notes 5:90 sub 0, 2
## SUB 3 | Forest (Conservation) Act 1980 / Van Adhiniyam
[90:3:0] - **Enacted 1980** (27 Dec 1980, in force from **25 Oct 1980**) – not 1976, 1978, 1981 or 1988.
[90:3:1] - **Sec 2:** **prior approval of the Central Government (MoEF)** to de-reserve a forest or use forest land for **non-forest purposes** – not the Collector, State or FSI. FCA = the Act that **regulates forest-land diversion**.
[90:3:2] - **2023 amendment:** renamed **Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980** (w.e.f. 29 Dec 2023); covers land notified as forest under IFA 1927 or any law, and land **recorded as forest on or after 25 Oct 1980**; **excludes land converted before 12 Dec 1996**.
[90:3:3] - **Exemptions:** strategic linear projects within **100 km** of IB/LoC/LAC; zoos, safaris, eco-tourism; no Gram Sabha consent required (a key criticism).
[90:3:4] - **Compensatory afforestation:** equal non-forest land, else **twice** the degraded forest land + **NPV**.
[90:3:5] - **T.N. Godavarman (1995 writ; 1996 order):** "forest" = every area recorded as forest **irrespective of ownership**; the 2023 amendment narrows this.
[90:3:6] - **AP diversions:** **3,474.57 ha** (1 Jan 2015-5 Feb 2019); **149.5687 ha** (1 Apr-31 Dec 2025).
[90:3:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 06 p.80-83; Rocket Environment p.12, p.18; app notes 5:90 sub 6

## SUB 5 | National Forest Policy 1952 and 1988
[90:5:0] TABLE head: Feature | NFP 1952 | NFP 1988
  [90:5:0.0] National target | One-third | One-third (33%) of geographical area
  [90:5:0.1] Hills | 60% | Two-thirds
  [90:5:0.2] Classes / thrust | National, Protected, Village forests + Tree-lands (National Park not a class) | Ecological stability, people's participation
[90:5:1] - **NFP 1988 includes:** afforestation and wasteland development; reforestation in existing forests; **wood substitutes and alternative fuels** – NOT wide use of **insecticides and pesticides**. "Two-thirds of the country under forest" is wrong (hills only).

## SUB 6 | Biological Diversity Act 2002 and Which Act Protects What
[90:6:0] TABLE head: Act | What it does
  [90:6:0.0] WPA 1972 | Wild animals, birds, plants and habitats
  [90:6:0.1] EPA 1986 | Umbrella law – protection and improvement of the environment
  [90:6:0.2] FCA 1980 | Forest-land diversion
  [90:6:0.3] PCA Act 1960 | Cruelty to animals; parent Act of AWBI
  [90:6:0.4] BD Act 2002 | CBD, access and benefit sharing
[90:6:1] - **"Nature (Protection) Act, 2000"**, "Forests Protection Act 1982", "EPA 1996" do **not exist**.
[90:6:2] - **NGT** cannot hear **WPA 1972** or FRA 2006 cases.
[90:6:3] - **BD Act 2002:** three tiers – **NBA (Chennai, 2003)**, State Biodiversity Boards, **Biodiversity Management Committees** (prepare **People's Biodiversity Registers**); **Biodiversity Heritage Sites** under **Sec 37** (State Government).
[90:6:4] - **2023 amendment (in force 1 April 2024):** offences **decriminalised** (civil penalties); exemption for AYUSH practitioners and **BMC-certified** cultivated medicinal plants; NBA approval **180 → 90 days**; foreign entities **still need** prior NBA approval.
[90:6:5] - **AP (May 2026):** more than **13,500 BMCs**, **12,000 PBRs**.
[90:6:6] - **Constitution:** 42nd Amendment (1976) moved forests (**Entry 17A**) and wild animals and birds (**Entry 17B**) to the Concurrent List; inserted **Art 48A** (DPSP) and **Art 51A(g)** (Fundamental Duty). A State Bill amending WPA (e.g. Maharashtra 2026, Sec 12; leopard **Schedule I → II**) needs the **President's assent** because it amends a **central law**.
[90:6:7] ~~ Sources: Rocket Environment p.12, p.40, p.66; NextGen Environment APPSC 06 p.14; app notes 5:90 sub 1, 7, 9
## SUB 7 | Protected Areas and Community Reserves under WPA
[90:7:0] - **Five kinds:** wildlife sanctuary, national park, conservation reserve, community reserve, tiger reserve.
[90:7:1] - **Community Reserve (Sec 36C):** private/community land, declared by the **State Government**; run by a **Community Reserve Management Committee (Sec 36D)** – not the Chief Wildlife Warden.
[90:7:2] - In a community reserve **hunting is not allowed** and people may **collect non-timber forest produce**, but **traditional agriculture (jhum) is not allowed** → UPSC 2023: "only two" statements correct.
[90:7:3] - **NP vs sanctuary:** NP – no grazing, no human activity except as the Chief Wildlife Warden permits; sanctuary – regulated grazing.
[90:7:4] - **India's PAs:** **1,75,169.42 sq km = 5.32%** of land (2026).
[90:7:5] - **Eco-sensitive zone:** the **National Chambal Sanctuary (MP)** was notified as an ESZ on **2 March 2020**.
[90:7:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 06 p.71-72; Rocket Environment p.25-26; https://www.gktoday.in/national-chambal-sanctuary-declared-as-eco-sensitive-zone (web search); app notes 5:90 sub 5, 9

## SUB 0 | Afforestation, Forestry Terms and Their Benefits
[91:0:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [91:0:0.0] Afforestation | New forest on denuded, degraded or unproductive land
  [91:0:0.1] Reforestation | Re-establishing forest cover in existing / depleted forest areas
  [91:0:0.2] Social forestry | Trees for community needs; term used by the National Commission on Agriculture (1976)
  [91:0:0.3] Extension forestry | Trees along roadsides, canals and railway strips
  [91:0:0.4] Agroforestry | Trees + crops + livestock on the same land
[91:0:1] - **Benefits:** **afforestation controls floods** and **checks soil erosion by running water** (roots bind soil, slow run-off) – deforestation, overgrazing, urbanisation, tilling or removing topsoil worsen them.
[91:0:2] - **Floods worsened by:** unscientific agriculture, disturbing natural drainage channels, colonising flood-plains and river-beds – **NOT reforestation**.
[91:0:3] - **Criticism of social forestry:** drifted into **commercial monocultures** (eucalyptus); fix = mixed native species.
[91:0:4] - **Miyawaki method:** dense, multi-layered **mini-forests of native species in urban areas**; Japanese botanist **Akira Miyawaki**, **1970s**.
[91:0:5] - **Seed bomb (seed ball):** a ball of **mud/clay, dung and seeds** thrown on land to grow trees.
[91:0:6] - **Craib's thinning:** heavy early (advance) thinning of plantations, done in a regular crop **in anticipation of suppression** (I.J. Craib, South Africa).
[91:0:7] - **Wild animals** help natural regeneration through **seed dispersal** (e.g. elephants, hornbills).
[91:0:8] - **Vanamahotsav:** **K.M. Munshi, 1950**; first week of July.

## SUB 5 | Compensatory Afforestation and CAMPA
[91:5:0] - **CA:** forest land diverted → **equal non-forest land**, or **twice the degraded forest land** (Central PSUs / no non-forest land); the agency pays **CA cost + NPV** before the project.
[91:5:1] - **CAMPA:** ad hoc by SC order (2004/2006); statutory via **Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act, 2016**: National and State Funds in the Public Accounts – **non-lapsable, interest-bearing**; split **90% States : 10% National**.
[91:5:2] - **National CAMPA** headed by the **Union Environment Minister**; exists at **national and State** levels; **people's participation is not mandatory** under the Act (UPSC 2019).
[91:5:3] - **Uses:** regeneration, wildlife, catchment treatment, village relocation, human-wildlife conflict, fires; **not** private urban housing. **₹66,000+ crore** held (2024), only **30-35%** used.
[91:5:4] - **FC Rules 2022:** **Accredited CA** = plantations raised **in advance** (land banks); dropped forest-dwellers' **consent** before diversion – disputed by **NCST**.
[91:5:5] - **Van (S.E.S.) Rules 2023:** afforestation under the **Green Credit Programme** can be swapped for CA.

[92:0:0] # The greenhouse gases
[92:0:1] TABLE head: Gas | Formula | Key fact
  [92:0:1.0] Water vapour | H2O | Most abundant GHG and the biggest overall (natural) contributor to the greenhouse effect (about half of it)
  [92:0:1.1] Carbon dioxide | CO2 | Main anthropogenic GHG; product of fossil-fuel combustion; about 66% of radiative forcing by long-lived GHGs (WMO); ~0.04% of air
  [92:0:1.2] Methane (marsh gas) | CH4 | Paddy fields, wetlands, cud-chewing animals, coal mines, landfills; main component of natural gas and biogas
  [92:0:1.3] Nitrous oxide (laughing gas) | N2O | Nitrogen fertilisers, burning fuels and crop residue; also a weak anaesthetic (used in anaesthesia)

  [92:0:1.5] CFCs, HFCs, PFCs, SF6 | — | Synthetic (entirely man-made) fluorinated gases; CFCs are both GHG and ozone-depleting

[92:0:3] - **Most important set:** **CO2, methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour**. **Most responsible pair for warming:** **CO2 and methane**. Four principal human-driven GHGs: CO2, CH4, N2O (+ fluorinated gases) – **never oxygen**.
[92:0:4] - **Statement sets:** "CO2, CFCs, CH4 and N2O are the main GHGs" – true; "marshes, paddy fields, cattle sheds and biogas plants add methane" – true; "CO2 and methane are commonly known GHGs" – true.
[92:0:5] - **Soot (black carbon), sulphur hexafluoride and water vapour** all warm the atmosphere (UPSC 2010: all three).
[92:0:6] - **Tropospheric ozone, methane, nitrous oxide** = a correct GHG group; aerosols, ammonia, lead and methanol are not GHGs.
[92:0:7] - **CFCs:** used as refrigerants, inactive (stable) in the lower atmosphere, destroy stratospheric ozone **and** are GHGs – "CFCs are not responsible for the greenhouse effect" is the **false** statement.
[92:0:8] - **Methane uses:** decomposition of methane gives **carbon black**, used in **printing ink**; Kigali Amendment (2016) phases down **HFCs** – 85% cut avoids about 70-72 billion t CO2e **by 2050**.

  [92:0:10.0] Nitrogen (N2, di-nitrogen) – 78% of air | Does not absorb infrared
  [92:0:10.1] Oxygen (O2) – 21% of air | Does not absorb infrared
  [92:0:10.2] Argon (Ar) – 0.93% | Inert gas

## SUB 1 | The Greenhouse Effect and Heating of the Atmosphere
[92:1:0] # Definition and mechanism
[92:1:1] - **Greenhouse effect** = natural warming of the **lower atmosphere** (surface and troposphere) by **absorption and re-radiation of heat** by GHGs – exam words: "**increase in atmospheric temperature due to concentration of GHGs**"; a **naturally occurring** phenomenon (not caused by biodegradable waste in water).
[92:1:2] - Concept first proposed by **Joseph Fourier** in the **1820s**.
[92:1:3] - Without it, Earth's average temperature would be about **−18°C** (Shankar: −19°C) instead of **+15°C**.
[92:1:4] - **Step 1:** atmosphere is **transparent to incoming short-wave** solar radiation.
[92:1:5] - **Step 2:** the surface re-emits **long-wave infrared** (8,000-25,000 nm) – **not ultraviolet**.
[92:1:6] - **Step 3:** CO2 and water vapour **strongly absorb infrared** (14,000-25,000 nm) – the atmosphere is **opaque to outgoing long-wave**.
[92:1:7] - **Step 4:** GHGs re-radiate heat in all directions, partly back to the surface: GHGs warm the air by **trapping heat** (not generating it).
[92:1:8] - **Glass analogy:** the glass of a greenhouse or of a **solar cooker** lets light in and stops long-wave heat going out – the same principle.
[92:1:9] - **Global warming** = gradual long-term rise in the average temperature of the atmosphere and oceans; mainly human (fossil fuels); GHGs **intensify** the natural effect (TSPSC: all four statements true).
[92:1:10] # Heating of the atmosphere – heat budget
[92:1:11] TABLE head: Insolation (100 units) | Share
  [92:1:11.0] Reflected (albedo): 27 from cloud tops, 2 from snow/ice, rest scattered | 35
  [92:1:11.1] Absorbed by the atmosphere | 14
  [92:1:11.2] Absorbed by the surface | 51
[92:1:12] - The atmosphere is heated **mainly by long-wave terrestrial radiation**, indirectly from the earth, not by incoming solar radiation (UPSC 2024: Statement I wrong, II right).
[92:1:13] - **Clouds and gases reflect about one-fourth** of insolation (27 units from cloud tops); the surface does **not** re-emit heat as UV (CGPSC 2023: only statement 2).
[92:1:14] - **Cloudy nights are warmer**: clouds stop heat radiating away from the ground and send it back.
[92:1:15] - **Fresh snow** has the highest albedo – reflects about **80-85%** of sunlight (more than sand desert, paddy or prairie).
[92:1:16] - UPSC 2025: heat trapped by the atmosphere maintains the average temperature, and CO2 is a good absorber (II, III true); "below freezing **everywhere** without an atmosphere" is too sweeping – the day side would be very hot, like the Moon.
[92:1:17] > The atmosphere is warmed from below: short-wave in, long-wave out. "Heated more by incoming solar radiation" is always the wrong statement.

## SUB 2 | Sources – Fossil Fuels, Deforestation, Paddy and Livestock
[92:2:0] - **Largest source of GHGs:** **burning fossil fuels** (coal, oil, gas) for electricity, transport and industry, plus **deforestation** – the human activities that contribute most to warming.
[92:2:1] - **Primary cause of present global warming** = **increase in GHG concentrations**, not ozone depletion, over-population or solar flares.
[92:2:2] - **Deforestation increases CO2** (stored carbon released, fewer sinks) and causes **loss of biodiversity**; "deforestation is decreasing CO2" is the **incorrect** statement. **Forest regeneration** mitigates the greenhouse effect.
[92:2:3] - **Primary causes of climate change (UPSC/UPPSC 2017):** excessive burning of fossil fuels, exploding numbers of oil-driven automobiles, heavy deforestation – **not increasing solar flares** (I, II and IV).
[92:2:4] - **AP Group-IV 2023:** coal and petroleum burning, deforestation, methane from animal waste – **all three** contribute.
[92:2:5] - Reducing warming: public transport, less refrigerant use, **afforestation** – all help.

## SUB 3 | Contribution Order and Global Warming Potential
[92:3:0] # Two different measures
[92:3:1] TABLE head: Measure | Order and shares
  [92:3:1.0] Whole (natural) greenhouse effect | Water vapour biggest (about half; most abundant)
  [92:3:1.1] Share of enhanced (human) warming – textbook (NCERT Biology XII; NextGen) | CO2 60% > CH4 20% > CFCs 14-15% > N2O 5-6%
  [92:3:1.2] Radiative forcing by long-lived GHGs (WMO) | CO2 ~66% > CH4 ~16% > N2O ~6% > CFCs
[92:3:2] - **Increasing order of contribution (TSPSC 2023):** **N2O < CH4 < CO2**.
[92:3:3] - **Highest contribution to total global warming / main component / largest concentration among CO2, CH4, N2O, CFCs** = **CO2**. **Second most abundant anthropogenic GHG** after CO2 = **methane**.
[92:3:4] - **Water vapour**: humans raise it only **indirectly**, by warming the air (warmer air holds more water vapour) – APPSC Degree Lecturers 2023.
[92:3:5] # Global Warming Potential (GWP)

=== NOTES 5:93 | Ocean Acidification – Causes, Effects on Marine Ecosystems and Coral Reefs
## SUB 0 | Effects on Calcifying Organisms and Marine Life
[93:0:0] - **Calcifiers at risk:** corals, molluscs (**snails, oysters**, mussels, clams), **sea urchins**, crustaceans, **calcareous plankton** – all of them; they need carbonate ions to build calcium carbonate shells and skeletons.
[93:0:1] TABLE head: Organism | Effect
  [93:0:1.0] Pteropods ("sea butterflies") | Shells dissolve; key food for fish
  [93:0:1.1] Corals, bivalve larvae | Use aragonite, more soluble than calcite → more sensitive
  [93:0:1.2] Mussels, oysters | Calcification falls at high CO2
[93:0:2] - **UPSC 2012:** acidification harms **calcareous phytoplankton**, **coral reefs** and animals with **phytoplanktonic larvae** (I, II, III – official key). NextGen also notes less **DMS** from phytoplankton may reduce cloud formation.
[93:0:3] - **Saturation horizon** (below which CaCO3 dissolves) is rising towards the surface.
[93:0:4] - **Warming oceans:** marine fish are shifting **towards the poles** (cooler water).
[93:0:5] > "All of the above" is the usual key for calcifier lists.
[93:0:6] ~~ Sources: Shankar Environment 15 (OCR) p.16-18; NextGen Environment APPSC Part 8.2 p.8; PMF Environment Part 4 p.18
## SUB 1 | Cause and Chemistry of Ocean Acidification
[93:1:0] - **Ocean acidification** = ongoing **fall in ocean pH** caused by the **uptake (dissolution) of atmospheric CO2** – the **primary cause**; not oil spills, sewage, microplastics or acid rain (acid rain matters only locally).
[93:1:1] - Called the **"evil twin of global warming"** and **"the other CO2 problem"**.
[93:1:2] - Oceans absorb about **one-third** of human CO2 (NextGen: 30-40%), buffering climate change.
[93:1:3] # Chemistry
[93:1:4] - **Step 1:** CO2 + H2O → **carbonic acid** → H+ + bicarbonate.
[93:1:5] - **Step 2:** extra **H+ binds carbonate ions** – carbonate concentration falls.
[93:1:6] - **Step 3:** **less carbonate** left for shells and skeletons; the ocean's ability to neutralise CO2 (buffering) falls.
[93:1:7] - Surface pH **8.2 (pre-industrial) → ~8.1**: a fall of 0.1 = about **26-30% rise in acidity**; the ocean is **still alkaline**.
[93:1:8] # Factors and results (APPSC NTR Health Univ. match)
[93:1:9] TABLE head: Factor | Result
  [93:1:9.0] CO2 uptake by oceans | Steady decline in ocean pH
  [93:1:9.1] Melting of Arctic ice | Exposes water to air → more surface CO2, faster acidification
  [93:1:9.2] Plankton blooms (eutrophication) | Bloom collapse → bacterial respiration adds CO2
  [93:1:9.3] Decreased carbonate ion concentration | Lower carbonate → less ability to neutralise CO2
[93:1:10] - Worse in **cold, temperate and Arctic waters**: CO2 is more soluble in cold water.
[93:1:11] - **Cement** sector: about 5-8% of global anthropogenic CO2.
[93:1:12] > pH falls only 0.1, yet acidity rises ~30% (log scale). The ocean has not turned acidic.
[93:1:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC Part 8.2 p.7-8; Shankar Environment 15 (OCR) p.16-17; PMF Environment Part 4 p.16-18

[93:4:0] - Reef-building corals secrete **calcium carbonate** skeletons; symbiotic algae **zooxanthellae** give food and colour.
[93:4:1] - **Bleaching:** heat-stressed corals lose zooxanthellae; warming raises bleaching, acidification cuts calcifying ability.
[93:4:2] - **4th global bleaching event** (declared by **NOAA and ICRI**, Apr 2024): Jan 2023-Mar 2025 hit **84%** of reefs; earlier events **1998, 2010, 2014-17**.

## SUB 0 | Observed Impacts – Ice Sheets, Sea Level, Tipping Points and Species
[94:0:0] # Already observed (cryosphere)
[94:0:1] - **Observed consequence (APPSC Degree Lecturers 2023):** a **significant reduction in ice-sheet mass** – Greenland and Antarctica have lost mass for about two decades (Greenland ~278 bn t/yr and Antarctica ~155 bn t/yr in 2006-15; loss has **quadrupled since the 1990s**). Falling global temperature, more forest cover and fewer heatwaves are the opposite of what is observed.
[94:0:2] - **Effects of climate change (NextGen):** rising sea levels, **shrinking mountain glaciers**, accelerating loss of the cryosphere, ocean acidification.
[94:0:3] - **Most fragile, first affected (UKPSC 2010):** the **Arctic and Greenland ice sheet** – the Arctic warms **about four times** faster than the global average (polar amplification).
[94:0:4] - **WMO (2025 report):** oceans hold **over 90% of excess heat**; Arctic sea ice near record low; glacier loss threatens water for **about 2 billion people** (not 200 million).
[94:0:5] - **Ice core = cryogenic indicator** of past climate (UPPCS 2015): drilled from Antarctic/Greenland ice sheets or mountain glaciers; the **lower layers are older**; trapped air bubbles record past CO₂. Pollen, tree rings and evaporites are not cryogenic.

[94:0:7] # Sea-level rise
[94:0:8] - **Long-term (eustatic) change:** caused by **melting of ice sheets** and thermal expansion of seawater (CDS 2016). Atmospheric pressure, water density and floating **icebergs** cause only short-term or no change.
[94:0:9] - **Principal causes:** **thermal expansion + melting glaciers and ice sheets**; global sea level up **~20 cm (1901-2018)**, ~11 cm since 1993; the rate has doubled to ~4.5 mm/yr. **Melting polar ice and sea-level rise** = most important effect of warming.
[94:0:10] - "Sea-level rise of one metre by 2070" is **not** a correct statement (IPCC: ~0.43-0.84 m by 2100).
[94:0:11] # Tipping points
[94:0:12] - **Tipping point:** a threshold where **small changes trigger large, self-perpetuating, potentially irreversible** shifts.
[94:0:13] - **Loss of Arctic (sea) ice = potential tipping point (APPSC G2 2025):** bright ice (high albedo) melts → darker ocean absorbs more heat → more melting. Cutting CO₂ or less solar radiation are not tipping points.
