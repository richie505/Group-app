=== Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 – Year, Scope and Key Sections
# Year and scope
- **Enacted by Parliament in 1972** (assent 9 Sept 1972) – not 1962, 1974, 1982, 1986 or 1990; first comprehensive wildlife law.
- **Protects wild animals, birds and plants** and manages their habitats – **not animals only**; it is the Act that **regulates National Parks and Wildlife Sanctuaries** (not EPA 1986, FCA 1980 or BD Act 2002).
- **Poaching** = illegal hunting of wild animals – barred by the Act.
- **66 sections, 7 chapters**; amended 1982, 1986, 1991, 1993, 2002, 2006, 2013, **2022**.
# Key sections
| Section | Provision |
|---|---|
| 2(1) | "Animal" = amphibians, birds, mammals, reptiles, their young, eggs of birds and reptiles |
| 2(37) | "Wildlife" |
| 9 | Hunting of scheduled animals **prohibited**, subject to exceptions |
| 11 | Animal **dangerous to human life** (or crops), diseased or disabled beyond recovery may be captured/killed with the Chief Wildlife Warden's permission |
| 12 | Permits for education, research, scientific management, translocation |
| 38J | **Teasing, molesting, injuring or feeding** animals in a zoo prohibited |
| 39(1) | Wild animals = **State Government property**; in a Centre-declared sanctuary/NP = Central Government |
| 38V(3) | Tiger Conservation Plan for every TR (2006) |
| 62 | Centre may notify a non-Schedule I animal as vermin |
- **Gharial, Indian wild ass, wild buffalo** cannot be hunted by anyone except as the law permits (UPSC 2017: all three).
- **UPSC 2022 (official key "1 and 2"):** wild animals are government property; a protected animal gets equal protection inside or outside PAs; mere *apprehension* of danger is not enough to kill it.
- **Protected animal may be killed** when it endangers human life **and** when it is diseased – both true, but disease does not explain the danger clause.
- **Mining:** barred inside NPs and sanctuaries; SC bars mining within **1 km** of every NP/sanctuary unless a larger ESZ is notified.

=== WPA Schedules and the 2022 Amendment
| Before 2022 (6 schedules) | After 2022 (4 schedules) |
|---|---|
| I and II: absolute protection (tiger, rhino) | I: highest protection |
| III and IV: lesser (game species) | II: lower protection |
| V: **vermin** (common crow, fruit bats, rats, mice) | III: **protected plants** |
| VI: **specified plants** – cultivation only with a **licence** | IV: **CITES**-listed specimens |
- **Number of schedules:** **6** in the 1972 Act (the usual key); **4** since the 2022 amendment (in force 1 April 2023).
- **Schedule I effect:** a tortoise in Schedule I gets **the same protection as the tiger** (not "extinct in the wild" or "endemic").
- **Schedule VI (old):** a **licence is required to cultivate** the plant; a specified plant cannot be picked, uprooted or traded without permission.
- **Flying fox** = a **fruit bat** (Pteropus), not a kite, stork or vulture; it does **not** feed on blood. It was **vermin (Schedule V)**; since 2022 it is in **Schedule II**.
- **Schedule I examples:** Asiatic lion, tiger, elephant, snow leopard, rhino, Gangetic dolphin, dugong, GIB, **blackbuck**; mugger (Kurnool, Sep 2026) and rock eagle-owl (Dharmavaram, Aug 2026) in news.
# 2022 amendment
- **Schedules 6 → 4**; vermin schedule deleted (Sec 62 power stays with the **Central Government**); **CITES** provisions (Management and Scientific Authorities); Centre regulates **invasive alien species** (African catfish, Lantana).
- Voluntary **surrender of captive animals without compensation**; Chief Wildlife Warden in every State; Centre may notify conservation reserves.
- Fines: specially protected animals – minimum **₹25,000** (from ₹10,000); general violations – maximum **₹1 lakh** (from ₹25,000).
# Earlier amendments
- **2002:** **NBWL** replaced the Indian Board for Wildlife; **Conservation Reserves** (government land, e.g. **Tsarap Chu**) and **Community Reserves** (private/community land).
- **2006:** **NTCA and WCCB**.

=== Protected Areas and Community Reserves under WPA
- **Five kinds:** wildlife sanctuary, national park, conservation reserve, community reserve, tiger reserve.
- **Community Reserve (Sec 36C):** private/community land, declared by the **State Government**; run by a **Community Reserve Management Committee (Sec 36D)** – not the Chief Wildlife Warden.
- In a community reserve **hunting is not allowed** and people may **collect non-timber forest produce**, but **traditional agriculture (jhum) is not allowed** → UPSC 2023: "only two" statements correct.
- **NP vs sanctuary:** NP – no grazing, no human activity except as the Chief Wildlife Warden permits; sanctuary – regulated grazing.
- **India's PAs:** **1,75,169.42 sq km = 5.32%** of land (2026).
- **Eco-sensitive zone:** the **National Chambal Sanctuary (MP)** was notified as an ESZ on **2 March 2020**.

=== Forest (Conservation) Act 1980 / Van Adhiniyam
- **Enacted 1980** (27 Dec 1980, in force from **25 Oct 1980**) – not 1976, 1978, 1981 or 1988.
- **Sec 2:** **prior approval of the Central Government (MoEF)** to de-reserve a forest or use forest land for **non-forest purposes** – not the Collector, State or FSI. FCA = the Act that **regulates forest-land diversion**.
- **2023 amendment:** renamed **Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980** (w.e.f. 29 Dec 2023); covers land notified as forest under IFA 1927 or any law, and land **recorded as forest on or after 25 Oct 1980**; **excludes land converted before 12 Dec 1996**.
- **Exemptions:** strategic linear projects within **100 km** of IB/LoC/LAC; zoos, safaris, eco-tourism; no Gram Sabha consent required (a key criticism).
- **Compensatory afforestation:** equal non-forest land, else **twice** the degraded forest land + **NPV**.
- **T.N. Godavarman (1995 writ; 1996 order):** "forest" = every area recorded as forest **irrespective of ownership**; the 2023 amendment narrows this.
- **AP diversions:** **3,474.57 ha** (1 Jan 2015-5 Feb 2019); **149.5687 ha** (1 Apr-31 Dec 2025).

=== Indian Forest Act 1927 and Colonial Forest Laws
| Year | Law | Point |
|---|---|---|
| 1865 | First Indian Forest Act | **Drafted 1865** |
| 1878 | Indian Forest Act | Created **reserved, protected, village** forests |
| 1927 | Indian Forest Act | Consolidating law, still in force |
- **Degree of protection:** **Reserved > Protected > Village** forest; a **village forest** is a **reserved forest** assigned to a village community.
- **Reserved forest (Ch II):** everything banned unless permitted; **Sec 26** = acts prohibited in a reserved forest, incl. punishable **trespass**.
- **Protected forest:** activities allowed unless prohibited – people could take **head-loads of wood**, collect produce for their own use and **graze cattle** (entry not barred).
- **Unclassed forest:** outside the reserved and protected categories. **Sec 35:** State may regulate non-government forests.
- **2017 amendment** (ordinance 2017, Act 2018): **bamboo grown outside forest areas** removed from the definition of "tree" (**Sec 2(7)**) – may be felled and moved without permit. Bamboo **inside** forests is not freed.
- **Colonial plantations:** British policy encouraged **monocropping through plantations** (tea, coffee, teak), a cause of environmental degradation.

=== National Forest Policy 1952 and 1988
| Feature | NFP 1952 | NFP 1988 |
|---|---|---|
| National target | One-third | **One-third (33%)** of geographical area |
| Hills | 60% | **Two-thirds** |
| Classes / thrust | **National, Protected, Village forests + Tree-lands** (National Park not a class) | Ecological stability, people's participation |
- **NFP 1988 includes:** afforestation and wasteland development; reforestation in existing forests; **wood substitutes and alternative fuels** – NOT wide use of **insecticides and pesticides**. "Two-thirds of the country under forest" is wrong (hills only).
- **Ecological balance:** Forest Policy, EPA 1986, Industrial and Educational Policy all relate to it (UPPSC: "all the above").
- **Draft NFP 2018**; **NAPCC launched 2008** (not 2018).
- **Order:** IFA 1927 → NFP 1952 → WPA 1972 → FCA 1980 → EPA 1986 → NFP 1988 → BD Act 2002 → FRA 2006.

=== Biological Diversity Act 2002 and Which Act Protects What
| Act | What it does |
|---|---|
| WPA 1972 | Wild animals, birds, plants and habitats |
| EPA 1986 | Umbrella law – **protection and improvement of the environment** |
| FCA 1980 | Forest-land diversion |
| PCA Act 1960 | Cruelty to animals; parent Act of AWBI |
| BD Act 2002 | CBD, access and benefit sharing |
- **"Nature (Protection) Act, 2000"**, "Forests Protection Act 1982", "EPA 1996" do **not exist**.
- **NGT** cannot hear **WPA 1972** or FRA 2006 cases.
- **BD Act 2002:** three tiers – **NBA (Chennai, 2003)**, State Biodiversity Boards, **Biodiversity Management Committees** (prepare **People's Biodiversity Registers**); **Biodiversity Heritage Sites** under **Sec 37** (State Government).
- **2023 amendment (in force 1 April 2024):** offences **decriminalised** (civil penalties); exemption for AYUSH practitioners and **BMC-certified** cultivated medicinal plants; NBA approval **180 → 90 days**; foreign entities **still need** prior NBA approval.
- **AP (May 2026):** more than **13,500 BMCs**, **12,000 PBRs**.
- **Constitution:** 42nd Amendment (1976) moved forests (**Entry 17A**) and wild animals and birds (**Entry 17B**) to the Concurrent List; inserted **Art 48A** (DPSP) and **Art 51A(g)** (Fundamental Duty). A State Bill amending WPA (e.g. Maharashtra 2026, Sec 12; leopard **Schedule I → II**) needs the **President's assent** because it amends a **central law**.
