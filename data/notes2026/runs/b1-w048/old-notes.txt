=== NOTES 1:48 | Satavahana Art and Architecture – Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda and Rock-cut Caves
## SUB 0 | Amaravati Mahachaitya
[48:0:0] - **Where:** ancient **Dhanyakataka** (Amaravati–Dharanikota) on the **Krishna**, Andhra Pradesh (UPSC 2006 match); old Guntur district (now Palnadu district).
[48:0:1] - **Built under:** the **Satavahanas** (APPSC PYQ) – not Vengi Chalukyas, Ikshvakus or Salankayanas; NextGen dates the main building to **Pulomavi II**.
[48:0:2] - **Enlarged, with the stone railing round the Mahachaitya:** in the reign of **Yajnasri Satakarni**, by his contemporary **Acharya Nagarjuna** (G2 2001; Sachivalayam ×2; G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ).
[48:0:3]   - Options printed "Gautamiputra Yagnasri" = Yajnasri Satakarni. When the question asks for the king, do not mark Nagarjuna.
[48:0:4] - **⚠ Trap:** Vasishthiputra Satakarni (earlier vedika sculptures), Vasishthiputra Pulomavi, Gautamiputra and Srimukha are distractors; final enlargement under the Ikshvakus.
[48:0:5] - **Origin:** Ashokan age – **Mahadeva Bhikshu**, first Buddhist teacher in Andhra, enshrined the Buddha's relics; Naga king **Nagashoka** helped build it at Dharanikota.
[48:0:6] # Size
[48:0:7] - **Biggest stupa in Andhra Pradesh:** Amaravati (G2 2019 prelims; G2 PYQ) and the **largest in South India** (Welfare Officers 2017); Bhattiprolu is the **oldest**.
[48:0:8] - **Larger and more artistic:** than Sanchi and Bharhut (Welfare Officers 2017).
[48:0:9] - **⚠ Sources differ:** NextGen calls Sanchi "the largest stupa in India".
[48:0:10]   - Settled: Amaravati's railing was ~59 m across vs Sanchi's 36.6 m dome, so Amaravati was larger (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaravati_Stupa; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanchi).
[48:0:11] # Ayaka platform
[48:0:12] - **Platform with ayaka (Aryaka) pillars:** hallmark of the **Amaravati** stupa (UPPCS 2022); unique to Andhra stupas.
[48:0:13] - **Layout:** 4 ayaka platforms at the cardinal points × 5 pillars = five events of the Buddha's life.
[48:0:14] TABLE head: Event | Symbol
  [48:0:14.0] Birth | Lotus
  [48:0:14.1] Great Departure | Horse
  [48:0:14.2] Enlightenment | Bodhi (peepal) tree
  [48:0:14.3] First sermon | Wheel
  [48:0:14.4] Mahaparinirvana | Stupa
[48:0:15] # Discovery and the Amaravati school
[48:0:16] - **Identified:** Col. **Colin Mackenzie**, 1797; excavated by Robert Sewell, 1877; James **Fergusson** (Tree and Serpent Worship): "epitome of Indian architecture".
[48:0:17] - **Ayaka gift:** the artisan **Siddhattha** gave five ayaka pillars at the eastern gate.
[48:0:18] - **Amaravati school:** white **Palnadu limestone**; narrative Jataka reliefs; early aniconic Buddha (tree, throne, footprints), later human form.
[48:0:19] - **Sculptures today:** British Museum (London), Government Museum (Chennai), Amaravati site museum.
[48:0:20] - **Birth symbol in full:** **lotus and bull**.
[48:0:21] - **Amaravati style:** marked by **movement and emotional expression**; its Jataka and Buddha-life subjects do not explain the style.
[48:0:22] - **Colin Mackenzie:** the first **Surveyor General of India**.
[48:0:23] - **UNESCO tentative list:** **Lepakshi Veerabhadra Temple** (monolithic Nandi) was added in 2022.
[48:0:24] - **Amaravati and UNESCO:** the app key puts the **Amaravati Buddhist Stupa** on India's UNESCO tentative list.
[48:0:25] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.10, p.21-22, p.29-30; Rocket AP History p.25, p.34; app notes 1:48 (sub 1, 2, 10); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaravati_Stupa
[48:0:26] - **Why the Deccan got great Buddhist monuments:** royal patronage (**Satavahanas, Ikshvakus**), easy local stone of the Deccan plateau, and the **Krishna–Godavari** rivers for logistics and long worker stays – not the (later) Bhakti movement.
## SUB 1 | Stupa, Chaitya, Vihara – Terms
[48:1:0] - **Chaitya:** **prayer hall / place of worship** (Chaitya-shala, Chaitya-griha); rectangular, ending in an **apse (horseshoe plan)** with the stupa; horseshoe **chaitya window** on the facade.
[48:1:1] - **Vihara:** **monastery – dwelling of the monks**; square, cells round a central hall (**Madhyashala**).
[48:1:2] > Most-asked angle (UPSC 2013; IAS 2013; UP 2001; APPSC PYQ): "Chaitya = place of worship, Vihara = dwelling of monks".
[48:1:3] - **Stupa, chaitya and vihara:** all **Buddhist** structures (State PSC) – not Ajivika, Vaishnava or Shaiva.
[48:1:4] - **Stupa:** a **mound**, generally a **repository of relics**, and a **votive and commemorative** structure.
[48:1:5] - **⚠ Trap:** the stupa concept is **not Buddhist in origin** – it predates Buddhism (UPSC 2023: only two of three statements correct).
[48:1:6] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [48:1:6.0] Stupa | Mound
  [48:1:6.1] Garbhagriha | Place where the deity's image is installed
  [48:1:6.2] Mandapa | Place in temples where people assemble
  [48:1:6.3] Pradakshina patha | Circular path around the stupa
[48:1:7] > Match angle (APPSC): 1-(b), 2-(a), 3-(d), 4-(c).
[48:1:8] - **Arama:** stupa + vihara + chaitya together (e.g. Guntupalli).
[48:1:9] - **Stupa types:** Dhatugarbha (bodily relics), Paribhogika (objects used by the Buddha), Uddeshika (symbolic).
[48:1:10] - **Uposatha:** monks' meeting in the vihara's central hall every **15 days**.
[48:1:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.22; app notes 1:48 (sub 0)

[48:2:1] - **Centres of Buddhism in AP:** Amaravati, Bhattiprolu, Guntupalli – "all the above" (AEE 2011).

[48:2:7] - **House viharas:** a distinctive feature of **Guntupalli**.

[48:2:9] - **Thotlakonda finds:** its 1988–92 excavations found the main stupa, votive stupas and **Satavahana and Roman coins**.
[48:2:10] - **AP Buddhist International Tourism Circuit:** Amaravati, Thotlakonda-Bavikonda, Bojjannakonda-Sankaram, **Salihundam**, Ghantasala.

## SUB 3 | Rock-cut Caves – Karle, Guntupalli
[48:3:0] - **Karle (Pune):** the **largest and most evolved rock-cut chaitya hall of the Hinayana phase** (UPRO 2014); not Pitalkhora, Junnar or Bedsa. Bhaja is earlier.
[48:3:1] - **Guntupalli (West Godavari, now Eluru district):** its early chaityagrihas and viharas are **rock-cut** ✓ (UPPCS 2017 ×2).
[48:3:2] - **⚠ Trap:** eastern Deccan chaityas and viharas are usually **structural**, not rock-cut – Guntupalli is the exception → "only 1 is correct".
[48:3:3] - **⚠ Outdated:** "Guntupalli in West Godavari" — now **Eluru district** (Kamavarapukota mandal) after the 2022 reorganisation.
[48:3:4] - **Later imitation:** Buddhists cut the first rock-cut chaityas; Vaishnavas, Shaivas and Jainas imitated them later ✓ – at the same sites, not far away ✗ (NDA 2011).
[48:3:5] - **Western Deccan caves:** Nasik, Naneghat, Bhaja, Kanheri, Junnar, Bedsa, Pitalkhora; **Ajanta** Caves 9-10 – Shwetagaja (Chhaddanta) Jataka painting in Cave 10.
[48:3:6] - **Peak of rock-cut activity:** the **Sahyadris** of the western Deccan (Nasik, Naneghat, Karle, Bhaja, Kanheri, Junnar, Ajanta, Bedsa, Pitalkhora).
[48:3:7] - **Karle details:** **12 dance-couple** sculptures; **Pulumavi II** donated a village to it.
[48:3:8] - **Bedsa (Pune):** chaitya with **bell-capital pillars**.
[48:3:9] - **Nasik:** viharas bearing **Satavahana inscriptions**.
[48:3:10] - **Sankaram and Bagh:** Sankaram (Anakapalli) has horseshoe-shaped caves; the **Bagh** cave viharas (MP) are linked to the Satavahanas in exam keys.
[48:3:11] - **Bhairavakonda (Nellore–Prakasam border):** **8 rock-cut cave temples of Shiva** (Pallava style) – Hindu, not Buddhist.
[48:3:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.22; Rocket AP History p.8; app notes 1:48 (sub 9)
[48:3:13] - **Earliest surviving historical paintings of India:** Ajanta Caves 9–10, of the **Satavahana period** (2nd c. BCE) – earlier than Gupta, Chalukya or Paramara work.

## SUB 6 | Nagarjunakonda (Ikshvaku)
[48:6:0] - **Dynasty:** the **Ikshvakus** built the stupas, viharas and temples at Nagarjunakonda, backing both Vedic and Buddhist traditions (Welfare Officers) – not Mauryas, Satavahanas or Pallavas.
[48:6:1] - **Names:** **Vijayapuri** / **Sriparvata** – the Ikshvaku capital; tutelary goddess **Hariti**.
[48:6:2] - **Monuments:** 30+ stupas, viharas, apsidal chaityas; Ikshvaku queens chief donors; a Sinhala vihara; the first structural Hindu temples of Andhra.
[48:6:3] - **Satavahana link:** **Yajnasri Satakarni** built the 1,500-room **Sriparvata Sangharama** for Nagarjuna.
[48:6:4] - **Sects:** Bahushrutiya, Aparasaila and **Mahishasaka** (the only Hinayana sect there).
[48:6:5] - **Discovery:** A.R. Saraswati, 1926; excavated by Longhurst, 1927-31; drowned by the Nagarjunasagar reservoir – monuments rebuilt on an island.
[48:6:6] - **Golden Temple (Swarna Devalayam):** of Ehuvala Chamtamula at Nagarjunakonda – bears the **first Sanskrit inscription** of Andhra and South India.
[48:6:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Satavahanas p.10, p.30; app notes 1:48 (sub 3)

## SUB 0 | Royal Women Donors
[51:0:0] - **Dual pattern:** kings were **Vedic**; **royal women** were the main patrons of Buddhism at Nagarjunakonda, giving **without male intervention** – a trend of women endowing Buddhism while the men patronised Hindu cults (Tribal Welfare Officer PYQ: statements 1 and 2 true, "women took no part in donations" false).
[51:0:1] - **Nagarjunakonda inscriptions** record gifts by the Ikshvaku family both to **Hindu temples** and to **Buddhist monks**.
[51:0:2] - **Santisri (Chamtisri) and Harmyasri** = **sisters of Vasishthiputra Santamula (Chantamula I)** (Welfare Officers 2017; APPSC PYQ) – not of Virapurushadatta or Ehuvala.

## SUB 1 | Agrarian Economy and Agraharas
[51:1:0] - **Sri Chantamula (Vasishthiputra Santamula)** tried to make the **intelligentsia partners in agricultural development** (G2 2012) – gave **gold, cows, ploughs and land**; brought **Brahmins from the north** and gave them **agraharas**.
[51:1:1] - **Agraharas** = grants of land and its royal income, mostly to **Brahmins** (JA Panchayat Raj 2012; NTR Univ. JA) – not to Shudras, Kshatriyas, leaders, goldsmiths or widows.
[51:1:2] - He performed **Ashvamedha, Vajapeya, Agnistoma** – altar found at **Nagarjunakonda**.
[51:1:3] - **Revenue split four ways:** administration + army; temples + festivals; poets + scholars; gifts to religious bodies; some on **tanks and canals** – not on overseas colonies.
[51:1:4] - **Shift:** from the Satavahana **trade economy** to an **agrarian, agrahara-based** early medieval order.

## SUB 3 | Society
[51:3:0] - **Shudras** were the majority; Brahmins rose through **Brahmadeya** grants (exempt from **18 taxes**) – a new **landlord class**.
[51:3:1] - **Cross-cousin marriage** began with the Ikshvakus; alliances with the **Kshatrapas of Ujjain** and the **Chutus** (Kodabalisri married Vishnurudra Sivalananda Satakarni).
[51:3:2] - Records show **matronymics** (Vasishthiputra, Mathariputra), naming after the **grandfather** (Ehuvala Santamula) and **sati** (Satisahagamanam).
