=== Capital Vijayapuri and the Dynasty at a Glance
# Capital
- **Capital:** **Vijayapuri** = modern **Nagarjunakonda**, in the Nagarjuna Sagar valley, now **Palnadu** district (asked 7 times: G2 2008 ×3, G2 2011 ×3, G2 2012).
- **⚠ Trap:** not **Amaravati** (Dhanyakataka, a Satavahana centre), not Vengi (Salankayanas), not Vemulawada, Srikakulam or Pithunda.
- **⚠ Option clash (G2 2012):** "Vijayapuri" and "Nagarjunakonda" both appear – the exam key is **Vijayapuri**, the ancient name of the capital.
- **Name:** Vijayapuri is thought to be named after Satavahana **Vijaya Satakarni**; the hill was **Sriparvata**.
- **Excavated** at Nagarjunakonda by the Andhra archaeologist **R. Subrahmanyam** (1954–60 rescue dig).
- **Sites and ports:** **Ghantasala (Kantakasala)** and **Motupalli** = ports of the Ikshvaku period; Phanigiri (Suryapet, Telangana) – a pot of **3,730 lead coins** of the Ikshvaku period found in **2024**.
# The dynasty in one look
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Successors of | **Satavahanas** in the Krishna valley (3rd c. CE); first their feudatories (**Mahatalavaras**) |
| Period | About **75 years**, c. **AD 225–300** (3rd century CE) |
| Kings | **Puranas: 7**; **inscriptions confirm 4** |
| Symbol / court language | **Lion** / **Prakrit** |
| Official religion | Vedic-Brahmanical (Shaivism); Buddhism patronised by the queens |
- **Immediate successors of the Satavahanas** in the Krishna valley = **Ikshvakus**, not the Pallavas or Vishnukundins.
- **Bridge dynasty:** carried the **Amaravati school to its peak**, built the **first temples** and moved Andhra towards an **agrarian, Puranic order**; they did **not** replace Buddhism with Jainism.
- **Deccan dynasty that promoted Buddhism** (G2 2000 backlog): the **Ikshvakus** – their period is the golden age of Buddhism in Andhra.

=== Rulers – Founder to Last King
| No. | King | Exam fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vasishthiputra Sri Santamula (Chamtamula, Shantamula I)** | **Founder**; overthrew the last Satavahana **Pulomavi III**; Vedic sacrifices |
| 2 | **Mathariputra Virapurushadatta** | **Greatest** king; golden age of Buddhism; **Sri Parvatadhipati**, "Ashoka of the South" |
| 3 | **Ehuvala Santamula (Shantamula II)** | Son of Virapurushadatta and **Bhatti Mahadevi**; ruled about **24 years**; first Sanskrit inscriptions |
| 4 | **Rudrapurushadatta** | **Last** king; **Gurajala (Gurazala)** inscription |
- **Founder:** **Vasishthiputra Santamula** (ASO 2011) – not Vishnuvardhana, Satakarni or Ganapatideva.
- **Lineage:** **Veera Purushadatta** belongs to the **Ikshvakus** (G1 1995) – not the Rashtrakutas, Vemulawada Chalukyas or Salankayanas.
- **Last king:** **Rudrapurushadatta** (G2 2019 ×2); Sri Chantamula, Virapurushadatta, Ehuvala are distractors.
- **Gurajala (Gurazala) inscription:** issued by **Rudrapurushadatta** (APPSC PYQ ×2) – Jaggayyapeta belongs to Virapurushadatta.
# Vasishthiputra Santamula – Vedic king and agrarian patron
- **Sacrifices:** **Ashvamedha, Vajapeya, Agnishtoma, Agnihotra, Rajasuya**; evidence = the **Ashvamedha platform** found at **Nagarjunakonda**.
- **Intelligentsia in farming:** **Sri Chantamula** tried to make the **intelligentsia partners in agricultural development** (G2 2012 ×2) – gifts of land, ploughs, bullocks and cows to Brahmins.
- **Titles:** **Gosatasahasra**, **Halasatasahasra** (Satasahasrahalaka), **Mahadanapati** – giver of lakhs of cows and ploughs; crores of gold coins.
- **Inscriptions:** Rentala, Kesanapalli, Dechepalli.
# Virapurushadatta
- **Sri Parvatadhipati** (lord of Sriparvata) = **Virapurushadatta** (APPSC PYQ ×2) – not Shantamula I or II or Rudrapurushadatta.
- Only Ikshvaku king to turn to **Buddhism**; his queens and aunts were great Buddhist donors.
# Ehuvala Santamula and Rudrapurushadatta
- **Ehuvala** was named after his **grandfather** (Santamula) – an Ikshvaku naming custom; under him **Sanskrit** entered the records and Brahmanical temples rose beside Buddhist ones.
- **Gummadidurru** inscription = Ehuvala Santamula.
- **Rudrapurushadatta:** Gurajala record (land grant to **Halampuraswami**, i.e. Nagulapuram, Palnadu); defeated by Pallava **Simhavarman** (Manchikallu).

=== Puranic Names, Origin Theories and the Ayodhya Claim
# Puranic names
- **Sriparvatiyas (Sri Parvatiya Andhras):** the **Puranas** call the Ikshvakus "Sriparvatiyas" (UPPCS 2020) – not the Vakatakas, Sakas or Kharavela.
- They are also called **Andhrabhrityas** – a Puranic name of the dynasty, not a title of Ehuvala Santamula.
# Origin theories
| Theory | Proponent |
|---|---|
| **Andhra / Telugu people** | **Bishop Caldwell** (G2 2011 ×2) – the accepted view |
| Tamil | **K. Gopalachari** |
| Kannada | **Vogel** |
| North Indian | **Rapson, Buhler** |
| Name from **ikshu** (sugarcane, their tribal symbol) | **B.S.L. Hanumantha Rao** |
- **Proof of Andhra origin:** southern names (Bapi, Adavi, Chelike) + the southern custom of marrying the **maternal uncle's daughter** (cross-cousin marriage).
- **⚠ Trap:** V.A. Smith and Oldenberg are distractors; Gopalachari = Tamil, not Telugu.
# The Ayodhya claim (G2 Mains 2025)
- **Incorrect statement (official final key):** "In their inscriptions, the Ikshvakus of Vijayapuri described themselves as descendants of the **Ikshvakus of Ayodhya**."
- **True statements in the same question:** Puranas call them **Sri Parvathiyas**; matrimonial ties with the **Sakas / Western Satraps of Ujjain**; **Virapurushadatta's** reign = golden age of Buddhism in Andhra.
- The **Ayodhya** link comes from texts (Ramayana, Puranas, the Kannada Jain *Dharmamrita* of Nyayasena); the Vijayapuri records use the Ikshvaku name (a record of Ehuvala traces him to the legendary Ikshvakus) but do not name Ayodhya – hence the key.

=== Marriage Alliances
- **Princess of Ujjain:** **Virapurushadatta** married **Rudradhara Bhattarika**, daughter of the Saka (Western Kshatrapa) ruler of **Ujjain** (Rudrasena II) → tie with the **Sakas / Western Kshatrapas** (APPSC PYQ).
- **⚠ Trap:** she came from Ujjain (Sakas), not from the Pallavas; not the wife of Santamula or Ehuvala.
- **Santamula's sister Santisri** married **Skandasri of the Pugiya** family; his daughter **Adavi Santisri** married **Skanda Visakha of the Dhanika** family.
- **Virapurushadatta's daughter Kodabalisri** married **Vishnurudra Sivalananda Satakarni of the Chutus**.
- **Cross-cousin marriage** (maternal uncle's daughter) was practised – Virapurushadatta married his aunt's daughters **Bapisri** and **Shastisri**.

=== Inscriptions and the End of the Dynasty
| Inscription | Issuer | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Alluru | Virapurushadatta (NextGen; Rocket names Ehuvala) | Gifts to many sections and institutions; Ikshvakus as Satavahana vassals |
| Nagarjunakonda | Virapurushadatta and royal women | Founding of the kingdom at Vijayapuri by Santamula; Buddhist gifts (ayaka pillars) |
| Jaggayyapeta | Virapurushadatta | Ikshvaku rule in the lower Krishna |
| Gummadidurru | Ehuvala Santamula | Later Ikshvaku rule |
| Gurajala | Rudrapurushadatta | Last Ikshvaku king |
| **Manchikallu** | Pallava **Simhavarman** | Found near Vijayapuri; Rudrapurushadatta defeated → Pallava rule |
| **Mayidavolu** plates | **Sivaskandavarman** (crown prince, from Kanchi) | Grant concerning Dhanyakataka → Pallava hold on the Krishna–Guntur region |
- **Who ended the Ikshvakus:** **D.C. Sircar** – Simhavarman helped bring them down (end of the 3rd c. CE); **T.V. Mahalingam** – Simhavarman bore no royal title, **Sivaskandavarman I** completed the takeover.
- An **Abhira** inscription at the capital is read by some as an Abhira occupation; by the mid-4th century the **Pallavas** held the region south of the Krishna, the **Brihatphalayanas** the north.
- **Firsts:** first dated inscriptions (year, month, day) and first sculptors' names; first structural Hindu temples (Pushpabhadraswami, Ashtabhuja Narayana at Nagarjunakonda).
