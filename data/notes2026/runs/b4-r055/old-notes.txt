=== NOTES 4:55 | World Population - Distribution, Density, Factors and Theories (malthus)
## SUB 0 | Malthusian Theory
[55:0:0] # Core Logic
[55:0:1] - **Thomas Malthus** (18th century), **An Essay on the Principle of Population**, **1798**.
[55:0:2] - Population grows **geometrically** (exponentially); food grows only **arithmetically**.
[55:0:3] - Unchecked growth leads to **scarcity and famine**.
[55:0:4] - Malthus = the scientist whose theory says population is too large with severe consequences: the **overpopulation** theory (APPSC).
[55:0:5] # Two Checks
[55:0:6] TABLE head: Check | Acts on | Examples
  [55:0:6.0] Preventive | Lowers births | Moral restraint, late marriage, celibacy
  [55:0:6.1] Positive | Raises deaths | Famine, disease, war
[55:0:7] Preventive = fewer births; Positive = more deaths. Do not swap them.
[55:0:8] # Criticism
[55:0:9] - **Technology, innovation and sustainable resource management** outpaced his predictions.
[55:0:10] - "Population Bomb" fears drove policy for decades; now overtaken by **fertility decline**.
## SUB 1 | Neo-malthusian Works and Other Theories
[55:1:0] # Book - Author
[55:1:1] TABLE head: Book / Report | Author | Year
  [55:1:1.0] An Essay on the Principle of Population | Thomas Malthus | 1798
  [55:1:1.1] The Population Bomb | Paul R. Ehrlich | 1968
  [55:1:1.2] The Limits to Growth | Donella Meadows and others, for the Club of Rome | 1972
[55:1:2] - The Population Bomb opens with "**The battle to feed all of humanity is over**" (APPSC 2019).
[55:1:3] - Limits to Growth used the **World3** computer model: growth limits reached within about 100 years if trends continue.
[55:1:4] - "**New Population Bomb**" = rapid **urban growth in the Third World**.
[55:1:5] # Other Theories
[55:1:6] TABLE head: Theory | Thinker | Idea
  [55:1:6.0] Demographic transition | Warren Thompson (1929); Frank Notestein (1945, named it) | Death rates fall first with industrialisation, birth rates later
  [55:1:6.1] Optimum population | Edwin Cannan (also Marshall, Sidgwick) | Size that gives the highest output per head; beyond it = surplus
  [55:1:6.2] Marxian / anti-Malthusian | Karl Marx | Hunger comes from exploitation, not overpopulation
## SUB 2 | Distribution of World Population
[55:2:0] - Most people live in the **Northern Hemisphere mid-latitudes** and **tropics of Asia**.
[55:2:1] - Two major clusters: **East Asia** (eastern China, Japan) and **South Asia** (Ganga plains, Bangladesh delta).
[55:2:2] - **Ganges-Brahmaputra delta**: among the most densely populated areas on Earth.
## SUB 3 | Factors Affecting Distribution
[55:3:0] # Physical vs Socio-economic
[55:3:1] TABLE head: Factor | Effect | Example
  [55:3:1.0] Water | River valleys densest | Nile valley
  [55:3:1.1] Landform | Plains favoured, mountains sparse | Ganga plains
  [55:3:1.2] Climate | Moderate climates attract; hot/cold deserts repel | Sahara, Arctic
  [55:3:1.3] Soil | Fertile alluvium supports dense farming | Java, eastern China
  [55:3:1.4] Minerals | Mining draws workers | Katanga-Zambia copper belt
  [55:3:1.5] Industry, urbanisation | Jobs concentrate people | Kobe-Osaka
  [55:3:1.6] Social-cultural | Religious centres attract; unrest repels | Pilgrim towns
[55:3:2] # The Exception
[55:3:3] - **North-eastern USA** (New York, Philadelphia, Boston, Pittsburgh, Buffalo): dense but **not directly dependent on agriculture**; due to **industrial and urban** development (MPPSC 2021).
[55:3:4] - Nile valley, Java, eastern China = agriculture-based dense areas.
[55:3:5] Odd-one-out: North-eastern USA is industrial; Nile, Java, eastern China are agricultural.

=== NOTES 4:56 | World Population Growth, Demographic Transition and Regional Patterns
## SUB 0 | Growth Terms and Formulas
[56:0:0] # Key Terms
[56:0:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [56:0:1.0] Natural increase | Births - deaths
  [56:0:1.1] Actual growth | Natural increase + net migration
  [56:0:1.2] Positive growth | Births > deaths, or permanent net in-migration
  [56:0:1.3] RNI (%) | (CBR - CDR) / 10 (both per 1,000)
  [56:0:1.4] Over-urbanisation | Urban growth faster than urban jobs and services; leads to slums
[56:0:2] # Worked RNI Example (APPSC-G1 2018, 2019)
[56:0:3] - 1. CBR = **44** per 1,000.
[56:0:4] - 2. CDR = **11** per 1,000.
[56:0:5] - 3. 44 - 11 = **33 per 1,000 = 3.3%** natural increase a year.
[56:0:6] RNI is CBR minus CDR divided by 10; the answer is 3.3%, not 33% or 0.33%.
