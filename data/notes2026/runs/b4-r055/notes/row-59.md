=== Demographic Dividend – Meaning, Age Group and Window
# Definition
- **Demographic dividend** = the **rise in economic growth due to a rise in the share of working-age people** in the population – when the **working-age population outnumbers the non-working (dependent) population** (APPSC Group II 2017 key).
- Not female work participation, not a rise in child population, not "skilled workers" alone.
| Source | Working-age group |
|---|---|
| UNFPA / UN / ILO | **15–64** |
| Indian usage (Census, PMF, APPSC keys) | **15–59** – India's workforce age group |
- **Channels:** labour supply, savings and investment, lower dependency ratio, human capital – a rising share of **dependants hurts** it.
# India's numbers
- Over **62%** aged 15–59 and more than 54% below 25; about **65% below 35** (2011).
- In **2020** the average Indian was to be only **29 years** old (China and USA 37, West Europe 45, Japan 48) – the youngest major population. Median age **28** (SRS 2024), **40 by 2051**.
| Estimate | Window / peak |
|---|---|
| UNFPA | Longest in the world: **2005-06 to 2055-56**; opens at different times in different States |
| NITI Aayog | Dividend lasts to about 2055 |
| ES 2018-19 | Peak about **2041** (59% aged 20–59) |
| ES 2025-26 | Peak about **2030** |
| MoHFW (Mar 2026), 15–59 | Peak **1,009.0 mn in 2041**; closes after 2041 |
- **RBI:** **Kerala and Tamil Nadu** have crossed the demographic turning point; Bihar and UP have a wider window.
# Terms
- "**Demographic nightmare**" (ES 2018-19): the dividend lost without **health, education, skills and jobs**.
- "**Demographic winter**": fertility below 2.1, inverted pyramid, persistent decline. "**Shrinconomics**": AP Health Minister, 1 Apr 2026.

=== Ageing and the Elderly
- Elderly 60+: **8.6% (2011)** → **16% by 2041** → over 20% by 2050; median age 24 (2011) → 36 (2036) → **40 (2051)**.

- **NITI Aayog:** 70% of elderly depend on others; **78% have no pension** cover.

=== Fertility – TFR in India and the States
- **TFR** = children a woman is expected to have over ages **15–49**; replacement **2.1**; births per 1,000 = crude birth rate, not TFR.
| Source | India TFR |
|---|---|
| NFHS-4 / NFHS-5 | 2.2 / **2.0** |
| NFHS-6 (2023-24, released 29 May 2026) | 2.0; **31 of 36** States/UTs below 2.1; lowest **A&N (0.9)**, highest **Bihar (2.7)** |
| SRS 2024 | **1.9** (rural 2.1, **urban 1.5**) |
| SRS 2021 | Highest **Bihar 3.0** |
- SRS 2024 States: **Delhi 1.2** lowest; Kerala, TN, WB 1.3; above replacement **Bihar 2.9**, UP 2.6, MP 2.4, Rajasthan 2.3.
- At/below replacement: **TN, AP, Kerala, Maharashtra** and most large States.
- **UP** draft Population (Control, Stabilization and Welfare) Bill **2021**; **Assam**: two-child rule for government jobs.

=== Population Policy and Family Planning
# National Population Policy 2000
| Objective | Target |
|---|---|
| Immediate | Meet the **unmet need for contraception**, health infrastructure and personnel |
| Medium-term | **TFR 2.1 by 2010** |
| Long-term | **Stable population by 2045** |
# Family planning
- **1952**: India's national family planning programme – the **first in the world**; Population Policy **1976** first comprehensive policy.
- **Emergency (1975–77)**: coercive mass sterilisation; renamed **Family Welfare Programme in 1977**.
- **Female sterilisation** dominant: **37.9%** of married women (NFHS-5).
- **Mission Parivar Vikas (2016)**: **146** districts with TFR 3+ in **7 States**.
- **ASHA spacing advice:** **2 years** after marriage before the first child, **3 years** between births.

=== Andhra Pradesh – Ageing, Dividend and Population Policy
- AP **TFR 1.5** vs India 2.0; AP **CPR 71.1%** (NFHS-5); **median age 32.5** vs India 28 – AP is **ageing faster** than India because of **low TFR and a high median age**.
- 60+ about 10% → 19% (2036) → 23% (2047); under-14 share 25% (2011) → 15% (2036).
- Low growth (9.21%) plus a large youth workforce = a classic dividend, but literacy (67.35%) lags – focus on learning and skills.
- **AP Population Management Policy (Feb–Mar 2026):** shift from population control to **managing ageing and the dividend**.
- Southern States fear **lower tax devolution and fewer seats** after delimitation.
