## SUB 0 | Demographic Dividend – Meaning, Age Group and Window
[0:0] # Definition
[0:1] - **Demographic dividend** = the **rise in economic growth due to a rise in the share of working-age people** in the population – when the **working-age population outnumbers the non-working (dependent) population** (APPSC Group II 2017 key).
[0:2] - Not female work participation, not a rise in child population, not "skilled workers" alone.
[0:3] TABLE head: Source | Working-age group
  [0:3.0] UNFPA / UN / ILO | 15–64
  [0:3.1] Indian usage (Census, PMF, APPSC keys) | 15–59 – India's workforce age group
[0:4] - **Channels:** labour supply, savings and investment, lower dependency ratio, human capital – a rising share of **dependants hurts** it.
[0:5] # India's numbers
[0:6] - Over **62%** aged 15–59 and more than 54% below 25; about **65% below 35** (2011).
[0:7] - In **2020** the average Indian was to be only **29 years** old (China and USA 37, West Europe 45, Japan 48) – the youngest major population. Median age **28** (SRS 2024), **40 by 2051**.
[0:8] TABLE head: Estimate | Window / peak
  [0:8.0] UNFPA | Longest in the world: 2005-06 to 2055-56; opens at different times in different States
  [0:8.1] NITI Aayog | Dividend lasts to about 2055
  [0:8.2] ES 2018-19 | Peak about 2041 (59% aged 20–59)
  [0:8.3] ES 2025-26 | Peak about 2030
  [0:8.4] MoHFW (Mar 2026), 15–59 | Peak 1,009.0 mn in 2041; closes after 2041
[0:9] - **RBI:** **Kerala and Tamil Nadu** have crossed the demographic turning point; Bihar and UP have a wider window.
[0:10] # Terms
[0:11] - "**Demographic nightmare**" (ES 2018-19): the dividend lost without **health, education, skills and jobs**.
[0:12] - "**Demographic winter**": fertility below 2.1, inverted pyramid, persistent decline. "**Shrinconomics**": AP Health Minister, 1 Apr 2026.

## SUB 2 | Fertility – TFR in India and the States
[2:0] - **TFR** = children a woman is expected to have over ages **15–49**; replacement **2.1**; births per 1,000 = crude birth rate, not TFR.
[2:1] TABLE head: Source | India TFR
  [2:1.0] NFHS-4 / NFHS-5 | 2.2 / 2.0
  [2:1.1] NFHS-6 (2023-24, released 29 May 2026) | 2.0; 31 of 36 States/UTs below 2.1; lowest A&N (0.9), highest Bihar (2.7)
  [2:1.2] SRS 2024 | 1.9 (rural 2.1, urban 1.5)
  [2:1.3] SRS 2021 | Highest Bihar 3.0
[2:2] - SRS 2024 States: **Delhi 1.2** lowest; Kerala, TN, WB 1.3; above replacement **Bihar 2.9**, UP 2.6, MP 2.4, Rajasthan 2.3.
[2:3] - At/below replacement: **TN, AP, Kerala, Maharashtra** and most large States.
[2:4] - **UP** draft Population (Control, Stabilization and Welfare) Bill **2021**; **Assam**: two-child rule for government jobs.

## SUB 3 | Population Policy and Family Planning
[3:0] # National Population Policy 2000
[3:1] TABLE head: Objective | Target
  [3:1.0] Immediate | Meet the unmet need for contraception, health infrastructure and personnel
  [3:1.1] Medium-term | TFR 2.1 by 2010
  [3:1.2] Long-term | Stable population by 2045
[3:2] # Family planning
[3:3] - **1952**: India's national family planning programme – the **first in the world**; Population Policy **1976** first comprehensive policy.
[3:4] - **Emergency (1975–77)**: coercive mass sterilisation; renamed **Family Welfare Programme in 1977**.
[3:5] - **Female sterilisation** dominant: **37.9%** of married women (NFHS-5).
[3:6] - **Mission Parivar Vikas (2016)**: **146** districts with TFR 3+ in **7 States**.
[3:7] - **ASHA spacing advice:** **2 years** after marriage before the first child, **3 years** between births.
[3:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy P1 04 p.24 (first programme, 1976 policy); OnlyIAS NCERT Human Geography 04 p.1 (1952); app notes 4:59 (sub 6, 8)
## SUB 4 | Andhra Pradesh – Ageing, Dividend and Population Policy
[4:0] - AP **TFR 1.5** vs India 2.0; AP **CPR 71.1%** (NFHS-5); **median age 32.5** vs India 28 – AP is **ageing faster** than India because of **low TFR and a high median age**.
[4:1] - 60+ about 10% → 19% (2036) → 23% (2047); under-14 share 25% (2011) → 15% (2036).
[4:2] - Low growth (9.21%) plus a large youth workforce = a classic dividend, but literacy (67.35%) lags – focus on learning and skills.
[4:3] - **AP Population Management Policy (Feb–Mar 2026):** shift from population control to **managing ageing and the dividend**.
[4:4] - Southern States fear **lower tax devolution and fewer seats** after delimitation.
