## SUB 0 | Growth Terms – Natural Increase, RNI and Demographic Gap
[0:0] # Terms
[0:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:1.0] Natural increase | Births − deaths (crude birth rate − crude death rate); migration and marriage are not part of it
  [0:1.1] Demographic gap | The difference between the birth rate and the death rate (widest in stage 2 of the transition)
  [0:1.2] Actual growth | Natural increase + net migration (in-migrants − out-migrants)
  [0:1.3] Positive growth | Births exceed deaths, or permanent net in-migration
  [0:1.4] Negative growth | Births below deaths, or out-migration
  [0:1.5] Over-urbanisation | Urban growth faster than urban jobs and services (slums)
[0:2] - **CBR** = live births per 1,000 people a year; **CDR** = deaths per 1,000 a year.
[0:3] # Rate of natural increase (RNI)
[0:4] - **RNI (%) = (CBR − CDR) / 10** when both are per 1,000.
[0:5] - **CBR 44, CDR 11:** 44 − 11 = 33 per 1,000 = **3.3%** a year (not 33% or 0.33%).
[0:6] - CBR 30, CDR 10 → **2.0%**; CBR 25, CDR 7 → **1.8%**.
[0:7] ~~ Sources: OnlyIAS NCERT Human Geography 03 p.17-18 (CBR, CDR, natural and actual growth); PMF Human & Economic Geography 01 p.31; app notes 4:56 (sub 0)
## SUB 1 | World Growth – Milestones, Explosion and Projections
[1:0] - First billion took **over a million years**; **5 billion (1987) to 6 billion (1999) took only 12 years** – the late-20th-century **population explosion** (so the period is **1987–1999**).
[1:1] - World population "exploded" after the **Industrial Revolution** (18th century) as death rates fell.

  [1:2.1] 2030 | 8.5 billion
  [1:2.2] 2050 | 9.7 billion
  [1:2.3] 2100 | 10.4 billion
[1:3] - **Africa** is the fastest-growing continent (sub-Saharan Africa high growth); Europe and Northern America low to negative.

## SUB 2 | Demographic Transition Model
[2:0] TABLE head: Stage | Birth / death rates | Growth | Example
  [2:0.0] 1 High stationary | Both high | Slow | Pre-industrial; India before 1921
  [2:0.1] 2 Early expanding | High BR, falling DR | Rapid (explosion) | Niger, sub-Saharan Africa; India 1951–81
  [2:0.2] 3 Late expanding | Falling BR, low DR | Slowing | Brazil; India now
  [2:0.3] 4 Low stationary | Both low | Stable | Andhra Pradesh
  [2:0.4] 5 Declining | BR below DR | Falling | Japan, Germany
[2:1] - Order with development: **high birth–high death → high birth–low death → low birth–low death** (UPSC 2012: 2, 3, 1).
[2:2] - AP's policy shift from population control to "**Pillale Sampada**" (children are wealth).
[2:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Human & Economic Geography 01 p.32 (stages, UPSC 2012); NextGen Indian Economy P1 03 p.21 (India's stages); app notes 4:56 (sub 3), 4:59 (sub 4)
## SUB 3 | Low Fertility and Ageing Countries
[3:0] # Total fertility rate
[3:1] - **Replacement-level TFR = 2.1**; world average **2.2**.
[3:2] TABLE head: Country | TFR
  [3:2.0] South Korea | 0.72 (2023), world's lowest
  [3:2.1] Japan / Finland / USA | 1.3 / 1.4 / 1.6
  [3:2.2] Niger | ~6.6, world's highest
[3:3] - **AP Draft Population Management Policy (5 March 2026):** raise TFR from **1.5 to 2.1**; Rs 30,000 for a third child, Rs 40,000 for a fourth.
[3:4] # Ageing
[3:5] - In the news for **low birth rates, ageing or declining population: Italy, Japan, South Korea** – so **1, 2 and 4** (UPSC 2024); Nigeria and South Africa are young, growing populations.
[3:6] - **Asia-Pacific** ages fastest (65+ to 18.6% by 2050); world 65+ share 10% (2022) to 16% (2050).
[3:7] - Oldest median age: **Monaco**; youngest: **Niger**. South Asia 65+: about **7.4%** (2025, World Bank).
[3:8] - India (SRS 2024): elderly 9.7% – **Kerala** highest, **Assam** lowest; India 60+ to **325.3 million (20.5%) by 2051**, median age **40** by 2051.
[3:9] # Dependency ratio
[3:10] - **Dependency ratio** = population aged **0–14 and 65+** ÷ working-age population; a lower ratio means a potential **demographic dividend**.
[3:11] - Economic Survey 2025-26: India's dependency ratio will **double**, making **geriatric care** a growing job sector.
[3:12] - Highest working-age share (2021): **UAE 85.7%**, Qatar 85.6%. India's window: **2005-06 to 2055-56**, closing **after 2041**; dividend age group **15–64** (UN).
[3:13] ~~ Sources: PMF Human & Economic Geography 01 p.37 (ageing), p.43-44 (UAE, Qatar); UPSC CSE 2024 key; app notes 4:56 (sub 4, 5, 6, 7)
