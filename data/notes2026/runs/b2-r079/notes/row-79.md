=== Meaning and Objectives of Democratic Decentralisation
# Meaning
- **Democratic decentralisation:** transfer of power to **elected local bodies** so that people take part in the decisions that affect them – the scheme that "ultimately came to be known as **Panchayati Raj**" (Laxmikanth).
- **Term coined by:** the **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** (report November 1957), not the Ashok Mehta Committee.
- **Local self-government / a constitutional provision for local government** = an exercise in **democratic decentralisation** (UPSC 2017; AP Sachivalayam) – not delegation, de-concentration, federalism or a confederation.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Democratic decentralisation** | Power passed to **elected** local bodies |
| De-concentration | Field offices of the same government get work; no transfer to elected bodies |
| Delegation | Power lent by a superior authority – **revocable at will** |
| Federalism | Union–State division of power |
| Confederation | Union of sovereign States |
| Direct democracy | Citizens decide **without representatives** |
# Objectives and purpose of Panchayati Raj
- **Fundamental objects:** **people's participation in development** and **democratic decentralisation** – code "**1 and 3 only**" (UPSC 2015, WBCS 2019).
- **Political accountability** and **financial mobilisation** are not its fundamental objects (they may follow from it).
- **Main purpose:** to enable people to **participate in developmental administration** (UPPSC 2004) – not merely to raise farm output, jobs or political awareness.
- **Why adopted:** **to decentralise the power of democracy** (UPPSC 2016).
- **What PRIs brought:** **formal representation of the weaker sections** in village governance (SCs, STs, women through reservation) – not eradication of untouchability, land to depressed classes or spread of education.
- **Democratic governance:** **participation of the public in power** – power rests with the people and is exercised by them or their representatives.
- After the **73rd Amendment**, effective PRIs need decentralisation of **decision-making**, of **administrative powers** and of **the system as a whole** – **not judicial powers**.
> Code trap: a single-answer version of the UPSC 2015 item has two right options (participation + democratic decentralisation) – the full answer is both.

=== Local Self-Government – Features, Direct Democracy, Imperium in Imperio
| Is a feature | Is NOT a feature |
|---|---|
| Elected by and accountable to local people | **Sovereignty** |
| Defined local area and functions | **Legislative supremacy** |
| Own funds / power to tax | **Judicial powers** (Nyaya Panchayats are separate) |
| Autonomy within State law | Central control |
- **Sovereignty and legislative supremacy** belong to **Parliament and State legislatures**, never to local bodies.
- **Core principle (Rocket):** democratic decentralisation, community participation and **local accountability** – NOT central control or mere administration.
- **Direct democracy example: the Gram Sabha** – all registered voters of the village decide directly; the **L.M. Singhvi Committee** called the Gram Sabha the **embodiment of direct democracy**.
- **Imperium in imperio** = "an **empire within an empire**" = a **State within a State** – how panchayats are described as self-governing units inside the State (APPSC G2 2011); not State immunity, liability or division of a State.
- **Charles Metcalfe** called Indian village communities "**Little Republics**".

=== Colonial Roots – Mayo (1870) and Ripon (1882)
| Viceroy | Resolution | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Lord Mayo | **1870** (14 December) | **Financial decentralisation**; fixed grants to provinces; aim mainly fiscal relief |
| **Lord Ripon** | **18 May 1882** | Resolution on Local Self-Government – the **'Magna Carta'** of local self-government |
- **Ripon = "Father of Local Self-Government in India"** (APPSC G2 2008) – not Canning, Minto, Mayo or Mountbatten.
# Points of Ripon's resolution
- Local bodies as instruments of **political and popular education** and better administration.
- Local affairs run by rural and urban local bodies with **definite duties** and **suitable sources of revenue**.
- **Non-officials in majority**, **elected** where possible; **non-official chairmen**.
- **Official interference to the minimum** – to **revise and check, not to dictate** (control from outside); so "**direct State control** over local bodies" is **NOT** part of it.
- Official sanction still needed for loans, new taxes, alienation of property, big works, bye-laws.
- Later: Royal Commission on Decentralisation 1907-09 (**Hobhouse**); **GoI Act 1919** – local self-government a **transferred subject**; GoI Act 1935 – provincial subject.
- India's **municipal model** comes from the **British** pattern (Ripon's boards).

=== Committees – Recommendations and Preconditions
| Committee | Key point / precondition |
|---|---|
| **Balwant Rai Mehta** (1957) | Coined "democratic decentralisation"; **three-tier** PRI; **genuine transfer of power and adequate resources** (not constitutional status); NDC accepted it **January 1958** |
| Ashok Mehta (1977-78) | Two-tier; compulsory taxation powers; **no supersession (or elections within 6 months)**; social audit; **district** the first point of decentralisation |
| Dantwala (1978) | **Block-level** planning |
| Hanumantha Rao (1984) | **District** planning, coordinated by the **Collector** |
| G.V.K. Rao (1985) | Development bureaucratised – "**grass without roots**"; restore the **Zila Parishad**'s role |
| **L.M. Singhvi** (1986) | **Constitutional recognition** of PRIs – a new chapter in the Constitution; Gram Sabha = direct democracy |
| Committee on Policy and Programmes (1988) | Constitutional status, 5-year term, direct elections, State Finance and Election Commissions |
- **Constitutionalisation of PRIs** was recommended by the **L.M. Singhvi Committee** – realised by the **73rd Amendment (1992)**.
- **Mehta tiers:** Gram Panchayat (village) – Panchayat Samiti (block) – Zila Parishad (district).
- **Whose cooperation:** the Mehta Committee held **local public participation** indispensable for development.
# First rollouts
- **First State:** **Rajasthan – Nagaur, 2 October 1959**; **second: Andhra Pradesh – 1 November 1959**.
- AP's **13 Zilla Parishads** are being reorganised into **28** (one per district) after their terms ended on 24 Sep 2026 (as of 2026).

=== Gram Swaraj, Article 40 and RGSA
- **Gram Swaraj:** **Mahatma Gandhi**'s self-reliant **village republic** (Hind Swaraj, 1909) – not Vinoba Bhave, JP or Dayananda.
- **Article 40 (DPSP):** the **constitutional root** of Gram Swaraj – the State shall organise village panchayats as units of self-government.
- **Rashtriya Gram Swaraj Abhiyan (RGSA):** Ministry of **Panchayati Raj**, from **2018-19**; objective – develop PRIs' governance capabilities so rural local bodies become **self-sustainable, financially stable and more efficient**; **trains PRI members**.
  - Revamped for **1 Apr 2022 – 31 Mar 2026**; funding **60:40**, **NE and hill States 90:10**.
~~ Sources: Rocket Polity #37 p.94; Praveen CA360 May 2026; myscheme.gov.in (RGSA); app notes 2:79

=== Decentralised Planning and Participatory Budgeting
- **Participatory budgeting:** citizens **deliberate and negotiate** over distribution of public resources, **directly decide** how and where money is spent, and **historically excluded** citizens gain access to decision-making – all three make local bodies transparent and accountable (CDS 2013: I, II and III).
- **District Planning Committee:** constitutional coordinating body under **Article 243ZD**.
- **Kerala People's Plan Campaign:** launched **17 August 1996**; about **35-40%** of plan funds devolved to local bodies (not 60-70%); turned 30 in Aug 2026.
- **T.M. Thomas Isaac** proposed "**People's Plan Campaign 2.0**" – student internships and an online portal (25 Aug 2026).
- **Economic Survey 2025-26:** PRI own-source revenue, **e-GramSwaraj**, **SabhaSaar** (**AI tool** for writing **Gram Sabha minutes**); low Gram Sabha participation.
- J&K DDCs' term ended 24 Feb 2026 with no fresh polls.
