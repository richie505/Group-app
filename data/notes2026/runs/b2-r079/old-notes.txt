=== NOTES 2:79 | Democratic Decentralisation – Meaning, Objectives and Preconditions
## SUB 0 | Meaning of Democratic Decentralisation
[79:0:0] - **Democratic decentralisation**: transferring power to **elected local bodies** so people take part in decisions that affect them.
[79:0:1] - **Local self-government** is best explained as an **exercise in democratic decentralisation** (UPSC 2017).
[79:0:2] - The term was **coined by the Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** (1957).
[79:0:3] - What PRIs brought: **formal representation of the weaker sections** in village governance (**APPSC 2019**).
[79:0:4] - Why adopted: **to decentralise the power of democracy** (UPPSC 2016).
[79:0:5] # Confusable Terms
[79:0:6] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [79:0:6.0] Democratic decentralisation | Power passed to elected local bodies
  [79:0:6.1] Federalism | Union–State division of power
  [79:0:6.2] Delegation | Revocable at will
  [79:0:6.3] Direct democracy | Citizens decide without representatives
## SUB 1 | Attributes of Local Self-government
[79:1:0] TABLE head: Is a feature | Is NOT a feature
  [79:1:0.0] Elected by and accountable to local people | Sovereignty
  [79:1:0.1] Defined local area and functions | Legislative supremacy
  [79:1:0.2] Own funds / power to tax | Judicial powers (Nyaya Panchayats are separate)
  [79:1:0.3] Autonomy within State law | Central control
[79:1:1] Sovereignty and legislative supremacy belong to Parliament and State legislatures, never to local bodies.
## SUB 2 | Colonial Roots
[79:2:0] TABLE head: Viceroy | Resolution | Significance
  [79:2:0.0] Lord Mayo | 1870 (14 December) | Financial decentralisation; fixed grants to provinces; aim mainly fiscal relief
  [79:2:0.1] Lord Ripon | 18 May 1882 | Resolution on Local Self-Government; local bodies as instruments of political and popular education; elected non-official majorities and non-official chairmen
[79:2:1] - **Ripon = "Father of Local Self-Government in India"**.
[79:2:2] - India's **municipal model** comes from the **British** pattern (Ripon's boards).
## SUB 3 | Committees – Recommendations and Preconditions
[79:3:0] TABLE head: Committee | Key point / precondition
  [79:3:0.0] Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) | Three-tier PRI; genuine transfer of power and adequate resources (not constitutional status); NDC accepted it January 1958
  [79:3:0.1] Ashok Mehta (1977-78) | Compulsory taxation powers, no supersession (or elections within 6 months), social audit, district as the first point of decentralisation
  [79:3:0.2] Dantwala (1978) | Block-level planning
  [79:3:0.3] Hanumantha Rao (1984) | District planning, coordinated by the Collector
  [79:3:0.4] G.V.K. Rao (1985) | Development bureaucratised – "grass without roots"; restore the Zila Parishad's role
  [79:3:0.5] L.M. Singhvi | Constitutional status for PRIs
  [79:3:0.6] Committee on Policy and Programmes (1988) | Constitutional status, 5-year term, direct elections, State Finance and Election Commissions
[79:3:1] # Mehta Three Tiers
[79:3:2] TABLE head: Tier | Level
  [79:3:2.0] Zila Parishad | District
  [79:3:2.1] Panchayat Samiti | Block
  [79:3:2.2] Gram Panchayat | Village
[79:3:3] - **Whose cooperation**: Mehta Committee held **local public participation** indispensable for development.
## SUB 4 | Objectives of Panchayati Raj
[79:4:0] - **Fundamental objects**: **people's participation in development** and **democratic decentralisation** (UPSC 2015).
[79:4:1] - After the **73rd Amendment**, needed: decentralisation of **decision-making**, of **administrative powers**, and of **the system** as a whole – **not judicial powers** (BPSC 2022).
[79:4:2] **Political accountability** and **financial mobilisation** are not the keyed fundamental objects.
## SUB 5 | First Rollouts
[79:5:0] TABLE head: FIRST | Fact
  [79:5:0.0] First State | Rajasthan – Nagaur, 2 October 1959
  [79:5:0.1] Next | Andhra Pradesh – 1 November 1959
[79:5:1] - AP's **13 Zilla Parishads** are reorganised into **28** (one per district) after their terms ended on 24 Sep 2026.
## SUB 6 | Gram Swaraj and Article 40
[79:6:0] - **Gram Swaraj**: Gandhi's self-reliant **village republic**.
[79:6:1] - **Article 40 (DPSP)**: the **constitutional root** of Gram Swaraj – State to organise village panchayats.
[79:6:2] - **Rashtriya Gram Swaraj Abhiyan** (Ministry of **Panchayati Raj**), from **2018-19**: **trains PRI members**; revamped for 1 Apr 2022 – 31 Mar 2026; funding **60:40**, **NE and hill States 90:10**.
## SUB 7 | Decentralised Planning
[79:7:0] - **District Planning Committee**: constitutional coordinating body under **Article 243ZD**.
[79:7:1] - **Kerala People's Plan Campaign**: launched **17 August 1996**; about **35-40%** of plan funds devolved to local bodies (not 60-70%); turned **30** in Aug 2026.
[79:7:2] - **T.M. Thomas Isaac** proposed "**People's Plan Campaign 2.0**" – student internships and an online portal (25 Aug 2026).
[79:7:3] - **Economic Survey 2025-26**: PRI own-source revenue, **e-GramSwaraj**, **SabhaSaar** (**AI tool** for writing **Gram Sabha minutes**); low Gram Sabha participation (1 Jul 2026).
[79:7:4] - J&K DDCs' term ended 24 Feb 2026 with no fresh polls.
