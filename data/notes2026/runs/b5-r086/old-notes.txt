=== NOTES 5:86 | Loss of Biodiversity – Causes, Invasive Alien Species, Iucn Red List Categories
## SUB 0 | Causes of Biodiversity Loss
[86:0:0] - The word **biodiversity** was coined by **W. G. Rosen**.
[86:0:1] - **Most important cause:** **habitat loss / destruction** (forest cutting, filling wetlands, dams, destruction of aquatic habitats). IPBES also names habitat destruction the main driver.
[86:0:2] - Main reason for decrease = natural habitat destruction: loss of plants and the animals that depend on them. **Maximum danger** = destruction of natural habitats and vegetation.
[86:0:3] - Main cause of destruction is **human influence**: habitat alteration and loss, exotic species, GMOs.
[86:0:4] - In India: habitat loss (urbanisation, infrastructure, mining, farming) comes first, then climate change, invasives, pollution, overexploitation.
[86:0:5] # Evil Quartet vs HIPPO
[86:0:6] TABLE head: Evil Quartet (NCERT) | HIPPO (popularised by E. O. Wilson)
  [86:0:6.0] Habitat loss and fragmentation | Habitat destruction
  [86:0:6.1] Over-exploitation | Invasive species
  [86:0:6.2] Invasive alien species | Pollution
  [86:0:6.3] Co-extinctions | human Population
  [86:0:6.4] — | Over-harvesting
[86:0:7] - Newer lists add **climate change** and **disease**.
[86:0:8] Co-extinction belongs to the Evil Quartet, not HIPPO; pollution and population belong to HIPPO only.
[86:0:9] # Which is NOT a Cause
[86:0:10] - Accepted causes: **modern agriculture**, **loss of genetic variation**, habitat destruction, **global warming**, **habitat fragmentation**, **alien species invasion**.
[86:0:11] - NOT causes/threats: "**loss of appetite**", "**promotion of vegetarianism**", "expansion of protected areas".
[86:0:12] - Over-exploitation extinctions: **Steller's sea cow**, **passenger pigeon**.
[86:0:13] APPSC 2025: Deforestation destroys natural habitats and drives species to extinction.
[86:0:14] - **HIPPO:** popularised by **E. O. Wilson**: Habitat destruction, Invasive species, Pollution, human Population, Over-harvesting.
## SUB 1 | Deforestation and Habitat Fragmentation
[86:1:0] - **Primary driver** of deforestation and habitat loss: **agricultural expansion and urban development** (PYQ 2022).
[86:1:1] - Tropical rainforest cover fell from **14% to 6%** of land surface.
[86:1:2] # Chain to Extinction
[86:1:3] - 1. Agricultural expansion and urban development
[86:1:4] - 2. Habitat destroyed and fragmented
[86:1:5] - 3. Edge effects
[86:1:6] - 4. Interior species decline
[86:1:7] - 5. Extinction
[86:1:8] # Fragmentation and Edge Effect
[86:1:9] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [86:1:9.0] Fragmentation | Large habitats broken into small patches; hits migratory animals and wide-ranging mammals and birds hardest (disrupts movement, separates populations)
  [86:1:9.1] Edge effect | Patch boundaries get more light, wind, heat, predators, weeds; as fragments shrink, interior species decline
[86:1:10] # Disturb vs Restore Ecological Balance
[86:1:11] TABLE head: Disturbs balance | Restores balance
  [86:1:11.0] Clear-felling, shifting cultivation, mining, quarrying | Afforestation, eco-restoration of degraded land
  [86:1:11.1] Draining wetlands, mangrove-to-shrimp-farm conversion | Protected areas, wildlife corridors, eco-bridges
  [86:1:11.2] Urban sprawl on coasts and wetlands | Removal of invasives with native restocking
## SUB 2 | Climate Change and Consequences
[86:2:0] # Climate Change as Driver
[86:2:1] - Warming causes **coral bleaching**, ocean acidification, shifting ecosystem boundaries and **disrupted migration**.
[86:2:2] - Rising temperatures **shift species ranges** and raise extinction risk in hotspots (5 Jun 2026).
[86:2:3] - A warming Arctic is opening to **alien plants** (12 Feb 2026).
[86:2:4] # Consequences of Loss
[86:2:5] - Disrupts species interactions, **pollination**, **nutrient cycling**, water and soil services; once lost, biodiversity cannot be recreated.
[86:2:6] - Reduces **food security** of resource-dependent communities; cultural and ethical loss.
[86:2:7] - **Zoonotic spillover:** emerging diseases via habitat disruption and wildlife trade; answer = **One Health** approach (links health of humans, animals, ecosystems).
## SUB 3 | Extinction
[86:3:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [86:3:0.0] Extinction | Death of the last individual of a species, globally or locally
  [86:3:0.1] Co-extinction | Loss of one species drags down dependants: a plant and its obligate pollinator; parasites of an extinct host fish
  [86:3:0.2] Background extinction | Slow natural loss at a low steady rate
  [86:3:0.3] Mass extinction | High percentage of species lost in a short geological period
  [86:3:0.4] Sixth extinction | Present, human-driven wave; rates tens to hundreds of times background (IPBES)
[86:3:1] - **Five** mass extinctions in the past 500 million years; the **fifth** = end-Cretaceous, about **66 million years ago** (dinosaurs).
[86:3:2] - Sixth extinction is linked to mankind's **over-exploitation** of natural resources, habitat fragmentation/loss, ecosystem destruction, pollution and global climate change; humans are the primary cause; it is happening faster than feared.
[86:3:3] - **High-risk traits:** large body size, small population, low reproductive rate, narrow specialised niche, endemism.
[86:3:4] # Recorded Extinctions
[86:3:5] - IUCN Red List (2014): **784** extinctions in 500 years; 5 Australian marsupials confirmed extinct in Red List 2026-1.
[86:3:6] - **Cheetah** declared extinct in India in **1952**: the only large mammal lost in India since Independence.
[86:3:7] - Recorded extinct from India (ZSI): **cheetah, pink-headed duck, mountain quail** (NOT the passenger pigeon).
[86:3:8] # Extinct vs Extinct in the Wild vs Living
[86:3:9] TABLE head: Status | Examples
  [86:3:9.0] Extinct (EX) | Passenger pigeon, Steller's sea cow; cheetah in India (regional)
  [86:3:9.1] Extinct in the Wild (EW) | Hawaiian crow, Père David's deer (survive in captivity)
  [86:3:9.2] Living threatened | Great Indian Bustard (CR), tiger (EN)
## SUB 4 | Invasive Alien Species
[86:4:0] - **Definition:** non-native species that spreads without control and causes measurable harm ("arrive, survive and thrive").
[86:4:1] - Three tests: **non-native origin, uncontrolled spread, measurable harm** (NOT commercial usefulness).
[86:4:2] - Invasives contributed to about **40%** of known animal extinctions since the **17th century**; threaten about **1 in 10** Red List species.
[86:4:3] - Ecological damage per invasive species is higher in the **Global South**.
[86:4:4] # Invasives in India – Origin and Impact
[86:4:5] TABLE head: Species | Origin | How it came / impact
  [86:4:5.0] Water hyacinth (Eichhornia, now Pontederia crassipes) | South America (Amazon) | "Worst aquatic weed in the world" (APPSC 2024); "Terror of Bengal"; monotypic stands in lakes, rivers, rice paddies
  [86:4:5.1] Parthenium hysterophorus (Congress grass) | Tropical America | With imported wheat in the 1950s; cropland and pasture; allergies, dermatitis
  [86:4:5.2] Lantana camara | Central/South America | As an ornamental; family Verbenaceae (not Asteraceae); forest understorey; allelopathy; IUCN 100 worst; ~25% of Amrabad TR forest floor
  [86:4:5.3] Prosopis juliflora (Seema Chintakaya, Ganda babool, Vilayati Kikar, karuvelam) | Mexico, South America, Caribbean | Dry land; lowers groundwater, raises soil salinity (Rayalaseema); degraded Banni grasslands; reduces biodiversity
  [86:4:5.4] African catfish (Clarias gariepinus) | Africa | Predatory; banned in India
  [86:4:5.5] Senna spectabilis | South America (not North America) | Nilgiri-Bandipur-Wayanad
  [86:4:5.6] Red-eared slider | North America | Pet trade
  [86:4:5.7] Mosquitofish | United States | Mosquito control
  [86:4:5.8] Giant African Snail | Africa | Takes over native habitats
[86:4:6] - Invasive fish of concern: **African catfish, Nile tilapia, red-bellied piranha, alligator gar**.
[86:4:7] - **Cashew** is native to **Brazil**, introduced by the Portuguese about five centuries ago, first in **Goa**; of cashew, papaya and red sanders only **red sanders** is native to India.
[86:4:8] # Laws and Actions
[86:4:9] TABLE head: Law / framework | Provision
  [86:4:9.0] Destructive Insects and Pests Act, 1914 | Stops entry of crop pests and diseases into India and their inter-state spread
  [86:4:9.1] Plant Quarantine Order, 2003 | Pest risk analysis and phytosanitary checks
  [86:4:9.2] DPPQS | Under Agriculture Ministry (not MoEFCC); fumigates uncertified imports with methyl bromide
  [86:4:9.3] CBD Article 8(h) | Alien species
  [86:4:9.4] WPA Amendment 2022 | Centre regulates/prohibits invasive alien species under Section 62A
[86:4:10] New **Schedule IV** lists **CITES-appendix specimens**, NOT invasive species.
[86:4:11] - **Ujani reservoir, Maharashtra:** **BNHS** started India's **first community-driven** removal and native restocking (suckermouth catfish, tilapia, African catfish) (Aug 2026).
[86:4:12] - **Tamil Nadu** cleared Prosopis from **517 villages in 32 districts** (7 May 2026); recovery depends on the landscape's earlier condition. Removal alone does not always restore landscapes; blanket eradication is debated.
[86:4:13] # Global Targets – Kunming-Montreal GBF
[86:4:14] - **Target 6:** cut the introduction and establishment of invasive alien species by at least **50% by 2030**, and eradicate or control them in priority sites.
[86:4:15] - **Target 3 (30x30):** conserve at least **30%** of land and sea areas by 2030.
## SUB 5 | Iucn, Red List and Reports
[86:5:0] - **IUCN:** founded **1948**; HQ **Gland, Switzerland** (APPSC 2025); a union of governments and civil-society organisations, **NOT a UN agency**.
[86:5:1] - **Species Survival Commission** runs specialist groups, e.g. **Invasive Species Specialist Group (ISSG)**, which developed the **Global Invasive Species Database** (1998-2000).
[86:5:2] - IUCN World Conservation Congress 2025: **Abu Dhabi**, "Our World, Our Future".
[86:5:3] - **CITES** regulates wildlife trade; legally binding but does not replace national laws.
[86:5:4] - **Phoenix Species Project** (Aug 2026): **$200 million**, **Re:wild and Bezos Earth Fund**, to recover 100 most threatened species.
[86:5:5] # Red Data Book / Red List
[86:5:6] - Published by **IUCN**; lists **threatened plants and animals** (not endemic species of hotspots, not protected sites).
[86:5:7] - IUCN's endangered species list = **Red List** (APPSC 2025); Brown, White and Black lists are distractors.
[86:5:8] - Started **1964**; nickname "**Barometer of Life**"; measures **extinction risk** to guide conservation.
[86:5:9] - **IUCN Green Status (2021)** measures species **recovery**.
[86:5:10] TABLE head: Page colour | Meaning
  [86:5:10.0] Red / pink | Endangered / critically endangered
  [86:5:10.1] Green | Formerly threatened, now recovered
[86:5:11] - Red List **2026-1 (July 2026)**: **175,909** assessed; **49,505 (~28%)** threatened (44,000+ in 2024).
[86:5:12] - **India's Red List Roadmap:** **70,000** species by **2027**, led by the **NBA**. 7,076 Indian animal species in Red List categories.
[86:5:13] # Report → Publisher
[86:5:14] TABLE head: Report / database | Publisher
  [86:5:14.0] Global Assessment on Biodiversity (2019): ~25% of species threatened | IPBES ("Intergovernmental Panel on Nature"), not WWF/ZSL
  [86:5:14.1] Living Planet Report / Living Planet Index (vertebrate populations since 1970) | WWF with Zoological Society of London
  [86:5:14.2] Red List / Red Data Book | IUCN
  [86:5:14.3] Global Invasive Species Database; 100 of the World's Worst IAS | IUCN SSC ISSG
## SUB 6 | Iucn Red List Categories
[86:6:0] - **Nine** categories, highest to lowest risk: **EX → EW → CR → EN → VU → NT → LC**, plus **DD** and **NE**.
[86:6:1] TABLE head: Code | Full form | Meaning
  [86:6:1.0] EX | Extinct | No reasonable doubt the last individual has died, after full surveys (not automatic after ten years unrecorded)
  [86:6:1.1] EW | Extinct in the Wild | Only in captivity or cultivation, outside natural range
  [86:6:1.2] CR | Critically Endangered | Extremely high risk in the wild; more severe than EN
  [86:6:1.3] EN | Endangered | Very high risk; "species in danger of extinction"
  [86:6:1.4] VU | Vulnerable | High risk
  [86:6:1.5] NT | Near Threatened (not "Not Threatened") | Close to threatened
  [86:6:1.6] LC | Least Concern | Widespread, abundant
  [86:6:1.7] DD | Data Deficient | Not enough data
  [86:6:1.8] NE | Not Evaluated | Not yet assessed
[86:6:2] - **Threatened = CR + EN + VU.**
[86:6:3] APPSC 2023 and 2025 (G2 Mains P2): CR = Critically Endangered; there is no "Critically Extinct" category.
[86:6:4] # Criteria A-E
[86:6:5] TABLE head: Criterion | Basis
  [86:6:5.0] A | Declining population
  [86:6:5.1] B | Geographic range (size, fragmentation)
  [86:6:5.2] C | Small population size
  [86:6:5.3] D | Very small or restricted population
  [86:6:5.4] E | Quantitative extinction-risk analysis (PVA)
[86:6:6] - Economic value in trade is NOT a criterion.
