=== Causes of Biodiversity Loss – Habitat Destruction First
# The most-asked fact
- **Greatest / most important / main cause:** **habitat destruction (loss of natural habitats and vegetation)** – NextGen: "the primary and major reason for biodiversity loss"; IPBES also ranks it first. Not climate change, invasives, over-hunting or pollution alone.
- **Deforestation → biodiversity loss:** cutting and burning forests **destroys natural habitats, driving species to extinction and disrupting ecosystems**.
- **Primary driver of deforestation and habitat loss:** **agricultural expansion and urban development** (also land clearance for agriculture, mines, dams, harbours, industries, settlements); in the world the major cause of deforestation is **expansion of agriculture** (not paper industry, firewood or fires).
- Main cause overall = **human influence**: habitat alteration and loss, exotic species, over-exploitation, pollution, GMOs; **over-consumption of resources** destroys habitats, raises pollution and drives climate change.
- In India: habitat loss (urbanisation, infrastructure, mining, farming) comes first, then climate change, invasives, pollution, over-exploitation.
- Loss of biodiversity is a major problem resulting from **modern agriculture** (monoculture, agro-chemicals).
# Evil Quartet vs HIPPO
| Evil Quartet (NCERT / NextGen) | HIPPO (popularised by E. O. Wilson) |
|---|---|
| **Habitat loss and fragmentation** | Habitat destruction |
| **Over-exploitation** (Steller's sea cow, passenger pigeon) | Invasive species |
| **Invasion of alien species** (Nile perch → 200+ cichlids lost in Lake Victoria) | Pollution |
| **Co-extinctions** | human Population |
| — | Over-harvesting |
- **Co-extinction** is only in the Evil Quartet; **pollution** and **population** only in HIPPO. Newer lists add **climate change** and **disease**.
# Fragmentation and edge effect
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fragmentation | Large habitats broken into small patches; hits **migratory animals and wide-ranging mammals and birds** hardest |
| Edge effect | Patch boundaries get more **light, wind, heat, predators and weeds**; as fragments shrink, **interior species decline** |
- Tropical rainforest cover fell from **14% to 6%** of the land surface; Amazon cleared for soybean and cattle.
# Disturbs vs restores ecological balance
| Disturbs (a cause of loss) | Restores (NOT a cause) |
|---|---|
| Roads, **urbanisation**, **extension of agriculture**, clear-felling, **jhum (shifting) cultivation**, mining, quarrying, encroachment, exploitation of forest produce | **Afforestation of degraded land**, **sacred groves**, protected areas, **biosphere reserves**, wildlife corridors |
| Draining wetlands, mangroves to shrimp farms | **Removal of invasives with native restocking** |
> Exam angle: "greatest / main / most important cause" (habitat destruction ×8), "all of the above", primary driver (agriculture + urban development), and NOT-a-cause pickers (afforestation, sacred groves, biosphere reserves).

=== Threats in Statement Questions and Species Declines
# Accepted threats vs distractors
| Threat (correct) | NOT a threat (distractor) |
|---|---|
| **Global warming**, **habitat fragmentation**, **invasion of alien species** | **Promotion of vegetarianism** |
| **Loss of habitat**, **loss of genetic variation** | **Loss of appetite** |
| Habitat pollution, over-exploitation, illegal wildlife trade, forest fires | Creation of biosphere reserves, expansion of protected areas |
- UPSC 2012 / RAS 2016: threats = **1, 2 and 3** (global warming, fragmentation, alien species), not vegetarianism.
- Direct threats (NextGen): habitat destruction, exotic species, diseases, pollution, **genetic assimilation**.
- **Extinction-prone traits:** large body size, small population, low reproductive rate, **narrow (specialised) niche**, **lack of genetic variability**, endemism; a **broad niche** makes a species adaptable – NOT a cause.
- When a species becomes extinct, **its obligately associated species also go extinct** (co-extinction) – so "associated species do not become extinct" is false.
# Specific declines
| Group | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Vultures** (Gyps) | **Diclofenac**, a veterinary NSAID given to cattle – kidney failure in vultures eating carcasses; vet use banned **2006**; meloxicam is the safe substitute |
| **Birds** in India | **Habitat reduction** and **pesticides, chemical fertilisers, mosquito repellents** (not bio-pesticides or organic manure) |
| **House sparrow** | Popularly linked to **cell-tower radiation** (besides loss of nesting sites, pesticides); IUCN: LC |
| Wildlife in general | Loss of habitat, illegal trade, forest fires; population growth acts mainly indirectly |
> Exam angle: statement-code questions (1, 2 and 3), NOT-a-cause, vulture–diclofenac (UPSC 2012), extinction-prone traits.

=== IUCN Red List Categories and Criteria
- **Nine** categories, highest to lowest risk: **EX → EW → CR → EN → VU → NT → LC**, plus **DD** and **NE**.
| Code | Full form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| EX | Extinct | **No reasonable doubt that the last individual has died**, after exhaustive surveys – no fixed number of years |
| EW | Extinct in the Wild | Survives only in **cultivation, captivity** or as a naturalised population well outside its past range |
| CR | **Critically Endangered** | **Extremely high** risk of extinction in the wild (not "critically extinct") |
| EN | Endangered | **Very high** risk; "species in danger of extinction"; numbers reduced to a critical level |
| VU | Vulnerable | **High** risk; continuous decline from over-exploitation or habitat destruction |
| NT | **Near Threatened** (not "Not Threatened") | Close to qualifying for a threatened category |
| LC | Least Concern | Widespread and abundant |
| DD | Data Deficient | Inadequate information |
| NE | Not Evaluated | Not yet assessed |
- **Threatened = CR + EN + VU.** "Rare" (older Indian usage) = not endangered or vulnerable now but at risk.
- **50-year rule:** older books say a species is extinct if **not seen in the wild for 50 years** (a 1980s suggestion); IUCN today uses "no reasonable doubt" instead.
- **Extirpation** = local extinction: a species gone **from a specific area but surviving elsewhere** (e.g. tigers extirpated from Kawal and Sri Venkateswara NP).
# Criteria A-E
| Criterion | Basis |
|---|---|
| A | Declining population (past, present, projected) |
| B | Geographic range (size, fragmentation, decline) |
| C | Small population size and decline |
| D | Very small or restricted population |
| E | Quantitative analysis of extinction risk (PVA) |
- Economic value in trade is NOT a criterion.
> Exam angle: CR full form (APPSC Group 2), "species in danger of extinction" = endangered, extirpation, 50-year rule.

=== Red Data Book, IUCN and Biodiversity Reports
# Red Data Book / Red List
- Published by **IUCN**; lists **threatened (endangered) plants and animals** in danger of extinction – **not** endemic species of hotspots, **not** protected sites.
- IUCN's endangered-species list = **Red List**; Brown, White, Black, Green, Yellow lists are distractors. "Red" symbolises danger.
- Red List started **1964**; first Red Data Book volumes issued **1966** by IUCN's Species Survival Commission; nickname "**Barometer of Life**"; measures **extinction risk**.
| Page colour | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Red / pink | Endangered / critically endangered |
| Green | Formerly threatened, now recovered |
- **IUCN Green Status (2021)** measures species **recovery**.
- Red List **2026-1 (July 2026)**: **175,909** assessed; **49,505 (~28%)** threatened. India's **Red List Roadmap**: **70,000** species by **2027**, led by the **NBA**; 7,076 Indian animal species in Red List categories.
# IUCN
- **International Union for Conservation of Nature** (and Natural Resources); founded **1948**; HQ **Gland, Switzerland** (older books: Morges); a union of governments and NGOs – **NOT a UN agency**.
- **Species Survival Commission** → **Invasive Species Specialist Group (ISSG)**, which manages the **Global Invasive Species Database** and the "100 of the World's Worst" list.
- World Conservation Congress 2025: **Abu Dhabi**, "Our World, Our Future". **Phoenix Species Project** (Aug 2026): **$200 million**, Re:wild and Bezos Earth Fund, to recover **100** most threatened species.
# Report → publisher
| Report / database | Publisher |
|---|---|
| Red List / Red Data Book | IUCN |
| Global Invasive Species Database | IUCN SSC ISSG |
| **Living Planet Report** (biennial, since 1998) / Living Planet Index (vertebrate populations since 1970) | **WWF** with Zoological Society of London |
| Global Assessment on Biodiversity (2019): ~25% of species threatened, up to 1 million face extinction | **IPBES** (not WWF) |
- **Living Planet Report 2022:** global biodiversity and planet health; average **69% decline** in monitored wildlife populations **since 1970** (LPR 2024: 73%).
> Exam angle: "Red Data Book published by / contains" (×10), IUCN full form, ISSG parent body (UPSC 2023), LPR 2022 statements.

=== Invasive Alien Species
- **Invasive alien species:** non-native organisms introduced outside their natural range that "**arrive, survive and thrive**", outcompete natives and cause economic, environmental and health harm; exotic plants that **crowd out native plants** = **invasive plants**.
- Three tests: **non-native origin, uncontrolled spread, measurable harm** (NOT commercial usefulness). Defined in India under the **WPA 1972** (Amendment 2022, **Section 62A**: Centre regulates/prohibits IAS; new **Schedule IV** = CITES-appendix specimens, NOT invasives).
- Invasives contributed to nearly **40%** of known animal extinctions since the **17th century**; threaten **1 in 10** Red List species; damage per species is higher in the **Global South**.
- Invasive plants cut **medicinal plants, fruits and fodder grass** used by indigenous communities, and degrade the **herbivore forage on which tigers depend** – both harms are real.

=== Extinction – Mass Extinctions and Co-extinction
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Extinction** | Disappearance of **all members** of a species (death of the last individual), globally |
| Extirpation | Local extinction only |
| **Co-extinction** | Loss of one species drags down its obligate partners (plant–pollinator, host–parasite) |
| Background extinction | Slow natural loss at a low steady rate |
| Mass extinction | High percentage of species lost in a short geological period |
| **Sixth extinction** | Present, **human-driven** wave – over-exploitation of resources, habitat loss and fragmentation, ecosystem destruction, pollution, climate change |
- **Five** mass extinctions so far; the **fifth** (end-Cretaceous, ~**66 million years ago**) ended the dinosaurs – **asteroid-impact theory of Luis Alvarez** (1980, with Walter Alvarez).
- Sixth extinction: rates **100-1,000 times** pre-human rates (NextGen); IPBES: tens to hundreds of times background. It is not about GM crops, monoculture or a feared meteorite.
- **Dodo** (Mauritius): classic **co-extinction** case – the tree whose seeds it dispersed (*Calvaria*/tambalacoque) failed to reproduce.
- IUCN Red List (2014): **784** extinctions in 500 years; 128 bird species lost in 500 years. Extinct: **passenger pigeon, Steller's sea cow** (over-exploitation), Bali, Javan and Caspian tigers.
- **Extinct in the Wild:** Hawaiian crow, Père David's deer (only in captivity).
- **India:** **cheetah** declared extinct **1952** – the only large mammal lost since Independence; ZSI lists **cheetah, pink-headed duck, mountain quail** as extinct from India (not the passenger pigeon).
> Exam angle: definition (extinction, extirpation), sixth extinction (UPSC 2018 ×2), dodo (UPSC 1998 ×2), Alvarez (SSC 2019).

=== Climate Change and Consequences of Loss
- Warming causes **coral bleaching**, ocean acidification, shifts in ecosystem boundaries and **disrupted migration**; rising temperatures **shift species ranges** and raise extinction risk in hotspots (Jun 2026); a warming Arctic is opening to **alien plants** (Feb 2026).
- Loss disrupts species interactions, **pollination**, **nutrient cycling**, water and soil services → lower **food security** for resource-dependent communities; once lost, biodiversity cannot be recreated.
- **Zoonotic spillover** rises with habitat disruption and wildlife trade → **One Health** approach (humans, animals, ecosystems).
> Exam angle: own MCQs only (assertion-reason, consequences).
