=== Western and Eastern Ghats and the Southern Hills
# Western Ghats vs Eastern Ghats
| Feature | Western Ghats (Sahyadri) | Eastern Ghats |
|---|---|---|
| Extent | **Tapti valley to near Kanyakumari**, about **1,600 km** | **Mahanadi (Odisha) to the Vaigai (Tamil Nadu)**, about 1,750 km |
| Continuity | **Continuous**; steep west face, gentle east slope; watershed of the Peninsula | **Discontinuous**, eroded groups of hills; **cut by the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri** (not the Narmada) |
| Height | 900–1,600 m; **rises from north to south** (higher in the south) | Average about **600 m** (610 m) |
| Rocks | Deccan lava in the north (horizontal sheets → **stepped, "landing stair" look**) | **Khondalite and charnockite** |
| Highest | **Anamudi 2,695 m** (Anaimalai, **Kerala**) = highest in South India and in the Peninsula | **Jindhagada 1,690 m** (PMF, Rocket) / **Arma Konda 1,680 m** = highest peak of **Andhra Pradesh** (Rocket); Mahendragiri ~1,501 m (Odisha) |
| Passes / gaps | **Thal Ghat** (Mumbai–**Nashik**), **Bhor Ghat** (Mumbai–**Pune**), **Pal Ghat** | River gaps |
- **"Ghat"** in Hindi means both **river-landing steps (stairs)** and a **mountain pass**; the WBCS 2017 key gives **"gap or pass"**.
- **Local names, north → south:** **Sahyadri** (Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka) → **Nilgiri** → **Anaimalai** → Palani → **Cardamom (Yela Mala)**.
- Sahyadri peaks: **Kalsubai (1,646 m, Maharashtra)**, Salher, Mahabaleshwar; **Mullayanagiri 1,892 m = highest of Karnataka** (Baba Budan hills).
- A statement "the Western Ghats are higher in their northern region" is **false**; the Narmada and Tapi valleys are old rift valleys and the Tapi lies south of the Satpura.
- **Vandaravu (Palani), Perumal Malai (Palani), Velliangiri (Coimbatore) and Anamudi** are peaks/hills of the **Western Ghats**.
- Goran Ghat is **not** a Western Ghats pass.
# Nilgiris – where the two Ghats meet
- The **Eastern and Western Ghats meet at the Nilgiri Hills** (Gudalur), the **tri-junction of Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu**; "Blue Mountains".
- **Doddabetta (2,637 m)** = highest of the Nilgiris, second in South India; **Ooty** lies in the Nilgiris; home of the **Toda** tribe.
- **Pal Ghat (Palakkad gap)** at the southern end of the Nilgiris, **between the Nilgiris (north) and the Anaimalai (south)**; 144 m, about 24–30 km wide; misfit river Ponnani; Kerala–TN rail and road link.
# Hills of the far south
| Hills | Place | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Anaimalai | Kerala–TN | Anamudi; three ranges radiate from it |
| Palani (Palni) | Dindigul, TN | **Kodaikanal** |
| Cardamom (Yela Mala) | **Kerala–Tamil Nadu border** | **Southernmost range; closest to Kanyakumari**; Agastyamalai; tea at **Kanan Devan** (Munnar) |
| Shevaroy (Servarayan) | Salem, **Tamil Nadu** (Eastern Ghats) | **Yercaud** |
| Javadi, Biligirirangan | TN / **Karnataka** | Eastern Ghats remnants |
| Seshachalam | **Southern Andhra Pradesh** (Eastern Ghats, Tirupati) | Not part of the Western Ghats or Nilgiri/Palani |
- **Tea** grows in Kanan Devan, the Nilgiris and Darjeeling; **Girnar** (Gujarat) has **no tea**.
- **UPSC 2023:** none of the three statements is correct – Amarkantak joins the **Vindhya and Satpura** (not Sahyadri); Biligirirangan is in **Karnataka** (Eastern Ghats, not Satpura); Seshachalam is in **Andhra Pradesh** (Eastern Ghats, not the southernmost Western Ghats).
- **Eastern Ghats in AP:** Nallamala (Chenchus; **Srisailam Tiger Reserve**), Erramala (Krishna–Penna divide), Seshachalam (**red sanders**), Velikonda, Palkonda (Tirupati), **Papikondalu** (Godavari gorge); Araku Valley = "**Ooty of Andhra Pradesh**".
> Exam angle: the Nilgiri "meeting point" is asked again and again (UPSC 2008, UPPSC, CGPSC 2024, MPPSC 2025); Anamudi vs Doddabetta is the classic trap.

=== Peninsular Plateau – Structure, Plateaus and the Deccan Traps
# Structure
- **Oldest and largest** physiographic division; a **Precambrian shield**, part of **Gondwana land**; one of the most **stable** blocks; **not** of volcanic origin overall; rich in **metallic minerals**.
- About **16 lakh sq km**; **triangular**; outer edge: Delhi ridge (NW), **Rajmahal hills (E)**, Gir (W), Cardamom hills (S); extensions: **Meghalaya / Karbi Anglong plateau** and Kutch-Kathiawar.
- **Tilts east** (most rivers flow east); Narmada and Tapi flow west in rift valleys.
- NCERT: three broad groups – **Deccan Plateau, Central Highlands, North-Eastern Plateau** (Kutch is not one).
- **S.P. Chatterjee** divides the Peninsula into 8 parts; the **Mahanadi basin** falls in the **Northern Deccan Plateau** (old app key).
# Plateaus
| Plateau | Where | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Malwa** | **Western MP and SE Rajasthan** | **Between the Aravalli and the Vindhya** (Vindhya to its south); Bundelkhand to its **east**; lava, **black (lava) soil**; **two drainage systems** – to the Arabian Sea (Narmada, Mahi) and to the Bay of Bengal (Chambal, Betwa → Yamuna) |
| **Chota Nagpur** | **Mainly Jharkhand**; also Chhattisgarh, Odisha, WB, Bihar | **Storehouse of minerals**, "Ruhr of India", **biggest mineral deposits**; series of plateaus (patlands: Ranchi, Hazaribagh, Palamu) – "a front sloping" plateau; source of **Damodar** (Khamarpat hill), **Koel, Subarnarekha**; **Rajmahal hills on its north-eastern edge** (Jharkhand) |
| **Deccan** | South of the Narmada; ~7 lakh sq km | Largest plateau; northern boundary **Satpura, Mahadeo, Maikal**; Western and Eastern Ghats on the sides; **Andhra Pradesh lies on the Deccan plateau**; **Vidarbha** is the eastern region of **Maharashtra** |
| **Meghalaya (Shillong)** | Meghalaya | Part of the **Peninsular Plateau**, cut off by the **Malda (Garo–Rajmahal) gap**; **Garo, Khasi, Jaintia** hills (Bhuban hills of Assam are **not** part); **living root bridges** of the Khasis in **Meghalaya** |
| **Dandakaranya** | **Chhattisgarh and Odisha**, also Telangana, Maharashtra, AP | ~89,078 sq km; central-eastern highland |
| **Bhander** | MP | **Southern part of the Vindhyan scarpland** |
| Karnataka (Mysore) | Karnataka | **Malnad** = hilly forested western Karnataka (not AP) |
- **Lowest-lying region of India:** **Kuttanad** (Kerala), about 2 m below sea level.
# Deccan Traps
- **Fissure eruptions** of basaltic lava → **solidification of lava**; at the **end of the Cretaceous** (Cretaceous–Eocene), in the **north-west** of the Peninsula.
- 'Trap' = Swedish for **step**; once ~10 lakh sq km, now ~5 lakh sq km; thickness 3,000 m (west) → 150 m (east); weathers into **regur** (black cotton soil).
| Formation | Age (UPSC 1997) |
|---|---|
| Deccan Traps | **Cretaceous–Eocene** |
| Western Ghats | **Late Cenozoic** |
| Aravalli | **Pre-Cambrian** |
| Narmada–Tapti alluvium | **Pleistocene** |
- **Structural statements:** Meghalaya plateau = extension of the Deccan/Peninsular plateau; Kashmir valley formed in a **synclinorium**; the Gangetic plain in a **foredeep** (all true).
> Exam angle: oldest region = Peninsular Plateau (CGPSC ×2); Chota Nagpur as the mineral storehouse (×4).

=== Central Ranges – Vindhya, Satpura, Maikal, Aravalli and Peaks
# Ranges
| Range | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Vindhya** | North of the Narmada; an **escarpment**; separates the **Northern Plains from the Peninsula** (watershed of Ganga and southern rivers); source of **Betwa**, Ken, Chambal; continues east as Kaimur (MP, UP, Bihar) |
| **Satpura** | "Seven folds"; **between the Narmada and the Tapi** (a horst); ~900 km; **central India**; **northern boundary of the Deccan**; Dhupgarh highest |
| **Mahadeo hills** | **Part of the Satpura** (Pachmarhi, MP) |
| **Maikal** | **Eastern end of the Satpura** (MP–Chhattisgarh); **Amarkantak** = source of **Narmada, Son, Johila**; Ramgiri hills (Chhattisgarh) placed in the Maikal (CGPSC key) |
| **Aravalli** | **Oldest** fold range of India (Delhi to Gujarat, ~800 km); Guru Shikhar highest |
| **Ajanta** | Only in **Maharashtra** (a range in a single State) |
| Satmala, Harishchandra, Balaghat | **Maharashtra** (spurs of the Western Ghats) |
| **Mandav hills** | **Gujarat** (Kathiawar) – not Maharashtra |
| Garhjat | Odisha |
- **West → east:** **Satpura – Mahadeo – Maikal – Chota Nagpur**; **Barwani – Mahadeo – Maikal**; **Mahadeo – Maikal – Chota Nagpur – Khasi**.
- **North → south:** **Mahadeo hills – Satpura range – Sahyadri** (UPSC 2004).
- **Narmada** flows between the **Vindhya (north) and the Satpura (south)**.
- **UPSC 2014 pairs:** **Mahadeo – Central India** and **Mikir – North-East** are right; Cardamom is not on the Coromandel coast, Kaimur not on the Konkan.
- The Aravalli is the **oldest**, not the youngest, range; Visakhapatnam and Bhubaneswar lie along the Eastern Ghats belt.

=== Coastal Plains
# Divisions
| Coast | Extent | State / features |
|---|---|---|
| Kachchh–Kathiawar | — | Gujarat; Kachchh built by **silt of the Indus**; Kathiawar part of the plateau |
| **Konkan** | **Daman to Goa** (northern part of the west coast) | Maharashtra–Goa; marine erosion – cliffs, shoals, reefs, islands; Janjira, Ratnagiri, Sindhudurg |
| **Kannad (Kanara)** | **Goa to Mangaluru** – the **central stretch** between Konkan and Malabar | Karnataka |
| **Malabar** | **Mangaluru to Kanyakumari** | **Kerala**; lakes, lagoons, **backwaters (kayals)** – **Vembanad** (largest, **Kochi port**), Ashtamudi; dunes = teris |
| Utkal | Subarnarekha to Rushikulya | Odisha; Mahanadi delta |
| **Northern Circars** | **Mahanadi to the Krishna** | Odisha–AP; **Chilika lake** (largest brackish lagoon) |
| **Coromandel** | **Krishna delta to Cape Comorin (Kanyakumari)**; **Pulicat to Kanyakumari** | **Tamil Nadu** (and southern AP); **Buckingham Canal** (796 km, Kakinada–Parangipettai); **Karaikal** |
- **Cape Comorin = Kanyakumari**, where the eastern and western coastal plains meet.
- **West coast north → south:** Janjira (Raigad) – Sindhudurg – Kannur – Nagercoil.
- **West coast:** narrow, **submergent**, wetter (more rain), estuaries suit ports. **East coast:** broad, **emergent**, deltas, drier; it grows **more rice** than the west coast (A true, R false – the east coast gets **less** rain).
- **Gulf of Mannar** (not the Gulf of Khambhat) lies between Tamil Nadu (Nagercoil, Rameswaram) and Sri Lanka; **Gulf of Khambhat** (Gujarat) has the largest tidal energy potential.
- India's coastline is straight because of **faulting** (Cretaceous–early Tertiary break-up of Gondwana).
- **Kerala** became India's first fully **digital banking** State (7 Jan 2023) – a CA item asked with the coast.
# Coastline and lagoons
- **Coastline: 11,098.81 km** (2023-24 remeasurement by the **National Hydrographic Office and Survey of India**; PIB 4 Dec 2025): **Gujarat 2,340.62 km (1st)**, **Tamil Nadu 1,068.69 km (2nd)**, **Andhra Pradesh 1,053.07 km (3rd)**; old figures 7,516.6 km and AP 973.7 km ("974 km, 2nd"). Islands counted: **1,389**.
- AP: longest district coast **Srikakulam (~193 km)**.
- **Pulicat** (AP–TN): 2nd-largest brackish lagoon; **Sriharikota** separates it from the sea. **Kolleru**: once a lagoon, now an **inland freshwater lake between the Godavari and Krishna**.
> Exam angle: Coromandel (×7) and Buckingham Canal (×2) dominate; Settled fact – AP is now **3rd** in coastline.

=== Indian Desert and the Rann of Kutch
# Thar (Great Indian Desert)
- Lies **north-west / west of the Aravallis**; undulating sandy plain with **barchans and longitudinal (seif) dunes**; rainfall **below 150 mm**; **Marusthali** (true desert, west) and **Bagar** (semi-arid strip along the Aravallis).
- Order of observation (APPSC key): **semi-arid/arid weather → sand dunes → thorny bushes → seasonal streams** that appear only with rain.
- Extent: from the edge of the **Rann of Kutch** across the **Luni** to the Aravalli face (APPSC key).
- **Luni** = the only large river of the region; rises in the **Aravalli near Ajmer**; **"Indian desert" and "Thar desert" are the same desert**; it is **lost in the Rann of Kutch** – does not reach the sea or the Gulf of Kutch.
- Under the sea in the Mesozoic (Akal wood fossils, Jaisalmer); salt lakes – **Sambhar** largest.
# Rann of Kutch
- **Seasonal salt marsh** (Great Rann north, Little Rann south), Gujarat–Sindh; once a shallow arm of the Arabian Sea / Kutch an island; **repeated falls in sea level** left the present marshland (UPSC 2023).
- **Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary** – Little Rann, **Gujarat**; **Banni grasslands** at the edge of the salt marsh – **Gujarat**.
- **Agarias** = traditional **salt-pan workers** of the Little Rann, Gujarat (UPSC 2009).

=== Physiographic Divisions of India
- **Six divisions** (NCERT): **Himalayas, Northern Plains, Peninsular Plateau, Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, Islands**.
- **Plains** cover the largest share of land (about **43%**), mountains ~30%, plateaus ~27%.
- **Oldest** = Peninsular Plateau; **youngest** = Northern Plains (NextGen p.13).
- Statements: the **Sahara** is **not** in the subcontinent; the **Eastern Ghats** run along the east coast; the **Deccan** is a **basaltic plateau**, not fertile lowland.
| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Himalaya | World's highest range, peaks over 29,000 ft |
| Thar | Arid region of sand dunes and extreme temperatures |
| Deccan | High plateau covering much of southern India |
| Ganga–Brahmaputra delta | Vast alluvial plain where two great rivers meet |
| Chota Nagpur | Central/eastern India, rich in minerals |
| Malwa | MP and Rajasthan |
| Karnataka plateau | Karnataka, TN, Telangana parts |
| Deccan plateau (match) | Southern India, between the Eastern and Western Ghats |

=== Northern Plains
- **Aggradational** plain built by Himalayan rivers (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra); ~6.5–7 lakh sq km; 2,400–3,200 km long; northern edge = **Himalayan Frontal Fault**; geologically **youngest** division.
- Very gentle slope: **about 200 m drop over ~1,600 km** from the Yamuna at Delhi to the Bay of Bengal (average plain height ~200 m).
| Belt | Feature |
|---|---|
| Bhabar | Pebbles at the Shiwalik foot; streams sink |
| **Terai** | **Swampy, marshy**, forested belt where streams re-emerge |
| Bhangar | Older alluvium, kankar |
| Khadar | Newer floodplain alluvium |
| **Bhur** | **Undulating aeolian sandy deposit** in the upper Ganga–Yamuna doab |
- Punjab plain = doabs of Indus tributaries; Ganga plain divisions: Ganga–Yamuna doab, Rohilkhand, Avadh, Bihar, Bengal (not Malwa); **Kosi** = "Sorrow of Bihar", a mega-fan; Ganga–Brahmaputra delta = deltaic plain; Chambal = ravines/badlands; loess plains = wind-blown silt.
