=== NOTES 3:80 | Reserve Bank of India – Establishment, Organisation and Functions
## SUB 0 | Establishment of Rbi
[80:0:0] # Basics
[80:0:1] - RBI = the **central bank** of India; a **statutory body** under the **RBI Act, 1934**; called the **Banker's Bank**.
[80:0:2] - Set up on the recommendation of the **Hilton Young Commission** (Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance, **1926**); **Ambedkar's 1925** evidence to it shaped the concept.
[80:0:3] # Timeline
[80:0:4] TABLE head: Event | Date
  [80:0:4.0] Hilton Young Commission recommends a central bank | 1926
  [80:0:4.1] RBI Act passed | 1934
  [80:0:4.2] RBI starts functioning (private shareholders' bank, Central Office Calcutta) | 1 April 1935
  [80:0:4.3] Central Office moves Calcutta → Bombay (permanent) | 1937
  [80:0:4.4] Nationalised under RBI (Transfer to Public Ownership) Act, 1948 | 1 January 1949
  [80:0:4.5] RBI@90 | 1 April 2024
[80:0:5] Act **1934** → functioning **1935** → nationalised **1949** (Act of 1948). RBI was established in 1935, not 1949; nationalised in 1949, not 1937.
[80:0:6] - **Seal**: **tiger and palm tree** (tiger replaced the lion of the East India Company double mohur).
[80:0:7] - **Accounting year**: January–December (1935) → **July–June** (from 1940) → **April–March** from **2020-21** (transition year of nine months, July 2020–March 2021).
## SUB 1 | Organisation and Governance
[80:1:0] # Central Board and Governors
[80:1:1] - **Central Board of Directors**: **21** members, appointed by the Government of India under the RBI Act; directors hold office **4 years**.
[80:1:2] - **Governor and Deputy Governors**: appointed by the **Government of India** (ACC approves); tenure **up to 5 years**.
[80:1:3] - **Four Deputy Governors**: two from within RBI + one commercial banker + one **economist** (heads monetary policy). **Rohit Jain** appointed May 2026 for 3 years, replacing T. Rabi Sankar.
[80:1:4] - Headquarters: **Mumbai** ("Mint Street"); offices in 31 locations. Regional representations (4): New Delhi, Chennai, Kolkata, Mumbai.
[80:1:5] # Governors – Firsts and Chronology

  [80:1:6.2] Manmohan Singh | 1982-85

  [80:1:6.7] Sanjay Malhotra | Since Dec 2024; chairs MPC and Payments Regulatory Board
[80:1:7] **Raja J. Chelliah** was NOT an RBI Governor.
[80:1:8] # Offices and Institutes
[80:1:9] - Training: **Reserve Bank Staff College, Chennai**; **College of Agricultural Banking, Pune**.
[80:1:10] - **Autonomous institutes**: **NIBM, IGIDR, IDRBT** (Staff College is NOT one).
[80:1:11] - **AP angle**: RBI's Andhra Pradesh regional office began on **22 Aug 2022** (working from Hyderabad); moved to **Vijayawada** in 2025; currency work for AP stays with the **Hyderabad** office.
[80:1:12] - **Local Boards:** **four** – Mumbai (West), Kolkata (East), Chennai (South), New Delhi (North); members appointed by the **Central Government** for **4 years**; each sends **one director** to the Central Board. Not functioning since 2022 for lack of quorum (a Standing Committee of the Central Board acts for them).
## SUB 2 | Central Banking Functions
[80:2:0] # Functions and Legal Basis
[80:2:1] TABLE head: Function | Legal basis / content
  [80:2:1.0] Bank of issue | Sec 22: sole right to issue notes except the ₹1 note; exchanges and destroys unfit notes
  [80:2:1.1] Banker to Government | Sec 20 (Centre), Sec 21/21A (States, by agreement); public debt, Ways and Means Advances, overdrafts
  [80:2:1.2] Banker's bank | Keeps SCBs' deposits and reserves; lender of last resort (emergency liquidity)
  [80:2:1.3] Monetary authority | Price stability, growth in mind; six-member MPC sets repo
  [80:2:1.4] Custodian of forex | Manages forex reserves under FEMA 1999; represents India at IMF, World Bank
  [80:2:1.5] Controller of credit | Controls money and credit (qualitative and quantitative measures); controls credit creation by commercial banks
  [80:2:1.6] Fiscal agent and adviser | Agent of Government for India's IMF membership; adviser in monetary and financial matters
  [80:2:1.7] Regulator | Banks, NBFCs, AIFIs, HFCs (since 2019); payments under PSS Act, 2007; regulates ECBs
[80:2:2] - **Public Debt Office** functions under RBI; Public Debt Management Cell eases the debt-vs-monetary-policy conflict.
[80:2:3] - RBI is banker to **all State Governments except Sikkim** (Sikkim: limited agreement for public debt only).
[80:2:4] - **Bank Rate**: rate at which RBI lends to / rediscounts bills of commercial banks. Repo rate is set by RBI.
[80:2:5] # What RBI Does NOT Do
[80:2:6] - Accept deposits from or **lend to the public**; **mint coins**; issue the **₹1 note**.
[80:2:7] - **Credit creation** (done by commercial banks); **regulation of foreign trade**; income tax collection.
[80:2:8] - Does not fix the **PLR** (banks' Prime Lending Rate) or savings account rates (deregulated **25 Oct 2011**).
[80:2:9] - Does not regulate securities (**SEBI**), insurance (**IRDAI**), pensions (**PFRDA**).
[80:2:10] Monetary policy = RBI (MPC since 2016); **fiscal policy = Ministry of Finance**; inflation target set by **Finance Ministry in consultation with RBI**.
## SUB 3 | Currency Management
[80:3:0] # Issuer
[80:3:1] TABLE head: Item | Issued by | Signed by | Legal tender
  [80:3:1.0] ₹1 note | Government of India (Ministry of Finance) | Finance Secretary | –
  [80:3:1.1] Remaining notes | RBI | RBI Governor | Unlimited
  [80:3:1.2] Coins | Government of India (RBI only puts them into circulation) | – | Limited (issued up to ₹1,000, Coinage Act)
[80:3:2] # Reserve Backing
[80:3:3] TABLE head: System | Rule
  [80:3:3.0] Proportional Reserve System (till 1956) | 2/5 of backing in gold or sterling; gold at least ₹40 crore
  [80:3:3.1] Minimum Reserve System (1956) | ₹515 crore = ₹400 crore foreign securities + ₹115 crore gold
  [80:3:3.2] Amended 1957 | ₹200 crore of gold and forex, of which ₹115 crore gold
[80:3:4] - Beyond that floor RBI can issue currency **without limit**.
[80:3:5] - Assets backing notes (**Sec 33**): gold coin, bullion, rupee coin, GoI rupee securities, foreign securities, held in the Issue Department.
[80:3:6] # Denominations and Demonetisation
[80:3:7] - Highest denomination permitted: **₹10,000** (**Sec 24**); first printed **1938**, demonetised **1946**, revived **1954**, demonetised **1978**.
[80:3:8] - Demonetisation power: **Sec 26(2)**.
[80:3:9] TABLE head: Year | Notes
  [80:3:9.0] 1946 | High-denomination notes
  [80:3:9.1] 1978 | ₹1,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000 (HDBN Demonetisation Act)
  [80:3:9.2] 8 Nov 2016 | ₹500, ₹1,000; SC upheld 4:1 (2023)
[80:3:10] - **₹2,000** note withdrawn **May 2023**, still legal tender.
[80:3:11] # Note Features
[80:3:12] - **17 languages**: Hindi and English in front; **language panel of 15** at the back.
[80:3:13] - **MANI** (Mobile Aided Note Identifier) app for visually impaired (RBI, 2020).

## SUB 5 | Regulatory and Supervisory Role
[80:5:0] # Legal Basis
[80:5:1] TABLE head: Act / Section | Provision
  [80:5:1.0] BR Act, 1949 | Licensing, branches, management, amalgamation, SLR, audit, inspection
  [80:5:1.1] BR Act Sec 22(1) | Licensing of payments banks (e.g. IPPB, 2018)
  [80:5:1.2] BR Act Sec 26A | DEA Fund (2014): deposits unclaimed 10+ years; depositor's right survives
  [80:5:1.3] RBI Act Chapter IIIB | NBFC regulation
[80:5:2] - No bank or NBFC may open without an **RBI licence**; on-tap licensing of UCBs to resume after 20+ years; 1,457 UCBs (Aug 2026).
[80:5:3] - **Branch expansion**: banks, SFBs, payments banks, LABs, RRBs may open branches without prior approval if **25%** of new branches are in **unbanked rural** areas (Aug 2026).
[80:5:4] - **Mergers** approved by RBI; RBI can seek **winding up** of a bank.

## SUB 7 | Rbi Income and Surplus Transfer
[80:7:0] - Income: interest on domestic and foreign securities, loans to Governments and banks, **forex gains**, fees (buying/selling G-bonds and foreign currency).
[80:7:1] - **Bimal Jalan Committee** (Economic Capital Framework, **2018**): separated realised equity from revaluation balances; **Contingent Risk Buffer** 5.5–6.5% → **4.5–7.5%** (revised 2025).
[80:7:2] - Surplus **FY 2025-26: record ₹2.86 lakh crore**, 6.7% above FY25 (₹2.6+ lakh crore).
