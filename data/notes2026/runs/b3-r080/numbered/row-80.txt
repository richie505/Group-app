## SUB 0 | Establishment, Nationalisation and Headquarters
[0:0] # Origin
[0:1] - **Hilton Young Commission** = **Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance (1926)**: recommended a central bank; the RBI was set up **on its recommendation** (not Narasimham, Gorwala or Malhotra, which came later).
[0:2] - **B.R. Ambedkar's 1925** evidence to this Commission shaped the idea of the RBI.
[0:3] - Earlier, the **Royal Commission on Indian Finance and Currency (1913)** was chaired by **Austen Chamberlain** (Chamberlain Commission) – not Keynes, who was only a member.

## SUB 1 | Organisation and Governors
[1:0] # Governance
[1:1] - **Central Board of Directors**: **21** members, appointed by the Government of India under the RBI Act; directors hold office for **4 years**.
[1:2] - **Governor and Deputy Governors**: appointed (nominated) by the **Central Government**; tenure **not more than 5 years**. The head of the RBI is called the **Governor**; the Governor draws power from the **RBI Act**.
[1:3] - **Four Deputy Governors**: two from within RBI + one commercial banker + one **economist** (heads monetary policy). **Rohit Jain** appointed May 2026 for 3 years, replacing T. Rabi Sankar.
[1:4] - **Local Boards: four** – Mumbai (West), Kolkata (East), Chennai (South), New Delhi (North); members appointed by the Central Government for 4 years; each sends one director to the Central Board (not functioning since 2022 for lack of quorum). Hyderabad has none.

## SUB 2 | Central Banking Functions – Banker to Government and Banker's Bank
[2:0] # The central bank
[2:1] - The **RBI performs the central banking functions** in India; it is the **Central Bank** (not SBI, NABARD, SBH or Central Bank of India, which are banks/DFIs). It is the regulator of the entire banking system.
[2:2] TABLE head: Function | Legal basis / content
  [2:2.0] Bank of issue | Sec 22: sole right to issue notes except the ₹1 note
  [2:2.1] Banker, agent and adviser to Government | Sec 20 (Centre), Sec 21/21A (States, by agreement); payments, receipts, public debt management, borrowing programme, Ways and Means Advances, overdrafts
  [2:2.2] Banker's bank | Holds SCBs' deposits and reserves (CRR); lender of last resort; guides and advises banks
  [2:2.3] Monetary authority | Price stability with growth in mind; MPC sets the repo rate
  [2:2.4] Custodian of forex | Manages forex reserves under FEMA 1999; stabilises the rupee's external value
  [2:2.5] Controller of credit | Controls money and credit (quantitative and qualitative measures)
  [2:2.6] Payments | Regulates and upgrades payment systems (PSS Act 2007)
  [2:2.7] Regulator | Banks, NBFCs (Chapter IIIB), AIFIs, HFCs
[2:3] # Banker to Government
[2:4] - Manages **public debt of Centre and States** and handles the **Government of India's borrowing programme** (not the Department of Expenditure – UPSC 2004); maintains Government accounts; **Public Debt Office** functions under it.
[2:5] - Banker to **all State Governments except Sikkim** (Sikkim: limited agreement, public debt only).
[2:6] - **Fiscal agent and adviser** to Government in monetary and financial matters; **represents India at the IMF** (Government's agent for India's IMF membership) and World Bank.
[2:7] - **UPSC 2001:** banker to Centre, formulates monetary policy, agent for IMF membership, handles GoI borrowing – **all four correct**.
[2:8] # Banker's bank and lender of last resort
[2:9] - **Banker's bank** (IAS 2012): (i) other banks **keep deposits/reserves with the RBI**, (ii) the RBI **lends to banks in need**, (iii) it **advises banks on monetary matters** – **all three**.
[2:10] - **Lender of last resort (LoLR)** – exclusive RBI function: **emergency liquidity to banks facing a temporary crisis** (UPSC 2021: **2 only**); it is **not** lending to trade/industry or financing budget deficits.
[2:11] - **Bank Rate** = rate at which the **RBI lends to / rediscounts bills of commercial banks** (also called the discount rate) – not the rate banks charge borrowers or pay depositors.
[2:12] - **FEMA A-R (APPSC):** RBI administers FEMA 1999 "to facilitate external trade and payments and promote orderly development of the forex market"; it also upgrades **payment systems** – both true, **R does not explain A**.

## SUB 3 | Monetary Authority, Credit Control and What RBI Does Not Do
[3:0] # Monetary policy and credit control
[3:1] - **Monetary (and credit) policy is formulated and implemented by the RBI** (MPC since 2016; six members) – the RBI **regulates and announces the repo and reverse repo rates**, not the Finance Ministry, PM, President, CAG or SBI.
[3:2] - **Price stability / controlling inflation** = **RBI** (flexible inflation targeting; target set by the **Finance Ministry in consultation with the RBI**) – not the FSDC, Dept of Consumer Affairs or Expenditure Management Commission.
[3:3] - RBI **controls "money and credit"** and **controls credit creation by commercial banks** through quantitative (Bank Rate, repo, CRR, SLR, OMO) and qualitative (selective credit control, margins, moral suasion) tools.
[3:4] - Other than market forces, the **RBI intervenes (buys/sells dollars) to manage the exchange rate**.
[3:5] - **A-R (APPSC 2024):** institutions ensure stability through monetary policy **because** central banks use repo and CRR to manage liquidity and inflation – **R explains A**.
[3:6] - **Regulating and controlling banks** = an activity of the **RBI** (powers under the **Banking Regulation Act 1949**).
[3:7] - 2016 framework (MPC + inflation-targeting law) gave **operational independence** on policy rates.
[3:8] # What the RBI does NOT do
[3:9] - **Credit creation** – done by **commercial banks**; the RBI only **controls** it.
[3:10] - **Regulation of foreign trade** (Commerce Ministry/DGFT) – the RBI regulates **currency, credit and forex reserves**.
[3:11] - **Fiscal policy** – formulated by the **Ministry of Finance** (not RBI, Finance Commission or Planning Commission).
[3:12] - **Granting corporate deeds**; lending to the public; minting coins; issuing the ₹1 note; income-tax collection.
[3:13] - **Savings bank interest rate** in nationalised banks: fixed by the **RBI until it was deregulated on 25 Oct 2011** – now each bank fixes it; never the Finance Ministry, Finance Commission or IBA (UPSC 2010: **None of the above**).
[3:14] - Does not regulate securities (SEBI), insurance (IRDAI) or pensions (PFRDA).

## SUB 4 | Currency Management – Note Issue, Reserve System and Demonetisation
[4:0] # Who issues what
[4:1] TABLE head: Item | Issued by | Signed by | Legal tender
  [4:1.0] ₹1 note | Government of India (Ministry of Finance) | Finance Secretary | Unlimited
  [4:1.1] All other notes (₹2 upward; ₹10 and above in the new series) | RBI (Sec 22, sole right, on behalf of the Centre) | RBI Governor | Unlimited
  [4:1.2] Coins | Government of India (minted by GoI; RBI only distributes them as GoI's agent) | – | Limited (up to ₹1,000, Coinage Act)
[4:2] - So "RBI issues **all** types of currency notes" is **wrong**; the ₹100 note carries the **Governor's** signature.
[4:3] - RBI can issue notes up to **₹10,000** (Sec 24); first ₹10,000 note 1938, demonetised 1946, re-introduced 1954, demonetised 1978.
[4:4] # Reserve system for note issue
[4:5] TABLE head: System | Rule
  [4:5.0] Proportional Reserve System (till 1956) | 2/5 of assets in gold or sterling securities; gold at least ₹40 crore
  [4:5.1] Minimum Reserve System (1956) | ₹515 crore = ₹400 crore foreign securities + ₹115 crore gold
  [4:5.2] Amended 1957 (present) | ₹200 crore of gold and forex, of which at least ₹115 crore in gold
[4:6] - India's note issue is based on the **Minimum Reserve System**; beyond the floor the RBI can issue currency **to any extent**.
[4:7] - Notes are issued against **gold coin and bullion, foreign securities, rupee coin and GoI securities** (Sec 33) – "all of the above".
[4:8] # Demonetisation
[4:9] TABLE head: Year | Notes withdrawn
  [4:9.0] 1946 | High-denomination notes (before Independence)
  [4:9.1] 16 Jan 1978 | ₹1,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000 – first after Independence (HDBN Demonetisation Act; Morarji Desai)
  [4:9.2] 8 Nov 2016 (from midnight) | ₹500 and ₹1,000 (Mahatma Gandhi series); SC upheld 4:1 (2023)
[4:10] - Power: **Sec 26(2) of the RBI Act** – the Government notifies **on the recommendation of the RBI Central Board** (the 2016 notification followed the Board's recommendation).
[4:11] - **Specified Bank Notes (Cessation of Liabilities) Act, 2017**: holding old ₹500/₹1,000 notes above the limit → fine of **₹10,000** or five times the face value, whichever is higher.
[4:12] - **₹2,000** note: issued Nov 2016, withdrawn from circulation **May 2023**, still legal tender.
[4:13] - Printing cost of a new ₹500 note: about **₹2.87–3.09** (2016-17) → nearest **₹3**.

## SUB 6 | Regulatory Role, Committees and Publications
[6:0] # Regulation of banks
[6:1] - **Banking Regulation Act, 1949** gave the **RBI** extensive powers: licensing, branches, management, amalgamation, inspection, audit.
[6:2] - **UPSC 2013:** the RBI regulates banks in **liquidity of assets, branch expansion, merger and winding-up** – **all four**.
[6:3] TABLE head: Act / Section | Provision
  [6:3.0] BR Act Sec 22(1) | Licensing of banks, incl. payments banks
  [6:3.1] BR Act Sec 26A | DEA Fund (2014): deposits unclaimed 10+ years; depositor's right survives
  [6:3.2] RBI Act Chapter IIIB | NBFC regulation
  [6:3.3] PSS Act 2007 | Payment systems
[6:4] - No bank or NBFC may open without an **RBI licence**; **mergers approved by the RBI**, which can seek the **winding up** of a bank.
[6:5] - **Branch expansion**: banks, SFBs, payments banks, LABs, RRBs may open branches without prior approval if **25%** of new branches are in **unbanked rural** areas.

## SUB 7 | RBI Autonomy, Income and Developmental Role
[7:0] # Key sections of the RBI Act
[7:1] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [7:1.0] 7 | Centre may give directions in public interest after consulting the Governor (a power in the RBI Act, not the Constitution)
  [7:1.1] 22 | Sole right of note issue
  [7:1.2] 24 | Highest denomination ₹10,000
  [7:1.3] 26(2) | Demonetisation
  [7:1.4] 30 | Centre can supersede the Central Board
  [7:1.5] 47 | Net profit after provisions → Central Government (surplus transfer)
[7:2] - **UPSC 2021:** Governor appointed by the Centre ✓; directions power is in the **RBI Act**, not "provisions in the Constitution" ✗; Governor draws power from the RBI Act ✓ → **1 and 3 only**.
[7:3] - **Finance Commission** is formed by the President under **Article 280** (not an RBI body).
[7:4] # Income and surplus
[7:5] - Income: **interest on GoI securities and profit from buying/selling Government bonds (OMO)**, **interest and gains on foreign currency assets (buying/selling forex)**, interest on loans to Governments and banks, fees/commission.
[7:6] - **Not income:** printing and distributing notes (a **cost** – currency printing is an expense), pension fund management (PFRDA), lending to private companies, income tax → **UPSC 2025: 1 and 2 only** (official key).
[7:7] - **Bimal Jalan Committee** (Economic Capital Framework, 2018-19): separated **realised equity from revaluation balances**; **Contingent Risk Buffer 5.5–6.5%** → revised **4.5–7.5%** (2025).
[7:8] - Surplus **FY 2025-26: record ₹2.86 lakh crore**, 6.7% above FY25.
[7:9] # Developmental role
[7:10] - RBI helped set up **IDBI, NABARD, SIDBI, NHB** (ownership later moved to the Government; RBI sold its NABARD stake to GoI). **EXIM Bank** (Jan 1982) was **established by the Government of India** under the EXIM Bank Act, 1981.
[7:11] - SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA are separate regulators, not RBI creations.
