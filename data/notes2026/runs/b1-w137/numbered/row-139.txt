## SUB 0 | Later Mughals – Order, Reigns and Epithets
[0:0] TABLE head: Ruler | Reign | Key facts
  [0:0.0] Bahadur Shah I (Muazzam; Shah Alam I) | 1707-12 | Succeeded Aurangzeb (1707); released Shahu (1707); called "Shah-i-Bekhabar"
  [0:0.1] Jahandar Shah | 1712-13 | Wazir Zulfiqar Khan; mistress Lal Kunwar; "Lampat" (the profligate); defeated by his nephew Farrukhsiyar (with the Sayyids)
  [0:0.2] Farrukhsiyar | 1713-19 | Puppet of the Sayyid brothers; Banda Bahadur executed (1716); 1717 farman to the English; blinded and killed by the Sayyids
  [0:0.3] Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela' (Roshan Akhtar) | 1719-48 | Longest later-Mughal reign (~29 yrs); Sayyids removed (1720); Nadir Shah at Karnal, 1739; Abdali's first raid 1748
  [0:0.4] Ahmad Shah | 1748-54 | Rule left to Udham Bai (queen mother) and the eunuch Javed Khan – "eunuchs and ladies"; blinded and deposed by Imad-ul-Mulk (1754)

  [0:0.6] Shah Alam II (Ali Gauhar) | 1759-1806 | Panipat III (1761), Buxar (1764); first Mughal pensioner of the Company; blinded by Ghulam Qadir Rohilla (1788); English took Delhi 1803

  [0:0.8] Bahadur Shah II (Zafar) | 1837-57 | Son of Akbar II; last Mughal emperor; exiled to Rangoon after 1857 (died 1862)
[0:1] - **Order (APPSC G1, UPPSC 2018, UPRO 2023):** **Bahadur Shah I → Jahandar Shah → Farrukhsiyar → Muhammad Shah → Ahmad Shah → Alamgir II → Shah Alam II**.
[0:2] - **Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela':** wine, beauty, animal fights, apathy to administration; **last emperor to sit on the Peacock Throne** (Takht-i-Taus / Mayur Singhasan) – Nadir Shah carried it off in 1739.
[0:3] - **Eunuchs and ladies dominated the court under Ahmad Shah (1748-54)** – Udham Bai with the eunuch Javed Khan (PMF, Spectrum); some guides wrongly print Muhammad Shah.
[0:4] - **Shah Alam II's exile:** **not allowed into Delhi by Wazir Ghaziuddin (Imad-ul-Mulk)**; stayed away for years out of fear of his wazir and court intrigue (UPSC 2003 A-R: A and R true, R not the explanation); lived at **Allahabad** under Company protection after Buxar; returned to Delhi **1771-72** with **Mahadji Scindia**.
[0:5] - **Shrunken empire:** by Zafar's time Mughal authority ran only round Delhi to **Palam** – "**Chandni Chowk (Lal Qila) to Palam**"; the saying "Sultanat-e-Shah Alam, az Dilli ta Palam" was first made of Shah Alam II.
[0:6] - Not matched: Ahmad Shah = 1748-**54** (not 1757); exiled to Rangoon = **Bahadur Shah II**, not Muhammad Shah; Sayyids dominated **Farrukhsiyar**, not Jahandar Shah.

## SUB 1 | Asaf Jahi Hyderabad – Founding and the Nizams
[1:0] - **Founder:** **Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan Siddiqi = Chin Qilich Khan = Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I** – first Nizam; founded the **Asaf Jahi dynasty (Asafzahi clan)** and **Hyderabad State in 1724** (APPSC G1 2003, G2 1993, 2002, 2011, DL 2020).
[1:1] - **1724:** defeated the Mughal governor **Mubariz Khan** at **Shakar Kheda** → virtually independent. **Died 1748** (APPSC G2 1993, MPDO 1997).
[1:2] - Background: Hyderabad first envisaged by Deccan viceroy **Zulfiqar Khan** (through **Daud Khan**, from 1708); Farrukhsiyar made him **Subedar of the Deccan**; he helped **overthrow the Sayyids**; **Wazir** (1722-24) before leaving for the Deccan.
[1:3] - Tolerant: **Puran Chand** as Diwan; Mughal-type jagirdari administration; **kept the Marathas out** in his lifetime (but lost at Palkhed 1728).
[1:4] TABLE head: Title | Conferred by
  [1:4.0] Chin Qilich Khan | Aurangzeb
  [1:4.1] Nizam-ul-Mulk | Farrukhsiyar (1713)
  [1:4.2] Asaf Jah | Muhammad Shah (1725)
[1:5] - **CGPSC 2022 trap:** Kilich Khan was **not** Mubariz Khan (Mubariz was the man he defeated); the title Asaf Jah was **not** given by Zulfiqar Khan.
[1:6] TABLE head: Nizam | Rule / fact
  [1:6.0] Asaf Jah I (Nizam-ul-Mulk) | 1724-48
  [1:6.1] Nasir Jung, Muzaffar Jung, Salabat Jung | 1748-62 – not counted among the seven Nizams (never received the title Asaf Jah)
  [1:6.2] Nizam Ali Khan (Asaf Jah II) | 1762-1803; first Indian ruler to accept Wellesley's Subsidiary Alliance (1798)
  [1:6.3] Mir Mahbub Ali Khan | 1869-1911
  [1:6.4] Mir Osman Ali Khan | 1911-48; seventh and last Nizam; founded Osmania University
[1:7] - **Hyderabad city ≠ Hyderabad State:** the city was founded in **1591 by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah**; the **State** (Asaf Jahi dominion) by Asaf Jah I in 1724 (APPSC 2022: only statement I correct).
[1:8] - **Operation Polo (September 1948)** integrated Hyderabad State into the Indian Union.

## SUB 2 | Other Successor States – Awadh, Bengal, Carnatic, Rohilkhand, Rajputs, Jats
[2:0] TABLE head: State | Founder | Mughal post / fact
  [2:0.0] Awadh | Saadat Khan Burhan-ul-Mulk | Subedar of Awadh 1722; first Nawab of Oudh; independent under Muhammad Shah
  [2:0.1] Bengal | Murshid Quli Khan | Diwan (1700), then Nazim; started revenue farming (ijaradari)
  [2:0.2] Carnatic (Arcot) | Saadatullah Khan | Nawab under the Nizam; made nephew Dost Ali heir without the Nizam's approval
  [2:0.3] Rohilkhand | Ali Muhammad Khan | Afghan; carved it out during Nadir Shah's invasion
  [2:0.4] Farrukhabad | Muhammad Khan Bangash | Afghan adventurer
  [2:0.5] Jaipur (Amber) | Sawai Jai Singh II (1699-1743) | Founded Jaipur; astronomer
[2:1] - **Awadh:** Saadat Khan **joined the plot against the Sayyids** (siding with Muhammad Shah) – both statements true (UPRO 2023). **Safdar Jung** (nephew and son-in-law) = **Nawab of Awadh and Mughal Wazir** (from 1748) – hence the "**Nawab Wazir**" style; **Shuja-ud-Daula** was later wazir too, sided with Abdali at Panipat and lost at Buxar.
[2:2] - **First Nawab (Wazir) of Awadh = Saadat Khan** (CDS 2018 key); the wazirship itself began with Safdar Jung.
[2:3] - **Turanis** established Hyderabad; **Iranis** established Oudh. **Saadat Khan = Awadh; Saadatullah Khan = Carnatic**.
[2:4] - **Match (APPSC G1):** Husain Ali Khan – a Sayyid brother; Murshid Quli Khan – Bengal; Chin Qilich Khan – Nizam of Hyderabad; Safdar Jung – Awadh.
[2:5] - **Pairs (AEE):** Hyderabad – Nizam-ul-Mulk ✓; Bengal – **not** Saadat Ali Khan; Awadh – **not** Murshid Quli Khan.
[2:6] - **Sawai Jai Singh II:** **observatories (Jantar Mantar) at Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Varanasi and Mathura**; drew up the astronomical tables **Zij-i-Muhammad Shahi** (named for Muhammad Shah); law against lavish wedding expenses.
[2:7] - **Jats (Bharatpur):** Churaman, Badan Singh; **Suraj Mal** (1755-63) = "**Plato of the Jat tribe**", built **Lohagarh** fort.
[2:8] - Titles in options: Saadat Khan = **Burhan-ul-Mulk**; **Sarvalokeshvara = Vima Kadphises** (Kushana), not Saadat Khan.

## SUB 5 | Causes and Historiography of Decline
[5:0] - **Greatest extent under Aurangzeb:** Kashmir to **Jinji**, Hindukush to Chittagong; long **Deccan wars** (last ~27 years) drained the treasury → foundations weakened after him.
[5:1] - Causes: weak successors and noble factions (Iranis, Turanis, Hindustanis); **jagirdari and agrarian crises**; Maratha rise; **invasions of Nadir Shah and Abdali**; European companies. Power gap filled by **regional states → Marathas → East India Company**.
[5:2] TABLE head: Historian | Work | View
  [5:2.0] Jadunath Sarkar | History of Aurangzeb; Fall of the Mughal Empire | Aurangzeb's religious policy; weak successors
  [5:2.1] Irfan Habib | The Agrarian System of Mughal India (1963) | Agrarian crisis – the state "itself the principal instrument of exploitation" (CDS 2017)
  [5:2.2] Satish Chandra | Parties and Politics at the Mughal Court | Jagirdari crisis – too few jagirs for too many nobles
  [5:2.3] M. Athar Ali | The Mughal Nobility under Aurangzeb | Enlarged nobility (Deccanis, Marathas) worsened the jagir shortage
  [5:2.4] W.H. Moreland | From Akbar to Aurangzeb | Economic exploitation and stagnation
  [5:2.5] François Bernier | Travels in the Mogul Empire | No private property in land
[5:3] - **Revisionist view:** the 18th century = **regional growth**, not collapse; successor states had their own economy and law-and-order and controlled the peasant surplus (NextGen).
[5:4] - **Marathas = strongest native power** after the Mughals, but they had **no concept of a united Indian nation** (A true, R false).
[5:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 05 p.14 (books: Sarkar, Habib), p.35 (18th-century states); PMF Modern History 01 p.48 (Habib, Satish Chandra); PMF Modern History 01 p.66 (Maratha limits); old app notes 1:139 sub 7-8
