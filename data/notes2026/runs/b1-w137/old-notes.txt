=== NOTES 1:137 | Mughal Architecture – Fatehpur Sikri, Humayun's Tomb, Taj Mahal, Red Fort; Garden and Tomb Traditions
## SUB 0 | Fatehpur Sikri – Akbar's Capital
[137:0:0] - **Fatehpur Sikri** ("City of Victory") – Akbar's planned capital **1571–85**, about 37–40 km from Agra, at Sikri where the Chishti saint **Shaikh Salim Chishti** lived (title **Shaikh-ul-Hind**; Prince **Salim** named after him); mainly **red sandstone**; UNESCO 1986. The **finest monuments of Akbar** are here (RAS 1994) – not in the Agra, Lahore or Allahabad forts.
[137:0:1] TABLE head: Building | Exam fact
  [137:0:1.0] Buland Darwaza | "Gate of Magnificence", ~41 m; built by Akbar (c. 1575) to commemorate the victory over Gujarat (1572–73) – not Jahangir's birth (A true, R false – UP 1998, UPPCS 2025); southern entrance to the Jama Masjid; red and buff sandstone with marble inlay
  [137:0:1.1] Jama Masjid | Congregational mosque, among the first buildings of the city; called 'Shan-i-Fatehpur' (UKPCS 2016)
  [137:0:1.2] Tomb of Salim Chishti | White-marble tomb in the Jama Masjid courtyard (built under Akbar)
  [137:0:1.3] Panch Mahal | Five-storeyed, entirely columnar pavilion in the style of a Buddhist monastery / vihara (Jharkhand 2003; RPSC 2023); ground floor 84 pillars; Persian badgir (wind-catcher) – the Fatehpur Sikri building in lists with Moti / Rang / Hira Mahal (UPPCS 2004; CDS 2003)
  [137:0:1.4] Jodha Bai's palace | Largest palace; Hindu trabeate + Islamic arcuate; Gujarati and Rajasthani patterns
  [137:0:1.5] Maryam's palace | Gilded paintings – the Golden Palace (Sunehra Makan)
  [137:0:1.6] Diwan-i-Khas | Private audience; single central carved pillar
  [137:0:1.7] Ibadat Khana | House of Worship (1575) – debates of scholars of different religions
  [137:0:1.8] Others | Diwan-i-Aam, Birbal ki Kothi, Turkish Sultana's house, Anup Talao, Hiran Minar
[137:0:2] - **Not at Fatehpur Sikri:** **Akbari Mahal** – it is in **Agra Fort** (UKPCS 2012).
[137:0:3] - **Built in Akbar's reign (APPSC G1):** Tomb of Salim Chishti, Buland Darwaza, Jami Masjid (Fatehpur Sikri) **and** Humayun's Tomb (1565–72) – **all four**.
[137:0:4] - **UPSC 2018:** white marble was **not** the material of the Buland Darwaza and the Khanqah (red/buff sandstone), and the Bara Imambara and Rumi Darwaza of Lucknow are **brick and lime plaster**, not red sandstone and marble → **neither** statement correct.
[137:0:5] - Style: Persian, Indian, Central Asian; introduced the **Tudor (four-centred) arch**, continued at **Jahangir Mahal, Orchha**.

## SUB 2 | Tomb Tradition – Lodi to Bibi ka Maqbara
[137:2:0] TABLE head: Stage | Tomb | Feature
  [137:2:0.0] Lodi | Sikandar Lodi's tomb – built by son Ibrahim Lodi | First double dome
  [137:2:0.1] Sur | Sher Shah's tomb, Sasaram – he built it himself; set in a tank; finished under Islam Shah (c. 1545) | Transition Lodi → Mughal
  [137:2:0.2] Akbar | Humayun's tomb, Delhi (1565–72) | First garden tomb; first full Persian double dome; red sandstone with white marble; Persian + Indian synthesis (RAS 2003)
  [137:2:0.3] Jahangir | Itimad-ud-Daula, Agra (1622–28) – built by Nur Jahan for her father Mirza Ghiyas Beg ("Baby Taj") | First tomb wholly of white marble; first pietra dura
  [137:2:0.4] Shah Jahan | Taj Mahal | Climax
  [137:2:0.5] Aurangzeb | Bibi ka Maqbara, Aurangabad – tomb of Rabia-ud-Daurani (Dilras Banu), "Taj of the Deccan", "Second Taj" | Last major imitation of the garden tomb
[137:2:1] - **Humayun's tomb:** built by his widow **Haji Begum (Bega Begum)** – the woman who built her **emperor husband's** mausoleum (UPPSC 2004) – not Mumtaz Mahal or Nur Jahan; architects **Mirak Mirza Ghiyas** and son **Sayyid Muhammad**.
[137:2:2] - **Bibi ka Maqbara:** built in **Aurangzeb's** reign (keyed "Aurangzeb", RAS 1999) by his son **Azam Shah**; architect **Ata-ullah**, son of Ustad Ahmad Lahori.
[137:2:3] - **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan's tomb** – **Delhi**, near Humayun's tomb (UPPSC 2010).
[137:2:4] - **Chronology (UPPSC 2019):** Atala Mosque, Jaunpur (**1408**) → Sher Shah's tomb, Sasaram (c. 1545) → Humayun's tomb (1565–72) → Bibi ka Maqbara (c. 1660) = **IV, II, III, I**.
[137:2:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Indian History 02 p.15, p.20 (tombs table: Haji Begum, Itmad-ud-Daula 1622–1628, Azam Shah); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 05 p.15 (Humayun's Tomb – Haji Begum); PMF Art & Culture 04 p.3-4, p.8 (double dome, Itmad-ud-Daula first white-marble building, Atala Masjid); app notes 1:137 (sub 2)
[137:2:6] - **Tomb builders (statements):** Sikandar Lodi's tomb – by his son Ibrahim Lodi; Humayun's – under Haji Begum; **Akbar's mausoleum – begun by Akbar, completed by Jahangir**; Sher Shah's – built for himself.

## SUB 6 | Mughal Style and Features
[137:6:0] - Mughal style = **Indo-Islamic + Persian (Timurid) grandeur + Hindu** grace: bulbous domes, slender minarets, vaulted gateways, **charbagh**, **double dome**, **jali**, calligraphy.
[137:6:1] - **Akbar** built in **red sandstone**; **Shah Jahan** in **white marble** – his reign is the **"Golden Age"** of Mughal architecture.
[137:6:2] TABLE head: Feature | Meaning | First / best seen
  [137:6:2.0] Pietra dura | Decorating walls with floral designs of inlaid semi-precious stones | Began under Jahangir; first at Itimad-ud-Daula; peak at the Taj
  [137:6:2.1] Double dome | Inner and outer shells | Sikandar Lodi's tomb; first full Mughal one – Humayun's tomb
  [137:6:2.2] Charbagh | Four-quartered garden | Introduced by Babur (Ram Bagh)
  [137:6:2.3] Jali | Lattice work | Humayun's tomb
  [137:6:2.4] Sheesh Mahal | Mirror-work chambers | Agra Fort, Red Fort
[137:6:3] - Contrast: the **Bahmani Jama Masjid, Gulbarga** has a **fully covered courtyard**, unlike Mughal open-court mosques (and not Charminar).
[137:6:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Art & Culture 04 p.3 (Mughal style); NextGen Indian History - APPSC 04 p.17-18 (pietra dura from Jahangir); app notes 1:137 (sub 0, 7)

## SUB 4 | Mughal Painting – Emperor-wise Evolution, Zenith and European Influence
[138:4:0] # Emperor → emphasis
[138:4:1] TABLE head: Emperor | Emphasis
  [138:4:1.0] Babur, Humayun | Humayun laid the foundation: brought the Persian masters Mir Sayyid Ali (of Tabriz) and Khwaja Abdus Samad from Persia (met at Tabriz/Kabul) – they formed the nucleus of the Mughal school, fusing Persian (Islamic) and Indian (non-Islamic) elements
  [138:4:1.1] Akbar | Separate painting department / atelier Tasvir Khana of 100+ painters under Abdus Samad; painting karkhanas at Agra, Fatehpur Sikri, Lahore; frescoes at Fatehpur Sikri; mostly illustrated manuscripts (Hamzanama, Tutinama, Akbarnama, Razmnama) and portraits; 3-D figures, foreshortening; Persian style became Indianised
  [138:4:1.2] Jahangir | Zenith / golden age of Mughal painting; shift from illustrated manuscripts to albums and individual portraits; events of his own life, birds, flowers, animals (naturalism); his own private atelier, a keen judge who could name the artist of a painting; decorated margins; halo (nimbus) behind the emperor's head; patronised European and Persian art
  [138:4:1.3] Shah Jahan | More interested in architecture; formal court scenes, albums (muraqqas), floral borders, more gold and silver, artificial stillness; painters Mir Hashim, Anup, Chitra
  [138:4:1.4] Aurangzeb | Opposed pictorial art, withdrew patronage → painters moved to provincial courts (Awadh, Hyderabad, Mysore, Bengal), Rajasthan, the Punjab Hills and Bihar → fed the Rajput and Pahari schools
[138:4:2] - **Zenith under Jahangir** (UPSC 1996; State PSC; UKPSC Lower 2010) – not Akbar (built the atelier) or Shah Jahan (architecture). **Jahangir mainly patronised painting** (UPPCS 2016).
[138:4:3] - **Shift from illustrated manuscripts to albums and individual portraits = Jahangir** (UPSC 2019).
[138:4:4] - Subjects of Mughal painting: **battle scenes, animals-birds-nature, court scenes – all of the above** (UPPSC 1991).
[138:4:5] - NDA 2012: **Akbar's paintings were mostly book illustrations and portraits** (I) and **Mir Sayyid Ali + Abdus Samad formed the nucleus of the school fusing non-Islamic and Islamic elements** (II) – **both correct**.
[138:4:6] - **Foundation laid by Humayun** (UPPSC 2012), not Akbar.
[138:4:7] # European influence
[138:4:8] - European pictures reached **Akbar's court through the Portuguese (Jesuit priests)** → **portraiture** and **smaller distant objects for spatial depth**; Mughal style = muted naturalistic colour, perspective and shading. So **European painting was introduced in Akbar's court** (BPSC 2019) – not Jahangir's, and **not by Danish merchants**.
[138:4:9] - **First Jesuit mission from Goa** reached Akbar in **1580**: Father **Rudolf Acquaviva** with Father **Antonio Monserrate** and Brother Francis Henriques; they brought the **Polyglot Bible**, whose pictures Akbar showed to his painters.

## SUB 1 | Asaf Jahi Hyderabad – Founding and the Nizams
[139:1:0] - **Founder:** **Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan Siddiqi = Chin Qilich Khan = Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I** – first Nizam; founded the **Asaf Jahi dynasty (Asafzahi clan)** and **Hyderabad State in 1724** (APPSC G1 2003, G2 1993, 2002, 2011, DL 2020).
[139:1:1] - **1724:** defeated the Mughal governor **Mubariz Khan** at **Shakar Kheda** → virtually independent. **Died 1748** (APPSC G2 1993, MPDO 1997).
[139:1:2] - Background: Hyderabad first envisaged by Deccan viceroy **Zulfiqar Khan** (through **Daud Khan**, from 1708); Farrukhsiyar made him **Subedar of the Deccan**; he helped **overthrow the Sayyids**; **Wazir** (1722-24) before leaving for the Deccan.

[141:0:0] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [141:0:0.0] Date | 14 January 1761 (Panipat I = 1526, II = 1556)
  [141:0:0.1] Opponents | Marathas vs Ahmad Shah Abdali (Afghans) – not Mughals, Rajputs, Sikhs, English or Rohillas alone (APPSC G1 2019)
  [141:0:0.2] Maratha commander | Sadashiv Rao Bhau (overall); Vishwas Rao (Peshwa's son, nominal) – both killed

  [141:0:0.4] Peshwa | Balaji Baji Rao – died soon after (June 1761)

[141:0:2] - **Causes of defeat (CDS 2002):** **Bhau's tactical errors**, **Abdali's superior generalship**, **large numbers of non-combatants** and baggage; no northern allies (Jats, Rajputs alienated); supply collapse – treachery of Gardi is **not** a cause.

[141:0:4] - **Result:** Maratha northern ambition halted; vacuum helped the Company; "it decided not who was to rule India, but who was not"; recovery under **Madhav Rao I** within a decade.

## SUB 2 | Battles with the Nizam and the Three Anglo-Maratha Wars
[141:2:0] TABLE head: Battle / treaty | Year | Result
  [141:2:0.0] Palkhed → Treaty of Mungi-Shevgaon | 1728 | Baji Rao I beat Nizam-ul-Mulk; chauth and sardeshmukhi of the Deccan conceded
  [141:2:0.1] Bhopal → Doraha Sarai | 1737-38 | Nizam ceded Malwa
  [141:2:0.2] Udgir / Rakshasbhuvan | 1760 / 1763 | Nizam defeated
  [141:2:0.3] Kharda | 1795 | Nizam defeated; blamed Sir John Shore's neutrality → Subsidiary Alliance (1798)
[141:2:1] - **First war (1775-82):** after **Narayan Rao's murder**; **Treaty of Surat (1775)** – Bombay to back **Raghunath Rao** with 2,500 troops; **Treaty of Purandar (1776)** – **Marathas and the English** (Calcutta council) annulled Surat; **Convention of Wadgaon (1779)** rejected by **Warren Hastings**; **Treaty of Salbai (1782)** with **Mahadji Scindia** – status quo.
[141:2:2] - **Raghunath Rao (Raghoba)** = uncle of Madhav Rao I and Narayan Rao; father of Baji Rao II.
[141:2:3] - **Road to the second war:** **Nana Phadnavis died (1800)** → **Holkar and Scindia fought over the weak Peshwa** (Holkar won at Poona, 1802) → Baji Rao II fled to the British.
[141:2:4] - **Second war (1803-05):** **Treaty of Bassein (1802)** – Baji Rao II accepted Wellesley's Subsidiary Alliance; **Assaye** (Arthur Wellesley vs Scindia-Bhonsle), **Laswari** (Lord Lake vs Scindia); **Deogaon** (Bhonsle: Cuttack, Balasore), **Surji-Anjangaon** (Scindia).
[141:2:5] - **Third war (1817-18):** Governor-General **Lord Hastings**; Khadki, Koregaon, Sitabuldi, Mahidpur; **Peshwaship abolished in 1818** under **Baji Rao II**, pensioned to **Bithur near Kanpur**; small Satara state kept.
[141:2:6] - **Chronology (UPPCS 2024):** Nana Phadnavis's death (**1800**) → recall of Wellesley (**1805**) → **Vellore Mutiny (10 July 1806)** → Anglo-Nepalese war (**1814-16**).
