[0:2] - **Earth's twin:** **Venus** – close to the Earth in **size, mass and density** (APPSC G1; asked 4 times).
[0:3] - **Hottest = Venus, not Mercury** (thick CO₂ atmosphere traps heat although Mercury is nearer the Sun); **Saturn is warmer than Neptune** (nearer the Sun).
[0:4] - **Nearest planet to Earth:** traditional key **Venus** (closest approach 41.4 million km), then **Mars** (78.4 million km) when Venus is not offered; on a time-averaged basis Mercury is closest.
[0:5] - **Brightness:** Venus is the brightest planet, but the **Moon** is brighter than Venus, so "Venus is the brightest object after the Sun" is **false** (CDS 2009 A-R: A false, R true).
[0:6] - **Rings:** **Saturn** (famous), but **Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune also have rings** – "Saturn is the only ringed planet" is false.
[0:7] - **Atmospheres:** Jupiter, Saturn = mostly **hydrogen and helium**; Uranus, Neptune = H, He + **methane** (blue-green tint; "ice giants"); **Mars = carbon dioxide** (95%), so "H, He and methane on Mars" is wrong.
[0:8] - **Mars water:** a **subsurface lake** of liquid water under the **south polar ice cap** was reported in *Science* on **25–26 July 2018** (MARSIS radar, Mars Express).
[0:9] - **Pluto** (dwarf planet since 2006) has the longest year of all (**247.9 years**); it was farther in than **Neptune** from **1979 to 1999**, when Neptune was the farthest.

[1:2] - **Order from the Sun:** Mercury → Venus → **Earth** → Mars → **Jupiter → Saturn** → Uranus → Neptune (Pluto, dwarf, beyond).
[1:3]   - Between Mars and Uranus: **Jupiter and Saturn** (UPSC 2008); Earth lies between **Venus and Mars**; between the Sun and Earth: **Mercury and Venus**.
[1:4] - **Size (decreasing):** **Jupiter > Saturn > Uranus > Neptune > Earth > Venus > Mars > Mercury**; Venus is the only listed planet just **smaller than Earth**.
[1:5] - **Number of planets:** **8** since the IAU decision of **August 2006** (Pluto dropped); **9** before 2006.
[1:6] - **Period rises with distance:** a smaller orbit gives a **shorter year** (if the Sun–Earth distance fell by a quarter, the year would be shorter). Tropical year: **365 d 6 h 9 min 9.51 s**.
[1:7] # Classification
[1:8] - **Inner / terrestrial planets:** **Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars** – rocky, dense, solid surface; lie **between the Sun and the asteroid belt**.
[1:9] - **Outer / Jovian planets:** **Jupiter, Saturn** (**gas giants**), **Uranus, Neptune** (**ice giants**) – beyond the asteroid belt, thick H-He atmospheres.
[1:10] - **Planets are non-luminous** – they **shine by reflected sunlight**; stars twinkle, planets do not. The Sun's gravity gives the **centripetal force** that keeps planets in orbit.

## SUB 2 | Distance Units, Light Travel and the Universe
[2:0] # Units of astronomical distance
[2:1] TABLE head: Unit | Meaning | Value
  [2:1.0] Astronomical unit (AU) | Mean Earth–Sun distance | ~149.6 million km (149.598 × 10⁶ km)
  [2:1.1] Light year | Distance light travels in one year | 9.461 × 10¹² km (~9.46 trillion km)
  [2:1.2] Parsec | Largest of the three | 3.26 light years
[2:2] - **Light year = distance, not time** (asked 9 times); it is the unit for **distances of stars**. Sun–Earth = **1 AU**.
[2:3] - **Why light years:** the **speed of light is constant** (~3 × 10⁵ km/s) (UPSC 2021).
[2:4] - **"4.3 light years away"** (Alpha Centauri) = its light takes **4.3 years** to reach Earth.
[2:5] - **Sunlight reaches Earth in 8 min 19 s** (~**500 seconds**, "about 8 minutes").
[2:6] - **Order of size:** AU < light year < parsec. **Not** distance units: mile/fathom are not used for stars; "longsec" is not a unit at all.
[2:7] - **Cepheids** (stars that brighten and dim) are used to measure astronomical distances.
[2:8] # The universe
[2:9] - **Cosmology** = study of the origin, evolution and structure of the **universe**.
[2:10] - **Age of the universe: 13.8 billion years** (APPSC 2019). **Age of the Earth: ~4.54 billion years** (~4.6 billion for the solar system).
[2:11] - **Most abundant element** in the universe and in the Sun: **hydrogen**, then **helium** (Sun ~74% H, ~24% He).
[2:12] - **Milky Way** = **barred spiral galaxy**; the Sun lies in the **Orion Arm**, ~26,000 light years from the centre, and takes **~225–250 million years (~25 crore years)** to go once round the galactic centre (a "galactic year").
[2:13] - **Constellations:** **88** recognised by the **IAU** (founded 1919, Paris).
[2:14] - News terms **event horizon, singularity, string theory, Standard Model** belong to the observation and understanding of the **universe** (UPSC 2017).

## SUB 3 | Stars – Nearest Star, Colour, Brightness, Twinkling and Constellations
[3:0] # Nearest stars
[3:1] - **Nearest star to Earth: the Sun**; next: **Proxima Centauri** (4.2 ly), part of the **Alpha Centauri** system (4.37 ly).
[3:2] - **Sirius** (Dog Star, in Canis Major) = **brightest star** of the night sky; found by following the line of **Orion's belt** – as the Pole Star is found with Ursa Major.
[3:3] # Colour, brightness, twinkling
[3:4] - **Colour of a star = its surface temperature** (asked 3 times): **blue** (hottest, 50,000–28,000 K) → white → **yellow** (Sun, 6,000–4,900 K) → orange → **red** (coolest).
[3:5]   - Decreasing temperature: **Blue, White, Yellow, Orange, Red**.
[3:6] - **Apparent brightness** depends on a star's **size, temperature and distance from the Earth**.
[3:7] - **Magnitude:** lower number = brighter (logarithmic); **absolute magnitude** = brightness at **10 parsecs**.
[3:8] - **Twinkling:** starlight passing through the atmosphere is refracted by air of **fluctuating density / temperature** (changing **refractive index**); stars twinkle because they are **very distant** point sources; planets do not.
[3:9] - **In space** (no atmosphere): the **sky is black** and **stars do not twinkle**.
[3:10] - Hubble's first direct image of the surface of another star: **Betelgeuse** (red supergiant in Orion).
[3:11] # Constellations and the Pole Star
[3:12] - **Constellation** = a pattern of stars in the sky; its stars are **not equidistant** from Earth – they only look grouped.
[3:13] - **Saptarishi** (Big Dipper, part of **Ursa Major**) points to the **Pole Star** and so gives the direction of the pole.
[3:14] - **Polaris** (**Ursa Minor**) lies within 1° of the north celestial pole → always shows **north**; walking **east**, keep the Pole Star on your **left**. **Vega** will be pole star in ~**12,000 years**.
[3:15] - **Nebulae** (gas and dust clouds where stars form) are **not members of the solar system**; asteroids, comets and planets are.

## SUB 4 | Stars – Life Cycle, Black Holes and the Chandrasekhar Limit
[4:0] # Life cycle
[4:1] - **Nebula** → **protostar** → **main sequence**; then low/medium mass (Sun): **red giant** → planetary nebula → **white dwarf**; massive: **supergiant** → **supernova** → **neutron star** or **black hole**.
[4:2] - **Supernova** = the explosive death of a star (**a dying star**).
[4:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:3.0] Nebulae | Giant clouds of dust and gas
  [4:3.1] Cepheids | Stars that brighten and dim periodically
  [4:3.2] Pulsars | Spinning neutron stars formed when massive stars run out of fuel and collapse
  [4:3.3] Black hole | Remains of a collapsed massive star; no light escapes
[4:4] - UPSC 2023 pairs: Cepheids–"clouds of dust" ✗, Nebulae–"brighten and dim" ✗, Pulsars ✓ → **only one** correct.
[4:5] # Black holes
[4:6] - **Black hole:** gravity so strong (very **high density**) that **not even light escapes** → it **cannot be seen by a telescope** (A-R: both true, R explains A).
[4:7] - A black hole is a **collapsed star**, a celestial phenomenon of stars; Pulsar, Black Hole, Quasar are in space – **Brittle Star** is a sea animal (echinoderm).
[4:8] # Chandrasekhar
[4:9] - **S. Chandrasekhar** gave black-hole/white-dwarf theory (paper to the Royal Astronomical Society, London, **11 January 1935**; Nobel 1983).
[4:10] - **Chandrasekhar limit ≈ 1.44 solar masses**: stars below it end as **white dwarfs**; above it the core suffers **internal collapse** (neutron star or black hole).

## SUB 5 | The Sun – Composition, Layers and Activity
[5:0] - **Chief body of the solar system** and the **primary source of energy for the Earth**; **99.8%** of the solar system's mass; diameter **109 × Earth's**.
[5:1] - **Composition:** mostly **hydrogen** (~74%), then helium; energy from **nuclear fusion** of H into He in the core (**~15 million °C**).
[5:2] - **State:** the interior is **plasma (gas)** – the centre is in the **gaseous state only**, not liquid or solid.
[5:3] - **Light of the Sun is white** (all colours together); it looks yellow from Earth because of scattering.
[5:4] # Layers (centre outward)
[5:5] TABLE head: Layer | Key fact
  [5:5.0] Core | Fusion H → He; ~15 million °C
  [5:5.1] Photosphere | Bright visible outer surface emitting most radiation; ~5,500–6,000°C (5,778 K)
  [5:5.2] Chromosphere | Reddish lower atmosphere (~4,300°C), seen at eclipses
  [5:5.3] Corona | Outermost; visible only during a total solar eclipse; over 1 million °C
[5:6] # Solar activity
[5:7] TABLE head: Phenomenon | Fact
  [5:7.0] Sunspots | Cooler dark magnetic patches on the photosphere; the interval between two maxima (~11 years) = cycle of solar activity (solar cycle)
  [5:7.1] Solar flares | Bursts from magnetic reconnection; classes A, B, C, M, X (X strongest)
  [5:7.2] CMEs | Coronal plasma (not rock), 250–3,000 km/s, reach Earth in 1–3 days
  [5:7.3] Aurora | Borealis (north), Australis (south)

## SUB 6 | Origin of the Universe and the Solar System – Theories
[6:0] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent | Year
  [6:0.0] Geocentric (Earth at the centre) | Ptolemy | 2nd c. AD
  [6:0.1] Heliocentric | Copernicus | 1543
  [6:0.2] Nebular hypothesis | Immanuel Kant; revised by Laplace | 1755; 1796
  [6:0.3] Planetesimal hypothesis | Chamberlin and Moulton | 1904
  [6:0.4] Tidal hypothesis | James Jeans and Harold Jeffreys | –
  [6:0.5] Nova hypothesis | Hoyle and Lyttleton | –
  [6:0.6] Interstellar dust (gas and dust) | Otto Schmidt | 1943
  [6:0.7] Big Bang (expanding universe) | Georges Lemaitre | 1927

[6:1] - **Earth from gases and dust particles = O. Schmidt** (asked 4 times: APPSC G2 2008, IAS 1999); not Jeans (tidal) or Hoyle (nova).
[6:2] - **Big Bang** explains the **origin of the universe** (not continental drift, the Himalayas or volcanoes).
[6:3] - **Evidence for Big Bang / expansion:** **red-shift** of galaxies (**Hubble, 1929**) = **expansion of the universe**, and the **cosmic microwave background** → UPSC 2012 answer "**1 and 2**" (asteroids and supernovae are not evidence).
[6:4] - Lemaitre proposed the Big Bang (1927); Hubble only showed expansion (1929). Steady State = **Gold and Bondi** (with Hoyle).
[6:5] - **Present view:** planets formed from the same spinning disc; the hot inner disc kept only metals and rocky minerals, ices stayed in the cool outer disc (giant planets).

## SUB 9 | Earth, Moon and Eclipses
[9:0] # Earth's unique features
[9:1] - **Goldilocks (habitable) zone:** "just right" for liquid water; Earth lies in it (APPSC 2019). Earth-like **K2-18b**: possible biomarker molecule.
[9:2] TABLE head: Sphere | Evolution
  [9:2.0] Lithosphere | Differentiation: heavy iron sank to the core, light material formed the crust
  [9:2.1] Atmosphere | H-He lost to the solar wind → degassing (water vapour, N₂, CO₂) → photosynthesis added oxygen
  [9:2.2] Hydrosphere | Degassed vapour condensed → rain → oceans
  [9:2.3] Magnetic field | Geodynamo of the liquid-iron outer core; 2026 study: some reversals lasted ~70,000 years
[9:3] # Earth's orbit
[9:4] TABLE head: Basis | Perihelion | Aphelion
  [9:4.0] Date | ~3 January | ~4 July
  [9:4.1] Distance | ~147 million km | ~152 million km
  [9:4.2] Speed | Fastest | Slowest
  [9:4.3] N. Hemisphere | Winter | Summer
[9:5] - **Kepler's Second Law** (equal areas in equal times) explains the speed change. Seasons come from **revolution + axial tilt**, not distance.
[9:6] # The Moon
[9:7] - Mean distance **384,400 km**; gravity **1/6** of Earth's; rotation = revolution = **27 d 7 h 43 min** (same face); synodic (lunar) month **29.5 days**.
[9:8] - **Supermoon:** full moon when the Moon is **nearest the Earth (perigee)**, with Sun, Earth and Moon in line → bigger, brighter, higher tides. **Micromoon:** full moon at **apogee**, 6–7% smaller.
[9:9] - **Blue moon:** 2nd full moon in a calendar month; **blue micromoon** in 2026. Half moon: angle **90°**.
[9:10] TABLE head: Basis | Solar eclipse | Lunar eclipse
  [9:10.0] Position | Moon between Sun and Earth | Earth between Sun and Moon (Earth's shadow on the Moon)
  [9:10.1] Phase | New moon (Amavasya) | Full moon
  [9:10.2] Feature | Corona, diamond ring | Reddish "blood moon"
[9:11] - Longest total solar eclipse of the 21st century: **22 July 2009**; longest annular eclipse of the millennium: **15 January 2010**.
[9:12] - **Apollo 11** (20 July 1969): Armstrong and Aldrin walked, **Collins** orbited. **Blue Marble** photo: Apollo 17.
