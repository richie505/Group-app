=== NOTES 4:0 | The Universe and the Solar System – Origin Theories, the Sun, Planets, Asteroids, Comets, Meteors
## SUB 0 | Universe – Basics, Ages and Distance Units
[0:0:0] - **Cosmology** = study of the origin, evolution and structure of the universe.
[0:0:1] - Age of the universe: **13.8 billion years** (APPSC 2019). Age of the Earth and solar system: **4.6 billion years**.
[0:0:2] - Most abundant element in the universe and the Sun: **hydrogen**, then **helium** (Sun ~74% H, ~24% He).
[0:0:3] - **Milky Way** = **barred spiral galaxy** (disc, bulge, dark-matter halo).
[0:0:4] - The Sun's gravity gives the **centripetal force** keeping planets in orbit. Planets are **non-luminous** (reflect sunlight); stars twinkle, planets do not.
[0:0:5] # Distance Units
[0:0:6] TABLE head: Unit | Meaning | Value
  [0:0:6.0] Astronomical unit (AU) | Mean Earth–Sun distance | ~149.6 million km (149.598 × 10⁶ km)
  [0:0:6.1] Light year | Distance light travels in one year | 9.461 × 10¹² km (~9.46 trillion km)
  [0:0:6.2] Parsec | Largest unit | 3.26 light years
[0:0:7] - Sunlight takes **8 min 19 s** to reach Earth. Light years are used because the **speed of light is constant**.
[0:0:8] Order of size: AU < light year < parsec. A light year measures distance, not time.
## SUB 1 | Origin Theories
[0:1:0] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent | Year
  [0:1:0.0] Nebular hypothesis | Immanuel Kant; revised by Laplace | 1755; 1796
  [0:1:0.1] Planetesimal hypothesis | Chamberlin and Moulton | 1904
  [0:1:0.2] Tidal hypothesis | James Jeans and Harold Jeffreys | –
  [0:1:0.3] Nova hypothesis | Hoyle and Lyttleton | –
  [0:1:0.4] Interstellar dust | Otto Schmidt | –
  [0:1:0.5] Big Bang (expanding universe) | Georges Lemaitre (1894–1966) | 1927
  [0:1:0.6] Steady State | Hoyle, Bondi and Gold | 1948
[0:1:1] - Big Bang proof: **red-shift** (**Hubble, 1929**) and **microwave background** radiation.
[0:1:2] - Planets formed from one spinning disc: hot inner disc kept only metals and rocky minerals; ices stayed in the cool outer disc (giant planets).
[0:1:3] Lemaitre proposed the Big Bang (1927); Hubble only showed expansion (1929). Steady State = Gold and Bondi (with Hoyle).

## SUB 3 | Stars – Life Cycle and Terms
[0:3:0] # Life Cycle
[0:3:1] - **Nebula** → **protostar** → **main sequence**; then low/medium mass (Sun): **red giant** → planetary nebula → **white dwarf**; massive: **supergiant** → **supernova** → **neutron star** or **black hole**.
[0:3:2] - **Chandrasekhar limit** (~1.44 solar masses) = highest white-dwarf mass; above it the star collapses.
[0:3:3] # Terms
[0:3:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:3:4.0] Nebulae | Giant clouds of dust and gas
  [0:3:4.1] Cepheids | Brighten and dim; period–luminosity relation measures distance
  [0:3:4.2] Pulsar | Spinning neutron star from a collapsed massive star
  [0:3:4.3] Black hole | Remains of a large star's supernova; no light escapes (high density)
  [0:3:4.4] Magnitude | Lower number = brighter (logarithmic); absolute magnitude = brightness at 10 parsecs
[0:3:5] - **Colour = surface temperature**: **blue** (hottest, 50,000–28,000 K) → white → **yellow** (Sun, 6,000–4,900 K) → orange → **red** (coolest).
[0:3:6] - **Polaris** (**Ursa Minor**) is within 1° of the north celestial pole; **Vega** becomes pole star in ~**12,000 years**.
[0:3:7] - Nearest star: the **Sun**; next: **Alpha Centauri** system.
[0:3:8] - First direct image of another star's surface (Hubble): **Betelgeuse**. In space the sky is **black** and stars **do not twinkle**.
[0:3:9] Brittle Star is an echinoderm, not a space object.
## SUB 4 | The Sun
[0:4:0] - **99.8%** of the solar system's mass; diameter **109 × Earth's**; plasma core (an old key said "liquid").
[0:4:1] - Energy: **nuclear fusion** of hydrogen into helium in the core (**~15 million °C**). Mean distance 1 AU.
[0:4:2] # Layers (centre outward)
[0:4:3] TABLE head: Layer | Key fact
  [0:4:3.0] Core | Fusion H → He; ~15 million °C
  [0:4:3.1] Photosphere | Visible surface above convection zone; ~5,500–6,000°C (5,778 K)
  [0:4:3.2] Chromosphere | Reddish lower atmosphere, seen at eclipses
  [0:4:3.3] Corona | Seen in total eclipse; over 1 million °C
[0:4:4] # Solar Activity
[0:4:5] TABLE head: Phenomenon | Fact
  [0:4:5.0] Sunspots | Cooler dark magnetic patches; ~11-year cycle; poles reverse at peak
  [0:4:5.1] Solar flares | Bursts from magnetic reconnection; classes A, B, C, M, X (X strongest)
  [0:4:5.2] CMEs | Coronal plasma (not rock), 250–3,000 km/s, reach Earth in 1–3 days
  [0:4:5.3] Aurora | Borealis (north), Australis (south)
[0:4:6] - Effects: geomagnetic storms, satellite and grid damage.

[0:5:1] - Between Mars and Uranus: **Jupiter and Saturn**.

## SUB 7 | Earth, Moon and Orbit Terms
[0:7:0] # Earth's Unique Features
[0:7:1] - **Goldilocks (habitable) zone**: "just right" for liquid water; Earth lies in it (APPSC 2019).
[0:7:2] - Earth-like **K2-18b**: possible biomarker molecule.
[0:7:3] TABLE head: Sphere | Evolution
  [0:7:3.0] Lithosphere | Differentiation: heavy iron sank to core, light material formed crust
  [0:7:3.1] Atmosphere | H-He lost to solar wind → degassing (water vapour, N₂, CO₂) → photosynthesis added oxygen
  [0:7:3.2] Hydrosphere | Degassed vapour condensed → rain → oceans
  [0:7:3.3] Magnetic field | Geodynamo of liquid-iron outer core; shields from solar wind
[0:7:4] - 2026 study: some pole reversals lasted ~**70,000 years**, not 10,000.
[0:7:5] # Perihelion vs Aphelion
[0:7:6] TABLE head: Basis | Perihelion | Aphelion
  [0:7:6.0] Date | ~3 January | ~4 July
  [0:7:6.1] Distance | ~147 million km | ~152 million km
  [0:7:6.2] Speed | Fastest | Slowest
  [0:7:6.3] N. Hemisphere | Winter | Summer
[0:7:7] - **Kepler's Second Law** (equal areas in equal times). Seasons come from **tilt**, not distance.
[0:7:8] # The Moon
[0:7:9] - Mean distance 384,400 km; gravity **1/6** of Earth (weight falls to 1/6).
[0:7:10] - Rotation = revolution = **27 d 7 h 43 min** (tidal locking) → same face (⚠ APPSC key (2002) gave 'rotates opposite to the Earth'). Synodic month **29.5 days**.
[0:7:11] - **Perigee** (closest): supermoon, higher tides (bright full moon of 22 Dec 1999). **Apogee**: **micromoon**, 6–7% smaller, 10–14% dimmer.
[0:7:12] - **Blue moon**: 2nd full moon in a month (every 2–3 years); **blue micromoon** in 2026 after 5+ years. Half moon: angle **90°**.
[0:7:13] TABLE head: Basis | Solar eclipse | Lunar eclipse
  [0:7:13.0] Position | Moon between Sun and Earth | Earth between Sun and Moon
  [0:7:13.1] Phase | New moon (Amavasya) | Full moon
  [0:7:13.2] Feature | Corona, diamond ring (start/end of totality) | Reddish moon (blood moon, July 2018)
[0:7:14] - Totality is limited: the Moon's shadow is smaller than Earth. Longest annular eclipse of the millennium: **15 January 2010** (keyed 17 Jan).
[0:7:15] - **Longest total solar eclipse (21st century):** **22 July 2009**; totality up to ~6 min 39 s, seen across India, Nepal, Bangladesh, Bhutan, China.
[0:7:16] - **Apollo 11**: 20 July 1969; Armstrong and Aldrin walked, Collins orbited. **Blue Marble**: Apollo 17.
[0:7:17] -helion = Sun (Earth's orbit); -gee = Earth (Moon's orbit).
## SUB 8 | Asteroids, Comets, Meteors and Dwarf Planets
[0:8:0] TABLE head: Basis | Asteroids | Comets
  [0:8:0.0] Made of | Rock and metal | Ice, frozen gases, dust, rock
  [0:8:0.1] Where | Mars–Jupiter; largest Ceres (~1,000 km) | Kuiper Belt, Oort Cloud
  [0:8:0.2] Orbit | Less eccentric | Highly elliptical
  [0:8:0.3] Tail | Usually none | Coma and tail, points away from the Sun (radiation pressure + solar wind)
[0:8:1] - **Kuiper Belt** (30–50 AU, beyond Neptune) → short-period comets; **Oort Cloud** → long-period comets.
[0:8:2] TABLE head: Comet | Fact
  [0:8:2.0] Halley's | Short-period, ~76 yr; last 1986, next 2061
  [0:8:2.1] Hale-Bopp | Long-period, ~2,534 yr; Alan Hale and Thomas Bopp
  [0:8:2.2] Shoemaker-Levy 9 | Found 24 Mar 1993; hit Jupiter 16–22 July 1994
  [0:8:2.3] 3I/ATLAS | Third interstellar object; NASA found water; JWST found methane (a first)
[0:8:3] - **Meteoroid** = body in space; **meteor** = shooting star; **meteorite** = reaches ground; **bolide** = explodes in air.
[0:8:4] # Dwarf Planets
[0:8:5] - **IAU 26th General Assembly (14–25 Aug 2006)**: planet must orbit the Sun, be nearly round, and **clear its orbit**. Pluto fails → dwarf planet; trigger **Eris**.
[0:8:6] - Dwarf planets: **Ceres** (asteroid belt); **Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, Eris** (Kuiper belt). Pluto: Indian name **Yama**; 2,376 km.
[0:8:7] Eris is a dwarf planet, not a moon of Jupiter. Smallest planet: Pluto (old) vs Mercury (now).
[0:8:8] - **Leonid meteor shower:** debris of **Comet Tempel-Tuttle** (returns ~every 33 years); meteors appear to come from the constellation **Leo**; peaks around **17–18 November**.
