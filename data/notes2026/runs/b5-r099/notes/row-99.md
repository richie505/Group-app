=== Carbon Monoxide and Other Pollutants – Health Effects
# Carbon Monoxide (CO) – the most-asked pollutant
- **Source:** **incomplete combustion** of carbon fuels – **vehicle exhaust** (main pollutant gas in exhaust), **cigarette smoke**, coal/wood burning. CO2 comes from **complete** combustion.
- **Nature:** **colourless, odourless**, poisonous; "odourless poisonous gas in coal smoke" = CO.
- **Effect:** binds **haemoglobin** → **carboxyhaemoglobin**; affinity **200–250 times** that of oxygen → cuts the blood's **oxygen-carrying capacity**; can affect the **bloodstream leading to death** → usually called the "**most dangerous**" air pollutant.
- **Not** converted to CO2 in the body, does **not** destroy haemoglobin's structure, not mainly a digestive/liver/kidney poison (UPSC 2010, MPPSC 2012).
- "Forms an **irreversible** complex with haemoglobin" (UPPCS 1996) – books answer **CO**; strictly the bond is reversible but very stable (CO is released slowly).
- Flashcard: CO breathing is harmful because **it competes with oxygen in the blood**.
| Pollutant | Main source | Part / effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbon monoxide** | Incomplete combustion, vehicles, cigarettes | **Blood stream** – carboxyhaemoglobin, hypoxia, death |
| **Asbestos dust** | Asbestos mining, cement sheets | **Lung** – asbestosis |
| **Lead** | Leaded petrol (old), smelters, batteries, paints | **Brain** / nervous system; blood pressure, kidney |
| **Mercury** | Coal burning, industry | **Stomach** (UPPSC 2012 match key) / nervous system |
| **SO2** | Coal/oil burning, power plants, smelting | Eye, throat and lung irritation, asthma; acid rain |
| **NOx** | High-temperature combustion – vehicles, power plants, **lightning** | Eye/airway irritation; smog, acid rain |
| **H2S** | **Decaying vegetation and animal matter** (chief), sewage, refineries | Rotten-egg smell; toxic |
| **Fly ash** | **Burning coal** in thermal power plants | Lung disease; captured by ESP |
| **PM2.5 / PM10** | Combustion, road and construction dust, biomass | Heart/lung disease; PM2.5 enters blood, crosses placenta |
| **Ground-level O3** | Secondary (photochemical) | **Asthma**, chest pain, crop damage |
- **UPPSC 2012 match:** Asbestos – Lung, Lead – Brain, Mercury – Stomach, CO – Blood stream → **3 1 2 4**.
- **Lead** from **petrol** combustion (anti-knock **tetraethyl lead, TEL**) – the metal polluting city air with many vehicles; leaded petrol is **phased out in India (2000)**.
- **Most injurious exhaust components:** **CO and lead** (old leaded-petrol era).
- **Cigarette smoke:** **CO and benzene** (also nicotine, HCN, arsenic, formaldehyde).
- **Inorganic gaseous pollutants:** HCN, H2S, CO, CO2, SO2, NOx (methane and phosgene are carbon compounds usually grouped as organic).
- **Asthma** is caused / aggravated by **air pollution**; **rickets** is **not** caused by smog directly (smog cuts UV → less vitamin D; rickets is a deficiency disease).
- **Bio-gas / gobar gas** = mainly **methane** (50–65%) + CO2 (35–50%), traces of H2, N2, H2S.
- Gases: **ozone = O3**; **CFCs** deplete the stratospheric ozone layer.
- Other links: **ammonia** (fertilisers + livestock, 77–80%) drives secondary particles; **ammonium sulphate** (from SO2 + NH3) ≈ one-third of India's PM2.5; **magnetite** particles (combustion, brake wear) found in brain tissue – possible Alzheimer's link; **cadmium** from metal smelting, PAHs from incomplete burning; PM2.5 crosses the placenta (AIIMS, 3 June 2026).
- Burden: WHO ~**7 million** deaths a year; fossil-fuel air pollution ~**1.72 million** premature deaths in India (2022); NCR winter PM2.5 ~35 times the WHO limit; IIT Delhi: curbing power-plant SO2 could prevent ~1.24 lakh deaths a year.
> Exam angle: ≈20 PYQs ask the same fact – CO + haemoglobin. Pick CO whenever the stem says "blood", "oxygen-carrying", "colourless gas from exhaust and cigarettes", "incomplete combustion" or "most dangerous".

=== Sources of Air Pollution – Natural, Vehicles, Industry
| Basis | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Natural** | **Forest fires**, volcanic eruptions (SOx, ash), dust storms, natural decay (methane, H2S), lightning (NOx), pollen, spores |
| **Anthropogenic** | **Combustion of coal, petrol, diesel** (basic source of **air** pollution), vehicles, industries, thermal plants, fireworks |
| Point / line / area / diffuse | Chimneys of industries / automobile exhaust along roads / industrial estates, mining / sprayed fertilisers and pesticides |
- **Main source in Indian cities: automobile (vehicular) emissions** (APPSC G2 Mains 2025). Main pollutants of metros (MPPSC 2018 key): **CO and SO2**.
- **Composition of air:** N2 78%, O2 21%, CO2 0.04% – **N2, O2, helium are not pollutants**; CO, NO2, SO2, ground-level O3, lead dust, smoke are.
- **CO2** is the usual "**not an air pollutant**" answer (greenhouse gas, quantitative pollutant; not in NAQI).
- **Vehicles:** CO, NOx, unburnt hydrocarbons, PM, (old) lead. **Diesel engine:** much **CO at low temperature** (incomplete burning), much **NOx at high temperature**.
- **Steel industry:** oxides of **sulphur, nitrogen, CO and CO2 – all four** (UPSC 2014).
- **Largest SO2 source (US EPA, UPSC 2024): power plants using fossil fuels** (~73%), then other industry; not ships, locomotives or ore extraction.
- **SO2 + fossil fuel:** SO2 is the air-pollutant gas released by burning fossil fuel (H2, N2, O2 are not).
- **Delhi PM (IIT Kanpur study, 2016):** **road dust** (incl. construction dust) is the largest PM2.5/PM10 source annually (~38% PM2.5); in **winter** secondary particles, vehicles and biomass burning lead.
- **Delhi winter peak:** A true, but "vehicle combustion increases in winter" is false – winter peaks come from **temperature inversion, calm winds, stubble smoke**.
- **Stubble burning in Punjab** (and Haryana) contributes more to Delhi's air than Rajasthan or firewood use.
- **Transport and technology:** more travel → more pollution; transport releases lead, NOx, CO (ozone forms from them – secondary); burning fuel harms health.
- Blackened vessel bottom while cooking = **fuel not burning completely**.
- **Sita Ashok** tree = prevention of dust pollution (UPPCS 2007).
- **Eutrophication** = water pollution, not air (smog, acid rain, asbestosis are air-related).
- **PM2.5 main source in India** (printed key): **vehicular emissions**.
- **Silver oxide** is not a major primary air pollutant (SOx, CO, NOx are).
- NCR: **transport** is the largest primary source, ~**23%** of the winter load (CAQM synthesis report, Jan 2026).
- Fuels: **coal** most polluting; diesel gives more PM than petrol; CNG/LPG still release **CO2**; **LPG** burns without smoke; biogas mainly CO2 + traces of H2S; **hydrogen** gives only water (no CO, SO2, PM); wood and cow-dung give smoke, CO, PM.
> Exam angle: "natural source" = forest fires/volcanoes; "urban source" = vehicles; "steel / crop residue releases which" = all of them.

=== Acid Rain – Causes, Chemistry, pH and Effects
- **Definition:** precipitation (rain, snow, fog, mist) with **pH below 5.6**; normal rain ≈ 5.6 (carbonic acid from CO2). Term coined **1872** by **Robert Angus Smith** (Manchester coal burning).
- **pH scale** = **acidity / basicity**; 0–14; **7 neutral**, below 7 acidic; each unit = **tenfold** change. **Human blood ≈ 7.4** (slightly basic).
- **Causing gases: SO2 and NOx** (oxides of **sulphur and nitrogen**) – **not** CO, CO2, O3, CH4, CFCs, H2S, HCl or "oxides of hydrogen". Main single factor: **SO2**.
- **Ozone** only speeds up the oxidation (photo-oxidant) – it is not a cause (UPSC 2022: **2 and 4 only**).
- **Chemistry:** SO2 / NOx + water + O2 + photo-oxidants → **H2SO4 (~two-thirds)** + **HNO3 (~one-third)** → both **sulphuric and nitric acid** are components; acid falls from atmosphere to the ground.
- Acid rain occurs due to the **reaction between clouds (water) and sulphur dioxide** (UPPCS 2001).
| Gas | Anthropogenic | Natural |
|---|---|---|
| SOx | Coal power plants, smelting of sulphide ores, H2SO4 plants, refineries, **industries** | Volcanoes, oceans, decay |
| NOx | Vehicles, power plants, biomass burning | Lightning, forest fires |
- **Deposition:** wet (rain, snow, fog) and **dry** – about **half** of atmospheric acidity returns dry.
- **Effects:** acidifies lakes (fish die, aluminium released), damages forests and soils (leaches Ca, Mg), corrodes limestone/marble (**Taj Mahal "marble cancer"** – SO2, Mathura refinery), harms health (**respiratory ailments**).
- **Taj Trapezium Zone** ~10,400 sq km (Agra, Fatehpur Sikri, Agra Fort; not Sanchi); **M.C. Mehta v. UoI (1996)** barred coal/coke.
- **Transboundary:** Scandinavia receives acid rain from UK/central Europe; **India = largest SO2 emitter (2022)**.
- **Control:** low-sulphur fuel, **FGD scrubbers** (trap SO2), low-NOx burners, catalytic converters; **liming** = remedial (not preventive).
| Instrument | Year | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| LRTAP (UNECE) | 1979 | First binding treaty on transboundary air pollution |
| Helsinki Protocol | 1985 | 30% sulphur cut |
| Sofia Protocol | 1988 | Freeze NOx |
| Gothenburg Protocol | 1999 | SO2, NOx, NH3, VOCs |
> Exam angle: ~17 PYQs; the answer is almost always "SO2 and NOx" / "pH below 5.6" / "sulphuric and nitric acid".

=== AQI, NAAQS, WHO Guidelines and Monitoring Networks
# National Air Quality Index (NAQI)
- Launched **April 2015** under **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan** (MoEFCC/CPCB; developed with IIT Kanpur); first for **10 cities**; motto "**One Number – One Colour – One Description**".
- **8 pollutants** (up to 24-hour averaging): **PM10, PM2.5, NO2, SO2, CO, O3, NH3, Pb**. **Not included: CO2, methane, benzene**. UPSC 2016: of CO2, CO, NO2, SO2, methane → **CO, NO2, SO2 (2, 3 and 4 only)**.
| AQI | Category | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| 0–50 | Good | Dark green |
| 51–**100** | **Satisfactory** | Light green |
| 101–200 | Moderately polluted | **Yellow** |
| 201–300 | Poor | Orange |
| 301–400 | Very poor | Red |
| 401–500 | Severe | Maroon |
# NAAQS (CPCB, 2009)
- **12 pollutants**: the 8 above + **benzene, benzo(a)pyrene, arsenic, nickel**; **ozone** added for the first time in 2009.
| Pollutant | NAAQS annual | NAAQS 24-hour | WHO AQG 2021 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PM2.5** | **40 µg/m³** | 60 µg/m³ | **5 annual; 15 (24-hr)** |
| PM10 | 60 µg/m³ | 100 µg/m³ | 15 annual; 45 (24-hr) |
| SO2 | 50 (20 in eco-sensitive areas) | 80 µg/m³ | 40 (24-hr) |
| NO2 | 40 µg/m³ | 80 µg/m³ | 10 annual |
- **WHO AQG 2021 (UPSC 2022 → 1 and 4 only):** ozone peaks in **sunny** weather, not inclement weather; **PM2.5** (not PM10) crosses the lung barrier into blood; **excess ozone triggers asthma**.
- **RSPM:** CPCB treats RSPM as **PM10 (< 10 µm)**; old books/UPPCS 2017 key: "**less than 5 µm**".
# Monitoring networks
| Network | Feature |
|---|---|
| **NAMP** (CPCB, 1984) | Regular monitoring at all locations of **SO2, NO2, RSPM/PM10 and PM2.5** – **not CO2** |
| CAAQMS | Real-time stations; data via **SAMEER** app (CPCB) |
| **SAFAR** | MoES/**IITM Pune**; new SAFAR system for Delhi unveiled **2018 by Dr Harsh Vardhan**; app **SAFAR-Air** (city air quality, first 4 cities) |
| **AUM photonic system** | Indigenous (GVP-SIRC, Visakhapatnam; DST) real-time remote monitoring of **air quality** |
| WMO **Global Atmosphere Watch** | Global stations in Algeria (Assekrem), Kenya (Mt Kenya), Brazil …; **India has no GAW global station** (printed key) |
| Precipitation chemistry | **IMD**, under WMO GAW / BAPMoN |
- NAMP size: **1,646 stations in 603 cities** (March 2026).
> Exam angle: "how many pollutants" = 8 (NAQI) / 12 (NAAQS); CO2 and methane are never in the index.

=== Smog, Primary and Secondary Pollutants
| Type | Formed from | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| **Classical / London smog** (sulphurous, 1952) | **Smoke + fog + SO2** | Cold, humid; grey/black |
| **Photochemical / Los Angeles / summer smog** | **NO2/NOx + hydrocarbons (VOCs) + sunlight** → **ozone, PAN** (oxidising agents) | Warm, sunny, traffic; **brownish – "brown air"** |
- **Smog** = smoke + fog; essentially caused by **oxides of nitrogen and sulphur**; photochemical smog **always contains ozone**; ozone is **produced** during its formation (hydrocarbons, NOx are inputs, not products).
- TSPSC 2023 pairs: classical smog = smoke, fog, SO2 ✔; photochemical smog = high concentration of **oxidising agents** ✔ → **both**.
- **Primary** pollutants (emitted directly): CO, CO2, SO2, NOx, fly ash, lead. **Secondary** (formed in air): **O3, PAN, smog**, ammonium sulphate → "**not a primary pollutant**" = **ozone**; "secondary pollutant" = **smog**.
- **Tropospheric ozone precursors:** NOx, CO, methane/VOCs – **water vapour is not** one.
- Stratospheric ozone is **not** an air pollutant (shields UV); ground-level ozone is.
- Smog effects: breathing problems, bronchitis; heavy smog cuts UV → less vitamin D (rickets rise) – but **rickets is not a smog disease** (SSC 2018).
- **Asian Brown Cloud (UNEP, 2002):** layer of soot, black carbon, sulphate aerosols over **South Asia** (Indo-Gangetic plain) and the Indian Ocean, **January–March** (dry season), from biomass and fossil-fuel burning.
> Exam angle: "brown air", "always contains", "produced during", "secondary" – answer ozone / photochemical smog.

=== National Clean Air Programme (NCAP), PRANA and Swachh Vayu Survekshan
- **Launched 10 January 2019** by **MoEFCC** – PAN-India **time-bound national strategy** for prevention, control and abatement of air pollution at **regional and urban scales**, plus augmenting the monitoring network; also the answer to "GoI initiative to control industrial emissions" (APPSC G2 Mains 2025).
- **Original target: 20–30% cut in PM10/PM2.5 by 2024**, base **2017** (so "by 2025" is wrong).
- **Revised (September 2022): up to 40% PM10 reduction (or meeting NAAQS) by 2025-26** (the year asked = **2026**).
- Coverage: **130 cities in 24 States/UTs** (non-attainment = failed NAAQS for five years; earlier 131/102); coordinated by **CPCB with SPCBs**; legal base **Air Act 1981**; City Action Plans; performance-linked grant ₹16,425 crore.
- **PRANA** = Portal for Regulation of Air-pollution in Non-Attainment cities – **monitors NCAP implementation**; launched **7 September 2021**.
- **Highest % PM10 cut, 2017-18 → 2023-24: Varanasi (230 → 73 µg/m³, 68%)**, then Bareilly (61%), Firozabad (59%) (MoEFCC/PIB).
- **Swachh Vayu Survekshan**: annual city ranking under NCAP since 2022-23; awards on **Swachh Vayu Diwas, 7 September** (5th SVS awards, 7 Sep 2026). Indore first among million-plus cities in SVS 2023 and 2025; Surat in SVS 2024.
- Results: 2024-25 vs 2017-18 – 103 cities cut PM10; only 22 of 130 met PM10 NAAQS. NGT (May 2026): 86% of spend went to road dust.
- Programme match: MoEFCC – NCAP; MoES – SAFAR; Jal Shakti – Namami Gange (NRCP, NLCP are water programmes).
> Exam angle: year = **2019**; revised target = **40% by 2026**; PRANA = monitoring NCAP.

=== Control Devices and Cleaner Fuels
| Device | Removes |
|---|---|
| **Electrostatic precipitator (ESP)** | **Particulate matter** (fly ash) from industrial smoke |
| Bag filter (baghouse) | Dust by fabric filtration |
| **Cyclone separator / collector** | Coarse particles by centrifugal force ("**cyclone divider**" is not a device) |
| Wet / venturi scrubber | Gases (SO2) and particles |
| FGD | Up to 95% SO2 from flue gas |
- **Catalytic converter:** **platinum, palladium, rhodium** catalysts turn CO → CO2, HC → CO2 + H2O, NOx → N2; needs **unleaded petrol**.
- **Fuel efficiency (IAS 2000 key):** radial tyres, multipoint fuel injection, catalytic converter → **1, 3 and 4**; strictly a catalytic converter cuts emissions, and a streamlined body cuts drag.
- Fuel injection promotes **complete combustion**.
- **CNG = mainly methane (CH4)**; cleaner because it gives very little SOx/NOx, less pollution and fewer GHGs – all three true.
- Fuels by pollution: coal > diesel > petrol > CNG/LPG; **hydrogen** cleanest (only water).
- Other measures: BS-VI nationwide 1 April 2020 (BS-V skipped), PUC, RFID pollution charge on Delhi-bound trucks, FAME-II, SATAT (compressed biogas), E20 ethanol blending, Methanol Economy (NITI Aayog), vehicle scrappage policy (2021), OCEMS for red-category industries in Delhi-NCR, ban on pet coke and furnace oil.
- BS-III from 1 April 2005 in NCR + 10 cities. **FGD** mandated 2015 for coal plants; only **39 of 537** had it when the mandate was relaxed (2025).
- **WAYU** (Wind Augmentation PurifYing Unit) – CSIR-NEERI with IIT Bombay; roadside purifier installed in **Delhi** (ITO, Mukarba Chowk) in September 2018.
> Exam angle: ESP = particulates; converter = Pt/Pd/Rh; CNG = methane.

=== Bioindicators and Indoor Air Pollution
- **Lichens** (symbiotic **algae + fungi**) are the best **bioindicators of air pollution**, especially **SO2** – they **do not grow in polluted areas** (so "lichens grow well in polluted areas" is false). **Mosses** indicate heavy-metal deposition.
- **Indoor pollution:** solid fuels (wood, cow-dung, coal), tobacco smoke, VOCs, radon, CO, mosquito coils; **maximum indoor chemical pollution: burning coal** (Jharkhand 2021); worst hit **women and children**; the four most serious pollutants in biofuel smoke: particulates, CO, polycyclic organic matter, formaldehyde.
- Biological indoor pollutants: pollen, mites, pet hair, fungi → allergies, asthma.
- Intercity buses: cabin air 2–3 times WHO limits (IntrCity study, 15 Mar 2026).
> Exam angle: "bioindicator / indicator of SO2 / do not grow in polluted area" = lichen.

=== Stubble Burning, CAQM, GRAP and Cloud Seeding
- **Crop / biomass residue burning** (October–November, Punjab, Haryana, UP) releases **CO, methane, ozone (secondary), SO2** – all four (UPSC 2019), plus NOx, CO2, black carbon, VOCs.
- **Pusa bio-decomposer** (IARI): a mix of **several fungi**; Delhi Govt sprays it **free of cost** so stubble turns into manure – both statements true.
- In-situ tools: Happy Seeder, Super SMS, balers; CRM machinery scheme (2018); co-firing biomass in thermal plants.
- **CAQM** – Ordinance 2020 → **CAQM Act 2021** (statutory); replaced EPCA; enforces **GRAP**; coal banned in Delhi-NCR from 1 January 2023.
| GRAP stage | AQI |
|---|---|
| I | Poor (201–300) |
| II | Very poor (301–400) |
| III | Severe (401–450) |
| IV | Severe+ (above 450) |
- **Cloud seeding:** silver iodide, potassium iodide, **dry ice (solid CO2)**, common salt. **IIT Kanpur test flight, June 2023:** project of IIT Kanpur; a **Cessna** (not IndiGo) aircraft flew from its flight laboratory → statements I and II true.
> Exam angle: crop burning → "all of these"; bio-decomposer → both statements.
