## SUB 0 | Raja Rammohan Roy (1772–1833) – Life and Titles
[188:0:0] - Life: **1772–1833**; knew **Persian, Arabic, Sanskrit and English**; influenced by **Vedanta + Western rationalism**.
[188:0:1] - Respected the traditional philosophies of the East (Upanishads, Vedanta) but wanted a **rational, scientific approach** (UPSC 2025: both statements correct).
[188:0:2] - Went to **England in 1830** as envoy of Mughal emperor **Akbar II** to plead for his pension – among the first Indians to go abroad on such a mission.
[188:0:3] - **Died at Bristol, 27 September 1833**; his **Samadhi is at Arnos Vale, Bristol**.
[188:0:4] - Offered membership of **La Societe Asiatique of Paris** (est. 1822).

  [188:0:6.0] Father of Indian Renaissance; Father of Bengal Renaissance; harbinger of the modern Indian Renaissance | Rammohan Roy

  [188:0:6.3] Maharishi | Debendranath Tagore (NOT Rammohan Roy)

## SUB 4 | Brahmo Samaj – Founding and Tenets
[188:4:0] # Founding
[188:4:1] - Founded as **Brahmo Sabha**, **20 August 1828**, **Calcutta**, by **Raja Rammohan Roy** with Dwarkanath Tagore; later renamed **Brahmo Samaj** (by **Debendranath Tagore**).
[188:4:2] - Founder = **Rammohan Roy** (APPSC GS 2018), NOT Vivekananda, Dayananda or Ramakrishna.
[188:4:3] - APPSC G1 2008 asked the year with options 1826/1829/1834/1836 – correct **1828** was not offered (⚠ APPSC key (2008) gave 1829).
[188:4:4] # Tenets (statement type)
[188:4:5] - **Monotheism**: one **formless, eternal God**; no human-form God; universal worship.
[188:4:6] - Drew on the rational teachings of the **Upanishads, Bible and Koran**.
[188:4:7] - **Banned** idols, animal sacrifice and sectarian symbols in its halls.
[188:4:8] - Rejected **priestly ritual** (no need for a priestly class) and **caste**.
[188:4:9] - **No scripture as final authority**; did **not** hold the Vedas faultless; **no belief in avatars**.
[188:4:10] - **Collective (group) prayer**.
[188:4:11] - A theistic (**Astika**) reform **within Hinduism**, not a new religion.
[188:4:12] # Social Programme
[188:4:13] - Against: child marriage, polygamy, female infanticide, caste.
[188:4:14] - For: widow remarriage, women's education and property rights.
[188:4:15] Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj | reformist | reason and modern ideas
Arya Samaj, Deoband | revivalist | return to tradition
## SUB 5 | Debendranath Tagore (1817–1905)
[188:5:0] - Title: **"Maharishi"**.
[188:5:1] - Founded the **Tattvabodhini Sabha** – **1839, Calcutta (Kolkata)** – to spread Rammohan Roy's ideas; organ **Tattvabodhini Patrika** (Bengali); merged into the Brahmo Samaj in **1859**.
[188:5:2] - **Joined the Brahmo Samaj in 1843**; revived it after Roy's death.
[188:5:3] - Made **Keshab Chandra Sen** acharya (1862), later took the title away (1865).
[188:5:4] - Founder of the **Adi Brahmo Samaj** (after the 1866 split).
[188:5:5] - Magazine **Brahman Sevadi** – defended Hinduism from Christian missionaries.
[188:5:6] - Father of **Rabindranath Tagore** (youngest son).
## SUB 6 | Keshab Chandra Sen (1838–1884)
[188:6:0] - Most influential Brahmo of the later years.
[188:6:1] - **1862**: made **acharya** by Debendranath – **first non-Brahmin acharya**.
[188:6:2] - Took the Brahmo Samaj to **South India**; his **Bombay lectures inspired the Prarthana Samaj**.

  [188:6:3.3] 1880–81 | Nava Vidhan (New Dispensation)
[188:6:4] **Nava Vidhan = Keshab**, not Debendranath.

## SUB 8 | Maharashtra Reform
[188:8:0] # Paramahansa Mandali
[188:8:1] - **Poona, 1849**; founder **Dadoba Pandurang**; fought **caste discrimination**, promoted **women's education** (APPSC GS 2023).
[188:8:2] - Religious reforms in Bombay were started by the Paramahansa Mandali.
[188:8:3] # Prarthana Samaj
[188:8:4] - **1867, Bombay**; founder **Atmaram Pandurang**; inspired by **Keshab Chandra Sen**.
[188:8:5] - Leaders: **M.G. Ranade, R.G. Bhandarkar**.
[188:8:6] - Worked for widow remarriage, women's education, **inter-caste dining** – by **persuasion**, not confrontation.
[188:8:7] # Ranade and the National Social Conference
[188:8:8] - Ranade: **Widow Marriage Association**, **Deccan Education Society**; **National Social Conference (1887)** with Raghunath Rao = **social wing of the Congress sessions**.

## SUB 9 | Young Bengal Movement
[188:9:0] - Leader: **Henry Louis Vivian Derozio**, teacher at **Hindu College** (joined 1826); his students = **Derozians / Young Bengal**.
[188:9:1] - **Academic Association (1828)**: debating club of Derozio and his students; read Voltaire, Hume, Locke and Tom Paine.
[188:9:2] - Ideas: free thought, rationalism, attack on orthodoxy.
[188:9:3] - Derozio was removed from Hindu College in **1831** and died of cholera in December 1831.

## SUB 1 | Young Bengal Movement
[189:1:0] - Leader: **Henry Louis Vivian Derozio**, an **Anglo-Indian** teacher at **Hindu College, Calcutta** (joined 1826).
[189:1:1] - Inspired by the **French Revolution**.
[189:1:2] - Followers: **"Derozians"**; forum = **Academic Association (1828)**.
[189:1:3] - Ideas: **radical rationalism**, iconoclastic; attacked old traditions, decadent customs, **idol worship and casteism**; strongly for **women's rights** and education.
[189:1:4] - **Dismissed from Hindu College in 1831**; died the same year.
[189:1:5] APPSC GS 2022: **Derozio → Young Bengal**.
## SUB 2 | Paramahansa Mandali, 1849
[189:2:0] - **Secret society** – not an open public organisation (the Arya Samaj was not secret).
[189:2:1] - Founded by **Dadoba Pandurang** with **Durgaram Mancharam** of the **Manav Dharma Sabha, Surat**.
[189:2:2] - Believed in **one God**.
[189:2:3] - Members **broke caste rules** by eating food cooked by lower castes.
[189:2:4] - Aims: against **caste discrimination**; for **women's education** and **widow remarriage**.
[189:2:5] - Region: **Maharashtra (Bombay)**, with branches at Poona and Satara.
[189:2:6] APPSC GS 2023: "Established in **Poona in 1849**, aimed to fight caste discrimination and promote women's education" → **Paramahansa Mandali**.
## SUB 3 | Prarthana Samaj, 1867
[189:3:0] # Basics
[189:3:1] - **Bombay (Maharashtra), 1867**; founder **Dr. Atmaram Pandurang**.
[189:3:2] - Inspiration: visit of **Keshab Chandra Sen**; an **offshoot of the Brahmo Samaj**, reform **within Hinduism**.
[189:3:3] - Leaders: **Justice M.G. Ranade** (chief leader in Maharashtra) and **R.G. Bhandarkar** (Sanskrit scholar; joined **1870**).
[189:3:4] # Four-Point Social Agenda
[189:3:5] - 1. **Disapproval of the caste system**.
[189:3:6] - 2. **Women's education**.
[189:3:7] - 3. **Widow remarriage**.
[189:3:8] - 4. **Raising the age of marriage** for men and women.
[189:3:9] - Also: inter-dining, inter-marriage, **uplift of depressed classes**.
[189:3:10] - Method: **persuasion and social work**, not confrontation.
[189:3:11] Unlike the Brahmos, the Prarthana Samaj did **NOT forbid idol worship** or abandon caste practices – its focus was social, not theological.

## SUB 3 | Arya Samaj, 1875 – Founding and Base

[190:3:2] - Name means **"Society of Nobles"**.
[190:3:3] - Strongest base: **Punjab**; **HQ later moved to Lahore**; also strong in Rajasthan and the United Provinces.
[190:3:4] - **Ten Principles** drafted by Dayananda – truth, rational thought, moral action, **welfare of all**.
[190:3:5] - The principles stress that God is the source of all true knowledge, that the **Vedas** are the books of true knowledge, that one must accept truth and give up untruth, and that the aim of the Samaj is to do good to the whole world – physical, spiritual and social.
[190:3:6] - Caste was to be decided by **merit and conduct**, not by birth.
[190:3:7] Founded in **Bombay**, NOT Calcutta; it **rejects** idol worship (never "promotes idol worship").

## SUB 5 | Shuddhi Movement
[190:5:0] - Started by: **Arya Samaj** (Dayananda) – NOT Brahmo Samaj, Satyashodhak Samaj or Ramakrishna Mission.
[190:5:1] - Aim: ritual **"purification"** to **reconvert** Hindus who had become **Muslims or Christians**, and admit outsiders into the Hindu fold.
[190:5:2] - Later leader: **Swami Shraddhanand** – 1920s campaign through the **Bharatiya Hindu Shuddhi Sabha (1923)**.
[190:5:3] - Shraddhanand gave a speech on **Hindu-Muslim unity** from the pulpit of **Jama Masjid, Delhi**, on **4 April 1919**; assassinated **1926**.
[190:5:4] - Method: a purification ritual after which the person was accepted back into Hindu society; it also opened the fold to outsiders.
[190:5:5] - Impact: contributed to the **communalisation** of social life and, later, to communal political consciousness.
