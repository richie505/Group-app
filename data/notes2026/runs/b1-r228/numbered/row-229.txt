## SUB 0 | Satyagraha – Meaning, Rules and Weapons
[0:0] - **Satya + agraha** = insistence on (holding to) truth; "**soul force**" / "truth force" in place of physical force.
[0:1] - Born **1906 in the Transvaal**; early English name **passive resistance** – but Gandhi distinguished them: passive resistance = weapon of the weak, **satyagraha = weapon of the strongest**.
[0:2] - Gandhi is best known for **using passive resistance (satyagraha) to win Indian independence** – not violence, not an Islamic or anti-Hindu agenda.
[0:3] - Aim is **not to defeat the enemy** but to **change his heart**; **suffering** is its driving force; a satyagrahi has **no ill feeling** towards the opponent and stays **firm in his belief**.
[0:4] - **Weapon of satyagraha = ahimsa**; two-fold method for conflicts = **non-violence and satyagraha**.
[0:5] - Rules: **never insult the opponent**; bear assaults **without retaliation**; **submit to arrest**; obey jail discipline.
[0:6] - Forms: non-cooperation, boycott, picketing, hartal, strike, civil disobedience, **fasting**.
[0:7] - **Fasting = the LAST weapon**: "Fasting unto death is the **last and the most potent weapon** in the armoury of Satyagraha" (Harijan, 18 Aug 1946); used only when all other avenues have failed.
[0:8] TABLE head: Struggle | Year | Form / "first"
  [0:8.0] Champaran | 1917 | First civil disobedience
  [0:8.1] Ahmedabad | 1918 | First hunger strike
  [0:8.2] Kheda | 1918 | First non-cooperation
  [0:8.3] Rowlatt Satyagraha | 1919 | Hartal; first all-India mass movement
[0:9] > The old notes' "first step fasting, last step strike" (a printed key) is wrong: in Gandhi's words fasting is the **last** resort.

## SUB 1 | Truth and Ahimsa
[1:0] - **Truth is the end; non-violence (ahimsa) is the means** – two sides of one coin → non-violence is **a way to attain truth** (not an end in itself).
[1:1] - Truth = reality; described as **existence, consciousness and bliss** (sat-chit-ananda).
[1:2] - **"God is Truth"** = earlier formulation; **"Truth is God"** = later.
[1:3] - Ahimsa in **thought, word and deed**; a quality of the **strong (brave)**, not the weak; between **cowardice and violence**, Gandhi would choose **violence**.
[1:4] - **Cruellest form of violence = persistence of poverty**.
[1:5] - **Family planning**: **self-control** (not sterilisation or the loop).
[1:6] - **2 October** (birth anniversary) = **International Day of Non-Violence** (UN General Assembly, 2007).
[1:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.123; Spectrum (Ahir) printed p.316; PMF Art & Culture Part 6 p.5; old app notes (truth formulations, poverty, self-control)
## SUB 2 | Means, Ends, Politics and Swaraj
[2:0] TABLE head: Quotation | Point
  [2:0.0] "Wrong means never take us to right ends" | Means-ends purity (Gandhi)
  [2:0.1] "Means are after all everything. As the means so the end" | Means decide the end
  [2:0.2] "What is morally wrong can never be politically right" | Ethics in politics
  [2:0.3] "Politics bereft of religion is a death-trap" | Religion = morality of truth and non-violence
  [2:0.4] "Real swaraj … the capacity of all to resist authority when abused" | Swaraj and authority
  [2:0.5] "Swaraj will not be a free gift of the British Parliament; it will be a declaration of India's full self-expression" | Gandhi (Young India, 1922)
  [2:0.6] Gandhi's Talisman | Recall the face of the poorest and weakest
[2:1] - Politics = **activity for public welfare**; built on **ethics, religion and humanity** – **no place for authority**.
[2:2] - Key suggestion: **decentralisation through village panchayats**.
[2:3] - **Swaraj** = not only political freedom: **self-rule, self-control** and the capacity of the masses to govern themselves; linked principles **swadeshi, ahimsa, satyagraha, decentralisation, education for all** – **class dictatorship** is not among them.

## SUB 3 | Gandhism Compared and Influences
[3:0] TABLE head: Point | Gandhism | Marxism
  [3:0.0] Final goal | Stateless society | Stateless society (the one common agreement – UPSC 2020)
  [3:0.1] Method | Non-violence, change of heart | Class struggle, revolution
  [3:0.2] Property | Trusteeship | Abolition of private property
  [3:0.3] Basis | Moral / spiritual | Economic determinism
[3:1] - **Philosophical anarchist**: "**enlightened anarchy**"; the State = **violence in concentrated form**.
[3:2] - Preamble **socialism** = blend of Marxism and Gandhism, with a **Gandhian tilt**.
[3:3] - Not Gandhian: **class dictatorship**; heavy industrialisation (Nehru).
[3:4] - Influences: **Jainism – Raychandbhai**; Tolstoy (The Kingdom of God is Within You); Ruskin (Unto This Last); **Thoreau (Civil Disobedience)**; **Sermon on the Mount** (essence of Christ's teaching for him).
