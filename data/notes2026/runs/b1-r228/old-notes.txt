=== NOTES 1:228 | Gandhi – Early Life, South African Phase, Making of Satyagraha; Hind Swaraj
## SUB 0 | Early Life and Education
[228:0:0] - Born **2 October 1869** at **Porbandar** (Gujarat); father **Karamchand Gandhi** (Diwan of Porbandar), mother **Putali Bai**.
[228:0:1] - Early education at **Rajkot**; married **Kasturba** at the age of 13.
[228:0:2] - Studied at **Samaldas College, Bhavnagar**, then law at the **Inner Temple, London (1888-1891)**; called to the Bar as a **barrister**.
[228:0:3] - Legal practice at **Rajkot** and **Bombay** did not succeed.
[228:0:4] - **1893**: went to South Africa for **Dada Abdullah's** law case.
[228:0:5] Gandhi studied law at the **Inner Temple**, not Lincoln's Inn or Middle Temple; his Indian college was Samaldas College, Bhavnagar.
## SUB 1 | South Africa (1893-1914) – the Laboratory of Satyagraha
[228:1:0] # Events in Order
[228:1:1] - Duration: **1893-1914** (about **21 years**).
[228:1:2] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [228:1:2.0] 7 June 1893 | Thrown out of a first-class compartment at Pietermaritzburg station (on the way to Pretoria for Dada Abdullah's case)
  [228:1:2.1] 1894 | Founded the Natal Indian Congress
  [228:1:2.2] Boer War | Served the wounded (ambulance work)
  [228:1:2.3] June 1903 | Indian Opinion first issue, Durban
  [228:1:2.4] 1903 | Law firm at Johannesburg; idea of Trusteeship linked to this year
  [228:1:2.5] 1904 | Read Ruskin's Unto This Last on a train → founded Phoenix Settlement near Durban
  [228:1:2.6] 1906 | First satyagraha: against the Asiatic Registration Ordinance ("Black Act"), Transvaal; stretcher-bearers in the Zulu Rebellion
  [228:1:2.7] 1907-08 | Burning of registration certificates; first jail term 1908
  [228:1:2.8] 1910 | Tolstoy Farm near Johannesburg, with Hermann Kallenbach
  [228:1:2.9] 1913 | Great March against the £3 poll tax and invalidation of non-Christian (Indian) marriages; miners and plantation (indentured) workers joined
  [228:1:2.10] 1914 | Indian Relief Act: poll tax abolished, Indian marriages recognised; Gandhi left for India
[228:1:3] # Seva Dharma and the Method
[228:1:4] - Adopted **'Seva Dharma'** in South Africa: worked as a **compounder** in **Rustomji's charity hospital** and served the Boer War wounded.
[228:1:5] - Best known there for **passive resistance**, which he renamed **Satyagraha**.
[228:1:6] - South Africa = the **'laboratory'** where satyagraha was invented (**1906**).
[228:1:7] - The South African struggles **prepared Gandhi to lead the national movement** (not the militancy of Indians there).
[228:1:8] First satyagraha = **Transvaal 1906** (Black Act); first satyagraha in India = **Champaran 1917**.

## SUB 3 | Influences – Book and Author
[228:3:0] TABLE head: Book / source | Author | What Gandhi took
  [228:3:0.0] Unto This Last | John Ruskin | Dignity of labour, Sarvodaya; "the good of the individual is contained in the good of all"
  [228:3:0.1] Civil Disobedience | H.D. Thoreau | Right to break unjust laws
  [228:3:0.2] The Kingdom of God is Within You | Leo Tolstoy | Non-violent resistance; Tolstoy Farm named after him
  [228:3:0.3] Bhagavad Gita | — | Nishkama karma (action without attachment to fruits)
  [228:3:0.4] Jainism | — | Five vows: ahimsa, satya, asteya, aparigraha, brahmacharya
[228:3:1] - Copy of Unto This Last was given by **Henry Polak** in **1904**; it influenced Gandhi most.
[228:3:2] - Gandhian thought was influenced by **Ruskin, Thoreau and Tolstoy** – "All the above".
[228:3:3] - Philosophical pillars: ahimsa, satya, asteya, aparigraha, sarvodaya.
[228:3:4] Jain vows: first **four from Parshvanatha**; **brahmacharya added by Mahavira**.
## SUB 4 | Hind Swaraj (1909)
[228:4:0] - Written in **1909**, in **Gujarati**, aboard the ship **Kildonan Castle** (London → South Africa).
[228:4:1] - Themes: **swaraj as self-rule**, self-reliance, rural life; **against industrialisation** and modern civilisation.
[228:4:2] - Called the British Parliament **"sterile and prostitute"**.
[228:4:3] - **Banned** by the **Bombay Government (1910)**.
[228:4:4] - **Translated into English by Gandhi himself** (to avoid the ban).
[228:4:5] Hind Swaraj: written 1909 in Gujarati; banned 1910; English version by Gandhi himself, not by a disciple.

## SUB 6 | Return to India
[228:6:0] - Landed at **Bombay** on **9 January 1915** → observed as **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas**.
[228:6:1] - **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**, his **political guru**, advised him to spend **one year studying India** before any movement.
[228:6:2] - Received the **Kaisar-i-Hind gold medal** in **1915** for ambulance work in South Africa.

=== NOTES 1:229 | Gandhian Philosophy – Truth, Ahimsa, Satyagraha, Means and Ends
## SUB 0 | Truth and Ahimsa
[229:0:0] - **Truth is the end; non-violence (ahimsa) is the means** – two sides of one coin, inseparable.
[229:0:1] - Truth = reality; described as **existence, consciousness and bliss**.
[229:0:2] - **"God is Truth"** = earlier form; **"Truth is God"** = later form (truth given the central place).
[229:0:3] - Ahimsa in **thought, word and deed**; a quality of the **strong (brave)**, not the weak.
[229:0:4] - Between **cowardice and violence**, Gandhi would advise **violence**.
[229:0:5] - **Family planning**: self-control is the best means.
[229:0:6] "God is Truth" came first; "Truth is God" later.
## SUB 1 | Satyagraha
[229:1:0] - **Satya + agraha** = insistence on truth; **"soul force"** in place of physical force.
[229:1:1] - Born **1906 in the Transvaal**; early English name **passive resistance** (not "weak" resistance).
[229:1:2] - Aim is **not to defeat the enemy**; **suffering** is its driving force.
[229:1:3] - **First step: fasting** ("Fire Arrow"); **last step: strike**.

  [229:1:4.0] Champaran | 1917 | First civil disobedience (Salt March 1930 also civil disobedience)
  [229:1:4.1] Ahmedabad | 1918 | First hunger strike
  [229:1:4.2] Kheda | 1918 | First non-cooperation (NCM 1920-22)
  [229:1:4.3] Rowlatt Satyagraha | 1919 | Hartal; first all-India mass movement
  [229:1:4.4] NCM, Swadeshi | — | Boycott, picketing
[229:1:5] - Rules: **never insult the opponent**; bear assaults **without retaliation**; **submit to arrest**; obey jail discipline.
[229:1:6] First mass movement by Gandhi = Champaran; first all-India = Rowlatt (some call NCM 1920 the first nationwide).
## SUB 2 | Means, Ends and Politics
[229:2:0] TABLE head: Quotation | Point
  [229:2:0.0] "Wrong means never take us to right ends" | Means-ends purity

## SUB 3 | Swaraj
[229:3:0] - Not only political freedom: **self-rule, self-control** and capacity of the masses to govern themselves without coercion.
[229:3:1] - Linked principles: **swadeshi, ahimsa, satyagraha, decentralisation, education for all**.
## SUB 4 | Influences

## SUB 3 | Gandhian Economy
[230:3:0] - **Non-violent economy**: centralisation leads to exploitation and inequality → **decentralise**.
[230:3:1] - Needs **decentralisation and self-reliance**; **production for need (consumption), not profit**.
[230:3:2] - Priority to **agriculture and village / cottage industries** over heavy industry.
[230:3:3] - Against mechanisation that displaces labour in labour-surplus India.

## SUB 4 | Trusteeship
[230:4:0] - Idea linked to **South Africa, 1903** (law firm at Johannesburg).
[230:4:1] - Applied to both **capitalists and zamindars** (e.g. Ahmedabad mill owners).
[230:4:2] - **Ahmedabad Mill Strike 1918**: Gandhi's fast; **35% wage rise**.
[230:4:3] - **Textile Labour Association (1920)**: arbitration and non-violence.
[230:4:4] - A **"Practical Trusteeship Formula"** was drafted by co-workers **Narhari Parikh and Kishorelal Mashruwala** while Gandhi was detained at the **Aga Khan Palace**, and refined by **M.L. Dantwala**; it proposed a minimum living wage and a ceiling on income.
[230:4:5] - **Roots of trusteeship:** Gandhi drew the idea from the Gita's teaching of non-possession (aparigraha) and from the English law of trusts (Snell's Equity), as he tells in his autobiography.
