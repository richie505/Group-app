=== NOTES 2:107 | Marriage – Forms, Rules of Endogamy and Exogamy, Changing Trends and Legislation
## SUB 0 | Marriage Laws – Acts, Ages, Registration, Personal Law
[107:0:0] # Acts – year and key point
[107:0:1] TABLE head: Act | Key point (exam answer words)
  [107:0:1.0] Special (Civil) Marriage Act 1872 (the Native Marriage Act) | first legislative provision for inter-caste (civil) marriage; monogamous civil marriage; ages 14 (girl), 18 (boy)
  [107:0:1.1] Indian Divorce Act 1869 + Christian Marriage Act 1872 | Christians; adultery, bigamy, conversion; mutual consent added 2001
  [107:0:1.2] Age of Consent Act 1891 | B.M. Malabari's effort; girls below 12 – an early measure, not a restraint law
  [107:0:1.3] Sharda (Sarda) Act 1929 = Child Marriage Restraint Act | first child marriage restraint law; curbed marriage of young girls; fixed minimum age 14 for girls, 18 for boys (raised to 15 in 1949, 18/21 in 1978)
  [107:0:1.4] Anand Marriage Act 1909 | Sikh; registration added 2012
  [107:0:1.5] Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act 1936 | re-enacted the 1865 Parsi law; registration compulsory
  [107:0:1.6] Dissolution of Muslim Marriage Act 1939 | wife's grounds for divorce
  [107:0:1.7] Special Marriage Act 1954 | inter-religious civil marriage before a Marriage Officer; ages 21/18; registration compulsory; divorce Sec. 27
  [107:0:1.8] Hindu Marriage Act 1955 | first codified Hindu marriage law after Independence; Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs; STs only if notified; registration (Sec. 8) optional
  [107:0:1.9] Foreign Marriage Act 1969 | –
  [107:0:1.10] PCMA 2006 (Prohibition of Child Marriage Act) | ages 18 (women) / 21 (men); child marriage voidable at the child's option; punishes an adult male who marries a child (Sec. 9), whoever performs or abets it (Sec. 10), and parents/guardians who promote it or fail to stop it (Sec. 11)
  [107:0:1.11] Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act 2019 | instant triple talaq void and illegal; up to 3 years' jail + fine; Parliament passed it 30 July 2019 (Rajya Sabha)
[107:0:2] - **Sarda Act statements (TSPSC 2023):** "known as Sharda Act" and "to curb child marriage" are correct; "fixed girls' age at **15**" is wrong (it was **14**) and "minimum three years' RI" is wrong (only simple imprisonment/fine).
[107:0:3] - **Registration compulsory:** under the **Special Marriage Act** (the marriage certificate is the registration); not under the HMA.
[107:0:4] - **Muslim law:** marriage (Nikah) is a **contract**, solemnised by a **Kazi** (Kazis Act 1880) through a *nikahnama*; **Mehr** = the sum fixed at marriage and **payable to the bride** (prompt or on divorce/death); **khula** = divorce at the wife's instance by mutual consent.
[107:0:5] - **Talaq-e-Hasan:** talaq once a month over **three months**; challenged in the SC after the 2017 triple-talaq verdict; **Talaq-e-Ahsan** = a single pronouncement + iddat.
[107:0:6] - **Sikh Anand Karaj** is solemnised in a **gurdwara**.
[107:0:7] # Goa and the Uniform Civil Code
[107:0:8] - **Goa**: long the **only State with a common civil code** – the **Portuguese Civil Code 1867**, brought into Goa in **1870**.
[107:0:9] - **Uttarakhand UCC** in force since **27 January 2025** – the first State to enforce a UCC after Independence; live-in registration mandatory.
[107:0:10] - 2026 (app notes): Amit Shah said a UCC will cover all **21 NDA States by 2029**; Assam, Gujarat, MP UCC Bills await assent.
[107:0:11] > Exam angle: "only State with a common civil code" = Goa in older papers; add Uttarakhand (2025) for current questions.

[107:0:13] - **Muta:** a **temporary marriage** for a fixed period recognised among **(Shia) Muslims**; Nikah is the regular permanent marriage.
## SUB 1 | Forms of Marriage – Monogamy, Polygamy, Levirate, Sororate
[107:1:0] # Forms by number of spouses
[107:1:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [107:1:1.0] Monogamy | one man – one woman (the most common form)
  [107:1:1.1] Polygamy | more than one spouse at a time: polygyny (one man, many wives) or polyandry (one woman, many husbands)
  [107:1:1.2] Sororal polygyny | a man marries several sisters
  [107:1:1.3] Fraternal (adelphic) polyandry | a woman's husbands are brothers (Toda, Khasa)
  [107:1:1.4] Group marriage | several men with several women
  [107:1:1.5] Serial monogamy | remarriage after death/divorce, one spouse at a time
[107:1:2] # Preferential widow/widower marriage
[107:1:3] TABLE head: Term | Telugu term in APPSC papers | Meaning
  [107:1:3.0] Levirate | Devara Nyayam ("divine justice") | a widow marries her deceased husband's (younger) brother (Gond, Munda)
  [107:1:3.1] Sororate | Bharya Bhagini Nyayam ("justice for spouses / wife's sister") | a widower marries his deceased wife's sister
[107:1:4] - **Levirate = husband's brother; Sororate = wife's sister** – both after the spouse's death.
[107:1:5] - **Toda (Nilgiris):** fraternal **polyandry → polygyny (polyandry with several wives) → monogamy** – the order asked by UPSC 1997.
[107:1:6] - Polyandry tends to arise where one man cannot support a family (extreme poverty).
[107:1:7] - **Legal position today:** polygamy and polygyny are prohibited for Hindus (HMA Sec. 11 – bigamous marriage void); monogamy prevails.
[107:1:8] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Society p.19 (marriage terms); NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.19-20, p.43 (Toda, Khasa polyandry); OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 2 (src/society/onlyias-ncert-indian-society-02.txt) l.177-189; app notes 2:107 sub 0
[107:1:9] - **Levirate** is practised in **patriarchal (patrilineal)** societies – it keeps the widow and her children in the husband's lineage.
[107:1:10] - **Widow remarriage:** **Islam** permitted it from the start; for Hindus it was legalised only by the **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856**.
## SUB 2 | Endogamy, Exogamy, Hypergamy and Incest Taboo
[107:2:0] # Rules of mate selection
[107:2:1] TABLE head: Rule | Meaning
  [107:2:1.0] Endogamy (Telugu *antar-vivaham*, "intermarriage" in translated papers) | marriage within one's own caste, tribe, religion – keeps caste boundaries
  [107:2:1.1] Exogamy (*bahir-vivaham*, "extramarriage") | marriage outside a group: sapinda exogamy, sagotra exogamy, village exogamy
  [107:2:1.2] Hypergamy = Anuloma | higher-caste man + lower-caste woman (woman marries up) – tolerated
  [107:2:1.3] Hypogamy = Pratiloma | lower-caste man + higher-caste woman (woman marries down) – condemned
[107:2:2] - **Ghurye**: endogamy is the **defining feature of caste**.
[107:2:3] - **North India**: caste endogamy + gotra exogamy + **village exogamy**; daughters marry into distant villages.
[107:2:4] - **Incest taboo**: a social prohibition that **forbids sexual contact between certain relatives** (parents–children, siblings) – the basis of exogamy.
[107:2:5] - **Irawati Karve** analysed sapinda and gotra.
[107:2:6] # Sapinda and prohibited degrees (HMA)
[107:2:7] - **Sec. 3(f)** sapinda: **3 generations** on the mother's side, **5** on the father's.
[107:2:8] - **Sec. 5(iv)** bars prohibited degrees; **Sec. 5(v)** bars **sapinda** marriage – both unless **custom** allows.
[107:2:9] - **Delhi High Court (Jan 2024)** upheld **Sec. 5(v)** (sapinda bar).
[107:2:10] - **⚠ Trap:** anuloma ≠ pratiloma – in both terms the direction is the **woman's** marriage relative to her own caste.
[107:2:11] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Society p.18-19, p.51; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 1 (src/society/onlyias-ncert-indian-society-01.txt) l.1419-1425; OnlyIAS Part 2 l.205-218; NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.20, p.27; app notes 2:107 sub 3
[107:2:12] - **HMA Sec. 5 conditions:** no living spouse, sound mind, ages **21/18**, not within prohibited degrees, not sapindas – belonging to the **same sub-caste is not a condition**.
[107:2:13] - **Menarikam** (Telugu): **cross-cousin marriage** – between children of **opposite-sex siblings** (mother's brother's / father's sister's daughter); preferred in South India, barred in the North.
## SUB 3 | Hindu Marriage – Aims, Manu's Eight Forms, Ancient Customs
[107:3:0] # Definitions and aims
[107:3:1] - **Malinowski**: marriage is a **contract for the production and maintenance of children** ("a covenant to bear and nurture children").
[107:3:2] - **Lowie**: a relatively permanent bond between permissible mates; **Leach**: a **bundle of rights** (legitimating offspring, access to the spouse, affinal ties, domestic rights).
[107:3:3] - Hindu marriage = a **sacrament** (homa, panigrahana, saptapadi); Muslim marriage = a **contract**.
[107:3:4] - **Aims (K.M. Kapadia)** in descending order: **Dharma** (religious duty) → **Praja** (progeny) → **Rati** (pleasure).
[107:3:5] # Manu Smriti – 8 forms (4 approved, 4 disapproved)
[107:3:6] TABLE head: Form | Meaning | Status
  [107:3:6.0] Brahma | daughter given to a learned, virtuous groom; no dowry or bride price | approved; highest
  [107:3:6.1] Daiva | daughter given to a priest officiating at a sacrifice | approved
  [107:3:6.2] Arsha | after symbolic gifts (cow and bull) from the groom | approved
  [107:3:6.3] Prajapatya | duty, family welfare, procreation | approved
  [107:3:6.4] Gandharva | love, mutual consent (Shakuntala–Dushyanta) | disapproved but conditionally accepted
  [107:3:6.5] Asura | purchase of the bride – groom pays bride price | disapproved
  [107:3:6.6] Rakshasa | forcible abduction; linked with Kshatriyas, seen in the epics | disapproved
  [107:3:6.7] Paisacha | deceit, intoxication, no consent | most condemned
[107:3:7] # Other customary unions
[107:3:8] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [107:3:8.0] Niyoga | a wife cohabits with a designated man to beget a child when her husband is impotent or dead
  [107:3:8.1] Sambandham | consensual, informal union of Namboothiris and Nairs under Kerala's matrilineal system
[107:3:9] - **Later Vedic** changes: child marriage began, **Swayamvara** declined, widow remarriage discouraged, same-gotra marriage banned.
[107:3:10] - **Satyashodhak marriage** (started by **Savitribai Phule**): no dowry, no Brahmin priest.
[107:3:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.19-20; app notes 2:107 sub 1-2; general knowledge, not checked (Malinowski, Kapadia)
[107:3:12] - **K.M. Kapadia:** "marriage is the only sacrament (rite) for women, hence marriage is necessary for them".
## SUB 4 | Divorce and Marriage Offences
[107:4:0] # Divorce
[107:4:1] - **Divorce** = the **legal dissolution of a marriage by a court**; **W.P. Scott**: "an institutionalised arrangement for terminating the marriage relationship and allowing each partner the right to remarry". Living apart is only separation.
[107:4:2] - **Desertion** (husband leaving the family to fend for itself) = **"the poor man's divorce"**.
[107:4:3] - First divorce laws: **Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act 1865**, **Indian Divorce Act 1869** (Christians) – not "Parsi Act 1869".
[107:4:4] - **HMA Sec. 13** grounds: adultery, cruelty, desertion, conversion, unsoundness of mind, renunciation, not heard of for **7 years**; **13B** mutual consent; **Sec. 11** bigamous marriage void; **Sec. 12** voidable marriages.
[107:4:5] - **Irretrievable breakdown** is not an HMA ground; **May 2023 (Shilpa Sailesh)**: the SC can dissolve such a marriage under **Art. 142(1)** even if one party opposes.
[107:4:6] - **Muslim law:** 1939 Act grounds – no maintenance for **2 years**, imprisonment of 7+ years, cruelty; 2019 Act gives subsistence allowance and custody of minor children.
[107:4:7] # Offences (IPC → BNS)
[107:4:8] - **IPC Chapter XX (Sec. 493–498)** = **offences relating to marriage** = **BNS 81–84**; bigamy IPC **494** = **BNS 82**.
[107:4:9] - **Assam Prohibition of Polygamy Bill 2025** (app notes): cognizable, non-bailable; up to **7 years'** RI.

[107:5:2] # Trends
[107:5:3] - Aim shifts from **dharma to companionship**; age at marriage rising; more divorce and desertion; **live-in** and **cohabitation** (unmarried partners sharing an intimate, committed daily life) gaining acceptance.
[107:5:4] - **Patricia Uberoi**: companionate marriage, serial monogamy.
[107:5:5] - **Inter-caste** marriages only about **5%**; **Dr Ambedkar Scheme**: Rs **2.5 lakh** to inter-caste couples; Ambedkar: intermarriage the real remedy for caste.
[107:5:6] - **Nepal**: **first South Asian** nation to register a same-sex marriage (2023).

## SUB 6 | Child Marriage – Causes, Data, Campaigns
[107:6:0] - **Causes:** **poverty**, **low education of girls**, **social customs and traditions**, dowry, safety of girls, the old belief in pre-puberty marriage – "status symbol" is not a cause.
[107:6:1] - More prevalent in **lower castes, poorer classes** and **joint families**.
[107:6:2] - **International Day of the Girl Child**: 11 October; first observed **2012**, theme **"Ending Child Marriage"**.
[107:6:3] TABLE head: Data | Fact
  [107:6:3.0] NCRB 2021 (PCMA cases) | Karnataka 273, Tamil Nadu 169, Assam 155 – the top three
  [107:6:3.1] NCRB 2023 | 6,038 cases (about six times 1,002 of 2022); Assam nearly 90%
  [107:6:3.2] NFHS-6 | 20.1% of women aged 20–24 married before 18 (app notes)
[107:6:4] - **PCM (Amendment) Bill 2025** (app notes): women's age to **21**; **Bal Vivah Mukt Bharat**: cut child marriage **10% by 2026**, eliminate by **2030**; AP adolescent-education programme in **9,675 schools**.
[107:6:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.24-25; web: factly.in (NCRB 2021 state figures), assamtribune.com (NCRB 2023); app notes 2:107 sub 9

=== NOTES 2:108 | Kinship – Descent, Lineage, Clan, Kinship Terminology; North-south Indian Kinship Contrast
## SUB 0 | Kinship – Meaning, Consanguineal, Affinal and Ritual Kin
[108:0:0] # Meaning
[108:0:1] - **Kinship**: a system of social relationships based on **blood (consanguineal) and marriage (affinal)** – the set of relationships among **consanguineous and marital relatives**.
[108:0:2] - **Radcliffe-Brown**: kinship = a system of **dynamic relations** between persons, regulated by **social usage**; a field of rights and obligations.
[108:0:3] - **Murdock**: a structured system of relationships binding individuals by complex interlocking ties.
[108:0:4] - Best short definition (UPSC 1994 key): kinship = **social identity expressing genealogical relationship**.
[108:0:5] - **Marriage creates kinship** (Westermarck, APPSC 2000 key): when two people marry they become kin to one another.
[108:0:6] # Types
[108:0:7] TABLE head: Type | Basis | Example
  [108:0:7.0] Consanguineal (blood) | birth / descent | parent–child, siblings, uncle–nephew; an adopted son is treated as consanguineous kin
  [108:0:7.1] Affinal ("relatives by marriage", affines; "matrimonial kinship") | marriage | husband–wife (ego's wife), son-in-law–father-in-law, brothers-in-law
  [108:0:7.2] Ritual / fictive | functional social relationship, not biology | godparenthood, dharam bhai/behen, rakhi ties
[108:0:8] - **Genetic traits** pass only through **blood relatives**, never through affines.
[108:0:9] - A father–son tie by birth is consanguineal, not fictive; husband–wife is the classic **non-consanguineous** pair.
[108:0:10] - **Inheritance** = transfer of property (immovable) from a dead person to heirs.
[108:0:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.25, p.31; Rocket Indian Society p.18, p.31; OnlyIAS NCERT Indian Society Part 2 l.175-176; app notes 2:108 sub 0; general knowledge, not checked (Westermarck, adopted son)
[108:0:12] - **Cognates:** all **consanguineous (blood) relatives** together form a **cognate group**; **agnates** = kin related through males only.
## SUB 1 | Kinship Usages and Terminology
[108:1:0] # Usages
[108:1:1] TABLE head: Usage | Meaning
  [108:1:1.0] Avoidance | restraint, e.g. daughter-in-law and father-in-law
  [108:1:1.1] Joking relationship | licensed teasing, e.g. brother-in-law – sister-in-law (a man and his wife's younger sister)
  [108:1:1.2] Teknonymy | referring to a person through a child ("father of Ramu")
  [108:1:1.3] Avunculate | special authority of the maternal uncle (matrilineal societies)
  [108:1:1.4] Amitate (Telugu translation "patriarchal relationship") | special respect/authority of the father's sister (patrilineal societies)
  [108:1:1.5] Couvade (*Kuhana prasuti*) | the husband simulates pregnancy / observes his wife's birth taboos – Khasi, Toda
  [108:1:1.6] Complementary filiation (Meyer Fortes) | in a patrilineal society, the ritual and social role of the mother's brother and mother's kin
[108:1:2] - **Match (UPSC 2002):** avoidance – kinship practice; joint family – kinship group; gotra – exogamy; sororate – marriage.
[108:1:3] # Terminology
[108:1:4] TABLE head: Basis | Types
  [108:1:4.0] Meaning | classificatory (one word for many kin: "uncle") vs descriptive (one kin: "father")
  [108:1:4.1] Use | terms of address vs terms of reference

=== NOTES 2:109 | Caste – Features, Theories of Origin, Varna-jati Distinction
## SUB 0 | Jajmani System and Village Service Relations
[109:0:0] - **Jajmani system** = a **socio-economic and ritual** arrangement in which one caste secures the services of another; everyone works for a family or group to which he is **hereditarily** linked – so it is at once economic, political and socio-cultural.
[109:0:1] - **Jajman** = the **landholding** patron caste; **kamin** = the castes rendering hereditary services; paid in kind (grain share) at harvest.
[109:0:2] - Features: **functional interdependence** of castes, **hereditary** ties, **inequality** of status between groups; critics (Beidelman) call it exploitative, but "feudalism" is not a defining feature.
[109:0:3] - Pre-independence rural inter-caste relations rested on **dominance and reciprocity** (patron dominance + exchange of services).
[109:0:4] - **William H. Wiser**, ***The Hindu Jajmani System*** (**1936**); with Charlotte Wiser *Behind Mud Walls*. (*India's Villages* – Srinivas ed.; *Caste in Modern India* – Srinivas; *Hindu Social Organization* – P.H. Prabhu.)
[109:0:5] - **⚠ Trap:** jajmani is a **caste-village** system; economic exchange **between tribes** is mainly **barter**.

=== NOTES 2:110 | Caste in Modern India – Dominant Caste, Sanskritisation, Caste Associations, Caste and Politics
## SUB 0 | Caste and Politics; Caste Associations
[110:0:0] - **Lloyd and Susanne Rudolph**, *The Modernity of Tradition* (**1967**): caste associations = **agents of democratisation** (**Nadars, Vanniyars**); three types of political mobilisation – **vertical**, **horizontal** and **differential**; coined **"bullock capitalists"**.
[110:0:1] - **Caste association** = a voluntary, membership-based body of a caste acting as a **pressure group**; Rocket: first caste association = **Justice Party (1916)**.
[110:0:2] - **Justice Party** (South Indian Liberal Federation, 1916–17): to **counter Brahmin supremacy** in politics and services; intermediate castes (Kammas, Reddys, Naidus); led to the **Communal G.O. 1921** (Madras Presidency).
[110:0:3] - **Minto-Morley Reforms 1909** (separate electorates): root of caste/communal vote-bank politics.
[110:0:4] - **Rajni Kothari** edited *Caste in Indian Politics* (**1970**); **Jaffrelot**, *India's Silent Revolution*: lower castes rise through politics; conflict now about **power**, not ritual status.
[110:0:5] - Castes that rose through organisation and politics: **Jat Sikhs (Punjab), Nadars (TN), Jatavs (UP), Vokkaligas (Karnataka)** – all four (UPSC 2000).
[110:0:6] - **RAS 2013 A-R:** castes are politicised **and** casteism in politics is rising – both true, but R does not explain A (official key).
[110:0:7] - **Jan Breman**: south Gujarat – **Halpati** labourers under **Anavil** landowners (**hali** bonded labour), "footloose labour".

## SUB 1 | Dominant Caste
[110:1:0] - Concept coined and systematised by **M.N. Srinivas** from **Rampura village, Mysore** (1955) – not Ghurye, Dube, Béteille or Bose.
[110:1:1] - Traditional criteria: sizeable **arable land**, **numerical strength**, high **ritual status**; later additions: **Western education**, administrative jobs, urban income.
[110:1:2] - Examples: Yadavs (Bihar, UP), Jats, Vokkaligas, **Reddys, Kammas** (AP); pre-independence **Bihar**: Bhumihar, Rajput, Kayastha (**not Kurmi**).

## SUB 2 | Characteristics and Classification of Tribes
[111:2:0] # Meaning and features
[111:2:1] - **'Tribe'** comes from the **Latin 'tribus'**. **Marshall Sahlins:** a tribe is a **segmental** organisation of equivalent, **unspecialised multi-family groups, clans or bands**.
[111:2:2] - **Features (NextGen):** **segmentary but egalitarian**; **no mutual interdependence** (unlike caste); **direct access to land**; primitive subsistence technology (**shifting cultivation**, hunting, gathering); **animism**; monogamy with polygamy, **polyandry** among **Toda** and **Khasa**; mostly patrilineal, **matrilineal** among **Khasi, Jaintia, Garo**.
[111:2:3] - **Animism** = belief in and worship of **nature spirits**; **Totemism** = a clan's sacred bond with an **animal or plant totem**, revered and **not killed**; goes with **clan exogamy**.
[111:2:4] - **Shifting cultivation** = slash-and-burn; called **Jhum** in North-East India.
[111:2:5] - **Division of labour** in tribal society rests mainly on **age and sex (gender)**; **age-groups (age-sets)** are a basis of tribal stratification.
[111:2:6] - **Gender taboos:** among the **Santhal**, women may **not touch the plough** or **thatch (cover) the roof** of a house.
[111:2:7] - **Kula ring** (Trobriand Islands, Malinowski): ceremonial exchange of **shell necklaces (soulava)** and **armshells/bracelets (mwali)**, moving in opposite directions.
[111:2:8] - **Change:** loss of languages (e.g. **Bo**, Andamans, extinct 2010), new economic classes among Chotanagpur tribes in Assam tea gardens.
[111:2:9] # Classification
[111:2:10] TABLE head: Basis | Types / examples
  [111:2:10.0] Racial – H.H. Risley | Dravidian (Paniyan, Santals), Mongoloid (Himalayan, NE)

## SUB 3 | Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs)
[111:3:0] # Origin, criteria, numbers
[111:3:1] - **Dhebar Commission (1960-61)** flagged the least-developed groups; **PTG** category created **1973**; renamed **PVTGs** in **2006**.
[111:3:2] - **Criteria:** **pre-agricultural** technology, **low literacy**, economic backwardness, **declining or stagnant population**.
[111:3:3] - **75 PVTGs** in **18 States + 1 UT (Andaman and Nicobar)** – not 95.
[111:3:4] - **Odisha 13** (highest), then **AP with Telangana 12**; Bihar 9.
[111:3:5] - **Sahariyas** = largest PVTG; **Sentinelese** and Andamanese = smallest.

## SUB 3 | Ethnicity – Concept, Identity and Minority
[112:3:0] # Concept
[112:3:1] - **Ethnic group** = people who identify with each other on a common **language/dialect, history, society, culture or nation**; markers – language, religion, race, region, culture.
[112:3:2] - **Consensus approach:** groups **coexist** with separate identities; **conflict approach:** ethnic groups are **interest groups competing unequally** for power and resources (South Africa, Sri Lankan Tamils).
[112:3:3] - **Xenocentrism** = preferring another group's culture and **rejecting one's own**; opposite of **ethnocentrism**.
[112:3:4] # Views on ethnic identity
[112:3:5] TABLE head: View | Core idea | Scholars
  [112:3:5.0] Primordialist | Identity given by birth; "primordial attachments" | Clifford Geertz, Edward Shils
  [112:3:5.1] Instrumentalist | Elites mobilise identity for power | Paul Brass; Rajni Kothari (*Politics in India*); Christophe Jaffrelot (*India's Silent Revolution*)
  [112:3:5.2] Constructivist | Identity socially made, kept by boundaries | Fredrik Barth (*Ethnic Groups and Boundaries*); Benedict Anderson (*Imagined Communities*)
[112:3:6] # Minority
[112:3:7] TABLE head: Scholar | Contribution
  [112:3:7.0] T.K. Oommen | Separates nation, nationality, ethnicity and citizenship
  [112:3:7.1] Arnold Rose | Minority = group set apart by race, nationality, religion or language, regarded as different by itself and others
  [112:3:7.2] Giddens | Minority marked by disadvantage, group solidarity, isolation
[112:3:8] - **National minority:** non-dominant citizen group with its own ethnic, religious or linguistic identity; term from **European minority-rights law**.
[112:3:9] - Abroad: **Rohingya** – Sunni Muslim minority of **Rakhine State, Myanmar**; China recognises **55** minority groups, **Hui** = largest Muslim group (Uyghurs too); China's "ethnic unity and progress" law (Mar 2026); Sri Lanka Tamil-Muslim common platform (Jul 2026).
