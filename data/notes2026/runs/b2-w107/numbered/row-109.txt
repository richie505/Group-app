## SUB 0 | Jajmani System and Village Service Relations
[0:0] - **Jajmani system** = a **socio-economic and ritual** arrangement in which one caste secures the services of another; everyone works for a family or group to which he is **hereditarily** linked – so it is at once economic, political and socio-cultural.
[0:1] - **Jajman** = the **landholding** patron caste; **kamin** = the castes rendering hereditary services; paid in kind (grain share) at harvest.
[0:2] - Features: **functional interdependence** of castes, **hereditary** ties, **inequality** of status between groups; critics (Beidelman) call it exploitative, but "feudalism" is not a defining feature.
[0:3] - Pre-independence rural inter-caste relations rested on **dominance and reciprocity** (patron dominance + exchange of services).
[0:4] - **William H. Wiser**, ***The Hindu Jajmani System*** (**1936**); with Charlotte Wiser *Behind Mud Walls*. (*India's Villages* – Srinivas ed.; *Caste in Modern India* – Srinivas; *Hindu Social Organization* – P.H. Prabhu.)
[0:5] - **⚠ Trap:** jajmani is a **caste-village** system; economic exchange **between tribes** is mainly **barter**.

## SUB 1 | Theories of Origin and Scholars of Caste
[1:0] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent | Idea
  [1:0.0] Traditional / Divine | Purusha Sukta (Rig Veda 10.90) | varnas from head, arms, thighs, feet
  [1:0.1] Racial | Herbert Risley | racial difference; Aryans kept apart
  [1:0.2] Occupational | Nesfield | hereditary occupations → guilds → castes
  [1:0.3] Brahminical / Political | Abbé Dubois | a Brahmin device for purity
  [1:0.4] Cultural | S.C. Roy | commensality and marriage bars
[1:1] - **Abbé J.A. Dubois** (*Hindu Manners, Customs and Ceremonies*, 19th c.): caste **saved Hindu society from barbarism**; also wrote that a Hindu woman could go anywhere alone without fear of insult.
[1:2] - **J.C. Nesfield** is credited (APPSC key) with first using the word **"untouchable"** for these castes.
[1:3] - Early Vedic: no rigid varna; **Later Vedic**: four hereditary varnas crystallise; **post-Maurya/Gupta**: Manu Smriti, rigid caste.
[1:4] TABLE head: Scholar | Definition / idea
  [1:4.0] S.V. Ketkar | *History of Caste in India* (1909): membership by birth + bar on marrying outside
  [1:4.1] Risley | common name, claimed mythical ancestor, same hereditary calling; ranked castes in the 1901 Census; first to define caste scientifically
  [1:4.2] C. Bouglé | hereditary specialisation, hierarchy, repulsion
  [1:4.3] A.W. Green | ideally no mobility up or down
  [1:4.4] J.H. Hutton | caste developed through social and religious disabilities
  [1:4.5] Louis Dumont | *Homo Hierarchicus* (1966): pure vs impure
  [1:4.6] McKim Marriott | ranking by exchange of "coded substances" (food, water, services)

## SUB 2 | Varna and Jati; Features of Caste
[2:0] - "Caste" from **Portuguese casta** (race, breed).
[2:1] TABLE head: Varna | Jati
  [2:1.0] theoretical 4-fold scheme of the Vedas | real local groups; thousands of sub-castes
  [2:1.1] excludes "untouchables" | regional variations
  [2:1.2] almost no mobility | mobility possible, esp. middle castes
[2:2] - **Ghurye** (*Caste and Race in India*, **1932**), **six features**: segmental division; hierarchy; restrictions on feeding and social intercourse; civil and religious disabilities and privileges; **lack of free choice of occupation**; restrictions on marriage (endogamy).
[2:3] - Two dimensions: **vertical** (ranking) and **horizontal** (grouping).
[2:4] - Cultural traits: pollution by touch, untouchability, Sanskritic rituals, commensal taboos (not universal inter-caste commensality).
[2:5] - **1931 Census**: **4,147** castes; caste enumeration began with the **1871** Census.
[2:6] - **Telugu dependent castes:** the **Viramushtis** are bards/beggars dependent on the **Komatis (Vaishyas)**.
[2:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.32-34; NextGen Society G2 Part 3 p.33, p.36; app notes 2:109 sub 0, 2; general knowledge, not checked (Viramushti)
