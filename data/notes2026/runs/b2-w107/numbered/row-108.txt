## SUB 0 | Kinship – Meaning, Consanguineal, Affinal and Ritual Kin
[0:0] # Meaning
[0:1] - **Kinship**: a system of social relationships based on **blood (consanguineal) and marriage (affinal)** – the set of relationships among **consanguineous and marital relatives**.
[0:2] - **Radcliffe-Brown**: kinship = a system of **dynamic relations** between persons, regulated by **social usage**; a field of rights and obligations.
[0:3] - **Murdock**: a structured system of relationships binding individuals by complex interlocking ties.
[0:4] - Best short definition (UPSC 1994 key): kinship = **social identity expressing genealogical relationship**.
[0:5] - **Marriage creates kinship** (Westermarck, APPSC 2000 key): when two people marry they become kin to one another.
[0:6] # Types
[0:7] TABLE head: Type | Basis | Example
  [0:7.0] Consanguineal (blood) | birth / descent | parent–child, siblings, uncle–nephew; an adopted son is treated as consanguineous kin
  [0:7.1] Affinal ("relatives by marriage", affines; "matrimonial kinship") | marriage | husband–wife (ego's wife), son-in-law–father-in-law, brothers-in-law
  [0:7.2] Ritual / fictive | functional social relationship, not biology | godparenthood, dharam bhai/behen, rakhi ties
[0:8] - **Genetic traits** pass only through **blood relatives**, never through affines.
[0:9] - A father–son tie by birth is consanguineal, not fictive; husband–wife is the classic **non-consanguineous** pair.
[0:10] - **Inheritance** = transfer of property (immovable) from a dead person to heirs.

## SUB 1 | Kinship Usages and Terminology
[1:0] # Usages
[1:1] TABLE head: Usage | Meaning
  [1:1.0] Avoidance | restraint, e.g. daughter-in-law and father-in-law
  [1:1.1] Joking relationship | licensed teasing, e.g. brother-in-law – sister-in-law (a man and his wife's younger sister)
  [1:1.2] Teknonymy | referring to a person through a child ("father of Ramu")
  [1:1.3] Avunculate | special authority of the maternal uncle (matrilineal societies)
  [1:1.4] Amitate (Telugu translation "patriarchal relationship") | special respect/authority of the father's sister (patrilineal societies)
  [1:1.5] Couvade (*Kuhana prasuti*) | the husband simulates pregnancy / observes his wife's birth taboos – Khasi, Toda
  [1:1.6] Complementary filiation (Meyer Fortes) | in a patrilineal society, the ritual and social role of the mother's brother and mother's kin
[1:2] - **Match (UPSC 2002):** avoidance – kinship practice; joint family – kinship group; gotra – exogamy; sororate – marriage.
[1:3] # Terminology
[1:4] TABLE head: Basis | Types
  [1:4.0] Meaning | classificatory (one word for many kin: "uncle") vs descriptive (one kin: "father")
  [1:4.1] Use | terms of address vs terms of reference
[1:5] - **Hawaiian** system (fewest terms, all cousins = siblings) goes with **bilateral (cognatic)** descent.
[1:6] - **Kinship diagrams:** **circle (O) = female**, **triangle = male**, "=" = marriage.
[1:7] - **L.H. Morgan**: classificatory–descriptive distinction, *Systems of Consanguinity and Affinity* (**1871**); *Ancient Society* (1877): **savagery → barbarism → civilisation**.
[1:8] - **K.M. Kapadia** linked kinship terms to marriage and inheritance rules.

## SUB 2 | Descent, Residence and Descent Groups
[2:0] # Descent rules
[2:1] TABLE head: Rule | Meaning | Example
  [2:1.0] Patrilineal | through the father | most of North India; Kondhs, Santhals, Bhils
  [2:1.1] Matrilineal | through the mother | Khasi, Garo, Nair, Lakshadweep Muslims
  [2:1.2] Bilateral / cognatic | both sides equal | –
  [2:1.3] Double (bilineal) | movable via mother, immovable via father | –
  [2:1.4] Parallel / cross | women → daughters, men → sons / mother → son, father → daughter | –
[2:2] # Residence after marriage
[2:3] TABLE head: Term | Couple lives with
  [2:3.0] Patrilocal = virilocal | the husband's (groom's) parents
  [2:3.1] Matrilocal = uxorilocal | the wife's family
  [2:3.2] Avunculocal (Telugu *matula-sthanika*) | the groom's mother's brother
  [2:3.3] Neolocal | a new, separate home
  [2:3.4] Duolocal | each spouse in own natal home; husband visits at night – Lakshadweep matrilineal Muslims, old Nayars
  [2:3.5] Bilocal / ambilocal | either family, alternately
[2:4] # Descent groups
[2:5] TABLE head: Group | Definition
  [2:5.0] Lineage | unilineal group from a known ancestor; exogamous
  [2:5.1] Clan | believes in a common ancestor; link not demonstrated
  [2:5.2] Gotra | exogamous Hindu clan; common male ancestor; same-gotra bar from the Later Vedic period
  [2:5.3] Phratry | two or more related clans
  [2:5.4] Moiety | society divided into two halves
[2:6] - Order (small to large): **lineage → clan → phratry → moiety**.
[2:7] - **Corporate group** (tribal societies): a **durable, property-holding group based on kinship**.
[2:8] - **Sapinda**: kin within the prohibited degrees of blood relationship – not a large unilineal group.

## SUB 3 | Matrilineal Kinship – Nair Tharavad, Khasi
[3:0] - Matrilineal communities: **Nairs** (Kerala), **Khasis** and **Garos** (Meghalaya); "pro-matriarchal" pair asked: **Nairs and Khasis**.
[3:1] - **Tharavad (Nayar)**: a **matrilineal, matrilocal** joint family of a woman, her brothers and sisters, and her own and her sisters' **sons and daughters** (and daughters' children); husbands do not live in it – no husband–wife or father–child relation inside.
[3:2] - **Nayar marriage**: the husband had **no legal paternity** – children have no legal genitor/father in the tharavad (Gough's test case of marriage).
[3:3] - **Khasi**: matrilineal; women free; children go to the mother after divorce; clan exogamy, parallel cousins barred.
[3:4] - In matrilineal societies the **maternal uncle** holds authority (avunculate).
[3:5] - **⚠ Trap:** Namboodiris are patrilineal Brahmins (they joined Nair women only in **sambandham**); Coorgs are patrilineal.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.28, p.30; app notes 2:108 sub 8; general knowledge, not checked (Gough)

## SUB 5 | North–South Kinship Contrast (Karve)
[5:0] - **Irawati Karve**, *Kinship Organization in India* (**1953**): **4 zones – North, Central, South, East**; basis = language, caste, family organisation; "Mother of Indian Kinship Studies".
[5:1] TABLE head: North | South
  [5:1.0] caste endogamy, gotra and village exogamy; sasan/sapinda bars; four-gotra rule | cross-cousin marriage preferred; parallel cousins barred
  [5:1.1] brides go to higher-status families | marriage with the elder sister's daughter (Telugu menarikam), FZD or MBD
  [5:1.2] distinct terms for blood and affinal kin; by generation | terms by older/younger
  [5:1.3] bride breaks with the natal family | continuity with the natal family
[5:2] - **Cross-cousin** = child of the **mother's brother** or father's sister; **parallel cousin** = child of the father's brother or mother's sister.
[5:3] - Uncle–niece (elder sister's daughter) marriage is allowed among **Marathas of south Maharashtra and Vokkaligas of Karnataka** (Karve).
[5:4] - **Dumont** on the South: **immediate exchange**; kin clustered in a narrow area; **no sharp consanguine–affine line**; more freedom for women.
[5:5] - **Pauline Kolenda**: *Regional Variations in Hindu Kinship* (**1968**).
