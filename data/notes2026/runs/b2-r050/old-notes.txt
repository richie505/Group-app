=== NOTES 2:50 | Centre-state Financial Relations – Articles 264-293; Taxes, Grants-in-aid, Borrowing
## SUB 0 | Constitutional Framework – Part Xii
[50:0:0] - **Part XII** (Finance, Property, Contracts and Suits): **Arts 264-293**; financial relations = **Arts 268-293**.
[50:0:1] - **Distribution of revenues** = **Arts 268-281** (Union taxes, grants-in-aid, Finance Commission).
[50:0:2] - Budget procedure is in **Part V** (Arts 112-116).
[50:0:3] TABLE head: Article | Subject
  [50:0:3.0] 265 | No tax levied or collected except by authority of law
  [50:0:3.1] 266 | Consolidated Funds and Public Accounts – CFI receives all Union revenues, loans raised and loan repayments
  [50:0:3.2] 267 | Contingency Funds
  [50:0:3.3] 274 | Bills affecting taxation of agricultural income
  [50:0:3.4] 275 | Statutory grants-in-aid
  [50:0:3.5] 276 | Profession tax (State)

  [50:0:3.7] 282 | Discretionary grants

  [50:0:3.10] 148 | CAG – certifies net proceeds for the divisible pool
[50:0:4] - Mutual exemption exception: Centre can tax a State's **commercial operations** and State-owned companies.

## SUB 2 | Amendments Changing Tax Distribution
[50:2:0] TABLE head: Amendment | Year | Change
  [50:2:0.0] 60th | 1988 | Profession tax ceiling ₹250 → ₹2,500
  [50:2:0.1] 80th | 2000 (effective 1 April 1996) | 10th FC "Alternative Scheme of Devolution"; omitted Art. 272; all central taxes shareable
  [50:2:0.2] 88th | 2003 | Inserted Art. 268A (service tax)
  [50:2:0.3] 101st | 2016 | Inserted 246A, 269A, 279A; omitted 268A
[50:2:1] - **80th Amendment:** States got a share of **gross proceeds** of Union taxes – **29%** for 1996-97 to 1999-2000.
[50:2:2] - It moved **stock-exchange taxes, estate duty, railway fares, newspapers** (old Art. 269) into the **Art. 270 pool**; **surcharges stayed out**.
[50:2:3] 60th = 1988; 80th = 2000 (from 1996); 88th = 2003; 101st = 2016.
## SUB 3 | Profession Tax – Article 276
[50:3:0] - States tax **professions, trades, callings and employments** (Art. 276).
[50:3:1] - **Art. 276(2):** the Constitution limits the total each person pays – **₹2,500 per person per year**.
[50:3:2] - Raised from **₹250** by the **60th Amendment, 1988** (PYQ 2017).
## SUB 4 | Gst and Fiscal Autonomy
[50:4:0] # GST Framework
[50:4:1] - **101st Amendment, 2016**; GST in force **1 July 2017**; **destination-based**; replaced about **17** taxes (CGST, SGST, IGST, UTGST).
[50:4:2] - **122nd Amendment Bill** became the **101st Act**; GST subsumed **indirect** taxes only, **not direct** taxes.
[50:4:3] - **Art. 246A:** Parliament and States both make GST law; **only Parliament** for **inter-State supply**.
[50:4:4] - **Art. 269A:** IGST; revenue to the **destination State**.
[50:4:5] - Amended Arts 248-250, 268-271, 286, 366, 368 and the Sixth/Seventh Schedules.
[50:4:6] - **GST 2.0:** effective **September 2025**.
[50:4:7] - **AP HC (May 2026):** Central and State GST authorities probing the same entity must decide who proceeds – **no dual action**.
[50:4:8] # GST Council – Article 279A
[50:4:9] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [50:4:9.0] Chair | Union Finance Minister
  [50:4:9.1] Members | Union MoS (Finance) + State Finance Ministers
  [50:4:9.2] Votes | Centre 1/3, all States 2/3
  [50:4:9.3] Decision | 3/4 of weighted votes – neither side alone
  [50:4:9.4] Legal status | Mohit Minerals (2022): advice persuasive, not binding
[50:4:10] - **Art. 279A functions:** recommends the **turnover threshold** for GST exemption; designs its own **dispute-resolution** mechanism; acts not invalid due to a **vacancy** or defect.
[50:4:11] # Compensation and Critique
[50:4:12] - **Compensation cess** on **luxury and sin goods** paid States' GST revenue loss; guaranteed for **5 years (2017-22)**; its end is a fiscal stress (Kerala, May 2026).
[50:4:13] - Critique: States gave up independent indirect-tax powers; weighted voting gives the Centre an effective **veto**.
[50:4:14] - **Art. 279A:** provides for the **GST Council**; inserted by the **101st Amendment, 2016**.
## SUB 5 | Cesses, Surcharges and Vertical vs Horizontal Devolution
[50:5:0] - **Vertical devolution** = Centre-State share of the divisible pool; **horizontal** = distribution **among States (inter se)**; both recommended by the **Finance Commission** (Art. **280(3)**).
[50:5:1] - **Cesses and surcharges** stay outside the pool: ~**18%** of Centre's gross tax revenue, so effective devolution is near **31-32%** against the **41%** award.
[50:5:2] - **Chalo Delhi (2024):** southern States protested **population-based** devolution criteria.

## SUB 7 | Grants-in-aid – Statutory vs Discretionary
[50:7:0] TABLE head: Feature | Statutory (Art. 275) | Discretionary (Art. 282)
  [50:7:0.0] Recommended by | Finance Commission | Earlier Planning Commission; now Union ministries
  [50:7:0.1] Charged on CFI | Yes | No
  [50:7:0.2] Purpose | Needy States; ST welfare, Scheduled Areas | Any public purpose, even outside the law-making body's field
[50:7:1] - Art. 275 grants cover State-specific needs that devolution formulas miss (Aug 2026).
[50:7:2] Statutory grants are on the Finance Commission's advice, NOT NITI Aayog's (APPSC-GS 2019).
[50:7:3] - **Shift from grants to devolution:** more **untied** money and **autonomy** for States; fewer conditional transfers.

[50:8:7] - **AP:** post-2014 demand for special category status.

## SUB 10 | Emergencies and Fiscal Disputes
[50:10:0] - **Art. 354:** in National Emergency the President may **modify revenue distribution**; lasts till the end of the **financial year** in which the emergency ends.
[50:10:1] - **Art. 360:** Union directs States on **financial propriety**; **Money/Financial Bills** reserved for the President.
[50:10:2] - **Disputes:** Supreme Court's **original jurisdiction (Art. 131)**; matters referred to the **Finance Commission are excluded**.
[50:10:3] Centre-State fiscal disputes go to the Supreme Court (Art. 131), not to the Finance Commission.

=== NOTES 2:52 | Emergency Provisions – National Emergency (352), President's Rule (356), Financial Emergency (360); S.r. Bommai Case
## SUB 0 | Source and Scheme of Emergency Provisions
[52:0:0] - Emergency provisions are in **Part XVIII** (Articles 352-360).
[52:0:1] - Source of emergency provisions: **Government of India Act, 1935**; the ordinance power (Arts **123/213**) is also a 1935 Act relic.
[52:0:2] - Suspension of Fundamental Rights during emergency: borrowed from the **Weimar Constitution** (Germany).
[52:0:3] - **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** hoped Art. 356 would remain a "**dead letter**".
[52:0:4] # Three Emergencies Compared
[52:0:5] TABLE head: Feature | National (Art. 352) | President's Rule (Art. 356) | Financial (Art. 360)
  [52:0:5.0] Ground | War, external aggression, armed rebellion (also imminent danger); whole or part of India | President satisfied, on the Governor's report or otherwise, that the State cannot be governed per the Constitution | Financial stability or credit of India or any part threatened
  [52:0:5.1] Approval within | 1 month | 2 months | 2 months
  [52:0:5.2] Majority | Special majority in each House | Simple majority | Simple majority
  [52:0:5.3] Duration | 6 months at a time, extendable indefinitely | 6 months per approval, max 3 years | No maximum; continues till revoked
  [52:0:5.4] Revocation | President any time; must revoke if Lok Sabha disapproves by simple majority | President any time; no parliamentary approval needed | President any time
  [52:0:5.5] Instances | 1962, 1971, 1975 | First: Punjab, 1951 | Never declared
[52:0:6] Approval: 1 month (352) – 2 months (356, 360) – each approval of 352/356 lasts 6 months
## SUB 1 | National Emergency (article 352)
[52:1:0] # Grounds and Procedure
[52:1:1] - Proclaimed by the **President** when the security of India is threatened by war, external aggression or armed rebellion.
[52:1:2] - **44th CAA, 1978** replaced "**internal disturbance**" with "**armed rebellion**".
[52:1:3] - **Written advice of the Union Cabinet** mandatory – **Art. 352(3)** (44th CAA). The word "**Cabinet**" appears only once in the Constitution – in Art. 352(3).
[52:1:4] - Approval period cut from **two months to one month** by the 44th CAA; approval needs **special majority** (two-thirds present and voting + majority of total membership).
[52:1:5] - Notice by **1/10 of Lok Sabha members** – special sitting within **14 days** to consider disapproval.
[52:1:6] Written Cabinet advice was inserted as Art. 352(3), NOT a new "Article 352A".
[52:1:7] # Instances of National Emergency
[52:1:8] TABLE head: Instance | Date | Ground
  [52:1:8.0] 1st | 1962 | Chinese aggression (war)
  [52:1:8.1] 2nd | 1971 | Indo-Pak war (external aggression)
  [52:1:8.2] 3rd | 25 June 1975 | "Internal disturbance" – proclaimed by President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed
[52:1:9] - 1975 background: **Allahabad High Court** cancelled Indira Gandhi's 1971 election (**Indira Nehru Gandhi v Raj Narain**).
[52:1:10] - **25 June** observed as "**Samvidhan Hatya Diwas**" since 2025.
[52:1:11] - **Twenty Point Programme:** launched by the Government of India in **1975**, during the Emergency; restructured in **1982, 1986 and 2006** (TPP-2006, in force from 1 April 2007); monitored by the **Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation**.
## SUB 2 | Consequences of National Emergency
[52:2:0] # Centre-State Relations and Terms
[52:2:1] TABLE head: Area | Effect
  [52:2:1.0] Legislative | Parliament legislates on State List (Art. 250); such laws end 6 months after Emergency ends
  [52:2:1.1] Executive | Centre can direct States on any matter
  [52:2:1.2] Financial | President can modify distribution of revenues – till end of the financial year in which the Emergency ends
  [52:2:1.3] Terms | Lok Sabha (Art. 83(2)) and Assemblies extendable by law 1 year at a time, not beyond 6 months after Emergency ceases
[52:2:2] # Article 358 vs Article 359
[52:2:3] TABLE head: Basis | Article 358 | Article 359
  [52:2:3.0] What | Suspends the 6 freedoms of Art. 19 | Suspends the right to move courts for specified FRs
  [52:2:3.1] How | Automatic on proclamation | Needs a separate Presidential Order
  [52:2:3.2] Ground | Only war or external aggression – not armed rebellion | External emergency and armed rebellion
  [52:2:3.3] Limit | Only laws related to the Emergency protected (44th CAA) | Arts. 20 and 21 can never be suspended (44th CAA)
[52:2:4] - Acts done during the emergency under Art. 358 cannot be challenged even afterwards.
[52:2:5] - **Kesavananda Bharati (1973):** Parliament can amend FRs but not the **basic structure**.
[52:2:6] "Arts. 20 and 21 can be suspended under Art. 359" = WRONG.
## SUB 3 | President's Rule (article 356)
[52:3:0] # Grounds, Approval, Extension
[52:3:1] - On the Governor's report **or otherwise**; **no State Legislature consent** needed.
[52:3:2] - Approval within **2 months**; each approval lasts **6 months**; maximum **3 years**.
[52:3:3] - **Beyond 1 year** only if a **National Emergency is in operation AND the Election Commission certifies** that elections cannot be held (Art. 356(5)).
[52:3:4] Both conditions are needed – "AND", not "or".
[52:3:5] # Consequences
[52:3:6] TABLE head: Organ | Effect
  [52:3:6.0] Executive | President assumes State functions; Council of Ministers dismissed; Governor administers on his behalf
  [52:3:6.1] Legislature | Assembly suspended or dissolved; Parliament legislates (or delegates to the President)
  [52:3:6.2] Judiciary | High Court untouched – President cannot assume or suspend HC powers
[52:3:7] # Article 355 and Article 365
[52:3:8] - **Art. 355:** Union's duty to protect States from **external aggression and internal disturbance** and ensure constitutional governance – basis for Art. 356.
[52:3:9] - **Art. 365:** failure of a State to comply with **Union directions** – President may hold that constitutional machinery has failed (justifies President's Rule).
[52:3:10] - **'Constitutional emergency':** = Art. 356 **President's Rule**; salary cuts belong to Financial Emergency (Art. 360) ⚠ APPSC key (2002) gave salary cut.
[52:3:11] # Misuse Debate
[52:3:12] - Art. 356 is among the most **controversial** provisions (PYQ 2025); partisan Governor reports.
[52:3:13] - **ONOE** critique: deferred State polls could prolong PR, clashing with Art. 356(5) (9 Mar 2026).

## SUB 5 | S.r. Bommai v Union of India (1994)
[52:5:0] - **9-judge bench**; landmark on Art. 356.
[52:5:1] - **Judicial review:** proclamation is reviewable (mala fide or irrelevant grounds).
[52:5:2] - **Floor test:** majority tested on the **floor of the Assembly**; Governor's opinion not conclusive.
[52:5:3] - **Assembly** may only be suspended until Parliament approves; no arbitrary dissolution; court can **restore** a dismissed government.
[52:5:4] - **Secularism and federalism** are **basic features**; a State government acting against secularism can be dismissed.
