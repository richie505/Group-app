## SUB 5 | Notified Minorities and Welfare Schemes
[67:5:0] - **Section 2(c)** NCM Act 1992: minority = community notified by the Centre; criticised vs **T.M.A. Pai** State-level test.
[67:5:1] - **1993** notification: **Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Parsis**; **Jains** added **27 January 2014** (latest).
[67:5:2] - NCM Chairperson **Harjit Singh Grewal** – full 3-year term (Jul 2026).
[67:5:3] - **PMJVK** (formerly **MsDP**): revised 2022-23; all districts incl. 112 Aspirational.
[67:5:4] - Scholarships: NSP 2.0, DBT; **30% for girls**; post-matric income below ₹2.5 lakh.
[67:5:5] - **Jiyo Parsi** – Parsi population decline.
[67:5:6] - **Waqf (Amendment) Act 2025** – in force 8 Apr 2025; **UMEED** portal; audit for waqfs earning over **₹1 lakh**.
[67:5:7] - UCC: **Uttarakhand** since Jan 2025. **Census 2027**: six religions + "other"; parents' religion, birthplace.

## SUB 0 | Types of Migration and Theories
[69:0:0] - **Migration:** change of residence from one place to another; **Census** records it by **place of birth** (lifetime migrant) or **place of last residence**. **PLFS Usual Place of Residence**: **6 months**+.
[69:0:1] TABLE head: Basis | Types
  [69:0:1.0] Boundary | Internal (between villages, towns, districts, States) / international (legal, illegal immigrants, refugees)
  [69:0:1.1] Time | Temporary or seasonal / permanent; return
  [69:0:1.2] Distance / Number | Short or long / individual or mass
  [69:0:1.3] Cause | Economic, social, political, religious
  [69:0:1.4] Will | Voluntary (weighing push and pull) / forced (persecution, development-induced displacement, exploitation)
[69:0:2] - **Refugee**: fears persecution (race, religion, nationality, social/political group); cannot return.
[69:0:3] - **Step (stepwise) migration:** village → small town → larger town → **city**, in stages (Ravenstein's "migration by stages").
[69:0:4] - **Seasonal / circular migration** is the **dominant trend of migrant labour** in India – back-and-forth with the farm season.
[69:0:5] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent
  [69:0:5.0] Push-pull, "A Theory of Migration" (1966) | Everett S. Lee – migration as a permanent or semi-permanent change of residence
  [69:0:5.1] Laws of migration (1885) | E.G. Ravenstein
  [69:0:5.2] Mobility transition | Wilbur Zelinsky
  [69:0:5.3] Migration zone | Griffith Taylor
[69:0:6] - Demographic transition = **Notestein, Thompson**; diet theory = **Doubleday** – not Lee.

## SUB 1 | Streams, Causes and Census Data
[69:1:0] TABLE head: Stream | Feature
  [69:1:0.0] Rural → rural | Largest (2011); female, marriage; short-distance, intra-state
  [69:1:0.1] Rural → urban | Males, employment, inter-state; drives urbanisation
  [69:1:0.2] Urban → urban | Developed countries
  [69:1:0.3] Urban → rural | Smallest; return; counter-urbanisation
[69:1:1] - **Women migrate mainly for marriage**: PLFS 2020-21 female rate **47.9%**, **86.8%** for marriage; male rate **10.7%**, mainly for **employment** (22.8% in search of work; about half employment-related).
[69:1:2] - PLFS 2020-21 overall migration rate **28.9%** (rural 26.5%, urban 34.9%).
[69:1:3] TABLE head: Push (origin) | Pull (destination)
  [69:1:3.0] Low farm income, monsoon failure, drought, rural unemployment | Jobs, higher wages
  [69:1:3.1] Poverty, poor rural infrastructure | Education, health care
  [69:1:3.2] Conflict, political instability, disasters | Security, amenities
[69:1:4] - Adults most mobile; information networks (**Keralites, Sikhs**). Rural-urban: no rural jobs, urban labour demand, unbalanced development.
[69:1:5] - Migration is the main cause of the rural-urban growth gap (natural increase differs little).
[69:1:6] - **Census 2011:** **455.8 million** migrants (last residence) = **37.6%** of the population (nearest option "35%"); 141.2 million moved in 2001-11; **4.1 crore+** inter-state.
[69:1:7] - **Source States:** **UP, Bihar** lead. **Destination:** **Delhi, Maharashtra** lead (also TN, Gujarat, AP, Kerala); **Delhi's largest inflow is from Uttar Pradesh**.
[69:1:8] - **MoSPI/NSO Migration Survey (Jul 2026-Jun 2027)**: first since **2007-08**; short-term = **15 days-6 months** (earlier 1-6 months).
[69:1:9] > Exam angle: largest stream = rural-rural (not rural-urban); women = marriage; men = employment.

## SUB 2 | International Migration, Diaspora and the GCM
[69:2:0] - **GCM** (Global Compact for Safe, Orderly and Regular Migration): adopted at **Marrakech, Dec 2018**; endorsed by the **UNGA 19 Dec 2018**; **non-binding**; **international migration only** (not internal displacement); **not adopted by all** UN members (US and others stayed out) – so all three UPSC 2023 statements are wrong.
[69:2:1] - **World Migration Report 2026** – **IOM** (1951); **biennial since 2000**; 13th edition.
[69:2:2] - Top share: **UAE**; most migrants: **Europe**; top percentage: **Oceania**.
[69:2:3] - India: **largest diaspora (35 million+)**; ~3.5 million in UAE.
[69:2:4] - **Top remittance recipient: USD 137 billion (2024)** – only one above USD 100 billion; USD 135 billion FY 2024-25, more from skilled professionals.
[69:2:5] ~~ Sources: app notes 4:69 (sub 6); PMF Human & Economic Geography 01 p.68-69
## SUB 3 | Migration from Andhra Pradesh
[69:3:0] - Gulf source: drought-prone **Rayalaseema – Anantapur, Kadapa, Chittoor** (domestic servants, unskilled labour).
[69:3:1] - **Konaseema**: ~15,000 to GCC (Dec 2025); **70%+ women**, housemaids.
[69:3:2] - Seasonal labour: **Rayalaseema**, north-coastal AP; **Srikakulam fishermen → Gujarat coast**.
[69:3:3] - Telugu diaspora: **Gulf + USA**. **APNRT** (AP Non-Resident Telugu Society and **APNRT Trust**): nodal agency for non-resident Telugus – Gulf help desk, and pooling NRT contributions for schools, hospitals and village infrastructure.
[69:3:4] - CM: **Bhogapuram airport** will end Uttarandhra migration.

## SUB 5 | Laws and Schemes for Migrants
[69:5:0] - **Inter-State Migrant Workmen (RECS) Act, 1979**: establishments with **5+** migrants; **displacement allowance 50%** of monthly wages + journey allowance; merged into **OSH Code 2020**.
[69:5:1] - **OSH Code**: self-migrating workers, annual travel allowance, PDS and social security portability; four Labour Codes (29 laws), **Nov 2025**.
[69:5:2] - **e-Shram (2021)**: One-Stop-Solution, 15 schemes, **22 languages (BHASHINI)**.
[69:5:3] - **ONORC**: ration portability, all **36** States/UTs.
[69:5:4] - **PDOT (Jan 2018)**: MEA, ECR emigrants. **GKRA (2020)**: returnees.
[69:5:5] ~~ Sources: app notes 4:69 (sub 7)

## SUB 4 | Concept – Human Development, Growth and Capital
[72:4:0] - Human development = **enlargement of people's choices**; **economic growth** is quantitative (income), **development** is quantitative + qualitative.
[72:4:1] - Growth, social policy and poverty reduction reinforce human development **both ways** (investment in health and education raises employability; income funds human development) – CDS 2013 keyed **all four statements** correct.
[72:4:2] TABLE head: Capital | Example
  [72:4:2.0] Social capital | Mutual trust, harmony, networks in society
  [72:4:2.1] Human capital | Literacy, skills, health
  [72:4:2.2] Physical capital | Buildings, infrastructure, machines
