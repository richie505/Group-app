## SUB 0 | Types of Migration and Theories
[0:0] - **Migration:** change of residence from one place to another; **Census** records it by **place of birth** (lifetime migrant) or **place of last residence**. **PLFS Usual Place of Residence**: **6 months**+.
[0:1] TABLE head: Basis | Types
  [0:1.0] Boundary | Internal (between villages, towns, districts, States) / international (legal, illegal immigrants, refugees)
  [0:1.1] Time | Temporary or seasonal / permanent; return
  [0:1.2] Distance / Number | Short or long / individual or mass
  [0:1.3] Cause | Economic, social, political, religious
  [0:1.4] Will | Voluntary (weighing push and pull) / forced (persecution, development-induced displacement, exploitation)
[0:2] - **Refugee**: fears persecution (race, religion, nationality, social/political group); cannot return.
[0:3] - **Step (stepwise) migration:** village → small town → larger town → **city**, in stages (Ravenstein's "migration by stages").
[0:4] - **Seasonal / circular migration** is the **dominant trend of migrant labour** in India – back-and-forth with the farm season.
[0:5] TABLE head: Theory | Proponent
  [0:5.0] Push-pull, "A Theory of Migration" (1966) | Everett S. Lee – migration as a permanent or semi-permanent change of residence
  [0:5.1] Laws of migration (1885) | E.G. Ravenstein
  [0:5.2] Mobility transition | Wilbur Zelinsky
  [0:5.3] Migration zone | Griffith Taylor
[0:6] - Demographic transition = **Notestein, Thompson**; diet theory = **Doubleday** – not Lee.
[0:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 13 p.1-3 (book p.169-171); PMF Human & Economic Geography 01 p.66-67; app notes 4:69 (sub 0, 3); step migration: general knowledge, not checked
## SUB 1 | Streams, Causes and Census Data
[1:0] TABLE head: Stream | Feature
  [1:0.0] Rural → rural | Largest (2011); female, marriage; short-distance, intra-state
  [1:0.1] Rural → urban | Males, employment, inter-state; drives urbanisation
  [1:0.2] Urban → urban | Developed countries
  [1:0.3] Urban → rural | Smallest; return; counter-urbanisation
[1:1] - **Women migrate mainly for marriage**: PLFS 2020-21 female rate **47.9%**, **86.8%** for marriage; male rate **10.7%**, mainly for **employment** (22.8% in search of work; about half employment-related).
[1:2] - PLFS 2020-21 overall migration rate **28.9%** (rural 26.5%, urban 34.9%).
[1:3] TABLE head: Push (origin) | Pull (destination)
  [1:3.0] Low farm income, monsoon failure, drought, rural unemployment | Jobs, higher wages
  [1:3.1] Poverty, poor rural infrastructure | Education, health care
  [1:3.2] Conflict, political instability, disasters | Security, amenities
[1:4] - Adults most mobile; information networks (**Keralites, Sikhs**). Rural-urban: no rural jobs, urban labour demand, unbalanced development.
[1:5] - Migration is the main cause of the rural-urban growth gap (natural increase differs little).
[1:6] - **Census 2011:** **455.8 million** migrants (last residence) = **37.6%** of the population (nearest option "35%"); 141.2 million moved in 2001-11; **4.1 crore+** inter-state.
[1:7] - **Source States:** **UP, Bihar** lead. **Destination:** **Delhi, Maharashtra** lead (also TN, Gujarat, AP, Kerala); **Delhi's largest inflow is from Uttar Pradesh**.
[1:8] - **MoSPI/NSO Migration Survey (Jul 2026-Jun 2027)**: first since **2007-08**; short-term = **15 days-6 months** (earlier 1-6 months).
[1:9] > Exam angle: largest stream = rural-rural (not rural-urban); women = marriage; men = employment.

## SUB 2 | International Migration, Diaspora and the GCM
[2:0] - **GCM** (Global Compact for Safe, Orderly and Regular Migration): adopted at **Marrakech, Dec 2018**; endorsed by the **UNGA 19 Dec 2018**; **non-binding**; **international migration only** (not internal displacement); **not adopted by all** UN members (US and others stayed out) – so all three UPSC 2023 statements are wrong.
[2:1] - **World Migration Report 2026** – **IOM** (1951); **biennial since 2000**; 13th edition.
[2:2] - Top share: **UAE**; most migrants: **Europe**; top percentage: **Oceania**.
[2:3] - India: **largest diaspora (35 million+)**; ~3.5 million in UAE.
[2:4] - **Top remittance recipient: USD 137 billion (2024)** – only one above USD 100 billion; USD 135 billion FY 2024-25, more from skilled professionals.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: app notes 4:69 (sub 6); PMF Human & Economic Geography 01 p.68-69
## SUB 3 | Migration from Andhra Pradesh
[3:0] - Gulf source: drought-prone **Rayalaseema – Anantapur, Kadapa, Chittoor** (domestic servants, unskilled labour).
[3:1] - **Konaseema**: ~15,000 to GCC (Dec 2025); **70%+ women**, housemaids.
[3:2] - Seasonal labour: **Rayalaseema**, north-coastal AP; **Srikakulam fishermen → Gujarat coast**.
[3:3] - Telugu diaspora: **Gulf + USA**. **APNRT** (AP Non-Resident Telugu Society and **APNRT Trust**): nodal agency for non-resident Telugus – Gulf help desk, and pooling NRT contributions for schools, hospitals and village infrastructure.
[3:4] - CM: **Bhogapuram airport** will end Uttarandhra migration.

## SUB 5 | Laws and Schemes for Migrants
[5:0] - **Inter-State Migrant Workmen (RECS) Act, 1979**: establishments with **5+** migrants; **displacement allowance 50%** of monthly wages + journey allowance; merged into **OSH Code 2020**.
[5:1] - **OSH Code**: self-migrating workers, annual travel allowance, PDS and social security portability; four Labour Codes (29 laws), **Nov 2025**.
[5:2] - **e-Shram (2021)**: One-Stop-Solution, 15 schemes, **22 languages (BHASHINI)**.
[5:3] - **ONORC**: ration portability, all **36** States/UTs.
[5:4] - **PDOT (Jan 2018)**: MEA, ECR emigrants. **GKRA (2020)**: returnees.
[5:5] ~~ Sources: app notes 4:69 (sub 7)
