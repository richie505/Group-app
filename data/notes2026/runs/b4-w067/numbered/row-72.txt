## SUB 1 | HDI – Dimensions, Indicators and Categories
[1:0] # Three dimensions
[1:1] TABLE head: Dimension | Indicator | Goalposts
  [1:1.0] Long and healthy life | Life expectancy at birth | 20–85 years
  [1:1.1] Knowledge | Mean years of schooling (adults 25+) + expected years of schooling | MYS 0–15; EYS 0–18
  [1:1.2] Decent standard of living | GNI per capita (PPP, log) | $75,000 cap
[1:2] - HDI = **geometric mean** of the three indices since **2010** (earlier arithmetic mean; literacy and gross enrolment then gave way to MYS and EYS).
[1:3] - **NOT in HDI:** **infant mortality rate**, **military expenditure**, inequality, poverty, gender gaps, sustainability, political freedom, MPI.
[1:4] # Categories
[1:5] TABLE head: Category | HDI value (since 2010)
  [1:5.0] Very High | 0.800+
  [1:5.1] High | 0.700–0.799
  [1:5.2] Medium | 0.550–0.699
  [1:5.3] Low | below 0.550
[1:6] - Before 2010 there were three groups and **High human development = 0.800 and above** (the BPSC 2011 answer).
[1:7] > Exam angle: "not a component" – military expenditure, infant mortality; "High from" – 0.700 now, 0.800 in the old three-tier scheme.

[2:0] TABLE head: Person / body | Role
  [2:0.0] UNDP (not World Bank, FAO, WHO) | Publishes the global HDR and HDI; first HDR 1990
  [2:0.1] Mahbub ul Haq | Pakistani economist; conceived the HDR
  [2:0.2] Amartya Sen | Co-designed the HDI with Haq; capability approach

[2:3] - **Amartya Sen:** capabilities = **substantive freedoms** to lead lives people value; "**Development as Freedom**" (**1999**); **Nobel in Economics 1998**.

## SUB 3 | National Human Development Reports, State HDI and MPI
[3:0] # NHDR
[3:1] - **First NHDR of India: 2001** (Planning Commission), released **April 2002** – **Kerala** at the top (highest HDI and highest **life expectancy**); **Bihar** lowest.
[3:2] - NHDR 2001: India's HDI **0.472** (0.381 in 1991); **Andhra Pradesh 10th** among States, below the national average.
[3:3] - **India Human Development Report 2011:** **Institute of Applied Manpower Research** (Planning Commission), Oct 2011; Kerala first, then Delhi, Himachal Pradesh, Goa.
[3:4] - Sub-national HDI series: **Kerala and Goa** above 0.86; **Bihar lowest 0.650** (India 0.732 in that series – do not mix with UNDP's 0.685).
[3:5] # Multidimensional poverty
[3:6] - **NITI Aayog (Jan 2024):** MPI headcount **29.17% (2013-14) → 11.28% (2022-23)**; **24.82 crore** moved out; biggest fall **Uttar Pradesh** (5.94 crore), then Bihar, MP. **12 indicators**, **Alkire-Foster** method.
[3:7] - Global MPI: **UNDP + OPHI** (Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative).
[3:8] - MPI extremes: **Kerala 0.002** vs Bihar about 0.16.
[3:9] - **AP:** MPI **0.053 (2015-16) → 0.025 (2019-21)**; headcount **6.06%** vs national **14.96%**; lowest district **West Godavari (2.42%)**.

## SUB 4 | Related Indices
[4:0] TABLE head: Index | Measures
  [4:0.0] IHDI | HDI discounted for inequality; lower than HDI wherever inequality exists – the gap is the loss due to inequality
  [4:0.1] GDI | Female-to-male HDI ratio; near 1 = parity
  [4:0.2] GII | Reproductive health, empowerment, labour market; 0 = equality, 1 = worst
  [4:0.3] MPI | Deprivations in health, education, living standards (UNDP-OPHI)
  [4:0.4] PQLI | Literacy, infant mortality, life expectancy (Morris; pre-HDI)
  [4:0.5] Human Poverty Index | Forerunner of the MPI
[4:1] - HDI criticisms: ignores inequality, sustainability, gender and political freedom.

## SUB 5 | Concept – Human Development, Growth and Capital
[5:0] - Human development = **enlargement of people's choices**; **economic growth** is quantitative (income), **development** is quantitative + qualitative.
[5:1] - Growth, social policy and poverty reduction reinforce human development **both ways** (investment in health and education raises employability; income funds human development) – CDS 2013 keyed **all four statements** correct.
[5:2] TABLE head: Capital | Example
  [5:2.0] Social capital | Mutual trust, harmony, networks in society
  [5:2.1] Human capital | Literacy, skills, health
  [5:2.2] Physical capital | Buildings, infrastructure, machines
