=== NOTES 1:36 | Ashokan Inscriptions – Rock Edicts, Pillar Edicts, Sites, Scripts, James Prinsep
## SUB 0 | The Name 'Ashoka', His Titles and the Decipherment
[36:0:0] # Titles in the edicts
[36:0:1] - **Usual title:** **Devanampiya Piyadasi** – Devanampiya (Devanampriya) = "**Beloved of the Gods**"; Piyadasi (**Priyadarshi**) = "of pleasing appearance" (IAS 1995; MPPSC 2015; APPSC G1) – not Chakravarti, Dharmadeva or Dharmakirti; not a title of Samudragupta, Kanishka or Harsha.
[36:0:2] - **Other names:** **Buddhashakya** – Maski; **Dharmasoka** – Sarnath inscription; **Ashokavardhana** – Puranas.
[36:0:3] - **Bhabru (Bairat):** calls himself "**Piyadasi Raja**" of **Magadha** – the only edict where he declares himself king of Magadha (State PSC; it is a minor rock edict, though the paper called it a "pillar"); Maski, Rummindei and the Queen's edict are distractors.
[36:0:4] # Edicts that name "Ashoka"
[36:0:5] TABLE head: Edict | Location | Wording
  [36:0:5.0] Maski (found 1915) | Raichur, Karnataka | "Devanampiya Asoka" – the edict that proved Piyadasi = Ashoka
  [36:0:5.1] Gujarra | Datia district, Madhya Pradesh (on the Ujjain–Bharuch road) | "Devanampiya Piyadasi Asoka"
  [36:0:5.2] Nittur, Udegolam | Ballari, Karnataka | Also carry the name
  [36:0:5.3] Kanaganahalli | Near Sannati, Kalaburagi, Karnataka, on the Bhima | Stone portrait of Ashoka with his queens, label "Ranyo Asoko" (King Ashoka) – the only inscribed portrait
[36:0:6] - **Personal name asked:** **Maski** (UPSC 1997 ×2; RAS 2007) and **Gujarra** (UPPCS 2015). Kalsi, Rummindei, the Special Kalinga Edict, Bhabru, RE XIII, Ahraura, Brahmagiri and Sarnath use only the titles.
[36:0:7] - **Ranyo Ashoka with his portrait:** **Kanaganahalli** (UPSC 2019 ×2) – Ashoka is depicted at Sanchi and Amaravati but not named; Shahbazgarhi and Sohgaura are distractors.
[36:0:8] # Decipherment and firsts
[36:0:9] - **James Prinsep** (British, "Princep") **deciphered Brahmi and Kharosthi in 1837** – the first scholar to read the Ashokan edicts and the first Briton to study them (UPPCS 2010; CGPSC 2003; IAS 2016). Not Buhler, Max Muller, William Jones, Cunningham.
[36:0:10] - **First edicts read:** the **Delhi-Topra** pillar edicts. George Turnour later linked "Devanampiya Piyadasi" with Ashoka from the Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa.
[36:0:11] - **First Indian ruler to speak directly to his people through inscriptions** – on **natural rocks and polished pillars**, in **Prakrit**: **Ashoka** (APPSC; AP Hostel Welfare Officer) – not Chandragupta Maurya, Kharavela, Gautamiputra Satakarni or the Guptas.
[36:0:12] - **Form borrowed from Persia:** the third-person opening and the word "**lipi/dipi**" follow the inscriptions of **Darius**. Ashoka called his edicts **Dhammalipi**.
[36:0:13] > Exam angle: "name Ashoka" → **Maski / Gujarra**; "Ranyo Ashoka + portrait" → **Kanaganahalli**; "first deciphered" → **James Prinsep, 1837**.

## SUB 1 | Major Rock Edicts – Number and Subject
[36:1:0] TABLE head: RE | Subject
  [36:1:0.0] I | Ban on animal sacrifice and on festive gatherings (samaja); killing in the royal kitchen cut down
  [36:1:0.1] II | Medical care for men and animals, herbs and trees; names the Cholas, Pandyas, Satiyaputtas, Keralaputtas, Tamraparni (Sri Lanka) and the Yavana king Antiyoka
  [36:1:0.2] III | Quinquennial (five-yearly) tours of rajukas, pradeshikas and yuktas (12th year)
  [36:1:0.3] IV | Dhammaghosha in place of Bherighosha
  [36:1:0.4] V | Dhamma Mahamattas appointed, 13 years after consecration; mentions his brothers and sisters
  [36:1:0.5] VI | Pativedakas may report to the king at all times
  [36:1:0.6] VIII | Dhammayatras replace royal pleasure tours; the first to Bodh Gaya
  [36:1:0.7] XII | Religious tolerance – respect for all sects, restraint in praising one's own sect
  [36:1:0.8] XIII | Kalinga War, remorse, Dhammavijaya; five Greek kings; the Andhras named
  [36:1:0.9] XIV | Summary – why and how the edicts were engraved
[36:1:1] - **RE I:** bans animal sacrifice at festive gatherings (RAS 2013) – not PE V, RE IX or XI.
[36:1:2] - **RE II:** names the **South Indian kingdoms** (UPPCS Mains 2016). **Sangam kingdoms: RE II and XIII** (UPSC 1998 ×2).
[36:1:3] - **RE XII:** "Whosoever praises his religious sect or blames other sects … injures his own sect very severely" = **Ashoka** (UPSC 2020 ×2); the edict of **religious tolerance / synthesis** (UP Lower 2008; UPPCS 2022) – not RE XIII, PE VII or Bhabru.
[36:1:4] - **RE XIII:** the **account of the Kalinga War**, its victory and casualties (UPPSC 2016; IAS 1998) – not Rummindei, Minor RE I or Hiuen Tsang.
[36:1:5] - **Andhras:** the **definite mention of the Andhras** is in **RE XIII** (APPSC Asst. BC & Tribal Welfare Officer 2017 ×2) – listed (with the Palidas) among peoples within Ashoka's domain who follow his Dhamma. There is no 15th RE (only 14).
[36:1:6] - **Prisoners:** released on **every anniversary of his coronation (abhisheka)** – 25 releases in 26 years (PE V) (APPSC G1) – not his birth, Kalinga or conversion.
[36:1:7] - **Sri Lanka (Tamraparni)** was a neighbour named in RE II, **not part of the empire** (State PSC); Afghanistan, Bihar and Kalinga were.
[36:1:8] - **What the inscriptions tell:** his life (Kalinga), internal policy (officials, Dhamma) and foreign policy (Greek kings, southern kingdoms) – **all of these** (SSC 2018).
[36:1:9] > Exam angle: **I** sacrifice, **II** medical care + south, **V** Mahamattas, **XII** tolerance, **XIII** Kalinga + Greeks + Andhras.
[36:1:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.44-45; NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.5; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.124-125; app notes 1:36 (sub 3)
[36:1:11] - **Number:** Ashoka issued **14 Major Rock Edicts**, found at 10 places, mostly as a full set of 14.
## SUB 2 | Scripts and Languages
[36:2:0] TABLE head: Script | Language | Where
  [36:2:0.0] Brahmi (left to right) | Prakrit | Most edicts – Ganga plain, Deccan, Andhra (Erragudi, Rajula Mandagiri), Girnar, Kalsi
  [36:2:0.1] Kharosthi (right to left) | Prakrit | North-west only: Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra (Pakistan)
  [36:2:0.2] Greek | Greek | Kandahar (Afghanistan)
  [36:2:0.3] Aramaic | Aramaic | Taxila, Laghman, Kandahar
  [36:2:0.4] Greek + Aramaic (bilingual) | – | Kandahar (Shar-i-Kuna)
[36:2:1] - **Script of most edicts:** **Brahmi** (APPSC FRO) – not Devanagari, Kharosthi or Sharada. **Language:** **Prakrit** (State PSC) – not Sanskrit, Pali or Hindi.
[36:2:2] - **Kharosthi:** written **right to left** (UPSC 1997), derived from **Aramaic**; the Kharosthi edict among Kalsi, Girnar, Shahbazgarhi, Meerut is **Shahbazgarhi** (CGPSC 2011).
[36:2:3] - **APPSC G1 2023 statements:** I. Brahmi, Kharosthi, Greek, Aramaic ✓; II. name Ashoka only at Maski and Gujarra ✓ (the key's view); III. RE XIII names five Greek rulers ✓; IV. the war of succession in RE IV and V ✗ (RE V only mentions his brothers' families) → **I, II and III**.
[36:2:4]   - **⚠ Disputed:** strictly the name also occurs at **Nittur and Udegolam** (PMF), so II is loose; the key still takes it as correct.
[36:2:5] - **Brahmi** is the source of the **Telugu** script (it was not used only for Telugu). No edict names the Four Noble Truths or Nibbana; the edicts never mention varna or sati.
[36:2:6] - Inscriptions fall into three classes: **rock, pillar and cave** (Barabar) inscriptions.
[36:2:7] > Exam angle: Kharosthi = **right to left, NW, Shahbazgarhi/Mansehra**; most edicts = **Prakrit in Brahmi**.
[36:2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.3; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.122, p.125-126; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.43; app notes 1:36 (sub 1)
## SUB 3 | Pillar Edicts and the Pillars
[36:3:0] TABLE head: Pillar | Content
  [36:3:0.0] Rummindei (Lumbini), Nepal | Visit 20 years after consecration; marks the Buddha's birthplace; Lumbini freed from bali, bhaga cut from 1/6 to 1/8
  [36:3:0.1] Nigali Sagar, Nepal | Kanakamuni stupa enlarged (14th year)
  [36:3:0.2] Sarnath | Schism edict; lion capital = national emblem
  [36:3:0.3] Allahabad-Kosam | PE I–VI, Schism and Queen's edict (gifts of Karuvaki); later Samudragupta's prashasti and Jahangir's inscription
  [36:3:0.4] Delhi-Topra | The only pillar with PE VII – rajukas, Ajivikas, Nirgranthas; Dhamma spreads better by persuasion than by law
  [36:3:0.5] Lauriya Nandangarh | West Champaran, Bihar; single lion capital; PE I–VI
[36:3:1] - **Lumbini tax cut to 1/8:** **Rummindei pillar** (APPSC G2 2024) – not Maski, Kalsi or Vaishali.
[36:3:2] - **Confirms the Buddha was born at Lumbini:** **Rummindei** (UPUDA 2010) – not Basarh, Nigali Sagar, Rampurwa; so the dynasty whose inscription marks Lumbini = **Mauryan** (UPUDA 2006).
[36:3:3] - **Sarnath pillar:** built by **Ashoka** (UP Lower 2008) – not Harsha, the Buddha or Kanishka.
[36:3:4] - **Topra and Meerut pillars** were brought to Delhi by **Firuz Shah Tughluq** (UPPCS 1996); the Kosam pillar went to the Allahabad fort traditionally under **Akbar**.
[36:3:5] - **PE II** defines Dhamma ("few faults, many good deeds"); **PE IV** (26th year) – rajukas' judicial power, 3-day respite; **PE V** – no cattle branding, prisoner releases.
[36:3:6] - **Schism edict:** dissenting monks and nuns expelled – **Sarnath, Sanchi, Kaushambi**.
[36:3:7] - Pillars cluster in the **Ganga plain** – river transport for the heavy Chunar sandstone monoliths.
[36:3:8] > Exam angle: Rummindei = **pillar in Nepal, Lumbini, 1/8**; Firuz Shah = **Topra + Meerut**.
[36:3:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.43, p.45; NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.3-4; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.126-127; app notes 1:36 (sub 5)
## SUB 4 | Ashokan Edicts in Andhra and the Minor Rock Edicts
[36:4:0] TABLE head: Site | Location | Content
  [36:4:0.0] Erragudi (Yerragudi / Jonnagiri) | Pattikonda, Kurnool district, AP | 14 Major RE + Minor RE; teachers must instil Dhamma in their pupils
  [36:4:0.1] Rajula Mandagiri | Kurnool district, AP | Minor RE only; guru–disciple relationship; proof of Mauryan rule in Andhra
  [36:4:0.2] Maski | Raichur, Karnataka | Name "Ashoka"
  [36:4:0.3] Brahmagiri, Siddapura, Jatinga-Rameshwara | Chitradurga, Karnataka | Minor RE I and II – southern limit
  [36:4:0.4] Bhabru (Bairat) | Rajasthan | Faith in the Buddha, Dhamma and Sangha; recommends Buddhist texts
[36:4:1] - **Teachers to inculcate religious thought in disciples:** **Erragudi** (APPSC G2 2008; AP History PYQ) – Bhattiprolu and Dhanyakataka are not Ashokan sites.
[36:4:2]   - **⚠ Disputed:** most books and keys give **Erragudi**; strictly **Rajula Mandagiri** carries the same Minor RE II text on gurus and disciples (AP History APPSC 01).
[36:4:3] - **Ashoka's inscriptions in AP:** **Erragudi** (AP History PYQ) – not Nagarjuna Hill, Amaravati or Bhattiprolu.
[36:4:4] - **Earliest inscriptions in Andhra:** **Ashoka's** (AP History PYQ) – in **Prakrit, Brahmi**; they show Mauryan rule reached Rayalaseema. Nashik, Hathigumpha and the Rakshasa caves are later.
[36:4:5] - **Erragudi's district:** **Kurnool** (Pattikonda mandal; ASI site on the Gooty–Pattikonda road) – not Nandyal.
[36:4:6] - **Connection with Buddhism:** **Minor Rock Edict I** – Ashoka calls himself an **upasaka** who drew closer to the Sangha (NDA 2017); RE XIII, RE VI and PE IV are distractors. Minor edicts are the earliest Ashokan records and are densest in the Andhra–Karnataka area.
[36:4:7] > Exam angle: AP edict sites = **Erragudi and Rajula Mandagiri (Kurnool)**; Bhattiprolu = Kubiraka casket, not Ashoka.

[36:5:1] - **Kalsi:** famous for Ashoka's rock edicts (UP Lower 2008); the Ashokan edict of **Uttarakhand** (UKPSC 2005; UK UDA 2007) – not Devprayag, Kedarnath, Rishikesh, Nainital, Pauri or Tehri.
[36:5:2] - **UPSC 2022 pairs:** Dhauli–Odisha ✓, Erragudi–AP ✓, Jaugada–MP ✗ (Odisha), Kalsi–Karnataka ✗ (Uttarakhand) → **only two pairs**.
[36:5:3] - **Separate Kalinga Edicts** (Dhauli, Jaugada) replace RE XI–XIII there; addressed to officers of **Tosali and Samapa**; "**All men are my children**".
[36:5:4] - **Not Ashokan:** **Besnagar** – Heliodorus pillar. Rummindei is a pillar, not a Major RE site. The spread of edict sites is the main evidence for the **extent of the empire**; Kashmir comes through Kalhana's Rajatarangini (son **Jalauka**).
[36:5:5] > Exam angle: Kalsi = **Uttarakhand**; Jaugada and Dhauli = **Odisha**; Girnar = **Gujarat**.

## SUB 4 | Saptanga Theory and the Nature of the State

  [37:4:0.0] 1 | Swami | King (comes first)

## SUB 0 | Stupas – Sanchi, Bharhut and the Stupa Cult
[38:0:0] - **Stupa cult:** the idea predates Buddhism (Vedic burial mounds); **Ashoka popularised it** – by tradition he redistributed the relics of the **8** original stupas into **84,000**. Stupas held relics of the Buddha and monks and stood in towns **near trade routes**, funded by merchants and guilds – **not in rural areas** (CDS 2018).
[38:0:1] - **Sanchi Stupa was built by Ashoka** (brick core) (MPPSC 1995; UPPCS 1991) – not Chandragupta, Kautilya, Bimbisara, Harsha or Pushyamitra.
[38:0:2] TABLE head: Stupa | Fact
  [38:0:2.0] Sanchi (ancient Kakanaya / Kaknam) | Raisen district, MP, near Vidisha; Ashoka's brick core → Sunga stone casing, railings and toranas → Satavahana carving of gateways → Gupta shrines; considered the best stupa (UPPCS 2008); oldest stone structure in India among Ajanta, Konark, Brihadeeswara
  [38:0:2.1] Sanchi No. 2 / No. 3 | Relics of 10 monks (incl. Dhamma Mahamatras) / Sariputta and Mahamoggallana
  [38:0:2.2] Bharhut | Satna, MP; Sunga, c. 150 BCE; found by Cunningham
  [38:0:2.3] Dharmarajika | Sarnath and Taxila
  [38:0:2.4] Dhamek | Sarnath; present form Gupta (c. 500 CE)
  [38:0:2.5] Kanakamuni | Niglihawa (Nepal); enlarged in Ashoka's 14th year
  [38:0:2.6] Ranabhar | Kushinagar; built by the Mallas

[38:1:0] - **Megasthenes** (envoy of **Seleucus**, author of **Indica**) divided society into **seven classes** (State PSC): philosophers, farmers, herdsmen/hunters, artisans and traders, soldiers, overseers/spies, councillors and assessors.

=== NOTES 1:39 | Decline of the Mauryan Empire – Competing Explanations
## SUB 0 | Kharavela of Kalinga – the Hathigumpha Inscription
[39:0:0] - **Hathigumpha inscription** (2nd–1st c. BCE): the year-by-year record of **Kharavela** of Kalinga (UPPSC 2018) – not Ashoka, Harsha or Kanishka; it is not the Girnar, Junagadh or Sarnath inscription.
[39:0:1] - **Place:** **Udayagiri** hill near Bhubaneswar, Odisha – **not Khandagiri**.
[39:0:2] - **Udayagiri and Khandagiri:** both natural and artificial caves; **Udayagiri 18, Khandagiri 15** caves (CGPSC 2021: statements i and ii correct).
[39:0:3] - **Dynasty:** **Mahameghavahana** family of the **Chedi** line (UPPCS 2018 keys Mahameghavahana; Chedi names the same line).
[39:0:4] - **Religion:** a **Jaina** – the king who "heavily leaned towards Jainism" (not Dasharatha, Brihadratha or Huvishka); gave caves to Jain monks; brought back from Magadha the **Jina image** taken by the Nandas.
[39:0:5] - **Campaigns:** sent an army west **ignoring Satakarni**; raided Magadha; southern victories; canal work begun by a Nanda king.
[39:0:6] - **Krishna river = "Kannabenna"** (Kanhabemna) in the Hathigumpha inscription (APPSC AP History).
[39:0:7] - **Other pairs:** Nasik – Gautami Balashri; **Nanaghat – Naganika**; Junagadh – Rudradaman.
[39:0:8] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian History 01 p.7; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.144; app notes 1:39 (sub 5)
## SUB 1 | End of the Dynasty and the Successors
[39:1:0] - **Brihadratha**, the last Maurya and **7th king after Ashoka**, was killed by his **senani Pushyamitra Sunga** at an **army review** – a military coup.
[39:1:1] - **Immediate successors of the Mauryas in Magadha: the Sungas** (APPSC G2 2003, 2008) – not Kushanas, Pandyas or Satavahanas; Sunga dynasty founded **187 BC** (NextGen; PMF: 185 BCE).
[39:1:2] - **In the Deccan the Mauryas were succeeded by the Satavahanas** (APPSC ASO) – earlier Mauryan feudatories; founder **Simuka (Srimukha)**; the Puranas call them **Andhra-bhrityas**.
[39:1:3] - **Pushyamitra:** Brahmana of the **Bharadvaja gotra**, **Senapati**; two Ashvamedhas (**Ayodhya inscription of Dhanadeva**); **Malavikagnimitram** – grandson **Vasumitra** beat a Yavana army on the Sindhu.
[39:1:4] - **Kanvas:** **Vasudeva Kanva** killed the last Sunga **Devabhuti**; **four** kings (c. 73–28 BC); the last, **Susharman**, fell to the **Satavahanas**, not Menander.
[39:1:5] - **Persecution debate:** the Divyavadana calls Pushyamitra a persecutor, yet **Sanchi and Bharhut** grew under the Sungas.
[39:1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.45; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.139-140; app notes 1:39 (sub 3, 4)
## SUB 2 | Theories of Decline → Historian
[39:2:0] TABLE head: Theory | Historian | Core argument
  [39:2:0.0] Brahmanical reaction | H.P. Shastri | Pro-Buddhist policy and ban on sacrifices angered Brahmins; Sungas and Kanvas were Brahmins
  [39:2:0.1] Ashoka's pacifism | H.C. Raychaudhuri | Non-violence after Kalinga dulled the army
  [39:2:0.2] Financial crisis | D.D. Kosambi | Army, bureaucracy and gifts to the Sangha emptied the treasury; heavy taxes even on actors and prostitutes
  [39:2:0.3] Over-centralisation, no national consciousness | Romila Thapar | Rule depended on the king; weak successors
  [39:2:0.4] Oppressive provincial rule | Divyavadana evidence | Revolts at Taxila against wicked officials
[39:2:1] - **Financial-crisis evidence:** spending on **Dhamma missions** and the **Sangha**; **debasement of later punch-marked silver coins**; gold images melted.
[39:2:2] - **Other factors:** spread of iron and material culture to outlying areas; neglect of the **north-west frontier** while China built the Great Wall (c. 220 BC).
[39:2:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.140-141; app notes 1:39 (sub 0)

[39:3:0] - **Later Mauryas:** Kunala, Dasharatha, **Samprati** (Jain patron), Shalishuka, Devavarman, Shatadhanvan, **Brihadratha** – the Vishnu Purana lists seven successors.

## SUB 1 | Pushyamitra Sunga – Coup, Ashvamedhas and Buddhism
[41:1:0] - **Mauryan commander-in-chief (senani)**, a **Brahmin**; killed Brihadratha while showing him the army (Harshacharita) – the last Mauryan king was killed by **Pushyamitra Shunga** (APPSC 2023), not Dattamitra, Vibhuti or Indrabhattarika.
[41:1:1] - **Ancestors of the Sungas came from Ujjain** (MPPSC 2018); heartland **Magadha, Malwa–Vidisha, Narmada valley**.
[41:1:2] - **Two Ashvamedhas** – recorded in the **Ayodhya inscription of Dhanadeva** (UPPCS 2018) – not Sarnath, Besnagar or Hathigumpha; not three.
[41:1:3] - **Wars:** beat the Indo-Greeks (Demetrius), fought the Deccan powers and **Kharavela** of Kalinga.
[41:1:4] - **Buddhism:** Pushyamitra **did not contribute to the expansion of Buddhism** (CGPSC 2018) – unlike Ashoka, Kanishka and Harsha; the **Divyavadana** and **Taranatha** call him a persecutor, yet Bharhut and Sanchi were renovated under him (Romila Thapar, Lamotte: exaggerated). Hostility was probably aimed at monasteries allied with the **Indo-Greeks**.
[41:1:5] - **Bodhi Tree:** tradition links its destruction to Pushyamitra and later **Shashanka of Gauda**.
[41:1:6] - **Society:** feudatories rose – **Bhojas, Maharathis, Rastrikas**; classical **Sanskrit** revived; **Manusmriti** took shape, classing foreigners as **Vratya Kshatriyas**; Brahmanical revival with the rise of Bhagavatism and stone Buddhist art.

=== NOTES 1:42 | Indo-greeks – Menander, Milindapanho and Indo-greek Coinage
## SUB 0 | Milindapanho – Menander and Nagasena
[42:0:0] # The text
[42:0:1] - **Milindapanho (Milinda-Panha, "Questions of Milinda"):** a **Pali** Buddhist text – a dialogue on Buddhist doctrine between the Indo-Greek king **Milinda = Menander** and the Buddhist monk **Nagasena** (UPSC 1997 ×2; UPPCS 2023; UKPCS 2012).
[42:0:2] - **⚠ Trap:** the monk is **Nagasena**, not Nagarjuna (later Mahayana Madhyamika thinker), Nagabhatta (Pratihara king) or Kumarila Bhatta (Mimamsa philosopher); the text is in **Pali**, not Sanskrit.
[42:0:3] - **Which king it throws light on:** **Menander** – not Demetrius, Diodotus II or Strato I (UKPCS 2012).
[42:0:4] - **Agriculture:** the Milindapanho explains **eight phases of agricultural work** (UKPCS 2021); Avadanashataka, Aryamanjushrimulakalpa and Dipavamsa are distractors.
[42:0:5] # Menander (Milinda), c. 165-145 BCE
[42:0:6] - **Greatest Indo-Greek:** capital **Sakala (Sialkot)**, not Taxila; the only Indo-Greek king named in Indian sources; turned **Buddhist** under Nagasena.
[42:0:7] - **Coins:** early coins carry Greek deities; later ones Buddhist motifs – **elephant, bull, wheel of Dharma**.
[42:0:8] - **Bairat (Rajasthan):** a hoard of **16 coins of Menander** (RAS 2018); Nagari, Rairh and Nagar are distractors.
[42:0:9] - **Raids:** invaded the Ganga-Yamuna doab but could not hold it against the Shungas.
[42:0:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.146-147; NextGen Indian History APPSC 02 p.6; Rocket Indian History 01 p.40, p.55; app notes 1:42 (sub 2)
## SUB 1 | Indo-Greek Coins – Firsts, Deities and Metals
[42:1:0] # Firsts
[42:1:1] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [42:1:1.0] Coins definitely attributed to kings | First issued by the Indo-Greeks – first inscribed coins with names and portraits of kings
  [42:1:1.1] Gold coins | First issued in India by the Indo-Greeks (rare); increased under the Kushanas
  [42:1:1.2] Metals | Mostly silver, also copper; gold rare
  [42:1:1.3] Legends | Bilingual: Greek on the obverse, Prakrit in Kharoshthi on the reverse (rarely Brahmi)
  [42:1:1.4] Number of kings | More than 30 Indo-Greek kings are known mainly from their coins
[42:1:2] - **Kushana trap:** Kushana coins did NOT precede Indo-Greek portrait coins; Indo-Greeks = first gold, **Kushanas** = large-scale gold.
[42:1:3] # Indian deities on coins
[42:1:4] - **Sankarshana (Balarama) and Vasudeva (Krishna):** on the coins of **Agathocles** (c. 190-180 BCE) from **Ai-Khanoum** – the first known depiction of Krishna (UPPCS Mains 2017); not Huvishka, Kanishka or Samudragupta.
[42:1:5]   - Agathocles held the Paropamisadae and also issued **Greek + Brahmi** coins.
[42:1:6] - **Indian deities on Indo-Greek coins = Agathocles:** a question asking which Indo-Greek ruler "issued coins depicting Indian deities" is answered **Agathocles**; Menander's coins carry Buddhist **symbols** (wheel, elephant), not Indian deities (APPSC ASO; printed key Menander I).
[42:1:7] - **Balarama holds the plough** (hala) – hence **Halayudha**; Krishna, Kartikeya and Maitreya are not shown with it (UPPSC Mains 2007).
[42:1:8] # Other coin facts
[42:1:9] - **Lead coins:** **Strato II** (UPPSC 2014); Strato I, Demetrius and Menander are distractors.
[42:1:10] - **Largest ancient gold coin:** **Eucratides** (20 staters).
[42:1:11] - **Other early coinages:** Saka-Kshatrapas (Rudradaman) – **silver and copper**; **Chandragupta II** – silver **Rupaka** after defeating the Sakas; **Muhammad Ghori** – coins with **Lakshmi**, some with the Arabic **Kalma**; Kanishka – the Buddha; Mahmud of Ghazni – silver coins with Arabic and Sanskrit legends.
[42:1:12] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.147-148; Rocket Indian History 01 p.55; app notes 1:42 (sub 1, 3, 8); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agathocles_of_Bactria
## SUB 2 | Greek Impact – Military Governors, Heliodorus and Culture
[42:2:0] - **Military governorship:** first introduced in India by the **Greeks** (UPSC 2000) – governors called **strategos**; not the Shakas, Parthians or Mughals.
[42:2:1] - **Heliodorus:** a **Bhagavata** from **Takshashila (Taxila)** (UP UDA/LDA 2010), envoy of **Antialcidas** to the Shunga king **Bhagabhadra**; set up the **Besnagar (Vidisha) Garuda pillar** in honour of **Vasudeva**.
[42:2:2] - **Other envoys:** **Daimachos** – Antiochus I to **Bindusara**; **Megasthenes** – Seleucus I to Chandragupta Maurya.
[42:2:3] - **Why foreigners chose Buddhism:** Greeks, Kushanas and Shakas embraced Buddhism because it **provided easier access to Indian society** – no caste barrier to entry (UPSC 1998); "caste-ridden Hinduism did not attract them" is the tempting half-truth.
[42:2:4] - **Absorption:** the Smritis call the absorbed Greeks **Vratya Kshatriyas** (second-class Kshatriyas).
[42:2:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [42:2:5.0] Yavanika | Stage / theatre curtain
  [42:2:5.1] Hora | Horoscope
  [42:2:5.2] Romaka Siddhanta | Greek influence on Indian astronomy
[42:2:6] - **Art:** Hellenistic art fed the **Gandhara** school of the Kushana period.
[42:2:7] - **Philosophical exchange:** Milindapanho; religious interaction: Heliodorus; artistic synthesis: Gandhara.
[42:2:8] - **Match:** Milindapanho – Menander; **Taxila copper plate – Patika** (a Shaka); Junagadh – Rudradaman I; Gandhara art – Kushanas.
[42:2:9] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.5-6, p.20-21; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.148; app notes 1:42 (sub 2, 5)
## SUB 3 | Bactria, Rulers and the Order of Invaders
[42:3:0] - **Bactria (north Afghanistan):** the Indo-Greek kingdom set up there at the start of the 2nd century BC (UPSC 1999); Scythia = land of the Sakas, Gedrosia = **Balochistan**, Aria = Herat region.
[42:3:1] - **Independence:** **Diodotus** revolted against the **Seleucids** (mid-3rd c. BCE) and founded the Graeco-Bactrian kingdom.
[42:3:2] - **Why they came south:** Scythian (Saka) pressure behind them and the weak successors of Ashoka in India.
[42:3:3] TABLE head: Ruler | Exam fact
  [42:3:3.0] Demetrius | First Bactrian Greek to cross the Hindu Kush and invade India (c. 180 BCE); Greek defeat is credited to Pushyamitra Shunga, and Kharavela's Hathigumpha mentions a Yavana king retreating
  [42:3:3.1] Menander | Greatest; Sakala; Milindapanho
  [42:3:3.2] Antialcidas | Sent Heliodorus to Vidisha
  [42:3:3.3] Eucratides | 20-stater gold coin
  [42:3:3.4] Strato | Menander's son; Strato II issued lead coins
  [42:3:3.5] Hermaius | Last; Greek rule south of the Hindu Kush ended with him
[42:3:4] - **Raids:** Patanjali's **Mahabhashya** says the Yavanas reached **Saketa** and **Madhyamika**; the Yuga Purana takes them towards **Pataliputra**.
[42:3:5] - **Successors:** the **Sakas** replaced the Indo-Greeks first (Azes defeated the last Indo-Greek kings), then the Parthians, then the Kushanas.
[42:3:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.145-146; PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.5; app notes 1:42 (sub 0, 1)
[42:3:7] - **Order of rulers:** **Ashoka** (c. 268–232 BCE) → **Milinda / Menander** (c. 165–145 BCE) → **Kanishka** (Kushana, from 78 CE or 127 CE).
