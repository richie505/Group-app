## SUB 0 | The Name 'Ashoka', His Titles and the Decipherment
[0:0] # Titles in the edicts
[0:1] - **Usual title:** **Devanampiya Piyadasi** – Devanampiya (Devanampriya) = "**Beloved of the Gods**"; Piyadasi (**Priyadarshi**) = "of pleasing appearance" (IAS 1995; MPPSC 2015; APPSC G1) – not Chakravarti, Dharmadeva or Dharmakirti; not a title of Samudragupta, Kanishka or Harsha.
[0:2] - **Other names:** **Buddhashakya** – Maski; **Dharmasoka** – Sarnath inscription; **Ashokavardhana** – Puranas.
[0:3] - **Bhabru (Bairat):** calls himself "**Piyadasi Raja**" of **Magadha** – the only edict where he declares himself king of Magadha (State PSC; it is a minor rock edict, though the paper called it a "pillar"); Maski, Rummindei and the Queen's edict are distractors.
[0:4] # Edicts that name "Ashoka"
[0:5] TABLE head: Edict | Location | Wording
  [0:5.0] Maski (found 1915) | Raichur, Karnataka | "Devanampiya Asoka" – the edict that proved Piyadasi = Ashoka
  [0:5.1] Gujarra | Datia district, Madhya Pradesh (on the Ujjain–Bharuch road) | "Devanampiya Piyadasi Asoka"
  [0:5.2] Nittur, Udegolam | Ballari, Karnataka | Also carry the name
  [0:5.3] Kanaganahalli | Near Sannati, Kalaburagi, Karnataka, on the Bhima | Stone portrait of Ashoka with his queens, label "Ranyo Asoko" (King Ashoka) – the only inscribed portrait
[0:6] - **Personal name asked:** **Maski** (UPSC 1997 ×2; RAS 2007) and **Gujarra** (UPPCS 2015). Kalsi, Rummindei, the Special Kalinga Edict, Bhabru, RE XIII, Ahraura, Brahmagiri and Sarnath use only the titles.
[0:7] - **Ranyo Ashoka with his portrait:** **Kanaganahalli** (UPSC 2019 ×2) – Ashoka is depicted at Sanchi and Amaravati but not named; Shahbazgarhi and Sohgaura are distractors.
[0:8] # Decipherment and firsts
[0:9] - **James Prinsep** (British, "Princep") **deciphered Brahmi and Kharosthi in 1837** – the first scholar to read the Ashokan edicts and the first Briton to study them (UPPCS 2010; CGPSC 2003; IAS 2016). Not Buhler, Max Muller, William Jones, Cunningham.
[0:10] - **First edicts read:** the **Delhi-Topra** pillar edicts. George Turnour later linked "Devanampiya Piyadasi" with Ashoka from the Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa.
[0:11] - **First Indian ruler to speak directly to his people through inscriptions** – on **natural rocks and polished pillars**, in **Prakrit**: **Ashoka** (APPSC; AP Hostel Welfare Officer) – not Chandragupta Maurya, Kharavela, Gautamiputra Satakarni or the Guptas.
[0:12] - **Form borrowed from Persia:** the third-person opening and the word "**lipi/dipi**" follow the inscriptions of **Darius**. Ashoka called his edicts **Dhammalipi**.
[0:13] > Exam angle: "name Ashoka" → **Maski / Gujarra**; "Ranyo Ashoka + portrait" → **Kanaganahalli**; "first deciphered" → **James Prinsep, 1837**.
[0:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.3-4; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.42-44; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.122, p.125; app notes 1:36 (sub 0)
## SUB 1 | Major Rock Edicts – Number and Subject
[1:0] TABLE head: RE | Subject
  [1:0.0] I | Ban on animal sacrifice and on festive gatherings (samaja); killing in the royal kitchen cut down
  [1:0.1] II | Medical care for men and animals, herbs and trees; names the Cholas, Pandyas, Satiyaputtas, Keralaputtas, Tamraparni (Sri Lanka) and the Yavana king Antiyoka
  [1:0.2] III | Quinquennial (five-yearly) tours of rajukas, pradeshikas and yuktas (12th year)
  [1:0.3] IV | Dhammaghosha in place of Bherighosha
  [1:0.4] V | Dhamma Mahamattas appointed, 13 years after consecration; mentions his brothers and sisters
  [1:0.5] VI | Pativedakas may report to the king at all times
  [1:0.6] VIII | Dhammayatras replace royal pleasure tours; the first to Bodh Gaya
  [1:0.7] XII | Religious tolerance – respect for all sects, restraint in praising one's own sect
  [1:0.8] XIII | Kalinga War, remorse, Dhammavijaya; five Greek kings; the Andhras named
  [1:0.9] XIV | Summary – why and how the edicts were engraved
[1:1] - **RE I:** bans animal sacrifice at festive gatherings (RAS 2013) – not PE V, RE IX or XI.
[1:2] - **RE II:** names the **South Indian kingdoms** (UPPCS Mains 2016). **Sangam kingdoms: RE II and XIII** (UPSC 1998 ×2).
[1:3] - **RE XII:** "Whosoever praises his religious sect or blames other sects … injures his own sect very severely" = **Ashoka** (UPSC 2020 ×2); the edict of **religious tolerance / synthesis** (UP Lower 2008; UPPCS 2022) – not RE XIII, PE VII or Bhabru.
[1:4] - **RE XIII:** the **account of the Kalinga War**, its victory and casualties (UPPSC 2016; IAS 1998) – not Rummindei, Minor RE I or Hiuen Tsang.
[1:5] - **Andhras:** the **definite mention of the Andhras** is in **RE XIII** (APPSC Asst. BC & Tribal Welfare Officer 2017 ×2) – listed (with the Palidas) among peoples within Ashoka's domain who follow his Dhamma. There is no 15th RE (only 14).
[1:6] - **Prisoners:** released on **every anniversary of his coronation (abhisheka)** – 25 releases in 26 years (PE V) (APPSC G1) – not his birth, Kalinga or conversion.
[1:7] - **Sri Lanka (Tamraparni)** was a neighbour named in RE II, **not part of the empire** (State PSC); Afghanistan, Bihar and Kalinga were.
[1:8] - **What the inscriptions tell:** his life (Kalinga), internal policy (officials, Dhamma) and foreign policy (Greek kings, southern kingdoms) – **all of these** (SSC 2018).
[1:9] > Exam angle: **I** sacrifice, **II** medical care + south, **V** Mahamattas, **XII** tolerance, **XIII** Kalinga + Greeks + Andhras.
[1:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.44-45; NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.5; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.124-125; app notes 1:36 (sub 3)
## SUB 2 | Scripts and Languages
[2:0] TABLE head: Script | Language | Where
  [2:0.0] Brahmi (left to right) | Prakrit | Most edicts – Ganga plain, Deccan, Andhra (Erragudi, Rajula Mandagiri), Girnar, Kalsi
  [2:0.1] Kharosthi (right to left) | Prakrit | North-west only: Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra (Pakistan)
  [2:0.2] Greek | Greek | Kandahar (Afghanistan)
  [2:0.3] Aramaic | Aramaic | Taxila, Laghman, Kandahar
  [2:0.4] Greek + Aramaic (bilingual) | – | Kandahar (Shar-i-Kuna)
[2:1] - **Script of most edicts:** **Brahmi** (APPSC FRO) – not Devanagari, Kharosthi or Sharada. **Language:** **Prakrit** (State PSC) – not Sanskrit, Pali or Hindi.
[2:2] - **Kharosthi:** written **right to left** (UPSC 1997), derived from **Aramaic**; the Kharosthi edict among Kalsi, Girnar, Shahbazgarhi, Meerut is **Shahbazgarhi** (CGPSC 2011).
[2:3] - **APPSC G1 2023 statements:** I. Brahmi, Kharosthi, Greek, Aramaic ✓; II. name Ashoka only at Maski and Gujarra ✓ (the key's view); III. RE XIII names five Greek rulers ✓; IV. the war of succession in RE IV and V ✗ (RE V only mentions his brothers' families) → **I, II and III**.
[2:4]   - **⚠ Disputed:** strictly the name also occurs at **Nittur and Udegolam** (PMF), so II is loose; the key still takes it as correct.
[2:5] - **Brahmi** is the source of the **Telugu** script (it was not used only for Telugu). No edict names the Four Noble Truths or Nibbana; the edicts never mention varna or sati.
[2:6] - Inscriptions fall into three classes: **rock, pillar and cave** (Barabar) inscriptions.
[2:7] > Exam angle: Kharosthi = **right to left, NW, Shahbazgarhi/Mansehra**; most edicts = **Prakrit in Brahmi**.
[2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.3; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.122, p.125-126; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.43; app notes 1:36 (sub 1)
## SUB 3 | Pillar Edicts and the Pillars
[3:0] TABLE head: Pillar | Content
  [3:0.0] Rummindei (Lumbini), Nepal | Visit 20 years after consecration; marks the Buddha's birthplace; Lumbini freed from bali, bhaga cut from 1/6 to 1/8
  [3:0.1] Nigali Sagar, Nepal | Kanakamuni stupa enlarged (14th year)
  [3:0.2] Sarnath | Schism edict; lion capital = national emblem
  [3:0.3] Allahabad-Kosam | PE I–VI, Schism and Queen's edict (gifts of Karuvaki); later Samudragupta's prashasti and Jahangir's inscription
  [3:0.4] Delhi-Topra | The only pillar with PE VII – rajukas, Ajivikas, Nirgranthas; Dhamma spreads better by persuasion than by law
  [3:0.5] Lauriya Nandangarh | West Champaran, Bihar; single lion capital; PE I–VI
[3:1] - **Lumbini tax cut to 1/8:** **Rummindei pillar** (APPSC G2 2024) – not Maski, Kalsi or Vaishali.
[3:2] - **Confirms the Buddha was born at Lumbini:** **Rummindei** (UPUDA 2010) – not Basarh, Nigali Sagar, Rampurwa; so the dynasty whose inscription marks Lumbini = **Mauryan** (UPUDA 2006).
[3:3] - **Sarnath pillar:** built by **Ashoka** (UP Lower 2008) – not Harsha, the Buddha or Kanishka.
[3:4] - **Topra and Meerut pillars** were brought to Delhi by **Firuz Shah Tughluq** (UPPCS 1996); the Kosam pillar went to the Allahabad fort traditionally under **Akbar**.
[3:5] - **PE II** defines Dhamma ("few faults, many good deeds"); **PE IV** (26th year) – rajukas' judicial power, 3-day respite; **PE V** – no cattle branding, prisoner releases.
[3:6] - **Schism edict:** dissenting monks and nuns expelled – **Sarnath, Sanchi, Kaushambi**.
[3:7] - Pillars cluster in the **Ganga plain** – river transport for the heavy Chunar sandstone monoliths.
[3:8] > Exam angle: Rummindei = **pillar in Nepal, Lumbini, 1/8**; Firuz Shah = **Topra + Meerut**.
[3:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.43, p.45; NextGen Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.3-4; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.126-127; app notes 1:36 (sub 5)
## SUB 4 | Ashokan Edicts in Andhra and the Minor Rock Edicts
[4:0] TABLE head: Site | Location | Content
  [4:0.0] Erragudi (Yerragudi / Jonnagiri) | Pattikonda, Kurnool district, AP | 14 Major RE + Minor RE; teachers must instil Dhamma in their pupils
  [4:0.1] Rajula Mandagiri | Kurnool district, AP | Minor RE only; guru–disciple relationship; proof of Mauryan rule in Andhra
  [4:0.2] Maski | Raichur, Karnataka | Name "Ashoka"
  [4:0.3] Brahmagiri, Siddapura, Jatinga-Rameshwara | Chitradurga, Karnataka | Minor RE I and II – southern limit
  [4:0.4] Bhabru (Bairat) | Rajasthan | Faith in the Buddha, Dhamma and Sangha; recommends Buddhist texts
[4:1] - **Teachers to inculcate religious thought in disciples:** **Erragudi** (APPSC G2 2008; AP History PYQ) – Bhattiprolu and Dhanyakataka are not Ashokan sites.
[4:2]   - **⚠ Disputed:** most books and keys give **Erragudi**; strictly **Rajula Mandagiri** carries the same Minor RE II text on gurus and disciples (AP History APPSC 01).
[4:3] - **Ashoka's inscriptions in AP:** **Erragudi** (AP History PYQ) – not Nagarjuna Hill, Amaravati or Bhattiprolu.
[4:4] - **Earliest inscriptions in Andhra:** **Ashoka's** (AP History PYQ) – in **Prakrit, Brahmi**; they show Mauryan rule reached Rayalaseema. Nashik, Hathigumpha and the Rakshasa caves are later.
[4:5] - **Erragudi's district:** **Kurnool** (Pattikonda mandal; ASI site on the Gooty–Pattikonda road) – not Nandyal.
[4:6] - **Connection with Buddhism:** **Minor Rock Edict I** – Ashoka calls himself an **upasaka** who drew closer to the Sangha (NDA 2017); RE XIII, RE VI and PE IV are distractors. Minor edicts are the earliest Ashokan records and are densest in the Andhra–Karnataka area.
[4:7] > Exam angle: AP edict sites = **Erragudi and Rajula Mandagiri (Kurnool)**; Bhattiprolu = Kubiraka casket, not Ashoka.
[4:8] ~~ Sources: Rocket AP History (src/ap-history/rocket-ap-history.txt, inscriptions table); AP History APPSC 01 p.6, p.11; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.125-126; deccanchronicle.com (Jonnagiri edict, Pattikonda mandal, Kurnool); app notes 1:36 (sub 4)
## SUB 5 | Major Rock Edict Sites
[5:0] TABLE head: Site | Present location
  [5:0.0] Kalsi | Dehradun, Uttarakhand
  [5:0.1] Girnar | Junagadh, Gujarat – same rock bears Rudradaman's inscription
  [5:0.2] Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra | Pakistan (Kharosthi)
  [5:0.3] Dhauli | Near Bhubaneswar, Odisha – Separate Kalinga Edicts
  [5:0.4] Jaugada | Ganjam, Odisha – Separate Kalinga Edicts
  [5:0.5] Sopara | Maharashtra
  [5:0.6] Erragudi | Andhra Pradesh
  [5:0.7] Sannati | Karnataka
  [5:0.8] Kandahar | Afghanistan (Greek)
[5:1] - **Kalsi:** famous for Ashoka's rock edicts (UP Lower 2008); the Ashokan edict of **Uttarakhand** (UKPSC 2005; UK UDA 2007) – not Devprayag, Kedarnath, Rishikesh, Nainital, Pauri or Tehri.
[5:2] - **UPSC 2022 pairs:** Dhauli–Odisha ✓, Erragudi–AP ✓, Jaugada–MP ✗ (Odisha), Kalsi–Karnataka ✗ (Uttarakhand) → **only two pairs**.
[5:3] - **Separate Kalinga Edicts** (Dhauli, Jaugada) replace RE XI–XIII there; addressed to officers of **Tosali and Samapa**; "**All men are my children**".
[5:4] - **Not Ashokan:** **Besnagar** – Heliodorus pillar. Rummindei is a pillar, not a Major RE site. The spread of edict sites is the main evidence for the **extent of the empire**; Kashmir comes through Kalhana's Rajatarangini (son **Jalauka**).
[5:5] > Exam angle: Kalsi = **Uttarakhand**; Jaugada and Dhauli = **Odisha**; Girnar = **Gujarat**.
