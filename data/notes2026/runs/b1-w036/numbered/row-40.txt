## SUB 1 | Andhras in Vedic, Epic and Greek Sources
[1:0] TABLE head: Source | Reference
  [1:0.0] Aitareya Brahmana | First mention of the word "Andhra" – as a race, in the Sunahshepa story: Vishwamitra cursed his 50 sons to become Andhras, Pundras, Pulindas (APPSC SI 2011)
  [1:0.1] Rigveda | No mention of the Andhras – the text that does not mention the Telugu people (APPSC G2 2002); the Arthashastra and Rajatarangini do
  [1:0.2] Mahabharata | Andhras fought on the Kaurava side (APPSC 2011; Tribal Welfare 2012)
  [1:0.3] Vayu Purana | Places the Andhra people in the Deccan
  [1:0.4] Megasthenes' Indica | The Greek ambassador who mentions the Andhras – first foreign source; 30 walled towns (first to mention Andhra forts), 1,00,000 infantry, 2,000 cavalry, 1,000 elephants (report preserved by Pliny)
  [1:0.5] Ashoka's 13th Rock Edict | Andhras among peoples within the empire following his Dhamma
[1:1] - **Megasthenes** was the envoy of **Seleucus** – not Pliny, Ptolemy or Marco Polo, who wrote later.
[1:2] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History 01 p.6-8; app notes 1:40 (sub 0, 1, 6)
## SUB 2 | Early Buddhist Centres and Excavated Sites
[2:0] TABLE head: Site | Fact
  [2:0.0] Bhattiprolu (Guntur/Bapatla) | Oldest stupa of Andhra; very large bricks; dome 132 ft across on a base of 148 ft (the figure APPSC asked as its "height"); relic caskets name Raja Kubhiraka (Kuberaka), ruler of Bhattiprolu in the 3rd c. BCE
  [2:0.1] Dhanyakataka (Amaravati–Dharanikota) | On the Krishna in Andhra; Buddhist centre of the Mahasanghikas; capital of the later Satavahanas; Buddhism here predates the Mauryas (UPSC 2023)
  [2:0.2] Gottiprolu (Nellore region) | ASI dig 2019: early-historic trade centre in a massive brick wall (2nd–1st c. BCE), near an ancient port; imitation amphorae; declared a Monument of National Importance (July 2026)
  [2:0.3] Gudiwada Dibba (Krishna) | Early historic mound; occupation from Mauryan to Satavahana times; Siddharthaka chaitya identified with Gudivada
  [2:0.4] Adurru (East Godavari) | Mahasanghika Buddhist centre
  [2:0.5] Vaddamanu (near Amaravati) | Jain stupa, excavated 1981–85
  [2:0.6] Sriparvata / Vijayapuri | Nagarjunakonda
[2:1] - **Guntupalli** = Rajagirika centre; **P.C. Ray** identified the river at Amaravati-Dharanikota as the Krishna.

## SUB 3 | Assaka, Mulaka and the Bavari Legend
[3:0] TABLE head: Janapada | Capital | Note
  [3:0.0] Assaka (Asmaka) | Bodhan (Potali / Podana) (APPSC G2 2012) – not Paithan, Kotilingala or Dhanyakataka | The only Mahajanapada in the Deccan, on the Godavari
  [3:0.1] Mulaka | Pratishthana | Neighbour of Assaka
[3:1] - **Bavari:** a Brahmin teacher from **Kosala** who settled on the **Godavari** in Assaka territory; sent **16 disciples** to the Buddha (Suttanipata) – Buddhism reached the Godavari region **before the Mauryan expansion**.
[3:2] - **Trade:** Andhra lay on the **Dakshinapatha**; merchant guilds used Kalingapatnam and later Masulipatnam.
[3:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History 01 p.6; app notes 1:40 (sub 4, 5)
## SUB 4 | Ashokan Edicts and Mauryan Contact
[4:0] - **Before the Satavahanas** Andhra was already a distinct people and region; by the 3rd c. BCE it was ruled by **Mauryan feudatories**, and the Mauryan phase made Buddhism **institutional** here.
[4:1] TABLE head: Site | District | Content
  [4:1.0] Erragudi | Kurnool (now Nandyal) | Major and minor rock edicts; teachers must inculcate Dhamma in pupils
  [4:1.1] Rajula Mandagiri | Kurnool | Minor rock edict (not a pillar edict)
  [4:1.2] Amaravati | Guntur | Fragment of an Ashokan pillar edict
[4:2] - **Mahadeva Bhikshu:** by Andhra tradition sent by Ashoka to **Amaravati** – first Buddhist teacher in Andhra; enshrined the Buddha's bone relics.
[4:3] - **End of Mauryan rule:** Andhra rulers declared independence; the **Satavahanas** rose.
[4:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen AP History 01 p.6; PMF Ancient & Medieval 02 p.141; app notes 1:40 (sub 7)
