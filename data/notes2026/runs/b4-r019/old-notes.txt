=== NOTES 4:19 | Atmospheric Disturbances – Tropical Cyclones, Temperate Cyclones, Anticyclones, Thunderstorms, Tornadoes
## SUB 0 | Disturbances at A Glance
[19:0:0] TABLE head: Disturbance | Nature | Scale | Clouds
  [19:0:0.0] Tropical cyclone | Low-pressure system over warm tropical oceans | Large | Cumulonimbus, rainbands
  [19:0:0.1] Temperate (extra-tropical) cyclone | Mid-latitude low from air-mass interaction, with fronts | Large | Stratiform and cumuliform
  [19:0:0.2] Anticyclone | High-pressure centre, outward sinking air | Large | Generally clear
  [19:0:0.3] Thunderstorm | Violent convective storm with lightning, thunder | Local | Cumulonimbus
  [19:0:0.4] Tornado (twister) | Violently rotating funnel | Very local | Cumulonimbus supercell
  [19:0:0.5] Western Disturbance | Extratropical storm affecting NW India | Regional | –
  [19:0:0.6] Cloudburst, squall | Intense local rain; sudden gusty wind | Local | –
[19:0:1] **Fronts** belong to **temperate cyclones only**; tropical cyclones, anticyclones, thunderstorms and tornadoes have none.
## SUB 1 | Tropical Cyclones – Formation and Limits
[19:1:0] # Conditions
[19:1:1] TABLE head: Condition | Role
  [19:1:1.0] Sea-surface temperature above 26.5°C over a large area | Heat and moisture – most essential
  [19:1:1.1] Large supply of warm, moist air | Latent heat of condensation = energy source
  [19:1:1.2] Coriolis force | Rotation; too weak within about 5° of the Equator
  [19:1:1.3] Unstable troposphere | Vigorous convection
  [19:1:1.4] Low vertical wind shear | Keeps vertical structure organised
  [19:1:1.5] Pre-existing disturbance | Seed low / cyclonic circulation
[19:1:2] - Form **only over warm oceans**, not over rivers or lakes (**APPSC-GS 2025**); cold currents suppress them.
[19:1:3] - Force decreases away from the sea; they **weaken after landfall** (cut off from moisture).
[19:1:4] # Where They Form and Do Not
[19:1:5] - Belt: **between 30°N and 30°S** (**APPSC 2022 key**); mainly 5°–30° N and S, western parts of tropical oceans.
[19:1:6] - **None at the Equator** and none in the **South Atlantic and south-east Pacific** (low SST, strong shear, ITCZ seldom there).
[19:1:7] - A tropical cyclone in May is linked to local temperature variation.
[19:1:8] - Marine heatwaves raise cyclone winds by up to 20% and rain by up to 12% (2026 study).
## SUB 2 | Structure, Rotation and Steering
[19:2:0] TABLE head: Part | Feature
  [19:2:0.0] Eye | Calm, clear, sinking air; lowest pressure; higher temperature; 20–50 km across
  [19:2:0.1] Eyewall | Ring of towering cumulonimbus; strongest winds, heaviest rain
  [19:2:0.2] Spiral rainbands | Curved bands of rain and wind
[19:2:1] - Air moves **inward and rises**; rotation **anticlockwise in NH, clockwise in SH** (Coriolis).
[19:2:2] - Trade winds push cyclones **west**; mature storms may **recurve north-east** under westerlies.
[19:2:3] - Threats: high winds, heavy rain, flooding, **storm surge** (abnormal rise of sea water – **biggest killer**).
## SUB 3 | Cyclone vs Anticyclone
[19:3:0] TABLE head: Basis | Cyclone | Anticyclone
  [19:3:0.0] Centre | Low pressure | High pressure
  [19:3:0.1] Air | Inward, rising | Outward, sinking (diverging)
  [19:3:0.2] Weather | Cloudy, stormy | Clear, dry, calm, settled
  [19:3:0.3] NH rotation | Anticlockwise | Clockwise
  [19:3:0.4] SH rotation | Clockwise | Anticlockwise
[19:3:1] - **Heat dome**: a stalled warm high traps hot air (27 May 2026).
## SUB 4 | Temperate Cyclones and Western Disturbances
[19:4:0] TABLE head: Feature | Tropical | Temperate
  [19:4:0.0] Latitude | 5–30° | 35–65° (mid and high)
  [19:4:0.1] Origin | Only over warm seas | Land and sea
  [19:4:0.2] Fronts | None | Clear frontal system (warm, cold; occluded may develop)
  [19:4:0.3] Size, winds | Smaller, very intense | Larger area, gentler winds; rain over wide area, longer period
  [19:4:0.4] Movement | East to west (trades) | West to east (westerlies)
[19:4:1] **APPSC-G1 2024**: "Temperate cyclones do not have a clear frontal system" is the INCORRECT statement.
[19:4:2] - **Western Disturbance**: weak cyclonic (extratropical) disturbance from the **Mediterranean**; reaches **NW India in winter** at the occluded stage; about 10–15 cm rain for **rabi** crops, snow on mountains; rain **decreases west to east** across the NW plain.
## SUB 5 | Tornadoes, Thunderstorms and Local Storms
[19:5:0] # Tornadoes
[19:5:1] - Rotating column from a **thunderstorm** to the ground; dark funnel; winds **105–322 km/h**; moves up to about 90–100 km/h; lasts minutes (rarely over 15–20).
[19:5:2] - From rotating **supercells**; an ordinary thunderstorm is a short-lived single-cell cumulonimbus.
[19:5:3] - Mostly **USA** (**Tornado Alley**): warm moist **Gulf of Mexico** air meets cold dry continental air with strong shear.
[19:5:4] TABLE head: Feature | Tornado | Hurricane
  [19:5:4.0] Size, life | Small, minutes | Hundreds of km, days
  [19:5:4.1] Where | Land-based | Ocean-born
[19:5:5] - **Waterspout** = tornado over water; seen over the **Brahmaputra, Barpeta (Assam)**, 26 Aug 2026.
[19:5:6] # Thunderstorms and Local Storms
[19:5:7] - Cumulonimbus up to 15 km tall in unstable, moist air. **Thunder** = sound of air expanding explosively after **lightning** heats it.
[19:5:8] TABLE head: Storm | Season / region
  [19:5:8.0] Kaal Baisakhi / Nor'westers | Pre-monsoon; West Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Assam ("tea showers"); approach from NW
  [19:5:8.1] Aandhi (dust storm) | Summer; dry plains of NW India
  [19:5:8.2] Cloudburst | Himalaya, June–August; about 100 mm/hour over 20–30 sq km
[19:5:9] - **Hailstorms**: strong instability, moisture, updrafts; synoptic causes = Western Disturbances and wind discontinuity lines; plateaus and hills boost convection.
[19:5:10] - **Damini App**: lightning alerts in **15 languages**; **104** detection locations.
[19:5:11] - **Mango showers:** pre-monsoon showers at the end of summer in **Kerala and coastal Karnataka**; they help the early ripening of mangoes. **Blossom (cherry blossom) showers** make **coffee** flowers blossom in Kerala and nearby areas.
[19:5:12] - **Blizzard:** a severe winter snowstorm with winds of **35 mph (about 56 km/h)** or more and visibility of a quarter mile or less for at least **3 hours** (US NWS).

## SUB 9 | India's Exposure and Major Cyclones
[19:9:0] - India faces about **10%** of world tropical cyclones (**APPSC-GS 2023**). Coastline: old **7,516.6 km**; remeasured 2023–24 = **11,098.81 km** (+47.7%); about 5,900 km directly exposed.
[19:9:1] - **Bay of Bengal : Arabian Sea ≈ 4:1**; Bay gives **75–80%** (high SST, Pacific typhoon remnants). East coast (Odisha, AP, WB, TN) more prone; **Odisha** faces most disasters.
[19:9:2] - Arabian Sea: fewer but more intense; 2019 storms **Vayu, Hikka, Kyarr, Maha, Pavan**.
[19:9:3] - Season **bimodal**: main **Oct–Dec**, secondary Apr–May; in monsoon at onset (June) and withdrawal (Sept).
[19:9:4] - **INCOIS (Hyderabad)**, 1995–2019: El Nino may cut post-monsoon Bay cyclones (**15** in 9 El Nino years vs **33** in 11 La Nina years).
