## SUB 0 | Swachh Bharat Mission and Swachh Survekshan
[0:0] # SBM basics
[0:1] - Launched **2 October 2014** (**Gandhi Jayanti**) – October 2014; goal **ODF and Clean India by 2 October 2019** (Gandhi's **150th** birth anniversary) – the only one of Jan Dhan, BBBP, Janshree Bima and SBM with that deadline.
[0:2] - Two components: **Rural (Grameen)** and **Urban (Shahari)**; SBM-Gramin also targeted an ODF rural India by **2 October 2019**.
[0:3] - Focus on **collective behavioural change** of the whole community; open defecation is part of the usual early-morning walk, crop check and socialising for many (mainly rural India).
[0:4] - **Swachh Yug** (2016): to make **villages along the Ganga ODF**.
[0:5] - **SBM-U 2.0** launched **1 October 2021**: garbage-free cities.
[0:6] # Swachh Survekshan
[0:7] - **2017 assessment areas**: waste collection, sweeping and transportation; processing and disposal; ODF; **capacity building (SBM e-learning portal)**; **IEC and behaviour change** – **not "level of micro particles"** in the air.
[0:8] TABLE head: Garbage Free City star | Prerequisite certification
  [0:8.0] 1 Star | ODF
  [0:8.1] 3 Star | ODF+
  [0:8.2] 5 Star | ODF++
  [0:8.3] 7 Star | Water+
[0:9] - **Water+ (Aug 2021)**: AP became the first State with more than one Water+ city – **three: Visakhapatnam, Vijayawada, Tirupati**.
[0:10] - GFC vision: no garbage or litter visible in the city at any time of day.
[0:11] - **Indore**: cleanest city (10 lakh+), year after year; **Swachh Survekshan 2024-25 (July 2025)**: Indore top for the **8th year in a row**; new **Super Swachh League** of 23 cities.
[0:12] - Super Swachh League, 10 lakh+ group: **Indore, Surat, Navi Mumbai, Vijayawada (AP)**.
[0:13] - Ranked "cleanest big city" (10 lakh+) in 2024-25: **Ahmedabad**, then Bhopal, Lucknow.
[0:14] - Visakhapatnam: SafaiMitra Surakshit Shehar award (sanitation worker safety).
[0:15] - **Swachh Vayu Survekshan** (MoEFCC, under the **National Clean Air Programme**): ranks cities on clean-air actions; million-plus top city **Indore** (2023, announced by Bhupender Yadav; also 2025); **2026** (5th edition): **Lucknow** 1st, Indore 2nd, Jabalpur 3rd.

## SUB 1 | Smart Cities Mission
[1:0] # Selection
[1:1] - Launched **25 June 2015** by MoHUA; **100 cities** – a holistic city rejuvenation programme via competitive challenge.
[1:2] - Two-stage; rounds: Round 1 (Jan 2016) 20, fast track 13, Round 2 27, **Round 3 (June 2017) 30** (incl. **Amaravati**), **Round 4 (Jan 2018) 9** + Shillong (June 2018) = 100.
[1:3] - City shares among States: **equal weight to urban population and number of statutory towns** (not districts).
[1:4] - **Uttar Pradesh: 10 smart cities** – Lucknow, Kanpur, Agra, Varanasi, Prayagraj, Aligarh, Jhansi, Moradabad, Bareilly, Saharanpur.
[1:5] - **Logo text**: "Smart City – **MISSION TRANSFORM-NATION**".
[1:6] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:6.0] Area-Based Development | Retrofitting/redevelopment of at least 50 acres
  [1:6.1] Pan-City | Tech solutions; Integrated Command and Control Centres
  [1:6.2] SPV | One per city; company under Companies Act, 2013; 50:50 equity of State/UT and ULB
  [1:6.3] CITIIS 2.0 (2023) | 18 smart cities; integrated waste management; partners AFD, KfW, EU, NIUA
[1:7] # Financing
[1:8] - Centre **Rs 48,000 crore** – **Rs 100 crore per city per year, about Rs 500 crore per city** over the mission – matched by State/ULB.
[1:9] - Other sources: ULB own funds, Finance Commission grants, municipal bonds, PPP, loans, convergence.
[1:10] - **Water and sewage financed through property tax**.
[1:11] - Progress (11 Jul 2025): Rs 47,459 crore (**99%**) of central share claimed; mission extended to **2025-26**.
[1:12] - **BRICS Smart Cities Conference**: **Jaipur, 17-19 August 2016** (MEA with ORF).

## SUB 4 | JNNURM and Earlier Urban Schemes
[4:0] - Announced in Budget 2005-06; launched **3 December 2005**; a **7-year** **urban renewal programme**.
[4:1] - Urban infrastructure and basic services in **63 mission cities** – mainly the **million-plus cities** (over 60), State capitals and heritage cities; succeeded by AMRUT and Smart Cities (2015).
[4:2] - Objectives: urban infrastructure (water supply, sewerage, solid waste, roads, **urban transport**, renewal of old city/**heritage areas**), houses and basic services for the poor; **urban electrification was NOT an objective**; not rural housing or employment.
[4:3] - Components: **UIG** (Urban Infrastructure and Governance) + **BSUP** (Basic Services to Urban Poor); smaller towns via **UIDSSMT** + **IHSDP** (Integrated Housing and Slum Development Programme).
[4:4] - Reforms: **25% of municipal budget** for the urban poor; **25% of developed land** for EWS/LIG; **7-point charter** – tenure, housing, water, sanitation, education, health, social security.
[4:5] - **ILCS** (Integrated Low Cost Sanitation), **1980-81**: end dry latrines and manual scavenging; funded on **sharing basis – Centre 75%, State 15%, beneficiary 10%**.
[4:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 18 p.6 (JNNURM BSUP, IHSDP, 25% budget, 7-point charter, 25% land); app notes 3:43 sub 1
## SUB 5 | AMRUT and AMRUT 2.0
[5:0] - Launched **25 June 2015**; revamps **urban infrastructure** (basic services) – initially **500 cities** (15 merged → 485) covering about **60% of urban population** (so "400 cities" is wrong).
[5:1] - Mission cities: all ULBs with **1 lakh+** population; other State/UT capitals; all **HRIDAY cities**; cities on stems of main rivers; hill-State, island and tourist cities. **Dausa was NOT** a Rajasthan mission city (till March 2024); Jaipur, Ajmer, Alwar, Tonk qualified.
[5:2] - Outlay: **Rs 1 lakh crore**, incl. Rs 50,000 crore central (2015-16 to 2019-20); delivered through **State Annual Action Plans**.
[5:3] - **Thrust areas**: **water supply**; **sewerage and septage**; **storm-water drains to reduce flooding**; **public / non-motorised urban transport**; **green spaces and parks with recreation centres for children**; capacity building. NOT: high-rise buildings, Wi-Fi, uninterrupted power, crop procurement.
[5:4] - **AMRUT 2.0** (1 Oct 2021 to 2025-26): **universal water supply in all statutory towns**; 100% sewerage/septage in the 500 AMRUT cities; Rs 2.79 lakh crore projects sanctioned.
[5:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society - Group 2 Prelims 02 p.3 (AMRUT civic amenities); app notes 3:43 sub 3
