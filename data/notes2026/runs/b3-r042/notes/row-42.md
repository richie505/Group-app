| Item | Census 2001 | Census 2011 |

# Phases
| Phase | Period | Urban share |
|---|---|---|
| Slow | 1901-31 | 10.8% → 12% |
| **Moderate / medium** | **1931-61** | 12.2% → 18.3% (urban population +45.46 million) |
| Rapid | 1961 onward | 18% → 31.16% |
- Urban share: **11% (1901) → 31.1% (2011)**; growth is fuelled by a steady **village-city flow**.
- India's urban population (2001: 28.6 crore) exceeded the **USA's total population** (28.1 crore); the USA has a far higher **level** of urbanisation, which does not explain India's larger numbers.
- India's level of urbanisation is **lower than China's**; poor planning of Indian cities is true but is not the reason.
- Rapid urbanisation after 2001 (million cities 35 → 53) is true; the mobile revolution is true but unrelated.
- India is **not over-urbanised** (31.1%), though large cities lack infrastructure.
- **Urban growth** = rise in urban population and in the number of urban centres (UPPSC key: also rise in total population and urban income – "all").
- Roots of **urban poverty lie in rural areas** – low rural literacy pushes migrants into low-paid city work (UPPSC A-R key: R explains A).
- Most urban religious group (2011): **Jains (~80%)**; Buddhists 43%; Muslims, Christians ~40%; Hindus 29%; Sikhs 28%.
- India **2nd** in absolute urban population, after China; cities ~**3% of land**, ~**60% of GDP**.
- **Hidden urbanisation**: satellite **DEGURBA** estimate (EAC-PM) about **63% urban**; under-counting starves peri-urban areas of municipal funds.
> Number traps: 31.16% (not 31.36 or 33.16); 27.81% in 2001; 53 million cities; 3,894 census towns; 7,935 towns.

=== Cities – Million-plus Cities and Megacities
- **1901: Kolkata** the only million city (~15 lakh). Million cities: 12 (1981), 23 (1991), **35 (2001)**, **53 (2011)**.
- Million-plus share of urban population: 37.8% (2001), 42.6% (2011).
- **2001:** most million cities in **Uttar Pradesh (6)** – Kanpur, Lucknow, Agra, Varanasi, Meerut, Allahabad (so "only 5 in UP" is false); Maharashtra 4, TN 3, WB 2. **Ludhiana, Kochi, Surat and Nagpur** were all million-plus.
- **KAVAL towns** (Kanpur, Agra, Varanasi, Allahabad, Lucknow) are NOT the only million cities; the **North-East** had no million city (2001 and 2011).
- **2011:** most million-plus UAs – **UP and Kerala (7 each)**, Maharashtra 6. **No million-plus city in Odisha**; Haryana – Faridabad; J&K – Srinagar; Jharkhand – Jamshedpur, Dhanbad, Ranchi.
- **2001 megacities (6)**: Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Hyderabad, Bengaluru – **not Ahmedabad**. 2011: nine UAs above 40 lakh (adds Ahmedabad, Pune, Surat).

=== Census Definitions – Urban Area, Towns and Size Classes
| Type | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Statutory town** | Any place with a municipal corporation, municipality, nagar panchayat, cantonment board or notified town area – notified by the State by law |
| **Census town** (= urban area test, Census 2001/2011) | All 3: population **≥5,000**; **≥75% of male main workers** in non-agricultural work; density **≥400 per sq km** |
- **No minimum area** and **no electricity criterion** (e.g. "60% households electrified" is not a test; "60% males" is wrong – it is 75%).
- Rural area = all areas not urban; 3/4 or more engaged in primary activities.
- **Census 2027** keeps the 1981 census-town criteria; RGI retained them despite **MoHUA's** objection before the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Housing and Urban Affairs (chair **Magunta Sreenivasulu Reddy**).
# Size classes (six)
| Class | Population |
|---|---|
| **Class I** | **1 lakh and above** (468 UAs/towns in 2011, about **70%** of urban population) |
| Class II | 50,000-99,999 |
| Class III | 20,000-49,999 |
| Class IV | 10,000-19,999 |
| Class V | 5,000-9,999 |
| Class VI | below 5,000 |
- **Small towns = Class IV, V and VI** (under 20,000).
- Class I share of urban population: 65.2% (1991); **2001: 62.32% in UPPSC keys** (other compilations about 68-69%); ~70% (2011).
- Highest growth: **Class I** towns (Census 1991); **Class III** (Census 2001). Class IV had the largest number of towns in 2001.
- **Slum (Census)**: a compact area of at least **300 population or about 60-70 households** of poorly built, congested tenements in unhygienic conditions.
- A village turns into a town when **traders and manufacturers** appear – they depend on village resources, but not wholly.

=== Causes and Social Effects of Urbanisation
- Main component: **migration** (natural increase similar in rural and urban areas).
- **Push**: monsoon failure, rural unemployment, **lack of rural jobs**, non-growing farm land, **rural-urban imbalance**.
- **Pull**: **magnetic features of cities** – jobs, public facilities (health, education), transport, industrial growth.
- **Philip M. Hauser** – "**Population Implosion**" = rural-urban concentration of people. "**New Population Bomb**" = rapidly growing **Third World urban population**.
- **Crime rises with urbanisation and industrialisation** – industrial-urban life causes family and cultural disorganisation (UPPSC A-R: R explains A).
> Statement sets mixing push and pull factors are usually **all correct**.

=== Urban Challenges – Slums, Housing, Heat, Governance
# Slums
- **Mumbai**: slums **41.3% of urban households** – highest share among metros (Census 2011); Kolkata 29.6%, Chennai 28.5%, Delhi 14.6%.
- Among **million-plus cities**: **Greater Visakhapatnam ~44%** of population in slums; Greater Mumbai and Vijayawada ~41%.
- Slum population 2001: Greater Mumbai 6.5 m, **Delhi 1.9 m** (more than Chennai 0.8 m, Bangalore, Surat).
- 2011: 6.55 crore slum dwellers = 17.4% of urban population; World Bank estimate ~**177 million**.
# Housing, waste, heat, governance
- Urban housing shortage ~**18.78 million units** (2012 Technical Group); costs exceed middle-class incomes → cramped, unserviced housing (NextGen).
- Sewerage, solid waste and public-transport gaps; private vehicles cause congestion.
- **Urban heat island** = a city much **warmer than nearby rural areas** (3-5°C).
- Governance: **fragmented authority**, little **fiscal autonomy** (ES 2025-26 ch.15); urbanising areas left under panchayats.
- **Urban design** = the arrangement and design of buildings, **streets and public spaces**; urban (city) planning = wider land-use regulation (master plans, **zoning**).
- **India Urban Observatory** – **New Delhi** (MoHUA office); data hub for evidence-based planning.
- **MoHUA (2017)** = merger of Ministry of Urban Development + **Ministry of Housing and Urban Poverty Alleviation** (MoHUPA – earlier nodal ministry for identifying urban BPL families).
- **e-lala** – e-commerce portal for small traders (CAIT); Ministry of Urban Development, Nov 2015.
- Social Forestry is NOT under the Minimum Needs Programme.

=== Municipal Finance
- **Property tax** = most important own revenue; also advertisement tax, professional tax, **octroi** (levied and collected by **local bodies**; now subsumed in GST), fees, fines; plus State grants.
- **Property tax gap:** Economic Survey 2016-17 used **satellite imagery** of Bengaluru; cities collect only **5-20%** of potential.
- **74th Amendment – Twelfth Schedule: 18 functions** – urban planning, water supply, public health and sanitation, slum improvement, urban poverty alleviation, fire services, urban forestry, vital statistics (not police).

| First municipal bond | Bangalore Mahanagara Palike, 1997 |
| First without State guarantee | Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation, 1998 |
- Budget 2026-27: **City Economic Regions** – ₹5,000 crore per CER over 5 years.

|---|---|
| 15th FC | ULBs 36% of local body grants |
