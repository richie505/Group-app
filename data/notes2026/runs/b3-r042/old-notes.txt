## SUB 0 | Urban Settlement Classification
[42:0:0] # Statutory vs census town
[42:0:1] TABLE head: Type | Criteria
  [42:0:1.0] Statutory town | Any place with a municipal corporation, municipality, nagar panchayat, cantonment board or notified town area; notified by the State by law; run by a ULB
  [42:0:1.1] Census town | Administratively a village but meets all 3: population ≥5,000; ≥75% of male workers non-agricultural; density ≥400/sq km
[42:0:2] - Same three tests define an **urban area** (Census 2001).
[42:0:3] - Census towns: **3,892** in 2011 (3,894 provisional); **1,362** in 2001.
[42:0:4] - Towns (2011): 7,933-7,935 ≈ **4,041 statutory + 3,892 census towns**.
[42:0:5] - **Census 2027** keeps the 1981 census-town criteria; RGI retained them despite **MoHUA's** objection before the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Housing and Urban Affairs (chair **Magunta Sreenivasulu Reddy**).
[42:0:6] "60% of males in non-agricultural work" is wrong – correct is **75%** (UPPSC 2010).
[42:0:7] Census town = 5,000 – 75% – 400 (population – male non-farm workers – density)
[42:0:8] # Size classes
[42:0:9] - **Six** population-size classes, Class I to VI (CGPSC answer "06").
[42:0:10] TABLE head: Class | Population
  [42:0:10.0] Class I | 1 lakh+
  [42:0:10.1] Class III | 20,000-49,999
  [42:0:10.2] Class IV, V, VI | Small towns (under 20,000)
  [42:0:10.3] Class VI | Below 5,000
[42:0:11] - Class I share of urban population: **62.32%** (2001); **70%** now; 468 Class I UAs/towns (2011 provisional).
[42:0:12] - Highest growth: **Class I** towns (Census 1991); **Class III** (Census 2001).
[42:0:13] - A village becomes a town when **traders and manufacturers** appear (not fully dependent on village resources).
## SUB 1 | Urbanisation Trends – India
[42:1:0] # Phases
[42:1:1] TABLE head: Phase | Period | Urban share
  [42:1:1.0] Slow | 1901-31 | 10.8-12%
  [42:1:1.1] Medium / moderate | 1931-61 | 12.2% → 18.3%; urban population +45.46 million (135.86%)
  [42:1:1.2] Rapid | 1961 onward | –
[42:1:2] - Urban share: **11% (1901) → 31.1% (2011)**; cities grow fast through a steady village-city flow.
[42:1:3] # Census 2011
[42:1:4] - Urban population **37.71 crore (377.1 million) = 31.16%**; rural 68.84%; up from **27.81% (2001)**.
[42:1:5] - India **2nd** in absolute urban population, **after China**.
[42:1:6] - India's urban population (2001: 28.6 crore, 27.8%) exceeded the **USA's total population** (28.14 crore); USA has a higher level of urbanisation.
[42:1:7] - India is **not over-urbanised** (31.1%), but large cities lack infrastructure.
[42:1:8] - Cities: ~**3% of land**, ~**60% of GDP**; doubling city size lifts productivity ~12%.
[42:1:9] - **Hidden urbanisation**: satellite **DEGURBA** estimate (EAC-PM) ~**63% urban**; under-counting leaves peri-urban areas without municipal funds.
[42:1:10] - Urbanisation rose rapidly after 2001 (million cities 35 → 53) – unrelated to the mobile revolution.
[42:1:11] - Most urban religious group: **Jains (~80%)**; Buddhists over 43%; Muslims, Christians ~40%; Hindus 29%.

## SUB 5 | Causes of Urbanisation
[42:5:0] - Main component: **migration** (natural increase similar in rural and urban).
[42:5:1] - Push: **monsoon failure**, rural unemployment, non-growing farm land, rural-urban imbalance.
[42:5:2] - Pull: **magnetic features of cities** – jobs, amenities; plus industrialisation, commercialisation, transport, social change.
[42:5:3] - **Philip M. Hauser** – "**Population Implosion**" (rural-urban concentration); "**New Population Bomb**" = fast-growing Third World urban population.
[42:5:4] - Crime rises with urbanisation and industrialisation – family and cultural disorganisation.
[42:5:5] Statement sets mixing push and pull factors are usually **all correct**.
## SUB 6 | Urban Challenges
[42:6:0] # Slums
[42:6:1] - World Bank: ~**177 million** slum dwellers – largest in the world.
[42:6:2] - **Mumbai**: slums **41.3% of urban households** – highest share (Census 2011).
[42:6:3] - Among **million-plus cities**: **Greater Visakhapatnam ~44%** of population in slums; Greater Mumbai and Vijayawada ~41%.
[42:6:4] - Slum population (2001): Greater Mumbai 6.5 m, Delhi 1.9 m, Kolkata 1.5 m, Chennai 0.8 m.
[42:6:5] # Housing, waste, climate, governance
[42:6:6] - Urban housing shortage ~**18.78 million units** (2012 Technical Group).
[42:6:7] - Municipalities overwhelmed by **solid waste** and water demand.
[42:6:8] - **Urban heat island** 3-5°C; two-thirds of urban dwellers face pluvial flooding by 2030.
[42:6:9] - Governance: **fragmented authority**, little **fiscal autonomy** (ES 2025-26, ch.15 "Making India's Cities Work for its Citizens"); urbanising areas left under panchayats.
[42:6:10] - **India Urban Observatory** – New Delhi (MoHUA); evidence-based planning.
[42:6:11] - **MoHUA (2017)** = merger of Ministry of Urban Development + Ministry of Housing and Urban Poverty Alleviation.
[42:6:12] - **e-lala** – e-commerce portal for small traders (CAIT); Ministry of Urban Development, Nov 2015.
[42:6:13] Social Forestry is NOT under the Minimum Needs Programme.

## SUB 1 | Jnnurm – the First Mega Urban Mission
[43:1:0] - Launched **3 December 2005**; a **7-year programme**.
[43:1:1] - Urban infrastructure and basic services in **63 mission cities** (million-plus cities, State capitals, heritage cities); succeeded by **AMRUT** and **Smart Cities** (2015).
[43:1:2] - Objectives: urban infrastructure, houses and basic services for the poor; **urban electrification was NOT an objective**.
[43:1:3] - Two sub-missions: **UIG** (Urban Infrastructure and Governance) + **BSUP** (Basic Services to Urban Poor); small towns via **UIDSSMT** + **IHSDP**.
[43:1:4] # JNNURM Reforms
[43:1:5] - **25% of municipal budget** kept for the urban poor.
[43:1:6] - **25% of developed land** reserved for EWS/LIG.
[43:1:7] - 7-point charter: tenure, housing, water, sanitation, education, health, social security.
[43:1:8] BSUP and IHSDP belong to JNNURM; CLSS and ISSR belong to PMAY-U.
[43:1:9] # Earlier Sanitation Scheme
[43:1:10] - **ILCS** (Integrated Low Cost Sanitation), **1980-81**: end dry latrines, free manual scavengers, new latrines for EWS.
[43:1:11] - Funded on sharing basis; **central subsidy 75%**.
