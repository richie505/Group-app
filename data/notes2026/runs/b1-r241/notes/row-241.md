=== The Quit India Resolution – Causes, Dates and Venue
# Why 1942
| Cause | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Failure of the Cripps Mission** (March-April 1942) | No immediate transfer of power – the **main trigger**; QIM was launched **in response to the Cripps Proposals** (UPSC 2013) – not the Cabinet Mission, Simon Report or Wavell Plan |
| Japanese advance | Malaya, Singapore overrun; **Rangoon fell in March 1942**; Japanese troops at India's eastern border |
| Economic distress | Rising prices, shortage of rice and salt, boats commandeered in Bengal and Orissa |
| Loss of faith in British rule | Deposits withdrawn from banks; racist evacuation from South-East Asia ("Black Road" / "White Road") |
- **British PM:** **Winston Churchill**; **Viceroy:** **Lord Linlithgow**.
# Meetings and dates
| Date | Meeting / place | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| 6 July 1942 | CWC (Wardha) | Gandhi first discussed his Quit India plan; **Congress President = Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** (1940-46) |
| **14 July 1942** | **CWC at Wardha** | Resolution accepted – the **"Wardha Resolution"**; Gandhi authorised to lead a non-violent mass struggle |
| **7-8 August 1942** | **AICC, Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay** (now **August Kranti Maidan**) | Resolution **ratified on 8 August** – the **"August Resolution"**; "Do or Die" speech the same night |
| **9 August 1942** | All India | Leaders arrested at dawn; the movement **began** (keys: "began 9 August", "started August 1942") |
- **Year:** **1942** – not 1940 (Individual Satyagraha), 1946 or 1947; "August Kranti" is the other name of the movement (not October Revolution etc.).
- **Moved by:** **Jawaharlal Nehru**, **seconded by Sardar Patel** (Spectrum) – not Narendra Deva, Rajendra Prasad or Kripalani.
- **President of the Congress when it passed:** **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** – not Gandhi, Patel or Nehru.
- **Launched at:** **Bombay** – not Sabarmati, Calcutta, Madras, Lucknow, Lahore or Karachi; Wardha was only the CWC meeting.
- **Leader:** **Mahatma Gandhi** was named leader of the struggle.
- **⚠ Trap:** the AICC ratified it on **8 August**, not 6 August (a slip in one NextGen sheet); Gandhi's paper was **Harijan**, not Young India.
# What the resolution demanded
- **Immediate end of British rule**; a **provisional government** after withdrawal; free India to resist fascism; a **mass civil disobedience** struggle under Gandhi.
- NOT in it: continuing British rule until the war ended.
# Gandhi's instructions (spelt out at Gowalia Tank, never formally issued)
| Group | Instruction |
|---|---|
| Government servants | **Do not resign** – declare allegiance to the Congress |
| Soldiers | **Do not leave the army** – but do not fire on compatriots |
| Princes | **Accept the sovereignty of your own people** |
| Students | Leave studies if confident |
| Peasants | Pay agreed rent to anti-government zamindars; none to pro-government ones |
> Statement angle (UPSC 2005): "asked government servants to resign" ✗, "asked soldiers to leave their posts" ✗, "asked princes to accept the sovereignty of their people" ✓ → **only III**.
# Order of events
- **CDM (1930) → Individual Satyagraha (Oct 1940) → Cripps Mission (March 1942) → Quit India (Aug 1942)** = 1, 2, 4, 3.
- On **8 August 1942** the AICC adopted the Quit India Resolution (UPSC 2021) – Congress ministries had resigned in 1939, the Executive Council was expanded in July 1941, Cripps came in March 1942.

=== "Do or Die" and Other Slogans
# Do or Die
- **"Do or Die" ("Karo ya Maro")** – given by **Mahatma Gandhi** in his Gowalia Tank speech of **8 August 1942** – the slogan of the **Quit India Movement** (UPSC 2009; APPSC G1 2002 ×2).
  - Not Tilak, Nehru, Bose or Bhagat Singh; not the Swadeshi, Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, Khilafat or Dandi campaigns.
- **Full mantra:** "Do or Die. **We shall either free India or die in the attempt**; we shall not live to see the perpetuation of our slavery" – Gandhi (not Nehru, Azad or Savarkar), Quit India.
- **"Personally I am so sick of slavery that I am even prepared to take the risk of anarchy"** – Gandhi in 1942, the Quit India phase (NDA 2000).
- **"Freedom is within our reach and we should hold it very tight"** – Gandhi (not Tagore), Quit India.
- **"Quit India" (phrase):** coined by **Yusuf Meherally** (MPPSC 2018) – not Gandhi, Nehru or Aruna Asaf Ali.
- **Harijan**, not Young India (closed in 1932), carried Gandhi's Quit India writings.
> A-R angle: "QIM awakened and encouraged the people" ✓ because "people took up 'Do or Die'" ✓ → R explains A.

=== Arrests, Detention and Repression
# Operation Zero Hour
- **Pre-dawn arrests of 9 August 1942** – all top leaders taken to unknown places; CWC, AICC and PCCs declared **unlawful** (Criminal Law Amendment Act 1908) → the movement became **leaderless, spontaneous and violent**.
- **Baldev Sahai** resigned as **Advocate General (Bihar)** in **1942** in protest against the arrests.
# Where the leaders were held
| Place | Who |
|---|---|
| **Aga Khan Palace, Poona** | **Gandhi** (with Kasturba, Mahadev Desai, Sarojini Naidu) – about **21 months** (Aug 1942 – May 1944) |
| **Ahmednagar Fort** | Nehru, Azad, Patel and the other CWC members |
- **Samadhis of Kasturba and Mahadev Desai:** in the **Aga Khan Palace, Pune** – not Yerwada, Ahmednagar Fort or Alipur jail; Kasturba died there in **1944**.
- **Gandhi's fast:** **21 days from 10 February 1943** at the Aga Khan Palace, against the violence of the State → **three members of the Viceroy's Executive Council resigned**.
# Repression and the Viceroy
- **Lord Linlithgow** (1936-43, longest-serving Viceroy) called the movement **"by far the most serious rebellion since 1857"** – because of the **massive peasant upsurge** in eastern UP and Bihar (UPSC 1999: A and R true, R explains A).
- **Linlithgow** called Gandhi's movement **"political blackmail"** – not Chelmsford, Wavell or Montagu.
- Crowds lathi-charged, fired on, even machine-gunned from the air; **about 10,000 killed**; press muzzled; villages fined and flogged.
- Martyrs: brothers **Gangaram and Kheemdev** fell in police firing at **Almora** (Uttarakhand) in 1942.

=== Underground Activity and Women
# Underground leaders
| Leader | Activity |
|---|---|
| **Jayaprakash Narayan** | **Escaped Hazaribagh Jail** on Diwali, **9 November 1942** (with **Yogendra Shukla** and others); formed the **Azad Dasta** at **Bakri Ka Tapu** (Nepal Terai) – guerrilla war; became a **national leader** through Quit India |
| **Usha Mehta** | Ran the **secret Congress Radio** from Bombay ("42.34 metres") – UPSC 2011 |
| **Ram Manohar Lohia** | Made **regular broadcasts** on the Congress Radio |
| **Aruna Asaf Ali** | **Hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank on 9 August**; main woman organiser of the underground (UPSC 2009) |
| **Sucheta Kripalani** | Led the Gandhian underground group while in hiding |
| Others | Achyut Patwardhan, Biju Patnaik, Chhotubhai Puranik, R.P. Goenka |
- **Yogendra Shukla** was rearrested at **Muzaffarpur** on **7 December 1942** and held in Buxar jail (Wikipedia); the Bihar PYQ key "brought to **Patna**" is from the book key only.
- **⚠ Trap:** Sachchidananda Sinha, Jagjivan Ram, Rafi Ahmed Kidwai did not escape from Hazaribagh; Kripalani, Lohia, Patwardhan did not break jail.
- **Sabotage:** bridges, railway tracks and telegraph lines – most intense in **eastern UP and Bihar**.

=== Nature, Parties and Significance
# Nature
- **Spontaneous:** the arrests left it leaderless; historian **F.G. Hutchins** called it a **"Spontaneous Revolution"** (book, 1971) – not Gordon Johnson, David Arnold or Peter Robb.
- **Not wholly non-violent:** peaceful at first, then attacks on police stations, post offices and railways after the firings.
- **Labour did join:** strikes in **Ahmedabad (textile mills, over three months)**, Bombay, Jamshedpur, Ahmednagar, Poona.
> UPSC 2011 "NOT true": the key is **"It did not attract the labour class in general"** (most keys); some keys give "led by Gandhi" (he was arrested at once).
- **Storm centres:** **Bihar and eastern UP** (Azamgarh, Ballia, Gorakhpur; Gaya, Bhagalpur, Saran), Midnapore, Maharashtra, Karnataka.
  - ⚠ Spectrum and PMF: there were **no communal riots** during Quit India – a "communal riots" stem means the disturbances.
# Attitude of parties
| Party | Stand |
|---|---|
| **Muslim League** | **Opposed / condemned** it – British and Muslims drew closer (A and R true, R explains A) |
| **Hindu Mahasabha** | **Boycotted** it |
| **CPI** | Did **not support** it – "People's War" after Germany attacked the USSR (ban lifted 23 July 1942) |
| **Unionist Party (Punjab)**, princes | Did not support |
| **RSS** | Kept away |
- **All of these** (Hindu Mahasabha, CPI, Unionists) did not support QIM.
# Significance
- **Gandhi's last mass movement**; the **culmination** of the national movement – after it, only the **mode of transfer of power** remained to be settled (A and R true, R explains A).
- Put **immediate independence** on the agenda; showed India could not be ruled against Indians' will.
- **Struggle-Truce-Struggle (S-T-S):** Bipan Chandra's name for the Congress strategy of mass phases and truces; the UPPSC 2019 key links its prominence to the Quit India call.
# Quit India today
- **9 August 2016:** **Maharashtra** launched **"Quit India Movement 2"** against social evils at August Kranti Maidan, Mumbai (CM Devendra Fadnavis) – the anniversary event.
