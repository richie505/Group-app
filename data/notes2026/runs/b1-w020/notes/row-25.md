=== Capitals of Magadha – Rajgriha, Pataliputra, Vaishali
# Capitals in order
| Capital | Ruler | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Girivraja / Rajgriha** (modern **Rajgir**) | Brihadratha line (Jarasandha); **Bimbisara**, Ajatashatru | **First capital**; ringed by **five hills**, walled – impregnable |
| **Pataliputra** (fort) | **Ajatashatru** | Fortified the village **Pataligrama** and built the city as a base against the Vajjis |
| **Pataliputra** (capital) | **Udayin** | **Shifted the capital** from Rajgriha to Pataliputra |
| **Vaishali** | **Shishunaga** | Capital moved there **temporarily** |
| **Pataliputra** again | **Kalashoka** | Capital moved back; it stayed the capital of the Mauryas and Guptas |
- **First / earliest / initial capital:** **Girivraja (Rajgriha, Rajgir)** – asked 6 times (State PSC ×3, BPSC 2004, NDA 2001). Not Pataliputra, Vaishali, Champa (capital of **Anga**) or Varanasi.
- **⚠ Option missing:** a State PSC paper asked the first capital with only Pataliputra, Vaishali, Champa and Anga – none is right; the answer is **Rajgriha**.
- **Vaishali:** capital of the **Lichchhavi (Vajji) republic**, named after King **Vishal**; near **Mahavira's birthplace**; venue of the **Second** (not First) Buddhist Council.
- **Not a Magadhan capital:** **Kaushambi** – capital of **Vatsa** (CGPSC 2011).
# Who founded Pataliputra
- **Ajatashatru** fortified Pataligrama and **built the city of Pataliputra** – the answer when Udayin is not offered (APPSC G1 2002 and G1 PYQ: Ajatashatru; Dhanyakataka, Nalanda, Lumbini are distractors).
- **Udayin** made it the **capital**; Jain texts call him the **founder of Pataliputra** – the answer when Ajatashatru is not offered ("who founded Pataliputra": Udayin, not Ashoka, Bimbisara, Mahapadma).
- **⚠ Disputed:** with both offered, most books and keys give **Ajatashatru** for the city and **Udayin** for the capital.
- **"First chose Pataliputra as capital":** **Udayin** (not Ajatashatru, Kalashoka or Kanishka).
# Pataliputra – site and remains
- **Site:** modern **Patna**, at the **confluence of the Ganga and the Son**; surrounded by rivers – a **water-fort (jaladurga)**.
- **Excavated remains:** **Kumrahar** (Mauryan pillared hall) and **Bulandibagh** (wooden palisade) – both = Pataliputra, not Vaishali, Kapilavastu, Shravasti or Varanasi.
- **Decline:** Hiuen-Tsang (7th c. CE) found it in ruins.
# Rajgir today
- **Vishwa Shanti Stupa:** the World Peace Pagoda on **Ratnagiri hill, Rajgir** – billed as the highest in the world (BPSC 2008).
- **Rajgriha** was also the place of **Mahavira's first sermon** and of the **First Buddhist Council** (Saptaparni cave).
> Exam angle: "first capital" → **Rajgriha**; "built Pataliputra / Pataligrama fort" → **Ajatashatru**; "made it the capital" → **Udayin**; Kumrahar, Bulandibagh → **Pataliputra**.

=== Haryankas – Bimbisara, Ajatashatru, Udayin
# Bimbisara (c. 545–493 BCE)
- **Founder of the Haryanka dynasty** (NextGen Rocket; APPSC; SSC CGL 2019) – PMF adds that many scholars give the founding to his grandfather, but exams key **Bimbisara**. Brihadratha belongs to the earlier legendary line.
- **Real founder of the Magadhan empire**; also called **Seniya / Shrenika** (WBCS 2017).
- **Contemporary of the Buddha** and of Mahavira (APPSC GS 2018; FRO 2020; Sachivalayam).
  - **⚠ Disputed:** Ajatashatru was also a contemporary – the Buddha died in his reign – but keys give **Bimbisara** when both are offered.
- **Conquest:** defeated Brahmadatta of **Anga** and put **Kunika (Ajatashatru)** as viceroy at **Champa**.
- **Diplomacy:** friendship with **Pradyota of Avanti**; when Pradyota had jaundice, Bimbisara sent his royal physician **Jivaka** to Ujjain.
- **Jivaka:** physician of Bimbisara's court (UP UDA 2006; State PSC); son of the Rajgriha courtesan **Salavati** (MPPSC 2009) – not Amrapali (Vaishali).
| Queen | Clan / kingdom | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Kosaladevi (Mahakosala)** | Kosala, sister of Prasenajit | Brought **Kashi** as dowry |
| **Chellana** | **Lichchhavi** of Vaishali | **Mother of Ajatashatru** (not Udayin) |
| **Khema** | **Madra** (Punjab) | – |
- **Death:** imprisoned and starved to death by his son **Ajatashatru**.
# Ajatashatru (c. 493–462 BCE)
- **Parricide:** killed his father Bimbisara (UPPCS 2011) – Jain name **Kunika**, "Pitruhanta"; he was in turn **killed by his son Udayin** (UPPCS 2007; UPPSC 2007).
- **Patricide chain (Buddhist texts):** **Bimbisara → Ajatashatru → Udayin**.
- **Kosala:** war with **Prasenajit** over Kashi; Prasenajit gave his daughter **Vajira** with Kashi as dowry; later annexed Kosala.
- **Vajji:** annexed after a **16-year** war; minister **Vassakara** split the Lichchhavi chiefs.
- **War engines:** **mahashilakantaka** (stone-throwing catapult) and **rathamusala** (chariot fitted with a mace).
- **Buddhism:** met the Buddha (Bharhut relief); hosted the **First Buddhist Council** at Rajgriha.
# Udayin (c. 462–444 BCE)
- **Shifted the capital to Pataliputra**; a devout Jain in Jain tradition. Not to be confused with **Udayana of Vatsa**.
- Probably killed at the urging of **Palaka of Avanti**; his weak successors ended with **Nagadasaka**.
# Contemporaries
- **Milinda (Menander)**, the 2nd-century-BCE Indo-Greek, was **not** a contemporary of Bimbisara, the Buddha and **Prasenajit (Kosala)** (IAS 2005).
> Exam angle: "founder of Haryanka" → **Bimbisara**; "killed his father and was killed by his son" → **Ajatashatru**; "Jivaka" → **Bimbisara's court**.

=== Shishunagas and Nandas
# Shishunaga dynasty (c. 413–345 BCE)
- **Shishunaga:** a viceroy at Banaras / amatya **chosen by the people** after Udayin's weak successors; moved the capital to **Vaishali**.
- **Malwa:** Shishunaga **destroyed Avanti** (capital **Ujjayini**) and annexed Avanti and Vatsa – the expansion over Malwa (UP Lower 2008 ×2).
- **Kalashoka:** moved the capital **back to Pataliputra**; **Second Buddhist Council** at Vaishali (383 BCE).
- **End:** the last Shishunaga, **Mahanandin**, was killed by **Mahapadma Nanda**.
# Conquests in order
| Ruler | Annexed |
|---|---|
| Bimbisara | **Anga** |
| Ajatashatru | **Kosala, Vajji** |
| Shishunaga | **Avanti, Vatsa** |
| Mahapadma Nanda | **Kalinga**, Kshatriya kingdoms of the north |
# Nanda dynasty (c. 345–321 BCE)
- **Sequence:** Haryanka → **Shishunaga → Nanda → Maurya** (State PSC; BPSC 2005).
- **Mahapadma Nanda:** founder; **first non-Kshatriya dynasty**; **first empire-builder** of India; titles **Ekarat** (sole sovereign) and **Sarvakshatrantaka** (destroyer of Kshatriyas) → hence **Aparoparashurama**, "a second Parashurama" (UPUDA 2010).
- **Hathigumpha inscription** of Kharavela: a **Nanda king dug a canal in Kalinga** and carried off a Jina image (UPPCS 1999).
- **Dhana Nanda:** **last Nanda** (RRB 2001); Greek **Agrammes / Xandrames**; ruled at **Alexander's invasion** (326 BCE) – the dynasty ruling North India then was the **Nanda** (UPSC 2000).
- **Fall:** Chandragupta Maurya, with Chanakya, overthrew Dhana Nanda in **c. 321 BCE**.
- **Army (Greek accounts):** about 200,000 infantry, 60,000 cavalry, 3,000–6,000 elephants.
> Exam angle: "after the Shishunagas" → **Nandas**; "after the Nandas" → **Mauryas**; "Malwa added" → **Shishunaga**; "Aparoparashurama" → **Mahapadma Nanda**.

=== Causes of Magadhan Supremacy
- **Most powerful and richest Mahajanapada** (6th–4th c. BCE); it became the base of the **Mauryan Empire**. Puranas: the first dynasty was the **Brihadratha** (Jarasandha ruled from **Girivraj**).
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Iron mines** nearby (Jharkhand) | Weapons and tools; forest clearing |
| **Alluvial soil, ample rainfall** | Agricultural surplus, taxes |
| **Ganga and Son** (navigable) | Cheap transport and trade |
| **Elephants** from the eastern forests | First large-scale use of war elephants |
| **Naturally fortified capitals** | Rajgriha (five hills), Pataliputra (water-fort) |
| **Unorthodox society** | Little Vedic hold; ambitious rulers and ministers |
- **⚠ Not a cause:** control of western sea ports, gold mines, superior cavalry or a republican system.
- **Buddhist councils linked to Magadhan kings:** First – **Ajatashatru** (Rajgriha); Second – **Kalashoka** (Vaishali); Third – **Ashoka** (Pataliputra).
