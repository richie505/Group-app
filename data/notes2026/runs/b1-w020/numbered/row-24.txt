## SUB 0 | Guilds (Shreni), Merchants and Trade Terms
[0:0] - **Shrenis:** associations of artisans, merchants, moneylenders under elected or hereditary heads (**jetthaka / pamukha**). Craft shrenis trained workers, procured raw materials, sold products; merchant shrenis organised trade.
[0:1] - **Powers (UPSC 2012 → II and III):** fixed **wages, rules of work, standards and prices**; had **their own laws (shreni-dharma)** and **judicial powers** over members. They were **autonomous – the king did not interfere**, so "every guild registered with the central authority, king the chief administrator" is **wrong**.
[0:2] - **Guilds as banks:** deposits invested at interest – **Nashik inscription** records guild deposits at fixed interest.
[0:3] - **Satapatha Brahmana:** first reference to **money-lending**; usurer = **kusidin**.
[0:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:4.0] Sarthavaha (satthavaha) | Caravan / convoy of merchants, or its leader
  [0:4.1] Setthi / Shreshthi | Rich merchant-banker
  [0:4.2] Gahapati | Wealthy householder / landowner
  [0:4.3] Vanija | Trader
  [0:4.4] Kusidin | Usurer

## SUB 1 | Punch-marked Coins and Early Money
[1:0] - **Punch-marked (Aahat) coins:** **earliest extant coins** of India (7th–6th c. BC to the Mauryan period); **mostly silver**, some copper; symbols only (**sun, animals, trees, hills**), no writing.
[1:1] - Called **panas / karshapanas**; issued under Haryankas, Nandas and Mauryas; first definite literary references in **Pali texts**.
[1:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [1:2.0] Nishka | Gold / a gold ornament in Vedic texts (nishka-griva = gold necklace); a prestige object, later a unit – not cows or copper/silver coins
  [1:2.1] Karshapana / kahapana | Standard silver or copper punch-marked coin
  [1:2.2] Satamana | Value or weight unit of 100 mana
  [1:2.3] Suvarna | Gold coin (Satavahana usage)
[1:3] - **Satavahanas** first used inherited punch-marked coins, then die-struck coins of **lead**, potin, copper, silver; **ship-mast** coins of **Yajnasri Satakarni**.
[1:4] - **Gold coins in large numbers:** **Kushanas** (not Mauryas).
[1:5] - **Vaishya Tekri** (near Ujjain): Mauryan-era (BCE) stupa bigger than Sanchi; punch-marked and cast Avanti coins; MP to re-excavate it (2026).

## SUB 2 | Northern Black Polished Ware and the Second Urbanisation
[2:0] - **NBPW:** fine, glossy black, mirror-like **deluxe tableware of the rich**; **c. 700–200 BCE**; **after Painted Grey Ware**; the hallmark of the second urbanisation.
[2:1] - **NBPW sites:** mainly the Ganga valley – Hastinapur, Kaushambi, Shravasti, Ayodhya, Varanasi, Rajgir, Pataliputra, Vaishali, Champa; also Ujjain, Vidisha, Taxila (not Lothal).
[2:2] TABLE head: Cause | Effect
  [2:2.0] Iron tools | Forest clearance in the Ganga plain; surplus
  [2:2.1] Trade, crafts, coinage | Taxes and wages in money
  [2:2.2] State formation | Mahajanapada capitals became administrative centres
  [2:2.3] New merchant classes | Vaishyas backed Buddhism and Jainism
[2:3] - **Over 60 towns** in 600–300 BCE; the Mahajanapadas and Magadha = the **second urbanisation**.
[2:4] - **Rajghat (Varanasi):** pre-NBPW levels, punch-marked coins, **Greek-inscribed seals**; BHU re-study (Sep 2026) puts urban Varanasi at **c. 800 BCE**.
[2:5] TABLE head: Development | Period
  [2:5.0] Iron at Mayiladumparai (AMS dating) | c. 2172 BCE; southern Iron Age c. 2100 BCE – older than the Gangetic Iron Age
  [2:5.1] Iron ploughshares, axes in the Ganga valley | Later Vedic, c. 1000–600 BCE
  [2:5.2] Urban Varanasi | c. 800 BCE
  [2:5.3] NBPW | c. 700–200 BCE
  [2:5.4] Punch-marked coins begin | 7th–6th c. BCE
  [2:5.5] Gold coins in large numbers | Kushanas, 1st–2nd c. CE
