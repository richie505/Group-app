## SUB 0 | Varna Order, Untouchability and Guilds
[0:0] - Society stratified in the order **Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra** = the **Varna system** (varna came to rest on **birth**; spread of jatis is post-Vedic).
[0:1] TABLE head: Varna | Role
  [0:1.0] Brahmana | Priests, teachers; most powerful
  [0:1.1] Kshatriya (Rajanya) | Rulers, warriors
  [0:1.2] Vaishya | Farmers, herders, traders, artisans; only tribute-payers
  [0:1.3] Shudra | Service; no upanayana, no Vedas, no Sabha
[0:2] - First statement of the four varnas: **Purusha Sukta, Rigveda 10th Mandala**; **Dvija** (twice-born) = upper three, entitled to upanayana.
[0:3] - **Aitareya Brahmana**: Shudra = "**servant of another**", could be expelled or beaten at will.
[0:4] - Outside the varna order: **Vratyas, Nishadas**.
[0:5] - Varna + ashrama = **Varnashrama Dharma**.
[0:6] # Untouchability
[0:7] - **Chhandogya Upanishad**: the **Chandala** as a person of despised birth – an early sign only; untouchability was absent in the Rigvedic age.
[0:8] - Untouchability took **clear, formal shape in the Dharmashastra phase** (early Christian era), not in the Rigvedic or Later Vedic phase.
[0:9] # Guilds and money-lending
[0:10] - **Sarthavaha** (caravan leader, head of a trade guild / Srenipramukh) = **Vaishya**; guild rules = **Samay, Srenidharma**; Vaishya guilds (gana) headed by a **Shreshthin**.
[0:11] - First reference to **money-lending**: **Shatapatha Brahmana**; usurer = **kusidin**.

## SUB 1 | Ashramas, Purusharthas and Pancha Mahayajnas
[1:0] TABLE head: Ashrama | Duty
  [1:0.0] Brahmacharya | Student; Vedic study
  [1:0.1] Grihastha | Householder; daily Pancha Mahayajnas
  [1:0.2] Vanaprastha | Gradual withdrawal (forest)
  [1:0.3] Sannyasa | Complete renunciation; added last
[1:1] - **Four ashramas** are described; they are the custom that **emerged in the post-Vedic (Later Vedic) period** – the varnas go back to the Rigveda's Purusha Sukta and Indra-Surya-Rudra-Maruts are Rigvedic gods.
[1:2] - Only the first three were established in Later Vedic times: first three in the **Chhandogya Upanishad**; all four first in the **Jabala Upanishad** (PMF: early Dharmasutras).
[1:3] - **Pancha Mahayajnas:** **Brahma** (Vedic study), **Deva** (gods), **Pitri** (ancestors), **Bhuta** (living beings), **Nri / Atithi** (hospitality to guests).
[1:4] - **Purusharthas:** Dharma (duty), Artha (wealth), Kama (desire) = **trivarga**; **Moksha** (liberation) added later under Upanishadic ideas.
[1:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.136; old app notes 1:20
## SUB 2 | Gotra and Kinship
[2:0] - **Gotra** literally = **cow-pen** (word first in the **Rigveda**, meaning cow shelter); as a **clan descended from a common male ancestor** it was established in **Later Vedic (post-Vedic) times**; the Atharvaveda mentions gotra in the lineage sense.
[2:1] - **Gotra exogamy**: no marriage within the same gotra (with caste endogamy and **sapinda** rules).
[2:2] - **Kula** = family; **Vamsha** = lineage; **Gotra** = clan; **Kosa = treasury (odd one out)**.
[2:3] - **Irawati Karve**: *Kinship Organization in India* (1953) – four kinship zones.
[2:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.29, p.32; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.136; old app notes 1:20
## SUB 3 | Samskaras, Upanayana and Education
[3:0] - **Samskaras = 16** (shodasha): 3 before birth (Garbhadhana, Pumsavana, Simantonnayana), Jatakarma, Chudakarma (tonsure, 3rd year) … Upanayana, Vivaha, Antyeshti.
[3:1] - **Upanayana** = sacred-thread initiation into Vedic study for the **upper three varnas**, in the boy's **8th year**; full description in the **Shatapatha Brahmana**; **Shudras and women excluded**.
[3:2] TABLE head: Varna | Upanayana metre
  [3:2.0] Brahmana | Gayatri
  [3:2.1] Kshatriya | Trishtubh
  [3:2.2] Vaishya | Jagati
[3:3] - **Gurukula**: the pupil **lived with the guru**.
[3:4] TABLE head: Teacher | Meaning
  [3:4.0] Acharya | Initiated the pupil; taught the whole Veda free
  [3:4.1] Upadhyaya | Taught Veda / Vedangas for a livelihood
[3:5] > Kshatriya = Trishtubh; Upadhyaya paid, Acharya free.
[3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.31-32; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.117; old app notes 1:20
## SUB 4 | Forms of Marriage
[4:0] TABLE head: Form | Nature
  [4:0.0] Brahma | Duly dowered daughter given to a Veda-learned man of good conduct
  [4:0.1] Daiva | Daughter given to the officiating priest as fee
  [4:0.2] Arsha | Groom gives a token cow and bull
  [4:0.3] Prajapatya | No dowry or bride price; couple jointly perform dharma
  [4:0.4] Asura | Marriage by purchase (bride price)
  [4:0.5] Gandharva | Mutual love / consent
  [4:0.6] Rakshasa | Marriage by capture
  [4:0.7] Paishacha | Seduction of a sleeping or drunk girl; lowest
[4:1] - **8 forms** listed by Manu and Kautilya; first four approved.
[4:2] - **Anuloma** = higher-varna man + lower-varna woman; **Pratiloma** = lower-varna man + higher-varna woman (disapproved).
[4:3] - **Satavahana Andhra (AP angle):** Gautamiputra Satakarni claimed to protect varnashrama dharma; anuloma offspring = **Ugra**, pratiloma offspring = **Chandala**.
[4:4] > Asura = purchase; Rakshasa = capture; Gandharva = love.
[4:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.33; old app notes 1:20
## SUB 5 | Status of Women
[5:0] - **Declined**: lost upanayana and political rights (barred from the **Sabha**), excluded from most rituals, could not choose husbands; child marriage appears; symbolic widow self-immolation (sati as a practice is later).
[5:1] TABLE head: Text | Statement
  [5:1.0] Aitareya Brahmana | Daughter = source of misery; son = protector
  [5:1.1] Shatapatha Brahmana | Wife is half her husband
  [5:1.2] Maitrayani Samhita | Liquor, dice and women = three evils
  [5:1.3] Brihadaranyaka Upanishad | Gargi and Maitreyi debated with Yajnavalkya in Janaka's court
[5:2] - Learned women of Vedic literature: **Gargi, Maitreyi** (Upanishadic); Apala, Ghosha, Lopamudra, Vishvavara (Rigvedic hymn-composers).
[5:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.32-33; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.138, p.149, p.151; old app notes 1:20
