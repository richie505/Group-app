=== NOTES 1:20 | Later Vedic Society – Varnas, Ashramas, Gotra, Status of Women
## SUB 0 | Varna Order, Untouchability and Guilds
[20:0:0] - Society stratified in the order **Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra** = the **Varna system** (varna came to rest on **birth**; spread of jatis is post-Vedic).
[20:0:1] TABLE head: Varna | Role
  [20:0:1.0] Brahmana | Priests, teachers; most powerful
  [20:0:1.1] Kshatriya (Rajanya) | Rulers, warriors
  [20:0:1.2] Vaishya | Farmers, herders, traders, artisans; only tribute-payers
  [20:0:1.3] Shudra | Service; no upanayana, no Vedas, no Sabha
[20:0:2] - First statement of the four varnas: **Purusha Sukta, Rigveda 10th Mandala**; **Dvija** (twice-born) = upper three, entitled to upanayana.
[20:0:3] - **Aitareya Brahmana**: Shudra = "**servant of another**", could be expelled or beaten at will.
[20:0:4] - Outside the varna order: **Vratyas, Nishadas**.
[20:0:5] - Varna + ashrama = **Varnashrama Dharma**.
[20:0:6] # Untouchability
[20:0:7] - **Chhandogya Upanishad**: the **Chandala** as a person of despised birth – an early sign only; untouchability was absent in the Rigvedic age.
[20:0:8] - Untouchability took **clear, formal shape in the Dharmashastra phase** (early Christian era), not in the Rigvedic or Later Vedic phase.
[20:0:9] # Guilds and money-lending
[20:0:10] - **Sarthavaha** (caravan leader, head of a trade guild / Srenipramukh) = **Vaishya**; guild rules = **Samay, Srenidharma**; Vaishya guilds (gana) headed by a **Shreshthin**.
[20:0:11] - First reference to **money-lending**: **Shatapatha Brahmana**; usurer = **kusidin**.
[20:0:12] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.29, p.32; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.135-137; old app notes 1:20
## SUB 1 | Ashramas, Purusharthas and Pancha Mahayajnas
[20:1:0] TABLE head: Ashrama | Duty
  [20:1:0.0] Brahmacharya | Student; Vedic study
  [20:1:0.1] Grihastha | Householder; daily Pancha Mahayajnas
  [20:1:0.2] Vanaprastha | Gradual withdrawal (forest)
  [20:1:0.3] Sannyasa | Complete renunciation; added last
[20:1:1] - **Four ashramas** are described; they are the custom that **emerged in the post-Vedic (Later Vedic) period** – the varnas go back to the Rigveda's Purusha Sukta and Indra-Surya-Rudra-Maruts are Rigvedic gods.
[20:1:2] - Only the first three were established in Later Vedic times: first three in the **Chhandogya Upanishad**; all four first in the **Jabala Upanishad** (PMF: early Dharmasutras).
[20:1:3] - **Pancha Mahayajnas:** **Brahma** (Vedic study), **Deva** (gods), **Pitri** (ancestors), **Bhuta** (living beings), **Nri / Atithi** (hospitality to guests).
[20:1:4] - **Purusharthas:** Dharma (duty), Artha (wealth), Kama (desire) = **trivarga**; **Moksha** (liberation) added later under Upanishadic ideas.
[20:1:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.136; old app notes 1:20
## SUB 2 | Gotra and Kinship
[20:2:0] - **Gotra** literally = **cow-pen** (word first in the **Rigveda**, meaning cow shelter); as a **clan descended from a common male ancestor** it was established in **Later Vedic (post-Vedic) times**; the Atharvaveda mentions gotra in the lineage sense.
[20:2:1] - **Gotra exogamy**: no marriage within the same gotra (with caste endogamy and **sapinda** rules).
[20:2:2] - **Kula** = family; **Vamsha** = lineage; **Gotra** = clan; **Kosa = treasury (odd one out)**.
[20:2:3] - **Irawati Karve**: *Kinship Organization in India* (1953) – four kinship zones.
[20:2:4] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.29, p.32; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.136; old app notes 1:20
## SUB 3 | Samskaras, Upanayana and Education
[20:3:0] - **Samskaras = 16** (shodasha): 3 before birth (Garbhadhana, Pumsavana, Simantonnayana), Jatakarma, Chudakarma (tonsure, 3rd year) … Upanayana, Vivaha, Antyeshti.
[20:3:1] - **Upanayana** = sacred-thread initiation into Vedic study for the **upper three varnas**, in the boy's **8th year**; full description in the **Shatapatha Brahmana**; **Shudras and women excluded**.
[20:3:2] TABLE head: Varna | Upanayana metre
  [20:3:2.0] Brahmana | Gayatri
  [20:3:2.1] Kshatriya | Trishtubh
  [20:3:2.2] Vaishya | Jagati
[20:3:3] - **Gurukula**: the pupil **lived with the guru**.
[20:3:4] TABLE head: Teacher | Meaning
  [20:3:4.0] Acharya | Initiated the pupil; taught the whole Veda free
  [20:3:4.1] Upadhyaya | Taught Veda / Vedangas for a livelihood
[20:3:5] > Kshatriya = Trishtubh; Upadhyaya paid, Acharya free.
[20:3:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.31-32; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.117; old app notes 1:20
## SUB 4 | Forms of Marriage
[20:4:0] TABLE head: Form | Nature
  [20:4:0.0] Brahma | Duly dowered daughter given to a Veda-learned man of good conduct
  [20:4:0.1] Daiva | Daughter given to the officiating priest as fee
  [20:4:0.2] Arsha | Groom gives a token cow and bull
  [20:4:0.3] Prajapatya | No dowry or bride price; couple jointly perform dharma
  [20:4:0.4] Asura | Marriage by purchase (bride price)
  [20:4:0.5] Gandharva | Mutual love / consent
  [20:4:0.6] Rakshasa | Marriage by capture
  [20:4:0.7] Paishacha | Seduction of a sleeping or drunk girl; lowest
[20:4:1] - **8 forms** listed by Manu and Kautilya; first four approved.
[20:4:2] - **Anuloma** = higher-varna man + lower-varna woman; **Pratiloma** = lower-varna man + higher-varna woman (disapproved).
[20:4:3] - **Satavahana Andhra (AP angle):** Gautamiputra Satakarni claimed to protect varnashrama dharma; anuloma offspring = **Ugra**, pratiloma offspring = **Chandala**.
[20:4:4] > Asura = purchase; Rakshasa = capture; Gandharva = love.
[20:4:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.33; old app notes 1:20
## SUB 5 | Status of Women
[20:5:0] - **Declined**: lost upanayana and political rights (barred from the **Sabha**), excluded from most rituals, could not choose husbands; child marriage appears; symbolic widow self-immolation (sati as a practice is later).
[20:5:1] TABLE head: Text | Statement
  [20:5:1.0] Aitareya Brahmana | Daughter = source of misery; son = protector
  [20:5:1.1] Shatapatha Brahmana | Wife is half her husband
  [20:5:1.2] Maitrayani Samhita | Liquor, dice and women = three evils
  [20:5:1.3] Brihadaranyaka Upanishad | Gargi and Maitreyi debated with Yajnavalkya in Janaka's court
[20:5:2] - Learned women of Vedic literature: **Gargi, Maitreyi** (Upanishadic); Apala, Ghosha, Lopamudra, Vishvavara (Rigvedic hymn-composers).
[20:5:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.32-33; PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.138, p.149, p.151; old app notes 1:20

## SUB 9 | Layers of Vedic Literature – Shruti
[21:9:0] - Order: **Samhitas → Brahmanas → Aranyakas → Upanishads**; Vedangas, Sutras, Smritis come later.
[21:9:1] - All are **Shruti** ("heard"), **apaurusheya** (not of human origin); Smriti = remembered (Dharmashastras, epics, Puranas).
[21:9:2] - **Samhitas + Brahmanas = Karma-kanda**; **Aranyakas + Upanishads = Jnana-kanda**.
[21:9:3] - **Brahmanas**: prose commentaries that **explain the Vedic hymns** and the meaning of sacrifices (karma-marga; "liturgies").
[21:9:4] - **Aranyakas**: "**forest texts**", concluding parts of the Brahmanas; symbolic / secret meaning of ritual; bridge karma-marga → jnana-marga. **Atharvaveda has no Aranyaka**.
[21:9:5] - **Veda Vyasa (Krishna Dvaipayana)** compiled and classified the Vedas. Veda from *vid* = to know.

[22:5:1] - **Astika:** the six darshanas. **Nastika:** **Charvaka, Buddhism (Bauddha), Jainism, Ajivika**.
[22:5:2] - **Charvaka = Lokayata** ("of the common people"); credited to **Brihaspati**; **materialism**; **perception** the only valid knowledge; no soul, rebirth or afterlife.
[22:5:3] - **Ajita Kesakambali** – early materialist of the Buddha's time.
[22:5:4] > Odd-one-out lists slip a nastika school (Charvaka, Bauddha, Ajivika) among the six.

## SUB 6 | Nature of Vedic Religion – Rita, Dharma, Sacrifice
[22:6:0] - **Nature worship**; male gods dominate; worship for material gains; **sacrifice (yajna) central**, in the open air – **no temples, no idols** (UPPSC 1993: "sacrifice and rituals").
[22:6:1] - **Henotheism** (term of **Max Müller**): each god praised in turn as supreme.
[22:6:2] - **Rita** = cosmic and moral order, upheld by **Varuna**; **Dharma** = obligation and duty to oneself and others, grown from Rita → UPSC 2011 "both".
[22:6:3] - Iranian parallels: **Mitra–Mithra, Rita–Asha, Soma–Haoma**.

  [22:6:4.0] Hotri (Rig), Adhvaryu (Yajur), Udgatri (Sama) | Priests
  [22:6:4.1] Ceremonial chanting | Samaveda
  [22:6:4.2] Agnistoma | Regular Soma sacrifice
  [22:6:4.3] Rajasuya, Vajapeya, Ashvamedha | Royal sacrifices
  [22:6:4.4] Grihya Sutras | Domestic rites
[22:6:5] - **Sanskaras: 16** = 3 before birth, 12 during life, **1 after death**.
[22:6:6] - **Later Vedic:** elaborate, costly sacrifices; Prajapati supreme; Indra and Agni decline; priests grow powerful.

## SUB 7 | Upanishadic Philosophy
[22:7:0] TABLE head: Concept | Meaning
  [22:7:0.0] Brahman | Ultimate reality (cosmic principle)
  [22:7:0.1] Atman | The self – not body or mind
  [22:7:0.2] Karma | Links action with consequence
  [22:7:0.3] Samsara | Rebirth – transmigration first in the Brihadaranyaka
  [22:7:0.4] Moksha | Liberation – not a Vedic Samhita term; first discussed in the Upanishads
[22:7:1] - **Main theme = philosophy** (UPPSC 2004, UP Lower 1998); anti-ritualistic; **Atman is Brahman**.
[22:7:2] - Criticised **mechanical sacrifice**; stressed meditation and the guru-disciple relationship; **ignorance** = cause of sorrow.
[22:7:3] - **Katha (Katha) Upanishad:** **Nachiketa** questions **Yama** on death and the Atman (UPSC 1997).
[22:7:4] - Basic approach is universal – it does not reject other schools or scriptures.

## SUB 0 | Guilds (Shreni), Merchants and Trade Terms
[24:0:0] - **Shrenis:** associations of artisans, merchants, moneylenders under elected or hereditary heads (**jetthaka / pamukha**). Craft shrenis trained workers, procured raw materials, sold products; merchant shrenis organised trade.
[24:0:1] - **Powers (UPSC 2012 → II and III):** fixed **wages, rules of work, standards and prices**; had **their own laws (shreni-dharma)** and **judicial powers** over members. They were **autonomous – the king did not interfere**, so "every guild registered with the central authority, king the chief administrator" is **wrong**.
[24:0:2] - **Guilds as banks:** deposits invested at interest – **Nashik inscription** records guild deposits at fixed interest.
[24:0:3] - **Satapatha Brahmana:** first reference to **money-lending**; usurer = **kusidin**.
[24:0:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [24:0:4.0] Sarthavaha (satthavaha) | Caravan / convoy of merchants, or its leader
  [24:0:4.1] Setthi / Shreshthi | Rich merchant-banker
  [24:0:4.2] Gahapati | Wealthy householder / landowner
  [24:0:4.3] Vanija | Trader
  [24:0:4.4] Kusidin | Usurer
[24:0:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.15-17; NextGen Indian History Part 3 (guilds' own laws); old app notes row 24
## SUB 1 | Punch-marked Coins and Early Money
[24:1:0] - **Punch-marked (Aahat) coins:** **earliest extant coins** of India (7th–6th c. BC to the Mauryan period); **mostly silver**, some copper; symbols only (**sun, animals, trees, hills**), no writing.
[24:1:1] - Called **panas / karshapanas**; issued under Haryankas, Nandas and Mauryas; first definite literary references in **Pali texts**.
[24:1:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [24:1:2.0] Nishka | Gold / a gold ornament in Vedic texts (nishka-griva = gold necklace); a prestige object, later a unit – not cows or copper/silver coins
  [24:1:2.1] Karshapana / kahapana | Standard silver or copper punch-marked coin
  [24:1:2.2] Satamana | Value or weight unit of 100 mana
  [24:1:2.3] Suvarna | Gold coin (Satavahana usage)
[24:1:3] - **Satavahanas** first used inherited punch-marked coins, then die-struck coins of **lead**, potin, copper, silver; **ship-mast** coins of **Yajnasri Satakarni**.
[24:1:4] - **Gold coins in large numbers:** **Kushanas** (not Mauryas).
[24:1:5] - **Vaishya Tekri** (near Ujjain): Mauryan-era (BCE) stupa bigger than Sanchi; punch-marked and cast Avanti coins; MP to re-excavate it (2026).

## SUB 0 | Haryankas – Bimbisara, Ajatashatru, Udayin
[25:0:0] # Bimbisara (c. 545–493 BCE)
[25:0:1] - **Founder of the Haryanka dynasty** (NextGen Rocket; APPSC; SSC CGL 2019) – PMF adds that many scholars give the founding to his grandfather, but exams key **Bimbisara**. Brihadratha belongs to the earlier legendary line.
[25:0:2] - **Real founder of the Magadhan empire**; also called **Seniya / Shrenika** (WBCS 2017).
[25:0:3] - **Contemporary of the Buddha** and of Mahavira (APPSC GS 2018; FRO 2020; Sachivalayam).
[25:0:4]   - **⚠ Disputed:** Ajatashatru was also a contemporary – the Buddha died in his reign – but keys give **Bimbisara** when both are offered.
[25:0:5] - **Conquest:** defeated Brahmadatta of **Anga** and put **Kunika (Ajatashatru)** as viceroy at **Champa**.
[25:0:6] - **Diplomacy:** friendship with **Pradyota of Avanti**; when Pradyota had jaundice, Bimbisara sent his royal physician **Jivaka** to Ujjain.
[25:0:7] - **Jivaka:** physician of Bimbisara's court (UP UDA 2006; State PSC); son of the Rajgriha courtesan **Salavati** (MPPSC 2009) – not Amrapali (Vaishali).
[25:0:8] TABLE head: Queen | Clan / kingdom | Note
  [25:0:8.0] Kosaladevi (Mahakosala) | Kosala, sister of Prasenajit | Brought Kashi as dowry
  [25:0:8.1] Chellana | Lichchhavi of Vaishali | Mother of Ajatashatru (not Udayin)
  [25:0:8.2] Khema | Madra (Punjab) | –
[25:0:9] - **Death:** imprisoned and starved to death by his son **Ajatashatru**.
[25:0:10] # Ajatashatru (c. 493–462 BCE)
[25:0:11] - **Parricide:** killed his father Bimbisara (UPPCS 2011) – Jain name **Kunika**, "Pitruhanta"; he was in turn **killed by his son Udayin** (UPPCS 2007; UPPSC 2007).
[25:0:12] - **Patricide chain (Buddhist texts):** **Bimbisara → Ajatashatru → Udayin**.
[25:0:13] - **Kosala:** war with **Prasenajit** over Kashi; Prasenajit gave his daughter **Vajira** with Kashi as dowry; later annexed Kosala.
[25:0:14] - **Vajji:** annexed after a **16-year** war; minister **Vassakara** split the Lichchhavi chiefs.
[25:0:15] - **War engines:** **mahashilakantaka** (stone-throwing catapult) and **rathamusala** (chariot fitted with a mace).
[25:0:16] - **Buddhism:** met the Buddha (Bharhut relief); hosted the **First Buddhist Council** at Rajgriha.
[25:0:17] # Udayin (c. 462–444 BCE)
[25:0:18] - **Shifted the capital to Pataliputra**; a devout Jain in Jain tradition. Not to be confused with **Udayana of Vatsa**.
[25:0:19] - Probably killed at the urging of **Palaka of Avanti**; his weak successors ended with **Nagadasaka**.
[25:0:20] # Contemporaries
[25:0:21] - **Milinda (Menander)**, the 2nd-century-BCE Indo-Greek, was **not** a contemporary of Bimbisara, the Buddha and **Prasenajit (Kosala)** (IAS 2005).
[25:0:22] > Exam angle: "founder of Haryanka" → **Bimbisara**; "killed his father and was killed by his son" → **Ajatashatru**; "Jivaka" → **Bimbisara's court**.

## SUB 1 | Capitals of Magadha – Rajgriha, Pataliputra, Vaishali
[25:1:0] # Capitals in order
[25:1:1] TABLE head: Capital | Ruler | Fact
  [25:1:1.0] Girivraja / Rajgriha (modern Rajgir) | Brihadratha line (Jarasandha); Bimbisara, Ajatashatru | First capital; ringed by five hills, walled – impregnable
  [25:1:1.1] Pataliputra (fort) | Ajatashatru | Fortified the village Pataligrama and built the city as a base against the Vajjis
  [25:1:1.2] Pataliputra (capital) | Udayin | Shifted the capital from Rajgriha to Pataliputra
  [25:1:1.3] Vaishali | Shishunaga | Capital moved there temporarily
  [25:1:1.4] Pataliputra again | Kalashoka | Capital moved back; it stayed the capital of the Mauryas and Guptas
[25:1:2] - **First / earliest / initial capital:** **Girivraja (Rajgriha, Rajgir)** – asked 6 times (State PSC ×3, BPSC 2004, NDA 2001). Not Pataliputra, Vaishali, Champa (capital of **Anga**) or Varanasi.
[25:1:3] - **⚠ Option missing:** a State PSC paper asked the first capital with only Pataliputra, Vaishali, Champa and Anga – none is right; the answer is **Rajgriha**.
[25:1:4] - **Vaishali:** capital of the **Lichchhavi (Vajji) republic**, named after King **Vishal**; near **Mahavira's birthplace**; venue of the **Second** (not First) Buddhist Council.
[25:1:5] - **Not a Magadhan capital:** **Kaushambi** – capital of **Vatsa** (CGPSC 2011).
[25:1:6] # Who founded Pataliputra
[25:1:7] - **Ajatashatru** fortified Pataligrama and **built the city of Pataliputra** – the answer when Udayin is not offered (APPSC G1 2002 and G1 PYQ: Ajatashatru; Dhanyakataka, Nalanda, Lumbini are distractors).
[25:1:8] - **Udayin** made it the **capital**; Jain texts call him the **founder of Pataliputra** – the answer when Ajatashatru is not offered ("who founded Pataliputra": Udayin, not Ashoka, Bimbisara, Mahapadma).
[25:1:9] - **⚠ Disputed:** with both offered, most books and keys give **Ajatashatru** for the city and **Udayin** for the capital.
[25:1:10] - **"First chose Pataliputra as capital":** **Udayin** (not Ajatashatru, Kalashoka or Kanishka).
[25:1:11] # Pataliputra – site and remains
[25:1:12] - **Site:** modern **Patna**, at the **confluence of the Ganga and the Son**; surrounded by rivers – a **water-fort (jaladurga)**.
[25:1:13] - **Excavated remains:** **Kumrahar** (Mauryan pillared hall) and **Bulandibagh** (wooden palisade) – both = Pataliputra, not Vaishali, Kapilavastu, Shravasti or Varanasi.
[25:1:14] - **Decline:** Hiuen-Tsang (7th c. CE) found it in ruins.
[25:1:15] # Rajgir today
[25:1:16] - **Vishwa Shanti Stupa:** the World Peace Pagoda on **Ratnagiri hill, Rajgir** – billed as the highest in the world (BPSC 2008).
[25:1:17] - **Rajgriha** was also the place of **Mahavira's first sermon** and of the **First Buddhist Council** (Saptaparni cave).
[25:1:18] > Exam angle: "first capital" → **Rajgriha**; "built Pataliputra / Pataligrama fort" → **Ajatashatru**; "made it the capital" → **Udayin**; Kumrahar, Bulandibagh → **Pataliputra**.

[25:1:20] - **Not Magadhan:** **Tughlaqabad** (Delhi) was built by **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq**, not Firoz Shah Tughlaq; Udayin succeeded Ajatashatru.
## SUB 2 | Shishunagas and Nandas
[25:2:0] # Shishunaga dynasty (c. 413–345 BCE)
[25:2:1] - **Shishunaga:** a viceroy at Banaras / amatya **chosen by the people** after Udayin's weak successors; moved the capital to **Vaishali**.
[25:2:2] - **Malwa:** Shishunaga **destroyed Avanti** (capital **Ujjayini**) and annexed Avanti and Vatsa – the expansion over Malwa (UP Lower 2008 ×2).
[25:2:3] - **Kalashoka:** moved the capital **back to Pataliputra**; **Second Buddhist Council** at Vaishali (383 BCE).
[25:2:4] - **End:** the last Shishunaga, **Mahanandin**, was killed by **Mahapadma Nanda**.
[25:2:5] # Conquests in order
[25:2:6] TABLE head: Ruler | Annexed
  [25:2:6.0] Bimbisara | Anga
  [25:2:6.1] Ajatashatru | Kosala, Vajji
  [25:2:6.2] Shishunaga | Avanti, Vatsa
  [25:2:6.3] Mahapadma Nanda | Kalinga, Kshatriya kingdoms of the north
[25:2:7] # Nanda dynasty (c. 345–321 BCE)
[25:2:8] - **Sequence:** Haryanka → **Shishunaga → Nanda → Maurya** (State PSC; BPSC 2005).
[25:2:9] - **Mahapadma Nanda:** founder; **first non-Kshatriya dynasty**; **first empire-builder** of India; titles **Ekarat** (sole sovereign) and **Sarvakshatrantaka** (destroyer of Kshatriyas) → hence **Aparoparashurama**, "a second Parashurama" (UPUDA 2010).
[25:2:10] - **Hathigumpha inscription** of Kharavela: a **Nanda king dug a canal in Kalinga** and carried off a Jina image (UPPCS 1999).
[25:2:11] - **Dhana Nanda:** **last Nanda** (RRB 2001); Greek **Agrammes / Xandrames**; ruled at **Alexander's invasion** (326 BCE) – the dynasty ruling North India then was the **Nanda** (UPSC 2000).
[25:2:12] - **Fall:** Chandragupta Maurya, with Chanakya, overthrew Dhana Nanda in **c. 321 BCE**.
[25:2:13] - **Army (Greek accounts):** about 200,000 infantry, 60,000 cavalry, 3,000–6,000 elephants.
[25:2:14] > Exam angle: "after the Shishunagas" → **Nandas**; "after the Nandas" → **Mauryas**; "Malwa added" → **Shishunaga**; "Aparoparashurama" → **Mahapadma Nanda**.
[25:2:15] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 2 p.101-102, p.105-106; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 2 p.40-41; app notes 1:25 (sub 4, 5)
## SUB 3 | Causes of Magadhan Supremacy
[25:3:0] - **Most powerful and richest Mahajanapada** (6th–4th c. BCE); it became the base of the **Mauryan Empire**. Puranas: the first dynasty was the **Brihadratha** (Jarasandha ruled from **Girivraj**).
[25:3:1] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [25:3:1.0] Iron mines nearby (Jharkhand) | Weapons and tools; forest clearing
  [25:3:1.1] Alluvial soil, ample rainfall | Agricultural surplus, taxes
  [25:3:1.2] Ganga and Son (navigable) | Cheap transport and trade
  [25:3:1.3] Elephants from the eastern forests | First large-scale use of war elephants
  [25:3:1.4] Naturally fortified capitals | Rajgriha (five hills), Pataliputra (water-fort)
  [25:3:1.5] Unorthodox society | Little Vedic hold; ambitious rulers and ministers
[25:3:2] - **⚠ Not a cause:** control of western sea ports, gold mines, superior cavalry or a republican system.
[25:3:3] - **Buddhist councils linked to Magadhan kings:** First – **Ajatashatru** (Rajgriha); Second – **Kalashoka** (Vaishali); Third – **Ashoka** (Pataliputra).
