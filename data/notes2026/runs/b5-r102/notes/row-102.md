=== Noise Pollution – Units, Sound Levels and Sources
# Units
| Quantity | Unit |
|---|---|
| Sound / noise level (intensity, loudness level) | **Decibel (dB)** – logarithmic; **dB(A)** is weighted to human hearing |
| Perceived loudness | **Sone** (phon = loudness level) |
| Frequency (pitch) | **Hertz (Hz)** |
| Hearing loss | **Decibels** (dB HL on an audiogram) |
- **Noise pollution is measured in decibels**, not Celsius, Daltons, cusecs, magnitude, nanometres or hertz.
- **Every 10 dB rise ≈ doubles the loudness** (NextGen); WHO: indoor sound **below 30 dB** is safe.
# Sound levels
| Sound / limit | Level |
|---|---|
| **Rustling leaves** | **~20 dB** (Rocket) |
| WHO safe indoors | < 30 dB |
| Normal conversation | ~60 dB |
| Tolerable limit for humans (exam answer) | **~80 dB**; 100–125 dB very uncomfortable |
| Prolonged exposure → hearing loss | **> 75 dB** (NextGen, Shankar); permanent damage commonly quoted **> 85 dB** |
| Heavy traffic | ~90 dB |
| **Rocket engine** | **~180 dB** (Rocket) |
- Among heavy traffic, election meetings, rock music and **jet flight**, the **maximum noise** comes from **jet flight**.
# Sources and effects
- Noise pollution is **completely man-made**: construction, vehicle horns, industries, defence equipment, space rockets, domestic gadgets, loudspeakers, music concerts, **fire-crackers**.
- Effects: annoyance, raised pulse and blood pressure, **constriction of blood vessels**, digestive spasms, **impaired night vision**, sleeplessness, loss of hearing; infrasonic/ultrasonic waves can damage buildings.
> Exam angle: unit of noise / hearing loss = decibel (×8), sone vs decibel, rustling leaves 20 dB, tolerable 80 dB, maximum noise = jet flight.

=== Noise Rules 2000, Fire-cracker Standards and Noise Control
# Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000
- Framed under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**; noise is also an "air pollutant" under the Air Act, 1981; SC links freedom from noise to **Art. 21**.
| Zone | Day dB(A) Leq | Night dB(A) Leq |
|---|---|---|
| Industrial | 75 | 70 |
| Commercial | 65 | 55 |
| **Residential** | **55** | **45** |
| Silence zone | 50 | 40 |
- **Day = 6 am–10 pm; night = 10 pm–6 am.** **Loudspeakers / public address systems are banned 10 pm–6 am** except in closed premises (auditoria, banquet halls) – a rule of the **Noise Rules**, not the IPC or RP Act.
- **Silence zone:** not less than **100 m** around hospitals, educational institutions, courts (and religious places so declared).
- **NANMN** (Real Time National Ambient Noise Monitoring Network, CPCB, launched 2011): **70 stations in 7 metros** – Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Lucknow.
# Fire-crackers
- Noise standard (EP (Second Amendment) Rules 1999, amended **2006**): manufacture, sale or use of crackers exceeding **125 dB(AI) or 145 dB(C)pk at 4 m** from the point of bursting is prohibited; for series crackers the limit falls by 5 log10(N).
- Supreme Court (2017–18): banned **heavy metals** (lead, mercury, arsenic, antimony, lithium) and **barium salts** in crackers; only **green crackers** (CSIR-NEERI) allowed; series crackers banned.
# Control
- At source (silencers, machine maintenance, loudspeaker limits), in the path (**barriers, green belts**, building design), at receiver (ear protection, job rotation).
- **Green muffler:** **4–5 rows of trees/shrubs along roads** and near noisy places that **absorb noise** (also some gases and dust) – a **noise-pollution** control, not air, water or soil.
- Trees also absorb noise, but "absorbing excess noise from small industries and irrigation" is **not** listed as a benefit of tree planting for poor communities (APPSC AEE 2023 key); shade, soil quality and income are.
> Exam angle: residential day 55 dB, 10 pm–6 am loudspeaker ban (Noise Rules), cracker 125 dB(AI)/145 dB(C)pk at 4 m, green muffler = noise (×4).

=== Radioactive Pollution – Sources, Effects and Units
# Sources
| Source | Examples |
|---|---|
| Natural | Cosmic rays; radionuclides in the crust – **radium-224, uranium-235/238, thorium-232, radon-222, potassium-40** |
| Building materials | **Thorium** (exam answer; granite, monazite, fly-ash bricks also carry uranium and K-40); its decay gas **radon** collects indoors |
| Medical / isotopes | X-rays, **carbon-14, iodine-125, phosphorus-32** |
| Man-made nuclear | Reactor waste, nuclear fuel, mining and refining of uranium/thorium/plutonium, **weapon tests (Sr-90, Cs-137, I-131)**; accidents – **Chernobyl 1986** (Ukraine), Fukushima 2011 |
- **Radon:** colourless, odourless radioactive **noble gas** from uranium/radium decay in rocks and soil; builds up in poorly ventilated houses; **major cause of lung cancer after smoking**; used in **cancer treatment**.
- **Chernobyl** = **radioactive** pollution (reactor accident), not oil spill, acid rain or CO2.
# Effects
- Depend on intensity, rate of diffusion, exposure time and **half-life**.
- Short range: burns, hair loss, impaired metabolism, changes in blood corpuscles, death of cells.
- Long range: **genetic mutations (hereditary changes)**, **cancers (carcinogenesis)**, tumours, embryo changes; **Sr-90 → bone cancer**; I-131 → blood, bone marrow, thyroid.
- Rocket: "Radioactive pollution → **hereditary changes + carcinogenesis**" (not soil-mineral imbalance or hindered blood circulation).
- **Ionising** (X-rays, gamma, cosmic) vs **non-ionising** (UV, cell towers).
# Units
| Quantity | Units |
|---|---|
| Activity | Becquerel, curie |
| Absorbed dose | Gray, rad |
| Biological (equivalent) dose | Sievert, rem |
> Exam angle: building material (thorium / radon), statement code on effects (UPPSC 2009), Chernobyl, units.

=== Radiological Emergencies – NDMA, MRDS, AERB and Tummalapalle
- **MRDS = Mobile Radiation Detection Systems**: NDMA plan to give States/UTs MRDS for metros, State capitals and big cities to detect **unclaimed (orphan) radioactive material** (APPSC 2021, asked twice).
- Precedent: **Mayapuri, Delhi (2010)** – scrap-market **cobalt-60** exposure.
- **NDMA guidelines** on nuclear and radiological emergencies: **2009**; **District Collector** = Incident Commander off-site; NDRF CBRN teams respond.
- **AERB** (1983, under the **Atomic Energy Act, 1962**): independent regulator; approves radioactive waste disposal. **DAE** = nodal technical agency with the **Crisis Management Group**; Environmental Survey Laboratories only monitor.
- **AP: Tummalapalle** uranium mine and mill (**UCIL**), **YSR Kadapa** district (not Kurnool). Villagers blamed seepage from the **tailings pond** for uranium in groundwater; **APPCB show-cause notice to UCIL (Aug 2019)**; Centre/UCIL (BARC isotope study) say the uranium comes from the **natural ore body**.
> Exam angle: MRDS full form (×2), regulators, AP uranium site.

=== Soil Pollution – Causes, Heavy Metals and Control
- **Definition:** build-up of toxic compounds, chemicals, salts, **radioactive materials** or pathogens in soil that harms plants, animals and humans.
- **Natural causes:** floods with contaminated water, **acid rain**, minerals from weathering of rocks. **Man-made:** industrial waste, agricultural runoff (**pesticides, fertilisers**), urban waste, landfill **leachate**, mining, e-waste, **coal ash**, oil spills.
| Source | Soil pollutants |
|---|---|
| Industrial waste | Lead, **mercury**, copper, zinc, **cadmium**, cyanides, **chromates**, acids, alkalis |
| Pesticides | Insecticides (DDT, aldrin), herbicides, fungicides, rodenticides |
| Fertilisers | NPK, urea – cadmium from phosphate fertilisers |
| Discarded materials | Concrete, plastics, glass, cans, carcasses |
| Radioactive wastes | Uranium, thorium, radium |
# Heavy metals
| Heavy metal | Main source | Disease |
|---|---|---|
| Mercury | Chlor-alkali effluent, fungicides | **Minamata** (biomagnifies as methyl mercury) |
| Cadmium | Phosphate fertilisers, batteries, mining | **Itai-itai** |
| Chromates | **Tanneries** | – |
| Copper, zinc, lead | Smelting, industrial waste | – |
| **Lead (Pb)** | Old petrol, paints, **printing ink on newspapers** | Brain and kidney damage |
- **DDT** is a POP under the **Stockholm Convention**; DDT and mercury **biomagnify**.
- **Fly ash** must be used in bricks, cement, roads within **300 km** of thermal plants.
- **Contaminated Sites Rules** (EPA 1986): test against **189 hazardous chemicals**; **Polluter Pays**; exempt radioactive waste, mining, marine oil spills, dumpsites. **EP Fund Rules:** Centre keeps **25%**.
# Control
- **Treat waste before dumping**, **minimise pesticides** and chemical fertilisers, **crop rotation**, biofertilisers, biopesticides, **IPM**, organic farming, the 4 Rs, afforestation, solid-waste treatment.
- **Setting up watersheds is NOT a soil-pollution control** (it is soil and water conservation) – APPSC key.
> Exam angle: NOT-a-control picker (watersheds), heavy-metal pairs, lead in newspapers.

=== Bioremediation and Remediation of Contaminated Soil
- **Bioremediation:** microorganisms (bacteria, fungi) degrade contaminants into less toxic forms; its **primary application is soil contamination** (oil, pesticides, solvents, heavy metals); microbes can be **genetically engineered**; **heavy metals (Cd, Pb) cannot be fully removed** by it (Rocket "heavy-metal limitation").
| Type | Agent / method |
|---|---|
| Bioaugmentation | **Adds** pollutant-degrading microbes |
| Biostimulation / bioventing | Adds **nutrients** (or air) to boost native microbes |
| Biosparging | Air injected below the water table |
| **Phytoremediation** | **Plants** take up heavy metals (hyperaccumulators; water hyacinth) |
| Mycoremediation | **Fungi** (oyster mushroom, Pleurotus) |
| Landfarming, bioreactors, composting | Ex-situ methods |
- **Oil Zapper (and Oilivorous-S):** **TERI** bacterial consortium for oil spills and oily sludge – not a chemical dispersant.
- **Heavy-metal removal from soil:** **immobilisation**, **soil washing**, **phytoremediation**; **keeping land fallow does NOT remove** heavy metals.
- Alternatives to conventional control: **green technology, bioremediation, carbon capture and storage**; **incineration** is a conventional method, not an alternative.
> Exam angle: primary application (soil), NOT-a-removal-method (fallow), types.
~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 13 p.2-4, p.6; NextGen Rocket Environment p.63, p.70; PMF Environment Part 4 p.58 (phytoremediation immobilises contaminants)

=== Marine Pollution – Microplastics, Oil Spills and GloLitter
- Oceans are the **ultimate sink**: rivers, sewage, garbage, oil, grease, detergents, **radioactive wastes**, offshore oil mining, **oil spills**, plastics. Marine pollution harms **humans too** (seafood toxins, tourism, fisheries) and **plastic is not its only form** – so "only marine life" and "only plastic" are both false.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Microplastics** | Plastic particles **smaller than 5 mm**; microbeads in cosmetics |
| Ghost nets | Abandoned, lost or discarded fishing gear |
| Dead zones | Hypoxic waters from nutrient runoff (Gulf of Mexico) |
| Blue Flag | Beach eco-label of the **Foundation for Environmental Education** (Denmark); AP: **Rushikonda**, Visakhapatnam (2020) |
# Conventions and programmes
| Instrument / body | Fact |
|---|---|
| **GloLitter Partnerships** | **IMO + FAO** (launched Dec 2019), funded by **Norway**; helps developing countries cut **sea-based** marine plastic litter from **shipping and fisheries**; India among 30 partner countries – **not UNEP**, not "all sources" |
| MARPOL 73/78 | IMO; 6 Annexes; bans plastic dumping at sea |
| Ballast Water Convention | IMO; invasive species; in force **2017** |
| SACEP | South Asia Co-operative Environment Programme, 1982; secretariat **Colombo**; 8 members |
- **Ennore oil spill (28 Jan 2017):** LPG tanker **BW Maple** and oil tanker **MT Dawn Kanchipuram** collided off Kamarajar (Ennore) port near Chennai.
- Response tools: booms, skimmers, dispersants, **Oil Zapper**; **Samudra Pratap** (Goa Shipyard) – India's first indigenous **Pollution Control Vessel**.
> Exam angle: microplastic size, GloLitter statements (APPSC G1 2023), Ennore ship names, statement-truth on marine pollution.

=== Thermal Pollution
- **Definition:** heat becoming a pollutant – rise (or fall) in temperature of a natural water body by human activity; when heat energy is the pollutant it is **thermal pollution**.
- **Main source:** **thermal and nuclear power plants** using water as **coolant** and returning hot water; so **harnessing nuclear energy often causes thermal pollution**. Also industries, deforestation of shorelines, release of cold reservoir water.
- **Effects:** **warm water holds less dissolved oxygen**; higher metabolic rate, fish kills, algal blooms, loss of biodiversity.
- **Control:** **cooling towers, cooling ponds**, cogeneration, retaining shoreline vegetation.
> Exam angle: definition (flashcard), nuclear power → thermal pollution.
~~ Sources: NextGen Environment APPSC 10 p.9, p.11; Shankar Environment Part 6 p.17, p.19-20

=== Light Pollution and Dark Sky Places
- **Light pollution:** excessive, misdirected artificial light that causes **skyglow**, hides stars and disturbs ecosystems and circadian rhythms.
- **Dark sky place / reserve:** public or private land with an exceptional starry night, protected by responsible lighting; certified by **DarkSky International** (formerly International Dark-Sky Association), a **US-based non-profit** (Tucson).
| FIRST | Place |
|---|---|
| World's first Dark Sky Reserve | **Mont-Mégantic, Quebec, Canada** (2007) – not Russia |
| India's first Dark Sky Reserve | **Hanle, Ladakh** (2022), in Changthang WLS – not Himachal Pradesh |
| India's first Dark Sky Park | **Pench (Maharashtra)**, 2024 |
- APPSC G1 2023: statements I and III are correct, II and IV wrong; as no option offers "I and III only", the key is **"None of the above"**.
> Exam angle: statement question on dark sky reserves.
