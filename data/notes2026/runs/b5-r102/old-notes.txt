## SUB 1 | Soil Pollution
[102:1:0] - Build-up of toxic chemicals or radioactive material in soil → toxic crops, fewer plants, soil fauna imbalance.
[102:1:1] - Natural causes: floods, acid rain, weathered rock. Human: pesticides (**DDT**, aldrin, malathion), NPK, heavy metals, landfill **leachate**, e-waste, coal ash, mining.
[102:1:2] TABLE head: Heavy metal | Source | Disease
  [102:1:2.0] Mercury | Chlor-alkali effluent, fungicides | Minamata
  [102:1:2.1] Cadmium | Phosphate fertilisers, batteries, mining effluent | Itai-itai
  [102:1:2.2] Copper, zinc, lead | Smelting, industrial waste | –
  [102:1:2.3] Chromates | Tanneries | –
[102:1:3] - **DDT**: persistent organic pollutant under the **Stockholm Convention**; DDT and mercury biomagnify in marine food chains (dissolved oxygen does not).
[102:1:4] - **Fly ash**: use in bricks, cement, roads within **300 km** of thermal plants (**50 km** = limit for using treated STP water).
[102:1:5] - **Contaminated Sites Rules** (EPA 1986): test against **189 hazardous chemicals**; Polluter Pays; exempt radioactive waste, mining, marine oil, dumpsites.
[102:1:6] - Environment (Protection) Fund Rules: Centre keeps **25%**.
[102:1:7] - Control: less chemicals, **biofertilisers, IPM, crop rotation**, organic farming, afforestation, biomining of legacy dumps.
[102:1:8] Watersheds are NOT a soil-pollution control (APPSC 2019).
## SUB 2 | Bioremediation and Green Technologies
[102:2:0] TABLE head: Type | Agent
  [102:2:0.0] Bioaugmentation | Adds pollutant-degrading microbes
  [102:2:0.1] Biostimulation | Adds nutrients to boost native microbes
  [102:2:0.2] Phytoremediation | Plants take up toxic substances such as heavy metals from soil and water; hyperaccumulator plants store very high levels
  [102:2:0.3] Mycoremediation | Fungi (e.g. oyster mushroom, Pleurotus) break down or absorb pollutants such as oil, DDT and heavy metals
[102:2:1] - **Oil Zapper**: bacterial consortium from **TERI** (not a chemical dispersant); eats oily sludge and crude from spills.
[102:2:2] - Alternatives to conventional control: **green technology, bioremediation, carbon capture and storage**.
[102:2:3] **Incineration** is conventional – not an alternative (APPSC G2 2025).
## SUB 3 | Noise Pollution
[102:3:0] - Unit: **decibel (dB)** (APPSC 2025), logarithmic; **dB(A)** weighted to human hearing; **+10 dB ≈ loudness doubles**. Hertz = frequency.
[102:3:1] TABLE head: Sound / limit | Level
  [102:3:1.0] Rustling leaves | ~20 dB
  [102:3:1.1] WHO safe indoors | < 30 dB
  [102:3:1.2] Conversation | ~60 dB
  [102:3:1.3] Permanent hearing loss | > 85 dB
  [102:3:1.4] Heavy traffic | ~90 dB
  [102:3:1.5] Rocket engine | ~180 dB
[102:3:2] - Maximum noise among options: **jet flight**. ~27% industrial workers suffer deafness.
[102:3:3] - Long exposure: narrowed blood vessels, digestive spasms, **impaired** night vision.
[102:3:4] # Noise Rules, 2000 (under EPA 1986)
[102:3:5] TABLE head: Zone | Day dB(A) | Night dB(A)
  [102:3:5.0] Industrial | 75 | 70
  [102:3:5.1] Residential | 55 | 45
  [102:3:5.2] Silence | 50 | 40
[102:3:6] - Day = **6 am–10 pm**. **Silence zone**: ≥ **100 m** around hospitals, educational institutions, courts.
[102:3:7] - CPCB bans crackers above **125 dB at 4 m**.
[102:3:8] - **NANMN**: CPCB, **70 stations in 7 metros** (Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Lucknow, Hyderabad); Delhi 65–85% sites breach limits.
[102:3:9] # Control
[102:3:10] - Silencers, **noise barriers**, green belts, zoning, loudspeaker limits, traffic management.
[102:3:11] - **Green mufflers**: 4–5 rows of trees/shrubs along roads absorb noise; also absorb gases and dust.
[102:3:12] - **NANMN** stands for CPCB's **Real Time National Ambient Noise Monitoring Network**.
## SUB 4 | Thermal Pollution
[102:4:0] - Change in water temperature from **hot coolant water** of thermal and **nuclear** plants and industries.
[102:4:1] - **Warmer water holds less dissolved oxygen**; sudden changes stress or kill aquatic life.
[102:4:2] - Control: **cooling towers, cooling ponds**, recirculation, regulated discharge temperature.
## SUB 5 | Marine Pollution
[102:5:0] - Oceans = final sink for radioactive waste, plastics, oil, detergents, sewage.
[102:5:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [102:5:1.0] Microplastics | < 5 mm; microbeads in cosmetics
  [102:5:1.1] Ghost nets | Abandoned, lost or discarded fishing gear
  [102:5:1.2] Dead zones | Hypoxic waters from nutrient runoff (Gulf of Mexico)
  [102:5:1.3] Blue Flag | Beach eco-label by Foundation for Environmental Education (Denmark)
  [102:5:1.4] Instrument / body | Fact
  [102:5:1.5] MARPOL 73/78 | IMO; 6 Annexes; bans plastic dumping at sea
  [102:5:1.6] Ballast Water Convention | IMO; invasive species; in force 2017
  [102:5:1.7] GloLitter Partnerships | IMO and FAO (2019); funded by Norway; India a partner; sea-based litter from shipping and fisheries
  [102:5:1.8] SACEP | 1982; secretariat Colombo; 8 members incl. India; South Asian Seas litter work
[102:5:2] - **Samudra Pratap**: India's first indigenous **Pollution Control Vessel**, **Goa Shipyard**. Other tools: booms, skimmers, dispersants, Oil Zapper.
[102:5:3] GloLitter is IMO-FAO, not UNEP.
[102:5:4] - **Blue Flag in AP:** **Rushikonda beach, Visakhapatnam** got the Blue Flag in **October 2020**, among India's first **eight** Blue Flag beaches; the flag was restored in **2025** after a brief withdrawal.
[102:5:5] - **SACEP:** South Asia Co-operative Environment Programme, set up in 1982; secretariat in Colombo.
## SUB 6 | Radioactive Pollution
[102:6:0] TABLE head: Source | Examples
  [102:6:0.0] Natural | Cosmic rays, radon-222, U-235, Th-232, potassium-40
  [102:6:0.1] Building materials | Thorium (APPSC 2019); radon from granite
  [102:6:0.2] Medical | X-rays, I-125, P-32, C-14
  [102:6:0.3] Man-made nuclear | Reactor waste, tests (Sr-90, Cs-137, I-131), uranium mining
[102:6:1] - X-ray machines = **ionising**; cell towers = **non-ionising**.
[102:6:2] - **Radon**: colourless, odourless noble gas from radium decay in soil; **most important indoor pollutant**; **leading cause of lung cancer after smoking**; used in **cancer treatment** (APPSC G1 2018).
[102:6:3] - Harm depends on intensity, exposure time, half-life; DNA damage, mutations.
[102:6:4] TABLE head: Quantity | Units
  [102:6:4.0] Activity | Becquerel, curie
  [102:6:4.1] Absorbed dose | Gray, rad
  [102:6:4.2] Biological dose | Sievert, rem
[102:6:5] Rad is NOT a unit of activity.
## SUB 7 | Radiological Emergency Management
[102:7:0] - **AERB** (1983, Atomic Energy Act 1962): independent regulator; approves radioactive waste disposal.
[102:7:1] - **DAE** = nodal technical agency, with the **Crisis Management Group**; CCS handles NBC emergencies.
[102:7:2] - **NDMA guidelines** on nuclear and radiological emergencies: **2009**.
[102:7:3] - **MRDS** (Mobile Radiation Detection Systems): NDMA plan for metros and State capitals to detect **orphan** radioactive material (APPSC 2021, twice).
[102:7:4] - Precedent: **Mayapuri, Delhi (2010)** – cobalt-60.
[102:7:5] - Environmental Survey Laboratory only monitors; NDRF CBRN battalions respond; **District Collector** = Incident Commander off-site.
[102:7:6] - AP: **Tummalapalle** uranium mine and mill (**UCIL**), **YSR Kadapa** (not Kurnool).
[102:7:7] - **Tummalapalle concerns:** villagers blame seepage from the unlined **tailings pond** for uranium in **groundwater**, crop loss and skin and reproductive problems; **APPCB** issued a **show-cause notice to UCIL (Aug 2019)**; its expert committee found seepage risk at the pond but could not fix the source; Centre and UCIL say high uranium comes from the **natural ore body** (BARC isotope study).
