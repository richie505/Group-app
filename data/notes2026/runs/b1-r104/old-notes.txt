=== NOTES 1:104 | Sources for Medieval India - Persian Chronicles, Inscriptions, Travellers' Accounts
## SUB 0 | Nature of Medieval Sources
[104:0:0] - Literary (Persian/Arabic chronicles, Sanskrit and regional texts), **foreign travellers**, **inscriptions and coins**, **architecture and paintings**.
[104:0:1] - Foreign accounts **supplement** Indian texts and must be **cross-verified** with inscriptions and archaeology.

## SUB 2 | Al-biruni
[104:2:0] - From **Khwarazm (Khiva)**, present **Uzbekistan**; came with **Mahmud of Ghazni**, **11th century** (c. 1030).
[104:2:1] - Mahmud also patronised **Firdausi** and **Utbi**.
[104:2:2] - Learned **Sanskrit**; wrote in **Arabic** on religion, philosophy, astronomy, **caste**.
[104:2:3] - Recorded **Diwali** on **Kartika Amavasya**.
[104:2:4] - Order of arrival: **Al-Biruni → Ibn Battuta → Tavernier → Manucci**.

## SUB 8 | Biases in the Chronicles
[104:8:0] - **Court bias**: histories glorify the patron; **Amir Khusrau** = court poet of **Alauddin Khalji**, went with **Malik Kafur's 1310** Warangal invasion.
[104:8:1] - **Barani**: orthodox **Sunni** viewpoint; theorised kingship.
[104:8:2] - Firishta's story of **Deva Raya kidnapping Nehal**: rejected by modern historians.

=== NOTES 1:105 | Slave / Mamluk Dynasty - Aibak, Iltutmish, Razia, Balban
## SUB 0 | Dynasty Names and Period
[105:0:0] - **Mamluk** = Arabic "**owned**": an owned **military slave** (not a domestic slave). Also called **Slave dynasty**; ruled **1206-1290**.
[105:0:1] - First Muslim dynasty to rule north India from **Delhi**.
[105:0:2] TABLE head: Line | Period | Founder
  [105:0:2.0] Qutbi | 1206-1211 | Qutbuddin Aibak
  [105:0:2.1] First Ilbari | 1211-1266 | Iltutmish
  [105:0:2.2] Second Ilbari | 1266-1290 | Balban
[105:0:3] # Order of Rulers
[105:0:4] - Aibak → Aram Shah → **Iltutmish** (1210-36) → Ruknuddin Firuz (1236) → **Razia** (1236-40) → Bahram Shah (1240-42) → Alauddin Masud (1242-46) → **Nasiruddin Mahmud** (1246-66) → **Balban** (1266-86) → Kaiqubad → Kaimurs.
[105:0:5] # Sultanate Dynasties
[105:0:6] TABLE head: Dynasty | Period
  [105:0:6.0] Mamluk | 1206-1290
  [105:0:6.1] Khalji | 1290-1320
  [105:0:6.2] Tughlaq | 1320-1414
  [105:0:6.3] Sayyid | 1414-1451
  [105:0:6.4] Lodi | 1451-1526
[105:0:7] Odd one out: Ruknuddin Firuz, Alauddin Masud, Nasiruddin Mahmud ARE Mamluks; Jalaluddin Khalji and the Tughlaqs are NOT.
## SUB 1 | Qutbuddin Aibak (1206-1210)
[105:1:0] - Born in **Turkestan**; sold first to the **Qazi of Nishapur**, then slave of **Muhammad Ghori** (APPSC G1 2004).
[105:1:1] - Rose to **Amir-i-Akhur** (Master of Stables), then general; after **Tarain II (1192)** Ghori's representative in India.
[105:1:2] - Title: **Lakh Bakhsh** ("giver of lakhs"). Capital: **Lahore**.
[105:1:3] - Monuments begun: **Qutb Minar**, **Quwwat-ul-Islam** mosque (first mosque in Delhi), **Adhai Din ka Jhonpra** (Ajmer).
[105:1:4] - Fought **Tajuddin Yildiz** for Ghurid lands after 1206.
[105:1:5] - Death: **1210** at Lahore, fell from his horse playing **chaugan (polo)**; buried near **Anarkali Bazaar**, Lahore. Polo was brought to India by the Turks.
## SUB 2 | Iltutmish (1210-1236)
[105:2:0] # Rivals, Capital, Legitimacy
[105:2:1] - Rivals: **Yildiz** (defeated at **Tarain, 1216**) and **Nasiruddin Qabacha** (fought for Lahore).
[105:2:2] - Moved capital from **Lahore to Delhi**.
[105:2:3] - **Investiture ("mansur")** from the **Abbasid Caliph** of Baghdad, **1229** - first Caliphal investiture.
[105:2:4] # Institutions and Coins
[105:2:5] TABLE head: Institution | Meaning
  [105:2:5.0] Turkan-i-Chahalgani | "The Forty" / Chalisa: Turkish military elite
  [105:2:5.1] Iqta | Revenue assignments to officers (muqtis) instead of cash salaries
  [105:2:5.2] Silver tanka, copper jital | Regular, purely Arabic coinage
[105:2:6] - First Sultan to bring in **regular coins** and make **Delhi** capital.
[105:2:7] The Forty were Turkish military nobles, not Persian revenue clerks.
[105:2:8] # Mongol Threat and Monuments
[105:2:9] - Refused shelter to **Jalaluddin Mangbarni** of Khwarazm → kept **Chengiz Khan** out of India.
[105:2:10] - Completed the **Qutb Minar** (dedicated to **Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki**).
[105:2:11] - **Sultan Ghari** tomb → Iltutmish called "**Father of tomb building**".
## SUB 3 | Razia Sultan (1236-1240)
[105:3:0] - **First and only woman ruler** of the Delhi Sultanate.
[105:3:1] - **Discarded purdah**, wore **male attire**, personally **led the army**.
[105:3:2] - Opposition: Turkish nobles resented her favour to **Yaqut**, an **Abyssinian**.
[105:3:3] - **1240**: **Altuniya**, governor of **Bhatinda (Sirhind)**, revolted → Yaqut killed, Razia imprisoned.
[105:3:4] - End: Razia **married Altuniya**, marched on Delhi, was defeated and killed (1240).
[105:3:5] - Razia made Balban **Amir-i-Shikar**.
## SUB 4 | Nasiruddin Mahmud and the Rise of Balban
[105:4:0] - Nasiruddin Mahmud (1246-66) **immediately preceded** Balban; **Tabaqat-i-Nasiri** named after him.
[105:4:1] - Balban was his **principal minister (naib)** and leader of the Forty; title **Ulugh Khan**.
[105:4:2] - Briefly displaced by **Imaduddin Raihan**, then returned.
[105:4:3] - Balban's early life: born c. **1205** as **Bahauddin**; captured by Mongols, bought by Iltutmish → **water-carrier** → member of the Forty → Amir-i-Shikar.
## SUB 5 | Balban (1266-1286)
[105:5:0] - Took the regnal title **Ghiyasuddin Balban** on accession (1266).
[105:5:1] # Theory of Kingship and Court
[105:5:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [105:5:2.0] Zil-i-Ilahi | Sultan = shadow of God
  [105:5:2.1] Sijda | Prostration (greeting lying on the ground)
  [105:5:2.2] Paibos | Kissing the feet
  [105:5:2.3] Nauroz | Persian festival brought in by Balban
[105:5:3] - Court copied **Iranian kings**; strict etiquette.
[105:5:4] # Blood and Iron, Army
[105:5:5] - **Blood and iron** policy; **broke the power of the Forty** by promoting **junior Turks**.
[105:5:6] - Created military department **Diwan-i-Arz**, separating it from **Diwan-i-Wizarat** (finance).
[105:5:7] - **Outposts and forts** against the Mongols.
[105:5:8] Iltutmish created the Forty; Balban broke its power.
## SUB 6 | End of the Dynasty and Rise of the Khaljis
[105:6:0] - Balban (d. 1286) → grandson **Kaiqubad** (murdered; body thrown into the **Yamuna**) → infant **Kaimurs**.
[105:6:1] - **Jalaluddin Firoz Khalji** crowned himself at **Kilokhari (1290)** → end of Mamluk rule, start of the **Khalji dynasty**.

[106:0:2] Alauddin's son Khizr Khan (set aside 1316) is NOT the Sayyid Sultan Khizr Khan (1414-1421).
## SUB 1 | Jalaluddin Khalji (1290-1296)
[106:1:0] - Khalji from **Turkestan**; had fought the Mongols. **Crowned himself at Kilokhari, 1290**, aged about **70**.
[106:1:1] - **First Sultan with a benevolent attitude to Hindus**; lenient to rebels such as **Malik Chhajju**.
[106:1:2] - Mongols: defeated a Mongol invasion; converted and settled Mongols = "**New Mussalmans**"; married his daughter to **Ulugh Khan**, a descendant of Chengiz Khan.
[106:1:3] - End: nephew and son-in-law **Alauddin** raided **Devagiri (1294-96)** = **first Turkish invasion of the south**; Alauddin **murdered Jalaluddin at Kara (1296)**.
## SUB 2 | Alauddin Khalji (1296-1316)
[106:2:0] # Name, Posts, Titles
[106:2:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [106:2:1.0] Ali Gurshasp | Earlier name
  [106:2:1.1] Amir-i-Tuzuk | Master of ceremonies (post under Jalaluddin)
  [106:2:1.2] Ariz-i-Mumalik | Army minister (post under Jalaluddin)
  [106:2:1.3] Sikandar-i-Sani | "Second Alexander", on his coins
[106:2:2] Ariz-i-Mumalik = army minister, not minister of law.
[106:2:3] # Religious Ambitions and the Shariat
[106:2:4] - Wished to be a **world conqueror** and to **found a new religion**; dropped both on the advice of the **Kotwal of Delhi Ala-ul-Mulk** (Barani's uncle); Ulemas opposed.
[106:2:5] - **Barani** records his talk with **Qazi Mughisuddin of Bayana**: told his acts violated the Shariat, Alauddin replied he acted for the **interest of the state**.
[106:2:6] - Barani separated **Shariat** from **Zawabit** (state laws made by the ruler).
[106:2:7] # Nobles
[106:2:8] - **Ulugh Khan, Nusrat Khan, Zafar Khan, Ain-ul-Mulk Multani, Malik Kafur, Ghazi Malik**. Court poet: **Amir Khusrau**.

[106:4:0] # Origin and Epithet
[106:4:1] - Taken by **Nusrat Khan** in the Gujarat campaign; called "**Hazar Dinari**" (bought for a thousand dinars).

=== NOTES 1:107 | Alauddin Khalji's Market Control, Revenue and Military Reforms
## SUB 0 | Why Market Control
[107:0:0] - 1. **Mongol invasions** →
[107:0:1] - 2. need for a **large standing army paid in cash** →
[107:0:2] - 3. **low, fixed salaries** →
[107:0:3] - 4. **fix prices** of daily goods →
[107:0:4] - 5. soldiers live comfortably on low pay (**APPSC G2 2017** key).
[107:0:5] - Military need directly shaped economic policy.
[107:0:6] - Aims: **fixed prices**, sure **supply**, **controlled distribution** (rationing), **end of monopolies**, enforcement by a separate administration.
[107:0:7] Price control was NOT meant to pay lower wages to palace artisans.
## SUB 1 | Markets and Officials
[107:1:0] # The Four Markets
[107:1:1] TABLE head: Market | Goods
  [107:1:1.0] Mandi | Food grains
  [107:1:1.1] Sarai Adl | Cloth, sugar, dry fruits, butter, oil
  [107:1:1.2] Third market | Horses, slaves, cattle
  [107:1:1.3] Fourth market | Miscellaneous goods
[107:1:2] Mandi = grain; Sarai Adl = cloth and other goods.
[107:1:3] # Officials and Departments
[107:1:4] TABLE head: Official / Department | Role
  [107:1:4.0] Shahna-i-Mandi | Superintendent of each market (e.g. Malik Kabool)
  [107:1:4.1] Diwan-i-Riyasat | Ran the whole market system, under the Naib-i-Riyasat
  [107:1:4.2] Barids | Intelligence officers; daily reports
  [107:1:4.3] Munhiyan | Secret spies; daily reports
  [107:1:4.4] Diwan-i-Mustakhraj | Recovered revenue arrears from officials
  [107:1:4.5] Ariz-i-Mumalik | Army minister
  [107:1:4.6] Malik Naib | Deputy; rank Malik Kafur rose to
[107:1:5] - **Slave boys** made test purchases; cheating merchants punished.
[107:1:6] Diwan-i-Riyasat = markets; Diwan-i-Mustakhraj = revenue arrears.
## SUB 2 | Revenue Reforms
[107:2:0] - **First Sultan to measure land (masahat)** for revenue; standard unit **biswa**; assessed on measured area and yield.
[107:2:1] - Measurement limited to **Delhi and nearby areas**, not the whole empire.
[107:2:2] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [107:2:2.0] Kharaj | Land tax = 50% (half) of yield (BPSC 2018)
  [107:2:2.1] Ghari | House tax (new)
  [107:2:2.2] Charai | Grazing tax on milch animals (new)
  [107:2:2.3] Khuts, muqaddams, chaudharis | Intermediaries; privileges abolished, taxed like others
  [107:2:2.4] Kismat-i-khuti | Headman's cess; abolished
  [107:2:2.5] Milk, waqf, inam | Reclaimed → khalisa expanded
[107:2:3] - State collected revenue **more directly**; also curbed the powers of provincial governors.
[107:2:4] Kharaj = one-half, not one-fifth. Middlemen's privileges were abolished, not expanded. Ghari = house; Charai = grazing.
## SUB 3 | Military Reforms
[107:3:0] - **First Sultan with a large permanent standing army**, **paid in cash**.
[107:3:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [107:3:1.0] Dagh | Branding of horses
  [107:3:1.1] Huliya / Chehra | Descriptive roll of each soldier
[107:3:2] - Purpose: stop fraud and false musters.
[107:3:3] - Mongols beaten: **Jalandhar (1298)**, **Kili (1299)**, **Amroha (1305)**, **Ravi (1306)**; Ravi became the boundary river; frontier forts repaired.
[107:3:4] - Later copy: **Mahmud Gawan** (Bahmani) brought in dagh, chehra, land measurement and a larger khalisa.
[107:3:5] Dagh is NOT a land-revenue measure; huliya is NOT an assessment of nobles' wealth.
## SUB 4 | Ordinances and Social Measures
[107:4:0] TABLE head: Cause of Revolt (Barani) | Ordinance
  [107:4:0.0] Nobles' wealth | Confiscated religious grants and free lands (inam, waqf)
  [107:4:0.1] Weak spy system | Reorganised spies (barids, munhiyan)
  [107:4:0.2] Drinking | Prohibited liquor, intoxicants and gambling
  [107:4:0.3] Nobles' intermarriage and gatherings | Feasts and marriage alliances needed royal permission
[107:4:1] - **Severe punishments**; rape punished severely.
[107:4:2] - **APPSC G1 2018**: prohibition + market control + severe punishment = Alauddin.
## SUB 5 | Kingship, Religion and the Ulema
[107:5:0] - **Rejected the ulema's advice**; acted in the **interest of the state** (told Qazi Mughisuddin of Bayana so).
[107:5:1] - Revived **Balban's** harsh, centralised rule, but **without** depending on religious sanction.
[107:5:2] - Title **Sikandar-i-Sani**; plans of **world conquest** and a **new religion** dropped on advice of **Kotwal Ala-ul-Mulk**.
[107:5:3] - Earlier posts under Jalaluddin: **Amir-i-Tuzuk** and **Ariz-i-Mamalik** (army, not law).
