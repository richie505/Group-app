## SUB 0 | Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764) – the Most Decisive Battle
[0:0] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [0:0.0] Date | 22 October 1764 (some books 22-23 Oct)
  [0:0.1] Place | Buxar, Bihar
  [0:0.2] British commander | Major Hector Munro (not Clive)
  [0:0.3] Allies defeated | Mir Qasim (deposed Nawab of Bengal), Shuja-ud-daula (Nawab of Awadh), Shah Alam II (Mughal emperor = ruler of Delhi)
  [0:0.4] Nawab of Bengal at the time | Mir Jafar (restored 1763)
[0:1] - Shuja fled, sought help from the **Rohillas and Marathas**, then surrendered; Shah Alam had already joined the English.
[0:2] - **Buxar = the most decisive battle** for British supremacy: an **open military victory** over the Nawab of Bengal, the Nawab of Awadh and the Mughal emperor together; it **confirmed Plassey** (Malleson: "amongst the most decisive battles ever fought").
[0:3] - Consequence specific to Buxar: **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** and Awadh made a dependent buffer.
[0:4] TABLE head: Point | Plassey (1757) | Buxar (1764)
  [0:4.0] Nature | Treachery | Open fighting
  [0:4.1] Enemy | One Nawab | Bengal + Awadh + Mughal emperor
  [0:4.2] Called | Foundation of British rule | Most decisive battle
[0:5] - **Pairs:** Buxar – Mir Qasim and allies vs Munro (not Mir Jafar vs Clive); Wandiwash – French vs Company; Chilianwala (1849) – Sikhs; Kharda (1795) – Marathas vs Nizam.
[0:6] - **Order:** Ambur (1749) → Plassey (1757) → Wandiwash (1760) → Buxar (1764).
[0:7] > Buxar: 1764, Munro, three allies, Mir Jafar was Nawab.

## SUB 1 | Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765) and the Diwani
[1:0] - **Clive** returned to Calcutta in **May 1765** as Governor of Bengal for the **second** time and made two treaties at Allahabad in **August 1765**.
[1:1] TABLE head: Party | Terms
  [1:1.0] Shah Alam II | Farman granting the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa (dated 12 August 1765) for Rs 26 lakh a year; got Kora and Allahabad; lived at Allahabad under Company protection → first Mughal pensioner of the Company
  [1:1.1] Shuja-ud-daula (Awadh) | Dominions restored except Kora and Allahabad; war indemnity Rs 50 lakh; duty-free trade for the Company in Awadh; Banaras left to Balwant Singh; Awadh = buffer state
  [1:1.2] Nawab of Bengal | Company paid Rs 53 lakh for nizamat; real power with the Company
[1:2] - **Grantor of the Diwani:** the **Mughal emperor Shah Alam II** – not the Nawab, not Farrukhsiyar or Shuja; year **1765** (not 1757/1760/1763).
[1:3] - Diwani = the Company's **legal foothold**: right to collect revenue and administer **civil justice**.
[1:4] - **After Bengal**, duty-free trade was next secured in **Awadh** (Nawab of Awadh).
[1:5] - **Varanasi was not given to Shah Alam** – he got only Kora and Allahabad.
[1:6] - **Deputy Diwan of Murshidabad:** **Mohammad Reza Khan**, appointed by Clive (also Naib Subahdar under Najm-ud-daula).
[1:7] - **Khasis** – the first hill tribe the British came into contact with after the 1765 Diwani (UPSC 2002).
[1:8] - **Chronology:** Mir Qasim made Nawab (1760) → Buxar (Oct 1764) → **Clive returns (May 1765)** → **Allahabad (Aug 1765)** → **Warren Hastings Governor (1772)**.
[1:9] - **1772:** Hastings **stopped the Rs 26 lakh** to Shah Alam, took back Kora and Allahabad and sold them to Awadh; ruled "by right of conquest".
[1:10] TABLE head: Treaty | Year | Context
  [1:10.0] Allahabad | 1765 | After Buxar
  [1:10.1] Madras | 1769 | With Haidar Ali
  [1:10.2] Salbai | 1782 | First Anglo-Maratha War
[1:11] > Diwani 1765: Shah Alam II → Company (Clive), Rs 26 lakh; Awadh Rs 50 lakh.
[1:12] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.124-125; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.16, 18; old app notes row 164
## SUB 2 | Mir Qasim (1760-63)
[2:0] - **Son-in-law of Mir Jafar**; placed on the throne by Governor **Vansittart (1760)**; earlier **Faujdar of Purnia and Rangpur**; the ablest successor of Alivardi.
[2:1] - **Treaty of 1760:** ceded **Burdwan, Midnapur and Chittagong** to the Company.
[2:2] - **Capital moved Murshidabad → Munger (Monghyr), Bihar** – to keep away from Calcutta.
[2:3] - **Army on European lines** – the Armenian **Gurgin Khan**; **gun factory at Monghyr**.
[2:4] - **Abolished all duties on internal trade** (1763) → Indian merchants competed on equal terms; the Company's dastak advantage vanished → war of 1763.
[2:5] - Defeated at Katwah, Giria, Munger (1763) → **fled to Awadh** → alliance with Shuja and Shah Alam → Buxar.
[2:6] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.121-123; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.15; old app notes row 164
## SUB 3 | Mir Jafar, Najm-ud-daula and the Plassey Settlement
[3:0] - **Mir Jafar – two tenures: 1757-1760 and 1763-1765**.
[3:1] - After Plassey he paid about **Rs 1.77 crore** and gave the **zamindari of the 24 Parganas**; the Company got **duty-free trade in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** and resources to fight the French.
[3:2] - Intrigued with the **Dutch** → Company victory at **Chinsura/Bidara (1759)**.
[3:3] - **Removed 1760** – he could not meet the Company's demands.
[3:4] - His son **Najm-ud-daula** became Nawab (1765); defence and administration went to Naib Subahdar **Muhammad Reza Khan**.
[3:5] - **Order of Nawabs:** Alivardi Khan → Siraj-ud-daula (last independent Nawab) → Mir Jafar → Mir Qasim.
[3:6] - **Robert Clive:** Governor of Bengal **1757/58-60 and 1765-67**; banned **double batta** (1766); called "heaven-born general" by **William Pitt**.
[3:7] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.121-124; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.15, 17; old app notes row 164
## SUB 4 | Key Terms – Farman, Dastak, Diwani, Nizamat
[4:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [4:0.0] Farman of 1717 | Farrukhsiyar let the Company trade in Bengal duty-free for Rs 3,000 a year – the "Magna Carta" of the Company
  [4:0.1] Dastak | Free pass for duty-free trade, misused for private trade
  [4:0.2] Diwani | Revenue collection + civil justice
  [4:0.3] Nizamat | Police, criminal justice, law and order – formally kept by the Nawab under Dual Government
[4:1] - **Dual Government (1765-72):** Company held the **Diwani** (through naib diwans **Reza Khan** for Bengal, **Raja Shitab Rai** for Bihar) – "power without responsibility"; it led to the **Bengal famine of 1770** (Governor **John Cartier**).
[4:2] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.12, 16-17; Spectrum Modern India p.89, 125; old app notes row 164
