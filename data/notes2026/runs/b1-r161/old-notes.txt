## SUB 3 | Lally's Campaign and Battle of Wandiwash
[161:3:0] - **1758**: Lally took **Fort St. David** (Cuddalore); failed at **Madras** (no money or naval support) and **Thanjavur** (raid for funds).
[161:3:1] - **Wandiwash (Vandavasi), 22 January 1760**: **Eyre Coote** (English) routed **Count de Lally** (French); **Bussy taken prisoner**.

=== NOTES 1:162 | Anglo-french Rivalry – the Three Carnatic Wars and the English Ascendancy
## SUB 0 | The Three Carnatic Wars – Overview
[162:0:0] - The **Carnatic Wars** were fought between the **English and the French** companies in South India.
[162:0:1] - Disciplined European infantry beat much larger Indian armies; the wars made the **English the kingmakers of South India**.
[162:0:2] TABLE head: War | Years | European context | Concluding treaty | Result
  [162:0:2.0] First | 1746–48 | Extension of the War of Austrian Succession (Europe, 1740) | Aix-la-Chapelle (18 Oct 1748) | Madras returned to the English in exchange for Louisburg (North America); politically inconclusive
  [162:0:2.1] Second | 1749–54 | No European war; Hyderabad and Carnatic successions | Pondicherry (1754) | Inconclusive; Dupleix recalled
  [162:0:2.2] Third | 1756–63 (UPSC: 1758–63) | Extension of the Seven Years' War (1756–63) | Paris (1763) | Decisive English victory; French left with trading enclaves only
[162:0:3] - **Chronology:** capture of Madras (1746) → Aix-la-Chapelle (1748) → Pondicherry (1754) → Paris (1763).
[162:0:4] The Second war was NOT ended by the Treaty of **Yandabo** – that closed the First Anglo-Burmese War (1826).
## SUB 1 | Causes and Participants
[162:1:0] # First War (1746–48)
[162:1:1] - Offshoot of Anglo-French rivalry in the Austrian War of Succession.
[162:1:2] - **Immediate cause:** British **Commodore Barnett** captured some French ships.
[162:1:3] - Fought between the **British and the French**; Nawab **Anwaruddin** of the Carnatic sided with the English.
[162:1:4] - The First war did **not** bring lasting British supremacy.
[162:1:5] # Second War (1749–54) – two succession disputes
[162:1:6] TABLE head: Dispute | English candidate | French candidate
  [162:1:6.0] Hyderabad (after Nizam-ul-Mulk died, 1748) | Nasir Jang (son) | Muzaffar Jang (grandson)
  [162:1:6.1] Carnatic | Anwaruddin; after 1749 his son Muhammad Ali | Chanda Sahib
[162:1:7] # Third War (1756–63)
[162:1:8] - Part of the Seven Years' War; it began before the Treaty of Pondicherry could be ratified.
## SUB 2 | Dupleix and the Policy of Interference
[162:2:0] - **Dupleix** – Governor of **Pondicherry from 1742**; architect of **interference in Indian politics** through succession disputes.
[162:2:1] - Backed Muzaffar Jang and Chanda Sahib; after **Ambur** Muzaffar Jang became **Subedar of the Deccan** and rewarded the French with grants.
[162:2:2] - **Capture of Madras (Fort St. George), 1746:** taken by **La Bourdonnais** (Governor of **Mauritius**) with his fleet; he agreed to ransom Madras back to the English, but **Dupleix overruled him** and kept it.
[162:2:3] - **Godeheu** replaced Dupleix and made peace.
## SUB 3 | Treaty of Pondicherry (1754)
[162:3:0] - Both companies **not to interfere** in the quarrels of Indian princes.
[162:3:1] - Each kept its **existing possessions**; Dupleix recalled.
[162:3:2] - Failed: the **Seven Years' War** broke out before England and France ratified it → Third war.

## SUB 6 | Reasons for English Success

  [162:6:0.0] Naval superiority | Beat d'Aché's fleet off Pondicherry (1759); he withdrew to Mauritius; French naval strength badly reduced (Voltaire: hardly a battleship left)

## SUB 5 | Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757)
[163:5:0] # Facts
[163:5:1] - Fought at **Palashi** (West Bengal) on the **Bhagirathi**, about 150 km north of Calcutta, near Murshidabad.
[163:5:2] - **Robert Clive** (East India Company) vs **Siraj-ud-Daula**.
[163:5:3] - **Mir Madan** fought for Siraj; **Mir Jafar** (commander-in-chief) and **Rai Durlabh** (Diwan) led divisions but did not join the fight.
[163:5:4] # Conspirators
[163:5:5] TABLE head: Conspirator | Who
  [163:5:5.0] Mir Jafar | Commander of the Nawab's army; promised the throne
  [163:5:5.1] Jagat Seth | "World banker" – Bengal's great banking house
  [163:5:5.2] Omichund | Merchant; deceived with a decoy treaty
  [163:5:5.3] Rai Durlabh | Nawab's Diwan; secret deal with the English
  [163:5:5.4] Yar Lutuf Khan | Officer in the plot
[163:5:6] Plassey = a **victory of treachery**; a political-financial deal, not a contest of equal armies.
[163:5:7] # Rewards and gains
[163:5:8] - Mir Jafar made Nawab; Company got the **zamindari of the 24 Parganas**, **duty-free trade in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** and a large payment.
[163:5:9] - Company turned from traders to a political power; Bengal money paid for the defeat of the French.

[164:2:0] - **23 June 1757**, **Palashi**, on the **Bhagirathi**, **Nadia district**, West Bengal.
[164:2:1] - **Clive** (about 3,000) defeated **Siraj-ud-Daulah** (about 50,000), who had **French support**.
[164:2:2] - **Causes:** misuse of dastaks; English **fortified Calcutta** without consent (the **French obeyed** his order to stop; the English did not); Company sheltered his enemies.
[164:2:3] - **Black Hole (June 1756):** Siraj took Qasim Bazar → Calcutta (Fort William); Governor **Drake** (British side, with **Holwell**) **fled to Fulta**; British used it as a pretext for war.
[164:2:4] - **Treaty of Alinagar (Feb 1757):** restored the 1717 farman privileges; the Company still plotted to remove Siraj.

[164:4:0] - **Son-in-law of Mir Jafar**; placed on the throne by Governor **Vansittart (1760)**; earlier **Fauzdar of Purnia and Rangpur**; most efficient successor of Alivardi.

[164:4:4] - **Army modernisation:** Mir Qasim built a new army trained on **European lines**; the Armenian **Gurgin Khan** organised its artillery and musketeers, and a **gun factory (arsenal)** was set up at **Monghyr**.

## SUB 8 | Robert Clive and Dual Government (1765–72)
[164:8:0] # Clive
[164:8:1] - **"Founder of the British Empire in India"** (APPSC 2019); **"heaven-born general"** (William Pitt).
[164:8:2] - **Governor of Bengal twice: 1758–60 and 1765–67**.
[164:8:3] - **Reforms:** civil – banned gifts and private business, made internal tax compulsory; military – banned **double batta** (from 1766).
[164:8:4] # Dual Government
[164:8:5] - **Company = Diwani**; **Nawab = Nizamat**; Mughal emperor = nominal sovereign.
[164:8:6] - **Naib diwans:** **Muhammad Reza Khan (Bengal)**, **Raja Shitab Rai (Bihar)** – collected revenue for the Company.
[164:8:7] - **Criticism:** **"power without responsibility"**.
[164:8:8] - **End: Warren Hastings (1772)** ended the Mughal pretence, ruled by right of conquest, stopped the tribute to Shah Alam.
## SUB 9 | Bengal Famine of 1770
[164:9:0] - Dual Government separated power from responsibility; revenue was squeezed as famine spread → **1770 Bengal famine** (Governor **John Cartier**'s time).

=== NOTES 1:165 | Dual Government in Bengal and Its Consequences; the Bengal Famine of 1770
## SUB 0 | Dual Government (1765–1772)
[165:0:0] - **Basis:** **First Treaty of Allahabad (12 August 1765)** – **Shah Alam II** gave the **Diwani** of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa; tribute **₹26 lakh**.
[165:0:1] - **Architect:** **Robert Clive**, in his second term (**1765–67**).
[165:0:2] TABLE head: Diwani (with EIC) | Nizamat (with Nawab)
  [165:0:2.0] Revenue collection and finance (plus civil justice) | Police, criminal justice, general administration
  [165:0:2.1] Real power without responsibility | Responsibility without power or funds
[165:0:3] - Conflict between the two: local nobility sidelined; growing resentment.
[165:0:4] - **Why "dual":** hide imperial ambitions; avoid **British Parliament's scrutiny**; keep other European companies paying duties to the "Nawab"; lack of British manpower. Verdict: **"a sham"**.
## SUB 1 | Naib Diwans
[165:1:0] - The Company did not collect revenue itself at first; it worked through **Indian deputies**.
[165:1:1] TABLE head: Deputy | Post | Region
  [165:1:1.0] Muhammad Reza Khan | Deputy (Naib) Diwan of Murshidabad, appointed by Clive after Allahabad; also Naib Nazim | Bengal
  [165:1:1.1] Raja Shitab Rai | Naib Diwan | Bihar (Patna)
[165:1:2] - Both were removed and tried when the system ended (1772).
## SUB 2 | Consequences
[165:2:0] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
