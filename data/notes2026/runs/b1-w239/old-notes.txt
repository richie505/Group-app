## SUB 0 | Gandhi-Irwin Pact (Delhi Pact), 1931
[239:0:0] - **Signed 5 March 1931** at Delhi (talks began 14 February 1931); the CWC, released unconditionally on 25 January 1931, authorised Gandhi to talk to Viceroy **Lord Irwin**.
[239:0:1] - **Mediators: Tej Bahadur Sapru and M.R. Jayakar** (Liberals; with V.S. Srinivasa Sastri) – not Motilal Nehru, Annie Besant or Chintamani.
[239:0:2] - **Main purpose:** to bring the **Congress into the Round Table Conference** – the Congress **suspended the CDM** and agreed to attend the **Second RTC**.
[239:0:3] TABLE head: Irwin agreed | Irwin refused | Congress agreed
  [239:0:3.0] Release of political prisoners not convicted of violence | Public inquiry into police excesses | Suspend the CDM
  [239:0:3.1] Remission of fines not yet collected | Commutation of the death sentences of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru | Attend the Second RTC
  [239:0:3.2] Return of confiscated lands not yet sold to third parties (lands already sold were NOT returned) | – | Discuss federation, Indian responsibility and reservations and safeguards (defence, external affairs, minorities, financial credit of India) – Clause 2
  [239:0:3.3] Salt-making by coastal villagers for own use (not sale); peaceful picketing; withdrawal of ordinances | – | –
[239:0:4] - **Not in the Pact:** the **18% reservation for depressed classes** (that is the **Poona Pact**, 1932, in the Central Legislature).
[239:0:5] - Irwin's biographer **Alan Campbell Johnson** called Gandhi's gains **"Consolation Prizes"** (UPPSC revised key; first key had said S.C. Bose).
[239:0:6] - **Sarojini Naidu** called Gandhi and Irwin the **"Two Mahatmas"**.
[239:0:7] - The Pact placed the Congress **on an equal footing** with the government; youth and the left opposed it because Bhagat Singh was not saved.
[239:0:8] > Delhi Pact = 5 March 1931; Sapru–Jayakar mediated; CDM suspended → Congress to RTC-2; police inquiry and commutation refused.

## SUB 2 | Karachi Session, March 1931 – Fundamental Rights Resolution
[239:2:0] - **29-31 March 1931**, special session to **endorse the Gandhi-Irwin Pact**; **President: Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**.
[239:2:1] - **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were executed on 23 March 1931** (Lahore Conspiracy Case), six days before the session; Gandhi met **black-flag demonstrations**.
[239:2:2] - Congress **admired their bravery** but **disapproved of political violence**; **reiterated Purna Swaraj** (= **Complete Independence**); chose Gandhi for RTC-2.
[239:2:3] - **Resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme drafted by Jawaharlal Nehru** (with **M.N. Roy**'s help; revised by Gandhi) – not Ambedkar, Rajendra Prasad or Patel.
[239:2:4] TABLE head: Fundamental Rights | Economic Programme
  [239:2:4.0] Free speech, press, assembly, association | Big cuts in rent and revenue; no rent on uneconomic holdings
  [239:2:4.1] Equality before law irrespective of caste, creed, sex | Relief from agricultural debt; control of usury
  [239:2:4.2] Universal adult franchise | Living wage, limited hours, protection of women workers; unions
  [239:2:4.3] Religious neutrality of the State; free primary education | State ownership of key industries, mines, transport
[239:2:5] - First time the Congress spelt out what **swaraj meant for the masses**; it **foreshadowed Parts III and IV** of the Constitution.
[239:2:6] > Karachi 1931: Patel presided, Nehru drafted.
[239:2:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 4 p.164, p.199-200; Spectrum Modern India p.413-414 (book p.381-382); old app notes row 239
[239:2:8] - **Subhas Chandra Bose** regarded the **Karachi session (1931)** as the **pinnacle of Gandhi's popularity and prestige**.
## SUB 3 | Resumption and Withdrawal of the CDM, 1932-34
[239:3:0] TABLE head: Date | Event
  [239:3:0.0] 29 Dec 1931 | CWC decides to resume CDM after RTC-2 failure
  [239:3:0.1] 4 Jan 1932 | Gandhi arrested; Congress banned; "civil martial law" under Willingdon
  [239:3:0.2] May 1933 | Mass movement suspended (individual satyagraha from 14 July 1933)
  [239:3:0.3] April 1934 | CDM formally withdrawn by Gandhi
  [239:3:0.4] 1934 | Congress returns to council entry, contests Central Assembly elections
[239:3:1] - After the CDM Gandhi turned to **constructive programmes**, above all **service to Harijans** – not violence or mere negotiation.
[239:3:2] > Withdrawn April 1934 (not 1932/1933/1935).
[239:3:3] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.420-421 (book p.388-389); PMF Modern History Part 4 p.197-198; old app notes row 239
[239:3:4] - **Gandhi resigned from the Congress (October 1934)** to devote himself to constructive work – **eradication of untouchability**, village industries and **preaching ahimsa**; he never rejoined as a member, so at **Independence he was not a member of the Congress**.
## SUB 4 | Round Table Conferences, 1930-32
[239:4:0] TABLE head: RTC | Dates | Congress | Key facts
  [239:4:0.0] First | 12 Nov 1930 – 19 Jan 1931, London | Boycotted (CDM on; Lahore 1929 saw no benefit) | Opened by King George V, chaired by Ramsay MacDonald; Ambedkar, Sapru, Jinnah attended; federation agreed in principle
  [239:4:0.1] Second | 7 Sept – 1 Dec 1931 | Gandhi = sole representative (only RTC he attended) | Viceroy now Willingdon; deadlock on separate electorates (Minorities' Pact); MacDonald threatened a unilateral Communal Award
  [239:4:0.2] Third | 17 Nov – 24 Dec 1932 | Absent (also the Labour Party) | Only 46 delegates → White Paper (March 1933) → Joint Select Committee → Government of India Act 1935
[239:4:1] - **Second RTC attendees:** Gandhi, **Sarojini Naidu** (Indian women), **Madan Mohan Malaviya**, Iqbal, Ambedkar, Jinnah, G.D. Birla; **Dr Rajendra Prasad did NOT attend** (nor Nehru).
[239:4:2] - **V.V. Jogayya Pantulu** went to London to assist Gandhi and handed him the **separate-Andhra resolution** (not Konda Venkatappayya).
[239:4:3] - Ambedkar, Jayakar, Aga Khan, Sapru, N.M. Joshi attended **all three**.
[239:4:4] - **Lord Willingdon** (Viceroy 1931-36): "It's a **beautiful world if it wasn't for Gandhi**" (private letter to his sister).

## SUB 5 | Communal Award and Poona Pact, 1932
[239:5:0] - **Communal Award: announced 16 August 1932 by British PM Ramsay MacDonald** (not Baldwin, Chamberlain or Churchill), after RTC-2 deadlock; based on the Lothian (Franchise) Committee.
[239:5:1]   - Separate electorates for Muslims, Sikhs, Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans, **depressed classes** (declared a minority, **71 seats**, **double vote** for 20 years), even Marathas (7 seats, Bombay).
[239:5:2]   - Congress **neither accepted nor rejected** it.
[239:5:3] - **Gandhi's fast unto death** in **Yerwada Jail, Poona**, from **20 September 1932**.
[239:5:4] - **Poona Pact: 24 September 1932** – signed by **Ambedkar** (depressed classes) and **Madan Mohan Malaviya** for caste Hindus; Gandhi broke fast on 26 September.
[239:5:5]   - **Joint electorate** with reserved seats: **148** in provincial legislatures (vs 71), **18%** of the Central Legislature seats.
[239:5:6]   - Root of reserved seats – **Articles 330 and 332**.
[239:5:7] - **Harijan Sevak Sangh** (first the Anti-Untouchability League), 30 September 1932.
[239:5:8] TABLE head: Person | Event
  [239:5:8.0] MacDonald | Communal Award
  [239:5:8.1] Linlithgow | August Offer
  [239:5:8.2] Dalhousie | Doctrine of Lapse
  [239:5:8.3] Chelmsford | Dyarchy (1919 Act)
[239:5:9] > Award (16 Aug) → Poona Pact (24 Sept) → RTC-3 (17 Nov 1932).
[239:5:10] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India p.421-424 (book p.389-392); PMF Modern History Part 4 p.205-207; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 6 p.59 (gives 25 Sept; Spectrum and PMF: 24 Sept); old app notes row 239
[239:5:11] - **Harijan Sevak Sangh:** first president **G.D. Birla**, first secretary **Amritlal V. Thakkar (Thakkar Bapa)**.

## SUB 0 | Cripps Mission, 1942 – Context and Proposals
[240:0:0] - **Arrived March 1942** (Delhi, 22-23 March); Cripps returned **mid-April 1942** after talks broke down.
[240:0:1] - Sent by British PM **Winston Churchill** (Conservative; PM 1940-45) – not MacDonald, Baldwin or Chamberlain.
[240:0:2] - **Sir Stafford Cripps** = left-wing **Labour** leader, Leader of the House of Commons, member of Churchill's **War Cabinet**; later also in the **Cabinet Mission (1946)**.
[240:0:3] - **Why sent:** Japanese advance (Pearl Harbor Dec 1941; **Rangoon fell March 1942**); pressure from the Allies (**Roosevelt, Chiang Kai-shek**); Congress offer to help if power was transferred.
[240:0:4] TABLE head: Proposal (Draft Declaration) | Detail
  [240:0:4.0] Status | Indian Union with Dominion status soon after the war, free to leave the Commonwealth – not complete independence, not a republic, not immediate partition
  [240:0:4.1] Constitution | Constitution-making body after the war, elected by provincial lower houses (proportional representation, electoral college 1/10th of their size) + nominees of the princes – the first concrete plan for an elected constituent assembly
  [240:0:4.2] Provincial option | Any province (or state) unwilling could stay outside and form a separate Union – "fatal flaw", Pakistan conceded in principle
  [240:0:4.3] Minorities | Treaty between the constitution-making body and Britain to protect minorities
  [240:0:4.4] Interim | Defence stays with Britain; Governor-General's powers intact; no equal-representation executive council (that was the Wavell Plan)
[240:0:5] - **Constitution-making body: yes; full independence: no** (UPSC 2009). All provinces and states were NOT bound to join.
[240:0:6] - Cripps on the urgency: **"Here is a revolution taking place and we should move quickly."**
[240:0:7] > Cripps = March 1942, Churchill, Dominion status after the war, elected constituent body, provincial opt-out, defence with Britain.

## SUB 2 | Rejection of Cripps – Negotiators, Remarks and the Road to Quit India
[240:2:0] - **Official Congress negotiators: Jawaharlal Nehru and Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**; Azad = **Congress President 1940-46**, negotiated with both **Cripps** and **Wavell**.
[240:2:1] - **Congress objected to:** Dominion status instead of independence; princely nominees; **provincial secession** (Balkanisation); **no immediate transfer of effective power**, no real share in defence – the main reason.
[240:2:2] - **Muslim League** rejected it: opposed a **single Indian Union**; Pakistan not explicitly conceded.
[240:2:3] - Cripps' **"take it or leave it"** attitude and Churchill/Linlithgow's obstruction added to the failure; the mission (Cripps and British staff) had **no Indian member**, but that was **not** why Congress rejected it.
[240:2:4] - **Gandhi** called the offer **"a post-dated cheque"**; "**on a crashing / failing / tottering bank**" was added in popular quotation (often credited to Nehru).
[240:2:5] - **Failure of Cripps → Quit India Movement (August 1942)**; Gandhi used "Quit India" in **Harijan** (April 1942). AP angle: rumours of Japanese bombing of **Vizag and Kakinada** during the mission.

## SUB 4 | Individual Satyagraha, 1940-41
[240:4:0] - Launched by **Gandhi on 17 October 1940** after the **August Offer was rejected**; started by **Vinoba Bhave** at **Paunar (Pavnar), Wardha**.
[240:4:1] - **Aim:** assert **freedom of speech against the war** (anti-war declaration; "wrong to help the British war effort with **men or money**"), show nationalist patience was not weakness – **not mass mobilisation**, so as not to embarrass Britain.
[240:4:2] - **First satyagrahi: Vinoba Bhave**; **second: Jawaharlal Nehru**; third: **Brahma Dutt** – not Rajaji, Patel, Sarojini Naidu or Bose. Also called **Individual Civil Disobedience**; "Delhi Chalo" march planned if not arrested.
[240:4:3] - **25,000** convicted by May 1941; suspended by the CWC in December 1941.
[240:4:4] - Government arrested **Vinoba, Nehru and Patel**; **Ongole Municipality** condemned the arrests (AP angle). Bihar: **Shyam Narayan Singh**.
[240:4:5] - APPSC AEE key: **only statement "Vinoba was the first" is correct** – the satyagraha's declared aim was free anti-war speech, not opposing the August Declaration.
[240:4:6] - **Mangal Bharat** – credited to **Vinoba Bhave** in the APPSC SI 2019 key (not verified).
[240:4:7] > 17 Oct 1940; Vinoba → Nehru → Brahma Dutt; free speech against the war.

## SUB 5 | August Offer, 1940
[240:5:0] - Made by Viceroy **Lord Linlithgow** on **8 August 1940** (at Simla), in reply to Ramgarh / the Congress offer, when Britain was losing in Europe.
[240:5:1] TABLE head: Proposal | Detail
  [240:5:1.0] Goal | Dominion status
  [240:5:1.1] Constitution | Representative constitution-making body after the war – "mainly" Indians to frame it
  [240:5:1.2] Executive | Expanded Viceroy's Executive Council with more Indians; War Advisory Council
  [240:5:1.3] Minorities | No constitution without minorities' consent (League's veto)
  [240:5:1.4] Not included | Immediate transfer of defence / a national government
[240:5:2] - **Significance:** **first time** the British conceded that **framing the constitution was primarily the Indians' responsibility** (NDA 2020) – the Cripps plan (1942) made it "solely" Indian and gave the concrete elected body.
[240:5:3] - **Responses:** Congress **rejected** it – **Nehru: Dominion status "dead as a doornail"**; the League welcomed the veto but wanted partition.
[240:5:4] - Constituent Assembly demand first raised by **M.N. Roy (1934)**; Congress adopted it in 1935.
[240:5:5] > August Offer = Linlithgow, 8 Aug 1940, first acceptance of an Indian-framed constitution.

## SUB 3 | Nature, Parties and Significance
[241:3:0] # Nature
[241:3:1] - **Spontaneous:** the arrests left it leaderless; historian **F.G. Hutchins** called it a **"Spontaneous Revolution"** (book, 1971) – not Gordon Johnson, David Arnold or Peter Robb.
[241:3:2] - **Not wholly non-violent:** peaceful at first, then attacks on police stations, post offices and railways after the firings.
[241:3:3] - **Labour did join:** strikes in **Ahmedabad (textile mills, over three months)**, Bombay, Jamshedpur, Ahmednagar, Poona.
[241:3:4] > UPSC 2011 "NOT true": the key is **"It did not attract the labour class in general"** (most keys); some keys give "led by Gandhi" (he was arrested at once).
[241:3:5] - **Storm centres:** **Bihar and eastern UP** (Azamgarh, Ballia, Gorakhpur; Gaya, Bhagalpur, Saran), Midnapore, Maharashtra, Karnataka.
[241:3:6]   - ⚠ Spectrum and PMF: there were **no communal riots** during Quit India – a "communal riots" stem means the disturbances.

  [241:3:8.4] RSS | Kept away

[241:3:10] # Significance
[241:3:11] - **Gandhi's last mass movement**; the **culmination** of the national movement – after it, only the **mode of transfer of power** remained to be settled (A and R true, R explains A).
[241:3:12] - Put **immediate independence** on the agenda; showed India could not be ruled against Indians' will.
[241:3:13] - **Struggle-Truce-Struggle (S-T-S):** Bipan Chandra's name for the Congress strategy of mass phases and truces; the UPPSC 2019 key links its prominence to the Quit India call.

[241:4:0] # Operation Zero Hour
[241:4:1] - **Pre-dawn arrests of 9 August 1942** – all top leaders taken to unknown places; CWC, AICC and PCCs declared **unlawful** (Criminal Law Amendment Act 1908) → the movement became **leaderless, spontaneous and violent**.

## SUB 0 | Indian National Army – Origin, First Phase and Second Phase
[243:0:0] # First INA (1942) – Mohan Singh
[243:0:1] - **Idea and founder:** the idea of an army of Indian POWs was **Captain Mohan Singh's** (Spectrum); he is the **founder and first commander** of the INA (Azad Hind Fauj) – not Subhas Bose, who took over only in 1943.
[243:0:2] - **Brainchild debate:** many books credit the idea to **Giani Pritam Singh Dhillon** (IIL, Bangkok) and **Major Iwaichi Fujiwara** (head of the Japanese **F-Kikan**), who brought the POWs to Mohan Singh – UPPSC 2001 key "I and IV".
[243:0:3] - **Where and when:** formed in **Singapore** (then British Malaya) in **February 1942**, after the **fall of Singapore**; ~40,000 volunteers by end-1942; first division of **16,300** men formally raised **1 September 1942**.
[243:0:4] - **Dissolved December 1942:** Mohan Singh quarrelled with the Japanese (they wanted a token force of 2,000) and was taken into custody.
[243:0:5] # Indian Independence League – Rash Behari Bose
[243:0:6] - **Rash Behari Bose** fled to Japan in **1915** (after the Hardinge bomb case), became a Japanese citizen, founded the Indian Club of Tokyo and the **Indian Independence League (IIL), Tokyo, 1942** (Tokyo conference 28-30 March 1942).
[243:0:7] - **Bangkok Conference (June 1942):** INA placed under the IIL; Rash Behari Bose president; Mohan Singh commander; Subhas Bose invited.
[243:0:8] - **Rash Behari Bose ≠ Rash Behari Ghosh** (Moderate). Rash Behari Bose helped Subhas set up the second INA and died in Japan on **21 January 1945** – so he was never in the Red Fort dock.
[243:0:9] - **Quote (CGPSC 2019 key):** "Avenge the blood of Indian Martyrs… Stand up against the entire Anglo-American enemy" – attributed to **Rash Behari Bose**.
[243:0:10] # Second phase under Subhas Bose (1943)

  [243:0:11.5] January 1944 | INA headquarters shifted to Rangoon (Burma)

## SUB 1 | Forward Bloc, 1939 and the Andhra Link
[243:1:0] - **Founded by Subhas Chandra Bose** in **May 1939** (at Makur, Unnao), after resigning the Congress presidency (April 1939) – a **party within the Congress** to unite the **radical, left-wing and anti-imperialist** forces (UPSC 2005: not "Indian Freedom Party", "Azad Hind Fauj" or "Revolutionary Front").
[243:1:1] - **Disciplinary action:** after his 9 July protest call, Bose was removed as Bengal PCC president and **debarred from Congress office for 3 years (August 1939)**.
[243:1:2] - **Ramgarh, March 1940:** Anti-Compromise Conference of the Forward Bloc and the Kisan Sabha.
[243:1:3] - **Sheel Bhadra Yajee** (Bihar): joined Bose in founding the **All India Forward Bloc**; also active in the INA movement.
[243:1:4] - **Mian Akbar Khan:** Forward Bloc head of the NWFP who arranged Bose's 1941 escape.
[243:1:5] # Andhra – Madduri Annapurnaiah
[243:1:6] - **Madduri Annapurnaiah** – the Andhra leader who **joined the Forward Bloc** of Subhas Bose (APPSC; Group-II 2019); led it in Andhra with **Peta Bapayya**; called **"Andhra Netaji"**.

=== NOTES 1:244 | Ina Trials and the Naval Mutiny of 1946; Impact on British Decision to Quit
## SUB 0 | INA (Red Fort) Trials, 1945-46
[244:0:0] # Venue, accused, verdict
[244:0:1] - **Venue:** **Red Fort, Delhi**; first trial opened **5 November 1945**; about ten court-martials, **November 1945 – May 1946**.
[244:0:2] - **First (joint) trial – the accused:** **Prem Kumar Sahgal** (Hindu), **Shah Nawaz Khan** (Muslim), **Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon** (Sikh) – a symbol of unity (UPSC 2021: they are "officers of the INA").
[244:0:3] - **Not in the dock:** **Mohan Singh**, Rash Behari Bose (died January 1945), Subhas Bose; Bhulabhai Desai was **counsel**, not an accused.
[244:0:4] - **Verdict:** guilty of waging war against the King-Emperor → transportation for life, **remitted by C-in-C Claude Auchinleck (January 1946)**; all three released.
[244:0:5] - **Rashid Ali:** INA officer given a **seven-year** sentence → Calcutta upsurge of **11 February 1946**.
[244:0:6] - Later lives: Shah Nawaz Khan became a **Union minister**; P.K. Sahgal married **Capt. Lakshmi**.
[244:0:7] # INA Defence Committee
[244:0:8] - Set up by the **Indian National Congress** (AICC Bombay, September 1945).
[244:0:9] - **Lead counsel: Bhulabhai Desai** – his last great contribution; with **Tej Bahadur Sapru, Asaf Ali, K.N. Katju, Jawaharlal Nehru** (also Sarat Chandra Bose).
[244:0:10] - **Not** in the defence: **Sardar Patel**, **Jinnah**, C. Rajagopalachari; **C.R. Das** and **Motilal Nehru** were dead by then.
[244:0:11] > Exam angle: venue → **Red Fort, 1945**; accused trio; lead counsel → **Bhulabhai Desai**; 7 years → **Rashid Ali**.
[244:0:12] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Brief History of Modern India p.496-498 (book p.464-466); PMF Modern Indian History Part 5 p.80-82; NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.67, p.76; app notes 1:244 (sub 0, 1)
[244:0:13] - **Not INA:** **B.C. (Balai Chandra) Dutt** was a rating of HMIS Talwar in the RIN mutiny of 1946, not an INA officer.
## SUB 1 | Royal Indian Navy Mutiny, February 1946
[244:1:0] - **Start:** **18 February 1946**, ratings of **HMIS Talwar**, **Bombay**; on 17 Feb they refused a meal of watery, stone-filled food.
[244:1:1] - **Immediate cause:** **bad food and the racial arrogance / abuse of British officers**.
[244:1:2] - **Demands:** better food and **living conditions**, civilised treatment, **equal pay** with white sailors, **release of INA prisoners** and political prisoners, withdrawal of Indian troops from Indonesia (Java) and China.
[244:1:3] - **Spread:** Karachi, Calcutta, Madras, **Visakhapatnam**, Cochin …; about **20,000** ratings, **78 ships**, **20 shore establishments**; RIAF sympathy strikes.
[244:1:4] - **Flags:** Congress tricolour, League green and Communist red hoisted together.
[244:1:5] - **Naval Central Strike Committee** – president **M.S. Khan**; **B.C. Dutt** was a Talwar rating (not INA).
[244:1:6] - **Support:** Communists and **Aruna Asaf Ali**; Congress and League opposed it; **Patel and Jinnah** persuaded the ratings to **surrender on 23 February 1946**.
[244:1:7] - RIN set up **1934**; ratings got freedom-fighter pensions in **1973**; 80th anniversary **18 February 2026**.
[244:1:8] > Exam angle: ship → **HMIS Talwar**; reason / immediate demand → **food and treatment**; surrender → **Patel and Jinnah**.
[244:1:9] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern Indian History Part 5 p.82-84; Spectrum Brief History of Modern India p.499-500 (book p.467-468); NextGen Indian History APPSC Part 6 p.76; app notes 1:244 (sub 3)
## SUB 2 | INA Campaign and the Upsurges of 1945-46
[244:2:0] - **Unprecedented campaign:** INA Week **5-11 November 1945**; **INA Day 12 November 1945**; press, meetings, funds; supported by Congress, Muslim League, Communists, Akalis, Justice Party, RSS, Hindu Mahasabha …
[244:2:1] - Loyalists, government employees and armed-forces men also sympathised – "an edge of a volcano".

## SUB 3 | Impact on the British Decision; Chronology
[244:3:0] - INA trials + RIN mutiny showed the British could **no longer rely on the loyalty of the Indian armed forces** (the 2.5-million Indian Army).
[244:3:1] - **Attlee** (to Justice P.V. Chakraborty): the INA and the RIN mutiny, not Quit India, made the British leave; Gandhi's role "minimal".
[244:3:2] - RIN mutiny = "last nail in the coffin"; **Cabinet Mission announced 19 February 1946** (a day after), arrived **24 March 1946**.
[244:3:3] - **20 February 1947:** Attlee – British to leave by **June 1948**.
