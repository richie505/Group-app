## SUB 0 | The Quit India Resolution – Causes, Dates and Venue
[0:0] # Why 1942
[0:1] TABLE head: Cause | Detail
  [0:1.0] Failure of the Cripps Mission (March-April 1942) | No immediate transfer of power – the main trigger; QIM was launched in response to the Cripps Proposals (UPSC 2013) – not the Cabinet Mission, Simon Report or Wavell Plan
  [0:1.1] Japanese advance | Malaya, Singapore overrun; Rangoon fell in March 1942; Japanese troops at India's eastern border
  [0:1.2] Economic distress | Rising prices, shortage of rice and salt, boats commandeered in Bengal and Orissa
  [0:1.3] Loss of faith in British rule | Deposits withdrawn from banks; racist evacuation from South-East Asia ("Black Road" / "White Road")
[0:2] - **British PM:** **Winston Churchill**; **Viceroy:** **Lord Linlithgow**.
[0:3] # Meetings and dates
[0:4] TABLE head: Date | Meeting / place | What happened
  [0:4.0] 6 July 1942 | CWC (Wardha) | Gandhi first discussed his Quit India plan; Congress President = Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (1940-46)
  [0:4.1] 14 July 1942 | CWC at Wardha | Resolution accepted – the "Wardha Resolution"; Gandhi authorised to lead a non-violent mass struggle
  [0:4.2] 7-8 August 1942 | AICC, Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay (now August Kranti Maidan) | Resolution ratified on 8 August – the "August Resolution"; "Do or Die" speech the same night
  [0:4.3] 9 August 1942 | All India | Leaders arrested at dawn; the movement began (keys: "began 9 August", "started August 1942")
[0:5] - **Year:** **1942** – not 1940 (Individual Satyagraha), 1946 or 1947; "August Kranti" is the other name of the movement (not October Revolution etc.).
[0:6] - **Moved by:** **Jawaharlal Nehru**, **seconded by Sardar Patel** (Spectrum) – not Narendra Deva, Rajendra Prasad or Kripalani.
[0:7] - **President of the Congress when it passed:** **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** – not Gandhi, Patel or Nehru.
[0:8] - **Launched at:** **Bombay** – not Sabarmati, Calcutta, Madras, Lucknow, Lahore or Karachi; Wardha was only the CWC meeting.
[0:9] - **Leader:** **Mahatma Gandhi** was named leader of the struggle.
[0:10] - **⚠ Trap:** the AICC ratified it on **8 August**, not 6 August (a slip in one NextGen sheet); Gandhi's paper was **Harijan**, not Young India.
[0:11] # What the resolution demanded
[0:12] - **Immediate end of British rule**; a **provisional government** after withdrawal; free India to resist fascism; a **mass civil disobedience** struggle under Gandhi.
[0:13] - NOT in it: continuing British rule until the war ended.
[0:14] # Gandhi's instructions (spelt out at Gowalia Tank, never formally issued)
[0:15] TABLE head: Group | Instruction
  [0:15.0] Government servants | Do not resign – declare allegiance to the Congress
  [0:15.1] Soldiers | Do not leave the army – but do not fire on compatriots
  [0:15.2] Princes | Accept the sovereignty of your own people
  [0:15.3] Students | Leave studies if confident
  [0:15.4] Peasants | Pay agreed rent to anti-government zamindars; none to pro-government ones
[0:16] > Statement angle (UPSC 2005): "asked government servants to resign" ✗, "asked soldiers to leave their posts" ✗, "asked princes to accept the sovereignty of their people" ✓ → **only III**.
[0:17] # Order of events
[0:18] - **CDM (1930) → Individual Satyagraha (Oct 1940) → Cripps Mission (March 1942) → Quit India (Aug 1942)** = 1, 2, 4, 3.
[0:19] - On **8 August 1942** the AICC adopted the Quit India Resolution (UPSC 2021) – Congress ministries had resigned in 1939, the Executive Council was expanded in July 1941, Cripps came in March 1942.

## SUB 1 | "Do or Die" and Other Slogans
[1:0] # Do or Die
[1:1] - **"Do or Die" ("Karo ya Maro")** – given by **Mahatma Gandhi** in his Gowalia Tank speech of **8 August 1942** – the slogan of the **Quit India Movement** (UPSC 2009; APPSC G1 2002 ×2).
[1:2]   - Not Tilak, Nehru, Bose or Bhagat Singh; not the Swadeshi, Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, Khilafat or Dandi campaigns.
[1:3] - **Full mantra:** "Do or Die. **We shall either free India or die in the attempt**; we shall not live to see the perpetuation of our slavery" – Gandhi (not Nehru, Azad or Savarkar), Quit India.
[1:4] - **"Personally I am so sick of slavery that I am even prepared to take the risk of anarchy"** – Gandhi in 1942, the Quit India phase (NDA 2000).
[1:5] - **"Freedom is within our reach and we should hold it very tight"** – Gandhi (not Tagore), Quit India.
[1:6] - **"Quit India" (phrase):** coined by **Yusuf Meherally** (MPPSC 2018) – not Gandhi, Nehru or Aruna Asaf Ali.
[1:7] - **Harijan**, not Young India (closed in 1932), carried Gandhi's Quit India writings.
[1:8] > A-R angle: "QIM awakened and encouraged the people" ✓ because "people took up 'Do or Die'" ✓ → R explains A.
[1:9] # Slogans used as distractors

## SUB 2 | Arrests, Detention and Repression
[2:0] # Operation Zero Hour
[2:1] - **Pre-dawn arrests of 9 August 1942** – all top leaders taken to unknown places; CWC, AICC and PCCs declared **unlawful** (Criminal Law Amendment Act 1908) → the movement became **leaderless, spontaneous and violent**.
[2:2] - **Baldev Sahai** resigned as **Advocate General (Bihar)** in **1942** in protest against the arrests.
[2:3] # Where the leaders were held
[2:4] TABLE head: Place | Who
  [2:4.0] Aga Khan Palace, Poona | Gandhi (with Kasturba, Mahadev Desai, Sarojini Naidu) – about 21 months (Aug 1942 – May 1944)
  [2:4.1] Ahmednagar Fort | Nehru, Azad, Patel and the other CWC members
[2:5] - **Samadhis of Kasturba and Mahadev Desai:** in the **Aga Khan Palace, Pune** – not Yerwada, Ahmednagar Fort or Alipur jail; Kasturba died there in **1944**.
[2:6] - **Gandhi's fast:** **21 days from 10 February 1943** at the Aga Khan Palace, against the violence of the State → **three members of the Viceroy's Executive Council resigned**.
[2:7] # Repression and the Viceroy
[2:8] - **Lord Linlithgow** (1936-43, longest-serving Viceroy) called the movement **"by far the most serious rebellion since 1857"** – because of the **massive peasant upsurge** in eastern UP and Bihar (UPSC 1999: A and R true, R explains A).
[2:9] - **Linlithgow** called Gandhi's movement **"political blackmail"** – not Chelmsford, Wavell or Montagu.
[2:10] - Crowds lathi-charged, fired on, even machine-gunned from the air; **about 10,000 killed**; press muzzled; villages fined and flogged.
[2:11] - Martyrs: brothers **Gangaram and Kheemdev** fell in police firing at **Almora** (Uttarakhand) in 1942.
[2:12] ~~ Sources: Spectrum p.449, p.451-453 (pdf p.481-485); PMF Modern History Part 5 p.57-58, p.63; NextGen AP History Part 19 p.6; old app notes 1:241 (sub 3)

## SUB 5 | Nature, Parties and Significance
[5:0] # Nature
[5:1] - **Spontaneous:** the arrests left it leaderless; historian **F.G. Hutchins** called it a **"Spontaneous Revolution"** (book, 1971) – not Gordon Johnson, David Arnold or Peter Robb.
[5:2] - **Not wholly non-violent:** peaceful at first, then attacks on police stations, post offices and railways after the firings.
[5:3] - **Labour did join:** strikes in **Ahmedabad (textile mills, over three months)**, Bombay, Jamshedpur, Ahmednagar, Poona.
[5:4] > UPSC 2011 "NOT true": the key is **"It did not attract the labour class in general"** (most keys); some keys give "led by Gandhi" (he was arrested at once).
[5:5] - **Storm centres:** **Bihar and eastern UP** (Azamgarh, Ballia, Gorakhpur; Gaya, Bhagalpur, Saran), Midnapore, Maharashtra, Karnataka.
[5:6]   - ⚠ Spectrum and PMF: there were **no communal riots** during Quit India – a "communal riots" stem means the disturbances.
[5:7] # Attitude of parties
[5:8] TABLE head: Party | Stand
  [5:8.0] Muslim League | Opposed / condemned it – British and Muslims drew closer (A and R true, R explains A)
  [5:8.1] Hindu Mahasabha | Boycotted it
  [5:8.2] CPI | Did not support it – "People's War" after Germany attacked the USSR (ban lifted 23 July 1942)
  [5:8.3] Unionist Party (Punjab), princes | Did not support
  [5:8.4] RSS | Kept away
[5:9] - **All of these** (Hindu Mahasabha, CPI, Unionists) did not support QIM.
[5:10] # Significance
[5:11] - **Gandhi's last mass movement**; the **culmination** of the national movement – after it, only the **mode of transfer of power** remained to be settled (A and R true, R explains A).
[5:12] - Put **immediate independence** on the agenda; showed India could not be ruled against Indians' will.
[5:13] - **Struggle-Truce-Struggle (S-T-S):** Bipan Chandra's name for the Congress strategy of mass phases and truces; the UPPSC 2019 key links its prominence to the Quit India call.
[5:14] # Quit India today
[5:15] - **9 August 2016:** **Maharashtra** launched **"Quit India Movement 2"** against social evils at August Kranti Maidan, Mumbai (CM Devendra Fadnavis) – the anniversary event.
