=== Meaning and Definitions
# Indian Meaning
- **NextGen:** communalism is the belief that because a group follows one religion it also has **common social, political and economic interests**.
- In India, communalism is most often seen as **religious difference** between groups that leads to tension and rioting.
- A group promotes its own interests **at the expense of others**.

- **⚠ Trap:** The name is **T.K. Oommen**, not "T.R. Oommen". Oommen = six dimensions; Bipan Chandra = three stages.

=== Bipan Chandra's Three Stages
| Stage | Features |
|---|---|
| 1. Communal nationalism | Followers of one religion share common secular (political, economic) interests; still nationalist in outlook |
| 2. Liberal communalism | Interests of one community seen as different but reconcilable within one nation |
| 3. Extreme communalism | Interests seen as mutually antagonistic; politics of fear, hatred and violence |
- After the **1937 elections**, the Muslim League turned to extreme communalism "based on fear, hatred and violence"; echoed by the **Hindu Mahasabha and RSS**.
- **⚠ Trap:** Order: communal nationalism → liberal communalism → extreme communalism.

=== Causes of Communalism
| Type | Causes |
|---|---|
| Historical / colonial | British divide and rule; separate electorates; caste and religion recorded in the first census (1871-72); 19th-century revivalist movements |
| Political | Vote-bank and identity politics; religion-based parties |
| Economic | Poverty, unemployment, lopsided development, relative deprivation |
| Legal / administrative | Delayed justice, lenient punishment, police inaction, biased intelligence |
| Social / media | Fake news and hate messages on social media; media amplifying communal narratives |
| Psychological / international | Prejudices, rumours; foreign funding |
- **1920s:** Arya Samaj **Shuddhi and Sangathan** vs Muslim **Tabligh and Tanzim**.

=== Growth in Colonial India (1887-1940)
# Lord Minto and the 1909 Act
- **Oct 1906 – Simla Deputation** led by the **Aga Khan** to **Lord Minto** demanded separate electorates.
- The **1909 Act** gave the **first legal separate electorate** for Muslims.
- Lord Minto is called the "**Father of the Communal Electorate**".

=== Communal Award and Two-nation Theory
- **Syed Ahmad Khan** = ideological father of the **two-nation theory** (Hindi-Urdu controversy; advised Muslims to stay out of Congress).
- **Communal Award (16 Aug 1932):** announced by British PM **Ramsay MacDonald** (not Churchill, Attlee or Pitt); separate electorates for Muslims, Sikhs, Christians and **Depressed Classes** (for **20 years**); **71 seats** for depressed classes.
- **Poona Pact:** replaced Dalit separate electorates with reserved seats in **joint electorates**; **71 → 148** reserved seats in provincial legislatures.
- **⚠ Trap:** 71 = Communal Award seats; 148 = Poona Pact reserved seats; 20 years = Award's separate electorates

=== Forms – Oommen's Six Dimensions
| Dimension | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Assimilationist | Small religious groups absorbed into a larger one | Claiming STs, Jains, Sikhs, Buddhists are Hindus |
| Welfarist | Works for one community's welfare only | Christian or Parsi associations |
| Retreatist | Small community keeps away from politics | Bahai community |
| Retaliatory | Tries to harm members of other communities | Riots, revenge cycles |
| Separatist | Separate territorial state within India | Gorkhaland, Bodoland |
| Secessionist | Independent state outside India | Khalistan, "Azad Kashmir" |
- Other forms: **Political** = uses religion for votes; **Economic** = grows from competition for jobs and resources.
- **⚠ Trap:** Separatist = within India; Secessionist = outside India.

=== Religious Fundamentalism vs Communalism
- **Fundamentalism:** strict adherence to religious beliefs; often rejects modern interpretations.
- Features: **literal reading of scripture**, conservatism, resistance to change, **group exclusiveness**.
- Causes: **identity crisis**, globalisation, **social insecurity**, political mobilisation, economic inequality.
| Religious fundamentalism | Communalism |
|---|---|
| Return to "fundamentals"; mainly doctrinal | Political mobilisation of religious identity |
| Reforms one's own community (often anti-modern) | Directed against other communities; seeks power and resources |
| Can exist without inter-group conflict | Always "us vs them" |
- **⚠ Trap:** Group exclusiveness is a **feature** of fundamentalism, not a cause.

| Art. 14-16 | Equality |

| Art. 29-30 | Minority rights |

- **Match (ATWO PYQ):** Secular State – **Preamble & Arts. 25-28**; minority rights – **Arts. 29-30**; SC/ST seats in legislatures – **Arts. 330, 332**; universal adult franchise – **Art. 326**.
- Bodies: **National Integration Council**, **National Foundation for Communal Harmony**, MHA communal harmony guidelines.
- Other amendments (distractors): **24th** = Parliament's amending power; **44th (1978)** = undid parts of 42nd; **52nd (1985)** = anti-defection.
- APPSC GS 2007 key treated the **Shah Bano case** as a development that "contributed to the spirit of communal harmony".
