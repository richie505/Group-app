=== NOTES 2:115 | Rural, Urban and Tribal Society in India – Features and Interlinkages
## SUB 0 | Rural Community
[115:0:0] - Elements: **territory, we-feeling, fair self-sufficiency, small size, homogeneity**.
[115:0:1] - **Charles Metcalfe**: villages as self-reliant **"little republics"**.
[115:0:2] - **Redfield**, *The Little Community*: distinctiveness, smallness, homogeneity, self-sufficiency.
[115:0:3] **Cultural diversity** is NOT a rural element; diversity is urban (UPPSC 2021).
[115:0:4] # Village Studies

## SUB 1 | Rural-urban Continuum
[115:1:0] - **Redfield**: **folk-urban continuum** (Mexico, **Yucatan**) – a gradual scale, not two boxes.
[115:1:1] TABLE head: Feature | Village | Town/City
  [115:1:1.0] Occupation | Agriculture | Non-agricultural
  [115:1:1.1] Size | Small, sparse | Large, dense
  [115:1:1.2] Relations | Primary, face-to-face | Secondary, impersonal
  [115:1:1.3] Control | Informal (caste, custom) | Formal (law, police)
[115:1:2] - Village-to-town transition begins with **traders and manufacturers** (not wholly dependent on village resources).
## SUB 2 | Urban Society and Census Data
[115:2:0] - Urban life: **competition, impersonal relations, anonymity, weak control** – **not informal ties** (JPSC 2021).
[115:2:1] - **Louis Wirth**, *Urbanism as a Way of Life* (**1938**): **size, density, heterogeneity**.
[115:2:2] - **Urbanism** = social-cultural side; **urbanisation** = population shift.
[115:2:3] - **Urban area** = statutory towns (Municipality, Corporation, Cantonment Board, Notified Town Area Committee) + census towns.
[115:2:4] - **Census town**: **5,000** people; **75%** (not 60%) male workers non-farm; **400/sq km**; unchanged since **1981**; kept for **Census 2027** (17 Sep 2026), though MoHUA called it outdated.
[115:2:5] - Urban share **11% (1901) → 27.81% (2001) → 31.16% (2011)**; centres 1,827 → 7,935.
[115:2:6] - **2001-11 urban jump:** mainly **reclassification** of villages as census towns (about **2,500** added); urban share 31.1%.
[115:2:7] - **Largest UAs (2001):** **Greater Mumbai > Kolkata > Delhi**; by 2011 Delhi passed Kolkata.
[115:2:8] - 2011: **68.8% rural**, **6,40,867 villages**.
[115:2:9] - **Hidden urbanisation** (DEGURBA): ~**63%** urban (2015).
[115:2:10] - **16th FC (2026-31)**: ULB grant share **36% → 45%**; 41% urban by 2031.
## SUB 3 | Primary vs Secondary Urbanisation
[115:3:0] TABLE head: Primary | Secondary
  [115:3:0.0] Orthogenetic | Heterogenetic
  [115:3:0.1] Folk culture → Great Tradition | New thought replaces old
  [115:3:0.2] Moral order (temple centres) | Technical order
[115:3:1] - Typology: **Redfield and Singer**; **Singer** studied Madras Hinduism. **Marriott**: universalisation and parochialisation.
## SUB 4 | Urban Problems
[115:4:0] - **Industrialisation** drives urbanisation; **density** raises **crime**; maladjustment, rich-poor segregation, alienation.
[115:4:1] - **ES 2025-26**: informality (slums, informal labour) is **structural** – integrate, not eradicate; cities lack **fiscal autonomy**; **35%** rural households lack healthcare within 5 km.
[115:4:2] - Caste persists through slums and job networks.
[115:4:3] - **Jockin Arputham:** Magsaysay awardee; led the **National Slum Dwellers Federation**; **Dharavi** (Mumbai) is among Asia's largest slums.
## SUB 5 | Tribal Society
[115:5:0] - Features: common **name, dialect**, territory, ancestor, **animism/totemism**, clans, **youth dormitories**, simple economy.
[115:5:1] TABLE head: Tribe | Peasant
  [115:5:1.0] Isolated, kinship-based, egalitarian | Part of wider caste-class society
  [115:5:1.1] Common land and forest | Market-linked; individual land
[115:5:2] - **N.K. Bose**: Hindu method of tribal absorption; **Surajit Sinha**: **tribe-caste continuum**; **T.K. Oommen**: tribal autonomy.
[115:5:3] - STs: **89.97% rural**; **705** groups.
## SUB 6 | Migration
[115:6:0] - **Jan Breman**, *Footloose Labour* (**1996**): circular migrants in seasonal, casual, informal jobs.
[115:6:1] - Sources: **UP, Bihar**; push: monsoon failure, unemployment; urban wages **1.7-2x** rural.
[115:6:2] - **SPMRM (2016)**: "rural soul, urban amenities"; **PMEGP** (KVIC).

=== NOTES 2:117 | Communalism – Meaning, Causes, Forms and Growth in India
## SUB 0 | Meaning and Definitions
[117:0:0] # Indian Meaning
[117:0:1] - In India, communalism is most often seen as **religious difference** between groups that leads to tension and rioting.
[117:0:2] - A group promotes its own interests **at the expense of others**.
[117:0:3] # Definition → Scholar
[117:0:4] TABLE head: Scholar | Definition / view
  [117:0:4.0] Robert Melson and Howard Wolpe | "The political assertiveness of a community to maintain its identity in a plural society undergoing modernisation" (asked in APPSC G2 2025)
  [117:0:4.1] Bipan Chandra | The belief that because a group follows one religion, it also has common social, political and economic interests; gave the three stages
  [117:0:4.2] Ram Ahuja | An ideology that society is divided into religious communities whose interests differ and often oppose each other; "communal persons" do politics through religion
  [117:0:4.3] T.K. Oommen | Six dimensions of communalism (assimilationist to secessionist)
  [117:0:4.4] W.C. Smith (*Modern Islam in India*) | Ideology that treats each religious group as the basic social, political and economic unit and stresses antagonism between such groups
[117:0:5] The name is **T.K. Oommen**, not "T.R. Oommen". Oommen = six dimensions; Bipan Chandra = three stages.
## SUB 1 | Bipan Chandra's Three Stages
[117:1:0] TABLE head: Stage | Features
  [117:1:0.0] 1. Communal nationalism | Followers of one religion share common secular (political, economic) interests; still nationalist in outlook
  [117:1:0.1] 2. Liberal communalism | Interests of one community seen as different but reconcilable within one nation
  [117:1:0.2] 3. Extreme communalism | Interests seen as mutually antagonistic; politics of fear, hatred and violence
[117:1:1] - After the **1937 elections**, the Muslim League turned to extreme communalism "based on fear, hatred and violence"; echoed by the **Hindu Mahasabha and RSS**.
[117:1:2] Order: communal nationalism → liberal communalism → extreme communalism.

## SUB 4 | Communal Award and Two-nation Theory
[117:4:0] - **Syed Ahmad Khan** = ideological father of the **two-nation theory** (Hindi-Urdu controversy; advised Muslims to stay out of Congress).
[117:4:1] - **Communal Award (1932):** separate electorates for Muslims, Sikhs, Christians and **Depressed Classes** (for **20 years**); **71 seats** for depressed classes.
[117:4:2] - **Poona Pact:** replaced Dalit separate electorates with reserved seats in **joint electorates**; **71 → 148** reserved seats in provincial legislatures.
[117:4:3] 71 = Communal Award seats; 148 = Poona Pact reserved seats; 20 years = Award's separate electorates
## SUB 5 | Forms – Oommen's Six Dimensions
[117:5:0] TABLE head: Dimension | Meaning | Example

  [117:5:0.2] Retreatist | Small community keeps away from politics | Bahai community

## SUB 6 | Religious Fundamentalism vs Communalism
[117:6:0] - **Fundamentalism:** strict adherence to religious beliefs; often rejects modern interpretations.
[117:6:1] - Features: **literal reading of scripture**, conservatism, resistance to change, **group exclusiveness**.
[117:6:2] - Causes: **identity crisis**, globalisation, **social insecurity**, political mobilisation, economic inequality.
[117:6:3] TABLE head: Religious fundamentalism | Communalism
  [117:6:3.0] Return to "fundamentals"; mainly doctrinal | Political mobilisation of religious identity
  [117:6:3.1] Reforms one's own community (often anti-modern) | Directed against other communities; seeks power and resources
  [117:6:3.2] Can exist without inter-group conflict | Always "us vs them"
[117:6:4] Group exclusiveness is a **feature** of fundamentalism, not a cause.

=== NOTES 2:119 | Regionalism – Meaning, Causes, Forms and Sons-of-the-soil Movements
## SUB 0 | Meaning of Regionalism
[119:0:0] # Terms
[119:0:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [119:0:1.0] Region | Area shaped by history, with its own language, culture, identity and political implications
  [119:0:1.1] Regionalism | Emotional attachment to one area; may resist a dominant culture or challenge the nation-state
  [119:0:1.2] Supra-state | Several States sharing interests (e.g., the North-East)
  [119:0:1.3] Inter-state | Conflict between States
  [119:0:1.4] Intra-state | A region within a State seeking recognition
[119:0:2] # Positive vs Negative Regionalism
[119:0:3] TABLE head: Positive | Negative
  [119:0:3.0] Regional parties give backward regions a voice | Parochialism; hostility to migrants (Shiv Sena, MNS)
  [119:0:3.1] Cultural pride, balanced development, local innovation | Law-and-order breakdowns; openings for extremists
  [119:0:3.2] Solidarity (Tripura Tribal Autonomous District Council) | Inter-state disputes; new linguistic minorities
[119:0:4] - Regional feeling turns **negative** when exploited for votes by regional parties.
## SUB 1 | Causes
[119:1:0] TABLE head: Factor | Example
  [119:1:0.0] Economic inequality | 1948: Bombay and West Bengal held 59% of industrial capital; backward regions with low literacy, few industries
  [119:1:0.1] Fear of losing cultural identity | Gorkhas in Bengal
  [119:1:0.2] Linguistic identity | 22 official languages, about 1,635 mother tongues → linguistic State demands
  [119:1:0.3] Political neglect | Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Telangana
  [119:1:0.4] Migration and job competition | Migrants vs local educated youth for middle-class jobs
[119:1:1] UPPSC 2008: uneven development, fear of losing cultural identity and political prominence are all causes – "all the above".
## SUB 2 | Forms of Regionalism
[119:2:0] TABLE head: Form | Examples
  [119:2:0.0] Secessionism (out of India) | Dravida Nadu (1960s), Khalistan (1980s), Greater Nagaland (NSCN, 1980)
  [119:2:0.1] Separatism (new State inside the Union) | Uttarakhand, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Telangana
  [119:2:0.2] Autonomy demands | Tamil Nadu, Punjab, Andhra Pradesh, West Bengal (since 1960s)
  [119:2:0.3] Inter-state boundary disputes | Maharashtra-Karnataka (Belagavi), Assam-Mizoram, Assam-Nagaland, Haryana-HP (Parwanoo), Ladakh-HP (Sarchu)
  [119:2:0.4] Capital dispute | Chandigarh (Punjab vs Haryana)
  [119:2:0.5] Sons-of-the-soil | Shiv Sena, Mulki rules (section 4)
