## SUB 0 | Meaning of Regionalism
[0:0] - **NextGen definition:** regionalism = a feeling of **loyalty to a particular part of a country** and a wish for it to be **more politically independent**; a culmination of socio-economic and political factors, not just a territorial unit.
[0:1] - **Best represents regionalism:** regional demands for autonomy or, at the extreme, **secession by certain States**; preference for one's religion = **communalism**, caste loyalty = **casteism**, favouring one's language = **linguism**.
[0:2] # Terms
[0:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [0:3.0] Region | Area shaped by history, with its own language, culture, identity and political implications
  [0:3.1] Regionalism | Emotional attachment to one area; may resist a dominant culture or challenge the nation-state
  [0:3.2] Supra-state | Several States sharing interests (e.g., the North-East)
  [0:3.3] Inter-state | Conflict between States
  [0:3.4] Intra-state | A region within a State seeking recognition
[0:4] # Positive vs Negative Regionalism
[0:5] TABLE head: Positive | Negative
  [0:5.0] Regional parties give backward regions a voice | Parochialism; hostility to migrants (Shiv Sena, MNS)
  [0:5.1] Cultural pride, balanced development, local innovation | Law-and-order breakdowns; openings for extremists
  [0:5.2] Solidarity (Tripura Tribal Autonomous District Council) | Inter-state disputes; new linguistic minorities
[0:6] - Regional feeling turns **negative** when exploited for votes by regional parties.
[0:7] # Regionalism and tribal welfare (ATWO PYQs)
[0:8] - Regionalism **empowers local governance** for better tribal welfare and **strengthens State-centric tribal-welfare policies** (not centralisation, not 'no effect').
[0:9] - **Sub-regionalism** **enhances the focus on local tribal needs within smaller regions** (agency tracts, autonomous councils).
[0:10] - Intra-state regionalism is a **positive** form – self-dependence and development of the region (Saurashtra, East UP, Vidarbha).
[0:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.80, p.82-83; app notes 2:119 sub 0

## SUB 3 | Forms of Regionalism
[3:0] TABLE head: Form | Examples
  [3:0.0] Secessionism (out of India) | Dravida Nadu (1960s), Khalistan (1980s), Greater Nagaland (NSCN, 1980)
  [3:0.1] Demand for full statehood | UTs such as Arunachal Pradesh (1987) and Sikkim
  [3:0.2] Separatism (new State inside the Union) | Uttarakhand, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Telangana
  [3:0.3] Autonomy demands | Tamil Nadu, Punjab, Andhra Pradesh, West Bengal (since 1960s)
  [3:0.4] Inter-state boundary disputes | Maharashtra-Karnataka (Belagavi), Assam-Mizoram, Assam-Nagaland, Haryana-HP (Parwanoo), Ladakh-HP (Sarchu)
  [3:0.5] Capital dispute | Chandigarh (Punjab vs Haryana)
  [3:0.6] Sons-of-the-soil | Shiv Sena, Mulki rules (section 4)
[3:1] - **NSCN:** formed **31 January 1980** by Isak Chishi Swu, Th. Muivah and S.S. Khaplang after rejecting the **Shillong Accord (1975)**; split into NSCN-IM and NSCN-K in **1988**.
[3:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Secessionism = out of India (Khalistan); Separatism = new State inside the Union (Telangana).
[3:3] ~~ Sources: NextGen Society G2 Part 1 p.82, p.85; PMF Internal Security Part 1 p.29 (NSCN 1980); app notes 2:119 sub 2

## SUB 5 | Sons-of-the-soil Movements
[5:0] - **Doctrine:** a State belongs to its main linguistic group; others are "outsiders", however long settled; grew with **1960s-70s job shortages**.
[5:1] TABLE head: Movement | State | Target / issue
  [5:1.0] Shiv Sena (1960s) | Maharashtra | South Indians
  [5:1.1] MNS (2008) | Maharashtra | North Indians
  [5:1.2] Mulki rules | Hyderabad / Telangana | Job preference for local residents; agitations of 1969 and 1972-73
  [5:1.3] Telangana movements | Andhra Pradesh | 1969 agitation; 2009-14 phase → statehood 2014
  [5:1.4] Anti-Hindi agitations | Tamil Nadu | 1937, 1965, 1986 → two-language formula

## SUB 6 | Causes
[6:0] TABLE head: Factor | Example
  [6:0.0] Economic inequality | 1948: Bombay and West Bengal held 59% of industrial capital; backward regions with low literacy, few industries
  [6:0.1] Fear of losing cultural identity | Gorkhas in Bengal
  [6:0.2] Linguistic identity | 22 official languages, about 1,635 mother tongues → linguistic State demands
  [6:0.3] Political neglect | Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Telangana
  [6:0.4] Migration and job competition | Migrants vs local educated youth for middle-class jobs
[6:1] - **⚠ Trap:** UPPSC 2008: uneven development, fear of losing cultural identity and political prominence are all causes – "all the above".
