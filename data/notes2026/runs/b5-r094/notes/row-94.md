=== Observed Impacts – Ice Sheets, Sea Level, Tipping Points and Species
# Already observed (cryosphere)
- **Observed consequence (APPSC Degree Lecturers 2023):** a **significant reduction in ice-sheet mass** – Greenland and Antarctica have lost mass for about two decades (Greenland ~278 bn t/yr and Antarctica ~155 bn t/yr in 2006-15; loss has **quadrupled since the 1990s**). Falling global temperature, more forest cover and fewer heatwaves are the opposite of what is observed.
- **Effects of climate change (NextGen):** rising sea levels, **shrinking mountain glaciers**, accelerating loss of the cryosphere, ocean acidification.
- **Most fragile, first affected (UKPSC 2010):** the **Arctic and Greenland ice sheet** – the Arctic warms **about four times** faster than the global average (polar amplification).
- **WMO (2025 report):** oceans hold **over 90% of excess heat**; Arctic sea ice near record low; glacier loss threatens water for **about 2 billion people** (not 200 million).
- **Ice core = cryogenic indicator** of past climate (UPPCS 2015): drilled from Antarctic/Greenland ice sheets or mountain glaciers; the **lower layers are older**; trapped air bubbles record past CO₂. Pollen, tree rings and evaporites are not cryogenic.
| Place | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Okjökull, Iceland** | **First glacier declared "dead"** (2014; memorial plaque 2019) |
| Quelccaya, Peru | **Largest tropical ice cap**; retreating ~200 ft/yr |
| Tebua Tarawa and Abanuea (**Kiribati**) | Uninhabited islets **submerged in 1999** |
| **Kiribati, Tuvalu** | Inhabited islands at risk of submergence |
| **Maldives** | **World's first underwater Cabinet meeting (2009)** to highlight sea-level rise |
| **IndARC** | India's first **underwater observatory in the Arctic** (Kongsfjorden, Svalbard, 2014; UPSC 2015) – not a radar, satellite or Antarctic base |
# Sea-level rise
- **Long-term (eustatic) change:** caused by **melting of ice sheets** and thermal expansion of seawater (CDS 2016). Atmospheric pressure, water density and floating **icebergs** cause only short-term or no change.
- **Principal causes:** **thermal expansion + melting glaciers and ice sheets**; global sea level up **~20 cm (1901-2018)**, ~11 cm since 1993; the rate has doubled to ~4.5 mm/yr. **Melting polar ice and sea-level rise** = most important effect of warming.
- "Sea-level rise of one metre by 2070" is **not** a correct statement (IPCC: ~0.43-0.84 m by 2100).
# Tipping points
- **Tipping point:** a threshold where **small changes trigger large, self-perpetuating, potentially irreversible** shifts.
- **Loss of Arctic (sea) ice = potential tipping point (APPSC G2 2025):** bright ice (high albedo) melts → darker ocean absorbs more heat → more melting. Cutting CO₂ or less solar radiation are not tipping points.
| Tipping elements (listed) | NOT tipping elements |
|---|---|
| Greenland and West Antarctic ice sheets, **AMOC**, permafrost, warm-water coral reefs, **Amazon**, **Arctic sea ice** (near record lows) | **Sahara dunes**, Deccan basalts, European temperate deciduous forests |
- **UNSG Brief #12 (Jun 2026):** warming ~1.3°C; **coral 1.2°C threshold already crossed** (most fragile; recovery from mass bleaching takes **more than 15 years**); the **Amazon** beyond ~2°C may flip to savanna and turn from a **carbon store into a carbon source**; beyond 3°C the land biosphere may become a source.
- **Amazon:** ~17-18% already lost; makes ~50% of its own rain.
| Impact (IPCC SR1.5, 2018) | 1.5°C | 2°C |
|---|---|---|
| Coral reefs lost | **70-90%** | >99% |
| Ice-free Arctic summer | **Once a century** | Once a decade |
# Global effects by region and species
- **TSPSC 2023 match:** **bush fires – Australia/USA**; **locust swarming – Africa/Middle East**; **heat wave – Antarctica** (record 2022 heat); **permafrost melt – Arctic** → A-IV, B-III, C-I, D-II.
- **Rutgers University study (2019):** warming wipes out about **twice as many ocean species** as land species (APPSC G1).
- **IPBES 2019:** ~8 million species; **up to 1 million threatened**; **~40% of amphibians**.
- Species shift **poleward and upslope** (cooler toward higher latitudes and elevations).

=== Heat Waves, IMD Alerts and Wet-bulb Temperature
- **Heat wave (IMD):** max at least **40°C in plains** and at least **30°C in hilly regions** (37°C at coastal stations), with a departure of 4.5-6.4°C from normal; or actual max **≥45°C regardless of normal** (severe ≥47°C). "30°C plains / 40°C hills" → **neither** statement correct.
- **Causes:** meandering **Rossby waves → heat domes**; **loo** over the Gangetic plains; global warming – **not** cold Arabian Sea currents.
| Colour | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Normal | No action |
| Yellow | Watch / alert | **Be updated** |
| **Orange** | Severe | **Be prepared** |
| **Red** | Extreme | **Take action** |
# Wet-bulb temperature
- Lowest temperature air reaches by evaporative cooling; combines **heat + humidity**; equals **dry-bulb at 100% humidity**.
- **~35°C wet-bulb = survivability limit**: **sweat can no longer cool the body**. **World Bank:** India among the first places to cross it routinely → implication = survival of humans and animals hit because shedding body heat by perspiration fails (**II only**, UPSC 2025); floods, cyclones and droughts in peninsular India are not what the wet-bulb warning implies.
- Indians faced **19.8 heatwave days** in 2024 (Lancet Countdown).
# Heat action and health
- **Ahmedabad (2013): South Asia's first Heat Action Plan**.
- **AP:** heat rising in **Palnadu, Kurnool, Anantapur**; hottest place **Rentachintala (Palnadu)**; **APSDMA Heatwave Action Plan 2026** – heatwaves kill more in AP than any other natural disaster; the **AP Heat Wave Action Plan** is cited as an **early-warning and preparedness** measure; **Rayalaseema – heat stress and chronic drought**.
- **Health:** warmer and wetter → **dengue and malaria** spread; floods → cholera; waterlogging → leptospirosis.

=== Agriculture, Monsoon and Adaptation
# Agriculture
- **Climate-Smart Village** approach = part of **CCAFS** (Climate Change, Agriculture and Food Security) research programme of **CGIAR** (headquartered in **France**, Montpellier); **ICRISAT (Patancheru, Hyderabad)** is a CGIAR centre → all three statements correct (UPSC 2021).
- Climate change is already **reducing wheat** output; **wheat/maize lose 4-10% yield per 1°C**; extreme heat cuts **milk yield 15-25%** (FAO-WMO, Apr 2026).
| Programme | Detail |
|---|---|
| **NICRA** | ICAR project, **2011**, led by **CRIDA, Hyderabad** |
| ACASA-India | **GIS atlas of crop vulnerability** to climate change |
| Varieties | **2,996** climate-resilient varieties released 2014-2025; heat-tolerant wheat **HD 3385** |
# Monsoon, water and drought
- **Over 80% of rain in under 100 days**; SW monsoon ≈ **75%** of annual rain; **NE monsoon (Oct-Dec) ≈ 11%**.
- **Each 1°C warming → ~7% more moisture** → heavier rain; extreme **pre-monsoon** rain events **doubled** (1981-2020). **El Niño weakens** the SW monsoon.
- **68%** of India drought-prone; droughts = **meteorological, agricultural, hydrological**. **National Water Mission:** **20%** better water-use efficiency.
- **Urban floods:** floodplain encroachment, wetland loss, poor drainage and more intense rain.
# Social impact and adaptation
- **UPPSC 2017 A-R:** climate change is increasing **social tension** (A); more frequent and intense extreme water events threaten **food security** (R) → both true, **R explains A**.
- **AP and PMFBY (APPSC G2 A-R):** extreme weather (floods, droughts, cyclones) caused big crop losses in AP (**R true**), but AP's PMFBY coverage did **not** rise steadily – AP left PMFBY for its own free crop-insurance scheme from 2019-20 (**A false**).
| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Crop insurance | **PMFBY (2016)**; weather-based insurance |
| Bio-shields | **Mangroves and Casuarina shelterbelts** on the AP coast |
| Early warning | CWC flood forecasts at **~350 stations** (not 3,500) |

=== Coasts and Cyclones – India and Andhra Pradesh
- **Cyclone-prone area:** about **8% of India's land area** (NDMA); ~5,700 km of coastline cyclone-prone.
- Cyclones are **more frequent in the Bay of Bengal**, but Arabian Sea cyclones rose **52%** (2001-2019) and very severe ones 150%.
- **Marine heatwaves** doubled since 1982; raise cyclone **winds up to 20%** and **rain up to 12%** → rapid intensification; in 2025 **more than 90%** of oceans had marine heatwaves.
- **INCOIS (Hyderabad):** Coastal Vulnerability Index from **7 parameters**. **NCCR, Chennai:** **33.6%** of India's coastline eroding (1990-2018).
# AP coast
- **AP coastline 1,053 km** (3rd; revised 2025), Srikakulam to Nellore; Godavari delta = **Konaseema**; erosion hotspot **Uppada (Kakinada)**.
- AP cyclones (**Hudhud, Titli, Michaung**) peak **October-December**.
