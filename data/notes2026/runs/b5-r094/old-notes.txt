## SUB 1 | Heat Waves, Wet-bulb Temperature and Health
[94:1:0] # IMD Definitions and Colour Alerts
[94:1:1] - **Heat wave**: max at least **40°C in plains**, at least **30°C in hilly regions**; or actual max **≥45°C** whatever the normal.
[94:1:2] TABLE head: Colour | Meaning | Action
  [94:1:2.0] Green | Normal | No action
  [94:1:2.1] Yellow | Heat alert | Stay updated
  [94:1:2.2] Orange | Severe heat | Be prepared
  [94:1:2.3] Red | Extreme heat | Take action
[94:1:3] - Causes: winding **Rossby waves → heat domes**; **loo** over Gangetic plains; **not** cold Arabian Sea currents.
[94:1:4] # Wet-Bulb Temperature
[94:1:5] - Lowest temperature air reaches by evaporative cooling; combines **heat + humidity**; equals **dry-bulb at 100% humidity**; measures heat stress better than air temperature.
[94:1:6] - **Survivability limit ~35°C wet-bulb**: sweat can no longer cool the body. **World Bank**: India among the first to cross it often (UPSC 2025).
[94:1:7] - Kerala and Tamil Nadu: wet-bulb about 31°C (7 Jun 2026). CMIP6: ~40 coastal districts may warm >1°C by 2040.
[94:1:8] - Indians faced **19.8 heatwave days** in 2024; 6.6 due to climate change (Lancet Countdown).
[94:1:9] # Heat Action and Health
[94:1:10] - **Ahmedabad (2013)**: **South Asia's first Heat Action Plan**.
[94:1:11] - AP: heat rising in **Palnadu, Kurnool, Anantapur**; hottest place **Rentachintala (Palnadu)**; **APSDMA Heatwave Action Plan 2026**: heatwaves kill more in AP than any other disaster. **AP Heat Wave Action Plan** = model of early warning.
[94:1:12] - **Dasra "Under the Weather"**: nearly **40% of districts** at high risk.
[94:1:13] - Warmer, wetter → **dengue and malaria** spread; floods → cholera, hepatitis; urban waterlogging → leptospirosis, typhoid.
## SUB 2 | Monsoon, Water and Drought
[94:2:0] - **Over 80% of rain in under 100 days** of SW monsoon; SW monsoon = about **75%** of annual rain; **NE monsoon (Oct-Dec) ~11%**.
[94:2:1] - **Each 1°C warming → ~7% more moisture** → more extreme rain, flash floods (Brahmaputra, Ganga, Krishna basins).
[94:2:2] - Extreme **pre-monsoon** rain events **doubled 1981-2020**.
[94:2:3] - **El Niño weakens** SW monsoon (cuts rice, sugarcane); **La Niña boosts** it.
[94:2:4] - **68%** of India drought-prone (NDMA); drought types: **meteorological, agricultural, hydrological**; main cause: inadequate and uneven rain; peninsular surface water scarce in poor years.
[94:2:5] - **National Water Mission** (NAPCC, **30 Jun 2008**): **20%** rise in water-use efficiency. Agriculture uses ~80% of fresh water.
[94:2:6] - **Urban floods**: floodplain encroachment, wetland loss, poor drainage, heavier rain.
## SUB 3 | Agriculture and Livestock
[94:3:0] - Climate change already **cutting wheat** output; **wheat/maize lose 4-10% yield per 1°C**.
[94:3:1] - Extreme heat cuts **milk yield 15-25%** (FAO-WMO, 23 Apr 2026); heat stress above 25°C.
[94:3:2] TABLE head: Programme | Detail
  [94:3:2.0] NICRA | ICAR project, 2011, led by CRIDA, Hyderabad; 448 climate-resilient villages in 151 districts
  [94:3:2.1] Climate-Smart Village | Under CCAFS of CGIAR (HQ France) (UPSC 2025); ICRISAT (Patancheru, Hyderabad) is a CGIAR centre
  [94:3:2.2] ACASA-India | GIS crop vulnerability atlas
[94:3:3] - Climate-resilient varieties: **2,996** released 2014-2025; heat-tolerant wheat **HD 3385**; digital soil-health tools.
## SUB 4 | Himalayan Glaciers and GLOFs
[94:4:0] TABLE head: Event | Place | Date
  [94:4:0.0] Chamoli disaster | Uttarakhand | Feb 2021
  [94:4:0.1] South Lhonak lake GLOF | Sikkim | Oct 2023
  [94:4:0.2] Thame floods | Nepal | 2024
  [94:4:0.3] Nepal flash floods | Nepal | 26 Aug 2026, >1,000 dead
[94:4:1] - **Gangabal and Nundkol lakes** (Harmukh, Ganderbal): highly GLOF-prone; ice-contact lakes grew 26% (1992-2024); Seismic Zone V.
[94:4:2] - **Zanskar** glaciers move slower than 30 years ago (IIT Bombay).
[94:4:3] - **Gangotri** melting = **global warming** (APPSC-G2 2025); retreated about **1,727 m** since 1935, faster recently.
[94:4:4] - **Hanging glaciers**: stranded on steep slopes (not valley floors); permafrost melt → ice-rock avalanches → LLOFs/GLOFs.
[94:4:5] - **Permafrost thaw** releases CH4 + CO2, damages infrastructure.
[94:4:6] - **ICIMOD** (Kathmandu) **Snow Update**: snow persistence **23.6% below normal** in 2025 (23-year low); ~27% below in 2025-26.
[94:4:7] # Bodies and Missions
[94:4:8] - **GSI**: glacier mass balance, GLOF mapping, Himalayan glacier inventory.
[94:4:9] - **Himansh**: **NCPOR** station, above **4,000 m**, **Lahaul-Spiti**, **2016**.
[94:4:10] - **NMSHE** (NAPCC): **12 thematic areas**; Himalaya supports **51 million hill farmers**; melt threatens lean-season flows of **Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra** and hydropower.
## SUB 5 | Sea-level Rise, Coasts, Cyclones and Fisheries
[94:5:0] - Causes: **thermal expansion + melting glaciers and ice sheets**; sea level up **~11 cm since 1993**. **Melting polar ice and sea-level rise** = most important effect of warming.
[94:5:1] - "Sea-level rise of one metre by 2070" = **not** a correct statement.
[94:5:2] - **INCOIS (Hyderabad)**: **Coastal Vulnerability Index** maps from **7 parameters**; **NCCR, Chennai**: **33.6%** of India's coastline eroding (1990-2018).
[94:5:3] # AP Coast
[94:5:4] - Coastline **1,053 km**, Srikakulam to Nellore. Godavari delta = **Konaseema**.
[94:5:5] - **Erosion hotspots: Uppada (Kakinada)** + Krishna coast.
[94:5:6] - Cyclones (**Hudhud, Titli, Michaung**) peak **October-December**; worst hit **North Coastal AP, Nellore/Bapatla**; harm paddy and aquaculture but feed rabi crops and tanks. **Rayalaseema**: chronic drought.
[94:5:7] # Cyclones and Marine Heatwaves
[94:5:8] - Cyclones **more frequent in Bay of Bengal**; but Arabian Sea cyclones rose **52%** and very severe ones **150%** (2001-2019).
[94:5:9] - **Marine heatwaves**: **doubled since 1982**; raise cyclone **winds up to 20%**, **rain up to 12%** → rapid intensification.
[94:5:10] - Fisheries: **>90%** of oceans had MHWs in 2025 → lower oxygen, harm fish.
## SUB 6 | Climate Vulnerability and Climate Risk Index
[94:6:0] - **NICRA**: 651 districts assessed → **310** highly/very highly vulnerable. Over 80% of urban people in hazard-prone districts.
[94:6:1] - **DST (2021)** most vulnerable states: **Jharkhand, Mizoram, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Assam, Bihar, Arunachal Pradesh, West Bengal**.
[94:6:2] - **AP SAPCC**: **north and central** very high; **southern coast moderate**; **south-west** relatively low. SAPCC 2025-30: **8 adaptation measures** for energy (heat → thermal power; erratic rain → hydropower).
[94:6:3] # Germanwatch Climate Risk Index
[94:6:4] - **Germanwatch**: German NGO (**Bonn**); released at COPs.
[94:6:5] - **CRI 2026** (COP30, Nov 2025; 1995-2024): India **9th** most affected; **430 events**, **$170 billion** losses, 1.3 billion people hit; annual rank **10th → 15th**.
[94:6:6] - **CRI 2021** = **16th edition**, India **7th** (score 16.67); 2022 and 2023 editions not published.
[94:6:7] - World Bank: **45 million** Indians into extreme poverty within 15 years. ODI: India could lose 3-10% of GDP a year by 2100.

[94:7:0] - **Already observed** (APPSC-GS 2023): **significant reduction in ice-sheet mass**; Greenland and Antarctic losing mass for two decades.
[94:7:1] - **WMO 2025**: oceans hold >90% of excess heat; Arctic sea ice near record low; glacier loss threatens water for **~2 billion people**.
[94:7:2] - **Ice core**: cryogenic indicator; from Antarctic/Greenland or mountain glaciers; **lower layers older**.

[94:7:5] - **Rutgers study**: warming wipes out about **twice as many ocean species as land** (APPSC-G1 2019).
[94:7:6] - **IPBES 2019**: ~8 million species; **up to 1 million threatened**; **40% of amphibians**.
[94:7:7] - Species shift **poleward and upslope**; krill loss → Emperor penguin breeding failure.
## SUB 8 | Tipping Points and 1.5°C vs 2°C
[94:8:0] - **Tipping point**: limit where small changes cause large, **self-feeding, irreversible** shifts.
[94:8:1] - **Loss of Arctic sea ice**: bright ice melts → darker ocean absorbs heat → self-feeding warming (APPSC-G2 2025).
[94:8:2] TABLE head: Tipping elements (listed) | NOT tipping elements
  [94:8:2.0] Greenland and West Antarctic ice sheets, AMOC, permafrost, warm-water coral reefs, Amazon, Arctic sea ice | Sahara dunes, Deccan basalts, European temperate deciduous forests
[94:8:3] - **UNSG Brief #12 (Jun 2026)**: warming ~1.3°C; **coral 1.2°C threshold already crossed** (most fragile; recovery >15 years); **Amazon** beyond ~2°C may flip to savanna (carbon source).
[94:8:4] - Beyond **3°C**, land biosphere may turn from sink to source.
[94:8:5] TABLE head: Impact (IPCC SR1.5, 2018) | 1.5°C | 2°C
  [94:8:5.0] Coral reefs lost | 70-90% | >99%
  [94:8:5.1] Ice-free Arctic summer | Once a century | Once a decade
  [94:8:5.2] Sea-level rise by 2100 | ~10 cm lower | Higher
[94:8:6] - Paris Agreement: well below 2°C, pursue 1.5°C.
[94:8:7] - **Amazon facts:** About **17-18%** of the Amazon forest has already been lost, and the forest makes about **50% of its own rain** by recycling moisture through its trees.
