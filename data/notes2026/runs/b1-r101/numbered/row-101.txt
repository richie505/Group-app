## SUB 3 | Kreedabhiramam and Kakatiya Court Works
[3:0] # Kreedabhiramam
[3:1] - **Author:** **Vinukonda Vallabharaya (Vallabhamatya)**, early 15th c.; a Telugu rendering of **Ravipati Tripurantakudu's** Sanskrit **Premabhiramam**.
[3:2] - **Form:** a **vidhi natakam** (street play) in **verse** – asked as **"poetry"** (APPSC PYQ); "Kreeda" = amusement, not a sports text; not grammar. English: *A Lover's Guide to Warangal* (Velcheru Narayana Rao and David Shulman).
[3:3] - **City described:** **Orugallu (Warangal)** in great detail – markets, temples, nightlife, festivals (APPSC G1 2002 ×2; G2 2002; APPSC PYQ ×2) – not Rajasekhara Charitra, Kalapurnodayam, Nilambari; not Amaravati, Rajamahendravaram or Pithapuram.
[3:4] - **"Abode of actors / drama artists":** **Orugallu** (G2 2011 ×3) – not Dwarasamudram, Devagiri or Madhura.
[3:5] - **Character Machaladevi:** a famous courtesan (dancer) of Warangal in **Kreedabhiramam** – not Himabindu, Veyipadagalu or Amuktamalyada.

## SUB 5 | Nannaya – Adikavi and Andhra Shabda Chintamani
[5:0] - **Court poet, teacher and adviser** of **Rajaraja Narendra**; the Chalukyas, claiming lunar descent like the Pandavas, encouraged him to "Andhraise" the Mahabharata (c. 1045–65), helped by **Narayana Bhattu**.
[5:1] - **Andhra Shabda Chintamani** (Andhra Bhashanushasanam) = the first grammar of Telugu, **written in Sanskrit**, **attributed to Nannaya** (G2 Mains 2017; G2 PYQ; APPSC PYQ) – not Tikkana, Errana or Peddana. ⚠ Modern scholars doubt the attribution, but exams credit Nannaya.
[5:2] - **Statements (G2 PYQ):** I. translated the Mahabharata as Andhra Mahabharatam ✓; II. credited with Andhra Shabda Chintamani ✓; III. "Nandampudi donated to Nannaya" ✗ – Nandampudi went to **Narayana Bhattu**.
[5:3] - **Title Vaganusasana / Vagaanushaasanudu ("maker of speech")** = **Nannaya** (ATWO) – not Peddana, Veeresalingam or C. Pullaiah; also **Adikavi**, Vidyadvaitudu, **Vagadheeshwara**, Shabda Shasanudu.
[5:4] - **Other works:** **Indravijayam**, **Chamundika Vilasam**; pioneered the **Champu** style (prose + verse); used the **Akkara** metre borrowed from **Kannada**.
[5:5] - **Significance:** Nannaya **fixed the grammar and literary register** of classical Telugu and began the Kavitrayam tradition; his **Andhra Mahabharatamu = first book in Telugu**.
[5:6] - **Word "Tenugu":** first used by **Nannaya** (NextGen); ⚠ some books credit Ketana with "Telugu".

## SUB 6 | Telugu Language, Earliest Inscriptions and Court Languages
[6:0] # The language
[6:1] - **Family:** **Dravidian** (South-Central branch, with **Gondi and Kui**) – not Aryan, Sanskrit or Pali families (Dy. EO 2008).
[6:2] - **Oldest** spoken and literary language of South India = **Tamil** (Polytechnic Lecturers 2012); Tamil got classical status first (2004).
[6:3] - **"Italian of the East":** **Telugu** – coined by the Venetian **Niccolo de' Conti** (15th c.) because its words **end in vowels**.
[6:4] - **"Desa bhashalandu Telugu lessa":** **Krishnadevaraya**, in Amuktamalyada.
[6:5] - **Official languages of AP:** **Telugu** (since 1966) and **Urdu** as second official language (**all districts** since the 2022 amendment).
[6:6] - **Classical language:** Telugu and **Kannada**, **31 October 2008**; total **11** classical languages after **3 October 2024**.
[6:7] - **Telugu Language Day:** **29 August**, birth anniversary of **Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy** (not Gurajada).
[6:8] - **Trilinga** theory: Srisailam, Kaleshwaram, Draksharama.
[6:9] # Before written Telugu literature
[6:10] - **Satavahana royal language = Prakrit**; **Hala's Gathasaptasati** is in **Prakrit** (Maharashtri), with Telugu words like atta, potta, pilla, pandi.
[6:11] - **First Telugu word:** **Nagabu** (Amaravati; found by **Veturi Prabhakara Sastri**); **Vepuru** – Myakadoni; **-ambul** – Chikkulla plates (Vishnukundins).
[6:12] # Earliest inscriptions
[6:13] TABLE head: Inscription | Issuer | Significance
  [6:13.0] Kalamalla (Kadapa) | Renati Chola Erikal Mutturaju Dhananjaya, c. 575 | First Telugu inscription (prose)
  [6:13.1] Erragudipadu | Renati Chola Punyakumara | First fully available; geya (song) style
  [6:13.2] Vipparla | Jayasimha (Vallabha) I, Eastern Chalukya | First complete early Telugu text (G2 2017)
  [6:13.3] Chejarla | Kubja Vishnuvardhana | Over 50% Telugu words
  [6:13.4] Addanki | Panduranga, commander of Gunaga Vijayaditya III | First poetic inscription – Taruvoja metre
  [6:13.5] Kandukur, Dharmavaram | Gunaga Vijayaditya | Seesa
  [6:13.6] Bezawada | Yuddhamalla II | First Madhyakkara
[6:14] - Other early records: Lakshmipuram (Jayasimha I, first complete Telugu prose), Sataluru (Champakamala), **Chebrolu** (**Marga and Desi** forms); Motupalli is a later Kakatiya record, not pre-Nannaya.
[6:15] - **Pitikayagulla** (Prakasam, 8th c.): shows post-Badami Chalukya influence on the script – not the first poetic inscription and not Gunaga Vijayaditya's.
[6:16] - **June 2026:** an ASI survey of the **Nallamala** Tiger Reserve copied **25 inscriptions** (2nd–16th c.).
[6:17] - **Bhattiprolu** (c. 400 BCE, Guntur): **Prakrit** inscriptions in a **variant of Brahmi**, carrying early Telugu place and personal names.
[6:18] - Script: Brahmi (Bhattiprolu) → common **Old Kannada–Telugu** script (Kadamba–Chalukya age) → separate rounded Telugu script **11th–14th c.**
[6:19] # Court languages
[6:20] - **Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi:** asked "official language" → **Telugu** (G2 2008; NextGen previous papers; Rocket) – Telugu entered royal records and literature under them; ⚠ the prasasti (praise) of their grants stayed in **Sanskrit**, grant details in **Telugu**.
[6:21] - **Kakatiyas:** inscriptions **mostly in Telugu** (APPSC PYQ key) – Telugu became the administrative language; ⚠ NextGen calls Sanskrit their royal language; Telugu grammar, poetics and desi metres grew in this age.
