=== NOTES 1:101 | Telugu Language and Literature to the 14TH Century – Nannaya, Tikkana, Errana, Palkuriki Somanatha
## SUB 0 | Telugu Language – Family, Status and Names
[101:0:0] # Family and Status
[101:0:1] - **Dravidian** family; sub-branch **South-Central Dravidian** (sister languages **Gondi and Kui**); Tamil and Malayalam are **South** Dravidian.
[101:0:2] - One of the **22 Eighth Schedule** languages; **official language of AP**; **Urdu** = second official language in **all districts** since the **2022 amendment** (earlier only a few districts).
[101:0:3] - **Classical language since 2008**.
[101:0:4] - **Telugu Language Day – 29 August**, birth anniversary of **Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy** (not Gurajada); Telugu Language Awards the same day; **Telugu Mahotsavam** every year in AP.
[101:0:5] - Old traditional view (not accepted linguistically): Telugu grew from the Desi bhasha (Paisachi) of Prakrit; D.C. Sircar found Desi words close to Telugu.
[101:0:6] # Names and Epithets
[101:0:7] TABLE head: Name / epithet | Origin / coined by
  [101:0:7.0] Telugu (Trilinga theory) | From Trilinga – three Shaiva centres Srisailam, Kaleshwaram, Draksharama
  [101:0:7.1] Andhra / Andhrika; Tenugu | Other names; Ketana first used the words Tenugu/Telugu
  [101:0:7.2] "Italian of the East" | Niccolo de' Conti, 15th-c. Venetian traveller – words end in vowels (APPSC GS 2021)
  [101:0:7.3] "Desa bhashalandu Telugu lessa" | Krishnadevaraya, in Amuktamalyada
[101:0:8] Italian of the East = a foreign traveller (Conti); Desa bhashalandu Telugu lessa = a king (Krishnadevaraya).
[101:0:9] - **Classical language status:** **Telugu** and **Kannada** were declared classical on **31 October 2008** (Ministry of Culture). Criterion (2005): **high antiquity of early texts/recorded history over 1500–2000 years**. Total classical languages now **11** (Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese, Bengali added on **3 October 2024**).

[101:2:7] # Court Language and Pre-Nannaya Telugu
[101:2:8] - **Eastern Chalukyas**: official language **Sanskrit** (G2 2008 key); Telugu = people's language; bilingual records – **praise in Sanskrit, grant details in Telugu**.
[101:2:9] - **Kakatiya**: most inscriptions **in Telugu**, which grew as an administrative language; Telugu grammar, poetics and desi metres grew.
[101:2:10] - **Pre-Nannaya written Telugu**: Kalamalla, Vipparla, Addanki, Bezawada (**not Motupalli**, a later Kakatiya record) → written verse existed before the Mahabharata; no pre-Nannaya literary text survives.
[101:2:11] - **Nannaya** made Telugu a language of high literature; fixed its grammar and literary style.

## SUB 4 | Nannaya, Narayana Bhattu and Grants
[101:4:0] # Nannaya
[101:4:1] - Court poet, teacher and adviser of Rajaraja Narendra; **first translator** of the Mahabharata; **Andhra Mahabharatamu = first book in Telugu**.
[101:4:2] - **Andhra Shabda Chintamani** = Telugu grammar **written in Sanskrit** (G2 2017 key; the G2 2025 key did not accept this attribution); modern scholars doubt his authorship.
[101:4:3] - Pioneered **Champu** (prose + verse); fitted Sanskrit metres; used **Akkara** metre borrowed from **Kannada**.
[101:4:4] - Other works: Indra Vijayam, Chamundika Vilasam.
[101:4:5] - Titles: Adikavi, **Vagadheeshwara, Vidyadvaitudu, Shabda Shasanudu**; Tikkana praised him as Kavitva Visharadudu.
[101:4:6] # Narayana Bhattu and Grants
[101:4:7] TABLE head: Grant | Recipient | Fact
  [101:4:7.0] Nandampudi agrahara | Narayana Bhattu, from Rajaraja Narendra | Nandampudi donation inscription (written by Nannaya) = proof of his help in the translation (G2 2019 key)
  [101:4:7.1] Navakhandavada agrahara | Pavuluri Mallana, from Rajaraja Narendra | –
  [101:4:7.2] Pantur | Eight Brahmins (Jayavarma) | –
  [101:4:7.3] Pedavegi inscription | Grant to Vishnu (Nandi Varma II) | –
[101:4:8] - Narayana Bhattu: knew **eight languages** – **Ashtabhasha Kavisekhara**; Kaviraja Shekhara, Karibha Vajrankudu, **Vangmaya Durandharudu**; **Someshwara I of Kalyani** made him his representative at Rajamahendravaram.
[101:4:9] G2 2017 key and G2 2025 statement III accepted "Nandampudi given to Nannaya"; one key gave the **Kupama** inscription as the evidence. Follow the question's wording.
## SUB 5 | Tikkana and Errana – Other Works
[101:5:0] - **Tikkana**: **Harihara Advaita** (unity of Shiva and Vishnu) – dedicated Bharatam to **Hariharanatha**; first work **Nirvachanottara Ramayanam** (to Manumasiddhi); **Kavi Vakbandham**, Krishna Satakam; disciple **Ketana**; first poet to receive a literary dedication.
[101:5:1] - **Errana**: **Harivamsam** (to Prolaya Vema, not Nannaya); **Narasimha (Nrusimha) Puranam** = first **Kshetra-mahatmya** kavya (G2 2017 key); **first prabandha writer**.

[101:6:0] TABLE head: Poet | Work | Note
  [101:6:0.0] Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya | Sivatattvasaram (~400 kanda poems) | Originator of the Sataka form

[101:6:1] - **Palkuriki Somanatha** (Palakurthi; Kakatiya period): **Prathama Andhra Viplava Kavi**; led the **Desi kavita movement** against Sanskrit-heavy marga style; popularised **dwipada**, ragada, manjari, kanda.
[101:6:2] - **Basava Puranam**: in **dwipada** (not Champu); life of **Basava** and **Veerashaivism**; not by Vemulavada Bhimakavi.
[101:6:3] - **Panditaradhya Charitra**: called the first Telugu **encyclopaedia**.
[101:6:4] - **Vrushadhipa Satakam**: 100 verses – a "first Satakam" claim.

## SUB 11 | Srinatha and the Prabandha Age
[101:11:0] - **Srinatha** (c. 1365–1441): bridge between Kavitraya and Prabandha; patron **Pedakomati Vema Reddi**; also at **Deva Raya II's** court, who gave the title **Kavisarvabhauma**.
[101:11:1] TABLE head: Work | Note
  [101:11:1.0] Sringara Naishadham | From Sriharsha's Naishadhiyacharita (Nala–Damayanti)
  [101:11:1.1] Haravilasam | To merchant Tippayya Setti; sea trade
  [101:11:1.2] Kashikhandam | At Kataya Vema's request
  [101:11:1.3] Bhimakhandam | Bhimeswara of Draksharama
  [101:11:1.4] Palnati Veera Charitra | Battle of Palnadu; instruments Sannai, Mukha Veena
  [101:11:1.5] Sivaratri Mahatmyam, Marutratcharitra | –
[101:11:2] - **Veerabhadra Vijayam** is by **Bammera Potana**, NOT Srinatha. The APPSC 2025 key: **Madanavilasa Bhanam** is NOT Srinatha's (Haravilasam, Sivaratri Mahatmyam and Palnati Veera Charitra are his).
[101:11:3] - **Prabandha**: epic kavya → prabandha; **strict metrical rules**, elaborate descriptions, strong shringara. Reddi courts → Srinatha and Errana; Vijayanagara Nayaka courts spread it; peak = **Ashtadiggajas**. Telugu literature "first influenced" by the **Vijayanagara Empire** (APPSC GS 2019 key).
