=== NOTES 3:11 | Overall Achievements and Failures of Planning in India; End of the Plan Era (2017)
## SUB 0 | Plan Era and Growth Record
[11:0:0] - **First Plan** launched **1 April 1951**; focus **agriculture** and irrigation. Plan system ran **1951-2017**.
[11:0:1] - Plans I-XI averaged **~4.5%** growth vs **~1%** before Independence; agriculture **2-3%** (vs 0.3%); industry **6-8%**.
## SUB 1 | Third Plan Failure and Plan Holiday
[11:1:0] - Third Plan grew only **2.2%** vs **5.6%** target.
[11:1:1] - Causes: **Sino-Indian War (1962)**, **Indo-Pak War (1965)**, **1965-66 droughts**.
[11:1:2] - **Plan Holiday: 1966-69** – only Annual Plans.
## SUB 2 | Fourth Plan – Gadgil Strategy
[11:2:0] - **Growth with stability** + progress towards **self-reliance**.
[11:2:1] - Targets: agriculture **5%** a year; industry 8-10%; **exports ~7% a year**.
[11:2:2] - Result: only **3.3%** vs **5.7%** (droughts, 1971 war); **14 banks nationalised (1969)**.
[11:2:3] - State plan aid: **Gadgil formula (1969)** → Gadgil-Mukherjee (1991); **Special Category** 90% grant : 10% loan, **General Category** 30 : 70.
## SUB 3 | Achievements vs Failures
[11:3:0] TABLE head: Achievements | Failures
  [11:3:0.0] Rise in NDP, savings, investment, per-capita income | High rural poverty
  [11:3:0.1] Self-sufficiency in basic, capital, consumer-goods industries | Rising unemployment – root cause of poverty
  [11:3:0.2] Foodgrain self-sufficiency, food security: Green Revolution 1966-67, FCI (1965) | Income inequality widened after reforms
  [11:3:0.3] Infrastructure – transport, irrigation, telecom | Land reforms poorly implemented; unequal land ownership
  [11:3:0.4] Education, scientific manpower, IT, space; IITs | Regional disparities persist
[11:3:1] **Chief drawback** of Indian planning = **implementation failure**.
[11:3:2] - **Other drawbacks:** lack of a **financial strategy**, misplaced faith in **investment** alone, besides **implementation failure**.
[11:3:3] Regional disparities = a **failure**, not an achievement.
## SUB 4 | Critics of Planning
[11:4:0] TABLE head: Book | Author
  [11:4:0.0] Planning and the Poor (1974) | B.S. Minhas
  [11:4:0.1] India: Planning for Industrialization (1970) | Padma Desai and Jagdish Bhagwati
[11:4:1] - **Padma Desai** – critic of planning; **first Asian woman** with a Harvard Economics PhD (1960).
## SUB 5 | Planning After 1991
[11:5:0] - After the Seventh Plan, public outlay cut; private investment rose.
[11:5:1] - **"Directed" → "indicative"**: plan shows **priority sectors**; private sector invests.
[11:5:2] - ★ Plans kept after 1991 because they give a **long-term perspective** in a market economy.
[11:5:3] Planning is **not a constitutional requirement**.
## SUB 6 | End of the Plan Era

  [11:6:0.0] Last plan | Twelfth (2012-17), ended 31 March 2017

  [11:6:0.2] Successor | NITI Aayog, 1 Jan 2015, by government resolution
  [11:6:0.3] NDC (1952) | Inactive; functions to NITI Governing Council; formal abolition pending
[11:6:1] - Abolition ended **discretionary plan grants**; NITI has **no financial powers** – Governing Council coordinates, does not redistribute.
[11:6:2] - 11th NITI Governing Council (19 Jun 2026): PM urged states to estimate **district-level GDP**.
[11:6:3] - AP: three economic regions – Visakhapatnam, Amaravati (**9 districts**, 44,962 sq km, ~35.15% of GSDP), Tirupati.

=== NOTES 3:12 | Niti Aayog – Structure, Mandate, Verticals, Differences from the Planning Commission
## SUB 0 | Niti Aayog – Basics and Status
[12:0:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact

  [12:0:0.4] Status | Non-constitutional, non-statutory; apex public policy think tank; advisory
  [12:0:0.5] Chairperson | Prime Minister (ex officio) – no full-time Chairman
[12:0:1] - A **think tank, not a fund allocator**.
[12:0:2] - **Finance Commission (Article 280), CAG, NCBC** = constitutional; NITI is not.
[12:0:3] - **Zonal Councils** = **statutory** (States Reorganisation Act, 1956); chaired by the **Union Home Minister**; a NITI representative attends as adviser.
[12:0:4] NITI was set up in **2015** – a printed key showing "2012" is wrong. How it was made (Cabinet resolution) does not explain why it was made.

## SUB 3 | Mandate and Objectives
[12:3:0] # Four Mandate Areas
[12:3:1] - 1. **Knowledge and innovation**
[12:3:2] - 2. **NITI for States** (cooperative and competitive federalism)
[12:3:3] - 3. **Driving transformational change**
[12:3:4] - 4. **Monitoring and evaluation**
[12:3:5] # Objectives in the Cabinet Resolution
[12:3:6] - **Shared vision** of national priorities with active involvement of States.
[12:3:7] - **Cooperative federalism**: structured support to States – strong States make a strong nation.
[12:3:8] - **Village-level planning**: credible plans at village level, aggregated progressively upwards.
[12:3:9] - National security in economic policy; partnerships with think tanks; knowledge, innovation and entrepreneurial support; monitoring and evaluation; capacity building.
[12:3:10] # Guiding Approach
[12:3:11] - **Bottom-up**; **cooperative ("marble-cake") federalism**; **Antyodaya**; inclusive, participatory development; focus on **small enterprises** (not sole preference for large enterprises).
[12:3:12] - **State rankings** (e.g. SDG India Index) → **competitive federalism**.
[12:3:13] - **Competitive sub-federalism:** competition among **sub-national units** (States, cities) on governance indicators.
[12:3:14] Assertion-Reason (G2 Mains 2025): A (non-constitutional advisory body) and R (village plans aggregated upwards) are **both true, but R does not explain A**.
