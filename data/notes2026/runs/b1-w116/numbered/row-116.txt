## SUB 0 | Amir Khusrau – Life, Titles and Languages
[0:0] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [0:0.0] Name, birth | Abul Hasan Yaminuddin Khusrau (1253–1325); born at Patiyali, then Etah district (now Kasganj, UP)
  [0:0.1] Title | Tuti-i-Hind ("Parrot of India") – he called himself so; PMF: given by Alauddin Khalji
  [0:0.2] Court | Chiefly Alauddin Khalji's court poet; poet laureate with a state pension under Ghiyasuddin Tughluq
  [0:0.3] Sultans | Served about seven Sultans (Kaiqubad, Jalaluddin, Alauddin, Mubarak Shah, Ghiyasuddin …)
  [0:0.4] Guru | Disciple of Chishti saint Nizamuddin Auliya – a Sufi, NOT a Bhakti figure
  [0:0.5] Roles | Poet, historian and musician – "all the above"
[0:1] - **Languages:** savant of both **Persian and Hindavi (Hindi)**; **first to use Urdu** (emerging Hindavi/Rekhta) for poetry; called **Father of Khari Boli**; riddles and verses in Hindavi grew into Rekhta/Urdu.
[0:2] - **Persian poetry:** founder of **Sabak-i-Hindi** (Indian style); **first Persian poet to depict the Indian environment**; said "Na Lafze Hindiwast az Farsi Kam".
[0:3] - **Peasant quote:** "Every pearl in the royal crown is the crystallised drop of blood fallen from the tearful eyes of the poor peasant" – **Amir Khusrau**.
[0:4] TABLE head: Title | Person
  [0:4.0] Tuti-i-Hind | Amir Khusrau
  [0:4.1] Sadi-i-Hind (Sadi of India) | Amir Hasan Dehlavi (Sijzi), for his ghazals
  [0:4.2] Homer of the East | Firdausi
[0:5] - **⚠ Disputed (seven Sultans):** keys say **Khusrau**; Nizamuddin Auliya also lived under seven or more Sultans.

[1:5] # Ziauddin Barani

[1:7] - **Jahandari** (kingship) follows the **Persian model** – awe and force; Sultans ruled by **zawabit** (state laws), so the state was not truly Islamic.
[1:8] - High office only for the **high-born**.

## SUB 2 | Music – Sitar, Tabla, Qawwali
[2:0] - **Sitar:** credited to **Amir Khusrau**; combines the Indian **veena** with the Iranian **tambura** – the classic example of **Hindu-Muslim harmony** in instruments.
[2:1] - **Tabla:** also credited to **Amir Khusrau** (developed from the pakhawaj).
[2:2] - **Origin:** sitar and sarod owe their origin to **Persian influence**; the **sarangi** is an **indigenous Indian** bowed instrument → answer to "NOT of Indo-Islamic origin" (⚠ MPPSC 2013 official key: shehnai).
[2:3] - **Khusrau's forms:** **qawwali**, **qaul**, **tarana**, khayal; ragas **Aiman (Yaman)**, Zilaf, Sazgiri.
[2:4] - **Chishti** khanqahs popularised **sama** and institutionalised **qawwali**.

  [2:5.0] *Ghunyat-ul-Munya* (1375) | Earliest Persian treatise on Indian music
  [2:5.1] *Rag Vibodh* | Somnath
  [2:5.2] *Man Kautuhal* | Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior

## SUB 4 | Ibn Battuta and Other Travellers
[4:0] - **Ibn Battuta:** **Moroccan**, born **Tangiers** (1304); reached Delhi **1333**; **Qazi of Delhi** under **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**; sent as **envoy to China** (1342); in India 1333–1347.
[4:1] - ***Rihla*** (in **Arabic**) – the travelogue that throws light on **MBT's reign**; detailed account of the **postal system** (horse post **uluq**, foot post **dawa**), espionage, sati, slave markets.

## SUB 6 | Nizamuddin Auliya and Sufi Literature
[6:0] - **Nizamuddin Auliya** (Mehboob-i-Ilahi) lived through seven or more reigns but **never visited the court**.
[6:1] - Refused **Alauddin Khalji**: "My house has **two doors** – if the Sultan enters by one, I will leave by the other." When **Jalaluddin** tried to meet him through Khusrau, he left for **Ajodhan**.
[6:2] - ***Fawaid-ul-Fuad*** = his conversations (malfuzat), recorded by **Amir Hasan Sijzi**.
