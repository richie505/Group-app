## SUB 0 | Amir Khusrau – Life, Titles and Languages
[116:0:0] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [116:0:0.0] Name, birth | Abul Hasan Yaminuddin Khusrau (1253–1325); born at Patiyali, then Etah district (now Kasganj, UP)
  [116:0:0.1] Title | Tuti-i-Hind ("Parrot of India") – he called himself so; PMF: given by Alauddin Khalji
  [116:0:0.2] Court | Chiefly Alauddin Khalji's court poet; poet laureate with a state pension under Ghiyasuddin Tughluq
  [116:0:0.3] Sultans | Served about seven Sultans (Kaiqubad, Jalaluddin, Alauddin, Mubarak Shah, Ghiyasuddin …)
  [116:0:0.4] Guru | Disciple of Chishti saint Nizamuddin Auliya – a Sufi, NOT a Bhakti figure
  [116:0:0.5] Roles | Poet, historian and musician – "all the above"
[116:0:1] - **Languages:** savant of both **Persian and Hindavi (Hindi)**; **first to use Urdu** (emerging Hindavi/Rekhta) for poetry; called **Father of Khari Boli**; riddles and verses in Hindavi grew into Rekhta/Urdu.
[116:0:2] - **Persian poetry:** founder of **Sabak-i-Hindi** (Indian style); **first Persian poet to depict the Indian environment**; said "Na Lafze Hindiwast az Farsi Kam".
[116:0:3] - **Peasant quote:** "Every pearl in the royal crown is the crystallised drop of blood fallen from the tearful eyes of the poor peasant" – **Amir Khusrau**.

  [116:0:4.1] Sadi-i-Hind (Sadi of India) | Amir Hasan Dehlavi (Sijzi), for his ghazals
  [116:0:4.2] Homer of the East | Firdausi
[116:0:5] - **⚠ Disputed (seven Sultans):** keys say **Khusrau**; Nizamuddin Auliya also lived under seven or more Sultans.

## SUB 2 | Music – Sitar, Tabla, Qawwali
[116:2:0] - **Sitar:** credited to **Amir Khusrau**; combines the Indian **veena** with the Iranian **tambura** – the classic example of **Hindu-Muslim harmony** in instruments.
[116:2:1] - **Tabla:** also credited to **Amir Khusrau** (developed from the pakhawaj).
[116:2:2] - **Origin:** sitar and sarod owe their origin to **Persian influence**; the **sarangi** is an **indigenous Indian** bowed instrument → answer to "NOT of Indo-Islamic origin" (⚠ MPPSC 2013 official key: shehnai).
[116:2:3] - **Khusrau's forms:** **qawwali**, **qaul**, **tarana**, khayal; ragas **Aiman (Yaman)**, Zilaf, Sazgiri.
[116:2:4] - **Chishti** khanqahs popularised **sama** and institutionalised **qawwali**.
[116:2:5] TABLE head: Treatise | Author / patron
  [116:2:5.0] *Ghunyat-ul-Munya* (1375) | Earliest Persian treatise on Indian music
  [116:2:5.1] *Rag Vibodh* | Somnath
  [116:2:5.2] *Man Kautuhal* | Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior

## SUB 4 | Ibn Battuta and Other Travellers
[116:4:0] - **Ibn Battuta:** **Moroccan**, born **Tangiers** (1304); reached Delhi **1333**; **Qazi of Delhi** under **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**; sent as **envoy to China** (1342); in India 1333–1347.
[116:4:1] - ***Rihla*** (in **Arabic**) – the travelogue that throws light on **MBT's reign**; detailed account of the **postal system** (horse post **uluq**, foot post **dawa**), espionage, sati, slave markets.

## SUB 6 | Nizamuddin Auliya and Sufi Literature
[116:6:0] - **Nizamuddin Auliya** (Mehboob-i-Ilahi) lived through seven or more reigns but **never visited the court**.
[116:6:1] - Refused **Alauddin Khalji**: "My house has **two doors** – if the Sultan enters by one, I will leave by the other." When **Jalaluddin** tried to meet him through Khusrau, he left for **Ajodhan**.
[116:6:2] - ***Fawaid-ul-Fuad*** = his conversations (malfuzat), recorded by **Amir Hasan Sijzi**.

  [116:6:3.1] *Mrigavati* | Qutban
  [116:6:3.2] *Padmavat* | Malik Muhammad Jayasi (Urdu version by Ghulam Ali, court poet of Tana Shah)

=== NOTES 1:117 | Provincial Kingdoms – Bahmani Sultanate and Mahmud Gawan; Bengal, Gujarat, Malwa, Jaunpur, Kashmir
## SUB 0 | Bahmani Sultanate and the Vijayanagara Wars
[117:0:0] - **Founder:** **Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah** (**Hasan Gangu**, Zafar Khan) revolted against **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** – **1347**; the **first independent Muslim kingdom of the Deccan**.
[117:0:1] - **Capitals:** **Gulbarga** (renamed **Hasanabad**) → **Bidar**, shifted by **Ahmad Shah I (Wali, 1422–36)** after annexing Warangal (not Muhammad Shah I, not Firoz).
[117:0:2] - **Muhammad Shah I (1358–75):** built the **Gulbarga Jama Masjid** (compared to Cordoba); fought Bukka I.
[117:0:3] - **Firuz Shah (1397–1422):** scholar-king, observatory near Daulatabad, Hindus in administration; defeated Deva Raya I.
[117:0:4] - **Last Sultan:** **Kalimullah**; kingdom ended **1527**.
[117:0:5] - **Bone of contention:** the **Raichur Doab** between the **Krishna and Tungabhadra**; also the **Krishna-Godavari delta** and Marathwada.
[117:0:6] - **Gunpowder:** the **first recorded use** of firearms in India was by the **Bahmanis (Muhammad Shah I) against Vijayanagara**, c. **1366–68**; Babur only popularised field artillery (1526).
[117:0:7] - **Bidriware** (Bidar): blackened **zinc alloy inlaid with silver**; under the **Bahmanis**, not Vijayanagara.

## SUB 1 | Jaunpur – the Sharqi Kingdom
[117:1:0] - **City:** founded by **Firoz Shah Tughlaq**, named after his cousin **Muhammad bin Tughlaq (Jauna Khan)**.

## SUB 0 | Kohinoor, Golconda Diamonds and the English at Machilipatnam
[118:0:0] # Kohinoor – the most-asked fact of this topic
[118:0:1] - **Mine:** the **Kohinoor** came from the **Kollur (Kolluru)** mine on the **Krishna** (Guntur / Palnadu) – APPSC G2 2011; AP History PYQ ×2. Not Paritala, Gollapalli, Malapalli, Panna or Hazaribagh.
[118:0:2] - **Kingdom:** Kollur lay in the **Golconda** kingdom, so "excavated from the mines of" = **Golconda** (SSC 2006) – not Bijapur, Odisha or Chota Nagpur.
[118:0:3] - **Found:** **1656** (NextGen); it is now in the British Crown Jewels.
[118:0:4] - **Presented to Shah Jahan by:** **Mir Jumla**, minister of **Abdullah Qutb Shah**, who went over to the Mughals (UPPSC 2015 ×2) – not Aurangzeb, Murad or Abul Hasan.
[118:0:5] - **⚠ Trap:** **Tavernier**, the **French** jeweller who visited the mines under **Abdullah Qutb Shah** and wrote *Six Travels*, only **recorded** the Kohinoor; he did not give it.
[118:0:6] - **Duarte Barbosa** (Portuguese, early 16th c.) also described the region's **diamond trade**; Tavernier gave the fullest account.

## SUB 9 | Qutb Shahi Administration and Revenue
[118:9:0] # Administration
[118:9:1] - Hierarchy: **Sultan → Taraf (Tarafdar) → Sarkar (Faujdar) → Pargana (Tahsildar) → Village**; Abdullah: **6 Tarafs**; Abul Hasan: **37 Sarkars, 517 Parganas**.
[118:9:2] TABLE head: Official | Duty
  [118:9:2.0] Peshwa / Wakil | Prime minister; renamed Diwan under Abul Hasan (held by Madanna)
  [118:9:2.1] Mir Jumla | Finance minister
  [118:9:2.2] Ain-ul-Mulk | Military department
  [118:9:2.3] Tarafdar | Governor of a Taraf: law and order, revenue, troops
  [118:9:2.4] Faujdar | Head of a Sarkar
  [118:9:2.5] Tahsildar / Deshmukh | Head of a Pargana
  [118:9:2.6] Mukaddam / Kulkarni | Village head / village accountant
  [118:9:2.7] Kotwal | City police; also ran the mint
  [118:9:2.8] Mahasibs / Mustasibs | In charge of public morals (like the Mughal Muhtasib)
  [118:9:2.9] Shahbandar | Chief port officer, customs
  [118:9:2.10] Majumdar / Dabir | Auditor / secretary
  [118:9:2.11] Kayastha | Scribes (record-keepers)
[118:9:3] - **Mahasibs / Mustasibs** (2023 screening) = **public morals** – not chief justice, Taraf head or civil cases.
[118:9:4] - **Kayastha** (AP History PYQ) = caste of **scribes** – not traders, physicians or professionals.
[118:9:5] - Justice: Sultan → **Shariat Panch** (chief justice) → **Qazi** → **Pargana Majlis**. **Sar Lashkar** = army chief (Akkanna).
[118:9:6] # Revenue
[118:9:7] - **Main source of income: land revenue** (2023 screening) – collected by **tax farming**, rights auctioned to the highest bidder; not tourism, customs or feudal levies.
[118:9:8] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [118:9:8.0] Mustajirs | Tax farmers (highest bidders, mostly Hindus)
  [118:9:8.1] Haveli | Crown lands
  [118:9:8.2] Dasabandhu lands | Paid only one-tenth
[118:9:9] - **Machilipatnam** was leased for **1,80,000 pagodas** a year.
[118:9:10] - Other sources: trade taxes, port customs, diamond-mine leases, taxes on courtesans and professions (military service did not replace taxes).
[118:9:11] - **State monopolies: salt, betel leaf, tobacco**.
