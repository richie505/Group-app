## SUB 2 | RPwD Act 2016 – Number Game
[140:2:0] - First **rights-based** disability law; replaced the 1995 Act; meets UNCRPD obligations.
[140:2:1] - **Sec. 2(s)**: PwD = long-term physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairment which, with barriers, blocks full participation.
[140:2:2] TABLE head: Provision | Value
  [140:2:2.0] Specified disabilities | 21 (from 7), e.g. autism, acid attack victims, sickle cell disease; Centre may add
  [140:2:2.1] Benchmark disability | At least 40%
  [140:2:2.2] Government jobs | 4%
  [140:2:2.3] Higher education | 5% seats
  [140:2:2.4] Free education | Benchmark-disability children 6–18
  [140:2:2.5] Monitoring | Chief Commissioner, Sec. 75(1)
[140:2:3] - Survivors of **forcible acid ingestion** recognised as acid attack victims (SC, 5 May 2026).
[140:2:4] "Passed in 2018" is wrong: passed 2016, in force 19 April 2017. 4% jobs vs 5% higher education.

## SUB 4 | Accessibility – Sugamya Bharat
[140:4:0] - **Accessible India Campaign (Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan)**: launched **3 December 2015**; DEPwD flagship.
[140:4:1] - **Three verticals**: **built environment, transport, ICT**.
[140:4:2] - **Sugamya Bharat App**: crowdsourced reporting of accessibility gaps.
[140:4:3] - **Accessible elections**: accessible booths, **Braille-enabled EVMs**.
[140:4:4] - **Sugamya Pustakalaya**: online library – DAISY, audio, Braille books.
[140:4:5] - SC: **safe footpaths** part of Art. 21, minimum width **1.8 m**; **digital access** part of Art. 21, citing UNCRPD; **prison manuals** to be revised (Dec 2025).
[140:4:6] 3 December = Disability Day **and** launch of Accessible India Campaign (2015).
[140:4:7] - **Prisoners with disabilities (SC, Dec 2025):** prisons must be made accessible and **prison manuals revised** in line with the **RPwD Act, 2016**.

=== NOTES 2:141 | Rights of the Elderly – Senior Citizens Act 2007 and Schemes
## SUB 0 | Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007
[141:0:0] # Enactment and Obligations
[141:0:1] - Enacted in **2007**; nodal ministry **MoSJE** (Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment).
[141:0:2] - Brought into force **state by state** from **29 December 2007** (not one date for all).
[141:0:3] - Creates a **legal obligation** on adult **children (biological, adopted, step)** and **legal heirs** to maintain parents and senior citizens who cannot maintain themselves.
[141:0:4] - The Act **punishes neglect** of old parents by children.
[141:0:5] - **Abandonment** of a senior citizen: up to **3 months** jail and/or **Rs 5,000** fine.
[141:0:6] - **Old age homes:** at least **one per district**.
[141:0:7] # Key Sections
[141:0:8] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [141:0:8.0] Section 20 | Medical care of senior citizens; basis of the NPHCE
  [141:0:8.1] Section 23 | Property transfer/gift made on condition of care can be declared void (cancelled) by the Tribunal if the transferee neglects the parent/senior citizen
[141:0:9] # Tribunals (AP)
[141:0:10] TABLE head: Feature | Detail
  [141:0:10.0] Maintenance Tribunal | Sub-division level; in AP headed by RDO / Sub-Collector
  [141:0:10.1] Appellate Tribunal | District level; in AP headed by the District Collector
  [141:0:10.2] Maximum maintenance | Rs 10,000 per month
  [141:0:10.3] Time limit | Case decided within 90 days
  [141:0:10.4] Appeal | Allowed, to the Appellate Tribunal
[141:0:11] # 2019 Amendment Bill and Earlier State Law
[141:0:12] - **2019 Amendment Bill:** proposed **removing the Rs 10,000 cap** and covering **in-laws and grandchildren**; it **lapsed** (not passed).
[141:0:13] - **First State law:** **Himachal Pradesh** Maintenance of Parents and Dependents Act, **2001**.
## SUB 1 | National Policies
[141:1:0] TABLE head: Policy | Year / ministry | Focus
  [141:1:0.0] NPOP (National Policy on Older Persons) | 1999; MoSJE | Older person = 60 years or above; 14 principal areas of intervention incl. health care and nutrition
  [141:1:0.1] Senior Citizens Act | 2007 | Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens
  [141:1:0.2] NPHCE | 2010-11; MoHFW (not MoSJE) | Health care of the elderly
  [141:1:0.3] NPSC (National Policy for Senior Citizens) | 2011 | Elderly women, "ageing in place"; harsh punishment for elder abuse; income security, homecare, health insurance; destitution among rural elderly
## SUB 2 | Central Schemes for the Elderly
[141:2:0] # Scheme Table
[141:2:1] TABLE head: Scheme | Ministry / agency | Benefit
  [141:2:1.0] IGNOAPS (under NSAP) | MoRD | Rs 200/month (60-79 years), Rs 500 (80+), BPL; Centre's share frozen at Rs 200 since 2007; about 2.2 crore beneficiaries (June 2026)
  [141:2:1.1] Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana | MoSJE; ALIMCO | Free physical aids (walking sticks, wheelchairs, hearing aids, dentures) to BPL seniors (60+, or income up to Rs 15,000/month); funded from the Senior Citizens' Welfare Fund (unclaimed deposits); at least 30% women per district; launched 1 April 2017 at Nellore, AP
  [141:2:1.2] AVYAY (Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana) | MoSJE | April 2021, renamed from National Action Plan for Senior Citizens; umbrella of 4: Integrated Programme for Senior Citizens, State Action Plan, RVY, Elder Line; Rs 287.81 crore utilised (ES 2025-26)

=== NOTES 2:142 | Population Problem, Ageing and Problems of the Elderly
## SUB 0 | Population Theories
[142:0:0] # Malthusian Theory
[142:0:1] - **T.R. Malthus**, *An Essay on the Principle of Population* (**1798**).
[142:0:2] - Population grows in **geometric progression** (1, 2, 4, 8, 16); food grows in **arithmetic progression** (1, 2, 3, 4).
[142:0:3] TABLE head: Check | Meaning | Examples
  [142:0:3.0] Positive checks | Raise the death rate | Famine, disease, war, misery
  [142:0:3.1] Preventive checks | Lower the birth rate | Moral restraint: late marriage, celibacy
[142:0:4] - Malthus opposed contraception; critics say he did not foresee advances in farm technology.
[142:0:5] # Other Theories
[142:0:6] TABLE head: Theory | Propounder
  [142:0:6.0] Demographic transition | Warren Thompson and Frank Notestein
  [142:0:6.1] Optimum population | Edwin Cannan (rejected Malthus's view of an inherent tendency to overpopulation)
## SUB 1 | Demographic Transition
[142:1:0] - Stages: **high birth and death rates** → **death rate falls** → **birth rate falls** → **both low**.
[142:1:1] - India now: **youth-bulge** stage.
[142:1:2] - **Dependency ratio** = (0-14 + 65+) ÷ (15-64).
[142:1:3] - **Demographic dividend:** growth from a rising **working-age (15-64)** share and falling dependency ratio; India's window lasts till about **2055**.
## SUB 2 | Population Growth in India
[142:2:0] # Growth Phases
[142:2:1] TABLE head: Period | Phase
  [142:2:1.0] 1911-21 | Decline
  [142:2:1.1] 1921 | "Great Divide"
  [142:2:1.2] 1921-51 | Steady growth
  [142:2:1.3] 1951-81 | Explosion (over 2% a year)
  [142:2:1.4] 1981-2011 | High but slowing
[142:2:2] # Census 2011 and Age Structure
[142:2:3] - Population about **121.09 crore**; **68.9% rural**; decadal growth (2001-11) **17.7%**.
[142:2:4] - **Census 2011:** **15th** census (7th after Independence); houselisting began **1 April 2010**; slogan "Our Census, Our Future"; ⚠ APPSC key (2010) gave 1 March 2010.

## SUB 4 | Family Planning and Population Policy
[142:4:0] # Family Planning Programme
[142:4:1] - India was the **first country** to launch a **national family planning programme** (**1952**); early approach was **clinic-based**, mainly in cities.
[142:4:2] - **ICPD, Cairo 1994**: shift from population control to **individual well-being** and reproductive health.
[142:4:3] - **April 1996**: **target-free approach** – method-specific contraceptive targets dropped.

=== NOTES 2:143 | Transgender Persons – Nalsa Judgment, 2019 Act; Provisions for the Elderly
## SUB 0 | Nalsa v. Union of India (2014)
[143:0:0] - Delivered **15 April 2014** by Justices **K.S. Radhakrishnan** and **A.K. Sikri**.
[143:0:1] - Recognised transgender persons as the **"third gender"**.
[143:0:2] - **Gender identity is self-determined** – not by biological examination or State verification.
[143:0:3] # Directions
[143:0:4] - Treat as **SEBC** (socially and educationally backward classes) for reservation in education and jobs → a **vertical, OBC-type** quota.
[143:0:5] - Separate **public toilets**, health facilities and welfare schemes.
[143:0:6] NALSA gave a **vertical** quota; the community demands **horizontal** reservation.

## SUB 3 | Transgender Persons (protection of Rights) Act, 2019
[143:3:0] # Definition and Identity
[143:3:1] - **Definition:** person whose gender does not match that assigned at birth; includes **trans men/women**, **intersex variations**, **genderqueer** and socio-cultural identities (**kinner, hijra, aravani, jogta**).
[143:3:2] - Objects cited NALSA and the right to **self-perceived gender identity**.
[143:3:3] - Administered by **MoSJE**.
[143:3:4] - **Certificate of identity:** issued by the **District Magistrate**; a **revised certificate after surgery**.
[143:3:5] # Offences and Criticism
[143:3:6] - Forced/bonded labour, denial of public places, removal from household, abuse = **6 months to 2 years + fine**.
[143:3:7] - Criticism: **lighter penalties** than for similar crimes against women; **DM gatekeeping** of identity; **no reservation**.
[143:3:8] # 2019 Act vs 2026 Amendment
[143:3:9] TABLE head: Aspect | 2019 | 2026
  [143:3:9.0] Identity | Self-perceived (Objects) | Self-perceived excluded; limited to socio-cultural identities, intersex/congenital variations, those forced to present as transgender
  [143:3:9.1] Section 4(2) | Self-determination | Omitted
  [143:3:9.2] Certificate | DM alone | DM on a medical board's recommendation
  [143:3:9.3] Offences | 6 months-2 years + fine | Adds kidnapping, forcing a person to present as transgender
[143:3:10] - 2026 Act challenged in SC by **Laxmi Narayan Tripathi** and **Zainab Javid Patel** as contrary to NALSA; Aug 2026: Centre assured older ID cards remain valid.
## SUB 4 | Rules 2020 and National Council
