=== Population Growth in India – Phases and Census Data
| Period | Phase |
|---|---|
| 1901-21 | Stagnant (negative growth 1911-21) |
| 1921 | "Great Divide" |
| 1921-51 | Steady growth |
| **1951-81** | **Population explosion** – rapid fall in deaths, high births; ~2.2% a year |
| 1981-2011 | High but slowing |
- **1991-2001 (Census 2001):** highest growth **Nagaland** (64.53%; annual 4.97%), lowest **Kerala** (9.43%; annual 0.90%); 2001-11 Nagaland **negative**, **Meghalaya** highest.
- **Census 2011:** **15th** census; population about **121.09 crore**; **68.9% rural**; decadal growth (2001-11) **17.7%**; houselisting began 1 April 2010.
- **Most populous:** **UP > Maharashtra > Bihar** (2011), then West Bengal.
- **Sex ratio 2011:** India **943**; **Kerala** 1084 highest, **Haryana** 879 lowest.
- **Workforce 2011:** work participation **39.8%**; **agricultural labourers outnumbered cultivators** for the first time.
- **Religion 2011:** Hindus 79.8%; highest Hindu share **Himachal Pradesh** (~95%).
- **AP**: 495.77 lakh (**4.09%** of India, 10th) (2025-26). SRS 2024: working age 66.4%; children 24%.
- **Next Census: 2027.**
- **New Year's Day 2020 (UNICEF):** **India** recorded the most births (about **67,385**), ahead of China (46,299).
> Trap: explosion = **1951-81** (not 1921-51); 1921 = Great Divide.

=== Population Theories and Demographic Transition
- **T.R. Malthus**, *An Essay on the Principle of Population* (**1798**): population grows in **geometric progression**, food in **arithmetic progression**.
| Check | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Positive checks | Raise the death rate | Famine, disease, war |
| **Preventive checks** | Lower the birth rate | **Moral restraint**: late marriage, celibacy |
| Theory | Propounder |
|---|---|
| Demographic transition | Warren Thompson and Frank Notestein |
| Optimum population | **Edwin Cannan** |
- Stages of transition: **high birth and death rates** → **death rate falls** → **birth rate falls** → **both low**; India now: **youth-bulge** stage.
- **Dependency ratio** = (0-14 + 65+) ÷ (15-64). **Demographic dividend** window lasts till about **2055**.

=== Fertility, Family Planning and Population Policy
| Source | India | Others |
|---|---|---|
| NFHS-5 (2019-21) | TFR 2.0 | AP 1.7; **Bihar** 3.0 (highest) |
| SRS 2024 | TFR 1.9 | Rural 2.1, urban 1.5; Delhi 1.2 (lowest) |
- India: **first national family planning programme** in the world (**1952**); early approach **clinic-based**, mainly in cities.
- **ICPD, Cairo 1994**: shift from population control to individual well-being; **April 1996**: **target-free approach**.
- **NPP 2000:** immediate – unmet need for contraception, health infrastructure; medium-term – TFR 2.1 by 2010; long-term – **stable population by 2045**.
- **National Commission on Population** (May 2000): chaired by the **Prime Minister**.

=== Effects of Population Growth and Control Debates
- Jobs needed for about **9.7 million** new working-age people a year (5 Aug 2026); land – **smaller land holdings**.
- Southern States' concern: population has **15-25%** weight in **Finance Commission** formulas.
- **Javed v. State of Haryana (2003)**: SC **upheld** the two-child norm for local body polls.
- AP's two-child norm for local polls dated from **1994**; removed by Bills in **2024**.
- **AP Draft Population Management Policy (March 2026)**: from **control to management**; **Rs 25,000** for a second or third child (an AP, not Kerala, policy).

=== Ageing – Indices and Problems of the Elderly
- **Quality of Life for Elderly Index** (2021): by **Institute for Competitiveness** at the request of **EAC-PM**; top: aged States Rajasthan, relatively aged Himachal Pradesh, North-East Mizoram, UTs **Chandigarh**.
- **LASI** Wave 1 **2017-18** (IIPS); **Elderly in India 2021** (NSO): AP elderly 12.4% vs India 10.1%.
| Problem | Detail |
|---|---|
| Health | Chronic disease; insurance age caps; elderly = **30-35%** of hospitalisation spending |
| Dependency | **70%** depend on others; **78%** have no pension (NITI, 2026) |
| Social | Ageism, loneliness, abuse, **feminisation of ageing** (more elderly women, widows) |
- **Integrated Programme for Older Persons**: NGOs get up to **90%** of the cost of old age homes.
- **NPOP 1999**; **NPSC 2011** (ageing in place; destitution among **rural elderly**).
- **Kerala**: first State with a Senior Citizens' Welfare Department, Commission and Elderly Budget (2026).
