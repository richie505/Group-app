=== Ryotwari System – Read, Munro, Baramahal; Features and Areas
# Origin and First Areas
- **Devised by:** **Captain Alexander Read** (first experiment) and **Thomas Munro** – not Cornwallis, Elphinstone or Holt Mackenzie; also called the **"Munro system"**.
- **First experiment:** **Baramahal (1792)** – territory ceded by Tipu after the Third Mysore War (Treaty of Srirangapatnam, 1792); Read was Superintendent of Revenue, Munro his assistant (1792-99).
  - Baramahal = present **Salem** and environs (Krishnagiri, Dharmapuri, Tiruvannamalai) – not Anantapur.
- **First in Andhra:** the **Ceded Districts (Rayalaseema)** by Munro, after his survey (1802-07); old notes date it **1802** (one APPSC stem says 1808 – still Munro).
- **Whole Madras Presidency:** officially enforced by Munro as **Governor (1820)**; approved by Lord Hastings.
- **Bombay:** extended by Munro's disciple **Mountstuart Elphinstone** after 1818 (Pringle's Ricardo-based survey; later Wingate and Goldsmid).
- **Areas:** **Madras**, **Bombay**, **Assam**, Coorg, Burma – about **51%** of British India; so Ryotwari = **South India** first, West India (Bombay) second.
> "First in Madras Presidency" = Baramahal; "first in Andhra" = Rayalaseema; "prevalent in" = South India (Madras).
# Features
- **Settlement directly with the ryot**, who paid revenue **directly to the Government** – no intermediary; peasant recognised as **owner (proprietor)**.
- Ryots **registered and given pattas**; land **surveyed and assessed before being taxed** (UPSC 2012: all three statements correct).
- Ryot could **sell, mortgage, gift, lease or sublet**; not evicted while the assessment was paid.
- Assessment fixed on the **land, not the crop**; **revised periodically – about every 30 years** (20-30).
- First demand **50%** of estimated produce (not 30%); **no automatic remission** after a bad harvest – only occasional reduction.
- Munro: **"The Ryotwari is a system which has always prevailed in India."**
# In Practice – Criticism
- "Many zamindars replaced by **one giant zamindar**" – the State as **ultimate landlord**; revenue very high, enhanced at will, payable even in drought.
- Arbitrary **'putcut'** assessment where no survey; over-assessment, poor records, unclear boundaries.

=== Thomas Munro and the Ceded Districts (Rayalaseema)
| Years | Munro's post |
|---|---|
| 1761 | Born 27 May, Glasgow; reached Madras as a military cadet 15 Jan 1780 |
| 1792-99 | Assistant to Read in Baramahal |
| 1800-07 | **First Principal Collector of the Ceded Districts**, HQ **Anantapur**, four sub-collectors |
| 1802-07 | Field survey of the Ceded Districts; Ryotwari introduced |
| 1814-18 | **President of the Judicial Commission** |
| 1820-27 | **Governor of Madras** – "Father of Ryotwari" |
- **Ceded Districts (1800):** Bellary, Anantapur, Cuddapah, Kurnool – ceded by the Nizam under the Subsidiary Alliance.
- **Palegars (poligars) suppressed** by Munro with **General Campbell** in **18 months** – 80 palegars removed (49 Cuddapah, 31 Bellary-Kurnool); not Wellesley, Metcalfe or the French (Dupleix, Bussy).
- **Why Rayalaseema:** no strong zamindars; he distrusted palegars and big intermediaries – **direct contact with cultivators**.
- **Spoke Telugu fluently** and ran the administration without interpreters (unlike other collectors); revered as **"Mandava Rishi"**.
- **Died of cholera at Pattikonda (Kurnool), 6 July 1827**; buried at **Gooty**.
- After Munro left (1807-08), **village-lease settlements** replaced ryotwari in the Ceded Districts until ryotwari was restored (about 1820).

=== Permanent Settlement (Zamindari) – Cornwallis 1793
# Origin
- Warren Hastings' five-year farming (1772) → annual leases → Cornwallis' **decennial (10-year) settlement, 1789** → declared **permanent in 1793**.
- **Introduced by Lord Cornwallis, 1793**, in **Bengal and Bihar**, then Orissa, the northern districts of Madras (Circars, 1802) and Varanasi – about **19%** of British India; **planned by John Shore** (with James Grant).
- "Introduced in **1795**" is wrong; Madras and Coorg were **not** under it.
# Features
- Made with **zamindars**, recognised as **owners (proprietors)**; rights **hereditary, transferable, saleable** (alienable, rentable, heritable).
- Revenue **fixed for ever**; zamindar paid **10/11** of the rental, kept **1/11**.
- **Sunset Law:** revenue due by sunset of the date, else the estate was sold.
- Cultivators reduced to **tenants-at-will**; many zamindars **did not issue pattas** because there was **no official check** on them.
- **Regulations of 1799 (Haftam) and 1812 (Panjam)** let zamindars seize tenants' property without a court.
# Why a fixed, high demand
- Stable income for the Company, cheap collection through few zamindars, a **loyal landlord class (political allies)**; officials hoped that with ownership and a fixed demand **zamindars would invest in land** so that area and yield rose.
- Drawback for the Company: after **1810 prices rose**, the zamindars gained, the Company could not raise its share → it was **rarely extended beyond Bengal** (UPSC-type: Statement I true, II false).
# Results
- **Ryots at the mercy of zamindars**; stable income for the Company; zamindars **allies of the Company** (not deprived of ownership).
- By 1815 about half of Bengal's estates sold for arrears → new landlords: **urban merchants and moneylenders**; **absentee landlordism, sub-infeudation, rack-renting**.
- **Litigation rose after 1793** because of Cornwallis' **judicial reforms (abolition of court fees, separate civil courts)** – not the land provisions (UPSC 2011 key: none of the three).
- Profits of agriculture went to the **zamindars**, not the cultivators or the Company.
- **Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885:** tenants' (occupancy) rights in **Bengal and Bihar**.

=== Mahalwari and Taluqdari; the Three Systems Compared
# Mahalwari
- **Devised by Holt Mackenzie, 1822** (Regulation VII of 1822) in the **North-Western Provinces of the Bengal Presidency** – the "father" of Mahalwari and the first officer to try it.
- **1833:** revised under **William Bentinck** by **R.M. Bird** (Regulation IX of 1833, "father of land settlement in north India"), carried on by **James Thomason**.
- **Areas:** **North-Western Provinces (Agra-Oudh), Punjab**, Gangetic valley, parts of Central India – about **30%**.
- **Unit:** the **mahal** (village or group of villages); land owned **jointly by the village community** (not the government); common ownership = **"Bhai Chara" / "Mahali"**.
- Revenue fixed per village, collected by the **village headman (lambardar)**; each cultivator paid his share, **village collectively liable**; **periodically revised** (not fixed).
# Taluqdari
- **Taluqdari = Awadh (Oudh)** – "Taluqdari – Bombay" is the wrong pair; taluqdar = landlord-middleman.
# Comparison
| Basis | Permanent | Ryotwari | Mahalwari |
|---|---|---|---|
| Settled with | Zamindar | Individual ryot | Village (mahal) |
| Owner | Zamindar | Ryot | Village community |
| Revenue | Fixed for ever | Revised ~30 years | Revised periodically |
| Area | Bengal, Bihar, Orissa | Madras, Bombay, Assam | NWP, Punjab |
| Man | Cornwallis, John Shore | Read, Munro | Holt Mackenzie, Bird |
- All three made land **saleable, mortgageable and alienable** – a commodity.
- Different systems in different regions created **different classes** in the peasantry.

=== Effects of the Land Systems; Revenue in Andhra
# Effects
- Rigid **cash revenue**, payable even when crops failed → loans from **moneylenders** → mortgage and **loss of land**; the moneylender became the **greatest evil** of the village economy.
- Cash revenue pushed **commercial crops** (indigo, cotton, opium, jute); the **American Civil War** cotton slump (1866) → defaults → land to moneylenders.
- New classes: **merchant-landlords** in Zamindari areas; **moneylender-landlords** in Ryotwari and Mahalwari areas.
- **Alice Thorner:** Ryotwari destroyed the village system and isolated farmers from the government.
# Andhra
| Year | Event |

| 1765-66 | Northern Circars (Sarkars) obtained – Mughal farman and treaty with the Nizam |
| 1788 | Guntur acquired |
| 1794 | Battle of Padmanabham – Vijayarama Gajapati Raju of Vizianagaram killed |
| 1800 | Ceded Districts given by the Nizam |

| 1846 | Uyyalawada Narasimha Reddy's revolt – attacked the Koilkuntla (Kovela Kuntla) treasury |
| 1858 | Madras Inam Commission – checked rent-free inam titles |

- **Pattern:** **Zamindari** in the coastal Circars (zamindars paid **peshcush**); **Ryotwari** in **Rayalaseema** (Ceded Districts); Mahalwari-type (headmen) in **Nellore**.
- **Haveli** = land under direct Company control; grouped into **mootahs** and auctioned (1802) – held as proprietary estates, many fell into arrears; **Moturpha** = tax on professions, trades and looms; **Inam** = rent-free title.
- Permanent Settlement in Andhra (1802): **auction system abolished, tax fixed**.
- **Mirasidar** (Madras) = holder of mirasi rights, the designated revenue payer; **Banian** = Indian agent of the Company; **Aurang** = warehouse/workshop (not the treasury) – UPSC 2020.
