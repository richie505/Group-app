[1:0:3] - **Not the presiding officer:** **Nehru** (he moved the Objectives Resolution) and **Ambedkar** (he chaired only the Drafting Committee).
[1:0:4] # Constitutional Adviser – B.N. Rau
[1:0:5] - **Sir B.N. (Benegal Narsing) Rau** – **Constitutional / legal Adviser**; prepared the **first draft** in **October 1947**: **243 Articles, 13 Schedules**, placed before the Drafting Committee.
[1:0:6] - Later a judge of the **International Court of Justice** (1952-53).
[1:0:7] - **⚠ Not** Sachchidananda Sinha, K.M. Munshi, Alladi or M.C. Setalvad (Setalvad = first Attorney General).
[1:0:8] # Drafting Committee
[1:0:9] - Set up by a resolution of the Assembly on **29 August 1947**; **7 members**; sat **141 days**.
[1:0:10] - **Chairman: Dr B.R. Ambedkar** – the **"architect" / "Father" of the Constitution**, "Modern Manu".
[1:0:11] - Members: **N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar**, **Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar**, **K.M. Munshi**, **Mohammad Saadullah**, **B.L. Mitter** (replaced by **N. Madhava Rau**), **D.P. Khaitan** (died 1948; replaced by **T.T. Krishnamachari**).
[1:0:12] - **NOT members:** **Nehru**, **Rajendra Prasad**, **J.B. Kripalani**, **Sardar Patel**, **Vijayalakshmi Pandit**, B.N. Rau (adviser, not member).
[1:0:13] - **Preamble:** proposed before the Drafting Committee by **Jawaharlal Nehru** (it was based on his Objectives Resolution).
[1:0:14] # Artwork of the original Constitution
[1:0:15] - **Calligraphy (English):** **Prem Behari Narain Raizada** (flowing italic); Hindi copy: Vasant Krishan Vaidya.
[1:0:16] - **Illustrations:** artists of **Shantiniketan** led by **Nandalal Bose**; **National Emblem** sketched by **Dinanath Bhargava** (under Bose); Preamble page illuminated by **Beohar Rammanohar Sinha**.

[1:1:1] # Adoption vs commencement
[1:1:2] - **Adopted, enacted and given to ourselves** by the **Constituent Assembly** on **26 November 1949** (not by the British Parliament, the GG or Parliament) – Preamble: "this **twenty-sixth day of November, 1949**".
[1:1:3] - **In force on 26 Nov 1949:** **citizenship**, **elections**, provisional Parliament, temporary and transitional provisions (Arts. 5-9, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391-393) – **not** emergency provisions or appointment of judges.
[1:1:4] - **Rest in force on 26 January 1950** – chosen because the Congress had observed **26 January 1930** as **Purna Swaraj (Independence) Day** (Lahore session resolution, **December 1929**, Nehru presiding) – ⚠ the resolution was not of "26 January 1929".
[1:1:5] - **Anniversaries:** 26 Nov 2019 = **70th** anniversary of adoption; Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas) observed since **2015**.
[1:1:6] > Statement angle (Raj PCS 2023): final session 24 Jan 1950 ✓ and Prasad declared elected President of India in it ✓ → **both correct**.

## SUB 2 | Composition and Method of Election
[1:2:0] TABLE head: Item | Figure
  [1:2:0.0] Total planned strength | 389
  [1:2:0.1] British India | 296 = 292 Governor's provinces + 4 Chief Commissioners' provinces (Delhi, Ajmer-Merwara, Coorg, British Baluchistan)
  [1:2:0.2] Princely States | 93
  [1:2:0.3] Ratio | 1 seat per 10 lakh (one million) population
  [1:2:0.4] After partition | 299 (provinces 229 + states 70) – members from Pakistan areas withdrew
  [1:2:0.5] 1946 result | Congress 208, Muslim League 73 (second largest), others/independents 15
  [1:2:0.6] Women members | 15
[1:2:1] - **Provincial seats** split among **Muslims, Sikhs and General** by population.
[1:2:2] - **Indirectly elected** by the members of the **Provincial Legislative Assemblies** (set up under the 1935 Act) – each community's MLAs voting by **proportional representation with single transferable vote** (the 292 provincial members).
[1:2:3] - **NOT** directly elected, **NOT** adult franchise (restricted franchise of the **1935 Act, Sixth Schedule** – tax, property, education), not nominated by the British, the GG, the Congress/League or Gandhi.
[1:2:4] - **Princely states' representatives nominated by the rulers**; so the body was **partly elected, partly nominated**.
[1:2:5] - **Minorities** (Christians, Anglo-Indians, Parsis…) were represented; it worked through many committees.
[1:2:6] - **Not members:** **Mahatma Gandhi** and **M.A. Jinnah** (all other leading personalities were in it).
[1:2:7] - **Ambedkar** – first elected from **Bengal** (eastern part, later East Pakistan); then from the **Bombay** seat vacated by **M.R. Jayakar** → he sat for **Bombay Presidency**.
[1:2:8] - **Provinces represented:** **Patel – Bombay**, **Rajendra Prasad – Bihar**, **H.C. Mookherjee – West Bengal**, **Durgabai Deshmukh – Madras**; Bihar also sent **A.N. Sinha**, **Jagjivan Ram**, Sachchidananda Sinha.
[1:2:9] - Princely states joined from the third session (**28 April 1947**: Baroda, Bikaner, Jaipur, Patiala, Rewa, Udaipur).

## SUB 3 | Demand for a Constituent Assembly and the Cabinet Mission
[1:3:0] TABLE head: Year | Step
  [1:3:0.0] 1895 | Constitution of India Bill – first explicit demand for fundamental rights
  [1:3:0.1] 1922 | Gandhi: India's destiny to be decided by Indians
  [1:3:0.2] 1931 | Karachi Congress – Resolution on Fundamental Rights and Economic Policy
  [1:3:0.3] 1934 | M.N. Roy first put forward the idea; Swaraj Party – first formal demand (Ranchi, May 1934); Congress adopted it (Patna, May 1934; CWC resolution June 1934)
  [1:3:0.4] 1938 | Nehru – CA elected on adult franchise
  [1:3:0.5] 1940 | August Offer – British accepted the principle
  [1:3:0.6] 1942 | Cripps Mission – proposal for an elected Assembly
  [1:3:0.7] 1946 | Constituted under the Cabinet Mission Plan (16 May 1946)
[1:3:1] - **Set up under the Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946** – **not** the 1935 Act, Cripps, Wavell Plan, Simon Commission or the Independence Act.
[1:3:2] - **Cabinet Mission** (arrived **24 March 1946**): **Pethick-Lawrence** (Secretary of State for India), **Stafford Cripps** (Board of Trade), **A.V. Alexander** (Admiralty) – **NOT William Wood**.
[1:3:3] - **Cripps proposals (1942):** Assembly **elected** by the lower houses of provincial legislatures by PR (princely states to **send** representatives); **any province not accepting** the new Constitution could **sign a separate agreement** with Britain.

## SUB 7 | National Symbols Adopted by the Assembly
[1:7:0] - **National Flag** – design adopted **22 July 1947** (resolution moved by Nehru); the wheel = **Ashoka Chakra (Dharmachakra)**, **24 spokes**, the **wheel of law** – stands for **justice** (dharma).
[1:7:1] - **Flag Code of India, 2002:** ratio of length to height **3:2**; standard sizes include 900×600 mm and 450×300 mm – **600×400 mm is not** a standard size.
[1:7:2] - **Jana Gana Mana** (Tagore, originally **Bengali**) – adopted in its Hindi version as the **National Anthem** on **24 January 1950** (Prasad's statement, not a vote); full rendition **52 seconds**; first sung at the Calcutta Congress, 27 Dec 1911.
[1:7:3]   - Its regions include **Sindh**, now in **Pakistan** (Utkala, Dravida, Maratha are in India).
[1:7:4]   - **January 2017:** lakhs of devotees at **Khodaldham, Kagvad (near Rajkot)** set a **Guinness record** for **collective singing of the National Anthem**.
[1:7:5] - **Vande Mataram** (Bankimchandra) – originally in **Sanskrit** (in Bengali script), **not Bengali**; declared **National Song** on 24 Jan 1950, **honoured equally**; first political singing 1896 Congress.
[1:7:6] - **Art. 51A(a)** names the **Flag and Anthem**, not the Song; the **Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Act, 2026** (passed July 2026) gives the Song statutory protection like the Anthem; **Bijoe Emmanuel v State of Kerala (1986)** – standing respectfully without singing is no offence.
[1:7:7] - **Satyameva Jayate** (Mundaka Upanishad) – national **motto**, adopted with the **State Emblem** on **26 January 1950**; inscribed below the emblem on currency notes and national documents.
[1:7:8] - **National Calendar (Saka):** 1 Chaitra = **22 March** (21 March in a leap year).
[1:7:9] - **National flower: Lotus** (*Nelumbo nucifera*).
[1:7:10] > UPSC 2003 NOT-correct statement: "Vande Mataram composed originally in **Bengali**" ✗ (it was Sanskrit).
[1:7:11] - **Language – Munshi-Ayyangar formula (14 Sep 1949):** **Hindi in Devanagari** as official language of the Union, **English continued for 15 years**, **14** other languages in the Eighth Schedule – **not** an immediate end of English.

## SUB 8 | Indian Independence Act, 1947 and the Transition
[1:8:0] # Mountbatten Plan and the Act
[1:8:1] - **3 June 1947 Plan (Mountbatten)** – partition, accepted by Congress and League – the **basis of the Indian Independence Act**.
[1:8:2] - Bill introduced in the Commons **4 July 1947**; passed / **royal assent 18 July 1947**.
[1:8:3] - **Radcliffe** chaired the **Punjab** and **Bengal** Boundary Commissions; award published **17 August 1947**.
[1:8:4] # Provisions
[1:8:5] - Ended **British sovereignty**; two independent **Dominions – India and Pakistan** – from **15 August 1947**.
[1:8:6] - **Governor-General** (common or separate) to act on **Dominion ministers'** advice; British monarch nominal head; **Crown lost its veto**.
[1:8:7] - **Constituent Assembly** = sovereign constitution-maker **and** Dominion legislature; the **Central Legislative Assembly ended**.
[1:8:8] - **Paramountcy lapsed**: about **565** princely states theoretically free; expected to join India or Pakistan.
[1:8:9] - **Instrument of Accession:** ceded **defence, external affairs, communications** (NOT finance); **Standstill Agreement** – Hyderabad, November 1947; **Kashmir** acceded **26 October 1947**; UN ceasefire 1 January 1949.
[1:8:10] - Dominion status ended with the Republic, **26 Jan 1950**.
[1:8:11] # Governors-General and after
[1:8:12] - **Mountbatten**: last Viceroy and **first GG of independent India**; **C. Rajagopalachari**: last and only Indian GG (June 1948 – Jan 1950); he assented to the **Madras Estates (Abolition) Act** on 2 April 1949.
[1:8:13] - **Nehru's "Tryst with Destiny"** ("At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom") – night of **14–15 August 1947**, in the Assembly.
[1:8:14] - After 26 Jan 1950 the Assembly worked as the **Provisional Parliament** till **17 April 1952**; the **first Lok Sabha** was constituted on 17 April 1952 and its **first session began 13 May 1952**.
[1:8:15] - Significance: sovereignty came with partition; Congress accepted dominion status for a quick, peaceful transfer.
