## SUB 1 | Sitting Days and Productivity
[1:0] - **Art. 85(1):** **not more than six months** may pass between the last sitting of one session and the first of the next; **no minimum** number of sitting days. States: **Art. 174**.
[1:1] - Average sittings: **~135 days/year** (**1st Lok Sabha**) → **~55** (**17th**, 2019-24); the Lok Sabha now sits **60-70 days** a year vs **150+** in the **UK House of Commons**.
[1:2] - **17th Lok Sabha:** Question Hour ran only about **60%** of its scheduled time.

  [1:3.2] Oral answers | 2 | 16
[1:4] - **Joint sittings** held only **three** times (1961, 1978, 2002).

## SUB 3 | Weak Scrutiny and Bypass Routes
[3:0] TABLE head: Lok Sabha | Bills referred to committees
  [3:0.0] 14th | 60%
  [3:0.1] 15th | 71% (highest)
  [3:0.2] 16th | 25%
  [3:0.3] 17th | 16%
[3:1] - 17th LS: **58%** of Bills passed within two weeks of introduction; **35%** debated for under an hour.
[3:2] - India: **less than one-fifth** of Bills go to committees; **UK**: every Bill examined by committees. Regular **DRSC** system only from **1993**.
[3:3] - **Delegated legislation:** the legislature delegates **rule-making power to the executive** under a parent Act; its growth (with **ordinances, Art. 123**) is a cause of Parliament's decline.
[3:4] - **Ordinances:** proposals to define "immediate necessity", let courts test urgency per **Krishna Kumar Singh (2017)**, publish reasons.
[3:5] - **Money Bill route (Art. 110):** can **bypass the Rajya Sabha** for laws not strictly within Art. 110 – e.g. the **Aadhaar Act, 2016**.
