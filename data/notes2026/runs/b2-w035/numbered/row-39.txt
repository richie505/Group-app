## SUB 0 | Office of the Governor – Articles 153-161, Term, Removal, Immunity
[0:0] # Articles on the office
[0:1] TABLE head: Article | Subject
  [0:1.0] 153 | A Governor for each State; proviso added by the 7th Amendment, 1956 – the same person may be Governor of two or more States
  [0:1.1] 154 | Executive power of the State vested in the Governor, exercised directly or through subordinate officers
  [0:1.2] 155 | Appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal – neither directly nor indirectly elected
  [0:1.3] 156 | Term – holds office during the pleasure of the President; resigns to the President; normal term 5 years (156(3))
  [0:1.4] 157 | Qualifications – citizen of India (not necessarily by birth) and completed 35 years
  [0:1.5] 158 | Conditions – not a member of Parliament or a State Legislature (seat vacated on appointment), no office of profit; emoluments fixed by Parliament by law, not reduced during the term
  [0:1.6] 159 | Oath before the Chief Justice of the High Court (in his absence, the senior-most judge)
  [0:1.7] 160 | President provides for the Governor's functions in an unforeseen contingency
  [0:1.8] 161 | Pardoning power (offences against State laws)
[0:2] - **Nominal / constitutional head** of the State and its **executive head**; the **Chief Minister and Council of Ministers** are the **real executive**.
[0:3] - **Article 168**: the Governor is an **integral part of the State Legislature**, though not a member.
[0:4] - Emoluments **charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State** (Art. 202(3)); a Governor of two or more States has them shared in the **proportion the President fixes** (Art. 158(3A)).
[0:5] - **Conventions:** usually an **outsider** to the State; the State **CM is consulted** (not compulsory).
[0:6] # Term and removal
[0:7] - Normal term **5 years**, but he **continues until his successor enters office** (Art. 156(3)) – "quits immediately after his term expires" is **wrong**.
[0:8] - The five years are **not secure**: he serves at the President's pleasure (= Union Council of Ministers) and can be **removed or transferred at any time**, and reappointed.
[0:9] - **No procedure or grounds for removal** are laid down in the Constitution; he is **not impeached** and **the State Legislature cannot remove** him (President, Election Commissioner and HC judges are removed by Parliament; the Governor is not).
[0:10] - He is **responsible to the President**; **not answerable** to either House of Parliament, either House of the State Legislature or the State Council of Ministers.
[0:11] - **B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010)**: the pleasure doctrine is **not unfettered** – removal must not be **arbitrary** or merely because the **Government at the Centre changed**; limited judicial review.
[0:12] # Immunity – Article 361
[0:13] - **361(1)**: not answerable to **any court** for acts done in the exercise of his powers and duties.
[0:14] - **361(2)**: **no criminal proceedings** instituted or continued during the term – not even with a court's permission; **361(3)**: no arrest or imprisonment.
[0:15] - **361(4)**: **civil proceedings** for **personal acts** only after **two months' written notice** – so "a Governor cannot be sued for any act" is **false**.
[0:16] - Members of a State Legislature are separately immune for anything said or any vote in the House (**Art. 194(2)**).
[0:17] > UPSC 2013: "No procedure has been laid down in the Constitution for the removal of a Governor" = correct. UPSC 2025: Art. 361(1), 361(2) and 194(2) all correct.

## SUB 3 | Discretionary Powers – Article 163
[3:0] # Article 163
[3:1] - **163(1)**: a Council of Ministers headed by the CM **aids and advises** the Governor **except where he is required to act in his discretion** – the Constitution **explicitly mentions** the Governor's discretion (it gives the President none).
[3:2] - **163(2)**: the Governor's decision on **whether a matter is within his discretion is final** – his discretionary acts cannot be called in question (not complete immunity after case law).
[3:3] - **163(3)**: courts **cannot inquire** into the advice tendered by Ministers to the Governor.
[3:4] - So the Governor is **not bound by advice in all matters**; he can **reserve a Bill even against ministerial advice**. His discretion is **wider than the President's**.
[3:5] - Of four categories of the Governor's orders (executive action, orders on aid and advice, pardon, discretion), the one **not subject to judicial review** is where he **acts in his own discretion** without aid and advice.
[3:6] - The President cannot **suo motu** reserve a State Bill; only the Governor reserves it.
[3:7] # Constitutional vs situational discretion
[3:8] TABLE head: Constitutional (express) | Situational
  [3:8.0] Reserving a Bill for the President (Art. 200) | Appointing a CM in a hung Assembly
  [3:8.1] Report recommending President's Rule (Art. 356) | Dismissing a ministry that lost majority (Art. 164 pleasure clause)
  [3:8.2] Seeking information from the CM (Art. 167) | Dissolving the Assembly when the ministry lost majority
  [3:8.3] Administrator of an adjoining UT (Art. 239(2)) | Asking the Government to prove majority
  [3:8.4] Sixth Schedule royalty share; special responsibilities under Arts. 371-371J | –
[3:9] - **Not discretion**: **appointing Ministers** (on the CM's advice) and **making rules of business** (Art. 166(3)); consulting the High Court is not a Governor's power at all.
[3:10] - **Art. 239(2)**: as administrator of an adjoining UT the Governor acts **independently of his State Council of Ministers**.
[3:11] - Special responsibility for law and order: **371A Nagaland**, **371H Arunachal Pradesh**.
[3:12] - **Fifth Schedule**: Governor **reports to the President** on Scheduled Areas; makes regulations for **peace and good government** after consulting the **Tribes Advisory Council** (not more than **20** members, about three-fourths ST MLAs). **Sixth Schedule**: Autonomous District Councils.
[3:13] - A CM who loses majority **must resign or may be dismissed** – e.g. **West Bengal, May 2026**: Mamata Banerjee refused to resign; the Art. 164 **pleasure clause** was cited.
[3:14] # Hung Assembly – order of invitation (Sarkaria / Punchhi)
[3:15] - 1. **Pre-poll alliance** → 2. largest single party with support → 3. post-poll coalition → 4. post-poll alliance with outside support – then an **early floor test**.
[3:16] - **Tamil Nadu, May 2026**: Governor invited **C. Joseph Vijay** on **120 letters of support** in the 234-member House with an early floor test (Sarkaria-Punchhi order).
[3:17] - **Justice Kurian Joseph Committee (TN, 2026)**: proposes a **new Schedule** codifying the Governor's discretion, appointment from a State panel and removal by Assembly resolution.

## SUB 4 | President's Rule – Article 356
[4:0] # Provisions
[4:1] - Imposed when the government of a State **cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution** – on the **Governor's report** or **otherwise** (suo motu).
[4:2] - Second ground: **Art. 365** – a State **fails to comply with Union directions** (Art. 355 = Centre's duty to ensure States run per the Constitution).
[4:3] - Approval by both Houses within **2 months** (simple majority); **6 months** at a time; **maximum 3 years**.
[4:4] - **Beyond 1 year** only if a **National Emergency** is in force and the **Election Commission certifies** elections cannot be held (**44th Amendment**).
[4:5] - Effects: the President takes over the State executive, administered by the **Governor on his behalf**; the Legislature's powers go to **Parliament** (**Art. 357**) – **Parliament**, not the Union Cabinet, passes State Bills and budget; the Assembly is **suspended or dissolved** (not automatically dissolved); **Art. 19 is not suspended**; the High Court is untouched.
[4:6] - Under President's Rule there is **no State Council of Ministers** to advise the Governor (it exists even after dissolution or the CM's resignation, as caretaker).
[4:7] # Firsts and frequency

  [4:8.0] First imposition | Punjab, 20 June 1951; PEPSU 1953 was second
  [4:8.1] First dismissal of an elected (non-Congress) government | Kerala, 1959
  [4:8.2] Never imposed | Telangana, Chhattisgarh
  [4:8.3] Most often | Manipur; latest spell from 13 Feb 2025, revoked Feb 2026 (Y. Khemchand Singh CM)

[4:9] - **Ayodhya (Dec 1992)**: BJP governments of **UP, MP, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh** dismissed and Assemblies dissolved; **Bihar's was not** (Bihar had a brief spell in 1995).
[4:10] - **Former J&K**: 6 months of **Governor's Rule under Sec. 92** of the J&K Constitution (a provision **no other State had**), then Art. 356 (e.g. Dec 2018); **Art. 360** did not apply and the **right to property** stayed a FR there. [4:11] # Judicial review – S.R. Bommai (1994, 9 judges)
[4:12] - Proclamation **judicially reviewable**; Centre must disclose **relevant material**.
[4:13] - Majority tested on the **floor of the Assembly**, not in Raj Bhavan.
[4:14] - Assembly **not dissolved before Parliament approves**; a dismissed ministry **can be restored**.
[4:15] - Upheld dismissal of MP, Rajasthan, HP governments on **secularism**.

## SUB 5 | Bills – Articles 200 and 201; Assent Cases
[5:0] # Governor's options – Art. 200
[5:1] - 1. **Assent**, 2. **withhold** assent, 3. **return** for reconsideration (**not a Money Bill**), 4. **reserve** for the President – **Art. 200**, not 201.
[5:2] - A returned Bill **re-passed** with or without amendment – the Governor **must assent**.
[5:3] - Constitution fixes **no time limit** ("as soon as possible") for returning a Bill.
[5:4] - **Must reserve**: a Bill that **derogates from the powers of the High Court**. **May reserve**: ultra vires Bills, against DPSP or national interest, compulsory acquisition (Art. 31A).
[5:5] - Reservation of State Bills is an example of **central control over the States**.
[5:6] # Reserved to get validity or protection
[5:7] TABLE head: Article | Bill
  [5:7.0] 288(2) | State tax on water / electricity of an inter-State river authority – has no effect unless reserved and assented
  [5:7.1] 31A / 31C | Compulsory acquisition; DPSP laws – reservation only for protection
  [5:7.2] 254(2) | Concurrent List law repugnant to a Union law – prevails only if reserved and assented
  [5:7.3] 304(b) | Restrictions on inter-State trade – previous sanction of the President before introduction
  [5:7.4] 360 | Money Bills during a Financial Emergency
[5:8] # President's options – Art. 201
[5:9] - Assent, withhold, or (non-Money Bill) direct the Governor to **return** it; the Legislature reconsiders **within 6 months**; the re-passed Bill goes **back to the President**, who is **not bound** to assent.
[5:10] - **No fixed time limit** for the President. A Bill reserved once can be reserved again only if **materially altered**.
