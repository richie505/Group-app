## SUB 1 | Public Accounts Committee – Functions, Chairman, Reports
[1:0] - **Oldest parliamentary committee:** PAC, first set up in **1921** under the **Government of India Act, 1919**.
[1:1] - **Function:** examines the **Appropriation Accounts, Finance Accounts** and the **CAG's audit reports** – the financial accounts of the Government; checks that money was spent "**within the scope of the demand**"; looks at waste, loss and nugatory expenditure.
[1:2] - **"Watchdog"** on departmental expenditure and irregularities; the **CAG** is its "guide, friend and philosopher" and assists it.
[1:3] - **Limits:** a **post-mortem** check; not concerned with **policy**; recommendations **advisory**; cannot appoint officials or issue orders.
[1:4] - **Chairman:** **appointed (nominated) by the Speaker** from its members; since **1967** an **Opposition** member by **convention** – not a rule of procedure, law or Presidential order.
[1:5] - **Reports:** submitted to the **Speaker / Lok Sabha** (i.e. to Parliament; a copy laid in the RS) – not the President, PM, FM or CAG.

## SUB 2 | Estimates Committee and Committee on Public Undertakings
[2:0] # Estimates Committee
[2:1] - **Origin:** traces to the Standing Financial Committee (1921); first post-independence Estimates Committee **1950** on the recommendation of **John Mathai** (then FM); 25 members, raised to **30 in 1956**.
[2:2] - **Function (Rule 310):** report what **economies, improvements in organisation, efficiency or administrative reform** consistent with the policy underlying the estimates may be effected; suggest **alternative policies** for efficiency and economy in administration; suggest the form of the estimates → a **"continuous economy committee"**.
[2:3] - **⚠ Key:** "Estimates Committee – which reform to ensure efficiency?" → **administrative reform** (the words of the rule), not new legislation, defence, electoral or judicial reform.
[2:4] - **Limits:** examines estimates only after they are voted; cannot question policy laid down by Parliament; no CAG assistance; advisory.
[2:5] - **Chairmen asked:** **Ummareddy Venkateswarlu** (1999-2004, 13th LS); **Sanjay Jaiswal** (18th LS, as of 2026).
[2:6] # Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)
[2:7] - Created **1964** on the recommendation of the **Krishna Menon Committee**; 15 (10 LS + 5 RS) raised to **22 (15 + 7) in 1974**.
[2:8] - Examines reports and accounts of public undertakings and the CAG's reports on them; whether they run on sound business principles; not major policy or day-to-day administration.

## SUB 3 | Other Standing Committees – Petitions, Subordinate Legislation, Assurances, Offices of Profit
[3:0] # Six groups of standing committees (Laxmikanth)
[3:1] - **Financial** (PAC, Estimates, COPU); **24 DRSCs**; **to inquire** (Petitions, Privileges, Ethics); **to scrutinise and control** (Government Assurances, Subordinate Legislation, Papers Laid, **Welfare of SCs and STs**, Empowerment of Women, Offices of Profit); **day-to-day business** (Business Advisory, Private Members' Bills, Rules, Absence of Members); **house-keeping** (General Purposes, House, Library, Salaries of MPs).
[3:2] - **Welfare of SCs and STs** is itself a standing committee (30 = 20 LS + 10 RS; **Ministers cannot serve**; examines the reports of the **National Commissions for SCs and STs** and welfare programmes) – it is the **only one of PAC / Estimates / COPU / SC-ST that is not a financial committee**.
[3:3] - **No parliamentary committee on Welfare of Minorities** (there are committees on Public Undertakings, Government Assurances, Estimates; OBC welfare is an LS committee).

## SUB 4 | Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs)
[4:0] - **Set up 1993** (17) on the recommendation of the **Rules Committee** of the Lok Sabha; **24 since 2004** = **16 under the Lok Sabha + 8 under the Rajya Sabha**.
[4:1] - **Each: 31 members = 21 LS + 10 RS** → standing committees draw LS and RS members in the ratio **2 : 1**; **quorum 11**; **Ministers ineligible**; term one year.
[4:2] - **Functions:** examine **Demands for Grants** (between general discussion and voting), **Bills referred**, **annual reports** and long-term policy documents of their ministries; no cut-motion-type suggestions.
[4:3] - **Regulators:** the **DRSCs** and **ad hoc committees** (e.g. the 2G JPC) may review independent regulators (telecom, insurance, electricity) – not the Finance Commission, FSLRC or NITI Aayog (UPSC 2019 → 1 and 2).
[4:4] - **Chairs asked (APPSC G1 2004):** Railways – **Basudeb Acharia**, Defence – **Balasaheb Vikhe Patil** (14th LS) and External Affairs – Laxminarayan Pandey are given as correct; the Committee on **Food – A. Narendra** pairing is the **wrong** one (printed key; chair lists not independently verified).

## SUB 5 | Ad Hoc Committees, JPCs and the Role of Committees
[5:0] - **Role:** Parliament exercises control over the **administration** mainly **through parliamentary committees** – detailed scrutiny the whole House has no time for (not via ministries' advisory committees or writs).
[5:1] - **Ad hoc committees:** temporary, end with the report – (i) **Inquiry committees** (e.g. on the conduct of members during the President's address, Railway Convention Committee, MPLADS); (ii) **Advisory committees** – **Select / Joint Committees on Bills**, Committee on the **Draft Five Year Plan** → "all the above".
[5:2] - A Bill goes to a Select Committee (one House) or Joint Committee (both Houses) by a **motion adopted in the House**.
