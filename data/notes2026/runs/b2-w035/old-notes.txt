[35:0:5] - **⚠ Trap:** "Estimates = 20 LS + 10 RS" is **false** (that is the SC/ST Welfare or Women's Empowerment committee).
[35:0:6] - **Budget** = control **before** spending; financial committees = scrutiny **after** – they support, not replace, the budgetary process.

## SUB 1 | Public Accounts Committee – Functions, Chairman, Reports
[35:1:0] - **Oldest parliamentary committee:** PAC, first set up in **1921** under the **Government of India Act, 1919**.
[35:1:1] - **Function:** examines the **Appropriation Accounts, Finance Accounts** and the **CAG's audit reports** – the financial accounts of the Government; checks that money was spent "**within the scope of the demand**"; looks at waste, loss and nugatory expenditure.
[35:1:2] - **"Watchdog"** on departmental expenditure and irregularities; the **CAG** is its "guide, friend and philosopher" and assists it.
[35:1:3] - **Limits:** a **post-mortem** check; not concerned with **policy**; recommendations **advisory**; cannot appoint officials or issue orders.
[35:1:4] - **Chairman:** **appointed (nominated) by the Speaker** from its members; since **1967** an **Opposition** member by **convention** – not a rule of procedure, law or Presidential order.
[35:1:5] - **Reports:** submitted to the **Speaker / Lok Sabha** (i.e. to Parliament; a copy laid in the RS) – not the President, PM, FM or CAG.

[35:1:8] > Exam angle: "who nominates PAC chairman" (asked ×6) → Speaker; "PAC submits report to" → Speaker/Lok Sabha; "Opposition chair – why?" → convention.

[35:1:10] - **Became a parliamentary committee:** after the Constitution, the PAC became a committee of Parliament under the Speaker in **1950** (set up 1921).
## SUB 2 | Other Standing Committees – Petitions, Subordinate Legislation, Assurances, Offices of Profit
[35:2:0] # Six groups of standing committees (Laxmikanth)
[35:2:1] - **Financial** (PAC, Estimates, COPU); **24 DRSCs**; **to inquire** (Petitions, Privileges, Ethics); **to scrutinise and control** (Government Assurances, Subordinate Legislation, Papers Laid, **Welfare of SCs and STs**, Empowerment of Women, Offices of Profit); **day-to-day business** (Business Advisory, Private Members' Bills, Rules, Absence of Members); **house-keeping** (General Purposes, House, Library, Salaries of MPs).
[35:2:2] - **Welfare of SCs and STs** is itself a standing committee (30 = 20 LS + 10 RS; **Ministers cannot serve**; examines the reports of the **National Commissions for SCs and STs** and welfare programmes) – it is the **only one of PAC / Estimates / COPU / SC-ST that is not a financial committee**.
[35:2:3] - **No parliamentary committee on Welfare of Minorities** (there are committees on Public Undertakings, Government Assurances, Estimates; OBC welfare is an LS committee).
[35:2:4] TABLE head: Committee | Function | Strength / note
  [35:2:4.0] Petitions | Petitions on Bills and public-interest matters; representations of citizens – the "Ombudsman" of Parliament | LS 15, RS 10
  [35:2:4.1] Subordinate Legislation | Whether rules, regulations, sub-rules, bye-laws made under powers delegated by Parliament or conferred by the Constitution are properly exercised by the Executive | 15 each House (1953)
  [35:2:4.2] Government Assurances | Tracks ministers' assurances till implemented | LS 15, RS 10 (1953)
  [35:2:4.3] Joint Committee on Offices of Profit | Which offices should disqualify MPs | 15 = 10 LS + 5 RS
  [35:2:4.4] Privileges | Breach of privilege | LS 15, RS 10
  [35:2:4.5] Ethics | Members' conduct | RS 4 Mar 1997, LS 16 May 2000
  [35:2:4.6] Business Advisory | Allots time for Government and other business | LS 15, Speaker chairs
[35:2:5] - **Delegated legislation** = rule-making power **delegated by the legislature to the executive**; watched by the Committee on Subordinate Legislation.
[35:2:6] - **Lok Sabha-only:** Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions; Committee on Absence of Members.
[35:2:7] - **Watchdog group** (NextGen): Subordinate Legislation, Government Assurances, Estimates, PAC, COPU, DRSCs – **not** the Business Advisory Committee.

[35:2:9] - **Committee on Empowerment of Women:** set up for both Houses on **29 April 1997**; **30 = 20 LS + 10 RS**.
[35:2:10] - **Committee on Papers Laid on the Table:** checks that papers laid by Ministers (other than statutory orders, which go to Subordinate Legislation) comply with the Constitution, the Act or rule concerned.
[35:2:11] - **Speaker as ex-officio chairperson:** Business Advisory Committee, **Rules Committee** and General Purposes Committee (not PAC, Estimates or COPU, whose chairs he appoints).
[35:2:12] - **Ethics Committees:** idea mooted in the Rajya Sabha in **1992**; **RS Ethics Committee constituted 4 March 1997** (functioning from May 1997); **Lok Sabha** Ethics Committee first set up as an **ad hoc** committee on **16 May 2000**, made a **permanent** part of the LS Rules in **2015**.
[35:2:13] - **Ministers barred:** Ministers cannot be members of the Committee on Petitions, the Committee on Subordinate Legislation or the Welfare of SCs and STs Committee (nor of the financial committees).
## SUB 3 | Estimates Committee and Committee on Public Undertakings
[35:3:0] # Estimates Committee
[35:3:1] - **Origin:** traces to the Standing Financial Committee (1921); first post-independence Estimates Committee **1950** on the recommendation of **John Mathai** (then FM); 25 members, raised to **30 in 1956**.
[35:3:2] - **Function (Rule 310):** report what **economies, improvements in organisation, efficiency or administrative reform** consistent with the policy underlying the estimates may be effected; suggest **alternative policies** for efficiency and economy in administration; suggest the form of the estimates → a **"continuous economy committee"**.
[35:3:3] - **⚠ Key:** "Estimates Committee – which reform to ensure efficiency?" → **administrative reform** (the words of the rule), not new legislation, defence, electoral or judicial reform.
[35:3:4] - **Limits:** examines estimates only after they are voted; cannot question policy laid down by Parliament; no CAG assistance; advisory.
[35:3:5] - **Chairmen asked:** **Ummareddy Venkateswarlu** (1999-2004, 13th LS); **Sanjay Jaiswal** (18th LS, as of 2026).
[35:3:6] # Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)
[35:3:7] - Created **1964** on the recommendation of the **Krishna Menon Committee**; 15 (10 LS + 5 RS) raised to **22 (15 + 7) in 1974**.
[35:3:8] - Examines reports and accounts of public undertakings and the CAG's reports on them; whether they run on sound business principles; not major policy or day-to-day administration.
[35:3:9] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth, Indian Polity 8th ed. p.398-400 (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt); NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.366 (src/polity/nextgen-polity-full.txt); Lok Sabha Rules of Procedure Rule 310; Wikipedia, Estimates Committee (list of chairpersons, citing loksabha.nic.in)
[35:3:10] - **"Continuous economy committee":** the **Estimates Committee** (it suggests economies in expenditure year after year).
[35:3:11] - **Chairs 2024-25 (18th LS):** PAC – **K.C. Venugopal**; Estimates – **Sanjay Jaiswal**; COPU – **Baijayant Panda** (so "Estimates – Baijayant Panda" is wrong).
## SUB 4 | Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs)
[35:4:0] - **Set up 1993** (17) on the recommendation of the **Rules Committee** of the Lok Sabha; **24 since 2004** = **16 under the Lok Sabha + 8 under the Rajya Sabha**.
[35:4:1] - **Each: 31 members = 21 LS + 10 RS** → standing committees draw LS and RS members in the ratio **2 : 1**; **quorum 11**; **Ministers ineligible**; term one year.
[35:4:2] - **Functions:** examine **Demands for Grants** (between general discussion and voting), **Bills referred**, **annual reports** and long-term policy documents of their ministries; no cut-motion-type suggestions.
[35:4:3] - **Regulators:** the **DRSCs** and **ad hoc committees** (e.g. the 2G JPC) may review independent regulators (telecom, insurance, electricity) – not the Finance Commission, FSLRC or NITI Aayog (UPSC 2019 → 1 and 2).
[35:4:4] - **Chairs asked (APPSC G1 2004):** Railways – **Basudeb Acharia**, Defence – **Balasaheb Vikhe Patil** (14th LS) and External Affairs – Laxminarayan Pandey are given as correct; the Committee on **Food – A. Narendra** pairing is the **wrong** one (printed key; chair lists not independently verified).
[35:4:5] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth, Indian Polity 8th ed. p.400-405 (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt); PRS India, UPSC 2019 answer explanation
[35:4:6] - **DRSC chairpersons:** appointed by the **Speaker** for the 16 Lok Sabha DRSCs and by the **Chairman, Rajya Sabha** for the 8 RS DRSCs.
## SUB 5 | Ad Hoc Committees, JPCs and the Role of Committees
[35:5:0] - **Role:** Parliament exercises control over the **administration** mainly **through parliamentary committees** – detailed scrutiny the whole House has no time for (not via ministries' advisory committees or writs).
[35:5:1] - **Ad hoc committees:** temporary, end with the report – (i) **Inquiry committees** (e.g. on the conduct of members during the President's address, Railway Convention Committee, MPLADS); (ii) **Advisory committees** – **Select / Joint Committees on Bills**, Committee on the **Draft Five Year Plan** → "all the above".
[35:5:2] - A Bill goes to a Select Committee (one House) or Joint Committee (both Houses) by a **motion adopted in the House**.

  [35:5:3.0] Bofors | 1987 | B. Shankaranand

[35:5:4] - **Currently before JPCs (2026):** Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024 (One Nation One Election); FCRA (Amendment) Bill, 2026; **Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhisthan Bill, 2025** (a JPC, not a single-House select committee).
[35:5:5] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth, Indian Polity 8th ed. p.395-396 (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt); NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.365 (src/polity/nextgen-polity-full.txt); app notes 2:35 (JPCs)
[35:5:6] - **Consultative Committees:** constituted by the **Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs** (for each ministry, and informal ones for Railway Zones); MPs of both Houses, chaired by the Minister – they are **not** parliamentary committees in the strict sense.
[35:5:7] - **2G spectrum JPC (2011):** **30 members = 20 LS + 10 RS**, chaired by **P.C. Chacko** (the 15 + 7 split is the PAC / COPU).

[36:0:0] # The 52nd Amendment, 1985
[36:0:1] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [36:0:1.0] Law | 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985 – added the Tenth Schedule (anti-defection law); amended Arts 101, 102, 190 and 191
  [36:0:1.1] Bill | Passed by Parliament 30-31 January 1985; Presidential assent 15 February 1985 (the date most keys give as "Bill passed")
  [36:0:1.2] In force | 1 March 1985
  [36:0:1.3] Aim | Curb defections "induced by the lure of office", ensure party discipline and stable governments; ban floor crossing by members elected on a party ticket
  [36:0:1.4] First | First constitutional recognition of political parties (1985) – the term "political party" appears in the Tenth Schedule
[36:0:2] - **Asked as:** "52nd Amendment relates to" → **Defection**; "Tenth Schedule deals with" → **anti-defection / defections** (not official languages = Part XVII/8th Sch., not land reforms = 9th, not Panchayati Raj = 11th); "anti-defection enacted in" → **1985**; "which amendment first introduced anti-defection" → **52nd** (not 44th, 54th, 61st, 86th, 92nd, nor the Representation of the People Act).
[36:0:3] - **Applies to:** members of **both Houses of Parliament and the State Legislatures** (MPs and MLAs, incl. **MLCs**) – party, **independent** and **nominated** members.
[36:0:4] - **Not applicable to the presidential election:** the Schedule covers voting **in the House**; no binding whip operates in the electoral college.

## SUB 1 | Grounds of Disqualification, Merger and Split
[36:1:0] TABLE head: Member | Disqualified if | Exception / allowed
  [36:1:0.0] Party member | Voluntarily gives up party membership (inferred from conduct); votes or abstains against the whip without prior permission (Para 2(1)(b)) | Prior permission, or the party condones within 15 days
  [36:1:0.1] Independent | Joins any political party after election | May become a Minister without joining a party
  [36:1:0.2] Nominated | Joins a party after six months of taking his seat | May join within six months; not joining is no ground
  [36:1:0.3] Presiding officer | Joins another party (Para 5) | May give up party membership on election to the Chair and rejoin after demitting office – both are exceptions
[36:1:1] - **Giving up membership ≠ resigning:** conduct such as campaigning for a rival party is enough (Ravi S. Naik 1994).
[36:1:2] - **Expelled member:** does **not lose his seat**; he still belongs to the party for Tenth Schedule purposes (G. Viswanathan 1996) and sits as unattached; joining another party then = giving up membership.
[36:1:3] - **Merger (Para 4, in force):** no disqualification when the **original party merges** with another and **not less than two-thirds** of its legislature party agree.
[36:1:4] - **Split (Para 3, one-third):** **deleted by the 91st Amendment (2003)** – a one-third group that breaks away is now disqualified.
[36:1:5]   - Number test: party of **100** MLAs – no break-away faction is protected now; a merger needs **67**. **34** (one-third) was the pre-2003 answer.
[36:1:6] - **Statements commonly marked wrong:** "one-third split protects" ✗; "disqualified defector can become a Minister" ✗; "nominated member who fails to join a party in six months is disqualified" ✗; "expelled member loses his seat" ✗; "Speaker not disqualified if he joins another party" ✗.
[36:1:7] - **Criticism – en-bloc defections:** it "banned only **retail** defections and legalised **wholesale** defections" – **en-bloc (bulk) defection** via merger is not covered; individual defection, defections in spells and votes against the whip are.
[36:1:8] - **Main criticism:** it stifles intra-party **dissent** and members' free expression, yet has not stopped mass defections (the two-thirds merger route can **defend mass defection**).
[36:1:9] > Exam angle: "merger fraction" = **two-thirds** (asked ×3); "not covered by the law" = **en-bloc defections**; UPSC 2022 – nominated member six-month statement ✗, no time-frame ✓.

## SUB 4 | Working of the Law, AP Angle and Reform Proposals
[36:4:0] - **Record:** the law has not stopped defections; its main effect has been to make **presiding officers** the deciders of defection cases (often criticised for partisan delay) – APPSC ASO 2017 key.
[36:4:1] - **Resort politics:** legislators sequestered before a floor test to prevent poaching.
[36:4:2] - **Resignation route:** MLAs **resign**, lowering the majority mark, and re-contest on the new party's ticket (**Karnataka 2019, MP 2020**).
[36:4:3] - **AP 2014-19:** **23 YSRCP MLAs** moved to the TDP; the petitions were **never decided**.
[36:4:4] - **June 2019:** **4 of 6 TDP** Rajya Sabha MPs "merged" with the BJP under **Para 4**; the RS Chairman accepted it.
[36:4:5] - **Apr 2026 (app notes):** **7 of 10 AAP** Rajya Sabha MPs claimed a merger with the BJP – cited as misuse of the merger exception.
[36:4:6] TABLE head: Body | Recommendation
  [36:4:6.0] Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990) | Disqualify only for voluntarily giving up membership or defying the whip on a confidence motion; decision by President/Governor on EC advice
  [36:4:6.1] Law Commission 170th Report (1999) | Delete both split and merger exceptions; treat pre-poll alliances as parties
  [36:4:6.2] NCRWC (2002) | Defectors barred from office; defector's vote invalid
  [36:4:6.3] Election Commission | President/Governor to decide on the EC's binding advice
  [36:4:6.4] Manish Tewari Bill (2010) | Limit the law to confidence and money votes
[36:4:7] - Chronology of proposals: **Goswami (1990) → Law Commission 170th (1999) → NCRWC (2002) → Tewari Bill (2010)**.
[36:4:8] - Limiting the whip to votes that threaten the government lets members **dissent freely** on other matters.

## SUB 1 | Parliamentary System – Meaning and Why India Is Parliamentary
[38:1:0] - **Names:** **cabinet government**, **responsible government**, Westminster model – the executive stays in power only while it has a **majority (confidence) in the popular House**.
[38:1:1] - **Defining test:** India is parliamentary because the **Council of Ministers is responsible to the Lok Sabha** (Art. 75(3); States Art. 164(2)) – not because the Lok Sabha is directly elected, Parliament can amend the Constitution or the Rajya Sabha cannot be dissolved (UPSC 2015).
[38:1:2] - **Parliamentary executive** = an executive **dependent on the support of a majority in Parliament** – not "elected by Parliament", not "Parliament acting as executive".
[38:1:3] - **Main principle:** **responsibility of the executive to the legislature**; the legislature controls the executive. "Legislature and Executive are independent" is the **presidential** feature.
[38:1:4] - **Removal:** the government is **responsible to Parliament and can be removed by it** (no-confidence) – not "all parties represented", not "elected by the people", not "fixed term" (UPSC 2020).
[38:1:5] - **Basis:** **fusion of powers** – co-operation of legislature and executive; presidential = separation of powers.
[38:1:6] - **Source of authority:** the Constitution sets up the parliamentary system at the **Centre and in the States**; both governments draw authority from the **Constitution**.
[38:1:7] - India = **secular, federal, parliamentary** – "presidential system" ✗; "government by experts" ✗ (ministers are amateurs). India is parliamentary, cabinet and responsible government.
[38:1:8] - **Statements:** "President **represents the nation but does not rule** it" ✓; "executive independent of and not accountable to the legislature" ✗.
[38:1:9] > Exam angle: "There is parliamentary government in India because…" = **CoM responsible to the Lok Sabha** (UPSC 2015 ×2); A-R UPPSC 2025: both true, R explains A.
[38:1:10] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth, Indian Polity 8th ed. ch.13 p.206-209 (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt); app notes 2:38 (sub 0)
## SUB 2 | Presidential System – Features and Contrast
[38:2:0] - President = **Head of State and Head of Government** – **single executive**; all executive power vested in him.
[38:2:1] - **Elected** for a **fixed term** (US: electoral college, 4 years); removable only by **impeachment**; **not accountable** to the legislature; **cannot dissolve** the legislature.
[38:2:2] - **Appoints his own Council of Ministers** (secretaries), who are not members of the legislature and are responsible only to him.
[38:2:3] - **Principle:** **separation of powers** with **checks and balances** – President and legislature check each other (veto, Senate confirmation, impeachment). "Fusion of powers" = parliamentary; "division of powers" = federalism.
[38:2:4] - **Origin:** first formed in the **USA** (oldest and purest form); USA has a presidential form – India, Canada and Britain are parliamentary.
[38:2:5] - APPSC G2 key: head of State ✓, head of Government ✓, directly elected ✓, "**accountable to the legislature**" ✗ → "i, ii and iii".
[38:2:6] TABLE head: Parliamentary | Presidential
  [38:2:6.0] Nominal head + real head (PM) – dual executive | One person is both heads – single executive
  [38:2:6.1] Collective executive (cabinet); ministers are legislators | Secretaries outside the legislature
  [38:2:6.2] Fusion of powers | Separation of powers
  [38:2:6.3] Removable by no-confidence | Fixed tenure; only impeachment; fixed term
  [38:2:6.4] Lower House can be dissolved early | No dissolution
[38:2:7] - **Semi-presidential:** directly elected President with real powers + **PM responsible to the legislature** – **France (Fifth Republic, 1958), Russia, Sri Lanka (1978 Constitution)**; **not India** (President indirectly elected, nominal).
[38:2:8] - **Parliamentary examples:** UK, Japan, Canada, India.

## SUB 4 | Merits, Demerits and the Constituent Assembly's Choice
[38:4:0] TABLE head: System | Merits | Demerits
  [38:4:0.0] Parliamentary | Responsible government – executive continuously accountable; harmony of legislature and executive; prevents despotism; ready alternative government | Unstable government; no continuity of policy; cabinet dictatorship with a large majority; "government by amateurs"; against separation of powers
  [38:4:0.1] Presidential | Stable, fixed-term executive; continuity of policy; experts as secretaries | Executive-legislature deadlock; personality cult; irresponsible executive
[38:4:1] - **Main advantage** of the parliamentary form: the **executive remains responsible to the legislature** (UPSC 2017) – "continuity of policy" is the presidential merit.
[38:4:2] - **Why the Constituent Assembly chose it:** **familiarity** from the **1919 and 1935 Acts**; vast size and diversity; avoid US-style deadlock.
[38:4:3] - **Dr B.R. Ambedkar:** a democratic executive must satisfy **stability and responsibility**, which cannot both be maximised; the Draft preferred **more responsibility** to more stability. The parliamentary system gives a "**daily as well as a periodic assessment**" of the Government (CAD, 4 Nov 1948).
[38:4:4] TABLE head: Person / body | Contribution
  [38:4:4.0] Vasant Sathe | Proposed a presidential form, 1970s-80s
  [38:4:4.1] NCRWC (Venkatachaliah, 2000-02) | Advised keeping the parliamentary system
[38:4:5] - **One Nation, One Election:** amendments to Arts **83, 172, 327**; critics say fixed tenures graft presidential mechanics onto the system.
[38:4:6] - **Shamsher Singh (1974):** a Governor publicly criticising Cabinet policy runs "**counter to the parliamentary system**".

[38:4:8] - **Oligarchy** = rule by a **few** under a group's control ("minority system of governance"); **gerontocracy** = rule by the old; **autocracy** = rule by one; **anarchy** = no government.

## SUB 0 | Office of the Governor – Articles 153-161, Term, Removal, Immunity
[39:0:0] # Articles on the office
[39:0:1] TABLE head: Article | Subject
  [39:0:1.0] 153 | A Governor for each State; proviso added by the 7th Amendment, 1956 – the same person may be Governor of two or more States
  [39:0:1.1] 154 | Executive power of the State vested in the Governor, exercised directly or through subordinate officers
  [39:0:1.2] 155 | Appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal – neither directly nor indirectly elected
  [39:0:1.3] 156 | Term – holds office during the pleasure of the President; resigns to the President; normal term 5 years (156(3))
  [39:0:1.4] 157 | Qualifications – citizen of India (not necessarily by birth) and completed 35 years
  [39:0:1.5] 158 | Conditions – not a member of Parliament or a State Legislature (seat vacated on appointment), no office of profit; emoluments fixed by Parliament by law, not reduced during the term
  [39:0:1.6] 159 | Oath before the Chief Justice of the High Court (in his absence, the senior-most judge)
  [39:0:1.7] 160 | President provides for the Governor's functions in an unforeseen contingency
  [39:0:1.8] 161 | Pardoning power (offences against State laws)
[39:0:2] - **Nominal / constitutional head** of the State and its **executive head**; the **Chief Minister and Council of Ministers** are the **real executive**.
[39:0:3] - **Article 168**: the Governor is an **integral part of the State Legislature**, though not a member.
[39:0:4] - Emoluments **charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State** (Art. 202(3)); a Governor of two or more States has them shared in the **proportion the President fixes** (Art. 158(3A)).
[39:0:5] - **Conventions:** usually an **outsider** to the State; the State **CM is consulted** (not compulsory).
[39:0:6] # Term and removal
[39:0:7] - Normal term **5 years**, but he **continues until his successor enters office** (Art. 156(3)) – "quits immediately after his term expires" is **wrong**.
[39:0:8] - The five years are **not secure**: he serves at the President's pleasure (= Union Council of Ministers) and can be **removed or transferred at any time**, and reappointed.
[39:0:9] - **No procedure or grounds for removal** are laid down in the Constitution; he is **not impeached** and **the State Legislature cannot remove** him (President, Election Commissioner and HC judges are removed by Parliament; the Governor is not).
[39:0:10] - He is **responsible to the President**; **not answerable** to either House of Parliament, either House of the State Legislature or the State Council of Ministers.
[39:0:11] - **B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010)**: the pleasure doctrine is **not unfettered** – removal must not be **arbitrary** or merely because the **Government at the Centre changed**; limited judicial review.
[39:0:12] # Immunity – Article 361
[39:0:13] - **361(1)**: not answerable to **any court** for acts done in the exercise of his powers and duties.
[39:0:14] - **361(2)**: **no criminal proceedings** instituted or continued during the term – not even with a court's permission; **361(3)**: no arrest or imprisonment.
[39:0:15] - **361(4)**: **civil proceedings** for **personal acts** only after **two months' written notice** – so "a Governor cannot be sued for any act" is **false**.
[39:0:16] - Members of a State Legislature are separately immune for anything said or any vote in the House (**Art. 194(2)**).
[39:0:17] > UPSC 2013: "No procedure has been laid down in the Constitution for the removal of a Governor" = correct. UPSC 2025: Art. 361(1), 361(2) and 194(2) all correct.

## SUB 3 | Discretionary Powers – Article 163
[39:3:0] # Article 163
[39:3:1] - **163(1)**: a Council of Ministers headed by the CM **aids and advises** the Governor **except where he is required to act in his discretion** – the Constitution **explicitly mentions** the Governor's discretion (it gives the President none).
[39:3:2] - **163(2)**: the Governor's decision on **whether a matter is within his discretion is final** – his discretionary acts cannot be called in question (not complete immunity after case law).
[39:3:3] - **163(3)**: courts **cannot inquire** into the advice tendered by Ministers to the Governor.
[39:3:4] - So the Governor is **not bound by advice in all matters**; he can **reserve a Bill even against ministerial advice**. His discretion is **wider than the President's**.
[39:3:5] - Of four categories of the Governor's orders (executive action, orders on aid and advice, pardon, discretion), the one **not subject to judicial review** is where he **acts in his own discretion** without aid and advice.
[39:3:6] - The President cannot **suo motu** reserve a State Bill; only the Governor reserves it.

## SUB 4 | President's Rule – Article 356
[39:4:0] # Provisions
[39:4:1] - Imposed when the government of a State **cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution** – on the **Governor's report** or **otherwise** (suo motu).
[39:4:2] - Second ground: **Art. 365** – a State **fails to comply with Union directions** (Art. 355 = Centre's duty to ensure States run per the Constitution).
[39:4:3] - Approval by both Houses within **2 months** (simple majority); **6 months** at a time; **maximum 3 years**.
[39:4:4] - **Beyond 1 year** only if a **National Emergency** is in force and the **Election Commission certifies** elections cannot be held (**44th Amendment**).
[39:4:5] - Effects: the President takes over the State executive, administered by the **Governor on his behalf**; the Legislature's powers go to **Parliament** (**Art. 357**) – **Parliament**, not the Union Cabinet, passes State Bills and budget; the Assembly is **suspended or dissolved** (not automatically dissolved); **Art. 19 is not suspended**; the High Court is untouched.
[39:4:6] - Under President's Rule there is **no State Council of Ministers** to advise the Governor (it exists even after dissolution or the CM's resignation, as caretaker).

## SUB 5 | Bills – Articles 200 and 201; Assent Cases
[39:5:0] # Governor's options – Art. 200
[39:5:1] - 1. **Assent**, 2. **withhold** assent, 3. **return** for reconsideration (**not a Money Bill**), 4. **reserve** for the President – **Art. 200**, not 201.
[39:5:2] - A returned Bill **re-passed** with or without amendment – the Governor **must assent**.
[39:5:3] - Constitution fixes **no time limit** ("as soon as possible") for returning a Bill.
[39:5:4] - **Must reserve**: a Bill that **derogates from the powers of the High Court**. **May reserve**: ultra vires Bills, against DPSP or national interest, compulsory acquisition (Art. 31A).
[39:5:5] - Reservation of State Bills is an example of **central control over the States**.

## SUB 2 | Appointment of the CM – Article 164 and the Six-Month Rule
[40:2:0] # Who appoints – Article 164(1)
[40:2:1] - **Appointed by:** the **Governor** under **Article 164(1)** (not Art. 163, 165 or 166; not the President, a High Court judge or the Attorney General). Other Ministers are appointed by the Governor **on the CM's advice**.
[40:2:2]   - Exception: in **Delhi (Art. 239AA)** and **Puducherry (Art. 239A / GUT Act 1963)** the **President** appoints the CM.
[40:2:3] - **Constitutional (nominal) executive head** of the State = the **Governor**; the **real executive** = the CM and the Council of Ministers (not the Chief Secretary or a Deputy CM).
[40:2:4] - **Hung Assembly:** the Constitution lays down **no criteria**; the Governor uses discretion. The principal consideration = the person **most likely to command a stable majority** (not simply the largest party), followed by an early **floor test**.
[40:2:5]   - **Sarkaria (1988) / Punchhi (2010) order:** 1. pre-poll alliance → 2. single largest party with support of others → 3. post-poll coalition (all partners join) → 4. post-poll alliance with some outside support.
[40:2:6] # Article 164 – clause by clause
[40:2:7] TABLE head: Clause | Provision
  [40:2:7.0] 164(1) | Governor appoints the CM; other Ministers on the CM's advice; Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the Governor; Tribal Welfare Minister proviso
  [40:2:7.1] 164(1A) | Size: maximum 15% of the Assembly, minimum 12 (91st Amendment, 2003)
  [40:2:7.2] 164(2) | Council collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly (not the Council or the Governor)
  [40:2:7.3] 164(3) | Oath of office and secrecy administered by the Governor
  [40:2:7.4] 164(4) | A non-member may be a Minister for six consecutive months; then he ceases to be a Minister
  [40:2:7.5] 164(5) | Salaries and allowances = as the State Legislature by law determines (until then, Second Schedule) – not the CM, Governor or Chief Secretary
[40:2:8] - **Six months, not 3 months, 1 year or 3 years:** a Minister (incl. the CM) can continue without being elected to the State Legislature for **six months** (Art. 164(4)).
[40:2:9] - **Case – P. Shanmugam (Puducherry):** a non-member CM, resigned in **October 2001** after failing to get elected within six months; N. Rangasamy succeeded him (27 Oct 2001). Jayalalithaa (Sept 2001) went after the Supreme Court annulled her appointment – a different ground.
[40:2:10] - **Term:** **not fixed** by the Constitution; the CM stays while he has the **Assembly's confidence** (on losing it – resign or be dismissed). Resignation is addressed to the **Governor**.

## SUB 4 | Position and Powers of the CM; Duties under Article 167
[40:4:0] # Position
[40:4:1] - CM = **real executive head** and head of the Government; State counterpart of the **PM (Arts. 74-75)**; the State has no counterpart of the Vice-President.
[40:4:2] - **Art. 163(1):** Council of Ministers headed by the CM aids and advises the Governor, **except in his discretionary functions**.
[40:4:3] - Council of Ministers: Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers; collectively answerable to the Assembly.
[40:4:4] # Powers
[40:4:5] - Recommends names of Ministers, **allocates portfolios**, can ask a Minister to resign.
[40:4:6] - Advises the Governor on **summoning and prorogation**; can recommend **dissolution** of the Assembly.
[40:4:7] # Article 167 – duties of the CM
[40:4:8] - **Article 167** defines the CM's duties towards the Governor (not Arts. 162, 164 or 165):
[40:4:9]   - 1. **Communicates** all Council decisions on administration and **proposals for legislation**.
[40:4:10]   - 2. **Furnishes information** the Governor calls for (a constitutional discretion of the Governor).
[40:4:11]   - 3. If the Governor requires, **submits for the Council's consideration** a matter decided by a single Minister.
[40:4:12] - CM = the **chief link** between the Governor and the Council of Ministers.
[40:4:13] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.298-299; Laxmikanth Indian Polity 8th ed. ch.31 (src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-02.txt p.17-21)
[40:4:14] - **Cabinet meetings:** **presided over by the CM** (the Governor does not attend).
[40:4:15] - **Council survives** the dissolution of the Assembly and the CM's resignation (as caretaker until a successor), but under **President's Rule (Art. 356)** the State Council of Ministers is **dismissed**.
[40:4:16] - **Art. 163(3):** whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by Ministers to the Governor **shall not be inquired into in any court** (no legal responsibility of Ministers).
