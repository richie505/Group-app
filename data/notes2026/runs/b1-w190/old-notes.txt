=== NOTES 1:190 | Arya Samaj and Dayananda Saraswati – Satyarth Prakash, Shuddhi, Dayanand Anglo-vedic Schools
## SUB 0 | Arya Samaj, 1875 – Founder and Founding
[190:0:0] - **Founder:** **Swami Dayananda Saraswati** (Mool Shankar, 1824-1883), born in a **Brahmin** family in **Morvi state (Tankara), Gujarat**; guru **Swami Virajananda** of Mathura; died at Ajmer, 1883.
[190:0:1] - **First Arya Samaj:** **Bombay, 1875** (10 April); headquarters later at **Lahore** (1877); strongest base Punjab.
[190:0:2] - Founder = **Dayananda** – not Vivekananda, Gokhale, Ramakrishna, Ranade, V.R. Shinde, Chandavarkar or Keshab.
[190:0:3] - **Wrong date trap:** "Arya Samaj founded in 1835" ✘ (IAS 2001).
[190:0:4] - **Current linkage:** **International Arya Mahasammelan 2025**, New Delhi, **30 Oct-2 Nov 2025** – 150 years of the Arya Samaj and Dayananda's **200th birth anniversary**.
[190:0:5] > Arya Samaj = Dayananda, Bombay 1875, HQ Lahore.

[190:0:7] - **IAS 2001 statements:** Keshab's Brahmo Samaj campaigned for women's education ✔; Vinoba Bhave's **Sarvodaya Samaj** worked among refugees ✔; Lajpat Rai did not oppose the Arya Samaj ✘ → **III and IV**.
## SUB 1 | Doctrines, Satyarth Prakash and Titles
[190:1:0] - **'Back to the Vedas':** Vedas = **infallible**, "India's Rock of Ages"; a revival of Vedic learning and purity, not of Vedic times; rejected the **Puranas**.
[190:1:1] - **Attacked:** idolatry, polytheism, caste rigidity and untouchability, child marriage, priestly ritual; caste by **merit/occupation, not birth** (CDS 2009: all three statements ✔).
[190:1:2] - **Supported:** widow remarriage, inter-caste marriage, women's education; minimum marriage age 25 (boys) and 16 (girls); **ten principles**.
[190:1:3] - **Satyarth Prakash** ('The Light of Truth', 1875, **Hindi**, revised 1882) – Dayananda's main work and the **Arya Samaj's** sacred text.
[190:1:4] - **Revivalist** in form (cf. reformist Brahmo/Prarthana Samaj).
[190:1:5] # Titles and slogans

  [190:1:6.0] 'India for the Indians' | Dayananda Saraswati (political message)
  [190:1:6.1] Martin Luther of India | Dayananda Saraswati
  [190:1:6.2] First to use the term 'Swaraj' | Dayananda (as usually given)
  [190:1:6.3] Prophet of Indian Nationalism | not Dayananda – Aurobindo
[190:1:7] > Dayananda: Back to the Vedas, Satyarth Prakash, India for the Indians, Martin Luther of India.
[190:1:8] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India (Ahir 2019) file p.252-254, p.291; PMF Modern History Part 3 p.162, p.186-187; Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.49; old app notes row 190
[190:1:9] - Dayananda is also called the **'Grandfather of the Indian Nation'** (as usually given in quiz books).
[190:1:10] - **Dayananda is also credited with:** "**Good government is no substitute for self-government**" and the **first call for Swadeshi and the boycott of foreign goods**.
## SUB 2 | Shuddhi, DAV Schools, the 1893 Split and Social Base
[190:2:0] - **Shuddhi** (purification): started by the **Arya Samaj** (Dayananda) to **reconvert** to Hinduism those who had become Muslims or Christians; also tried to raise untouchables; aggressive campaign of the 1920s (Shraddhanand) sharpened communalism.
[190:2:1] - **DAV:** **Dayananda Anglo-Vedic School/College, Lahore, 1886** (after Dayananda's death) – **Lala Hansraj** first headmaster/founder; Western-style education.
[190:2:2] - **1893 split:** **College Party** – Lala Hansraj, Lala Lal Chand, **Lala Lajpat Rai** (kept DAV); **Mahatma / Gurukul Party** – Guru Datta Vidyarthi and **Munshi Ram (Swami Shraddhanand)**, who set up the **Gurukul** (Gujranwala 1900, **Kangri, Haridwar 1902**).
[190:2:3] - **Swami Shraddhanand** spoke on Hindu-Muslim unity from the pulpit of the **Jama Masjid, Delhi, 4 April 1919** (Rowlatt satyagraha).
[190:2:4] - **Impact (A-R):** gave Hindus **self-respect and self-confidence**, which **undermined the myth of white superiority** – R explains A.
[190:2:5] - **Social base:** **Punjab and western U.P.**, mainly the **trading castes**; wider than the Brahmo Samaj, whose influence hardly went beyond Bengal; famous for social service in calamities.
[190:2:6] - **Lala Lajpat Rai:** Arya Samaj leader, ran the paper **The People** (not "unconnected"); **Al Hilal** (1912) = **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**.
[190:2:7] - **Hyderabad, 1938-39:** Arya Samaj satyagraha against the **Nizam's curbs on religious and civil rights**, alongside the State Congress satyagraha.
[190:2:8] > Shuddhi = Arya Samaj; DAV Lahore 1886 = Hansraj; Gurukul Kangri 1902 = Shraddhanand; split 1893.
[190:2:9] ~~ Sources: Spectrum Modern India (Ahir 2019) file p.243, p.254-255; PMF Modern History Part 3 p.187; PMF Modern History Part 5 p.173; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 06 p.73; old app notes row 190
[190:2:10] - **Bharatiya Hindu Shuddhi Sabha** – 1923, led by Swami Shraddhanand.
[190:2:11] - **Andhra Shuddhi Samajam:** formed at **Guntur, January 1934**, Secretary **Upputuru Venkata Punnarao**.
[190:2:12] - The Arya Samaj was called a **'nursery of militant nationalists'** – it shaped Lajpat Rai and Shraddhanand.

[191:0:6] # Ideas
[191:0:7] - **Practical Vedanta:** Vedanta to be lived in daily life; serving the poor is serving God – **"Daridra Narayana"** (God manifest in the poor); "service of jiva is worship of Shiva". Not Dayananda, Rammohan or Gandhi.
[191:0:8] - Followed **Vedanta, "a fully rational system"**; criticised Indians for **losing touch with the outside world** ("stagnant and mummified"); **condemned the caste system**, ritual and superstition – but did **not** call the Veda infallible (that is Dayananda) → CDS 2011: **I, II and III**.
[191:0:9] - **Vedanta the religion of all**; revived the best Hindu traditions; admired the **status of women in the West** → CDS 2009: **I, II and III**.
[191:0:10] - **"Our religion is in the kitchen"** – his attack on touch-me-not orthodoxy ("Our God is the cooking-pot").
[191:0:11] - Humanitarian relief = a **monastic duty**; knowledge without action is useless.

## SUB 2 | Ramakrishna Mission, 1897 – Founder, Year, Work
[191:2:0] - **Founded 1 May 1897 by Swami Vivekananda**, at Balaram Bose's house, **Baghbazar, Calcutta**, in his guru's name – not by Ramakrishna (died 1886), Vidyasagar, Dayananda, Rammohan Roy, Gokhale, Shyamji Krishna Varma, M.N. Das Gupta or Ranganathananda.

[191:2:2] - **HQ: Belur Math** on the Hooghly, Howrah (land bought **1898**); Math and Mission are twin bodies, legally and financially separate.
[191:2:3] - Motto: **"Atmano Mokshartham Jagad Hitaya Cha"** – for one's own liberation and the welfare of the world.
[191:2:4] - **Two wings:** (1) a **monastic order** of renunciation and practical spirituality; (2) **lay disciples** – preaching, philanthropy, humanitarian work.
[191:2:5] - **Work:** schools, hospitals, orphanages, **famine and disaster relief**; social and spiritual – **non-political, non-sectarian, not proselytising**; unlike the Arya Samaj it accepts image worship.

=== NOTES 1:192 | Muslim, Sikh and Parsi Reform – Aligarh, Deoband, Ahmadiya, Wahabi, Faraizi, Singh Sabha
## SUB 0 | Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and the Aligarh Movement
[192:0:0] # Sir Syed (1817-1898)
[192:0:1] - In the Company's **judicial service** in 1857; **loyalist**; knighted 1888; **never connected with the Congress** – asked Muslims to keep away from it (Ferozshah Mehta, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Ghaffar Khan were all Congressmen).
[192:0:2] - Aim: modernise Muslims through **English / Western scientific education** and a **rational reading of the Quran**; promoted **Urdu**; opposed purdah and polygamy.
[192:0:3] - Early view: India a bride, Hindus and Muslims "**her two eyes**"; turned after the **Hindi-Urdu controversy (1867)**.
[192:0:4] TABLE head: Body / work | Year | Note
  [192:0:4.0] Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind | 1859 | Sir Syed, "Causes of the Indian Revolt"
  [192:0:4.1] Scientific Society | 1864, Ghazipur (PMF: 1863) → Aligarh | Sir Syed – translated Western science, history into Urdu; not Cornwallis or a company
  [192:0:4.2] MAO school (Madarsatul Uloom Musalmanan-i-Hind) | 1875 | Sir Syed Ahmad Khan – not Jinnah, Muhammad Ali or Shaukat Ali
  [192:0:4.3] Renamed Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College | 1877 | Modelled on Oxford and Cambridge – the centre of the Aligarh Movement
  [192:0:4.4] Muhammadan Educational Conference | 1886 | Sir Syed
  [192:0:4.5] United Indian Patriotic Association | 1888 | Sir Syed + Raja Shiva Prasad of Benaras – to counter the Congress
  [192:0:4.6] MAO Defence Association | 1893 | Sir Syed with Theodore Beck; loyalist, anti-Congress
  [192:0:4.7] Aligarh Muslim University | 1920 | MAO College raised to AMU
  [192:0:4.8] Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq | – | Sir Syed's journal
  [192:0:4.9] The Indian Musalmans | 1871 | W.W. Hunter – not Sir Syed

[192:0:6] # Aligarh Movement and MAO principals
[192:0:7] - **Initial objective:** promote **modern Western scientific education** among Indian Muslims (not a Muslim state, not traditional madrasa reform, not Hindu-Muslim unity against the British); social reform; built a **modern educated Muslim middle class**.
[192:0:8] - **Principals:** first **H.G.I. Siddons**; then **Theodore Beck (1883-99)** – encouraged Muslim separatism / communalism and **turned Sir Syed against the Congress**; then Theodore Morison.
[192:0:9] - **Pairs (IAS 1998):** Beck – MAO College ✔; Ilbert Bill – Ripon ✔; Pherozeshah Mehta – INC ✔; **Badruddin Tyabji – Muslim League ✘** (he was INC President 1887) → **1, 2 and 3**.
[192:0:10] - Later Aligarh leaders: Chiragh Ali, Altaf Husain Hali, Nazir Ahmad, Mohsin-ul-Mulk (not Mahmud-ul-Hasan – Deoband).
[192:0:11] - **Mohammedan Literary Society: Calcutta, 1863, Nawab Abdul Latif** – "Father of Muslim Renaissance in Bengal" (not Sir Syed, Ameer Ali, Salimullah); not Aligarh.

## SUB 1 | Faraizi, Taiyuni and Ahmadiyya Movements
[192:1:0] - **Faraizi (Fara'idi) Movement:** founded **1818** by **Haji Shariatullah** in **East Bengal (Faridpur)** – the Faraizis = **followers of Haji Shariatullah** (not Dadu Dayal, Arya Samaj or Muslim League); aim: enforce Islamic duties (*faraiz*), purge un-Islamic practices.
[192:1:1] - **Dudu (Dadu) Miyan**, his son, made it revolutionary from **1840** – peasants against **zamindars and indigo planters**; told followers not to pay rent; arrested 1847, died 1862 → **leader of the Farazi rebellion** (not Wazir Ali, Shamsher Gazi, Aga Muhammad Raza).
[192:1:2] - Founder vs leader trap: *founder* = Haji Shariatullah; *leader / rebellion* = Dudu Miyan.
[192:1:3] - **Taiyuni movement:** **Karamat Ali Jaunpuri** (Bengal) – opposed Faraizi radicalism; pairing it with Rashid Ahmad Gangohi (Deoband) is the **wrong pair**.
[192:1:4] - **Ahmadiyya:** **Mirza Ghulam Ahmad, Qadian (Gurdaspur, Punjab), 1889** (not 1866); claimed to be the Messiah; liberal, spread Western education, **opposed jihad**; rejected by orthodox Muslims.
[192:1:5] - Pairs (APPSC G2): Aligarh – Sir Syed ✔; Faraizi – Haji Shariatullah ✔; Ahmadiyya – Mirza Ghulam Ahmad ✔; Taiyuni – Gangohi ✘.
[192:1:6] > Faraizi = Haji Shariatullah 1818 (Dudu Miyan led); Ahmadiyya = Qadian 1889.

[192:1:8] - **Faraizis and Wahabis:** the Faraizis later joined the Wahabi movement.
## SUB 2 | Wahabi Movement – Barelvi, Patna, Anti-British; Hyderabad Angle
[192:2:0] - Inspired by **Abdul Wahab of Arabia** and **Shah Waliullah** (1702-63); popularised by Shah Abdul Aziz; Indian founder **Syed Ahmad Barelvi** (of Rae Bareli).
[192:2:1] - Aim: return to pure Islam; convert **Dar-ul-Harb into Dar-ul-Islam**; fought the **Sikhs** in Punjab, then after 1849 the **British** → character: **anti-British** (APPSC G2 2011, three versions) – not pro-British, anti-Muslim or merely pro-Muslim.
[192:2:2] - **Chief centre: Patna** (base at Sithana in the NW tribal belt) – not Lahore, Amritsar or Pune.
[192:2:3] - Main followers: **Muslim peasants and weavers / artisans**; trials at **Ambala (1864)** and **Patna (1865)**; crushed in the 1870s.
[192:2:4] - **Titu Mir** (Narkelberia, 1831), a disciple of Barelvi, led Muslim peasants of Bengal against zamindars and indigo planters.
[192:2:5] - **Hyderabad:** Wahabi conspiracy **1839** led by **Mubariz-ud-Daula**, the Nizam's brother, who resisted British influence in the Deccan; arrested 1839, died in prison.
[192:2:6] - **Nasaraiah** (Sayyid Muslim, d. 1825): popular among the **Madigas**; preached **one God**, opposed idol worship; **Vinukonda–Narasaraopet** (Guntur).
[192:2:7] - **Osmania University** (Hyderabad, 1918) taught through **Urdu**.
[192:2:8] > Wahabi = Barelvi, Patna, anti-British; Hyderabad 1839 = Mubariz-ud-Daula.

## SUB 3 | Deoband School (Darul Uloom) and Nadwatul Ulama
[192:3:0] - **Darul Uloom, Deoband, 1866** (PMF: 1867), **Saharanpur district, United Provinces = Uttar Pradesh**, by **Muhammad Qasim Nanautavi and Rashid Ahmad Gangohi** – not 1885, 1888 or 1900.
[192:3:1] - **Aims:** propagate pure teachings of the **Quran and Hadis**, **keep alive jihad against foreign rulers**, **train religious leaders** – not Western education.
[192:3:2] - Politics: **welcomed the Congress**; **fatwa (1888)** against Sir Syed's United Patriotic Association and MAO Association; **Mahmud-ul-Hasan** gave it political content → **Jamiat-ul-Ulema** (1919).
[192:3:3] TABLE head: Feature | Aligarh | Deoband
  [192:3:3.0] Learning | Western, scientific | Quran and Hadis
  [192:3:3.1] British | Loyal | Anti-British
  [192:3:3.2] Congress | Opposed | Welcomed
[192:3:4] - **Nadwatul Ulama:** decided at **Kanpur** 1893, first session 1894; prime founder and first manager **Muhammad Ali Mungeri**; office moved to **Lucknow 1898**.
[192:3:5] - **Shibli Numani** – supporter of Deoband who wanted English and European science taught; "**founded the Nadwatal Ulama and Darul Uloom in Lucknow in 1894-96**" (Spectrum) → so "Dar-ul-Ulum was established by" (UPPCS 2014, options Shibli / Hussain Ahmad / Chakralavi / Ahmad Raza Khan) = **Shibli Numani**. (Deoband's own Darul Uloom = Nanautavi and Gangohi.)
[192:3:6] > Deoband 1866 (Nanautavi, Gangohi), UP; Nadwa Lucknow (Shibli, Mungeri).

## SUB 0 | Kuka (Namdhari) Movement – Punjab, Ram Singh, First Boycott
[193:0:0] - **Region: Punjab** (not U.P., Malabar, Bihar, Bengal or Maharashtra); founded **1840** by **Bhagat Jawahar Mal (Sian Sahib)** in western Punjab (Spectrum) – he **started** the Kuka revolt.
[193:0:1] - **Baba (Satguru) Ram Singh** – disciple of Balak Singh; **organised** it and founded the **Namdhari** sect (**1857**, **Bhaini, Ludhiana**) – not Guru Ram Das, Nanak or Gobind Singh, not Nankana Sahib.
[193:0:2] - Practices: **white clothes**, no meat, alcohol or drugs, a **lathi**; against idol, tomb, tree and snake worship and **Brahmin-led rituals**; widow remarriage, intermarriage.
[193:0:3] - **Boycott** of British **goods, English education, law courts and the postal system** → **Ram Singh = first to use non-cooperation and boycott (Swadeshi) as a political weapon** (APPSC 2025) – before Tilak, Pal or Gandhi.
[193:0:4] - Suppression: **Malerkotla clash, 1872** – Kukas blown from cannons; Ram Singh **deported to Rangoon (Burma)**; Kuka movement under Viceroy Northbrook.
[193:0:5] - Kuka and Wahabi both began as religious revolts and became anti-British political movements.
[193:0:6] > Kuka = Punjab; begun by Jawahar Mal (1840); Ram Singh, Bhaini, boycott; Malerkotla 1872.

[193:0:8] - **Namdhari (Kuka) movement** – among the earliest movements to resist British rule in Punjab.

[193:2:0] - **Singh Sabha:** **Amritsar, 1873**; more radical **Lahore Singh Sabha, 1879**; aims – modern Western education for Sikhs through **Khalsa schools**, **Gurmukhi/Punjabi** literature, remove un-Sikh practices, counter missionaries.

## SUB 3 | Parsi Reform – Rahnumai Mazdayasnan Sabha, 1851
[193:3:0] - **Rahnumai Mazdayasnan Sabha** (Religious Reform Association), **Bombay, 1851** – to reform the **Parsis** (not Sikhs, Muslims or Anglo-Indians).
[193:3:1] - Founders: **Naoroji Furdonji** (first president), **Dadabhai Naoroji**, **K.R. Cama**, **S.S. Bengalee**.

## SUB 0 | Justice Party (South Indian Liberal Federation)
[194:0:0] # Background, aim and social base
[194:0:1] - **Why:** Brahmins were about **3%** of the Madras Presidency population but held **over 90%** of government jobs.
[194:0:2] - **Aim:** to further the interests of **non-Brahmins** (APPSC 2008, AP History PYQ ×2) – end Brahmin dominance in jobs, education, politics and religion; basically an **anti-Brahmin party** – "the first political party in India founded against Brahmins".
[194:0:3] - **Agenda of SILF:** social justice, a rightful place in services, political representation (information technology is an anachronism).
[194:0:4] - **Demand:** separate **communal representation** for non-Brahmins, on the lines given to Muslims by the Morley-Minto Reforms; opposed the Brahmin-dominated Congress.
[194:0:5] - **Social base:** non-Brahmin landed gentry, **intermediate landed castes** (Andhra: **Reddys, Kammas, Kapus**). Dalits built a parallel "Adi" movement.
[194:0:6] - **British view:** backed it to weaken **Home Rule**; used against Home Rule and Gandhi's movements; it **supported the British in the Second World War** (Congress, Forward Bloc, Yugantar did not).
[194:0:7] - **Andhra Mahasabha (Vijayawada, 1917)** sought reserved seats for non-Brahmins – **Ponaka Pattabhirama Reddy** and **Tripuraneni Ramaswamy Chowdary**.

## SUB 1 | Jyotiba Phule and the Satyashodhak Samaj
[194:1:0] # Jyotiba Phule (1827-1890)
[194:1:1] - Born **1827**, Satara; a gardener's son – **Mali community**, Maharashtra.
[194:1:2] - Title **"Mahatma"** given by **Vithalrao Krishnaji Vandekar (1888)**; called **"Father of Indian Social Revolution"**.
[194:1:3] - **Creed:** all men have the same features and intellect – **nobody is sacred by birth**; aimed at complete abolition of caste; against Sanskritic Hinduism.
[194:1:4] - **1848**: girls' school, **Pune**; taught by **Savitribai Phule** (Maharashtra) – **India's first woman teacher**; home for widows 1854; pioneer of widow remarriage → the Indian women's movement is credited largely to Phule's inspiration.
[194:1:5] - **Savitribai** headed the Samaj's women's section; worked for lower-caste girls.
[194:1:6] # Satyashodhak Samaj ("Society of Truth Seekers")
[194:1:7] - Founded **24 September 1873, Pune**, by **Jyotiba Phule** – its **first president**; an **anti-caste movement in Maharashtra** (UPSC 2016 ×3) – not a tribal, temple-entry or peasant movement.
[194:1:8] - Aim: **upliftment of the backward classes** – **Shudras and Ati-Shudras** against **Arya-Brahmin** exploitation (not for Arya-Brahmins); end of **priestly monopoly**; education of women and lower castes; social service.
[194:1:9] - Leaders from malis, telis, kunbis, saris, dhangars.
[194:1:10] - **Satyashodhak marriages**: **without priests**; allowed **inter-caste** marriage.
[194:1:11] - Symbol: **King Bali** (the oppressed) against the Brahmanical symbol of **Rama**.
[194:1:12] - **Ideas (CDS 2015 – all three true):** set up **1873** ✔; Brahmins = progeny of **'alien' Aryans** ✔; his 1880s-90s focus on the **Kunbi** peasantry privileged **Maratha identity** ✔.
[194:1:13] - **Din Bandhu** (Marathi weekly, **Pune, 1877**) – the Samaj's organ, started by **Krishnarao Bhalekar**, credited to **Phule's** movement; spread it to remote Maharashtra; later edited by **N.M. Lokhande**. Phule also ran Dinmitra.

## SUB 3 | Adi-Andhra and Adi-Hindu Movements
[194:3:0] # Terms
[194:3:1] - **"Adi"** = original inhabitants; rejected "Panchama". **Adi-Andhra** = Dalits of the Andhra (Madras) region; **Adi-Hindu** = Dalits of Hyderabad State.
[194:3:2] - Early root: **Adi-Andhra Sabha (1890)**, first called the Pariya Mahasabha.
[194:3:3] # Madiri Bhagya Reddy Varma (born 1888, Hyderabad)
[194:3:4] - Original name **Madari Bhagaiah**; **"Father of the Dalit movement"** in Hyderabad/Andhra; used the terms **Adi-Andhra and Adi-Hindu** for Panchamas.
[194:3:5] - **Jagan Mitra Mandali (1906)** (not 1960) → **Manya Sangham (1911)** → **Adi-Hindu Social Service League**; journal **Adi Hindu**.
[194:3:6] - **Sahapankti bhojanams** (community dinners) and **Buddhism** (Buddha Jayanti from 1913) for Dalit consciousness; against child marriage, liquor, Devadasi system.
