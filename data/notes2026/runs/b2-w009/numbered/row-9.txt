## SUB 0 | Articles 14–18 – Which Article Says What
[0:0] # The five Articles of the Right to Equality
[0:1] - The Right to Equality = **five Articles, 14 to 18** (Part III) – not 13–17, 15–19 or 16–20. Article 13 is not one of them; **Art. 19 belongs to the Right to Freedom** (the odd one out in a 14/15/16/19 list).
[0:2] TABLE head: Article | Content | Available to
  [0:2.0] 14 | "The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India" | All persons (citizens and foreigners)
  [0:2.1] 15 | Prohibition of discrimination on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth | Citizens only
  [0:2.2] 16 | Equality of opportunity in public employment | Citizens only
  [0:2.3] 17 | "Untouchability" abolished; its practice in any form forbidden; enforcing any disability from it is an offence punishable by law | All (also binds private persons)
  [0:2.4] 18 | Titles abolished, except military and academic distinctions | —
[0:3] - **Match (UPSC 2004):** 14 = equality before law / equal protection; 15 = no discrimination on religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth; 16 = equal opportunity in public employment; 17 = untouchability abolished.
[0:4] - Art. 14 operates **within the territory of India** (not "deep space" or "high seas").
[0:5] - "All are equal before law" / "equal protection of the laws" = **Article 14** (not 12, 13, 15, 21 or 22).
[0:6] - Article 15 prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth – not Arts. 19, 23 or 25.
[0:7] - Art. 14 = all persons; **Arts. 15, 16, 19, 29** = citizens only. Equal protection of the law is open to all persons.
[0:8] - Related: **Art. 23** prohibits **human trafficking** and forced labour (Right against Exploitation); **Art. 30** – minorities' right to establish educational institutions; Art. 29 – cultural and educational rights of any section.
[0:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.169, p.184-185, p.188-197; PMF Polity Notes p.61-62
## SUB 1 | Articles 15 and 16 – Clauses and Special Provisions
[1:0] # Article 15 – clauses
[1:1] TABLE head: Clause | Content | Added by
  [1:1.0] 15(1) | State shall not discriminate on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth (descent is not among them) | Original
  [1:1.1] 15(2) | Access to shops, public restaurants, hotels, places of entertainment; use of wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads – binds private persons as well as the State | Original
  [1:1.2] 15(3) | Special provisions for women and children | Original
  [1:1.3] 15(4) | Special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes (SEBCs/OBCs), SCs and STs – "nothing in this Article or in clause (2) of Article 29 shall prevent the State …" | 1st Amendment, 1951 (after Champakam Dorairajan)
  [1:1.4] 15(5) | Reservation for SEBC/SC/ST in admission to educational institutions, including private aided or unaided; excludes Art. 30 minority institutions | 93rd Amendment, 2005
  [1:1.5] 15(6) | EWS – up to 10% in education | 103rd Amendment, 2019
[1:2] - The wording is "socially and **educationally** backward classes" – **not "socially and economically"**; a statement using "economically" is the wrong one.
[1:3] - Article 15 special provisions cover women, children, SEBCs, SCs, STs, EWS; **aged persons are NOT covered**.
[1:4] - Art. 15 is **limited to citizens** – "Art. 15 is not limited to citizens" is the wrong statement.
[1:5] # Article 16 – clauses
[1:6] TABLE head: Clause | Content | Added by
  [1:6.0] 16(1) | Equality of opportunity for all citizens in employment or appointment to any office under the State | Original
  [1:6.1] 16(2) | No citizen ineligible or discriminated on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth, residence | Original
  [1:6.2] 16(3) | Parliament only may prescribe a residence requirement for certain jobs | Original
  [1:6.3] 16(4) | Reservation in appointments for backward classes not adequately represented in State services | Original
  [1:6.4] 16(4A) | SC/ST reservation in promotion – then with consequential seniority (nullified the "catch-up" rule) | 77th (1995); 85th (2001)
  [1:6.5] 16(4B) | Backlog (carried-forward) vacancies a separate group, outside the 50% ceiling | 81st (2000)
  [1:6.6] 16(5) | Office in a religious or denominational institution may be kept for persons of that religion/denomination | Original
  [1:6.7] 16(6) | EWS – up to 10% in public employment | 103rd (2019)
[1:7] - **Equal opportunity in public employment** = **16(1) and 16(2)**, for **all citizens** (not only citizens born in India, not all persons).
[1:8] - **Reservation in employment** = **16(4)**; **OBC reservation** = **Arts. 15(4) and 16(4)**.
[1:9] - **Reservation in promotions:** first provided by the **77th Amendment, 1995** (Art. 16(4A)); the **85th (2001)** extended it with consequential seniority. 16(4A) is **enabling** – it lets, not obliges, the State to reserve.
[1:10] - **103rd Amendment, 2019** (passed as the **124th Amendment Bill**) – Arts. **15(6) and 16(6)**, EWS up to **10%**; the 102nd = NCBC, not EWS.
[1:11] - **105th Amendment (2021)** restored the States' power to identify SEBCs (reversed the Maratha-case reading of the **102nd**).

## SUB 2 | Article 14 – Equality Before Law, Equal Protection, Reasonable Classification
[2:0] # Two faces of Article 14
[2:1] TABLE head: Equality before law | Equal protection of the laws
  [2:1.0] British origin (Dicey) | American origin (US 14th Amendment)
  [2:1.1] Negative concept: absence of special privileges; no one above law | Positive concept: like treated alike in equal circumstances
  [2:1.2] All equally subject to ordinary law and ordinary courts | Permits reasonable classification
[2:2] - Art. 14 contains **both** concepts – "Art. 14 has only equality before law, not equal protection" is false.
[2:3] - An implication of equality is the **absence of privileges** (not of restraints, competition or ideology).
[2:4] - "Equals must be treated equally, unequals differently": the State **can treat unequals differently** to create a level playing field, and **among equals the law should be equal and equally administered** (A and R both true, R explains A).
[2:5] # Reasonable classification and arbitrariness
[2:6] - Art. 14 **forbids class legislation**, **not reasonable classification** (not rural/urban legislation).
[2:7] - **Twin test**: **intelligible differentia** + **rational nexus** with the law's object.
[2:8] - **E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu (1974)**: "Equality and arbitrariness are **sworn enemies**" / equality is **antithetic to arbitrariness**.
[2:9] - **Maneka Gandhi (1978)**: any arbitrary State action breaks Art. 14. A law giving the executive **unguided and uncontrolled discretionary power** violates **Article 14** (UPSC 2021; not Arts. 28, 32, 44).
[2:10] - Article 14 puts a **limitation on legislative power** (UPPCS 1999 key: Art. 14).
[2:11] - **11 Apr 2026**: SC held the State cannot treat **pensioners** differently from serving employees on **dearness allowance** without valid classification (Kerala/KSRTC).
[2:12] - **Feb 2026**: SC questioned the caste-discrimination clause of the **UGC equity regulations** (**Mritunjay Tiwari v. Union of India**).
[2:13] # Exceptions – equality is not absolute
[2:14] TABLE head: Provision | Immunity / special status
  [2:14.0] Art. 361 | President and Governors not answerable to courts; no criminal case during term; civil case only after two months' notice
  [2:14.1] Art. 361A (44th, 1978) | No liability for a true, non-malicious report of proceedings of Parliament/State legislatures
  [2:14.2] Arts. 105 / 194 | MPs / MLAs: immunity for anything said or vote given in the House
  [2:14.3] International law | Foreign sovereigns, ambassadors, diplomats and UN agencies immune from civil and criminal proceedings
[2:15] - APPSC G1: **Arts. 194 and 361A** show equality in India is **not absolute**; in India equality is guaranteed by the **Constitution** (Dicey's "rights from courts" idea does not fit India).
[2:16] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.184-188; PMF Polity Notes p.61; E.P. Royappa (1974) and Maneka Gandhi (1978) as cited there
## SUB 3 | Rule of Law – Dicey
[3:0] - **A.V. Dicey** (British jurist), *Introduction to the Study of the Law of the Constitution* (**1885**). India borrowed the Rule of Law from **Britain** (with parliamentary government, single citizenship, cabinet system) – **not from the GoI Act 1935** (that gave the federal scheme, Governor, emergency powers).
[3:1] - **Three elements**: (1) **absence of arbitrary power** (**limitation of powers**) – regular law supreme, no punishment except for a distinct breach of law; (2) **equality before law**; (3) **primacy of individual rights** (**liberty and civil rights**) – rights from judicial decisions.
[3:2] - **Not an element**: "**people's responsibility to the Government**" → features asked = **1, 2 and 4** (UPSC 2018). "The State has inherent unlimited police powers" – false.
[3:3] - Rule of Law means every citizen is **equal before law**, gets **equal protection**, suffers **no discrimination** and enjoys **no special privileges**.
[3:4] - Dicey opposed the French **droit administratif** (officials subject only to special administrative tribunals).
[3:5] - **India accepts (1) and (2) but reverses (3)**: in India the **Constitution is the source of rights**.
[3:6] - Rule of Law in India rests on **Article 14**; it is a **basic feature** – **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**, **Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)**.
[3:7] - Rule of Law operates in a **democracy** (not dictatorship, monarchy, autocracy); APPSC calls it the force that **moves the machine of democracy**.
[3:8] TABLE head: Rule of Law | Rule by Law
  [3:8.0] Government subordinate to law | Ruler above law; law as a weapon of state control
  [3:8.1] Laws just, fair, non-arbitrary | Governs citizens only
[3:9] - **Rule of Law Index** – released by the **World Justice Project** (not Amnesty, ICJ or OHCHR); measuring since 2008.
[3:10] > The Rule of Law is British, not American: the Indian Constitution resembles the **US** in **Fundamental Rights** (Bill of Rights), judicial review and equal protection.
[3:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.155 (borrowed features), p.184-187; PMF Polity Notes p.61; World Justice Project (worldjusticeproject.org)
## SUB 4 | Article 17 – Untouchability
[4:0] - **Article 17** abolishes untouchability and forbids its practice in any form (not Arts. 14, 15, 16, 18, 19 or 21). Adopted amid cries of "**Mahatma Gandhi ki Jai**".
[4:1] - "Untouchability" is **not defined** in the Constitution or the Act. Art. 17 also operates **against private individuals**.
[4:2] - Untouchability as a social practice = **boycotting**, **insult and oppression**, **exploitation** (not equality or integration).
[4:3] - **Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955** → renamed **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** by the **1976** amendment (the title keeps 1955). A person convicted under it is disqualified for election to Parliament/State legislature.
[4:4] - **SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989** – further law against caste atrocities.
[4:5] - **False evidence (Sec. 3(2)(i), PoA Act):** a non-SC/ST person who fabricates false evidence so that an SC/ST member is convicted of a **capital offence** gets **imprisonment for life and fine**; if the innocent SC/ST member is **executed**, the punishment is **death**.
[4:6] - MPPSC 2015: the Article through which SCs/STs are given fundamental socio-economic and cultural protection = **Article 17**.
[4:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.195-196; PMF Polity Notes p.62
## SUB 5 | Article 18 – Titles and Awards
[5:0] TABLE head: Clause | Provision
  [5:0.0] 18(1) | State confers no title except military or academic distinctions
  [5:0.1] 18(2) | A citizen shall not accept a foreign title at all – no consent route
  [5:0.2] 18(3) | A non-citizen holding an office of profit or trust needs the President's consent to accept a foreign title
  [5:0.3] 18(4) | No office-holder may accept a present, emolument or office from a foreign State without the President's consent
[5:1] - So the Constitution bans **both** the State conferring titles **and** citizens accepting foreign titles.
[5:2] - **Abolishes titles = Article 18** (not 17, 24 or 25).
[5:3] - **Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Shri** are given under **Article 18** (military/academic-type distinctions, not titles).
[5:4] - **Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India (Dec 1995)**: **Bharat Ratna and Padma awards are not titles**, but must **not be used as prefixes or suffixes**; equality admits distinction of merit, not hereditary titles.
[5:5] - Art. 18 helps **social equality** by ending artificial distinctions.
[5:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.196-197; PMF Polity Notes p.62
## SUB 6 | Types of Equality
[6:0] TABLE head: Type | Articles
  [6:0.0] Social | 15, 17, 18 – Arts. 17 and 18 provide social equality
  [6:0.1] Economic | DPSP 39(b), 39(c), 39(d)

[6:1] - **Economic equality is NOT part of the FR to equality.** Included: equality before law (14), social equality (15), equal opportunity (16).
[6:2] - "**Political freedom without economic equality is an illusion**" – attributed in Indian textbooks to **G.D.H. Cole** (the idea goes back to Bakunin's "a pretence, a fraud, a lie").
[6:3] - Preamble promises "**equality of status and of opportunity**".
[6:4] - **Ambedkar**: liberty, equality, fraternity = an **inseparable trinity**.
