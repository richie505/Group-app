=== Article 19 – the Six Freedoms
- **Article 19** guarantees **six freedoms** to citizens (not Arts. 14, 18, 20, 21 or 22) – **seven originally** (asked as "seven freedoms" in older papers); the 7th, property, was deleted.
- **Right to Freedom** group = **Articles 19–22** (four Articles); Arts. 14–16 belong to the Equality group.
| Clause | Freedom | Restricted by |
|---|---|---|
| **19(1)(a)** | **Speech and expression** | 19(2) |
| **19(1)(b)** | **Assemble peaceably and without arms** | 19(3) |
| **19(1)(c)** | **Form associations or unions** or **co-operative societies** (co-ops added by **97th, 2011**) | 19(4) |
| **19(1)(d)** | **Move freely throughout the territory of India** | 19(5) |
| **19(1)(e)** | **Reside and settle** in any part of India | 19(5) |
| 19(1)(f) | Acquire, hold and dispose of **property** – deleted by the **44th Amendment, 1978** | — |
| **19(1)(g)** | **Practise any profession**, or carry on any **occupation, trade or business** | 19(6) |
- **Match:** 19(1)(a) = speech and expression; (b) = assemble peaceably without arms; (c) = associations or unions; (d) = move freely throughout India.
- **Not in Article 19(1):** **equal opportunity** (Art. 16), **property** (deleted 1978), **moving out of the country** (travel abroad = **Art. 21**, Maneka Gandhi 1978), **freedom of conscience** (Art. 25).
- **Property** was removed as a Fundamental Right by the **44th Amendment Act, 1978** (now Art. 300A, a legal right).
- **97th Amendment (2011)**: the new right to **form co-operative societies** in 19(1)(c); also **Article 43B** and **Part IXB**.
- In force from **26 January 1950** – Art. 19 of the original Constitution provided freedom of expression.
- Related Articles: **Art. 20** – protection in respect of conviction for offences; **Art. 21** – protection of life and personal liberty; **21A** (86th, 2002) – education; **Art. 22** – arrest and detention, preventive detention without an **Advisory Board**: max **three months**.

=== Freedom of Speech and Expression – Press and Implied Rights
- Freedom of speech is **the most essential ingredient of democracy** among the freedoms.
- **Freedom of the press is not named in the Constitution**; it is **implied in / secured under 19(1)(a)** (not specially provided, not from a law of Parliament, executive power, Art. 361A or the Rule of Law). Trade, residence and association **are** expressly listed.
- Speech is protected **irrespective of the medium of communication** – press, TV, internet.
- The **right to know, receive and impart information** is part of 19(1)(a); RTI itself is not named in the Constitution.
| Implied right | Case |
|---|---|
| **Press** and circulation | Romesh Thappar (1950); **Sakal Papers (1961)**; Indian Express (1985) |
| **Hoisting the National Flag** (also on private buildings) – **19(1)(a)** | **Naveen Jindal (2004)** – industrialist Naveen Jindal's PIL in the Delhi HC; the Flag Code is only executive instructions |
| **Right to silence** (not singing the National Anthem) | **Bijoe Emmanuel v. Kerala (1986)** – speech and conscience (Art. 25) |
| Right to information / to know | **State of UP v. Raj Narain (1975)**; voters' right to know candidates |
| Commercial speech | **Tata Press (1995)** |
| Speech and trade **over the internet** | **Anuradha Bhasin (2020)** – 19(1)(a), 19(1)(g) |
| Telecast; no government monopoly on airwaves | **Cricket Association of Bengal (1995)** |
| Against phone tapping | **PUCL (1997)** – safeguards |
- **National Anthem:** compelling it violates speech/expression and conscience, and **no law obliges anyone to sing it** (the 1971 Act punishes only preventing its singing) → all three statements correct (UPSC 1996).
- **Internet access** is linked to **Article 19** (SSC 2020 key; Anuradha Bhasin) – "Right to access internet: Art. 21" is the mismatched pair; privacy and shelter are Art. 21 rights.
- **Films may be pre-censored**, unlike the press, because they **reach larger audiences**.

=== Reasonable Restrictions – 19(2) to 19(6)
- In a democracy the right to freedom = **freedom with reasonable restrictions** – not unlimited, not as fixed by the Government or civil society.
| Clause | Freedom | Grounds |
|---|---|---|
| **19(2)** | Speech | Eight: **sovereignty and integrity**, **security of State**, **friendly relations with foreign States**, **public order**, **decency or morality**, **contempt of court**, **defamation**, **incitement to an offence** |
| **19(3)** | Assembly | Sovereignty and integrity; **public order** (not morality, not security of State) |
| **19(4)** | Association | Sovereignty and integrity; public order; **morality** |
| **19(5)** | Movement, residence | **General public interest**; **protection of Scheduled Tribes** |
| **19(6)** | Profession, trade | General public interest; **professional/technical qualifications**; **State trade or monopoly** |
- **Not a 19(2) ground**: **sedition**, **views opposing the Government**, **unbecoming criticism**, "general public interest", economic grounds. The grounds are **exhaustive** (**Kaushal Kishor, 2023**).
- **Defamation**: **slander = spoken** (oral), libel = written; civil (tort) and criminal liability.
# Grounds added by amendments
| Amendment | Added |
|---|---|
| **1st (1951)** | **Public order, friendly relations, incitement**; rewrote 19(6) – qualifications and State monopoly |
| **16th (1963)** | **Sovereignty and integrity** of India in 19(2), (3), (4) |
# Test of reasonableness
- **State of Madras v. V.G. Row (1952)**: nature of the right, purpose, extent and urgency of the evil, proportionality.
- **Courts** decide reasonableness; a restriction must **not eclipse the right**; it must safeguard the **interest of the general public** and weigh **prevailing social values and needs**.
- **Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar (1962)** – the landmark judgment on **sedition**: s.124A IPC valid only for incitement to violence or public disorder.

=== Availability – Citizens Only; Detenus; Golden Triangle
- Art. 19 is for **citizens only** – not foreigners, not a company as a legal person (citizen shareholders may sue). Freedom of speech is guaranteed **only to citizens**; life and liberty, equality before law and freedom of religion extend to foreigners too.
- A **British citizen** in India cannot claim **freedom of trade and profession** (19(1)(g)).
- Available against **State action**; but **Kaushal Kishor (2023)**: Arts. 19 and 21 enforceable against **non-State actors** too.
- A person whose movement is taken away by **imprisonment or detention** does **not** lose his **freedom of expression** – he may write and publish books (State of Maharashtra v. Prabhakar Pandurang Sanzgiri, 1966).
- **Golden triangle**: Arts. **14, 19, 21** – **Maneka Gandhi (1978)**.

=== Assembly, Association, Movement and Profession
- Assembly must be **peaceful and unarmed**; excludes violent or **armed** gatherings. A **gherao** of officials is **not** part of 19(1)(b); 19(3) allows public-order limits.
- Right to protest is read from 19(1)(a), (b) and 21. **22 May 2026**: SC – peaceful protest is everybody's right but **must not disturb other citizens** or occupy public space.
| Action | Status | Case |
|---|---|---|
| Demonstration | Protected; strike not | **Kameshwar Prasad (1962)** |
| Strike | Not an FR – **only a legal right** | All India Bank Employees' Assn (1962) |
| Strike by govt employees | No right | **T.K. Rangarajan (2003)** |
| Bandh | Illegal – curbs others' freedoms | **CPI(M) v. Bharat Kumar** |
- **FCRA**: Centre defends curbs under **19(4)** (25 Jun 2026); **INSAF (2020)** – only associations in **active politics** may be barred from foreign funds.
- **19(5)**: entry and settlement of outsiders may be limited to protect **Scheduled Tribes** (Inner Line areas).
- **19(6)**: State may prescribe **professional or technical qualifications** and run a trade **to the exclusion of citizens** (State monopoly) – inserted by the **1st Amendment**. **No right to a dangerous or immoral trade** (e.g. trafficking).

=== Article 19 During Emergency – 358 vs 359
| Article 358 | Article 359 |
|---|---|
| **Art. 19 suspended automatically** – all **six** freedoms | Needs a **Presidential order** |
| Only for emergency on **war or external aggression** (44th Amendment), not armed rebellion | Suspends only enforcement of the rights named |
| Covers only Art. 19 | **Arts. 20 and 21 can never be suspended** (44th) |
- "Article ___ is suspended when emergency is imposed" → **Article 19** (not 21, 27 or 41).

=== New Criminal Laws (from 1 July 2024)
- **BNS, 2023** replaced the IPC; **BNSS, 2023** replaced the CrPC (statutory **Zero FIR**, video recording of search and seizure).
| Subject | Old | New |
|---|---|---|
| Sedition | s.124A IPC (in abeyance 2022) | **s.152 BNS** – endangering sovereignty, unity, integrity |
| Criminal defamation | IPC 499-500 | **s.356 BNS** |
| Hate speech | — | **s.196 BNS** |
| Unlawful assembly (five or more) | IPC s.141 | **s.189 BNS** |
| Prohibitory orders | CrPC s.144 | **s.163 BNSS** |
