=== Articles 14–18 – Which Article Says What
# The five Articles of the Right to Equality
- The Right to Equality = **five Articles, 14 to 18** (Part III) – not 13–17, 15–19 or 16–20. Article 13 is not one of them; **Art. 19 belongs to the Right to Freedom** (the odd one out in a 14/15/16/19 list).
| Article | Content | Available to |
|---|---|---|
| **14** | "The State shall not deny to any person **equality before the law** or the **equal protection of the laws** within the **territory of India**" | **All persons** (citizens and foreigners) |
| **15** | **Prohibition of discrimination** on grounds **only** of **religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth** | **Citizens only** |
| **16** | **Equality of opportunity in public employment** | **Citizens only** |
| **17** | **"Untouchability" abolished**; its practice in any form forbidden; enforcing any disability from it is an **offence** punishable by law | All (also binds private persons) |
| **18** | **Titles abolished**, except military and academic distinctions | — |
- **Match (UPSC 2004):** 14 = equality before law / equal protection; 15 = no discrimination on religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth; 16 = equal opportunity in public employment; 17 = untouchability abolished.
- Art. 14 operates **within the territory of India** (not "deep space" or "high seas").
- "All are equal before law" / "equal protection of the laws" = **Article 14** (not 12, 13, 15, 21 or 22).
- Article 15 prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth – not Arts. 19, 23 or 25.
- Art. 14 = all persons; **Arts. 15, 16, 19, 29** = citizens only. Equal protection of the law is open to all persons.
- Related: **Art. 23** prohibits **human trafficking** and forced labour (Right against Exploitation); **Art. 30** – minorities' right to establish educational institutions; Art. 29 – cultural and educational rights of any section.

=== Articles 15 and 16 – Clauses and Special Provisions
# Article 15 – clauses
| Clause | Content | Added by |
|---|---|---|
| **15(1)** | State shall not discriminate on grounds **only** of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth (**descent is not among them**) | Original |
| **15(2)** | Access to shops, public restaurants, **hotels**, places of entertainment; use of wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads – binds **private persons** as well as the State | Original |
| **15(3)** | Special provisions for **women and children** | Original |
| **15(4)** | Special provisions for **socially and educationally backward classes** (SEBCs/OBCs), **SCs and STs** – "nothing in this Article or in clause (2) of Article 29 shall prevent the State …" | **1st Amendment, 1951** (after Champakam Dorairajan) |
| **15(5)** | Reservation for SEBC/SC/ST in **admission to educational institutions, including private aided or unaided**; **excludes Art. 30 minority institutions** | **93rd Amendment, 2005** |
| **15(6)** | **EWS** – up to **10%** in education | **103rd Amendment, 2019** |
- The wording is "socially and **educationally** backward classes" – **not "socially and economically"**; a statement using "economically" is the wrong one.
- Article 15 special provisions cover women, children, SEBCs, SCs, STs, EWS; **aged persons are NOT covered**.
- Art. 15 is **limited to citizens** – "Art. 15 is not limited to citizens" is the wrong statement.
# Article 16 – clauses
| Clause | Content | Added by |
|---|---|---|
| **16(1)** | **Equality of opportunity for all citizens** in employment or appointment to any office under the State | Original |
| **16(2)** | No citizen ineligible or discriminated on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, **descent**, place of birth, **residence** | Original |
| **16(3)** | **Parliament only** may prescribe a **residence** requirement for certain jobs | Original |
| **16(4)** | **Reservation in appointments** for backward classes not adequately represented in State services | Original |
| **16(4A)** | **SC/ST reservation in promotion** – then with **consequential seniority** (nullified the "catch-up" rule) | **77th (1995)**; **85th (2001)** |
| **16(4B)** | **Backlog (carried-forward) vacancies** a separate group, outside the 50% ceiling | **81st (2000)** |
| **16(5)** | Office in a **religious or denominational institution** may be kept for persons of that religion/denomination | Original |
| **16(6)** | **EWS** – up to **10%** in public employment | **103rd (2019)** |
- **Equal opportunity in public employment** = **16(1) and 16(2)**, for **all citizens** (not only citizens born in India, not all persons).
- **Reservation in employment** = **16(4)**; **OBC reservation** = **Arts. 15(4) and 16(4)**.
- **Reservation in promotions:** first provided by the **77th Amendment, 1995** (Art. 16(4A)); the **85th (2001)** extended it with consequential seniority. 16(4A) is **enabling** – it lets, not obliges, the State to reserve.
- **103rd Amendment, 2019** (passed as the **124th Amendment Bill**) – Arts. **15(6) and 16(6)**, EWS up to **10%**; the 102nd = NCBC, not EWS.
- **105th Amendment (2021)** restored the States' power to identify SEBCs (reversed the Maratha-case reading of the **102nd**).
# Related reservation Articles
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| **340** | President appoints a commission on the conditions of SEBCs |
| **338B** | **NCBC** – constitutional body (**102nd Amendment**) |
| **342A** | **President** specifies SEBCs for the Central List; States keep their own lists (102nd, 105th) |

=== Article 14 – Equality Before Law, Equal Protection, Reasonable Classification
# Two faces of Article 14
| Equality before law | Equal protection of the laws |
|---|---|
| **British** origin (Dicey) | **American** origin (US 14th Amendment) |
| **Negative** concept: **absence of special privileges**; no one above law | **Positive** concept: like treated alike in equal circumstances |
| All equally subject to ordinary law and ordinary courts | Permits **reasonable classification** |
- Art. 14 contains **both** concepts – "Art. 14 has only equality before law, not equal protection" is false.
- An implication of equality is the **absence of privileges** (not of restraints, competition or ideology).
- "Equals must be treated equally, unequals differently": the State **can treat unequals differently** to create a level playing field, and **among equals the law should be equal and equally administered** (A and R both true, R explains A).
# Reasonable classification and arbitrariness
- Art. 14 **forbids class legislation**, **not reasonable classification** (not rural/urban legislation).
- **Twin test**: **intelligible differentia** + **rational nexus** with the law's object.
- **E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu (1974)**: "Equality and arbitrariness are **sworn enemies**" / equality is **antithetic to arbitrariness**.
- **Maneka Gandhi (1978)**: any arbitrary State action breaks Art. 14. A law giving the executive **unguided and uncontrolled discretionary power** violates **Article 14** (UPSC 2021; not Arts. 28, 32, 44).
- Article 14 puts a **limitation on legislative power** (UPPCS 1999 key: Art. 14).
- **11 Apr 2026**: SC held the State cannot treat **pensioners** differently from serving employees on **dearness allowance** without valid classification (Kerala/KSRTC).
- **Feb 2026**: SC questioned the caste-discrimination clause of the **UGC equity regulations** (**Mritunjay Tiwari v. Union of India**).
# Exceptions – equality is not absolute
| Provision | Immunity / special status |
|---|---|
| **Art. 361** | President and Governors not answerable to courts; **no criminal case** during term; civil case only after **two months'** notice |
| **Art. 361A** (44th, 1978) | No liability for a true, non-malicious report of proceedings of Parliament/State legislatures |
| **Arts. 105 / 194** | MPs / MLAs: immunity for anything said or vote given in the House |
| International law | **Foreign sovereigns, ambassadors**, diplomats and UN agencies immune from civil and criminal proceedings |
- APPSC G1: **Arts. 194 and 361A** show equality in India is **not absolute**; in India equality is guaranteed by the **Constitution** (Dicey's "rights from courts" idea does not fit India).

=== Rule of Law – Dicey
- **A.V. Dicey** (British jurist), *Introduction to the Study of the Law of the Constitution* (**1885**). India borrowed the Rule of Law from **Britain** (with parliamentary government, single citizenship, cabinet system) – **not from the GoI Act 1935** (that gave the federal scheme, Governor, emergency powers).
- **Three elements**: (1) **absence of arbitrary power** (**limitation of powers**) – regular law supreme, no punishment except for a distinct breach of law; (2) **equality before law**; (3) **primacy of individual rights** (**liberty and civil rights**) – rights from judicial decisions.
- **Not an element**: "**people's responsibility to the Government**" → features asked = **1, 2 and 4** (UPSC 2018). "The State has inherent unlimited police powers" – false.
- Rule of Law means every citizen is **equal before law**, gets **equal protection**, suffers **no discrimination** and enjoys **no special privileges**.
- Dicey opposed the French **droit administratif** (officials subject only to special administrative tribunals).
- **India accepts (1) and (2) but reverses (3)**: in India the **Constitution is the source of rights**.
- Rule of Law in India rests on **Article 14**; it is a **basic feature** – **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**, **Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)**.
- Rule of Law operates in a **democracy** (not dictatorship, monarchy, autocracy); APPSC calls it the force that **moves the machine of democracy**.
| Rule of Law | Rule by Law |
|---|---|
| **Government subordinate to law** | Ruler above law; law as a weapon of state control |
| Laws just, fair, non-arbitrary | Governs citizens only |
- **Rule of Law Index** – released by the **World Justice Project** (not Amnesty, ICJ or OHCHR); measuring since 2008.
> The Rule of Law is British, not American: the Indian Constitution resembles the **US** in **Fundamental Rights** (Bill of Rights), judicial review and equal protection.

=== Article 17 – Untouchability
- **Article 17** abolishes untouchability and forbids its practice in any form (not Arts. 14, 15, 16, 18, 19 or 21). Adopted amid cries of "**Mahatma Gandhi ki Jai**".
- "Untouchability" is **not defined** in the Constitution or the Act. Art. 17 also operates **against private individuals**.
- Untouchability as a social practice = **boycotting**, **insult and oppression**, **exploitation** (not equality or integration).
- **Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955** → renamed **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** by the **1976** amendment (the title keeps 1955). A person convicted under it is disqualified for election to Parliament/State legislature.
- **SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989** – further law against caste atrocities.
- **False evidence (Sec. 3(2)(i), PoA Act):** a non-SC/ST person who fabricates false evidence so that an SC/ST member is convicted of a **capital offence** gets **imprisonment for life and fine**; if the innocent SC/ST member is **executed**, the punishment is **death**.
- MPPSC 2015: the Article through which SCs/STs are given fundamental socio-economic and cultural protection = **Article 17**.

=== Article 18 – Titles and Awards
| Clause | Provision |
|---|---|
| **18(1)** | **State confers no title** except **military or academic** distinctions |
| **18(2)** | A **citizen** shall **not accept a foreign title** at all – no consent route |
| **18(3)** | A **non-citizen** holding an office of profit or trust needs the **President's** consent to accept a foreign title |
| **18(4)** | No office-holder may accept a present, emolument or office from a foreign State without the President's consent |
- So the Constitution bans **both** the State conferring titles **and** citizens accepting foreign titles.
- **Abolishes titles = Article 18** (not 17, 24 or 25).
- **Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Shri** are given under **Article 18** (military/academic-type distinctions, not titles).
- **Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India (Dec 1995)**: **Bharat Ratna and Padma awards are not titles**, but must **not be used as prefixes or suffixes**; equality admits distinction of merit, not hereditary titles.
- Art. 18 helps **social equality** by ending artificial distinctions.

=== Types of Equality
| Type | Articles |
|---|---|
| **Social** | 15, **17, 18** – Arts. 17 and 18 provide **social equality** |
| **Economic** | DPSP **39(b), 39(c), 39(d)** |
| **Political** | **325, 326** – no exclusion from rolls, adult suffrage |
- **Economic equality is NOT part of the FR to equality.** Included: equality before law (14), social equality (15), equal opportunity (16).
- "**Political freedom without economic equality is an illusion**" – attributed in Indian textbooks to **G.D.H. Cole** (the idea goes back to Bakunin's "a pretence, a fraud, a lie").
- Preamble promises "**equality of status and of opportunity**".
- **Ambedkar**: liberty, equality, fraternity = an **inseparable trinity**.

=== Reservation Jurisprudence
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **Champakam Dorairajan** | 1951 | Madras **Communal G.O.** struck down under **Art. 29(2)** → 1st Amendment, Art. 15(4) |
| **M.R. Balaji v. State of Mysore** | 1962 | **Caste not the sole test**; reservation reasonable, generally **below 50%** |
| Janki Prasad Parimoo v. J&K | 1973 | Caste alone not the sole determinant of backwardness under 15(4) |
| **Indra Sawhney (Mandal)** | 16 Nov 1992 | 9 judges, **6:3**; upheld **27% OBC**; **creamy layer** excluded; **50% ceiling**; **no reservation in promotions**; ordered a permanent body (NCBC) |
| **Samatha v. State of AP** | 1997 | No **tribal land** leases in Schedule V areas to non-tribals/private parties |
| **M. Nagaraj** | 2006 | Upheld **77th, 81st, 85th** with conditions: quantifiable data on backwardness, inadequate representation, efficiency (**Art. 335**) |
| **Ashoka Kumar Thakur** | 2008 | Upheld the **93rd Amendment**; 27% OBC in central institutions |
| **Niyamgiri (Orissa Mining Corp.)** | 2013 | **Gram Sabha**'s role under the Forest Rights Act, 2006; Dongria Kondh |
| **Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta** | 2018 | **No proof of backwardness** for SC/ST promotions; **creamy layer** extended to SC/ST promotions |
| **Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil (Maratha)** | 2021 | Maratha quota struck for crossing **50%** |
| **Janhit Abhiyan** | 2022 | Upheld **103rd**, 10% EWS, **3:2** |
| **State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh** | 1 Aug 2024 | 7 judges, **6:1**; overruled **E.V. Chinnaiah (2004)**; sub-classification of SCs/STs allowed |
- Order: **Champakam → Balaji → Indra Sawhney → Nagaraj**.
- Creamy layer and the 50% cap **originate in Indra Sawhney (1992)**; OBC creamy layer **₹8 lakh a year (2017)**.
- Exceptions to 50%: **carried-forward vacancies (81st)**; extraordinary situations; **EWS 10%** (Janhit Abhiyan). No State legislature has free power to cross the ceiling.
- **Apr 2026**: Andhra Pradesh aligned its OBC creamy layer with the **central income limits**.
- **Aug 2026**: Centre **opposes** an income-based creamy layer for **SCs and STs**.
