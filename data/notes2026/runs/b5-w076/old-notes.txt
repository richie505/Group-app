## SUB 1 | Environment (Protection) Act 1986
[76:1:0] # Background and Nature
[76:1:1] - Trigger: **Bhopal Gas Tragedy (December 1984)**; gives effect to the **Stockholm Conference, 1972**.
[76:1:2] - Basis: **Article 253**. Purpose: **protect and improve the environment and control pollution** (APPSC 2025).
[76:1:3] - Called **umbrella legislation** – coordinates central and state authorities under the Water and Air Acts; the **Central Government** is the primary authority. Last amendment mentioned: 1991.
[76:1:4] # Key Sections and Powers
[76:1:5] TABLE head: Section | Content
  [76:1:5.0] 3 | Centre's powers – emission/discharge standards, location of industries, hazardous waste, public health; supports EIA and CRZ notifications
  [76:1:5.1] 3(3) | Constitute authorities
  [76:1:5.2] 5 | Directions, including closure
  [76:1:5.3] 15-21 | Penalties
  [76:1:5.4] 16 | Environment Protection Fund
[76:1:6] - Authorities under Section 3(3): **Central Ground Water Authority (January 1997)** – regulates groundwater development; **National Ganga River Basin Authority (2009)**.
[76:1:7] # Environment Protection Fund
[76:1:8] - In the **Public Account**; holds fines under the Air, Water and EP Acts; **75% to States/UTs, 25% to Centre**.
[76:1:9] - **Environmental (Protection) Fund Rules, 2026** notified by MoEFCC.
[76:1:10] Water Cess → Consolidated Fund of India; EP Fund → Public Account.
[76:1:11] # Notifications and Rules under EPA
[76:1:12] TABLE head: Notification | Key point
  [76:1:12.0] Doon Valley (1989) | No industry burning over 24 MT coal/fuel a day
  [76:1:12.1] Revdanda Creek (1989) | No industries around the creek
  [76:1:12.2] CRZ (1991) | Regulates coastal stretches; no ash/waste dumping; CRZ under EPA confirmed (Jul 2026)
  [76:1:12.3] Dhanu Taluka (1991) | Ecologically fragile; no power plants
  [76:1:12.4] EIA (1994, revised 2006) | Prior environmental clearance
  [76:1:12.5] Eco-Sensitive Zones; Plastic Waste Management Rules | Notified under EPA
  [76:1:12.6] Green Credit Rules (2023) | Voluntary tree planting on degraded land earns Green Credits
[76:1:13] - Green Credit afforestation can be swapped for compensatory afforestation under Van (S&S) Rules 2023; guidelines 16 Sep 2025.
[76:1:14] - **UPSC 2019:** EPA empowers the Centre to **lay down emission/discharge standards** – but not to "state the requirement of public participation" (2 only).
[76:1:15] - **CRZ 2011:** Sundarbans and other ecologically sensitive areas identified under the EPA are **Critical Vulnerable Coastal Areas (CVCA)**, managed **with coastal communities incl. fisherfolk** (both statements true).
[76:1:16] - **Recognised labs (Section 12):** EPA lets the Centre establish or recognise environmental laboratories; **EPTRI, Hyderabad** (state-government institute) is on the list of recognised government/semi-government labs.

## SUB 2 | CPCB, SPCBs and MoEFCC
[76:2:0] # CPCB
[76:2:1] - **Statutory body**, constituted **September 1974** under the **Water Act**; under **MoEFCC**; gives technical services to the Ministry under EPA 1986.
[76:2:2] - National level; deals with water (1974 Act) and air (1981 Act) pollution; an **enforcement body under the EPA framework**.
[76:2:3] - Functions: **coordinates SPCBs**, technical assistance, research, training, national monitoring.
[76:2:4] TABLE head: Item | CPCB | SPCB
  [76:2:4.0] Level | National | State
  [76:2:4.1] Chairperson nominated by | Central Government | State Government
  [76:2:4.2] Functions | Coordination, standards, research | Inspection, sample analysis, granting consents
[76:2:5] - Monitoring: **NAMP** – 1,646 stations in 603 cities (March 2026); **National AQI 2015**; **SAMEER app** gives real-time air quality (SAFAR is a separate forecasting system); **AUM Photonic** – indigenous real-time remote air monitoring.
[76:2:6] - **NCAP (2019)**: **40% PM10 cut by 2025-26** over 2019-20 in **130 cities, 24 States/UTs**.
[76:2:7] # MoEF / MoEFCC
[76:2:8] - **Department of Environment 1980 → Ministry of Environment and Forests 1985 → MoEFCC 2014**.
[76:2:9] - Bodies: CPCB (statutory), Central Zoo Authority (1992, under WPA 1972).

## SUB 3 | Air Act 1981
[76:3:0] - Purpose: **prevention, control and abatement of air pollution**; maintain **air quality standards** (APPSC G2 2025); Central and State Boards set and enforce standards.
[76:3:1] - Enacted **29 March 1981**; in force **16 May 1981**; modelled on the Water Act.
[76:3:2] - Structure: **7 chapters, 54 sections**.
[76:3:3] - The existing **CPCB got air functions**; SPCBs enforce in States.
[76:3:4] - **Noise** added to the definition of "air pollutant" by the **1987 amendment** (not 1991).
[76:3:5] - **Air pollution control areas**: declared by the **State Government** – Chapter IV, Sections 19-31; a special power of the Air Act, not the Water Act.
[76:3:6] - Emission standards for industry and automobiles. **Section 31A**: directions; Boards can levy compensation.
[76:3:7] # Water Act vs Air Act
[76:3:8] TABLE head: Feature | Water Act 1974 | Air Act 1981
  [76:3:8.0] Aim | Wholesomeness of water | Prevent, control, abate air pollution
  [76:3:8.1] Structure | 8 chapters, 64 sections | 7 chapters, 54 sections
  [76:3:8.2] Basis | Article 252 | Modelled on Water Act
  [76:3:8.3] Boards | Created CPCB and SPCBs | Same Boards given air functions
  [76:3:8.4] Special power | Effluent discharge standards | Air pollution control areas
  [76:3:8.5] Amended | 1988 (CPCB name) | 1987 (noise added)
  [76:3:8.6] Compensation | Section 33A | Section 31A
[76:3:9] - Common: both require **prior consent** of the Board to establish and operate a polluting unit.
[76:3:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment Part 6 p.8; NextGen Rocket Environment p.36; PMF Environment Part 6 p.62

## SUB 5 | Consent Mechanism and Industry Categories
[76:5:0] # CTE and CTO
[76:5:1] - Two-stage consent from the **SPCB**: **Consent to Establish (CTE)** before setting up → **Consent to Operate (CTO)** before production.
[76:5:2] - **29 Jan 2026 reform**: single application for Air and Water consents plus waste authorisations; **CTO valid until cancelled** (earlier periodic renewal).

## SUB 6 | Penalties and Jan Vishwas Act 2023
[76:6:0] - EPA original penalty: up to **5 years or Rs 1 lakh**, plus **Rs 5,000 per day** for continuing offence.
[76:6:1] - **Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023** **decriminalised** the EPA penalty regime – monetary penalties fixed by an **adjudicating officer**; appeals go to the **NGT**. The Water and Air Acts were also amended.
[76:6:2] - Minor offences decriminalised by amendment in 2024.
[76:6:3] - **PLI Act 1991** (amended by Jan Vishwas): penalty instead of imprisonment; penalty rates moved to the rules; Environmental Relief Fund money can go to CPCB/SPCBs for restoration; relief for death raised from **Rs 25,000 to Rs 5 lakh**.
[76:6:4] The 5 years / Rs 1 lakh / Rs 5,000 per day figures belong to the EPA; Water Act jail is 1.5-6 years.
[76:6:5] - **Economic instruments:** a **pollution tax** (polluter pays) is considered the most effective instrument of pollution control – better than subsidies, moral persuasion or common law.
[76:6:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment Part 6 p.61-64; app notes 5:76 sub 5
## SUB 7 | Water Act 1974 and Water Cess Act 1977
[76:7:0] # Water Act 1974
[76:7:1] - Purpose: **prevent and control water pollution** and **maintain or restore the wholesomeness of water**.
[76:7:2] - Passed under **Article 252** – applies in States that adopt it by resolution.
[76:7:3] - Created the **Central Board for the Prevention and Control of Water Pollution (1974)** – renamed **CPCB** by the **1988 amendment** – and the State Boards.
[76:7:4] - Structure: **8 chapters, 64 sections**; standards for **discharge of effluents**; prior consent of the Board needed before discharging effluent.
[76:7:5] - **Section 33A**: directions, including **environmental compensation**.
[76:7:6] - Penalties: **1.5 to 6 years' jail**. Rules: Water Rules 1975.
[76:7:7] # Water Cess Act 1977
[76:7:8] - Cess on water consumed by specified industries and local authorities – funds the Boards.
[76:7:9] - Proceeds go to the **Consolidated Fund of India**; subsumed in **GST in 2017**.
[76:7:10] - **Water (Amendment) Act 2024:** minor offences decriminalised – penalties Rs 10,000 to 15 lakh; Centre may exempt some plants from consent.
[76:7:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Environment Part 6 p.8-10; NextGen Rocket Environment p.37; PMF Environment Part 6 p.60-61

## SUB 2 | EIA – Meaning, Origin and Related Tools
[77:2:0] # What EIA is
[77:2:1] - **EIA:** systematic evaluation of the likely environmental impacts of a **proposed project before it is implemented** (studying the environmental effects of a project – not a census, pollution check or plantation); linked with **environmental clearance (EC)**; governed by the **EP Act 1986**.
[77:2:2] - **Global origin:** **NEPA, USA, 1969**.
[77:2:3] - **India:** **1976-77** – Planning Commission asked the **DST** to examine **river-valley projects** (first sector); **1978** – EIA first used; until 1994 EC was only an administrative decision.
[77:2:4] - **R2 Code of Practices is NOT EIA** – it is for **responsible electronics recycling**.
[77:2:5] # EIA vs related tools
[77:2:6] TABLE head: Tool | Focus
  [77:2:6.0] EIA | A specific project, before it starts
  [77:2:6.1] LCA (Life-Cycle Assessment) | A product or process across its whole life – cradle to grave
  [77:2:6.2] SEA (Strategic Environmental Assessment) | Policies, plans, programmes – upstream of projects (e.g. a transport policy)
  [77:2:6.3] Cumulative Impact Assessment | Combined effect of several projects in one area
  [77:2:6.4] Environmental audit | Compliance of a running unit, after the fact
[77:2:7] - **Cleaner production:** cuts waste and pollution **at the source** (process change, input substitution) – preventive, not end-of-pipe.
[77:2:8] - **Green chemistry:** **12 principles – Paul Anastas and John Warner, 1998**.
[77:2:9] - **Sector-wide EIAs** strengthen **strategic and cumulative** assessment.
[77:2:10] > Exam angle: EIA = project before start; LCA = product life cycle (APPSC 2025).

## SUB 1 | Ramsar Convention and India's Wetlands
[79:1:0] # Convention basics
[79:1:1] - **Convention on Wetlands of International Importance** ("waterfowl convention"): adopted **2 February 1971** at **Ramsar, Iran** (hence **World Wetlands Day = 2 February**); in force **1975**; secretariat **Gland, Switzerland** (hosted by IUCN); not part of the UN system.
[79:1:2] - Purpose: **conservation and wise use of wetlands** and their resources – the **only global environmental treaty dealing with a particular ecosystem**.
[79:1:3] - **Three pillars:** **wise use** of all wetlands; designating **wetlands of international importance** (Ramsar List); **international cooperation** – never a ban on all human use.
[79:1:4] - **Wise use** = conserve sites through an **ecosystem approach while allowing their sustainable use** (UPSC 2010) – not keeping them inaccessible or allowing only tourism.
[79:1:5] - **Montreux Record** (1990): Ramsar sites whose ecological character is changing from human interference. Indian sites now: **Keoladeo NP + Loktak Lake**; **Chilika removed (2002)** after restoration.
[79:1:6] - **Wetlands International** is a **global NGO** (a Ramsar International Organisation Partner), **not an intergovernmental body**; it works at ground level and uses field experience to advocate better policies (UPSC 2014 → "2 only").
[79:1:7] - **World Wetlands Day 2017 theme:** **Wetlands for Disaster Risk Reduction**.
[79:1:8] - **Ramsar Wetland Conservation Award 2018 (Wise Use):** **Fundación Global Nature, Spain** (presented at CoP13, Dubai).
[79:1:9] - Ramsar **CoP15** (July 2025): India's resolution on **sustainable lifestyles for wise use of wetlands** adopted.
[79:1:10] # India's Ramsar sites

## SUB 4 | CITES – Basics, Appendices and India
[79:4:0] # Basics
[79:4:1] - **Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora** = the **Washington Convention**: drafted after a **1963 IUCN** resolution, signed **3 March 1973, Washington D.C.** (hence **World Wildlife Day = 3 March**); in force **1 July 1975** (not 1985); members are called **Parties**.
[79:4:2] - Aim: ensure that **international trade** in wild animals and plants **does not threaten their survival** – a watchdog on trade in endangered species (not conservation of migratory species = CMS, nor heritage = WHC).
[79:4:3] - **Legally binding** on Parties, but it **does not take the place of national laws**. Secretariat **Geneva**, administered by **UNEP**.
[79:4:4] - Coverage: about **40,900 species – ~6,610 animals and ~34,310 plants** (2026; older books: ~5,000 animals and ~29,000 plants).
[79:4:5] - CoP meets **every 2-3 years**; species move between Appendices **only by a vote of the CoP**.

[79:7:0] # Convention on Biological Diversity (1992)
