=== NOTES 5:115 | Recent Trends in India's Disease Burden – Communicable and Non-communicable Diseases
## SUB 0 | Burden of Disease – Key Terms
[115:0:0] # Dual and Triple Burden
[115:0:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [115:0:1.0] Dual burden of disease | Rising rates of both infectious and non-communicable diseases; lasting communicable diseases (TB, malaria, dengue, diarrhoeal diseases) exist alongside rising NCDs (diabetes, cardiovascular disease)
  [115:0:1.1] Triple burden | Adds injuries (road accidents, trauma) to CDs + NCDs
  [115:0:1.2] Dual burden of malnutrition | Undernutrition + obesity (adult overweight)
  [115:0:1.3] DALY | YLL (years of life lost to premature death) + YLD (years lived with disability)
[115:0:2] - DALY does **not** add years gained through treatment.
[115:0:3] # Who Measures the Burden
[115:0:4] - **India State-Level Disease Burden Initiative** = **ICMR + PHFI + IHME**; part of the **Global Burden of Disease (GBD)** study.
## SUB 1 | Epidemiological Transition
[115:1:0] # Omran's Three Stages
[115:1:1] TABLE head: Stage | Feature
  [115:1:1.0] 1. Age of Pestilence and Famine (first) | High deaths from infections and famine
  [115:1:1.1] 2. Age of Receding Pandemics | Infectious deaths fall as sanitation and vaccination improve
  [115:1:1.2] 3. Age of Degenerative and Man-Made Diseases | NCDs dominate
[115:1:2] - India's path: a "**linear shift**" from infectious diseases to NCDs.
[115:1:3] # State-wise Transition
[115:1:4] - The "**Nations within a nation**" study (Lancet, 2017; data 1990–2016) placed states in **4 Epidemiological Transition Level (ETL) groups**: low, lower-middle, higher-middle and high.
[115:1:5] - ETL is based on the ratio of communicable-disease DALYs to NCD + injury DALYs; a **low ratio = high ETL** (more advanced).
[115:1:6] - India as a whole: NCD + injury DALYs overtook communicable DALYs in **2003**; the gap between state groups was **24 years**.
[115:1:7] - **High ETL**: **Kerala, Goa, Tamil Nadu, Punjab**. Karnataka and Delhi are **higher-middle**.
[115:1:8] - **EAG and North-East** states still carry a much higher infectious-disease burden.
[115:1:9] High ETL means the transition is MORE advanced (NCDs dominate), not a higher infectious burden.
## SUB 2 | Key Data – Mortality and Health Gains
[115:2:0] TABLE head: Indicator | Fact
  [115:2:0.0] NCD share of all deaths in India | 57% (Economic Survey 2025-26)
  [115:2:0.1] Leading cause of death in India | Cardiovascular diseases
  [115:2:0.2] World's single biggest killer (WHO) | Ischaemic heart disease
  [115:2:0.3] Life expectancy | 49.7 (1973) → 70.3 years (2023)
  [115:2:0.4] MMR since 1990 | Fallen 86%
  [115:2:0.5] Under-five mortality since 1990 | Fallen 78%
[115:2:1] - Most countries cut NCD deaths in 2001–2019, but India's probability of dying from an NCD **rose** – especially **women over 40** and **men over 55** (Lancet).
## SUB 3 | Communicable vs Lifestyle Diseases
[115:3:0] # Classification
[115:3:1] TABLE head: Communicable (pathogen-caused) | Lifestyle / NCD
  [115:3:1.0] Malaria, dengue, TB, typhoid, cholera, chikungunya, JE, Nipah | Diabetes, hypertension, obesity, heart disease, stroke, cancer, COPD
[115:3:2] - **Malaria** is communicable (Plasmodium, spread by mosquitoes) – **NOT a lifestyle disease** (odd-one-out PYQ).
[115:3:3] - **Coronary artery disease** – result of **plaque buildup** in the arteries.
[115:3:4] - **"Big four" NCDs**: **cardiovascular diseases, cancers, chronic respiratory diseases, diabetes**.
[115:3:5] # Vectors and Spread
[115:3:6] TABLE head: Disease | Vector / route
  [115:3:6.0] Dengue, chikungunya, Zika | Aedes
  [115:3:6.1] Japanese encephalitis | Culex
  [115:3:6.2] Meningococcal meningitis | Person to person by respiratory droplets, not mosquitoes; vomiting, neck stiffness
## SUB 4 | Emerging and Re-emerging Infections
[115:4:0] - TB, dengue, Nipah, Zika and avian influenza persist alongside NCDs – the core of the dual burden.
[115:4:1] - ICMR initiatives: **India TB Research Consortium**; **MERA** (malaria elimination).
[115:4:2] - **2026**: ICMR **wastewater surveillance** expanded from **5 to 50 cities**; tracks **10 viruses** including avian influenza, plus AMR.
[115:4:3] - Vaccination registration platform: **U-WIN**.
## SUB 5 | Lifestyle Risk Factors and Fats
[115:5:0] # Risk Factors
[115:5:1] - Main NCD risk factors: **unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, tobacco, alcohol**; also urbanisation and ageing.
[115:5:2] - **Trans fats**: bakery products made with **hydrogenated fats** contain trans fats; excess trans fats raise NCD risk – both statements correct (PYQ).
[115:5:3] - **Obesity**: excess energy intake + low physical activity → obesity and heart disease; sedentary life + energy-dense food → degenerative diseases – both correct (PYQ).
[115:5:4] - Overweight/obese (**NFHS-5**, 2019-21): **24% women, 23% men**.
[115:5:5] - **Blood pressure generally increases with age.**
[115:5:6] # Good and Bad Fats
[115:5:7] - **Trans fat** raises **LDL** and **lowers HDL** ("good cholesterol"); high HDL reduces heart-disease risk.
[115:5:8] - **FSSAI** caps trans fat at **2%** in fats and oils.
[115:5:9] - Excess **saturated fat** raises blood lipids and cholesterol.
[115:5:10] - Cook in **oil rather than vanaspati ghee**: oils hold unsaturated fats (mono- and polyunsaturated) that lower cholesterol and CVD risk.
[115:5:11] - **Sunflower oil**: suits heart patients; low saturated fat; more **vitamin E** than any other vegetable oil.
[115:5:12] - **Eskimos (Inuit)**: little heart disease despite a high meat/fat diet – rich in **omega-3** from fish and marine mammals.
[115:5:13] - **Linseed/flaxseed**: richest source of **alpha-linolenic acid (omega-3)**; stops fat buildup in blood vessels (also canola, soyabean, walnut oil).

  [115:5:15.8] Stearic | Meat, cocoa butter
  [115:5:15.9] Butyric | Butter

## SUB 7 | Mental Health and Road Injuries
[115:7:0] - **Mental disorders** (GBD, 2017): **197.3 million** Indians – about **1 in 7**; most common: **depressive (45.7 mn)** and **anxiety disorders (44.9 mn)**.
[115:7:1] - **Tele-MANAS**: 24x7 tele-mental-health support in **all 36 States/UTs** (Jun 2026).
[115:7:2] - **Road injuries** (MoRTH, 2022): **4,61,312 accidents**, **1,68,491 deaths**; two-wheeler users the largest group of victims.
[115:7:3] - **SDG 3** target: **halve road-traffic deaths**.
