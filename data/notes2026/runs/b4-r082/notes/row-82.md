=== Growth Conditions – Climate and Soil
| Crop | Temperature | Rainfall | Soil / site |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cotton** | 21-30°C (up to 37°C); **210 frost-free days**; hard frost harmful; subtropical | **50-100 cm** (light to moderate) | **Black (regur)** soil of the Deccan; light, well-drained, moisture-retaining; deep alluvium in the north |
| **Jute** | 24-35°C, hot and humid; kharif | **125-200 cm** | Fresh **alluvium** of flood plains; **Ganga-Brahmaputra delta**; low plain land |
| **Tea** | 20-30°C, frost-free | **150-300 cm** (150-250 cm in many books), evenly spread | Deep, well-drained, **acidic** (not lime-rich), humus- and iron-rich **hill slopes**; no waterlogging; below 1,800 m |
| **Coffee** | 15-28°C, **shade**; no frost or strong sun | 150-250 cm | Well-drained loams with humus; Western Ghats |
| **Rubber** | 25-35°C, hot humid, equatorial | **Over 200 cm** | Laterite of the Western Ghats; not delta flood plains |
| **Sugarcane** | 21-27°C, hot humid; 10-18 months | 75-150 cm or irrigation | Alluvial / black soil |
| **Groundnut** | Warm, tropical | 50-125 cm | Light sandy loam; **gypsum** (calcium) for pod filling |
- **Cotton** is mainly a **kharif** crop; "100 frost-free days" is **not** its requirement (it needs about 210); rainfall 50-75 cm in some books (R.C. Tiwari).
- Hot humid area, **200 cm rain, hill slopes up to 1,100 m, 15-30°C** → **black pepper**.
- **Lower Gangetic plain** (hot, humid, floods with fresh silt) → **paddy and jute**.
- Over 200 cm rain on sloping hills → **tea**.
- **Black soils** (moisture-retentive) → **cotton, chillies, tobacco**, jowar, oilseeds; red soils → groundnut, ragi, tobacco; laterite (with manure) → tea, coffee, rubber, cashew.
- **Sugarcane** = **least water-efficient** crop (1,400-2,300 mm subtropics; 2,000-3,500 mm tropics); millets, pulses and oilseeds are water-efficient.
- **Red chilli:** kharif, warm climate; Andhra Pradesh. Soybean needs a warm season – being an oilseed does not explain it.
- In **Maharashtra's black-soil belt** sugarcane competes with cotton because **irrigation** has expanded.
> Exam angle: 210 frost-free days and 50-100 cm = cotton; 150-300 cm, acidic slopes = tea; over 200 cm = rubber.
~~ Sources: PMF Human & Economic Geography 05 p.34 (200 frost-free days), p.67 (tea: 20-30°C, 150-300 cm, humus- and iron-rich soils, below 1,800 m), p.73 (coffee: 15-28°C, 150-250 cm); NextGen Indian Geography 08 p.6-7 (black soil: cotton, chillies, tobacco; laterite: plantation crops); Rocket Indian Geography p.81 (crop table); app notes 4:82 (sub 1)

=== Cash, Fibre, Food and Plantation Crops
- **Cash crops** (grown for sale): **cotton, sugarcane, jute, tobacco, oilseeds (groundnut/peanut), tea, rubber, bananas**.
- **Food crops:** wheat, rice, maize, **jowar, millets**, gram, peas – the usual "not a cash crop" answer.
- **Soybean** is a commercial oilseed too; in the tobacco–cotton–soybean–rubber set it is keyed as "not a cash crop".
- **Fibre crops:** **cotton, jute, sunhemp** (also flax/mesta); jute–wheat–cotton mixes a food crop.
- **Plantation crops** (tea, coffee, rubber, coconut): high-value, **labour- and capital-intensive**; colonial plantations (tea, coffee, rubber, indigo) were **European-dominated and export-oriented**.
- **Medicinal crop:** **aloe vera** (not sugarcane, cotton or mahua).
- **Tea** earns the most foreign exchange among tea, tobacco, flax and wheat.
- Tea and coffee are **substitutes**: if tea gets cheaper, **coffee consumption decreases**.

=== Tea and Coffee – Regions, History and Araku
- **First tea plantations:** **Brahmaputra valley, Assam**, first half of the 19th century (1830s, after Robert Bruce found wild tea), before Darjeeling.
- **Barak Valley** (Cachar, south Assam): **tea**.
- **Tea-producing States:** Assam, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, **Tripura, Himachal Pradesh (Kangra)**, Uttarakhand; **AP** only in **Araku** – UPSC 2022 official key counts **all four** (AP, Kerala, HP, Tripura).
| Area | Crop | State |
|---|---|---|
| Brahmaputra valley, Barak valley | Tea | Assam |
| Darjeeling, Dooars | Tea | West Bengal |
| Nilgiris | Tea, coffee | Tamil Nadu |
| Munnar, Wayanad, Idukki | Tea | Kerala |
| Kangra | Tea | Himachal Pradesh |
| **Baba Budan hills** (Chikkamagaluru), Kodagu | **Coffee** (first introduced; Arabica) | Karnataka |
| **Araku valley** (ASR district) | Arabica coffee | Andhra Pradesh |
- **Africa:** tea first grown at the **Durban Botanical Garden (1850)**; now Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda – so "no tea plantations in Africa" is false.
- Coffee varieties: **Arabica** and **Robusta**.
- **Araku coffee:** first retail store in **Paris** (August 2017); **Araku Valley Arabica coffee GI** (2019).
- **GI:** a sign on products of a specific geographical origin; **Darjeeling Tea = India's first GI**; GI protection is **not** granted through a "Paris Agreement and Protocol".
- India is a **major tea exporter** (3rd-4th; Kenya 1st) but **consumes about 80%** of its tea at home – domestic consumption is not poor.
- **Supreme Court:** **tea** is **not a "foodstuff"** under the Essential Commodities Act, 1955 (AIR 2005 SC 218).
- **Green Gold** = tea.

=== Sugarcane – Tropical vs Sub-tropical
| Point | Tropical (south) | Sub-tropical (north) |
|---|---|---|
| States | Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, AP | **UP**, Bihar, Punjab, Haryana |
| Yield and sucrose | **Higher** | Lower |
| Area | Smaller | **Larger** |
| Crushing season | **October to May/June** (longer) | **November to February** |
| Why | **Cool nights with clear skies, latitude, long sunshine**, no frost or loo, maritime influence | Alluvium rich in lime and potash, level land, dense market |
- **Sugar industry is shifting south**: higher yield and juice per hectare explain it (A and R true, R explains A).
- **UP** produces more cane and sugar than Maharashtra but has **lower productivity**; most Maharashtra mills are **cooperatives** (true, but not the explanation).
- **Frost** at ripening **lowers sucrose**; excess rain lowers sugar content.
- **Adsali** cane (Maharashtra): planted July-August, ripens in about **18 months** (normal crop 10-12).
- **Sustainable Sugarcane Initiative (SSI):** fewer seed setts (**low seed cost**), suits **drip irrigation**, more **intercropping**; it does **not** ban chemical fertilisers.
- Pest **Pyrilla** (leafhopper); disease **red rot**; variety **Co. 1148**.
- **UP = "Sugar Bowl of India"**; western UP is the mill belt; mills sit near fields because cane is **weight-losing**.
- **Pricing: FRP**, not MSP.
> Exam angle: "southern cane yields more – why" = cool clear nights, latitude, long sunshine (APPSC G-1).
~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Geography 10 p.10 (higher yields and sucrose in the south; crushing seasons); PMF Human & Economic Geography 05 p.64 (north vs south, shift south), p.63; app notes 4:82 (sub 4)

=== Oilseeds – Seasons, Varieties, Production
| Kharif | Rabi | Both |
|---|---|---|
| Groundnut, **soybean**, sesamum, castor, nigerseed | **Rapeseed-mustard**, linseed, safflower | **Sunflower** |
- **Rabi-only oilseed = mustard**.
- **Varieties:** mustard **Varuna, Pusa Bold, Pitambari** (yellow mustard); groundnut **Kaushal**, Chitra; linseed (not "Surya"); field pea Sapna.
- **Groundnut:** **pegging** (the peg enters the soil and the pod grows there); needs **gypsum**; came from **Brazil**; "poor man's cashew". **Punjab** – small area but very high yield (when asked).
- **Soybean** = "vegetable meat"; MP has the **largest area**.
- **Yellow Revolution** = oilseeds (not jute or pulses).
- **Order of output:** 1990s **groundnut > mustard > soybean > sesamum**; 2024-25 **soybean 15.27 > rapeseed-mustard 12.67 > groundnut 11.94 > sesamum 0.83** MT.
- **Import dependence:** farmers prefer foodgrains with support prices; oilseeds are mostly **rain-fed**; tree-borne oilseeds and rice bran remain unexploited.
- **India** = largest producer and exporter of **castor** (Gujarat about 86%).
- **MSP:** **23** commodities – 7 cereals, 5 pulses, **7 oilseeds** (groundnut, rapeseed-mustard, soybean, sesamum, sunflower, safflower, nigerseed) + commercial crops; coffee, areca nut, turmeric are not MSP crops.
- **Oilseeds 2025-26:** area, production and productivity all rose; **NMEO-Oilseeds** + **NMEO-Oil Palm**; **AP leads oil palm** (area, production, productivity).
