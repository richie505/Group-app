## SUB 0 | Merchant Guilds – Ayyavole, Manigramam, Nanadesi
[0:0] TABLE head: Body | Base / activity
  [0:0.0] Ayyavole-500 (Ainnurruvar) | Originated at Aihole (Ayyavolu), Karnataka; pan-regional and overseas
  [0:0.1] Manigramam | Corporation of merchants in long-distance and overseas trade (IAS 1997)
  [0:0.2] Nanadesi | "Of many countries" – itinerant guild in foreign trade (UPPSC 2018)
  [0:0.3] Ubhaya Nanadesi | Both domestic and foreign trade
  [0:0.4] Nagaram (Nakaram) | Local merchant assembly of a market town
  [0:0.5] Anjuvannam | Guild of West Asian foreign merchants
[0:1] - **Guilds** (shrenis, pugas) had their own code (**bananju-dharma**), symbols and militias, and acted as bankers.
[0:2] - **Independence:** as Chola power declined the guilds became **more independent**; they were not confined to local trade.
[0:3] - **⚠ Distractors:** Chaturvedimangalam (Brahmin village), Parishad, Ashtadiggaja (Krishnadevaraya's poets), Sreni (general guild).
[0:4] - **NDA 2018:** "Ayyavole originated at Aihole" ✓; "Manigramam was subordinated to the Anjuvannam in the 13th century" ✗ → **only I**.
[0:5] # AP angle
[0:6] - **Penugonda** (West Godavari) = central trade centre of medieval Andhra (**APPSC G2 2012**); Ayyavole and Manigramam had **18 branches** across Vengi.
[0:7] - Eastern Chalukya **Nakaras** were headed by **Shreshthis**; **Tagara** = trade centre between Kalyan and Vengi.

## SUB 1 | Temple Economy and the Chudamani Vihara
[1:0] - **Donations to temples:** **land, money and livestock** (cows, sheep for lamps), guild gifts and royal grants of paddy – **slaves** were not a donation category (CDS 2008).
[1:1] - **Chudamani Vihara, Nagapattinam:** Buddhist vihara built (**1006**) by the Srivijaya king **Sri Mara Vijayottunga Varman**; Rajaraja I granted it Anaimangalam village.
[1:2] ~~ Sources: NCERT Class VII Our Pasts-II ch.2; app notes 1:88 (sub 5)
## SUB 2 | Overseas Trade – China, Srivijaya, Ports
[2:0] - **China:** Chola embassies reached the **Song** court in **1015** and **1033**; **Kulottunga I** sent **72 merchants** in **1077**.
[2:1] - **Srivijaya:** friendly at first (Chudamani Vihara) → hostile under **Rajendra I** over the China route (1025).
[2:2] - **Guild records abroad:** **Barus (Lobu Tua)**, Sumatra – Ayyavole-500 Tamil inscription, **1088**; **Takua Pa**, Thailand – **Manigramam**; **Pagan**, Myanmar – Nanadesi Vishnu temple.
[2:3] - **Significance:** guild networks carried Chola trade and Dravidian culture across the Bay of Bengal.
[2:4] TABLE head: Port | Note
  [2:4.0] Nagapattinam | Main Chola port; Chudamani Vihara
  [2:4.1] Kaveripattinam (Puhar) | Sangam Chola port at the Kaveri mouth
  [2:4.2] Quilon (Kollam) | Kerala port; Arab and Chinese trade
[2:5] - **⚠ Trap:** **Kaveripattinam** was the early (Sangam) port-capital; **Nagapattinam** was the port of the later Cholas, not an early capital.
[2:6] - **West coast:** Arab merchants traded **horses and spices**; the **Anjuvannam** represented foreign merchants.
[2:7] - **Terms:** **Nagarams** = market centres; **Erivirappattanas** = protected mercantile towns; **Kasu** = gold coin.
[2:8] TABLE head: Exports | Imports
  [2:8.0] Textiles, pepper, spices, precious stones | Horses, Chinese silk, porcelain, camphor
