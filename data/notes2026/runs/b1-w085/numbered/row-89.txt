## SUB 0 | Chola Bronzes – Nataraja
[0:0] - **Technique:** **lost-wax (cire perdue)** casting; bronze images were already cast in the **Pallava** period (8th–9th c.), but the **Chola** period (10th–12th c.) produced the finest; queen **Sembiyan Mahadevi** was a key patron.
[0:1] - **Nataraja period:** the bronze Nataraja developed under the **Imperial Cholas** (APPSC G1 2002; APPSC G1 PYQ; CGPSC 2013 "Chola art") – not the Pandyas, Cheras, Pallavas, Gandhara, Mathura or Mauryan art.
[0:2] - **Greatest creation** of South Indian sthapatis: **Nataraja** (IAS 1993) – not Mahishasuramardini, Rama or Somaskanda.
[0:3] - **APPSC "cire perdue Nataraja" PYQ:** options lacked the Cholas; of those given, the **Pallavas** (earliest South Indian bronzes) is the defensible answer (Ikshvakus, Vishnukundins, Eastern Chalukyas did not make them).
[0:4] TABLE head: Element | Meaning
  [0:4.0] Four hands (Ananda Tandava) | Standard form (UPSC 1995; UPRO 2021)
  [0:4.1] Damaru (upper right) | Creation – first sound
  [0:4.2] Agni (upper left) | Destruction
  [0:4.3] Abhaya mudra (lower right) | Protection
  [0:4.4] Lower left hand | Points to the raised foot – liberation
  [0:4.5] Dwarf Apasmara under the right foot | Ignorance
  [0:4.6] Man's earring right, woman's left | Fusion of male and female principles
[0:5] > Statements (UPRO 2021): "four hands" ✓; "man's earring right, woman's left" ✓ → **both**.

## SUB 1 | Temple Life – Dance, Devadasis, Colleges
[1:0] - **Dance and music:** **Rajaraja I** appointed about **400 dancing girls** (with musicians and staff, paid by grants) at the Thanjavur temple; hymns of the Alvars and Nayanars sung in temples.
[1:1] - **Devadasis** ("female servants of God"): unmarried women dedicated to temple service, music and dance.
[1:2] - **Devadasis in the news (UPSC 1996 ×2):** the **Jagannath temple, Puri**, whose Devadasis (**Maharis**) served its rituals – not Pashupatinath, Kandariya Mahadev or Chausath Yogini.
[1:3] - **Vedic colleges:** Chola inscriptions record temple colleges at **Ennayiram** and **Tribhuvani** (near Pondicherry, South Arcot) (APPSC G2 2000; APPSC PYQ) – Chola, not Pallava or Pandya.
[1:4] - **Uttaramerur (Parantaka I):** important for the **kudavolai** election system.
[1:5] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 3 p.214-215 (OCR); PMF Ancient & Medieval India p.416; app notes 1:89 (sub 4)
## SUB 2 | Chola Temples and the Dravida Style
[2:0] - **Dravida style** = South India (APPSC G1 2008); reached its **peak under the Cholas**; continued under the Kalyani Chalukyas and Hoysalas.
[2:1] - "The **Pallavas laid the foundation**, the **Cholas** developed it on a monumental scale"; early Chola temples (**Vijayalaya's Narthamalai**, Pudukottai region) are of stone.
[2:2] - **Gopurams:** the mighty **gateways** of South Indian temples (State PSC) – not shikharas or mandapams.
[2:3] TABLE head: Temple | Builder | Feature
  [2:3.0] Koranganatha, Srinivasanallur | Parantaka I | Early Chola
  [2:3.1] Brihadeeswara (Rajarajeswara, Dakshina Meru), Thanjavur | Rajaraja I, 1010 | Shiva; granite; vimana 216 ft; capstone ~80 tonnes; largest and tallest in Tamil Nadu
  [2:3.2] Gangaikondacholapuram | Rajendra I | Vimana 180 ft, concave curvilinear profile
  [2:3.3] Airavatesvara, Darasuram | Rajaraja II | Horse-drawn stone chariot; Musical Steps
  [2:3.4] Kampaharesvara, Tribhuvanam | Kulottunga III | Late Chola
  [2:3.5] Sun temple, Kumbakonam | Kulottunga I | First of its kind in the south
[2:4] - **⚠ Trap (CDS 2009):** Brihadeeswara is a **Shiva** temple (huge linga; Tripurantaka) – "dedicated to Vishnu" is the wrong statement.
[2:5] - **Chidambaram** Nataraja temple: enlarged by the Cholas; **Chit Sabha** with **108 dance poses**; **not** in the UNESCO group.
[2:6] - **UNESCO "Great Living Chola Temples":** Thanjavur (**1987**) + Gangaikondacholapuram and **Darasuram** (2004).
[2:7] - **Order:** Pallava Shore temple → Brihadeeswara → **Gangaikondacholapuram** (latest of the three).
[2:8] - **Frescoes:** Brihadeeswara ambulatory – Shiva as **Tripurantaka**, **Rajaraja I with guru Karuvur Thevar**; Narthamalai paintings – Shiva and Parvati.
[2:9] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 3 p.207-215 (OCR); PMF Ancient & Medieval India p.420-421; app notes 1:89 (sub 1, 3)
## SUB 3 | Temple Styles and Andhra Temples

[3:4] - **Pairs:** Kailasanathar, Kanchi = **Rajasimha** style; Virupaksha = **Hampi**; **Dilwara** = Mount Abu (not Morena); **Dashavatara, Devgarh** = **Gupta**; Jagannath, Puri founded by **Anantavarman Chodaganga**.
[3:5] # Andhra
[3:6] - **Kapoteswara temple:** **Chejarla** (Palnadu district) – a Buddhist apsidal shrine, later Shaiva (APPSC Welfare 2017 ×2; APPSC PYQ) – not Nallajerla, Velpur, Srikakulam or Alampur.
[3:7] - **Draksharama Bhimeswara** = **Chalukya-Chola** style; **Padmakshi, Hanamkonda** = Kakatiya (not Pallava).
