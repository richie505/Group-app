=== NOTES 1:85 | Imperial Cholas – Vijayalaya, Rajaraja I, Rajendra I; Gangaikonda Title and Naval Expeditions
## SUB 0 | Naval Expeditions – Sri Lanka, Maldives, Srivijaya and the "Chola Lake"
[85:0:0] # Who conquered what overseas
[85:0:1] TABLE head: Ruler | Naval / overseas conquest
  [85:0:1.0] Rajaraja I (985–1014) | Built the Chola navy; destroyed the Chera navy at Kandalur Salai (Trivandrum); northern Sri Lanka (Anuradhapura) after defeating Mahinda V; the Maldives
  [85:0:1.1] Rajendra I (1012–1044) | Whole of Sri Lanka – captured Mahinda V and his royal insignia; Srivijaya expedition (1025) – took Kadaram (Kedah) and places in Malaya and Sumatra
  [85:0:1.2] Kulottunga I | Kalinga wars (land)
[85:0:2] - **Navy founder:** **Rajaraja I** was the first Chola king to create a naval army (CGPSC 2014; UPPSC 2004).
[85:0:3] - **Northern Sri Lanka:** **Rajaraja I** – made it the province **Mummudicholamandalam** (UPPSC 2014; "province of his empire" PYQ).
[85:0:4] - **"Conquered Ceylon":** **Rajendra I** completed the conquest of the whole island (IAS 2001 key); Rajaraja I had taken only the north.
[85:0:5]   - Sri Lanka stayed Chola for about half a century until **Vijayabahu I** drove the Cholas out (c. 1070).
[85:0:6] # The Srivijaya expedition (1025 CE)
[85:0:7] - **Leader:** **Rajendra I** (UPSC 2025; APPSC G2 2000, 2003; APSLPRB SI 2019) – not Rajaraja I, Rajendra II, Rajadhiraja, Kulottunga II or Rajaraja III.
[85:0:8] - **Target:** **Srivijaya**, ruled by the **Sailendra** dynasty over the **Malay Peninsula, Sumatra, Java** and nearby islands; Srivijaya was forced to accept Chola power.
[85:0:9] - **Cause:** Srivijaya controlled the **Strait of Malacca**, harassed Tamil traders and blocked the **China route** – the campaign was **trade-driven**, a punitive raid, not colonisation (Srivijaya was not annexed).
[85:0:10] - **Earlier ties:** friendly under Rajaraja I – the Srivijaya king built the **Chudamani Vihara at Nagapattinam**.
[85:0:11] > Statements (UPSC 2003, asked thrice): "Cholas defeated Pandya and Chera rulers and dominated peninsular India" ✓; "sent an expedition against the Sailendra empire and conquered some areas" ✓ → **both**.
[85:0:12] # Significance
[85:0:13] - **"Chola Lake":** **K.A. Nilakanta Sastri** – the Chola navy turned the **Bay of Bengal** into a Chola lake, after **Rajendra I**'s expeditions (UPPSC 2008).
[85:0:14] - **Naval power of the south:** the **Cholas** (UPPSC 2004 ×2) – not the Chalukyas, Hoysalas, Pandyas, Cheras, Pallavas or Rashtrakutas.
[85:0:15] - **Maritime trade:** the Cholas = the South Indian dynasty of **maritime trade with South-East Asia** and China (APPSC ASO).
[85:0:16] - **Firsts:** the Imperial Cholas were the **first Indian power to carry out overseas naval campaigns**; their contacts spread **Dravidian art** to South-East Asia.
[85:0:17] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval India p.403-405; Rocket Indian History #1, #11; app notes 1:85 (sub 3, 4)
## SUB 1 | Chola–Eastern Chalukya Marriages and the Chalukya-Chola Line
[85:1:0] # Marriages
[85:1:1] TABLE head: Chola princess | Father | Husband (Eastern Chalukya)
  [85:1:1.0] Kundavai | Rajaraja I | Vimaladitya – not Saktivarman
  [85:1:1.1] Ammanga Devi | Rajendra I | Rajaraja Narendra (1019–1061), married 16 Aug 1022
  [85:1:1.2] Madhurantaki | Rajendra II | Rajendra Chalukya = Kulottunga I
[85:1:2] - **Vimaladitya's father-in-law:** **Rajaraja I** (APSLPRB SI 2019 ×2).
[85:1:3] - **Rajaraja Narendra's wife:** **Ammanga Devi** (APPSC PYQ); their son = **Rajendra Chalukya (Kulottunga I)**.
[85:1:4] - **Statements PYQ (G2):** "Saktivarma married Kundavai" ✗; the other two ✓ → **only I incorrect**.
[85:1:5] - **Saktivarman I:** Vimaladitya's elder brother; ruled Vengi with Rajaraja I's help after **Jatachoda Bhima** was ousted (1002).
[85:1:6] # Kulottunga I – the first Chalukya-Chola
[85:1:7] - **First Chalukya-Chola king:** **Kulottunga I** (G2 2011 ×2; G2 2012; APPSC PYQ) – not Rajaraja Narendra, Rajendra I, Rajadhiraja or Vijayalaya.
[85:1:8] - **Born:** **Rajendra Chalukya** (counted as **Rajendra II** of Vengi), son of Rajaraja Narendra and Ammanga Devi; took the Chola throne in **1070** as Kulottunga (CDS 2001).
[85:1:9] - **1075:** after **Vijayaditya VII** (last Eastern Chalukya) died, he **merged Vengi** with the Chola state – uniting Tamil and Andhra kingdoms.
[85:1:10] - **Vengi viceroys:** his sons, titled **Rajapratinidhi** – Rajaraja (Mummadi Chola; commander **Gonka I**; installed **Anantavarman Chodaganga** in Kalinga), Vira Chola, Rajaraja Chola Ganga (crowned at **Jananathapura = Draksharama**; **Tekkali** inscription).
[85:1:11] - **Last Chalukya-Chola ruler in Vengi:** **Parantaka Chola**; then the **Mandalika Yugam** (**1076 to c. 1210**).
[85:1:12] # Kulottunga I's reign
[85:1:13] - **China:** sent **72 merchants** to China in **1077** (UPPSC 1992).
[85:1:14] - **Sri Lanka:** **Vijayabahu I** made the island independent; Kulottunga accepted it and married his daughter to the Sinhala prince **Virapperumal** (UPPSC 2012).
[85:1:15] - **Kalinga war:** sung in **Jayamkondar's Kalingattuparani**; abolished tolls (**Sungam Tavirtta**).
[85:1:16] - **Decline after him:** weakening central bureaucracy, growth of **local chieftains**, rise of the Pandyas.

## SUB 2 | Founder, Succession and Extent
[85:2:0] # Founder
[85:2:1] - **Founder:** **Vijayalaya**, a **Pallava feudatory at Uraiyur**, captured **Thanjavur** from the **Muttaraiyars** in **850 CE** and founded the imperial line (9th century) (RAS 2016; APPSC G1).
[85:2:2] - **⚠ Trap:** "founded by **Parantaka I**" or "by Rajendra I" is false (APPSC G1 statement; APPSC 2023 Deputy).
[85:2:3] - **Successor:** **Aditya I** (871–907) – defeated the last Pallava **Aparajita** and ended Pallava rule.
[85:2:4] # Kings in order
[85:2:5] TABLE head: Order | Ruler | Exam fact
  [85:2:5.0] 1 | Vijayalaya (850) | Founder; took Thanjavur
  [85:2:5.1] 2 | Aditya I (871–907) | Ended the Pallavas
  [85:2:5.2] 3 | Parantaka I (907–953) | Maduraikonda; Uttaramerur inscriptions; lost Takkolam (949) to Rashtrakuta Krishna III
  [85:2:5.3] 4 | Sundara Chola (Parantaka II) | Anbil plates
  [85:2:5.4] 5 | Rajaraja I (Arumolivarman, 985–1014) | Accession began the golden age; Brihadeeswara; navy
  [85:2:5.5] 6 | Rajendra I (co-regent 1012; 1014–1044) | Ganga (1022) and Srivijaya (1025) expeditions
  [85:2:5.6] 7 | Rajadhiraja I | Killed at Koppam (1053) on an elephant
  [85:2:5.7] 8 | Rajendra II | Crowned on the battlefield of Koppam; victory pillar at Kolhapur
  [85:2:5.8] 9 | Virarajendra → Adhirajendra | Won Kudalasangamam; line ended 1070
  [85:2:5.9] 10 | Kulottunga I (1070–1122) | First Chalukya-Chola king
  [85:2:5.10] Last | Rajendra III (–1279) | Defeated by Pandya Maravarman Kulasekara; Chola rule ended 1279
[85:2:6] - **Order often asked:** **Vijayalaya → Aditya I → Rajaraja I → Rajendra I**.
[85:2:7] - **Dominant power under Rajaraja I (985–1014):** the **Cholas** (Social & Cultural History of AP).
[85:2:8] - **Successor in the king's lifetime:** Rajaraja I named Rajendra I heir in **1012**; they ruled together for two years.
[85:2:9] # Extent and period
[85:2:10] - **Period:** c. **850–1279 CE** (not the 4th–6th century) – from the **Kaveri delta**.
[85:2:11] - **Extent:** the **Coromandel Coast** and parts of the Deccan (Tungabhadra–Krishna) (UPPSC 1991).
[85:2:12] - **Coromandel** comes from **Cholamandalam**; Tondaimandalam was the Pallava country.
[85:2:13] - **Capital:** **Tanjore (Thanjavur)** from Vijayalaya; later **Gangaikondacholapuram** (Rajendra I). **Uraiyur** = earliest (Sangam) Chola capital; Kaveripattinam (Puhar) = Sangam port (U.P. Lower 2009).
[85:2:14] - **Granary of South India:** **Thanjavur** in the Kaveri delta (WBCS 2020).

## SUB 5 | Battles and the Struggle for Vengi
[85:5:0] TABLE head: Battle | Sides | Result
  [85:5:0.0] Sripurambiyam (c. 885) | Pallava Aparajita (+ Ganga Prithvipati I, Chola Aditya I) vs Pandya Varaguna II | Aparajita won
  [85:5:0.1] Takkolam (949) | Rashtrakuta Krishna III vs Parantaka I (prince Rajaditya killed) | Rashtrakutas won
  [85:5:0.2] Kalidindi (near Eluru) | Rajendra I's army vs Vijayaditya VII + Kalyani Chalukyas | Cholas; Rajaraja Narendra built three Shiva temples for the fallen Chola commanders
  [85:5:0.3] Dharanikota | Rajadhiraja I vs Vijayaditya VII and the Kalyani army | Cholas; then burnt Kollipaka
  [85:5:0.4] Koppam (1053) | Rajadhiraja I / Rajendra II vs Someshvara I | Rajadhiraja I killed; Rajendra II crowned on the field
  [85:5:0.5] Kudalasangamam (c. 1062) | Virarajendra vs Someshvara I | Cholas
[85:5:1] - **Sripurambiyam:** the Pallava Aparajitavarman defeated the Pandyas there (APPSC G2 2000; APPSC PYQ) – not Peruvalanallur or Kanchipuram.
[85:5:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Takkolam was a **Chola defeat**.
[85:5:3] - **Cause of Chola–Chalukya wars:** control of **Vengi** (each side backed a claimant) and supremacy in the Deccan.
[85:5:4] - **Western Chalukyas expelled from Vengi:** they held it c. **1120–1137** (Vikramaditya VI); the Cholas recovered it **with the help of local chiefs** – the **Velanati Chodas** (Gonka II) (Social & Cultural History of AP).

=== NOTES 1:86 | Chola Local Self-government – Uttaramerur Inscriptions, Ur, Sabha, Nagaram, Variyams
## SUB 0 | Village Autonomy – the Chola Hallmark
[86:0:0] - **Special feature of Chola polity:** **autonomy of village administration** / autonomous village panchayats (UPPSC 1995; CDS 2000) – not mandalam divisions, monarchy, ministers or tax methods.
[86:0:1] - **Famous for:** **village assemblies** (RAS 1993); **excellent village administration** (UPPSC 1991); **elaborate local self-government** (State PSC) – the **Cholas**, not the Chalukyas, Cheras, Solankis or Parmars.
[86:0:2] - **Early Panchayati Raj:** "Chola village administration was an early example of the Panchayati Raj system" – autonomous elected village bodies evolved by the **Dravidians (Cholas)** (RAS 1997).
[86:0:3] - **Real powers:** the sabha, ur and nagaram were **elected bodies with real powers** over revenue, irrigation, temples and justice – not symbolic councils.
[86:0:4] - **Nature of the gram sabhas (Degree Lecturers 2011):** fully **autonomous**, **helped the public** (tanks, roads, justice) and acted as **bankers and trustees** (raised loans, held endowments) → **all of the above**.
[86:0:5] - **Historians:** K.A. **Nilakanta Sastri** – a remarkable "grassroots democracy"; **Burton Stein** – a **segmentary state**.
[86:0:6] TABLE head: Dynasty | Local government
  [86:0:6.0] Cholas | Culmination: elected assemblies, committees, lottery
  [86:0:6.1] Pallavas | Sabha and ur already appear – the base the Cholas built on
  [86:0:6.2] Badami Chalukyas | Highly centralised
  [86:0:6.3] Rashtrakutas | Village assemblies active in disputes

## SUB 1 | Uttaramerur Inscriptions
[86:1:0] - **What:** two inscriptions on the temple walls at **Uttaramerur**, a **brahmadeya** village in **Kanchipuram district**, Tamil Nadu.
[86:1:1] - **Date and king:** **919 and 921 CE** (c. 920), reign of **Parantaka I** – not Rajaraja I.
[86:1:2] - **Source of Chola local government:** Uttaramerur (APPSC G2 2000, 2003; APPSC PYQ ×3; IAS 1993; APPSC G1) – not Kudumiyanmalai, Gangaikondacholapuram, Kanchipuram, Thanjavur, Uraiyur, Silappadikaram, Tolkappiyam or Periya Puranam.
[86:1:3] - **Contents:** constitution of the sabha, **qualifications**, **disqualifications**, mode of election (**kudavolai**), committees, functions and the **right to recall**.
[86:1:4] - **⚠ Trap:** the **Thanjavur** inscription of Rajaraja I records military feats (Chera navy at Kandalur), not village government.

## SUB 2 | Kudavolai, Terms and Variyams
[86:2:0] # Kudavolai (pot-ballot)
[86:2:1] - 1. Village divided into **30 wards (kudumbus)**; 2. names of eligible persons written on **palm leaves**; 3. put in a **pot (kudam)**; 4. a **young boy** drew the lots in public.
[86:2:2] - **Term:** **360 days** (one year); removed at once for an offence; a person who served in the last three years was barred.
[86:2:3] - **⚠ Sources differ:** NextGen says committee members "served three-year terms" – the inscription fixes a **one-year** term and a **three-year bar** on re-selection (PMF).

=== NOTES 1:88 | Chola Economy and Merchant Guilds – Ayyavole, Manigramam; Overseas Trade with China and Srivijaya
## SUB 0 | Merchant Guilds – Ayyavole, Manigramam, Nanadesi
[88:0:0] TABLE head: Body | Base / activity
  [88:0:0.0] Ayyavole-500 (Ainnurruvar) | Originated at Aihole (Ayyavolu), Karnataka; pan-regional and overseas
  [88:0:0.1] Manigramam | Corporation of merchants in long-distance and overseas trade (IAS 1997)
  [88:0:0.2] Nanadesi | "Of many countries" – itinerant guild in foreign trade (UPPSC 2018)
  [88:0:0.3] Ubhaya Nanadesi | Both domestic and foreign trade
  [88:0:0.4] Nagaram (Nakaram) | Local merchant assembly of a market town
  [88:0:0.5] Anjuvannam | Guild of West Asian foreign merchants
[88:0:1] - **Guilds** (shrenis, pugas) had their own code (**bananju-dharma**), symbols and militias, and acted as bankers.
[88:0:2] - **Independence:** as Chola power declined the guilds became **more independent**; they were not confined to local trade.
[88:0:3] - **⚠ Distractors:** Chaturvedimangalam (Brahmin village), Parishad, Ashtadiggaja (Krishnadevaraya's poets), Sreni (general guild).
[88:0:4] - **NDA 2018:** "Ayyavole originated at Aihole" ✓; "Manigramam was subordinated to the Anjuvannam in the 13th century" ✗ → **only I**.
[88:0:5] # AP angle
[88:0:6] - **Penugonda** (West Godavari) = central trade centre of medieval Andhra (**APPSC G2 2012**); Ayyavole and Manigramam had **18 branches** across Vengi.
[88:0:7] - Eastern Chalukya **Nakaras** were headed by **Shreshthis**; **Tagara** = trade centre between Kalyan and Vengi.
[88:0:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams 3 p.204; app notes 1:88 (sub 0); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manigramam
## SUB 1 | Temple Economy and the Chudamani Vihara
[88:1:0] - **Donations to temples:** **land, money and livestock** (cows, sheep for lamps), guild gifts and royal grants of paddy – **slaves** were not a donation category (CDS 2008).
[88:1:1] - **Chudamani Vihara, Nagapattinam:** Buddhist vihara built (**1006**) by the Srivijaya king **Sri Mara Vijayottunga Varman**; Rajaraja I granted it Anaimangalam village.
[88:1:2] ~~ Sources: NCERT Class VII Our Pasts-II ch.2; app notes 1:88 (sub 5)
## SUB 2 | Overseas Trade – China, Srivijaya, Ports
[88:2:0] - **China:** Chola embassies reached the **Song** court in **1015** and **1033**; **Kulottunga I** sent **72 merchants** in **1077**.
[88:2:1] - **Srivijaya:** friendly at first (Chudamani Vihara) → hostile under **Rajendra I** over the China route (1025).
[88:2:2] - **Guild records abroad:** **Barus (Lobu Tua)**, Sumatra – Ayyavole-500 Tamil inscription, **1088**; **Takua Pa**, Thailand – **Manigramam**; **Pagan**, Myanmar – Nanadesi Vishnu temple.
[88:2:3] - **Significance:** guild networks carried Chola trade and Dravidian culture across the Bay of Bengal.
[88:2:4] TABLE head: Port | Note
  [88:2:4.0] Nagapattinam | Main Chola port; Chudamani Vihara
  [88:2:4.1] Kaveripattinam (Puhar) | Sangam Chola port at the Kaveri mouth
  [88:2:4.2] Quilon (Kollam) | Kerala port; Arab and Chinese trade
[88:2:5] - **⚠ Trap:** **Kaveripattinam** was the early (Sangam) port-capital; **Nagapattinam** was the port of the later Cholas, not an early capital.
[88:2:6] - **West coast:** Arab merchants traded **horses and spices**; the **Anjuvannam** represented foreign merchants.
[88:2:7] - **Terms:** **Nagarams** = market centres; **Erivirappattanas** = protected mercantile towns; **Kasu** = gold coin.
[88:2:8] TABLE head: Exports | Imports
  [88:2:8.0] Textiles, pepper, spices, precious stones | Horses, Chinese silk, porcelain, camphor
[88:2:9] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval India p.405, p.414; app notes 1:88 (sub 1-4)
[88:2:10] - **Cultural impact on South-East Asia:** India exported **Buddhism, Sanskrit and temple architecture** (Angkor, Borobudur) – but **not the hierarchical caste system**.

## SUB 0 | Chola Bronzes – Nataraja
[89:0:0] - **Technique:** **lost-wax (cire perdue)** casting; bronze images were already cast in the **Pallava** period (8th–9th c.), but the **Chola** period (10th–12th c.) produced the finest; queen **Sembiyan Mahadevi** was a key patron.
[89:0:1] - **Nataraja period:** the bronze Nataraja developed under the **Imperial Cholas** (APPSC G1 2002; APPSC G1 PYQ; CGPSC 2013 "Chola art") – not the Pandyas, Cheras, Pallavas, Gandhara, Mathura or Mauryan art.
[89:0:2] - **Greatest creation** of South Indian sthapatis: **Nataraja** (IAS 1993) – not Mahishasuramardini, Rama or Somaskanda.
[89:0:3] - **APPSC "cire perdue Nataraja" PYQ:** options lacked the Cholas; of those given, the **Pallavas** (earliest South Indian bronzes) is the defensible answer (Ikshvakus, Vishnukundins, Eastern Chalukyas did not make them).

## SUB 1 | Temple Life – Dance, Devadasis, Colleges

## SUB 2 | Rulers – Vishnuvardhana, Ramanuja and the Ballalas
[90:2:0] - **Region:** most of **Karnataka**, parts of Tamil Nadu; feudatories of the **Western Chalukyas** (early chief **Nripa Kama II**).
[90:2:1] - **Rise:** independent by the **close of the 12th century**; ended at the **beginning of the 14th** – none of Hoysala, Gahadavala, Kakatiya, Yadava arose in the early 8th century (UPSC 2023: **none**).
[90:2:2] - **Vishnuvardhana** (original name **Bittideva / Bittiga**, from 1108): fought Vikramaditya VI and the Cholas; took **Talakad** from the Cholas → **Talakadugonda**; built Belur (1117).
[90:2:3] - **Ramanuja:** fled **Chola** persecution; Vishnuvardhana converted from **Jainism to Vaishnavism** under him; queen **Shantala Devi** stayed Jain.
[90:2:4] - **Aug 2026:** a **Veeragallu** (hero stone) at **Neeragunda** (Arsikere) may settle the date of Vishnuvardhana's death.

  [90:2:5.2] Ballala III (1292–1343) | Beat Yadava Ramachandra's forces (1305); lost to Kakatiya commander Pedarudru after 1316; surrendered to Malik Kafur

## SUB 3 | Malik Kafur and the End of the Hoysalas
[90:3:0] - **Malik Kafur's victims:** Yadava **Ramachandra** of Devagiri (**1307**, taken to Delhi), Kakatiya Prataparudra (1309–10), Hoysala **Vira Ballala III** (Dwarasamudra, **1310–11**), then the Pandyas.
[90:3:1] - **G1 1990-91 key:** "defeated by Malik Kafur" = **Yadava Ramachandra**; Vira Ballala III also submitted, Rudramadevi had died in 1289.
[90:3:2] - **Wars:** constant Hoysala–Yadava wars – **Singhana** defeated the Hoysalas.
[90:3:3] - **End:** Ballala III was killed by **Ghiyas-ud-din**, Sultan of Madurai, at **Kannanur** (1342–43); Hoysala land passed to **Vijayanagara** (by 1346).

=== NOTES 1:91 | Yadavas of Devagiri – Bhillama, Singhana, Hemadri and the Hemadpanti Style
## SUB 0 | Dynasty, Capital, Rulers and the Kakatiya Link
[91:0:0] # The dynasty
[91:0:1] - **Name:** **Seuna (Sevuna) Yadavas** of Devagiri; first **feudatories of the Western (Kalyani) Chalukyas** (and earlier the Rashtrakutas); ruled the western Deccan (Maharashtra) c. **1187–1317**.
[91:0:2] - **Mother tongue:** **Marathi** (APPSC G2 2000; APPSC PYQ) – not Telugu, Kannada or Urdu; the Yadavas **encouraged Marathi**; **Sanskrit** = language of scholarship (not Telugu at court).
[91:0:3] - **Founder of the independent kingdom:** **Bhillama V** threw off Kalyani Chalukya rule and built **Devagiri** (c. **1187**) as capital (APPSC G2 2000) – not Prola I, Prola II or Ganapati (Kakatiyas), not Shankaradeva.
[91:0:4] - **Daulatabad:** name given to Devagiri by **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** when he shifted his capital there (**1327**).
[91:0:5] # Rulers
[91:0:6] TABLE head: Ruler | Exam fact
  [91:0:6.0] Bhillama V | Founder; built Devagiri; beaten by Hoysala Veera Ballala II
  [91:0:6.1] Jaitugi (Jaitrapala) | Killed Kakatiya Rudradeva; held young Ganapatideva captive, released him (1199) and married his daughter Somaladevi to him
  [91:0:6.2] Singhana II (c. 1210–1246) | Greatest Yadava; title Teluguraya Sthapanacharya; defeated the Hoysalas; son Sarangapanideva later served at Rudramadevi's court
  [91:0:6.3] Krishna (1246–1261) | Fought the Hoysalas
  [91:0:6.4] Mahadeva (1261–1271) | His attack on the Kakatiyas was repulsed by Rudramadevi
  [91:0:6.5] Ramachandra (1271–1311) | Ruler at Alauddin Khalji's 1296 raid; later a vassal
  [91:0:6.6] Shankaradeva (1311–1313) | Died in Malik Kafur's invasion
  [91:0:6.7] Harapaladeva (1313–1317) | Last ruler; killed in Mubarak Khalji's invasion
[91:0:7] - **Rudradeva's killer:** **Jaitugi** (APPSC PYQ) – recorded by Hemadri and confirmed by the **Paithan copper plate** of Ramachandra; not Narasimha Deva or Namadeva.
[91:0:8] - **Why Ganapati was freed (1199):** as a counterweight to Hoysala **Veera Ballala II's** push into the **Krishna–Tungabhadra** region.
[91:0:9] - **Rudrama's victory over Mahadeva:** recorded in the **Bidar fort inscription** (APPSC PYQ) – not Bayyaram, Chandupatla or Hanumakonda.
[91:0:10] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval India p.410; NextGen AP History - Kakatiyas p.45, p.47; app notes 1:91 (sub 0, 1, 2)
## SUB 1 | Scholars – Hemadri, Sarngadeva, Changadeva; the Hemadpanti Style
[91:1:0] # Hemadri
[91:1:1] - **Chaturvarga Chintamani:** Sanskrit encyclopaedia by **Hemadri** (APPSC G2 2000 ×2; APPSC PYQ) – on **dharma, rituals, charity, pilgrimage and vratas**; not Vidyanatha, Jayapa Senani or Palkuriki Somanatha.
[91:1:2] - **Office:** **Shrikaranadhipa** (head of secretariat and records) under **Mahadeva and Ramachandra**.
[91:1:3] - **On Kakatiya Rudra:** Hemadri claimed **Jaitugi captured and sacrificed Rudra**.
[91:1:4] - **Hemadpanti style:** credited to Hemadri – local **black basalt**, dressed stones **interlocked without mortar** (**dry masonry**).
[91:1:5] # Other scholars and works
[91:1:6] TABLE head: Person | Work / role
  [91:1:6.0] Sarngadeva | Court musician of Singhana II; Sangita Ratnakara – base text of both Hindustani and Carnatic music (not Hemadri)
  [91:1:6.1] Changadeva | Court astronomer of Singhana II; grandson of Bhaskaracharya; school at Patan to teach Bhaskara's works
  [91:1:6.2] Bhaskaracharya (Bhaskara II) | Lilavati, the mathematics treatise (Uttarakhand PCS 2010) – not Ramanuja, Kautilya or Amartya Sen
  [91:1:6.3] Dnyaneshwar | Dnyaneshwari – Marathi commentary on the Bhagavad Gita, 1290, Nevasa, in Ramachandra's reign
  [91:1:6.4] Chakradhar Swami | Founder of the Mahanubhava sect (not Dnyaneshwar)
  [91:1:6.5] Mhaimbhat | Lilacharitra = first Marathi prose
[91:1:7] - Later Bhakti saints Namdev, Tukaram and Ramdas carried Upanishad and Gita ideas into Marathi.
[91:1:8] ~~ Sources: app notes 1:91 (sub 3, 4); https://www.britannica.com/biography/Bhaskara-II
## SUB 2 | Fall of Devagiri to Delhi
[91:2:0] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [91:2:0.0] 1296 | First Muslim invasion of the South: Alauddin Khalji, then governor of Kara under Sultan Jalaluddin, raids Devagiri and defeats Ramachandra; promised the yearly revenue of Ellichpur but did not send it
  [91:2:0.1] 1307–08 | Malik Kafur: Ramachandra submits and is taken to Delhi, then restored as a vassal
  [91:2:0.2] 1313 | Shankaradeva dies in Kafur's invasion
  [91:2:0.3] 1317–18 | Harapaladeva killed by Mubarak Khalji; kingdom annexed
  [91:2:0.4] After 1323 | Malik Burhanuddin of Devagiri given charge of Andhra; Devagiri = the Sultanate's Deccan base
[91:2:1] - **Significance:** Devagiri was the **first southern kingdom to fall** to Delhi and opened the road to Warangal, Dwarasamudra and Madurai.
[91:2:2] - **⚠ Trap:** Kafur's 1307–08 campaign brought the submission of **Ramachandra**, not Harapaladeva.
