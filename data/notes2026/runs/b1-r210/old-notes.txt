## SUB 0 | Andhra in the National Movement – Background
[210:0:0] # Vandemataram Phase and Bipin Chandra Pal
[210:0:1] - **Bipin Chandra Pal** toured Andhra in **1907** (Vandemataram / Swadeshi movement) at the invitation of **Mutnuri Krishna Rao**, editor of the **Krishna Patrika (Machilipatnam)**.
[210:0:2] - Host at Machilipatnam (APPSC 2008 key): **Kopalle Hanumantha Rao**, who founded the **Andhra Jatheeya Vidya Parishad** there on **17 Nov 1907**.
[210:0:3] - His tour covered Visakhapatnam, Vizianagaram, Kakinada and **Rajahmundry** before Madras, where he gave five lectures on the Madras Beach (May 1907).
[210:0:4] - Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya took part in the Vandemataram movement and the **2nd Andhra Students Conference (1909)**.
[210:0:5] - Match: **Bipin Chandra Pal – Vande Mataram**; Kandukuri Veeresalingam – **Gadya Tikkana**.

  [210:0:7.0] Chirala-Perala (Bapatla taluk, Guntur) | Against municipal tax | Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya | 1921
  [210:0:7.1] Palnadu (Guntur) | Pullari (grass tax) / forest satyagraha | Kanneganti Hanumanthu | 1921-22

[210:0:8] - **"Glorious Decade"** of Andhra = **1921-1931**.
[210:0:9] - 1921-22 Guntur saw the NCM's most original methods in Andhra: mass exodus, officials resigning, no-tax campaign.
[210:0:10] # Link with AICC Decisions and the NCM Programme

  [210:0:11.0] Bezawada AICC (1921) | Brought Gandhi's programme into Andhra
  [210:0:11.1] Delhi AICC (5 Nov 1921) | Let provincial committees start civil disobedience, including no-tax → Guntur did
[210:0:12] - Chirala-Perala municipal tax fight and the Palnadu forest satyagraha were local forms of the same NCM call.
## SUB 1 | Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya
[210:1:0] # Life and Education
[210:1:1] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [210:1:1.0] Life | 1884-1929; born 2 June at Penuganchiprolu (Krishna district)
  [210:1:1.1] Education | M.A. (Hons) Economics, University of Edinburgh, 1916; Secretary, Edinburgh Indian Association
  [210:1:1.2] Meeting Gandhi | 1915, London – introduced by Ananda K. Coomaraswamy as "the coming man of your country"
  [210:1:1.3] Career | Resigned as professor, Rajahmundry Government College; founded Andhra Vidyapeetha Goshthi near Chirala (national education)
  [210:1:1.4] Titles | "Andhra Ratna"; also called "Ramadasu"
  [210:1:1.5] Volunteer corps | Ramadandu (1921) – not Shanti Sena
  [210:1:1.6] After NCM | Joined the Swaraj Party (C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru) as an Andhra secretary
[210:1:2] - **Ramadandu** supported satyagraha and civil disobedience; it built huts and dug wells for the people who moved to Ramnagar.
[210:1:3] Ramadandu = Duggirala (Chirala-Perala); Shanti Sena = Parvataneni Veeraiah Chowdary (Pedanandipadu).
[210:1:4] # Chirala-Perala Movement, 1921
[210:1:5] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [210:1:5.0] Place | Chirala and Perala, Bapatla taluk, Guntur district; weavers and small farmers (~15,000 people)
  [210:1:5.1] Cause | Villages made a municipality (proposed Nov 1919, formed Jan 1920); yearly tax rose from Rs 4,000 to Rs 40,000
  [210:1:5.2] Exodus | On Gandhi's advice, midnight of 25 April 1921: 13,572 of 15,326 residents left
  [210:1:5.3] New camp | "Ramnagar" (Ramanagaram) outside municipal limits
  [210:1:5.4] Self-rule | Parallel all-caste assembly and arbitration court; lasted about 11 months
  [210:1:5.5] Arrest | Speech at the Andhra Mahasabha, Berhampur (Sept 1921) → two-month ban on speaking → broke it → arrested 1 October 1921; tried by Rutherford at Srikakulam; jailed 1 year
  [210:1:5.6] Failure | Lack of funds and local Congress rivalry
  [210:1:5.7] Outcome | Municipality abolished in 1938 by Bezawada Gopala Reddy, Local Self-Government Minister, Rajaji ministry
[210:1:6] - Book: ***Chirala Perala Vanavasam*** – **Pisapati Narayana Sastri**.
[210:1:7] - 12 women jailed; **first woman jailed: Ravuri Alivelu Mangatayaramma**; **Duvvuri Subbamma ("Desabandhavi")** also arrested, but was not the first.
## SUB 2 | No-tax Movements of Guntur
[210:2:0] # Palnadu Pullari (Grass Tax) Movement, 1921-22
[210:2:1] - Region: **Palnadu (Guntur)** villages – Macherla, Veldurthi, Sirigiripadu, **Minchalapadu**.
[210:2:2] - Tax refused: **pullari** = grazing (grass) tax on forest land.
[210:2:3] - Leader: **Kanneganti Hanumanthu**, killed in police firing at **Minchalapadu**; backed by Unnava Lakshminarayana.
[210:2:4] # Pedanandipadu No-Tax Campaign, 1922
[210:2:5] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [210:2:5.0] Place | Pedanandipadu firka, Bapatla taluka, Guntur district
  [210:2:5.1] Date | January-February 1922 (when the first land-revenue instalment fell due)
  [210:2:5.2] Tax refused | Land revenue
  [210:2:5.3] Leader | Parvataneni Veeraiah Chowdary ("Andhra Shivaji") with his Shanti Sena
  [210:2:5.4] Shanti Sena | Decided by Guntur DCC, Dec 1921
  [210:2:5.5] Resignations | 18 of 19 village officers in the firka (29 in Guntur district), led by Chandavolu karanam Machiraju Ramamurthy
  [210:2:5.6] Backed it | Konda Venkatappayya, Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri
  [210:2:5.7] Urged restraint | Nyapathi Subbarao
  [210:2:5.8] Inquiry committee | T. Prakasam, Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao, Dantu Narayana Raju – praised it, advised waiting for Bardoli
  [210:2:5.9] Withdrawal | 11 Feb 1922 – Gandhi's message to Konda Venkatappayya reached the Guntur DCC → suspended
[210:2:6] # Government Response
[210:2:7] - Revenue Board member **L.T. Harris** blamed the Congress and was sent to Guntur.
[210:2:8] - **Rutherford** was deployed; his plan to **ban the Shanti Sena was rejected by the Madras Government**.
[210:2:9] - The government declined to accept the resignations of village officers; Gandhi's halt ended the campaign at its peak.

## SUB 5 | Earlier Rampa Rebellions (19TH Century)
[210:5:0] # Rampa (1879-80) vs Rekapalle (1879)
[210:5:1] TABLE head:  | Rampa (1879-80) | Rekapalle (1879)

  [210:5:1.1] Cause | Munsubdar Madhava Bhupati (made Munsubdar 1848) exploiting tribals and eroding the muttadars | Axe tax on podu raised from 4 annas to 4-12 annas in a famine; forest limits on podu

## SUB 7 | Rampa / Manyam Rebellion, 1922-24
[210:7:0] # Nature and Span
[210:7:1] - **August 1922 – May 1924** (about 1 year 9 months), Agency tracts of **East Godavari and Visakhapatnam** (**Gudem hills**).
[210:7:2] - Armed tribal guerrilla war, outside the Congress and non-violence, yet it worried the British more than the NCM in that region.
[210:7:3] - Method: **raided police stations** to seize British arms and used them against the British.
[210:7:4] # Chronology
[210:7:5] TABLE head: Date | Event
  [210:7:5.0] 22 Aug 1922 | Chintapalli – the first police station attacked; arms seized
  [210:7:5.1] Aug 1922 | Krishnadevipeta and Rajavommangi stations raided
  [210:7:5.2] 24 Sept 1922 | Damanapalli ghat: officers Scott Coward and Heiter killed
  [210:7:5.3] Sept 1922 | Panjerighat (Damanghat) – direct battle with British forces (APPSC 2012)
  [210:7:5.4] 6 Dec 1922 | Peddagaddapalem – first defeat, by the Malabar Special Police; Raju wounded at Lingapuram
  [210:7:5.5] 17 Sept 1923 | Gam Mallu Dora arrested
  [210:7:5.6] 7 May 1924 | Raju shot at Koyyuru
  [210:7:5.7] 7 June 1924 | Gam Gantam Dora killed – end
[210:7:6] - **Assam Rifles** and the Malabar Special Police were brought in to crush it.
[210:7:7] # British Officers
[210:7:8] TABLE head: Officer | Post / part
  [210:7:8.0] Rutherford | Special Commissioner to crush the revolt; exiled 55 helpers, warrants for 183
  [210:7:8.1] Goodall | Major in whose presence Raju was shot
  [210:7:8.2] Keirns | Arrested Mallu Dora
  [210:7:8.3] Scott Coward, Heiter | Officers killed at Damanapalli ghat
[210:7:9] Rutherford appears thrice: Rampa Special Commissioner, Duggirala's trial at Srikakulam, Pedanandipadu.
[210:7:10] # Latest Developments (AP Angle)
[210:7:11] - Agency district renamed **Alluri Sitarama Raju district**.
[210:7:12] - **30-ft bronze statue** unveiled by the PM at **Bhimavaram** for his **125th birth anniversary** (Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav).
[210:7:13] - **23 Jul 2026**: **Alluri Sitarama Raju International Airport, Bhogapuram (Vizianagaram)**.

## SUB 9 | Women in the Andhra Freedom Struggle

=== NOTES 1:212 | Revolutionary Literature in Telugu – Sri Sri, Abhyudaya Movement
## SUB 0 | Path of Telugu Poetry
[212:0:0] - Telugu poetry turned from the **nationalist** struggle to the **class struggle**.
[212:0:1] - 1. **Nationalist poetry** (Gurajada, Rayaprolu, Garimella)
[212:0:2] - 2. **Abhyudaya (progressive)** – 1930s-40s, organised by **Arasam (1943)**
[212:0:3] - 3. **Sri Sri's Shashtipurti, Vizag (Feb 1970)** – the spark
[212:0:4] - 4. **Viplava (revolutionary)** – **1970, Virasam**, after the **Srikakulam** struggle
[212:0:5] Abhyudaya = progressive (Arasam); Viplava = revolutionary (Virasam).
[212:0:6] # Three Phases Compared
[212:0:7] TABLE head: Feature | Bhava Kavitvam | Abhyudaya | Viplava
  [212:0:7.0] Meaning | Romantic poetry | Progressive poetry | Revolutionary poetry
  [212:0:7.1] Period | Before Abhyudaya | 1930s-40s | From 1970
  [212:0:7.2] Leading figure | Rayaprolu Subbarao | Sri Sri | Sri Sri (Virasam president)
  [212:0:7.3] Body | — | Arasam (1943) | Virasam (1970)
  [212:0:7.4] Theme | Love, nature, personal emotion | Poor, workers, social injustice, class | Armed change, class war, Srikakulam
  [212:0:7.5] Key text | Andhravali | Mahaprasthanam | Maro Prasthanam, Jhamjha
  [212:0:7.6] Form | Freed from rigid prosody | Free verse in spoken idiom | Songs, free verse
[212:0:8] - Progressive (Abhyudaya) writers called the romanticism of Bhava Kavitvam **"escapist"**.
[212:0:9] - Abhyudaya sought **gradual change** by bringing social consciousness into literature; Viplava called for **revolution**.
[212:0:10] - Progressive Telugu writing had appeared by **1933** (Jayabheri), ten years before Arasam was organised.
