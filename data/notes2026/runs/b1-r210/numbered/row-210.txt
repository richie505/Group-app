## SUB 0 | Chirala-Perala Movement and Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya, 1921
[0:0] # Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya
[0:1] - **Leader** of the **Chirala-Perala movement** (1921) – not Veeraiah Chowdary, Konda Venkatappayya, Prakasam or N.G. Ranga.
[0:2] - **Title:** **"Andhra Ratna"** – not Andhra Bhakta, Deshabandhu or Deenabandhu; also called "Ramadasu".
[0:3] - **Volunteer corps:** **"Ramadandu"** (1921) – not Shiva Sena, Girisena or Shanti Sena (Shanti Sena = Parvataneni Veeraiah Chowdary, Pedanandipadu).
[0:4] - **Life:** 1884-1929, born at **Penuganchiprolu** (Krishna); M.A. (Hons) Economics, **Edinburgh** (1916); met Gandhi in London (1915), introduced by **Ananda K. Coomaraswamy**.
[0:5] - **Education work:** founded the **Andhra Vidyapeetha Goshthi** near Chirala (national education); after the NCM an Andhra secretary of the **Swaraj Party**.
[0:6] # The movement
[0:7] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [0:7.0] Place | Chirala and Perala, Bapatla taluk, Guntur district – weavers and small farmers
  [0:7.1] Cause | The two villages were made a municipality; yearly tax rose from Rs 4,000 to Rs 40,000
  [0:7.2] Method | No-tax campaign (March 1921); Gandhi met Duggirala at the Vijayawada AICC (30-31 March 1921) and visited Chirala on 6 April 1921
  [0:7.3] Exodus | Midnight of 25 April 1921: 13,572 of 15,326 residents left their homes
  [0:7.4] New township | Ram Nagar (Ramnagar) outside municipal limits, with a parallel all-caste assembly (self-rule); Ramadandu built huts and dug wells; stay lasted 11 months
  [0:7.5] Arrest | Duggirala arrested after his speech at the Andhra Mahasabha, Berhampur (Sept-Oct 1921), for breaking a ban on speaking; tried by Rutherford at Srikakulam; one year's rigorous imprisonment
  [0:7.6] Failure | Lack of funds (the PCC did not grant even Rs 4,000), rivalry of Konda Venkatappayya and Pattabhi, no leader after his arrest
  [0:7.7] Gandhi's view | *Young India*: the municipality meant only more taxes and interference with liberty
  [0:7.8] Outcome | Municipality abolished in 1938 by Bezawada Gopala Reddy, minister in the Rajaji ministry
[0:8] - Book: ***Chirala Perala Vanavasam*** – **Pisapati Narayana Sastri**.
[0:9] - Women: 12 arrested; **first woman jailed – Ravuri Alivelu Mangatayaramma**; Duvvuri Subbamma was also arrested but was not the first.
[0:10] > Ramadandu = Duggirala = Chirala-Perala = Andhra Ratna; Ram Nagar = the township of the exodus.

## SUB 1 | Rampa (Manyam) Rebellion, 1922-24
[1:0] # Nature and span
[1:1] - **Leader and period:** **Alluri Sitarama Raju** (not Aggi Raju, Gantam Dora, Ambul Reddy, Velu Thampi); **1922-1924** (first raid **22 Aug 1922** – Raju shot **7 May 1924**) – not 1919-20, 1921-22 or 1925-26.
[1:2] - **Area:** Agency tracts of East Godavari and Visakhapatnam – the **Gudem hills**; base among the **Koya** tribe (with Konda Doras and Konda Reddis) – not Bhils or Gonds.
[1:3] - **Character:** armed tribal guerrilla war, **outside the Congress**; caused the British more trouble than the NCM in the region.
[1:4] - **Method:** **raided police stations to seize British arms and used them against the British** – not non-cooperation or open war.
[1:5] # Chronology and places
[1:6] TABLE head: Date | Event
  [1:6.0] 22 Aug 1922 | Chintapalli – the first police station attacked (not Rampachodavaram, Krishnadevipeta, Rajavommangi, Annavaram or Addateegala)
  [1:6.1] Aug 1922 | Krishnadevipeta and Rajavommangi stations raided
  [1:6.2] Sept 1922 | Damanghat / Panjerighat – the first face-to-face battle with the British army; officers Scott Coward and Heiter killed
  [1:6.3] 6 Dec 1922 | Peddagaddapalem – first defeat of Raju's forces, a surprise attack by the Malabar Special Police; Raju escaped
  [1:6.4] April 1923 | Rebellion resumed – Annavaram police station attacked
  [1:6.5] 18 Sept 1923 | Lieutenant Gam Mallu Dora captured – first major setback (so 1923, not 1924)
  [1:6.6] 6 May 1924 | Aggi Raju (Pericherla Suryanarayana) captured
  [1:6.7] 7 May 1924 | Raju captured and shot at Koyyur
  [1:6.8] June 1924 | Gam Gantam Dora killed – end
[1:7] # British officers and forces
[1:8] TABLE head: Name | Role
  [1:8.0] Assam Rifles | Two companies brought in to end the rebellion (paramilitary force) – not "Madras / Malabar / Pune Rifles"
  [1:8.1] Rutherford | Special Commissioner appointed to suppress Raju – a civil (colonial) administrator, not an army or Bombay police officer
  [1:8.2] Major Goodall | Officer in whose presence (on whose orders) Raju was shot
  [1:8.3] Jamedar Kunchu (Kanchu) Menon | Captured Raju
  [1:8.4] Bastian | Deputy Tahsildar of Gudem, whose excesses sparked the revolt
[1:9] - **Endu Padal** was a follower of Raju from Peddavalasa who wanted to become a muttadar – "Endu Padal – muttadar of Pedda Valasa" is the **wrong pairing**.
[1:10] - Associates: **Gam Mallu Dora** (later an MP, 1952), **Gam Gantam Dora**, **Aggi Raju**.
[1:11] - **Keirns** arrested Gam Mallu Dora (app notes); the Malabar Special Police were used before the Assam Rifles.
[1:12] > First raid = **Chintapalli**; first battle = **Panjerighat**; force that ended it = **Assam Rifles**; Special Commissioner = **Rutherford**.

## SUB 2 | Alluri Sitarama Raju – Life, Death and Memory
[2:0] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [2:0.0] Born | 1897; birthplace in most books and keys Mogallu (Moggallu), West Godavari – other accounts give Pandrangi (near Vizianagaram) or Bhimavaram
  [2:0.1] Title | "Manyam Veerudu" (Hero of the Jungle) – not Komaram Bheem or Karam Tammanna Dora
  [2:0.2] Training | 1921: visited Chittagong and met Bengal revolutionaries (a British report)
  [2:0.3] Stance | Influenced by the NCM but believed in force; not a Congressman
  [2:0.4] Death | Captured by Jamedar Kunchu Menon, shot at Koyyur on 7 May 1924 (Major Goodall)
  [2:0.5] Burial / tomb | Krishnadevipeta (Visakhapatnam), 8 May 1924 – not Mogallu, Chintapalli or Sankhavaram
  [2:0.6] Biographer | Erramalli Narasimha Rao
[2:1] - Andhra Patrika (8 May 1924) hailed him as **a great national hero** (exam key).
[2:2] - Nickname: **Chittibabu**.
[2:3] - Memory: Agency district named **Alluri Sitarama Raju district**; 30-ft statue at **Bhimavaram** for his 125th birth anniversary; **Alluri Sitarama Raju International Airport, Bhogapuram** (Vizianagaram).
[2:4] > Born **Mogallu** (exam answer) – shot at **Koyyur** – tomb at **Krishnadevipeta**.

## SUB 3 | Guntur No-Tax Campaigns – Pedanandipadu and Palnadu, 1921-22
[3:0] # Pedanandipadu no-tax campaign
[3:1] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [3:1.0] Place | Pedanandipadu firka, Bapatla taluka, Guntur district – "the" no-tax campaign of Andhra (not Chirala, Perala or Nellore)
  [3:1.1] Leader | Parvataneni Veeraiah Chowdary – "Andhra Shivaji"; volunteer force Shanti Sena
  [3:1.2] Origin | Grievances of village officers; 18 of 19 village officers resigned (led by Machiraju Ramamurthy); people refused land revenue
  [3:1.3] Period | 1921-22 (instalment of Jan-Feb 1922)
  [3:1.4] End | Gandhi advised suspension – suspended 11 Feb 1922; ended by March 1922
[3:2] - Others: Konda Venkatappayya and Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri backed it; **Nyapathi Subbarao** urged restraint; Congress inquiry by **T. Prakasam, Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao, Dantu Narayana Raju**; Revenue Board member **L.T. Harris** sent to Guntur; Rutherford's plan to ban the Shanti Sena rejected by the Madras Government.
[3:3] # Palnadu forest (Pullari) satyagraha
[3:4] - **Pullari** = tax on **grazing cattle in forest lands** (grass tax) – the "Forest Satyagraha", first in **Palnadu** (Guntur).
[3:5] - **Period:** **1921-22**. **Leader:** **Kanneganti Hanumanthu**, killed in police firing on **26 Feb 1922** (Minchalapadu).
[3:6] - **Unnava Lakshminarayana** went to Palnadu in **July 1921** to study the agitation against the forest laws (not Vedam Venkatachari, Ramadasu Pantulu or Swami Sitaram); arrested with Vedantam Narasimhachari.
[3:7] - Social boycott of officials at Macherla.
[3:8] - **Shanti Sena** was decided upon by the **Guntur DCC in Dec 1921**; the campaign was suspended after Gandhi's message to Konda Venkatappayya reached the DCC.
[3:9] # NCM background in Andhra
[3:10] - **Bezawada (Vijayawada) AICC, 31 March - 1 April 1921** brought Gandhi's programme into Andhra; **Delhi AICC (5 Nov 1921)** let provinces start civil disobedience, including no-tax.
[3:11] - **"Glorious Decade"** of Andhra = **1921-1931**.
[3:12] TABLE head: Place | Movement | Leader
  [3:12.0] Chirala-Perala | Against municipal tax | Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya
  [3:12.1] Palnadu | Pullari / forest satyagraha | Kanneganti Hanumanthu
  [3:12.2] Pedanandipadu | No-tax (land revenue) | Parvataneni Veeraiah Chowdary
  [3:12.3] Rampa | Armed tribal rebellion | Alluri Sitarama Raju
[3:13] > **Andhra Shivaji** = Parvataneni (Pedanandipadu); Pullari = grazing tax (Palnadu) – not Duggirala.

## SUB 4 | Agency Grievances and the Earlier Revolts of 1879
[4:0] # Grievances behind the Rampa rebellion
[4:1] TABLE head: Grievance | Effect
  [4:1.0] Madras Forest Act, 1882 | Reserved forests cut podu (shifting cultivation), grazing and forest-produce rights
  [4:1.1] Begar | Forced (unpaid) labour for road building – not voluntary or paid labour
  [4:1.2] Muttadari system | Extortion by muttadars
  [4:1.3] Tahsildar Bastian | Excesses of the local official
[4:2] - **Permanent Settlement was not a cause.**
[4:3] # 1879 revolts
[4:4] - **Rampa revolt (1879-80):** against Mansabdar Madhava Bhupati; leaders Dwarabandala Chandrayya, Ambul Reddy; crushed 1880.
[4:5] - **Rekapalli (Rekapalle) revolt (1879):** Bhadrachalam taluk – rising against the **forest laws** and higher taxes on podu after the area joined the Madras Presidency (1874); NextGen's PYQ list puts the 1879 forest revolt at **Chinturu**, a village of the same tract.
[4:6] - **Alluri did not lead the 1879-80 revolt** – he was born in 1897.
[4:7] - Details (app notes): Rekapalli's spark – axe tax on podu raised from **4 annas to 4-12 annas** in a famine; **Ambul Reddy** is linked with both the Rampa and Rekapalli revolts; **Dwarabandala Chandrayya** burnt the Addateegala police station; **Sullivan** removed Madhava Bhupati and the revolt was crushed in **Nov 1880**.
[4:8] TABLE head: Year | Revolt
  [4:8.0] 1794 | Vizianagaram – Vijayarama Raju killed at Padmanabham
  [4:8.1] 1845-48 | Gulugonda tribal revolt – Chinna Bhupathi
  [4:8.2] 1879 | Rampa and Rekapalli revolts
  [4:8.3] 1922-24 | Alluri's Rampa rebellion
[4:9] > Forest revolt of 1879 = **Rekapalli** (Chinturu in one key); Rampa 1922 = **Forest Act of 1882**.
