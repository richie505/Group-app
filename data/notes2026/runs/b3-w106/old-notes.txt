=== NOTES 3:106 | Frbm Act - Targets, Escape Clause, N.k. Singh Committee; Fiscal Consolidation Path
## SUB 0 | FRBM Act, 2003 – Origin, Objectives and Targets
[106:0:0] - **Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act** – Bill introduced **2000** (Vajpayee Government; FM **Yashwant Sinha**); enacted **26 August 2003**; in force (with Rules) **5 July 2004** (UPPSC 2008: 2003).
[106:0:1] - Aim: **fiscal discipline** and prudence (APPSC G2) by **reducing revenue and fiscal deficits** (APPSC Panchayat Secretary) and public debt; it concerns **both FD and RD** (UPSC 2006).
[106:0:2] - Objectives: **inter-generational equity**, long-run macro stability, eliminate RD, cut FD, debt management, **transparency**. Fixing the **repo rate** is NOT an FRBM objective.
[106:0:3] - FRBM is a **fiscal** reform – not part of the post-1991 **financial-sector** reforms (capital adequacy, NPA norms, SARFAESI) (RAS 2024).
[106:0:4] - First Budget of India: **James Wilson, 1860**.
[106:0:5] # Original Targets
[106:0:6] TABLE head: Target | Detail
  [106:0:6.0] Fiscal deficit | 3% of GDP – by 31 March 2008 in the Act, later 31 March 2009 (APPSC AE: 3 percent)
  [106:0:6.1] Revenue deficit | Eliminated – 31 March 2008, revised to 31 March 2009
  [106:0:6.2] Annual cut (Rules 2004) | FD 0.3%, RD 0.5% of GDP a year
  [106:0:6.3] Guarantees | Extra guarantees on Consolidated Fund ≤ 0.5% of GDP a year
  [106:0:6.4] RBI | Centre not to borrow from RBI except WMA; RBI out of primary G-Sec issues from 2006-07
[106:0:7] - **Elimination of primary deficit** was **never** an FRBM target (UPSC 2010).
[106:0:8] - A public-debt cap of **50% of GDP by 2011** was in the **FRBM Bill, 2000**, not in the Act as passed (WBCS 2017 key: "all of the above").
[106:0:9] - **Tax-GDP** is not a legal target; it is one of the **four indicators** in the MTFP Statement (RD, FD, tax-GDP, total outstanding liabilities).
[106:0:10] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.18.17-18.18 (Bill 2000, enacted 26 Aug 2003, in force 5 July 2004, RD 2008 → 2009, 0.5/0.3 cuts, WMA, RBI 2006-07); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.6-7 (introduced 2000, passed 2003, FD 3%, RD 0%, four indicators); app notes 3:106 sub 0; 50% debt cap of the 2000 Bill: general knowledge, not checked
## SUB 1 | What It Prescribes and Does Not; Statements Laid
[106:1:0] TABLE head: Prescribes | Does NOT
  [106:1:0.0] Limits for fiscal and revenue deficits | Penal provisions for missing targets
  [106:1:0.1] Statements laid with the Budget | Make the Centre answerable for States' failures
  [106:1:0.2] Disclosures (borrowings, contingent liabilities), quarterly review by FM | Apply to States – they have their own FR Acts
[106:1:1] - "FRBM does NOT provide penal provisions" – the APPSC G2 2017 key; it also does not apply at State level.
[106:1:2] - Under the Act it is mandatory for the Centre to cut FD to 3% and phase out RD by **March 2009** and to keep transparency/limit RBI borrowing – but **not to include States' failures in its own targets** (APPSC G2 Mains 2017).
[106:1:3] TABLE head: Statement (Sec 3) | Content
  [106:1:3.0] Macro-Economic Framework | Growth, inflation, fiscal and external balance, assumptions – mandated by the FRBM Act, not by Art. 112/113 or convention (UPSC 2020 ×2)
  [106:1:3.1] Medium-Term Fiscal Policy | 3-year rolling targets for RD, FD, tax-GDP, total outstanding liabilities
  [106:1:3.2] Fiscal Policy Strategy | Tax, spending, borrowing strategy
  [106:1:3.3] Medium-Term Expenditure Framework | 3-year expenditure ceilings; added 2012, laid after the Budget
[106:1:4] - The **Annual Financial Statement** is laid under **Art. 112**, not under the FRBM Act.
[106:1:5] - Rolling targets **suspended since 2021-22**. Budget 2026-27 laid the MEFS and a combined MTFPS-cum-FPSS.
[106:1:6] - Significance: made **fiscal discipline a legal duty** and added disclosure statements to the Budget.
[106:1:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.10-11 (three statements); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.6-7 (statements, four indicators); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.18.18 (statements, quarterly review); app notes 3:106 sub 1, 2
## SUB 2 | N.K. Singh Review Committee and the 2018 Amendment
[106:2:0] - Announced Budget **2016-17**; formed **May 2016**; **five members**; report **January 2017**; reflected in Budget **2017-18**.
[106:2:1] - Chair **N.K. Singh**; members included **Sumit Bose, Urjit Patel, Rathin Roy** (APPSC G2 Mains 2017).
[106:2:2] TABLE head: Recommendation | Value
  [106:2:2.0] Principal anchor | Debt-to-GDP; FD = operational target
  [106:2:2.1] General govt debt | 60% by 2023 = Centre 40% + States 20%
  [106:2:2.2] FD | 3% for the next three years (to 2019-20/March 2020), then 2.5% by 2022-23 (APPSC Fisheries; G2 Mains 2019)
  [106:2:2.3] RD | 0.8% by 2023
  [106:2:2.4] Institution | Independent, advisory Fiscal Council (no binding powers)
  [106:2:2.5] Escape clause | Deviation up to 0.5% of GDP; trigger includes far-reaching structural reforms
[106:2:3] # 2018 Amendment (Finance Act 2018; Budget 2018-19)
[106:2:4] - **Debt anchor**: general govt **60%**, Centre **40%** by 31 March 2025; **FD 3% by 31 March 2021**.
[106:2:5] - Government targets **debt and fiscal deficit** simultaneously and **does away with the revenue deficit (and ERD) target** (APPSC G2 Mains 2019).
[106:2:6] - **Escape grounds**: national security, war, **national calamity**, agricultural collapse, structural reforms, sharp output fall – real growth **3 percentage points below** the average of the previous four quarters. Not a routine tax shortfall.
[106:2:7] - **Limit**: deviation **≤ 0.5% of GDP** a year; government must place a **justification and a path back** before Parliament.
[106:2:8] - **Buoyancy clause**: sharp rise in growth requires an FD cut of **at least 0.25%** of GDP.
[106:2:9] - Monetised RBI borrowing allowed only on escape-clause grounds.
[106:2:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.7 (5-member committee; debt 60 = 40 + 20; 3% FD three years; escape 0.5%); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.18.19 (report Jan 2017, debt rule, glide path, escape clause, structural reforms trigger); app notes 3:106 sub 4, 5, 6
## SUB 3 | State Fiscal Laws and Andhra Pradesh
[106:3:0] - States passed Fiscal Responsibility Laws after the **12th FC** tied debt relief to them (**Debt Consolidation and Relief Facility**); the Union FRBM does not apply to States – they operate under **their own** FR legislation.
[106:3:1] - **AP FRBM Act, 2005**: **eliminate RD**, **FD 3% of GSDP**; amended 2010, 2011, **2020** (raised RD limits), 2021. AP FRBM limits restrain AP's **open-market borrowing**.
[106:3:2] - After bifurcation AP **could not meet** its FRBM targets: CAG put AP's 2014-15 FD at **₹31,717 crore = 6.10% of GSDP** (2 June 2014-31 March 2015 accounts), double the 3% ceiling.
[106:3:3] - AP Budget 2019-20 (12 July 2019): FD **₹35,261 crore, about 3.30% of GSDP** (PRS: 3.26%); RD 0.16%.
[106:3:4] - AP BE 2026-27: **FD 3.84%**, RD **1.11%** of GSDP. Slippage: FD 5.05% vs 4% ceiling in 2024-25.
[106:3:5] TABLE head: Year (15th FC) | States' net borrowing limit (% GSDP)
  [106:3:5.0] 2021-22 | 4
  [106:3:5.1] 2022-23 | 3.5
  [106:3:5.2] 2023-24 to 2025-26 | 3 + 0.5 for power-sector reforms
[106:3:6] - **14th FC** horizontal criteria: population 1971 and **2011**, area, **forest cover**, income distance – **fiscal discipline was dropped** (it was a 13th FC criterion).
[106:3:7] ~~ Sources: millenniumpost.in "Andhra fails to achieve fiscal reform target in FY15: CAG" (6.10%); prsindia.org Andhra Pradesh Budget Analysis 2019-20 (web search result, 3.26%); app notes 3:106 sub 8; 14th FC criteria: general knowledge, not checked
## SUB 4 | Kelkar Task Force, Amendments and the Consolidation Path
[106:4:0] - **Task Force on Implementation of the FRBM Act (2004)**, chaired by **Vijay Kelkar** – meet targets through **tax reform**: wider base + **comprehensive GST**. Kelkar later headed the **13th Finance Commission**.
[106:4:1] TABLE head: Year | Change
  [106:4:1.0] 2004 | Rules notified; RD (and FD) deadline moved to 2009
  [106:4:1.1] 2008-09 | Targets suspended after the global financial crisis (stimulus)
  [106:4:1.2] 2012 | ERD target and MTEF Statement added
  [106:4:1.3] 2015 | Dates deferred (FD 3% by 2017-18)
  [106:4:1.4] 2018 | Debt anchor, escape and buoyancy clauses; RD/ERD targets dropped
  [106:4:1.5] 2020-21 | COVID: escape clause used; FD 9.2%; no amendment
[106:4:2] - Government's deviation statement admitted missing the **3% FD** and **40% Central debt** targets.
[106:4:3] TABLE head: Year | FD (% GDP)
  [106:4:3.0] 2020-21 | 9.2 (pandemic peak)
  [106:4:3.1] 2024-25 | 4.8
  [106:4:3.2] 2025-26 | 4.4 – "below 4.5%" target (set FY 2021-22) met
  [106:4:3.3] 2026-27 BE | 4.3
[106:4:4] - **Halving commitment** (9.2% in five years) met at **4.4% in 2025-26** – a commitment, not a legislative target.
[106:4:5] - **New debt rule (Budget 2026-27)**: FD set yearly to bring Central debt to **50 ± 1% by 31 March 2031** (**55.6%** BE 2026-27, 56.1% RE 2025-26); above FRBM's 40%.
[106:4:6] - **16th FC**: Centre FD **3.5% by 2030-31**; States **3% of GSDP**; State fiscal rules "**effectively unenforced**"; wider deficit definition including **off-budget borrowing**.
[106:4:7] - Criticism: frequent escape-clause use, deferrals, no penalty, **no fiscal council set up yet**.
[106:4:8] ~~ Sources: Union Budget 2026-27 Speech para 92-94 (50 ± 1% by 2030-31, 55.6%, below 4.5% met, 4.3%); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.18.18 (2009 revision); app notes 3:106 sub 3, 5, 7

## SUB 1 | Receipts, Expenditure and Deficits – Classification
[107:1:0] # Receipts
[107:1:1] TABLE head: Basis | Revenue receipts | Capital receipts
  [107:1:1.0] Liability | Create no liability | Create a liability
  [107:1:1.1] Assets | Do not reduce assets | Or reduce assets
  [107:1:1.2] Examples | Tax: income, corporation tax, GST, customs, excise; non-tax: interest receipts, dividends and profits, fees, fines, grants | Borrowings (market loans, T-Bills, World Bank loans), small savings/NSC, recovery of loans, disinvestment
[107:1:2] - **Order of classification:** **Revenue receipts → Tax revenue → Non-tax revenue → Capital receipts** (APPSC: A → C → D → B; the question was later deleted).
[107:1:3] - **UPSC 2025:** capital receipts create a liability or reduce assets (I ✓); borrowings and disinvestment are capital receipts (II ✓); **interest received** is **non-tax revenue**, it creates no liability (III ✗) → **I and II only**.
[107:1:4] - **Disinvestment** = capital receipt (reduces assets), **not** a revenue receipt (CDS 2017).
[107:1:5] - **Small savings** – not in the revenue account; debt receipts in the capital account (RAS 2018).
[107:1:6] - **Current (revenue) account income:** **corporation tax, profits of public enterprises, excise duties**; NSC sales and World Bank loans are capital (UPPCS 1998).
[107:1:7] - **Non-tax revenue of the Centre:** mainly interest, dividends and profits, fees; among mines, forests, opium and roads, the biggest head is **Roads and Bridges** (national-highway fees: ₹3,681 crore in 2012-13 BE, against forestry ₹23 crore, non-ferrous mining ₹27.5 crore); mines and forests yield mainly to the **States**.
[107:1:8] - **Paper taxes:** wealth tax, gift tax, estate duty – never large, now abolished.
[107:1:9] # Expenditure
[107:1:10] TABLE head: Pair | Distinction
  [107:1:10.0] Revenue vs capital | Revenue creates no asset (salaries, pensions, interest, subsidies, grants); capital creates assets or cuts liabilities (capital works, loans given, debt repayment)
  [107:1:10.1] Plan vs non-plan (to 2016-17) | Non-plan = interest, salaries, pensions, defence, subsidies, public administration (WBCS 2018)
  [107:1:10.2] Developmental vs non-developmental | Development = economic and social services (railways, power, S&T, education, health, grants to States); defence = non-development
[107:1:11] - **Interest payments** = **largest non-plan item (2015-16)** and the **most important revenue-account expenditure** (UPPCS 2008, 2015); still the largest single item of spending.
[107:1:12] - **Plan/non-plan ended 2017-18** (revenue/capital only), not 2010-11.
[107:1:13] - **Capital receipts < revenue receipts** – in 2010-11 and now (2026-27 BE: revenue ₹35.33 lakh crore, capital ₹18.14 lakh crore).
[107:1:14] # Deficits
[107:1:15] - **Budget (budgetary) deficit** = **total expenditure − total receipts** (all receipts, revenue + capital); discontinued from **1996-97**.
[107:1:16] - **Fiscal deficit** = total expenditure − receipts excluding borrowings; **primary deficit** = FD − interest; **revenue deficit** = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts.
[107:1:17] - Worked: FD ₹80,000 crore − interest ₹25,000 crore = **PD ₹55,000 crore**.
[107:1:18] - **Golden rule:** borrow only to invest, not to finance revenue spending.

## SUB 3 | Per Capita Income – Concept
[110:3:0] - **PCI = NSDP ÷ population** (net); **standard of living** is shown by PCI.
[110:3:1] - PCI **rises only when income grows faster than population**; it **can fall in a developing country even as national income rises** when **population grows faster than income** (A and R true, R explains A).
[110:3:2] - PCI is an **average**: it **hides inequality** and cost-of-living differences.
[110:3:3] - Per capita GSDP and PCI are **not interchangeable** terms.
