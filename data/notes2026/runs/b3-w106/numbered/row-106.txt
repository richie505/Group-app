## SUB 0 | FRBM Act, 2003 – Origin, Objectives and Targets
[0:0] - **Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act** – Bill introduced **2000** (Vajpayee Government; FM **Yashwant Sinha**); enacted **26 August 2003**; in force (with Rules) **5 July 2004** (UPPSC 2008: 2003).
[0:1] - Aim: **fiscal discipline** and prudence (APPSC G2) by **reducing revenue and fiscal deficits** (APPSC Panchayat Secretary) and public debt; it concerns **both FD and RD** (UPSC 2006).
[0:2] - Objectives: **inter-generational equity**, long-run macro stability, eliminate RD, cut FD, debt management, **transparency**. Fixing the **repo rate** is NOT an FRBM objective.
[0:3] - FRBM is a **fiscal** reform – not part of the post-1991 **financial-sector** reforms (capital adequacy, NPA norms, SARFAESI) (RAS 2024).
[0:4] - First Budget of India: **James Wilson, 1860**.
[0:5] # Original Targets
[0:6] TABLE head: Target | Detail
  [0:6.0] Fiscal deficit | 3% of GDP – by 31 March 2008 in the Act, later 31 March 2009 (APPSC AE: 3 percent)
  [0:6.1] Revenue deficit | Eliminated – 31 March 2008, revised to 31 March 2009
  [0:6.2] Annual cut (Rules 2004) | FD 0.3%, RD 0.5% of GDP a year
  [0:6.3] Guarantees | Extra guarantees on Consolidated Fund ≤ 0.5% of GDP a year
  [0:6.4] RBI | Centre not to borrow from RBI except WMA; RBI out of primary G-Sec issues from 2006-07
[0:7] - **Elimination of primary deficit** was **never** an FRBM target (UPSC 2010).
[0:8] - A public-debt cap of **50% of GDP by 2011** was in the **FRBM Bill, 2000**, not in the Act as passed (WBCS 2017 key: "all of the above").
[0:9] - **Tax-GDP** is not a legal target; it is one of the **four indicators** in the MTFP Statement (RD, FD, tax-GDP, total outstanding liabilities).
[0:10] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.18.17-18.18 (Bill 2000, enacted 26 Aug 2003, in force 5 July 2004, RD 2008 → 2009, 0.5/0.3 cuts, WMA, RBI 2006-07); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.6-7 (introduced 2000, passed 2003, FD 3%, RD 0%, four indicators); app notes 3:106 sub 0; 50% debt cap of the 2000 Bill: general knowledge, not checked
## SUB 1 | What It Prescribes and Does Not; Statements Laid
[1:0] TABLE head: Prescribes | Does NOT
  [1:0.0] Limits for fiscal and revenue deficits | Penal provisions for missing targets
  [1:0.1] Statements laid with the Budget | Make the Centre answerable for States' failures
  [1:0.2] Disclosures (borrowings, contingent liabilities), quarterly review by FM | Apply to States – they have their own FR Acts
[1:1] - "FRBM does NOT provide penal provisions" – the APPSC G2 2017 key; it also does not apply at State level.
[1:2] - Under the Act it is mandatory for the Centre to cut FD to 3% and phase out RD by **March 2009** and to keep transparency/limit RBI borrowing – but **not to include States' failures in its own targets** (APPSC G2 Mains 2017).
[1:3] TABLE head: Statement (Sec 3) | Content
  [1:3.0] Macro-Economic Framework | Growth, inflation, fiscal and external balance, assumptions – mandated by the FRBM Act, not by Art. 112/113 or convention (UPSC 2020 ×2)
  [1:3.1] Medium-Term Fiscal Policy | 3-year rolling targets for RD, FD, tax-GDP, total outstanding liabilities
  [1:3.2] Fiscal Policy Strategy | Tax, spending, borrowing strategy
  [1:3.3] Medium-Term Expenditure Framework | 3-year expenditure ceilings; added 2012, laid after the Budget
[1:4] - The **Annual Financial Statement** is laid under **Art. 112**, not under the FRBM Act.
[1:5] - Rolling targets **suspended since 2021-22**. Budget 2026-27 laid the MEFS and a combined MTFPS-cum-FPSS.
[1:6] - Significance: made **fiscal discipline a legal duty** and added disclosure statements to the Budget.
[1:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy Part 1 - APPSC 04 p.10-11 (three statements); NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.6-7 (statements, four indicators); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.18.18 (statements, quarterly review); app notes 3:106 sub 1, 2
## SUB 2 | N.K. Singh Review Committee and the 2018 Amendment
[2:0] - Announced Budget **2016-17**; formed **May 2016**; **five members**; report **January 2017**; reflected in Budget **2017-18**.
[2:1] - Chair **N.K. Singh**; members included **Sumit Bose, Urjit Patel, Rathin Roy** (APPSC G2 Mains 2017).
[2:2] TABLE head: Recommendation | Value
  [2:2.0] Principal anchor | Debt-to-GDP; FD = operational target
  [2:2.1] General govt debt | 60% by 2023 = Centre 40% + States 20%
  [2:2.2] FD | 3% for the next three years (to 2019-20/March 2020), then 2.5% by 2022-23 (APPSC Fisheries; G2 Mains 2019)
  [2:2.3] RD | 0.8% by 2023
  [2:2.4] Institution | Independent, advisory Fiscal Council (no binding powers)
  [2:2.5] Escape clause | Deviation up to 0.5% of GDP; trigger includes far-reaching structural reforms
[2:3] # 2018 Amendment (Finance Act 2018; Budget 2018-19)
[2:4] - **Debt anchor**: general govt **60%**, Centre **40%** by 31 March 2025; **FD 3% by 31 March 2021**.
[2:5] - Government targets **debt and fiscal deficit** simultaneously and **does away with the revenue deficit (and ERD) target** (APPSC G2 Mains 2019).
[2:6] - **Escape grounds**: national security, war, **national calamity**, agricultural collapse, structural reforms, sharp output fall – real growth **3 percentage points below** the average of the previous four quarters. Not a routine tax shortfall.
[2:7] - **Limit**: deviation **≤ 0.5% of GDP** a year; government must place a **justification and a path back** before Parliament.
[2:8] - **Buoyancy clause**: sharp rise in growth requires an FD cut of **at least 0.25%** of GDP.
[2:9] - Monetised RBI borrowing allowed only on escape-clause grounds.
[2:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 10 p.7 (5-member committee; debt 60 = 40 + 20; 3% FD three years; escape 0.5%); Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.18.19 (report Jan 2017, debt rule, glide path, escape clause, structural reforms trigger); app notes 3:106 sub 4, 5, 6
## SUB 3 | Kelkar Task Force, Amendments and the Consolidation Path
[3:0] - **Task Force on Implementation of the FRBM Act (2004)**, chaired by **Vijay Kelkar** – meet targets through **tax reform**: wider base + **comprehensive GST**. Kelkar later headed the **13th Finance Commission**.
[3:1] TABLE head: Year | Change
  [3:1.0] 2004 | Rules notified; RD (and FD) deadline moved to 2009
  [3:1.1] 2008-09 | Targets suspended after the global financial crisis (stimulus)
  [3:1.2] 2012 | ERD target and MTEF Statement added
  [3:1.3] 2015 | Dates deferred (FD 3% by 2017-18)
  [3:1.4] 2018 | Debt anchor, escape and buoyancy clauses; RD/ERD targets dropped
  [3:1.5] 2020-21 | COVID: escape clause used; FD 9.2%; no amendment
[3:2] - Government's deviation statement admitted missing the **3% FD** and **40% Central debt** targets.
[3:3] TABLE head: Year | FD (% GDP)
  [3:3.0] 2020-21 | 9.2 (pandemic peak)
  [3:3.1] 2024-25 | 4.8
  [3:3.2] 2025-26 | 4.4 – "below 4.5%" target (set FY 2021-22) met
  [3:3.3] 2026-27 BE | 4.3
[3:4] - **Halving commitment** (9.2% in five years) met at **4.4% in 2025-26** – a commitment, not a legislative target.
[3:5] - **New debt rule (Budget 2026-27)**: FD set yearly to bring Central debt to **50 ± 1% by 31 March 2031** (**55.6%** BE 2026-27, 56.1% RE 2025-26); above FRBM's 40%.
[3:6] - **16th FC**: Centre FD **3.5% by 2030-31**; States **3% of GSDP**; State fiscal rules "**effectively unenforced**"; wider deficit definition including **off-budget borrowing**.
[3:7] - Criticism: frequent escape-clause use, deferrals, no penalty, **no fiscal council set up yet**.

## SUB 4 | State Fiscal Laws and Andhra Pradesh
[4:0] - States passed Fiscal Responsibility Laws after the **12th FC** tied debt relief to them (**Debt Consolidation and Relief Facility**); the Union FRBM does not apply to States – they operate under **their own** FR legislation.
[4:1] - **AP FRBM Act, 2005**: **eliminate RD**, **FD 3% of GSDP**; amended 2010, 2011, **2020** (raised RD limits), 2021. AP FRBM limits restrain AP's **open-market borrowing**.
[4:2] - After bifurcation AP **could not meet** its FRBM targets: CAG put AP's 2014-15 FD at **₹31,717 crore = 6.10% of GSDP** (2 June 2014-31 March 2015 accounts), double the 3% ceiling.
[4:3] - AP Budget 2019-20 (12 July 2019): FD **₹35,261 crore, about 3.30% of GSDP** (PRS: 3.26%); RD 0.16%.
[4:4] - AP BE 2026-27: **FD 3.84%**, RD **1.11%** of GSDP. Slippage: FD 5.05% vs 4% ceiling in 2024-25.
[4:5] TABLE head: Year (15th FC) | States' net borrowing limit (% GSDP)
  [4:5.0] 2021-22 | 4
  [4:5.1] 2022-23 | 3.5
  [4:5.2] 2023-24 to 2025-26 | 3 + 0.5 for power-sector reforms
[4:6] - **14th FC** horizontal criteria: population 1971 and **2011**, area, **forest cover**, income distance – **fiscal discipline was dropped** (it was a 13th FC criterion).
