## SUB 4 | Bifurcation's Impact and AP in India
[4:0] # What bifurcation did to the economy
[4:1] - AP bifurcated on **2 June 2014** (AP Reorganisation Act, 2014); residual AP had **13 districts**.
[4:2] - **Hyderabad alone ≈ 48%** of undivided AP's total revenue of **₹70,548 crore (2012-13)**.
[4:3] - **APRA, 2014 gives AP no share of Hyderabad's tax revenue**: taxes go to the State where the property is or where the tax is assessed (Sec. 50).
[4:4] - Loss of Hyderabad's revenue base → AP a **chronic revenue-deficit State**; capital and major institutions stayed with Telangana → new capital **Amaravati**.
[4:5] - Hyderabad-centred services and industry went to Telangana → **agriculture's share rose**; new IT parks at **Visakhapatnam, Tirupati, Vijayawada**.
[4:6] - **Assertion-reason (2017):** better governance and service delivery was stressed (R true), but there was **no turnaround in fiscal performance** (A false) – keyed "A false, R true".
[4:7] - **1999-2006:** AP's **primary sector ranked 2nd among States by compound growth rate**.
[4:8] # AP in national aggregates

  [4:9.0] Area | 4.96% of India
  [4:9.1] Population | 4.09%, 10th (Census 2011)
  [4:9.2] Coastline | 1,053.07 km, 3rd (Gujarat, Tamil Nadu ahead)
  [4:9.3] Largest port by cargo | Visakhapatnam

  [4:9.5] Mines and minerals | 50% of own non-tax revenue (2024-25 RE)
[4:10] - **Sixth Economic Census (2013-14):** undivided AP **7.25%** of India's establishments, **5th**; in present AP **74%** of establishments are **rural**; employment in establishments grew **28%** between the 5th and 6th Censuses.

[4:13] - **Chairman = Chief Minister** (not the Governor); publishes export reports.
