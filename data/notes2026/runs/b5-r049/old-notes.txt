=== NOTES 5:49 | Missiles Beyond Igmdp – Brahmos, Nirbhay, Astra, Pralay, Hypersonic Technology, Mission Shakti (asat)
## SUB 0 | Missile Classes – Ballistic vs Cruise
[49:0:0] # Ballistic vs Cruise
[49:0:1] TABLE head: Feature | Ballistic missile | Cruise missile
  [49:0:1.0] Power | Rocket-powered only in the boost phase | Air-breathing, powered throughout
  [49:0:1.1] Path | High parabolic (free-fall) arc | Flies low, constant speed, uses aerodynamic lift
  [49:0:1.2] Examples | Agni, Prithvi, Pralay, Shaurya | BrahMos, Nirbhay
[49:0:2] # Classification by Speed
[49:0:3] TABLE head: Class | Speed | Indian example
  [49:0:3.0] Subsonic | Below Mach 1 | Nirbhay
  [49:0:3.1] Supersonic | Mach 1–5 | BrahMos (Mach 2.8–3.0)
  [49:0:3.2] Hypersonic | Above Mach 5 | BrahMos-II (planned), LR-AShM, HSTDV
[49:0:4] # Terms
[49:0:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [49:0:5.0] HGV (Hypersonic Glide Vehicle) | Rocket-boosted, then glides at Mach 5+ and manoeuvres in the upper atmosphere; hard to intercept
  [49:0:5.1] MIRV | One missile, several warheads, different targets; first Indian test on Agni-V in Mission Divyastra (11 March 2024)
  [49:0:5.2] FOBS (Fractional Orbital Bombardment System) | Warhead put into a (partial) low-Earth orbit, then de-orbits over the target; can come from any direction
  [49:0:5.3] Scramjet | Supersonic combustion, no rotating parts; uses air, does not carry its oxidiser
  [49:0:5.4] SFDR (Solid Fuel Ducted Ramjet) | Uses atmospheric air; demonstrated from ITR Chandipur; for long-range air-to-air missiles
[49:0:6] Mission Divyastra MIRV used **Agni-V**, not K-4 (K-4 is submarine-launched).
## SUB 1 | Brahmos – India-russia Supersonic Cruise Missile
[49:1:0] # Partnership and Name
[49:1:1] - Joint venture of **India and Russia** (asked in **APPSC G2 Mains 2025**).
[49:1:2] - **Inter-Governmental Agreement** signed in **Moscow, 1998** → created **BrahMos Aerospace**.
[49:1:3] - Ownership: **DRDO 50.5%** + **NPO Mashinostroyeniya (NPOM), Russia 49.5%**.
[49:1:4] - Name: **Brahmaputra** (India) + **Moskva** (Russia) rivers.
[49:1:5] # Technical Features
[49:1:6] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [49:1:6.0] First successful test | 12 June 2001, Chandipur, Odisha
  [49:1:6.1] Speed class | Supersonic cruise, Mach 2.8–3.0 (~3,400–3,700 km/h); one of the fastest cruise missiles
  [49:1:6.2] Propulsion | Solid booster → liquid ramjet
  [49:1:6.3] Navigation | Inertial + satellite (GPS/GLONASS); fire-and-forget from a Transport Launch Canister
  [49:1:6.4] Flight profile | Cruises at ~15 km; dives to ~10 m in terminal phase; low radar cross-section
  [49:1:6.5] Range | 290 km originally → 290–400 km (extended range); raised towards ~450 km after India joined MTCR (2016)
[49:1:7] # Variants and Launch Platforms
[49:1:8] - Base BrahMos can be launched from all **four platforms**: land, ship, submarine and air (**Su-30MKI**).
[49:1:9] TABLE head: Variant | Feature
  [49:1:9.0] Ship-launched | Warships, e.g. P-17A frigates
  [49:1:9.1] Extended range | ~400+ km
  [49:1:9.2] BrahMos-NG | Lighter (~1.5 t vs 2.5 t), for Tejas, smaller warships, submarines; ~Mach 3.5
  [49:1:9.3] BrahMos-II | Planned hypersonic, above Mach 5 (up to Mach 7), under development
[49:1:10] # Test Sites and Milestones
[49:1:11] - **January 2009** and **29 March 2009**: land-attack Block-II tests at **Pokhran** (Mobile Autonomous Launcher; 290 km).
[49:1:12] - **21 March 2010**: fired from moving warship **INS Ranvir** off the Odisha coast (Bay of Bengal).
[49:1:13] - **BrahMos Integration and Testing Facility, Lucknow** (UP defence corridor): inaugurated **11 May 2025**; first batch within five months.
[49:1:14] - **First reported combat use: Operation Sindoor (May 2025).**
[49:1:15] APPSC G2 2025: "BrahMos is developed by India and ___" = **Russia**.
## SUB 2 | Nirbhay, Itcm and Astra
[49:2:0] # Nirbhay and ITCM
[49:2:1] - **Nirbhay**: DRDO's first indigenous **subsonic long-range land-attack cruise missile (LR-LACM)**.
[49:2:2] - Range **~1,000 km**; **turbojet**; payload ~200–300 kg; conventional or **nuclear** warheads.
[49:2:3] - Terrain-hugging at low altitude; inertial + GPS/NavIC navigation.
[49:2:4] - Flight-tested **7 November 2017** from **ITR Chandipur**.
[49:2:5] - **ITCM** (Indigenous Technology Cruise Missile): Nirbhay successor, **'Manik' small turbofan engine** by **GTRE**.
[49:2:6] - Jun 2026: the family is now called **LR-LACM**.
[49:2:7] # Astra (BVRAAM)
[49:2:8] - India's **first indigenous Beyond Visual Range Air-to-Air Missile** (DRDO); all-weather; **active radar seeker**.
[49:2:9] - BVR = engages beyond 20 nautical miles (37 km).
[49:2:10] - First successfully tested **9 May 2003 (Balasore)**; ballistic flight tests at **ITR Chandipur**.
[49:2:11] TABLE head: Variant | Facts (10 Jul 2026)
  [49:2:11.0] Astra Mk-1 | 80–110 km, Mach 4.5, up to 20 km altitude; on Su-30MKI; planned for Tejas Mk-1A and Rafale
  [49:2:11.1] Astra Mk-2 | ~200 km (2026), dual-pulse solid motor
  [49:2:11.2] Astra Mk-3 'Gandiva' | Under development, SFDR engine, 350+ km
[49:2:12] - **23 August 2023**: LCA Tejas (LSP-7) test-fired Astra off **Goa**; **12 March 2025**: fired from an LCA AF Mk1 prototype.

## SUB 4 | Hypersonic Technology – Indian Systems and Firsts
[49:4:0] - Hypersonic = faster than **Mach 5**.
[49:4:1] - **HSTDV** (Hypersonic Technology Demonstrator Vehicle): indigenous **scramjet**; flew above **Mach 6**; proved scramjet combustion and thermal protection above **2,000°C**; scramjet combustor run over **12 minutes (Jan 2026)**. It is not a submarine-launched missile.
[49:4:2] - **16 November 2024**: first flight-trial of India's **first long-range hypersonic missile**.
[49:4:3] - **LR-AShM**: hypersonic **glide anti-ship** missile for Navy coastal batteries; ~1,500 km; **peak Mach 10, average Mach 5** via **"skipping" manoeuvres**; shown at the 77th Republic Day Parade.
[49:4:4] - AP angle: ranges along the **Andhra Pradesh coast** used for **LR-AShM validation rounds** (Jan 2026).
## SUB 5 | Air-defence Shields – Bmd, Kusha, Sudarshan Chakra
[49:5:0] # Ballistic Missile Defence Layers

  [49:5:1.1] Phase-II | AD-1 (IRBM-class threats) | AD-2 (under development)
[49:5:2] # New Shields
[49:5:3] - **Mission Sudarshan Chakra**: multi-layered air and missile defence network; operational maturity by **2035** (Aug 2026).
[49:5:4] - **Project Kusha**: DRDO **long-range** (not short-range) air defence; interceptors **M1/M2/M3 ≈ 150 / 250 / 350–400 km**.
[49:5:5] - **THAAD** (USA): hit-to-kill interception of short-, medium- and intermediate-range ballistic missiles in the terminal phase; deployed in **South Korea (2017)**.
[49:5:6] PAD = Pradyumna = exo; AAD = Ashwin = endo.

## SUB 1 | Nuclear Weapon Technology
[50:1:0] # Fission vs Fusion
[50:1:1] TABLE head: Fission (atomic) weapon | Fusion (thermonuclear / hydrogen) weapon
  [50:1:1.0] Heavy nucleus (U-235 / Pu-239) splits in a neutron chain reaction | Light nuclei (hydrogen isotopes) fuse
  [50:1:1.1] Example: Pokhran-I | A fission "primary" triggers fusion; tested in Pokhran-II
[50:1:2] - U-235 fission: neutron hit → two smaller nuclei (e.g. barium + krypton) + 2–3 neutrons.
[50:1:3] - **Krytron** = high-speed switch that fires a nuclear bomb's detonators (not a MOSFET, relay or solenoid).
