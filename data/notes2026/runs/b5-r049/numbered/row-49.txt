## SUB 0 | BrahMos – India-Russia Supersonic Cruise Missile
[0:0] # Partnership and name
[0:1] - **Joint venture of India and Russia:** **DRDO 50.5%** + **NPO Mashinostroyeniya (NPOM), Russia 49.5%** = **BrahMos Aerospace**, created by an Inter-Governmental Agreement signed in **Moscow, 1998** (not USA, France, Israel, Japan, China).
[0:2] - **Name:** **Brahmaputra** (India) + **Moskva** (Russia) rivers – "BRAHMOS", not Brahmastra.
[0:3] - **Production:** missiles are integrated at the **BrahMos Integration Complex, Kanchanbagh, Hyderabad** (first production unit; later Thiruvananthapuram, and **Lucknow** – BrahMos Integration and Testing Facility, inaugurated **11 May 2025**). Not Bangalore, Mumbai, Ludhiana or Dehradun.
[0:4] # Type and technical features
[0:5] TABLE head: Item | Fact
  [0:5.0] Type | Supersonic cruise missile (not ballistic, not ICBM); one of the world's fastest cruise missiles
  [0:5.1] Speed | Mach 2.8–3.0 (~3,400–3,700 km/h)
  [0:5.2] Role | Essentially anti-ship, also land-attack; not anti-aircraft, not anti-tank
  [0:5.3] Propulsion | Solid booster → liquid ramjet
  [0:5.4] Navigation | Inertial + satellite; fire-and-forget from a Transport Launch Canister
  [0:5.5] Range | 290 km originally → 400–450 km after India joined MTCR (2016); BrahMos-ER 450–800 km. Never 1,200 km
  [0:5.6] Platforms | Land, ship, submarine and air (Su-30MKI) – not "only submarines"; in service with Army, Navy and Air Force
[0:6] - **Variants:** BrahMos-ER (extended range); **BrahMos-NG** – lighter (~1.5 t vs 2.5 t), for Tejas, smaller warships, submarines, ~Mach 3.5; **BrahMos-II** – planned **hypersonic** (above Mach 5, up to Mach 7–8), scramjet.
[0:7] # Tests and milestones
[0:8] TABLE head: Date | Event
  [0:8.0] 12 June 2001 | First successful test, Chandipur, Odisha
  [0:8.1] 12 Feb 2003 | Third test flight
  [0:8.2] 13 June 2004 | 7th launch from a mobile launcher; can locate an enemy ship 290 km away
  [0:8.3] 2005 | First version inducted into the Navy (INS Rajput)
  [0:8.4] Jan and 29 March 2009 | Land-attack Block-II tests at Pokhran, Rajasthan (Mobile Autonomous Launcher)
  [0:8.5] 21 March 2010 | Fired from moving warship INS Ranvir, Bay of Bengal, off Odisha
  [0:8.6] March 2017 | Extended-range (~400 km) test
  [0:8.7] 17 Dec 2019 | Test from ITR Chandipur
  [0:8.8] May 2025 | First reported combat use – Operation Sindoor
[0:9] # Exports
[0:10] - **Philippines** – first export customer: **US$375 million** deal (**January 2022**) for **three batteries** of shore-based anti-ship BrahMos; first batch delivered **April 2024**. Export range about **290 km** (so "490 km" is false).
[0:11] - **UAE**: BrahMos and Akashteer only in **early-stage talks** (23 Jun 2026) – nothing delivered.
[0:12] > Most-asked missile of the topic: partner country (**Russia**) ×7, "supersonic cruise" ×5, NOT-correct statements (range, platforms) ×4, test sites.

## SUB 1 | Astra and Nirbhay
[1:0] # Astra (BVRAAM)
[1:1] - India's **first indigenous Beyond Visual Range Air-to-Air Missile** (DRDO); all-weather, day-night; **active radar seeker**. So Astra = **air-to-air** (not surface-to-surface, surface-to-air or air-to-surface).
[1:2] - BVR = beyond about 20 nautical miles (37 km).
[1:3] - First successfully tested **9 May 2003, Balasore**; ballistic flight test at ITR **Chandipur, 20 May 2011**.
[1:4] TABLE head: Variant | Facts
  [1:4.0] Astra Mk-1 | 80–110 km (max ~110 km head-on), Mach 4.5, up to 20 km altitude; on Su-30MKI, Tejas
  [1:4.1] Astra Mk-2 | ~160–200 km, dual-pulse solid motor
  [1:4.2] Astra Mk-3 'Gandiva' | Under development, SFDR engine, 300–350+ km
[1:5] - **23 August 2023:** LCA **Tejas (LSP-7)** test-fired Astra off **Goa**; **12 March 2025:** fired from an LCA AF Mk1 prototype.
[1:6] - **Export:** **Indonesia** – Astra Mk-1 deal Jul 2026, first-ever Astra export.
[1:7] # Nirbhay and ITCM
[1:8] - **Nirbhay:** India's first indigenous **long-range subsonic cruise missile** (land-attack, LR-LACM) – **not supersonic**.
[1:9] - Range **~1,000 km**; **turbojet**; about **Mach 0.8**; conventional or **nuclear** warheads; **terrain-hugging**; surface-to-surface; seen as India's counter to Pakistan's **Babur**.
[1:10] - Flight-tested **7 November 2017** from **ITR Chandipur**.
[1:11] - **ITCM** (Indigenous Technology Cruise Missile): Nirbhay successor, **'Manik' small turbofan** by **GTRE**; Jun 2026: family now called **LR-LACM**.
[1:12] > Astra is asked as "air-to-air / first BVR" ×8; Nirbhay as "subsonic" (the wrong statement is always "supersonic").

## SUB 2 | Shaurya, Rudram and Other New Indian Missiles
[2:0] TABLE head: Missile | Type / role
  [2:0.0] Rudram-I | India's first indigenous anti-radiation missile, air-to-surface, from Su-30MKI, ~150–200 km; homes on radar emissions to destroy enemy radars and communication sites
  [2:0.1] Shaurya | Canister-launched, hypersonic, nuclear-capable surface-to-surface (quasi-)ballistic missile by DRDO's ASL, Hyderabad; tested 12 Nov 2008 and 24 Sep 2011; ~700–800 km with 1,000 kg (variants up to ~1,900 km); max speed Mach 7.5
  [2:0.2] Pralay | Quasi-ballistic short-range SSM, 150–500 km, conventional warhead, manoeuvrable re-entry; salvo of two fired 31 Dec 2025
  [2:0.3] Trishul | IGMDP short-range quick-reaction SAM, supersonic (~Mach 2), tested from Chandipur; discontinued
  [2:0.4] VSHORADS | DRDO (RCI, Hyderabad) 4th-generation man-portable air-defence system (MANPADS); DAC-approved buy Jul 2026
  [2:0.5] VL-SRSAM | Vertical-launch short-range SAM for Navy ships, derived from Astra
  [2:0.6] QRSAM | Quick Reaction SAM, Army's mid-range IADWS layer against low-flying threats
  [2:0.7] MPATGM | Man-portable, third-generation, fire-and-forget ATGM; DAC approval Jul 2026
  [2:0.8] Prahaar | ~150 km tactical ballistic missile
  [2:0.9] LRGB 'Gaurav' | 1,000 kg glide bomb, ~100 km, from Su-30MKI (Apr 2025)
  [2:0.10] Pinaka LRGR-120 | 120 km guided rocket; first flight 29 Dec 2025
  [2:0.11] NASM-SR | First indigenous helicopter-launched anti-ship missile, IIR seeker
  [2:0.12] NASM-MR | Anti-ship missile; maiden flight 14 Jun 2026
[2:1] - Shaurya traps: range is **not 900 km** and not more than 1,600 km; speed is **not more than Mach 8**.
[2:2] - A **ballistic** missile is a trajectory class – the radar-killer is the **anti-radiation** missile.

## SUB 5 | Missile Classes, FOBS and Hypersonic Technology
[5:0] # Ballistic vs cruise
[5:1] TABLE head: Feature | Ballistic missile | Cruise missile
  [5:1.0] Power | Rocket-powered only in the boost phase | Air-breathing (jet), powered throughout
  [5:1.1] Path | High parabolic (free-fall) arc | Flies low, constant speed, aerodynamic lift
  [5:1.2] Examples | Agni, Prithvi, Pralay, Shaurya | BrahMos, Nirbhay
[5:2] - UPSC 2023: "ballistic = jet-propelled subsonic; cruise = rocket only at start" and "Agni-V cruise, BrahMos ICBM" are **both wrong** (neither).
[5:3] TABLE head: Class | Speed | Indian example
  [5:3.0] Subsonic | Below Mach 1 | Nirbhay
  [5:3.1] Supersonic | Mach 1–5 | BrahMos (Mach 2.8–3.0)
  [5:3.2] Hypersonic | Above Mach 5 | BrahMos-II (planned), LR-AShM, HSTDV
[5:4] # Terms
[5:5] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [5:5.0] FOBS (Fractional Orbital Bombardment System) | Warhead put into a (fractional) low Earth orbit, then de-orbits over the target; can come from any direction (e.g. over the South Pole); Soviet Cold War concept
  [5:5.1] HGV (Hypersonic Glide Vehicle) | Rocket-boosted, then glides at Mach 5+ and manoeuvres in the upper atmosphere; hard to intercept
  [5:5.2] MIRV | One missile, several warheads, different targets; first Indian test on Agni-V in Mission Divyastra (11 March 2024), not K-4
  [5:5.3] Scramjet | Supersonic combustion, no rotating parts; uses air, does not carry its oxidiser
  [5:5.4] SFDR (Solid Fuel Ducted Ramjet) | Uses atmospheric air; demonstrated from ITR Chandipur; for long-range air-to-air missiles
[5:6] # Indian hypersonic systems
[5:7] - **HSTDV:** indigenous **scramjet** demonstrator; flew above **Mach 6**; scramjet combustion and thermal protection above **2,000°C**; combustor run over 12 minutes (Jan 2026). Not a submarine-launched missile.
[5:8] - **16 November 2024:** first flight-trial of India's first long-range hypersonic missile.
[5:9] - **LR-AShM:** hypersonic **glide anti-ship** missile for Navy coastal batteries; **~1,500 km**; **peak Mach 10**, average Mach 5 via "skipping"; at the 77th Republic Day Parade; **AP coast ranges** used for validation rounds (Jan 2026).
