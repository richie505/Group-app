## SUB 0 | Prisons, Custody and Parole – Law and Administration
[0:0] TABLE head: Law / rule | Provision
  [0:0.0] Seventh Schedule | 'Prisons / persons detained therein' = State List, Entry 4 – States manage prisons with their own rules
  [0:0.1] Prisons Act 1894 | Colonial law that kept prisons with the Provincial (now State) Governments (power to make rules); with State jail manuals; BPR&D advisory
  [0:0.2] Model Prisons Act 2023 | MHA guide for States to replace the 1894 Act; not binding
  [0:0.3] BNSS s.479 | Undertrial freed after half the maximum term (one-third for first-timers)
[0:1] - **UPSC 2023:** Statement I (States manage prisons) and Statement II (1894 Act kept prisons with Provincial Governments) are **both correct, and II explains I** (widely given key).
[0:2] - **Judicial custody:** the accused is in the **magistrate's custody** and lodged in **jail** (not the police station); police **cannot interrogate without the court's approval**. Police custody = lock-up at the police station.
[0:3] - **Parole/furlough:** **not an absolute right** – even a sufficient case does not make parole a matter of right; **each State has its own Prisoners Release on Parole Rules** (UPSC 2021).
[0:4] - **FIR on websites:** **Youth Bar Association of India v UoI (2016)** – copies of FIRs (except sensitive cases) to be **uploaded to the police/State website within 24 hours** of registration (48 hours in remote areas).
[0:5] - **Aug 2025:** SC ordered release of prisoners held beyond their sentence.

## SUB 1 | Custodial Violence and Encounter Deaths

  [1:0.0] Nilabati Behera (1993) | Compensation (Arts 32/226) for custodial death; no sovereign immunity
  [1:0.1] Joginder Kumar (1994) | Arrest must be justified; friend/relative informed
  [1:0.2] D.K. Basu (1997) | Arrest safeguards: arrest memo, officer ID, informing kin, medical check

[1:1] - **CrPC s.176(1A)** – mandatory **judicial inquiry** into custodial death (now **BNSS s.196**); **BNSS s.218** – sanction before cognisance against public servants.
[1:2] - **2,253** police-custody deaths, only **3 convictions** (1999-2023); most in **Bihar**.
[1:3] - **AP:** **Gade Sai Krishna** custodial death – **Krishnalanka** police, **Vijayawada**.
[1:4] # Encounter deaths
[1:5] - **PUCL v Maharashtra (2014):** **16-point** guidelines – record tip-off, **FIR**, independent probe by **CID**, **magisterial inquiry**, inform **NHRC/SHRC**; **no out-of-turn promotion or gallantry award** until cleared.
[1:6] - **NHRC norms:** report custodial death **within 24 hours**, post-mortem **videography**, **independent investigation** (not by the same police station).

## SUB 4 | Death Penalty Debate and Mercy
[4:0] TABLE head: Case | Holding
  [4:0.0] Jagmohan Singh (1973) | Death penalty constitutional
  [4:0.1] Bachan Singh v State of Punjab (1980) | Constitution Bench – "rarest of rare"; life the rule, death the exception (first laid down)
  [4:0.2] Machhi Singh (1983) | Categories; aggravating vs mitigating factors
  [4:0.3] Epuru Sudhakar (2006) | Pardon open to limited judicial review
  [4:0.4] Shatrughan Chauhan (2014) | Inordinate delay in mercy → commute; 14 days' notice before execution; solitary confinement illegal
[4:1] - **Path:** Sessions Court → **HC confirmation** → SC (**Arts 132, 134, 136**) → mercy (**Art. 72** President **can pardon**; **Art. 161** Governor **cannot pardon** a death sentence, may commute/remit/suspend).
[4:2] - **Capital offences (old IPC):** s.121 waging war, s.302 murder (now **BNS s.103**), s.396 dacoity with murder, etc.
[4:3] - **Amnesty:** **China** the biggest executioner; **23 countries** executed in 2017; **2025 report:** at least **2,707** executions (excluding China), highest since 1981; **Iran** led.
