## SUB 0 | Concept of Human Rights – Meaning, Thinkers and Milestones
[0:0] # Meaning
[0:1] - **Human rights:** rights given to **all human beings by nature**, simply because they are human – **inherent, inalienable, universal**; essential conditions of social life without which no one can lead a normal life.
[0:2] - **Core value:** **dignity of man as a human being** (UDHR Preamble: inherent dignity and equal rights); **indivisible and interdependent** – civil-political and socio-economic rights stand together (**Vienna Declaration, 1993**).
[0:3] - **Rights vs the State:** rights are **claims of the citizens against the State** – limits on State power, not claims of the State on citizens or privileges of a few.
[0:4] - Every **Fundamental Right** is a human right, but **not every human right is a Fundamental Right**.
[0:5] - **Kinds of rights:** **economic rights** give a **reasonable and legal source of livelihood** (work, wages, leisure); **political rights** = vote, contest, hold office (the **right to vote is a political right**; in India only a **statutory right** – **Sec. 62, RP Act 1951**, not an FR); civil rights = life, liberty, religion.
[0:6] - **Values:** "respect for human rights and responsibilities" is a **constitutional value**; in a **democracy** equality is accepted as a **legal norm**, which makes it easier to guarantee women's freedom and dignity (it does not ensure equal representation).
[0:7] - **Violations (NDA 2010):** detaining a person on the way to vote and killing a civilian in a combing operation are **both** human-rights violations.
[0:8] # Thinkers
[0:9] TABLE head: Thinker | Idea
  [0:9.0] John Locke (English/British thinker) | Natural rights – life, liberty, property; popularised the doctrine; "father" of the human-rights idea
  [0:9.1] J.J. Rousseau | "Man is born free, but everywhere he is in chains" (Social Contract, 1762)
  [0:9.2] Ronald Dworkin (printed "Ronald Darwin") | Rights as trumps: a protective shield for the individual when the State interferes with civil liberties
  [0:9.3] Jack Donnelly (printed "John Dowski") | Human rights as a new standard of civilisation (1998)
  [0:9.4] Olympe de Gouges | Declaration of the Rights of Woman and the Female Citizen (1791), French Revolution
  [0:9.5] Mary Wollstonecraft | A Vindication of the Rights of Woman (1792) – not the 1791 Declaration

## SUB 1 | UDHR 1948 and Human Rights Day
[1:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [1:0.0] Adopted by | UN General Assembly, 10 December 1948 (Paris)
  [1:0.1] Human Rights Day | 10 December every year (UNGA resolution 423(V), 1950)
  [1:0.2] Drafting chair | Eleanor Roosevelt
  [1:0.3] Structure | Preamble + 30 articles
  [1:0.4] Legal status | A declaration – not a treaty, not legally binding
  [1:0.5] Core value | Human dignity
[1:1] - **Covers** civil, political, economic, social and cultural rights; **guides the SDGs Agenda** (not the Paris Agreement, Magna Carta or NAFTA).
[1:2] - **Included:** dignity, liberty, equality (Art. 1); **life, liberty, security (Art. 3)**; equality before law (Art. 7); **privacy (Art. 12)**; social security (Art. 22); **work (Art. 23)**; **adequate standard of living (Art. 25)**; **education (Art. 26)**; **duties to the community (Art. 29)**.
[1:3] - **Art. 25(1):** security in the event of **unemployment, sickness, disability, widowhood, old age** – linked to an adequate standard of living (food, clothing, housing, medical care).
[1:4] - **NOT included:** **right to universal basic income** (JPSC 2023).
[1:5] - **⚠ Privacy IS a UDHR right (Art. 12)** and an ICCPR right (Art. 17); the JPSC 2016 key that called privacy "not a UN-listed human right" is wrong.
[1:6] - **Context dates:** UN Day **24 Oct 1945**; India's Constitution adopted 26 Nov 1949.

## SUB 2 | Generations of Human Rights
[2:0] - **Karel Vasak (1977)** classified rights into three generations, linked to **liberty, equality, fraternity**.
[2:1] TABLE head: Generation | Ideal | Rights
  [2:1.0] First | Liberty | Civil and political: life, speech, fair trial, vote, participation in political life
  [2:1.1] Second | Equality | Economic, social, cultural: work, education, health, social security, eradication of social discrimination
  [2:1.2] Third | Fraternity / solidarity | Collective: healthy environment, development, peace
[2:2] - **Right to Education** and **Right to Work** are both **second-generation** (ICESCR) rights; Liberty and Equality before law are first-generation.

## SUB 3 | International Bill of Human Rights and Core Treaties
[3:0] - **International Bill of Human Rights = UDHR + ICCPR + ICESCR + optional protocols.**
[3:1] TABLE head: Instrument | Adopted | In force | Key content
  [3:1.0] ICESCR | 16 Dec 1966 | 3 Jan 1976 | Work, social security, health, education; realised progressively
  [3:1.1] ICCPR | 16 Dec 1966 | 23 Mar 1976 | Life (Art. 6), no torture (Art. 7), fair trial (Art. 14), privacy (Art. 17), expression (Art. 19), child rights (Art. 24)
  [3:1.2] ICCPR First Optional Protocol | 1966 | – | Individual complaints
  [3:1.3] ICESCR Optional Protocol | 2008 | – | Complaints
[3:2] - **ICESCR entered into force first** (Jan 1976), ICCPR in March 1976.
[3:3] - **India** acceded to both Covenants on **10 April 1979**; has **not signed the ICCPR First Optional Protocol**.
[3:4] - **Child rights:** **ICCPR Art. 24** (also Art. 24 of the Indian Constitution – child labour).

## SUB 4 | UN Machinery – Human Rights Council, OHCHR, Paris Principles
[4:0] # Human Rights Council (UNHRC)
[4:1] - Set up **2006**, replacing the **UN Commission on Human Rights**; based in **Geneva**.
[4:2] - **47 members** elected by the UNGA; **3-year terms**, at most **two consecutive** terms; **two-thirds** UNGA vote can suspend a member.
[4:3] TABLE head: Region | Seats
  [4:3.0] Africa | 13
  [4:3.1] Asia-Pacific | 13
  [4:3.2] Latin America and Caribbean | 8
  [4:3.3] Western Europe and Others | 7
  [4:3.4] Eastern Europe | 6
[4:4] - **Universal Periodic Review (UPR):** peer review of every UN member's record; since **2008**, cycles of about **4.5 years**.
[4:5] - **Special rapporteurs** ("special procedures") = **unpaid independent experts**; **treaty bodies** monitor each core treaty.
[4:6] - **OHCHR** set up **1993**, after the **Vienna Conference**.
[4:7] # Paris Principles and NHRIs
[4:8] - Adopted by the **UNGA in 1993** (drafted in Paris, Oct 1991): **independence and autonomy**, **pluralist** composition, adequate resources, **powers to investigate** (not dependence on the executive).
[4:9] - **GANHRI** accredits NHRIs through its **Sub-Committee on Accreditation**: **"A" = fully**, "B" = partly compliant.
[4:10] - India's **NHRC** (1993, under the PHRA 1993) is a **statutory** NHRI.
[4:11] # Rights newly recognised by the UNGA
[4:12] TABLE head: Right | Recognition
  [4:12.0] Right to development | Declaration, 1986
  [4:12.1] Right to water and sanitation | 28 July 2010
  [4:12.2] Right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment | 28 July 2022 – a universal human right
