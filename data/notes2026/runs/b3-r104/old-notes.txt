=== NOTES 3:104 | Deficit Financing - Meaning, Methods and Consequences
## SUB 0 | Meaning - Indian vs Western Usage
[104:0:0] TABLE head: Usage | Meaning
  [104:0:0.0] Indian | Budget gap met by borrowing from the RBI; RBI creates new money (government "buys money")
  [104:0:0.1] Western | Any financing of a budget deficit, mainly borrowing from the public/market
  [104:0:0.2] Effect | Increase in money supply; creates extra paper currency
[104:0:1] - Deficit financing is **inflationary by nature**.
[104:0:2] - **Fiscal deficit** (Union Budget) = budgetary deficit + net increase in internal and external borrowings and other liabilities.
[104:0:3] In India, deficit financing = borrowing from the **RBI**, not from the public.
## SUB 1 | Purpose in Indian Planning
[104:1:0] - Purpose: **raising resources for economic development**; a "pragmatic tool" to finance the **Five Year Plans**.
[104:1:1] - Needed after 1947 because of a **low tax base**, poor compliance, weak private banking and **low domestic savings** (not a high savings rate).
[104:1:2] # Phases and Plans
[104:1:3] - Independence to **late 1960s**: little use, to fund plans.
[104:1:4] - **Second Plan**: deficit financing about **₹1,100 crore** against ₹1,200 crore planned.
[104:1:5] - **Third Plan**: reliance on deficit financing to be **strictly limited**.
[104:1:6] - **1970s onwards**: heavy reliance (oil shocks, welfare spending) led to debt and inflation.
## SUB 2 | Public Debt Classification
[104:2:0] TABLE head: Head | Items
  [104:2:0.0] Internal debt | Market loans, Treasury Bills, special securities to NSSF
  [104:2:0.1] Other liabilities (Public Account) | Small savings, State Provident Funds, reserve funds and deposits
  [104:2:0.2] External debt | Loans from foreign sources
[104:2:1] - **Market loans** = 75% of **AP's** debt.
## SUB 3 | Methods of Deficit Financing
[104:3:0] - **Borrowing from the RBI** (monetising the deficit).
[104:3:1] - **Ad hoc Treasury Bills** - automatic RBI financing, now abolished.
[104:3:2] - **Ways and Means Advances (WMA)** - temporary RBI advances from **1 April 1997**.
[104:3:3] - Market borrowing; liquidity now managed by **OMOs**: sale of G-Secs **drains** liquidity, purchase **adds** it.
[104:3:4] - 15 Sep 2026: RBI announced **OMO sales of ₹1 lakh crore** with 91/182/364-day T-bill auctions.
## SUB 4 | Control Over Deficit Financing
[104:4:0] TABLE head: Year | Step
  [104:4:0.0] Sep 1994 | RBI-Government agreement began phasing out ad hoc T-bills
  [104:4:0.1] Mar 1997 | Agreement ended ad hoc T-bills; WMA from 1 April 1997
  [104:4:0.2] 2003 | FRBM Act
  [104:4:0.3] 1 Apr 2006 | RBI barred from subscribing to primary issues of G-Secs
  [104:4:0.4] 2018 | Monetised borrowing only on escape-clause grounds
[104:4:1] - Escape clause: slip limited to **0.5% of GDP** a year.
## SUB 5 | Consequences
[104:5:0] TABLE head: Effect | Mechanism
  [104:5:0.0] Demand-pull inflation | Money supply and demand rise without more goods; hurts the poor most
  [104:5:0.1] Crowding out | Market borrowing raises interest rates, cuts private investment
  [104:5:0.2] Debt trap | Borrowing to pay interest; little room for health, education
  [104:5:0.3] Rating downgrades | Higher borrowing cost for government and firms
[104:5:1] # Inflationary Ranking
[104:5:2] TABLE head: Mode | Effect
  [104:5:2.0] Creation of new money | Most inflationary
  [104:5:2.1] Borrowing from banks | High
  [104:5:2.2] Borrowing from public | Lower (existing savings)
  [104:5:2.3] Repayment of debt | Not inflationary (withdraws money)
[104:5:3] # Current Data
[104:5:4] - Interest = **20 paise** of every rupee spent (Budget 2026-27); about **40% of revenue receipts**.
[104:5:5] - States borrowed ₹5.23 trillion till Oct 2025 (62% of FY26 plan); RBI wants a shift from subsidies to **capital expenditure**.
[104:5:6] - **AP** fiscal deficit 2024-25: **5.05%** vs 4% limit.

[104:6:1] # FIRST / LAST / SPECIAL FACTS
[104:6:2] - Last automatic monetisation tool: ad hoc T-bills (ended 1997).
[104:6:3] - Special: only escape-clause monetisation allowed since 2018.

=== NOTES 3:105 | Fiscal Policy - Objectives, Instruments; Fiscal, Revenue, Primary and Effective Revenue Deficits
## SUB 0 | Fiscal Policy - Meaning and Evolution
[105:0:0] - **Fiscal policy** = government policy on its **revenue (taxation) and expenditure**, plus **public debt**, to shape aggregate demand, allocation and distribution.
[105:0:1] - Word from Latin **fiscus** = basket / public purse.
[105:0:2] - Made by the **Ministry of Finance**; Union Budget prepared by the **Department of Economic Affairs (DEA)**.
[105:0:3] - **Article 112** calls it the **"Annual Financial Statement"**; the word **"Budget" is not in the Constitution**. The budget is itself an instrument of fiscal policy.
[105:0:4] # Persons and FIRSTs
[105:0:5] TABLE head: Person | Contribution
  [105:0:5.0] J.M. Keynes | General Theory (1936); fiscal policy as a tool against the Great Depression; active demand management
  [105:0:5.1] V.P. Singh | Long-Term Fiscal Policy, laid in Parliament on 19 Dec 1985 (Budget year 1985-86) as Finance Minister
  [105:0:5.2] Richard Musgrave | Three budget functions; popularised fiscal federalism (1959)
  [105:0:5.3] N.K. Singh | Committee 2016, report 2017: general govt debt 60% = Centre 40% + States 20%
## SUB 1 | Objectives and Functions (musgrave)
[105:1:0] TABLE head: Function | Meaning
  [105:1:0.0] Allocation | Provide public goods; the primary purpose of a budget
  [105:1:0.1] Distribution | Reduce inequality through progressive taxation and welfare spending
  [105:1:0.2] Stabilisation | Control inflation and unemployment; smooth the cycle
[105:1:1] - Objectives: **full employment, price stability, economic growth, equitable distribution**, resource mobilisation, **balanced regional development**.
[105:1:2] - Progressive taxation evidence: direct taxes = **58.8%** of total taxes (FY25); ITRs 6.9 crore (FY22) to 9.2 crore (FY25).
[105:1:3] # What is NOT a Fiscal Objective
[105:1:4] - **Directing interest rates**, managing **liquidity and money supply** = monetary policy objectives.
[105:1:5] - **Regulation of inter-state trade** - not a fiscal objective.
[105:1:6] - **Equitable distribution of income and assets** - NOT a monetary policy objective (it is fiscal).
[105:1:7] Components (taxation, expenditure, debt) and objectives (growth, employment, price stability, equity) are different things; the objectives do not explain the components.
[105:1:8] - **Progressive taxation:** the tax rate rises as income rises, so the rich pay more; this reduces inequality (distribution function).
## SUB 2 | Instruments - Fiscal vs Monetary Policy
[105:2:0] - Fiscal tools: **taxation**, **public expenditure** (capex as **pump priming**), **public borrowing/debt**, the **budget**, subsidies/DBT, PLI, transfer payments, deficit financing.
[105:2:1] - Public borrowing: **internal** (bonds, T-Bills, small savings) and **external** (multilateral, sovereign bonds); external borrowing carries **currency risk**.
[105:2:2] - Public expenditure (Centre + States) ≈ **28% of GDP** (ES 2016-17); Union alone **13.6%** (BE 2026-27).
[105:2:3] TABLE head: Policy | Authority | Tools | Speed
  [105:2:3.0] Fiscal | Govt (MoF) | Taxation, expenditure, public debt, subsidies, DBT, PLI | Slow (budget cycle)
  [105:2:3.1] Monetary | RBI / MPC | Repo, CRR, SLR, OMOs, LAF | Fast (MPC bi-monthly)
[105:2:4] - Monetary policy is **complementary** to fiscal policy.
[105:2:5] **Interest rate / repo rate** = monetary instrument, NOT a fiscal tool.
## SUB 3 | Budget Types, Receipts and Expenditure
[105:3:0] # Budget Types
[105:3:1] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [105:3:1.0] Balanced | Receipts = expenditure
  [105:3:1.1] Surplus | Receipts > expenditure
  [105:3:1.2] Deficit | Expenditure > receipts
  [105:3:1.3] Zero-based | Every item justified afresh, no base from last year
  [105:3:1.4] Performance | Spending linked to work done
  [105:3:1.5] Outcome | Outlays against measurable results; first 2005-06
  [105:3:1.6] Gender | First Gender Budget Statement with Union Budget 2005-06
[105:3:2] # Receipts
[105:3:3] TABLE head: Receipt | Types
  [105:3:3.0] Revenue | Tax and non-tax (e.g. PSU dividends)
  [105:3:3.1] Capital - non-debt | Recovery of loans, disinvestment, asset monetisation
  [105:3:3.2] Capital - debt | Market borrowings, small savings, other liabilities
[105:3:4] - Budget 2026-27: **borrowings and other liabilities = 24 paise** of every rupee - largest single receipt item.
[105:3:5] # Expenditure
[105:3:6] - **Revenue vs capital**: capital creates assets or reduces liabilities.
[105:3:7] - **Plan vs non-plan**: distinction **ended in Budget 2017-18** (Rail Budget merged the same year).
[105:3:8] - Developmental (growth, social services) vs non-developmental (interest, defence, administration).
[105:3:9] - **Charged (Art. 112(3))** - not voted by Parliament: President's emoluments, **debt charges**, salaries of **Supreme Court judges**. Rest is **voted**.
## SUB 4 | Deficit Definitions and Formulas
[105:4:0] TABLE head: Deficit | Formula | Shows
  [105:4:0.0] Revenue (RD) | Revenue expenditure - revenue receipts | Borrowing for day-to-day consumption (dissaving)
  [105:4:0.1] Effective RD (ERD) | RD - grants for creation of capital assets | True consumption deficit (Rangarajan concept)
  [105:4:0.2] Fiscal (FD) | Total expenditure - (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts incl. recovery of loans) | Total borrowing requirement
  [105:4:0.3] Primary (PD) | FD - interest payments | Current imbalance, excluding past debt
  [105:4:0.4] Budget (old) | Total expenditure - total receipts including borrowings | Ad hoc T-bill era
  [105:4:0.5] Monetised | Part financed by RBI (high-powered money) | Inflationary
  [105:4:0.6] Gross primary | GFD - interest payments | -
[105:4:1] - **Fiscal deficit = budget deficit + borrowings and other liabilities** (market borrowings). FD always equals borrowings and other liabilities.
[105:4:2] - **GFD** = total borrowing requirement; formula starts with **net borrowing at home**. Net FD = gross FD - net lending of the Centre.
[105:4:3] - **ERD** introduced in **Union Budget 2011-12**; added to FRBM by the **2012 amendment**. ERD shows capital receipts used for consumption.
[105:4:4] - Budget deficit stopped being the headline measure after ad hoc T-bills ended (1997).
[105:4:5] Budget deficit is NOT FD minus interest; that is the primary deficit. PD + interest = FD.
[105:4:6] - **Headline marker:** the **fiscal deficit** is the headline marker of fiscal health; revenue deficit and effective revenue deficit show what the borrowing is used for.
## SUB 5 | Relationship Between Deficits and Calculation
[105:5:0] - **FD > PD** always (positive interest). **FD > RD** always in practice. **ERD ≤ RD** (never greater).
[105:5:1] - **FD > budgetary deficit**: it includes borrowings from RBI plus other liabilities.
[105:5:2] - Largest deficit in the GoI budget = **fiscal deficit**. Descending order: **fiscal > revenue > primary**.
[105:5:3] - 2026-27 BE: FD 4.3 > RD 1.5 > PD 0.7 (% GDP).
[105:5:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [105:5:4.0] Zero primary deficit | FD = interest; borrowing only to pay interest - debt-trap signal
  [105:5:4.1] Debt trap | Borrowing to make interest payments
  [105:5:4.2] Zero RD | Balanced revenue budget
  [105:5:4.3] Primary surplus | Non-interest spending covered by non-debt receipts
[105:5:5] # Worked Examples
[105:5:6] - FD ₹1,50,000 crore - interest ₹1,20,000 crore = **PD ₹30,000 crore**.
[105:5:7] - Gross primary deficit: ₹50,000 - ₹1,500 = **₹48,500 crore** (non-debt capital receipts not deducted again).
[105:5:8] - FD ₹4 crore on GDP ₹400 crore = **1% of GDP**.
## SUB 6 | Deficit Financing and Financing the Fd
[105:6:0] - **Deficit financing (India)**: drawing down **cash balances**, **borrowing from RBI**, **issuing new currency**. RBI money-printing = **most inflationary** route; public borrowing absorbs liquidity.
[105:6:1] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [105:6:1.0] Ad hoc T-bills | Automatic monetisation from the mid-1950s; 1994 RBI-Government agreement; abolished from 1 April 1997 (ended 31 March 1997)
  [105:6:1.1] WMA | Temporary RBI advances for short receipt-payment gaps, from 1 April 1997
  [105:6:1.2] Overdraft | WMA used beyond the limit
[105:6:2] - From **2006-07** RBI barred from subscribing to **primary issues** of G-Secs, except under escape clause.
[105:6:3] - Sources of FD finance: **market borrowings**, **small savings/NSSF**, **provident funds**, **external loans**; indirect bank borrowing via **SLR**.
[105:6:4] - **Domestic borrowings** fill most of the FD (about **98-99%**).
[105:6:5] - **8th Plan** public outlay ₹4,34,100 crore - largest source **government borrowings (46.6%)**.
[105:6:6] - External liabilities in the Budget at **current exchange rates**, not historical; high borrowing kept real interest rates high.
[105:6:7] **PSU dividends** are **non-tax revenue**, NOT a source of financing the fiscal deficit.
## SUB 7 | Fiscal Stance, Crowding Out, Multiplier, Twin Deficit
[105:7:0] TABLE head: Stance | Action | Use / risk
  [105:7:0.0] Expansionary | More spending / lower taxes | Recession; inflation risk
  [105:7:0.1] Contractionary | Less spending / higher taxes | Overheating
  [105:7:0.2] Neutral | Balanced effect | Stable times
[105:7:1] - **Counter-cyclical**: deficits in slumps (**Atma Nirbhar Bharat, 2020**), consolidation in booms. Pro-cyclical deepens volatility.
[105:7:2] - **Recession**: increase spending on **public projects**; fiscal stimulus = **cutting tax rates**, **more spending** - abolishing subsidies is NOT.
[105:7:3] - **Double-dip recession:** recession → short recovery → **another recession**; a recession = usually **two consecutive quarters** of falling GDP.
[105:7:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [105:7:4.0] Crowding out | Govt borrowing raises interest rates, private investment falls
  [105:7:4.1] Crowding in | Public capex draws private investment
  [105:7:4.2] Fiscal multiplier | Change in GDP ÷ change in govt spending or taxes
  [105:7:4.3] Capex multiplier | 2.5-3 in India - largest for capital expenditure
[105:7:5] - **Quality of deficit**: high **RD/FD** ratio = borrowing funds consumption; 2026-27 RD 1.5 / FD 4.3 ≈ one-third.
[105:7:6] - **Twin deficit** = FD + **CAD**. Higher FD widens CAD: dissaving cuts national savings, widens the savings-investment gap. Best CAD financing = **FDI**; FPI unstable.

=== NOTES 3:106 | Frbm Act - Targets, Escape Clause, N.k. Singh Committee; Fiscal Consolidation Path
## SUB 0 | Frbm Act, 2003 - Origin and Objectives
[106:0:0] - **Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act** - introduced by FM **Yashwant Sinha**; enacted **August 2003**; Rules notified 2004, in force **5 July 2004**.
[106:0:1] - Aim: **fiscal discipline and prudence** by cutting **fiscal deficit, revenue deficit and public debt**. It concerns both FD and RD.
[106:0:2] - Objectives: **inter-generational equity**, long-run macro stability, eliminate RD, cut FD, debt management, **transparency**, balanced budget over time.
[106:0:3] - First Budget of India: **James Wilson, 1860**; of free India: **R.K. Shanmukham Chetty, 1947**.
[106:0:4] Fixing the **repo rate** is NOT an FRBM objective.
[106:0:5] # Original Targets
[106:0:6] TABLE head: Target | Detail
  [106:0:6.0] Fiscal deficit | 3% of GDP by 31 March 2008
  [106:0:6.1] Revenue deficit | Eliminated by 31 March 2009
  [106:0:6.2] Annual cut (Rules) | FD 0.3%, RD 0.5% of GDP a year
  [106:0:6.3] Guarantees | Extra guarantees on Consolidated Fund ≤ 0.5% of GDP a year
[106:0:7] - **Tax-GDP** is not a legal target; it is one of four indicators in the MTFP Statement.
## SUB 1 | What It Prescribes and Does Not
[106:1:0] TABLE head: Prescribes | Does NOT
  [106:1:0.0] Deficit limits | Penal provisions for missing targets
  [106:1:0.1] Statements laid with the Budget | Make the Centre answerable for States
  [106:1:0.2] Disclosures (borrowings, contingent liabilities) | Apply to States - they have own Acts
[106:1:1] "FRBM does NOT provide penal provisions" - the favourite APPSC trap (Group 2, 2017).
## SUB 2 | Statements Laid in Parliament (section 3)
[106:2:0] TABLE head: Statement | Content
  [106:2:0.0] Macro-Economic Framework | Growth, inflation, external balance, assumptions, risks
  [106:2:0.1] Medium-Term Fiscal Policy | 3-year rolling targets for RD, FD, tax-GDP, total outstanding liabilities
  [106:2:0.2] Fiscal Policy Strategy | Tax, spending, borrowing strategy
  [106:2:0.3] Medium-Term Expenditure Framework | 3-year expenditure ceilings; added 2012, laid after the Budget
[106:2:1] - Rolling targets **suspended since 2021-22** (post-pandemic flexibility). Budget 2026-27 laid the MEFS and a combined MTFPS-cum-FPSS.
## SUB 3 | Kelkar Task Force, 2004
[106:3:0] - **Task Force on Implementation of the FRBM Act**, chaired by **Vijay Kelkar**.
[106:3:1] - Meet targets through **tax reform**: wider base + **comprehensive GST**.
[106:3:2] - Kelkar later headed the **13th Finance Commission** (2010-15).
## SUB 4 | N.k. Singh Frbm Review Committee
[106:4:0] - Announced Budget **2016-17**; formed **May 2016**; report **January 2017**; reflected in Budget **2017-18**.
[106:4:1] - Chair **N.K. Singh**; members included **Sumit Bose, Urjit Patel, Rathin Roy**.
[106:4:2] TABLE head: Recommendation | Value
  [106:4:2.0] Principal anchor | Debt-to-GDP; FD = operational target
  [106:4:2.1] General govt debt | 60% by 2023 = Centre 40% + States 20%
  [106:4:2.2] FD | 3% for the next three years (to 2019-20), then 2.5% by 2022-23
  [106:4:2.3] RD | 0.8% by 2023
  [106:4:2.4] Institution | Independent, advisory Fiscal Council (no binding powers)
  [106:4:2.5] Escape clause | Deviation up to 0.5% of GDP
## SUB 5 | Amendments and Suspension Episodes
[106:5:0] TABLE head: Year | Change
  [106:5:0.0] 2004 | Rules notified; RD deadline moved to 2009
  [106:5:0.1] 2008-09 | Targets suspended after the global financial crisis (stimulus)
  [106:5:0.2] 2012 | ERD target and MTEF Statement added
  [106:5:0.3] 2015 | Dates deferred (FD 3% by 2017-18)
  [106:5:0.4] 2018 | Debt anchor: general govt 60%, Centre 40% by 31 March 2025; FD 3% by 31 March 2021; RD/ERD targets dropped; escape and buoyancy clauses
  [106:5:0.5] 2020-21 | COVID: escape clause used; FD 9.2%; no amendment
[106:5:1] - Status: FRBM Act, 2003 (amended 2018). Government's deviation statement admitted missing the 3% FD and 40% Central debt targets.
## SUB 6 | Escape Clause and Buoyancy Clause
[106:6:0] - **Escape grounds**: national security, **war**, **national calamity**, **agricultural collapse**, **structural reforms**, sharp output fall - real growth **3 percentage points below** the average of the previous four quarters. NOT a routine tax shortfall.
[106:6:1] - **Limit**: deviation **≤ 0.5% of GDP** in a year; government must place a justification and a path back before Parliament.
[106:6:2] - **Buoyancy clause** (2018): sharp rise in growth requires FD cut of **at least 0.25%** of GDP - never an automatic increase.
[106:6:3] Escape = up to 0.5% deviation; buoyancy = at least 0.25% cut.
## SUB 7 | Fiscal Consolidation Path
[106:7:0] TABLE head: Year | FD (% GDP)
  [106:7:0.0] 2020-21 | 9.2 (pandemic peak)
  [106:7:0.1] 2024-25 | 4.8
  [106:7:0.2] 2025-26 | 4.4 - "below 4.5%" target (set FY 2021-22) met
  [106:7:0.3] 2026-27 BE | 4.3
[106:7:1] - **Halving commitment** (9.2% in five years) met at 4.4% - a commitment, **not a legislative target**.
[106:7:2] - **New debt rule (Budget 2026-27, 1 Feb 2026)**: FD set yearly to bring Central debt to **50 ± 1% by 31 March 2031** (55.6% BE 2026-27); criticised as above FRBM's **40%**.
[106:7:3] - FY27 FD: 26.8% of target by July (1 Sep 2026).
[106:7:4] - ES 2025-26 (30 Jan 2026): delay strict FRBM-type targets to keep flexibility.
[106:7:5] - **16th FC**: Centre FD **3.5% by 2030-31**; States **3% of GSDP**; State fiscal rules "**effectively unenforced**"; wider deficit definition including **off-budget borrowing** - Union deferred amending the law (11 Mar 2026).
[106:7:6] - Criticism: frequent escape-clause use, deferrals, no penalty, no fiscal council yet.
