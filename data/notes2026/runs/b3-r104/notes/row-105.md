=== Deficit Definitions and Formulas
| Deficit | Formula | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue (RD) | **Revenue expenditure − revenue receipts** | Borrowing for day-to-day consumption (dissaving) |
| Effective RD (ERD) | **RD − grants for creation of capital assets** | True consumption deficit (C. Rangarajan concept) |
| Fiscal (FD / GFD) | **Total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts, i.e. recovery of loans + disinvestment)** | **Total borrowing requirement** |
| Primary (PD) | **FD − interest payments** | Current imbalance, excluding past debt |
| Budget deficit (old) | **Total expenditure − total receipts** (incl. borrowings) | Ad hoc T-bill era measure |
| Monetised | Part of FD financed by the **RBI** (high-powered money) | Inflationary |
| Net FD | **GFD − net lending of the Centre** | e.g. 1,200 − 400 = **₹800 crore** |
- **FD** = total expenditure minus total receipts **excluding borrowings** = **budget deficit + borrowings and other liabilities** (market borrowings) (IAS 1994; APPSC Lecturers 2023; State PSC).
- FD "closest to" the **interest-bearing borrowings during a year** (APPSC G2 2017) – a flow, not accumulated debt.
- Loose exam wording: "excess of total expenditure over revenue receipts" = **fiscal deficit** (APPSC G2 2011) – strictly non-debt capital receipts are also deducted.
- **GFD (financing side) = net borrowing at home + borrowing from RBI + borrowing from abroad** (RAS 2023). Net borrowing at home includes small savings and, indirectly, bank SLR holdings.
- **FD = RD + capital expenditure − non-debt capital receipts**; RD is a part of FD.
- **PD = FD − interest** (= **gross primary deficit** when taken from GFD) (APPSC G2 2017, UPPSC ×4); **PD + interest = FD** (UPPSC 2010). It is NOT revenue deficit minus interest.
- **ERD** introduced in **Union Budget 2011-12**; added to FRBM by the **2012 amendment**; dropped as a target in 2018. ERD shows capital receipts used for consumption.
- RD, FD, PD were adopted as official terms from **1997-98** (Ramesh Singh); budget deficit stopped being the headline measure after ad hoc T-bills ended (1997).
- **Headline marker:** the **fiscal deficit** is the headline marker of fiscal health; RD and ERD show what the borrowing is used for.
# Worked Examples
- FD ₹1,50,000 crore − interest ₹1,20,000 crore = **PD ₹30,000 crore**.
- Gross primary deficit: ₹50,000 − ₹1,500 = **₹48,500 crore** (non-debt capital receipts not deducted again).
- FD ₹4 crore on GDP ₹400 crore = **1% of GDP** (NextGen).
> Trap: "Primary deficit = revenue deficit − interest" is wrong; PD = **fiscal** deficit − interest.

=== Relationships Between Deficits
- **FD > PD** always (interest is positive); **FD > RD** in practice (FD also covers borrowing for capital spending) (UPPSC 2008); **ERD ≤ RD**.
- **PD is never higher than FD** – "PD always higher than FD" is false, "PD = FD − interest" is true.
- **FD > budgetary deficit**: FD also counts borrowings (incl. from the RBI) and other liabilities (UPSC 1999 A-R: both true, R explains A).
- Largest deficit in the GoI budget = **fiscal deficit** (UPPSC 2002 ×3). Descending order: **fiscal > revenue > primary** (2026-27 BE: **4.3 > 1.5 > 0.7**).
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Zero primary deficit | FD = interest; **borrowing just equals interest payment** – debt-trap signal |
| Debt trap | Borrowing to make interest payments |
| Zero RD | Balanced revenue budget |
| Primary surplus | Non-interest spending covered by non-debt receipts |

=== Fiscal Policy – Meaning, Makers and Persons
- **Fiscal policy** = government policy on **taxation, expenditure and public debt** (its income, spending and debt) to shape aggregate demand, allocation and distribution (MPPSC 1996; APPSC Dental 2019).
- Word from Latin **fiscus** = basket / public purse.
- Formulated by the **Ministry of Finance** – not the RBI (monetary policy), Finance Commission or Planning Commission (APSLPRB 2012; UPPSC 2012); Union Budget prepared by the Budget Division, **Department of Economic Affairs (DEA)**.
- **Article 112** calls it the **"Annual Financial Statement"**; the word "Budget" is not in the Constitution. The **budget is an instrument of fiscal policy** (JPSC 2013).
| Person / body | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **J.M. Keynes** | *General Theory* (1936); fiscal policy as the tool against the **Great Depression** (UKPSC 2012) |
| **V.P. Singh** | **Long-Term Fiscal Policy**, laid in Parliament Dec 1985 (Budget year 1985-86) as Finance Minister |
| LTFP's key reform | **MODVAT** – introduced in the 1986-87 Budget (from 1 March 1986), on L.K. Jha Committee lines |
| **Richard Musgrave** | Three budget functions; **differential tax incidence**; popularised "fiscal federalism" (1959) |
| **Montek Singh Ahluwalia** | Chaired the RBI **Advisory Group on Fiscal Transparency** (report 2001) |
| N.K. Singh | Committee 2016, report 2017: general govt debt 60% = Centre 40% + States 20% |

=== Objectives and Instruments; Fiscal vs Monetary
# Objectives (Musgrave functions)
| Function | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Allocation | Provide **public goods** (judiciary, lighthouse, fireworks display); education is a merit good, not a pure public good |
| Distribution | Reduce inequality: **progressive taxation + progressive expenditure** (UPSC 1996) |
| Stabilisation | Control inflation and unemployment – **minimising the risk of inflation** is a fiscal aim (APPSC G2 2011) |
- Objectives: **economic growth, distributive justice and price stability** (APPSC G2 2011), **full employment**, resource mobilisation, **balanced regional development**.
- **Regulation of inter-state trade** is NOT a fiscal objective (UPPSC 2006); directing interest rates and managing money supply are monetary objectives.
- **Economic growth, full employment, exchange stabilisation and price stability** = objectives of **monetary policy** (APPSC G2 2011) – exchange-rate stability is the monetary marker.
- Progressive taxation evidence: direct taxes = **58.8%** of total taxes (FY25).
- Components (taxation, expenditure, debt) and objectives (growth, employment, price stability, equity) are different things; the objectives do not explain the components.
# Instruments
- Fiscal tools: **taxation (tax policy)**, **public expenditure** (capex as **pump priming**), **public borrowing/debt**, the **budget**, subsidies/DBT, transfer payments, **deficit financing** (State PSC; RAS 2023).
- **Interest rate, CRR, bank rate, credit ceiling** = monetary tools, NOT fiscal (APPSC FSO; RAS 2023).
- Public borrowing: **internal** and **external**; external borrowing carries **currency risk**.
- Public expenditure (Centre + States) ≈ **28% of GDP** (ES 2016-17); Union alone **13.6%** (BE 2026-27).
| Policy | Authority | Tools | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fiscal | Govt (MoF) | Taxation, expenditure, public debt, subsidies | Slow (budget cycle) |
| Monetary | RBI / MPC | Repo, CRR, SLR, OMOs, LAF | Fast (MPC **bi-monthly**) |
- Monetary policy is **complementary** to fiscal policy (UPPSC RO 2016).
- **IS-LM classical range** (vertical LM): **fiscal policy is ineffective** (full crowding out) even if investment is interest-elastic; monetary policy is effective.

=== Financing the Fiscal Deficit
- Sources: **market borrowings (G-Secs)**, T-bills, **small savings/NSSF**, **State Provident Funds**, external debt, **draw-down of cash**.
- **Domestic borrowings** fill most of the FD (about **98-99%**) (UPPSC 2005); 2026-27 BE: net market borrowings **₹11.73 lakh crore** of ₹16.96 lakh crore, external debt only ₹15,385 crore.
- **Ad hoc T-bills**: automatic monetisation from the mid-1950s; **1994** RBI-Government agreement; abolished **31 March 1997**; **WMA** from 1 April 1997.
- From **2006-07** RBI barred from subscribing to **primary issues** of G-Secs, except under escape clause; WMA beyond the limit = **overdraft**.
- **8th Plan** public outlay ₹4,34,100 crore – largest source **government borrowings (46.6%)**.
- UPSC 2002 (all four true): **external liabilities in the Union Budget are shown at historical (book-value) exchange rates**; continued high borrowing kept **real interest rates high**; rising FD/GDP **crowds out private investment**; **interest payments = single largest item of non-plan revenue expenditure**.
- Since the 2018 FRBM debt definition, the debt statement also values external debt at **current** exchange rates.
- **PSU dividends** are **non-tax revenue**, NOT a source of financing the fiscal deficit.
- Best way to finance a **current account deficit** = **FDI**; twin deficit = FD + **CAD** (dissaving cuts national savings).

=== Fiscal Stance, Recession and Crowding Out
| Stance | Action | Use / risk |
|---|---|---|
| Expansionary | More spending / lower taxes | Recession; inflation risk |
| Contractionary | Less spending / higher taxes | Overheating |
| Neutral | Balanced effect | Stable times |
- **Recession**: **increase expenditure on public projects** (UPSC 2021); **fiscal stimulus = cutting tax rates + more government spending** – abolishing subsidies is NOT (UPSC 2010 ×2).
- **Counter-cyclical**: deficits in slumps (**Atma Nirbhar Bharat, 2020**), consolidation in booms.
- **Double-dip recession:** recession → short-lived recovery → **another recession**; an ordinary recession = two consecutive quarters of falling GDP (APPSC G2 Mains 2017).
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Crowding out** | **Government borrowing raises interest rates, reducing private investment** (UPSC 2026) |
| Crowding in | Public capex draws private investment |
| Fiscal multiplier | Change in GDP ÷ change in govt spending or taxes |
| Capex multiplier | **2.5-3** in India – largest for capital expenditure |
- **Quality of deficit**: high **RD/FD** ratio = borrowing funds consumption; 2026-27 RD 1.5 / FD 4.3 ≈ **one-third**.
~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Indian Economy 06 p.18.11 (crowding out); app notes 3:105 sub 7; UPSC 2010/2021/2026 PYQ explanations checked against these lines

=== Reducing the Deficit; Subsidies
- Measures: **downsizing bureaucracy** (cuts spending) and **selling/offloading PSU shares** (disinvestment, raises receipts) – **not** FDI inflows or privatising higher education (UPSC 2010 ×3: 3 and 4 only).
- **Reducing revenue expenditure** and **rationalising subsidies** cut the deficit; new welfare schemes add to it (UPSC 2015: I and III).
- Spending side: **rationalising subsidies through DBT**, cutting unproductive spending; receipts side: widening tax base, disinvestment.
- **Financial subsidy** is a big source of budget deficit (true); "subsidy is higher in Indian agriculture than in developed countries" – **false** (UPPSC 2002).
- Major heads: **food, fertiliser, petroleum (LPG)**; total subsidies ≈ **8.5%** of Union expenditure (2026-27). **Urea** = largest fertiliser subsidy item.
- **Fertiliser DBT (2018)**: paid to companies after retail sale on **PoS machines** with **Aadhaar** authentication.
- **Hidden subsidies**: **tax concessions, cheap credit**, under-priced health and education. Forex reserves are not a subsidy head.
- Q1 FY27 major subsidies up **37.4%** on crude prices.
- Development needs a **multi-dimensional approach** (true); the Government focuses on both micro and macro issues (UPPSC 2015: A true, R false).

=== Budget Types and Expenditure Heads
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Balanced** | **Revenue = expenditure** (APPSC G2 2008) |
| Surplus | Receipts > expenditure |
| Deficit | Expenditure > receipts |
| **Zero-based** | Every scheme **reviewed and justified afresh**, no base from last year; Peter Pyhrr, Jimmy Carter; **introduced in India** (UPPSC 2011: A and R true, R explains A) |
| **Performance** | Spending linked to work done – **work management, unit costs, performance standards**; NOT personnel selection (APPSC APRO 2008) |

| Gender | First Gender Budget Statement with Union Budget **2005-06** |
# Receipts
| Receipt | Types |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Tax and non-tax (e.g. **PSU dividends**) |
| Capital – non-debt | **Recovery of loans, disinvestment**, asset monetisation |
| Capital – debt | Market borrowings, small savings, other liabilities |
- Budget 2026-27: **borrowings and other liabilities = 24 paise** of every rupee received – largest single item.
- **Plan vs non-plan** distinction **ended in Budget 2017-18**. Developmental vs **non-developmental** (interest, defence, administration).
- **Charged (Art. 112(3))** – **not voted** by Parliament: President's emoluments, debt charges, SC judges' salaries.
