## SUB 1 | Direct Action Day, 16 August 1946
[249:1:0] - League withdrew its acceptance of the **Cabinet Mission Plan** and on **29 July 1946** called for **"Direct Action"**; Jinnah also condemned Wavell's invitation to Congress to form the interim government.
[249:1:1] - Observed **16 August 1946** to press for **Pakistan** ("bandh" call).
[249:1:2] - Riots began in **Calcutta** – the **"Great Calcutta Killings"** → spread to **Noakhali** (East Bengal), **Bihar** and north India.
[249:1:3] 29 July 1946 = call; 16 August 1946 = Direct Action Day.
## SUB 2 | Gandhi in 1946–48

  [249:2:0.1] March 1947 | Bihar – to calm anti-Muslim riots
  [249:2:0.2] 15 August 1947 | Beliaghata, Calcutta – fasting and praying; took part in no Independence Day celebration, saddened by partition
  [249:2:0.3] 30 January 1948 | Assassinated by Nathuram Godse, New Delhi
[249:2:1] - Outcome at Noakhali: he **failed to stop the enmity and violence**.
[249:2:2] - **Mountbatten** called Gandhi the **"one-man boundary force"** (50,000 soldiers in Punjab vs one man in Bengal).
## SUB 3 | Attlee's Statement and Alternative Schemes
[249:3:0] # Attlee's Statement, 20 February 1947
[249:3:1] - **Clement Attlee** – **Labour Party**; British PM **1945–1951**; PM at independence.
[249:3:2] - Declared in the **House of Commons** that British rule would end by **30 June 1948**, even without agreement among Indian parties.
[249:3:3] - Power could go to **provincial governments** (hint of Balkanisation); appointed **Mountbatten** in place of **Wavell**.
[249:3:4] - Attlee quotes: "**Imperialism is dead as Dodo**"; "The British Government is **not responsible** for the partition of India".
[249:3:5] # Alternative Schemes
[249:3:6] TABLE head: Scheme | Proponent | Proposal and fate
  [249:3:6.0] Plan Balkan / "Dickie Bird Plan" (May 1947) | Mountbatten | Power to provinces (Bengal, Punjab) to join India, Pakistan or stay independent; Nehru rejected it as Balkanisation; soon dropped
  [249:3:6.1] United Bengal plan (1947) | Sarat Chandra Bose + H.S. Suhrawardy | Sovereign undivided Bengal; rejected by both Congress and League
  [249:3:6.2] Dominion status first | V.P. Menon | Immediate transfer on dominion status, without waiting for a new constitution
[249:3:7] - Mountbatten's instructions: **keep India united if possible**.
## SUB 4 | Mountbatten Plan (3 June Plan), 1947
[249:4:0] - **Mountbatten** became Viceroy in **March 1947**; plan announced **3 June 1947** – basis of independence and of partition.
[249:4:1] - Rationale: accepted the Pakistan demand to avoid civil war and administrative collapse.
[249:4:2] # Provisions
[249:4:3] - **Punjab and Bengal:** Legislative Assemblies to meet in **two groups** (Hindu-majority / Muslim-majority districts) and vote by simple majority on partition → **both voted to divide**.
[249:4:4] - **Sindh:** to decide on its own.
[249:4:5] - **Referendums:** **NWFP** → joined Pakistan despite the **Khudai Khidmatgar** boycott (Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan); **Sylhet** (Assam) → joined East Pakistan.
[249:4:6] - **Boundary Commission** for Punjab and Bengal.
[249:4:7] - **Princely states:** paramountcy to lapse; join India or Pakistan – **no third option** of remaining independent.
[249:4:8] - Common Governor-General permitted, but Pakistan chose **Jinnah**.
[249:4:9] - Armed forces divided; power transferred on the basis of the **GoI Act 1935**.
[249:4:10] - **Advanced the transfer date: June 1948 → 15 August 1947**.
[249:4:11] # Congress's Acceptance
[249:4:12] - To avoid **Balkanisation**, secure a **strong Centre** and end **communal bloodshed**.
[249:4:13] - Congress accepted the plan but **never accepted the Two-Nation Theory**.
[249:4:14] - **AICC, Delhi (14/15 June 1947)** accepted the partition resolution, moved by **Govind Ballabh Pant**, under President **J.B. Kripalani**.
[249:4:15] Attlee deadline = 30 June 1948; Mountbatten advanced it to 15 August 1947.
## SUB 5 | Indian Independence Act, 1947
[249:5:0] # Stages
[249:5:1] TABLE head: Stage | Date
  [249:5:1.0] Bill introduced in the House of Commons by Attlee | 4 July 1947
  [249:5:1.1] Passed by Parliament | July 1947
  [249:5:1.2] Royal assent (Act enacted) | 18 July 1947
  [249:5:1.3] Came into effect | 15 August 1947
[249:5:2] - Purpose: gave **legal force to the Mountbatten Plan**.
[249:5:3] 4 July = introduced (not passed); 18 July = royal assent.
[249:5:4] # Provisions
[249:5:5] - **Two independent dominions**, India and Pakistan, with the right to leave the Commonwealth.
[249:5:6] - **Offices ended:** **Viceroy** and **Secretary of State for India**.
[249:5:7] - Each dominion's **Governor-General** acts on its cabinet's advice (constitutional head); a common GG allowed.
[249:5:8] - **Constituent Assemblies made sovereign** – could frame any constitution and **repeal any British law, even this Act**; also acted as legislatures. The Constituent Assembly (as Provisional Parliament) took sovereign power at **midnight of 14/15 August 1947**.
[249:5:9] - **Paramountcy lapsed** → the **565 princely states** free to accede to India or Pakistan.
[249:5:10] - Armed forces divided; **Boundary Commissions** for Punjab and Bengal.
[249:5:11] - **Interim law:** **GoI Act 1935, as adapted**, until new constitutions.
[249:5:12] - British Government to have **no control** over either dominion from 15 August 1947; yet the **British sovereign did appoint** the Governor-General of free India.
[249:5:13] Most distinctive feature: an **"Act without a constitution"** – transferred power but left constitution-making to Indians. Dominion status let India and Pakistan become free before framing constitutions.

## SUB 7 | Radcliffe Boundary Commissions
[249:7:0] - Formed by **Mountbatten** on **30 June 1947**; two commissions – **Punjab** and **Bengal**.
[249:7:1] - Each = **2 Congress + 2 League** nominee judges; **Sir Cyril Radcliffe**, a British lawyer with **no knowledge of Indian conditions beyond the map**, chaired **both**.
[249:7:2] - The **Radcliffe Line** award was ready on **12 August** but published on **17 August 1947**, after independence, so Britain would not be blamed for the violence.
## SUB 8 | Consequences of Partition
[249:8:0] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [249:8:0.0] Assets | Partition Council divided assets and liabilities; Pakistan took 17.5%
  [249:8:0.1] Armed forces | Forces, services and assets divided between the two dominions
  [249:8:0.2] Migration | An estimated 10–15 million crossed the borders; hundreds of thousands killed
  [249:8:0.3] Rehabilitation | Ministry of Relief and Rehabilitation – refugee camps, resettlement, evacuee property
  [249:8:0.4] Kashmir | UN ceasefire 1 January 1949
  [249:8:0.5] Goa | Struggle continued against the Portuguese after 1947; Operation Vijay
[249:8:1] - Criticism: the **hasty advanced date** and the **late Radcliffe award** worsened the violence.

## SUB 3 | Key Interventions – Articles
[251:3:0] TABLE head: Article / provision | Ambedkar's view
  [251:3:0.0] Article 32 (Constitutional Remedies) | The "heart and soul" of the Constitution
  [251:3:0.1] Article 17 | Abolishes untouchability, makes it punishable
  [251:3:0.2] Articles 15–16 | Non-discrimination, equal opportunity
  [251:3:0.3] Article 46 | Welfare of SCs/STs
  [251:3:0.4] DPSP | Equally important for social and economic justice; a "novel feature", like the instruments of instructions
  [251:3:0.5] Emergency (Art. 356) | Hoped it would remain a "dead letter"
[251:3:1] - **Universal adult suffrage**, including women, from the start.
[251:3:2] Article 32 | "heart and soul" | remedies
Article 356 | "dead letter" | emergency
## SUB 4 | Federalism and the 1935 Act
[251:4:0] - A **"Union of States"**: the Union is **indestructible**; states have **no right to secede**.
[251:4:1] - Can work as **unitary or federal** as times require; favoured a **strong Centre** to protect weaker sections.
[251:4:2] - Criticised as a **"copy of the Government of India Act, 1935"** and a **"lawyers' paradise"**.
[251:4:3] - Reply (**4 November 1948**): no need to **apologise** for borrowing tested provisions from the **1935 Act**.
[251:4:4] - **Constitutional morality** "is not a natural sentiment; it **has to be cultivated**" – term taken from **George Grote** (4 Nov 1948).
## SUB 5 | Final Speech – 25 November 1949
[251:5:0] - "**Political democracy cannot last unless there lies at the base of it social and economic democracy.**"
[251:5:1] # Three Warnings
[251:5:2] - 1. Give up **bloody methods**; civil disobedience, non-cooperation and satyagraha now are the **"grammar of anarchy"**.
[251:5:3] - 2. Do not lay liberties at the feet of a great man – **bhakti or hero-worship** in politics leads to **dictatorship**.
[251:5:4] - 3. Turn **political democracy into social democracy** – on **26 January 1950** India enters **"a life of contradictions"** (political equality, social and economic inequality).
[251:5:5] He did NOT call for abolishing the parliamentary system. 4 Nov 1948 = 1935 Act reply + constitutional morality; 25 Nov 1949 = three warnings.
## SUB 6 | Hindu Code Bill
[251:6:0] - Aim: codify Hindu personal law – **monogamy**, **divorce**, **inheritance rights of daughters and widows**.
[251:6:1] - **Fate:** the omnibus bill **stalled** in Parliament; Ambedkar **resigned as Law Minister in September 1951**.
[251:6:2] - Later passed in parts (**1955–56**): **Hindu Marriage Act 1955** (monogamy, divorce), **Hindu Succession Act 1956** (daughters as Class I heirs), Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act.
[251:6:3] - **2005** amendment to the Succession Act gave daughters equal **coparcenary** rights.

[251:7:1] - Significance: embedded a **"social revolution"** – rights, reservation, abolition of untouchability – in the legal framework.
