## SUB 2 | Key Interventions and Speeches
[2:0] TABLE head: Article / theme | Ambedkar's view
  [2:0.0] Article 32 (Constitutional Remedies) | The "heart and soul" of the Constitution
  [2:0.1] Article 17 | Abolishes untouchability
  [2:0.2] Article 356 | Hoped it would remain a "dead letter"
  [2:0.3] DPSP | A "novel feature", like the Instrument of Instructions of the 1935 Act
  [2:0.4] "Union of States" | The Union is indestructible; states have no right to secede; unitary or federal as times require
[2:1] - **4 November 1948** (moving the Draft): the Draft "only provides a **machinery for the government** of the country… not a contrivance to install any particular party in power"; no need to apologise for borrowing from the **GoI Act 1935**; **constitutional morality** "has to be cultivated" (term from **George Grote**); village a "sink of localism".
[2:2] - **25 November 1949** (final speech): "Political democracy cannot last unless there lies at the base of it **social and economic democracy**"; three warnings – give up bloody methods (satyagraha now the **"grammar of anarchy"**), no **hero-worship** (leads to dictatorship), make political democracy social – on **26 January 1950** India enters **"a life of contradictions"** (political equality, social and economic inequality). He did NOT ask to abolish the parliamentary system.
[2:3] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth Indian Polity Part 1 p.66, p.70, p.87, p.93, p.158, p.174, p.268; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 05 p.16; old app notes row 251
## SUB 3 | Law Minister and the Hindu Code Bill
[3:0] - **First Law Minister** of independent India (Nehru's cabinet, 1947) – not M.C. Setalvad (first Attorney-General).
[3:1] - **Hindu Code Bill:** monogamy, divorce, inheritance rights of daughters and widows; it **stalled** → Ambedkar **resigned in September 1951**.
[3:2] - Passed in parts: **Hindu Marriage Act 1955** (monogamy, divorce), **Hindu Succession Act 1956** (daughters Class I heirs), Minority and Guardianship Act, Adoptions and Maintenance Act; **2005** amendment – equal **coparcenary** rights for daughters.
[3:3] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.201; Spectrum Modern India p.624; old app notes row 251
