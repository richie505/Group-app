## SUB 1 | Annihilation of Caste and the Case for Political Power
[1:0] - **1936**, an **undelivered** address for the **Jat-Pat Todak Mandal, Lahore**; the invitation was withdrawn, so Ambedkar **self-published** it.
[1:1] - Caste has **religious sanction in the Shastras**, so the **whole caste system**, not just untouchability, must go; **inter-caste marriage** = the real remedy; **Gandhi replied in Harijan**.
[1:2] - Caste is "not just a division of labour, but a **division of labourers**".
[1:3] - He backed **separate electorates** for the depressed classes because **some political power was necessary for them to protect themselves**; under the Poona Pact he settled for **148 reserved seats** in joint electorates; later felt the Poona Pact made Dalits **dependent on caste Hindus**; criticised Congress for refusing to treat caste as a political issue.
[1:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.202; Spectrum Modern India p.422-425; old app notes row 252

## SUB 3 | State Socialism, Village and Gandhi
[3:0] - **States and Minorities (1947):** State ownership of key industries and **agricultural land**, **collective farming**, **nationalised insurance** – to be written into the **Constitution** so a legislative majority could not undo it.
[3:1] - **Individual**, not the village, the basic unit; rejected **Gram Swaraj** and **trusteeship**; wanted a **strong, centralised** democracy; in the Constituent Assembly (**4 November 1948**) he called the village "a **sink of localism**, a den of ignorance, narrow-mindedness and communalism".

## SUB 4 | Linguistic States, Women, Labour and Education
[4:0] - **Thoughts on Linguistic States (1955):** "**one state, one language**", not "one language, one state"; divide UP, Bihar, MP; **Hyderabad as second capital**.
[4:1] - **Labour Member (1942–46):** backed the **8-hour working day**; Independent Labour Party (1936); resigned as Law Minister in **1951** over the stalled **Hindu Code Bill**.
[4:2] - **Education:** People's Education Society (1945); Siddharth College (1946).
