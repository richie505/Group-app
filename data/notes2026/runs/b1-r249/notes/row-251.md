=== Key Interventions and Speeches
| Article / theme | Ambedkar's view |
|---|---|
| **Article 32** (Constitutional Remedies) | The **"heart and soul"** of the Constitution |
| Article 17 | Abolishes untouchability |
| Article 356 | Hoped it would remain a **"dead letter"** |
| DPSP | A **"novel feature"**, like the **Instrument of Instructions** of the 1935 Act |
| "Union of States" | The Union is **indestructible**; states have **no right to secede**; unitary or federal as times require |
- **4 November 1948** (moving the Draft): the Draft "only provides a **machinery for the government** of the country… not a contrivance to install any particular party in power"; no need to apologise for borrowing from the **GoI Act 1935**; **constitutional morality** "has to be cultivated" (term from **George Grote**); village a "sink of localism".
- **25 November 1949** (final speech): "Political democracy cannot last unless there lies at the base of it **social and economic democracy**"; three warnings – give up bloody methods (satyagraha now the **"grammar of anarchy"**), no **hero-worship** (leads to dictatorship), make political democracy social – on **26 January 1950** India enters **"a life of contradictions"** (political equality, social and economic inequality). He did NOT ask to abolish the parliamentary system.

=== Law Minister and the Hindu Code Bill
- **First Law Minister** of independent India (Nehru's cabinet, 1947) – not M.C. Setalvad (first Attorney-General).
- **Hindu Code Bill:** monogamy, divorce, inheritance rights of daughters and widows; it **stalled** → Ambedkar **resigned in September 1951**.
- Passed in parts: **Hindu Marriage Act 1955** (monogamy, divorce), **Hindu Succession Act 1956** (daughters Class I heirs), Minority and Guardianship Act, Adoptions and Maintenance Act; **2005** amendment – equal **coparcenary** rights for daughters.
