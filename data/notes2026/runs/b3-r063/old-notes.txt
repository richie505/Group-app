## SUB 1 | Industrial Policy Resolution 1948
[63:1:0] - Announced **6 April 1948** by Industry Minister **Dr Shyama Prasad Mukherjee**.
[63:1:1] - Laid the foundation of a **mixed economy** – roles and spheres of both public and private sectors (APPSC 2019, 2023).
[63:1:2] TABLE head: Category | Industries | Arrangement
  [63:1:2.0] Exclusive Central monopoly | Arms and ammunition, atomic energy, railways | State only
  [63:1:2.1] Mixed sector (6) | Coal, mineral oils, iron and steel, aircraft, shipbuilding, telephone/telegraph apparatus | New units by State; existing private units 10 years, then review
  [63:1:2.2] Centrally regulated (18) | Salt, automobiles, heavy chemicals, fertilisers, cement, sugar, paper, textiles etc. | Private, under Central regulation
  [63:1:2.3] Residual | All others | Private and co-operative
## SUB 2 | Industrial Policy Resolution 1956
[63:2:0] # Background
[63:2:1] - Announced **30 April 1956** (placed before Parliament by **Nehru**); reviewed IPR 1948 after the First Plan.
[63:2:2] - Goal: **"Socialist pattern of society"** – Congress **Avadi session, 1955**.
[63:2:3] - Basis of the **Second Plan** (Mahalanobis model – heavy and basic industries).
[63:2:4] # Schedules
[63:2:5] TABLE head: Schedule | Content | Role of State
  [63:2:5.0] A | 17 basic/strategic industries (defence, heavy industry, minerals, transport, power); arms, atomic energy, railways, air transport = monopolies | Exclusive State
  [63:2:5.1] B | 12 industries (other minerals, road and sea transport, machine tools, ferro-alloys, basic chemicals, aluminium) | Progressively State-owned; private may supplement
  [63:2:5.2] C | All remaining | Private initiative, under licensing
[63:2:6] # Major Provisions
[63:2:7] - **Licensing, expansion of public sector, small and cottage industries** (APPSC 2023: all correct); also **balanced regional development, labour welfare**.
[63:2:8] - Licensing rested on the **IDR Act, 1951** → grew into the licence-permit raj; model lasted until 1991.
## SUB 3 | Licensing Committees and Mrtp
[63:3:0] TABLE head: Person / committee | Contribution
  [63:3:0.0] R.K. Hazari report (1967) | Industrial licensing; big business houses cornered licences
  [63:3:0.1] Subimal Dutt – Industrial Licensing Policy Inquiry Committee (1967, report 1969) | Led to the MRTP Act; proposed the joint sector
[63:3:1] - **MRTP Act 1969:** prevent concentration of economic power and monopolistic practices; replaced by the **Competition Act, 2002** (Competition Commission of India).
[63:3:2] - **Joint sector** (State + private partnership): set out by the **Dutt Committee (1969)**, adopted in the **Industrial Licensing Policy 1970** (non-core heavy-investment sector) and the 1973 joint-sector statement. IPR 1948 had created the "mixed sector".
[63:3:3] Joint sector is also linked in exam keys with **IPR 1956** (planned there); the formal joint-sector policy came from the Dutt report (1969-70).
[63:3:4] - Licence raj criticised for **rent-seeking, entry barriers**, firms kept too small.
## SUB 4 | Policy Statements 1973, 1977, 1980
[63:4:0] # 1973 Statement
[63:4:1] - Identified a **core sector** list; tightened MRTP.
[63:4:2] - **Reservation of items for SSI** began in **1967** and continued after 1991 (fully dereserved 2015).
[63:4:3] # Industrial Policy Statement 1977
[63:4:4] - **Janata Government**; drafted largely by **George Fernandes**.
[63:4:5] - Emphasis: **small-scale, tiny and cottage industries**, khadi, handlooms, **employment** (defined "tiny" units).
[63:4:6] - **District Industries Centres (DICs):** one-roof support (credit, raw material, machinery, marketing) in every district.
[63:4:7] - Curbed large houses via **MRTP and FERA** → **IBM and Coca-Cola** left India.
[63:4:8] - Small and cottage industries backed mainly because they generate **large employment**.
[63:4:9] # Industrial Policy Statement 1980
[63:4:10] - Congress government; built on **IPR 1956** with changes.
[63:4:11] - Focus: **technological upgradation**, modernisation, **export orientation** (first strong emphasis).
[63:4:12] - **Economic federalism:** tax holidays and cheap credit for **backward districts**.
[63:4:13] - Removed the "artificial barrier" between large and small units; MRTP firms could expand capacity **25%** without approval; **SSI investment limits raised**; excess capacity regularised.
## SUB 5 | New Industrial Policy 1991 – Background and Delicensing
[63:5:0] # Background
[63:5:1] - **24 July 1991**; **PM P.V. Narasimha Rao**, **FM Manmohan Singh**; after the **balance-of-payments crisis**.
[63:5:2] - Start of **LPG**; ended the **licence-permit-quota raj** – licensing, expansion limits, price controls, import-export curbs removed.
[63:5:3] - Liberalisation tools: **delicensing, import deregulation, lower import duty on capital goods** → cheaper technology and capital equipment, more competitive home manufacturing.
[63:5:4] - Government role moved from **regulator → facilitator**.
[63:5:5] # Compulsory Licensing
[63:5:6] TABLE head: Stage | Industries under licensing
  [63:5:6.0] July 1991 | 18
  [63:5:6.1] 1993 | Automobiles, white goods, leather delicensed
  [63:5:6.2] 1996-97 | Electronic goods, then 5 more delicensed
  [63:5:6.3] Older list | 6 (incl. drugs and pharmaceuticals)
  [63:5:6.4] Now | 5
[63:5:7] - **Present 5:** **alcohol, cigarettes/tobacco, electronic aerospace and defence equipment, industrial explosives, hazardous chemicals**.
[63:5:8] - Drugs and pharmaceuticals later delicensed. Delicensed units file only an **Industrial Entrepreneur Memorandum (IEM)**.
## SUB 6 | Public Sector Reservation and Psu Reform
[63:6:0] TABLE head: Stage | Reserved for public sector
  [63:6:0.0] IPR 1956 | 17 (Schedule A)
  [63:6:0.1] NIP 1991 | 8 – arms and ammunition, atomic energy, atomic minerals, coal and lignite, mineral oils, specified minerals, rail transport etc.
  [63:6:0.2] Later | 3 – arms and ammunition, atomic energy, rail transport
  [63:6:0.3] After 9 May 2001 (arms opened) / now | 2 – atomic energy, railway operations
[63:6:1] - NCERT public-sector-only list: defence equipment, atomic energy, railway transport (**never pharmaceuticals**).
[63:6:2] - **1991 PSU measures:** up to **20% disinvestment** in selected PSUs (began **1991-92**); autonomy via **MoUs**; chronically sick PSUs to **BIFR** (under **SICA 1985**); National Renewal Fund (1992).
[63:6:3] - **2016:** SICA repealed, BIFR dissolved → cases to the **IBC**.
[63:6:4] Today's two are NOT "atomic energy and defence". Reserved 17 → 8 → 3 → 2; licensing 18 → 6 → 5.
## SUB 7 | Nip 1991 – Competition, Foreign Investment and Finance
[63:7:0] # MRTP and Competition
[63:7:1] - **MRTP asset threshold scrapped** → no prior approval for expansion, mergers → **M&A rose**.
[63:7:2] - MRTP replaced by **Competition Act, 2002**.
[63:7:3] # Foreign Investment and Technology
[63:7:4] - **Automatic approval up to 51% foreign equity** in a list of **high-priority, high-investment, high-technology** industries (capital goods, entertainment electronics, food processing) – NOT all industries (APPSC 2012).
[63:7:5] - Automatic approval for **foreign technology agreements** in priority industries; foreign brand names allowed.
[63:7:6] - **FIPB** set up to speed approvals; **abolished June 2017** → FIF portal → National Single Window System (Aug 2022).
[63:7:7] - Equity in **SSI units** by other undertakings: up to **24%** (also foreign equity, 1995-96). SSI investment limit: **₹60 lakh**.
[63:7:8] - Now: **100% automatic route** in most sectors (negative-list approach).
[63:7:9] # Financial and Corporate Provisions
[63:7:10] - **Mandatory convertibility clause** (FIs converting term loans into equity) **abolished** for term loans of new projects – firms freed from the threat of State takeover.
[63:7:11] - Easier capital-market access; equity and technology norms eased.
## SUB 8 | Impact and Criticism of 1991 Policy
[63:8:0] # Impact
[63:8:1] - **M&A** freed from MRTP pre-approval.
[63:8:2] - Forex reserves record **USD 702.28 bn (Nov 2025)**.
[63:8:3] - FDI: repatriation by foreign investors $178.9 bn (2022-23 to 2025-26) → net FDI falling.
[63:8:4] - MSMEs hurt by **import competition** after protection was removed; SSI reservation continued after 1991.
[63:8:5] # Criticism vs Non-Criticism
[63:8:6] TABLE head: Criticisms | NOT criticisms (achievements)
  [63:8:6.0] Unclear policy for weak industries | Higher growth
  [63:8:6.1] Threat of global competition | FDI and forex surge
  [63:8:6.2] Regional concentration (Maharashtra, Gujarat, TN, Karnataka) | Increase in consumer choice
  [63:8:6.3] Small units closed by cheap imports; jobless, capital-intensive growth | Global Indian MNCs
[63:8:7] - **"Neglect of agriculture"** is NOT a listed criticism.
[63:8:8] - **SEZs:** discouraging foreign investment is NOT an objective.

=== NOTES 3:64 | Industrial Sickness – Meaning, Causes, Consequences and Remedies
## SUB 0 | Who Is A Sick Unit?
[64:0:0] TABLE head: Test | Sick if
  [64:0:0.0] SICA 1985 (original) | Registered ≥7 years; accumulated losses ≥ net worth; cash losses
  [64:0:0.1] 1993 amendment | Age cut to 5 years; cash-loss test dropped
  [64:0:0.2] RBI (micro and small) | Any borrowal account NPA > 3 months, or 50% net-worth erosion
  [64:0:0.3] RBI 2016 framework | Early stress: SMA-0/1/2
## SUB 1 | Causes
[64:1:0] TABLE head: Internal | External
  [64:1:0.0] Poor planning, diversion of funds, faulty appraisal | Competition, recession (demand)
  [64:1:0.1] Poor working-capital management, excess debt | Delayed, costly credit
  [64:1:0.2] Obsolete technology, low productivity | Power shortage, transport
  [64:1:0.3] – | Sudden EXIM, tax, environment policy changes
[64:1:1] - MSMEs: **delayed payments** by large buyers and government = major trigger.
## SUB 2 | Consequences and Sectors
[64:2:0] - **NPAs, locked capital, unemployment, revenue loss to the exchequer** (NOT higher revenue).
[64:2:1] - State took over sick private **textile and engineering** units mainly **to protect jobs** → lowered PSU returns.
[64:2:2] - Most sickness: **textiles (cotton mills), jute, sugar, engineering**.
## SUB 3 | Institutions – Irci to Bifr
[64:3:0] - **1971:** Industrial Reconstruction Corporation of India (**IRCI**) → **IRBI** (1985) – first rehabilitation institution.
[64:3:1] - **1985:** **SICA**, after the **Tiwari Committee** (1984-85).
[64:3:2] - **January 1987:** **BIFR** set up; appeals to **AAIFR**; under Department of Financial Services, Ministry of Finance.
[64:3:3] - BIFR functions: **early detection, revival of viable units, closure of unviable units**; covered **public and private** companies.
[64:3:4] "BIFR deals with?" → revival of sick industries (not stock markets or SSI promotion).
## SUB 4 | Bifr Failure and the Ibc
[64:4:0] - Failed due to **procedural delays, legal loopholes** (a SICA reference froze recovery), weak revival – NOT "rapid resolution".
[64:4:1] - BIFR and AAIFR dissolved **1 December 2016**.
[64:4:2] TABLE head: Old regime | Successor
  [64:4:2.0] SICA 1985 | IBC 2016
  [64:4:2.1] BIFR | NCLT (adjudicating authority)
  [64:4:2.2] AAIFR | NCLAT (appellate)
  [64:4:2.3] Debtor-in-possession | Creditor-in-control; regulator IBBI
[64:4:3] - Shift strengthened **creditors**; SICA had protected defaulting promoters.
## SUB 5 | National Renewal Fund and Other Measures
[64:5:0] - **NRF (1992):** social safety net – funded **VRS, retraining, redeployment** of displaced workers; aim = **safeguard workers**; wound up **2000**.
[64:5:1] - **"Golden Handshake"** = **Voluntary Retirement Scheme**, used by public and private firms.
[64:5:2] - **Liberalised Margin Money Scheme (June 1987):** reduced SSI sickness.
[64:5:3] - **RBI:** 2016 MSME revival framework; **one-time restructuring** of MSME loans without downgrade (**2019**, extended 2020-21).
[64:5:4] - Today: companies → IBC (NCLT); delayed payments → **MSME Samadhaan**; MSME Bill (Rajya Sabha, Aug 2026) – CPSEs settle invoices on **TReDS**.
[64:5:5] - **Udyog Bandhu** (now Invest UP) – UP body helping industrial units.
