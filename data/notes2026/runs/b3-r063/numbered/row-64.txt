## SUB 0 | Meaning, Indicators and Causes of Sickness
[0:0] TABLE head: Test | Sick if
  [0:0.0] SICA 1985 (original) | Registered ≥ 7 years; accumulated losses ≥ net worth at year-end; cash losses in that and the preceding year
  [0:0.1] 1993 amendment | Age cut to 5 years; cash-loss test dropped
  [0:0.2] Companies Act (2002 amendment) | Accumulated losses ≥ 50% of average net worth of the preceding 4 years
  [0:0.3] RBI (micro and small) | Any borrowal account NPA > 3 months, or 50% net-worth erosion
  [0:0.4] RBI 2016 framework | Early stress: SMA-0, SMA-1, SMA-2
[0:1] - **Reserves** = accumulated (retained) profits; **net worth** = share capital + reserves – accumulated losses, so sickness = losses wiping out net worth.
[0:2] - **Immediate indicators** (financial symptoms): **drop in profitability**, **shrinking of market credit**, **labour unrest** at the unit; a **decline in market share** is a slower market trend, not an immediate signal (SSC 2005 key).
[0:3] - **Types:** **sickness at birth** (project non-viable from the outset), genuine, induced, temporary.
[0:4] TABLE head: Internal causes | External causes
  [0:4.0] Management inefficiency, poor planning, diversion of funds, faulty appraisal | Competition, recession (demand)
  [0:4.1] Defective capital structure, inability to raise finance, poor working-capital management | Delayed, costly credit
  [0:4.2] Obsolete technology (internal production cause), low productivity | Power shortage, transport
  [0:4.3] – | Sudden EXIM, tax, environment policy changes
[0:5] - MSMEs: **delayed payments** by large buyers and government = major trigger.
[0:6] # Consequences and sectors
[0:7] - **NPAs, locked capital, unemployment, revenue loss to the exchequer** (NOT higher revenue).
[0:8] - State took over sick private **textile and engineering** units mainly **to protect jobs** → lowered PSU returns.
[0:9] - Most sickness: **textiles (cotton mills), jute, sugar, engineering** (not software services).

  [1:0.0] 1971 | Industrial Reconstruction Corporation of India (IRCI) – first rehabilitation body

  [1:0.2] 1997 | IRBI → IIBI (Industrial Investment Bank of India)
  [1:0.3] 1985 | SICA enacted, after the Tiwari Committee (1984-85)
  [1:0.4] January 1987 | BIFR set up (started functioning 1987); appeals to AAIFR
  [1:0.5] 30 Aug 2001 | Sick Industrial Companies (Special Provisions) Repeal Bill introduced in the Lok Sabha; Repeal Act 2003
  [1:0.6] 1 Dec 2016 | BIFR and AAIFR dissolved; cases → NCLT/NCLAT
[1:1] - **BIFR** = **reconstruction and financing of sick units**: early detection, revival of viable units, closure of unviable ones; covered **public and private** companies; under the **Ministry of Finance** (Department of Financial Services).
[1:2] - BIFR failed due to **procedural delays, legal loopholes** (a SICA reference froze recovery) – NOT "rapid resolution".
[1:3] TABLE head: Old regime | Successor

[1:4] - Shift strengthened **creditors**; SICA had protected defaulting promoters.
[1:5] - Rehabilitation needs **effective coordination among stakeholders** (banks, FIs, management, workers, government).

## SUB 2 | National Renewal Fund, VRS and Other Remedies
[2:0] - **National Renewal Fund (Feb 1992):** a **social safety net** – funded **VRS, retraining and redeployment** of workers displaced by restructuring; aim = **social security of workers**, not capital for modernising industries; wound up **2000**.
[2:1] - **"Golden Handshake"** = **Voluntary Retirement Scheme** – a generous payment for leaving service early; used by public and private firms.
[2:2] - **Liberalised Margin Money Scheme (June 1987):** to reduce SSI sickness.
[2:3] - **RBI:** 2016 MSME revival framework; **one-time restructuring** of MSME loans without downgrade (**2019**, extended 2020-21).
[2:4] - Today: companies → IBC (NCLT); delayed payments → **MSME Samadhaan**; MSME Bill (Rajya Sabha, Aug 2026) – CPSEs settle MSME invoices on **TReDS**.
[2:5] - **Udyog Bandhu** (now Invest UP) – UP body **helping industrial units solve time-bound problems** (single window), not a labour or finance agency.
