=== Guilds (Shreni), Merchants and Trade Terms
- **Shrenis:** associations of artisans, merchants, moneylenders under elected or hereditary heads (**jetthaka / pamukha**). Craft shrenis trained workers, procured raw materials, sold products; merchant shrenis organised trade.
- **Powers (UPSC 2012 → II and III):** fixed **wages, rules of work, standards and prices**; had **their own laws (shreni-dharma)** and **judicial powers** over members. They were **autonomous – the king did not interfere**, so "every guild registered with the central authority, king the chief administrator" is **wrong**.
- **Guilds as banks:** deposits invested at interest – **Nashik inscription** records guild deposits at fixed interest.
- **Satapatha Brahmana:** first reference to **money-lending**; usurer = **kusidin**.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sarthavaha** (satthavaha) | **Caravan / convoy of merchants**, or its leader |
| Setthi / Shreshthi | Rich merchant-banker |
| Gahapati | Wealthy householder / landowner |
| Vanija | Trader |
| Kusidin | Usurer |

=== Punch-marked Coins and Early Money
- **Punch-marked (Aahat) coins:** **earliest extant coins** of India (7th–6th c. BC to the Mauryan period); **mostly silver**, some copper; symbols only (**sun, animals, trees, hills**), no writing.
- Called **panas / karshapanas**; issued under Haryankas, Nandas and Mauryas; first definite literary references in **Pali texts**.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Nishka** | **Gold / a gold ornament** in Vedic texts (nishka-griva = gold necklace); a prestige object, later a unit – not cows or copper/silver coins |
| Karshapana / kahapana | Standard silver or copper punch-marked coin |
| Satamana | Value or weight unit of 100 mana |
| Suvarna | Gold coin (Satavahana usage) |
- **Satavahanas** first used inherited punch-marked coins, then die-struck coins of **lead**, potin, copper, silver; **ship-mast** coins of **Yajnasri Satakarni**.
- **Gold coins in large numbers:** **Kushanas** (not Mauryas).
- **Vaishya Tekri** (near Ujjain): Mauryan-era (BCE) stupa bigger than Sanchi; punch-marked and cast Avanti coins; MP to re-excavate it (2026).

=== Northern Black Polished Ware and the Second Urbanisation
- **NBPW:** fine, glossy black, mirror-like **deluxe tableware of the rich**; **c. 700–200 BCE**; **after Painted Grey Ware**; the hallmark of the second urbanisation.
- **NBPW sites:** mainly the Ganga valley – Hastinapur, Kaushambi, Shravasti, Ayodhya, Varanasi, Rajgir, Pataliputra, Vaishali, Champa; also Ujjain, Vidisha, Taxila (not Lothal).
| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Iron tools** | Forest clearance in the Ganga plain; surplus |
| Trade, crafts, coinage | Taxes and wages in money |
| **State formation** | Mahajanapada capitals became administrative centres |
| New merchant classes | Vaishyas backed **Buddhism and Jainism** |
- **Over 60 towns** in 600–300 BCE; the Mahajanapadas and Magadha = the **second urbanisation**.
- **Rajghat (Varanasi):** pre-NBPW levels, punch-marked coins, **Greek-inscribed seals**; BHU re-study (Sep 2026) puts urban Varanasi at **c. 800 BCE**.
| Development | Period |
|---|---|
| Iron at **Mayiladumparai** (AMS dating) | c. 2172 BCE; southern Iron Age c. 2100 BCE – older than the Gangetic Iron Age |
| Iron ploughshares, axes in the Ganga valley | Later Vedic, c. 1000–600 BCE |
| Urban Varanasi | c. 800 BCE |
| NBPW | c. 700–200 BCE |
| Punch-marked coins begin | 7th–6th c. BCE |
| Gold coins in large numbers | Kushanas, 1st–2nd c. CE |
