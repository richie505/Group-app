=== NOTES 1:22 | Vedic Religion, Ritual and Philosophy; the Six Orthodox Schools
## SUB 0 | Nature of Early Vedic Religion
[22:0:0] - **Nature worship** and **polytheism**: gods are forces of nature (storm, fire, sky, dawn); prayers for cattle, progeny, victory.
[22:0:1] - **Henotheism**: term applied by **Max Müller** – each god in turn praised as if supreme.
[22:0:2] - **Rita** = cosmic and moral order; upheld by **Varuna**; later idea of **dharma** grows from it.
[22:0:3] - Iranian parallels: **Mitra–Mithra, Rita–Asha, Soma–Haoma**.
[22:0:4] # Early vs Later Vedic Religion
[22:0:5] TABLE head: Feature | Early (Rigvedic) | Later Vedic
  [22:0:5.0] Chief gods | Indra, Agni, Varuna | Prajapati, Vishnu, Rudra
  [22:0:5.1] Worship | Simple prayers, hymns | Elaborate, costly sacrifices
  [22:0:5.2] Priests | Fewer; Hotri leading | Many priests; heavy dakshina
  [22:0:5.3] Thought | Nature worship | Upanishadic critique of ritual
[22:0:6] - The growing power of priests and the cost of ritual later helped the rise of **heterodox sects** (Buddhism, Jainism, Ajivikas, Charvaka).

## SUB 2 | Vedic Rituals
[22:2:0] TABLE head: Ritual / term | Associated with
  [22:2:0.0] Yajna procedures | Yajurveda
  [22:2:0.1] Purushamedha | Shatapatha Brahmana
  [22:2:0.2] Ceremonial chanting | Samaveda
  [22:2:0.3] Agnistoma | Regular Soma sacrifice
  [22:2:0.4] Rajasuya, Vajapeya, Ashvamedha | Royal sacrifices
  [22:2:0.5] Grihya Sutras | Domestic rites
  [22:2:0.6] Burial and post-burial rites | Megalithic culture
  [22:2:0.7] Fire altars (Kalibangan, Lothal); Cemetery R-37 (Harappa) | Indus fire and burial rituals
[22:2:1] - Priests: **Hotri** (Rig), **Adhvaryu** (Yajur), **Udgatri** (Sama).
[22:2:2] - **Sanskaras: 16** = **3 before birth, 12 during life, 1 after death**; **Upanayana** most important (with wedding and after-death rites).
[22:2:3] - AP angle: **Satavahanas** made Vedic religion official (**Nanaghat**: Indra, Vasudeva, Surya, Chandra, Yama, Varuna); Ikshvaku **Santamula** performed Ashvamedha, Rajasuya, Vajapeya, Agnistoma (**Ashvamedha altar at Nagarjunakonda**); Vakataka **Pravarasena I** did **four Ashvamedhas**; oldest Shivalinga at **Gudimallam (Chittoor)**.
## SUB 3 | Upanishadic Philosophy
[22:3:0] TABLE head: Concept | Meaning
  [22:3:0.0] Brahman | Ultimate reality (cosmic principle)
  [22:3:0.1] Atman | The self – not body or mind
  [22:3:0.2] Karma | Links action with consequence
  [22:3:0.3] Samsara | Rebirth – doctrine introduced by Brihadaranyaka
  [22:3:0.4] Moksha | Liberation – not used in the Vedas; first discussed in the Upanishads
[22:3:1] - **Atman = Brahman**: individual self is one with the cosmic principle.
[22:3:2] - Criticised **mechanical sacrifice**; stressed meditation and the guru-disciple relationship.
[22:3:3] - Aim of the soul = **moksha**, freedom from the cycle of birth and death; **ignorance** = cause of sorrow.
[22:3:4] - **Katha Upanishad**: **Nachiketa** asks **Yama** about death, Atman, immortality.
[22:3:5] - Main theme = **philosophy**; universal approach that rejects no other school, religion or scripture.
## SUB 4 | The Six Orthodox Schools (shad-darshanas)
[22:4:0] TABLE head: School | Founder | Key text | Core idea | Path to liberation
  [22:4:0.0] Samkhya | Kapila | Samkhya Sutra, Tattvasamasa | Purusha-Prakriti dualism; Satkaryavada | Knowing Purusha apart from Prakriti
  [22:4:0.1] Yoga | Patanjali | Yoga Sutras | Discipline of body and mind | Practical method for Samkhya
  [22:4:0.2] Nyaya | Gautama (Akshapada) | Nyaya Sutra | Logic; pramanas; 16 categories | Valid knowledge
  [22:4:0.3] Vaisheshika | Kanada | Vaisheshika Sutra | Atomism; 9 dravyas | –
  [22:4:0.4] Purva Mimamsa | Jaimini | Mimamsa Sutras | Vedic authority, yajna, mantra; Apurva | Through Karma (ritual)
  [22:4:0.5] Uttara Mimamsa (Vedanta) | Badarayana | Brahma Sutra | Brahman the only reality | Knowledge of Brahman
[22:4:1] - **Samkhya = oldest** (APPSC GS 2018).
[22:4:2] - Pairs: **Nyaya–Vaisheshika; Samkhya–Yoga; Mimamsa–Vedanta**.
[22:4:3] - "Nyaya" = decision or logic. Kapila: man's life shaped by **forces of nature, not a god**; Samkhya accepts rebirth; self-knowledge gives liberation.
[22:4:4] - Mimamsa holds the Vedas contain eternal truth; **theory of Karma** = Mimamsa (Karma/Purva/Dharma Mimamsa).
[22:4:5] - **Purva Mimamsa = Karmakanda; Uttara Mimamsa = Jnanakanda**.
[22:4:6] - **Uttara Mimamsa** exponent = **Badarayana** (Brahma Sutra), not Kapila (Samkhya); ⚠ APPSC key (2002) gave **Kapila**.
[22:4:7] - **Apurva** = unseen force linking a deed (karma) with its result.
[22:4:8] Lokayata, Kapalika and Smriti are NOT among the six darshanas.

## SUB 6 | Doctrine → School; Astika vs Nastika
[22:6:0] TABLE head: Doctrine | School
  [22:6:0.0] Satkaryavada (effect pre-exists in cause) | Samkhya
  [22:6:0.1] World formed by blending of nature (Prakriti) | Samkhya
  [22:6:0.2] Atom as basis of world | Vaisheshika
  [22:6:0.3] Niyati (fate) | Ajivika (Makkhali Gosala)
  [22:6:0.4] Materialism | Lokayata / Charvaka (credited to Brihaspati)
  [22:6:0.5] Pramanas | Nyaya, not Samkhya
  [22:6:0.6] Syadvada | Jaina (Kundakundacharya)
  [22:6:0.7] Shunyavada / Madhyamika | Nagarjuna
[22:6:1] - **Astika vs nastika** = belief in the authenticity of the Vedas.
[22:6:2] - **Astika**: the six darshanas. **Nastika**: **Charvaka, Buddhism, Jainism, Ajivika**.
[22:6:3] - **Bauddha** is nastika – it does not take the Vedas as valid knowledge.
[22:6:4] Odd-one-out lists slip one nastika school (Charvaka/Ajivika) among the six.
## SUB 7 | Mimamsa and Vedanta Exponents
[22:7:0] TABLE head: Exponent | School
  [22:7:0.0] Kumarila Bhatta | Purva Mimamsa (Slokavartika, Tantravartika)
  [22:7:0.1] Prabhakara | Purva Mimamsa
  [22:7:0.2] Adi Shankaracharya | Advaita
  [22:7:0.3] Ramanujacharya | Vishishtadvaita
  [22:7:0.4] Madhvacharya | Dvaita
  [22:7:0.5] Lakulisha | Pashupata Shaivism
  [22:7:0.6] Charvaka | Lokayata
[22:7:1] - **Kumarila** defended Vedic ritual (karma-kanda) and opposed Buddhist doctrines; seen as a link between Purva Mimamsa and Vedanta.
[22:7:2] - **Shankara**: founded **four mathas**; called a "**crypto-Buddhist**". **Ramanuja**: first promoter of the Bhakti movement.
[22:7:3] - **Vivekananda's Neo-Vedanta** reworked Advaita.
[22:7:4] # Three Vedanta Schools Compared
[22:7:5] TABLE head: School | Teacher | View of Atman and Brahman
  [22:7:5.0] Advaita | Shankara | Non-dual: Atman and Brahman are identical; mukti through jnana
  [22:7:5.1] Vishishtadvaita | Ramanuja | Qualified non-dualism; first promoter of Bhakti
  [22:7:5.2] Dvaita | Madhva | Dualism ("two")
[22:7:6] - Upanishadic legacy: these concepts form the base of later Hinduism.
[22:7:7] Kumarila Bhatta = Purva Mimamsa, not Vedanta (APPSC G2 2012, 2019).

## SUB 0 | Causes of Second Urbanisation
[24:0:0] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [24:0:0.0] Iron tools | Cleared forests of the Ganga plain; agricultural surplus
  [24:0:0.1] Trade, crafts, coinage | Taxes and wages paid in money
  [24:0:0.2] State formation | Mahajanapada capitals became administrative centres
  [24:0:0.3] New merchant classes | Vaishyas backed Buddhism and Jainism
[24:0:1] - Over **60 towns** flourished in **600–300 BCE**.
## SUB 1 | Northern Black Polished Ware (nbpw)
[24:1:0] - Period **c. 700–200 BCE**; came **after Painted Grey Ware (PGW)**.
[24:1:1] - Glossy, **deluxe ware of elites**; the **hallmark** of the second urbanisation.
[24:1:2] - **Rajghat (Varanasi)**: pre-NBPW levels about 3,000 years old; punch-marked coins and **Greek-inscribed seals**; urban Varanasi about **800 BCE**.
[24:1:3] PGW came first; NBPW followed.
[24:1:4] - **NBPW sites:** found mostly in the **Ganga valley** – **Hastinapur, Kaushambi, Shravasti, Ayodhya, Varanasi, Rajgir, Pataliputra, Vaishali, Champa**; also **Ujjain, Vidisha** and **Taxila**.
## SUB 2 | Chronology of Economic Firsts
[24:2:0] TABLE head: Development | Period
  [24:2:0.0] Iron at Mayiladumparai (AMS date) | c. 2172 BCE; southern Iron Age c. 2100 BCE
  [24:2:0.1] Iron ploughshares, axes (Ganga valley) | Later Vedic, c. 1000–600 BCE
  [24:2:0.2] Urban Varanasi | c. 800 BCE
  [24:2:0.3] NBPW | c. 700–200 BCE
  [24:2:0.4] Punch-marked coins | 7th–6th c. BC to the Mauryan period
  [24:2:0.5] Gold coins in large numbers | Kushanas (1st–2nd c. CE)
[24:2:1] Southern iron (Mayiladumparai) is older than the Gangetic Iron Age.
## SUB 3 | Punch-marked Coins (pmc)
[24:3:0] - Also **Aahat** coins; **earliest extant coins** of India; **mostly silver**, some copper; standard weights.
[24:3:1] - Called **panas / karshapanas**; symbols punched: **sun, animals, trees, hills**.
[24:3:2] - Silver PMC issued under Haryankas, Nandas and Mauryas.
[24:3:3] - **First definite literary references** to coins: **Pali texts** (kahapana, nikkha, kamsa, kakanika), backed by excavated PMC.
[24:3:4] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [24:3:4.0] Karshapana / kahapana | Standard silver or copper PMC
  [24:3:4.1] Nishka / nikkha | Originally a gold ornament or unit; later a coin
  [24:3:4.2] Satamana | Value or weight unit (100 mana)
  [24:3:4.3] Suvarna | Gold coin (Satavahana usage)
[24:3:5] - **Satavahanas** first used inherited PMC, then die-struck coins of **lead, potin, copper, silver**; **ship-mast coins** of **Yajnasri Satakarni**.
[24:3:6] - **Vaishya Tekri** (near Ujjain): BCE stupa **bigger than Sanchi**; gave PMC and cast **Avanti** coins; digging to restart after about 90 years.
[24:3:7] PMC = mostly silver, not gold.
## SUB 4 | Guilds (shreni) and Banking
[24:4:0] - Head of a guild: **jetthaka** or **pamukha** (Buddhist texts); held by heredity or chosen by the members.
[24:4:1] - Functions: bought raw materials, organised production, **controlled quality**, **fixed prices and wages**, found markets; own rules (**shreni-dharma**).
[24:4:2] - **Nashik inscription**: **guild deposits at fixed interest** – guilds acted as banks.
[24:4:3] - **Satapatha Brahmana**: first reference to **money-lending**; usurer = **kusidin**.
