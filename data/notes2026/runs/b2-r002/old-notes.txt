=== NOTES 2:2 | Sources of the Indian Constitution – Borrowed Features and Their Origin Countries
## SUB 0 | A "bag of Borrowings" – Overview
[2:0:0] - "**Drawn from various sources**" is a listed salient feature: most of the world's constitutions + the **GoI Act 1935** → the **lengthiest written constitution** in the world.
[2:0:1] - Original: **395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules** → now about **470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules**.
[2:0:2] - **Philosophical part** (FRs, DPSPs) and **political part** (parliamentary system) borrowed; **structure** largely from the 1935 Act.
[2:0:3] - Criticisms: **"bag of borrowings"**, **"elephantine"** (length), **"lawyers' paradise"** (legal detail).
[2:0:4] - Reply: features adapted to Indian conditions; Ambedkar called DPSP a **"novel feature"**; federal-unitary switch is India's own.
[2:0:5] - The **USA** has the world's **first written Constitution**, also the **shortest** (only **7 Articles**).
[2:0:6] - The **Constitution** is the highest authority in the Indian political system.
[2:0:7] Answer key view: **British Constitution** had the most profound influence (parliamentary government, cabinet, rule of law); the GoI Act 1935 is the single largest **textual** source.
## SUB 1 | Government of India Act 1935 – the Single Largest Source
[2:1:0] - Structural and administrative backbone:
[2:1:1] - 1. **Federal scheme** (federal system largely adopted from the 1935 Act; division of powers)
[2:1:2] - 2. **Office of Governor**
[2:1:3] - 3. **Judiciary** (Federal Court as model; integrated judiciary)
[2:1:4] - 4. **Public Service Commissions**
[2:1:5] - 5. **Emergency provisions**
[2:1:6] - 6. **Administrative details**
[2:1:7] - 1935 Act's **Federal, Provincial and Concurrent Lists** → Constitution's **Union, State and Concurrent Lists**.
[2:1:8] - Its administrative detail is a reason for the Constitution's **bulk**.
[2:1:9] - The 1935 Act did **NOT** provide for a written Constitution made by Indians; that demand was accepted in principle in the **August Offer, 1940**.
## SUB 2 | Parliamentary Features – Britain

  [2:2:0.0] Parliamentary government, conventions of cabinet system | Britain
  [2:2:0.1] Single citizenship (USA has dual) | Britain (APPSC GS 2019)
  [2:2:0.2] Bicameralism | Britain (APPSC GS 2024)
  [2:2:0.3] Rule of Law, legislative procedure (⚠ APPSC key (2010) gave Rule of Law as a US borrowing) | Britain
  [2:2:0.4] Office of Speaker and his role | Britain
  [2:2:0.5] Prerogative writs | Britain
  [2:2:0.6] Parliamentary privileges | Britain
  [2:2:0.7] Nominal head (President like the Crown) | Britain
[2:2:1] - Britain has **no single written constitution**; India borrowed the system and its conventions.
[2:2:2] "Which feature is from Britain?" – cabinet system, single citizenship, bicameralism. Distractors: USA, Canada.
## SUB 3 | Rights and Judicial Features – Usa
[2:3:0] TABLE head: Feature | Source
  [2:3:0.0] Fundamental Rights (Part III, Arts. 12-35) | USA
  [2:3:0.1] Judicial review | USA
  [2:3:0.2] Independence of judiciary | USA
  [2:3:0.3] Idea of the Preamble | USA
  [2:3:0.4] Post of Vice-President | USA
  [2:3:0.5] Impeachment of the President | USA
  [2:3:0.6] Removal of SC/HC judges | USA
[2:3:1] - Judicial review in India is **narrower**: Art. 21 uses "procedure established by law", not "due process".
[2:3:2] - **Integrated judiciary**: one chain of courts enforces both Central and State laws; the **USA** has separate federal and state courts.
[2:3:3] - FR chapter from the USA is **not** the reason for the Constitution's length.
## SUB 4 | Federal Features – Canada and Australia
[2:4:0] # Canada
[2:4:1] TABLE head: Feature | Source
  [2:4:1.0] Federation with a strong Centre; "Union of States" with a strong Centre (Art. 1) | Canada
  [2:4:1.1] Residuary powers with the Centre (Art. 248 – Parliament) | Canada
  [2:4:1.2] Appointment of Governors by the Centre | Canada
  [2:4:1.3] Advisory jurisdiction of the SC (Art. 143) | Canada
[2:4:2] - Indian federalism is based on the **Canadian** pattern; "Union of States": not the result of an agreement, no right to secede.
[2:4:3] - In the USA and Australia, residuary powers are with the **States**.
[2:4:4] # Australia
[2:4:5] TABLE head: Feature | Source
  [2:4:5.0] Concurrent List | Australia
  [2:4:5.1] Joint sitting of both Houses (Art. 108, presided by the Lok Sabha Speaker) | Australia
  [2:4:5.2] Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse | Australia
  [2:4:5.3] Language of the Preamble | Australia
[2:4:6] - Makers followed the Canadian scheme of division but **added a third list** – the Concurrent List.
[2:4:7] - Concurrent List: Parliament and State Legislatures both legislate; **Union law prevails** in a clash.
[2:4:8] Residuary powers | Canada | not USA
Concurrent List | Australia | not Canada

[2:5:1] Election of RS members = South Africa; nomination to RS + President's election = Ireland. Amendment procedure is NOT from Ireland or France.
## SUB 6 | Emergency, Japan, Ussr and France
[2:6:0] TABLE head: Feature | Source
  [2:6:0.0] Emergency provisions (general scheme) | GoI Act 1935 (APPSC G2 2023 key)

  [2:6:0.2] Procedure established by law (Art. 21) | Japan
  [2:6:0.3] Fundamental Duties (Art. 51A) | USSR
  [2:6:0.4] Ideals of social, economic and political justice (Preamble) | USSR
  [2:6:0.5] Five-Year Plans / Planning Commission | USSR
  [2:6:0.6] Republic; liberty, equality, fraternity (Preamble) | France
[2:6:1] - Fundamental Duties: **not** in the original Constitution; added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**; 11th duty by the **86th Amendment**.
[2:6:2] - In a National Emergency, FRs can be suspended **except Articles 20 and 21** (Art. 20 – protection in respect of conviction; Art. 21 – life and personal liberty); **Art. 19** is the most easily suspended.
[2:6:3] When a list gives "Emergency provisions except Financial Emergency – Weimar Germany" and no 1935 option, it is the accepted match; when both appear, Emergency = 1935 Act, suspension of FRs = Weimar.
[2:6:4] Fundamental Duties = USSR (not France); justice ideals = USSR (not Australia) – two separate USSR ideas.
