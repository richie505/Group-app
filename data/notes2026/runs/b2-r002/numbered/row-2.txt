## SUB 0 | Ireland and South Africa – Directive Principles, President's Election, Rajya Sabha, Amendment
[0:0] # Ireland (Eire, Constitution of 1937)
[0:1] - **Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Arts. 36-51)** – borrowed from the **Irish Constitution** (Ireland itself took the idea from **Spain**) (asked 11 times: APPSC G2 2003, MPDO 1998, TWO 2012, SI 2019; UPPSC, MPPSC 2023).
[0:2] - **Method of election of the President** (indirect, proportional representation by single transferable vote) – Ireland.
[0:3] - **Nomination of members to the Rajya Sabha** (12 by the President) – Ireland.
[0:4] - **⚠ Trap:** DPSP is **not** from Weimar Germany, South Africa, Canada or Britain; Ambedkar called the DPSP a **"novel feature"** of the Constitution.
[0:5] # South Africa
[0:6] - **Procedure for amendment of the Constitution (Art. 368)** – South Africa (SSC 2016; APPSC FSO).
[0:7] - **Election of members of the Rajya Sabha** (by elected MLAs, proportional representation) – South Africa (APPSC 2019).
[0:8] - **⚠ Trap:** the amendment procedure is **not** from France, Ireland, Britain or the USA (APPSC Lecturers 2018: "Amendment – France" is the wrong statement).
[0:9] > RS pair: **election** of RS members = **South Africa**; **nomination** to the RS and the **President's election** = **Ireland**.

## SUB 1 | USA – Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Preamble and the Judiciary
[1:0] TABLE head: Feature | Borrowed from
  [1:0.0] Fundamental Rights (Part III, Arts. 12-35) | USA (Bill of Rights)
  [1:0.1] Judicial review | USA
  [1:0.2] Independence of the judiciary | USA
  [1:0.3] Idea of the Preamble | USA
  [1:0.4] Post of Vice-President (ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha) | USA
  [1:0.5] Impeachment of the President | USA
  [1:0.6] Removal of Supreme Court and High Court judges | USA
  [1:0.7] Written Constitution | USA – the world's first written Constitution (1787), also the shortest (7 Articles)
[1:1] - **Fundamental Rights = USA** (asked 6 times: APPSC Degree Lecturers 2020; UPPSC, UPRO 2014, UDA 2010).
[1:2] - **Judicial review originated in the USA** – *Marbury v. Madison* (1803); the words "judicial review" appear in **neither** the US nor the Indian text; **Britain** (parliamentary sovereignty, no written constitution) has **no judicial review** of Acts of Parliament.
[1:3] - **Preamble idea = USA** (asked 5 times); only the **language** of the Preamble is from **Australia**; liberty, equality, fraternity = France.
[1:4] - Judicial review in India is narrower: Art. 21 uses **"procedure established by law"** (Japan), not the US **"due process of law"**.
[1:5] - **USA = presidential** form of government (India, Britain, Canada are parliamentary).
[1:6] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.154-155, p.164; Rocket Polity #75 (Rule of Law, Part 2 p.85-86); app notes 2:2 sub 3
## SUB 2 | Britain – Parliamentary Features, Rule of Law, Single Citizenship
[2:0] TABLE head: Feature | Borrowed from
  [2:0.0] Parliamentary government, cabinet system | Britain
  [2:0.1] Single citizenship (USA has dual citizenship) | Britain
  [2:0.2] Bicameralism | Britain
  [2:0.3] Rule of Law, legislative (law-making) procedure | Britain
  [2:0.4] Office of Speaker and his role | Britain
  [2:0.5] Prerogative writs ("petitions") | Britain
  [2:0.6] Parliamentary privileges | Britain
  [2:0.7] Nominal head (President, like the Crown) | Britain
[2:1] - "Which feature is from Britain?" – **cabinet system**, **single citizenship**, **bicameralism**, **writs** (APPSC 2019, FRO; SSC 2019). **Independence of judiciary** is US, not British (APPSC Panchayat Secy 2017 → **1, 2, 3**).
[2:2] - **Britain has no single written constitution**, but the **concept of constitutional government** goes back to the **Magna Carta (1215)**; the first **written** constitution is the US one.
[2:3] - **Rule of Law** – A.V. **Dicey** (1885): supremacy of law, equality before law, and rights of the individual secured by courts; its main objective is to secure the **liberty of citizens** against arbitrary power. A **basic feature** (Kesavananda).
[2:4] - **Single citizenship** – one of the framers' crucial steps to **tackle regionalism** and build national unity.
[2:5] - **Contempt of court:** the power of courts to punish for contempt comes from **English common law** (British-era courts; now Arts. 129, 215).
[2:6] - **Integrated judiciary:** one hierarchy (SC → HCs → subordinate courts) enforces both Central and State laws, unlike the US dual courts; this **single system of courts was adopted from the GoI Act 1935** (Laxmikanth), so "borrowed from the British system" is the false reason; India's judicial system follows the British common-law pattern.
[2:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.154-155, p.164-165, p.187-188; Rocket Polity #75 (Part 2 p.85-87); Laxmikanth, Indian Polity ch. Salient Features (integrated judiciary); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contempt_of_court_in_India; app notes 2:2 sub 2
## SUB 3 | Federal Features – Canada and Australia
[3:0] # Canada
[3:1] - **Federation with a strong Centre** – Indian federalism follows the **Canadian** pattern (SSC 2008; UPPSC 2017; APPSC DAO 2011).
[3:2] - **"Union of States" (Art. 1):** **not the result of an agreement** among States and **no right to secede**; the Republic of India rests on the **declared will of the people** ("We, the people of India").
[3:3] - **Residuary powers with the Centre** – **Art. 248** (Parliament) – Canada (SSC CGL 2019; APPSC HWO); in the **USA and Australia** residuary powers lie with the **States**.
[3:4] - **Appointment of State Governors by the Centre** – Canada.
[3:5] - **Advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court (Art. 143)** – Canada.
[3:6] # Australia
[3:7] - **Concurrent List** – Australia (asked 7 times: APPSC 2017, Social History 2019, G2; UPPSC 2015, 2016).
[3:8] - **Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse** – Australia (not Canada).
[3:9] - **Joint sitting** of the two Houses (**Art. 108**, presided over by the **Lok Sabha Speaker**) – Australia.
[3:10] - **Language of the Preamble** – Australia.
[3:11] - Makers followed the Canadian scheme of division but **added a third list** (Concurrent); in a clash, the **Union law prevails**.
[3:12] - **Seventh Schedule today:** Union **98**, State **59**, Concurrent **52** (originally **97, 66, 47**).
[3:13] > Residuary powers = **Canada**, not USA; Concurrent List = **Australia**, not Canada. With no 1935 option, "scheme of federation" = **Canada**.
[3:14] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.154-155; Rocket Polity #1 p.3; app notes 2:2 sub 4, sub 7
## SUB 4 | Government of India Act 1935 – the Largest Source, and the Length of the Constitution
[4:0] - **Single largest source:** federal scheme, **office of Governor**, **judiciary**, **Public Service Commissions**, **emergency provisions**, administrative details – "larger part" of the Constitution (APPSC JA 2012).
[4:1] - **Greatest / most profound influence** on the Constitution = **GoI Act 1935** (about 250 provisions), not the British Constitution (APPSC G1 2002; G1 PYQ – some answer keys gave "British Constitution").
[4:2] - **Federal system and distribution of powers** between Centre and States = the **1935 Act** scheme (UPSC 2012; APPSC G2).
[4:3] - **1935 lists:** Federal (**59**), Provincial (**54**), **Concurrent (36)** items (SI 2023) → today's **Union, State and Concurrent Lists**.
[4:4] - **Not** a 1935 constituent: a **written Constitution made by Indians** – accepted in principle only by the **August Offer, 1940** (UPPSC 2010).
[4:5] # Why the Constitution is the lengthiest
[4:6] - **Factors:** **vast geography and diversity**; **bulky 1935 Act**; a **single Constitution for Centre and States**; dominance of **legal luminaries**; detailed administrative and **social-economic** provisions; framers' wish to **safeguard the young democracy**.
[4:7] - **NOT a factor:** "adoption of the American model" (APPSC ATWO); **local bodies** were **not** in the original Constitution (Parts IX, IXA added in **1992**) (APPSC ASWO Mains).
[4:8] - **Original:** **395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules** → now about **470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules** (as of 2026).
[4:9] - **Criticisms:** **"bag of borrowings"** (sources), **"elephantine"** (length), **"lawyers' paradise"** (legal detail); reply – borrowed features were **adapted** to Indian conditions.
[4:10] - **Philosophical part** (FRs, DPSPs) from the USA and Ireland; **political part** (parliamentary system) from Britain; **structural part** from the 1935 Act.
[4:11] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams p.154-155, p.160-161; Indian_Polity Part 1 (lengthiest constitution); app notes 2:2 sub 0-1; rebuilt notes 2:0 (GoI Act 1935)
## SUB 5 | Emergency, USSR, France, Japan
[5:0] TABLE head: Feature | Borrowed from
  [5:0.0] Emergency provisions (general scheme) | GoI Act 1935; with no 1935 option → Germany (Weimar)

  [5:0.2] Fundamental Duties (Art. 51A) | USSR (added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976; 11th duty by the 86th, 2002)

[5:1] - **Emergency:** "on the lines of" Germany (APPSC 2017; NDA 2009 – Weimar); **not** from South Africa, the USA or the UK.
[5:2] - In a National Emergency FRs can be suspended **except Arts. 20 and 21**; **Art. 19** is the most easily suspended.
[5:3] - **⚠ Trap:** "Ideals of **Justice**" is the one Preamble ideal **not** from France – it is from the USSR (APPSC DAO 2020); Fundamental Duties = USSR, not France.
[5:4] - **Election Commission (Art. 324)** – an Indian provision, not inspired by any foreign constitution (JPSC 2023).
