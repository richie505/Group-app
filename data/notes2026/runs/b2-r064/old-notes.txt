## SUB 2 | Contesting More Than One Seat and the 1996 Amendment
[64:2:0] - Cap of **two** came in **1996** after **Devi Lal** contested **three** Lok Sabha seats in **1991**; before 1996 no cap.
[64:2:1] - Reforms: **one candidate, one seat** (Law Commission 255th); **vacating candidate pays** by-poll costs (ECI).
[64:2:2] - **RP (Amendment) Act, 1996**: two-seat cap; **higher deposits**; more proposers for independents/unrecognised parties; countermanding only on death of a **recognised-party** candidate (party may name a replacement); paid holiday (135B); liquor ban (135C); by-polls in 6 months (151A); disqualification for offences under the **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971**.
[64:2:3] - **Rajya Sabha** (2003 amendment): any registered elector in India can contest from any State; upheld in **Kuldip Nayar (2006)**.
[64:2:4] - Most Lok Sabha/Assembly elections were held in the **1990s** (frequent government changes).

## SUB 5 | Electoral Bonds
[64:5:0] - Scheme notified **2 January 2018** (via Finance Act 2017); aim stated: transparency.
[64:5:1] - Sold **only by SBI**; **bearer** bonds; **₹1,000 – ₹1 crore**; valid **15 days**.
[64:5:2] - Buyer: a **citizen of India** or a body incorporated in India – alone or jointly.
[64:5:3] - Cashable only by a registered party with **at least 1% votes** in the last election.
[64:5:4] - **ADR v Union of India, 15 Feb 2024**: **5-judge**, **unanimous** – scheme **unconstitutional**; violated voters' **right to information, Art. 19(1)(a)**; struck down removal of the **corporate donation cap** (Companies Act); **SBI** to give donor data to the **ECI**.
[64:5:5] 2024 = bonds **struck down**, not introduced.
## SUB 6 | State Funding of Elections
[64:6:0] TABLE head: Type | Meaning
  [64:6:0.0] Partial | State meets some costs, cash or in kind (airtime, fuel, rallies)
  [64:6:0.1] Full | State bears all fair costs; private donations banned
[64:6:1] - Recommended by **Indrajit Gupta (1998)**, **Dinesh Goswami (1990)** (government funding of Parliamentary polls), **2nd ARC** (partial).
[64:6:2] - **170th Report**: full funding desirable only if parties are barred from other sources.
[64:6:3] - Countries: **Germany, Sweden, Japan, Canada**.

[64:6:6] - Idea (Jan 2026): **National Election Fund** – pooled corporate donations shared by vote share.
## SUB 7 | Evm and Vvpat
[64:7:0] # Firsts
[64:7:1] TABLE head: Date | Milestone
  [64:7:1.0] 19 May 1982 | First EVM use – 70-Parur (Kerala); A.C. Jose v Sivan Pillai
  [64:7:1.1] 1989 | Sec. 61A – statutory backing
  [64:7:1.2] 1998 | 16 Assembly seats in MP, Rajasthan, Delhi
  [64:7:1.3] 1999 | Goa – first whole-State Assembly poll on EVMs (APPSC-G2 2025: true)
  [64:7:1.4] 2004 | 14th Lok Sabha – first all-EVM national election
  [64:7:1.5] 4 Sep 2013 | VVPAT first – 51-Noksen (Nagaland) by-election
[64:7:2] - Not Gujarat; Goa was NOT the first VVPAT.
[64:7:3] # Machines
[64:7:4] - Makers: **BEL (Bengaluru)**, **ECIL (Hyderabad)** – ECIL is AP/Telangana's link.
[64:7:5] - **M3**: up to **2,000 votes**; one control unit takes **24 ballot units = 384 candidates incl. NOTA**; tamper detection, self-diagnostics. **M2**: 4 BUs, 64 candidates; older models recorded up to 3,840 votes. VVPATs: M1, M2, M3.
[64:7:6] - **VVPAT** slip (serial no., name, symbol) seen through a window for **7 seconds** – not handed over. EVMs show **colour photos**.
[64:7:7] # Courts and Rules
[64:7:8] - **Subramanian Swamy v ECI (2013)**: VVPAT needed for free and fair polls, in phases.
[64:7:9] - **N. Chandrababu Naidu (2019)**: VVPAT tally **1 → 5 polling stations** per Assembly segment.
[64:7:10] - **ADR v ECI (April 2024)**: no 100% slip count, **no paper ballots**; **symbol-loading units sealed 45 days**; **2nd/3rd-placed** candidates may seek a check of **5% of EVMs** within **7 days**.
[64:7:11] - New ECI rules: VVPAT slips counted wherever EVM data ≠ **Form 17C**. Counting order: **postal ballots → penultimate EVM round → final round**.
[64:7:12] - ECI admitted EVMs "are still a subject of frequent public questioning" (2 Sep 2026).
[64:7:13] # RVM and Totaliser
[64:7:14] - **Remote EVM (RVM)**: for **domestic migrants**; up to **72 constituencies** from one remote station; shown to parties **Jan 2023**.
[64:7:15] - **Totaliser**: pools several control units to **hide booth-wise trends**; Law Commission 255th; SC sought Centre's response (Sep 2026); ECI: **no legal framework**.

=== NOTES 2:66 | Party System in India – One-party Dominance, Multi-party and Coalition Eras
## SUB 0 | Nature of the Indian Party System
[66:0:0] - India has a **multi-party system** because society divides along **region, language, caste, religion and class**; a two-party system (UK, USA) presumes one dominant axis.
[66:0:1] - The **original Constitution** had no provision on political parties; they entered via the **Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985)**.
[66:0:2] - **ECI classification**: national, State, registered-unrecognised parties (**cadre vs mass** is an organisational classification).
[66:0:3] - **RUPPs**: over **2,800**; only about **750** contested 2024.
[66:0:4] - Assertion-reason keys: no fixed party system due to too many parties, casteism, regionalism and regional leaders forming parties; parties are the **life-blood of democracy** and people blame them for bad governance (**R explains A**).
[66:0:5] - **Mahatma Gandhi** advised **disbanding the INC** as a political party after Independence.
[66:0:6] - Rise of the **BJP** → polarisation of voters on communal basis (per key). **TDP** is a regional party, not national.
