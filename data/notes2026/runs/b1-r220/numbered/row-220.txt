## SUB 0 | Early Labour Organisations and the First Trade Unions
[0:0] # Before 1918 – philanthropic phase
[0:1] - **Bombay workers' housing:** mill hands lived in **chawls** – multi-storey one-room tenements on a shared corridor (not wadas or kutiyas).
[0:2] - **Sasipada Banerjee:** Workingmen's Club, Bengal, **1870**; journal *Bharat Sramjeebi*.
[0:3] - **N.M. Lokhande:** associate of Phule; weekly *Dinbandhu* (1880); formed the **Bombay Millhands' Association (1890)** – the **first labour organisation** in India (not a trade union in the modern sense); hence the **pioneer of the labour movement**.
[0:4] # 1918-1922 – first trade unions
[0:5] TABLE head: Union | Year | Founder
  [0:5.0] Madras Labour Union – first trade union | April 1918 | B.P. Wadia (Annie Besant's associate), with V. Kalyanasundaram; Buckingham and Carnatic Mills
  [0:5.1] Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (Majoor Mahajan) | 1920, after the 1918 mill strike | Gandhi with Anasuya Sarabhai
  [0:5.2] Girni Kamgar Union (Bombay) | May 1928 | S.A. Dange and the communists – led the six-month 1928 textile strike
[0:6] - **ILO, Washington 1919:** **N.M. Joshi** went as the Indian workers' delegate.
[0:7] - **First May Day in India:** **Madras, 1923**, by **M. Singaravelu Chettiar** (Labour Kisan Party of Hindustan).
[0:8] - **1929 Bombay strike (GKU and railway workers):** keyed as a protest against the **dismissal of workers who took part in the 1928 strikes**.
[0:9] - **Phases:** 1918-26 early unions; **1926-39 militant (left-wing) phase** – communist-led strikes 1927-29, AITUC splits; 1939-45 war phase.
[0:10] > Lokhande = first labour association (1890); Wadia = first trade union (1918); Gandhi = Majoor Mahajan (1920).
[0:11] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.156-160; app notes 1:220
## SUB 1 | AITUC and the Later Central Trade Unions
[1:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [1:0.0] Founded | Bombay, 31 October 1920
  [1:0.1] Founders | N.M. Joshi, Lala Lajpat Rai, Joseph Baptista, Diwan Chaman Lal
  [1:0.2] First session president | Lala Lajpat Rai (not Joshi, C.R. Das or Bose)
  [1:0.3] Vice President / General Secretary | Joseph Baptista / Diwan Chaman Lal
  [1:0.4] 3rd and 4th sessions | C.R. Das presided
  [1:0.5] Nagpur 1929 | Jawaharlal Nehru presided; moderates under N.M. Joshi walked out → Indian Trade Union Federation (V.V. Giri president), later the National Trades Union Federation
  [1:0.6] 1931 | Communists left → Red Trade Union Congress (rejoined 1935)
[1:1] - N.M. Joshi = co-founder, later General Secretary; his March 1921 resolution led to the Trade Unions Act 1926.
[1:2] - **Order:** AITUC **1920** → ITUF 1929 → RTUC 1931 → **INTUC 1947** (Congress) → **HMS 1948** → UTUC 1949 → BMS 1955 → **CITU 1970** (CPI(M)).
[1:3] - **Hind Mazdoor Sabha:** Calcutta, **December 1948**, socialists – R.S. Ruikar President, **Ashok Mehta** General Secretary, **G.G. Mehta** Secretary, **T.S. Ramanujam** Vice-President.
[1:4] - Unions declined after the **1982 Bombay textile strike**; peak 1950s-70s (**1974 railway strike**).
[1:5] > The ITUF of 1929 was the moderates' (Joshi/Giri), not Dange's.
[1:6] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.159-162; app notes 1:220
## SUB 2 | Labour Laws and the Whitley Commission
[2:0] TABLE head: Law / body | Year | Provision
  [2:0.0] Factories Act | 1881 | First factory law – child labour (no child under 7; 9 hours for 7-12)
  [2:0.1] Factories Act | 1891 | Weekly holiday; women 11 hours a day
  [2:0.2] Trade Unions Act | 1926 | Registration and legal status of unions; from N.M. Joshi's 1921 resolution
  [2:0.3] Trade Disputes Act | 1929 (11 April) | Tribunals (Courts of Inquiry, Boards of Conciliation); banned strikes that coerced the government / hit public utilities
  [2:0.4] Public Safety Bill | 1929 | Against foreign communists such as Philip Spratt
  [2:0.5] Whitley Commission (Royal Commission on Labour) | Appointed 1929, reported 1931 | Conditions of labour – N.M. Joshi and Chaman Lal members
[2:1] - The 1881 Act did **not** fix wages or allow unions (UPSC 2017).
[2:2] - **Motilal Nehru** called the Trade Disputes Act **"the Slavery of India Bill"**.
[2:3] - **2026** = **100 years** of the Trade Unions Act, 1926.
[2:4] > Whitley = labour; Simon = constitution; Lee = civil services.
[2:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Modern History Part 5 p.35, p.157, p.160; Spectrum p.620; Rocket Indian History Part 2 p.95
## SUB 3 | Communist Party of India – Founding, Line and Bans
[3:0] # Founding
[3:1] TABLE head: Event | Fact
  [3:1.0] Founded abroad | Tashkent, October 1920 (17 Oct), by M.N. Roy under the Comintern
  [3:1.1] Tashkent founders | Roy, Abani Mukherji, Mohd. Ali, Mohd. Shafique, Evelyn Roy Trent, Rosa Fitingov, M.P.T. Acharya (not S.A. Dange)
  [3:1.2] Formal founding in India | Kanpur conference, 26 December 1925, organised by Satyabhakta, presided by Singaravelu
[3:2] - **M.N. Roy** = first Indian in the Communist International (2nd Comintern Congress, 1920); paper **Vanguard**.
[3:3] - CPI dates itself from **Kanpur 1925**; CPI(M) from **Tashkent 1920**.
[3:4] - **CPI and RSS both 1925** (RSS: 27 Sept 1925, Nagpur, **K.B. Hedgewar**).
[3:5] - Complete independence first demanded by communists at **Ahmedabad 1921** – moved by **Hasrat Mohani** and **Swami Kumarananda**.
[3:6] # Line, bans and offshoots
[3:7] TABLE head: Event | Year
  [3:7.0] British ban | 23 July 1934; lifted 1942
  [3:7.1] Revolutionary Communist Party of India | 1934, Saumyendranath Tagore
  [3:7.2] Radical Democratic Party | 1940, M.N. Roy
  [3:7.3] "People's War" line | From June 1941 – war became a people's war after the Axis attacked the Soviet Union; CPI backed Britain, stayed out of Quit India
  [3:7.4] Banned in Hyderabad State | 1946
  [3:7.5] First communist state government | Kerala, 1957 (E.M.S. Namboodiripad)
  [3:7.6] Split | 1964 → CPI and CPI (Marxist)

## SUB 5 | Congress Socialist Party, 1934
[5:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [5:0.0] Forerunner | Bihar Socialist Party, July 1931 – Jayaprakash Narayan Secretary (at Phulan Prasad Varma's house)
  [5:0.1] Founded | Patna, May 1934 – first All-India Congress Socialists' Conference, convened by JP
  [5:0.2] Policies fixed | Bombay, October 1934 (not January 1934)
  [5:0.3] President | Acharya Narendra Dev
  [5:0.4] General Secretary | Jayaprakash Narayan
  [5:0.5] Founders | Narendra Dev, JP, Lohia, Achyut Patwardhan, Ashok Mehta and others
  [5:0.6] Programme | Socialism inside the Congress; work among workers and peasants
[5:1] - Not CSP aims: boycott of goods and tax evasion, **dictatorship of the proletariat**, **separate electorates** (UPSC 2015 – none correct).
[5:2] - **Nehru never joined** the CSP; Lohia was a founder, not General Secretary.
[5:3] - **Nehru and socialism:** chaired the **first All India Socialist Youth Congress** (Calcutta, Dec 1928); in **1933** called for ending British rule, princes, landlordism and capitalism; attended the **Brussels Congress of Oppressed Nationalities, February 1927** (not Motilal); AITUC president 1929.
[5:4] - **Bombay Manifesto, 1936:** 21 Bombay businessmen **openly opposed Nehru's socialism**; it did **not** win wide business support.
