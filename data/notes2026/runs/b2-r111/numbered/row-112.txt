## SUB 3 | Ethnicity – Concept, Identity and Minority
[3:0] # Concept
[3:1] - **Ethnic group** = people who identify with each other on a common **language/dialect, history, society, culture or nation**; markers – language, religion, race, region, culture.
[3:2] - **Consensus approach:** groups **coexist** with separate identities; **conflict approach:** ethnic groups are **interest groups competing unequally** for power and resources (South Africa, Sri Lankan Tamils).
[3:3] - **Xenocentrism** = preferring another group's culture and **rejecting one's own**; opposite of **ethnocentrism**.
[3:4] # Views on ethnic identity
[3:5] TABLE head: View | Core idea | Scholars
  [3:5.0] Primordialist | Identity given by birth; "primordial attachments" | Clifford Geertz, Edward Shils
  [3:5.1] Instrumentalist | Elites mobilise identity for power | Paul Brass; Rajni Kothari (*Politics in India*); Christophe Jaffrelot (*India's Silent Revolution*)
  [3:5.2] Constructivist | Identity socially made, kept by boundaries | Fredrik Barth (*Ethnic Groups and Boundaries*); Benedict Anderson (*Imagined Communities*)
[3:6] # Minority
[3:7] TABLE head: Scholar | Contribution
  [3:7.0] T.K. Oommen | Separates nation, nationality, ethnicity and citizenship
  [3:7.1] Arnold Rose | Minority = group set apart by race, nationality, religion or language, regarded as different by itself and others
  [3:7.2] Giddens | Minority marked by disadvantage, group solidarity, isolation
[3:8] - **National minority:** non-dominant citizen group with its own ethnic, religious or linguistic identity; term from **European minority-rights law**.
[3:9] - Abroad: **Rohingya** – Sunni Muslim minority of **Rakhine State, Myanmar**; China recognises **55** minority groups, **Hui** = largest Muslim group (Uyghurs too); China's "ethnic unity and progress" law (Mar 2026); Sri Lanka Tamil-Muslim common platform (Jul 2026).
