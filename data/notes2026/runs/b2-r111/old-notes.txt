=== NOTES 2:111 | Tribe – Definition, Classification, Distribution and Characteristics
## SUB 0 | Meaning of Tribe
[111:0:0] - The word **'tribe'** comes from the Latin **'tribus'**.
[111:0:1] - **Marshall Sahlins**: a tribe is a **segmental** body of equal, **unspecialised multi-family groups, clans or bands**.
[111:0:2] - **Animism** = belief in and worship of **nature spirits**.
[111:0:3] - **Totemism** = a clan's sacred (mystic) bond with an **animal or plant totem**, which is revered as a group symbol and **not killed**; it goes with **clan exogamy**.
[111:0:4] - **Shifting cultivation** = slash-and-burn farming; called **Jhum** in North-East India.
## SUB 1 | Scheduled Tribes – Constitution and Criteria
[111:1:0] # Articles
[111:1:1] TABLE head: Article | Provision
  [111:1:1.0] Art. 366(25) | Defines STs as communities "deemed under Article 342" (the term took shape under the GoI Act 1935 and the Constitution)
  [111:1:1.1] Art. 342(1) | President notifies STs after consulting the Governor; first order = Constitution (Scheduled Tribes) Order, 1950
  [111:1:1.2] Art. 342(2) | Only Parliament, by law, can include or exclude a community
[111:1:2] - ST lists are **State/UT-specific**, not one list for all India.
[111:1:3] 342(1) = President notifies; 342(2) = only Parliament amends the list.
[111:1:4] # Criteria (NOT in the Constitution)
[111:1:5] - Drawn from **Census 1931**, **Kalelkar Committee (1955)**, **Lokur Committee (1965)** and **Chanda Committee (1969)**.
[111:1:6] - Lokur-type tests: primitive traits, distinct culture, geographical isolation, shyness of contact, backwardness.
[111:1:7] # Number of Notified STs
[111:1:8] - **705** individual ethnic groups are notified as STs, in **30 States/UTs** (Census 2011 count).
[111:1:9] - **Andhra Pradesh: 34** notified STs (undivided AP list under the **1976** Amendment Order = **33**).
[111:1:10] - Lists are revised by later amendment orders after the **SC and ST Orders (Amendment) Act, 1976**.

## SUB 4 | Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs)
[111:4:0] # Origin and Numbers
[111:4:1] - **Dhebar Commission (1960-61)** flagged the least-developed groups.
[111:4:2] - **PTG** category created in **1973**; renamed **PVTGs** in **2006**.
[111:4:3] - **75 PVTGs** in **18 States + 1 UT (Andaman and Nicobar)**.
[111:4:4] - **Odisha 13** (highest); **AP with Telangana 12**; Bihar 9.
[111:4:5] - Population about 27.68 lakh (2001); **Sahariyas** = largest PVTG; **Sentinelese** and Andamanese = smallest.
[111:4:6] # AP PVTGs
[111:4:7] - Joint list for "Andhra Pradesh and Telangana" = **12**: **Bodo Gadaba, Bondo Poroja, Chenchu, Dongaria Khond, Gutob Gadaba, Khond Poroja, Kolam, Konda Reddi, Konda Savara, Kutia Khond, Parengi Poroja, Thoti**.
[111:4:8] - Older spellings: Bondo Poraja, Parangiperja, Kuttiya Kondhs, Kondareddis, Konda Savaras.
[111:4:9] - **Kolam and Thoti** are on the Telangana side; the list was never split after 2014. Residual AP = **10** groups.
[111:4:10] - PVTG population in AP under PM-JANMAN: **4,92,552** (21 Nov 2024) – among the **top three States**.
[111:4:11] # PM-JANMAN and Rights
[111:4:12] - **PM-JANMAN** (PM Janjati Adivasi Nyaya Maha Abhiyan), **Ministry of Tribal Affairs**; launched **15 Nov 2023** (Janjatiya Gaurav Divas); covers **all 75 PVTGs**.
[111:4:13] - Budget 2023 announced **₹15,000 crore**; approved outlay **₹24,104 crore** (Centre ₹15,336 crore, States ₹8,768 crore) for **2023-24 to 2025-26**.
[111:4:14] - Housing, water, health, roads, livelihood.
[111:4:15] - **Bharia (Madhya Pradesh)** = first PVTG with **habitat rights** under **FRA 2006, Sec. 3(1)(e)**.
[111:4:16] - Census 2027: Tribal Affairs Ministry sought separate PVTG enumeration.
## SUB 5 | Denotified and Nomadic Tribes
[111:5:0] TABLE head: Year | Event
  [111:5:0.0] 1871 | Criminal Tribes Act branded ~200 communities "hereditary/born criminals" (amended 1924)
  [111:5:0.1] 1949 | All India Criminal Tribes Inquiry Committee (Ayyangar Committee)
  [111:5:0.2] 31 Aug 1952 | Act repealed → "Denotified" tribes; replaced by State Habitual Offenders Acts
  [111:5:0.3] 2008 | Renke Commission
  [111:5:0.4] 2017 | Idate Commission
  [111:5:0.5] 30 Jan 2026 | RGI agreed to enumerate DNTs in Census 2027; communities demand a "separate column"
[111:5:1] - **Nomadic tribes** = no permanent settlement (pastoralists, hunters, entertainers). DNTs were last counted in **Census 1931**.
[111:5:2] - Lists are state-specific: **Banchhada** (MP, Rajasthan), once a "criminal tribe", is listed as **SC**.
[111:5:3] Renke (2008) came before Idate (2017).

## SUB 8 | Tribal Firsts and News
[111:8:0] - **Verrier Elwin** – first anthropologist to study Indian tribes extensively.
[111:8:1] - **Tilka Manjhi** – first tribal freedom fighter against the British.
[111:8:2] - **Indira Gandhi National Tribal University** (Amarkantak, 2007) – first tribal university.
[111:8:3] - **Droupadi Murmu** – first ST (and first tribal woman) President.
[111:8:4] - **Ministry of Tribal Affairs** – set up **1999**; National Tribal Policy first drafted **2006**.
[111:8:5] - **Shompen** – voted for the first time in the **2024 Lok Sabha** election (19 April 2024, A&N seat; 7 members); protected by the **A&N (Protection of Aboriginal Tribes) Regulation, 1956**; Great Nicobar project dispute raises FRA Sec. 3(1)(e) habitat rights.

=== NOTES 2:112 | Ethnicity – Concept, Ethnic Identity and Ethnic Movements in India
## SUB 0 | Ethnicity – Concept and Markers
[112:0:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [112:0:0.0] Ethnic group | People who identify with each other on common language/dialect, history, society, culture or nation
  [112:0:0.1] Markers | Language, religion, race, region, culture
  [112:0:0.2] Intra-ethnic conflict | Within a group
  [112:0:0.3] Inter-ethnic conflict | Between groups
[112:0:1] # Approaches
[112:0:2] - **Consensus approach**: groups **coexist** with separate identities.
[112:0:3] - **Conflict approach**: groups are **interest groups competing unequally** for power and resources (e.g. **South Africa, Sri Lankan Tamils**).
[112:0:4] # Scholars on Ethnicity and Minority
[112:0:5] TABLE head: Scholar | Contribution
  [112:0:5.0] T.K. Oommen | India's foremost scholar on ethnicity; separates nation, nationality, ethnicity and citizenship (*Citizenship, Nationality and Ethnicity*)
  [112:0:5.1] Arnold Rose | Minority = group set apart by race, nationality, religion or language; sees itself and is seen by others as different, with negative connotations
  [112:0:5.2] Giddens | Minority marked by disadvantage, group solidarity, physical/social isolation
## SUB 1 | Views on Ethnic Identity
[112:1:0] TABLE head: View | Core idea | Scholars
  [112:1:0.0] Primordialist | Identity given by birth; deep "primordial attachments" | Clifford Geertz, Edward Shils
  [112:1:0.1] Instrumentalist | Elites mobilise identity for power and resources | Paul Brass; Rajni Kothari (*Politics in India*); Christophe Jaffrelot (*India's Silent Revolution*)
  [112:1:0.2] Constructivist | Identity socially made, kept by boundaries | Fredrik Barth (*Ethnic Groups and Boundaries*); Benedict Anderson (*Imagined Communities*)
[112:1:1] Barth = boundaries (constructivist); Geertz = primordial attachments; Kothari / Jaffrelot / Brass = instrumentalist.
## SUB 2 | Ethnic Groups, Languages and Minorities of India
[112:2:0] # Linguistic Diversity
[112:2:1] - **Census 2011**: **121 languages**, **270 mother tongues**; **22 Scheduled Languages**; **19,500+** mother tongues returned.

  [112:2:3.2] Khasi, Garo | Meghalaya | Official languages with English (2026); not in the Eighth Schedule

[112:2:5] # Notified Minorities (NCM Act, 1992)
[112:2:6] - **Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Parsis**, and **Jains** (added **2014**).
## SUB 3 | Ethnic Minorities and National Minority
[112:3:0] - **National minority**: a **non-dominant citizen group** with its own **ethnic, religious or linguistic** identity; the term comes from **European minority-rights law**.
[112:3:1] - **Ethnic Muslim minority groups** abroad:
[112:3:2]   - **Uyghurs** and **Hui** (China) – China has **55** recognised minority groups; the **Hui** are China's largest Muslim group.
[112:3:3]   - **Rohingya** – Sunni Muslim minority of **Rakhine State, Myanmar**; denied citizenship, effectively **stateless**.
[112:3:4] - **China's law on "ethnic unity and progress" (Mar 2026)**: covers **55 minority groups** (Uyghurs, Hui, Tibetans); critics say it erodes non-Han identity; **Han over 91%**.
[112:3:5] - **Sri Lanka (Jul 2026)**: Tamil and Muslim parties formed a **common platform**.

=== NOTES 2:113 | Processes of Social Change – Sanskritisation, Westernisation, Modernisation, Secularisation
## SUB 0 | Factors and Sources of Social Change
[113:0:0] TABLE head: Factor | Example
  [113:0:0.0] Technology | Industrial Revolution (Watt, Hargreaves); internet and social media – 820 million+ internet users (Dec 2025)
  [113:0:0.1] Education | Spread of modern education
  [113:0:0.2] Law | Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955; widow remarriage and sati laws
  [113:0:0.3] Media | Behaviour-change communication (Economic Survey 2025-26, SBCC); street plays in Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan
  [113:0:0.4] Migration / urbanisation | Industrialisation and urbanisation
  [113:0:0.5] Planning | Green Revolution (3rd Plan); inclusive growth (11th Plan); technology-led modernisation from the 7th Plan
[113:0:1] - **Media** is a modern social institution: spreads information, **creates awareness, forms public opinion**, drives change and **cultural diffusion**; types – print, electronic, social.
[113:0:2] - Dalit change came through **state policy, reform movements, Sanskritisation and Westernisation**.
[113:0:3] - New development model for developing societies stresses **non-material indicators** (quality of life, equity, wellbeing).
[113:0:4] - **ILO (2025)**: India's social protection coverage **64.3%**, up from **19% (2015)**; 25 crore → 100 crore people.
[113:0:5] **Seasonal migration of nomads** is a routine cycle – **NOT** a source of social change (APPSC 2019).
## SUB 1 | Theories of Social Change
[113:1:0] TABLE head: Theory | Proponents
  [113:1:0.0] Evolutionary (linear) | Comte (three stages), Herbert Spencer (simple → complex), L.H. Morgan, Durkheim (mechanical → organic solidarity)
  [113:1:0.1] Cyclical | Spengler (*Decline of the West*), Toynbee (challenge and response), Sorokin (sensate/ideational cultures), Pareto (circulation of elites)

## SUB 2 | Diffusion Processes and Cultural Terms
[113:2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [113:2:0.0] Acculturation | Change through sustained contact with another culture; influenced but stays distinct (APPSC 2019)
  [113:2:0.1] Assimilation | Absorption into the dominant culture; minority loses its identity
  [113:2:0.2] Integration | Participation while retaining identity
  [113:2:0.3] Cultural diffusion | Spread of culture
  [113:2:0.4] Cultural relativism | Understanding a culture on its own terms
  [113:2:0.5] Ethnocentrism | Own culture seen as superior
  [113:2:0.6] Cultural pluralism / Multiculturalism | Coexistence of different cultures
  [113:2:0.7] Ethnic group | Group sharing a common culture (clan, lineage, sapinda are kinship groups)
  [113:2:0.8] Xenophobia | Fear of foreigners
  [113:2:0.9] Regionalism / Communalism / Casteism | Loyalty to a region / religious group / caste
[113:2:1] - Ghurye's *The Aborigines So-Called and Their Future* argued for **tribal assimilation**; *The Tribal World of Verrier Elwin* – Elwin, on tribes.
[113:2:2] Acculturation = influenced but distinct; assimilation = distinct identity lost.
## SUB 3 | Sanskritisation (m.n. Srinivas)
[113:3:0] - **Coined by M.N. Srinivas** in *Religion and Society among the Coorgs of South India* (**1952**) (APPSC 2019); developed in *Social Change in Modern India*.
[113:3:1] - First called **"Brahminisation"**.
[113:3:2] - Meaning: a **low caste or tribe** adopts the customs, rituals and way of life of a higher, often **twice-born**, caste to claim higher rank.
[113:3:3] - **Reference groups**: **Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas** or the local **dominant caste**.
[113:3:4] - Brings **positional, not structural** change – mobility for a caste, but the **hierarchy stays intact**.
[113:3:5] - **De-sanskritisation**: rejecting Sanskritic norms and stressing own identity (e.g. **non-Brahmin / Dravidian movement**).
[113:3:6] - Srinivas's set: **Sanskritisation + Westernisation + Secularisation + Dominant Caste**; urbanisation and education change authority patterns but do **not end the joint family**.
## SUB 4 | Westernisation
[113:4:0] - Srinivas: changes from **150+ years of British rule**.
[113:4:1] - **Levels**: **technology, institutions, ideology, values**.
[113:4:2] TABLE head: Westernisation | Modernisation
  [113:4:2.0] Source-specific – West/British | Universal, any source
  [113:4:2.1] Ethically neutral (Srinivas's preference) | Value-loaded ("progress")
## SUB 5 | Modernisation
[113:5:0] - **Daniel Lerner**, *The Passing of Traditional Society* (**1958**): sequence **urbanisation → literacy → media exposure → participation**; linked **"empathy"** with **psychic mobility**.
[113:5:1] - **Yogendra Singh**, *Modernization of Indian Tradition* (**1973**): studies Sanskritisation, Islamisation, Westernisation and modernisation at **macro and micro** levels.
[113:5:2] - **Milton Singer**, *When a Great Tradition Modernizes*: **tradition and modernity coexist** (Madras temple culture).
[113:5:3] - **Indicators**: rationality, scientific temper, industrialisation, urbanisation, literacy, mass media, democratic participation.
## SUB 6 | Cultural Lag (ogburn)
[113:6:0] - **W.F. Ogburn**, *Social Change* (**1922**) – originator.
[113:6:1] - **Material culture** (technology) changes faster; **non-material culture** (beliefs, norms) trails = **delay in adjusting** to change.
[113:6:2] - **Lagging parts**: **religion, law, tradition** – **not politics** (UPPSC 2014).
[113:6:3] - Example: fast digital spread vs slow rules on children's online safety – **DPDP Act, 2023** (parental consent under 18).
## SUB 7 | Secularisation
[113:7:0] - **Sociological secularisation**: decline of religion's hold on social life; **changing beliefs and practices**; differentiation and **rationalisation**.
[113:7:1] - **Max Weber**: **"disenchantment of the world"**.
[113:7:2] TABLE head: Sociological secularisation | Constitutional secularism
  [113:7:2.0] Change in society | Duty of the state
  [113:7:2.1] Beliefs and practices change | State neutrality and equal respect (Arts. 25-28)

=== NOTES 2:114 | Social Stratification and Mobility in India
## SUB 0 | Stratification – Meaning, Bases, Features
[114:0:0] - **Stratification**: ranking of groups by unequal **wealth, prestige, power**.
[114:0:1] - Bases: **caste, class, gender, age, race, ethnicity**; traditional – caste, varna, tribe, religion; modern – education, occupation, income, **political power, urbanisation**.
[114:0:2] - **Tumin**: social, **ancient, universal**, diverse, **consequential** (decides life chances).
[114:0:3] - Muslims: **Ashraf, Ajlaf, Arzal**.
[114:0:4] **Language is NOT** a basis of stratification (APPSC 2019).
## SUB 1 | Theories
[114:1:0] TABLE head: Approach | Thinker | Idea
  [114:1:0.0] Functionalist | Davis and Moore (1945) | Unequal rewards pull scarce talent into key posts
  [114:1:0.1] Critique | Melvin Tumin | Wastes talent; passes inequality on
  [114:1:0.2] Conflict | Marx | Ownership of means of production; class struggle
  [114:1:0.3] Multi-dimensional | Weber | Class, status, party; market situation decides life chances
  [114:1:0.4] Ideological | Dumont, *Homo Hierarchicus* | Purity-pollution
## SUB 2 | Caste vs Class and Other Systems
[114:2:0] TABLE head: System | Feature
  [114:2:0.0] Caste | Birth, endogamy, inherited occupation; closed; cumulative inequality
  [114:2:0.1] Class | Education, wealth, occupation; open; dispersed inequality
  [114:2:0.2] Estate | Medieval Europe – clergy, nobility, commoners
  [114:2:0.3] Slavery | Person as property – Greece, Rome
[114:2:1] - **Varna** = four-fold, theoretical; **Jati** = real, local birth groups; ranking by **ritual purity**.
[114:2:2] - Harappa: citadel vs lower town, **no rigid caste**; varna hardened in **Later Vedic** period.
[114:2:3] - Caste is vertical (ranking by birth) and horizontal (separate groups).
[114:2:4] - **A.W. Green**: caste ideally allows **no mobility up or down**; **Ghurye**: hierarchy, endogamy, restrictions (*Caste and Race in India*).
[114:2:5] - **Caste status**: fixed by birth, linked to occupation. Hereditary occupations: **potter, blacksmith, weaver**; also kingship, property; weakened by education, industrialisation, urbanisation, **occupational mobility**.
[114:2:6] - Today: conflicts over **power, not ritual**; **Jajmani** weakened; dominance on secular factors.
## SUB 3 | Social Mobility
[114:3:0] TABLE head: Type | Meaning / example
  [114:3:0.0] Vertical | Up/down; farmer's son → IAS
  [114:3:0.1] Horizontal | Same level; clerk to equal job
  [114:3:0.2] Inter-generational | Parents → children; labourer's son → engineer
  [114:3:0.3] Intra-generational | Within one lifetime
  [114:3:0.4] Structural | Change in occupational structure
[114:3:1] - **Sorokin**, *Social Mobility* (**1927**): first set out vertical and horizontal mobility.
[114:3:2] - **Ascribed** (caste, gender, family) vs **achieved** (IAS officer, doctor, scientist).
[114:3:3] - Group mobility: **Sanskritisation** and **dominant caste** (**Srinivas**: numbers + arable land + fairly high ritual rank); **Kshatriyaisation** (**Kulke**) – Marathas, Jats, Yadavs; reproduces hierarchy.
## SUB 4 | Agrarian Classes
[114:4:0] - **Daniel Thorner**: by land rights and income – **Maliks** (landlords on rent), **Kisans** (own, family labour), **Mazdoors** (tenants, sharecroppers, landless).
[114:4:1] - **Andre Beteille**: *Caste, Class and Power* (**1965**), **Sripuram, Thanjavur**; caste, class, power **diverge**; *Studies in Agrarian Social Structure*.
## SUB 5 | Middle Class
[114:5:0] - **Macaulay's Minute, 1835** → English-educated middle class (**A.R. Desai**, *Social Background of Indian Nationalism*).
[114:5:1] - World Bank **$10-50**/day (PPP); NCAER **₹2-10 lakh** (2001-02); PRICE **₹5-30 lakh**.
[114:5:2] - **43.2 crore (31%) in 2021 → 61% by 2047**; OECD: world's largest by **2035**.
## SUB 6 | Mobility Routes, Dalits and OBCs
[114:6:0] - **ES 2025-26**: drivers – **health, education, technology, institutions**; poverty **21.9% (2011-12) → 2.3% (2023-24)**.
[114:6:1] - Reservation: **Periyar's Self-Respect Movement**; group mobility.
[114:6:2] - **Ezhavas** (Kerala): toddy tapping → tailoring.
[114:6:3] - Cities weaken caste; migrants keep networks.
[114:6:4] - **Dalits**: state policy, reform movements, Sanskritisation/Westernisation.
[114:6:5] - **Mandal**: OBCs **over 52%**; **11 indicators = 4 social + 4 economic + 3 educational**.
[114:6:6] - **Telangana survey**: **242 castes**; **135 (67%)** above average backwardness.
[114:6:7] - **Marathas, Gujjars, Patels** seek OBC status. AP: **Kakatiya** society relatively open (**Velamas, Kammas, Reddys**); **Virashaivism** a "lubricant" of mobility.
[114:6:8] - **ES 2025-26 on low mobility:** low social mobility **entrenches historical inequality**.
[114:6:9] - **Why OBC demands:** dominant peasant castes such as **Marathas, Gujjars and Patels** seek OBC status mainly for **greater life chances** (jobs, education), not ritual rank.
