=== NOTES 3:48 | Agricultural Inputs – Seeds, Fertilisers and Subsidy Regime, Pesticides, Mechanisation, Extension Services
## SUB 0 | Fertilisers – Urea, NBS, Neem Coating, Nano and DBT
[48:0:0] # Urea and the subsidy regime
[48:0:1] - **Urea:** white, highly water-soluble, **46% nitrogen** (closest exam option: 47%); loses N quickly by **volatilisation and leaching**, hence slow-release forms (**urea briquettes**, neem coating).
[48:0:2] - **Urea = most subsidised fertiliser**; stays under **statutory price control** (fixed MRP); **not** covered by NBS. Budget 2026-27: urea subsidy Rs 1,16,805 crore vs NBS Rs 54,000 crore.
[48:0:3] - **Nutrient Based Subsidy (NBS), 2010:** the policy now followed for **P&K fertilisers** – a **fixed per-kg subsidy on N, P, K and S**, announced yearly; covers **22 grades** of decontrolled P&K; **manufacturers set the MRPs**.
[48:0:4] - Fertiliser subsidy = **second largest subsidy after food**; Budget 2026-27 **Rs 1.71 lakh crore** (RE 2025-26: Rs 1.86 lakh crore).
[48:0:5] - India: **2nd largest consumer, 3rd largest producer** of fertilisers. National Investment Policy for Urea-2026 adds **10 MT** urea capacity.
[48:0:6] TABLE head: Reform | Year | Provision
  [48:0:6.0] NBS | 2010 | Fixed per-nutrient subsidy on P&K (and S)
  [48:0:6.1] Neem-coated urea | Notified May 2015; all domestic urea from 1 Sep 2015, imported from 1 Dec 2015 | Neem coating slows the release/dissolution of urea (nitrogen) in soil and stops diversion of farm urea to industry
  [48:0:6.2] DBT in fertilisers | Pilot from October 2016 | Subsidy paid to fertiliser companies after sale at PoS devices (Aadhaar-linked), not to farmers
  [48:0:6.3] PM-PRANAM | Approved 28 June 2023 | States keep 50% of the subsidy saved by cutting chemical fertiliser use
  [48:0:6.4] Nano DAP (liquid) | Launched by Amit Shah, April 2023 | IFFCO; 8% N, 16% P
[48:0:7] - **IFFCO Nano Urea:** one **500 ml bottle = one 45 kg bag**; foliar spray.
[48:0:8] # Balance and efficiency
[48:0:9] - **N:P:K = 10.9 : 4.1 : 1 (2023-24)** vs ideal **4:2:1** – the urea-centred subsidy **distorts the NPK ratio**.
[48:0:10] - **Balanced fertiliser use** = nutrients in the right proportion as per soil test → **corrects all deficiencies** (not just "adds NPK").
[48:0:11] - **Efficient fertiliser use** = **more crop per kg of fertiliser**; the grain response per kg NPK fell from 13.4 kg (1970) to 3.7 kg (2005).
[48:0:12] - **Indiscriminate use of urea** is the main human disturbance of the **nitrogen cycle** (nitrate leaching, N2O emission).
[48:0:13] # Fertiliser types and use by State
[48:0:14] - Nitrogenous: **ammonium sulphate**, ammonium nitrate, **urea**; phosphatic: **ammonium phosphate (DAP/MAP)**, superphosphate.
[48:0:15] - **Largest consumer by quantity: Uttar Pradesh**; in 2009 the top two were **UP and (undivided) Andhra Pradesh**; Punjab is high per hectare.
[48:0:16] ~~ Sources: Union Budget 2026-27 at a Glance p.20; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy (10th ed.) part 04 p.177; PMF Environment 07 p.2-3, p.13-14; app notes 3:48 sub 2
[48:0:17] - **Expenditure Reforms Commission** (K.P. Geethakrishnan, 2000-01): four-phase **decontrol of the fertiliser (urea) industry**, to be complete by **1 April 2006**.

## SUB 2 | Seeds – Seed Village, SRR, GM Crops and Seed Systems
[48:2:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [48:2:0.0] Seed Replacement Rate | % of a crop's area sown with certified/quality seed (not farm-saved seed)
  [48:2:0.1] Varietal Replacement Rate | % of area under varieties released in the last 3-5 years
  [48:2:0.2] Seed Village | Farmers given foundation seed and trained in quality seed production → produce certified seed locally and make it available to others at the right time and affordable cost
[48:2:1] - **Seed Village Programme:** ensures timely supply of quality certified seed of notified varieties at farmers' places at affordable prices and quick multiplication of new varieties in the mandal/district; **6.85 lakh seed villages** under the Sub-Mission on Seeds and Planting Materials (ES 2025-26).
[48:2:2] - **SRR constraint (UPSC 2014):** a **demand-supply gap in quality seed of low-value, high-volume crops**; India **has** a National Seed Policy (2002) and private firms **do** supply vegetable/horticulture seed.
[48:2:3] - Seed accounts for **20-25%** of productivity; problems: high cost of hybrids for small farmers, shortage of quality seed, unresolved GM seed issues.
[48:2:4] - **SATHI portal** (Seed Traceability, Authentication and Holistic Inventory): **Phase 1 launched 19 April 2023 by Narendra Singh Tomar** (Union Agriculture Minister).
[48:2:5] - **Indian Seed Congress 2017** (Kolkata) theme: **"Seed of Joy"**.
[48:2:6] - ICAR **2019-21: 562** new HYVs; **3,236 HYVs since 2014**; Jan 2026: **184 varieties of 25 crops**.
[48:2:7] - **Genome-edited rice (May 2025):** **DRR Dhan 100 Kamala** (from **Samba Mahsuri**) + **Pusa DST Rice 1** (from **MTU 1010**); SDN-1 edits **carry no foreign DNA** → outside GMO rules.
[48:2:8] TABLE head: Act / body | Provision
  [48:2:8.0] Seeds Act 1966 | Quality of notified seeds
  [48:2:8.1] PPV&FR Act 2001 | Farmers may save, use, exchange and sell farm-saved seed only unbranded
  [48:2:8.2] GEAC | Approves GM crops; Rules 1989 under EPA 1986; MoEFCC; cleared GM mustard DMH-11 in 2022
[48:2:9] - **GM crops with built-in toxins (Bt):** risk of pest resistance and of **encouraging more use of agricultural chemicals**; **Bt cotton** is GM, not a green manure.

## SUB 4 | Mechanisation and Pesticides
[48:4:0] - Private investment in agriculture goes mostly to **labour-saving mechanisation** – a response to **rising wages and a tighter labour market** (migration, non-farm jobs).
[48:4:1] - Mechanisation level: India **40-45%** (below 50% vs >90% in developed countries).
[48:4:2] - **Zero Till Seed-cum-Fertiliser Drill:** developed at **G.B. Pant University of Agriculture and Technology, Pantnagar**.
[48:4:3] - **Cage wheel:** fitted to tractors/power tillers for puddling in **wetland (paddy)** fields.
[48:4:4] - **SMAM** (Sub-Mission on Agricultural Mechanisation): subsidises machines, **custom hiring centres**, farm machinery banks; 25,689 CHCs (2014-15 to 2025-26).
[48:4:5] - **Namo Drone Didi:** drones to **women SHGs**; **Rs 1,261 crore** (2023-24 to 2025-26); target **15,000** drones; **80%** central assistance up to Rs 8 lakh.
[48:4:6] # Pesticides
[48:4:7] - **Chemical pesticides:** advantage = **high efficacy**, quick action; drawbacks = residues, harm to friendly insects, resistance.
[48:4:8] - **Biological control** (natural enemies, bio-pesticides such as **neem-based** products): **low cost**, eco-friendly.
[48:4:9] - **IPM:** biological, cultural and need-based chemical control.
[48:4:10] TABLE head: Law / event | Provision
  [48:4:10.0] Insecticides Act 1968 + Rules 1971 | Central Insecticides Board and Registration Committee
  [48:4:10.1] Draft Pesticides Management Bill 2025 | Replaces the 1968 Act
  [48:4:10.2] Endosulfan | Banned by Supreme Court, 2011
  [48:4:10.3] Aldrin (banned) | Killed mugger crocodiles in Kota
[48:4:11] - India still uses at least **66** pesticides banned elsewhere.

## SUB 5 | Soil Health Card, Manures and Organic Farming
[48:5:0] - **Soil Health Card Scheme:** launched **19 February 2015, Suratgarh (Rajasthan)**; part of NMSA (Soil Health Management); card shows nutrient status and fertiliser advice.
[48:5:1] - **Interval:** the 2015 scheme said a card **once in every 3 years** (AEE 2016 key); in practice cards are now issued **free every two years** (cycles 2015-17, 2017-19).
[48:5:2] - Impact: fertiliser use cut **8-10%**; over **25 crore** cards issued.
[48:5:3] - **Glyricidia = green leaf manure**; green manure crops: **sunn hemp, dhaincha, green gram** (Bt cotton is GM, not a green manure). **Vermicompost:** earthworms turn organic waste into manure.
[48:5:4] - **Organic farming = eco-friendly:** compost, green manure, bone meal; crop rotation, companion planting (HYV cultivation, glass houses and shifting cultivation are not).
[48:5:5] TABLE head: Scheme | Year | Feature
  [48:5:5.0] NPOP | 2001 | Commerce Ministry / APEDA certification
  [48:5:5.1] PKVY | 2015 | Cluster of 50 farmers / 50 acres; certified by PGS-India or NPOP
  [48:5:5.2] National Mission on Natural Farming | Nov 2024 | Stand-alone mission
  [48:5:5.3] NMSA | – | Merged into PM-RKVY from 2022-23; agroforestry "Har Medh Par Ped"
[48:5:6] - **Zero Budget Natural Farming:** **Subhash Palekar**.
[48:5:7] ~~ Sources: PMF Environment 06 p.125 (SHC every two years, 8-10% cut); web: PIB factsheet PDF Aug 2025 static.pib.gov.in doc2025816613501 (both "once every 3 years" and "every two years"); app notes 3:48 sub 3, sub 6
[48:5:8] - **Soil Health Cards in AP:** about 22.24 lakh cards issued by mid-2016 (cycle I); 2017-18 target 21 lakh.
## SUB 6 | Andhra Pradesh – Inputs and Extension
[48:6:0] - **Tenant farmers' cards:** **Loan Eligibility Cards / Certificates of Cultivation (COC)** earlier; now **Crop Cultivator Rights Cards (CCRCs)** under the **AP Crop Cultivators Rights Act 2019** (11-month agreement; **9,13,283** CCRCs by 3 Jan 2025) – give access to bank credit, insurance, input subsidy.
[48:6:1] - **D-Krishi:** Aadhaar-enabled **seed** distribution system of AP.
[48:6:2] - **ATMA in AP:** all **26 districts**, **60:40** funding; disseminates latest technologies and fills adoption gaps through **bottom-up planning (SREP)**.
[48:6:3] - **APCNF:** run by **Rythu Sadhikara Samstha (RySS)**; **jeevamrutham, ghanajeevamrutham**; **10.20 lakh farmers** (2025-26); **Gulbenkian Prize for Humanity 2024**.
[48:6:4] - **Rythu Seva Kendras** (formerly Rythu Bharosa Kendras): village one-stop centres for inputs, soil testing, e-Panta, CCRCs, insurance; **8,489** functional after rationalisation; buildings under **MGNREGS**.
[48:6:5] - Fertiliser consumption **39.39 LMT** (2024-25); 2015-16 actual use **80.78%** of allotment; pesticide use **2,650 MT → 1,716 MT** (2014-15 → 2024-25).
[48:6:6] ~~ Sources: AP Socio-Economic Survey 2024-25 p.8, p.55-58; app notes 3:48 sub 12; general knowledge (COC cards, D-Krishi)

=== NOTES 3:49 | Green Revolution – Components, Achievements and Criticism; White, Yellow, Blue and Rainbow Revolutions
## SUB 0 | Green Revolution – Meaning, Package and Nature
[49:0:0] - **Green Revolution** = large rise in foodgrain output (mainly **wheat and rice**) through **High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds** – **increasing output through HYVs**, not "making land green" or forest canopy.
[49:0:1] - **Scientific core / focus: HYV seeds**; nature = **Seed-Fertiliser-Water technology** (a package; HYVs are only its core) – the best description of the late-1960s Green Revolution (BPSC 2018).
[49:0:2] - HYVs need **assured irrigation**, so the benefits stayed confined to **areas with irrigation facilities** (Punjab, Haryana, western UP).
[49:0:3] TABLE head: Component | Note
  [49:0:3.0] HYV seeds | Dwarf varieties; the core
  [49:0:3.1] Chemical fertilisers | Urea (N), phosphate (P), potash (K)
  [49:0:3.2] Irrigation | Controlled water supply
  [49:0:3.3] Pesticides, herbicides | New seeds not acclimatised to local pests
  [49:0:3.4] Mechanisation | Pump sets, tractors
  [49:0:3.5] Credit, storage, marketing, MSP | Supporting – credit is not one of the "most important" components (HYV, fertiliser, irrigation)
[49:0:4] - **Not** part of it: **traditional seed varieties**, **crossbreeding of indigenous cows with European breeds** (that is the White Revolution), expansion of forests, three crop seasons.
[49:0:5] - **HYV traits:** **short stature (dwarf)** resists lodging; **quicker maturity** allows **double cropping**.
[49:0:6] - **Pioneer State: Punjab** – "**Granary of India**"; first crop transformed: **wheat**.
[49:0:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 20 p.5-6; NextGen Indian Economy P1 - APPSC 03 p.32; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy part 04 p.152-153; NextGen Indian Economy P1 - APPSC 04 p.25
## SUB 1 | Second Green Revolution, Evergreen Revolution and BGREI
[49:1:0] - **Second Green Revolution:** called for by **PM Manmohan Singh** (**3 Jan 2006, 93rd Indian Science Congress, Hyderabad**): first GR technologies had "run their course".
[49:1:1] - Aims: extend **seed-water-fertiliser technology to areas left out** (eastern, rainfed, dryland), crops beyond wheat-rice (**pulses, oilseeds, horticulture, non-food crops**), **integrate cropping with animal husbandry, social forestry and fishing**; based on **biotechnology**, not more HYVs of wheat/rice or more chemical pesticides.
[49:1:2] - **May 2006 programme** for the Second Green Revolution: a **seven-point strategy**.
[49:1:3] - **Dry zone:** agrarian economy, and has large potential for the Second Green Revolution – both true, but the potential does not explain why it is agrarian.
[49:1:4] - **Evergreen Revolution:** concept of **M.S. Swaminathan** – raise productivity **in perpetuity without ecological harm**; Aug 2025 centenary conference theme "**Evergreen Revolution, The Pathway to Biohappiness**".
[49:1:5] TABLE head: BGREI | Fact
  [49:1:5.0] Full name | Bringing Green Revolution to Eastern India
  [49:1:5.1] Launched | 2010-11
  [49:1:5.2] Parent | Sub-scheme of RKVY
  [49:1:5.3] Aim | Rice-based productivity in eastern India
  [49:1:5.4] States (7) | Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha, Eastern UP, West Bengal (Punjab is not one)
[49:1:6] ~~ Sources: Ramesh Singh Indian Economy part 04 p.154 (evergreen / second green revolution); app notes 3:49 sub 6; general knowledge (seven-point strategy, BGREI)
## SUB 2 | Yellow Revolution – Oilseeds
[49:2:0] - **Yellow Revolution = oilseeds** (edible oil production): began **1986** with the **Technology Mission on Oilseeds**; linked with **Sam Pitroda**; **rapeseed-mustard** is its emblem crop.
[49:2:1] - Not pulses, millets, cereals, turmeric, poultry or sunflower alone.
[49:2:2] - Now: **National Mission on Edible Oils** – **NMEO-Oilseeds** and **NMEO-Oil Palm**.
[49:2:3] - **Soybean** = the "**miracle crop**" (golden bean) – an oilseed rich in protein and oil.

## SUB 7 | Outcomes and Criticism
[49:7:0] - **Gains:** wheat (most), then rice; Punjab, Haryana, western UP; end of PL-480 dependence; foodgrains **72 MT (1965-66) → 357.73 MT (2024-25)**.
[49:7:1] - **Inequality:** **inter-regional, inter-personal (inter-class) and inter-crop** – farmers with **more than 10 ha** benefited most.
[49:7:2] - **Crop bias:** **pulses** (and oilseeds, coarse cereals) gained little.
[49:7:3] - **No true self-sufficiency (UPSC 1994):** GR **restricted to small pockets**, **food costly relative to the earnings of the poor**, **too much emphasis on wheat and paddy** over coarse grains (statements 1, 2, 3).
[49:7:4] - **Ecological cost:** soil fertility loss, falling water table, salinity, toxic residues – so "it did not involve much social and environmental cost" is **false**, though GR did modernise agriculture.
[49:7:5] - Groundwater: Punjab and Haryana extract **35-57% more than recharge**; nutrient use skewed at **N:P:K 10.9:4.1:1** (not balanced); rice-wheat monoculture.
[49:7:6] - **Johl Committee:** shift **20% of paddy area** (diversification).
[49:7:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 20 p.6; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy part 04 p.153-154; app notes 3:49 sub 5

[50:3:5] # Concepts and causes
[50:3:6] - **Intensive cultivation:** more **labour and capital** per hectare to raise output from the same land; extensive = more land under the plough.
[50:3:7] - **Cereal yield:** kg per hectare of **harvested** land under cereals. **TFP:** output growth not explained by input growth.
[50:3:8] - **Causes of low productivity:** small holdings, monsoon dependence and inadequate irrigation (rice ~67% irrigated vs pulses ~26%), skewed NPK, low seed replacement, ~30% cold-chain losses, climate (~8% yield fall per 1°C).
[50:3:9] - **Pathways:** irrigation, quality seed (SRR/VRR, genome editing), balanced fertiliser (Soil Health Card), plant protection, mechanisation (Custom Hiring Centres).
[50:3:10] - Growth has come **more from area than yield**; now led by livestock and fisheries.
[50:3:11] - **TFP drivers:** agricultural research and new varieties, irrigation, rural roads and markets, extension, education.
[50:3:12] - **El Niño and pulses:** acreage −2 to −9%, yield −5 to −25%, output −6 to −30%. Universal cold-chain access is NOT a cause of the yield gap – its lack is.
[50:3:13] - **Area trends:** cotton area down 18 lakh ha, output **290.91 lakh bales (2025-26)** from about 350 – India became a **net importer of raw cotton**; soybean area in MP fell about **10%** (2023 to 2024); Telangana's cultivated area 1.31 crore acres (2014) → **2.2 crore acres** (FY23).
[50:3:14] > A "which one is the pathway" stem with irrigation, seed, pesticides and fertilisers as options has no single answer – all four raise productivity.
