## SUB 0 | Green Revolution – Meaning, Package and Nature
[0:0] - **Green Revolution** = large rise in foodgrain output (mainly **wheat and rice**) through **High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds** – **increasing output through HYVs**, not "making land green" or forest canopy.
[0:1] - **Scientific core / focus: HYV seeds**; nature = **Seed-Fertiliser-Water technology** (a package; HYVs are only its core) – the best description of the late-1960s Green Revolution (BPSC 2018).
[0:2] - HYVs need **assured irrigation**, so the benefits stayed confined to **areas with irrigation facilities** (Punjab, Haryana, western UP).
[0:3] TABLE head: Component | Note
  [0:3.0] HYV seeds | Dwarf varieties; the core
  [0:3.1] Chemical fertilisers | Urea (N), phosphate (P), potash (K)
  [0:3.2] Irrigation | Controlled water supply
  [0:3.3] Pesticides, herbicides | New seeds not acclimatised to local pests
  [0:3.4] Mechanisation | Pump sets, tractors
  [0:3.5] Credit, storage, marketing, MSP | Supporting – credit is not one of the "most important" components (HYV, fertiliser, irrigation)
[0:4] - **Not** part of it: **traditional seed varieties**, **crossbreeding of indigenous cows with European breeds** (that is the White Revolution), expansion of forests, three crop seasons.
[0:5] - **HYV traits:** **short stature (dwarf)** resists lodging; **quicker maturity** allows **double cropping**.
[0:6] - **Pioneer State: Punjab** – "**Granary of India**"; first crop transformed: **wheat**.

## SUB 2 | Second Green Revolution, Evergreen Revolution and BGREI
[2:0] - **Second Green Revolution:** called for by **PM Manmohan Singh** (**3 Jan 2006, 93rd Indian Science Congress, Hyderabad**): first GR technologies had "run their course".
[2:1] - Aims: extend **seed-water-fertiliser technology to areas left out** (eastern, rainfed, dryland), crops beyond wheat-rice (**pulses, oilseeds, horticulture, non-food crops**), **integrate cropping with animal husbandry, social forestry and fishing**; based on **biotechnology**, not more HYVs of wheat/rice or more chemical pesticides.
[2:2] - **May 2006 programme** for the Second Green Revolution: a **seven-point strategy**.
[2:3] - **Dry zone:** agrarian economy, and has large potential for the Second Green Revolution – both true, but the potential does not explain why it is agrarian.
[2:4] - **Evergreen Revolution:** concept of **M.S. Swaminathan** – raise productivity **in perpetuity without ecological harm**; Aug 2025 centenary conference theme "**Evergreen Revolution, The Pathway to Biohappiness**".
[2:5] TABLE head: BGREI | Fact
  [2:5.0] Full name | Bringing Green Revolution to Eastern India
  [2:5.1] Launched | 2010-11
  [2:5.2] Parent | Sub-scheme of RKVY
  [2:5.3] Aim | Rice-based productivity in eastern India
  [2:5.4] States (7) | Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha, Eastern UP, West Bengal (Punjab is not one)

## SUB 3 | Yellow Revolution – Oilseeds
[3:0] - **Yellow Revolution = oilseeds** (edible oil production): began **1986** with the **Technology Mission on Oilseeds**; linked with **Sam Pitroda**; **rapeseed-mustard** is its emblem crop.
[3:1] - Not pulses, millets, cereals, turmeric, poultry or sunflower alone.
[3:2] - Now: **National Mission on Edible Oils** – **NMEO-Oilseeds** and **NMEO-Oil Palm**.
[3:3] - **Soybean** = the "**miracle crop**" (golden bean) – an oilseed rich in protein and oil.
[3:4] ~~ Sources: Rocket Indian Geography p.53 (Yellow Revolution oilseeds); app notes 3:49 sub 8; general knowledge (soybean miracle crop)
## SUB 4 | White Revolution – Operation Flood
[4:0] - **Operation Flood (1970)** by the **NDDB** = **milk production**; national milk grid on the **Anand (Amul) three-tier cooperative** model; world's largest dairy development programme.
[4:1] - White Revolution **started in Gujarat**; **Anand** is a district of **Gujarat**.
[4:2] - **GCMMF (1946)** markets milk (**AMUL**); **NDDB set up 1965** to copy the AMUL model.
[4:3] - **Dr Verghese Kurien** – **Father of the White Revolution**, with **Tribhuvandas Patel**; also behind the **Institute of Rural Management, Anand (IRMA)**.
[4:4] TABLE head: Phase | Years | Achievement
  [4:4.0] I | 1970-80 | Financed by the World Food Programme; 18 milk sheds linked to 4 metros
  [4:4.1] II | 1981-85 | Milk sheds 18 → 136
  [4:4.2] III | 1985-96 | 30,000 new dairy cooperatives
[4:5] - **White Revolution 2.0** (Sept 2024, **Ministry of Cooperation**): **75,000** new dairy cooperatives. Milk output **239.3 MT (2023-24)**; India = world's largest producer.

## SUB 6 | Outcomes and Criticism
[6:0] - **Gains:** wheat (most), then rice; Punjab, Haryana, western UP; end of PL-480 dependence; foodgrains **72 MT (1965-66) → 357.73 MT (2024-25)**.
[6:1] - **Inequality:** **inter-regional, inter-personal (inter-class) and inter-crop** – farmers with **more than 10 ha** benefited most.
[6:2] - **Crop bias:** **pulses** (and oilseeds, coarse cereals) gained little.
[6:3] - **No true self-sufficiency (UPSC 1994):** GR **restricted to small pockets**, **food costly relative to the earnings of the poor**, **too much emphasis on wheat and paddy** over coarse grains (statements 1, 2, 3).
[6:4] - **Ecological cost:** soil fertility loss, falling water table, salinity, toxic residues – so "it did not involve much social and environmental cost" is **false**, though GR did modernise agriculture.
[6:5] - Groundwater: Punjab and Haryana extract **35-57% more than recharge**; nutrient use skewed at **N:P:K 10.9:4.1:1** (not balanced); rice-wheat monoculture.
[6:6] - **Johl Committee:** shift **20% of paddy area** (diversification).
[6:7] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 20 p.6; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy part 04 p.153-154; app notes 3:49 sub 5
