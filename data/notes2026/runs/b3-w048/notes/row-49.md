=== Green Revolution – Meaning, Package and Nature
- **Green Revolution** = large rise in foodgrain output (mainly **wheat and rice**) through **High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds** – **increasing output through HYVs**, not "making land green" or forest canopy.
- **Scientific core / focus: HYV seeds**; nature = **Seed-Fertiliser-Water technology** (a package; HYVs are only its core) – the best description of the late-1960s Green Revolution (BPSC 2018).
- HYVs need **assured irrigation**, so the benefits stayed confined to **areas with irrigation facilities** (Punjab, Haryana, western UP).
| Component | Note |
|---|---|
| **HYV seeds** | Dwarf varieties; the core |
| **Chemical fertilisers** | Urea (N), phosphate (P), potash (K) |
| **Irrigation** | Controlled water supply |
| Pesticides, herbicides | New seeds not acclimatised to local pests |
| Mechanisation | Pump sets, tractors |
| Credit, storage, marketing, MSP | Supporting – **credit is not one of the "most important" components** (HYV, fertiliser, irrigation) |
- **Not** part of it: **traditional seed varieties**, **crossbreeding of indigenous cows with European breeds** (that is the White Revolution), expansion of forests, three crop seasons.
- **HYV traits:** **short stature (dwarf)** resists lodging; **quicker maturity** allows **double cropping**.
- **Pioneer State: Punjab** – "**Granary of India**"; first crop transformed: **wheat**.

=== Second Green Revolution, Evergreen Revolution and BGREI
- **Second Green Revolution:** called for by **PM Manmohan Singh** (**3 Jan 2006, 93rd Indian Science Congress, Hyderabad**): first GR technologies had "run their course".
- Aims: extend **seed-water-fertiliser technology to areas left out** (eastern, rainfed, dryland), crops beyond wheat-rice (**pulses, oilseeds, horticulture, non-food crops**), **integrate cropping with animal husbandry, social forestry and fishing**; based on **biotechnology**, not more HYVs of wheat/rice or more chemical pesticides.
- **May 2006 programme** for the Second Green Revolution: a **seven-point strategy**.
- **Dry zone:** agrarian economy, and has large potential for the Second Green Revolution – both true, but the potential does not explain why it is agrarian.
- **Evergreen Revolution:** concept of **M.S. Swaminathan** – raise productivity **in perpetuity without ecological harm**; Aug 2025 centenary conference theme "**Evergreen Revolution, The Pathway to Biohappiness**".
| BGREI | Fact |
|---|---|
| Full name | Bringing Green Revolution to Eastern India |
| Launched | **2010-11** |
| Parent | **Sub-scheme of RKVY** |
| Aim | Rice-based productivity in **eastern India** |
| States (7) | Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha, Eastern UP, West Bengal (Punjab is not one) |

=== Yellow Revolution – Oilseeds
- **Yellow Revolution = oilseeds** (edible oil production): began **1986** with the **Technology Mission on Oilseeds**; linked with **Sam Pitroda**; **rapeseed-mustard** is its emblem crop.
- Not pulses, millets, cereals, turmeric, poultry or sunflower alone.
- Now: **National Mission on Edible Oils** – **NMEO-Oilseeds** and **NMEO-Oil Palm**.
- **Soybean** = the "**miracle crop**" (golden bean) – an oilseed rich in protein and oil.

=== White Revolution – Operation Flood
- **Operation Flood (1970)** by the **NDDB** = **milk production**; national milk grid on the **Anand (Amul) three-tier cooperative** model; world's largest dairy development programme.
- White Revolution **started in Gujarat**; **Anand** is a district of **Gujarat**.
- **GCMMF (1946)** markets milk (**AMUL**); **NDDB set up 1965** to copy the AMUL model.
- **Dr Verghese Kurien** – **Father of the White Revolution**, with **Tribhuvandas Patel**; also behind the **Institute of Rural Management, Anand (IRMA)**.
| Phase | Years | Achievement |
|---|---|---|
| I | 1970-80 | Financed by the World Food Programme; 18 milk sheds linked to 4 metros |
| II | 1981-85 | Milk sheds 18 → 136 |
| III | 1985-96 | 30,000 new dairy cooperatives |
- **White Revolution 2.0** (Sept 2024, **Ministry of Cooperation**): **75,000** new dairy cooperatives. Milk output **239.3 MT (2023-24)**; India = world's largest producer.

=== Background and Timeline
- 1950s-60s food shortages → imports from the USA under **PL-480**; droughts **1965-66 and 1966-67** (foodgrains 72.3 and 74.2 MT) forced a new strategy.
| Programme | Year | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| **IADP** (Intensive Agricultural District Programme) | **1960-61** | Pilot in **7 districts of 7 States**; AP's district = **West Godavari** |
| **IAAP** (Intensive Agricultural Area Programme) | **1964-65 (1965)** | IADP extended to **114 districts**; **117 blocks in AP** |
| HYV programme | Rabi **1966-67** | Full-fledged from **1966**, during the **Plan Holiday (1966-69)** |
- **G.B. Pant University of Agriculture and Technology, Pantnagar** – **India's first agricultural university (1960)**; the Indian Green Revolution **started from Pantnagar**.
- 1966: **18,000 t** Mexican wheat seed imported.
| Phase | Years |
|---|---|
| Pre-Green Revolution | 1951-68 |
| Early Green Revolution | 1968-81 |
| Later Green Revolution | 1981-92 (spread to new areas) |
- Order (APPSC 2023): **IADP (1960) → end of first Green Revolution period → MSSRF (1988)**.

=== Outcomes and Criticism
- **Gains:** wheat (most), then rice; Punjab, Haryana, western UP; end of PL-480 dependence; foodgrains **72 MT (1965-66) → 357.73 MT (2024-25)**.
- **Inequality:** **inter-regional, inter-personal (inter-class) and inter-crop** – farmers with **more than 10 ha** benefited most.
- **Crop bias:** **pulses** (and oilseeds, coarse cereals) gained little.
- **No true self-sufficiency (UPSC 1994):** GR **restricted to small pockets**, **food costly relative to the earnings of the poor**, **too much emphasis on wheat and paddy** over coarse grains (statements 1, 2, 3).
- **Ecological cost:** soil fertility loss, falling water table, salinity, toxic residues – so "it did not involve much social and environmental cost" is **false**, though GR did modernise agriculture.
- Groundwater: Punjab and Haryana extract **35-57% more than recharge**; nutrient use skewed at **N:P:K 10.9:4.1:1** (not balanced); rice-wheat monoculture.
- **Johl Committee:** shift **20% of paddy area** (diversification).
