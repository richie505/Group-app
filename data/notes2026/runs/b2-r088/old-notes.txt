=== NOTES 2:88 | E-governance – Models, Types of Interaction, NeGP, Digital India and Governance Outcomes
## SUB 0 | E-governance – Meaning and Significance
[88:0:0] - E-governance = use of ICT to deliver government services, information and transactions; the aim is **faceless, paperless, cashless** governance.
[88:0:1] - Significance (APPSC 2025 key): **citizen engagement** and **better service delivery**.
[88:0:2] - Other gains: **fewer face-to-face contacts** (less scope for bribes) and **audit trails**.
[88:0:3] E-governance does NOT remove audit trails; digital systems **strengthen** them.
[88:0:4] # Why It Matters for Governance Outcomes
[88:0:5] - **Transparency**: rules, fees, timelines and file status are visible online, so discretion falls.
[88:0:6] - **Accountability**: every transaction leaves a digital record that can be audited later.
[88:0:7] - **Speed and convenience**: services reach citizens through a single window, a mobile app or a village CSC instead of many office visits.
[88:0:8] - **Participation**: platforms such as MyGov let citizens give suggestions on policy.
[88:0:9] - **Cost**: fewer visits and less paper lower costs for both government and citizen.
## SUB 1 | Models of E-governance – Arie Halachmi
[88:1:0] # Five Models
[88:1:1] TABLE head: Model (Arie Halachmi) | Meaning
  [88:1:1.0] Broadcasting / wider dissemination | Government information put in the public domain
  [88:1:1.1] Critical flow | Critical information channelled to a targeted audience (e.g. corruption data → ombudsman)
  [88:1:1.2] Comparative analysis | Learning from best practices elsewhere
  [88:1:1.3] E-advocacy / mobilisation and lobbying | Online civil-society networks influence decisions
  [88:1:1.4] Interactive service | Two-way G2C services (filing, payments, feedback)
[88:1:2] - Theorist: **Arie Halachmi** – exactly **five** models.
[88:1:3] Corruption data sent to an ombudsman = **critical flow**, not broadcasting.
## SUB 2 | Types of Interaction – G2C, G2B, G2G, G2E
[88:2:0] TABLE head: Type | Meaning | Indian examples
  [88:2:0.0] G2C | Government to Citizen | Online birth, income, caste certificates; UMANG, MeeSeva
  [88:2:0.1] G2B | Government to Business | MCA portal, GSTN, e-Procurement
  [88:2:0.2] G2G | Between departments/governments | NIC solutions, SWAN, e-Office
  [88:2:0.3] G2E | Government to its Employees | Online HR and administrative tools
[88:2:1] - **How to identify the type**: ask who receives the service. A citizen asking for a certificate = G2C; a company filing returns or bidding for tenders = G2B; one department sending files to another = G2G; an employee using an HR tool = G2E.
[88:2:2] - In AP, **MeeSeva** and **eSeva** are G2C; **e-Procurement** is G2B; **APSWAN** is the G2G backbone.
## SUB 3 | National e-GOVERNANCE Plan (NeGP) and e-KRANTI
[88:3:0] # NeGP, 2006
[88:3:1] - Approved by the Government on **18 May 2006** with **27 Mission Mode Projects (MMPs)**.
[88:3:2] - In **2011**, **4** MMPs added – **Health, Education, PDS, Posts** – taking the total to **31**.
[88:3:3] - Core infrastructure: **SWAN** (State Wide Area Network), **State Data Centres**, **CSCs**.
[88:3:4] - MMPs are of three kinds: **Central** (run by Union ministries), **State** (run by state governments) and **Integrated** (cutting across several departments and levels).
[88:3:5] # e-Kranti (NeGP 2.0)
[88:3:6] - Approved by the Union Cabinet on **25 March 2015**; it is a **pillar of Digital India**.
[88:3:7] - Vision: **"Transforming e-Governance for Transforming Governance"**.
[88:3:8] - Expanded MMPs from **31 to 44**: **15 Central, 17 State, 12 Integrated**.
[88:3:9] TABLE head: Feature | NeGP | e-Kranti
  [88:3:9.0] Approved | 18 May 2006 | 25 March 2015
  [88:3:9.1] MMPs | 27 (31 from 2011) | 44
  [88:3:9.2] Status | NeGP 1.0 | NeGP 2.0, part of Digital India
## SUB 4 | Digital India Programme
[88:4:0] # Basics
[88:4:1] - Launched **1 July 2015**; implementing ministry: **Ministry of Electronics and IT (MeitY)**.
[88:4:2] - Aims: digital infrastructure as a core utility to every citizen, governance and services on demand, and **digital empowerment** of citizens.
[88:4:3] # Nine Pillars (full list)
[88:4:4] - 1. **Broadband Highways**
[88:4:5] - 2. **Universal Access to Mobile Connectivity**
[88:4:6] - 3. **Public Internet Access Programme**
[88:4:7] - 4. **e-Governance**: reforming government through technology
[88:4:8] - 5. **e-Kranti**: electronic delivery of services
[88:4:9] - 6. **Information for All**
[88:4:10] - 7. **Electronics Manufacturing**
[88:4:11] - 8. **IT for Jobs**
[88:4:12] - 9. **Early Harvest Programmes**
[88:4:13] "Which is NOT a pillar": items such as a satellite launch or a defence programme are not among the nine; e-Kranti IS a pillar.
[88:4:14] - **2026**: CM Naidu declared 2026 the **Technology-Driven Decision Making Year** in AP.

## SUB 6 | Information Technology Act, 2000 – E-governance Provisions
[88:6:0] TABLE head: Section | Provision
  [88:6:0.0] Section 4 | Legal recognition of electronic records (equal to paper documents)
  [88:6:0.1] Section 5 | Legal recognition of electronic (digital) signatures – same as handwritten signatures
  [88:6:0.2] Section 6 | Use of electronic records and signatures in Government – online filing, electronic licences, digital receipt/payment
  [88:6:0.3] Section 7 | Retention of electronic records – a legal duty to keep records is met by electronic form
  [88:6:0.4] Section 9 | Sections 6–8 give no right to insist that a document be accepted in electronic form

## SUB 9 | Digital Divide and Governance Outcomes
[88:9:0] TABLE head: Initiative | Purpose | Status
  [88:9:0.0] BharatNet | Optical fibre broadband to Gram Panchayats | 2.15 lakh GPs connected (Mar 2026); Amended BharatNet (Aug 2023) targets 2.64 lakh GPs in ring topology
  [88:9:0.1] PM-WANI | Public Wi-Fi hotspots (Department of Telecom) | Launched December 2020; 4.09 lakh hotspots (Mar 2026)
  [88:9:0.2] PMGDISHA | Rural digital literacy | 6.39 crore individuals trained (31 Mar 2024)
[88:9:1] - Outcomes: transparency, speed, lower corruption; risks: **digital divide**, cyber security, data privacy.
[88:9:2] - **Digital divide** = the gap between people who have internet access and digital skills and those who do not (rural poor, elderly, women, remote areas). BharatNet closes the **infrastructure** gap, PM-WANI the **access** gap and PMGDISHA the **skills** gap.
[88:9:3] - The **Visakhapatnam Declaration (2025)** also targets this gap by extending NeSDA to low-connectivity regions such as Ladakh and the North-East.

=== NOTES 2:89 | Citizen-centric Administration – Citizens' Charter, Sevottam, Grievance Redress (cpgrams), Social Audit
## SUB 0 | Origins and Firsts
[89:0:0] TABLE head: FIRST / origin | Fact
  [89:0:0.0] Ombudsman | Sweden, 1809
  [89:0:0.1] Citizens' Charter | UK, 1991 (John Major government)
  [89:0:0.2] Citizens' Charter in India | 1997
  [89:0:0.3] Right to Services idea | Began in Great Britain (UK), out of the Citizens' Charter idea
  [89:0:0.4] First Right to Services law | Madhya Pradesh Lok Sewa Guarantee Act, 2010; Bihar followed in 2011
  [89:0:0.5] First social audit law | Meghalaya, 2017 – social audit of government programmes made mandatory
  [89:0:0.6] First Lokayukta | Maharashtra, 1971
  [89:0:0.7] Terms "Lokpal" and "Lokayukta" | Coined by L.M. Singhvi (Lok Sabha, 1963) – not Morarji Desai
  [89:0:0.8] Lokpal/Lokayukta recommended | First ARC (1966-70), headed by Morarji Desai (interim report 1966)
  [89:0:0.9] State-wide MGNREGA social audits | AP pioneered
[89:0:1] First RTS law = MP 2010, not Bihar (2011); first social audit law = Meghalaya 2017.
[89:0:2] - **First ARC on Lokpal and Lokayukta:** it recommended a Lokpal at the Union (Centre) level and Lokayuktas in the States.
## SUB 1 | Citizens' Charter
[89:1:0] # Purpose and Principles
[89:1:1] - A **written commitment** on service standards; main purpose = **time-bound service delivery and grievance redress** (APPSC 2025 key).
[89:1:2] - **Six principles**: **quality, choice, standards, value, accountability, transparency**.
[89:1:3] - **Participation is NOT** one of the six principles.
[89:1:4] # Basic Elements
[89:1:5] - Clear **service standards**; **information** to citizens; **grievance redress** mechanism; contact details of officers.
[89:1:6] - **What it is not**: a legally enforceable right – charters in India are **non-justiciable**.
[89:1:7] # Adoption in India
[89:1:8] - Adopted **1997**; **DARPG** coordinates formulation; nodal officers in every ministry; website **goicharters.nic.in**.
[89:1:9] - **Citizens Charter Bill, 2011** (time-bound delivery + grievance redress) never became law; **lapsed** with dissolution of Lok Sabha in **2014**.
[89:1:10] - **Second ARC** recommended **statutory status** for charters.
[89:1:11] UK 1991 → India 1997 → Bill 2011 → lapsed 2014
## SUB 2 | Sevottam Model
[89:2:0] - **Sevottam = Seva + Uttam** = excellence in service.
[89:2:1] - Suggested in the **Second ARC's 12th Report**; promoted by **DARPG**; implemented from **2009**.
[89:2:2] TABLE head: Module | Focus
  [89:2:2.0] 1. Citizens' Charter | Publish service standards
  [89:2:2.1] 2. Public Grievance Redress | Receive, track and resolve complaints
  [89:2:2.2] 3. Service Delivery Capability | Build organisational capacity
[89:2:3] - Certification: Sevottam-compliant certification follows **IS 15700:2005**.
## SUB 3 | Second Arc 12TH Report (2009)
[89:3:0] - Title: **"Citizen Centric Administration – The Heart of Governance"**; Second ARC set up **2005**, chairman **Veerappa Moily**.
[89:3:1] - Key recommendations: **Sevottam**; charters with **statutory backing**; strong grievance redress; **social audits** institutionalised in all major welfare schemes (beyond MGNREGA); e-governance; **right to public services** laws.
## SUB 4 | Right to Services Acts
[89:4:0] - Notified services within a **fixed time**; **appeals** against delay; **penalties** on officials.
[89:4:1] - They **do not abolish** other grievance mechanisms.
[89:4:2] - Order of enactment (UKPSC 2025 key): **Punjab (earliest) → Assam → Gujarat → Maharashtra**.
[89:4:3] - **AP**: **AP Public Services Delivery Guarantee Act, 2017** (Act 6 of 2018), in force from **18 Oct 2017**; time-bound delivery of notified services with appeal and penalty.
## SUB 5 | Cpgrams
[89:5:0] - **Centralised Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System**; launched **2007** by **DARPG**.
[89:5:1] - DARPG is under the **Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions** (not Home Affairs).
[89:5:2] - Access **24x7** via web, app and **UMANG**; grievances also through **5.1 lakh CSCs**.
[89:5:3] - **Disposal time limit**: DARPG circular of **23 Aug 2024** cut the maximum from **30 days to 21 days**.
[89:5:4] - **GRAI** (Grievance Redressal Assessment & Index): **11 indicators, 4 dimensions** – **efficiency, feedback, domain, organisational commitment** (**transparency is NOT** one).
[89:5:5] - Pensioners: separate platform **CPENGRAMS**.
[89:5:6] - Pendency: over **71,000** grievances pending on CPGRAMS (Sep 2025).
## SUB 6 | Ap Grievance Platforms
[89:6:0] TABLE head: Platform | Period / government | Purpose
  [89:6:0.0] Prajavani | Earlier AP initiative | Web-based online receipt, reply and tracking of grievances at District Collectorates (APPSC 2018 key)

[89:6:1] APPSC 2018 keyed **Prajavani**; APPSC 2021 keyed **Spandana**.
## SUB 7 | Social Audit
[89:7:0] - **MGNREGA 2005, Section 17**: **Gram Sabha** conducts social audit of all works.
[89:7:1] - Social audit = process to raise **accountability** in MGNREGA; communities check funds and works (APPSC 2018).
[89:7:2] - **AP SSAAT** (Society for Social Audit, Accountability and Transparency): set up **2009** as an independent society.
## SUB 8 | Lokpal, Lokayukta and Ombudsman – Compared
[89:8:0] TABLE head: Mechanism | Deals with | Nature
  [89:8:0.0] CPGRAMS / state portals | Service-delivery grievances | Executive, time-bound disposal

  [89:8:0.3] Social audit | Scheme works and funds | Community/Gram Sabha

=== NOTES 2:90 | Right to Information Act 2005 – Structure, Exemptions, Amendments and Issues
## SUB 0 | Background and Chronology
[90:0:0] TABLE head: Order | Year | Landmark
  [90:0:0.0] 1 | 1766 | Sweden – Freedom of the Press Act: world's first RTI law
  [90:0:0.1] 2 | 1948 | UN – UDHR Article 19: right to seek and receive information (a human right)
  [90:0:0.2] – | 1990 | MKSS (Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan), Rajasthan – Aruna Roy, Nikhil Dey, Shankar Singh (not Medha Patkar); jan sunwais demanding muster rolls and bills started the RTI movement
  [90:0:0.3] – | 1997 | Tamil Nadu: first Indian state RTI law (APPSC 2023); Goa the same year
  [90:0:0.4] 3 | 2002 | Freedom of Information Act 2002: never notified; repealed by the RTI Act 2005 (APPSC 2023)
  [90:0:0.5] 4 | 2005 | Right to Information Act, 2005
[90:0:1] Order (APPSC 2025): Sweden → UN → FOI Act → RTI Act. 1766 = world first; 1997 = Tamil Nadu (Indian state first).
## SUB 1 | Act Milestones, Preamble and Nature
[90:1:0] # Commencement
[90:1:1] TABLE head: Step | Date
  [90:1:1.0] Lok Sabha passed | 11 May 2005
  [90:1:1.1] Rajya Sabha passed | 12 May 2005
  [90:1:1.2] Presidential assent | 15 June 2005 (A.P.J. Abdul Kalam)
  [90:1:1.3] Fully in force | 12 October 2005 (after 120 days)
[90:1:2] - Some sections (**4(1), 5, 12, 13, 15, 16, 24**) came into force **at once**.
[90:1:3] - **CIC** constituted **12 October 2005**; first CIC **Wajahat Habibullah**; covers all Central public authorities.
[90:1:4] # Preamble and Objectives
[90:1:5] - Preamble terms: **transparency of information, revelation of information, informed citizenry, paramountcy of the democratic ideal** (all four).
[90:1:6] - Aim: informed citizenry and transparency to **contain corruption** and hold governments **accountable**, harmonised with confidentiality needs.
[90:1:7] - Objectives: transparency, **answerability**, preventing corruption, participation; **NOT** "empowering women and weaker sections".
[90:1:8] # Nature of the Right
[90:1:9] - RTI is a **statutory (legal) right**; it mandates a **timely response** to citizens' requests.
[90:1:10] - The **right to know** is read into **Article 19(1)(a)** (also Article 21) – **State of U.P. v. Raj Narain**, **S.P. Gupta v. Union of India**.
