## SUB 2 | Personal Laws, Succession and Adoption
[2:0] TABLE head: Law | Provision
  [2:0.0] Special Marriage Act 1954 | Civil, secular marriage irrespective of religion, after public notice
  [2:0.1] Hindu Marriage Act 1955 | Monogamy, divorce grounds, restitution of conjugal rights
  [2:0.2] Hindu Succession Act 1956 | Equal inheritance; ended female heir's limited estate; covers patrilineal (Mitakshara, Dayabhaga) and matrilineal (Marumakkattayam, Aliyasantana) systems (s.7, s.17); s.2(2) excludes notified STs
  [2:0.3] HSA (Amendment) 2005 | Daughters = equal coparceners
  [2:0.4] AP HSA Amendment | 1985 under NTR: unmarried daughters coparceners, before 2005
  [2:0.5] Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act 1956 | Male adopting a girl must be at least 21 years older (s.11)
  [2:0.6] Muslim Women (Divorce) Act 1986 | After Shah Bano
  [2:0.7] Muslim Women (Marriage) Act 2019 | Instant talaq-e-biddat void and illegal; up to 3 years
[2:1] - **HSA disqualification:** **conversion** (convert's descendants, s.26), murder (s.25); **no** disqualification for disease, defect or deformity (s.28).
[2:2] - **⚠ Trap:** the 1929 Hindu Law of Inheritance (Amendment) Act only reordered heirs under Mitakshara – the law that brought both matrilineal and patrilineal Hindus under one code is the **HSA 1956**.
[2:3] - **Art. 44 (UCC)** rests on the idea that **religion and personal law are different** in a civilised society (Sarla Mudgal 1995).
[2:4] - Maintenance: **Sec. 125 CrPC → Sec. 144 BNSS**. **SC (8 Oct 2025):** upheld HSA exclusion of STs.
