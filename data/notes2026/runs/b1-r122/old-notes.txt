## SUB 1 | Castes and Social Groups
[122:1:0] - **Four varnas** and about **18 castes**.
[122:1:1] TABLE head: Group | Occupation / note
  [122:1:1.0] Vaidikas / Niyogis | Brahmin priests / Brahmin officials
  [122:1:1.1] Vipravinodins | Brahmin jugglers/magicians who performed only before Brahmins, in their homes (APPSC 2017, 2019)
  [122:1:1.2] Settis, Komatis, Balijas (Balanjalu, Veerabalijas) | Traders, like the Ayyavalu; Arya Vaishyas and Kalinga Vaishyas
  [122:1:1.3] Sudras | Farmers – backbone of the kingdom; many became amaranayakas
  [122:1:1.4] Vira Panchalas (Panchanam) | Artisans – gold, iron, brass, wood, stone; 74 sub-sects in a Devaraya I inscription
  [122:1:1.5] Kaikkolars, Padmasalis, Jandra, Kuruba | Weavers
[122:1:2] Vaidikas = priests; Niyogis = officials.
## SUB 2 | Right-hand and Left-hand Divisions

  [122:2:0.1] Idangai (left-hand) | Mainly artisans (incl. Panchala smiths) and some traders
[122:2:1] - Began in **Chola** times; continued under Vijayanagara.
## SUB 3 | Women and Social Customs
[122:3:0] - **No strict purdah**; women served as bodyguards, wrestlers, accountants, dancers.
[122:3:1] - **Sati, polygamy, dowry** existed; **child marriages** were common.
[122:3:2] - **Marriage:** **Brahma vivaha** (father gives his daughter to a suitable groom) was the most popular form.
[122:3:3] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [122:3:3.0] Kanyashulkam | Bride price
  [122:3:3.1] Varashulkam | Dowry
  [122:3:3.2] Benabaga | Slavery
  [122:3:3.3] Devadasis | Temple dancers trained in music and dance; often land-owning and respected; temple dance = forerunner of Bharatanatyam
[122:3:4] - Prostitution state-recognised and **taxed** (12,000 panams a year per woman).
[122:3:5] - Learned women: **Gangadevi** and **Tirumalamba**.

## SUB 7 | Agriculture, Irrigation and Temple Economy
[122:7:0] # Porumamilla Inscription
[122:7:1] - **12 rules (Dwadasa Sutras)** for building a tank (APPSC G2 2012, 2019); issued by **Bhaskara Bhavadura** (son of Bukka I, prince of Udayagiri); records the **Anantasagaram** tank (**1369**); in **Kadapa**.
[122:7:2] Distractors: Tummalagudem, Korlagallu, Bitragunta, Palampet, Pakhala.
[122:7:3] # Tanks and Dams

[122:7:5] - **Saptasantanas:** tank-building counted among the **seven meritorious acts**; kings promoted tanks, dams and wells.
[122:7:6] # Temple Economy
[122:7:7] - **Main revenue**: land tax.
[122:7:8] - **Burhan-i-Maasir:** Nizam Shahi chronicle put **Sadasiva Raya**'s yearly income at **12 crore huns**

=== NOTES 1:124 | Folk Culture of Andhra - Burrakatha, Harikatha, Veedhi Natakam, Oggu Katha, Tolu Bommalata
## SUB 0 | Folk Forms Through the Ages
[124:0:0] - Folk performance = living proof of a **distinct Telugu culture**; it carried that consciousness to the villages during the **Andhra Movement**.
[124:0:1] # Vijayanagara Period
[124:0:2] TABLE head: Form | Audience
  [124:0:2.0] Yakshaganam | Royal courts and elites
  [124:0:2.1] Tolu Bommalata | Most popular entertainment of rural Andhra (APPSC G2 2019)
  [124:0:2.2] Vipra-Vinodins | Brahmin jugglers/actors for Brahmin households
[124:0:3] # Qutb Shahi Period
[124:0:4] - Yakshaganam became **folk theatre**; Bhagavata stories and **Parijatam** performed across the countryside; **Kuchipudi** flourished under Golconda.
## SUB 1 | Yakshaganam and Veedhi Natakam
[124:1:0] - **Koravanji** = ancient **precursor** of the Telugu Yakshagana.
[124:1:1] - **Sugreeva Vijayam** by **Kandukuri Rudra Kavi** = **first published Telugu Yakshaganam**.
[124:1:2] - Andhra Yakshaganam: performed in **Telugu**, rich costumes, epic and Puranic stories.
[124:1:3] - **Jakkulu:** Yakshaganam was performed by the **Jakkula** community; **Jakkula Purandhri** was its key female role.
[124:1:4] - **Veedhi Natakam / Veedhi Bhagavatam**: street dance-drama → forerunner of Kuchipudi.
## SUB 2 | Harikatha
[124:2:0] - Also **Harikathalu**; a **single artist** sings, acts and dances (about **3 hours**) with cymbals (**Chidatalu**).
[124:2:1] - Deeply rooted in **Vizianagaram**.
[124:2:2] # Founder – Adibhatla Narayana Dasu (1864-1945)
[124:2:3] - **Harikatha Pitamaha** / Father of Harikatha (APPSC G2 2012); born at **Ajjada**, **Vizianagaram** district; multilingual scholar.
[124:2:4] TABLE head: Work | Type
  [124:2:4.0] Na Yeruka | Autobiography
  [124:2:4.1] Jagajyothi, Navarasa Tarangini, Yadhartha Ramayanam | Kavyas
  [124:2:4.2] Sri Krishna Kathamrutam | Sanskrit Harikatha
  [124:2:4.3] Rama, Mukunda, Shiva, Mrutyunjaya Satakams | Satakams
## SUB 3 | Burrakatha
[124:3:0] # Form and Names
[124:3:1] - **Earlier name: Jangam Katha** (APPSC GS 2018); also **Jamukula Kathalu**; in Rayalaseema called **Thandana Katha**.
[124:3:2] - **Birthplace: Guntur**; folk form of **Andhra Pradesh** (APPSC GS 2019) and Telangana.
[124:3:3] # Troupe Roles and Instruments
[124:3:4] - **Group of three**: **main narrator** in the middle (plays the **burra**) + two **Vantalu** who respond and comment.
[124:3:5] - Instruments: **Gummata, Burra, Tambura, Ukki**; chorus "**Thandana Taana**".
[124:3:6] # Exponents and Political Use
[124:3:7] - **Sheikh Nazar** = **Burrakatha Pitamaha**; born **5 Feb 1920**, **Pannekallu, Guntur**; works **Palnati Yuddham**, **Bengal Karuvu**.
[124:3:8] - Popularisers: **Nazar, Nittala Brothers, Bhadrakali**.
[124:3:9] - Narrators carried **news, reform and nationalist messages** to unlettered villages.
[124:3:10] - **Praja Natya Mandali** used folk plays for **socialist ideals**; hit play **Mabhoomi** by **Sunkara Satyanarayana and Vasireddy Bhaskara Rao**.
[124:3:11] - Sunkara Satyanarayana's Burrakathas: **Kashtajeevi, Raithu Vijayam, Stalin, Alluri Sitaramaraju, Veeresalingam**.
## SUB 4 | Tolu Bommalata
[124:4:0] - **Material**: thin, **translucent leather** figures **painted on both sides**.
[124:4:1] - Moved behind a **white screen** lit by an **oil lamp**; puppeteer sings verses and dialogue from behind.
[124:4:2] - **Unique feature**: **moving the heads** of the puppets.
[124:4:3] - Themes: **Ramayana, Mahabharata, Puranas**, with classical music.
[124:4:4] - Region and family: **Nimmalakunta** (Anantapur) – GI tag for AP leather puppetry (**2008**).
[124:4:5] TABLE head: Work mentioning it | Author
  [124:4:5.0] Panditaradhya Charitra | Palakuriki Somanatha
  [124:4:5.1] Palnati Veeracharitra | Srinatha
  [124:4:5.2] Bhaskara Satakam | Marada Venkayya
[124:4:6] # Surabhi Theatre
[124:4:7] - **Vanarasa Govinda Rao**, of a **leather-puppet family** (not Bhagavata Mela), replaced puppets with **live actors** in **Keechaka Vadham**.
[124:4:8] - Staged at a wedding in **Sorugu** village near **Jammalamadugu** (Kadapa) → Sorugu came to be called **Surabhi**; family theatre about **140 years** old.
## SUB 5 | Kathas and Community Forms
[124:5:0] TABLE head: Form | Performing community | Instrument / note
  [124:5:0.0] Oggu Katha | Golla / Kuruma (shepherds) | Small oggu drum; ballad of Telangana; rural life and struggle
  [124:5:0.1] Asadi Katha | Asadi sub-caste of Madigas | Story of Ellamma; Javiki, Chedika
  [124:5:0.2] Bhagavatulu (Krishna Leelas) | Mostly Yadavas; Rayalaseema | Large bells (Jeganta), ankle bells, cymbals; dance round a pillar with Sita-Rama-Lakshmana idols
  [124:5:0.3] Pichukuntla Katha | Pichukuntla troupe of 3 | Sings Srinatha's Palnati Veeracharitra over 15 nights; turban, sword, shield
  [124:5:0.4] Chindu Bhagavatam | Chindu (Madiga sub-group), Telangana | Dance-drama
  [124:5:0.5] Veeranatyam | Veeramushti | "Dance of Veerabhadra" in Shiva temples
[124:5:1] Oggu = shepherds; Asadi = Madigas; Bhagavatulu = Yadavas.
## SUB 6 | Kuchipudi and the Bhagavata Mela
[124:6:0] - 1. **Bhagavata Mela** dance-drama of hereditary **Brahmin** families.
[124:6:1] - 2. **Siddhendra Yogi** founded Kuchipudi.
[124:6:2] - 3. **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (last Qutb Shahi) **gifted Kuchipudi village** to the performers.
[124:6:3] - 4. Named after **Kuchipudi village, Krishna district** – a village-theatre origin for a classical form.
