## SUB 0 | Malnutrition – Terms, Indicators and Burden
[59:0:0] TABLE head: Indicator | Measure | Type
  [59:0:0.0] Stunting | Low height-for-age | Chronic
  [59:0:0.1] Wasting | Low weight-for-height | Acute
  [59:0:0.2] Underweight | Low weight-for-age | Composite
[59:0:1] - **Undernutrition** = **protein-energy malnutrition + micronutrient deficiency + stunting + wasting** (i, ii, iii, v); overweight and **obesity** are **overnutrition**. Both together in one population = **double burden of malnutrition**.
[59:0:2] - Malnutrition results from **all** of: inadequate diet, disease/infection, poverty and food insecurity. Biggest factor: **poor dietary habits and food insecurity**; **open defecation and unsafe water** add through **disease-linked loss of nutrients** – it is multi-causal, not diet alone.
[59:0:3] - **Both rich and poor are malnourished** – the rich eat a wrong diet, the poor a poor diet (A and R true, R explains A).
[59:0:4] - **FAO estimate (c. 2012): about 245 million undernourished Indians** (SOFI 2022 revised series: 247.8 million in 2004-06 → **224.3 million in 2019-21**; SOFI 2025: 12.0% of the population).
[59:0:5] TABLE head: Indicator (under 5) | NFHS-5 | NFHS-6 (2026)
  [59:0:5.0] Stunting | 35.5% | 29.3%
  [59:0:5.1] Severe wasting | 7.7% | 5.2%
  [59:0:5.2] Underweight | 32.1% | 31.8% (barely moved)
  [59:0:5.3] Breastfed within 1 hour | 41.8% | 50.1%
  [59:0:5.4] Complementary feeding (6-8 months) | 45.9% | 59.5%
[59:0:6] - **Anaemia (NFHS-5):** children under 5 about **67%**, women about **57%**. **AP (NFHS-5):** stunting **31.2%**, wasting 16.1%, underweight 29.6%.
[59:0:7] TABLE head: Burden | State
  [59:0:7.0] Highest share of malnourished children (MoSPI 2011, ~60%) | Madhya Pradesh – "Ethiopia of India"
  [59:0:7.1] Stunting (Poshan Tracker, July 2025) | Uttar Pradesh (48.8%)
  [59:0:7.2] Wasting | Bihar (9.3%)
  [59:0:7.3] Underweight | Madhya Pradesh
[59:0:8] - **Anaemia** (mainly **iron** deficiency) is a leading cause of **maternal mortality**, rising in AP. FSSAI: no HFSS food sale within **50 m of schools**.

[59:0:10] - NSSO: the share of households reporting **seasonal or chronic hunger** (not getting two square meals) has **decreased** steadily since the 1980s.
[59:0:11] - Malnutrition is **higher in rural India** (NFHS-5 stunting: rural 37.3% vs urban 30.1%), but remains a problem in urban India too.
[59:0:12] - **Soybean** (~40% protein) is the richest common plant source of protein – above urad, chana and pea (~20-25%).

## SUB 2 | PM POSHAN (Mid-Day Meal)
[59:2:0] - **NP-NSPE** (National Programme of Nutritional Support to Primary Education) launched **15 August 1995** as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme in 2,408 blocks (started in UP the same year); renamed **PM POSHAN in 2021**.
[59:2:1] - Covers **Bal Vatika and classes I-VIII** in government and aided schools; financed and managed by the **Ministry of HRD (now Education)**; Centre-State **60:40** (non-NER States/UTs).
[59:2:2] - Cooking cost for Bal Vatika and primary raised to **₹6.78** per child per day.
[59:2:3] - Organised by **school management, PRIs and SHGs**; a **contractor** does not initiate or organise it. Partner NGO: **Akshay Patra Foundation** (2000, Bengaluru) – mid-day meals for school students.
[59:2:4] ~~ Sources: app notes 3:59 sub 7; Ramesh Singh Indian Economy part 04 p.165 (MDMS)
## SUB 3 | POSHAN Abhiyaan and Anaemia Mukt Bharat
[59:3:0] - **POSHAN Abhiyaan (2018):** goal **Suposhit Bharat**; pillars **convergence, technology, Jan Andolan** (people's movement); targets stunting, underweight, anaemia and low birth weight – **not adult obesity**.
[59:3:1] - **Rashtriya Poshan Maah = September**; **Poshan Pakhwada** (April, two weeks; 2025 edition) – to **improve nutrition**, especially of women and children.
[59:3:2] - **Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0** (2021-22 to 2025-26): **2 lakh** Saksham AWCs; **POSHAN Tracker** = real-time monitoring of Anganwadi services.
[59:3:3] - **Anaemia Mukt Bharat:** **IFA tablets, deworming**, outreach for **6 target groups**; **WIFS** for adolescents **10-19**; **delayed cord clamping** campaign; **periodic deworming**; addresses **non-nutritional causes** (malaria, haemoglobinopathies, fluorosis). It does **not** give prophylactic **calcium** → **only three** of the four UPSC 2023 statements are correct.

## SUB 1 | Veterinary Practice – Vaccination, Feeding and Livestock Management
[60:1:0] # Vaccination
[60:1:1] - **Purpose of vaccinating animals:** **prevention of infectious diseases** – not treatment, not non-infectious diseases.
[60:1:2] - **Brucellosis:** female calves vaccinated once at about **4-6 (up to 8) months** (S19 / RB51); the live **Brucella S19 vaccine can infect the vaccinator** – handle carefully.
[60:1:3] - **Foot-and-mouth disease:** controlled with **inactivated (killed)** vaccine.
[60:1:4] - **Broilers:** LaSota (Newcastle, lentogenic) and IBD vaccines are used; **R2B (Mukteswar, mesogenic)** is a booster for older layers – **not used in broilers**.
[60:1:5] # Feeding and digestion
[60:1:6] - **Concentrate for dairy cows:** about **0.4 kg per litre of milk** (1 kg per 2.5 litres) over maintenance; buffalo about 0.5 kg per litre.
[60:1:7] - **Excess protein** to ruminants → ammonia in the rumen, high pH → **alkaline indigestion**; excess grain → acid indigestion.
[60:1:8] - **Hay** (well-cured) keeps **15-18% moisture**; wetter hay moulds.
[60:1:9] # Breeding and poultry
[60:1:10] - **Frozen semen extender:** **Tris** (tris-egg yolk-citrate-glycerol) – the most widely used.
[60:1:11] - **Semen collection from cocks** by abdominal massage = **'milking'**; candling = checking eggs; brooding = rearing chicks.
[60:1:12] - **Egg turning** during chicken incubation: **day 1 to 18**; stopped in the last three days (hatcher).
[60:1:13] - **CO2 in the incubator** for optimum hatchability: **0.3-0.5%**.
[60:1:14] # Meat animals and identification
[60:1:15] - **Dressing percentage** highest in the **pig** (~70-80%); cattle, sheep, goats ~45-55%.
[60:1:16] - **Pigs** are marketed at about **80-90 kg** live weight (6-8 months).
[60:1:17] - **Rabbit** = the most **prolific meat animal** (short gestation, large litters).
[60:1:18] - **Sheep and goats** are commonly identified by **ear tags** (ear notching); hot-iron branding is for cattle.

[61:0:4] # Textiles
[61:0:5] - **Cotton textiles = oldest large-scale industry**; **largest textile centre = Mumbai** (also Ahmedabad).
[61:0:6] - **Textiles = largest agro-based industry**; sugar is next.
[61:0:7] - **Largest employer after agriculture** (and the industry with most workers): **textiles** – about **4.5 crore direct**.
[61:0:8] - **Largest cottage industry = handloom**; **powerlooms** make most cloth (mill, powerloom, handloom segments).
[61:0:9] - **National Handloom Development Programme:** concessional credit, marketing assistance, block-level clusters; benefits include loan waivers, concessional loans, fairs, loom upgradation, training – **both statements true**.
[61:0:10] # Sugar, cement, steel and others

[61:4:2] - Sectors: **mining, manufacturing, electricity**. **NOT included: construction**, agriculture, services.
