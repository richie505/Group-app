## SUB 2 | Role, Contribution and Problems
[2:0] # Why small and cottage industries
[2:1] - **Labour-intensive, low capital** → **generate large employment** (the primary reason to encourage them); disperse industry; reduce **concentration of economic power**; ancillaries to large industry; priority since the **First Plan**.
[2:2] - Small-scale sector = about **40% of manufacturing gross turnover** (since 1992).
[2:3] - **Industrial clusters** help MSMEs (inclusiveness, technology, efficiency), exist in handloom, handicrafts and modern SMEs, are run by several ministries – they **do not promote monopoly**.
[2:4] TABLE head: Indicator (Rajya Sabha, Aug 2026) | Figure
  [2:4.0] Share in GDP | 31%
  [2:4.1] Share in manufacturing output | 36%
  [2:4.2] Share in exports | 41%
  [2:4.3] Outstanding MSME credit | ₹38.35 lakh crore
[2:5] - Udyam: **6.63 crore** registered (1 July 2020 – 31 July 2025); **micro = 95.88%**.
[2:6] # Problems
[2:7] TABLE head: Problem | Detail
  [2:7.0] Finance / capital | Shortage of working capital = main SSI problem
  [2:7.1] Marketing | Weak marketing and exports
  [2:7.2] Raw materials | Scarce, costly
  [2:7.3] Delayed payments | MSME Samadhaan (2017)
  [2:7.4] Others | No latest technology, power, poor managerial skills, sickness
[2:8] - **IPR 1948** listed handicaps: **raw materials, finance, marketing, technology** → "all of the above".
[2:9] - **Strikes and lockouts** = problem of **large** industries, NOT SSIs; excess cheap credit is not a problem.
[2:10] - **Globalisation and WTO** hit **small-scale industrialists and businessmen** hardest (cheap imports, dereservation).

## SUB 3 | MSMED Act, Procurement and Payments
[3:0] - **Chapter V (delayed payments):** pay an MSE within **45 days**; else **compound interest at 3 times the RBI bank rate**; disputes → **MSE Facilitation Council**.
[3:1] - **MSMED (Amendment) Bill 2026:** every **CPSE to settle MSME invoices on TReDS**.
[3:2] - **Public Procurement Policy for MSEs Order, 2012:** **25%** of annual procurement from MSEs (from **20% in 2018**); **4% SC/ST**, **3% women**; achieved FY26 (to 1 Dec 2025) **44.76%**.
