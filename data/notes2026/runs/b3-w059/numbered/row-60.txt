## SUB 0 | Animal Husbandry Practice – Breeding, Health, Poultry and Meat
[0:0] - **Artificial insemination (AI):** semen of **superior bulls can reach every village**, it **avoids contagious (venereal) diseases** and it is **economical** – so "all of the above".
[0:1]   - **First scientific AI: Lazzaro Spallanzani** (Italy, in a dog, 1780); Ivanoff (Russia) developed it for farm animals.
[0:2]   - **First AI in India: Palace Dairy Farm, Mysore (1939)**, by Sampath Kumaran; Izzatnagar (UP) is the seat of IVRI.
[0:3] TABLE head: Delivery / reproduction term | Meaning
  [0:3.0] Calving / Lambing / Kidding / Whelping | Cow / sheep (ewe) / goat / dog gives birth
  [0:3.1] Prolapse | Falling out of part of the womb (uterus or vagina) before or after calving
  [0:3.2] Pyometra | Pus in the uterus (endometritis = inflammation of its lining)
  [0:3.3] Oestrus in buffalo | Lasts about 24-36 hours (longer than in cows)
  [0:3.4] Service period (cross-bred cow) | About 3 months (60-90 days) after calving → calving interval about 12-13 months
[0:4] - **Small ruminants = sheep and goats**; cattle and buffalo are large ruminants; horses, donkeys, pigs and rabbits are not ruminants.
[0:5] - **Epidural anaesthesia in cows: lignocaine (2%)** – thiopentone and propofol are general anaesthetics, morphine an opioid.
[0:6] - **Ranikhet (Newcastle) disease** of poultry shows **respiratory, nervous and gastrointestinal signs** – "all of these".
[0:7] - **Small dog breed: pug** (toy); boxer and Dalmatian are large, bulldog medium.
[0:8] TABLE head: Poultry practice | Norm
  [0:8.0] Storing hatching eggs | Best set within 1 week of laying (cool store)
  [0:8.1] Egg turning in the setter | At least 6-8 times a day
  [0:8.2] Setter → hatcher transfer | 18th day (chicken eggs hatch in 21 days)
  [0:8.3] Floor space, layer on deep litter | About 2 sq ft per hen
[0:9] TABLE head: Meat and slaughter-house norm | Value
  [0:9.0] Thawing meat for feeding zoo animals | 4-6 °C (in a refrigerator, not at room temperature)
  [0:9.1] Shelf life of canned meat | About 2 years
  [0:9.2] Floor gradient in a slaughter house | About 2 cm per metre (1 in 48 to 1 in 60 towards the drains)
[0:10] - **Rotational grazing:** livestock moved through a **sequence of paddocks (six or more)** several times a season so each paddock rests and regrows; leader-follower and creep grazing are its variants.

## SUB 1 | Horticulture – Fruits, Mango, Spices and GI
[1:0] - **Fruit ranks:** India is the **2nd largest producer of fruits and vegetables (China 1st)** – so "India ranks first in fruit production" is false; India is the largest producer of **mango, banana, guava, papaya**, spices, coconut and cashew.
[1:1] - **Top fruit by output in India: banana** (ahead of mango); **national fruit: mango**.
[1:2] - **Mango exports:** **Alphonso** (Maharashtra, Konkan) is the most exported and the preferred export variety; Kesar, Banganapalli and Totapuri follow.
[1:3] TABLE head: Mango GI | Year / State
  [1:3.0] Banganapalle (AP) | GI in May 2017
  [1:3.1] Marathwada Kesar | GI 30 Nov 2016 (Maharashtra)
  [1:3.2] Laxman Bhog, Himsagar | GI 2008 (West Bengal)
[1:4] - All four of these mangoes are now GI-registered; before May 2017 **Banganapalle** was the one not yet registered.
[1:5] - **GI tag** improves the **marketability** (premium price, protection from imitation) – it is not ornamental.
[1:6] - **'Kanchan'** is an improved variety of **aonla / amla (Indian gooseberry)**.
[1:7] - **Turmeric:** India is the **largest producer, consumer and exporter** (over 70% of world output); **30+ varieties**; major States **Maharashtra, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu** (and AP) – all three UPSC 2025 statements correct. **National Turmeric Board – Nizamabad**.
[1:8] - **Flowers:** the 2004 key said **Karnataka** (old floriculture leader around Bengaluru); now **Tamil Nadu** leads (about 21%), then Karnataka (16%), Madhya Pradesh, West Bengal.
[1:9] - **ICAR-IIHR** (Indian Institute of Horticultural Research) is at **Hesaraghatta, Bengaluru (Karnataka)**; it held the National Horticultural Fair 2023.
[1:10] - **FPO (Food Products Order) mark**: mandatory on processed fruit products (jams, squashes, pickles); ISI is for industrial goods, BEE for energy labels, Hallmark for gold.

## SUB 2 | Food Processing – SAMPADA, Mega Food Parks, Operation Greens
[2:0] - **PM Kisan SAMPADA Yojana (2017)**: umbrella scheme of the **Ministry of Food Processing Industries (MoFPI)**; aim – **supplement agriculture, modernise processing and decrease agri-waste**. Its 'Unit' scheme: **32 projects sanctioned (2020)**.
[2:1] - **Mega Food Parks:** to **improve infrastructure for food processing**; cluster approach linking farmers, processors and retailers; aims (UPSC 2011): **good infrastructure, more processing of perishables / less wastage, and emerging eco-friendly technologies** – all three.
[2:2]   - Capital grant **50%** of project cost (75% in difficult / ITDP areas), ceiling ₹50 crore.
[2:3]   - **Italy's first Mega Food Park in India (June 2021): Fanidhar, Mehsana (Gujarat)**.
[2:4] - **Operation Greens (2018)**: on the lines of Operation Flood, for **Tomato-Onion-Potato (TOP)** price stabilisation; under **MoFPI**; promotes FPOs, agri-logistics and processing.
[2:5] - **PMFME**: micro food units under **One District One Product (ODOP)**.
[2:6] - **APEDA (1985, Ministry of Commerce)** promotes agri exports – e.g. an awareness camp for **potato** exports in **Agra** (Jan 2020).
[2:7] - **Processed food exports** = about **23%** of agri-food exports (23.4% in 2023-24, up from 13.7% in 2014-15; APPSC 2025 key "around 23").

## SUB 4 | Dairy – Operation Flood, Cooperatives and Milk Science
[4:0] - **White Revolution = increase in milk production**; **Operation Flood** (NDDB) is the programme behind it – related to **milk production**, not floods or irrigation.
[4:1] - **Operation Flood (1970-1996)**: three phases; world's biggest dairy development programme; built on the **Anand pattern cooperatives (Amul)** – the engine of its success; India passed the USA as the largest milk producer in 1998.
[4:2] - **NDDB**: set up **1965 at Anand, Gujarat**.
[4:3] - **Co-operative dairying is most successful in Gujarat** (Anand, Amul, GCMMF) – the pioneer of India's cooperative dairy movement.
[4:4] - **Milk:** India 1st (about 25% of world), 471 g/day per capita; top States UP, Rajasthan, MP, Gujarat, Maharashtra. Yield **per cow is low in India**; **Denmark** has among the world's highest yields (ahead of the Netherlands and Great Britain).
[4:5] - **Milk science:** milk is a **colloid (emulsion)**; **skimmed milk has a higher specific gravity than whole milk** (fat, the lightest part, is removed: about 1.035 vs 1.032).
[4:6] - **HTST pasteurisation: 72 °C for 15 seconds** (batch/LTLT: 63 °C for 30 minutes).
[4:7] - India also leads world output of **butter and ghee**.
[4:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Indian Economy - APPSC 21 p.1-2 (Operation Flood, Anand pattern; its "launched in 1966" is a slip for 1970); PMF Human & Economic Geography 06 p.31 (Gujarat pioneer of cooperative dairy); app notes 3:60 sub 4-5; general knowledge (milk density, HTST, Denmark yield)
## SUB 5 | Fisheries – Blue Revolution, Production and PMMSY
[5:0] - **Blue Revolution = fish production** (pisciculture); **NFDB** (autonomous, 2006, **HQ Hyderabad**) promotes it and handles **training and capacity building under PMMSY** (not MPEDA).
[5:1] - **China is the largest fish producer** (capture and aquaculture); (**36%** of world output in 2022, SOFIA 2024); **India 2nd** (about 8-9% of world); India **leads the world in inland capture** (2.2 MT, SOFIA 2026).
[5:2] - Output: 95.79 lakh t (2013-14) → **197.75 lakh t (2024-25)**; fisheries = about 7.43% of agri GVA.
[5:3] - **Inland fisheries** = raising and capturing fish in **fresh water**; inland = **about 77%** of output (15.16 MT of 19.78 MT, 2024-25), and most of it is **inland aquaculture (culture fisheries)** – capture is only about 2.2 MT. So the most important fish industry today is **culture inland**, not natural inland or deep-sea.
[5:4] - **PMMSY**: 2020, **₹20,050 crore**, FY 2020-21 to 2024-25, extended to 2025-26; Central Sector 100% (training, quarantine, harbour modernisation, data systems); Centrally Sponsored 60:40, 90:10 NE/Himalayan, 100% UTs.
[5:5] - **PM-MKSSY**: **Central Sector** sub-scheme under PMMSY (formalisation, micro-enterprises).
[5:6] - **FIDF**: fishing harbours and landing centres; credit guarantee up to ₹12.5 crore; KCC limit for fisheries raised to ₹5 lakh.
[5:7] - **MPEDA**: Act of Parliament **1972**; **HQ Kochi** (not Kolkata); **Ministry of Commerce and Industry**; seafood exports ₹62,408 crore (2024-25).
[5:8] - Fish growth rate 2024-25 (APPSC AO 2025 options 5.3 / 10.2 / 15.6 / 20.1): no official figure matching these options was found; production grew about 7.5% in 2024-25.

## SUB 11 | Veterinary Practice – Vaccination, Feeding and Livestock Management
[11:0] # Vaccination
[11:1] - **Purpose of vaccinating animals:** **prevention of infectious diseases** – not treatment, not non-infectious diseases.
[11:2] - **Brucellosis:** female calves vaccinated once at about **4-6 (up to 8) months** (S19 / RB51); the live **Brucella S19 vaccine can infect the vaccinator** – handle carefully.
[11:3] - **Foot-and-mouth disease:** controlled with **inactivated (killed)** vaccine.
[11:4] - **Broilers:** LaSota (Newcastle, lentogenic) and IBD vaccines are used; **R2B (Mukteswar, mesogenic)** is a booster for older layers – **not used in broilers**.
[11:5] # Feeding and digestion
[11:6] - **Concentrate for dairy cows:** about **0.4 kg per litre of milk** (1 kg per 2.5 litres) over maintenance; buffalo about 0.5 kg per litre.
[11:7] - **Excess protein** to ruminants → ammonia in the rumen, high pH → **alkaline indigestion**; excess grain → acid indigestion.
[11:8] - **Hay** (well-cured) keeps **15-18% moisture**; wetter hay moulds.
[11:9] # Breeding and poultry
[11:10] - **Frozen semen extender:** **Tris** (tris-egg yolk-citrate-glycerol) – the most widely used.
[11:11] - **Semen collection from cocks** by abdominal massage = **'milking'**; candling = checking eggs; brooding = rearing chicks.
[11:12] - **Egg turning** during chicken incubation: **day 1 to 18**; stopped in the last three days (hatcher).
[11:13] - **CO2 in the incubator** for optimum hatchability: **0.3-0.5%**.
[11:14] # Meat animals and identification
[11:15] - **Dressing percentage** highest in the **pig** (~70-80%); cattle, sheep, goats ~45-55%.
[11:16] - **Pigs** are marketed at about **80-90 kg** live weight (6-8 months).
[11:17] - **Rabbit** = the most **prolific meat animal** (short gestation, large litters).
[11:18] - **Sheep and goats** are commonly identified by **ear tags** (ear notching); hot-iron branding is for cattle.
