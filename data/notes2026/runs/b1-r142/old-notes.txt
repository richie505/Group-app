=== NOTES 1:142 | Bhakti Movement – Origins, Alvars and Nayanars, Nirguna and Saguna Streams, Social Base
## SUB 0 | Meaning, Roots and Origin
[142:0:0] - **Bhakti** from the root **"bhaj"** – to share or participate.
[142:0:1] - Roots: **Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, Bhagavata Purana** (not the Arthashastra).
[142:0:2] - **Gita Chapter 12 = Bhakti Yoga**: both Saguna and Nirguna valid; Saguna upasana easier for ordinary people.
[142:0:3] - **Bhagavata Purana**: first to put bhakti at the centre of religion; **ninefold (navadha) bhakti** – shravana, kirtana, smarana, padasevana, archana, vandana, dasya, sakhya, atmanivedana. Most scholars date it c. 9th-10th c.
[142:0:4] - Began in the **Tamil South** with the **Alvars and Nayanars (6th-9th c.)**; reached the north by the **15th c.**; overall span **6th-18th c.**
[142:0:5] - Revival in the **15th-16th c.** (Kabir, Tulsi, Nanak, Sur, Mirabai); a line traces its revival to **Adi Shankaracharya's Advaitavada (8th c.)** against Buddhism's influence.
[142:0:6] - Movement **started by the Alvar saints** (with the Shaiva Nayanars).
[142:0:7] # Why It Arose
[142:0:8] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [142:0:8.0] Reaction | Against ritualism, priestly dominance and caste
  [142:0:8.1] Hard paths | Gyana marga and karma marga too hard for ordinary people
  [142:0:8.2] Debate | Older historians: reaction to Islam; modern scholars: indigenous Alvar-Nayanar roots – Islam shaped only the later egalitarianism
[142:0:9] - Core beliefs: **monotheism**; **guru essential**; salvation through devotion.

## SUB 3 | Nirguna and Saguna Streams
[142:3:0] TABLE head: Stream | Idea of God | Saints
  [142:3:0.0] Nirguna | Formless, attributeless; rejects idols (e.g. the Sikh Nirankar) | Kabir (APPSC GS 2024), Guru Nanak, Dadu Dayal, Ravidas (Raidas)
  [142:3:0.1] Saguna – Ramamargi | Rama | Tulsidas
  [142:3:0.2] Saguna – Krishnamargi | Krishna | Surdas, Mirabai, Chaitanya, Vallabha
[142:3:1] - Both streams drew on the Upanishadic **Advaita**; movement basically **monotheistic**.
[142:3:2] - Nirguna saints rejected idol worship, ritual, pilgrimage, fasting, caste; **Saguna saints kept idol worship**.
[142:3:3] - **Dadu Panth** – founded by **Dadu Dayal in Rajasthan**, in Kabir's tradition (not by Kabir).
[142:3:4] - **Raskhan** (Sayyid Ibrahim, also Sujan Raskhan) – 16th-c. Muslim poet devoted to **Krishna** (not Rama); **Prem Vatika (c. 1614)** in Braj.
[142:3:5] APPSC GS 2024: Nirguna saint = **Kabir**.
## SUB 4 | Nathpanthis, Siddhas and Yogis
[142:4:0] - Spread devotional religion in **Northern India** (BPSC 2023).
[142:4:1] - Leading figures: **Gorakhnath, Matsyendranath**.
[142:4:2] - Practices: **hatha yoga**, meditation, **renunciation**; criticised **ritualism, caste and orthodox religion**.
[142:4:3] - Used the vernacular; influenced **Kabir's nirguna** idiom.
[142:4:4] - **Siddhacharyas – Charyapada:** the **Charyapada** (Charyagiti) are mystic songs of the Buddhist **Siddhacharyas** (Luipa, Kahnapa and others, 9th-11th c.) – the **earliest Bangla poems**. **Haraprasad Shastri** found the palm-leaf manuscript in the **royal court library of Nepal (1907)**.
## SUB 5 | Kabir, Guru Nanak and the Sikh Gurus
[142:5:0] # Kabir
[142:5:1] - Disciple of **Ramananda**; a **weaver**; raised by Muslim weaver couple **Niru and Nima**; called the greatest saint of the cult.
[142:5:2] - **One God, many names** (Ram, Rahim, Allah, Hari); condemned ritual on both sides.
[142:5:3] - **Bijak** = sacred book of his followers.
[142:5:4] # Guru Nanak and Successors
[142:5:5] - Nirguna; rejected caste discrimination; stressed virtue over caste; **Guru ka Langar** (common dining) began at **Kartarpur**.
[142:5:6] - Successor **Guru Angad** (Lehna): standardised the **Gurmukhi** script; systematised the langar.
[142:5:7] - **Adi Granth (Guru Granth Sahib)** compiled by **Guru Arjan Dev**, completed and installed in the Harmandir Sahib, **Amritsar, 1604**; scribe **Bhai Gurdas**; includes **Kabir** and other Bhagats.
[142:5:8] - **Guru Gobind Singh (1706)** added Guru Tegh Bahadur's hymns and declared it the Guru.

## SUB 7 | Maharashtra Dharma – Varkari Sect
[142:7:0] - Centred on **Vithoba (Vitthal)** at **Pandharpur**; annual pilgrimage on **Ekadashi**.
[142:7:1] - **Jnaneshwar** – credited with founding the sect; **Jnaneshwari** (Marathi commentary on the Gita), Amritanubhava.
[142:7:2] - **Namdev** – **most influential** saint of the Maharashtra tradition; his bhakti showed Islamic influence.
[142:7:3] - **Eknath** – Eknathi Bhagavata; **Tukaram** – 17th c., thousands of **abhangas**; also **Narhari**.
## SUB 8 | Virashaiva / Lingayat Movement
[142:8:0] - **Basavanna (Basaveshwara)**: head of the treasury at **Kalyana** under Kalachuri king **Bijjala**; **Anubhava Mantapa** – assembly of sharanas of every caste, men and women, presided by **Allama Prabhu**.
[142:8:1] - **Vachanas**: short Kannada devotional sayings signed **Kudalasangamadeva**.
[142:8:2] - **Akkamahadevi** – 12th-c. woman saint of Karnataka.
[142:8:3] - **AP angle**: Veerashaivism reached Andhra through **Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya** and **Palkuriki Somanatha**.
## SUB 9 | Social Base – Gurus, Disciples, Caste and Women
[142:9:0] # Guru → Disciple
[142:9:1] TABLE head: Guru | Disciple(s)
  [142:9:1.0] Ramanuja | Ramananda (in his tradition)
  [142:9:1.1] Ramananda | Kabir (weaver), Raidas/Ravidas (shoemaker), Dhanna (Jat cultivator), Sena (barber)
  [142:9:1.2] Ravidas | Mirabai
  [142:9:1.3] Vallabhacharya | Surdas
  [142:9:1.4] Charandas (Charandasi Sampradaya) | Daya Bai (RPSC 2018) – Dayabodh, Vinay Malika
[142:9:2] - **Ramananda** – forerunner who brought bhakti to the north; **first medieval reformer to openly denounce caste**; admitted disciples of all castes.
[142:9:3] - **Chronological order**: Ramananda → Kabir → Nanak → Chaitanya → Tulsidas.
[142:9:4] # Women and Anti-Caste Voices
[142:9:5] - Women saints: **Andal, Karaikkal Ammaiyar, Akkamahadevi, Mirabai, Daya Bai**; **Sahajobai** – Sahaj-Prakash (guru bhakti, Nirgun).
[142:9:6] - Ganesh Dev Leela – wrongly matched with Gangabai.
[142:9:7] - **Palnati Brahmanayudu** (AP): set up **Chapakudu** (common dining for all castes); Veeravaishnavism in Palnadu; treated Mala devotee **Kannama Dasu** as his son.
[142:9:8] - **Vemana** and **Potuluri Veerabrahmam** condemned caste.
[142:9:9] - Earlier challenges: **Buddha** (birth does not decide spiritual worth); **Mahavira** (Kshatriya of Kundagrama; first disciple Jamali).
[142:9:10] - **Impact**: brought Hindus and Muslims closer; weakened caste chains but **did not end caste** or achieve lasting Hindu-Muslim unity.
[142:9:11] - **Devotion over birth:** Bhakti saints taught that devotion, not birth or social status, decides a person's worth before God; in this way they challenged the caste hierarchy.

=== NOTES 1:143 | Bhakti Saints and Their Teachings – Ramanuja, Madhva, Nimbarka, Vallabha, Ramananda
## SUB 0 | Bhakti Background
[143:0:0] - Loving devotion towards God; originated in **South India** (Tamil region): **Alvars – Vishnu; Nayanars – Shiva**.
[143:0:1] - The acharyas gave Bhakti a **philosophical base against Advaita** and opened temple worship to lower groups.
[143:0:2] TABLE head: Saint | Key fact

  [143:0:2.4] Shankaradeva | Brought Vaishnavism to Assam (Kamarupa) and Cooch Behar; Ekasarana sect – one deity (Krishna), against rituals and idolatry
## SUB 1 | Acharya → School, Teaching and Deity
[143:1:0] TABLE head: Acharya | School | Teaching emphasis | Deity
  [143:1:0.0] Shankaracharya (788-820) | Advaita (non-dualism) | Brahman the only reality; mukti through knowing Atman = Brahman; knowledge | –
  [143:1:0.1] Ramanuja (1017-1137) | Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism, "non-dualism with differentiation") | Salvation through intense devotion (prapatti) to Vishnu; souls and universe dependent parts of the Supreme; reaction against Advaita | Vishnu / Narayana with Lakshmi
  [143:1:0.2] Madhvacharya (13th c.) | Dvaita (dualism) | God, souls and world eternally distinct; Saguna bhakti | Vishnu (Krishna at Udupi)

[143:7:7] - **Statue of Equality**, Hyderabad – sitting statue honouring Ramanuja; world's second-tallest sitting statue, inaugurated by the PM (UPSC 2022).

## SUB 2 | Kabir
[144:2:0] - Birth legend: born to a **Brahmin widow**; raised by Muslim weaver **Niru and Nima**.
[144:2:1] - Guru: **Ramananda**. Stream: **Nirguna Bhakti** (APPSC-GS 2024).
[144:2:2] - One God called by many names: **Ram, Rahim, Allah, Khuda, Hari, Govinda**.
[144:2:3] - Condemned **rituals and idol worship** of both Hindus and Muslims; opposed **caste**; stood for **Hindu-Muslim unity**; called the greatest saint of the Bhakti cult.
[144:2:4] - **Dohas and sakhis** collected in the **Bijak**, the main text of the **Kabirpanthis**.
[144:2:5] - "**Pothi Padh Padh Jag Mua ... Dhai Akhar Prem Ka**" - Kabir.
[144:2:6] - AP parallel: **Vemana** and **Potuluri Veerabrahmam** attacked caste like Kabir.
## SUB 3 | Guru Nanak
[144:3:0] - Born **1469 at Talwandi** (Nankana Sahib, near the Ravi); founded the **Sikh religion**; one formless God (**Ik Onkar**).

[144:3:2] - "**God knows man's virtues and inquires not his caste**" - Guru Nanak.
[144:3:3] - Companion/disciple: **Mardana**. Language **Punjabi** (Guru Angad later introduced the **Gurmukhi** script).
## SUB 4 | Chaitanya Mahaprabhu
[144:4:0] - Born **1486 at Nabadwip (Nadia, West Bengal)**, Brahmin family; parents **Jagannath Mishra and Shachi Devi**; childhood name **Nimai**.

[144:4:2] - Founded **Gaudiya Vaishnavism**; popularised **Sankirtan** (collective devotional singing), later written up as **Padavali kirtans**.
