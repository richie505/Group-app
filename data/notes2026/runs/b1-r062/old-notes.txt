  [62:0:0.3] 4 | Samudragupta | c. 335–375 CE; Prayag Prashasti; Napoleon of India (V.A. Smith, *Early History of India*); first Parama Bhagawat
  [62:0:0.4] 5 | Ramagupta | Brief, disputed reign
  [62:0:0.5] 6 | Chandragupta II | c. 380–415 CE
  [62:0:0.6] 7 | Kumaragupta I | c. 415–455 CE

[62:0:1] - Exam key: Gupta dynasty ruled **319–500 AD**.
[62:0:2] APPSC G1 2018: **Samudragupta → Chandragupta II → Kumaragupta I → Skandagupta**.
[62:0:3] Distractor rulers: **Bindusara** extended Mauryan power into the Deccan; **Chandragupta Maurya** founded the Mauryan empire; **Harsha** patronised Banabhatta.
## SUB 1 | Chandragupta II Vikramaditya
[62:1:0] # Ramagupta Episode and Accession
[62:1:1] - Source: **Devichandraguptam** of **Vishakhadatta**.
[62:1:2] - Elder brother **Ramagupta** (son of Samudragupta) could not resist the Sakas and agreed to surrender queen **Dhruvadevi** to a Saka king; Chandragupta II killed the Saka king, then Ramagupta, and married Dhruvadevi (BPSC 2022).
[62:1:3] # Titles and Names
[62:1:4] - **Vikramaditya** (linked with the Saka victory), **Sakari** (Saka-conqueror), **Simhavikrama** (lion-slayer coins; his father = tiger-slayer coins).
[62:1:5] - **Devagupta** (Sanchi and Vakataka inscriptions), **Devaraja** (Sanchi), **Devashri**.
[62:1:6] # Saka Conquest
[62:1:7] - Defeated **Rudrasimha III**, the **last Saka** ruler – took **western Malwa and Gujarat** and the western sea ports.
[62:1:8] - Made **Ujjain** his **second capital**.
[62:1:9] - Evidence: **Udayagiri cave** inscription of his minister **Virasena**; and his **silver coins** (Rupak) on the Saka model, about **30–33 grains** – the **strongest evidence**.
[62:1:10] - **First Gupta ruler to issue silver coins.**
[62:1:11] The Saka-model coins were **silver**, not gold. Harishena was Samudragupta's poet, not Chandragupta II's minister.
[62:1:12] # Marriage Alliances
[62:1:13] TABLE head: Alliance | Detail
  [62:1:13.0] Naga | Married Kuberanaga
  [62:1:13.1] Vakataka | Daughter Prabhavati Gupta married Rudrasena II; ruled as regent after his death – Gupta influence in the Deccan without annexation
  [62:1:13.2] Dhruvadevi | Married after killing Ramagupta
[62:1:14] # Court and Fa-Hien
[62:1:15] - **Navaratnas** (tradition): **Kalidasa, Amarasimha, Varahamihira**, Dhanvantari, Vararuchi, Shanku, Ghatakarpara, Kshapanaka, Vetalabhatta.
[62:1:16] - **Fa-Hien (Faxian)** came via the Silk Route and Central Asia, c. **399–412 CE** (about ten years), also Sri Lanka; **did not name the king**. First of three Chinese pilgrims – the others **Xuan Zang** and **I-Qing** (7th c.).
[62:1:17] Fa-Hien was a pilgrim, **not** one of the Navaratnas.
## SUB 2 | Kumaragupta I (c. 415–455)
[62:2:0] - Titles: **Shakraditya, Mahendraditya**.
[62:2:1] - Founded **Nalanda** (c. 450 AD). An APPSC G1 key gave Asoka, but Kumaragupta I is the usual answer.
[62:2:2] - Nalanda: identified by **Alexander Cunningham** (1861); ASI excavations from **1915–16**; destroyed by **Bakhtiyar Khalji** (end of 12th c.).
[62:2:3] - Coins: gold type of king **feeding a peacock + Kartikeya**; Ashvamedha.
[62:2:4] - Records: **Bilsad** (Gupta year 96 = 415 CE, earliest); **Damodarpur** copper plates (guilds acting as banks); **Mandsaur** prashasti by **Vatsabhatti** (silk-weavers' Sun Temple, Bandhuvarman).
[62:2:5] - Threats: **Pushyamitras** and the first Huna attack late in his reign.
## SUB 3 | Skandagupta (c. 455–467)
[62:3:0] - Repulsed the **Hunas twice**; title **Vikramaditya**; "Gupta ruler who defeated the Hunas" (UPPSC 2015).
[62:3:1] - **Bhitari pillar** (Saidpur, Ghazipur, UP): victories over the **Pushyamitras and Hunas** (UPPSC 2014).
[62:3:2] - **Junagadh:** governor Parnadatta repaired the Sudarshana lake.
[62:3:3] - Gold coins: **bow and arrow, Garuda standard, Lakshmi**.
[62:3:4] - Central control weakened; governors became **hereditary feudatories**.
## SUB 4 | Huna Invasions
[62:4:0] - Hunas = **Hephthalites / White Huns** from Central Asia via the **Oxus** valley; took **Gandhara** (mid-5th c.).
[62:4:1] TABLE head: Wave / leader | Gupta reign | Outcome
  [62:4:1.0] First attack | Late Kumaragupta I | Checked by prince Skandagupta; he beat them again as king (Guptas = dynasty most troubled by Hunas)
  [62:4:1.1] Toramana (second invasion) | Weak successors (late 5th c.) | Conquered up to Eran; by 485 CE Hunas held Punjab, Rajasthan, Kashmir, eastern Malwa, central India
  [62:4:1.2] Mihirakula (Toramana's son, c. 515) | Narasimhagupta | Tyrant, iconoclast, persecuted Buddhists; defeated by Yashodharman (and Narasimhagupta Baladitya, Maukharis)
[62:4:2] Mihirakula was defeated by **Yashodharman**, not Skandagupta.
[62:4:3] # Yashodharman of Mandasor
[62:4:4] - **Aulikara** king / Gupta feudatory of Malwa; defeated Mihirakula in **528 CE**.
[62:4:5] - Victory pillars at **Sondani** (near Mandasor), c. **532 CE**; Sondani inscription composed by **Vasula Kavi**.
[62:4:6] - His rise **weakened** Gupta authority; it did not revive the empire.
## SUB 5 | Inscriptions on Hunas and Successors
[62:5:0] TABLE head: Inscription | Records
  [62:5:0.0] Bhitari pillar | Skandagupta vs Hunas
  [62:5:0.1] Eran boar (Varaha) | Toramana's first regnal year – Huna rule over Eran
  [62:5:0.2] Eran, 510 CE | Goparaja (Bhanugupta's general) – earliest sati record
  [62:5:0.3] Mandasor / Sondani pillars | Yashodharman's defeat of Mihirakula
  [62:5:0.4] Gwalior | Mihirakula's territory, including Gwalior
  [62:5:0.5] Maukhari copper seal | Sarvavarman; found at Asirgarh (MP), not Gaya; genealogy from Harivarman
[62:5:1] - Banabhatta's **Harshacharita** also mentions the Hunas.
## SUB 6 | Causes of Gupta Decline
[62:6:0] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [62:6:0.0] External | Repeated Huna invasions drained treasury and army; Pushyamitras
  [62:6:0.1] Weak successors | After Skandagupta
  [62:6:0.2] Feudatory | Land grants and hereditary governors created feudatories who broke away (Yashodharman; Valabhi, Bengal)
  [62:6:0.3] Economic | Land grants cut revenue – no professional army; loss of western India cut trade and Roman silk income; silk weavers migrated from Gujarat to Malwa (AD 473)
  [62:6:0.4] Religious policy | Later Guptas' leaning to Buddhism listed as a cause in some questions
[62:6:1] - Guptas donated the most villages to temples and Brahmins.
[62:6:2] # Coinage Debasement
[62:6:3] - Later Gupta gold coins = **less gold, more alloy**; coins slowly replaced by **payments in land**.
[62:6:4] - No Gupta coins or inscriptions in **western Malwa and Saurashtra** after Skandagupta – loss of control.
[62:6:5] The **Arab invasion was not** a cause (UKPSC 2012). Decline led to regional kingdoms and a feudal order, not a return to Mauryan centralisation.
## SUB 7 | Successor Powers and Regional Kingdoms
[62:7:0] # North India before Harsha
[62:7:1] TABLE head: Power | Seat
  [62:7:1.0] Later Guptas | Magadha (mid-6th c. to c. 675 CE; not the imperial line)
  [62:7:1.1] Maukharis | Kannauj (Bihar–UP)
  [62:7:1.2] Maitrakas | Valabhi (Gujarat) – Dharapatta, Guhasena
  [62:7:1.3] Pushyabhutis | Thanesar (Haryana), later Kannauj
  [62:7:1.4] Aulikaras | Mandsaur (Yashodharman)
  [62:7:1.5] Gaudas | Bengal
[62:7:2] UPSC 2021: Guptas of Magadha, Pushyabhutis of Thanesar, Maukharis of Kannauj, Maitrakas of Valabhi – **not** Paramaras of Malwa or Yadavas of Devagiri (both later).
[62:7:3] # Deccan and South
[62:7:4] TABLE head: Dynasty | Region | Key fact
  [62:7:4.0] Vakatakas | Vidarbha, northern Maharashtra | Brahmins; Gupta contemporaries; Harishena patronised Ajanta; Prabhavati Gupta queen
  [62:7:4.1] Kadambas | Banavasi (Karnataka) | Founded by Mayurasharma (not Nalanda)
  [62:7:4.2] Vishnukundins | Andhra | Madhava Varma II (440–495 CE), greatest – over 100 battles, 11 Ashvamedhas; Indrabhattaraka Varma defeated by Maukhari Ishanavarma (Haraha inscription)

=== NOTES 1:63 | Gupta Administration – Feudalism Debate, Land Grants, Agraharas, Revenue and Local Bodies
## SUB 0 | Nature of Gupta Kingship
[63:0:0] - **Divine claims:** kings likened to **Vishnu**; **Samudragupta** equated with **Kubera (Dhanada), Varuna, Indra and Yama (Antaka)**.
[63:0:1] - **Succession:** hereditary, but **primogeniture was not firm**.
[63:0:2] - Imperial titles: Maharajadhiraja, Parameshvara, Paramabhattaraka.
[63:0:3] # Centralised Mauryas vs Decentralised Guptas
[63:0:4] TABLE head: Point | Mauryas | Guptas
  [63:0:4.0] Control | Centralised, state-regulated | Decentralised – run through samantas (feudatories) and grantees
  [63:0:4.1] Offices | Salaried bureaucracy | Many offices hereditary (e.g. Harisena inherited his post)
  [63:0:4.2] Pay | Cash | Often in land or kind
[63:0:5] - Samudragupta **reinstated** some defeated rulers as feudatories.
[63:0:6] - **Tax trend:** land taxes rose, trade taxes fell.
## SUB 1 | Officials and Their Functions
[63:1:0] TABLE head: Official | Function
  [63:1:0.0] Kumaramatya | Top officers ranked with princes; ministers and commanders chosen from them; own office (adhikarana); often paid in cash
  [63:1:0.1] Sandhivigrahika | Minister of peace and war
  [63:1:0.2] Mahadandanayaka | Chief judicial or military officer
  [63:1:0.3] Dandapashika | District police officer
  [63:1:0.4] Akshapataladhikrita | Keeper of royal records
  [63:1:0.5] Pustapala | Keeper of land-transfer records
  [63:1:0.6] Dutaka | Spy or messenger
[63:1:1] - **Amatya** (Satavahana period) – minister who advised the king.
[63:1:2] Dandapashika = police, not judge; Akshapataladhikrita = royal records, Pustapala = land records.
## SUB 2 | Administrative Units
[63:2:0] TABLE head: Unit (largest to smallest) | Level | Head
  [63:2:0.0] Bhukti (desha / rashtra) | Province | Uparika (governor appointed by king; sometimes "Maharaja")
  [63:2:0.1] Vishaya (pradesha) | District | Vishayapati
  [63:2:0.2] Vithi (esp. eastern India) | Group of villages | Ayuktaka
  [63:2:0.3] Grama | Village | Gramika / Gramadhyaksha; with kutumbis and mahattaras
[63:2:1] - Village land transactions needed the **consent of the village headman**.
[63:2:2] UPPSC 2023: **Bhukti → Vishaya → Vithi → Grama** (ascending order = Grama → Vithi → Vishaya → Bhukti).
[63:2:3] Uparika heads the **bhukti** (province); Vishayapati heads the **vishaya** (district).
## SUB 3 | Local Bodies – Town and District Councils
[63:3:0] - **Damodarpur copper plates:** a **district council** helped the district officer.
[63:3:1] # Town Board (Adhikarana)
[63:3:2] TABLE head: Member | Represented
  [63:3:2.0] Nagarashreshthi | Chief banker / merchant; chaired the board
  [63:3:2.1] Sarthavaha | Chief caravan trader
  [63:3:2.2] Prathama kulika | Chief artisan (craft guilds)
  [63:3:2.3] Prathama kayastha | Chief scribe
[63:3:3] APPSC G2 2012: "Kayastha" = scribes.
## SUB 4 | Judicial System
[63:4:0] - More developed than before; **civil and criminal law clearly separated**: **property disputes = civil**; **theft, adultery = criminal**.
[63:4:1] - Law books of the period: **Narada, Brihaspati, Katyayana** smritis – the leading authorities on legal procedure; **Narada** survives complete and is devoted to civil law (vyavahara); Brihaspati and Katyayana survive in fragments.
## SUB 5 | Revenue Terms
[63:5:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [63:5:0.0] Bhaga | King's share of produce – main land revenue; one-sixth per law books
  [63:5:0.1] Bhoga | Periodic supplies in kind (fruits, firewood, flowers)
  [63:5:0.2] Kara | Periodic tax
  [63:5:0.3] Udranga | Land revenue / land tax in Gupta inscriptions
  [63:5:0.4] Uparikara | Additional (new) farm tax, probably on temporary tenants
  [63:5:0.5] Udakabhaga | Irrigation tax
  [63:5:0.6] Halivakara | Plough tax
  [63:5:0.7] Hiranya | Cash tax (Mauryan) instead of produce
  [63:5:0.8] Bali | Changed from voluntary to compulsory (oppressive)
  [63:5:0.9] Sita | Revenue from crown land
  [63:5:0.10] Sulka (Shulka) | Customs duty / tolls
  [63:5:0.11] Uparnika | Tax on farmers living on border land
  [63:5:0.12] Klipta / Upaklipta | Taxes on land sale and purchase
  [63:5:0.13] Valiam | Commodity tax
[63:5:1] - Revenue share: the king took **one-fourth to one-sixth** of produce; **one-sixth** is the prescribed rate, called **Udranga and Bhaga** in inscriptions.

[63:5:3] Bhaga = produce share; Bhoga = supplies in kind; Hiranya = cash; Halivakara = plough; Udakabhaga = irrigation.
## SUB 6 | Vishti and Peasant Condition
[63:6:0] - **Vishti** = **forced (unpaid) labour** for the army, officials, state or donee (Telugu **vetti**).
[63:6:1] - Treated as a **source of state income**, a kind of tax; imposed by state, governor or local chief.
[63:6:2] - Mentioned in the **Junagadh inscription** and Gupta copper inscriptions.
[63:6:3] - Peasants sank to a **semi-servile** status – tenant farming, sharecropping, tenancy at will, many taxes.

## SUB 8 | Land Grants and Agrarian Structure
[63:8:0] # Grant → Grantee
[63:8:1] TABLE head: Grant | Grantee | Rights
  [63:8:1.0] Brahmadeya | Brahmanas, singly or jointly | Tax exemption + administrative rights
  [63:8:1.1] Agrahara | Brahmanas (religious functionaries) | Tax-free, hereditary land/village
  [63:8:1.2] Devagrahara / Devadana | Temples and monasteries | Revenue for worship
  [63:8:1.3] Secular grants | Officials, feudatories | Revenue in lieu of salary
[63:8:2] - Tax-free farm land went to **Brahmins** and religious functionaries (MPPSC 2014).
[63:8:3] # Effects
[63:8:4] - 1. **Revenue + administrative, judicial and police rights** passed to grantees, who collected taxes from peasants.
[63:8:5] - 2. **Royal officials barred** from entering – led to **sub-infeudation**; grants did not bring direct royal rule.
[63:8:6] - 3. **Tribal frontiers:** grants in forest areas brought virgin land under farming and absorbed **tribes into the caste order as new sub-castes** (APPSC GS 2023).
[63:8:7] - 4. **Peasantry** became semi-servile.
[63:8:8] # FIRST and AP Angle
[63:8:9] - **Earliest land grants:** the **Satavahanas** – **100 nivartanas** to Buddhist monks (1 nivartana = about 1.5 acres) and agrahara-type grants to Brahmanas.
[63:8:10] - **Satavahana and Ikshvaku** brahmadeya grants stopped royal officials from interfering.
## SUB 9 | The Indian Feudalism Debate
[63:9:0] - **Definition:** grant of land by an overlord to a donee with financial, administrative and judicial rights.
[63:9:1] - **Essential elements (UPSC 2015):** administration based on **control of land**, **lord–vassal** ties, **weak central authority**.
[63:9:2] - **Evidence cited (7th c.):** decline in long-distance trade and **gold coinage**, shrinking towns, closed village economy.
[63:9:3] TABLE head: Historian | Position
  [63:9:3.0] R.S. Sharma | Proponent – feudalism via land grants, sub-infeudation, vishti, decline of trade, urban decay, fewer gold coins
  [63:9:3.1] D.C. Sircar | Critic – grants transferred revenue rights, not ownership; no feudalism in the European sense
  [63:9:3.2] Harbans Mukhia | Critic – "Was there feudalism in Indian history?"; no serfdom, a largely free and autonomous peasantry
  [63:9:3.3] B.D. Chattopadhyaya | Integrative / processual model – grants spread state society into new areas
[63:9:4] A **strong centre** with weak local authority is **not** a feudal feature.

=== NOTES 1:65 | Gupta Religion – Bhagavatism, Vaishnavism, Saivism; Beginnings of Temple Architecture (deogarh, Bhitargaon)
## SUB 0 | Nature of Gupta Religion
[65:0:0] - **Theistic and devotional** Hinduism replaced **sacrificial Brahmanism**: shift from the Vedic fire altar to **temple idol worship and bhakti**.
[65:0:1] - Supreme deities: **Brahma, Vishnu, Mahesh**.
[65:0:2] - **Purusharthas:** Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha; the first three = **trivarga**.
[65:0:3] - Smriti texts: **Yajnavalkya, Narada, Brihaspati, Katyayana**; Smarta practices spread; the **six schools of philosophy** took final shape.
[65:0:4] - Comparison: Mauryan religion = Vedic + Shramana; Gupta change = sacrifice → temple and bhakti.
## SUB 1 | Bhagavatism and Vaishnavism
[65:1:0] # Origin and Doctrine
[65:1:1] - Centre of worship: **Vasudeva-Krishna** (son of Devaki) of the **Vrishni (Satvata)** clan.
[65:1:2] - **First worshippers** of Vasudeva-Krishna = the **Bhagavatas (Vasudevaks)** (UPPSC 1997); the name is known in **Panini's** age.
[65:1:3] - Doctrine: **avatara** theory – **ten incarnations** of Vishnu; foundations = **karma, bhakti, ahimsa**.
[65:1:4] - Vaishnavism developed from the Bhagavata religion.
[65:1:5] # Heliodorus Pillar
[65:1:6] - **Earliest epigraphic evidence** of Bhagavatism = **Garuda pillar at Besnagar** (Vidisha).
[65:1:7] - Set up to Vasudeva by **Heliodorus**, Greek envoy of Indo-Greek king **Antialcidas** at the court of **Sunga Bhagabhadra**; he called himself a Bhagavata – a **foreign patron**.
[65:1:8] # Gupta Royal Religion
[65:1:9] - The **Guptas** made the greatest contribution to the Bhagavata sect (BPSC 1994) – virtually the state religion; most rulers titled **Parama Bhagavata**.
[65:1:10] - **First Gupta to take "Parama Bhagavata": Samudragupta** (UPPSC 2009, 2015); **Chandragupta II's** coins also carry the title.
[65:1:11] - **Dynastic emblem: Garuda**, Vishnu's mount.
[65:1:12] - Vaishnava symbols: **chakra, shankha, Garuda**; Saiva symbol: **linga**.
[65:1:13] - **AP angle:** **Salankayanas** followed Vaishnavism (APHQ 2011), though their rulers used the Shaiva title **Parama Mahesvara**; **Nandivarman II's Pedavegi** inscription = first in AP on Vishnu temples.
[65:1:14] # Varaha Panel at Udayagiri
[65:1:15] - Colossal **Varaha (boar) Vishnu** panel at **Udayagiri** (MP) carved under **Chandragupta II**; the cave was his **personal temple**.
## SUB 2 | Puranic Hinduism
[65:2:0] - **18 Mahapuranas** (APPSC G2 2011); compiled over centuries, **final shape in the Gupta period** (c. 4th–6th c. CE).
[65:2:1] - Principal Puranas: **Vishnu, Bhagavata, Matsya, Vayu, Brahmanda, Markandeya, Shiva, Kurma**.
[65:2:2] - **Pancha-lakshana** (five topics): creation, re-creation, genealogies, manvantaras, dynastic history.
[65:2:3] - **Kalika Purana** (Kali / Sati Purana) – Shaktism; an **upapurana**, likely written in **Assam**; credited to sage Markandeya.
## SUB 3 | Saivism – Sects and Linga Forms
[65:3:0] TABLE head: Sect | Key facts
  [65:3:0.0] Pashupata | Oldest Shaiva sect; founder Lakulisha (Nakulisha); pashu = individual soul, pati = Supreme soul
  [65:3:0.1] Kapalika | Extremist, skull-bearing sect; Bhairava, cremation ashes; came from the Misra Saivam school (APPSC GS 2018); Bhairavakonda caves (AP) belong to them
  [65:3:0.2] Kalamukha | Extreme sect; followers of Kalanana, a disciple of Lakulisha; strong in medieval Andhra (Simha Parishat) and Karnataka (Shakti Parishat)
  [65:3:0.3] Mattamayura | Found in Chedi inscriptions
  [65:3:0.4] Nathpanth | Matsyendranath, Gorakhnath; Shiva as Adinath
  [65:3:0.5] Lingayats | Followed Basava

  [65:3:2.0] Gudimallam | Parasurameswara temple near Renigunta, Tirupati district – earliest known Shiva linga, with a standing figure of Shiva (APPSC G2 2008)
