=== Chandragupta II Vikramaditya – Saka Conquest, Silver Coins, Titles and Fa-hien
# Accession – the Ramagupta episode
- **Killed his elder brother:** **Chandragupta II** took the throne after killing his elder brother **Ramagupta** and married his widow **Dhruvadevi (Dhruvaswamini)** – story in **Vishakhadatta's Devichandraguptam** (State PSC).
  - Ramagupta, defeated by the Sakas, had agreed to surrender Dhruvadevi to the Saka king; Chandragupta II, in disguise, killed the Saka king, then Ramagupta.
- **Order:** Samudragupta → (Ramagupta, brief and disputed) → Chandragupta II, c. **380–415 CE** (NextGen 380–413; PMF 380–412).
# Saka conquest and Ujjain
- **Saka-conqueror (Sakari):** Chandragupta II defeated **Rudrasimha III**, the last Western Kshatrapa (Saka) ruler, and annexed **western Malwa and Gujarat** with the western sea ports (UP UDA/LDA 2010).
- **Second capital:** **Ujjain (Ujjayini)** (RPSC 2008) – Malwa and Saurashtra are regions, Mehrauli the site of his iron pillar.
- **Evidence:** the **Udayagiri cave** inscription of his minister **Virasena**, and above all his **silver coins**.
# Silver coins – the strongest evidence
- **First Gupta to issue silver coins:** **Chandragupta II**, after the Saka conquest, on the model of the Western Kshatrapa silver drachms (UP UDA/LDA Mains 2010; MPPSC 2023). Earlier Guptas issued gold.
  - ⚠ NextGen Indian History 03 p.2 says "Chandragupta I was the first Gupta king to mint silver coins" – an error: PMF, the app notes and numismatic sources credit Chandragupta II.
- **UPPCS 2022 statements:** the silver coins are the **strongest evidence of the Saka victory**, and they weigh about **30–33 grains** like the Saka drachms → **both** statements correct.
- **Coin name:** silver = **rupaka**; gold = **dinara**. Lion-slayer coins gave the title **Simhavikrama** (his father = tiger-slayer coins).

# Marriage alliances
| Alliance | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Vakataka** | Daughter **Prabhavati Gupta** married **Rudrasena II**; she ruled the Vakataka kingdom as **regent** for her sons (RAS 2023; JPSC 2023) – indirect Gupta control of the Deccan |
| **Naga** | Married the Naga princess **Kuberanaga** (Prabhavati's mother) |
| **Dhruvadevi** | Married after killing Ramagupta |
# Fa-hien and the court
- **Fa-hien (Faxian):** came **c. 399–412 CE**, in the reign of **Chandragupta II** (UPSC 2025; State PSC) – not Samudragupta, Kumaragupta I or Skandagupta; he **did not name the king** – his interest was Buddhism. First of the three Chinese pilgrims (later **Xuan Zang**, **I-Qing**, 7th c.).
- **Navaratnas** (tradition): **Kalidasa, Amarasimha, Varahamihira**, Dhanvantari, Vararuchi, Shanku, Ghatakarpara, Kshapanaka, Vetalabhatta – Fa-hien was **not** one of them.
- **Not his minister:** **Harishena** was Samudragupta's court poet (Prayag prashasti), not Chandragupta II's minister.

=== The Hunas – Skandagupta, Toramana, Mihirakula and Yashodharman
# Who the Hunas were
- **Hunas = Hephthalites / White Huns** of Central Asia; strong in the **Oxus** valley, they entered India by taking **Gandhara** (mid-5th c.). **Toramana** was a **Huna** (IAS 1995) – not a Saka, Scythian or Yuezhi.
# Waves of invasion
| Wave / leader | Gupta reign | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| First attack | End of **Kumaragupta I**'s reign | Checked by **prince Skandagupta** |
| Main invasion | **Skandagupta** (c. 455–467) | Hunas **repulsed twice**; title **Vikramaditya** |
| **Toramana** (second invasion) | Weak successors, late 5th c. | Conquered up to **Eran**; Eran boar inscription of his 1st regnal year |
| **Mihirakula** (Toramana's son, c. 515) | **Narasimhagupta** | Tyrant, persecuted Buddhists; defeated by **Yashodharman** |
- **Gupta ruler who defeated the Hunas:** **Skandagupta** (UPPSC Lower 2015; UPPCS Mains 2004; State PSC) – not Samudragupta, Chandragupta II, Ramagupta or Bhanugupta.
- **Reign of the Huna invasion:** **Skandagupta** (UPPCS Mains 2006); the **Guptas** were the dynasty most troubled by the Hunas (UPPCS 1993).
- **"First Huna invasion faced by" (APSC 2017):** the usual key is **Skandagupta** – PMF: Skandagupta "was the one who really faced the Hunas invasion"; NextGen adds that the first attack came when Kumaragupta I was very old and was repulsed by prince Skandagupta.
# Records
| Inscription | Records |
|---|---|
| **Bhitari pillar** (Saidpur, Ghazipur, UP) | Skandagupta's victories over the **Pushyamitras and Hunas** (UPRO 2014) – not Allahabad, Mandsaur or Udayagiri |
| **Junagadh (Girnar)** | Skandagupta's governor **Parnadatta** repaired the Sudarshana lake |
| **Eran boar (Varaha)** | Huna rule over Eran in **Toramana's** first regnal year |
| **Gwalior** | Record of **Mihirakula**'s rule, including Gwalior |
| **Sondani / Mandsaur pillars** | **Yashodharman**'s victory over Mihirakula; Sondani composed by **Vasula** (MPPSC 2024) |
# Yashodharman of Mandasor
- **Defeated Mihirakula:** **Yashodharman**, the **Aulikara** king of Malwa (Mandasor), in **c. 528 CE** (UPPSC 2016 ×2) – not Budhagupta, Shashanka or Prabhakaravardhana; **Narasimhagupta Baladitya** and the Maukharis also fought him.
- **Victory pillars at Sondani:** c. **532 CE**; his rise **weakened** Gupta authority further.
- **Skandagupta:** gold coins with **bow and arrow, Garuda standard and Lakshmi**; last great Gupta; governors became **hereditary feudatories**.

=== Gupta Chronology, Kumaragupta I and Nalanda
# Rulers in order
| Order | Ruler | Period / note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sri Gupta** | Founder, title **Maharaja** (c. 275 CE) |
| 2 | Ghatotkacha | Maharaja |
| 3 | **Chandragupta I** | 319–335; **Maharajadhiraja**; started the **Gupta era (319–20 CE)** |
| 4 | **Samudragupta** | c. 335–375; Prayag prashasti |
| 5 | Ramagupta | Brief, disputed |
| 6 | **Chandragupta II** | c. 380–415 |
| 7 | **Kumaragupta I** | c. 415–455 |
| 8 | **Skandagupta** | c. 455–467; last great Gupta |
| 9 | Purugupta, Budhagupta, **Narasimhagupta (Baladitya)**, Kumaragupta III | Later main line |
| 10 | **Vishnugupta** | c. 540–550; **last recognised king** of the line (SSC CGL 2019) |
- **Order asked (APPSC G1):** **Samudragupta → Chandragupta II → Kumaragupta I → Skandagupta** = (iii), (i), (iv), (ii).
- **"Gupta dynasty ruled" (CGPSC 2003):** **319–500 AD** is the keyed span (from Chandragupta I's era; the line actually lasted to c. 550).
- **Distractors:** Bimbisara (Haryanka) and Ashoka (Maurya) were not Guptas; **Bindusara** extended Mauryan power into the Deccan.
# Kumaragupta I (c. 415–455)
- **Titles:** **Shakraditya, Mahendraditya**; devotee of **Kartikeya** – gold coins with the king **feeding a peacock** and Kartikeya.
- **Nalanda:** founded by **Kumaragupta I** (c. 450 CE) (BPSC 2015; State PSC) – not Chandragupta Vikramaditya, Dharmapala (Pala patron of Vikramashila) or Pushyagupta.
  - Site identified by **Alexander Cunningham** (1861); destroyed by **Bakhtiyar Khalji** (end of 12th c.).
- **Mandsaur (Dashpur) prashasti:** records the achievements of **Kumaragupta I** and his governor **Bandhuvarman**; composed by **Vatsabhatti** (UPRO Mains 2021).
- **Other records:** **Bilsad** (Gupta year 96 = 415 CE, his earliest), **Damodarpur** copper plates (guilds as banks), Karamdanda.
- **Threats late in his reign:** the **Pushyamitras** and the first Huna attack.

=== Gupta Decline and the Successor States
# Causes of decline
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Huna invasions** | Drained treasury and army; Hunas held Punjab, Rajasthan, Malwa by c. 485–500 |
| **Weak successors** | After Skandagupta |
| **Feudal set-up** | Land grants and hereditary governors created feudatories who broke away (Yashodharman, Maitrakas) |
| **Economic** | Land grants cut revenue – no professional army; loss of western India cut trade income; silk weavers left Gujarat for Malwa |
| **Buddhism of later Guptas** | Listed as a cause in some books (pacifist policy) |
- **NOT a cause:** the **Arab invasion** (UKPCS 2012) – the Arabs came much later (Sindh 712 CE).
- **Signs of collapse:** later gold coins had **less gold, more alloy**; coins slowly replaced by **payments in land**; no Gupta coins or inscriptions in **western Malwa and Saurashtra** after Skandagupta.
# Successor states before Harsha
| Power | Seat | Exam fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Later Guptas** (Guptas of Magadha) | Magadha | Not the imperial line |
| **Maukharis** | **Kannauj** | Ishanavarman (Haraha), Ishvaravarman (Jaunpur), Sarvavarman |
| **Maitrakas** | **Valabhi** (Gujarat) | Rulers **Dharapatta, Guhasena** (APPSC 2023) |
| **Pushyabhutis** | **Thanesar** (Haryana), later Kannauj | Harsha's family |
| **Aulikaras** | Mandsaur | Yashodharman |
- **UPSC 2021:** between the Guptas and Harsha the north was held by the **Guptas of Magadha, Pushyabhutis of Thanesar, Maukharis of Kannauj and Maitrakas of Valabhi** (1, 3, 4, 6) – **not** the **Paramaras of Malwa** (9th–14th c.) or the **Yadavas of Devagiri** (12th–14th c.).
