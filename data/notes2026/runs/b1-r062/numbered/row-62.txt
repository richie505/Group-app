## SUB 0 | Chandragupta II Vikramaditya – Saka Conquest, Silver Coins, Titles and Fa-hien
[0:0] # Accession – the Ramagupta episode
[0:1] - **Killed his elder brother:** **Chandragupta II** took the throne after killing his elder brother **Ramagupta** and married his widow **Dhruvadevi (Dhruvaswamini)** – story in **Vishakhadatta's Devichandraguptam** (State PSC).
[0:2]   - Ramagupta, defeated by the Sakas, had agreed to surrender Dhruvadevi to the Saka king; Chandragupta II, in disguise, killed the Saka king, then Ramagupta.
[0:3] - **Order:** Samudragupta → (Ramagupta, brief and disputed) → Chandragupta II, c. **380–415 CE** (NextGen 380–413; PMF 380–412).
[0:4] # Saka conquest and Ujjain
[0:5] - **Saka-conqueror (Sakari):** Chandragupta II defeated **Rudrasimha III**, the last Western Kshatrapa (Saka) ruler, and annexed **western Malwa and Gujarat** with the western sea ports (UP UDA/LDA 2010).
[0:6] - **Second capital:** **Ujjain (Ujjayini)** (RPSC 2008) – Malwa and Saurashtra are regions, Mehrauli the site of his iron pillar.
[0:7] - **Evidence:** the **Udayagiri cave** inscription of his minister **Virasena**, and above all his **silver coins**.
[0:8] # Silver coins – the strongest evidence
[0:9] - **First Gupta to issue silver coins:** **Chandragupta II**, after the Saka conquest, on the model of the Western Kshatrapa silver drachms (UP UDA/LDA Mains 2010; MPPSC 2023). Earlier Guptas issued gold.
[0:10]   - ⚠ NextGen Indian History 03 p.2 says "Chandragupta I was the first Gupta king to mint silver coins" – an error: PMF, the app notes and numismatic sources credit Chandragupta II.
[0:11] - **UPPCS 2022 statements:** the silver coins are the **strongest evidence of the Saka victory**, and they weigh about **30–33 grains** like the Saka drachms → **both** statements correct.
[0:12] - **Coin name:** silver = **rupaka**; gold = **dinara**. Lion-slayer coins gave the title **Simhavikrama** (his father = tiger-slayer coins).
[0:13] # Titles and names
[0:14] TABLE head: Title / name | Note
  [0:14.0] Vikramaditya | Linked with the Saka victory (SSC 2010); also used by Skandagupta
  [0:14.1] Sakari | "Destroyer of the Sakas"
  [0:14.2] Simhavikrama | Lion-slayer coins
  [0:14.3] Devagupta, Devaraja, Devashri | Devagupta in Sanchi and Vakataka records; Devaraja at Sanchi (UPPCS Mains 2007)
[0:15] # Marriage alliances
[0:16] TABLE head: Alliance | Detail
  [0:16.0] Vakataka | Daughter Prabhavati Gupta married Rudrasena II; she ruled the Vakataka kingdom as regent for her sons (RAS 2023; JPSC 2023) – indirect Gupta control of the Deccan
  [0:16.1] Naga | Married the Naga princess Kuberanaga (Prabhavati's mother)
  [0:16.2] Dhruvadevi | Married after killing Ramagupta
[0:17] # Fa-hien and the court
[0:18] - **Fa-hien (Faxian):** came **c. 399–412 CE**, in the reign of **Chandragupta II** (UPSC 2025; State PSC) – not Samudragupta, Kumaragupta I or Skandagupta; he **did not name the king** – his interest was Buddhism. First of the three Chinese pilgrims (later **Xuan Zang**, **I-Qing**, 7th c.).
[0:19] - **Navaratnas** (tradition): **Kalidasa, Amarasimha, Varahamihira**, Dhanvantari, Vararuchi, Shanku, Ghatakarpara, Kshapanaka, Vetalabhatta – Fa-hien was **not** one of them.
[0:20] - **Not his minister:** **Harishena** was Samudragupta's court poet (Prayag prashasti), not Chandragupta II's minister.

## SUB 1 | The Hunas – Skandagupta, Toramana, Mihirakula and Yashodharman
[1:0] # Who the Hunas were
[1:1] - **Hunas = Hephthalites / White Huns** of Central Asia; strong in the **Oxus** valley, they entered India by taking **Gandhara** (mid-5th c.). **Toramana** was a **Huna** (IAS 1995) – not a Saka, Scythian or Yuezhi.
[1:2] # Waves of invasion
[1:3] TABLE head: Wave / leader | Gupta reign | Outcome
  [1:3.0] First attack | End of Kumaragupta I's reign | Checked by prince Skandagupta
  [1:3.1] Main invasion | Skandagupta (c. 455–467) | Hunas repulsed twice; title Vikramaditya
  [1:3.2] Toramana (second invasion) | Weak successors, late 5th c. | Conquered up to Eran; Eran boar inscription of his 1st regnal year
  [1:3.3] Mihirakula (Toramana's son, c. 515) | Narasimhagupta | Tyrant, persecuted Buddhists; defeated by Yashodharman
[1:4] - **Gupta ruler who defeated the Hunas:** **Skandagupta** (UPPSC Lower 2015; UPPCS Mains 2004; State PSC) – not Samudragupta, Chandragupta II, Ramagupta or Bhanugupta.
[1:5] - **Reign of the Huna invasion:** **Skandagupta** (UPPCS Mains 2006); the **Guptas** were the dynasty most troubled by the Hunas (UPPCS 1993).
[1:6] - **"First Huna invasion faced by" (APSC 2017):** the usual key is **Skandagupta** – PMF: Skandagupta "was the one who really faced the Hunas invasion"; NextGen adds that the first attack came when Kumaragupta I was very old and was repulsed by prince Skandagupta.
[1:7] # Records
[1:8] TABLE head: Inscription | Records
  [1:8.0] Bhitari pillar (Saidpur, Ghazipur, UP) | Skandagupta's victories over the Pushyamitras and Hunas (UPRO 2014) – not Allahabad, Mandsaur or Udayagiri
  [1:8.1] Junagadh (Girnar) | Skandagupta's governor Parnadatta repaired the Sudarshana lake
  [1:8.2] Eran boar (Varaha) | Huna rule over Eran in Toramana's first regnal year
  [1:8.3] Gwalior | Record of Mihirakula's rule, including Gwalior
  [1:8.4] Sondani / Mandsaur pillars | Yashodharman's victory over Mihirakula; Sondani composed by Vasula (MPPSC 2024)
[1:9] # Yashodharman of Mandasor
[1:10] - **Defeated Mihirakula:** **Yashodharman**, the **Aulikara** king of Malwa (Mandasor), in **c. 528 CE** (UPPSC 2016 ×2) – not Budhagupta, Shashanka or Prabhakaravardhana; **Narasimhagupta Baladitya** and the Maukharis also fought him.
[1:11] - **Victory pillars at Sondani:** c. **532 CE**; his rise **weakened** Gupta authority further.
[1:12] - **Skandagupta:** gold coins with **bow and arrow, Garuda standard and Lakshmi**; last great Gupta; governors became **hereditary feudatories**.

[2:2] - **Order asked (APPSC G1):** **Samudragupta → Chandragupta II → Kumaragupta I → Skandagupta** = (iii), (i), (iv), (ii).
[2:3] - **"Gupta dynasty ruled" (CGPSC 2003):** **319–500 AD** is the keyed span (from Chandragupta I's era; the line actually lasted to c. 550).
[2:4] - **Distractors:** Bimbisara (Haryanka) and Ashoka (Maurya) were not Guptas; **Bindusara** extended Mauryan power into the Deccan.
[2:5] # Kumaragupta I (c. 415–455)
[2:6] - **Titles:** **Shakraditya, Mahendraditya**; devotee of **Kartikeya** – gold coins with the king **feeding a peacock** and Kartikeya.
[2:7] - **Nalanda:** founded by **Kumaragupta I** (c. 450 CE) (BPSC 2015; State PSC) – not Chandragupta Vikramaditya, Dharmapala (Pala patron of Vikramashila) or Pushyagupta.
[2:8]   - Site identified by **Alexander Cunningham** (1861); destroyed by **Bakhtiyar Khalji** (end of 12th c.).
[2:9] - **Mandsaur (Dashpur) prashasti:** records the achievements of **Kumaragupta I** and his governor **Bandhuvarman**; composed by **Vatsabhatti** (UPRO Mains 2021).
[2:10] - **Other records:** **Bilsad** (Gupta year 96 = 415 CE, his earliest), **Damodarpur** copper plates (guilds as banks), Karamdanda.
[2:11] - **Threats late in his reign:** the **Pushyamitras** and the first Huna attack.

## SUB 3 | Gupta Decline and the Successor States
[3:0] # Causes of decline
[3:1] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [3:1.0] Huna invasions | Drained treasury and army; Hunas held Punjab, Rajasthan, Malwa by c. 485–500
  [3:1.1] Weak successors | After Skandagupta
  [3:1.2] Feudal set-up | Land grants and hereditary governors created feudatories who broke away (Yashodharman, Maitrakas)
  [3:1.3] Economic | Land grants cut revenue – no professional army; loss of western India cut trade income; silk weavers left Gujarat for Malwa
  [3:1.4] Buddhism of later Guptas | Listed as a cause in some books (pacifist policy)
[3:2] - **NOT a cause:** the **Arab invasion** (UKPCS 2012) – the Arabs came much later (Sindh 712 CE).
[3:3] - **Signs of collapse:** later gold coins had **less gold, more alloy**; coins slowly replaced by **payments in land**; no Gupta coins or inscriptions in **western Malwa and Saurashtra** after Skandagupta.
[3:4] # Successor states before Harsha

  [3:5.2] Maitrakas | Valabhi (Gujarat) | Rulers Dharapatta, Guhasena (APPSC 2023)
  [3:5.3] Pushyabhutis | Thanesar (Haryana), later Kannauj | Harsha's family
  [3:5.4] Aulikaras | Mandsaur | Yashodharman
[3:6] - **UPSC 2021:** between the Guptas and Harsha the north was held by the **Guptas of Magadha, Pushyabhutis of Thanesar, Maukharis of Kannauj and Maitrakas of Valabhi** (1, 3, 4, 6) – **not** the **Paramaras of Malwa** (9th–14th c.) or the **Yadavas of Devagiri** (12th–14th c.).
