## SUB 1 | Trade Decline and Urban Decay
[1:0] # Causes of trade decline
[1:1] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [1:1.0] Fall of the West Roman Empire (476 CE) | The standard cause of the decline of North Indian trade, 7th–10th c. (APPSC G2 2017) – not the collapse of China or SE Asia or loss of artisan skills
  [1:1.1] Eastern Roman (Byzantine) trade | Silk and spices till c. 550 CE; fell when the Romans learnt to rear silkworms
  [1:1.2] Hunas | Blocked the north-western route; loss of western India cut the inflow of gold and silver
  [1:1.3] Sassanid decline | After the rise of Islam – hurt overland trade
[1:2] - **New direction:** as the Roman market was lost, Indian merchants relied more on **South-East Asian trade** (UPSC 1999) – it did not revive the northern economy.
[1:3] - **Silk weavers:** a guild left Lata (Gujarat) for **Mandsaur** (mid-5th c.) and took up other professions – falling silk demand.
[1:4] # Urban decay
[1:5] - **Gradual decline of towns:** a feature of the **Gupta period** (State PSC; BPSC 1995) – not the Pratihara, Rashtrakuta or Satavahana eras; **Pataliputra** had become a village when Hiuen-Tsang came.
[1:6] - **Post-Gupta decline of trade (AP recruitment):** **TRUE** – towns such as **Sanghol, Hastinapur, Mathura, Shravasti, Kaushambi** declined, and there is a **paucity of coins**; **FALSE** – that it "became more pronounced only by the middle of the 9th century" (it was marked from the 6th–7th c.).
[1:7] - **R.S. Sharma's view:** shrinking towns, self-reliant villages, few gold coins – the basis of the **urban decay and feudalism** thesis; critics note coins still appear in texts and inscriptions.
[1:8] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval 03 p.68, p.82; NextGen Indian History APPSC 03 p.6-7; app notes 1:64 (sub 8)
## SUB 2 | Varna, Jati, Women and Post-Gupta Society
[2:0] # Caste
[2:1] - **Proliferation of jatis:** castes multiplied into sub-castes by absorbing **foreigners** (Hunas → Rajput clans) and **tribes** (through land grants).
[2:2] - **Post-Gupta caste (AP recruitment):** the **Abhira** tribe split into Abhira Brahmanas, Kshatriyas and Shudras; **peasants** were placed in the **Shudra** varna; **Kayasthas** worked as record-keepers and scribes – all true; but Brahmanas **were** divided into many sub-castes, so (b) is false → **a, c, d**.
[2:3] - **Kayastha = scribes** (APPSC G2 2012): a new caste of scribes and accountants from the land-grant bureaucracy; **prathama kayastha** sat on town boards.
[2:4] - **Jajmani system:** hereditary service castes serving landowning patrons (**jajmans**) for a share of produce – became prevalent in the **post-Gupta** period (APPSC G1 2010).
[2:5] - **Medhatithi:** the post-Gupta commentator on Manu who declared that **Shudras were not slaves by nature** (MPPSC 2010) – not Vijnaneshvara, Narada or Jimutavahana.
[2:6] - **Shudras and untouchables:** Shudras' position improved (seen as agriculturists by the 7th c.; could hear the epics), but **untouchability** grew – **chandalas** lived outside villages; **Narada Smriti** lists **15 types of slaves**.
[2:7] # Women
[2:8] - **Marriage:** pre-puberty marriage advised; **svayamvara** gave way to marriages arranged by parents; widows to stay celibate.
[2:9] - **Property:** no general property rights, but control over **stridhana** (marriage gifts); Katyayana let women sell their stridhana.
[2:10] - **Earliest sati record:** **Eran, 510 CE** – wife of **Goparaja**, a general/friend of Bhanugupta; sati was still rare.
[2:11] - **Land:** Amarasimha's **Amarakosha** lists **12 types of land** (urvara = fertile, usara = barren, maru = desert).

## SUB 3 | Guilds and the Dashpur (Mandsaur) Inscription
[3:0] # Guilds (shrenis)
[3:1] - **Autonomous bodies** of artisans and merchants with their own rules, seals and **judicial powers**; some maintained their **own armed bodies**; they took deposits and lent money (**banks**).
[3:2] - **NOT a feature (APPSC G1 2023):** "**non-autonomous bodies**" – the guilds were autonomous.
[3:3] - **Head:** **Sreshthi / Setti**; caravan trader = **Sarthavaha**.
[3:4] # Dashpur (Mandsaur) inscription
[3:5] - **Silk weavers' guild:** the **Dashpur inscription** of Kumaragupta I and his governor **Bandhuvarman** records the guild of **silk weavers** (from Lata) who built a **Sun temple** (436–37 CE) and repaired it (473 CE) (MPPSC 2019) – not the Prayag, Eran or Hathigumpha records.
[3:6] - **"First advertisement":** its praise of silk cloth makes it the **oldest example of advertisement** in India / "the world's first advertisement" (MPPSC 2021).
[3:7] - **Composer:** **Vatsabhatti** – NOT **Bhavabhuti** (MPPSC 2024); it calls Dashpur **Pashchimapur**.

  [3:8.2] Damodarpur copper plates (Kumaragupta I) | Guilds as banks; town board

## SUB 4 | Fa-hien's Account
[4:0] - **Mission:** to **visit Buddhist institutions and collect copies of Buddhist manuscripts** (CDS 2009) – not to study administration, women or peasants; he never named the king.
[4:1] - **Hospital:** a **free hospital at Pataliputra**, endowed by citizens – poor patients got doctors, food and medicine (CGPSC 2019, 2020); **Tamralipti** was only his port of departure.
[4:2] - **Dates and route:** **c. 399–412 CE** (Chandragupta II); came overland via Central Asia and Gandhara, returned by sea via Tamralipti and **Sri Lanka**; account = **Fo-kwo-ki** ("Record of Buddhist Kingdoms").
[4:3] - **What he saw:** mild administration, no spy system, safe roads, capital punishment rare; **20 viharas at Mathura**; decay at Kapilavastu, Gaya, Kushinagar; **chandalas** struck a piece of wood to announce their approach; **cowries** for daily exchange.
