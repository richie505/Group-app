=== NEW NOTES row 6
## SUB 0 | Hallmarks of the IVC – Town Planning, Burnt Bricks, Bronze Age
[0:0] # What the exams ask
[0:1] - **Hallmark / specialisation:** **town planning** (urban centres, grid, drains) is the single most distinctive feature of the IVC (APPSC G2 2011) – not "all of these".
[0:2] - **Most significant feature:** **burnt-brick buildings** – large-scale use of burnt bricks in almost all constructions (BPSC 2018, JPSC 2023). Egypt used mainly dried bricks; no "first true arches", no buildings of worship.
[0:3] - **Known for (UK UDA 2003):** town planning, Mohenjodaro and Harappa, agriculture and craft industry (bead, shell, metal factories) → "all of these".
[0:4] - **No temples or palaces** clearly found, unlike Mesopotamia and Egypt.
[0:5] - **Not known:** the **true arch** (only corbelled), and **iron** – IVC is a **Bronze Age** culture; iron came c. 1000 BC (Later Vedic). Pillars (Mohenjodaro assembly hall, 20 pillars), wells, drains and bows-and-arrows were all known.
[0:6] # IVC vs Vedic / Aryan
[0:7] TABLE head: Point | IVC (Harappan) | Vedic
  [0:7.0] Life | Urban, planned cities | Rural, pastoral
  [0:7.1] Script | Pictographic (undeciphered) | No script
  [0:7.2] Metal | Copper, bronze; no iron | Iron in Later Vedic age
  [0:7.3] Pottery | Red ware painted in black | Painted Grey Ware
[0:8] - **Why IVC is placed before the Aryans (UPPCS 1990):** **pottery** – the stratified pottery sequence (from Mehrgarh to the black-on-red Harappan ware) lies below the Painted Grey Ware of the Vedic people.
[0:9] - **IVC different from Vedic (UPPCS 2004):** city amenities + pictographic script + no iron → "all of the above".
[0:10] > Trap: "the IVC knew iron" is always the false statement.
[0:11] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.83-86, p.101, p.107; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.5, p.7; old app notes row 6
## SUB 1 | Citadel and Lower Town – Dholavira's Three Parts
[1:0] TABLE head: Site | Plan
  [1:0.0] Mohenjodaro, Harappa | Citadel on the west (smaller, higher, on a mud-brick platform, elite and public buildings) + lower town on the east (larger, commoners)
  [1:0.1] Kalibangan | Citadel + lower town, both fortified
  [1:0.2] Dholavira | Only city in three parts: citadel, middle town, lower town
  [1:0.3] Chanhudaro | No citadel (only Harappan city without one)
  [1:0.4] Lothal, Surkotada | Citadel not separately walled; inside one fortified enclosure
  [1:0.5] Sutkagendor | Citadel + lower city
[1:1] - **Great Bath, granary, college of priests, assembly hall** stood on the **citadel** of Mohenjodaro → both "citadel on the west" and "Great Bath in the citadel" are correct (AP Sachivalayam).
[1:2] - Pataliputra is NOT an IVC site; Kalibangan, Ropar and Lothal are.

## SUB 2 | Bricks, Streets and Houses
[2:0] # Bricks
[2:1] - **Burnt bricks** common; **mud bricks** for fillings and platforms and at **Kalibangan**. Both baked and raw bricks used.
[2:2] - Standard ratio **1:2:4** (thickness : width : length) = 4:2:1.
[2:3] - **Same ratio, different sizes:** house bricks **7.5×15×30 cm** (≈7×14×28), city-wall bricks **10×20×40 cm** → "the same size of brick for all constructions" is **false**.
[2:4] - **Dholavira**: **sandstone and limestone** blocks, not baked brick.
[2:5] - **Ornamental bricks** = Kalibangan; **curved bricks** = Chanhudaro.
[2:6] # Streets
[2:7] - **Grid layout**: streets ran north-south and east-west, crossing at **right angles**, splitting the city into blocks → **broad and straight** (not narrow, zig-zag).
[2:8] - Main streets about **10 m (30 feet)** wide (Rocket: main road of Mohenjodaro ≈9.15 m). **Banawali** = only city with **radial streets**.
[2:9] # Houses
[2:10] - Rooms round a **central courtyard**; brick-paved **bathrooms** and toilets; no ground-level windows (privacy).
[2:11] - Doors and windows opened on **side lanes**, not main streets (Lothal is the exception – entrances on the main street).
[2:12] - **Storeys:** most houses single-storeyed but **staircases** show many had an upper storey. CGPSC 2022 key treats "mostly all houses single storey" as untrue along with "same size of bricks" → **(i) and (ii)**; "main roads ~10 m" is true.
[2:13] - **Arch:** Harappans used only the corbelled arch, never the true arch (MPPSC 2009).
[2:14] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.85-86; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.5; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.19; old app notes row 6
## SUB 3 | Wells, Drainage and Water Management
[3:0] TABLE head: Site | Feature
  [3:0.0] Mohenjodaro | About 700 brick wells, many inside houses → most wells (UPPCS 2004)
  [3:0.1] Dholavira | Water management: 16 reservoirs, dams, embankments, channels; some rock-cut (UPSC 2021, CDS 2012, UPPCS 2010)
  [3:0.2] Kalibangan | Many houses had their own wells; wooden drains
  [3:0.3] Banawali | No systematic drainage
  [3:0.4] Allahdino (Sindh) | Well irrigation
[3:1] - **Drainage** = the most advanced IVC feature: house drains → sump/cesspit → **covered** street drains (bricks or stone slabs) with **manholes**; streets sloped for flow. Lothal: mud-brick houses but burnt-brick drains.
[3:2] - **Dholavira reservoirs:** water from the **Manhar and Mansar** rivulets; huge rock-cut reservoir about **73–79 m long, 7 m deep**; stepwells cut in sandstone = Harappan **rock-cut architecture** (UPPSC 2006).
[3:3] > Trap: Dholavira = reservoirs (water harvesting); Mohenjodaro = wells.
[3:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.85-86, p.102-103; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.14; Rocket Indian History Part 1 p.21-22; old app notes row 6
## SUB 4 | The Great Bath
[4:0] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [4:0.0] Site | Mohenjodaro, on the citadel mound, Sindh – not Harappa, Lothal, Ropar or Kalibangan
  [4:0.1] Size | 11.88 × 7.01 m, 2.43 m deep; two flights of steps on north and south
  [4:0.2] Material | Burnt bricks set in gypsum mortar; bitumen (natural tar) waterproofing
  [4:0.3] Water | From a large well in a side room; drain in one corner
  [4:0.4] Purpose | Ritual bath (most view); public bath ruled out as houses had bathrooms

## SUB 6 | Dholavira – Plan, Stadium and Signboard
[6:0] - **Kachchh** (Khadir island), Gujarat; rectangular plan; one of the **five largest** cities (with Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Ganweriwala, Rakhigarhi).
[6:1] - **Stadium** (~283 × 47 m) between citadel and middle town; special cemetery structures; **ten-sign signboard** near the **North Gate** = largest Harappan signs.
[6:2] - Settled in seven stages, **c. 3000–1500 BCE** (APPSC 2018 marked 3000–1800 BCE).
[6:3] - **UNESCO 2021**: India's **40th** WHS; **first IVC site in India** on the list.
[6:4] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.102; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.14, p.16; old app notes row 6
