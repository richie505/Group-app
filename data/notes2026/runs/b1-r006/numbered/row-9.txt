## SUB 0 | Seals and Sealings – Uses and Value As A Source
[0:0] - The seal is the **most distinctive artefact** of the IVC (APPSC FRO; APPSC GS 2024) – not megaliths, DNA or royal palaces.
[0:1] - With the script unread, **seals** are the **main source** of knowledge about the Harappan people (RAS 1996); broadly, the culture is known only from **archaeological excavations** (UPPSC 1993) – no inscriptions read, no religious books.
[0:2] - A **sealing** is the clay impression of a seal pressed on the knot of a package; an intact sealing proved the goods were untouched and named the sender.
[0:3] - **Uses:** facilitating **long-distance communication** and trade (APPSC G1 → **seals and sealings**), authentication, **ownership**, amulets – **not coins**.
[0:4] - **Uniform seals and weights** = main proof of **commercial and economic development** and regulated trade (SSC CPO 2002).
[0:5] ~~ Sources: PMF Ancient & Medieval Part 1 p.96; NextGen Indian History - APPSC Exams Part 1 p.6; old app notes row 9

## SUB 2 | Seals – Material and Shape
[2:0] - Mostly **steatite (soapstone)** – easy to carve; also agate, chert, copper, faience, terracotta, ivory.
[2:1] - Standard seal: **square** plaque about 2 × 2 inches of steatite.
[2:2] TABLE head: Shape | Feature / site
  [2:2.0] Square | Animal motif + inscription
  [2:2.1] Rectangular | Inscription only
  [2:2.2] Mesopotamian-type cylinder | Rare; Mohenjodaro, Kalibangan
  [2:2.3] Button seal, Persian Gulf-type | Lothal
[2:3] > Cylinder seals are Mesopotamian-type and rare; the normal Harappan seal is square steatite.

## SUB 3 | Weights and Measures
[3:0] - Weights mostly **chert** (also limestone, steatite), **cubical** (not spherical), no markings.
[3:1] TABLE head: Denomination | System
  [3:1.0] Lower | Binary: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64
  [3:1.1] Higher | Decimal multiples: 160, 320, 640, 1600, 3200
[3:2] - Unit weight about **13.63 g**; "16" ratio (16 annas = 1 rupee tradition).
[3:3] - Same weights over about **8,00,000 sq km** → **central regulation** of trade; Dilmun weights followed this standard.
[3:4] TABLE head: Scale | Site | Unit
  [3:4.0] Ivory scale | Lothal (APPSC G2 2011) | Smallest division ~1.70 mm
  [3:4.1] Shell scale | Mohenjodaro | ~6.7 mm
  [3:4.2] Copper scale | Harappa | –
[3:5] - Foot ≈ 37.6 cm; cubit 51.8–53.6 cm. Ivory weight balance at Mohenjodaro and Lothal.
[3:6] > Ivory = Lothal; shell = Mohenjodaro; copper = Harappa.

## SUB 4 | Harappan Script – Features
[4:0] TABLE head: Feature | Fact
  [4:0.0] Nature | Mostly pictographic (not alphabetic); fish = commonest sign
  [4:0.1] Direction | Right to left; some boustrophedon (Kalibangan)
  [4:0.2] Number of signs | About 375–400 (estimates 62 to 676)
  [4:0.3] Average / longest inscription | About 5 / 26 signs
  [4:0.4] Corpus | About 5,000 inscribed objects (≈3,500 seals)
  [4:0.5] Found on | Seals, tablets, pottery, copper tools, bone rods, jewellery, a signboard
  [4:0.6] Status | Undeciphered
[4:1] - First noticed 1853; seals with unknown symbols reported by **J. Fleet, 1912**.
[4:2] - Unread because **no bilingual text** (like the **Rosetta Stone**) has been found and the language is unknown.
[4:3] - **Dholavira signboard:** **ten large signs** near the **North Gate** – largest Harappan letters; Dholavira dug by **R.S. Bisht, 1990**.
