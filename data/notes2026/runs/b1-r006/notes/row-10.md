=== Burials – Types and Sites
- **Common burial system (HPPSC 2012):** **extended earth burial with the head generally to the north**, and **grave goods** (pots, ornaments, copper mirrors) buried with the dead → "both a and b". Sitting-posture burial is not Harappan.
- **Three forms:** complete (extended) burials, fractional burials, post-cremation burials.
| Burial practice | Site |
|---|---|
| Extended burials with goods; coffin burial | R-37, Harappa (head generally to the north) |
| Post-cremation / fractional, Late Harappan | Cemetery H, Harappa |
| Joint / double burial | Lothal (UPPSC 2016) |
| Dog buried with human, oval pit burials | Ropar |
| Pot burials; megalith-type grave with a big rock | Surkotada |
| **Large cemetery of human burials** (125 graves, N–S) | **Sanauli (Sinauli)**, Baghpat, UP (UP Lower 2004) |
| Double burials; Mound 7 cemetery; women with jasper, agate beads, shell bangles | Rakhigarhi |
- **Kalibangan – three types:** extended burials in rectangular pits; pot burials; circular pits with grave goods but **no skeleton**.
- Grave goods → belief in an **afterlife**; a copper mirror with cotton thread was found in a Harappa grave. Overall the Harappans did not bury precious things.
- **Rakhigarhi Mound 7:** 56 skeletons incl. a ~4,600-year-old woman.
- **Huddled skeletons** at Mohenjodaro (16 with ornaments) – once read as a "massacre", now as **multiple burials**.
> Double burial = Lothal; dog with human = Ropar; pot burial = Surkotada; R-37 = extended; Cemetery H = Late Harappan.

=== Social Stratification – Evidence
| Evidence | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Town layout | Citadel (west) – elite and public buildings vs lower town (east) – commoners |
| Houses | One to twelve rooms; single-roomed barracks at Harappa |
| Burial goods | Variation in goods and ornament quality = best sign of social differentiation |
- **Uniform bricks** prove organisation and authority, **not status**.
- **No proof** of a rigid caste system; caste became fixed in the **Later Vedic** period. On the whole the Harappans seem relatively **egalitarian**.
- Ranks and polity are rebuilt only from **material remains**; the script gives no rulers' names.

=== Polity and Authority
- Uniform pottery, seals, **weights**, **1:2:4 bricks** and **grid towns** over ~**8,00,000 sq km** show complex decisions made and carried out → a significant authority.
| View | Based on |
|---|---|
| Priestly rule | Citadels, Great Bath, steatite "Priest-King" (not conclusive – no temples) |
| Merchant rule | Trade-driven uniformity, few weapons |
| Several rulers / city-states | Several large cities, no single capital |
| Single state | Most plausible per PMF |
- Political structure **not clear**; it **seems corporate**; municipal-type civic administration (NextGen). Later Vedic = hereditary monarchy, weakening Sabha and Samiti.

=== No Palaces, No Temples
- **No royal palaces or temples** found. "Possessed great palaces and temples" = **WRONG** (UPSC 2013).
- Worship was probably **domestic or open-air** (trees, animals); power **not personalised** in a king.
- Public buildings were mainly **civic**: Great Bath, granary, pillared hall.

=== Dress, Ornaments, Pastimes and Weapons
- **Cotton and wool**; two garments (upper and lower); **Priest-King wears a shawl** with trefoil pattern; **no proof of stitching**.
- **Ornaments:** gold, silver, ivory, copper, lapis lazuli; Allahdino gold and silver necklaces.
- **Lipstick** = **Chanhudaro**; also bronze mirrors, ivory combs, collyrium sticks.
- **Toy factory** = Chanhudaro; terracotta carts, animals, marbles, rattles, whistles (Karanpura); **dice** at Harappa and Mohenjodaro; board games (Lothal chess-like set).
- **Weapons:** maces, axes, spears, bows and arrows of copper, bronze and stone; few and simple → relatively **peaceful**; **no horse-drawn chariots** (UPSC 2013).
