=== NOTES 1:6 | Ivc Town Planning – Grid Layout, Citadel and Lower Town, Drainage, Great Bath, Granaries
## SUB 0 | Town Planning – Hallmarks
[6:0:0] - **Town planning** is the hallmark of the IVC (APPSC G2 2011); **no temples or palaces** clearly found (unlike Mesopotamia, Egypt).
[6:0:1] - **Grid layout**: streets ran **north-south and east-west**, crossing at **right angles**, splitting cities into **blocks** (Mohenjodaro, Harappa).
[6:0:2] - Main streets about **10 m (30 feet)** wide. **Banawali** = only city with **radial streets**.
[6:0:3] - Standard bricks, grid, streets and drains point to a **civic authority** and early engineering skill; absence of palaces does not disprove it.
[6:0:4] - IVC = **Bronze Age**; iron came much later (c. 1000 BC). Pottery is the main reason to place IVC before the Aryans; early pottery at **Mehrgarh**.
## SUB 1 | Citadel and Lower Town
[6:1:0] TABLE head: Site | Plan
  [6:1:0.0] Mohenjodaro, Harappa | Citadel (west), raised and fortified+ lower town (east)
  [6:1:0.1] Kalibangan | Citadel + lower town also fortified
  [6:1:0.2] Dholavira | Only city in three parts: citadel, middle town, lower town (CGPSC 2015)
  [6:1:0.3] Chanhudaro | No citadel
  [6:1:0.4] Lothal | Citadel and lower town in one enclosure
  [6:1:0.5] Sutkagendor | Citadel + lower city
[6:1:1] Pataliputra is NOT an IVC site; Kalibangan, Ropar, Lothal are.
## SUB 2 | Bricks and Materials
[6:2:0] - **Burnt bricks** common; **mud bricks** for fillings and platforms and at **Kalibangan**. Both baked and raw bricks used.
[6:2:1] - Standard ratio **1:2:4** (thickness : width : length) = 4:2:1.
[6:2:2] - Same ratio, **different sizes**: house **7x14x28 cm**; city wall **10x20x40 cm**.
[6:2:3] - **Dholavira**: **sandstone and limestone** blocks, not baked brick.
[6:2:4] - **Ornamental bricks** = Kalibangan; **curved bricks** = Chanhudaro.
## SUB 3 | Houses and Wells
[6:3:0] - Rooms round a **central courtyard**; **staircases** show multi-storey houses.
[6:3:1] - Doors and windows opened on **side lanes**, not main streets.
[6:3:2] - **Mohenjodaro** has the **most wells**, inside houses (UPPSC 2004). Well irrigation at **Allahdino** (Sindh).
## SUB 4 | Drainage
[6:4:0] - **Most advanced** IVC feature; better than Mesopotamia and Egypt.
[6:4:1] - House drains → **covered** street drains (slabs) with **manholes** → **soak pits** / main channels; streets sloped for flow.
[6:4:2] - **Kalibangan** = wooden drains; **Banawali** = no systematic drainage.
## SUB 5 | Great Bath
[6:5:0] TABLE head: Angle | Fact
  [6:5:0.0] Site | Mohenjodaro citadel mound, Sindh (UPPSC 2010)
  [6:5:0.1] Size | ~12 m x 7 m, 2.4 m deep; steps at north and south
  [6:5:0.2] Material | Burnt bricks in gypsum mortar; bitumen waterproofing
  [6:5:0.3] Purpose | Ritual bathing (most view); public tank (others)
## SUB 6 | Granaries and Special Structures
[6:6:0] TABLE head: Structure | Site
  [6:6:0.0] Great Granary, largest building, on citadel | Mohenjodaro (APPSC G2 2008)
  [6:6:0.1] Granary of 12 rooms in two rows of six, near river | Harappa
  [6:6:0.2] Warehouse on raised platform beside dock, seal impressions | Lothal
  [6:6:0.3] Dockyard (tidal; debated) – only port proof | Lothal
  [6:6:0.4] Pillared assembly hall | Mohenjodaro
  [6:6:0.5] Fire altars | Kalibangan (seven), Lothal
[6:6:1] - Granaries = stores of farm surplus (reading debated).
[6:6:2] - Harappa finds: **Cemetery H**, **R-37**, coffin burial, wheat and barley in wooden mortar, dice, unicorn seal.
[6:6:3] Not Harappa: dockyard = Lothal; horse bones = Surkotada; bronze chariot = Daimabad.
## SUB 7 | Dholavira
[6:7:0] - **Kachchh**, Gujarat; rectangular plan; one of **five largest** cities (with Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Ganweriwala, Rakhigarhi).
[6:7:1] - **Reservoirs, dams, embankments**; bunds, check-dams and channels; **16 reservoirs** ≈ 10% of site; some **rock-cut** (UPSC 2021).
[6:7:2] - Huge rock-cut reservoir ~**79 m x 12 m, 7 m deep**.
[6:7:3] - **Stadium** (~283 x 47 m), special cemetery structures; **ten-sign signboard** at **North Gate** = largest inscription.
[6:7:4] - Seven stages, **c. 3000–1500 BCE** (APPSC 2018 marked 3000–1800 BCE).

## SUB 10 | High-probability Mcq Traps
[6:10:0] VERY IMPORTANT
[6:10:1] - 1. Great Granary = **Mohenjodaro**; two rows of six = **Harappa**.
[6:10:2] - 2. **Gypsum** = mortar; **bitumen** = waterproofing.
[6:10:3] - 3. "Same brick size for all" = **false**.
[6:10:4] - 4. "Houses mostly single-storey" = **false**.
[6:10:5] - 5. Wooden drains = Kalibangan; no drainage system = Banawali.
[6:10:6] - 6. Reservoirs = **Dholavira**, not Harappa.
[6:10:7] - 7. Lower town fortified = **Kalibangan**.
[6:10:8] - 8. IVC people did **not** know iron.

=== NOTES 1:9 | Ivc Seals, Weights and Measures, Harappan Script
## SUB 0 | Seals and Sealings – Material and Shape
[9:0:0] - Seals mostly of **steatite (soapstone)** – easy to carve; also **terracotta, copper, ivory**.
[9:0:1] - Generally **square** (or oblong plaque).
[9:0:2] TABLE head: Shape | Feature / site
  [9:0:2.0] Square | Standard seal: animal motif + inscription
  [9:0:2.1] Rectangular | Inscription only
  [9:0:2.2] Mesopotamian-type cylinder | Rare, not common; Mohenjodaro, Kalibangan
  [9:0:2.3] Button seal, Persian Gulf-type seal | Lothal
[9:0:3] Cylinder seals are Mesopotamian-type and rare; the normal Harappan seal is a square steatite seal.
## SUB 1 | Uses and Value As A Source
[9:1:0] - A **sealing** is the clay impression of a seal pressed on a package; it proved the goods were untouched.
[9:1:1] - Uses: **trade regulation, authentication, ownership, identity marking** and **long-distance communication** – not coins.
[9:1:2] - Seals are a **distinctive feature** of the IVC (APPSC GS 2024) and, with the script unread, the main source on the people's beliefs, animals and trade.
[9:1:3] - **Uniform seals and weights** = main proof of **standardisation and regulated trade**.
[9:1:4] APPSC G1 2017: long-distance communication → **seals and sealings**.
## SUB 2 | Famous Seals → Site

  [9:2:0.0] Pashupati seal ("Proto-Shiva": seated yogic figure ringed by animals) | Mohenjodaro
