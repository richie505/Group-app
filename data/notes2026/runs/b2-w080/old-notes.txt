=== NOTES 2:80 | Evolution of Panchayati Raj – Community Development Programme, Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta, G.v.k. Rao and L.m. Singhvi Committees
## SUB 0 | Balwant Rai Mehta Model – Three Tiers and Detailed Recommendations
[80:0:0] TABLE head: Tier | Level | Election (BRM) | Role
  [80:0:0.0] Gram Panchayat | Village | Direct | Village body
  [80:0:0.1] Panchayat Samiti | Block | Indirect | Executive body; block (samithi) = basic unit of planning and development
  [80:0:0.2] Zila Parishad | District | Indirect | Advisory, coordinating and supervisory
[80:0:1] - **Three-tier** system – **first recommended** by BRM; tiers "**organically linked**" through **indirect elections**; samithis filled by **indirect election from the village panchayats** (not from graduates, co-operatives or sarpanches alone).
[80:0:2] - **Panchayat Samiti** is an **administrative (executive) authority** at the **block level** – not advisory/consultative; it is the body for planning (APPSC Panchayat Secretary).
[80:0:3] - **District Collector = chairman of the Zila Parishad**.
[80:0:4] - **All planning and development activities** entrusted to these bodies; **genuine transfer of power and responsibility**; **adequate resources** to be transferred; further devolution in future.
[80:0:5] - BRM did **NOT** recommend: a **two-tier** system, **direct election to the Zila Parishad**, the ZP as **executive**, a **village panchayat of indirectly elected members**, a samiti as **advisory**, or PRI power to levy **income tax / property tax** at all levels.
[80:0:6] - **Report details (APPSC Social Welfare Mains):** each **municipality lying as an enclave in a samithi elects one member** to the samithi; a samithi **can be superseded in public interest**; the **village panchayat collects land revenue as an agency on payment of commission**.
[80:0:7] - **Not a tier:** **Nyaya Panchayat**; **not a PRI:** a **Gram Cooperative Society** (Gram Sabha, Gram Panchayat, Nyaya Panchayat are PR bodies).
[80:0:8] - Today the PR set-up (73rd Amendment) is also **three-tier – village, block (intermediate), district**; middle tier optional below 20 lakh population.
[80:0:9] - Under the **73rd Amendment**, Panchayat Samiti members are **directly elected** (UP name: **Kshetra Panchayat**) – unlike BRM's indirect model.
[80:0:10] > Code items: BRM = c and d (planning to PRIs; Collector ZP chairman); not a and b (two tiers; direct ZP elections).
[80:0:11] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.96; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 23 p.14; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 05 p.2; app notes 2:80
[80:0:12] - **Features:** three tiers; plans for **economic development and social justice** (Art. 243G); MPs/MLAs may be represented in panchayats (Art. 243C).
## SUB 1 | Ashok Mehta Committee (1977-78)
[80:1:0] - Appointed **December 1977** by the **Janata government** (PM **Morarji Desai**); chairman **Asoka (Ashok) Mehta**; report **August 1978**, **132 recommendations** – a committee on PRIs (not Abid Hussain, Khusro, Chakravarty).
[80:1:1] - **Finding:** PRIs had declined, yet they had **generated a new leadership** committed to development.
[80:1:2] - **Two-tier:** **Zila Parishad** (district, **executive**, district planning) + **Mandal Panchayat** (group of villages, **15,000-20,000** people) – the **Mandal Panchayat system** was its idea (not BRM, Vengal Rao, Narasimham); **executive powers to Mandal Panchayats**; the Mandal Panchayat to **assist Regional Rural Banks** with infrastructure.
[80:1:3] - **Term 4 years**; **district = first point of decentralisation** below the State (not the village or block).
[80:1:4] - **Official participation of political parties**; **periodic elections at all levels**; elections by the State CEO with the CEC; no supersession – elections **within 6 months**.
[80:1:5] - **Compulsory taxation powers**: besides grants, panchayats must **mobilise their own resources** – no democratic body stays viable on external resources (1978).
[80:1:6] - **Social audit** by a district agency and a legislators' committee; **Minister for PR** in the State cabinet; SC/ST reservation by population; separate **Nyaya Panchayats** under a qualified judge; **constitutional recognition**; **voluntary agencies** to mobilise support.
[80:1:7] - **Fate:** Janata government fell → no central action; **Karnataka, West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh** acted on it ("second generation panchayats"; WB Act 1973, polls June 1978); Sixth Plan (1978-83) gave PRIs new life.
[80:1:8] TABLE head: Point | Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) | Ashok Mehta (1978)
  [80:1:8.0] Tiers | 3 | 2
  [80:1:8.1] Unit | Block (Samiti executive) | District (ZP executive)
  [80:1:8.2] Elections | Direct at village, indirect above | Party participation at all levels
  [80:1:8.3] Collector | ZP chairman | Reduced role
  [80:1:8.4] Constitutional status | Not proposed | Recommended
[80:1:9] > Incorrect-statement items: "village as first point of decentralisation" and "three tiers" are NOT Ashok Mehta.
[80:1:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 23 p.14-15; Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.98-99; Rocket Polity Part 1 p.94
[80:1:11] - **Members** of the Ashok Mehta Committee included **M.G. Ramachandran** and **E.M.S. Namboodiripad** (who wrote a dissent note).
[80:1:12] - **Approach:** the Ashok Mehta Committee adopted the **'concentrated circles' approach** – interaction and interdependence of the levels (APPSC Panchayat Secretary key).
## SUB 2 | Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) – Appointment, Report and Democratic Decentralisation
[80:2:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [80:2:0.0] Appointed | 16 January 1957, by the Government of India (Team for the Study of Community Projects and NES; NDC/Planning-side review)
  [80:2:0.1] Chairman | Balwantrai Gopalji Mehta
  [80:2:0.2] Reviewed | Community Development Programme (1952) and National Extension Service (1953) – not IRDP
  [80:2:0.3] Report | 24 November 1957 (so "constituted / reported" = 1957)
  [80:2:0.4] Accepted | National Development Council, January 1958
[80:2:1] - **Remedy proposed:** the scheme of **"democratic decentralisation"**, which "ultimately came to be known as **Panchayati Raj**" – the committee **first recommended / used** the idea; Ashok Mehta, Rajagopalachari, Kripalani, Pannikar and Kunjru are distractors.
[80:2:2] - **Balwant Rai Mehta = "Architect / Father of Panchayati Raj"**; the present pattern of panchayats "owes its parentage" to this committee; the **Panchayati Raj system was recommended by its 1957 report** – not by the GoI Act 1935, Cripps Mission or Independence Act.
[80:2:3] - **Associated with:** **Panchayati Raj** (it was set up to review Community Development, but its report is identified with Panchayati Raj); also the basis of **decentralised planning** through PRIs (APPSC G1 2002) – not Mahalanobis or Gadgil.
[80:2:4] - **Finding:** public participation is indispensable – Panchayati Raj needs the **full co-operation of the local public**; PR stands for **decentralisation of powers, participation of the people and community development** ("all of these"), i.e. **grass-root democracy**.
[80:2:5] - **NDC (January 1958):** did **not insist on a single rigid pattern** – States free to **evolve their own patterns suited to local conditions**, basic principles identical.
[80:2:6] - Other heads: **Jivraj Mehta, Shriman Narayan, Jagjivan Ram, B.R. Ambedkar, Krishna Iyer** – never chaired a PR committee.
[80:2:7] > Most-asked: chairman, year (1957), CDP + NES reviewed, "democratic decentralisation", NDC flexibility.
[80:2:8] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 23 p.13-14; Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.96-97; Rocket Polity Part 1 p.94; PMF Polity Part 2 p.128

## SUB 5 | Community Development Programme (1952) and National Extension Service (1953)
[80:5:0] # Grow More Food Inquiry Committee (1952)
[80:5:1] - Chairman: **V.T. Krishnamachari** (APPSC G2 2008) – not Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta or G.V.K. Rao; campaign (1943) found too narrow → **integrated village development** and a **nationwide extension organisation** → CDP and NES.
[80:5:2] # CDP
[80:5:3] - Launched **2 October 1952** (Gandhi Jayanti) by **Nehru**, **First Five Year Plan**; **55** pilot community projects; pioneered by **S.K. Dey** (later Union Minister of Community Development).
[80:5:4] - **First major rural development programme / first effort at local governing bodies** in independent India; "mother of social development programmes"; launched **before** BRM (1957).
[80:5:5] - **Aim:** **development of villages through public participation**; a step towards **Article 40**; raising the standard of living under **Article 47**.
[80:5:6] - **Components:** agriculture, irrigation, communications, health, housing, education and **social education** (adult literacy, community centres) – social education was part of the **CDP**.
[80:5:7] - **Statements (APPSC JA):** launched 1952 for comprehensive rural development ✔; focuses on infrastructure, education, health, agriculture ✔; "top-down, communities minimal" ✘ (its idea was people's participation).
[80:5:8] - **Community development approach:** asset-based (build existing strengths), inclusive of diversity, aims at **sustainable self-sufficiency** and solving **community** issues – not individual prosperity in place of the community.
[80:5:9] # NES and extension
[80:5:10] - **National Extension Service (1953)** – described as the **first programme** of community development (spread the CDP pattern); NES followed CDP.
[80:5:11] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [80:5:11.0] Block | Unit of planning and development (about 100 villages)
  [80:5:11.1] Block Development Officer | Heads the block team of extension officers
  [80:5:11.2] Village Level Worker (Gram Sevak) | Multipurpose extension worker for a group of villages
[80:5:12] - **Extension principle:** the extension worker translates laboratory findings so farm families adopt them **voluntarily** = **principle of applied science and democratic approach**.
[80:5:13] - **Failure:** seen as **government-imposed**, inadequate people's participation → BRM review.
[80:5:14] TABLE head: Year | Programme
  [80:5:14.0] 1952 | CDP
  [80:5:14.1] 1953 | NES
  [80:5:14.2] 1975 | ICDS
  [80:5:14.3] 1977 | Food for Work
  [80:5:14.4] 2 Oct 1978 | National Adult Education Programme (15-35)
  [80:5:14.5] 1978-79; nationwide 2 Oct 1980 | IRDP
[80:5:15] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 05 p.2; Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.96; Rocket Polity Part 1 p.5; app notes 2:80
[80:5:16] - **Why the CDP failed:** government-imposed and bureaucratic, little people's participation, benefits cornered by better-off farmers, lack of coordination – a **'scientific approach in planning' was NOT a reason**.

## SUB 7 | Road to Constitutional Status (1989-1993)
[80:7:0] TABLE head: Step | Fact
  [80:7:0.0] 64th Amendment Bill | Rajiv Gandhi government, Lok Sabha, July 1989; passed LS August 1989; based on Singhvi, Thungon, Gadgil
  [80:7:0.1] Fate | Defeated in the Rajya Sabha (October 1989) – special majority not secured
  [80:7:0.2] 65th Amendment Bill | Nagarpalika (municipalities) bill; also defeated in the Rajya Sabha (1989)
  [80:7:0.3] National Front (V.P. Singh) bill | September 1990 – lapsed when the government fell / Lok Sabha dissolved
  [80:7:0.4] Narasimha Rao bills | Introduced September 1991 → Joint Parliamentary Committee → passed December 1992
  [80:7:0.5] 73rd Amendment Act, 1992 | Panchayati Raj constitutionalised; in force 24 April 1993; Part IX (Art. 243-243O); Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects)
[80:7:1] - Opposition objection: **Local Government is a State List** subject (entry 5) – the Bill would **centralise power**.
[80:7:2] - Effect: PRIs made **justiciable**; constitutional obligation on States; **uniform** three-tier structure.
[80:7:3] - Key Articles: **243B(2)** middle tier optional below **20 lakh**; **243D(3)** at least **one-third** seats for women; **243K** State Election Commission; regular remuneration to members is **not** a provision.
[80:7:4] - **National Panchayati Raj Day: 24 April**.
[80:7:5] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.102-103; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 23 p.13, 15
[80:7:6] - **Women 50%:** AP and 20 other States + 2 UTs reserve **50%** of PRI seats for women by State law (MoPR, 2026); the constitutional minimum (Art. 243D) is one-third.
[80:7:7] - **National Panchayati Raj Day 2025:** main event at **Madhubani, Bihar** (24 April 2025, PM).

## SUB 2 | The 73rd Amendment and Part IX – Significance and Articles 243 to 243O
[81:2:0] - **73rd Amendment Act, 1992:** deals with **Panchayati Raj** (rural local government); the **74th** deals with Municipalities (Nagar Palika). Bill introduced **September 1991** (Narasimha Rao Government), Act of **1992**, in force **24 April 1993**.
[81:2:1] - **Third tier of the federal system:** added in **1992** (73rd and 74th Acts; in force 1993).
[81:2:2] - Added **Part IX "The Panchayats"** – **Articles 243 to 243O** = **16 Articles** – and the **Eleventh Schedule**.
[81:2:3] - Gave a **practical shape to Article 40** (DPSP: organise village panchayats as **units of self-government**) and **constitutional status** to PRIs.
[81:2:4] - **Justiciable part:** it brought PRIs under the **justiciable part** of the Constitution – States are under a **constitutional obligation** to adopt the system; formation of panchayats and regular elections no longer depend on the State Government's will.
[81:2:5] - **Significance:** a **landmark in the evolution of grassroots democratic institutions**; turns **representative into participatory democracy**; rural local bodies **rejuvenated**.
[81:2:6] - It did **not** create **caste panchayats** or khap panchayats – only the **Gram Panchayat** is a body under the Panchayati Raj system.

## SUB 3 | Gram Sabha – Article 243(b) and 243A
[81:3:0] - **Gram Sabha (Art. 243(b)):** body of **persons registered in the electoral rolls** relating to a village within the Panchayat area – i.e. **all registered voters**, not all residents, all adults, elders or employees.
[81:3:1] - **Minimum age:** **18 years** (voting age); the **age to contest** a Panchayat seat is **21**.
[81:3:2] - **Article 243A:** Gram Sabha exercises such powers at the village level as the **State Legislature provides by law** – in function it is to the village what the **State Legislature** is to the State.
[81:3:3] - **Compulsory:** a Gram Sabha **must be constituted in each village** (or group of villages) – not optional, not abolishable; it is the **electorate** for direct elections (243C).
[81:3:4] - **Direct democracy:** the **Gram Sabha** is the best example – voters decide directly; Gram Panchayat, Nagar Panchayat and Zila Panchayat are representative bodies; caste panchayats, opinion polls and people's courts are not.
[81:3:5] - **Meetings:** at least **twice a year** (NextGen: April 13 and Oct 3; State Acts fix dates); Gram Sabha meetings may be held in each village (by rotation); Gram Panchayat meetings at the GP headquarters.

## SUB 0 | The 74th Amendment and Part IXA – Dates, Names, 73rd vs 74th
[82:0:0] - **74th Amendment Act, 1992:** gave **constitutional status to urban local governments** (municipalities); State governments are under a constitutional obligation to adopt the new system.
[82:0:1] - **Popular name:** the **Nagarpalika Act**; the Bill that became it was the **Municipalities Bill** (Sep 1991, **P.V. Narasimha Rao** government) – Part IXA is titled "**The Municipalities**".
[82:0:2] - **Dates:** passed by both Houses **December 1992**; Presidential assent **April 1993** (20 April 1993); in force **1 June 1993** – so "provision for Panchayats and Municipalities was made in **1993**" (1992 Acts, in force 1993).
[82:0:3] - **Inserted:** **Part IXA** (Articles **243P to 243ZG**) and the **Twelfth Schedule** (18 items, linked to **Art. 243W**).
[82:0:4] - **Part IXA = urban local bodies only**; rural Panchayats are in **Part IX** (73rd Amendment, 1992) – so the 74th is "not concerned with the Panchayats".
[82:0:5] - **DPC (243ZD) and MPC (243ZE)** were both added by the **74th**, not the 73rd.
[82:0:6] - **Earlier attempt:** the **65th Amendment Bill (Nagarpalika Bill), 1989** of the Rajiv Gandhi government passed the Lok Sabha but was **defeated in the Rajya Sabha** (Oct 1989).
[82:0:7] - **Local Government** is in the **State List** (Seventh Schedule).
[82:0:8] - **Existing laws:** inconsistent State municipal laws could continue for at most **one year** (243ZF).

  [82:0:9.0] Concerns | Rural – Panchayats | Urban – Municipalities

  [82:0:9.2] Schedule | Eleventh – 29 items | Twelfth – 18 items
  [82:0:9.3] In force | 24 April 1993 | 1 June 1993

[82:0:10] - **Parts added by amendments:** Part IVA – 42nd (1976); Part IXA – 74th (1992); **Part IXB (Co-operative Societies) – 97th (2011)**.

[82:2:0] - **Composition (243R):** **all members directly elected** by the people from territorial constituencies called **wards**; State law may add persons with **municipal-administration experience (no vote)**, MPs/MLAs of the area, Rajya Sabha/MLC members registered there, chairpersons of committees.

[82:2:2] - **Reservation (243T):** SC and ST seats **in every municipality** in proportion to their population; **not less than one-third of SC/ST seats for SC/ST women**; **not less than one-third of all directly-elected seats for women** (including SC/ST women); backward-class reservation optional.
[82:2:3] - **Duration (243U):** **five years** from the date appointed for its **first meeting**; reasonable opportunity of being heard before dissolution; fresh election before expiry or **within six months** of dissolution.

[82:2:5] - **Being on the electoral roll** of the area is the basic condition for contesting – no party membership or permission needed.

[82:2:7] - **Finance (243Y):** the same **State Finance Commission** (243I) reviews municipal finances every five years.
[82:2:8] - **Mayor's tenure:** fixed by **State law** – Laxmikanth: in a majority of States **one year, renewable**; States with directly elected Mayors (e.g. UP) give **five years**, matching the municipality's term (UPPSC key: 5 years).
