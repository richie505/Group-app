## SUB 0 | The 74th Amendment and Part IXA – Dates, Names, 73rd vs 74th
[0:0] - **74th Amendment Act, 1992:** gave **constitutional status to urban local governments** (municipalities); State governments are under a constitutional obligation to adopt the new system.
[0:1] - **Popular name:** the **Nagarpalika Act**; the Bill that became it was the **Municipalities Bill** (Sep 1991, **P.V. Narasimha Rao** government) – Part IXA is titled "**The Municipalities**".
[0:2] - **Dates:** passed by both Houses **December 1992**; Presidential assent **April 1993** (20 April 1993); in force **1 June 1993** – so "provision for Panchayats and Municipalities was made in **1993**" (1992 Acts, in force 1993).
[0:3] - **Inserted:** **Part IXA** (Articles **243P to 243ZG**) and the **Twelfth Schedule** (18 items, linked to **Art. 243W**).
[0:4] - **Part IXA = urban local bodies only**; rural Panchayats are in **Part IX** (73rd Amendment, 1992) – so the 74th is "not concerned with the Panchayats".
[0:5] - **DPC (243ZD) and MPC (243ZE)** were both added by the **74th**, not the 73rd.
[0:6] - **Earlier attempt:** the **65th Amendment Bill (Nagarpalika Bill), 1989** of the Rajiv Gandhi government passed the Lok Sabha but was **defeated in the Rajya Sabha** (Oct 1989).
[0:7] - **Local Government** is in the **State List** (Seventh Schedule).
[0:8] - **Existing laws:** inconsistent State municipal laws could continue for at most **one year** (243ZF).
[0:9] TABLE head: Basis | 73rd (1992) | 74th (1992)
  [0:9.0] Concerns | Rural – Panchayats | Urban – Municipalities
  [0:9.1] Part / Articles | IX / 243–243O | IXA / 243P–243ZG
  [0:9.2] Schedule | Eleventh – 29 items | Twelfth – 18 items
  [0:9.3] In force | 24 April 1993 | 1 June 1993
  [0:9.4] Bodies | Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad | Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council, Municipal Corporation
[0:10] - **Parts added by amendments:** Part IVA – 42nd (1976); Part IXA – 74th (1992); **Part IXB (Co-operative Societies) – 97th (2011)**.
[0:11] - **Local self-government amendments:** **73rd and 74th**.
[0:12] > UPSC 2005: "Art. 243Q envisages **two** types of municipalities" is false – it envisages **three** (Nagar Panchayat, Council, Corporation).

## SUB 2 | Composition, Reservation, Term, Disqualification and Elections
[2:0] - **Composition (243R):** **all members directly elected** by the people from territorial constituencies called **wards**; State law may add persons with **municipal-administration experience (no vote)**, MPs/MLAs of the area, Rajya Sabha/MLC members registered there, chairpersons of committees.
[2:1] - **Chairperson / Mayor:** manner of election left to the **State Legislature**.
[2:2] - **Reservation (243T):** SC and ST seats **in every municipality** in proportion to their population; **not less than one-third of SC/ST seats for SC/ST women**; **not less than one-third of all directly-elected seats for women** (including SC/ST women); backward-class reservation optional.
[2:3] - **Duration (243U):** **five years** from the date appointed for its **first meeting**; reasonable opportunity of being heard before dissolution; fresh election before expiry or **within six months** of dissolution.
[2:4] - **Disqualification (243V):** as for elections to the **State Legislature** or under a State law; **minimum age 21** – no one is disqualified for being under 25 if he/she is 21.
[2:5] - **Being on the electoral roll** of the area is the basic condition for contesting – no party membership or permission needed.
[2:6] - **Elections (243ZA):** superintendence, direction and control of rolls and all municipal elections vested in the **State Election Commission** (not the State Government).
[2:7] - **Finance (243Y):** the same **State Finance Commission** (243I) reviews municipal finances every five years.
[2:8] - **Mayor's tenure:** fixed by **State law** – Laxmikanth: in a majority of States **one year, renewable**; States with directly elected Mayors (e.g. UP) give **five years**, matching the municipality's term (UPPSC key: 5 years).
[2:9] > APPSC 2011 key gave "25" for the minimum age because 21 was not offered – the constitutional age is **21**.
[2:10] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.40 p.120-122, 126; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 24 p.10-15; NextGen 25 p.1
## SUB 3 | Types of Municipalities (243Q) and Urban Local Bodies
[3:0] TABLE head: Type (243Q) | Area
  [3:0.0] Nagar Panchayat | Transitional area – in transition from rural to urban
  [3:0.1] Municipal Council | Smaller urban area
  [3:0.2] Municipal Corporation | Larger urban area – big cities
[3:1] - **Who specifies the areas:** the **Governor**, by public notification, on population, density, revenue, share of non-agricultural employment, economic importance.
[3:2] - **Industrial township** (243Q proviso): where an **industrial establishment** provides municipal services, the Governor may specify it as an industrial township and no municipality need be constituted.
[3:3] - **Municipal Corporations** are for big cities because urban administration there is very complex (CGPSC 2021 A-R: both true, R explains A); set up by **State Acts** (Acts of Parliament in UTs).
[3:4] - **Corporation's three authorities:** **Council** (headed by the **Mayor**), **Standing Committees**, **Municipal Commissioner** (chief executive, appointed by the State, usually IAS).
[3:5] - **Municipal Council:** council headed by a **president/chairman** (real executive pivot), standing committees, chief executive officer.
[3:6] - **Eight types of ULBs:** Municipal Corporation, Municipality, **Notified Area Committee** (entirely nominated), Town Area Committee, **Cantonment Board**, Township, **Port Trust**, Special Purpose Agency.
[3:7] - **Municipal Corporations, Municipal Councils and Nagar Panchayats** are all **urban** local governments.
[3:8] - **Cantonment Board:** outside 243Q; set up under the **Cantonments Act, 2006** (replaced the **1924 Act**); under the **Ministry of Defence**; 62 boards. **MoHUA** is the main ministry for ULBs; Home Affairs for UTs.
[3:9] - **Panchayat vs Cantonment Board dispute** (Chhattisgarh Municipalities Act, 1961, s.79(2)): decided by the **State Government, subject to Central Government approval**.

## SUB 5 | Wards Committees (243S) and Area Sabhas
[5:0] - **Wards Committee:** compulsory in every municipality with a **population of three lakh or more**; consists of **one or more wards**.
[5:1] - **State Legislature** decides its composition, territorial area and how seats are filled; the ward **councillor** is a member.
[5:2] - Nagar Panchayat wards committee (Chhattisgarh law, CGPSC 2017 key): formed of some wards; elected ward councillors members; **two residents nominated**; chairman not nominated by the President.
[5:3] - **Area Sabha:** all voters of a **polling-booth area** – proposed in the **Model Nagara Raj Bill, 2008**; the **Community Participation Law** was a mandatory reform under **JNNURM**.
[5:4] > 3 lakh = Wards Committee; 10 lakh = metropolitan area (MPC).
[5:5] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.40 p.120; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 24 p.11, 14; app notes 2:82

## SUB 9 | Urban Missions – AMRUT 2.0, Smart Cities Mission, UIDF
[9:0] - **AMRUT 2.0:** launched **1 October 2021** by the **Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA)** for five years (to 2025-26); urban water security, water supply to all statutory towns, wastewater recycling, a circular water economy.
[9:1] - **Smart Cities Mission:** launched **25 June 2015** (MoHUA) for **100 cities**; after extensions it **formally ended on 31 March 2025**; legacy – Integrated Command and Control Centres (ICCCs).
[9:2] - **Urban Infrastructure Development Fund (UIDF):** announced in **Budget 2023-24** for **Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities**; operated / managed by the **National Housing Bank (NHB)** from priority-sector lending shortfall.
