## SUB 0 | Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) – Appointment, Report and Democratic Decentralisation
[0:0] TABLE head: Particular | Fact
  [0:0.0] Appointed | 16 January 1957, by the Government of India (Team for the Study of Community Projects and NES; NDC/Planning-side review)
  [0:0.1] Chairman | Balwantrai Gopalji Mehta
  [0:0.2] Reviewed | Community Development Programme (1952) and National Extension Service (1953) – not IRDP
  [0:0.3] Report | 24 November 1957 (so "constituted / reported" = 1957)
  [0:0.4] Accepted | National Development Council, January 1958
[0:1] - **Remedy proposed:** the scheme of **"democratic decentralisation"**, which "ultimately came to be known as **Panchayati Raj**" – the committee **first recommended / used** the idea; Ashok Mehta, Rajagopalachari, Kripalani, Pannikar and Kunjru are distractors.
[0:2] - **Balwant Rai Mehta = "Architect / Father of Panchayati Raj"**; the present pattern of panchayats "owes its parentage" to this committee; the **Panchayati Raj system was recommended by its 1957 report** – not by the GoI Act 1935, Cripps Mission or Independence Act.
[0:3] - **Associated with:** **Panchayati Raj** (it was set up to review Community Development, but its report is identified with Panchayati Raj); also the basis of **decentralised planning** through PRIs (APPSC G1 2002) – not Mahalanobis or Gadgil.
[0:4] - **Finding:** public participation is indispensable – Panchayati Raj needs the **full co-operation of the local public**; PR stands for **decentralisation of powers, participation of the people and community development** ("all of these"), i.e. **grass-root democracy**.
[0:5] - **NDC (January 1958):** did **not insist on a single rigid pattern** – States free to **evolve their own patterns suited to local conditions**, basic principles identical.
[0:6] - Other heads: **Jivraj Mehta, Shriman Narayan, Jagjivan Ram, B.R. Ambedkar, Krishna Iyer** – never chaired a PR committee.
[0:7] > Most-asked: chairman, year (1957), CDP + NES reviewed, "democratic decentralisation", NDC flexibility.

## SUB 1 | Balwant Rai Mehta Model – Three Tiers and Detailed Recommendations
[1:0] TABLE head: Tier | Level | Election (BRM) | Role
  [1:0.0] Gram Panchayat | Village | Direct | Village body
  [1:0.1] Panchayat Samiti | Block | Indirect | Executive body; block (samithi) = basic unit of planning and development
  [1:0.2] Zila Parishad | District | Indirect | Advisory, coordinating and supervisory
[1:1] - **Three-tier** system – **first recommended** by BRM; tiers "**organically linked**" through **indirect elections**; samithis filled by **indirect election from the village panchayats** (not from graduates, co-operatives or sarpanches alone).
[1:2] - **Panchayat Samiti** is an **administrative (executive) authority** at the **block level** – not advisory/consultative; it is the body for planning (APPSC Panchayat Secretary).
[1:3] - **District Collector = chairman of the Zila Parishad**.
[1:4] - **All planning and development activities** entrusted to these bodies; **genuine transfer of power and responsibility**; **adequate resources** to be transferred; further devolution in future.
[1:5] - BRM did **NOT** recommend: a **two-tier** system, **direct election to the Zila Parishad**, the ZP as **executive**, a **village panchayat of indirectly elected members**, a samiti as **advisory**, or PRI power to levy **income tax / property tax** at all levels.
[1:6] - **Report details (APPSC Social Welfare Mains):** each **municipality lying as an enclave in a samithi elects one member** to the samithi; a samithi **can be superseded in public interest**; the **village panchayat collects land revenue as an agency on payment of commission**.
[1:7] - **Not a tier:** **Nyaya Panchayat**; **not a PRI:** a **Gram Cooperative Society** (Gram Sabha, Gram Panchayat, Nyaya Panchayat are PR bodies).
[1:8] - Today the PR set-up (73rd Amendment) is also **three-tier – village, block (intermediate), district**; middle tier optional below 20 lakh population.
[1:9] - Under the **73rd Amendment**, Panchayat Samiti members are **directly elected** (UP name: **Kshetra Panchayat**) – unlike BRM's indirect model.
[1:10] > Code items: BRM = c and d (planning to PRIs; Collector ZP chairman); not a and b (two tiers; direct ZP elections).
[1:11] ~~ Sources: Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.96; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 23 p.14; NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 05 p.2; app notes 2:80

  [2:0.1] Andhra Pradesh | 1 November 1959 | Second – Shadnagar; CM N. Sanjiva Reddy

[2:5] - **No PRIs:** **Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram** (traditional/ADC bodies); Kerala, Assam, Tripura have PRIs.

## SUB 3 | Community Development Programme (1952) and National Extension Service (1953)
[3:0] # Grow More Food Inquiry Committee (1952)
[3:1] - Chairman: **V.T. Krishnamachari** (APPSC G2 2008) – not Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta or G.V.K. Rao; campaign (1943) found too narrow → **integrated village development** and a **nationwide extension organisation** → CDP and NES.
[3:2] # CDP
[3:3] - Launched **2 October 1952** (Gandhi Jayanti) by **Nehru**, **First Five Year Plan**; **55** pilot community projects; pioneered by **S.K. Dey** (later Union Minister of Community Development).
[3:4] - **First major rural development programme / first effort at local governing bodies** in independent India; "mother of social development programmes"; launched **before** BRM (1957).
[3:5] - **Aim:** **development of villages through public participation**; a step towards **Article 40**; raising the standard of living under **Article 47**.
[3:6] - **Components:** agriculture, irrigation, communications, health, housing, education and **social education** (adult literacy, community centres) – social education was part of the **CDP**.
[3:7] - **Statements (APPSC JA):** launched 1952 for comprehensive rural development ✔; focuses on infrastructure, education, health, agriculture ✔; "top-down, communities minimal" ✘ (its idea was people's participation).
[3:8] - **Community development approach:** asset-based (build existing strengths), inclusive of diversity, aims at **sustainable self-sufficiency** and solving **community** issues – not individual prosperity in place of the community.
[3:9] # NES and extension
[3:10] - **National Extension Service (1953)** – described as the **first programme** of community development (spread the CDP pattern); NES followed CDP.
[3:11] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [3:11.0] Block | Unit of planning and development (about 100 villages)
  [3:11.1] Block Development Officer | Heads the block team of extension officers
  [3:11.2] Village Level Worker (Gram Sevak) | Multipurpose extension worker for a group of villages
[3:12] - **Extension principle:** the extension worker translates laboratory findings so farm families adopt them **voluntarily** = **principle of applied science and democratic approach**.
[3:13] - **Failure:** seen as **government-imposed**, inadequate people's participation → BRM review.
[3:14] TABLE head: Year | Programme
  [3:14.0] 1952 | CDP
  [3:14.1] 1953 | NES
  [3:14.2] 1975 | ICDS
  [3:14.3] 1977 | Food for Work
  [3:14.4] 2 Oct 1978 | National Adult Education Programme (15-35)
  [3:14.5] 1978-79; nationwide 2 Oct 1980 | IRDP

## SUB 4 | Ashok Mehta Committee (1977-78)
[4:0] - Appointed **December 1977** by the **Janata government** (PM **Morarji Desai**); chairman **Asoka (Ashok) Mehta**; report **August 1978**, **132 recommendations** – a committee on PRIs (not Abid Hussain, Khusro, Chakravarty).
[4:1] - **Finding:** PRIs had declined, yet they had **generated a new leadership** committed to development.
[4:2] - **Two-tier:** **Zila Parishad** (district, **executive**, district planning) + **Mandal Panchayat** (group of villages, **15,000-20,000** people) – the **Mandal Panchayat system** was its idea (not BRM, Vengal Rao, Narasimham); **executive powers to Mandal Panchayats**; the Mandal Panchayat to **assist Regional Rural Banks** with infrastructure.
[4:3] - **Term 4 years**; **district = first point of decentralisation** below the State (not the village or block).
[4:4] - **Official participation of political parties**; **periodic elections at all levels**; elections by the State CEO with the CEC; no supersession – elections **within 6 months**.
[4:5] - **Compulsory taxation powers**: besides grants, panchayats must **mobilise their own resources** – no democratic body stays viable on external resources (1978).
[4:6] - **Social audit** by a district agency and a legislators' committee; **Minister for PR** in the State cabinet; SC/ST reservation by population; separate **Nyaya Panchayats** under a qualified judge; **constitutional recognition**; **voluntary agencies** to mobilise support.
[4:7] - **Fate:** Janata government fell → no central action; **Karnataka, West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh** acted on it ("second generation panchayats"; WB Act 1973, polls June 1978); Sixth Plan (1978-83) gave PRIs new life.
[4:8] TABLE head: Point | Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) | Ashok Mehta (1978)
  [4:8.0] Tiers | 3 | 2
  [4:8.1] Unit | Block (Samiti executive) | District (ZP executive)
  [4:8.2] Elections | Direct at village, indirect above | Party participation at all levels
  [4:8.3] Collector | ZP chairman | Reduced role
  [4:8.4] Constitutional status | Not proposed | Recommended
[4:9] > Incorrect-statement items: "village as first point of decentralisation" and "three tiers" are NOT Ashok Mehta.
[4:10] ~~ Sources: NextGen Polity - APPSC Exams 23 p.14-15; Laxmikanth 8th ed. ch.39 p.98-99; Rocket Polity Part 1 p.94

## SUB 8 | Road to Constitutional Status (1989-1993)
[8:0] TABLE head: Step | Fact
  [8:0.0] 64th Amendment Bill | Rajiv Gandhi government, Lok Sabha, July 1989; passed LS August 1989; based on Singhvi, Thungon, Gadgil
  [8:0.1] Fate | Defeated in the Rajya Sabha (October 1989) – special majority not secured
  [8:0.2] 65th Amendment Bill | Nagarpalika (municipalities) bill; also defeated in the Rajya Sabha (1989)
  [8:0.3] National Front (V.P. Singh) bill | September 1990 – lapsed when the government fell / Lok Sabha dissolved
  [8:0.4] Narasimha Rao bills | Introduced September 1991 → Joint Parliamentary Committee → passed December 1992
  [8:0.5] 73rd Amendment Act, 1992 | Panchayati Raj constitutionalised; in force 24 April 1993; Part IX (Art. 243-243O); Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects)
[8:1] - Opposition objection: **Local Government is a State List** subject (entry 5) – the Bill would **centralise power**.
[8:2] - Effect: PRIs made **justiciable**; constitutional obligation on States; **uniform** three-tier structure.
[8:3] - Key Articles: **243B(2)** middle tier optional below **20 lakh**; **243D(3)** at least **one-third** seats for women; **243K** State Election Commission; regular remuneration to members is **not** a provision.
[8:4] - **National Panchayati Raj Day: 24 April**.
