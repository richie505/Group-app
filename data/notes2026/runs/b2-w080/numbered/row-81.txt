## SUB 0 | Eleventh Schedule – 29 Subjects (Article 243G)
[0:0] - **Eleventh Schedule:** added by the **73rd Amendment**; **29 functional items** of the Panchayats, linked to **Article 243G** – not 18 (that is the **Twelfth Schedule**, Municipalities).
[0:1] - **Nature:** a menu – the State Legislature "**may**" devolve them; it divides powers between the **State Legislature and the Panchayats** (not Union–State, not Gram Sabha–Panchayat).
[0:2] - **First item:** **Agriculture**, including agricultural extension. **Last (29th) item:** **Maintenance of community assets**.
[0:3] TABLE head: No. | Items (Eleventh Schedule)
  [0:3.0] 1–7 | Agriculture; land improvement, land reforms, land consolidation, soil conservation; minor irrigation, watershed; animal husbandry, dairying, poultry; fisheries; social and farm forestry; minor forest produce
  [0:3.1] 8–15 | Small-scale industries incl. food processing; Khadi, village and cottage industries; rural housing; drinking water; fuel and fodder; roads, culverts, bridges, ferries, waterways; rural electrification; non-conventional energy sources
  [0:3.2] 16–22 | Poverty alleviation programme; education incl. primary and secondary schools; technical training and vocational education; adult and non-formal education; libraries (item 20); cultural activities; markets and fairs
  [0:3.3] 23–29 | Health and sanitation; family welfare; women and child development; social welfare incl. handicapped; welfare of weaker sections, SCs and STs; public distribution system; maintenance of community assets
[0:4] - **NOT in the Eleventh Schedule:** **higher education**, **rural sports**, **public order**, police; and the **Twelfth Schedule** (municipal) items – **slum improvement and upgradation**, **regulation of land use**, **urban planning**, **slaughter houses**, **burials and burial grounds**, **fire services**, cattle pounds.
[0:5] - **Model Accounting System (MAS) for PRIs:** prescribed by the **C&AG** (with MoPR, 2009); its **major accounting heads follow the 29 Eleventh Schedule functions** – so PRIASoft keeps **29** major heads.
[0:6] > Most-asked angle: the number (29) – asked 10+ times; then "which is / is NOT an item" (libraries vs land use, slum improvement, higher education, rural sports).

## SUB 1 | The 73rd Amendment and Part IX – Significance and Articles 243 to 243O
[1:0] - **73rd Amendment Act, 1992:** deals with **Panchayati Raj** (rural local government); the **74th** deals with Municipalities (Nagar Palika). Bill introduced **September 1991** (Narasimha Rao Government), Act of **1992**, in force **24 April 1993**.
[1:1] - **Third tier of the federal system:** added in **1992** (73rd and 74th Acts; in force 1993).
[1:2] - Added **Part IX "The Panchayats"** – **Articles 243 to 243O** = **16 Articles** – and the **Eleventh Schedule**.
[1:3] - Gave a **practical shape to Article 40** (DPSP: organise village panchayats as **units of self-government**) and **constitutional status** to PRIs.
[1:4] - **Justiciable part:** it brought PRIs under the **justiciable part** of the Constitution – States are under a **constitutional obligation** to adopt the system; formation of panchayats and regular elections no longer depend on the State Government's will.
[1:5] - **Significance:** a **landmark in the evolution of grassroots democratic institutions**; turns **representative into participatory democracy**; rural local bodies **rejuvenated**.
[1:6] - It did **not** create **caste panchayats** or khap panchayats – only the **Gram Panchayat** is a body under the Panchayati Raj system.
[1:7] TABLE head: Article | Content
  [1:7.0] 243 | Definitions – Gram Sabha (243(b)), intermediate level, Panchayat, village
  [1:7.1] 243A | Gram Sabha
  [1:7.2] 243B | Constitution of Panchayats – three tiers in every State
  [1:7.3] 243C | Composition – by State law; direct election of members
  [1:7.4] 243D | Reservation of seats (SC/ST, women, BCs optional)
  [1:7.5] 243E | Duration – 5 years
  [1:7.6] 243F | Disqualifications – minimum age 21
  [1:7.7] 243G | Powers, authority and responsibilities of Panchayats – Eleventh Schedule
  [1:7.8] 243H | Power to impose taxes; Panchayat funds
  [1:7.9] 243I | State Finance Commission
  [1:7.10] 243J | Audit of accounts of Panchayats
  [1:7.11] 243K | State Election Commission
  [1:7.12] 243L | Application to Union Territories
  [1:7.13] 243M | Part not to apply to certain areas
  [1:7.14] 243N | Continuance of existing laws
  [1:7.15] 243O | Bar to interference by courts in electoral matters (last Article)
[1:8] - **Article 243G:** powers and functions of **Panchayats** (village, intermediate, district) – not finances (243H), not Nyaya Panchayats.
[1:9] - **Article 243J:** the **State Legislature** may provide for maintenance and **audit of Panchayat accounts**.
[1:10] - **Pair check (CGPSC 2024):** 243A = Gram Sabha, 243B = Constitution, **243C = Composition** – only the 243C pair is right.
[1:11] - **Exemption Articles:** **243M** (Part IX) vs **243ZC** (Part IXA); Part IXA = **243P–243ZG**, Twelfth Schedule, 18 subjects, in force **1 June 1993**.
[1:12] - **Ministry of Panchayati Raj:** created **27 May 2004**; handles Part IX, PESA and District Planning Committees.

## SUB 2 | Gram Sabha – Article 243(b) and 243A
[2:0] - **Gram Sabha (Art. 243(b)):** body of **persons registered in the electoral rolls** relating to a village within the Panchayat area – i.e. **all registered voters**, not all residents, all adults, elders or employees.
[2:1] - **Minimum age:** **18 years** (voting age); the **age to contest** a Panchayat seat is **21**.
[2:2] - **Article 243A:** Gram Sabha exercises such powers at the village level as the **State Legislature provides by law** – in function it is to the village what the **State Legislature** is to the State.
[2:3] - **Compulsory:** a Gram Sabha **must be constituted in each village** (or group of villages) – not optional, not abolishable; it is the **electorate** for direct elections (243C).
[2:4] - **Direct democracy:** the **Gram Sabha** is the best example – voters decide directly; Gram Panchayat, Nagar Panchayat and Zila Panchayat are representative bodies; caste panchayats, opinion polls and people's courts are not.
[2:5] - **Meetings:** at least **twice a year** (NextGen: April 13 and Oct 3; State Acts fix dates); Gram Sabha meetings may be held in each village (by rotation); Gram Panchayat meetings at the GP headquarters.
[2:6] # Gram Sabha's statutory roles
[2:7] - **MGNREGA social audit:** vested in the **Gram Sabha** (MGNREGA s.17).
[2:8] - **PMAY-Gramin:** the **Gram Sabha verifies the housing-deprivation (SECC) data** for selecting beneficiaries.
[2:9] # Gram Panchayat Development Plan (GPDP)
[2:10] - **People's Plan Campaign "Sabki Yojana Sabka Vikas":** from **2 October 2018**; GPDPs prepared through Gram Sabhas.
[2:11] - **GPDP** converges all village plans – sanitation, MGNREGS labour budget, nutrition, agriculture, school education, water, livelihoods, natural-resource and disaster plans (**all of them**).
[2:12] - **Vital first step:** **collection of data and situation analysis**, before prioritisation and resource mapping.
[2:13] - **Viksit Gram Panchayat Plans** are linked to **PM Gati Shakti**.

## SUB 3 | Three-tier Structure, Composition, Term and Qualification
[3:0] - **Article 243B:** Panchayats at **village, intermediate and district** levels in **every State**; a State with population **not exceeding 20 lakh may not** constitute the intermediate tier – so intermediate Panchayats do **not** exist in all States ("may not", not "shall not").
[3:1] - **Composition (243C):** made by the **Legislature of the State** by law; a **Gram Panchayat can cover more than one village**.
[3:2] - **Area:** the intermediate level and villages are specified by the **Governor / State Government** by public notification (e.g. the territory of a Kshetra Panchayat).
[3:3] TABLE head: Who | How chosen
  [3:3.0] Members at all three levels (incl. Panchayat Samiti) | Directly elected from territorial constituencies
  [3:3.1] Intermediate and district chairpersons | Indirectly, by and from among elected members
  [3:3.2] Village chairperson (Sarpanch) | As State law provides
  [3:3.3] MPs, MLAs, MLCs | May be given representation (voluntary)
[3:4] - **Qualification (243F):** minimum age **21 years** (not 25, not 30); other disqualifications by State law.
[3:5] - **Term (243E):** **5 years from the date of the first meeting**; if dissolved, fresh polls **within six months** (not three); no poll if **less than six months** remain.
[3:6] - **Reconstituted Panchayat:** serves only the **remainder** of the term – first meeting **10 Feb 2014** → new body ends **10 Feb 2019**.
[3:7] - **Proxy rule:** a woman Sarpanch **cannot nominate her husband** to attend meetings ("Sarpanch-pati" is not legal); ex-officio MLAs/MPs may send representatives under some State Acts.
[3:8] - **Nominated members** attend but have no vote (State Acts).
[3:9] TABLE head: Intermediate tier name | State
  [3:9.0] Mandal Parishad (Mandal Praja Parishad) | Andhra Pradesh
  [3:9.1] Janpad Panchayat | Madhya Pradesh (also Chhattisgarh)
  [3:9.2] Kshetra Panchayat | Uttar Pradesh (Uttarakhand)
  [3:9.3] Taluka Panchayat / Taluka Parishad | Gujarat (Taluk Panchayat – Karnataka)
  [3:9.4] Anchalik Panchayat | Assam
  [3:9.5] Panchayat Samiti | Most States, e.g. Rajasthan, Maharashtra, West Bengal
[3:10] - **CGPSC 2019 code** pairs Anchalik Parishad with **West Bengal** (code 3 4 2 1); Anchalik Panchayat is Assam's term.
[3:11] - **AP tiers:** Gram Panchayat – **Mandal Parishad** – Zilla Parishad.

## SUB 4 | State Election Commission and State Finance Commission
[4:0] # State Election Commission (Article 243K)
[4:1] - **SEC:** superintendence, direction and control of **preparing electoral rolls** and **conducting all elections to the Panchayats** – not the Election Commission of India; municipal polls under **243ZA** by the same SEC "referred to in 243K".
[4:2] - **State Election Commissioner:** appointed by the **Governor** (conditions and tenure also by the Governor); removable only like a **High Court judge**; service conditions cannot be varied to his disadvantage.
[4:3] - **Law on Panchayat elections:** made by the **State Legislature** (243K(4)).
[4:4] - **Constitutional authorities:** **SEC (243K)**, **SFC (243I)** and the **District Panchayat (243B)**; the **Chief Electoral Officer** is a statutory officer (RPA 1950, s.13A).
[4:5] - **Created by the 73rd and 74th Amendments:** **SEC, SFC, District Planning Committee (243ZD, 74th)** – not a property-tax valuation board.
[4:6] - **Rajasthan SEC:** constituted in **July 1994** (Governor's order 17 June 1994, working from 1 July 1994); its **Secretary is also the Chief Electoral Officer** of the State.
[4:7] - **Madhya Pradesh SEC:** constituted **1 February 1994**; Panchayat general elections held **five** times by 2020 (1994, 1999-2000, 2004-05, 2009-10, 2014-15).
[4:8] # State Finance Commission (Article 243I)
[4:9] - Constituted by the **Governor** within one year of 24 April 1993 and then **every five years**; reviews Panchayat finances (and Municipalities' – **243Y**).
[4:10] - **Recommends** to the Governor: sharing of **net proceeds of State taxes**, duties, tolls and fees between State and Panchayats; **taxes assigned** to Panchayats; **grants-in-aid**; measures to improve finances.
[4:11] - Governor lays the report before the **State Legislature** with an **action-taken memorandum**; it does **not** appoint the State Election Commissioner.
[4:12] - **Article 243H:** the **State Legislature** may authorise Panchayats to **levy, collect and appropriate** taxes, duties, tolls and fees.
[4:13] - **Own tax domain:** the **Gram Panchayat** has the main own taxes (house/property tax), so it is better placed for own revenue; the intermediate and district tiers depend mostly on grants, tolls and fees.
[4:14] - **Union FC link:** **Art. 280(3)(bb)** (Panchayats) and **280(3)(c)** (Municipalities) – measures to augment a State's Consolidated Fund on SFC recommendations.
[4:15] TABLE head: Basis | Union FC | SFC
  [4:15.0] Article | 280 | 243I (Panchayats), 243Y (Municipalities)
  [4:15.1] Appointed by | President | Governor
  [4:15.2] Divides | Union ↔ States | State ↔ local bodies
[4:16] > Code trap: "SFC = Article 243" (it is 243I); "SEC appointed by CEC/President" (it is the Governor).

## SUB 7 | Reservation – Article 243D
[7:0] - **Article 243D:** seats for **SCs and STs** in proportion to population at all levels; **not less than one-third** of seats (and of chairpersons, **243D(4)**) for **women** – a floor, not a ceiling, not "50% for SC/ST women".
[7:1] - **Rotation:** reserved seats are **allotted by rotation** – not fixed for 10/25/50 years.
[7:2] - **BC reservation:** optional (**243D(6)**).
[7:3] TABLE head: Case | Year | Holding
  [7:3.0] K. Krishna Murthy v. Union of India | 2010 | Upheld 243D(6)/243T(6); political backwardness ≠ social/educational backwardness; vertical reservation normally within 50%
  [7:3.1] Vikas Kishanrao Gawali v. Maharashtra | 2021 | Triple test: dedicated commission, local-body-wise proportion, SC+ST+OBC within 50%
[7:4] - **AP:** **34% BC quota** plus SC/ST shares must stay within Gawali's **50% ceiling**.
[7:5] # Women's 50% and data
[7:6] - **Bihar** – first to give **50%** to women (2006). By **2009-10**, **Bihar, Himachal Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh** had 50% (Govt statement, 112th Amendment Bill 2009); many States now, including **AP**.
[7:7] - **MoPR, April 2026:** **12.14 lakh of 24.41 lakh** elected PRI representatives are women (**49.75%**); short of 50%: **Haryana 42.12%**, Gujarat 49.9%, Tripura 45.25%.
[7:8] - **Proxy problem:** IRMA's **State of Panchayats Report 2008-09** – about **59%** of SC/ST women representatives were proxies.
[7:9] - **2026:** **Sashakt Panchayat Netri Abhiyan** and **Model Women-Friendly Gram Panchayats** – against token representation.
[7:10] - **EWS link:** **Gujarat** was the first State to implement the 10% EWS quota (January 2019).
