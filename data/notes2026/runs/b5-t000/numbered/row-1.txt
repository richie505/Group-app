[0:9] - **Heart disease risk:** high BP, raised glucose, raised lipids – **not** vitamin A deficiency.

## SUB 1 | Proteins, Amino Acids and Malnutrition
[1:0] - **Proteins** = **body-building** nutrients (growth and repair; most important for growing children and athletes building muscle) – ~**4 kcal/g**; **amino acids** are their **building blocks** (20 common, ~9–10 essential; not stored).
[1:1] - **Essential amino acids** (from food): histidine, **isoleucine**, leucine, lysine, **methionine**, phenylalanine, **threonine**, **tryptophan**, valine. **Non-essential:** **tyrosine**, alanine, aspartic acid, cysteine, glycine, **proline** (an amino acid).
[1:2] - **Egg** = **highest biological value** – contains **all essential amino acids** in the right proportion.
[1:3] TABLE head: Protein | Found in
  [1:3.0] Casein (~80% of milk protein) | Milk (primary group); whey proteins the rest
  [1:3.1] Gluten | Wheat

  [1:3.3] Keratin | Hair, nails, horn
  [1:3.4] Myosin (with actin) | Muscle
  [1:3.5] Albumin | Egg white, blood
  [1:3.6] Collagen / fibroin | Connective tissue / silk
[1:4] - "**Only proteins**" pair: **keratin and myosin** (fructose, maltose and sucrose are sugars).
[1:5] - **Lactoderm** = the sticky **protein film (skin) on heated milk**.
[1:6] # Protein-energy malnutrition
[1:7] TABLE head: Disease | Cause | Sign
  [1:7.0] Kwashiorkor | Protein deficiency (calories adequate), children over 1 | Oedema, swollen belly, skin and hair changes
  [1:7.1] Marasmus | Severe lack of both protein and calories, infants | Extreme wasting, no oedema
  [1:7.2] Marasmic kwashiorkor | Deficiency of both proteins and calories with oedema | Wasting + oedema
[1:8] - Not PEM: scurvy (C), goitre (iodine), mumps (virus), achondroplasia (genetic dwarfism), rickets (D).
[1:9] - **Protein per 100 g (increasing):** egg white (~11 g) < fish (~20 g) < Bengal gram dal (~21 g) < groundnut (~25 g) < **skimmed milk powder (~35 g)**; soybean is the **highest-protein grain** (~40 g).
[1:10] - Nursing mother needs **65 g** protein a day.
[1:11] - **Pepsin** (stomach) breaks proteins into **peptides**; trypsin works later in the small intestine.

[2:1] - **Starch** = a **polysaccharide** (glucose polymer); the **iodine test** turns starch **blue-black**.
[2:2] - **Fructose** = **fruit sugar**, the **sweetest natural sugar**; gives fruits their sweet taste.
[2:3] - **Honey:** mainly **fructose** (~38%) > glucose (~31%); sucrose and maltose minor; honey is not a colloid.
[2:4] - **Relative sweetness (descending): fructose > sucrose > glucose > maltose** (> lactose).
[2:5] - **Invert sugar** = glucose + fructose from **hydrolysis of sucrose**; **sweeter than sucrose** because of its free fructose (the hydrolysis is how it is made, not why it is sweet).
[2:6] - **Sucrose** = glucose + fructose (disaccharide); maltose, lactose = disaccharides; glucose, fructose, ribose = monosaccharides.
[2:7] - **Soybean** is rich in **all three** – protein (~40%), fat (~20%) and carbohydrate.
[2:8] - Plants make the essential foods **carbohydrates, fats and proteins**; **nucleic acids** are genetic material, not a "food".
[2:9] - **Oatmeal** = a **cereal-grain** product; sago and arrowroot are starches from stems/roots, soya flour is from a legume.

## SUB 7 | Human Body, Disorders and Health Technology
[7:0] # Genetic and blood disorders
[7:1] - **Thalassemia:** autosomal recessive; **reduced rate of synthesis of one of the globin chains** (α or β) of haemoglobin → anaemia (not X-linked, not a clotting defect, not phenylalanine build-up = PKU).
[7:2] - **Haemophilia:** **X-linked recessive** defect of a **clotting factor** – a cut keeps bleeding.
[7:3] - **Viruses** contain **nucleic acid** (DNA or RNA) in a protein coat; no enzymes for metabolism of their own.
[7:4] - **Scorpion venom** toxins are **proteins** (peptides).

## SUB 11 | Everyday Chemistry – Atoms, Reactions, Acids, Metals and Organic Compounds
[11:0] # Chemical reactions
[11:1] - **Rusting** (hydrated iron oxide) is a **chemical change** – the nail's **weight increases**; **tarnishing of silver** (Ag2S) is **corrosion**; freezing, dissolving salt, a glowing filament are physical changes.
[11:2] - **Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen**; **acid + base → salt + water** (neutralisation); C + O2 → CO2 is **exothermic**.
[11:3] - **Signs of a chemical reaction:** gas evolved, colour change, heat change – **evaporation** is only a change of state (physical).
[11:4] - **Limestone:** CaCO3 → CaO + **CO2** on heating – CO2 **puts out a flame**; **quicklime + water → slaked lime Ca(OH)2 + much heat**; acid (HCl) + egg shell → CO2 – turns **lime water milky**.
[11:5] - **Propane combustion:** C3H8 + **5 O2** → 3 CO2 + 4 H2O; **Haber:** N2 + 3 H2 ⇌ 2 NH3 (X = 1, Y = 3, Z = 2).
[11:6] - **Combustion** needs fuel, oxygen and ignition temperature – **not water**; **magnesium** in crackers – bright light from **oxidation** to MgO.
[11:7] - **Displacement:** iron nail in copper sulphate gets a **brown copper** coat (solution pale green); iron + dilute HCl → **iron chloride + hydrogen**; **silver bromide** turns **grey** in sunlight (photolysis).
[11:8] - **Physical change:** dissolving salt in water; burning wax/wood and curdling milk are chemical.
[11:9] - **Ignition temperature:** a substance catches fire only once heated to it – at 100 °C, substances igniting at 95 °C and 98 °C burn, those at 105 °C and 108 °C do not.
[11:10] # Atoms and the periodic table
[11:11] - **Helium:** noble gas, Group 18, period 1 – **top right corner** of the periodic table; inert, so safe in balloons; **hydrogen** is lighter but **inflammable** (least safe).
[11:12] - **Atom:** electrons held round the nucleus by the **electrostatic (Coulomb) force**; nucleons held by the **strong nuclear force**; **protium (¹H)** has atomic number = mass number = 1.
[11:13] - **Periodic table:** metals on the **left**, metalloids on the zigzag, **non-metals on the right**; **Group IA = alkali metals** (Li, Na, K…); **potassium** is soft enough to **cut with a knife**.
[11:14] - **Spectral (emission) line:** electron **jumps from a higher to a lower orbit**, emitting a photon.
[11:15] - **Organic compound** must contain **carbon**; **ammonia** = N + H (NH3); **~94** elements occur naturally (118 known – "about 100"); **hydrogen** = most common element in the **Universe**.
[11:16] - **Transition (d-block) metals** form **coloured compounds** (d–d transitions).
[11:17] # Organic chemistry
[11:18] - **Alcohols as reducing agents:** they are themselves **oxidised to aldehydes or ketones**.
[11:19] - **Hydrogenation** of unsaturated hydrocarbons/oils uses a **nickel** catalyst (vanaspati); **Wurtz reaction** – alkyl halides + **sodium** in dry ether → higher alkanes; **vulcanisation** of rubber with **sulphur** (Goodyear, 1839).
[11:20] - **Perspex** (PMMA) – monomer methyl methacrylate made with **methyl alcohol**; **tear gas CN** = **α-chloroacetophenone**.
[11:21] - **Named reactions:** **Etard** – aromatic methyl → aldehyde (chromyl chloride); **Stephen** – nitrile → aldehyde (SnCl2/HCl); Gattermann–Koch = formylation (CO + HCl).
[11:22] - **Functional groups:** alcohols – **–OH (hydroxyl)**; **esters** – fruity smell; smallest **ketone** = acetone (**3 C**); **C2H6O** = ethanol; alcohol + conc. H2SO4 at 170 °C → **alkene** (dehydration); ethanol + alkaline KMnO4 → **acid**, acid + ethanol → **ester**.
[11:23] - **Alkaloids:** **nicotine** – tobacco; morphine – opium poppy; reserpine – Rauwolfia; **benzene** (crude oil, petrol) – carcinogen causing **leukaemia**.
[11:24] # Metals
[11:25] - **Gold** = most **malleable** and ductile metal (silver next); **aluminium** = most used metal in nearly pure form (foil, cans, conductors).
[11:26] - **Thermite:** **iron oxide + aluminium** – welds rails; **galvanisation** = **zinc** coating; **silver** = best electrical conductor; **lead** = toxic metal of car batteries.
[11:27] - **Electrolytic refining of copper:** impure anode dissolves; **pure copper deposits at the cathode**; excess **lithium in LiCl → pink** (F-centres; K in KCl violet, Na in NaCl yellow).
[11:28] - **Smelting** = extracting metal from ore by reduction at high temperature; **blast furnace:** coke → CO, limestone → CaO + CO2, CO reduces Fe2O3 (no FeSO4 decomposition); **seasoning** with oil stops **cast iron** rusting.
[11:29] # Soaps, water and household chemistry
[11:30] - **Saponification** = fat/oil + strong base (NaOH) → **soap + glycerol**; the soap's **hydrocarbon tail** grips oily dirt while the ionic head faces water (micelles).
[11:31] - **Alum** purifies water by **coagulation** of suspended mud (Al³⁺ neutralises the charge); **rain water** = purest natural water (natural distillation).
[11:32] - **Safety match:** **red phosphorus** on the **striking strip**; head = potassium chlorate + antimony trisulphide (most keys say "head = phosphorus" – old strike-anywhere matches).
[11:33] - **Melamine** – unbreakable crockery; **tincture of iodine** – antiseptic; enzymes = biocatalysts; **DNA fingerprinting** – identifying criminals; **borax** dissolves shellac for water-based varnish.
[11:34] # Salts and their uses
[11:35] - **Salts:** sodium carbonate – washing soda, glass; sodium benzoate – food preservative; calcium carbonate – flux in metal extraction; calcium phosphate – fertiliser.
[11:36] - **Sodium thiosulphate** – antidote for **cyanide**; **normal saline** = **0.9% NaCl** (isotonic); **gunpowder** = sulphur + charcoal + **potassium nitrate**.
[11:37] - **Common names:** **alum** = potassium aluminium sulphate dodecahydrate; **aspirin** = acetylsalicylic acid; chalk = CaCO3; **barium nitrate** – oxidiser giving **green** flame in fireworks (strontium red, copper blue).
[11:38] # Acids and bases
[11:39] - **All acids contain hydrogen** (H⁺ in water); **pH = "potential of hydrogen"**; **NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H2O** = neutralisation, a **double displacement**.
[11:40] - **Acids** conduct electricity in water, taste sour, change indicators and **react with active metals** (H2); **litmus:** **purple** in neutral, **blue → red** in acid; **ammonia** turns red litmus blue (**weak base**).
[11:41] # Carbon and non-metals
[11:42] - **Graphite** = **inorganic** solid lubricant (lanolin, mineral oil, PTFE are organic).
[11:43] - **Sulphur** = common **fungicide**; **bromine** = only **non-metal liquid** at room temperature (mercury is a metal).
[11:44] - **Noble gases in air:** argon, neon, helium… – **radon** is the one usually said to be absent (strictly, traces near the ground); **dry ice** = **solid CO2** (sublimes at −78.5 °C) – vaccine transport.
[11:45] # Mixtures and colloids
[11:46] - **Aerosol** = tiny liquid droplets (or solid particles) dispersed in **air/gas** – fog, mist, sprays; **sea water** = a mixture (table salt, sugar, lime are compounds).
[11:47] - **Solubility in water (25 °C):** **glucose ~90 g/100 mL** > ammonium chloride ~38 > table salt ~36 > alum ~14.
[11:48] - **Butter** = water-in-fat **emulsion** (NCERT lists butter as a gel – liquid in solid); vinegar, sugar solution and honey are solutions.
[11:49] # Laboratory
[11:50] - **Titration** uses a **burette**; pipette transfers a fixed volume; **dry litmus + dry HCl gas → no change** (no ionisation without water); **turmeric** is a natural acid–base indicator (red-brown in base).
[11:51] # Industrial processes
[11:52] - **Haber process:** N2 + H2 → **ammonia** (iron catalyst, high T and P); **Contact process:** SO2 → SO3 over **V2O5** → **sulphuric acid**.

## SUB 12 | Human Physiology – Organs, Blood, Nerves, Senses and First Aid
[12:0] # Medicine and first aid
[12:1] - **Diuretics:** furosemide (loop), mannitol (osmotic), acetazolamide (carbonic-anhydrase inhibitor) – all increase urine.
[12:2] - **Catgut** (collagen from animal gut) = classic **absorbable suture**; silk and nylon are non-absorbable.
[12:3] - **Burns:** **1st degree** – epidermis only, redness and pain; 2nd – partial thickness, blisters; 3rd – full thickness.
[12:4] - **Heat stroke:** body cooling fails and core temperature **rises quickly** (> 40 °C); give **lemon water or buttermilk** (fluids and salts).
[12:5] - **Wounds:** **abscess** = cavity with **pus**; hematoma = collected blood; **contusion (bruise)** = classic **closed wound**; **root canal therapy** saves an abscessed tooth.
[12:6] - **Dosage forms:** syrup = liquid; tablets, capsules, boluses = solid; suppositories and enemas are rectal – **ecbolics** (oxytocics) are injected.
[12:7] - **Burn first aid:** cool the part in **cold running water**; do not pull off stuck cloth or apply butter.
[12:8] - **Capsule shells** = **gelatin**; **Dettol** = **chloroxylenol** + α-terpineol; **KOH mount** clears skin scrapings for fungi/mites; **sulphur ointment** controls **mange**.
[12:9] - **Antipyretics** (paracetamol) lower fever; analgesics relieve pain; **bronchoscopy** examines airways – not a nervous-system test (EEG, myelography are).
[12:10] - **Anorexia nervosa, bulimia** = **eating disorders**.
[12:11] # Digestion
[12:12] - **Digestion:** pancreas – digestive enzymes **and** insulin; **small intestine** – most absorption; **gall bladder stores bile** (made by the liver).
[12:13] - **Enzymes:** **lipase** – fat; **amylase** – starch; **trypsin** – protein; **lactase** – lactose; **oesophagus** links pharynx to stomach.
[12:14] - **Digestive system** = breaks food down and **absorbs nutrients**.
[12:15] - **Saliva** pH ~**6.7**, **salivary amylase (ptyalin)** digests starch; **lipase** digests fats; HCl from **gastric glands**.
[12:16] - **Fat digestion:** bile (made in the **liver**, stored in gall bladder) emulsifies, lipase completes it in the **small intestine**; digested fats go into **lacteals** – carried by **lymph**.
[12:17] - **Alimentary canal** = mouth → **anus**; **small intestine** = longest part (6–7 m), starts with the **duodenum**; **stomach** = cardiac, fundic, body, pyloric parts (serosa is a wall layer).
[12:18] # Heart and blood
[12:19] - **Resting heart rate** of an adult = **60–100 beats/min** (~72).
[12:20] - **Sphygmomanometer** measures **blood pressure**.
[12:21] - **WBCs** (with platelets) are **~1%** of blood volume (buffy coat).
[12:22] - **Blood pH 7.35–7.45** (slightly alkaline); **hypernatraemia** = excess **sodium**; **4 heart valves** (tricuspid, pulmonary, mitral, aortic).
[12:23] - **Vessels:** **capillaries** = narrowest (5–10 µm, one cell thick); **aorta** = largest artery; **arteries** carry blood from the heart to the body; **pulmonary artery** carries CO2-rich blood to the lungs; **renal** – kidney, **hepatic** – liver, **coronary** – heart.
[12:24] - **Blood cells:** **erythrocytes = RBC** (formed in **red bone marrow**), **leucocytes = WBC** (colourless, no haemoglobin), **thrombocytes = platelets**.
[12:25] # Brain and nerves
[12:26] - **Right cerebral hemisphere** – **spatial abilities**, face recognition; left – language.
[12:27] - **Adult brain** ≈ 1.3–1.4 kg (~**3 pounds**); **axon** = the long fibre carrying impulses away from the cell body (dendrites short, branched); **Nissl's granules** are in the neuron cell body (not excretory); **trigeminal (V)** = largest cranial nerve, sensory + motor.
[12:28] - **Synapse** = junction (gap) between two neurons.
[12:29] - **Neurons** send and receive information; **medulla oblongata** – involuntary actions (BP, salivation, vomiting, **breathing**).
[12:30] # Skeleton and muscles
[12:31] - **Axial skeleton** = skull, vertebral column, ribs, sternum; **femur** = longest and strongest bone (tibia = lower leg).
[12:32] - **Ossification** = deposition of calcium and minerals on cartilage → bone.
[12:33] - **Tooth enamel** = hardest substance in the body – **calcium phosphate** (hydroxyapatite); **calcium + phosphorus** give bones their strength; **milk teeth** fall from **6–8 years** (most keys; strictly ~6 to 12).
[12:34] - **Skeletal (striated) muscle** = **voluntary**; smooth and cardiac – involuntary.
[12:35] # Teeth, eye and senses
[12:36] - **Adult dentition (32):** **8 incisors, 4 canines, 8 premolars, 12 molars**.
[12:37] - **Eye:** visual acuity **20/200** = **legally blind**; **ciliary muscles** change the lens curvature (accommodation); one eye only → loss of depth – a **two-dimensional** world.
[12:38] - **Iris** gives the eye its colour – pigment made by **melanocyte cells**.
[12:39] # Kidney and excretion
[12:40] - **Excretory system:** nephron – **Bowman's capsule**, glomerulus, **Henle's loop**; ureters – **not** alveoli (lungs), colon or Schwann cells (myelin of nerves).
[12:41] - **Kidneys** filter waste and excess water from the blood.
[12:42] # Respiration
[12:43] - **Lungs:** gas exchange in the **alveoli**; total lung capacity ≈ **6 L** (vital capacity ~4.5–4.8 L).
[12:44] - **Alveoli** spread out ≈ a **tennis court** (~70 m²); **sneezing** = a reflex of the **respiratory system**.
[12:45] # Body composition
[12:46] - **Human body by mass:** **oxygen ~65%** > carbon > hydrogen > nitrogen > calcium.
[12:47] - **Anatomy** = structure (physical arrangement) of body parts; **physiology** = how they function.
[12:48] - **Trace elements:** official key treats **arsenic** as having a functional requirement (its essentiality in humans is unproven).
[12:49] # Immunity and disease
[12:50] - **Innate immunity** (first line): skin, mucus, **tears** (lysozyme) flushing dust from the eye.
[12:51] - **Pneumonia** bacteria: **Haemophilus influenzae**, Streptococcus pneumoniae (Lactobacillus, S. thermophilus – curd).
[12:52] - **Tears** contain **lysozyme** (antibacterial); ptyalin in saliva.
[12:53] # Reproduction
[12:54] - **Vas deferens** = **male** sperm duct – not female (female: ovary, fallopian tube with fimbriae, uterus, endometrium, cervix).
[12:55] # Glands and hormones
[12:56] - **Goitre** = **enlargement of the thyroid gland**, mostly from iodine deficiency.

[13:17] - **Pollination:** bright, scented, nectar-rich flowers → **insects (entomophily)**; **anemophily = wind**; hydrophily = water; ornithophily = birds.
[13:18] - After fertilisation the **ovary → fruit** and the ovules → seeds.
[13:19] - **Sporopollenin** (pollen exine) = most resistant organic material: withstands high temperature, strong acids and alkalis; no enzyme degrades it – so pollen fossilises well.
[13:20] - Repeated **self-pollination** → inbreeding depression, **weak seeds**; cross-pollination gives vigour.

[13:23] - **Vallisneria:** the **whole male flower** floats to the female flower (water pollination).
[13:24] # Leaf, photosynthesis and respiration
[13:25] - **Leaf:** **stomata** – pores for gas exchange and transpiration; **guard cells** open and close them; **chloroplasts** hold chlorophyll; enzymes = biocatalysts.
[13:26] - **Photosynthesis:** green plants **make their own food** (glucose) from CO2 + water with sunlight and chlorophyll; light absorbed by chlorophyll, CO2 **reduced** (not oxidised); the O2 released comes from **water** (photolysis); **oxygen is a product, not a requirement**.
[13:27] - **Stomata** = pores for **gas exchange**, opened and closed by **guard cells** through **potassium ions** moving in and out.
[13:28] - **Inputs of photosynthesis:** **chlorophyll**, light, CO2, water (O2 and glucose are products).
[13:29] - **C4 types:** **Panicum** – PEP-CK; maize, sugarcane – NADP-ME; Amaranthus – NAD-ME.
[13:30] - **Chlorophyll a** = **blue-green**; chlorophyll b = yellow-green.
[13:31] # Water, roots and transport
[13:32] - **Root pressure** is **lowest in summer** (heavy transpiration) and highest in the rainy season.
[13:33] - **Xylem** carries **water and minerals upward** from roots to leaves; **phloem** carries food (**translocation**); a **vascular bundle** holds both; vessel **end walls and pit pores** stop cavitation spreading.
[13:34] - **Transpiration** = evaporation at the **leaf–air** surface; the tension **pulls water up** (not loss at the root surface).

## SUB 14 | Everyday Physics – Motion, Gravity, Fluids, Heat and States of Matter
[14:0] # Heat and states of matter
[14:1] - **Boiling points:** mercury ~357 °C > water 100 °C > benzene 80 °C > ethanol 78 °C (mercury boils last).
[14:2] - **Conduction:** iron from the fridge feels **colder** than wood at the same temperature – a good conductor draws heat from the hand faster.
[14:3] - **Changes of state:** **sublimation** = solid → gas; **deposition** = gas → solid.
[14:4] - **Convection** – air-conditioners and heaters circulate air (cool air sinks, warm air rises).
[14:5] - **Specific heat** = heat to raise **1 g by 1 °C**.
[14:6] - **Water expands ~9% on freezing** – a corked glass bottle full of water **breaks** in the freezer.
[14:7] - **Supercooling** = cooling a liquid **below its freezing point** without it solidifying.
[14:8] - **Water has a high specific heat** – a watermelon stays cool longer than an apple; kerosene ≈ **0.5 cal/g °C** (water 1); **1 calorie ≈ 4.2 J** (4.186).
[14:9] - **Dissolved salt raises the boiling point and lowers the freezing point** (colligative): salt in boiling water stops the boil until it reaches the new, higher boiling point; **rock salt melts ice** on roads.
[14:10] - **Pressure lowers the melting point of ice** (regelation) – ice skating; **melting ice stays at 0 °C** (latent heat of fusion); **triple point of water = 0.01 °C** (273.16 K, ~611.657 Pa).
[14:11] - **Mothballs (naphthalene)** work by **sublimation**.
[14:12] - **Anomalous expansion:** water contracts from 0 °C to **4 °C (maximum density)** and expands above it.
[14:13] - **Sound-proofing:** rockwool, wood, laminated glass – **not iron sheets**; **copper-bottomed pans** – copper conducts heat better than steel; a **small LPG burner** saves gas.
[14:14] # Motion, force and gravity
[14:15] - **Free fall:** without air resistance all bodies fall with the same g – brick and feather land **together** (Galileo; Apollo 15).
[14:16] - **Friction** acts in brakes, striking a match, walking – **not** in planets' revolution (gravity in near-vacuum).
[14:17] - A ball released from an orbiting satellite keeps its orbital speed and **revolves in the same orbit**.
[14:18] - **Motion** = change of position with time (strictly, change of position = displacement); **Newton's 1st law** (inertia) – rest or uniform motion unless an unbalanced force acts; 2nd F = ma; 3rd action–reaction.
[14:19] - **g decreases with depth** below the surface (and with height) – weight **decreases slightly** in a deep mine.
[14:20] - **Displacement** = shortest path between initial and final position (distance = actual path); at **maximum height** the velocity of a body thrown up is **0**.
[14:21] - **Friction** slows a moving object; **writing with a pencil** = friction (not gravity); gravity – planets, rolling rock, tides.
[14:22] - **Levers** are of **3 classes** (fulcrum, load or effort in the middle).
[14:23] - **Conservation of momentum:** no external force → momentum cannot change (a man on frictionless ice can move by whistling – printed key: both true, R not the explanation).
[14:24] # Pressure, fluids and gases
[14:25] - **Boyle's law:** V ∝ 1/P at constant T – an air bubble **grows** as it rises in a lake; **Charles's law:** V ∝ T at constant P.
[14:26] - **Bernoulli:** fast air blown between two sheets lowers the pressure there → the papers **come closer**.
[14:27] - **Surface tension** lets insects (water striders) walk on water.
[14:28] - **Floating:** stones in a boat displace their weight of water; thrown in, only their volume – the **lake level falls**.
[14:29] - **Density of water** ≈ **1 g/cm³** (1000 kg/m³), highest at 4 °C.
[14:30] - **Charles's law in daily life:** a balloon in **hot water swells** (air expands on heating).

## SUB 15 | Cell Biology, Biomolecules, Genetics and Respiration
[15:0] # Respiration
[15:1] - **Aerobic respiration** needs **oxygen** → CO2 + water + energy; anaerobic in muscle → lactic acid; in yeast → ethanol + CO2.
[15:2] - **Respiration** is **continuous** (day and night) and **exothermic**; **yeast fermentation** = **anaerobic respiration**; **sweating** (evaporation) is **endothermic**; composting is exothermic.
[15:3] - **Glycolysis** (1st stage of glucose oxidation) → **pyruvic acid**; ATP made via the electron transport chain = **oxidative phosphorylation** (photophosphorylation in photosynthesis).
[15:4] # Genetics and chromosomes
[15:5] - **Humans:** 46 chromosomes = **23 pairs** (22 autosome pairs + 1 sex pair).
[15:6] - **Genetic code:** **triplet codon**, nearly universal; **AUG** = start (methionine); **stop** = UAA, UAG, UGA.
[15:7] - **44 autosomes** + **XY sex chromosomes** (XX female, XY male; ZW in birds); genes pass through **both sperm and egg**.
[15:8] - **Variation** = differences among individuals of a species; the **limit of growth** is fixed by **internal (genetic, hormonal) factors**.
[15:9] - **DNA content** changes over the cell cycle: G1, telophase = 2C; G2, prophase = 4C; **somatic : gamete chromosomes = 2 : 1** (2n : n).
[15:10] # Cell, osmosis and diffusion
[15:11] - **Egg shell** = ~95% **calcium carbonate**; dissolved in acid, the egg's membrane is semi-permeable – the egg **shrinks** in salt water (hypertonic), swells in plain water.
[15:12] - **Cell organelles:** **nucleus** = control centre; **mitochondria** = **powerhouse** (ATP) – removing them **stops energy metabolism**; chloroplasts make food; nucleolus makes ribosomes.
[15:13] - **Plant cells only:** **cellulose cell wall** (both have vacuoles, membrane, Golgi); **ribosomes** – protein synthesis; **cell membrane** – regulates passage.
[15:14] # Biomolecules
[15:15] - **Cell composition (% of mass):** water 70–90; **proteins 10–15**; **nucleic acids 5–7**; **carbohydrates 3**; **lipids 2**.
[15:16] - **ATP → ADP** releases ~**30.5 kJ/mol** (7.3 kcal); **cellulose** = structural carbohydrate of plant cell walls; **chlorophyll's** central ion = **magnesium**.
[15:17] - **ATP = adenosine triphosphate** – the cell's **"energy molecule"** / energy currency.
[15:18] - **Cellulose** = **homopolymer** of β-glucose and the **most abundant organic compound** on Earth.
[15:19] # Reproduction
[15:20] - **Asexual reproduction:** **binary fission** – Amoeba; **budding** – yeast, Hydra; **regeneration** – Planaria; **spores** – Rhizopus.
[15:21] - **Amoeba** – binary fission, but **not all unicellular organisms are only asexual** (Paramecium conjugation, Chlamydomonas, yeast).
[15:22] # Evolution and origin of life
[15:23] - **Adaptive radiation** (one species → many forms for different niches) – printed key "adaptation"; **biogeny sequence:** coacervates → first cells (mycoplasma-like) → heterotrophs → aerobes.
[15:24] # Microbes and sterilisation
[15:25] - **Hot-air oven** sterilises glassware at **160 °C for 60 min** (170 °C/30 min, 180 °C/20 min).

## SUB 18 | Everyday Physics – Light, Sound, Electricity and Magnetism
[18:0] # Light and colour
[18:1] - **Visible spectrum (VIBGYOR):** wavelength rises from violet to **red** (orange > yellow > indigo > violet); an opaque object shows the colour it **reflects**.
[18:2] - **TV remote** = **infrared** LED.
[18:3] - **Refraction** = change of direction (bending) of light passing into another medium: tank looks **shallower**; **prism dispersion** (each colour refracted differently); **twinkling of stars** and **Sun seen ~2 min before sunrise/after sunset** = **atmospheric refraction**.
[18:4] - **Scattering:** **blue sky**, **red Sun at sunrise/sunset**; the **rainbow** is **dispersion**, not scattering; a **pinhole camera's** smallest aperture is limited by **diffraction**.
[18:5] - **Grass looks green** – it **reflects green**; **red** has the longest visible wavelength (~620–750 nm).
[18:6] - **Dispersion** = splitting of white light into seven colours by a prism.
[18:7] - **Primary colours of light:** red, green, blue – **yellow is not** one.
[18:8] # Electricity
[18:9] - **Rectifier:** AC → DC (inverter: DC → AC).
[18:10] - **Indian supply: 230 V, 50 Hz** – a **110 V heater** draws ~4× its power and **burns out** within seconds/minutes.
[18:11] - **Resistivity** depends on the material and temperature, **not on shape** (resistance does); insulators – practically **infinite** resistance; a **semiconductor's resistance decreases** on heating (metal's increases).
[18:12] - **Short circuit:** resistance ≈ 0 → **current increases heavily**; a **fuse** melts on heavy current.
[18:13] - **Galvanometer:** detects current and its **direction**; null detector in bridges; more sensitive, not more accurate, than an ammeter.
[18:14] - **Transformer** works on **mutual induction**.
[18:15] # Sound
[18:16] - **Sound** is a **longitudinal** mechanical wave: **speed rises with temperature** (~0.6 m/s per °C in air); **solids** carry both longitudinal and transverse waves; **cannot travel in vacuum** (ultrasound too).
[18:17] - **Loudness** depends on **amplitude**; **pitch** on frequency.
[18:18] - **Echo:** reflected sound must arrive ≥ 0.1 s later → reflector at least **17.2 m** away (344 m/s); **women's voices** have **higher pitch** (shorter, thinner cords).
[18:19] - **Frequency** depends only on the **source** (same in every medium); speed and wavelength change with the medium.
[18:20] # Magnetism
[18:21] - **Magnetic shielding:** put the instrument inside **soft iron of high permeability**.
