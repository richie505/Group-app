=== NOTES 4:22 | Oceans – Relief of the Ocean Floor, Continental Shelf, Ridges, Trenches
## SUB 0 | Oceans and Earth's Water
[22:0:0] - Size order: **Pacific > Atlantic > Indian > Southern > Arctic**; **Arctic = smallest** (**APPSC GS 2018**).
[22:0:1] - **Pacific**: largest, deepest; larger than all land combined.
[22:0:2] - Oceans cover about **70–71%** of Earth's surface (**APPSC GS 2019**) and hold about **97%** of its water (**APPSC GS 2025**); ice ~2%; usable fresh water ~1%. **Groundwater** stores far more fresh water than lakes and rivers.
## SUB 1 | Depth Mapping and Ocean Zones
[22:1:0] TABLE head: Term | Meaning
  [22:1:0.0] Bathymetry | Measuring ocean depth and mapping sea-floor relief (APPSC GS 2018); narrower than oceanography
  [22:1:0.1] Fathom | Unit of depth (APPSC GS 2019) = 6 feet (about 1.83 m)
  [22:1:0.2] Isobath | Line joining points of equal depth
  [22:1:0.3] Hypsometric curve | Share of Earth's surface at each height and depth; shows ocean bottom topography
  [22:1:0.4] Echo sounding / SONAR | Reflection of ultrasonic waves; depth = speed of sound × half the echo time
[22:1:1] - **SONAR** uses: **fish location** (fishing), **submarine detection** (military), **depth measurement**, seabed mapping.
[22:1:2] TABLE head: Zone | Extent
  [22:1:2.0] Neritic | Water over the continental shelf
  [22:1:2.1] Pelagic | Open-ocean water beyond the shelf
  [22:1:2.2] Mesopelagic ("twilight zone") | 200–1,000 m; drives the biological carbon pump
  [22:1:2.3] Benthic / hadal | Sea floor / trenches
## SUB 2 | Ocean Bottom Relief
[22:2:0] TABLE head: Division | Depth | Key feature
  [22:2:0.0] Continental shelf | To about 200 m | "Submerged prolongation of land"; richest fishing; most oil deposits
  [22:2:0.1] Continental slope | 200–3,000 m | Canyons and trenches (APPSC G2 2023)
  [22:2:0.2] Continental rise | Foot of slope | Sediment apron
  [22:2:0.3] Deep sea (abyssal) plain | 3,000–6,000 m | Most extensive (over 76%); flattest, smoothest surface; polymetallic nodules
  [22:2:0.4] Oceanic deeps / trenches | Deepest | Volcanoes and earthquakes (APPSC G2 2023); subduction zones; mostly Pacific
[22:2:1] - Depth order: deeps > deep-sea plains > slopes > shelves.
[22:2:2] - Deep sea below 200 m = **oldest, largest biome**.
[22:2:3] Canyons and trenches = slope; volcanoes and earthquakes = deeps; most extensive = deep-sea plain.
## SUB 3 | Minor Relief Features
[22:3:0] TABLE head: Feature | Description
  [22:3:0.0] Seamount | Undersea volcanic peak that does not reach the surface; cobalt-rich crusts
  [22:3:0.1] Guyot | Flat-topped seamount, planed by waves, then sunk
  [22:3:0.2] Atoll | Ring- or horseshoe-shaped coral reef round a central lagoon (Lakshadweep, Maldives; Addu Atoll = southernmost Maldives)
  [22:3:0.3] Submarine canyon | Deep, steep-sided valley across shelf and slope, often off river mouths: Hudson Canyon; Mahadevan Canyon off Visakhapatnam
[22:3:1] - **Deep Ocean Mission** surveys: about **25 seamounts** in the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal; 2 active + 2 inactive hydrothermal vents.
[22:3:2] - **Tsunamis** are most common in the Pacific.

## SUB 5 | Continental Shelf of India
[22:5:0] TABLE head: Coast | Shelf
  [22:5:0.0] West | Wide; broadest off Gujarat and part of Maharashtra
  [22:5:0.1] East | Narrow
[22:5:1] - **Bombay (Mumbai) High**: oilfield on the wide western shelf, **160 km** off Mumbai (not 60); run by **ONGC**; found by a Russian-Indian team.

## SUB 6 | Ocean Deposits
[22:6:0] - **Terrigenous**: land gravel, sand, silt, mud on **shelves and slopes**; **finer away from shore**. Coastal gravel = terrigenous.
[22:6:1] - **Pelagic**: deep-sea oozes and red clay on deep plains and deeps.
[22:6:2] TABLE head: Pelagic type | Make-up
  [22:6:2.0] Calcareous ooze | Lime shells (globigerina, pteropods)
  [22:6:2.1] Siliceous ooze | Silica skeletons (diatoms, radiolarians); colder, deeper water
  [22:6:2.2] Red clay | Fine inorganic clay + volcanic dust; most widespread, largest in deep Pacific; not from lime shells
[22:6:3] **APPSC G1 2024**: "Coarseness increases seaward" is WRONG; deposits get finer seaward.
## SUB 7 | Coral Reefs
[22:7:0] - Built of **calcium carbonate** from coral **polyps**; polyps live with algae (**zooxanthellae**).
[22:7:1] - Need warm (rarely below ~18–20°C), **shallow, clear, sunlit, saline** water free of silt.
[22:7:2] TABLE head: Type | Form
  [22:7:2.0] Fringing | Platform close to the shore
  [22:7:2.1] Barrier | Farther out, with a deep lagoon/channel between reef and land
  [22:7:2.2] Atoll | Ring reef round a central lagoon, no central island
[22:7:3] - **Darwin (1842)** subsidence theory: fringing → barrier → atoll as land sinks.
[22:7:4] - **India**: **Gulf of Kachchh** (least diverse: turbidity), **Gulf of Mannar**, **Palk Bay** (fringing), **Andaman & Nicobar** (most extensive, richest), **Lakshadweep** (atolls).
## SUB 8 | Temperature, Density and Salinity
[22:8:0] - **Horizontal**: falls equator to poles; **NH oceans warmer** (more land). **Tropics: western side warmer** (trades pile warm water west, warm currents); **temperate: eastern side warmer** (westerlies, North Atlantic Drift). Annual range smaller in the **Pacific** than the Atlantic.
[22:8:1] - **Vertical**: warm surface layer (to ~**500 m**; 20–25°C in tropics) → **thermocline** (~500–1,000 m; rapid fall) → cold deep layer (0–4°C).
[22:8:2] - **Density** depends on temperature and salinity: colder and saltier = denser; cold water sinks to fill the deep.
[22:8:3] - Oceans absorb **over 90%** of excess heat; record heat content 2025.
[22:8:4] - **Salinity**: grams of salt per 1,000 g of water; ocean ~**35 ppt**; brackish **5–35 ppt**. Raised by **evaporation, freezing**; lowered by **rain, rivers, ice melt**. Highest in the **subtropics (20–30°)**; lower at the rainy equator.
[22:8:5] TABLE head: Water body | Salinity (‰) | Reason
  [22:8:5.0] Lake Van (Turkey) | 330 | Closed soda lake
  [22:8:5.1] Dead Sea | 238 | Closed, intense evaporation
  [22:8:5.2] Great Salt Lake (Utah) | 220 | Closed basin
  [22:8:5.3] Red Sea | ~41 | High evaporation
  [22:8:5.4] Black Sea | 17–18 | –
  [22:8:5.5] Baltic | Lowest (under ~10) | Many rivers, cold
[22:8:6] **APPSC G2 2023**: Lake Van > Dead Sea > Great Salt Lake.
[22:8:7] - **Deep Ocean Mission**: approved **June 2021**, **₹4,077 crore**, **MoES**; **MATSYA-6000** passed tests; mining machine **Varaha** trialled at **5,270 m**.

=== NOTES 4:23 | Physical and Chemical Characteristics of Ocean Water – Temperature and Salinity Distribution
## SUB 0 | Distribution of Earth's Water
[23:0:0] TABLE head: Store | Share | Rank
  [23:0:0.0] Oceans | About 97% (96.5%) (APPSC GS 2025) | 1
  [23:0:0.1] Polar ice and glaciers | About 2% (1.74%) | 2
  [23:0:0.2] Groundwater | About 1.7% | 3
  [23:0:0.3] Lakes, soil moisture, atmosphere | Tiny | 4
  [23:0:0.4] Rivers | 0.0002% (smallest) | Last
[23:0:1] - Descending order: **oceans > ice and glaciers > groundwater > rivers** (UPPSC 2025).
[23:0:2] - **Largest store of fresh water = glaciers and ice caps** (over 68%); next = **groundwater** (a little over 30%); surface water about 0.3%.
[23:0:3] Density of water is **maximum at 4°C**: a lake freezes at the top while the bottom stays liquid (anomalous behaviour).
## SUB 1 | Salinity – Basics
[23:1:0] - **Salinity** = total dissolved salts in sea water, in **parts per thousand (‰, ppt)** or **PSU** (Practical Salinity Units).
[23:1:1] - **Average 35‰** = 35 g of salt per 1 kg of sea water (by mass); open ocean 33–37‰.
[23:1:2] - **Isohaline** = line joining places of equal salinity.
[23:1:3] - **Main source of salts = land**: weathered rocks carried by **rivers**; volcanic / hydrothermal vents are a smaller source.
[23:1:4] - **Salinity reduces evaporation** (**APPSC GS 2018**); evaporation raises salinity.
## SUB 2 | Factors Controlling Salinity
[23:2:0] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [23:2:0.0] Evaporation | Raises
  [23:2:0.1] Precipitation | Lowers
  [23:2:0.2] River inflow | Lowers (Black Sea: Danube, Dnieper, Dniester)
  [23:2:0.3] Freezing | Raises
  [23:2:0.4] Ice melting | Lowers
  [23:2:0.5] Currents, winds | Redistribute saline water
  [23:2:0.6] Volcanoes | Add salts
## SUB 3 | Dissolved Salts

  [23:3:0.6] Magnesium bromide | 0.2%
[23:3:1] - By ions: **chloride and sodium** dominate, then sulphate and magnesium.

  [23:4:0.2] Polar seas | Lower | Low evaporation, ice melt, rivers

## SUB 5 | Temperature Distribution
[23:5:0] - **Horizontal**: falls from equator to poles; enclosed tropical seas warmest; **NH oceans warmer** (more land).
[23:5:1] - **Tropics: western side warmer** (trade winds pile warm water west; warm currents on east coasts of continents). **Temperate: eastern side warmer** (westerlies, North Atlantic Drift).
[23:5:2] - **Annual range smaller in the Pacific** than the Atlantic (vast; heats and cools slowly).
[23:5:3] - **Three layers**: warm surface (to ~500 m; 20–25°C in tropics) → **thermocline** (~500–1,000 m; rapid fall) → cold deep layer (0–4°C).
## SUB 6 | Density and "-cline" Layers
[23:6:0] - **Density rises** as **temperature falls**, **salinity rises**, and **pressure** (depth) increases.
[23:6:1] - Denser water sinks, lighter rises: drives deep currents; cold polar water sinks and creeps towards the equator.
[23:6:2] TABLE head: Layer | Rapid change with depth in | Prefix
  [23:6:2.0] Thermocline | Temperature | thermo = heat
  [23:6:2.1] Halocline | Salinity | halo = salt
  [23:6:2.2] Pycnocline | Density; drives deep circulation | pycno = density
  [23:6:2.3] Chemocline | Chemistry (oxygen / hydrogen sulphide); Black Sea (meromictic: layers rarely mix) | –
[23:6:3] - Every cline is a **barrier to vertical mixing**.
## SUB 7 | Ocean Heat and Chemistry in News
[23:7:0] - Oceans absorb **over 90% of excess heat** and about a quarter of human CO2 → **acidification**; **pH ~8.1** and falling.
[23:7:1] - **2025**: record ocean heat content (about **23 zettajoules** more); about **90%** of the ocean surface saw marine heatwaves (WMO).
[23:7:2] - **AP**: **NIOT**'s satellite station at **Nellore** runs **OMNI buoys**: real-time temperature, salinity, currents for **cyclone and IOD** forecasts.

## SUB 2 | Tides – Causes, Types and Timing
[24:2:0] # Causes
[24:2:1] - Gravitational pull of the **Moon (greater)** and Sun (lesser); **centrifugal force** counters gravity.
[24:2:2] - **Direct tide** = bulge facing the Moon (gravity); **indirect tide** = opposite side (centrifugal force).
[24:2:3] - Tidal water channelled into bays/estuaries forms **tidal currents**.

  [24:2:5.1] Moon phase | New / full moon | First / third quarter
  [24:2:5.2] Tidal range | Maximum | Minimum (high tide lower, low tide higher)
[24:2:6] # Types and Timing
[24:2:7] TABLE head: Type | Feature
  [24:2:7.0] Semi-diurnal | Two highs + two lows a day (most common)
  [24:2:7.1] Diurnal | One high + one low a day
  [24:2:7.2] Mixed | Unequal heights
[24:2:8] - High tides recur about every **12 h 25 min**, so high tide comes about **50 minutes** later each day (a Chennai 8.00 am high tide comes later next day).

## SUB 4 | Ocean Currents – Drivers
[24:4:0] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [24:4:0.0] Planetary winds | Main driver; trades → equatorial currents west; westerlies → drifts east
  [24:4:0.1] Coriolis force | Deflects right in N, left in S; coastlines and floor relief also steer
  [24:4:0.2] Solar heating | Water expands near equator → slope
  [24:4:0.3] Temperature, salinity | Cold, salty water sinks at poles, moves slowly to equator (thermohaline)
[24:4:1] - **Equatorial Counter Current** flows **west to east** between the N and S Equatorial Currents through the **doldrums**, because their convergence piles water in the west.
[24:4:2] - **Warm** currents: equator → poles, on **east coasts** (low-mid latitudes); **cold**: poles → equator, on **west coasts**. Currents keep Earth's heat balance.
