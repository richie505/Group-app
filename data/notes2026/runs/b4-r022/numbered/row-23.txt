## SUB 0 | Salinity – Meaning, Factors and Distribution
[0:0] # Basics
[0:1] - **Salinity** = total dissolved salts in sea water: grams of salt per **1,000 g (1 kg)** of water, in **‰ / ppt** or PSU. **Average 35‰** (35 g per kg); open ocean 33–37‰; **Indian Ocean average 35‰**, Atlantic about 36‰.
[0:2] - **Isohaline** = line joining places of equal salinity. Brackish water: about 5–35 ppt (NCERT upper limit 24.7‰).
[0:3] - **Main source of salts = land** (weathered rocks), **carried by rivers**; volcanoes add a little. Salinity **reduces evaporation**.
[0:4] TABLE head: Factor | Effect
  [0:4.0] Evaporation | Raises
  [0:4.1] Freezing (salt left behind) | Raises
  [0:4.2] Precipitation | Lowers
  [0:4.3] River inflow | Lowers (Black Sea: Danube, Dnieper, Dniester)
  [0:4.4] Ice melting | Lowers
  [0:4.5] Currents, winds | Redistribute
[0:5] # Horizontal distribution
[0:6] TABLE head: Region | Salinity | Reason
  [0:6.0] Equator | Relatively lower | Heavy rain
  [0:6.1] Subtropics (about 20°–30°/40° N and S) | Highest – near the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn | Strong evaporation, little rain (UPPSC 2023 true statement)
  [0:6.2] Polar seas | Lower | Low evaporation, ice melt, rivers – so "salinity is high in polar regions" is false, though freezing does raise the salinity of the water left (CDS 2009: A false, R true)

## SUB 1 | Distribution of Earth's Water
[1:0] TABLE head: Store | Share of all water | Rank
  [1:0.0] Oceans | About 97% (96.5%) | 1
  [1:0.1] Polar ice and glaciers | About 1.74% (about 68–70% of fresh water) | 2

[1:1] - Descending order: **oceans > ice and glaciers > groundwater > rivers** (UPPSC 2025: 1, 3, 4, 2).
[1:2] - **Largest fresh-water store = glaciers and ice caps**; of the rest, the **largest = groundwater** (UPSC 2013, asked twice); groundwater far exceeds lakes and rivers, and **ice caps hold more than groundwater** (UPSC 2021: statement 2 only).
[1:3] - Fresh water = about 2.5–3%; usable fresh water under 1%. The **total amount of water on Earth is constant** (water cycle); it is not vanishing and the oceans are not getting saltier because they are drying.
[1:4] - **2003** = UN International Year of Freshwater.

## SUB 2 | Density, Clines and Water's Anomalous Behaviour
[2:0] - Water is **densest at 4°C**: a lake freezes at the **top** while the bottom stays liquid at about 4°C (UPSC 2011: density maximum at 4°C).
[2:1] - **Density of sea water rises** as **temperature falls**, **salinity rises**, and **pressure (depth)** increases; colder and saltier water sinks and drives deep currents.
[2:2] - **Sea water is denser than fresh water**: swimming is easier in sea water (CDS 2008: A and R true, R explains A); an egg sinks in tap water but **floats in strong salt solution** because the solution is denser than the egg; people float in the Dead Sea.
[2:3] TABLE head: Layer | Rapid change with depth in
  [2:3.0] Thermocline | Temperature
  [2:3.1] Halocline | Salinity (salinity gradient; asked twice)
  [2:3.2] Pycnocline | Density; drives deep circulation
  [2:3.3] Chemocline | Chemistry; Black Sea (layers rarely mix)
[2:4] - Every cline is a **barrier to vertical mixing**.

## SUB 3 | Temperature of Ocean Water
[3:0] - Oceans are **store-houses** of solar energy: they heat and cool slowly and absorb **over 90%** of the excess heat of global warming, and about a quarter of human CO2 (ocean **pH ~8.1** and falling).
[3:1] - **Horizontal**: falls from equator to poles (about 0.5°C per degree of latitude); highest just north of the equator; **NH oceans warmer** (more land; averages 19°C vs 16°C).
[3:2] - **Tropics: western side warmer** (trade winds pile warm water west); **temperate zone: eastern side warmer** (westerlies, North Atlantic Drift) – UPSC 2021: **both statements correct**.
[3:3] - **Annual range smaller in the Pacific** than the Atlantic (vast) and **greater in the Northern Hemisphere** than the Southern (UPSC 2007: only II).
[3:4] - **Vertical, three layers**: warm surface layer (about **500 m**, 20–25°C) → **thermocline** (**500–1,000 m**, rapid fall) → cold deep layer (**0–4°C**); about 90% of ocean water lies below the thermocline.
[3:5] - **Blue colour of sea water** = water absorbs red light and **scatters** blue (molecular scattering, C.V. Raman 1921), not interference.
[3:6] - **2025**: record ocean heat content; about **90%** of the ocean surface saw marine heatwaves (WMO). **AP**: NIOT's station at **Nellore** runs **OMNI buoys** (temperature, salinity, currents).
[3:7] ~~ Sources: NCERT Fundamentals ch.12 (OnlyIAS Physical Geography 08 p.20–22); Rocket General Geography p.62; Nobel Prize Physics 1930 citation (Raman); WMO State of the Climate 2025; app notes 4:23 sub 5, 7
