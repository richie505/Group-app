=== Ocean Currents of the World – Warm and Cold
- **Warm** currents flow from the equator towards the poles, usually on the **east coasts** of continents in low and middle latitudes; **cold** currents flow towards the equator, usually on the **west coasts** (NCERT).
| Current | Type | Ocean / region |
|---|---|---|
| **Gulf Stream** | **Warm** | **North Atlantic**; starts in the Gulf of Mexico as the **Florida Current**; poleward, warms the US east coast and NW Europe |
| North Atlantic Drift, Norwegian, Florida | Warm | North Atlantic |
| North Equatorial, North Pacific Drift | Warm | Tropics / North Pacific |
| **Kuroshio** | **Warm** | **North Pacific** (Japan) – the Pacific twin of the Gulf Stream |
| **Brazil** | **Warm** | South Atlantic |
| **Agulhas, Mozambique** | **Warm** | Indian Ocean (SE Africa) |
| **East Australian** | **Warm** | South Pacific |
| Caribbean | Warm | Caribbean Sea |
| **Labrador** | **Cold** | North Atlantic (NE Canada) |
| **Canary** | **Cold** | **North Atlantic** (NW Africa) – not Pacific, not South Atlantic |
| **Benguela** | **Cold** | **South Atlantic** (Namibia) – not North Atlantic |
| **Falkland** | **Cold** | South Atlantic |
| **Humboldt (Peru)** | **Cold** | **SE Pacific** (Peru–Chile) |
| **California** | **Cold** | NE Pacific |
| **Oyashio (Kurile), Kamchatka** | **Cold** | **North Pacific** |
| **West Australian** | **Cold** | Indian Ocean |
| West Wind Drift / **South Atlantic Drift** | Cold | Southern Ocean / South Atlantic |
- Odd ones: **Humboldt is NOT warm** (APPSC G1); **Agulhas is NOT cold**; **Kuroshio is NOT cold**; **Canary is NOT warm** and **not in the Pacific**; **Benguela does not flow in the North Atlantic**; **Peruvian** is the only Pacific current among Benguela, Brazil, South Equatorial.
- Pairs of warm currents: **North Atlantic Drift and Brazil**; all cold: Humboldt and Benguela; Canaries and Benguela.
- NDA 2011 match: Kuroshio – warm Pacific; Peru – cold Pacific; Labrador – cold Atlantic; Florida – warm Atlantic (**3, 4, 2, 1**).
- The **Gulf Stream does not sustain West Pacific coral reefs** (CDS 2017).
- **Falkland Islands** are in the **South Atlantic**, not the Indian Ocean.

=== Waves and Tsunamis
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Wavelength** | Distance between two successive crests – two consecutive points of the **same phase** |
| Wave height / amplitude | Trough to crest / half the height |
| Period / frequency | Time between crests / waves per second |
| Swells | Slow, steady, long-period waves from distant storms |
| Breakers | Waves break when depth < **half the wavelength** |
| Rip current | Narrow, fast channel flowing away from the shore |
| Kallakkadal | Kerala swell surge from far southern storms |
- Waves carry **energy, not water**; wind gives them energy.
# Tsunami
- Japanese "**harbour wave**". Caused by sudden **displacement of the sea floor**: **tectonic activity**, chiefly **fault movement in undersea earthquakes** (most important cause), also submarine landslides and volcanoes – not wind, the Moon or sunspots.
- Speed depends on depth (about √(g × depth)): **faster in deep water** (over 700 km/h), so in the open sea it is a **gentle rise and fall** and a **ship in the deep sea is not affected**.
- Near the shore it **slows down** and grows to **enormous height** (NDA 2013: I and II) – so its impact is greater at the coast; "speed is more in shallow water" is **false** (APPSC FRO 2022: A true, R false).
- **Warning sign**: the sea **recedes far, exposing the sea floor**. Mitigation: build houses on **elevated sites**.
- **26 Dec 2004** (Sumatra quake, M 9.1): in India the **Coromandel coast** (Tamil Nadu) was hit hardest; Indira Point was submerged.
- **ITEWS** (INCOIS, **Hyderabad**, since 2007) confirms a tsunami with **bottom pressure recorders** in the deep ocean (seismic stations detect only the quake; tide gauges are coastal). Tide gauges, BPRs and seismic networks are the working tools; satellite observation is not an effective real-time tsunami warning system (APPSC key).
| Institution | Role |
|---|---|
| **INCOIS, Hyderabad** | Runs ITEWS; **PFZ** advisories; **SAMUDRA** app |
| IMD | Cyclone and multi-hazard warnings |
| **NIOT, Chennai** | **OMNI buoys** (temperature, salinity, currents); station at **Nellore** (AP) |
| RTGS Vijayawada (AP) | Cyclone, high-wave and fish-shoal alerts to fishermen |

=== Tides
# Causes
- Tides = periodic rise and fall of sea level, once or twice a day, caused by the gravitational pull of the **Sun and Moon on the Earth**; the **Moon's pull is greater** (nearer). **Centrifugal force** balances gravity.
- **Direct tide** = bulge facing the Moon (gravity); **indirect tide** = opposite side (centrifugal force).
- Earth rotating west to east is true but does not explain tides (CDS 2018: both true, II not the explanation).
- **Surges** = irregular, weather-driven (winds, pressure) – not tides; sea water spreading over nearby low land = coastal **inundation** ("oceanic logging").
# Spring and neap tides
| Basis | Spring tide | Neap tide |
|---|---|---|
| Sun–Earth–Moon | In a straight line | At right angles |
| Moon phase | **New and full moon** | **First and third quarter** (not new moon) |
| Height | About **20% higher** than normal | About 20% lower |
| Tidal range | Maximum | **Minimum: high tide lower, low tide higher** |
- **Tidal range** = difference between high and low tide. **Perigean** tides: unusually high when the Moon is closest.
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| Semi-diurnal | Two highs + two lows a day (most common) |
| Diurnal | One high + one low |
| Mixed | Unequal heights |
- High tides recur about every **12 h 25 min**, so about **50 minutes** later each day (not exactly every 12 h).
# Tidal places and energy
| Place | Feature |
|---|---|
| Bay of Fundy (Canada) | World's greatest tidal range |
| **Gulf of Khambhat (Bhavnagar)**, Gujarat | 5–10 m; about 7,000 MW; India's **most potential tidal-energy area** |
| Gulf of Kutch | 5–8 m |
| Sundarbans | About 5 m |
| **Chandipur (Odisha)** | Sea **recedes a few km at ebb tide** twice a day; you can walk on the sea floor (UPSC 2017) |
- Gulfs with **wide fronts and narrow rears** get the highest tides (so "narrow fronts and wider rears" is wrong, UPSC 2004); a tidal **bore** forms in narrow, shallow estuaries; the Hooghly's tides matter to **Kolkata port**.
- Tidal energy: **barrage** (tidal range) or **underwater turbines** (tidal stream).

=== Effects of Currents – Fishing Grounds, Climate and Upwelling
- **Best fishing grounds** form **where warm and cold ocean currents meet** (mixing brings nutrients and plankton), mainly between **40° and 60°** – asked 4 times (APPSC G1 2002 twice, UPSC 2013 twice).
| Place | Current(s) | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Grand Banks** (SE of Newfoundland, **east coast of North America**) | **Labrador (cold) + Gulf Stream (warm)** | Fishing ground; **dense fog** |
| Off Japan | Kuroshio + Oyashio | Fishing ground |
| **NW Europe, Norway, Murmansk** | **North Atlantic Drift** | **Ice-free ports**, mild winters; warm air meeting polar air brings rain to Western Europe |
| Peru–Chile | **Humboldt** | **Upwelling** of cold water; fisheries; **Atacama** desert |
| Namibia | Benguela | Namib, Kalahari deserts |
| California | California | Sonoran desert |
| Somalia | Findlater / Somali | Upwelling |
- The Labrador Current meets the Gulf Stream at the Grand Banks, **not near Vancouver Island** (NDA 2010: I and II only).
- **Cold currents** on west coasts help form **arid** climates; warm currents warm the coasts beside them; currents keep Earth's **heat balance**.

=== Causes of Ocean Currents
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Planetary winds** | **Main driver**; trades drive equatorial currents west, westerlies drive drifts east |
| **Coriolis force** | Deflects **right in the NH, left in the SH** → gyres |
| Solar heating | Water expands near the equator → slight slope |
| **Temperature, salinity (density)** | **Saltier and colder water is denser**; cold polar water sinks and creeps slowly to the equator (**thermohaline**) |
| Coastline shape, floor relief | Deflect / steer currents (a landmass does not always obstruct them) |
- **Revolution of the Earth is NOT a cause** (CDS 2013).
- Ocean currents = large masses of surface water circulating in regular patterns; the slow **surface movement** of water, set in motion **mainly by prevailing winds**, help keep the **heat balance**, are affected by the **configuration of the ocean** – UPSC 2002: **all four** statements correct.
- **Equatorial Counter Current** flows **west to east** between the North and South Equatorial Currents (through the doldrums) because their **convergence piles up water in the west** (UPSC 2015, asked twice).
- Surface currents = upper 400 m, about 10% of ocean water; deep currents = 90%.
- Waves = oscillations moving energy; tides = periodic vertical rise and fall; **currents = continuous horizontal flow** with major effects on **climate, fisheries and navigation**; currents **redistribute heat**, so they influence climate.

=== Gyres and the Sargasso Sea
| Gyre | Components |
|---|---|
| North Atlantic | N Equatorial → Gulf Stream → N Atlantic Drift → **Canary** |
| South Atlantic | S Equatorial → **Brazil** → **West Wind Drift** → **Benguela** (no Canary) |
| North Pacific | N Equatorial → Kuroshio → N Pacific Drift → California |
| South Pacific | S Equatorial → East Australian → West Wind Drift → **Humboldt** |
| South Indian | S Equatorial → Agulhas → West Wind Drift → West Australian |
- Gyres turn **clockwise in the NH**, anticlockwise in the SH.
- **Sargasso Sea** (**North Atlantic**): the **only sea with no coastline** – bounded by currents: **Gulf Stream (W), North Atlantic Drift (N), Canary (E), North Equatorial (S)**; calm, warm, salty; named after its floating **Sargassum** weed (**typical marine vegetation**).

=== Indian Ocean Currents
- **North Indian Ocean** currents **reverse with the monsoon** (**monsoon drift**) – the reason the Indian Ocean's currents change direction (UPSC 1997, asked twice); the South Indian Ocean keeps a permanent anticlockwise gyre.
| Season | Bay of Bengal / Arabian Sea | WICC | EICC |
|---|---|---|---|
| SW monsoon (summer) | Clockwise | Southward | Northward |
| NE monsoon (winter) | Anticlockwise | **Northward (poleward), November–February** | Southward |
- **Findlater Current / Somali Jet**: western **Arabian Sea**; upwelling off the **Horn of Africa**.
