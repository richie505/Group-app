[1:5] # Rudradaman – Junagadh (Girnar)
[1:6] - **Junagadh rock inscription of Rudradaman I** (c. 150 CE): **first long inscription in chaste Sanskrit**, **earliest specimen of Kavya style**; Brahmi; records his **achievements**, his conflict with the Satavahanas and his **repair of the Sudarshana lake without forced labour**.
[1:7] - **Sudarshana lake:** built under **Chandragupta Maurya** by governor **Pushyagupta**, canals under **Ashoka** (Tushaspha) → the inscription **names both Chandragupta and Ashoka** and **proves Chandragupta's sway over western India**.
[1:8] - **Same rock** also carries **Ashoka's 14 major rock edicts** (boulder near Uparkot fort) and a **Skandagupta** record (Parnadatta repaired the lake).
[1:9] - **⚠ Trap:** Rudradaman = **Junagadh**, not Nanaghat (Naganika).
[1:10] # Ashoka's edicts – what they mention
[1:11] - **Maski** (Karnataka): first edict naming "**Asoka**" (minor rock edict).
[1:12] - **Kalinga war:** told in **Major Rock Edict XIII** (not at Maski); Hathigumpha records Kharavela's own wars, not Ashoka's victory.
[1:13] - **Southern kingdoms named (RE II, XIII):** **Chola, Pandya, Satiyaputra, Keralaputra** – the **Satavahanas are NOT mentioned** (they rose after Ashoka).
[1:14] - **13th Major Rock Edict:** lists the **Andhras** among peoples inside the Mauryan empire.
[1:15] - **Yerragudi** (Kurnool): proves Mauryan rule in Andhra.

## SUB 2 | Scripts, Language of the Earliest Inscriptions and Decipherment
[2:0] # Language of the earliest inscriptions
[2:1] - **Earliest stone inscriptions = Ashoka's edicts**, in the **Prakrit language**, mostly in the **Brahmi script** – "Prakrit in Brahmi" (Brahmi is a script, not a language; not Sanskrit or Pali).
[2:2] - **Prakrit** = language of the earliest stone inscriptions and of the earliest AP inscriptions.
[2:3] # Scripts
[2:4] TABLE head: Script | Use / region | Decipherment
  [2:4.0] Brahmi | Most Ashokan edicts (Prakrit); left to right; parent of the Telugu script | James Prinsep, 1837, from Ashokan inscriptions
  [2:4.1] Kharosthi | Right to left; north-west (Gandhara); Ashokan Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra (Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, near Peshawar) – Prakrit in Kharosthi | Prinsep and others (Lassen, Norris), using bilingual Indo-Greek coins
  [2:4.2] Greek and Aramaic | Ashoka at Kandahar | –
  [2:4.3] Tamil-Brahmi (Tamizhi) | Earliest Tamil script, c. 3rd c. BCE | Mangulam, Sittanavasal, Arittapatti (Pandya region)
[2:5] - **Statement (UKPSC 2025):** "most Ashokan edicts are Prakrit in Brahmi" ✓ and "Mansehra, Shahbazgarhi are Prakrit in Kharosthi" ✓ → **both**.
[2:6] - **⚠ Trap:** Ashokan edicts are **not** in Sanskrit or Devanagari.
[2:7] # Decipherment
[2:8] - **James Prinsep** (Asiatic Society secretary) first deciphered **Brahmi (1837)** and read Ashoka's edicts – not Buhler, Max Muller, William Jones, Cunningham or A.H. Dani.
[2:9] - **Read from:** short inscriptions cut in **stone** (Sanchi donative records, rock and pillar edicts) – exam key "**stone tablets**"; coins helped only for Kharosthi.
[2:10] - **Ashoka identified:** George Turnour matched "Devanampiya Piyadasi" with Ashoka from the Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa.
[2:11] - **Pre-Ashokan Brahmi** (c. 4th c. BCE): **Anuradhapura** (Sri Lanka), Kodumanal and Keezhadi (Tamil Nadu); also Piprahwa, Sohgaura, Mahasthan.

## SUB 7 | Types of Sources and Dating Methods
[7:0] - Three groups: **archaeological** (inscriptions, coins, monuments, sites), **literary** (religious and secular texts) and **foreign accounts**.
[7:1] - **Inscriptions** are the **most reliable** source; foreign accounts only **supplement** Indian sources.
[7:2] - **Earliest land-grant records:** **Satavahana**.
[7:3] TABLE head: Method | Principle
  [7:3.0] Stratigraphy | Superposition: lower layer is older
  [7:3.1] Radiocarbon (C-14) | Decay of C-14 in organic remains; half-life about 5,730 years
  [7:3.2] Dendrochronology | Counting annual tree rings
  [7:3.3] Thermoluminescence | Light given on heating pottery/burnt material
