The Fertilizers Notes Fertilizers : • Fertilizer is any material of natural or synthetic origin that is applied to soils or to plant tissues to supply one or more plant nutrients essential to the growth of plants. • Fertilizers are used by farmers and families to help crops and gardens grow. • With the global population steadily growing, it is necessary that enough crops are produced each year to provide adequate food, clothing and other agricultural products to people around the world. These crops deplete soil nutrients in different ways and rates. • Fertilizers play an important role in providing crops with the nutrients they need to grow and be harvested for nutritious food. Manure : • Manure is an organic material that is used to fertilize land, usually consisting of the feces and urine of domestic animals, with or without accompanying litter such as straw, hay, etc. • Animals manure is less rich in nitrogen, phosphorus and potash than synthetic fertilizers and hence must be applied in much greater quantities than the latter. • Manure is rich in organic matter and improves the soil's capacity to absorb water and store water, thus helping reduce runoff and erosion. • Much of the potassium and nitrogen in manure can be lost through leaching, if the material is exposed to rainfall before being applied to the field. These nutrient losses may be minimized/reduced by such methods as stacking manure under cover or in pits to prevent leaching. • Compost is a managed aerobic decomposition product of manure. • Green manure is a type of cover crop, grown to add nutrients and organic matter to the soil. Typically, a green manure crop is grown for a specific period of time, and then ploughed under and incorporated into the soil while it is green or shortly after flowering. • The legumes (mainly pea family plants) used as green manuring crops have nitrogen fixing symbiotic bacteria in their root nodules that fix atmospheric nitrogen in a form that plant can use as well as providing organic matter (humus) to the soil. Such plants are clover and vetch. • Continuous use of chemical fertilizers on soil can destroy soil fertility because the organic matter in the soil is not replenished. As a result, the food produced in these soils may have less vitamins and nutrient contents. The number of microorganisms in soil may decrease. Repeated ammonium-based nitrogen fertilizers may acidify soil. So the application of organic manure has been recommended along with artificial manure. Vermicompost : • Nowadays vermicompost is widely used as an organic soil amendment. The most significant benefit of vermicompost is that the nutrients in it are generally very easily absorbed by the roots of plants. Vermicompost is not easily flushed from the soil because of the worm mucus that it contains. Plants have longer time to obtain the nutrients and get the maximum benefit.
