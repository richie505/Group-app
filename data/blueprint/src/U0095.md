### Nayankara system and military organisation — features
## 1. Nayankara system
- Definition: Military commanders (Nayakas) made administrators of Sthalas/Mandalams; each protected a specific fort
- Who introduced it: Prataparudra II (APPSC 2025 key) → reorganised the kingdom into nayakships
- Other notes: Rudrama Devi began it in Ganapatideva's time; Prataparudra strengthened it
- Number: 72 Nayankaras, each guarding a fort; other notes: 75 (or 77) nayakships/principalities
- Not in your sources: the number of bastions of the Warangal fort tied to the Nayakas.
- Land instead of salary:
- Villages called Nayakasthalam
- Nayankaras controlled one-fourth (1/4) of the kingdom's land
- Collected revenue → kept a share for a fixed army for the king → paid fixed annual sum/tribute, remitting the rest
- Sources: Baddena's Neeti Sastra Muktavali; Ekamranatha's Pratapa Charitra
- Legacy: Continued by Vijayanagara as Amara Nayankara / Amara-Nayankara system
- Land = Amaram; tribute = Kappam/kist
- Its break-up → Poligars
- Exam angle: statement questions on "who introduced the Nayankara system" → Prataparudra II.
## 2. Military organisation
- Chaturanga bala: Four arms
- Prataparudra's army = 100 elephants, 20,000 cavalry, 900,000 infantry
- Two divisions: Mula Balam (king's standing army) + Nayankara army
- Fort types:
- Giridurga (hill), Jaladurga (water), Vanadurga (forest), Sthaladurga (plain)
- Warangal fort = concentric walls + moat
- ⚠ Mula Balam = king's own army; Nayankara army = Nayakas' contingents

## 3. Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam
- Issuer & date: Ganapatideva, 1244 CE, at Motupalli; also called Motupalli pillar inscription / Abhaya Sasana
- Purpose:
- Safety to foreign merchants
- Remitted arbitrary taxes; stopped harassment by officials
- Compensation for losses from shipwreck or theft
- Official: Siddayya Devudu posted at Motupalli (APPSC PYQ)
- Do not confuse:
- Later bilingual (Telugu-Tamil) Motupalli protection charter of Anavota Reddi (Reddi period)
- Motupalli Dharma Sasanam (1416) of Vijayanagara's Deva Raya I, after he took Motupalli from Kondavidu
- Exam angle: "Abhaya" = assurance of safety; issuer Ganapatideva, not Rudrama.
- ⚠ 1244 Abhaya Sasanam = Ganapatideva; 1416 Dharma Sasanam = Deva Raya I
