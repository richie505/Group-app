# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:76 - Pallavas - Simhavishnu, Mahendravarman I, Narasimhavarman I Mamalla, Nandivarman II
### Origin & territory
- Manchikallu inscription of Simhavarman proves Pallavas replaced the Ikshvakus of Vijayapuri
- Territory: Tondaimandalam (APPSC-GS 2023); capital Kanchipuram
- Origin theories: Parthian (Pahlava); Andhra (earliest Prakrit charters, Krishna-Guntur); local Tamil
- Early Pallavas replaced Ikshvakus in Krishna valley
### Charters → ruler
| Charter | Ruler | Point |
| Mayidavolu plates | Sivaskandavarman (yuvaraja) | Prakrit; earliest records |
| Manchikallu | Simhavarman | Conquest of Vijayapuri from Ikshvakus |
| Kudumiamalai | Mahendravarman I | Music inscription |
### Rulers & events
- Early — Sivaskandavarman: Kanchi under Pallavas; Vishnugopa: faced Samudragupta
- 575-600 — Simhavishnu: founder of imperial line; defeated Kalabhras
- 600-630 — Mahendravarman I: wars with Pulakesin II; cave temples
- 642 — Narasimhavarman I killed Pulakesin II (Manimangala), took Vatapi via Paranjoti
- c. 731 — Nandivarman II king at 12; lost Kanchi to Vikramaditya II
- 897 — Aditya I Chola killed Aparajita - end
### Titles → ruler

| Vichitrachitta, Mattavilasa, Gunabhara, Chitrakarapuli | Mahendravarman I |
| Mamalla, Vatapikonda | Narasimhavarman I |
| Pallavamalla | Nandivarman II |
### Literature
| Book | Author |

### Religion & temples
- Mahendravarman I
- Rock-cut: Mandagapattu (no brick, timber, metal, mortar)
- Narasimhavarman I
- Monolithic rathas, Mahabalipuram
- Two naval expeditions to Sri Lanka (Manavarma)
- Nandivarman II
- Vaishnava; Vaikuntha Perumal, Kanchi
- Contemporary of Alvar Tirumangai
### Kanchi & legacy
- Mayurasharman insulted at Kanchi ghatika → founded Kadamba kingdom (Talagunda)
- Hiuen Tsang visited Kanchi c. 640 CE
- Grantha script = parent of Southeast Asian scripts
- Renati Chodas = early Pallava feudatories
### Exam corner
- APPSC-G2 2025: Manchikallu & Maidavolu = Pallavas
- APPSC-G1 2024 match: Sivaskandavarman-Kanchi; Vishnugopa-Samudragupta; Narasimhavarman I-Vatapi; Nandivarman II-Vikramaditya II
- APPSC-G1 2012: Mattavilasa Prahasana = Mahendravarman I
- ⚠ Appar → Mahendravarman I; Tirumangai → Nandivarman II
### Pallavas → origin, territory, period and capital
- Territory: Tondaimandalam (APPSC-GS 2023); capital Kanchipuram
- Origin theories:
- Parthian (Pahlava) foreign origin
- Andhra origin - earliest charters (Prakrit) from Krishna-Guntur region; early Pallavas replaced Ikshvakus there
- Local Tamil origin in Tondaimandalam
### Inscriptions and charters → ruler and point
| Charter | Ruler | Point |
| Mayidavolu (Maidavolu) plates | Sivaskandavarman (as yuvaraja) | Prakrit; among the earliest Pallava records; issued from Kanchipura [GK] |
| Hirahadagalli plates | Sivaskandavarman | Prakrit; he performed Agnishtoma, Vajapeya, Ashvamedha[GK] |
| Manchikallu inscription | Simhavarman | records the Pallava conquest of Vijayapuri after defeating the Ikshvakus |
| Kudumiamalai music inscription | Mahendravarman I | credited to his patronage |
- End of Ikshvakus: Last Ikshvaku Rudrapurushadatta defeated by Pallava Simhavarma (Manchikallu) → Pallava rule in Krishna valley
- PYQs:
- APPSC-G2 2025: Manchikallu and Maidavolu inscriptions = Pallavas
- APPSC-GS 2018: Manchikallu inscription of Simhavarman proves Pallavas replaced Ikshvakus of Vijayapuri
- Loss recorded: Krishna-Godavari lands of Mahendravarman I to Pulakesin II (Maruturu, Kopparam inscriptions)
### Pallava rulers → period and political event
## 1. Ruler → event
| Ruler | Event |
| Sivaskandavarman | Kanchi came under the Pallavas |
| Vishnugopa | faced Samudragupta's invasion (Allahabad pillar) |
| Simhavishnu (c. 575-600) [GK] | founder of the imperial line; defeated the Kalabhras, extended rule to the Kaveri [GK] |
| Mahendravarman I (600-630) | wars with Pulakesin II; cave temples |
| Narasimhavarman I (630-668) | defeated Pulakesin II, took Vatapi (642) |
| Nandivarman II | defeated by Vikramaditya II, who took Kanchi |
- APPSC-G1 2024 match: Sivaskandavarman-II, Vishnugopa-I, Narasimhavarman I-IV, Nandivarman II-III
- Main line: Simhavishnu → Mahendravarman I → Narasimhavarman I → Paramesvaravarman I → Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha) → Nandivarman II
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes place Simhavishnu in the 3rd century - he belongs to the late 6th
## 2. Key reigns
- Mahendravarman I: c. 600-630 CE; son of Simhavishnu; many-sided - painting, music, literature
- Narasimhavarman I - Chalukya war: Defeated and killed Pulakesin II (Manimangala, 642); sacked Vatapi via general Paranjoti
- Narasimhavarman I - Sri Lanka: Two naval expeditions to restore ally prince Manavarma
- Nandivarman II - accession: Nandivarman II Pallavamalla - throne as a boy of 12; chosen by ministers + nobles from a collateral line after Paramesvaravarman II died (c. 731)
- Nandivarman II - setback: Lost Kanchi temporarily to Vikramaditya II (Chalukya)
## 3. LAST - later Pallavas and end
- Later rulers: Dantivarman, Nandivarman III, Nripatunga, Aparajita
- End: Aditya I Chola killed Aparajita (c. 897-898 CE; dates given as 897 or 898)

## 2. Ghatika and scholars
- Mayurasharman: Brahmin student at Kanchi ghatika, insulted by a Pallava horseman → took up arms → founded Kadamba kingdom (Talagunda inscription)
- Hiuen Tsang: Visited Kanchi c. 640 CE; praised Narasimhavarman with Harsha and Pulakesin II
### Religion and temple building → ruler
- Mahendravarman I - religion: Began as a Jain → converted to Shaivism by Nayanar Appar (Tirunavukkarasar); staunch Shaivite thereafter
- Mahendravarman I - cave temples: Start of rock-cut temples in South India
- Mandagapattu - temple "without brick, timber, metal or mortar"
- Narasimhavarman I - architecture: Monolithic rathas at Mahabalipuram; staunch Shaivite
- Nandivarman II - religion: Ardent Vaishnavite; built Vaikuntha Perumal temple, Kanchi; contemporary of Alvar Tirumangai
- ⚠ Appar converted Mahendravarman I (Shaiva); Tirumangai = contemporary of Nandivarman II (Vaishnava)
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Pioneers of Dravida temple art
- Grantha script = parent of Southeast Asian scripts
- Bhakti movement (Alvars, Nayanars) grew under them
- AP angle: Early Pallava charters (Mayidavolu, Manchikallu) in Andhra; Renati Chodas = early Pallava feudatories
