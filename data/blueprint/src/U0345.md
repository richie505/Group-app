### CAG — official name, status and the 1971 Act
## 1. Status
- Full name: Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (not "Controller", not "Attorney")
- Part V, Chapter V — Articles 148 to 151; an independent constitutional authority
- Guardian of the Public Purse / Guardian of Public Finance; head of the Indian Audit and Accounts Department
- Dr. B.R. Ambedkar: The most important officer under the Constitution
- Four bulwarks of democracy: CAG, Supreme Court, UPSC, Election Commission of India
## 2. The 1971 Act and the 1976 change
- CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act: 1971
- 1976 departmentalisation: Accounting separated from auditing — CAG relieved of preparing Union accounts from 1 March 1976 (the 1976 amendment, 1976)
- Union accounts now compiled by the Central Government Accounts authority under the Ministry of Finance; CAG still compiles and maintains the accounts of most States (CAG still keeps most state accounts)
### CAG — Articles 148 to 151
## 1. Duties and powers — Articles 149 to 151
- Article 148: Appointment, oath, tenure, removal, post-retirement ban, salary and charged expenses (oath, conditions of service)
- Article 149: Duties and powers — audits the receipts, expenditures, stores and accounts of the Union and the States
- Article 150: Form of accounts — prescribed by the President on the advice of the CAG (the CAG does not prescribe it unilaterally); covers the Form of accounts of Union and states
- Article 151(1): Union audit reports → President → laid before both Houses of Parliament
- Article 151(2): State audit reports → Governor → laid before the State Legislature
- Ultimate responsibility for taking action on the findings rests with Parliament (Union reports) / the State Legislature (State reports)

### CAG — appointment, tenure and removal
## 1. Appointment and oath — Article 148
- Appointed by: The President — Article 148(1), by warrant under his hand and seal
- ⚠ Not the Prime Minister, the Finance Minister or the Public Accounts Committee
- Oath: Before the President, form in the Third Schedule
## 2. Tenure and removal
- Tenure: Six years or 65 years of age, whichever is earlier — a dual tenure cap under the CAG Act, 1971 and Article 148(3) (⚠ not five years / 62 years)
- 6 years or age 65, whichever earlier (APPSC 2022 key)
- Longest fixed term among President, VP, MP (APPSC-G2 2017)
- Removal: By the President through the parliamentary removal process — Article 148(1) read with Article 124(4): same grounds and manner as a Supreme Court Judge (like a Supreme Court judge)
- Address by both Houses, passed by a special majority, on proved misbehaviour or incapacity
- Does not hold office at the President's pleasure
- After retirement — Article 148(4): Complete bar — no further office under the Government of India or of any State
- Contrast: the UPSC Chairman also cannot take another job; judges can head commissions after retirement
### CAG — salary, charged expenses and term compared
## 1. Salary and independence
- Salary and service conditions: Fixed by Parliament by law — Article 148(3) and the Second Schedule; cannot be varied to his disadvantage after appointment
- Salary equal to an SC judge; salary, leave, pension and retirement age cannot be varied to his disadvantage after appointment
- Leave: Granted by the President
- Administrative expenses — Article 148(6): Charged on the Consolidated Fund of India — salaries, allowances and pensions of the CAG and staff (non-votable)
- Includes office expenses — charged on the Consolidated Fund of India (CFI) - not votable
- Parliament: No minister represents the CAG in Parliament

### CAG duties — what he audits (Art. 149)
## 1. What the CAG audits
- All expenditure from the Consolidated Funds of India, the States and Union Territories with Legislative Assemblies (receipts and expenditure of the Consolidated Fund of India, each State and each UT with a legislature)
- All transactions of the Contingency Funds and Public Accounts of the Union and the States
- Manufacturing, trading and profit and loss accounts of government entities; stores and stocks; public sector undertakings
- Performance audits: Economy, efficiency and effectiveness
- Audit timing: Post-expenditure (ex-post facto)
## 2. What Prep adds
- Audits:
- Bodies substantially financed by government
- Local bodies: Audited when requested by the President/Governor; Sixth Schedule councils also covered
- Limit: Cannot audit secret service expenditure - must accept the competent authority's certificate
### CAG limits — an Auditor General, not a Comptroller
## 1. Limits
- No day-to-day control over the exchequer; does not control the issue or drawal of money from the treasury — unlike the British CAG
- Only an Auditor-General, not a Comptroller: He is only the Auditor General — no control over issue of money / no control over the release/issue of money from the Consolidated Fund
- In Britain the C&AG does control issue
- No power to disallow expenditure, enforce recovery or initiate criminal prosecutions
- Public Accounts Committee: Friend, Philosopher and Guide — attends its meetings; assists its line-by-line examination of audit reports
- ⚠ False claims:
- "CAG submits audit reports directly to the PAC / Parliament" — reports go first to the President or Governor
- "CAG ensures no money is drawn from the Consolidated Fund of India without prior sanction"
- ⚠ Trap: "CAG controls the entire financial system of the country" (some notes) = the wrong statement (APPSC-G2 2017)
- Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2017 wrong statement - "CAG controls the entire financial system of the country".
### CAG duties and types of audit — Art. 149, limits — Types of audit
## 1. Types of audit
- Types:
- Obligatory = legal + regulatory audit (compliance) + financial attest
- Discretionary = propriety audit (wisdom, economy of expenditure) + performance audit
| Type | Nature | Tests |
| Legal and regulatory (compliance) | Obligatory | Money legally available and spent as rules require |
| Financial attest | Obligatory | Accounts true and per reporting framework |
| Propriety | Discretionary | Wisdom, faithfulness, economy; flags extravagance |
| Performance | Discretionary | Economy, efficiency, effectiveness of schemes |
- CAG+1: Social audits feeding CAG performance audits; MGNREGA social audit standards 2016
## 2. Audit of PSUs
| Category | Example |
| Audited directly by CAG | DVC, ONGC |
| Private auditors appointed on CAG's advice, CAG supplementary audit | Central Warehousing Corp., IFCI |
| Wholly private audit | LIC, RBI |
### Audit reports, Public Accounts Committee and landmark reports
## 1. Audit reports (Art. 151)
- Union reports: → President → laid before both Houses → examined by the Public Accounts Committee (PAC)
- Three Union reports: appropriation accounts, finance accounts, public undertakings
- PSU reports → Committee on Public Undertakings
- State reports: → Governor → laid before the state legislature
- AP reports: Go to the Governor of AP (Art. 151(2))
- After 2014: pre-bifurcation reports went to the Governors of both AP and Telangana (AP Reorganisation Act, 2014 - to each successor State)
- Not in your sources: latest CAG State Finances Audit Report on AP (revenue receipts composition - own tax, non-tax, central transfers).
## 2. Public Accounts Committee
- PAC (2026-27): 22 members (15 LS + 7 RS), elected annually (yearly) by STV; no minister
- Chairperson: Appointed by the Speaker, from the Opposition since 1967; chaired by K.C. Venugopal
- Set up: 1921
- Recommendations: Not binding; final action rests with the executive through Action Taken Notes
- CAG's role: "friend, philosopher and guide" of the PAC
