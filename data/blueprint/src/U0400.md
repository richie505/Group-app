# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:126 - NHRC and SHRCs - composition, powers, functions and limitations
### Status & establishment
- "Toothless tiger": non-binding advice, one-year bar, armed-forces limit
- Statutory, not constitutional: PHRA 1993, per Paris Principles
- Ordinance 28 Sept 1993; constituted 12 October 1993; assent Jan 1994
- First Chairperson: Justice Ranganath Misra; present: Justice V. Ramasubramanian
- SHRC is optional: State "may" constitute
### Composition: NHRC vs SHRC
|  | NHRC | SHRC |
| Chair | Former CJI or SC Judge | Former HC Chief Justice or Judge |
| Members | SC judge; HC CJ/judge; 3 experts (1 woman) | Three (cut from five, 2006) |
| Selection head | Prime Minister (six-member) | Chief Minister |
| Appointed by | President | Governor |
| Removed by | President | President |
| Resign to | President | Governor |
| Report to | Central Govt → Parliament | State Govt → State Legislature |
### NHRC selection committee
- ▲ Prime Minister — Chairperson
- ▸ Speaker of Lok Sabha; Union Home Minister — Members
- ▸ LoP Lok Sabha; LoP Rajya Sabha — Members
- ▸ Deputy Chairman, Rajya Sabha — ⚠ not the Chairman
### Deemed members (chairpersons)
| Before 2019 (4) | Added 2019 (3) |
| NCM, NCW | NCBC |
| NCSC, NCST (2006) | NCPCR |
| - | Chief Commissioner for PwDs |
### Amendments 2006 & 2019
| Year | Change |
| 2006 | Visit jails without intimating State Govt; interim relief during inquiry |
| 2006 | SHRC five → three; transfer complaints to SHRC |
| 2019 | Term 3 years or 70 (was 5); reappointment allowed |
| 2019 | Chair: CJI or SC judge; deemed members 4 → 7; UT functions to SHRC |
### Powers & functions
- Civil court powers (CPC 1908): summon, documents, public records, affidavits
- Inquiry suo motu, on petition or court direction - violations by public servants
- Recommend compensation, prosecution, interim relief; govt reports action in one month
- Bonded labour monitoring entrusted by SC (1997); custodial death report in 24 hours
- ⚠ Cannot punish violators
### Limitations
- No inquiry after one year of the act
- Armed forces: only seek Central report; action in three months
- Advisory, not binding; sub judice matters not taken
- No overlap with SHRC / other commission; Delhi handled by NHRC
### Exam corner
- APPSC-GS 2023: "constitutional body", "established 1994" = both false
- APPSC-G2 2025: constituted 1993; APPSC 2025: appointed by President
- ⚠ one month (general) vs three months (armed forces); time bar one year
- Both NHRC & SHRC chairs removed by the President
### NHRC — status, establishment, FIRSTs and Chairpersons
## 1. Status
- Nature: Statutory, not constitutional body (a statutory body) → created by the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 (PHRA)
- Conformity: Paris Principles
- ⚠ Trap: "constitutional body" and "established 1994" are both WRONG (APPSC 2023)
- Optional body: Constituting an SHRC is optional → a State Government "may" constitute one under the PHRA, 1993
## 2. Establishment timeline
| Event | Date |
| Constituted | 12 October 1993 (APPSC 2025: 1993) |
| Act received assent | Jan 1994 |
## 3. Chairpersons → FIRST and present
| Position | Person |
| First Chairperson | Justice Ranganath Misra |
| Present Chairperson | Justice V. Ramasubramanian (as of Feb 2026) |
- Exam angle: "NHRC is a constitutional body" and "established in 1994" are both false (APPSC-GS 2023); "constituted in 1993" is correct (APPSC-G2 2025).
### Composition → NHRC vs SHRC members
## 1. NHRC composition
| Component | Composition |
| Chairperson | Former Chief Justice of India or a Judge of the Supreme Court (after 2019; before 2019: only former CJI) |
| Member | One former or serving SC Judge |
| Member | One former or serving Chief Justice of an HC or HC judge |
| Members | Three persons with human-rights knowledge, at least one woman (earlier two) |
| Deemed members | Chairpersons of NCM, NCSC, NCST, NCW + (2019) NCBC, NCPCR, Chief Commissioner for PwDs |
- Deemed members: chairpersons are distinct from members
- Secretary-General: May exercise all administrative and financial powers (not judicial) under the Chairperson's control
## 2. Deemed members → chairpersons of other national commissions
| Before 2019 (4) | Added in 2019 (3) |
| National Commission for Minorities | National Commission for Backward Classes |
| National Commission for SCs | National Commission for Protection of Child Rights |
| National Commission for STs | Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities |
| National Commission for Women | - |
- CCPD: Functions under Sec. 75(1) of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 (2026)
## 3. SHRC composition
- Chairperson: Former Chief Justice or Judge of a High Court (2019; earlier only CJ)
- Size: three members (cut from five, see Acts page)
- Chairperson + one HC judge/district judge with 7 years' experience + one person with human-rights knowledge
- ⚠ NHRC chair = former CJI or SC Judge; SHRC chair = former HC Chief Justice or Judge
### Appointment, term, resignation and removal → NHRC vs SHRC — Appointed by
## 1. NHRC composition and appointment
- Chairperson: A former Chief Justice of India or a former Supreme Court judge; plus judicial members, expert members and specified ex-officio representation
- Appointed by: The President of India — on the recommendations of a six-member High-Level Selection Committee:
- Prime Minister — Chairperson (the selection head)
- Speaker of the Lok Sabha
- Union Home Minister
- Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha
- Leader of the Opposition in the Rajya Sabha
- Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha — he represents the Rajya Sabha
- ⚠ Trap: The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is not a member of the committee
## 2. Appointed by — NHRC vs SHRC
| Post | Appointed by | Recommended by | Removed by |
| NHRC Chairperson/members | The President | six-member committee (PM-led) | President |
| SHRC Chairperson/members | Appointed by the Governor | CM-led committee | removable only by the President (same grounds as NHRC) |
- Vacancy in committee: does not invalidate its decisions
- Exam angle: "appointed by" = President (APPSC 2025).
## 3. SHRC selection committee
| SHRC selection committee member | Role |
| Chief Minister | Chairperson |
| Speaker of the Legislative Assembly | Member |
| State Home Minister | Member |
| Leader of Opposition in the Assembly (LoP) | Member |
| Chairman and LoP of the Council (where it exists, i.e. bicameral States) | Members |
### Appointment, term, resignation and removal → NHRC vs SHRC — Consultation
## 1. Consultation
- NHRC: Sitting SC judge or HC Chief Justice appointed only after consulting the CJI
- SHRC: Sitting HC judge or district judge appointed only after consulting the Chief Justice of the High Court
- Acting Chairperson (SHRC): On a vacancy, the Governor may authorise a member to act
## 2. Term and after office
- Term (both): 3 years or age 70, whichever earlier (since 2019); eligible for reappointment
- After office: Ineligible for further employment under Centre or States
## 3. Resignation and removal
- Resignation: NHRC → in writing to the President; SHRC → addressed to the Governor (both 2006 amendment)
- Removal by the President:
- Insolvency; paid employment outside duties; infirmity of mind/body; unsound mind; conviction for offence of moral turpitude
- Also proved misbehaviour or incapacity after a Supreme Court inquiry on the President's reference
### Acts and amendments → year and change
## 1. Amendment of 2006 → changes
| 2006 amendment | Change |
| Deemed members | NCSC and NCST chairpersons added by the 2006 amendment |
| Resignation | NHRC to the President; SHRC to the Governor |
| Jails | NHRC may visit even without intimating the State Govt |
| Relief | Interim relief / compensation may be recommended even during inquiry |
| SHRC size | Cut from five to three members |
| Complaints | NHRC enabled to transfer complaints to the concerned SHRC |
## 2. Protection of Human Rights (Amendment) Act 2019 → before vs after
| Item | Before 2019 | After 2019 |
| Term | 5 years or 70 | 3 years or 70 |
| Reappointment | For one more 5-year term | 5-year limit removed |
| SHRC Chairperson | Only a former Chief Justice of a High Court | Former CJ or Judge of a High Court (HC Chief Justice or Judge) |
| Deemed members | 4 commission chairs | 7 (3 added) |
- Exam angle: "Member is not eligible for re-appointment" is the WRONG statement (MPPSC); after office, chairperson/members are ineligible for further government employment.
### Functions and powers → detail
## 1. Powers and functions
- All the powers of a civil court while inquiring into complaints:
- Requisition public records
- Inquires into human rights violations and negligence by public servants in preventing them
- Visits jails and detention centres to study the living conditions of inmates — suggests reforms
- Promotes human rights literacy; encourages non-governmental organizations
## 2. More functions and powers
| Function / power | Detail |
| Inquiry | Suo motu, on petition, or on court direction, into violations by a public servant |
| Court proceedings | Intervene in court proceedings involving human-rights allegations, with the court's approval [GK] |
| Jails | 2006: even without intimating the State Govt |
| Review | Constitutional and legal safeguards + factors inhibiting rights (incl. terrorism); study international treaties |
| Relief | Recommend compensation, prosecution, interim relief (2006: even during inquiry) |
- Action taken: Government must inform the Commission of action taken within one month
- Not a function: Punishing violators or enforcing its own recommendations
- SHRC powers: Same inquiry powers (civil court), same one-year bar and advisory nature as NHRC
## 3. Specialised NHRC mandates
| Mandate | Detail |
| Bonded labour | SC (1997) entrusted NHRC with monitoring the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976 |
| Custodial deaths | Report to NHRC within 24 hours; post-mortem videography; independent investigation |
| Encounter deaths | Reporting + magisterial inquiry per NHRC guidelines and PUCL v Maharashtra (2014) |
| Transgender welfare | Advisory 2.0 (May 2026) → Census categories, legal-aid cells, Garima Greh expansion |
### Jurisdiction and limitations → NHRC vs SHRC — Division of functions
## 1. Division of functions
- SHRC jurisdiction: Only violations on subjects in the State List (List II) and Concurrent List (List III); NHRC covers all
- UTs (2019): The Central Government may confer a UT's human-rights functions on an SHRC; Delhi's functions handled by the NHRC itself
- No overlap: SHRC will not inquire into a case already being inquired into by the NHRC or any other statutory commission
- Likewise NHRC does not take up a matter pending before an SHRC
- Transfer: 2006 amendment enabled the NHRC to transfer complaints to the concerned SHRC
### Jurisdiction and limitations → NHRC vs SHRC — Limitations
## 1. Limitations
| Limitation | Detail |
| Time bar | No inquiry after one year from the alleged act; review committee wanted this relaxed for sufficient cause |
| Armed forces | May only seek a report from the Central Government and recommend; Centre informs it of action within three months |
| Purview | Only violations by public servants; not a matter already before an SHRC or another commission |
| Non-binding | Recommendations are advisory / not binding → Government must respond but cannot be compelled |
- "Armed forces" = naval, military and air forces, incl. any other armed forces of the Union (Sec. 2)
- No punishment: No power to punish violators or award relief itself → only recommend compensation (may do so even during inquiry, 2006)
- Jurisdiction questioned: Allahabad HC said NHRC exceeded its mandate by directing an EOW probe into 558 aided madrasas (30 Apr 2026)
- ⚠ Action taken: one month (general) vs three months (armed forces); time bar one year

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:126
### Status & establishment
- NHRC — statutory body under PHRA 1993; not constitutional
- UPSC and CAG — constitutional bodies
- Follows the Paris Principles — standards of independence, pluralism, broad mandate (UNGA 1993)
- First Chairperson — Justice Ranganath Misra; NHRC constituted 12 October 1993
- SHRC — optional; a State Government may form one under PHRA 1993
### Composition: NHRC vs SHRC
## NHRC composition
- Three persons with knowledge of human rights, at least one woman
- 2019 amendment — seven deemed members: Chairpersons of NCBC, NCPCR and Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities added to the four earlier
- Deemed members are not equal in status to ordinary members
- Chairperson — before 2019 only a former CJI; after 2019 also a Supreme Court Judge
## Appointment & removal (NHRC)
- Selection committee (six) — PM (chair), Lok Sabha Speaker, Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha, Leaders of Opposition of both Houses, Union Home Minister; CJI not a member
- Sitting SC Judge / sitting HC Chief Justice appointed only after consulting the CJI
- Resignation addressed to the President
- Removal for misbehaviour/incapacity — President refers to the Supreme Court; insolvency etc. — direct removal
- After office — ineligible for further jobs under Central or State Govt
## SHRC
- Chairperson appointed by Governor, removed only by the President
- Selection committee — Chief Minister (chair), Speaker, State Home Minister, Leader of Opposition; HC Chief Justice only consulted
- 2006 amendment — three members (from five): Chairperson, judicial member, human-rights expert
- Covers State List and Concurrent List (NHRC — all subjects)
- No power to punish or enforce as decrees — advisory
## Jurisdiction limits
- SHRC will not look into a case already being probed by another statutory commission
- NHRC barred after one year from the alleged act
- GANHRI accredits national human-rights institutions ('A' fully compliant, 'B' others)
- Gade Sai Krishna case — NHRC directed the AP SHRC, DGP and Chief Secretary to report
### NHRC selection committee
- Deputy Chairman (not Chairman) of the Rajya Sabha sits on the committee
- PM chairs the six-member committee; President appoints on its advice
### Amendments 2006 & 2019
## 2019 amendment
- Removed the earlier five-year limit — members eligible for reappointment
- Term — 3 years or age 70, whichever earlier
## 2006 amendment
- NHRC may transfer complaints to the concerned SHRC
- SHRC Chairperson resigns in writing to the Governor
## Powers
- NHRC may join court proceedings with approval of the court concerned
- NHRC may visit jails to study living conditions; civil-court powers; recommendations advisory; cannot directly investigate armed forces
### Powers & functions
- Jurisdiction — violations or negligence by public servants (not purely private complaints)
- Inquiry powers — civil court (summon, examine on oath, documents, public records)
- Relief — may recommend compensation, prosecution, interim relief; interim relief during inquiry since 2006
- 1997 — Supreme Court entrusted NHRC with monitoring the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976
- Custodial deaths — reported to NHRC within 24 hours; post-mortem videography, independent investigation
### Limitations
- Government informs NHRC of action on its report within one month
- Armed-forces cases — NHRC seeks a report from the Central Government and recommends; Centre informs action within three months
- Recommendations — advisory, but Government must respond
- 'Toothless tiger' — mainly non-binding advice (plus one-year bar, armed-forces limits)
- UTs (2019) — Centre may confer a UT's human-rights functions on an SHRC; Delhi handled by the NHRC
- SHRC annual report — to State Government, then laid before State Legislature with a memorandum of action taken
- AP SHRC — constituted by the AP Government under PHRA 1993
### NHRC — status, establishment, FIRSTs and Chairpersons
- ★ NHRC constituted in 1993 under PHRA 1993 — India's primary statutory body on human rights violations
### Composition → NHRC vs SHRC members
