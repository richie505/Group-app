### Water pollution — sources and terms → meaning (DO, BOD, COD, solids)
## 1. Sources
| Type | Examples |

## 2. Oxygen demand and pollution level
| Parameter | Measures | Clean-water (unpolluted) value |
| DO | Oxygen dissolved in water | > 8.0 mg/L |
| BOD | Oxygen (O2) bacteria need to decompose organic matter | < 4.0 mg/L; high BOD = very polluted (a heavily polluted water body) |
| COD | Oxygen needed to oxidise all organic matter (biodegradable + non-biodegradable) | < 5.0 mg/L |
| NTU | Turbidity | - |
- Measure of river pollution: its dissolved oxygen
## 3. Other terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Coliform count | (MPN/100 ml) shows faecal contamination |
| Suspended solids | retained on a filter; examples: silt, clay |
| Dissolved solids | salts that pass through the filter (e.g. sodium) |
| Turbidity | measured in NTU |
| TDS | = dissolved salts |

### Eutrophication — causes, process and stages
## 1. Fertilizers and nutrient enrichment
- Fertilizers: Contribute to soil, water and air pollution; nitrates and phosphates cause eutrophication
## 2. Eutrophication — causes, process and stages
- Cause: Nutrient (nitrogen, phosphorus) enrichment
- Triggers: fertiliser runoff, sewage, detergents, aquaculture effluent, airborne nitrogen
- Process: enrichment → algal blooms → algae decay → decomposition uses up DO (dissolved oxygen falls) → hypoxia, fish kills
- Stages: oligotrophic → mesotrophic → eutrophic (→ hypereutrophic)
- Ritual offerings: e.g. dairy effluent can have BOD far above sewage → deplete DO fast (15 Apr 2026)

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 5:101
### Key parameters
- High BOD — heavy organic pollution; BOD = oxygen bacteria need to decompose organic matter
- Oxygen to oxidise all organic matter (biodegradable and non-biodegradable) — COD
- NTU — turbidity; TDS — dissolved salts; coliform count — faecal contamination
- Ganga bathing standard, faecal coliform — 2,500 MPN/100 ml
- CPCB minimum DO: Class A 6, B 5, C 4, D 4 mg/L
- CPCB Class A (drinking source, disinfection only) — total coliform ≤ 50 MPN/100 ml
- Eutrophication chain: nutrient enrichment → algal bloom → decomposition → DO depletion (hypoxia, fish kills)
### Eutrophication
- ★ Harmful algal blooms in Indian seawater — nutrient discharge from estuaries, monsoon land run-off, upwelling (all three)
- ★ Blooms of pigmented dinoflagellates in estuaries — red tides
- Triggers: fertiliser runoff, sewage, detergents, aquaculture effluent, airborne nitrogen (not a reduction of atmospheric nitrogen)
- Groundwater nitrate — mainly fertiliser use; 56% of districts reportedly cross 45 mg/L
### Groundwater contaminants
- Fluoride (BIS IS 10500) — 1.0 mg/L acceptable, 1.5 mg/L permissible
- CGWB Ground Water Quality Report 2024: fluoride above 1.5 mg/L at 9.04% sites — Rajasthan highest, then Haryana, Karnataka
- Fluorosis: fluoride replaces hydroxyl in hydroxyapatite → fluorapatite
- Nalgonda technique (fluoride removal) — alum, lime, bleaching powder
- Arsenic belt — Ganga-Brahmaputra plains: West Bengal, Bihar, Assam; extends into Bangladesh
- Uranium — Punjab's Malwa
- Nitrate → methaemoglobinaemia (blue baby syndrome) in infants
