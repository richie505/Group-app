### Universe and solar system — basics, ages and distance units
## 1. Basics
- The Solar System is centred on the Sun — 4.6 billion years old (age of Earth and solar system ~4.6 billion years)
- Main components: The Sun, eight planets, dwarf planets, satellites, asteroids, comets, meteoroids and cosmic dust
- Gravity keeps bodies in orbit
- Number of planets: Current — eight; former — nine, before Pluto's reclassification
- Pluto reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006 by the IAU
## 2. Definitions and ages
- Cosmology: Study of the origin, evolution and structure of the universe
- Standard Model of cosmology: Big Bang + dark matter + dark energy + CMB
- Age of the universe: 13.8 billion years (APPSC 2019)
- Most abundant element: hydrogen, then helium (universe and the Sun)
- Sun = ~74% H and ~24% He
- Present view: All planets formed from the same spinning protoplanetary disc as the Sun
- Hot inner disc → only metals and rocky minerals solid (Mercury to Mars)
- Cool outer disc → water, ammonia and methane ices (giant planets)
## 3. Distance units
- Units: AU = mean Earth-Sun distance ~149.6 million km < light year (distance light travels in a year) ~9.46 x 10^12 km (~9.46 trillion km) < parsec = 3.26 light years
- ⚠ Order of size: AU < light year < parsec

### The Sun — nature, layers and solar activity
## 1. Nature
- A star at the centre of the Solar System — the closest star to Earth; Alpha Centauri is the next closest star mentioned
- Contains 99.8% of the Solar System's mass; diameter 109 times Earth's diameter
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say over 99% of the solar system's mass
- Interior: A plasma state; core temperature 15 million°C
- Photosphere: The visible bright layer — about 5,500°C
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say about 5,500-6,000°C[GK]
- Energy: Nuclear fusion — converts hydrogen into helium and releases energy
- Distance: Mean Earth-Sun distance = 1 AU (~150 million km)
- Future: Becomes a red giant after exhausting its hydrogen
## 2. Solar phenomena
- Sunspot: A dark patch on the photosphere
- Aurora: Produced by solar particles interacting with the atmosphere — Aurora Borealis in the north; Aurora Australis in the south
- Corona: Visible during a (total) solar eclipse
| Layer | Key fact |
| Core | Nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium (H → He); about 15 million °C[GK] |
| Radiative and convective zones | Between the core and the photosphere |
| Photosphere | Visible surface; supergranule cells ~30,000 km, ~24 h life |
| Chromosphere | Reddish lower atmosphere, seen at eclipses |
| Corona | Outer atmosphere, a faint halo seen in a total eclipse; over a million °C - hotter than the surface [GK] |
| Sunspots | Cooler, darker magnetic patches/regions of intense magnetic activity; markers of the ~11-year solar cycle (minimum to maximum) |
| Solar flares | Sudden bursts of radiation from magnetic reconnection; classed A, B, C, M, X (X strongest) |
| CMEs | Plasma clouds from the corona at 250-3,000 km/s; reach Earth in 1-3 days |
- Sunspot cycle: Matched by plasma currents in the 35,000-km-deep Near-Surface Shear Layer
- Sun's magnetic poles reverse at each peak
- Effects on Earth: geomagnetic storms, auroras; damage to satellites, GPS, aviation, radio, communication, navigation and power grids
- ⚠ Corona (outer) is hotter than the photosphere (surface)

### The Moon — perigee, apogee, phases and eclipses
## 1. The Moon
- Earth's natural satellite — diameter 3,475 km
- Distance from Earth: Average 384,400 km; minimum 363,300 km; maximum 405,500 km
- Orbital and rotation period: 27 days 7 hours 43 minutes — shows the same face toward Earth
- Atmosphere: Essentially absent; gravity: one-sixth of Earth's gravity
- Lunar month: 29.5 days
- Apollo 11 (1969): The first human landing on the Moon — in the Sea of Tranquility
## 2. Perigee and apogee
- Perigee: The Moon's closest position to Earth — stronger gravity and higher tides; a full moon near / at perigee is a supermoon [GK]
- Apogee: The Moon's farthest position from Earth — weaker gravity; full moon = micromoon, ~6-7% smaller, 10-14% dimmer
## 3. Phases
- Full moon: Earth approximately between the Sun and Moon
- New moon: The Moon between the Sun and Earth
- Half moon: The Sun–Earth–Moon angle is 90°
- Blue moon: Two full moons in one calendar month (the calendar definition); alternatively an extra full moon in an astronomical season; stated frequency — 2.7154 years
- Blue Micromoon: Second full moon of a month falling at apogee; occurred in 2026 after more than five years
## 4. Eclipses

### The Sun: layers
- Layers, centre outward: core → radiative zone → convective zone → photosphere → chromosphere → corona

- Coronal mass ejections: plasma clouds from the corona (not rock); ~250–3,000 km/s; reach Earth in 1–3 days
- Earth's magnetic field: geodynamo of moving liquid-iron outer core; shields from solar wind
- Southern aurora Aurora Australis; northern Aurora Borealis
