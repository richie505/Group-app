### Seismic waves — types, evidence and shadow zone
## 1. Seismic waves as evidence
- Earthquakes generate seismic waves, which travel through different materials and undergo reflection and refraction
- Velocity changes reveal differences in materials; shadow zones help infer internal boundaries (wave speed changes with density → waves reflect and refract at layer boundaries → how discontinuities were found)
- Seismic-wave evidence identifies the crust, mantle and core
## 2. P, S and surface waves
| Basis | P-waves | S-waves | Surface waves |
| Name | Primary — compressional or longitudinal push-pull waves | Secondary — transverse or shear shake waves | Surface waves — L-waves |
| Speed | The fastest — arrive first; reach the inner core at 11.23 km/s | Slower than P-waves — S-waves travel more slowly; P-waves travel faster than S-waves — do not arrive first | The slowest — do not arrive first (arrive last) |
| Medium | Solids, liquids and gases | Solids only — not through liquids or gases; S-waves vanish in the outer core → outer core is liquid | — |
| Through Earth's interior | Yes | Yes | No |
| Particle vibration | Parallel to the direction of propagation | Perpendicular to the direction of propagation | — |
| Destructive power | Lower than surface waves | — | The highest (do the most damage) |
## 3. Evidence for the liquid outer core
- S-waves cannot cross the liquid outer core — they disappear because it is liquid; S-wave behaviour establishes the existence of a liquid outer core
- P-waves: Strongly refracted at the mantle–core boundary
- P-wave shadow zone: Standard — approximately 103°–142°; also given as approximately 105°–140°
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the shadow zone is the belt 105°-145° from the epicentre (no direct P or S waves; P-waves not recorded between about 105° and 145°)
- S-waves do not reappear beyond the core-related shadow zone — S-waves are absent everywhere beyond 105° → S-wave shadow much larger; proves outer core is liquid
- Each earthquake has its own shadow zone: set by where its epicentre is
- (CGPSC 2020: Statement I true)
- ⚠ P = solids + liquids + gases; S = solids only

## 3. Density
- Earth's average density: approximately 5.5 g/cm³ — Earth is the densest planet
- Density generally increases inward — heavier materials are concentrated in Earth's core
- Density gradient: 2.8-3.0 (crust) → 4.6 (mantle) → 11-13.6 (core) (inner core 13+)
- ⚠ Mantle = 83% volume, 68% mass; core = 16% volume, 32% mass

### Lithosphere and asthenosphere — terms → meaning
## 1. Lithosphere vs asthenosphere
| Basis | Lithosphere | Asthenosphere |
| Nature | Rigid — relatively strong — the rigid upper zone associated with the mantle | Weak and ductile — mechanically weaker; an important upper zone of the mantle; weak, plastic, partly molten ("astheno" = weak) |
| Made of / position | The crust and uppermost mantle — about 100 km thick | Underlies — the upper mantle portion below the lithosphere; low-velocity zone at ~100-200 km (extends to ~400 km); earthquake waves slow down |
| Plates | Broken into plates — tectonic plates that move over the semi-fluid asthenosphere | Allows plate movement — lets lithospheric plates move |
## 2. Role of the mantle
- Mantle movement drives plate movement — a major role in plate tectonics, volcanism and endogenic activity
- The major source region for magma; the layer below the crust is the mantle
- Asthenosphere significance: main source of magma; lithospheric plates glide over it
- Layer sequence: Lithosphere/Crust → Asthenosphere → Mesosphere → Barysphere (core)
- Delamination: Indian plate beneath Tibet is delaminating - dense lower layer peeling into the mantle ~100 km down
- Delamination evidence: 94 seismic stations, helium isotopes

### Core and magnetic field — features
## 1. Core
- Core: Begins at a depth of 2,900 km — outer core of liquid iron and nickel; inner core of solid iron
- Boundaries: begins at ~2,900 km; splits at 5,150 km
- Outer core (2,900-5,150 km): liquid (molten)
- Proof: loss of S-waves
- Inner core (5,150-6,371 km): solid
- Density 13.3-13.6; P-waves cross at 11.23 km/s
- Stays solid under immense pressure despite the heat
- Role of iron: accounts for Earth's magnetism and rigidity
## 2. Geodynamo
- Movement and convection of conducting material in the liquid outer core produce the geodynamo, which generates Earth's magnetic field (dynamo action of convecting molten iron in the outer core; iron in NIFE indicates the interior's magnetism)
- Magnetic-field reversals: Linked to changes in molten-core movement
- Axis inclination: magnetic axis inclined ~11.5° to the geographic axis
- ⚠ Outer core liquid, inner core solid (pressure)
### Temperature, pressure and density — gradients and geothermal places
- Trend: All three increase with depth (Temperature, pressure and density all rise with depth)
- Temperature: rises ~1°C per 32 m in the upper crust; rate slows deeper
- Pressure: rises with weight of overlying rock → keeps the inner core solid

### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- Stanford's first global map of continental mantle earthquakes (80+ km below Moho; weak surface shaking)
- Indian-plate delamination under Tibet
- Magnetic reversals lasting up to ~70,000 years
- Significance:
- Diamonds form in the mantle, reach crust via kimberlite pipes; Madhya Pradesh has ~90% of India's reserves
- Liquid outer core makes the magnetic field that shields life from solar wind and cosmic radiation
- Comparative note: Moho = crust/mantle; Gutenberg = mantle/core; Lehmann = outer/inner core - the most confused trio

### Seismic waves
- ★ Earthquakes make P, S and L waves
- P-waves travel fastest (arrive first); P and S = body waves (go through the interior)
- P-waves: longitudinal (push–pull) — particles vibrate parallel to propagation
- S-waves: transverse/shear — particles shake crosswise; travel only through solids, stop at liquids
- Surface waves do the most damage
### Shadow zone
- P-wave shadow zone: about 103°–142° from the epicentre
- S-waves missing beyond 105° → S-wave shadow zone bigger than P-wave shadow zone
- S-waves disappear at the outer core because the outer core is liquid (S-waves cannot pass through liquids)
### Layers
## Order and state
- Order: crust → mantle → outer core → inner core (centre)
- Seismic waves show discontinuities: speed changes with density → reflection and refraction at boundaries
- Mantle: mostly solid but flows plastically over long ages (drives convection)
- Outer core: liquid iron and nickel (why S-waves stop); inner core: solid (iron)
- Core is mainly iron and nickel; core starts at about 2,900 km
