### Attorney General (Art. 76)
- Highest / first law officer of India; chief legal adviser to GoI
- No fixed tenure; removal procedure/grounds not specified
- Remuneration by President; not charged on Consolidated Fund
- Not a member of the Union Cabinet
- Resigns traditionally when Council of Ministers changes
### AG qualification (= SC judge)
- ▸ HC judge — at least 5 years
- ▸ Advocate of a HC — at least 10 years
- ▸ Distinguished jurist — in opinion of the President
### AG duties
- 1. Advise GoI on legal matters referred by President (via Ministry of Law) ↓
- 2. Appear for GoI in all Supreme Court cases ↓
- 3. Represent GoI in Art. 143 references ↓
- 4. Appear in any High Court when required
### SG & ASGs
- Not mentioned in the Constitution
- Law Officers (Terms and Conditions) Rules, 1987
- SG = second law officer; 3-year term; assisted by ASGs
- Appears in courts + tenders legal advice
- AG & SG: ex officio members of Bar Council of India (Advocates Act, 1961)
### Advocate General (Art. 165)
- First law officer of a state; appointed by Governor (Art. 165(1))
- Pleasure of Governor (Art. 165(3)); ⚠ not of State Cabinet / CM
- Art. 177: speak in state legislature; no right to vote
- Assisted by Additional Advocates General

### Attorney General — Article 76, appointment, term, removal
## 1. Office and appointment
- Highest law officer of the Government of India — a constitutional post under Part V, Article 76
- The Attorney General is the highest / first law officer of India + chief legal adviser to GOI (APPSC 2018, 2019); only the AG is created by the Constitution
- Appointed by: The President; holds office during the pleasure of the President
- No fixed tenure in the Constitution (Term not fixed); the President can remove him at any time — removal procedure/grounds not specified
- Resignation to the President; traditionally resigns when the Council of Ministers changes (appointed on its advice)
- Remuneration: Determined by the President — not fixed by Parliament or the Constitution (Remuneration determined by the President; not charged on the Consolidated Fund)
- AG and Cabinet: AG is not a member of the Union Cabinet; Law Minister handles legal matters at government level
## 2. Qualifications — equal to a Supreme Court judge
- Must be qualified to be a Supreme Court judge:
- Citizen of India, and one of:
- High Court judge for at least five years (5 years)
- Advocate of a High Court for at least ten years (10 years)
- A distinguished jurist in the opinion of the President (eminent jurist)
### Attorney General — Article 76, appointment, term, removal, duties — Duties
## 1. Duties
- Duties:
- Advise GoI on legal matters referred by the President
- Appear for GoI in all Supreme Court cases concerning it
- Represent GoI in Art. 143 references
- References: Made to the AG through the Ministry of Law; AG consulted only on legal matters of real importance
- Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2025 - "qualified to be an SC judge" and "advises GoI" are correct; a "fixed term" statement is wrong (LENS Oct 2025 practice question also tests "fixed five-year term" - false).
- Exam angle: "Who is the highest law officer?" → AG (not CJI, not Law Minister).
### Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General — status, appointment, role
## 1. Legal status
- SG and ASGs: Not mentioned in the Constitution (SG and ASGs are not mentioned in the Constitution)
- Statutory/non-constitutional posts under the Law Officers (Terms and Conditions) Rules, 1987
## 2. Appointment and role
- SG: Second law officer; assists the AG; 3-year term; assisted by several ASGs
- Appears in courts for the Union and also tenders legal advice to Government
- Appointment: By the Centre through the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet
- Conventions: Law officers resign when a new government comes in
- Bar Council: AG and SG are ex officio members of the Bar Council of India (Advocates Act, 1961) (18 Aug 2026)
### Advocate General — Article 165, duties, Art. 177
## 1. Office — Article 165
- Highest law officer of the State — the official legal adviser to the State Government; Part VI, Article 165
- The Advocate General (Art. 165) = the first law officer of a state, corresponding to the Attorney General (Art. 76)
- Appointed by: The Governor — Article 165(1) (not the President)
- Qualification: Must be qualified to be a High Court Judge (Article 217)
- Tenure: No fixed tenure — holds office during the pleasure of the Governor (Article 165(3)); changes with the state government by convention
- ⚠ False claims: "During the pleasure of the State Cabinet" or "of the Chief Minister"
- Duties (Article 165(2)): Advises the State Government on legal matters; other legal duties assigned by the Governor or the Constitution
- Remuneration: Remuneration determined by the Governor (Remuneration is fixed by the Governor.)
- Order of precedence: No. 25, below the Additional Solicitor General
- Assisted by: Additional Advocates General
## 2. Rights
- Art. 177: Right to speak and take part in either House of the state legislature and its committees; no right to vote
- Right of audience in any court in the state
- Art. 194(4): Enjoys the privileges of a member of the state legislature
- AP: Advocate General of AP appointed by the Governor of AP under Art. 165
