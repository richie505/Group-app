# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:181 - Revolt of 1857 - political causes (annexations, Doctrine of Lapse, subsidiary alliance)
### Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley, 1798)
- Princes' fear of annexation turned a sepoy mutiny into a wider revolt
- Ruler disbands own army; keeps British subsidiary force at his cost
- Accepts a British Resident; no independent foreign policy
- 1800 Nizam treaty ceded Rayalaseema = "Ceded Districts"
### States that accepted
- Sept 1798 — Nizam of Hyderabad (first), again 1800
- 1799 — Mysore; Tanjore (October)
- Nov 1801 — Awadh
- Dec 1802 — Peshwa (Treaty of Bassein)
- 1803-04 — Bhonsle of Berar; Scindia
### Doctrine of Lapse
- Dalhousie (1848-56): no natural male heir → state lapses
- Adoption only for private property
- First lapse: Satara (1848); Jhansi (1853)
- Withdrawn by Queen Victoria's Proclamation, Allahabad, 1 November 1858
- Princes = "breakwaters to the storm" (Canning)
### Annexation of Awadh, 1856
- 1. Dalhousie: ground "misgovernment", not lapse ↓
- 2. Wajid Ali Shah deposed, exiled to Calcutta ↓
- 3. Summary Settlement 1856: taluqdars dispossessed, disarmed, forts demolished ↓
- 4. Nawab's soldiers jobless; Awadh sepoys of Bengal Army hurt ↓

### Doctrine of Lapse — Dalhousie, meaning and withdrawal
- Meaning: Policy of Lord Dalhousie (1848-56), from 1848
- Ruler of a dependent state dying without a natural male heir → adopted son could not inherit the throne → state "lapsed" to the Company
- Adoption allowed only for private property
- APPSC-GS 2018 also called it "the policy of annexation devised by the EIC"
- Withdrawal: Queen Victoria's Proclamation (read by Canning at the Allahabad Durbar, 1 November 1858)
- Abolished it + recognised adoption; annexation abandoned
- Princes became "breakwaters to the storm" (Lord Canning)
- ⚠ Lapse = no natural male heir (Dalhousie, 1848); Awadh = "misgovernment", not lapse (1856)
### Governor-Generals → titles, pensions and Mughal succession
| Measure | By / year | Detail |
| Titles abolished | 1855 | Carnatic Nawab's and Tanjore Raja's |
| Pension denied | - | Nana Saheb (adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II, the last Peshwa) denied his father's pension |
| Mughal succession | Dalhousie | Bahadur Shah's successors would have to leave the Red Fort (move to the Qutub, Mehrauli) |
| Mughal succession | Canning (1856) | After Bahadur Shah Zafar successors would lose the imperial title and be mere princes |
- ⚠ Dalhousie = successors leave the Red Fort; Canning = successors lose the imperial title
### Annexation of Awadh, 1856 — misgovernment, summary settlement, taluqdars and sepoys
- Annexation: By Dalhousie, 1856 (February), on ground of "misgovernment" (maladministration), not lapse
- Nawab Wajid Ali Shah deposed (exiled to Calcutta)
- Summary Revenue Settlement (1856): Settlement directly with village proprietors
- Taluqdars dispossessed of their estates, disarmed and their forts demolished
| Group | Effect |
| Sepoys | Bengal Army drew heavily from Awadh → hurt directly (families bore the new revenue burden) |
- "Deeply alienated the sepoys and the landed aristocracy"

### Doctrine of Lapse
- Linked with Lord Dalhousie; used from 1848
- Adopted son could inherit private property, but not the throne
- Satara (1848) — first state annexed
- Jhansi (1853) — annexed; adopted son Damodar Rao of Rani Lakshmibai not recognised
- Nagpur — later; Awadh (1856) — annexed for misgovernment, not lapse
- Dropped by Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1 November 1858), read by Canning at the Allahabad Durbar; accepted adoption
### Annexation of Awadh, 1856
- Wajid Ali Shah — removed and exiled to Calcutta after the annexation
- ★ Wajid Ali Shah (last Nawab of Awadh) — "Daro-deewar pe hasrat ki nazar karte hain, khush raho ahle-vatan ham to safar karte hain"; sung by revolutionaries in prison
- Summary Revenue Settlement (1856) — made directly with village proprietors; taluqdars lost estates and arms, forts destroyed
- Bengal Army sepoys angered — their families in Awadh hit by the new revenue burden
- Begum Hazrat Mahal — opposed annexation; declared her son Birjis Qadr ruler
- ★ Hazrat Mahal — led at Lucknow; Nana Saheb — led at Kanpur; Mangal Pande — hanged March 29, 1857
