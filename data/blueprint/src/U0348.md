### NCSK — Act, lapse and tenure extensions
## 1. Legal status
- Origin: Under the NCSK Act 1993 as a statutory body; set up in 1993 (UPSC notes) / 1994

### NHRC and SHRCs — composition, appointment, powers — NHRC establishment
## 1. NHRC establishment
- Set up: 12 Oct 1993 under the PHRA 1993 (first an Ordinance of 28 Sep 1993)
- Paris Principles: Conforms to them (Paris, Oct 1991; endorsed by the UNGA on 20 Dec 1993)
## 2. Composition — before and after 2019
| Item | Before 2019 | After PHR (Amendment) Act 2019 |
| Chairperson | Only a former Chief Justice of India (ex-CJI only) | Former CJI or a Judge of the Supreme Court (ex-Judge of the SC) |
| Human-rights members | 2 | 3, at least one woman |
| Term | Five years or age 70 | Three years or age 70; reappointment allowed |
- Deemed (ex officio) members: Chairpersons of NCM, NCSC, NCST, NCW (before 2019) + (added 2019) NCBC, NCPCR and the Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities (Chief Commissioner for PwD)
- Group-II table (NHR C): Chair + 3 members + deemed members; 3 years or 70; removal by the President after an SC inquiry for misbehaviour or incapacity
### NHRC and SHRCs — composition, appointment, powers — Appointment
## 1. Appointment
- Appointment: By the President on a committee of:
- PM (chair), Lok Sabha Speaker, Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman
- Leaders of Opposition in both Houses, Union Home Minister
- 2019 amendment: Centre may give UT human-rights functions to SHRCs; Delhi stays with the NHRC
## 2. Powers and limits
- Powers: Of a civil court in inquiries (summon witnesses, call for documents, evidence on affidavit)
- Binding?: Recommendations not binding; government must respond, but Commission cannot compel
- Time bar: Cannot inquire after one year (Cannot inquire after 1 year) from the date of the alleged violation / the incident
- Example: Notices to the AP DGP and Chief Secretary on the Sai Krishna custodial death (28 Jun 2026)
### NHRC and SHRCs — composition, appointment, powers — SHRC
## 1. SHRC
- Legal basis: SHRCs under PHRA 1993
- Jurisdiction: Only violations on State List and Concurrent List subjects; won't take a case already before NHRC or another statutory commission
- Chairperson (after 2019): Former Chief Justice or Judge of a High Court (Chair = former Chief Justice or Judge of a High Court, 2019 amendment)
- Appointment: By the Governor on a committee of CM, Speaker, Home Minister, Leader of Opposition (+ Council Chairman and LoP where a Council exists)
- Removal: Only by the President
- RTI question: Supreme Court asked whether SHRC chairs could act as State Information Commissions in States with few RTI appeals (11 Feb 2026)
- ⚠ SHRC: appointed by the Governor, removed by the President

## 2. NCW — status
- Legal basis: Statutory body under the NCW Act, 1990; constituted January 1992 (G2-GS 2019 key)
- Ministry link: One of MWCD's three statutory bodies, with NCPCR and CARA
- Composition: Chairperson, five Members and a Member-Secretary, all nominated by the Central Government
### NCW, NCPCR, NCM — composition and functions — NCW — functions (Section 10)
## 1. NCW — functions (Section 10)
- Section 10 of the NCW Act, 1990 lists the functions:
- Review/investigate constitutional and legal safeguards for women
- Recommend legislative measures + measures to the Union and States; review laws and suggest amendments
- Take up violations; handle complaints and take suo motu notice (rights denied, laws not implemented); facilitate grievance redressal
- Commission special studies on discrimination and atrocities
- Consultation: The Central Government must consult the NCW on all major policy matters affecting women
- Procedure: May appoint committees + co-opt outsiders; regulates its own procedure
- Jan Sunwai: Public hearings of women's grievances
### NCW, NCPCR, NCM — composition and functions — NCPCR and SCPCRs
## 1. NCPCR and SCPCRs
- Legal basis: Statutory body, set up 2007 under the CPCR Act, 2005 (2026)
- Mandate: Keep laws and policies in line with the Constitution + UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (checks laws against the UNCRC; India acceded 1992)
- NCPCR (2007): Monitors RTE, POCSO and JJ Acts (years on the Acts page)
- POCSO monitoring: NCPCR + State Commissions (SCPCRs)
- SCPCRs: Under the CPCR Act 2005, with NCPCR; both oversee child-rights directions (e.g. SC's menstrual-hygiene ruling for schools, Feb 2026)
## 2. Minorities — notified minorities and State Minorities Commissions
- Notified minorities: Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Parsis + Jains (27 Jan 2014)
- State Minorities Commissions: 15 listed — AP, Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Delhi, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Maharashtra, MP, Manipur, Rajasthan, TN, Uttarakhand, UP, West Bengal

### NHRC: before & after 2019
- NCST — Art. 338A; NCBC — Art. 338B; NCW is statutory (no Art. 338C)
- SHRCs — Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993; Delhi human-rights work stays with the NHRC

- 2019: SHRC Chairperson may be a former Chief Justice or Judge of a High Court
- NCW can form committees, add outsiders and regulate its own procedure
### NHRC appointment & limits
- Selection committee chaired by the Prime Minister (six members; incl. Union Home Minister, Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha)
- NHRC cannot inquire after one year from the alleged violation
- Removal for misbehaviour/incapacity: President, after a Supreme Court inquiry (no parliamentary resolution)
- Zonal Councils — common Chairman: Union Home Minister
### SHRC
- SHRC covers State List and Concurrent List matters, not already before the NHRC or another statutory commission
- Appointed by the Governor, removed only by the President
- Recommending committee: Chief Minister, Speaker, Home Minister, Leader of Opposition (+ Council Chairman and its LoP where a Council exists)
- AP SHRC functions from Kurnool
- Reports of State commissions → Governor, placed before the State Legislature
### NCW vs NCM
- NCW: Chairperson + five Members (at least one SC, one ST) + Member-Secretary — all nominated by the Central Government (not the President)
- NCM: appointed by the Central Government; five of seven members incl. Chairperson from minority communities
