### ECI — constitutional status and composition
## 1. Constitutional status
- Nature: A permanent, independent, constitutional body — Part XV, Articles 324 to 329 relate to it; constitutional basis Article 324 (Part XV)
- Also described as a permanent, independent constitutional body (APPSC-GS 2024); common to the Centre and the states
- Established: 25 January 1950 — celebrated annually as National Voters' Day since 2011
- Article 324(1): Vests the superintendence, direction and control of elections in the Election Commission of India
- Seat: Nirvachan Sadan, New Delhi
- Training arm: IIIDEM, New Delhi
## 2. Composition
- One Chief Election Commissioner + two Election Commissioners — a multi-member body continuously since 1 October 1993
- Strength: CEC + as many ECs as the President fixes; now CEC + 2 ECs / CEC plus 2 ECs = three-member body (APPSC-G2 2025); CEC chairs/presides
- Equal powers; matters decided by majority — CEC and ECs have equal powers; differences settled by majority
- Appointed by: The President of India
- Regional Commissioners: May be appointed by the President after consulting the EC

### Tenure, salary and removal
## 1. Tenure, salary, removal
- Tenure: Six years or until the age of 65, whichever is earlier; 2023 Act bars reappointment
- Salary: salary equivalent to that of Supreme Court Judges — Salary equal to a Supreme Court judge; CEC and ECs equal; service conditions cannot be varied to their disadvantage
- Removal:
- CEC: Only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court Judge — removal safeguard in Article 324(5)
- Grounds: proven misbehaviour or incapacity; special majority in both Houses
- Motion needs 100 Lok Sabha or 50 Rajya Sabha signatures
- Other Election Commissioners and Regional Commissioners: Removed by the President on the recommendation of the CEC (only on the CEC's recommendation); Section 11 of the 2023 Act gives the procedure
- ⚠ CEC removed like an SC judge; ECs only on the CEC's recommendation; SEC like an HC judge
### Constitutional gaps (listed flaws)
## 1. Constitutional gaps (listed flaws)
- Flaws: Constitution prescribes no qualifications and no term for members
- Post-retirement: The Constitution does not bar / Constitution does not debar retiring ECs from further government appointment/office → a listed flaw
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes say the CEC cannot hold any office of profit after retirement; the Constitution has no such bar
- Contrast: CAG (Art. 148(4)) and UPSC Chairman (Art. 319) have constitutional bars
### Powers and functions — administrative
- Range: Decides every step from announcement to results (APPSC-G1 2022)
## 1. Administrative
- Rolls: Prepares and revises electoral rolls
- Special Intensive Revision (SIR) ordered under Art. 324 + Section 21, RPA 1950
- Process: Notifies schedules; scrutinises nominations; regulates expenses
- Constituencies: Decides territorial constituencies under the Delimitation Commission Act
- Model Code of Conduct: Enforces it
- MCC is not a statute; rests on Art. 324; applies from the day the schedule is announced (Harbans Singh Jalal, 1997)
- Delimitation examples:
- J&K: Commission under Justice Ranjana Desai (2022)
- Assam: delimited by the ECI itself (effective Aug 2023)
### Powers and functions — quasi-judicial and advisory
## 1. Quasi-judicial and advisory
- Parties & symbols: Recognises parties and allots symbols under the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968 (issued under RPA 1951 + Art. 324)
- Settles symbol and recognition disputes like a court
- Disqualification advice:
- Advises the President on MPs' disqualification (Art. 103)
- Governor on MLAs' (Art. 192)
- Anti-defection (Tenth Schedule) cases go to the Speaker/Chairman instead
## 2. Election disputes — limits
- Art. 329(b) petitions: Tried by the High Court, appeal to the Supreme Court
- ECI's limits: ECI cannot review a result; does not try election petitions
- It decides party and symbol disputes and advises on disqualification
- ⚠ Disqualification (Art. 103/192) → ECI advises; anti-defection (Tenth Schedule) → Speaker/Chairman

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:62
### Status & composition
## Constitutional base
- Article 324 — Election Commission; lies in Part XV (Articles 324-329)
- Article 324(1): ECI oversees elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President, Vice-President
- Does NOT cover Panchayats and Municipalities — these go to the State Election Commissions
- ECI decides every step: from announcement of schedule to declaration of results
- Model Code of Conduct — not a law; based on Article 324; applies from announcement of the election schedule (Harbans Singh Jalal, 1997)
## Members
- CEC and ECs — equal powers; differences settled by majority
- Salary equal to a Supreme Court judge; service conditions cannot be changed to their disadvantage
- Demand: ECI expenses be charged on the Consolidated Fund of India (ECI depends on DoPT, Law and Home Ministries)
## Related provisions and bodies
- Article 71 — disputes on Presidential and Vice-Presidential elections
- 329(a) bars court challenges to delimitation; 329(b) needs an election petition
- Chief Electoral Officer — State-level officer under ECI control; does NOT conduct local-body polls (State Election Commission does)
- J&K delimitation (2022) — Commission under Justice Ranjana Desai; Assam delimited by the ECI
## National Voters' Day
- ★ 25 January — National Voters' Day, observed since 2011; marks ECI's foundation day (25 January 1950)
### Milestones
- ★ Golden Jubilee of the ECI — 2001
- Diamond Jubilee — 2010; 75 years completed in 2025
- 61st Amendment lowered voting age from 21 to 18; then two ECs added in 1989
- V.S. Ramadevi — first woman CEC, served in 1990; T.N. Seshan — tenth CEC
- ECI is a multi-member (three-member) body continuously since 1 October 1993; members paid like Supreme Court judges
### Cases & Acts
- Article 324 as reservoir — used where no law holds the field; cannot normally override an existing law
- T.N. Seshan v. Union of India (1995) — upheld multi-member EC and majority rule
- Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2023) — interim selection panel: PM, Leader of Opposition (or largest opposition party leader) in Lok Sabha, CJI (until Parliament made a law)
- 2023 Act — replaced the CJI with a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM
- Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968 — party recognition and symbol allotment; issued under the RPA 1951 and Article 324
### Appointment (2023 Act)
## CEC and Other ECs Act, 2023
- Search Committee — headed by the Law Minister; two Secretary-rank members; shortlists five names
- Selection Committee — may pick names outside the shortlist; recommends to the President
- Government majority of 2:1 on the Selection Committee — challenged by ADR and Jaya Thakur; SC said CJI's place was only temporary, until a law
## Other points
- Tarkunde Committee — set up by Jayaprakash Narayan; proposed a three-member EC
- Post-retirement bar: CAG (Article 148(4)) and UPSC Chairman (Article 319); no express bar for retiring Election Commissioners
- ECI advises the Governor on disqualification of State legislators under Article 192; the President under Article 103
- Anti-defection cases go to the Speaker or Chairman
## Election machinery
- Each State: Chief Electoral Officer; removed by the Election Commission (not like a HC judge)
- Only the State Election Commissioner has High Court judge-like protection
- District level: Collector/District Election Officer appoints Returning and Presiding Officers
- Gram Panchayat (all three tiers) elections — conducted by the State Election Commission, not the ECI
### Tenure & removal
- CEC — removed in the same way and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge; safeguard in Article 324(5)
- Removal motion: at least 100 signatures (Lok Sabha) or 50 (Rajya Sabha)
- Election Commissioners and Regional Commissioners — removed only on the CEC's recommendation
- Constitution sets no qualifications and no fixed term for EC members
- ECI: one CEC + two ECs, appointed by the President; term six years or age 65, whichever earlier
- State Election Commissioner — removed like a High Court judge
### Functions
- Special Intensive Revision of rolls — ordered under Article 324 and Section 21 of the RPA, 1950
- ECI cannot review a declared result and does not hear petitions; challenge only through an election petition
### ECI vs SEC
- Article 243ZA — municipal elections (SEC); 243K — Panchayat elections; 324 — ECI
- Chairman of a State Legislative Council — elected by the Council itself under Article 182, not by the ECI
- Presiding officers are chosen by their own Houses
### Exam corner
- Election petitions on Parliament / State Legislature elections — tried by the High Court; appeal to the Supreme Court
- T.N. Seshan era reforms did NOT include transferring election petitions to the EC (they stay with High Courts)
- Gyanesh Kumar — CEC facing the first removal motion against a CEC appointed under the 2023 Act
- Andhra Pradesh: Anil Chandra Punetha — new State Election Commissioner for local-body polls
### ECI — constitutional status and composition
- ★ ECI holds elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President; Constitution gives it superintendence, direction and control of elections
- ★ CEC and ECs appointed by the President — Article 324(2), under law made by Parliament
- ★ ECs removed only by the President on the recommendation of the CEC
- ★ CEC term — up to 6 years or 65 years of age
