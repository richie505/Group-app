### Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961 → sections and penalties — Commencement and definition
## 1. Commencement and definition
- In force: 1 July 1961 (APPSC 2025)
- Dowry s.2 (Section 2): Property or valuable security given or agreed to be given, directly or indirectly, before, at or any time after marriage in connection with it
- Excludes dower/mahr (Muslim personal law)
- Amendments: Amendments of 1984 and 1986
- 1986 brought in dowry death (Sec. 304B IPC) + presumption in Sec. 113B of the Evidence Act
- Presumption of dowry death now in BSA s.118 (Evidence Act 113B)
- Cruelty: Cruelty s.85/86 (BNS; IPC 498A)
### Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961 → sections and penalties — Section → offence / penalty
## 1. Section → offence / penalty
- Scope: Penalises giving, taking or abetting dowry (2026)
| Section | Offence / provision |
| s.3 | Giving, taking or abetting dowry: imprisonment of not less than 5 years + fine of not less than ₹15,000 (Rs 15,000) or the value of the dowry, whichever is more [GK] |
| s.4 | Demanding dowry: 6 months to 2 years + fine up to Rs 10,000 [GK] |
| s.8A | Burden of proof on the accused[GK] |
| s.8B | Dowry Prohibition Officers appointed by the State Govt |
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes say "up to five years" → the Act fixes five years as the minimum
- Nature: Cognisable, non-bailable and non-compoundable
- Cognisance: Courts may act on their own knowledge or on complaints by recognised welfare bodies
- Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2025 - the Act came into force on 1 July 1961.
### Domestic Violence Act, 2005 → definition, authorities, reliefs
## 1. Nature and definition
- In force: 26 October 2006
- Nature: A civil law (only civil); protects women irrespective of the relationship's status with the respondent
- ⚠ Trap: CDI's list of criminal laws wrongly includes it
- Domestic violence (s.3): Physical, sexual, verbal/emotional and economic abuse
## 2. Authorities
- Protection Officers (appointed by State Govt)
- Registered Service Providers assist the aggrieved woman
## 3. Section → relief
| Section | Relief |
| s.18 | Protection orders |
| s.19 | Residence orders |
| Sec. 17 | right to reside / Right to residence |
| s.20 | Monetary relief |
| s.21 | Temporary custody of children |
| s.22 | Compensation |
- Multiple reliefs in one case; disposal/judgment within 60 days
- Exam angle: UPPSC 2015 - the PWDVA came into force on 26 October 2006.
### POSH Act, 2013 → committees and time limits
## 1. Scope
- Addresses: Sexual harassment of women at the workplace (APPSC 2018)
- Definition: Unwelcome physical contact/advances, demands for sexual favours, sexually coloured remarks, showing pornography
- In force: Dec 2013
## 2. Committee → where → composition
| Committee | Where | Composition / key rule |
| Internal Committee (IC/ICC) | Every employer/workplace with 10 or more employees | Senior woman employee as Presiding Officer; 2+ employees; 1 external NGO member; half the members women |
| Local Committee (LC/LCC) | District level; set up by the District Officer | For workplaces with fewer than 10 employees, or complaints against the employer |
- ⚠ IC = workplace with 10 or more; LC = District Officer, fewer than 10
## 3. Time limits → days
| Step | Time limit |
| Complaint | Within 3 months (extendable by 3) |
| Inquiry | Within 90 days |
| Employer acts | Within 60 days |
## 4. Employer duties
- Exam angle: APPSC-GS 2018 - the "Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal Act 2013" deals with sexual harassment of women at the workplace.
### Cases → Supreme Court directives (Vishaka, Laxmi)
| Case | Year | Directive / outcome |
| Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan | 1997 | SC laid down Vishaka guidelines (1997) until a law was enacted → led to the POSH Act, 2013 (in force Dec 2013) |
| Laxmi v. Union of India | SC, 2013-15; Laxmi v Union of India (2013) | Acid sale regulation, compensation, free treatment |
- Laxmi case directives:
- Sale: Over the counter only against a photo ID + stated purpose; sellers keep a sales register
- No sale to under-18s
- Compensation: At least ₹3 lakh
