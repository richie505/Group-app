# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:102 - Public expenditure - revenue and capital, developmental and non-developmental, trends; subsidies
### Revenue vs capital expenditure
- Revenue = running the government; Capital = creating assets
| Revenue (no asset) | Capital (asset / liability) |
| Running the government; recurring | Creates assets; mostly non-recurring |
| Salaries, pensions, interest payments | Roads, airports, irrigation, buildings |
| Subsidies (food, fertiliser) | Defence equipment (capital outlay) |
| Grants to States (even if assets) | Loans to States, PSUs |
| Maintenance of existing assets | Repayment of loans |
### Laws of expenditure growth
- Wagner's law
- Spending rises faster than national income
- Law of increasing state activity
- Peacock-Wiseman
- Rises in steps; wars raise tolerable tax
- Displacement effect
### Union spending 2026-27 BE
- Total ₹53,47,315 crore = 13.6% of GDP; capex ₹12.22 lakh crore (3.1%)
- Revenue exp. share 88% (2014-15) → about 77%
- Rupee goes to: States' taxes 22% > interest 20% > central schemes 17% > defence 11%
- Interest ₹14,03,972 crore = 40% of revenue receipts
- Committed expenditure = 65.3% of revenue receipts
- Effective capex ₹17.15 lakh crore (4.4% of GDP); SASCI ₹1.85 lakh crore
### Subsidies
- 2026-27 total ₹4,54,773 crore; food ₹2,27,629 crore + fertiliser ₹1,70,799 crore = 87%
- Major subsidies ₹4.11 lakh crore = 1.0% of GDP; overshoot ≈ ₹50,000 crore (West Asia)
- NBS from 1 April 2010: fixed per kg of N, P, K, S; 22 grades
- ⚠ Urea is outside NBS
- Oil Pool Account dismantled April 2002 → oil bonds
- 16th Finance Commission: targeted subsidies, sunset clauses

### Revenue vs capital expenditure — definitions and classifying items
## 1. Revenue expenditure
- Does not create assets
- Includes: Salaries (and allowances), pensions, subsidies, interest payments, administrative expenses
## 2. Capital expenditure
- Directed toward acquiring, creating, upgrading and extending the life of assets — asset creation or acquisition
- Strengthens long-term productive capacity — long-term productive investment
- Examples: Land, buildings (land and buildings), equipment, computers, software, new technology, infrastructure or asset creation
## 3. Classification test (Prep)
| Revenue expenditure (no asset, no liability reduction) | Capital expenditure (creates asset / reduces liability) |
| Does not create assets; running the government | Creates physical/financial assets |
| Salaries, pensions, interest payments | Roads, airports, irrigation, buildings |
| Subsidies (food, fertiliser, PDS) | Purchase of defence equipment (capital outlay) |
| Grants to State governments (even if they create assets for States) | Loans and advances to States, PSUs, foreign governments |
| Maintenance of existing assets | Repayment of loans (reduces liability) |
- Test: Expense that changes Govt's assets or liabilities = capital; all else = revenue
- Thumb rule: Revenue = running the government; Capital = creating assets
- Exam angle: food and fertiliser subsidies are revenue expenditure (APPSC-G2 2008).
- ⚠ Grants to States = revenue even if they create assets; loans to States = capital
## 4. AP composition
- AP order: Revenue Expenditure > Capital Outlay > Loans and Advances
- AP share: Revenue expenditure ~83% of 2026-27 BE total
- AP revenue spending: Rice subsidy, power subsidy, welfare pensions, housing
### Economists → laws of public expenditure growth
| Economist / hypothesis | Statement |
| Wagner's law (Adolph Wagner) | Public expenditure rises faster than national income as economy industrialises → law of increasing state activity |
| Peacock-Wiseman hypothesis (1961, UK data) | Public spending rises in steps, not smoothly; wars/crises raise the tolerable tax burden = displacement effect; spending does not fall back afterwards |
- Exam angle: "displacement effect" = Peacock-Wiseman; "continuous rise with income" = Wagner.
### Public expenditure data — Union, States and capex — Union expenditure
## 1. Union expenditure
- Public expenditure (Centre + States): ≈ 28% of GDP (ES 2016-17; APPSC-G2 2017)
- Union BE 2026-27:
- Total expenditure ₹53,47,315 crore = 13.6% of GDP (+7.7%)
- Revenue expenditure ₹41.25 lakh crore (10.5% of GDP)
- Capex ₹12.22 lakh crore (3.1%)
- Revenue expenditure trend:
- Share in total expenditure 88% (2014-15) → about 77% (2026-27 BE)
## 2. Largest items (rupee goes to, 2026-27)
- Ranking: States' share of taxes 22% > interest 20% > central sector schemes 17% > defence 11% > CSS 8% > FC and other transfers 7% = other 7% > major subsidies 6% > pensions 2%
- Interest payments: ₹14,03,972 crore
- 26% of total expenditure
- 40% of revenue receipts (37% in 2013-14; 42% in 2020-21)
- Committed expenditure (salaries, pensions, interest) = 65.3% of revenue receipts
### Public expenditure data — Union, States and capex — Capital expenditure push
## 1. Capital expenditure push
- Capex 2026-27: ₹12,21,821 crore (+11.5%)
- = Capital outlay ₹9,43,042 crore + loans ₹2,78,780 crore
- Growth: 28% in Apr-Nov 2025; 23.7% in Q1 2026-27
- Effective capital expenditure: Capex + grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets
- BE 2026-27 = ₹17.15 lakh crore (4.4% of GDP); of which grants ₹4.93 lakh crore
- Effective capex / fiscal deficit = 101.1%
- SASCI: 50-year interest-free loans to States for capex; ₹1.85 lakh crore in 2026-27

### Subsidies — size, explicit vs hidden, NBS, Oil Pool Account
## 1. Size of subsidy bill
- Budget 2026-27 total: ₹4,54,773 crore (-3.1% vs 2025-26 RE)
- Food ₹2,27,629 crore + fertiliser ₹1,70,799 crore = 87% of bill
- Major subsidies (food, fertiliser, petroleum): ₹4.11 lakh crore = 1.0% of GDP
- Q1 2026-27: Major subsidies up 37.4% on crude-oil prices (West Asia crisis); likely overshoot ≈ ₹50,000 crore
## 2. Explicit vs hidden subsidy
| Explicit subsidy | Implicit (hidden) subsidy |
| Shown in Budget as a line item - food, fertiliser, LPG, export incentives, interest subvention | Under-pricing of publicly provided goods - higher education in public universities, public health, irrigation water, power [GK] |
- Exam angle: hidden subsidy = public university education (APPSC 2018).
## 3. Fertiliser and petroleum mechanisms
| Term | Meaning |
| NBS (Nutrient Based Subsidy) | From 1 April 2010; fixed subsidy per kg of nutrient (N, P, K, S) on decontrolled P&K fertilisers (22 grades incl. DAP, MOP, SSP) |
| Oil Pool Account (Administered Price Mechanism) | Dismantled April 2002; later under-recoveries compensated through oil bonds |
- ⚠ Trap: Urea is outside NBS
## 4. Reform
- 16th FC: Retain only well-targeted subsidies; exclusion criteria; sunset clauses; no off-budget financing of subsidies

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:102
### Revenue vs capital expenditure
## Revenue expenditure
- Does not create assets — salaries, pensions, subsidies, interest, administrative expenses
- ★ Most important item of Union revenue expenditure — interest payment
- Maintenance of existing assets and interest payments — revenue expenditure
- Grants to States — revenue expenditure
- Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts
## Capital expenditure
- Creates assets or reduces liabilities
- Loans and advances to States — capital (affects financial assets); repayment of loans — capital (reduces liabilities)
- Defence equipment purchase — can be capital outlay
- Builds long-term capacity — buys, creates, upgrades or extends life of assets
- Effective capital expenditure = capex + grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets
- Union capex 2026-27 — ₹12,21,821 crore (+11.5%) = capital outlay ₹9,43,042 crore + loans and advances ₹2,78,780 crore
- SASCI — 50-year interest-free loans to States for capex
- Golden Rule — borrow only for asset creation (capital expenditure)
### Laws of expenditure growth
- Wagner's law (law of increasing state activity) — public expenditure grows faster than national income as economy industrialises
- Peacock-Wiseman hypothesis — displacement effect: wars and crises raise tolerable tax burden, stepwise rise in spending
### Union spending 2026-27 BE
