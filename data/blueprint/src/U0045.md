### Kushana rulers → contribution and titles
## 1. Rulers
- Kujula Kadphises: Circulated copper coins
- Vima Kadphises: The father of Kanishka I
- Title: Sarvalokeshvara — Sarvalokeshwar: "Lord of the Whole World"
- The first Kushana ruler to issue gold coins — introduced them for regular use in India; the first to issue them on a large scale
- Kanishka I: A major Kushana ruler — son of Vima Kadphises
- Buddha was depicted on his coins
- Chinese texts identify him as Yue-tche; he requested marriage with a Han princess — refused
- The Kushanas marched against Pan Chao in 86 AD with 70,000 troops — defeated by a smaller Chinese force
- Coronation: 78 AD — many historians consider 78 AD the origin of the Saka Samvat; the Saka–Vikrama difference was 135 years

### Era of 78 CE — founder debate and later name
- Founder debate:
- Commonly credited to Kanishka (UPSC)

### Fourth Buddhist Council — place, patron, president
- Held at Kundalvana in Kashmir in the 1st century AD, during Kanishka's reign
- President: Vasumitra (a Buddhist scholar who headed it); vice-president: Ashvaghosha
- Resulted in the division of Buddhism into Hinayana and Mahayana — Mahayana treated the Buddha as God; idol worship became associated with it

### Kushana coinage — FIRST large-scale gold, deities, portraits
## 1. Yaudheyas
- Yaudheya copper coins: Six-headed Kartikeya with a peacock — associated with the Kushana period; the Yaudheyas are mentioned in the Puranas and the Ashtadhyayi
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the Yaudheya coins show a Standing Kartikeya with a peacock
## 2. Kushana coinage
- Kushanas: Associated with the first gold coins — gold coins had wider, regular and large-scale circulation; copper coins circulated extensively; Kushana coins contained gold and copper
- Vima Kadphises: The first Kushana ruler to issue gold coins — used them regularly on a large scale
- Kujula Kadphises: Early Kushana copper coinage
- Kanishka: Gold and copper coins bearing Buddha
- FIRST large-scale gold: Vima Kadphises first issued gold coins on a large scale (BPSC 2018)
- Gold dinara:
- Roman weight standard, about 8 g (120 grains)
- Gold of 22-23 carat (UPSC)
- Daily trade: plentiful copper and lead coins
- Portrait style: Kanishka standing in peaked helmet, long coat and boots, sacrificing at an altar (UPSC)
- ⚠ Copper = Kujula Kadphises; large-scale gold = Vima Kadphises
### Items introduced by Shakas and Kushanas — dress and cavalry
- The Manusmriti described Shakas, Kushans, Hunas and Pratiharas who did not follow priestly customs or local traditions as "Fallen Kshatriyas"
- APPSC G1 2024 key: trousers, heavy long coat, helmet, boots
- UPSC notes (and NCERT) also list turbans, saddles and armour - follow the APPSC key for APPSC, but know the other version
- Military: better cavalry; wider use of saddles and reins in riding (UPSC)
### Universal angles
- Current relevance (2026):
- Stupas at Zehanpora (Kashmir) show the valley's Gandhara Buddhist heritage
- Significance:
- Silk Road middlemen between Han China, Rome and India
- Trade → "second wave of urbanisation" - Mathura, Taxila, Ujjain, Shravasti
- AP angle:
- Nagarjuna (Madhyamika) tied to Andhra - Sriparvata and the Satavahana court
- Amaravati school flourished alongside Gandhara and Mathura
- FIRST/LAST: first large-scale gold coinage = Vima Kadphises
