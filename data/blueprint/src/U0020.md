### Territorial kingship — terms and titles
## 1. From tribe to territory
| Term | Meaning |
| rashtra | Territory/state - word first appears |
| Ekarat | "Sole ruler"; title of paramount kingship in the Aitareya Brahmana |
- Shift: jana (tribe) → janapada (territorial kingdom)
## 2. Nature of kingship
- Succession: hereditary (usually the eldest son) in the Kshatriya varna; traces of election remain
## 3. Titles by region (Aitareya Brahmana)
- Forms of rule (Aitareya Brahmana): Rajya, Bhojya, Swarajya, Vairajya, Samrajya, by region
- ⚠ East = Samrat; North = Virat; West = Svarat; South = Bhoja
### Kingdoms → region
| Kingdom | Region |
| Kuru | Delhi-Meerut / upper Doab; capital Hastinapur; Kurukshetra |
| Panchala | Central Doab-Rohilkhand; Ahichchhatra [GK] |
| Kosala | Eastern UP |
| Kashi | Varanasi region [GK] |
| Videha | North Bihar (beyond the Sadanira/Gandak); King Janaka [GK] |
- Kuru-Panchala alliance: Dominated the upper Ganga valley through marriage and military links
- East: Kosala, Kashi, Videha rose → leading to the Mahajanapadas
### Texts → theory of divine origin of kingship
| Text | Statement on kingship |
| Aitareya Brahmana | Earliest account of the origin of kingship: gods losing to the Asuras → chose a king to lead them; King = protector of Brahmins |
| Shatapatha Brahmana | Links the king to Prajapati (creator god, now dominant) |
- Aitareya view: Kingship arose from need for a war leader; later backed by divine sanction
- Rites: Rajasuya + Vajapeya meant to give the king divine qualities
- Legitimacy: Brahmanas' ritual monopoly supported royal legitimacy
### Royal sacrifices → purpose and titles
| Sacrifice | Purpose |
| Rajasuya | Royal consecration conferring supreme power; five major rites incl. Ratnahavimshi (offerings at the ratnins' houses) |
| Ashvamedha | Horse sacrifice: supremacy over other kings and unquestioned control over the area the horse roamed (Aitareya and Shatapatha Brahmanas) |
| Vajapeya | "Drink of strength"; includes a chariot race won by the king: superiority over his kinsmen/people |
- Titles won through yajnas (Ikshvaku kings):
- Vajapeya = Samrat/Virat
- Ashvamedha = Swarat
- Sources: Aitareya and Shatapatha Brahmanas
- Effect:
- Large Shrauta sacrifices needed many priests + heavy dakshina → raised the power/position of Brahmanas
- ⚠ Ashvamedha = over other kings; Vajapeya = over kinsmen; Rajasuya = supreme power
### Assemblies, army and taxes — fate and nature
## 1. Assemblies
| Assembly | Later Vedic fate |
| Vidatha | Vidatha disappeared. |
| Village assemblies | Handled local cases |
- Sabha and Samiti continued: Survived but came under control of chiefs, Brahmanas and wealthy nobles
## 3. Taxes — bali, shulka, bhaga
| Tax | Nature |
| Bali | Earlier voluntary; now a regular tribute |
| Bhaga | King's share of produce |
| Shulka | Customs/toll |
- Collection: Collected by the Bhagadugha; managed by the Sangrahitri (treasurer)
- Payers: Vaishyas = main tribute-payers
- Source conflict on Bali:
- Some notes still call Bali "voluntary offerings" in this period
- Others describe regular, compulsory taxation → follow this view
### Universal angles
- Significance: Tribal chiefdom → hereditary territorial monarchy; prepared the way for the Mahajanapadas of the 6th century BCE
- Comparative note:
- Early Vedic = elective chief, voluntary bali, strong assemblies
- Later Vedic = hereditary king, regular bali-bhaga-shulka, ratnins, assemblies in decline
- AP angle: Early AP dynasties (Satavahanas, Ikshvakus, Vishnukundins) performed Ashvamedha, Rajasuya and Vajapeya to proclaim sovereignty
- Ashvamedha altar found at Nagarjunakonda
