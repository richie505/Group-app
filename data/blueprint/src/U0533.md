### Money market vs capital market — definitions and instruments
| Basis | Money market | Capital market |
| Tenure | Up to 1 year (364 days) | Above 1 year |
| Regulator | RBI | SEBI |
| Instruments | T-bills (91/182/364 days; see G-Secs), CP, CD | Equities = capital-market instrument |
- Participation: Individuals do not directly participate in the money market; no physical location
| Instrument | Maturity | Issuer / notes |
| Commercial Paper (1990, Vaghul Committee) | 14 days-12 months (APP); RBI now 7 days-1 year [GK] | Corporates (net worth ≥ ₹5 crore), rated by CRISIL/ICRA etc.; unsecured |

| Call/notice money | Overnight / 2-14 days [GK] | Inter-bank |
| TREPS (replaced CBLO) | Short term | Tri-party repo; third party handles collateral and settlement |
| Commercial bills | Usually 90 days | Trade bills drawn by firms, discounted by banks |
- ⚠ Vaghul Committee: CD 1989, CP 1990
### Government securities — types, Treasury Bills and bonds
## 1. Securities
- Government dated securities — G-Secs: Long-term government securities
- Treasury bills: Short-term government debt instruments
- Market-based securities: Issued through auctions
## 2. Types
| Security | Issued by | Maturity |
| Treasury Bills | The Central Government (RBI for Centre/GoI; zero-coupon, sold at a discount, no interest) | Short-term — less than one year (91, 182, 364 days; original maturity < 1 year) |
| Government Bonds or Dated Securities (G-Secs) | The Central Government | Long-term — at least one year (1 year or more) |
| State Development Loans (State Development Loans (SDLs)) | State Governments — state government bonds or dated securities | — (long term) |
| Cash Management Bills | Centre - temporary cash mismatches | Very short term |
## 3. Treasury Bills
- Short-term debt instruments — money-market instruments
- Zero-coupon securities — do not pay periodic interest; issued at a discount to face value and redeemed at face value on maturity
- Tenors: 91 days, 182 days, 364 days
- Sold by the Reserve Bank of India through auction
- First issued in India in 1917
## 4. Government Bonds
- Maturity of at least one year; interest-bearing securities
- Issued by the Central Government or State Governments
- Returns through coupon payments and principal repayment
### Gilt-edged market and government securities — types, Acts, participants
## 1. Gilt-edged market and G-Secs
| Term | Meaning |
| Gilt-edged market | Market for Treasury Bills and Government bonds (govt securities + those guaranteed by govt); "gilt-edged" because default risk ≈ zero |
| G-Sec | Tradeable instrument issued by the Central or State Governments, acknowledging government debt |
- Act: Issued by RBI on behalf of the Government under the Government Securities Act, 2006
- Exam angle: "Gilt-edged market deals with" - government securities, not corporate paper.
## 2. Market participants and access
- Primary dealers: RBI-regulated underwriters/market makers in G-Secs; SBI DFHI = largest
- RBI Retail Direct: Individuals buy G-Secs directly via a Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) account
- Who trades: Banks, PDs, insurers, MFs, provident funds, FPIs, individuals (via Retail Direct)
- OMOs: RBI sells G-Secs → absorb liquidity; buys → inject
### Capital market — structure, instruments and AIF categories
## 1. Structure
- Gilt-edged market
- Industrial securities market (primary + secondary)
- Development financial institutions (IFCI, ICICI, IDBI, SIDBI, UTI)
- Financial intermediaries (merchant banks, MFs, leasing, venture capital companies)
## 2. Instruments
| Type | Instruments |
| Pure | Equity, preference shares, debentures, bonds |
| Hybrid | Convertible preference shares, partly/optionally convertible debentures |
| Derivatives | - |
| Venture capital | Long-term start-up capital to new entrepreneurs |
| Private equity | Investment in unlisted firms |
| ETFs | Trade on exchanges close to NAV (CPSE ETF, then Bharat-22 ETF) |
## 3. Alternative investment funds
- Venture capital funds: Category I
- Hedge funds: Category III
- AIFs: Privately pooled vehicles regulated by SEBI; SEBI added an AIF category only for accredited investors
| SEBI AIF category | Examples |
| Category I [GK] | Venture capital, angel, SME, social venture, infrastructure funds |
| Category II [GK] | Private equity, debt funds - e.g. Self-Reliant India Fund (MSME) |
| Category III [GK] | Hedge funds, leveraged trading; allowed in commodity derivatives |
- REITs vs InvITs: REITs (real estate) and InvITs (infrastructure) pool income-yielding assets
- Used for asset monetisation
- First public InvIT planned for 2026
### Bonds — key terms and yield drivers
## 1. Terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Bond | A debt instrument through which the issuer borrows money from investors |
| Bondholder | A creditor or lender to the issuer — receives interest or returns according to the bond's terms |
| Issuer | A government, company or other eligible entity raising funds |
| Maturity | The date when the principal or face value becomes repayable |
| Face value / par value | The nominal value of a bond |
| Coupon / interest | The stated interest payment on a bond |
| Yield | The return earned by an investor |
- ⚠ Market yield and bond price generally move in opposite directions (Core rule: Price and yield move inversely)
## 2. What moves yields
| Factor | Effect on yields |
| Higher policy rates, inflation, government borrowing | Yields rise |
| RBI buying (OMO) + foreign inflows | Yields fall |

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:89
### Money vs capital market
- ★ Call money market — most unstable part of the organised money market; repayable on demand, overnight to 14 days
- Money market (up to one year) — regulated by the RBI; capital market (above one year) — by SEBI
- Hybrid instruments — convertible preference shares, partly or optionally convertible debentures; equity shares are pure; T-Bills and CPs are money market
### Money market instruments
- Commercial Paper (CP) — unsecured short-term promissory note of eligible corporates; 7 days to 1 year
- Vaghul Committee — CP (1990) and CDs (1989)
- Call money — overnight inter-bank; notice money — 2 to 14 days; TREPS — tri-party repo
- Industrial securities market — primary and secondary segments
- Budget 2026–27: market-making for corporate bonds, derivatives on corporate bond indices, larger municipal bond markets
### Government securities
- G-Sec — tradeable paper showing debt of the Central or a State Government; issued by the RBI under the Government Securities Act, 2006
- Treasury Bills — sold by RBI auction for the Centre; 91, 182 and 364 days; zero-coupon
- Cash Management Bills — Centre's temporary cash mismatches
- Dated securities — one year or more; issued by the Central Government
- State Development Loans (SDLs) — State Governments' long-term borrowing / dated securities
- Gilt-edged market — Treasury Bills and Government bonds; default risk almost zero
### T-bills & gilt market
- ★ Gilt-edged market — market of Government securities; interest and repayment on due date are sure
- SBI DFHI — largest primary dealer; primary dealers are RBI-regulated underwriters and market makers in G-Secs
- OMO: RBI sells G-secs to absorb liquidity; buying adds liquidity
- Treasury Bills first issued in India — 1917 (First World War)
- Yield — investor's return at market price; face value — nominal value printed on the security
### SEBI AIF categories
- Category III AIF — hedge funds, leveraged trading (may trade commodity derivatives); Category II — private equity and debt funds
- September 2025 — SEBI added an AIF category only for accredited investors
- REITs pool real-estate assets; InvITs pool infrastructure assets
- Bond terms: bondholder — creditor to the issuer; maturity — repayment date; coupon — interest; face value — nominal value
### Bond types
- Masala bond — rupee-denominated, issued overseas; investor bears currency risk
- Sovereign Gold Bond (2015) — 2.5% interest plus gold-price-linked returns; new issues stopped
- First green bond — European Investment Bank, 2007; World Bank labelled green bond 2008
- Treasury Bills — zero-coupon; Government bonds pay interest
- Bond price and yield move in opposite directions
### What moves yields
- RBI OMO purchases of G-secs → lower yields (support prices, add liquidity)
- Higher policy rates, inflation and government borrowing → raise yields
### Exam corner
- ★ Commercial paper — credit source for corporate industry; started 1990; unsecured promissory note; minimum 7 days
- CCP regulators: RBI — CCIL (money, G-Sec, forex markets); SEBI — NSCCL and ICCL (capital markets); IFSCA only IFSC entities
### Money market vs capital market — definitions and instruments
- ★ Capital market — long-term funds (debt and equity): Government Bond Market and Stock Market
- ★ Money market: Call Money Market, Treasury Bill Market
- ★ CBLO (Collateralised Borrowing and Lending Obligation) — money market instrument; by CCIL from 20 January 2003; needs collateral
### Government securities — types, Treasury Bills and bonds
- ★ T-Bills maturities — 91, 182 and 364 days
- ★ T-Bills sold by the RBI on auction basis; first issued 1917
- ★ As per the PYQ key, RBI is not a money-market participant; money market covers overnight to one year
### Gilt-edged market and government securities — types, Acts, participants
- ★ A US debt default does not cancel Treasury bondholders' legal claims to payment
- US government debt — not backed by hard assets, only by the faith of the Government
### Capital market — structure, instruments and AIF categories
- ★ Promoting venture capital — entrepreneur development measure

- ★ AIFs: hedge funds and venture capital (not bonds, stocks); privately pooled from expert investors; defined under SEBI Regulations, 2012
- ★ InvIT dividends and interest income — fully taxable at the investor's slab rate
### Bonds — key terms and yield drivers
