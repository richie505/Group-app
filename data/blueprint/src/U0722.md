# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 5:32 - Data protection and privacy - Puttaswamy judgment, Digital Personal Data Protection Act, data localisation
### Puttaswamy (2017)
- Privacy is intrinsic to life and liberty under Article 21
- Right to Privacy = Fundamental Right under Art. 21
- Nine-judge bench, unanimous; 24 August 2017
- Overruled M.P. Sharma (1954), Kharak Singh (1962)
- Tests: legality, legitimate aim, proportionality
- Aadhaar (26 Sep 2018): 5 judges, 4:1; PAN linking upheld, bank & SIM linking struck down
### Road to DPDP Act
- 2012 — Justice A.P. Shah Group of Experts on Privacy
- July 2018 — Justice B.N. Srikrishna draft PDP Bill
- 2019 — PDP Bill → JPC
- 2021 — JPC report, 93 amendments
- 2022 — Bill withdrawn
- 11 Aug 2023 — DPDP Act assent
- 14 Nov 2025 — DPDP Rules notified
### DPDP terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Data Principal | Individual whose data is processed |
| Data Fiduciary | Decides purpose and means |
| Significant Data Fiduciary | DPO in India, auditor, impact assessment |
| Consent Manager | Gives, manages, withdraws consent |
### Penalties (maximum)
- ▲ Rs 250 crore — Security safeguards failure
- ▸ Rs 200 crore — Children's data
- ▸ Rs 150 crore — SDF duties
- ▸ Rs 50 crore — Residual breach
- ▸ Rs 10,000 — Data Principal duties
### Board, rights & exemptions
- Data Protection Board: adjudicatory, New Delhi; appeals to TDSAT
- Rights: correction, erasure (Sec. 12), withdrawal of consent
- Children: verifiable parental consent under 18; no targeted ads
- Sec. 17: broad State exemptions; Sec. 7 legitimate uses
- Sec. 44(3) amended RTI Sec. 8(1)(j): no public-interest override
- Consent Manager: November 2026; full compliance May 2027
### DPDP vs GDPR

| Scope | Only digital data | All personal data |
| Penalties | Financial only | Financial + criminal |

### Right to privacy — Puttaswamy and earlier cases
## 1. Puttaswamy (2017)
- K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017) (K.S. Puttaswamy v UoI): Declared Right to Privacy a Fundamental Right under Art. 21 (Article 21) and Part III; also linked to Art. 19
- Privacy intrinsic to life and liberty (PYQ 2022)
- Bench: Nine-judge bench (9-judge bench, unanimous); 24 August 2017
- Overruled: M.P. Sharma (1954) and Kharak Singh (1962) to the extent they denied privacy
## 2. Cases → point
| Case | Point |
| Kharak Singh v State of UP (1962) | Early recognition of privacy concerns under Art. 21 (CDI) |
| Gobind v State of MP (1975) | Privacy recognised as part of personal liberty (CDI) |
| Puttaswamy (2017) | Privacy a fundamental right; informational privacy (CDI) |
## 3. Tests and follow-up
- Tests for state intrusion/restriction: Legality (a law), legitimate aim / need, proportionality
- Later applied to Aadhaar (2018)
- Follow-up: Took note of the government's expert committee (Justice Srikrishna) on a data-protection law; later followed by → Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, with a Data Protection Board
- ⚠ Puttaswamy = 9 judges, unanimous (2017); Aadhaar = 5 judges, 4:1 (2018)

### DPDP Act, 2023 — scope, terms → meaning, exemptions
## 1. The Act
- Scope:
- Data processed digitally or collected digitally; excludes non-personal data
- Covers entities in India + those offering goods/services to people in India from abroad
## 2. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Data Principal | Individual whose data is processed (gender-neutral term); parent/guardian for a child (under 18) |
| Data Fiduciary | Decides purpose and means of processing |
| Significant Data Fiduciary | Notified by Centre on volume/sensitivity/risk; extra duties: must appoint a DPO in India, independent auditor, impact assessment |
| Consent Manager | Registered entity through which a principal gives, manages and withdraws consent |
## 3. Exemptions and State processing
- Exemptions (Sec. 17): Centre can exempt state instrumentalities - notified State agencies (sovereignty, security, public order)
- E.g., law enforcement exempt from consent during biometric surveillance
- Research, archiving and statistics exempt
- Startups can be exempted; journalism gets no exemption
- Section 7: "Legitimate uses" lets the State process data without consent
### DPDP — rights, duties, Board and penalties
## 1. Data Principals' rights and duties
- Rights: Information/access, correction, erasure, grievance redress, withdrawal of consent, nomination
- Duties: No false information or impersonation; no frivolous complaints
## 2. Children's data
- Consent: Verifiable parental consent for under-18s
- Bans: No tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted ads aimed at children
## 3. Data Protection Board of India
- Nature: Adjudicatory body set up by the Centre; HQ New Delhi; members appointed by the Central Government
- Appeals: Lie to TDSAT
- Commencement: Board provisions took effect immediately with the final Rules (Nov 2025)
- Earlier (2026 notes): Board "prepared", awaiting commencement
## 4. Penalties
| Breach | Maximum penalty |
| Failure of reasonable security safeguards | Rs 250 crore |
| Obligations regarding children's data | Rs 200 crore |
| Additional duties/obligations of SDFs | Rs 150 crore (₹150 crore) |
| Residual breach | Rs 50 crore |
| Duties of Data Principals (false information) | Rs 10,000 (up to ₹10,000) |
- ⚠ 250 – 200 – 150 – 50 crore = security safeguards – children – SDFs – residual
### DPDP Rules 2025 and the RTI amendment → dates and provisions
## 1. DPDP Rules 2025 — phased implementation
- Timeline:
- Draft Rules released 3 Jan 2025
- Final DPDP Rules notified 14 November 2025
| Phase | What comes into force |
| Immediately (Nov 2025) | Provisions establishing the Data Protection Board |
| November 2026 (12 months) | Consent Manager provisions |
| May 2027 (18 months) | Full operational compliance |
- Consent: Clear, specific, informed consent; plain-language notices
- Fiduciary duties: Purpose limitation, data minimisation, retention controls
## 2. RTI–privacy: Sec. 44(3) DPDP Act
- Sec. 44(3) DPDP Act (Section 44(3)): Amended Sec. 8(1)(j) RTI Act (RTI Act Section 8(1)(j))
- Authorities can refuse all "personal information" outright
- Removed the earlier public-interest override and the "no relationship to public activity" test
## 3. Litigation → persons
- Reference: Challenges referred to a Constitution Bench
- Scope: SC to examine "personal data" vs "public data" on a plea by journalist Geeta Seshu and SFLC
- Aug 2026: SC asked whether the law is "crippling RTI"
### Data localisation and cross-border transfers
- RBI (April 2018): All payment-system data stored only in India
- DPDP Act cross-border: Blacklist (negative-list) approach - transfers permitted/allowed except to countries the Centre (government) restricts; critics call it light-touch
- National standards: India's first national standards for Cloud Computing, Data Centres and Ethical AI
- Focus on data sovereignty, algorithmic bias, privacy; aligned with DPDP Act
- ⚠ DPDP = blacklist (allowed unless restricted); GDPR = whitelist / adequacy
### Global frameworks — GDPR, Right to be Forgotten, DPDP vs GDPR
## 1. GDPR
- GDPR: Adopted by the European Union in April 2016; implemented from 25 May 2018 (UPSC 2019 PYQ)
## 2. Right to be Forgotten (RTBF)
- Meaning: Removal, masking or de-indexing of outdated, irrelevant or harmful personal information
- Origin: EU case Google Spain v. AEPD (2014); formalised as the 'Right to Erasure' in GDPR (Art. 17)
- In India:
- Part of privacy under Art. 21 (Puttaswamy); not absolute
- Does not erase judicial records; only limits name-based searches
- DPDP Act gives a Right to Erasure (Sec. 12) (DPDP Section 12); no standalone law
- UoI framework to de-index name searches of acquitted persons
- ⚠ Right to Erasure = GDPR Art. 17; DPDP Sec. 12
## 3. DPDP Act vs GDPR
| Point | DPDP Act 2023 (India) | GDPR (EU) |
| Scope | Only digital personal data | All personal data |
| Terms | Data Principal / Data Fiduciary | Data Subject / Data Controller |
| Regulator | Data Protection Board of India (adjudicatory) | Data Protection Authority in each member state (one DPA per member state) |
| Cross-border | Blacklist (allowed unless restricted) | Whitelist / adequacy |
| Penalties | Financial only, up to Rs 250 crore | Financial + criminal in some states |
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- Delhi HC in Laksh Vir Singh Yadav v UoI: framework to de-index name-based results for acquitted persons
- Consent Manager rules due Nov 2026
- National standards on cloud, data centres and ethical AI linked to DPDP Act
- Criticism & debates: Broad Sec. 17 State exemptions; no data portability; Board independence; RTI dilution via Sec. 44(3); journalists as "data fiduciaries"
- AP angle: AP Police AI4AP pilot in Chittoor, Guntur, Annamayya raises DPDP compliance issues
