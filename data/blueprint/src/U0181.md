### Ilbert Bill controversy, 1883 — aim, "White Mutiny", compromise
- Introduced (1883): Under Viceroy Lord Ripon, drafted by Law Member C.P. Ilbert
- Aim: Let Indian magistrates (district magistrates and sessions judges) try European British subjects - Europeans till then tried only by European judges
- Opposition: Europeans protested violently - fierce agitation (the "White Mutiny")
- Compromise: Ripon watered the Bill down (diluted) - a European accused tried by an Indian judge could claim a jury of which at least half were Europeans (1884)
- Significance: Showed racial discrimination persisted; exposed British racism + showed Indians the value of organised agitation
- Indian Association: Backed the Bill against the European agitation
