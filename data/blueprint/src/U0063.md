### Chandragupta II Vikramaditya
- Killed elder brother Ramagupta, married Dhruvadevi (Devichandraguptam)
- Defeated Rudrasimha III, last Saka → Sakari; western Malwa, Gujarat
- Ujjain = second capital; Udayagiri inscription of Virasena
- Silver coins on Saka model, about 33 grains
- Daughter Prabhavati → Vakataka Rudrasena II; queen Kuberanaga
- Fa-Hien c. 399-414; Navaratnas: Kalidasa, Amarasimha, Varahamihira

### Chandragupta II Vikramaditya → accession, titles, conquests, alliances, court — Ramagupta episode and accession
## 1. Rulers — known as, called
| Personality | Title |
| Chandragupta I | Maharajadhiraja |
| Samudragupta | The Napoleon of India |
| Chandragupta II | Vikramaditya, Devagupta, Devaraja, Devashri, Sakari — the Saka Conqueror |
## 2. Ramagupta episode and accession
- Source: Devichandraguptam of Vishakhadatta
- Story:
- Ramagupta (elder brother) agreed to surrender his queen Dhruvadevi to a Saka king
- Chandragupta II killed the Saka → then killed Ramagupta + married Dhruvadevi
- Succession: Chandragupta II took the throne after killing his elder brother (BPSC 2022 key)
## 3. Titles and coins
- Coins: Lion-slayer type (his father appears as tiger-slayer)
### Chandragupta II Vikramaditya → accession, titles, conquests, alliances, court — Sakas and second capital
## 1. Sakas and second capital
- Saka conquest: Defeated Rudrasimha III → annexed western Malwa and Gujarat, reached the western sea
- Second capital: Made Ujjain the second capital
- Evidence:
- Udayagiri cave inscription of Virasena records his campaign
- Silver coins: Modelled on Saka silver coins, issued after the Saka conquest for the new region → strongest evidence of the Saka victory
- Weight about 33 grains (MPPSC 2023, UPPSC 2022 keys)
- ⚠ Trap: "Gold coins modelled on Saka silver coins" is wrong → the Saka-model coins were silver
- ⚠ Trap: Crediting Chandragupta I with the first silver coins and Saka victory is wrong
## 2. Alliances
| Alliance | Detail |
| Naga | Married Kuberanaga, a Naga princess |
| Vakataka | Daughter Prabhavati(gupta) married Rudrasena II; indirect control over the Vakataka kingdom |
| Dhruvadevi | Married after killing Ramagupta |
### Chandragupta II Vikramaditya → accession, titles, conquests, alliances, court — Fa-Hien and court
## 1. Fa-Hien and court
- Fa-Hien: Visited India in his reign (UPSC 2025 key), c. 399-414 CE
- Never named the king → focus was Buddhism
- Navaratnas (nine gems, a tradition): Kalidasa, Amarasimha, Dhanvantari, Varahamihira, Vararuchi (Prakrit Prakasha), Shanku, Ghatakarpara, Kshapanaka, Vetalabhatta (Vetala Bhatta)
- ⚠ Trap: Fa-Hien listed as a court member → he was a pilgrim, not a courtier
### Kumaragupta I and Skandagupta → titles and contributions
## 1. Kumaragupta I
- Titles: Shakraditya and Mahendraditya
- Institution: Founded Nalanda University
- Coins: New gold types showing Kartikeya + king feeding a peacock
- Threats: Pushyamitras + first Huna attack late in his reign (413-455) → repulsed by prince Skandagupta
## 2. Skandagupta
- Reign: Skandagupta (455-467)
- Invasions faced:
- Pushyamitras (end of Kumaragupta's reign)
- Hunas → beat back / repulsed twice (twice, per some notes); title Vikramaditya
- UPPSC 2015: the Gupta ruler who defeated the Hunas
- Gold coins: Bow and arrow + Garuda standard + Lakshmi
- Administration: Central control weakened → governors became hereditary feudatories
- ⚠ Kumaragupta I coins = Kartikeya + peacock; Skandagupta coins = Garuda + Lakshmi
### Huna invasions → leader, Gupta reign, opponent
## 1. Gupta-period conflicts
- Saka conflict: Chandragupta-II Vikramaditya defeated Rudrasimha-III — the last Saka ruler — during the Gupta period; a Western India campaign; Chandragupta-II assumed the Sakari title and defeated the Sakas
- Gupta–Huna conflict: Skandagupta defeated the Hunas — the first Huna invasion occurred during his reign — recorded in the Bhitari Pillar Inscription
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the first Huna attack came late in Kumaragupta I's reign and was repulsed by prince Skandagupta
- Toramana led the second Huna invasion; Mihirakula was defeated by Yashodharman
## 2. Huna waves
- Origin: Hunas (Hephthalites / White Huns) from Central Asia via the Oxus valley → occupied Gandhara in the mid-5th century
| Huna wave / leader | Gupta reign | Repelled / defeated by |
| First attack (Hephthalites / White Huns via the Oxus, took Gandhara) | Late Kumaragupta I | Prince Skandagupta |
| Later waves | Skandagupta | Skandagupta (repulsed twice; Bhitari pillar) |
| Toramana | Budhagupta and after, c. 500 CE [GK] | Occupied Malwa and central India (by 485 CE Hunas held eastern Malwa, APP) |
| Mihirakula (son of Toramana, persecuted Buddhism) | Narasimhagupta [GK] | Yashodharman of Malwa (APP) and Narasimhagupta Baladitya[GK] |
- Toramana (late 5th c.): Under weak successors, conquered western India up to Eran (near Bhopal)
- By 485 CE: Hunas held Punjab, Rajasthan, Kashmir, eastern Malwa, central India
- Mihirakula (c. 515 CE): Toramana's son; tyrant + iconoclast who persecuted Buddhists
- Defeated by Yashodharman of Malwa, Narasimhagupta Baladitya and the Maukharis
## 3. Yashodharman of Mandasor
- Role: Gupta feudatory in Malwa who defeated Mihirakula
- Victory pillars: At Mandasor (Sondani), c. 532 CE
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes misread this as "532 victory pillars"
- Effect: His rise undermined Gupta authority → victory over the Hunas did not revive the empire
- Exam angle: Kumaragupta I = first attack; Skandagupta = repulsed; Yashodharman = defeated Mihirakula (UPPSC 2016).
### Inscriptions → what they record
## 1. Early-medieval north
- Gwalior Inscription: Records the territory of Mihirakula; Nagabhata I (Gurjara-Pratiharas) destroyed the army of a Mlechcha ruler — interpreted in the source as probably the Arab ruler of Sindh
- Sondani Inscription: Yashodharman — composed by Vasula Kavi
- Maukhari Copper-Seal: Sarvavarman — found at Asirgarh Fort, Madhya Pradesh; gives the Maukhari genealogy, beginning with Harivarman
- Chausath Yogini Temple Inscription: Morena, Madhya Pradesh — 1323 CE; the Kachchhapaghata dynasty; records that Devapala built the temple
## 2. Gupta inscriptions
| Inscription | Records |
| Bilsad | Kumaragupta I - Gupta year 96 = 415 CE, his earliest |
| Damodarpur copper plates | Kumaragupta I - guilds acting as banks |
| Bhitari pillar (Ghazipur, UP) [GK] | Skandagupta's victory over the Hunas (UPPSC 2014); victories over the Pushyamitras and Hunas |
| Eran boar (Varaha) | Built in Toramana's first regnal year - Huna rule over Eran [GK] |
| Mandasor pillars | Yashodharman's defeat of Mihirakula[GK] |
- Eran (510 CE): Inscription of Bhanugupta's general Goparaja → earliest sati record
- Literary: Banabhatta's Harshacharita also mentions the Hunas
### Causes of Gupta decline — factor → effect
## 1. Causes
| Factor | Effect |
| External | Repeated Huna invasions (Huna invasions drained treasury and army) + Pushyamitra attack |
| Weak successors | Weak successors after Skandagupta; last ruler Vishnugupta (c. 540-550 CE) |
| Religious policy | Later Guptas' leaning to Buddhism listed as a cause in some questions |
- Feudatory:
- Land grants + hereditary governors created feudatories
- Feudatories broke away: Yashodharman; Bihar, Bengal, MP, Valabhi, Gujarat, western Malwa
- Rise of Maukharis, Maitrakas, Later Guptas, Pushyabhutis
- Economic:
- Land grants cut revenue + falling trade revenue → no professional army could be kept
- Loss of western India → lost trade income + Roman silk trade
- Silk weavers migrated from Gujarat to Malwa (AD 473), left their craft
- ⚠ Trap: The Arab invasion was not a cause (UKPSC 2012)
## 2. Coinage debasement as evidence
- Debasement: Later Gupta gold coins = less gold, more alloy
- Disappearance: Coins gradually vanish post-Gupta → payments shifted to land
- Lost territory: No Gupta coins or inscriptions in western Malwa and Saurashtra after Skandagupta
### Successor powers and regional kingdoms → region
## 1. Successor powers in the north
| Power / dynasty | Region |
| Later Guptas | Magadha |
| Maukharis | Bihar and UP (Kannauj) |
| Maitrakas | Gujarat / western Malwa (capital Valabhi) |
| Pushyabhutis | Thaneshwar / Thanesar (Haryana), later Kannauj |
| Aulikaras | Mandsaur (Dashapura), Malwa - Yashodharman defeated Mihirakula |
- Aulikara area records:
- Mandsaur prashasti of the silk weavers' guild (Kumaragupta I + Bandhuvarman)
- Yashodharman's Mandsaur pillar inscriptions
- UPSC 2021 answer: Guptas of Magadha, Pushyabhutis of Thanesar, Maukharis of Kannauj, Maitrakas of Valabhi
- ⚠ Trap: Not the Paramaras of Malwa or the Yadavas of Devagiri (both later)
## 2. Regional kingdoms in the Deccan and south
| Dynasty | Region | Founder / key ruler |
| Vakatakas | Northern Maharashtra, Vidarbha | Harishena patronised Ajanta |
| Kadambas | Banavasi (Karnataka) | Founded by Mayurasharma |
| Vishnukundins | Andhra | Madhava Varma II (440-495 CE) = greatest |
- Vakatakas:
- Brahmins; successors of the Satavahanas; contemporaries of the Guptas
- Rudrasena II's marriage to Prabhavati Gupta → see Chandragupta II's alliances
- Vishnukundins:
- Madhava Varma II won over 100 battles; performed 11 Ashvamedhas
- Indrabhattaraka Varma defeated by the Maukhari Ishanavarma (Haraha inscription, UP)
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Chandragupta II took the empire to its zenith; Skandagupta = last great Gupta ruler
- Break-up marks the move from a single empire → regional kingdoms + feudal order
- Comparative note:
- "Vikramaditya" used by both Chandragupta II and Skandagupta → question usually wants Chandragupta II (APPSC-G2 2008)
- AP angle: Vishnukundins' clash with the Maukharis links Andhra to North Indian politics

### Causes of decline

- Later Gupta gold coins debased — less gold, more alloy; coins slowly replaced by payments in land
