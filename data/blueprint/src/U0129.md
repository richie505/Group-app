### Babur → origin, memoir, tactics, buildings

- After Panipat: Occupied Delhi and Agra (APPSC 2023)
| Term | Meaning |

- FIRST: First large-scale use of gunpowder artillery in Indian warfare
- Buildings:
- Kabuli Bagh Mosque (Panipat) to mark 1526
- Ram Bagh, Agra (first Mughal garden)
### Humayun → accession, battles, exile, return, death
## 1. Rajput resistance
- Rao Jaitsi: The ruler of Bikaner — defeated Kamran Mirza in the Bikaner conflict
## 2. Accession and problems
- Accession: 29 Dec 1530, three days after Babur's death
- Problems:
- Rival brothers Kamran, Askari, Hindal
- Afghans
- Bahadur Shah of Gujarat-Malwa
## 3. Battles in order
| Battle / event | Year | Outcome |
| Battle of Devara — also called Daurah (Dourah) | 1532 | Humayun defeated Afghan rebels — on the banks of the Gomati; drove Mahmud Lodi from Jaunpur; lifted siege of Kalinjar |
| Gujarat | 1535 | Pressed by Humayun, Bahadur Shah let the Portuguese fortify Diu |
| Sher Khan's rise | 1538 | Won at Surajgarh; took Gaur |
| Battle of Chausa | 1539 | Sher Shah Suri defeated Humayun — on 26 June 1539; Humayun and Sher Shah were the leaders |
| Battle of Kannauj / Kanauj — also called the Battle of Bilgram | 1540 | Sher Shah defeated Humayun — Humayun lost effective control over North India; decisive defeat → exile |
| Battle of Sirhind | 1555 | Humayun defeated Afghan forces — reoccupied Delhi |
- Chausa preceded Bilgram in the sequence of battles
- Kannauj and Bilgram = one battle (1540), a Sher Khan victory
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes list "Bilgram (1540)" as a Humayun victory and a "Battle of Samugarh (1555) Humayun vs Hemu" - both WRONG; Samugarh was 1658 (Aurangzeb vs Dara)
## 4. Exile
- Married Hamida Banu Begum; Akbar born at Amarkot (1542)
- Went to Persia; Safavid Shah Tahmasp of Persia helped him retake Kandahar and Kabul; alliance with the Safavids
## 5. Return and death
- Return: July 1555, after Sher Shah's death, exploiting quarrels among his successors
- Defeated Sikandar Sur at Machhiwara and Sirhind, then took Delhi and Agra
- Death: January 1556, fall from the stairs of the Sher Mandal library in the Purana Qila
- Humayun-nama: By his sister Gulbadan Begum
### Sher Shah and the Sur successors → rise, titles, conquests
## 1. Sher Shah
- Origin: Born Farid, of the Afghan Sur tribe; studied at Jaunpur; managed father's jagir from 1511
- Title "Sher Khan": Given by Bahar Khan of Bihar for slaying a tiger
- Early career: Served Babur → deputy regent (de facto ruler) of Bihar
| Step | Event |
| Surajgarh | Turning point; took Gaur (1538) |
| Chausa (1539) | Assumed title Sher Shah |
| Kannauj (1540) | Took the throne |
- Conquests:
- Raisen (Puran Mal)
- Marwar: Maldeo of Marwar resisted; hard-fought battle of Samel (Sammel/Giri-Sumel, 1544) → Maldeo's generals Jaita and Kumpa fought so hard that Sher Shah said he nearly lost Hindustan "for a handful of bajra"
- Death: 1545, gunpowder explosion during siege of Kalinjar
- ⚠ Titles: "Sher Khan" = from Bahar Khan (tiger); "Sher Shah" = assumed after Chausa (1539)
## 2. Sur successors and Hemu
- Islam Shah: Jalal Khan, 1545-53
- Later rivals: Muhammad Adil Shah (east) and Sikandar Sur, fighting each other
- Hemu:
- Adil Shah's Hindu minister-general
- Seized Agra and Delhi (1556); title "Vikramaditya"
- Lost Panipat II (5 Nov 1556): arrow struck his eye → captured; Afghan power ended
### Sur administration → officers and terms
| Level | Officers |
| Sarkar | Chief Shiqdar (shiqdar-i-shiqdaran: law and order) and Chief Munsif (munsif-i-munsifan: revenue/civil) |
| Pargana | Shiqdar, Amin (munsif), treasurer (fotedar), Hindi and Persian writers (karkuns) |
| dagh | Horse branding, against fraud |
| chehra/huliya | Descriptive roll of soldiers, against fraud |
- Transfers: Officers frequently transferred
- Police: Local headmen responsible for detecting crime; censors of public morals; efficient espionage system
- Justice: Uniform, harsh justice
### Sher Shah's land revenue → measurement, terms, legacy
## 1. Measurement and assessment
- Measurement: Land measured under a uniform system of survey
- Yard = gaz-i-Sikandari (sikandari gaz); rope/jarib
- Classification: Three categories (good, middling, bad) by produce
- State share: One-third of average produce; payment in cash or grain
- Some notes say "one-fourth to one-third"
- ⚠ Trap: APPSC-G1 2024 key → statements I (three categories) and II (uniform measurement) true; "one-fourth" false
## 2. Documents, officials and fees
| Term | Meaning |
| Patta | Title deed stating each peasant's revenue/dues; given to the cultivator |
| Qabuliyat | Peasant's deed of agreement/acceptance of the demand; signed by him |
| Amin | Assessment official (with shiqdar, qanungo, patwari) |
| Jaribana | Surveyor's fee |
| Muhasilana | Collector's fee |
- ⚠ Patta = given by the state; Qabuliyat = signed by the peasant
## 3. Nature and legacy
- Direct settlement: State-peasant settlement with cultivators; illegal demands removed; tenant rights recognised
- Some notes call Sher Shah the first to introduce a ryotwari-type system
- Legacy: Model for Todar Mal's Bandobast
### Sher Shah's currency, roads and sarais → coins, routes, dak-chowkis
## 1. Currency
| Coin | Metal | Note |
| Rupiya | Silver | About 178 grains[GK]; introduced 1540-45 (APPSC-GS 2019; PYQ); remained standard for centuries (APPSC-G2 2017) |
- Currency reform: Minted pure gold and silver coins; reform outlived the Sur dynasty
## 2. Roads
- Sadak-e-Azam / Grand Trunk Road: Restored/rebuilt by Sher Shah; Sonargaon (Bengal) to the Indus; still exists
- APPSC-G1 2022 answer: "Indus to Sonargaon"; APPSC-GS 2018: he restored it
## 3. Sarais
- Dak-chowkis: Sarais served as dak-chowkis (post stations; news and mail relays, with horses)
- Effect: Market towns grew around them

### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Sher Shah's short reign (1540-45) set the pattern for Mughal land revenue (via Todar Mal), coinage (the rupee) and roads
- Sher Shah's rupiya, roads and revenue system = base for Akbar's administration
- Comparative note:
- Sher Shah = 1/3 of average produce + three land classes
- Akbar-Todar Mal = kept 1/3 but used ten-year averages + four land classes (polaj, parauti, chachar, banjar)
- FIRST/LAST: Rupiya began with Sher Shah; Surs = last Afghan dynasty to rule Delhi
- Current relevance: PMF IAS practice item (Apr 2026) tested First Panipat: tulughma, Rumi method, gunpowder artillery, Kabuli Bagh Mosque
- AP angle: Sultan Quli Qutb Shah of Golconda (1518-43) contemporary with Babur, Humayun and Sher Shah; Mughals did not interfere in the Deccan then
