# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:20
### Milestones
- ★ Limits to Growth (1972) — propounded by the Club of Rome
- Club of Rome began at a meeting of 30 people from 10 countries at the Accademia dei Lincei, Rome (April 1968)
- Rio+20 Conference (2012), UN Conference on Sustainable Development — decided to develop the SDGs
- Agenda 2030 adopted in New York on 25 September 2015
### Brundtland & core idea
- Brundtland Commission = World Commission on Environment and Development (WCED), chaired by Gro Harlem Brundtland (PM of Norway)
- WCED — first formal definition of sustainable development, in Our Common Future
- Three pillars: economic growth, social inclusion and environmental protection (balance, not maximising one)
- Basic goal — meet present needs while keeping choices for future generations, within Earth's carrying capacity
- Carrying capacity — largest population an environment can support forever without degradation
- Earth Overshoot Day — date when humanity's yearly demand on nature exceeds what Earth can renew that year
### Three pillars: overlaps
- Three pillars: economic growth, social inclusion or equity, environmental protection
### Rio 1992 principles
- Endorsed at Rio 1992: precautionary principle, polluter pays principle, common but differentiated responsibilities
- Unrestricted resource exploitation — NOT endorsed; goes against sustainable development
### Agenda 2030
- Universal character — Agenda 2030 applies to all countries (developed, developing, least developed)
- Specific focus: ending all discrimination and violence against women and girls
- Two roots: Millennium Development Goals and the Rio+20 outcome
- 17 SDGs, 169 targets

### Sustainable development — milestones and FIRSTs
- ★ Prioritize individual wealth accumulation — NOT an objective of sustainable development
- ★ Concept popularised and formally defined in the 1987 Brundtland Report ('Our Common Future', by WCED)
- The Brundtland Report is NOT "The Limits to Growth"
- ★ Sustainable development — balancing the need to use resources and conserve them for the future
- ★ 1980 — term 'Sustainable Development' introduced; IUCN World Conservation Strategy
### Terms → meaning
- ★ Brundtland definition (meet present needs without harming future generations) is tied to carrying capacity
- Carrying capacity — maximum number, density or biomass of a population an area can support sustainably
- ★ Base of sustainable development — environmental approach
