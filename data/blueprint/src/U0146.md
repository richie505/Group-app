### Alvars and Nayanars → deity, number, saints, canon
## 1. Alvars vs Nayanars
|  | Alvars | Nayanars (Nayanmars) |
| Sect / deity | Vaishnava (Vishnu) | Shaiva (Shiva) (UPPSC 2014) |
| Traditional number | 12 | 63 (MPPSC 2022) |
| Women saints | Andal - only female Alvar (MPPSC 2025) | Karaikkal Ammaiyar (Tamil Shaiva; extreme asceticism) |
| Key saints | Nammalvar, Tirumangai Alvar, Andal, Periyalvar | Appar, Sundarar, Tirugnana Sambandar, Manikkavachakar |
| Canon | Nalayira Divya Prabandham (Sri Vaishnava scripture) | Tevaram - earliest, most sacred part of the Tirumurai |
- ⚠ Alvars = 12 = Vishnu; Nayanars = 63 = Shiva
## 2. Period, hymns and canon
- Period: c. 6th-9th centuries; hymns in Tamil
- Sacred geography: Sang Tamil hymns; identified sacred sites that grew into temple and pilgrimage centres; hymns became part of temple ritual
- Social base: Challenged caste and Brahmanical dominance; Nayanmars included Dalits, hunters and women
- Tirumurai: 12-volume Tamil Shaiva canon, compiled 7th-12th centuries; the "Fifth Veda" (JPSC 2021)
## 3. Chola ruler → patronage

### Annamacharya and the Tallapaka poets → life, works, titles — Annamacharya — life
## 1. Annamacharya — life
- Dates: 1408-1503; some notes give 1424-1508
- Contemporary ruler: Saluva Narasimha (APPSC-G2 2011)
- Wife of Annamayya: Timmakka - Subhadra Kalyanam [GK]
## 2. Sankirtanas
- Output: About 32,000 sankirtanas to Lord Venkateswara of Tirumala; only about 12,000 survive
- Preservation: On copper plates at Tirumala
- Veturi Prabhakara Sastry: Recovered/excavated + edited the copper-plate songs; said Annamacharya created the science of sankirtana
- Titles: Padakavita Pitamaha, Sankirtanacharya, Harikirtanacharya
- Father of the Telugu keertana; pioneer of padakavita / the sankirtana / pada form
## 3. Annamacharya — other works
- Telugu: Sringara Manjari (258 dvipadas); Sri Venkateswara Satakam; Dvipada Ramayanam; Alivelumangamma Satakam
- Sanskrit: Sankirtana Lakshanam, Venkatachala Mahatyam

### Vemana and Potuluri Veerabrahmam → anti-caste voices
## 1. Vemana
- Identity: Yogi-philosopher poet; satakam-style verses in colloquial Telugu for common people (Vemana padyalu) [GK]
- Themes: Satire on empty ritual, caste discrimination by birth, idol worship, superstition and hypocrisy; strongly condemned caste
- Verse form: Mostly the Ataveladi metre; short verses with refrain "Vishwadabhirama Vinura Vema" ("Viswadabhirama Vinura Vema")
- C.P. Brown: Collected verses from manuscripts, edited + translated into English → Verses of Vemana (1829)
- Dating disputed: 17th c. vs 14th-15th c. - learn both
## 2. Veerabrahmendra Swamy
- Potuluri Veerabrahmam: Prophecies in Kalagnanam
- Social message: Strongly condemned caste differences
- Disciples: Across castes and faiths, e.g. Siddaiah, a Muslim
- ⚠ C.P. Brown: Verses of Vemana 1829; Andhra Maha Bhagavatamu 1840
- Exam angle: Vemana is paired with Potuluri Veerabrahmam (Kaalagnanam) as Andhra's anti-caste voices.

### Vemana & Veerabrahmam
## Vemana
- Vemana — criticised caste, superstition, empty ritual, idol worship and hypocrisy; supported humanism and equality
- Metre: Ataveladi; refrain: "Vishwadabhirama Vinura Vema"
- C.P. Brown — collected, edited and translated 'Verses of Vemana' (1829)
## Veerabrahmendra Swamy
- Opposed caste differences; disciples across castes and faiths, incl. Siddaiah, a Muslim
- Alvars, Nayanars, Basava, Vemana and Potuluri Veerabrahmam criticised caste; some promoted common dining — Bhakti did not uniformly uphold Brahmanical dominance
## Related traditions
- ★ Gangadevi (author of Madhura Vijayam) — disciple of Agastya
- Ramanuja — Vishishtadvaita; worship of Narayana and Lakshmi
- Ishtalinga — worn on the body; personal worship of Shiva outside temples
- Kayaka — honest work; Dasoha — sharing surplus; Shunya Sampadane — later collection of discourses
- Virashaivas — rejected caste and untouchability, condemned child marriage, promoted widow remarriage, questioned rebirth
### Veerashaivism in Andhra
## Basavanna and the Virashaiva movement
- Basavanna (Basaveshwara) — founded the Virashaiva / Lingayat movement (12th c.)
- Minister of Bijjala II of the Kalachuri dynasty
- Theology: Shakti Vishishtadvaita — soul and Shiva basically identical
- Anubhava Mantapa ("religious parliament") — at Kalyana; talks collected in the Shunya Sampadane
## Virashaivism in Andhra
- Brought into Andhra by Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya; Pandita reformers spread it
- Aradhya Saivism — mild Andhra Shaiva sect (Panditaradhya); kept Vedic learning and varna while preaching Shiva bhakti
- Sivatattvasaram — by Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya (not Palkuriki Somanatha)
- Palkuriki Somanatha — Basava Puranam (Basava's teachings in simple Telugu), Panditaradhya Charitra, Basavana Ragada
- Vrushadhipa Satakam (Somanatha) — first Telugu satakam with all satakam features
