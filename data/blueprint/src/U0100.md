### Fall of Warangal (1323) and the liberation movement — FIRSTs, leader, chiefs
## 1. Background - fall of Warangal
- Background: Warangal fell in 1323; Muhammad bin Tughlaq renamed it Sultanpur (some notes: 'Sultanpurti')
- Administration: Andhra under Malik Burhanuddin, with Malik Maqbool / Malik Maqbul as deputy
- Survivors: Of Prataparudra's 75 generals, only three survived: Prolaya Nayaka, Recherla Singama and Prolaya Vema Reddi (also spelt Racherla)
- Kaluvacheru grant (Anitalli, 1423) names 75 Nayakas who backed Prolaya
- Opening: Tughluq's misrule (from 1326) + revolts across the empire → Andhra freed within about 2 years
## 2. Liberation movement
- Name: Vimochanodyamam - first organised resistance to Muslim rule in Andhra
- Leader of the liberation confederacy: Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka (APPSC GS 2018; G2 2019)
- Chiefs involved: Prolaya Vema Reddi, Recherla Singama, Koppula Prolaya Nayaka, Kolani Rudra, Vengi Bhupala, Manchikonda Ganapati Nayaka, Kapaya Nayaka
- Kingdom: 1325-1368
### Prolaya Nayaka - capital Rekapalli; Kapaya Nayaka - recovery of Warangal
## 1. Prolaya Nayaka - capital Rekapalli
- Origin: Son of Potinayaka; Kamma clan (Chaturthanvayas / Chaturthanvaya lineage)
- Rule: Freed the land between the Krishna and Godavari; ruled from Rekapalli (on the Godavari, forest near Bhadrachalam) from 1325 (capital: APPSC G2 2017)
- Vilasa copper-plate grant (1330) (Vilasa Tamra Sasanam, 1330 CE):
- Sufferings under Tughluq rule + Prolaya's leadership
- Agrarian reforms/relief + restoration of agraharas to Brahmins
- Succession: Childless → succeeded by his cousin/nephew Kapaya Nayaka
## 2. Kapaya Nayaka (1336-1368) - recovery of Warangal
- 1336 recovery: With Hoysala Veera Ballala III (+ Prolaya Vema Reddi, Singama) took Orugallu from Malik Maqbool in 1336 (Warangal from Malik Maqbul)
- Ruled the old Kakatiya realm from Orugallu
- Simultaneous campaigns:
- Singama cleared Nalgonda-Mahabubnagar
- Prolaya Vema Reddi cleared Kammanadu-Pakanadu south of the Krishna
### Kapaya Nayaka - administration and titles
## 1. Kapaya Nayaka - administration
- Vengi: Appointed nephew Anapota Nayaka (Anapotha Nayaka) as governor at Vengi
- Polavaram inscription: Issued by Kapaya; names 14 members of the lineage, only 3 ruled

### Kapaya and the Bahmanis → losses, truce, Battle of Bhimavaram
## 1. Founding help and losses
- Founding help: Kapaya helped Hasan Gangu (Zafar Khan) found the Bahmani kingdom (1347, Gulbarga), then lost to it
| Year | Loss |
| 1350 | Kaulas Durgam |
| 1356 | Bhuvanagiri Durgam |
| 1361/1362 | son Vinayaka Deva killed (APP: burned alive by Muhammad I at 'Velama town', 1361; CDX: 1362) |
| 1364-65 | Golconda Durgam, 400 elephants, 250 horses and 33 lakhs in treasure |
- Vinayaka Deva: Kapaya's son, captured and burnt alive by Muhammad Shah I (1361-62) → broke Kapaya's morale
- Truce (1364-65):
- Golconda fort ceded with huge wealth; Golconda became the boundary
- Allies sought: Allied with/sought help from Bukka I of Vijayanagara against the Bahmanis; sought help from Firoz Shah Tughlaq
## 2. Battle of Bhimavaram (1367-68)
- Battle of Bhimavaram (near Orugallu, 1367-68): Kapaya defeated and killed by the Recherla Velamas under Anapota Nayaka → ended the Musunuri line
- Telangana passed to the Velamas of Rachakonda
### Recherla Velama rulers → origin, founder, contribution
## 1. Origin
- Recherla gotra: Rachakonda and Devarakonda kings; began as loyal feudatories of the early Kakatiyas
- Family history: Velugoti Varivamsavali
- Founder of lineage: Betala Nayudu
- Duration: Ruled Telangana from Rachakonda and Devarakonda for about 150 years (1325-1475)
## 2. Rulers → contribution
| Ruler | Contribution |
| Singama Nayaka I (Singama Nayudu I) | Founder of independent kingdom; 1336 liberation: expelled the Muslims from Nalgonda and Mahabubnagar; Kapaya Nayaka failed to subdue him; expanded up to the Krishna and into the Krishna-Tungabhadra doab; Killed fighting the kings of Jallipalli (chiefs of Jallipalli) |
| Anapota Nayaka | Singama's son; moved the capital from Amanagallu to Rachakonda; Defeated and killed Kapaya Nayaka at the Battle of Bhimavaram (1367-68), near Orugallu → ended the Musunuri line |
- End of the kingdom: Sultan Quli Qutb Shah (Golconda, 1518-43) defeated the Velama chiefs; annexed Rachakonda and Devarakonda
### Velama-Reddi conflicts
## 1. Velama-Reddi conflicts
- Division: Reddis held coastal Andhra, "contested by the Velama chiefs to the north"
- After Kapaya: His decline let the Reddis take coastal Andhra; Velamas absorbed Musunuri Telangana
- Revenge for Singama: Madanayudu and nephew Naganayudu invaded the Reddi kingdom; defeated Anavota Reddi at Dharanikota
| Battle | Opponents | Outcome |
| Jallipalli | Singama Nayudu vs Jallipalli Kshatriyas (aided by the Reddis) | Singama killed |
| Dharanikota (Dannala Kota) | Madanayudu and Naganayudu vs Anavota Reddi | Velamas won; titles Jagadabbaganda, Rayavesa Bhujanga |
| Bhimavaram (1367-68) | Anapota Nayaka vs Musunuri Kapaya | Kapaya killed; Musunuri rule ended |
- Bhimavaram conflict: PYQ aph2019_11 explanation says the Reddis beat the Velamas at Bhimavaram and drove them out of Palnadu
- ⚠ Trap: Mark "Bhimavaram" when asked, but learn it as a Velama-Musunuri battle

### Universal angles
## 1. Significance
- Significance: First organised/successful Telugu resistance to the Delhi Sultanate
- But Kakatiya unity was lost → three successor states: Musunuri (1325-1368), Recherla Velama (1325-1475), Reddi (1325-1448)
- Vijayanagara and the Bahmanis emerged alongside
- Comparative note: Same wave - Harihara-Bukka founded Vijayanagara (1336); Hasan Gangu the Bahmani kingdom (1347)
## 2. FIRST/LAST
- FIRST/LAST:
- Kapaya was the first to bear the title Andhra Suratrana
- Bhimavaram (1367-68): last battle of the Musunuri kingdom; Anapota's victory ended the Musunuris
## 3. AP angle
- AP angle: Damerla (Kalahasti), Velugoti (Venkatagiri), Bobbili and Pithapuram carried the Velama line into AP's zamindari era
