### Red and black soils — parent rock, formation and properties
## 1. Black soil
- Mainly across the Deccan Plateau — Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu
- Developed largely from basaltic or lava rocks
- Very high water retention; clayey; develops cracks on drying
- Black because of its mineral composition — particularly iron- and titanium-related constituents
## 2. Black soils in AP
- Parent rock: Deccan Trap basalt in Prakasam, Palnadu, Guntur, East Godavari + crystalline schists and basic gneiss
- Texture: Fine, clayey; neutral to strongly alkaline
- Nutrients: Rich in calcium, potash, magnesium (Ca, K, Mg); poor in nitrogen, phosphorus, humus (N, P)
- Water: High water retention; deep cracks when dry → "self-ploughing"
## 3. Red soils in AP
- Parent rock: Ancient weathered granite, gneiss, Dharwar schists, greenstones
- Colour: Ferric oxides
- Properties: Well drained; poor in N, P, organic matter (nitrogen, phosphorus); low moisture retention
### Alluvial and laterite soils — formation and properties
## 1. Alluvial soil — nature
- Deposited by rivers — highly fertile; the largest and most widespread soil group
- Generally rich in potash, phosphoric acid and lime; generally poor in nitrogen and humus
- Deltaic alluvium (AP): Laid down as river-borne silt by the Godavari and Krishna; renewed with silt each flood
- Texture: Porous + loamy; high moisture retention
- Nutrients: Rich in potash and lime
## 2. Laterite soil
- Develops in the Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, South Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, West Bengal, Kerala, Jharkhand, Assam, Meghalaya
- Forms under high temperature and heavy rainfall, where wet and dry seasons alternate (High rainfall and high temperature)
- ⚠ Trap: "Formed in semi-arid low-rainfall regions" is the incorrect statement - not in semi-arid areas (APPSC 2023/2024 key; APPSC-G2 2023 and 2024 key)
- Formation: Intense leaching — removal of silica and lime; accumulation of iron and aluminium oxides (rich in hydrated iron and aluminium oxides)
- Generally of low fertility
- Nature: Acidic (high acidity), poor fertility, red to yellowish-red, well drained
- Nitrogen: Some notes say plentiful; standard fact = poor in nitrogen
- ⚠ Rich in Ca, K, Mg = black; rich in potash and lime = alluvium; rich in iron and aluminium oxides = laterite

### Desert, saline and problem soils — nature
## 1. Desert or arid soil
- Western Rajasthan; the Thar Desert; parts of Gujarat
- Sandy; low organic matter; low moisture retention; often affected by salinity or alkalinity
## 2. Saline or alkaline soil
- Saline soil: Contains excess soluble salts
- Occurs in Gujarat, Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and other irrigated or arid regions
- Largest area of saline soil: Gujarat; maximum alkali area: Uttar Pradesh
## 3. Nature of the problem in AP
- Salinity: Lowers yields + breaks down soil structure
- Alkalinity: High pH from limestone/basalt parent rock or alkaline irrigation water → cuts nutrient availability
- Acidic soils: Laterite soils
- Alkaline tendency: Black soils (neutral to strongly alkaline)
- Rayalaseema red soils: Shallow; low moisture retention + low organic matter
- Soil organic carbon: Sandy soils + steep slopes lose SOC (leaching, erosion); clay soils hold more

### Formation & properties
## Red soil
- ★ Red colour — iron oxides, mainly ferric oxide (oxidised in well-drained soil)
- ★ Made by weathering of old crystalline igneous and metamorphic rocks
- ★ Low in lime, phosphates, nitrogen and humus; fairly rich in potash; mainly siliceous
- AP red soils — low moisture-retention power
- Rayalaseema red soils — shallow, low moisture retention, low organic matter
## Black soil
- AP black soils — from Deccan Trap basalt, crystalline schists and basic gneiss
- Rich in calcium, potash, magnesium; poor in nitrogen, phosphorus and humus
- Colour from iron and titanium compounds; clayey
- Neutral to strongly alkaline
## Alluvial and laterite
- AP deltaic alluvium — laid by Godavari and Krishna, renewed with silt each flood; rich in potash and lime
- Alluvium — rich in potash, phosphoric acid, lime; poor in nitrogen and humus; ideal for rice and wheat
- Laterite — rich in hydrated iron and aluminium oxides; poor in organic matter, low fertility
- Laterite — acidic conditions
- Laterite less fertile; poor in nitrogen (heavy rain washes out nutrients)

### Desert, saline and problem soils — nature
- ★ Laterite — not moisture-retentive; from weathering of iron- and aluminium-rich rocks; red to reddish yellow
- ★ Salinization — makes some soils impermeable; common in arid irrigated regions
- ★ Black soil — holds water, needs little irrigation
- ★ Clay soil — maximum soil water available to plants
### Problem soils in AP → areas and causes
- ★ Saline soils — not suitable for cultivation; high salt content
- ★ Indian saline soils — extra sodium and potassium, high magnesium ratio, lime deficiency
