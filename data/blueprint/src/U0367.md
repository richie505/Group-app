# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:93
### LPG terms
- Privatisation — government leaving ownership/management, incl. outright sale of PSUs
- Globalisation — joining the world economy; the outcome (not precondition) of liberalisation and privatisation
- Disinvestment — sale of part of PSU equity to the public
### Industrial policies
- 1956 Resolution — Schedule B: 12 industries, mixed (public and private); Schedule C left to private sector
- 1977 Statement (Janata) — small/cottage industries; District Industries Centres
- 1980 Statement — technology upgrading, export orientation, backward-area development; MRTP firms 25% expansion
- 1991 Statement — de-licensing, MRTP restructuring, FDI, PSU autonomy via MoUs; did not expand licensing
- MRTP Act — 1969; relaxed 1980; replaced by Competition Act, 2002 (CCI)
- Licensing kept after 1991 — alcohol, cigarettes, hazardous chemicals, industrial explosives, electronic aerospace and defence, drugs
### 1991 crisis
- 67 tonnes of gold pledged with Bank of England and UBS
- IMF asked for about $7 billion, with structural-adjustment conditions
### Stabilisation vs structural adjustment
- Stabilisation — short-term; BoP, inflation, fiscal deficit
- Structural adjustment — medium/long-term; efficiency and competitiveness
### Part of 1991 LPG?
- Navratna — nine PSUs, greater autonomy in 1997 (NCERT-based sources: 1996)
- FDI — automatic approval up to 51% in 34 priority industries (1991)
- FERA → FEMA — 1999
### Reform generations
- Factor markets (labour, land, power) — second-generation reforms (2000s), not first
- Washington Consensus — trade and FDI liberalisation, privatisation, deregulation, fiscal discipline, tax reform
### PSUs & global context
- New PSE Policy for Atmanirbhar Bharat (2021) — strategic disinvestment
- Washington Consensus — John Williamson, 1989
### Exam corner
- 1991 NEP — PM P.V. Narasimha Rao, FM Dr. Manmohan Singh
- Capital account convertibility — not fully adopted; studied by Tarapore Committees (1997, 2006)
### LPG — terms → meaning; PSU categories
- ★ LPG announced as New Economic Policy by PM P.V. Narasimha Rao, 24 July 1991
