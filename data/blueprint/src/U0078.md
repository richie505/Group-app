### Phases of Pallava architecture → ruler → example
| Phase | Ruler | Type | Examples |
| Mahendra | Mahendravarman I (600-630) | rock-cut pillared mandapas | Mandagapattu, Mahendravadi, Dalavanur, Mamandur |
| Mamalla | Narasimhavarman I (630-668) | monolithic rathas and mandapas | Pancha Rathas, Mahabalipuram |
| Rajasimha | Narasimhavarman II | first structural temples | Shore Temple; Kailasanatha, Kanchi |
| Nandivarman / Aparajita | Nandivarman II and later | smaller structural temples [GK] | Vaikuntha Perumal, Kanchi [GK] |
- Evolution: Rock-cut → monolithic rathas → structural temples
### FIRSTs, largest, smallest, oldest
- Mandagapattu inscription: Mahendravarman I
- Claim: Temple for Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva "without brick, timber, metal or mortar" = beginning of rock-cut cave temples in South India
- Dvarapalakas: At the entrance - later common in South Indian temples
- Arjuna's Penance / Descent of the Ganga: largest open-air rock relief in India
- Ratha sizes:
- Dharmaraja ratha is the largest - precursor of the Dravida vimana
- Draupadi ratha the smallest (hut-shaped single cell)
- Kailasanatha, Kanchi: oldest surviving Pallava structural temple

### UNESCO sites → year: Mahabalipuram vs Ajanta
- UNESCO: Group of Monuments at Mahabalipuram (Shore Temple, Pancha Rathas, etc.) - 1984
- Setting: 7th-century Pallava port city
| Site | Faith / patron | UNESCO |
| Mahabalipuram | Hindu; Pallavas | 1984 |
| Ajanta | Buddhist; Satavahana and Vakataka phases; 25 viharas + 4 chaityas, Jataka paintings | 1983[GK]; called India's first UNESCO site; found 1819 by John Smith |
- Contrast:
- Ajanta = cave-painting + rock-cut monasteries in a cliff
- Mahabalipuram = open-air reliefs + monolithic rathas + structural temple
### Sculpture and painting → site
- Sittannavasal (Pudukkottai, TN): Jain cave with murals (lotus pond)
- Once credited to Mahendravarman I; inscription credits a 9th-century Pandya renovation
- Panamalai (Talagirishvara temple): Rajasimha-period painting of Parvati
### Pallava-style rock-cut caves in Andhra → location, attribution
| Cave | Location | Attribution | Key fact |
| Undavalli | Guntur dist., Krishna banks | Vishnukundins (APPSC G2 2025) | multi-storeyed; monolithic reclining Anantasayana Vishnu (Anantapadmanabha) |
| Mogalrajapuram | Vijayawada | Vishnukundins (CDI) | Shiva and Vishnu shrines; early ardhamandapa + garbhagriha |
| Bhairavakona | Nellore district (APPSC keys; CDI says Prakasam) | Vishnukundins (CDI); look like early Pallava caves | 8 caves; centre of Shaivism |
- Vishnukundins: Started Hindu cave temples in Telugu lands, imitating Pallava style
- Undavalli history: Originally a Jain abode → Buddhist → Hindu (some notes say Buddhist/Jain first)
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: A test (Aug 2026) framed Undavalli as a 4th-5th c. monolithic complex on the Krishna
- Significance: Pallavas = pioneers of Dravida temple architecture; rathas gave the vimana + ardhamandapa to early Andhra temples
- Comparative note: Rajasimha's structural temples → Chola Brihadeshwara; Kailasanatha → Virupaksha (Pattadakal)

### Mahabalipuram (UNESCO 1984)
## Narasimhavarman I (Mamalla)
- Pancha Rathas, Varaha Mandapa, Mahishasuramardini Cave
- Rathas — monolithic, each from one granite rock, copying wooden forms
- Dharmaraja ratha — largest; forerunner of the Dravida vimana
- Draupadi ratha — smallest, hut-shaped single cell
## Reliefs
| Monument | Theme |
| Varaha Mandapa | Varaha rescuing Bhudevi |
| Krishna Mandapa | Krishna lifting Govardhana |
| Mahishasuramardini Cave | Durga slaying Mahishasura |
- Arjuna's Penance / Descent of the Ganga — largest open-air rock relief in India; Bhagiratha's penance
## Shore Temple
- Built under Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha); a structural temple
- Two Shiva shrines + one Vishnu shrine; survivor of the Seven Pagodas
## Status
- UNESCO World Heritage: 1984
- Ajanta — cliff complex of cave paintings and rock-cut monasteries; not Pallava (only Mahabalipuram is)
### Kanchi temples
- Mandagapattu inscription — Mahendravarman I (not Narasimhavarman I); temple for Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva without brick, timber, metal or mortar
- Kailasanatha (Rajasimheshvara) — oldest surviving Pallava structural temple; sandstone, 8th c.; built by Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha)
- finished by Mahendravarman III; rich in Shiva sculptures; model for later Chola temples
- model for the Virupaksha temple, Pattadakal
- Vaikuntha Perumal — Vishnu temple; storeyed sanctum; panels on Pallava history; built by Nandivarman II
- Mahendravarman I — rock-cut mandapas; Narasimhavarman I — monolithic rathas
### Andhra rock-cut caves
- Undavalli, Mogalrajapuram, Bhairavakonda — Vishnukundins, copying Pallava cave style
- Undavalli — multi-storeyed; reclining Anantasayana Vishnu; not a Chola work
- Bhairavakonda — eight caves like early Pallava caves; a Shaiva centre (not Jain)
### Painting & sculpture
- Sittannavasal — Jain cave, Pudukkottai district; murals of a lotus pond
- Once linked to Mahendravarman I; inscription records a 9th-c. Pandya repair
### Exam corner
- ★ Seven Ratha temple, Mahabalipuram — Narasimhavarman I (630–668); Mamalla style; title Mahamalla
### FIRSTs, largest, smallest, oldest
- ★ Draupadi Rath — the smallest ratha (Mamalla style); no decoration
