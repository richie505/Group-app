### Sounding rockets — TERLS Thumba and Rohini series (FIRSTs)
## 1. TERLS Thumba
- Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station (TERLS): Near Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala
- Work began 1962 (per 2026 notes); set up 1963
- First launch 21 Nov 1963 (Nike-Apache) - start of the Indian space programme
- Site reason: Geomagnetic equator (magnetic equator) passes through it
## 2. Rohini sounding rockets
- Early rockets: Imported - M-100 (Russia), Centaure (France); indigenous rockets from 1965
- Rohini Sounding Rocket (RSR) programme: Consolidated in 1975
- Rohini (RH) rockets: Carry payloads for atmospheric research + qualify/test launch-vehicle subsystems for bigger rockets
- Number = diameter in mm (RH-200, RH-300, RH-560)
- Three operational versions: payload 8-100 kg, apogee 80-475 km
- RH-200: 200th consecutive successful launch from Thumba in Nov 2022 (APPSC 2021 PYQ)
- ⚠ Thumba = magnetic equator (sounding rockets); Sriharikota = east coast (orbital launches)

### Orbits and launch physics — terms → meaning
## 1. Orbit terms
| Term | Meaning |
| LEO | Low Earth Orbit (up to ~2,000 km) [GK] |
| SSO | Sun-Synchronous (polar) Orbit - PSLV's speciality |
| GTO | Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit - GSLV, LVM3 |
- GTO: Elliptical transfer orbit, apogee near 36,000 km
- GEO: = GSO over the equator → fixed in the sky
- Sun-synchronous orbit: Near-polar; passes each place at the same local solar time (imaging)
- Apogee vs perigee: apogee = farthest point; perigee = nearest
## 2. Launch direction
- Eastward launch: Rockets launched eastward gain Earth's rotational speed → saves fuel
- Sites nearer the equator gain the most
- Sriharikota location: East coast → rockets fly over the sea, away from populated land
- Polar/SSO launches: Go southward
- From Sriharikota → need a dog-leg around Sri Lanka
- Hence the southern SSLV site at Kulasekarapattinam
### SSLV and cryogenic engines → features
## 1. SSLV
- Capacity: 500 kg to 500 km LEO (APPSC G1 2022 statement I - correct)
- ⚠ Trap: "Two solid stages" is WRONG - it has three
- Flights:
- First flight SSLV-D1 (Aug 2022) failed to reach intended orbit
- D2 (2023) and D3 (2024) succeeded
- Transfer of Technology: HAL won a ₹511 crore deal to build and operate SSLV independently - first full ToT of an ISRO launch vehicle to industry
- Dedicated spaceport: Kulasekarapattinam (Thoothukudi, TN)
