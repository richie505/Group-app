### Weber's terms
- Locational triangle: 2 raw-material sources + 1 market
- MI = weight of localised raw materials / weight of product
- MI > 1 → at raw material; MI < 1 → at market
- Isodapanes: lines of equal extra transport cost
- Ubiquities (water, air); pure (cotton); gross / weight-losing (iron ore, coal, sugarcane)
- Steel: ~4 tonnes raw material → 1 tonne steel
### Location concepts

- Umland: area around a town economically linked to it
- Agglomeration: cost savings from clustering

### Location concepts — footloose, sunrise, umland, agglomeration
## 1. Concepts
- Footloose industry: Location not dependent on raw materials — e.g. electronics, software, watch-making
- Not tied to a raw material or a fixed location; light, high-value, low transport cost, low pollution
- Also diamond cutting; PYQ (APPSC-GS 2019 key): Diamond
- Sunrise industry: Information technology, electronics
- Umland: The area surrounding a town or city that is economically linked to it
## 2. Agglomeration economies
- Agglomeration economies: Cost savings when firms cluster
- Shared infrastructure, specialised suppliers, skilled labour pool
### Weber's location terms → meaning
## 1. Weber's terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Locational triangle | Two raw-material sources + one market; least-transport-cost point lies inside it |
| Material Index (MI) | MI = weight of localised raw materials / weight of finished product |
| Isodapanes | Lines of equal extra transport cost; labour or agglomeration savings pull a firm off the least-transport point |
- MI > 1 → locate at raw material
- MI < 1 → at market
## 2. Material types
| Material type | Meaning | Example |
| Ubiquities | Available everywhere | Water, air [GK] |
| Pure localised | Lose no weight | Cotton [GK] |
| Gross (weight-losing) | Lose weight in processing | Iron ore, coal, sugarcane [GK] |
- Cotton textiles: Non-weight-losing → not bound to raw-material sites
