# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:38 - India - climate: mechanism of the monsoon, theories, seasons and their characteristics
### Theories of the monsoon
- Monsoon: seasonal winds that reverse direction with the season - sea to land in summer, land to sea in winter
| Theory | Core idea |
| Thermal (Halley, 1686) | Land heats faster → summer low over land draws moist sea winds |
| ITCZ shift | ITCZ over Ganga plain = monsoon trough; SE trades cross equator as SW winds |
| Jet streams | STJ north of Himalayas → onset; back south → withdrawal; TEJ with SW monsoon |
| Tibetan plateau | Heat island → low pressure, stronger TEJ |
| ENSO / IOD | El Nino weakens; La Nina aids; positive IOD offsets El Nino |
### Key terms
- "Monsoon" from Arabic Mausim (season)
- Seasonal (secondary) winds, not primary; within 20°N-20°S
- Mascarene High (near Madagascar) → cross-equatorial flow
- Breaks: trough shifts to foothills → plains dry
- LPA: 868.6 mm (1971-2020)
- OMT predicts rain better than SST

### Monsoon — meaning, wind type and features
- Etymology: Arabic "Mausim" (season); some notes say Urdu 'mausin' or Malay 'monsin'
- Wind type: Seasonal/periodic (secondary) winds that reverse direction with the season - not primary (planetary) winds
- Sea → land in summer; land → sea in winter
- ⚠ Trap: "primary winds" statement I is false (PYQ G2 2023)
- Latitudinal limit: Tropics between 20°N and 20°S (PYQ G2 2023/2024: II and III correct)
- Distribution control: Mainly relief
- Nature:
- Not steady; unequally distributed
- ⚠ Trap: "equally distributed" is NOT a feature (PYQ 2022)
- Sudden onset = "burst" (first week of June)
### Theories and persons → core idea
| Theory / driver | Core idea |
| Thermal / differential heating (classical; Sir Edmund Halley, 1686) | Land heats faster than sea, so summer low over land (drawing moist sea winds) and high over sea; reversed in winter [GK] |
| Dynamic / ITCZ shift | Northward shift of the ITCZ in summer: it moves over the Ganga plain as the monsoon trough, pulling SE trades across the equator as SW winds [GK]; its N-S swings set rainfall (UPSC CSAT) |
| Jet streams | The subtropical westerly jet (STJ) shifts north of the Himalayas in summer → onset; it returns south → withdrawal (APP). The TEJ (South China Sea to East Africa) coincides with the SW monsoon |
| Tibetan plateau heating | Plateau (~5,000 m) acts as a heat island: intense summer heating → low pressure, an upper-air anticyclone and a stronger Tropical Easterly Jet; permanent high in the S Indian Ocean (UPSC p.39) |
| ENSO / IOD / MJO | El Nino weakens, La Nina strengthens/aids. A positive IOD can offset El Nino. An unfavourable MJO phase stalls the monsoon (LENS Jul 2026) |
| Al Masudi (10th c.) | Noted the seasonal winds |
| Sir Edmund Halley | Thermal theory (1686) |
- ⚠ STJ north of Himalayas → onset; STJ back south → withdrawal
### Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Mascarene High | Permanent high-pressure cell in the south Indian Ocean (near Madagascar) → drives cross-equatorial flow; listed among the SW monsoon's formation factors |
| Somali jet | Low-level jet along the East African coast; with the Somalia current it intensifies monsoon winds |
| Nautapa | Traditional nine-day peak of summer heat in north India, just before the monsoon arrives |
| Dust aerosols | Heat the mid-troposphere over the Indo-Gangetic plain → help pull the monsoon inland |
| Breaks | Trough shifts to the Himalayan foothills, so the plains go dry while the hills get heavy rain [GK] |
| LPA | 868.6 mm (1971-2020) |
| October heat | Clear skies + hot, humid weather of the retreating monsoon |
| Ocean Mean Temperature (OMT) | Average temperature of upper Indian Ocean down to the 26 deg C isotherm; measured Jan-Mar; predicts monsoon rain better than SST |

### Tornadoes and hailstorms in India
## 1. Tornadoes in India
- Most prone: Eastern and North-Eastern India — West Bengal, Odisha, Jharkhand, Bihar, Assam
- Season: Pre-monsoon — March to May — especially with Nor'westers (Kal Baisakhi)
- Deadliest: 24 March 1998 at Belda (West Bengal–Odisha border) — about 250 deaths
- Other events: New Delhi (1978); Berhampore (1993); Kendrapara, Odisha (2009); recent cases in Odisha, Punjab, Haryana, Jammu and Kashmir
## 2. Hailstorms — season and regions
- Season: Mainly the pre-monsoon (March to May); also winter; rare during the monsoon
- India has a high frequency — among countries with frequent moderate to severe hail events
- Highest probability: Maharashtra — especially Vidarbha and Marathwada
- Central India: Madhya Pradesh; parts of Chhattisgarh
- North and Northwest India: Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh
- Others: Bihar, parts of Karnataka, Uttarakhand

### Theories of the monsoon
## Theories
- Thermal theory — Sir Edmund Halley, 1686; land heats faster than sea → summer low over land pulls moist sea winds
- Dynamic / ITCZ theory — ITCZ shifts north over the Ganga plain (monsoon trough); south-east trades cross the equator and turn south-westerly
## Upper air and heating
- Tropical easterly jet — from the South China Sea to East Africa
- Tibetan Plateau (~5,000 m) — summer heat island; creates an upper-air anticyclone, strengthens the tropical easterly jet
- Dust aerosols over the Indo-Gangetic Plain — heat the mid-troposphere, help pull the monsoon inland
## Before and after the monsoon
- Heat waves in NW and central India — peak in May–June (hottest April–June)
- Nautapa — traditional nine-day peak of summer heat in north India, just before the monsoon
- Withdrawal — begins in the north-west in October, complete by end of November
- Retreating monsoon: the westerly jet returns south of the Himalayas (not north)
- 2026: Indian Ocean Dipole watched as it may offset El Nino
### Key terms
## Meaning
- Monsoons — seasonal secondary winds that reverse direction with the season; not primary planetary winds
- 'Monsoon' — from Arabic 'Mausim' = season
## Drivers
- Mascarene High — drives the cross-equatorial flow towards the subcontinent
- Somali jet + Somalia Current — strengthen the monsoon winds
- Ocean Mean Temperature (OMT) — upper Indian Ocean averaged down to the 26 °C isotherm; predicts monsoon rain better than SST
## Rainfall and forecasting
- Break in the monsoon — trough shifts to the Himalayan foothills; plains dry, Himalayan region heavy rain
- LPA of SW monsoon rainfall (1971–2020) — 868.6 mm (about 87 cm)
- Tamil Nadu coast dry in SW monsoon — leeward / rain-shadow side; winds blow parallel to the coast
- Bharat Forecast System (Mission Mausam) — 6 km resolution, 10-day rainfall forecasts
