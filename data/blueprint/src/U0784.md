### Heat waves, wet-bulb temperature and health → heat data and hotspots
## 1. Heat data and hotspots
- Heatwave days: Indians faced 19.8 heatwave days in 2024; 6.6 due to climate change (Lancet Countdown 2025, May 2026)
- AP: More frequent + intense in Palnadu, Kurnool and Anantapur
- Hottest place: Rentachintala (Palnadu)
- Cities: Models may underestimate urban warming by 0.5-2 C
- Patiala could warm at double the modelled rate (UEA study, 5 Feb 2026)
- Survivability limit: Wet-bulb temperature of about 35°C - above it sweat cannot cool the body
- World Bank: India could be among the first to cross it routinely (UPSC 2025)
- CMIP6 study: Around 40 coastal districts could warm by more than 1°C by 2040
- Kerala and Tamil Nadu: wet-bulb about 31°C (7 Jun 2026)
### Heat waves, wet-bulb temperature and health → health impacts
## 1. Health impacts
- Heat Action Plans: Ahmedabad drew up the first in South Asia, 2013
- Dasra report "Under the Weather": Nearly 40% of districts at high risk from extreme weather
- Warmer, wetter → expanding dengue and malaria
- Floods → cholera, hepatitis; heat → heatstroke, cardiovascular strain
- Urban waterlogging (e.g. Mumbai): Spreads leptospirosis, typhoid and hepatitis A
- ⚠ Heat wave = ≥45°C; survivability = wet-bulb ~35°C; first Heat Action Plan = Ahmedabad (2013)
### Monsoon, water and drought → causes and changes
## 1. Drought — causes in India
- Mainly the inadequacy and uneven distribution of rainfall
- More than 80% of India's rainfall is received in less than 100 days during the southwest monsoon
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the SW monsoon = about 75% of annual rain (some notes give 70-90%)
- Large parts of India depend on rain-fed agriculture — vulnerable to drought
- Peninsular India: Surface-water availability itself becomes scarce in years of insufficient rainfall
- Drought types: Meteorological, agricultural, hydrological
## 2. Monsoon and extreme rainfall
- NE monsoon (Oct-Dec) = about 11% of annual rain
- Extreme rainfall: Extreme pre-monsoon rainfall events doubled between 1981 and 2020
- Each 1°C of warming → about 7% more atmospheric moisture
- Flash floods surged in Brahmaputra, Ganga and Krishna basins
## 3. Water resources
- National Water Mission (NAPCC, 30 Jun 2008): Raise water-use efficiency by 20%
- Agriculture: Uses ~80% of fresh water
- ⚠ 1°C warming → 7% more moisture; National Water Mission → 20% efficiency
### Agriculture → yield impacts and programmes
## 1. Yield impacts
- Wheat: Climate change already cutting wheat productivity in India
- Yield loss: Wheat/maize lose 4-10% yield per 1 C
- El Niño vs La Niña: El Niño usually weakens SW monsoon + suppresses rice and sugarcane yields (Jul 2026); La Niña usually boosts monsoon rain
- Livestock:
- Extreme heat cuts dairy milk yield by 15-25% (FAO-WMO report, 23 Apr 2026)
- Heat stress above 25 C

### Observed global impacts → places, FIRSTs and data — Biodiversity
## 1. Biodiversity
- Rutgers University (US) study: Warming can wipe out about twice as many ocean species as land species (APPSC-G1 2019)
- IPBES 2019 (Bonn; 130 members incl. India):
- ~8 million species; up to 1 million threatened
- 40% of amphibians, 33% of reef corals, over a third of marine mammals

- Range shifts: Species moving poleward + upslope to track cooler climates
- ⚠ Okjokull = first dead glacier (Iceland); Quelccaya = largest tropical ice cap (Peru)
### Tipping points and 1.5 C vs 2 C → elements and projections
## 1. Tipping points
- Tipping point: Threshold where small changes trigger large, self-perpetuating, irreversible shifts; behaviour non-linear
- UNSG Scientific Advisory Board Brief #12 (London Climate Action Week, Jun 2026):
- Warming ~1.3 C
- Corals: 1.2 C threshold for large-scale warm-water reef collapse already crossed; recovery from mass bleaching takes >15 years
- Beyond ~2 C parts of the Amazon may become savanna → carbon store to source
| Tipping element | Status/note |
| Arctic sea ice (APPSC-G2 2025 answer) | Near record lows |
| Warm-water coral reefs | Threshold already crossed - most fragile |
| Amazon rainforest | ~17-18% lost; makes ~50% of its own rain, so tree loss breaks the cycle (LENS, May 2026) |
| Greenland and West Antarctic ice sheets, AMOC, permafrost | Other elements scientists list [GK] |
## 2. Projected impacts - 1.5 C vs 2 C
- Paris Agreement: Well below 2 C, pursue 1.5 C; 195 parties

- ⚠ Coral threshold 1.2 C already crossed; Amazon risk beyond ~2 C
