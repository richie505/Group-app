### Biodiversity hotspot — definition, criteria and FIRSTs
## Definition
- Hotspot criterion: Richness, endemism and threat
- Habitat destruction: The main factor causing biodiversity loss
- Definition: Biogeographic region with large reservoir of biodiversity threatened with destruction
- Proposer: Norman Myers, 1988
## Criteria (both needed)
- Criterion 1: At least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species (over 0.5% of world total)
- Criterion 2: Must have lost at least 70% of its original habitat
- APPSC-G2 2017 answer: Hotspots characterised by endemic flowering plants and threat perception
## Types
| Type | Meaning |
| Hottest hotspots | 8 hotspots with highest endemism under greatest threat; include Western Ghats-Sri Lanka |
| Coldspots | Areas of relatively low diversity losing habitat fast; still matter for rare species, key ecosystem functions, genetic diversity |
| Temperate hotspots | Mediterranean Basin, California Floristic Province, North American Coastal Plain |
- Distribution: Not only tropical
- Most lie in tropics; South-East Asian islands among the richest

### Western Ghats — profile, UNESCO status and threats by hotspot
## Profile
- Extent: Western Ghats (Sahyadri) run about 1,600 km through 6 states (six States)

### Acts → Biological Diversity Act 2002 and 2023 amendment
## Basics
- Purpose: Gives effect to India's obligations under CBD (1992)
- Assent: 5 February 2003
- Three pillars: Conservation, sustainable use and access and benefit sharing (ABS)
| Tier | Body | Role |
| National | National Biodiversity Authority (NBA), Chennai, set up 2003 | regulates foreign/commercial access and IPR on Indian bioresources; advises the Centre; has civil court powers (LENS Nov 2025) |
| State | State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs); UT Biodiversity Councils | regulate commercial use by Indian entities |
| Local | Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) | document biodiversity, prepare PBRs, levy collection fees |
## Sections and amendment
| Provision | Year | Fact |
| Section 38 | - | About 1,500+ plant species declared threatened by States |
| Section 37 | - | Biodiversity Heritage Sites (see BHS page) |
| Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023 | in force 1 April 2024 | 2023 amendment |
| Biological Diversity Rules, 2024 | 2024 | Companion framework + statutory National Biodiversity Authority Fund (NBAF) |
- 2023 main changes:
- Offences decriminalised → replaced with penalties
- AYUSH practitioners + codified traditional knowledge exempted from prior intimation to SBBs
- Cultivated medicinal plants exempted
- ⚠ Act 2002, assent 5 February 2003, NBA set up 2003

### Access and benefit sharing — concept, Kani model, earnings, fund rules
## Concept
- ABS: Commercial benefits from bioresources shared with providing communities
- Covers plant, animal, microbial resources + traditional knowledge
- Excludes human genetic material
- Timeline: CBD introduced it 1992 → Nagoya Protocol operationalised it 2010
## Kani model (first well-known benefit-sharing model)
| Item | Fact |
| Plant | Arogyapacha (Trichopus zeylanicus) |
| Drug | anti-fatigue drug "Jeevani" |
| Sharing | Licence fee + royalty shared with a Kani trust |
## Earnings and rules
- NBA's ABS has raised over ₹266 crore since 2008:
- Red Sanders = 45% (₹120 crore)
- Seed sector = 32.3%
- New fund rules (NBA):
- Identifiable repository → 25-40% of ABS funds to it + 60-75% to local communities via SBBs/UTBCs
- ABS Regulations 2025: Cover Digital Sequence Information
- APSBB:
- NBA sanctioned ₹82 lakh for Red Sanders conservation
### Hotspots vs Key Biodiversity Areas vs Global 200 ecoregions
| Concept | Body | Basis |
| Biodiversity hotspot | Conservation International (Myers) | endemic plants of 1,500 or more and 70% or more of habitat lost; large regions |
| Key Biodiversity Area (KBA) [GK] | IUCN-led partnership (global standard, 2016) | individual sites that contribute significantly to the global persistence of biodiversity |
| Global 200 [GK] | WWF | priority ecoregions (terrestrial, freshwater, marine) chosen for representativeness, not only threat |
