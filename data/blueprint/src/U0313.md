### Governor — position, appointment and qualifications → Article
## 1. Position
- Nominal / constitutional head of the State — the Chief Minister and the Council of Ministers are the real executive
- Article 153: A Governor for each State — the same person can be Governor of two or more States (Seventh Amendment) (APPSC-GS 2021 key)
- 7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956 → one person to be Governor of 2 or more states (same person to be Governor of two or more states) (APPSC-GS 2025, 2021; APPSC-GS 2025 key)
- Article 154: Executive power of the State vested in the Governor
- Integral part of the State Legislature — Article 168
## 2. Appointment and qualifications → Article
- Not elected — appointed (nominated) by the President by warrant under his hand and seal (Article 155)
- President by warrant under hand and seal; neither directly nor indirectly elected
- Qualifications: Citizen of India; completed 35 years of age; not an MP or MLA (must vacate any such seat); no office of profit
- Art. 157: must be a citizen (citizen of India), at least 35 years old (35 or above; completed 35 years)
- Art. 158: not a member of Parliament or a state legislature (not a member of any legislature); holds no office of profit
- Emoluments fixed by Parliament by law, cannot be reduced during the term
- Oath (Article 159): By the Chief Justice of the concerned High Court — Chief Justice of the High Court (in his absence, the senior-most judge) [GK]
- Conventions: an outsider to the state; state CM is consulted
- Exam angle: minimum age of 35 (APPSC-GS 2019); "pleasure of the President" makes the office structurally dependent on the Centre (CDI).
### Term, removal and President vs Governor
## 1. Term and removal
- Article 156: Holds office during the pleasure of the President — not the Chief Minister
- Normal term: Five years — can be removed earlier or continue beyond it
- Term: normal term 5 years, but continues until his successor enters office (Art. 156(3))
- ⚠ Trap: "quits office immediately after the term expires" is the wrong statement (APPSC-G1 2019)
- Resigns to the President; may be transferred and reappointed
- Removal: No grounds for removal in the Constitution (grounds not stated) → limits set by B.P. Singhal (2010) (see cases page)
- Accountability: not answerable to either House of Parliament, the state legislature, or the Council of Ministers (PYQ 2019)
- ⚠ Appointed by the President, resigns to the President; oath from the Chief Justice of the High Court

### Emoluments and immunities
## 1. Emoluments and immunities
- Emoluments (Article 202): Charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State — for a Governor of two or more States, the President decides how the salary is shared
- charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State (Art. 202(3)) (some notes say Consolidated Fund of India)
- Common Governor: one Governor for two states → cost shared among states as the President decides
## 2. Immunity — Article 361
- No criminal proceedings during the term of office
- No criminal proceedings or arrest during term
- Civil proceedings only after two months' notice
- Civil proceedings for personal acts need 2 months' notice
- Not answerable to any court for official acts
### Governor's powers → Articles (executive, pardoning, emergency)
## 1. Powers
- Executive:
- Appoints the Chief Minister, other Ministers (on the CM's advice), the Advocate-General, the State Election Commissioner, the Chairman and members of the State Public Service Commission
- Appoints District Judges in consultation with the High Court
- Article 167: Can seek information from the Chief Minister
- Pardoning (Article 161): Pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions; suspend or commute sentences — only for offences against State laws
- Emergency: Can recommend President's Rule — Article 356

### Legislative powers
## 1. Governor's role — sessions and address
- Article 174: Summons, prorogues and dissolves the Assembly; maximum gap between sessions six months
- Part of the legislature without being a member
- Article 176: Addresses the Assembly at the commencement of the first session after each general election and the first session of each year — a mandatory special address
- Delivered on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers (Nabam Rebia 2016; 2026)
- Art. 175: may address or send messages to the House(s) at any time (optional)
## 2. Other legislative powers
- Bills (Article 200): Assent, withhold assent, or reserve for the President
- Ordinances (Article 213): When the Legislature is not in session
- Nominates one-sixth of the Legislative Council (where the State has one) (nominates 1/6th)
- Disqualification of MLAs under Art. 191(1) (not anti-defection): decides on the opinion of the Election Commission (Art. 192); anti-defection cases go to the Speaker/Chairman
- Reports: places reports of the CAG, SPSC and State Finance Commission before the legislature
- ⚠ 175 = optional address; 176 = mandatory special address

### Article 164 provisions — Union vs State Council of Ministers
## 1. Union vs State Council of Ministers
| Basis | Union | State |
| Aids and advises | President — Article 74 | Governor — Article 163 |
| Collectively responsible to | Lok Sabha — Article 75(3) | Legislative Assembly — Article 164(2) |
| Ministers hold office during the pleasure of | President (not the PM) | Governor (not the CM) |
| Maximum size (91st Amendment, 2003) | 15% of the Lok Sabha | 15% of the Assembly; minimum 12 |
| Non-member Minister — maximum | Six consecutive months — Article 75(5) | Six consecutive months — Article 164(4) |
| Cabinet (smaller inner core) | Prime Minister + Cabinet Ministers | Chief Minister + Cabinet Ministers |
- Responsibility: Only to the Lower House — not to the Rajya Sabha, Parliament as a whole, or the President/Governor
- Collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly — Article 164(2) — not to the Legislative Council
- A passed No-Confidence Motion → the entire Council, including the CM, must resign
- 91st Constitutional Amendment Act (2003): Total Ministers including the CM ≤ 15% of the Assembly's total strength; minimum 12 (including the CM) in smaller States (Art. 164(1A))
- "Cabinet": Inserted into Article 352 by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978 — which also lets the President return the Council's advice once; he is bound after reconsideration
- Governor: Bound by ministerial advice except in discretionary areas
## 2. Article 164 provisions
- Art. 164(1): CM is appointed by the Governor; other ministers on CM's advice; hold office during Governor's pleasure (APPSC-GS 2021 key: Art. 164)
- Art. 164(4): non-member minister must become MLA/MLC within six months
- Tribal Welfare Minister: a Minister for Tribal Welfare (may also handle SC and backward class welfare) mandatory in Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha
- Bihar dropped by the 94th Amendment, 2006
- ⚠ 91st (2003) = 15% cap and minimum 12; 94th (2006) = Bihar dropped from the tribal-minister list
### CM's role and the hung House
## 1. CM's role
- Art. 167: CM must communicate Cabinet decisions to the Governor + furnish information he calls for
- CM = chief link between Governor and Council
- Loss of majority: CM must resign or be dismissed
- Once a new CM is sworn in, the old one ceases; only one CM can hold office
## 2. Hung House — discretion flashpoint
- Hung House (Art 164): order of invitation
- 1. Pre-poll alliance
- 2. Largest party with support
- 3. Post-poll coalition
- 4. Post-poll alliance with outside support
- Early floor test follows
- Governor-state conflicts - Hung assembly, Tamil Nadu (May 2026):
- Governor invited C. Joseph Vijay on 120 letters of support (Art. 164), per Sarkaria/Punchhi order, with an early floor test; sworn in as CM
### Discretionary powers — Article 163
## 1. Article 163
- Art. 163(1): Council of Ministers headed by the CM aids and advises the Governor, except where he must act in his discretion
- Art. 163(2): Governor's own decision on whether a matter is discretionary (what falls within his discretion) is final
- Governor's decision is final; his acts cannot be questioned on that ground
- Art. 163(3): advice tendered by ministers cannot be inquired into in any court
### Discretion and special responsibilities — types of discretion
## 1. Discretion and special responsibilities
- Discretionary powers: Appoint a CM in a hung Assembly; dismiss a Council that has lost its majority; dissolve the Assembly; reserve a Bill for the President (even against the Council's advice); recommend President's Rule
- Not bound by the Council of Ministers' advice in all matters
- Fifth Schedule: Special responsibility for Scheduled Areas — report to the President
- Sixth Schedule: Special powers over Autonomous District Councils; special responsibility in certain States and tribal areas
## 2. Types of discretion
| Constitutional (express) discretion | Situational discretion |
| Reserving a bill for the President (Art. 200) | Appointing a CM when no party has a clear majority (hung Assembly) |
| Recommending President's Rule (Art. 356) | Dismissing a ministry that has lost majority |
| Seeking information from the CM (Art. 167) | Dissolving the Assembly when the ministry has lost majority |
| Administrator of adjoining UT (Art. 239) | - |
| Sixth Schedule royalty share (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram); special responsibilities under Art. 371-371J (e.g., law and order under 371A Nagaland, 371H Arunachal) | - |
### Discretionary powers — scope of discretion
## 1. Scope of discretion
- vs President: Governor's discretion is wider (wider than the President's); the Constitution gives the President no such discretion
- Art. 239(2): as administrator of an adjoining UT, the Governor acts independently of his own state Council of Ministers
- Express discretion (UPSC)
- Discretion is subject to judicial review (see the Governor cases page: Rameshwar Prasad, Nabam Rebia)
- ⚠ Hung Assembly = situational discretion; Art. 200 reservation, Art. 356 report, Art. 167 information = constitutional discretion
### Bills → Article 200 - Governor's options
## 1. Assent and ordinances
- Articles 200 and 201: The Governor can give assent, withhold assent, return an ordinary Bill (no fixed time limit to act on a returned Bill), or reserve it for the President
## 2. Article 200 - Governor's options
- Four options: assent, withhold assent, return once for reconsideration (not a Money Bill), or reserve for the President
- Re-passed bill: Governor must assent; cannot withhold again (APPCA 2026)
- Bill may be reserved only once; a re-enacted bill cannot be reserved again unless materially altered
- Mandatory reservation: a bill that derogates from the powers of the High Court (endangering the High Court's position)
- APPSC-G1 2024 key "Only III"
### Bills reserved to get protection → Article
## 1. Bills reserved to get protection → Article
- Reserved to get protection (not a general veto):
- Compulsory acquisition of property (Art. 31A/31C)
- Concurrent-List repugnancy - bills repugnant to central law (Art. 254(2))
- Taxes on water/electricity in inter-state river projects (Art. 288)
- Inter-state trade restrictions (Art. 304(b)) - need prior Presidential sanction
- Money bills during a Financial Emergency (Art. 360)
- Central control: reservation = a form of central control over states (APPSC-GS 2025; APPSC 2025 key: reservation is an example of central control over states)
- Centre's control over state legislation: Previous sanction of the President before introducing bills restricting inter-state trade - Art. 304(b) (PYQ 2018)
### Article 201 - President's options
## 1. Article 201 - President's options
- Options: assent, withhold, or (non-Money Bill) direct the Governor to return it
- Legislature must reconsider within 6 months; President not bound to assent afterwards
- Absolute veto: President has an absolute veto over reserved bills
- Time limit: none prescribed (Apr 2025: President withheld assent to Tamil Nadu's anti-NEET Bill)
- ⚠ Only mandatory reservation = bill derogating from the High Court's powers; 304(b) = prior sanction, not reservation after passage

### Assent time limits — Punjab 2023, TN April 2025, Reference Nov 2025
## 1. Assent time limits — Punjab 2023, TN April 2025, Reference Nov 2025
- State of Punjab v. Principal Secy to Governor (2023): Governor must act "as soon as possible" and cannot keep bills pending indefinitely + a re-passed bill must get assent
- State of Tamil Nadu v. Governor of TN (8 April 2025): read time limits into Art. 200
- Referring 10 re-enacted bills to the President was unconstitutional
- 1 month if withheld on advice
- 3 months against advice
- 1 month after re-passage (re-presented bill)
- Used Art 142 to grant "deemed assent" to 10 bills (declared them assented)
- ⚠ Trap: some notes (APPCA 2026) date it to 2023 and treat deemed assent as good law; decided Apr 2025 (State of TN v. Governor (April 2025))
- Art 143 Reference (President Murmu) - 16th Presidential Reference - Presidential Reference (Art. 143) opinion of November 2025 (5-judge bench; Five-judge opinion, Nov 2025):
- no court-imposed timelines - courts cannot fix timelines
- deemed assent rejected - courts cannot declare deemed assent; "deemed assent" is unconstitutional
- No indefinite inaction either: prolonged, unexplained inaction → limited mandamus (limited direction to decide)
- Judicial review limited to mala fides and procedure
- President need not seek SC advice on each reserved bill
- Governor not bound by advice in choosing among the Art 200 options
- Governor = "guide, philosopher and friend" of the government
- Exam angle: statements on the four options, the one mandatory reservation, and whether courts can fix timelines (current position: they cannot).
- Exam angle: the April 2025 timelines and deemed assent do not survive the Nov 2025 Reference opinion.
- ⚠ Art 142 = deemed assent granted (Apr 2025); Art 143 Reference = deemed assent rejected (Nov 2025)
### President's Rule — Article 356: provisions
## 1. President's Rule — Article 356
- When: The government of a State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution
- How: On the Governor's report or otherwise (suo motu)
- Beyond 1 year only if:
- A National Emergency is in operation in all of India or any part of the State, and
- The Election Commission of India certifies that Assembly elections cannot be held
## 2. Provisions
| Feature | Rule |
| Duration | 6 months at a time, maximum 3 years (APPSC 2019 key) [GK] |
| Beyond 1 year | Only with a National Emergency in force and an EC certificate that elections cannot be held (44th Amendment) [GK] |
| Assembly | Suspended or dissolved; Parliament legislates for the state (Art 357) [GK] |
- Ground: state's government cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution
- Punjab extension: 68th Amendment stretched Punjab's 1987 proclamation to 5 years (2026)
- Central laws under PR: reach a state only through an Art 357(1)(a) resolution
- Example: Water Amendment Act, extended to Manipur (Dec 2025)
### Article 356 — FIRSTs and frequency
## 1. FIRSTs and frequency
- FIRST: Punjab, 1951
- Kerala, 1959 = first dismissal of an elected government
- Frequency: used over 125 times
- Never used in Telangana or Chhattisgarh (APPSC 2019 key)
- Most often in Manipur; 2025 was its 11th time
- Recent: Manipur, from 13 Feb 2025 (APPSC 2024 key: Art 356)
- Revoked on 4 Feb 2026; Y. Khemchand Singh became CM
- AP instance: Andhra State had it in 1954-55
- ⚠ Punjab = first use (1951); Kerala = first dismissal of an elected government (1959)
## 2. Special cases
- Former J&K:
- 6 months of Governor's Rule under Sec 92 of the J&K Constitution, then Art 356 (e.g., 2018)
- After 2019: President's Rule under the Reorganisation Act until Oct 2024
- Ayodhya (Dec 1992): BJP governments of UP, MP, Rajasthan and HP removed
- Bommai upheld MP, Rajasthan, HP dismissals on the ground of secularism (UPSC)
### Judicial review of Article 356 — Bommai guidelines, proper vs improper use
- Bommai guidelines (UPSC):
- Proclamations judicially reviewable; Centre must show relevant material
- Majority tested by a floor test, not in Raj Bhavan
- Assembly not dissolved before Parliament approves; ministry can be restored
- Secularism is a basic feature; a government violating it can be dismissed
| Proper use | Improper use |
| Hung House with no alternative | Intra-party disputes |
| Flagrant defiance of directions | Maladministration alone |
| Physical breakdown | Ordinary law and order |
- AP High Court episode: the Supreme Court stayed it
