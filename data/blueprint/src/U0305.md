### Speaker → constitutional basis, resignation, dissolution and removal
## 1. Constitutional basis
- Part V — Articles 93, 94, 95, 96, 100, 110 and 118
- Election (Article 93): By the Lok Sabha from among its sitting members — must already be an elected MP (a non-member cannot be chosen under the six-month relaxation rule)
- Date of election: Fixed by the President
- Oath: No separate oath as presiding officer — only the oath as an ordinary Member of Parliament
- Lok Sabha Secretariat: Functions under the Speaker's control and guidance
## 2. Speaker — resignation, dissolution and removal
- Resignation: In writing to the Deputy Speaker — Article 94(b) (laterally, not upward to the President)
- On dissolution: Does not vacate office immediately — continues until just before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha
- Removal — Article 94(c): Resolution passed by a majority of all the then members (effective majority), after at least 14 days' written notice
- Speaker vacates office on ceasing to be a member (Art 94)
### Presiding officers → oath and resignation/removal table
## 1. All presiding officers
- No separate oath of office as presiding officers — the Speaker, Deputy Speaker and Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman take oath only as ordinary Members of Parliament
| Office | Chosen how | Resignation addressed to | Removal |
| Speaker of the Lok Sabha | LS elects Speaker and Deputy Speaker from its members (Art 93) | Deputy Speaker — Article 94(b) — ⚠ not the President | Lok Sabha resolution by an effective majority — Article 94; 14 days' notice |
| Deputy Speaker of the Lok Sabha | Election: By the LS after the Speaker | Speaker — Article 94(b) — ⚠ not the President | Lok Sabha resolution by an effective majority — Article 94; 14 days' notice |
| Chairman (RS) | Vice-President is ex officio Chairman (Art 64, 89) | - | Only when removed as VP |
| Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha | Art 89(2), 90; elected from RS members | Chairman of the Rajya Sabha | Rajya Sabha resolution by an effective majority — Article 90; 14 days' notice |
- 14 days' notice: At least 14 days' advance written notice is required for the removal of the President, the Lok Sabha Speaker, the Lok Sabha Deputy Speaker and the Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman
- ⚠ Speaker → resigns to Deputy Speaker; Deputy Speaker → to Speaker; Deputy Chairman → to Chairman
### Speaker → normal sittings vs own removal resolution; Pro-Tem Speaker
## 1. Normal sittings vs own removal resolution
| Situation | Presides | Speaks / takes part | Votes in the first instance | Casting vote |
| Normal sittings | Yes | Yes | No | Yes — on an equality of votes (Article 100) |
| Own removal resolution (Article 96) | No — even if present | Yes | Yes — like an ordinary member | No — not in a tie |
- While a removal resolution is pending:
- Speaker (Art 96) → cannot preside, but can speak, take part and vote in the first instance (not on a tie)
- Chairman → cannot preside + cannot vote, but may speak
- Salary (Speaker): Charged on the Consolidated Fund (Art 97)
- ⚠ Removal pending: Speaker can vote (first instance); Chairman cannot vote
## 2. Regular Speaker vs Pro-Tem Speaker
| Basis | Regular Speaker | Pro-Tem Speaker |
| Basis | Article 93 | Article 95(1) |
| Chosen by | Elected by Lok Sabha members | Appointed by the President — selection follows a convention (practice): usually the senior-most elected MP of the new House |
| Role | Regular presiding officer; enforces rules and business | Administers the oath to newly elected MPs; enables the election of the regular Speaker |
| Tenure | Continues through dissolution until the next House meets | Temporary — ceases the moment the regular Speaker is elected |
- On dissolution:
- Continuity: Speaker continues till immediately before the first meeting of the new LS
- Speaker Pro Tem: Senior-most member, sworn in by the President → presides and conducts the new Speaker's election
### Speaker → powers and Articles
## 1. Powers
- Money Bill — Article 110(4): Sole and final authority to certify a Money Bill — the endorsement is final when the Bill goes to the Rajya Sabha and to the President
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the Speaker's final say on whether a bill is a Money Bill rests on Art 110(3)
- Joint sitting — Article 118(4): Presides; in his absence the Deputy Speaker, before the Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha
- Quorum (Article 100): One-tenth of the House — adjourns or suspends the sitting when there is no quorum
- Adjournment sine die
- Anti-defection (Tenth Schedule): Decides disqualification — subject to judicial review (Kihoto Hollohan)
- Rule 8(1): Fixes the date for the election of the Deputy Speaker
## 2. Power → Article
| Power | Article / basis |
| Casting vote: Only in case of a tie | Art 100(1) |
| Money Bill certification: Final say on whether a bill is a Money Bill | Art 110(3) |
| Joint sitting: Presides over a joint sitting | Art 108; Art 118(4) |
| Anti-defection: Decides cases under Tenth Schedule | Subject to judicial review (Kihoto Hollohan, 1992) |

| Other powers: Rulings protected | Art 122 |
### Deputy Speaker → Articles 93 to 96, powers and position
## 1. Deputy Speaker — Articles 93 to 96
- Elected by the Lok Sabha from its sitting members (Article 93)
- Date fixed by the Speaker (Rule 8(1)); communicated to members by the Secretary-General
- From the Opposition: Only a convention (practice) — not a constitutional requirement
- Oath: No separate oath — only as an ordinary MP
- Resignation: To the Speaker — Article 94(b) (not to the President or the Prime Minister)
- Removal: Effective majority — a majority of all the then members of the Lok Sabha (not a simple or two-thirds majority); fourteen days' notice
- On dissolution: Vacates office immediately — the protection of continuing after dissolution applies only to the Speaker
## 2. Deputy Speaker — powers and position
- In the Chair: Same powers as the Speaker — no appeal to the Speaker against his ruling
- Joint sittings: Presides when the Speaker is absent — Article 118(4)
- Parliamentary Committee: Automatically becomes its chairman when appointed to it
- When not in the Chair (normal proceedings): Sits as an ordinary member of the House — can participate in debates, move motions, vote in the first instance
- Own removal resolution (Article 96): Cannot preside even if present; can speak, participate and vote in the first instance; no casting vote when votes are equally divided
- Presiding role: When the Speaker is absent or office vacant
- Status: Not subordinate to the Speaker; becomes chairman of any committee he is appointed to
### Chairman of Rajya Sabha and panels → roles
## 1. Chairman of Rajya Sabha
- Vote: Casting vote only
- Acting President: Draws the President's salary; Deputy Chairman presides (Art 91)
## 2. Panels
- Panel of Chairpersons: Nominated by the Speaker (up to 10)
- Member presides in absence of both, but not when Speaker's office is vacant
- RS equivalent: Panel of Vice-Chairpersons nominated by the Chairman

### Summoning and the six-month rule — Article 85
## 1. Summoning — Article 85 (Part V)
- The President summons each House at such time and place as he thinks fit
- Maximum gap between two sessions: six months — between the last sitting of one session and the first sitting of the next (APPSC G2 2017)
- So Parliament must meet at least twice a year (at least two sessions a year)
- ⚠ Not in the Constitution:
- A fixed number of sessions — Budget, Monsoon and Winter sessions are a convention
- A minimum number of sitting days — not 60, 90 or 100 per year
- Three sessions (by convention):
- Budget (Feb-May, longest)
- Quorum: One-tenth of total members incl. the presiding officer → Art 100(3)
- Some notes list it as Art 100(1); 100(1) actually carries the casting vote
- States (Art 174): State Assemblies averaged only 24 sittings in 2025 (Odisha 43, Nagaland 7) (PRS, 4 Jun 2026)
## 2. President's address
- Purpose: Outlines government policy; debated on a Motion of Thanks
### Secretariat → Article 98
## 1. Secretariat - Art 98
- Posts common to both Houses may be created
- Recruitment: Parliament may regulate by law; until then rules by the President after consulting Speaker/Chairman
- Head: Secretary-General of each House
- Appointed by the presiding officer; rank = Cabinet Secretary
- Independent of the executive
- ⚠ Quorum = 100(3); casting vote = 100(1)
### Ending a sitting or session → adjournment, prorogation, dissolution
## 1. Ending a sitting or session
| Device | By whom | Ends | Effect |
| Adjournment | Presiding Officer (Speaker or Chairman) | A sitting (hours/days/weeks) | Suspends the sitting for a specified time — hours, days or weeks; session unaffected; pending business stays active |
| Adjournment sine die | Presiding Officer | Sitting, no date fixed for next | Ends the sitting indefinitely — no day named for reassembly; precedes prorogation; bills remain alive for the next session |
| Prorogation — Article 85(2)(a) | President, on the advice of the Council of Ministers (Governor in States) | The session | Terminates the session; pending bills unaffected; notices lapse unless re-introduced |
| Dissolution — Article 85(2)(b) | President (LS only) | Life of the House | Ends the very life of the Lok Sabha — pending bills, motions and resolutions lapse (subject to Article 107 rules); the Rajya Sabha is permanent |
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say on prorogation pending notices lapse except notices to introduce bills
- On LS dissolution: Many bills lapse (Dissolution row above)
- Adjournment sine die (APPSC 2025): Pending bills continue → can be taken up in the next session
## 2. Terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Session | First sitting → prorogation/dissolution |
| Recess | Prorogation → next session |
| Quorum | One-tenth of total members incl. the presiding officer |
- ⚠ Adjournment / sine die = presiding officer; prorogation / dissolution = President

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:31
### Presiding officers
## Speaker and Deputy Speaker
- Speaker must already be an elected Lok Sabha member; the six-month grace is only for Ministers
- Speaker and Deputy Speaker take only the oath as MPs; no extra oath as presiding officer
- Removing the Speaker — Article 94(c): effective majority (majority of all the then members), not simple majority
- Removal notice — at least 14 days
- Speaker resigns to the Deputy Speaker, not to the President
- Deputy Speaker's removal also needs an effective majority (not two-thirds) after 14 days' notice
- Deputy Speaker post vacant throughout the 17th Lok Sabha (and the 18th)
## Panel of Chairpersons
- Nominated by the Speaker; up to ten members
- A panel member presides when both Speaker and Deputy Speaker are absent, not when the Speaker's post is vacant
## Rajya Sabha and others
- Deputy Chairman resigns to the Chairman; removed by an effective majority after 14 days' notice
- When the Vice-President acts as President, the Deputy Chairman does the Chairman's work
- Governor — removed by the President at will, no notice; the other removals need 14 days' notice
### Speaker: status
- Salaries of Speaker and Deputy Speaker — charged on the Consolidated Fund of India (Article 97)
- Speaker's election date fixed by the President; Deputy Speaker's by the Speaker
- Deputy Speaker — elected by the House from its members; an Opposition member is only a convention, not a constitutional rule
- Only the Speaker stays in office after dissolution; both are elected by the House, so that is not the reason for the difference
## Pro-Tem Speaker
- Usually the senior-most member of the House
- Appointed by the President (Article 95(1)), not elected by the House; sworn in by the President
- Gives the oath to new MPs (administers oath) and holds the regular Speaker's election
- Stays until the regular Speaker is elected
### Normal sitting vs own removal (Art. 96)
| Situation | Speaker | Deputy Speaker |
| Normal sitting (Art. 100) | No vote in first instance; casting vote in a tie | Out of the Chair: ordinary member — can debate, move motions, vote in first instance |
| Own removal resolution (Art. 96) | Cannot preside; may speak, take part and vote in first instance; no casting vote | Cannot preside; may speak and vote in first instance; no casting vote |
### Speaker's powers
## Articles
- Article 93 — House chooses two members as Speaker and Deputy Speaker
- Speaker's provisions — Articles 93–96 (election, vacation, removal), 100 (casting vote), 110 (Money Bill certificate), 118 (joint sitting rules)
- Article 86 — President may address either/both Houses, call members and send messages on pending Bills
## Powers
- ★ Speaker certifies the Money Bill; decision final — no court, House or President can question it
- Speaker decides Tenth Schedule cases, but these stay subject to judicial review
- Leader of the Opposition recognised by Speaker — party needs at least one-tenth of the House's strength; post legal since the 1977 Act
- President appoints the Pro-Tem Speaker; adjourning sine die, Tenth Schedule decisions and fixing the Deputy Speaker's election date are the Speaker's
## Deputy Speaker and joint sitting
- Deputy Speaker is not subordinate to the Speaker while doing the office's work
- Deputy Speaker's ruling from the Chair cannot be appealed to the Speaker
- Joint sitting (Art. 118(4)) — chaired by Speaker, then Deputy Speaker, then the Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman

## Secretariat and Money Bill
- Secretariat staff rules (if no law) — made by the President after consulting the Speaker and Chairman
- Lok Sabha Secretariat — directly under the Speaker's control, not the executive
- Article 110(4) — only Speaker certifies a Money Bill; certificate final; Rajya Sabha cannot overrule it
### Ending a sitting or session
## Terms
- Adjournment — stops work in a sitting for a set time
- Prorogation — ends a session only (not the House); by President under Article 85(2)(a)
- Recess — time from prorogation to the next session
- Dissolution — ends the Lok Sabha itself; Article 85(2)(b); Article 123 is ordinances
## Effects
- Bills do not lapse on prorogation; pending notices lapse, except notices to introduce bills
- On dissolution pending business lapses, subject to Article 107; the Rajya Sabha is permanent
- Speaker does not vacate office on dissolution; stays until just before the new House first meets
- Quorum — one-tenth of total members, counting the presiding officer (Article 100(3))
### Sessions & Secretariat
## Sessions
- Max gap of six months — from the last sitting of one session to the first sitting of the next
- Constitution fixes no number of sessions and no minimum sitting days; the six-month gap means at least two sessions a year
- Budget, Monsoon and Winter sessions — a convention
- President may summon the two Houses separately; each must meet within six months of its last sitting
- Article 87 — President gives special address at the first session after each general election and the first session each year; not after every prorogation
- President's address debated through a Motion of Thanks
## Secretariat
- Article 98 — each House has separate secretarial staff; common posts for both Houses may be made
- Secretary-General — appointed by the presiding officer of the House, not the Union Government
- Deputy Speaker's election date — set by the Speaker under Rule 8(1); told to members by the Secretary-General
### Exam corner
- Lok Sabha Deputy Speaker post empty since June 2019; also empty in eight State Assemblies
- First AP Legislative Council Chairman — Madapati Hanumantha Rao
- Kalluri Subbarao — Deputy Speaker (first AP Legislature)
### Speaker → constitutional basis, resignation, dissolution and removal
## Election and oath
- ★ Article 93 — Speaker is chosen by the House from among its members
- ★ Elected by all the then members of the Lok Sabha
- ★ No non-member can be chosen; Speaker must be a member at the time of election (no six-month window)
- ★ Speaker and Deputy Speaker do not take an oath of office
- ★ Pro-tem Speaker — administers oath to newly elected members
## Resignation, dissolution, removal
- ★ Article 94 — Speaker resigns in writing to the Deputy Speaker
- ★ On dissolution the Speaker does not vacate office until just before the first meeting of the new House
- ★ Removed by a resolution of the Lok Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members — Article 94(c)
## Joint sitting and Secretariat
- ★ Joint session called by the President; chaired by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha
- ★ Lok Sabha Secretariat — directly controlled and supervised by the Speaker; works through the Secretary (Article 98)
