### Preamble text — order of words, date, original vs absent words
## 1. Nature of the State
- People of India resolved to constitute India into a: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic
- Order of words: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic
- "We, the People of India": Constitution derives its authority from the people
- Opens the Preamble; enacting clause - the people "adopt, enact and give to ourselves" the Constitution
- ⚠ Trap: "Socialist" and "Secular" were not in the original Preamble; the word "Federal" is not mentioned in the Preamble at all
## 2. Date in the Preamble
- 26 November 1949: The Constituent Assembly adopted, enacted and gave to itself the Constitution
- The date mentioned is the date of: Adoption - not commencement (26 Jan 1950)
- Ends with: "IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION."
## 3. Original vs absent words
- Original 1949 text: "SOVEREIGN DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC" + "unity of the Nation"
- At commencement (26 Jan 1950): India described as a "Sovereign Democratic Republic"
- Words absent in 1949: Socialist, Secular, Integrity
- Not in the Preamble at all (absent from the Preamble): Federal/federation, Fundamental Duties, "welfare state", "adult franchise"
- ⚠ Trap: Liberty "of action" and equality "of rights" are NOT in it

### Is the Preamble part of the Constitution? — landmark cases
## 1. Is the Preamble part of the Constitution
| Case | Year | Held |
| Berubari Union Case (President's reference) | 1960 | Preamble is not a part of the Constitution. It is a key to the framers' minds and can guide interpretation where an article is ambiguous |
| Golaknath Case | 1967 | Preamble is a part of the Constitution |
| Kesavananda Bharati Case | 1973 | Preamble is a part of the Constitution (settled); it can be amended; the basic structure cannot be destroyed. Overruled Berubari; amendable under Art. 368, but not its basic structure |
| LIC of India Case | 1995 | Reaffirmed: Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution [GK] |
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say it was Kesavananda Bharati (1973) that first overruled Berubari and held the Preamble IS part of the Constitution, and list Golaknath only as a distractor
- Exam angle: APPSC GS 2025 asked "Preamble is an integral part - which case?" The answer is Kesavananda Bharati. Golaknath, Minerva Mills and Gopalan are the distractors.
### Amendability and secularism as basic structure
## 1. Amendability and secularism as basic structure
- Amendable under: Article 368 — but the basic structure cannot be altered
| Case | Year | Holding |
| Kesavananda Bharati | 1973 | Preamble can be amended under Art. 368, but its basic features cannot be destroyed - Parliament cannot alter its basic features |
| Waman Rao | 1981 | Basic-structure review applies to amendments made after 24 Apr 1973 |
| S.R. Bommai | 1994 | Reaffirmed secularism as basic structure → a state government acting against it could be dismissed |
| Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India | 25 Nov 2024 | SC upheld 'Socialist' and 'Secular' in the Preamble; rejected challenges to the 42nd Amendment insertions |
- Pre-1976 position: Secularism was part of the basic structure even before the word was inserted - secularism was part of the basic structure even before 1976
### Status and legal effect of the Preamble
## 1. Status and legal effect
- Reflects: The philosophy, ideals and objectives of the Constitution
- Non-justiciable: Cannot be enforced in a court of law (no court can enforce it)
- Powers and rights: Does not confer any power and does not create any right by itself
- Not a source of power to the legislature + not a limit on powers expressly granted (neither a source of power nor a limit on power)
- Used to interpret ambiguous provisions (aid to interpretation)
- Enactment sequence: Preamble enacted after the rest of the Constitution had been adopted, so that it matched the text
### 42nd Amendment, 1976 → words added and other changes
## 1. The only amendment — 42nd Amendment Act, 1976
- 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976): Known as the Mini Constitution ("Mini-Constitution" due to sweeping changes)
- Times amended: Only once so far (42nd Amendment Act, 1976 - amended only once; amendments so far: One only)
- Words added: Socialist, Secular and Integrity
- Change: "Unity of the Nation" → "Unity and Integrity of the Nation"
- ⚠ Trap: UPSC notes say "1976 Second Amendment" - wrong; it is the 42nd
- Context: Enacted during the Emergency (1975-77) under Indira Gandhi
- Other changes:
- Fundamental Duties
- New DPSPs
- Curbs on judicial review
## 2. Minerva Mills (1980)
- Struck down clauses of the 42nd Amendment — clauses (4) and (5) of Article 368, and clauses giving absolute precedence to DPSP over Fundamental Rights
- i.e. clauses giving Parliament unlimited amending power and excluding judicial review
- Restored the balance between Part III and Part IV
- Exam angle: APPSC asked "Secular added by?" in G2 2023 and G2 2025, and "Socialist and Secular added by?" in GS 2025. The answer is always the 42nd. Distractors are the 44th, 43rd, 45th, 52nd and 86th.
