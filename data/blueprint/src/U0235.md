### Khilafat and NCM — aims, background, launch and FIRSTs
## 1. Khilafat — aim and trigger
- Objective: Protect/restore the Ottoman Sultan/Khalifa (Caliph, Sultan of Turkey) and the territorial integrity of the Ottoman Empire after its defeat in WWI
- Trigger: Treaty of Sevres, May 1920; British PM had promised Turkey would not be humiliated
- Organisation: Khilafat Committee (1919)
- End: Lost relevance when Turkey abolished the Caliphate (1924)
## 2. NCM — background
- Rowlatt Act + Jallianwala Bagh (1919)
- "Inadequate" Montagu-Chelmsford reforms (Amritsar session 1919, president Motilal Nehru, called them inadequate and disappointing)
## 3. Launch
- Launch: 1 August 1920 (the day Tilak died); Gandhi returned his Kaiser-i-Hind medal
- Khilafat Committee started non-cooperation on 31 August 1920
- Year of launch = 1920:
- Approved at the Calcutta special session (Sept 1920)
- Formally adopted at Nagpur (Dec 1920)

### Chauri Chaura and the withdrawal — date, place, reactions
## 1. Chauri Chaura
- Date: 4 February 1922 (many notes say 5 Feb 1922; a "2022" print is a misprint for 1922)
- Place: Gorakhpur district, UP
- Events: Police fired on/lathi-charged a procession (Bhagwan Ahir among those beaten) → crowd burnt the police station
- Killed 22 policemen (some notes say 21)
- Defence: Madan Mohan Malaviya defended the accused; of 172 sentenced to death, 19 finally hanged
## 2. Withdrawal
- Withdrawn Feb 1922: After Chauri Chaura; Gandhi fasted 3 days and called off the movement
- Bardoli resolution (11-12 Feb 1922): Congress turned to constructive work
- Reactions: Subhas Bose - "sprinkling cold water", "national calamity"; M.N. Roy - weak leadership
- Result: Khilafat issue died; Hindu-Muslim unity broke; communal riots 1921-27
- Exam angle: statement pairs - NCM made nationalism a mass movement (true); withdrawn after Jallianwala (false - after Chauri Chaura).
### Gandhi's arrest and the Great Trial — date, charge, judge
## 1. Arrest and Great Trial (1922)
- Arrest: 10 March 1922 at Sabarmati, Ahmedabad
- Charge: Sedition (Sec. 124A IPC) for three Young India articles (incl. "Tampering with Loyalty", "Shaking the Manes")
- "Great Trial": At Ahmedabad, 18 March 1922, before Judge C.N. Broomfield
- Gandhi pleaded guilty, asked for the highest penalty
- Sentence: 6 years; released 1924 on health grounds
- Gandhi's papers: Young India, Navajivan, Indian Opinion, Harijan
## 2. First arrest and sedition trial
- April 1919: Stopped and detained while going to Punjab (at Palwal) → sparked riots
- ⚠ Arrest 10 March (Sabarmati) → trial 18 March 1922 (Ahmedabad)

### NCM chronology
## Khilafat
- Aim — restore the Ottoman Sultan as Khalifa and protect his territories
- Opposed the Treaty of Sevres (May 1920)
- Leaders: Ali brothers, Abul Kalam Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Hasrat Mohani, Dr. M.A. Ansari; Tej Bahadur Sapru — not a Khilafat leader (mediator of Gandhi-Irwin Pact, 1931)

- ★ Hakim Ajmal Khan gave up title Haziq-ul-Mulk in the Khilafat agitation

## Launch
- Gandhi chaired the All-India Khilafat Conference, Delhi, November 1919 — first proposed non-violent non-cooperation

### Khilafat and NCM — aims, background, launch and FIRSTs
- ★ NCM launched — 1920
- ★ Khilafat objective — protect the territorial integrity of the Ottoman Empire
- ★ Founded during NCM: Kashi Vidyapith (Varanasi, 1921), Gujarat Vidyapith (Ahmedabad, 1920), Jamia Millia Islamia (Aligarh, 1920, later Delhi)
- ★ Kashi Hindu Vishva Vidyalaya — not an NCM institution
- ★ E.V. Ramaswami Naicker (Periyar) — took part in NCM; left Congress in 1925
### Leaders → role in Khilafat, NCM and after
- ★ Chitta Ranjan Das — lawyer from East Bengal, very active in NCM; gave up legal practice
- ★ Others who gave up practice: Motilal Nehru, Rajendra Prasad, Jawaharlal Nehru, Vithalbhai Patel, Vallabhbhai Patel
- ★ Maulana Hasrat Mohani — proposed Swaraj = complete independence (Ahmedabad session, 1921); coined "Inquilab Zindabad"
- ★ Legal defence after Chauri Chaura — Madan Mohan Malaviya and Krishna Kant
- ★ 170 sentenced to death by the Gorakhpur Sessions Judge
- ★ C. Rajagopalachari — not in the Swaraj Party; Swarajist leaders C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru, Vithalbhai Patel
- ★ Burning foreign cloth a "ruthless waste" — Rabindranath Tagore (preferred constructive programme)
### Sessions and conferences → president and decision
- ★ Nagpur session (1920) — led by C. Vijayaraghavachariar
- ★ Congress first stated its policy on the Indian (princely) States — wanted full responsible government
- ★ Aim: Swaraj by all legitimate and peaceful means; programme in stages, with civil disobedience and non-payment of taxes later
- ★ Gandhi: Swaraj within a year if non-cooperation is followed
- ★ Khilafat Committee at Allahabad (June 1920) asked Gandhi to lead the NCM
- ★ Gaya session (1922) — Chittaranjan Das; he had defended Aurobindo Ghosh in the Alipore Bomb Case
### NCM programme — boycott and constructive items
- ★ Programme asked for boycott of legislatures and elections — contesting elections not part of it
- ★ Included: boycott of law courts and foreign cloth, strict non-violence, panchayats for settling disputes
- ★ Retention of titles — NOT in the programme (only three of the four listed)
- ★ Programme accepted at the Calcutta special session (1920), then Nagpur (December 1920)
### National institutions → founding year and founder
- ★ Jamia Millia Islamia and Kashi Vidyapith — founded during NCM
- ★ Besant Theosophical College, Madanapalle and Aligarh Muslim University — older; not from NCM
- ★ Jai Prakash Narayan — left Patna College with 20 days left for exams; joined Bihar Vidyapeeth

- ★ Pedanandipadu no-tax campaign (1921-22) — Parvataneni Veeraiah Chowdary, called "Andhra Shivaji"

### National movement in Andhra — other movements and leaders
- ★ Salt Law — not a cause of the NCM (NCM: 1920 – February 1922, after Jallianwala Bagh)
- Salt Satyagraha centres: Mypadu — Nellore; Chinnapuram — near Machilipatnam; Devarampadu — near Ongole
### Chauri Chaura and the withdrawal — date, place, reactions
- ★ NCM turned nationalism from an elite into a mass movement; it was not withdrawn after Jallianwala Bagh
- ★ Gandhi stopped the NCM in 1922 because of violence at Chauri-Chaura (Gorakhpur, UP)
- ★ Sequence: police firing → police station set on fire → movement postponed → Gandhi arrested
- ★ Crowd protested the arrest of their leader while campaigning against liquor sale
- ★ Chauri-Chaura (4 Feb 1922) → Bardoli Resolutions (12 Feb 1922) → suspension of NCM
- ★ Gandhi was at Bardoli when Chauri-Chaura happened
- ★ Champaran (1917) → Amritsar case / Jallianwala (13 April 1919) → Moplah (1921) → Chauri-Chaura
- ★ Main outcome of NCM / result of Khilafat — Hindu-Muslim unity (differences reduced); Gandhi called Khilafat a golden opportunity for it
### NCM chronology — event → date
- ★ Moplah (Mappila) Rebellion, 1921 — Malabar, Kerala; anti-landlord and anti-British, linked to Khilafat; later communal
