- Founding: 1347 (14th century) by Hasan Gangu (Zafar Khan); capital Gulbarga (PYQ UPPSC 2005, 2016)
- Helper: Kapaya Nayaka helped Hasan Gangu found it
- Capital shift: Ahmad Shah I moved the capital from Gulbarga to Bidar (PYQ G2 2003)
### Bahmani sultans → incursions into Andhradesa
| Sultan / commander | Incursion |
| Muhammad Shah I | Burned Kapaya's son Vinayaka alive (1361); forced Kapaya to cede Golconda, 400 elephants and 250 horses (1364-65) |
| Firoz Shah (1397-1422) | Beaten at Kambhammetta (1396) by Gajarao Tippa; repulsed at Kandukuru (1398) by Machareddi; allied with Pedakomati against Rajamahendravaram (1417) |
| Muhammad III and Mahmud Gawan | Put down the Telangana revolts (from 1453); Gawan subdued Orugallu (Warangal); took Kondaveedu and pushed into Odisha (1471) |
- Peak extent: Kingdom largest under Gawan
- Gawan's execution: 1482 (some notes say 1481) → broke the kingdom apart
### Gajapati rulers → campaigns and conquests in Andhra
## 1. Dynasty
- Title meaning: Gajapati = "Lord of Elephants"
- Extent in Andhra: Whole of north coastal Andhra under Gajapati rule
## 2. Rulers → Andhra campaign
| Ruler | Andhra campaign |
| Kapilendra / Kapileswara (founder, r. 1434-1467 per CDI; APP dates his death to 1470) | Attacked Tiran Andhra (1448, Penugonda inscription); annexed Rajamahendravaram after Devaraya II's death, ending the Rajahmundry Reddis; took Kondaveedu (c. 1450), Bellamkonda, Velugodu, Addanki and Srisailam, and besieged Vijayanagara |
| Hamvira (Kapileswara's son) | Defeated the Bahmani army that attacked Devarakonda; 1471: Bahmanis briefly installed him as a puppet → overturned by Purushottama |
| Purushottama | Won the throne after a struggle; refused to help Muhammad III; recovered ground from the Bahmanis |
| Prataparudra | Lost Udayagiri, Kondaveedu and Kondapalli to Krishnadevaraya (1513-1519) |
## 3. Places → conquest
| Place | Gajapati conquest |
| Rajamahendravaram | Taken by Kapileswara after 1446; Reddis had been helped by Vijayanagara commander Mallappa Odayar → recorded in Draksharama inscription (1444) |
| Kondaveedu | Rajamahendra, with Velama support, occupied Varam → marched on Kondaveedu; under Vijayanagara until 1449 → fell to Gajapatis c. 1450 |
| Udayagiri (Nellore) | Major Gajapati stronghold in southern Andhra |
### Gajapati administration → officer titles
- Southern revenue officers: In the Gajapati southern (Andhra) regions = Naidu (PYQ GS 2025)

- Exam angle: the title for southern revenue officers is Naidu.
### Gajapati-Vijayanagara conflict → battles, marriage, peace terms
## 1. Saluva Narasimha

## 2. Krishnadevaraya's campaigns (1513-1519)
- Duration: 1513 to 1519
- Sequence:
- Udayagiri fell after an 18-month siege

## 3. Wars and peace with Prataparudra Gajapati - terms

- Peace terms:

- Also gave the Balakrishna idol
- Date of settlement: Use c. 1518-19
- ⚠ Trap: "Treaty of Udayagiri (1480s)" (some notes) clashes with Krishnadevaraya's reign (1509-29)

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:100
### Bahmani kingdom
- ★ Bahmani kingdom founded 1347 by Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah — Hasan Gangu / Zafar Khan
- capital Gulbarga, named Hasanabad
- four regions: Gulbarga, Daulatabad, Berar, Bidar
- ★ Capital shifted from Gulbarga to Bidar — Ahmad Shah I
- Muhammad Shah I fought Kapaya Nayaka
- Muhammad III ruled with Mahmud Gawan as minister
### Bahmani incursions
- Muhammad Shah I burned Kapaya's son Vinayaka alive in 1361
- Mahmud Gawan (with Muhammad III) — suppressed Telangana revolts from 1453, subdued Orugallu, took Kondaveedu
- executed 1481 or 1482 (dates differ)
- Machareddi stopped Firoz Shah at Kandukuru (1398)
- Gajarao Tippa beat Firoz Shah at Kambhammetta (1396)
### Gajapati rulers
- Gajapati rule — c. 1434–1541
- Order of Andhra campaigns: Kapilendra → Purushottama → Prataparudra
- Kapilendra — attacked Tiran Andhra in 1448; captured Kondaveedu c. 1450
- Kondaveedu under Vijayanagara till 1449, fell to the Gajapatis c. 1450
- Hamvira, son of Kapileswara Gajapati — defeated the Bahmani army attacking Devarakonda
- ★ Prataparudra Gajapati — defeated by Krishnadevaraya; his daughter Annapurna Devi married Krishnadevaraya
- lost Udayagiri, Kondaveedu and Kondapalli to Krishnadevaraya (1513–1519)
### Krishnadevaraya's campaign (1513-1519)
- Campaign began with Udayagiri → Kondaveedu → Kondapalli → Bejawada → march to Cuttack (1518)
### Peace and administration
- Naidus — revenue officers in southern Andhra under the Gajapatis
- Nayakas and samantas — collected revenue, kept order; Reddi-era social order kept
- Peace terms (Krishnadevaraya–Prataparudra): marriage alliance, Balakrishna idol, lands north of the Krishna returned to the Gajapati
- Cuttack / the Gajapati heartland not taken over
- Madala Panji — temple chronicle of Jagannath at Puri; source for Gajapati history
