### GDP and GNP — meaning and difference
## 1. GDP
- GDP: The value of final goods and services produced within domestic territory
- Gross: Before deducting depreciation
- Domestic production: Within the country irrespective of nationality
- GDP definition: GDP = market value of all final goods and services produced within the geographical boundaries of a country during a year, whoever produces them (GS 2025 key)
- GDP formula: GDP = Q x P; counts production by both residents and non-residents inside the territory
## 2. GNP
- Gross National Product (GNP): The total monetary value of final goods and services produced by the normal residents of a country during a given period
- Includes production occurring inside and outside the country's boundaries
- ⚠ GDP is not equal to GNP — GNP considers nationality or factor income
## 3. GDP vs GNP
| Basis | GDP | GNP |
| Based on | The domestic territory of a country | The normal residents or nationals of a country |
| Measures | Production within the country | Production by residents within the country and outside the country |
| Nature | Location-based | Residence-based |
## 4. Stems → term
| Stem | Term |
| Value of all final goods and services produced within the country in a year | GDP |
| Final output of the country's nationals/normal residents, wherever located | GNP |
| Wages, rent, interest, profit earned by the factors of production | Factor income |
### Terms → meaning (national income, SDP and other terms)
## 1. National income
- Definition: NNP at factor cost = National Income
- Total factor income (wages, rent, interest, profits) of a country's normal residents in a year
- = Net domestic factor income + NFIA
- Flow vs stock: National income = flow (per year); national wealth = stock
## 2. State Domestic Product
- State Domestic Product: Aggregate economic value of all goods and services produced within the State's boundaries, counted without duplication, during a specified period, usually a year (APSES wording; APPSC 2025; APSES 2024-25; GS 2025)
## 3. Other terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Depreciation (consumption of fixed capital) | Deducted because output that only replaces worn-out capital adds nothing new |
| NFIA (Net Factor Income from Abroad) | Factor income received from abroad - factor income paid abroad |
| Production taxes | Levied irrespective of output (e.g. land revenue, stamp duty, property tax) |
| Product taxes | Levied per unit of product (e.g. excise, sales tax/GST) |
| Transfer earnings | Not payments for factor services → not included in national income |
- Transfer earnings: APPSC-G2 2025 A-R: both true, R explains A
- Comparative note: GDP = territorial; GNP = nationality/residence
### Aggregates → formula
| Aggregate | Formula |
| GDP (MP) | Value of final output within domestic territory |
| NDP | GDP minus (subtracting) depreciation |
| GNP | GDP plus (adding) Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA); also NNP plus depreciation |
| NNP — Net National Product | NDP plus NFIA; also GNP minus depreciation |
| NNP at market price | GNP at market price minus depreciation |
| NNP (FC) | NNP (MP) - net indirect taxes |
| National Income | NNP at factor cost = GNP at market price minus depreciation minus indirect taxes plus subsidies |
| Domestic to National | GNP = GDP + NFIA (NFIA = factor income received from abroad - factor income paid abroad) |
| Gross to Net | NDP = GDP - Depreciation; NNP = GNP - Depreciation |
| Market price to Factor cost | FC = MP - Net Indirect Taxes (indirect taxes - subsidies) |
| National Income | NNP at FC = NDP at FC + NFIA |
- NFIA form: GNP = GDP + X - M (X = income of nationals abroad; M = income of foreigners in India)
### Aggregates → formula — Market price vs factor cost
## 1. Market price vs factor cost
- Difference: GDP at MP vs FC differ by indirect taxes and subsidies - not direct taxes (APPSC-G2 2025)
- Market price = factor cost + indirect taxes - subsidies
- GDPfc = GDPmp - Indirect taxes + Subsidies = GDP (FC) = GDP (MP) - indirect taxes + subsidies
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes print "- subsidies": sign is wrong - subsidies are added back
## 2. Three price levels
- GVA at factor cost + net production taxes = GVA at basic prices
- GVA at basic prices + net product taxes = GVA at market prices (GDP)
- GVA vs NVA: GVA includes depreciation, NVA excludes it
### Aggregates → formula — Personal, disposable and per capita income
## 1. Personal, disposable and per capita income
- Personal Income: Personal Income = NI - undistributed corporate profits - social security contributions - corporate tax + government and business transfer payments + net interest paid by government.
- Personal Disposable Income: Personal Disposable Income = Personal Income - personal direct taxes - non-tax payments (fines, fees) = what individuals can spend or save
- National savings: National disposable income - consumption = national savings
- Per Capita Income: Per Capita Income = National Income / Population (APPSC 2025; GS 2025 key)
- PCI rises only when NI grows faster than population
### GNP vs GDP — rule and worked examples
## 1. When one exceeds the other
- Rule: GNP > GDP when NFIA is positive (residents earn more abroad than foreigners earn at home); GNP < GDP when NFIA is negative
- Indians abroad: Remittance-type factor income earned (e.g. Indians earning in Saudi Arabia) is excluded from GDP but included in GNP
- India: NFIA usually negative → GNP lower than GDP
## 2. Worked examples
- Example 1: GDPmp 500, depreciation 50, NFIA -10, NIT 40
- GNPmp = 490; NNPmp = 440; NNPfc (NI) = 400; NDPfc = 410
- Example 2: GDP (MP) Rs 110 crore
- Indians earn Rs 20 crore abroad; foreigners earn Rs 10 crore in India → NFIA = +10 → GNP = 120
- Depreciation 15 → NNP (MP) = 105
- Net indirect taxes 5 → NI = NNP (FC) = 100
- Exam angle: numerical questions give GDP, NFIA, depreciation and net indirect taxes and ask for NI.
### Included vs excluded in national income
| Included | Excluded |
| Wages and salaries, rent, profits, dividends, interest on productive loans | Transfer payments - pensions, scholarships, unemployment allowance, prizes (no productive service rendered in return); added back when moving from NI to personal income |
| Value added at each stage | Intermediate goods (double counting) |
| Commission on resale [GK] | Second-hand sales (already counted when produced) [GK] |
| - | Interest on public debt, tax revenue (transfers, not factor payments) |
- ⚠ Transfer earnings are not included in national income - not payments for factor services
### Nominal vs real GDP and the GDP deflator
## 1. Nominal vs real
- Nominal GDP: Measured at current-year prices (includes price change)
- Real GDP: Measured at constant or base-year prices — base-year prices are used to calculate it; real GDP growth is a better indicator of actual output growth
- Base year now 2022-23 (earlier 2011-12)
- Example: nominal GDP 1,000 → 1,200 while price index 100 → 120 → real GDP unchanged
- Growth gap: Nominal growth exceeds real by roughly the inflation rate
- 2025-26: nominal 8.0% vs real 7.4% (estimates, old series)
## 2. GDP deflator
- Formula: GDP deflator = Nominal GDP (current prices) / Real GDP (constant prices) x 100 = GDP at current prices / GDP at constant prices x 100 (APPSC-G2 2025)
- Comprehensiveness: Most comprehensive measure of inflation (G2 2025 key)
- Covers all goods and services in GDP (consumption, investment, government, exports), not a fixed basket like CPI/WPI
- Purchasing power: Rising deflator = higher price level → purchasing power of money falls / lower purchasing power of money
- GNP deflator: Same ratio for GNP
- Double deflation (2022-23 series): Deflates both output and input prices for manufacturing and agriculture, replacing single deflation
- Sectoral deflators (2025-26): Agricultural GDP deflator grew fastest → services → industry → manufacturing
- Hence manufacturing's nominal share fell while real share stayed about 17-18%
### GVA — meaning, headline measure, PPP and India's rank
## 1. GVA
- Gross Value Added (GVA): Measures the value added by producers and by sectors to the economy — a production-side measure
- GDP: A broader measure of domestic production after accounting for net taxes on products
- Real GVA: Calculated at constant prices using a base year — the base year removes the effect of price changes
- Nominal GVA: Calculated at current prices
## 2. Headline measure
- GVA at basic prices: Headline sectoral measure since the 2011-12 series; still used in the 2022-23 series
- CSO/NSO headline growth is on GVA at basic prices
- Q1 2026-27: Real GDP Rs 81.36 lakh crore vs real GVA Rs 73.82 lakh crore (difference = net product taxes) (Sep 2026)
- Current: CEA projected a rise in household disposable income in 2025; GDP growth 7.8% in Q1 FY26
## 3. PPP exchange rates
- PPP: Compares GDP by pricing a common basket at each country's own prices → removes price-level differences
- Estimation: Under the International Comparison Program (ICP) of the UNSD
- World Bank = global implementing agency
- NSO = India's nodal agency
- Reason: Lower prices in India → PPP GDP much larger than market-rate GDP
## 4. India's rank
| Measure | India's rank |
| Nominal (market exchange rate) GDP | 4th (overtook Japan in 2025, about USD 4.18 trillion) |
| PPP GDP | 3rd, after USA and China (ICP 2021) |
- ⚠ Sources differ: IMF WEO April 2026 puts India 6th (USD 4.15 trillion); 4th is the 2025 position
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: New GDP series (base 2022-23) released by NSO on 27 Feb 2026
- Brought in double deflation and Supply-Use Tables
- GDP at current prices: Rs 261.18 / 289.84 / 318.07 lakh crore for 2022-23 / 2023-24 / 2024-25

### From GDP (MP) to National Income
- GNP = NNP + depreciation
- National income = NNP at factor cost
### Key links
## Per capita income
- ★ Per capita income — average income of a country = national income ÷ population
- Per capita income rises only when income grows faster than population
- AP per capita income computed on Net State Domestic Product (NSDP)
- ★ Sign of economic growth — sustained increase in real per capita income
## Deflator and GVA
- GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100, i.e. GDP at current prices / GDP at constant prices × 100
- Rising GNP/GDP deflator — higher prices, decrease in purchasing power of money
- GVA — production-side measure; GDP = GVA + taxes on products − subsidies (wider measure)
### Terms
## NFIA, GDP and GNP
- NFIA = factor income received from abroad − factor income paid abroad; added to GDP to get GNP
- Indian resident's factor income earned in Saudi Arabia — counts in India's GNP only
- India's GNP usually lower than GDP — NFIA is negative
- GDP — place-based: all production inside the country's borders; does NOT include NFIA
- Residents' production inside and outside the country — GNP (nationality-based)
## Other terms
- Production tax (charged whatever the output) — stamp duty, property tax; product taxes — GST, excise, sales tax
- Personal disposable income = personal income − personal direct taxes − non-tax payments (fines, fees)
- PPP under International Comparison Program — NSO is India's nodal agency; World Bank the global implementing agency
### Included vs excluded
- National income at factor cost — wages, rent, interest and profits of the country's normal residents
- Commission on resale — payment for productive service, included; NOT a transfer payment
- Transfer payments: pensions, scholarships, unemployment allowances
- Value added at each stage — included; intermediate goods excluded to avoid double counting
- Excluded: second-hand sales, interest on public debt
### Worked example
## Concepts
- National wealth — a stock (point of time); national income — a flow (over a period)
- National income = NNP at factor cost; NDP = GDP − depreciation; NNP = GNP − depreciation
- GDP at FC = GDP at MP − indirect taxes + subsidies (not direct taxes)
## Worked examples
- GDP(MP) ₹500 cr, depreciation ₹50 cr, NFIA −₹10 cr, net indirect taxes ₹40 cr → GNP(MP) ₹490 cr → NNP(MP) ₹440 cr → national income ₹400 crore
- Nominal GDP ₹1,000 → ₹1,200 cr with price index 100 → 120 — real GDP unchanged
## Personal income and AP
- Personal income = national income + government and business transfer payments; deducted: undistributed profits, social security contributions, corporate tax
- AP per capita income 2024-25 (FAE) — Rs. 2,68,653; India Rs. 2,00,162
### Real GDP, deflator, PPP
- GDP — value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a year, whoever produces them
- Depreciation removed — output that only replaces worn-out capital is not new output
- Real GDP — at constant (base-year) prices; nominal GDP at current prices
- Real GVA — base year used to remove the effect of price changes
- GDP deflator — most comprehensive measure of inflation (covers all goods and services in GDP; WPI, CPI, CPI-IW price fixed baskets)
- 2022-23 series: GVA at basic prices still used; double deflation replaced single deflation for manufacturing and agriculture (deflates output and input prices separately)
- India's PPP GDP much larger than market-exchange-rate GDP — prices are lower in India
### Exam corner
- Transfer earnings — not payments for factor services; excluded from national income
- Rent, productive interest, profit — factor incomes
### GDP and GNP — meaning and difference
- ★ Real GDP — value of production at prices of a single (base) year
- ★ National income — money value of final goods and services produced (no intermediate goods)
- ★ Prices and money income both doubled — real income unchanged
### Terms → meaning (national income, SDP and other terms)
- ★ Income method GDP = domestic factor incomes − transfer payments
- ★ Main difference between GDP and GNP — net factor income from abroad
- ★ National Income = Net National Product at factor cost
- ★ NDP = GDP − depreciation (not plus)
### Aggregates → formula
- ★ GNP — Gross National Product
- ★ GDP at MP = GNP at MP − net income from abroad
- ★ National Income = GNP at MP − depreciation − indirect taxes + subsidies
- ★ NI = NNP at MP − (indirect taxes − subsidies)
- ★ NNP at MP = GNP at MP − depreciation
### Aggregates → formula — Market price vs factor cost
- ★ Difference between GDP at FC and GDP at MP — indirect taxes and subsidies
- GDP at MP = GDP at FC + indirect taxes − subsidies
### Nominal vs real GDP and the GDP deflator
- ★ GNP deflator — ratio of nominal GNP to real GNP, as a percentage
- ★ Rise in GNP deflator — decreases purchasing power of money (rising prices)
- ★ Per capita income grows faster at current prices than at constant prices — constant prices allow for increase in price level
- ★ Hindu growth rate — related to GDP
### GVA — meaning, headline measure, PPP and India's rank
- ★ Most appropriate measure of economic growth — per capita real income (per capita product / per capita NNP)
- ★ Not considered in economic growth — growth in financial aid from World Bank; considered: GDP, GNP, per capita GNP
