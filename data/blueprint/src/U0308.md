### Budget basics
- The word "budget" is not used in the Constitution - it is the Annual Financial Statement (Art. 112)
- Annual Financial Statement = Art. 112 (APPSC-GS 2018, 2023)
- Financial year 1 April - 31 March; laid before both Houses
- Prepared by Budget Division, DEA; starts with Budget Circular
- First Union budget: 26 Nov 1947, R.K. Shanmukham Chetty
- "bougette" = French little bag; Halwa ceremony = printing
### Budget Articles in order
- 1. 112: AFS; 112(3) charged items ↓
- 2. 113: Demands for Grants ↓
- 3. 114: Appropriation Bill (no withdrawal from CFI without law) ↓
- 4. 115: supplementary, additional, excess ↓
- 5. 116: vote on account, credit, exceptional ↓
- 6. 117: Financial Bills

### Budget → Article 112, terms and FIRSTs
## 1. Constitutional basis
- Annual Financial Statement (Article 112): The constitutional name for the Union Budget
- Article 112: Annual Financial Statement (Union Budget) — 112(3) lists the charged items
- Article 113: Procedure for estimates — Demands for Grants
- Budget = Annual Financial Statement: Under Article 112 (APPSC-GS 2018, 2023)
- Estimated receipts and expenditure for financial year 1 April - 31 March
- ⚠ Trap: The word "budget" is not used in the Constitution
- Laying: President causes it to be laid before both Houses
- Presentation: In Lok Sabha by the Finance Minister (Union Budget 2023-24: Nirmala Sitharaman - APPSC-GS 2023)
- Structure: Covers three funds (Consolidated, Contingency, Public Account); two parts → Revenue Budget + Capital Budget
## 2. Terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Budget Circular | Starts the process (September) |
| Halwa ceremony | Marks printing (North Block) |
## 3. Preparation and FIRST
- Prepared by: Budget Division, Department of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Finance
- First Union budget: 26 Nov 1947 (APPSC-GS 2018), by R.K. Shanmukham Chetty
### Funds → Articles 266-267
## 1. Consolidated Fund vs Public Account vs Contingency Fund
| Basis | Consolidated Fund of India | Public Account of India | Contingency Fund of India |
| Article | 266(1) | 266(2) | 267(1) |
| States | Art. 266(1) | - | Art. 267(2) |
| Contents | All revenues (taxes, duties, non-tax revenue), all loans (treasury bills, market loans, foreign loans) and repayments of loans; T-bills, internal/external loans | Money held in trust or custody — provident funds, judicial deposits, savings bank deposits; also small savings, deposits, remittances | An imprest at the disposal of the President for unforeseen expenses; unforeseen expenditure pending approval; Contingency Fund of India Act, 1950 |
| Controlled by | Parliament | Executive (Government) | President — administered by the Finance Secretary on his behalf (held by the Finance Secretary (DEA)) |
| Nature | Dynamic main treasury account | Operational banking account — operates as a banker (govt acts as banker/trustee) | Fixed statutory corpus |
| Withdrawal | Only under an Appropriation Act (Article 114) passed by Parliament; withdrawal only by appropriation law (Art. 114(3)) | Executive disbursements — no prior parliamentary voting; no appropriation needed | Advances — no prior voting; ex-post facto approval later; advances by executive, recouped later [GK] |
- ⚠ Cannot authorise withdrawals from the Consolidated Fund on their own authority: the President, Prime Minister, Finance Minister, CAG
- Contingency Fund corpus:
- ₹30,000 crore from FY 2021-22
- Public Account: Art. 266(2)
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes put it under 266(1)
- State Contingency Fund: Established by the state legislature by law (Art. 267(2)); at the Governor's disposal
- Audit: CAG audits all three funds of Union and states
### Cut motions → terms
## 1. Cut Motions — on a demand for grant
| Cut Motion | Reduction | Purpose |
| Policy Cut | Reduce to ₹1 (Re 1) | Complete disapproval of the underlying policy (disapproval of the policy behind the demand [GK]) |
| Economy Cut | By a specified amount | Administrative economy — demands economy in expenditure [GK] |
| Token Cut | By ₹100 (Rs 100) | Ventilate a specific grievance within government responsibility [GK] |
- Passing any Cut Motion in the Lok Sabha signals loss of financial control — the Government must resign
- A passed cut motion signals loss of confidence
- House: Moved only in Lok Sabha during voting on demands
- ⚠ Re 1 = policy cut; Rs 100 = token cut
### Articles → Appropriation Bill and charged expenditure (114)
## 1. Budget Articles 112 to 116
- Article 114: Appropriation Bills — authority for withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund (114(3): every withdrawal needs an Appropriation Act)
- Article 115: Supplementary, additional and excess grants
- Article 116: Vote on Account, Votes of Credit and Exceptional Grants
## 2. Appropriation Bill - Art. 114
- Appropriation Bill (Article 114): Authorises withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund of India — for voted grants and charged expenditure
- Charged Expenditure: Non-votable — paid directly out of the Consolidated Fund of India; can only be discussed
- Coverage: Grants voted by Lok Sabha + charged expenditure, not exceeding the amount in the AFS
- Art. 114(3): No money withdrawn from the CFI except under appropriation made by law
- Amendment bar: No amendment may vary the amount or alter the destination of a voted grant, or vary charged expenditure
- Repeal: Carries an automatic repeal clause
### Charged expenditure → meaning and items on the Consolidated Fund
## 1. Charged expenditure
- Non-votable: Not submitted to the vote of Parliament (or a State Legislature) — but can be discussed by both Houses
- Purpose: Guarantees the financial independence of constitutional offices
- Three separate questions: The authority that determines the salary (Parliament or a State Legislature, by law) is distinct from the fund that pays it, which is distinct from whether it is charged or voted
- Schedules: Second Schedule — salaries (emoluments, allowances and privileges) of the President, Governors, Presiding Officers, Supreme Court and High Court Judges, CAG; Third Schedule — forms of oaths
## 2. Charged on the Consolidated Fund of India
- President: Emoluments, allowances and office expenses
- Chairman and Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha; Speaker and Deputy Speaker of the Lok Sabha — salaries and allowances
- Supreme Court judges — salaries, allowances and pensions
- High Court judges — only their pensions — Article 112(3)(d)(iii)
- CAG — salary, allowances, pension and administrative expenses
- Debt charges of the Government of India — interest and sinking fund charges
- Any sum to satisfy a judgment, decree or arbitral award against the Government of India

## 2. Special cases
- Vice-President: No salary as Vice-President — no separate fund and not charged as VP; draws salary solely in the capacity of Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (charged on the CFI)
- One individual appointed Governor of two or more States: Emoluments and allowances payable allocated among the States in the proportion the President fixes by order (Article 158)
- Article 125: Salaries, allowances and rights of Supreme Court Judges; Article 221: High Court Judges
### Grants → Art. 115 and 116
## 1. Vote on Account and Vote of Credit
- Vote on Account (Article 116): An advance grant by the Lok Sabha for estimated expenditure for part of the financial year, pending passage of the budget
- Vote of Credit: An unexpected, blank-check grant — for an emergency or indefinite demand whose details cannot be stated in a budget
## 2. Grants - Art. 115 and 116
| Grant | Article | Purpose |
| Vote on account | 116 | Advance for part of the year (about two months, one-sixth) till Appropriation Act; 3-5 months in election years |
| Vote of credit | 116 | Unexpected demand of indefinite magnitude/character [GK] |
| Exceptional grant | 116 | Not part of the current service of any year [GK] |
| Supplementary grant | 115 | Sanctioned amount insufficient |
| Additional grant | 115 | New service not in the budget [GK] |
| Excess grant | 115 | Spent more than granted; needs PAC approval first [GK] |
- Vote on account: Since the 1 Feb budget (2017), usually needed only in interim-budget years
- Token grant (re-appropriation): Must also be voted by LS
- ⚠ 115 = supplementary, additional, excess; 116 = vote on account, vote of credit, exceptional

## Appropriation
- President cannot withdraw alone; Article 114 needs an Appropriation Act

- No amendment may change a voted grant or charged expenditure; Appropriation Bill has an automatic repeal clause
## Grants
- Cut motions — only in the Lok Sabha, during voting on Demands for Grants; if passed, show loss of confidence
- Vote on account — since the 1 February Budget (2017), usually needed only in interim-Budget years
- Excess grant — spending crossed the grant; needs prior approval of the PAC
- Token grant — linked to re-appropriation; must be voted by the Lok Sabha

### Funds → Articles 266-267
## Consolidated Fund
- ★ All tax revenues and other receipts — go to the Consolidated Fund of India (Article 266)
- ★ CFI also gets loans via treasury bills, ways and means advances, and loan repayments
- ★ Withdrawal from the CFI — allowed by the Parliament of India (Article 114, Appropriation Bill)
- ★ Right to issue money from the CFI (UPPCS 2000 key) — "none of the above"
- ★ Parliament's control over finance — placing the Annual Financial Statement; withdrawal only after the Appropriation Bill; supplementary grant rules
## Vote on Account
- ★ Needed when the regular Budget is not expected to be passed in time (e.g. elections near)
- ★ Allows withdrawal from the CFI before the Budget (Article 116)
## Contingency Fund
- ★ Article 267 — Contingency Fund, an imprest for the President's use, set up by Parliament by law
- ★ For unforeseen expenditure; President spends before authorisation of Parliament
- ★ State Contingency Fund — set up by State Legislature; run by the Governor
### Cut motions → terms
- ★ Cut motion — proposal to reduce expenditure (demands for grants) in the Budget; moved in the Lok Sabha
- ★ Aim — draw the House's attention to the matter named

- ★ Appropriation Bill — has an automatic repeal clause; allows withdrawals from the CFI
- ★ Charged expenditure (e.g. salaries of constitutional authorities) — cannot be voted but can be discussed
