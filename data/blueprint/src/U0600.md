### Weathering processes
| Process | What happens |
| Physical | Frost wedging, exfoliation, salt growth |
| Carbonation | Carbonic acid dissolves limestone |
| Hydration | Absorbs water, swells: anhydrite → gypsum |
| Hydrolysis | Feldspar → clay; muscovite → vermiculite (APPSC 2024) |
| Oxidation | Iron rusts: red, yellow soils |
| Biological | Roots, lichens, micro-organisms |
### Weathering notes
- Fastest in hot, wet climates
- Landforms: exfoliation domes, tors, block disintegration
- Agents: water most powerful, then wind, ice, gravity

- Mass movement under gravity = landslide (APPSC 2019)

### Denudation and weathering — process → what happens; weathering landforms
## 1. Denudation
- Sequence: Denudation = weathering → mass wasting/erosion → transportation → deposition
- Agents: Water (most powerful), wind, ice, gravity
## 2. Weathering process → what happens
| Process | What happens / example |
| Physical (mechanical) | Rock breaks without chemical change - frost wedging, heating and cooling (exfoliation), salt crystal growth [GK] |
| Solution | Soluble minerals (rock salt, gypsum) dissolve [GK] |
| Carbonation | CO2 in rain forms carbonic acid, which dissolves limestone; carbon stored as bicarbonate (LENS Jul 2025) |
| Hydration | Minerals absorb water and swell, e.g. anhydrite to gypsum [GK] |
| Hydrolysis | Water reacts with silicate minerals - feldspar to clay; muscovite's potassium replaced by hydronium to form vermiculite (APPSC 2024) |
| Oxidation | Iron minerals rust - red, yellow soils [GK] |
| Biological | Roots, burrowing animals, lichens and micro-organisms break and decay rock [GK] |
- Fastest: In hot, wet climates
- Freeze-thaw: Breaks rocks in the high Himalaya
- ⚠ Hydration = swelling (anhydrite → gypsum); hydrolysis = reaction (feldspar → clay, muscovite → vermiculite)
## 3. Weathering landforms → meaning
| Landform | Meaning |
| Exfoliation domes | Onion-like peeling of granite |
### Mass wasting — type → nature; conditions and regions
- Definition: Downslope movement of earth material under gravity = landslide (APPSC 2019)
| Type | Nature |
| Rockfall | Free fall of rocks from steep cliffs |
| Slide | Coherent mass slips along a surface |
| Slump | Rotational slip along a curved surface in weak, saturated soils |
| Debris flow / mudflow | Fast water-soil-rock mix, after heavy rain or snowmelt |
| Creep | Very slow movement by wetting-drying and heating-cooling |
- Conditions: Slopes above 30 degrees, weak/fractured rock, bare slopes
- Triggers: Rainfall (SW monsoon) main trigger in India → earthquakes next
- Most at risk: Himalaya and North-East
- ⚠ Slump = rotational, curved surface; creep = very slow
### Geomorphologists → cycle of erosion; peneplain, monadnock, inselberg
## 1. Proponent → theory
| Person | Contribution |
| W.M. Davis (1899) | Landscape = structure + process + stage; stages youth → maturity → old age; end = low, almost flat peneplain + resistant residual hills = monadnocks |
| W. Penck (Walther Penck) | Landform depends on ratio of uplift to erosion, not time-stages; uplift and erosion act together; slopes retreat parallel |
- ⚠ Davis = time-stages; Penck = uplift vs erosion, parallel slope retreat
## 2. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| peneplain | Low, almost flat end-surface of the Davis cycle |
| monadnocks | Resistant residual hills on a peneplain |
| Inselbergs | Residual island-hills of arid/semi-arid regions; formed by desert erosion (wind + sheet-wash) |
- Indian example: Peninsular plateau = old peneplain, rejuvenated in places
### Soil-forming factors — active vs passive; AP soil formation
## 1. Active vs passive factors
| Active factors | Passive factors |
| Climate, biological activity (vegetation, micro-organisms) | Parent material, topography (relief), time |
- APPSC G1 2024: Active factors = climate and biological activity (III and IV only)
## 2. AP soil formation factors - parent rock, climate, relief, organisms, time
- Factors (APSES 2024-25 line): Climate, altitude and bedrock composition; uneven rainfall + excessive heat give AP soils their character
- Composition: Mainly a mix of sand, silt and clay
### Soil texture data and soils → climate region; in-situ vs transported
## 1. Soil texture - particle size and water holding
| Particle | Size |
| Sand | 2-0.05 mm |
| Silt | 0.05-0.002 mm |
| Clay | Below 0.002 mm |
- Water-holding capacity: Clay > silt > sand
- Clay and silt get saturated (landslide-prone); sand drains well
- Clay binds organic carbon; sandy soils lose it by leaching (Nov 2025)
- Rainforest soils: Poor despite lush forest - heavy rain leaches nutrients + dead matter decays fast
## 2. Zonal soils → climate / vegetation
| Soil | Climate / vegetation |
| Podzol | Cold taiga (boreal conifer forests); thin, acidic, leached, humus-poor |
| Laterite | Hot, heavy-rain tropics; intense leaching of silica and lime leaves iron and aluminium oxides; acidic |
| Terra rossa | Mediterranean, over limestone; red [GK] |
| Chernozem | Temperate grasslands (steppes, prairies); black, humus-rich [GK] |
| Oxisols | Equatorial-tropical land surfaces stable for very long periods (APPSC 2019) |
- ⚠ Podzol = cold taiga; Chernozem = temperate grassland; Laterite = hot heavy-rain tropics
## 3. In-situ vs transported
| Type | Formation / region |
| Residual / in-situ (sedentary) | Form where the rock weathered; mainly Peninsular India; APPSC 2018: in-situ = sedentary soil |
| Transported / drifted (ex-situ) | Moved by rivers, wind, ice; mainly North India; alluvium covers 45.6% of India |

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:11
### Denudation
- Denudation order: weathering (first stage) → mass wasting / erosion → transportation → deposition
### Weathering processes
| Process | Nature | Example |
| Hydrolysis | chemical | clay from feldspar; muscovite → vermiculite (potassium replaced by hydronium) |
| Oxidation | chemical | rusting of iron-bearing minerals; may give red or yellow soils |
| Carbonation | CO2 in rainwater forms carbonic acid | dissolves limestone; stores carbon as bicarbonate |
| Hydration | absorption of water by minerals, which swell | anhydrite → gypsum |
### Weathering notes
- Weathering is fastest in hot and wet climates
- Exfoliation domes — onion-like peeling of granite (from pressure or temperature)
### Mass wasting
- Slump — turning slip along a curved surface in weak, wet soil
- Creep — very slow downhill movement from repeated wetting-drying or heating-cooling
- Rockfall — free fall of rocks from cliffs
- Slide — whole mass slipping along a surface
- Debris flow — fast water-soil-rock mix
### Landslide conditions
- India: main trigger of mass wasting is rainfall (southwest monsoon), next earthquakes
- Most vulnerable regions: Himalaya and North-East
### Cycle of erosion
- Davis's cycle ends in a near-flat peneplain with leftover monadnocks; stressed structure, process and time
- Walther Penck — stressed uplift and erosion acting simultaneously
- Peninsular plateau of India — old peneplain, rejuvenated in places
- Inselbergs — leftover island-hills of arid and semi-arid regions (wind and sheet-wash erosion)
### Zonal soils
- ★ Loam — contains all types of particles (about 40% sand, 18% clay, 42% silt)
- Podzol — cold taiga / boreal conifer forests; thin, acidic, washed-out, humus-poor
- Terra rossa — Mediterranean regions over limestone
- Chernozem — temperate grasslands; laterite — hot wet tropics; oxisols — stable equatorial surfaces
### Soil formation
## Particles
| Particle | Diameter |
| ★ Clay | less than 0.002 mm |
| Silt | 0.002–0.05 mm |
| Fine sand | 0.05–2 mm |
- Water-holding capacity: clay highest > silt > sand (drains easily)
## Factors
- Five classical factors: parent material, climate, relief/topography, organisms, time — latitude is not one
- Active factors: climate and biological activity; passive: parent material and topography
- AP: bedrock composition does influence soil formation, with climate and altitude
## Types and shares
- In-situ (residual) soils = sedentary soils (APPSC 2018)
- Transported alluvial soil (mainly rivers, North India) covers about 45.6% of India
- AP cultivated land: red soil about 60%; black soil about one-fourth; laterite about 1%
- Strongest cause of soil erosion: water (sheet, rill, gully); wind leads only in areas like the Thar
### Exam corner
- Rainforest soils poor in nutrients: heavy rain leaches nutrients and dead matter decomposes rapidly
### Soil-forming factors — active vs passive; AP soil formation
- ★ Active factors: climate and biological activity (drive weathering and organic processes); parent material, topography, time are the others
- ★ Rainwater holds carbon dioxide in solution and atmospheric oxygen — both help chemical weathering
- Higher temperature and more rainfall speed up chemical weathering
### Soil texture data and soils → climate region; in-situ vs transported
- ★ Clay holds maximum water (smaller particles)
- ★ Clay pore spaces are small, not large
