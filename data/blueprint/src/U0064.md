### Revenue terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Bhaga / Bhag | The share of agricultural produce paid as tax — under the Guptas the cultivator paid one-sixth of production → main land revenue |
| Bali | An important source of revenue in ancient India → at first voluntary, later compulsory (APP: "oppressive") |
| Sita | Revenue from crown or state land |
| Hiranya | A cash tax under the Mauryans — a cash payment instead of payment in produce |
| Udrang / Udranga | Land revenue under the Guptas |
| Udakabhog (Udakabhaga) | An irrigation tax under the Guptas (water tax [GK]) |
| Uparnika | A tax on farmers living on border land under the Guptas |
| Vishti | Forced labour under the Guptas — treated as a source of state income or tax; associated with Junagadh inscriptions and Gupta copper inscriptions → unpaid, for the army and officials, or for state or donee (Telugu vetti) |
| Irai and Vari | General Chola terms for tax or revenue |
| Bhoga | Periodic supplies to the king / supplies in kind (fruits, firewood, flowers) |
| Kara | Periodic tax |
| Uparikara | Additional tax; new tax, probably on temporary tenants |
| Halivakara | Plough tax |
| Sulka / Shulka | Customs duty / tolls |
| Klipta / Upaklipta | Taxes on land sale and purchase |
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say Udranga was an additional (new) tax, possibly a water or police tax
- Gupta tax-free land grants: Agrarian land given to Brahmins and religious functionaries
- Prescribed share = 1/6: Law books prescribe one-sixth
- Trend: Land taxes rose, trade taxes fell
- ⚠ Bhaga = share of produce; Bhoga = supplies in kind; Hiranya = cash; Halivakara = plough

### Land grants → grantee, rights, tenures and effects
## 1. Grant → grantee → rights
| Grant | Grantee | Rights |
| Brahmadeya | Brahmanas, individually or collectively (singly or jointly) | Tax exemption + administrative rights |
| Agrahara | Brahmanas (religious functionaries) - tax-free land / village | Tax-free, hereditary, often with administrative rights |
| Devagrahara / Devadana | Institutions - temples and monasteries | Revenue for worship |
| Secular grants | Officials, feudatories | Revenue in lieu of salary |
- ⚠ Term clash: One set of notes (Block01) reverses the terms ("Agrahara for Brahmanas, Brahmadeya for religious institutions"); the glossary above is the usual meaning
- MPPSC 2014: tax-free land went to Brahmins → brahmadeya usually = grant to Brahmanas
## 3. Rights and effects
- Rights transferred: Revenue plus administrative, judicial and police rights; grantees could collect taxes from peasants
- Royal officials barred from entering → led to sub-infeudation
- Tribal frontiers: Grants in tribal and forest areas brought virgin land under cultivation → absorbed tribes into the caste order as new sub-castes (APPSC-GS 2023 key)
- Peasantry: Reduced to (sank to) semi-servile status
- Tenant farming, sharecropping, tenants-at-will, forced labour (vishti), many taxes
## 4. FIRST
- Earliest land grants in India: The Satavahanas
- 100 nivartanas to Buddhist monks + agrahara-type grants to Brahmanas
### Historians → positions on Indian feudalism
## 1. Definition and elements
- Definition: Feudalism = grant of land by an overlord to a donee with financial, administrative and judicial rights
- Essential elements (UPSC 2015):
- Administrative structure based on control and possession of land
- Lord-vassal relationship
- Weak central authority
- ⚠ Trap: A strong centre with weak local authority is not a feudal feature
## 2. Historian → position
| Historian | Position |
| R.S. Sharma | Proponent - Indian feudalism crystallised through land grants, sub-infeudation, vishti, decline of trade, urban decay (CDI) and fewer gold coins |
| D.C. Sircar | Critic - grants did not create feudal lords; transferred revenue rights, not ownership; no feudalism in the European sense [GK] |
| Harbans Mukhia | Critic - "Was there feudalism in Indian history?" - no serfdom, a largely free peasantry; peasants kept autonomy [GK] |
| B.D. Chattopadhyaya | Integrative / processual model - grants spread state society into new areas; rejects decay as the full story [GK] |
- Evidence cited: 7th-century fall in long-distance trade + gold coinage; shrinking towns; closed village economy
- ⚠ Sharma = proponent; Sircar and Mukhia = critics; Chattopadhyaya = integrative model
### Universal angles
- Criticism & debates: Feudalism debate → R.S. Sharma's land-grant thesis vs critics who see no serfdom
- Significance: Gupta land grants anchor the "Indian feudalism" debate
- Comparative note: Mauryan state centrally regulated vs Gupta administration relying on feudatories and grantees
- AP angle:
- Satavahana grants to Buddhist monks (e.g. 100 nivartanas, 1 nivartana = about 1.5 acres) = earliest land grants
- Satavahana and Ikshvaku brahmadeya grants barred royal officials
