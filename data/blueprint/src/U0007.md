# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:6 - IVC town planning - grid layout, citadel and lower town, drainage, Great Bath, granaries
### Grid & houses
- Standard bricks, grid planning, streets and drains → civic authority + early engineering skill
- Hallmark: town planning (APPSC G2 2011); no temples found
- Streets N-S and E-W, crossing at right angles (Mohenjodaro, Harappa)
- Main streets ~10 m wide; Banawali = radial streets
- Rooms round a central courtyard; staircases → multi-storey
- Doors open onto side lanes, not main streets
- Wells inside houses: most at Mohenjodaro (UPPSC 2004)

### Great Bath
- Site
- Mohenjodaro citadel (UPPSC 2010)
- Size
- ~12 m x 7 m, 2.4 m deep
- Steps at north + south
- Materials
- Burnt bricks in gypsum mortar
- Bitumen waterproofing
- Purpose
- Ritual bathing (most)
- Others: public tank
### Bricks
- Burnt bricks, ratio 1:2:4 (thickness : width : length)
- House 7x14x28 cm; city wall 10x20x40 cm
- Dholavira: sandstone, limestone blocks
- Mud bricks for fillings, platforms; Kalibangan: wooden drains
### Dholavira water system
- Giant reservoirs, partly rock-cut (UPSC 2021)
- Bunds (check-dams) + channels
- 16 reservoirs ≈ 10% of site
- One of five largest cities
### Exam corner
- Great granary found at Mohenjodaro (APPSC G2 2008)
- "Same-size bricks for all" & "mostly single-storey" = false (CGPSC 2022)
- Gypsum = mortar; bitumen = waterproofing
- Only three-part city = Dholavira (CGPSC 2015); no citadel = Chanhudaro
- No systematic drainage = Banawali
### Town planning features — streets, houses and wells
## 1. Hallmarks and streets
- Hallmark: Town planning (PYQ APPSC G2 2011); no temples found
- Grid layout / grid pattern: Streets run north-south and east-west, crossing at right angles → city divided into blocks (Mohenjodaro, Harappa)
- Mohenjodaro shows the first street layout
- Main streets: About 30 feet (~10 m) wide
- Banawali: Radial streets (only city)
- ⚠ Grid = Mohenjodaro, Harappa; radial = Banawali only
## 2. Houses - features
| Feature | Detail |
| Plan | Rooms round a central courtyard |
| Multi-storeyed | Staircases show multi-storey houses |
| Bathrooms | Burnt-brick floors connected to the drains |
| Privacy | Doors and windows opened onto side lanes, not main streets |
## 3. Wells → site
- Wells:
- Inside houses or neighbourhoods in the developed stage at Mohenjodaro (PYQ UPPSC 2004)
- Mohenjodaro has the most wells
- Well irrigation at Allahdino (Sindh)
### Sites → layout — citadel, lower town, three-part plan
## 1. Dholavira — three-part city
- The Rann of Kachchh, Gujarat — associated with water management and urban planning
- A three-part city — three urban divisions: the Citadel, Middle Town and Lower Town
- Dholavira's citadel = castle + bailey
- Three-part plan: Only IVC city divided into three parts (citadel, middle town, lower town); remnants of three townships (CGPSC 2015)
## 2. Divisions
- Citadel (west): Western side = raised, fortified citadel on a platform with public buildings
- Lower town (east): Eastern side = lower town for commoners' houses
- Seen at Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Kalibangan
## 3. Site → plan
| Site | Plan |
| Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Kalibangan | Citadel + lower town |
| Kalibangan | Citadel + fortified lower town (lower town also fortified) |
| Dholavira | Three parts (see above) |
| Chanhudaro | No citadel (only such city) |
| Lothal | Citadel and lower town in one enclosure [GK] |
| Sutkagendor | Citadel + lower city |
- ⚠ Chanhudaro = no citadel; Dholavira = three parts; Kalibangan = lower town also fortified
### Harappa — granary, burials and finds
## 1. Harappa
- Granary: Twelve rooms in two rows of six rooms each
- Burials: Cemetery R-37; Cemetery H; coffin burial; a graveyard
- Religion: The Mother Goddess; phallus and Lingam-Yoni worship
- Finds: Wheat and barley in a wooden mortar; dice; a copper scale; a copper mirror; a unicorn seal
- The first artefact at Harappa: a stone seal with a unicorn and an inscription — the Harappan script is undeciphered
### Granaries and special structures → site
## 1. Granary → site
| Site | Granary |
| Mohenjodaro | Great Granary - the largest building of the city, on the citadel (PYQ APPSC G2 2008) |
| Harappa | Two rows of six granaries each (12 units; see above) near the river, with circular working platforms and single-roomed barracks; largest wheat finds |
| Lothal | Warehouse on a raised platform beside the dock, with seal impressions |
| Rakhigarhi | Granary also reported |
- Interpretation: Stores of farm surplus; this reading is debated
- ⚠ Great Granary = Mohenjodaro; two rows of six = Harappa
- Exam angle: "The great granary has been found at" - Mohenjodaro (APPSC G2 2008).
## 2. Special structure → site
| Structure | Site |
| Dockyard (tidal; debated - possibly / may be an irrigation tank) | Lothal |
| Multi-pillared assembly hall | Mohenjodaro |
| Water reservoirs, dams, embankments | Dholavira |
| Stadium / ceremonial ground (~283 x 47 m) | Dholavira |
| Fire altars | Kalibangan (seven), Lothal |
| Largest inscription (10-sign signboard) | Dholavira (North Gate) |
### Dholavira → water harvesting and other features
## 1. Water and structures
- Water: Huge reservoirs; water storage facilities; dams; embankments; an advanced water-harnessing system; rock-cut reservoirs
- A Great Bath; a stadium-like structure; special cemetery structures
- A ten-sign inscription — ten large-sized Harappan signs (the signboard inscription)
## 2. Water management (UPSC 2021)
- Giant reservoirs, partly cut into the rock
- Series of bunds (check-dams) + channels feeding the reservoirs
- 16 reservoirs cover about 10% of the site (largest ~73 x 29 m)
## 3. Other features
- Stadium: ceremonial ground
- Rank: Dholavira = one of the five largest Harappan cities (with Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Ganweriwala, Rakhigarhi)
- Occupation: Occupied through seven stages, c. 3000-1500 BCE (APPSC 2018 key: 3000-1800 BCE)
### Building materials — bricks, ratio and statement traps
- Common material: Burnt (kiln-fired) bricks, standard ratio 1:2:4 (thickness : width : length)
- Mud bricks: Fillings and platforms; at Kalibangan
- Sizes: Not the same for every construction - same ratio, different size
| Use | Brick size |
| House bricks | 7x14x28 cm |
| City-wall bricks | 10x20x40 cm |
- Exception - Dholavira: Sandstone and limestone blocks, not baked brick (vs baked brick at Mohenjodaro, Harappa)
- PYQ (CGPSC 2022):
- ⚠ Trap: "Same size of brick for all buildings" = false (house and wall bricks differ)
- ⚠ Trap: "Most houses were single-storeyed" = false (staircases prove multi-storey)
- "Main roads about 10 m wide" = true
- Exam angle: statement questions - "same size bricks for all constructions" and "houses mostly single-storey" are both false (CGPSC 2022).
### Drainage — features
- Status: Most advanced feature of IVC towns
- Flow: House drains → street drains → soak pits/main channels
## 1. Design
- Drains covered, with manholes for cleaning
- Slope: streets sloped for natural flow
## 2. Site → drainage
- Coverage: Even small settlements had planned drainage
- Kalibangan: Wooden drains
- Banawali lacked a systematic system (no systematic drainage)
- ⚠ Wooden drains = Kalibangan; no systematic drainage = Banawali
### Great Bath — site, size, materials, purpose
| Angle | Fact |
| Site | Mohenjodaro (PYQ UPPSC 2010), in the citadel mound |
| Size | About 12 m x 7 m, 2.4 m deep; flights of steps at north + south ends |
| Materials | Burnt bricks set in gypsum mortar, sealed with a layer of bitumen (waterproofing) |
- ⚠ Gypsum = mortar; bitumen = waterproofing layer
- Purpose debate:
- Most: ritual bathing/purification
- Others: a public water tank
- Ritual bathing may survive in later temple tanks and ghats
### Universal angles
- Significance: Standardisation of bricks, grid planning, streets and drains → civic authority + early engineering skill
- Comparative note:
- Unlike Mesopotamia and Egypt, no palaces or temples clearly identified
- Drainage more advanced than contemporary Mesopotamia/Egypt
