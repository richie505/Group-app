### Conflict with Pulakesin II
- Harsha was defeated by Pulakesin II on the Narmada — sources: the Aihole Inscription and Hiuen Tsang's account
- Record: Pulakesin II's Aihole inscription; Hiuen Tsang confirms; Pulakesin took title Paramesvara
- Date: Some notes date it 637 AD, but the Aihole inscription itself is of 634 CE → war came earlier, c. 618-619 CE (standard date)
- ⚠ Bhaskaravarman = ally; Dhruvasena II = vassal and son-in-law; Pulakesin II = the one who defeated Harsha

### Harsha's religion and the Kannauj and Prayag assemblies
- Kannauj Assembly: A religious assembly presided over by Harshavardhana — attended by rulers of 20 countries, Brahmins, Shramanas and courtiers
- Mahamoksha Council: At the Prayagraj Sangam — every fifth year
## 1. Religion - stages and sects patronised
- Stages: Devout Saiva → Hinayana Buddhism → Mahayana (converted by Hiuen Tsang)
- Influence of sister Rajyashri and Xuanzang
- Pro-Buddhist measures:
- Banned animal food; punished killing of living beings
- Tolerance: Kept patronising Brahmanical scholars
## 2. Kannauj vs Prayag assembly
| Feature / point | Kannauj assembly | Prayag assembly (Moksha Parishad) |
| Year / frequency | 643 CE, one-time | Every five years (every 5 years) |
| Place | Kannauj | Confluence of Ganga-Yamuna at Prayag (Allahabad) |
| Purpose | Honour Hiuen Tsang, propagate / spread Mahayana doctrine | Charity (dana) - Harsha gave away his whole treasury, keeping only personal clothes |
| Attendance | 20 kings (some notes: kings of 18 countries), 1000 Nalanda scholars, 3000 Hinayanists/Mahayanists, 3000 Brahmins and Jains | Representatives of all religions, honoured with gifts |
| Duration / events / outcome | 23 days (some notes: 21-day debate); violence, arson and an assassination attempt on Harsha | Hiuen Tsang attended the sixth such assembly |
- Prayag eclecticism: Buddha, Sun and Shiva worshipped on successive days
- ⚠ Kannauj = 643, one-time, Mahayana debate; Prayag = every five years, charity

### Nalanda in Harsha's time → founder, head, curriculum
## 1. Foundation and heads
- Founder: Gupta ruler Kumaragupta I; "Nalanda" = giver of knowledge
- Character: Mahayana centre (Valabhi = Hinayana)
- Head in Harsha's time: Silabhadra (Chancellor) (APPSC G1 2022)
- ⚠ Trap: not Harsha, not Hiuen Tsang, not Kumarajiva
## 2. Funding and teaching
- Funding: Revenue of 100 villages (Hiuen Tsang; some notes say 100-200 villages) + alms from patrons like Harsha
- Students: 10,000 students; free boarding and training; large staff
- Method: Tough entrance exam; Sanskrit medium; debate over lecture
## 3. Harsha and Hiuen Tsang
- Harsha: Did not found it but major patron → multi-storied monastery built by Harsha (UPSC)
- Hiuen Tsang: Studied and later taught at Nalanda (some notes give nine years)
- Exam angle: statement sets mixing "founded by Harsha" / "Hiuen Tsang was chancellor" with the correct one (Silabhadra).
### Hiuen Tsang → profile and observations
## 1. Profile
- Identity: Chinese Buddhist monk, "prince of pilgrims" (some notes: "King of Travelers")
- Visit: In Harsha's reign; arrived in India in 629 AD; at Harsha's court 631 AD; came via Central Asia/Afghanistan
- Duration: Varies in notes (8 years at court; 9 years at Nalanda)
- Work: Si-Yu-Ki ("Records of the Western World") - longest, most detailed account of Central and South Asia
- Praised: Harsha, Pulakesin II (Chalukya), Narasimhavarman (Pallava)
## 2. Observations
| Aspect | Hiuen Tsang's observation |
| Taxation | Light taxation - burden not heavy; subjects free from oppression by officials |
| Law and order | Well-governed, no revolts - yet he was robbed twice |
| Punishment | Ordeals of water, fire, weighing, poison |
| Economy | Signs of decline of trade; rise of a self-sufficient, agrarian economy |
| Caste | Rigid; mixed/sub-castes; untouchables/outcastes mentioned |
| Women | Position declined, though not treated as inferior |
| Religion | Brahmanism, Buddhism popular; Buddhism declining, Brahmanism rising; he does not mention Jainism |

### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Last great Buddhist patron-king of the north before the rise of Rajput states
- Last great empire / unified north India of ancient times; court at Kannauj = intellectual centre
- Ended with his death in 647 CE / 647 AD (no successor)
- AP angle:
- Hiuen Tsang visited Andhra (Dhanakataka) (639-640 CE, Kubja Vishnuvardhana's time): 20 viharas, ~1,000 monks; Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda in decay
- He praised Pulakesin II and the Pallava Narasimhavarman along with Harsha
- Vishnukundin founder Indra Varma began as a Vakataka vassal
- Comparative note:
- Harsha, like Ashoka, turned to Buddhism (Mahayana) and patronised Nalanda
- Pulakesin II stopped Harsha at the Narmada (c. 618-619 CE); Aihole inscription records it; Pulakesin took title Paramesvara
- Criticism & debates: Figures on the Kannauj assembly and Harsha-Pulakesin date differ across sources

### Exam corner
- ★ Maukhari capital — Kannauj; Gupta feudatories originally from Gaya
- ★ Rulers: Hari Varma, Aditya Varma, Ishan Varma, Sarva Varma, Graha Varma
- Maukhari dynasty began at Gaya (Bihar), earliest inscriptions there
- Harsha's first capital Thanesar; later moved to Kannauj (former Maukhari capital)
- Vardhana dynasty also named Pushyabhuti after its founder; rulers once Gupta feudatories
- Harshacharita — not by Harsha but Banabhatta; Harsha wrote Ratnavali, Priyadarshika (romantic comedies) and Nagananda (Buddhist, self-sacrifice)
- State revenue divided into four parts — king's use, public servants, scholars, religious activities
- Harsha — last great Buddhist patron-king of north India before the Rajput states

- ★ Pravarasena II — interested in literature; wrote Setubandha

### Court poets and scholars → patrons and works
- ★ Bana (Banabhatta) — supported by Harsha (7th c.); wrote Harshacharita and Kadambari

### Harsha's religion and the Kannauj and Prayag assemblies

- ★ Harsha's great Buddhist convention in U.P. — at Prayag
- ★ Hiuen Tsang: Mathura famous for cotton cloth; Thanesar prosperous through trade

### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Last great Buddhist patron-king of the north before the rise of Rajput states
- Last great empire / unified north India of ancient times; court at Kannauj = intellectual centre
- Ended with his death in 647 CE / 647 AD (no successor)
- AP angle:
- Hiuen Tsang visited Andhra (Dhanakataka) (639-640 CE, Kubja Vishnuvardhana's time): 20 viharas, ~1,000 monks; Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda in decay
- He praised Pulakesin II and the Pallava Narasimhavarman along with Harsha
- Vishnukundin founder Indra Varma began as a Vakataka vassal
- Comparative note:
- Harsha, like Ashoka, turned to Buddhism (Mahayana) and patronised Nalanda
- Pulakesin II stopped Harsha at the Narmada (c. 618-619 CE); Aihole inscription records it; Pulakesin took title Paramesvara
- Criticism & debates: Figures on the Kannauj assembly and Harsha-Pulakesin date differ across sources
