### Viceroys → tenure, associations and key measures (Canning to Elgin II)
## Canning
- ★ GG during the Revolt of 1857; last Company GG, first Crown Viceroy
- ★ Indian Councils Act, 1861 — portfolio (departmental) system made the Viceroy stronger over his council; let the GG issue Ordinances (max 6 months)
- Indian High Courts Act 1861 — High Courts at Bombay and others
## Lawrence to Lytton
- ★ Masterly Inactivity — John Lawrence (period from Ellenborough to Northbrook)
- ★ Financial Decentralisation — Lord Mayo, 1870
- ★ Vernacular Press Act (1878) — introduced by Lord Lytton (1876-80); not Curzon
- Magistrates could make publishers sign a bond without prior government permission
- One APPSC G1 key names Warren Hastings's tenure; its explanation gives Lytton (1878)
- ★ Lytton — 'Forward Policy' towards Afghanistan (replaced Masterly Inactivity); appointed by Disraeli
- ★ Lytton not associated with the Ilbert Bill
## Ripon
- ★ Repealed the Vernacular Press Act (1882)
- ★ First Factory Act (1881) — limited hours of women and children; banned employment of children below 7; local governments to make rules
- ★ Local self-government — Ripon's 1882 resolution; appointed under Gladstone's Liberal government (1880)
- ★ First census (1872) — Mayo; regular census (1881) — Ripon
## Dufferin (1884-88)
- ★ Viceroy when the INC was founded (1885)
- ★ Mocked Congress as a 'microscopic minority'
- ★ Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885) — Burma added 1 January 1886; last major extension of British India
## Others
- ★ Rowlatt Act — Lord Chelmsford
- ★ Capital shift to Delhi — announced 1911 under Hardinge II (an APPSC G1 key names Lansdowne as having proposed it)
- ★ Asaf Ali — Railway (and Transport) in the 1946 interim Government; Ancient Monuments Preservation Act (1904) — Curzon
### Viceroys → tenure, associations and key measures (Curzon to Rajagopalachari)
## Curzon (1899-1905)
- ★ Longest tenure among Viceroys (about 7 years; Hardinge 1910-16, Dufferin 1884-88, Mayo 1869-72)
- ★ Partition of Bengal — announced 20 July 1905, effective 16 October 1905; annulled at the 1911 Delhi Durbar
- ★ ASI established in his period (PYQ key) — efforts began with the Asiatic Society (Calcutta, 1784, William Jones); Canning appointed Cunningham in 1861
- ★ ASI — under the Ministry of Culture; separate department from 1871
## Hardinge II (1910-16)
- ★ Capital moved Calcutta → Delhi; George V and Queen Mary at the (third) Delhi Durbar, 1911
- ★ Bengal partition of 1905 undone in 1911 (announced 12 December 1911)
- ★ Sent Gokhale to South Africa (1912) to support Gandhi's campaign
## Later
- ★ Rowlatt Act (1919) — detention without trial; Lord Chelmsford (1916-21)
- ★ Civil Disobedience (1930) — Lord Irwin
- ★ Lord Reading (Rufus Isaacs) — only Jewish Viceroy (1921-26)
- ★ Mountbatten — held Jinnah most responsible for Partition
- ★ Order: Home Rule (1916) → Rowlatt Act (1919) → Simon Commission (1927) → Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931)
