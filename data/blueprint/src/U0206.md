### Simla Deputation and the founding of the Muslim League, 1906
- Simla Deputation (1 October 1906):
- Led by Aga Khan (III) to Lord Minto
- Demanded separate electorates + weightage for Muslims for their "contribution to the empire's defence"
- All India Muslim League:
- Founded 30 December 1906 at Dacca, at the session of the All-India Muhammadan Educational Conference
- Proposed by Nawab Salimullah of Dacca; Aga Khan = first president
- Aims: Promote loyalty to the British Empire; safeguard Muslim political interests; keep Muslims away from the Congress

### Indian Councils Act 1909 — name, aim and provisions
## 1. Background and name
- Popular name: Morley-Minto (Minto-Morley) Reforms
- Introduced by: John Morley (Secretary of State) + Lord Minto (Viceroy)
- Aim: Rally the Moderates + reward Muslim loyalty
## 2. Provisions
- Councils: Central Legislative Council enlarged (additional members to 60); provincial councils enlarged
- Majorities:
- Official majority retained at the Centre
- Non-official majority in provinces, but Governor's veto left them powerless
- Electorates: Separate electorates for Muslims with weightage (more seats than population share) - most critical feature; indirect, multi-tier elections
- Powers: Councils could debate the budget - discuss budget, ask supplementary questions, move resolutions
- Could not vote on the budget or question military affairs
- Executive Council: Indians in the Executive Council - S.P. Sinha (Law Member)
### Lucknow session and Lucknow Pact 1916 — terms
## 1. League's younger leadership
- 1912-24: League dominated by younger Muslim nationalists; their nationalism took a communal view of politics
## 2. Lucknow session (Dec 1916) — historic for
- Reunion of Moderates and Extremists after the 1907 Surat Split (Tilak's return)
- Lucknow Pact - Congress-League alliance; League led by the "Young Party" (Jinnah, Ali Brothers)
## 3. Lucknow Pact - terms
| Key clause | Detail |
| Goal | Self-government accepted by both as common goal |
| Separate electorates | Congress accepted Muslim separate electorates - a major concession |
| Weightage | Fixed proportion of seats for Muslims above their population ratio |
| Communal veto | No bill on a community passed if three-fourths of that community's members opposed |
| Others | Separation of executive and judiciary; elected majority in provincial legislatures |
- Limit: Congress and League still held separate annual sessions with distinct agendas

### Indian Councils Act 1909
- Aim — win the Moderates and reward Muslim loyalty (limited concessions, separate electorates)
- Separate electorates for Muslims — introduced by the Indian Councils Act, 1909; legislative councils enlarged
- Indirect, multi-tier elections — not direct universal elections
- Central Legislative Council — additional members up to 60; official majority kept at the Centre
- Members could discuss the budget, ask supplementary questions, move resolutions, but not vote on the budget

### Lucknow Pact terms
- Congress accepted separate electorates for Muslims — major concession
- Common goal — self-government; separation of executive and judiciary; elected majorities in provincial legislatures
- Weightage — Muslims in minority provinces got more seats than their population share
- Communal veto — bill stopped if three-fourths of the affected community's members opposed it
- Provincial Dyarchy (1919) — subjects split into reserved and transferred; abolished by the 1935 Act
### Separate electorates extended
- 1919 Act — communal representation extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans
- 1935 Act — extended to depressed classes, women and labour; also gave provincial autonomy
