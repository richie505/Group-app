### Vasco da Gama — voyage, arrival at Calicut, Zamorin, later voyages
## 1. Vasco da Gama
- From Portugal — arrived in India on 20 May 1498 CE; reached Calicut (Kozhikode) on the western coast
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say he reached Calicut (Kozhikode) on 17 May 1498; APPSC-GS 2019 key uses 20 May 1498 (landing date; many books) - both appear; year 1498 / voyage 1497-98
- Welcomed by the Zamorin (Samuthiri) - the Hindu ruler of Calicut
- Discovered a new or alternative sea route to India from Europe — opened a new era of Portuguese–Indian trade
- His first return or revisit mentioned in the notes: 1502 CE; later visited in 1524 CE
## 2. Voyage
- Departure: Lisbon on 8 July 1497, four ships (São Gabriel, São Rafael, Berrio + supply ship)
- Route: Lisbon - Cape of Good Hope - East African coast - Calicut
- Guided by trader Abdul Majid (some notes say Najit) to Calicut (Kozhikode)
- Arrival ships: three ships - São Gabriel (Vasco), São Rafael (Paulo da Gama), Berrio (Nicolau Coelho)
- Profit: Spices sold in Europe at 60 times the cost
- Contemporaries in 1498: Manuel I (Portugal), Zamorin (Calicut), Sikandar Lodi (Delhi), Immadi Narasimha Raya (Vijayanagara)
## 3. Three voyages
| Voyage | Outcome |
| 1st (1497-99) | Calicut; Zamorin's welcome |
| 2nd (1502) | Zamorin refused trade; war, Calicut occupied, settled at Kochi |
| 3rd (1524) | Came as Viceroy [GK]; died at Kochi, 1524 |
- Exam angle: CGPSC 2022 - only "Zamorin ruled Calicut" was correct; he did not stay a year in India.
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: India-Brazil ties recalled on PM's visit for the 17th BRICS Summit (Jul 2025)
- Cabral reached Brazil in 1500, two years after da Gama
- AP angle:
- Brazil's Nelore cattle (80% of its livestock) come from Nellore, AP
- Machilipatnam = Europeans' first Andhra port
- Significance: 1453/1498 mark the beginning of the Modern Age for India and Andhra
