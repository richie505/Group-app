### Sects → founders, traits and tenets
## 1. Jainism
- Rulers: Early Kakatiyas were Jains (APPSC G2 2011) → Shaivas from Prola II / Rudradeva
- Decline: Kalamukha + Veerashaiva hostility
- Kakatiyas credited with final eradication of Jainism in the region
## 2. Shaiva sects
| Sect | Founder / trait |
| Pasupata | oldest Shaiva sect; Lakulisa (Nakulisa) |
| Kalamukha | Kalasana/Kalanana, disciple of Lakulisa; temple managers |
| Kapalika | offshoot of Pasupata (APPSC G2 2019); skull-bowl ascetics |
- Patronage: Kakatiyas patronised the "extremist" Pasupata and Kalamukha sects
- Pasupata Shaivism: Popular faith into the Reddi period (APPSC G2 2011) → declined under Vijayanagara
- Exam angle: "Kapalikas arose from which doctrine" and "popular religion of the Reddi Rajas" - both answer Pasupata.
## 3. Veerashaivism
- Founder: Basaveshwara
- Followers = Lingayats; gurus = Jangamas; texts = Agamas
- Tenets:
- Rejected Vedas, caste, untouchability, child marriage, animal sacrifice
- In Andhra: "Veerashaiva" absent from inscriptions → followers = Veeravratulu / Asankhyakulu
- Aradhya stream: Brahmin Shaivas → kept Vedic ritual + varna, unlike radical Veerashaivas
- Kakatiya stance: Neutral - grants to orthodox Brahmin sects, no persecution of Lingayats
- ⚠ Aradhyas kept varna; radical Veerashaivas rejected caste
### Religious centres and temple institutions
## 1. Centres
| Place | Religion / feature |
| Padmakshi temple | Built by Prola II; originally a Jain shrine |
| Bodhan, Vemulawada, Patancheru, Kolanupaka, Potlacheruvu | Jain centres |
| Srisailam, Alampur, Vemulawada, Kaleshwaram, Tripurantakam, Mahanandi | Kalamukha / Shaiva centres |
| Srisailam | Main Veerashaiva centre in Andhra |
| Yelledu | Ramanatha temple (queen Lakshmidevi) |
## 2. Temples as institutions
- Landed corporations: Srisailam Mallikarjuna held lands in 70 villages (some notes say 52 villages)
- Staff: Sthanapatis, Manulas, Sanus, Nibandhakaras
- Also monasteries + feeding houses (Kalamukhas gave anna dana)
- Temple women: Temple women + courtesans (Kalavatis) sustained dance and music; festivals = royal occasions
## 3. Social customs
- Hero-stones (viragallu): Commemorated warriors who died in battle or cattle raids
### Universal angles
- Significance: Kakatiya age = "Shaiva era"
- Svayambhu Shiva = dynastic deity, yet Vaishnava shrines (Thousand Pillar Temple) endowed → religious tolerance + syncretism
