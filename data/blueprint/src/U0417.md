- 2019 = self-perceived identity in the Objects; 2026 = self-perceived identity excluded

### Transgender identity — definitions and terms
| Term | Meaning |
| "Third gender" | Legal status of transgender persons recognised by NALSA (2014) |
| Gender identity (NALSA) | Self-determined (right to self-identify gender), not by biology or state verification |
| Transgender person - Definition (2019) | Person whose gender does not match that assigned at birth; includes trans men/women, intersex variations, genderqueer |
| Socio-cultural identities | kinner, hijra, aravani, jogta and others |
| Transgender person - 2026 definition | Limited to socio-cultural identities, specified intersex/congenital variations, and those forced to present as transgender; excludes self-perceived identity |
| SEBC | socially and educationally backward classes → NALSA's basis for reservation |
| Vertical quota | NALSA's OBC-type quota in education and jobs |
| Horizontal reservation | Community demand; quota cutting across SC/ST/OBC/General |
| "Bhiksha Vritti Mukt Bharat" | Begging-free India → goal of SMILE |
- Objects (2019 Act): Cited NALSA + the right to self-perceived gender identity
- ⚠ 2019 = self-perceived identity in the Objects; 2026 = self-perceived identity excluded
### Cases and Bills → year, mover, holding
## 1. NALSA v. Union of India (2014)
- Delivered: 15 April 2014 by Justices K.S. Radhakrishnan and A.K. Sikri
- Core holding: NALSA (2014) recognised transgender persons as the "third gender"
- Gender identity is self-determined, not by biology or state verification
- Directions:
- Treat as SEBC (socially and educationally backward) for reservation in education and jobs → a vertical OBC-type quota
- Separate public toilets, health facilities and welfare schemes
- Criticism: 2026 amendment said to go against NALSA
## 2. Rights of Transgender Persons Bill 2014
- Mover: Private member's bill by Tiruchi Siva (DMK)
- Fate:
- Passed by the Rajya Sabha in April 2015 → first private member's bill passed by RS in over four decades
- not passed by Lok Sabha
## 3. Challenge to the 2026 Amendment
- Challenge: In the Supreme Court by Laxmi Narayan Tripathi and Zainab Javid Patel → contrary to NALSA
- SC, Aug 2026: Centre assured ID cards issued before the 2026 Act remain valid
- ⚠ 2014 = NALSA judgment + Tiruchi Siva Bill; 2015 = RS passes the Bill
### Acts and Rules → provisions: 2019 Act vs 2026 Amendment
## 1. Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019
- In force: January 2020 (10 Jan 2020) (UPSC notes)
- Administered by: MoSJE
- Certificate of identity: Issued by the District Magistrate; revised certificate after surgery
- Rules 2020: Set application procedure for the certificate through the national portal
- Offences: Forced/bonded labour, denial of public places, removal from household, abuse = 6 months to 2 years + fine
## 2. Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Amendment Act 2026
- Passage: Bill introduced by Virendra Kumar in Lok Sabha, 13 Mar 2026 → President's assent by Apr 2026
| Aspect | Change |
| Definition | Limited to socio-cultural identities, specified intersex/congenital variations, and those forced to present as transgender; excludes self-perceived identity |
| Section 4(2) | Omitted - ends legal recognition of self-determination |
| Certificate | DM issues it after a designated medical board's recommendation |
| Offences | Adds kidnapping and forcing a person to present as transgender |
- Key changes: self-perceived identity excluded; Sec. 4(2) omitted; DM issues certificate on a medical board's advice
- ⚠ Certificate: 2019 = DM alone; 2026 = DM on a medical board's recommendation
