### Bhagavatism and Vaishnavism — origin, persons, royal titles, FIRSTs
- Vaishnavism: Associated with the Bhagavata tradition — developed from the Bhagavata religion
- Heliodorus and the Besnagar Garuda Pillar: Associated with the Bhagavata religion
- The Guptas: Vaishnavism — called Parambhagavatas; Garuda symbolism
## 1. Origin and doctrine
- Centre of worship: Vasudeva-Krishna of the Vrishni (Satvata) clan; his followers were its first worshippers
- FIRST: The Bhagavatas first worshipped Vasudeva-Krishna (UPPSC 1997); Panini refers to Vasudeva worship
- Doctrine: Avatara theory of Vishnu (ten incarnations) preached; foundations = karma, bhakti and ahimsa
## 2. Persons → contribution
| Person | Contribution |
| Heliodorus | Greek envoy of Indo-Greek king Antialcidas at the court of Sunga Bhagabhadra; set up the Garuda pillar at Besnagar (Vidisha) to Vasudeva; called himself a Bhagavata |
| Guptas | Patronised Bhagavatism - worship of Bhagavat (Vishnu); contributed most to the sect (BPSC 1994) |
| Chandragupta II | Many notes credit him as first to assume 'Parama Bhagavata' (his coins carry it); colossal Varaha (boar) Vishnu panel at Udayagiri carved under him |
| Samudragupta | UPPSC keys (2009, 2015) name him as the first Gupta to take the title Parama Bhagavata |
- ⚠ Trap - first 'Parama Bhagavata': many notes = Chandragupta II; UPPSC keys (2009, 2015) = Samudragupta
- Dynastic emblem: Garuda, Vishnu's mount
## 3. Varaha panel at Udayagiri
- Cave status: His personal temple
### Puranic Hinduism, Shaktism and Tantrism — texts and practices
## 1. Puranas
- Number: 18 Mahapuranas (APPSC-G2 2011 key)
- Composition: Compiled over centuries; final shape / present form in the Gupta period (c. 4th-6th c. CE) → Puranas and epics expanded then
- Principal Puranas: Vishnu, Bhagavata, Matsya, Vayu, Brahmanda, Markandeya, Shiva, Kurma
- Five topics (pancha-lakshana): creation, re-creation, genealogies, manvantaras, dynastic history
## 2. Nature of religion and worship
- Nature of religion: Theistic and devotional, replacing sacrificial Brahmanism
- Supreme deities = Brahma, Vishnu, Mahesh
- Worship: Idol worship in temples common → shift from Vedic fire altar to temple
- Purusharthas: Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha (first three = trivarga)
## 3. Texts and schools
- Smriti texts: Yajnavalkya, Narada, Brihaspati, Katyayana
- Smarta practices spread
- Six schools of philosophy took final shape
## 4. Shaktism, Tantrism and Surya
- Shaktism: Associated with Hinduism — worship of Goddess Shakti
- Ardhanarishvara: Shaivism and Shaktism — the union of Shiva and Parvati
- Mother goddesses: Durga, Amba, Kali, Chandi worshipped; Gaja-Lakshmi appears on seals
- Tantrism:
- Rituals, shakti (feminine energy); open to women and shudras
- Touched Shaivism, Vaishnavism, Buddhism and Jainism

### Puranic Hinduism & Shaktism
## Religious change
- Theistic and devotional Hinduism replaced sacrificial Brahmanism; temple idol worship common
- Shift from sacrifice to temple worship and bhakti; Gupta age also called the Golden Age of art
- 18 Mahapuranas (final shape in Gupta period) — e.g. Vishnu, Bhagavata, Matsya, Vayu, Brahmanda, Markandeya, Shiva, Kurma
- Durga, Amba, Kali, Chandi — worshipped as mother goddesses
- Tantrism — spread from about the fifth century; open to women and Shudras; centred on shakti
- Mathura — major centre of Jain art (Tirthankara images)
## Temples
- Earliest surviving free-standing Hindu temples — Gupta period
- Gupta temple elements: square garbhagriha, pillared mandapa, pradakshina path, raised jagati platform
- Pedavegi inscription of Nandivarman II — first inscription in AP on Vishnu temples
