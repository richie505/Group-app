### Salinity — basics, salts and factors
## 1. Basics
- Average ocean salinity: Approximately 35‰ — approximately 35 grams of dissolved salts per 1 kilogram of seawater
- Main source of ocean salts: Land; rivers are the major carriers of salts to the ocean
- Controls of salinity: Evaporation, precipitation, river water, winds, ocean currents, volcanoes
- Isohalines: Represent salinity on maps
- Definition: Total dissolved salts in sea water, in parts per thousand (per mille, ppt) or PSU
- Source of salts (extra detail): Weathered rock material carried by rivers; volcanic/hydrothermal emissions on the sea floor
## 2. Factor → effect on salinity
| Factor | Effect on salinity |
| Evaporation | Raises it [GK] |
| Precipitation | Lowers it [GK] |
| River inflow | Lowers it - e.g. Black Sea fed by the Danube, Dnieper, Dniester (LENSD Jul 2026) |
| Ice melting / freezing | Melting lowers, freezing raises [GK] |
| Currents and winds | Redistribute saline water [GK] |
- Effect: Salinity reduces evaporation (APPSC GS 2018)
## 3. Dissolved salts
| Salt | Share of dissolved salts |
| Sodium chloride — the major contribution | Approximately 77.8% |
| Magnesium chloride | Approximately 10.9% |
| Magnesium sulphate | Approximately 4.7% |
| Calcium sulphate | Approximately 3.6% |
| Potassium sulphate | Approximately 2.5% |
| Calcium carbonate | Approximately 0.3% |
| Magnesium bromide | Approximately 0.2% |
- By ions: Chloride + sodium dominate, followed by sulphate and magnesium
### Distribution of salinity → regions and seas
## 1. Distribution of salinity
| Region | Salinity | Reason |
| Equatorial regions | Relatively lower | Heavy rainfall |
| Subtropical and desert belts — the 20°–40° N/S belts | Higher — generally high | High / strong evaporation; low humidity (low rain) |
| Polar regions | Relatively lower | Low evaporation; freshwater from ice melt; rivers |
- ⚠ Highest = subtropics, not the equator
## 2. Seas
| Sea | Salinity | Reason |
| Arabian Sea | Approximately 36‰ — higher (saltier than the Bay of Bengal) | Higher evaporation |
| Bay of Bengal | Approximately 30‰ — lower | Large freshwater input (Ganga-Brahmaputra water) |
| Red Sea | Very high — extremely saline water | High evaporation; very low precipitation; river input is very limited; a narrow connection through Bab-el-Mandeb |
| Persian Gulf, Mediterranean | High | Hot, dry areas |
| Baltic, Black Sea | Low | Rivers pour in |
### Density and "-cline" layers → terms
## 1. "-cline" layers
| Layer | Indicates | Prefix |
| Thermocline | A layer where temperature changes rapidly with depth | "thermo" — temperature |
| Halocline | Rapid salinity change with depth | "halo" — salt |
| Pycnocline | Rapid density change with depth — a density-gradient layer; drives deep-ocean circulation | "pycno" — density |
| Chemocline | Chemistry (e.g. oxygen/hydrogen sulphide) - typical of meromictic basins like the Black Sea, where upper and lower layers rarely mix (LENSD Jul 2026) | - |
- Role: Each cline acts as a barrier to vertical mixing of water
- ⚠ Halocline = salinity; Pycnocline = density
## 2. Density
- Factors: Density rises as temperature falls + as salinity rises; pressure (depth) also raises it
- Effect: Denser water sinks, lighter water rises → drives currents
- Cold polar water sinks and creeps towards the equator
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- Ocean heat content record in 2025: about 23 zettajoules more heat
- About 90% of ocean surface saw marine heatwaves in 2025 (WMO 2025)
- AP angle: NIOT's satellite station at Nellore operationalised OMNI deep-sea buoys → real-time temperature, salinity, currents for cyclone and IOD forecasting
- Significance: Oceans absorb over 90% of excess heat + about a quarter of human CO2 → acidification (pH ~8.1 falling)
