### Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007 → provisions and Sections
## 1. Enactment, obligations and penalties
- Enacted (passed) in 2007 — came into force through a state-wise notification rollout beginning on December 29, 2007
- Creates — places — a legal obligation on adult children and heirs to provide maintenance
- Obligation: Children (biological, adopted, step) and legal heirs must maintain parents and senior citizens who cannot maintain themselves (Apr 2025)
- Neglect: The Act punishes neglect by children; invites punishment for children neglecting old parents (APPSC 2018)
- Abandonment: Up to 3 months and/or Rs 5,000 fine
## 2. Tribunals and provisions
| Feature | Provision / detail |
| Maintenance Tribunal | Sub-division level; in AP headed by RDO / Sub-Collector (2024-25) |
| Appellate Tribunal | District level; in AP headed by the District Collector |
| Maximum maintenance | Rs 10,000 per month |
| Time limit | Case to be disposed within 90 days |
| Property (Section 23) | Transfer/gift made on condition of care can be declared void / revoked (cancelled by the Tribunal) if the transferee neglects the senior citizen/parent (Apr 2025) |
| Medical care | Section 20 - medical care of senior citizens (basis of NPHCE) |
- Old age homes: At least one per district
- ⚠ Sec 23 = property; Sec 20 = medical care; cap Rs 10,000; 90 days
## 3. Amendment bill, earlier State law, AP cases

- Remove the Rs 10,000 cap

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:141
### Senior Citizens Act 2007
## The Act
- Maintenance duty on biological, adopted and step-children, and legal heirs (as relevant)
- Section 20 — medical care of senior citizens; basis of the National Programme for Health Care of the Elderly
- Section 23 — Tribunal may declare a property transfer void if transferee fails to give promised care
- Brought into force state by state from 29 December 2007 (not one date for all)
- 2019 Amendment Bill (remove maintenance ceiling) — not passed
- Himachal Pradesh — first State law: Maintenance of Parents and Dependents Act, 2001
## Schemes
- Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana (BPL seniors) — funded from Senior Citizens' Welfare Fund; at least 30% women beneficiaries per district
- IPOP (AVYAY component) — up to 90% of project cost for NGOs (old-age homes, day-care centres, mobile medicare units)
### Tribunals (AP)
- AP Maintenance Tribunal — sub-division level, headed by Revenue Divisional Officer or Sub-Collector
- Maximum maintenance Rs 10,000 per month; case to be decided within 90 days; appeal allowed
### National policies
| Policy / law | Year | Note |
| NPOP (National Policy on Older Persons) | 1999 | older person = 60 years or above |
| Senior Citizens Act | 2007 | Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens |
| NPHCE | 2010-11 | launched by Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (not MoSJE) |
| NPSC (National Policy for Senior Citizens) | 2011 | harsh punishment for elder abuse; income security, homecare, health insurance; ageing in place |
- AVYAY components: IPSrC, State Action Plan, Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana, Elder Line (National Family Benefit Scheme is an NSAP component)
- HelpAge India — elderly-care services; elder-abuse surveys
### Schemes
- Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana — launched 1 April 2017 at Nellore (AP); through MoSJE and ALIMCO
- PMVVY (2017) — 8% assured pension for 10 years; max purchase ₹7.5 lakh; max pension ₹5,000/month
- IGNOAPS (NSAP) — Ministry of Rural Development; assistance frozen at Rs 200 since 2007
- Ayushman Vay Vandana card (AB-PMJAY) — all aged 70+, irrespective of income
