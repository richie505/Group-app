### Rules of mate selection — endogamy, exogamy, gotra, sapinda
## 1. Endogamy and exogamy
- Endogamy: Marriage within one's own social group — within a caste, tribe or religion (at varna, caste, sub-caste level)
- Preserves group identity; maintains caste boundaries; controls property transfer; promotes cultural continuity and social solidarity; preserves traditions
- Exogamy: Marriage outside one's own social group — bars marriage within one's gotra
- Hypergamy — also known as Anuloma: Marriage into a higher caste
- Hypogamy — also known as Pratiloma: Marriage into a lower caste
## 2. Gotra, sasan and sapinda
| Term | Meaning |
| Sapinda (HMA Sec. 3(f)) | 3 generations on mother's side, 5 on father's |
- North India: Caste endogamy + clan/gotra exogamy + village exogamy
- ⚠ Sapinda = 3 (mother's side) / 5 (father's side)
- Exam angle: the Delhi HC upheld HMA Sec. 5(v) (the sapinda bar) (APPSC 2025 PYQ).
### Hindu marriage forms of Manu Smriti → the eight forms and status
## 1. The eight forms
- Manu Smriti recognizes eight types of Hindu marriages
| Form | Nature | Status in Manusmriti |
| Brahma | Giving a daughter to a learned and worthy groom | Approved |
| Daiva | Giving a daughter to a priest performing a sacrifice | Approved |
| Arsha | Receiving symbolic gifts from the groom | Approved |
| Prajapatya | Based on duty and family life | Approved |
| Gandharva | Based on mutual love and consent | Conditionally accepted |
| Asura | Purchasing the bride by giving wealth to her family | Disapproved |
| Rakshasa | Forceful abduction after battle | Disapproved |
| Paisacha | Taking a woman through deceit or exploitation | The most condemned form |
- Approved or Prashasta: Brahma, Daiva, Arsha, Prajapatya
- Disapproved or Aprashasta: Gandharva, Asura, Rakshasa, Paisacha — ⚠ Gandharva is listed among the disapproved or Aprashasta marriages although conditionally accepted
### Hindu marriage forms of Manu Smriti → in detail
- Brahma: The father voluntarily gives his daughter to a qualified, educated, virtuous groom knowledgeable in the Vedas
- No dowry; no bride price — considered the highest form of marriage
- Modern equivalent: An arranged marriage based on qualifications and compatibility
- Daiva: The daughter is given to a Ritvik priest who officiates a sacrifice — linked with religious rituals; the groom is a priest
- Arsha: Performed after receiving a symbolic gift such as a cow and a bull from the groom — only a symbolic exchange; common among sages and hermits
- Prajapatya: Performed with blessings emphasizing duty — emphasizes family welfare and procreation; focuses on Dharma; husband and wife share responsibilities
- Gandharva: Mutual love and consent of the couple — no parental approval required; a love marriage; a voluntary union
- Example: Shakuntala and Dushyanta; many references occur in epics
- Asura: The groom gives wealth to the bride's family — purchasing the bride; a bride price; wealth is the primary consideration
- Rakshasa: The forceful abduction of a woman — associated with Kshatriyas; after warfare or conflict; abduction-type marriages are mentioned in epics
- Paisacha: Deceit, intoxication, coercion — exploitation of a woman without her consent; the most condemned form; considered immoral and sinful

### Marriage laws — FIRSTs and Acts → year and provisions
## 1. Firsts in marriage law
- Age of Consent Act: The first legal measure against child marriage — 1891
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the Special (Civil) Marriage Act 1872 prohibited child marriage (UPSC)
- Sharda Act — the Child Marriage Restraint Act: The first Child Marriage Restraint Law — the first comprehensive child marriage law — 1929
- Special Marriage Act: The first Special Marriage Law — 1872
- Hindu Marriage Act: The first codified Hindu Marriage Law after Independence — 1955
## 2. Marriage Acts
| Act | Key provisions |
| Special (Civil) Marriage Act 1872 | marriageable age 14 (girl) and 18 (boy) (APPSC 2025 PYQ); monogamous civil marriage [GK]. Also called the Native Marriage Act; it prohibited child marriage (UPSC) |
| Indian Christian Marriage Act 1872 + Divorce Act 1869 | divorce for adultery, bigamy, conversion etc.; mutual consent added 2001 (IYB) |
| Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act 1936 | "Parsi" means Parsi Zoroastrian; registration compulsory (IYB) |

| Goa | Portuguese Civil Code 1867, a form of common civil code (VIS, APPCA) |

### Sections on bigamy and divorce — HMA, IPC/BNS, Muslim law
## 1. Bigamy and offences
- IPC → BNS: IPC Chapter XX (Sec. 493-498) = BNS Sec. 81-84
- Bigamy: Second marriage void (HMA Sec. 11); punishable under Sec. 494 IPC (old IPC 494-495) = BNS 82
## 2. Hindu law
- HMA grounds (Sec. 13): Adultery, cruelty, desertion, conversion, unsoundness of mind, renunciation, not heard of for 7 years
- Sec. 13B: Mutual consent
- Irretrievable breakdown (IBM): Not a statutory ground; SC grants it under Art. 142
## 3. Muslim law
- Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act 2019: Triple talaq void + illegal; up to 3 years' jail + fine; subsistence allowance; custody of minor children
- Muslim wife's routes:
- Khula (mutual consent)
- Dissolution of Muslim Marriage Act 1939 grounds, e.g. no maintenance for 2 years, 7+ years' imprisonment, cruelty
## 4. Family courts
- Family Courts Act 1984: 953 courts as of 31 Jul 2025
- ⚠ HMA Sec. 11 = void bigamous marriage; Sec. 13 = grounds; 13B = mutual consent
- ⚠ Bigamy: IPC 494 = BNS 82
