### Devices of parliamentary control — purpose, list and FIRSTs
## 1. Purpose
- Executive accountability: Parliamentary hours and procedural motions are the main means of enforcing executive accountability, scrutinising government policies and actions, and controlling the Council of Ministers
- Accountability triangle: Question Hour + Supplementary Questions + Adjournment Motions — directly ensure cabinet (executive) responsibility to the legislature
- Indigenous Indian contributions: Zero Hour and the Calling Attention Motion
## 2. List
- Tools:
- Question Hour, Zero Hour, half-an-hour discussion, short-duration discussion
- Calling attention motion, adjournment motion, censure motion, no-confidence motion
- Committee system: Government Assurances, Petitions, Subordinate Legislation, PAC, Estimates
- Budget approval, Motion of Thanks
- States: Same devices + estimates/public accounts committees

### Question Hour vs Zero Hour → timing, nature, notice
## 1. Question Hour vs Zero Hour
| Basis | Question Hour | Zero Hour |
| Timing — Lok Sabha | First hour of every sitting — 11:00 AM to 12:00 Noon | Immediately follows Question Hour — 12:00 Noon to 1:00 PM |
| Timing — Rajya Sabha | 12:00 Noon to 1:00 PM (shifted after 2014) | 11:00 AM to 12:00 Noon |
| Nature | Formal procedure — codified in the Rules | Informal convention — an Indian innovation of the early 1960s |
| Purpose | Asking and answering questions addressed to Ministers on administrative actions | Raising urgent matters of public importance without prior notice |
| Duration | Maximum one hour | Generally up to one hour — some notes say it is not fixed |
- Question Hour: First hour of every sitting — 11 AM to 12 Noon; a formal procedure under the Rules of Procedure; for asking and answering classified questions; lasts one hour
- Ministers answer searching questions; maximum attendance of members (APP)
- Significance: Most effective vigil on the executive
- Zero Hour: Follows Question Hour — 12 Noon to 1 PM; urgent matters of great public importance without prior formal notice
### Zero Hour → rules status, timing, notice, reply
## 1. Zero Hour — more
- Rules status: Not mentioned in the Rules of Procedure; informal
- Origin: In use since 1962
- Timing (LS): Starts immediately after Question Hour; lasts until regular business is taken up; no fixed duration in rules
- Timing (RS): Urgent matters raised in the first hour (Zero Hour precedes Question Hour)
- Notice: Without the prior notice a question needs; members raise any important matter, only intimating the Presiding Officer the same day, who decides whom to allow [GK]
- Reply: Ministers not bound to reply
- Follow-up: Chair's directions / assurances → Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs sends extract to ministry concerned for action
- Exam angle: "time after Question Hour when members raise matters without prior notice" = Zero Hour (APPSC-GS 2025; G2 2023, 2025).

### Rules of Procedure → devices; Rules vs Constitution
## 1. Rules and conventions
- Rule 184 (Lok Sabha): Discussion on a matter of public interest with a formal motion and voting — defeat can mean potential collapse of the ruling party
- Rule 377 (Lok Sabha): Raise urgent matters not covered under Questions, Calling Attention or other motions
- Written notice to the Secretary-General
- Number of matters raised daily increased from 20 to 30 in 2015
- Interpellation: The formal right of asking questions of the Executive — demanding explanations of government actions or policies
## 2. Rules vs Constitution
- Constitution: Gives the broad institutional framework; day-to-day proceedings follow the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha
- Not in the Constitution — derive from parliamentary rules or conventions: No-Confidence Motion, Zero Hour, Rule 377
- Primary mechanisms for executive accountability: Question Hour, Supplementary Questions, Adjournment Motions
## 3. Rule numbers
| Rule | Device |
| Rule 377 (LS) / Special Mention Rule 180A-E (RS) | Matters not raisable under any other device; members raise matters that are not points of order ("special mention"); Rajya Sabha equivalent is Special Mentions [GK] |
| Rule 193 (LS) | Short-duration discussion, no voting |
| Rule 184 (LS) | Discussion followed by vote |
| Rule 198 (LS) | No-confidence motion |
| Rule 222 (LS), Rule 187 (RS) | Privilege motion (LENSD, May 2026) |
- Short-duration discussion: Max 2 hours; Indian innovation (1953)
- Half-an-hour discussion: Matter sufficiently debated needing factual elucidation; no formal motion or vote
- ⚠ 193 = no vote; 184 = vote
### Calling Attention vs Adjournment Motion
## 1. Calling Attention vs Adjournment Motion
| Basis | Calling Attention Motion | Adjournment Motion |
| Origin | Indian parliamentary innovation (since 1954) | Traditional parliamentary device |
| House | Either House | Lok Sabha only |
| Permission | Presiding Officer's permission | Presiding Officer's consent; 50 members' support |
| Objective | Draw a Minister's attention to an urgent public matter; seeks authoritative statement from minister | Discuss a definite matter of urgent public importance; sets aside House business |
| Business | Does not disrupt the daily agenda | Interrupts / suspends normal business; debate at least 2 h 30 min |
| Censure | No element of condemnation | Involves censure or condemnation of the Government |
- Adjournment Motion conditions: A single, definite, specific matter of recent occurrence; cannot raise a question of privilege; only one per sitting; admissible only when other tools (e.g. Short Duration discussions) are too late
- Restriction: Calling Attention notices restricted the scope of the Adjournment Motion — not of Question Hour or Zero Hour
- Calling Attention Motion: Resembles an Adjournment Motion but has no censure element; in the Rules of Procedure
- Rule 377: Raise matters of general public importance that cannot be raised under Questions, Calling Attention or other Motions
- Written notice + the Speaker's consent; daily limit 20 → 30 in 2015
- Rule 184: A substantive motion — discussion on a specific matter of public interest followed by voting; significant political consequences
- ⚠ Adjournment and no-confidence → both Lok Sabha only, both need 50 members
### Privilege and cut motions
- Privilege Motion: Against a Minister for withholding or distorting facts — either House; Presiding Officer's consent; referred to the Committee of Privileges
| Motion | House | Key requirement |
| Privilege | Both | Breach by minister/member; Rule 222 (LS), Rule 187 (RS) (LENSD, May 2026) |
| Cut motions | Lok Sabha [GK] | On demands for grants |
- Cut motions (on demands for grants):
- Policy cut = reduce demand to Re 1 → disapproval of policy
- Economy cut = reduce by a specified amount
- Token cut = reduce by Rs 100 → a specific grievance
### No-confidence motion → basis, procedure, Speaker's role
## 1. Basis and procedure
- No-Confidence Motion (Rule 198): Moved by the Opposition in the Lok Sabha — Lok Sabha only; against the entire Council of Ministers — not an individual minister
- Written notice to the Secretary-General; support of at least 50 members for leave; no reasons need be stated
- Tests the majority support of the Council of Ministers
- Confidence Motion: Moved by the Government (Council of Ministers) to demonstrate its majority on the floor
- Constitutional status: Not mentioned in the Constitution; flows from Article 75(3) → Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha
- Effect: If passed, whole Council of Ministers must resign → enforces collective responsibility
- Speaker's role: Decides admissibility — whether the motion is in order; admits or rejects (APPSC-GS 2025 key)
- ⚠ Key: Speaker has discretion to admit or reject the motion (APPSC-GS 2025)
- ⚠ Trap: Speaker does not resign or dissolve the House
- Status: Most powerful weapon of executive accountability (APPSC-G2 2025 key)
- Contrast: Question Hour = most effective method of day-to-day vigil on the executive
### Censure vs no-confidence → motions on the Government's survival, instances
## 1. Motions on the Government's survival
| Motion | Moved by / where | Key points |
| No-Confidence Motion | Opposition — Lok Sabha only — Rule 198 | Tests the Council of Ministers' majority; needs the support of 50 members; if passed, the Government must resign; first moved by J. B. Kripalani in 1963 against Jawaharlal Nehru |
| Confidence Motion | Government (Council of Ministers) — Lok Sabha only | Proves majority floor support; if defeated, the Government falls |
| Censure Motion | Lok Sabha only | Strong disapproval / criticism of specific policies; reasons must be stated; does not force resignation — shows political weakness |
- Censure Motion: Against an individual minister or the entire Council
| Feature | Censure motion | No-confidence motion (Rule 198 LS) |
| Reasons | Must state reasons | Not required |
| Against | Council of Ministers, a minister or a group [GK] | Entire Council of Ministers; only in Lok Sabha; needs 50 members [GK] |
| Effect if passed | Not bound to resign; seeks disapproval of a policy [GK] | Council of Ministers must resign |
- "reasons must be stated" = censure motion.
## 2. Instances

| First | Against Nehru (1963, J.B. Kripalani) |
| Andhra State | Gouthu Latchanna's motion (Nov 1954); 69 for, 68 against → Prakasam ministry fell by one vote after 13 months |

### Resolutions and the Whip → features
## 1. Resolutions
- Nature: Self-contained proposal, always a substantive motion
- Mover cannot withdraw without leave of the House
- Must be put to vote
- Same subject barred for one year
- Types:
- Private member's: Taken up on alternate Fridays, afternoon
- Government: Moved by a minister (e.g., approving treaties)
- Statutory: Moved under a constitutional/statutory provision
- AP Legislature: Uses same devices; e.g., AP Legislative Council adjourned amid Question Hour/short-duration discussion row on the Tirumala laddu issue (Feb 2026)
## 2. Office of Whip
- Basis: Not mentioned in the Constitution, the Rules of the House or any statute (parliamentary statute); rests on conventions of parliamentary government
- ⚠ Key: Only the convention statement valid (APPSC-G1 2024)
- Function: Each party appoints a whip/chief whip → ensure attendance and voting per party line
- Penalty: Defying the whip on a vote can attract Tenth Schedule disqualification
- Coordination: Minister of Parliamentary Affairs keeps close contact with chief whips of parties in both Houses
### Terms → closures, guillotine, point of order, yielding the floor, whip lines
## 1. Closure and guillotine
- Closure / Guillotine Motion: Lok Sabha only — when the allotted debate time expires, debate closes immediately and all undiscussed financial demands and clauses are put to the vote
- Closure Motion: Cuts short debate and puts the matter to vote
- Guillotine: A form of closure when the allotted time expires — all remaining discussed and undiscussed clauses or demands for grants go to vote immediately
- Yielding the Floor: Convention — the Speaker orders a speaking member to yield (stop) so that another member can speak
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the member speaking gives way to another member or the Chair [GK]
## 2. Terms
| Device | Meaning |
| Point of order | Raised when proceedings depart from rules/Constitution; no debate; Presiding Officer rules [GK] |
| Simple closure | "Matter sufficiently discussed; be put to vote" |
| Closure by compartments | Clauses grouped into parts; debate and vote part by part |
| Kangaroo closure | Only important clauses debated; others taken as passed |
| Guillotine | All pending clauses put to vote when allotted time ends, even undiscussed ones |
| One-line whip | Informative |
| Two-line whip | Attendance |
| Three-line whip | Strict voting direction |
- ⚠ Kangaroo = only important clauses debated; guillotine = undiscussed clauses voted when time ends

### Survival motions
| Motion | Moved by | Reasons | Effect if passed |
| No-confidence (Rule 198) | Opposition; Lok Sabha only; 50 members | Not needed | Government must resign |
| Censure | — | Must be stated | Does not compel resignation |
| Confidence | Government, to prove majority | — | If defeated, the Government falls |
- ★ Censure Motion differs from the No-confidence motion in aim and procedure; no separate "leave of the House" needed
- First no-confidence motion — against Nehru in 1963 by J.B. Kripalani
## Privilege motion
- Against a Minister withholding or distorting facts; censure motion shows disapproval of specific policies
- Rule 222 (Lok Sabha) and Rule 187 (Rajya Sabha)
- Needs presiding officer's consent; goes to the Committee of Privileges; does not force resignation
## Other
- Rule 184 — discussion ending in a formal motion and vote; losing can threaten the ruling party
- Statutory resolution — moved under a constitutional or statutory provision; Government resolution — moved by a minister (e.g. approve a treaty)
- Rule 180A-E (RS) — Special Mentions

### Closures & whips
## Closures
| Type | Meaning |
| Simple closure | Matter declared sufficiently discussed and put to vote |
| Kangaroo closure | Only important clauses debated; others taken as passed |
| Guillotine | Allotted time expires; all remaining clauses/demands put to vote at once |
## Whips
| Whip | Meaning |
| One-line | Informative |
| Two-line | Direction to attend |
| Three-line | Strict voting direction |
- Defying the Whip — disqualification under the Tenth Schedule
## Other
- Half-an-hour discussion — matter already debated enough, needing factual elucidation; no motion or vote
- Cut motions — economy cut: by a specified amount; policy cut: to Re 1; token cut: by Rs 100
- PAC recommendations — not binding; checks spending within the demand, not policy
- Urjit Patel committee — inflation targeting 4% ± 2%; Nachiket Mor — financial inclusion

### Rules of Procedure → devices; Rules vs Constitution
- ★ Governments dislike Rule 184 — discussion followed by voting; losing the vote may cost power

### Calling Attention vs Adjournment Motion
- ★ Adjournment Motion — discussion on a definite matter of urgent public importance; Lok Sabha; needs 50 members' support
- ★ Calling Attention notices narrowed the scope of the Adjournment Motion; idea given by India
### Privilege and cut motions
- ★ Privilege motion against a Minister who withholds facts or gives distorted versions
- ★ Cut motion — related to the Union Budget
- ★ Censure — strong disapproval, usually of Government policies; Calling Attention — with Speaker's permission, draws a Minister's attention to an urgent matter
### No-confidence motion → basis, procedure, Speaker's role
- ★ No Confidence Motion — strongest tool of executive accountability; if passed, the whole Council of Ministers resigns
- ★ Minimum 50 members to table it
### Censure vs no-confidence → motions on the Government's survival, instances
- ★ No Confidence Motion against the Council of Ministers — tabled by at least 50 Lok Sabha members
