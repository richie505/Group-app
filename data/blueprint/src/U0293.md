### 44th Amendment, 1978 → Emergency safeguards and property
- 44th Amendment (1978): Nullified emergency-era distortions; restored fundamental safeguards
- Removed the Right to Property from Fundamental Rights → now Article 300A
- Inserted the term "armed rebellion" — "internal disturbance" → "armed rebellion"
- The President may return the Council's advice once for reconsideration
- Enacted: 1978, by the Janata government (PM Morarji Desai)
- Purpose: corrective to the 38th, 39th and 42nd; restored 5-year term of LS and Assemblies + judicial review
- 39th reversed: its bar on courts hearing election disputes of the President, VP, PM and Speaker (errata, Apr 2026)
- Emergency safeguards:
- Arts. 20 and 21 cannot be suspended

- ⚠ 42nd term = 6 years; 44th restored 5-year term

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:19
### 1951-1978
## First Amendment, 1951
- Added Articles 31A and 31B and the Ninth Schedule; changed grounds of restriction in Article 19(2); added Article 15(4)
- Passed by the Provisional Parliament under PM Nehru; President Rajendra Prasad gave assent on 18 June 1951 (75th anniversary 18 June 2026)
- Champakam Dorairajan → Article 15(4); Arts. 31A, 31B and Ninth Schedule answered the land-reform rulings
- Article 31B — validates Ninth Schedule Acts; Article 31A — saves estate-acquisition laws; Article 15(4) — special provisions for backward classes, SCs and STs
## Other amendments of the period
| Amendment | Change |
| 7th (1956) | Reorganisation of States; abolished Part A, B, C, D classification; repealed Part VII; dealt with UTs |
| 24th (1971) | Inserted Article 13(4); Parliament's power to amend FRs; presidential assent to amendment Bills obligatory |
| 25th (1971) | After Bank Nationalisation case, 'amount' for 'compensation' in Art. 31; inserted Article 31C |
| 26th | Abolished privy purses |
| 32nd (1973) | Inserted Articles 371D and 371E (AP Six-Point Formula, Central University) |
| 69th | Articles 239AA and 239AB — Delhi's framework |
## 42nd Amendment
- Added 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' to the Preamble ('Justice' was already there)
- Moved education, forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals and birds, administration of justice to the Concurrent List
- Widened Art. 31C; the 44th removed property from Part III
### The Emergency pair
## 42nd Amendment (1976)
- Bill introduced in the Lok Sabha in September 1976, during the Emergency
- Known as the 'Mini-Constitution'
- Extended term of Lok Sabha and State Assemblies from five to six years
- Added Part XIV-A (tribunals) and Part IVA / Article 51A (Fundamental Duties); froze delimitation on the 1971 Census
- Allowed National Emergency in a part of India
- Added 'socialist' and 'secular' to the Preamble; SC holds secularism is part of the basic structure (built into the scheme)
- Made ministerial advice binding on the President; shifted education to the Concurrent List
## 44th Amendment (1978)
- Restored the five-year term of Lok Sabha and State Assemblies; restored judicial review in important respects
- 'Armed rebellion' replaced 'internal disturbance' as a ground for National Emergency
- Lok Sabha can disapprove/revoke a Proclamation of Emergency
- Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended during an Emergency
- Removed Arts. 19(1)(f) and 31; property became a legal right under Article 300A (Part XII)
- Restored courts' power over election disputes of President, Vice-President, PM and Lok Sabha Speaker (undid the 39th Amendment bar)
- President may return ministerial advice once for reconsideration
