### Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026
## 1. Basics
- Notified by: MoEFCC; in force 1 April 2026; ⚠ replaces SWM Rules, 2016
- Waste data: 620 lakh tonnes/year; ~1.85 lakh t/day generated; 1.14 lakh t/day processed; ~40,000 t/day landfilled
- Hierarchy: Prevention → Reduction → Reuse → Recycling → Recovery → Disposal (last resort)
## 2. Four-stream segregation (mandatory)
- ⚠ SWM Rules 2016 had three categories (wet, dry, domestic hazardous)
## 3. Bulk Waste Generators
- Definition: built-up area ≥ 20,000 sq m, or water use ≥ 40,000 litres/day, or waste ≥ 100 kg/day
- Extended Bulk Waste Generator Responsibility (EBWGR): on-site wet waste processing, portal registration, certification-based compliance
- Bulk generators produce ~30% of solid waste
## 4. Enforcement and energy
- Polluter Pays: environmental compensation; higher landfill fees for unsegregated waste; CPCB frames guidelines, SPCBs enforce
- Centralised online portal tracking full lifecycle
- Legacy dumpsites mapped by 31 October 2026
- Waste with calorific value ≥ 1,500 kcal/kg → energy recovery (RDF, co-processing)
- RDF in industries: 6% initially → 15% within six years
- District Collectors oversee landfills; special provisions for hilly areas and islands

### Bomb cyclone hits northern US
## 1. News
- Date: 29 December 2025 — northern United States (Midwest, East Coast)
## 2. Concept
- Bomb cyclone: intense mid-latitude (extra-tropical) storm
- Definition: central pressure drops by at least 24 millibars in 24 hours — process called bombogenesis
- Formation: cold Arctic/Canadian air meets warm moist air (often over oceans)
- Season: any season; most frequent in fall and winter
- Where: mostly western North Atlantic (energy from Gulf Stream); also NW Pacific, occasionally Mediterranean
- Study: Northern Illinois University (2021) — ~7% of non-tropical lows near North America (1979–2019) were bomb cyclones; avg 18 per year
- Hazards: winds 80 kmph+, blizzards, lakeshore flooding, thundersnow (snow with lightning)
| Aspect | Bomb cyclone | Hurricane |
| Type | Mid-latitude (extra-tropical) | Tropical cyclone |
| Energy | Temperature contrast | Warm ocean waters |
| Season | Fall and winter | Summer, early autumn |
| Core | Cold-core | Warm-core |
- ⚠ Bomb cyclones are not powered by warm ocean water (that is hurricanes)
- ⚠ Eye of a tropical cyclone is warmer, not colder, than surroundings

### Bomb cyclone hits northern US
## 1. News
- Date: 29 December 2025 — northern United States (Midwest, East Coast)
## 2. Concept
- Bomb cyclone: intense mid-latitude (extra-tropical) storm
- Definition: central pressure drops by at least 24 millibars in 24 hours — process called bombogenesis
- Formation: cold Arctic/Canadian air meets warm moist air (often over oceans)
- Season: any season; most frequent in fall and winter
- Where: mostly western North Atlantic (energy from Gulf Stream); also NW Pacific, occasionally Mediterranean
- Study: Northern Illinois University (2021) — ~7% of non-tropical lows near North America (1979–2019) were bomb cyclones; avg 18 per year
- Hazards: winds 80 kmph+, blizzards, lakeshore flooding, thundersnow (snow with lightning)
| Aspect | Bomb cyclone | Hurricane |
| Type | Mid-latitude (extra-tropical) | Tropical cyclone |
| Energy | Temperature contrast | Warm ocean waters |
| Season | Fall and winter | Summer, early autumn |
| Core | Cold-core | Warm-core |
- ⚠ Bomb cyclones are not powered by warm ocean water (that is hurricanes)
- ⚠ Eye of a tropical cyclone is warmer, not colder, than surroundings
