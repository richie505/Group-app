### Three "Councils" in Indian Polity
## 1. Three "Councils" in Indian Polity
- Council of Ministers: An executive body aiding and advising the President (Union — Article 74) or the Governor (State — Article 163)
- Council of States: The Rajya Sabha — Upper House of Parliament, Article 80
- State Legislative Council: The Vidhan Parishad — Upper House of bicameral State Legislatures, Articles 168, 169 and 171
- Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha; the Vice-President is ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha but not a member of Parliament (not a member of the Council of States)
### Legislature types — definitions and country examples
- Definitions: Unicameral = one House vs bicameral = two Houses
- Bicameralism first introduced at the Central level by the GoI Act, 1919 — Council of State and Central Legislative Assembly
- India: Parliament is bicameral → Council of States (Rajya Sabha) + House of the People (Lok Sabha)
- Unicameral examples: China (National People's Congress), Israel (Knesset), New Zealand, Sweden, Denmark

### State legislatures — Part VI and second chambers in States
## 1. State legislatures — Part VI
- Part VI: Articles 168 to 212
- Bicameral: Governor + Legislative Assembly (Lower House) + Legislative Council (Upper House); unicameral: Governor + Legislative Assembly
- Bicameral States — 6: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh — largest Council: Uttar Pradesh, 100 seats
- Older notes list six/seven bicameral states including J&K
- Unicameral examples: Goa, Mizoram, Sikkim (UPSC)
## 2. Articles on Legislative Councils
| Article / Act | Provision |
| Art. 312 | Creation of new All-India Services |
| Art. 169 | Parliament creates or abolishes a Legislative Council by law (not an Art. 368 amendment) |
| Art. 171 | Council size: not more than one-third of the Assembly; at least 40 |
- Art. 169 pre-condition: Assembly resolution passed by a majority of total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting
