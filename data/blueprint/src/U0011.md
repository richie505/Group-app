### Social stratification — evidence
| Evidence | What it shows |
| Town layout | Citadel on the west (public buildings, elite) vs lower town on the east (commoners' houses) |
| Houses | Vary in size - three building types (residential, larger buildings, public); single-roomed barracks at Harappa |
| Burial goods | Variation in goods and ornament quality → differentiation; quantity and quality of goods reflect status |
- Caste: No textual evidence of a rigid caste system; caste crystallised only in Later Vedic period
### Views on the polity → basis
## 1. View → basis
| View | Basis |
| Priest rule | Citadel, Great Bath, steatite Priest-King; citadel may have held rulers or temples |
| Merchant elites / civic administrators | Trade-driven uniformity; no kings or palaces (CDI) |
- Evidence: No clear-cut evidence of political structure; "appears corporate" - unlike monarchical Later Vedic polity
- City-state argument: Several large cities; no single capital identified
- Centralised-state argument: Uniformity over a huge area
## 2. Uniformity as evidence of central authority
- Standardisation:
- Bricks in standard 1:2:4 ratio
- Binary weights; uniform seals
- Grid-plan towns across about 8,00,000 sq km
- Craft centres: Lothal beads, Chanhudaro shells
### Absence of palaces and temples; weapons
## 1. No monumental temples or palaces
- Finding: No royal palaces and no temples
- ⚠ Trap: "Possessed great palaces and temples" is WRONG (UPSC 2013)
- Readings:
- Worship domestic or open-air
- Power not personalised in a king
- Public structures civic (Great Bath, granary, pillared hall)
## 2. Warfare and weapons
- Weapons: Maces, axes, spears, bows and arrows of copper and bronze
- No horse-drawn chariots (UPSC 2013)
- Reading: Weapons few and simple → relatively peaceful society
- Mohenjodaro skeletons: "Huddled together" - once read as massacre → now multiple burials

- Cosmetics: Bronze mirrors, ivory combs, collyrium sticks

### Burial types → site
## 1. Burial type → site
| Type | Site / feature |
| Extended (complete) burial with grave goods | Mohenjodaro, Harappa (cemetery R-37; coffin burial) |
| Fractional burial (bones buried after exposure) | Mohenjodaro, Harappa |
| Post-cremation burial (ashes with pottery) | Harappa (Cemetery-H), Mohenjodaro, Lothal |
| Pot burial | Surkotada (four pot burials, stone-covered grave) |
| Joint/double burial | Lothal |
| Dog buried with human (oval pits) | Ropar |
- ⚠ Double burial = Lothal; dog with human = Ropar; pot burial = Surkotada
## 2. Sites → burial features
- Kalibangan: Three types
- Extended burial in rectangular pits
- Pot burials
- Circular pits with grave goods but no skeleton
- Sanauli (Baghpat district, UP): Large Harappan-period cemetery of human burials
- Harappa R-37: Extended burials, head generally to the north (some notes: east-west axis)
- Rakhigarhi (Mound 7): Burial plot
- Female skeletons wore jasper and agate beads + shell bangles
## 3. Grave goods and beliefs
- Grave goods: Pottery, ornaments, mirrors, beads, food → belief in afterlife
- Exam angle: double burial → Lothal (UPPSC 2016); Sanauli → human burials (UPPSC 2004).
### Universal angles
- Current relevance (Jun 2026):
- Rakhigarhi = about 550 ha, largest known Harappan settlement
- Criticism & debates: Stratification and polity inferred only from material remains; script gives no names of rulers
- Comparative note: Harappan polity corporate vs Later Vedic hereditary monarchy with declining Sabha and Samiti
