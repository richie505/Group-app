### 1973 Oil Price Crisis and Strait of Hormuz disruption
## 1. Why in news
- West Asia conflict disrupted oil flows through Strait of Hormuz — comparisons with 1973 Oil Crisis
## 2. 1973 crisis
- Trigger: Arab members of OPEC imposed oil embargo on countries supporting Israel in Yom Kippur War (1973)
- First major use of oil as a geopolitical weapon; prices rose ~fourfold (~400%, 1973–74) → stagflation
- Other factors: end of gold standard (1971); territories lost in 1967 war
- Outcomes: petrodollar system; strategic petroleum reserves; creation of International Energy Agency (IEA)
- ⚠ 1973 = political embargo; present crisis = physical disruption of supply routes
- 1979 Oil Crisis — also caused stagflation (linked to Iranian Revolution, not OPEC price-stabilising cuts)
## 3. How India handled 1973 and 1979 shocks
- 1973: OAPEC embargo; prices $3 → $5+ (~70% initial rise); India's oil import bill $500 million (1973) → $1.3 billion (1974); inflation ~20%; Fifth Five-Year Plan assumptions upset
- 1979: Iranian Revolution; prices $13 (1979) → ~$34 (1980); India's BoP crisis → ⚠ approached IMF after 1979 (not 1973)
## 4. India's energy basket today
- India = third-largest crude consumer; coal ~48.4%, crude ~24.7% of energy demand (IEA)
- Domestic crude meets ~13%; import dependence ~88.5% (FY26)
- Strategic Petroleum Reserves: ~5.33 million tonnes capacity
## 5. Straits — static GK
- Strait of Hormuz: between Iran and Oman (Musandam); links Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman; ~33 km at narrowest; ~one-fifth of global petroleum; Qatar LNG; ⚠ no direct link to Red Sea
- Bab-el-Mandeb: Yemen and Djibouti/Eritrea — Red Sea ↔ Gulf of Aden
- Connected to Red Sea: Gulf of Aden, Suez Canal, Bab-el-Mandeb
