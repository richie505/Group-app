# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:98 - Constitutional, statutory, executive, regulatory and quasi-judicial bodies - definitions and how to distinguish them
### How to tell them apart
- Constitutional = in the Constitution itself; statutory = an Act of Parliament; executive = a resolution
| Type | Basis | Examples |
| Constitutional | An Article of the Constitution | FC, CAG, NCBC (since 2018), Art. 350B officer, Inter-State Council |
| Statutory | Act of Parliament | NHRC (1993), NCM (1992), Zonal Councils (1956) |
| Executive | Cabinet resolution / order | NITI Aayog, Planning Commission, NDC, Law Commission |
| Quasi-judicial | Statute; summon, sanction; not a court | NHRC, SEBI, CIC |

### Body types — definitions and spotting the odd one out
| Type | Meaning |
| Constitutional body | Provided for by the Constitution itself; composition, powers, independence set in an Article/Part (e.g. Part XV, Art. 324, Election Commission) |
| Statutory body | Created by an Act of Parliament → changeable by amending that Act |
| Executive / non-statutory body | Resolution-based, advisory (NITI Aayog, Planning Commission, NDC, Law Commission) |
| Constitutional | Statutory |
| Finance Commission, CAG, NCBC (since 2018), Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities, Inter-State Council (Art. 263) | NHRC (1993 Act), NCM (1992 Act), Zonal Councils (States Reorganisation Act 1956) |
- Odd one out: NITI Aayog among NCBC, CAG, Finance Commission (APPSC-GS 2025)
- Count question: among NITI Aayog, NCM, Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities, NHRC → only one constitutional = Special Officer, Art. 350B (APPSC-G2 2023)
- Linguistic minorities: not notified under NCM Act → overseen by Art. 350B officer (LENS, 21 Jul 2026)
- ⚠ NCM = statutory (1992 Act); Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities = constitutional (Art. 350B)
- Exam angle: "which is NOT a constitutional body" and "how many are constitutional" counts.
### Quasi-judicial bodies → features and civil court powers
- Definition: non-judicial body that can interpret + apply law, examine, summon, impose sanctions
- Authority from a statute, but not a court
- Examples: NHRC, SEBI, CIC, Mental Health Review Commission
- Bodies with civil court powers (summon witnesses, requisition records):
- NCBC (since 2018)
- NCSC and NCST
- CIC (under RTI Act)
- Not quasi-judicial: advisory bodies such as Law Commission, NITI Aayog
- Law Commission: Advisory research body that makes recommendations to the Government. Not constitutional, not statutory, not quasi-judicial; reports to the Ministry of Law and Justice, not the President [GK]
- Exam angle: APPSC-G1 2024 keyed "All incorrect" for statements calling the Law Commission a quasi-judicial body that advises the President.

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:98
### How to tell them apart
## Definitions
- Constitutional body — provided for by the Constitution
- Statutory body — made by an Act of Parliament; changed by amending its parent Act
- Quasi-judicial body — not a court; can interpret and apply law, summon persons, impose sanctions under statutory authority
- Quasi-judicial examples — NHRC, SEBI, CIC; NITI Aayog is advisory, not quasi-judicial
- NCBC — civil-court powers (summon witnesses, demand records), esp. after constitutional status in 2018
## Constitutional basis
- ★ Inter-State Council — source is the Constitution (Article 263)
- ★ Planning Commission — not a constitutional organ
- ★ Planning Commission — Union Cabinet proposal 15 March 1950; abolished; NITI Aayog from 1 January 2015 (no longer in existence)
- Sarkaria Commission — NDC to get constitutional status under Article 263, renamed National Economic and Development Council
## Statutory and executive bodies
| Body | Basis |
| Water Disputes Tribunals | Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956 (context Article 262) |
| North Eastern Council | North Eastern Council Act, 1971 |
| Zonal Councils | States Reorganisation Act, 1956 (Section 15) |
| National Security Council | executive order, 1998 — extra-constitutional |
| Law Commission | executive order, set up from time to time — extra-constitutional |
| NDC, Planning Commission | executive resolutions |
- NITI Aayog — chaired by PM; Governing Council includes all CMs and LGs of UTs
- Zonal Councils (statutory) and Inter-State Council (Article 263) — different because their basis differs
- SC Commission, Finance Commission, State Election Commission — constitutional

- Linguistic Minorities Officer report — to President, laid before Parliament, sent to States
- Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities — constitutional; NITI Aayog executive; NCM and NHRC statutory

- Attorney General — holds office at President's pleasure; must be qualified to be SC judge (qualification does not explain tenure)
- CAG — six years or age 65; removed like an SC judge
- Article 317 — UPSC and SPSC members removed only by the President after SC inquiry; Governor appoints SPSC members but cannot remove
- State Finance Commission — formed by Governor (not President); reviews finances of Panchayats and Municipalities
- CEC — removed like a Supreme Court Judge (not High Court)
- NCBC, ECI, NCSC, NCST, linguistic minorities officer — appointed by President (not PM)
- Finance Commission — members appointed by President; qualifications fixed by Parliament by law
