### Economic Survey — preparer, FIRSTs and format
- Prepared by: Economic Division, Department of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Finance, under the CEA
- FIRSTs: First Survey 1950-51; presented separately from the Budget since 1964
- Presentation: In both Houses during the Budget Session, before the Union Budget
- A convention, not a constitutional obligation (unlike the Annual Financial Statement, Art. 112) (12 Apr 2026)
- Format:
- Two volumes in the Subramanian years; single volume + statistical appendix since 2021-22
- ES 2025-26 reconfigured to 17 chapters (16 + Chapter 16 in two parts); added AI and urbanisation chapters
- ⚠ Survey = convention; Budget (AFS) = Art. 112 obligation

### Economic Survey 2025-26 — theme, chapters and statistical reforms
## 1. Theme and chapters
- Theme: "Building Strategic Indispensability" + entrepreneurial policy making in a world of geopolitical friction
- Chapter 1: "State of the Economy: Pushing the Growth Frontier"
- ES 2025-26 climate chapter: Adaptation over mitigation
- "Scarce fiscal resources" not to be diverted from health, agriculture, poverty to near-term emission cuts
- Climate change "unlikely to produce civilisational collapse"
- Conclusion: Sreya vs Preya (Katha Upanishad)
- Global scenarios for 2026: Systemic Shock Cascade (10-20%) worse than 2008
## 2. Statistical reforms
- National Accounts and IIP rebased to 2022-23 (release Feb 2026)
- ASISSE + a Household Income Survey

## 1. Fiscal and monetary response
- Fiscal response:
- Fiscal deficit 4.8% of GDP (FY25, vs 4.9% budgeted); FY26 target 4.4%
- S&P upgrade BBB- to BBB (first major upgrade in ~two decades)
- Concern at State revenue deficits + unconditional cash transfers
- Monetary response: Repo cut 125 bps since Feb 2025; GNPA at multi-decade low 2.2%
- ⚠ Potential growth 7.0% (now) vs 6.5% (2022-23 Survey); FY27 projection 6.8% - 7.2%
### Reforms and recurring frameworks — deregulation, Viksit Bharat, Ease of Living
- Reforms of 2025:
- GST overhaul; 100% FDI in insurance
- Nuclear power opened to private sector
- Four Labour Codes notified; QCOs put on hold
- Deregulation:
- ES 2023-24: "letting go" of non-essential sectors; cut licensing/inspection burden on MSMEs
- ES 2024-25: deregulation as central theme
- ES 2025-26: shift from "regulation and control" to "enabling"; trust-based compliance replacing inspection; "process reforms more important than policy reforms"
- Viksit Bharat @2047: Infrastructure as growth pillar; public capex ₹12.2 lakh crore proposed for FY2026-27
- Ease of Living:
- Budget 2026-27 pillar "Ease of Doing Business and Ease of Living"
- ES 2020-21 Bare Necessities Index measured it at State level

- Presented in Parliament (both Houses) before the Union Budget
