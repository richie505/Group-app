# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:25 - President of India - election, qualifications, term, impeachment; executive, legislative, financial, judicial powers
### Electoral college (Arts. 54, 55)
- Election: elected MLAs vote, nominated MPs do not; impeachment: nominated MPs take part, elected MLAs do not
| Votes | Does NOT vote |
| Elected MPs of both Houses | Nominated MPs and MLAs |
| Elected MLAs of States | All members of Legislative Councils |
| Elected MLAs of Delhi, Puducherry (70th Amdt, 1992) | UTs without Assemblies |
### Election method
- proportional representation by Single Transferable Vote, secret ballot; from Ireland (Art. 55(3))
- MLA vote = State population / elected MLAs x 1/1000; varies State to State
- MP vote = total MLA votes / elected MPs → parity Union-States
- Population base: 1971 Census frozen (84th Amdt)
- Nomination: 50 proposers + 50 seconders
- Disputes: Supreme Court only (Art. 71); vacancy in college no bar
### Qualifications, term, oath
- Art. 58: citizen, 35 years, qualified for Lok Sabha, no office of profit
- Term 5 years (Art. 56); re-election unlimited (Art. 57)
- Oath by CJI (Art. 60); resignation to Vice-President
- Emoluments: Consolidated Fund, Second Schedule; not diminished
### Impeachment (Art. 61)
- 1. Ground: violation of the Constitution only ↓
- 2. Charge in either House: signed by 1/4 members, 14 days' notice ↓
- 3. Passed by 2/3 of total membership ↓
- 4. Other House investigates, passes by 2/3 → impeached ↓
- 5. No President ever impeached
### President vs Vice-President
| Basis | President | Vice-President |
| Electors | Elected MPs + MLAs | All MPs (incl. nominated) |
| Qualification linked to | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
| Removal | Impeachment | RS resolution + LS agrees |
| Resigns to | VP | President |
| Vacancy | Election within 6 months; full 5-year term | No 6-month deadline (Art. 68) |
### Powers
- Executive: appoints PM, Governors, judges, CAG, AG, UPSC, ECs; Art. 78 info from PM
- Legislative: part of Parliament (Art. 79); summon, dissolve LS, joint sitting; address Arts. 86, 87; nominates 12 to RS; ordinance Art. 123
- Financial: money bills on recommendation; Contingency Fund
- Judicial: pardon Art. 72; advisory opinion Art. 143 (not binding)
- Emergency: 352 national, 356 State, 360 financial; Supreme Commander

### Election of the President — electoral college (Art 54)
## 1. Election — indirect (not directly elected)
- Electoral College — elected members of:
- Both Houses of Parliament
- Legislative Assemblies of the States
- Legislative Assemblies of Delhi and Puducherry — added by the 70th Amendment (70th Amendment, 1992)
- Do not vote: Nominated members of Parliament; members of State Legislative Councils
- Cannot vote: Nominated Members of Parliament, nominated Members of State Legislative Assemblies and Members of State Legislative Councils — the Councils are excluded from the electoral college
- Nomination: 50 proposers and 50 seconders
## 2. Composition (Art 54) — Prep detail
| Votes in President's election (Arts. 54, 55) | Does NOT vote |
| Elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Elected MPs: LS + RS) | Nominated members of Parliament (Nominated MPs of both Houses) and Assemblies |
| Elected members of State Legislative Assemblies | All members of Legislative Councils (elected and nominated) |
| Elected members (MLAs) of Delhi and Puducherry Assemblies | Other UTs without Assemblies |
- Who does not: a CM who sits in the Legislative Council cannot vote (APPSC-GS 2025 key)
- APPSC-G2 2023 key: A (elected MPs), C (elected MLAs of States), E (Delhi/Puducherry)
### Election of the President — voting system, value of vote, disputes
## 1. Voting system & value of vote
- Method: Proportional representation by Single Transferable Vote; secret ballot
- System (Art 55(3)): borrowed from Ireland
- Vote value: MLA votes → uniformity among the states; MP votes → parity between the Union and the states
- An MLA's vote varies from State to State — based on population ratios; an MP's vote is uniform across all Members of Parliament
- Principle (Art 55(1)): uniformity among States + parity between States and Union (States as a whole)
- MLA vote = (State population / elected MLAs) x 1/1000
- MP vote = total value of all MLAs' votes / total elected MPs
- Population base: 1971 Census, frozen (till the first Census after 2026 - 84th Amdt)
## 2. Disputes (Art. 71)
- Disputes: Decided by the Supreme Court — its decision is final (decided only by the Supreme Court)
- Article 71(4): The election cannot be challenged or delayed because vacancies exist in the electoral college (vacancy in the electoral college)
- Acts done: before the election is voided remain valid
### Qualifications, term, oath, resignation and emoluments
## 1. Qualifications (Article 58), term and removal
- Qualifications (of a candidate): Citizen of India; completed 35 years of age; qualified for election to the Lok Sabha; holds no office of profit
- Office of profit = under the Union, a State or any local/other authority
- Term: Five years (Article 56) (Term 5 years); eligible for re-election any number of times (Article 57) → no limit on terms (eligible for re-election)
- Oath: Administered by the Chief Justice of India (CJI) (Art 60) (in absence, senior-most SC judge)
- Swears to "preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law"
- Resignation: Addressed to the Vice-President (in writing) → informs the Speaker of the Lok Sabha
## 2. Prep detail
- Art. 59: not a member of any legislature; official residence rent-free
- Emoluments fixed by: Parliament by law; listed in the Second Schedule
- Protection: emoluments fixed by Parliament and not diminished during term
- Not in your sources: the current monthly emolument figure - add from the President's Emoluments and Pension Act as last amended.
### Impeachment (Art. 61)
## 1. Impeachment (Article 61)
- Impeachment (Article 61): For violation of the Constitution
- Charge can be preferred by either House; needs a special majority in both Houses
- ⚠ Elected MLAs take part in the election but not in the impeachment
- Ground: "violation of the Constitution" only; procedure Art. 61 (APPSC-GS 2022 key)
- Word "impeachment" used only for the President → judges and the VP are "removed"
- Procedure:
- Charges in either House: resolution signed by one-fourth of its members after 14 days' notice
- Passed by two-thirds of the total membership of the initiating House
- Other House investigates (President may appear or be represented); must also pass by 2/3 of total membership → President stands impeached
- Participation: Nominated MPs take part; elected MLAs do not
- Record: No President has ever been impeached
- ⚠ Election: elected MLAs vote, nominated MPs do not; impeachment: nominated MPs take part, elected MLAs do not
- Exam angle: "Removal of the President is under Article ___" - answer 61 (APPSC 2022).

### Vacancy and acting President — Articles 62, 65
## 1. Vacancy
- Death, resignation, removal or other vacancy: Vice-President acts as President (Article 65)
- Both offices vacant: Chief Justice of India acts — or the senior-most Supreme Court Judge — under the President's Succession Act
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the Chief Justice of India (CJI) → then senior-most SC judge under the President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969
## 2. Causes & election time limit (Art 62)
- Causes: expiry of term, death, resignation, impeachment/removal, disqualification, election declared void
- Expiry of term: election completed before the term ends (Art. 62(1))
- Outgoing President continues until successor enters office (Art. 56)
- Death, resignation, removal, disqualification, void election: election within 6 months
- New President: gets a full 5-year term
- Contrast: VP vacancy (Art 68) has no 6-month deadline
## 3. Acting President (Art 65)
- Vice-President acts: VP acts as President; while acting stops functioning as Rajya Sabha Chairman (Deputy Chairman presides)

### Powers of the President — judicial, emergency, military, financial, legislative
## 1. Judicial, emergency and military powers
- Article 72: Pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions; suspend or commute sentences
- Article 143: Advisory opinion of the Supreme Court — not binding
- Emergencies:
- National Emergency — Article 352
- President's Rule / State Emergency — Article 356
- Financial Emergency — Article 360
- War and peace: Declares war and concludes peace on the advice of the Council of Ministers
- Diplomatic/Military: treaties negotiated in his name; Supreme Commander of the armed forces
## 2. Financial and legislative powers
- Financial: budget laid; money bills/demands for grants on his recommendation; Contingency Fund advances
- Legislative:
- President is part of Parliament (Art. 79) → no Bill becomes law without assent
- Sessions: summon/prorogue both Houses; dissolve Lok Sabha; summon joint sitting (presided by Speaker)
- Address: under Arts. 86, 87 (first session after general election + first session of each year); send messages
- Other: nominates 12 to RS; prior recommendation for Art. 3 and money bills; ordinances (Art. 123)
### Position of the President — Article 74, amendments and limited discretion
## 1. Limited discretion
- Hung Parliament: May appoint a Prime Minister
- Lost majority: May dismiss the government
- Dissolving the Lok Sabha; the pocket veto
- Return advice of the Council of Ministers for reconsideration once — added by the 44th Amendment
## 2. Article 74 and amendments
- Nature: President = nominal/de jure executive; PM = real/de facto executive
- Council of Ministers headed by PM "to aid and advise the President"
- Art 74(1): Council of Ministers aids and advises
- 42nd Amendment (1976): advice binding
- 44th Amendment (1978): may send back once for reconsideration → then must act on advice
- Art 74(2): advice tendered cannot be inquired into by any court
- ⚠ 42nd = advice binding; 44th = one reconsideration, then binding
### Pardoning power — terms and Article 72 scope
## 1. Pardoning terms
- Pardon: Completely absorbs and removes both the sentence and the conviction — the offender is in the same position as if the offence had never been committed
- Commutation: Substitutes a lighter form of punishment for a heavier one — e.g. death sentence → rigorous life imprisonment
- Remission: Reduces the period of a sentence without changing its character — e.g. two years of rigorous imprisonment → one year
- Respite: Awards a lesser sentence in place of the original penalty, due to special personal circumstances — pregnancy, mental or physical disability
- Reprieve: A temporary stay / postponement of execution — especially for the death penalty — giving time to seek a Presidential pardon
- ⚠ Commutation = lighter form; remission = shorter period, same character (Reduces the period)
## 2. Article 72 scope
- Art 72 scope: pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions, or suspend, remit or commute sentence of any person convicted of an offence where:
- (a) sentence by a court-martial
- (b) offence against a Union law
- (c) death sentence
- Limits: limited to Art 72(1)(a)-(c) and Union executive matters; amnesty outside Art 72
### Pardoning power — Article 72 vs 161 and cases
## 1. Article 72 vs Article 161 (Governor)
| Point | President (Art. 72) | Governor (Art. 161) |

### Election of the President — electoral college (Art 54)
## Electoral college
- ★ Elected members of both Houses of Parliament + elected members of State Legislative Assemblies
- ★ 70th Amendment — added elected members of NCT Delhi and Puducherry Assemblies; nominated members excluded
- ★ Elected members of State Legislative Councils — not included
- ★ State Legislative Assemblies — in the electoral college but not in impeachment (only Parliament, Article 61)
## Nature
- ★ Indirect election; PR by STV; secret ballot
- ★ Indirect because the electoral college has elected MPs and elected MLAs (R explains A)
- ★ Election cannot be delayed because the electoral college is incomplete; a dissolved Assembly cannot take part
### Election of the President — voting system, value of vote, disputes
- ★ Article 55 — manner of election; electoral college by PR through single transferable vote; nominated members of Parliament or Assemblies not in the electoral college
- ★ India uses direct election on adult suffrage and PR by single transferable vote (President, VP, Rajya Sabha, Legislative Council: indirect PR-STV)
- ★ Proportional representation gives representation of minorities
- ★ Elected MLAs vote for both Lok Sabha (as citizens) and Rajya Sabha, and for the President
