### Background to the 1773 Act
- 1773 began Parliamentary control; 1784 set the dual system that lasted until 1858
- 2. 1765: Shah Alam grants Diwani of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa ↓

### Regulating Act, 1773
- First step by British Parliament to control the Company
- Recognised Company's political and administrative functions
- Governor of Bengal → GG of Bengal; Bombay and Madras subordinated
- Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774): 1 Chief Justice + 3 judges; civil + criminal
- Court of Directors to report revenue, civil, military affairs to British Government
- Did not abolish the Court of Directors

### Regulating Act, 1773 — background, committees and FIRSTs
- Background: after Buxar (1764), Shah Alam granted the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa to the Company in 1765
- 1772 committees: Parliament appointed a Select Committee (Burgoyne) + a Secret Committee to inquire into Company affairs → reports led to the Regulating Act
- FIRST - significance: First step by the British Parliament to control the Company; recognised its political and administrative functions
- Exam angle: "incorrect statement" - it did not abolish the Court of Directors (APPSC-GS 2019).
### Acts → year → provisions (1773, 1781, 1786, 1793)
## 1. Regulating Act, 1773
- Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774): 1 Chief Justice + 3 judges; civil + criminal jurisdiction
- Reporting: Court of Directors to report on revenue, civil and military affairs to the British Government
## 2. Amending Act, 1781
- Other name: Act of Settlement
- Exemption: Governor-General, Council and Company servants exempted from Supreme Court jurisdiction for acts in their official capacity
- Also:
- Revenue matters excluded from the Court's jurisdiction
- Court told to respect the personal laws of Hindus and Muslims
## 3. Act of 1786
- Why passed: to meet Lord Cornwallis's condition for accepting office
- Powers: GG could override his council in special cases + also be Commander-in-Chief
- Extension: Charter Act of 1793 extended the overriding power to the governors of the presidencies ("greater control over Bombay and Madras")
- ⚠ 1781 = Act of Settlement (curbs Supreme Court); 1786 = override power (Cornwallis)
### Defects of 1773 → remedies
- Defects: of the Regulating Act 1773
| Defect | Remedy |
| GG had no veto; could be outvoted by his council | Act of 1786 - override power |
| Supreme Court's jurisdiction over the Council and over Indians undefined → Council-Court clashes | 1781 Act of Settlement - official acts and revenue exempted |
- Context: the 1786 Act corrected the 1773 defect under which Hastings was repeatedly outvoted
### Universal angles
- Significance: 1773 began Parliamentary control; 1784 set the dual system that lasted until 1858, when the GoI Act ended "Double Government"
- AP angle - judicial system in Andhra:
- Madras Supreme Court (1801) for British subjects

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:173
### Regulating Act, 1773
## Background and the Act
- Select Committee of 1772 on Company affairs — headed by Burgoyne (with a Secret Committee)
- Regulating Act, 1773 — first parliamentary step to control the East India Company; recognised its political and administrative roles
- Did not abolish the Court of Directors; Directors had to report to the British Government on revenue, civil and military affairs
- Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774) — one Chief Justice and three judges; civil and criminal jurisdiction
## Act of 1786
- Passed to meet Lord Cornwallis's condition for accepting office: GG could override his Council and act as Commander-in-Chief
- Fixed the 1773 defect of the GG (incl. Hastings) being repeatedly outvoted by his Council
## Not a British-era landmark
- ★ Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 — post-independence; earlier the Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955; amended and renamed in 1976
### Acts compared
- ★ Regulating Act, 1773 — set up India's first Supreme Court at Fort William, Calcutta; Chief Justice and three puisne judges
- ★ Sir Elijah Impey — first Chief Justice
- Order: Regulating Act 1773 → Amending Act 1781 → Pitt's India Act 1784 → Act of 1786
- Amending Act, 1781 (Act of Settlement) — Court to respect Hindu and Muslim personal laws; exempted official acts
- Double Government of Pitt's India Act (1784) — ended by the Government of India Act, 1858
### Defects of 1773 → remedies
- Under the 1773 Act the GG had no veto and could be outvoted by his Council; override power came only with the Act of 1786
### Exam corner
- Ministry of Law and Justice (oldest organ of GoI) — traces origin to the Charter Act of 1833 (Law Member, first Law Commission)
### Acts → year → provisions (1773, 1781, 1786, 1793)
- ★ Regulating Act — passed 1773; appointed a Governor-General of Fort William in Bengal with supervisory powers
- ★ Act of 1786 — let Lord Cornwallis overrule his Council (right to reject the Council's decision); passed to get Cornwallis to accept the post
- Cornwallis wanted the powers of both Governor-General and Commander-in-Chief
