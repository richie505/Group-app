### Terms → meaning (employment elasticity, jobless growth, gig work, feminisation)
## 1. Employment elasticity
- High: Growth generates relatively more employment; low: growth generates relatively less employment
- Employment elasticity = % change in employment / % change in output (GDP); low or near-zero value → jobless growth
## 2. Other terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Jobless growth | At the height of economic growth, unemployment of labour increases (APPSC 2019); term coined by Nick Perna, early 1990s |
| "Missing middle" | Services-led path skipping manufacturing stage |
| Gig economy | Independent contractors/freelancers instead of permanent workers (APPSC 2019); delivery, ride-sharing, freelancing |
| U-shaped hypothesis | FLFPR falls with early income growth → rises later with education + service jobs |
| Feminisation of agriculture | Basic reason = growing rural-to-urban migration of men (APPSC 2018) |
- ⚠ Jobless growth coined by Nick Perna (early 1990s) - not an Indian economist
### Jobless growth — post-1991 causes and scale
- Post-1991 reasons:
- Growth driven by capital-intensive sectors (IT, finance), not labour-intensive manufacturing
- Services-led path skipping manufacturing stage ("missing middle")
- Employment growth lagged labour-force growth (creeping unemployment, mostly unorganised sector)
- Scale: GDP grew 6-7% but formal job creation lagged
- About 12 million persons enter the job market each year; some notes say 7-10 million young Indians a year (15 May 2026)
