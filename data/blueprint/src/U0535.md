### SEBI — establishment, Act, composition and functions
## 1. Basics
- SEBI: Securities and Exchange Board of India — the stock exchange regulator in India
- Initially established in 1988 as a non-statutory body; statutory status on 30 January 1992 under the SEBI Act, 1992 — its parent law
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say SEBI was set up on 12 April 1988 (non-statutory) and got statutory powers on 30 January 1992, SEBI Act, 1992; statutory powers came with the 1991-92 Narasimham-era reforms (some notes say it was set up on Narasimham's advice - that committee came only in 1991)
- Headquarters: Mumbai
## 2. Purpose and objectives
- Core purposes / main objectives: Investor protection — protect investors; promote, develop and regulate the securities market
## 3. Establishment facts (Prep)
| Item | Fact |
| Statutory powers | Took over from the Controller of Capital Issues |
| Chairperson | Tuhin Kanta Pandey (took charge 2025; TH, Mar 2026) |
| Ministry | Ministry of Finance |
- ⚠ 1988 = set up (non-statutory); 1992 = statutory under the SEBI Act
- Board composition:
- 2 members from Finance + Law ministries
- 1 from RBI
- Functions: Registration, fees, investor education, inspection/audit
- Mandate: Govt gave regulatory powers to SEBI to prevent capital-market scams (APPSC 2025 key)

### SEBI powers and the Securities Appellate Tribunal
## 1. SEBI's powers

## 2. Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)
- Basis: Under the SEBI Act; set up 1995 (some notes say 1992)
- Composition: Presiding Officer + members
- Jurisdiction: Hears appeals against SEBI and exchanges
- Further appeal: SAT → Supreme Court
- NSE case: SC disposed of SEBI's appeals against NSE (co-location, dark fibre) after a ₹1,492 crore settlement (₹1,224 cr + ₹268 cr)

### Recent SEBI reforms and the Securities Markets Code Bill, 2025
## 1. Market reforms
- T+0 (same-day) settlement: Optional, alongside T+1; India among the first globally
- Goals: liquidity, lower settlement and counterparty risk, efficiency (APPSC-G2 2025 key: I, II, III)
- BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report):
- Prescribed by SEBI (not RBI) for the top 1,000 listed companies
- Replaced the BRR
- BRSR Core needs reasonable assurance on key ESG metrics
- SME IPOs: Surged 87% (FY23-25); SEBI chief says SME market is under-scaled + lacks intermediaries
- Finfluencers:
- Stock tips/return claims = advisory work needing registration
- Regulated entities cannot work with unregistered finfluencers
- SEBI-Google verified "tick mark" for broker apps
## 2. Securities Markets Code Bill, 2025
- Introduced: Lok Sabha, Dec 2025
- Merges: SEBI Act 1992, SCRA 1956, Depositories Act 1996
- Changes: SEBI board up to 15; MIIs get statutory status
- Not in your sources: whether the Securities Markets Code has been passed or notified (check Aug-Sep 2026 news).
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: SEBI-NSE ₹1,492 crore settlement closed the co-location case
- AP angle: AP MSME Development Corporation + NSE ran an SME IPO awareness programme in Vijayawada
# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:91
### Establishment
- SEBI — set up 12 April 1988 as a non-statutory body; statutory from 30 January 1992; took over from the Controller of Capital Issues
- Order: SEBI non-statutory (1988) → statutory (April 1992) → NSE set up (November 1992) → NSE recognised (April 1993)
- Securities Appellate Tribunal — 1995, under the SEBI Act
- Forward Markets Commission — set up 1953, regulated commodity futures; merged into SEBI in 2015
- Securities Markets Code Bill, 2025 (Lok Sabha, December 2025) — merges SEBI Act 1992, SCRA 1956, Depositories Act 1996
- SEBI Act Preamble: investor protection, development and regulation of the securities market (bank lending rates are RBI's)
- Primary market — new securities; secondary — existing ones
### Structure
- Tuhin Kanta Pandey — SEBI Chairperson from 2025
- SEBI Board has one member from the RBI
- SEBI powers: investigative — inspection, search, seizure; quasi-legislative — framing regulations; quasi-judicial — orders, penalties, disgorgement
- SAT — hears appeals against SEBI and stock exchanges; further appeal to the Supreme Court
- PFUTP Regulations — ban fraudulent and unfair trade practices → investor protection
### Regulator map
- PFRDA — pensions; SEBI — capital market; RBI — money and banking; NABARD — agricultural credit
### SEBI regulates
- ★ Credit rating agencies in India: CRISIL, CARE, ICRA

- Market Infrastructure Institutions (SEBI): stock exchanges, clearing corporations, depositories
- SEBI regulates mutual funds, AIFs and FPIs
