### Universal angles
## 1. Significance
- Significance: First great direct threat to British rule; heroes entered folklore, invoked in the Quit India and INA phases
- Government of India Act 1858: Power to the Crown; created a Secretary of State; Governor-General made a Viceroy
## 2. Criticism & debates
- Criticism & debates: Failure - Lack of coordination, central leadership, programme and resources; narrow/limited territorial and social base; urban elites and big princes stayed away
- Hindu-Muslim unity: Later cited against the two-nation theory
## 3. AP angle
- AP angle: Nizam's loyalty - Kept the Deccan quiet
- Local risings: Turrebaz Khan (Hyderabad); Korukonda Subba Reddy (Errannagudem); Dandasena (Paralakimidi) - latter two both August 1857
## 4. FIRST/LAST
- FIRST/LAST: First outbreak - Barrackpore (29 Mar 1857)
- Great outbreak: Meerut (10 May 1857)
- Last major leader executed: Tantia Tope

### Centres & leaders
- General Bakht Khan — real military leader at Delhi; came from Bareilly (reached 3 July 1857); led a 10-member 'Court of Soldiers'
- Begum Hazrat Mahal (Lucknow) — declared her son Birjis Qadr ruler of Awadh
- Kunwar Singh — Arrah (Bihar); Hazrat Mahal — Lucknow; Nana Saheb — Kanpur; Bahadur Shah Zafar — Delhi
- Tantia Tope — mainly Central India; Bakht Khan — Delhi; Hazrat Mahal — Awadh
- Order of rising: Barrackpore (29 March) → Delhi (11 May) → Lucknow (4 June) → Kanpur (5 June)
