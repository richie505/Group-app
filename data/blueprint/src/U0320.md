### Federalism — definitions, types and FIRSTs — Federalism - meaning
## 1. Federalism - meaning
- Definition: sharing of power between the Union and State governments, each supreme in its sphere, under a written constitution with an independent umpire (APPSC-GS 2018 answer: federalism)
- vs Unitary: units draw power from the Centre
- vs Confederation: loose league of sovereign States that keep right to secede
## 2. Unitary government - characteristics, merits and demerits
- Characteristics: one supreme central government; local units are its creations with delegated powers; no constitutional division of powers
- Merits: uniform laws and policy; quick decisions; strong crisis response
- Demerits: over-centralisation; local needs neglected; poorly suited to large, diverse countries
- Hence India is "federal in structure with unitary features" (IYB 2026)
## 3. Types of federation - holding-together vs coming-together
| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| Coming-together | independent States pool sovereignty, keep equal power | USA, Switzerland, Australia |
| Holding-together | large country divides power among units to hold diversity together; stronger Centre | India, Spain, Belgium |
### Federalism — definitions, types and FIRSTs — Genesis of the Indian federation
## 1. Genesis of the Indian federation
- USA = federation by agreement of states (integration)
- India = unitary colonial system converted to federal → "centripetal bias" (CDI view: India's federation formed by "disintegration" vs US by "integration")
- National-integration design: framed after Partition + integration of 14 provinces and 500+ princely States → centralisation looked indispensable (TH 17 Feb 2026)
- ⚠ Integration = USA (coming together); disintegration = India (holding together)

| Canada, Australia, Argentina | France, Italy, Japan, China |

### Federal vs unitary features — statement-based identification — Exam statements
## 1. Judicial review - a federal, not unitary, element
- Federal role: Reinforces the federal principle, preserves the distribution of powers, enforces constitutional limits — it is not a unitary element
- Unitary elements: All India Services, the Governor's appointment, Emergency provisions
- What judicial review is not: Not questioning the political wisdom, policy merits or advisability of legislation
## 2. Exam statements
- Exam angle: PYQ 2025 - Governor, Central Services, CAG and Emergency powers all "violate federal principles" (answer: A, B, C and D).
- Exam angle: assertion-reason - A: India is quasi-federal; R: residuary powers/emergency powers lie with the Centre.
- Exam angle: "not a federal feature" answers are Office of the Governor (APPSC-G1 2018) and CAG (APPSC-G2 2017). "Allocation of financial resources" is not a prominent federal feature (APPSC-G2 2017).
## 3. Federal system with unitary bias - form of government
- Parliamentary form (British model):
- Nominal head = President
- Real executive = Council of Ministers under the PM, collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Art. 74(1), 75)

### Articles 1-4, 370 and 371 → Union of States and asymmetric federalism — Indestructible Union
## 1. Indestructible Union
- No "Federation": the word "Federation" is not used anywhere = "Federation" is nowhere used in the Constitution
- Article 1(1) (Art. 1): "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States"
- States and UTs specified in First Schedule
- Territory = States + UTs + territories acquired
- Integral whole: divided into States only for administrative convenience
## 2. Destructible States
- Article 3: Parliament can form new States, alter areas, boundaries or names by simple majority
- Bill needs President's recommendation + reference to the state legislature, whose views are not binding
- Article 4 (Art. 4): such laws amend the First and Fourth Schedules; not Art. 368 amendments
- Result: no territorial guarantee → "an indestructible Union of destructible States"
- Contrast: USA = "indestructible Union of indestructible states"
- ⚠ India = indestructible Union of destructible States; USA = indestructible Union of indestructible states

### Emergency provisions → switching from federal to unitary mode — Mode switch
## 1. Framework
- Part XVIII: Articles 352 to 360
- Federation becomes unitary without a formal amendment
- K.M. Nambiar: Called the President's emergency powers a "fraud on the Constitution"
- Unique feature: change of mode needs no constitutional amendment
## 2. Mode switch
| Situation | Unitary effect |
| National Emergency (Art. 352) - war, external aggression, armed rebellion | See effects table below |
| Art. 249 - Rajya Sabha resolution (2/3 of members present and voting) in national interest | Parliament legislates on a State List subject (resolution valid 1 year, renewable) |
| Art. 356 - failure of constitutional machinery | President's Rule; Parliament/President exercise the State's powers |
| Financial Emergency (Art. 360) | See Financial Emergency below |
### National Emergency - proclamation and effects
## 1. Proclamation
- Only on the written advice of the Union Cabinet
- 1975 Emergency: Proclaimed 25 June 1975 on the ground of Internal Disturbance; withdrawn 21 March 1977
## 2. Effects on legislation and finance
- State List: Parliament gets overriding jurisdiction (Art. 250) — such laws cease 6 months after the Emergency is revoked
- Terms of the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies: Parliament may extend them by law 1 year at a time — at most 6 months after the Emergency ceases
- Article 354: The President may modify the distribution of revenues between the Union and the States — valid until the end of the financial year in which the proclamation ceases
- i.e. modify Centre-State distribution of revenues: reduce or cancel transfers to States (subject to Parliament's approval)
## 3. Other effects
| Effect of National Emergency (Art. 352) | Detail |
| Executive | Union's executive power extends to directing any State on how it exercises its executive power |
| Fundamental Rights | Art. 19 suspended only in an external emergency (Art. 358); other rights suspended by order under Art. 359, except Arts. 20 and 21 |
### National Emergency - safeguards (44th Amendment)
## 1. 44th Amendment Act, 1978
- Replaced "Internal Disturbance" with "Armed Rebellion" in Article 352
- ⚠ Trap: "internal disturbance" is no longer a ground (APPSC-GS 2018)
- Cut the parliamentary approval time for a National Emergency from 2 months to 1 month
- Established the non-suspendability of Articles 20 and 21 — they can never be suspended under Article 359
## 2. Other safeguards
- Proclamation: only on written advice of the Union Cabinet
- Parliament approval: within 1 month
- Special sitting: notice by 1/10th of Lok Sabha members → sitting within 14 days to consider disapproval
- Rights: Arts 20-21 made non-suspendable
- Judicial review: 38th Amendment barred it → Minerva Mills reopened it
### Emergency provisions → switching from federal to unitary mode — Financial Emergency (Art. 360)
## 1. Financial Emergency (Art. 360)
- Centre's powers:
- Direct States to follow canons of financial propriety (Centre directs financial propriety)
- Require State money bills to be reserved for the President
- Reduce salaries of Union employees and SC/HC judges
- Exam angle: the President can reduce or cancel fund transfers, but "increase the transfer" is the wrong statement (APPSC-G2 2017). Modifying the distribution of finances and giving directions to States are correct consequences (APPSC-G2 2023).
### Emergency provisions → switching from federal to unitary mode — Emergency types compared - Arts 352, 356, 360
## 1. Comparison
| Basis | National Emergency — Article 352 | President's Rule — Article 356 | Financial Emergency — Article 360 |
| Ground | War, external aggression or armed rebellion ("internal disturbance" replaced by the 44th Amendment, 1978) | Failure of constitutional machinery in a State; Art 365 | Threat to the financial stability or credit of India or any part |
| Approval by Parliament | Within 1 month (was 2 months — reduced by the 44th Amendment) | Within 2 months | Within 2 months |
| Majority | Special majority in both Houses | Simple majority | Simple majority |
| Duration | 6 months at a time (renewable every 6 months) — no maximum limit | 6 months at a time — maximum 3 years; beyond 1 year only with a national emergency plus ECI certification (Art. 356(5)) | Until revoked by the President |
| Lok Sabha dissolved | Survives 30 days from the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha | 30 days | 30 days |
| Effect | Parliament legislates on the State List; Articles 358 and 359 operate | State Assembly dissolved or suspended; Parliament passes the State Budget | Executive gives financial directions; salaries may be cut — including Supreme Court and High Court judges |
| Fundamental Rights | Articles 20 and 21 can never be suspended | Not directly suspended | Not directly suspended |
| Used | 3 times — 1962, 1971, 1975 | Over 100 times across States; used many times since 1951 | Never invoked since independence (Never declared) |
- ⚠ Approval: 352 = 1 month; 356 and 360 = 2 months
- Exam angle: "Art. 311 - Financial Emergency" is the mismatch; Financial Emergency is Art. 360.

### Federal vs unitary features
## Federal and unitary systems
- Federalism — Union and States share power, each supreme in its own sphere under a written Constitution, with an independent umpire
- Independent judiciary as umpire in Union-State disputes — an essential feature of federalism

- Unitary government: no constitutional division of powers; local units get power from the Centre
- Unitary merits: uniform laws, quick decisions, strong crisis response; regional diversity through autonomous governments is a federal trait
- United Kingdom — unitary; Parliament holds all law-making power
