### Achaemenid rulers
- Cyrus — founder of the Achaemenid Empire; first to campaign into the Indian north-west (Gandhara)
- Darius I (522–486 BC) — annexed Punjab and Sindh west of the Indus; became the twentieth satrapy
- Sudas (Bharatas) — fought a confederacy of ten kings; Rigveda Mandala VII, near the Parushni (Ravi)
### Persian legacy
- Herodotus — Indian satrapy most populous and fertile; tribute of 360 talents of gold dust (one-third of empire's revenue)
- Kharosthi (from Aramaic) — written right to left
- Ashokan edicts — terms lipi / dipi; third-person opening shifting to first person (Darius model); Aramaic edicts, satrapy system
- Persian contact (6th–5th c. BC) — lasting influence on script and art; Alexander's nineteen-month campaign — almost no lasting political mark
### Why Alexander succeeded
- North-west divided into small independent states — Taxila, Porus's Punjab, Gandhara
- Help from Indian allies like Ambhi, political disunity, superior army
- Porus (defeated at the Hydaspes, 326 BC) — not executed; kingdom restored and later enlarged
- Twelve stone altars — northern bank of the Beas; army refused to go beyond the Beas (not the Ravi)
- Withdrew down the Indus; Nearchus led the fleet
- Conquered area divided into three parts under Greek governors; most states returned to rulers
