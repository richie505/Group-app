### Free nationwide HPV vaccination for adolescent girls
## 1. Programme
- Union Health Ministry — HPV vaccination for girls aged 14; voluntary and free
- Vaccine: Gardasil (quadrivalent) — HPV 16 & 18 (cervical cancer) + HPV 6 & 11 (warts); single-dose (optional second dose after 3–5 years)
- Procured via Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance; ⚠ indigenous Cervavac not yet WHO-approved for programme
- Based on NTAGI recommendation; only at government facilities (Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, CHCs, hospitals, medical colleges); tracked on U-WIN
## 2. Burden
- Cervical cancer — 2nd most common cancer among Indian women; ~80,000 cases, 42,000+ deaths a year; HPV 16/18 cause >80%
- ⚠ Vaccine does not replace screening — Pap smear from 21 years; HPV DNA test after 30
- WHO 90–70–90 targets by 2030 (WHA 73.2): 90% girls vaccinated by 15; 70% women screened (35 and 45); 90% treated
- Mission Indradhanush — immunisation of children and pregnant women

### VB-G RAM G Bill, 2025 to replace MGNREGA
## 1. News
- Bill: Viksit Bharat–Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Bill — VB-G RAM G — to replace MGNREGA (2005)
- Called: largest structural change in 20 years
## 2. MGNREGA vs VB-G RAM G
| Feature | MGNREGA | VB-G RAM G |
| Guaranteed days | 100 (up to 150 in special cases) | 125 (Section 5(1)) |
| Wage funding | Centre 100% | 60:40; 90:10 for NE and Himalayan states |
| Allocation | Bottom-up labour budget from Gram Panchayat | Normative — set by Centre |
| Agriculture pause | None | 60 days in peak sowing/harvest |
| Cards | Job cards valid 5 years | Gramin Rozgar Guarantee Cards valid 3 years |
| Penalty (Section 27) | ₹1,000 | ₹10,000 |
- Cost: central ₹95,692 crore; total ₹1.51 lakh crore; extra burden on states about ₹30,000 crore/year
- Average MGNREGA employment: about 50 days per household
- Planning: Viksit Gram Panchayat Plans — water security, core rural infrastructure, livelihood infrastructure, extreme weather mitigation; integrated with PM Gati Shakti
- Special cards: single women, PwDs, elderly, released bonded labourers, PVTGs, transgender persons
## 3. Data
- Rural female LFPR: 24.6% (2017–18) → 47.6% (2023–24)
- Nominal rural wages (2015–25): grew only 3.6–6.4% a year (Labour Bureau)
- MGNREGA eligibility: adult members of any rural household
### National Action Plan on AMR 2.0 (2025–29)
## 1. News
- Launched: NAP-AMR 2.0 for 2025–29; earlier NAP-AMR 1.0 in 2017
- Approach: One Health — human, animal and environmental health interlinked
- Governance: inter-sectoral supervision under NITI Aayog via a Coordination & Monitoring Committee; States urged to set up State AMR Cells; national dashboard proposed
- NAP 1.0 State plans: only Kerala, MP, Delhi, AP, Gujarat, Sikkim, Punjab
## 2. Key facts
- Watch-group antibiotics: about 59% of consumption (2022); about 8 in 10 hospital entrants carry drug-resistant bacteria
- Animal antibiotics: India 4th largest consumer; use to rise about 82% by 2030
- Musi River (Hyderabad): antibiotic levels about 1,000× safe limits
- Newborns: 50,000+ deaths/year from resistant sepsis; India has the highest MDR-TB burden
## 3. Terms and measures
| Term | Meaning |
| NDM-1 | New Delhi Metallo-β-lactamase — resistance to β-lactams incl. carbapenems |
| Schedule H1 | Drugs & Cosmetics Rules — select antibiotics only on prescription, register kept |
| Red Line Campaign | Red vertical line on antibiotic strips |
| Delhi Declaration on AMR | 2017 inter-ministerial commitment |
| Chennai Declaration | Medical societies' consensus on rational antibiotic use |
| Kerala | PROUD and AMRITH initiatives |
- Ban: colistin in animal feed
- Resistance mechanisms: limiting uptake, target modification, efflux pumps, drug inactivation (β-lactamases)
### ULLAS literacy scheme and Bihar's non-participation
## 1. News
- Goal: 100% literacy by 2030 (NEP 2020, SDG-4)
- Issue: Bihar refuses to join ULLAS; runs own Akshar Anchal scheme (15 years; Dalits, Mahadalits, minorities, EBCs, women 15–45)
## 2. ULLAS
- Full form: Understanding Lifelong Learning for All in Society; launched 2022 by Union Education Ministry
- Target: non-literates aged 15+; literacy up to Class 3 level; test → certification
- Benchmark (August 2024): 95% literacy = 100% "fully literate"
- Literacy definition 2024: reading, writing, numeracy + digital, financial literacy, life skills
- Fully literate declared: Himachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Goa, Tripura, Ladakh
## 3. Data (PLFS 2023–24)
- ⚠ Andhra Pradesh lowest literacy — 72.6%; Bihar 2nd lowest — 74.3% (male 82.3%, female 66.1%)
- Bihar non-literates (15–59): about 2 crore, incl. 1.32 crore women; Census 2011 literacy 61.8% (lowest then)
- Bihar funds 2023–24: ₹35 crore (Centre ₹21 crore, State ₹14 crore) — zero utilisation; 7% interest penalty

## 2. Key facts
- Watch-group antibiotics: about 59% of consumption (2022); about 8 in 10 hospital entrants carry drug-resistant bacteria

- Newborns: 50,000+ deaths/year from resistant sepsis; India has the highest MDR-TB burden
