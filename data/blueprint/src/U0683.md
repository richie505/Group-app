### Transmission media and submarine cables → features and links
## 1. Optical fibres
- Structure: The core has a higher refractive index than the cladding (which has a lower one); the angle of incidence at the core-cladding interface must satisfy i > C
- Advantages: Low loss; high bandwidth; immune to interference
- Uses:
- Telecommunication — internet, telephone and cable-TV signals
- Medical endoscopy — viewing internal organs; precise laser delivery in surgery
- Sensors — temperature, pressure, strain
- Military and aircraft — lightweight data transmission
- Decorative and medical illumination
- Endoscope: A bundle of optical fibres using TIR — one bundle for illumination, one for image transmission; flexible enough for body cavities; non-invasive or minimally invasive viewing
## 2. Medium → signal → capacity
| Medium | Signal | Speed/capacity |
| Optical fibre | Light pulses by total internal reflection | Fastest, highest bandwidth (low loss, immune to electromagnetic interference - see above) [GK] |
| Copper (twisted pair/coaxial) | Electrical signals | Lower bandwidth, loss over distance, interference-prone [GK] |
| Satellite | Microwave via satellite | Reaches remote areas; high latency for geostationary orbit (~36,000 km) [GK] |
- OFC: Backbone of internet and data connectivity
- Exam angle: "fastest form of data transmission" = optical fibre (APPSC 2025).
