### Depth mapping, SONAR and ocean zones
## 1. SONAR — Sound Navigation And Ranging
- Echo method — reflection of ultrasonic waves: sends ultrasonic pulses toward an object, which reflects them; measures the time interval between sending and receiving
- Why ultrasound: High frequency, less diffraction, greater directionality
- Uses: Detecting submarines (a military use); ocean depth; mapping the seabed; locating fish (fishing industry); underwater navigation by ships and submarines; locating wrecks and sunken ships or objects; limited use in underwater communication
## 2. Depth mapping
| Term | Meaning |
| Bathymetry | Measuring ocean depth + study and mapping of the sea floor (APPSC GS 2018) |
| Fathom | Unit of depth (APPSC GS 2019) = 6 feet (1.83 m; ~1.8 m) [GK] |
| Echo sounding / sonar | Times a sound pulse to the floor and back; depth = speed of sound in water x half the time for the echo to return [GK] |
| Isobaths | Lines joining points of equal depth [GK] |
| Hypsometric curve | Graph of the share of earth's surface at each height and depth, land and sea [GK] |
| CTD sensors | Measure conductivity (salinity), temperature and depth; in 2026 fitted even to sharks for hurricane forecasting |
## 3. Ocean zones
| Zone | Extent |
| Pelagic | Open-ocean water column beyond the shelf [GK] |
| Mesopelagic ("twilight zone") | 200-1,000 m - lanternfishes, deep-sea shrimps; drives the biological carbon pump (CMLRE report, Aug 2026) |
| Benthic / hadal | Sea floor / trenches [GK] |

### Ocean deposits → types and make-up
- Terrigenous deposits: From the land (gravel, sand, silt, mud); laid (settle) mainly on the continental shelves and slopes (APPSC G1 2024, statement I correct)
- Coarseness decreases away from shore: coarse near coast → fine mud farther out (get finer away from the shore)
- ⚠ Trap: "Coarseness increases seaward" was the wrong statement (APPSC G1 2024)
- Pelagic deposits: Deep-sea oozes (calcareous, siliceous) + red clay; cover the deep-sea plains and deeps
| Pelagic type | Make-up |
| Calcareous ooze (globigerina, pteropod) | Lime shells of tiny organisms [GK] |
| Siliceous ooze (diatom, radiolarian) | Silica skeletons; colder or deeper water [GK] |
| Red clay | Fine inorganic clay and volcanic dust; the most widespread deep-sea deposit, largest in the deep Pacific [GK] |
### Ocean temperature — horizontal distribution; density
## 1. Density
- Depends mainly on temperature and salinity
- Higher salinity generally produces higher density; lower temperature generally produces higher density
## 2. Temperature — horizontal distribution
- Latitude: Falls from the equator to the poles; enclosed tropical seas are warmest
- Hemispheres: Northern hemisphere oceans warmer than southern (more land)
- Tropical zone: Western side warmer than eastern
- West: warm currents along continents' east coasts; trade winds pile warm water westward
- Temperate zone: Westerlies make eastern sections warmer (reversed; e.g. North Atlantic Drift)
- Annual range: Smaller in the vast Pacific than in the Atlantic
- ⚠ Tropics: west warmer; temperate: east warmer
### Ocean temperature — vertical distribution and heat; salinity factors
## 1. Temperature
- Ocean surface temperature: Mainly controlled by latitude and insolation
- Generally decreases with depth
| Basis | Surface ocean water | Deep ocean water |
| Temperature | Relatively warm | Colder |
| Density | Less dense | Denser |
- Ocean temperature influences ocean density, ocean circulation and marine life
## 2. Vertical distribution and heat
- Layers: Warm surface layer (to ~500 m; 20-25 degC in the tropics [GK]) → thermocline (temperature drops fast with depth, ~500-1,000 m) → cold deep layer near 0-4 deg C
- Heat uptake: Oceans take up >90% of excess heat
- Marine heatwave: SST 3-4 deg C above average for at least 5 days
## 3. Salinity — definition and factors
- Definition: Grams of dissolved salt per 1,000 g of water (per mille / ppt)
- Marine water = ~35 ppt; brackish water = 5-35 ppt
- Raised by: Evaporation + freezing
- Lowered by: Precipitation, river inflow + ice melt
- Pattern: Highest in subtropics (~20-30 deg); lower at the rainy equator
### Water bodies → salinity ranking
| Water body | Salinity (per mille) | Why / note |
| Lake Van (Turkey) | 330 | Closed soda lake - the world's largest alkaline lake |
| Dead Sea | 238 | Closed basin, intense evaporation |
| Great Salt Lake (Utah) | 220 | Closed basin |
| Red Sea | ~41 (among the saltiest seas) | High evaporation, little river inflow |
| Black Sea | 17-18 ppt | About half the ocean average |
| Baltic Sea | Lowest (under ~10) - among the least saline | Many rivers, low evaporation, cold |
- Exam angle: the APPSC G2 2023 key is Lake Van > Dead Sea > Great Salt Lake (NCERT figures).

### Ocean bottom relief
- ★ Oil deposits — mostly on the continental shelf (shallow, sediment-rich)
- Depth order: oceanic deeps (deepest) > deep-sea plains > continental slopes > continental shelves
- Deep-sea (abyssal) plain — flattest, smoothest surface; polymetallic nodules
- Oceanic deeps/trenches — mostly in the Pacific; linked with volcanoes and earthquakes; at subduction zones
- Deep sea below 200 m — Earth's oldest and largest biome (about 90–95% of habitable space)
- Terrigenous deposits — land gravel, sand, silt, mud on shelves and slopes; finer away from shore
- Yearly temperature range smaller in the Pacific than the Atlantic — it is vast and larger, heats and cools slowly
