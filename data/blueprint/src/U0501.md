### MSP — concept, purpose and authority
## 1. MSP
- MSP: Minimum Support Price — a government-announced minimum price for selected agricultural commodities
- Floor price announced before each Rabi/Kharif sowing season
- Purpose: Protect farmers against a sharp fall in market prices
- Protects farmers from price crashes / distress sales in bumper years
- Market price < MSP → government agencies buy the stock offered
- A market intervention by the government — a price support mechanism; provides a remunerative and stable price environment
- MSP-based procurement helps build food stocks, which support the Public Distribution System (PDS)
- MSP chain: MSP promotes price stability → price stability protects farmer income → farmer income protection creates a production incentive → production incentives support food security
## 2. Authority
- CACP recommends MSP; the Government of India fixes MSP
- Recommended by the CACP; approved by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)
- ⚠ Trap: Old APPSC key (APPSC G2 2017) credits CACP with "determining" MSP, since CACP recommends
- Legal status: Administrative decision, not a legal right; no statute backs it
- Exam angle: "recommending vs approving body" (CACP vs CCEA) is the favourite trap.
### CACP — origin, objective, MSP vs FRP and factors
## 1. APC to CACP
- Agricultural Prices Commission (APC): Established 1965 (January 1965) — renamed the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP) (renamed CACP in 1985)
- Main objective of the CACP: Incentivise farmers
- Incentivising farmers promotes the adoption of modern technology → modern technology increases productivity → increased productivity leads to increased production
| Item | Fact |
| Status | Attached office of the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, not a statutory body |
| Composition | Chairman + Member Secretary + one official member + two non-official members |
| Also recommends | FRP for sugarcane |
- ⚠ APC 1965 → CACP 1985
## 2. MSP vs FRP
| Basis | MSP | FRP — Fair & Remunerative Price |
| Applies to | MSP commodities | Sugarcane |
| Recommended by | CACP | CACP |
| Fixed / approved by | The government (CCEA) | The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) |
## 3. Factors considered
- cost of production
- demand and supply
- Domestic and international prices / price trends
- inter-crop price parity
- Terms of trade between agriculture and non-agriculture
- minimum 50% margin over cost
- Likely effect on consumers
### Terms → meaning — cost concepts and sugarcane prices
## 1. Cost concepts
| Concept | Meaning |
| A2 | The actual paid-out costs incurred by the farmer: seeds, fertilisers, chemicals, hired labour, fuel, irrigation |
| FL | The imputed value of family labour |
| A2 + FL | Paid-out cost plus family labour |
| C2 | A2 + FL + land rent + fixed capital costs and other costs (rent on owned land + interest on fixed capital; comprehensive cost) |
- Current policy: MSP ≥ 1.5 times the cost of production = minimum 50% margin over A2+FL, not C2
- Exam angle: "MSP = 1.5 × C2" is the trap; the actual formula is 1.5 × A2+FL.
- Swaminathan Commission (NCF, 2004-06): Wanted C2 + 50% (National Commission on Farmers, 2004)
- Government applies 50% over A2+FL
- SKM: paddy at C2+50% should be ₹3,243/q (₹3,243/quintal) for 2026-27 vs ₹2,441 fixed
## 2. Sugarcane prices
| Term | Meaning |
| FRP | Legally mandated minimum price sugar mills must pay cane farmers/growers; fixed by CCEA on CACP recommendation; linked to mill's sugar recovery % (recovery rate) |
| Statutory Minimum Price (SMP) | Replaced by FRP from 2009-10 |
| State Advised Price (SAP) | Higher price some States (e.g., UP, Punjab, Haryana) announce, usually above FRP |
- FRP legal basis: Sugarcane (Control) Order, 1966 under Essential Commodities Act, 1955
- Latest hike: ₹10/q (₹10/quintal) for sugar season 2026-27 (Oct-Sept)
- PYQ (CGPSC 2021): FRP relates to sugarcane
- ⚠ SMP → FRP from 2009-10; SAP is set by States, above FRP

### Procurement — agencies, open-ended vs limited crops, wheat RMS 2026-27
## 1. Crop → procuring agency
| Crop | Procurement |
| Wheat and paddy | FCI + State agencies buy all stock meeting specifications at MSP (under price support) → effectively open-ended |
| Coarse grains | States procure with GoI's prior approval; state agencies buy for the Central Pool |
| Pulses, oilseeds and copra | Limited PSS procurement, capped at 25% of production |

### PM-AASHA — components, outlay and performance — Basics
## 1. Basics
- Full form: Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay SanraksHan Abhiyan (Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay Sanrakshan Abhiyan)
- Launch: September 2018, Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare
- Purpose: Make MSP reach farmers, incl. small and marginal farmers
- Beneficiaries: farmers – a farmer-welfare scheme (PYQ; APPSC 2018; CGPSC 2024)
- Outlay: ₹35,000 crore for extension to 2025-26 (15th FC cycle) (APPSC-GS 2023 key)
### PM-AASHA — components, outlay and performance — Components
## 1. Components
| Component | How it works / what it does |
| PSS (Price Support Scheme) | Physical procurement at MSP of pulses, oilseeds, copra by NAFED and NCCF at a state's request. Only registered farmers with valid land records qualify. From 2024-25, procurement is capped at 25% of production. |
| PDPS (Price Deficiency Payment Scheme) | No physical purchase/procurement: the farmer is paid MSP minus market price (the gap) in cash, directly. Mainly for oilseeds. |
| PPSS (Private Procurement & Stockist Scheme, pilot) | 2018 pilot in which private stockists/agencies procured at MSP for oilseeds (original 2018 component) |
| MIS (Market Intervention Scheme) | Perishables not under MSP (onion, tomato, apple, chillies, grapes, garlic). Triggered by a ≥10% price fall or ≥10% production rise (10% or greater price fall or production rise). Centre-state losses shared 50:50 (75:25 for NE) - 75:25 for the NE States. |
- 2026 components: PSS, PDPS and MIS; PPSS belongs to the original 2018 design
- ⚠ PSS = physical buying; PDPS = cash gap, no buying; MIS = perishables

### Criticism and the legal-guarantee debate
## 1. Bias in coverage
| Factor | Effect |
| Crop bias | MSP covers 22 crops but procurement mostly paddy and wheat → MSP for pulses, oilseeds, coarse grains largely symbolic |
| Regional bias | Concentrated in Punjab, Haryana, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh and Chhattisgarh; eastern + NE farmers gain little |
| Shanta Kumar HLC (2015) | Only about 6% of farmers sell to procurement agencies at MSP |
## 2. Legal guarantee demand
- Legal status: Administrative decision, not a legal right; farmer groups demand legal guarantee
- Farmer unions: Law binding procurement at MSP
- Economists' view: Legal MSP could distort trade, encourage/entrench monoculture, breach WTO norms → prefer PDPS-type deficiency payments
- Alternatives: wider procurement, price-deficiency payments (PM-AASHA), direct income support (PM-KISAN), decentralised procurement, digital procurement via e-NAM
- Supreme Court: Issued notice to Centre on plea for MSP ≥ weighted average cost of production (14 Apr 2026)
- ES 2025-26: Use procurement-efficiency savings to fund voluntary crop diversification away from water-guzzling rice and wheat
