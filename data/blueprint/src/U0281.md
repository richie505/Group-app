### Part II → constitutional framework and Articles 5–11
## 1. Constitutional framework
- Part II: Articles 5 to 11 — primarily identifies citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution (26 January 1950)
- Only citizens at commencement (on 26 Jan 1950); the rest is left to Parliament (Art. 11)
- Who regulates citizenship: Parliament alone, by law, under Article 11 — acquisition, termination and all related matters
- No power to make citizenship laws: State Legislatures, the President, the Election Commission
- Citizenship Act: 1955 — Citizenship Rules made under Section 18
## 2. Articles 5 to 11
- Article 5 — citizenship at commencement: Domicile in India (domiciled on 26 Jan 1950) plus any one of:
- Either parent born in India
- Residence in India for at least five years before 1950 (ordinarily resident 5 years)
- Article 6: Rights of persons who migrated from Pakistan to India — migration before and after the permit cut-off date, 19 July 1948
- Article 7: Certain migrants to Pakistan — those who went after 1 March 1947; covers those who later returned under a resettlement permit (6 months' residence)
- Article 8: Persons of Indian Origin residing abroad — registration as a citizen by an Indian diplomatic or consular representative
- Article 9: Voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship → automatic loss of Indian citizenship (the single citizenship rule) - no longer a citizen
- Article 10: Continuance of the rights of citizenship — subject to laws made by Parliament
- Article 11: Parliament's comprehensive power to regulate citizenship by law (Parliament's power)
- ⚠ Art. 6 = from Pakistan; Art. 7 = to Pakistan
### Citizen-only rights, competent authority and common traps
## 1. Common traps
- President (Article 58): In India both a citizen by birth and a naturalized citizen are eligible; in the USA only a natural-born citizen can become President
- Do not confer citizenship: Depositing money in Indian banks; purchasing property in India
- Do not by themselves end citizenship: Marrying a foreign citizen; criticising the government or its policies
- ⚠ Trap: "A naturalized citizen can never be deprived of citizenship" is false — deprivation under Section 10
## 2. Citizen-only rights and offices
- Citizen-only rights: Arts. 15, 16, 19, 29, 30; vote and contest
- Citizen-only offices: President, VP, judges, Governor, AG
## 3. Competent authority to regulate citizenship
| Authority | Role |
| Art. 11 | Parliament alone legislates on and regulates citizenship |
| Rules | Union Home Ministry frames them (Citizenship Rules 2009, amended 2026) |
| Election Commission | Checks citizenship only for electoral rolls, under Art. 324 |
### Single vs dual citizenship — features and other countries
- Single citizenship and a single domicile across India — unitary features of the federal structure
- Contrast: the USA has dual (federal and state) citizenship
- Single citizenship: Allegiance only to the Union; no separate state citizenship
- Source: Britain (UPSC borrowed-features list); Canada also has it
- USA and Switzerland have double citizenship (national + state)
- Dual citizenship: India does not allow dual citizenship (Art. 9) - A and R both true, R explains A (PYQ 2023)
- Not proof of citizenship: Aadhaar, voter ID, ration card (MEA, Jun 2026)
### Citizenship Act 1955 → acquisition - five modes (Sections 3-7)
## 1. Five modes of acquiring citizenship — mnemonic B-D-R-N-I
- Birth: Depends on the place of birth — jus soli with modified criteria
- Descent: Born outside India to Indian citizen parents — jus sanguinis
- Registration (Section 5):
- Person of Indian Origin ordinarily resident in India for seven years before applying
- Persons married to Indian citizens
- Naturalization (Section 6): Foreigners meeting residency and character criteria
- Incorporation of territory: India acquires a new territory — e.g. Goa, Pondicherry, Sikkim
## 2. Sections and conditions
- Five modes (PYQ 2023): Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, Incorporation of territory
| Acquisition - 5 modes | Section | Key condition |
| Birth | 3 [GK] | Born in India 26 Jan 1950 to before 1 July 1987 (26 Jan 1950 - 30 Jun 1987): citizen whatever the parents' nationality; from 1 July 1987 (1 Jul 1987 - 2 Dec 2004): at least one parent an Indian citizen; from 3 Dec 2004: both parents citizens, or one parent Indian and the other not an illegal migrant |
| Descent | 4 [GK] | Father or mother (either parent) an Indian citizen at the birth; birth registered at an Indian consulate |
| Registration | 5 [GK] | PIOs and spouses of citizens after the prescribed residence, usually 7 years (older notes say 6 months); current rule = 7 years; spouses and minor children of citizens, etc. |
| Naturalisation | 6 [GK] | Ordinary residence of 12 years (11 of the preceding 14 years + the last 12 months); some notes: normally 11 years; conditions waivable for distinguished service to science, art, literature, world peace etc.; oath of allegiance |
| Incorporation of territory | 7 [GK] | Govt of India (Centre) specifies / notifies who become citizens when a territory joins India |
- Excluded from birth: Children of foreign diplomats and enemy aliens
- Marriage: Not a separate mode (a registration category)
- Other Sections: 7A - registration of OCI cardholders [GK]; 14A - National Register of Indian Citizens and national identity card (TH, 7 Jan 2026)
### Citizenship Act 1955 → loss - three modes (Sections 8-10)
## 1. Three modes of losing citizenship — mnemonic R-T-D
- Renunciation: Voluntary surrender by an Indian national
- Termination: Automatic loss when a citizen voluntarily acquires another country's citizenship — linked to Article 9
- Deprivation (Section 10): Compulsory termination by the Central Government for:
- Unlawful trading with an enemy during war
- Imprisonment of at least two years within five years of naturalization or registration
## 2. Sections and extra conditions
| Loss - 3 modes | Section | How |
| Renunciation | Sec. 8 [GK] | By declaration; minor children lose it too but may resume within 1 year of majority |
| Termination | Sec. 9 [GK] | Automatic on voluntarily acquiring foreign citizenship |
| Deprivation | Sec. 10 [GK] | By the Centre, only for citizens by naturalisation, registration or Art. 5(c) - fraud, disloyalty, aiding / helping an enemy in war, etc. |

### Terms → meaning: NRI, PIO, OCI, Commonwealth citizen; OCI rights
## 1. Overseas Citizen of India (OCI)
- Provision: Section 7A
- Strictly ineligible: Persons who were citizens of Pakistan or Bangladesh, or whose parents or grandparents belonged to Pakistan or Bangladesh
- Eligible: Foreign citizens of Indian origin whose ancestors were Indian nationals + their spouses and children
- Benefit: Lifelong visa - lifelong, visa-free entry; OCI card alone is enough to enter India (since 2016)
- Not available: Voting, contesting elections, constitutional offices, public employment as a rule (cannot vote or hold certain public posts); OCI holders are not citizens
- Update 2026: Citizenship (Amendment) Rules 2026
- e-OCI cards + central OCI database
- Online application and renunciation (ociservices.gov.in)
- A minor cannot hold another country's passport alongside an Indian one
## 2. Terms
| Term / category | Meaning |
| NRI | An Indian citizen ordinarily residing outside India (abroad); holds an Indian passport |
| PIO | A foreign citizen of Indian origin whose ancestors were Indian nationals; the PIO card was merged into OCI in 2015 |
| OCI | A foreign national of Indian origin registered as an Overseas Citizen of India cardholder under the Act (Sec. 7A) |
| Commonwealth citizen | A citizen of a country that is a member of the Commonwealth of Nations |
- Exam angle: NRI = citizen abroad; PIO and OCI = foreign citizens.
