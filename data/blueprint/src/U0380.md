### Family — institution, definitions and types
## 1. Family as an institution
- A basic and primary social institution
- Functions: Reproduction; socialization; emotional support; economic security; cultural transmission
- Important scholars of family: George Peter Murdock, Talcott Parsons, Irawati Karve
## 2. Definitions and types
- Definitions:
- Murdock: Common residence, economic co-operation, reproduction
- Orientation vs procreation: Family of orientation (born into) vs family of procreation (set up after marriage)
| Basis | Types |
| Structure/size | nuclear (conjugal); joint/extended (2+ generations) |
| Residence | patrilocal, matrilocal, neo-local, bilocal, duo-local (spouses stay with their own natal families) |
| Descent | patrilineal, matrilineal |
| Authority | patriarchal, matriarchal |
| Mates | monogamous; polygamous (polygyny/polyandry) |
- ⚠ Orientation = born into; procreation = set up after marriage
- ⚠ Duo-local = spouses stay with their own natal families; neo-local = new home
### Family functions → socialization, identity, biological, protective, economic, emotional
## 1. Socialization and education
- The first school of the child — teaches language, customs, values, traditions and norms
- Encourages acceptable behaviour; discourages deviant behaviour
- Teaches basic education before school; develops discipline and moral values; encourages learning habits
- A child learns language, manners and respect for elders in the family
## 2. Identity and status
- Provides caste, religious, language and national identity; provides social status
- Transfers traditions from one generation to another
## 3. Biological and protective functions
- Ensures the continuity of society through reproduction — produces the next generation
- Provides socially approved sexual relations — prevents social disorder by regulating sexual behaviour
- Protects infants, children, elderly and sick members
- Protects members from social and economic risks; supports children, women, elderly persons and disabled persons
## 4. Economic functions
- Provides food, shelter and clothing; members perform different roles
- Transfers wealth and property through inheritance; consumes goods and services collectively
## 5. Emotional and religious functions
- Provides love, affection and care; builds confidence and self-esteem; helps members cope with stress and crises
- Introduces religious beliefs and rituals; celebrates festivals and ceremonies — Diwali, Eid, Christmas, Gurpurab; preserves religious traditions
## 6. Prep summary
- Functions: Biological, economic, socialisation, psychological
- Dysfunction: Persistent failure of these functions (abuse, alcoholism)
### Family functions by scholar → Murdock, Ogburn, Parsons
## 1. Murdock's four universal functions
- George Peter Murdock identified four universal functions of the family (study of 250 societies [GK]):
| Function | Meaning |
| Sexual regulation | Regulation of sexual relations |
| Reproductive | The birth of children |
| Economic | Economic cooperation |
| Educational | Socialization of children |
## 2. Ogburn and Parsons
| Scholar | Functions |
| Ogburn | 7: economic, protective, religious, recreational, educational, status-giving, affectional; all except affection are passing to outside agencies ("loss of functions") [GK] |
| Talcott Parsons | 2 irreducible: primary socialisation of children, stabilisation of adult personality [GK] |
- ⚠ 4 – 7 – 2 = Murdock – Ogburn – Parsons
- ⚠ "Loss of functions" = Ogburn; only affection stays with the family

### Hindu family laws, schools of inheritance and family courts
## 1. Hindu family laws and cases

| Hindu Succession Act | 1956 — came into force on June 17, 1956; amended on September 9, 2005 | Property (inheritance) rights — the 2005 amendment gave daughters equal coparcenary rights by birth (Sec. 6); part of the Hindu Code; Ambedkar its main advocate (UPSC) |

### Changes in the family — factors and urban typology
## 1. Factors of change
| Factor | How it changes the family (CDX) |
| Industrialisation | the family moves from a production unit to a consumption unit; economically independent youth |
| Urbanisation/migration | limited housing; rural-urban migration breaks up joint families |
| Women's employment | women enter the labour market; more female-headed households |
## 2. Urban family typology (T.K. Oommen)
- Reading urban families: Livelihood, source of income, authority structure, social milieu and value pattern
- Urban categories by income:
- Proprietary class (joint; strong elder-male authority)
- Entrepreneurial-cum-professional
- Exam angle: UPPSC 2014 asked how T.K. Oommen distinguished urban families. Answer: all of mode of earning and value pattern, structure of authority, and urban milieu and social ecology.
### High-risk families → nature, causes and types
## 1. Nature
- Families facing severe social, economic, health, psychological or environmental challenges
- Characteristics: Poverty; unemployment; domestic violence; substance abuse; single-parent households; chronic illness or disability; child neglect and abuse; family conflicts
## 2. Causing factors
| Factor | Examples |
| Economic | Poverty, debt burden, job insecurity |
| Social | Family breakdown, migration, social exclusion |
| Psychological | Mental stress, depression, addiction |
## 3. Types in India
- Single-parent / female-headed households: Rising, due to widowhood, marital instability, male migration
- Dual-income urban households: Pressure on child care + caregiving (UPSC)
- Elderly care: Depends on filial piety; nuclearisation + migration strain support → alternative care needed (UPSC)
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: CM Chandrababu Naidu urged a return of the joint family + incentives for 2+ children as fertility falls (Jun 2026)
- Criticism & debates: "Breakdown" vs "adaptation" of the joint family
