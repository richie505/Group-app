### Diastrophism — endogenic processes, definition and types
## 1. Endogenic processes
- Internal heat produces endogenic processes, which drive mantle dynamics; mantle dynamics drive plate movement
- Mantle heat causes convection or thermal movement
- Plate movement produces folding, faulting, earthquakes, volcanism and mountain building — plate movement causes earthquakes
## 2. Diastrophism
- Definition: all processes that move, raise/elevate or build up the crust
| Orogenic | Epeirogenic |
| Mountain building by severe folding in long narrow belts | Continent building: uplift or warping of large continental areas |
| Horizontal (tangential) compressive/tensional forces | Vertical (radial) forces (UKPSC 2024) |
- Included: orogeny, epeirogeny, earthquakes, plate tectonics/movements
- ⚠ Trap: "heterogenic" NOT included (MPPSC 2023)
- Peninsular block: escaped folding but had vertical movements (transgression, regression) → epeirogenic effect
### Scholars → mountain-building and isostasy theories
| Scholar | Theory |
| Kober's | Geosynclinal theory: long, shallow, sinking sea troughs (geosynclines) collect sediments between rigid forelands (kratogens) → forelands squeeze sediments into mountains |
| Airy | Crust of uniform density, different thickness; mountains have deep "roots" [GK] |
| Pratt | Crust of different density, uniform depth of compensation; higher blocks are lighter [GK] |
- Geosyncline stages: lithogenesis (sedimentation) → orogenesis (folding, mountain building) → gliptogenesis (gradual rise, denudation)
- Indian example: Tethys geosyncline uplifted into the Himalaya (Tertiary / Aryan era)
- Isostasy: balance of crustal blocks "floating" on denser material below
- ⚠ Airy = same density, different thickness; Pratt = different density, same depth
### Folds — terms → meaning
| Fold | Meaning |
| anticline | Up-arch |
| syncline | Down-trough |
| nappe | Recumbent fold broken, thrust far forward |
- Siwaliks = simple anticline-syncline folds, Jura type
- Cuddapah: sediments laid in synclinal troughs
### Mountain types → formation and examples
## 1. Types of mountains
| Type | Formation | Features | Examples |
| Young fold mountains | Compression and folding — recent plate collision — associated with the Tertiary period | Very high; rugged terrain; steep slopes; sharp peaks | The Himalayas, Alps, Andes, Rockies |
| Old fold mountains | Ancient folding and prolonged denudation — ancient and eroded | Lower than young fold mountains; rounded; highly eroded | The Aravallis, Appalachians, Urals, Pennines |
| Block mountains (block or fault-block) | Faulting — vertical displacement along faults | Uplifted blocks; steep fault scarps; linked with rift valleys | The Sierra Nevada, Vosges, Black Forest (Rhine valley), Salt Range [GK] |
| Volcanic mountains | Accumulation of lava and volcanic material — volcanic activity | Conical peaks | Kilimanjaro (Africa), Fuji / Fujiyama (Japan), Mount St. Helens, Cotopaxi, Etna (Italy) |
| Residual mountains | Erosion leaves resistant rocks — remnants of older elevated surfaces | — | The Aravallis, Appalachians, Pennines; Eastern Ghats; Nilgiris [GK] |
| Dome mountains | Magma pushes upward without erupting through the crust | Dome-shaped uplifts | The Black Hills, Bighorns |
## 2. Fold vs block vs volcanic
| Basis | Fold mountains | Block mountains | Volcanic mountains |
| Main force | Compression | Faulting | Volcanism |
| Basic process | Folding | Uplift and downthrow | Lava accumulation |
| Common form | Rugged, parallel ranges | Fault scarps | A cone shape |
| Associated with | Fold belts | Horsts and grabens | Craters |
| Examples | The Himalaya, Alps, Andes | The Sierra Nevada, Vosges | Fuji, Kilimanjaro |
- ⚠ Trap: Sierra Nevada in "young folded" lists = block (fault) mountain (MPPSC 2021)
### Fold mountains by age, key facts and the Aravalli
## 1. Key facts
- The world's highest peaks mostly occur in young fold mountains — the Andes and the Himalayas are young fold mountains (Highest peaks: in young fold mountains - Himalaya, Everest)
- The Appalachians and the Aravallis are old fold or residual mountains
- The Rhine Valley and the Vosges form a block mountain–rift valley system
- Mount Kilimanjaro: A volcanic mountain
- The Andes (South America): The longest major mountain range
| Age group | Examples |
| Very old (Precambrian) | Aravalli - one of the oldest fold mountains of the world, now residual (300-900 m); formed from Dharwar sediments |
| Old (Palaeozoic) | Appalachians, Urals, Pennines [GK] |
| Young (Tertiary / Alpine) | Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies [GK for Andes, Rockies] |
- Young vs old: young = rugged relief, high conical peaks (Himalaya, Alps); old = worn down
- Himalaya: Tethys sediments pushed up by northward-moving Indian plate (Eocene to Pliocene); uplift began ~70 million years ago, continues
- Exam angle: APPSC G2 2024 - of Appalachians, Aravallis, Alps, Urals, only the Alps are young fold mountains.
## 2. Aravalli Range
- Located in Rajasthan, Haryana and Delhi — its northern extension reaches Delhi
- One of the oldest mountain ranges — geological age about 570 million years; a residual mountain range
- Highest peak: Guru Shikhar — 1,722 metres; Sirohi district, Rajasthan
### Block mountains and rift valleys — horst, graben → location
## 1. Rift valleys
- Formation: Divergence or tension causes faulting; faulting causes the central block to subside (large areas break, displaced vertically)
- An elongated depression associated with faulting
- Horst: An uplifted fault block; graben: a down-faulted block (rift valley)
- Horsts = block mountains (APPSC 2022)
- Examples: Death Valley (USA); the Rhine Rift Valley; the Narmada Valley; the Tapi Valley — the Narmada and Tapi are rift valleys
| Feature | Type / location |
| Vosges (France), Black Forest (Germany) | Horsts flanking the Rhine rift valley (graben) - Europe |
| Great Rift Valley (East Africa) | Graben; Lake Malawi lies in it |
| Death Valley (California) | Graben [GK] |
| Narmada, Tapi | Flow west through rift valleys; Narmada between Vindhyas (north) and Satpuras (south) |
- Exam angle: UPSC 2024 - "Vosges - Central Asia - fold" and "Alps - block" are wrong pairs; Appalachians (fold, N. America) and Andes (fold, S. America) are right.
## 2. Key landform contrasts
- Mountains are steep or pointed, whereas plateaus are elevated and flat-topped
- Young fold mountains are high and rugged, whereas old fold mountains are low and rounded
- Fold mountains result from compression, whereas block mountains result from faulting
- A canyon is a more extensive and deeply eroded gorge
- V-shaped valleys are formed by rivers, whereas U-shaped valleys are formed by glaciers
- A hanging valley is glacial, whereas a rift valley is tectonic
- A delta is dominated by deposition, whereas an estuary is associated with tidal or drowned-mouth processes
- A stalactite hangs from a roof, whereas a stalagmite rises from a floor
- A horst is an uplifted block, whereas a graben is a down-faulted block
- A barchan is crescent-shaped, whereas a seif dune is longitudinal
- A spit projects from the coast, whereas a tombolo connects an island to the mainland

### Endogenic chain
- ★ Evidence of the Himalayas as young fold mountains: deep gorges, U-turn river courses, parallel ranges, steep gradients causing landslides (all four)
- ★ Youngest mountain chain: Shiwaliks — outermost (outer Himalayas), last stage formed

### Diastrophism
- Orogenic: mountain building by horizontal (tangential) forces in narrow belts
- Epeirogenic: vertical rise or sinking of large areas — e.g. Peninsular Block (transgression, regression)
- Barchan: crescent-shaped dune; seif: long narrow dune parallel to wind
### Mountain types
| Type | Formed by | Examples |
| Young fold | recent compression (folding); high, rugged, sharp | ★ Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies — world's highest peaks |
| Old fold | ancient folding + long denudation; lower, rounded, eroded | Aravallis (Precambrian), Appalachians, Urals (Palaeozoic) |
| Block | faulting | Vosges, Black Forest, Sierra Nevada (not young fold) |
| Volcanic | erupted lava | ★ Mt. Etna (Sicily), Kilimanjaro, Fujiyama, Mount St. Helens, Cotopaxi |
| Dome | magma bulges crust without erupting | Black Hills, Bighorns |
| Residual | erosion | Aravalli, Eastern Ghats |
- ★ Everest and Mont Blanc are fold mountains, not volcanic
- V-shaped valley: river (downward) erosion; U-shaped, hanging = glacial; rift = tectonic
### Mountain-building theories
- ★ Appalachian: old range (Caledonian period) — odd one out among Tertiary/Alpine Alps, Andes, Himalayas, Rockies, Atlas
- Isostasy: balance of crustal blocks floating on denser material below
- Airy: uniform density, varying thickness, deep roots
- Pratt: varying density, common compensation depth; higher blocks are lighter
- Kober: geosynclinal theory — long shallow troughs between hard blocks (kratogens)
- Stages: lithogenesis (sedimentation) → orogenesis (folding) → gliptogenesis (uplift, denudation)
### Folds by rising pressure
- Nappe: recumbent fold broken and thrust far forward
- Cuddapah sediments laid down in synclinal troughs
- Siwaliks: simple Jura-type anticline–syncline folds, not block mountains
### Horsts, grabens, rifts
- Horst: uplifted fault block; graben: down-faulted block
- Rift valley: middle block sinks between faults as crust pulls apart
- ★ Narmada valley = rift valley; Narmada forms no delta (deposits no silt)
- Narmada flows west between the Vindhyas (north) and Satpuras (south)
- Vosges (France) and Black Forest (Germany): horsts beside the Rhine graben
- Grabens: Great Rift Valley, Death Valley, Rhine Rift Valley; Sierra Nevada is a horst
### Aravalli
- Diastrophism: all crust movements that move, raise or build up the crust (incl. earthquakes, plate movements)
- Aravalli: Precambrian fold mountain (Appalachians Palaeozoic; Himalaya, Alps Tertiary)
- Highest peak: Guru Shikhar, 1,722 m, Mount Abu
- Aravalli hill limit: 100 m above local relief (not 500 m)
- Supreme Court paused new Aravalli mining leases (November 2025)
- Collapse of hanging glaciers in seismic Himalayan zones → landslide-lake outburst floods and GLOFs
- Tombolo: joins an island to the mainland; spit: built by longshore drift
### Exam corner

- AP's Eastern Ghats: old, much eroded leftover hills of the Eastern Ghats Mobile Belt

### Diastrophism — endogenic processes, definition and types
