### Provincial units → heads and number of subas
| Unit (largest to smallest) | Head(s) |
| Suba (province) | Subedar + provincial diwan |
| Sarkar (district) | Faujdar + amalguzar |
| Pargana (tehsil) | Shiqdar + amil, qanungo, chaudhari |
| Village | Muqaddam, patwari, chowkidar, panchayat |
- Chakla: Unit between suba and pargana but not identical to the sarkar (PYQ)
- Prominent in Bengal under Murshid Quli Khan
- Number of subas:
- Akbar started with 12 subas
- Aurangzeb = 21 (Golconda became the 21st)
- Reach of the state: Effectively ended at the pargana; villages largely self-governing
- Exam angle: ascending order - Pargana - Sarkar - Suba (UPSC 2021); Mughal "district" = Sarkar.
### Provincial and revenue officials → functions
| Official | Function |
| Subedar (sipahsalar/nazim) | Governor - law and order, imperial decrees |
| Diwan (provincial) | Revenue and finance; independent of the subedar - a check on him |
| Bakhshi | Military pay, musters; also news reports [GK] |
| Faujdar | Sarkar-level military command and law and order |
| Kotwal | City police chief; markets, watch on newcomers |
| Diwan-i-Tan | In charge of salaries (tankhwah) and jagir assignments[GK] |
- Sadr and qazi: Provincial level, for grants and justice
| Official | Level | Duty |
| Amalguzar (amil) | Sarkar | Revenue collector in charge of a district (APPSC-G1 2022); assessment and collection |
| Bitikchi | Sarkar | Accountant - kept records of land, crops and assessment; assistant to the amalguzar; prepared revenue papers and accounts |
| Potdar (fotedar / khazandar) | Sarkar | Treasurer - sarkar treasurer = khazanadar / fotadar |
| Qanungo | Pargana | Keeper of land records (hereditary) |
| Chaudhari | Pargana | Revenue collector (local notable) |
| Patwari | Village | Village accountant |
| Muqaddam (patel) | Village | Village headman; collected revenue |
- ⚠ Amalguzar = collector; Bitikchi = accountant; Potdar = treasurer (all sarkar level)
- ⚠ Patwari = village accountant; Muqaddam = village headman
### Mansabdar-jagirdar-zamindar linkage → flow and zamindar categories
- Flow:
- Mansabdar gets rank → paid by a tankhwah jagir
- Jagirdar's agents collect the assessed revenue (jama) → through zamindars (keep a hereditary share)
- Peasant pays about one-third of produce
| Jagirdar | Zamindar |
| Assignee of revenue in lieu of salary for state (mainly military) service | Holder of hereditary rights over land/revenue in a locality |
| Not hereditary; transferred often | Hereditary, saleable rights [GK] |
| Category | Rights and role |
| Intermediary zamindars | Collected revenue from primary zamindars for the state; got a commission [GK] |
| Primary zamindars | Proprietary, hereditary rights over cultivated land [GK] |
- Conflicts (local face of the jagirdari crisis):
- Short-tenure jagirdars pressed for more
- Zamindars resisted or rebelled
- Peasants fled
- Exam angle: UPSC 2019 - "jagirs in lieu of judicial/police duties" and "jagirs hereditary" are both wrong (answer: neither).
### Universal angles
- Significance: Subedar-diwan separation + zabti system = model for later revenue administration
- Comparative note:
- Sultanate iqtas (muqti), shiq (shiqdar) and pargana (amil) preceded the Mughal pattern
- British district collector combined revenue, magisterial and police powers the Mughals kept apart
- AP angle (Qutb Shahi Golconda):
- Units: tarafs and simalu (seemas); under Tana Shah 37 sarkars and 517 parganas
- Officials: village mukaddam, accountant kulkarni, pargana accounts officer desai
- Revenue farmed out by auction
- Northern Circars zamindars date from the Qutb Shahi era
