### Industry → raw materials and location factors — Cotton textiles
## 1. Cotton textiles
- Location: Began near black-soil cotton tracts of Maharashtra and Gujarat → dispersed (Tamil Nadu, MP) as cotton is not weight-losing and markets, power, rail spread
- Spread from Mumbai to Gujarat and Maharashtra driven by raw cotton, power, market
- Tamil Nadu (Coimbatore) spinning: Hydel power, cheap labour, local cotton
## 2. Jute
- Reasons (Hugli belt): Ganga-Brahmaputra delta raw jute/soil; soft water for retting; Hooghly waterway; Kolkata port for export + machinery; cheap labour from WB, Bihar, UP; big gunny-bag market
## 3. Sugar
- Mill location: Near cane (within ~20 km) - cane perishable, yields only 9-12% sugar
- UP-Bihar pull: Alluvial soil rich in lime and potash, level land, cheap labour, dense market, bagasse fuels the boilers
## 4. Cement
- Inputs: Limestone, silica, alumina, gypsum; coal; plants near limestone deposits
## 5. Paper
- Location: Forest- and bagasse-based; mills near bamboo/softwood forests or cane belts
- Raw materials: Bamboo, bagasse, poplar, eucalyptus, softwood, rags [GK]
### Fertilisers and petrochemicals — production and feedstock
## 1. Production
- Urea: Made from ammonia and CO₂ — the Haber process and CO₂
- Natural gas (methane): The raw material for ammonia used in nitrogen fertilizers
- Phosphatic fertilizers: Rock phosphate + sulfuric acid — the sulfur comes from oil refineries; superphosphate = rock phosphate + H₂SO₄
## 2. Feedstock (fertilisers and petrochemicals)
- Feedstock (fertilisers and petrochemicals):
- Natural gas (HBJ pipeline)
- Steel by-products (slag, coke)
- Rock phosphate + sulphur for phosphatics; gypsum [GK]

### Changes — sugar's north-south shift and steel's coastal shift
## 1. Sugar: north vs south
| North (UP, Bihar) | South (Maharashtra, Karnataka, TN, AP) |
| Subtropical cane, lower sucrose | Tropical cane, higher sucrose and yield |
| Many private mills (Bihar mostly non-cooperative) | Cooperative mills, esp. Maharashtra (Nasik, Pune, Satara, Sangli, Kolhapur, Sholapur) |
- Why South gained:
- Higher yields (TN ~105 t/ha vs UP ~81) from tropical cane with more sucrose
- Longer crushing season (200-250 days vs 150-180)
- Well-run cooperative mills (Maharashtra, see table)
- North has older private mills → shift south
## 2. Steel: western-coast shift
- Western-coast shift: Coking coal largely imported → port sites save transport; ports also serve export markets

## Cement
- India — second-largest cement producer; more than 8% of world output
- Largest cement state (2026): Rajasthan, then AP, Telangana, Gujarat, Chhattisgarh, Tamil Nadu (older sources: AP first by capacity)
- Per capita consumption: India about 290 kg, world about 540 kg
## Fertiliser
- Largest fertiliser region (Gujarat–Maharashtra): Vadodara, Kalol, Kandla, Trombay
- Largest urea producer: Uttar Pradesh; overall fertiliser: Gujarat; India — second-largest fertiliser consumer
### North vs South sugar
- South India — tropical cane, higher sucrose content and yield; North — subtropical cane, lower sucrose
- North (UP, Bihar) crushing season: November to February
- North has many private mills (Bihar mostly non-cooperative); cooperatives strong in South, mainly Maharashtra
- Maharashtra cooperative centres: Nasik, Pune, Satara, Sangli, Kolhapur, Sholapur
