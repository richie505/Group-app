# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:1 - Structural change - sectoral composition of output and employment; the missing manufacturing transition
### Three-sector scheme
- India moved agriculture → services without a manufacturing-led stage: the "missing middle"
| Sector | Other name | Activities |
| Primary | Agricultural | Crops, livestock, fisheries, mining & quarrying |
| Secondary | Industrial | Manufacturing, water supply, utilities, construction |
| Tertiary | Service | Trade, hotels, transport, banking, public admin |
| Quaternary | Knowledge | R&D, software, consulting |
| Quinary | Top decision-making | Policy, scientific research |
### Clark-Fisher prediction vs India
- 1. Income rises ↓
- 2. Labour & output shift primary → secondary → tertiary ↓
- 3. Lewis: surplus farm labour → industry (Lewis turning point) ↓
- 4. India: output → services, labour stays on farms (~46% in agriculture)

### Economists and models → prediction
| Model | Author | Prediction |
| Three-sector (Clark-Fisher) | Allan Fisher and Colin Clark[GK] | As income rises, labour and output shift primary → secondary → tertiary[GK] |
| Dual-sector | W. Arthur Lewis (1954) [GK] | Surplus farm labour (marginal productivity ~zero) moves to the modern industrial sector at a constant wage until the surplus is exhausted - the Lewis turning point[GK] |
- India deviates: output shifted to services while labour stayed on farms → Lewis transfer is slow (~46% of workers still in agriculture)
- EAC-PM Chairman: services-led growth cannot be sustained without a manufacturing base (Nov 2025)

### Terms and debates → the missing manufacturing stage

| "Premature leapfrogging" | Services absorb value, manufacturing fails to absorb surplus farm labour |
| Nominal vs real share | Fall in manufacturing's nominal share = relative-price effect; in real terms stable at 17-18% |
| Share logic | Sector growing slower than GDP loses share - agriculture's slow growth lowers its share even when output rises |
