# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:132
### Titles & firsts
- Coronations: hurried first in 1658 after Samugarh; grand second in 1659 after Deorai
- Jahanara — Sahibat-uz-Zamani (Mistress of the Age); Roshanara — Padshah Begum
- Child's War (1686-90) — first war between a Mughal emperor and the English
### Religious measures
- Banned court music, yet played the veena; Persian works on Indian music written in his reign
- 1669 temple-demolition order — Kashi Vishwanath and Keshava Rai (Mathura)
- Newly built temples — ordered to be destroyed
- Muhtasibs — enforced the Sharia
- Banned Navroz; stopped Jharokha darshan
### Revolts
| Leader / group | Fact |
| Rajaram (Jat) | plundered Akbar's tomb at Sikandra |
| Gokula | 1669 Mathura revolt |
| Churaman | founded the power that became Bharatpur |
| Satnamis | 1672 revolt of peasants and artisans at Narnaul (Haryana) |
- Jaswant Singh of Marwar died 1678 → Rajput war of 1679-81; Durgadas Rathore backed his posthumous heir Ajit Singh
### Fall of Golconda
- Golconda fell 1687 — ended Qutb Shahi rule over Andhra; became a Mughal suba
- Abdullah Pani — traitor who opened the gates of Golconda
- Abdur Razzaq Lari — defender; honoured as Mustafa Khan
- Tana Shah (Abul Hasan Qutb Shah) — imprisoned, exiled to Daulatabad (not Gwalior, where Murad Bakhsh was imprisoned)
- Aurangzeb died at Ahmednagar (1707); simple grave at Khuldabad near Aurangabad (Tana Shah also buried there)
### Marathas & last years
- Shivaji at Agra (1666) — insulted, under house arrest; escaped in fruit baskets
### Debates
- ★ Aurangzeb banned music as anti-Islamic, yet played the veena; most books on Indian classical music in Persian written in his reign
- Handling of Marwar and Mewar — broke the Rajput alliance that had supported Akbar's empire
- 'Deccan Ulcer' — 27-year Deccan war drained the Mughal treasury (cf. Napoleon's Spanish Ulcer)
### Golconda conquest → persons and dates
- ★ Aurangzeb took Bijapur (1686) and Golconda (1687); ruler — Abul Hasan Qutb Shah
- ★ Fort opened with 'keys of gold' — as Akbar opened Asirgarh
### Universal angles
- ★ Aurangzeb invaded the "impenetrable" Golconda in 1687
