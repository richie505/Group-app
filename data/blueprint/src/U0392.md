| Case / law | Year | Point |

- Communal Violence Bill debate:
- PM Modi: law that "sought to hold the majority community responsible for riots"

- Enforcement data:
- Only 20.2% conviction under Sec. 153A (NCRB)
- Hate-speech cases rose 500% between 2014 and 2020
- FIRs rise 30-50% before elections (Dec 2025)
- ⚠ 153A → 196; 295A → 299; 505 → 353 (IPC → BNS)
### Consequences and remedies — effects, police, institutions
| Consequence | Effect |
| Ghettoisation | Communities move into religiously segregated neighbourhoods after riots |
| Displacement | Partition = largest forced migration in history (UPSC) |
| Loss of trust and rule of law | Insecurity, instability, loss of faith in police and courts; e.g., 1984 and 2002: guilty not brought to book (UPSC Indian Society p.83) |
| Polarisation | Weaker social capital; threat to unity in diversity; harm to India's global image and the diaspora |
| Gendered violence | Veena Das (Critical Events) analysed impact of Partition and communal riots on women |
## 1. Administrative failure as a cause
- Srikrishna Commission found this in the Mumbai riots
- Partition: police complicit or helpless
## 2. Institutional remedies
- National Integration Council: Chaired by the PM; Union ministers + CMs + eminent persons
- Criticised as inactive (UPSC Polity)
- National Foundation for Communal Harmony: Supports child victims of violence + promotes inter-faith dialogue
- Home Guards: First raised Dec 1946 to help police control civil disturbance and communal riots (2026)
## 3. Local and administrative remedies
- MHA Guidelines for Communal Harmony: Cover prevention, containment and response
