# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:130 - Akbar's institutions - Mansabdari system, Todar Mal's bandobast, dahsala, zabti and other revenue systems
### Road to dahsala
- Mansabdari + zabti-dahsala = salaried, transferable nobility + predictable cash revenue
- 1. Sher Shah's survey system ↓
- 2. Karori experiment (1574): circles of one crore dams ↓
- 3. Karoris collect revenue + cultivation data ↓
- 4. Dahsala (1580), Todar Mal
### Revenue systems
| System | Meaning | Where |
| Zabti (Bandobast) | Land measured; cash rates per crop per bigha | Core North Indian provinces |
| Dahsala | Average produce & prices of last ten years | 1580 (APPSC 2017) |
| Batai / Ghalla-bakhshi | Crop-sharing | Thatta, Kabul, Kashmir |
| Nasaq | From past records, no fresh measurement | Bengal |
| Kankut | Estimate on standing crop | Where measuring impractical |
| Plough | By number of ploughs | Deccan |
### Todar Mal & units
- Diwan (finance minister); one of the Navratnas
- Earlier served Sher Shah
- Gaz-i-Ilahi: length unit, about 82 cm
- Jarib: bamboo pieces joined with iron rings, not hemp rope
- State share: one-third, in cash (dam)
### Land classes
- ▲ Polaj — cultivated every year
- ▸ Parauti — fallow 1-2 years
- ▸ Chachar — fallow 3-4 years
### Revenue terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Khalisa | Crown land; revenue straight to imperial treasury |
| Jagir | Right to collect revenue in lieu of salary; not ownership |
| Madad-i-maash / Suyurghal | Revenue-free grant to scholars, holy men |
| Maal | Land revenue, principal state income |
### Administrative units
- ▲ Suba — Subedar, diwan, bakhshi, sadr, qazi; 12 in 1580
- ▸ Sarkar — Faujdar (law & order), amalguzar
- ▸ Pargana — Shiqdar, amil, qanungo
- ▸ Town — Kotwal
- ▸ Village — Muqaddam, patwari, chowkidar
### Exam corner
- "Bandobast" = Dahsala (JPSC 2013)
- 1574 = Karori; 1580 = dahsala
- Batai = crop-sharing; Kankut = estimate; Nasaq = past records
- Mansabdars: civil-military, non-hereditary, paid by jagir vs ICS: civil, fixed salary
- Golconda: one-third; deshmukhs and deshpandes
### Raja Todar Mal → service, office and measurement units
| Person | Contribution |
| Raja Todar Mal | Earlier served Sher Shah; built on his survey-based system |
| Raja Todar Mal | Diwan (finance minister) of the empire; one of the Navratnas |
| Karoris | Officers over circles each yielding one crore dams (Karori experiment) |
| Gaz-i-Ilahi | Unit of length (about 82 cm) |

- Run-up to dahsala (chronology):
- Karori experiment (1574): Core provinces split into circles, each yielding one crore dams, under karoris
- Collected revenue + reported cultivation data → groundwork for dahsala (1580)
- ⚠ 1574 = Karori experiment; 1580 = dahsala
### Revenue systems → meaning and region
- Dahsala and Zabti: Under Akbar — date to 1580; associated with Todarmal; Bandobast is another name for Dahsala — associated with Akbar's Dahsala
| System | Meaning | Where / when |
| Zabti (bandobast) | Land measured; cash rates fixed per crop per bigha | Core provinces of North India (Lahore to Allahabad, Malwa, Gujarat) [GK] |
| Dahsala (Ain-i-Dahsala) | Revenue fixed on the average produce and prices of the last ten years, then commuted to cash | Introduced 1580[GK]; APPSC-G2 2017 answer |
| Batai / Ghalla-bakhshi | Crop-sharing between state and peasant | Areas such as Thatta, Kabul, Kashmir [GK] |
| Nasaq | Rough assessment from past revenue records, without fresh measurement | Mainly Bengal[GK] |
| Kankut | Yield estimated on the standing crop | Used where measurement was not practical [GK] |
| Plough assessment | Assessment by number of ploughs | Deccan, in parts (UPPSC 2017 key) |
- Bandobast: "Bandobast system" = Dahsala (JPSC 2013 key); some notes use "Zabti or Bandobast" for the same Todar Mal reform
- ⚠ Batai = crop-sharing; Kankut = standing-crop estimate; Nasaq = past records (Bengal)
- Other pre-colonial systems — introduced by:
| System | Introduced by / period | Note |
| Iqta system | Iltutmish | Involved revenue and military administration |
| Mir Bakshi office | Belonged to the Mughal period (Sultanate counterpart: Ariz-i-Mumalik) | A military and administrative office |
### Revenue terms → meaning (state share, land classes, grants)
- State share: One-third of average produce, paid in cash (dam); payment in kind allowed in some areas
| Land class | Meaning |
| Polaj | Cultivated every year |
| Parauti | Fallow 1-2 years |
| Chachar | Fallow 3-4 years |
| Madad-i-maash | Revenue-free grant to scholars, holy men and the needy [GK] |
| Suyurghal | Another name for such charitable revenue-free grants [GK] |
| Maal (mal) | Land revenue - the principal state income[GK] |
| Khalisa | Crown land whose revenue went straight to the imperial treasury; reserve against which cash (naqd) salaries were paid |
| Jagir | Assignment of the right to collect revenue in lieu of salary - not ownership |
- ⚠ Parauti (1-2 yrs) vs Chachar (3-4 yrs) vs Banjar (5+ yrs)
### Administrative units → officials
| Unit | Officials |
| Suba (12 in 1580, later 15 [GK]) | Subedar, diwan, bakhshi, sadr, qazi |
| Sarkar | Faujdar (law and order), amalguzar (revenue) |
| Pargana | Shiqdar, amil, qanungo |
| Town | Kotwal (police and municipal order) [GK] |
| Village | Muqaddam (headman), patwari, chowkidar |
### Universal angles
- Significance: Mansabdari + zabti-dahsala → salaried, transferable nobility + predictable cash revenue
- Comparative note: Mughal mansabdars = combined civil-military, non-hereditary ranks paid by jagir vs British ICS = purely civil, fixed salary from the treasury
- AP angle: Qutb Shahi Golconda
- Land classes: wet, dry, garden, waste; generally took one-third

### Revenue systems → meaning and region
- ★ Batai (Bantai) — a revenue system: crop-sharing, actual produce divided between state and peasant
- Sher Shah's three systems: Nasq / Muktai / Kankut, Nakadi or Jabti, Galla Bakshi or Bantai
- ★ Zabti — introduced by Akbar; measurement-based, dasturs with per-bigha cash rates, Ain-i-Dahsala
- ★ Todarmal — land revenue reforms; average produce and prices over ten years, 1571-1580; one-third as state share
- ★ Deccan under Akbar — revenue based on number of plows (J.N. Sarkar, History of Aurangzeb)
