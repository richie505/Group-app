### Hydropower — forms of energy and energy conversion
## 1. Forms of energy
- Kinetic energy: Translational or rotational
- Potential energy: Gravitational, elastic / spring, electric, chemical, nuclear
- Mechanical energy equals kinetic energy plus potential energy
- Thermal / heat energy: Internal energy due to molecular motion
- Other types: Sound, light, electrical, magnetic, nuclear
## 2. Energy conversion
| Device / process | Converts |
| Generator / dynamo | Mechanical → electrical |
| Electric motor | Electrical → mechanical |
| Battery / cell | Chemical → electrical |
| LED / bulb | Electrical → light |
| Solar cell | Light → electrical |
| Nuclear power plant | Nuclear → heat → electrical |
| Free fall, pendulum, roller coaster | Potential → kinetic |
| Friction in brakes | Kinetic → heat |
## 3. Conversion in a hydro plant
- Stages:
- Stored water = potential energy
- Falling water → kinetic energy
- Spins the turbine → mechanical energy
- Generator → electrical energy by electromagnetic induction
- Net transformation: Potential → Electrical (APPSC 2023; APPSC-GS 2023 key: potential to electrical)
### Hydropower — types and classification
## 1. Types of hydro plants
- Run-of-the-river: no large dam/reservoir; uses the natural flow without much altering the watercourse

- Transfer history: plants up to 3 MW → MNRE in 1989; up to 25 MW in November 1999

### Large dams — sustainability and trade-offs
## 1. Sustainability
- Strengths: clean (near-zero operating emissions), renewable water cycle, flexible
- Benefits: flood control, irrigation, drinking water, flexible power for grid stability
- Verdict: hydro is the best source for sustainable development (UPPSC 2012) - hydro-electricity cheap and clean once built (UPSC)
- Weakness: drought/snowpack variability
## 2. Trade-offs
| Trade-off | Detail |
| Displacement | Bhakra Nangal, Sardar Sarovar, Tehri displaced millions; 18.5 million affected by projects 1950-1990; over 3,300 dams built |
| Seismicity | Seismic risk in the Himalayan region |
| Sedimentation | Reduces reservoir capacity over time |
| Ecology | Alters river ecosystems, blocks fish migration |

### Pumped storage — principle, grid role and projects outside AP
## 1. Principle and grid role
- Principle: surplus hours (high solar/wind) → water pumped from a lower to an upper reservoir; peak demand → released to generate power
- Nature: a "giant water battery" - the fastest-responding large-scale storage
- Grid role: lets solar and wind stand in for coal at peak hours - needed for the 500 GW and 60% (2035) goals
- Exam context: UPSC 2024 - pumped-storage hydropower discussed under long-duration energy storage
- Storage need (2035-36): capacity doubling to 1,121 GW → 174 GW / 888 GWh storage incl.
