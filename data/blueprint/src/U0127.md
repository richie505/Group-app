# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:126 - Battle of Talikota (1565), the Aravidu dynasty and the legacy of Vijayanagara
### Battle of Talikota
- After Talikota, the centre of gravity shifted to the Telugu country (Penukonda, Chandragiri)
- 23 January 1565 (⚠ other notes: 26 January)
- Names: Talikota (Sewell), Rakshasi-Tangadi (Heras), Bannihatti (Sherwani)
- Aliya Rama Raya, regent of Sadasiva Raya, captured and killed
- Vs Deccan Sultanates, not Bahmani; Berar stayed out
### The confederacy
| Sultanate | Ruler |
| Bijapur | Ali Adil Shah |
| Ahmadnagar | Hussain Nizam Shah (beheaded Rama Raya) |
| Golconda | Ibrahim Qutb Shah (key organiser) |
| Bidar | Ali Barid Shah |
### Causes → results
- 1. Rama Raya's divide-and-rule; Ahmadnagar razed (1557-63) ↓
- 2. Marriage links (Chand Bibi) + shelter to Jagadeva Rao unite Sultans ↓
- 3. Talikota; Muslim contingents desert ↓
- 4. Hampi looted ~six months ↓
- 5. Golconda takes Kondavidu, Machilipatnam; Nayakas autonomous
### Aravidu rulers (1570-1680)
| Ruler | Fact |
| Tirumala Raya (1570-72) | Founder; crowned at Penukonda aged 90 |
| Venkatapati II (1585-1614) | Greatest, "Second Krishnadevaraya" |

| Venkatapati III (1630-43) | Grant of Madras |
| Sriranga III (1643-80) | Last; lost Vandavasi (1642) |

## 2. Sides
- Sides: Vijayanagara Empire vs Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda, Bidar) (APPSC-G1 2018)

- De-facto ruler: Aliya Rama Raya, regent of Tuluva king Sadasiva Raya

### Aliya Rama Raya → regency, policy and causes of Talikota
## 1. Rama Raya
- Aliya (son-in-law) Rama Raya: Married Krishnadevaraya's daughter
- Regent for nominal king Sadashiva Raya (1543-)
## 2. Policy toward the Sultans
| Years | Move | Gain |
| 1546-47 | Backed Ahmadnagar vs Bijapur and Bidar | Took the Raichur doab |
| 1557-63 | Backed Bijapur | Razed Ahmadnagar |
## 3. Causes of Talikota
| Cause | Effect |
| Interference in the Sultanates' affairs | Sacked Ahmadnagar, 1557-63 |
| Marriage links | Bibi Jamal married Ibrahim; Chand Bibi married Ali Adil |
| Shelter to a rebel | Rama Raya sheltered Jagadeva Rao, a rebel from Golconda |
### Talikota → course and results
## 1. Course
- Rama Raya captured and killed; Venkatadri also killed
## 2. Results
| Area | Consequence |
| Capital (Hampi) | Looted for about six months; severe but not total damage (Sewell's "graveyard" account may exaggerate) |
| Flight | Sadashiva and Tirumala fled to Penukonda |
| Nayakas | Became independent/autonomous → later zamindars and poligars |
### Aravidu dynasty → origin, span, capitals
- Name: From Araviti (Aravidu) village, Kurnool district
- Rule: 1570-1680; 7 rulers
- End: Death of Sriranga III in 1680; some notes date the empire's end to 1646
- Sadasiva Raya: Lived on as nominal king; death 1576 per some notes, while others give his reign as 1542-1570
- Capitals: Penukonda (1570) → Chandragiri (under Venkata II) → Vellore (1600)
- ⚠ Order: Penukonda → Chandragiri → Vellore (1600)
### Aravidu rulers → period and contribution — Rulers
## 1. Rulers
| Ruler | Key fact / contribution |
| (Aliya Rama Raya, regent 1543-65) | De facto ruler under Sadashiva |
| Tirumala Raya (1570-72) | Founder; crowned at Penukonda in 1570 (LENS Jun 2026) at the age of 90; wrote a commentary on the Gita Govinda; Vasucharitra was dedicated to him |
| Sriranga Raya I (1572-85) | Lost Kondaveedu to Ibrahim Qutb Shah |
| Venkatapati Raya II (1585-1614) | Greatest and longest-ruling ruler, called the "Second Krishnadevaraya"; his Vilapakam inscription (1601) is the key source for Tuluva-Aravidu genealogy |

| Venkatapati III (1630-43) | Grant of Madras |
| Sriranga Raya III (1643-80) | Last ruler; defeated at Vandavasi (1642) by Mir Jumla of Golconda; later held Penukonda as a chieftain; died 1680, the end of the empire |
### Aravidu rulers → period and contribution — Venkatapati Raya II (1585-1614)
## 1. Venkatapati Raya II (1585-1614)
- Status: Greatest and longest-ruling Aravidu king (29 years)
- Titles: "Second Krishnadevaraya", "Second Andhra Bhoja"
- Vilapaka inscription (1601): Genealogy of the Tuluva and Aravidu dynasties
- Europeans: Ties/alliance with the Portuguese and Philip II of Spain; let the Dutch trade at Pulicat (Dutch factory)
- Jesuits: Allowed churches at Chandragiri and Nellore (APPSC-G1 2022 key)
- Court: Appayya Dikshita (Shaivarka Mani Dipika)
- Military: Defeated Veerappa Nayaka of Madurai at Vallankota
- ⚠ Vilapaka = Venkatapati II's (1601); Siddhavatam fort inscription = Venkatapati II's (1605)
### Nayakas and Palegars → terms, places and the grant of Madras
## 1. Terms

## 2. Places and battles

| Siddhavatam fort (Kadapa) | 1605 inscription of Venkatapati II → Matli Ananta built its fort walls (22 Aug 2026) |
## 3. Grant of Madras

| Name | Madras (Chennapatnam / Chennapattanam), after Damarla's father Chennappa |

### Universal angles
- Current relevance: Revisited (Jun 2026): Sadasiva Raya (1542-1570), Rama Raya's death at Talikota (Raksasa Tangadi), Caesar Frederick's 1567 visit, Tirumala's 1570 shift of capital to Penukonda
- AP angle: After 1565 empire's centre moved into AP (Penukonda, Chandragiri); Aravidus from Kurnool; Madras grant from a Chandragiri-era Nayaka
- FIRST/LAST:
