### Water Act 1974 and Water Cess Act 1977 — provisions
## 1. Water Act 1974
- Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974: To prevent and control water pollution, and maintain / restore the wholesomeness of water — created the CPCB and SPCBs
- Status: First pollution law
- Basis: Passed under Article 252 (on resolution of states) → applies in states that adopt it
- Boards: Created Central Board for the Prevention and Control of Water Pollution (1974) → renamed CPCB by 1988 amendment; + State Boards
- Section 33A: Directions and penalties
- Rules: Water Rules 1975, Cess Rules 1978
## 2. Water Cess Act 1977
- Water Cess Act: 1977
- Cess: On water consumed by specified industries and local authorities → funds the Boards
- Proceeds: Consolidated Fund of India
- Fate: Subsumed/repealed with GST in 2017
- ⚠ Water Act = Article 252; EPA = Article 253
### Air Act 1981 — provisions
## 1. Air Act
- Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act: Enacted 1981; amended 1987 — for the prevention, control and abatement of air pollution
- Dates: Enacted 29 March 1981; in force 16 May 1981; modelled on the Water Act
- Structure: 7 chapters, 54 sections
- Purpose: i.e., maintain air quality standards (APPSC-G2 2025); set up Central and State Boards
- Boards: CPCB (already existing under Water Act) got air functions; SPCBs enforce
- Noise: Air pollutant definition includes noise - added by 1987 amendment
- Air pollution control areas: Declared by State Government (Chapter 4, Sections 19-31)
- Section 31A: Power to give directions; PCBs can levy compensation
## 2. Water Act vs Air Act
| Feature | Water Act 1974 | Air Act 1981 |
| Aim | Maintain and restore wholesomeness of water | Prevent, control and abate air pollution |
| Structure | 8 chapters, 64 sections | 7 chapters, 54 sections |
| Boards | Created CPCB and SPCBs | Same boards given air functions |
| Special power | Standards for discharge of effluents | Declare air pollution control areas; emission standards for industry and automobiles |
- ⚠ Water Act 8 chapters, 64 sections; Air Act 7 chapters, 54 sections; noise added 1987
### Environment (Protection) Act 1986 — background and constitutional basis
## 1. The Act
- Enacted 1986 — umbrella legislation; framework to coordinate central and state authorities set up under the Water Act 1974 and Air Act 1981
- Background: The Bhopal Gas Tragedy (Dec 1984); a major source — the Stockholm Conference of 1972 (implements its decisions)
- Constitutional basis: Article 253 (Art. 253)
- Purpose: Protection and improvement of the environment
- PYQ wording: Framework for protecting and improving the environment (to protect and improve the environment) + control pollution (APPSC 2025 answer)
- Last amendment mentioned: 1991
- Central Government: A major coordinating and enabling role - the primary authority
## 2. Constitutional provisions
- 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act: 1976
- Article 48A: The State shall protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife
- Article 51A(g): Citizens shall protect and improve the natural environment and show compassion for living creatures
### Environment (Protection) Act 1986 — powers and authorities
## 1. Key sections
- Section 3(3): Power to constitute authorities; Section 5: directions and measures; Sections 15–21: penal provisions
## 2. Powers of the Centre
- Lay down emission/discharge standards
- Regulate location of industries
- Issue directions including closure (Section 5)
## 3. Authorities under Sec 3(3)
- Central Ground Water Authority (1997)
- National Ganga River Basin Authority (2009)

### Penalties and decriminalisation — Jan Vishwas Act 2023

### Organisations → role — CPCB, SPCBs and MoEFCC
## 1. Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)
- A statutory organization / statutory body — constituted in September 1974 under the Water Act, 1974; under MoEFCC; also gives technical services to the Ministry under EPA 1986
- The national-level pollution-control board — functions at the central level; deals with water and air pollution
- Prevents, controls and abates water pollution; controls air and water pollution; improves air quality
- State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs): Implement pollution-control functions at the state level
- CPCB and SPCBs: Monitor water bodies and industrial discharges; can take action against violators
- Chairperson: Nominated by Central Government (SPCB chair by State Government)
- Functions: CPCB co-ordinates SPCBs; technical assistance, research, training, national monitoring
- Enforcement body under EPA framework (PYQ): CPCB
- Monitoring:
- NAMP network 1,646 stations in 603 cities (March 2026) (earlier: 516 cities, 1,400+ stations)
- Real-time via SAMEER app (12 Sep 2026)
- National AQI launched 2015
## 2. MoEF/MoEFCC
- Evolution: Department of Environment 1980 → Ministry of Environment and Forests 1985 → renamed MoEFCC in 2014
- Bodies under it:
- CPCB (statutory)
- Central Zoo Authority (1992, under WPA 1972)
### Organisations → role — AP Pollution Control Board
## 1. AP Pollution Control Board
- Role (PYQ): APPCB implements environmental laws and rules in AP
- Laws implemented: Water, Air and EP Acts + waste rules (Hazardous, Bio-medical, Plastic, Solid)
- Other functions:
- Siting guidelines for industries
- SOPs for marine outfall monitoring and hazardous waste
- Ease of Doing Business: Computerised inspection allocation - high-risk industries inspected more often (APSES 2025-26)
- APPCB head office: Vijayawada, with 3 Zonal and 13 Regional Offices; constituted 1976 as the State Water Board, later renamed APPCB (APPSC 2022). Not in your sources: the name of its online consent system.
### Consent mechanism — CTE/CTO and industry colour categories
## 1. Consent stages
- Prior consent: Both the Water and Air Acts require prior consent of the Board to establish/operate a polluting unit; industries need it before discharging effluent
- Two-stage consent from SPCB:
- Consent to Establish (CTE): before setting up
- Consent to Operate (CTO): before production
- Jan 2026 amendment: Single integrated application for Air and Water Act consents + waste-rule authorisations
- CTO valid until cancelled (earlier periodic renewal) (29 Jan 2026)
## 2. Industry colour categories
| Category | CPCB Pollution Index score | Pollution level |

| Orange | 41-59 | Moderately polluting |
| Green | 21-40 | Low polluting |
| White | 20 or less | Practically non-polluting - no consent/EC needed (do not need environmental clearance) |
- Blue category (new): Essential environmental services (e.g. Compressed Biogas) (Economic Survey 2025-26)
- Basis: Emissions, effluents and hazardous waste generated (CPCB, Jun 2026)
- ⚠ Red ≥ 60, White ≤ 20; Blue = essential environmental services

## Common provisions and APPCB
- Water Act and Air Act both require prior consent of the Board to establish and operate a polluting unit
- APPCB — set up 1976 under the Water Act
- APPCB implements Water, Air and EP Acts plus rules on hazardous, biomedical, plastic and solid waste
- APPCB also issues siting guidelines for industries; SOPs for marine outfall monitoring and hazardous waste

### Water Act vs Air Act
- Water Act, 1974 — India's first comprehensive pollution-control law; standards for discharge of effluents
- Air Act amended 1987 (not 1991) — added noise to definition of "air pollutant"
- State Government declares air pollution control areas — Chapter IV, Sections 19–31 of Air Act; a special power of the Air Act, not the Water Act
- CPCB function — coordinating SPCBs; SPCB functions — inspection, sample analysis, granting consents
- Environmental compensation — Section 31A (Air Act), Section 33A (Water Act); upheld, but only when harm is clear or imminent
### EPA 1986: umbrella law
## Water Act and Cess Act

## EPA 1986
- Purpose — protect and improve the environment and control pollution
- Basis — Article 253 (laws to implement international agreements); gives effect to Stockholm Conference, 1972
- Background — Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984); called umbrella legislation

- Section 5 — directions; Section 15 — penalties
- Section 16 — Environment Protection Fund (Public Account); fines under Air, Water, EP Acts; 75% States/UTs, 25% Centre
- Notified under EPA — Green Credit Rules, Eco-Sensitive Zone notifications, Plastic Waste Management Rules
- Original penalty — up to 5 years or fine Rs 1 lakh, plus Rs 5,000/day for continuing offence
