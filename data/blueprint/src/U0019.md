### Iron — terms, sites and earliest dates — Vedic metal terms
## 1. Vedic metal terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Ayas (Rigveda) | Copper/bronze |
| Krishna Ayas / Shyama Ayas | Iron ("black metal") in Later Vedic texts (also written shyama ayas / krishna ayas) |

## 2. Iron in North India
- Dating: Iron introduced c. 1000-800 BCE; common at most PGW sites; smiths and smelters important after 1000 BCE
- Sites: Atranjikhera, Hastinapur, Kaushambi, Taxila/Gandhara (c. 1000 BC)
- Uses:
- Iron axes + fire cleared the Ganga (Gangetic) forests (Videgha Madhava legend)
- Iron weapons + horse-drawn chariots helped the eastward push
### Iron — terms, sites and earliest dates — Earliest iron — new dates from South India
## 1. Earliest iron — new dates from South India
- New dates (South India): South Indian Iron Age Now placed around 2100 BCE, earlier than the Gangetic Iron Age (reported Jun 2026)
- Tamil Nadu iron dates push Indian iron use far before the Gangetic c. 1000 BCE date
| Site | District (TN) | Finding |
| Mayiladumparai | Krishnagiri | AMS-dated to 2172 BCE; was the earliest claim until the Sivagalai dates (2025) |
| Sivagalai | Thoothukudi | Iron (artefacts) dated to about 2953 BCE (paddy + iron) |
| Thirumalapuram | Tenkasi | Iron Age urn chamber, early-mid 3rd millennium BCE |
| Adichanallur | Thoothukudi | Type-site for urn burials |
- ⚠ Krishna ayas = iron; lohita ayas = copper; Rigvedic ayas = copper/bronze
- Exam angle: BPSC 2022 asked when iron use began, with options 6,000/9,000/12,000 years ago (range 6,000-12,000); the key was "none of the above".
### Agriculture, settlements, crafts and trade — features
## 1. Agriculture
- Chief crops: rice (vrihi) + wheat; barley continues
- Rice recommended for Vedic rituals
- Ploughing:
- Ploughs drawn (yoked with) 6, 8, 12 and even 24 oxen
## 2. Settlements
- Settlements: villages of mud/wattle houses
- burnt bricks not prevalent; Hastinapur's mud structures modest
- True towns: Only after the Vedic period, in the NBPW phase ("second urbanisation": Shravasti, Kausambi, Hastinapur)
## 3. Crafts and trade
- Crafts: Iron-working, copper, weaving (by women), leather, pottery, carpentry
- Trade:
- Shresthins → merchant groups
- Nishka = unit of value; barter still main
### Pottery — PGW sites and the protohistoric sequence — Later Vedic pottery
## 1. Later Vedic pottery
- Later Vedic phase: 1000-600 BC
- Four wares: BRW, BSW, PGW, Red Ware
- Full names: Black and Red Ware, Black Slipped Ware, Painted Grey Ware, Red Ware
- ⚠ Trap: not NBPW - list questions exclude it (APPSC-G1 2024)
## 2. Painted Grey Ware — features
- PGW features: Grey pottery with linear painted designs; mostly bowls and dishes; probably used by the rising upper strata
- Dating: PGW is Later Vedic (c. 1000-600 BCE), Gangetic plain
- ⚠ Trap: some notes put PGW in the Early Vedic period - wrong
- Number: About 700 Painted Grey Ware (PGW) sites, mostly in the upper Ganga basin
- Finds: Iron weapons + earthen bowls and dishes + copper objects
- Copper possibly from Khetri, Rajasthan

## 2. Pottery sequence of Indian protohistory — order and period
| Order | Pottery | Period / association |
| 1 | Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) | Post/late-Harappan, c. 2nd millennium BCE; before PGW |
| 2 | Black and Red Ware (BRW) | Chalcolithic onwards; also Later Vedic; Megalithic/Iron Age south |
| 3 | Black Slipped Ware (BSW) | Later Vedic |
| 4 | Painted Grey Ware (PGW) | Later Vedic, 1000-600 BC, Gangetic plain (c. 1000-600 BCE) - Later Vedic hallmark/marker |
| 5 | Red Ware | Later Vedic |
| 6 | Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) | Not Later Vedic - c. 600 BCE onwards - Mahajanapadas, second urbanisation |
- ⚠ OCP → BRW → BSW → PGW → Red Ware → NBPW; only NBPW is post-Vedic

### Later Vedic gods → rise and decline
## 1. Later Vedic gods
| God | Later Vedic position |
| Prajapati | Creator; became the supreme god |
| Vishnu | Rose as protector/preserver (a minor god in the Rigveda) |
| Rudra | God of animals; grew and later merged with Shiva |
| Pushan | Cattle god; became the god of the Shudras |

## 2. Nature of worship
- Trend: Ritual-heavy worship → prompted the Upanishadic critique
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- BHU opened 22 sealed boxes of 10,000+ artefacts from Rajghat (Varanasi, Kashi Janapada capital)
- Finds: pre-NBPW pottery about 3,000 years old
- Dates Varanasi's urban origins to about 800 BCE (Sep 2026)
- AP angle:
- Iron Age in Andhra (AP) = Megalithic culture/burials with Black and Red Ware / Black-and-Red Ware (BRW pottery; Brahmagiri, Nagarjunakonda, Gudivada, Jonnawada)

- Comparative note: Early Vedic = tribe, cattle, voluntary bali; Later Vedic = territory, agriculture (rice vrihi and wheat), compulsory taxes
