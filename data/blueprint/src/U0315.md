### Assembly — term, membership, reservation and quorum
## 1. Term — Article 172
- Five years; dissolvable (temporary); cannot be abolished
- Term: 5 years (Art. 172); may be dissolved earlier
- National Emergency (Article 352): Parliament may extend it by law one year at a time — at most up to six months after the Emergency ceases
- Extended by Parliament 1 year at a time during a National Emergency, not beyond 6 months after the Emergency ends
- Council of Ministers: Collectively responsible to the Assembly — Article 164(2); capped at 15 percent of the Assembly (Article 164, 91st Amendment Act, 2003); minimum 12 ministers including the Chief Minister (minimum size); a non-member minister for at most six consecutive months
## 2. Tenures, sessions and quorum
- Five years: The President, the Lok Sabha, a State Legislative Assembly, a Panchayat and a Municipality — the constitutional tenure
- Governor: A five-year term — holds office during the pleasure of the President; continues in office until the successor assumes office
- Sessions (Art. 174): No more than 6 months between sittings
- Quorum: 10 members or 1/10th (10 members or one-tenth) of the House (Art. 189(3)), whichever is greater
## 2. Membership and reservation
- Minimum age (Article 173): Assembly 25 years; Council 30 years
- Reservation (Article 332): Seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in every Assembly, in proportion to their population in the State
- 104th Amendment Act (2020): Ended Anglo-Indian nomination — Article 333
- 106th Amendment Act (2023): About one-third reservation for women in directly elected seats — effective after post-census delimitation

### Legislative Council — size, tenure and officers
## 1. Size
- Size: Maximum one-third of the Assembly; minimum 40 members (Art. 171)
- Maximum = 1/3 of the Assembly's total strength (one-third of the Assembly); minimum = 40 (at least 40)
- Actual strength fixed by Parliament → keeps the directly elected House predominant
- State Legislative Council — Article 171: Maximum one-third of the strength of the State Legislative Assembly; minimum 40 members
- Uttar Pradesh Council — largest Legislative Council: 100 members
## 2. Tenure and eligibility
- Permanent body: Cannot be dissolved (not even on the Governor's recommendation) — member's term six years; one-third retire every two years
- A permanent body (not dissolved); 6 years per member; 1/3 of members retire every second year (one-third retiring every two years)
- Six years: A Rajya Sabha member and a State Legislative Council member — one-third of members retire every two years
- Minimum age for membership: 30 years (Art. 173)
- Chairman: Elected by the Council from among its members — Article 182 (not nominated by the Governor)
- Called: The Upper House / House of Elders
### Article 169 and States with Legislative Councils
## 1. Article 169 — creation and abolition
- State Assembly initiates it by passing a resolution by special majority — a majority (at least 50 percent) of the total membership and at least two-thirds of members present and voting
- Prep wording: a majority of its total membership + at least 2/3 of members present and voting
- The Assembly is the base House for creation or abolition
- The resolution is recommendatory — it does not legally compel the Union to introduce a bill (does not compel the Union to introduce a Bill in Parliament)
- Parliament then enacts the law creating or abolishing the Council by simple majority, like an ordinary bill — not an amendment of the Constitution under Article 368
- This law is not deemed an amendment under Art. 368 (APPSC-GS 2018: Art. 169)
- Governor's role: The Governor has no role in initiating or approving the resolution
- Limits vs strength: Constitution fixes the Council's maximum and minimum limits; Parliament fixes its strength
- Implication: The Council's existence depends on the will of the Assembly
- ⚠ Assembly = special majority; Parliament = simple majority; Governor = no role

### Presiding officers — election, tenure and vacating office
## 1. Election, tenure and vacating office
- Speaker and Deputy Speaker (Article 178): Elected by the Assembly from among its sitting members
- On dissolution: The Speaker does not vacate office immediately — continues until just before the first meeting of the new Assembly
- Holds office for the life of the Assembly
- Resignation (Article 179): Speaker → Deputy Speaker; Deputy Speaker → Speaker (Resigns to the Deputy Speaker, APPSC-GS 2018 key)
- Other ways of vacating (Art. 179):
- Removed by a resolution of a majority of all the then members, after 14 days' notice
- During a removal resolution (Art. 181):
- Speaker cannot preside (does not preside)
- May speak and votes in the first instance only

### Presiding officers — powers
## 1. Powers
- Voting: The Speaker cannot vote in the first instance — has a casting vote when votes are equally divided (Art. 189)
- Money Bill: Final decision on whether a bill is a Money Bill. → certification by the Speaker (Art. 199(3))
- House management: Adjourns or suspends the House for lack of quorum; maintains order
- Anti-defection: Decides Tenth Schedule disqualification → subject to judicial review (Kihoto Hollohan 1992)
- Other powers:
- Allows a secret sitting
## 2. Deputies and Council officers
- Officers:
- Assembly = Speaker / Deputy Speaker
- Council = Chairman / Deputy Chairman (resign to each other)
- Deputy Speaker (Art. 178): Acts with full powers when the Speaker's post is vacant or he is absent
- Council: The Chairman resigns to the Deputy Chairman

### Money Bills and Ordinary Bills in the states
## 1. Money Bills — Articles 198, 199 and 207
- Introduced only in the Assembly, on the Governor's prior recommendation (Article 207)
- Introduced only here (only in the Assembly), by a minister (APPSC-GS 2018 key: Governor)
- State Money Bill: Originates in the Legislative Assembly with the Governor's recommendation; the Council gets 14 days
- Speaker's certification of a Money Bill is sole and final
- Council: Only recommends; must return it within 14 days — cannot amend or reject it; the Assembly may accept or reject its recommendations; the Assembly has final authority (Art. 198)
## 2. Ordinary Bills — Article 197
- The Assembly is the originating and overriding chamber; its will prevails
- Council's delay: Up to three months the first time; after the Assembly re-passes it, one month the second time — four months in all; the bill is then deemed passed
- Legislative Council can delay an Ordinary Bill three months the first time and one month the second time — maximum four months
- No veto — the Council cannot permanently block ordinary legislation
- ⚠ Council delay: 14 days (Money Bill); 3 months + 1 month (ordinary bill)
- Council's power: Only delaying; much weaker than the Rajya Sabha's
- Constitutional amendments: Council has no role in ratifying them
## 3. Reservation
- Reservation: The Governor can reserve a State Bill for the President — Article 200; the President considers it — Article 201
