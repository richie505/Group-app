### Terms → meaning (hazard, vulnerability, disaster, risk), vulnerability types and hazard types
## 1. Types of vulnerability
| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| Physical | Susceptibility of buildings, infrastructure and physical assets | Weak houses in earthquake zones; settlements in floodplains |
| Social | Based on age, gender, caste, disability, social status | Women, children, elderly, SC/ST communities, persons with disabilities |
| Economic | Poverty; lack of livelihood options; low income | Daily wage workers, small farmers, informal sector workers |
| Environmental | Degradation of natural systems that increases risk | Deforestation → landslides; loss of mangroves → greater cyclone impact |
| Systemic / institutional | Weak governance; poor coordination; lack of preparedness | Inadequate early warning systems; delayed response |
- Community vulnerability factors: kutcha housing, dependence on farm labour, thin health infrastructure, low crop-insurance cover, weak early warning
## 2. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Hazard | Potentially damaging event |
| Disaster | Hazard meeting a vulnerable population → Hazard x Vulnerability = Disaster; Sec. 2(d) of the DM Act: Disaster = catastrophe causing substantial loss beyond the community's coping capacity |
| Risk | Likelihood of loss; rises with hazard, exposure, vulnerability; falls with coping capacity |
- Earthquake in an empty desert = hazard, not a disaster
## 3. Hazard types
| Type | Examples |
| Geological | Earthquakes, landslides, volcanoes, tsunamis |
| Hydro-meteorological | Cyclones, floods, droughts, cloudbursts, heat waves |
| Biological | Epidemics, pest attacks |
### HPC 1999 and the road to the DM Act
- High Powered Committee (HPC): Constituted in August 1999 for a systematic, comprehensive and holistic approach to disasters — first Chairman J.C. Pant
- Original mandate: Plans for natural disasters only — later expanded to man-made disasters
- Report: Submitted 2001 — theme "building a culture of prevention"; a shift from relief-centric approaches to prevention, mitigation and preparedness
- Classified disasters into five broad categories
- Bhuj earthquake, Gujarat (2001): Influenced institutional reforms
- Nodal ministry: The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) — subjects shifted from the Department / Ministry of Agriculture to MHA after the Gujarat earthquake of 2001 (in 2002); MHA is nodal at the Central level after 2002
- Indian Ocean Tsunami (2004): A major catalyst — it exposed gaps in India's disaster response system
- First responder: The community — neighbours and local people
- Uttarakhand: The first state to establish a dedicated Ministry of Disaster Management
- Key approach: Shifted from hazard-centric management to multi-hazard risk reduction
- Sendai's first priority: Understanding disaster risk, including hazards

### DM Act 2005 and NPDM 2009 — background and objective
## 1. The Act
- Full name of the law: The Disaster Management Act, 2005 — Act No. 53 of 2005; enacted 2005
- Enacted after the 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami exposed gaps in India's disaster response system — the tsunami acted as a major catalyst for the Act
- Passed / Presidential assent: December 23, 2005; came into force progressively from 2006 onwards
- National-level legislation — a legal and institutional framework for effective disaster management; extends to the whole of India
- 11 Chapters; 79 Sections
- Background (Prep): High-powered committee 1999; National Committee after the Gujarat earthquake; Enacted: Dec 2005 (23 Dec 2005)
- Core shift: from relief-centric response to proactive prevention, mitigation and preparedness
## 2. Ministry of Home Affairs
- The nodal ministry for disaster management — at the Union level (except drought, pest attack and epidemics); Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) - overall coordination
- Shifted from the Department of Agriculture after the Gujarat Earthquake of 2001
- Overall coordination at the national level; provides support, coordination and resources to the State Governments
- Primary responsibility lies with the State Governments
## 3. National Policy on Disaster Management (NPDM)
- Issued 2009 — approved by the Union Cabinet on 22 October 2009; prepared by the NDMA; formulated in pursuance of the DM Act, 2005
- Vision: A safe and disaster-resilient India — holistic, proactive, multi-disaster oriented and technology-driven
- Core approach: Shifted from relief-centric response to prevention, mitigation and preparedness
### Shift in approach — 3Rs to 3Ps, and mitigation
| Basis | Pre-2005 | Post-DM Act 2005 |
| Approach | The three Rs — rescue, relief, rehabilitation | The three Ps — prevention, preparedness, proofing / mitigation |
| Nature | Relief-centric; reactive | Holistic; multi-phase; a continuous cycle; proactive — Build Back Better |
- The disaster management cycle is continuous — lessons from recovery lead to better prevention
- Sendai Framework (2015–2030): Global alignment for disaster management
## 1. Mitigation
- Definition (in the DM Act, 2005): Measures aimed at reducing the risk, impact or effects of a disaster or threatening disaster situation
- Belongs to the pre-disaster phase — with prevention and preparedness; the most cost-effective phase of the Disaster Management Cycle
- Goal: Reduce vulnerability; lessen the impact of hazards
- Paradigm shift: From relief-centric (the 3Rs) to mitigation-centric (prevention, mitigation and preparedness)
- General measures: Risk assessment; non-structural mitigation — community awareness and education programmes

### NDRF — the specialist response force
## 1. Establishment and control
- NDRF — National Disaster Response Force: A disaster response force — response force operations
- Established 2006 — legal basis Section 44 of the DM Act, 2005; Raising Day 19 January
- Parent ministry: Ministry of Home Affairs; controlling authority: the NDMA (Prep: Superintendence: NDMA)
- Headquarters: New Delhi; regional HQ for AP, Telangana and Karnataka — Vijayawada; NDRF Academy — Nagpur
- Motto: "Aapada Seva Sadaiv Sarvatra" — "Disaster Service Always Everywhere"
- Director General: A senior IPS officer — rank of ADG
## 2. Strength and composition
- The world's largest dedicated disaster response force
- 16 battalions — expanded from an initial 8; about 1,149 personnel per battalion; a total strength of more than 18,000
- Each battalion: 18 self-contained Search and Rescue teams — engineers, technicians, electricians, dog squads, medical and paramedic personnel
- Parent forces (on deputation): BSF, CRPF, CISF, ITBP, SSB, Assam Rifles — Prep: drawn from the central paramilitary forces
## 3. Roles and skills
- Responds to: Floods, earthquakes, cyclones, landslides, avalanches, building collapses, industrial accidents, and chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear emergencies
- Skills: Collapsed Structure Search and Rescue (CSSR), flood rescue, mountain rescue, Medical First Response
- Pre-positions teams before disasters — strategic placement based on the vulnerability profile across India; multiple Regional Response Centres for faster response
- AP presence: NDRF 10th Battalion + an NIDM southern campus near Vijayawada/Gannavaram, AP
- Not in your sources: the current number of NDRF battalions and the names and places of recent NDRF exercises.
### SDRF — the State Disaster Response Force
- SDRF — State Disaster Response Force: A state-level specialised response force — the counterpart of the NDRF
- Legal basis: The DM Act, 2005 — including Section 44 and related rules
- Raised by the respective State or Union Territory Governments — the first responder within the state, during the first 24–48 hours
- Trained by the NDRF or the NDRF Academy — similar training standards
- Drawn mainly from the State Police; also Armed Police, Fire Services, Home Guards, Civil Defence
- Organisation: A battalion model — similar to the NDRF; multi-disciplinary teams
- Specialises in search and rescue, flood rescue, collapsed-structure rescue, and CBRN response in some states
- Gap: SDRFs not yet set up in all states → strain on NDRF during simultaneous disasters

### Disaster management cycle — phases
- Disaster management has four phases
| Phase | When | Aim | Includes |
| Prevention | Before a disaster | Stop a hazard from becoming a disaster — proactive and long-term | Land-use zoning; regulating deforestation; controlling industrial siting |
| Mitigation | Before a disaster | Reduce disaster risk and the severity / impact; minimise future losses — the most cost-effective phase | Earthquake-resistant buildings, embankments, building codes, afforestation, insurance, hazard mapping |
| Preparedness / preparation | Before a disaster | Readiness — ensures a ready and effective response; capacity building; the first and critical step | Preparedness planning, (Early) Warning Systems, evacuation arrangements, community preparedness, emergency resources, coordination, mock drills, stockpiling, training through Aapda Mitra, Emergency Operation Centres, contingency plans |
| Response | During or immediately / just after | Immediate action — save lives; meet basic needs; time-critical | Search and rescue, evacuation, first aid, temporary shelters, food and water, law and order |
| Recovery | After a disaster | Restore normal life / normalcy; reduce future vulnerability; Build Back Better — long-term; builds resilience | Rehabilitation, reconstruction, livelihood support, psycho-social care, resilient infrastructure |
- Prep additions by phase: Prevention/Mitigation - retrofitting, land-use planning; Preparedness - DM plans, training responders (Aapda Mitra), stockpiles, early warning (CAP-SACHET); Response - search and rescue (NDRF, armed forces), shelter, food, medical aid, psychological support, restoring critical services; Recovery and Reconstruction - damage assessment, compensation, livelihoods, "Build Back Better", psycho-social care
- Prep merges Prevention/Mitigation into one phase; Response = During (some notes: Post-disaster)
- Risk assessment leads to mitigation
- Non-structural mitigation: Community awareness and education programmes
- Cyclone mitigation: Planting trees along coastal areas; earthquake mitigation: reframing and implementing building-construction guidelines
### Disaster stages, response and IMD colour code
## 1. Stages
| Stage | Covers | Focus |
| Pre-disaster | Prevention, mitigation, preparedness | Risk reduction and readiness |
| During-disaster | Response | Immediate life-saving action |
| Post-disaster (post-crisis) | Recovery, rehabilitation, reconstruction | Rebuild better and reduce future risk |
- Modern view: Phases = overlapping continuum, not a line
## 2. Response
- The community is usually the first responder
- Urban response: Civic authorities, the fire brigade, local police — the district administration has the basic responsibility; the Collector plays a pivotal role
- Search and Rescue: A major role in the post-disaster scenario
## 3. Disaster sequence
- During a disaster: Early warning → evacuation → rescue → shelters → emergency medical or water supply
- After a disaster: Relief → restoration → reconstruction → resilient rebuilding

### GIS in disaster management → what it is and uses across the cycle
## 1. What GIS is

### Key terms
## Hazard, disaster, mitigation
- Hazard — a possibly harmful event (e.g. an earthquake in an uninhabited desert with no damage)
- becomes a disaster when it hits a vulnerable population or exposed assets
- Section 2(d), DM Act 2005 — disaster causes loss the affected community cannot cope with (not the State Government)
- Mitigation (DM Act) — measures to reduce the risk, impact or effects of a disaster or threatening disaster situation
- Mitigation Funds (National and State) — for long-term reduction of disaster risk, not immediate relief alone
## Vulnerability types
| Type | Example |
| Environmental | mangrove loss (raises cyclone impact), deforestation |
| Physical | weak houses |
| Social | caste and gender discrimination |
| Economic | poverty |
| Systemic / institutional | weak governance, poor coordination, lack of preparedness |
## Phases of the cycle
- Prevention — proactive, long-term; e.g. regulating deforestation
- CAP-SACHET — early warning dissemination; a preparedness measure (like mock drills, stockpiling)
- Response — time-critical; includes restoring critical services (long-term activity = recovery)
- Recovery and reconstruction — compensation and psychosocial care
## Laws, policy and plans
- 2009 National Policy on DM — prevention, mitigation, community participation, mainstreaming DRR (not relying only on post-disaster relief)
