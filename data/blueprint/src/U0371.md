# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:97 - Public-private partnerships - models (BOT, BOOT, HAM), issues; disinvestment and strategic sale policy
### PPP models & risk
| Model | Who finances / bears risk |
| BOT (Toll) | Private finances, collects toll; bears revenue risk |
| BOT (Annuity) | Govt pays fixed annuity; govt bears revenue risk |
| BOOT / BOO | Private owns during concession / no transfer back |
| EPC | Govt funds entirely; private only constructs |
| HAM | Govt 40% in construction; developer 60% as semi-annual annuities over ~15 years; govt bears traffic risk |
| TOT | Toll rights on built highways to private |
### PPP institutions & terms
- NIIF (2015): quasi-sovereign fund; Centre 49%
- Swiss challenge: unsolicited proposal → counter-bids → proponent may match
- IPAT = tribunal; IPRC = review committee; aggressive bidding = winner's curse
- HAM first outside roads: Namami Gange STPs
- MoRTH 2026: HAM net worth 20% (from 15%); funds may bid for BOT

### PPP models → expansion and risk allocation
| Model | Expansion | Who finances / bears the risk |
| BOT (Toll) | Build-Operate-Transfer | Private player finances, builds, operates and collects toll; bears revenue risk |
| BOT (Annuity) | Build-Operate-Transfer | Government pays a fixed annuity; government bears revenue risk |
| BOOT | Build-Own-Operate-Transfer | Private player owns the asset during the concession, then transfers it [GK] |
| BOLT | Build-Own-Lease-Transfer | Private player builds and leases the asset to government, then transfers it [GK] |
| DBFO(T) | Design-Build-Finance-Operate-(Transfer) | Private player also designs and finances [GK] |
| EPC | Engineering, Procurement, Construction | Government funds entirely; private player only constructs |
| HAM | Hybrid Annuity Model | Government pays 40% during construction; developer's 60% repaid as semi-annual annuities over about 15 years; government collects tolls and bears traffic risk |
| TOT | Toll-Operate-Transfer | Toll rights on already-built highways transferred to private players |
- HAM: blends EPC + BOT-Annuity
- ⚠ Trap: HAM 40 – 60 = government – developer
- FIRST outside roads: Namami Gange STPs pioneered HAM outside roads → performance-linked payments under One City One Operator model
- MoRTH changes in 2026:
- Revised BOT guidelines (May 2026): sovereign wealth funds, pension and infrastructure funds and private equity may bid for BOT (earlier only TOT)
- Stricter HAM norms: developer net worth = 20% of project cost (up from 15%); disqualification for major structural collapse within 2 years (LENS, May 2026)
### PPP institutions and terms → meaning
## 1. Institutions
| Institution | Role [GK unless marked] |
| PPPAC (PPP Appraisal Committee) | Appraises central-sector PPP projects; chaired by the Secretary, Department of Economic Affairs |
| VGF scheme (2006) | Capital grant for economically justified but commercially unviable PPPs: up to 20% of project cost from the Centre plus 20% from the sponsoring authority (up to 30% + 30% for social-sector projects) |
| IIFCL (2006) | Long-term debt for infrastructure projects |
| NIIF (2015) | Quasi-sovereign wealth fund; Centre holds 49%; co-invests with global investors |
## 2. Terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Swiss challenge | Private party submits an unsolicited proposal → govt invites counter-bids → original proponent may match the best one |
| Aggressive bidding | Winner's curse |
| IPAT | Adjudication tribunal → faster dispute resolution |
| IPRC | Project review committee → faster project review |
| Asset monetisation | Alternative mode of financing → revenue from under-utilised public assets via private participation; asset not sold |
| InvITs and REITs | New financial structures used by public asset owners under asset monetisation |
### Asset monetisation — National Monetisation Pipeline 1.0 and 2.0
- Strategic objective: unlock value of investments in brownfield public-sector assets by tapping institutional + long-term capital
- Gives public asset owners the opportunity to use new financial structures → InvITs and REITs
- Nature of transfer: rights for a limited period (lease/licence, not indefinite) → assets return to government
- Only public assets covered, not private
| Feature | NMP 1.0 | NMP 2.0 |
| Launched | Aug 2021, NITI Aayog [GK] | Asset Monetisation Plan 2025-30, announced in Budget 2025-26 |
| Period | FY22-FY25 [GK] | FY26-FY30 |
| Size | About ₹6 lakh crore[GK] | ₹16.72 lakh crore, including ₹5.8 lakh crore of private investment |
| Sectors | Roads, railways, power, pipelines, telecom and others [GK] | 12: highways 26%, power 17%, railways 16%, ports 16%, coal 13%; also petroleum and gas, civil aviation, warehousing, urban infrastructure, mines, telecom, tourism |
- NMP 2.0 proceeds: mostly Consolidated Fund of India → direct private investment → PSU/Port Authority allocation → State Consolidated Funds
- Exam angle: APPSC-G1 2024 asked the "strategic objective": public asset owners using new financial structures, not private assets or indefinite leases.
### Disinvestment — DIPAM, types and methods
## 1. DIPAM
- Role: under Ministry of Finance; manages Centre's equity in CPSEs; runs disinvestment via strategic sale, OFS and other methods
- Evolution:
- Began as Department of Disinvestment in 1999 (APP says 2000)
## 2. Types of disinvestment
| Type | Government's position |
| Minority disinvestment | Keeps >51% and management control |
| Majority disinvestment | Hands over control, keeps a minority stake |
| Strategic sale | Transfers a block of shares plus management control to a strategic partner |
| Complete privatisation | Sells 100% ownership and control |
## 3. Methods - OFS, ETFs, IPO, strategic sale
| Method | Meaning |
| IPO | First-time public issue |
| FPO | Further issue by an already listed PSE |
| OFS | Auction of shares on stock exchange; used widely since 2012 |
| IPP | Placement to Qualified Institutional Buyers only |
| ETFs | CPSE ETF + Bharat-22 ETF |
| Buyback | PSE buys back shares, government tenders its stake [GK] |
- ⚠ Trap: IPO = first-time issue; FPO = already listed PSE
### Disinvestment phases, FIRSTs and strategic sales → buyer
## 1. Phases of disinvestment
- 1991: government announced 20% disinvestment in selected PSUs
- Phases:
- 1991-98: raised only ₹17,557 crore → held back by coalition politics
- Until 1999-2000: only minority stakes sold
- 1999-2004: shift to strategic sales (e.g. VSNL, BALCO)

### Policies and Acts → year and provisions (New PSE Policy 2021)
## 1. New PSE Policy 2021
- New Public Sector Enterprise Policy 2021 (Atmanirbhar Bharat): exit non-strategic sectors; bare-minimum presence in strategic sectors
- Four strategic sector groups:
- Atomic energy, space and defence
- Transport and telecommunications
- Power, petroleum, coal and other minerals
- Banking, insurance and financial services
- DPE: handles closure/privatisation proposals for non-strategic sector along with DIPAM
## 2. Recent steps
- LIC IPO (17 May 2022) = India's biggest issue at the time
- GIC Re OFS: 3.39% sold on 4-5 Sept 2024 → about ₹2,345.55 crore (Rs 2,345.55 crore) raised
- General Insurance Business (Nationalisation) Act amended in 2021
## 3. Trend — from selling PSUs to earning dividends
- Trend: focus shifted from selling PSUs to earning from them (TH, 1 Mar 2026)
- Disinvestment revenue fell every year except a 2022-23 surge
- Dividends grew every year
### Committees on PPP and disinvestment → year, chair and recommendation
| Committee | Year | Subject / key recommendation |
| C. Rangarajan | 1993 | Disinvestment: up to 49% in PSUs reserved for the public sector; wider ownership and listing |
| Disinvestment Commission (G.V. Ramakrishna) | 1996 | Strategic sale of selected PSUs; privatisation roadmap |
| Vijay Kelkar | 2012 | Fiscal consolidation roadmap; faster disinvestment and corporate governance reforms (CDI) |
| Vijay Kelkar (on revisiting and revitalising PPP) | 2015 [GK] | Renegotiation mechanism, periodic review of concession agreements, an Infrastructure PPP Project Review Committee (IPRC), an Infrastructure PPP Adjudication Tribunal (IPAT) headed by a former SC/HC judge, and discouraging PSUs from bidding for toll PPPs |
- ⚠ Trap: Rangarajan 1993 = up to 49%; Ramakrishna 1996 = strategic sale; Kelkar 2015 = PPP
### PPP issues — stalled projects, renegotiation, Swiss challenge
- Stalled projects: aggressive bidding (winner's curse) + traffic/revenue shortfalls + land/clearance delays → projects stalled
- Renegotiation: contracts then need renegotiation → Kelkar Committee wanted a built-in mechanism
- Swiss challenge: criticised for information asymmetry; Kelkar Committee (2015) discouraged it
- Proposed bodies: IPAT (adjudication tribunal) + IPRC (project review committee)
### AP PPP projects — Enabling Act, ports, PPP Policy-2026
- AP Infrastructure Development Enabling Act, 2001: legal framework for PPP projects in AP, incl. Swiss-challenge proposals
| Project | PPP detail [GK] |

| Gangavaram Port (Visakhapatnam) | PPP deep-water port; acquired by Adani Ports (2021) |
- Draft AP PPP Policy-2026: approved by AP Cabinet on 6 Aug 2026 (TH, 7 Aug 2026)
- Sectors: transport, logistics, ports, airports, healthcare, education, renewables, tourism, urban infrastructure
- VGF: ₹1,500 crore earmarked + a proposed bridge fund
- Background: CM mooted the PPP-VGF Policy 1.0 on 16 Jul (TH 17 Jul 2026)
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- NMP 2.0 (₹16.72 lakh crore, FY26-30)
