### Nehru's Panchsheel for tribal development — five principles
## 1. Five principles
- (1) develop along their own genius
- (2) respect tribal rights in land and forests
- (3) rely on and train tribal people for their own administration
- (4) avoid over-administration; work through their own institutions
- (5) judge results by quality of human character evolved, not money spent

### Forest Rights Act 2006 — provisions — Basics
## 1. Forest Rights Act, 2006
- Also known as FRA — enacted (passed) in 2006; came into force on December 31, 2007; rules notified on January 1, 2008
- Recognizes tribal forest rights — recognizes and vests individual and community forest rights in forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers
- Full name: Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006
- Purpose: undo the "historical injustice" to forest dwellers
- Nodal ministry: Tribal Affairs (UPSC 2021 key)
- Overriding effect: Act overrides inconsistent laws (UPSC)
## 2. Sections and procedure
| Section / body | Provision |
| Sec. 6 - Gram Sabha | Gram Sabha initiates: determination of nature and extent of individual/community rights + passes resolutions on claims (UPSC 2013 key) |
| Sec. 5 | Duties: protect forests and wildlife |
| Committees | six-member Sub-Divisional and District Level Committees (Revenue, Tribal, Forest officials + elected panchayat members) examine claims → issue titles |
## 3. Eligibility - cut-off 13 December 2005
| Category | Eligibility |
| Forest-dwelling STs (FDST) | Living in, dependent on and occupying forest land before 13 December 2005 (APSES, TH) |
| Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (OTFD) | Resided in/depended on forest for 3 generations (75 years) before 2005 (LENS) |
### Forest Rights Act 2006 — provisions — Rights and ceiling
## 1. Rights and ceiling
- Individual rights: individual land, habitation, self-cultivation
- Community rights: nistar, grazing, fishing, CFR management
- Rights list:
- ownership of minor forest produce (MFP)
- habitat rights for PVTGs
- community forest resources rights to protect and manage
- Ceiling: up to 4 hectares per family (4 ha)
- Public utilities: forest land diversion for schools, dispensaries etc. on Gram Sabha recommendation; up to 1 ha per project
## 2. Habitat rights for PVTGs
- Habitat rights:
- Four State departments (Forest, Revenue, Tribal, Panchayati Raj) coordinate, with UNDP help
- First: Bharia (MP); then Kamar and Baiga (see FIRSTs page)

## FRA 2006 — basics
- Nodal ministry — Ministry of Tribal Affairs
- Aim — undo historical injustice to forest dwellers
- Recognises individual and community forest rights of forest-dwelling STs and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers
- Passed 2006; in force 31 December 2007; rules notified 1 January 2008
- STs — occupation of forest land before 13 December 2005
- OTFDs — residence/dependence for three generations (75 years) before 2005
- Ceiling — 4 hectares per family
- Overrides inconsistent laws
- Sub-Divisional and District Level Committees — six-member bodies
- Rights include minor forest produce, CFR management, PVTG habitat rights; no automatic ownership of minerals
## Rights under FRA
| Right | Type |
| Habitation and self-cultivation | individual |
| Nistar | community |
| Grazing, fishing | community |
| Ownership and disposal of MFP | community |
| Community forest resource management | community |
- Bamboo — a minor forest produce; owned by forest dwellers
- Indian Forest (Amendment) Act, 2017 — removed bamboo grown outside forest areas from the definition of "tree" (no right to fell bamboo in forests)
- First PVTG with habitat rights — Bharia (Madhya Pradesh); then Kamar and Baiga (Chhattisgarh)
- Critical Wildlife Habitat — areas of national parks/sanctuaries kept inviolate, notified after an open process with communities

## AP land laws and cases
