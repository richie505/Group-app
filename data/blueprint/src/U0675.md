### Factors of location and industry orientation — Factors
## 1. Factors
- Factors: Raw material (cuts transport cost), power, market, labour (skilled and cheap), transport (roads, railheads, ports), water, government policy (tax breaks, subsidies, SEZs), clustering, environment
- Indian examples:
- Minerals (Chotanagpur steel, coal)
- Steel belt: On the Chota Nagpur Plateau (Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal) - raw materials are weight-losing
## 2. Orientation of industries
| Orientation | Located near | Examples |
| Raw-material (weight-losing, perishable) | - | iron & steel (~4 t input per t), sugar (cane), cement (limestone) |
| Power | - | aluminium (14,000-15,000 kWh/t) |
| Market based (weight-gaining, fragile) | Consumers | Bakery, soft drinks [GK] |
| Footloose (market / footloose) | Anywhere with skills and links | cotton textiles (cotton is a "pure" material), software, electronics |
### Factors of location and industry orientation — Footloose industries
## 1. Footloose industries
- Footloose industries: Light inputs, negligible transport cost; depend on internet, logistics, skilled people
- e.g. diamond polishing, electronics assembly, software
- Clusters: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Noida
- PYQs:
- Footloose = software / diamond (APPSC 2019)
- Raw material not the main factor for electronics (UPPSC 2015)
## 2. Weber's least-cost theory
- Alfred Weber (1909): Plant locates where total transport cost is lowest, then adjusts for labour + agglomeration
- MI > 1: Weight-losing (e.g. iron & steel, sugar) → near raw material
- MI < 1: Or ubiquitous inputs (water) → near market
- Pure raw material: No weight loss (e.g. cotton) → free to locate anywhere between source and market

### Regional imbalance — policies and criticism
## 1. Policy measures
| Policy | Year | Provision |
| IPR 1956 (1956 Industrial Resolution) | 1956 | Sought/recognised balanced regional growth; public sector placed in backward regions |
| Fourth Plan (1969-74) | 1969-74 | Called for measures to attract industry to backward areas |
- Subsidies: Centre gave subsidies to units in backward areas
- PSU siting (policy override in India): Steel plants placed away from raw-material bases - Salem (TN), Vizag/Visakhapatnam (AP), Vijayanagar (Hospet, Karnataka)
- Correctives: SEZs (e.g. Vallarpadam, Nanguneri), NIMZs and corridors
## 2. Imbalance and criticism
| Factor | Effect |
| Regional pattern | South and West (TN, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Karnataka) are powerhouses; East, North-East and parts of the North lag |
| PSUs in regions lacking location factors | Struggled; skilled-labour gaps; costly subsidies; political concentration (Criticism) |
| Most Indian clusters too small + regulatory rigidities | Limited integration into global value chains (ES 2025-26) |

### SEZs, NIMZs and industrial parks — Acts and schemes
## 1. SEZs
- SEZ Act 2005: In force 10 Feb 2006; single-window clearance

- Sugar — raw-material-based (cane weight-losing, perishable)

- Bakery — market-based; market-based products are weight-gaining or fragile (bakeries, soft drinks)

## Footloose and Weber
- ★ Software — footloose (can be located anywhere, not tied to resources or transport)

- Footloose examples — diamond polishing, electronics assembly, software
- Material Index above 1 (weight-losing: iron and steel, sugar) — pulls to raw material, not market
- Pure raw material (cotton, no weight loss) — locate anywhere between source and market
