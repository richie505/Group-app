### Data — land degradation, restoration and cost
## 1. Global land and rivers
- About one-third of global land transformed by humans
- 60%+ of rivers dammed or diverted
- About 40% of Earth's land degraded
## 2. ISRO-SAC Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas (2021)
- 97.85 Mha = 29.77% of India's area degraded (2018-19)
- Processes: Water erosion (10.98% of area) and vegetation degradation (8.91%) lead
## 3. Restoration
- 21.76 Mha restored in 2011-2020 (461.14 Mt carbon) → beat the original Bonn pledge
- Telangana first (4.18 Mha); AP among the top five
## 4. Cost of environmental degradation to India (World Bank)
- World Bank (2013): India's annual cost of environmental degradation ≈ 5.7% of GDP (Rs 3.75 lakh crore, 2009)
- Mechanism: Higher healthcare spending + lower labour productivity
### Debates — growth vs environment trade-off
| View | Argument |
| Trade-off view | Environmental rules slow growth; "grow first, clean up later" (EKC logic) |
| Necessity view | Degradation raises health costs + lowers labour efficiency (World Bank estimates) |

### Functions of the environment
- ★ Environment supplies both renewable and non-renewable resources — "renewable only" is incorrect
- ★ Recycling — not a function of the environment
- ★ "It disorganizes waste that benefits human life" — not a function of the environment
### Key terms
- Absorptive capacity — power to take in degradation and neutralise pollutants harmlessly
- Resource depletion — use faster than regeneration
- Trade-off view — "grow first, clean up later" (Environmental Kuznets Curve logic)
- Green GDP — conventional GDP adjusted for resource depletion and environmental degradation
- conventional GDP ignores depletion, so growth can hide loss of natural capital
- Uttarakhand — first State to adopt green GDP as Gross Environment Product (GEP)
- Green economy — builds green industries and green jobs; separates development from resource use and pollution
### Green Revolution damage
- HYV crops lowered the water table through their irrigation requirements
- monoculture and chemicals hurt soil fertility and biodiversity
- AP Common Zoning Regulations 2026 — development-restricted: forests, hills, water bodies, eco-sensitive areas, CRZ
### Causes
- India's low EPI score — urban particulate pollution and coal-based power
- India's two environmental threats — poverty-induced degradation and pollution from affluence and rapid industrial growth
- Human-driven causes: groundwater over-extraction, industrial emissions, urban expansion — wildlife conservation is not one
- A sustainably managed forest keeping biodiversity — not resource depletion

- India's environment accounts (UN SEEA) — compiled by MoSPI; ecosystem and natural-asset accounts since 2018
