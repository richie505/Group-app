### Acts and amendments → provisions
## 1. Energy Conservation Act 2001
- Designated consumers: Mandatory energy audits + norms for designated consumers
- EE norms: For appliances, equipment and buildings with connected load >100 kW or contract demand >120 kVA
- Enforcement: Penalties for non-compliance
## 2. Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act 2022
- Passage: Energy Conservation (Amendment) Bill passed in December 2022 (PYQ 2021; APPSC 2021 PYQ)
- Lok Sabha Aug 2022 → Rajya Sabha Dec 2022
| 2022 amendment | Provision |
| Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) | Legal basis to a carbon market - Section 14AA |
| Non-fossil obligation | Designated consumers must meet a minimum share of energy from non-fossil sources [GK] |
| Building codes | Extended to large residential buildings - Energy Conservation and Sustainable Building Code [GK] |
| Vehicles and vessels | Efficiency norms can be set [GK] |
- ⚠ EC Act = 2001; BEE operational 1 March 2002; amendment passed December 2022

### Schemes and programmes → year and feature
## 1. PAT scheme - mechanism
- Nature: Market mechanism under NMEEE (NAPCC)
- Targets: energy-reduction targets for Designated Consumers
- Trading:
- Over-achievers earn ESCerts; under-achievers (laggards) buy them
- ESCerts traded on power exchanges (IEX)
- Cycle I (2012-15): 478 units in 8 sectors; now rolling cycles
- Original sectors: thermal power, iron & steel, cement, aluminium, fertilisers, chlor-alkali, pulp & paper, textiles
- Later added: railways, DISCOMs, refineries (PAT-II), petrochemicals and commercial buildings (later cycles)
- Shift after CCTS: as CCTS covers 9 heavy industries, PAT will keep only 4 sectors - thermal power, railways, DISCOMs, commercial buildings (LENS, Jun 2026)
## 2. Other schemes
| Scheme | Year / body | Feature |
| UJALA (Unnat Jyoti by Affordable LEDs for All) | January 2015; EESL | LED distribution |
| Street Lighting National Programme (SLNP) | January 2015; EESL | LED street lights replace sodium vapour |
| ADEETIE (Jul 2025, ₹1,000 crore) | Jul 2025 | MSME efficiency - audits + interest subvention: 5% micro/small, 3% medium; 60 clusters, 14 sectors |
| CAFE norms | First notified 2017, enforced by BEE | Average fuel-efficiency targets for M1 cars |
| National Efficient Cooking Programme | EESL | 20 lakh induction stoves, 25-30% cost advantage over traditional cooking, with MECS (APP) |
| SAKSHAM (Sanrakshan Kshamata Mahotsav) 2026 | Launched by PCRA at Chandigarh (Jun 2026) | Fuel conservation and awareness |

| CCTS feature | Fact |
| Notified | 2023 - India's first mandatory compliance carbon market (LENSD, 9 Sep 2026) |

### Lighting and everyday conservation — lamp types and practices
## Fluorescent lamps
- ★ Mercury vapour gives ultraviolet radiation when switched on; UV strikes phosphor coating which gives visible light
- ★ Fluorescent tubes filled with low-pressure mercury vapour and argon (sometimes xenon, neon, krypton)
- ★ CFL = Compact Fluorescent Lamp; about 75% less energy than traditional bulbs
## Street lights
- ★ Yellow street lamps use sodium; mercury vapour lamps give white light
- ★ Sodium vapour light is monochromatic yellow and will not split through water droplets
## LED
- ★ LED longer life than CFL because it is more energy efficient (uses less); gallium arsenide main emitter
- LED — highest efficacy, longest life, no mercury
