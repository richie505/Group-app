### Soil pollution — definition, causes and heavy metals → source
## 1. Definition and causes
- Definition: build-up of toxic chemicals or radioactive material in soil
| Cause type | Examples |
| Natural causes | floods, acid rain, mineral deposition from weathered rock |
| Human causes | Pesticides (DDT, aldrin, malathion), NPK fertilisers, farm runoff; heavy metals (Pb, Hg, Cd, Cu, Zn); industrial waste, urban waste, landfill leachate, e-waste, coal ash; oil spills, mining, acid rain |
## 2. Heavy metals → source
| Heavy metal | Source |
| Mercury | Industrial effluent (chlor-alkali), fungicides [GK partly]; Minamata disease |
| Cadmium | Mining effluent, phosphate fertilisers, batteries [GK partly]; itai-itai disease |
| Copper, zinc, lead | Smelting and industrial waste (APP) |
| Chromates | Tanneries and industrial waste (APP) |
- ⚠ Mercury → Minamata; Cadmium → itai-itai
## 3. Terms and effects
- DDT: persistent organic pollutant; resists breakdown, biomagnifies up food chains; listed under Stockholm Convention
- Effects: less vegetation, soil fauna imbalance, toxic crops, clogged drains, foul smell
### Soil rules, control and bioremediation technologies
## 1. Rules and funds → provision
| Rule | Provision |
| Fly ash | MoEFCC mandates use in bricks, cement and roads within 300 km of thermal plants (not dumped on soil) |
| Contaminated Sites Rules | (under EPA, 1986) identify + remediate chemically contaminated sites; tested against 189 hazardous chemicals; Polluter Pays; exempt: radioactive waste, mining, marine oil pollution, dumpsites (Jun 2026) |
| Environment (Protection) Fund Rules | money usable for soil-pollution control and site remediation; 25% kept by the Centre (Jan 2026) |
## 2. Control measures
- Measures: less chemical fertiliser/pesticide, biofertilisers and IPM, afforestation, treat waste before dumping/scientific disposal, biomining of legacy dumps, crop rotation, organic farming
- ⚠ Trap: watersheds are NOT a soil-pollution control (APPSC 2019)
## 3. Bioremediation types → agent
| Type | Agent |
| Bioaugmentation | Adding pollutant-degrading microbes [GK] |
| Biostimulation | Adding nutrients to boost native microbes [GK] |
- Oil Zapper: bacterial consortium from TERI; eats oily sludge and crude from oil spills
- Alternatives to conventional control: green technology, bioremediation, carbon capture and storage
- ⚠ Trap: Incineration is conventional → NOT an alternative (APPSC-G2 2025)
### Noise pollution — unit, thresholds and data
## 1. Unit and measurement
- Green muffler: Noise pollution control — noise measured in decibels; rustling leaves about 20 dB; a rocket engine about 180 dB
- Unit: decibels (dB) (APPSC 2025); dB(A) is weighted to human hearing
- Loudness: every 10 dB rise ≈ loudness doubles
## 2. Thresholds
| Level | Meaning |
| WHO: < 30 dB indoors | Below 30 dB safe |
| ≈ 40-45 dB | India acceptable level |
| >85 dB (Above 85 dB) | permanent hearing loss |
## 3. Common sounds
| Sound | Level |
| Rustling leaves | about 20 dB |
| Conversation | ~60 dB |
| Heavy traffic | ~90 dB |
| Rocket engine | about 180 dB |
## 4. Effects data
- Effects: hearing - about 27% of industrial workers suffer from deafness (Sep 2025)
- Long exposure: constricts blood vessels, digestive spasms, impaired night vision
### Noise Rules 2000 → zone limits; monitoring and control
## 1. Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000
- Legal base: Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 under EPA, 1986; CPCB sets limits by zone
| Zone | Day limit dB(A) | Night limit dB(A) |
| Industrial | 75 | 70 |
| Residential | 55 | 45 |
| Silence zone | 50 | 40 |
- Zone limits: Residential 55/45 (day/night); Day: 6 am-10 pm
- Silence zone: at least 100 m around hospitals, educational institutions (schools) and courts
- Firecrackers:
- CPCB bans crackers above 125 dB at 4 m from point of bursting
## 2. Monitoring
- Monitoring: NANMN = CPCB's Real Time National Ambient Noise Monitoring Network (NANMN)
- 70 real-time stations in 7 metros: Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Lucknow, Hyderabad
- Delhi: 65-85% of sites breach limits (Sep 2025)
## 3. Control
- Control: silencers/mufflers, sound barriers/noise barriers, green belts along highways, zoning near hospitals and schools, loudspeaker curbs/limits, traffic management, vehicle maintenance
- Green mufflers: rows of trees and shrubs along roads to absorb noise
- Exam angle: unit (decibel), the residential night limit, and which effects are linked to noise.
### Thermal and marine pollution — sources, effects and terms → meaning
## 1. Thermal pollution
- Sources: hot cooling water from thermal and nuclear plants (nuclear power plants) using water as coolant; industries discharging hot water
- Effects:
- Warmer water holds less dissolved oxygen → lower dissolved oxygen
- Sudden temperature changes → stress/death of aquatic life
- Control: cooling towers, cooling ponds, recirculating cooling water, regulated discharge temperatures (UPSC)
## 2. Oceans as sink
- Final sink: oceans = sink for radioactive waste, plastics, oil, detergents, sewage
- Biomagnification: DDT and mercury biomagnify in marine food chains
## 3. Marine terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Microplastics / microbeads | Plastic pieces < 5 mm; microbeads are added to cosmetics and scrubs [GK] |
| Ghost nets | Abandoned, lost or discarded fishing gear that keeps trapping marine life [GK] |
| Dead zones | Hypoxic waters where DO is too low for life, caused by nutrient runoff and eutrophication (e.g. Gulf of Mexico) [GK] |
| Blue Flag | Beach eco-label from the Foundation for Environmental Education (Denmark)[GK] |

### Radioactive pollution — sources, radon, effects and units
## 1. Sources
- Human-made sources:
- Weapons testing
- Improper disposal of medical/industrial/research waste
- X-ray machines → ionising radiation; cell towers → non-ionising radiation (UPSC)
- Natural sources: radon gas, cosmic rays, radioactive minerals in rocks
| Source type | Examples |
| Natural | Cosmic rays; radon-222, uranium-235, thorium-232, radium-224, potassium-40 |
| Building materials | Thorium (APPSC 2019 key); radon from granite [GK] |

## 2. Radon
- Radon: (Rn-222) radioactive noble gas from the Earth's crust

- Radon hazard:
- Seeps from uranium-bearing soil/rock + granite and other stone building materials

## 3. Effects
- Environment: soil infertile for decades; marine life destroyed
- Harm depends on: intensity, exposure time and half-life

### Nuclear and radiological emergency management — institutions, AERB, NDMA
## 1. Nuclear emergency management in India — institutions
- Department of Atomic Energy (DAE): The nodal agency for man-made radiological emergencies in the public domain — the nodal technical agency
- Crisis Management Group (CMG): Associated with the Department of Atomic Energy
- Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS): Handles nuclear, biological and chemical emergencies
- Atomic Energy Regulatory Board (AERB): An independent regulator for nuclear and radiation safety
- National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA): Issued the National Guidelines on Management of Nuclear and Radiological Emergencies (2009)
- BARC/DAE Emergency Response Centres: A network of specialised centres for detection and response
- National Disaster Response Force (NDRF): Specially trained CBRN battalions for response
- District Administration: Leads the off-site emergency — the District Collector is the Incident Commander

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 5:102
### Type → major source
## Thermal pollution
- ★ Nuclear energy harnessing often causes thermal pollution
- Thermal pollution = change in water temperature; main cause: discharge of hot water (coolant) from thermal and nuclear power plants and industries
- Warmer water holds less dissolved oxygen (oxygen-holding capacity falls as temperature rises)
## Other sources
- Ghost nets (marine pollution) — abandoned, lost or discarded fishing gear that keeps catching marine life
- Radioactivity: strontium-90 fallout from weapons testing is man-made; cosmic rays, radon, potassium-40 are natural
- Cell towers — non-ionising radiation; X-ray machines — ionising radiation

| Water | Sewage discharge |
| Air | Industrial emissions |
| Soil | Chemical fertilisers and pesticides |
| Noise | Construction |
## Constitution
- Article 51A(g) — fundamental duty of citizens to protect and improve the natural environment
- Article 48A — Directive Principle for the State on protection and improvement of the environment; separate provisions (48A does not explain 51A(g))
### Soil pollution
## Definition and pollutants
- Soil pollution — build-up of toxic chemicals or radioactive material in soil; causes toxic crops, fewer plants
| Pollutant | Source / association |
| Mercury | Chlor-alkali effluent and fungicides; Minamata disease |
| Cadmium | Phosphate fertilisers and batteries; itai-itai disease |
| Chromates | Tanneries |
- DDT — persistent organic pollutant under the Stockholm Convention
- DDT and mercury biomagnify in marine food chains; dissolved oxygen does not (not a pollutant)
## Rules and law
- Contaminated Sites Rules (under Environment (Protection) Act, 1986) — test sites against 189 hazardous chemicals
- Fly ash — must be used in bricks, cement and roads within 300 km of thermal plants; 50 km = limit for using treated STP wastewater
- IMO Ballast Water Management Convention (invasive species via ballast water) — in force 2017
- APPCB set up 1976 under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 (Air Act 1981, EP Act 1986, Noise Rules 2000 came later)
## Control
- Soil-pollution controls: crop rotation, biofertilisers, integrated pest management; watersheds are NOT a soil-pollution control (APPSC 2019 trap)
- Biostimulation — adds nutrients to boost native microbes; bioaugmentation — adds pollutant-degrading microbes
- Oil Zapper — bacterial consortium from TERI (bioremediation agent, not a chemical dispersant); eats oily sludge and crude oil from oil spills
- Alternatives to conventional pollution control: green technology, bioremediation, carbon capture and storage; incineration is itself conventional, not an alternative (APPSC-G2 2025 trap)
### Noise Rules 2000 (dB(A))
- Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 — made under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986
| Zone | Night limit |
| Residential | 45 dB(A) |
| Silence zone | 40 dB(A) |
| Industrial | 70 dB(A) |
- Silence zone — at least 100 metres around hospitals, educational institutions and courts
- Effects of long noise: narrowed blood vessels, digestive spasms, impaired night vision (it does not improve night vision)
### Noise facts
- Noise intensity measured in decibels (dB) (APPSC 2025); dB(A) weighted to human hearing
- Hertz = frequency; sievert = biological radiation dose; becquerel = radioactivity

- Rustling leaves ~20 dB; rocket engine ~180 dB
- CPCB bans firecrackers above 125 dB at 4 m from point of bursting
- NANMN (CPCB Real Time National Ambient Noise Monitoring Network) — 70 stations in seven metros: Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Lucknow, Hyderabad
- Green mufflers — rows of trees and shrubs along roads that absorb noise
