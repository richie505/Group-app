### Dadabhai Naoroji and Gokhale → contributions
## 1. Dadabhai Naoroji
- Title: "Grand Old Man of India"
- INC president 3 times: 1886 Calcutta, 1893 Lahore, 1906 Calcutta
- 1906 Calcutta - first demand for Swaraj as the goal (APPSC 2025)
- Founding role: A founding figure of the INC
- Exam angle: APPSC 2019 keyed "Naoroji founded the INC with Hume and Wacha" as wrong - Hume is the founder.
- First Indian MP: British House of Commons, 1892, Liberal Party, Central Finsbury
- Writings: Poverty and Un-British Rule in India → drain theory
## 2. G.K. Gokhale
- Gokhale (1866-1915):
- Gandhi's political guru; Gandhi returned to India (1915) on Gokhale's advice (Feb 2026)
- M.G. Ranade = Gokhale's own guru
- Banaras INC 1905 (Benaras 1905): President - condemned the partition + backed Swadeshi in Bengal
- Methods: Constitutional; pushed social and educational reform
- ⚠ Ranade → Gokhale → Gandhi = guru chain

### Moderate methods — beliefs, 3 Ps, limitations and official attitude
## 1. Beliefs and methods
- Belief: British rule "providential"; British just but ill-informed → faith in British justice and fair play
- Methods 3 Ps: PPP - Prayers, Petitions, Protests; resolutions, meetings, pamphlets; strictly constitutional agitation
- Two-pronged strategy: Build public opinion in India + persuade the British public and government
## 2. Limitations
- Session length: Met only 3 days a year (three days); no machinery to work between sessions
- Naive faith: Mild methods; faith in British justice exposed by the Partition of Bengal (1905)
## 3. Official attitude to the Moderates
| Official | Statement |
| Dufferin | Congress represents a "microscopic minority" |
| Curzon (1900) | "The Congress is tottering to its fall"; hoped to assist it to "a peaceful demise" |
### Moderate demands — councils, ICS, military expenditure, tax relief, civil rights
| Area | Demands |
| Constitutional | Larger councils (expansion of councils), elected members (elective principle), power over the budget; "No taxation without representation" |
| Administrative | Simultaneous ICS exams in India and England (London); higher ICS age; Indianisation of services; separation of judiciary from executive |
| Economic | Lower land revenue; abolition of salt tax; cuts in military spending on imperial wars (Afghan, Burma); tariff protection for industry; welfare spending (irrigation) |
| Civil rights | Freedom of speech, press and association; protests at (outrage at) Tilak's arrest and the deportation of the Natu brothers without trial |

### Ideas and theories → the economic critique of colonialism (drain of wealth)
## 1. Theories
| Idea | Associated with | Meaning — states, holds |
| Drain Theory | Dadabhai Naoroji; R.C. Dutt | India's wealth was being transferred to Britain — an economic critique of colonialism; colonial policies caused Indian economic exploitation |
| Doctrine of Lapse | Lord Dalhousie | Annexation of states where the ruler died without a natural heir |
| Divide and Rule | A British colonial policy | Used political and communal divisions to weaken nationalism |
| Safety Valve Theory | A.O. Hume — an early INC debate | Viewed the INC as a means of channelling political discontent |
| Two-Nation Theory | Muhammad Ali Jinnah; the Muslim League | Muslims and Hindus constituted separate nations |
| Hindutva | V.D. Savarkar | A cultural-national identity centred on Hindutva |
| Swaraj | Bal Gangadhar Tilak — a nationalist objective advocated by him | Self-rule — political self-government |
| Satyagraha | Mahatma Gandhi | Truth-force and non-violent resistance |
| Trusteeship | Mahatma Gandhi | Wealth holders should treat wealth as a social trust |
## 2. Drain of Wealth
- Naoroji, R.C. Dutt and Dinshaw Wacha showed Britain drained India's wealth via:
- Home Charges
- Salaries and pensions
- Unequal trade + British monopoly
- Naoroji: Britain was "bleeding India white"
- Called the Moderates' greatest contribution; later adopted by Extremists and Gandhi
