# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:106 - FRBM Act - targets, escape clause, N.K. Singh Committee; fiscal consolidation path
### FRBM Act, 2003
- FRBM does NOT provide penal provisions - the favourite trap
- Introduced by Yashwant Sinha; enacted August 2003; effective 5 July 2004
- Aim: fiscal discipline; cut FD, RD and public debt; inter-generational equity
- Original: FD 3% by 31 March 2008; RD eliminated by 31 March 2009
- Annual cut: FD 0.3%, RD 0.5% of GDP
- New guarantees capped at 0.5% of GDP a year
- Does NOT: penal provisions; Centre not answerable for States (APPSC-G2 2017)

### FRBM Act, 2003 — origin, FIRSTs, objectives and original targets
## 1. FRBM Act
- Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act (FRBM Act) — enacted in August 2003; effective on 5 July 2004
- Aim: Fiscal discipline and fiscal prudence
- Main focus: Reduce the fiscal deficit, the revenue deficit and public debt
- Objectives:
- Deficit reduction; debt management
- Discipline in public finances; transparency in fiscal operations
- Improving the management of public funds; strengthening fiscal prudence
- Improving macroeconomic management; moving towards a balanced budget
- Also (Prep): Inter-generational equity; long-run macroeconomic stability; elimination of the revenue deficit; institutionalise fiscal discipline
## 2. Origin and FIRSTs
| FIRST / origin | FACT |
| Introduced by | Yashwant Sinha |
| Enacted | 2003 for fiscal discipline (APPSC 2019) |
| Rules | FRBM Rules notified 2004, in force from 5 July 2004 |
| First Budget in India | 1860 (James Wilson) |
| First Budget of free India | 1947 (R.K. Shanmugham Chetty) |
## 3. Original targets
| Original target | Detail |
| Fiscal deficit | Fiscal deficit 3% of GDP by 31 March 2008 (original deadline) |
| Revenue deficit | Eliminate - Revenue deficit eliminated by 31 March 2009 (deadline pushed to March 2009) |
| Annual reduction (Rules) | Annual reduction: FD by 0.3% and RD by 0.5% of GDP each year |
- Tax-GDP ratio: Not a legal target; one of the four indicators projected in the Medium-Term Fiscal Policy Statement
### FRBM Act — mandated statements; what it prescribes and does not
## 1. Statements
| Statement (laid under Section 3 of the Act) | Content |
| Macro-Economic Framework Statement | GDP growth prospects, inflation, external balance (CAD, capital flows), savings-investment, monetary conditions, assumptions and risks |
| Medium-Term Fiscal Policy Statement | 3-year rolling targets (Three-year rolling targets) for 4 indicators: RD, FD, tax revenue (tax-GDP), total outstanding liabilities (as % of GDP) |
| Fiscal Policy Strategy Statement | Tax, expenditure, borrowing and debt strategy for the year; how current policy fits FRBM |
| Medium-Term Expenditure Framework Statement | 3-year expenditure projections / ceilings (added by the 2012 amendment); laid after the Budget |
- Mandate: All four are statements the Act mandates to be laid in Parliament (2026)
- Rolling targets: MTFPS targets not given since 2021-22 - three-year rolling targets suspended since 2021-22 for post-pandemic flexibility
- Budget 2026-27: Laid the MEFS and a combined MTFPS cum FPSS
## 2. Prescribes vs does not
- Prescribes:
- Deficit limits + statements laid with the Budget
- Transparency disclosures (borrowings, contingent liabilities)
- Cap on guarantees: additional guarantees against the Consolidated Fund capped at 0.5% of GDP a year
- Does NOT:
- Provide penal provisions for missing targets (APPSC-G2 2017)
- Make the Centre answerable for States' failures/targets (APPSC-G2 2017)
- Apply to States → States have their own Acts
- Exam angle: "FRBM does NOT provide penal provisions" - the favourite trap.
### Committees on FRBM → chair → recommendations — Kelkar Task Force and N.K. Singh Committee
## 1. FRBM Review Committee (N.K. Singh)
- The N.K. Singh Committee — chairman N.K. Singh; formed in May 2016; report in January 2017
- Announced in Budget 2016-17
- Key recommendation: Limit combined Centre and State debt to 60% of GDP — Centre 40%; States 20%
- 5 members: N.K. Singh (chair), with Sumit Bose, Urjit Patel, Rathin Roy among members
- Implementation: Recommendations reflected in Budget 2017-18; debt anchor + escape clause written into the Act by the 2018 amendment
- Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2017 - head of the FRBM review panel = N.K. Singh (Urjit Patel, Sumit Bose, Rathin Roy in options were members).
## 2. Kelkar Task Force, 2004
- Body: Task Force on Implementation of the FRBM Act, 2004, chaired by Vijay Kelkar
- Recommendation: Meet FRBM targets mainly through tax reform → widen the base + comprehensive GST
- Other role: Kelkar also chaired the 13th Finance Commission (2010-15)

| Year | Change |

### Escape clause and buoyancy clause — conditions and deviation limit
- Grounds (escape clause triggers):
- National security, war, national calamity (security threats, disasters)
- Structural reforms with unanticipated fiscal effects
- Severe economic slowdown / sharp fall in real output growth (3 percentage points below the average of the previous four quarters) (2018 amendment)
- Deviation limit: Not more than 0.5% of GDP in a year
- Accountability: Government must justify the deviation + give a path back to the target, placed before Parliament
- Buoyancy clause: If real output growth rises sharply → FD must fall by at least 0.25% of GDP
- ⚠ Escape clause = deviation up to 0.5%; buoyancy clause = FD cut of at least 0.25%

## 1. Past targets & deviation
- 2018 amendment targets: FD 3% by 31 March 2021; general government debt 60% + Central debt 40% of GDP by 31 March 2025
- Deviation acknowledged: From the Act's 3% FD and 40% Central debt targets (FRBM deviation statement)
- Halving commitment: Halve 2020-21 FD (9.2%) in five years → met: 4.4% in 2025-26 (not a legislative target)
## 2. New fiscal rule (from 2026-27)
- New anchor / debt rule: FD set each year to keep Central debt falling to 50 ± 1% of GDP by 31 March 2031 (55.6% in BE 2026-27); FD 4.3% (2026-27 BE)
- Shift: Debt, not FD, is now the primary target; 50% is above FRBM's 40%
## 3. Views & recommendations
- ES 2025-26: Argued for delaying strict FRBM-type targets to keep flexibility in a volatile geopolitical environment; warned States' finances are worsening (30 Jan 2026)
- 16th FC: Centre's FD to 3.5% of GDP by 2030-31; States 3% of GSDP
### State fiscal responsibility laws — AP FRBM Act, 2005
## 1. Background
- Trigger: States enacted Fiscal Responsibility Legislation mostly after the 12th FC tied debt relief (Debt Consolidation and Relief Facility) to it
- Model: Several States passed their own FRBM Acts on the central model
## 2. AP FRBM Act
- Name: Andhra Pradesh Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act, 2005 (AP FRBM Act, 2005; some notes say 2009)
- Targets: Eliminate the revenue deficit; fiscal deficit at 3% of GSDP
- Amendments: From time to time to match Finance Commission borrowing limits
- Effect: FRBM limits restrain AP's open-market borrowing
- AP BE 2026-27: FD 3.84%, RD 1.11% of GSDP → above (remains above) the 3% FRBM norm (15 Feb 2026)
- Slippage: FD 5.05% vs mandated 4% ceiling; borrowings ₹81,071 crore (7 Mar 2026)
