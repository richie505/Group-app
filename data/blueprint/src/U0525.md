### Monetary aggregates — M0 (high-powered money) to M4, NM0-NM3, L1-L3 — High-powered money (M0)
## 1. High-powered money (M0)
- Names: Reserve money, monetary base, base money, central bank money, high-powered money (M0)
- Role: Basis of credit creation; most important money indicator for RBI
- Uses (components) side: currency held by the public + cash reserves of banks (cash in hand + deposits with RBI) + other deposits with RBI
- Formula (PYQ): H = currency held by the public + cash reserves of commercial banks (with themselves and with RBI) + other deposits with RBI (APPSC-G2 2025 answer)
- Currency in circulation: Notes + rupee coins + small coins = currency with the public + cash in hand with banks
- 'Other' deposits with RBI: Quasi-government and financial institutions (incl. primary dealers), foreign central banks and governments, international agencies such as the IMF
### Monetary aggregates — M1 and M3, components and liquidity
## 1. M1 and M3
- M1 — narrow money: Currency with the public + demand deposits + other deposits with the RBI
- High liquidity — directly spendable transaction money
- M3 — broad money: M1 plus time deposits with banks — the most commonly used measure of broad aggregate money supply ("aggregate monetary resources"; main aggregate RBI tracks)
- M3 minus M1 equals time deposits with banks
## 2. Components
| Component | In M1 | In M3 | Nature |
| Currency with the public | Included | Included | Immediate cash in hand |
| Demand deposits with banks | Included | Included | Can be withdrawn instantly on demand |
| Other deposits with the RBI | Included | Included | Highly liquid balances |
| Time deposits with banks | ⚠ Not included — excluded | Included | Locked for a fixed period — the least liquid deposits |
## 3. Liquidity
- Liquidity order: Currency, demand deposits, savings deposits, time deposits
- Cash: The most liquid form of money; fixed or time deposits: the least liquid
### Monetary aggregates — M0 (high-powered money) to M4, NM0-NM3, L1-L3 — M1 to M4 (Second Working Group, 1977)
## 1. M1 to M4 (Second Working Group, 1977)
| Aggregate | Components | Type |
| M0 (Reserve money) | Currency in circulation + Bankers' deposits with RBI + 'Other' deposits with RBI | Monetary base |
| M1 | Currency with the public + demand deposits with banks + other deposits with RBI | Narrow money, most liquid |
| M2 | M1 + post office savings bank deposits | Narrow |
| M3 | M1 + time deposits with the banking system | Broad money ("aggregate monetary resources"; main aggregate RBI tracks) |
| M4 | M3 + all post office (savings bank) deposits (excluding NSCs) | Broad, least liquid |

- Liquidity order: M1 > M2 > M3 > M4 → M1 most liquid, M4 least liquid
- ⚠ M2 = M1 + PO savings; M3 = M1 (not M2) + time deposits
- Sources of broad money: Net bank credit to Government + bank credit to commercial sector + net foreign exchange assets of the banking sector + Government's currency liabilities to the public − net non-monetary liabilities of the banking sector

### Money multiplier and credit creation — withdrawal, formula
## 1. Withdrawal and the multiplier
- ⚠ A cash withdrawal does not change the aggregate money supply — withdrawing ₹1 lakh from demand deposits reduces demand deposits by ₹1 lakh and increases currency with the public by ₹1 lakh
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say a cash withdrawal raises cdr + reduces bank reserves → lower multiplier, money supply contracts
- Money multiplier: Total money supply divided by base money — expressed as M3 divided by M0 (broad money / reserve money, M3/M0)
- An increase in banking habits increases the money multiplier (stronger banking habit raises it - UPSC 2019)
## 2. Concept and formula
- Formula: m = (1 + cdr) / (cdr + rdr)
- cdr = currency-deposit ratio (preference for cash)
- rdr = reserve-deposit ratio (CRR, SLR-driven)
- Credit creation: Banks keep reserves (CRR, SLR) and lend the rest → each loan creates a new deposit
| Money creator | Creates |
| RBI | Reserve money (notes) |
| Government | Coins and the ₹1 note |
| Commercial banks | Deposit money by lending beyond reserves they must hold (CRR/SLR) |
### Policy effects on credit creation — SLR, CRR and other factors
## 1. Changing the SLR
| Basis | SLR increase | SLR decrease |
| Funds | Banks lock a higher percentage of funds in liquid assets — reduces investible loan funds | Banks hold fewer funds in mandatory liquid assets — frees up capital for loans; expands lending capacity |
| Credit creation | Decreases | Increases |
| Effect | Money supply contracts; controls inflationary pressures | Stimulates broader economic activity |
- Raising the SLR: A direct contractionary quantitative credit control tool — the central bank uses a higher SLR during inflation; it reins in excessive commercial bank credit growth
- An increase in the cash reserve ratio decreases credit creation
## 2. Policy effects
| Factor | Effect |
| Higher CRR, higher SLR or higher cdr | Lower multiplier |
| RBI buying G-secs from the public (OMO purchase); Government borrowing from RBI (monetisation) | Raise money supply - injects reserve money |
| RBI selling G-secs (UPSC 2012) | Reduce money supply |
| Weak banking habits | High cash preference limits India's credit multiplier |
- Example: 10% reserves → ₹100 of base can expand deposits towards ₹1,000
