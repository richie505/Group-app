# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:51 - Agricultural strategy and policy - National Agriculture Policy, doubling farmers' income, National Missions
### Phases & policy milestones
- Doubling Farmers' Income shifted the focus from production to income
- 1951-68 — Pre-GR: land reforms, irrigation, PL-480 imports
- 1968-81 — Early Green Revolution: HYV (wheat first) - New Agricultural Strategy
- 1970-76 — National Commission on Agriculture (Nathu Ram Mirdha); coined "social forestry"
- July 2000 — National Agriculture Policy: over 4% growth; contract farming; Rainbow Revolution
- 18 Nov 2004 — National Commission on Farmers (M.S. Swaminathan): MSP C2 + 50%
- 2006 — NRAA - rainfed areas, watershed approach
- 2016-18 — Ashok Dalwai Doubling Farmers' Income Committee: 14 volumes; target 2022, base 2015-16
### Doubling Farmers' Income
- Shift "production" → "income"
- 7 sources: crop & livestock productivity, resource-use efficiency, cropping intensity, high-value crops, better prices, non-farm jobs
- Tools: value addition, clusters, e-NAM/APMC reform, land-leasing laws
- Govt MSP = 1.5 x A2+FL (not C2); 22 mandated crops

### Agricultural strategy — phases, definitions and goals
## 1. Phases of agricultural development
| Phase | Period | Defining feature |
| Pre-Green Revolution | 1951-68 (foundations 1950-67) | Land reforms, irrigation, Community Development; PL-480 imports; droughts 1965-67 |
| Early Green Revolution | 1968-81 | HYV seeds (wheat first), fertiliser, irrigation - New Agricultural Strategy |
| Later Green Revolution | 1987-92 | Spread to rice and other regions |
- Foodgrains: 72.3 MT (1965-66) → record 357.73 MT (2024-25)
## 2. Sustainable agriculture
- Meaning: Using land so its quality remains intact, meeting present needs without compromising future generations (APPSC 2019 key)
- Ultimate goal: All three pillars (APPSC 2018 key)
- Environmental health + economic profitability + social and economic equity
## 3. Rainfed areas
- Rainfed farming: About 50-60% of net sown area (some notes say 50%) + ~40% of food production

### Liberalisation and WTO — changes and effects since 1991
## 1. Subsidy boxes (WTO AoA)
| Box | Nature | Examples | Status |
| Green Box | Minimal or no trade distortion — permitted | Decoupled income support; environmental programmes; regional development; agricultural research; farmer training; pest and disease control; also infrastructure, food security | Not trade-distorting; allowed |
| Amber Box | Distorts trade or production — subject to limits | Price support; production-linked subsidies; MSP, input subsidies (power, fertiliser, seed, irrigation) | Reduction commitment; de minimis 5% developed, 10% developing |
| Blue Box | Amber-type support with production-limiting conditions — combines Amber Box support with production limitation | Direct payments tied to limiting production | Trade-distorting |
- ⚠ MSP = Amber Box; de minimis 10% for developing countries
## 2. Liberalisation impacts
| Factor | Effect |
| Liberalisation after 1991 | Decline in government investment in agriculture (APPSC 2023 key, statement I correct) |
| Input costs | Fertiliser, seed, pesticide rose over 300% since early 2000s; real farm incomes stagnated |
| WTO entry 1995 | Import competition rose; subsidies as a share of farm income declined |
- Not in your sources: components of the "Agricultural Renewal Action Plan" (statement-type keyword).

### National Missions → launch, crops and features — NMSA components
## 1. NMSA components
- Rainfed Area Development (RAD) - ICAR Integrated Farming System models; aid ₹30,000 per family for IFS

- Per Drop More Crop - micro-irrigation subsidy up to 55%

## 2. Older and new missions

- New (2025-26):
- PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana - Central Sector, 100 low-performing agri-districts for 6 years
- Pulses mission - 100% MSP procurement of tur, urad, masoor for four years via NAFED/NCCF
