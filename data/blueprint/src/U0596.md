### Theories → proponents, year and mechanism
| Theory | Proponent / year | Mechanism |
| Continental drift | Alfred Wegener (1912) (APPSC 2018) | Driving forces: pole-fleeing force (from Earth's rotation) + tidal force (Sun and Moon) |
| Convection current theory | Arthur Holmes (1930s) | Radioactive decay heat in mantle → convection currents; rising limbs split crust (ridges); sinking limbs drag it down (trenches) → carry plates |
| Sea-floor spreading | Harry Hess (1961) (early 1960s) | Magma rises at mid-oceanic ridges → new crust spreads sideways → destroyed at trenches |
| Palaeomagnetic stripes | Vine and Matthews, 1963 | symmetrical bands of normal and reversed polarity on either side of ridge |
| Plate tectonics | 1967, McKenzie and Parker, independently Morgan | Lithosphere broken into rigid plates floating on semi-fluid asthenosphere; driving force: mantle convection (Arthur Holmes) |
- Wegener's weakness: forces judged too weak → theory not accepted
- Holmes's significance: gave drift the driving force Wegener lacked
- ⚠ 1912 Wegener → 1930s Holmes → 1961 Hess → 1967 plate tectonics

### Evidences — continental drift and sea-floor spreading
| Evidence | Example |

- Palaeomagnetism: rocks record the magnetic field of their time
- symmetrical bands of normal and reversed polarity on either side of ridge (Vine and Matthews, 1963)

### Plate boundaries → motion and examples
## 1. Plate boundaries
- Convergence: Mountains and subduction
- Divergence: New crust
- Subduction: Destruction and recycling of crust — old crust is destroyed by subduction
- New crust is added by seafloor spreading
## 2. Motion and examples
| Boundary | Motion | Landforms / examples |
| Divergent (constructive) | Plates move apart; new crust forms | Mid-Atlantic Ridge; rift valleys; Red Sea / East African Rift (Afar triple junction) - Nubian, Somalian, Arabian plates diverging |
| Convergent (destructive) | Collision or subduction | Himalayas (India-Eurasia) - continent-continent (Indian vs Eurasian); Andes - ocean-continent (Nazca under South American) [GK]; Mariana Trench - ocean-ocean subduction [GK]; trenches, island arcs and the Andes [GK] |
| Transform (conservative) | Plates slide past each other horizontally | San Andreas Fault; Sagaing Fault (Myanmar) between the Indian and Eurasian plates |

- Convergent: subduction friction (Pacific Ring of Fire)
### India in Gondwanaland and the Indian plate — drift, collision, new findings
- India's parent: India is part of Gondwana Land (APPSC 2018); Indian craton once part of Pangaea
- Peninsular plateau: old Gondwana shield; part of the African landmass until the Cretaceous
- Owen Fracture Zone now separates the Arabian and Indian plates
- Timeline:
- Gondwanaland broke up ~225 million years ago; Indian craton slid towards Eurasian craton
- Himalayas rose ~65 million years ago
- Direction & rate: northward; moves north at ~5 cm/year into Eurasian plate
- Palaeomagnetic evidence: magnetic latitudes in Indian rocks show India drifting from south of the equator northward (far south before the Cretaceous)
- Deccan Traps: erupted as India crossed near the equator over the Reunion hotspot
- Collision timing: ~4-5 crore (40-50 million) years ago; older notes say 50-55 million
- Process: Indian plate subducted under Eurasia, closed the Tethys; sediments folded and thrust up → Himalayas, still rising
- New findings:
- India underthrust Asia 45-20 million years ago, lifting Tibetan Plateau
- Pushed deeper under western Tibet → west more rugged
- Delamination: denser lower layer peeling off and sinking into the mantle at ~100 km beneath Tibet, seen with 94 seismic stations in southern Tibet (seismic imaging, helium isotopes)
- Ladakh Magmatic Arc: between Indus and Shyok suture zones; formed by northward subduction of Neo-Tethys plate
- ⚠ Owen Fracture = Arabian/Indian plates; Reunion hotspot = Deccan Traps
### Folding and fold mountains — terms and examples
- Cause: Compression bends layers into folds
- anticline = up-arch; syncline = down-trough
- Sequence with rising pressure: symmetrical → asymmetrical → overfold (overturned) → recumbent → nappe (recumbent fold broken by a thrust, pushed far forward)
- Siwaliks: simple anticline-syncline folds of the "Jura" type (Alps)

### Faults → force, movement and places
| Fault | Force | Movement / example |

### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- New findings on the Indian plate: underthrusting beneath the Eurasian plate, delamination beneath Tibet, Ladakh Magmatic Arc
- First direct in-situ observation of a complete sea-floor spreading episode: seafloor sank 4 m, spread over 1 m, 160 million cubic metres of lava in 16 days → spreading happens in bursts
- FIRST/LAST: Stanford's first global map of continental mantle earthquakes, 80+ km below the Moho
- Significance:
- Plate movement explains mountains, earthquakes, volcanoes, long-term climate change
- Plate collision explains the Himalayas, Indo-Gangetic foreland basin, India's earthquake risk

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:7
### Theories in order
## Continental drift
- Alfred Wegener — continental drift, 1912
- Pole-fleeing force tied to Earth's rotation; tidal force to Sun and Moon — both too weak, so theory rejected
- Tillites (glacial deposits) in India and southern continents → ancient glacial conditions, landmasses once joined
## Convection and sea-floor spreading
- Arthur Holmes — convection current theory (1930s); powered by radioactive decay in the mantle
- Rising currents split crust at ridges; sinking currents pull plates at trenches
- Harry Hess — sea-floor spreading (early 1960s): new crust at mid-ocean ridges, destroyed at trenches
- Normal and reversed magnetic bands lie symmetrically on both sides of a ridge
- A fast episode seen directly → spreading may come in episodic bursts, not at a steady rate
## Plate tectonics
- Put together in 1967 by McKenzie and Parker, and separately by Morgan
## Folds by rising pressure
- Symmetrical → asymmetrical → (overfold) recumbent → nappe
### Pangaea & parts
- Panthalassa: mega-ocean around Pangaea
- Tethys Sea: between northern Laurasia and southern Gondwanaland
- Gondwanaland: South America, Africa, India, Australia, Antarctica, Madagascar, Arabia
- Mesosaurus fossils: South Africa and Brazil (not India and Australia)
- Indo-Gangetic Plain bordered by the Himalayan Frontal Fault (north) and a normal fault (south)
### Evidences
- Ancient rock belts (~2,000 million years) on the Brazil coast match those of Western Africa
- Ghana's coastal placer gold has its source rock in the Brazil plateau → once joined
- Away from a mid-ocean ridge: age, density, thickness of oceanic crust and sediment cover increase
### Plate boundaries
- Convergent boundaries: earthquakes from subduction friction; mountain building and subduction
- Divergence adds new crust; sea-floor spreading creates (does not destroy) crust
- San Andreas Fault: transform boundary (not divergent)
- Ladakh Magmatic Arc: northward subduction of the Neo-Tethys plate; between Indus and Shyok suture zones
### Plates
- Plates (rigid lithosphere) float on the semi-fluid asthenosphere
- Seven major plates: Pacific, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian, Antarctic
- Nazca = minor plate, between South American and Pacific plates
- Plate movement tracked with GPS data
| Feature | Setting |
| Sagaing Fault (Myanmar) | transform boundary, Indian–Eurasian plates |
| Red Sea, East African Rift | divergence of Nubian, Somalian, Arabian plates near the Afar triple junction |
| Himalayas, Indo-Gangetic foreland basin | India–Eurasia collision (also India's earthquake risk) |
### Indian plate's journey
- Gondwanaland broke up about 225 million years ago
- Deccan Traps erupted when the Indian Plate passed near the equator over the Reunion hotspot
- India–Eurasia collision began about 4–5 crore (40–50 million) years ago; older guesses 50–55 million
- Indian Plate moves north at about 5 cm per year → Himalayas still rising
- Delamination (southern Tibet): denser lower layer of the plate peels off and sinks into the mantle at about 100 km
### Faults & folds
- Anticline: upward-arched fold; syncline: downward fold (horst and graben are fault blocks)
- Normal fault: hanging wall moves down; reverse: moves up; strike-slip: horizontal (shear)
- Thrust fault = low-angle reverse fault; Himalayan MCT, MBT, HFF are thrust faults
- Aravallis: old, worn-down fold mountains, among India's oldest
- Rockies: young fold (North America); Appalachians: old fold
- Satpura: block mountain, between rift valleys of Narmada and Tapi
### Exam corner
- Peninsular Plateau (old Gondwana shield) part of the African landmass until the Cretaceous
- Siwaliks: youngest Himalayan range
- Lake Malawi: southern end of the East African Rift; Lake Victoria between the two branches, not a rift lake
- Eastern Ghats: old, much eroded, discontinuous hills of the stable Gondwana shield (not young fold)
