### Political and administrative causes — factor → effect
| Political cause | Effect |
| Doctrine of Lapse | Took Satara, Jhansi and Nagpur; Rani Lakshmibai's adopted son refused the throne; Nana Saheb denied his pension |
| Awadh annexed (1856) | On grounds of "misgovernance"; Wajid Ali Shah deposed → lords, officials, retainers, soldiers lost livelihoods |
| The Mughal emperor | Bahadur Shah Zafar told successors would lose the Red Fort; Canning announced loss of royal title too |
| Administrative cause | Effect |
| Exclusion | Indians excluded from high civil and military posts / shut out of high administrative posts |
| Corruption | Rampant in lower courts and police; justice slow and costly, favouring the rich; administration inefficient and prejudiced |
| Unemployment | Annexations threw thousands of officials, retainers and soldiers of princely courts out of work |
### Economic causes — peasants, landed classes, artisans
| Class | Grievance → result |
| Peasants | Heavy land revenue / High land revenue collected rigidly under Permanent/Zamindari, Ryotwari and Mahalwari settlements → debt → land lost to moneylenders and traders |
| Peasants | Commercialisation: forced cash crops under high taxes |
| Taluqdars of Awadh | About 21,000 in Awadh dispossessed / lost estates under the Summary Settlement of 1856 |
| Estate owners | Inam Commission (1852, Bombay): confiscated some 20,000 estates whose owners could not show title |
| Artisans and handicrafts | Artisans were ruined by cheap British machine-made goods after the Industrial Revolution, esp. textiles |
- ⚠ Trap: Taluqdars were not allowed to keep their arms and forts (UPSC 2026 key)
- ⚠ 21,000 = taluqdars of Awadh (1856); 20,000 = estates confiscated by the Inam Commission (1852)
- Outcome: Taluqdars and zamindars lost their estates → fiercely anti-British landed class
- Artisans: British goods entered duty-free vs heavy tariffs on Indian goods → deindustrialisation
- Annexations ended princely/court patronage
- Absentee sovereignty: Company rule seen as absentee sovereignty + drain of wealth to Britain
### Socio-religious causes — social laws → year, missionaries
| Measure | Year | Why resented |
| Abolition of Sati (Bentinck) | 1829 | Interference in custom |
| Lex Loci Act / Religious (Caste) Disabilities Act / Caste Disabilities Removal Act | 1850 | Converts to Christianity could inherit ancestral property - seen as a state incentive to convert |
| Hindu Widow Remarriage Act | 1856 | Challenged orthodox norms |
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes date the Religious Disabilities Act to 1856; the Act is of 1850
- Christian missionaries: Preached under police protection; attacked Hindu and Islamic practice
- Modern fears: Western education, railways and telegraph seemed to threaten caste and religion → fears of mass conversion
- Other grievances:
- Racial arrogance: of officials; social discrimination against Indians
### Military grievances — GSEA, bhatta, discrimination, troop ratio
- "Peasants in uniform": Sepoys' grievances mirrored the village's; carried rural grievances into the army
| Grievance | Detail |
| General Service Enlistment Act 1856 (Canning) | New recruits to serve anywhere, including overseas → crossing the sea = loss of caste for orthodox Hindus |
| Bhatta | Foreign service allowance withdrawn for service in Sindh and Punjab, now British territory |
| Pay and promotion | Lower pay than British soldiers; racial contempt from officers; Indian could rise no higher than Subedar |
| Troop ratio | About 2.3 lakh sepoys to 45,000 Europeans (roughly 5:1); Europeans drawn off for the Crimean and Persian wars |
| Bengal Army composition | Awadh (annexed 1856) = "nursery of the Bengal Army" → annexation angered mostly high-caste sepoys from that region |
- APPSC-GS 2023: Subedar ceiling + greased cartridges both correct
- Not a cause:
- ⚠ Trap: Officers' inefficiency was NOT a cause (BPSC 2018 key)
- Missionary pressure, the order to travel by ship and the stopping of bhatta WERE causes

### Immediate cause — Enfield rifle, rumours and events of 1857 → date
- Enfield rifle: New (Pattern 1853 Enfield) rifle introduced in the Army in December 1856 (BPSC key)
- Greased-paper cartridges believed to contain cow and pig fat; sepoys had to bite them open → offended both Hindus and Muslims
- APPSC-GS 2019 key: immediate cause = introduction of the Enfield rifle
- Rumours: Bone dust mixed in Company flour; mysterious chapatis passed village to village across North India in early 1857
| Event | Date |
| Berhampur: 19th Native Infantry (19th NI) refused the new cartridges/rifle | February 1857 |
| Barrackpore: Mangal Pandey (34th Bengal NI) attacked Lt Baugh and Sergeant-Major Hewson | 29 March 1857 |
| Mangal Pandey hanged/executed; regiment disbanded | 8 April 1857 |
| Meerut: 85 sepoys of the 3rd Cavalry (3rd Native Cavalry) court-martialled, sentenced to 10 years | 9 May 1857 |
| Meerut rising: officers shot, comrades freed, "Delhi Chalo" | 10 May 1857 |
| Rebels reach Delhi; Bahadur Shah II proclaimed Shahenshah-e-Hindustan | 11-12 May 1857 |
- Barrackpore date: attack on 29 March; some notes date it to April 1857 (month of the execution)

## Background grievances
- Lower courts — slow, costly, corrupt; favoured the rich
- Satara (with Jhansi, Nagpur) — annexed under the Doctrine of Lapse; Awadh (1856) — for misgovernance

- After 1858 — Crown policy of religious neutrality

## Army
- General Service Enlistment Act, 1856 — Lord Canning; recruits to serve anywhere, including overseas (feared loss of caste)
- Bhatta — foreign service allowance; stopped for service in Sindh and Punjab
- Highest rank for an Indian sepoy — Subedar
- Sepoys to European soldiers — about 5:1 (2.3 lakh to 45,000)
- "Peasants in uniform" — sepoys carried village grievances into the army
- Chapatis passed village to village across North India
