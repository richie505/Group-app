### Cryogenic technology — propellants, Indian programme, Russian deal
## 1. Propellants and properties
- Propellants: Fuel liquid hydrogen (-253°C) + oxidiser liquid oxygen (-183°C) (UPPSC 2004)
- Exhaust: Only water vapour
- Specific impulse: ~450 s (highest)
- LH2 density: Only 0.071 g/ml → huge tanks; hard to store long → unsuited to military missiles
- Uses: Rocket upper stages/space shuttle (UPSC 1995, UPPSC 1993)
- Discovery's tanks held LH2 + LOX (UPPSC 2006)
## 2. Indian programme → year
| Step | Year / fact |
| Cryogenic Upper Stage Project (CUSP) | Begun 1994, cost ₹300 crore |
| Full cryogenic stage test | 2006 (UPSC 2007 - statement 1 correct) |
| GSLV Mk II with indigenous cryo stage (CE-7.5) | Flew on 5 Jan 2014 |
## 3. Russian deal and MTCR
- Glavkosmos deal (1991): Agreed to sell engines + technology
- US sanctions (1992): MTCR-based → stopped technology transfer
- Outcome: Russia supplied 7 engines only

### Green and electric propulsion — Hall thrusters, LOX-methane, POEM
- Electric propulsion: ISRO completed a 1,000-hour life test of a 300 mN Stationary Plasma Thruster
- Type: Hall-type, xenon
- Specific impulse ~6x chemical systems
- Will replace chemical propulsion on future communication satellites
- LOX-methane: Cleaner + cheaper; key for Mars-return and In-Situ Resource Utilization (methane from Martian resources) (2026)
- POEM: = PSLV Orbital Experimental Module → spent PS4 stage reused as orbital platform for start-up/academic payloads
- Not in your sources: the list of TDF-funded start-up payloads flown on POEM.
### Scramjet and air-breathing propulsion — ramjet vs scramjet, ATV, HSTDV
| Engine | Compression | Combustion | Speed |
| Ramjet | Forward speed, no compressor | Subsonic airflow | Above Mach 2 |
| Scramjet | Forward speed | Supersonic airflow | Mach 5-6+ |
- Principle: Scramjets use atmospheric oxygen → cut oxidiser weight
- Challenge: "a candle in a hurricane"
- ISRO Advanced Technology Vehicle (ATV): Flight-tested scramjet engines in Aug 2016 from Sriharikota
- India = 4th country (after US, Russia, China) to demonstrate scramjet flight
- HSTDV (Hypersonic Technology Demonstrator Vehicle): DRDO's scramjet vehicle
- DRDO combustor: Actively cooled full-scale scramjet combustor ran 20 minutes on indigenous endothermic hydrocarbon fuel (May 2026)
- ⚠ ATV = ISRO (2016); HSTDV = DRDO
### Reusable launch systems and NGLV "Soorya"
## 1. RLV-TD "Pushpak"
- RLV-TD "Pushpak": ISRO's reusable winged vehicle
- Completed LEX-03 (autonomous landing experiment)
- Aim: Two-Stage-To-Orbit (TSTO) system
- Benefits: Launch cost cut up to 80% + less space debris + rapid turnaround
- Challenges:
- Reusable Thermal Protection System
- Autonomous precision landing
- High R&D cost
## 2. Foreign reusable rockets → company
| Reusable rocket | Company | Status |
| Falcon 9 | SpaceX (APPSC 2024 PYQ: "Falcon") | First stages recovered 520+ times (TH, Jan 2026) |
| Starship | SpaceX | Fully reusable; ~1,200 t liquid methane + LOX; needs 10+ in-orbit refuellings for NASA's moon landing (TH, Mar 2026) |
| New Glenn | Blue Origin | Booster recovered by vertical landing |
| Zhuque-3 | LandSpace (China) | Attempted recovery of orbital-class stages |
## 3. NGLV "Soorya"
- NGLV "Soorya": ISRO's planned reusable heavy-lift rocket
- Payload: 20,000 kg to LEO (~2.5x LVM3)
- Propulsion: semi-cryogenic
- Launch site: Third Launch Pad, Sriharikota
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: Skyroot's Vikram-1 reached orbit on Mission Aagaman (Jul 2026) → India's first private orbital rocket
- 3 solid + 1 liquid stage; 290 kg to 500 km SSO
- Significance: Reusability + advanced propulsion needed for commercial launch competition and cost-effective human spaceflight
- Comparative note: Starship's cryogenic propellant transfer in orbit flagged by NASA as a top risk to its moon-landing plan (Mar 2026)
