### Extremists (Assertive Nationalists) vs Moderates — nature and comparison
## 1. Nature
- Extremists: Also called Assertive Nationalists — the aggressive nationalist wing; influence increased after 1906
- Main objective: Swaraj — complete independence from English rule
- Methods: Self-sacrifice, mass movements, aggressive resistance — opposed Moderate methods; rejected Prayer, Petition and Protest
- Supported Swadeshi and Boycott — wanted to extend the movement (a wider and extended Swadeshi–Boycott movement); lacked faith in Moderate negotiations with the British
## 2. Moderates vs Extremists
| Basis | Moderates | Extremists |
| Period | 1885–1905 (1885-1905) | Assertive nationalism (1905-1916) |
| Goal — sought | Gradual self-government — Gradual reform — supported; self-government within the Empire | Swaraj or complete independence — More immediate Swaraj (complete freedom) |
| Method — used | Prayer, Petition and Protest (3 Ps: prayers, petitions, protests); Constitutional methods — petitions and persuasion (followed) | Self-sacrifice and resistance; Militant methods and agitation — mass mobilisation (3 Ss: self-reliance, self-sacrifice, strong will; mass agitation) |
| Strategy | Constitutional agitation | Mass movement |
| View of British | Initially had faith in British liberalism (faith in British justice) | Increasing distrust of British rule — Increasingly distrusted British rule (British rule exploitative) |
| Mass politics / base | Limited mass politics; urban educated elite | Supported wider popular mobilisation; students, women, workers, peasants |
| Swadeshi–Boycott | Less willing to extend it | Wanted it wider and extended |
| Social reform | Pro-reform | Orthodox (Tilak opposed the Age of Consent Bill) |
| Representative / key leaders | Dadabhai Naoroji, Gokhale (Gopal Krishna Gokhale) | Tilak, Lajpat Rai, Bipin Chandra Pal, Aurobindo Ghosh (Bal Gangadhar Tilak) |
- ⚠ 3 Ps = Moderates; 3 Ss = Extremists
## 3. Classification

### Sessions — Calcutta 1906 and Surat Split 1907 → president, issues, outcome
## 1. Calcutta 1906 background
- 1906 event: The Calcutta Session — Dadabhai Naoroji — Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education
- 1906 Calcutta (Naoroji): Split averted by compromise
- Resolutions passed: Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott and National Education
## 2. Surat Split 1907
- A conflict between the Moderates and the Extremists — major issues: Leadership, methods, Congress strategy and Swadeshi–Boycott
- After the Surat Split (1907): Naram Dal — the Moderates; Garam Dal — the Extremists; the 1907 Surat Session event — the Congress Split involved Moderates and Extremists
- 1907 Surat: President Rash Behari Ghosh (Moderates' choice)
- Demands:
- Extremists: Lajpat Rai or Tilak as president; boycott extended to all of India
- Moderates: drop the 1906 resolutions; keep movement to Bengal
- Leaders: Extremists under Tilak vs Moderates under Gokhale (and Mehta) (UPSC)
- Cause: Extremists' lack of faith in Moderates' capacity to negotiate (UPSC 2016 key); dispute over the four resolutions
- Outcome: Moderates, with their majority, pushed Extremists out; scuffle → session suspended
- Aftermath: Repression (Newspapers Act 1908, Tilak jailed); Moderates dominated
- Reunion: Split healed at Lucknow in 1916
- ⚠ Session → president: Calcutta 1906 = Naoroji; Surat 1907 = Rash Behari Ghosh
