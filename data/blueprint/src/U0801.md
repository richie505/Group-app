## 2. GMOs and transgenic organisms
- GMO — genetically modified organism: Genetic makeup (genome) altered / changed through genetic engineering
- Transfers a gene from one organism to another — in ways that would not naturally occur; to produce desired traits or products
- Approximately synonymous with transgenic organism in the relevant context
- Transgenic organism: A foreign gene introduced — receives a gene from another organism through genetic engineering, for a desired characteristic or product; biotechnology produces transgenic organisms carrying introduced genes
### Recombinant DNA technology — concept, steps and FIRSTs
## 1. Recombinant DNA technology
- Recombinant DNA: Formed by combining genetic material — the technology transfers genes between organisms, even microbial genes to higher organisms
- Gene cloning: Amplifies or copies a desired gene
- Recombinant plasmid: A desired DNA fragment or gene inserted into a plasmid — introduced into a bacterium through transformation
- Bacteria multiply rapidly — producing large quantities of DNA or product

### Genetic engineering tools — restriction enzymes, ligase, RNAi, plasmids and vectors
## 1. RNAi and restriction enzymes
- RNAi — RNA interference: Based on gene silencing — targets gene expression; reduces or prevents the expression of the target gene
- Restriction enzyme (restriction endonuclease): Cleaves DNA into fragments — cuts at or near specific recognition sites (restriction sites); a role in genetic engineering by cutting DNA — popularly molecular scissors
## 2. DNA ligase
- Joins DNA fragments — forms or seals DNA linkages; joins desired DNA with vector DNA in genetic engineering
- Described as a molecular stitcher and molecular glue
## 3. Plasmids and vectors
- Plasmid: A small DNA molecule within a cell, separate from chromosomal DNA — replicates independently; commonly circular, double-stranded DNA
- Found in bacteria — also in some archaea and eukaryotes; natural plasmid genes may confer advantages such as antibiotic resistance
- Biotechnology: To clone, transfer and manipulate genes — an experimental plasmid is a vector
- Plasmid + gene = a recombinant plasmid; its entry into bacteria = transformation; bacteria act as factories to copy recombinant DNA
- pBR322: A classic vector — an early widely used Escherichia coli cloning vector; the letter p denotes plasmid; the letters BR refer to Bolivar and Rodriguez
- Other vectors: BACs, YACs; plasmid antibiotic-resistance genes are used for selection
- Plants: Agrobacterium tumefaciens ("nature's genetic engineer")
- Gene put in disarmed Ti plasmid; T-DNA carries it into the plant nucleus
### PCR, gene cloning and stem cells
## 1. PCR — Polymerase Chain Reaction
- Core purpose: Amplification of DNA — many copies of a selected DNA sequence
- Uses: Molecular and genetic analysis; detection and diagnostic testing; DNA-based research; analysis of small quantities of DNA
## 2. Stem cells and cloning
- Stem cells: Key property — the ability to differentiate into other cell types; can be isolated and grown in the laboratory
- Gene transfer: Embryonic stem cells — genetic engineering inserts a desired gene; the modified stem cells are returned to an organism or embryonic system
- Gene cloning: The copying / amplification of a desired gene or DNA fragment — plasmid cloning inserts a gene into a plasmid vector; bacteria in a bacterial cloning system rapidly copy recombinant DNA
- Whole-animal cloning: Produces a complete organism
- Cloning technology: A genetic and biotechnological technique
### Bt technology → gene source, mode of action, versions
- Gene source: Soil bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis
- During sporulation forms crystal proteins (delta-endotoxins) toxic to insects at low doses
- Mode of action: Inactive protoxin → activated in the alkaline gut of larva → binds midgut cells → creates pores → insect dies
| Version | Gene [GK] |
| Bollgard I (2002) | Cry1Ac |
| Bollgard II (2006) | Cry1Ac + Cry2Ab |
- Target trait: Insect resistance
- Soil persistence: Feared, but experiments show Bt proteins degrade in soil

### CRISPR-Cas9 — mechanism, discoverers, uses
## 1. CRISPR-Cas9 — genome editing
- CRISPR: Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats — specialized stretches of DNA; originated as a natural defence mechanism of bacteria and archaea
- Cas9: An enzyme that cuts DNA strands — nicknamed molecular scissors; guide RNA (the navigator) guides Cas9 to the desired genomic location
- Gene editing: Alters DNA sequences; modifies gene function; can remove existing or add new genes
- Uses: Correct genetic defects; treat or prevent disease; improve crops — ⚠ raises ethical concerns
## 2. Discovery and advantage
- Discoverers: Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier demonstrated it in 2012 → Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2020
- Advantage: Simpler, cheaper, more accurate than ZFNs and TALENs; can target many genes at once

### Genetic medicine — gene therapy, personalised medicine, terms
## 1. Gene therapy
- A biotechnology and genetic-engineering application — targets genetic abnormalities or diseases
- Basic idea: Alter, replace or correct defective genetic function — genetic engineering is used; CRISPR has the potential to correct genetic defects
## 2. Personalised (precision) medicine
- Adapts treatment using individual biological or genetic information — goal: more individually tailored prevention, diagnosis and treatment
- Tools: Genome sequencing — genomic information; DNA sequencing — sequence or variant information; genetic testing — identifies relevant variants; pharmacogenomics — links genetics to drug response
- Personalised medicine: Built on genetic make-up = CRISPR (UKPSC 2025)
## 3. Genetic medicine — terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Genetic medicines | Correct/compensate for faulty genes; carriers = engineered viruses and lipid nanoparticles (UPSC 2026: all statements correct) |
| Mitochondrial replacement therapy | "Three-parent baby" technique - ethical issue with germline editing and designer babies |
| Recombinant vaccines | Hepatitis B, HPV, COVID-19 |
| Synthetic biology | Insulin, artemisinin by microbes, semaglutide grown in baker's yeast |
- Regulation: CDSCO gene therapy rules 2023
- Risk: 6-year-old in China died (March 2025) after a first-of-its-kind brain base-editing therapy
### Environmental risks → effects
- Gene flow: Transgenes move by pollen (cross-pollination) into non-GM crops and wild relatives = primary environmental concern with GM crops (PYQ)

| Gene flow | Raised weediness; altered native gene pools |
| Gene flow | Can push endangered varieties of the same genus towards extinction |
| Non-target harm | Bt toxins or engineered traits may harm beneficial insects (pollinators, natural enemies), soil nematodes/microbes or aquatic organisms |
- Highest risk: Crop's centre of origin/diversity (many wild relatives)
- India = centre of diversity of brinjal → key argument against Bt brinjal
