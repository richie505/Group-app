### Terms → meaning: quotes, NEER/REER, devaluation vs depreciation — Exchange rate terms
## 1. Exchange rate terms
- Exchange rate: The price or value of one currency expressed in terms of another currency
- Flexible or floating exchange rate: The market determines the currency value
| Term | Meaning |
| Direct quote | Units of domestic currency per one unit of foreign currency (e.g. ₹95 = $1; e.g., Rs 96 = $1) [GK] |
| Indirect quote | Units of foreign currency per one unit of domestic currency [GK] |
| Spot / forward rate | Rate for immediate delivery (settlement within two days) / rate agreed now for delivery at a future date [GK] |
| Arbitrage | Buying currency cheap in one market and selling it dearer in another at the same time (PYQ key) |
| Hot currency | Temporary name for any hard currency in the forex market (hard = highest global faith; soft = easily available) |
| Import cover | Months of imports that reserves can pay for |
| Hedging | Covering exchange-rate risk (e.g., via forwards) |
- Quotes: ⚠ Trap: The PYQ's "false" statement swapped the direct and indirect quote definitions
- Exam angle: "which statement is false" - the swapped definition of a direct quote (foreign units per one domestic unit) is the false one.
- Market participants: Commercial banks, authorised dealers/brokers, monetary authorities
### Terms → meaning: quotes, NEER/REER, devaluation vs depreciation — NEER and REER
## 1. NEER and REER
- Nominal Effective Exchange Rate (NEER): A weighted average of bilateral nominal exchange rates — against a basket of trading-partner currencies (trade-weighted; major trading partners)
- Base 2015-16 = 100
- Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER): The NEER adjusted for relative price or inflation differentials — considers differences between domestic and foreign economies
- REER = NEER x (domestic prices / foreign prices)
- High REER = overvalued rupee, low = undervalued; the better indicator of competitiveness
- ⚠ An increase in NEER = rupee appreciation (imports cheaper, exports less competitive); an increase in REER does not automatically mean better competitiveness
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say an increase in NEER does not indicate appreciation (in the convention they cite)
- RBI indices: 40-currency and 6-currency trade-weighted indices
### Terms → meaning: quotes, NEER/REER, devaluation vs depreciation — Devaluation vs depreciation, appreciation vs revaluation
## 1. Four terms
| Term | System | Meaning | Done by |
| Devaluation | Fixed | An official reduction — an officially reduced currency value against a reference currency/basket; government raises the exchange rate (Rs per $), rupee cheaper | Government/monetary authority |
| Revaluation | Fixed | An officially increased currency value — government lowers the rate, rupee dearer | Government/monetary authority |
| Appreciation | Flexible | A market-driven rise in currency value — exchange rate falls, fewer rupees per dollar (market lowers Rs per $) | Market demand-supply |
| Depreciation | Flexible | A market-driven fall in currency value — exchange rate rises, more rupees per dollar (market raises Rs per $, e.g., Rs 80 → Rs 96) | Market demand-supply |
- A change from $1 = ₹60 to $1 = ₹58 represents rupee appreciation; a change from $1 = ₹60 to $1 = ₹65 represents rupee depreciation
- Reading a quote:
- ₹94 → ₹96 per $ = rupee depreciated
- ₹96 → ₹94 = rupee appreciated
- ⚠ Depreciation/appreciation = market (floating); devaluation/revaluation = government (fixed)
### Exchange rate regimes → types
- Exchange rate regime: System by which a country manages its currency; two broad types - fixed and floating
| Regime | Meaning |
| Fixed/pegged | Value fixed to a currency, basket or gold; government fixes the rate and RBI buys/sells to hold it |
| Floating / flexible | Market-determined (demand and supply) |
| Managed float | Hybrid - market-set with central-bank intervention; India's system; direct (buy/sell forex) or indirect (interest rates) action |
- Post-Bretton Woods classes:
- Fixed: currency unions, currency boards (e.g. Argentina 1991-Jan 2002), pegs
- Intermediate: bands, crawling pegs
- Flexible: managed and independent float
- Bretton Woods (1944): Fixed rates on the dollar

### Changes → effects of devaluation, determinants, impossible trinity — Effects of devaluation/depreciation
## 1. Effects of devaluation/depreciation
- Devaluation: Makes the domestic currency cheaper — domestic exports relatively cheaper abroad; imports relatively expensive
- May improve export competitiveness — ⚠ does not necessarily improve the trade balance
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the trade balance tends to improve (Assertion "devaluation improves the balance of trade" = true, APPSC-G2 2025)
- Expenditure-switching policy: Devaluation makes home goods cheaper for foreigners + imports costlier at home → spending switches to home-produced goods
- Recognised BoP correction methods: devaluation, exchange depreciation, exchange control, monetary/fiscal measures, import curbs
- Trade effect: Devaluation/depreciation makes exports cheaper (in foreign currency) + imports dearer → trade balance tends to improve
- Limits:
- Imported inflation
- Inelasticity trap - inelastic oil demand → falling rupee widens the CAD
- Costlier ECB repayment
- Net impact (ES 2025-26): Weaker currency helps the trade balance more than it hurts the financial channel (capital outflows) → net impact positive
- A falling rupee (vs a weak one) makes buyers delay orders
- Exam angle: A-R pair "Devaluation improves the balance of trade (A true) because it raises export prices (R false)".
- A-R key: Devaluation improves the balance of trade because it lowers export prices abroad and raises import prices
- ⚠ Trap: Reason "devaluation increases price of exports" is false (APPSC-G2 2025: A true, R false)

### IMF reserve assets → SDR nature, basket, year
## 1. SDR — nature & form
- Special Drawing Rights — an international reserve asset created by the IMF in 1969 (an IMF-created reserve asset)
- Under the IMF's First Amendment (some notes date it to 1967, when it was agreed)
- Interest-bearing; exchangeable for freely usable currencies
- Created to supplement member countries' official reserves — known as paper gold
- Defined in terms of gold (originally 35 SDR = 1 ounce of gold); redefined in terms of a basket of currencies after the collapse of the Bretton Woods System in 1973
- Form: Book-keeping/accounting entries (APPSC-G2 2025 key; PYQ 2025)
- ⚠ Trap: Where options are only paper currency/gold/silver, the key chose paper currency (PYQ 2025)
## 2. SDR basket & allocation
- Basket (since 1 Oct 2016): US dollar, euro, Chinese renminbi, Japanese yen, pound sterling
- Older notes list only four currencies, without the renminbi
- Largest general allocation: About $650 bn (SDR 456 bn) in August 2021
## 3. Reserve tranche
- Reserve (gold) tranche: Part of a member's IMF quota paid in reserve assets (historically gold); drawable any time without conditions
### Swap arrangements → SAARC framework and India-Japan BSA
- SAARC Currency Swap Framework:
- Instituted by RBI in 2012 for short-term BoP liquidity support to SAARC members

### Rupee internationalisation → framework, terms, symbol, bonds
## 1. INR trade settlement

- ★ Appreciation — rise in value of one currency relative to another
- ★ $1 = Rs 60 → Rs 58 — rupee appreciated, dollar depreciated
- ★ Rs 60 → Rs 65 by market forces — depreciation of rupee

### India's exchange rate history → year, devaluations, committees, Acts — Rupee devaluation episodes since independence
- ★ First devaluation — 1949

### Changes → effects of devaluation, determinants, impossible trinity — Effects of devaluation/depreciation
## Devaluation
- ★ Devaluation improves balance of trade; it makes exports cheaper, not dearer
- ★ Devaluation promotes exports — products' international price falls
- ★ After 1991, export growth rose due to devaluation (~18%, 1 and 3 July 1991)
## Exchange law and market
- ★ Flexible exchange rate — currency value set by demand and supply of foreign currency
- ★ Exchange banks (AD Category-I) — sanction foreign exchange for imports under FEMA
- ★ FERA replacement: APPSC key credits "Foreign Exchange Control Act"; the documented replacement is FEMA, 1999
- ★ Limit on forex for current-account heads lowered in 2000 (FEMA); FCA actually rose
