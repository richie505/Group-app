### Food management → objectives, missions and aims
## 1. Department of Food & PD
- Works via: procurement, storage and movement, public distribution, and buffer stocks
## 2. Objectives
| Arm | Objective / aim |
| PDS | Guaranteed minimum grain at affordable prices for low-income groups + equitable distribution + control of price swings |
| Procurement | Assure MSP to farmers + free grain for the weak under PMGKAY + market intervention to check prices |
- ⚠ Trap: Export of foodgrains is NOT an objective of food management (BPSC 2020)
## 3. FCI missions
- (1) efficient procurement at MSP
- (2) storage and distribution (storage, movement and distribution) of foodgrains
- (3) operational and buffer stocks for food security
- (4) Foodgrains accessible at reasonable prices to weaker sections through TPDS
- ⚠ Trap: Not an FCI mission = regulating markets in agricultural products → job of State APMC laws (APPSC-GS 2018)

### Procurement and storage systems → features
## 1. Procurement systems
| System | Feature |
| Centralised | FCI (directly or via state agencies) procures + moves grain to central pool |
| DCP (1997) | Designated States procure, store, distribute grain for TPDS |
| Open-ended | Wheat and paddy – everything at MSP meeting specifications bought |
| Target-based | PSS for pulses and oilseeds, capped at 25% of production |
- DCP — more:
- Centre pays difference between State's economic cost and CIP
- Benefits: local procurement, less transport, more MSP reach
- OMSS(D): FCI sells wheat and rice from the Central Pool in open market at pre-determined prices to moderate market prices
## 2. Storage
- CAP (Cover and Plinth): Open storage on raised plinths under polythene covers, for surplus grain
- Steel silos: Built under PPP to modernise bulk storage
- Grain Storage Plan in the cooperative sector:
- PACS get silos of 50/100/200 MT, procurement centres, FPS, processing units, custom hiring centres
- PACS picked by District Cooperative Development Committees; NCDC implements
- Not in your sources: total national storage capacity (FCI + CWC + State agencies).
### Price terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| MSP | Floor price announced before sowing (CACP → CCEA) |
| Procurement price | Price at which the government actually buys grain for PDS and buffer stock (earlier announced separately at harvest; later merged into one price) (APP) |
| Central Issue Price (CIP) | Price at which the Centre issues grain to States and beneficiaries for PDS; states fix the FPS retail price (UPSC notes) |
| Ceiling price | Maximum price that may be charged, to protect consumers |
| Economic cost | MSP + bonus + procurement incidentals + distribution cost (UPSC 2019) |
| Food subsidy | Economic cost minus CIP, reimbursed to FCI and to DCP States (IYB 2026) |
| Explicit subsidy | Shown in budget |
| Hidden subsidy | Cost not shown as subsidy, e.g., FCI's carrying cost financed by borrowing instead of budget releases |
- Table note: p.192 (procurement price, CIP, ceiling price)
- Economic cost of FCI: MSP + bonus (if any) + procurement incidentals + distribution costs
- Pooled grain cost (MSP + State bonuses) ≈ two-thirds of economic cost
- Retail price: States fix the retail price at FPS; State's role in PDS is operational
- ⚠ MSP = floor, before sowing; ceiling price = maximum, for consumers; CIP = Centre's issue price, not FPS retail

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:58
### FCI & food management
- Food management = procurement, storage, movement, public distribution, buffer stocks; export of foodgrains is NOT an objective
- FCI set up under the Food Corporations Act, 1964, on the advice of the Food Grains Prices Committee (L. K. Jha, 1964)
- Regulation of agricultural markets is NOT an FCI task; it comes under State APMC laws
- Indian Grain Storage Management and Research Institute — HQ Hapur (UP); field stations at Hyderabad and Ludhiana
- Stocks far above buffer norms → higher carrying costs
### Procurement systems
- FCI — main agency buying wheat and paddy under price support, with State agencies; does NOT regulate markets (State APMC laws)
- Wheat and paddy buying — open-ended if produce meets standards
- PSS buying of pulses and oilseeds — target-based, limited to 25% of production
- OMSS (Domestic) — FCI sells wheat and rice from the Central Pool in the open market at predetermined prices to calm market prices
- Advice: buying in surplus States (Punjab, Haryana, Andhra Pradesh) given to those States so FCI can focus on the eastern States
### Price terms
- Economic cost of FCI = MSP + bonus + procurement incidentals + distribution costs (not MSP and bonus alone)
- Ceiling price — maximum price that may be charged; protects consumers (a floor price for farmers before sowing is different)
- Central Issue Price (CIP) — price at which the Centre issues grain to States (and beneficiaries) for PDS
- Floor price announced before sowing = MSP; FPS retail price fixed by States; OMSS(D) sales at separate predetermined prices
- Food subsidy — two components: FCI subsidy (NFSA, other welfare schemes, strategic reserve) and subsidy to DCP States
- Hidden subsidy — cost not shown as subsidy, e.g. FCI's carrying cost financed by borrowing (NSSF loans) instead of budget money
- Amount shown in the budget (incl. budgeted reimbursement to DCP States) = explicit subsidy
### PDS evolution
## Stages
- PDS rationing began in the 1940s against the Bengal famine; restarted in the food shortages of the 1960s
- Sequence: PDS → RPDS → TPDS → AAY
| Scheme | Launch | Note |
| RPDS | June 1992 | backward, hilly and drought-prone areas |
| TPDS | June 1997 | separate BPL and APL entitlements |
| AAY | 25 December 2000 | poorest households, 35 kg per family per month |
- NFSA, 2013 — legal food entitlement to about 800 million people (not 400 million)
- Extended PMGKAY — free foodgrains to 80 crore+ NFSA beneficiaries from 1 January 2023, extended five years to 31 December 2028
## Procurement and storage
- Decentralised Procurement (DCP) — chosen States procure, store and distribute grain for TPDS themselves; Centre pays the gap between State's economic cost and CIP
- CAP storage — Cover and Plinth: grain on raised plinths under polythene covers
## Digital reforms
- SARTHAK-PDS — tracks grain from buying to Fair Price Shop with live stock checks (stops leakage)
- Approved by the CCEA for 2026-2031; includes SMART-PDS (April 2023 - March 2026)
- End-to-End Computerisation of TPDS ran October 2012 - March 2020
- ONORC — migrant takes full or part ration at an ePoS-enabled FPS after biometric/Aadhaar authentication; family takes the rest at the home location
### Food subsidy (₹ cr)
- PDS — sure minimum grain at low prices to low-income groups; fair distribution, control of price swings
- Buying aims — assure MSP, give grain to weaker sections under PMGKAY, check prices
- FPS retail price — fixed by State Governments (operational role in PDS)
- Market regulation is NOT an FCI function or challenge — State APMC laws
- Food subsidy, earlier partly off-budget via NSSF loans to FCI, put back in budget in Budget 2021-22
- Anti-leakage measures: e-PoS, ONORC, biometric authentication (ending procurement is not one)
- SMART-PDS — part of SARTHAK-PDS (which also covers intra-state movement and FPS dealer margins)
