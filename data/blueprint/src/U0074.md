### Badami Chalukya dynasty and rulers → period and achievement
- Also called the Chalukyas of Badami or Vatapi Chalukyas — c. 543 to 753 CE
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes give the dynasty span as c. 543-757 CE (Kirtivarman II overthrown c. 753-757 CE)
- Capital: Vatapi — present-day Badami in Bagalkot, Karnataka; Badami and Vatapi were associated with the Chalukyas
- Chalukya rule was mainly confined to the Deccan and Southern India — ⚠ not mainly in North India
| Ruler | Ruled — reign | Key facts |
| Pulakeshin I (Pulakesin I) | c. 543 to 566 CE | Founded — the real founder of — the Vatapi Chalukya dynasty; the Ashvamedha; independent kingdom, capital Badami |
| Kirtivarman I | c. 566 to 598 CE | Annexed the Kadambas, Gangas and Mauryas of Konkan; expansion; Bahusuvarna-Agnishtoma (Mahakuta pillar inscription) |

### Pulakesin I, Mangalesha and the end of the line
## 1. Pulakesin I - capital and Vedic sacrifices
- Capital: Vatapi (modern Badami, Bagalkot district, Karnataka)
- Setting: Sandstone hills around Agastya lake
- Fortified by: Pulakesin I - Badami cliff inscription of 543 CE
- Sacrifices:
- Pulakesin I: Ashvamedha
- Other Chalukya sacrifices: Vajapeya, Hiranyagarbha - to claim Kshatriya status
## 2. Succession war - Mangalesha's regency
- Regency: Kirtivarman I left minor sons → brother Mangalesha ruled as regent
- Outcome: Exiled Pulakesin II attacked and killed Mangalesha at Elapattu-Simbige (Bana territory, c. 609-10 CE)
## 3. LAST - end of the dynasty
- Last ruler: Kirtivarman II - overthrown by Dantidurga, founder of the Rashtrakutas (c. 753-757 CE)
### Pulakesin II → reign, titles, Harsha and Vatapi
- Defeated or stopped Harshavardhana on the Narmada — 630–634 CE; Pulakeshin II and Harsha were an important enemy pair
- Title: Dakshinapatheshwara — "Lord of the Deccan"
- Pallava king Narasimhavarman I captured and destroyed Vatapi in 642 CE — assumed the title Vatapikonda; Narasimhavarman I and Pulakeshin II were an important enemy pair
- Lohnara inscription: 630 CE — does not mention Harsha among the defeated enemies
- Reign: 609-642 CE; greatest Badami Chalukya (UPPSC 1991)
- Titles:
- Dakshinapatheshwara (vs Harsha's Uttarapatheshwara)
- Paramesvara (after defeating Harsha)
- Satyashraya, Prithvivallabha
### Pulakesin II → conquests, Vengi and foreign contacts
- Received a Persian embassy from Khusrau II around 626 CE
## 1. Eastern campaign
- Conquests: Kosala and Kalinga → marched on Vengi (held by Durjaya dynasty) → defeated Durjayas → then Mahendravarman I
- Vengi: Kubja Vishnuvardhana appointed in 617 CE → declared independence 624 CE = Eastern Chalukyas
## 2. Foreign contacts → source
- Persian embassy: Exchange of embassies with Khusrau II
- Ajanta Cave 1 painting once read as this embassy - identification now doubted
- Chinese accounts:
- Hiuen Tsang visited; praised him with Harsha and Narasimhavarman (Si-yu-ki)
- China-India relations under Chalukyas → Ma Twan-lin (UPPSC 2014 key: "Matwalin")
- ⚠ 617 CE = Kubja Vishnuvardhana appointed; 624 CE = independence
### Aihole inscription → author, language, date, poets, victories
- Aihole Inscription: A key source — composed by Ravikirti; Sanskrit and Southern Brahmi; dated 634 CE according to the Saka era; preserved at the Meguti Jain Temple
- Mentions Kalidasa and Bharavi — Ravikirti compared his fame with theirs
| Point | Fact |
| Author | Ravikirti, Jain court poet of Pulakesin II |
| Language / form | Sanskrit prasasti in verse [GK] |

- Victories listed:
- Harsha (Narmada); Kadambas (Banavasi); Gangas; Alupas; Mauryas of Konkan
- Latas, Malavas, Gurjaras, Kosala, Kalinga, Pishtapura (Pithapuram), Pallavas
- Kirtivarman I: Described as "the night of doom" to Nalas, Mauryas and Kadambas

### Universal angles
- Other records: A seventh-century inscription concerning Vikramaditya I — discovered at Madapura Lake in Davangere; concerns land-grant practices
- Current relevance: First epigraphic reference to Guntur (as Kumturu) = Pune copper plates of Eastern Chalukya Vishnuvardhana II, 669 CE (ASI Epigraphy)
- AP angle: Vengi conquest created the Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi (capital Pithapuram); Dandin called Vengi "Andhra Nagaram"
- Significance: Chalukyas bridged North and South; Aihole inscription = key dated prasasti
- FIRST/LAST: First South Indian gold coins = Pulakesin II; last ruler = Kirtivarman II

### Rulers
## Period and order
- Badami (Vatapi) Chalukyas: c. 543–753 CE (Pulakeshin I to Dantidurga's overthrow of Kirtivarman II)
- Order: Pulakesin I → Kirtivarman I → Mangalesha → Vikramaditya I
## Rulers
- Pulakesin I — walled Vatapi, 543 CE (Badami cliff inscription); performed the Ashvamedha; Badami as capital
- Kirtivarman I — took Kadambas, Gangas, Mauryas of Konkan
- 'night of doom' (Aihole) to the Nalas, Mauryas, Kadambas

- Mangalesha — earliest rock-cut caves at Badami
- Pulakesin II — title Dakshinapatheshwara
- Vikramaditya I — revived Chalukya power after the Pallava occupation of Vatapi
- Vikramaditya II — took Kanchi three times but spared it; queen Lokamahadevi built the Virupaksha temple, Pattadakal
- Kirtivarman II — last ruler; overthrown by Dantidurga (c. 753–757)
## Capital
- Capital Vatapi (Badami, Bagalkot district, Karnataka); Kanchi was the Pallava capital
- Pallava Narasimhavarman I destroyed Vatapi in 642 CE → title Vatapikonda
### Pulakesin II
- Killed Mangalesha at Elapattu-Simbige in Bana territory (c. 609–10 CE)
- Eastern campaign: Kosala & Kalinga → march to Vengi → defeat of the Durjayas → Kubja Vishnuvardhana appointed at Vengi
- Satara inscription (617 CE) — appointment of brother Kubja Vishnuvardhana as viceroy of Vengi
- Kubja Vishnuvardhana became independent 624 CE (Eastern Chalukyas), capital Pithapuram
- Embassies with Persian king Khusrau II (Ajanta Cave 1 painting of it now doubted)
- Stopped Harsha at the Narmada
- Aihole inscription (Ravikirti) — Sanskrit, Southern Brahmi script; key source on Pulakeshin II
### Pulakesin II: titles & firsts
- Paramesvara — taken after defeating Harsha (not Mahendravarman I)
- Other titles: Dakshinapatheshwara, Satyashraya, Prithvivallabha
- Lohnara inscription (630 CE) — does not list Harsha among his defeated enemies
- Aihole inscription (634 CE) — does record the victory over Harsha
### Aihole inscription
- ★ Mentions poet Kalidasa; author Ravikirti claimed fame like Kalidasa and Bharavi
- ★ In Sanskrit, southern Brahmi; reliable source for Pulakesin II
- On the Meguti Jain temple, Aihole (634–35 CE)
- Records defeat of Harsha on the Narmada; victories over Kosala, Kalinga, the Pallavas
### Andhra inscriptions
- Maruturu and Kopparam inscriptions — Pulakesin II defeated Pallava Mahendravarman I; won land between Krishna and Godavari
- Dandin called Vengi Andhra Nagaram
- Pune copper plates of Eastern Chalukya Vishnuvardhana II (669 CE) — first reference to Guntur as Kumturu
