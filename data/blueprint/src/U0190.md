### Reformers → ideas and contributions
## 1. Derozio and the Young Bengal Movement
- Leader: Henry Louis Vivian Derozio, teacher at Hindu College, Calcutta (late 1820s [GK])
- Inspired by the French Revolution
- Dismissed from Hindu College in 1831; died that year
- Ideas: radical, rationalist, iconoclastic
- Attacked old traditions, decadent customs, idol worship and casteism
- Strongly for women's rights and education
- Followers: "Derozians"
## 2. Bombay-Poona press reformers
- Bal Shastri Jambhekar: Attacked Brahmanical orthodoxy and tried to reform Hinduism in Bombay (APPSC-G2 2017); started the Marathi paper Darpan (1832) [GK]
- Gopal Hari Deshmukh: "Lokahitawadi" (APPSC-GS 2021, G2 2008); wrote the Shatapatre (hundred letters) in the weekly Prabhakar [GK]
## 3. Ranade and Bhandarkar
- Ranade's view: Rigid religious belief blocks social, economic and political progress; social + religious reform go together
- National Social Conference: Reason - INC kept to political issues; met at the same time and place as the Congress sessions, to discuss social reform
- Justice M.G. Ranade + R.G. Bhandarkar (some notes misspell him "R.C.") = leaders of the Prarthana Samaj
- Bhandarkar: Sanskrit scholar [GK]; joined in 1870

### Paramahansa Mandali and Prarthana Samaj → nature, aims, agenda
## 1. Paramahansa Mandali, 1849
- Nature: secret society; one God; members broke caste rules by eating food cooked by lower castes
- Aims: Against caste discrimination; for women's education + widow remarriage
## 2. Prarthana Samaj, 1867
- Aims (ROCKET): Opposed the caste system; supported widow remarriage; promoted women's education; to reform marriage customs
- Nature: offshoot of the Brahmo Samaj, reform within Hinduism
- Inspiration: Keshab Chandra Sen's visit
- Four-point social agenda:
- (1) Disapproval of the caste system
- (2) women's education
- (3) widow remarriage
- (4) Raising the age of marriage for men and women
- Additional aims: Inter-dining, inter-marriage, uplift of the depressed classes
- Method: persuasion and social work, not confrontation
- Idol worship: Unlike the Brahmos, did not outright forbid idol worship or abandon caste; focus social, not theological (p.84)
- ⚠ Trap: p.83 claims it rejected idol worship - treat p.84 as the exam-safe version

### Young Bengal (Derozians)
- Young Bengal — radical rationalism, attacked social orthodoxy; against idol worship, casteism; for women's rights
- Inspired by the French Revolution
- Derozio — dismissed from Hindu College in 1831; died the same year
### Mandali vs Prarthana Samaj
## Paramahansa Mandali
- Secret society believing in one God; broke caste rules; backed women's education and widow remarriage
- Founded by Dadoba Pandurang with Durgaram Mancharam (Manav Dharma Sabha, Surat)
- Not an open public organisation; the Arya Samaj was not secret
## Prarthana Samaj
- Bombay 1867, inspired by the visit of Keshab Chandra Sen
- Leaders — M.G. Ranade and R.G. Bhandarkar
- Worked by persuasion and social work; raising marriage age, helping depressed classes
- Unlike Brahmos, did not forbid idol worship or abandon caste practices
## Others
- Sharada Sadan (Pandita Ramabai) — home for widows
- Arya Samaj — rejected idol worship and ritualism
### Bombay-Poona reformers & journals
- ★ Lokahitvadi — Gopal Hari Deshmukh (1823–1892, Pune); articles in the weekly Prabhakar (Shatapatre, hundred letters)
