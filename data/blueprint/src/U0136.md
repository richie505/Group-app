# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:135 - Mughal economy - agriculture, karkhanas, trade, silver influx, Indian Ocean commerce
### Agriculture & revenue
- India = "sink of precious metals"; 17th century = the largest producer of industrial goods in the world
- About 15% of rural produce marketed (APPSC G2 2017)
- Jins-i-kamil (perfect crops): cotton, sugarcane, indigo, oilseeds, opium
- New World crops via Portuguese: tobacco, maize, chillies
- Zabt of Raja Todarmal: one-third of average produce of past ten years, paid in cash
- Land classes: Polaj (annual), Parauti (fallow 1 yr), Chachar (3-4 yrs), Banjar
- Patta = title deed; Qabuliyat = deed of agreement
- Shahab Nahr: restored Firuz Shah's Rajabwah canal

### Agriculture — crops, marketed share and irrigation
## 1. Marketed share
- Share marketed: About 15% of rural agricultural produce (APPSC G2 2017 key)
- Cash revenue demand pushed peasants to sell
- Exam angle: "approximately what % was marketed?" - 15%.
## 2. Crops → region
| Crop group | Crops |
| Commercial crops (jins-i-kamil, "perfect crops") | cotton, sugarcane, indigo, oilseeds, opium |
| Cash crops and staples | rice, wheat, sugar(cane), cotton, indigo, pepper and spices |
| New World crops | Via the Portuguese = tobacco, maize (also chillies) |
- Regional exports: Gujarat = indigo + food grains; Bengal = sugar + rice
- Tobacco, cocoa and rubber domesticated in the New World (UPSC 2019 PYQ)
## 3. Irrigation and condition
- Shahab Nahr: Built by restoring Firuz Shah's Rajabwah canal (UPPSC 2020 key)
- 18th century: Agriculture stagnant, technologically backward
- Peasants paid the state, zamindars, jagirdars and revenue-farmers
### Land revenue terms → meaning — Zabt / Bandobast of Raja Todarmal

- ⚠ Patta = title deed; Qabuliyat = deed of agreement
### Agrarian classes and trading communities → role — Agrarian and social classes
## 1. Agrarian and social classes
| Class | Features |
| Nobility (Umara) | High mansabdars; mix of Turanis, Iranis, Hindustanis and Rajputs; wealthy but dependent on the Emperor's favour |
| Zamindars | Hereditary rural superior-right holders; took a large share with jagirdars and revenue-farmers |
| Peasants (Raiyats) | Khud-kasht: cultivated land in their own village; Pahi-kasht: cultivated land in another village |
- 18th-century society: Extreme poverty alongside extreme wealth
## 2. Trader classes
| Class / community | Function / role |
| Banjaras | Carried bulk goods over long distances (grain, salt) on oxen, in bullock caravans - PYQ answer |
| Seths and Bohras | Specialised in long-distance trade |
| Baniks | Local (retail) traders |
| Mahajans | Moneylenders-cum-merchants; merchant-bankers [GK] |
| Sarrafs (shroffs) | Money-changers and bankers (see credit instruments) |
- Exam angle: "traders who carried bulk goods over long distances" = Banjaras (APPSC G2 2017).
- ⚠ Seths and Bohras = long-distance; Baniks = local
### Agrarian classes and trading communities → role — Trading communities → region
## 1. Trading communities → region

### Karkhanas — royal workshops and artisans
- Supervision: Mir Saman (Khan-i-Saman)
- Products for court: Costly gold and silver articles, fine textiles, carpets, arms, jewellery, luxury goods
- Origin: Tradition going back to Sultanate karkhanas
- Artisans: Often in guilds; worked in state karkhanas or private enterprises
### Credit and coin terms → meaning
## 1. Credit instruments
| Instrument | Meaning |
| Hundi | Letter of credit issued by Sarrafs/Shroffs to a cash depositor: cash deposited at one place, collected (encashed) later or at another place after a journey (APPSC-GS 2018 key) |
| Bima | Insurance of goods in transit, underwritten by sarrafs for a premium [GK] |
- Hundi: Allowed secure transactions over long distances
- Shroffs / sarrafs: Ran the credit network; money-changers and bankers - tested coins, took deposits (deposit keepers), lenders, issued hundis
- Exam angle: distractors are Farman (royal order), Parcha, Rokad (cash).
## 2. Coins — metal and name
| Coin / term | Metal / meaning |
| Mohur | Gold |
| Rupiya | Silver (standardised by Sher Shah, 178 grains; continued by Akbar) |
| Dam | Copper; 40 dams = 1 rupee |
| Chalni (chalani) | Coins of the current reign in circulation |
| Khazana | Coins of a previous reign, accepted at a discount (JPSC 2023 key) |
| Sikka | Right of coinage in the ruler's name - a mark of sovereignty with khutba |
- ⚠ Mohur – Rupiya – Dam = Gold – Silver – Copper
- ⚠ Chalni = current reign; Khazana = previous reign
- Golconda: Mughal silver coins circulated; Golconda minted only copper (Thevenot)
### Ports and trading centres → feature

### Silver influx and Indian Ocean commerce — causes and effects
| Factor | Effect |
| European companies paid in bullion (gold and silver) from Europe | Export surplus drew a large inflow of silver/bullion (Bernier) |
| New World (Spanish American) silver, via Europe and the Pacific | Reached India; Careri (c.1690) described (traced) silver's global route, crossing the globe to India |
| Silver inflow | More silver rupee coinage + rise in prices |
- India = "sink of precious metals"; 17th century = "the largest producer of industrial goods in the world"
- Merchant groups:
- Asian = Gujaratis, Bohras, Chettis, Malabar Muslims, Armenians
- European = Portuguese, Dutch, English EIC (1600), French
- Trade balance: Favoured India until mid-18th century

### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Monetised, credit-backed economy with a trade surplus - base from which the EIC built its power
- Cash revenue under zabt monetised the rural economy, linked peasants to markets
- Current relevance: India more than doubled import duties on gold and silver from 6% to 15% in May 2026; bullion imports stayed robust (17 Jul 2026) - India's pull for precious metals persists
- AP angle:
- Machilipatnam (Masulipatnam) = Qutb Shahi gateway and first European trade centre in Andhra (Dutch 1605)
- AP among top three producers of tobacco (New World crop), about 25% of India's output; Tobacco Board in Guntur (Mar 2026)
- Criticism & debates: Bernier's "no private property in land" view contested by modern historians
