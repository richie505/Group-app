## 1. Uranium isotopes and enrichment
- Natural uranium: U-238 99.28%, U-235 0.72%, U-234 0.0057%
- Enrichment: raising the share of U-235 (the fissile isotope) by isotope separation - not U-238
- ⚠ Trap: APPSC-G1 2022 - "Neither I nor II" (both statements false)
- Enrichment levels:
- LWRs = low-enriched uranium (3-5% U-235)
- Plutonium: not natural - bred from U-238
- ⚠ Trap: APPSC 2007 keyed "U-238" as the better fuel; U-238 is fertile - fissile Pu-239 is the fuel
## 2. Non-fuels and hydrogen isotopes
- Not nuclear fuels: moderators, coolants and absorbers - graphite, heavy water, sodium, boron, cadmium, zirconium
- Deuterium: 1 proton + 1 neutron (heavy hydrogen, stable)
- Tritium:
- 1 proton + 2 neutrons; made by bombarding lithium with neutrons
- Half-life 12.3 years; beta emitter; fusion fuel

### Terms → meaning — fission, fusion, bombs and radioactivity — Atomic vs hydrogen bomb
## 1. Atomic vs hydrogen bomb
- Atom vs hydrogen bomb: Atom bomb = uncontrolled fission; hydrogen bomb = fusion, triggered by a fission bomb
- Bombs used:
- Hiroshima (Little Boy) = U-235
- Nagasaki (Fat Man) = Pu-239
## 2. Radioactivity
- Radioactivity: A nuclear phenomenon — ⚠ not a chemical phenomenon; emits alpha particles, beta particles and gamma radiation — alpha, beta and gamma rays are all emitted in radioactivity
- Discovered by Henri Becquerel (1896)
| Emission | Nature | Charge | Mass | Penetration |
| Alpha particle | A helium nucleus — Helium-4 nucleus (2p + 2n) | +2 | About 4 atomic mass units | Stopped by paper/skin |
| Beta particle | An electron or positron — fast electrons/positrons | −1 (electron) / +1 (positron) | About the mass of an electron | Few mm of aluminium |
| Gamma ray | Electromagnetic radiation — electromagnetic waves [GK] | Zero | Zero | Highest; needs lead/concrete [GK] |
- Natural decay series:
- Uranium: U-238 → Pb-206
- Thorium: Th-232 → Pb-208
- Neptunium series = artificial
- ⚠ Hiroshima = U-235; Nagasaki = Pu-239
### Reactor components and reactor types
## 1. Reactor components
| Part | Function | Substances |
| Fuel rods | Pellets of fissile material | - |
| Moderator | Slows neutrons so U-235 can fission | Heavy water, light water, graphite [GK] |
| Coolant | Carries heat to make steam | Heavy water (PHWR), light water (LWR), liquid sodium (FBR) |
| Control rods | Absorb neutrons, control the rate | Boron, cadmium [GK] |
| Steam generator / turbine | Heat → steam → electricity | - |
- Control rods & moderator: without control rods the chain reaction runs away (meltdown risk); without a moderator a thermal reactor stops
## 2. Reactor types
| Reactor | Fuel | Moderator | Coolant |
| BWR | Enriched uranium | Light water | Light water (boils in core) [GK] |
| PWR / VVER | Low-enriched uranium | Light water | Pressurised light water |
| AHWR (Stage III) | Th-232 + U-233/Pu-239 | Heavy water | Boiling light water |
| SMR | Varies | Varies | Up to 300 MWe per unit |
- Power reactor types: Tarapur = BWR; Kudankulam = VVER
- LWRs: >85% of world capacity; light water as coolant/moderator; India now prioritises LWRs for fast capacity addition
### Homi Bhabha and the three-stage programme — PHWR → FBR → AHWR — Homi Bhabha
## 1. Homi Bhabha
- Title: "Father of Atomic Energy / Indian nuclear programme" (UKPSC 2007)
- Three-stage plan: conceived by Homi J. Bhabha, designed in the 1950s to get round scarce uranium using abundant thorium
- Aim: self-reliance through a closed fuel cycle
## 2. The three stages
| Stage | Reactor | Fuel | Output |
| I | PHWR (heavy water / D2O moderator + coolant) | Natural uranium | Power / electricity + Pu-239 from spent fuel |
| II | FBR (e.g. PFBR; liquid sodium, no moderator) | MOX: Pu-239 + U-238 | Power; breeds more Pu-239 than it burns; Th-232 blanket makes U-233 |
| III | AHWR / thorium reactors (thorium breeders) | Th-232 + U-233 | Power + U-233; large-scale thorium use |
### Homi Bhabha and the three-stage programme — PHWR → FBR → AHWR — Advanced Heavy Water Reactor (Stage III)
## 1. Advanced Heavy Water Reactor (Stage III)
- Advanced Heavy Water Reactor: 300 MW, thorium-based (Th-232 + U-233); conceptual prototype designed at BARC
- Role: India's flagship thorium reactor; core of Stage III
- Bridge: bridge from Stage I to Stage III - advances thorium use without passing through Stage II
- Design: heavy-water moderated, boiling light-water coolant
- Purpose: large-scale thorium use
- ⚠ Trap: "AHWR envisages large-scale use of plutonium-bearing spent fuel" is FALSE (APPSC-G2 2025)
## 2. Emerging shortcut - ANEEL fuel
- ANEEL fuel: thorium + HALEU (US firm Clean Core Thorium Energy)
- Finished high-burnup tests at Idaho National Laboratory
- Works in existing PHWRs; could hasten Stage III (May 2026)
- BARC's cautions on HALEU-thorium:
- Shutdown rods become ~26% less effective
- Stabilisation takes 7-10 years
- ⚠ Stage I PHWR = natural uranium; Stage II FBR = MOX; Stage III AHWR = Th-232 + U-233
### Places — nuclear minerals, thorium reserves and uranium mines
## 1. Thorium and reserves
- Reserves: ~2% of world uranium vs ~25% of thorium; thorium ~3,60,000 tonnes vs natural uranium ~70,000 t; the world's 3rd-largest thorium reserve (older 2025 notes say 21%)
- Thorium vs uranium (UPSC 2012):
- More abundant
- More energy per unit mass of mined mineral

### Fast breeder reactors — breeding principle, PFBR Kalpakkam, FBTR — Breeding principle
## 1. Breeding principle
- Breeding: core surrounded by a U-238 blanket; fast neutrons turn it into Pu-239 → more fuel than consumed (APPSC-G1 2022: statements I and II correct)
- Moderator & coolant: No moderator (fast neutrons); liquid sodium coolant
- Fuel: MOX (U-Pu oxide) = Pu-239 + U-238, from reprocessed Stage-I spent fuel
- Fuel use: FBR ~10%+ vs ~1% in PHWR; burn-up ~8,000 → ~1,00,000 (~100,000) units

### Fusion research — ITER, Indian tokamaks, ignition
## 1. ITER
- ITER: International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor, Cadarache, southern France
- Design: a tokamak fusion (not fission) device; world's largest magnetic confinement experiment (APPSC 2018)
- Members: EU, India, China, Japan, South Korea, Russia, USA
- Aim: demonstrate fusion as a large-scale, carbon-free power source; clean, abundant fuel
- Designed for 500 MW output from 50 MW input (about 10 times more power out than in)
## 2. Indian fusion research
- Institute for Plasma Research, Gandhinagar: tokamaks Aditya (first Indian tokamak) and SST-1 (Steady State Superconducting Tokamak)
## 3. Ignition and critique
- First fusion ignition / net energy gain (Dec 2022): NIF - National Ignition Facility, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, USA; achieved by laser
- Cost critique: experts say fusion cost models are too optimistic - "experience rate" ~2% (Apr 2026)
- ⚠ ITER = magnetic confinement (tokamak), France; NIF ignition = laser, USA
