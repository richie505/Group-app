### Origin & roots
- Bhakti from the root "bhaj" - to share or participate
- Roots: Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, Bhagavata Purana
- Gita Chapter 12 = Bhakti Yoga; Bhagavata Purana: ninefold (navadha) bhakti
- Began in the Tamil South: Alvars, Nayanars (6th-9th c.)
- Reached the north by the 15th c.; span 6th to 18th c.

### Bhakti → meaning, scriptural roots, origin and spread
## 1. Meaning and roots
- Etymology: bhakti from the root "bhaj" (to share or participate)
- Roots: Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, Bhagavata Purana
- Gita, Chapter 12 (Canto 12) = Bhakti Yoga: Both Saguna and Nirguna valid; Saguna upasana easier for ordinary people / embodied beings vs Nirguna harder (17 Aug 2026)
- Bhagavata Purana: First to put bhakti at the centre of religion
- Dating: some notes say 6th c. (6th century AD); most scholars c. 9th-10th c.
- Ninefold bhakti (navadha): shravana, kirtana, smarana, padasevana, archana, vandana, dasya, sakhya, atmanivedana
## 2. Origin and spread
- Began in the South (Tamil region first): Alvars and Nayanars (6th-9th c.)
- Alvars: Vishnu devotees, 12
- Nayanars: Shiva devotees, 63
- Spread: From the Tamil region → reached the northern belt by the 15th c.
- Overall span: 6th to 18th c.
- ⚠ Alvars = 12 (Vishnu); Nayanars = 63 (Shiva)
### Sacred texts → tradition and compiler
| Text | Tradition | Note |
| Nalayira Divya Prabandham | Alvars (Sri Vaishnavism) | 4,000 hymns, compiled by Nathamuni[GK]; the "Tamil Veda" [GK] |
| Tevaram | Nayanars | Hymns of Appar, Sambandar and Sundarar; the first seven books of the Tirumurai [GK] |
| Tiruvasagam | Nayanars | Manikkavachakar[GK] |
| Tirumurai | Shaiva canon (Nayanars) | 12 books compiled by Nambi Andar Nambi[GK]; called the "Fifth Veda" (JPSC 2021) |
| Periya Puranam | Shaiva hagiography | By Sekkizhar under Kulottunga II, on the lives of the 63 Nayanars; the 12th Tirumurai [GK] |
- ⚠ "Tamil Veda" = Nalayira Divya Prabandham; "Fifth Veda" = Tirumurai

### Nirguna, Saguna and Nathpanthi streams → idea and saints
## 1. The two streams
| Stream | Idea of God | Saints |
| Nirguna | Formless and attributeless; rejects idol worship (e.g. the Sikh Nirankar) | Kabir (APPSC GS 2024), Guru Nanak, Dadu Dayal [GK for Dadu], Ravidas (Raidas) |
| Saguna | God with form and attributes: avatars and personal deities | Vaishnavas: Ramamargis - Tulsidas (Rama); Krishnamargis - Surdas, Mirabai, Chaitanya, Vallabha (Krishna) |
- Common base: Both streams drew on the Upanishadic doctrine of Advaita; movement essentially monotheistic
- ⚠ Ramamargi = Tulsidas; Krishnamargi = Surdas, Mirabai, Chaitanya
## 2. Nathpanthis, Siddhas and Yogis
- Role: Made devotional religion popular in Northern India (BPSC 2023)
- Leading figures: Gorakhnath, Matsyendranath
- Practices: Hatha yoga, meditation, renunciation
- Criticised / rejected: Ritualism, caste system, orthodox religion
- Influence: Used vernacular; influenced Kabir's nirguna idiom
### Gurus, disciples and social base → challenges to caste
## 1. Early challenges to caste
- Buddha: Challenged caste distinctions and ritualism — birth does not determine spiritual worth
- Mahavira: Belonged to the Kshatriya class — Kshatriya-Kundagrama; Jamali was his first disciple — warrior-caste followers
- Guru Nanak: Rejected caste discrimination — emphasized virtues over caste
- Guru Ka Langar: Common dining without caste discrimination — promoted social equality
- Bhakti Movement: Emphasized devotion over birth and status — challenged caste hierarchy
- Namdev: The Maharashtra Bhakti movement — his Bhakti tradition showed Islamic influence
## 2. Gurus → disciples and social base
- Alvars and Nayanars: From all castes, high and low; challenged Brahmanical dominance
