# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:24 - Structure of the Indian government - separation of powers and checks and balances
### Core ideas
- Constitutional government = limited government: it defines, restrains and checks power
- Constitutional government = limited government, limited by the terms of the Constitution (UPSC 2020, 2021)
- Best safeguard of liberty in a liberal democracy: separation of powers (UPSC 2021)
- Montesquieu: "The Spirit of Laws" (1748); drew on the British constitution
- Constitutionalism: limits + rule of law + checks & balances + judicial review + FRs
- PIL: strengthens access to justice but not indispensable for Rule of Law (Arts. 14, 32, 226)
- Ambedkar: preferred responsibility over stability
### Degree in India
- No rigid separation; implicit, not absolute; overlapping functions
- Parliamentary = fusion: executive drawn from and accountable to legislature
- Nominal head (President) vs real head (Council of Ministers)
- Art. 50 (DPSP): separate judiciary from executive
- Judiciary insulated: Arts. 50, 121, 211, 235

### Separation of powers — constitutional government, thinkers and degree in India
## 1. Constitutional government and PIL
- Constitutional Government = Limited Government: Defines, restrains and checks power — provisions prevent executive tyranny
- = limited government → limited by the terms of the Constitution (UPSC 2020, 2021 keys)
- Public Interest Litigation: Promotes and strengthens access to justice for disadvantaged persons — but is not indispensable for the Rule of Law, which existed before PIL through Articles 14, 32 and 226
- Constitutionalism: powers within constitutional limits + rule of law + checks and balances + judicial review + protection of FRs
## 2. Separation of powers — thinkers and degree
- Best safeguard of liberty: in a liberal democracy = separation of powers (UPSC 2021 key)
| Person | Statement / contribution |
| Montesquieu | Division of powers = most effective way to protect liberty; "The Spirit of Laws" (1748); drew on the British constitution |
| Ambedkar | Preferred responsibility over stability |
| Executive | Drawn from and accountable to legislature |
| Heads | Nominal (President) and real (Council of Ministers) |
- Why parliamentary system: familiarity (1919/1935 Acts) + diversity + avoid American-style executive-legislature conflict
- Degree in India: no rigid separation; separation implicit, not absolute; overlapping functions → checks and balances
- Art. 50 (DPSP): State shall separate the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State
- Legislature-executive fusion: parliamentary system; ministers must be legislators
- Judiciary insulated: Arts. 50, 121, 211, 235 (control of subordinate courts)

### Judicial checks → Articles
- Judicial review: of laws and executive action (Arts. 13, 32, 226); laws declared ultra vires are void
- Bar on discussing judges:
- Art. 121: no discussion in Parliament on conduct of an SC/HC judge in discharge of duties, except on a motion for removal
- Art. 211: no discussion in a State Legislature on conduct of an SC/HC judge
- Reverse bar (Arts. 122, 212): courts cannot inquire into validity of proceedings of Parliament/State Legislature on grounds of irregular procedure
- Art. 142 "complete justice": fills gaps (Vishaka guidelines before the POSH Act, 2013)
- Risk: overreach; scope undefined
- ⚠ 121 / 211 = legislature cannot discuss judges; 122 / 212 = courts cannot question legislative procedure

- Ambedkar preferred responsibility over stability → parliamentary system
