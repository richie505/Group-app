### DPSP — basics, definitions, source and antecedents
## 1. Basics
- Location: Part IV — Articles 36 to 51 (16 articles)
- Inspired by (borrowed from): The Irish Constitution (Ireland)
- Precisely: Irish Constitution of 1937 (Directive Principles of Social Policy) - inspired by the Irish Constitution (UPSC)
- Ireland copied the idea from the Spanish Constitution
- "Ireland → DPSP"
- Non-justiciable (Article 37): Cannot be enforced by any court — yet fundamental in the governance of the country
- Aim: Social and economic democracy; a welfare state
- Called: Instrument of Instructions; Conscience of the Constitution
- Article 36: Defines the State; Article 37: Application of the Directive Principles
## 2. Welfare state - concept, aims, DPSP mandate
- Welfare state: "The State is everywhere: it leaves hardly a gap" (JPSC 2016 key)
## 3. Indian roots / antecedents

### Article 37 — non-justiciability and the FR–DPSP balance
## 1. Fundamental Rights vs Directive Principles
| Basis | Fundamental Rights | Directive Principles |
| Justiciability | Justiciable — enforced in courts | Non-justiciable — cannot be enforced in courts |
| Aim | Political democracy | Social and economic democracy |
| Sanction | Legal | Moral and political |
| In a conflict | Generally prevail — except Articles 39(b) and 39(c) in some cases | Subordinate, with exceptions |
| Nature of obligation | Mostly negative obligations on the State | Positive obligations |
## 2. Article 37 - non-justiciability
- Art. 37: Part IV "shall not be enforceable by any court" / "shall not be enforced by any court"
- But principles are "fundamental in the governance of the country"
- Duty of the State to apply them in making laws
- A-R (APPSC G2 2025):
- A: DPSPs not independently enforceable = true
- R: they act as a guidepost for legislation = true
- Official key: both true and R is the correct explanation of A
## 3. Courts and the FR-DPSP balance
- Basic Structure: balance between Part III and Part IV (Minerva Mills, 1980)
- Interpretive aids: courts use harmonious construction; many DPSPs read into Art. 21 (UPSC)
### Part IV — Articles 36-37 and socialistic principles (38-43A)
## 1. Articles 36 and 37
- Article 36: Defines "State" for DPSP — "State" has the same meaning as in Part III
- Article 37: DPSP not enforceable but fundamental in governance
## 2. Socialistic principles
- Article 38: Social order and welfare — promote the welfare of the people and minimise inequalities (made more comprehensive/strengthened by the 44th Amendment)
- Welfare through a social order of social, economic, political justice; minimise inequalities of income, status, facilities, opportunities
- Article 39 — principles of policy: Adequate means of livelihood; 39(d): equal pay for equal work; protection of children
- Also: material resources, wealth concentration, workers' and children's health, children's development (clauses on the next page)
- Article 39A: Equal justice and free legal aid — added by the 42nd Amendment
- Article 41: Right to work, right to education, public assistance (unemployment, old age, sickness, disablement)
- Article 42: Just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief
- Article 43: Living wage and a decent standard of life; also promote cottage industries (Gandhian)
- Article 43A: Workers' participation in management of industries (Participation of workers in management) — added by the 42nd Amendment
### Part IV — Gandhian and liberal-intellectual principles (40-51)
## 1. Gandhian principles
- Article 40: Organisation of village panchayats (Village Panchayats) as units of self-government
- Article 43B: Promotion of cooperative societies (co-operative societies) — added by the 97th Amendment
- Article 46: Educational and economic interests of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and other weaker sections
- Article 47: Raise the level of nutrition and the standard of living; improve public health; prohibit consumption of intoxicating drinks and drugs (prohibit intoxicating drinks; injurious drugs)
- Article 48: Prohibit slaughter of cows, calves and other milch animals — including cow protection (ban on slaughter of milch and draught cattle; ban cow slaughter)
## 2. Liberal-intellectual principles
- Article 44: Uniform Civil Code for citizens — still not fully implemented
- Article 45: Early childhood care and education — originally free and compulsory education up to 14 years; changed by the 86th Amendment to education for children below 6 years
- Article 48: Organisation of agriculture and animal husbandry — on scientific (modern) lines
- Article 48A: Protect and improve the environment; safeguard forests and wildlife — added by the 42nd Amendment
- Environmental protection: Article 48A as a Directive Principle; Article 51A(g) as a Fundamental Duty
- Article 49: Protection of monuments and places of national importance
- Article 50: Separation of the Judiciary from the Executive
- Article 51: Promotion of international peace and security; respect for international law
- Exam angle: match directive → Article; "which is NOT a DPSP" statements (APPSC GS 2024 tested Art. 39A).

- ★ DPSPs in Part IV include Equal Pay for Equal Work (39(d)) and UCC (44)
- Mother-tongue education at primary level — Article 350A, outside Part IV
- ★ Environment, forests and wildlife — both DPSP (48A) and Fundamental Duty (51A(g))
- ★ DPSP provisions — organising Village Panchayats, promoting cottage industries
- ★ Gandhian Articles include 40, 47, 48
- ★ National Social Assistance Programme (15 August 1995) — meets the DPSPs (right to work, education, public assistance)
### Directives outside Part IV; ideals shared with Fundamental Duties and Preamble
- ★ Separation of Judiciary from Executive — DPSP, Article 50 (Part IV)
- ★ Valuing and preserving rich heritage — Fundamental Duty, Article 51A(f) (Part IVA)
- ★ Article 24 — Fundamental Right (Part III)
### Acts, programmes and cases implementing DPSPs
- ★ NALSA — formed under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987
- ★ Free and competent legal services to weaker sections; organises Lok Adalats
- ★ First-ever national meet of DLSAs (30-31 July 2022) — New Delhi
- ★ Abolition of Untouchability — not a DPSP
- ★ Article 47 — prohibition of consumption of liquor, nutrition, public health (a DPSP)
- Child labour, forced labour and untouchability prohibitions are not DPSPs
