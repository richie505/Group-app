### NITI Aayog — full form, establishment, status and mandate
## 1. NITI Aayog
- NITI: National Institution for Transforming India
- Established on January 1, 2015 through a (Union) Cabinet Resolution — replaced the Planning Commission
- A public policy think tank — a policy think tank
- Chairperson: The Prime Minister; first Vice-Chairman: Arvind Panagariya
- Core approach / model: Cooperative federalism and a bottom-up approach — promotes greater involvement of States in policy-making
## 2. Establishment, status and mandate
- Establishment: Union Cabinet resolution of 1 January 2015; the Planning Commission had been dissolved in 2014
- ⚠ Trap: APPSC-GS 2025 key marking 2012 is wrong → correct year 2015
- Status: Non-constitutional and non-statutory (executive) body; India's apex public policy think tank (May 2026)
- Contrast: Finance Commission, CAG and NCBC are constitutional (APPSC-GS 2025)
- Mandate (four areas):
- Knowledge and innovation
- NITI for States (cooperative and competitive federalism)
- Driving transformational change
- Monitoring and evaluation
- Exam angle: APPSC-GS 2025 asked the year of establishment; the printed key ("2012") is an error in a garbled paper - the correct year is 2015.
### Objectives and guiding approach — Cabinet resolution
## 1. Objectives
- Shared vision: national development priorities with active involvement of States
- Cooperative federalism: structured support to States - strong States make a strong nation
- Village-level planning: mechanisms for credible plans at the village level, aggregated progressively at higher levels
- ⚠ Trap: this is an objective, but not the reason NITI is non-constitutional
- APPSC-G2 2025 Assertion-Reason key: Both A (non-constitutional advisory body) and R (village-level plans aggregated upwards) true, but R does not explain A
- Others:
- national security in economic policy
- partnerships with think tanks
- knowledge, innovation and entrepreneurial support
- monitoring and evaluation; capacity building
## 2. Guiding approach
- Approach: bottom-up; cooperative ("marble-cake") federalism; Antyodaya; inclusive + participatory development; focus on small enterprises

### Governing Council and Regional Councils
## 1. Governing Council
- Brings together the Prime Minister, Chief Ministers and Union Territory leadership — Chairperson: the Prime Minister
- Includes:
- The Chief Ministers of all States
- The Chief Ministers of Union Territories with legislatures — Delhi and Puducherry
- The Lieutenant Governors of other Union Territories
- A Vice-Chairperson; full-time members; ex-officio members; special invitees
- Constitution: first constituted Feb 2015; reconstituted Feb 2021

### NITI Aayog vs Planning Commission — comparison
## 1. Planning Commission
- Established on March 15, 1950 — created by a Cabinet Resolution
- Non-constitutional and non-statutory in nature; an advisory body — characterized as a quasi-political body
## 2. Comparison
| Aspect | Planning Commission (1950) | NITI Aayog (2015) |
| Set up | 15 March 1950, Cabinet resolution; first chairman Nehru | 1 January 2015, Cabinet resolution |
| Money / funds | Allocated plan funds and grants to ministries and States | No fund-allocation power (no power to allocate funds); advisory |
| Approach / planning | Top-down, one-size-fits-all Five-Year Plans | Bottom-up, cooperative federalism; Vision-Strategy-Action Agenda |
| States' role | Limited to the National Development Council (NDC) and annual plan meetings/talks | Every CM sits on the Governing Council; gives States a direct role |
| Members | Up to 8 full-time; no part-time members | Full-time + up to 2 part-time members |
| Top official / secretary | Deputy Chairman (Cabinet rank) and Member-Secretary through usual process | Vice-Chairperson and CEO appointed by the PM |
- Plan approval (old): Planning Commission plans went to the NDC for approval
- NDC members: PC members, CMs, UT administrators, Union Ministers
