### Public policy — meaning, Indian bases and Lowi types
## 1. Meaning
- Working definition: Systematic course of action adopted by the state to address public issues, achieve development goals and regulate behaviour → bridge between political intent and administrative action
- Public policy: Government actions, laws and rules to address specific problems
## 2. Indian bases of policy
- Judicial interpretation (e.g. Right to Food under Art. 21)
- NITI Aayog agendas, Budget, Economic Survey
## 3. Types of policy — Lowi classification
| Lowi type [GK] | Meaning | Indian example [GK] |
| Distributive | Benefits spread to specific groups; costs borne by all | Farm input subsidies, PM-KISAN |
| Redistributive | Transfers resources from one group to another | Land reforms, progressive income tax |
| Regulatory | Limits individual or firm behaviour | Licensing, environmental and food-safety standards |
| Constitutive | Creates or reorganises institutions and rules | Setting up NITI Aayog, delimitation |
- ⚠ Distributive = costs borne by all (PM-KISAN); Redistributive = one group to another (land reforms)
- ⚠ Constitutive = creates institutions (NITI Aayog, delimitation); Regulatory = limits behaviour (licensing)

### Policy cycle — stages from agenda setting to termination
- Cycle: agenda-setting → formulation → adoption → implementation → evaluation
| Stage | What happens |
| Agenda setting | Problem identified; media, advocacy and expert analysis push it onto the agenda |
| Formulation | Research, stakeholder consultation, cost-benefit analysis and impact assessment of options |
| Implementation | Planning, resource allocation, coordination; may need new regulations or programmes |
| Monitoring and evaluation | Data and metrics test whether goals are met; feedback leads to adjustment |
| Termination or revision | Ineffective policies ended or revised; policy learning feeds back to agenda setting |
- Cross-cutting: Communication + transparency run through every stage to build trust
- Example: Draft National Policy for Women 2016, based on Pam Rajput Committee
- ⚠ Cost-benefit analysis = formulation stage; policy learning = termination/revision stage
### Policy instruments → Indian illustration
| Instrument | Indian illustration |
| Regulation | Licensing, standards, mandates, e.g. Delhi EV policy: only e-three-wheelers and N1 trucks registered from 1 Jan 2027, e-two-wheelers from 1 Apr 2028 (TH, 30 Jun 2026) |
| Taxes and subsidies | Tax rates, subsidies, DBT-based subsidy reform, PLI incentives, public borrowing (CDI Fiscal Policy) |
| Direct provision | Government delivers the service itself (schools, PHCs) |
| Information | Proactive disclosure (RTI Act Sec. 4), public campaigns |
| Nudges | Behavioural design; concept from Thaler and Sunstein [GK] |
- Hood's "NATO" tools: Nodality, Authority, Treasure, Organisation
### Implementation — approaches, causes of failure and remedies
- Top-down: Policy set at the centre, carried out faithfully
- Pressman and Wildavsky, "Implementation" (1973): chains of clearances derail programmes
- Bottom-up: Front-line officials shape real outcomes
- "Street-level bureaucracy" → Michael Lipsky (1980)
- "Backward mapping" → Richard Elmore
- Causes of failure:
- Weak inter-departmental coordination + resource shortfalls + poor targeting
- Remedies: Phased roll-out with milestones + real-time monitoring + public outcome tracking
- AP's Action Taken Reports against seven sector white papers from 22 Jun 2026
