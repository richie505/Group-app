### Social groups and customs → terms
## 1. Social groups → occupation
| Group | Occupation / status |
| Arasar | Ruling class, governance and law and order |
| Anthanar | Brahmins - priests, political and religious influence |
| Vanigar | Traders / merchants |
| Vellalar | Rich peasants / agricultural landowners; the fourth caste in the Tolkappiyam's list |
| Uzhavar | Ordinary farmers (tillers) (CDI) |
| Kadaisiyar, Adimai | Landless labourers and slaves (lowest rung) |
| Panar | Wandering bards / minstrels who sang for chiefs [GK] |
| Parayar | Drummers; worked with animal skins [GK] |
- Tolkappiyam - four castes: arasar, anthanar, vanigar, vellalar
- Varna: Rigid varna system had not fully developed in early Sangam society → social mobility possible
- ⚠ Vellalar = landowners (fourth caste); Uzhavar = tillers; Kadaisiyar = labourers
- Exam angle: "Vellalars were rich peasants who formed the fourth caste" (APPSC-G1 2024); distractors were poets, labourers and forest tribes.
## 2. Customs → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Vattakirutal | A king defeated in battle commits ritual suicide by starving himself (UPSC 2023 key) |
| Kalavu | Pre-marital (secret) love [GK] |
| Karpu | Married life / wedded chastity [GK] |
| Viraliyar | Dancers who performed at courts; patronised by kings and nobles |
## 3. Women
- Women poets: Avvaiyar, Nachchellaiyar, Kakkaipadiniyar → contributed to Sangam poetry
### Religion — deities, hero stones, burial
- Chief deity: Seyon / Murugan
- Six abodes = Arupadai Veedu
- Other deities: Mayon (Vishnu), Vendan (Indra), Varunan, Korravai (goddess of war/victory)
- Hero stones (Nadukal): For warriors who died / fell in battle (UPSC notes); carved with deeds + inscriptions; found across Tamil Nadu
### Ports → kingdom, coast, classical name
| Port | Kingdom / coast — was | Classical name / note |
| Puhar — also known as Kaveripattinam | A Chola port and capital; east coast | Cotton, muslin, silk; Yavana (foreign) quarter prospered |
| Korkai | A Pandya port; east coast | Pearl-fishery port; exported pearls and shells |
| Muziris — also known as Mushiri (Musiri) | A Chera port; west (Malabar) coast | Pattanam, Ernakulam (KCHR excavations); pepper port; Periplus: "abounding with ships" |
| Tondi | A western coast port — a Sangam-age port (Chera) | Tyndis; spices, textiles |
| Naura | A western coast port — a Sangam-age port | - |
| Arikamedu | Roman trade; near Puducherry, east coast | Poduke; "market street of the Romans" (Periplus) |
| Motupalli | A Kakatiya seaport — exports, imports and customs; Marco Polo | - |
| Uraiyur | Chola inland capital[GK] | Excavated urban and craft centre (CDI); not a port |
- Other ports: Nelkynda, Bakare (Kerala), Marakkanam
- Overseas partners: Tamil ports traded with Berenike / Myos Hormos (Egyptian Red Sea coast, 2026 notes)
- ⚠ Uraiyur = inland capital, not a port
- Exam angle: Korkai, Poompuhar and Muchiri were ports (UPSC 2023).
### Indo-Roman trade — items, terms, coins and hoards
## 1. Exports and imports
| Exports | Imports |
| Pepper and spices (ginger, cardamom, cinnamon, turmeric), cotton and muslin, pearls, ivory, precious stones | Gold, horses, sweet wine; Roman coins |
## 2. Terms → meaning
- Yavanapriya: 'Beloved of the Yavanas' = black pepper → Rome's most prized Indian import
- Amphora: Tall, double-handled jar for wine and oil
- Found at Arikamedu, Kodumanal, Uraiyur, Alagankulam
## 3. Partners and embassies
- South Indian power trading with Rome: Cheras (BPSC 2022 key)
- Embassies: Indian embassies visited the Roman Empire in several reigns
- Best known: Pandya embassy to Augustus
## 4. Coins and hoards
- Roman coins: Gold and silver coins of Augustus, Tiberius and Nero found across Tamil Nadu
- Hoards: Akkanpalle, Kondapur and Poduke (Arikamedu)
### Excavators and writers → Indo-Roman evidence
| Person / source | Evidence |
| Sir Mortimer Wheeler | Excavated Arikamedu, 1945 |
| Periplus of the Erythraean Sea | c. 60 CE, anonymous Greek merchant → main source |
| Pliny (Natural History) | Rome lost 55 million sesterces a year to India |
- Arikamedu finds: Roman amphorae, Arretine ware, rouletted ware, beads, Tamil-Brahmi potsherds
- Foremost Indo-Roman trading station on the east coast (JPSC 2021 key)
- Tamil-Brahmi (Tamizhi) abroad: Potsherds as far west as Berenike, Quseir al-Qadim and the Valley of the Kings (Egypt)
### Kalabhras — period, religion, dark age debate, end
- Period: Ruled the Tamil country after the Sangam age, c. 3rd-6th century CE
- Religion: Patrons of Jainism and Buddhism rather than Vedic Brahmanism
- "Dark age" debate:
- Called the "dark age" of Tamil history → later Brahmanical records show them as evil rulers + scarce sources
- End: Late 6th century, by Pandya Kadungon and Pallava Simhavishnu
### Universal angles
- Current relevance (Jun 2026): KCHR's Pattanam excavations identify Muziris with Pattanam (Ernakulam); NextGen CA 2026 lists Indo-Roman ports and hoards
- Comparative note: Indo-Roman trade peaked in the Satavahana-Sangam age (1st c. BCE-2nd c. CE)
- Did not flourish after the Satavahanas (APHQ G2 2025)
