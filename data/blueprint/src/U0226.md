### Muslim proposals → Delhi Proposals (1927) and Fourteen Points (1929)
- Delhi Proposals (March 1927): Muslim leaders offered to drop separate electorates for joint electorates + reserved seats
- Conditions: one-third of central seats; separation of Sindh; reforms in NWFP and Baluchistan; representation by population in Punjab and Bengal
- All Parties Muslim Conference (Delhi, opened 31 Dec 1928): rejected Nehru Report
- Jinnah's Fourteen Points: March 1929: at Muslim League session in Delhi
- Federal constitution + residuary powers to provinces
- One-third Muslim seats at the centre + separate electorates
- Muslim-majority safeguards
- ⚠ Delhi Proposals = drop separate electorates (1927); Fourteen Points = keep separate electorates (1929)
### Nehru Report, 1928 → recommendations and reactions
## 1. Origin
- Trigger: Birkenhead's challenge (1927) that Indians could not draft an agreed constitution
- Report date: August 1928
- ⚠ Trap: "10 Aug 1929" is WRONG; 1928 is correct
## 2. Recommendations
- Status: dominion status
- Federal structure: bicameral centre + a Supreme Court
- Provinces: linguistic provinces (incl. Andhra)
- Electorates: joint electorates + reserved seats for minorities
- Rights: universal adult suffrage + fundamental rights
## 3. Reactions
- Muslim League: rejected it; Jinnah's amendments failed at Calcutta All Parties Convention (Dec 1928)
- Young radicals: Jawaharlal, Bose wanted complete independence
- Congress: one-year ultimatum → Purna Swaraj at Lahore (1929)

### 1927-1935 in order
## Nehru Report (1928)
- ★ Prepared by an All-Party Committee chaired by Motilal Nehru (secretary Jawaharlal Nehru); demanded Dominion Status; response to the Simon Commission
- ★ Recommended linguistic reorganisation of provinces
- Muslim League rejected it; Nehru and Bose demanded complete independence
- Congress gave Britain a one-year ultimatum; complete independence declared at Lahore, 1929
- All Parties Muslim Conference, Delhi (from 31 Dec 1928), under the Aga Khan — rejected it

- ★ Jinnah's Fourteen Points — March 1929, Muslim League session at Delhi; protest against the Nehru Report
- Butler Committee — Harcourt Butler (not Lord Irwin); princely states and the Paramount Power
- Deepavali Declaration (1929) — Lord Irwin; promised a Round Table Conference after the Simon report
- Prakasam — 'Andhra Kesari', bared his chest and dared the police to shoot during the Simon protest

### Nehru Report (1928)
- ★ Committee headed by Motilal Nehru; subject — constitutional arrangements in India
- Secretary — Jawaharlal Nehru
- Birkenhead's challenge (1927) — Indians could not agree on a constitutional scheme → All Parties Conference
- Published August 1928 (1929 is a trap)
- Recommended a Supreme Court; proposed ending dyarchy
- Simon report on electorates — continuation of separate communal electorates, with abolition of dyarchy and provincial autonomy
- ★ Second RTC (London, 7 Sep–1 Dec 1931) — Jawaharlal Nehru did not attend; Gandhi, Sarojini Naidu, Annie Besant, Malviya, Mahadev Desai, Ambedkar did
### Muslim proposals
- Delhi Proposals — joint electorates if given safeguards (separation of Sindh, one-third Muslim representation at the Centre)
- Fourteen Points — one-third Muslim representation at the Centre; residuary powers to provinces, separate electorates, safeguards for Muslim-majority provinces
### Simon report (May 1930)
- Nehru Report — joint electorates with reserved seats for minorities, not permanent separate electorates
- Simon report — new province of Orissa; separation of Sindh and Burma; ignored Andhra's demand
- Neglect of Andhra plus Prakasam's 'Andhra Kesari' moment → demand for a separate Andhra Province
### Round Table Conferences
- All three RTCs held in London under PM Ramsay MacDonald
- ★ Second RTC — soon after the Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931)
- ★ Gandhi — sole Congress representative at the Second RTC; Congress boycotted the First and Third
- Second RTC ended in deadlock over the minorities question; federal structure agreed at the First
- After returning — Gandhi resumed Civil Disobedience, jailed at Yerwada; Communal Award followed (August 1932)
- Oriyas pressed the Simon Commission and got Orissa; Andhras boycotted it
