# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:21 - Basic Structure doctrine - evolution and content; Kesavananda Bharati and after (Indira Gandhi, Minerva Mills, Waman Rao, I.R. Coelho)
### Meaning & origin
- Parliament may amend any part under Art. 368 - but cannot alter, damage or destroy its core features
- What cannot be amended under Art. 368 (APPSC-G1 2018)
- Judge-made, nowhere defined; not by any Act (APPSC-G1 2024)
- Idea from Germany (Basic Law); jurist Dietrich Conrad (1965): implied limits
- Justice Mudholkar (Sajjan Singh dissent) first said "basic features"
- List never exhaustively defined; grows case by case
### Evolution
- 1951 — Shankari Prasad: 368 covers FRs
- 1967 — Golaknath (11 judges, 6:5): FRs "transcendental and immutable"
- 1971 — 24th Amendment: Arts 13, 368 amended
- 1973 — Kesavananda Bharati: 13 judges, 7:6, CJI S.M. Sikri; doctrine propounded
- 1975 — Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain: first application; 39th clause struck
- 1980 — Minerva Mills: 42nd's 368(4), (5) struck
- 1981 — Waman Rao: cut-off 24 April 1973
- 2007 — I.R. Coelho (9 judges): no blanket immunity
- 2015 — NJAC case: 99th struck, 4:1

### Basic structure — meaning, origin, persons and FIRSTs — Meaning
## 3. Judicial review and basic structure
- Judicial Review: An inherent power of constitutional courts — examines legislative enactments and executive actions against constitutional validity
- Basic Structure Doctrine: Kesavananda Bharati (1973) — core structural features cannot be abrogated or destroyed, even through Article 368
## 4. Traps
- ⚠ "Basic Structure is explicitly defined in Part XX" — false: it was judicially formulated
- ⚠ "Fundamental Rights can never be amended after Kesavananda" — false: they can be, if the amendment does not violate the Basic Structure
## 1. Meaning
- Amending power: Parliament may amend any part under Art. 368 (including FRs and the Preamble)
- Limit: cannot damage or destroy the basic structure = features that give the Constitution its identity, i.e. it cannot alter, damage or destroy its core features
- PYQ 2024: "core features cannot be amended or altered by Parliament" (APPSC-GS 2024 key)
- Definition: Basic structure = what cannot be amended in exercise of the power under Art. 368 (APPSC-G1 2018 key)
- ⚠ Trap: it is not "the Preamble alone"
- Preamble: Kesavananda held the Preamble is part of the Constitution and amendable, subject to the basic structure
### Basic structure — meaning, origin, persons and FIRSTs — Origin & nature
## 1. Origin & nature
- Country: Germany (Basic Law)
- Judge-made: judge-made and nowhere defined; nowhere in the text; not added by any Act of Parliament
- Propounded by SC in Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)
- ⚠ Trap: "incorporated by an Act of Parliament" = false (APPSC-G1 2024; PYQ G1 2024, statement I false)
- Not exhaustive: SC has never exhaustively defined it; list grows case by case
- Exam angle: statement sets testing "judge-made vs inserted by amendment" and "can Parliament amend FRs today" (yes, subject to basic structure).
## 2. Persons → contribution
| Person | Contribution |

| Justice Mudholkar | Dissent in Sajjan Singh (1965) first spoke of "basic features" |

### Ninth Schedule and Arts. 31A, 31B, 31C → origin, cut-off, Coelho test — Origin & purpose
## 1. Origin & purpose
- Created by: 1st Amendment (1951) with Art. 31B (and 31A)
- 2026 = 75 years of the 1st Amendment (assent by President Rajendra Prasad)
- Also added Art. 15(4) + grounds of public order, friendly relations, incitement to Art. 19(2)
- Purpose: protect land-reform/zamindari-abolition laws from challenge for violating Fundamental Rights (PYQ G1 2024: I and II correct)
- Coverage: both Central and State laws
- How laws enter: only by Parliament through a constitutional amendment
- Example: 76th Amendment, 1994 placed Tamil Nadu's 69% reservation Act
### Ninth Schedule and Arts. 31A, 31B, 31C → origin, cut-off, Coelho test — Judicial review of entries
## 1. Judicial review of entries
| Case | Year | Ninth Schedule rule |
| Kesavananda Bharati | 1973 | Amendments (including Ninth Schedule inclusions) cannot destroy the basic structure |
| Waman Rao | 1981 | Cut-off date 24 April 1973 - laws/entries added after it are open to challenge on basic structure |
| I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu | 2007 | nine-judge bench (9 judges); No blanket immunity, because judicial review is basic; Rights / direct-impact test: post-1973 laws invalid if they damage the basic structure or abrogate the essence of Arts. 14, 19, 21 |
## 2. Articles 31A, 31C - saving laws
| Article | Saves | Inserted by |
| 31A | Laws on acquisition of estates, agrarian reform etc. from Arts. 14, 19 | 1st Amendment, 1951 |
| 31C | Laws implementing Art. 39(b), (c) from Arts. 14, 19 | 25th Amendment, 1971 |
- ⚠ 13 Acts at start → 282 now; cut-off 24 April 1973 = date of Kesavananda

### Doctrines → origin (Supreme Court vs Parliament)
| Doctrine / rule | Origin |
| Basic structure | Supreme Court - Kesavananda (1973) |
| Eclipse, severability, pith and substance, colourable legislation, harmonious construction, territorial nexus | Supreme Court (judge-made interpretive doctrines - CDI; APP) |
| Prospective overruling | Supreme Court - Golaknath (1967) [GK] |
| DPSP primacy over Arts. 14, 19 (Art. 31C) | Parliament - 25th Amendment (1971), widened by 42nd |
| Unlimited amending power; amendments non-justiciable (Art. 368(4), (5)) | Parliament - 42nd Amendment; struck down in Minerva Mills |
- Art. 31C: Kesavananda struck down the part barring courts from questioning a law declared to give effect to DPSP → judicial review is basic
### Scope, supremacy debate and criticism
## 1. Scope - HC power over central laws, amendments
- High Courts can declare central laws invalid: 42nd Amendment (1976) bar removed by 43rd Amendment (1977)
- Constitutional amendments can be challenged (Basic Structure)
## 2. Supremacy debate
- Judicial review principle: accepts the theory of supremacy of Courts = supremacy of the Constitution and the courts over Parliament (APPSC-G2 2017 key; PYQ G2 2017)
- Basic structure as a limit: supremacy of Constitution, not of courts or Parliament
- Parliament not sovereign: Indian Parliament is not sovereign in the British sense
- Reasons: written supreme Constitution + FRs + judicial review + federal division + limited amending power
## 3. Criticism vs defence
| View | Argument |
| Criticism | No textual basis; "rewriting of the Constitution" by unelected judges; list is open-ended → content uncertain |
| Defence | Among the biggest contributions of the Indian judiciary to constitutionalism; checks majoritarian misuse of Art. 368 |
### Universal angles
## 1. Current relevance
- 1st Amendment at 75: 18 June 2026 = 75 years of the 1st Amendment (created the Ninth Schedule); I.R. Coelho limit restated
- Current relevance: free and fair elections as basic structure cited in 2026 debate on making voting a fundamental right
- Argument: voting should be more than a statutory right, as democracy and free and fair elections are basic features
- Simultaneous polls: JPC on the 129th Amendment Bill, 2024
- Ex-CJI B.R. Gavai: does not violate basic structure
- Ex-CJIs U.U. Lalit and Sanjiv Khanna: raised basic-structure doubts
## 2. Debates & significance
- Criticism & debates: vague and unlisted; critics call it "judicial supremacy"
- Support: Constitution's shield against majorities
- Significance: keeps Art. 368 limited; judicial review itself is basic → cannot be removed even by amendment
- FIRST/LAST: first case to apply the doctrine = Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)

### Who made the doctrine?
## Kesavananda Bharati
- ★ Gave the doctrine of basic structure (1973, 13 judges, 7:6); Parliament cannot damage or destroy the basic structure
- ★ Supreme Court interpreted the basic features of the Constitution
- Struck down the part of Article 31C stopping courts from asking whether a law carries out the DPSPs; judicial review basic
## Nature
- Judge-made, growing case by case; not defined in the Constitution
- FRs may be amended, provided the basic structure is not damaged
- Parliament is not sovereign in the British sense — no unlimited amending power; written Constitution, FRs, judicial review, federal division limit it
## Articles and doctrines
- Article 31C — protects laws implementing Art. 39(b) and 39(c) from challenge under Arts. 14 and 19

- Created by amendments: Art. 31C primacy, Art. 13(4), unlimited amending power
- Prospective overruling — first applied in Golaknath (1967)
### Supremacy & debate
- Rests on supremacy of the Constitution and judicial review

- 43rd Amendment Act, 1977 — removed the 42nd's bar on High Courts reviewing central laws
- 129th Amendment Bill, 2024: former CJI B.R. Gavai told the JPC it did not violate basic structure; U.U. Lalit and Sanjiv Khanna raised doubts
- Judicial review — inherent power of constitutional courts; checks laws and executive action
- Basic structure cannot be destroyed even under Article 368
