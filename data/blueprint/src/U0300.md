### Ordinance (Article 123) — basis, conditions and force
## 1. Basis
- President: Article 123 (Part V); Governor: Article 213 (Part VI) (Governor's parallel power: Art. 213)
- Nature: A legislative power of the Executive — not an executive or administrative function
- When: Only when the Legislature is in recess — not when both Houses (or the single House of a unicameral state) are in session
- President: when either or both Houses of Parliament are not in session; Governor: when either or both Houses of the State Legislature are not in session
- Satisfaction: Circumstances exist requiring immediate action
- Force: Same force and effect as an Act — and subject to the same limitations
## 2. Conditions & force — Prep detail
- Conditions: Parliament (either House) not in session + President satisfied immediate action needed; on ministerial advice
- Force: of an Act; can be retrospective, amend or repeal an Act
- Void if beyond Parliament's competence or violating Fundamental Rights
- Scope co-extensive with Parliament's power; cannot amend the Constitution
- Origin: relic of the GoI Act, 1935
### Ordinance — scope, limits, laying and life
## 1. Scope and limits
- Can: Amend an ordinary Act; modify or repeal existing Central or State laws within legislative competence; operate retrospectively (like an ordinary statute); introduce or amend tax provisions
- Cannot: Amend the Constitution under Article 368 (limited to ordinary legislative power); violate Fundamental Rights — subject to Article 13(2), void if it takes away or abridges Part III rights
- Laying: Must be laid before the Legislature on reassembly; ceases six weeks after reassembly unless approved earlier
- Houses reassembling on different dates → six weeks from the later date
- Maximum life: Six months and six weeks — maximum gap between sessions is six months (Articles 85 and 174) + the six-week approval window
- ⚠ Trap: "The President cannot repeal or withdraw an ordinance once issued" is false
## 2. Linked time limits
- Ordinance (Article 123): Valid six weeks after reassembly; maximum life without approval six months plus six weeks
- Absence of an MP (Article 101(4)): Unexcused absence for 60 days can lead to vacation of the seat — the calculation excludes periods of prorogation and adjournments of more than four consecutive days
## 3. Life, lapse — Prep detail
- Lapse: 6 weeks after reassembly (six weeks after Parliament reassembles, from the later date)
- Earlier if both Houses disapprove
- President may withdraw at any time (withdrawn)
- Maximum life: 6 months + 6 weeks
### Amendments and cases on ordinance satisfaction and re-promulgation
## 1. Judicial review
- Subject to judicial review: D.C. Wadhwa (1987); Krishna Kumar Singh (2017)
- Re-promulgation without legislative ratification is a subversion of the Constitution
## 2. Amendments and cases — Prep detail
- Satisfaction: 38th Amendment (1975) (38th Amdt (1975)) made satisfaction under Arts 123, 213, 352 non-justiciable (made final)
- 44th (1978) reversed it
| Case | Holding |

- ⚠ 38th = satisfaction non-justiciable; 44th = reversed; Krishna Kumar Singh (2017) = justiciable

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:26
### Veto by type of bill
- Money Bill — President may assent or withhold assent, but cannot return it for reconsideration
- Money Bills are usually brought with the President's prior recommendation
- Constitutional Amendment Bill — presidential assent mandatory under the 24th Amendment, 1971; cannot reject or return it
### Vetoes actually used
- Pocket veto possible because Article 111 sets no time limit in India
- The 10-day limit (Bill becomes law if not decided) is a US feature, not Indian
- Governor has no pocket veto — Supreme Court (2025): endless inaction under Article 200 is justiciable
- Giani Zail Singh — pocket veto on the Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986
### Ordinance: basis
- Article 123 — President's ordinance power; Article 213 — parallel power of the Governor
- Ordinance power is a leftover of the Government of India Act, 1935 (Governor-General's power to issue ordinances)
- Issued only when the legislature is in recess and the Head of State is sure quick action is needed
- None can be issued while both Houses are sitting
- Same force, effect and limits as an Act of Parliament / State Legislature; legislative, not executive
- Cannot amend the Constitution
- Article 13(2) — ordinance is 'law', so void if it abridges Fundamental Rights
- Can make or change tax provisions within legislative power
- Article 85 — max six-month gap between two sessions of Parliament; Article 174 — same for State Legislatures
### Ordinance: can vs cannot
- Within legislative power an ordinance can: amend an ordinary Act, act retrospectively, change taxes
- An ordinance cannot amend the Constitution under Article 368
### Life of an ordinance
- Must be laid before the legislature on reassembly
- Houses meeting on different dates — six weeks counted from the later date
### Satisfaction & judicial review
## Amendments
- 38th Amendment (1975) — made the President's satisfaction final
- 44th Amendment — reversed that position
- Ordinances are subject to judicial review
## Cases
| Case | Year | Held |
| D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar | 1987 | Routine re-promulgation without placing before the legislature = fraud on the Constitution (Bihar) |
| Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar | 2017 | Re-promulgation can be a fraud on the Constitution; laying before the legislature is mandatory |
## Criticism
- Frequent ordinances bypass parliamentary deliberation
- Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026 — revived the debate on ordinance misuse and executive overreach
### Exam corner
- Constitutional Amendment Bill — cannot be returned (assent mandatory)
- The one-time return power can apply to Financial Bills of Categories I and II
- US President — only a qualified veto, overridden by a two-thirds majority of Congress
- MP's unexcused absence (60 days, Article 101(4)) — periods of prorogation and adjournments of more than four consecutive days are left out
### Veto by type of bill and pocket veto
- ★ Gyani Zail Singh — used the pocket veto by withholding assent to the Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill (1986)
- ★ Pocket veto = taking no action on a Bill (neither assenting nor returning it); used only once in India by the President
- ★ Dr. Rajendra Prasad — absolute veto, on Council of Ministers' advice, over the PEPSU Appropriation Bill, 1954
### Ordinance (Article 123) — basis, conditions and force
- ★ Article 123 — President issues ordinances during recess of Parliament
- ★ Ordinance has the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament
- ★ President can withdraw an ordinance at any time (so "can never repeal it" is wrong)
- ★ Issuing ordinances is a legislative power of the President (a part of Parliament)
- ★ Ordinances must be approved by Parliament within six weeks of its reassembly
### Ordinance — scope, limits, laying and life
- ★ Article 123 — President's power to promulgate ordinances (and give assent to them)
- ★ 2002 ordinance on electoral reforms, returned by the Union Cabinet unchanged — assented under Article 123
- ★ Ordinance power is available except when both Houses are in session, when the President is satisfied circumstances need quick action
- ★ Scope of an ordinance = scope of Parliament's ordinary legislative power — can amend any Central Act, but not the Constitution
