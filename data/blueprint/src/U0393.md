### Regionalism — terms, types, positive vs negative
| Term | Meaning |
| Region | Area shaped by history, with its own language, culture, identity + political implications |
| Regionalism | Emotional attachment to a particular area; can resist a dominant culture or challenge the nation-state |
| Supra-state | Several states sharing interests (e.g., the North-East) |
| Inter-state | Conflict between states |
| Intra-state | A region inside a state seeking recognition |
| Positive | Negative |
| Regional parties give backward regions a voice | Parochialism; hostility to migrants (Shiv Sena, MNS) |
| Cultural pride, balanced development, local innovation, accountability | Law-and-order breakdowns, political exploitation, openings for extremists |
| Intergroup solidarity (e.g., Tripura Tribal Autonomous District Council) | Inter-state disputes; new linguistic minorities |
### Causes of regionalism — factor → effect
| Factor | Effect / example |
| Economic inequality | Uneven industrialisation: Bombay and West Bengal held 59% of capital in 1948; infrastructural + social backwardness (low literacy, poor health, few industries) |
| Cultural dominance / fear of losing identity | Marginalisation of minority linguistic groups (e.g., Gorkhas in Bengal) |
| Linguistic identity | 22 official languages + about 1,635 mother tongues → demands for linguistic states |
| Political/administrative neglect | Sub-regions ignored → new-state demands (Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Telangana) |
| Migration and job competition | Sons-of-the-soil feeling strongest where migrants and local educated youth compete for middle-class jobs |
- Exam angle: UPPSC 2008 - uneven development, fear of losing cultural identity and political prominence are all causes (answer: "all the above").

### Linguistic states and regionalism — FIRSTs
- Andhra State: The first state formed on a linguistic basis — the first linguistic state — 1953
- Formed 1 October 1953, capital Kurnool
- Andhra Movement: The first major ethnic movement leading to state formation — the first movement leading to linguistic state formation
- Potti Sriramulu: The first person to fast unto death for a linguistic state
- States Reorganisation Commission: The first Linguistic Reorganisation Commission — first established 1953; Fazl Ali — its first Chairman
- Chhattisgarh: The first new State after the 2000 reorganisation
- 7th Constitutional Amendment of 1956: The first Constitutional Amendment addressing regional imbalance indirectly
- Sequence: Dar Commission (1948) → Visalandhra Mahasabha (1949) → Swami Sitaram's fast (1951) → SRC (1953)
### Commissions and Acts on linguistic reorganisation → year
| Body | Year / members | Recommendation |
| Dhar Commission (Linguistic Provinces Commission) | 1948, S.K. Dhar | Rejected language as the basis; preferred administrative convenience [GK] |
| States Reorganisation Commission | Dec 1953; Fazl Ali (chair), K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru | Report 1955: 16 states + 3 territories; rejected "one language, one state" [GK] |
| States Reorganisation Act, 1956 | 1956 | 14 states + 6 UTs; merged Andhra State with Telangana → Andhra Pradesh |
- Negatives: Sons-of-the-soil movements, xenophobia, new linguistic minorities (e.g., Telugus in Tamil Nadu), demands for further splits
- ⚠ SRC report = 16 states + 3 territories; SR Act 1956 = 14 states + 6 UTs
### Sons-of-the-soil movements → state
- Doctrine (since 1950): A state belongs to its main linguistic group ("locals")
- Everyone else = "outsider", however long they have lived there
- Grew out of the job shortage of the 1960s-70s
| Movement | State | Target / issue |
| Shiv Sena (1960s) | Maharashtra | South Indians; later MNS against North Indians (2008) |
| Mulki rules | Hyderabad/Telangana | Local-residence preference in jobs; led to the 1969 and 1972-73 agitations |
| Telangana movements | Andhra Pradesh | 1969 agitation and the 2009-14 phase, leading to statehood in 2014 |
| Anti-Hindi agitations | Tamil Nadu | 1937, 1965, 1986; led to the two-language formula |
- ⚠ Shiv Sena = South Indians (1960s); MNS = North Indians (2008)
### Articles and state laws on domicile job quotas
- Constitutional objection: Local job reservation said to contradict Articles 14, 15, 16 and 19
| Article / law | Provision / quota | Status |
| Art. 16(2) | Bars discrimination in public employment incl. place of birth and residence | - |
| Art. 16(3) | Only Parliament can prescribe a residence requirement | Public Employment (Requirement as to Residence) Act, 1957 |
| AP Employment of Local Candidates in the Industries/Factories Act, 2019 | 75% of private industrial jobs for locals | First such state law; challenged in AP High Court [GK] |
| Haryana State Employment of Local Candidates Act, 2020 | 75% of private jobs up to a salary ceiling | Struck down by the Punjab & Haryana High Court (Nov 2023) as unconstitutional [GK] |
| Jharkhand State Employment of Local Candidates in Private Sector Act, 2021 | 75% of lower-salary private jobs | [GK] |
- Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2025 asked which state's 75% private-sector local quota was struck down by the High Court - Haryana.
- ⚠ First 75% law = AP (2019); struck down = Haryana (2020 Act, Nov 2023)

- Regional parties — rose in late 20th century; demand more powers and funds; add political diversity
- Steps against regionalism — NIC, Articles 44 and 51A, Fifth, Sixth, Eighth Schedules (not the Eleventh)
- 131st Amendment Bill, 2026 defeated — southern states feared ending the seat freeze based on the 1971 Census; industrialised states dislike being net fiscal donors
- Andhra Movement — first ethnic movement to win a state
### Sons-of-the-soil movements
- Doctrine — State belongs to its main linguistic group; others outsiders however long settled; grew with 1960s–70s job shortages
- Shiv Sena (1960s) — targeted South Indians; MNS (2008) — North Indians
- Mulki rules — job preference for local residents in Hyderabad / Telangana; agitations of 1969 and 1972–73
- Anti-Hindi agitations (Tamil Nadu: 1937, 1965, 1986) — two-language formula
### Domicile job quotas
- Andhra Pradesh — first law: 75% private industrial jobs for locals, 2019 (Employment of Local Candidates in the Industries/Factories Act)
- Followed by Haryana (2020) and Jharkhand (2021)
- Haryana Act — struck down November 2023 by the Punjab and Haryana High Court
- Article 16(3) — only Parliament may set residence rules; Public Employment (Requirement as to Residence) Act, 1957
- Article 16(2) — bars discrimination by place of residence
