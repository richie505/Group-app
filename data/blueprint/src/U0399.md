### Fundamental Rights — Articles at a glance and milestones
## 1. Fundamental Rights (Articles 12 to 35)
- Article 12: Defines "State" for Fundamental Rights
- Article 13: Laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights are void — provides for judicial review
- Article 14: Equality before law
- Article 15: Prohibits discrimination; permits special provisions — Article 15(4) introduced by the First Amendment
- Article 16: Equality of opportunity in public employment; permits reservation — 16(4) and 16(4A)
- Article 17: Abolishes untouchability; Article 18: Abolishes titles
- Article 19: Six freedoms — 19(1)(a) to 19(1)(g)
- Article 20: Protection in respect of conviction
- Article 21: Life and personal liberty; Article 21A: Right to Education — by the 86th Amendment
- Article 22: Protection against arrest and detention
- Article 23: Prohibits traffic in human beings and forced labour; Article 24: Prohibits employment of children
- Articles 25 to 28: Freedom of religion; Articles 29 and 30: Cultural and educational rights of minorities
- Article 32: Right to Constitutional Remedies — "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution (Ambedkar)
- Articles 33 to 35: Power to modify Fundamental Rights for forces and related groups
## 2. Milestones in the demand
- 1895: First demand — Swaraj Bill
- 1928: Motilal Nehru Report
- 1931: Karachi Session of the Congress
### Constitutional Articles → content and UDHR parallel — Fundamental Rights and the UDHR
## 1. UDHR — adoption and rights recognised
- A foundational international document — recognizes the fundamental rights and freedoms of all human beings
- Adopted by the United Nations General Assembly on 10 December 1948 (Human Rights Day); 30 articles
- Prepared after the atrocities of World War II — to safeguard human dignity
- Rights recognized: Right to life; freedom of speech; freedom of religion; equality before law; right to work; right to education; right to health; right to social security
## 2. Fundamental Rights and the UDHR
- FR vs human right: Every Fundamental Right is a human right, but not every human right is an FR; right to vote = only a statutory right

### Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 — status, timeline, objective
## 1. Status and commencement
- Protection of Human Rights Act: 1993 — enforced 28 September 1993; concerns life, liberty, equality and dignity
- Established the NHRC (on 12 October 1993), State Human Rights Commissions and Human Rights Courts
- NHRC: A statutory and independent body — not constitutional — established under the Protection of Human Rights Act (PHRA), 1993, effective 28 September 1993
- Three levels: NHRC (national level) and SHRC (state level) under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993; Human Rights Courts — a district- or trial-level mechanism for speedy trials
- Timeline:
- Ordinance: 28 Sept 1993
- Act deemed in force from 28 September 1993
- NHRC constituted: 12 October 1993
- Paris Principles: NHRC in conformity with them (Paris, Oct 1991; endorsed by UNGA 20 Dec 1993)
- Objective: NHRC + SHRCs + Human Rights Courts for better protection of human rights
- ⚠ Ordinance/in force 28 Sept; NHRC 12 Oct 1993
### PHRA, 1993 — definitions, public servants and NHRC limits

| Section | Subject |
| 3 | NHRC |
| 21 | SHRC |
| 30 | Human Rights Courts |
| 31 | Special Public Prosecutors |
