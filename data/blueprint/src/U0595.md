### Composition of the Earth — crustal elements by rank (data)
| Rank | Element | % by weight of crust |
| 1 | Oxygen (O) | 46.6 |
| 2 | Silicon (Si) | 27.7 |
| 3 | Aluminium (Al) | 8.1 - most abundant metal |
| 4 | Iron (Fe) | 5.0 - second-most abundant metal |

- SIAL (Si+Al): granitic, forms continents, density ~2.7

### Minerals → class, order of crystallisation, hardness
- Feldspar: silicon and oxygen occur in all feldspars; sodium, potassium, calcium, aluminium in particular varieties
- About half the crust is feldspar, not one-fourth
- ⚠ Trap: "one-fourth" is WRONG (APPSC G1 2024: statement I only)
- Quartz (SiO2): hard, resists weathering; major part of granite and sandstone
| Silicate class (structure) | Example minerals |
| Inosilicate - single chain | Augite (pyroxene) [GK] |
| Inosilicate - double chain | Hornblende (amphibole) [GK] |
| Phyllosilicate (sheet) | Muscovite, biotite (mica) - APPSC 2024 |
| Tectosilicate (framework) | Albite, orthoclase (feldspar), quartz [GK] |
- Bowen's reaction series: order in which minerals crystallise from cooling magma (APPSC 2024 answer)
- Discontinuous branch: olivine → pyroxene → amphibole → biotite
- Then K-feldspar → muscovite → quartz last (most stable)
- Mafic minerals form first, at the highest temperature
- Mohs hardness scale (1-10): talc, gypsum, calcite, fluorite, apatite, orthoclase, quartz (7), topaz, corundum, diamond (10)
- ⚠ Mica = phyllosilicate (sheet); olivine first, quartz last in Bowen's series
### Rock types → examples; igneous rocks
## 1. Rock types
| Rock | Formation | Examples |
| Igneous | Cooling or solidification of magma or lava | Granite — intrusive; basalt — extrusive; gabbro, dolerite, pegmatite |
| Sedimentary | Surface deposition | Sandstone, limestone, shale, conglomerate, coal, chalk |
| Metamorphic | Existing rock altered by heat or pressure | Marble, slate, quartzite, gneiss, schist |
- Petrology: study of rocks (branch of geology); three types = igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic
- Odd-one-out traps:
- Marble/slate/quartzite/gneiss = metamorphic, though parents (limestone, shale, sandstone, granite) are not
- Coal = sedimentary (organic)
## 2. Igneous rocks
- Origin: cooling and solidifying of magma/lava; "primary" rocks (APPSC 2019)
- Fossils: hold no fossils
| Type | Formation | Examples |
| Intrusive (plutonic) | cool slowly at depth → coarse-grained (large grains) | granite, gabbro (APPSC G2 2024: granite = intrusive igneous) |
| Extrusive (volcanic) | lava cools fast at surface → fine-grained | basalt, rhyolite, andesite |
| Composition | Character | Intrusive (coarse) |
| Felsic / acid (Felsic (acidic)) | Silica and feldspar rich, light-coloured, low density; rich in silica, aluminium, sodium, potassium (feldspar + quartz) [GK] | Granite |
| Intermediate | - | Diorite [GK] |
| Mafic / basic | Rich in iron, magnesium, calcium; dark, dense; olivine, pyroxene [GK] | Gabbro[GK] |
- ⚠ Trap: APPSC 2024 Assertion-Reason - "felsic rocks contain high iron, magnesium and calcium" is false → describes mafic rocks
- Fracture energy: volcanic rocks (andesite, dacite, rhyolite, basalt) need more energy to fracture than many sedimentary rocks
### Sedimentary rocks — modes of formation and terms
- Process: sediments from broken rocks transported, deposited, compacted and hardened (lithified) / cemented in layers (strata) → "stratified rocks"
| Mode of formation | Examples [GK] |
| Mechanically (clastic) | Sandstone, conglomerate, shale, loess |
| Chemically | Limestone (chemical), rock salt, gypsum, halite, chert |
| Organically | Coal, chalk, coral limestone, geyserite |
- Types (notes' grouping): mechanically formed = sandstone (of sand grains), conglomerate, shale; organically formed = coal, limestone, chalk; chemically formed = rock salt, gypsum
- ⚠ Limestone: chemical (table) vs organic (notes' grouping)
| Term | Meaning / examples |
| Porosity | Pore space |
| permeability | Ability to pass water |
| Porous and permeable | chalk and sandstone |
| Porous but impermeable | clay |
| Neither | Granite |
- Fossils: these rocks may contain fossils (sandstone may hold plant, animal, micro-organism fossils); host coal, petroleum, natural gas
- Igneous and Archaean rocks hold none: Archaean = "no fossils" zone (formed before life)
- Dravidian (Palaeozoic) rocks: fossil-rich - plenty of marine fossils
### Metamorphic rocks — parent → metamorphosed rock
| Parent rock | Metamorphic rock |
| Limestone | Marble |
| Shale / clay | Slate (then schist) |
| Sandstone | Quartzite |
| Granite | Gneiss |
| Coal | Graphite (via anthracite) |
- Formation: igneous or sedimentary rocks recrystallise under heat and pressure (no melting)
- Typical minerals: garnet, kyanite, sillimanite, staurolite
### Rock cycle — processes linking the three types
- Cycle: Magma → (cooling) igneous → (weathering, erosion by wind and water, deposition, lithification) sedimentary → (heat, pressure) metamorphic → (melting) magma.
- Surface → weathering, erosion, transportation, deposition → sedimentary (burial, compaction, cementation)
- Deeper burial, heat and pressure → metamorphic
- Melting → igneous → uplift back to surface
- Key rule: any type can turn into any other; exposed metamorphic rock can weather into sediment
- Mantle link: rock material comes up in eruptions; subducted crust melts back into the mantle
- Carbon cycling: CO2 in rain → carbonic acid → weathers rock → stored as bicarbonate/limestone

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:6
### Three rock types
## Igneous
- ★ Granite is igneous, not metamorphic; granite = intrusive, basalt = extrusive
## Sedimentary
- ★ Coal is sedimentary; sedimentary rocks cover 75% of Earth's surface; hold most fossils and mineral oil
- All sedimentary: sandstone, limestone, shale, conglomerate
- Called stratified rocks: sediments settled, pressed, cemented in successive layers (strata)
- Ways formed: chemically — rock salt; mechanically — sandstone; organically — coal
## Metamorphic
- ★ Marble = metamorphic, from limestone by heat and pressure
- From igneous: granite → gneiss; basalt → amphibolite, schist
- Marble, slate, quartzite metamorphic; limestone is sedimentary (odd one out; parent of marble)
- Causes of metamorphism: heat and pressure (full melting not needed — would give magma)
- Typical metamorphic minerals: garnet, kyanite, sillimanite; halite is not (chemical sedimentary)
## Mineral
- Quartz = silicon dioxide (SiO2); hard, weathering-resistant; in granite and sandstone
### Igneous: texture & make-up
- Intrusive rocks cool slowly at depth → coarse grains (granite, gabbro)
- Felsic: silica-rich, light-coloured, fairly light; mafic: rich in Fe, Mg, Ca — dark and dense
| Rock | Type |
| Rhyolite | felsic extrusive |
| Granite | felsic intrusive |
| Diorite | intermediate intrusive |
| Basalt | mafic extrusive |
- Lithology: physical nature of a rock unit — texture, grain size, composition
### Parent → metamorphic
| Parent | Metamorphic |
| Limestone | Marble |
| Shale | Slate |
| Sandstone | Quartzite |
| Coal | Graphite |
### Rock cycle
- Weathering → erosion → deposition → lithification → sedimentary rock; heat and pressure → metamorphic; melting → magma
- Exposed metamorphic rocks can wear into sediment; any rock type can become another
- CO2 in rainwater makes carbonic acid → weathering
- Enhanced rock weathering: crushed basalt spread on farmland; locks CO2 as bicarbonate
### Minerals
## Abundance
- Crust: oxygen (~46.6%) first, silicon (~27.7%) second; iron = second-commonest metal (~5.0%)
- Earth as a whole by mass: iron 32.1% > oxygen 30.1% > silicon 15.1% > magnesium 13.9%
- Feldspar: about one-half of the crust
- Suess: SIAL (silicon-aluminium) — granitic, continental; SIMA — basaltic; NIFE — nickel-iron core
## Classes and crystallisation
- Muscovite — phyllosilicate; albite — tectosilicate; hornblende — double-chain, augite — single-chain inosilicate
- Bowen's series: quartz crystallises last, most stable
- Discontinuous branch: olivine (first) → pyroxene → amphibole → biotite; mafic minerals form first at highest temperature
- Mohs hardness: quartz 7; apatite 5, orthoclase 6, topaz 8
## Time scale
- Geological time scale made in 1760 by Giovanni Arduino; Standard scale set in Italy in 1881; Indian scale backed by T.H. Holland
