### Champaran (1917) and Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)
## 1. Champaran (1917)
- Plantation owners increased taxes on peasants and imposed other illegal exactions — associated with indigo exploitation; Gandhi led the Champaran movement against it
- Teen Kathiya: Forced indigo cultivation on three-twentieths of the land — associated with indigo cultivation in Champaran
- Place & year: 1917, Bihar - first mass movement, but in Bihar, not Gujarat
- Precursor: Bengal indigo revolt of 1859 (see Current relevance)
- Action: Gandhi defied order to leave the district (Motihari)
- Toured villages with lawyers recording complaints → forced a government (official) inquiry
## 2. Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)
- Date: Feb-Mar 1918, Gujarat
- Dispute: mill owners withdrew "plague bonus"
- Workers demanded 50% wage hike (wartime inflation); owners offered 20%
- Outcome: Gandhi advised 35% + first hunger strike in India → tribunal awarded 35%
### Kheda (1918)
## 1. Kheda (1918)
- Crop failure led to revenue demands — Kheda farmers faced them despite crop failure and sought relief from land revenue
- The Kheda revenue demand led to the Kheda Satyagraha
- Place: Kheda district, Gujarat, 1918
- Grievance: crop failure (drought/famine) → peasants sought remission (suspension) of land revenue; government refused
- Peasants refused to pay despite threats of land confiscation
- Called off: government agreed revenue recovered only from those able to pay (APPSC 2023 key)
- Suspended for poor peasants (tax suspended for the year); confiscated property returned

### Jallianwala Bagh massacre, 13 April 1919 — the incident, officials
## 1. The incident
- Place: Amritsar, Punjab — date: 13 April 1919; occasion: Baisakhi
- Viceroy: Lord Chelmsford

- Peaceful crowd in walled garden protesting arrests + Rowlatt Act
- Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer ordered the firing — ordered troops to open fire
- Dyer blocked the only exit, fired without warning for about 10 minutes, 1,650 rounds
- Michael O'Dwyer: The Lieutenant Governor of Punjab — Dyer was associated with the firing, O'Dwyer with Punjab administration
- Punjab Lt. Governor Sir Michael O'Dwyer handed Amritsar to Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer (Brig.-Gen. Reginald Dyer) → banned assemblies
