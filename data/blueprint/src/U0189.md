### Raja Rammohan Roy (1772-1833)
- Brahmo Samaj = reformist (reason, modern ideas), unlike revivalist Arya Samaj
- "Father of Modern India"; Father of the Indian Renaissance
- 'Raja' title from Mughal emperor Akbar II; went to England 1830
- Died at Bristol, 1833
- Hindu College 1817 (with David Hare); Vedanta College 1825
- Against child marriage, polygamy; for widow remarriage, women's property
- Opposed Jury Act 1827, 1823 Press Ordinance

### Brahmo tenets and social programme (statement type)
## 1. Religious tenets
- Monotheism: Worship of one formless, eternal God
- Sources: Rational teachings of the Upanishads, Bible and Koran
- Banned: idols, animal sacrifice and sectarian symbols in its halls
- Rejected: Priestly ritualism + caste distinctions
- Scripture: None treated as final authority; no belief in avatars
- Nature: Theistic (Astika) reform within Hinduism, not a new religion
- Collective prayer
## 2. Social programme
- Against: child marriage, polygamy, female infanticide, caste
- For: widow remarriage, women's education and property rights
- UPSC 2025 key: He respected the traditional philosophies of the East and wanted a rational, scientific approach - both statements correct

### Keshab Chandra Sen → contributions; splits of 1866 and 1878
## 1. Keshab Chandra Sen
- Life: 1838-1884; most influential Brahmo of the later years
- Acharya: Made acharya (minister) of the Samaj by Debendranath (1862) - first non-Brahmin acharya
- Spread: Took the Brahmo Samaj to South India
- His lectures in Bombay inspired the Prarthana Samaj
- Law: Native Marriage Act (Brahmo Marriage Act, Act III of 1872) - legalised inter-caste marriage and fixed minimum marriage ages; 1872 [GK]
## 2. The splits
| Split | Cause | New body | Leader |
