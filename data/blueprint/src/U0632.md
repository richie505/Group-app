### Soil — composition, classification and types
## 1. Composition
- Good soil by volume (approx.): Mineral matter ~45%, organic matter ~5%, water ~25%, air ~25%
- Soil organic carbon (SOC):
- Binds particles into aggregates, raises infiltration, feeds microbes
- Clay soils hold more SOC than sandy soils; topsoil holds the most
- India's average SOC reportedly fell from 1% to 0.3% over 70 years
## 2. ICAR/USDA classification

| Vertisols (black soils) | ~8.5% |
- ⚠ Largest order = Inceptisols; smallest = Mollisols; Vertisols = black soils
## 3. Origin and number of types

- Residual (in situ) → dominate Peninsular India
- Drifted (ex situ) → dominate North India

### Soil erosion — types and factors — Types of erosion
## 1. Types of erosion
| Type | Process | Example |
| Sheet | Thin uniform top layer removed evenly by overland flow; widespread and least noticed | Gentle cultivated slopes |
| Rill | Small channels cut by running water; intense rain | Intense rain on bare fields; cultivated slopes |
| Gully | Deep trenches from concentrated, continuous flow; land becomes unfit | Chambal ravines |
| Wind | Deflation, saltation, surface creep; removal of soil by strong winds in arid areas, dust storms | Thar Desert |
| Mass (landslide) | Gravity / downslope movement on slopes | Himalayan monsoon landslides |
| Tunnel | Subsurface removal creating sinkholes | Parts of Australia, Africa |
- Water erosion sequence: Splash → sheet → rill → gully; water = most powerful erosion agent
- Wind erosion: Worst on bare, dry, fine-textured soils with strong, frequent winds
### Soil erosion — types and factors — Factors controlling erosion rate
## 1. Factors controlling erosion rate
- Key factors: Rainfall intensity and duration, slope gradient, vegetation cover and land use, soil type/texture
- ⚠ Key: Intensity matters more than total rainfall
- Infiltration excess (rainfall factor): Intense, concentrated bursts exceed the soil's infiltration capacity → water pools and runs off or evaporates instead of recharging soil
- Two 2026 studies (Nature, Nature Geoscience) link concentrated rainfall to drier soils
- This drying effect roughly cancels the gain from more annual rain
- RUSLE: Predicts long-term water erosion on slopes; a 2026 study pairs it with GIS for NE India's hills
- Soil & slope: Clay soils hold more soil organic carbon; steep slopes lose carbon-rich topsoil
### Soil conservation methods → how they work
| Method | How it works |

## Soil basics
- Soil horizons, surface downward — O → A → B → C → R (organic litter, topsoil, subsoil, weathered parent material, bedrock)
- B horizon — subsoil where minerals washed down from upper layers accumulate
- Good soil by volume — mineral 45%, organic 5%, water 25%, air 25%
- Soil organic carbon — binds particles into aggregates, raises infiltration, feeds microbes
- Residual (in situ) soils dominate Peninsular India; drifted (ex situ) dominate North India
- Eight major soil types: alluvial, black, red and yellow, laterite, arid, saline and alkaline, peaty and marshy, forest and mountain
## Soil types
| Soil | Key features | Crops / regions |
| Black (regur) | from Deccan Trap basalt; neutral to alkaline; rich in calcium, potassium, magnesium; poor in N, P, humus | cotton, jowar, chillies, tobacco; Deccan lava plateau |
| Red | red from iron oxide, yellow when hydrated; from granite and gneiss | ragi, groundnut |
| Laterite | heavy leaching, rainfall > 200 cm, high temperature; rich in iron and aluminium oxides; acidic, infertile; poor in N, phosphate, calcium; excess iron oxide and potash | tea, coffee, rubber (manured); summits of Western and Eastern Ghats |
| Arid (desert) | sandy; poor in nitrogen and humus; kankar at lower depths; improves with irrigation | — |
| Forest and mountain | humus-rich, on slopes; poor in potash and phosphorus; mountain meadow soils above tree line | — |
