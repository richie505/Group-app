### Terms → meaning (online behaviour and information disorder)
## 1. Online behaviour terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Cyber venting | Using websites to pour out one's grievances (JPSC PYQ) |
| Doxxing | Publicly sharing someone's private information (real name, location, workplace) online without consent to harm or shame (LENSD, 1 Aug 2026) |
| Cyberbullying | Repeated online harassment/humiliation, esp. of children (IYB 2026 lists it among online risks) |
| Trolling | Deliberately provocative or abusive posts to upset or derail discussion [GK] |
| Cyberstalking | Repeatedly monitoring or contacting a person online against their will [GK] |
| Nomophobia | Irrational fear of being without one's mobile phone (no signal, battery, balance) (APPSC PYQ) |
| Digital footprint | Trail of data a person leaves online; keeping it low reduces exposure to fraud |
## 2. Information disorder
| Term | Meaning |
| Misinformation | False content spread without intent to deceive (innocent mistakes, rumours) (UPSC notes) |
| Disinformation | False content deliberately created to achieve political/social/economic goals (UPSC notes) |
| Malinformation | Genuine information shared to cause harm (e.g., leaked private data) [GK] |
| Filter bubbles/echo chambers | Algorithms prioritise content by user preference → isolate users from other viewpoints |
- ⚠ Misinformation = no intent; Disinformation = deliberate; Malinformation = genuine info shared to harm

### Netiquettes and digital citizenship — dos and don'ts
- Core rule: engage in constructive online dialogue, respect privacy
- "digital freedom should be accompanied by digital responsibility"
| Medium | Do | Don't |
| Email | Clear subject line, polite greeting, check recipients before sending | Type in ALL CAPS (reads as shouting), forward chain mails |
| Social media | Verify before sharing, respect others' privacy, credit sources | Post others' photos/data without consent, troll or abuse |
| Online meetings | Join on time, mute when not speaking, use chat/raise-hand | Talk over others, record without permission |
- Responsible digital citizen: identifies misinformation, verifies sources, protects personal data, respects privacy, engages in civil discourse
- Rights side: online grievance redressal, e-consultations, open government data, e.g. AP Grama and Ward Sachivalayam system
### Online safety practices — recommended vs not
- Recommended: (APPSC PYQ)
- strong passwords
- verifying information before sharing
- Caution while sharing personal/sensitive information
- NOT recommended: accepting friend requests from unknown people without caution
- Cyber-hygiene tips (Telangana Cyber Security Bureau, #OnlineButUnafraid, 20 Mar 2026):
- Don't rush to pay a "friend" asking for money (ID may be hacked)
- never click a link or scan a QR code to receive money
- Keep your digital footprint low
- Use WPA3 Wi-Fi
- Exam angle: "which is NOT a safe practice" and odd-one-out questions.

### Acts, rules, sections and court cases
## 1. IT Rules 2021 and Fact Check Unit
| Step | What happened |
| IT (Intermediary Guidelines & Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 | Notified under Sec. 87 of IT Act, 2000; grievance officer, nodal contact; SSMIs need Resident Compliance Officer, monthly compliance reports (LENS May 2025) |
| April 2023 amendment | Intermediary loses safe harbour if content flagged "fake" by the PIB FCU about Central Govt business (LENS May 2025; TH 11 Mar 2026) |
| March 2024 notification | FCU under PIB formally notified (TH, 11 Mar 2026) |
| Court challenge | Challenged in Bombay HC (executive overreach, no due process) (LENS May 2025); SC hearing (Bench of CJI Surya Kant, Mar 2026): Centre says Rules do not curb humour, satire or criticism (TH, 11 Mar 2026) |
- Platform duties (IT Rules 2021): due diligence, prompt removal of harmful content, grievance processes esp. for content harmful to children
- Court challenges:
- Supreme Court stayed the FCU notification (March 2024)
- Bombay HC struck down the 2023 FCU amendment (September 2024)

### Fact-check bodies and counter-measures → body — PIB Fact Check Unit
## 1. PIB Fact Check Unit
- PIB Fact Check Unit: set up November 2019 to debunk fake news about the Government of India, its ministries, PSUs and central bodies
- Reporting channels: WhatsApp +91 8799711259, factcheck@pib.gov.in, factcheck.pib.gov.in
## 2. Platform and MHA measures
| Measure | Body / feature |
| WhatsApp tip line | Launched to curb fake news ahead of the 2019 Lok Sabha election (APPSC PYQ) |
| SAHYOG Portal | Run by I4C (MHA) for takedown notices, alongside the Cyber Crime Reporting Portal; MHA sends ~290 takedown notices a day |
| Digital Detox | Karnataka Government initiative with the All India Game Developers Forum (AIGDF) on gaming and social-media use (APPSC-G2 2025 PYQ) |
- Takedown timeline: Platforms must remove flagged unlawful content within three hours of a court order or reasoned government intimation
### Fact-check bodies and counter-measures → body — Deepfakes - responses
## 1. Deepfakes - responses
- ECI advisory (mandatory labels on AI content in campaigns)
- DPDP Act penalties; I4C takedowns
- EU AI Act mandates watermarking
## 2. Other counter-measures and minors
- Counter-measures: media-literacy programmes, fact-checkers, platform policies to remove/down-rank fake content, regulation that avoids curbing free speech (ECI labelling of AI content: see Deepfakes - responses)
