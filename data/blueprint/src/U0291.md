- 8 - 10 - 11 = suggested by Swaran Singh - added in 1976 - total after 2002

### Article 51A (a) to (k) — the eleven duties
## 2. The eleven duties — every citizen shall
- 51-A(a): Abide by the Constitution; respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem — Civic
- 51-A(b): Cherish and follow the noble ideals that inspired the national struggle for freedom (noble ideals of the national struggle) — Moral
- 51-A(c): Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India — Civic
- 51-A(d): Defend the country and render national service when called upon — Civic
- 51-A(e): Promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood among all the people of India — Civic/moral
- Renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women (renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women)
- 51-A(f): Value and preserve the rich heritage of India's composite culture — Moral
- 51-A(g): Protect and improve the natural environment — forests, lakes, rivers and wild life; have compassion for living creatures — Civic
- 51-A(h): Develop scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform — Moral
- 51-A(i): Safeguard public property and abjure violence — Civic
- 51-A(j): Strive towards excellence in every sphere of individual and collective activity — so the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavour and achievement — Moral
- 51-A(k): A parent or guardian shall provide opportunities for education to their child or ward aged 6 to 14 years (six to fourteen; 6-14) — the 11th duty, 86th Amendment (2002) — Civic
- Count: 10 in 1976 → 11 now (UPSC)
- Types: moral duties (b, f, h, j) + civic duties (a, c, d, g, i) - e.g. civic duty (a), moral duty (b) (UPSC)

### Enforceability — non-justiciable nature and cases
## 3. Nature
- Non-justiciable: Cannot be enforced directly by courts; no legal sanction for violating them — moral obligations on citizens
- Non-justiciable like DPSPs - no direct constitutional/court enforcement, no legal sanction or penalty in the Constitution
- Parliament can make laws to enforce them
- Use in courts: Help in interpreting ambiguous laws and in determining the constitutionality of laws
- Reasonableness: courts may look to FDs when judging the reasonableness of a law restricting an FR
- Contrast: Fundamental Rights are justiciable and available against the State
## 2. Cases
| Case | Year | Held |
| Bijoe Emmanuel | (1986) | Limit: duties under 51A cannot override FRs in Part III; respect for national symbols needs only respectful conduct, not compelled participation (24 Aug 2026) |
| AIIMS Students' Union | 2002 | Correlative: rights and duties correlative - FDs as important as FRs |
- Counterpart of rights: freedom of speech (Art. 19(1)(a)) ↔ duty to uphold sovereignty and integrity (51A(c))
- Aim: a code of conduct for citizens, civic virtue and patriotism
- Exercise of rights: with restraint and respect for others' rights

### Enforceability & cases
## Nature
- ★ Rights are correlative with duties (every right has a corresponding duty)
- ★ Holland — others holding back is called a duty
- Taken mainly from the Constitution of the former USSR; FRs drew much from the US Constitution
- Non-justiciable — courts cannot directly enforce or punish
- Legislation may enforce them; courts may use them in interpretation
- Courts may use them to judge reasonableness of a law limiting an FR
- Called a 'code of moral precepts' — non-justiciable, no direct punishment; critics call them superfluous
- Criticisms — not exhaustive (voting, taxes missing), an appendage to Part IV, vague
- Use — remind citizens of duties, help courts on reasonableness, back action against anti-national activities
- Do not give a ground for suspending Fundamental Rights
