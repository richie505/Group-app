### Missile classes — definitions, types and terms
## 1. Ballistic vs cruise
| Ballistic missile | Cruise missile |
| Rocket-powered only in the boost phase, then follows a high parabolic path (free-fall arc outside/high in the atmosphere) | Air-breathing, powered throughout, flies low at constant speed and low altitude using aerodynamic lift |
| Examples: Agni, Prithvi, Pralay | Examples: BrahMos, Nirbhay |
## 2. Classification by speed
| Basis | Types |
| Speed | Subsonic (Nirbhay) vs supersonic (BrahMos) vs hypersonic (BrahMos-II, LR-AShM) |
## 3. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Hypersonic | Faster than Mach 5 |
| HGV (Hypersonic Glide Vehicle) | Rocket-boosted → glides at Mach 5+ and manoeuvres in upper atmosphere → hard to intercept |
| MIRV (Multiple Independently Targetable Re-entry Vehicles) | One missile, several warheads, different targets; first tested on Agni-V in Mission Divyastra (11 Mar 2024) |
| FOBS (Fractional Orbital Bombardment System) | Warhead placed in partial low orbit → comes down / approaches from an unexpected direction (any direction) |
| SFDR (solid fuel ducted ramjet) | Uses atmospheric air for combustion; demonstrated from ITR Chandipur → for long-range air-to-air missiles |
### Hypersonic technology → Indian systems and FIRSTs
- HSTDV (Hypersonic Technology Demonstrator Vehicle):
- Indigenous scramjet (supersonic combustion, no rotating parts)
- Flew at Mach 6+
- Validated scramjet combustion + thermal protection above 2,000°C
- Scramjet combustor run over 12 minutes (Jan 2026)
- Long-range hypersonic missile: India's first long-range hypersonic missile, 16 Nov 2024 (first flight-trial)
- LR-AShM: Hypersonic glide anti-ship missile for Navy coastal batteries; shown at the 77th Republic Day Parade
- Range ~1,500 km; peak Mach 10 (1,500+ km, up to Mach 10)
- Average Mach 5 via "skipping" manoeuvres
- ⚠ HSTDV = scramjet demonstrator (Mach 6+); LR-AShM = glide missile (peak Mach 10, average Mach 5)
### BrahMos → partnership, features, variants and combat use — Partnership and name
## 1. Partnership and name
- Partners: Joint venture of India and Russia (APPSC-G2 2025 key)
- Inter-Governmental Agreement was signed in Moscow in 1998 → created BrahMos Aerospace
- Ownership: DRDO (50.5%) + NPOM, Russia (49.5%) (NPO Mashinostroyeniya)
- Name: Brahmaputra + Moskva = Brahmaputra + Moskva rivers
- Exam angle: "BrahMos is developed by India and ___" (Russia). This was asked in APPSC G2 2025.
## 2. Technical features
- First successful test: 12 June 2001, Chandipur, Odisha
- Speed class: Supersonic cruise missile, Mach 2.8-3.0 (~3,400-3,700 km/h) → one of the fastest cruise missiles in the world
- Propulsion: Solid booster → liquid ramjet
- Navigation: Inertial + satellite (GPS/GLONASS); fire-and-forget from a Transport Launch Canister
- Flight profile: Cruises at ~15 km altitude → dives to 10 m in terminal phase; low radar cross-section
- Range: 290 km originally → 290-400 km in extended-range versions
### BrahMos → partnership, features, variants and combat use — Variants and launch platforms
## 1. Variants and launch platforms
- Platforms: Base BrahMos can be launched from all four platforms → land, ship, submarine, air (Su-30MKI)
| Variant | Launch platform / feature |
| Ship-launched | Warships, e.g. P-17A frigates carry BrahMos |
| Extended range | about 400+ km; range raised from 290 km to ~450 km after India joined the MTCR (2016) [GK] |
| BrahMos-NG | Lighter (about 1.5 t against 2.5 t), for Tejas, smaller warships and submarines; about Mach 3.5 |
| BrahMos-II | Planned hypersonic, Mach 5 or more (> Mach 5, up to Mach 7), under development |
## 2. Facilities and combat use
- BrahMos Integration and Testing Facility, Lucknow: Inaugurated 11 May 2025; first batch flagged off within five months
- Combat use: First reported combat use in Operation Sindoor (May 2025)
### Cruise and air-to-air missiles → Nirbhay, ITCM and Astra
## 1. Nirbhay and ITCM
- Nirbhay: India's subsonic long-range land-attack cruise missile (LR-LACM)
- Range ~1,000 km; turbojet powered
- Terrain-hugging at low altitude → evades radar
- Navigation: inertial + GPS/NavIC
- Conventional or nuclear warheads; payload ~200-300 kg
- ITCM (Indigenous Technology Cruise Missile): Nirbhay successor
- Engine: indigenous 'Manik' small turbofan engine / Manik small turbofan engine (GTRE)
- Family now called Long-Range Land Attack Cruise Missile (LR-LACM) (Jun 2026)
- ⚠ Nirbhay = subsonic, turbojet; BrahMos = supersonic, ramjet
## 2. Astra (BVRAAM)
- Class: Beyond Visual Range Air-to-Air Missile (BVRAAM) family by DRDO; active radar seeker
- BVR = engages beyond 20 nautical miles (37 km)
| Variant | Range and features (10 Jul 2026) |
| Astra Mk-1 | 80-110 km, speed Mach 4.5, altitude up to 20 km. On the Su-30MKI; planned for the Tejas Mk-1A and Rafale |
| Astra Mk-2 | about 200 km (2026; older notes say 160 km), dual-pulse solid motor |
| Astra Mk-3 'Gandiva' | Under development, SFDR (solid fuel ducted ramjet) engine, 350+ km |
- LCA test: ADA test-fired Astra from an LCA AF Mk1 prototype on 12 Mar 2025
- Export: see the exports page (Indonesia, Jul 2026)
