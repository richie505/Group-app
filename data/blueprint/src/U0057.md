### Undavalli caves — features
- Structure: Four storeys + 3 main caves
- Carved top-down: upper levels finely detailed; the ground floor is left crude and unfinished (29 Jul 2026)
- Centrepiece: Monolithic reclining Vishnu, Ananta Padmanabha Swamy, about 5 m (16 ft) long
- Earlier use: A Jain abode, then used by Buddhist monks, then made a Hindu temple (2026)
- Reclining (some notes: padmasana) Buddha image was replaced by Vishnu
- Date: 4th-5th c. CE; regional form of the Gupta rock-cut style
- Exam angle: Aug 2026 test - "built by the Cholas as a Hindu temple, later Buddhist" is false; the true statements are the Krishna-bank location and the Ananta Padmanabha statue.
### Mogalrajapuram and Bhairavakonda caves — features
## Mogalrajapuram
| Cave | Feature |
| Caves 1-3 | Sanctums behind a pillared mandapa, for the Trimurti (Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva) |
| Cave 4 | Durga cave; Ardhanarishvara painted on the back wall |
| Cave 5 (largest) | Shiva Tandava Nataraja |
- Caves: 5 caves in all
- Sculpture: Trimurti figures (some notes: three goddesses) on the kapota (eave) above pillar capitals
- Confusion: Some notes put these details under an "Akkana-Madanna caves" heading; assign them to Mogalrajapuram
## Bhairavakonda
- Caves: 8 rock-cut caves, all to Shiva (G2 2012)
- ⚠ Trap: 4, 9, 12
- Religion: Centre of Shaivism (G2 2008) - specifically the Kapalika sect
- ⚠ Trap: Buddhism, Jainism, Vaishnavism
- Attribution debate:
- Madhava Varma I (Vishnukundin) - most notes
- Pallava period (7th-8th c.) - some notes
- Close likeness to the early Pallava caves of Mahabalipuram
- ⚠ 5 caves = Mogalrajapuram; 8 caves = Bhairavakonda; 4 storeys = Undavalli
### Architecture terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Pillared mandapa (ardhamandapa) | Early hall in front of one or more rock-cut garbhagriha cells (Plan); Mogalrajapuram shrines sit behind the mandapa |
| Kumbha (pot) capitals | Bhairavakonda = lion-based pillars with kumbha (pot) capitals |
| Kapota | Decorated eave above the capitals at Mogalrajapuram |
| Dwarapalas | Door guardians flanking shrine entrances, as in contemporary Pallava caves |
- Style: Blend of Ikshvaku and Vakataka traditions + borrowings from the Pallavas
- Structural temples: Brick, with garbhagriha, antarala-mandapa, mukhamandapa (Keesaragutta)
### Vishnukundin vs Pallava (Mahendravarman) caves
| Point | Vishnukundin caves | Pallava (Mahendravarman I) caves |
| Date | 4th-6th c. (Undavalli 4th-5th c.) | 7th c. |
| Plan | Mandapa + garbhagriha; storeyed at Undavalli | Mandapa + garbhagriha introduced |
| Pillars | Lion pedestals, pot capitals (Bhairavakonda) | Lion-based (simha-mukha) pillars |
| Influence | Gupta-Vakataka base | Later taken up by the Telugu Chodas and Eastern Chalukyas |
- Pallava influence: Undavalli shows Pallava influence; Vishnukundins "imitated the Pallava style"
