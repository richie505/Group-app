### Qutbuddin Aibak and Iltutmish → career and contributions
## 1. Qutbuddin Aibak (1206-1210)
- Early life: Born in Turkestan; sold first to the Qazi of Nishapur
- Then slave of Muhammad Ghori (APPSC G1 2004)
- Rose to Master of Stables (Amir-i-Akhur) → general
- Rise: After Tarain II (1192) = Ghori's representative in India
- Sultan on Ghori's death; ruled 1206-1210
- Capital: Lahore
- Death: 1210, fell from horse playing chaugan (polo)
## 2. Iltutmish (1210-36)
- Capital: Moved from Lahore to Delhi
- Legitimacy: Investiture ("mansur") from Abbasid Caliph of Baghdad, 1229
- Institutions: Turkan-i-Chahalgani (the Forty / Chalisa); Iqta system (see terms page)
- Mongols: Refused shelter to Jalaluddin Mangbarni of Khwarazm → kept Chengiz Khan out of India
- ⚠ Aibak's capital = Lahore; Iltutmish moved it to Delhi
### Razia, Nasiruddin Mahmud and Balban → career and contributions
## 1. Razia Sultan (1236-1240)
- Reign: 1236-1240 → first and only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate
- Conduct: Discarded purdah, wore male attire, led the army
- Opposition:
- Turkish nobles resented her favour to Yaqut (Abyssinian)
- 1240: Altuniya, governor of Bhatinda (Sirhind), revolted → Yaqut killed, Razia imprisoned
- End:
- Razia married Altuniya, marched on Delhi → defeated and killed (1240)
## 2. Nasiruddin Mahmud (1246-66)
- Balban's role: Principal minister (naib) + leader of the Forty
- Briefly displaced by Imaduddin Raihan → returned
## 3. Balban (1266-86)
- Early life: Born c. 1205 as Bahauddin
- Captured by Mongols → bought by Iltutmish
- Water-carrier → member of the Forty
- Razia made him Amir-i-Shikar; title Ulugh Khan
- Blood and iron policy:
- Broke the power of the Forty by promoting junior Turks
- Army:
- Reorganised; created military department Diwan-i-Arz
- Outposts + forts vs the Mongols
- ⚠ Iltutmish created the Forty; Balban broke its power

### End of the dynasty and rise of the Khaljis
- After Balban (d. 1286):
- Grandson Kaiqubad → murdered (body thrown into the Yamuna)
- Then infant Kaimurs
- Khalji takeover: Jalaluddin Firoz Khalji crowned himself at Kilokhari in 1290 → end of Mamluk rule
### Universal angles
- Significance: First Muslim dynasty to rule north India from Delhi
- Core institutions: iqta, the Forty, tanka/jital, Diwan-i-Arz
- FIRST/LAST:
- First mosque in Delhi (first major Sultanate mosque) = Quwwat-ul-Islam
- First Caliphal investiture = Iltutmish, 1229

## Mamluk rulers
- Mamluk rulers include Ruknuddin Firuz, Alauddin Masud, Nasiruddin Mahmud; Jalaluddin Khalji was a Khalji, not a Mamluk
- Qutbuddin Aibak — title Lakh Bakhsh = "giver of lakhs"; capital at Lahore
- Iltutmish — shifted capital from Lahore to Delhi
- Nasiruddin Mahmud (1246–66) — immediately preceded Balban (1266–86)
- Under Nasiruddin Mahmud, Balban was principal minister / naib and leader of the Forty; briefly removed by Imaduddin Raihan
- Balban broke the power of the Forty by promoting junior Turks
- Jalaluddin Firoz Khalji crowned himself at Kilokhari (1290) — end of Mamluk rule, start of Khalji dynasty
### Key rulers
- Aibak — rose to Amir-i-Akhur (Master of Stables), then general
- Iltutmish refused shelter to Jalaluddin Mangbarni of Khwarazm — kept Chengiz Khan out of India
- Razia — discarded purdah, wore male attire, personally led the army
- Turkish nobles disliked Razia's favour to Yaqut, an Abyssinian; led to revolt under Altuniya
- Balban's rise — captured by Mongols, bought by Iltutmish → water-carrier → member of the Forty → Amir-i-Shikar (made by Razia)
- Balban created the military department Diwan-i-Arz; built outposts and forts against the Mongols
### Terms
- ★ Balban brought in Persian festival Nawruz in India; his court copied Iranian kings
- Balban — Sultan as Zil-i-Ilahi (shadow of God); required sijda (greeting lying on the ground) and paibos (kissing the feet)
- Mamluk — Arabic, "owned"; an owned military slave (not a domestic slave)
- Turkan-i-Chahalgani (the Forty) — Turkish military elite, not Persian revenue clerks
### Monuments
- Qutb Minar — begun by Aibak, completed by Iltutmish
- Qutb Minar dedicated to Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki
- Sultan Ghari tomb (Iltutmish) — Iltutmish called Father of tomb building
### Exam corner
- End of Mamluk rule: Balban (d. 1286) → grandson Kaiqubad (murdered) → infant Kaimurs → Jalaluddin Firoz Khalji (1290)
### Qutbuddin Aibak and Iltutmish → career and contributions
- ★ Aibak died 1210 at Lahore after a fall from his horse while playing chaugan (polo)
- ★ Aibak buried in Lahore near Anarkali Bazaar; founder of the Slave Dynasty
- ★ Iltutmish — first Sultan to bring in regular coins and make Delhi capital
### Razia, Nasiruddin Mahmud and Balban → career and contributions
- ★ Balban (1266–1286) created Diwan-i-Arz (military department), separating it from Diwan-i-Wizarat (finance)
- ★ Polo brought to India by the Turks; Aibak fond of polo (chaugan)
