### Population policy — milestones and FIRSTs
- First country: India = first country in the world to launch a national family planning programme, in 1952 (APPSC-GS key)
| Phase | Approach |
| 1950s (1st-2nd Plans) | Clinic-based approach |
| 1976 | First National Population Policy (first comprehensive policy, 16 April; marriage age raised to 21/18); 42nd Amendment put population control in the Concurrent List; coercive sterilisation during the 1976-77 Emergency |
| 1977 | Renamed the Family Welfare Programme after the Emergency's forced sterilisations |
| 1996-97 | Target-free approach (1996), then Reproductive and Child Health (RCH) from 1997 |
| 2000 | NPP 2000: voluntary, informed choice, target-free; declared 15 February 2000 |
- ⚠ 1952 = first programme; 1976 = first policy; 1977 = renamed Family Welfare; 2000 = NPP
### NPP 2000 — objectives, targets, bodies and shortcomings
## 1. Basics
- Approach: Target-free, voluntary, informed choice; promotes the two-child norm
- Delivery: Government + industry + NGOs
## 2. Objectives and stabilisation target
| Objective | Content / target |
| Immediate | Meet the unmet need for contraception, health infrastructure and personnel, integrated reproductive and child health services |
| Medium-term | Bring TFR to replacement level (2.1) by 2010 |
| Long-term | Stable population by 2045 (UKPSC 2024 key; some notes say 2046) |
- Stabilisation year: 2045
- ⚠ Trap: some notes say 2046 - WRONG; the policy says 2045 (PYQ keys)
- Revised: population stabilisation target later pushed from 2045 to 2070 (UPPCS 2014, 2023 keys).
## 3. Population bodies and reports
| Body / report | Fact |
| National Commission on Population | Set up 2000 under NPP 2000; chaired by the Prime Minister; members include Chief Ministers + Union ministers |
| State of World Population report | Published by UNFPA |
| NFHS | MoHFW, through IIPS Mumbai |
## 4. Shortcomings
- TFR 2.1: Reached only around NFHS-5 (2019-21), not by 2010

### Amendments and Articles → seat freeze and delimitation
| Amendment / Article | Year | Provision |
| 42nd Amendment | 1976 | Froze Lok Sabha + Assembly seats on the 1971 Census until 2000 |
| 84th Amendment | 2001 | Extended freeze until the first Census after 2026 - motivational measure under NPP strategy; boundary readjustment on 1991 Census |
| 87th Amendment | 2003 | Boundary readjustment on 2001 Census |
| Art. 82 and 170 | - | Seats allotted on population |
- Boundary readjustment = no change in seat numbers
- Delimitation fear: Southern States could lose 8-10 Lok Sabha seats
### Migration — streams, reasons and theorists
- Census measures: place of birth + place of last residence
- 141.2 million moved during 2001-2011
- Inter-state female migration (last residence, 2011): Urban→Urban > Rural→Urban > Rural→Rural > Urban→Rural (JPSC 2023 key)
- Largest stream overall: Rural→rural, driven by marriage
- Reasons: marriage for women; work for men; Gulf migration (UPSC)
| Theory | Proponent |
| Laws of migration (1885) | E.G. Ravenstein[GK] |
| Push-pull / intervening obstacles (1966) | Everett Lee[GK] |
| Expected-income rural-urban model | Michael Todaro[GK] |
### Ageing — data, policy and Acts
## 1. Elderly population
- Share: 9.7% of population (SRS 2024; Kerala 15%)
- Projection: About 100 mn (2011) to 230 mn (2036) = about 1 in 7 Indians
- Growth impact: Ageing could reduce GDP growth by 70 basis points a year over 2025-50 (IMF)
- Pension gap: Nearly 70% of elderly women lack formal pension vs about 40% of elderly men
## 2. Policy and law
| Policy / Act / body | Year | Provision |
| National Policy on Older Persons 1999 (Ministry of Social Justice) | 1999 | 14 areas of intervention, incl. health care + nutrition |
| Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007 | 2007 | Maintenance = legal duty of children and relatives |
| NPHCE | Launched 2010-11 | 60+ health care |
| NCOP (National Council for Senior Citizens) | - | Highest advisory body on elderly policy |
### AP — Population Management Policy 2026
- Draft policy: Population Management Policy (Mar 2026) - shift from control to management
- ₹25,000 incentive for a second or third child
- AP two-child norm: Draft policy removes the two-child norm for local body elections
- Five pillars:
| Pillar | Meaning |
| Matrutva | Maternal health |
| Shakti | Women's work participation |
| Kshema | Elderly care |
| Naipunyam | Skills - State plans a skill drive |
| Sanjeevani | Digital health |
- "Shrinconomics" warning: Health Minister (Satya Kumar Yadav) warned of "shrinconomics"; AP TFR = 1.50
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: NFHS-6 released 29 May 2026; TFR steady at 2.0; CPR 69.1%
- Criticism & debates:
- Coercion vs choice (1976-77 vs NPP 2000)
- Southern pro-natalist turn vs delimitation + Finance Commission population weights (15-25% of devolution criteria)

## NPP 2000
- ★ New National Population Policy adopted 2000; declared on 15 February 2000
- Aimed at TFR replacement level by 2010 and stable population by 2045 (later modified to 2070)
- Approach — target-free, voluntary and informed choice, delivery through government, industry and NGOs
