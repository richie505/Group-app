# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:104 - Deficit financing - meaning, methods and consequences
### Meaning: Indian vs Western usage
- Creation of new money = the most inflationary way to finance a deficit
| Usage | Meaning |
| Indian | Gap met by borrowing from the RBI → RBI creates new money |
| Western | Any deficit financing, mainly borrowing from public/market |
| Effect | Increase in money supply (UPPSC 2016) |
### Purpose & phases
- Purpose: resources for economic development (APPSC 2019); "pragmatic tool" for Five Year Plans
- Needed after 1947: low tax base, weak banking, low domestic savings
- Up to late 1960s: limited use to fund plans
- 1970s onwards: heavy reliance (oil shocks, welfare) → debt, inflation
### Curbs on deficit financing
- Sep 1994 — RBI-Govt agreement: phase out ad hoc T-bills
- 1 Apr 1997 — Ad hoc T-bills ended → WMA (temporary RBI advances)
- 2003 — FRBM Act (APPSC 2019)
- 1 Apr 2006 — RBI not to subscribe to primary issues of G-Secs
- 2018 — Monetised borrowing only under escape clause; cap 0.5% of GDP
### Mode → inflationary effect
| Mode of financing | Inflationary effect |
| Creation of new money | Most inflationary (UPSC 2021) |
| Borrowing from banks | High |
| Borrowing from the public | Lower (existing savings) |
| Repayment of public debt | Not inflationary |
### Consequences
- Inflation
- Demand-pull; hurts the poor most
- Crowding out
- Borrowing absorbs loanable funds
- Interest rates ↑ → private investment ↓
- Debt trap
- Borrowing to pay interest
- Rating downgrades
- Higher borrowing cost for govt + firms
### Current data
- Interest = 20 paise of every rupee spent; ~40% of revenue receipts
- RBI OMO sales of ₹1 lakh crore + 91/182/364-day T-bill auctions (15 Sep 2026)
- AP: market loans 75% of debt; FD 2024-25 5.05% vs 4% ceiling
### Exam corner
- Deficit financing in India = borrowing from RBI (APPSC 2019)
- Fiscal deficit = budgetary deficit + net increase in borrowings
- Liquidity tool now: OMOs (sale absorbs, purchase injects)
- Ranking: new money > banks > public > debt repayment
### Deficit financing — meaning, purpose and plan phases
## 1. Meaning
| Term | Meaning |

### Acts and agreements controlling deficit financing
| Agreement / Act | Year | Provision |
| RBI-Government agreement | September 1994 | Began phasing out ad hoc T-bills |
| RBI-Government agreement | March 1997 | Ended them → WMA from 1 April 1997 |
| FRBM Act, 2003 (APPSC 2019) | 2003 | RBI not to subscribe to primary issues of central government securities from 1 April 2006 → bars RBI from financing the Centre in normal times |
| 2018 amendment | 2018 | Permits it only on escape-clause grounds → monetised borrowing only under escape clause |
- Escape-clause cap: Deviation capped at 0.5% of GDP a year
- Liquidity tool now: OMOs → sale of G-Secs absorbs liquidity; purchase injects it

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:104
### Meaning: Indian vs Western usage
- ★ Deficit financing — inflationary by nature (raises money supply)
- ★ Most inflationary — creation of new money to finance a budget deficit (monetising the deficit)
- Indian usage — borrowing from the RBI, which creates new money
- Western usage — any financing of a deficit
### Purpose & phases
- Used mainly to finance the Five Year Plans (resources for development)
- Used little until the late 1960s; more from the 1970s
### Curbs on deficit financing
- FRBM escape clause — slip limited to 0.5% of GDP a year
- Since 2018 — monetised borrowing allowed only on escape-clause grounds
- Ad hoc T-bills phase-out began September 1994; March 1997 agreement ended them
- Ways and Means Advances — from 1 April 1997
### Mode → inflationary effect
- Need after Independence — low tax base, poor compliance, weak private banking, low domestic savings (not a high savings rate)
- Inflationary order — creation of new money (most) > bank borrowing > public borrowing
- Debt repayment — withdraws money
### Consequences
- Crowding out — heavy market borrowing raises interest rates, cuts private investment
- Debt trap — borrowing to pay interest
- Demand-pull inflation — money supply and demand rise without matching rise in goods and services; burden falls most on the poor
### Current data
- AP fiscal deficit 2024–25 — 5.05% (above the 4% limit)
- Market loans — 75% of AP's debt
### Exam corner
- OMOs — sale of G-Secs drains liquidity; purchase adds liquidity
- RBI wants shift from subsidies to capital expenditure
### Deficit financing — meaning, purpose and plan phases
- ★ In India, deficit financing raises resources for economic development
- ★ Most inflationary — creating new money to finance a budget deficit (monetisation)
- ★ Creates extra paper currency; if it fails, causes inflation
- ★ Fiscal deficit (Union budget) — budgetary deficit + net increase in internal borrowings and other liabilities
