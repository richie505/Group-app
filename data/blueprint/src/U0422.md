- Idealism → realism; non-alignment → multi-alignment; the constant is strategic autonomy

### Phases of foreign policy → label, period and features
| Phase | Label | Features |
| Phase 1 (1946-62) | "Optimistic non-alignment" | Idealism + Panchsheel + NAM + Commonwealth membership + anti-imperialism |
| Phase 2 (1962-71) | "Realism and recovery" | Defence build-up; 1965 war + Tashkent (1966); refusal to sign the NPT (1968) |
| Third phase (1971-1991) | "regional assertion" (Jaishankar) | India the leading power in South Asia |
| Phase 4 (1991-98) | "Safeguarding strategic autonomy" | After the USSR's collapse + the 1991 economic crisis |
| Jaishankar's phase 5 (1998-2013) | India as a "balancing power" | Pokhran-II, NFU, Indo-US nuclear deal |
| Phase 6 (2014 onwards) | "Energetic engagement" | Multi-alignment |
- ⚠ Order of labels: optimistic non-alignment → realism and recovery → regional assertion → safeguarding strategic autonomy → balancing power → energetic engagement

- LPG reforms → attracting FDI and markets became a foreign-policy goal

- US: security and technology (Quad)

### Treaties, wars and events, 1946-1991 → year
## 1. Nehruvian years
- Korean War: India chaired the Neutral Nations Repatriation Commission (Gen. K.S. Thimayya) for prisoners of war
## 2. 1971 and its aftermath
- Indo-Soviet Treaty of Peace, Friendship and Cooperation: 9 August 1971 (20 years)
- Purpose: counter the US-China-Pakistan axis during the Bangladesh crisis
- Bangladesh Liberation War, December 1971: East Pakistan broke away → Bangladesh born (APPSC 2018; MPPSC 1996)
- Pakistan's army surrendered in Dhaka on 16 December 1971
## 3. Nuclear and regional moves
| Event | Year | Fact |
| Simla Agreement | 1972 | Bilateralism, no third-party role |
| Pokhran-I | 18 May 1974 | Called a "peaceful nuclear explosion"; drew international concern + tighter global nuclear controls |
| Katchatheevu ceded to Sri Lanka | 1974 | — |
| Farakka Accord | 1977 | Ganga water sharing with Bangladesh |
| IPKF in Sri Lanka | 1987-90 | Under the India-Sri Lanka Accord, 1987 (Rajiv Gandhi-Jayewardene) |
- Other events:
- Rajiv Gandhi's technology push
- Indira Gandhi assassinated 1984; Rajiv Gandhi 1991
- ⚠ 9 Aug 1971 = Indo-Soviet Treaty; 16 Dec 1971 = Dhaka surrender; 18 May 1974 = Pokhran-I
### Post-Cold War and post-1998 events → year
## 1. 1991-98
- Look East Policy: Launched 1991-92 under P.V. Narasimha Rao; moved India closer to ASEAN
- Gujral Doctrine (1996): Goodwill to neighbours without asking for anything in return
## 2. Pokhran-II and fallout
- Status: India declared itself a nuclear-weapon state
- No First Use: Announced by the Vajpayee government (UKPSC 2021); formal doctrine 2003
- Sanctions: US (Glenn Amendment), Japan and others
- Followed by Jaswant Singh-Strobe Talbott talks
## 3. India-Pakistan
- Lahore Declaration, February 1999: Came with Delhi-Lahore bus diplomacy
- Kargil War, 1999: First conflict after both had tested nuclear weapons; US pressure forced Pakistan to withdraw
## 4. Indo-US civil nuclear deal
| Step | Date |
| Hyde Act | 2006 |
| NSG waiver | 6 September 2008 |
| 123 Agreement signed | October 2008 |

### Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Strategic autonomy | Freedom to judge each issue on its merits without joining military blocs; not "neutrality" |
| Multi-alignment | Engaging rival powers at once (Quad + BRICS/SCO) to maximise national interest; BRICS New Delhi Declaration 2026 described as "multi-vector engagement" |
| Issue-based coalitions | Partnerships chosen issue by issue (security with US; energy and defence with Russia), not fixed alliances |
| Global South voice | India as champion of developing countries in reformed multilateralism, climate finance and DPI |
