# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:68 - Tripartite struggle for Kanauj - Palas, Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas; causes and outcome
### Why Kannauj?
- After Harsha, Kannauj = symbol of imperial sovereignty: whoever held it claimed to be Samrat of North India
- Old names: Mahodaya (UPPSC 2012), Mahodaya Shri (UPPSC 2015), Kanyakubja
- Political: title of Samrat of North India
- Economic: Gangetic-Doab agrarian wealth
- Arabian Sea trade via Gujarat, bypassing Arab-held Indus delta
### The three contenders
| Dynasty | Base | Rulers |
| Gurjara-Pratiharas | Rajasthan-Gujarat / Avanti | Vatsaraja, Nagabhata II, Mihira Bhoja, Mahendrapala I |
| Palas | Bengal, Bihar | Dharmapala, Devapala, Mahipala I |
| Rashtrakutas | Deccan, Manyakheta | Dhruva, Govinda III, Amoghavarsha, Indra III |
### Phases
- c. 780-800 — Vatsaraja takes Kannauj, beats Dharmapala; Dhruva defeats both, withdraws
- Early 9th c. — Dharmapala removes Indrayudha, installs puppet Chakrayudha
- Phase III — Nagabhata II ousts Chakrayudha; Govinda III defeats both, retreats
- Late 9th c. — Mihira Bhoja, Mahendrapala I hold Kannauj
- c. 916 — Indra III sacks Kannauj (defeats Mahipala I)
### Inscriptions
| Source | Dynasty | Tells us |
| Sanjan plates | Rashtrakuta | Govinda III's Himalayan campaign |
| Gwalior prashasti | Pratihara | Nagabhata II beat Chakrayudha, Dharmapala |
| Khalimpur plates | Pala | Election of Gopala; Chakrayudha installed |
### Outcome
- 1. Rashtrakuta raids: loot, prestige, no lasting territory ↓
- 2. Pratiharas finally held Kannauj ↓
- 3. All three exhausted ↓
- 4. Pratihara decline after Indra III's raid ↓
- 5. New Rajput states: Chandelas, Chauhans
### Exam corner
- Three = Pratiharas, Palas, Rashtrakutas (APPSC G2 2003)
- Not part: Cholas (UPPSC 2015), Pallavas, Chauhans
- UPSC 2020 order: Mahendravarman I → Gopala → Bhoja → Parantaka I
- Dhruva beat Vatsaraja; Govinda III beat Nagabhata II
- Duration: 8th-9th c. (⚠ other notes: 8th-10th c.)
- AP: Dhruva defeated Vengi's Vishnuvardhana IV
### Kannauj → ancient names and causes of the struggle
- After Harsha, Kannauj became a centre of attraction for competing powers — also known as Mahodaya and Mahodayashri

| Mahodaya | UPPSC 2012 |
| Mahodaya Shri / "Nagar Mahodaya Shri" | UPPSC 2015 |
- Political value: After Harsha = symbol of imperial sovereignty → title of Samrat of North India
- Economic value:
- Gangetic-Doab agrarian wealth
- Access to Arabian Sea trade via Gujarat, bypassing Arab-held Indus delta
### Contending dynasties → base, rulers, distractors
- Objective: Control of Kannauj — 8th–9th century CE
- Participants: The Pala, Gurjara-Pratihara and Rashtrakuta dynasties
| Dynasty | Base | Rulers in the struggle |
| Gurjara-Pratiharas | Rajasthan-Gujarat / Avanti, later Kannauj | Vatsaraja, Nagabhata II, Mihira Bhoja, Mahendrapala I |
| Palas | Gauda (Bengal) and Magadha (Bihar) | Dharmapala, Devapala, Mahipala I |
| Rashtrakutas | Deccan, capital Manyakheta (Malkhed) | Dhruva, Govinda III, Amoghavarsha, Indra III |
- The three: Pratiharas, Palas, Rashtrakutas (APPSC G2 2003)
- Not part of it: Cholas (UPPSC 2015), Pallavas, Kalyani Chalukyas, Chauhans, Gahadavalas
- Chronology (UPSC 2020): Pallava Mahendravarman I → Pala Gopala → Pratihara Bhoja → Chola Parantaka I
### Phases → Kannauj rulers installed and removed
- Beginning: Vatsaraja (c. 780-800 CE) clashed with Dharmapala and Dhruva (UPSC)
| Phase | Events |
| I (late 8th c.) | Vatsaraja takes Kannauj, defeats Dharmapala; Dhruva defeats both and withdraws |
| II (early 9th c.) | Dharmapala removes Pratihara nominee Indrayudha, installs puppet Chakrayudha; grand durbar at Kannauj |
| III | Nagabhata II ousts Chakrayudha, beats Dharmapala; Govinda III defeats both, then retreats |
| IV (late 9th c.) | Mihira Bhoja and Mahendrapala I hold Kannauj for decades; empire Himalayas to Narmada |
- ⚠ Indrayudha = Pratihara nominee removed; Chakrayudha = Dharmapala's puppet installed
### Rashtrakuta rulers → northern campaigns (defeated whom)
| Rashtrakuta ruler | Defeated | Result |
| Dhruva (780-793 CE) | Vatsaraja and Dharmapala | Marched north; took booty, returned south; APPSC GS 2023: Dhruva "jumped into the fight" |
| Govinda III | Nagabhata II (and Dharmapala) | Himalayan campaign, then retreat |
| Indra III | Pratihara Mahipala I | Sacked Kannauj c. 916 CE |
- Sanjan plates (on Govinda III, not Dhruva): "his horses drank the icy waters of Himalayan streams, his elephants tasted the Ganga"
- ⚠ Trap: some UPSC notes say Dhruva beat Nagabhata II / that Nagabhata I was defeated by Dhruva; Dhruva's opponent was Vatsaraja, and Govinda III's was Nagabhata II
### Inscriptions → dynasty and content
- Khalimpur Inscription: Dharmapala — mentions the selection or election of Gopala to end Matsya Nyaya (the Law of the Jungle)
- Dharmapala: The title Param Saugat — Buddhist affiliation
- Sanjan and Radhanpur Copper Plates: Govinda III (Rashtrakuta dynasty) — his victory over Nagabhata II; he defeated the Gurjara-Pratihara dynasty
| Source | Dynasty | Tells us |
| Sanjan plates (Amoghavarsha I, 871 CE [GK]) | Rashtrakuta | Govinda III's Himalayan campaign |
| Gwalior prashasti (Mihira Bhoja) [GK] | Pratihara | Nagabhata II's victories over Chakrayudha and Dharmapala |
| Khalimpur plates (Dharmapala) [GK] | Pala | Chakrayudha installed at Kannauj with approval of northern kings |
- ⚠ Sanjan = Rashtrakuta; Gwalior = Pratihara; Khalimpur = Pala
### Outcome → winner, duration, effects
- Winner: Pratiharas finally held Kannauj (Bhoja, Mahendrapala I); no power held it permanently for long
- Duration: The struggle for Kannauj ran in the 8th–9th century CE
- Effects: All three exhausted; Pratihara decline after Indra III's raid → new Rajput states (Chandelas, Chauhans)
### Universal angles
- Significance: Shift of power to regional kingdoms in early medieval India
- AP angle: Rashtrakuta pressure on the Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi ran in parallel (Dhruva defeated Vishnuvardhana IV)
- Comparative note: Kannauj's primacy began under Harsha (Hiuen Tsang: Pataliputra lost place to Kannauj)
# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:68
### Why Kannauj?
- After Harsha, Kannauj = symbol of imperial sovereignty; title of Samrat of North India
- Controlled the Doab's farm wealth and Arabian Sea trade via Gujarat, avoiding the Arab-held Indus delta
- Significance: shows the shift of power to regional kingdoms in early medieval India
### The three contenders
- Rashtrakuta capital: Manyakheta (Malkhed) in the Deccan; the Pratiharas later held Kannauj
- Struggle began (c. 780–800 CE) when Pratihara Vatsaraja clashed with Dharmapala and Rashtrakuta Dhruva
- Dhruva defeated Vatsaraja and Dharmapala, took loot and returned south without holding northern land
### Phases
- Dharmapala removed the Pratihara choice Indrayudha and put Chakrayudha as puppet at Kannauj (second phase)
- Nagabhata II later removed Chakrayudha
- Rashtrakuta Indra III looted Kannauj c. 916 CE after defeating Pratihara Mahipala I
### Inscriptions

- Nagabhata II was a Gurjara-Pratihara king, not a Pala
## Records

- Gwalior prashasti of Mihira Bhoja — Nagabhata II's victories over Chakrayudha and Dharmapala
- Khalimpur inscription — records Gopala's selection
- Matsya Nyaya = law of the jungle
- Dharmapala founded Vikramashila; Param Saugat = a devout Buddhist
### Outcome
- ★ The three contenders: Pala, Gurjara-Pratihara and Rashtrakuta (not the Cholas)
- ★ Kannauj compared with Magadha of the Gupta period as a prize after Harsha
- ★ Kings and dynasties (only two of the four pairs correct):

- Kannauj finally held by the Pratiharas under Bhoja and Mahendrapala I (no power kept it long)
- Tiring of all three + Pratihara decline after Indra III's raid → rise of Rajput states: Chandelas, Chauhans
