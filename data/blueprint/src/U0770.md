# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 5:80 - Sustainable development - meaning, nature, scope and components
### Three pillars
- Use resources today without compromising future generations' ability to meet their needs
- 1. Economic development
- 2. Social equity
- 3. Environmental protection
### Meaning & scope
- First definition: Brundtland Report (Our Common Future), 1987
- Includes: present needs + equity across generations + "leave no one behind"
- Excludes: growth that runs down natural capital
- Triple bottom line: People, Planet, Profit
- ⚠ Political, military, cultural are not classic pillars
- National Environment Policy 2006: conservation and livelihood linked, not opposed
### Principles
| Principle | Meaning |
| Precautionary | No full certainty ≠ reason to delay prevention |
| Polluter pays | Polluter bears cost of control |
| CBDR | Developed nations carry more |
| Carrying capacity | Use within what ecosystems support |
### Summits
- 1972 — Stockholm (UNCHE): first; origin of UNEP
- 1992 — Rio Earth Summit (UNCED): Agenda 21, Rio Declaration (27 principles), UNFCCC, CBD
- 1997 — Rio+5, New York: review
- 2002 — Rio+10 (WSSD), Johannesburg
- 2012 — Rio+20 (UNCSD): "The Future We Want"
- 2015 — 2030 Agenda: 17 SDGs, 169 targets, 232 indicators
### Measures
| Measure | Meaning |
| Green GDP | GDP minus resource depletion & damage |
| GPI | Adds unpaid work; subtracts pollution, crime |
| Ecological footprint | Land & sea needed, in global hectares |
| Earth Overshoot Day | Demand exceeds Earth's regeneration |
### Cases & AP
- Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum (1996): precautionary + polluter pays in Indian law
- M.C. Mehta (1996): polluter pays
- India 99th of 167 in SDR 2025 (UN SDSN): first time in top 100
- Swarna Andhra @2047: renewables ~30% → >75%; Ten Sutras
### Exam corner
- Odd one out as a goal: maximising growth at any cost
- 17 - 169 - 232 = SDGs - targets - indicators
- First Earth Summit = Rio 1992; Rio+5 in New York

### Sustainable development — scope and National Environment Policy 2006
## 1. National Environment Policy, 2006
- The policy framework for environmental conservation and sustainable development
- Wetlands (Conservation & Management) Rules, 2010: Framed on the recommendation of an Expert Panel — constituted under the National Environment Policy, 2006
- National Environment Policy 2006: Conservation and livelihood security are linked, not opposed
## 2. Scope - includes and excludes
- Includes: Meeting present needs (development not rejected) + equity within and across generations + inclusion ("leave no one behind")
- Excludes: Growth that runs down natural capital; treating environment and development as opposites
- Assertion-reason logic: Present generation may use resources, but not in a way that compromises future generations' ability to meet their needs
### Sustainable development — three pillars
## 1. Three pillars
- Three interlinked pillars:
- Economic development
- Social equity
- Environmental protection
- Alternate phrasing (2024-25): economic growth, social inclusion, environmental protection
- Triple bottom line: People, Planet, Profit = business version of the three pillars
- ⚠ Trap (odd-one-out): Political, military or cultural dimensions are not among the three classic pillars
### Principles → meaning
| Principle | Meaning |
| Intergenerational equity | The present must not deplete resources at the cost of future generations |
| Precautionary principle | Lack of full scientific certainty is no reason to postpone cost-effective preventive measures |
| Polluter pays | The polluter bears the cost of preventing and controlling pollution |
| CBDR | Shared but differentiated duties; developed nations carry more |
| Carrying capacity | Human use must stay within what ecosystems can support (the finite carrying capacity of Earth) |
- Endorsement: Principles endorsed at Rio 1992; underpin international environmental law
- ⚠ Trap (odd-one-out as a "goal"): Maximising economic growth at any cost

### Meaning & scope
## Natural capital
- ★ Natural capital — renewable and non-renewable resources created by nature: air, water, land, minerals, forests
- ★ Roads — not natural capital (man-made)
## Scope of sustainable development
- Meets present needs with equity within and across generations, plus inclusion
- Rejects using up natural capital and setting environment against development
- Three classic pillars: economic development, social equity, environmental protection (military security is not one)
- Triple bottom line — People, Planet, Profit (social, environmental, economic)
- Our Common Future (Brundtland Report) — 1987
- Order: Stockholm Declaration 1972 → Our Common Future 1987 → Rio Declaration 1992 → Johannesburg Declaration 2002
## National Environment Policy, 2006 and NGT
- NEP 2006 — conservation and livelihood security are linked objectives
- Wetlands (Conservation & Management) Rules, 2010 — from an Expert Panel under the NEP 2006
- NGT (HQ New Delhi) — benches at Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata, Chennai; no bench at Hyderabad
### Principles
- Precautionary principle — lack of full scientific certainty is no reason to postpone cost-effective measures against environmental harm
- CBDR (Common but Differentiated Responsibilities) — shared responsibility, developed nations carry greater duties (capacity, historical contribution)
- NGT must apply: sustainable development, precautionary principle, polluter-pays principle
