### Land reforms — phases, types and FIRSTs
## 1. Phases
- Phase I (1947-70) → Phase II (1970 onwards)
## 2. Types (measures)
| Measure | Key fact |
| Abolition of intermediaries | most successful reform; about 2 crore tenants brought into direct contact with the State; covered about 40% of cultivated area |
| Tenancy reform | rent capped at 1/4 to 1/6 of produce; defeated by "voluntary surrenders"; exception: Operation Barga (1977), West Bengal |
| Ceiling | two rounds (1950s, early 1970s); family made the unit from 1972; benami transfers |
| Consolidation | succeeded in Punjab, Haryana, western UP |
- Raj Krishna's classification: liberative, distributive, organisational and developmental
- Components included: abolition of intermediaries, tenancy reform, ceiling, consolidation, cooperative farming, computerisation of land records
## 3. FIRSTs

### Land reform measures
## Background
- Prakasam Committee (1938) — advised abolition of intermediaries
- Kumarappa Committee (1949) — set the land reform agenda; Phase I of land reform 1947–70
- First Constitutional Amendment — protected zamindari abolition measures from legal challenge
## Measures and results
- Abolition of intermediaries — most successful reform; brought two crore tenants into direct touch with the State
- Telangana: Jagir and Sarfekhas lands became Diwani (ryotwari) lands
- 1967 extension of the Andhra Inams Act to Telangana — struck down by the High Court
- Operation Barga (1977) — West Bengal (not Kerala); exception to failed tenancy reform
- Ceiling guidelines 1972 — unit = the family, not the individual
- Land consolidation — success in Punjab, Haryana, western UP
- Benami transfers — land in others' names to escape ceilings; oral leases and exemptions also weakened reform
- Failure on ceilings and tenancy — helped cause the Naxalbari revolt (1967)
## Andhra Pradesh
- AP (Andhra Area) Tenancy Act, 1956 — written lease deeds; minimum lease six years; in force 1961
- AP Land Reforms (Ceiling) Act — 1973, after Land Reforms Bill under CM P.V. Narasimha Rao; ceiling 10–27 acres wet or 35–54 acres dry per family unit
- First residential school (1971) — Sarvail, Nalgonda, under P.V. Narasimha Rao
- Rs 2 per kg rice scheme — N.T. Rama Rao, 1983 (with subsidised housing, Janata cloth)
- Planned AP New Tenancy Act, 2024 — tenants get rights over the crop (not land), entitlement cards, crop loans
