### Antibiotics and biofilms — discovery, sources, resistance
## 1. Antibiotics — discovery and nature
- Alexander Fleming: Discovered penicillin in 1928 from Penicillium notatum
- Main sources: Fungi and bacteria — Streptomyces and Bacillus are important bacterial sources
- Mostly natural or semi-synthetic — some are fully synthetic
- Act against bacteria only; chloramphenicol is broad-spectrum
- Selman Waksman: Coined the term antibiotic in 1942
## 2. Antibiotic sources and uses
| Antibiotic | Produced by | Used against |
| Penicillin | Penicillium | Bacterial infections |
| Streptomycin | Streptomyces | Tuberculosis |
| Tetracycline | — | Bacterial infections |
| Chloramphenicol | — | Typhoid; bacterial infections |
| Several antibiotics | Bacillus | — |
- Microorganisms generally produce antibiotics
## 3. Biofilms
- Biofilm: A microbial community embedded in a self-produced matrix — the EPS matrix (extracellular polymeric substances) protects the microbes; a microbial sessile community — cells adhere to a surface or interface and to each other
- Occur on / in: Catheters, pipes, implants (medical implants), human tissues, food and food-processing surfaces
- Effects: Reduces antibiotic penetration and effect; increases microbial tolerance and resistance (antibiotic resistance); greater tolerance to host defence and external stress — the matrix gives additional resistance or protection; causes persistent or chronic infection — difficult-to-eradicate infections
- Food industry: Spoilage and contamination
- Resistance progression in bacteria: MDR → XDR → TDR
### Antimicrobial resistance — causes
## 1. Causes of antibiotic resistance
- Incorrect dose: Selection of resistant microbes
- Incomplete course: Resistant survivors multiply
- Overuse: Selection pressure; unnecessary use causes resistance
- Livestock use: Resistant strains
- Repeated exposure: Selection of resistant organisms
- Horizontal gene transfer: Spreads resistance genes
- Mutation: Can generate antibiotic resistance
- Poor infection control: Spreads resistant organisms
## 2. Causes — One Health view
- Causes: Overuse/misuse of antibiotics in humans and animals/livestock/farms
- Self-medication, unregulated veterinary drugs
- Poor sanitation, poor waste control, pharma effluents
- → a One Health problem; complicates India's infectious-disease burden
