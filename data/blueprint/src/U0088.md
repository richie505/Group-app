### Chola kingship → succession, titles and image
## 1. Succession
- Yuvaraja practice: King named his successor (yuvaraja) in his own lifetime (named their successor in their own lifetime) + made him co-regent (UPPSC 2001 - Chola)
- e.g. Rajendra I co-regent in 1012, king in 1014
## 2. Titles and image of the king
- Rajaraja I: "Ulakalanada Perumal" / Ulakalanda Perumal = one who measured the earth (like Vishnu's three strides)
- Divine status: King supreme, compared to gods; images of kings and queens installed beside deities ("Rajaraja temple")
- Ideal king: Warrior, patron of arts, giver to Brahmanas, protector of varnashrama dharma
- Security: Administrative tours; hereditary bodyguards paid in land revenue
- Marco Polo noted bodyguards ready to die for the king
- Exam angle: "Which dynasty's king declared his successor during his own tenure?" - Chola.

### Land survey, revenue and tax terms → meaning
## 1. Survey and assessment
- Land survey: Rajaraja I → detailed survey and assessment + revenue reorganisation → earned "Ulakalanda Perumal"
- Periodic land surveys repeated; recorded holdings and transfers
- Main income: Land revenue, normally 1/6th of produce
- Paid in cash, kind or both; collected through village assemblies
- Revenue department = Puravu-varitinaikkalam / Shrikaranam
## 2. Taxes and dues
| Term | Meaning |
| Kadamai | Land revenue collected in kind |
| Antarayam | Rural tax in cash |
| Eccoru | Obligation / duty to feed visiting state officials |
| Vetti (also muttaiyal, kudimai) | Forced labour owed by villagers to the state |
| Nettal | Compulsory labour |
| Ayam | Royal tax / dues[GK] |
| Irai | Tax / levy on land [GK] |
| Kasu | Gold coin |
- ⚠ Trap: Kadamai = in kind; Antarayam = in cash; Vetti / Nettal = labour, not money
## 3. Remission
- Tax exemptions / remission: Land exemptions handled and granted through the ur (village assembly)
- Kulottunga I: Abolished tolls → Sungam Tavirtta Chola (abolisher of tolls)

- Ghatikas: Colleges generally attached to temples — centres of learning
- Agraharas: Settlements and land grants associated with Brahmins and learning
- Eripatti: Land whose revenue was set apart for the maintenance of village tanks
- Taniyurs: Very large villages administered as a single unit — ⚠ not Brahmin villages

### Irrigation and the temple → functions
- Kallanai — also the Grand Anicut: Karikala Chola — irrigation and agricultural development
- Tank irrigation: The Cholas — a major feature of the agricultural economy
- Tanks and reservoirs: The Vijayanagara rulers — irrigation and water management
- Large irrigation tanks: The Kakatiyas — agricultural expansion and water management
## 1. Irrigation
- Tanks (eri): Every village tank run by the eri variyam (tank committee) → distributed water to fields
- Upkeep: Eripatti land financed repairs/maintenance
- Cauvery delta channels: Built on Sangam-era Grand Anicut (Kallanai) on the Kaveri by Sangam Chola Karikala; kept up by later Cholas
## 2. Temple as administrative-economic centre
- Grants: Large devadana lands
- Royal direction: Rajaraja I directed the Sabhas of Cholamandalam to perform services for the Brihadeeswara temple (Thanjavur inscriptions)
- Roles: Biggest landholder, employer and lender of the locality; venue for village assembly meetings
### Inscriptions and copper plates → ruler and content
- Chola inscriptions: Most voluminous and detailed in South India; Tamil = language of administration
- Records: Royal land grants recorded on copper plates
| Record | Point |
| Anbil plates | Sanskrit genealogy, cited with the Leiden grant as royal prashasti; issued under Sundara Chola[GK] |
| Tiruvalangadu plates | Copper plates of Rajendra I giving Chola genealogy and conquests [GK] |
| Larger Leiden (Anaimangalam) plates | Ordered by Rajaraja I, engraved under Rajendra I; grant of Anaimangalam village (near Nagapattinam) to the Chulamanivarma-vihara built by Srivijaya king Sri Mara Vijayotunga Varman |
- Leiden plates:
- 21 large + 3 small sheets
- Languages: Sanskrit (genealogy) + Tamil (grant)
- Smaller plates (3 sheets) = Kulottunga I's: Extended grant after two Javanese envoys appealed
- 4,500 kalam of paddy plus land, on top of Rajaraja I's 8,943 kalam (granted by Rajaraja I in the Leiden plates)
- Grant to the Chudamani Vihara at Nagapattinam (Srivijaya king, Chola grants)
