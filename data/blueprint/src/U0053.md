### Ikshvaku kings → religious policy
| King | Religious policy |
| Vasishthiputra Sri Santamula (founder) | Vedic: Ashvamedha, Rajasuya, Vajapeya, Agnistoma; worshipped Virupakshapati (Shiva) and Mahasena (Skanda); agraharas to northern Brahmins |
| Virapurushadatta | The only Ikshvaku king to adopt Buddhism (earlier Vedic); his reign was the golden age of Buddhism; rebuilt the Maha Chaitya under Acharya Bhadanta Ananda; titles "Ashoka of the South", "Sri Parvatadhipati"; sculptures at Nagarjunakonda show a Shivalinga trampled underfoot |
| Ehuvala Santamula | Revived Brahmanism; first to build Hindu temples; issued the first Sanskrit inscriptions; still built a vihara for the Bahushrutiyas in his mother's name; Upasika Bodhisri served Buddhism in his reign |
| Rudrapurushadatta (last) | Vedic; presiding deity Mahasena; the custom of chhaya-stambhas (shadow pillars) near graves began in his reign |
- Dual patronage:
- Kings (all except Virapurushadatta) performed Vedic sacrifices
- Queens, princesses and rich lay women funded stupas + viharas
- Exam angle: greatest patronage = Veerapurushadatta (G2 2008); Bodhisri's service = reign of Ehuvala Chamtamula (G2 2012). Some notes list Bodhisri under Virapurushadatta; the APPSC key follows Ehuvala.

### Mahachaitya — relic stupa, builders, inscriptions
- Type: Dhatugarbha (relic) stupa over the Buddha's bodily relics
- Builder: Chamtisri, in Virapurushadatta's 6th year, with 9 Ayaka pillars
- Rebuilt: The ruined Paravata Mahachaitya, first built by Yajnasri Satakarni for Acharya Nagarjuna
- Bodhisri's works:
- Stone mandapa at the eastern gateway of the Maha Chaitya at Kantakasala (Ghantasala)
- One opposite the Paravata Vihara
### Bodhisri's inscription → countries and foreign links
- Bodhisri's viharas for foreign monks: Tamravarna (Sinhala), Kashmir, China, Gandhara
- Also seven viharas at Papili
- Countries receiving the Dhamma (her inscription): Kashmir, Gandhara, China, Chila(ta), Tosali, Aparanta, Vanga, Vanavasi, Yavana, Damila, Palura
- Sri Lanka ties: Ceylonese monks made Nagarjunakonda their Hinayana base

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:52
### Kings & religious policy
- ★ Shantisri and Harmyasri — sisters of Vasishthiputra Santamula (I)
- Sri Santamula — worshipped Virupakshapati and Mahasena; gave agraharas to northern Brahmins
- Virapurushadatta — titles Ashoka of the South and Sri Parvatadhipati
- adopted and strongly supported Buddhism; did not fully drop Brahmanical ties
- Ehuvala Santamula — revived Brahmanism; built a vihara for the Bahushrutiyas in his mother's name
- Rudrapurushadatta — custom of chhaya-stambhas (shadow pillars) near graves began
- Dual patronage: kings did Vedic sacrifices; queens, princesses and rich lay women funded stupas and viharas
### Persons & Sriparvata
- Sriparvata — identified with Nagarjunakonda / Vijayapuri; traditionally Dakshina Gaya
- Hiuen Tsang (7th c.) — found the Sriparvata vihara neglected
- Itsing (c. 700 CE) — saw Indian students memorising Nagarjuna's Suhrllekha (letter to Yajnasri Satakarni)
- Ceylonese monks — made Nagarjunakonda their base for Hinayana
- Bodh Gaya — Hinayana; Sriparvata — Mahayana
