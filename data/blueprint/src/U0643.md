### Climatologists → classification schemes and regions
## 1. Trewartha (modified Koeppen)
- Basis: Trewartha modified Koeppen (same temperature-rainfall logic, simpler for India)

| Am | tropical rainforest | west coast, NE |
| Aw | tropical savanna | most of the Peninsula |
| BS/BW | steppe and desert | Rajasthan, rain-shadow Deccan |

- ⚠ Trewartha Caw = Koeppen Cwg; Trewartha H = Koeppen Dfc/E
## 2. Thornthwaite (moisture-based)
- Basis: Thornthwaite classified climates by:
- precipitation effectiveness (moisture index)
- thermal efficiency (potential evapotranspiration)
- Moisture types: perhumid/wet (A) → humid (B) → sub-humid (C) → semi-arid (D) → arid (E)
| Thornthwaite type (India) | Places |
| Wettest (perhumid) | Western Ghats-west coast and the North-East |
| Arid | western Rajasthan (Thar) + Kutch |
| Semi-arid | rain-shadow interior Deccan |

### AP rainfall zones, temperature extremes and agro-climatic zones
## 1. Rainfall zones
- Heavy rainfall: The Sileru and Sabari valleys — 140–152 cm; a relatively higher-rainfall area in coastal and north-eastern Andhra Pradesh
- Low rainfall: The Hagari River valley — less than 51 cm; the Anantapur–Hagari valley is the lowest-rainfall region; Anantapur — the district with the lowest rainfall
- Rayalaseema: Experiences droughts and famines — hence very low population density
- Rainfall gradient decreases from the Srikakulam–Visakhapatnam–Agency region (125–200 cm) → the Kadapa–Kurnool region (75–100 cm) → the Kadapa–Kurnool–Chittoor–Anantapur region (less than 70 cm)
- Highest in north coast → decreasing towards Rayalaseema
- Seasonal peaks:
- Rayalaseema = rain-shadow; peak Oct-Nov (NE monsoon)

## Thornthwaite
- Basis — moisture index (precipitation effectiveness) and potential evapotranspiration (thermal efficiency)
- Wettest to driest — perhumid (wet), humid, semi-arid, arid
- Rain-shadow interior Deccan — semi-arid; western Rajasthan and Kutch — arid
- Rayalaseema — rain-shadow of SW monsoon; peak rain October–November from north-east monsoon
### AP climatic regions
- ★ Arid regions — about one-third of Earth; largest share
- Semi-arid Rayalaseema — low rainfall, droughts, extreme heat
- Coast humid and wet; deltas fertile, river-fed; hills cool
- Coastal erosion at Uppada (near Kakinada) and Krishna coast — rising sea levels and cyclonic waves
- Rayalaseema droughts — due to low, uneven rainfall; low population density is a result, not cause
