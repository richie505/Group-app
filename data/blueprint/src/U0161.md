# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:160
### Trigger & rival claimants
- Carnatic claimants: Chanda Sahib — French-backed; Muhammad Ali — English-backed
### Trigger
- Caused by Nizam-ul-Mulk's death in 1748 and Chanda Sahib's release from Maratha captivity
### Battles & events
- After Ambur (1749) — Anwaruddin killed; Chanda Sahib Nawab; Muhammad Ali fled to Trichinopoly
- Nasir Jang (English-backed, Hyderabad) — killed in a Dupleix plot by Himmat Khan, Nawab of Kurnool
- Arcot (1751) — Clive took it with about 210 men; held it about 53 days against Raja Sahib, son of Chanda Sahib
### Key persons
- Salabat Jang — fourth son of Nizam-ul-Mulk; installed by Bussy right after Muzaffar Jang's death
- Morari Rao — Maratha chief who joined the English, with Mysore and Tanjore
- Robert Clive and Stringer Lawrence — freed Trichinopoly; French commander Law surrendered
- Godeheu — succeeded Dupleix in 1754; made peace with the English
### Siege of Arcot
- Chanda Sahib and the French were surrounding Muhammad Ali at Trichinopoly
- Clive attacked Arcot (Carnatic capital) as a diversion to help Muhammad Ali
### Rewards to Dupleix & recall
- Muzaffar Jang made Dupleix Governor of Mughal territory south of the Krishna; gave him Valapadu as a jagir
- 1754 — Dupleix recalled for the heavy cost and failures of intervention, mainly defeat at Trichinopoly
- Second Carnatic War — Indian succession disputes; major French gains on the Andhra coast
- First Carnatic War — extension of the War of Austrian Succession
### Exam corner
- Dupleix won the Deccan (influence at Hyderabad) but lost the Carnatic (Arcot, Trichinopoly)
### Battles and events → year and outcome
- ★ Battle of Ambur — 1749 (not 1752); first major battle of the Second Carnatic War
- ★ Muzaffar Jang, Chanda Sahib and the French (Dupleix) together against Anwar-ud-din, who was defeated
### Rewards to Dupleix and his recall (1754)
- ★ Muzaffar Jung, after becoming Nizam, made Joseph Dupleix governor of the territory south of the Krishna
- ★ Dupleix — first European to take part in Indian princes' quarrels to gain territory
- first to use the European army at Indian courts at Indian kings' cost; tactics later copied by the English
