### Gandhara and Mathura schools → region, patron, features
## 1. Trade and art
- Evidence of ports and trading centres: The Periplus of the Erythraean Sea; the Arikamedu excavation
- Kushana trade operated through the Persian Bay and the Red Sea
- Gandhara school: Developed and flourished during the Kushana period — based on the Indo-Greek style; influenced by Greek art; Kanishka encouraged and protected it
- Gandhara and Mathura developed distinct sculptural styles
## 2. Gandhara
- Region: North-West frontier - Peshawar, Taxila, Swat Valley (modern Pakistan/Afghanistan)
- Influence: Hellenistic (Greek), Roman and Persian
- Indian craftsmen + Greeks and Romans → Indo-Greek / Greco-Buddhist art
- Patron dynasty: Kushanas (introduced by them; flourished under Kanishka's line)
- Period: c. 1st-5th century CE
- Theme: predominantly Mahayana Buddhist - Buddha with Bodhisattvas (Avalokiteshvara, Maitreya)
- Exam angle: "Indian + Greek features = ?" → Gandhara; "flourished under which dynasty?" → Kushanas.
## 3. Mathura
- Nature: indigenous school of the Ganga plains; not restricted to Buddhism
- Earliest images: earliest surviving iconic images of Hindu deities (Shiva, Vishnu, Surya) and of Jain Tirthankaras + Yaksha-Yakshi figures
- Kushana royal portraits: red sandstone - e.g. headless standing statue of Kanishka from Mat near Mathura
- Gupta continuity: continued into the Gupta period with greater elegance and softness

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:45
### Three schools compared
## At a glance
| School | Region | Material | Character |
| Gandhara | north-west | grey schist, later stucco | foreign-influenced, Buddhist (Mahayana) |
| Mathura | Ganga valley | spotted red sandstone | indigenous and multi-religious |
| Amaravati | Andhra | white Palnadu limestone | indigenous and narrative |
## Gandhara
- Mainly Mahayana Buddhism; Buddha shown in human form with Bodhisattvas (Avalokiteshvara, Maitreya), not only through symbols
- Realistic, Apollo-like faces and spiritual calm
- Heavy toga-like robes, wavy hair with a top-knot
## Mathura
- Jnanachakra halo behind the Buddha's head — Mathura feature
- Standing Buddha images prominent in the Gupta phase
- Gupta stone sculpture — carries on the Mathura school (more grace and softness)
## Amaravati
- Slender, dynamic, emotional figures with strong movement
- Crowded narrative compositions; Yakshas and Gandharvas dancing around the Buddha
- Mature phase linked to the Ikshvakus and Mahayana themes (not the Kushanas)
## Periods
- Gandhara and Mathura — Kushana-period schools
- Sarnath style — Gupta period
- Pala school — 8th–12th centuries
### Buddha image firsts
- Buddha first widely shown in human form in the Kushana period (1st c. CE), by both Gandhara and Mathura; which came first is debated; Amaravati was not first
- Before human images: aniconic worship through throne, footprints, wheel, tree and stupa
- Chaitika Vada (symbol worship) in Andhra — started by Mahadeva
- Amaravati Ayakapatas show the Buddha through symbols, not in human form
- Proof of Kushana-period ports and trade — Periplus of the Erythraean Sea and Arikamedu dig
### Gandhara & Mathura
- Gandhara — Hellenistic Greek, Roman and Persian influences mixed with Indian traditions
- Mathura — early iconic images of Shiva, Vishnu and Surya; also Jain Tirthankaras and Yaksha-Yakshi figures
### Amaravati school (AP)
## Praise and influence
- James Fergusson — popularised Amaravati art in Tree and Serpent Worship; called it the 'Epitome of Indian architecture'
- Percy Brown — called it the pinnacle of Indian art
- Influence reached Sri Lanka (esp. Anuradhapura), South-East Asia and later Gupta art
- Purna-Kumbha motif — emblem of the Andhra Pradesh State Government
## Sites and makers
- Oldest stupa in AP — Bhattiprolu
- Nagarjunakonda — main centre under the Ikshvakus; Ghantasala another main centre
- Sculptors of the Amaravati school — Selavardhakulu
- First depiction of Sati in Indian art — Amaravati-school sculpture of the Ikshvaku period
