### Agriculture and settlement — oldest evidence
## 1. Agriculture and settlement
- Mehrgarh: The oldest evidence of settled life; earlier accepted as the oldest evidence of agriculture (the earlier accepted agricultural site); the earliest evidence of wheat
- Lahuradeva — Lahuradewa: Currently the oldest evidence of paddy cultivation — the earliest evidence of agriculture in the Indian subcontinent and of rice cultivation
- Koldihwa: Earlier considered the oldest site for paddy
- Belan Valley (the Prayagraj region): Earliest evidence of rice near the Belan River
- Barley and paddy: Among the oldest cultivated food grains — barley was the first cereal used by humans
- Atranjikhera: The earliest evidence of iron in India
- Indus Valley Civilization: The earliest known urban civilization
- Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro: The earliest public water tank; Lothal: the earliest known tidal dockyard (world's earliest tidal dockyard)
### Crops → site of evidence; FIRSTs
## 1. Crops through history
- Barley: The first cereal cultivated by humans — around 8000 BC; Mehrgarh provides early evidence (Mahagara = Neolithic rice and cattle-pen site)
- Paddy: Lahuradeva — the oldest evidence; Koldihwa was earlier considered the oldest site
- Harappan crops: Wheat, barley and rice; cotton agriculture — textiles and trade
- Uraiyur: Associated with cotton trade
- Ibn Battuta: Rice (in suitable regions), wheat, sugarcane and cotton could be cultivated thrice a year in medieval India
- Maize: ⚠ Not cultivated in India during the 13th–14th centuries — the Portuguese brought or introduced it

### Metals and raw materials → source region
## 1. Metals
- The first metal used by humans: copper
- Indus Valley: Copper and bronze — Harappan evidence includes gold, silver, copper and brass; ⚠ iron was not found at Harappan sites
- Also used: lead; Bronze = copper + tin: Tools, weapons, the Dancing Girl

- Iron was unknown: Stone tools (chert blades from Rohri) continued alongside metal
- Rigvedic Aryans: Gold, silver, copper
- Iron: Spread in North India during 1000–600 BC — the Iron Age in India during the Later Vedic period, around 1000 BC
- Exam angle: "Which metal has not been found at Harappan sites" → Iron (CGPSC 2011).

### Seals → form, motifs, find sites abroad
## 1. Form and motifs
- Shape: Mostly square; cylindrical seals (Mesopotamian type) at Mohenjodaro + Kalibangan
- Square = animal + inscription; rectangular = inscription only
- Button seal at Lothal
- Motifs: Unicorn (most common) - one-horned bull-like figure (CDI); APP notes say the humpless bull; also bull, elephant; human figures + short line of script
- Pashupati seal (Mohenjodaro): Seated figure surrounded by animals → proto-Shiva
## 2. Find sites → seal
| Find site | Seal |

| Rakhigarhi (LENS, Jun 2026) | Cylindrical steatite seal - alligator motif and five characters |
- ⚠ Button seal + Persian Gulf seal = Lothal; cylindrical = Mohenjodaro + Kalibangan
## 3. Uses
- Authentication + ownership of traded goods; sealing packages
- Exam angle: bull seal → Mohenjodaro (RPSC 2008).
### Weights and measures → site
## 1. Weights
- Material: Chert (also limestone, steatite); generally cubical
- Standard unit: About 13.63 g (the "16" ratio); accuracy within 1-2%
- Regulation: Graduated scales + uniform weights across ~8 lakh sq km → central regulation of trade
## 2. Scale find → site
| Scale find | Site |

- ⚠ Ivory = Lothal; shell = Mohenjodaro; copper = Harappa
## 3. Unit length
- Scale found: Lothal (APPSC G2 2011); ivory scale → Lothal (UPPSC 2014)
- Exam angle: "In which IVC city was a scale discovered" - Lothal (APPSC G2 2011).
