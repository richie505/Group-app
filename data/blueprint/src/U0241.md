### August Offer, 1940 — context, proposals, significance
## 1. August Offer, 1940
- Context: Answered Ramgarh's offer (support for the war if a provisional national government formed at the Centre)
- Proposals:
- (1) Representative constitution-making body after the war
- (2) More Indians in the Viceroy's Executive Council
- (3) A War Advisory Council
- Dominion Status as the goal; minority veto
- Significance: First official acceptance that Indians would frame their own constitution
### Cripps Mission, 1942 — context and proposals
## 1. Cripps Mission — 1942
- Arrived in March 1942 — headed by Sir Stafford Cripps; sent by British Prime Minister Winston Churchill
- Under pressure from US President Roosevelt + China's Chiang Kai-shek
- Context: World War II and the Japanese advance
- Japan's entry into the war (Pearl Harbor 1941; Malaya, Singapore overrun; Rangoon fell March 1942)
- Main objective: To secure Indian cooperation in the war
- Proposals:
- Offered Dominion Status after the war
- A constitution-making body
- Provinces could opt out of the proposed Union
- Congress negotiators: Jawaharlal Nehru and Maulana Abul Kalam Azad
- Gandhi described the offer as a "post-dated cheque on a crashing/falling bank"
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say Gandhi said only "post-dated cheque" and Nehru added "on a crashing bank"
- The proposals were rejected — the negotiations failed
## 1. Cripps proposals
| Proposal (Draft Declaration) | Detail |
| Dominion Status | Indian Union with dominion status, with power to secede, after the war |
| Constitution-making body | Constituent Assembly after the war; members elected by provincial lower houses as an electoral college (1/10th of their size, proportional representation) plus nominees of Princely States |
| Provincial option | Any province/princely state unwilling to join could stay out and form separate unions - Pakistan conceded in principle ("the fatal flaw") |
| Interim period | Defence and control of the army stay with the British till then; Governor-General's powers retained |
- Exam angle: month/year (March 1942), Cripps's party (Labour), what the plan offered (Dominion Status after the war).

### Responses, failure and results
## 1. August Offer
- Congress rejected it
- League welcomed the minority veto but said nothing short of partition would satisfy it
## 2. Cripps Mission
- Congress negotiators: Nehru and Maulana Azad - objected to the right to secede + no immediate transfer of power
- Congress rejected: No immediate power/independence; risk of Balkanisation
- Muslim League's response: Rejected - opposed a single Indian Union; wanted Pakistan conceded explicitly
| Failure cause | Effect |
| Cripps's "take it or leave it" attitude; Churchill's sabotage | Talks broke down |
| Too radical for Churchill, too conservative for Indians | Rejected by all sides |
- Result: Failure directly led to the Quit India Movement (August 1942)
- ⚠ Both Congress AND League rejected Cripps - for opposite reasons (no immediate power vs no explicit Pakistan)
