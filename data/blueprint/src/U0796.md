### Nuclear and radiological disasters, protective actions and notified disasters
## 1. Nuclear and radiological disasters
- Nuclear disaster: An uncontrolled release of radioactive material causing significant harm to life, health, property or the environment
- A man-made or technological disaster in the CBRN category — ⚠ a nuclear explosion is not a natural disaster
- Nuclear disasters: Include nuclear accidents involving reactors
- Radiological disasters: Include orphan-source incidents, transport accidents and dirty-bomb incidents
## 3. Notified disasters

- MHA added heatwave and lightning on 16th Finance Commission's advice
- ⚠ No man-made calamity is notified (7 and 27 Aug 2026)

### Heatwaves → nature, nodal agency and management
## 1. Heatwave — nature
- Heatwave: A period of abnormally high temperatures exceeding the normal maximum temperature during the summer season
- Season in India: Mainly March to June; sometimes extends to July
- Nodal agency for forecasting heatwaves: India Meteorological Department — early warning through colour-coded alerts
- NDMA Guidelines for Preparation of Action Plan – Prevention and Management of Heat Wave: Key guidelines; updated 2019
- A notified disaster: Included in the list of notified disasters eligible for SDRF and NDRF assistance — as per the latest updates
## 2. Heatwave — management
- Heat Action Plans: Prepared by states and cities with support from NDMA and IMD
- Cool roofs: Reflective coatings or white painting of roofs
- Public awareness measures: Communicating do's and don'ts; ORS distribution; establishing cooling centres
- Structural measures: Shade structures; drinking water points; adjusted working hours
### Cold waves → nature, cold day, impacts and measures
## 1. Cold wave — nature
- Cold wave: A significant drop in minimum temperature below the normal climatological average during the winter season
- Main season in India: November to March; peak impact December to February
- Nodal agency for forecasting cold waves: India Meteorological Department — issues colour-coded cold-wave alerts: Yellow, Orange and Red
- NDMA Guidelines on Cold Wave and Frost: Issued 2021 — focus on preparedness, vulnerability mapping and community awareness
- A notified disaster eligible for assistance under the SDRF and NDRF
## 2. Cold day
- Cold day: Maximum temperature departure of –4.5°C to –6.4°C while the minimum temperature is already low
- Severe cold day: Maximum temperature departure less than –6.4°C
## 3. Cold wave — impacts
- Health: Hypothermia; respiratory diseases; frostbite; increased mortality among elderly people and children
- Groups most vulnerable: Homeless people, elderly people, children, outdoor workers, street vendors, poor households
- Agriculture: Crop damage due to frost; affects livestock
- Power demand: Increases
## 4. Cold wave — measures
- Protective measures: Distributing warm clothing; providing night shelters; establishing community kitchens; medical preparedness
- Agricultural measures: Frost-protection techniques for crops

### Sendai Framework 2015–2030 — adoption, four priorities, guiding principles
## 1. Adoption and nature
- Adopted on 18 March 2015 at the Third UN World Conference on Disaster Risk Reduction — Sendai, Japan; duration 2015–2030
- Succeeded the Hyogo Framework for Action (HFA) 2005–2015
- Custodian: UNDRR
- A voluntary, non-binding global agreement
- Expected outcome: A substantial reduction of disaster risk and of losses in lives, livelihoods, health and in economic, physical, social, cultural and environmental assets
## 2. Four priorities
| Priority | Emphasizes |
| 1. Understanding disaster risk | Risk assessment in all dimensions — hazard, vulnerability, exposure, capacity |
| 2. Strengthening disaster risk governance | Laws, institutions, policies and coordination at all levels |
| 3. Investing in DRR for resilience | Public and private investment in prevention and mitigation |
| 4. Enhancing disaster preparedness for effective response and to "Build Back Better" | Preparedness, recovery, rehabilitation, reconstruction |
## 3. Guiding principles
- States have the primary role in reducing disaster risk
- All-of-society engagement: Government, private sector, civil society, academia, media
- Multi-hazard approach: Natural and man-made hazards
- Build Back Better: Recovery as an opportunity to reduce future risk
- Inclusive and people-centred: Special attention to vulnerable groups
### Sendai Framework — seven global targets
## 1. Seven global targets
| Target | Aim |
| A | Substantially reduce global disaster mortality by 2030 |
| B | Substantially reduce the number of affected people globally by 2030 |
| C | Reduce direct economic loss in relation to global GDP by 2030 |
| D | Substantially reduce disaster damage to critical infrastructure and disruption of basic services by 2030 |
| E | Substantially increase the number of countries with national and local DRR strategies by 2020 |
| F | Substantially enhance international cooperation to developing countries by 2030 |
| G | Substantially increase the availability of and access to multi-hazard early warning systems and disaster risk information by 2030 |
- In short, the global targets: reducing disaster mortality, the number of affected people, economic loss and damage to infrastructure; increasing DRR strategies, international cooperation and early warning systems
- ⚠ Sendai = 4 priorities, 7 targets
