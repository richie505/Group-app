# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:91 - Yadavas of Devagiri - Bhillama, Singhana, Hemadri and the Hemadpanti style
### The dynasty
- Devagiri = first southern kingdom to fall to Delhi; it opened the road to Warangal, Dwarasamudra, Madurai
- Also Seuna Yadavas; ruled about 1187-1317 AD
- Capital: Devagiri
- Mother tongue Marathi; Sanskrit for scholarship
- Yadavas encouraged Marathi
### Rulers
| Ruler | Achievement |
| Bhillama V | Founded Devagiri (c. 1187); beaten by Veera Ballala II |
| Jaitugi | Killed Kakatiya Rudradeva; freed Ganapatideva (1199) |
| Singhana II (1200-1247) | Greatest; beat Hoysalas; Teluguraya Sthapanacharya |
| Mahadeva (1261-1271) | Repulsed by Rudramadevi |
| Ramachandra (1271-1311) | Defeated 1295 → feudatory |
| Harapaladeva (1313-1317) | Last; died in Mubarak Khalji's invasion |
### Scholars & works
| Person | Work |
| Sarngadeva | Sangita Ratnakara: Hindustani + Carnatic base text |
| Hemadri (minister) | Chaturvarga Chintamani (APPSC G2 2000) |
| Dnyaneshwar | Dnyaneshwari (Gita in Marathi), 1290, Nevasa |
| Chakradhar Swami | Founded Mahanubhava sect |
| Mhaimbhat | Lilacharitra = first Marathi prose |
| Changadeva | Grandson of Bhaskaracharya; school at Patan |
### Hemadri & Hemadpanti
- Office
- Shrikaranadhipa under Mahadeva & Ramachandra
- Hemadpanti style
- Local black basalt
- Stones interlocked without mortar
### Fall to Delhi
- 1295 — First Muslim invasion of the South: Alauddin Khalji beats Ramachandra (⚠ other notes: 1296)
- 1307-08 — Malik Kafur: Ramachandra taken to Delhi
- 1317-18 — Harapaladeva killed; kingdom ends
- After 1323 — Malik Burhanuddin given Andhra; Devagiri = Deccan base

### Yadava rulers → contributions, Bhillama V to Harapaladeva
## 1. Yadavas of Devagiri
- Initially feudatories of the Western Chalukyas; also known as the Seuna rulers of Devagiri (Seuna Yadavas)
- Capital: Devagiri — the realm was in western India and the Deccan, centred on Devagiri
## 2. Yadava rulers
- Language: Mother tongue Marathi; Sanskrit = language of scholarship (APHQ)
| Ruler | Achievement |
| Bhillama V | Threw off Kalyani Chalukya overlordship; founded Devagiri as capital (c. 1187) [GK]; defeated by Hoysala Veera Ballala II (CDX) |
| Jaitugi (Jaitrapala) | Killed Kakatiya Rudradeva (Mahadeva also died fighting him); held Ganapatideva captive, then released him (1199) and married his daughter Somaladevi to him (CDX, APP) |
| Singhana II (1200-1247) | Greatest Yadava; defeated the Hoysalas; title 'Teluguraya Sthapanacharya' (installer of the Telugu king) (CDX) |
| Krishna (1246-1261) | Fought the Hoysalas (CDX) |
| Mahadeva (1261-1271) | Defeated by the Hoysalas (CDX); his attack on the Kakatiyas was repulsed by Rudramadevi (APP, A-77) |
| Ramachandra (Ramadeva) (1271-1311) | Defeated by the Delhi forces in 1295, became a feudatory (CDX) |
| Shankaradeva (1311-1313) | Died in Malik Kafur's invasion (CDX) |
| Harapaladeva (1313-1317) | Died in Mubarak Khalji's invasion - end of the Yadava kingdom (CDX) |
## 3. Singhana II - Hoysala and Kakatiya policy
- Hoysalas: Defeated them
- Kakatiyas: Released Ganapatideva as a counterweight to Hoysala Veera Ballala's push into the Krishna-Tungabhadra region
- Son: Sarangapanideva later served at Rudramadevi's court
- ⚠ Bhillama V founded Devagiri; Singhana II = greatest; Harapaladeva = last
### Scholars and saints → works (books → authors)
| Person | Role | Work / contribution |
| Sarngadeva | Court musician of Singhana II | Sangita Ratnakara = basic text for both Hindustani + Carnatic music |
| Astronomer Changadeva | Grandson of Bhaskaracharya | School at Patan to teach Bhaskara's works |
| Hemadri | Scholar, administrator and minister of the Devagiri Yadavas (APHQ) | Sanskrit encyclopaedia Chaturvarga Chintamani (dharma, rituals, charity, pilgrimage, vratas) (APPSC G2 2000 key) |
| Saint-poet Dnyaneshwar | Bhakti saint | Dnyaneshwari = Marathi commentary on the Gita; written 1290 at Nevasa, in Ramachandra's reign |
| Chakradhar Swami | Founder | Mahanubhava sect |
| Mhaimbhat | Mahanubhava author | Lilacharitra = first Marathi prose work |
- Patronage: Yadavas encouraged Marathi (APHQ)
- Bhakti saints: Dnyaneshwar, Namdev, later Tukaram, Ramdas → took Upanishad + Gita ideas into Marathi
- Hemadri's office: Served Mahadeva and Ramachandra as Shrikaranadhipa (head of the secretariat/records)
- Claim on Kakatiyas: Court poet Hemadri claimed Jaitugi captured and sacrificed Kakatiya Rudra
- ⚠ Sangita Ratnakara = Sarngadeva; Chaturvarga Chintamani = Hemadri; Lilacharitra = first Marathi prose
### Hemadpanti style — features
- Architecture: Hemadri credited with the Hemadpanti style of temple architecture (APHQ)
| Hemadpanti feature | Detail |
| Material | Local black basalt[GK] |
| Masonry | Dressed stones interlocked without mortar (dry masonry) [GK] |
### Delhi Sultanate raids and Kakatiya conflicts — events by year
| Year | Event |
| 1295 | First Muslim invasion of the South, on Devagiri: Alauddin Khalji (for Sultan Jalaluddin) defeats Ramachandra; Ellichpur taken; Yadavas become Delhi vassals (CDX, APP; many texts date this raid 1296) |
| 1307-08 | Malik Kafur's campaign; Ramachandra submits and is taken to Delhi [GK] |
| 1317-18 | Harapaladeva killed in Mubarak Khalji's invasion; kingdom ends (CDX) |
- After 1323: Devagiri's vizier Malik Burhanuddin given charge of conquered Andhra → Devagiri = Sultanate's Deccan base
- Paithan copper plate of Ramachandra: Confirms Jaitugi killed Rudradeva + imprisoned Ganapatideva
### Universal angles
- Significance: Devagiri = first southern kingdom to fall to Delhi → opened road to Warangal, Dwarasamudra, Madurai (UPSC)
- AP angle: Yadava-Kakatiya rivalry
- Jaitugi killed Rudradeva
