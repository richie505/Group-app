### Rupee as a barometer of credibility
## 1. Context (2026)
- Rupee under pressure — BoP stress; CAD financing hard due to negative net FDI inflows and volatile FPI
## 2. Theories
- Dornbusch Overshooting Model: exchange rate overreacts in short run (sticky prices)
- Mundell-Fleming / Impossible Trinity: cannot have fixed exchange rate + free capital mobility + independent monetary policy together; India — managed float
## 3. Exam angle
- 2013 Taper Tantrum (US Fed) — rupee fell; RBI tightened short-term rates, oil-dollar swap; govt raised gold import duty
- ⚠ RBI buying foreign currency → expands domestic money supply (sterilisation may follow)
- ⚠ Higher FDI inflows → rupee appreciates (not depreciates)
- Depreciation → imported inflation, higher external debt burden
- Fowler Committee (1898): Gold Exchange Standard — rupee pegged to sterling at 1s 4d (15 rupees = £1) — for Home Charges

### LPG shortage and storage gap
## 1. News
- LPG shortage due to West Asia conflict — 85%+ of India's LPG imports pass via Strait of Hormuz
- NRAI warned ~60% of restaurants could shut; IEA flagged lack of storage as a key weakness
## 2. Consumption and imports
- India — second-largest LPG consumer; ~3 million t/month (~80,000 t/day); 85%+ used by households
- Imports: ~20 million t/year (tripled 2011-12 to 2024-25); dependency 47% (2015) → ~60%
- 33 crore domestic connections; ~10 crore added after 2017 under PMUY
## 3. Underground LPG caverns
- Total: 1.4 lakh t — ⚠ less than two days of consumption; MoPNG: no more caverns planned
- Crude/products reserves: about two months; global norm 90 days; EU gas storage must be 90% full before winter
## 4. Storage options
- Depleted gas fields: KG Basin, Cambay, Mumbai Offshore
## 5. Response
- LPG and CNG on priority list under Essential Commodities Act
- IEA release of ~400 million barrels from strategic reserves
- US deal: 2.2 million t LPG/year — shipping ~45 days
- ⚠ Strait pairs: Hormuz–Persian Gulf, Bab-el-Mandeb–Red Sea, Malacca–Andaman Sea, Sunda–Java Sea
## 6. LPG vs LNG
| Feature | LPG | LNG |
| Composition | Propane + butane | Methane |
| Source | Crude refining, gas processing | Natural gas |
| Liquefaction | Moderate pressure — 1/260th volume | Cooled to ~–160°C — 1/600th volume |
| Storage | Cylinders | ⚠ Cryogenic tanks |
| Density | ⚠ Heavier than air | Lighter than air |
| Use | Cooking | PNG, CNG, industry |
## 7. Crisis response
- LPG: ~60% imported, ~90% of imports via Hormuz → ~54% of supply vulnerable; 33.3 crore households
- Gas: ~50% imported, 55–60% of it from West Asia → ~30% of LNG affected; PNG/CNG prioritised over industry
- Refineries maximised LPG — domestic output +40%; domestic share 40% → 55%
- Cylinder booking interval: urban 25 days, rural 45 days; ~60 lakh households can shift to PNG

### Rice production and exports — need for a rethink
## 1. India's rice leadership
- Largest rice exporter since 2011–12; exported 21.69 million tonnes in 2024–25
- ⚠ Surpassed China to become the world's largest rice producer (150 mt) in 2024–25
## 2. Water footprint
- System: conventional transplanting-cum-continuous flooding; field water level 5 cm; 20–30 irrigations
- One irrigation of 1 acre at 2.5 cm = 101,171.5 litres; total ≈ 5 million litres per acre
- Water per kg: paddy ≈ 2,000 litres; milled rice ≈ 3,000 litres — virtual water export; stress in Punjab–Haryana
## 3. Basmati vs non-basmati
- Non-basmati exports double in volume but earnings similar; basmati earns 2–2.5 times more per kg
- Transplanting: non-basmati in June (peak summer); basmati in July (monsoon) — less irrigation
- Aroma compound: 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline (2-AP) — forms when grain filling occurs in October at 30–31°C
- Basmati GI region: 6.2 million ha — Punjab 3.1, Western UP 1.5, Haryana 1.3, Uttarakhand 0.12, Jammu 0.1, HP 0.05 mh; current cultivation only 2.1 mh
