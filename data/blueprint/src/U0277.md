## 2. Reasons for bulk (causes → length)
- Reasons for its length:
- India's size, diversity and complexity
- Heavy borrowing from / influence of the 1935 Act (Government of India Act 1935)
- A single Constitution for the Centre and the states (one constitution for both Centre and States)
- Dominance of legal luminaries (lawyers) in the Assembly
### Terms → meaning: constitutionalism, supremacy, democracy, republic
| Term | Meaning |
| Constitutionalism | Government limited by a written, supreme constitution; power by rules, not the ruler's will (absolute rule) |
| Supreme law | Constitution is the supreme law; Union and States derive powers from it; inconsistent laws void (judicial review, Art. 13) |
| Supreme authority | Supreme authority is the Constitution, not Parliament → Parliament = "a creature of the Constitution" |
| Representative (indirect) democracy | People elect representatives; a parliamentary executive governs; direct-democracy devices (referendum, initiative, recall) not part of the national system |
| Sovereign democratic republic | Elected head of state; ultimate authority with the people ("We, the people") |
| Ultimate source of authority | "We, the People of India" (Preamble) |
## 1. Constitutionalism in India
- In India: Parliament is a creature of the Constitution, not sovereign in the British sense
- Limits: written constitution, Fundamental Rights (Art. 13), judicial review, federal division of powers, basic structure limit on amendment (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973)
- Basic-structure elements: Supremacy of the Constitution, rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, limited amending power
### Parliamentary form — features and comparisons with the USA and Britain
## 1. Features and reasons
- Model: British model, not the American presidential one
- Features:
- Collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha (Art. 74(1), Art. 75)
- Ministers are members of the legislature
- Why the framers chose it:
- Already familiar under the 1919 and 1935 Acts
- India's size and diversity
## 2. India vs USA
| Parliamentary (India) | Presidential (USA) |
| Cooperation and coordination between legislature and executive | Separation of powers |
## 3. India vs Britain
- Contrast with Britain: British pattern but with basic differences
| India | Britain |
| Parliament not sovereign: bound by a written Constitution, FRs, judicial review, federal division, basic structure | Sovereignty of Parliament |
- Exam angle: APPSC GS 2019 keyed "India's head of state is elected; Britain rests on parliamentary sovereignty; Indian Parliament is not sovereign" as correct, and "Britain's head is elected" as wrong.
### Other salient features → provisions, Articles and amendments
## 1. Judiciary
- Synthesis of parliamentary sovereignty (Britain) and judicial supremacy (USA): Courts can strike down laws
- Judicial review narrower than USA - Art. 21 uses "procedure established by law"
- Integrated and independent judiciary: Single integrated judiciary for Union and States enforces both Central and state laws
## 2. Rights, duties and franchise
| Feature | Provision / number |
| Fundamental Rights | Six categories; property moved to Art. 300A; all suspendable in a National Emergency except Arts. 20 and 21 |
| Fundamental Duties | 11 (42nd Amendment + one by the 86th) |
| Universal adult franchise | Voting age cut from 21 to 18 (61st Amendment, 1988) |
## 3. Bodies, emergency and tiers
- Independent bodies: ECI, CAG, UPSC, SPSC (SPSCs)
- Emergency provisions: Part XVIII, Arts. 352-360
- National, State (President's Rule), Financial
- Federal structure turns unitary without formal amendment
- Three-tier government: 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992) added local government (Panchayats, Municipalities; Part IX, IXA) to the original dual polity
### Which is NOT a feature — odd-one-out sets
- IYB 2026 description: "a union of states, a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic with a parliamentary system of government"
- Features: Single citizenship, secular state, republic, independent judiciary, supremacy of the Constitution, division of powers, federalism
- NOT features:
- Dual citizenship (APPSC GS 2025)
- Religious state (APPSC GS 2018; no state religion)
- Unwritten constitution, presidential form, hereditary head of state
| Feature | Non-feature (odd one out) |
| Single citizenship | Dual citizenship (APPSC 2025) |
| Parliamentary form | Presidential form |
| Secular state | Religious state (APPSC 2018) |
| Universal adult franchise (from the start) | Property/education-based franchise |
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Features form the base of the basic structure list: supremacy of the Constitution, federalism, secularism, parliamentary system, judicial review, independence of judiciary
- Explain why India is "quasi-federal" and why Parliament is powerful but not supreme
- Criticism & debates: "Borrowed constitution", "carbon copy of the 1935 Act", "un-Indian", "elephantine", "lawyers' paradise"
- Comparative note:
- US states have own constitutions + dual citizenship; Indian states have neither
- Written like USA, parliamentary like Britain, strong centre like Canada, concurrent list like Australia, amendment procedure like South Africa

## Parliamentary system
- Council of Ministers — collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha
- Chosen because familiar through the GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935
- also fits India's size and diversity; avoids executive–legislature conflict
- India described as a Union of States with a parliamentary system
### India vs USA vs Britain
- ★ Republic — no hereditary rulers; head of State elected (in India, by indirect election)
- head of State, the President, elected, not hereditary; final power is with the people
- Parliament's power to amend limited by the basic structure doctrine (Parliament made by the Constitution)
- India — representative (indirect) democracy; no referendum, initiative or recall nationally
- Indian Parliament — not sovereign in the British sense: bound by written Constitution, Fundamental Rights, judicial review
- Judicial review narrower than the USA — Article 21 uses 'procedure established by law'
- Single integrated court system applies both Central and State laws
- India has a parliamentary, not presidential, form of government
### Other features
- Right to Property — moved out of Fundamental Rights; now under Article 300A
- Emergency provisions (Arts. 352-360) — Part XVIII: National, State (President's Rule), Financial
- Voting age 21 → 18 — 61st Amendment, 1988 (not 42nd, 44th or 73rd)
- India — secular state; no state religion
### Constitutionalism
- Constitutionalism — government power limited by a supreme Constitution, used by rules, not the ruler's wish
- Supremacy of the Constitution — a basic-structure part
- with rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, limited amending power
### Exam corner
- Original Constitution — 8 Schedules (395 Articles, 22 Parts); 12 Schedules now
- Written Constitution — linked with the USA
- strong Centre — Canada; Concurrent List — Australia; amendment procedure — South Africa
- Indian States — no separate constitutions, no dual citizenship (the USA has both)
