### Caste and tribe — institution, scholars and definitions
## 1. Caste and tribe
- Caste: A birth-based social institution unique to Indian society — endogamy, hierarchy, hereditary occupation, social restrictions
- Scholars: G. S. Ghurye, M. N. Srinivas; concepts: Sanskritization, Dominant Caste, Westernization
- Tribe: A social group sharing common territory, culture, language and ancestry — practices endogamy; political organization
## 2. Scholars → definitions of caste
| Scholar | Definition / book |
| S.V. Ketkar | History of Caste in India (1909): membership by birth plus a ban on marrying outside [GK] |
| H.H. Risley | families with a common name, claimed mythical ancestor, same hereditary calling [GK]; the racial theory (CDX) |
| C. Bouglé | three elements: hereditary specialisation, hierarchy, repulsion[GK] |
| Martindale and Monachesi | obligations and privileges fixed by birth, sanctioned by religion (CDX) |
| A.W. Green | ideally, no mobility up or down the status ladder (APP) |
- ⚠ Repulsion = Bouglé
### Theories and periods of origin of caste
| Theory | Proponent | Core idea (CDX) |
| Traditional / Divine | Rig Veda, Purusha Sukta (RV 10.90) | Brahmins from the head, Kshatriyas from the arms, Vaishyas from the thighs, Shudras from the feet |
| Racial | Herbert Risley | caste as a by-product of racial difference; Aryans kept apart from the original inhabitants (Pratiloma bar) |
| Occupational | Nesfield | hereditary occupations became guilds and then castes |
| Brahminical / Political | Abbé Dubois | a device of the Brahmins to keep superiority and purity |
| Cultural | Sarat Chandra Roy | restrictions on commensality and intermarriage to preserve culture |
- ⚠ Racial = Risley; occupational = Nesfield; Brahminical = Abbé Dubois
- Stages of caste:
- Early Vedic: no rigid varna; Purusha Sukta a late addition
- Later Vedic: four varnas + hereditary divisions crystallise
- Post-Maurya/Gupta: Manu Smriti + rigid caste
- British: caste councils' judicial powers transferred to courts
- Exam angle: APPSC 2007 asked when caste "began" as a new social organisation (after the Vedic period / Mauryan / Gupta / after Gupta). The printed key is garbled in your bank. Your notes date the crystallisation to the Later Vedic age.
### Features of caste — Ghurye's six and other lists
- Segmental division; hierarchy; restrictions on feeding and social intercourse
- G.S. Ghurye's six features (Caste and Race in India, 1932):
- Segmental division
- Hierarchy
- Restrictions on feeding and social intercourse
- Civil and religious disabilities and privileges
- Lack of free choice of occupation
- Other features list: Ascribed by birth, endogamy, hierarchy, hereditary occupation, commensal taboos, pollution, caste panchayat as a court
- Traits: Commensal taboos, pollution-by-touch, untouchability, Sanskritic rituals
- Dimensions: Vertical (ranking) + horizontal (grouping)
### Scholars → books and ideas on hierarchy and the tribe-caste continuum
| Scholar | Book / idea |
| Louis Dumont | Homo Hierarchicus (1966); hierarchy rests on pure vs impure; the religious status encompasses power (CDX); Homo Hierarchicus vs Homo Aequalis |
| McKim Marriott | Interactional approach; castes ranked by exchange of "coded substances" (food, water, services) |
| William Wiser | The Hindu Jajmani System (1936) |
| F.G. Bailey | tribe and caste = poles of a continuum (Tribe, Caste and Nation, 1960) |
| Surajit Sinha | tribes = "little tradition" dimension, understood only in relation to Hindu "great tradition"; secondary state formation: tribal chiefs taking Raja titles |
| N.K. Bose | The Hindu Method of Tribal Absorption (1941) |
| Ghurye | tribes as "backward Hindus"; contested by Xaxa and Elwin |
- ⚠ Pure vs impure = Dumont; coded substances = Marriott; continuum = Bailey
