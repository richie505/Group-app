### FR vs DPSP
- FRs are means, DPSPs are the ends - complementary, not competing
| Point | FRs | DPSPs |

### FR vs DPSP — differences, legal status and doctrine
## 1. Differences
| Point | Fundamental Rights | DPSP |
| Justiciability | Justiciable | Non-justiciable (Art. 37) |
| Nature | Mostly negative - restrain the State | Positive - direct the State |
| Aim | Political democracy; individual welfare [GK] | Social and economic democracy; community welfare |
| Obligation | Laws violating them are void (Art. 13) | Courts cannot strike a law for violating them |
- Main difference: DPSPs are not enforceable in courts, FRs are (UPSC 2009)
- Legal status: FRs are superior, but DPSPs are "fundamental in governance" (UPSC)
## 2. Harmonious construction - doctrine
- Approach: read Part III and Part IV together so both operate
- Relationship: FRs and DPSPs complementary, not competing - "FRs are means, DPSPs are the ends"; an integrated scheme (UPSC)
- Cases: Kerala Education Bill (1958), Minerva Mills (1980)
- Justice Chandrachud: the two together = conscience of the Constitution (UPSC)
## 3. DPSPs as interpretive aids
- Art. 45 → Art. 21A: Right to Education (86th Amendment, 2002)
- Art. 48A + Art. 21: right to a healthy environment
- Validity test: DPSP law abridging FRs tested against the basic structure + golden triangle (Arts. 14, 19, 21)
### Landmark cases → position: Gopalan (1950) to Sanjeev Coke (1983)
## 1. Landmark cases
- Champakam Dorairajan (1951): Fundamental Rights superior to Directive Principles
- Golaknath (1967): Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights
- Kesavananda Bharati (1973): Established the Basic Structure principle
- Minerva Mills (1980): Balance — Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles are equally important

### Material resources of the community - Art. 39(b)
## 1. Material resources of the community - Art. 39(b)
- State of Karnataka v. Ranganatha Reddy (1977): Justice Krishna Iyer (minority) - "material resources of the community" includes privately owned resources
- Followed in Sanjeev Coke (1983)
- Property Owners Association v. State of Maharashtra (5 Nov 2024):
- Nine-judge Bench (9-judge), 7:2; majority by CJI D.Y. Chandrachud
- Not every privately owned resource is a "material resource of the community" under 39(b)
- Test: resource's nature, scarcity, effect on community well-being, consequences of concentration in private hands
- Rejected the broad Krishna Iyer view
- Unamended 31C held to survive for 39(b)-(c) laws
- Caution: your notes and current-affairs files do not cover this judgment - check against a 2025-26 polity text
- ⚠ Krishna Iyer (1977, minority) = all private resources; Property Owners Association (2024, 7:2) = not every private resource
### Ninth Schedule and Art. 31B
## 1. Ninth Schedule and Art. 31B
- First Amendment Act (1951): Added Articles 31A and 31B and the Ninth Schedule — to insulate agrarian and land-reform laws from judicial review on Fundamental Rights grounds
- Enacted largely to overcome early judicial interpretations restricting state welfare and land acquisition measures
- Art. 31B (1st Amendment, 1951): Ninth Schedule laws shielded from FR challenge; meant for land reforms, grew to over 280 entries
| Case | Year | Rule |
| Waman Rao | (1981) | post-24 April 1973 entries open to challenge |
| I.R. Coelho | 2007 | No blanket immunity; post-1973 entries invalid if they damage the Basic Structure or the essence of Arts 14, 19, 21 ("golden triangle") |
- Reservation acts: SC held those placed in the Ninth Schedule are open to judicial review (UPSC)

### Article 31C — 25th Amendment (1971) to Minerva Mills (1980)
| Step / Stage, Year | What happened |
| 25th Amendment, 1971 | Inserted Art. 31C: (1) laws giving effect to Art. 39(b) and (c) not void for violating Arts 14, 19 (and 31); (2) a law declaring it gives effect to that policy cannot be questioned in court on that ground |
| Kesavananda Bharati, 1973 | Upheld the first provision/part; struck down the second provision / second part (exclusion of / bar on judicial review) |
| 42nd Amendment, 1976 | Extended 31C protection to laws implementing any DPSP (any DPSP), not only 39(b)-(c) (UPSC) |
| Minerva Mills, 1980 | Struck down the extension; 31C back to its pre-1976 position (39(b), (c) only) |
- ⚠ Trap (APPSC G1 2024): "first provision of 31C was invalidated in Kesavananda" is wrong - it was the second provision
- Art. 31C cover: only laws implementing 39(b) and (c) protected against Arts. 14 and 19 - narrow, not blanket, primacy
- PYQ: amendment protecting 39(b)-(c) laws from Arts 14/19 challenge = 25th (1971) (UPPSC 2009)
- DPSP primacy via amendments:
- Only 39(b)-(c) via 25th (1971)
- 42nd (1976): extended 31C to give precedence to all DPSPs over FRs (Arts 14, 19); also claimed unlimited amending power under Art. 368 (UPSC) → Minerva Mills struck it down
- ⚠ Trap (RPSC 2013): "the 44th Amendment gave all DPSPs primacy over 14 and 19" is false - the 44th did not give DPSPs precedence; the attempt was the 42nd; struck down (RPSC 2013: statement I false)
- After Minerva Mills: 31C restored to 39(b)-(c) (UPSC); also held an Emergency proclamation is subject to judicial review
- Property Owners Association (2024): original Art. 31C, as upheld in Kesavananda, continues in force
- Scope today: only narrow DPSP primacy - for 39(b)-(c) laws
