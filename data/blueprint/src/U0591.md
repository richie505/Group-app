### Rotation — two motions, direction, period, speed and data
## 1. Two primary motions
- Earth has two primary motions: rotation and revolution
- Rotation: Earth spins on its own axis — movement on Earth's axis
- Earth's axis: An imaginary line joining — through — the North and South Poles
| Motion | Meaning | Time | Produces |
| Rotation | The spinning of Earth on its axis — from west to east | 24 hours | Day and night — the daily cycle |
| Revolution | The movement of Earth around the Sun (direction: around the Sun) | 365.25 days | A year and seasons — with axial tilt |
- Aryabhatta: Explained the apparent daily motion of the Sun through Earth's rotation
## 2. Rotation facts
- Direction: From west to east — appears counter-clockwise when viewed from the North Pole
- Exceptions: only Venus and Uranus rotate the other way
- Period: Completes one rotation — a full rotation of 360° — in 24 hours
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say one rotation = 23 h 56 m 4 s
| Term | Meaning |
| Sidereal day | Relative to a star = 23 h 56 m 4 s |
| Solar day | Sun to Sun = 24 h - ~4 min longer because Earth also moves along its orbit |
- Angular speed: 15° per hour — 1° every 4 minutes
- Linear rotational speed: Maximum at the Equator (~1,670 km/h at the Equator, falls with latitude); zero at the Poles
- Results: Day and night (the main result); time differences and time zones; the Coriolis effect (force); a centrifugal effect
- Why not felt: Uniform motion (no acceleration) + atmosphere rotates with Earth
## 3. Changes in rotation
- Earth's rotation axis has shifted slightly over time; the length of day has changed by a very small measurable amount
- Ice-sheet melting redistributes mass and water toward lower latitudes — this redistribution can influence Earth's rotation and axis
- Climate change is slowing rotation: raises moment of inertia → slowing rotation; days longer by ~1.33 ms per century (about 1.33 milliseconds per century)
- Solar flares and coronal mass ejections affect the upper atmosphere — ⚠ not the primary explanation in the relevant question
- UPSC 2025: Tested that a shift in Earth's rotation and axis is under way
- ⚠ Sidereal day (23 h 56 m 4 s) is shorter than the solar day (24 h)

- Other evidences of rotation: Coriolis deflection of winds/currents; equatorial bulge; apparent east-to-west motion of stars and Sun
### Day and night — terms → meaning
## 1. Day and night
- Day: The part of Earth facing the Sun; night: the part turned away from the Sun
- Circle of illumination: Divides the illuminated and dark portions of Earth
- Caused by Earth's rotation on its axis — ⚠ revolution does not cause day and night; axial tilt alone does not either
| Term | Meaning |
| Circle of illumination / terminator | Line dividing day from night; also called "grey line" / "twilight zone" (APPSC 2019); passes through both poles only at the equinoxes |
- Unequal lengths: tilted axis (23½°) + revolution
## 2. The Equator
- Day and night approximately equal throughout the year — about 12 hours of day and 12 hours of night; seasonal variation in day length is very small
- The Sun is overhead at the Equator during an equinox
- No distinct winter or summer — generally wet and dry seasons instead
- Variation grows with latitude; Poles: ~6 months day + 6 months night
## 3. Midnight Sun
- The Sun remains visible around midnight — north of the Arctic Circle and south of the Antarctic Circle
- Strongest around the respective summer solstice — can produce 24-hour daylight
- Example: Norway (northern Norway, "Land of the Midnight Sun")
## 4. Twilight
- Shortest at the Equator: Sun rises/sets vertically
- Longest at high latitudes: Oblique path; weeks-long near the poles
### Effects of rotation — Coriolis force, centrifugal effect and tides
## 1. Coriolis effect
- Caused by Earth's rotation — the apparent deflection of moving objects or air (APPSC-G1 2019; BPSC 2022)
- Deflected to the right in the Northern Hemisphere; to the left in the Southern Hemisphere = Ferrel's law
- Zero or absent at the Equator; maximum at the Poles — increases with latitude and velocity (UPSC 2024: both correct)
- Proportional to 2Ωv sin(latitude)
- Applies to winds, ocean currents and cyclones
- Effects:
- Cyclones need enough Coriolis force → rarely form within 5° of the Equator
- Cyclones spin anticlockwise in NH
- Ocean gyres and current deflection
## 2. Centrifugal effect
- Caused by Earth's rotation — the centrifugal force is maximum at the Equator; minimum or zero at the Poles
- Causes equatorial bulging and polar flattening — Earth's shape resulting from this is an oblate spheroid
- Weight: Minimum at the Equator; increases toward the Poles; maximum at the Poles — rotation makes it so
## 3. Tides
- Tides: Each place passes through two tidal bulges daily → two high and two low tides (~12 h 25 min apart)
| Tide | Position |
| Spring tides | Sun-Earth-Moon in line (new and full moon) |
| Neap tides | Sun and Moon at right angles (~7 days later) |
- Spring and neap tides: Each occurs twice a month
- Perihelion/aphelion slightly alter tidal ranges
- ⚠ Coriolis: zero at the Equator, maximum at poles (opposite of rotation speed)
### Revolution — data and leap year
## 1. Revolution
- Revolution: Earth's movement around the Sun in an elliptical orbit — revolution changes Earth's position around the Sun
- Orbit slightly elliptical, Sun at one focus [GK]
- Period: 365.25 days — also given as 365 days, 6 hours, 9 minutes and 9.51 seconds (365 days 6 hours)
- Average orbital speed: 29.78 km/s (Orbital speed: 29.8 km/s; ~1 lakh km/h)
- Results: A year — the annual cycle (the major result); seasons along with axial tilt
- Centripetal force: Sun's gravity keeps Earth revolving
## 2. Leap year
- A normal calendar year: 365 days — the extra fraction is one-fourth of a day, which accumulates to one day in four years
- Leap year: 366 days — the extra day is 29 February (every 4 years)
- Century years only if divisible by 400 (2000 yes, 1900 no)
## 3. Rotation vs revolution
| Basis | Rotation | Revolution |
| Movement | On Earth's axis | Around the Sun |
| Period | 24 hours | 365.25 days |
| Direction / path | West-to-east | An elliptical orbit |
| Main result | Day and night | A year |
| Seasonal role | Indirect | Direct — combined with axial tilt |
| Coriolis effect | Has a role | No primary role |
| Centrifugal effect | Has a role | — |
| Time zones | Produces | Does not produce |
| Solstices and equinoxes | ⚠ Not the direct cause | Causes them along with axial tilt |
| Perihelion and aphelion | Does not cause | Associated |

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:2
### Rotation vs revolution
## Rotation
- Rotation: Earth spins on its axis, west to east, once in 24 hours; gives day and night
- Venus and Uranus spin opposite to Earth
- Spin speed maximum at the Equator, zero at the poles
- Equator: about 12-hour days all year; variation grows with latitude
- Coriolis effect comes from rotation (not revolution); it shapes cyclones
## Revolution
- Revolution: oval path around the Sun, about 365¼ days
- Perihelion and aphelion come from revolution; day and night, Coriolis effect, time zones come from rotation
### Rotation facts
- Rotation not felt: motion is nearly uniform; atmosphere and objects move with Earth
- Sidereal day (measured against a far star): 23 h 56 min 4 s, about 4 min shorter than the 24-hour solar day
- Climate-driven ice melt makes days longer by about 1.33 ms per century (mass moves towards Equator, higher moment of inertia)
- Axis moves mainly from mass redistribution: melting ice, groundwater loss; solar flares only disturb the upper atmosphere
### Evidence & names
- Foucault pendulum, Paris, 1851: slow turning of the plane of swing proved Earth's rotation
### Day & night
- Day and night come from rotation; circle of illumination (grey line) divides them
- Circle of illumination passes through both poles only at the equinoxes
- Six-month polar day and night: revolution with axial tilt (also seasons, solstices, equinoxes, opposite seasons)
- Midnight sun: Sun above horizon 24 h in summer, north of the Arctic Circle and south of the Antarctic Circle (e.g. Norway); most near summer solstice
- Twilight: shortest at the Equator (Sun rises and sets almost straight), longer towards poles (weeks-long near poles)
- Equator: seasons marked by wet and dry periods
### Coriolis effect
- Northern Hemisphere: turning to the right; Southern Hemisphere: to the left
- NH tropical cyclones rotate anticlockwise
- Coriolis force (2Ωv sin latitude): zero at the Equator, maximum at the poles, grows with wind speed
### Centrifugal effect
- Coriolis effect: moving objects turn aside
- Centrifugal effect of rotation: most at Equator, zero at poles; causes polar flattening (equatorial bulge)
- Weight minimum at the Equator (gravity felt least)
### Tides
- Proofs of rotation: Coriolis turning, equatorial bulge, apparent sky motion; Earth-Sun distance is not constant (oval orbit)
- Earth passes two tidal bulges daily: two high and two low tides; like tides about 12 h 25 min apart
- Neap tides: Sun and Moon at right angles; spring tides: at new and full Moon (aligned)
### Revolution & leap year
- Centripetal force keeping Earth in orbit: Sun's gravitational force
- Extra quarter day each year gives a leap day in 4 years: 366 days with 29 February
- Gregorian rule: century year leap only if divisible by 400: 2000 leap, 1900 not
### Exam corner
- Cyclone nearing AP coast usually pushed north-northwest (steering winds, trade winds, Coriolis)
