### Karachi Session, March 1931 — president, endorsements, resolution provisions
## 1. Karachi — 1931
- Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme resolution: Adopted in 1931 at the Karachi Session — Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel presided; Jawaharlal Nehru drafted it — associated with Patel and Nehru
- Karachi Resolution on Fundamental Rights: Addressed civil liberties, equality, freedom and citizens' rights
- Karachi Resolution on the Economic Programme: Addressed labour welfare, economic justice and worker protection — assigned responsibility to the state
## 2. Session
- President: Vallabhbhai Patel
- Context: Held days after the execution of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru - admired their bravery, disapproved of political violence
- Endorsements: Gandhi-Irwin (Delhi) Pact; reiterated Purna Swaraj
## 3. Resolution provisions
- Political: Civil liberties, universal adult franchise, religious neutrality, free primary education
- Economic: Rent/revenue cuts, debt relief, state control of key industries

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:239
### Karachi Session, March 1931
- Presided by Vallabhbhai Patel; Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme resolution drafted by Jawaharlal Nehru
- Endorsed the Gandhi-Irwin Pact
- Resolution included civil liberties, universal adult franchise, religious neutrality, free primary education
- Foreshadowed Constitution Parts III (Fundamental Rights) and IV (DPSP)
- On Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru — admired their bravery, but rejected political violence
- Purna Swaraj = Complete Independence
### Second RTC, 1931
- Gandhi — sole Congress representative; only RTC he attended (1931, not 1932)
- Sarojini Naidu represented Indian women; Malaviya also attended
- First RTC (1930) boycotted by Congress; Gandhi absent from the Third (1932)
- V.V. Jogayya Pantulu — helped Gandhi; separate-Andhra resolution handed to Gandhi there
- Failed over separate electorates for minorities and depressed classes → MacDonald threatened a one-sided award
### Resumption to withdrawal
- Mass Civil Disobedience suspended 14 July 1933
- ★ CDM formally withdrawn April 1934; Congress then contested the 1934 Central Assembly elections
### Why the CDM ended
- CDM resumed January 1932 after Gandhi returned from the Second RTC — Gandhi arrested, Congress banned, harsh ordinances
