### Whistle Blowers Act 2014
- Whistle-blower: any public servant or other person (incl. NGO) making a public interest disclosure
- Identity confidential; complaint within 7 years; no anonymous ones
- Revealing identity: 3 years + ₹50,000; false complaint: 2 years + ₹30,000
- Authority: CVC (Centre); Union Ministers → PM; State Ministers → CM
- 2015 Bill passed LS; lapsed 2019; SPG excluded

### Prevention of Corruption Act 1988 and the 2018 amendment → provisions
## 1. PCA 1988 - scope
- Origin: Tougher bribery laws (Santhanam Committee) → consolidated as PCA 1988
- Public servant (Sec 2(c)): Any government or local-authority employee, any Judge, or anyone whose office requires a public duty
## 2. Prevention of Corruption (Amendment) Act, 2018
- Purpose: Align with UNCAC
- UNCAC adopted 2003; signed by India 2005; India ratified it in 2011
| Change | Detail |
| Bribe-giving | Made a direct offence, up to 7 years. A coerced bribe-giver is protected if they report within 7 days[GK] |
| Commercial organisations | Liable/punishable for bribes offered on their behalf; offence framed around "undue advantage" (LENS Jul 2026) |
| Sec 17A | Government's prior approval needed before inquiry or investigation into a public servant's decisions in official functions (VIS 2026) |
| Punishment | Bribed public servant = 3 to 7 years + fine |
| Trial | Within 2 years, extendable to a maximum of 4 years [GK] |
- ⚠ Trap: UNCAC 2003 adopted → 2005 signed → 2011 ratified
### Sanction for prosecution — Sections 17A, 19, 218 BNSS and cases
## 1. Sanction stages
| Stage | Provision |
| Before inquiry or investigation | Sec 17A PCA, government approval |
| Before a court takes cognizance | Sec 19 PCA, prior sanction; covers retired public servants since 2018 [GK] |
| General protection for public servants | Sec 218 BNSS (earlier Sec 197 CrPC; BNSS in force from 1 July 2024) [GK] |
- Exam angle: 17A (approval before investigation) and 19 (sanction before prosecution) are often swapped in statements.
## 2. Cases → held
| Case | Held |
| Vineet Narain (1997) | Struck down prior-sanction rules |
| Subramanian Swamy (2014) | Struck down prior-sanction rules |

## 2. Definition and competent authority
- Whistleblower / whistle-blower: A whistle-blower is any public servant or other person (including an NGO) who makes a public interest disclosure to the public or a higher authority (APPSC 2019 key)
- On corruption, wilful misuse of power/discretion or a criminal offence by a public servant

| MPs (not Ministers) | Chairman, Rajya Sabha / Speaker, Lok Sabha |
### Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014 and the 2015 Bill — Protections, complaints and penalties
## 1. Protections, complaints and penalties
- Protection:
- Act protects the complainant's identity: identity kept confidential (complainant's identity goes to the competent authority, which keeps it secret)
- Protection against victimisation
- Complaints:
- Must be made within 7 years
- No anonymous complaints
- False or frivolous complaints punishable
- Penalties:
- Revealing complainant's identity = up to 3 years + ₹50,000
- Knowingly false complaint = up to 2 years + ₹30,000
- ⚠ Trap: Identity revealed = 3 years + ₹50,000; false complaint = 2 years + ₹30,000
## 2. 2015 Amendment Bill and weaknesses
- 2015 Amendment Bill: Passed by the Lok Sabha in May 2015
- Would bar disclosures in RTI Sec 8(1)-type categories (sovereignty, security, Official Secrets, Cabinet papers)
- Lapsed in 2019 with the 16th Lok Sabha

### Whistle Blowers Act 2014

- Whistleblower — any public servant or other person, incl. NGO, making public interest disclosure to the Competent Authority
- SPG (Special Protection Group) — excluded from the rules
| Complained against | Competent authority |
| Union Minister | Prime Minister |
| Central employees | CVC |
| State Ministers | Chief Minister |
| MPs | Rajya Sabha Chairman / Lok Sabha Speaker |
## Protection and penalties
- Anonymous complaints not accepted as a rule; identity given to authority, kept confidential
- Revealing complainant's identity — up to 3 years + fine up to ₹50,000
- Knowingly false complaint — up to 2 years + ₹30,000
- Amendment Bill 2015 — would bar Section 8(1)-type disclosures; lapsed with 16th Lok Sabha (2019)
- Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013 — every State to set up Lokayukta within one year
