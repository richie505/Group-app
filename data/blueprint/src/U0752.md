### Organisations — IAEA, safeguards, NSG and export-control regimes — IAEA
## 1. IAEA
- IAEA: Founded 1957; HQ Vienna, Austria (APPSC 2021: Vienna)
- Status: autonomous body in the UN system; ~180 members; India a founding member (joined 1957)
- Functions:
- Safeguards and verification (reports non-compliance to the UNSC)
- Nuclear security (ITDB database)
## 2. India's safeguards
- Separation plan (2006): under the Indo-US deal India separated civil and military facilities; placed civil reactors under IAEA safeguards so NSG members could supply fuel
- Imported fuel: goes only to safeguarded reactors/PHWRs (e.g. Canada, Australia deals)
- India-Specific Safeguards Agreement: signed after the 2008 India-US deal (some notes date it 2008, others 2009)
- Additional Protocol: signed 2009, in force 2014; wider IAEA inspection access, limited to India's declared civilian facilities
### Organisations — IAEA, safeguards, NSG and export-control regimes — Nuclear Suppliers Group (NSG)
## 1. Nuclear Suppliers Group (NSG)
- Origin: Formed 1975 (the "London Club") in response to India's 1974 Pokhran-I test
- Structure: 48 members; works by consensus; no permanent secretariat
- India's bid: Applied in 2016; India not yet a member - blocked mainly by China (objects on NPT grounds: India is not an NPT party)
- Australia reaffirmed support for India's NSG bid (Jul 2026)
- Membership benefits: Access to latest technology (assured technology access) + say in rule-making (export rules)
- ⚠ Trap: NSG membership does not make a country an NPT member (UPSC 2018)
## 2. Other export-control regimes
- India's memberships: MTCR (2016), Wassenaar Arrangement (2017), Australia Group (2018); NSG still pending
- ⚠ MTCR 2016 → Wassenaar 2017 → Australia Group 2018; NSG bid 2016 still pending
### Treaties → content and India's stand
| Treaty | Content | India |
| NPT (1968; in force 1970) | Only the P5 are nuclear-weapon states | Not signed: calls it discriminatory (LENS Jul 2026) |
| CTBT (1996) | Bans all nuclear test explosions | Not signed: no time-bound disarmament; subcritical tests still allowed (LENS) |
| PTBT (1963) | Bans tests in air, space and underwater | Signed and ratified[GK] |
| TPNW (in force 2021) | First binding treaty banning nuclear weapons | Not signed: adds nothing to customary law (LENS) |
- NPT: India has never signed it - treats India's post-1970 weapons capability as outside the treaty → India pursued full fuel-cycle independence
- Doctrine: India follows No First Use (UPSC IR)
### Acts — nuclear liability, CLNDA 2010 to the SHANTI Act 2025
## 1. CLNDA 2010
- Operator liability: no-fault, capped at ₹1,500 crore; the Centre pays beyond the cap
- Supplier recourse: Section 17 / Sec 17(b) right of recourse against suppliers for defective equipment; Sec 46 kept other civil laws open
- Indian Nuclear Insurance Pool: created to implement it / cover suppliers
- Bodies: Nuclear Damage Claims Commission; Nuclear Liability Fund
## 2. SHANTI Act 2025
| SHANTI Act stage | Date |
| Introduced, Lok Sabha | 15 Dec 2025 |
| Presidential assent | 20 Dec 2025 |
- SHANTI Act 2025 repealed CLNDA: Repeals the Atomic Energy Act 1962 and CLNDA 2010
- Ends NPCIL monopoly: Indian private firms and JVs can build, own, operate
- Foreign-incorporated firms excluded; FDI capped at 49% (Bill, Dec 2025)
- Liability:
- Graded caps ₹3,000 crore (large) / ₹1,500 crore (medium) / ₹100 crore (SMRs)
- Total capped at 300 million SDR (~₹3,900 crore)
- Excess via a Nuclear Liability Fund
- Bodies: APTEL (Appellate Tribunal for Electricity) as appellate authority; Nuclear Damage Claims Commission; Atomic Energy Redressal Advisory Council
- Patents: Patents Act s.4 amended to allow peaceful nuclear patents
- Government keeps: enrichment, spent-fuel reprocessing, heavy water
- ⚠ CLNDA Sec 17(b) = supplier recourse; SHANTI = supplier liability removed
### Nuclear safety — AERB, zones around a reactor, safety terms — AERB - origin, status, powers
## 1. AERB - origin, status, powers
- Set up: 15 Nov 1983 by executive order under the Atomic Energy Act 1962; worked under the DAE
- Independence debate: a regulator under the DAE (the promoter) is not independent; Nuclear Safety Regulatory Authority Bill never enacted
- Now statutory: SHANTI Act 2025 made the AERB statutory, answerable to Parliament (Jan 2026); the "no separate Act" note is outdated
- Mandate & powers:
- Licenses plants and fuel-cycle facilities; reviews design, construction, operation and decommissioning
- Radiation safety in medicine/industry; waste management; transport of radioactive material
- Inspections; can suspend or revoke licences
## 2. Zones around a reactor
- Exclusion zone: A radius of 1–1.5 km around a reactor — public habitation is prohibited
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say no habitation within 1.6 km of the reactor, under NPCIL/AERB control
- Emergency Planning Zone (EPZ): Concentric zones for protective actions — 16 km
| Zone | Distance / rule |
| Exclusion zone | 1–1.5 km radius; public habitation prohibited |
| Emergency planning zone | 16 km |
- ⚠ Trap: APPSC-GS 2022 key = 1 km (options 1/1.5/2/2.5 km) → expect "1 km" in APPSC's framing
### Nuclear safety — AERB, zones around a reactor, safety terms — Safety practice
## 1. Safety practice
- Design: Defence in Depth; ALARA principle
- Dose limits:
- Occupational 20 mSv/yr averaged over 5 years
- 30 mSv max in a year
- 100 mSv cumulative
- Post-Fukushima: 700 MW PHWRs carry advanced safety features; releases kept <1% of AERB limits (2026)
- Emergency preparedness: on-site + off-site plans, required by the Convention on Nuclear Safety (adopted 1994, in force 1996; India is a party)

### Nuclear security — dirty bombs, Nuclear Security Summits, ICONS
## 1. Dirty bomb
- Dirty bomb (radiological dispersal device): Conventional explosive that spreads radioactive material
- Sources: usually medical or industrial, e.g. Cs-137, Co-60
## 2. Summits and conferences
- Nuclear Security Summits: Four held, started by US President Obama
- 2010 Washington → 2012 Seoul → 2014 The Hague → 2016 Washington
- 2010 summit: India announced the Global Centre for Nuclear Energy Partnership (now at Bahadurgarh)
- ICONS: IAEA's ministerial International Conference on Nuclear Security (2013, 2016, 2020, 2024)
