### Chronology and FIRSTs of the economy
## 1. Punch-marked coins
- Also called Aahat coins — early India; mainly silver; the earliest extant coins
- Date from the 7th–6th century BC to the Mauryan period
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say punch-marked coins date c. 6th-5th c. BCE [GK]
## 2. Chronology
| Development | Period |

| NBPW | c. 700-200 BCE |

- South India iron (dated earlier):
- Mayiladumparai c. 2172 BCE (AMS)
- Iron Age in the south c. 2100 BCE - before the Gangetic Iron Age

## Northern Black Polished Ware
- NBPW (c. 700-200 BCE): Followed Painted Grey Ware
- Hallmark of the second urbanisation
- Glossy deluxe ware used by elites
- ⚠ Painted Grey Ware came first; NBPW followed
- Rajghat (Varanasi):
- Pre-NBPW levels c. 3,000 years old
- PMC + Greek-inscribed seals
- Urban Varanasi dated to c. 800 BCE
### Punch-marked coins — features and coin terms
- PMC: Mostly silver (UPPSC 2010), some copper
- Called panas/karshapanas; standard weights
- Symbols punched on: sun, animals, trees, hills
| Term | Meaning |
| Karshapana / kahapana (Pali) | Standard silver (or copper) punch-marked coin |
| Nishka / nikkha | Gold ornament/unit, later a coin name [GK] |
| Satamana | Weight/value unit of 100 (mana) in later Vedic texts [GK] |
| Suvarna | Gold coin (Satavahana usage) |
- Satavahanas: First used inherited PMC → then die-struck lead, potin, copper, silver coins
- ⚠ PMC = mostly silver, not gold
