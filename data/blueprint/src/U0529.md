### Basel Accords — bodies, years and FIRSTs
## 1. Basel Committee
- Originally named the Committee on Banking Regulations and Supervisory Practices — established at the end of 1974
- Background: International currency and banking disturbances; the failure of Bankhaus Herstatt was an important trigger
- Secretariat and headquarters: At the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) in Basel
- Purpose: Improve banking supervision; promote financial stability
- Framer: Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) under the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) (UPSC)
- BIS est. 1930 → world's oldest international financial organisation
- Coverage: Capital risk, market risk, operational risk
## 2. Basel accords
| Accord | Year | Key associations | Focus / core content |
| Basel I | 1988 | Capital adequacy | Credit risk only; five risk buckets (0, 10, 20, 50, 100%); minimum capital standard 8% of risk-weighted assets |
| Basel II | 2004 | Capital adequacy; supervisory review; market discipline | Three pillars: minimum capital, supervisory review, market discipline |
| Basel III | 2010 (post-2008 crisis, reply to 2007-09 crisis); in India from 1 Jan 2013 | Stronger capital and regulation; risk management | More/better capital (CET1), capital buffers, leverage ratio, LCR, NSFR, counterparty credit risk |
- Basel III three pillars (as your notes phrase them): absorb shocks; improve risk management and governance; improve transparency and disclosure
## 2. Basel Accords vs Basel Convention
|  | Basel Accords | Basel Convention |
| Body | BCBS / BIS, Basel (Switzerland) | UNEP-administered treaty [GK] |
| Subject | Bank capital, liquidity and risk | Transboundary movement of hazardous wastes and their disposal (adopted 1989, in force 1992) [GK] |
### Data — capital adequacy norms, RBI vs BCBS
- India: RBI introduced CRAR in 1992 as part of reforms, following BIS standards; set at 9% under Basel I (1999)
| Norm | BCBS minimum | RBI norm |
| Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) | 4.5% | 5.5% |
| Tier 1 | 6% | 7% |
| Minimum / total CRAR | 8% | 9% (UPSC) |
| Capital conservation buffer | 2.5% | 2.5% |
| Countercyclical buffer | 0-2.5% | Framework since 2015, not activated (20 May 2026) |
| Leverage ratio | 3% | 4% for D-SIBs, 3.5% for others |
- RBI vs BCBS: RBI norm 9% stricter than BCBS's 8%
- Some notes list "Tier I 4.5%" and "total CRAR 9.5%" → 4.5% is the BCBS CET1 floor
- Actual bank figure: HDFC Bank CAR 19.6% vs regulatory norm 11.9% (19 Jul 2026)
- Oct 2025 proposal: RBI proposed (draft) lower differentiated risk weights for MSMEs and real estate under Basel III → lower minimum capital needs
### Terms → meaning: capital, buffers and liquidity ratios
| Term | Meaning |
| CAR/CRAR | Capital / risk-weighted assets |
| Tier 1 | Pure equity (paid-up capital, reserves, undistributed profits) |
| Tier 2 | Equity-debt mix (e.g. subordinated debt) |
| Capital conservation buffer (CCB) 2.5% | Buffer of 2.5% |
| Counter-cyclical buffer (CCyB) 0-2.5% | Checks excess credit growth; introduced in India by RBI in 2015; main trigger = credit-to-GDP gap |
| Leverage ratio | Tier-1 capital / total (non-risk-weighted) exposure |
| LCR >= 100% | Stock of high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) to survive a 30-day stress |
| NSFR | Stable funding over a 1-year horizon (2026) |
| AT-1 bonds | Perpetual debt with a call option |
- Leverage ratio: Caps lending relative to own capital → floor that stops balance sheets growing on thin capital (APPSC-GS 2019)
- HQLA levels: Level 1 HQLA = cash, G-secs, central-bank reserves; Level 2 = highly rated corporate bonds
- RBI deferred revised LCR norms to 31 Mar 2026
- Exam angle: match each ratio to its purpose (LCR = 30 days, NSFR = 1 year, CCyB = credit boom); RBI vs BCBS minimums.

### Acts and institutions — DICGC, Banking Laws (Amendment) Act 2025
## 1. Deposit insurance — DICGC
- DICGC: Set up in 1978 by merging the Deposit Insurance Corporation (1962) + Credit Guarantee Corporation
- Wholly owned RBI subsidiary
- Cover: ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank (raised from ₹1 lakh in the 2020-21 Budget, after the PMC Bank crisis) (11 May 2026)
- Covered: All commercial banks (incl. foreign bank branches), RRBs, co-operative banks
- Excluded:
- Deposits of foreign, Central and State governments
- Inter-bank deposits
- NBFC deposits
- Premium: Paid by banks, not depositors
## 2. Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025
- In force: 1 Aug 2025; 19 amendments across RBI Act, BR Act, SBI Act + Banking Companies Acts of 1970 and 1980

### PSB revival
## Recapitalisation and governance
- M. S. Verma Committee — 7 parameters to identify weak banks
- Indradhanush (August 2015) — seven-point plan for PSB capitalisation and governance
- Banks Board Bureau — from Indradhanush, working 2016; replaced by FSIB in 2022
- P. J. Nayak Committee — Bank Investment Company to hold Government stake; stake under 50%; professional boards
- October 2017 recapitalisation — ₹2.11 lakh crore (₹1.35 lakh crore via recapitalisation bonds)
- Recap bonds — Government gives bonds to banks, puts money back as equity; no cash spent now
- 4R — Recognition, Resolution, Recapitalisation, Reforms
- EASE — run by the IBA's EASE Steering Committee, not a board of FI heads
## Liquidity and deposits
- LCR — cash, G-secs, central-bank reserves = Level 1 HQLA; highly rated corporate bonds = Level 2
- DICGC covers all commercial banks incl. Indian branches of foreign banks; not inter-bank, NBFC, government deposits
- Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025 — 'substantial interest' ₹5 lakh → ₹2 crore; co-op bank director tenure 8 → 10 years
## Other
- UDAY (November 2015) — States took on 75% of DISCOM debt; RDSS 2021
- Budget 2026–27 — restructuring of PFC and REC; High Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat
