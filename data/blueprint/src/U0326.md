### Emergency types → source, grounds, approval, duration
- Source: Government of India Act, 1935 - emergency provisions
- Ordinance power (Arts 123/213): also a 1935 Act relic
| Feature | National (Art. 352) | President's Rule (Art. 356) | Financial (Art. 360) |
| Ground | "internal disturbance" → "armed rebellion" (44th CAA, 1978); can be declared on imminent danger; whole or a part of India | President satisfied, on the Governor's report or otherwise, that the state's government cannot be carried on per the Constitution | financial stability or credit of India or any part is threatened |
| Approval | Both Houses within 1 month, by special majority | 6 months per approval | within 2 months, simple majority |
| Duration | Continues 6 months, extendable indefinitely with approval every 6 months | Maximum 3 years | no maximum period - continues till revoked |
| Revocation | President any time; must revoke if Lok Sabha disapproves by simple majority | By the President at any time; no parliamentary approval needed | - |
- Art. 352 - Cabinet advice: Written advice of the Union Cabinet mandatory (44th CAA) → inserted as Art. 352(3)
- ⚠ Trap: UPSC notes wrongly say "new Article 352A"
- Art. 352 - special sitting: Notice by 1/10 of Lok Sabha members → special sitting within 14 days
- Art. 356 - also deemed under Art. 365 (non-compliance with Union directions)
- Art. 355: Union's duty to protect states from external aggression + internal disturbance and ensure constitutional governance → basis for Art. 356
- Art. 356 beyond 1 year: only if a National Emergency is in operation AND the Election Commission certifies that elections cannot be held (2026)
- ⚠ Trap: both conditions needed - some notes say "or"
### Time limits → one, two and six months
## 1. One, two and six months
| Period | Matter | Basis |
| One month | Parliamentary approval of a National Emergency — by special majority; reduced from two months to one by the Forty-Fourth Amendment of 1978 | Article 352 |
| Two months | Parliamentary approval of President's Rule and of a Financial Emergency | Articles 356 and 360 |
| Six months | Presidential vacancy caused by death, resignation or removal must be filled — the Vice-President acts as President in the interim | Article 62(2) |
| Six months | Maximum gap between sessions of Parliament — Parliament must meet at least twice a year | Article 85 |
| Six months | An Emergency continues for six months at a time — the extension cycle | Article 352 |
| Six months | State laws on State List subjects — the six-month post-Emergency timeframe | Article 250 |
- ⚠ 1 month = 352 approval; 2 months = 356 and 360 approval; 6 months = how long each approval of 352/356 lasts
- ⚠ Sources differ: President's Rule (Art. 356) must be approved within two months; once approved it lasts six months at a time
### Amendments → changes to emergency provisions (38th, 39th, 42nd, 44th)
| Amendment | Change |
| 38th CAA (1975) | Made satisfaction non-justiciable → later reversed |
| 39th CAA | Put the PM's and Speaker's election disputes beyond courts |
| 42nd CAA (1976) | "mini-constitution"; raised Lok Sabha/Assembly terms to 6 years |

### Effects of National Emergency — Centre-state relations, terms, Articles 358 and 359
## 1. Article 358 vs Article 359
| Basis | Article 358 | Article 359 |
| What | Automatically suspends the 6 freedoms of Article 19 | Suspends the right to approach courts for enforcement of specified Fundamental Rights |
| How | Automatic on proclamation | Needs a separate Presidential Order |
| Ground | Only war or external aggression (external emergency) — not armed rebellion | Both external and internal (armed rebellion) emergencies |
| Exception | — | Articles 20 and 21 can never be suspended under any circumstances (after the 44th Amendment; 44th CAA) |
- Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights but cannot destroy the Basic Structure — Kesavananda Bharati (1973)
- Lasting protection (Art. 358): acts done during the emergency cannot be challenged even afterwards
- Exam angle: LENS Jul 2026 MCQ - the statement "Arts 20 and 21 can be suspended under Art. 359" is wrong.
- ⚠ 358 = Art. 19, automatic; 359 = Presidential order, never Arts. 20 and 21

## 1. S.R. Bommai (1994) - principles
- Judicial review: Proclamation under 356 is subject to judicial review (mala fide or irrelevant grounds)
- Floor test: in the Assembly = only test of majority, not the Governor's opinion
- Assembly: may only be suspended until Parliament approves; no arbitrary dissolution; court can restore a dismissed government
- Basic features: Secularism + federalism; a state government acting against secularism can be dismissed

- No maximum period; continues until revoked; never declared in India
## Related rules
- Presidential vacancy (death, resignation, removal) — Vice-President acts; filled within six months (Art. 62(2))

### Key rules
- Emergency provisions and ordinance power (Arts 123, 213) — from the Government of India Act, 1935; suspending Fundamental Rights in emergency — Weimar Constitution
- 44th Amendment — Art. 352(3): written advice of the Union Cabinet mandatory
- One-tenth of Lok Sabha members give notice — special sitting on disapproval within 14 days
- 39th Amendment — election disputes of the Prime Minister and Speaker beyond courts
- Revenue sharing changed during Emergency — lasts until end of the financial year in which the Emergency ends
- Lok Sabha (and State Assembly) term — extended by law one year at a time, not beyond six months after Emergency ceases
- President's Rule beyond one year — needs both a National Emergency in operation and an Election Commission certificate that elections cannot be held
- S.R. Bommai — Governor's opinion not conclusive; majority tested on the floor of the Assembly
### Art. 358 vs Art. 359
- 44th Amendment, 1978 — Art. 359 orders cannot suspend Articles 20 and 21
- Art. 359 — suspends the right to move courts for specified Fundamental Rights; applies in external emergency and armed rebellion
- Art. 358 — does not apply to an Emergency on the ground of armed rebellion
### Amendments & emergencies
- 44th Amendment Act, 1978 — "internal disturbance" replaced by "armed rebellion" in Art. 352
- 25 June 1975 Emergency — proclaimed by President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed on ground of internal disturbance

### Effects

- President's Rule — Governor governs for the President; Parliament legislates; High Court unaffected
- AP's first and longest President's Rule — 18 January 1973 to 10 December 1973

### Emergency types → source, grounds, approval, duration
- ★ Article 352 — National Emergency proclaimed by the President when security of India threatened (war, external aggression, armed rebellion)
- ★ Internal disturbance — no longer a ground; replaced by armed rebellion (44th Amendment, 1978)
- ★ Proclaimed on written advice of the Union Cabinet; "Cabinet" appears only once in the Constitution — Art. 352(3)
- ★ Approval by Parliament within one month (originally two months; cut by 44th Amendment, 1978)
- Approval resolution needs a special majority (two-thirds present and voting); the Emergency can be revoked by a simple majority
### Effects of National Emergency — Centre-state relations, terms, Articles 358 and 359
- ★ President can modify distribution of finances between Centre and States
- ★ Centre can direct States on any matter
- ★ Art. 359 — President can suspend enforcement of Fundamental Rights except Articles 20 and 21
- ★ Fundamental Rights can be suspended during Emergency (Arts 358, 359); Right to life and personal liberty (Art. 21) never, after the 44th Amendment (1978)
- ★ Lok Sabha term (Art. 83(2)) — extendable by law one year at a time, not beyond 6 months after Emergency ends
- ★ Article 352 — deals with declaration of emergency
### Instances and FIRSTs — National Emergencies, PR in AP, Financial Emergency
- ★ Emergency on ground of "internal disturbances" — 1975
- ★ Emergency financial powers can include cutting salaries and allowances of government employees
