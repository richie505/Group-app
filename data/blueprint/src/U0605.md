### Pressure terms → meaning; instruments → what they measure
## 1. Temperature and pressure
- Increasing temperature → air expands → air density decreases → low pressure
- Decreasing temperature → air contracts → air density increases → high pressure
- Increasing — higher — altitude decreases atmospheric pressure — lower atmospheric pressure occurs at higher altitude
- Temperature differences create pressure differences
## 2. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Isobars | Join places of equal pressure (reduced to sea level) |
| Standard sea-level pressure | 1013.25 mb (hPa) |
| Pressure gradient | Fall of pressure per unit distance → sets wind speed (APPSC-G2 2011) |
- Air moves from high to low pressure
- Closely spaced isobars = steep gradient = strong wind (Mar 2026)
## 3. Barometer reading → weather
- Falling/steady fall: Low pressure → cloud, rain, storm (stormy/rainy weather)
- Rising: Fair, settled weather
## 4. Instruments → measure
| Instrument | Measures [GK] |
| Barometer (mercury, aneroid); barograph | Pressure - millibar/hectopascal |
| Anemometer | Wind speed - km/h, knots |
| Wind vane | Wind direction |
- ⚠ Anemometer = speed; wind vane = direction
### Wind controls — pressure gradient, Coriolis force (Ferrel's law), friction, geostrophic wind
## 1. Wind
- Horizontal pressure differences produce wind — the pressure gradient initiates wind; a greater pressure gradient produces stronger winds
- Earth's rotation deflects wind — the Coriolis force; latitude changes the Coriolis effect
- Relief channels and blocks winds; mountain relief modifies rainfall and wind
- Pressure belts control major global circulation
- Seasonal heating causes seasonal wind reversal
## 2. Coriolis force
- Cause: Rotation of the Earth
- Deflection: Right in the northern hemisphere (NH), left in the southern hemisphere (SH) (APPSC-G1 2019) = Ferrel's law
- Magnitude: Zero at the equator, maximum at the poles; directly proportional to wind speed
- Consequence: Cyclones form only beyond 5° latitude
## 3. Friction & geostrophic wind
- Friction: Slows surface winds (stronger over land); negligible in the upper air
- Wind modifiers: Coriolis force (direction) + friction (speed)
- Geostrophic wind: Upper-air wind blowing parallel to isobars when pressure gradient force is balanced by Coriolis force (no friction)

### Monsoon mechanism, ITCZ and seasonal shift
## 1. Monsoon mechanism
- Summer heating produces low pressure over heated land; a pressure gradient draws moisture-bearing winds
- The ITCZ shifts northward during summer — this northward shift helps monsoon circulation
- Winter cooling reverses the pressure pattern — seasonal pressure reversal, which causes seasonal wind reversal
## 2. ITCZ and seasonal shift
- Definition: Inter Tropical Convergence Zone = low-pressure zone near the equator where trade winds converge (APPSC 2019; APPSC-GS 2019)
- Seasonal shift of belts: About 5-10° north in the NH summer and south in winter → follows the overhead sun (apparent movement)
- NH summer: ITCZ shifts north over the Indian plains (monsoon trough)
- ITCZ shift over India drives the monsoon (UPSC)
- Winter: ITCZ migrates south of India
- + high-pressure cells over Tibet and Siberia → north-east monsoon
- Southward shift of the doldrums → heavy rain in southern states (bimodal rainfall)

### Jet streams and teleconnections → position and role; discoverer
## 1. Jet streams and teleconnections
- Subtropical Westerly Jet: Connected with winter weather and Western Disturbances
- Western Disturbances: Winter precipitation over northwestern India
- Tropical Easterly Jet: Supports summer monsoon circulation
- ENSO or El Niño: Generally weakens the Indian monsoon
- IOD: Modulates monsoon variability — a positive IOD can partly offset the effect of El Niño
- SST anomalies: Influence atmospheric circulation and rainfall
## 2. Jet streams
- Nature: Narrow, high-velocity upper-level westerly/easterly winds at 10-12 km
- Influence: Regulate monsoon onset and withdrawal
- Discovery (FIRST):
- First identified by Wasaburo Ooishi (Japan, 1920s)
- Widely known during World War II → noticed by high-flying bomber pilots
| Jet | Position / season | Key facts / role in Indian monsoon |
| Sub-Tropical Westerly Jet (STJ) | South of the Himalayas in winter; shifts north of the Himalayas in summer | Carries western disturbances; its withdrawal lets the monsoon "burst"; returns in October to end it |
| Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ) | Summer only, 5-20°N, June to early October (South China Sea to East Africa) | Comes with the SW monsoon; creates upper-air anticyclone and surface low over Indian plains; strengthens SW monsoon |
| Somali (Findlater) Jet | Low-level, off the East African coast | With the Somali current, intensifies / strengthens monsoon winds |
- ⚠ STJ = westerly, winter, western disturbances; TEJ = easterly, summer, strengthens SW monsoon
### Polar vortex
## 1. Polar vortex
- Definition: Year-round low-pressure, cold-air reservoir around the poles, held in by a circular jet; strongest in winter
- Layers:
- Stratospheric part: 15-50 km (seasonal)
- Role: Anticlockwise flow keeps cold air locked near the poles; strong vortex keeps the cold air up north and warm air to the south (APPSC 2018; APPSC-GS 2018)
- Breakdown: Rossby waves / sudden stratospheric warming weaken it → winds reverse, vortex splits → cold air spills into mid-latitudes (cold spells)
### Periodic winds — daily breezes → time and direction
## 1. Daily breezes
| Breeze | Time | Direction |
| Sea breeze | Day | Sea → hotter land (land heats faster) |
| Land breeze | Night | Cooler land → warmer sea (land cools faster) |
| Valley (anabatic) breeze | Day | Flows uphill by day |

- ⚠ Anabatic = valley breeze, uphill by day; katabatic = mountain breeze, downhill at night
- Coasts: Land and sea breezes both marked along AP's coast → moderate coastal temperatures; sea breeze cools coastal cities like Mumbai (Mar 2026)
### Monsoon — term, mechanism and theories → proponent
## 1. Monsoon
- A seasonal reversal of wind pattern — from the Arabic word Mausim, meaning season
- Main mechanism: Differential heating and cooling of land and sea
- Summer heating produces low pressure; winter cooling produces high pressure — seasonal pressure reversal produces monsoons
- Monsoonal winds reverse direction according to the season — seasonal wind reversal produces monsoons
- Summer: From sea to land; winter: from land to sea
- ⚠ Land and sea breezes are daily or diurnal, rather than annual; mountain and valley breezes are periodic
- Monsoonal climate: Wet and dry seasons; seasonal rainfall is associated with the Indian monsoon
## 2. Monsoon theories
| Theory | Year | Idea |
| Halley's theory | 1686 | Monsoon = giant land-sea breeze (land-sea breeze on a large scale) |
| Modern monsoon theory | 1957 | Built on the northward shift of the ITCZ + jet streams + Tibet plateau (Tibet heat island / heating) + ENSO |
