### Rowlatt Act, 1919 → committee, provisions, repeal
## 1. Background — Rowlatt Satyagraha
- Rowlatt Act: March 1919 — popularly described as "No Vakil, No Appeal, No Daleel"; Gandhi responded through the Rowlatt Satyagraha (the immediate context)
- Country-wide strike: 6 April 1919
- Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr. Satyapal: The Punjab leaders arrested on 9 April 1919
## 2. The Act
- Official name: Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919
- Basis: Sedition Committee (Rowlatt Committee) / Sedition (Rowlatt) Committee report, headed by Justice Sidney Rowlatt
- Passed: 10 March 1919
- Provisions: Extended Defence of India Act (1915) powers
- Arrest and detention without trial / without warrant (up to two years)
- In-camera trials by a 3-judge High Court panel with no appeal; special tribunals
- Repealed: Under Lord Reading, along with the Press Act 1910
- ⚠ Trap: passed 10 March 1919; repealed under Lord Reading with the Press Act 1910
### Inquiries — Hunter Committee and Congress committee
## 1. Hunter Committee (official inquiry)
- Official inquiry: The Hunter Committee — also called the Disorders Inquiry Committee; chairman Lord William Hunter
- Eight members — five British (incl. chairman Hunter) and three Indian: C.H. Setalvad, Pandit Jagat Narayan, Sultan Ahmad Khan
- Criticised Dyer's actions — described them as an "Error in judgement"
- Appointment: By the government (Oct 1919) to probe the Jallianwala Bagh massacre and Punjab disturbances
- Indian members (Chimanlal Setalvad, Jagat Narayan, Sultan Ahmed Khan) wrote a minority report more critical of Dyer
- Findings: Censured Dyer → relieved of command, but no real punishment
- House of Lords backed him
- Gandhi called the findings a "whitewash"
## 2. Congress inquiry committee
- Congress investigation: Conducted by the Congress Inquiry Committee — Gandhi was associated with drafting the report
- Body: Congress's own non-official inquiry (Punjab Sub-Committee)
- Members: Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Abbas Tyabji, M.R. Jayakar and Gandhi
- Findings: Dyer's firing = inhuman; martial law = disproportionate; demanded action against Dyer
- ⚠ Trap: Hunter = official (Oct 1919); Punjab Sub-Committee = Congress, non-official

### Jallianwala Bagh — reactions and impact
## 1. Reactions
- Rabindranath Tagore renounced or returned his Knighthood on 31 May 1919
- Shankaran Nair resigned from the Viceroy's Executive Council
- Montagu described the action as "Preventive Murder"
- Udham Singh killed Michael O'Dwyer — assassinated on 13 March 1940 at Caxton Hall in London
## 2. Impact
- Deepened hostility towards British rule; faith in British justice declined; strengthened the nationalist movement
- A major turning point in Gandhi's political career — an important background to the Non-Cooperation Movement
- The Amritsar Congress Session took place in December 1919
