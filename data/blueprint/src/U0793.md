### Waste — definitions, categories and biodegradable vs non-biodegradable — Biodegradable vs non-biodegradable
## 1. Basics
- Solid waste requires collection, processing and disposal
- Biodegradable waste: Decomposes naturally by microorganisms — includes sewage and domestic organic waste; biodegradable municipal solid waste includes food, organic and yard waste
- Non-biodegradable waste: Plastic, DDT, asbestos
## 2. Polymers by degradability
- Biodegradable: PHBV, Nylon-2-Nylon-6, polylactic acid, polyglycolic acid, starch, cellulose, proteins
- Degraded by microorganisms — eco-friendly; used in packaging, orthopaedic devices, controlled drug release
- Non-biodegradable: Most synthetic plastics — polythene, PVC, polystyrene, nylon, Teflon
- Persist in the environment; cause pollution; produce microplastics; landfill issues; harm wildlife
- Semi-biodegradable / modified: Some starch-blended plastics — partial degradation
## 3. Biodegradable vs non-biodegradable — decomposition and items
- Biodegradable: decomposed by microorganisms (mainly bacteria) into simpler substances
- Aerobic decomposition → compost
- Anaerobic decomposition → methane (CH4)/biogas
- Non-biodegradable: mainly inorganic; not broken down by microbes; accumulates in environment
| Item | Class |
| Food/kitchen waste, vegetable waste/peels, fruits, green waste, cow-dung, paddy husk | Biodegradable (decomposed by bacteria) |
| Paper, sewage | Biodegradable |
| Natural fibres - wool, cotton | Biodegradable |
| Metals - tin, aluminium foil, metal scrap | Non-biodegradable |
| Plastics (cups, bags, containers), glass bottles | Non-biodegradable |
| DDT, heavy metals | Non-biodegradable |
| Synthetic rubber / tyres | Non-biodegradable |
- Exam angle: "which is NOT biodegradable" - tin/metal/glass/plastic; "which IS biodegradable" - wool, peels.

### Disposal methods — urban India, RDF and waste → method
- Urban India methods: composting, incineration and landfilling - all used ("all the above") (APPSC-G2 2025)

### Solid waste rules → year → provisions (MSW 2000, SWM 2016, SWM 2026)
- SWM Rules, 2016: The key provision — criteria for landfill and waste-processing sites
| Rules | Key points |
| MSW (Management and Handling) Rules 2000 | Primary goal: promoting waste segregation (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| SWM Rules 2016 (replaced 2000 Rules; from 8 Apr 2016) | Extended beyond municipal areas to UAs, census towns, industrial townships, railways, airports, SEZs, religious/historical places; 3 streams (biodegradable, dry, domestic hazardous); user fees and spot fines; EPR for producers/brand owners; no untreated waste to landfill (CDI) |
| SWM Rules 2026 (replace 2016; in force 1 Apr 2026) | 4 streams: wet, dry, sanitary, special care (domestic hazardous); landfills only for non-recyclable, non-energy-recoverable and inert waste; landfill fee for unsegregated waste higher than the cost of processing; legacy dumps mapped with time-bound biomining and quarterly reports; EBWGR duty on bulk generators (LENS Feb 2026; TH 12 May 2026) |

- Environmental Compensation on polluter-pays principle
- Legacy waste: map all legacy dumpsites; time-bound biomining/bioremediation with quarterly reporting

### SWM rules compared
## Basics
- Solid waste — thrown-away material in solid, liquid, semi-solid and containerised gaseous form
- Biodegradable waste ~55–60% of Indian MSW
- Anaerobic breakdown → methane/biogas; aerobic → compost
- Expanded 5R hierarchy adds Refuse and Repurpose (or Recover)
- Biodegradable: sewage; non-biodegradable: plastic, DDT, asbestos, PVC (PHBV, starch, proteins are biodegradable polymers)
- Rural waste moving from biodegradable towards plastic, sanitary and other non-biodegradable waste
- Invisible e-waste — vapes, USB cables, LED accessories thrown in regular waste
## Rules timeline
| Rules | Key point |
| MSW (Management and Handling) Rules, 2000 | Primary goal: promoting segregation (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| E-waste (M&H) Rules, 2011 | EPR first introduced |

| SWM Rules, 2026 | 4 streams: wet, dry, sanitary, special care |

### Disposal method → waste
## MSW processing
- Collection chain: door-to-door → segregated transport → transfer station → MRF → processing/recycling → residual landfill
- Indore method — aerobic composting, turned at intervals; Bangalore method — anaerobic pits, alternate layers of MSW and night soil
- Vermicompost — made by earthworms, so a biological method
- Non-recyclable high-calorific dry waste → RDF / co-processing in cement kilns, not landfilling
- Incineration — controlled high-heat burning; pyrolysis — heating without oxygen
- Waste-to-energy: pyrolysis, plasma gasification; compost and vermicompost give manure, not energy
- Oilzapper — bacteria mix cleaning oil spills and oily sludge; microbial fuel cell treats wastewater; EPR = producer duty
- UV purification — kills microorganisms in drinking water
- Hydrogen — clean fuel; fuel cells emit only water vapour

### Waste — definitions, categories and biodegradable vs non-biodegradable — Biodegradable vs non-biodegradable
## Biodegradable
- Biodegradable pollutants — broken into simpler, harmless substances by micro-organisms (bacteria)
- ★ Sewage — biodegradable pollutant; also domestic garbage, urine, agricultural residues, cattle dung
- ★ Wool — biodegradable (natural fibre of keratin); also vegetable peels, fruit waste, paddy husk, cow-dung
- ★ Rubber — biodegradable (synthetic rubber is not)
- Biodegradable polymers — eco-friendly because microorganisms degrade them
## Non-biodegradable
- ★ Tin / tin scrap — metal, not broken down by microorganisms
- ★ Plastic, tin, lead — not destroyed by bacteria
- Aluminium foil, glass, synthetic rubber — non-biodegradable
- ★ Polythene — a polymer of ethylene (C2H4) (carbon and hydrogen); non-biodegradable, so bags cannot be destroyed
- ★ PVC (polymer of vinyl chloride) — not biodegradable; cellulose, starch, protein are biodegradable natural polymers
## BPA
- ★ Bisphenol A (BPA) — industrial chemical used in food-packaging materials; in polycarbonate plastics and epoxy resins
