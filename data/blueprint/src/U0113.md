# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:112 - Sultanate provincial and military administration - iqta system, dagh and huliya, army organisation
### Iqta system
- Alauddin = cash pay, dagh and huliya; Firoz = hereditary iqtas
- Iqta = land revenue assigned instead of cash salary; not ownership of land
- Holder: iqtadar / muqti (wali for a province)
- Muqti: kept troops, collected revenue, law & order; sent surplus (fawazil) to centre
- First grant: Hansi, by Muhammad Ghori to Aibak (⚠ other notes: Kuhram)
- Iltutmish institutionalised it (UPPSC 2010)
- Turkish, non-indigenous; transferable until Firoz
### Changes by Sultan
- Iltutmish — Organised system; iqtas to Turkish officers
- Balban — Tried to resume old soldiers' iqtas; khwajas audit muqtis
- Alauddin — First large standing army paid in cash; enlarged khalisa
- Ghiyasuddin — Inquiry into grants; unlawful grants seized
- Firoz — Iqtas hereditary; jagir revival hurt army efficiency
### Decimal army ranks
- ▲ Khan — 10 maliks (highest)
### Army & dagh-huliya
- Dagh (horse branding) + huliya (descriptive roll): Alauddin
- Stopped substandard horses, proxies; Sher Shah revived dagh
- Turkish: mounted archers, iron stirrup, armour; horses from West Asia

### Iqta system — definition, origin and FIRSTs
- Definition: Iqta = territory whose land revenue was assigned to an officer instead of a cash salary
- Holder = iqtadar/muqti (or wali for a province)
- Muqti duties: Kept troops, collected revenue, kept law and order, sent the surplus (fawazil) to the centre
- Fawazil: Surplus revenue left after the muqti's costs → had to go to the treasury
- Origin:
- First iqta grant: Hansi, by Muhammad Ghori to Qutbuddin Aibak; some notes say Kuhram
- Some notes credit Aibak with introducing it
- Iltutmish institutionalised it / built it into a system (UPPSC 2010 key: Iltutmish)
- Nature: Turkish, non-indigenous institution
- ⚠ Trap: "ancient indigenous institution" is false (UPSC 2019)
- Transferability: Iqtas transferable → muqtis moved periodically until Firoz made them hereditary
### Sultans → changes in the iqta and army pay
| Sultan | Change in the iqta system / mode of army pay |
| Iltutmish | Organised the system; iqtas granted to Turkish officers (Shamsi) [GK for Shamsi]; iqtas to officers and troopers [GK] |
| Balban | Iqtas to officers and troopers [GK]; tried to resume the iqtas of old soldiers; appointed khwajas (auditors) to check muqtis' accounts [GK] |
| Alauddin Khalji | First large permanent standing army paid in cash from the royal treasury; market control kept that pay adequate; enlarged the khalisa; tightened audit of muqtis [GK for audit] |
| Ghiyasuddin Tughluq | Inquiry into grants/jagirs; unlawful grants seized |
| Firoz Tughlaq | Back to assignments: reinstated iqtas and made them hereditary; the revival of the jagir let nobles control recruitment, promotion and discipline, which hurt army efficiency |
- ⚠ Alauddin = cash pay; Firoz = hereditary iqtas

### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Iqta financed the army + integrated distant provinces
- Ancestor of the Mughal jagir/mansab system
- AP angle:
- Vijayanagara: standing Siddha Sainyam (Nuniz: about 50,000; paid every 4 months) + feudal Amara Nayankara army
- Qutb Shahis: Siddha Sainyam + Jagirdar Sainyam; Ain-ul-Mulk headed the army (Thevenot)
- Kakatiya posts: Gajasahini, Ashwasahini; royal bodyguards = Lenkas
- Comparative note: Shivaji kept a standing army paid in cash, avoided jagir grants, set up Sindhudurg and Vijaydurg naval bases
# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:112
### Iqta system
## Origin
- Origin in the Caliph's empire: land granted to army officials for limited periods instead of a regular wage
- Turkish, foreign institution (not an ancient Indian one)
- First iqta in India: Hansi (some sources say Kuhram) — granted by Muhammad Ghori to Qutbuddin Aibak
- ★ Iltutmish introduced the iqta in the Delhi Sultanate and built it into a systematic administrative institution
## Nature and working
- Iqta = revenue assignment, not private ownership; given to officials in place of salary
- Funded the army: linked military duties with running assigned areas
- Muqti duties: keep troops, collect revenue, keep law and order, send the surplus to the centre (not exempt from law and order)
- Khalisa = crown land, revenue for court and royal household; inam = grants to religious men and institutions
### Changes by Sultan
| Sultan | Change |
| Balban | appointed khwajas (auditors) to check muqtis' accounts |
| Alauddin Khalji | large-scale cash payment to standing army |
| Ghiyasuddin Tughluq | improved postal system with runners and horsemen |
| Firoz Tughlaq | made iqtas hereditary; revived the jagir system (did not abolish it); returned to assignment-based payment |
- Before Firoz: iqtas periodically transferable between muqtis
- Firoz's revival adversely affected army efficiency
- Iltutmish and Balban paid through iqtas; Balban and Alauddin tightened control over the system
- Iqta = ancestor of the Mughal jagir and mansab system
### Decimal army ranks
- Ranks, lowest to highest: sar-i-khail → sipah-salar → amir → malik → khan
- Khan = highest rank
### Army & dagh-huliya
- Alauddin Khalji introduced dagh (branding of horses) and huliya (descriptive roll of each soldier)
- Sher Shah later revived dagh
- Purpose: stop substandard horses and proxy/substitute soldiers at muster
- Turkish military innovations: iron stirrup, horse-based warfare, mounted archery, armour, stronger fortifications
### Post & intelligence
- Ulaq = horse post; used even to bring Khorasan fruit to Delhi
- Dawa = foot post
- Alauddin Khalji's daily market reports: barids and munhiyans (munhiyans = secret spies)
### Land & revenue terms

- Mansabdars were paid through jagirs or cash
