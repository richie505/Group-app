### Articles 23-28 — classification, the four religion Articles and who gets them
## 1. Right to Freedom of Religion — Articles 25 to 28 (four Articles)
- Article 25: Freedom of conscience; freely profess, practise and propagate religion (Freedom of conscience and free profession, practice and propagation of religion)
- Article 26: Freedom to manage religious affairs (religious denominations)
- Article 27: Freedom from taxes for the promotion of any religion (taxes for promotion of any particular religion)
- Article 28: Freedom from attending religious instruction in certain educational institutions (religious instruction/worship)
## 2. Classification
- Classification:
- Right against exploitation = Arts 23-24
- Freedom of religion = Arts 25-28
- Nature: right to religion is a Fundamental Right (not a legal right or DPSP) (PYQ 2019) (APPSC 2019)
- Exam angle: clause-text matching; distractors are Arts 19(1), 26(1), 29(1).
## 3. Who gets them
- Art. 23 available to: citizens and non-citizens; protects against both the State and private persons
- Arts. 23 and 24 enforceable against private individuals too
- Available to: Arts. 25, 26, 27, 28 → all persons - citizens and foreigners alike
| Provision | Available to |
| Art. 25, 27, 28 | All persons - citizens and non-citizens |
| Art. 26 | Religious denominations or any section of them |
### Articles 23-24 — trafficking, begar and child labour
## 1. Right against Exploitation — Articles 23 and 24 (two Articles)
- Article 23: Prohibits traffic in human beings and forced labour
- Article 24: Prohibits employment of children below 14 years in factories and similar establishments (no child below 14 years / children below 14 employed in any factory, mine or other hazardous employment - factories, mines, hazardous work)
- Does not bar harmless/innocent work
## 2. Prep detail
| Article | Prohibits | Notes |
| 23(1) | Traffic in human beings, begar and other forced labour | Begar = forced unpaid labour exacted by officials/zamindars (British officers, zamindars); traffic covers buying/selling persons, immoral traffic incl. prostitution, devadasi system, slavery |
- Art. 23(2): State may impose compulsory service for public purposes (military/social service); need not pay, but cannot discriminate on religion, race, caste or class
- Art. 21 (life with dignity) = basis of mandatory victim rehabilitation
- Related DPSP: Art. 39(e)/(f) - tender age of children not abused
- Link with 21A: Art. 21A (free and compulsory education, 6-14 years, 86th Amendment 2002) + Art. 24 (child labour) together make the right against exploitation meaningful for children
- PYQ: answer "Articles 21A and 24" (APPSC G1 2022)

### Article 25(1) — text and terms → meaning
## 1. Article 25(1) - text
- Art. 25(1): "all persons are equally entitled to freedom of conscience and the right freely to profess, practise and propagate religion"
- Subject to public order, morality and health and to the other provisions of Part III (other FR provisions)
- Exam angle: quoted text of 25(1) → identify the Article (asked 2018, 2023).
## 2. Terms → meaning
| Component | Meaning |
| Conscience | Inner freedom to mould one's relation with God/creatures as one desires; includes the freedom to follow no religion |
| Practise | Worship, rituals, ceremonies integral to the religion |
| Propagate | Spreading one's beliefs to others - does NOT include a right to convert another person (it would impinge on the other's freedom of conscience) (UPSC) |
### Article 25(2) — State powers, Explanations and limits
## 1. Article 25(2) - State powers
- 25(2)(a): regulate or restrict economic, financial, political or other secular activity associated with religious practice
- 25(2)(b): provide for social welfare and reform or throw open Hindu religious institutions of a public character to all classes and sections of Hindus (UPSC)
## 2. Explanations and limits
- Explanation I: wearing and carrying kirpans = part of the profession of the Sikh religion
- Explanation II: in Art. 25(2)(b), "Hindus" includes persons professing the Sikh, Jaina or Buddhist religion (Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists) (UPSC)
- Hindu religious institutions construed accordingly
- Limits:
- Public order, morality, health
- Art. 25 additionally subject to other Part III rights
- State can intervene to root out social evils
- No forcible conversion
- ⚠ Explanation I = kirpan (Sikh); Explanation II = "Hindus" includes Sikhs, Jains, Buddhists

### Secularism — meaning and Indian model
## 1. Meaning and Indian model
- Constitutional meaning: equal respect for all religions + no discrimination on grounds of religion (APPSC 2019); no state religion (UPPSC 2005)
- Indian model: Sarva Dharma Sambhava = state neutrality + positive intervention for reform
- Roots: Ashoka's Dhamma (RE 7, RE 12), Nehru Report (1928), Karachi Resolution (1931)
- Constituent Assembly: rejected adding 'God' to the Preamble (1949)
## 2. Components and provisions
- Components: freedom of religion, equality of all religions, state neutrality, no religious tax, minority rights
- ⚠ Trap: NOT a component = "not applicable to irreligious persons" (APPSC-G1 2018); freedom of conscience covers non-believers too
- Related Articles: Arts. 14, 15, 16, 25 (freedom of conscience; profess, practise, propagate), 26 (denominations manage their religious affairs), 27 (no tax to promote a particular religion), 29, 30, 325 (no exclusion from electoral rolls on religion)

### Secularism — Indian vs Western model; theocratic state; basic structure
