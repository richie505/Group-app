# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:12 - Major landforms - fluvial landforms and river profiles
### River course → work → features
- Canyon = variant of a gorge, not the reverse
| Course | Dominant work | Features |
| Upper (youth) | Vertical erosion, steep gradient | V-valleys, gorges, canyons, waterfalls, potholes |
| Middle (maturity) | Lateral erosion + transport | Wider valleys, meanders, floodplains begin |
| Lower (old age) | Deposition, gentle gradient | Levees, oxbow lakes, braided channels, deltas |
### Profile terms
- Graded profile: smooth concave; erosion = deposition
- Base level: sea level = ultimate; lakes, dams = local
- Stream capture (river piracy): headward erosion diverts neighbour's upper waters
- Drainage divide: sharp in young relief (Himalaya), faint on old plateaus
### Gorge vs canyon
| Basis | Gorge | Canyon |
| Width | Same top & bottom | Wider at top |
| Rock | Hard rocks | Horizontally bedded sedimentary |
| Example | Chisapani Gorge | Grand Canyon (Colorado River) |
### Erosional landforms
- Potholes: eddies drill bed; large one below waterfall = plunge pool
- Incised meanders: uplift rejuvenates river
- Rills → gullies; dense gullies = badland: Chambal ravines, Chos of Hoshiarpur
- Dihang gorge: Brahmaputra enters India (Arunachal)
- River terraces: down-cutting into own floodplain → erosional; paired vs unpaired
### Depositional landforms
- Alluvial fan: coarse load at break of slope
- Natural levee: raised banks of sand
- Braided channel: bars split channel into threads
- Meander: outer bend eroded (concave), inner slip-off built (convex)
### Delta vs estuary
| Basis | Delta | Estuary |
| Process | Deposition | Erosion / tidal flushing |
| Coast | Shallow | Deep navigable channels |
| Example | Ganga-Brahmaputra (largest) | Western Ghats rivers |
### Delta types
- Arcuate
- Nile, Ganga
- Bird's foot
- Mississippi
- Cuspate
- Tiber (Italy)
- Estuarine
- Drowned funnel mouth
- Narmada, Tapi
### Drainage patterns
| Pattern | Form | Example |
| Dendritic | Tree-like, uniform rock | Northern plains |
| Trellis | Folded rocks; right-angle joins | Appalachians, Singhbhum |
| Radial | Outward from high | Amarkantak |
| Centripetal | Into depression | Loktak |
| Rectangular | Joints/faults | Peninsular rocks |
| Annular | Eroded dome rings | Structural domes |
### AP coast
- Godavari (delta = Konaseema) + Krishna deltas
- Minor: Vamsadhara, Penna
- Kolleru: freshwater, between deltas; WLS 308.55 sq km (⚠ older: 250)
- Pulicat: saltwater lagoon, AP-TN border
### Exam corner
- APPSC 2022: canyon = upper course
- APPSC 2018 trap: "gorges are extended forms of canyons" WRONG
- APPSC 2023 Himalayan deposition: braided channels, alluvial fans, levees (terraces excluded)
- APPSC 2022 badland: Chambal basin
- West-flowing Deccan rivers: estuaries (steep gradient)
- Sinking deltas: 7 deltas ≈ 57% of sinking area
### Landform types at a glance → formation and examples
## 1. Major landforms and their formation
| Landform | Formed by | Examples |
| Mountains | Folding, erosion, faulting and volcanism | Major mountains: the Himalayas, Andes, Alps, Rockies, Sierra Nevada, Aravallis |
| Plateaus | Uplift, volcanism, erosion and deposition | Major plateaus: the Tibetan Plateau, Deccan Plateau, Pamir Plateau, Patagonia Plateau, the Meseta |
| Plains | Deposition, erosion and marine processes | Major plains: the Indo-Gangetic Plain, the Great Plains |
| Valleys | Rivers, glaciers, tectonic processes and karst processes | V-shaped, U-shaped, rift, hanging and blind valleys — listed as river landforms |
| River landforms | Erosion, transportation and deposition processes | Gorges, canyons, waterfalls, meanders, deltas, alluvial fans |
| Glacial landforms | — | Cirques, arêtes, horns, moraine |
| Desert landforms | Wind erosion and deposition | Dunes, yardangs, mushroom rocks, playas, loess |
| Karst landforms | The solution of limestone | Sinkholes, dolines, caves, stalactites, stalagmites, blind valleys |
| Coastal landforms | Waves, currents and tides | Cliffs, caves, arches, stacks, beaches, spits, lagoons |
| Tectonic landforms | Faulting, folding and crustal movement | Rift valleys, horsts, grabens, fold mountains |
### River course → work and features; profile terms → meaning
## 1. Course (stage) → dominant work → features
| Course (stage) | Dominant work | Typical features |
| Upper (youth) | Vertical erosion, steep gradient | V-shaped valleys, gorges, canyons, waterfalls, rapids, potholes |
| Middle (maturity) | Lateral erosion + transport | Wider valleys, meanders, floodplains begin |
| Lower (old age) | Deposition, gentle gradient | Broad floodplains, levees, oxbow lakes, braided channels, deltas/estuaries |
- APPSC 2022: A canyon forms in the upper course (floodplain, alluvial fan, delta do not)
## 2. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Graded profile | Smooth concave long profile; erosion = deposition |
| Base level | Lowest level a river can erode to; Sea level = ultimate base level; lakes and dams = local base levels |
| Stream capture (river piracy) | Headward-eroding stream breaks into a neighbour's basin and diverts its upper waters |
| Drainage divide | Ridge separating basins; sharp in young relief (Himalaya), faint on old plateaus |
### Erosional landforms → formation
## 1. Landform → formation
| Landform | Formation |
| V-shaped valley | Rills → gullies → valleys by vertical erosion |
| Waterfall | River drops over a resistant rock band or fault step |
| Potholes | Rock fragments whirled by eddies drill circular holes in the bed (pothole-drilling); large one below a waterfall = plunge pool |
| Incised (entrenched) meanders | Cut deep into hard rock when uplift rejuvenates the river |
| Gully / badland | Gully = water-worn channel of semi-arid areas; overland flow → rills → rills merge and deepen → gullies; dense gully network = badland topography |
### Gorges and canyons; places → erosional landform
## 1. Gorges and canyons
| Basis | Gorge | Canyon |
| Nature | A deep, narrow, steep-sided river valley | A very deeply eroded gorge — more deeply or widely eroded; very deep and steep-sided |
| Formed by | River erosion — vertical erosion | Prolonged river erosion — intense prolonged erosion |
| Width | Almost the same width at top and bottom | Wider at the top than at the bottom |
| Rock | Forms in hard rocks | Forms in horizontally bedded sedimentary rocks |
| Example | Chisapani Gorge | The Grand Canyon |
- Relation: Both are forms of the V-shaped valley; canyon = variant of a gorge (not the reverse)
- ⚠ Trap (APPSC 2018): "Gorges and canyons are V-shaped valleys" correct; "gorges are extended forms of canyons" WRONG
## 2. Places → erosional landform
| Place | Region | Feature |
| Grand Canyon | USA | Cut by the Colorado River |
| Dihang gorge | Arunachal (Brahmaputra entry) | Brahmaputra enters India here; called "Grand Canyon of the Tsangpo" (Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon, near Namcha Barwa) |
| Chambal valley ravines | Central India | India's classic badland (APPSC 2022: Chambal basin) |
| Chos of Hoshiarpur | Punjab | Gullied badland |
- Exam angle: badland region picked from options (Chambal vs Rann of Kutch, Godavari delta, western coastal plain).
### Depositional landforms → formation; meanders and terraces
| Landform | How it forms |
| Alluvial fan | River leaving steep hills drops its coarse load at the break of slope, spreading it as a fan/cone [GK] |
| Natural levee | Raised banks on both sides of the channel built by sand deposited at the edges; a natural barrier between channel and floodplain |
| Floodplain | Flat land beside the channel built by fine alluvium dropped when the river overflows [GK] |
| Braided channel | Sand deposits in the channel split it into many parallel threads (riverine islands/bars) |
| Delta | Final depositional form at the mouth where the river meets the sea |
| Oxbow lake | Sand deposited on the concave side steepens a meander loop until it is cut off; the stagnant water left is the oxbow |
- Meander: Outer bend eroded (cliff, concave) vs inner slip-off side built up (convex)
- River terraces:
- Mark old valley-floor/floodplain levels; formed when a stream cuts vertically into its own floodplain
- Paired = same height on both banks; unpaired = one bank only (areas of slow uplift)
- ⚠ Trap: Made by down-cutting, so NCERT lists them among erosional landforms → APPSC 2023 key on "depositional activity of Himalayan rivers" = braided channels, alluvial fans, natural levees (terraces excluded)
### Delta vs estuary; delta types → river; largest delta
## 1. Delta vs estuary
| Basis | Delta | Estuary |
| Dominant process | Deposition | Erosion or tidal flushing |
| Mouth | A deltaic river divides into distributaries | Usually a single funnel-shaped opening |
| Example | The Ganga-Brahmaputra | Many Western Ghats rivers |
- Estuary: Partly enclosed river mouth with free connection to the sea (an ecotone); most formed in the Holocene sea-level rise
- West-flowing Deccan rivers: Form estuaries, not deltas - mainly steep gradient (hard rock, short courses)
- Conditions for a delta: Slow, steady flow so silt settles (not all rivers form deltas) + heavy sediment load + shallow sea + weak tides and currents
## 2. Delta types
| Type | Shape | Example |
| Arcuate (fan) | Most common, arc-shaped | Nile, Ganga (Sundarbans) |
| Bird's foot | Long branching distributaries | Mississippi |
| Cuspate | Tooth/cusp-shaped | Tiber (Italy) |
| Estuarine | No delta; drowned funnel-shaped mouth | Narmada, Tapi [GK] |
- ⚠ Nile = arcuate; Mississippi = bird's foot; Tiber = cuspate
- Largest delta: Ganga-Brahmaputra (Sundarbans: world's largest mangrove forest)
### Drainage patterns → rock and place
| Pattern | Form / rock / relief | Example |
| Dendritic | Uniform rock; tree-like | Rivers of the northern plains |
| Trellis | Folded rocks; tributaries parallel, meeting (joining) at right angles | Appalachians; old folded belts; old folded Himalaya; Singhbhum |
| Radial | Streams flow outward from a central high | Amarkantak (Narmada, Son, Mahanadi) |
| Centripetal | Streams drain into a central depression | Loktak lake, Manipur |
| Rectangular | Jointed/faulted rock; right-angle bends along joints and faults in hard rock | Joint-controlled plateaus; jointed peninsular rocks |
| Annular | Eroded dome with rings of hard and soft rock | Structural domes |
- ⚠ Amarkantak = radial (outward); Loktak = centripetal (inward)
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: Sinking deltas (Apr 2026)
- Seven major deltas (Ganga-Brahmaputra, Nile, Mekong, Yangtze, Amazon, Irrawaddy, Mississippi) = about 57% of world's sinking delta area
- Deltas cover 1% of land but hold ~6% of people + 10 of 34 megacities
- Main causes: groundwater extraction + reduced silt
- AP angle: Coastal plain
- ~950 km long, ~60 km wide, up to 150 m
- Built by Godavari (delta = Konaseema) and Krishna deltas
- Minor deltas: Vamsadhara (Srikakulam) and Penna (Nellore)
- Kolleru: Freshwater lake between the two big deltas; Kolleru WLS 308.55 sq km (2024-25 annexure; older notes say 250 sq km)
- Pulicat: Saltwater lagoon on the AP-TN border, well south of the Penna mouth near Nellore (some notes place it at the Penna mouth)
# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:12
### River course → work → features
## River courses
- ★ Peneplain — formed by rivers; near-featureless gentle plain worn almost to base level
- Main work: upper course vertical erosion → middle lateral erosion and transport → lower deposition
- Middle course: side erosion, transport, wider valleys, meanders — vertical erosion not the only work
- Canyon — upper course feature (strong vertical erosion, youthful stage)
- V-shaped valley — rills → gullies → valleys, mainly by vertical erosion
## Agents and landforms
| Landform | Formed by |
| Mountains | folding, faulting, volcanism, erosion |
| Plains | deposition and marine processes |
| Valleys | river, glacier, tectonic faulting, karst solution |
| Cliffs, caves, arches, stacks, beaches | waves, currents and tides |
| Arêtes, horns, U-shaped and hanging valleys, moraines | glacial erosion and deposition |
| Dunes, yardangs, mushroom rocks, playas, loess | wind erosion and deposition (desert) |
| Sinkholes, dolines, caves, stalactites, stalagmites, blind valleys | karst |
### Profile terms
- Stream capture (river piracy) — headward erosion breaks a nearby divide and takes its upper waters
- Graded profile — smooth, concave long profile; erosion and deposition roughly balanced
- Base level — lowest level a river can erode to; ultimate base level = sea level
- Estuary — partly closed river mouth with free connection to the sea; an ecotone
- Young fold mountains: Alps, Andes, Rockies; old: Appalachians, Urals, Pennines, Aravallis
### Gorge vs canyon
- Gorge — deep, narrow, steep-sided river valley formed by river erosion
- Canyon — a much deeper gorge; a canyon is a variant of a gorge (not the reverse)
- ★ Grand Canyon — a gorge carved by the Colorado in Arizona, USA; about 446 km long, over 1.8 km deep
- Dihang gorge (Brahmaputra entry to India) — Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon near Namcha Barwa
### Erosional landforms
- Plunge pool — large pothole below a waterfall; potholes drilled by eddy-whirled fragments
- Incised / entrenched meanders — uplift rejuvenates river; meanders cut deep into hard rock
- Badland topography — dense gully network (rills merge and deepen into gullies)
- Unpaired terraces — on one bank only, often slow uplift; paired terraces at same height on both banks
### Depositional landforms
- Braided channel — sand deposits split the channel into many strands with riverine islands and bars
- Meander: outer bend concave, erosional (cliff); inner slip-off side convex, depositional
### Delta vs estuary
- ★ Tapti forms an estuary (zone between river and ocean)
- Strong tides and currents remove sediment — they do not help delta formation
- Sundarbans — Ganga-Brahmaputra delta; world's largest mangrove forest
### Delta types
- Godavari forms a delta; Narmada and Tapi have estuarine mouths
### Drainage patterns
| Pattern | Control | Example |
| Dendritic | uniform rock; tree-like | northern plains |
| Trellis | folded rocks; tributaries join at right angles | Appalachians |
| Centripetal | streams to a central depression | Loktak Lake, Manipur |
| Rectangular | joints and faults; right-angle bends | joint-controlled plateaus |
### AP coast
- Minor deltas: Vamsadhara — Srikakulam; Penna — near Nellore
- Kolleru — freshwater lake between Godavari and Krishna deltas
- Pulicat — saltwater lagoon on the AP–Tamil Nadu border
### Exam corner
- India's classic badlands: Chambal ravines; also the Chos of Hoshiarpur (Punjab)
- Himalayan river depositional features: braided channels, alluvial fans, natural levees (not terraces — vertical down-cutting)
- West-flowing Deccan rivers form estuaries: steep gradients, hard-rock and short courses
- Sinking of deltas: groundwater extraction and less silt supply
