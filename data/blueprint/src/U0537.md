### Inflation — definitions; headline vs core
## 1. Basics
- Inflation: An increase in the general price level — decreases the purchasing power of money
- Deflation: A decrease in the price level; ⚠ disinflation: a decrease in the inflation rate
## 2. Headline vs core inflation
| Basis | Headline inflation | Core inflation |
| Represents | Total inflation | The underlying inflation trend |
| Food | Includes food | Excludes volatile food |
| Fuel and energy | Includes fuel and energy — food and energy | Excludes volatile fuel and energy — food and fuel |
| Stability | More volatile | Relatively more stable |
- Headline (Prep): Inflation of the whole CPI basket incl. food and fuel - the "raw", volatile figure; what consumers feel
- Core (Prep): CPI excluding food and fuel (and electricity) - shows the underlying, demand-side trend
- Food: Rise in food prices - monsoon, supply shocks; measured by the Consumer Food Price Index (CFPI), with 10 sub-groups (cereals, meat and fish, egg, milk, oils, fruits, vegetables, pulses, sugar, spices)
### Inflation — types
| Type | Cause |
| Demand-pull | Demand increases — excess demand (aggregate demand > aggregate supply): expansionary/fiscal stimulus, public spending, loose money, higher purchasing power (UPSC 2021) |
| Cost-push | Production costs increase — rising input costs (wages, raw materials, fuel, rupee fall) → supply falls, prices go up; e.g. West Asia oil shock 2026 |
| Bottleneck | A supply constraint — structural shortages: poor farm productivity, storage, distribution |
| Stagflation | Combines inflation, slow growth and unemployment (high inflation + stagnant growth/unemployment) |
| Export-led | Strong export demand drains domestic supply and pushes prices up |
| Skewflation | Inflation (prices rise) in a few items (e.g. food) while overall inflation is low / others stay stable |
| Disinflation | Fall in the rate of inflation, still positive (e.g. 3% to 2%) |
| Deflation | Fall in the general price level (negative inflation) |
| Reflation | Inflation returning after deflation → a sign of recovery |
- By speed: Creeping (below 3%), walking/trotting (3-10%) (around 3-10% a year), galloping, hyperinflation
- ⚠ Disinflation = rate falls but stays positive; Deflation = prices fall

### Effects of inflation and deflation — gainers and losers
## 1. Effects of inflation
- Purchasing power: Inflation decreases the purchasing power of money (ROCKET) — lowers the purchasing power of money (APPSC 2025) → a rising GNP/GDP deflator decreases purchasing power (APPSC G2 2025)
| Group | Who |
| Gainers | Debtors (money repaid at lower real value), producers, holders of real assets |
| Losers | Creditors, fixed-income groups (pensioners, salaried people without DA), savers |
- Rule: Debtors gain and creditors lose
- Regressive: Food is a large share of poor households' spending; food & beverages = ~37% of the CPI basket
## 2. Effects of deflation
- Purchasing power: Rises
- Deflationary spiral: Postponed buying → lower profits → higher unemployment → heavier real debt burden
### Economists → curves: the Phillips curve
| Person | Statement / contribution |
| A.W. Phillips (1958) | Inverse relation between unemployment and (wage) inflation → lower unemployment comes with higher inflation |

## Basic terms
- ★ Deflation — a lasting fall in the general price level of goods and services; negative inflation

- Stagflation — inflation with slow growth and unemployment
- ★ Economic growth usually comes with (moderate) inflation — rising demand pushes prices up
- ★ Inflation helps debtors — they repay creditors with money worth less; creditors lose
