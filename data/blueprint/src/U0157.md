### Danish East India Company — founded (1616), settlements, sale (1845)
- Founded: Danish East India Company founded 1616 → Denmark's entry into Indian trade
- First settlement: Tranquebar (1620), near Tanjore, south-eastern (Coromandel) coast
- Second major settlement: Serampore (1699) near Calcutta – focus of Bengal activity
- Legacy: Commercial impact negligible; important mainly for Protestant missionary work
| Danish settlement | Present location |
| Tranquebar / Tharangambadi | Tamil Nadu |
| Serampore (Srirampur) | West Bengal (near Kolkata) |
- Sale (1845): Denmark (financial difficulty) sold Serampore and Tranquebar to the British
- Governor-General = Hardinge I
- End of Danish presence in India
### Missionaries → contributions (Ziegenbalg, Schulz, Serampore Trio)
| Missionary | Contribution |

### Danish story
- Danish company founded 1616
- Tranquebar (1620) — first Danish settlement; near Tanjore on the Coromandel coast
- Danish-Halle Mission, Tranquebar (1706) — founded by the Danes; earliest lasting Protestant mission in South India
- 1845 — Serampore and Tranquebar sold to the British under Governor-General Hardinge I
### Missionaries
- Bartholomaeus Ziegenbalg (1682–1719) — German pietist working in Tamil at Tranquebar
- Benjamin Schulz — Telugu Bible: begun 1727, published at Halle (not Serampore) in 1746–47
- Order: Danish company (1616) → Schulz begins translation (1727) → published (1746–47)
- Serampore Trio — Baptist missionaries William Carey, Joshua Marshman, William Ward
### Other minor companies

| Danish-Halle Mission | 1706 |

| Swedish East India Company | 1731 |
- Swedish company — traded mainly with China; minor in India
### Legacy
- Danish importance — Protestant missionary, educational and linguistic work; trade impact tiny
- Serampore Trio worked from Danish Serampore to bypass East India Company bans on missionaries
- Andhra missions following Tranquebar methods: Lutheran — Guntur, Baptist — Ongole
- Jesuits — Catholic, linked with the Portuguese
