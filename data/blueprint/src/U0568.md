### Acts and rules on agricultural marketing

- Mechanisms of the 1966 Act: Open auction, standard weights, recorded prices, licensed buyers → correct farmer-trader bargaining imbalance

### Market institutions → functions and features
## 1. Agricultural Market Committees and market yards
- Process: Govt declares notified area → Market Committee constituted → committee establishes notified market
- Supervision: District Agricultural Trade and Marketing Officer (DATMO)
## 2. Rythu Bazaars
- Concept: Farmer sells directly to the consumer, no middlemen
- Start: AP government, 1999; functioning from January 1999 (PYQ 2018)
- Management: Managed by an Estate Officer; under the Revenue Department; outside the purview of Agricultural Market Committees
- ⚠ Trap: "District Collector is the coordinating officer" = wrong (PYQ 2025 key); the Joint Collector coordinates
- Pricing (PYQ 2025 key): Prices "fixed aiming to be around 25%" (relative to open-market prices) = correct
- Number: 124 Rythu Bazars functioning (2024-25); Survey 2025-26 analysis: 129 functioning

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:124
### Acts & rules
- AP (AP&L) Markets Rules, 1969 — made under Section 33 of the 1966 Act
- 1966 AMC framework — remained operative after the 2020 farm laws were withdrawn (November 2021)
- Model APMC Act — single market fee; registration in place of licensing; farmers' and consumers' markets
### How a regulated market is set up
- Step 1: Government declares a notified area
- Step 2: forms a Market Committee
- Step 3: Market Committee opens the notified market
### Market yard vs Rythu Bazar
- AP (Agricultural Produce and Livestock) Markets Act, 1966 — allows licensed buyers (does not end them)
- covers crops, livestock and livestock products; supports open auctions, standard weights, recorded prices
- Rythu Bazar — run by an Estate Officer, under the Revenue Department
- Rythu Bazar: farmers sell directly to consumers; regulated market yard: farmers sell to licensed traders
### Rythu Bazaars
- Joint Collector — district officer who links Rythu Bazars (not the District Collector)
- Prices set at about 25% compared with open-market prices (above wholesale, below local retail)
- Started by AP government in 1999; working from January 1999
- For perishables: Government procures through Rythu Bazars to steady prices (quickest market-intervention channel)
### Market fee & storage
- AP warehouse godowns (2025-26): 1,052 godowns (9.65 lakh MT); 643 operational (5.92 lakh MT)
- Main after-harvest gap: limited operational storage and need for more cold storage
### Schemes
- Agriculture Infrastructure Fund — farm-gate infrastructure at Rythu Bharosa Kendras; turning PACS into multipurpose facility centres
- Raitu Bandhu (AMCs) — pledge loan: keep produce in market-yard godowns and borrow on it; avoids forced sales
### Exam corner
- e-NAM — mainly helped in price discovery; not yet fully working for payments or inter-mandi trade

- ★ Rytu Bazaars — farmer-to-consumer direct sale yards, opened by AP Government in 1999
