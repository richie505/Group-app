### Battles → place, date, victor and source
## 1. Battle of the Narmada (Chalukya-Harsha)
- Victor: Pulakesin II (UPPSC 2016)
- River: Harsha's southward march stopped on the Narmada (UPSC 2003)
- Source: Aihole inscription of Pulakesin II (by Ravikirti); confirmed by Hiuen Tsang's account
- Date: 618 AD / 618 CE (JPSC 2016 key)
- ⚠ Trap: Must predate 634 CE, when the Aihole inscription recorded it
## 2. Chalukya-Pallava battles → place + victor
| Battle | Victor [GK] |
| Pullalur (near Kanchi, c. 618-19) | disputed - Pallava records say Mahendravarman I destroyed his enemies; Chalukya records claim Pulakesin II |
| Manimangala (c. 642) | Narasimhavarman I over Pulakesin II (also Pariyala, Suramara) |
| Vatapi (642) | Narasimhavarman I - capital sacked; general Paranjoti |
### Chalukya-Pallava rivalry → rounds and rulers
## 1. Rounds and rulers
| Round | Chalukya | Pallava | Result |
| 1 | Pulakesin II | Mahendravarman I | Pulakesin II advanced to Kanchi; won Krishna-Godavari land (Maruturu, Kopparam inscriptions) |
| 2 | Pulakesin II | Narasimhavarman I | Pallava victory; Vatapi captured (642 CE), Pulakesin II killed |
| 3 | Vikramaditya I | Narasimhavarman I / Paramesvaravarman I | Vatapi recovered (c. 655) [GK]; Pallavas claim victory at Peruvalanallur [GK] |
| 4 | Vikramaditya II | Nandivarman II | Kanchi taken three times but spared |
- Vatapi occupation: Pallavas held it about 13 years till Vikramaditya I
- End of rivalry: Rashtrakutas replaced the Chalukyas (mid-8th c.)
## 2. Chalukya occupation of Kanchi - Vikramaditya II
- Ruler: Vikramaditya II entered Kanchi (c. 740, reign of Nandivarman II)
- Conduct: No plunder - gifted gold to Rajasimheshvara (Kailasanatha) temple
- Inscription: Kannada inscription on its pillar
- Commemoration: Lokamahadevi's Virupaksha temple, Pattadakal - copies the Kailasanatha of Kanchi
### Titles won in these wars → ruler
- Paramesvara / Dakshinapatheshwara: Pulakesin II (Harsha war)
- Title assumed: Paramesvara (Parameshvara); also Dakshinapatheshwara (lord of the south) vs Harsha's Uttarapatheshwara
- Vatapikonda ("conqueror of Vatapi"): Narasimhavarman I; also called Mamalla
- Kanchiyamkonda ("who took Kanchi"): Vikramaditya II
- ⚠ Vatapikonda = Pallava Narasimhavarman I; Kanchiyamkonda = Chalukya Vikramaditya II
### Chalukya administration → units, agraharas, feudatories
- Urban centres: Vatapi and Aihole = urban + trade centres
- Agraharas: Brahmin land grants spread widely; run by mahajanas
- Feudatories:
- Rashtrakutas (Dantidurga later overthrew them), Alupas, Gangas, Sendrakas
- Rayalaseema: Renati Chodas - feudatories first of the early Pallavas, then of the Badami Chalukyas
### Women in administration → queens and princesses
- The Chalukyas frequently gave high-ranking administrative positions to women (PYQ: UPPSC 2007 key)
- Vijaya Bhattarika: Queen of Chandraditya (son of Pulakesin II) — issued two copper plates in her own name; a poetess; administered the kingdom after her husband's death
- Kumkumdevi: Sister of Vikramaditya I — her recommendation led to the grant of a village to a Jain monastery
| Woman | Role |
| Vijayabhattarika (Vijaya-mahadevi) | Queen of Chandraditya (son of Pulakesin II); governed + issued land grants in her own name |
| Queens Lokamahadevi and Trailokyamahadevi (of Vikramaditya II) | temple patrons → Virupaksha and Mallikarjuna temples, Pattadakal |
- Comparison:
- Kalyani Chalukya princess Akkadevi governed a province (Kisukad)
- Eastern Chalukya queens (Ayyana Mahadevi, Chamakamba) = temple builders, not governors
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- 8th-century Telugu inscription in early Telugu script at Pitikayagulla (Rayalaseema) → post-Badami Chalukya influence
- Records of Eastern and Kalyani Chalukyas in the Nallamala Tiger Reserve
- AP angle: Pallava-Chalukya wars made Rayalaseema politically active → rise of Renati Chodas, Banas, Vaidumbas
- Significance: Century-long rivalry shaped Deccan + Tamil politics and temple art (Kailasanatha → Virupaksha)

### Battle of the Narmada
- Aihole record of the Narmada victory — 634 CE, by Ravikirti; defeat of Harsha

### Chalukya-Pallava rounds
## Sources
- Pulakesin II–Harsha conflict: Aihole inscription (Ravikirti) + Hiuen Tsang (not the Kailasanatha inscription)
## The four rounds
| Round | Chalukya | Pallava |
| 1 | Pulakesin II — advanced to Kanchi; won Krishna-Godavari land | Mahendravarman I |
| 2 | Pulakesin II — defeated, Vatapi taken | Narasimhavarman I |
| 3 | Vikramaditya I | the Pallavas |
| 4 | Vikramaditya II — took Kanchi three times, spared it | Nandivarman II |
- Vikramaditya II entered Kanchi c. 740 CE (Nandivarman II's reign); title Kanchiyamkonda
- gave gold to the Rajasimheshvara temple; left a Kannada inscription on a pillar
- Pallava occupation of Vatapi: 642 to c. 655
- Badami Chalukyas replaced by the Rashtrakutas (Dantidurga), not the Cholas; Nandivarman II did not overthrow the Rashtrakutas
## Society
- Vatapi and Aihole were urban and trade centres
- Vijayabhattarika (Vijaya-mahadevi) — queen of Chandraditya; issued land grants in her own name
### Titles won
- Pulakesin II — Paramesvara (after Harsha) and Dakshinapatheshwara ('lord of the south'), against Harsha's Uttarapatheshwara
- Narasimhavarman I — Vatapikonda ('conqueror of Vatapi')
### Administration
- Renati Chodas (Rayalaseema) — feudatories of the early Pallavas, then the Badami Chalukyas
- Pallava–Chalukya wars → rise in Rayalaseema of Renati Chodas, Banas, Vaidumbas
### Women in power
- Lokamahadevi and Trailokyamahadevi — queens of Vikramaditya II; ordered the Virupaksha and Mallikarjuna temples, Pattadakal
### Exam corner
- Pitikayagulla inscription (8th c., early Telugu script) — post-Badami Chalukya influence in Rayalaseema
- Vijaya Bhattarika — ruled after her husband Chandraditya's death; Badami Chalukyas often gave women high posts
