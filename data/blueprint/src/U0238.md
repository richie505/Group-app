### Nehru Report and the Calcutta ultimatum, 1928
## 1. Nehru Report and ultimatum — 1928
- Nehru Report: Issued in 1928 — the All-Parties Convention; Motilal Nehru; provided a constitutional framework for India
- All Parties Conference committee; chairman Motilal Nehru, secretary Jawaharlal; asked for dominion status
- Ultimatum on the Nehru Report: Occurred in 1928 at the Calcutta Session — demanded acceptance within the year; failure to accept would lead to a mass movement
- Calcutta 1928: Accepted the Nehru Report with a one-year ultimatum
- ⚠ Trap: Purna Swaraj adopted only at Lahore - crediting Calcutta 1928 is WRONG
### Lahore Session, 1929 — Purna Swaraj, resolutions and FIRSTs
## 1. Purna Swaraj — 1929
- Purna Swaraj Resolution: Adopted in 1929 at the Lahore Session — Jawaharlal Nehru; adopted Complete Independence as the Congress goal
- Flag-hoisting and Complete Independence declaration: 31 December 1929 at Lahore — Jawaharlal Nehru; the national flag was hoisted on the Ravi
- Purna Swaraj Day: Observed on 26 January 1930 following the Lahore decision
## 2. Session and resolutions
- Venue & date: Lahore, December 1929; resolution passed 31 Dec 1929
- President: Jawaharlal Nehru; Viceroy Lord Irwin
- Resolutions:
- Rejected the Nehru Report's Dominion Status (one-year ultimatum of Calcutta 1928 had lapsed)
- Goal = Purna Swaraj (complete independence)
- 26 January to be observed every year as Independence Day
- CWC authorised to launch Civil Disobedience

### Dandi March, 1930 — demands, date, route, marchers, remarks
## 1. Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience
- Non-Cooperation Movement: Began in 1920 — involved boycott and non-cooperation; withdrawn after the Chauri Chaura incident (1922)
- Civil Disobedience Movement: Began in 1930 — Gandhi led the Dandi March, which began on 12 March 1930 and violated the Salt Law
## 1. Prelude
- Ultimatum: Gandhi's 11 demands / 11-point ultimatum to Viceroy Irwin (letter of 30 Jan 1930; sent/ignored 2 March 1930)
- Abolition of salt tax was central; Irwin refused
- Why salt: Universal necessity of rich and poor; salt tax/monopoly = most visible face of colonial exploitation/taxation
## 2. The march
- Start: 12 March 1930 from Sabarmati Ashram (Ahmedabad) with 78 followers
- APPSC 2025: start date = 12 March
- Route: About 240 miles (385 km) through Gujarat to Dandi (Navsari coast)
- Salt law broken: 6 April 1930
- Only Telugu marcher: Yerneni (Erneni) Subrahmanyam of Krishna district; later founded an ashram at Komaravolu
- Successors after Gandhi's arrest: Abbas Tyabji first, Sarojini Naidu second

### Gandhi-Irwin Pact and Karachi session — date, mediators, terms
## 1. Gandhi–Irwin Pact — 1931
- Signed on 5 March 1931 — Gandhi and Lord Irwin; associated with Congress in 1931; Congress withdrew the Civil Disobedience Movement
- The 1931 Pact provided for:
- Release of non-violent political prisoners
- Salt-making in coastal areas without salt tax
- Congress agreed to participate in the Second Round Table Conference
- Sarojini Naidu called Gandhi and Irwin "Two Mahatmas" — associated with the Gandhi–Irwin discussions
## 2. Date, terms, mediators
- Signed: 5 March 1931 (Delhi Pact); CWC authorised Gandhi
- ⚠ Trap: Dating the Pact to March 1930 is WRONG - correct is 1931
