### Bhakti and Sufi — common principles
| Principle | Bhakti | Sufi | Common? |
| Monotheism (one God, many names) | One God (Kabir: Ram, Rahim, Allah are one); Nirguna saints | Tawhid; Wahdat-ul-Wujud | Yes |
| Religious tolerance, universal love | Kabir and Nanak criticised both Hindu and Muslim orthodoxy | Love and the unity of humanity | Yes |
| Guru / pir (guide-disciple bond) | Guru is central | Pir-murid bond is central | Yes |
| Devotion over ritual | Bhakti over Vedic ritual and priests | Inner experience over the formal Shariat | Yes |
| Idol worship | Bhakti saints (Kabir, Nanak) criticised it | Sufis rejected it | No |
- Common principles: I, II and III only (monotheism, asceticism, tolerance) (APPSC-G1 2022)
- ⚠ Trap: Idol worship was NOT common - Sufis and Nirguna saints rejected it
- Also shared: Vernacular preaching, spiritual equality of all castes
- Exam angle: APPSC 2022 and 2023 both keyed I, II and III only (monotheism, asceticism, tolerance) - "accepted idol worship" is the trap.

### Nathpanthi Hath Yoga — practitioners and Sufi borrowings
- Practitioners: Hath Yoga practised by the Nathpanthis = Kanphata (split-eared) yogis of Gorakhnath's Nath tradition
- Sufis adopted its techniques → UPPSC 2019: both statements are correct
- Dialogue: Nath yogis and Sufis held spiritual dialogues; Sufis adopted yogic meditation
- Amritakund: Hath-yogic text translated into Arabic and Persian in the 13th century → gave Sufis breath control (all orders except Naqshbandis)
- Exam angle: "Hath Yoga practised by Nathpanthis and adopted by Sufis" - both correct.

### Reformers → impact on caste, including Andhra
## 1. Caste
- Priestly dominance: Weakened priestly and Brahmin dominance (Brahmanical monopoly over religion)
- Low-caste saints: Kabir the weaver, Ravidas the tanner, Sena the barber, Namdev the tailor
- Khanqahs: Welcomed all social groups; fed and sheltered the poor
| Reformer | Contribution |
| Ramananda | First medieval reformer to denounce caste outright; disciples Dhanna (Jat cultivator), Sena (barber), Raidas (shoemaker), Kabir (weaver) |

### Outcomes, limitations and debates
## 1. Outcomes
- Shared shrines: Sufi shrines = common religious and cultural centres; rulers sought Sufi blessings for legitimacy
- Result: Composite / syncretic Indo-Islamic culture
- Political echo: Early Sikh and Maratha awakening
## 2. Limitations
| Factor | Effect |
| Caste | Caste inequalities could not be eradicated despite Srivaishnavism (Ramanuja) and Veerashaivism |
| Veerashaiva violence | At times turned to physical violence (Himsa); spread by the "Sivapasa" (sword) |
| Sectarianisation | Later hardened into separate sects and panths |
| Orthodox pushback | Naqshbandi orthodoxy against Sufi-Bhakti liberalism |
| Women | Did not end purdah or restrictions on women's public life |
- Medieval norms grew more restrictive for women and lower castes: purdah, limited education and work
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Early feelings of national awakening among the Marathas (Tukaram, Ramdas and Shivaji) and the Sikhs
- Revived Hinduism around devotion; weakened priestly dominance; built a composite culture
- Criticism & debates: Egalitarian preaching did not dismantle caste; sects often hardened into new communities
- AP angle: Pre-colonial reform line - Veerashaivism / Veerasaivism, Srivaishnavism, Brahmanayudu (Brahmanaidu's Chaapakudu), Vemana, Veerabrahmam, Nasaraiah, Molla
# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:148
### Common principles
- Bhakti — devotion over Vedic ritual and priestly go-betweens; Sufism — inner experience over formal Shariat
- Shared: guide-disciple bond, devotion over ritual, vernacular preaching, monotheism, asceticism, religious tolerance
- Not shared: idol worship (rejected by Sufis and Nirguna saints)
- Impact: weakened priestly and Brahmanical dominance; khanqahs welcomed different groups; caste not fully abolished
### Persons of interaction
## Bhakti-Sufi meeting points
- Kabir and Nanak — criticised orthodoxy of both Hinduism and Islam; spiritual equality
- Sant tradition (Kabirpanth) — Kabir, Nanak, Ravidas, Dadu; blended Hindu and Islamic ideas
- Nath yogis-Sufis — dialogues, meditation, breath control (not confined to temple rituals)
- Hamiduddin Nagori — equated Nath 'Alakh Niranjan' with 'Khuda' in Hindi verse
- Dara Shikoh — carried on Akbar's Sulh-i-kul / Din-i-Ilahi syncretism; Ahmad Sirhindi attacked these
- Naqshbandi — opposed sama and musical practices (unlike Chishti, Suhrawardi)
## Rishi movement (Kashmir)
- Universal love, service to all castes, vegetarianism (though Shariat permitted meat)
- Lal Ded (Lalla) — Kashmir; inspired the Rishi movement
- Sheikh Nuruddin — Rishi movement; Basaveswara — Lingayat / Veerasaiva
### Yoga & music
- Gorakhnath — Nathpanthi Hath Yoga; followed by the Kanphata Yogis
- Amir Khusrau — introduced the Qaul (early qawwali)
- Carnatic — stayed tied to temple Bhakti; Hindustani adopted Persian, Bhakti and Sufi influences
### Vernacular literature
- Jnaneshwari — Jnaneshwar, Marathi (not Bengali)
- Bengali — Padavali kirtans
- Kaalagnanam — Potuluri Veerabrahmam
- Vemana (17th c.) — satire in common Telugu against birth-based inequality
- Saints used the people's speech, not Sanskrit alone — regional languages grew
| Saint | Background |

- Women — the movements gave room for open spiritual expression
