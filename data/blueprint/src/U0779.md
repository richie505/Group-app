### Project Tiger → launch, reserves, NTCA and AP
## 1. Launch and spread
- Launch: 1973, on advice of a task force set up by Indira Gandhi; WWF support
- First nine: Palamau (Jharkhand) among the first nine (first = Corbett)
- Start: 9 reserves, 18,278 sq km
- Now: 58 reserves in 18 States, 84,488 sq km = about 2.56% of India's area (~2.6%) (29 Jul 2026)

## 2. NTCA
- NTCA: Statutory body via 2006 amendment to WPA (WPA 1972); implements Project Tiger under MoEFCC
- Tiger Task Force led to it and to the WCCB
- Chair: Chaired by the Union Environment Minister
- Section 38V(3): Each State must prepare a Tiger Conservation Plan for every reserve
- Management Effectiveness Evaluation 2022 (MEE): By 2022, 51 TRs assessed, 12 "Excellent", average score 78% (Aug 2026)
## 3. AP and corridors
- AP - NSTR: Anti-poaching camps going up from 89 to 150 (30 Jul 2026)
- Corridor issue: 3rd railway line threatens Satpura-Melghat corridor
- Projects in tiger corridors need NBWL Standing Committee clearance (4 Aug 2026)
- ⚠ 9 reserves at start (1973) → 58 reserves now; NTCA = 2006 amendment, chaired by the Union Environment Minister

### Project Elephant → objectives, reserves and biology
## 1. Launch and objectives
- Launch: February 1992 as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme (some notes: "1991-92")
- ⚠ Trap: "Launched in 1990" is wrong (APPSC 2022)
- Objectives: Protect elephants, their habitats and corridors; mitigate man-elephant conflict; welfare of captive elephants
- ⚠ Trap: "Only wild elephants" is incorrect (UPPSC 2017)
## 2. Reserves
- Reserves: 33 Elephant Reserves in 14 States
- Overlap tiger reserves, sanctuaries and reserved forests (WPA 1972, Indian Forest Act 1927)
## 3. Biology
- Tusks: Only male Asian elephants; tuskless males = "makhnas"
- Gestation: About / up to 22 months - longest of any mammal
- National Heritage Animal: Declared 2010
- ⚠ Elephant = National Heritage Animal (2010); Gangetic dolphin = National Aquatic Animal (2009)
## 4. AP angle
- Koundinya WLS (Chittoor): Borders Tamil Nadu; AP's only elephant habitat
- Part of Rayala Elephant Reserve (Anantapuramu Circle) - AP's only elephant reserve (2024-25)
- Nearby: Brahmagiri-Nilgiri-Eastern Ghats Elephant Reserve - one of the world's largest Asian elephant populations
- ⚠ Koundinya = sanctuary (Chittoor); Rayala = elephant reserve (Anantapuramu Circle)

### Big cats → Project Cheetah and Project Lion — Project Lion
## 1. Project Lion
- Project Lion (2020): Conserve world's last free-ranging Asiatic lions (Gir) via modern surveillance, research, disease management
- Lion translocation debate: Kuno first chosen as second home for Gir's Asiatic lions
- Gujarat did not carry out the SC order → Kuno went to cheetahs
- Second-home debate: Barda WLS (Gujarat) proposed
- Critics: too close to Gir for a real metapopulation safeguard (6 Jul 2026)
- ⚠ Kuno = first chosen for lions, went to cheetahs; Barda = proposed lion second home
### Birds → Great Indian Bustard, vultures, Amur falcon
## 1. Great Indian Bustard
- Identity: Great Indian Bustard (Ardeotis nigriceps) = Godawan; Rajasthan's state bird
- Status: Critically Endangered (CR); fewer than 150 birds left
- Range: Mainly the Thar (Rajasthan); also Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka
- Main threat: Collisions with overhead power lines in solar and wind zones
- Conservation breeding: Jaisalmer, Rajasthan
- Programmes: One of MoEFCC's five new national species projects + on the Species Recovery Programme (2026)
## 2. Vultures
- Cause of decline: Vultures ate cattle carcasses with veterinary painkiller diclofenac → kidney failure (visceral gout)
- Decline noticed at Keoladeo, 1998
- Safe alternative: meloxicam
- Breeding centre: Vulture Conservation and Breeding Centre, Pinjore (Haryana); Vulture Safe Zones
## 3. Amur falcon
- Route: Breeds in SE Russia and northern China → flies to southern Africa
- Stopover: Huge numbers in Nagaland - Doyang reservoir; Pangti = "Falcon Capital of the World"
- Feature: One of the longest raptor migrations; non-stop crossing of the Arabian Sea
- ⚠ Diclofenac = killer drug; meloxicam = safe alternative
### Aquatic and coastal species → status, range and sites — Gangetic dolphin
## 1. Gangetic dolphin
- Name: Ganges River Dolphin (Platanista gangetica)
- National Aquatic Animal: Declared India's National Aquatic Animal on 5 October 2009
- ⚠ Trap: Not Olive Ridley, humpback dolphin or gharial (APPSC 2019)
- 5 October = National Dolphin Day
- Protection: Schedule I, WPA 1972
- Range: Ganga-Brahmaputra-Meghna and Karnaphuli-Sangu systems - India, Nepal, Bangladesh
- Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary: Bhagalpur, Bihar
- National Dolphin Research Centre: Patna
- Project Dolphin (2020): Covers both riverine and marine dolphins + other cetaceans
- New (Jan 2026): Dolphin Rescue Ambulance + Aqua Life Conservation Monitoring Centre (WII Dehradun, Namami Gange)
- Threats: Dams, barrages and water diversion; pollution; bycatch/entanglement in fishing nets; poaching and killing for oil; boat traffic
