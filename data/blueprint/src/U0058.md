### Varna, caste terms and marriage types — Satavahana varna order
## 1. Caste
- Satavahana rule: The fourfold caste system existed; the Satavahanas belonged to the Brahmin caste
- Samayamu: Caste associations during the Kakatiya period
- Kayasthas: The caste of record-keepers
## 2. Satavahana varna order
- Basis: Ancient Andhra society predominantly shaped by the Vedic varna system; Satavahanas played a crucial role (APPSC-GS 2024 statement pair)
- Brahmins: Tax-free villages; Nanaghat inscription mentions Brahmins trained in Vedic learning and ritual
- Vishnukundins: Followed varnasramadharma
- Vaisyas = internal + external trade
- Sudras (carpenters, sculptors, handloom weavers) = recognised place
- Farmers were "the backbone"; received land grants
## 3. Kshatriya claims
- Kshatriya claims by assimilation:
- Groups: Greeks, Sakas, Pahlavas, Kushanas (+ Kardhamakas, Western Kshatrapas) → Indian names/faiths → Kshatriya status
- Examples: Saka Nahapana's daughter Dakshamitra, son-in-law Rishabhadatta, Rudradaman
### Varna, caste terms and marriage types — Occupational castes → term
## 1. Occupational castes → term
- Caste by profession: Varna-sankara + occupational diversification → many sub-castes (Gathasaptasati, Kamasutra)
| Term | Occupation |

## 2. Marriage types and customs
- Nature: Marriage = sacred institution
- Polygamy: Among upper classes
| Type | Union | Offspring termed |
| Anuloma | Higher-caste man + lower-caste woman | Ugra |
| Viloma (Pratiloma) | Lower-caste man + higher-caste woman | Chandala |
- ⚠ Anuloma → Ugra; Viloma → Chandala
- Inter-caste marriages: Mentioned in Kathasaritsagara - caste not fully rigid at first
- Sati: Nagarjunakonda sculpture shows sati; Greek writer Strabo also noted it
- Widows: Expected celibacy

## 2. Status of women
- Family: Patriarchal with a joint family, yet women had considerable respect and freedom
- Public life: Joined men in festivals, assemblies and public life; took part equally with men in festivals and gatherings
- Donative inscriptions record gifts on such occasions
- Matronymics: Gautamiputra Satakarni, Vasishthiputra Pulumavi
- Titles from husbands: Mahasenapatni, Mahabhojaki, Maharathi
## 3. Learning
- Education/learning: Female poets (Hala's Gathasaptasati): Madhavi, Revati, Anupamalabdha, Anulakshmi
## 4. Women patrons → record
- Property and patronage: Large gifts to viharas and chaityas
| Woman | Dynasty | Record |

- Exam angle: Ikshvaku woman patron of Buddhism (Bodhisiri); matronymics; female poets of the Gathasaptasati.

## 3. Religion
- Vishnukundins:
- Family deity Sriparvataswamy
- Titles 'Paramamaheswara', 'Parama Brahmanya'
- Ikshvakus: Kings performed Vedic sacrifices (Vasishthiputra Chantamula - Asvamedha, Vajapeya); queens patronised Buddhism
- Brahmanical shrines at Nagarjunakonda, e.g. Pushpabhadraswami
- Amaravati reliefs: Popular worship of nagas, yakshas and sacred trees beside Buddhist symbols
### Sources → festivals, amusements, dress and ornaments
## 1. Literary evidence and festivals
- Gathasaptasati: By King Hala; most important literary source for Satavahana socio-cultural life (APPSC 2019)
- About 700 verses in Maharashtri Prakrit
- Everyday life: family, marriage, women, agriculture, trade, festivals, occupations
- 'Maiputa': Used by dancers = Kusuma Lepanam (APPSC 2019 key)
- Amphitheatre/stadium: At Nagarjunakonda (Ikshvaku capital Vijayapuri) for performances + games
- Exam angle: "most important literary source of Satavahana society" → Gathasaptasati (not Puranas, Aitareya Brahmana or Dharmasutras).
## 2. Food, dress and ornaments
- Evidence: Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda reliefs = main visual evidence of dress + adornment
- Men: lower cloth + turban
- Women: heavy anklets, bangles, girdles and necklaces
- Not in your sources: specific food items and named garments of the Satavahana-Ikshvaku age (add from a Satavahana culture chapter).
### Universal angles
- Significance: Early Andhra = Vedic varna order + strong Buddhist patronage, much of it by women
