### Foundation and the Charter of 1600
## 1. Foundation
- Established in 1600 — an English joint-stock trading company; received a royal charter from Queen Elizabeth I
- Early objective: Eastern trade — especially included trade in spices and textiles
- Bengal: The first major territorial base of the Company
## 2. Akbar and the English
- Akbar (lived — reigned — 1556 to 1605) was contemporary with Queen Elizabeth I (1558 to 1603)
- The English East India Company was formed in 1600 — during Akbar's reign
- Ralph Fitch: Visited India 1583–1586 — Agra and Fatehpur Sikri; the first English merchant to visit Akbar's court
## 3. Charter → provisions
- Charter: 31 December 1600 by Queen Elizabeth I (APPSC-G2 2025: EIC founded 1600)
- Name: "The Governor and Company of Merchants of London Trading into the East Indies"
- "Merchant Adventurers" had formed the association c.1599
- Monopoly: 15-year monopoly (later perpetual) - between the Cape of Good Hope and the Magellan Straits
- APPSC-GS 2024 key: exclusive trading rights (not territorial governance or legislative power)

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:154
### Charter of 1600
## The charter
- 31 December 1600 — charter granted by Elizabeth I; Company established 1600
- Formed during the reign of Akbar (1556–1605)
- Monopoly of East Indies trade for fifteen years, later made permanent
- Given trading rights, not territorial power
## Organisation and powers
- Run by a Governor and a 24-member committee (later the Court of Directors)
- Shareholders formed the General Court (Court of Proprietors)
- Charles II and James II gave powers: coining money, jurisdiction, making war and peace with non-Christians
- Cromwell — 1657 charter for a permanent joint stock
- James I sent Hawkins and Roe to Jahangir; Charles II received Bombay as dowry
## Early trade
- Indian goods paid for mainly with imported bullion
- Ralph Fitch — in India 1583–1586; visited Agra and Fatehpur Sikri; first English merchant at Akbar's court
- Farman of 1717 ("Magna Carta" of Company trade) — issued by Farrukhsiyar; increased trading privileges
- Other dates: 1757 Plassey, 1764 Buxar, 1773 Regulating Act
### Factories & presidencies
## Factories
- Globe reached Machilipatnam in 1611 under Captain Anthony Hippon — first English factory in Andhra (first temporary factory)
- Battle of Swally (1612) — Captain Thomas Best defeated the Portuguese near Surat, winning the English a factory at Surat
- Surat (1613) — first permanent English factory
| Factory | Power | Year |
| Masulipatnam | English | 1611 |
| Surat | English | 1613 |
| Surat | Dutch | 1616 |
| Surat | French | 1668 |
## Presidencies
- Madras (with Fort St. George) — founded 1639 by Francis Day
- grant from Chandragiri ruler Venkatapati Raya III, with efforts of the Damerla brothers (Venkatadri and Ayyappa)
- Fort St. George — seat of the Madras Presidency; Fort William — Calcutta (Bengal)
- Bombay — leased to the Company by Charles II in 1668 for an annual rent of £10; replaced Surat in the west
- 1698 — Company bought zamindari of Sutanuti, Gobindapur and Kalikata — basis of Calcutta
- Sutanuti — an English trading centre and Calcutta base; Batavia was the Dutch eastern centre
### Andhra coast settlements

| Pulicat (second English settlement) | 1621 |

- Armagaon — at Dugarajapatnam (now Tirupati district); named after Karanam Armugam Mudali
- Bhimili — first municipality in Andhra, 1861
### Farman of 1717 (Farrukhsiyar)
- Bengal trade duty-free for Rs 3,000 a year, with the right to issue dastaks
- Surat duties dropped for Rs 10,000
- Bengal — Company's first major territorial base, after Plassey (1757) and Diwani (1765); Madras and Bombay were trading settlements
### Dastak misuse to rule
- John Surman — led the mission to Farrukhsiyar that secured the Farman of 1717
- Dastak misuse by Company servants for private trade — drained the Nawab of Bengal's revenue; a deeper cause of Plassey
- Battle of Buxar — 1764 (after Plassey 1757; Company rule ended 1858)
### Other milestones
- Early objective — eastern trade, especially spices and textiles; territorial rule over Bengal only after 1757
- Child's War (1686–1690) — Company crushed by Aurangzeb, forgiven after paying a fine
- Golden Farman (Sultan of Golconda) — free trade in Golconda ports for 500 pagodas a year
- Madras — India's first municipality, 1687; Bhimili — Andhra's first, 1861
### Exam corner
- Shivaji — sacked Surat twice and threatened the English factory there
- Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah — allowed the first English factory at Machilipatnam (1611)
