# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:247 - Integration of princely states - instrument of accession, Junagadh, Hyderabad (Operation Polo), Kashmir
### Legal frame & Instrument of Accession
- Three hard cases, three methods: police action (Hyderabad), plebiscite (Junagadh), accession then war (Kashmir)
- Indian Independence Act 1947: join India, Pakistan or stay independent
- IoA ceded only Defence, External Affairs, Communications
- In return: privy purses + internal autonomy
- Hold-outs on 15 August 1947: Kashmir, Hyderabad, Junagadh
- Patel heads States Department; V.P. Menon secretary, chief negotiator
### Hard cases
| State | Ruler | Resolution |
| Junagadh | Nawab Mahabat Khanji III; Dewan Shah Nawaz Bhutto | Blockade, Arzi Hukumat; plebiscite Feb 1948 |
| Kashmir | Maharaja Hari Singh | Tribal invasion → IoA 26 Oct 1947; UN ceasefire Jan 1949 |
| Hyderabad | Nizam Mir Osman Ali Khan (7th) | Operation Polo, Sept 1948 |
| Travancore | Dewan C.P. Ramaswami Iyer | Declared independence (thorium), then joined |
| Jodhpur | Hanwant Singh | Tempted by Jinnah |
### Road to Operation Polo
- 1. Standstill Agreement, 29 November 1947, one year ↓
- 2. Nizam bans Indian currency, imports arms from Pakistan ↓
- 3. Razakars (MIM, Kasim Razvi) violence ↓
- 4. 13-17 September 1948: Polo; Nizam surrenders ↓
- 5. Military Governor J.N. Chaudhuri; Part B state, Nizam Rajpramukh
### Hyderabad: who's who

| Burgula Ramakrishna Rao | First elected CM (1952) |
### Congress policy & consolidation
- 1927 — AISPC founded; Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen emerged
- 1938 — Haripura: Purna Swaraj covers states, people's own struggle
- 1939 — Tripuri: non-interference dropped; Nehru AISPC president
- 1946-51 — Telangana armed struggle (Doddi Komaraiah killed 1946)
- 1956 — 7th Amendment ends Rajpramukh; Hyderabad dissolved 1 November
- Dec 1961 — Operation Vijay: Goa (K.P. Candeth)
- 1971 — 26th Amendment: privy purses abolished (Art. 291, 362)
### Menon's four phases
- ▲ 1. Extinction — 216 small states merged into provinces
- ▸ 2. Unions — Saurashtra, Rajasthan Union, PEPSU, Madhya Bharat
- ▸ 4. Privy purses abolished — 1971
### Exam corner

- Standstill, Laik Ali, Moin Nawaz Jung (APPSC G2 2012)
- Polo (Hyderabad 1948) vs Vijay (Goa 1961); Asaf Jahi ≠ Qutb Shahi
- Hyderabad outside India about 13 months
- 1956: Marathwada → Bombay; Raichur/Gulbarga → Mysore
### Organisations and movements → founder, year, leaders
## 1. All India States Peoples' Conference (AISPC)
- Founded: All India States Peoples' Conference: founded 1927
- Aim: Coordinate people's movements in princely states against autocratic rule
- Presidents: Jawaharlal Nehru (1939) (president from 1939); Pattabhi Sitaramayya
## 2. Praja Mandal leaders
| Leader | State / movement [GK] |
| Vijay Singh Pathik | Rajasthan (Bijolia; Rajasthan Seva Sangh) |
| Sewa Singh Thikriwala | Punjab (Patiala; Riyasti Praja Mandal) |
| Sarangadhar Das | Orissa (Dhenkanal Praja Mandal) |
| Swami Ramananda Tirtha | Hyderabad State Congress (1938) |
- Swami Ramananda Theertha: Led the "Join Indian Union" movement (Hyderabad State Congress) (APPSC G2 2012 key)
## 3. Razakars & MIM
- Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen: Emerged in 1927 (APPSC G2 2008 key)
- Razakars: Militant volunteer force/militia of the Majlis-e-Ittehadul Muslimeen (MIM), led by Kasim (Qasim) Razvi
- Their violence against the population forced military action (APHQ 2011 key)
- Also targeted the communist-led Telangana armed struggle
## 4. Telangana armed struggle
- Peasant revolt (1946-51): Led by the Communists and the Andhra Mahasabha against the Nizam, the deshmukhs and later the Razakars
- Trigger: killing of Doddi Komaraiah (1946)
- Link to integration:
- Unrest + Razakar violence → Operation Polo (1948)
- Indian Army then put down the armed squads; struggle withdrawn in October 1951
- ⚠ 1927 = AISPC founded and Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen emerged; 1938 = Hyderabad State Congress
### Acts, agreements and Congress policy → year, provision
## 1. Congress policy on the states
| Year / session | Policy |
| 1920s | Non-interference in internal affairs of states |
| Haripura (1938) | Purna Swaraj covered the states too, but struggles to be waged by the states' own people |
| Tripuri (1939) | Dropped non-interference; backed the movements openly |
## 2. Legal frame
- Indian Independence Act 1947: Princely states could join India, join Pakistan or stay independent
## 3. Instrument of Accession (IoA)
- IoA: Ceded only Defence, External Affairs and Communications to the Centre, in return for privy purses; rulers kept internal autonomy at first
- Hold-outs on 15 August 1947: All states except Kashmir, Hyderabad and Junagadh had signed
- Some notes list Travancore as a fourth
## 4. Agreements and amendments
| Agreement / Act | Year | Provision |
| Standstill Agreement (Hyderabad) | Signed 29 November 1947 for one year | Kept existing arrangements with India while talks went on; the Nizam's defiance continued (APPSC G2 2012 key) |
| Delhi Agreement (Kashmir) | 1952 | - |
| 26th Amendment Act (Indira Gandhi) | 1971 | Privy purses + royal titles abolished; it omitted Articles 291 and 362 |
| 7th Amendment | 1956 | Ended the office of Rajpramukh |
- ⚠ 26th (1971) = privy purses; 7th (1956) = Rajpramukhs

### Hyderabad State — dynasty, data and dates
## 1. Hyderabad State & Nizam
- Ruling dynasty at integration: Asaf Jahi (Nizams)
- Status: Largest and richest princely state; ranked 1st in population among princely states (1,63,38,534 in 1951)
- Last Nizam: Mir Osman Ali Khan, the 7th Nizam (VIIth Nizam)
- Wanted independence or accession to Pakistan; India suspected secret deals with Pakistan
## 2. The Nizam's defiance
- Violations by the Nizam:
- Sought an independent "Dominion of Hyderabad"
- Outside India: About 13 months (15 Aug 1947 - 17 Sept 1948)
## 3. After 1948 - Part B state to dissolution in 1956
- Military governor: J.N. Chaudhuri
- Part B state (1950-56): Nizam as Rajpramukh
- Dissolved 1 November 1956 (SRC recommendations):
| Region | Went to |
| Telugu districts | Andhra Pradesh |
| Marathwada | Bombay |
| Raichur/Gulbarga | Mysore |
### Operations → date, commander, result
## 1. Operation Polo ("Police Action")
- Dates: 13-17 September 1948; ended 17 September 1948
- Duration: About four to five days (some notes: four days; others count five, 13-17 Sept)
- Result: Nizam's forces + Razakars defeated; the Nizam surrendered and acceded
- Exam angle: "Operation Polo" vs Blue Star/Meghdoot/Vijay; Asaf Jahi vs Qutb Shahi; Razakar leader = Kasim Razvi.
## 2. French and Portuguese possessions - Operation Vijay
| Power | Possessions after 1947 | How they came to India |
| Portugal | Goa, Daman, Diu; Dadra and Nagar Haveli | Operation Vijay, December 1961 (Goa, Daman, Diu) |
- Operation Vijay: Portuguese surrendered in December 1961 → end of European colonial rule in India (UPSC)
- Exam angle: Operation Vijay (Goa 1961) vs Operation Polo (Hyderabad 1948) - both asked by APPSC.
### Consolidation — Menon's four phases, Part A-D states and terms
## 1. Menon's four phases
- (1) Extinction: 216 small states merged into provinces
- (2) Consolidation into unions: Saurashtra (Kathiawar states), Rajasthan Union, PEPSU, Madhya Bharat
- (4) Abolition of privy purses
## 2. Part A, B, C, D states

## 3. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Privy purses | Guaranteed annual payments to rulers for accession |
| Rajpramukhs | Heads of the Part B states (former princely unions) |
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Hyderabad police action removed a hostile state in the heart of India; followed by closer Andhra-Telangana relations
- Three hard cases, three methods: police action (Hyderabad), plebiscite (Junagadh), Instrument of Accession followed by war (Kashmir)
- AP angle:
- 17 September 1948 ended Nizam rule over Telangana

- Hyderabad's Telugu districts joined Andhra State in 1956 to form Andhra Pradesh

- FIRST/LAST: Goa, Daman and Diu (1961) = last European possessions freed
