### Tughlaq rulers → rise, reign, contribution, end — Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (1320-25)
## 1. Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (1320-25)
- Earlier name: Ghazi Malik; son of a Turkish slave + a Jat woman
- Under Alauddin: Governor of Punjab against the Mongols
- Accession: Killed Khusrau Shah → Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq Shah in 1320
- Agrarian policy:
- Land revenue rise limited to about one-tenth, raised gradually
- Remission in drought
- Waste land brought under the plough
- First Sultan to start irrigation/canal works
- Warangal:
- 1321-22: Prince Juna Khan (later MBT) attacked → false news of Sultan's death caused retreat
- 1323: Warangal fell after a 5-month siege → renamed Sultanpur
- Prataparudra II: Taken towards Delhi; died in the Narmada
- Tughlaqabad: Built the fort
- Death (1325): Wooden pavilion collapsed at Afghanpur → Ibn Battuta suspects a plot by Juna Khan
### Tughlaq rulers → rise, reign, contribution, end — Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1351-88)
## 1. Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1351-88)
- Accession: Raised to throne at Thatta after MBT died there (1351); ruled 1351-88
- Wazir: Khan-i-Jahan Maqbul → paid compensation to those who suffered under Muhammad bin Tughlaq
## 2. Successors and end
- Weak successors: Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq II, Abu Bakr, Nasiruddin Muhammad Shah → fought each other
- Last Tughlaq: Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah Tughlaq, on throne during Timur's invasion (1398)
- Some notes call him "Nasiruddin Muhammad Tughlaq"
- End:
- After Sultan Mahmud's death Daulat Khan Lodi briefly recognised
- Khizr Khan took Delhi in 1414
- Tughlaq period: 1320-1414
### Muhammad bin Tughlaq's five experiments → cause and result
| Experiment | Cause / aim | Result / outcome |
| Transfer of capital, Delhi to Devagiri, renamed Daulatabad, 1327 [GK] | Central base to control the Deccan | Mass migration, hardship and disruption; the court returned to Delhi (later reversed) |
| Token currency of copper and brass (c. 1329-30) [GK] | Silver shortage; the idea of a token coin | Forged widely (mass forgery); the coins were recalled and redeemed at a heavy cost to the treasury |
| Higher taxation / tax rise in the Doab [GK] | More revenue | Coincided with (came during) a famine; peasants revolted and fled |
| Khurasan expedition [GK] | Conquest in Central Asia | A huge army was raised, then disbanded unpaid / unused |
| Qarachil expedition (Kumaon hills) [GK] | Securing the hill frontier | Army destroyed in the hills |
- Famine relief: During Doab famine, Sultan moved to a Swarga-dwari / Swargadwari famine-relief camp on the Ganga near Kannauj [GK] → relief and cultivation during the famine
- Exam angle: statement questions ("token currency was copper and brass"; "the Doab tax coincided with famine").

### Ibn Battuta and historians → statements on MBT
## 1. Ibn Battuta - country, Sultan served, office, work, missions
- Origin: Moroccan, from Morocco; born in Tangiers, 1304
- Real name: Abu Abdullah Muhammad
- Arrival: Came in Muhammad bin Tughlaq's reign (1333)
- Office: MBT made him Qazi of Delhi
- Mission: Envoy to China (1342)
- Work: Rihla (Kitab-ul-Rehla), in Arabic → conditions in the Sultanate + the Deccan
- Vijayanagara: First foreign traveller to visit Vijayanagara, in Harihara I's reign (APPSC PYQ)
## 2. Ibn Battuta's observations
- On MBT: Eccentric, severe in temper / quick-tempered, but generous
- Postal system: uluq (horse) + dawa (foot) couriers (see terms page)
- Sati: Required the Sultan's permission

### Firoz Shah → revenue, jizya, iqta, books and campaigns
## 1. Revenue, jizya and iqta
- Revenue:
- Wrote off MBT's taqavi loans + all debts owed to the state
- Fixed land revenue permanently
- Abolished about 24 taxes; kept only the four Islamic/Sharia taxes (kharaj, jizya, khams, zakat)
- Jizya: Separate tax on Hindus (ulema's advice); levied even on Brahmins, extending it to Brahmins for the first time
- Iqta: Restored iqtas + made iqtas hereditary
- ⚠ Note: Some sources say 23 taxes were abolished
## 2. Translations and books
- Various scholars translated Sanskrit texts into Persian during his rule — his Translation Department translated Sanskrit texts into Persian
- The Jawalamukhi library: Approximately 1,300 Sanskrit manuscripts — translated into Persian by Azizuddin Khan; associated with Firuz Tughlaq and Nagarkot

### Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1351-88)
## Accession and administration
- Placed on the throne at Thatta (1351) after death of Muhammad bin Tughlaq; ruled till 1388
- Made iqtas hereditary; wrote off taqavi loans, fixed land revenue, kept the four Islamic taxes
- Made jizya a separate tax; extended it to Brahmins for the first time (ulema's advice)
- Set up a separate Public Works Department for canals, towns, buildings
- Wazir Khan-i-Jahan Maqbul — originally the captured Brahmin Jagannatha Pandit; paid compensation to those who suffered under MBT
## Canals
- Haq-i-sharb — one-tenth of produce; canals from Yamuna and Sutlej to Hisar
- First Sultan to dig canals — Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq; most canals — Firoz Shah
## Culture and campaigns
- Ashokan pillars brought to Delhi from Topra (Ambala) and Meerut
- Autobiography — Futuhat-i-Firoz Shahi
- About 1,300 Sanskrit manuscripts from the Jawalamukhi temple library (Nagarkot); some translated into Persian by Azizuddin Khalid Khani
- Two failed Bengal expeditions against Haji Iliyas; Jam Babaniya of Sind agreed to pay tribute
## Sultan–policy
- Firoz Shah — canals, irrigation, public works; Alauddin — market control; Balban — Blood and Iron; MBT — token currency, capital transfer
