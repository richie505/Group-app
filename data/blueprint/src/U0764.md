### Biogeochemical cycles — basics and types
## 1. Basics
- Recycling: nutrients recycled indefinitely between living organisms, air and the Earth's crust; energy is not
- Driver: energy flow; all cycles linked to the water cycle
- Decomposers: removing them would block mineral movement - nutrients locked in dead matter never return to the soil (APPSC 2025)
## 2. Types by main reservoir
- gaseous (atmosphere and oceans) vs sedimentary (Earth's crust)
| Type | Cycles | Main reservoir |
| Gaseous | Carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, water | Atmosphere / ocean |
| Sedimentary | Phosphorus, sulphur, calcium, potassium | Earth's crust: soil and rocks |
- Nitrogen cycle: the one whose main reservoir is NOT the soil (atmosphere, ~78% N2) (APPSC-G2 2025)
- Phosphorus cycle: no atmospheric phase; released by weathering of rocks; ends in ocean sediments
### Water regulation by wetlands and forests; human disruption of cycles
## 1. Functions of wetlands
- Flood buffers; water storage; water purification; aquifer recharge
- Erosion control; sediment retention; nutrient cycling
- Biodiversity support; microclimate regulation
- Wetlands: Act as sponges - store floodwater, recharge aquifers, sustain lean-season flow
## 2. Water cycle regulation - forests
- Role of water: Carries elements through the ecosystem; biogeochemical cycles would stop if water did not cycle
- Forests: Regulate water flow, conserve soil and moisture, recharge groundwater, moderate floods
## 3. Human disruption - factor → effect
| Factor | Effect |
| Fossil fuels | Burning fossil fuels + deforestation raise atmospheric CO2 → climate change |
| Chemical pesticides | Alter soils and microbial networks, weaken resilience |
### Carbon and nitrogen cycles — processes → agents
## 1. Carbon cycle
- CO2 added by: Respiration, decomposition, combustion (of fossil fuels) + volcanic activity (volcanoes), deforestation
- CO2 removed by: Photosynthesis (plants, algae, some bacteria) + dissolving in the ocean (dissolution in oceans), carbonate sedimentation, silicate weathering
- Reservoirs and sinks:
- Atmosphere, oceans, forests and soils
- Long-term: carbonate rocks and fossil fuels
- Forests = vital carbon sinks
## 2. Nitrogen cycle
- N added to soil by:
- Biological fixation (enzyme nitrogenase)
- Lightning (atmospheric fixation)
- Industrial fixation (fertilisers)
- Ammonification of dead matter
- N removed: Denitrification returns N2 to air
| Process | What happens | Agents [GK] |
| Fixation | N2 in air becomes ammonia or nitrate | Rhizobium in legume root nodules; Azotobacter; cyanobacteria (Anabaena, Nostoc); lightning; industrial Haber process |
| Ammonification | Organic N in dead matter becomes ammonia | Decomposer bacteria and fungi |
| Nitrification | Ammonia becomes nitrite, then nitrate | Nitrosomonas, then Nitrobacter |
| Denitrification | Nitrate becomes N2 gas, returned to air | Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus |
- Net effect: Fixation, ammonification and nitrification add usable N to soil; denitrification removes it
- ⚠ Nitrification = Nitrosomonas → Nitrobacter; denitrification = Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus

### Ecological succession — terms, persons and types
## 1. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Succession | gradual, orderly, sequential replacement of one community by another in an area over time; change in species composition |
| Pioneer species / community | first hardy colonisers |
| Seral communities | intermediate stages; whole sequence = sere; each step = seral stage |
| Climax community | final stable community, in equilibrium with (set by) climate (forest, grassland, coral reef) |
| Seasonal succession | cyclic change driven by environmental rhythms, not by a disturbance (disaster) |
## 2. Persons → theory

## 3. Primary vs secondary

### Succession — Clements' phases and sere sequences
## 1. Phases (Clements)
| Phase | What happens |
| 1. Nudation | Bare area forms (erosion, landslide, volcanic activity, fire) |
| 2. Invasion | Migration of seeds/spores, ecesis (successful establishment), aggregation |
| 3. Competition and co-action | Intra- and inter-specific competition; unfit eliminated |
| 4. Reaction | Organisms modify soil, water, light, temperature - the most important stage |
| 5. Stabilisation | Climax community |
## 2. Xerosere
- Bare rock pioneers: lichens = symbiotic association of algae and fungi
- Xerosere (bare rock or dry land): crustose lichens → foliose lichens → mosses → herbs and grasses → shrubs → forest climax
- Pioneer trees (birch, oak) need high light; shade-tolerant species form the climax
## 3. Hydrosere
- Hydrosere (lakes): phytoplankton → submerged rooted plants → floating plants (duckweed Lemna, Pistia; the alga Chara) → reed-swamp → sedge-meadow (marsh) → woodland → forest
- Ends in wet woodland = fen carr
- Lake sediments keep a permanent record → hydroseres better studied than xeroseres
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: Invasive Prosopis juliflora = "ecological first responder" - pioneer species spreading because hydrology and soil chemistry have changed (TH, 7 May 2026)
- AP angle:
- AP has the second-largest mangrove ecosystem in India (Godavari and Krishna estuaries), per AP Socio-Economic Survey
- AP's forests act as carbon sinks, regulate water flow, conserve soil and moisture → intangible ecosystem services
- FIRST/LAST: Lichens first on bare rock; climax community = last stage
- Significance: Decomposers and nutrient cycles keep ecosystems running indefinitely; energy is lost, matter is reused
- Criticism & debates: Pricing nature (Costanza, TEEB) aids decisions but undervalues cultural and spiritual services

### Types of cycles

- Wetlands — microclimate regulators: stored water evaporates and holds heat, steadying local temperature and humidity
### Cycle basics
## Carbon cycle
- Photosynthesis — does not add CO2 (it removes CO2)
- Processes that add CO2: respiration, decomposition, fossil-fuel combustion, volcanic activity
- Processes that remove CO2: photosynthesis, dissolving in oceans, carbonate sedimentation, silicate weathering
## Reservoirs
- Nitrogen cycle — main reservoir is the atmosphere (N2 about 78% of air), not soil
- Phosphorus cycle — no atmospheric phase; weathering releases P from rocks, much ends in ocean sediments
## Ecosystem links
- Removing decomposers blocks mineral movement — nutrients in dead matter not returned to soil
- Wetlands act as a sponge — store floodwater, recharge aquifers, keep dry-season flow
- Chemical pesticides do not always increase ecosystem resilience — may change soils and microbial networks and weaken it
- Prosopis juliflora (invasive) — "ecological first responder": spreads when hydrology and soil chemistry have changed
### Nitrogen cycle
- Ammonification — organic N in dead matter to ammonia (decomposer bacteria and fungi)
- Nitrification stages: Nitrosomonas (ammonia to nitrite), Nitrobacter (nitrite to nitrate)
- Denitrification — removes N from soil, returns it to the atmosphere as N₂
- Fixation, ammonification and nitrification add usable N to soil

### Succession phases (Clements, 1916)
- Clements' sequence: nudation → invasion → competition and co-action → reaction → stabilisation
- Invasion — right after nudation
- Ecesis — successful establishment of migrated organisms (invasion phase)
- Reaction — most important phase; organisms change soil, water, light, temperature, making site fit for later species
- Seasonal succession — cyclic, driven by environmental rhythms; not caused by disturbance
### Primary vs secondary succession
- Secondary succession — generally faster than primary, because soil, seeds or roots may remain
### Seres & theories
- Monoclimax — Frederic Clements; polyclimax — Tansley; climax-pattern — Whittaker
