# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:27 - Vice-President; Prime Minister and the Union Council of Ministers - Articles 74, 75, 77, 78
### Vice-President: office & election
- All executive action of the Government of India is taken in the President's name (Art. 77)
- Art. 63; modelled on American VP; ex-officio RS Chairman (Art. 64), casting vote only
- Elected by all MPs, elected and nominated; State legislatures no role (Art. 66)
- PR-STV, secret ballot; disputes: Supreme Court (Art. 71)
- Qualified for Rajya Sabha, 35 years; term 5 years (Art. 67)
- Removal: RS resolution by majority of all then members + LS agrees; 14 days' notice
- Acting President (Art. 65): max 6 months; draws President's salary
### Oath, resignation, removal

| Governor (159) | HC Chief Justice | President | At pleasure, Art. 156 |
| CM, Ministers (164(3)) | Governor | Governor | Loss of Assembly majority |
### Prime Minister
- Appointed by President (Art. 75(1)); may be from either House
- Non-member: must enter House within six months (Art. 75(5)); S.R. Chaudhuri (2001): no repeat appointment
- Responsible only to Lok Sabha
- Art. 78: communicates CoM decisions, furnishes information
- Chairs NITI Aayog, National Development Council, Inter-State Council, National Disaster Management Authority

### Vice-President — office, qualifications and election (Arts 63, 64, 66)
## 1. Position and election
- Article 63: There shall be a Vice-President — second-highest office in the Order of Precedence; modelled on the American Vice-President
- Article 64: Ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha — presides over its sittings; casting vote in a tie (only a casting vote)
- Does not preside while a resolution for his own removal is under consideration
- Not a member of either House — not a part of Parliament (the President is); holds no other office of profit
- Elected (not appointed) (Art. 66): Indirectly, by members of both Houses — elected and nominated MPs
- State Legislative Assemblies do not take part — the key difference from the Presidential election
- State Legislatures — Assembly and Council members — are completely excluded; no role in the election (State legislatures have no role → State MLAs are NOT in it; nominated MPs ARE)
- Method: Proportional representation by Single Transferable Vote; secret ballot (PR-STV)
- Disputes (Article 71): All doubts and disputes decided by the Supreme Court — final
## 2. Prep detail
- Ex officio: also President of ICWA (Sep 2026)
- Electoral college size: = all MPs, 788 (543 LS + 245 RS) when full
- Conduct: by ECI under the Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952
- Exam angle: "Who elects the VP" (both Houses only) and "Where can the removal resolution first be moved" (Rajya Sabha).
### Vice-President vs President — oath, term, removal
## 1. Qualifications, term and removal
- Qualifications: Citizen; completed 35 years; qualified for election to the Rajya Sabha; no office of profit
- Term: Five years (Article 67); re-election any number of times
- Term 5 years from entering office; continues till successor takes charge (Art 67(c))
- Oath: Administered by the President (Art 69)
- Resignation: Addressed to the President
- Removal: Rajya Sabha resolution passed by a majority of all the then members of the Council of States — the Lok Sabha must agree to it
- Fourteen-day notice; no formal impeachment like the President's
## 2. Resignation, removal, vacancy - compared with the President
| Point | President | Vice-President |
| Qualified to be member of | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
| Resignation to | Vice-President | President (Art 67(a)), effective immediately |
| Removal | Impeachment, Art 61, either House, 2/3 | Art 67(b): resolution only in RS, by majority of all its then members (effective majority); LS agrees by simple majority; 14 days' notice; no grounds stated |
| Vacancy election | Within 6 months (Art 62) | "As soon as possible", no deadline (Art 68) |
### Acting President (Art. 65) and election disputes (Art. 71)
## 1. Acting as President (Article 65)
- When: Presidential vacancy — death, resignation, removal; or the President is unable to discharge functions — absence, illness
- Maximum period: Six months — a new President must be elected within six months
- Pay: As Chairman of the Rajya Sabha draws salary and allowances — not charged on the Consolidated Fund of India
- When acting as President: the President's salary, allowances and privileges (the President's salary, not the Chairman's)
- As a nominated member: Does not take part in the Presidential election
- While acting as President (Art 65): ceases to be RS Chairman → Deputy Chairman presides
## 2. Election disputes - Article 71
- Forum: decided by the Supreme Court under Art 71[GK]
- History: 39th Amendment (1975) removed SC's power over disputes on elections of President, VP, PM and Speaker → 44th Amendment (1978) restored it
- Other matters: regulated by Parliament (Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952)
- Void election: acts done before SC's decision remain valid

### Prime Minister — appointment, oath and membership (Art 75)
## 1. Appointment, oath and membership
- Appointed by the President (Article 75(1)) — normally the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha (by convention, Lok Sabha majority)
- Other ministers = appointed on the PM's advice
- Oath: Administered by the President; resignation addressed to the President
- Member of either House: Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha (PM may belong to either House)
- Rajya Sabha PMs: Indira Gandhi (initially), Manmohan Singh
- Need not be an MP when appointed — must become one within six months (Article 75(5)) → a non-member can be PM for six months (6 months)
- Responsible only to the Lok Sabha — not to the Rajya Sabha
## 2. Non-member ministers (Art. 75(5)) — Prep detail
- Art. 75(5): any person may be a minister; a non-MP must become a member of either House within six months (six consecutive months), else ceases to be a minister
- S.R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001): non-member cannot be re-appointed repeatedly without getting elected (UPSC notes spell it "S.R. Chauhan")
### Prime Minister — powers and functions
## 1. Powers and functions
- Ministers: Selects Ministers, allocates portfolios, can ask any Minister to resign; advises the President on appointing other Ministers
- Link between President and Council of Ministers (Article 78):
- Communicates all decisions and administrative proposals of the Council to the President
- Furnishes information required by the President
- Submits for the Council's reconsideration any matter decided by an individual Minister, if the President requires
- Advises the President on: summoning and prorogation of Parliament; dissolution of the Lok Sabha; Emergency (through the Cabinet's recommendation)
- Chairman of: NITI Aayog (earlier the Planning Commission), National Development Council, National Integration Council, Inter-State Council, National Disaster Management Authority
### Union Ministers — appointment, tenure and status (Art 75)
## 1. Union Ministers
- Appointed by the President on the PM's advice; hold office during the pleasure of the President (Article 75(2)) — not legally the pleasure of the PM
- President removes a minister only on the PM's advice (UPSC)
- Oath of Office and Oath of Secrecy: Administered by the President — forms in the Third Schedule
- Membership: Either House (need not be the Lok Sabha); a non-member must become a member within six consecutive months or ceases to be a Minister
- In Parliament: Can speak and participate in the proceedings of both Houses and joint sittings; can vote only in the House of which he is a member
## 2. Tenure and status — Prep detail
- No fixed tenure: Holds office during the President's pleasure → in practice while he commands a Lok Sabha majority
- Ministers' tenure (Art. 75(2)): Hold office during the pleasure of the President
- Example: MoS Ravneet Singh Bittu's resignation accepted "under clause (2) of Article 75" (25 Jul 2026)
- Executive status: PM = de facto head of government vs President = de jure head of State

### Council of Ministers — size limit, 91st Amendment
## 1. Size limit — 91st Amendment Act, 2003 (a 15% ceiling on the number of Ministers)
- Union — Article 75(1A): Total Ministers, including the PM, not more than 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha (APPSC G2 2025)
- States — Article 164(1A): The number of Ministers cannot exceed 15% of the strength of the Legislative Assembly (including the Chief Minister); minimum 12 Ministers (not be less than 12) (APPSC-G2 2017)
- Example: West Bengal Assembly of 294 → max 44 ministers
## 2. Quorum and ministers
- Quorum: One-tenth of the total membership
- Non-member Minister: Must get elected within six months
- Council of Ministers: Limited to 15 percent of the total membership of the Lok Sabha — 91st Amendment (91st Amendment Act, 2003)
## 3. Prep detail
- Defection: member disqualified for defection cannot be minister until re-elected (Art 75(1B))
### Council of Ministers vs Cabinet; categories of ministers
## 1. Council of Ministers vs Cabinet
| Basis | Council of Ministers | Union Cabinet |
| Nature | Wider collective constitutional body | Smaller, compact inner core — apex policy-formulation and core decision-making body |
| Size | 60–80 members | 15–20 members |
| Composition | PM, Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State (independent charge), Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers | PM and Cabinet-rank Ministers only |
| Powers | Broad powers under Articles 74 and 75 | Highest executive category in the ranking |
- Word "Cabinet": Not in the original text — inserted into Article 352(3) by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978
- Four-tier classification of Ministers: a convention (tradition) — the Constitution does not classify Ministers into tiers (Constitution does not classify ministers into ranks → ranking decided by the PM's choice)

### Non-constitutional posts — Deputy PM, Leader of the House, PMO

### Vice-President: office & election
## Office
- Article 63 — creates the office; Article 64 — VP is ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha; 66 — election; 67 — term
- ★ Vice-President — ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha; not a member of the House
- Office based on the Vice-President of the USA
- Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha; the VP is not a constituent part of Parliament
## Election and qualifications
- ★ Elected by an electoral college of members of both Houses of Parliament (Art. 66(1))
- All MPs, elected and nominated; PR-STV, secret ballot; State legislatures have no role
- Qualifications: citizen, at least 35 years, qualified for election to the Rajya Sabha (not the Lok Sabha)
- Eligible for re-election any number of times
## Removal and Chair
- ★ Removal resolution can be proposed in the Rajya Sabha only
- Has a casting vote in a tie in the Rajya Sabha
- Does not preside while a resolution for his own removal is discussed
- While acting as President: the Deputy Chairman presides over the Rajya Sabha; VP draws the President's salary, not the Chairman's
- Article 67(a) — VP resigns by writing to the President; takes effect immediately
## Others
- ★ Neelam Sanjiva Reddy — became President without having been Vice-President
- 39th Amendment (1975) — removed the Supreme Court's power over Presidential election disputes; 44th Amendment (1978) — restored it
- President removed by impeachment (Article 61) — 14 days' notice, two-thirds of total membership of each House

## Deputy PM

### Prime Minister
## Appointment and membership
- ★ PM appointed by the President — Article 75(1); other Ministers on the PM's advice; 75(2) — hold office during the President's pleasure
- By convention the leader of the Lok Sabha majority
- ★ PM is generally a member of the Lok Sabha, but not compulsory — may be from the Rajya Sabha
- ★ Indira Gandhi — first PM from the Rajya Sabha at appointment (later joined the Lok Sabha)
- ★ Chandra Shekhar — Lok Sabha member (Balia) while PM (1990–91); Deve Gowda, I.K. Gujral, Manmohan Singh were not
- PM's oath administered by the President
- Non-member Minister (including PM) must join either House within six months
- UK PM must belong to the House of Commons; Indian PM — either House
## Tenure and responsibility
- No fixed tenure — stays while having the Lok Sabha's confidence; formally holds office at the President's pleasure
- PM and Council responsible only to the Lok Sabha (Article 75(3)), even if the PM is a Rajya Sabha member
- During a National Emergency the President stays bound by the Council of Ministers' advice
## Ministers in Parliament
- Article 88 — Ministers may speak in both Houses and joint sittings
- A Minister can vote only in the House he belongs to
- 75(2) pleasure tenure; 75(3) collective responsibility; 75(5) six-month rule
## Bodies chaired and duties
- ★ PM heads the National Defence Committee
- ★ PM presides over the National Development Council
- PM chairs NITI Aayog, NDC, Inter-State Council, and the National Integration Council
- PM's duties towards the President — Article 78 (not Article 74)
- PM advises the President on appointing Ministers and dissolving the Lok Sabha; not on the CJI (collegium)
- S.R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001) — a non-member cannot be repeatedly reappointed Minister without winning election
## Vice-President items
- VP — ex-officio President of the Indian Council of World Affairs (ICWA)
- Article 68 — VP vacancy filled as soon as possible; no six-month deadline (unlike the President's)
- VP can act as President for at most six months

## Election

- ★ State Legislative Assembly votes in electing the President, Rajya Sabha members and Legislative Council members
## Office
- ★ Articles 64 and 89(1) — VP is ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Council of States)
- ★ VP is not a member of either House of Parliament
- ★ Rajya Sabha — chaired by one who is not its member
- ★ Article 79 — Parliament = President + Council of States + House of the People; VP is not an integral part
## Removal
- ★ Removal resolution moved in the Rajya Sabha alone, passed by a majority of its members, then agreed to by the Lok Sabha
- ★ Requires 14 days' prior notice
### Vice-President vs President — oath, term, removal
- ★ Article 67(b) — VP removed by a resolution of the Council of States passed by a majority of all the then members, agreed to by the House of the People
- ★ Parliament = House of the People + Council of States + President
- ★ Convention, not written: PM resigns if he loses majority in the Lok Sabha (Article 75 only says the President appoints the PM)
### Acting President (Art. 65) and election disputes (Art. 71)
- ★ President resigns to the Vice-President (Article 56(1)(a)); VP immediately tells the Speaker (Art 56(2))
- ★ Vacancy in the President's office (death, resignation, removal) filled within six months
- ★ VP elected for five years; ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha
- ★ While doing the President's duties, the VP does not do the duties of Chairman of the Council of States
- ★ VP — second-highest constitutional office after the President (Article 63); acts as President during casual vacancies
### Vice-Presidents → FIRSTs, records and mid-term vacancies

- ★ Dr. Zakir Hussain — second VP (1962–1967)
- ★ Diplomat-turned-VP pairs: Radhakrishnan & Zakir Hussain; Radhakrishnan & V.V. Giri
- ★ V.V. Giri — High Commissioner to Sri Lanka (1947–1951); K.R. Narayanan — Ambassador to China (1976–1978)
- ★ Mohammad Hamid Ansari — 12th VP (from 2007); re-elected 2012
- ★ PM has full discretion in choosing Ministers — President appoints them on PM's advice (Article 75(1))
### Prime Minister — appointment, oath and membership (Art 75)
- ★ President — "represents the nation but does not lead the nation"; real executive power is with the PM
- ★ PM — leader of the Lok Sabha (leader of the majority party); if not a Lok Sabha member he can name another
- ★ Article 75(5) — Minister not an MP for six consecutive months stops being a Minister
- ★ Manmohan Singh — PM 2004–2014; Rajya Sabha member from Assam (first elected 1991)
### Prime Minister — powers and functions
## Bodies headed by the PM
- ★ Planning Commission (set up 1950) — PM is its (ex-officio) Chairman
- ★ Deputy Chairman of the Planning Commission — equivalent to a Cabinet Minister; first was Gulzarilal Nanda
- ★ NDMA — headed by the PM; formed under the Disaster Management Act (23 December 2005)
## Conventions
- ★ Budget not passed by the Lok Sabha — PM gives the resignation of the Council (loss of majority)
- ★ PM resigns on losing majority in the Lower House — convention, not stated in the Constitution
### Union Ministers — appointment, tenure and status (Art 75)
- ★ A Minister who is a Rajya Sabha member can take part in Lok Sabha debates
- ★ Vice-President — a non-member who takes part in the proceedings of a House of Parliament
- ★ PM from the Upper House cannot vote in the Lok Sabha (can speak, but votes only in his own House)
- ★ Giriraj Singh (MP from Begusarai, Bihar) — Union Minister of Textiles after the 18th Lok Sabha elections
### Oaths, resignations and removal → executive heads and ministers
## Oaths and removal
- ★ Oaths: President — by CJI; VP — by the President; PM — by the President
- ★ Speaker — does not take an oath of office
- ★ Article 67(b) — VP removal resolution moved first only in the Rajya Sabha, then agreed to by the Lok Sabha
- ★ Governor — no impeachment provision; appointed by the President, holds office at his pleasure
- ★ President removed by impeachment (Article 61)
## Executive
- ★ Article 53(1) — executive power of the Union given to the President
- ★ PM — appointed by the President; head of the Council of Ministers
- ★ Convention: the person with majority support in Parliament is invited to head the government
- ★ Article 54 — election of the President; Article 75 — appointment of PM and Council of Ministers
- ★ A non-member can join the Cabinet for up to 6 months (Article 75(5)) — membership is not limited to MPs
- Article 155 — appointment of the Governor; Article 164 — appointment of the Chief Minister and State Council of Ministers

- ★ PM — President of the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR)

### Articles 74, 77, 78 → content
- ★ During a National Emergency the President does not get discretionary powers; acts on the Council of Ministers' advice
- ★ Real executive power in the parliamentary form — Prime Minister (Article 75)
- ★ All executive power given to the President
- ★ Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the House of the People — a constitutional obligation, Article 75(3)
- ★ Article 74(3) does not exist
### Council of Ministers — size limit, 91st Amendment
- ★ 91st Amendment Act, 2003 — Council of Ministers incl. PM limited to 15% of the Lok Sabha's total membership
- ★ Added Article 75(1A) (Union); changed Article 164 — clause (1A): State ministry incl. CM within 15% of the Assembly
- ★ Before 2003 the number of ministers was not in the Constitution (PM's/CM's discretion)
- ★ Aims: limit the size of ministries; strengthen anti-defection law
- ★ APPSC key gave the 97th Amendment for "limiting cabinet size to 10%"; the 97th Amendment (2011) really dealt with cooperative societies
### Council of Ministers vs Cabinet; categories of ministers
- ★ Cabinet = PM + Ministers of Cabinet rank only; the supreme decision-making body
- ★ Article 75 — appointment of the Council of Ministers
- ★ 75(1) — President appoints PM, and other Ministers on the PM's advice; 75(2) — office during the President's pleasure
- ★ Article 77(3) — business rules; the President allocates portfolios among Ministers on the PM's advice
### Non-constitutional posts — Deputy PM, Leader of the House, PMO
- ★ Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel — first Deputy PM (15 Aug 1947 – 15 Dec 1950); also Home Minister; united 500+ princely states
- ★ Office of Deputy PM — not in the Constitution; an extra-constitutional growth (political need)
- ★ Naming a Deputy PM is a political decision; gives no power of the PM
