### Terms → meaning — sex ratio, CSR, SRB, "missing women"
| Term | Meaning |
| Sex ratio - India | Females per 1,000 males (APPSC-GS 2018 key); a social indicator of equality between men and women |
| Sex ratio - International (UN/world) convention | Males per 100 females |
| Child sex ratio | Girls per 1,000 boys aged 0-6 |
| Sex ratio at birth (SRB) | Girls born per 1,000 boys |
| Biological norm | About 105 boys per 100 girls = roughly 950 girls per 1,000 boys at birth |
| Missing women | Demographic deficit of women in Asia |
- Biological norm: More boys born, but male child mortality is naturally higher (UPSC 1999 key: A false, R true)
- Missing women - coiner: Nobel laureate Amartya Sen (1990)
- Causes: intra-household discrimination, neglect of female health, sex-selective abortion
- ⚠ India = females per 1,000 males; UN = males per 100 females
### National trend — sex ratio and child sex ratio, 1901 to 2011
## 1. Sex ratio
- Census trend: 1901: 972 → continuous decline to 945 (1941) → low of 927 (1991) → 933 (2001) → 943 (2011, final) (APPSC-GS 2019 key 943)
| Census | 1951 | 1961 | 1971 | 1981 | 1991 | 2001 | 2011 |
| Sex ratio [GK] | 946 | 941 | 930 | 934 | 927 (lowest) | 933 | 943 |
- Sharpest decline: 1961-71 (941 → 930); 1991 = all-time low
- Female share: 48.46% of India's population (Census 2011)
## 2. Child sex ratio (0-6)
- Trend: 927 (2001) → 918 (2011)

- ⚠ 2001: sex ratio 933 but CSR 927; 2011: sex ratio 943 but CSR 918

### Acts, schemes and cases — BBBP, PCPNDT Act, Cradle Baby Scheme
| Act / scheme | Year / place | Provision / aim |
| Beti Bachao Beti Padhao | 2015 - 22 Jan 2015 at Panipat, Haryana | Reverse falling CSR; improve CSR and SRB, survival, protection and education of the girl child |
| PCPNDT Act, 1994 | 1994 | Bans sex selection + prenatal sex determination |
| Tamil Nadu Cradle Baby Scheme (1992) | 1992 | Against female infanticide |
## 1. Beti Bachao Beti Padhao
- Approach: Multi-sectoral action in gender-critical districts
## 2. PCPNDT Act
- Tests only for genetic/chromosomal abnormalities
- Unintended effect (birth vs survival): After curbs on prenatal sex selection, firstborn girls → 25% higher child mortality than firstborn boys (discrimination shifts from birth to survival)
## 3. Supreme Court
- Supreme Court (Jun 2026): Justices Sanjay Karol and P.K. Mishra - CSR shows "clear signs of improvement" but sex selection reflects deep-seated patriarchy (bias "deep-seated" despite improvement)
- ⚠ PCPNDT 1994 (Act) vs Cradle Baby 1992 (TN) vs BBBP 2015 (Panipat)
