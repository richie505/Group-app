### Drain of wealth — meaning, components and effects — Meaning
## 1. Meaning
- Meaning: part of India's wealth sent to England with no economic or material return; began with the Diwani of Bengal (1765)
- Export surplus: India's large export surplus (and Indian revenue) brought no gold or silver - paid for British office expenses, war expenses (war and military expenditure), invisible imports
### Drain of wealth — meaning, components and effects — Components (home charges and more)
## 1. Components (home charges and more)
| Component | What it covered |
| Interest | On the public debt raised in Britain [GK] |
| Salaries and pensions | Of British civil and military officials (salaries, savings and pensions), remitted home (UPSC notes, CDI) |
| Guaranteed railway returns | Assured interest (5%[GK]) to British railway investors, whether lines earned or not |
| Military expenditure | India paid for the British Indian Army and imperial wars (Afghanistan, Burma) - the largest single budget head [GK]; war expenses |
| Profits | Of British capitalists, merchants and banks sent home |
- Unequal trade: also a component of the drain
- Drain reason per Naoroji (APPSC-GS 2024): "profits taken by British capitalists from India" (Profits of British capitalists = APPSC 2024 answer)
- ⚠ Trap: NOT an excess of imports
## 2. Effects
- Effects:
- Stifled capital formation and industrialisation
- Moderates demanded lower land revenue, abolition of salt tax, protective tariffs, cuts in military spending
- Exam angle: "popularised by" = Naoroji (APPSC 2025); reason-for-drain options (APPSC 2024).
### Deindustrialisation — chronology, causes and results
## 1. One-way free trade and chronology
- One-way free trade: After the Charter Act 1813 ended the Company's monopoly
- Machine-made British goods entered India at low duty (almost duty-free)
- Heavy duties blocked Indian manufactures in Britain (England)
- Chronology:
- Weavers coerced by Company agents (Bolt, 1772)
- By 1850 India imported one-fourth of all British cotton exports
## 2. Causes (B.D. Basu's list)
| Cause | How it hit Indian industry |
| Trade policy | Free trade forced on India; flood of cheap machine goods; tariffs in Britain |
| Raw materials | Export of raw materials (raw cotton) |
| Duties | Internal transit and customs duties |
| British privileges | Special privileges for British traders; monopolies of the Company undercut Indian entrepreneurs |
| Railways | Carried raw materials in and British goods inland/out |
## 3. Results
- Collapse: Town handicrafts suffered a "sudden and complete collapse"
- Marx: the British "inundated the very mother country of cotton with cottons"
- Overcrowding of agriculture: Artisans pushed onto the land → disguised unemployment
- The 1793 Act and famines pushed weavers into agriculture or wage labour

## 1. Causes and effects
- Compulsion: Peasants grew cash crops to pay the cash revenue, not by choice
- Crops: indigo, cotton, opium, jute and plantation crops in place of food crops
- Result: food security fell → famines frequent
- Triangular trade: Indian opium to China → Chinese tea (and silver) to Britain → British manufactures to India

### Economy on the eve of independence — data and sectors — Sectors
## 1. Sectors
- Agrarian base: over 75% of the workforce in agriculture, yet only about 50% of GDP → disguised unemployment, low productivity
- Industry:
- Cotton mills (Indian-owned) in Maharashtra and Gujarat
- Jute mills (foreign-owned) in Bengal
- Iron and steel from the early 20th century (TISCO, 1907)
- No capital-goods base; basic/capital goods only ~one-fourth of industrial output
- Public sector limited to railways, power, communications, ports
- Trade:
- Exports: primary products (raw silk, cotton, wool, sugar, indigo, jute)
- Imports: finished consumer and capital goods
- Suez Canal (1869) tightened British control
- British capital: before 1914 about 97% went to government projects, plantations and finance houses
## 2. Immediate challenges
- Immediate challenges:
- Large foodgrain imports and inflation → First Plan gave top priority to agriculture, irrigation, power
- Post-partition stabilisation
- ⚠ Cotton mills = Indian-owned; jute mills = foreign-owned
