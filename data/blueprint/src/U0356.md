| Reservation | 243D | 243T | Women: not less than one-third (at least 33 percent) of total seats and of chairperson positions |

## 2. Composition (Art. 243R) and reservation
- Members: Directly elected from wards
- Additions by State law:
- Persons with municipal-administration experience (no vote)
- Lok Sabha/Assembly members for the area
- Rajya Sabha/Council members registered as electors there
- Chairpersons of committees other than wards committees
- Reservation (243T): Mirrors 243D
- Chairperson: Election left to the State
### Composition, age, term and elections of municipalities — Articles 243R to 243ZA
## 1. Qualifications to contest (Art. 243V)
- Minimum age: Eligible for state legislature elections, but minimum age = 21 years (not 25)
- Disqualification: As under the law for State Legislature elections or a State law - disqualifications follow state law
## 2. Term (Art. 243U)
- Five years from first meeting
- Election before expiry, or within six months of dissolution
- Municipality is heard before dissolution
## 3. State Election Commission and State Finance Commission
- Elections (Art. 243ZA): Electoral rolls + all municipal polls under the State Election Commission (same SEC as Art. 243K)
- 243Y: The 243I Finance Commission also reviews municipal finances
- ⚠ 243K / 243ZA = SEC; 243I / 243Y = SFC (one body each for panchayats and municipalities)
### Wards committees and Area Sabhas — Article 243S and the Model Nagara Raj Bill

| Area Sabha | Model Nagara Raj Bill (2008): all voters in a polling-booth area, below the wards committee |
| Community Participation Law | A JNNURM mandatory reform |

### DPC and MPC — Articles 243ZD and 243ZE
| Feature | DPC | MPC |
| Article | 243ZD | 243ZE |
| Where / area | Every district | Every metropolitan area (population 10 lakh or more, Art. 243P) |
| Elected share | Not less than four-fifths elected by and from elected members of the district panchayat and municipalities, in proportion to rural:urban population | Not less than two-thirds elected by and from elected members of municipalities and chairpersons of panchayats in the area |
| Electors | District panchayat and municipal members | Municipal members and panchayat chairpersons |
| Plan | Consolidates plans of panchayats and municipalities into a draft district development plan | Draft development plan for the whole metropolitan area |
| Chairperson | Manner of choosing fixed by state law; chairperson forwards the plan to the State Government | Same |
### DPC and MPC — Articles 243ZD and 243ZE — District Planning Committee (DPC)
## 1. District Planning Committee (DPC)
- Source: In Part IXA - inserted by the 74th (not the 73rd) Amendment
- Purpose: Every State constitutes one at district level to consolidate the plans of Panchayats and Municipalities + prepare a draft development plan for the whole district
- Composition:
- Four-fifths of members elected by and from among elected members of the district panchayat and municipalities, in proportion to the district's rural-urban population ratio
- Rest per State law (ex-officio/nominated) → not fully elected
- ⚠ Trap: "All members elected" is false
- Draft plan considers: Common-interest matters (spatial planning, water and resource sharing, integrated infrastructure, environment) + available resources
- Forwarding: The chairperson forwards the plan to the State Government; State law settles composition and chairperson election
- Exam angle: APPSC-GS 2018 - "DPC provided by 74th Act; consolidates plans; all members elected" - only the first two ("provided by the 74th" and "consolidates panchayat and municipal plans") are correct.
### DPC and MPC — Articles 243ZD and 243ZE — Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC)
## 1. Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC)
- Mandatory for: Every metropolitan area = population of 10 lakh or more (may span districts)
- Composition: Two-thirds of members elected by elected members of municipalities + chairpersons of panchayats in the area, in proportion to urban-rural population
- Plan considers: Local plans, common-interest matters, Union/State objectives and investments
- Forwarding: Chairperson forwards the draft plan to the State Government
- ⚠ DPC four-fifths vs MPC two-thirds

### Road to the 74th
## Colonial to post-independence milestones
- Madras Corporation: first municipal corporation (1688; made by the 1687 charter)
- Bombay and Calcutta Municipal Corporations — 1726
- Lord Mayo's resolution (1870) — financial decentralisation
- Lord Ripon's resolution (1882) — "Magna Carta" of local self-government
- GoI Act 1919 — local self-government a transferred subject under dyarchy (elected Ministers), not reserved
- Chronology: Madras Corporation → Ripon (1882) → 1919 Act → Rajasthan Panchayati Raj (1959)
- Hanumantha Rao Committee (1984) — made the Collector central to district planning (Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta, G.V.K. Rao and ARC-I cut the Collector's role)
- Charles Metcalfe — called Indian village communities 'Little Republics'
## Making of the 74th Amendment
- 1989 Municipalities Bill lapsed; the P.V. Narasimha Rao government's Bill of September 1991 became the 74th Amendment
- Passed by both Houses in December 1992; Presidential assent April 1993
- In force from 1 June 1993 (73rd: 24 April 1993)
- Added Part IXA and the Twelfth Schedule (18 subjects; Eleventh has 29)
- DPCs and MPCs were added by the 74th Amendment (Part IXA); 73rd = Panchayats, Part IX
- Local Government stays in the State List
## Key provisions
- Voluntary: how the municipal Chairperson/Mayor is elected (left to State legislature)
- Articles: 243R composition, 243U duration, 243X taxes and funds, 243Z audit
- Women: at least one-third of seats and chairpersons' posts (Arts. 243D, 243T)
## Urban governance today
- Cantonment Boards — Ministry of Defence; MoHUA main ministry for ULBs; Home Affairs for ULBs in UTs
- ULBs called the "stepchild" of federalism: spend < 1% of GDP, employ ~10% of public workers
- Property tax — main own revenue of ULBs, always under-collected
### Three municipalities (243Q)
- Article 243Q types: Nagar Panchayat (area moving rural to urban), Municipal Council (smaller urban area), Municipal Corporation (larger urban area)
- Cantonment Board — not in 243Q classification
- Both 73rd and 74th systems linked with district planning bodies
- Municipal Corporation's three authorities: Council (headed by Mayor), Standing Committees, Commissioner
- Municipal Commissioner — chief executive of a Corporation, appointed by the State Government (usually IAS); Mayor heads the Corporation, Sarpanch the Gram Panchayat
- Area Sabha (Community Participation Law) — all voters in a polling-booth area; below the Wards Committee
- Panchayat accounts and audit (Art. 243J) — made by the State Legislature, not Parliament
### Part IXA essentials
## Structure
- 73rd: Part IX (Arts. 243–243-O); 74th: Part IXA (Arts. 243P–243ZG) + Twelfth Schedule
- Technical and vocational education — Eleventh Schedule, not Twelfth
## Articles
| Article | Content |
| 243Q proviso | Governor may specify an industrial township where an industrial establishment provides municipal services (no municipality needed) |
| 243R | State law may give representation to persons with special knowledge/experience of municipal administration — no vote |
| 243S | Wards Committees — mandatory where population is three lakh or more |
| 243U | Term: five years from date appointed for first meeting; reasonable opportunity of being heard before dissolution |
| 243W | Powers, authority and responsibilities; linked to the Twelfth Schedule |
| 243ZA | Municipal elections by the State Election Commission |
| 243F / 243V | Minimum age 21 for Panchayat/Municipality membership |
## Wards Committees
- May have one or more wards; State Legislature decides its make-up and area
- Ward councillor is a member of the committee
## Compulsory vs voluntary
- Compulsory: Ward Committees (3 lakh+), five-year terms, SC/ST reservation
- Voluntary: reservation for backward classes (State legislature)
## Elections after dissolution
- Election within six months of dissolution (not one year)
- Not required if less than six months of the term remain
## DPCs today
- DPCs weak in most States; making them work stays a duty of Ministry of Panchayati Raj
### DPC vs MPC
## Planning committees
- Article 243ZE — MPC for every metropolitan area of ten lakh or more (may span more than one district)
- MPC elected seats shared by urban–rural population (not area)
- DPC: at least four-fifths of members elected; rest by State law
- DPC consolidates panchayat and municipal plans into a draft district plan; MPC makes a plan for the whole metropolitan area
- Chairperson of DPC/MPC sends the draft plan to the State Government; way of choosing the chairperson set by State law, not Parliament
## Urban local bodies
- Eight types of ULBs: Municipal Corporation, Municipality/Municipal Council, Notified Area Committee, Town Area Committee, Cantonment Board, Township, Port Trust, Special Purpose Agency (not Zonal Development Authority)
- 243V disqualifications — law for elections to the State Legislature or a State law
- 243ZA — electoral rolls and municipal elections by the State Election Commission (same authority as Art. 243K)
