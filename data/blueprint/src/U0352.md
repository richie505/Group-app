### Centralisation and decentralisation — definitions, forms and dimensions
## 1. Meaning
- Democratic decentralisation — community participation and local accountability; not central control or mere administration
- Functions include: Public health, sanitation, water supply, rural development — ⚠ not maintenance of public order or judicial review
- Centralisation: Decisions concentrated at the apex - Union, State capital or department HQ
- Decentralisation: Authority transferred downward
## 2. Forms and dimensions
| Form | What happens | Who controls |
| Deconcentration | Work shifted to field offices of the same government | Centre keeps full control (the weakest form) [GK] |
| Delegation | Tasks entrusted to semi-autonomous bodies | Power can be withdrawn at will |
| Devolution | Powers transferred to elected local governments with their own mandate and revenue | Cannot be withdrawn at will; the strongest form, attempted by the 73rd and 74th Amendments |
- The test: If transferring authority can take power back at will → delegation, not devolution
- ⚠ Weakest = deconcentration; strongest = devolution
| Dimension | What is transferred |
| Political | Power to decide: elected local bodies |
| Administrative | Power to implement: staff, functions |
| Fiscal | Power to spend and raise: funds, taxes |
- Features of decentralisation: autonomy, people's participation and empowerment of local communities
- ⚠ Trap: Instilling non-confidence among local communities is not a feature (BPSC 2020)

## 1. Article 40 DPSP - organisation of village panchayats
- Compromise: Between Gandhi and Ambedkar → panchayats named but non-justiciable
- Practical shape: Given by the 73rd Amendment (UPSC)
- ⚠ 64th Bill (1989) failed in the Rajya Sabha, not the Lok Sabha
### Terms → meaning — subsidiarity and the 3Fs
| Term | Meaning |
| Subsidiarity | Function done at the lowest level competent to do it; moves up only if that level cannot |
| 3Fs | Funds, Functions, Functionaries |
- Subsidiarity: 2nd ARC invoked it for dividing functions among the three tiers
- 3Fs: Incomplete devolution → PRIs remain implementing agencies, not planning units
### Arguments and the Indian experience — issues and debates
| For centralisation | Against (i.e. for decentralisation) |
| Nation-building after Partition, unity and security | Proximity and information: local bodies know local needs |
| Planned heavy industry and food security needed a single command | Participation turns subjects into citizens |
| Local capacity is weak | Accountability and speed |
| Elite capture by locally dominant castes | Political education: a training ground for leaders |
## 1. Planning-era centralisation
- Deliberate centralisation: Five-Year Plans + CDP administered from above
- 1960s: Planning increasingly centralised; people's participation diluted
## 2. Outcome of devolution
- Outcome: India transferred political power, some administrative power, least fiscal power
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- SWM Rules, 2026 (in force 1 Apr 2026; under EPA 1986 and Art. 253): critics say they centralise local-body matters (13 May 2026)
