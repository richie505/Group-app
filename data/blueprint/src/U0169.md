### Northern Circars and the Mughal grant; Ceded Districts - region
## 1. Northern Circars and the Mughal grant
- Five Circars: Chicacole, Rajahmundry, Ellore, Mustafanagar (Kondapalli), Guntur (Murtazanagar)
- Modern districts: Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Visakhapatnam, East and West Godavari, Krishna + Guntur
- Mughal grant: Firman of Shah Alam II secured by Robert Clive, dated 1765 (some notes: 1766)
- Nizam at first refused it → Company negotiated a lease from him in 1766
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes call the 1753 grant to the French a Mughal grant + date Forde's capture to 1765-66; Forde's campaign was 1758-59 (see M1-A-52)
## 2. Ceded Districts - region and background
- Districts: Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur (AP) + Bellary (now Karnataka) = Kadapa (Cuddapah), Kurnool, Anantapur and Bellary = today's Rayalaseema (APPSC GS 2024; APHQ 2025)
- Background: Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan took these lands from Hyderabad
- Nizam Ali regained them as British ally in Mysore wars of 1790-92 and 1799
- Cession: Under the Subsidiary Alliance (1798) Nizam could not pay in cash → ceded them in 1800
### Ceded Districts - names; Carnatic districts, 1801
## 1. Ceded Districts - names
| Name | Fact |
| Dattamandalam / Dattamandalalu | Old name ("ceded lands") - used until 1928 |
| Rayalaseema | Suggested by Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao (APPSC G2 2012 key); adopted at the Andhra Mahasabha, Nandyal, 1928 (p.367), presided over by S. Radhakrishnan (some notes say 1927, p.357); other notes credit Chilukuri Narayana Rao |
## 2. Carnatic districts (Nellore, Prakasam area, part of Chittoor), 1801
- Earlier rule: Nellore and Chittoor under Aravidu Sriranga Raya III → then part of the Carnatic
- Muhammad Ali: Installed as Nawab by the British after the Second Carnatic War (secure only after 1763)
- Annexation: After Muhammad Ali's death, Company annexed the Carnatic districts in 1801
- Carnatic Treaty signed with Nawab Azim-ud-Daula

### Factories, Chiefs and Councils, Circuit Committee
## 1. Factories and the Chiefs and Councils
- Before acquisition: Company factories + godowns at Machilipatnam and Visakhapatnam; a supervising revenue body set over them
- Some notes call this body the "Board of Revenue", abolished 1794
- Chiefs and Councils: Provincial Chiefs and Councils at these stations governed the Circars until 1794 → replaced by district Collectors
- Chief-in-Council at Masulipatnam governed the Circars until then
## 2. Circuit Committee (Committee of Circuit)
- Purpose: Reviewed Circars' revenue; examined revenue administration
- Finding: Recommended against the auction (highest-bidder) system of revenue rights - hurt both government and ryots (p.417)
- Munro then abolished auctions in Rayalaseema
- Not in your sources: the year of appointment and the full findings on the Northern Circars (check the CDX Circars chapter).
### Collector system
## 1. Collector system
- British:
- 1794: The Collector's administration came into existence in place of the Board of Revenue
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say 1786 - Exam key: "Collector's administration in place of the Revenue Board" = 1786 (APPSC G2 2012); notes date the Collector system in coastal Andhra to 1794; in the exact PYQ wording, answer 1786
- 1802: The Office of Collector introduced in the North Sarkar region
- From 1794: Each district had a Collector - land revenue (main duty) + law and order + magistrate (District Magistrate/Judge)
- Judicial powers taken away in 1816 (separation)
- Under ryotwari, some magisterial powers returned to Collectors; civil justice stayed with the District Judge
## 2. Officers of the Company period
- G. N. Taylor: Sub-Collector of Rajahmundry — opened Anglo-vernacular schools
- V. Veeranna: An Andhra officer who helped Sir Arthur Cotton in the construction of the Godavari Barrage
## 3. Other eras (officers)
- Srinatha (Reddy period): Educational officer in the court of Pedakomati Vema Reddy

### Revenue terms → meaning — Moturpha, Peshkash, Haveli, Sayer, Abkari
| Term | Meaning |
| Moturpha | Tax on looms (APPSC G2 2011 key), part of a wider tax on trades and occupations (looms, shops, artisans) [GK]. It was NOT a salt tax. The Madras Native Association's 1861 petition complained about it |
| Peshkash / Peshcush | Share of revenue / tribute that zamindars paid to the British (APP; APP spells it "peshcush") (PYQ 2025: statement true) |
| Korunu | Revenue that zamindars collected from farmers (CDX) |
| Haveli lands | Lands under direct British control in coastal Andhra (APP) |
| Zamindari lands | Held by zamindars. 9/10 of the estimated revenue went to government and 1/10 to the zamindar; agreements ran for 10 years (CDX) |
| Sayer | Inland transit and customs duties on goods [GK] |
| Abkari | Excise on liquor and toddy [GK] |
- Exam angle: statement pairs. "Moturpha is a salt tax" is false; "Peshcush is the revenue collected from zamindars" is true.
- ⚠ Peshkash = zamindar → British; Korunu = farmer → zamindar
### Revenue systems — Permanent Settlement areas vs Ryotwari areas
- Before 1802: Zamindaris given by auction
- Permanent Settlement: Cornwallis, 1793 - replaced auctions with a fixed demand; applied in Andhra in 1802
| Feature | Zamindari (Permanent Settlement) | Ryotwari |
| Where | Estates in the Northern Circars: Vizianagaram, Bobbili, Pithapuram (CDI) | Rayalaseema first; later most of Coastal Andhra (CDI; APP) |
| Settled with | Zamindar (intermediary) | Ryot (cultivator) directly |
| Effect | Absentee landlordism; tenants impoverished (CDI) | Over-assessment (often 50%+ of produce); moneylenders; land alienation (CDI) |
- Mahalwari (village headmen collect): placed in Nellore
- Ryotwari:
- First region: Baramahal (1792) (now Salem/Krishnagiri, Tamil Nadu), under Captain Alexander Read with Munro
- In Andhra: began in the Ceded Districts in 1802
- Zamindari abolition: Madras Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act, 1948; in force 7 Sep 1949 (p.486; APHQ)
- Some notes say "1952" (p.358), but exam keys use the 1948 Act

### Munro's judicial reforms and police administration
## 1. Munro's judicial reforms
- Judicial Commission: Munro = President (1814); report submitted April 1816, mostly accepted
- Madras Regulations of 1816:
- Munsiff courts + Panchayat courts in every large village and district = courts of first instance
## 2. Police administration
- Cornwallis: Created thanas under darogas (Indian officers) + constables; darogas known for abuse of power
- Madras: Village watchmen supplemented by central police under a District Superintendent; feared for corruption
- Munro (1816): Moved police supervision from judges to Collectors

### Universal angles
## 1. Significance and debates
- Significance:
- Today's Andhra took shape via the Circars (1766), Guntur (1788), Ceded Districts (1800), Nellore-Chittoor (1801)
- Fertile, educated Circars vs arid, neglected Ceded Districts grew apart → later shaped the Sri Bagh Pact debate
- District Collector (revenue + magisterial + police powers) = pivot of British rule; ended village autonomy
- Criticism & debates: Collectors who did not know Telugu depended on corrupt dubashis / native subordinates
- AP angle:
- Munro = Telugu-speaking Collector, needed no interpreters (with Brown the exceptions - rare Telugu learners)
- Munro brought Ryotwari from the Bara Mahals to the Ceded Districts; died as Governor at Pattikonda (Kurnool), 1827
- Comparative note: Telangana stayed under the Nizam; coast + Rayalaseema passed to the Company
## 2. Current relevance
- Sericulture in Rayalaseema (20 Feb 2026): Traced to British promotion in the "Ceded Districts" in late 19th-early 20th centuries
- Silk hubs today: Dharmavaram and Madanapalle
- Revenue judicial service (1 May 2026): Supreme Court (CJI Surya Kant) issued notice on a plea for a separate "revenue judicial service" cadre
- Argues revenue officers without legal training should not decide land-title disputes, under Art. 14
