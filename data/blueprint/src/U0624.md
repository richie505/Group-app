### Northern Plains — regions and features
- Northern Plains: 6,52,000 sq km; 2,400 km long (W-E); 70-500 km wide
- Northern edge: Himalayan Frontal Fault
- Gradient: land drops only ~200 m over the 1,600 km from Delhi to the Bay
- Punjab plain: formed by Indus tributaries, doabs
- Ganga plain: about 3,75,000 sq km; alluvium 2,000-3,000 m thick
- 5 divisions: Ganga-Yamuna Doab (upper >200 m, middle 100-200 m, lower <100 m), Rohilkhand, Avadh, Bihar, Bengal plains
- Brahmaputra plain: Assam valley; river enters Bangladesh
- Kosi = "Sorrow of Bihar"
- Molassis basin: Mizoram in the Purvachal hills; soft unconsolidated deposits (NCERT)
### Indian Desert and the Rann of Kutch
- Thar location: west of the Aravallis; one of the mainland's four regions = "Thar - the desert region"
- IYB division:
- Great Desert: Rann of Kutch edge beyond the Luni, northward
- Order of features (APPSC-GS 2025 key): semi-arid/arid weather → sand dunes → thorny bushes → seasonal streams in rain
- Features: barchan and seif dunes, salt lakes, desertification spreading north
- Inland drainage: Luni, Machhu, Rupen, Saraswati, Banas, Ghaggar → drain into salt lakes or lost in sand
- Luni: only significant river; seasonal; ends in the Rann of Kutch
- Sambhar: saline lake
- Rann of Kutch origin: Kutch was an island amid seas and lagoons → aridity turned lowland into a salt plain
- Great Rann (north) vs Little Rann (south)
- Rann of Kutch (Gujarat): seasonal salt marsh; Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary; rich in solar energy
- Exam angle: the APPSC GS 2025 key orders desert features as arid climate → dunes → thorny bushes → seasonal streams.

### Plateaus of India
## 1. Indian plateaus
- Peninsular Plateau: An ancient, relatively stable crystalline landmass — includes the Deccan, Malwa and Chotanagpur regions
- Gondwana shield (Precambrian): triangular block of igneous and metamorphic rocks; tilts east → most rivers flow east
- Central Highlands: North of the Narmada; Bundelkhand and Baghelkhand are eastern extensions
- Malwa Plateau: Between the Aravalli and Vindhya ranges
- Bhander Plateau: The southern part of the Vindhyan scarpland
- Chotanagpur Plateau: Mineral-rich; drained by the Damodar — Damodar drainage
- Deccan Plateau: South of the Narmada — a predominantly basaltic volcanic origin; associated with the Deccan Trap — a basaltic lava plateau
- Meghalaya Plateau: A detached extension of the Peninsular Plateau
- Dandakaranya: A central-eastern — central or eastern Peninsular — highland region
## 2. Deccan Plateau
- Peninsular India — extends across Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu
- Extensive basaltic and lava formations; forms a major part of the Peninsular Plateau; associated with Peninsular India
## 3. Malwa and Bundelkhand
- Malwa Plateau: Extends across Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan — lies (is situated) between the Aravalli Range and the Vindhya Range
- The Aravalli lies north; the Vindhya lies south; Bundelkhand lies east — it extends eastward to Bundelkhand
- Bundelkhand Plateau: Central India — mainly Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh; east of the Malwa Plateau, forming its eastern border (the Malwa lies west of it)
## 4. Meghalaya Plateau
- A northeastern extension of the Peninsular Plateau — includes the Garo, Khasi and Jaintia Hills
- ⚠ The Bhuban Hills are distinct from the Meghalaya Plateau
### Peninsular Plateau — sub-divisions, Vindhya–Satpura and the Deccan Traps
## 1. Vindhyan and Satpura
- Vindhyan Range: Central India — north of the Narmada Valley; part of the central Indian highlands
- Mukandara Hills: An extension of the Vindhyan Range — southeastern Rajasthan
- Satpura Range: Central India — between the Narmada and Tapi valleys (flanked by the Narmada River and the Tapti River); part of the Deccan Plateau
- A major Central Indian range — an important physiographic range; highest peak Dhupgarh (see peaks table)
- The Vindhya and Satpura converge at Amarkantak — Amarkantak: in the Maikala range
## 2. Sub-divisions
| Sub-division | Location / rock | Facts |
| Central Highlands | North of Narmada; Aravalli, Malwa, Vindhyas | Avg 800-1,200 m, slope SW to NE; Aravalli = oldest; Vindhyas an escarpment (APP) |
| Malwa plateau | MP; Deccan Trap basalt | Vindhyas on its south (APP; CDI) |
| Deccan plateau | South of Narmada; (~7 lakh sq km) horizontal lava sheets; Deccan Trap = basalt (APPSC-GS 2025) | Inverted triangle, stable, oldest rocks (IYB 2026) |
| Chhotanagpur | Jharkhand (Ranchi plateau) | Iron, coal, manganese; Hundru Falls (APP) |
## 3. Deccan Traps
- Fissure eruptions of basaltic lava from end-Cretaceous to early Eocene
- Area: once about 10 lakh sq km → now about 5 lakh sq km (Kuchchh, Saurashtra, Maharashtra, Malwa, N Karnataka)
- Thickness: 3,000 m in the west → 150 m at the east
- Weathered into regur (black cotton soil)
