### Newspapers and writings → founders, editors, stand — Deshabhimani
## 1. Deshabhimani
- Anglo-Telugu paper, Guntur; only daily from British Andhra; early paper for the Andhra cause

- 4 Feb 1910: first call for a political meeting on Andhra problems

- Editor Seshagirirao: distant Madras capital + administrators ignorant of Telugu hurt Andhra

- Daily from 1914; moved to Madras
- Krishna Patrika founder: Konda Venkatappayya (with Dasu Narayana Rao), co-founded 1 Feb 1902; Mutnuri as editor

- Supporting papers: Krishna Patrika (1902), Andhra Patrika, Desamata, Darbar
- Swadesamitran: Tamil daily; backed Telugu-medium resolution of 1915

### Early meetings and the First Andhra Mahasabha, Bapatla 1913 → place, president, secretary — The province resolution deferred
## 1. The province resolution deferred
- Province demand: Separate province raised from 1911 onward
- Mover: Vemavarapu Ramadasu Pantulu (V. Ramadas); supported by V. Satyanarayana
- Opposed by: Nyapati Subba Rao, Mocherla Ramachandra Rao, Ganti Venkataramayya, M. Adinarayanaiah
- B.N. Sarma's caution: Separate-province demand might do "more harm than good" at that time (premature demand)
- Loyalty: Urged loyalty to the British + joint work for Andhra's uplift — "First we are Indians, then we are Andhras."
- Backwardness study: Alleged government favoured Tamil districts
- Outcome: Krishna Rao's amendment passed — collect public opinion, take up at next conference
- Committee under Pattabhi Sitaramayya set up to carry this out
- Disappointment: Krishna and Guntur delegates, who had campaigned for two years, disappointed by the deferment
- Exam angle: "The president of the first Mahasabha opposed a separate state. Who?" = B.N. Sarma (APPSC G2 2017).

## Potti Sriramulu
- Born 16 March 1901, in Madras, Arya Vysya family; ancestral village Gajula Dinne, Nellore
- Constructive work: khadi, prohibition, Harijan uplift
- Earlier fasts — Harijan causes and temple entry; 1952 his only fast for statehood
- Wardha (1948), 29 days — Harijan Day and a grant of one crore rupees; Madras marked Harijan Welfare Day on the 30th of every month
- Final fast began 19 October 1952 — at Fort St. George gate, then Bulusu Sambamurthy's house, Mylapore
- Three conditions: immediate announcement, unity among Andhra leaders, Madras a Chief Commissioner's State by agreement
- Died on the 58th day, 15 December 1952 — 'Amarajeevi' (Immortal Martyr); also "Andhra Martyra Martanda"
- Riots and police firing — seven died
- 19 December 1952 — Nehru announced Andhra State in Parliament from undisputed Telugu areas, excluding Madras city
- 58-foot 'Statue of Sacrifice', Amaravati — unveiled 16 March 2026 (125th birth anniversary) by CM Chandrababu Naidu

## 2. Significance: Andhra exception and movement
- "Andhra exception": Opened the way to the SRC and nationwide linguistic reorganisation
- Dhar and JVP both rejected language as a basis; yet JVP's Andhra exception (minus Madras) became the formula used in 1953
- Potti Sriramulu's death: Forced the Centre's hand → Andhra → SRC (Dec 1953) → States Reorganisation Act 1956
- Pattern for SRC: First state formed on language → SRC (Fazl Ali, Kunzru, Panikkar; announced 22 Dec 1953)
- Propaganda phase (1913-20): Four goals
- Andhra State + Andhra University + development of Telugu + cultural revival
- Bapatla: "Sowed the seeds" of the Andhra movement — first organised Telugu platform → Andhra State of 1 October 1953
- Annual resolutions and memorandums: Kept the "Andhra problem" on the national agenda until 1953
- 1920 linguistic PCCs: Congress's own promise of linguistic states; Andhra (1953) first to claim it
- University fight: Built a distinct Telugu intellectual identity; seen as a forerunner of Andhra State

## 5. Criticism & debates: regionalism and delays
- Regional suspicion: Rayalaseema and Nellore leaders saw the body as Circars/Brahmin-led → district-wise vote of 1917 was a response
- Leadership splits: Circars vs Rayalaseema; Prakasam vs Pattabhi — repeatedly slowed the movement
- University seat/jurisdiction: Left lasting Rayalaseema resentment → addressed in the Sri Bagh Pact
- Losing Madras: New state "administratively and economically orphaned"; had to use undeveloped Kurnool as capital
