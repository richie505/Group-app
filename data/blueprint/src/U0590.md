# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:1 - Earth - shape, size, geoid; latitudes and longitudes, the graticule
### Shape
- 1° of latitude ≈ 111 km everywhere; 1° of longitude shrinks to zero at the poles
- Geoid / oblate spheroid: bulges at equator, flat at poles
- Aristotle: first stated source for spherical Earth
- Weight: minimum at equator, maximum at poles (centrifugal force max at equator)
- Evidence: ship's mast seen first; circular shadow in lunar eclipse; circumnavigation

- Ice melt → equator bulges, rotation slows (~1.33 ms per century)

### Shape of the Earth — terms, evidences and changes
## 1. Shape
- Shape: A geoid or oblate spheroid — wider at the equator and flatter at the poles
- Aristotle: The first stated source for Earth's spherical shape
- Weight variation: Caused by rotation and its non-spherical shape
- Centrifugal force: Maximum at the equator
- An object's weight: Minimum at the equator; maximum at the poles
| Term | Meaning |
| Geoid | "earth-shaped" |
| Oblate spheroid | Flattened at the poles, bulging at the Equator |
- Evidences of sphericity:
- Earth's circular shadow in a lunar eclipse
- Circumnavigation; satellite images
- Nearest surface point to centre: The poles (polar radius shortest) → Arctic Ocean floor near the North Pole
- Ice melt effect: Mass moves toward the Equator → bulging the Equator and slowing rotation; days lengthen ~1.33 ms per century
### Data — dimensions of the Earth
- Equatorial diameter: 12,756 km; polar diameter: 12,714 km; equatorial circumference: 40,090 km

### Graticule — terms, counts, distances and traps
## 1. Parallels and meridians
- Parallels at 1° interval: 179 (89 N + 89 S + Equator; poles are points - counting them as parallels gives 181)
- Meridians: 360
| Term | Meaning |
| Great circle | Plane through Earth's centre: Equator (the only great-circle parallel) + every pair of opposite meridians |
| Small circles | All other parallels |
| Antipode | Diametrically opposite point: antipode of (x°N, y°E) = (x°S, (180 - y)°W) |
- Antipode example: 20°N 80°E → 20°S 100°W
## 2. Valid coordinates and special points
- Valid values: Latitude 0-90° N/S; longitude 0-180° E/W
- 0° lat, 0° long: Gulf of Guinea, Atlantic Ocean
## 3. Degree-to-distance
- 1° of latitude: ≈ 111 km everywhere
- 1° of longitude: ≈ 111 km at the Equator → ~55.5 km at 60° → zero at the poles (meridians converge)

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:1
### Shape
- ★ First to say Earth is round: Aristotle (384–322 BC), in "On the Heavens"
- Geoid = Earth-shaped; Earth is an oblate spheroid (flat at poles, wider at Equator)
- Proof of roundness: Earth's circular shadow on the Moon in a lunar eclipse (also mast-first ships, circumnavigation, satellite images)
- Weight minimum at the equator, maximum at the poles: spin and equatorial bulge lower felt gravity
- ★ Stars rise perpendicular to the horizon for an observer at the Equator (celestial poles on the horizon)
- ★ Arctic and Antarctica: near the North and South Poles
- India's point nearest the Equator: Indira Point (6°45' N); the Equator does not touch India
### Dimensions
- Water : land ≈ 71 : 29
- Equatorial diameter 12,756 km; polar diameter 12,714 km; equatorial circumference ~40,090 km
### Graticule facts
- ★ First to split the circle into 360 degrees: Hipparchus (Greek; took from Babylonian maths)
- Equator: the only great-circle parallel; all other parallels are small circles
- 0° lat, 0° long: Gulf of Guinea, Atlantic Ocean
- Antipode: swap N↔S and take longitude from 180° — 20°N, 80°E → 20°S, 100°W
- 179 parallels (89 N + 89 S + Equator) and 360 meridians
- One degree of longitude: 111 km at Equator, 55.5 km at 60°, zero at poles
### Key lines
- ★ Iceland lies south of the Arctic Circle; Arctic Circle passes through Finland, Norway, Sweden
- Equator passes through Kenya, Colombia, Indonesia; not Rwanda (just south) nor Equatorial Guinea
- Prime Meridian: UK, France, Spain, Algeria, Mali, Burkina Faso, Togo, Ghana; not Niger
- Arctic Circle 66.5°N; 23.5°N = Tropic of Cancer
- Noon Sun at the Tropic of Cancer on the December solstice: 43° (90° − 47°)
- North to south: Srinagar, Bhopal, Visakhapatnam, Chennai
### Firsts & maps
- ★ "Father of geography": Eratosthenes; first to measure Earth's circumference correctly, and coined "Geography"
- Anaximander: first Greek map; Ptolemy: used latitude and longitude
- Prime Meridian (Greenwich) fixed at the International Meridian Conference, 1884
- Roald Amundsen: first to the South Pole, 1911; Peary's North Pole claim doubted
