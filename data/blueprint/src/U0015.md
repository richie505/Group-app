### Linguistic and epigraphic evidence — Indo-European family, Boghazkoi, Avesta
## 1. Indo-European family
- Vedic Sanskrit: Belongs to the Indo-European language family (Europe, Asia, Middle East)
- Share: Indo-European (Aryan) family = ~73% of Indian speakers
- Branches: Iranian (outside India), Dardic (J&K), Indo-Aryan
## 2. Inscription and text → what it shows
| Evidence | Where / when | What it shows |
| Boghazkoi inscription | Boghazkoi, Asia Minor (in Anatolia, modern Turkey) [GK]; c. 14th century BCE (c. 1400 BCE) | Peace treaty between the Hittites and the rulers of Mitanni; invokes Vedic gods Indra, Mitra, Varuna and Nasatyas |
| Avesta | Iran - oldest Iranian (Zoroastrian) text [GK] | Language and ideas closely parallel the Rigveda: common Indo-Iranian stock |
- Significance of Boghazkoi:
- Vedic-speaking people/gods in West Asia → supports a Central Asian homeland
- Movement of Aryans from there towards Iran and India
- Exam angle: "Which inscription names Indra, Varuna, Mitra and Nasatyas?" - Boghazkoi (distractors: Mansehra, Shahbazgarhi, Junagadh, Ecbatana, Behistun).

### Archaeological cultures → region + link
- Migration arguments:
- Horse and (spoked-wheel) chariot archaeology; Harappans used solid wheels
- Changes in pottery, burial practices and settlements after Harappan decline
| Culture | Region | Link to the Aryan question |
| Andronovo | Central Asian steppes (Bronze Age, 2nd millennium BCE) [GK] | Steppe pastoralists with horses and chariots; linked to Indo-Iranian speakers |
| BMAC (Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex) | Turkmenistan-Uzbekistan-north Afghanistan [GK] | Contact zone the migrants passed through |
| Gandhara Grave culture | Swat valley, north-west Pakistan [GK] | Horse remains and new burial practices; often correlated with early Indo-Aryans |
- Open question: Link between PGW archaeology and Later Vedic textual geography - Vedic archaeology still unsettled
### Migration vs indigenous debate → proponents and arguments
| Theory | Proponents | Key arguments | Status |
| Migration | Max Muller, Mortimer Wheeler, Asko Parpola | Indo-European linguistics; Rigvedic geography; horse/chariot archaeology; genetics (R1a haplogroup) | Mainstream: Indo-Aryan speakers came from Central Asia c. 1500 BCE, then synthesis with local people |
| Indigenous / Out of India | S.R. Rao, B.B. Lal, Shrikant Talageri | Vedic-Sarasvati continuity; Harappan-script decipherment claims; astronomical dating of the Rigveda | Marginal view: weak linguistic and archaeological support |
| Synthesis | Romila Thapar, Upinder Singh | Small groups migrated; elite dominance; cultural assimilation; no "invasion" | Nuanced consensus: migration + interaction, not replacement |
- Indigenous school: Vedic-Sarasvati continuity - B.B. Lal: Sarasvati = Rigvedic river; PGW-Vedic link
- Rakhigarhi DNA: Female skeleton showed no "Steppe Pastoralist" gene → cited against invasion theories, for indigenous ancestral continuity
- Genetics overall: Complex mix of migration, admixture and continuity; no final answer - read with archaeology + linguistics
### Aryan–Harappan relationship — chronology and comparison
- Chronology: Harappan decline first → Vedic culture pastoral, mobile and warrior-like (Early Vedic 1500-1000 BCE)
| Feature | Harappan | Rigvedic |
| Settlement | Urban, planned cities | Rural, pastoral; fortified mud settlements |
| Horse | No evidence of awareness (per UPSC 2017 key) | Domesticated; central to warfare |
| Armour | No evidence | Coat of mail and helmet used in warfare |
| Transmission | Seals and script | Oral hymns |
### Universal angles
- Rakhigarhi:
- Mound 7 burial plot → 56 skeletons, incl. a ~4,600-year-old woman
- Site ~550 ha = largest known Harappan settlement; Hisar district; on the seasonal Drishadvati
- Criticism & debates: "Aryan" as race = colonial idea; indigenist views often called ideologically driven; genetics still contested
