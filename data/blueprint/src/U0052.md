### Donative inscriptions → features
- Nature: Mostly Prakrit ayaka-pillar records dated in Virapurushadatta's 6th regnal year
- Each names the donor by her tie to the king (aunt, sister, queen, daughter)
- Dating first: Ikshvakus first to date inscriptions by year, month and day
- Chamtisri's inscription: Prays for Virapurushadatta's long life, victory and prosperity
### Economic terms, guilds and titles → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Bhaga = 1/6 | Land tax (Revenue) of produce |
| Halikas | Farmers |
| Nigamalu (also spelt Negimalu) | Guild name - continuation of Satavahana srenis |
| Parnika Sreni | Betel-leaf traders (the most prominent) |
| Poosika Sreni | Sweetmeat traders |
| Sale / Telika / Kammaru | Weavers / oil pressers / blacksmiths (other trading groups) |
- ⚠ Parnika = betel leaf; Poosika = sweetmeat
### Agrarian economy — Chamtamula's gifts and use of revenue
- Donor: Vasishthiputra Sri Santamula
- Brahmins: Brought from the north + given agraharas
- Sacrifices: Ashvamedha, Vajapeya, Agnistoma (Ashvamedha altar found at Nagarjunakonda)
- Revenue split (four ways):
- Administration + army
- Temples + festivals
- Poets + scholars
- Gifts to religious bodies
- Some went to tanks and canals
### Trade, ports, exports-imports and coins
- Roman trade: Lower than under the Satavahanas
- Some notes go further: after Rome's fall maritime trade virtually collapsed → villages self-sufficient
- Foreign contact evidence:
- Roman gold and silver coins at Nagarjunakonda
- Roman-style amphitheatre at Vijayapuri (Romans said to have built a stadium under Sri Santamula)
| Trade item | Goods |
| Exports | Spices, iron, coconuts, cotton textiles |
| Imports | Gold, silver, wine, glassware |
- Coin symbols: Ujjain symbol, elephant, lion (royal emblem)
- Coin find-spots: Guntur, Krishna, Godavari districts
### Society — varna order, clans and marriage customs
- Brahmins: Rose through Brahmadeya grants (exempt from 18 taxes)
- Shudras: Majority
- New class: Landlord class emerged
- Cross-cousin marriage: (Maternal uncle's daughter) began with the Ikshvakus - Virapurushadatta married his aunts' daughters
- Alliances:
- Kshatrapas of Ujjain
- Chutus - via Kodabalisri's marriage to Vishnurudra Sivalananda Satakarni
- Matronymics: Kings used them (Vasishthiputra)
- Naming: Children named after grandfather (Ehuvala Santamula)
### Universal angles
- Significance: Andhra's move from Satavahana trade economy → agrarian, agrahara-based early medieval order

### Royal women donors
- Chamtisri — seen as the first woman donor
- sister of Sri Santamula, paternal aunt of Virapurushadatta
- built or restored the Maha Chaitya at Nagarjunakonda in Virapurushadatta's sixth regnal year
- Upasika Bodhisri — non-royal donor; daughter of Revanta of Amaravati
- linked with the Sinhala Vihara and viharas for foreign monks (not a vihara for the Aparamahavinaseliya sect)
### Donative inscriptions
- Royal women gave to Buddhism without male intervention (kings were Vedic)
- Most Nagarjunakonda records — Prakrit Ayaka-pillar inscriptions dated to Virapurushadatta's sixth regnal year
### Economic terms & guilds
| Term | Meaning |
| Bhaga | land tax, one-sixth of produce |
| Halikas | farmers |
| Nigamalu (also Negimalu) | guilds; continued the Satavahana sreni tradition |
| Sale | weavers |
| Telikas / Telika | oil pressers |
| Kammarus | blacksmiths |
| Parnika Sreni | betel-leaf traders |
| Poosika Sreni | sweetmeat traders |
### Agrarian economy
- Vasishthiputra Sri Santamula — brought Brahmins from the north; gave them agraharas
- Santamula — performed Ashvamedha, Vajapeya and Agnistoma; Ashvamedha altar at Nagarjunakonda
- Four-way division of state revenue: administration and army; temples and festivals; poets and scholars; gifts to religious bodies
- not spread of overseas colonies; some revenue also went to tanks and canals
- Ikshvaku period — shift from the Satavahana trade economy to an agrarian, agrahara-based early medieval order
### Trade & coins
- Roman trade under the Ikshvakus lower than under the Satavahanas; one account: sea trade almost collapsed after the fall of Rome
- Roman contact: Roman gold and silver coins at Nagarjunakonda; Roman-style amphitheatre at Vijayapuri
- Romans said to have built a stadium under Sri Santamula
- Exports: spices, iron, coconuts, cotton textiles; imports: gold, silver, wine, glassware
- Coins of lead and copper; symbols: Ujjain symbol, elephant, lion; found in Guntur, Krishna and Godavari districts
### Society
- Shudras — majority; Brahmins gained from Brahmadeya grants; new landlord class arose
- Marriage alliances with the Kshatrapas of Ujjain and the Chutus; cross-cousin marriage began with them
- Ikshvaku inscriptions record: naming after grandfather (Ehuvala Santamula), matronymics (Vasishthiputra), sati
