## 1. Wars, causes and treaties
| War | Years | European context | Concluding treaty | Key term / result |

- Exam angle: match-the-pair of war and treaty; "Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle ended the First war and returned Madras" - both correct.
### Wars → years, European context, treaty and participants — Treaty of Pondicherry (1754) - terms
## 1. Treaty of Pondicherry (1754) - terms
- Terms: Both companies not to interfere in the quarrels of Indian princes; each kept its existing possessions
- Failure: Seven Years' War broke out before England + France ratified it → Third war began before the Treaty of Pondicherry could be ratified
## 2. Participants of each war
- First: British vs French; Nawab Anwaruddin of the Carnatic on the English side (APPSC 2018: fought between the British and the French)
- Second: No war in Europe – two succession disputes
- Hyderabad (after Nizam-ul-Mulk died in 1748): Nasir Jang (son, English) vs Muzaffar Jang (grandson, French)
- Carnatic: Anwaruddin (English) vs Chanda Sahib (French)
- Muhammad Ali (Anwaruddin's son) = English candidate after 1749

- Clive — beat Raja Sahib and Chanda Sahib at Arni and Kaveripak (1751–52)
## Third war
- Bussy — kept Salabat Jang in power at Hyderabad; won the Northern Circars (1753); later captured at Wandiwash
- Count de Lally — captured Fort St. David (1758); surrendered Pondicherry (16 January 1761)
- Francis Forde — led the English at Chandurti (1758); captured Machilipatnam (1759)
- Pocock — English admiral; d'Ache — French admiral, defeated by the English fleet
- Chendurthi (1758) — the "Plassey of Andhra": drove the French out of the Northern Circars
