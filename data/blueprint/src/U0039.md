# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:38 - Mauryan economy, society and urban life; Mauryan art and architecture (pillars, Sarnath capital, Barabar caves, Didarganj Yakshi)
### Economy
- Shift from wood to stone: Mauryan stone art began Indian monumental architecture
- Sita: crown land; slaves and hired labour
- Rainfall measured in drona
- Bhaga 1/4 to 1/6 (Megasthenes)
- State monopoly: mining, forestry, salt, liquor, arms
- Interest 15% a year; pottery = NBPW
- Coin: silver Pana (APPSC G2 2011); copper Mashaka, Kakani

### Agriculture, state monopolies and taxes — terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Sita | Crown land cultivated directly for/under the state (Sitadhyaksha), often by slaves and hired labourers; large-scale use of slaves in farming |
| Drona | Unit for measuring rainfall (~13.2 kg water) near granaries, for six regions |
| Dronavapa | Land sown with one drona of seed |
| Bhaga (land revenue) | 1/4 to 1/6 of the produce, collected directly by royal officials (Megasthenes) |
| Kara | Tax on orchards |
- Famine relief: seed, cattle, tools in drought and famine
- Rain gauge: rainfall measured in drona
- State control over: mining, forestry, salt, liquor, arms and metallurgy
### Crafts, guilds, credit and coins
- Growth of crafts: Good roads + state incentives spread crafts
- Credit: Usury prevailed in absence of banking; interest 15% a year (Arthashastra); students, poor and sick got debt relief
- Pottery: NBPW = typical Mauryan pottery
- Guilds - patronage: merchants (shreshthis), artisans and shrenis financed early Buddhist centres, including in Andhra
- Guilds - military role: Arthashastra counts shreni troops among army types
- Imperial currency: Punch-marked silver coins with symbols such as peacocks and hills
- Punch-marked coins + NBPW sherds found as far as Bangladesh, Odisha, Andhra and Karnataka
- Punch-marked silver panas/karshapanas go back to the Mahajanapada age (6th century BCE)

- Three northern routes: Shravasti-Rajgir, Shravasti-Pratishthan, Rajgir-Sindhu-Sauvira

### Megasthenes' Indica → observations on society
- Indica: by Megasthenes (Greek; envoy of Seleucus)
- Seven classes (BPSC 2017): philosophers, farmers, herdsmen/hunters, artisans, soldiers, overseers, councillors
- Hereditary occupation + endogamy
- Other observations: said India had no slavery (no slaves); theft rare; no written law
- ⚠ Megasthenes: no slaves; Kautilya: nine categories of slaves - sources differ
### Arthashastra on society — castes, learning, slaves and women
## 1. Caste and learning
- Mixed castes: five mixed castes called Antyavasayin, outside Aryan society
- Learned men: called Sishtas
- Centres of learning: Taxila (most famous; UKPSC 2010), Ujjain, Varanasi
## 2. Slavery and labour
- Categories: Kautilya lists nine categories of slaves (dasas); Ashokan inscriptions also mention dasas
- Sources of slavery: birth, sale (self-sale), capture in war, judicial punishment
- No Arya could be made a slave
- Female slave: bore her master a son → became free; son legitimate, entitled to the legal status of the master's son
- ⚠ PYQ: UPSC 2022 set these three statements
- Protective rules:
- Penalty for selling a pregnant slave without arranging maternity
- Freedom by payment

## 3. Women
