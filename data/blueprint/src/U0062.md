### Gupta sources → ruler, author, record
## 1. Gupta inscriptions
| Inscription | Associated with | Key facts |
| Prayag Prashasti — the Allahabad Pillar inscription | Samudragupta — authored (composed) by Harisena | Sanskrit in Brahmi script; a panegyric or eulogy — Samudragupta's political and military achievements; southern expeditions; rulers of Dakshinapatha; Mahendra as a Koshala ruler |
| Mehrauli Iron Pillar | A king named Chandra — identified as Chandragupta II | Devoted to Vishnu — established as a Vishnu Dhvaja; associated with Vishnupada; claims (conquests in) NW India and most of Bengal |
| Bhitari Pillar | Skandagupta | Ghazipur, Uttar Pradesh — the Huna invasion or war (repulse of the Hunas [GK]) |
| Eran Inscription (Sagar, MP) | The wife of the military commander Gopraj; Samudragupta [GK]; later Bhanugupta | The first inscriptional evidence of Sati — 510 AD (Bhanugupta's record, 510 CE); governors called Lokapala (CDI) |
| Mandsaur or Dashpur Inscription | Kumaragupta I — governor Bandhuvarman (feudatory [GK]); composed by Vatsabhatti | Mandsaur or Dashpur in Malwa — a silk-weavers guild (Tantuwayas) from the Lata region or Gujarat built a Sun Temple |
| Udayagiri cave (near Vidisha) | Chandragupta II | Virasena, his minister - conquests; Varaha image |
| Junagadh (Girnar) | Skandagupta | Governor Parnadatta repaired the Sudarshana lake |
## 2. Literary sources → author
- Literary sources:
- Mudrarakshasa (Vishakhadatta): political drama on Chanakya + Chandragupta Maurya
- Devichandraguptam (Vishakhadatta): Ramagupta episode - Ramagupta, Dhruvadevi and Chandragupta II story
- Puranas (e.g. Vayu): describe early Gupta territory
- ⚠ Both Mudrarakshasa and Devichandraguptam = Vishakhadatta
## 3. Coins
- Coins: Mostly gold; show rulers, deities, rituals → evidence of titles, religion and prosperity
### Allahabad Prasasti — Harishena, language, the pillar
- Allahabad Pillar: Originally associated with Ashoka; originally located at Kaushambi; Akbar shifted it to Allahabad or Prayagraj
- Contains: An Ashokan inscription; the Queen's Edict of Karuvaki; Samudragupta's inscription composed by Harisena; a Persian inscription of Jahangir (UPSC Art & Culture)
- Allahabad Pillar (Prayaga Prashasti): Other name Prayaga-prashasti; inscribed on an Ashokan pillar [GK]
- Nature: Sanskrit eulogy of Samudragupta's conquests (APPSC-GS 2025 key); mixed prose + verse (champu style)
- Samudragupta's conquests: Recorded in the Allahabad Pillar → not Mehrauli, Junagadh or Nasik (APPSC 2025)
- Composer: Court poet Harisena (Harishena), author of the Prayaga prashasti
- Posts held at once: Kumaramatya, Sandhivigrahika + Mahadandanayaka
- ⚠ Trap: Not the later Jain author Harisena of the Brihatkatha Kosha → a different person
- Contrast: Glorifies war vs Ashoka's message of peace on the same pillar
### Gupta origin and Chandragupta I — FIRSTs
## 1. Origin
- Origin: Uncertain and debated; rose from modest beginnings
- Caste:
- Originally Vaishyas → later treated by brahmanas as Kshatriyas
- Brahmana origin also argued from the Dharana gotra of Prabhavatigupta
- Home:
- Sri Gupta ruled a small part of North Bengal and South Bihar
- Early territory = Magadha, Prayag and eastern UP
- Line: Sri Gupta (c. 240-280) → Ghatotkacha (c. 280-319), both "Maharaja"
## 2. Chandragupta I
- Status: Chandragupta I (c. 319-335 CE) = first important king / real founder
- Capital: Pataliputra
- Territory: South Bihar, eastern UP (Saketa, Prayaga), Bengal
- Marriage: Kumaradevi, a Lichchhavi princess (Nepal) → legitimacy, prestige and power
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes say Ghatotkacha married her → wrong; she was Chandragupta I's queen
- Gupta Era (Gupta Samvat): Started 319-20 CE = date of his accession
- Coins: First Gupta gold coins (dinaras)
- King-queen type showing Chandragupta I + Kumaradevi
- Issued in the joint names of king, queen and Lichchhavis
- Saka victory: He did not defeat the Sakas → that was Chandragupta II
- ⚠ Trap: APPSC-G1 2024 statement I is false; crediting Chandragupta I with silver coins + Saka victory mixes him up with Chandragupta II

### Samudragupta's campaigns — four-fold policy
- Classification: Opponents sorted into four groups (Prashasti)
| Group | Treatment | Regions |
| North (Aryavarta) | Rulers slain and annexed | Direct annexation of the Ganga-Yamuna region; Atavika rajyas (forest kingdoms of central India) subdued |
| Frontier kings | Paid tribute | Assam, Nepal, Bengal |
| Southern kings | Defeated but reinstated as tributaries | Dakshinapatha (south) |
| Distant kings | Accepted his suzerainty | Kushanas/Kushans, Sakas/Shakas, Sri Lanka (Ceylon), SE Asia → homage, diplomacy and tribute |
### Southern campaign (Dakshinapatha) → king and kingdom
- Kings: 12 kings between the Ganga and the Kaveri defeated, captured and reinstated as tributaries
- Policy: Grahana-moksha-anugraha (capture, release, favour) → no permanent annexation in the south (APPSC-G1 2024 key)

### Samudragupta — Ashvamedha, coin types, minister, foreign contacts

## 2. Court and foreign contacts
- Sri Lankan mission: Meghavarman, Buddhist king of Sri Lanka → sent a mission to Samudragupta
- Purpose: permission to build a monastery and rest house at Bodh Gaya
- Recorded in a Chinese source (BPSC 2023 key)
- Homage: Kushanas and Sakas paid homage; kings of Southeast Asia and Ceylon sent tribute
- Fa-Hien: Travelled in Chandragupta II's empire (UPSC 2025) → never names the king
