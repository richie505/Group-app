### Space sector reforms 2020 → FDI limits by segment
| Segment | Automatic route up to (revised FDI policy) |
| Satellite components and subsystems manufacturing | 100% |
| Satellite manufacturing and operation, data products | Up to 74% |
| Launch vehicles and spaceports | Up to 49% |
- Beyond limits: Government route applies
- Overall: FDI up to 100% in non-sensitive segments (2025-26)
- Segment limits from 20 Jul 2026 / May 2026 notes
- ⚠ 100 – 74 – 49 = components – satellites/data – launch vehicles/spaceports
### Indian Space Policy 2023 → roles of ISRO, IN-SPACe, NSIL, DoS and NGEs
- Approval: Approved by Union Cabinet 6 April 2023; released by DoS 20 April 2023
- Background: Follows June 2020 reforms that opened the sector to NGEs
| Entity | Role under the policy |
| ISRO | R&D in advanced space technology, deep-space and human spaceflight; to move out of making operational/commercial satellites and rockets over time |
| IN-SPACe | Independent regulator - promotes, authorises and supervises NGE activity |
| NSIL | Commercialises mature ISRO technology; launch and satellite services; builds launchers and satellites through industry |
| DoS | Overall policy, international cooperation, space diplomacy, treaty compliance, coordination |
| NGEs / private | End-to-end activity: satellites, launch vehicles (rockets), launches, ground stations, space services, data |
- Debate: ISRO's nine employee associations / unions sought clarity on outsourcing and ISRO's future role (Sept 2026)
- IN-SPACe + ISRO: ISRO "will not be privatised or reduced" (7-8 Sep 2026)
### Organisations → IN-SPACe and NSIL (vs Antrix)
## 1. IN-SPACe — role and leadership
- Nature: Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre, established 2020
- Autonomous single-window agency under DoS / within DoS
- Promote, authorise and supervise non-governmental entities (NGEs); gives them access to DoS facilities
- ⚠ Trap: It is the regulator; ISRO is not
- HQ: Ahmedabad
- Chairman: Dr Pawan Kumar Goenka (7 Sep 2026)
## 2. IN-SPACe — authorisations and schemes
- Applications: 834 applications received, 499 processed (294 satellites, 103 launch vehicles)
- Authorisations:
- First private launch Vikram-S (2022)
- Agnikul's launch
- PPP EOS constellation led by Pixxel with Dhruva Space, SatSure Analytics India and PierSight Space (LENSD, 20 Jul 2026)
- Enabled SSLV technology transfer to private consortia (Feb 2026)
- Schemes: IN-SPACe Seed Fund Scheme for space start-ups
## 3. NSIL — institution
- NewSpace India Limited: CPSE under DoS (Department of Space); incorporated 2019; HQ Bengaluru
- Commercial arm: technology transfer, launch services, satellite services
| Feature | Antrix Corporation | NSIL |
| Set up | September 1992, private limited company; Miniratna (2008) | 2019 CPSE |
| Model | Supply-driven: markets ISRO products, transponder leasing, remote-sensing data, consultancy | Demand-driven: owns satellites, industry production of launch vehicles, tech transfer |
- ⚠ HQ: IN-SPACe = Ahmedabad; NSIL = Bengaluru
### NSIL → demand-driven missions, DTH leasing, launches and technology transfer
- Demand-driven model: NSIL builds, owns, operates satellites against assured/committed customer demand
| Mission | Fact |
| GSAT-24 (2022) | NSIL's first demand-driven mission; launched from Kourou, French Guiana; capacity leased to Tata Play for DTH [GK for lessee] |
| GSAT-N2 (GSAT-20) (2024) | Fully funded and owned by NSIL; Ka-band broadband and in-flight connectivity; launched on SpaceX Falcon 9 (Nov 2024) [GK for launch details] |
- PYQ: APPSC-G1 2024 key accepted "first demand-driven satellite fully funded and owned by NSIL" for GSAT-N2 (statements I and III correct)
- Leasing: 10 in-orbit communication satellites transferred to NSIL in 2022
- Now operates 11 communication satellites, ~300 transponders, 150+ lease agreements
- Launches: 141 satellites (138 international/customer, 3 Indian) as of July 2026 (earlier: 127)
- LVM3's 2022 flight carrying 36 OneWeb satellites = first dedicated commercial mission
- Technology transfer: 70 Technology Transfer Agreements (Dec 2025); earlier 19 agreements, 8 technologies transferred
### Acts, Bills and conventions → space legislation and gaps
| Law / instrument | Year | Provision / status |
| Space Activities Bill (draft) | 2017 | First put out; proposed licensing of private space activity |
| Liability Convention | 1972 | Makes the launching State liable for damage |
- Status: India has no Space Activities Act yet; sector runs on policies + guidelines
- Gaps:
- No statutory framework on liability, dispute resolution and risk-sharing for private entities
- No sovereign-backed insurance (23 Jun 2026)
- Expected law: To cover dual-use regulation + treaty compliance
