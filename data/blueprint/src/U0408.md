### PCR Act 1955 and PoA Act 1989 — policies, enactment and applicability
## 1. Casteism and atrocities
- Protection of Civil Rights (PCR) Act: Enacted 1955
- SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Amendment Act: Enacted 2018
- National Toll-Free Helpline: 14566
- Mechanism: Automatic FIR registration without a preliminary inquiry; central assistance for atrocity victim relief; incentives for inter-caste marriage
## 2. Origin and scope — PCR Act
- Origin: Enacted under Art. 17 as the Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955; amended extensively + renamed in 1976 as the Protection of Civil Rights (PCR) Act, 1955
- Extent: Whole of India (APPSC 2019 key)
- Rules: PCR Rules 1977 (notified 1977)
## 3. SC and ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 — enactment and applicability
- Enacted (passed) in 1989; came into force on January 30, 1990 (IYB: "1990"); amended in 2018 — on August 20, 2018; 23 sections
- Prevents atrocities against Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes; contains an explicit list of atrocities
- Applicability: Only offences by non-SC/ST persons against SCs/STs
- BNS/BNSS: Since 1 July 2024, references to IPC and CrPC read as corresponding BNS/BNSS provisions
- Jammu & Kashmir: ⚠ UPSC note that the Act excludes J&K is outdated - applies there since 2019

### Amendments and cases — Section 18A, dilution and restoration
## 1. Section 18A
- Does not allow anticipatory bail — Section 18A bars anticipatory bail
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the bar on anticipatory bail is Sec. 18 (CrPC Sec. 438, now Sec. 482 BNSS), with 18A adding the bar "notwithstanding any court order"
- Section 18A removes the requirement of a preliminary inquiry and of prior sanction before arrest
## 2. Dilution and restoration
- Subhash Kashinath Mahajan v State of Maharashtra (20 March 2018): Seen as diluting the Act (APPSC G2 2024 key)
- Anticipatory bail allowed where no prima facie case
- Preliminary enquiry before FIR
- Prior approval before arrest
- 2018 Amendment Act: Inserted Sec. 18A
- No preliminary enquiry; no approval needed before arrest; FIR registered immediately (Madras HC ruling applied it)
- Anticipatory bail barred notwithstanding any court order
- ⚠ Trap: APPCA calls it "Section 4A"; the correct number is 18A
- Review (Oct 2019): SC recalled the Mahajan directions
- Prithvi Raj Chauhan v Union of India (Feb 2020): Upheld the 2018 amendment; courts may grant anticipatory bail or quash only where no prima facie case exists

### PCR Act 1955 and PoA Act 1989 — policies, enactment and applicability
## Enactment
- ★ PoA Act — Presidential assent 11 September 1989; in force 30 January 1990
- ★ PoA Act has 23 sections
- ★ Based on the doctrine of protective discrimination (Article 15(4))
- ★ Atrocity law implemented under the Act of 1989
- ★ Central Government makes rules — Section 23(1)
- ★ PCR Act extends to the whole of India — Section 1(2)
## Investigation and courts
- ★ Investigation by officer not below Deputy Superintendent of Police (Rules 1995)
- ★ Section 14 — Special Court and Exclusive Special Court for speedy trial; Section 15 — Special Public Prosecutor
- ★ State Government specifies a Court of Session as Special Court
- ★ Appeal to High Court on both facts and law
- ★ Section 18 — Section 438 (anticipatory bail) does not apply
- ★ Special Court cannot authorise a person other than police for extra investigation
- ★ Special Court cannot impose a collective fine
## Other provisions
- ★ Section 2(bc) — defines economic boycott
- ★ Section 6 — applies IPC Section 34, Chapters 3, 4, 5, 5-A, Section 149, Chapter 23
- ★ Section 8 — court may presume abetment, common intention, common object
- ★ Accused cannot claim the right of private defence
- ★ Rules 1995, Rule 17 — District Level Vigilance and Monitoring Committee in each district, by the District Magistrate
## PCR Act
- ★ Section 14 — offences by companies; Director, Manager or Secretary responsible at the time held guilty
- ★ Section 10 — abetment (public servant wilfully neglecting investigation held to abet)
- ★ Untouchability is not an offence when accused and victim belong to the same social group
