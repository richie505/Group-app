### Taxes and cesses → nature, payer, Sultan — Sharia taxes and booty
## 1. Sharia taxes and booty
| Tax | Nature | Payer |
| Kharaj | Land tax: usually 1/3 of produce, varying from 1/10 to 1/2 by crop and region (one-third to one-half of produce) | Non-Muslim cultivators (mainly Hindu peasants) |
| Ushr | Land tax at 1/10 on Muslim-held land (less on irrigated land); a lower rate (5-10%) | Muslim landholders |
| Jizya | Poll or "protection" tax on non-Muslims; Firoz Tughlaq extended it to Brahmins; sometimes waived | Non-Muslims |
| Zakat | Religious tax (about 2.5% of wealth) | Muslims only |
| Khams | One-fifth (1/5) of war booty and treasure-troves, due to the state | State's share of booty (APPSC-G1 2012) |
- Khams (booty share):
- Sharia: state = 1/5, soldiers = 4/5
- Alauddin and MBT took 4/5; Firoz restored 1/5
- Firoz: Kept the Sharia taxes, dropped the others: abolished many illegal cesses; kept only the four sharia taxes (kharaj, jizya, zakat, khams)
- Exam angle: "Khams = 1/5 of war booty" and "Zakat = tax on Muslims only" are the standard traps.
### Taxes and cesses → nature, payer, Sultan — Land revenue and land categories
## 1. Land revenue and land categories
- Land revenue: Peasants paid 1/3 (one-third) of produce, sometimes 1/2 (one-half); Alauddin raised it to 1/2
| Category | Meaning |
| Iqta | Assigned to officials in place of salary |
## 2. Additional cesses
- Haqq-e-Sharb (Firuz Tughlaq): An irrigation tax — 10% of the crop yield
| Cess | Nature | Sultan |
| Ghari | House tax | Alauddin Khalji[GK] |
| Charai | Grazing tax on milch cattle (cattle and sheep) | Alauddin Khalji[GK] |
| Haq-i-sharb (Sharb) | Irrigation tax, one-tenth (about 1/10 of produce) on canal-watered land; paid by users of canal water | Firoz Tughlaq[GK] |
- ⚠ Kharaj = non-Muslims' land tax; Ushr = Muslims' land tax at 1/10
### Agriculture — crops, irrigation, agrarian measures
## 1. Crops
- Crops grown: Wheat, barley, gram, rice, sugarcane, cotton, oilseeds
- Harvests: Ibn Battuta saw two a year (rabi and kharif); rice sown three times a year
- Not grown in the 13th-14th centuries: maize (UPPSC 2011) + tobacco
- Came later with the Portuguese / Europeans
## 2. Irrigation
- Araghatta: Water-wheel used for irrigation
- Persian wheel (saqiya): Popularised by the Turks
- Ghiyasuddin Tughluq:
- First canal-builder of the 14th century: Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (UPPSC 2017)
- Firoz Tughlaq: Built the most canals = extensive canal network
- Longest: Yamuna → Hisar
- Irrigation cess = haq-i-sharb
- ⚠ First canal-builder = Ghiyasuddin; most canals = Firoz
## 3. Agrarian measures of MBT
- Muhammad bin Tughlaq: Diwan-i-Kohi (Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi) + taccavi (sondhar) loans / taqavi loans to extend cultivation
- MBT + Firoz: Both pushed irrigation and "superior crops"
### Crafts, industries and Turkish technologies

- Turkish technologies:
- Paper-making (strengthened bureaucratic record-keeping), glass-making

### Coins → Sultanate and Sur rulers

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:113
### Sharia taxes
## The four Sharia taxes
- ★ Khams (Khama) = state's one-fifth (1/5) share of war booty and treasure-troves
- Kharaj — land tax paid mainly by non-Muslim cultivators; usually one-third
- Ushr — land tax on Muslim-held land; one-tenth of produce
- Jizya on non-Muslims; zakat on Muslims
- Firoz Tughlaq ended illegal cesses, kept only kharaj, jizya, zakat and khams
- Not Sharia: ghari, charai, haq-i-sharb (cesses), taccavi (loans), iqta/inam (land assignments)
## Related terms
- Iqta = land assigned to officials in place of salary
- Ulema = keepers and explainers of religious law; khuts and muqaddams collected revenue from peasants
- Silk and porcelain imported from China; West Asia supplied textiles, glassware and horses
- Kakatiya land: Maagani = irrigated; Metta = rain-fed
### Revenue & cesses
- Alauddin Khalji raised land revenue from one-third to one-half of produce
- Alauddin Khalji introduced Ghari (house tax) and Charai (grazing tax on milch cattle)
- ★ Sharb (Haqq-i-Sharb) = irrigation tax of Firuz Tughluq, at 10% of yield, on advice of religious scholars
### Agriculture & irrigation
## Irrigation
- ★ Araghatta = a large wheel with earthen pots tied to the outer ends of its spokes (Persian wheel); garland of pots on the rim; animal power lifts water from open wells
- ★ Word: Ar (spokes/wheel) + Ghata (pot); used in ancient and early medieval India
- Turks spread the Persian wheel (saqiya)
- Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq — first canal-builder of the 14th century
- Firoz Tughlaq — built the most canals; longest from Yamuna to Hisar
- Muhammad bin Tughlaq — Diwan-i-Kohi; taccavi (sondhar) loans to cultivators to extend cultivation
## Crops and labour
- Maize not grown in 13th–14th c.; brought later by the Portuguese
- Ibn Battuta mentioned slave markets; Firoz Tughlaq kept a separate department for slaves
### Towns & founders
## Founders

- Gold dinars popular after his South Indian conquests

## Other

- Khorasan fruit reached Delhi by the horse-post system

- Multanis and Khurasanis — overseas trade and financing; Bohras — inland trade
### Coins
## Sultanate coins and crafts
- Crafts that grew: leather, metal-work, carpet-weaving, textiles (not printing, tea, rubber)

### Taxes and cesses → nature, payer, Sultan — Land revenue and land categories

- ★ Firuz Tughluq — first to charge Haqq-i-Sharb (irrigation tax)
### Agriculture — crops, irrigation, agrarian measures
- ★ Araghatta = waterwheel (Persian wheel) for irrigation of land
- ★ Operated by animals (bullocks, buffaloes, camels); lifts water from open wells
- ★ Mentioned in the Panchatantra and Rajatarangini
### Coins → Sultanate and Sur rulers
## Sultanate
- ★ Iltutmish — first regular, standardised currency: silver Tanka and copper Jital; declared Delhi capital
- ★ Metals: Tanka silver, Shashgani silver, Jital copper
- ★ Muhammad Ghori — coins with Devi Lakshmi; Kalma in Arabic on some others
## Sher Shah and Akbar
- ★ Sher Shah — pure silver Rupiya (178 grains) and copper Daam (380 grains)
- ★ Exchange rate Rupiya : Daam = 1 : 64
- ★ Sher Shah introduced pure gold, silver and copper coins; ended mixed-metal coins; system continued under the Mughals, basis of British currency
- ★ Akbar, like Sher Shah, controlled the currency: gold coins, silver rupees and copper dams circulated
