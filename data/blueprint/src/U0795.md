### Pollution-control alternatives → meaning (terms)
| Alternative | Scope |
| Green technology | Cleaner processes and products - renewables, efficient kilns (precalciners, dry process), green cement (Portland limestone cement), alternative fuels (biomass, waste-derived fuel) |
| Carbon capture and storage (CCS) | Capture CO2 from point sources (e.g. cement plants) and store it; still largely at research stage - CCS research ongoing for cement plants (UPSC) |
| Bioremediation | Using microbes/plants to clean polluted water and soil; eco-friendly and cost-effective but slower, results vary with conditions (UPSC) |
- Incineration of waste: Conventional method, NOT an alternative (PYQ)
- Conventional end-of-pipe tools: Electrostatic precipitators, scrubbers, selective catalytic reduction (UPSC)
- ⚠ Trap: incineration = conventional; green tech, CCS, bioremediation = alternatives

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 5:105
### Alternatives vs conventional
- Portland limestone cement — green cement (green technology in cement industry)
- Incineration — a conventional waste-treatment method, not an alternative approach
- Jan Vishwas reforms — move from command-and-control to trust-based, technology-enabled regulation
### Acts & rules
- Jan Vishwas reforms — replaced jail with monetary penalties for minor offences
- Environmental (Protection) Fund (Section 16, EP Act; audited by CAG) — 75:25 States/UTs : Centre (2026 Rules)
- Supreme Court, August 2025 — upheld PCB power to charge environmental compensation under Section 33A, Water Act and Section 31A, Air Act
### Clean-up tools & programmes
- CCS — captures CO2 from point sources (e.g. cement plants); largely at research stage
- Bioremediation — eco-friendly, cost-effective but slower; results change with local conditions
- Revised industrial categories — new Blue category (e.g. compressed biogas); compulsory 33% green belt replaced by pollution-potential norms
- PARIVESH 3.0 — single-window portal for environmental clearances and compliance monitoring
- CETPs — serve clusters of small industries; SAAP (1992) — grants to strengthen Pollution Control Boards
- NAAQS — standards for 12 pollutants
### Clean-energy schemes
- PM E-DRIVE (2024) — EV and charging incentives; successor to FAME; PM-KUSUM = farm solar pumps
- UNEP-CCAC 'Hidden Assets' — PMUY a model clean-cooking programme; clean cooking and heating = South Asia's top solution towards 2035
### Eco-labels
- Ecomark Rules (September 2024) — replaced the 1991 scheme; aim to stop false green claims, ensure correct labelling
- PRAMAAN — voluntary third-party certification for sustainable forestry and agroforestry (forest and wood)
- Blue Flag — beach eco-label of the Foundation for Environmental Education (FEE), Denmark (not UNEP, MoEFCC, IUCN)
- 8 Indian beaches recommended for Blue Flag in 2020
### AP measures
- APPCB — computerised risk-based inspection allocation; SOPs for effluents, marine outfalls, hazardous waste
- September 2019 — AP decided to impose and collect a green tax from industrial houses
- BRAP — single-window system; APPCB — siting guidelines (est. 1976)
### Exam corner
- Original Ecomark categories: soaps and detergents, paper, plastics, cosmetics, aerosols; not drugs and antibiotics
### Eco-labels → organisation → scope (Ecomark, Blue Flag, PRAMAAN)

- ★ Launched 1991 by Government of India; drugs and antibiotics not notified under it
