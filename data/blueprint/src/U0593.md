### Causes of seasons — key reason, mechanism and traps
## 1. Why seasons occur
- Earth revolves around the Sun (in 365.25 days) and has an axial tilt of 23½° — Earth is tilted by 23½° as it revolves
- Key reason: Axis inclined 23½° from the perpendicular (66½° to the orbital plane) (APPSC 2018)
- Main consequence of revolution and axial tilt: A changing angle of insolation during the year — revolution and axial tilt produce the seasonal cycle
- ⚠ Earth's rotation causes day and night, not seasons; revolution alone is not sufficient — it needs axial tilt
- Axial tilt combined with revolution causes: Changing day length; solstices and equinoxes; opposite seasons in the hemispheres; six-month polar day and night
- Other factors: Parallelism of the axis (always points to the Pole Star) + revolution + spherical shape → changing Sun angle and day length
## 2. Mechanism
- Earth's position relative to the Sun changes during revolution; the axial tilt remains approximately parallel
- The hemispheres alternately tilt toward and away from the Sun — tilted toward the Sun = summer; other = winter
- The angle of solar rays — the angle of insolation — and the length of day change during the year; one hemisphere receives longer illumination at a time
- Changes in heating produce seasons; the seasons in opposite hemispheres are opposite (South is the exact opposite of the North)
| Term | Meaning |
| Perihelion | Earth nearest the Sun (~3 Jan) during NH winter |
- ⚠ Trap: Distance does not cause seasons; tilt does
### Summer vs winter and insolation — factors and distribution
## 1. Summer vs winter
| Basis | Summer | Winter |
| Hemisphere tilt | Tilted toward the Sun | Tilted away from the Sun |
| Solar rays / angle | More direct | More oblique — more slanting |
| Energy | Greater concentration — Earth receives greater solar energy | Lower concentration — lower solar energy |
| Days | Longer | Shorter |
| Sun's apparent altitude | Higher | Lower |
| Heating | Greater | Lower |
- More vertical or direct rays: Concentrated energy and greater heating; more oblique or slanting rays spread energy over a larger area — lower heating
- The summer hemisphere receives more direct rays and longer days; the winter hemisphere more oblique rays and shorter days
- Seasonal temperature depends on insolation and day length
## 2. Insolation
- Factors:
- angle of incidence (vertical rays heat a smaller area)
- duration of day
- transparency of the atmosphere
- land configuration
- Distribution: Falls from the tropics to the poles
- Maximum over subtropical deserts (cloudless skies), not the Equator
- ⚠ Maximum insolation = subtropical deserts, not the Equator

### Shadows and places in India → overhead Sun, day length, hot season
## 1. Shadows
- A higher Sun in the sky: a smaller shadow; a lower Sun: a longer shadow; a vertically overhead Sun: no shadow — a vertical Sun produces no noon shadow
- Noon shadow at the Tropic of Cancer: Minimum or absent on 21 June (no noon shadow on the Tropic of Cancer)
- North of the Tropic: noon shadows shortest in June, longest in December
- Noon shadow at the Equator: Absent in March and September
## 2. Places in India
- Overhead Sun: twice a year only between the Tropics ("zero-shadow days")
- Places north of 23½°N (Delhi, Srinagar) never get an overhead Sun
- June day length (increases northward): Srinagar > Delhi > Hyderabad > Chennai > Kanyakumari
- AP (12°41'N - 19°07'N): Between the Equator and the Tropic of Cancer → overhead Sun twice a year + high temperatures year-round
- India - hot weather season (March-May): Sun's northward march brings the hot weather season (March-May)
- ~50°C at Ganganagar (1935)

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:4
### Why seasons occur
- Seasons (Sun's angle + day length) come from axial inclination, parallelism of the axis, revolution and the spherical shape of the Earth
- What decides spread of sunlight: angle of incidence, duration of day, atmospheric transparency, land configuration
- Direction of ocean tides is NOT a factor
### Summer vs winter
- Hemisphere tilted towards the Sun = summer hemisphere (e.g. Northern Hemisphere tilted towards Sun → summer)
- Summer hemisphere heats more: more direct rays + longer days; higher Sun, focused energy
- Oblique rays → less heating: same energy spread over a larger area
- Vertically overhead Sun → no shadow at noon
- Higher Sun → shorter shadow; lower Sun → longer shadow
- Midnight sun = Sun visible above the horizon at midnight
### Distance is not the cause
- Seasons caused mainly by axial tilt (solar angle + day length), not distance
- Earth is nearest the Sun during northern winter
### Insolation
- Biggest seasonal changes in day length and sunlight: high latitudes (very large yearly swings)
- Sunlight generally decreases from the tropics towards the poles
- Equator is not always the highest: cloudless subtropical deserts can get the most
### Seasons by latitude
- ★ Christmas in Australia falls in summer — Australia is in the Southern Hemisphere (December = summer there; Northern Hemisphere has winter)
- Axis tilted about 23½°
- Southern Hemisphere's longest day: around 22 December; Northern Hemisphere's: around 21 June
- Four distinct seasons: mid-latitude regions
- Low latitudes: wet and dry periods; subtropics: hot summers, mild winters
