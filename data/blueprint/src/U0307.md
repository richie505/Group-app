- Every Money Bill is a Financial Bill, but not every Financial Bill is a Money Bill
| Government bill | Private member's bill |
| By a minister | Any MP not a minister |
| 7 days' notice | One month's notice |
| Rejection = loss of confidence | No bearing on govt |
| - | Only 14 became law; last 1970 |
### Ordinary bill stages
- 1. First reading: introduction, Gazette ↓
- 2. Second reading: general discussion, committee, clause-by-clause ↓
- 3. Third reading: accept/reject as whole ↓
- 4. Second House: pass, amend, reject or hold 6 months ↓
- 5. President (Art. 111): assent, withhold or return once
### Money Bill (Arts. 109, 110)
- Only in Lok Sabha, by minister, President's prior recommendation
- Taxes, borrowing, Consolidated Fund; fines/fees not money bills
- Speaker certifies, final (110(3))
- RS: recommend within 14 days; cannot amend/reject
- President cannot return; no joint sitting
- Aadhaar: Puttaswamy (2018) upheld; Rojer Mathew (2019) → 7-judge bench
### Money vs Financial Bills
| Feature | Money (110) | FB-I (117(1)) | FB-II (117(3)) |
| Art. 110 matters | Only | Plus others | None; CFI spending |
| Introduced in | LS | LS | Either House |
| Recommendation | Introduction | Introduction | Consideration stage |
| Rajya Sabha | Recommend only | Amend/reject | Amend/reject |
| Joint sitting | No | Yes | Yes |
### Joint sitting (Art. 108)
- Summoned by President; presided by LS Speaker (not VP)
- Grounds: rejection, disagreement on amendments, 6 months delay
- Simple majority of members present & voting; quorum one-tenth
- Excluded: Money bills, amendment bills
- Not summoned → bill ends (APPSC-G2 2017)
### Joint sittings held
- 1961 — Dowry Prohibition Bill
- 1978 — Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill
### Lapse on LS dissolution
| Lapses | Does not lapse |
| Pending in LS | Pending in RS, not passed by LS |
| Passed by LS, pending in RS | Joint sitting notified before dissolution |
| Passed by RS, pending in LS | Pending assent |
| - | Prorogation, adjournment: no lapse |

### Bills → basics; government vs private member's
## 1. Basics
- Bill: Proposed legislation — becomes an Act only after the President's assent (Article 111)
- Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha — the President is an integral part of Parliament but not a member of either House
- Private Member's Bill: Introduced by an MP who is not a Minister — not necessarily a nominated member
## 2. Government vs private member's bill
| Government bill | Private member's bill |
| Introduced by a minister | Any MP who is not a minister |
| 7 days' notice | 1 month's notice (one month's notice) |
| Drafted by department with Law Ministry | Drafted by the member |
| Rejection signals loss of confidence | Defeat has no bearing on / does not affect the government |
- LS committee: Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions
- Classifies them, allots time, examines constitutional amendment bills by private members before introduction
- RS has none → its Business Advisory Committee (BAC) allots time
- Record: Only 14 private members' bills have become law, the last in 1970
### Ordinary bill → stages and President's assent (Art. 111)
## 1. Introduction and readings
- General discussion on a Bill: Second Reading — not the First or Third Reading
- Introduction: In either House (government bill: by a minister, 7 days' notice; private member: 1 month)
- Three readings in each House (APPSC-GS 2018):
| Stage | What happens |
| First reading | Leave to introduce; introduction; title and objectives; published in the Gazette |
| Second reading | General discussion → committee stage (select/joint committee) → consideration/report stage: clause-by-clause voting on amendments |
| Third reading | Bill accepted or rejected as a whole; no amendments of substance [GK] |
## 2. Second House
- Transmission: Same stages in second House; may pass, amend, reject, or keep it for 6 months
- Deadlock: → joint sitting
## 3. President's assent - Art. 111
- Options: Give assent, withhold assent, return for reconsideration (non-money bills only)
- Re-passage: If re-passed with or without amendment → President must assent
- Pocket veto: No time limit fixed → "pocket veto" possible
### Money Bill → Articles 109 and 110, procedure and cases
## 1. Money Bill — Articles 109 and 110
- Introduced: Only in the Lok Sabha, with the President's prior recommendation; only by a minister (APPSC-GS 2019)
- Contents: Provisions on taxes, borrowings, custody of and withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund of India
- A Bill dealing merely with fines or penalties is not a Money Bill
- Rajya Sabha: Only a recommendatory role — recommendations within 14 days; cannot amend or reject it
- LS may accept or reject its recommendations; if not returned → deemed passed
- Certification: The Speaker's decision (Article 110(3)) is final
- Joint Sitting: Not possible
- Every Money Bill is a Financial Bill — but not every Financial Bill is a Money Bill
## 2. Contents - Art. 110(1)
- Scope: Deals only with Art. 110(1) matters
- Tax: imposition, abolition, remission, alteration, regulation
- Custody of Consolidated/Contingency Fund + money in or out of them
- Appropriation from CFI; declaring expenditure charged
- Receipt/custody/audit of CFI or Public Account money; incidental matters
- Not money bills: Fines/penalties, fees for licences or services, taxes by local bodies for local purposes
### Money Bill → Articles 109-111 procedure and Aadhaar cases
## 1. Procedure
| Article | Provision |
| Art. 110(3) | Speaker certifies it; decision is final |
| Art. 109 | Certificate endorsed when sent to the RS; Rajya Sabha must return within 14 days with or without recommendations |
| Art. 111 | Certificate endorsed when sent to the President; President may assent or withhold but cannot return for reconsideration |
- President: In practice assents, since introduced on his recommendation (some notes say he "cannot withhold")
## 2. Aadhaar cases
| Case | Year | Holding |
| K.S. Puttaswamy | 2018 | Upheld Aadhaar Act passed as a Money Bill |
| Rojer Mathew | 2019 | Money-Bill question referred to a larger (7-judge) bench |
### Money Bill vs Financial Bills (I, II) vs Finance Bill
## 1. Financial Bills — Article 117
- Financial Bill (I) — Article 117(1): Contains Article 110 matters plus general legislation
- Only in the Lok Sabha; needs the President's recommendation for introduction
- The Rajya Sabha can reject or amend it fully; Joint Sitting possible
- Financial Bill (II) — Article 117(3): Involves expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India; no Article 110 matters
- In either House; President's recommendation needed before consideration; Joint Sitting possible
## 2. Comparison
| Feature | Money Bill (Art. 110) | Financial Bill I (Art. 117(1)) | Financial Bill II (Art. 117(3)) |
| Art. 110 matters | Exclusively | Partially, plus other matters | Not at all - only expenditure from the CFI |
| Speaker's certificate | Required | Not required | Not required |
| House of introduction | Only LS | Only LS | Either House |
| Who introduces | Minister only | Minister only | Minister or private member |
| President's recommendation | At introduction | At introduction | At consideration stage only |
| Rajya Sabha | Recommend only | Amend or reject | Amend or reject |
| Joint sitting | Not allowed | Allowed | Allowed |
| President returns it? | Cannot | Can | Can |
- FB-I amendments:
- Amendments reducing/abolishing a tax need no recommendation
- Amendments increasing a tax need the President's recommendation
- Finance Bill: Gives effect to the Budget's tax proposals; usually certified a Money Bill
- Relation: Every money bill is a financial bill vs not every financial bill a money bill
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes reverse the definitions and say all financial bills start in LS only → FB-II can start in either House
### Articles → House of introduction and President's prior recommendation
## 1. Articles at a glance
- Article 108: Joint Sittings; Articles 109 and 110: Money Bills; Article 111: Presidential Assent
- Articles 112 to 114: Budget, Demands for Grants, Appropriation Bill; Article 116: Vote-on-Account; Article 117: Financial Bills
- Articles 200 and 201: Governor's and President's assent to State Bills; Article 368: Constitutional amendment
## 2. House of introduction and prior recommendation
- Only in the Lok Sabha (cannot originate in RS): Money Bills and Financial Bills (I)
| Bill | Article | President's prior recommendation |
| Bills to form new states or alter boundaries/names | Art. 3 | Required (either House) |
| Money bills and FB-I | Art. 117(1) | Required |
| FB-II / bills involving expenditure from the CFI | Art. 117(3) | Before consideration |
| Bills affecting taxation in which states are interested | Art. 274 | Required |
- State level: No bill restricting inter-state trade can be introduced in a state legislature without the President's previous sanction
### Joint sitting → Article 108 provisions and instances
## 1. Joint Sitting — Article 108
- Summoned by the President (who expresses the intention to summon it)
- Presided over by the Lok Sabha Speaker → in his absence the Deputy Speaker → then the Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha → a member chosen by those present
- ⚠ Trap: The VP/Chairman does not preside
- Quorum: One-tenth of the total membership; questions decided by simple majority (simple majority of members present and voting of both Houses together)
- Three Joint Sittings so far:
- 1961: Dowry Prohibition Bill [GK]
- 1978: Banking Service Commission Repeal Bill (Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill) [GK]
- 2002: Prevention of Terrorism Act (POTA) (Prevention of Terrorism Bill) [GK]
- ⚠ 1961 – 1978 – 2002 = Dowry – Banking Service Commission – POTA
## 2. Grounds (deadlock)
- After one House passes a bill, the other:
- Rejects it
- Houses finally disagree on amendments
- Does not pass it for more than six months (6 months); periods of prorogation/adjournment over 4 consecutive days excluded
## 3. Procedure
- Summoning: President "notifies his intention" → neither House proceeds with the bill
- ⚠ Key: If President does not summon it, the bill comes to an end (APPSC-G2 2017)
- Excluded: Money bills and constitutional amendment bills (Constitution Amendment Bills)
- Financial bills (I) and (II) can go to a joint sitting
## 4. Other rules and constitutional basis
- Rule 8(1): Election of the Deputy Speaker — date fixed by the Speaker, notified by the Secretary-General
- Money Bill: Article 110; Joint Sitting: Article 108
### Lapsing of bills — dissolution, prorogation, adjournment
## 1. Lapse of Bills on dissolution of the Lok Sabha
- Lapses: A Bill pending in the Lok Sabha; a Bill passed by the Lok Sabha but pending in the Rajya Sabha
- Does not lapse: A Bill pending in the Rajya Sabha not yet passed by the Lok Sabha; a Bill for which a Joint Sitting has been notified
- Prorogation does not cause a Bill to lapse
| Lapses | Does not lapse |
| Bill pending in LS (originated there or sent by RS) | Bill pending in RS not yet passed by LS |
| Bill passed by LS, pending in RS | Bill for which President has notified a joint sitting before dissolution |
| Bill passed by RS, pending in LS | Bill passed by both Houses, pending assent / returned by the President for reconsideration [GK] |
- Rajya Sabha: Never dissolved
- Joint sitting: Cannot be summoned for a bill that has already lapsed
- Exam angle: "passed by LS but not yet taken up in RS does not lapse" is false (APPSC-G1 2024 statement).
| Event | Bills | Motions / notices |
| Adjournment / adjournment sine die | Do not lapse - taken up next session (PYQ APPSC-GS 2025) | Continue [GK] |
| Prorogation | Do not lapse[GK] | Pending notices lapse, except notices for introducing bills [GK] |
| Dissolution of Lok Sabha | See table above [GK] | All pending before the LS lapse [GK] |

### Money Bill (Arts. 109, 110)
## Scope (Art. 110)
- Article 110(1) — putting, removing, reducing, changing or regulating any tax
- Article 110(2) — not a Money Bill just for fines or penalties, licence fees or local taxes; taxes, borrowing and the Consolidated Fund are Money Bill matters
## Procedure
- ★ Money Bill presented in the Lok Sabha
- Introduced only in the Lok Sabha, only by a minister, only on the President's recommendation
- No joint sitting for a Money Bill (governed by Article 109)
- Speaker's certificate needed for a Money Bill, not for a Financial Bill-I; joint sitting allowed for Financial Bills I and II
- President cannot return a Money Bill; can return Financial Bills I and II
- Article 274 — Bills on taxation in which States are interested; spending from the CFI needs recommendation under Article 117(3)
- No joint sitting for a Bill that has lapsed on dissolution
## Cases and States
- K.S. Puttaswamy (2018) — Aadhaar Act upheld as a Money Bill; Rojer Mathew (2019) — issue referred to a seven-judge bench
- AP Legislative Council — only recommendations within 14 days on a Money Bill; cannot reject or amend finally
- November 2025 advisory opinion — courts cannot fix timelines or declare deemed assent; did not uphold the Tamil Nadu deemed assent

| Ordinary Bill | 107–108 | Either House | — |
| Constitution Amendment | 368 | — | — |
- Article 110(3) — Speaker's decision on a Money Bill is final; certificate put on the Bill sent to RS and the President

## Private Members' Bills

## Joint sitting and dissolution
- Six-month count (Art. 108) skips prorogation and adjournments over four days running; other grounds: rejection, final disagreement on amendments
- Joint sitting chaired by Speaker → Deputy Speaker → RS Deputy Chairman → a member chosen by those present; the RS Chairman does not preside
- Lapses on dissolution — a Bill passed by the Lok Sabha, pending in the Rajya Sabha
- Article 79 — Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha; President is not a member of either House
### Joint sitting (Art. 108)
- Amendment reducing or abolishing a tax — no President's recommendation needed; increasing a tax needs it
- President does not summon a joint sitting — the Bill comes to an end
- Decision — simple majority of members of both Houses present and voting together
- Not in joint sitting — Money Bills and Constitution Amendment Bills; Financial Bills I and II can go

### Money Bill → Articles 109 and 110, procedure and cases
## Definition and introduction
- ★ Money Bill defined in Article 110; counts as one if it has only Art. 110 matters (taxes, borrowing, etc.)
- ★ Introduced only in the Lok Sabha; Article 109(1) — not in the Rajya Sabha
- ★ Speaker's decision on a Money Bill is final; no need to consult; no joint session
- ★ Every Money Bill is a Finance Bill, but not every Finance Bill is a Money Bill; both introduced only in the Lok Sabha on the President's recommendation
## Rajya Sabha's role
- ★ Rajya Sabha can amend or reject a Finance (financial) Bill; cannot alter a Money Bill
- ★ Financial bills — Money Bills (Art. 110), Financial Bills (I) (Art. 117(1)), Financial Bills (II) (Art. 117(3))
- ★ RS may hold a Money Bill at most 14 days; cannot amend or reject it (Art. 109)
- ★ Lok Sabha is not bound to accept the Rajya Sabha's recommendations
## President and joint sitting
- ★ President cannot return a Finance (Money) Bill — not his right (Article 111)
- ★ Joint sitting — Article 108 (not 109); chaired by the Speaker
### Money Bill vs Financial Bills (I, II) vs Finance Bill
- ★ Rajya Sabha returns a Money/Finance Bill within fourteen days; ordinary Bill — no time limit
- ★ Bill only involving expenditure (no Art. 110 matter) — can start in either House
- ★ "No bill imposing tax without the President's recommendation" — Article 117
- ★ Special rules on Financial Bills — Article 117; Art. 119 — regulation by law; Art. 121 — limit on discussion
### Articles → House of introduction and President's prior recommendation
- ★ Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend a Money Bill
- ★ Rajya Sabha cannot vote on the Demands for Grants, but can discuss the Annual Financial Statement
- ★ President's prior sanction needed for Bills on: new State, taxation in which States are interested, altering State boundaries, Money Bill
### Joint sitting → Article 108 provisions and instances
- ★ Joint sitting — Article 108; Article 109 is the Money Bill procedure
- ★ Applies only to ordinary bills; not to Money Bills or Constitution Amendment Bills (Art. 368)
- ★ Summoned by the President; needs the intention of the President
- ★ Grounds — Bill rejected by the other House, or Houses finally disagree on amendments, etc.
- ★ Chaired by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha (Art. 118(4))
### Lapsing of bills — dissolution, prorogation, adjournment
- ★ Joint session already summoned, then Lok Sabha dissolved — joint session held at the scheduled time
- ★ Bill already lapsed on dissolution — no joint sitting can be summoned
### Universal angles
- ★ Appropriation Bill — a Money Bill; RS need not pass it — treated as passed if not returned within 14 days
- ★ Money Bill greatly amended by RS — Lok Sabha may still proceed, accepting or rejecting the recommendations (Art. 109(2))
