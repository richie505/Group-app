## 1. Repugnancy - Article 254
- Art. 254: Concurrent List → Parliament's law prevails over a repugnant State law
- Art. 254(2): State law with President's assent prevails in that State unless Parliament later overrides it

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:23
### Doctrines → meaning
| Doctrine | Meaning |
| Colourable legislation | What cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly; about legislative competence, not motive |
| Harmonious construction | Read apparently conflicting provisions (incl. FRs and DPSPs) so both can operate |
| Incidental and ancillary powers | A legislative entry covers matters incidental to its subject; entries given a wide meaning |
| Constitutional morality | Fidelity to constitutional values over popular morality |
- 'Constitutional morality' — used by Ambedkar, after Grote
### Cases → doctrine
- State of Bombay v. F.N. Balsara (1951) — Prohibition Act upheld as a public-health law though it also touched imports
- State of Bombay v. R.M.D.C. (1957) — territorial nexus; State tax on prize competitions run from outside the State
- Sri Venkataramana Devaru (1958) — harmonious construction of Art. 25(2)(b) (temple entry) and Art. 26(b) (denominations' rights)
- A.S. Krishna (1957) — real nature of a law judged from the whole Act, its object and effect, not just its title

### Judicial & executive doctrines
- Prospective overruling — first applied in Golaknath (1967)
- Pleasure doctrine — Article 310, with safeguards in Article 311 against unfair dismissal, removal or reduction in rank
- Indian separation of powers — functional, not rigid (Ram Jawaya Kapur, 1955); part of the basic structure (Kesavananda Bharati)
### Repugnancy (Art. 254)
- Article 141 — law declared by the SC binds all courts in India (stare decisis)
- Article 254(2) — even after President's assent, Parliament may later override the State law
### Exam corner
- Pith and substance — settles Union-State law clashes by looking at a law's true nature
- Criticism of constitutional morality — subjective, a possible path to judicial overreach
