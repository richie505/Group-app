### Stock exchanges — NSE and BSE
## 1. Stock exchanges
- A stock exchange: An organized market for trading securities and shares — part of the securities market and the capital market
- Government securities can be traded through securities-market platforms
## 2. National Stock Exchange
- Established in November 1992 on the recommendation of the Manohar J. Pherwani Committee — headquarters Mumbai
- Recognized by SEBI in April 1993; began operations in 1994
- First segment: The Wholesale Debt Market; the Cash Market was subsequently launched
- Original promoters: IDBI, SBI, IFCI, LIC, GIC, IDFC
## 3. Bombay Stock Exchange
- BSE: Bombay Stock Exchange — moved to Dalal Street (Mumbai) in 1874; Dalal Street is associated with the BSE and the share market
- Sensex: A popular index of the BSE — a free-float market-capitalization-weighted index
- Represents the overall movement of the selected 30 companies
- A rise in Sensex indicates an overall rise in the prices of the group of 30 companies — ⚠ not a rise in the prices of all BSE shares
- CDSL: A depository — initially promoted by BSE
### Stock exchanges — places, years and FIRSTs

### Terms → meaning: market participants, depositories and investor claims
## 1. Market terms
- Bull, Bear, Stag and Lame Duck: Associated with the share market
- Bull: Expects prices to rise — a bull market has generally rising prices
- Bear: Expects prices to fall — a bear market has generally declining prices
- Blue-chip shares: Large, well-established, financially sound companies
- Beta: Measures a security's fluctuations — systematic risk — relative to the overall market (beta 1 = moves with market) [GK]
- STT: Securities Transaction Tax — applies to specified securities transactions on domestic exchanges
## 2. Other terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Stag | Buys IPO shares to sell quickly on listing [GK] |
| Short selling | Sell borrowed shares, buy back lower |
| Circuit breaker | Trading halt when the index or a stock moves beyond set limits [GK] |
| Sweat equity | Shares to employees/directors for know-how or services, often at a discount |
| Depositories | NSDL (first, Mumbai, 6 Nov 1996) and CDSL (promoted by BSE [GK]) - hold shares in demat form |
- Settlement: T+1 with optional T+0 same-day; older notes say rolling settlement of 5 days
## Investor claims
| Holder / instrument | Claim |
| Shareholders | Owners (dividends, residual claim) |
| Debenture/bondholders | Debenture/bondholders are creditors → paid before shareholders |
| Mutual funds | Pool money, priced at NAV; arbitrage funds must keep ≥65% in equity (SEBI) |

### Primary vs secondary market
- Primary market — company sells new securities to investors
- Secondary market — existing securities; investors trade on exchanges; SEBI regulates exchange operations
- Circuit breaker — halts trading when an index or stock moves past limits
- T+1 — settlement one trading day after the trade; optional T+0 available
### IPO terms
- Offer for sale — proceeds go to selling shareholders; only a fresh issue brings money to the firm
- Book building — bids within a price band (floor to cap); final price set from bids
- Anchor investors — QIBs bidding before the issue opens, with lock-in
- Listing within T+3 days of issue closing; SME IPOs on BSE SME and NSE Emerge
