### Congress ministries, 1937-39 → formation, measures, resignation
## 1. Formation (1937)
- Result: Congress formed ministries in 8 of 11 provinces
- July 1937: Bombay, Madras, Central Provinces, Orissa, Bihar, United Provinces
- Later: NWFP and Assam → total 8
- Clear majority on its own in 5 provinces (Madras, UP, CP, Bihar, Orissa)
- Gandhi's advice: hold office "lightly, not tightly", as a "crown of thorns"
## 2. Legislative record
| Area | Measure |
| Civil liberties | emergency powers lifted; political prisoners released; bans on leftist bodies lifted; press freedom restored |
| Agrarian | Tenancy Acts (e.g. UP); abolition of forced labour (begar); debt relief |
| Labour | Industrial Disputes Act (1938) + trade-union rights |
| Harijans | free education + other upliftment measures |
## 3. Resignation (1939)
- Resignation: Oct-Nov 1939 (some notes: October 1939; others: November 1939)
- Reason: Viceroy Linlithgow declared India a party to World War II (Sept 1939) without consulting Indian leaders
- League reaction: "Deliverance Day", 22 December 1939
- Exam angle: APPSC 2018 asked the reason: protest against making India a party to the war with Germany.
### Features borrowed into the Constitution → 1935 and 1919 Acts
| Source | Borrowed |
| GoI Act 1935 | Federal scheme, office of Governor, judiciary, Public Service Commissions, emergency provisions, administrative details |
| Instrument of Instructions (1935 Act) | Model for the Directive Principles (APPSC 2023) |
| GoI Act 1919 | Bicameralism and direct elections at Centre; Public Service Commission; separate provincial budget |
- Governor's address: Section 63 of 1935 Act → Governor addressed provincial legislature "in his discretion"
- Constitution's Article 175 (address) carries the idea; speech reflects the elected ministry's policy

- Current relevance: Governors' address rows in Opposition-ruled States traced to Sec. 63 of 1935 Act and Art. 175 (26 Jan 2026)
