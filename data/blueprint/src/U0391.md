### Communalism vs religious fundamentalism — meaning and FIRSTs
## 1. Religious fundamentalism
- Strict adherence to religious beliefs and practices — often rejects modern interpretations
- Features: Literal interpretation of scriptures; religious conservatism; resistance to change; group exclusiveness
- Causes: Identity crisis; globalization; social insecurity; political mobilization; economic inequalities
## 2. Communalism
- Indian perception: Communalism most often seen as religious difference between groups → tension and rioting
- A group promotes its interests at the expense of others
| Religious fundamentalism | Communalism |
| Return to the "fundamentals" and literal scripture; mainly doctrinal | Political mobilisation of religious identity against other communities |
| Directed at reforming one's own community (often anti-modern) | Directed against other communities; seeks power and resources |
| Can exist without inter-group conflict | Always relational - "us vs them" |
| 1909 Act | First legal separate electorate |
| 1906 | First communal political party (Muslim League) |
- ⚠ 1906 = first communal party (Muslim League); 1909 Act = first legal separate electorate

### Types and stages — Oommen's six dimensions, Bipan Chandra's three stages
## 1. Oommen's six dimensions
| Dimension | Meaning | Example |
| Assimilationist | Small religious groups absorbed into a larger one | Claiming STs, Jains, Sikhs, Buddhists are Hindus |
| Welfarist | Works for the welfare of one community only | Christian or Parsi associations |
| Retreatist | Small community keeps away from politics | Bahai community |
| Retaliatory | Attempts to harm members of other communities | Riots, revenge cycles |
| Separatist | Wants a separate territorial state within India for cultural specificity | Gorkhaland, Bodoland |
| Secessionist | Wants an independent state outside India | Khalistan, "Azad Kashmir" |
- Source: CDX Indian Society Pt 1 pp.38-39; UPSC Indian Society p.79
- ⚠ Separatist = state within India (Gorkhaland, Bodoland); Secessionist = state outside India (Khalistan)
## 2. Forms
- Political = uses religion for votes
- Economic = grows from competition for jobs and resources
## 3. Bipan Chandra's three stages
| Stage | Features |
| 1. Communal nationalism | Belief that followers of one religion share common secular (political, economic) interests; still nationalist in outlook |
| 2. Liberal communalism | Secular interests of one religious community are seen as different from those of others, but can be reconciled within one nation |
| 3. Extreme communalism | Interests seen as mutually antagonistic; politics of fear, hatred and violence |
- After the 1937 elections: Muslim League turned to extreme communalism "based on fear, hatred and violence"
- Echoed by Hindu Mahasabha and RSS (UPSC Modern History p.300)
### Causes of communalism — factor → cause
| Type | Causes |

| Legal / administrative | Delayed justice, lenient punishment, police inaction, biased intelligence (APP; CDX) |
| Social / media | Fake news and hate messages on social media; media amplifying communal narratives (APP; UPSC) |

## 1. Lucknow Pact - critique
- Lucknow Pact critique: Short-term Hindu-Muslim unity, but Congress accepted separate electorates and weightage
- → Legitimised communal politics = groundwork for Partition
## 2. Communal Award and Poona Pact
- Communal Award:
- Separate electorates for Muslims, Sikhs, Christians + Depressed Classes (for 20 years)
- Poona Pact: Gandhi vs/with Ambedkar
- Replaced Dalit separate electorates with reserved seats in joint electorates (71 → 148 seats)
- ⚠ Poona Pact: 71 → 148 reserved seats in joint electorates; Communal Award separate electorates were for 20 years
### Articles, Acts and bodies against communalism
## 1. Religion laws
- Places of Worship (Special Provisions) Act: Enacted 1991; came into force on July 11, 1991; a statutory safeguard
- Maintains — freezes — the religious character of places of worship as it existed on 15 August 1947 (August 15, 1947)
- Has specific statutory exceptions to its freeze
- Religious Institutions (Prevention of Misuse) Act: Enacted 1988 — prohibits the use of religious premises for political activities, harboring criminals and harboring arms (bars use of religious places for political or communal ends)
## 2. Constitutional and electoral provisions
| Provision | What it does |
| Preamble | "Secular" added by the 42nd Amendment (1976) |
| Art. 14-16 | Equality |
| Art. 25-28 | Freedom of religion |
| Art. 29-30 | Minority rights |
| Art. 51A(e) | Promote harmony |
| Sec. 123(3), RP Act 1951 | Appeal on the ground of religion = corrupt practice (electoral) |
- Institutional bodies: National Integration Council, National Foundation for Communal Harmony, MHA guidelines on communal harmony

- ⚠ Trap: APPSC-GS 2007 key treats it as the development that "contributed to the spirit of communal harmony"

### Fundamentalism vs communalism
- Religious fundamentalism — mainly doctrinal; return to basics, literal scripture
- Communalism — political mobilisation of religious identity against other communities for power and resources
- Fundamentalism can exist without inter-group conflict
- Fundamentalism features — group exclusiveness, literal reading of scripture, conservatism, resisting change
- Causes of fundamentalism — identity crisis, social insecurity (with globalisation); exclusiveness is a feature, not a cause
### Scholars & leaders
- Ram Ahuja — communalism an ideology: religious communities with different, often opposing interests; communal persons do politics through religion
- Robert Melson and Howard Wolpe — "the political assertiveness of a community to maintain its identity in a plural society undergoing modernisation"
- Bipan Chandra — followers of one religion share common social, political and economic interests
- Lord Minto — "Father of the Communal Electorate" (separate electorates for Muslims)
### Bipan Chandra's three stages
- Bipan Chandra — communalism: one religion = common social, political, economic interests
- Stages — communal nationalism → liberal communalism → extreme communalism
- Liberal — interests different but reconcilable within one nation
- Extreme — interests mutually antagonistic
- After 1937 elections — Muslim League to extreme communalism based on fear, hatred and violence; echoed by Hindu Mahasabha and RSS
### Oommen's six dimensions
- Secessionist — independent state outside India, e.g. Khalistan
