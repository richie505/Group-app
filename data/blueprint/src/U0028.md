### Split & sub-sects
- Origin: 12-year famine under Chandragupta Maurya (Parishishtaparvan)
- Formal split c. 3rd century CE
- Yapaniya: bridge sect, active in Andhra (Yapanacharyas)
- Sthanakvasi (against idol worship) & Terapanthi: Svetambara offshoots
### Jain canon
- 1. 14 Purvas (earliest, mostly lost) ↓
- 2. 12 Angas (Ganadharas) ↓
- 3. Svetambara: 12 Angas, 12 Upangas, 10 Prakirnas, 6 Cheda, 4 Mula Sutras ↓
- 4. Language: Ardhamagadhi Prakrit; Digambaras reject this canon

- Digambara: Sky-clad; Shwetambara: white-clad
- Origin of split: 12-year famine under Chandragupta Maurya (Parishishtaparvan)
- Formal split dated to around the 3rd century CE; another view links it to the first council (c. 300 BCE)

### Jain canon — terms → meaning
- Jain literature includes the Agam
| Term | Meaning |
| Agamas / Siddhanta | Sacred texts, in eastern Prakrit Ardhamagadhi |
| 14 Purvas | Earliest texts, now mostly lost |
| 12 Angas | Mahavira's Ganadharas compiled his teaching into these |
| Svetambara canon | 12 Angas, 12 Upangas, 10 Prakirnas, 6 Cheda Sutras, 4 Mula Sutras + others |
| Digambara canon | Digambaras reject it - hold all original Angas lost; separate canon |
| Acharanga Sutra | Code of conduct for monks |
| Bhagavati Sutra | Doctrines |
- Evolution: 14 Purvas (earliest, now mostly lost) → 12 Angas
- Key Angas: Acharanga, Bhagavati; early texts also Sutrakritanga, Kalpasutra
- Exam angle: odd-one-out - Jain texts (Acharanga, Bhagavati, Kalpasutra) vs Buddhist (Dipavamsa, Mahavamsa, Divyavadana, Milindapanho).

### Spread and Jain sites → region
## 1. Southward spread
- Southward spread: 12-year famine in Magadha under Chandragupta Maurya
- Bhadrabahu (6th Thera) led monks, with Chandragupta, to Shravanabelagola (Karnataka) - APPSC G2 2003
- Route through Rayalaseema (Kurnool, Anantapur); early ascetic camps in Erramala and Nallamala caves
- Other centres:
- Early Christian era: Mathura and Ujjain
- Later: Gujarat (Chalukyas), Karnataka (Gangas, Rashtrakutas)
- Survived in pockets of Gujarat, Rajasthan, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu

### Jainism in AP — FIRSTs, centres, patrons, decline
## 1. FIRSTs
- First royal dynasties in Andhra to follow Jainism: Satavahanas → Eastern Chalukyas → Kakatiyas
- Early Kakatiyas were Jains (Siddeshwara Charitra)
## 2. Centres
| Centre | Note |
| Potla Cheruvu (Potlacheruvu) | Famous Jain centre in the Eastern Chalukya period (APPSC G2 2017) |
| Nedumba Vasati at Bejawada (Vijayawada) | Nedumbasadi built by Ayyana Mahadevi, wife of Eastern Chalukya Vishnuvardhana; village Mushinikonda donated |
| Ramatirtham (Ramagiri, Vizianagaram) | Eastern Chalukya-era Jain hill centre; Ugraditya composed Kalyanakaraka here |
| Vaddamanu (Vaddemanu) | Samprati's vihara (also Kharavela); later Sadavanshiya patronage |
| Danavulapadu (Kadapa) | Rashtrakuta Indra III |
| Aryavatam (Bikkavolu), Kolanupaka | Other Jain centres |
## 3. Patrons
- Eastern Chalukyas: Vimaladitya; Durgaraja built the Kathikabharana Jinalaya at Dharmavaram
- Kakatiya: Minister Betana's wife Mailama built the Kadalalaya Basadi (1117 CE Padmakshi inscription)
- Vijayanagara: Devaraya II built a Jinalaya in Pan Supari Bazar
## 4. Decline
- Ganapatideva: Persecuted Jains (Siddeshwara Charitra)
- Rajaraja Narendra: A Jain monk fled his persecution to Hanamkonda (Orugallu Kaifiyat)
- ⚠ Nedumbasadi = Ayyana Mahadevi; Kadalalaya Basadi = Mailama; Kathikabharana Jinalaya = Durgaraja
### Contributions of Jainism — language, literature, social base
## 1. Language and literature
- Language: Early Jains discarded Sanskrit, preached in popular Prakrit - key to spread; canon in Ardhamagadhi
- Apabhramsa: Earliest important works in Apabhramsa + its first grammar
- Later literature: Rich Prakrit, Sanskrit and regional (Kannada, Tamil, Telugu) - grammar, poetry, mathematics
## 2. Social base
- Base: traders and commercial communities / traders and merchants; banking; urban growth
- Jain merchant guilds moved on Satavahana trade routes
- Social reform: Challenged caste hierarchy; action over birth; promoted vegetarianism
- Andhra: Under Eastern Chalukyas, Shudras adopted Jainism for social advancement
### Universal angles
- AP angle:
- Jainism held the rocky Rayalaseema + North Coastal borderlands; Buddhism held the Krishna-Godavari delta
- Kalamukha/Vira Shaiva hostility: Ganapatideva persecuted Jains at Anmakonda
- Anmakonda = refuge from Eastern Chalukya persecution
- Criticism & debates (decline): Loss of royal patronage, sect split, severe austerity, no missionary zeal, rival Bhakti revival
- Current relevance: PM inaugurated the Samrat Samprati Museum at Koba, Gujarat (Apr 2026); Samprati ruled c. 230-215 BCE
- Significance:
- Literature: enriched Prakrit, Apabhramsa, Kannada, Telugu
- Architecture: Dilwara, Ellora, Udayagiri
