### MRTP Act, 1969 → purpose, thresholds and replacement
## 1. Purpose & origin
- Purpose: Control private monopoly + concentration of economic power; curb monopolistic, restrictive and unfair trade practices (APPSC-GS 2019 key)
- Recommended by: Dutt Committee (Industrial Licensing Policy Inquiry Committee)
- Timeline: Enacted 1969, effective 1970
## 2. MRTP firm thresholds
| Year | Threshold |
| Original | Assets ₹20 crore+ or 33% market share |
| 1985 | Asset limit raised to ₹100 crore |
| NIP 1991 | Scrapped the MRTP asset threshold (Rs 100 crore since 1985) → large firms no longer needed prior approval to expand, merge or take over |
## 3. Replacement
- Replaced by Competition Act, 2002: MRTP Commission → CCI (APPSC-GS 2018 key: 2002)
- Some notes say "Competition Act, 2001"
- ⚠ MRTP market share 33% → 25% (1984); assets ₹20 crore → ₹100 crore (1985)
### Competition Act, 2002 and CCI → provisions and cases
- Recommended by: S.V.S. Raghavan Committee (2000); introduced by Arun Jaitley
- CCI:
- Established 14 Oct 2003
- Chairperson + 2-6 members

- Act amended 2007, 2009, 2023

| Abuse of dominant position (s.4) [GK] | Unfair pricing, denial of market access |
| Combinations (s.5-6) [GK] | Mergers/acquisitions above thresholds; deemed approval if CCI misses deadline (ES 2025-26) |

### LPG terms → meaning (disinvestment, privatisation, monetisation)
| Term | Meaning |
| Disinvestment | Sale of government equity in PSEs; government may keep majority and control - reduction of government share in public sector, the term for reducing government share (APPSC-GS 2019) |
| Privatisation | Transfer of ownership and management control to private sector (strategic sale) |
| Strategic sale | Block of shares (usually 50%+) + transfer of management control to a strategic partner |
| Liberalisation | Removal of licences, quotas and controls (not a sale); State acts as facilitator |
| Monetisation | Leases brownfield, de-risked public assets (roads, railways, power lines, pipelines) to private operators for a fixed period; Ownership stays with government; assets return |
| Type | Government stake / control |
| Minority disinvestment | Keeps more than 51% and control |
| Majority disinvestment | Control transferred, minority stake kept |
| Complete privatisation | 100% sold - ownership and control to buyer - most comprehensive mode |
- ⚠ Monetisation leases assets vs Disinvestment = sells equity permanently

### Disinvestment → rationale, DIPAM, phases and criticism

| Section | Covers |

## Related terms and bodies
- Disinvestment — sale of government equity in PSEs; liberalisation — removal of licences, quotas, controls
- BIFR (created under SICA 1985) — dissolved 1 December 2016; cases went to NCLT / NCLAT
- IBC — enacted 28 May 2016 after the T.K. Viswanathan-led BLRC (2015)
- NCLT — set up 1 June 2016 under Section 408, Companies Act 2013; NCLAT — Section 410
### Key terms
- Disinvestment — reduces government equity (government may keep control); privatisation — transfers ownership and management control to the private sector
- Minority disinvestment — government keeps more than 51% and control; majority disinvestment — transfers control, keeps a minority stake
- Strategic sale — block of shares, usually 50% or more, with transfer of management control to a strategic partner
- DIPAM instruments: CPSE ETF, Offer for Sale, IPO/FPO, strategic sale (SLR, CRR, repo are monetary/banking tools)
- Worries about disinvestment: undervaluation, loss of dividend income, national-security risks (oil, defence, transport)
- Asset monetisation — leases public assets for a fixed time, ownership stays with government; disinvestment — permanent sale of equity
